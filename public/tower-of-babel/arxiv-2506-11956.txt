Analysis of BDDC preconditioners for non-conforming polytopal hybrid discretisation methods Thanks: * Corresponding author.
Santiago Badia † *, Jerome Droniou ♯†, Jordi Manyer †, Jai Tushar †

Abstract
. In this work, we build on the discrete trace theory developed in [Badia, Droniou, Tushar, arXiv (2024)] to analyse the rate of convergence of the Balancing Domain Decomposition by Constraints (BDDC) preconditioner generated from non-conforming polytopal hybrid discretisation. We prove polylogarithmic condition number bounds for the preconditioner that are independent of the mesh parameter and number of subdomains, and hold on polytopal meshes. The analysis hinges on the continuity of a face truncation operator, which we prove in the fully discrete polytopal setting. To validate the theory, we present two numerical experiments: the first verifies the truncation estimate, and the second — a weak scalability test — verifies the robustness of the condition number bounds for BDDC when applied to second-order elliptic problems discretised using discontinuous skeletal methods, specifically Hybridizable Discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) and Hybrid High-Order (HHO) methods.



1.
Introduction

Polytopal methods are a class of
fem
that have gained popularity in recent years due to their ability to relax conformity constraints on meshes. This flexibility makes them well-suited for handling complex geometries. Moreover, the support for general polytopal elements facilitates adaptive mesh refinement and coarsening—key features for applications such as the modelling of geophysical flows
[
17
]
, where the mesh may include elements that are unnecessarily small or large in certain regions. In addition to addressing limitations of traditional
fem
from a mesh perspective, polytopal methods eliminate the need for predefined local spaces by reconstructing quantities of interest directly from degrees of freedom. This approach simplifies the extension to higher-order schemes. For more details, we refer to several popular polytopal
fem
s: the
hho
(
hho
) method
[
19
,
11
]
, the
hdg
(
hdg
) method
[
13
]
, the
vem
(
vem
)
[
5
]
, the
wg
(
wg
) method
[
35
]
, and the
dg
(
dg
) method
[
10
]
, and references therein.

The design of efficient, robust and scalable solvers for linear systems arising from these kinds of discretisations is important to make them competitive with traditional methods in real-world applications. These include, but are not limited to,
gmg
(
gmg
) and non-overlapping
ddm
(
ddm
) solvers. We mention for example
[
12
,
15
,
16
]
as a few references on the design and analysis of GMG solvers for polytopal methods. In this work, we focus on non-overlapping
ddm
-based solvers, which include
bddc
(
bddc
)
[
22
]
and
feti-dp
(
feti-dp
)
[
27
]
. These solvers rely on the exchange of information across inter-subdomain boundaries, and require three main ingredients: a
trace inequality
, which implies that the restriction of functions to the subdomain interface is stable; a
lifting result
, which lifts this restriction to the interior of the neighbouring subdomain; and continuity of a face truncation operator on piecewise polynomial functions in
H^{1}(\Omega)
. The trace and lifting results facilitate the stable propagation of “information” globally between subdomains. In other words, trace theory bounds the injection operator from the space of discontinuous solutions across subdomain boundaries into the original space, defined via a weighting operator and a subdomain-local discrete harmonic extension. The bound on the face restriction operator leads to a mesh-dependent logarithmic estimate observed in
bddc
and
feti-dp
preconditioners. We note that the condition number of
bddc
and
feti-dp
preconditioned systems are identical.

For gradient-conforming
fem
, this is realised with the help of continuous trace theory and standard piecewise polynomial truncation estimates
[
31
,
9
]
. These preconditioners
[
24
]
and their analyses have been extended to other conforming polytopal discretisations, such as the conforming
vem

[
6
,
7
]
and isogeometric analysis
[
4
]
. For non-conforming
fem
, the trace inequality and lifting property are non-trivial, since the trace of piecewise polynomial functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
do not have
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-regularity, where
\Omega
is the polytopal domain of interest in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d\geq 2)
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. In the current state-of-the art, the analysis of solvers for hybridised formulations relies on the existence of an interpolant onto a conforming finite element space and then leveraging the continuous trace theory. This idea was originally proposed in
[
14
]
for
bnn
(
bnn
) preconditioning of mixed
fem
and further extended to other non-conforming discretisations, e.g.,
dg

[
21
]
,
hdg

[
32
]
, and
wg

[
33
]
. However, since this approach hinges on the construction of a conforming interpolant, all the analyses so far have been limited to conforming simplicial or quadrilateral/hexahedral meshes. Furthermore, to the best of our knowledge, there is no literature available addressing non-conforming
vem
or
hho
.

In this work, we derive condition number bounds for
bddc
and
feti-dp
preconditioners for non-conforming polytopal hybrid discretisations on quasi-uniform general polytopal meshes (see Assumption
1.1
). In order to do this, we rely on the discrete trace theory recently developed in
[
2
]
for non-conforming polytopal methods. Without the need of conforming interpolants, this reference designs a discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm and shows that it enjoys properties analogous to the continuous trace seminorm, namely discrete trace inequality and discrete lifting with respect to a discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm. To be applicable to the analysis of
ddm
, we extend the theory by analysing the continuity of face truncation operators. Additionally, we have implemented the proposed solvers in GridapSolvers.jl
[
29
]
, a package that provides distributed linear solvers for Gridap.jl, a Julia library for the numerical approximation of
pde
(
pde
)
[
34
,
3
]
. Through extensive numerical experiments conducted on a supercomputer, we have demonstrated the robustness and weak scalability of our solvers, successfully scaling up to several hundreds of processors while maintaining optimal performance.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. We first recall the construction of the discrete trace seminorm and main results from
[
2
]
in Section
1.1
. In Section
2
, we extend the theory developed in
[
2
]
by analysing the continuity of the face truncation operator with respect to the discrete trace seminorm on the discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm (see Section
2
and Theorem
2.2
). The design of the
bddc
preconditioner based on hybrid spaces in a functional setting is described in Section
3.1
, with the main result on robustness of the preconditioner with respect to mesh size stated in Theorem
3.1
and proved in Section
3.3
. In Section
4
, we numerically verify the theoretical results. We design an experiment in Section
4.2
which verifies the truncation estimate of Theorem
2.2
. We also present weak scalability tests (see Section
4.3
) for the proposed preconditioner on two classes of discontinuous skeletal methods, namely
hdg
and
hho
. A brief description of the tested methods is given in Section
4.1
. The tests verify the theoretical condition number bounds of Theorem
3.1
and show robustness of the preconditioner with respect to mesh size and number of subdomains.

1.1.
Preliminaries

We briefly recall here the setting and main results in
[
2
]
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded polytopal domain for
d\geq 2
. We consider a partition of
\Omega
into disjoint polytopes gathered in a set
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, which we refer to as
cells
. The mesh
skeleton
is the union of the boundaries of the cells (composed of faces), and for each
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
denotes the set of faces of
t
. We define the set of boundary faces as

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\doteq\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\ :\ f\subset\partial\Omega\},

and the set of interior faces as

\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}\doteq\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}\ :\ f\subset\Omega\}.

The union of these two sets forms the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}
of all faces of the mesh.
The
hybrid
mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}\doteq(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathcal{F}_{h})
(see
[
19
, Definition 1.4]
) is the combination of cells and faces.
For any cell or face
Z
, we denote by
h_{Z}
its diameter, and we define the
mesh size
as
h=\max_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{t}
. We make the following assumption on the mesh.

Assumption 1.1

(Mesh regularity)
.

The mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is regular as per
[
19
, Definition 1.9]
, and quasi-uniform: there exists
\rho>0
independent of
h
such that, for all
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
h\leq\rho h_{t}
.

This assumption ensures that for each
Z\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}
there exists a point
x_{Z}\in Z
such that
Z
contains a ball of radius
\varpi h_{Z}
, with
\varpi
independent of
h
. From hereon, we fix such a
centroid
for each cell and face; hence, in the following,
x_{t}
or
x_{f}
refer to these centroids, for a cell
t
or a face
f
, respectively.

On the mesh, we define a
hybrid
space
\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}=U_{h}\times\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial}
of polynomials in the cells and on the faces.

Given
k\geq 0
, we set

U_{h}\doteq\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k}(t),\quad\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial}\doteq\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k}(f),

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
. The discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm on
\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}
is defined as

|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}\doteq\left[\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(t)}^{2}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}h_{t}^{-1}\|v_{f}-v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}\right)\right]^{1/2}.

We introduce the boundary space
\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\doteq\bigtimes_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}\mathbb{P}_{k}(f)

and define the trace operator
\gamma:\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}\to\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
by

\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{f}=v_{f}\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\,,\quad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}.

The discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
-seminorm on
\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
is given by

(1.1)

|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}\doteq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}h_{f}^{-1}\|w_{f}-\overline{w}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{w}_{f}-\overline{w}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}},

where

\overline{w}_{f}\doteq\frac{1}{|f|_{d-1}}\int_{f}w_{f}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}=\{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\times\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\,:\,f\neq f^{\prime}\}.

These discrete
H^{1}
-seminorm, trace operator, and
H^{1/2}
-seminorm satisfy the following trace and lifting properties, proved in
[
2
]
. Here and in the following,
a\lesssim b
means that
a\leq cb
for some constant
c>0
depending only on
\Omega
, the mesh regularity parameters (see Assumption
1.1
) and the polynomial degree
k
. Equivalence
a\simeq b
means that
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
.

Theorem 1.2

(Trace inequality)
.

The following discrete trace inequality holds:

(1.2)

|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{1/2,h}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}\qquad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}.

Theorem 1.3

(Lifting)
.

There exists a discrete linear lifting operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}:\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\rightarrow\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}
such that:

(1.3)

|\mathcal{L}_{h}(w_{h})|_{1,h}\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}\qquad\forall w_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}},\qquad\gamma\circ\mathcal{L}_{h}=\text{Id}_{\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}}.

2.
Truncation estimates for piecewise broken polynomial functions

In this section we establish an estimate in the discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm for truncated boundary functions, in the spirit of
[
8
, Equation 4.18]
,
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
,
[
9
, Section 7.5]
and
[
31
, Section 4.6]
. We consider a portion
\Gamma
of
\partial\Omega
that satisfies three key properties: it is compatible with the underlying mesh, its size is comparable to that of
\Omega
, and its boundary has finite
(d-2)
-dimensional measure. These requirements are formalized in the following assumption.

Assumption 2.1

(Regularity of
\Gamma
)
.

•

There is
\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)\subset\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
such that
\Gamma=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\mathrm{cl}(f)
, where
\mathrm{cl}(f)
is the closure of
f
.

•

There is
C_{\Gamma}>0
such that
C_{\Gamma}^{-1}\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{d-1}\leq|\Gamma|_{d-1}
and
|\partial\Gamma|_{d-2}\leq C_{\Gamma}\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{d-2}
.

We then take
\underline{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}
such that

(2.1)

\int_{\Gamma}\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})=0

and consider
w_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
equal to
\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})
on
\Gamma
and to
0
outside
\Gamma
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle w_{f}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{f}\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma),\\
&0\;\;\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;f\notin\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma),\end{aligned}\right.\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}.

The following theorem provides an estimate for the truncated trace function
\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})
. To establish this result, we rely on the uniform cone condition satisfied by the polytopal domain
\Omega
. According to
[
1
, Section 4.8]
, this condition ensures the existence of: (i) a fixed reference cone
C
, (ii) a finite collection of subsets
((\partial\Omega)_{i})_{i\in I}
covering
\partial\Omega
(with
\#I\sim 1
), and (iii) corresponding cones
(C_{i})_{i\in I}
such that each
C_{i}
is congruent to
C
and satisfies
(\partial\Omega)_{i}+C_{i}\subset\Omega
.

Theorem 2.2

(Estimate of truncated boundary function)
.

Let
\underline{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}
satisfy (
2.1
) and
w_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
be defined by (
2.2
). Under Assumption
2.1
, the following estimate holds:

(2.3)

\displaystyle|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}\lesssim\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}{h}\right)\right)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h},

where the hidden constant depends on the uniform cone condition parameters
(C,I,((\partial\Omega)_{i})_{i\in I})
for
\Omega
, the constant
C_{\Gamma}
from Assumption
2.1
, the mesh regularity parameters from Assumption
1.1
, and the polynomial degree
k
.

In the rest of this section, the hidden constants in the inequalities have the same dependencies as in this theorem.

Remark 2.3

(Scaling)
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\Omega
has unit diameter in our analysis. This assumption is justified by a scaling argument: when
\Omega
is scaled by a factor
\mu
, both the discrete
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorms scale with
\mu^{d-2}
. Consequently, the hidden constants remain independent of
\mbox{diam}(\Omega)
. Additionally, the constant
C_{\Gamma}
from Assumption
2.1
remains invariant under domain scaling.

Proof of Theorem

Let
\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}(\Gamma)
denote the set of all distinct face pairs
(f,f^{\prime})
where both faces lie in
\Gamma
. The discrete
H^{1/2}
-seminorm (
1.1
) of
w_{h}
(defined from
\underline{v}_{h}
via (
2.2
)) can be decomposed into four terms:

\displaystyle|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}h_{f}^{-1}\|v_{f}-\overline{v}_{f}\|_{L^{2}(f)}^{2}+\sum_{(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F\!F}_{h}(\Gamma)}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f^{\prime}}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}.

The first two terms represent the seminorm contributions from faces within
\Gamma
, while the last two terms account for interactions between faces in
\Gamma
and faces outside
\Gamma
. The first two terms are bounded by
|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{1/2,h}^{2}
, which includes these terms summed over all faces. The last two terms are identical due to the symmetric roles of
f
and
f^{\prime}
. This leads to:

(2.11)

|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}\leq|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{1/2,h}^{2}+2\underbrace{\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}}_{\doteq E_{0}}\overset{\eqref{eq:trace}}{\lesssim}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}+E_{0}.

Given that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=1
(as established in Remark
2.3
), the proof of (
2.3
) hinges on demonstrating the following bound:

(2.12)

\displaystyle E_{0}\lesssim(1+|\ln h|)^{2}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}.

We decompose the sum over
{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}
in
E_{0}
into concentric annuli around each face
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)
using the sets
\mathcal{W}_{l}
and
\mathcal{W}_{lf}
from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
):

(2.13)

\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\frac{|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}=\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{L}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{lf}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\frac{|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}},

where
L
is chosen so that
Lh\geq\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=1
, ensuring that
(\mathcal{W}_{lf})_{l=1,\dots,L}
forms a complete partition of
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
(the choice of
l_{0}\geq 1
will be discussed below). For faces
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{lf}
, the distance
|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|
satisfies
(l-1)h\leq|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|<lh
. When
l\geq 2
, the distances
(l-1)h
,
lh
, and
(l+1)h
are comparable, yielding
|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\simeq(l+1)h
. This scaling also holds for
l=1
since mesh regularity and
f^{\prime}\neq f
ensure
|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\gtrsim h
. Substituting
|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\simeq(l+1)h
into (
2.13
) gives

(2.14)

\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\frac{|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\simeq\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{L}\frac{1}{((l+1)h)^{d}}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{lf}}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}.

By
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
, the cardinality of
\mathcal{W}_{lf}
is bounded by
\#\mathcal{W}_{lf}\lesssim l^{d-2}
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
and
l\geq 1
. Together with the mesh quasi-uniformity bound
|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\simeq h^{d-1}
, this leads to

(2.15)

\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\frac{|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\lesssim\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{L}\frac{l^{d-2}h^{d-1}}{((l+1)h)^{d}}\leq\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{L}\frac{h}{((l+1)h)^{2}}.

To bound this sum, we observe that
\frac{1}{((l+1)h)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{x^{2}}
for
x\in[lh,(l+1)h]
, which implies

(2.16)

\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{L}\frac{h}{((l+1)h)^{2}}\leq\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{L}\int_{lh}^{(l+1)h}\frac{dx}{x^{2}}=\int_{l_{0}h}^{(L+1)h}\frac{dx}{x^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{l_{0}h}.

The value of
l_{0}
is determined by the requirement that
\mathcal{W}_{lf}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)\neq\emptyset
for (
2.14
) to be valid. This ensures the existence of a face
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)
with
|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\leq l_{0}h
, establishing that

\mbox{dist}(x_{f},\partial\Gamma)=\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{f},\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma)\leq|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|\leq l_{0}h

(where distances are measured along
\partial\Omega
). Using this
l_{0}
in (
2.16
) and combining with (
2.15
) yields

\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}\frac{|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(x_{f},\partial\Gamma)},

and consequently

(2.17)

E_{0}\lesssim\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)}|f|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}}{\mbox{dist}(x_{f},\partial\Gamma)}.

We now organize the faces in this sum according to the layers
\mathcal{G}_{r}
from (
2.6
). Taking
R
such that
Rh\simeq\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)=1
ensures that
(\mathcal{G}_{r})_{r=1,\dots,R}
forms a complete partition of
\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)
. This gives

E_{0}\lesssim\sum_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}}{\mbox{dist}(x_{f},\partial\Gamma)}\leq\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{1}{rh}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}.

Applying Proposition
2.4
leads to

(2.18)

E_{0}\lesssim\left(\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{h}{rh}\right)(1+|\ln h|)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}.

We bound the remaining sum by writing

(2.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{h}{rh}=1+\sum_{r=2}^{R}\frac{h}{rh}\leq 1+\sum_{r=2}^{R}\int_{(r-1)h}^{rh}\frac{ds}{s}=1+\int_{h}^{Rh}\frac{ds}{s}=1+\ln R.

Since
Rh\simeq 1
, we have
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{h}{rh}\lesssim 1+|\ln h|
. Substituting this into (
2.18
) proves (
2.12
). Finally, combining (
2.12
) with (
2.11
) establishes the desired estimate (
2.3
). ∎

Before proving Proposition
2.4
- a key ingredient in the preceding analysis - we first establish several technical lemmas that will streamline its demonstration.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\underline{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}
satisfy (
2.1
). For any layer index
r\geq 1
, the following bound holds for the weighted sum of squared face averages over faces in layer
\mathcal{G}_{r}
:

(2.20)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}\lesssim h(1+|\ln h|)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}.

Throughout this section, we denote by
D(x,r)
the disc centered at point
x
with radius
r
that lies on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

Lemma 2.5

(Cardinality bound for
\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}
)
.

For any boundary face
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)
, cell
t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and layer index
m\geq 0
, the number of faces in the set
\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}
is bounded by a constant independent of the mesh size, i.e.,
\#\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}\lesssim 1
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}
be a face (see (
2.9
)). By definition,
g
lies within a cylinder with base
D(x_{f},h)
in the plane orthogonal to
[x_{f},x_{t}]
and axis along
(x_{f},x_{t})
. For faces
g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}
(see (
2.10
)), we have the additional constraint that
g
must intersect the cylinder section between heights
mh-h
and
(m+1)h+h

Each face
g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}
belongs to a cell

that lies entirely within the
h
-enlargement of this cylinder section. This enlarged section has base
D(x_{f},2h)
and extends from height
(m-2)h
to
(m+3)h
along
(x_{f},x_{t})

.
By mesh quasi-uniformity, each cell
t^{\prime}
has measure
\simeq h^{d}
, so the number of such cells is
\lesssim h^{d}/h^{d}=1
. Mesh regularity further ensures that each cell
t^{\prime}
contains
\lesssim 1
faces
g
. Therefore,
\#\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}\lesssim 1
as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(Volume and Cardinality Estimates for
\mathcal{C}_{fd_{0}}
)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}
be fixed. Then:

(i)

The total volume of cells in
\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}
is bounded below by a constant independent of
h
:
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}}|t|_{d}\gtrsim 1
.

(ii)

For any interior face
g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}
, the number of cells in
\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}
that contain
g
in their interface set

is bounded above by:
\#(\{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}:g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\})\lesssim\frac{1}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{d-1}h}
.

Proof.

Proof of
(i)
: Consider the cone
x_{f}+C
and its section at distance
\geq d_{0}/2
from
x_{f}
, where
d_{0}\simeq 1
. This section has measure
\gtrsim 1
, with the hidden constant depending on the cone’s opening angle. The construction of
\mathcal{C}_{fd_{0}}
in (
2.8
) includes a
-h
offset that ensures
\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{fd_{0}}}t
fully covers this section. Hence,
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{fd_{0}}}|t|_{d}=|\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{fd_{0}}}t|_{d}\gtrsim 1
.

Proof of
(ii)
: Let
t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}
be any cell. By definition,
t
must intersect the portion of cone
x_{f}+C
between heights
d_{0}/2-h
and
d_{0}
. For a face
g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}
, we can restrict our attention to the portion of the cone spanned by the projection of

onto the sphere
S(x_{f},d_{0}/2)
centered at
x_{f}
with radius
d_{0}/2
(see Figure
3
). Through homothety – analogous to the case in

, Figure 3]
– this projection has diameter
\lesssim\frac{(d_{0}/2)}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|}\times h_{g}
.
It follows that

-enlargement of this cone portion, which is bounded by heights

and
d_{0}
and a disc of diameter
\lesssim\frac{(d_{0}/2)}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|}h_{g}
on

\lesssim(\frac{(d_{0}/2)}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|}h)^{d-1}\times(\frac{d_{0}}{2}+h)\simeq\frac{h^{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{d-1}}
. By mesh quasi-uniformity, we conclude:

\#(\{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}:g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\})\lesssim\frac{h^{d-1}}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{d-1}h^{d}}\simeq\frac{1}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{d-1}h}.

∎

Lemma 2.7

(Weighted sum bound for interior faces)
.

For any interior face
g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}
, the following weighted sum over boundary faces and their associated cells is bounded by a constant independent of the mesh size:

(2.21)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}\\
\text{s.t.}g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\end{subarray}}|f|_{d-1}|x_{f}-x_{g}|\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Let
J_{rg}=\{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}\,:\,\exists t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}\text{ s.t. }g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\}
. By Lemma
2.6
-
(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}\\
\text{s.t.}g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\end{subarray}}|f|_{d-1}|x_{f}-x_{g}|={}

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in J_{rg}}|f|_{d-1}|x_{f}-x_{g}|\times\#(\{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}:g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\})

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\sum_{f\in J_{rg}}\frac{1}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{d-2}}|f|_{d-1}.

Since
|x_{f}-x_{g}|\geq\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\Gamma)
, we obtain

(2.22)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}\\
\text{s.t.}g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\end{subarray}}|f|_{d-1}|x_{f}-x_{g}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)^{d-2}h}\sum_{f\in J_{rg}}|f|_{d-1}.

We now characterize the set
J_{rg}
. For any
f\in J_{rg}
, there exists a cell
t
intersecting the cone
x_{f}+C
with
g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}
. This means
g
intersects the
h
-enlargement of this cone, which in turn implies that
f
must intersect the
2h
-enlargement of the reversed cone
x_{g}-C
. Therefore,
f
lies within
\Gamma_{rg}
, defined as the intersection of
\Gamma
with the
3h
-enlargement of
x_{g}-C
. By mesh regularity and
g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}
, we have
\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)\gtrsim h
, so
\Gamma_{rg}
is contained in a disc on
\Gamma
of radius
\lesssim\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)
.
Let
G_{r}
denote the region covered by faces in
\mathcal{G}_{r}
. By construction,
G_{r}
forms a band of width
\lesssim h
that follows
\partial\Gamma
at a distance
\simeq rh
. Since
|\partial\Gamma|_{d-2}\lesssim 1
by Assumption
2.1
, any
f\in J_{rg}
must belong to
\Gamma_{rg}\cap G_{r}
.

The intersection
\Gamma_{rg}\cap G_{r}
is contained within the overlap of a disc of radius
\lesssim\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)
and a band of width
\lesssim h
following
\partial\Gamma
. This geometric constraint implies
|\Gamma_{rg}\cap G_{r}|_{d-1}\lesssim\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)^{d-2}h
, and thus

\sum_{f\in J_{rg}}|f|_{d-1}\leq|\Gamma_{rg}\cap G_{r}|_{d-1}\lesssim\mathop{\rm dist}(x_{g},\Gamma)^{d-2}h.

Substituting this bound into (
2.22
) completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
2.4
.

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

Let
t\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
(i.e.,
t
intersects
x_{f}+C
) and recall that
\mathcal{I}_{ft}
denotes the set of internal faces
g
intersected by
[x_{f},x_{t}]
. We can express
\overline{v}_{f}-\overline{v}_{t}
as a telescoping sum over these faces
g
of the differences
(\overline{v}_{t_{1}}-\overline{v}_{g})+(\overline{v}_{g}-\overline{v}_{t_{2}})
, where
t_{1},t_{2}
are the cells adjacent to
g
(with only one difference appearing for
g=f
), and
\overline{v}_{t^{\prime}}
represents the average of
v_{t^{\prime}}
over
t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. This yields

\displaystyle|\overline{v}_{f}|\leq|\overline{v}_{t}|+\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}\Delta_{g}\overline{v}

where
\Delta_{g}\overline{v}=|\overline{v}_{t_{1}}-\overline{v}_{g}|+|\overline{v}_{g}-\overline{v}_{t_{2}}|
. Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with carefully chosen weights gives

\displaystyle|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\overline{v}_{t}|^{2}+\left(\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}\Big[|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{1/2}h^{-1/2}|g|_{d-1}^{1/2}\;\Delta_{g}\overline{v}\Big]\times\Big[|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{-1/2}h^{1/2}|g|_{d-1}^{-1/2}\Big]\right)^{2}

(2.23)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\overline{v}_{t}|^{2}+\left(\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2}\right)\times\left(\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{-1}h|g|_{d-1}^{-1}\right).

By mesh quasi-uniformity (Assumption
1.1
),
|g|_{d-1}\simeq h^{d-1}
, so

\displaystyle T_{1}\doteq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{-1}h|g|_{d-1}^{-1}\lesssim\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|^{-1}\;h^{2-d}.

We partition this sum into slices
\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}
as defined in (
2.10
) and let
R\simeq 1/h
such that
(\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m})_{m=0,\ldots,R-1}
covers
\mathcal{I}_{ft}
. This gives

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2-d}\sum_{m=0}^{R-1}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}}\frac{1}{|x_{f}-x_{g}|}\lesssim\#\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{0}\,h^{2-d}\times h^{-1}+\sum_{m=1}^{R-1}\#\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}\,\frac{h^{2-d}}{mh},

where we used
|x_{f}-x_{g}|\gtrsim h
for the first term and the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{ft}^{m}
for the second term. By Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{1}\lesssim h^{1-d}\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{R-1}\frac{h}{mh}\right).

The sum can be bounded using (
2.19
) as
1+\sum_{m=1}^{R-1}\frac{h}{mh}\leq 1+\ln R
. Since
R\simeq 1/h
, this yields

\displaystyle T_{1}\lesssim h^{1-d}(1+|\ln h|).

Substituting this bound into (
2.2
) leads to

\displaystyle|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}\lesssim|\overline{v}_{t}|^{2}+h^{1-d}(1+|\ln h|)\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2}.

We multiply by
|t|_{d}\simeq h^{d}
and sum over
t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}
. Applying
(i)
from Lemma
2.6
gives

\displaystyle|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}}|t|_{d}\big)|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}}|t|_{d}|\overline{v}_{t}|^{2}+h(1+|\ln h|)\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+h(1+|\ln h|)\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2},

where
\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h}=((\overline{v}_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},(\overline{v}_{f})_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}})
denotes the projection of
\underline{v}_{h}
onto the lowest-order hybrid space on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
(obtained by projecting each component onto constant polynomials), and
\widehat{v}_{h}
is the piecewise constant function on
\Omega
defined from
(\overline{v}_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. Multiplying by
|f|_{d-1}
and summing over
f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}
yields

(2.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}\lesssim h\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+h(1+|\ln h|)\underbrace{\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}}|x_{f}-x_{g}|h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2}}_{\doteq T_{2}},

where the factor
h
in the first term comes from the fact that faces in
\mathcal{G}_{r}
lie within a band of width
\lesssim h
following
\partial\Gamma
at a distance
\simeq rh
from this boundary (see Figure
1
).

To estimate
T_{2}
, we first reorder the summations over faces, cells, and internal faces:

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{C}_{f{d_{0}}}\\
\text{s.t.}g\in\mathcal{I}_{ft}\end{subarray}}|f|_{d-1}|x_{f}-x_{g}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm in}}}h^{-1}|g|_{d-1}(\Delta_{g}\overline{v})^{2}\doteq|\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h}|_{\Omega,2}^{2}.

Inserting this estimate into (
2.24
) and applying the discrete Poincaré inequality from
[
23
, Lemma B.22]
(noting that the discrete
H^{1}
-seminorm in this reference is equivalent to
|{\cdot}|_{\Omega,2}^{2}
under mesh quasi-uniformity), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+h(1+|\ln h|)|\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h}|_{\Omega,2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h|\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h}|_{\Omega,2}^{2}+h\|\gamma(\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}+h(1+|\ln h|)|\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h}|_{\Omega,2}^{2}.

Arguing as in
[
19
, Lemma 6.33]
we have
|\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h}|_{\Omega,2}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}
, while noting that
\gamma{(\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h})}
is the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-projection of
\gamma{(\underline{v}_{h})}
onto piecewise constant functions on
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}
, so Jensen’s inequality yields
\|\gamma(\widehat{\underline{v}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
. Therefore,

(2.25)

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}\lesssim h\|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}+h(1+|\ln h|)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2}.

The boundary Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality from
[
2
, Lemma 5.5]
combined with (
2.1
) gives

\|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\partial\Omega)^{d}}{|\Gamma|_{d-1}}\right)^{1/2}|\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})|_{1/2,h}\lesssim|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}.

The final estimate relies on two crucial facts: (i)
|\Gamma|_{d-1}\gtrsim 1
by Assumption
2.1
and our parameter choices, and (ii) the trace inequality from Theorem
1.2
. Substituting this bound into (
2.25
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{r}}|f|_{d-1}|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}\lesssim h(1+|\ln h|)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}^{2},

which completes the proof of Proposition
2.4
.
∎

3.
BDDC preconditioner

This section presents the
bddc
preconditioner for hybrid methods. The
bddc
method is a non-overlapping domain decomposition method that enforces continuity of certain functionals across subdomain interfaces while maintaining the parallel nature of the algorithm.

3.1.
Functional setting and the condition number estimate

Consider a coarse mesh (subdomain partition)
\mathcal{T}_{H}
obtained by agglomeration of the fine mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We define the
coarse faces
as the intersections between pairs of subdomains with non-zero
(d-1)
-dimensional measure, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}_{H}\doteq\{F=\partial T\cap\partial T^{\prime}:T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{H},T\neq T^{\prime},|F|_{d-1}>0\},

where
|F|_{d-1}
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional measure of the face
F
. The coarse mesh
\mathcal{M}_{H}
is thus defined as the pair
(\mathcal{T}_{H},\mathcal{F}_{H})
, where
\mathcal{T}_{H}
consists of general polytopal subdomains and the coarse faces in
\mathcal{F}_{H}
are, in general, not flat, since both are constructed by agglomeration of the fine mesh. We assume that each coarse face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}
satisfies the regularity assumption
2.1
with respect to every subdomain
T
it belongs to. We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}(T)
and
\mathcal{F}_{h}(T)

The

, while the interior component is identical. In particular,
\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}=U_{h}\times\widetilde{U}_{h}^{\partial}

Theorem 3.1

(Condition number estimate)

Assume that every coarse face

w_{h}\in U_{h}^{\partial}(F)\mapsto\int_{F}w_{h}

Then, the following condition number bound for the BDDC preconditioner holds:

\kappa(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h})\lesssim\left(1+\ln\frac{H}{h}\right)^{2}.

The hidden constant is independent of

,
H
, and the number of subdomains

in the partition
\mathcal{M}_{H}
.

Proof.

See Section

. ∎

Remark 3.2

(Application to hybrid methods)
.

Theorem
3.1
applies to a wide class of hybrid numerical methods whose discrete spaces are built from polynomials on cells and faces, possibly with varying polynomial degrees and non-uniform distributions across the mesh; see
[
2
, Section 2.5]
. The result encompasses methods that may omit cell unknowns, such as certain
hho
variants
[
19
, Section 5.1]
, and is robust with respect to the specific structure of the discrete space. For methods involving only cell unknowns, such as polytopal
dg
methods, the framework can be extended by hybridizing the discretisation at least on coarse faces, following ideas from
[
25
]
. Thus, the condition number estimate remains valid under these generalizations, provided the key assumptions on the operator discretisation and coarse degrees of freedom are satisfied.

3.2.
Face restriction operator

Given a subdomain
T\in\mathcal{T}_{H}
and a coarse face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)
, the face restriction operator
\theta_{F}^{T}:\underline{U}_{h}(T)\to{\underline{H}}_{h}(T)
is defined as follows: given
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}(T)
,
\theta_{F}^{T}(\underline{v}_{h})\in{\underline{H}}_{h}(T)
is the unique element that is discretely harmonic (see (
3.2
)) and satisfies, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(T)
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{f}(\theta_{F}^{T}(\underline{v}_{h}))=\begin{cases}v_{f}&\text{if }f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(F),\\
0&\text{if }f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(T)\setminus\mathcal{F}_{h}(F).\end{cases}

We remark that the operator
\theta_{F}^{T}
depends solely on the face values of
\underline{v}_{h}
on the coarse face
F
. With a slight abuse of notation, we will also use
\theta_{F}^{T}
to denote the operator acting directly on these face values.

The following result is a direct consequence of the truncation estimate for piecewise broken polynomial spaces proved in Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)
satisfy the regularity Assumption
2.1
, and
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{U}_{h}(T)
be such that

\int_{F}\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})=0
.
Then, the following estimate holds:

|\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta_{F}^{T}(\underline{v}_{h}))|_{1/2,h,\partial T}\lesssim\left(1+{\ln\frac{H}{h}}\right)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h,T}.

The following result facilitates stable propogation between neighbouring subdomains.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
T,T^{\prime}
be two neighbouring subdomains and
F
be the coarse face common to
T
and
T^{\prime}
. Let
\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})\in\underline{U}_{h}(T^{\prime})
and denote by
w_{T,F}
the piecewise polynomial function on
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(T)
constructed by extending by
0
to
\partial T
the face values of
\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})
, that is:
w_{T,F}=\gamma_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime}))
on

and

2.1
with

(3.4)

|w_{T,F}|_{1/2,h,\partial T}\lesssim\left(1+\ln\frac{H}{h}\right)|\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})|_{1,h,T^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Considering the definition of

, and following same arguments as in the proof of Theorem

given in Section

(see in particular (

|w_{T,F}|_{1/2,h,\partial T}^{2}=|\gamma_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime}))|_{1/2,h,F}^{2}+2\underbrace{\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(F)}\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm bd}}(T)\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}(F)}|f|_{d-1}|f^{\prime}|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}}{|x_{f}-x_{f^{\prime}}|^{d}}}_{\doteq E_{0}(T)}.

We then apply (

(3.6)

E_{0}(T)\lesssim\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(F)}|f|_{d-1}\frac{|\overline{v}_{f}|^{2}}{\mbox{dist}(x_{f},\partial F)}.

The right-hand side in this upper bound no longer depends on

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

The condition number
\kappa
of the BDDC preconditioned system
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
is defined as

\displaystyle\kappa(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h})=\frac{\mu_{\max}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h})}{\mu_{\min}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h})},

where
\mu_{\max/\min}
denotes the maximal/minimal eigenvalue.
By applying the abstract additive Schwarz theory from
[
28
, Theorem 15]
, we obtain the following bound:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{\max}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h})}{\mu_{\min}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h})}\leq\max\left\{1,\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}^{2}}{\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}^{2}}\right\}.

Let
\widetilde{\underline{U}}_{h,0}
denote the subspace of functions in
\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}
on which all functionals in
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\Lambda_{F}
vanish. The space
\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}
admits the decomposition
\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}=\widetilde{\underline{U}}_{h,0}\oplus\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}
, where
\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}\doteq\underline{H}_{h}\cap\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\underline{U}}_{h,0}
is
\mathcal{A}_{h}
-orthogonal to
\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}
. Therefore, for any
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}
, we have
\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}\geq\|\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}
, since the discrete harmonic extension
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}
minimises the energy norm among all functions with the same degrees of freedom on the interfaces. Using this property, together with the facts that
\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}=\mathcal{Q}_{h}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}
and co-domain of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
is the space of harmonic functions, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}{\|\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{\widetilde{U}}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}{\|\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}\leq\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}{\|\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}{\|\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}}.

Therefore, combining (
3.7
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{\max}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{A}_{h})}{\mu_{\min}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}\widehat{A}_{h})}\lesssim 1+\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{h}}}{\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{h}}^{2}}.

Le
\underline{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}
and
\underline{w}_{h}\doteq\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}
.
It readily follows from the linearity of the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
that
\underline{w}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}\cap\widetilde{\underline{U}}_{h,0}
, i.e., all moments
\{\Lambda_{F}\}_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}}
of
\underline{w}_{h}
vanish. Besides, on each subdomain
T\in\mathcal{T}_{H}
,
\underline{w}_{h}
can be decomposed into harmonic face contributions:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\underline{w}_{h}(T)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)}\theta_{F}^{T}(\underline{w}_{h}).

The numerator in (
3.9
) can then be bounded as follows:

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}^{2}=\|\underline{w}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{H}}\|\underline{w}_{h}(T)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{H}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)}\|\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}^{2},

where the last bound relies on (
3.10
), the triangle inequality
and the fact that, by coarse mesh assumption,
\#\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)\lesssim 1
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{H}
.
Next, we consider each individual term in the sum. For any
F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)
, denoting by
T^{\prime}
the neighbouring subdomain sharing
F
and recalling that
\underline{w}_{h}=\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}=\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{h}W_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}
, the definition (
3.3
) of the weighting operator implies that

\displaystyle\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\partial T}\left(\theta^{T}_{F}\left(\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})|_{F}-\underline{v}_{h}(T)|_{F}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\partial T}\left(\theta^{T}_{F}\left((\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})|_{F}-\alpha_{F})-(\underline{v}_{h}(T)|_{F}-\alpha_{F})\right)\right),

for any
\alpha_{F}\in\mathbb{R}
. We infer the following bound:

\displaystyle\|\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h}))|_{1/2,h,\partial T}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})|_{F}-\alpha_{F}))|_{1/2,h,\partial T}^{2}+|\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T)|_{F}-\alpha_{F}))|_{1/2,h,\partial T}^{2},

where the first inequality follows from the norm equivalence (
3.1
) and the discrete lifting property (
1.3
) (which yield, since the harmonic extension minimises the energy norm,
\|\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{h,T}(\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h})))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}\lesssim|\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h}))|_{1/2,h,\partial T}
).
Since
\underline{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\underline{H}}_{h}
, the coarse degrees of freedom
\Lambda_{F}
applied to
\underline{v}_{h}
are continuous across coarse faces. Therefore, since the mean value belongs to
\Lambda_{F}
by assumption, we can choose
\alpha_{F}\doteq\int_{F}\gamma_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T))=\int_{F}\gamma_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime}))
.
This choice allows us to invoke Corollary
3.3
for the second term in (

3.4
for the first term to the function
\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})-\alpha_{F}

defined in Corollary

is then equal to
\gamma_{\partial T}(\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})|_{F}-\alpha_{F}))

\displaystyle\|\theta^{T}_{F}(\underline{w}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}^{2}\lesssim\left(1+\ln\frac{H}{h}\right)^{2}(|\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})|_{1,h,T^{\prime}}^{2}+|\underline{v}_{h}(T)|_{1,h,T}^{2}).

Combining (
3.11

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{h}\underline{v}_{h}-\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{H}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}(T)}\left(1+\ln\frac{H}{h}\right)^{2}(\|\underline{v}_{h}(T^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T^{\prime}}^{2}+\|\underline{v}_{h}(T)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h},T}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+\ln\frac{H}{h}\right)^{2}\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{h}}^{2}.

4.1.
Discontinuous skeletal methods

We adopt the general framework developed in
[

to describe discontinuous skeletal methods spanning

\bm{W}_{h}\doteq\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left[\mathbb{P}_{k}(t)\right]^{d}.

We define element-wise local linear operators

\mathcal{U}_{t}:\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial}|_{\partial t}\longrightarrow U_{h}|_{t},\;\;\mathcal{V}_{t}:L^{2}(t)\longrightarrow U_{h}|_{t},\;\;\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}:\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial}|_{\partial t}\longrightarrow\bm{W}_{h}|_{t}.

The action of these operators is defined by solving local problems and influences the design of numerical schemes. These local operators are concatenated to form their global counterparts
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{V}
, and
\bm{\mathcal{Q}}
. It can be shown that the bulk component
u_{h}
can be approximated using the skeleton component

via the transformation

u_{h}\approx\mathcal{U}u_{\partial\Omega}+\mathcal{V}f,

if
u_{\partial\Omega}
satisfies the equivalent statically condensed version of the problem
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\underline{u}_{h}=l_{h}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}u_{\partial\Omega},\mu\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f\mathcal{U}\mu\qquad\forall\mu\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial}.

Here,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}u_{\partial\Omega},\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{t}\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}u_{\partial t}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}\mu+s(u_{\partial t},\mu)\doteq\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a_{t}(u_{\partial t},\mu).

The bilinear form (
4.2
) is elliptic and continuous, and the
stabilisation
or
penalty
term
s(\cdot,\cdot)
within it is symmetric positive semi-definite. Without loss of generality, we assume homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}_{h}
, and the implementation of (
4.1
) results in a symmetric positive-definite matrix system. The choice of spaces, local operators, and stabilisation term completely characterise a discontinuous skeletal method.

In our numerical experiments, we test the proposed
bddc
preconditioner
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}
on the following hybrid methods:

•

HDG method

[
13
]
: Implemented on simplicial and tetrahedral meshes. The local operators
\mathcal{U}_{t}
and
\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}
map
u_{\partial t}
to
u_{t}\in U_{h}|_{t}
and
\bm{q}_{t}\in\bm{W}_{h}|_{t}
by solving the following local problems:

\displaystyle\int_{t}\bm{q}_{t}\cdot\bm{p}_{t}-\int_{t}u_{t}\nabla\cdot\bm{p}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial t}u_{f}\bm{p}_{t}\cdot\bm{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{p}_{t}\in\bm{W}_{h}|_{t},

\displaystyle\int_{\partial t}\gamma(\bm{q}_{t})\cdot\bm{n}-\int_{t}\bm{q}_{t}\cdot\nabla v_{t}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{q}_{t}\in\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}|_{t},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\gamma(\bm{q}_{t})

\displaystyle=\bm{q}_{t}+\tau_{t}(u_{t}-u_{f})\bm{n}.

Here,
\bm{n}
denotes the unit outward normal vector to
\partial t
. The stabilisation term in the bilinear form (
4.2
) is defined as

s(u_{\partial t},\mu)\doteq\tau_{t}\int_{\partial t}(\mathcal{U}_{t}u_{\partial t}-u_{\partial t})(\mathcal{U}_{t}\mu-\mu).

We set the penalty parameter
\tau_{t}=1
in our experiments for this scheme.

•

HDG+ method

[
26
]
: Used for polygonal or polyhedral meshes. This method follows the HDG framework above, but replaces the scalar bulk space with

U_{h}\doteq\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(t).

The trace of the double-valued vector function in (
4.3
) is replaced by

\gamma(\bm{q}_{t})=\bm{q}_{t}+\tau_{t}(\Pi^{k}_{\partial t}u_{t}-u_{f})\bm{n},

where the penalty parameter is chosen as
\tau_{t}\simeq h_{t}^{-1}
. Here,
\Pi^{k}_{\partial t}:L^{2}(\partial t)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
denotes the standard
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection, with
(\Pi^{k}_{\partial t}(w))|_{f}=\Pi^{k}_{f}(w|_{f})
.

•

HHO method

[
20
]
: For this method, the vector-valued bulk space is replaced with

\bm{W}_{h}=\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\nabla\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(t).

The operator
\bm{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}
solves the local potential reconstruction problem as defined in
[
16
, (3.8)–(3.9)]
, and
\mathcal{U}_{t}
solves the local problem described in
[
16
, (3.11)]
. We use the original HHO element-wise stabiliser proposed in
[
18
, (22)]
and
[
19
, (2.22)]
.

•

Mixed-order HHO method

[
11
]
: In this variant, the scalar replaced with

U_{h}\doteq\bigtimes_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(t).

The rest of the framework remains the same as for the HHO method above, except for the element-wise stabilisation term (which gives, after static condensation, the cell contribution to
s
in (
4.2
)), which is chosen as

h_{t}^{-1}\int_{\partial t}(\Pi^{k}_{\partial t}(u_{t}|_{\partial t}-u_{\partial t}))(\Pi^{k}_{\partial t}(v_{t}|_{\partial t}-v_{\partial t})).

4.2.
Verification of truncation estimates

In order to verify the truncation estimate in Theorem
2.2
, we begin by defining the operator
R_{\Gamma}
, which performs the truncation to
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
of boundary functions as defined in (
2.2
). We have proved that, for any
\underline{v}_{h}\in\widehat{\underline{U}}_{h}
with zero mean value on
\Gamma
,

(4.4)

|R_{\Gamma}({\gamma(\underline{v}_{h})})|_{1/2,h}\lesssim\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\mbox{diam}(\Omega)}{h}\right)\right)|\underline{v}_{h}|_{1,h}.

Since the discrete harmonic extension
\mathcal{E}_{h}
preserves the boundary values and minimises the discrete
H^{1}(\Omega)
-seminorm, (
4.4
) is equivalent to

(4.5)

|R_{\Gamma}(w_{h})|_{1/2,h}\lesssim\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\mbox{diam}(\Omega)}{h}\right)\right)|\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})|_{1,h}\qquad\forall w_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\text{ s.t. }\int_{\Gamma}w_{h}=0.

Furthermore, using Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
we can easily obtain the following seminorm equivalence:

(4.6)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})|_{1,h}\lesssim|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}\lesssim|\mathcal{E}_{h}(w_{h})|_{1,h}\qquad\forall w_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{H}}
denote the Gram matrix associated with discrete trace seminorm,
\bm{S}\doteq[\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi_{a}]_{1\leq a\leq\mbox{dim}(\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}})}
be a row vector,
\phi_{a}
denoting the shape functions of the boundary space
\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
, and
\hat{\bm{w}}
and
\bm{R}_{\Gamma}
be the vector representation of
\hat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
and the restriction operator, respectively. Then the relation (
4.6
) shows that (
4.5
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\mbox{diam}(\Omega)}{h}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}},\\
\int_{\Gamma}w_{h}=0\end{subarray}}\frac{|R_{\Gamma}(w_{h})|_{1/2,h}}{|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}}

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}\end{subarray}}\frac{|R_{\Gamma}(\hat{w}_{h}-\int_{\Gamma}\hat{w}_{h})|_{1/2,h}}{\big(|\hat{w}_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}+(\int_{\Gamma}\hat{w}_{h})^{2}\big)^{1/2}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\left(\max_{\hat{\bm{w}}}\frac{\hat{\bm{w}}^{\top}(I-\bm{1}\bm{S}_{\Gamma})^{\top}\bm{R}_{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\mathcal{H}}\bm{R}_{\Gamma}(I-\bm{1}\bm{S}_{\Gamma})\hat{\bm{w}}}{\hat{\bm{w}}^{\top}(\bm{\mathcal{H}}+\bm{S}_{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{S}_{\Gamma})\hat{\bm{w}}}\right)^{1/2}.

The maximum is taken over non-constant boundary functions and, in the second line, we have used the fact that if
\hat{w}_{h}\in\widehat{U}_{h}^{\partial,{\rm bd}}
and
w_{h}\doteq\hat{w}_{h}-\frac{1}{|\Gamma|_{d-1}}\int_{\Gamma}\hat{w}_{h}
, then
\int_{\Gamma}w_{h}=0
and
|w_{h}|_{1/2,h}=|\hat{w}_{h}|_{1/2,h}\leq\big(|\hat{w}_{h}|_{1/2,h}^{2}+(\int_{\Gamma}\hat{w}_{h})^{2}\big)^{1/2}
. The bound (
4.7
) can be checked by verifying the asymptotic trend as
h\to 0
of the maximum eigenvalue of

(4.8)

\displaystyle(\bm{\mathcal{H}}+\bm{S}^{\top}_{\Gamma}\bm{S}_{\Gamma})^{-1}(I-\bm{1}\bm{S}_{\Gamma})^{\top}\bm{R}_{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\mathcal{H}}\bm{R}_{\Gamma}(I-\bm{1}\bm{S}_{\Gamma}).

To numerically assess the behaviour of this eigenvalue, we consider
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
and uniformly discretised Cartesian meshes. We write
\partial\Omega
as a disjoint union of its top
(\Gamma_{T})
, bottom
(\Gamma_{B})
, left
(\Gamma_{L})
and right
(\Gamma_{R})
parts. In Figure
4
, we compare the maximum eigenvalues of (
4.8
) against the log of the mesh size
h
associated with the hybrid discretisation with a planar
\Gamma=\Gamma_{T}
and a non-planar
\Gamma=\Gamma_{T}\cup\Gamma_{R}
, respectively.
This figure clearly show that this maximum eigenvalue seems to exactly behave as (and not just be bounded above by) a linear function of
|\ln h|
. Moreover, on these Cartesian meshes at least, this eigenvalue appears to be independent of the polynomial order
k
.

4.3.
Weak scalability tests

As a benchmark, we consider the Poisson equation with unit diffusion coefficient, homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, and the following source term:

f(x,y)=-\Delta(\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2\pi y)x(x-1)y(y-1))

on the unit
d
-cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
. We consider two families of meshes: simplicial and polygonal. Each mesh is partitioned into subdomains, with each subdomain assigned to a single processor. We conduct weak scalability tests, where both the global problem size and the number of subdomains (processors) are increased proportionally, keeping the local subdomain problem size fixed.

For a given number of processors
N_{p}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}N_{p}^{i}
, we construct a Cartesian mesh with
N_{c}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}N_{c}^{i}
cells, where
N_{c}^{i}=N_{p}^{i}\,H/h
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. The simplicial mesh is obtained by subdividing each Cartesian cell into
d!
simplices, resulting in
N_{c,\mathrm{simplex}}=d!\,N_{c}
cells. The polygonal mesh is generated by computing the Voronoi tessellation of the simplexified mesh, associating each Voronoi cell with a Cartesian node, so that
N_{c,\mathrm{voronoi}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(N_{c}^{i}+1)
. Cell ownership in both the simplicial and polygonal meshes is determined by the ownership of the corresponding Cartesian entity (cell or node). Figure
5
illustrates examples of simplicial and polygonal meshes with their subdomain partitions for
d=2
, visualised using ParaView.

We solve the linear systems using the FGMRES iterative solver
[
30
]
, preconditioned with our
bddc
implementation in GridapSolvers
[
29
]
. Iterations are performed until the relative residual is reduced below
10^{-8}
, and we record the number of iterations required for convergence.

For
d=2
, we consider
N_{p}=6,12,24,48,96,192,384,768
processors, and test local problem sizes
H/h=8
and
H/h=16
. Figures
6
and
7
report the number of FGMRES iterations for simplicial and polygonal meshes, respectively. For
d=3
, we use
N_{p}=8,16,32,64,128,256,512,768,1536,3072
and
H/h=4
, with results shown in Figure
8
for tetrahedral meshes.

The results demonstrate that, for fixed
H/h
, the number of iterations remains essentially constant as
N_{p}
increases, confirming the weak scalability and the theoretical condition number bounds established in Theorem
3.1
. We also observe a mild increase in iteration counts as
H/h
grows, in agreement with the theoretical predictions.

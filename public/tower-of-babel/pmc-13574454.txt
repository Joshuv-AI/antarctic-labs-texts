Fusion Sling–Superficial Orbicularis Oris Nasalis Composite Flap: A Soft-tissue Suspension Technique for Columellar Base Augmentation in Asian Rhinoplasty
Authors: Takamasa Nakai, Kazushi Sasaki, Junya Fukabori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:In Asian rhinoplasty, midfacial harmony often requires concurrent enhancement of the nasolabial angle and columellar base projection. Conventional structural grafts—columellar struts, septal extension grafts, and premaxillary grafts—provide reliable support but may increase tip rigidity or disturb the upper lip–columellar relationship. Recent anatomical work has identified the fusion sling and the superficial orbicularis oris nasalis (SOON) as key soft-tissue contributors to nasal tip dynamics. Between 2022 and 2025, the fusion sling–SOON composite flap (FSCF) was performed in 214 consecutive Asian rhinoplasty patients (179 primary; the remainder selected revision cases with preserved fusion sling–SOON continuity). Indications included an acute nasolabial angle with inadequate anterior nasal spine or premaxillary support. Through a sub-superficial musculoaponeurotic system approach, the fusion sling and SOON were elevated as an 8- to 10-mm fasciomuscular composite flap and stabilized with a caudal septal soft-hinge or septal extension graft suspension. Three-dimensional Vectra analysis was performed in 63 patients. Mean follow-up was 12.4 months (range, 6–24 mo). Mean nasolabial angle increased from 93.4-degree angle to 101.0-degree angle (+7.6-degree angle), and columellar base projection increased by 1.9 mm. Graded mild, moderate, and large changes were reproducibly achieved. No major complications, upper lip stiffness, animation deformities, or FSCF-related revisions occurred. The FSCF is a reproducible, anatomy-based dynamic technique that improves the nasolabial angle and columellar base while preserving upper lip mobility, offering a balanced alternative to rigid structural augmentation in Asian rhinoplasty.

Takeaways

Question: How can columellar base projection and nasolabial angle be improved in Asian rhinoplasty without adding upper lip rigidity from conventional structural grafts?

Findings: In 214 consecutive Asian rhinoplasty patients (63 with 3-dimensional Vectra analysis), the fusion sling–superficial orbicularis oris nasalis composite flap (FSCF) increased the mean nasolabial angle by 7.6-degree angle and columellar base projection by 1.9 mm, with no major complications or upper-lip stiffness.

Meaning: The FSCF is a reproducible, anatomy-based dynamic alternative to rigid structural augmentation for balanced midfacial refinement in Asian rhinoplasty.

INTRODUCTION

Asian rhinoplasty often requires structural enhancement of the columellar base and improvement of the nasolabial angle to achieve refined tip projection and midfacial harmony. However, traditional augmentation methods such as columellar struts, caudal extension grafts, and costal cartilage may increase stiffness or impair upper-lip mobility, and may not sufficiently address the premaxillary retrusion common in Asians.

Recent anatomical investigations have emphasized the importance of soft-tissue suspensory structures—including the Pitanguy ligament, dermocartilaginous ligament, nasal superficial musculoaponeurotic system (SMAS), and the recently described fusion sling—in nasal tip dynamics.1–5

Although the present technique has been used clinically since 2022, subsequent publications by Neves et al clarified the anatomical continuity between the superficial orbicularis oris nasalis (SOON), fusion sling, and nasal tip complex, providing a framework for understanding the mechanism of the fusion sling–SOON composite flap (FSCF).1,2

These findings support the FSCF as a dynamic, physiologic method to enhance the columellar base and midfacial profile while preserving natural animation.

In Asian patients, achieving simultaneous improvement of the nasolabial angle and columellar base projection is often challenging, as traditional structural grafts may improve 1 parameter at the expense of the other. The FSCF addresses both features concurrently through a dynamic soft-tissue suspension mechanism, providing a balanced enhancement of nasal base aesthetics and midfacial projection.

METHODS

Between 2022 and 2025, the FSCF technique was performed in 214 consecutive Asian rhinoplasty patients, including both primary and revision cases. Of these, quantitative 3-dimensional (3D) analysis was conducted in 63 patients who consented to postoperative Vectra imaging. The mean follow-up period was 12.4 months (range, 6–24 mo). Of the 214 patients, 179 were primary rhinoplasty cases, and the remaining cases were selected revision rhinoplasty procedures. Revision cases were selectively included, as the FSCF requires sufficient preservation of the fusion sling and SOON from prior surgery to allow effective flap elevation.

All operations were performed using an open approach with sub-SMAS dissection preserving the Pitanguy and dermocartilaginous ligaments.3–6

The objective was to enhance the columellar base through a dynamic soft-tissue flap rather than relying exclusively on rigid grafts.

Through an inverted-V transcolumellar incision, the soft-tissue envelope is elevated in the sub-SMAS plane. The SOON is identified at the columellar–upper lip junction as a superficial fibromuscular extension of the orbicularis oris. The deeper fusion sling—a vertically oriented fibrous plate connecting the lower lateral cartilages to the anterior septal perichondrium—is then exposed.1,2

These 2 structures are elevated together as an 8- to 10-mm fasciomuscular composite flap, retaining vascularity and dynamic continuity with the upper lip.

Dynamic intraoperative assessment is essential. Gentle upper lip movement is used to verify synchronized motion and adjust flap tension. RESULTS

Among the 63 quantitatively assessed patients, the mean nasolabial angle increased from a 93.4-degree angle to a 101.0-degree angle (+7.6-degree angle). Columellar base projection increased by 1.9 mm.

No major complications (infection, necrosis, and tip deformity) occurred. No patient experienced upper lip stiffness, frozen smile, or animation deformity. No revision procedures attributable to the FSCF were observed during the follow-up period. Cadaveric evaluations demonstrated passive upper lip motion transmitted through the SOON–fusion sling complex to the columellar base, supporting the anatomical continuity described by recent studies.1–5

The FSCF may be particularly useful in selected Asian patients who require dynamic enhancement of the columellar base and nasolabial angle. Conventional techniques, such as columellar struts, SEGs, and premaxillary grafts, provide effective structural support. In contrast, the FSCF leverages the SOON–fusion sling continuity to preserve upper lip mobility while contributing to more physiologic tip support.1,2,8

Patient-reported satisfaction scores were not collected in a standardized manner in this series and should be incorporated in future studies.

CONCLUSIONS

The FSCF is a dynamic, anatomy-based method for enhancing the columellar base and nasolabial angle in Asian rhinoplasty. Integrating the mobility of the SOON with the suspensory strength of the fusion sling allows predictable projection without stiffness. SEG-based vector control enables individualized refinement from mild to large changes in midfacial definition. Clinical outcomes and cadaveric validation support the FSCF as a reproducible and physiologic adjunct that complements structural rhinoplasty in Asian patients.1–10

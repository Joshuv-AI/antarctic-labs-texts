Limits at infinity for functions in fractional Sobolev spaces
Angha Agarwal, Pekka Koskela, Kaushik Mohanta

Abstract
. We establish optimal results on limits at infinity for functions in fractional Sobolev spaces.



1.
Introduction

This article aims to study the ‘pointwise’ behavior of functions in homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces, near infinity. First, we need to be clear about what “limit at infinity” means, because elements of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
are not functions, rather than equivalence classes of functions. We start by fixing
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as the Euclidean space with dimension
n\geq 2
. It is known that every
u\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
has a representative
\tilde{u}
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{u}(t\xi)=0,

for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for any
1\leq p<\infty
, and, for
p>n
,
u(x)\to 0
uniformly as
x\to\infty
. The situation changes when we consider homogeneous Sobolev spaces. In this case, there is no reason for the supposed limiting value to be zero. Uspenskiĭ and Timan (see
[
15
]
and
[
13
]
) showed that
1\leq p<n
if and only if for every
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
there is some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a representative
\tilde{u}
of
u
such that

(2)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{u}(t\xi)=c

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We call the limit
c
in (
2
) the unique almost sure radial limit at infinity of the function
u
. Next, a function
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to have a unique almost sure vertical limit
c
at infinity if for almost every
\overline{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
we have

(3)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{u}(\overline{x},t)=c.

The existence of such limits has been studied by Fefferman
[
4
]
and Portnov
[
11
]
. They independently showed the existence of a unique almost sure vertical limit
c
at infinity for functions in
\dot{W}^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
under the assumption
1\leq p<n
. Moreover, it turns out that the values of the limits in (
2
) and (
3
) are the same.

The fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, defined in Section
2
, are regarded as the fractional counterparts of the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Both these spaces are special cases of the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}
:
F^{s}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
s\in(0,1)
and
F^{1}_{p,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. From Theorem 1.2 of
[
12
]
, and also from
[
14
]
, we know that, for
p,q\in[1,\infty)
,
s>\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{q}
, we have

F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{\max\{p,q\}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\mid[u]_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\right\},

where
[u]_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\left\|\left\|\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{q}+s}}\right\|_{L^{q}(dy)}\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}
. We define the homogeneous spaces
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
via this quasinorm (see Section
2
for details). These spaces may exhibit significantly different behaviour when
s<\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{q}
; for example, in this range of parameters,
C_{c}^{\infty}
is not contained in
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, see Remark 3.8 of
[
12
]
. To the best of our knowledge, the limits of functions
u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, in any pointwise sense, has not yet been studied even in the case
p=q
.

Our aim in this paper is to study vertical and radial limits at infinity, in a generalized sense, for functions in the space
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. The method of the proof for the classical results
[
4
,
11
]
uses the fact that
W^{1,p}(I)\subseteq W^{1,1}(I)
when
p\geq 1
and
I
is a bounded interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
. Due to the non-local nature of the
W^{s,p}_{q}
-seminorms, the methods used there cannot be used for our purpose. For instance, we do not have the embedding
W^{s,p}(I)\subseteq W^{s,1}(I)
, see
[
10
]
.

We now proceed to state our main results. We will take
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as the collection of all precise representatives
u^{*}
of measurable functions
u
of finite
W^{s,p}_{q}
-seminorm. The precise representative is defined in Definition
2.7
. Using this, we state our first main result showing the existence of a limit outside a “thin” set. The measure of the thinness at infinity in dimension depending on
s
and
p
is referred to as
(p,s)
-thin, defined in Definition
2.11
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u^{*}\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
0<s<1
,
0<p,q<\infty
and
sp<n
. Then there exists a set
E
which is
(p,s)
-thin at infinity and
K_{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}
so that

(4)

\lim_{x\to\infty,x\notin E}u^{*}(x)=K_{u^{*}}.

Let us single out two consequences.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p,q<\infty
,
1\leq k<sp<n
, and
u^{*}\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then, for each
(n-k)
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
centred at origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
u^{*}
has limit
K_{u^{*}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
at infinity along
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}
-a.e.
k
-plane perpendicular to
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p,q<\infty
,
1\leq k<sp<n
. Let
u^{*}\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then, for each
(n-k)
-dimensional subspace
V^{n-k}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
u^{*}
has limit
K_{u^{*}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
at infinity along
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}
-a.e.
k
-plane perpendicular to
V^{n-k}
.

In the above two statements, the term “limit at infinity” refers to the existence of limit at infinity along the
k
-plane in the usual sense. Especially, this limit exists along
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}
-every
k
-plane perpendicular to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}
. By choosing
k=1
, we obtain the existence of vertical limits. Similarly, Corollary
1.2
gives the existence of unique almost sure radial limits. The precise definitions are given in Section
2
(see Definitions
2.9
and
2.8
).

Theorem
1.1
also has a somewhat unexpected application: the space
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
only contains constant functions when
0<s<1
,
0<p<\infty
and
\frac{np}{n-sp}\leq q<\infty
, compare with
[
12
, Remark 3.8]
. Notice that
q\geq\frac{np}{n-sp}
is equivalent to
s\leq\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{q}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
0<s<1
,
0<p<\infty
,
q\geq\frac{np}{n-sp}
and
sp<n
. Then there is
K_{u^{*}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
so that
u(x)=K_{u^{*}}
for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Our technique also allows us to deal with the spaces
W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p,q<\infty
and
u^{*}\in{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then there exists a set
E
which is
(p,s)
-thin at infinity so that
u^{*}(x)\to 0
when
|x|\to\infty
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus E
.

One cannot deduce Theorem
1.1
from Corollary
1.5
for
0<q<\frac{np}{n-sp}
since there exist functions in
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for which
u-c\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for no
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, see Proposition
1.6
below. Also see
[
6
]
for related studies.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p<\infty
and
q\in(0,\frac{np}{n-sp})
. Then there exists a function
u\in\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for which
u-c\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for no
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let us discuss the case
sp\notin(1,n)
, not covered by Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
. We begin with the case
sp\geq n
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p,q<\infty
with
sp\geq n
. Then there exists a continuous function
u
in
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that for every curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\gamma(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}u(\gamma(t))=\infty.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
0<s<1
with
sp\geq n
. Then there exists a bounded continuous function
u
in
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that there exists no curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\gamma(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
, for which the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}u(\gamma(t))
exists.

We have the following result for
sp<1
.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p,q<\infty
with
sp<1
. Then there exists a bounded continuous function
u
in
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that there exists no curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\gamma(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
, for which the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}u(\gamma(t))
exists.

We are left with the case
sp=1
. In the case of the usual Sobolev spaces
W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we have the existence of both radial and vertical limits. We do not have counterexamples for all values of
q
when
sp=1
, but the following example covers the case
q\geq 1
.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-sp}
with
sp=1
. Then there exists a bounded continuous function
u
in
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that there exists no curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\gamma(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=\infty
, for which the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}u(\gamma(t))
exists.

The functions referred to in the propositions above do not have complicated definitions but checking the membership in the respective fractional Sobolev Space is tedious. This is caused by the non-locality of our norms.

and Theorem

are proved in Section

and Section

, respectively. The counterexamples mentioned above are constructed in Section

2.
Preliminaries

By a
cube
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we shall understand a set of the form
[a_{1},b_{1})\times\cdots\times[a_{n},b_{n})
for real numbers
a_{i}<b_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,n
. The standard Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is denoted by
|\cdot|
. The
\lambda
-dimensional Hausdorff content is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}
. The corresponding Hausdorff measure is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}
. For the convenience of the reader, we recall their definitions below. Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
and
\delta\in(0,\infty]
. Given a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, a
\delta
-cover of
A
is any countable family of sets
\{B_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
so that

(5)

A\subset\cup_{j\geq 1}B_{j}\text{ and }\operatorname{diam}(B_{j})\leq\delta

for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The
\lambda
-dimensional Hausdorff
\delta
-content is

(6)

\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{\lambda}(A):=\inf\left\{\sum_{j\geq 1}\operatorname{diam}(B_{j})^{\lambda}\mid A\subset\cup_{j\geq 1}B_{j},\text{ }\operatorname{diam}(B_{j})\leq\delta\right\}.

The
\lambda
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is defined as

(7)

\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}(A):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{\lambda}(A).

We record some well-known properties of Hausdorff content below for future reference.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
and
\delta\in(0,\infty]
. Let
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then

(i)

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{\lambda}(A)=0
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}(A)=0
.

(ii)
Let
L:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be Lipschitz continuous and
\delta>0
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{Lip(L)\delta}(L(A))\leq(Lip(L))^{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\delta}(A).

When
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an open set, we define the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to be the collection of all locally integrable functions
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
whose weak gradients
\nabla u
belong to
L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We consider this vector space to be equipped with the seminorm.

[u]_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For
s\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in(0,\infty)
, and an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we write
\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to denote the collection of all measurable functions
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
for which

(8)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+sq}}dy\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx<\infty.

The quantity

[u]_{W_{q}^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+sq}}dy\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

gives a (quasi) seminorm for the above-mentioned range of
p,q,s
. By quasinorm we mean that for
u,v\in\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we have

(9)

[u+v]_{W_{q}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C([u]_{W_{q}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+[v]_{W_{q}^{s,p}(\Omega)})

where
C>1
, depending on
p,q
when either or both
p,q<1
, and
C=1
when
p,q\geq 1
. In the case
p=q
, we denote
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{p}(\Omega)
by
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. The corresponding non-homogeneous spaces are
W^{1,p}(\Omega):=\dot{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
,
W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\dot{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
, and
W^{s,p}_{q}(\Omega):=\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
. The norm of
u\in W^{s,p}_{q}(\Omega)
is

(10)

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W_{q}^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

For an open set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the
(s,p,q)
-Capacity of
A
for
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
is defined to be

(11)

\inf\{\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}}^{p}\mid u\in W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\ u\geq 1\text{ on a neighbourhood of $A$}\}

and it is denoted by
\mathrm{Cap}_{s,p,q}(A)
. In the case
p=q
, we simplify notation by writing it as
\mathrm{Cap}_{s,p}(A)
.
For future reference, we mention the following results:

Lemma 2.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 5.1.9]

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
p>1
,
sp<n
, and let
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then
\mathrm{C}_{s,p}(A)=0
, whenever
\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}(A)<\infty
. Here,
\mathrm{C}_{s,p}(A)
denotes the Bessel capacity of the set
A
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
1
, Proposition 4.4.4]

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<p\leq n/s
. Then there is a constant
M
such that for all
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
q\in[1,\frac{np}{n-sp})

M^{-1}\mathrm{C}_{s,p}(E)\leq\mathrm{Cap}_{s,p,q}(E)\leq M\mathrm{C}_{s,p}(E).

For any measurable function
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
u
finite a.e. and a measurable subset
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of finite positive measure,
the median
of
u
, over the set
E
, is

m_{u}(E):=\sup\left\{M\in{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ |\{x\in E\ |\ u(x)<M\}|\geq\frac{|E|}{2}\right\}.

For a fixed set
E
, we define

(12)

E^{-}:=\left\{x\in E\ |\ u(x)\leq m_{u}(E)\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad E^{+}:=\left\{x\in E\ |\ u(x)\geq m_{u}(E)\right\}.

Note that
2|E^{+}|\geq|E|
and
2|E^{-}|\geq|E|
. We record two observations, which we shall use in the sequel.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E,F
be sets of finite positive measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
u
be a real-valued measurable function a.e. finite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then there exist
\tilde{E}\subset E,\tilde{F}\subset F
such that
|\tilde{E}|\geq\frac{|E|}{2},|\tilde{F}|\geq\frac{|F|}{2}
and

|m_{u}(E)-m_{u}(F)|\leq|u(x)-u(y)|

for all
x\in\tilde{E},y\in\tilde{F}
.

Proof.

Let
E,F
be two subsets of finite measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If

m_{u}(E)\leq m_{u}(F),

set
\tilde{E}=E^{-}
and
\tilde{F}=F^{+}
.
Then for all
x\in\tilde{E}
and
y\in\tilde{F}
we get

(13)

\displaystyle 0\leq m_{u}(F)-m_{u}(E)\leq u(y)-u(x)=|u(x)-u(y)|.

On the other hand if

m_{u}(F)\leq m_{u}(E),

set
\tilde{E}=E^{+}
and
\tilde{F}=F^{-}
. Then for all
x\in\tilde{E}
and
y\in\tilde{F}

(14)

\displaystyle 0\leq m_{u}(E)-m_{u}(F)|=u(x)-u(y)\leq|u(x)-u(y)|.

The claim follows from inequalities
(
13
)
and
(
14
)
.∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
E
be a set of finite positive measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
u
be a real-valued measurable function a.e. finite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then there exists a set
\tilde{E}\subset E
such that
|\tilde{E}|\geq\frac{|E|}{2}
and

|m_{u}(E)-c|\leq|u(x)-c|

for all
x\in\tilde{E}
.

Proof.

Fix
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a set of finite positive measure. Suppose,
m_{u}(E)\geq c
. Choose
x\in E^{+}
where
E^{+}
is as in (
12
), then

(15)

0\leq m_{u}(E)-c\leq u(x)-c\leq|u(x)-c|.

If
m_{u}(E)\leq c
then for
x\in E^{-}
where
E^{-}
is as in (
12
), we have

(16)

0\leq c-m_{u}(E)\leq c-u(x)\leq|u(x)-c|.

Therefore we get that

(17)

|m_{u}(E)-c|\leq|u(x)-c|

for a set
\tilde{E}\subset E
with
|\tilde{E}|\geq\frac{|E|}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
. Let
E,F
be subsets of finite measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
u
be a real-valued measurable function a.e. finite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then we have

(18)

\displaystyle|m_{u}(E)-m_{u}(F)|^{p}\leq 2^{1+\frac{p}{q}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptE}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptF}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{F}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{F}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{F}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}dy\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx.

Proof.

Using Lemma

|m_{u}(E)-m_{u}(F)|^{q}\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\tilde{F}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\tilde{F}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\tilde{F}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\tilde{F}}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}dy.

Hence, it follows that

Proof of Proposition
1.6
.

Write
\lambda=\frac{n}{p}
. Define a function
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by setting

(33)

u(x)=\begin{cases}1,\qquad&\text{if }|x|\leq 1,\\
|x|^{-\lambda},\qquad&\text{if }|x|>1.\end{cases}

Then

(34)

|\nabla u(x)|=\begin{cases}0,\qquad&\text{if }|x|\leq 1,\\
\lambda|x|^{-\lambda-1},\qquad&\text{if }|x|>1.\end{cases}

We will show that
u\in\dot{W}_{q}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})}\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u(x+rh)\cdot hdr\right|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\sup_{y\in B(0,2^{-1})}|\nabla u(x+y)|)^{p}\left(\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{1}{|h|^{n+sq-q}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&\leq C(n)\left(\frac{1}{q-sq}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(\sup_{z\in B(x,2^{-1})}|\nabla u(z)|)^{p}dx.\end{split}

Thus we get

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}&\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&\qquad\leq C(n,p,q,s)\left(\int_{B(0,1)}(\sup_{z\in B(x,2^{-1})}|\nabla u(z)|)^{p}dx+\int_{B(0,1)^{c}}(\sup_{z\in B(x,2^{-1})}|\nabla u(z)|)^{p}dx\right)\\
&\qquad\leq C(n,p,q,s,\lambda)\int_{B(0,1)^{c}}\frac{1}{|x|^{p(\lambda+1)}}dx\\
&\qquad\leq C(n,p,q,s,\lambda)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}dt}{t^{(\lambda+1)p}}<\infty,\end{split}

since
\lambda+1>\frac{n}{p}
.
We are left to estimate

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx&\leq\int_{B(0,2)}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx.\end{split}

First, let us deal with the first part of the right-hand side of the above inequality. We have

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,2)}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx&\leq\int_{B(0,2)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(0,2^{j})\setminus B(0,2^{j-1})}\frac{1}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&\leq C(n,p,q,s)\int_{B(0,2)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-jsq}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx<\infty.\end{split}

We are left to show that the second part of the right-hand side of (
3
) is finite. We split the integral into three parts:

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}&\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\

&\qquad\qquad+\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}\cap[\{|h|<2|x|\}\setminus B(-x,1)}]\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(-x,1)}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\
&:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

For
I_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}\cap[\{|h|\geq 2|x|\}\setminus B(-x,1)]}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\

since

\begin{split}I_{3}&=\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(-x,1)}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\

\begin{split}I_{2}&=\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}\cap[\{|h|<2|x|\}\setminus B(-x,1)]}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\

&+\int_{B(0,2)^{c}}\left(\int_{B(0,2^{-1})^{c}\cap[\{B(-x,3|x|)\setminus B(-x,\frac{|x|}{2})\}\setminus B(-x,1)]}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{q}}{|h|^{n+sq}}dh\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\\

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{2^{(n+sq+q)\frac{p}{q}}}\int_{B(0,2^{j_{0}})^{c}\setminus E}\left(\int_{A}\frac{1}{|x|^{n+sq}}dy\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx

(94)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{p}|A|^{\frac{p}{q}}}{2^{(n+sq+q)\frac{p}{q}}}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x|^{(n+sq)\frac{p}{q}}}dx

(95)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{p}|A|^{\frac{p}{q}}}{2^{(n+sq+q)\frac{p}{q}}}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{j}\setminus E|}{2^{j(n+sq+q)\frac{p}{q}}}

(96)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{p}|A|^{\frac{p}{q}}}{2^{(n+sq+q)\frac{p}{q}+1}}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{j}|}{2^{j(n+sq)\frac{p}{q}}}

(97)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{p}|A|^{\frac{p}{q}}}{2^{(n+sq+q)\frac{p}{q}+1}}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}2^{j(n-(n+sq)\frac{p}{q})}=\infty

since
q\geq\frac{np}{n-sp}
. This is not possible since
u
was assumed to be in the space
\dot{W}^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Hence the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-sp}
and
sp<n
. If
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a compact set with
\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}(E)<\infty
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is
u\in W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
u\geq 1
on
E
and
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a compact set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
so that
\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}(E)<\infty
. Then by Lemma
2.2
, we have
\mathrm{C}_{s,p}(E)=0
and hence
\mathrm{Cap}_{s,p,q}(E)=0
by Lemma
2.3
. Thus for any given
\varepsilon>0
there exist a
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
on a neighbourhood of
E
and
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\varepsilon
.
∎

Next, we show that the Hausdorff estimate in Lemma
4.3
is optimal, in the sense that we cannot control
\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\infty}(E_{t})
for
\lambda=n-sp
. This is formally stated in Remark
4.8
below.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-sp}
and
sp<n
. Let
Q
be a cube of edge length
\mathrm{1}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a function
u\in W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
so that
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\varepsilon
and
\sup_{t>0}t^{p}\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(E_{t})\geq C
for some fixed constant
C>0
, where
E_{t}=\{x\in Q\mid|u(x)-m_{u}(Q)|>t\}
.

Proof.

Let
E\subset\frac{1}{2}Q
be compact and
(n-sp)
-Ahlfors regular with
\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}(E)\equiv\frac{1}{2^{n-sp}}
. For construction of such sets see
[
9
]
. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q)
be such that
\psi=1
on
\frac{1}{2}Q
and
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subset\frac{2}{3}Q
. Then,
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{n-sp}(Q\setminus\text{supp}(\psi))\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(Q\setminus\frac{2}{3}Q)\geq\frac{1}{3}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Then by Lemma
4.6
we find
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
on a neighbourhood of
E
and
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\frac{\varepsilon}{\|\psi\|_{W^{s,p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}}
. Thus, for
v=u_{\varepsilon}\psi
, we have

(98)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(\{x\in Q\mid v(x)=0\})

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(Q\setminus\text{supp}(u_{\varepsilon}\psi))\geq\frac{1}{3}

and by
[
7
, Lemma 7.8]
we have

(99)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(\{x\in Q\mid v(x)=1\})

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(E)\geq C(C_{A},n,s,p)\frac{1}{2^{n-sp}}.

where
C_{1}
is the Ahlfors-regularity constant of
E
. Therefore, by the above two estimates, we have

(100)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3^{p}}\mathcal{H}^{n-sp}_{\infty}(\{x\in Q\mid|v(x)-m_{v}(Q)|>\frac{1}{3}\})

\displaystyle\geq C

where
C=\min\{C(C_{A},n,s,p)\frac{1}{2^{n-sp}},C(p)\frac{1}{3}\}
. Hence the claim follows.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

If we let

5.
The case of radial limits

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Without loss of generality, we assume

\mathbb{S}^{n-k}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}\times\{0\}^{k}\mid|x|=1\}.

Step-1:
Construction of
the set
F
of bad parameters
.

Consider
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, mentioned in Remark
4.4
. Set
E_{j}:=E\setminus B(0,j)
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Consider the orthogonal projection

\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n-k}

given by

\pi((x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))=\frac{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-k+1},0,\cdots,0)}{|(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-k+1},0,\cdots,0)|}.

Note that
\pi(P_{z}^{k})=\{z\}
. Define

F:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\pi(E_{j}).

Step-2:
Analysis of the complement of
F
inside
\mathbb{S}^{n-k}
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\setminus F
and
\varepsilon>0
be chosen arbitrarily. Since
a_{i}\to 0
, let
i_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
i>i_{0}
implies
a_{i}<\varepsilon
. Now, we have

z\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\setminus\pi(E_{j})\right).

That is, there is some
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
z\in\mathbb{S}^{n-k}\setminus\pi(E_{j_{0}})
, consequently, for every
j\geq j_{0}
,

\left(P_{z}^{k}\setminus B(0,j)\right)\cap E=P_{z}^{k}\cap E_{j}=\emptyset.

•

Choose
M_{1}>0
such that
B(z,M_{1})\supset B(0,j_{0})
.

•

By Lemma
4.1
, choose
M_{2}>0
such that and
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B(z,M_{2})
with
\ell(Q)=1
implies

(101)

|m_{u}(Q)-K_{u}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

•

Choose
M_{3}>0
such that
Q_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B(0,M_{3})
implies
i>i_{0}
. Consequently,
Q_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B(0,M_{3})
implies
a_{i}<\varepsilon
.

•

Choose
M=2\max\{M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\}
.

Choose
\xi\in P_{z}^{k}\setminus B(z,M)\subset P_{z}^{k}\setminus B(0,j_{0})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus E
. Then, for some

,
\xi\in Q_{i(\xi)}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B(0,M_{2})
. Also note that

, gives

6.
The case of vertical limits

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Without loss of generality let us assume that

V^{n-k}={\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}\times\{0\}^{k}.

Step-1:
Construction of
the set
\mathcal{S}
of bad parameters
.

Consider
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, mentioned in Remark
4.4
. Set
E_{j}:=E\setminus B(0,j)
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Consider the orthogonal projection

\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}

defined by
\pi((x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-k})
. Note that
\pi(P_{z}^{k})=\{z\}
. Define

Step-2:
Analysis of the complement of
\mathcal{S}
inside
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}\times\{0\}^{k}
.

Let, for some
x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}
,

. Then

That is, there is some
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
j\geq j_{0}
, we have
x_{1}\in\left({\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}\setminus\pi(E_{j})\right)

j_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}

j>j_{0}
, and every
x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}

x:=(x_{1},x_{2})\notin E_{j}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be chosen arbitrarily.

Since
a_{j}\to 0
, we may choose
j_{1}>j_{0}
such that
a_{j}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for
j>j_{1}
. Choose

be so large that

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\infty}(\mathcal{S})\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\infty}(\pi(E_{j}))\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\lambda}_{\infty}(E_{j})=0.

and

, we have

.

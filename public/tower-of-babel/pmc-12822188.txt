Days alive and out of hospital at 90 days as a composite outcome measure in patients undergoing elective surgery for colorectal cancer– a nationwide, observational study
Authors: Karoline Bendix Bräuner, Maliha Mashkoor, Rasmus Peuliche Vogelsang, Rasmus Dahlin Bojesen, Andreas Weinberger Rosen, Ismail Gögenur
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of this study was to investigate the validity of DAOH-90 in a contemporary cohort of electively operated patients with colorectal cancer and explore its relationship with postoperative outcomes.DesignThe study was a retrospective, observational cohort study.SettingsData on patients operated from April 2014 to April 2019 were collected from the Danish Colorectal Cancer Group database and enriched by information from the Danish National Patient Register. DAOH-90 was analyzed by quantile regression for the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile, using Mann-Whitney U-test, and association between DAOH-90 and 1-and 5-year mortality was analyzed using a Cox regression.PatientsA total of 16,183 patients with colorectal cancer were included. Median DAOH-90 was 85 (IQR: 82–87). The 90-day mortality was 2.2%.Main outcome measuresThe primary outcome was the association between DAOH-90 quantiles and long-term mortality (1 and 5 years).ResultsThere was significant association between DAOH-90 in the 25th, 50th and 75th quantile and death between 90 and 365 days (p = 0.0008-< 0.0001), and for death between 1 and 5 years (p = 0.0026-p < 0.0001). Both medical and surgical complications were associated with significantly lower DAOH-90 for all percentiles. The multivariable quantile regression confirmed that notably complications and poor performance status had great impact on DAOH-90.ConclusionDAOH-90 was associated with several demographic and comorbidity related variables, including severe surgical complications. DAOH-90 was also associated with long-term mortality.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Cancer care, Composite outcome, Colorectal surgery

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common cancer worldwide and the cause of the second most cancer-related deaths with 934,000 deaths in 2020​​ [1]. The treatment of CRC is multimodal, but curative treatment currently always involves surgery. Since any surgery comes with a risk of perioperative morbidity and mortality, there has been a strong focus on efficiency and safety. In the elective surgical setting for patients with CRC major improvements have been made in the past decades. For instance, data from the Danish Colorectal Cancer Group database (DCCG) have shown a decrease in 30-day mortality after elective CRC surgery from 7.6 in 2001 to 2.8 ​ in 2011 [2–5], and data from 2021 shows a further decrease in 30-day mortality to just 1.7%​​ [5] after elective surgery.

Most studies addressing morbidity and mortality after CRC surgery are more than a decade old, are not conducted in a contemporary setting or are not conducted on a general, national population but on specific subgroups [6–11].

As the heterogeneity in the population of patients with CRC continues to increase [12, 13]​ and still more elderly and frail patients are offered surgery, the sole focus on mortality after surgery may not correctly reflect the interests of patients and surgeons, who are increasingly concerned about patient centered outcomes such as quality of life. There is an increasing focus on retaining a functional capacity allowing for common activities of daily living and general quality of life after surgery, which may be impacted by complications or chronic side effects of the surgery [14]​​. Days Alive and Out of Hospital 90 (DAOH-90), is a composite outcome, which combines mortality, readmission, and length of hospital stays into one outcome, and has been used in multiple different patient populations to assess the impact of medical interventions on post-discharge mortality and morbidity [15, 16]​. DAOH-90 has been suggested to have a strong correlation with age and comorbidities in other surgical populations, but only limited data are present in an elective CRC setting [17, 18]​.

Therefore, the objective of this study was to investigate the association between DAOH-90 and parameters of comorbidity and complications in a large Danish CRC population using nationally covering observational health databases operated in a contemporary setting. Additionally, we investigated the relationship between DAOH-90 in 90-day survivors and long-term mortality​.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This was a retrospective, observational cohort study based on patients recorded in the DCCG database during 2014–2019. The DCCG database contains detailed diagnostic and perioperative information on patients diagnosed with CRC in Denmark [2]​. The database has national coverage and has a data coverage of 99 for the years after 2010​3. Additionally, the DCCG data was enriched with data from the Danish National Patient Register (DNPR) [19], specifically details on length of stay and hospital admissions. The DNPR contains trajectory data including all hospital admissions for all patients in Denmark treated in the secondary health care sector since 1974 [19]​. Data from the two databases were linked using the patients’ unique social security number.

Target and study populations

Eligible patients had to be registered in both DCCG, DNPR and undergoing curatively intended, elective surgery for colorectal cancer from January 1 st, 2014, to April 1 st, 2019. The included surgeries were oncological resections except for endoscopic resections such as endoscopic mucosa resections and transanal microsurgery and did not include metastasis surgery. Patients were excluded from the cohort if their primary admission exceeded 270 days, due to the likeliness of registration error leading to such a long admission. Patients had the exact date of surgery in DNPR and DCCG.

Calculation of DAOH-90

The primary outcome variable was the Days Alive and Out of Hospital 90 (DAOH-90), a composite outcome containing mortality within 90 days of surgery and length of stay during both primary and subsequent admissions [15]. The DAOH-90 is set to 0 if the patient dies within 90 days after surgery. If the patient survived for 90 days, the DAOH-90 would be the number of days the patient was out of hospital within the first 90 days after surgery. Since the DNPR database registration of admission time is not always real-time, readmission in this study must be unplanned and longer than 1 day as a short contact lasting few hours for a smaller issue, where admission was not deemed necessary should not count as a full readmission. Many colorectal departments in Denmark offer open contacts after colorectal cancer surgery to encourage patients to contact the department with any questions leading to short contacts without the need of full admissions. Covariates

We adjusted for the American Society of Anesthesiology (ASA) score, World Health Organization Performance Status (WHO-PS) [15] as recorded in the DCCG database before the surgery and Charlson’s Comorbidity Index (CCI)​ [16] retrieved from the DCCG database. Since for instance right sided surgery are known to have a lower incidence of perioperative mortality and morbidity than rectal surgery, we also adjusted for tumor topography and surgical procedure.

Additionally, there is a risk of information bias due to suboptimal registration of some types of data. The most obvious lacking variable in DCCG is complications of Clavien-Dindo grade [20] below 3b. Clavien-Dindo 3b complications mean complications that result in exploratory surgery, and the above grades represent admission to the intensive care unit (ICU), multi-organ failure, and death. These outcomes are generally well-reported, since most reporting to DCCG is done by the responsible surgeon. However, Clavien-Dindo grades of 1–2 are predominantly medical complications and have previously been shown to be underreported in the DCCG database [3]. To minimize the consequence of this lack of documentation of lower Clavien-Dindo grades, we decided to only include complications of Clavien-Dindo grade 3b and above in the complications analysis. We also compiled different specific complications for the later analyses including anastomotic leakage, intraabdominal bleeding, bowel obstruction, wound dehiscence, and intraabdominal abscess for surgical complications and acute myocardial infarction, stroke, and pulmonary, renal, or cardiac insufficiency for medical complications.

Analytical statistics

We performed quantile regression since we expected the consequence of different clinical and complication-related factors affected patients in the various DAOH-90 percentiles differently. We hypothesized that the patients with a low DAOH-90, meaning patients with long admissions, substantial readmissions, or mortality within 90 days, would be more affected by complications than patients with shorter admissions. Patients were split into five groups; 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles, in a multivariable quantile regression with the first including patient characteristic variables such as age, sex, ASA score, performance status, Charlson’s comorbidity index, and UICC stage, the second including a range of surgical and medical complications considered severe, and the third including both.

An additional outcome was exploring the relationship between DAOH-90 and 1- and 5-year mortality. A requirement for being included in the mortality analysis was that the patient survived the 90 days after surgery included in DAOH-90. This means that “1-year mortality” is considered mortality between the end of the 90-day window for inclusion in DAOH-90 and 1 year after surgery. Similarly, “5-year mortality” was mortality between the end of the 90th day after surgery and 5 years after surgery. To test for significant association between DAOH-90 and death between postoperative day (POD) 90 to 365 and POD 366 to 1825, we performed Cox regression analyses and made corresponding Kaplan-Meier plots.

Using R v.4.2.0 and the package Performance, we performed collinearity checks, and we found no significant correlation among variables and used the QuantReg package to perform quantile regression [21]. In addition to the quantile regression performed in the QuantReg package, we used a bootstrapping method to estimate 95%confidence intervals (CI).

Results were reported in accordance with the ‘Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology’ guidelines [22]​. Approval has been given by the Regional Data Protection of Region Zealand under REG-102–2020.

Results

Population

76,849 patients were recorded in DCCG from 2001 to 2019. After applying the exclusion, a total of 16,183 patients were included in the study cohort (Fig. 1).

Distribution of DAOH-90 over the full cohort

Using a Cox regression, we analyzed the association between DAOH-90 and mortality. We produced Kaplan-Meier curves (Figs. 3 and 4) for four groups based on DAOH-90, with group 1 being 0-25th quantile (DAOH-90 = 0–82), group 2 being 25th-50th quantile (DAOH-90 = 82–85), group 3 being 50th-75th quantile (DAOH-90 = 85–87), and group 4 being 75th-100th quantile (DAOH-90 = 87–90).

For mortality between day 90 to day 365, we found that in group 2 (DAOH-90 = 82–85) had a hazard ratio (HR) of mortality of 0.666 (95% CI: 0.538–0.824) compared with group 1 (DAOH-90 = 0–82), group 3 (DAOH-90 = 85–87) had an HR of 0.401 (95% CI: 0.319–0.502) compared with group 1, and group 4 (DAOH-90 = 87–90) had an HR of 0.346 (95% CI: 0.254–0.475) compared with group 1. This association was statistically significant (pgroup 2 = 0.0008, pgroup 3 < 0.0001, and pgroup 4 < 0.0001). In terms of mortality between postoperative day 366 to 1825 we found a HR of 0.851 (95% CI: 0.772–0.939) in group 2 when compared to group 1, who had DAOH-90 s0-82, an HR of 0.549 (95% CI: 0.497–0.607) in group 3, and an HR of 0.401 (95% CI: 0.348–0.463) in group 4, which again were statistically significant (pgroup 2 = 0.0026, pgroup 3 < 0.0001, and pgroup 4 < 0.0001).

We found that the impact patient characteristic measures such as high ASA score, high performance status, or high CCI impacted the DAOH-90 more in the lower percentiles (Table 2).

We found that major complications, in general, decreased the DAOH-90 for all patients except patients in the 10th percentile, who had a DAOH-90 of 2. (Table 3). For anastomotic leakage and bowel obstruction, DAOH-90 significantly decreased for all percentiles, with between 1.33 and 2.76 days for anastomotic leakage and 0.92–1.5 days for bowel obstruction. Most complications resulted in significant changes in DAOH-90, at least between the 25th and 75th percentile. Some complications like stroke, intraabdominal bleeding, and wound dehiscence were associated with a significantly higher DAOH-90 for some percentiles in the isolated complications analysis. When grouping medical and surgical complications and plotting a histogram of DAOH-90 for patients with and without complications (Fig. 5), medical complications (Fig. 1), and surgical complications (Fig. 7), patients with complications generally had lower DAOH-90 by comparison.

Discussion

Our findings of median DAOH-90 of 85 (IQR = 82–87) and mean DAOH-90 of 81.1 (SD = 14.4) correlate well with the expected, when considering the current literature, although most papers using DAOH-90 as an outcome for the CRC surgery population, tends to investigate subpopulations with expected longer admissions such as anemic patients [6, 23]. Using Cox regression, we found a significant difference between DAOH-90 and 90–365-day mortality and 5-year mortality. Knowing that the median DAOH-90 after elective colorectal cancer surgery is 85, mainly resulting from primary admission days and not readmissions, can assist with preoperative information for patients about how long they should expect hospital contacts to be in this trajectory. Additionally, knowing the risk factors for lower DAOH can help inform and prepare frailer patients before surgery and admission.

The novelty of this study includes the use of two nationally covering observational health databases to calculate DAOH-90 and the assessment of relationship between long-term mortality and DAOH-90. It may seem intuitive, that poor DAOH-90 is related with death, however, as was shown in a previous study in the colorectal cancer patient population, there was no significant difference in long-term mortality for old and young patients, if they survived the first 90 postoperative days, where the complications occur [24]. This may lead to the belief that if the patient survives the DAOH-90 period, they will have similar long-term outcomes, but even after censoring patients, who died within the first 90 postoperative days, there was still a significant association with 1- and 5-year mortality. Using quantile regression, we found an association between DAOH-90 and demographic variables, including comorbidities, cancer stage and surgery type, and postoperative complications, which means that DAOH-90 as an outcome embodies the diversity in this patient population well. We found that poor performance status was highly associated with lower DAOH-90 especially in lower percentiles [16]. Higher ASA scores, male sex, UICC stage IV, and higher Charlson’s comorbidity index were associated with lower DAOH-90 for the lower percentiles.

When investigating major complications after surgery, we opted to initially assess a range of relatively well-reported complications from DCCG rather than a simple assessment of Clavien-Dindo grades, which are known to have poorer reporting rates for lower grades [3]. We investigated both severe surgical and medical complications. For anastomotic leakage and bowel obstruction, we found significantly decreased DAOH-90 for all percentiles, and for most others, we found, from the 25th to 75th percentile, that the DAOH-90 was significantly different. However, not all complications resulted in a decrease in DAOH-90. Stroke, fascial dehiscence, and intraabdominal bleeding all had significantly higher DAOH-90 in quantile regression. This may be due to relatively low incidence rates. Lastly, we found that major complications seem to have less impact on DAOH-90 for patients in the upper 75th and 90th percentile, indicating that patients with generally shorter admission times, fewer and shorter readmissions, and who did not die within the first 90 days of surgery are less impacted by a complication than patients with generally worse DAOH-90. When using both patient characteristics and composite complication outcomes, we found that medical complications decreased DAOH-90 remarkably in the lower percentiles but less in the higher.

Patients who get operated on for CRC are well known to have both minor complications, medical complications, and severe complications, and 48% of patients operated on for CRC had at least one complication, and 14% had major surgical complication in 2020​6​. In this dataset, we saw major complications in 11.2% of the cases [18]. Additionally, approximately 12%of patients are readmitted at least once after the initial surgical admission [25–27]​, however, in this dataset, we found a readmission rate of 6.8%.

DAOH has in previous studies [25]​​ been validated in different time frames including 30, 90, 180, and 365 days. Due to the nature of the cohort in the previously mentioned study, which was patients undergoing emergency laparotomy, where the mortality after the intervention is markedly higher in an elective CRC cohort, they recommended DAOH-30 for their population to avoid noise in the form of many patients dying and having DAOH = 0. For the elective colorectal cancer population, however, where the mortality within 90 days is just 2.1%​3, this is not a good reason to decrease from DAOH-90 to DAOH-30. On the contrary, the nature of the CRC population means that patients may be readmitted relatively long after surgery, either due to late complications like stoma complications or side effects due to subsequent chemotherapy, which makes DAOH-90 a more generalizable outcome in an oncological population.

Strengths and limitations

The strengths of this study include detailed data sources and the high coverage of the data through DCCG and DNPR. The methods are frequently used in epidemiological studies and are well-described and understood. Using the DAOH-90 outcome was shown to be feasible with the data provided and correlated with data points, which have been correlated to complicated or longer-lasting patient trajectories such as high ASA score, performance status, many comorbidities, and rectal cancer surgery. Lastly, we found a statistically significant association between a low DAOH-90 score and death between 90 and 365 days after surgery and 90 to 1825 days after surgery.

The limitations of this study are related to the data sources used. Since this study is a register study, poor registration practice may lead to results not being directly translatable in a clinical setting. An example of this, and how we attempted to mitigate it, is the complications grading system. Unfortunately, registration of medical complications and minor complications may to be less complete than major surgical complications in observational health databases and certainly has been shown to be underreported in our data source (DCCG), which is why we opted to only include complications of Clavien-Dindo grade 3b or above, meaning complications resulting in surgery, since these are properly registered. In its essence, the Clavien-Dindo scale is very surgeon-centric, meaning that the impact of a certain complication on the patient’s life is not necessarily considered; rather, what is captured is the perception of severity from a surgical perspective​ [25]​. It may seem obvious that patients with complications have longer admissions and are readmitted, however, confirming this in a contemporary colorectal cancer cohort is important. Especially the trend where patients with the best DAOH-90 also have major surgical complications in only 1%of the cases, whereas the patients with the worst DAOH-90, 68%of patients had major surgical complications, clearly demonstrates that the postoperative trajectory, even after discharge is worse for patients with major complications.

We also found that many of the predictor variables were associated with each other, meaning that there was a high risk of confounding, which we attempted to address by multivariable analysis adjusting for these confounders. It should also be noted that since ASA score and CCI index are both markers of comorbidity, albeit ASA score being more subjective in daily use, there is strong multicollinearity between ASA score, CCI index, and performance status, which is why they are relevant to adjust for in multivariable analysis. Lastly, conducting this study on Danish data is a limitation as not all countries have the universal healthcare system Denmark has. Conducting a similar study in a more private healthcare system than Denmark may lead to differences in DAOH-90 due to insurance policies or patients’ financial capacity.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we confirm the validity of DAOH-90 as a surgical outcome for patients undergoing surgery for CRC. We showed a statistically significant relationship between DAOH-90 1-year mortality and 5-year mortality. DAOH-90 could be chosen in cohort studies where the scope is to choose a broad patient-centric outcome.

Subdimensional Disorder and Logarithmic Defect


Abstract
We study quenched disorder localized on a p p -dimensional subspacetime in a d d -dimensional conformal field theory. Motivated by the logarithmic behavior often associated with disorder, we introduce a defect setup in which bulk local operators transform in ordinary conformal representations, while defect local operators assemble into logarithmic multiplets. We refer to such objects as logarithmic defects and investigate their model-independent properties dictated solely by conformal symmetry and its representation theory, including correlation functions, logarithmic defect operator expansions, and conformal blocks. As a concrete example, we analyze the free scalar theory with a generalized pinning defect subject to random coupling fluctuations, and we identify a half-line of fixed points describing the corresponding logarithmic conformal defects. Along the way, we propose a candidate monotone governing defect renormalization group flows induced by subdimensional disorder. We comment on various generalizations and the broader program of bootstrapping logarithmic defects.

Subdimensional Quenched Disorder

In this section, we introduce the notion of subdimensional quenched disorder, and analyze the concrete models described in
Introduction
.
In Section
2.1
, we set up our notation and formulate the subdimensional disorder in its most general form. In Section
2.2
, we formulate the Harris-type criterion for conformality in the presence of subdimensional disorder. To illustrate this criterion, we consider the disordered theory (
1.6
) with bulk dynamics governed by a free scalar field. We show that subdimensional disorder produces a conformal defect only when
p=d-2
, as evidenced by the behavior of certain bulk correlators. In Section
2.3
, we analyze the fixed-point structure using the replica method. In particular, we derive the beta function for the disorder strength
c
and find that it is exactly marginal, implying that the disordered theory defines a defect conformal manifold parametrized by
c
. Moreover, through explicit computations of disorder-averaged correlation functions, we uncover logarithmic behavior, which serves as a characteristic signature of disordered fixed points.

2.1
Defect from Subdimensional Quenched Disorder

We now set up the general framework of subdimensional quenched disorder in
d
-dimensional Euclidean spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For later convenience, we split the coordinate for

\displaystyle x^{\mu}=(\hat{x}^{a},\,x_{\perp}^{i})\,,\qquad a=1,2,\dots,p\,,\quad i=p+1,\dots,d\,,

(2.1)

where
\hat{x}^{a}
parameterize the
p

denote the remaining transverse directions. As mentioned in

\displaystyle S[X,g]\equiv S_{\text{CFT}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\text{d}^{p}\hat{x}\,g(\hat{x})O(\hat{x})\,,

(2.2)

where

is the UV CFT action,

is some primary operator in the UV CFT and

represents the bulk fields collectively. Whenever the transverse coordinates are suppressed, they are taken to be at

We assume that the coupling
g(\hat{x})
is randomly fluctuating over
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and the probability distribution functional
P[g]
satisfies the following normalization condition:

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}g\,P[g]=1\,.

(2.3)

For a fixed configuration of the subdimensional coupling
g
, one can define the connected correlation function as

\displaystyle\langle\,\cdots\,\rangle_{g}^{\text{conn}}\equiv\frac{1}{Z_{g}}\int\mathcal{D}X(\cdots)e^{-S[X,g]}\,,\quad Z_{g}\equiv\int\mathcal{D}Xe^{-S[X,g]}\,,

(2.4)

where
\cdots
means some inserted operators, and the subscript
g
is attached just to emphasize that
g
is fixed. In terms of this, we introduce the subdimensional disorder-averaged correlator by

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\cdots\,\rangle}\equiv\int\mathcal{D}g\,P[g]\langle\,\cdots\,\rangle_{g}^{\text{conn}}\,.

(2.5)

In principle, one may consider various probability distributions (see relevant discussions in Section
5
). In this paper, for simplicity, we exclusively consider the Gaussian distribution defined by

\displaystyle P[g]\propto\exp{\left[-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\text{d}^{p}\hat{x}\,\left({g}(\hat{x})\right)^{2}\right]}\,,

(2.6)

where
c
is the width of the Gaussian function which characterizes the strength of the disorder. The omitted overall factor above can be fixed from the normalization condition (

). In this case, the one and two-point functions of subdimensional coupling

is written as

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,g(\hat{x})\,\rangle}=0\,,\quad\overline{\langle\,g(\hat{x})g(\hat{y})\,\rangle}=c^{2}\delta^{p}(\hat{x}-\hat{y})\,.

(2.7)

One can deduce all other correlation functions from this two-point function via the Wick’s theorem. For instance, the four-point function reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\overline{\langle\,g(\hat{x}_{1})g(\hat{x}_{2})g(\hat{x}_{3})g(\hat{x}_{4})\,\rangle}\\
&\quad=c^{4}\left[\delta^{p}(\hat{x}_{1}-\hat{x}_{2})\delta^{p}(\hat{x}_{3}-\hat{x}_{4})+\delta^{p}(\hat{x}_{1}-\hat{x}_{3})\delta^{p}(\hat{x}_{2}-\hat{x}_{4})+\delta^{p}(\hat{x}_{1}-\hat{x}_{4})\delta^{p}(\hat{x}_{2}-\hat{x}_{3})\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(2.8)

2.2
Conformality from Disorder and Free Theory Example

A natural way to assess the relevance of quenched disorder is to adapt the Harris criterion
[
46
]
to the defect setting (see also
[
36
]
for a review).
The strength of disorder is characterized by a dimensionless variance parameter
c
, which requires the random coupling
g(\hat{x})
to have dimension
p/2
, as dictated by (

).
It then follows that the subdimensional disorder in (
2.2
) is relevant if
\Delta_{O}<p/2
, marginal if
\Delta_{O}=p/2
, and irrelevant if
\Delta_{O}>p/2
, as summarized in (

We therefore expect disorder to generate nontrivial conformal defects only when
\Delta_{O}\leq p/2
.

5

for a discussion of “dangerously irrelevant” disorder, which can still affect infrared correlation functions through contact terms.

For a unitary CFT, however, this Harris bound is in tension with the unitarity bound for scalar operators, which imposes
p\geq d-2
. The borderline case is realized by the free scalar theory, which we now turn to.

As a concrete illustration of conformal defects arising from subdimensional disorder, let us consider the simplest bulk theory: a free massless scalar field in
d
dimensions.
We introduce quenched disorder by coupling the scalar to a localized random magnetic field
\hat{h}(\hat{x})
(corresponding to
g(\hat{x})
in (
2.2
)) along a
p
-dimensional subspace:

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{d}^{d}x(\partial\sigma)^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\text{d}^{p}\hat{x}\,{\hat{h}}(\hat{x})\sigma(\hat{x})\,,

(2.9)

where
\hat{h}(\hat{x})
is drawn from a Gaussian distribution.
This model can be regarded as a disordered generalization of a pinning defect, where the scalar couples to a random local magnetic field.
We will show that scale invariance is preserved only when the defect has codimension two, i.e.
p=d-2
.
From the Harris criterion discussed above, this corresponds precisely to the marginal case, since the free scalar has scaling dimension
\Delta_{\sigma}=\tfrac{d-2}{2}
.
This provides the first indication that the codimension-two case is distinguished and potentially admits a conformal fixed point.

To make this relation between scale invariance and the dimensional constraint
p=d-2
more concrete, let us examine the bulk two-point function of
\sigma
explicitly. For a given coupling
\hat{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\langle\,\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\rangle_{\hat{h}}}=\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}+C_{d}^{2}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\,\text{d}^{p}\hat{w}\,\frac{\hat{h}(\hat{z}){\hat{h}}(\hat{w})}{|x-\hat{z}|^{d-2}|y-\hat{w}|^{d-2}}\,,

(2.10)

where

\displaystyle C_{d}\equiv\frac{\Gamma\left(d/2-1\right)}{4\pi^{d/2}}\,.

(2.11)

Then, the averaging over the coupling
{h}
gives rise to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\langle\,\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\rangle}&=\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}+{c}^{2}C_{d}^{2}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\,\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{d-2}|y-\hat{z}|^{d-2}}\,.\end{aligned}

(2.12)

Suppose that the scale invariance does hold even with the disorder, the two-point function must be subject to the following Ward-Takahashi identity:

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\sigma(\lambda x)\sigma(\lambda y)\,\rangle}=\lambda^{-(d-2)}\overline{\langle\,\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\rangle}\,.

(2.13)

However the second term in the RHS of (
2.12
) shows the different scaling response in general

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\sigma(\lambda x)\sigma(\lambda y)\,\rangle_{\hat{h}}}=\lambda^{-(d-2)}\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{d-2}}+\lambda^{p-2d+4}\,\hat{c}^{2}C_{d}^{2}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\,\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{d-2}|y-\hat{z}|^{d-2}}\,.

(2.14)

This implies that the scale invariance for subdimensional disorder in the free theory requires that the bulk and defect dimensions must be related as
p=d-2
.

2.3
Half-line of Logarithmic Fixed Points in Free Theory

In the previous section, we saw that conformality of subdimensional disorder in the free scalar theory can only occur at codimension two (i.e.
p=d-2
). In this section, we turn to a fixed-point analysis for this case. Under this dimensional constraint, not only the localized magnetic field but also a localized mass term becomes marginal. It is therefore necessary to analyze the fixed points of the theory including both contributions. To this end, let us consider the following action:

\displaystyle S=S_{\text{bulk}}[\sigma]+S_{\text{D}}[\sigma,\hat{h}]\,,

(2.15)

where
S_{\text{bulk}}
and
S_{\text{D}}
are defined by

\displaystyle S_{\text{bulk}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{d}^{d}x(\partial\sigma)^{2}\,,\quad S_{\text{D}}\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\text{d}^{p}\hat{x}\,{\hat{h}}(\hat{x})\sigma(\hat{x})+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\text{d}^{p}\hat{x}(\sigma(\hat{x}))^{2}\,.

(2.16)

Here,
\gamma
is the defect coupling constant (to the mass term) and together with the disorder strength
c
, they are the deformation parameters of the theory. In what follows, we compute the corresponding beta functions, and discuss the stability of the fixed points.
We proceed by employing the replica method as in

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\langle\mathcal{O}(\sigma)\rangle}&=\overline{\langle\mathcal{O}(\sigma)\rangle\langle\mathbf{1}\rangle^{n-1}}\\

\displaystyle S_{\text{rep}}[\vec{\sigma}]\equiv\sum_{A=1}^{n}S_{\text{bulk}}[\sigma_{A}]+\sum_{A,B}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{x}\,m_{AB}\sigma_{A}(\hat{x})\sigma_{B}(\hat{x})\,,

(2.18)

where

\displaystyle m_{AB}\equiv-\frac{c^{2}}{2}+\gamma\,\delta_{AB}\,.

(2.19)

With the replica action (

and

\displaystyle\beta_{c^{2},n}\equiv\frac{\text{d}c^{2}}{\text{d}\log\mu}\,,\quad\beta_{\gamma,n}\equiv\frac{\text{d}\gamma}{\text{d}\log\mu}\,,

(2.20)

where the subscript

These beta functions are determined by solving the Callan–Symanzik equation for the bulk one-point function

\displaystyle\beta_{c^{2},n}=C_{d}S_{p-1}\left(2\gamma c^{2}-nc^{4}\right)+\cdots\,,\quad\beta_{\gamma,n}=C_{d}S_{p-1}\gamma^{2}+\cdots\,,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\beta_{c^{2},0}=2C_{d}S_{p-1}\gamma c^{2}+\cdots\,,\quad\beta_{\gamma,0}=C_{d}S_{p-1}\gamma^{2}+\cdots\,.

(2.22)

From these expressions, one immediately finds that there exists a half-line (due to the identification

(2.23)

This half-line of fixed points corresponds precisely to the exactly marginal disorder strength

2.5
Symmetry Restoration by Subdimensional Disorder

It is often observed that a symmetry that is explicitly broken in the absence of disorder becomes restored once disorder is introduced.
7
7

7

See
[
84
]
for discussions on this phenomenon from the view point of topological defects.
In this short section, we confirm this phenomenon in our pinning defect model (
2.9
). Before introducing the subdimensional disorder, the bulk spin-flip
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry is explicitly broken on the defect. Indeed, if we compute the bulk one-point function of
\sigma
in a fixed background configuration of the defect coupling
\hat{h}
, it turns out to be nonzero:

\displaystyle\langle\sigma(x)\rangle_{\hat{h}}=-C_{d}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{y}\frac{\hat{h}(\hat{y})}{|x-\hat{y}|^{p}}\,.

(2.29)

However, once we average over
\hat{h}
according to the Gaussian distribution, this one-point function vanishes,

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\sigma(x)\rangle}=0\,.

(2.30)

This clearly indicates that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry is restored by the inclusion of the subdimensional disorder. Physically, this is reasonable because the Gaussian distribution is symmetric under the transformation
\hat{h}\to-\hat{h}
.

2.6
First Pass: Logarithmic Behavior of Correlation Functions

We now turn to the study of correlation functions on the fixed line described in (
2.23
). Since the fixed line occurs at
\gamma=0
, it is sufficient to analyze the original action (
2.9
). In this subsection, we focus exclusively on defect two-point functions, while correlation functions involving bulk local operators will be discussed in the next section. Our main goal here is to demonstrate explicitly that the disorder-averaged CFT correlators exhibit logarithmic dependence in contrast to the power-law behavior in usual CFTs.

Two-point function:
\overline{\langle\,\sigma(\hat{x})\sigma(0)\,\rangle}

We firstly investigate the disorder-averaged two-point function
\overline{\langle\,\sigma(\hat{x})\sigma(0)\,\rangle}
on the defect, which can be deduced by setting
x_{\perp}\to 0
and
y\to 0
in (
2.12
):

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\sigma(\hat{x})\sigma(0)\,\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{d}}{|\hat{x}|^{p}}+{c}^{2}C_{d}^{2}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\,\frac{1}{|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|^{p}|\hat{z}|^{p}}\,.

(2.31)

The remaining integral clearly develops logarithmic singularities near the operator insertions at
\hat{z}=0
and
\hat{z}=\hat{x}
. Indeed, upon evaluating the integral, one finds that it behaves as

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\sigma(\hat{x})\sigma(0)\,\rangle}=\frac{C_{d}}{|\hat{x}|^{p}}+\frac{C_{d}c^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{\log\mu^{2}|\hat{x}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}|^{p}}\,,

(2.32)

where
\mu
is an energy scale from regulating the integral. This logarithmic behavior is a distinctive feature of disorder averaging, and it clearly indicates that
\sigma(\hat{x})
does not behave as an ordinary CFT primary operator.

Two-point function:
\overline{\langle\,\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\widetilde{D}_{j}(0))\,\rangle}

As a next step, we turn to a slightly more non-trivial example by considering a composite defect local operator
\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\equiv\sigma\partial_{i}\sigma(\hat{x})
that carries transverse spin one. Its two-point function can be computed from

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\widetilde{D}_{j}(\hat{y})\,\rangle}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\perp}^{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{\perp}^{j}}\overline{\langle\,\sigma^{2}(x)\sigma^{2}(y)\,\rangle}\right|_{x_{\perp}=y_{\perp}=0}\,.

(2.33)

After a straightforward calculation, one finds that the above two-point function can be expressed in terms of an integral of the form:

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\widetilde{D}_{j}(0)\,\rangle}=\frac{pC_{d}^{2}\delta_{ij}}{|\hat{x}|^{2p+2}}+\frac{pc^{2}C_{d}^{3}\delta_{ij}}{|\hat{x}|^{p+2}}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\frac{1}{|\hat{x}-\hat{z}|^{p}|\hat{z}|^{p}}\,.

(2.34)

This integral is the same as the one that we already encountered in the scalar two-point function (
2.31
). In particular, it exhibits a logarithmic divergence, confirming once again that the correlation functions do not follow a simple power-law behavior but instead display logarithmic corrections:

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\widetilde{D}_{j}(0)\,\rangle}=pC_{d}^{2}\delta_{ij}\left(\frac{1}{|\hat{x}|^{2p+2}}+\frac{c^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{\log\mu^{2}|\hat{x}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}|^{2p+2}}\right)\,.

(2.35)

In Section
4
, we will see that these two-point functions are consistent with the structure of a logarithmic conformal defect, where defect local operators form nontrivial logarithmic multiplets. In particular,
\tilde{D}_{i}
will be an operator in the logarithmic displacement multiplet.

3
Systematics of Logarithmic Defect CFT

From the defect two-point functions discussed in the previous sections and general discussion in
Introduction
, we expect defect local operators generated by subdimensional disorder to exhibit logarithmic behavior in their correlation functions. In particular, the appearance of logarithmic corrections indicates that these operators are naturally organized into logarithmic multiplets in logarithmic CFT (logCFT), rather than ordinary primaries and their descendants. Motivated by this perspective, we will refer to conformal defects whose local operator spectrum is built out of logarithmic multiplets as
logarithmic defects
. The main goal of this section is to extract the model-independent features of such logarithmic conformal defects. In Section
3.1
, we review the representation theory of logCFT by focusing on primaries and the structures of correlation functions. In Section
3.3
, we discuss how ordinary bulk CFT primary operators can be expanded in terms of logarithmic defect operators. Particularly, we determine the possible form of the defect-operator-expansion just from the representation theory. In Section
3.4
, we also discuss the bootstrap equation that constrains the CFT data associated with logarithmic defects.

3.1
Review of Logarithmic Conformal Representation Theory

In this subsection, we present a brief review on the representation theory of the logCFTs, following
[
62
]
. Readers who wish to explore this subject in more detail are referred to e.g.
[
62
,
56
]
. The conformal algebra
\mathfrak{so}(1,p+1)
is generated by the dilatation
D
, translation
P^{a}
, special conformal transformation
K^{a}
, and rotation
M^{ab}=-M^{ba}
in
p
dimensions. In logCFTs, primary operators with conformal dimension
\widehat{\Delta}
form a logarithmic multiplet of rank
N_{\widehat{\Delta}}\geq 1
, and such states are denoted as
\ket{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}}\,(I=1,2,\cdots,N_{\widehat{\Delta}})
.
8
8

8

As our primary aim in this subsection is to arrange
defect
primaries into logarithmic multiplets, we adopt the convention of attaching a hat to most logarithmic variables.
They satisfy the following highest-weight condition,

\displaystyle K^{a}\ket{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}}=0\,.

(3.1)

The descendant states are obtained by acting the translation operators
P^{a}
on the primary states, just like in the standard CFT. The action of the dilatation operator
D
on this logarithmic multiplet takes the following form,

\displaystyle D\ket{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}}=-\text{i}\widehat{\bm{\Delta}}_{I}^{\,J}\ket{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{J}}\,,

(3.2)

where
\bm{\Delta}
is a matrix of size
N_{\widehat{\Delta}}
. The crucial point is that this matrix cannot be diagonalized by any choice of basis. Instead, by fixing a basis, it can be brought into a Jordan block form

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\Delta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\widehat{\Delta}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\widehat{\Delta}&1\\
0&0&0&0&\widehat{\Delta}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(3.3)

It should be emphasized that, as will become evident later, the logarithmic behavior in logCFTs originates from the off–diagonal structure of the dilatation operator. By using the state-operator correspondence and translations, we can define the primary operator
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})
, which transforms as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{i}[P_{a},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})]&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{a}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})\,,\\
\text{i}[M_{ab},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})]&=\left(\Sigma_{ab}+\hat{x}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{b}}-\hat{x}_{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{a}}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})\,,\\
\text{i}[D,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})]&=\left(\bm{\widehat{\Delta}}_{I}^{\,J}+\delta_{I}^{J}\hat{x}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}}\right)\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{J}(\hat{x})\,,\\
\text{i}[K_{a},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})]&=-2\hat{x}_{a}\left(\bm{\widehat{\Delta}}_{I}^{\,J}+\delta_{I}^{J}\hat{x}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}}\right)\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{J}(\hat{x})+|\hat{x}|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{a}}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})+2\text{i}\hat{x}^{b}\Sigma_{ba}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})\,,\end{aligned}

(3.4)

where
\Sigma_{ab}
is the matrix representation of the
p
-dimensional rotation group associated to the primary operator
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})
. Notably, the action of
D
and
K_{a}
deviates from that in ordinary CFT, and the non-diagonal structure of
\bm{\Delta}
implies mixing among the operators within a logarithmic multiplet. This mixing structure underlies the distinctive feature of logarithmic CFTs and will play a central role in the construction of correlation functions in the following sections.

Finally, we present the finite form of the conformal transformation. From the above infinitesimal transformation rules (
3.4
), one can deduce the finite transformation of logarithmic primaries. For a rank
N_{\widehat{\Delta}}
logarithmic scalar primary operator
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{I}(\hat{x})
, the finite transformation under
\hat{x}\to\hat{x}^{\prime}
reads

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{I}(\hat{x}^{\prime})=\Omega(\hat{x})^{-\widehat{\Delta}/p}\Lambda_{I}^{\,J}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{J}(\hat{x})\,,

(3.5)

where
\Omega(\hat{x})
is the Jacobian of the coordinate transformation,

\displaystyle\Omega(\hat{x})\equiv\left|\frac{\partial(\hat{x}^{\prime})}{\partial(\hat{x})}\right|\,,

(3.6)

and
\Lambda_{I}^{\,J}
is an upper triangular matrix of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda_{I}^{\,J}=\frac{(-\ln\Omega)^{J-I}}{(J-I)!}\,,\quad J\geq I\,.

(3.7)

For instance, in the case of
N_{\widehat{\Delta}}=2
, the above transformation rule (
3.5
) reads,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{1}(\hat{x}^{\prime})&=\Omega(\hat{x})^{-\widehat{\Delta}/p}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(\hat{x})-\ln\Omega\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(\hat{x})\right)\,,\\
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{2}(\hat{x}^{\prime})&=\Omega(\hat{x})^{-\widehat{\Delta}/p}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(\hat{x})\,.\end{aligned}

(3.8)

3.2
Selected Correlation Functions

In this subsection, we investigate how certain correlation functions are constrained by the logarithmic conformal representation theory developed in the previous section. As discussed in
Introduction
, we consider a setup in which ordinary CFT primaries
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)
reside in the bulk, while logarithmic primary operators
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(\hat{x})
are localized on a logarithmic defect. In general, correlation functions in such a defect CFT must satisfy the conformal Ward-Takahashi (WT) identities,
9
9

9

We assume the identity operator on the defect is in an ordinary representation (invariant under the residual conformal symmetry).

\displaystyle\langle\,[G,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\cdots\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I_{1}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1}}(\hat{x}_{1})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I_{2}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}(\hat{x}_{2})\cdots]\,\rangle=0\,,

(3.9)

where
G
is a generator of the residual conformal symmetry
\text{SO}(1,p+1)\times\text{SO}(d-p)
. To illustrate these constraints explicitly, we consider two representative cases in order. First, we study two-point functions of logarithmic primaries living on the defect, showing how their structure is fixed by the conformal Ward-Takahashi identities (
3.9
). Second, we examine bulk–defect two-point functions and demonstrate how their form is similarly determined by these identities. This is without loss of generality since more general correlators are all determined by these basic correlators by operator-product-expansion (OPE).
Through these examples, we also highlight the characteristic features of logarithmic correlations and the interplay between bulk CFT primaries and defect logarithmic primaries.

3.2.1
Defect Two-point Function

To begin with, we determine the defect scalar two-point function
10
10

10

The determination of the defect two-point function follows essentially the same logic as in the case of logarithmic CFT without a defect, see
[
62
, section 2.2]
. Therefore, readers who are already familiar with the analysis in ordinary homogeneous logCFT may safely skip to Section
3.2.2
.

\displaystyle\langle\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1}}^{I_{1}}(\hat{x})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}^{I_{2}}(0)\,\rangle\,,\quad I_{i}=1,2,\cdots,N_{i}\,,

(3.10)

where
N_{i}\equiv N_{\widehat{\Delta}_{i}}
is the rank of a logarithmic multiplet of conformal dimension
\widehat{\Delta}_{i}
. Firstly, the translation and rotational symmetries imply that the defect two-point function depends only on
s\equiv|\hat{x}|
:

\displaystyle\langle\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1}}^{I_{1}}(\hat{x})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}^{I_{2}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)}{s^{\widehat{\Delta}_{1}+\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}}\,.

(3.11)

Here, we pull out the factor
s^{\widehat{\Delta}_{1}+\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}
for convenience, and
B_{I_{1}I_{2}}
is an
N_{1}\times N_{2}
matrix satisfying

\displaystyle B_{I_{1}I_{2}}=0\,,\quad I_{1}>N_{1}\text{ or }I_{2}>N_{2}\,.

(3.12)

Moreover, the conformal WT identities associated to the scale and special conformal transformations produce the following constraints,

\displaystyle s\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)

\displaystyle=-B_{(I_{1}+1)I_{2}}(s)-B_{I_{1}(I_{2}+1)}(s)\,,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left(s\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}+\widehat{\Delta}_{1}-\widehat{\Delta}_{2}\right)B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)

\displaystyle=-2B_{(I_{1}+1)I_{2}}(s)\,,

(3.14)

where we have fixed the basis as in (
3.3
). From these constraints, one can readily obtain

\displaystyle(-\widehat{\Delta}_{1}+\widehat{\Delta}_{2})B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)=B_{(I_{1}+1)I_{2}}(s)-B_{I_{1}(I_{2}+1)}(s)\,.

(3.15)

This implies that, in order for
B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)
to be non-trivial, the conformal dimensions for the two operators must be the same
\widehat{\Delta}\equiv\widehat{\Delta}_{1}=\widehat{\Delta}_{2}
, thereby the ranks are also the same
N\equiv N_{1}=N_{2}
. We thus obtain the result, familiar in the case of ordinary CFT, that two-point functions are only nontrivial for operators in the same conformal multiplet, which holds also in the logarithmic setting.
The constraint (
3.15
) further implies that
B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)
only depends on
I_{1}+I_{2}
:

\displaystyle B_{I_{1}I_{2}}(s)\equiv\beta_{I_{1}+I_{2}-1}(s)\,.

(3.16)

Note that the condition (
3.12
) demands that
\beta_{n}=0
for
n>N
.
In terms of
\beta_{n}(s)
, the conformal WT identity (
3.14
) takes the simpler form,

\displaystyle s\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}s}\beta_{n}(s)=-2\beta_{n+1}(s)\,.

(3.17)

By using
\beta_{N+1}=0
, we can iteratively solve this differential equation as

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(s)=\sum_{m=0}^{N-n}\frac{k_{n+m}}{m!}\left(-\log s^{2}\right)^{m}\,.

(3.18)

In summary, the defect scalar two-point function in logCFT can be fixed as

\displaystyle\langle\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1}}^{I_{1}}(\hat{x})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}^{I_{2}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{\delta_{N_{1},N_{2}}\delta_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1},\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}}{|\hat{x}|^{2\widehat{\Delta}_{1}}}\times\begin{cases}&\hskip-8.53581pt\beta_{I_{1}+I_{2}-1}(|\hat{x}|)\,,\quad 2\leq I_{1}+I_{2}\leq N_{1}+1\,,\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt0\quad\qquad\,,\quad\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(3.19)

We now extend the above discussion to defect two-point functions of logarithmic primaries
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1},i_{1}i_{2},\cdots i_{s}}^{I_{1}}
with transverse spin
s
associated to the rotation group
\mathrm{SO}(d-p)
.
For simplicity, we focus on operators transforming as symmetric traceless tensors under
\mathrm{SO}(d-p)
. This case is sufficient for the examples treated in the present paper. For defect operators, the rotational group
\text{SO}(d-p)
behaves as a global symmetry, and the only invariant tensor is

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{i_{1}i_{2},\cdots i_{s}}^{j_{1}j_{2},\cdots j_{s}}\equiv\delta^{(j_{1}}_{i_{1}}\delta^{j_{2}}_{i_{2}}\cdots\delta^{j_{s})}_{i_{s}}\,,

(3.20)

where the parenthesis is understood to impose the symmetric and traceless combination of indices. In terms of this invariant tensor, the spinning defect two-point function (
4.6
) can be determined as

\displaystyle\langle\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1},i_{1}i_{2},\cdots i_{s}}^{I_{1}}(\hat{x})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}^{I_{2},j_{1}j_{2},\cdots j_{s}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{{\delta}_{N_{1},N_{2}}\delta_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1},\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}}{|\hat{x}|^{2\widehat{\Delta}_{1}}}\,\mathcal{I}_{i_{1}i_{2},\cdots i_{s}}^{j_{1}j_{2},\cdots j_{s}}\times\begin{cases}&\hskip-8.53581pt\beta_{I_{1}+I_{2}-1}(|\hat{x}|)\,,\quad 2\leq I_{1}+I_{2}\leq N_{1}+1\,,\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt0\quad\qquad\,,\quad\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(3.21)

3.2.2
Bulk-defect Two-point Function

We next determine the two-point function of a bulk scalar primary
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
and a defect scalar logarithmic primary
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}
.
Here, we set the position of defect local operator to the origin by using the translation symmetry along the defect.
Firstly, recalling the form of the bulk-defect two-point function in normal defect CFT
[
52
]
, it is convenient to write,

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{C_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}^{I}(x)}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}}|x|^{2\widehat{\Delta}}}\,.

(3.22)

Our remaining task is to fix the function
C_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}^{I}(x)
from the conformal WT identities (
3.9
). Scale invariance forces this function to be subject to the following differential equation,

\displaystyle x\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(x)=-C^{I+1}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(x)\,.

(3.23)

On the other hand, special conformal transformation invariance requires,

\displaystyle-2\hat{x}^{a}\left(\Delta+x\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)F^{a}(x)+x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{a}}F^{a}(x)=0\,,

(3.24)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle-2\hat{x}^{a}x\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(x)+|x|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{x}^{a}}C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(x)=0\,.

(3.25)

The residual Poincaré invariance constrains the function
C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(x)
to depend only on the two variables
11
11

11

Remark that
\log|\hat{x}|
can be written in terms of
\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}
.

\displaystyle\tau\equiv\log\frac{|x_{\perp}|}{|x|^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\tau}\equiv\log|x_{\perp}|^{2}\,.

(3.26)

Although at first sight the correlator seems to depend on both
\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}
, it does depend only on
\tau
. Indeed, by using the following Leibniz rule,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}&=\left({+}\frac{x_{\perp}^{i}\delta_{\mu}^{i}}{|x_{\perp}|^{2}}{-}\frac{2x_{\mu}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+2\frac{x_{\perp}^{i}\delta_{\mu}^{i}}{|x_{\perp}|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\tau}}\,,\end{aligned}

(3.27)

the differential equation (
3.25
) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{\tau}}C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(\tau,\tilde{\tau})=0\,,

(3.28)

which clearly implies that the function
C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}
depends only on
\tau
and (

) becomes,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(\tau)=C^{I+1}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(\tau)\,.

(3.29)

This can be readily solved by the iterative method as before:

\displaystyle C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I}\frac{\lambda_{I+k}\tau^{k}}{k!}\,,

(3.30)

where the coefficients
\lambda_{I+k}
remain undetermined by residual conformal symmetry alone. We comment that this result is consistent with the method of images
[
85
]
, where the bulk-defect two-point function is
kinematically
equivalent to the three-point function in the absence of a defect. More precisely,

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\,\rangle_{\text{DCFT}}\cong\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta/2}(x)\mathcal{O}_{\Delta/2}(\bar{x})\mathcal{O}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\,\rangle_{\text{CFT}}\,,

(3.31)

where
\bar{x}\equiv(\hat{x}^{a},-x_{\perp}^{i})
.
The correlation function on the right hand side has been determined in
[
62
, equation (2.34)]
, and completely matches with our result (
3.22
) and (
3.30
) including the
\tau
-dependence.

We can also extend the above analysis to the case where the defect operator carries spin. For simplicity, we keep the bulk primary to be a scalar. In general, a defect local primary can carry either parallel and transverse spins associated to the rotational groups
\text{SO}(p)
and
\text{SO}(d-p)
, respectively. However, one can show that the bulk–defect two-point function involving a bulk scalar and a defect operator with parallel spin vanishes identically. Therefore, it suffices to consider defect primaries with transverse spin. Let
s
be the transverse spin, the result takes the following form

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta},i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{s}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{x_{\perp}^{(i_{1}}x_{\perp}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{\perp}^{i_{s})}}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}+s}|x|^{2\widehat{\Delta}}}C_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}^{I}(x)\,,

(3.32)

where
C^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}
has the same structure in (
3.30
), and the parenthesis is to impose the symmetric and traceless combination of indices.

3.3
Logarithmic Defect Operator Expansion

In homogeneous CFTs, it is well known that when two local operators approach each other, their product can be systematically expanded into a sum of local operators through the operator-product-expansion (OPE). In the presence of a defect, this framework is enriched by the so-called defect-operator-expansion (DOE), in which a bulk local operator can be expanded in terms of local operators residing on the defect. The purpose of this subsection is to explore the logarithmic defect-operator-expansion (logDOE), which generalizes the standard DOE to the case where the defect supports logCFT local operators. The significance of this expansion lies in the fact that it bridges the representations of the bulk CFT with those of the defect, thereby providing a powerful tool to analyze the interplay between bulk and defect degrees of freedom. This feature will play an important role in later sections.

For simplicity, in this work we focus on the logDOE of a bulk scalar primary
{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta}
. From the analysis in the Section
3.2.2
, it is clear that both scalar defect operators and spinning defect operators with transverse spin can contribute to the expansion.
Accordingly, we have the following general ansatz for the logDOE:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)=\sum_{\widehat{\Delta},s}\sum_{I=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}}\frac{x_{\perp}^{i_{1}}x_{\perp}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{\perp}^{i_{s}}}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}+s}}B^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(|x_{\perp}|,\widehat{\partial})\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta},i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{s}}(\hat{x})\,,

(3.33)

where the sum runs over defect logarithmic primaries (both scalar and spinning), and
N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}
represents the rank of a defect logarithmic primary with dimension
\widehat{\Delta}
and transverse spin
s
. Also, the function
B^{I}_{\Delta,\widehat{\Delta}}(|x_{\perp}|,\widehat{\partial})
encodes the dependence on the transverse distance as well as derivatives along the defect. For convenience, in the above expression we have explicitly extracted the overall power of
|x_{\perp}|
together with the tensor structure associated with the transverse spin
s
.

12

12

What remains is to be fixed are the coefficients

By plugging the expression of logDOE (

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\rangle=\sum_{\widehat{\Delta}^{\prime}}\sum_{J=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}^{\prime}}}\sum_{n=0}^{J-1}\frac{(\log|x_{\perp}|)^{n}}{n!}\frac{g_{J-n}(|x_{\perp}|^{2}\Box_{\hat{x}})}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}^{\prime}}}\langle\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{J}_{\widehat{\Delta}^{\prime}}(\hat{x})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\rangle\,.

(3.40)

Moreover, by using the explicit form of the defect two-point function (

), the above equation is rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\rangle&=\sum_{J=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I+1}\sum_{n=0}^{J-1}\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I-J+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log|x_{\perp}|)^{n}}{n!\,m!}\frac{k_{I+J-1+m}A^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}}_{J-n,\ell}}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}-2\ell}}\,\Box_{\hat{x}}^{\ell}\frac{(\log|\hat{x}|^{2})^{m}}{|\hat{x}|^{2\widehat{\Delta}}}\\

(3.41)

where in the second line, we have rearranged the sum by,

\displaystyle\sum_{J=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I+1}\sum_{n=0}^{J-1}\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I-J+1}f(J,n,m)=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I}\sum_{J=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I-k+1}\sum_{n=0}^{k}f(J+n,n,k-n)\,.

(3.42)

In what follows, we first focus on the coefficients

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\rangle=\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}}|\hat{x}|^{2\widehat{\Delta}}}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I}\sum_{J=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I-k+1}\frac{k_{I+J+k-1}A^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}}_{J,0}}{k!}\left(\log\frac{|x_{\perp}|}{|\hat{x}|^{2}}\right)^{k}+O\left(\frac{|x_{\perp}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}|^{2}}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(3.43)

On the other hand,
by expanding (

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{I}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(0)\rangle=\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{\Delta-\widehat{\Delta}}|\hat{x}|^{2\widehat{\Delta}}}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I}\frac{\lambda_{I+k}}{k!}\left(\log\frac{|x_{\perp}|}{|\hat{x}|^{2}}\right)^{k}+O\left(\frac{|x_{\perp}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}|^{2}}\right)\,.

(3.44)

\displaystyle\lambda_{I}=\sum_{J=1}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}-I+1}k_{I+J-1}A_{J,0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}}\,,\quad 1\leq I\leq N_{\widehat{\Delta}}\,.

(3.45)

\displaystyle A_{J,\ell}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}}=\frac{(-4)^{-\ell}}{\ell!\left(\widehat{\Delta}+z-\frac{p-2}{2}\right)_{\ell}}\sum_{m=1}^{J}(-1)^{J+m}A_{m,0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta}}}\sum_{0\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{J-m}\leq\ell-1}\prod_{r=1}^{J-m}\left(\widehat{\Delta}-\frac{p-2}{2}+k_{r}\right)^{\hskip-2.84526pt-1}\,.

(3.46)

3.4

3.4
Bulk Two-point Function and Bootstrap Constraints

In this subsection we consider the bulk scalar two-point function
\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\rangle
in the presence of a logarithmic defect. The residual conformal invariance allows us to write the bulk two-point function as

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\rangle=\frac{F(\xi,\phi)}{|x_{1\perp}|^{\Delta_{1}}|x_{2\perp}|^{\Delta_{2}}}\,,

(3.47)

where
\xi
and
\phi
are cross-ratios defined by

\displaystyle\xi\equiv\frac{|x_{12}|^{2}}{|x_{1\perp}||x_{2\perp}|}\,,\quad\cos\phi\equiv\frac{x_{1\perp}\cdot x_{2\perp}}{|x_{1\perp}||x_{2\perp}|}\,.

(3.48)

Studying this correlator provides the starting point for deriving the defect bootstrap equations.
The bootstrap equations encode the consistency of OPE: the result obtained by first fusing the two bulk operators must coincide with the one obtained by first expanding a bulk operator into defect operators. Since the bulk theory is an ordinary CFT, the corresponding bulk conformal blocks are identical to the well-known expressions in the literature
[
52
]
. On the other hand, the defect channel exhibits a much richer structure, and the associated defect conformal blocks develop new features characteristic of the logarithmic defect (the discussions on defect conformal blocks of ordinary defect CFT can be found in
[
52
]
). The main goal of this subsection is to derive the logarithmic defect conformal block explicitly.

Specific Gauge Choice

The two-point functions of logarithmic multiplets contain redundancies that correspond to a change of basis among the primaries that preserve the form of the dilatation operator in (
3.2
) (see
[
62
, Appendix. A]
for a detailed discussion).
To simplify the presentation below, we further make use of this freedom to fix

\displaystyle\langle\,\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1},i_{1}i_{2},\cdots i_{s}}^{I_{1}}(\hat{x})\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}^{I_{2},j_{1}j_{2},\cdots j_{s}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{k_{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}}\cdot\delta_{\widehat{\Delta}_{1},\widehat{\Delta}_{2}}\mathcal{I}_{i_{1}i_{2},\cdots i_{s}}^{j_{1}j_{2},\cdots j_{s}}}{(N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}-I_{1}-I_{2}+1)!}\frac{(-\log|\hat{x}|^{2})^{N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}-I_{1}-I_{2}+1}}{|\hat{x}|^{2\widehat{\Delta}}}\,,

(3.49)

and vanishes when

(3.50)

By using the commutation relation

4
More Details on Logarithmic Conformal Defect in Free Theory

In the previous section, we have discussed the universal properties of logarithmic conformal defects, i.e. structural features that do not rely on a specific model. In this section, we circle back to our main focus on such defects obtained from subdimensional disorder and discuss how the framework developed above can be applied to the generalized pinning field model (
2.9
) with random fluctuations. As anticipated in Section
2.6
, several defect local operators are expected to behave as logarithmic primaries. A natural question then arises: what is the rank of the corresponding logarithmic multiplets? To address this, in Section
4.1
, we analyze in detail how various primary operators, including the displacement operator and the tilt operator, organize themselves into logarithmic multiplets, sometimes making use of the bulk equations of motion. In Section
4.2
, we also clarify how the bulk-defect OPE data are determined in this setting. In Section
4.3
we present the bulk scalar two-point function together with its explicit conformal block decomposition, emphasizing both the similarities and the differences with respect to the disorder-free case. Finally in Section
4.4
, we determine the defect one-point functions for bulk operators in the OPE of the scalar and particularly find that the stress energy one-point function vanishes for this non-topological defect!

Displacement Operator Multiplets

A central and universal ingredient of any conformal defect (non-topological) is the displacement operator. This operator arises because the presence of the defect explicitly breaks the translational invariance of the bulk theory in the directions transverse to the defect. As a result, the Ward-Takahashi identity associated with transverse translations is not preserved in its usual form, but instead acquires an insertion localized on the defect. The operator that generates this insertion is precisely the displacement operator. To be more concrete, we recall that the (improved) energy-momentum tensor for the free theory is given by

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}\sigma\partial^{\nu}\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu\nu}\partial^{\rho}\sigma\partial_{\rho}\sigma-\frac{d-2}{4(d-1)}(\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}-\delta^{\mu\nu}\Box)\sigma^{2}\,.

(4.15)

By using the equation of motion

\displaystyle\Box\sigma(x)=\hat{h}(\hat{x})\delta^{(d-p)}(x_{\perp})\,,

(4.16)

the conservation law of the energy-momentum tensor becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu i}(x)={\hat{h}}(\hat{x})\partial^{i}\sigma(x)\delta^{(d-p)}(x_{\perp})\,.

(4.17)

From this, we can read off the displacement operator
D_{i}
as

\displaystyle D_{i}(\hat{x})\equiv\hat{h}(\hat{x})\partial_{i}\hat{\sigma}\,.

(4.18)

The displacement operator transforms as a vector under the transverse rotation group SO
(d-p)
, and it always has fixed conformal dimension
p+1
from (
4.17
). Interestingly, in our disordered system, the displacement operator inherits direct contributions from the randomly fluctuating source
\hat{h}(\hat{x})
. This naturally raises the question of whether this universal defect local operator actually organizes into a logarithmic multiplet. To answer this, one should examine the corresponding defect two-point functions, which as we will see, have features that differ from those of conventional defects in curious ways.

A first observation is that the two-point function of the displacement operator vanishes identically at separated points,

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,D_{i}(\hat{x})D_{j}(0)\,\rangle}=0\,,\quad\hat{x}\not=0\,.

(4.19)

This signals that, due to the effect of disorder averaging, the displacement operator does not behave as an ordinary CFT primary operator. Instead, it is expected to participate in a logarithmic multiplet. A natural candidate for its logarithmic partner is
\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\equiv\hat{\sigma}(\hat{x})\partial_{i}\hat{\sigma}(\hat{x})

which carries the same conformal dimension and SO
(d-p)
spin as the displacement operator. (This defect local operator has already shown up in Section
2.6
.) Computing the relevant correlation functions, one finds

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,D_{i}(\hat{x})\widetilde{D}_{j}(0)\,\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\frac{p\,C_{d}^{2}\,{c}^{2}\delta_{ij}}{|\hat{x}|^{2p+2}}\,,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\widetilde{D}_{i}(\hat{x})\widetilde{D}_{j}(0)\,\rangle}

\displaystyle=p\,C_{d}^{2}\delta_{ij}\left(\frac{1}{|\hat{x}|^{2p+2}}+\frac{C_{d}c^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{\log\mu^{2}|\hat{x}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}|^{2p+2}}\right)\,,

(4.21)

where the second correlator has already been computed in Section
2.6
. These results, together with (
4.19
) and the general discussion in Section
3
, demonstrate that the displacement operator
D_{i}
and its partner
\widetilde{D}_{i}
organize into a rank-2 logarithmic multiplet.

Although in this paper we just focus on the free theory as an explicit example, the logarithmic nature of the displacement operator is expected to persist even in interacting theories, as long as the defect is generated by subdimensional disorder. For instance, it has been shown that in two-dimensional (interacting) CFTs deformed by disorder over the entire space, the energy-momentum tensor acquires the logarithmic structure
[
54
,
56
]
. Importantly, this logarithmic structure emerges in the replica limit
n\to 0
. As discussed in Section
2.3
, the replica trick can also be applied to subdimensional disorder, and it is therefore natural to expect that the displacement operator develops a similar logarithmic structure in the replica limit.

Whenever a bulk continuous global symmetry is spontaneously broken on the defect, there exist universal defect local operators associated with the broken charges. \displaystyle J_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}=\sigma_{\alpha}\partial^{\mu}\sigma_{\beta}-\partial^{\mu}\sigma_{\alpha}\,\sigma_{\beta}\,.

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}=\hat{h}(\hat{x})(n_{\beta}\sigma_{\alpha}-n_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta})\delta^{(d-p)}(x_{\perp})\,,

\displaystyle t_{\alpha\beta}(\hat{x})\equiv\hat{h}(\hat{x})(n_{\beta}\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}(\hat{x})-n_{\alpha}\hat{\sigma}_{\beta}(\hat{x}))\,.

\displaystyle t_{\hat{\alpha}}(\hat{x})=\hat{h}(\hat{x})\hat{\sigma}_{\hat{\alpha}}(\hat{x})\,,\quad\hat{\alpha}=1,\cdots,N-1\,.

Note that from the conservation equation, the conformal dimension of tilt operators is fixed to be

In a similar way to the displacement operator, one may then ask whether these tilt operators also participate in logarithmic multiplets. To address this question, we again study the defect two-point function, and the result is clearly

\tilde{t}_{\alpha}=\hat{\sigma}_{N}\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}
, and its relevant correlation functions can be easily computed

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\langle\,t_{\hat{\alpha}}(\hat{x})\tilde{t}_{\hat{\beta}}(0)\,\rangle}&=-\frac{C_{d}^{2}c^{2}\delta_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}}{|\hat{x}|^{2p}}\,,\\
\overline{\langle\,\tilde{t}_{\hat{\alpha}}(\hat{x})\tilde{t}_{\hat{\beta}}(0)\,\rangle}&=C_{d}^{2}\delta_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\left(\frac{1}{|\hat{x}|^{2p}}+\frac{C_{d}c^{2}}{2\pi}\frac{\log\mu^{2}|\hat{x}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}|^{2p}}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(4.28)

These results indicate that, after disorder averaging, the tilt operator
t_{\hat{\alpha}}
belongs to a rank-2 logarithmic multiplet together with its partner
\tilde{t}_{\hat{\alpha}}
.

Despite the simplicity of the explicit example presented above, we expect that these special defect multiplets with protected conformal dimensions to become logarithmic in general interacting CFTs with subdimensional disorder. It would be interesting to understand whether the rank-two property of such multiplets found here continue to hold in the interacting examples.

Are there ordinary CFT primaries?

One may wonder whether, after disorder averaging, there still exist ordinary CFT primaries on the defect, namely rank-1 operators. Indeed, such multiplets do exist. They are given by defect local operators with nonzero transverse spin
s
, which take the following form:
20
20

20

It turns out that only the primaries with transverse spin
s\geq 1
show up in the defect operator spectrum. See Section
4.3
and remark at the end.

\displaystyle\Psi_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{s}}(\hat{x})\equiv\partial_{(i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{s})}\hat{\sigma}(\hat{x})\,.

(4.29)

Its two-point function takes the following form:

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\Psi_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{s}}(\hat{x})\Psi^{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{s}}(0)\,\rangle}=\frac{2^{s}s!\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)_{\hskip-2.84526pts}C_{d}}{|\hat{x}|^{p+2s}}\,\mathcal{I}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\,.

(4.30)

Interestingly, these two-point functions show no dependence on the disorder strength
c
(i.e. same as without the defect).
This indicates that the disorder averaging procedure leaves untouched sectors with nonzero transverse spin quantum numbers. This observation is consistent with our earlier conclusion (
4.6
) from the DOE: channels with nonzero transverse spin can only accommodate ordinary CFT primaries, rather than logarithmic ones.
This is suggestive of a general feature for subdimensional disorder with scalar operators. We leave the investigation of this feature and generalization to disorder terms involving spinning operators to future studies.

4.2
Bulk-defect OPE data from Subdimensional Disorder

In the previous section, we have seen how various defect local operators in our free theory model organize themselves into logarithmic multiplets. In a defect CFT, besides the usual conformal dimensions and spins, the DOE coefficients
A_{J,0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}}
constitute another set of essential CFT data that characterize the defect. In this section, we focus on explicitly computing the bulk-defect OPE coefficients in our model, with the goal of gaining a deeper understanding of the structure of logarithmic defects.

To derive the DOE coefficients, we firstly need to calculate the bulk-defect two-point function, and obtain the structure constants
\lambda_{I}
in (
3.30
). Then, we can translate this into DOE coefficients
A_{J,0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}}
via the relation (
3.45
) with the coefficients
k_{I}
of defect two-point functions. In what follows, we illustrate how this program works by focusing on the bulk operator
\sigma
. From the discussion in the previous section, we know that the defect local primary operators appearing in the DOE of the bulk field
\sigma
are precisely the following set:
21
21

21

See also footnote
20
.

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}(\hat{x})\,,\quad\hat{h}(\hat{x})\,,\quad\Psi_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{s}}(\hat{x})\,.

(4.31)

Therefore, to determine the DOE coefficients
A_{J,0}^{N_{\widehat{\Delta},s}}
associated with
\sigma
, it suffices to compute the bulk-defect two-point functions between
\sigma
and these defect local operators. In the following, we carry out this analysis in order.

We firstly analyze the two-point function of
\sigma(x)
and
\hat{\sigma}(\hat{y})
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\langle\sigma(x)\hat{\sigma}(0)\rangle}&=\frac{C_{d}}{|x|^{p}}+c^{2}C_{d}^{2}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{p}|\hat{z}|^{p}}\,.\end{aligned}

(4.32)

Using the Fourier transformation (
E.1
) and (
E.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{p}|\hat{z}|^{p}}=\frac{\pi^{p/2}\left(\psi\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)-\gamma_{\rm E}+\log 4\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)|x|^{p}}-\frac{4\pi^{p+1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{2}}\mathcal{I}_{1}\,,

(4.33)

where
\gamma_{\rm E}
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant and
\mathcal{I}_{1}
is the following

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{1}=\int\frac{\text{d}^{p}\hat{p}}{(2\pi)^{p+1}}K_{0}(|\hat{p}||x_{\perp}|)\log|\hat{p}|^{2}e^{\text{i}\hat{p}\cdot\hat{x}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{2\pi^{\frac{p}{2}+1}|x|^{p}}\left(\tau+\log 2+\psi\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(4.34)

Here,
K_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind, and

is defined in (

). We give the detailed derivation of this integral formula in Appendix

. Then, the two-point function is simplified to the following desired form:

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\sigma(x)\hat{\sigma}(0)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}\tau}{|x|^{p}}\,,

(4.35)

where
\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{1}=C_{d}\left(1-{c^{2}\over 4\pi}H_{\frac{p}{2}-1}\right)\,,\quad\tilde{\lambda}_{2}=-C_{d}{c^{2}\over 2\pi}\,,

(4.36)

where
H_{z}\equiv\psi(z+1)+\gamma_{\rm E}
is the harmonic number continued to the complex plane and
C_{d}
is defined in (
2.11
).
On the other hand, the two-point function of
\sigma
and
\hat{h}
can be easily evaluated:

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\sigma(x){\hat{h}}(0)\rangle}=-\frac{c^{2}C_{d}}{|x|^{p}}\,.

(4.37)

At this stage, it is convenient to switch to a specific basis (
4.12
) in which the two-point functions take a simpler form.
In this basis, the coefficients
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
appearing in bulk-defect two-point functions are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{C_{d}}{2}\left(1-\frac{c^{2}}{2\pi}H_{\frac{p}{2}-1}\right)\,,\quad\lambda_{2}=-\frac{C_{d}\,c^{2}}{2\pi}=k_{\{\hat{\sigma},\hat{h}\}}\,.

(4.38)

Then, the relation (
3.45
) and the defect two-point functions (
4.12
) can translate
\lambda_{I}
into the OE coefficients

(see Section

\displaystyle A_{1,0}^{N_{p/2,0}}=1\,,\quad A_{2,0}^{N_{p/2,0}}=-\frac{\pi}{c^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\frac{p}{2}-1}\,.

(4.39)

Recall that the first subscript
J
of

labels the primary operators in the logarithmic multiplet that enter in the DOE of rank

and

indexes the descendants (with

corresponding to the primaries).

In contrast, for the defect spinning operator
\Psi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}
discussed in the previous section, the situation is simpler: since it behaves as an ordinary CFT primary, the non-vanishing DOE coefficients are

\displaystyle A^{N_{s+p/2,s}}_{1,0}={1\over s!}\,.

(4.40)

We have thus completely fixed the logDOE coefficients of the bulk operator
\sigma
in the presence of subdimensional disorder.

4.3
Bulk Two-point Function and Deformation from Disorder

Finally, we analyze the bulk two-point function
\sigma
to illustrate the general discussion in Section
3.4
.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\langle\,\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\rangle}&=\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{p}}+{c}^{2}C_{d}^{2}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\,\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{p}|y-\hat{z}|^{p}}\\
&=\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{p}}+\frac{16\pi^{p+2}}{\Gamma(p/2)^{2}}{c}^{2}C_{d}^{2}\,{\cal I}_{2}\,,\end{aligned}

(4.41)

where
{\cal I}_{2}
is the momentum-space integral defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\cal I}_{2}=\int\frac{\text{d}^{p}\hat{p}}{(2\pi)^{p+2}}K_{0}(|\hat{p}||x_{\perp}|)K_{0}(|\hat{p}||y_{\perp}|)e^{\text{i}\hat{p}\cdot(\hat{x}-\hat{y})}\,,\end{aligned}

(4.42)

which follows from the Fourier transformation (
E.2
). In the following, we will unpack the conformal block decomposition of (
4.41
) which includes logarithmic contributions.

The first term on the RHS of (
4.41
) is obviously the two-point function with a trivial defect and admits the following conformal block decomposition (as in ordinary defect CFT),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x-y|^{p}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p/2}|y_{\perp}|^{p/2}}\left[\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p}{4},\frac{p+2}{4};1;\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\right)\right.

(4.43)

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(\frac{p}{2})_{s}}{s!}\cos(s\phi){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p+2s}{4},\frac{p+2s+2}{4};s+1;\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\right)\right]\,,

where
\chi
is the cross-ratio in (
3.66
).
Compared with the general discussion in Section
3.4
for logarithmic defects, the first term in the square bracket corresponds to (a part of) the defect conformal block with
(\widehat{\Delta},s)=(p/2,0)
, while the second term corresponds to the spinning defect conformal block with
(\widehat{\Delta},s)=(p/2+s,s)
with
s\geq 1
.
Using the notation of (
3.70
) and (
3.73
), this tree-level part of (
4.41
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{p}}=\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p/2}|y_{\perp}|^{p/2}}\left(C_{d}\mathsf{G}_{\{\hat{\sigma},\hat{h}\}}(\chi)+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}H_{\Psi_{s}}^{N_{p/2+s,s}=1}(\phi)G_{\Psi_{s}}^{N_{p/2+s,s}=1}(\chi)\right)\,.

(4.44)

Next, we turn to the analysis of the second term on the RHS of (
4.41
), which encodes the effects of the subdimensional disorder. To proceed, one needs to evaluate the integral in (
4.42
). We defer the details to the Appendix
E.2
and only present the final result here,

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{2}=-\frac{\Gamma(p/2)}{8\pi^{\frac{p}{2}+2}}\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p/2}|y_{\perp}|^{p/2}}\left[H_{\frac{p}{2}-1}\mathsf{G}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(\chi)+\left.\partial_{\widehat{\Delta}}\mathsf{G}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(\chi)\right]\right|_{\widehat{\Delta}=p/2}\,,

(4.45)

where
\mathsf{G}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(\chi)
is given by (
3.73
). Combined this with the tree-level part (
4.44
), the full bulk two-point function can be written as,

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\rangle}=\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p/2}|y_{\perp}|^{p/2}}\left(G_{\{\hat{\sigma},\hat{h}\}}(\chi)+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}H_{\Psi_{s}}^{N_{p/2+s,s}=1}(\phi)G_{\Psi_{s}}^{N_{p/2+s,s}=1}(\chi)\right)\,,

(4.46)

where we used (
3.74
), (
3.78
) and (
4.38
). As desired, the resulting expression matches the structure of the defect conformal block expansion developed in Section
3.4
. This expression also makes it clear that the disorder dependence (one-parameter family parametrized by the disorder strength
c
) is concentrated in the scalar defect
channel by comparing to (
4.3
).

Here we make a brief comment regarding the spectrum of defect spinning operators found from the above analysis. In Section
4.1
, when we impose constraints on the defect operator spectrum associated with bulk operator
\sigma
using the equation of motion, the spinning operators with
\widehat{\Delta}=p/2-|s|
are a priori not excluded. This is because unitarity is lost in general on the disordered defect. However, the present analysis of the bulk two-point function makes it clear that such operators do not actually appear in the spectrum. This observation is consistent with the remark made at the end of Section
4.1
, namely that the subdimensional disorder (by a local scalar deformation) does not affect the operators with nonzero transverse spin quantum numbers.

4.4
Bulk One-point Functions and Crossing

In the previous section, we have explicitly evaluated the bulk two-point function and verified that it takes the form consistent with the defect conformal block decomposition. In this section, we revisit the same two-point function from the viewpoint of the bulk conformal block expansion (see around (
3.84
)).

The bulk OPE of two identical free scalar operators
\sigma
takes the schematic form

\displaystyle\sigma\times\sigma\to\mathbf{1}+\sigma^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2\ell}}\,,

(4.47)

where
\Phi_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2\ell}}(y)
is bulk local primary operator with even spin
2\ell
(
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
),

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2\ell}}\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell}c_{2\ell,k}\,\partial_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\mu_{k}}\sigma\cdot\partial_{\mu_{k+1}}\cdots\partial_{\mu_{2\ell}}\sigma\,.

(4.48)

with coefficients given in
[
108
]
,

\displaystyle c_{2\ell,k}=\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+2\ell)\Gamma(2\ell+p-1)}{\Gamma(\frac{p-1}{2})}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!\,(2\ell-k)!\,\Gamma(k+\frac{p}{2})\Gamma(2\ell-k+\frac{p}{2})}\,.

(4.49)

They correspond to the higher even-spin conserved currents in the free theory.

In the following, we will show that only the identity operator and the scalar operator
\sigma^{2}
in (
4.47
) contribute in the bulk-channel, by directly evaluating the relevant one-point functions in the presence of the subdimensional disorder by Wick contractions and taking into account normal ordering. We will find the result to be consistent with the other form (
4.41
) of the two-point function derived before.

For the scalar
\sigma^{2}
, one finds,

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\sigma^{2}(x)\rangle}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{\Gamma(p)}C^{2}_{d}c^{2}\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p}}\,.

(4.50)

For the higher spin currents
\Phi_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2\ell}}
, it is convenient to introduce a null polarization vector
\zeta
(i.e.
\zeta\cdot\zeta=0
) and define,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2\ell}(x,\zeta)\equiv\zeta^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\zeta^{\mu_{2\ell}}\Phi_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2\ell}}(x)\,.

(4.51)

Then the bulk one-point function of
\Phi_{2\ell}(x,\zeta)
can be evaluated as follows,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \overline{\langle\Phi_{2\ell}(x,\zeta)\rangle}&=C_{d}\,c^{2}\lim_{y\to x}\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell}c_{2\ell,k}(\zeta\cdot\partial_{x})^{2\ell-k}(\zeta\cdot\partial_{y})^{k}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{p}|y-\hat{z}|^{p}}\\
&=2^{2\ell}C_{d}c^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell}c_{2\ell,k}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)_{2\ell-k}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\frac{(\zeta\cdot(\hat{z}-x))^{2\ell}}{|x-\hat{z}|^{2p+2\ell}}\\
&=0\,,\end{aligned}

(4.52)

where in the final line, we have used the following identity satisfied by (
4.49
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell}c_{2\ell,k}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)_{2\ell-k}=0\,.

(4.53)

The resulting bulk-channel conformal block decomposition of the scalar two-point function
follows from the general discussion around (
3.80
) (see notations thereof),

\displaystyle F(X,\overline{X})=C_{d}\xi^{-p/2}+\frac{\pi^{\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{\Gamma(p)}C^{2}_{d}c^{2}(X\overline{X})^{p/4}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p}{2},\frac{p}{2};p;1-X\overline{X}\right)\,.

(4.54)

As a consistency check, we also see the second term on the RHS reproduces the integral
{\cal I}_{2}
in (
4.42
) (see Appendix
F
for details).

The above result should be contrasted with what happens in ordinary DCFT, where typically an infinite tower of spinning operators (e.g. double-twist operators) contribute in the bulk-channel conformal block decompositions.
In particular, the one-point function of the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
generally acquires a nontrivial value
[
50
,
52
]
. However, as demonstrated above, in our free scalar theory with subdimensional disorder, averaged bulk one-point functions of the spinning operator s
\Phi_{2J}
all vanish, in particular

\displaystyle\overline{\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x)\rangle}=0\,.

(4.55)

In other words, even though the defect is non-topological, the one-point function of the energy-momentum tensor is zero!
22
22

22

Another indication of the quasi-topological nature of the defect arises from the correlators of the displacement operators
D_{i}
, which vanish except for contact terms (see (
4.19
)).
A similar phenomenon occurs for higher-spin symmetries. The corresponding Ward identities take the form

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{\mu i_{1}\dots i_{2J-1}}(x)=c_{2J}{\hat{h}}(\hat{x})\partial^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial^{i_{2J-1}}\sigma(x)\delta^{(d-p)}(x_{\perp}),,

(4.56)

where

in (

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{2}=\frac{1}{(8\pi\text{i})^{2}}\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}s\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}t\frac{2^{s+t}}{|x_{\perp}|^{s}|y_{\perp}|^{t}}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\int\frac{\text{d}^{p}\hat{p}}{(2\pi)^{p+2}}\frac{e^{\text{i}\hat{p}\cdot(\hat{x}-\hat{y})}}{|\hat{p}|^{s+t}}\,.

(E.11)

Firstly, we perform the integral over
\hat{p}
using (
E.5
) and the following formula,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\rho\rho^{\mu}J_{\nu}(a\rho)=\frac{2^{\mu}}{a^{\mu+1}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1+\mu+\nu}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1+\nu-\mu}{2})}\,,

(E.12)

and the result is

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{2}=-\frac{1}{256\pi^{p/2+4}}\frac{1}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{p}}\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}s\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}t\,\xi_{1}^{-s}\xi_{2}^{-t}\,\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\Gamma(\frac{p-s-t}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{s+t}{2})}\,.

(E.13)

Here,
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
are the cross-ratios defined by

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\equiv\frac{|x_{\perp}|}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|}\,,\quad\xi_{2}\equiv\frac{|y_{\perp}|}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|}\,.

(E.14)

We are interested in the defect block expansion, namely with
0<\xi_{i}<1\,(i=1,2)
.

We next evaluate the following integral,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal I}}_{2}\equiv\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}s\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}t\,\xi_{1}^{-s}\xi_{2}^{-t}\,\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\Gamma(\frac{p-s-t}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{s+t}{2})}\,,

(E.15)

by deforming the contour to the left and using the residue theorem. As in Figure
5
, this picks up the poles at
t=0,-2,-4,\cdots
and gives,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{-\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}^{\text{i}\infty+\epsilon}\text{d}t\,\xi_{2}^{-t}\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\Gamma(\frac{p-s-t}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{s+t}{2})}\\
&\ =4\pi\text{i}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{2}^{2n}\frac{\Gamma(n+\frac{p-s}{2})}{(n!)^{2}\Gamma(-n+\frac{s}{2})}\left(2\psi(n+1)-\psi\left(n+\frac{p-s}{2}\right)-\psi\left(-n+\frac{s}{2}\right)-2\log\xi_{2}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(E.16)

We next deform the contour associated to
s
-integral to the left, and pick up the poles at
s=0,-2,-4,\cdots
. This enables us to express the integral
\widetilde{{\cal I}}_{2}
as a double sum,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{{\cal I}}_{2}=-32\pi^{2}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\xi_{1}^{2})^{m}(-\xi_{2}^{2})^{n}}{(m+n)!}\binom{m+n}{m}^{2}\Gamma\left(m+n+\frac{p}{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\times\left(\psi(n+1)+\psi(m+1)-\psi(m+n+1)-\psi\left(m+n+\frac{p}{2}\right)-\log\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(E.17)

In summary so far, we obtain the following expression of
{\cal I}_{2}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{\frac{p}{2}+2}}\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p/2}|y_{\perp}|^{p/2}}\left[{\cal K}_{1}-{\cal K}_{2}-\log(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}){\cal K}_{3}+\log\chi\,{\cal K}_{3}\right]\,,\end{aligned}

(E.18)

where
{\cal K}_{1}
,
{\cal K}_{2}
and
{\cal K}_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\cal K}_{1}&\equiv\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\xi_{1}^{2})^{m}(-\xi_{2}^{2})^{n}}{(m+n)!}\binom{m+n}{m}^{2}\Gamma\left(m+n+\frac{p}{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\cdot\left(\psi(n+1)+\psi(m+1)-\psi(m+n+1)\right)\,,\\
{\cal K}_{2}&\equiv\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\xi_{1}^{2})^{m}(-\xi_{2}^{2})^{n}}{(m+n)!}\binom{m+n}{m}^{2}\Gamma\left(m+n+\frac{p}{2}\right)\psi\left(m+n+\frac{p}{2}\right)\,,\\
{\cal K}_{3}&\equiv\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\xi_{1}^{2})^{m}(-\xi_{2}^{2})^{n}}{(m+n)!}\binom{m+n}{m}^{2}\Gamma\left(m+n+\frac{p}{2}\right)\end{aligned}

(E.19)

Note that
\chi
is written in terms of
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
as

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}\,.

(E.20)

Introducing a new quantity
{\cal K}(\Delta)
as

\displaystyle{\cal K}(\Delta)\equiv\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\xi_{1}^{2})^{m}(-\xi_{2}^{2})^{n}}{(m+n)!}\binom{m+n}{m}^{2}\Gamma\left(m+n+\Delta\right)\,,

(E.21)

and the double sums
{\cal K}_{2}
and
{\cal K}_{3}
can be written in terms of
{\cal K}
as

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}=\partial_{\Delta}{\cal K}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\,,\quad{\cal K}_{3}={\cal K}\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\,.

(E.22)

This motivate us to firstly evaluate
{\cal K}(\Delta)
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\cal K}(\widehat{\Delta})&=\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+\widehat{\Delta})(\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2})^{\ell}}{\ell!}P_{\ell}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}{\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}}\right)\\
&=\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}t\,t^{\widehat{\Delta}-1}e^{-(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})t}I_{0}(2\xi_{1}\xi_{2}t)\\
&=\Gamma\left(\widehat{\Delta}\right)(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}-\widehat{\Delta}}\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\widehat{\Delta}}{2},\frac{\widehat{\Delta}+1}{2};1;\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(E.23)

From this, one can derive
{\cal K}_{2}
and
{\cal K}_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\cal K}_{2}&=\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\left[\left(\psi\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)-\log(1+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2})\right)\mathsf{G}_{\{\hat{\sigma},\hat{h}\}}(\chi)+\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}\partial_{\widehat{\Delta}}\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\widehat{\Delta}}{2},\frac{\widehat{\Delta}+1}{2};1;\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\right)\right|_{\widehat{\Delta}=p/2}\right]\\
{\cal K}_{3}&=\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p}{4},\frac{p+2}{4};1;\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\right)=\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\mathsf{G}_{\{\hat{\sigma},\hat{h}\}}(\chi)\,.\end{aligned}

(E.24)

By plugging these results into (
E.19
), one can obtain

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{2}=-\frac{\Gamma(p/2)}{8\pi^{\frac{p}{2}+2}}\frac{1}{|x_{\perp}|^{p/2}|y_{\perp}|^{p/2}}\left[H_{\frac{p}{2}-1}\mathsf{G}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(\chi)+\left.\partial_{\widehat{\Delta}}\mathsf{G}_{\widehat{\Delta}}(\chi)\right|_{\widehat{\Delta}=p/2}-{\cal R}\right]\,,

(E.25)

where
{\cal R}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(p/2)}{\cal K}_{1}+\gamma_{\text{E}}\mathsf{G}_{\{\hat{\sigma},\hat{h}\}}(\chi)+\chi^{-\frac{p}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p}{4},\frac{p+2}{4};a;\frac{4}{\chi^{2}}\right)\right|_{a=1}\,.

(E.26)

By expanding
\mathcal{R}
in powers of
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
and examining the coefficients, one finds that
\mathcal{R}=0
. The desired integral formula (
4.45
) then follows.

Appendix F
Bulk Conformal Block Expansion in Free Scalar Theory

In this Appendix, we derive the bulk-channel conformal block expansion (
4.54
) by directly evaluating the integral
{\cal I}_{2}
that appears in the bulk two-point function (
4.41
). In fact we will solve the following more general integral,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha,\beta}\equiv\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\,\frac{1}{|x-\hat{z}|^{2\alpha}|y-\hat{z}|^{2\beta}}\,,

(F.1)

which may be useful for more general DCFT setups.
Later we will specialize to the case
{\alpha}={\beta}={p\over 2}
, which is relevant for (
4.41
) that describes the free scalar theory with subdimensional disorder.

By using the Schwinger reparameterization, the integral (
F.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{\alpha,\beta}&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int\text{d}^{p}\hat{z}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}u\,u^{\alpha-1}e^{-u|x-\hat{z}|^{2}}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}v\,v^{\beta-1}e^{-v|y-\hat{z}|^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\pi^{p/2}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}u\text{d}v\,u^{\alpha-1}\,v^{\beta-1}\,(u+v)^{-p/2}e^{-\frac{uv}{u+v}|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}e^{-u|x_{\perp}|^{2}-v|y_{\perp}|^{2}}\,,\end{aligned}

(F.2)

where, in the final equality, we have performed the integral over
\hat{z}
. Next we expand the last exponential in terms of the power of
u
and
v
,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{p/2}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-|x_{\perp}|^{2})^{n}(-|y_{\perp}|^{2})^{m}}{n!\,m!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}u\text{d}v\,u^{n+\alpha-1}\,v^{m+\beta-1}\,(u+v)^{-p/2}e^{-\frac{uv}{u+v}|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}\,.

(F.3)

By changing the integral variables as

\displaystyle u=y\,t\,,\qquad v=y\,(1-t)\,,

(F.4)

under which the integration measure turns into

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}u\text{d}v\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}\,y\,\text{d}y\,\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,,

(F.5)

the integral becomes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{\alpha,\beta}&=\frac{\pi^{p/2}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-|x_{\perp}|^{2})^{n}(-|y_{\perp}|^{2})^{m}}{n!\,m!}\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{n+\alpha-1}(1-t)^{m+\beta-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{n+m+\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2}-1}e^{-yt(1-t)|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}\,.\end{aligned}

(F.6)

After another change of variable by,

\displaystyle y=\frac{\tilde{y}}{t(1-t)|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}\,,

(F.7)

the above integral can be written as,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{\alpha,\beta}&=\frac{\pi^{p/2}F_{\alpha,\beta}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2\alpha+2\beta-p}}\,,\end{aligned}

(F.8)

where
F_{\alpha,\beta}
is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F_{\alpha,\beta}&\equiv\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2})}{n!\,m!}A^{n}\,B^{m}\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{\frac{p}{2}-\beta-m-1}(1-t)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-n-1}\,,\\
A&\equiv-\frac{|x_{\perp}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}\,,\quad B\equiv-\frac{|y_{\perp}|^{2}}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}\,.\end{aligned}

(F.9)

We can evaluate
F_{\alpha,\beta}
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F_{\alpha,\beta}&\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(k+\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2})}{n!\,(k-n)!}A^{n}\,B^{k-n}\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{\frac{p}{2}-\beta-k+n-1}(1-t)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-n-1}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(k+\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2}\right)}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{\frac{p}{2}-\beta-k-1}(1-t)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-1}\left(B+\frac{At}{1-t}\right)^{k}\\
&=\Gamma\left(\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{\frac{p}{2}-\beta-1}(1-t)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-1}\left(1-\frac{A}{1-t}-\frac{B}{t}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-\beta}\\
&=\Gamma\left(\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2}\right)(-B)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1}(1-tx^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-\beta}(1-ty^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-\beta}\,,\end{aligned}

(F.10)

where
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
satisfy

\displaystyle x^{\prime}y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{B}\,,\quad x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}=-\frac{A-B-1}{B}\,.

(F.11)

Then, by using the following integral formula
[
52
, eq.(B.22)]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\text{d}t\,t^{w-1}(1-t)^{r-1}(1-tx^{\prime})^{-\rho}(1-ty^{\prime})^{-\sigma}=B(w,r)F_{1}(w,\rho,\sigma,w+r;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\,,

(F.12)

where
F_{1}
is the Appel function,
F_{\alpha,\beta}
can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F_{\alpha,\beta}=B\left(\alpha,\beta\right)\Gamma\left(\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2}\right)(-B)^{\frac{p}{2}-\alpha-\beta}F_{1}\left(\alpha,\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2},\alpha+\beta-\frac{p}{2},\alpha+\beta;x^{\prime};y^{\prime}\right)\,.\end{aligned}

(F.13)

Particularly, when
w+r=\rho+\sigma
, the Appel function is reduced to the hypergeometric function via the following identity,

\displaystyle F_{1}(w,\rho,\sigma,\rho+\sigma;x^{\prime};y^{\prime})=(1-y^{\prime})^{w}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(w,\rho,\rho+\sigma;\frac{x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}}{1-y^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(F.14)

In our case,
\alpha=\beta=p/2
, so
F_{\alpha,\beta}
can be simplified to

\displaystyle F_{p/2,p/2}=B\left(\frac{p}{2},\frac{p}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-y^{\prime}}{-B}\right)^{p/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p}{2},\frac{p}{2},p;\frac{x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}}{1-y^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(F.15)

To sum up, the bulk two-point function of
\sigma
is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\,\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{d}}{|x-y|^{p}}+\frac{\pi^{\frac{p}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{p}{2})}{\Gamma(p)}C_{d}^{2}{c}^{2}\frac{1}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{p}}\left(\frac{1-y^{\prime}}{-B}\right)^{p/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{p}{2},\frac{p}{2},p;\frac{x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}}{1-y^{\prime}}\right)\,.

(F.16)

Finally, by using the following identities,

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{\perp}||y_{\perp}|}{|\hat{x}-\hat{y}|^{2}}\left(\frac{1-y^{\prime}}{-B}\right)=(X\overline{X})^{1/2}\,,\quad\frac{x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}}{1-y^{\prime}}=1-X\overline{X}\,,

(F.17)

one can show that the expression (
F.16
) perfectly matches the desired form (
4.54
) from the bulk-channel conformal block expansion.

Poster 44. Abnormal Patellofemoral Morphology is associated with Anterior Knee Pain Two Years After BTB ACL Reconstruction
Authors: Naofumi Hashiguchi, Sean Hazzard, Peter Asnis, Miho J. Tanaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Anterior knee pain (AKP) is a recognized complication after anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction, with unclear etiology. This study aimed to evaluate whether preoperative patellofemoral morphology predicts AKP two years after ACL reconstruction using bone-patellar tendon-bone (BTB) autografts.Methods:We retrospectively reviewed patients who underwent primary BTB ACL reconstruction between 2016 and 2020. AKP was assessed at two years postoperatively using Kujala score items 'squatting' and 'prolonged sitting with knees flexed.' Patients were categorized as symptomatic or asymptomatic for each activity. Preoperative MRI measurements included patellar height (Caton-Deschamps Index [CDI], Insall-Salvati Index [ISI], Patella Trochlear Index [PTI]), trochlear dysplasia (bony and cartilaginous sulcus angles), malalignment (tibial tubercle-trochlear groove [TTTG] and tibial tubercle-posterior cruciate ligament [TTPCL] distances), and patellar tilt. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis were entered into multivariate logistic regression adjusted for age.Results:Sixty-five patients were included. For squatting, 10.8% reported pain; for prolonged sitting, 9.2% reported pain. Symptomatic squatting was associated with greater bony sulcus angle, indicating increased trochlear dysplasia (141.1° vs 135.8°, p = 0.04). Pain with prolonged sitting was associated with higher ISI, indicating greater patella alta (p = 0.03). In multivariate analysis, no predictors reached statistical significance. Trends suggested greater trochlear dysplasia may relate to squatting pain (OR 1.1 per 1° increase, p = 0.07), while higher patellar height and younger age may relate to prolonged sitting pain. Model discrimination showed AUC 0.74 for squatting and 0.84 for prolonged sitting.Conclusions:Preoperative trochlear dysplasia and patella alta may contribute to persistent AKP two years after BTB ACL reconstruction, particularly during squatting and prolonged sitting. Although associations did not reach statistical significance, these findings underscore the value of preoperative morphological assessment and may inform individualized rehabilitation strategies and graft selection. Prospective studies are warranted.

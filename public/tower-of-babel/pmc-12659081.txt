Single-molecule sensing with an anthrax nanopore enabled by pH-asymmetric ionic liquids
Authors: Zhirui Zhang, Yan Wang, Shuo Zhou, Yunjiao Wang, Shilong Liu, Xiyun Guan, Liang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The anthrax protective antigen self-assembles into a heptameric transmembrane channel under acidic conditions, exhibiting characteristic biological nanopore properties. However, the anthrax channel faces significant challenges in single-molecule nanopore sensing due to inherent instability issues such as sudden current attenuation, frequent channel occlusion and noise interference, primarily mediated by phenylalanine clamps within the channel lumen. To address these limitations on nanopore sensing, we designed four electrolyte systems (KCl, NaCl, LiCl, and 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride [BMIM]Cl) with asymmetric ionic strength and pH condition to stabilize the anthrax nanopore platform. While traditional alkali metal chlorides (KCl, NaCl, LiCl) showed partial improvement in electrical properties, the [BMIM]Cl system with pH asymmetry enabled a stable nanopore with optimal electrical characteristics, including diminished channel occlusion, enhanced signal-to-noise ratio, steady discrete constant and current strength of open-pore, all indicative of uniform ion transport. Furthermore, the stabilized anthrax nanopore demonstrated exceptional molecular detection capability, resolving single-molecule discrimination of homologous peptides of MAPKK gene families which are key signaling proteins involved in the regulation of various biology processes in eukaryotic organisms. Introduction

With the rapid advancement of nanopore-based sensing technology [1–7], the commonly used several nanopores (less than 10 protein nanopores) cannot meet the strong demands of various research fields. There are increasing demands on exploring novel nanopore systems. In addition, it is urgently necessary to improve structural stability and detection resolution of constructed nanopores to meet the needs of different application scenarios. In the molecular mechanism of anthrax toxicity, the acidic environment (pH 5.6) induces a conformational change in anthrax protective antigen that allows it to ensemble into a homo heptameric membrane-spanning β-barrel pore. The formed transmembrane channel exhibits characteristic biological nanopore features for critical transmembrane cargo transport, mediating the intermolecular translocation of toxic enzyme components like lethal factor and edema factor, thereby causing severe mammalian cell death [8]– [9]. In the structure aspect of the anthrax channel, it exhibits a sophisticated architecture with distinct structural domains that consists of a cis-opening mouth (diameter ~ 30 Å), the constrictive Φ-clamp (~ 6 Å), a throat region (~ 18 Å), and an elongated β-barrel stem (trans opening mouth: diameter 27 Å), which collectively enable molecular translocation [10]. This hierarchical geometry should provide multiple recognition interfaces that is likely to regulate ion flows and facilitate molecular interactions at single-molecule resolution. Moreover, anthrax nanopore is manifested as small single channel open current (~ 5 pA), and if the molecules could be efficiently detected and distinguished, it may offer more opportunities for biosensing applications with minor current modulation changes.

However, despite the great progress made in the past few decades in elucidating the toxic molecular mechanisms, the anthrax channel still faces significant challenges in single-molecule nanopore sensing due to inherent instability issues primarily mediated by phenylalanine clamps within the channel lumen [11], including sudden current attenuation, frequent channel occlusion (i.e., the “gating effect”) and noise interference [12, 13]. These limitations hinder the formation of stable single-channel currents, especially restricting its sensing application under traditional K+/Na+ electrolyte conditions of high concentrations (Fig. 1a). To overcome these limitations, a proof-of-concept study demonstrates a robust deep learning framework for nanopore sensing using simulated data with the anthrax toxin protective antigen [14]. Here, we systematically investigated the effect of four electrolyte systems (KCl, NaCl, LiCl, and 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride [BMIM]Cl) on the performance of an anthrax nanopore based on the anthrax protective antigen. Based on the similar mechanism of self-assemble anthrax protective antigen channel under acidic environment, we established an asymmetric pH gradient to allow the formation of functional anthrax nanopores by leveraging the proton-driven properties of the channel (Fig. 1b) [8, 15]. Subsequently, we measured a series of electrical properties upon nanopore formation. The results revealed that while traditional alkali metal chlorides (KCl, NaCl, LiCl) only partially improved electrical performance, the [BMIM]Cl system under asymmetric pH conditions enabled formation of a stable anthrax nanopore with optimal electrical characteristics and improved chemical stability. Despite the use of organic ionic liquids shift the nanopore sensing mechanism into more complex physicochemical regime, it is known that ionic liquids are a powerful reagents for enhancing the electrochemical performance and chemical stability of nanopore systems, their high thermal stability and low vapor pressure under high voltages enable suppression of protein nanopore degradation and alteration to a higher viscosity in aqueous solutions, thereby directly improving key sensing parameters of anthrax nanopore: altered translocation dynamics (steady discrete constant, σ), suppressed pore occlusion, enhanced signal-to-noise ratio, sensing resolution and pore longevity [16–20]. Due to the clearly discernible current events observed during ion/molecule translocation, the optimized anthrax nanopore exhibited exceptional molecular detection capability, resolving discrimination of homologous peptides of MAPKK gene families which are key signaling kinases involved in the regulation of various biology processes in eukaryotic organisms. This study not only achieves stable single-channel recording with anthrax nanopores but also paves the way for their application in high-precision small-molecule detection, representing a significant advancement in nanopore sensing technology.

Experiment section

Materials and reagents

All chemicals were procured from Sigma-Aldrich. The 0.22 μm filter were purchased from Shanghai Titan Scientific (China). The anthrax protein and homologous peptides were ordered from List Biological Laboratories, Inc (Listlabs, US).

Electrolyte solution Preparation

NaCl, KCl, LiCl, 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride ([BMIM]Cl) were dissolved in Milli-Q water (18.2 MΩ cm) to form an electrolyte solution at 0.5 M/1 M, pH 5.6 or pH 7.6, both containing 10 mM Tris.

Nanopore detection and data analysis

Before constructing the nanopore, a 150 μm hole was created on the Teflon membrane using a universal spark generator universal spark generator (Daedalon, SC-EM-09), and the Teflon membrane was fixed between two customized chambers to construct cis and trans reservoir. A mixture of hexadecane and pentane (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) was applied to make the hole hydrophobic for construction of lipid bilayers (1,2-diphytanoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine, Avanti Polar Lipids, Alabaster, USA). For the pH - asymmetric electrolyte measurement, 2 mL of 0.5 M [BMIM]Cl, 10 mM Tris, and pH 5.6 electrolyte solution was added to the cis side, and 2 mL of 0.5 M [BMIM]Cl, 10 mM Tris, and pH 7.6 electrolyte solution was added to the trans side. The Ag/AgCl electrodes were used for nanopore recording, with the ground electrode connected to the cis side and the working electrode connected to the trans side, stabilized in an electric-shields. To create a single nanopore, anthrax protective antigen was dissolved in glycerol and buffer (0.5 M [BMIM]Cl 10 mM Tris pH 5.6), and added to the cis side to form a single nanopore channel. The samples were added to the cis chamber for nanopore detection. Ion current recordings were performed using a patch-clamp amplifier (Axopatch 200B, Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, America) with a Digidata 1550B A/D converter. The signals were filtered at 2 kHz, recorded at a sampling rate of 100 kHz using Clampfit software (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, America), and analyzed with OriginLab 2021 (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, America). Unless otherwise noted, all nanopore experiments were conducted at room temperature from − 100 mV to 100 mV for at least three times. Current signal parameters, including amplitude (ΔI) and dwell time (τoff), were quantitatively analyzed using Gaussian distribution and exponential decay models implemented in Clampfit software (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, USA). For statistical validation, independent sample t-tests were performed on current blocking percentages using OriginPro software (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, USA), with calculated p-values demonstrating the significance of these observed differences. Error bars throughout the study represent the standard deviation (S.D.) obtained from a minimum of three independent experimental replicates.

Results and discussion

Anthrax nanopore electrical properties mediated by alkali metal chlorides (K+, Na+, Li+)

To systematically investigate the effects of electrolyte concentration and pH strengths on anthrax nanopore formation, we constructed four electrolytes’ systems using anthrax protective antigen under an applied voltage of −100 mV, generating a measurable ionic current that reflects the nanopore’s permeability and stability (Fig. 1b). System i (symmetric concentration and pH: 0.5 M KCl, pH 7.6), System ii (asymmetric concentration symmetric pH: 1 M cis/0.5 M trans, pH 7.6), System iii (asymmetric concentration and pH: 1 M cis/0.5 M trans, pH 5.6/7.6), and System iv (symmetric concentration asymmetric pH: 0.5 M KCl, pH 5.6/7.6).

In nanopore sensing, KCl, NaCl and LiCl are usually selected as the electrolyte for the electrical detection. The choice of one over the other may be dictated, for instance, by analytes whose physico-chemical properties and behavior do depend heavily on the choice of the salt. KCl was chosen as the primary electrolyte for ion transport-mediated nanopore detection. As previously mentioned, the open-pore current Io2 serves as a reliable indicator of ion flux through the anthrax nanopore, whereas Io1 was excluded from analysis due to its occlusion behavior. Among the tested systems, System i exhibited the highest Io2 at − 3.48 pA (Fig. 2a), while System ii showed a reduced Io2 of − 2.81 pA. Notably, Systems iii and iv (pH-asymmetric conditions) displayed nearly identical Io2 values (− 2.97pA and − 2.86 pA). The observed reduction in Io2 is likely due to altered ionic mobility near the pore constriction by difference of ionic strengths across the nanopore. This finding aligns with previous research that proton-driven allosteric clamps catalyze anthrax molecules translocation across membranes [8].

To further evaluate the electrical properties of the nanopore under different electrolyte conditions, we introduced the discrete constant σ. It can be used to compare the degree of discretization of different salts for nanopore electrical performance (Fig. 2b), particularly for the stability investigation [16, 21]. Therefore, a large σ indicates the larger degree of dispersion of nanopore current (unstable with strong current fluctuation); further implies stronger interactions between ions and water molecules in the nanopore. A lower σ value corresponds to a more uniform open-pore current distribution, reflecting stable ion transport and enhanced nanopore stability. Systems i and ii exhibited relatively high σ values of 33.45% and 31.42%, respectively. In contrast, Systems iii and iv (pH-asymmetric conditions) demonstrated significantly improved nanopore stability with lower σ values of 25.31% and 25.22%, respectively. These results clearly indicate that pH gradients promote a more stable ion transport regime in the anthrax nanopore. This enhanced stability was further corroborated by smooth current-voltage (I-V) characteristics in System iii (pH-asymmetric), which displayed low-noise current trajectories compared to the highly-frequent fluctuations and noise interference observed in other systems (Fig. 2c and Fig. S1). However, current attenuation could significantly hinder its sensing applications in all K⁺ based electrolyte conditions.

Following our experimental design, we implemented four NaCl-based systems using anthrax nanopores (Fig. 2d-f). These systems exhibited open-pore currents (Io₂) of −3.66 pA (System i), −2.07 pA (System ii), −2.47 pA (System iii), and − 2.65 pA (System iv), with corresponding σ values of 33.93%, 24.53%, 29.6%, and 27.78%, respectively (Fig. 2e). While Systems ii-iv demonstrated smooth current-voltage (I-V) characteristics, all systems displayed either current trajectories attenuation of high-frequency fluctuations and miscellaneous noise interference, existing similar limitations in NaCl systems (Fig. 2f and Fig. S2).

Moreover, lithium chloride (LiCl), a monovalent cation electrolyte, was also investigated due to its specific ionic conductivity, and is distinctive for nanopore relevance electrical determination. The LiCl-based electrolyte systems demonstrated enhanced stability compared to NaCl/KCl systems, which primarily stems from Li+’s unique physicochemical properties (Fig. 2g-i) [16]. Its small ionic radius (0.76 Å) with extensive hydration (5.2 water molecules) may create a stabilized microenvironment in nanopore, while simultaneously display clamping and non-clamping states (Fig. 2g and Fig. S3). To be more specific, the electrical characterization revealed distinct current profiles across different systems (Fig. 2g). System i exhibited an open-pore current (Io₂) of −3.89 pA with a clamping state current (Io₁) of −0.70 pA, while System ii showed reduced currents of −2.23 pA (Io₂) and − 1.61 pA (Io₁). Notably, Systems iii and iv, under the pH-asymmetric conditions, displayed only the non-clamping state with Io₂ values of −2.51 pA and − 3.62 pA respectively, as the proton-driven force eliminated the clamping state current (Io₁). As shown in Fig. 2h, Systems iv had significantly lower σ values of 23.34%, compared to others (27.24%, 35.21% and 37.46%). This enhanced anthrax nanopore stability was further corroborated by smooth current-voltage (I-V) characteristics in System iii and iv (Fig. 3i, pH-asymmetric).

These results are likely due to strong hydration capacity and size-selective ion transport, leading to lower current traces attenuation, more consistent open-pore currents, and prolonged detection stability. Hence, it is not unreasonable that we can observe two distinct states of clamping Io1 (occlusion state) and non-clamping Io2 (complete open-pore) in LiCl systems i and ii, while proton-driven allosteric mechanism only displays non-clamping Io2 in LiCl systems iii and iv (asymmetric pH). Although our results demonstrate that pH asymmetry enables sensitive electrical measurements with anthrax nanopores, all tested alkali metal chlorides (KCl, NaCl, LiCl) exhibited persistent noise interference (Fig. S3). These limitations ultimately constrain the sensing application with anthrax nanopores.

Ionic liquids enable a stable anthrax nanopore platform

Ionic liquids (herein refers to1-buty-3-methylimidazolium chloride, [BMIM]Cl), are gaining prominence application in nanopores [17, 20]. The organic cations [BMIM]+ with its different molecular structure from alkali metal cations offers adjustable ionic conductivity due to specific interactions with nanopore surfaces, thereby improve electrochemical properties of nanopore detection [17–19]. This makes [BMIM]Cl a valuable electrolyte for exploring unconventional ion-transport behaviors. Following similar experimental design, we examined the effects of four [BMIM]Cl-based systems on anthrax nanopores (Fig. 3a). A comparison of conditions (i) and (iv) imply some effect on current amplitude of the channel attributable to the pH gradient when same ionic strengths of the electrolyte was applied; however, the dominant factor is electrolyte concentration, as evidenced by the pronounced reduction in current from condition (i) to (ii). Moreover, the current remains virtually unchanged between conditions (ii) and (iii), which are identical apart from the pH gradient, confirming its modest role. The phenomena are reasonable in nanopore sensing, because ionic concentration primarily and directly governs the amplitude of the current, mediated the current primarily through the alteration of the solution’s electrical conductivity.

The radar plots in Fig. 3b comprehensively compare multiple performance metrics such as clamping frequency, conductance, σ, Io1, and Io2 across different systems. Notably, two distinct states were observed in [BMIM]Cl electrolyte systems (i and ii): a clamping current Io1 (occlusion state) and a non-clamping current Io2 (fully open-pore state). In contrast, only the non-clamped Io2 state occurred in systems iii and iv, attributable to a proton-driven transport mechanism. The radar network diagram highlights the most favorable experimental conditions, where an inner pentagonal shape (dashed line) indicates optimal performance. In this specific type of radar chart, the dashed line represents a “Standardization” and “Deviation is Undesirable”. Therefore, the basis for the evaluation is to visualize deviation from an ideal, standardized baseline for each parameter (e.g., the performance of an electrolyte). For the system to be considered “good” or “well-balanced,” it must perform as close as possible to this standard level for all parameters simultaneously [16]. The chart is a powerful tool for quickly identifying which electrolyte formulation offers the best compromise and most balanced set of properties.

For example, biological channels (e.g., voltage-gated ion channels) tend to be more stable in their occlusion (represents low-conductance) state, preventing energy loss and maintaining cellular homeostasis by minimizing ion leakage. Consequently, lower conductance reduces current fluctuations (e.g., ionic noise), resulting in more stable signal transmission. Among the tested systems, system iv (symmetric concentration asymmetric pH) demonstrated the most stable ion transport environment by proton-driven transport enhancement, with all electrical properties (e.g., conductance and discrete constant σ) converging toward the ideal pentagonal region. This stability is consistent with the measured conductance across different systems in Fig. S4. This enhanced nanopore stability was further supported by the smooth current trajectories and I-V characteristics (Fig. 3a, c and Table S1). Results confirm that pH-asymmetric ionic liquid optimizes the anthrax nanopore activity. More importantly, the nanopore maintains stability without current trace attenuation or persistent noise interference. It should be noticed that, considering the channel returns to its closed/gated state due to the possibly consequential pH equilibrium, all data were collected based on satisfied open state current traces. In conclusion, a stable anthrax nanopore system is achieved using symmetric ionic liquid concentration and asymmetric pH conditions, offering the potential for biosensing application. Among the solvents of ionic liquids with the potential to stabilize anthrax protein nanopore-based sensing, anthrax-[BMIM]Cl system emerge as a promising nanopore candidates, especially considering the improved nanopore electrical characteristics and chemical stability. The potential molecular interactions between the BMIM⁺ cation and the nanopore are possibly dependent on the nanopore’s material. The surface of anthrax nanopore is negatively charged and mainly hydrophilic, with hydrophobic patches seen at phenylalanine clamps of the mouth and at the middle of the nanopore lumen [10]. The positively charged BMIM⁺ cation will be strongly electrostatically attracted to these surfaces, leading to the formation of an electrical double layer (EDL) that is enriched with BMIM⁺ cations. This drastically alters the effective surface charge and ion distribution within the nanopore, which directly modulates ionic conductance and current rectification properties. In addition, the negative nanopore surface also has aromatic rings (i.e., phenylalanine clamps) with rich electron density, the BMIM⁺ cation carrying aromatic imidazolium ring can interact strongly with nanopore via π-π stacking and cation-π forces. The combination of these interactions means that BMIM⁺ doesn’t just behave as a charge carrier; it actively modifies the nanoconfinement within the pore, thereby are crucial for ionic liquids-based sensing schemes and signal modulation with anthrax nanopore. Previously, Hai-chen et al. constructed an elaborate system to accurately detect the π–π interactions between phenylalanine derivatives via molecular exchange processes inside a protein nanopore. In this work, it is also demonstrated and supported that π–π interactions directly modulates ionic conductance and current properties [22]. Noticed that, since the BMIM⁺ cation is relatively bulky compared with anthrax pore lumen and conventional small cations (K⁺, Na⁺, Li⁺), it seems unlikely that uniform binding occurs within the heptameric (7-mer) anthrax nanopore due to steric hindrance. However, the structural configuration of BMIM+-modified nanopore interface is essential for understanding the accurate molecular mechanism, which might be resolved by other methodologies like Cryo-EM or NMR.

Not every category of ionic liquids is able to stabilize protein nanopores or maintain their sensing function. Some types of ionic liquids can have interactions with proteins or DNAs, altering their conformations or activity, yet this is not always a drawback, these ionic liquids may meanwhile serve as inhibitors of unwanted protein reactions. Furthermore, interactions between the ionic liquids and protein nanopores can vary significantly with changes in the surrounding environment and ionic liquids concentration [18]. ∆pH indeed influence how [BMIM]Cl interact with anthrax nanopores, leading to either strengthened or modified outcomes. Besides, pH effect is intrinsically linked to the surface properties of the nanopore, mainly modulating the state of the current (e.g., noise level, rectification characteristics or blocking signal morphology). Change of pH offers a simple yet powerful method to improve analytes sensing and discrimination with other nanopores [23]– [24], whereby, for instance, the asymmetric, low pH-condition exerted a complexity arising from a combination of hydrodynamic effects in a restricted environment and electrostatics screening in hydrophobic nanopore confinement that ensured the capability of programmable sensing of spatially separated analytes [25].

Discrimination of MAPKK homologous peptides at single-molecule level

Nanopore technology is a powerful analytical tool capable of probing biomolecules with high resolution and precision, particularly in the study of peptides sensing [26–30]. To demonstrate the sensing ability of biomolecules with the established stable anthrax nanopore, a series of homologous peptides were selected for the electrical measurement. It should be noted that homologous peptides/proteins are usually expressed by certain gene families in biological system, and those molecules always bear nonuniform molecular sequences. Therefore, a satisfied nanopore sensors should be sensitive to produce the distinguishable current blockages promoting the peptide discrimination at single molecule level. Mitogen-activated protein kinase kinases (MAPKKs) are one of the protein kinase families that catalyze the phosphorylation of proteins, leading to conformational changes and regulating functional alteration such as cell division, development, metabolism and stress responses in eukaryotic systems [31–33]. To facilitate comparisons among MAPKKs orthologues from different gene families, various MAPKK peptides were synthesized (Fig. 4a and Table S2).

In the course of measuring five homologous peptides of MAPKK, we noticed that the anthrax nanopore produced distinguishable current blockage events in the presence of peptides under the experimental condition (Fig. S5-S9), and the channel was clean over the assay time; no clamping state and noise interference were observed in the presence of peptides. The values of ΔI/Io, S.D, and τoff for homologous peptides detection are favorable with significantly statistical differences (Fig. 4b-d, p ≤ 0.0001), indicating that the anthrax nanopore we have prepared is very sensitive for homologous peptides discrimination bearing nonuniform molecular sequences at the same length. Additional experiments were performed to clarify whether the detection is in a voltage-dependent fashion. Results showed that the detection was not affected by the applied voltage bias (Fig. 4e-g). Moreover, as shown in Fig. 4e, similar excluded volume ΔI/Io values were observed under different applied voltages, indicating that the 5 peptides being studied had similar volumes. Regarding the possible underlying principle that enables peptide detection and differentiation, anthrax nanopore carrying negatively charged and mainly hydrophilic lumen surface offers favorable capture of these positively charged analytes (pI > pH5.6 in cis) by electrical driven force with − 120 mV applied potential bias (Table S2). Furthermore, those peptides also contain different amount of aromatic and positive charged residues in the sequences, which may interact strongly with BMIM+-modified nanopore interface via π-π stacking and cation-π forces, directly modulates current properties of discriminative sensing performance. Therefore, an efficient discrimination can be obtained with that of quantitative analysis of nanopore electrical properties in response to the peptides` characters (Fig. 4h). Furthermore, the simultaneous detection of MAPKK peptides combining all these analytes was performed (Fig. 4i and Fig. S10). Considering the molecular properties of anthrax nanopore and MAPKK peptides (Fig. 4a and Table S2), it is not surprising that the distinctive distributions of the ΔI/Io demonstrated the capability of using this anthrax nanopore system to simultaneously discriminate MAPKK peptide analytes in one sample. This finding can be taken as clear evidence of the importance of peptide sequence-dependent recognition in anthrax nanopore biosensing.

Conclusion

The anthrax protective antigen nanopore is an emerging system for investigating protein translocation. However, its biosensing application has been limited by the unstable/large noise channel current and frequent self-gating of the wild-type anthrax channel. By using asymmetric pH [BMIM]Cl-electrolyte condition instead of the traditional inorganic salt solutions, our developed anthrax protective antigen nanopore platform can maintain stable ionic currents, and diminish channel occlusion and background noise interference. Furthermore, the stabilized anthrax nanopore demonstrated exceptional molecular detection capability, resolving homologous MAPKK peptides discrimination at single-molecule resolution. This research provides an efficient solution to resolve problems of signal attenuation and low-resolution detection in biological nanopore sensing. Consequently, this work not only overcomes the inherent limitations of anthrax nanopores but also establishes electrolyte optimization as a method of choice for satisfied nanopore biomedical sensing.

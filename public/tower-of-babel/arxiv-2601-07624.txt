Chris Lambie-Hanson, Pedro Marun

Abstract
. We add to the theory of preservation of topological properties under forcing. In particular, we answer a question of Gilton and Holshouser in a strong sense, showing that if player II has a winning strategy in the strong countable fan tightness game of a space at a point, then this continues to hold in every set forcing extension of the universe. The same is also true for the Rothberger game, but not for the countable fan tightness or Menger games.

1.
Introduction

Suppose that
V\subseteq W
are transitive models of set theory and that
(X,\tau)\in V
is such that
V\models
“
(X,\tau)
is a topological space”. From the point of view of
W
,
(X,\tau)
need not be a topological space, because
W
may contain new subsets of
\tau
whose union is not in
\tau
. On the other hand, from the point of view of
W
,
\tau
is a basis for some topology on the set
X
, and one can ask how this topology compares with
\tau
. In practice, one often starts with a topological space
(X,\tau)
satisfying a property
\Phi
in some ground model
V
, passes to a generic extension by some forcing
\mathbb{P}
, and asks whether the topology generated by
\tau
in
V^{\mathbb{P}}
satisfies property
\Phi
. In general, there is no straightforward answer to this question; the situation depends on the space
(X,\tau)
, the property
\Phi
and the forcing poset
\Phi
. The following examples illustrate this:

Example 1.1

(
[
3
, Example 4.1]
)
.

Let
X
be the closed unit interval
[0,1]
with the Euclidean topology. If
\mathbb{P}
is any forcing that adds a new real, then
X
will not be compact in
V^{\mathbb{P}}
, for it contains all the rationals between
0
and
1
and is therefore not closed as a subset of the closed unit interval of the generic extension, which is a compact Hausdorff space in
V^{\mathbb{P}}
.

A less trivial example:

Example 1.2

(Burke,
[
12
, Theorem 17]
)
.

The space
X={}^{\omega_{1}}2
with the product topology
1
1

1

where each copy of
2
is given the discrete topology
is compact by Tychonoff’s theorem, but is not even Lindelöf in the generic extension by
\mathrm{Add}(\omega_{1},1)
. Indeed, let
\dot{g}
be a name for the generic function
\omega_{1}\to 2
. For each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, let
\dot{U}_{\alpha}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name for the set

\{x\in X:x(\alpha)=\dot{g}(\alpha)\}.

Observe that the interpretation of
\dot{U}_{\alpha}
by any generic filter is a ground-model open set, hence it is forced that
\dot{U}_{\alpha}
is open in
X
(with respect to the new topology). An easy genericity argument shows that
\Vdash X=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}\dot{U}_{\alpha}
, but no countable sub-collection of
\{\dot{U}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
can cover
X
.

The preservation of the Lindelöf property under countably closed forcings has received the most attention in the published literature, so much so that “indestructible Lindelöf” usually means a Lindelöf space which remains so after any countably closed forcing. For a wealth of information on this matter, see
[
12
]
and
[
10
]
.

In this paper, we consider the preservation of game-theoretic versions of classical topological
properties, namely the Menger and Rothberger properties, which strengthen the Lindelöf
property, and (strong) countable fan tightness, a strengthening of countable tightness.
In
[
3
, Question 8.1]
, Gilton and Holshouser ask whether player II having a winning strategy in the strong countable fan tightness game is preserved by strongly proper forcings (for undefined notions, see section 2). Our main theorem shows that the answer is “Yes”, in a very strong sense:

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
(X,\tau)
is a topological space and player II has a winning strategy in the strong countable fan tightness game for
X
. Then this is preserved in any forcing extension of
V
.

The same proof will give the following theorem, which strengthens
[
3
, Theorem 7.2]
:

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
(X,\tau)
is a topological space and player II has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game for
X
. Then this is preserved in any forcing extension of
V
.

The crucial difference between the games mentioned in Theorems A and B and the closely related
countable fan tightness and Menger games lies in the fact that, in the former games, player II
plays a single point or open set, respectively, on each move, whereas in the latter, they play
a collection of points or open sets of arbitrary finite size on each move. As we will see, due
to this difference, the analogues of Theorems A and B for the countable fan tightness and Menger
games provably fail.

2.
Preliminaries

If
(X,\tau)
is a topological space and
\mathbb{P}
is a forcing notion, then we let
(X,\tau^{\mathbb{P}})
denote (a name for) the space with underlying set
X
and topology generated by
\tau
in the generic extension
V^{\mathbb{P}}
. We will abuse notation and speak of “the space
X
”, with the understanding that the model in which the topological properties are evaluated can be deduced from context. For example, when we write “
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}X
has property
\Phi
”, we really mean
X
as computed in the
\mathbb{P}
-generic extension, i.e. that it is forced that
(X,\tau^{\mathbb{P}})
has property
\Phi
. One way around these technicalities, suggested by Dow in
[
1
]
, is to redefine the notion of a topological space to mean a pair
(X,\mathcal{B})
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a basis for a (unique) topology on
X
. Being a basis is absolute for transitive models of
\sf ZF
, which renders mute the notational considerations above, but we will follow the more common, if slightly ambiguous, terminology and definitions.

If
\kappa
and
\lambda
are cardinals, with
\kappa

\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,\lambda)
is the poset for adding
\lambda

\kappa
. More precisely,
\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,\lambda)
consists of the set of partial functions
\lambda\times\kappa\rightharpoonup 2
of size less than
\kappa
. We order
\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,\lambda)
by (reverse) inclusion.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing poset. We will say that a regular cardinal
\theta
is
sufficiently large
(for
\mathbb{P}
) iff
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{P})\in H_{\theta}
. If other objects, like a topological space
(X,\tau)
, are at play, the phrase “sufficiently large” will also imply that these objects belong to
H_{\theta}
.

The following definition goes back to Mitchell in
[
8
]
:

Recall that a topological space
(X,\tau)
is a
Lindelöf space
iff every open cover has a countable subcover. We will be concerned with the following two strengthenings of the Lindelöf property:

•

A topological space
(X,\tau)
has the
Rothberger property
(or simply “is Rothberger”) iff for every sequence
{\langle{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
of open covers of
X
, there exists a sequence
{\langle{U_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
with
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
for every
n\in\omega
so that
\{U_{n}:n<\omega\}
covers
X
.

•

A topological space
(X,\tau)
has the
Menger property
(or simply “is Menger”) iff for every sequence
{\langle{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
of open covers of
X
, there exists a sequence
{\langle{\mathcal{F}_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
with
\mathcal{F}_{n}\in[\mathcal{U}_{n}]^{<\omega}
for every
n\in\omega
so that
\{\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{n}:n<\omega\}
covers
X
.
2
2

2

for a set
A
,
[A]^{<\omega}
denotes the family of finite subsets of
A
.

Clearly, every compact space is Menger, but being Rothberger is a stronger property:

Example 2.2
.

Let
X
be the closed unit interval with the Euclidean topology. Choose a sequence
{\langle{r_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
of positive numbers with
\sum_{n}r_{n}<1
. For each
n\in\omega
, let
\mathcal{U}_{n}
be the collection of open subsets of
X
which have Lebesgue measure at most
r_{n}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{U}_{n}
covers
X
. If
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
for every
n
, then
\bigcup_{n}U_{n}
has measure at most
\sum_{n}r_{n}<1
, hence does not cover
X
.
Thus,
X
is compact but not Rothberger.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space and

. We say that

•

(X,\tau)
has
strong countable fan tightness at

iff for every sequence
{\langle{A_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
of subsets of

{\langle{x_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}

for every

.

•

(X,\tau)

countable fan tightness at

iff for every sequence
{\langle{A_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
of subsets of

{\langle{F_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}

.

We will now introduce a series of game analogues of the aforementioned topological properties. For a survey on the topic of topological games, see
[
13
]
.

If
(X,\tau)
is a topological space and
x\in X
, then the
strong countable fan tightness game for
X
at the point
x
is a two-player game of length
\omega
that proceeds as follows. In each round, player I first plays a set
A_{n}\subseteq X
such that
x\in\mathrm{cl}(A_{n})
; then player II plays a point
x_{n}\in A_{n}
. Player II wins iff
x\in\mathrm{cl}(\{x_{n}:n<\omega\})
; otherwise, player I wins.

If
(X,\tau)
is a topological space and
x\in X
, then the
countable fan tightness game for
X
at the point
x
is a two-player game of length
\omega
that proceeds as follows. In each round, player I first plays a set
A_{n}\subseteq X
such that
x\in\mathrm{cl}(A_{n})
; then player II plays a finite set
F_{n}\subseteq A_{n}
. Player II wins iff
x\in\mathrm{cl}(\bigcup_{n<\omega}F_{n})
; otherwise, player I wins.

If
(X,\tau)
is a topological space, then the
Rothberger game for
X
is a two-player game of length
\omega
that proceeds as follows. In each round, player I first plays an open cover
\mathcal{U}_{n}
of
X
; then player II plays an open set
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
. Player II wins iff
\{U_{n}:n<\omega\}
covers
X
; otherwise player I wins.

If
(X,\tau)
is a topological space, then the
Menger game for
X
is a two-player game of length
\omega
that proceeds as follows. In each round, player I first plays an open cover
\mathcal{U}_{n}
of
X
; then player II plays a finite subset
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{n}
. Player II wins iff
\{\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{n}:n<\omega\}
covers
X
; otherwise player I wins.

We have the following well known fact.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space. Suppose that player II has a winning strategy in the Rothberger (respectively Menger, strong countable fan tightness, countable fan tightness) game. Then
(X,\tau)
is Rothberger (respectively is Menger, has strong countable fan tightness, has countable fan tightness).

Proof.

We prove the lemma for the Menger property, the other cases are analogous. Let
\sigma
be a winning strategy for player II in the Menger game. Given
{\langle{\mathcal{U}_{n}:n<\omega}\rangle}
a sequence of open covers of
X
, consider the run of the game where I plays
\mathcal{U}_{n}
on round
n
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{n}
be the play by II at round
n
according to
\sigma
. Then
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{F}_{n}
covers
X
, because
\sigma
is winning, and so
X
is Menger.
∎

3.
Strong Properness

The following proposition was proven by the second author in his PhD thesis (see
[
7
, Theorem 3.32]
), and independently and almost at the same time by Gilton and Holshouser (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.6]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing notion,
(X,\tau)
a topological space, and
\theta
a sufficiently large
regular cardinal. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a stationary subset of
[H_{\theta}]^{\omega}
and suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is
\mathcal{S}
-proper. If
(X,\tau)
is Lindelöf, then
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}X
is Lindelöf.

The following lemma appeared, in greater generality, in
[
4
, Lemma 1.12]
. The proof presented there has a very small gap, so we will use this opportunity to reprove the case that is of interest to us:

Lemma 3.2

(Gilton-Neeman)
.

Let
\kappa\geq\omega_{1}
and set
\mathbb{P}:=\mathrm{Add}(\omega,\kappa)
. Let
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name for a
\sigma
-closed forcing. Let
\theta
be a sufficiently large (for
\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
) regular cardinal and
M\prec H_{\theta}
be countable. Then the two-step iteration
\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
is
M
-strongly proper.

Proof.

Fix a surjection
\varphi:\omega\twoheadrightarrow M
and a condition
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{0})\in M\cap(\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}})
. Choose an ordinal
\alpha\in\omega_{1}\setminus M
and let
\dot{f}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name for the
\alpha
th real added by
\mathbb{P}
, i.e.
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\dot{f}=\bigcup\{p(\alpha,\cdot):p\in\dot{G}\}
, where
\dot{G}
is a name for the
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter.

Recursively define
{\langle{\dot{q}_{i}:i<\omega}\rangle}
as follows (with
\dot{q}_{0}
already defined): given
\dot{q}_{i}
, by the maximum principle we can find a name
\dot{q}_{i+1}
for a condition in
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
so that

\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\left(\varphi(\dot{f}(i))\leq\dot{q}_{i}\to\dot{q}_{i+1}=\varphi(\dot{f}(i))\right)\wedge\left(\varphi(\dot{f}(i))\not\leq\dot{q}_{i}\to\dot{q}_{i+1}=\dot{q}_{i}\right)\right].

Note that, for every
i<\omega
, we have that
\Vdash\dot{q}_{i}\in M[\dot{G}]
, but the names
\dot{q}_{i}
themselves need not belong to
M
. However, we have a ‘local’ version of this:

Claim 3.3
.

For every
n\in\omega
and every
p\in\mathbb{P}
with
\mathrm{dom}(p(\alpha,\cdot))\supseteq n
, there exists a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{q}\in M
for a condition in
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
p\Vdash\dot{q}=\dot{q}_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
, with the case
n=0
being trivial. Suppose the lemma holds for
n
and let
p\in\mathbb{P}
be such that
n+1\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(p(\alpha,\cdot))
. By the induction hypothesis, we can find
\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}\in M
, a
\mathbb{P}
-name for a condition in
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
, so that
p\Vdash{\dot{q}}_{n}^{\prime}=\dot{q}_{n}
. Since
n\in\mathrm{dom}(p(\alpha,\cdot))
, letting
m:=p(\alpha,n)
we have that
p\Vdash\dot{f}(n)=m
. Note that

\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\exists q\in\dot{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left(\varphi(m)\leq\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}\to\dot{q}=\varphi(m)\right)\wedge\left(\varphi(m)\not\leq\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}\to\dot{q}=\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}\right)\right].

Since all the parameters belong to
M
, by elementarity we can find
\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n+1}\in M
so that
\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n+1}\in\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
and

\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\left(\varphi(m)\leq\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n}\to\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n+1}=\varphi(m)\right)\wedge\left(\varphi(m)\not\leq\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n}\to\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n+1}=\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}\right)\right].

Since
p\Vdash\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n}=\dot{q}_{n}
, it follows that
p\Vdash\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n+1}=\dot{q}_{n+1}
.
∎

The sequence of names
{\langle{\dot{q}_{i}:i<\omega}\rangle}
is forced to be decreasing, so by countable closure we can find a
\mathbb{P}
-name for a condition
\dot{q}_{\omega}
forced to be a lower bound of
{\langle{\dot{q}_{i}:i<\omega}\rangle}
. We will argue that
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{\omega})
is strongly generic for
M
. To see this, fix
D
a dense subset of
(\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}})\cap M
, we will argue that every extension of
(p_{0},\dot{q}_{\omega})
can be further extended to force
D\cap\dot{K}\neq\emptyset
, where
\dot{K}
is a name for the
\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
-generic filter. Fix therefore
(s,\dot{r})\leq(p_{0},\dot{q}_{\omega})
and set
s_{0}:=s\cap M
, so that
s_{0}\in M
by elementarity. Applying the claim to
s
and
n:=\min\{k\in\omega:(\alpha,k)\not\in\mathrm{dom}(s)\}
, we can find
\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n}\in M
a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an element of
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
which satisfies
s\Vdash\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n}=\dot{q}_{n}
. In particular,
(s_{0},\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n})
is a condition in
(\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}})\cap M
, so, by the density of
D
, we can find
(t,\dot{u})\in D
with
(t,\dot{u})\leq(s_{0},\dot{q}^{\prime}_{n})
. Since
\varphi
is a surjection, we can fix
m\in\omega
with
\varphi(m)=\dot{u}
.

Set
s^{\prime}:=s\cup t\cup\{(\alpha,n,m)\}
. Since
t\leq s_{0}=s\cap M
and
(\alpha,n)\not\in\mathrm{dom}(s)
,
s^{\prime}
is a condition extending
s
. Therefore,
(s^{\prime},\dot{r})
is a condition in
\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
. We claim that it, moreover, extends
(t,\dot{u})
. Obviously,
s^{\prime}\leq t
, so we only need to argue that
s^{\prime}\Vdash\dot{r}\leq\dot{u}
. Note that

•

s^{\prime}\Vdash\dot{f}(n)=m
;

•

t\Vdash\dot{u}\leq\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}
;

•

s\Vdash\dot{q}_{n}^{\prime}=\dot{q}_{n}
;

so, by the choice of

,

s^{\prime}\Vdash\varphi(f(n))=\varphi(m)=\dot{u}\leq\dot{q}_{n}.

Therefore,
s^{\prime}\Vdash\dot{q}_{n+1}=\dot{u}
, and hence

s^{\prime}\Vdash\dot{r}\leq\dot{q}_{\omega}\leq\dot{q}_{n+1}=\dot{u}.

We have shown that
(s^{\prime},\dot{r})\leq(t,\dot{u})
. Since
(t,\dot{u})\in D
, in particular
(s^{\prime},\dot{r})\Vdash D\cap\dot{K}\neq\emptyset
, as desired.
∎

Combining Proposition
3.1

Corollary 3.4

(Dow)
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a Lindelöf space. Then, in
V^{\mathrm{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})}
,
X
is a Lindelöf space which is moreover indestructible under countably closed forcing.

Proof.

The fact that
X
is Lindelöf in
V^{\mathrm{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})}
follows by Proposition
3.1
, because
\mathrm{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})
is strongly proper for every countable
M\prec H_{\theta}
.

If
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
is an
\mathrm{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})
-name for a countably closed forcing, then the two-step iteration
\mathrm{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}

3.1
.
∎

In
[
10
, Problem 2]
, Scheepers and Tall ask whether adding a single Cohen real renders every ground model Lindelöf space indestructible. We do not know the answer to this question, but we can show that the strong properness machinery is not enough to solve this problem. Indeed, not only was
\kappa\geq\omega_{1}
key in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, the statement is false if
\kappa\leq\omega
, as the next lemma shows:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{R}:=\mathrm{Add}(\omega,1)\ast\dot{\mathrm{Add}}(\omega_{1},1)
,
\theta
a sufficiently large regular cardinal, and
M\prec H_{\theta}
countable. Then no condition in
\mathbb{R}
is strongly generic for
M
.

Proof.

To see this, fix
(p_{0},\dot{p}_{1})\in\mathbb{R}
; we need to argue that
(p_{0},\dot{p}_{1})
is not strongly generic. Setting
\delta=M\cap\omega_{1}
, we may assume upon extending
(p_{0},\dot{p}_{1})
if necessary that
p_{0}\Vdash\mathrm{dom}(\dot{p}_{1})\supseteq\delta
.

Since
\mathrm{Add}(\omega,1)
is countable, we can fix an enumeration
{\langle{q_{\alpha}:\alpha<\delta}\rangle}
of the set
\{q\in\mathrm{Add}(\omega,1):q\leq p_{0}\}
such that, for every
q\leq p_{0}
, the set
\{\alpha<\delta:q_{\alpha}=q\}
is unbounded in
\delta
.

Fix, for each
\alpha<\delta
, a condition
r_{\alpha}\leq q_{\alpha}
and an integer
k_{\alpha}
so that
r_{\alpha}\Vdash\dot{p}_{1}(\alpha)=k_{\alpha}
. Let
D
be the set

\left\{(t_{0},\dot{t}_{1})\in M\cap\mathbb{R}:t_{0}\perp p_{0}\vee\exists\alpha<\delta\left[t_{0}\leq r_{\alpha}\wedge t_{0}\Vdash(\alpha\in\mathrm{dom}(\dot{t}_{1})\wedge\dot{t}_{1}(\alpha)\neq k_{\alpha})\right]\right\}.

Claim 3.6
.

D
is dense in
M\cap\mathbb{R}

Proof.

Fix
(s_{0},\dot{s}_{1})\in M\cap\mathbb{R}
. We may assume that

Remark 4.3
.

If we try to extend Theorem
4.1
to the countable fan tightness case, we run into trouble with the proof of Claim

, because player II makes more than once choice in each round: if we let

, then by

we can find, for each

with

, but there is no reason to believe that

has a lower bound. In fact, Theorem
4.1
fails for the countable fan tightness game: let

with the product topology, and let

. By
[
11

, player II has a winning strategy in the countable fan tightness game for

, but
Y
is not countably tight in

. Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
, in
V^{\mathrm{Add}(\omega_{1},1)}
, player II does not have a winning strategy in the countable fan tightness game for

.

Theorem
4.1
also fails for the Menger game. Again consider the space

with the product topology. By Tychonoff’s theorem,

is compact, so player II has a very simple winning strategy in the Menger game: if player I’s play at stage

is

Introduction and Main Results

1.1.

Let
X
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex embeddable
CR manifold and let

A
be the Toeplitz operator on
X
associated with a Reeb vector field
\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,TX)
. Consider the operator
\chi_{k}(A)

defined by the functional
calculus of
A
, where
\chi
is a smooth function
with compact support in the positive real line and

\chi_{k}(\lambda):=\chi(k^{-1}\lambda)
. Let
\chi_{k}(A)(x,y)
denote the distribution kernel of
\chi_{k}(A)
. It was shown in
[
7
]
*Theorem 1.1 that
\chi_{k}(A)(x,y)
admits a full asymptotic expansion in
k
. In particular,

(1.1.1)

\chi_{k}(A)(x,x)\sim k^{n+1}b_{0}(x)+k^{n}b_{1}(x)+\cdots,

where
b_{j}(x)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
,
j=0,1,\ldots
. The expansion (
1.1.1
) can be seen as a CR analogue of the Tian-Yau-Catlin-Zelditch Bergman kernel asymptotics in complex geometry (we refer the reader to the book
[
13
]
). The term
b_{0}
in (
1.1.1
) was already calculated in
[
7
]
. For the Sasakian case the expansion (
1.1.1
) was obtained in
[
6
]
. It is a very natural (and fundamental question) to calculate
b_{1}
in (
1.1.1
). The calculation of
b_{1}
can be seen as some kind of local index theorem in CR geometry. On the other hand, the second coefficient of Bergman kernel asymptotic expansion in complex geometry plays an important role in Kähler geometry (see
[
13
]
).
The main goal of this work is to calculate
b_{1}
.

We now formulate our main result. We refer the reader to Section
2
for some terminology and notations used here.
Let
(X,T^{1,0}X)
be a compact orientable strictly pseudoconvex embeddable CR manifold of dimension
2n+1
,
n\geq 1
. Fix a global one form
\xi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,T^{*}X)
such that for any
x\in X
we have
\xi(x)\neq 0
,
\ker\xi(x)=\operatorname{Re}T_{x}^{1,0}X
and the Levi form
\mathcal{L}_{x}
is positive definite on
T^{1,0}_{x}X
. Here the Levi form
\mathcal{L}_{x}=\mathcal{L}^{\xi}_{x}
at
x\in X
(with respect to
\xi
) is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{x}\colon T^{1,0}_{x}X\times T^{1,0}_{x}X\to\mathbb{C},\,\,\,\mathcal{L}_{x}(U,V)=\frac{1}{2i}d\xi(U,\overline{V}).

There exists a unique vector field
\mathcal{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,TX)
such that
\mathcal{T}\lrcorner\xi\equiv 1
and
\mathcal{T}\lrcorner d\xi\equiv 0
, where
\lrcorner
denotes the interior product (contraction) between vectors and forms. We call
\mathcal{T}
the Reeb vector field on
X
with respect to
\xi
. Let
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
denote the Hermitian metric on
\mathbb{C}TX

induced by the Levi form
\mathcal{L}_{x}
(see (
2.3.2
)). The Hermitian
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
\mathbb{C}TX
induces a Hermitian metric

\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
\oplus_{p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},0\leq p,q\leq n}T^{*p,q}X
, where
T^{*p,q}X
denotes the bundle of
(p,q)
forms of
X
. Let
\Omega^{p,q}(X)

denote the space of smooth sections with values in
T^{*p,q}X
. We write
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X):=\Omega^{0,0}(X)
.

Fix a volume form
dV
on
X
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}dV\equiv 0
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}
denotes the Lie derivative of
\mathcal{T}
.
For
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
0\leq p,q\leq n
, let
(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,)
be the
L^{2}
inner product on
\Omega^{p,q}(X)
induced by
dV
and
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle
and let
L^{2}_{(p,q)}(X)
be the completion of
\Omega^{p,q}(X)
with respect to
(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,)
. We write
L^{2}(X):=L^{2}_{(0,0)}(X)
. Let
\overline{\partial}_{b}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{0,1}(X)

be the tangential Cauchy-Riemann operator. We extend
\overline{\partial}_{b}
to the
L^{2}
space:

\overline{\partial}_{b}:{\rm Dom\,}\overline{\partial}_{b}\subset L^{2}(X)\rightarrow L^{2}_{(0,1)}(X),

where
{\rm Dom\,}\overline{\partial}_{b}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}(X);\,\overline{\partial}_{b}u\in L^{2}_{(0,1)}(X)\right\}
. Let
H^{0}_{b}(X):={\rm Ker\,}\overline{\partial}_{b}
. Let

\Pi:L^{2}(X)\to H^{0}_{b}(X)

be the Szegő projection, i.e., the orthogonal projection onto
H^{0}_{b}(X)
with respect to
(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,)
. Let
\Pi(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X\times X)
denote the distribution kernel of
\Pi
. We recall
Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand’s fundamental theorem
[
1
]
(see also
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
) about the structure of the
Szegő kernel.
For any coordinate patch
(D,x)
on
X
and any positive function
\lambda\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}_{+})

there is a smooth function
\varphi:D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
with

(1.1.2)

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Im}\varphi(x,y)\geq 0,\\
&\varphi(x,y)=0~\text{if and only if}~y=x,\\
&d_{x}\varphi(x,x)=-d_{y}\varphi(x,x)=\lambda(x)\xi(x),\ \,\end{split}

such that we have on
D\times D
, the Szegő projector
can be approximated by a Fourier integral operator

(1.1.3)

\Pi(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{it\varphi(x,y)}s(x,y,t)dt+F(x,y),

where
F(x,y)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D\times D)
and
s(x,y,t)\in S^{n}_{\operatorname{cl}}(D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})

is a classical Hörmander symbol satisfying
s(x,y,t)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}s_{j}(x,y)t^{n-j}
in

S^{n}_{1,0}(D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,
s_{j}(x,y)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D\times D)
,
j=0,1,\ldots
, and if
\lambda(x)=1
,

(1.1.4)

s_{0}(x,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{dV}(x),

where

(1.1.5)

dV_{\xi}:=\frac{2^{-n}}{n!}\xi\wedge\left(d\xi\right)^{n}.

Let

A:=\Pi(-i\mathcal{T})\Pi:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

be the Toeplitz operator. We extend
A
to the
L^{2}
space:

(1.1.6)

\displaystyle A:=\Pi(-i\mathcal{T})\Pi:{\rm Dom\,}A\subset L^{2}(X)\rightarrow L^{2}(X),

where
{\rm Dom\,}A:=\left\{u\in L^{2}(X);\,Au\in L^{2}(X)\right\}
. It was shown in
[
7
]
*Theorem 3.3 that
A
is a self-adjoint operator. We consider the operator
\chi_{k}(A)

defined by functional
calculus of
A
, where
\chi
is a smooth function
with compact support in the positive real line and

\chi_{k}(\lambda):=\chi(k^{-1}\lambda)
. Let
\chi_{k}(A)(x,y)
denote the distribution kernel of
\chi_{k}(A)
. We recall the following consequence from the result obtained in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,T^{1,0}X)
be a compact orientable strictly pseudoconvex embeddable CR manifold of dimension
2n+1
,
n\geq 1
. Fix a global one form
\xi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,T^{*}X)
such that for any
x\in X
we have
\xi(x)\neq 0
,
\ker\xi(x)=\operatorname{Re}T_{x}^{1,0}X
and the respective Levi form
\mathcal{L}_{x}
is positive definite. Denote by
\mathcal{T}
the Reeb vector field associated to
\xi
, choose a volume form
dV
on
X
with
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}dV\equiv 0
and consider the operator
A
given by (
1.1.6
).
Let
(D,x)
be any coordinate patch and let

\varphi:D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
be any phase function satisfying
(
1.1.2
) and (
1.1.3
).
Then, for any
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
putting
\chi_{k}(t):=\chi(k^{-1}t)
,
k,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, one has for the distributional kernel
\chi_{k}(A)(x,y)
of
\chi_{k}(A)
that

(1.1.7)

\chi_{k}(A)(x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{ikt\varphi(x,y)}b^{\chi}(x,y,t,k)dt+O\left(k^{-\infty}\right)~\text{on}~D\times D,

where
b^{\chi}(x,y,t,k)\in S^{n+1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(1;D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
,

(1.1.8)

\begin{split}&b^{\chi}(x,y,t,k)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)k^{n+1-j}~\text{in $S^{n+1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(1;D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})$},\\
&b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D\times D\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}),~j=0,1,2,\ldots,\\
&b^{\chi}_{0}(x,x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{dV}(x)\,\chi(t)\,t^{n},\ \ \mbox{for every $x\in D$ with $\lambda(x)=1$ (see \eqref{Eq:PhaseFuncMainThm})},\end{split}

with
dV_{\xi}
given by (
1.1.5
) and for
I^{\chi}:=\operatorname{supp}\chi
,

(1.1.9)

\begin{split}{\rm supp\,}_{t}b^{\chi}(x,y,t,k),~{\rm supp\,}_{t}b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)\subset I^{\chi},\ \ j=0,1,2,\ldots~.\end{split}

(see Definition
2.3
for the meaning of
\operatorname{supp}_{t}
.)

In particular, we have

(1.1.10)

\chi_{k}(A)(x,x)\sim\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}b^{\chi}_{j}(x)k^{n+1-j}\ \ \mbox{in $S^{n+1}_{{\rm loc\,}}(1;X)$},

where
b^{\chi}_{j}(x)=\int b^{\chi}_{j}(x,x,t)dt
,
b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)
is as in (
1.1.8
),
j=0,1,\ldots
.

The expansion (
1.1.7
) can be seen as a semi-classical version of Boutet de Monvel-Sjöstrand’s result on strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds (see (
1.1.3
)). As mentioned above, it is a very natural (and fundamental question) to calculate
b^{\chi}_{1}(x)
in (
1.1.10
). In this work, we successfully calculate
b^{\chi}_{1}(x)
. The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

With the notations and assumptions in Theorem
1.1
, for
b^{\chi}_{1}(x)
in (
1.1.10
), we have

(1.1.11)

b^{\chi}_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}e^{-2(n+1)f(x)}\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{scal}(x)+(n+1)\Delta_{b}f(x)\right)\int\chi(t)t^{n-1}dt,

where
f(x):=\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\log\frac{dV(x)}{dV_{\xi}(x)}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
with
dV_{\xi}
given by (
1.1.5
),
R_{scal}
is the Tanaka-Webster scalar curvature with respect to the contact form
\xi
(see (
2.3.8
)) and
\Delta_{b}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
denotes the CR sublaplacian operator (see (
2.3.5
)).

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need to understand how the symbol
b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)
in (
1.1.8
) depends on
\chi
and
t
. We have the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

With the notations and assumptions in Theorem
1.1
, let
(D,x)
be any coordinate patch and let

\varphi:D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
be any phase function satisfying
(
1.1.2
) and (
1.1.3
). Then there exist smooth functions

(1.1.12)

\displaystyle a_{j,s}(x,y)\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(D\times D),\ \ j,s=0,1,\ldots,

such that for any
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
we can take
b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)
in (
1.1.8
),
j=0,1,\ldots
, so that

(1.1.13)

b^{\chi}_{j}(x,y,t)=\sum^{+\infty}_{s=0}a_{j,s}(x,y)\chi^{(s)}(t)t^{n+s-j},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,

where
\chi^{(s)}:=(\partial/\partial t)^{s}\chi
and the infinite sum in (
1.1.13
) converges uniformly in
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(K\times K\times I)
topology, for any compact subset
K\subset D
,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, for all
j=0,1\dots
.

Assume in addition that
\mathcal{T}
is a CR vector field, that is,
[\mathcal{T},\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,T^{1,0}X)]\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,T^{1,0}X)
. Then, there exists a phase function

\varphi:D\times D\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying
(
1.1.2
) and (
1.1.3
) such that

\mathcal{T}_{x}\varphi(x,y)\equiv 1
,
\mathcal{T}_{y}\varphi(x,y)\equiv-1
, and for any such phase function we can take the
a_{j,s}(x,y)
in (
1.1.12
),
j,s=0,1,\ldots
, such that for all
j\geq 0
we have

(1.1.14)

\begin{split}&a_{j,s}(x,y)=0,\ \ s=1,2,\ldots,\\
&\mathcal{T}_{x}a_{j,0}(x,y)=\mathcal{T}_{y}a_{j,0}(x,y)=0.\end{split}

Remark 1.4
.

(i) In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we show that for a fix point
p\in X
, we can find a phase
\varphi
satisfying (
1.1.2
) and (
1.1.3
) and
a_{j,s}(x,y)
,
j,s=0,1,\ldots
, such that
a_{1,s}(p,p)=0
, for all
s=1,\ldots
, and then we calculate

(see Theorem
3.8
for the details), where
a_{j,s}(x,y)
,
j,s=0,1,\ldots
, are as in (
1.1.13
).

(ii) To get (
1.1.11
), we need to generalize the result in
[
12
]
to a more general setting (see Theorem
3.4
for the details).

(iii) The first part of Theorem
1.3
holds for a more general choice of the Toeplitz operator

(see Theorem

for the details).

From Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
and by using integration by parts in
t
, we can reformulate our Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

With the notations and assumptions in Theorem
1.1
, there exist smooth functions
\{a_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)

such that for any
\chi\in\mathscr{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
we have

(1.1.15)

\chi_{k}(A)(x,x)\sim\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}k^{n+1-j}a_{j}(x)\int\chi(t)t^{n-j}dt\ \ \mbox{in $S^{n+1}_{{\rm loc\,}}(1;X)$}.

Furthermore, we have

(1.1.16)

\begin{split}&a_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}\frac{dV_{\xi}}{dV}(x),\\
&a_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{n+1}}e^{-2(n+1)f(x)}\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{scal}(x)+(n+1)\Delta_{b}f(x)\right),\end{split}

where
f(x):=\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\log\frac{dV(x)}{dV_{\xi}(x)}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
with
dV_{\xi}
given by (
1.1.5
),
R_{scal}
is the Tanaka-Webster scalar curvature with respect to the contact form
\xi
(see (
2.3.8
)) and
\Delta_{b}:\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X)
denotes the CR sublaplacian operator (see (

)).

We note that the formula for the coefficient

is a direct consequence from the results in
[
7
]
. Furthermore, it was also shown there that for the circle bundle case the expansion of the form described in (

extends the results in
[

]
by showing that the expansion of the form described in (

.

Remark 1.6
.

The functions
a_{j}
,
j\geq 0
, in Theorem
1.5
are uniquely determined by (
1.1.15
).
Furthermore, for any
j=0,1,\ldots
and any point

the value
a_{j}(x)
of the function
a_{j}
in Theorem
1.5
is determined by the restriction of
T^{1,0}X
,
\xi
and
dV
to any neighborhood of

. More precisely, we have the following: Given a compact CR manifold
(X^{\prime},T^{1,0}X^{\prime})
with one form
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X^{\prime},T^{*}X^{\prime})
and volume form
dV^{\prime}
such that
(X^{\prime},T^{1,0}X^{\prime})
,
\xi^{\prime}
and
dV^{\prime}
satisfy the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
denote by
\{a^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X^{\prime})
the smooth functions given by Theorem
1.5
associated to the data
(X^{\prime},T^{1,0}X^{\prime})
,
\xi^{\prime}
and
dV^{\prime}
. Let
D\subset X
be an open neighborhood of a point
x\in X
and let
D^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
be an open neighborhood of the point
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
. If
F\colon D^{\prime}\to D
is a CR diffeomorphism with
F(x^{\prime})=x
,
F^{*}\xi=\xi^{\prime}
and
F^{*}dV=dV^{\prime}
on
D^{\prime}
we have
a_{j}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=a_{j}(x)
for
j=0,1,\ldots
(see Remark
3.9
). In particular, choosing

we have that the functions
a_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
, become pseudo-Hermitian invariants for the triple
(X,T^{1,0}X,\xi)
.

Example 1.7
.

Let
(L,h)\to M
be a positive holomorphic line bundle with Hermitian metric
h
over a compact complex manifold
M
of dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=n
and volume form
dV_{M}
. Denote the Bergman kernel function for
H^{0}(M,L^{k})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, with respect to the
L^{2}
norm

\|s\|_{k}:=\sqrt{\int_{M}|s|^{2}_{h^{k}}dV_{M}},\,\,\,\,s\in H^{0}(M,L^{k})

by
P_{k}
that is
P_{k}(x):=\sup\{|s(x)|^{2}_{h^{k}}\mid s\in H^{0}(M,L^{k}),\|s\|_{k}\leq 1\}
,
x\in M
. Here
L^{k}
denotes the
k
-th tensor power of
L
and
h^{k}
is the Hermitian metric on
L^{k}
induced by
h
. It is well known (see
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
) that
P_{k}
has an asymptotic expansion

P_{k}(x)\sim\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}k^{n-j}b_{j}(x)\ \ \mbox{in $S^{n}_{{\rm loc\,}}(1;M)$},

for smooth functions
b_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
,
j\geq 0
.
Put

X:=\{v\in L^{*}\mid|v|_{h^{*}}=1\}\subset L,\,\,\,T^{1,0}X:=\mathbb{C}TX\cap T^{1,0}L^{*}.

Here
L^{*}
denotes the dual line bundle with metric
h^{*}
induced by
h
. Then
(X,T^{1,0}X)
is a compact strongly pseudoconvex CR manifold with a transversal CR
S^{1}
-action given by
S^{1}\times X\ni(\lambda,v)\mapsto\lambda v\in X
. Denote by
\mathcal{T}
the infinitesimal generator of this action and let
\xi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,T^{*}X)
be the uniquely defined real one form with
\mathcal{T}\lrcorner\xi\equiv 1
and
\xi(T^{1,0}X)=0
. Let
\pi\colon X\to M
denote the projection map. Putting
dV_{X}=\xi\wedge\pi^{*}dV_{M}
it follows that the setup satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
1.5
. Hence there exist smooth functions
a_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
j\geq 0
, such that for any
\chi\in\mathscr{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
we have

(1.1.17)

\displaystyle\chi_{k}(A)(x,x)\sim\sum^{+\infty}_{j=0}k^{n+1-j}a_{j}(x)\int\chi(t)t^{n-j}dt\ \ \mbox{in $S^{n+1}_{{\rm loc\,}}(1;X)$}.

We note that (
1.1.17
) was already obtained in
[
7
]
for the circle bundle case and it was shown that
a_{j}=\frac{1}{2\pi}b_{j}\circ\pi
,
j\geq 0
. For a more detailed study of the relation between the coefficients
a_{1}
and

we refer to Remark
3.5
.

Notations

We use the following notations throughout this article

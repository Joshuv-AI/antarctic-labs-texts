Permutohedron’s volume via Dyck paths
Damian de la Fuente

Abstract
In a recent project, Castillo, Libedinsky, Plaza, and the author established a deep connection between the size of lower Bruhat intervals in affine Weyl groups and the volume of the permutohedron, showing that the former can be expressed as a linear combination of the latter. In this paper, we provide a formula for the volume of this polytope in terms of Dyck paths. Thus, we present a shorter, alternative, and enlightening proof of a previous formula given by Postnikov.

In

More precisely, to each dominant coweight

2.2 Volumes and polytopes

The Euclidean volume on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the unique translation invariant measure
\mathrm{Vol}_{n+1}
scaled so that
\mathrm{Vol}_{n+1}([0,1]^{n+1})=1
, where
[0,1]^{n+1}
is the unit cube.
It satisfies the property

|\mathrm{det}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})|=\mathrm{Vol}_{n+1}(\Pi_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}}),

where
\Pi_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}}
is the parallelepiped spanned by the vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

For a
k
-dimensional subspace
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the induced volume
\mathrm{Vol}_{U}
is determined by the property

\mathrm{Vol}_{U}(\Pi_{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}})=|\mathrm{det}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},v_{k+1},\ldots,v_{n+1})|,

where
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}
is a basis of
U
and
\{v_{k+1},\ldots,v_{n+1}\}
an orthonormal basis for the orthogonal complement of
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
We write
\mathrm{Vol}_{k}=\mathrm{Vol}_{U}
, by abuse of notation and we call it the
k
-dimensional volume.

We now recall the classical
pyramid formula
for convex polytopes (see
[
6
, Lemma 5.1.1]
, for example).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
O\in\mathsf{P}\subset U
be a convex polytope
\mathsf{P}
with an interior point
O
, sitting in a
k
-dimensional subspace
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Then

\mathrm{Vol}_{k}(\mathsf{P})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\mathsf{F}\text{ facet of }\mathsf{P}}d(O,\mathsf{F})\,\mathrm{Vol}_{k-1}(\mathsf{F}),

(2.5)

where
d(O,\mathsf{F})
is the distance from
O
to the space spanned by
\mathsf{F}
.

The pyramid formula, as the name suggests, is derived by partitioning
\mathsf{P}
into pyramids from
O
to its facets.
This formula will be crucial to us when applying it to the permutohedron in
Lemma

3.2
.

Theorem 3.10
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the following equality holds

\mathcal{V}_{n}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{D}.

(3.17)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
.
If
n=0
the result is trivial.
So we fix
n>0
and assume the result holds for
m<n
.
For
1\leq k\leq n
we define
\mathcal{D}_{n}^{k}
to be the set of all
D\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that
D
passes through
(k,k)
but
D
does not pass through
(k^{\prime},k^{\prime})

To each
D\in\mathcal{D}_{n}^{k}
we associate two Dyck paths

and

, as follows:

•

D_{b}
is the path obtained from the sub-path of

(0,1)
to

by decreasing the

•

is the path obtained from the sub-path of
D
from
(k,k)
to
(n,n)
by decreasing the

and

k
.

Note that for

\Gamma^{\prime}_{D}=\Gamma^{\prime}_{n,k}\,\Gamma^{\prime}_{D_{b}}\,\Gamma^{\prime}_{D_{t}}[k],

(3.18)

where the polynomial

comes from the north step from

to

, and the shift in

is a bijection.
We get

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{D}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{n}^{k}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{D}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{n}^{k}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{n,k}\,\Gamma^{\prime}_{D_{b}}\,\Gamma^{\prime}_{D_{t}}[k]

\displaystyle\ \ (\mbox{\lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{eq: gamma decomp in three}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma^{\prime}_{n,k}\left(\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{k-1}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{D}\right)\left(\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{n-k}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{D}[k]\right)

\displaystyle\ \ (f_{k}\mbox{ is a bijection})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma^{\prime}_{n,k}\mathcal{V}_{k-1}\mathcal{V}_{n-k}[k]

\displaystyle\ \ (\mbox{Induction})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{n}.

\displaystyle\ \ (\mbox{\lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{prop: recurrence}})

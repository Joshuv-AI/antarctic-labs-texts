1.
Introduction

We study the tail behavior of a solution
X
to the stochastic fixed-point equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}AX+B,\qquad X\mbox{ and }(A,B)\mbox{ are independent}.

When such a solution exists, it is referred to as a perpetuity. to Our primary focus is on the light-tail regime. Throughout the paper, we assume that

A
and
B
are almost surely nonnegative and nonzero with positive probability, thus excluding trivial cases. For comprehensive background material, we refer to
[
3
]
.

The influence of the joint distribution of
(A,B)
on the tail behavior of
X
is notably diverse. When conditions ensure a light-tailed solution (see
[
13
,
17
,
16
,
21
,
5
]
), the analysis of (
1.1
) differs fundamentally from that in the heavy-tailed setting (cf.
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
12
,
15
,
9
]
). Roughly, the distinction between the light- and heavy-tailed cases hinges upon whether
\mathbb{P}(A\leq 1)=1
with light-tailed
B
or
\mathbb{P}(A>1)>0
.
In particular, for light-tailed distributions, the dependence structure between
A
and
B
significantly affects the tail asymptotics. Conversely, for heavy-tailed solutions, marginal distributions predominantly determine asymptotics, with dependence structure influencing only multiplicative constants (see e.g.
[
11
,
19
,
8
]
).

Our methodology builds upon the framework established by
[
5
]
, which analyzed the left-tail behavior (near
0^{+}
) of
X
. Following these ideas, we introduce a local dependence measure (
\mathrm{LDM}
) along with its associated Legendre-type transform. These tools form the cornerstone of our analysis and effectively describe the logarithmic right-tail asymptotics of
X
.

Specifically, we assume the following limit exists for all
y>0
: (a
\mathrm{LDM}
)

g(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)},

where the function
t\mapsto-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)
is regularly varying with index
\rho>0
.
Our first main result (Theorem
6.1
) shows that if a solution to (
1.1
) exists and the function
g
satisfies a technical condition (termed admissibility), then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)}=\lambda^{\ast},

where
\lambda^{\ast}
is the unique nonzero fixed point of the function
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)=\inf_{y>0}\{y^{\rho}\lambda+g(y)\}
. Moreover, we show that
\lambda^{\ast}=\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{\frac{g(y)}{1-y^{\rho}}\right\}
.

It is natural, given (
1.1
), to consider the recursive sequence

(1.2)

\displaystyle X_{n}=A_{n}X_{n-1}+B_{n},\quad n\geq 1,

where
(A_{n},B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are i.i.d. copies of
(A,B)
, and
X_{0}
is independent of this sequence. This sequence forms a Markov chain whose stationary distribution solves (
1.1
). Our second main result (Theorem
7.1
) identifies an upper envelope for
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
when
X_{0}=0
. We construct a deterministic scaling sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{f_{n}}

is almost surely positive and finite, with its deterministic explicit value provided. To the best of our knowledge, this constitutes the first characterization of the upper envelope for a sequence defined by (
1.2
). The proof extends techniques from
[
5
]
, which derived a lower envelope under related but distinct assumptions.

Our study is inspired by several previous investigations into the tail behavior of
X
. A common strategy in earlier work (cf.
[
15
,
14
,
23
,
4
]
) involves bounding the tail probability of
X
from above and below as

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>t)\leq\mathbb{P}(X>t)\leq\mathbb{P}(X_{n}^{\prime}>t),

where both
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(X_{n}^{\prime})_{n\geq 0}
satisfy (
1.2
) for some special
X_{0}
and
X_{0}^{\prime}
.
Thus, understanding
\mathbb{P}(X>t)
effectively reduces to analyzing the tail asymptotics of
\mathbb{P}(AY+B>t)
, with
Y
independent of
(A,B)
but not necessarily satisfying (
1.1
).

1.1.
Relation to the literature

We note that similar ideas to the definition of
\mathrm{LDM}
have appeared in related literature. In
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, for instance, it is assumed that there exists a finite function
f
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Ay+B>t)}{\mathbb{P}(B>t)}.

If
A
and
B
are independent,
A
has a finite moment generating function, and
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(B>\log t)
is regularly varying with index
-\alpha\leq 0
, then by the Breiman Lemma (see e.g.

[
10
]
) one obtains explicit form of
f(y)=\mathbb{E}[\exp(\alpha yA)]
. However, if
A
and
B
are not independent, yet (
1.3
) holds,
the form of
f
may differ (see
[
4
, Remark 2.3]
).
Under (
1.3
) with
\mathbb{P}(B>t)\sim a\,t^{c}e^{-bt}
,
\mathbb{E}[e^{bB}\mathbf{1}_{A=1}]<1
and some technical assumptions,
[
4
]
established that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{\mathbb{P}(B>t)}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[f(X)]}{1-\mathbb{E}[e^{bB}\mathbf{1}_{A=1}]}.

A closely related scenario is presented in
[
8
]
, where it is assumed that
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(A>t)
is regularly varying with index
-\alpha<0
, along with the conditions
\mathbb{E}[A^{\alpha}]<1
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(B>t)/\mathbb{P}(A>t)<\infty
. In this setting,
f
is defined as

f(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Ay+B>t)}{\mathbb{P}(A>t)},

and, under further technical conditions on the distribution of
A
, the tail asymptotics of
X
are described by

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{\mathbb{P}(A>t)}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[f(X)]}{1-\mathbb{E}[A^{\alpha}]}.

In the special case of independence between
A
and
B
, explicit computation of
f
becomes possible.

Thus, our contribution can be viewed as an advancement of this line of research, relaxing independence assumptions and thereby enabling analysis of a broader class of models.

Additionally, logarithmic asymptotics of the tail behavior of
X
were considered in
[
22
]
, under the assumption that the functions

\displaystyle t\mapsto-\log\mathbb{P}(1/(1-A)>t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad t\mapsto-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)

are regularly varying (or when either
A
or
B
is bounded), again requiring independence of
A
and
B
. For general dependent
A
and
B
, however, only a lower bound on the tail was provided.
As noted, the light tails heavily depend on the dependence structure of
(A,B)
. This relation seems to be captured quite well by a function
h
, which was defined in
[
22
]
by

h(t)=\inf_{s\geq 1}\left\{-s\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{1-A}>s,B>\frac{t}{s}\right)\right\},\qquad t>0.

There it was shown (see
[
22
, Theorem 5.1]
) that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{h(t)}\geq-c

with an explicit constant
c
depending on the index of regular variation of
h
. In Section
8
we present an example demonstrating that this lower bound is generally not optimal.

In contrast, our paper addresses this gap by deriving precise logarithmic asymptotics within a general framework built upon the local dependence measure. Our results extend and complement previous findings in light-tailed scenarios where the tail behavior of
X
is predominantly governed by the distribution of
B
rather than
A
.

In
[
5
]
a less common problem in the theory of (
1.1
) was considered. If
A
and
B
are nonnegative, then
X
, if exists, is also nonnegative. Thus, one may investigate the tail behavior of
X
near the left endpoint of the support.
One of the main results in
[
5
]
establishes that if the limit

g(y)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(A\varepsilon y+B<\varepsilon)}{\log\mathbb{P}(B<\varepsilon)}

exists for
y\in[0,\infty)
, where
\varepsilon\mapsto-\log\mathbb{P}(B<\varepsilon)
is a regularly varying function with index
\rho<0
at
0
, then—under some technical assumptions (analogous to the admissibility condition in the present paper)—one has

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X<\varepsilon)}{\log\mathbb{P}(B<\varepsilon)}=\inf_{y>1}\left\{\frac{y^{\rho}}{y^{\rho}-1}g(y)\right\}.

Another result in
[
5
]
concerns the lower envelope of the sequence (
1.2
) with
X_{0}=0
; specifically, there exists an explicit deterministic scaling sequence
(h_{n})_{n}
such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{h_{n}}

is deterministic, positive and finite.

There are clear analogies between the statements and proofs in the present paper and those in
[
5
]
. However, aside from Lemmas
5.6
and
7.5
, which are taken directly from
[
5
]
, all other results in our paper have independent proofs. Notably, our analysis reveals quantitatively different behavior for
\rho\in(0,1]
and
\rho>1
(see, e.g., Theorem
3.11
), a phenomenon that was not observed in
[
5
]
. While
[
5
]
applied the left-tail results to a Fleming–Viot-type process, in Section
8
we illustrate our findings using a new family of distributions.

We also note that the assumptions in the present paper and in
[
5
]
are compatible. Consequently, one can readily construct a distribution of
(A,B)
such that both the left and right tail behaviors of
X
satisfying (
1.1
) are asymptotically available and the sequence
X_{n}=A_{n}X_{n-1}+B_{n}
exhibits explicit lower and upper envelopes. For example, assume that the random pair
(A,B)
is positively quadrant dependent (see Section
3.1
) and that for
\rho,\sigma>0
one has

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)}{t^{\rho}}=\lambda_{+}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(B<\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{-\sigma}}=\lambda_{-}

together with

a_{-}={\mathrm{ess}}\inf(A)\geq 0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)<1.

Under these conditions, since
\mathbb{E}[\log A]<0
,
\mathbb{E}[\max\{\log B,0\}]<\infty
,
Theorems
6.1
and
3.11
in this paper together with
[
5
, Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 5.4]
yield

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{t^{\rho}}=c_{+}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X<\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{-\sigma}}=c_{-},

where

c_{+}=\lambda_{+}\begin{cases}\left(1-a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\right)^{\rho-1},&\mbox{if }\rho>1,\\
1,&\mbox{if }\rho\in(0,1),\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad c_{-}=\lambda_{-}\left(1-a_{-}^{\sigma/(1+\sigma)}\right)^{-(1+\sigma)}.

Moreover, if
X_{0}=0
, then by Theorem
7.1
and
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{(\log n)^{1/\rho}}=c_{+}^{-1/\rho}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{(\log n)^{-1/\sigma}}=c_{-}^{1/\sigma}.

1.2.
Organization of the paper

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we give a short review of the theory of regularly varying functions. Section
3
introduces the local dependence measure and its Legendre-type transform, and establishes their key properties. We show that when
A
and
B
are independent (or more generally, positively quadrant dependent), the LDM admits an explicit representation. In Section
4
, we derive the asymptotics of
t\mapsto\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)
under independence between
X
and
(A,B)
, where
X
does not necessarily satisfy (
1.1
). This is a result that is instrumental in controlling the asymptotics of
\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>t)
. Section
5
discusses the existence, uniqueness, and basic properties of solutions to (
1.1
). Section
6
is devoted to our first main result, the precise logarithmic tail asymptotics of the solution to (
1.1
). In Section
7
we prove our second main result: the identification of the upper envelope for the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
X_{0}=0
. Finally, in Section
8
we describe a family of distributions of pairs
(A,B)
for which the LDM is computable and provide its general form. We use our earlier results to demonstrate that the lower bound in
[
22
]
is not optimal in general.

2.
Regular variation

We write
f(x)\sim g(x)
if
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
.
A measurable function
f\colon(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to be regularly varying of index
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
f(\lambda x)\sim\lambda^{\rho}f(x)
for all
\lambda>0
.

The class of regularly varying functions of index
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
. In particular, elements of the class
\mathcal{R}^{0}
are called slowly varying functions.

We gather all the necessary properties of regularly varying functions in the lemma below.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
with
\rho>0
. Then:

(i)

f(x)=x^{\rho}\ell(x)
for some slowly varying function
\ell\in\mathcal{R}^{0}
.

(ii)

\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty
.

(iii)

For any
C>1
and
\delta>0
, there exists
K
such that for
x,y\geq K
,

\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}\leq C\max\left\{\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\rho+\delta},\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\rho-\delta}\right\}.

(iv)

There exists
g\in\mathcal{R}^{1/\rho}
such that

f(g(x))\sim g(f(x))\sim x

and
g
is determined uniquely to within asymptotic equivalence
\sim
.

(v)

There exists
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
such that
\tilde{f}
continuous, strictly increasing and
\tilde{f}(x)\sim f(x)
.

Proof.

(i) follows directly from the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
.
(ii) is proved in
[
2
, Proposition 1.5.1]
,
(iii) is known as the Potter bounds,
[
2
, Theorem 1.5.6]
.
(iv) is proved in
[
2
, Theorem 1.5.12]
.
For (v), fix
X>0
so large that
f
is locally bounded on [X,
\infty
) and define
\tilde{f}(x)=\rho\int_{X}^{x}t^{-1}f(t)\mathrm{d}t
for
x\in(X,\infty)
. Extend the definition of
\tilde{f}
to
(0,X]
in a way that it is continuous and strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
. By Karamata’s Theorem,
[
2
, Theorem 1.5.11]
, we have
\tilde{f}(x)\sim f(x)
.
∎

3.
Exponential decay and local dependence measure

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f
be a function defined on a neighborhood of infinity such that
f(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.
We say that a nonnegative random variable
X
has an exponential-
f
-decay tail if

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}=\lambda,

where
\lambda\in[0,\infty]
. We call such a random variable an
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\lambda)
-random variable.

We note that if
X
is a bounded random variable, then it is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\infty)
for any
f
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(A,B)
be a pair of nonnegative random variables, and let
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
with
\rho>0
. A function
g\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to be the local dependence measure of
(A,B)
(denoted
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}
), if the limit

g(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)},\qquad y\in[0,\infty)

exists.

In general, for an arbitrary pair
(A,B)
and a function
f
, the existence of
g
on
[0,\infty)
is not guaranteed. Nevertheless, we will show that
g(y)=0
for
y>a_{+}^{-1}
, where

a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A).

Furthermore, if
A
and
B
are positively quadrant dependent (PQD) and
g(0)<\infty
, then
g
exists on
[0,\infty)
and its explicit form can be derived—it depends only on
a_{+}
and
g(0)
( see Section
3.1
for details). Another family of distributions for which an explicit expression for
g
is available is presented in Section
8
.

Assume that
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
is such that
\tilde{f}
continuous, strictly increasing and
\tilde{f}(x)\sim f(x)
.
Clearly, we have
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ED}_{\tilde{f}}(\lambda)
and
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}=\mathrm{LDM}_{\tilde{f}}^{\rho}
. In view of Lemma
2.1
, without loss of generality, we will assume from now on that every regularly varying function
f
is continuous and strictly increasing.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(A,B)
are nonnegative random variables and
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
with
\rho>0
.
Suppose
g(0)
exists and
a_{+}>0
. Then:

(i)

For every
y>a_{+}^{-1}
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}=0.

(ii)

For every
y\in[0,a_{+}^{-1})
,

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\geq g(0)(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}.

(iii)

If
y>x\geq 0
and both
g(x)
and
g(y)
exists, then
g(y)\leq g(x)
,

Proof.

(i)

Assume
y>a_{+}^{-1}
. Clearly,
\mathbb{P}(A>1/y)>0
. Notice that
\{A>1/y\}=\{Aty>t\}\subset\{Aty+B>t\}
for
t>0
and therefore,

0\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\\
\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(A>1/y)}{f(t)}=0.

(ii)

Since
\mathbb{P}(A\leq a_{+})=1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\leq\mathbb{P}(a_{+}ty+B>t)=\mathbb{P}(B>t(1-a_{+}y)).

Thus, for
y\in[0,1/a_{+})
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t(1-a_{+}y))}{f(t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t(1-a_{+}y))}{f(t(1-a_{+}y))}\cdot\frac{f(t(1-a_{+}y))}{f(t)}

\displaystyle=g(0)(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}.

(iii)

Let
y>x\geq 0
. We note that since
A
is a nonnegative random variable, for all
t>0
, we have the inclusion
\{Atx+B>t\}\subset\{Aty+B>t\}
. By the monotonicity of
\mathbb{P}
and the assumption that both
g(x)
and
g(y)
are well defined we get that
g(y)\leq g(x)
.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Lemma
3.3
implies several key properties of the local dependence measure
g
:

•

On the interval
(a_{+}^{-1},\infty)
, the limit always exists and equals
0
.

•

If
g
is well defined on
[0,a_{+}^{-1})
, then it is bounded below by
g(0)(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}
on this interval.

•

If
g
exists on the entire
[0,\infty)
, then it is a nonincreasing function.

In Section
3.1
, we show that the lower bound is attained for positively quadrant dependent
A
and
B
.

Definition 3.5
.

For a function
g\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
, let
\phi_{\rho}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be a Legendre-type transform defined by

\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)=\inf_{y>0}\left\{y^{\rho}\lambda+g(y)\right\},\qquad\lambda\in[0,\infty).

From this point forward, unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume that
g
exists on
[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
g
be the
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}
for the nonnegative random variables
(A,B)
such that
a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)>0
, where
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
and
\rho>0
. Let
\phi_{\rho}
be its Legendre-type transform. Then:

(i)

\phi_{\rho}
is finite, nondecreasing, concave.

(ii)

\phi_{\rho}(0)=\phi_{\rho}(0^{+})=0
.

(iii)

\phi_{\rho}
is continuous.

(iv)

\sup_{y>0}\left\{g(y)\right\}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)
.

(v)

If there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)>\lambda
, then
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)=\lambda
for at most one
\lambda>0
.

(vi)

With
\gamma=g(0)
and
a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)>0
,

\displaystyle\lambda a^{-\rho}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)\geq\begin{cases}\left(\gamma^{1/(1-\rho)}+a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\lambda^{1/(1-\rho)}\right)^{1-\rho},&\mbox{ if }\rho>1,\\
\min\left\{\gamma,\lambda a_{+}^{-\rho}\right\},&\mbox{ if }0<\rho\leq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i)

Finiteness follows from Lemma
3.3
(i), monotonicity is obvious,
\phi_{\rho}
as the point-wise infimum of a family of affine functions is concave.

(ii)

We have
\phi_{\rho}(0)=\inf_{y>0}\left\{g(y)\right\}=0
by Lemma
3.3
(i). Moreover, by the definition of
\phi_{\rho}
we get

\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)\leq y^{\rho}\lambda+g(y)
for any
y>0
. Thus, by taking
y
sufficiently large (recall Lemma
3.3
(i)) and letting
\lambda\downarrow 0^{+}
, we obtain
\phi_{\rho}(0^{+})\leq 0
.

(iii)

From (ii) we get continuity in
0
. Continuity on
(0,\infty)
is a consequence of the fact that
\phi_{\rho}
is concave, which was stated in (i).

(iv)

By the definition of
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)
, we have
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)\leq y^{\rho}\lambda+g(y)
for all
y>0
. Letting
y\downarrow 0^{+}
(noting that
g
is a nonincreasing function, so its limit as
y\to 0^{+}
exists, although it may be infinite), we obtain
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)\leq g(0^{+})=\sup_{y>0}\{g(y)\}
.

(v)

Define
\psi(\lambda)=\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda
. Then
\psi
is concave, continuous, and
\psi(0)=0
. Suppose there exist
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
with
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda_{1})=\lambda_{1}
and
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda_{2})=\lambda_{2}
, so that
\psi(0)=\psi(\lambda_{1})=\psi(\lambda_{2})=0
. By concavity,
\psi\equiv 0
on
[0,\lambda_{2}]
and
\psi(\lambda)\leq 0
for
\lambda>\lambda_{2}
. Now, if some
\lambda_{0}
satisfies
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda_{0})>\lambda_{0}
(i.e.,
\psi(\lambda_{0})>0
), then we obtain a contradiction. Hence, the equation
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)=\lambda
can have at most one solution for
\lambda>0
.

(vi)

The upper and lower bounds on
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)
are based on properties (i) and (ii) from Lemma
3.3
. The derivation of these bounds follows from the proof of Theorem
3.11
.

∎

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}
function
g
is admissible if either:
there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)>\lambda
or
g(0)=0
(in which case
\phi_{\rho}\equiv 0
).

The notion of admissibility is introduced to exclude those distributions of
(A,B)
and choices of the function
f
for which our analytical framework fails (see Lemma
6.3
). To illustrate, consider the following example. Assume that the nonnegative random variables
A
and
B
are independent,
a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)\in(0,1]
and
f(t)=-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)
is regularly varying with index
-\rho<0
. Then, by Theorem
3.11
,
g
exists and is given by
g(y)=\max\{1-a_{+}y,0\}^{\rho}
for
y\geq 0
. In this case, one can verify that the admissibility condition holds.

In contrast, consider the same model but with the choice
f(t)=t^{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in(0,\rho)
. In this setting, the
\mathrm{LDM}
is given by

g(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{t^{\sigma}}=\begin{cases}\infty,&\mbox{ if }y<a_{+}^{-1},\\
0,&\mbox{ if }y>a_{+}^{-1},\end{cases}

for any
\sigma\in(0,\rho)
. A direct calculation shows that the corresponding Legendre-type transform satisfies
\phi_{\sigma}(\lambda)=\lambda
for all
\lambda>0
, thereby violating the condition
\phi_{\sigma}(\lambda)>\lambda
. Consequently, this LDM is non-admissible.

Definition 3.8
.

For a
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}
function
g
, we define

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\ast}=\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{\frac{g(y)}{1-y^{\rho}}\right\}.

Lemma 3.9
.

(i)

Assume
c\geq 0
. Then,
\phi_{\rho}(c)\geq c
if and only if
c\leq\lambda^{\ast}
.

(ii)

If
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
, then
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda^{\ast})=\lambda^{\ast}
.

(iii)

With
\gamma=g(0)
and
a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)>0
,

\lambda^{\ast}\geq\begin{cases}\gamma\left(1-a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\right)^{\rho-1},&\mbox{ if }\rho>1,\\
\gamma,&\mbox{ if }0<\rho\leq 1.\end{cases}

(iv)

\lambda^{\ast}=\infty\iff g(0)=\infty
.

Proof.

(i)

We have

\displaystyle\phi_{\rho}(c)\geq c

\displaystyle\iff\inf_{y>0}\left\{y^{\rho}c+g(y)\right\}\geq c\iff\forall\,y>0\,\,\,y^{\rho}c+g(y)\geq c

\displaystyle\iff\forall\,y\in(0,1)\,\,\,y^{\rho}c+g(y)\geq c\iff c\leq\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{\frac{g(y)}{1-y^{\rho}}\right\}.

(ii)

Let
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
be a sequence such that
\lambda_{n}\downarrow\lambda^{\ast}
. Then, by (i) we have
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda_{n})<\lambda_{n}
and by continuity of
\phi_{\rho}
, we obtain
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda^{\ast})\leq\lambda^{\ast}
. Setting
c=\lambda^{\ast}
in (i), we obtain reversed bound.

(iii)

The lower bound on
\lambda^{\ast}
quickly follows from the lower bound on
g
in Lemma
3.3
(ii).

(iv)

If
g(0)=\infty
, then by (iii) we obtain
\lambda^{\ast}\geq\infty
. If
\lambda^{\ast}=\infty
, then by definition of
\lambda^{\ast}
, we have for any
y\in(0,1)
,
g(y)\geq(1-y^{\rho})\lambda^{\ast}
. Since
g
is nonincreasing,
g(0)=\infty
.

∎

3.1.
Positive quadrant dependence

Definition 3.10
.

We say that random variables
A
and
B
are positively quadrant dependent (PQD for short) if, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A>a,B>b)\geq\mathbb{P}(A>a)\mathbb{P}(B>b).

It turns out that for a PQD pair
(A,B)
, the
\mathrm{LDM}
always exists
[0,\infty)
and, moreover, has explicit form.

Recall that
a_{+}={\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)=\inf\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathbb{P}(A>x)=0\right\}
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume that nonnegative random variables
A
and
B
are PQD. Suppose that
\gamma:=g(0)
exists and is finite for some
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
with
\rho>0
. Then, the local dependence measure
g
of
(A,B)
exists on
[0,\infty)
. Suppose
a_{+}>0
.

(i)

For
y\geq 0
,

\displaystyle g(y)=\gamma\max\{1-a_{+}y,0\}^{\rho}.

(ii)

If
a_{+}\in(0,1]
, then for
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(\gamma^{1/(1-\rho)}+a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\lambda^{1/(1-\rho)}\right)^{1-\rho},&\mbox{ if }\rho>1,\\
\min\left\{\gamma,\lambda a_{+}^{-\rho}\right\},&\mbox{ if }0<\rho\leq 1,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\lambda^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\gamma\left(1-a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\right)^{\rho-1},&\mbox{ if }\rho>1,\\
\gamma,&\mbox{ if }0<\rho\leq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i)

By Lemma
3.3
(ii), for
y\in[0,a_{+}^{-1})
we have

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\geq\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}.

Since we assume that
g(0)
is finite, the above inequality extends to
y=a_{+}^{-1}
.

We now show that for
y\in[0,a_{+}^{-1}]
, the quantity
\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}
is an upper bound for the superior limit.
Fix
\delta\in(0,a_{+})
. Then, if
0\leq y\leq a_{+}^{-1}<(a_{+}-\delta)^{-1}
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\geq\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t,A>a_{+}-\delta)\geq\mathbb{P}((a_{+}-\delta)ty+B>t,A>a_{+}-\delta)\\
\geq\mathbb{P}(A>a_{+}-\delta)\,\mathbb{P}(B>t(1-(a_{+}-\delta)y)),

where we have used the fact that
A
and
B
are PQD.
Therefore,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(A>a_{+}-\delta)-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t(1-(a_{+}-\delta)y))}{f(t)}

Since
f(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, and from the fact that
\mathbb{P}(A>a_{+}-\delta)>0
, we obtain

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\\
\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t(1-(a_{+}-\delta)y))}{f(t(1-(a_{+}-\delta)y))}\cdot\frac{f(t(1-(a_{+}-\delta)y))}{f(t)}=\gamma(1-(a_{+}-\delta)y)^{\rho}.

By letting
\delta\downarrow 0^{+}
, we conclude that for
0\leq y\leq a_{+}^{-1}
,

\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\\
\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\leq\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}.

Thus,
g
exists on
[0,a_{+}^{-1}]
and is equal to
\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}
on this interval. Finally, by Remark
3.4
, for
y>a_{+}^{-1}
we have that
g(y)
exists and equals
0
. This completes the proof of (i).

(ii)

Since
g(y)=0
for
y\geq a_{+}^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y>0}\left\{\lambda y^{\rho}+g(y)\right\}=\inf_{y\in(0,a_{+}^{-1})}\left\{\lambda y^{\rho}+g(y)\right\}=\inf_{y\in(0,a_{+}^{-1})}\left\{h_{1}(y)\right\},

where
h_{1}\colon(0,a_{+}^{-1})\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
h_{1}(y)=\lambda y^{\rho}+\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}
.

If
0<\rho<1
, then
h_{1}
is increasing on
(0,K)
and decreasing on
(K,a_{+}^{-1})
, where
K=(a_{+}+(\tfrac{a_{+}\gamma}{\lambda})^{1/(1-\rho)})^{-1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\inf_{(0,a_{+}^{-1})}\left\{h_{1}(y)\right\}=\min\left\{h_{1}(0^{+}),h_{1}((a_{+}^{-1})^{-})\right\}=\min\left\{\gamma,\lambda a_{+}^{-\rho}\right\}.

The case
\rho\in(0,1)
easily extends to
\rho=1
.

If
\rho>1
, then
h_{1}
is decreasing on
(0,K)
and increasing on
(K,a_{+}^{-1})
, where
K
is as before.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\inf_{(0,a_{+}^{-1})}\left\{h_{1}(y)\right\}=h_{1}(K)=\frac{\lambda\gamma}{\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{\rho-1}}+a_{+}^{\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}}\gamma^{\frac{1}{\rho-1}}\right)^{\rho-1}}=\left(\gamma^{1/(1-\rho)}+a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\lambda^{1/(1-\rho)}\right)^{1-\rho}.

Moreover, it is easy to see that
h_{1}(K)\leq\lambda a_{+}^{-\rho}
.

We have

\displaystyle\lambda^{\ast}=\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{\frac{g(y)}{1-y^{\rho}}\right\}=\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{h_{2}(y)\right\},

where
h_{2}\colon(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
h_{2}(y)=\gamma(1-a_{+}y)^{\rho}/(1-y^{\rho})
.
If
\rho\in(0,1]
, then
h_{2}
is increasing on
(0,1)
and therefore

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{h_{2}(y)\right\}=h_{2}(0^{+})=\gamma.

If
\rho>1
, then
h_{2}
is increasing on
(a_{+}^{1/(\rho-1)},1)
and decreasing on
(0,a_{+}^{1/(\rho-1)})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{h_{2}(y)\right\}=h_{2}\left(a_{+}^{1/(\rho-1)}\right)=\gamma\left(1-a_{+}^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\right)^{\rho-1}.

∎

4.
Tails of
AX+B

Recall our standing assumption that
\mathbb{P}(A>0)>0
. In this section,
X
is assumed to be independent of
(A,B)
but does not necessarily satisfy (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
g
be the
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}
for the nonnegative random variables
(A,B)
, where
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
with
\rho>0
.
Suppose
X
is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\lambda)
with
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
. Then
AX+B
is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda))
.

Proof.

First, we prove that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)}{f(t)}\leq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda).

For any
y>0
and
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X>ty)\geq\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t,X>ty)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\,\mathbb{P}(X>ty).

Thus,

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)}{f(t)}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty)}{f(t)}}

\displaystyle\leq g(y)+\limsup_{t\to\infty}{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty)}{f(ty)}\cdot\frac{f(ty)}{f(t)}}=g(y)+\lambda y^{\rho}.

By taking
\inf_{y>0}
on both sides, we obtain (
4.1
).

Next, we establish the lower bound,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)}{f(t)}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda).

If
\lambda=0
, then by Lemma
3.6
(ii), we have
\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)=0
, and the above inequality is trivial. Now suppose
\lambda>0
. Since
\phi_{\rho}
is finite, there exists
a>0
such that
\lambda a^{\rho}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)
. We note that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\liminf_{t\to\infty}&\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X>ta)}{f(t)}\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ta)}{f(t)}\\
&=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ta)}{f(ta)}\cdot\frac{f(ta)}{f(t)}=\lambda a^{\rho}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda).\end{split}

Moreover, by Lemma
3.6
(iv), we have
\sup_{y>0}\{g(y)\}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)
.
Fix
\eta>0
. There exists
b\in(0,a)
such that
g(b)\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\eta/2
.
We have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X\leq tb)}{f(t)}&\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Atb+B>t)}{f(t)}=g(b)\\
&\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\frac{\eta}{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we deduce that there exists
M>0
such that for all
t>M
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X>ta)+\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X\leq tb)\leq 2\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\eta)).

Now, fix
y,h>0
such that
h<y
. Then,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,t(y-h)<X\leq ty)&\leq\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t,t(y-h)<X)\\
&=\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\,\mathbb{P}(X>t(y-h)).\end{split}

Since

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t(y-h))}{f(t)}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t(y-h))}{f(t(y-h))}}\cdot\frac{f(t(y-h))}{f(t)}=\lambda(y-h)^{\rho},

for sufficiently large
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X>t(y-h))\leq\exp(-f(t)(\lambda(y-h)^{\rho}-\eta)).

If
g(y)<\infty
, then, from the definition of
g
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\exists\,M>0\mbox{ such that }\forall\,t>M\quad\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\leq\exp(-f(t)(g(y)-\eta)).

Thus, from (
4.5
), we get for sufficiently large
t
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,\text{ }t(y-h)<X\leq ty)\leq\exp(-f(t)(g(y)+\lambda(y-h)^{\rho}-2\eta)).\end{split}

If
g(y)=\infty
, then for any
G>0
there exists
N>0
such that for all
t>N
, we obtain a bound

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,\text{ }t(y-h)<X\leq ty)\leq\exp(-f(t)(G+\lambda(y-h)^{\rho})).

We now consider two cases:
(i)
\rho\geq 1
and (ii)
\rho\in(0,1)
.

(i) By the convexity of
(0,\infty)\ni x\mapsto x^{\rho}
, we obtain

\lambda y^{\rho}-\lambda(y-h)^{\rho}\leq\lambda h\rho y^{\rho-1}.

Using (
4.6
), the definition of
\phi_{\rho}
, and the above inequality, we conclude that if
g(y)<\infty
, then for all
y>h>0
and sufficiently large
t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,t(y-h)<X\leq ty)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-f(t)(g(y)+\lambda y^{\rho}-\lambda y^{\rho}+\lambda(y-h)^{\rho}-2\eta))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho y^{\rho-1}-2\eta)).

The same bound holds when
g(y)=\infty
by choosing
G
sufficiently large in (
4.7
).

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
h_{0}=0
,
h_{k}-h_{k-1}=\frac{a-b}{n}=h
for
k=1,\ldots,n
. Then for sufficiently large
t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,tb<X\leq ta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,t(a-h_{k})<X\leq t(a-h_{k-1}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho(a-h_{k-1})^{\rho-1}-2\eta))}

\displaystyle\leq n\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho a^{\rho-1}-2\eta)).

Thus, for sufficiently large
t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X\in t(b,a])+\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X\notin t(b,a])

\displaystyle\leq n\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho a^{\rho-1}-2\eta))+2\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\eta))

\displaystyle\leq(n+2){\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho a^{\rho-1}-2\eta))}.

Therefore, for sufficiently large
t
,

\displaystyle\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)}{f(t)}\geq\frac{-\log(n+2)}{f(t)}+\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho a^{\rho-1}-2\eta.

Taking
\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}
on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)}{f(t)}\geq\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h\rho a^{\rho-1}-2\eta.

By letting
n\uparrow\infty
(recall that
h=(a-b)/n
) and then
\eta\downarrow 0^{+}
, we obtain (
4.2
).

(ii) For
\rho\in(0,1)
, we have
y^{\rho}-(y-h)^{\rho}\leq h^{\rho}
for
y>h>0
.
Similarly to case (i), for all
y>h>0
and sufficiently large
t
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,t(y-h)<X\leq ty)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-f(t)(g(y)+\lambda y^{\rho}-\lambda y^{\rho}+\lambda(y-h)^{\rho}-2\eta))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h^{\rho}-2\eta))

and this bound also holds when
g(y)=\infty
.
Proceeding as in (i),
we obtain that for sufficiently large
t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)\leq(n+2){\exp(-f(t)(\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)-\lambda h^{\rho}-2\eta))}.

The remaining part of the proof is analogous.
∎

5.
The equation
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}AX+B

The stochastic fixed-point equation (
1.1
) associated with the affine recursion
X_{n}=A_{n}X_{n-1}+B_{n}
has been extensively studied in the literature. We summarize key results on the existence and uniqueness of the solution below,
[
3
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
\mathbb{P}(A\geq 0,B\geq 0)=1
. If

\mathbb{E}[\log A]<0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}[\max\{\log B,0\}]<\infty,

then there exists a unique solution
X
to

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}AX+B,\qquad(A,B)\mbox{ and }X\mbox{ are independent}.

Moreover,
X
is given by the a.s. convergent series representation

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}A_{k},

where
(A_{n},B_{n})_{n}
are independent copies of
(A,B)
.

Additionally, if
X_{n}=A_{n}X_{n-1}+B_{n}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
, where
X_{0}
is independent of
(A_{n},B_{n})_{n}
, then
X_{n}
converges in distribution to
X
.

We say that a real random variable
Y
is stochastically majorized by
Z
and we write
Y\leq_{st}Z
if
\mathbb{P}(Y\leq t)\geq\mathbb{P}(Z\leq t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(A_{n},B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent copies of a generic pair
(A,B)
. Let
X_{0}
be independent of
(A_{n},B_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and define
X_{n}=A_{n}X_{n-1}+B_{n}
. Assume that
A\geq 0
a.s.

(i)

If
X_{1}\geq_{st}X_{0}
, then
X_{n}\geq_{st}X_{n-1}
for all
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

If
X_{1}\leq_{st}X_{0}
, then
X_{n}\leq_{st}X_{n-1}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

(i) We proceed by induction, assume that
X_{n}\geq_{st}X_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
. Then, since
A_{n+1}\geq 0
a.s. and
X_{n}
is independent of
(A_{n+1},B_{n+1})
, we obtain

X_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}A_{n+1}X_{n}+B_{n+1}\geq_{st}A_{n+1}X_{n-1}+B_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}X_{n}.

Point (ii) is proved in the same way.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

If
X_{n}
converges in distribution to
X
, then
X_{1}\geq_{st}X_{0}
implies that
X\geq_{st}X_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and if
X_{1}\leq_{st}X_{0}
, then
X\leq_{st}X_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
A\geq 0
a.s., then

\mathrm{ess}\sup(X)=\mathrm{ess}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right).

Proof.

Denote
x_{+}=\mathrm{ess}\sup(X)
and

x_{0}=\mathrm{ess}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right).

Let
g_{a,b}(t)=a\,t+b
. With

G(A,B)=\left\{g_{(a_{1},b_{1})}\circ\ldots\circ g_{(a_{n},b_{n})}\colon(a_{i},b_{i})\in\mathrm{supp}(A,B),i=1,\ldots,n,n\geq 1\right\}

by
[
3
, Proposition 2.5.3]
, we have

\mathrm{supp}(X)=\overline{\left\{\frac{b}{1-a}\colon g_{a,b}\in G(A,B),a<1\right\}}.

Thus,

\overline{\left\{\frac{b}{1-a}\colon(a,b)\in\mathrm{supp}(A,B),a<1\right\}}\subset\mathrm{supp}(X)

and therefore
x_{+}\geq x_{0}
. If
x_{0}=\infty
, then
x_{+}=\infty
and there is nothing to prove.

Assume that
x_{0}<\infty
.
By
[
3
, Lemma 2.5.1]
, for every
(a,b)\in\mathrm{supp}(A,B)
we have

a\,\mathrm{supp}(X)+b\subset\mathrm{supp}(X)

and therefore
ax_{+}+b\leq x_{+}
for all
(a,b)\in\mathrm{supp}(A,B)
. Thus,
Ax_{+}+B\leq x_{+}
a.s. and

B\mathbf{1}_{A\geq 1}\leq x_{+}(1-A)\mathbf{1}_{A\geq 1}\leq x_{0}(1-A)\mathbf{1}_{A\geq 1}\quad\mbox{a.s.},

where the latter inequality follows from the fact that
x_{+}\geq x_{0}
.

By definition of
x_{0}
we have

\displaystyle B\mathbf{1}_{A<1}

\displaystyle\leq x_{0}(1-A)\mathbf{1}_{A<1},\quad\mbox{a.s.}

and therefore
B\leq x_{0}(1-A)
a.s.
Thus,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{1}\ldots A_{k-1}B_{k}\leq x_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{1}\ldots A_{k-1}(1-A_{k})=x_{0},\quad\mbox{a.s.},

which implies that
\mathbb{P}(X\leq x_{0})=1
, i.e.,
x_{0}\geq x_{+}
.
∎

We have established above that the right endpoint of the support of
X
coincides with the right endpoint of the conditional distribution of
B/(1-A)
given
A<1
. Note that if this endpoint is finite, the asymptotic behavior of
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(X>t)
as
t\to\infty
becomes trivial, and this case should thus be excluded from further analysis. It turns out that suitable conditions involving
g(1)
or
\lambda^{\ast}
are sufficient to ensure this exclusion, but only under the assumption that
\mathbb{P}(A\in[0,1))=1
. In what follows, we explain that restricting our analysis to such distributions does not lead to any significant loss of generality.

By
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
, if
\mathbb{P}(A>1)>0
, then the tail of
X
is at least of power‐law type:

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{\log t}>-\infty.

Since this behavior lies outside the light-tail regime we consider, we henceforth restrict to the case where
A\in[0,1]
a.s. Moreover, when
\mathbb{P}(A=1)>0
, and under suitable nondegeneracy conditions,
[
1
, Theorem 1.7]
shows that the moment generating function
\mathbb{E}[\exp(tX)]
is finite if and only if

\mathbb{E}[e^{tB}\mathbf{1}_{A=1}]<1.

In addition,
[
7
, Lemma 5]
establishes that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{t}=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]<\infty\}=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathbb{E}[e^{tB}\mathbf{1}_{A=1}]<1\}=:t_{0}.

If
t_{0}<\infty
, the analysis for the case
\mathbb{P}(A=1)>0
is complete. Under
\mathbb{P}(A=1)>0
, we have
t_{0}=\infty
if and only if
\mathbb{P}(B=0\mid A=1)=1
. In this degenerate scenario, one can show that

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\tilde{A}X+\tilde{B},\qquad X\mbox{ and }(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})\mbox{ are independent},

where
(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(A,B)\mid\{A<1\}
. Indeed, for a bounded continuous function
f
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(X)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[f(AX+B)]=\mathbb{E}[f(AX+B)\mathbf{1}_{A=1}]+\mathbb{E}[f(AX+B)\mathbf{1}_{A<1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[f(X)]\,\mathbb{P}(A=1)+\mathbb{E}[f(\tilde{A}X+\tilde{B})]\,\mathbb{P}(A<1),

which immediately implies that
\mathbb{E}[f(X)]=E[f(\tilde{A}X+\tilde{B})]
.

Therefore, to avoid these solved cases, we eventually assume that
\mathbb{P}(A\in[0,1))=1
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
g
be a local dependence measure of
(A,B)
with
\mathbb{P}(A\in[0,1),B\geq 0)=1
for
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\rho}
,
\rho>0
. Then:

(i)

If
g(1)<\infty
, then
\mathrm{ess}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right)=\infty
.

(ii)

If
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
, then
g(1)<\infty
.

Proof.

(i)

First we notice that since
A<1
a.s., then
\mathrm{ess}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right)=\mathrm{ess}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\right)
. Moreover,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{B}{1-A}>t\right)}{f(t)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(At+B>t)}{f(t)}=g(1)<\infty.

Then for sufficiently large
t
we get that
\mathbb{P}(\frac{B}{1-A}>t)>0
, which proves the assertion.

(ii)

Condition
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
implies that there exists
y_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
g(y_{0})<\infty
. Since
g
is nonincreasing, we have
g(1)\leq g(y_{0})<\infty
.

∎

We will also need the following result, which was proved in
[
5
, Lemma 6.10]
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
X\overset{d}{=}AX+B
, where
X
and
(A,B)
are independent. For any bounded, uniformly continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
and any increasing positive integer sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
, a.s.,

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}f(X_{n_{k}})\geq\mathbb{E}[f(X)].

6.
Tails of
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}AX+B

Throughout this section, we assume that
\mathbb{P}(A\in[0,1))=1
(and thus
\mathbb{E}[\log A]<0
) and
\mathbb{E}[\max\{\log B,0\}]<\infty
, which implies that there exists a unique solution
X
to

X\overset{d}{=}AX+B,\qquad\mbox{$X$ and $(A,B)$ are independent}.

Recall that our standing assumption is that both
A
and
B
are nonzero. The main result in this section is as follows.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
g
be an admissible
\mathrm{LDM}_{f}^{\rho}
for the nonnegative random variables
(A,B)
, where
f\in\ \mathcal{R}^{\rho}
and
\rho>0
. Then
X
is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\lambda^{\ast})
, where
\lambda^{\ast}\in[0,\infty]
is defined in (
3.1
).

The proof of Theorem
6.1
will rely on several lemmas.

Lemma 6.2
.

If there exists
\kappa>0
such that
\kappa<\phi_{\rho}(\kappa)
, then there exists a nonnegative
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\kappa)
-random variable
Z_{\kappa}
such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle Z_{\kappa}\geq_{st}AZ_{\kappa}+B,\qquad Z_{\kappa}\mbox{ and }(A,B)\mbox{ are independent}.

Proof.

Let
Z_{0}
be a nonnegative random variable independent of
(A,B)
, with the distribution defined by (recall that, without loss of generality, we assumed that
f
is strictly increasing and continuous)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t)=\exp(-\kappa f(t)),\quad t>0.

Clearly,
Z_{0}
is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\kappa)
.

Thus, by Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(AZ_{0}+B>t)}{f(t)}=\phi_{\rho}(\kappa)>\kappa=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t)}{f(t)},

which implies that
there exists
M>0
such that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\forall\,t\geq M\,\,\,\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t)\geq\mathbb{P}(AZ_{0}+B>t).

Let
Z_{\kappa}
be a random variable, independent of
(A,B)
, with the distribution defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{\kappa}\in\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}\in\cdot\mid Z_{0}>M).

For
t\geq M
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(AZ_{\kappa}+B>t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(AZ_{0}+B>t\mid Z_{0}>M)\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}(AZ_{0}+B>t)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>M)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{(2.2)}}}{{\leq}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>M)}=\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t\mid Z_{0}>M)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{\kappa}>t),

while for
t<M
, we have
\mathbb{P}(Z_{\kappa}>t)=1\geq\mathbb{P}(AZ_{\kappa}+B>t)
. Thus,
(
6.1
) holds true.

For
t\geq M
, we have as
t\to\infty,

\displaystyle\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{\kappa}>t)}{f(t)}=\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t\mid Z_{0}>M)}{f(t)}=\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>t)+\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>M)}{f(t)}\to\kappa.

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
,

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\left(-f(t)^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)\right)\geq\lambda^{\ast}.

Proof.

The inequality holds trivially in the case
\lambda^{\ast}=0
and in the case where
X
has a bounded support (so that
{\mathrm{ess}}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right)<\infty
, recall Lemma
5.4
), in which we have
\lambda^{\ast}=\infty
.

Assume that
\lambda^{\ast}\in(0,\infty]
and
{\mathrm{ess}}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right)=\infty
, which, by Lemma
5.4
, implies that
\mathbb{P}(X>t)>0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, since
\lambda^{\ast}>0
, we must have
g(0)>0
. The admissibility of
g
ensures that there exists
\kappa>0
such that
\kappa<\phi_{\rho}(\kappa)
.
Let
Z_{\kappa}
be a random variable whose distribution is constructed in Lemma
6.2
. Let
X_{0}=Z_{\kappa}
. From (
6.1
), we obtain
X_{1}\leq_{st}X_{0}
,
which, by Corollary
5.3
, implies

\displaystyle\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}\quad\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}\geq\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>t)}{f(t)}.

Taking
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>t)}{f(t)}=\kappa.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}\geq\sup\left\{\kappa>0\colon\phi_{\rho}(\kappa)>\kappa\right\}.

By Lemma
3.9
, we have

\left\{c\geq 0\colon\phi_{\rho}(c)\geq c\right\}=\begin{cases}[0,\lambda^{\ast}],&\lambda^{\ast}<\infty,\\
[0,\infty),&\lambda^{\ast}=\infty.\end{cases}

However, by Lemma
3.6
(v), the set
\left\{c\geq 0\colon\phi_{\rho}(c)=c\right\}
contains at most two elements. Therefore,

\sup\left\{\kappa>0\colon\phi_{\rho}(\kappa)>\kappa\right\}=\sup\left\{c\geq 0\colon\phi_{\rho}(c)\geq c\right\}=\lambda^{\ast}.

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, if additionally

s=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left(-f(t)^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)\right)<\infty,

then
s\leq\lambda^{\ast}
.

Proof.

For all
t>0
and
y>0
, we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(X>t)&=\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t)\geq\mathbb{P}(AX+B>t,X>ty)\\
&\geq\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t,X>ty)=\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\mathbb{P}(X>ty).\end{split}

Thus, for
y>0
,

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty\ }\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty)}{f(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty\ }\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}+\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty)}{f(ty)}\frac{f(ty)}{f(t)}=g(y)+s\,y^{\rho}.

Now, using the assumption that
s<\infty
, we obtain
s(1-y^{\rho})\leq g(y)
.
Therefore

\displaystyle s\leq\inf_{y\in(0,1)}\left\{\frac{g(y)}{1-y^{\rho}}\right\}=\lambda^{\ast}.

∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, if additionally
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
, then
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left(-f(t)^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)\right)<\infty
.

Proof.

The assumption

\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
implies that there exists
y\in(0,1)
such that
g(y)<\infty
. Fix
\eta>0
. By the definition of
g
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\exists\,M>0\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq M\quad\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{f(t)}\leq g(y)+\eta.

Using (
6.3
), we obtain for
t>0
and
k=0,1,\ldots
,

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{k})+\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty^{k+1}+B>ty^{k})

\displaystyle\leq f(ty^{k})(g(y)+\eta),

provided
ty^{k}\geq M
.
Summing these inequalities for
k=0,\ldots,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

(6.4)

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)+\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{n+1})\leq(g(y)+\eta)\sum_{k=0}^{n}f(ty^{k}),

provided
ty^{k}\geq M
for
k=0,\ldots,n
.
Set
n=n_{t}=\left\lfloor\log(\frac{M}{t})/\log(y)\right\rfloor
. It is easy to verify that

(6.5)

\displaystyle ty^{n_{t}}\geq M\geq ty^{n_{t}+1}.

Thus, (
6.4
) implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}+\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{n_{t}+1})}{f(t)}\right\}\leq(g(y)+\eta)\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{t}}\frac{f(ty^{k})}{f(t)}.

Using Potter bounds, Lemma
2.1
(iv), for
C=2
and
\delta=\rho/2
, we conclude that for sufficiently large
t
,

\displaystyle\frac{f(ty^{k})}{f(t)}\leq 2y^{k\rho/2}.

Since
y\in(0,1)
, the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f(ty^{k})}{f(t)}
is finite as
t\to\infty
. Finally, we observe that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}+\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{n_{t}+1})}{f(t)}\\
\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\{\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(X>t)}{f(t)}+\frac{\log\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{n_{t}+1})}{f(t)}\right\}<\infty.

By (
6.5
), we obtain
\mathbb{P}(X>ty^{n_{t}+1})\geq\mathbb{P}(X>M)
.
Since
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
, by Lemma
5.5
, we have
\mathrm{ess}\sup\left(\frac{B}{1-A}\mid A<1\right)=\infty
, which, by Lemma
5.4
, implies that
\mathrm{ess}\sup(X)=\infty
.
Therefore
\mathbb{P}(X>M)>0
and the second term on the left-hand side above is
0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

If
\lambda^{\ast}=\infty
, then
X
is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\lambda^{\ast})
by Lemma
6.3
. If
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty
, then Lemma
6.3
gives the lower bound, which, by Lemma

, is the same as the upper bound of Lemma

.
∎

7.
Upper envelope for

Similarly as in the previous section, we assume that
(A,B)

\mathbb{P}(A\in[0,1))=1
and
\mathbb{E}[\max\{\log B,0\}]<\infty

to (

Let
(A_{n},B_{n})_{n\geq 1}

In view of Lemma

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
g

Lemma 7.2

Almost surely, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{f^{-1}(\log n)}\leq(\lambda^{\ast})^{-1/\rho}.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0

\delta\in(0,1)

\gamma:=(1-\delta)(1+\epsilon)>1

By Theorem
6.1
,
X
is
\mathrm{ED}_{f}(\lambda^{\ast})
. Since
\lambda^{\ast}<\infty

M>0

\displaystyle\forall\,t\geq M\,\,\,\mathbb{P}(X>t)\leq e^{-\lambda^{*}f(t)(1-\delta)}

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{f^{-1}\left(\frac{(1+\epsilon)\log n}{\lambda^{\ast}}\right)}\leq 1.

In the following lemma, we assume that

Lemma 7.3
.

For any

\displaystyle f(ty_{\ast}^{k})\leq(1+\alpha)y_{\ast}^{k(\rho-\eta)}f(t),\qquad k=0,...,n-1.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let

\displaystyle c\geq f^{-1}\left(\frac{\log k_{n+1}}{\lambda^{\ast}(1+\epsilon)}\right)y_{\ast}^{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}

In this section, we present a family of distributions of
(A,B)
, which admit an explicit
\mathrm{LDM}
, but is not PQD. Thus, allowing us to illustrate our results on explicit example outside PQD pairs. This family is indexed by a continuous, nondecreasing function
\alpha\colon[0,1)\mapsto[1,\infty)
.

Let
\rho>0
, and consider a random vector
(A,B)

whose law consists of a unique atom and an absolutely continuous component. Specifically, assume that

\mathbb{P}(A=0,B=1)=1-e^{-1}
,
and that

\mathbb{P}(A>a,B>b)=\exp\left(-\alpha(a)\beta(b)\right),\qquad a\in[0,1),b\geq 1,

with

\beta(b)=b^{\rho}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathrm{ess}}\sup(A)=1.

Since
e^{-1}=\mathbb{P}(A>0,B>1)=\exp(-\alpha(0)\beta(1))
, we deduce that
\alpha(0)=1
. In addition, one may verify that
\alpha(1^{-})=\infty
, that
\alpha(a)=-\log\mathbb{P}(A>a)
for
a\geq 0
and that
\log\mathbb{P}(B>b)=-b^{\rho}
for
b\geq 1
.

Our goal is to compute the
\mathrm{LDM}^{\rho}_{f}
function for
(A,B)
with
f(t)=-\log\mathbb{P}(B>t)=t^{\rho}
.
Note that
A
and
B
are not positively quadrant dependent, so that the results of Section
3.1
do not apply.

By Lemma
3.3
(i), we have
g(y)=0
for
y>1
. Fix
y\in[0,1)
and consider
t>1/(1-y)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{t(1-ya)}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{2}\exp(-\alpha(a)\beta(b))}{\partial a\,\partial b}\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial\exp(-\alpha(a)\beta(b))}{\partial a}\big|_{b=t(1-ya)}\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=t^{\rho}\int_{0}^{1}(1-ya)^{\rho}e^{-t^{\rho}(1-ya)^{\rho}\alpha(a)}\alpha^{\prime}(a)\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=t^{\rho}\int_{1}^{\infty}(1-y\alpha^{-1}(u))^{\rho}e^{-t^{\rho}(1-y\alpha^{-1}(u))^{\rho}u}\mathrm{d}u,

where the substitution
u=\alpha(a)
was used (with
\alpha^{-1}
denoting the generalized inverse of
\alpha
). Since
(1-y)^{\rho}\leq(1-y\alpha^{-1}(u))^{\rho}\leq 1
, we obtain

(1-y)^{\rho}t^{\rho}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-t^{\rho}(1-y\alpha^{-1}(u))^{\rho}u}\mathrm{d}u\leq\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)\leq t^{\rho}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-t^{\rho}(1-y\alpha^{-1}(u))^{\rho}u}\mathrm{d}u.

By the Laplace method, it follows that
g
exists and equals for
y\in(0,1)
,

g(y)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}(Aty+B>t)}{t^{\rho}}=\inf_{u\in[1,\infty)}\{(1-y\alpha^{-1}(u))^{\rho}u\}=\inf_{a\in[0,1)}\left\{(1-ya)^{\gamma}\alpha(a)\right\}.

We obtain

and

\phi_{\rho}(\lambda)=\inf_{y>0}\left\{y^{\rho}\lambda+g(y)\right\}\\
=\begin{cases}\min\{1,\lambda\},&\rho\in(0,1],\\
\min\left\{\inf_{a\in[0,1)}\left\{\left(\alpha(a)^{1/(1-\rho)}+a^{\rho/(\rho-1)}\lambda^{1/(1-\rho)}\right)^{1-\rho}\right\},\lambda\right\},&\rho>1.\end{cases}

Moreover, one can easily show that

=\begin{cases}\inf_{a\in[0,1)}\left\{\alpha(a)\right\}=1,&\rho\in(0,1],\\
\inf_{a\in[0,1)}\left\{\alpha(a)(1-a^{\rho/(\rho-1)})^{\rho-1}\right\},&\rho>1.\end{cases}

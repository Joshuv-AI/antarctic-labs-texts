Two compound heterozygous MEIOSIN (likely) pathogenic variants cause female infertility with premature ovarian insufficiency through the disruption of meiosis-related gene transcription
Authors: Xuan Sha, Yan Zhang, Xun Xia, Haitian Ding, Yuqian Li, Guotong Li, Rong Hua, Kuokuo Li, Rui Guo, Danli Du, Yuping Xu, Yunxia Cao, Huan Wu, Yingchun Liu

Background

Upwards of 15% of all couples in the world are impacted by infertility, which represents a pressing public health concern [1]. The definition of infertility is the failure to conceive with 12 months of routine, unprotected intercourse. Among the most common causes of female infertility is premature ovarian insufficiency (POI), characterized by menstrual disturbances, including amenorrhea or oligomenorrhea lasting for more than four months in women under 40, along with elevated follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) levels and reduced estrogen concentrations [2]. POI affects ~ 1% of women aged 30–40, 0.1% of those less than 30, and 0.01% of those less than 20 [3]. The condition has diverse etiologies, including genetic defects (accounting for 25–30% of cases), autoimmune disorders (10–15%), iatrogenic causes, environmental exposures, and idiopathic factors [1, 4–7]. Despite these known contributors, a substantial proportion of POI cases remain unexplained. While POI impacts 1–5% of women of childbearing age [8], spontaneous pregnancies occur in approximately 3.5% of patients with amenorrhea or oligomenorrhea [9]. Moreover, POI has been linked to reduced lifespan and increased all-cause mortality risk in affected individuals [10, 11].

Advancements in whole-exome sequencing (WES) have expanded the spectrum of genetic variants associated with POI, particularly in individuals with a familial predisposition [1, 7]. In mammals, germ cell fate is determined by signals from neighboring somatic cells following the migration of primordial germ cells to the gonads during fetal development [12]. In female embryos, meiotic entry is regulated by key signaling pathways, including retinoic acid (RA), bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) family members, and the Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis [13–15]. The BMP pathway modulates the expression of meiotic genes, while Wnt/β-catenin signaling governs the timing of meiosis initiation in female primordial germ cells [14, 16]. Additionally, negative regulators such as DMRT1 and polycomb repressive complex 1 (PRC1) fine-tune meiotic timing to ensure coordinated oogenesis [17, 18]. RA, an active vitamin A metabolite, plays a pivotal role in cellular differentiation and promotes the expression of key meiotic transcription factors [19]. These factors control the mitosis-to-meiosis transition, regulating essential meiotic processes including homologous chromosome pairing, synapsis, and recombination during prophase I [20]. Recent studies have identified MEIOSIN as a master regulator of male and female germ cell meiotic initiation [21]. Variants in MEIOSIN can impair oocyte development, leading to accelerated ovarian aging, reduced ovarian reserve, and premature follicular depletion—hallmarks of POI [22].

Here, two MEIOSIN variants—c.980T > C (p.Leu327Pro) and c.1068_1092del (p.His356fs*14)—were identified in two affected siblings. Functional analysis revealed that the frameshift variant MEIOSIN1068_1092del results in the formation of a truncated protein with altered intracellular distribution. Conversely, the missense variant MEIOSIN980T> C was found not to affect protein expression or localization but to instead significantly alter the three-dimensional (3D) structure of the protein. Both variants compromised MEIOSIN’s transcriptional activity, disrupting the regulation of meiosis-related genes. These findings highlight the pathogenicity of MEIOSIN variants and further establish biallelic MEIOSIN variants as a recurrent genetic cause of POI, thereby expanding the known genetic landscape of the disorder.

Materials and methods

Participant information

Two female patients diagnosed with POI were recruited from the Reproductive Medicine Center at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University, China. Blood was collected from the patients and their family members, including parents and siblings. Peripheral blood was drawn into EDTA tubes, and genomic DNA extraction was performed for genetic analysis. All participants gave written informed consent before sample collection, as per ethical guidelines. The study protocol, which was consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki, received approval from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University Ethics Committee.

Whole-exome and sanger sequencing

Peripheral blood was processed using the QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Germany) to extract genomic DNA. DNA quality and concentration were quantified via NanoDrop spectrophotometry and agarose gel electrophoresis. Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was conducted on the BGISEQ-500 platform. Raw sequencing reads were processed using FastQC and aligned to the GRCh38/hg38 reference genome. The Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) was used for variant calling, and identified variants were annotated with ANNOVAR. Candidate variants were validated via Sanger sequencing using an ABI 3730XL Analyzer. PCR primers were designed using Primer3, and the QIAquick PCR Purification Kit (QIAGEN) was employed for the purification of amplification products. Chromatograms were assessed using Chromas software to confirm sequence accuracy and reproducibility.

Plasmid preparation

The complementary DNA (cDNA) sequences encoding both wild-type (WT) and mutant forms of MEIOSIN (c.980T > C and c.1068_1092del) were synthesized and inserted into the pcDNA3.1-Flag expression vector, with the FLAG epitope fused to the N-terminus of the MEIOSIN protein. To ensure sequence fidelity, all plasmid constructs underwent verification through Sanger sequencing.

Cell culture and transfection

HEK293T cells were cultured in DMEM (High Glucose; Biochannel, BC-M-005), with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Biochannel, BC-SE-FBS01) and 100 IU/mL penicillin-streptomycin solution (Biosharp, BL505A) in a 37 °C humidified 5% CO2 incubator. Transient transfection was carried out with Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, L3000015) in accordance with the provided instructions.

Western blotting

Western blotting was performed following previously established methodologies [23]. In brief, total protein extracts were obtained from transfected cells using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013B) containing protease (Beyotime, P1005) and phosphatase inhibitors (Beyotime, P1086). Protein samples were denatured, resolved on 8% SDS-polyacrylamide gels, and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Merck Millipore). After the use of 5% non-fat milk for blocking, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies: anti-DDDDK (MBL, PM020; 1:2000) and anti-GAPDH (Proteintech, 60004-1-Ig; 1:3000). HRP-conjugated secondary goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) (Elabscience, E-AB-1003; 1:5000) and goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) (Elabscience, E-AB-1001; 1:5000), were applied, and a chemiluminescence imaging system (Tanon, Shanghai, China) was used for visualization.

Immunofluorescence staining

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining was conducted according to established protocols [24]. HEK293T cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Servicebio, G1101-500ML) for 20 min, followed by 0.2% Triton X-100 (Biosharp, BS084-500 ml) permeabilization for 20 min. After blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (Sigma-Aldrich, 810531) for 2 h, cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (MBL, PM020, 1:2000). Following three washes with PBS, samples were incubated with secondary Alexa Fluor™ 555-conjugated donkey anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) (Thermo Fisher, A-31572) for 2 h at room temperature. Nuclear counterstaining was performed using Hoechst 33,342 (Beyotime, C1025), followed by the use of an LSM 980 confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss AG) for imaging.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Reporter constructs containing promoter regions of PRDM9, SPO11, and SYCE3 were cloned into ProDuoLuci vectors (Wuhan BioEagle Biological Technology & Science, China). HEK293T cells, seeded in 12-well plates, were co-transfected with 0.25 µg of either mock vector, WT MEIOSIN, or mutant MEIOSIN plasmids, as well as 0.25 µg of the respective pProDuoLuci reporter construct. Following incubation for 36 h, luciferase activity was quantified with the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). Firefly luciferase activity levels were normalized against Renilla luciferase activity, with the mock vector serving as the control.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9. P < 0.05 was deemed significant. All assays were conducted independently at least three times to ensure reliability and reproducibility. Data are means ± standard deviation unless otherwise noted.

Results

Identification of two compound heterozygous MEIOSIN variants in two siblings with POI

WES and bioinformatics analyses were performed to identify potential causative variants in two siblings diagnosed with POI. Initially, WES identified 51,395 and 50,134 raw variants in the probands, respectively. Sequential filtering was applied as follows: (1) quality control (depth ≥ 30×, quality score ≥ 50, exclusion of low-confidence variants using GATK), resulting in 38,219 and 36,411 variants, respectively; (2) variant allele frequency optimization, with a threshold of ≥ 30% to balance the detection of heterozygous and homozygous states while minimizing sequencing and mosaicism errors; (3) pathogenicity assessment, prioritizing both biallelic (homozygous/compound heterozygous) and monoallelic variants under autosomal recessive and dominant models, which led to the identification of 633 variants across 597 genes and 670 variants across 617 genes, respectively; (4) functional impact filtering, where non-synonymous variants predicted to be pathogenic by bioinformatics tools were selected; and (5) population frequency exclusion, eliminating variants with a minor allele frequency > 0.1% in the 1000 Genomes, ExAC, and gnomAD databases. This filtering process resulted in the identification of 16 candidate variants in 8 genes (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). Priority was given to genes harboring rare variants with ovarian-specific expression or those with a known role in mammalian reproduction (Supplementary Table 3). Ultimately, two compound heterozygous variants in MEIOSIN were identified in the affected siblings. The first, NC_000019.10:g.45757245T > C (NM_001310124.2:c.980T > C; p.Leu327Pro), is a missense variant that is extremely rare in the general population, with an allele frequency of 4.06 × 10⁻⁶ in TOPMed (rs1305989192). The second, NC_000019.10: g.45758933_45758957del (NM_001310124.2: c.1068_1092del; p.His356Profs*14), is a novel frameshift variant that has not been previously documented in any public variant databases (Fig. 1A). Evolutionary conservation analysis confirmed that the variant amino acid residues are highly conserved across species (Fig. 1B). These variants are exceedingly rare in major genomic databases and were predicted to be highly pathogenic through computational tools (Table 1). Based on the above genetic, evolutionary, and functional evidence, according to the ACMG/AMP guidelines, the frameshift variant c.1068_1092del (p.His356fs*14) was classified as pathogenic based on the criteria PVS1 (null variant in a gene where loss of function is a known disease mechanism), PM2 (absent or extremely rare in population databases), and PP3 (multiple computational predictions supporting a deleterious effect). The missense variant c.980T > C (p.Leu327Pro) was classified as likely pathogenic based on PM2, PP3, and PS3, supported by functional assays demonstrating impaired MEIOSIN transcriptional activity.

MEIOSIN-mutant patient characterization

Both patients exhibited a normal 46,XX karyotype. The elder sister, who experienced menarche at 12, received a diagnosis of primary amenorrhea secondary to hypogonadism at age 23 and required estrogen-progestin therapy for menstrual regulation. She has been married for three years and has been unable to conceive for the past two years. The younger sister, with menarche at 14, received a similar diagnosis at age 21. Despite continuous attempts to conceive for over a year, she remains infertile. She is currently receiving estrogen–progestin replacement therapy to maintain regular menstrual cycles. Hormonal profiling revealed elevated FSH and LH levels, coupled with markedly reduced AMH concentrations. Ultrasonographic examination revealed a normal-sized uterus and small, follicle-deficient ovaries in both patients (Table 1).

Two MEIOSIN variants result in structural abnormalities, truncation, and mislocalization

To evaluate the structural impact of the c.980T > C variant, AlphaFold modeling was employed (Fig. 2B). The substitution of leucine with proline introduced a cyclic side chain that restricted backbone flexibility, potentially compromising MEIOSIN function. The frameshift variant (p.His356Profs*14) introduces a premature stop codon, which may lead to the production of a truncated protein consisting of 369 amino acids. To further investigate the functional consequences of these variants, WB and IF assays were conducted in HEK293T cells transfected with Flag-tagged MEIOSIN980T> C or MEIOSIN1068_1092del. WB revealed that MEIOSIN1068_1092del led to protein truncation, consistent with the predicted molecular weight of the MEIOSINHis356fs mutant (~ 42 kDa), whereas MEIOSIN980T> C had no adverse effect on MEIOSIN expression (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Fig. 1). IF staining-based analyses of intracellular MEIOSIN localization revealed that the WT version of this protein exhibited perinuclear localization, a pattern that was also observed for the protein encoded by MEIOSIN980T> C. However, the truncated MEIOSIN1068_1092del protein exhibited abnormal nuclear accumulation with a punctate distribution pattern, distinct from the diffuse perinuclear localization observed for the WT protein (Fig. 2C). These results suggest that the truncated protein may be functionally impaired due to its mislocalization. Overall, these findings demonstrate that these MEIOSIN variants induce structural alterations, disrupt expression, or alter subcellular localization, ultimately influencing reproductive function.

MEIOSIN980T> C and MEIOSIN1068_1092del impair MEIOSIN transcription factor activity

Both altered amino acids detected in this study are positioned upstream of the high-mobility group (HMG) domain of MEIOSIN, a crucial DNA-binding domain essential for its transcription factor activity [25]. To determine whether these variants affect MEIOSIN’s transcriptional regulatory function, a dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed. PRDM9, a meiosis-specific histone H3 methyltransferase, is a key determinant of recombination hotspots, whereas the meiosis-specific endonuclease SPO11 initiates meiotic DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs). SYCE3, a structural synaptonemal complex component, is crucial for homologous chromosome synapsis. As MEIOSIN is a direct regulator of these genes, they were chosen as reporter genes in this assay. These findings demonstrated that both mutant MEIOSIN proteins significantly decreased the transcriptional activation of PRDM9, SPO11, and SYCE3 compared to the WT protein (Fig. 2D and Supplementary Fig. 2), demonstrating that these variants impair MEIOSIN’s ability to promote the transcription of essential meiotic genes.

Discussion

To date, only a single homozygous MEIOSIN variant has been reported in association with POI, although the patient also carried a homozygous splice-site variant in STRA8, which may have contributed to the development of POI [22]. Indeed, further studies are required to definitively implicate the causal role of MEIOSIN in POI. In this study, two compound heterozygous variants in MEIOSIN were identified in two siblings with POI, one of which is novel. Functional analyses, including protein structural modeling, demonstrated that these variant sequences lead to altered 3D conformation, protein truncation, and mislocalization. Furthermore, functional assays revealed the significantly reduced transcriptional activity of the mutant MEIOSIN proteins, confirming their pathogenic impact. These findings broaden the spectrum of MEIOSIN variants and provide new insights into the genetic etiology of POI.

Meiotic initiation in oocytes is a highly orchestrated process controlled by multifactorial regulation. Once primordial germ cells migrate to the developing ovary, DAZL is expressed in these cells, which primes them for responsivity to ovarian signals and entry into oogenesis [26]. The RSPO1/WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway is crucial for meiotic entry timing and sex differentiation [27, 28]. Concurrently, BMP signaling and the ZGLP1 transcription factor engaged downstream facilitate the activation of critical genes for meiotic prophase initiation. Retinoic acid enhances meiotic gene expression by stimulating gatekeeper genes including STRA8 and MEIOSIN, both critical for initiating meiosis. Additional regulators, including DMRT1, MSX1, and MSX2, contribute to this process via the modulation of STRA8 expression [29, 30]. Disruptions in these pathways can prevent ovarian germ cells from entering meiosis, resulting in oogenesis failure and female infertility, as demonstrated in mouse models [21]. While genetic studies in POI cohorts have not identified causative variants in most of these genes, pathogenic variants in STRA8 and MEIOSIN have been reported. In one patient with idiopathic POI, a homozygous splice-site variant in STRA8 (c.258 + 1G > A) and a homozygous missense variant in MEIOSIN (c.1735 C > T, p.Arg579Trp) were identified [22]. Functional analyses showed that the MEIOSIN c.1735 C > T variant, located in the HMG domain, impaired transcriptional activity, and the corresponding knock-in mouse model exhibited defects in meiotic prophase I and premature oocyte depletion [22]. In a separate study, a homozygous splice-site variant in STRA8 was reported in another patient with POI, and this variant was shown to cause exon skipping and an in-frame deletion of multiple amino acids [7]. MEIOSIN has also been established as a key meiosis-initiation factor that cooperates with STRA8 to direct the mitosis-to-meiosis transition in mammalian germ cells [21].

MEIOSIN is a transcription factor and thus primarily localizes to the nucleus in pre-leptotene germ cells in mice, where it works together with STRA8 to drive the activation of meiosis, facilitating the transition from mitosis to meiosis [21]. The MEIOSIN-STRA8 complex likely functions upstream of transcriptional activation, with MEIOSIN enhancing the expression of meiotic genes before meiosis initiation [31]. MEIOSIN harbors two functional domains: the HLH domain and the HMG domain, both of which are indispensable for transcriptional regulation. Sequence analysis has demonstrated that MEIOSIN homologs containing these conserved domains are present in various vertebrates, including humans and rodents [32]. The strong evolutionary conservation of these domains underscores MEIOSIN’s essential role in regulating the onset of meiosis by directly activating a series of key meiotic genes [21]. Consistent with this, MEIOSIN inhibition suppresses spermatocyte meiosis progression in mice by downregulating multiple meiosis-related genes [33]. Moreover, in Meiosin-knockout mouse embryos, germ cells exhibit normal mitotic progression but fail to initiate pre-meiotic DNA replication, meiotic chromosome condensation, synapsis, and recombination, ultimately leading to meiotic arrest and gametogenesis failure [21].

In this study, two biallelic MEIOSIN variant were identified, including a heterozygous frameshift variant (c.1068_1092del, p.His356fs*14) and a heterozygous missense variant (c.980T > C, p.Leu327Pro), in two siblings diagnosed with POI. The MEIOSIN1068_1092del variant leads to a truncated protein that alters its nuclear localization, causing it to disperse within the nucleus in vitro. Of note, this frameshift variant introduces a premature termination codon and is therefore predicted to trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD), potentially resulting in the absence of MEIOSIN protein in vivo. Since intronless cDNA constructs cannot be used to infer NMD in a physiological context, we further generated a construct mimicking the deletion of exon 10 (ΔExon10). Western blot analysis showed that, unlike the frameshift transcript construct (M2), the ΔExon10 construct did not produce any detectable MEIOSIN protein (Supplementary Fig. 4), suggesting a complete loss-of-function effect. Therefore, if exon 10 skipping or a similar splicing outcome occurs in vivo, the pathogenic consequence would be expected to be at least as severe as, or even more severe than, that caused by the truncated protein. Therefore, biophysical assays such as fluorescence recovery after photobleaching are warranted to further investigate the solubility and dynamic behavior of this truncated protein. The MEIOSIN980T> C variant affects the 3D structure of MEIOSIN but does not alter its expression levels or nuclear localization in vitro. These affected residues are located upstream of the HMG domain, a functional region known for its strong DNA-binding affinity and key role in the regulation of transcription. Strikingly, both variants were found to significantly reduce MEIOSIN’s ability to activate transcription of key meiotic genes, including PRDM9, SPO11, and SYCE3. These variant may therefore either directly or indirectly compromise the functionality or structural integrity of the HMG domain, ultimately impairing MEIOSIN’s role in reproductive activity. A previously reported homozygous missense variant in MEIOSIN (c.1735 C > T, p.Arg579Trp), located within the HMG domain, has been identified as a causative driver of POI in humans and mice. This variant disrupts the transcriptional regulation of meiotic genes, ultimately leading to oocyte depletion [22]. Additionally, MEIOSINR579W fails to localize properly within the nuclei of murine germ cells. Together with the mislocalization of MEIOSINH356fs, these findings suggest that the nuclear localization signal of MEIOSIN may be associated with its HMG domain, underscoring the functional importance of this conserved region. Additional research will be essential to explore these mechanisms in greater detail.

Even with the significance of the present findings, certain limitations must be acknowledged. For one, these functional analyses were performed in HEK293T cells, which are valuable tools for gain- or loss-of-function research, but fail to effectively replicate the physiological conditions found within ovarian germ cells. Subsequent validation in appropriate animal models will be vital to confirm the reliability of these results. Secondly, the present study was based on a small patient cohort, emphasizing the need for larger cohort studies to better clarify the clinical relevance of MEIOSIN variants as a cause of POI. Lastly, the molecular processes by which MEIOSIN variants affect downstream meiotic processes remain to be clarified in detail.

Conclusions

These findings support the notion that biallelic MEIOSIN variants may constitute a rare yet potentially causative genetic factor underlying POI in humans. Furthermore, they underscore the essential role of MEIOSIN in meiotic activity, attributable to its function as a transcription factor. These results help to extend the known spectrum of genetic abnormalities linked to human oogenesis failure and may contribute to improved genetic counseling and fertility management for impacted individuals.

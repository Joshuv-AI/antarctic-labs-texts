Rigidity and reconstruction in matroids of highly connected graphs
Dániel Garamvölgyi

Abstract
A graph matroid family ℳ \mathcal{M} is a family of matroids ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) defined on the edge set of each finite graph G G in a compatible and isomorphism-invariant way. We say that ℳ \mathcal{M} has the Whitney property if there is a constant c c such that every c c -connected graph G G is uniquely determined by ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) . Similarly, ℳ \mathcal{M} has the Lovász-Yemini property if there is a constant c c such that for every c c -connected graph G G , ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) has maximal rank among graphs on the same number of vertices. We show that if ℳ \mathcal{M} is unbounded (that is, there is no absolute constant bounding the rank of ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) for every G G ), then ℳ \mathcal{M} has the Whitney property if and only if it has the Lovász-Yemini property. We also give a complete characterization of these properties in the bounded case. As an application, we show that if some graph matroid families have the Whitney property, then so does their union. Finally, we show that every 1 1 -extendable graph matroid family has the Lovász-Yemini (and thus the Whitney) property. These results unify and extend a number of earlier results about graph reconstruction from an underlying matroid.

Introduction

Matroids arise as an abstraction of linear independence in vector spaces and cycle-freeness in graphs. In addition to their intrinsic appeal, matroids have become a valuable tool in discrete mathematics and combinatorial optimization, for the same reason as many successful abstractions: they distill a fundamental concept – in this case, the notion of linear independence – to its essence, while discarding what is only incidental.
Nonetheless, it is natural to wonder how much information is lost in this process of abstraction.

This question was answered by Whitney
[
40

for graphs and their graphic matroids. More precisely, Whitney gave a characterization of the edge maps between two graphs that induce isomorphisms between their respective graphic matroids. It follows as a corollary to his result that
3
-connected graphs are uniquely determined by their graphic matroids, in a certain (strong) sense.
In other words, every
3
-connected graph is
reconstructible
from its graphic matroid.

2.1
Dimensionality

We say that a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}

As far as we are aware, these definitions are new. Nonetheless, the idea underlying the definitions is implicit in a number of papers. In particular, the following lemma, which shows that the rank function of a nontrivial graph matroid family grows linearly, is a special case of a theorem of Kalai

Lemma 2.1
.

(Linearity of rank.)

Let

Fix

. Consider a vertex

, and let

be obtained from

by deleting all but

\mathcal{M}
-circuit has minimum degree at least

.

Let us fix an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit

∎

Lemma 2.2

(Vertex addition.)

\displaystyle r(G)\leq r(K_{V(G)})

\displaystyle=d(n_{1}+n_{2}-k-t)+r(K_{t}),\text{ and}

We say that a graph matroid family

The following lemma provides a useful characterization of the rank of a bounded graph matroid family.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be a graph matroid family with rank function

(a)

-independent.

(b)

(a)
If

\mathcal{M}
-independent. Conversely, if

(b)
For

-dependent. With this choice,

We prove the statement by induction on

\mathcal{M}
with rank
r(\mathcal{M})
, let us say that an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit is
small
if it has at most
r(\mathcal{M})
edges.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a bounded graph matroid family.
If there is a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with minimum degree one, then there exists an integer
m\geq 2
such that the star
K_{1,m}
is the unique (up to isomorphism) small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with at most
m
edges and with minimum degree one.

Proof.

Let
r
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}
. Assuming that there is a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with minimum degree one, let
m
denote the minimum number of edges in such an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit, and let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of small
\mathcal{M}
-circuits with
m
edges and with minimum degree one. Note that
m\geq 2
, since if
m=1
, i.e., if a single edge is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit, then
r(\mathcal{M})=0
, and thus there cannot be any small
\mathcal{M}
-circuits.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{C}
contains a graph that is not a star. We will show that
\mathcal{C}
contains the graph consisting of
m
disjoint edges. Since the members of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathcal{M}
-circuits, this implies, by
Lemma

2.4
(b), that
r(\mathcal{M})=m-1
, contradicting the assumption that
m
was the number of edges of a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit.

We start by introducing a pair of operations. Given a graph
G
and a pair of distinct edges
e
and
uv
, where
v
is a leaf vertex, the
pruning
operation deletes
e
from
G
and adds a new vertex
v^{\prime}
and an edge
uv^{\prime}
. (If the deletion of
e
creates an isolated vertex, then we also delete this vertex from the graph.) Similarly, given a graph
G
and a component
P
of
G
that is a path of length
2
, the
path cutting
operation deletes
P
from
G
and adds two disjoint edges
uv
and
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
, where
u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}
are new vertices. Note that both of these operations preserve the property of having minimum degree one.

Claim 2.6
.

\mathcal{C}
is closed under the pruning and path cutting operations.

Proof.

Fix
C_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
, and let
e
and
uv
be a pair of distinct edges of
C_{1}
, where
v
is a leaf vertex. Let us also consider the isomorphic copy
C_{2}
of
C_{1}
obtained as
C_{1}-v+uv^{\prime}
, where
v^{\prime}
is a new vertex. The strong circuit elimination axiom now implies that there is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit
C_{0}
in
C=(C_{1}\cup C_{2})-e
with
uv\in E(C_{0})
. Note that
C
is precisely the graph obtained from
C_{1}
by the pruning operation on
e
and
uv
. In particular, it is a graph on
m
edges with minimum degree one. Since
uv\in E(C_{0})
,
C_{0}
also has minimum degree one, and hence by the minimality of
m
, we must have
C_{0}=C
. Thus
C\in\mathcal{C}
.

The proof for the path cutting operation is similar. Fix
C_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
, and let
P=\{u,v,w\}
be a component of
C_{1}
that induces a path
\{uv,vw\}
. Let us consider the isomorphic copy
C_{2}
of
C_{1}
obtained as
C_{1}-w+uv^{\prime}
, where
v^{\prime}
is a new vertex. It follows from the strong circuit elimination axiom that there is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit
C_{0}
in
C=(C_{1}\cup C_{2})-uv
with
vw\in E(C_{0})
. Now
C
is precisely the graph obtained from
C_{1}
by the path cutting operation on
P
; in particular, it is a graph on
m
edges with minimum degree one. As in the previous case, the minimality of
m
implies that
C_{0}=C
, and thus that
C\in\mathcal{C}
, as desired.
∎

Claim 2.7
.

\mathcal{C}
contains a graph with at least two components and such that one of the components is a single edge.

Proof.

By the indirect assumption, there is a graph
C\in\mathcal{C}
that is not a star. First, assume that
C
has a single component. Since
C
has minimum degree one, it has a leaf vertex
v
. Let
u
be the vertex adjacent to
v
.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
denote the non-leaf neighbors of
u
; since
C
is not a star, we have
k\geq 1
. By applying the pruning operation on
e_{i}=uv_{i}
and
uv
, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and using
Claim

2.6
, we obtain a member of
\mathcal{C}
with at least two components.

Hence we may assume that
C
has at least two components. Let
u
be the neighbor of a degree one vertex of
C
, and let
C^{\prime}
be a component of
C
that does not contain
u
. Now by repeatedly applying the pruning operation to delete an edge from
C^{\prime}
and add a new edge incident to
u
, we can reduce
C^{\prime}
to a single edge. By
Claim

2.6
, the resulting graph is a member of
\mathcal{C}
, as desired.
∎

Now we are ready to finish the proof. Let
C
be a member of
\mathcal{C}
that has the maximum number
\ell
of components consisting of a single edge. By
Claim

2.7
,
\ell\geq 1
, and hence there is a component of
C
consisting of some edge
uv
. If
\ell<m
, then
C
also contains a component with at least two edges; let
e
be an edge in such a component. By applying the pruning operation with
uv
and
e
, and then the path cutting operation to the component containing
uv
, we obtain a graph
C^{\prime}
with at least
\ell+1
components that consist of a single edge. Since
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
by
Claim

2.6
, this contradicts the maximal choice of
C
.

Hence
\ell=m
, which means that
C
consists of
m
disjoint edges. We deduce, using
Lemma

2.4
(b), that
r(\mathcal{M})=m-1
, contradicting the
definition of
m
.
∎

2.3
Examples and operations

We close this section by illustrating the concepts introduced so far through several examples. We also recall the notion of an abstract rigidity matroid; as we will see, families of abstract rigidity matroids fit naturally within our framework of graph matroid families. Finally, we define and investigate the union and de-coning operations on graph matroid families.

Graphic, bicircular, and even cycle matroids.

The prototypical example of a graph matroid family is the family
\mathcal{G}
of graphic matroids. A graph is
\mathcal{G}
-rigid if and only if it is connected, and the
\mathcal{G}
-circuits are simply the cycle graphs. Thus each
\mathcal{G}
-circuit has minimum degree two, and the smallest
\mathcal{G}
-circuit—the triangle—has three vertices. Hence
\mathcal{G}
has dimensionality
1
and threshold
2
.

Apart from
\mathcal{G}
, the most basic examples of graph matroid families may be the family
\mathcal{B}
of
bicircular matroids
, and the family
\mathcal{E}
of
even cycle matroids
. A graph
G
is
\mathcal{B}
-independent if and only if
G
is a pseudoforest (i.e., each component of
G
contains at most one cycle), and
G
is
\mathcal{B}
-rigid if and only if each component of
G
contains a cycle. Similarly,
G
is
\mathcal{E}
-independent if and only if
G
is a pseudoforest without cycles of even length, and
G
is
\mathcal{E}
-rigid if and only if each component of
G
contains a cycle of odd length. These examples show that rigidity with respect to a graph matroid family does not, in general, imply connectivity. Both
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{E}
have dimensionality
1
and threshold
3
.

As we will see shortly, there are infinitely many graph matroid families with dimensionality one. However, it is shown in
[
33
, Theorem 5]
that
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{E}
are the only graph matroid families with dimensionality one in which all circuits are connected graphs.

Count matroids.

Let
k
and
\ell
be a pair of integers with
k\geq 1
and
\ell\leq 2k-1
. We say that a graph
G
is
(k,\ell)
-sparse
if
i_{G}(X)\leq k|X|-\ell
holds for every set of vertices
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|\geq 2
. Here,
i_{G}(X)
denotes the number of edges induced by
X
in
G
. We define the family
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
of
(k,\ell)
-count matroids
by letting a graph be
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
-independent if and only if it is
(k,\ell)
-sparse. It is known that
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
is indeed a graph matroid family
[
26
]
, and different choices of
k
and
\ell
give rise to different graph matroid families. Using this notation, we have
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{M}_{1,0}
.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
has dimensionality
k
. Calculating the threshold is nontrivial; it can be deduced from the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
that when
\ell\geq 0
, the threshold
t_{\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}}
is as follows:

\displaystyle t_{\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}}=\begin{cases}2k-1\hskip 10.00002pt&\text{if }k<\ell\leq 2k-1,\\
2k&\text{if }0<\ell\leq k,\\
2k+1&\text{if }\ell=0.\end{cases}

Schmidt
[
31
]
used the following modification of
\mathcal{M}_{k,0}
to construct uncountably many distinct graph matroid families. Let
k\geq 2
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a collection of
3
-connected
2k
-regular graphs. We define the family
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
by letting a graph be
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
-independent if it is
(k,0)
-sparse and does not contain any copy of a graph in
\mathcal{C}
as a subgraph.
Schmidt showed that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is indeed a graph matroid family, and that different choices of
\mathcal{C}
give rise to different graph matroid families. It is unclear whether there are uncountably many graph matroid families with dimensionality one.

Abstract rigidity matroids.

The graph matroid families we have seen so far are defined in a purely combinatorial way. Another set of examples comes from discrete geometry, the most prominent being the family of generic rigidity matroids.

Given a graph
G
and an embedding
p:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
rigidity matrix

R(G,p)
is a real matrix that has one row for each edge of
G
,
d
columns for each vertex of
G
, and whose entries are given by

R(G,p)=\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&\cdots&u&\cdots&v&\cdots\\
\vdots&&&&&\\
uv&0&p(u)-p(v)&0&p(v)-p(u)&0\\
\vdots&&&&&\\
\end{array}.

The
generic
d
-dimensional rigidity matroid

\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)
of
G
is the row matroid of
R(G,p)
for a generic embedding
p:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is known that this is well-defined, and that in this way we obtain a nontrivial graph matroid family
\mathcal{R}_{d}
with dimensionality
d
. We call
\mathcal{R}_{d}
the
family of generic
d
-dimensional rigidity matroids
.

The combinatorial characterization of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-rigid graphs is a difficult problem that is wide open already in the
d=3
case.
To explore this problem and to capture some key combinatorial properties of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
, Graver
[
14
]
introduced the notion of an abstract rigidity matroid. Let
n
and
d
be positive integers with
n\geq d
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a matroid on the edge set of
K_{n}
with closure operator
\cl_{0}
. We say that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a
d
-dimensional abstract rigidity matroid
if it satisfies the following pair of conditions.

•

If
E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq E(K_{n})
and
|V(E_{1})\cap V(E_{2})|\leq d-1
, then
\cl_{0}(E_{1}\cup E_{2})=\cl_{0}(E_{1})\cup\cl_{0}(E_{2})
.

•

If
E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq E(K_{n})
and
V_{i}=V(E_{i}),i\in\{1,2\}
are such that
|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|\geq d
and
\cl_{0}(E_{i})=E(K_{V_{i}}),i\in\{1,2\}
, then
\cl_{0}(E_{1}\cup E_{2})=E(K_{V_{1}\cup V_{2}})
.

Nguyen gave the following characterization of abstract rigidity matroids.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
29
, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2]

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers with
n\geq d
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a matroid on the edge set of
K_{n}
.

(a)

\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a
d
-dimensional abstract rigidity matroid if and only if its rank is
dn-\binom{d+1}{2}
and the addition of vertices of degree at most
d
preserves independent sets in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is an abstract rigidity matroid, then every copy of
E(K_{d+2})
in
K_{n}
is a circuit of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

We stress that an abstract rigidity matroid need not be symmetric, i.e., invariant under automorphisms of
K_{n}
. Nonetheless, the most studied abstract rigidity matroids do have this property, and in fact, they are usually part of a graph matroid family. This motivates the investigation of families of abstract rigidity matroids.

We say that a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
is a
family of
d
-dimensional abstract rigidity matroids
if
\mathcal{M}(K_{n})
is a
d
-dimensional abstract rigidity matroid, for every
n\geq d
. It is immediate from
Theorem

2.8
(a) that in this case
\mathcal{M}
is nontrivial and has dimensionality
d
.

The following result provides a simple characterization of families of abstract rigidity matroids. We note that this result is implicit in
[
23
, Section 10]
. In fact, the notion of
d
-hypergraphic matroids
introduced in
[
23
]
coincides with the notion of
d
-dimensional abstract rigidity matroid families.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an unbounded, nontrivial graph matroid family with dimensionality
d
and threshold
t
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{M}
is a family of abstract
d
-dimensional rigidity matroids,

(b)

K_{d+2}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit,

(c)

we have
t=d+1
.

Proof.

Let
r
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}
. The first implication is given by
Theorem

2.8
(b), while
(b)

\Rightarrow

(c)
follows from the definition of the threshold. Finally,
(c)

\Rightarrow

(a)
follows from the observation that by
Lemma

2.2
,
K_{d+1}
is
\mathcal{M}
-independent, and hence
Lemma

2.1
implies that

r(K_{n})=d(n-d-1)+\binom{d+1}{2}=dn-\left(d(d+1)-\binom{d+1}{2}\right)=dn-\binom{d+1}{2},

for every
n\geq d
. Thus
\mathcal{M}
is an abstract rigidity matroid family by
Theorem

2.8
(a).
∎

We can also show that families of abstract
d
-dimensional rigidity matroids are the graph matroid families of dimensionality
d
that have, in a sense, smallest rank.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an unbounded, nontrivial graph matroid family with dimensionality
d
.

(a)

For every
n\geq d+2
, we have
r(K_{n})\geq dn-\binom{d+1}{2}
, and equality holds for some
n
if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is a family of abstract
d
-dimensional rigidity matroids.

(b)

(See
[
15
, Theorem 3.11.8]
) The only
1
-dimensional family of abstract rigidity matroids is the family of graphic matroids.

Proof.

(a)
By the definition of dimensionality,
K_{d+1}
is
\mathcal{M}
-independent, and the addition of vertices of degree
d
preserves
\mathcal{M}
-independence. It follows that for every
n\geq d+2
, we have

r(K_{n})\geq d(n-d-1)+\binom{d+1}{2}=dn-\binom{d+1}{2},

as claimed. Moreover, if
\mathcal{M}
is not a family of abstract
d
-dimensional rigidity matroids, then by
Proposition

2.9
,
K_{d+2}
is
\mathcal{M}
-independent, and hence by an analogous reasoning we have

r(K_{n})\geq d(n-d-2)+\binom{d+2}{2}>dn-\binom{d+1}{2}

for every
n\geq d+2
.

(b)
We will show that if
\mathcal{M}
is a
1
-dimensional abstract rigidity matroid, then the
\mathcal{M}
-circuits are precisely the cycle graphs
C_{n},n\geq 3
. In fact, it suffices to show that cycle graphs are
\mathcal{M}
-circuits. Indeed,
\mathcal{M}
has dimensionality one, so forests are
\mathcal{M}
-independent, and since every graph that is not a forest contains a cycle, if cycles are
\mathcal{M}
-circuits, then they must be the only
\mathcal{M}
-circuits.

We prove that the cycle
C_{n}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit by induction on
n
. The
n=3
case follows from
Proposition

2.9
. Thus, let us assume that
n\geq 4
and that
C_{n-1}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit. By gluing a triangle onto an edge
e
of
C_{n-1}
, we can write
C_{n}
as
(C_{n-1}\cup C_{3})-e
. Thus by the circuit elimination axiom,
C_{n}
must contain an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit. Since forests are
\mathcal{M}
-independent, this
\mathcal{M}
-circuit must be all of
C_{n}
, as desired.
∎

Besides the family
\mathcal{R}_{d}
of
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroids, the most studied families of abstract rigidity matroids are the family
\mathcal{H}_{d}
of
d
-hypergraphic matroids introduced by Kalai
[
23
]
, and the family
\mathcal{C}_{d}
of generic
C^{d-2}_{d-1}
-cofactor matroids introduced by Whiteley
[
39
]
. These are also defined as row matroids of certain symbolic matrices.

Another way of constructing families of abstract rigidity matroids is by fixing an irreducible affine variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and letting
\mathcal{R}_{X}(G)
be the row matroid of the rigidity matrix
R(G,p)
, where
p:V(G)\to X
is a generic embedding. Here, “generic” is meant in the sense of “generic among embeddings of
G
into
X
”, as opposed to a generic embedding of
G
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in which the points happen to lie on
X
. It can be shown using standard techniques that if
X
is not contained in any affine hyperplane, then
\mathcal{R}_{X}
is a family of abstract
d
-dimensional rigidity matroids.

By taking
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we recover the family
\mathcal{R}_{d}
of generic rigidity matroids.
As far as we are aware, the only other case of this construction that has been considered previously is when
X
is the moment curve in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which was recently investigated by Crespo Ruiz and Santos
[
5
]
.

Bounded matroid families.

The matroid families we have seen so far all have positive dimensionality. Let us also consider some bounded families. The most basic example is the family
\mathcal{U}_{k}
of rank-
k
uniform matroids, defined by letting
\mathcal{U}_{k}(G)
be the rank-
k
uniform matroid on
E(G)
, for every graph
G
. More generally, for any graph matroid family
\mathcal{M},
we can take its
rank-
k
truncation

\mathcal{M}_{k}
, so that a graph
G
is
\mathcal{M}_{k}
-independent if and only if
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-independent and
|E(G)|\leq k
.

However, not all bounded graph matroids arise as the truncation of some unbounded graph matroid family. Indeed, if
\mathcal{M}
is unbounded, then every
\mathcal{M}
-circuit has minimum degree at least two. Since every small
\mathcal{M}_{k}
-circuit is also an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit, this means that small
\mathcal{M}_{k}
-circuits must also have minimum degree at least two. (Recall that an
\mathcal{M}_{k}
-circuit is
small
if it has at most
r(\mathcal{M}_{k})
edges.) This is not true for all bounded graph matroid families, as shown by the next construction.

Let
k
be a positive integer, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a set of (isomorphism classes of) graphs, each with
k
edges. We say that
\mathcal{X}
is
union-stable
if for any pair of graphs
G,H\in\mathcal{X}
and any
e\in E(G)\cap E(H)
, either
(G\cup H)-e
has at least
k+1
edges, or
(G\cup H)-e\in\mathcal{X}
. It is not difficult to show that for such an
\mathcal{X}
, the assignment

G\text{ is }\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X}}\text{-independent}\Leftrightarrow|E(G)|\leq k\text{ and }G\notin\mathcal{X}

defines a bounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X}}
, see
[
17
, Lemma 2.5]
.

For example, let
\mathcal{X}
consist of (isomorphism classes of) the star
K_{1,k}
. If
k\geq 3
, then
\mathcal{X}
is union stable, and in the associated matroid family
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X}}
,
K_{1,k}
is the only small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit.

Another, perhaps more interesting example of a bounded graph matroid family can be constructed as follows.
Let
t
be a positive integer, let
G
be a graph, and fix a generic embedding
p:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{t}
. The set of symmetric tensor products
\{p(u)\otimes p(v)+p(v)\otimes p(u),uv\in E(G)\}
, viewed as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{t}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{t}
, defines a linear matroid
\mathcal{S}_{t}(G)
on the edge set of
G
. This yields a graph matroid family
\mathcal{S}_{t}
which we call the family of
rank-
t
generic symmetric tensor matroids
. It was recently shown by Brakensiek et al.
[
2
]
that
\mathcal{S}_{t}(K_{n})
is dual to the generic rigidity matroid
\mathcal{R}_{n-t-1}(K_{n})
. From this it follows (and it is not difficult to see directly) that
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is a bounded family with rank
\binom{t+1}{2}
, and that
K_{1,t+1}
is a small
\mathcal{S}_{t}
-circuit.

Jordán
[
21
]
and Grasegger, Guler and Jackson
[
13
]
characterized
\mathcal{R}_{d}(K_{d+t+1})
for all
d
whenever
t\leq 5
. By duality, this yields a characterization of
\mathcal{S}_{t}(K_{n})
for all
n
, and hence of
\mathcal{S}_{t}(G)
for all graphs
G
, for
t\leq 5
. (See
[
18
]
for an alternative proof of this result.) No combinatorial characterization is known when
t\geq 6
.

Unions and de-coning.

To end this subsection, we briefly consider a pair of operations for constructing new graph matroid families from old ones.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{i}=(E,\mathcal{I}_{i}),i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
be a collection of matroids on a common ground set
E
. The
union
of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{0}^{k}
, denoted by
\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{M}^{i}_{0}
, is the matroid on ground set
E
whose family of independent sets is

\mathcal{I}=\{I_{1}\cup\ldots\cup I_{k}:I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\in\mathcal{I}_{k}\}.

Given graph matroid families
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
, we define their
union

\mathcal{M}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{M}_{i}
by letting
\mathcal{M}(G)=\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{M}_{i}(G)
for every finite graph
G
. It is immediate from the definitions that
\mathcal{M}
is also a graph matroid family. The following lemma shows that dimensionality is additive under taking unions.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the union of the graph matroid families
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
. If each of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
is nontrivial, then so is
\mathcal{M}
. In this case the dimensionality of
\mathcal{M}
is the sum of the dimensionalities of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

Proof.

Suppose that each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is nontrivial, let
d_{i},t_{i}
and

denote its dimensionality, threshold, and rank function, respectively, and set
d=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}
and
t=\max_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}
. Let
r
be the rank function of
\mathcal{M}
. For every integer
n\geq t
we have

r(K_{n})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(K_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}(n-t)+r_{i}(K_{t})=d(n-t)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(K_{t}),

where the first equality uses
Lemma

2.1
. Thus
\mathcal{M}
is nontrivial, and it follows from the same lemma that its dimensionality is at most
d
.

To show that the dimensionality of
\mathcal{M}
is at least
d
, it suffices by
Lemma

to show that addition of vertices of degree

preserves
\mathcal{M}
-independence. This follows immediately from the facts that addition of vertices of degree
d_{i}
preserves
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-independence, and that a graph is
\mathcal{M}
-independent if and only if it can be written as the union of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-independent graphs.
∎

In particular,
Lemma

2.11
implies that if some of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
is unbounded, then so is
\mathcal{M}
.

For the threshold of the union, we have the following bound. The proof is an adaptation of
[

, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the union of the graph matroid families
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
. Suppose that for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is nontrivial and has dimensionality
{d}_{i}
and threshold
{t}_{i}
. Let
t=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\max({t}_{i},2{d}_{i})
.

(a)

If a graph
G
contains edge-disjoint
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid spanning subgraphs
G_{i},i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, then
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid.

(b)

Conversely, if
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid and has at least

vertices, then
G
contains edge-disjoint
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid spanning subgraphs
G_{i},i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

(c)

The threshold of
\mathcal{M}
is at most

.

Proof.

Part

follows from the observation that

r(G)\leq r(K_{V(G)})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(K_{V(G)})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(G_{i})\leq r(G),

where

and

denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
, respectively.

For
(b)
, let

denote the number of vertices of

. It suffices to consider the case

. Indeed, if

contains edge-disjoint
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid spanning subgraphs, then

r(G)=r(K_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}(K_{n}),

and hence

must also contain suitable spanning subgraphs.
We construct the subgraphs
G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}
as follows. Let us partition the vertex set of

into sets of vertices
V_{i}^{j},i\in\{1,\ldots,k\},j\in\{1,2\}
such that for all

,
|V_{i}^{1}|\geq{d}_{i},|V_{i}^{2}|\geq{d}_{i},
and
|V_{i}^{1}\cup V_{i}^{2}|\geq{t}_{i}
. For each

, let

consist of the complete graph on

, plus the edge sets

\bigcup_{\ell<i}\left(E(V^{1}_{i},V^{1}_{\ell})\cup E(V^{2}_{i},V^{2}_{\ell})\right)\cup\bigcup_{\ell>i}\left(E(V^{1}_{i},V^{2}_{\ell})\cup E(V^{2}_{i},V^{1}_{\ell})\right).

Now by
Lemma

2.1
,
G_{i}
is
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid, since it has a spanning subgraph obtained from a complete graph on at least
{t}_{i}

. It is also easy to see that
G_{i}
and

are edge-disjoint for

.

For
(c)
, fix edge-disjoint
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid spanning subgraphs
G_{i},i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
in
K_{t}
, and let
G
denote the union of

. Since
t\geq{t}_{i}
, adding a new vertex of degree

to

also results in an
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid graph, and thus adding a new vertex

of degree

to

also results in a graph

that has edge-disjoint
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid spanning subgraphs for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. By
(a)
, this implies that
G^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid. Hence adding another edge between

and some vertex of

must create an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit

in

such that
1\leq d_{C}(v)\leq d+1
. By the definitions of the dimensionality and the threshold, we must have

t_{\mathcal{M}}\leq|V(C)|-1\leq|V(G^{\prime})|-1=t.\vskip-10.00002pt

∎

Lemma

2.12
(c) gives a tight bound on the threshold for the
k
-fold union of the family
\mathcal{G}
of graphic matroids. However, the bound is not tight in general, even for count matroids. See
[
11
, Lemma 5.4]
for a better bound for the union
\mathcal{R}_{d}\cup\mathcal{G}
.

There is no clear way to define the dual of a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
. Indeed, taking the dual matroid
\mathcal{M}(G)^{*}
for each graph
G
gives a well-defined, but not compatible family of matroids. That is, the restriction of
\mathcal{M}(G)^{*}
to the edge set of a subgraph
H
of
G
does not, in general, coincide with
\mathcal{M}(H)^{*}
; instead, it is dual to the matroid obtained from
\mathcal{M}(G)
by contracting (in a matroidal sense) the edges not in
H
.

Similarly, we cannot, in general, define the contraction of a graph matroid family. However, given an unbounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
, we can define another graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
by letting
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(K_{n})
be the matroid obtained by the contraction of a vertex star in
\mathcal{M}(K_{n+1})
. More explicitly, a graph is
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
-independent if and only if its cone graph is
\mathcal{M}
-independent, where the
cone graph
of a graph
G
is obtained by adding a new vertex to
G
that is connected to every vertex of
G
.

Let us call the construction of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{M}
the
de-coning
operation.
It is a classical result of Whiteley
[
38
]
that by applying this operation to the family of
(d+1)
-dimensional generic rigidity matroids, we recover the family of
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroids. It is also not difficult to see that by de-coning the family of
(k,\ell)
-count matroids, where
k\geq 2
, we obtain the family of
(k-1,\ell-k)
-count matroids. Since we will not investigate this operation further, we omit the details.

3
The Lovász-Yemini and Whitney properties

We are now ready to begin our investigation of the main concepts of the paper: the Lovász-Yemini and the Whitney properties. We start by giving a rigorous definition of reconstructibility with respect to a graph matroid family.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a graph matroid family, and let
G
be a graph. We say that
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible
if
\mathcal{M}(G)
uniquely determines
G
, in the sense that if
H
is another graph and
\psi:E(G)\rightarrow E(H)
is an isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}(H)
, then
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism. (A graph isomorphism
\varphi:V(G)\to V(H)

induces

\psi
if
\psi(uv)=\varphi(u)\varphi(v)
holds for every edge
uv\in E(G)
.)

We say that a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
has the
Whitney property (with constant
c
)
if there is a nonnegative integer
c
such that every
c
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible.
Similarly, let us say that a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
has the
Lovász-Yemini property (with constant
c
)
if there is a nonnegative integer
c
such that every
c
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid.

In the next subsection we prove
Theorem

1.1
, which says that for an unbounded graph matroid family, the Whitney property is equivalent to the Lovász-Yemini property. The bounded case is investigated in
Section

3.2
: we observe that every bounded graph matroid family has the Lovász-Yemini property, and we give a characterization of the Whitney property for a bounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
in terms of the existence of certain
\mathcal{M}
-circuits. In
Section

3.3
, we show that taking unions of graph matroid families preserves both the Whitney and the Lovász-Yemini properties. Finally, in
Section

3.4
, we define the class of
1
-extendable graph matroid families and show that every
1
-extendable graph matroid family has the Lovász-Yemini (and thus the Whitney) property.

3.1
The unbounded case

We begin by proving the easy direction of
Theorem

1.1
; namely, that for an unbounded graph matroid family, the Whitney property implies the Lovász-Yemini property. The key observation is the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is an unbounded graph matroid family and
G
is an
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible graph with at least two edges, then
\mathcal{M}(G)
is bridgeless.

Proof.

Suppose in the contrapositive that
\mathcal{M}(G)
contains a bridge
e
. Let
v\in V(G)
be a vertex not incident to
e
, and let
u_{1},u_{2}
be a pair of new vertices. It follows from
Lemma

2.2
that
vu_{1}
is a bridge in
G_{1}=G-e+vu_{1}
, and similarly,
u_{1}u_{2}
is a bridge in
G_{2}=G-e+u_{1}u_{2}
. Hence
\mathcal{M}(G)
,
\mathcal{M}(G_{1})
and
\mathcal{M}(G_{2})
are all isomorphic. Since
G
cannot be isomorphic to both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we conclude that
G
is not
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If an unbounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property with constant
c
, then it has the Lovász-Yemini property with constant
c
.

Proof.

Let us suppose, for a contradiction, that every
c
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible, but there is some
c
-connected graph that is not
\mathcal{M}
-rigid. Then there is a pair of nonadjacent vertices
u,v
in
G
such that
uv
is a bridge in
\mathcal{M}(G+uv)
. Now
G+uv
is a
c
-connected, and hence
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible graph on at least two edges, such that
\mathcal{M}(G+uv)
contains a bridge. But this contradicts
Lemma

3.1
.
∎

Our main goal in this subsection is to prove the other direction of
Theorem

1.1
, in the following, more precise form.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family with threshold
t
and rank function
r
. If
\mathcal{M}
has the Lovász-Yemini property with constant
c
, then
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property with constant
c^{\prime}=\max(c,t)+r(K_{\max(c,t)-1})+2
.

Our proof relies on the notion of vertical connectivity of matroids, which we introduce next.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}=(E,r)
be a matroid with rank function
r
, and let
k
be a positive integer.
We say that a bipartition
(E_{1},E_{2})
of
E
is a
vertical
k
-separation
of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
if

r(E_{1}),r(E_{2})\geq k

(1)

and

r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})\leq r(E)+k-1

(2)

hold. Note that
1
and
2
together imply
r(E_{i})<r(E)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
We say that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is
vertically
k
-connected
if
k\leq r(\mathcal{M}_{0})
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}
does not have vertical
k^{\prime}
-separations for any positive integer
k^{\prime}<k
. For
k=1
, the latter condition is vacuous, and hence every matroid with positive rank is vertically
1
-connected.

The following lemma shows that to certify that a matroid is not vertically
k
-connected, it suffices to consider “vertical separations”
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
in which
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are not necessarily disjoint.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}=(E,r)
be a matroid, and let
E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq E
be subsets of
E
, not necessarily disjoint, with
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
. Suppose that
r(E_{1}),r(E_{2})\geq k
and
r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})\leq r(E)+k-1
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{0}
has a vertical
k^{\prime}
-separation for some
k^{\prime}\leq k
.

Proof.

Let
E^{\prime}_{2}=E_{2}-E_{1}
. Then
(E_{1},E^{\prime}_{2})
is a bipartition of
E
, and it is straightforward to check that it is a vertical
k^{\prime}
-separation for
k^{\prime}=k-(r(E_{2})-r(E_{2}^{\prime}))
.
∎

It is well-known that a graph without isolated vertices is
k
-connected if and only if its graphic matroid is vertically
k
-connected (see, e.g.,
[
30
, Theorem 8.6.1]
).
The main ingredients to our proof of
Theorem

3.3
,
Proposition

3.6
and
Proposition

3.7
below, are partial generalizations of this fact to arbitrary graph matroid families.
Proposition

3.6
states that given an unbounded, nontrivial graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
, if
\mathcal{M}(G)
has sufficiently high vertical connectivity, then
G
must have high vertex-connectivity (provided that it has no isolated vertices).
Conversely,
Proposition

3.7
shows that if
G
has high vertex-connectivity and is highly vertex-redundantly
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, then
\mathcal{M}(G)
must have high vertical connectivity. Finally, if
\mathcal{M}
has the Lovász-Yemini property, then high vertex-connectivity implies high vertex-redundant
\mathcal{M}
-rigidity. By combining this triangle of implications with ideas from previous Whitney-type results, we can deduce that the Lovász-Yemini property implies the Whitney property.

Let us note that this proof strategy roughly follows the strategy used in
[
12
]
to show that the family of generic
C^{1}_{2}
-cofactor matroids has the Whitney property. The crucial difference is that in that case, a combinatorial characterization of the rank function was available, while here we can only use the assumptions that
\mathcal{M}
is unbounded, nontrivial, and has the Lovász-Yemini property.

We start by giving a bound on the number of vertices of a graph
G
, provided that
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
k
-connected. The proof of this simple statement turns out to be surprisingly subtle.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family with dimensionality
d
.
Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. If
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
k
-connected, then its minimum degree is at least
k+d-1
. In particular,
|V|\geq k+d
.

Proof.

Let
r
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}
.
Note that since
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
2
-connected, it is bridgeless, and hence it follows from
Lemma

2.2
that every vertex of
G
has degree at least
d+1
.

Let
v\in V
be a vertex of smallest degree in
G
. Set
k_{0}=d_{G}(v)-d+1
, and let
E_{1}
be a set of
k_{0}
edges incident to
v
. (Note that
d\geq 1
and thus
k_{0}\leq d_{G}(v)
.)
We will show that
(E_{1},E-E_{1})
is a vertical
k_{0}
-separation of
\mathcal{M}(G)
. Since the latter is vertically
k
-connected, it follows that
k_{0}\geq k
, and hence that
d_{G}(v)\geq k+d-1
, as claimed.

To see that
(E_{1},E-E_{1})
is a vertical
k_{0}
-separation, note that by
Lemma

2.4
,
E_{1}
is independent in
\mathcal{M}(G)
, and hence
r(E_{1})=k_{0}
. Moreover, since
d_{E-E_{0}}(v)\leq d-1
, it follows from
Lemma

2.2
that adding any edge to
E-E_{1}
incident to
v
increases the rank, and thus we have
r(E)\geq r(E-E_{1})+1
and

r(E_{1})+r(E-E_{1})\leq r(E)+k_{0}-1.

Hence all that is left to show is that
r(E-E_{1})\geq k_{0}
.

First, suppose that either
d\geq 2
or
v
is not adjacent to every other vertex in
G
. In both cases we can find a vertex
w
such that
d_{E-E_{1}}(w)=d_{G}(w)
, and thus, using
Lemma

2.4
again,

r(E-E_{1})\geq d_{E-E_{1}}(w)=d_{G}(w)\geq d_{G}(v)\geq k_{0},

as claimed.

Thus we may assume that
d=1
and that
v
is adjacent to every vertex in

. Since
v
has smallest degree, it follows that
G
is a complete graph. Note that in this case
k_{0}=d_{G}(v)=|V(G-v)|
. Now if
\mathcal{M}
is not the family of graphic matroids, then we can use

to deduce that
r(E-E_{1})=r(G-v)\geq|V(G-v)|=k_{0}
, as required. Finally, if
\mathcal{M}
is the family of graphic matroids, then by the definition of vertical
k
-connectivity, we have
k+1\leq r(G)+1=|V|
, and hence the minimum degree of
G
is
|V|-1=k
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family with dimensionality
d
and threshold
t
.
Let
k
be an integer with

. If
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
(r(K_{k})+2)

G
is
(k+1)
-connected.

Proof.

It follows from
Lemma

2.10
that
r(K_{k})\geq k-1
, and hence from

3.5
that
|V|\geq r(K_{k})+d+2\geq k+2
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that there exists a set
S\subseteq V

is disconnected.
By adding vertices to
S
, we may suppose that

|V|\geq k+2

Let

\displaystyle r(K_{V_{i}\cup S})

\displaystyle=d|V_{i}|+r(K_{k}),\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,2\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle r(K_{V})

\displaystyle=d|V_{1}\cup V_{2}|+r(K_{k}).

r(G_{i})\geq r(K_{k})-(a_{2}-b_{2})+1,\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,2\},

To show that

\displaystyle r(G_{1})+r(G_{2})

\displaystyle=d|V_{1}|+r(K_{k})-a_{1}+d|V_{2}|+r(K_{k})-a_{2}

\displaystyle=d|V_{1}\cup V_{2}|+2r(K_{k})-(a_{1}+a_{2})

, as required.

Given a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
and a nonnegative integer
k
, we say that a graph
G
is

-vertex-redundantly
\mathcal{M}
-rigid
if it is

-rigid and remains so after the deletion of any set of at most

\mathcal{M}
-rigid graphs are precisely the

-rigid graphs.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
\mathcal{M}
be a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family with rank function
r
, dimensionality
d
, and threshold
t
. If
G
is
k
-connected,
k
-vertex-redundantly
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, and has at least
k+t
vertices, then
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
(k+1)
-connected.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
k
; the
k=0
case is trivial. Let us thus assume that
k\geq 1
. Note that since
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, we have

r(G)\geq r(K_{k+t})\geq k+t-1\geq k+1,

where the second inequality follows from
Lemma

2.10
.

Let us suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{M}(G)
has a vertical
k^{\prime}
-separation
(E_{1},E_{2})
for some
k^{\prime}\leq k
. Since
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are both nonempty and
G
is connected, there exists a vertex
v\in V(E_{1})\cap V(E_{2})
. Note that
G-v
is
(k-1)
-connected and
(k-1)
-vertex-redundantly
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, and hence by the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{M}(G-v)
is vertically
k
-connected. Also,
G-v
and
G
are both
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, and since they both have at least
t
vertices, we have
r(G)=r(G-v)+d
.

Let
E_{i}^{\prime}=E_{i}-\partial_{G}(v)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. From the assumption on
G
, we have

d_{G}(v)\geq d+k\geq d+1,

for otherwise by deleting a suitable set of at most
k
vertices, we would obtain an
\mathcal{M}
-rigid graph on at least
t
vertices in which
v
has degree less than
d
, contradicting
Lemma

2.2
. We also have
d_{E_{1}}(v),d_{E_{2}}(v)\geq 1
. Using
Lemma

2.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle r(E_{1}^{\prime})+r(E_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})-(d+1)

\displaystyle\leq r(G)+(k^{\prime}-1)-d-1

\displaystyle=r(G-v)+(k^{\prime}-1)-1.

It follows that

r(E_{1}^{\prime})+r(E_{2}^{\prime})=r(G-v)+c-1

(5)

for some integer
c
with
1\leq c\leq k^{\prime}-1\leq k-1
.
If
r(E_{1}^{\prime}),r(E_{2}^{\prime})\geq c,
then
(E_{1}^{\prime},E_{2}^{\prime})
is a vertical
c
-separation of
\mathcal{M}(G-v)
, a contradiction. Thus we must have
r(E_{1}^{\prime})\leq c-1
or
r(E_{2}^{\prime})\leq c-1
; by symmetry, we may suppose that it is the former.

Now
5
implies that
r(E_{1}^{\prime})=c-1
and
r(E_{2}^{\prime})=r(G-v)
.
If
d_{E_{2}}(v)\geq d
, then by
Lemma

2.2
, we have
r(E_{2})=r(E_{2}^{\prime})+d=r(G-v)+d=r(G)
, contradicting the fact that
(E_{1},E_{2})
is a vertical
k^{\prime}
-separation. Hence
d_{E_{2}}(v)\leq d-1
. It follows from
Lemmas

2.2
and
2.4
that

\displaystyle r(E_{1})+r(E_{2})

\displaystyle\geq d_{E_{1}}(v)+r(E^{\prime}_{2})+d_{E_{2}}(v)=r(G-v)+d_{G}(v)

\displaystyle\geq r(G-v)+d+k=r(G)+k

\displaystyle>r(G)+k^{\prime}-1.

But this contradicts, again, the fact that
(E_{1},E_{2})
is a vertical
k^{\prime}
-separation.
∎

Let us note that by combining
Proposition

3.6
and
Proposition

3.7
, we recover the fact that a graph is
k
-vertex-connected if and only if its graphic matroid is vertically
k
-connected.

Note that if
\mathcal{M}
has the Lovász-Yemini property with constant
c
, then every
(c+k)
-connected graph is
k
-vertex-redundantly
\mathcal{M}
-rigid.
Thus the following is an immediate corollary of
Proposition

3.7
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
\mathcal{M}
be a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family with rank function
r
, dimensionality
d
, and threshold
t
. Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
has the Lovász-Yemini property with constant
c
. If
G
is
(\max(t,c)+k)
-connected for some positive integer
k
, then
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
(k+1)
-connected.

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

3.3
.
We shall use the following folklore statement.

Lemma 3.9
.

[
12
, Lemma 5.1]

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be graphs without isolated vertices, and let
\psi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
be a bijection that “sends stars to stars”, that is, such that for every
v\in V
, there is a vertex
v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
with
\psi(\partial_{G}(v))=\partial_{H}(v^{\prime})
. Then
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.

Proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
denote the dimensionality of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let us fix a
c^{\prime}
-connected graph
G
, let
H
be a graph without isolated vertices, and let
\psi:E(G)\rightarrow E(H)
be an isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}(H)
. Our goal is to show that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism.

Fix
v\in V(G)
and consider
F=E(G)-\partial_{G}(v)
. Set
F^{\prime}=\psi(F)
and
H^{\prime}=H[F^{\prime}]
; note that
\mathcal{M}(G-v)
and
\mathcal{M}(H^{\prime})
are isomorphic matroids. By
Proposition

3.8
,
\mathcal{M}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}(G-v)
are both vertically
\left(r(K_{c-1})+2\right)
-connected, and hence
Proposition

3.6
implies that
H
and
H^{\prime}
are both
c
-connected. It follows from the Lovász-Yemini property that
G,G-v,H
, and
H^{\prime}
are all
\mathcal{M}
-rigid.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is unbounded and
F
is the edge set of an induced subgraph of
G
, it is closed in
\mathcal{M}(G)
, and hence
F^{\prime}
is closed in
\mathcal{M}(H)
. On the other hand,
F^{\prime}
induces the
\mathcal{M}
-rigid graph
H^{\prime}
, and thus adding edges of
H
induced by
V(H^{\prime})
to
F^{\prime}
cannot increase its rank. We deduce that every such edge must already be contained in
F^{\prime}
; in other words, that
H^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
H
.
Also note that

r(H)=r(G)=r(G-v)+d=r(H^{\prime})+d.

Since
H
and
H^{\prime}
are both
\mathcal{M}
-rigid and have at least
t
vertices, it follows that
|V(H^{\prime})|=|V(H)|-1
.

Thus
H^{\prime}
, being an induced subgraph of
H
on
|V(H)|-1
vertices, can be written as
H-w
for some
w\in V(H)
. This shows that
\psi
maps complements of vertex stars to complements of vertex stars. Since
\psi
is a bijection, it must also map vertex stars to vertex stars.
Lemma

3.9
now implies that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism, as desired.
∎

Theorem

1.1
follows immediately from
Lemma

3.2
,
Theorem

3.3
, and the observation that the trivial graph matroid family has neither the Lovász-Yemini nor the Whitney properties.

As an immediate consequence of
Proposition

3.6
, we can also deduce the following reconstructibility result, in which the requirement is not that
G
is highly connected, but rather that
\mathcal{M}(G)
is highly vertically connected.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an unbounded graph matroid family with rank function
r
, and let
G
be a graph. Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property with constant
c
. If
\mathcal{M}(G)
is vertically
(r(K_{c-1})+2)
-connected, then
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible.

Let us end this subsection by specializing the previous results to the family
\mathcal{R}_{d}
of
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroids. As noted in the introduction, Villányi recently established the Lovász-Yemini property for this family.

Theorem 3.11
.

[
36
]

Every
d(d+1)
-connected graph is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-rigid.

By combining
Theorems

3.3
,
3.10
and
3.11
, we can deduce the following Whitney-type result for
\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)
. This answers positively a question of Brigitte and Herman Servatius
[
1
, Problem 17]
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
G
be a graph. If
G
is
d(d+1)^{2}
-connected, or
\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)
is vertically
(d(d+1))^{2}
-connected, then
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-reconstructible.

Proof.

Theorems

3.3
and
3.11
imply that
\mathcal{R}_{d}
has the Whitney property with constant

\displaystyle c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=d(d+1)+d\big(d(d+1)-1\big)-\binom{d+1}{2}+2

\displaystyle=d(d+1)(d+1/2)-d+2

\displaystyle\leq d(d+1)^{2}.

This means that every
d(d+1)^{2}
-connected graph is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-reconstructible. The second part of the statement follows from
Theorem

3.10
by noting that

\displaystyle d(c^{\prime}-1)-\binom{d+1}{2}+2\leq d\big(d(d+1)^{2}-1\big)-\binom{d+1}{2}+2\leq\big(d(d+1)\big)^{2}.

∎

Theorem

3.12
shows that
\mathcal{R}_{d}
has the Whitney property, for all
d
. Previously this was only known for
d=1
, given by Whitney’s theorem, and for
d=2
, where Jordán and Kaszanitzky
[
19
]
showed that the constant
7
suffices.
It is apparent from the proof of
Theorem

3.12
that the constant
d(d+1)^{2}
is not tight, and we believe it to be far from optimal. In fact, for
d\geq 2
it is open whether every
d(d+1)
-connected graph is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-reconstructible.

3.2
The bounded case

Next, we consider rigidity and reconstructibility in bounded graph matroid families.
We start with a simple observation.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a graph matroid family. If
\mathcal{M}
is bounded, then it has the Lovász-Yemini property.

Proof.

Let
r
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{M}
, and let
k=2(r(\mathcal{M})-1)+1
. It is not difficult to see that every
k
-connected graph contains a matching of size at least
r(\mathcal{M})
. It follows from
Lemma

2.4
(b) that such a matching has rank
r(\mathcal{M})
. This implies that every
k
-connected graph has rank
r(\mathcal{M})
, and hence is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid.
∎

On the other hand, not every bounded graph matroid family has the Whitney property. For example, the family
\mathcal{U}_{k}
of rank-
k
uniform matroids does not: in this case
\mathcal{U}_{k}(G)
is determined by the number of edges of
G
, rather than
G
itself.

Theorem

3.17
below provides a complete characterization of bounded graph matroid families with the Whitney property. It turns out that the example of
\mathcal{U}_{k}
is characteristic, in the sense that if a bounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
does not have the Whitney property, then there exist arbitrarily highly connected graphs
G
for which
\mathcal{M}(G)
is a uniform matroid.

One of the main tools in the proof of
Theorem

3.17
is the following result from extremal graph theory.
For a finite family of graphs
\mathcal{C}
, let
ex(\mathcal{C},n)
denote the maximum number of edges in a graph on
n
vertices that does not contain a copy of any member of
\mathcal{C}
as a subgraph.

Theorem 3.14
.

(See, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 2.33]
)
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite family of graphs. If
\mathcal{C}
does not contain a forest, then there exists a positive constant
c
such that
ex(\mathcal{C},n)\geq n^{1+c}
.

Theorem

3.14
lets us construct
\mathcal{C}
-free graphs with arbitrarily high average degree. The following classical theorem of Mader allows us to replace “high average degree” with “high vertex-connectivity”.

Theorem 3.15
.

[
28
]
(see also
[
7
, Theorem 1.4.3]
)
Let
G
be a graph, let
\delta=2|E(G)|/|V(G)|
denote the average degree of
G
, and let
k
be a positive integer. If
\delta\geq 4k
, then
G
contains a
k
-connected subgraph.

We shall also need the following simple combinatorial lemma.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
m\geq 2
be an integer. If
|E(G)|\geq 2m-2
, then
G
contains a subgraph on
m
edges and with minimum degree one.

Proof.

If
G
has a vertex of degree at least
m
, then it contains the star
K_{1,m}
as a subgraph. On the other hand, if
G
has a vertex
v
of degree at least
1
and at most
m-1
, then
G-v
has at least
m-1
edges. Hence we may take an arbitrary subgraph of
G-v
on
m-1
edges, plus an edge incident to
v
.
∎

Recall that given a bounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
with rank
r(\mathcal{M})
, we call an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit
small
if it has at most
r(\mathcal{M})
edges.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a bounded graph matroid family. Then
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property if and only if there is a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with minimum degree one.

More precisely, we shall see that if
m
is the minimum number of edges in a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with minimum degree one, then
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property with constant
c=\max(2m-2,4)
.

Proof.

First, suppose that there are no graphs among the small
\mathcal{M}
-circuits with minimum degree one; in particular, this means that there are no small
\mathcal{M}
-circuits that are forests. We shall show that for every positive integer
k
, there exists a
k
-connected graph
G
that is not
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of (isomorphism classes of) small
\mathcal{M}
-circuits. It follows from
Theorem

3.14
that there exists a
\mathcal{C}
-free graph
G^{\prime}
with
2|E(G^{\prime})|/|V(G^{\prime})|\geq 4k
, and by
Theorem

3.15
,
G^{\prime}
has a
k
-connected subgraph
G
. Since
G
does not contain small circuits of
\mathcal{M}
, every
\mathcal{M}
-circuit in
G
has size
r(\mathcal{M})+1
. Combining this with the fact that every graph on
r(\mathcal{M})+1
edges is
\mathcal{M}
-dependent, we deduce that
\mathcal{M}(G)
is a uniform matroid. It follows that any permutation of
E(G)
induces an automorphism of
\mathcal{M}(G)
. On the other hand, not every permutation of
E(G)
is induced by an automorphism of
G
(unless
G
is a star or a triangle, which is ruled out for
k\geq 3
), since a permutation induced by an automorphism must map incident edges to incident edges. This means that
G
is not
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible, as required.

Now let us suppose that there is a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with minimum degree one. By
Proposition

2.5
, there is a positive integer
m\geq 2
such that the star
K_{1,m}
is the unique small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit with minimum degree one on at most
m
edges.

Let
c=\max(2m-2,4)
. We shall show that every
c
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible. In fact, we show that minimum degree at least
c
suffices. Let
G
be a graph with minimum degree at least
c
, and suppose that
\psi:E(G)\to E(H)
is an isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}(H)
, for some graph
H
without isolated vertices. Fix a vertex
v\in V(G)
, and consider the subgraph
H^{\prime}
of
H
induced by
\psi(\partial_{G}(v))
. Since
|E(H^{\prime})|=d_{G}(v)\geq 2m-2
,
Lemma

3.16
implies that
H^{\prime}
has a subgraph
C
on
m
edges and with minimum degree one.

Note that since
K_{1,m}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit, every subset of
m
edges in
\partial_{G}(v)
induces an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit, and thus every subset of
m
edges in
H^{\prime}
induces an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit. This means that
C
is an
\mathcal{M}
-circuit, and hence by the choice of
m
,
C
is a star. In particular,
H^{\prime}
has a component of size at least
m+1
.

By the same reasoning, every forest of size
m
in
H^{\prime}
is a star. This implies that
H^{\prime}
is a star, for if it contained a pair of disjoint edges, then we could augment it to a forest in
H^{\prime}
of size
m
that is not a star. (It is easy to verify that apart from vertex stars, every graph on at least four edges has a pair of disjoint edges.)

This shows that
\psi
maps vertex stars to vertex stars. It follows by
Lemma

3.9
that
\psi
is induced by a graph isomorphism, as desired.
∎

3.3
Reconstructibility and matroid unions

Using the results of the previous subsections, we can give a quick proof of
Theorem

1.2
.
We will need the following recent result.

Theorem 3.18
.

[
11
, Theorem 1.6]

For every pair of positive integers
k
and
c
, there exists a positive integer
c^{\prime}
such that every
c^{\prime}
-connected graph contains
k
edge-disjoint
c
-connected spanning subgraphs.

In fact,
Theorem

3.18
is proved in
[
11
]
by showing, using our terminology, that the
k
-fold union of the family of
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroids has the Lovász-Yemini property.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

(a)
By assumption, there exists a constant
c
such that every
c
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. It follows from
Theorem

3.18
that there exist a constant
c^{\prime}
such that every
c^{\prime}
-connected graph contains
k
edge-disjoint spanning
c
-connected subgraphs. This also means that every
c^{\prime}
-connected graph contains edge-disjoint
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-rigid spanning subgraphs for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and hence by
Lemma

2.12
(a) that every
c^{\prime}
-connected graph is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid.

(b)

First, let us suppose that there is an unbounded graph matroid family among
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
, say
\mathcal{M}_{1}
.
By
Theorem

1.1
and
Lemma

3.13
, each of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
has the Lovász-Yemini property, and hence by part
(a)
, so does
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is unbounded, so is
\mathcal{M}
. It follows from
Theorem

1.1
that
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property.

Thus, we may assume that each of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{k}
is bounded. By
Theorem

3.17
and
Proposition

2.5
, there exist positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
such that
K_{1,m_{i}}
is the unique (small)
\mathcal{M}_{i}
-circuit with at most
m_{i}
edges that is a forest, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Let
m=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(m_{i}-1)+1
. It is easy to verify now that
K_{1,m}
is a small
\mathcal{M}
-circuit. It follows from
Theorem

3.17
that
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property.
∎

We note, without details, that for a nontrivial graph matroid family of dimensionality at least two, the de-coning operation (introduced at the end of
Section

2.3
) also preserves the Lovász-Yemini and the Whitney properties.

3.4
Extendability

Let us recall the following pair of definitions from the introduction. Given a graph
G
and a positive integer
d
, the
d
-dimensional edge split operation
replaces an edge
uv
of
G
with
a new vertex joined to
u
and
v
, as well as to
d-1
other vertices of
G
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family with dimensionality
d\geq 1
. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is
1
-extendable
if the
d
-dimensional edge split operation preserves
\mathcal{M}
-independence.

Nguyen defined
1
-extendability in the context of abstract rigidity matroids
[
29
]
. The naming comes from the fact that the
d
-dimensional edge-split operation is sometimes also called the
d
-dimensional
1
-extension operation.

Our goal in this subsection is to prove
Theorem

1.3
, which says that
1
-extendable graph matroid families have the Lovász-Yemini property. This can be shown by adapting the proof of
Theorem

3.11
in
[
36
]
. Since we are not concerned with optimizing the connectivity constant
c
, we instead follow another, more straightforward argument, also due to Villányi
[
35
]
. The same proof strategy was used in
[
11
]
to establish the Lovász-Yemini property for unions of generic rigidity matroids.

Given a graph
G
and a positive integer
k
, a set of vertices
U\subseteq V(G)
is a
k
-dominating set
in
G
if every vertex
v\in V(G)-U
has at least
k
neighbors in
U
in
G
. It is well-known that high minimum degree forces the existence of small
k
-dominating sets, in the following sense.

Theorem 3.19
.

(See, e.g.,
[
16
, Corollary 1]
)
Let
k
be a positive integer. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a positive integer
\delta
such that every graph
G
with minimum degree at least
\delta
has a
k
-dominating set
U
of size at most
\varepsilon|V(G)|
.

Let us fix a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
with dimensionality
d
, threshold
t
and rank function
r
for the remainder of this section. Let
r_{0}=r(K_{t})-d\cdot t
, so that for every
n\geq t
,
r(K_{n})=dn+r_{0}
. Let
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{6d}
, and let
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
be a positive integer such that every graph
G
with minimum degree at least
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
has a
d
-dominating set of size at most
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot|V(G)|
. The existence of
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
is guaranteed by
Theorem

3.19
. We may also assume that
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}\geq\max(6r_{0},t)
.

The following is the main technical lemma in our proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
be as in the above discussion and let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. If the minimum degree of
G
is at least
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
, then
G
has a vertex
v_{0}\in V
such that
r(G)\leq r(G-v_{0})+d
.

Proof.

Let
n
denote the number of vertices of
G
; note that
n\geq\delta_{\mathcal{M}}+1>\max(6r_{0},t).
By the definition of
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
, there exists a
d
-dominating set
U\subseteq V
in
G
of size at most
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot n
. Observe that

\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{6d}=\frac{1}{3d}-\frac{1}{6d}<\frac{1}{3d}-\frac{r_{0}}{dn},

where for the last inequality we used the assumption that
n>6r_{0}
.

Let us construct a subgraph
G_{0}=(V,E_{0})
of
G
by fixing
d
edges between
v
and
U
, for every
v\in V-U
. It follows from
Lemma

2.2
that
G_{0}
is
\mathcal{M}
-independent. Thus we may extend
G_{0}
to an
\mathcal{M}
-independent subgraph
G_{1}=(V,E_{0}\cup E_{1})
of
G
with
r(G_{1})=r(G)
.

Now

|E_{1}|=r(G)-r(G_{0})\leq dn+r_{0}-d(1-\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}})n=r_{0}+dn\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}}<r_{0}+(\frac{n}{3}-r_{0})=\frac{n}{3}.

Hence there are at least
\frac{n}{3}>\varepsilon_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot n
vertices not incident to any edge in
E_{1}
. In particular, there is such a vertex
v_{0}\in V-U
. By construction, this means that
d_{G_{1}}(v_{0})=d
. It follows that

r(G-v_{0})\geq r(G_{1})-d=r(G)-d,

as claimed.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Let
c=\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
, where
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}
is defined as above, and let
G=(V,E)
be a
c
-connected graph. We prove, by induction on
|V|
, that
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid. This shows that
\mathcal{M}
has the Lovász-Yemini property with constant
c
.

In the base case,
G
is a complete graph on
c+1
vertices, which is trivially
\mathcal{M}
-rigid. Let us thus assume that
|V|\geq c+2
. It follows from
Lemma

3.20
that
G
has a vertex
v_{0}\in V
such that
r(G)\leq r(G-v_{0})+d
. Note that
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}\geq t
, and hence
d_{G}(v_{0})\geq\delta_{\mathcal{M}}\geq t\geq d+1
.

We claim that for every pair of nonadjacent vertices
x,y\in N_{G}(v_{0})
, we have
r(G-v_{0})=r(G-v_{0}+xy)
. Indeed, otherwise we could take an
\mathcal{M}
-independent subgraph
H
of
G-v_{0}+xy
that contains
xy
and has
r(G-v_{0})+1
edges. We can obtain an
\mathcal{M}
-independent subgraph
H^{\prime}
of
G
with
r(G-v_{0})+d+1
edges by performing a
d
-dimensional edge split on
x
and
y
, where the newly added vertex is
v_{0}
, and using the fact that
\mathcal{M}
is
1
-extendable. It follows that
r(G)\geq r(G-v_{0})+d+1
, contradicting the choice of
v_{0}
.

Let
G^{\prime}=G-v_{0}+K_{N_{G}(v_{0})}
. The argument in the previous paragraph shows that
r(G-v_{0})=r(G^{\prime})
. Note that
G^{\prime}
is also
c
-connected, since it can be obtained from
G+K_{N_{G}(v_{0})}
by the deletion of
v_{0}
, and it is easy to see that deleting a vertex whose neighbors form a clique cannot destroy
c
-connectivity, unless the graph has only
c+1
vertices. By induction,
G^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, and hence so is
G-v_{0}
. Since
d_{G}(v_{0})\geq c\geq d
,
Lemma

2.2
implies that
G
is also
\mathcal{M}
-rigid, as desired.
∎

Among the graph matroid families considered in
Section

2.3
, the family of count matroids
\mathcal{M}_{k,\ell}
, the families
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
constructed by Schmidt, the family of generic rigidity matroids
\mathcal{R}_{d}
, and the family
\mathcal{C}_{d}
of generic
C^{d-1}_{d-2}
-cofactor matroids are all
1
-extendable. It is also not difficult to show that the operations of taking unions and de-coning both preserve
1
-extendability.

However, the family
\mathcal{E}
of even cycle matroids is not
1
-extendable, since applying the
1
-dimensional edge split operation (i.e., edge subdivision) to an odd cycle, which is
\mathcal{E}
-independent, results in an even cycle, which is not. Also note that
\mathcal{E}
does not have the Lovász-Yemini property, since a bipartite graph on at least three vertices is not
\mathcal{E}
-rigid, no matter how highly vertex-connected it is. It is unclear if there exists a nontrivial, unbounded graph matroid family that is not
1
-extendable, but does have the Lovász-Yemini property.

4
Concluding remarks

We conclude by discussing potential extensions of the ideas considered throughout the paper.

Other properties.

Both the Lovász-Yemini and the Whitney properties are assertions about sufficiently highly vertex-connected graphs.
We may also consider other one-parameter families of graph properties
\mathcal{P}(c)
besides
c
-connectivity. (Here the range of the parameter
c
is the natural numbers, and we assume that property
\mathcal{P}(c+1)
implies property
\mathcal{P}(c)
, for every
c
.) Let us say that a graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}

has the Lovász-Yemini property with respect to
\mathcal{P}
if there is some constant
c
such that every graph satisfying
\mathcal{P}(c)
is
\mathcal{M}
-rigid. We also define the Whitney property w.r.t.
\mathcal{P}
similarly.
Lemma

3.2
generalizes to this setting as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(c)
be a one-parameter family of graph properties such that
\mathcal{P}(c)
is edge-monotone for every nonnegative integer
c
. If an unbounded graph matroid family
\mathcal{M}
has the Whitney property w.r.t.
\mathcal{P}
, then it has the Lovász-Yemini property w.r.t.
\mathcal{P}
.

It would be interesting to identify other properties
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(c)
for which the converse holds.

Other settings.

Viewed abstractly, a graph matroid family consists of a collection of matroids defined on some class of combinatorial objects (in this case, edge sets of finite simple graphs) that is invariant under certain maps (in this case, graph isomorphisms), as well as compatible, in the sense that the matroid assigned to a sub-object (in this case, a subgraph) is obtained as a restriction of the original matroid.

This blueprint can be easily adapted to other settings, such as

•

bipartite graph matroid families, where the objects are edge sets of finite bipartite graphs, and the morphisms are rooted isomorphisms (graph isomorphisms that preserve the color classes);

•

multigraph matroid families, where the objects are edge sets of finite multigraphs, and the morphisms are graph isomorphisms;

•

k
-hypergraph matroid families, where the objects are edge sets of
k
-uniform hypergraphs, and the morphisms are hypergraph isomorphisms.

These models can capture additional matroid families appearing in the literature, such as bipartite rigidity matroids
[
22
]
, count matroids on hypergraphs
[
39
]
, or low-rank tensor completion matroids
[
6
]
.
It would be interesting to investigate whether some analogues of
Theorems

1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
hold in these settings.

In this direction, it was recently shown
[
10
]
that the family of
generic
(a,b)
-birigidity matroids
, defined on the edge sets of bipartite graphs, has the Lovász-Yemini-property, in the sense that every sufficiently highly connected bipartite graph has the same rank in these matroids as the complete bipartite graph on the same bipartition. More generally, following the methods of
[
10
]
it can be shown that every bipartite graph matroid family in which the so-called
double
1
-extension
operation preserves independence has the Lovász-Yemini property. We suspect that these families also have the (bipartite version of the) Whitney property. See
[
25
]
for basic results about the structure of bipartite graph matroid families.

We note that
k
-hypergraph matroid families were investigated by Kalai
[
24
]
under the name of symmetric
k
-matroids. Given such a family of matroids
\mathcal{M}
, the
growth function
of
\mathcal{M}
is the function mapping each positive integer
n
to the
\mathcal{M}
-rank of the complete
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices. Kalai gave a characterization of growth functions of
k
-hypergraph matroid families. In particular, he proved a generalization of
Lemma

2.1
, saying that the growth function of
\mathcal{M}
is eventually polynomial, for any
\mathcal{M}
.

Reconstruction from matroids.

As noted in the introduction, throughout the paper, the term
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructibility is used in the sense that the problem of reconstructing
G
from
\mathcal{M}(G)
has a unique solution, up to isomorphism.
Theorems

1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
provide many examples of graph matroid families
\mathcal{M}
for which every sufficiently highly connected graph is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible in this sense.

Nonetheless, even if
G
is
\mathcal{M}
-reconstructible, it is unclear whether we can actually construct
G
from
\mathcal{M}(G)
efficiently (e.g., using a polynomial number of
\mathcal{M}
-independence oracle calls). Finding such reconstruction algorithms for particular graph matroid families remains an interesting open problem. It seems that such an algorithm is only available for the family of graphic matroids
[
32
]
.

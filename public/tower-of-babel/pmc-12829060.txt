The phenotypic landscape of p.Ala117Ser transthyretin amyloidosis: distinct clinical profiles and outcomes versus wild-type ATTR
Authors: Phakin Tuntiwongkosee, Patchara Kochaiyapatana, Prasit Phowthongkum, Jakkrit Amornvit, Khanidta Saicharoen, Pairoj Chattranukulchai, Krailerk Chettakulanurak, Sarinya Puwanant
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to (1) compare the clinical phenotype and outcomes of patients with transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR) harboring the p.Ala117Ser variant versus those with wild-type ATTR (wtATTR), and (2) delineate the clinical spectrum of ATTR at our institution.MethodsWe conducted a single-center observational study of 43 consecutive symptomatic ATTR patients and 7 asymptomatic carriers of pathogenic ATTR variants. All underwent structured evaluation in the cardiomyopathy (CM) clinic. The primary endpoint was a composite of all-cause mortality or heart/heart–liver transplantation.ResultsAmong the 50 patients enrolled with ATTR, hereditary ATTR (hATTR) accounted for 53%. Within the hATTR group, the p.Ala117Ser variant was the most frequently identified pathogenic variant, representing 40% of cases. Of the 43 symptomatic patients, 41 (95%) were diagnosed with ATTR-CM. Symptomatic p.Ala117Ser patients were younger, had universal neuropathy, and exhibited less advanced cardiac involvement compared with wtATTR. Three-year event-free survival was higher in p.Ala117Ser patients, remaining significant after excluding asymptomatic carriers (log-rank p = 0.006). Univariate predictors of adverse outcomes included wtATTR, prior heart failure hospitalization, elevated NT-proBNP, older age, increased wall thickness, and higher NYHA class. Multivariate analysis identified p.Ala117Ser variant as independently associated with improved outcomes (HR 0.23, 95% CI 0.06–0.95).ConclusionsThe p.Ala117Ser variant emerged as the most common hATTR in our cohort. Symptomatic carriers were younger, had universal neuropathy (100%), had less advanced cardiac disease, and demonstrated superior survival and transplant-free outcomes compared with wtATTR. These findings highlight the value of genotype-guided evaluation and the potential for precision therapies tailored to regional TTR variants.Trial registrationClinical Trial Number: Not Applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05313-3.Keywords: Cardiomyopathy, Transthyretin, Transthyretin amyloidosis, ATTR, P.Ala117Ser, Ala97Ser, HATTR

Introduction

Transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR) is a systemic disorder characterized by extracellular deposition of misfolded transthyretin protein, primarily involving the myocardium and peripheral nervous system [1–5]. Genotypically, ATTR is classified into wild-type (wtATTR) and hereditary (hATTR) forms. While wtATTR results from abnormal aggregation of relatively normal transthyretin, hATTR is caused by autosomal dominant single-point variants in the TTR gene, leading to the production and aggregation of variant transthyretin [6–10]. Numerous genetic variants linked to hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR), such as p.Val50Met (Valine-to-Methionine substitution at position 50) and p.Val142Ile variant (Valine-to-Isoleucine substitution at position 142), have been documented; however, the distribution of genotypes and their corresponding phenotypes exhibit notable geographic variability [3, 11–15]. Of particular interest, the p.Ala117Ser variant (Alanine-to-Serine substitution at position 117) has been reported as the predominant variant in previous studies conducted in regions across Asia, including Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan, and Southern China [15–20]. Consistent with these findings, our cohort demonstrated that the p.Ala117Ser variant is the most frequently encountered genotype among patients presenting with symptomatic ATTR-related polyneuropathy [21]. However, the clinical features and prognosis of patients harboring the p.Ala117Ser variant remain poorly characterized. Furthermore, research on transthyretin amyloidosis in Thailand is limited, with ongoing efforts to establish patient registries aimed at elucidating the disease’s natural history and outcomes. Therefore, this study aims to (1) delineate our institutional experience with both hereditary and wild-type ATTR, and (2) compare the clinical characteristics of patients harboring the p.Ala117Ser variant against those diagnosed with wtATTR.

Methods

Study design and population

This is a single-center observational study conducted at King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital between January 1, 2010, and July 15, 2025. Consecutive eligible adult participants (aged ≥ 18 years) included symptomatic patients diagnosed with transthyretin amyloidosis (Supplement file 1) and asymptomatic individuals harboring confirmed pathogenic variants in the TTR gene. All participants were evaluated at the cardiomyopathy clinic of our institution. The case record form used in this study was developed originally for this purpose and can be found in the Supplemental Material file.

Diagnosis of transthyretin amyloidosis cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM)

ATTR cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) was diagnosed using either (1) invasive criteria, defined by histological confirmation of amyloid deposition in endomyocardial biopsy specimens, or (2) noninvasive criteria, characterized by cardiac involvement with left ventricular wall thickness > 12 mm on echocardiography and a Perugini grade ≥ 2 on 99mTc-pyrophosphate (PYP) scintigraphy. Both diagnostic pathways required the absence of urine or serum free light chains or monoclonal proteins, as confirmed by immunofixation techniques. Supportive diagnostic features included late gadolinium enhancement patterns consistent with cardiac amyloidosis (e.g., subendocardial or patchy distribution) and elevated T1 values and extracellular volume (ECV) on cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and/or a family history of ATTR. Hereditary ATTR-CM (hATTR-CM) was defined as ATTR-CM with confirmed pathogenic variants in the TTR gene, whereas wtATTR-CM was defined by the absence of such variants.

Diagnosis of transthyretin amyloidosis polyneuropathy (ATTR-PN)

ATTR-PN was diagnosed when electromyography (EMG) confirmed a length-dependent polyneuropathy together with a confirmed diagnosis of ATTR-CM in a patient with a previously confirmed diagnosis. EMG was performed using the Viking system on the Nicolet EDX platform (Viking software version 21; Natus Medical Incorporated, San Carlos, CA, USA) in accordance with established protocols [21]. In addition, quantitative sensory testing (QST) was conducted with the Computer-Assisted Sensory Evaluator IV (CASE IV™) system (WR Medical Electronics Co., Maplewood, MN, USA) to evaluate multiple sensory modalities. Specifically, vibration detection threshold (VDT) at the great toe and index finger, cold detection threshold (CDT), and heat-pain sensation (HPS) on the dorsal aspects of the hands and feet were measured using the standard CASE IV protocol. Test results were considered abnormal if values fell outside the 5th − 95th percentile of age and sex-matched reference data provided by the manufacturer. All QST evaluations were administered by trained technicians, and each stimulus modality was tested using the method-of-limits algorithm according to the device’s standard settings. Neuropathy symptom severity and functional impairment were graded using the modified Polyneuropathy Disability (mPND) score, as previously described [22]. The mPND score was recorded for each patient at the time of diagnosis, with higher scores indicating more advanced disability, ranging from sensory disturbances only to wheelchair-bound status.

Predominant phenotypes

The predominant cardiac phenotype was defined by the presence of: (1) diagnostic criteria for ATTR-CM; (2) current or prior clinical syndromes of heart failure or clinical symptoms related to rhythm disturbances (e.g. heart block, sick sinus syndrome, atrial fibrillation); and (3) no more than mild neurological abnormalities, corresponding to an mPND score of 0–I. The predominant neurologic phenotype was defined by: (1) fulfillment of diagnostic criteria for ATTR-PN; and (2) neurologic symptoms corresponding to an mPND score of III–IV, with or without concurrent ATTR-CM. A mixed phenotype was defined as meeting diagnostic criteria for both ATTR-CM and ATTR-PN with approximately equal clinical severity, failing to meet the criteria for either predominant cardiac or neurological phenotypes. Isolated ATTR-CM was defined as the presence of ATTR-CM without concomitant ATTR-PN, whereas isolated ATTR-PN was defined as the presence of ATTR-PN in the absence of ATTR-CM.

Clinical assessment

At the initial visit to the cardiomyopathy (CM) clinic, patients completed the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) to assess quality of life, underwent mPND scoring, and provided a detailed three-generation family history. In line with clinic protocol, laboratory evaluation included cardiac troponin, N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and serum prealbumin. The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the CKD-EPI equation, and National Amyloidosis Center (NAC) staging was determined from eGFR and NT-proBNP values as previously described [23–25]. Clinical data were collected by a single investigator (P.T.) from CM clinic records, electronic medical files, and the IntelliSpace Cardiovascular (ISCV) Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS). Symptomatic patients were scheduled for clinic visits every 8 to 12 weeks if clinically stable, whereas asymptomatic carriers underwent annual clinical evaluations.

Cardiac evaluation

A 12-lead ECG was recorded in accordance with established procedures [26]. Low voltage was defined as a QRS amplitude of < 5 mm in all limb leads or < 10 mm in all precordial leads on standard calibration tracings. Pseudo-infarct patterns were characterized by pathologic Q waves in leads V1–V3 or poor R-wave progression in the absence of myocardial infarction and bundle branch block. All patients underwent transthoracic echocardiography using commercially available ultrasound systems, including the EPIQ CVx and IE33 (Philips Medical Systems, Andover, MA) and the Vivid 7 (GE Healthcare, Vingmed Ultrasound, Horten, Norway). Image acquisition and interpretation followed standard transthoracic imaging protocols in accordance with the guidelines of the American Society of Echocardiography. When clinically warranted, cardiac MRI examinations were performed on both 1.5-T (Signa HD, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, USA; MAGNETOM Aera, Siemens Healthineers, Germany) and 3.0-T (Achieva, Philips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands; MAGNETOM Skyra and MAGNETOM Vida, Siemens Healthineers, Germany) scanners, utilizing synchronized electrocardiographic and respiratory gating techniques. Imaging sequences included steady-state free precession cine imaging, first-pass myocardial perfusion, and late gadolinium enhancement, all acquired following standardized protocols [27]. Endomyocardial biopsies were conducted using routine approaches, with a minimum of four tissue samples collected per procedure [28, 29]. Scintigraphy using 99mTc-PYP was conducted following intravenous administration of 15 mCi of the tracer, with imaging performed at 1 h and 3 h post-injection. Myocardial uptake of 99mTc-PYP was assessed using a semi-quantitative visual grading scale, referencing the intensity of rib uptake: Grade 0 indicated no myocardial uptake with normal bone uptake; Grade 1 corresponded to myocardial uptake less than that of the rib; Grade 2 represented uptake equal to rib intensity; and Grade 3 denoted myocardial uptake exceeding rib uptake, accompanied by mild or absent rib visualization [30]. Symptomatic patients underwent annual echocardiographic evaluations when clinically stable, whereas asymptomatic carriers underwent echocardiographic assessments every three years.

Pathological analysis

Histological examination of endomyocardial or nerve tissue samples was performed after fixation in formalin. Five-micrometer sections from the interventricular septum were stained with hematoxylin and eosin and Masson’s trichrome. Congo red staining and immunohistochemical analysis were conducted according to standard protocols[30].

Genetic analysis

Peripheral blood samples were collected from patients for genetic testing. Genomic analysis was performed either at Labcorp/Invitae (San Francisco, CA, USA) using next-generation sequencing panels targeting the TTR gene and other amyloidosis-associated genes, or at Genetic and Medico-Genetic Laboratory “Genetica” (Sofia, Bulgaria) for targeted variant detection using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification followed by Sanger sequencing (Sponsored by Pfizer (Thailand). Identified variants were classified according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines [31].

Outcome

The primary outcome was overall event-free survival, defined as the interval from diagnosis to death or heart/heart-liver transplantation. Follow-up data were obtained during regularly scheduled visits. For patients who had not attended a follow-up visit within one year of their last appointment, investigators from the enrolling center contacted the patient or their relatives by telephone to update vital status and clinical information.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on the distribution assessed by the coefficient of variation. Categorical data were reported as percentages. Between-group comparisons for continuous variables utilized either Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test according to normality and homogeneity of variance assumptions. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test when expected cell counts were low. Survival was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier approach, and comparisons between the p.Ala117Ser and wild-type ATTR groups were made with the log-rank test. Potential predictors of outcomes were explored through univariate Cox proportional hazards regression. Given the small number of outcome events, multivariable Cox models for the overall cohort were limited to a maximum of two covariates per analysis to minimize overfitting. Owing to the small number of outcome events, multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were constrained to include no more than two covariates per analysis to reduce the potential for model overfitting. The association between the p.Ala117Ser variant and clinical outcomes was then examined using a multivariate model adjusted for SGLT2 inhibitor use, thereby assessing its potential role as an independent prognostic factor. No formal correction for multiple testing was applied due to the limited sample size; however, key findings were derived from multivariate analyses, which help mitigate the risk of Type I error. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed p-value < 0.05. Statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS software, version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY), and STATA software, version 19.5 (STATA Corp., College Station, TX).

Results

Genotype-phenotypic distribution

Among the 50 patients enrolled with ATTR, hereditary ATTR (hATTR) accounted for 53% of patients. Within the hATTR group, the p.Ala117Ser variant was the most frequently identified pathogenic variant, representing 40% of cases (Fig. 1). The p.Val50Met variant was observed in 9% of patients. In the total cohort, 7 individuals were asymptomatic gene carriers, including 6 with the p.Ala117Ser variant and 1 with the p.Val142Ile variant. Baseline characteristics of symptomatic patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant and those with wtATTR are compared in Table 1. Compared to patients with wtATTR, symptomatic individuals with the p.Ala117Ser variant were younger, more frequently female, had a younger age at diagnosis, and presented with less advanced disease and higher estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Polyneuropathy was universally present in symptomatic individuals with the p.Ala117Ser variant, whereas cardiomyopathy was present in all patients with wtATTR (Fig. 2).

Distribution of clinical phenotypes in symptomatic transthyretin amyloidosis patients by genotype

Cardiac involvement

Of the 43 symptomatic patients, 41 (95%) were diagnosed with ATTR cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), including 51% with wtATTR, 24% with the p.Ala117Ser variant, and 25% with other variants. Compared to patients with wtATTR, symptomatic individuals with the p.Ala117Ser variant exhibited lower rates of biventricular systolic dysfunction, atrial fibrillation, and elevated troponin levels. Low-voltage findings on electrocardiography were present in 22% of the overall cohort, with no significant difference between the wtATTR and p.Ala117Ser subgroups. Cardiac MRI was performed in 70.7% of ATTR-CM patients, with no significant difference between the p.Ala117Ser and wtATTR groups (70.0% vs. 76.2%, p = 1.000), primarily to exclude alternative causes of cardiomyopathy. Among patients with ATTR-CM, 37 were diagnosed with stage C or D heart failure (HF). Of these, 24 (65%) had heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF) or preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF). The prevalence of HF among symptomatic patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant was 75%, compared to 90.5% in those with wtATTR-CM (p = 0.242).

Survival and transplantation outcomes

Overall cohort

Survival data were available for all patients. During a median follow-up of 43 months, 17 patients (34%) died, and 2 patients (4%) underwent organ transplantation—one received a combined heart-liver transplant, and the other received an isolated heart transplant. Median survival for the overall cohort was 61 months. When stratified by genotype, the median survival for the wild-type group was 52 months (95% CI: 30 to 74 months), while the median survival was not reached in the p.Ala117Ser group. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated significantly lower rates of mortality and organ transplantation in patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant compared to those with wtATTR (log-rank p = 0.006) (Fig. 3A). In univariate analysis, the p.Ala117Ser variant, history of heart failure hospitalization, elevated NT-proBNP levels, age, left ventricular posterior wall thickness, and NYHA functional class were all identified as predictors of adverse outcomes (Table 2S). In multivariate analysis, p.Ala117Ser variant remained statistically significant after adjusted for history of heart failure hospitalization.

Symptomatic patients

Survival analysis demonstrated significantly lower rates of mortality and organ transplantation among symptomatic patients carrying the p.Ala117Ser variant compared to those with wtATTR (log-rank p = 0.049) (Fig. 3B). Univariate analysis using the Cox proportional hazards model identified a history of heart failure hospitalization and elevated NT-proBNP levels as significant predictors of adverse outcomes, while neurological involvement was associated with better prognosis. Multivariate analysis identified the p.Ala117Ser variant as a significant predictor of survival after adjusting for SGLT-2 inhibitor use (Table 2).

Asymptomatic carriers

During a median follow-up period of 43 months, none of the seven carriers met the primary outcome or developed symptoms or clinical findings of transthyretin amyloidosis.

Discussion

This is the first study to compare clinical characteristics and prognosis of patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant ATTR and those with wtATTR. Our findings highlight that the p.Ala117Ser variant is the most prevalent genetic variant among patients with hATTR in our cohort. All symptomatic individuals with this variant exhibited polyneuropathy (100%); 50% presented with a mixed (cardiac, and neurologic) predominant phenotype. Compared to patients with wtATTR, symptomatic individuals with the p.Ala117Ser variant were younger, more frequently female, had a younger age at diagnosis, and presented with less advanced cardiac disease based on clinical and echocardiographic findings.

Genotype-phenotypic distribution and the p. Ala 117 Ser variant

Our study presents the first genotype–phenotype mapping of patients with ATTR in Thailand. We found that the majority of cases in our cohort were either wtATTR or p.Ala117Ser variant. While the p.Val50Met and p.Val142Ile variants are the most common variants associated with hATTR worldwide [3, 4, 11–13, 32, 33], the p.Ala117Servariant was distinctively the most prevalent in our cohort. This distinct variant has also been commonly reported in Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan, and China, suggesting a shared ancestral origin[15–20]. Patients with ATTR harboring the p.Ala117Ser variant exhibit a predominant and severe polyneuropathy phenotype, accompanied by a relatively high proportion of female patients, comprising nearly 35% of the cohort. This proportion exceeds that reported in previous studies, which documented female representation of less than 25% [20, 34] In contrast, all patients with wtATTR in this cohort were male. The underlying basis for this male predilection remains incompletely understood. The observed gender disparity may reflect genetic predisposition or hormonal influences, including a potential protective role of estrogen[35]. Additionally, LV wall thickness tends to be lower in older women, which may contribute to the underdiagnosis of wtATTR in female patients[36]. In our cohort, the age of onset in patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant was younger than in those with wtATTR. Given the strong association of the p.Ala117Ser variant with severe polyneuropathy, the presence of neurologic symptoms may trigger earlier medical evaluation leading to diagnosis at a younger age.

Cardiac involvement

Compared to patients with wtATTR, symptomatic individuals with the p.Ala117Ser variant exhibited less advanced cardiac disease and a lower prevalence of atrial fibrillation. A plausible explanation may lie in the influence of the genetic variant itself or the possibility that patients with the p.Ala117Servariant tend to present at an earlier stage of disease progression compared to those with wtATTR[37]. Additionally, atrial fibrillation is more frequently observed in elderly populations, which may partially account for its higher prevalence in the wild-type group[38]. Batra et al.reported that although women were diagnosed with ATTR-CM approximately eight years later than men, their LV function and survival outcomes were comparable[39]. This finding implies that disease progression in females may be less aggressive, potentially contributing to the diagnostic challenge in this population. LV wall thickness in ATTR patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant in our cohort was comparable to the findings reported by Lai H-J et al. [40] However, the measurements were greater than those reported in several other studies[19, 41]. This discrepancy may be attributed to the inclusion of asymptomatic gene carriers in earlier cohorts, which could have resulted in overall lower LV wall thickness values. Low-voltage findings on electrocardiography were observed in 29% of patients with ATTR overall, and in 22% of those with the p.Ala117Servariant. This represents a lower proportion compared to previous studies[40]. In the THAOS registry, low voltage was reported in 32% of patients with wtATTR and in 46% of those with the p.Val142Ilevariant[3]. Pseudo-infarct patterns were frequently observed in our cohort, present in nearly half of the symptomatic patients. These findings are consistent with previous studies, which reported a prevalence of this electrocardiographic pattern ranging from 23% to 69% across their cohorts [42, 43]. The degree of left ventricular systolic dysfunction observed in patients with wtATTR in our cohort was markedly greater than that reported in previous studies [3, 44], suggesting a more advanced stage of disease at the time of diagnosis.

Several studies have highlighted the association between HF-pEF and cardiac amyloidosis, particularly ATTR. For instance, Pablo G-P et al. found that approximately 17% of elderly patients with HF-pEF had underlying cardiac amyloidosis [45], underscoring that ATTR may be an underrecognized contributor to HF-pEF in older adults. This finding supports the growing recognition of ATTR-CM as an important etiology in this patient population. In our cohort, patients with HF-pEF and HF-mrEF accounted for 65% of the study population with stage C/D heart failure, further emphasizing the clinical overlap between ATTR-CM and these heart failure phenotypes[45–47]. The substantial proportion of HFpEF/HFmrEF patients in this cohort aligns with current literature and supports the need for routine evaluation for ATTR-CM in this subgroup, especially in elderly patients or those with concomitant neuropathic symptoms.

Predictors of survival and transplantation outcomes

We found that 3-year survival among patients with ATTR in this cohort was 75%, consistent with findings from a study by Tseng W-H et al., which evaluated patients with ATTR-related polyneuropathy in Taiwan[34]. In our cohort, symptomatic patients with ATTR associated with the p.Ala117Servariant had a more favorable prognosis compared to those with wtATTR. Given the distinct pathophysiological mechanisms of hereditary versus wtATTR, survival differences have been hypothesized not only between these two groups but also among specific hereditary variants, which may be associated with distinct clinical phenotypes[2]. Several studies have evaluated outcomes in patients with the common p.Val142Ilevariant relative to wtATTR, demonstrating trends toward worse prognosis in the variant group, although statistical significance has varied[33, 48]. However, data on less common variants remains limited. To our knowledge, this is the first study to directly compare clinical outcomes between the p.Ala117Ser variant and wtATTR. Overall, survival was significantly higher in the p.Ala117Ser group and was still significantly higher when excluding asymptomatic carriers. The favorable prognosis may, in part, be attributed to the earlier stage of disease at presentation in patients with ATTR associated with the p.Ala117Ser variant.

In our cohort, heart failure hospitalization, advanced NYHA functional class, and elevated NT-proBNP levels were identified as significant predictors of death or heart transplantation, with heart failure hospitalization emerging as the strongest predictor (hazard ratio 8.75). These findings underscore the pivotal role of cardiac involvement as the primary determinant of adverse outcomes[2, 5]. The p.Ala117Ser variant was also significantly associated with a more favorable prognosis, even after adjustment for heart failure hospitalization in multivariate analysis. In the subgroup of symptomatic patients, heart failure hospitalization and elevated NT-proBNP remained the only independent predictors of unfavorable outcomes, further highlighting the association between the severity of cardiac involvement and poor prognosis. Notably, univariate analysis did not identify the p.Ala117Ser variant as a significant predictor of outcomes, which may have been confounded by the significantly lower rate of SGLT-2 inhibitor use compared with wtATTR patients, as prior studies have linked SGLT-2 inhibitors to reduced mortality [49]. However, when the multivariate analysis was adjusted for SGLT-2 inhibitor use, the p.Ala117Ser variant was again significantly associated with better prognosis inhibitors with reduced mortality. However, multivariate analysis, adjusted for SGLT-2 inhibitor use, demonstrated that the p.Ala117Ser variant was significantly associated with better prognosis.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. Although this represents the largest cohort of ATTR patients reported in Thailand to date, the overall sample size remains modest compared to studies from Western populations. We acknowledge that multiple univariate analyses were performed, without correction methods. However, in this limited sample size context, correction methods may be too strict and reduce the power to find true differences. Therefore, results of univariate analyses should be interpreted with caution. Dividing patients into two comparison groups further reduced statistical power and restricted the use of multivariate analysis to 2 variables in a single model. This limitation was amplified when comparing multiple groups, such as distribution of NYHA class, and NAC staging among genotypes, or comparing KCCQ-12 scores among NYHA class, and NAC staging groups. As an observational study, our design is inherently subjected to limitations such as missing data, selection bias, and limited ability to adjust for confounding variables. Additionally, two independent laboratories employing different methodologies (next-generation sequencing and polymerase chain reaction-Sanger) were utilized to identify pathogenic variants. However, cross-validation between the two methods was not performed; thus, potential variability in the results cannot be excluded.

Conclusions

ATTR is increasingly recognized in Thailand, with wtATTR and the p.Ala117Ser variant being the 2 most common forms—the latter representing the most frequently identified variant. Symptomatic patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant were younger, predominantly exhibited a neurologic phenotype, and presented with less advanced disease compared to those with wtATTR. Patients with the p.Ala117Ser variant demonstrated a more favorable prognosis with respect to survival and freedom from organ transplantation, both in the entire cohort and symptomatic subgroup.

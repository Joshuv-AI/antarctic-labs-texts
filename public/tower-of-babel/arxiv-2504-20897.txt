Elliptic leading singularities and canonical integrands
E. Chaubey, V. Sotnikov

Abstract
In the well-studied genus zero case, bases of d log \differential\log integrands with integer leading singularities define Feynman integrals that automatically satisfy differential equations in canonical form. Such integrand bases can be constructed without input from the differential equations and without explicit involvement of dimensional regularization parameter ϵ \epsilon . We propose a generalization of this construction to genus one geometry arising from the appearance of elliptic curves. We argue that a particular choice of algebraic one-forms of the second kind that avoids derivatives is crucial. We observe that the corresponding Feynman integrals satisfy a special form of differential equations that has not been previously reported, and that their solutions order by order in ϵ \epsilon yield pure functions. We conjecture that our integrand-level construction universally leads to such differential equations.

We confirm the existence of such DEs in several state-of-the-art examples and conjecture that our integrand-based construction universally leads to such a form.

Canonical integrands

II.1
Logarithmic leading singularities

For completeness, we briefly review the method of Ref.
[
55

A logarithmic

Taking the LS corresponds to computing

(5)

This establishes the connection between the space of cycles (homology) and the differential forms (cohomology).
Considering specific cycles that encircle (a subset of) denominators in

1

One then searches for a spanning set

(6)

It has been conjectured

,
60
]
and confirmed in numerous practical applications that the basis of Feynman integrals

corresponding to the integrands

satisfies a differential equation in the canonical form

\differential{\bar{G}}=\epsilon A~\bar{G},\qquad A=\sum_{i}A_{i}\differential{\log{W_i}},

(7)

where

are algebraic functions of

It is well understood, however, that there exist cases where the integrand cannot be fully localized by products of circles as in

5

and presents a geometrical obstruction to the construction outlined above.

II.2

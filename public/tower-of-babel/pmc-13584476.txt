Nurse-led respiratory care bundle and pneumonia prevention in critically ill patients with abdominal sepsis: a multicenter before-and-after controlled study

Background

Abdominal sepsis is a life-threatening condition in the intensive care unit, characterized by rapid progression, extensive inflammation, and a mortality rate of 30%–50% [1]. This condition is associated with several pathological mechanisms, including increased intra-abdominal pressure (IAP), intestinal microbial imbalance, and tissue damage from surgery, which increase the risk of respiratory complications. Previous studies have reported that approximately 20%–40% of patients with abdominal sepsis develop pneumonia. Pneumonia is one of the most common complications in these patients [2–4].

Patients with abdominal sepsis are at an elevated risk of pneumonia due to an impaired cough reflex, prolonged bed rest, and respiratory muscle dysfunction. Bedside respiratory care measures, including positioning, airway clearance, oral hygiene, and lung recruitment, have been demonstrated to reduce postoperative pulmonary complications after major abdominal surgery and to improve clinical outcomes in patients with sepsis-associated pulmonary infection when delivered using a nurse-led, goal-directed approach [5–7]. In China, intensive care unit (ICU) nurses primarily deliver these respiratory care measures, reflecting their central role in providing continuous bedside care. However, relevant clinical practice guidelines generally lack specific operating standards and technical pathways for this nurse-led respiratory care. Consequently, its application in clinical practice relies on individual clinical experience, and evidence regarding the effectiveness of a nurse-led respiratory care bundle in preventing pneumonia among patients with abdominal sepsis remains limited [8].

In the past, Cui et al. developed and evaluated a nurse-led respiratory care bundle and conducted systematic clinical validation studies in patients with pneumonia complicated by sepsis [7, 9]. Based on these findings, we extended this intervention to patients with abdominal sepsis. Patients with abdominal sepsis are often sedated, receive analgesics, have limited mobility after surgery, and experience elevated IAP, all of which impair secretion clearance. Therefore, standardized pulmonary care is urgently needed.

This study aimed to evaluate the association of a nurse-led respiratory care bundle with the incidence of pneumonia and early clinical outcomes in patients with abdominal sepsis and to provide evidence supporting standardized respiratory care for this high-risk population.

Methods

Research design and enrolled population

This was a multicenter before-and-after study conducted to evaluate the association of a nurse-led respiratory care bundle with the risk of pneumonia and the duration of hospitalization in patients with abdominal sepsis. The study population comprised patients with abdominal sepsis admitted to the Department of Intensive Care Medicine (ICU) of Peking Union Medical College Hospital and four other tertiary hospitals in Beijing. Immunosuppressed patients were excluded from this study, as their altered immune status might confound the evaluation of pulmonary outcomes and obscure the true effect of the nurse-led respiratory care bundle. The study comprised two parts. The first stage comprised the control group, and patients with abdominal sepsis were retrospectively identified from ICU admissions of Peking Union Medical College Hospital between January 1, 2021, and June 30, 2023. All patients in this group received traditional respiratory care. The second stage comprised an intervention group, and from August 1, 2023, to January 31, 2026, patients with abdominal sepsis were prospectively enrolled in the ICUs of five medical centers in Beijing. The five participating medical centers were Peking Union Medical College Hospital (center 1), Beijing Hospital (center 2), Beijing Jishuitan Hospital (center 3), Beijing Shijitan Hospital (center 4), and Beijing Chao-yang Hospital (center 5). These centers had established clinical and research collaborations, with comparable general surgical diagnostic and treatment facilities and critical care capabilities. All centers followed the same intervention protocol, ensuring procedural consistency and improving the generalizability of the study findings. All patients in the intervention group received a nurse-led respiratory care bundle. A one-month training period was arranged for July 2023. To ensure that the intervention measures across all research centers were uniform, the organizers arranged for all medical staff in the participating hospitals to undergo joint training before the start of the study. The training covered the intervention protocol, operating procedures, evaluation criteria, and data collection procedures for the nurse-led respiratory care bundle program. Throughout the study period, the main research group regularly visited the participating centers for on-site guidance and conducted remote supervision. These measures ensured protocol adherence across all participating centers.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was approved by the relevant ethics committees. The retrospective component of this study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (Approval No.I-24PJ1174 ). The prospective component was approved by the institutional ethics committees of all five participating centers, with the corresponding approval numbers as follows: [center 1, No. I-22PJ1104]; [center 2, No. 2023BJYYEC-150-01]; [center 3, No. Jilun [K2023] No. [195]-00]; [center 4, No. IIT2023-007-002]; and [center 5, No. 2025 − 869]. For the prospective component, written informed consent was obtained from all patients or their legally authorized representatives before enrollment.

The diagnostic criteria for ICU-acquired pneumonia required fulfillment of both of the following criteria (according to the 2016 IDSA/ATS guidelines) [12]: (1) new or progressive pulmonary infiltrates appearing 48 h after ICU admission and (2) at least two of the following clinical criteria concurrently (fever > 38 °C, white blood cell count > 10 × 109/L or < 4 × 109/L, and purulent respiratory secretions). In accordance with the guideline recommendations, microbiological positivity was not required to diagnose pulmonary infection in this study; the relevant diagnoses were determined by two senior intensivists based on radiological, clinical, and laboratory data.

Nurse-led respiratory care bundle intervention group

Four components were added or modified relative to standard care, as follows (Fig. 1).

Early mobilization was guided by a composite assessment of confusion assessment method for the ICU (CAM-ICU) delirium screening (every 6 h), IAP, and norepinephrine-equivalent dose (NED). Patients were stratified into three tiers: passive limb training for unconscious or persistently delirious patients; seated bed exercises for those with intermittent delirium, NED ≥ 0.2 µg/kg/min, or IAP ≥ 20 mmHg; and progressive ambulation (wheelchair, standing, short-distance walking) for patients meeting all criteria of no delirium, NED < 0.2 µg/kg/min, and IAP < 20 mmHg [14, 15].

The frequency of oral care was dynamically adjusted based on daily reassessment using the Beck Oral Assessment Scale (BOAS). BOAS scores of 5, 6–10, 11–15, and 16–20 corresponded to oral care every 12, 8, 6, and 4 h, respectively [16].

Airway clearance was performed using a vibrating expectorator at 20–30 Hz for 20–30 min every 6 h. Sputum viscosity was assessed every 6 h using a three-tier classification (level 1: watery; level 2: moderately viscous; and level 3: highly viscous), with airway humidification adjusted to maintain secretion viscosity at levels 1–2 [17].

Lung status was monitored through regular auscultation, nurse-performed bedside ultrasonography, and a bedside chest radiograph. Atelectasis prompted immediate postural drainage and lung recruitment. For unilateral consolidation, a high-angle lateral positioning (> 90°) was adopted. For bilateral lung consolidation, prone positioning was implemented as soon as possible when IAP was < 20 mmHg. If IAP was ≥ 20 mmHg, only the high-angle lateral positioning strategy was used. A quality control team composed of ICU nurses was established to verify implementation of all measures daily and to provide feedback and training.

In brief, while the control group received a fixed, protocol-independent regimen of basic pulmonary care, the intervention bundle applied criterion-driven, individually titrated versions of the same care component, with adjustments made according to predefined clinical criteria and each patient’s condition.

Data collection and outcomes

Baseline data collected on the day of ICU admission included demographic characteristics, comorbidities, acute physiology and chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II) score, SOFA score, vital signs, blood gas analysis, and additional laboratory parameters. Life-sustaining interventions were also recorded. For variables measured repeatedly, the worst value within the first 24 h of ICU admission was used as a reference. For patients who developed pneumonia, microbiological characteristics, including pathogen identification and sputum or airway secretion culture results, were collected. In addition, the total duration of MV and 28-day ventilator-free days (VFD28, defined as 28 minus the number of days on MV within the first 28 days after intubation, with VFD28 set to 0 for patients who died within 28 days or who remained ventilator-dependent throughout the 28-day window) were calculated for all patients.

The primary outcome was the incidence of ICU-acquired pneumonia. Secondary outcomes included the ICU length of stay, total hospital stay, and 28-day mortality.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed in R software (version 4.5.0). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (IQR) and compared using independent t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages.

Multivariable binary logistic regression was used to examine the association between the nurse-led respiratory care bundle and pneumonia risk, with covariates selected based on clinical judgment and univariate analysis (P < 0.10). Five sensitivity analyses were conducted, including propensity score matching (PSM), inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), generalized estimating equations (GEE), generalized linear mixed model (GLMM), and bootstrap resampling. Group balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD < 0.10).

Secondary outcomes were analyzed as follows: hospital/ICU length of stay using negative binomial regression (incidence rate ratio [IRR] and 95% confidence interval [CI]), 28-day mortality using multivariable logistic regression (odds ratio [OR] and 95% CI), and VFD28 using the Mann–Whitney U test with Hodges–Lehmann median difference (95% CI). All tests were two-sided, with significance set at P < 0.05.

To explore the potential influence of temporal trends and changes in center composition, given that the control and intervention periods did not overlap in calendar time and four additional centers were incorporated during the intervention period, a set of analyses was conducted to assess time-trend bias. These included a trend test within the control group, a before–after comparison within center 1 (the only center spanning both periods), and a monthly aggregated interrupted time series (ITS) analysis with corresponding robustness checks.

Association between nurse-led respiratory care bundle and incidence of pneumonia

Incidence of ICU-acquired pneumonia and multivariable analysis

ICU-acquired pneumonia occurred in 111 of 263 patients (42.2%) in the control group and 155 of 606 patients (25.6%) in the intervention group.

Univariate logistic regression identified continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), chronic heart failure, fentanyl use, remifentanil use, SOFA score, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), age, vasopressor use, partial pressure of arterial oxygen, APACHE II score, and hematological disease as candidate variables (P < 0.10; Table S2), all of which were entered into the multivariable model. Multivariable logistic regression confirmed the nurse-led respiratory care bundle as an independent protective factor (OR = 0.39; 95% CI, 0.28–0.54; P < 0.001; Table 2). CRRT was the strongest independent risk factor (OR = 3.59; 95% CI, 2.40–5.42; P < 0.001), followed by COPD (OR = 2.25; 95% CI, 1.12–4.48; P = 0.022) and chronic heart failure (OR = 1.61; 95% CI, 1.08–2.38; P = 0.018). SOFA score, age, and the other candidate variables were not independently significant (all P > 0.05).

Sensitivity analyses

Five sensitivity analyses consistently supported an association between the nurse‑led respiratory care bundle and a lower risk of pneumonia (Table S3; Fig. 3). These included PSM (OR = 0.47; 95% CI, 0.32–0.69), IPTW (OR = 0.43; 95% CI, 0.31–0.59; with weight distribution presented in Figure S1), GEE accounting for within‑center correlation (OR = 0.36; 95% CI, 0.30–0.43), a random‑intercept mixed model (OR = 0.39; 95% CI, 0.28–0.54; with an ICC approximately 0), and bootstrap resampling (OR = 0.39; 95% bias-corrected and accelerated confidence interval [BCa CI], 0.29–0.56). All yielded P-values < 0.001.

Center-specific subgroup analyses demonstrated broadly consistent associations across all five sites: center 1 (OR = 0.40; 95% CI, 0.25–0.64), center 2 (OR = 0.39; 95% CI, 0.25–0.61), center 3 (OR = 0.36; 95% CI, 0.18–0.69), center 4 (OR = 0.39; 95% CI, 0.23–0.65), and center 5 (OR = 0.42; 95% CI, 0.20–0.83) (Fig. 4).

Assessment of time‑trend bias

The time-trend bias analyses exhibited no significant spontaneous downward trend in the control group (P = 0.134). In the before–after comparison within center 1, the nurse-led respiratory care bundle remained an independent protective factor for pneumonia after covariate adjustment (OR = 0.40; 95% CI, 0.25–0.64), consistent with the primary model. The monthly aggregated ITS analysis revealed a trend in the same direction but did not reach statistical significance (Table S4; Figure S2).

Clinical and microbiological characteristics of pneumonia cases

Among the 266 patients who developed pneumonia (control, 111; intervention, 155), neither CPIS (9.00 [8.00–10.00] versus 9.00 [8.00–10.00], P = 0.751) nor the extent of radiographic consolidation (P = 0.207) differed significantly between the groups. Microbiological profiles were also comparable between groups (all P > 0.05; Table S5).

Association between nurse-led respiratory care bundle and secondary clinical outcomes

The nurse-led respiratory care bundle was associated with shorter hospital stays (IRR = 0.80; 95% CI, 0.71–0.90; P < 0.001) and lower 28-day mortality (OR = 0.66; 95% CI, 0.45–0.95; P = 0.025). Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first multicenter before-and-after study to evaluate the association between a nurse-led respiratory care bundle and pneumonia incidence in critically ill patients with abdominal sepsis. Across five centers, implementation of the bundle was associated with a lower incidence of pneumonia, and this association remained significant after multivariable adjustment. Additionally, the intervention group was associated with a shorter hospital stay and lower 28-day mortality, whereas ICU length of stay did not differ significantly between groups. These findings remained consistent across multiple adjusted and sensitivity analyses, supporting the stability of the observed associations.

Patients with abdominal sepsis are at a relatively high risk of pulmonary complications compared to those who have undergone general abdominal surgery and those with general sepsis [18–20]. Elevated IAP is a common feature of abdominal sepsis. Intestinal paralysis, inflammatory effusion, and postoperative peritoneal effusion increase IAP to different extents, lift the diaphragm, and increase the risk of atelectasis [21]. Additionally, abdominal surgery, sedation, and analgesia increase the risk of aspiration of the upper airway secretions. Simultaneously, intestinal flora translocation and endotoxin release trigger systemic inflammation, exacerbating alveolar epithelial injury, and reducing respiratory tolerance to infection [22]. These pathophysiological changes may collectively contribute to the increased risk of pneumonia in patients with abdominal sepsis. Despite this high burden, current guidelines provide only limited recommendations regarding the implementation of pulmonary care, and standardized protocols specifically tailored to abdominal sepsis are largely lacking [12, 23]. These findings provide additional evidence addressing an important gap in current critical care practice.

The core component of this study was the construction of a nurse-led respiratory care bundle. Because the bundle was implemented and evaluated as a single integrated intervention, the independent contribution of each component could not be determined in this study. The mechanistic discussion that follows should therefore be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than as a definitive explanation of the present findings. The four bundled interventions were based on nurses’ daily bedside tasks. Because ICU nurses provide continuous bedside care, they can observe changes in patients’ health, promptly respond to deviations from the norm, and enhance the continuity, safety, and personalization of respiratory care. This advantage may be reflected in the following interventions. Early mobilization helps promote the recovery of the lungs and intestines; therefore, it is one of the first components of this program. Given that the prevalence of delirium among mechanically ventilated ICU patients can reach 80% [24], delirium status was incorporated as a real-time safety stratification criterion for determining the intensity of early mobilization. Nurses regularly assessed the degree of sedation and delirium every 6 h using the RASS and CAM-ICU scales, promptly adjusted sedative and analgesic dosages if needed, and guided patients to participate in activities as early as possible. Based on the BOAS score, nurses flexibly adjusted the frequency of oral hygiene management to provide a risk-stratified approach to oral care rather than using a fixed-interval method. This approach may help reduce oral bacterial adherence and lower the risk of secondary infection in patients with abdominal sepsis undergoing MV. Nurses performed vibration-assisted sputum expulsion for patients every 6 h to manage airway secretions, and the target airway humidity was adjusted dynamically according to the sputum viscosity score. This approach was intended to avoid two potential risks: insufficient humidification, which can lead to thick sputum and difficulty in clearing secretions, and excessive humidification, which can cause the accumulation of secretions and increase the risk of aspiration. Otherwise, individualized airway management may be difficult to achieve [25]. Postural drainage and lung recruitment are based on combined bedside auscultation, bedside lung ultrasound, and chest radiograph findings to determine whether there is lung consolidation or atelectasis, and appropriate postural changes and intermittent lung recruitment were performed. This approach may reduce delays in imaging assessment and may facilitate timely intervention and can be used promptly and conveniently.

The program was designed to account for the characteristic pathobiological changes in abdominal sepsis when determining treatment strategies. Reduced chest wall compliance and alveolar recruitment occur in the presence of intra-abdominal hypertension. Large fluctuations in the transpulmonary pressure due to high driving pressure may further worsen lung injury [26, 27]. Accordingly, IAP serves as a basic safety limit for selecting the position and lung recruitment pressure. Thus, position selection and CPAP pressure for the high lateral decubitus and prone positions are adjusted to maximize the benefit of lung recruitment within a safe range [28, 29]. Most patients with abdominal sepsis were regularly administered vasoactive medications. Therefore, NED and IAP were used jointly to assess safety. The timing of early exercise was determined based on the patient’s degree of delirium, required level of hemodynamic support strength, and IAP intensity. This approach was intended to minimize potential adverse effects on respiratory function after prolonged sedation and immobilization, while potentially reducing the risk of accidental movement during periods of hemodynamic instability or IAP. The dynamic hierarchical management model is consistent with the concept of early identification and hierarchical management of delirium in the 2025 Society of Critical Care Medicine (SCCM) Guidelines and meets the requirements for early goal-oriented activities [14, 30].

Overall, this plan has two key characteristics: nurses continue to manage patients at the bedside, and it meets the pathogenic mechanism of abdominal sepsis. The bundle integrates early mobilization, oral hygiene, airway clearance, lung humidity control, postural drainage, and lung recruitment into an active, multi-level respiratory care system. This integrated approach may have contributed to the observed association between the bundle and lower pneumonia incidence and more favorable early clinical outcomes. All five participating hospitals were tertiary general hospitals with established collaborations. This collaborative framework facilitated standardized training and implementation of the respiratory care bundle across centers, which may have contributed to the consistency of the observed findings. Center-specific analyses demonstrated broadly consistent associations. These findings suggest that the observed associations were broadly consistent across participating centers; however, their applicability to other healthcare settings requires further evaluation.

This study has several limitations. First, although extensive statistical adjustments and multiple sensitivity analyses were performed, the multicenter before-and-after design remains susceptible to residual confounding and secular trends that cannot be fully excluded, as the time-trend analyses were exploratory in nature and do not eliminate the possibility of temporal confounding. Second, the nurse-led respiratory care bundle was evaluated as an integrated intervention, precluding assessment of the independent contribution of individual components. Third, although the multicenter design enhanced the generalizability of these findings, differences in routine clinical documentation and data collection across participating centers limited the availability of several clinically relevant variables, such as cumulative sedative and analgesic exposure and extubation timing. The absence of these data may have introduced residual confounding bias. Finally, all participating hospitals were tertiary academic centers, which may limit the generalizability of these findings to other healthcare settings. Future multicenter randomized controlled trials are required to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

A nurse-led respiratory care bundle was associated with a lower incidence of pneumonia, shorter hospital stays, and lower 28-day mortality in patients with abdominal sepsis. The observed associations were broadly consistent across participating centers and across multiple sensitivity analyses. These findings support the need for further evaluation of standardized nurse-led respiratory care protocols in high-risk populations. Randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm these findings and determine the independent contributions of individual intervention components.

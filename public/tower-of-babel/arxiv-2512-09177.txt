Primordial magnetic field from chiral plasma instability with sourcing Preprint: NORDITA-2025-146
Murman Gurgenidze, Andrew J. Long, Alberto Roper Pol, Axel Brandenburg, Tina Kahniashvili

Abstract
In an electron-positron plasma, an imbalance in the number of right- and left-chiral particles can lead to the growth of a helical magnetic field through a phenomenon called the chiral plasma instability (CPI). In the early universe, scattering reactions that violate chirality come into thermal equilibrium when the plasma cools below a temperature of approximately 80 ​ TeV 80\,\mathrm{TeV} . Since these reactions tend to relax any pre-existing chiral asymmetry to zero as the system approaches equilibrium, the standard lore is that primordial magnetogenesis via the CPI is not viable below 80 ​ TeV 80\,\mathrm{TeV} . In this work, we propose that the presence of a source for chirality can allow the CPI to operate even below 80 ​ TeV 80\,\mathrm{TeV} , we explore the implications of this scenario, and we derive predictions for the resultant magnetic field helicity using a combination of analytical methods and direct numerical simulation.

II
A source for chirality

While baryogenesis is taking place in the early universe, chirality may be sourced by out-of-equilibrium scatterings, decays, or oscillations.
Even though chirality violation is not a necessary ingredient for baryogenesis, both charge and parity violation are necessary ingredients
53
.
Given the close connection between chirality and parity, many models of baryogenesis also entail some level of chirality violation.

|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle u$}}{}|^{2}
.
The model parameters are the electric conductivity
\sigma
, the magnetic diffusivity
\eta=c^{2}/\sigma
, the kinematic viscosity
\nu
, the chiral diffusion coefficient
D_{5}
, the chiral feedback parameter
\lambda
, the chiral erasure rate
\Gamma_{5}
, and the rescaled chiral source
\tilde{S}_{5}(t)

Note that all of the parameters and dynamical variables are comoving quantities (i.e., they have been scaled by a number of

factors equal to their mass dimension);
\textstyle x
is the comoving spatial coordinate,
t
is the conformal time coordinate, and
\textstyle u

The physical variables are related to the comoving ones as
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}=a\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}_{\rm phys}

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}=a^{2}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}_{\rm phys}

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}{}=a^{2}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}{}_{\rm phys}

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle J$}}{}=a^{3}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle J$}}{}_{\rm phys}

\tilde{\mu}_{5}=a\tilde{\mu}_{5,{\rm phys}}

\lambda=\lambda_{\rm phys}/a^{2}
,
\Gamma_{5}=a\Gamma_{5,{\rm phys}}

The equation of motion for the chiral chemical potential (
7b
) is derived as follows.
Let
j_{5}^{\mu}(x)=\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\psi=\bar{\psi}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{R}-\bar{\psi}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{L}=(cn_{5},\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle j$}}{}_{5})
denote the Noether current density associated with the axial-vector symmetry transformation.
It is normalized such that the Noether charge density
n_{5}
counts
+1
for right-chiral fermions and
-1
for left-chiral fermions.
The Noether current is not conserved locally, because the axial-vector symmetry is anomalous in QED, but also because the interactions giving rise to the washout and source terms must explicitly break the symmetry.
Consequently, the continuity equation for chiral charge is

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}n_{5}+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle j$}}{}_{5}=8\tfrac{1}{\hbar}\tfrac{\alpha}{4\pi}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}-\Gamma_{5}n_{5}+S_{5}\;.

(8)

The first term on the right is the axial-vector anomaly,
\partial_{\mu}j_{5}^{\mu}=-2\tfrac{1}{\hbar}\tfrac{\alpha}{4\pi}F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
, where the factor of
2
accounts for electrons and positrons.
See Ch. 19 of Ref.
48

To evaluate the chiral current density, we assume a linear constitutive relation
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle j$}}{}_{5}=n_{5}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle u$}}{}-D_{5}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}n_{5}
.
Here we have introduced the parameter
D_{5}
, which is the diffusion coefficient for chiral charge.
To evaluate the chiral charge density, we assume that the system consists of an electron-positron plasma at temperature
T
with a small chiral asymmetry (
\mu_{5}\ll T
).
Then, the chiral charge density is approximately
n_{5}\approx(k_{B}^{2}/[\hbar^{3}c^{3}])(\mu_{5}T^{2}/3)
, where
\mu_{5}=(\mu_{R}-\mu_{L})/2=(\mu_{e_{R}^{+}}+\mu_{e_{R}^{-}}-\mu_{e_{L}^{+}}-\mu_{e_{L}^{-}})/4
.
Multiplying Eq. (
8
) by
(\hbar^{3}c^{3}/k_{B}^{2})(3/T^{2})(2\alpha/[\pi\hbar c])
yields Eq. (
7b
), and we identify the scaled source:

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{5}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\tfrac{\hbar^{3}c^{3}}{k_{B}^{2}}\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac{3}{T^{2}}\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac{2\alpha}{\pi\hbar c}\bigr)S_{5}(t)

(9)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}\,\frac{t}{t_{\phi}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-(t^{2}-t_{\phi}^{2})/(2t_{\phi}^{2})}\;,

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}

\displaystyle=3\sqrt{g_{E}}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathrm{e}}}{5}}\frac{M_{\mathrm{pl}}\alpha}{cm_{\phi}t_{\phi}^{2}}\epsilon\beta\Omega_{\phi}\;.

Note that we have introduced the reduced Planck mass
M_{\mathrm{pl}}=\sqrt{(\hbar c)/(8\pi G)}\approx 4.3\times 10^{-9}\,{\rm kg}
and used the relation
\rho_{\rm rad}(\tau_{\phi})=3cM_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}H^{2}(\tau_{\phi})/\hbar=M_{\mathrm{pl}}k_{B}^{2}T^{2}(\tau_{\phi})\pi\sqrt{g_{E}}H(\tau_{\phi})/(\sqrt{10}\hbar^{2}c)
, where
H(\tau_{\phi})
is the Hubble rate at the cosmic time
\tau_{\phi}
.
This relation is satisfied during radiation domination.
We can read off the chiral feedback parameter, which is found to be
\lambda=(\hbar^{3}c^{3}/k_{B}^{2})(3/T^{2})(2\alpha/[\pi\hbar c])(8/[\hbar c])(\alpha/[4\pi])
giving

\displaystyle\lambda=\lambda_{\star}\equiv\biggl(\frac{\hbar c}{k_{B}^{2}}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{12\alpha^{2}}{\pi^{2}T^{2}}\biggr)\;,

(10)

which agrees with the value used in Ref.
14
.
For our numerical studies, we explore different values of
\lambda
, so we denote this theoretically-preferred “fiducial” value by
\lambda_{\star}
.

It is worth reiterating that all of the formulas here are expressed in terms of comoving quantities, such as the comoving chemical potential
\mu_{5}=a\mu_{5,\mathrm{phys}}
and the comoving temperature
T=aT_{\mathrm{phys}}
.
While the universe expands and cools adiabatically,
T
is approximately static.

IV
Chiral plasma instability

The equations of
\chi
MHD admit an instability, known as the chiral plasma instability (CPI), toward the growth of a helical magnetic field
32
.
To identify the instability, it is sufficient to set
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle u$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle u$}}{}=0
in Eq. (
7a
), which becomes

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}-\eta\nabla^{2}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}-\eta\tilde{\mu}_{5}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\times\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}=0\;.

(11)

Note that we have used the radiation gauge with
V=0
and
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}=0
, where the latter is a consequence of
V=0
and the neutrality of the plasma
Q=0
, such that
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}{}=0
.
The vector potential may be decomposed into Fourier modes
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{},t)=\int\!\!\tfrac{{\rm d}{}^{3}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}}(t)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{{\rm i}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{}}
, and further decomposed onto a basis of right- and left-handed circular polarization modes
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}}(t)=A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},+}(t)\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},+}+A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},-}(t)\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},-}
.
In terms of these variables, the equation of motion becomes

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\pm}+\bigl(\eta|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|^{2}\mp\eta\tilde{\mu}_{5}|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|\bigr)A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\pm}=0\;.

(12)

If
\tilde{\mu}_{5}=0
, then
A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\pm}\propto\mathrm{exp}[-\eta|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|^{2}t]
decays at a rate controlled by the magnetic diffusivity
\eta
.
If
\tilde{\mu}_{5}>0
, then the positive-helicity modes
A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},+}
with
|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|<\tilde{\mu}_{5}
are unstable.
If
\tilde{\mu}_{5}<0
, then the negative-helicity modes
A_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},-}
with
|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|<-\tilde{\mu}_{5}
are unstable.
This is a tachyonic instability that only impacts long-wavelength modes.

It is customary to study the CPI in a system without chiral sources (
\tilde{S}_{5}=0
), without chirality erasure (
\Gamma_{5}=0
), and with a nonzero initial chiral
asymmetry (
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{i})\neq 0
).
For such a system, it is useful to identify
k_{\text{\sc cpi}}=|\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{i})|
and note that the long-wavelength modes with
|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|<k_{\text{\sc cpi}}
are unstable.
The instability develops most quickly for modes with wavenumber
|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|=k_{\text{\sc cpi}}/2
, which begin to grow exponentially (while
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
is initially approximately constant) on a time scale
t_{\text{\sc cpi}}=4/\eta k_{\text{\sc cpi}}^{2}
, defined such that
A_{k_{\text{\sc cpi}},\pm}\propto{\mathrm{e}}^{t/t_{\text{\sc cpi}}}
, where the unstable mode is determined by the sign of
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
.

As the CPI develops, the chiral asymmetry is converted into magnetic helicity density.
This can be understood from Eq. (
8
) by noting that

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{M}=-2c\left\langle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\right\rangle=-\tfrac{\pi c\hbar}{\alpha}\tfrac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left\langle n_{5}\right\rangle\;,

(13)

where we have defined the magnetic helicity density

h_{M}=\left\langle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{V}\int\!{\rm d}{}^{3}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{}\,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\;.

(14)

Angle brackets denote volume average, and the terms corresponding to divergence of fluxes vanish in a periodic volume.
Note that we have assumed
\tilde{S}_{5}=\Gamma_{5}=0
for this conservation law to be satisfied, and we allow
\lambda\neq\lambda_{\star}
in our numerical studies.
In terms of the chiral chemical potential, the resultant comoving helicity density is estimated as

\displaystyle\Delta h_{M}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{B}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}c^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda}\frac{\pi}{3\alpha}\,\Delta\!\left\langle\mu_{5}\right\rangle\,T^{2}\;,

(15a)

and recall that
\tilde{\mu}_{5}=2\alpha\mu_{5}/(\pi\hbar c)
.
Since the derivation has assumed a small chiral asymmetry, this formula is only valid for
|\Delta\!\left\langle\mu_{5}\right\rangle|\ll k_{B}T
.
We estimate the maximum comoving helicity density to be

\begin{split}\Delta h_{M}&\approx-(1.06\times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{G})^{2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\,a_{0}^{3}\\
&\qquad\times\bigl(\tfrac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda}\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac{\Delta\left\langle\mu_{5}\right\rangle}{k_{B}T}\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac{g_{S}}{106.75}\bigr)^{-1}\;,\end{split}

(15b)

where
g_{S}
is the adiabatic number of degrees of freedom in the plasma at the time when the CPI develops.
For this estimate, we have assumed radiation domination with
g_{E}=g_{S}=106.75
effective relativistic degrees of freedom, we assume comoving entropy conservation between the electroweak epoch and today, and we denote the scale factor today as
a_{0}
.
We will consider these assumptions for all the remaining numerical estimates.
Note that to convert between energy density and G
2
, we use the relation
\mathrm{G}^{2}/2\approx 24.8\,{\rm GeV}/\mathrm{cm}^{3}
in Eq. (
15b
).
For reference, the TeV blazar observations based on time-delayed
\gamma
-ray emission favor a nonzero magnetic field with

B^{2}\lambda_{B}\gtrsim(1.8\times 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{G})^{2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\,a_{0}^{3}
; see the diagonal boundary on Fig. 5 of Ref.
1
.
For a maximally-helical magnetic field,

|\Delta h_{M}|\approx B^{2}\lambda_{B}
,
and otherwise the helicity is smaller.
Therefore, as emphasized by the authors of Ref.
15
, the CPI is unable to generate a strong enough magnetic field to explain the blazar observations.
The preceding discussion assumes that the chiral asymmetry is not being sourced.
In the next section, we discuss how the development of the CPI is affected by the presence of a source.

V
Development of CPI with a source

We are interested in a system with chiral sources, which cause the chiral chemical potential to grow in time.
If the parameters are chosen such that
\Gamma_{5}^{-1}\ll t_{\mathrm{cross}}\ll t_{\phi}
, where
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
is defined below, then the evolution with conformal time
t
can be understood as follows.
We assume that the comoving
\Gamma_{5}
is constant in time during the sourcing process.

t_{i}<t<\Gamma_{5}^{-1}\mathrm{:}

Initially, the chemical potential rises from zero
\propto t^{2}
, because

\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)\approx\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\!{\rm d}{}t^{\prime}\tilde{S}_{5}(t^{\prime})\approx\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}\,\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}t^{2}/(2t_{\phi})\;.

(16)

This follows from Eq. (
7b
) when
\tilde{\mu}_{5}\approx 0
and
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\approx 0
.

\Gamma_{5}^{-1}<t<t_{\mathrm{cross}}\mathrm{:}

Subsequently the chemical potential rises
\propto t^{1}
and tracks

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)\approx\tilde{S}_{5}(t)\,/\,\Gamma_{5}\;,

(17)

where
\tilde{S}_{5}(t)
is given by Eq. (
9
).
Since chirality-violating reactions are in equilibrium on time scales that are longer than
\Gamma_{5}^{-1}
, the washout term in Eq. (
7b
) would drive
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
to zero if no sources were present.
However, the nonzero source shifts the equilibrium point away from zero, and prevents the complete erasure of the chiral asymmetry.

t=t_{\mathrm{cross}}\mathrm{:}

While
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)
is growing, the time scale for the CPI is decreasing

t_{\text{\sc cpi}}(t)=4/[\eta k_{\text{\sc cpi}}^{2}(t)]=4/[\eta|\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)|^{2}]
.
The CPI begins to develop when
t
first exceeds
t_{\text{\sc cpi}}(t)
, which happens at a time

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{cross}}=t_{\text{\sc cpi}}(t_{\mathrm{cross}})\approx\frac{2^{2/3}t_{\phi}^{2/3}\Gamma_{5}^{2/3}}{\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}^{2/3}\eta^{1/3}}\;.

(18)

The first equality defines
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
.
The second equality was derived assuming
\Gamma_{5}^{-1}<t_{\mathrm{cross}}<t_{\phi}
, using Eq. (
17
), and approximating
\tilde{S}_{5}\approx\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}\,\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}\,t/t_{\phi}
.
If instead
t_{\mathrm{cross}}>t_{\phi}
then the source turns off before the CPI occurs, and there is no magnetic field amplification.
Note that the instability scale is set by

\displaystyle k_{\mathrm{cross}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}k_{\text{\sc cpi}}(t_{\mathrm{cross}})\approx\frac{\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}^{1/3}}{2^{1/3}t_{\phi}^{1/3}\Gamma_{5}^{1/3}\eta^{1/3}}\;,

(19)

which represents the most rapidly growing mode at
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
.
The CPI leads to the amplification of a helical magnetic field from an initial seed field, as we discussed at Eq. (
15
).

t_{\mathrm{cross}}<t<t_{\phi}\mathrm{:}

The source remains active while the CPI is developing.
As a result we expect to see a sustained production of helical magnetic field across a range of scales.
This is because the instability begins at time
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
on scales set by
k_{\mathrm{cross}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{\mathrm{cross}})
, but soon afterward
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)
grows
\propto t
to track the increasing source (
17
), which shifts the CPI to larger
k=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)
and, hence, smaller length scales.

t=t_{\phi}\mathrm{:}

As the source reaches its maximum value
\tilde{S}_{5}(t_{\phi})=\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}
, the equilibrium chemical potential (
17
) also reaches its maximum
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{\phi})\approx\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}/\Gamma_{5}
.
At this time the CPI is active at the largest wavenumbers, with the fastest growth occurring at

\displaystyle k_{\phi}=\tfrac{1}{2}k_{\text{\sc cpi}}(t_{\phi})=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{\phi})\approx\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}\,/\,\Gamma_{5}\;.

(20)

All in all, because the source grows the chiral chemical potential, the CPI instability scale scans across a range of wavenumbers
k_{\mathrm{cross}}<k<k_{\phi}
.

t_{\phi}<t\mathrm{:}

The source term becomes exponentially suppressed for
t\gtrsim t_{\phi}
.
If no CPI has occurred, then
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)
will track the equilibrium solution (
17
) and drop to zero.
However, if the CPI has taken place, then the system contains a helical magnetic field.
The slowly-decaying helicity of the magnetic field also acts as a source of chirality
21
;
33
;
34
;
26
;
22
, which corresponds to the Chern-Simons term in Eq. (
7b
),
\lambda c\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle E$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}=\tfrac{1}{c}\lambda\eta\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle J$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}-\eta\tilde{\mu}_{5}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}^{2}
.
As a result, a new equilibrium solution is reached where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{5}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{},t)\approx

\displaystyle\,\frac{\lambda\eta}{\Gamma_{5}+\lambda\eta\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}^{2}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\times\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}

(21)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\,\frac{\lambda\eta}{\Gamma_{5}}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\times\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\;.

The second approximation is satisfied when
\lambda\eta|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}|^{2}/\Gamma_{5}\ll 1
.
As the magnetic field slowly decays, the chiral chemical potential relaxes toward zero.
In this phase, the chiral chemical potential becomes inhomogeneous due to the sourcing from the helical magnetic field.
In previous stages, since the source is assumed to be homogeneous, also
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
remains approximately homogeneous.

In the remainder of this section we estimate the magnetic helicity that is expected to arise from these dynamics.
Previously at Eq. (
15
) we presented a conservation law for magnetic helicity that was derived by setting
\tilde{S}_{5}=\Gamma_{5}=0
.
When either the source or washout are nonzero, the more general relation may be derived from the equation of motion for
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
(
7b
) by integrating over time and averaging over space.
Doing so leads to the following conservation law,

\Delta h_{\rm tot}(t)=\frac{2}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}\!{\rm d}{}t^{\prime}\,\bigl[\tilde{S}_{5}(t^{\prime})-\Gamma_{5}\langle\tilde{\mu}_{5}\rangle(t^{\prime})\bigr]\;,

(22)

where
h_{\rm tot}=h_{M}+\tfrac{2}{\lambda}\langle\tilde{\mu}_{5}\rangle
is the total net helicity, composed of the magnetic helicity and the chiral chemical potential.

During the initial stage of the evolution, before
t_{\mathrm{cross}}
, the CPI has not yet kicked in and, hence, we do not expect significant evolution of the magnetic helicity but just fluctuations from the initial conditions.
However, during this period, we expect to have production of
h_{\rm tot}
in the form of the chiral chemical potential, which will be eventually converted into magnetic helicity.
During the initial regime of the evolution
t_{i}<t<\Gamma_{5}^{-1}
,

\Delta h_{\rm tot}(t<\Gamma_{5}^{-1})\approx\frac{2}{\lambda}\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)\approx\frac{\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}\,\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}t^{2}}{\lambda t_{\phi}}\;.

(23)

At time
\Gamma_{5}^{-1}<t\lesssim t_{\phi}
, the chiral chemical potential is the result of a balance between the source and the washout terms,
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t)\approx\tilde{S}_{5}(t)/\Gamma_{5}
in Eq. (
17
).
During this period, the integrand in Eq. (
22
) is close to zero.
However, it is not exactly zero, as we have a residual dependence from the equation of motion (
7b
),

\tilde{S}_{5}-\Gamma_{5}\langle\tilde{\mu}_{5}\rangle\approx\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle\tilde{\mu}_{5}\rangle+\lambda\eta\langle(\tilde{\mu}_{5}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}-\tfrac{1}{c}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle J$}}{})\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\rangle\;.

(24)

While the strength of the magnetic field is negligible,

\tilde{S}_{5}-\Gamma_{5}\langle\tilde{\mu}_{5}\rangle\approx\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle\tilde{\mu}_{5}\rangle\;,

(25)

which implies
\Delta h_{\rm tot}\approx\tfrac{2}{\lambda}\Delta\tilde{\mu}_{5}
also during this stage.
Hence, as
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
grows following the source,
h_{\rm tot}
grows and, after the source (and
\tilde{\mu}_{5}
) reaches its maximum value, it starts to decrease.
Once the CPI starts at
t\gtrsim t_{\mathrm{cross}}
, the magnetic helicity grows exponentially and saturates shortly after
t_{\phi}
, at
t_{\rm sat}\gtrsim t_{\phi}
.
After this time, the chiral chemical potential balances with the Chern-Simons term as described in Eq. (
21
).
Therefore, the saturated value of the magnetic helicity can be estimated as

\displaystyle h_{M}(t>t_{\rm sat})\approx

\displaystyle\,\frac{\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}}{\lambda t_{\phi}\Gamma_{5}^{2}}+\frac{2}{\lambda}\bigl[\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{\rm sat})-\tilde{\mu}_{5}(\Gamma_{5}^{-1})\bigr]

\displaystyle\,-2\eta\int_{t_{\rm sat}}^{t}{\rm d}{}t^{\prime}\langle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\nabla$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\nabla$}}{}\times\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\rangle\;,

(26)

where we have assumed that the condition
\lambda\eta|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}|^{2}/\Gamma_{5}\ll 1
is satisfied.
If this is not the case, it is enough to include the additional term in the denominator in Eq. (
21
).
Furthermore, if the amplitude of the magnetic field resultant from the CPI is small, then the terms proportional to
\langle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle J$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle J$}}{}\rangle
can be neglected, leading to the following estimate of the magnetic helicity produced from the CPI with a source,

h_{M}\approx\frac{\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}}{\lambda t_{\phi}\Gamma_{5}^{2}}\approx\frac{\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}}{2t_{\phi}\Gamma_{5}}\times\frac{2\tilde{\bar{S}}_{5}}{\lambda\Gamma_{5}}\;,

(27)

where we have also assumed that
\tilde{\mu}_{5}(\Gamma_{5}^{-1})\approx\tilde{\mu}_{5}(t_{\rm sat})
.
Note that the second equality expresses
h_{M}
as a fraction
\sqrt{{\mathrm{e}}}/(2t_{\phi}\Gamma_{5})
of the magnetic helicity that would be produced in the absence of washout and source terms if the maximum chiral chemical potential at
t_{\phi}
in our model would be converted into magnetic helicity.
In the following sections we compare this analytical approximation with the result of direct numerical computation.

Since the next two figures show spectra, let us first establish our conventions.
The magnetic energy spectrum
{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}/{\rm d}{}\ln k
is defined such that

\displaystyle\rho_{B}(t)

\displaystyle={\textstyle{1\over 2}}\langle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}^{2}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{},t)\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{{\rm d}{}k}{k}\,\frac{{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}\;,

(36)

and the magnetic helicity spectrum
{\rm d}{}h_{M}/{\rm d}{}\ln k
is defined such that

\displaystyle h_{M}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle A$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle A$}}{}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{},t)\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle B$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}{}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}{},t)\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{{\rm d}{}k}{k}\,\frac{{\rm d}{}h_{M}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}\;,

(37)

where
k=|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}|
.
These spectra are related to the power spectra used in Ref.
19
by the relations
P_{B}(k,t)=(2\pi^{2}/k^{3})\,{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}/{\rm d}{}\ln k
and
P_{aB}(k,t)=(\pi^{2}/k^{2})\,{\rm d}{}h_{M}/{\rm d}{}\ln k
.
The helicity spectrum is bounded by the energy spectrum via the realizability condition
-P_{B}\leq P_{aB}\leq P_{B}
, which implies
-\tfrac{{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}\leq\tfrac{k}{2}\tfrac{{\rm d}{}h_{M}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}\leq\tfrac{{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}
, and we define the helicity fraction
\varepsilon_{H}=\bigl(\tfrac{k}{2}\tfrac{{\rm d}{}h_{M}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac{{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}}{{\rm d}{}\ln k}\bigr)^{-1}
, which is bounded by
-1\leq\varepsilon_{H}\leq 1
.
To compare with the conventions in other work, e.g.
15
;
13
;
10

E_{M}(k,t)=k^{-1}{\rm d}{}\rho_{B}/{\rm d}{}\ln k
and
H_{M}(k,t)=k^{-1}{\rm d}{}h_{M}/{\rm d}{}\ln k
.

VIII
Summary and conclusion

Since chirality-violating interactions come into equilibrium when the temperature of the primordial plasma drops below
80\,{\rm TeV}
, any preexisting chiral asymmetry will be erased.
However, a source for chirality counteracts its washout, allowing for a nonzero chiral asymmetry even below
80\,{\rm TeV}
.
The presence of a chiral asymmetry opens the possibility of helical magnetic field generation via the chiral plasma instability.
In this work, we have studied the chiral plasma instability in the presence of such a source, together with nonzero chiral erasure rate.

We model the system as a fluid, whose constituents carry both electric and chiral charge, which is therefore coupled to electromagnetism.
We study the dynamics of this system with the equations of
\chi
MHD extended to include a source term.
We assume a particular functional form for the source’s time dependence, which we motivate by considering the out-of-equilibrium decay of a metastable scalar field.
When selecting parameters, it is important to ensure that the time scale for the CPI is short compared to the lifetime of this scalar field, since otherwise the source will deactivate before the instability has time to develop.
We solve the equations of
\chi
MHD using analytical approximations and numerical methods, the latter implemented in the open-source
Pencil Code
.

Our calculations reveal that a source of chirality is sufficient to induce the growth of a helical magnetic field via the CPI even at plasma temperatures below
80\,{\rm TeV}
where the chirality-washout reactions are in equilibrium.
We track the evolution of the chiral chemical potential and magnetic helicity in our analytical calculations, which provide an understanding of the general time dependence, and in our numerical calculations, which validate the analytical approximations.
The resultant magnetic helicity is predicted in two ways.
The relation in Eq. (
27
) is the approximate comoving magnetic helicity density that we expect to result from the CPI in the presence of both a source and washout channel for chirality.
We validate this approximate relation using direct numerical simulation of the
\chi
MHD field equations.
For the parameter values corresponding to
\mathbf{A}^{\ast}
in Table
2
, our analytical approximation in Eq. (
27
) gives
h_{M}\approx(1.6\times 10^{-13}\,{\rm G})^{2}\,{\rm Mpc}\,a_{0}^{3}
, and our numerical calculation gives
h_{M}\approx(2.4\times 10^{-13}\,{\rm G})^{2}\,{\rm Mpc}\,a_{0}^{3}
in Eq. (
35
).
The excellent agreement gives us confidence in our analytical approximations.
Eq. (
27
) is one of the main results of our paper.

We conclude that when a source for chirality is present, the chiral charge erasure can be avoided.
This allows the chiral plasma instability to develop even after the chirality-erasing reactions are in thermal equilibrium at plasma temperatures below
80\,{\rm TeV}
.
We expect that a sourced CPI may play an important role in magnetic field generation during the cosmological electroweak or QCD phase transition.
However the parameters that we have explored in our numerical work were chosen to facilitate numerical calculations.
If we instead use the Standard Model fiducial values of the parameters
\lambda
and
\Gamma_{5}
, see Eq. (
10
) and below Eq. (
28
), then Eq. (
27
) evaluates to

\displaystyle h_{M}

\displaystyle\approx\bigl(3.8\times 10^{-21}\,{\rm G}\bigr)^{2}\,{\rm Mpc}\,a_{0}^{3}

(38)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl(\frac{k_{B}T_{\mathrm{{\rm phys}},\ast}}{100\,{\rm GeV}}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{\epsilon\beta\Omega_{\phi}}{10^{-5}}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{m_{\phi}c^{2}}{100\,{\rm GeV}}\biggr)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl(\frac{t_{\phi}}{0.05\,t_{\ast}}\biggr)^{-3}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma_{5}}{\Gamma_{5,\star}}\biggr)^{-2}\biggl(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\biggr)^{-1}\;,

where we have also made reasonable choices for the parameters associated with the source, namely
\epsilon
,
\beta
,
\Omega_{\phi}
,
m_{\phi}
, and
t_{\phi}
.
This level of magnetic helicity would be inadequate to explain blazar observations that provide evidence for a nonzero intergalactic magnetic field.
Nevertheless, even such a weak field could be strong enough to seed the galactic magnetic fields.

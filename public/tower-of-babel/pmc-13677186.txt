Left Ventricular Structural, Physiological, and Transcriptomic Abnormalities in a Novel Porcine Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Model
Authors: Eric M Green, Carlos del Rio, Kamel Shibbani, David K Meyerholz, Joshua Gorham, Seong Kim, Daniel M DeLaughter, Marcus P Henze, Christopher S Rogers, Lindsey M Gifford, Melissa Kafa, Frank Rohret, J Adam Goeken, Jared M McLendon, Ryan L Boudreau, Christine E Seidman, Jessica C Sieren, Robert M Weiss, Jonathan G Seidman, Abhay Divekar, Ferhaan Ahmad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Visual AbstractKey words: cardiac myosin inhibitor, hemodynamics, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, pig, transcriptome

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is the most common autosomal dominant cardiovascular disease, with a prevalence of up to 1 in 200.1 HCM is characterized by ventricular hypertrophy in the absence of exogenous stimuli such as hypertension, along with enhanced contractility. The pattern of hypertrophy is frequently asymmetric with greater septal involvement, but other patterns can occur, including concentric, apical, lateral wall, and right ventricular predominance. Myofibrillar disarray and fibrosis are present. The overall annual mortality rate is 1% to 5%, secondary to arrhythmias, stroke, and heart failure.2 HCM is a leading cause of sudden death in competitive athletes.3, 4, 5

The first genetic variant causing HCM, an Arg403Gln (R403Q) substitution in the gene encoding β myosin heavy chain (MYH7), was identified in 1990.6 Since then, pathogenic variants in at least 8 genes, most of them encoding sarcomere proteins, have been associated with HCM, with variants in MYH7 and cardiac myosin binding protein C (MYBPC3) being the most common.7 Soon after identification of the first pathogenic variant in humans, the first genetically engineered mouse model was constructed.8 Mouse models have been invaluable in elucidating the mechanisms by which genetic variants lead to the HCM phenotype. Hyperdynamic contractility, poor relaxation, and increased energy consumption all contribute to the phenotype.9 However, these mouse models are subject to several limitations. The heart rate in mice is approximately 600 beats per minute, 10-fold greater than in humans, and the cardiac stroke volume is 10 μL in mice, compared with ∼75 mL in humans. Consequently, the predominant sarcomere protein isoforms in mice differ from humans. For example, mouse hearts are dependent on the fast α isoform of myosin heavy chain, encoded by Myh6. In fact, the human MYH7 R403Q variant was introduced into Myh6, rather than Myh7, in the first genetically engineered mouse model of HCM.8 The rapid heart rate in mice also leads to differences in ion channel expression, such as a greater role for sarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ transporting ATPase 2 in Ca2+ removal as contrasted with the Na + - Ca2+ exchanger in humans. Large animal models, such as pigs, are more amenable to translational studies, allowing multimodality imaging, conscious hemodynamic studies, and molecular studies that more closely recapitulate the human heart. Thus, pigs represent a more faithful model to support mechanistic studies and drug discovery and development. Therefore, we generated knockin Yucatan minipigs heterozygous for the human HCM variant, MYH7 R403Q,10 using homologous recombination and somatic cell nuclear transfer.

We opted to establish the pig model with the MYH7 R403Q variant for several reasons. Variants in MYH7 are the most frequent cause of HCM, accounting for 30% to 40% of familial cases and 15% to 25% of sporadic cases.11 Among pathogenic MYH7 variants, the R403Q variant is one of the most common.12 The penetrance of variants in MYH7 is 60%, the highest among genes associated with HCM, including the other frequently associated gene, MYBP3, for which the penetrance is 55%.11 Furthermore, MYH7 variants are associated with earlier age of onset of HCM and a higher risk of heart failure events.13, 14, 15, 16 It also appears that the incidence of sudden cardiac death is higher in 4 specific MYH7 variants (R403Q, R453 C, G716 R, and R719 W), among which is R403Q.17 Finally, the R403Q variant has been one of the most widely studied in different systems, including reconstituted muscle fibers, human induced pluripotent stem cell–derived cardiomyocytes, human engineered cardiac tissue, and mice, and has been the target of novel experimental therapies.18,19 This extensive previous work shows that the R403Q variant produces a robust and reproducible HCM phenotype characterized by hypercontractility, altered crossbridge kinetics, increased energetic cost of contraction, cellular hypertrophy, and arrhythmogenic susceptibility. These well-established molecular and physiological consequences make R403Q particularly advantageous for translational studies aimed at linking genotype to phenotype and evaluating emerging therapies. Because Yucatan minipigs undergo puberty at approximately 6 months of age, we longitudinally observed the pigs to at least age 12 months, the equivalent of young adulthood in humans.

Male cloned MYH7 R403Q pigs underwent detailed longitudinal phenotype analysis up to 1 year of age, including assessment of serum cardiac biomarkers, electrocardiograms, echocardiograms, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) studies, in vivo hemodynamic studies at baseline, and with acute exposure to the cardiac myosin inhibitor MYK-581, an analog of mavacamten,10 and cardiac tissue collection for histology, and bulk tissue RNA sequencing (RNAseq) and single nuclei RNA sequencing (snRNAseq). Our detailed phenotype analysis of the MYH7 R403Q pigs confirmed that they replicate many features consistent with the human disorder, including enhanced contractility, hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and myocyte disarray. Thus, our studies in MYH7 R403Q pigs identified pathophysiological mechanisms of HCM that may be targeted for therapeutic interventions, including by cardiac myosin inhibitors.

Methods

Additional detailed methods are provided in the online Supplemental Appendix.

Study design

All studies were approved by the institutional animal care and use committees at the University of Iowa and Exemplar Genetics. Genomic editing was used to generate Yucatan minipigs heterozygous for the MYH7 R403Q variant. Male pigs underwent serial phenotype analyses up to 12 months of age. Puberty in Yucatan minipigs occurs at approximately 6 months of age. These analyses included in vivo circulating biomarker analysis, cardiac imaging, and hemodynamic studies, and ex vivo biomechanical, histologic, and multi-omic analyses of cardiac tissue samples.

Generation of MYH7 R403Q pigs

A targeting vector was constructed containing a c.1208 G > A substitution in the MYH7 gene, corresponding to an arginine to glutamine missense variant at residue 403 in exon 13 (p.R403Q), along with an intronic BlastR cassette and 5′ and 3ʹ flanking homology arms (Supplemental Figure 1). The targeting vector was packaged into a recombinant adeno-associated virus for delivery to fetal fibroblasts from Yucatan minipigs. Fibroblasts with successful homologous recombination of the targeting vector were confirmed by Southern blot, polymerase chain reaction, and direct sequencing. Somatic cell nuclear transfer of nuclei from targeted fibroblasts into enucleated oocytes was performed, and the resultant embryos were transferred into maternal hosts to generate cloned males that were heterozygous for the MYH7 R403Q pathogenic variant.

Blood biomarkers

Plasma troponin I was measured by a chemiluminescent immunoassay at a commercial laboratory (Marshfield Labs). Plasma B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) was measured by radioimmunoassay as previously reported20,21 and described in the online supplemental materials.

Twelve-lead electrocardiograms

All pigs underwent 12-lead electrocardiogram acquisition with cutaneous leads following sedation of pigs with 1.2% isoflurane or 0.6 mg/kg midazolam and 25 to 27 mg/kg ketamine.

Transthoracic echocardiography

Pigs were sedated with 0.6 mg/kg midazolam and 25 to 27 mg/kg ketamine intramuscularly and placed in the supine position. Transthoracic echocardiography was performed with parasternal long- and short-axis, apical, and subcostal views to obtain 2-dimensional, M-mode, pulsed wave Doppler, continuous wave Doppler, and color Doppler images as described in the online supplemental materials.

CMR

Pigs were sedated with 1.1 mg/kg ketamine, 1.1 mg/kg xylazine, and 2.2 mg/kg telazol intramuscularly, followed by maintenance with inhaled 0.5% to 5% isoflurane. An endotracheal tube was then inserted and mechanical ventilation initiated. CMR imaging was performed to assess left ventricular end-systolic and end-diastolic (LVEDV) volumes, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and end-diastolic left ventricular (LV) septal and lateral wall thicknesses. For analyses of myocardial perfusion, short-axis slices were acquired in pairs, targeted to end diastole, commencing simultaneously with bolus intravenous injection of 0.1 mmol/kg gadolinium at rest and during vasodilator stress (dipyridamole 0.6 mg/kg infused intravenously over 4 minutes). Late gadolinium enhancement was assessed to determine cardiac fibrosis. Detailed image acquisition protocols are described in the online supplemental materials.

Cardiac catheterization

Cardiac catheterization was performed to assess hemodynamics and to perform endomyocardial biopsies. Before catheterization, the pigs received 81 mg aspirin orally daily for 5 days. Pigs were anesthetized, intubated, and ventilated as for the CMR studies. Hemodynamic information (right atrial pressure, right ventricular pressures, pulmonary artery pressures, aortic pressures, and LV pressures) was recorded and thermodilution cardiac output was obtained.

A pressure-volume (PV) catheter was used to acquire PV loops with the ventilator turned off (maximum time 20 seconds). LV PV loops were first recorded at baseline. To derive various load-independent indices of systolic function, PV loops were acquired with balloon occlusion of the inferior vena cava for approximately 30 seconds followed by balloon deflation. Finally, PV loops were acquired following fluid bolus with either 10 mL/kg (to a maximum of 500 mL) of normal saline or lactated Ringer's solution intravenously over 10 minutes. A continuous infusion of dobutamine was started in increments up to 20 μg/kg/min. At each level, hemodynamic assessment, thermodilution cardiac output, and LV PV loops were recorded after equilibrium was reached. Next, the right atrium was paced for 15 minutes each at 20%, 40%, and 60% above the baseline heart rate. Following each increase in right atrial pacing, the previous measurements were recorded. Finally, pigs received the selective cardiac myosin inhibitor MYK-581 at a dose of 0.25 mg/kg intravenously. Pigs were allowed to stabilize for up to 10 minutes, and data collection matching those tests and challenges performed at baseline were carried out. At the end of the procedure, a bioptome was advanced to the LV and endomyocardial biopsies of the apical septum were performed to collect approximately 10 samples per pig.

Ex vivo and in vitro tissue studies

Endomyocardial biopsy tissue samples from the apical septum, and tissue samples acquired at necropsy from the LV basal and apical septum and the LV basal and apical lateral wall, were immediately flash frozen in liquid nitrogen for later RNA and protein extraction or placed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for histological analyses. Histological analyses included myocardial disarray, fibrosis, and capillary density. In addition, freshly isolated LV papillary muscle tissue at necropsy was used for biomechanical studies. Detailed protocols are described in the online supplemental materials.

RNA sequencing and pathway analysis

Bulk RNA expression was assessed by RNAseq as described previously.22 In brief, total RNA was isolated from cardiac tissue with TRIzol (Invitrogen), reverse transcribed, converted to complementary DNA, and introduced into Nextera XT complementary DNA libraries (Illumina). Twenty to 40 million 75–base pair paired end DNA sequence reads were obtained per sample.

Raw data obtained from RNAseq were used for data analysis through Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) software (version 73,620,684, Qiagen) as we have described.23 For IPA analysis, differentially expressed genes were defined as a log2 fold change > 1 and a P < 0.05 between MYH7 R403Q and wild-type (WT) pigs, adjusted for multiple testing by correcting for the number of expressed RNAs using the Bonferroni method. This bioinformatic analysis included canonical pathways, upstream analysis, diseases and functions analysis, regulator effects analyses, molecule analysis, and network analysis.

snRNAseq

Nuclei from cardiac tissues were isolated as described previously.24 In brief, this protocol involves isolation of single nuclei from flash-frozen tissues using a mechanical homogenizer, Qiagen TissueLyser II (Qiagen, catalog no. 85300) followed by fluorescence-activated cell sorting to remove debris. Nuclei were then loaded on the Chromium Controller (10x Genomics) with a targeted nuclei recovery of 4,000 to 10,000 per reaction. Gene expression libraries were prepared using v3 Chromium Single Cell Reagent Kits (10x Genomics) according to the manufacturer's instructions and sequenced on a NextSeq 500 (Illumina). Reads were aligned to the Sus scrofa (pig) genome assembly Sscrofa11.1 (Ensemble) using Cell Ranger software (10x Genomics). Clustering and gene expression analysis were performed using Seurat software.25 Cardiomyocyte nuclei identified in the global Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection were subset and clustered. Marker genes in each cardiomyocyte state were identified by Seurat FindMarkers; cell states were named using the nomenclature used to name human cardiomyocyte states.26,27

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean. Data were assessed for normality with the Shapiro-Wilk normality test. Comparisons between 2 groups were made using Student's t-test. For comparisons among 3 or more groups, 1-way analysis of variance with Bonferroni post hoc test for multiple pairwise comparisons was used. In the absence of normality, Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare 2 groups. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 31).28 P value and Z scores calculated by IPA have been previously described.29

Independent data access and analysis

The corresponding author had full access to all study data and is accountable for its integrity and the corresponding data analyses.

Materials and data availability

MYK-581 is available from MyoKardia under a materials transfer agreement. MYH7 R403Q pigs are commercially available from Exemplar Genetics.

Results

Construction of MYH7 R403Q pigs and study design

MYH7 R403Q Yucatan minipigs were generated by homologous recombination in fibroblasts using a targeting vector with a c.1208 G > A substitution in MYH7 and somatic cell nuclear transfer into enucleated oocytes (Supplemental Figure 1).10 As expected in a heterozygous autosomal dominant model, approximately 50% of the MYH7 mRNA was mutant in different LV segments. MYH7 R403Q pigs and WT herd mates underwent the following studies at approximately 3 months (juvenile cohort, <6 months) and 9 months (adult cohort, 6-12 months) of age: blood biomarkers (plasma BNP and troponin I), electrocardiogram, echocardiogram, CMR study, invasive hemodynamic studies, and LV biopsies and necropsies for histology and omic studies. Cardiac skinned fiber biomechanical studies were performed at ages 1, 3, and 9 months.

Structural and functional abnormalities consistent with HCM on cardiac imaging of MYH7 R403Q pigs

MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited features of typical HCM on echocardiography and CMR imaging (Figures 1A and 1B). MYH7 R403Q pigs had thickening of the interventricular septum relative to their WT counterparts that appeared to be progressive from younger (<6 months) to older ages (6-12 months) (Figure 1C). In contrast, no hypertrophy of the LV posterior wall was evident in MYH7 R403Q pigs (Table 1). Similarly, the LV mass, both absolute and normalized to body mass, was greater in MYH7 R403Q pigs at an older age (6-12 months) (Figure 1D, Table 1). Interestingly, both juvenile (<6 months) and adult (6-12 months) MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited left atrial enlargement (Figure 1E). Late gadolinium enhancement showed no evidence of focal fibrosis, consistent with the diffuse interstitial fibrosis observed on histological examination (see the following text).

Juvenile MYH7 R403Q pigs (<6 months) had LV hypercontractility, reflected by elevated LVEF compared with juvenile WT pigs (Figure 1F, Table 1). However, the LVEF decreased with age and was the same in adult MYH7 R403Q pigs as in adult WT pigs. Similarly, there was a decrease in right ventricular ejection fraction with age in MYH7 R403Q pigs (66.5% ± 2.8% vs 54.2% ± 2.4%, P = 0.006). Despite septal hypertrophy and LV hypercontractility, systolic blood flow in the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) was laminar and there was no evidence of LVOT obstruction on echocardiography at any point in time in WT and MYH7 R403Q pigs (Figure 1A, Video 1).

Electrocardiographic abnormalities in MYH7 R403Q pigs

Electrocardiography documented several abnormalities in MYH7 R403Q pigs that are associated with increased arrhythmic burden. MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited a prolonged T-peak to T-end and longer QRS duration at <6 months and 6 to 12 months of age. In addition, QTc was longer at 6 to 12 months of age (Supplemental Table 1).

Myofibrillar disarray, interstitial fibrosis, and upregulation of profibrotic and proinflammatory pathways in MYH7 R403Q pigs

Analyses of cardiac tissue in MYH7 R403Q pigs at <6 months and 6 to 12 months of age identified histopathological abnormalities consistent with human HCM (Figure 2, Supplemental Figure 2). MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited myofibrillar disarray, and interstitial and perivascular fibrosis at ages <6 months and 6 to 12 months (Figures 2A to 2C). Quantification of collagen on Masson trichrome staining of LV tissue specimens documented greater fibrosis in MYH7 R403Q pigs relative to WT pigs at both ages of <6 months and 6 to 12 months (Figure 2B). Blinded scoring of myofibrillar disarray was approximately 4-fold greater in MYH7 R403Q pigs relative to WT pigs (Figure 2C). These histopathological abnormalities were accompanied by an increase in mRNA expression of the profibrotic genes TGFβ1 and TGFβ2 (Figure 2D). Although cardiomyocyte size showed little or no difference (Supplemental Figure 2A), nuclear size was approximately 50% greater in MYH7 R403Q pigs relative to WT pigs at both <6 months and 6 to 12 months (Figure 2E).

RNAseq was performed on LV septal endomyocardial biopsies at ages <6 months and 6 to 12 months. Pathway analysis showed that production of nitric oxide had the highest level of activation at both ages (Supplemental Table 2). In addition, there was upregulation of many proinflammatory pathways in the MYH7 R403Q pigs. At < 6 months, a total of 14 proinflammatory canonical pathways were significantly upregulated with a Z score ≥2 (Supplemental Table 2). These canonical pathways included the complement system, endothelin-1 signaling, interleukin (IL)-8 signaling, PI3K signaling in B lymphocytes, and FAT10 cancer signaling pathway. Upstream analysis revealed the presence of 120 regulators that had an absolute Z score ≥2. Cytokines featured heavily in the activated upstream regulators and included tumor necrosis factor, interferon G, IL-1B, IL-3, IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-12 B. At 6 to 12 months, 7 proinflammatory canonical pathways were significantly activated with a Z score ≥2 (Supplemental Table 2). Other upregulated canonical pathways included GP6 signaling pathway, insulin-like growth factor-1 signaling pathway, and Fcγ receptor-mediated phagocytosis. Upstream analysis revealed that the lipopolysaccharide upstream regulator was the most significantly upregulated with a Z score = 6.36, tumor necrosis factor was second with a Z score = 4.75, and vascular endothelial growth factor was third with a Z score = 4.16.

Vascular abnormalities in the myocardium of MYH7 R403Q pigs

Intimal hyperplasia of arterioles was present in the myocardium of MYH7 R403Q pigs (Figure 2F). Arterioles in myocardial segments with greater hypertrophy exhibited greater intimal hyperplasia. Perivascular inflammation and arteriolar thrombosis were evident (Figure 2G). In addition, capillary density in MYH7 R403Q myocardium was decreased at <6 months (Figure 2H). Myocardial flow reserve in response to vasodilator challenge was impaired in MYH7 R403Q pigs compared with WT (4.62 ± 0.76 vs 2.89 ± 0.36, P = 0.018). The anatomical and physiological vascular abnormalities in MYH7 R403Q pigs were associated with consistently elevated levels of plasma troponin I, a marker of cardiomyocyte necrosis (Figure 2I), possibly related to impaired myocardial perfusion and chronic ischemia from the small vessel abnormalities. The cardiomyocyte degeneration and loss observed on histology in MYH7 R403Q pigs (Figure 2A) is consistent with the elevated troponin I.

Age-dependent alterations in calcium sensitivity, stiffness, and crossbridge kinetics in in vitro isolated myofibers from MYH7 R403Q pigs

Cardiac skinned myofibers from MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited age-dependent biophysical abnormalities. The pCa50 was increased in MYH7 R403Q pigs at ages 1 and 3 months, consistent with increased calcium sensitivity, and the Hill slope was decreased, consistent with decreased cooperativity of binding (Figures 3A and 3B). However, the pCa50 and the Hill slope were no different in MYH7 R403 pigs compared with WT pigs at age 9 months. Concomitantly, maximal tension was similar to WT at age 1 month but decreased at ages 3 and 9 months. Initial stiffness and stiffness at relax were similar in WT and MYH7 R403Q pigs at ages 1 and 3 months but were decreased in MYH7 R403Q pigs at age 9 months (Figures 3C to 3E). However, MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited a progressive increase with age in muscle stiffness relative to tension, whereas the stiffness/tension relationship remained relatively stable with age in WT pigs (Figure 3F). Finally, whereas in WT fibers there was a progressive decrease in Ktr with age, Ktr remained relatively preserved at all ages for MYH7 R403Q fibers (Figure 3G).

Altered myofilament dynamics and functional reserve in vivo in MYH7 R403Q pigs

In vitro myofilament abnormalities were corroborated by invasive in vivo hemodynamic studies (Figure 4A). MYH7 R403Q pigs at age <6 months showed hypercontractility as reflected by increased end-systolic LV elastance, preload recruitable stroke work (PRSW), and LVEF (Figure 4B). MYH7 R403Q pigs also exhibited diastolic impairment and stiffer ventricles with markedly elevated end-diastolic LV elastance (Eed), LV end-diastolic filling pressures (EDP), and Ded (Figure 4B). Cardiac dysfunction was associated with increased plasma BNP levels in MYH7 R403Q pigs (Figure 4C). Despite enhanced inotropy at baseline, mutant pigs had limited cardiac reserve, characterized by a blunted β-adrenergic recruitment of systolic and diastolic function in response to dobutamine, including cardiac index, PRSW, stroke volume, dP/dtmin, tau, and Eed (Figure 4D).

Acute treatment with MYK-581, an analog of the cardiac myosin inhibitor mavacamten,10 reversed the in vivo hemodynamic impairments observed in MYH7 R403Q pigs (Figure 4E). MYK-581 decreased measures of hypercontractility, including end-systolic LV elastance and PRSW, and improved measures of diastolic function, including EDP and Eed, while maintaining stroke volume (Figure 4F). Furthermore, relative to WT pigs, MYH7 R403Q pigs showed smaller decreases in ejection fraction and dP/dtmax after acute treatment with MYK-581 (Figure 4G).

LV cardiomyocyte and fibroblast gene expression assessed by snRNAseq

We performed snRNAseq from LV tissue of WT and MYH7 R403Q pigs at early (age 2-5 months; n = 3) or late (age 6-20 months; n = 10) time points (Figure 5A, Supplemental Figure 3). After quality control, a total of 154,261 LV nuclei were clustered and displayed on a Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (Figure 5). Eleven clusters, each representing a different cell type, were identified by their marker gene expression (Figures 5A and 5B, Supplemental Table 3 and 4, Supplemental Figure 3) and were remarkably similar to the cardiac cell types observed in human cardiac tissue.26,27 Cell type distribution was similar in WT pig hearts and MYH7 R403Q pig hearts and was similar in young and old pigs (data not shown). More than 339 RNAs had altered expression (false discovery rate = 0.001) in R403Q pig cardiomyocytes compared with WT pig cardiomyocytes (Supplemental Table 5). Of the 375 genes with altered expression in MYH7 R403Q cardiomyocytes compared with WT cardiomyocytes, 39 genes have been associated in human genome-wide association studies with a cardiovascular phenotype (Supplemental Table 5). Moreover, multiple RNAs that are perturbed in tissues from human or mouse cardiomyopathy were also differentially expressed in the MYH7 R403Q heart, including NNT, PDE1, CMYA5, CORIN, MYH6, MYH7, and NPPB.

The ratio of β-cardiac myosin heavy chain (encoded by MYH7) to α-cardiac myosin heavy chain (encoded by MYH6) is increased in many cardiomyopathy models. The MYH7:MYH6 ratio in MYH7 R403Q hearts (ratio = 210 ± 33) was also significantly increased (P = 0.003) compared with WT hearts (ratio = 50 ± 14). Furthermore, NPPB expression was significantly increased in MYH7 R403Q hearts (mean R403Q NPPB = 1. 7 ± 0.4 vs WT NPPB = 0.08 ± 0.03; P = 0.008).

In WT pig hearts, snRNAseq identified 7 different cardiomyocyte states (Figure 5B), analogous to human cardiomyocyte states.26,27 Importantly, cardiomyocyte state CM1 was significantly decreased, whereas CM3, a stressed cardiomyocyte state, was significantly increased (Figure 5C), thereby accounting for most changes in RNA expression. RNA expression changes between MYH7 R403Q cardiomyocytes and WT cardiomyocytes were in the same direction in all cardiomyocyte states (for example, NPPB was increased in each CM state; Figure 5D).

Nine fibroblast (FB) cell states found in WT and MYH7 R403Q pig hearts (Figure 5E) were analogous to cell states found in human cardiac tissue.26,27 The distribution of 4 FB states differed between MYH7 R403Q hearts and WT hearts (Figure 5F). MYH7 R403Q fibroblasts FB1 and FB4 were less abundant than in WT hearts, whereas states FB1.3 and FB2 were increased compared with WT. A total of 359 RNAs were dysregulated in fibroblasts (false discovery rate <0.001) (Supplemental Table 6). Unlike in cardiomyocytes, several of these RNAs, including SHROOM4, LTBP3, and CORIN, were significantly upregulated in 1 FB state, FB2, compared with other FB states (Figure 5G). Notably, the transforming growth factor (TGF)-β receptor RNAs, TGFBR1 and TGFBR3, were also upregulated in this FB state.

Discussion

We have characterized a minipig HCM model that recapitulates many features of human HCM at molecular, cellular, and organ levels. The hemodynamic abnormalities are most congruous with nonobstructive HCM. Because this model is amenable to detailed analysis of the phenotype, including in vivo invasive hemodynamic assessments, imaging with echocardiography and CMR, histological analysis, skinned myofiber biomechanical studies, transcriptomics, and circulating biomarker analysis, it offers insights into the mechanisms by which the MYH7 R403Q pathogenic variant leads to HCM. We anticipate that further molecular and physiologic studies of this large animal model will lead to novel therapies for HCM, as evidenced by our evaluation of the in vivo hemodynamic effect of MYK-581. Furthermore, the LV fibrosis and diastolic impairment in this model, documented by the in vivo hemodynamic studies and supported by the isolated fiber mechanics and histology, are relevant to heart failure with preserved ejection fraction more generally.

MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited cardiac morphological abnormalities that were similar to human HCM. In vivo cardiac imaging showed cardiac hypertrophy beginning early in life, which was corroborated ex vivo by an increased heart-mass/body-mass ratio. Despite some asymmetric septal hypertrophy, MYH7 R403Q pigs did not have evidence of significant LVOT obstruction. Among human patients, approximately one-third have obstruction, one-third have no obstruction, and one-third have labile obstruction that is evident only on provocation.30 Although there are no published data on the relative prevalences of nonobstructive and obstructive HCM in patients with the specific MYH7 R403Q variant, the respective prevalences are 76% and 26% in patients with all MYH7 variants collectively.31 Because of limited availability of pigs, some of our measurements approached but did not achieve statistical significance. However, there was a dramatic increase in left atrial area, another common feature of HCM, which may be caused by noncompliance of the left ventricle,32 mitral regurgitation, and primary atrial myopathy caused by the genetic variant. Histologically, MYH7 R403Q pigs had cardiomyocyte nuclear hypertrophy, myofibrillar disarray, and interstitial fibrosis, which are also characteristic of human disease.33 These histopathological abnormalities were accompanied by an increase in mRNA expression of the profibrotic genes TGFβ1 and TGFβ2. These histological changes may cause electrophysiological perturbations that are one mechanism leading to arrhythmias and death in MYH7 R403Q pigs. T-peak to T-end, which was prolonged in MYH7 R403Q pigs, is thought to represent a spatial dispersion of repolarization either transmurally or globally, that predisposes to arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death in different disorders.

MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited structural and functional vascular abnormalities. Perivascular fibrosis and inflammation, arteriolar intimal hyperplasia, and arteriolar thrombosis were observed. In addition, capillary density in MYH7 R403Q myocardium was decreased. Myocardial flow reserve in response to vasodilator challenge was impaired in MYH7 R403Q pigs. Furthermore, the severity of intimal hyperplasia appeared to be associated with the severity of cardiac hypertrophy. Therefore, the pathogenic mechanisms leading to myocardial hypertrophy in different pigs or even in different segments of individual pigs may be also responsible for small vessel abnormalities. It is also possible that these vascular abnormalities are responsible for chronic ischemic injury, reflected by the elevated plasma troponin I in MYH7 R403Q pigs, leading to replacement fibrosis. However, we cannot rule out other mechanisms for chronic injury, such as biophysical stress to cardiomyocytes from sarcomere dysfunction (see the following text). In human patients, similar remodeling of intramyocardial coronary arterioles, with medial hypertrophy and/or intimal hyperplasia, perimysial and intra-arterial fibrosis, and luminal narrowing, have been observed,34 along with decreased myocardial capillary density.35 Microvascular structural and functional abnormalities are evident even in human carriers of HCM-associated genetic variants who have little or no cardiac hypertrophy.36,37

Systolic and diastolic abnormalities in MYH7 R403Q pigs on in vivo cardiac imaging and invasive hemodynamic studies showed remarkable correlation with in vitro myofiber studies. At <6 months of age, before the onset of puberty, MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited hypercontractility and impaired relaxation. Muscle fibers at the same age showed decreased maximal tension and Ca2+ cooperativity with elevated Ca2+ sensitivity. Increased Ca2+ sensitivity likely drove the hypercontractile phenotype. The increased stiffness/tension ratio suggests an increased contribution of myosin to developed force, potentially through an increased number of engaged heads or increased force per head. Whereas WT fibers showed a decreasing Ktr with increasing tension, the Ktr was maintained in MYH7 R403Q fibers and appeared to be independent of tension. At similar tensions, changes in Ktr typically reflect changes in the rate of either crossbridge attachment or detachment. MYH7 R403Q pigs also had limited cardiac reserve in response to adrenergic stimulation and elevated filling pressures, suggesting a potential mechanism for exertional intolerance in patients, particularly those without LVOT obstruction. By 9 months of age, MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited a decline in contractile function. Although muscle fibers continued to show decreased maximal tension, Ca2+ cooperativity and Ca2+ sensitivity had normalized. Decreased maximal tension in the absence of elevated Ca2+ sensitivity likely contributed to decreased contractile function. Thus, the decrease in systolic function was likely not merely a reflection of chronic myocardial injury and fibrosis, but a consequence of biophysical changes at the myofiber level. The decreased passive stiffness observed in MYH7 R403Q fibers at 9 months of age indicates increased compliance of a nonmyosin origin. Although mild age-dependent decreases in Ca2+ sensitivity have been documented in WT rat cardiomyocytes,38 to our knowledge, this study is the first to document Ca2+ sensitivity that is abnormally high at an early age but decreases markedly at later ages. The mechanism of this age-dependent change in Ca2+ sensitivity remains to be elucidated.

The MYH R403Q variant has been documented in vitro to shift in the equilibrium toward an on-state of myosin (disordered relaxed state) and away from an off-state (super relaxed state).10 Mavacamten stabilizes the super relaxed state of β-cardiac myosin. We have shown for the first time in a large animal model of HCM that the mavacamten analog MYK-581 attenuates cardiac hypercontractility and diastolic dysfunction in vivo. Although these findings are consistent with clinical data from human patients with HCM, it has been difficult to differentiate direct pharmacological effects from the benefit of relieving LVOT obstruction. We have correlated for the first time the in vivo hemodynamic effects with the molecular biomechanical mechanisms that likely underlie the clinical benefit observed in human patients.

Given the considerable morphologic changes in hearts undergoing hypertrophic remodeling, we used snRNAseq to measure changes in cell type and cell state distribution as well as RNA expression. No statistically significant differences were identified in MYH7 R403Q cardiac cell type distribution compared with the distribution of WT cell types. A limitation of the current study is that because the relative contribution of endothelial cells and mural cells to the pig LV is small (endothelial cells ∼5% and mural cells ∼2%), changes in cell composition are not accurately measured in the present study. We anticipate that future studies will isolate endothelial cells and mural cells and eventually determine the mechanisms that lead to the histologic changes observed in capillary density and arterial morphology.

However, there were significant differences in both cardiomyocyte and fibroblast state distributions found in MYH R403Q and WT hearts. That is, the stressed cardiomyocytes state CM3 and fibroblast states FB1.3 and FB2 were increased in MYH R403Q hearts compared with WT hearts. The pig CM3 state shares many of the same genes as in the human CM3 state that we have previously defined.26,27,39 A total of 339 cardiomyocyte genes were found to be dysregulated in R403Q hearts compared with WT hearts. Because genetic polymorphisms in 24 of these genes are associated with cardiovascular traits (Supplemental Table 5 and the NHGRI-EBI Catalog of human genome-wide association studies),40 we anticipate that further studies of the MYH R403Q pig will define the mechanisms that cause the different genome-wide association studies–associated cardiac phenotypes to be associated with expression of these genes. In follow-up studies, we have identified gene expression changes in MYH R403Q hearts that are associated with microarchitectural changes in cardiomyocytes and increases in extracellular volume.39

These snRNAseq data are the first to be reported from a genetically engineered model of HCM. They complement studies on human cardiac tissue samples, which are limited by genetic, demographic, pharmacological, and environmental heterogeneity of the subjects and by the use of tissue from subjects with more advanced disease.

A few other genetically engineered pig models of HCM have recently been reported: MYH6 R453C,41 MYH7 R453C (constructed by 2 different investigator groups),41,42 MYH7 R723 G,43 MYBPC3 R495Q,44 and biallelic MYBPC3 truncating variants in exons 6 and 23.42 Almost all of these models exhibited in utero or early postnatal death, associated in most cases with severe cardiac abnormalities such as hypertrophy and fibrosis. Although a small proportion of pigs generated survived birth, they exhibited failure to thrive and were euthanized within days.41 Therefore, none of the other models allowed longitudinal phenotype characterization into adulthood.

Although the MYH6 R453 C pigs exhibited no evident cardiac phenotype, they had low birth weight and were weaker.41 One of 3 liveborn MYH6 R453C pigs died at 4 days of age and the other 2 were euthanized. Like our MYH7 R403Q pigs, the MYH7 R453C pigs generated by the same investigators showed LV hypertrophy, elevated cardiac expression of NPPA and NPPB, and decrease in microvascular density. However, the MYH7 R453C pigs had much more extensive cardiac fibrosis. All the MYH7 R453C pigs generated by another group of investigators were stillborn. Although the heart-mass/body-mass ratio was comparable to WT and no difference was detected in cardiac collagen expression, the ventricular-mass/body-mass ratio was 48% greater in the MYH7 R453C pigs. Of 16 MYH7 R723G pigs, 11 were stillborn and 5 were euthanized within 24 hours for failure to thrive.43 Like our MYH7 R403Q pigs, the MYH7 R723G pigs exhibited mild myocyte disarray, nuclear enlargement, and an increase in the MYH7:MYH6 ratio. The MYH7 R723 G pigs exhibited no cardiac hypertrophy, but at a very early age that we did not assess in our MYH7 R403Q pigs. Of 3 MYBPC3 R495Q pigs, all 3 died within 10 days of birth.44 Like our MYH7 R403Q pigs, the MYBPC3 R495Q pigs exhibited elevated serum biomarkers of cardiac injury and cardiac fibrosis. However, these pigs also had elevated serum inflammatory cytokines and severe myocardial inflammatory cell infiltration, likely to a greater degree than in our MYH7 R403Q pigs. Of 9 pigs with biallelic MYBPC3 truncating variants, 1 was stillborn and 8 were mummified.42 The heart-mass/body-mass ratio was increased 47% relative to WT. Like our MYH7 R403Q pigs, the MYBPC3 truncating variant exhibited elevated cardiac expression of NPPA and NPPB.

Whereas many of the porcine models exhibited early cardiac inflammation and fibrosis, preceding cardiac hypertrophy, inflammatory cell infiltration in human patients is variable and can be mild.33 Therefore, the data from this study and from other porcine models suggest that porcine cardiomyocytes may have a unique response to sarcomere gene variants that is distinct from murine or human cardiomyocytes, given the robust cardiac inflammation and fibrosis that develops independently of severe hypertrophy.

To assess the translational relevance of this model, we compared the phenotype of MYH7 R403Q pigs with that reported in human patients with the same pathogenic variant (Supplemental Table 7).6,15,45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 Early clinical studies suggested that the MYH7 R403Q variant is associated with relatively high penetrance, early disease expression, substantial hypertrophy, ventricular arrhythmias, and an increased risk of sudden cardiac death compared with many other HCM-causing variants. Although contemporary studies indicate considerable heterogeneity among affected families, the MYH7 R403Q variant continues to be regarded as a prototypic MYH7 mutation with a well-defined gain-of-function mechanism characterized by hypercontractility and increased energetic cost of contraction. Compared with many MYBPC3 variants, which often demonstrate later and less penetrant disease expression, the MYH7 R403Q variant generally produces earlier and more reproducible phenotypic manifestations.

The MYH7 R403Q pig model recapitulates the major structural, histopathologic, physiological, and molecular features of human MYH7 R403Q-associated HCM, including ventricular hypertrophy, myocyte disarray, interstitial fibrosis, preserved systolic function, coronary microvascular abnormalities, conduction-system remodeling, and energetic dysfunction. Importantly, the MYH7 R403Q pigs also exhibit progressive myocardial inflammatory and immune activation, providing mechanistic insights that are difficult to obtain from human studies. However, several clinical manifestations of human disease have not yet been observed, including atrial fibrillation, sudden cardiac death, overt heart failure, and dynamic LVOT obstruction. These differences likely reflect species-specific factors as well as the relatively young age of the animals studied. Overall, the MYH7 R403Q pig reproduces many of the core features of human HCM while providing experimental access to disease mechanisms that cannot be readily investigated in patients.

Future studies should include long-term monitoring for atrial and ventricular arrhythmias, for which we have identified substrates, including cardiac fibrosis and electrocardiographic changes. Long-term longevity data will determine potential causes of death, such as atrial fibrillation and stroke, ventricular arrhythmias, and heart failure. Although the hypercontractility observed in juvenile pigs was no longer evident in older pigs, they were not monitored to an age when they may have progressed to end-stage HCM with abnormally low LVEF.

The MYH7 R403Q pigs represent a novel genetic model of nonobstructive HCM with LV fibrosis and diastolic impairment that is amenable to detailed analyses, including in vivo invasive hemodynamic assessments, in vivo cardiac imaging, skinned myofiber biomechanical studies, histological analysis, and transcriptomics. Our data support the hypothesis that HCM is initially characterized by hypercontractility, chronic ischemia, myocardial disarray, inflammation, and fibrosis, later progressing to normal or reduced contractility, elevated ventricular filling pressures, and increased risk of sudden death. Our LV snRNAseq data are the first to be derived from a genetically engineered model of HCM. This translational dataset strengthens the mechanistic link between in vivo HCM phenotypes, biophysical twitch mechanics, and sarcomere variants while supporting the hypothesis that myofilament-targeted therapies may be beneficial in patients with HCM. This disease model has shown great utility in evaluating the effect of the cardiac myosin inhibitor MYK-581 and will enable detailed studies of additional candidate therapies for HCM and possibly heart failure with preserved ejection fraction more generally.

COMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: MYH7 R403Q pigs exhibited myocardial vascular abnormalities, including decreased capillary density and intimal hyperplasia and decreased luminal diameters of intramyocardial arterioles. Plasma troponin I and B-type natriuretic peptide were elevated in MYH7 R403Q pigs. In vivo hemodynamic studies in MYH7 R403Q pigs documented hypercontractility, elevated end-diastolic pressures, stiffer ventricles, blunted β-adrenergic enhancement of systolic and diastolic function, and increased Ca2+ sensitivity with decreased cooperativity.

TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: The cardiac myosin inhibitor MYK-581, an analog of mavacamten, reversed many of these biomechanical abnormalities. RNAseq analysis of MYH7 R403Q pigs showed upregulation of profibrotic and proinflammatory pathways. Thus, our studies in MYH7 R403Q pigs identified pathophysiological mechanisms of HCM that may be targeted for therapeutic interventions, including by cardiac myosin inhibitors.

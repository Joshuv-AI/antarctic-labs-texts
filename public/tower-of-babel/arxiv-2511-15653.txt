The dga of planar loops when 2 ​ n = 4 2n=4
Guy Boyde

Abstract
. The dga of planar loops was introduced in recent work of the author, Boyd, Randal-Williams, and Sroka, where a minimal model for it was given. This dga enjoys two natural ‘reflection’ involutions. In the first nontrivial case, 2 ​ n = 4 2n=4 , we give a new model which incorporates these involutions, as well as a more explicit description of the existing model.

We must argue that deleting a bar cannot decrease the number of dividers, and can increase it by at most one. After a bar deletion, any preexisting divider certainly still doesn’t intersect the graffito, but it may:

•

have moved ‘off the end’ of the graffito, if the deletion was one of the endmost bars, or

•

have been identified with an adjacent divider, if the bar between them was deleted.

In either of these cases, the bar deletion must have unpinned a loop:

•

if there was a divider next to an endmost bar, then necessarily there was a loop supported only on that bar, and

•

if there were two adjacent dividers, then the bar between them contained a loop supported only on that bar.

In other words, if the number of dividers decreases, then actually we have

Also, a bar deletion performs only one multiplication, so creates only one new diagram, and hence can increase the number of dividers by at most one.

(\mathbb{Z}[a],a)
, which enjoys a natural map to
(\mathbb{Z},0)
. We consider
\mathbb{Z}[a]
as a graded ring, with
|a|=1
. A
\mathbb{Z}[a]
-module with additional grading
is then a graded module over the graded ring
\mathbb{Z}[a]
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
f\colon C\to D
be a map of chain complexes of finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}[a]
-modules with additional grading.

(1)

If
f\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z}
is a weak equivalence, then
f
is a weak equivalence.

(2)

If
f
is a weak equivalence, then
f\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}R
is a weak equivalence for all pointed rings
(R,a)
.

Proof.

Note first that if
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}[a]
-module with additional grading, and
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z}=0
, then
M=0
. This is because all elements of
M
are divisible by
a
, so
M_{i}\neq 0
implies
M_{i-1}\neq 0
, but finite generation means that
M_{i}=0
for
i\ll 0
. For (1), we can equivalently show that
H_{*}(E)=0
, where
E
is the mapping cone of
f
. Since
C
and
D
are degree-wise free,
E
is also degree-wise free, so
E\xrightarrow{a\cdot-}E
is injective, and we get a short exact sequence
0\to E\xrightarrow{a\cdot-}E\to E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z}\to 0
, hence a homology injection
H_{*}(E)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z}=\mathrm{Coker}(H_{*}(E)\xrightarrow{a\cdot-}H_{*}(E))\hookrightarrow H_{*}(E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z})
, which is zero by assumption since
E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z}
is the mapping cone of
f\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}\mathbb{Z}
. Setting
M=H_{*}(E)
gives the result (
E
is degree-wise finitely generated because
C
and
D
are, so
H_{*}(E)
is too because
\mathbb{Z}[a]
is Noetherian).

For (2), we have by assumption that
E
is acyclic. Since
E
is also degree-wise free, we may write
H_{*}(E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[a]}R)=\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}[a]}_{*}(0,R)=0
, as required.
∎

We now prove the main results. For any
(R,a)
we have defined maps

(T_{R}[x_{1},x_{3}],d)\xrightarrow{\psi}(T_{R}[x,\hat{x},r,y],d)\xrightarrow{\varphi}(L(4;R,a),d)

and we wish to show that both of these maps are equivalences. We first explain how to give everything an additional grading in the universal case
(\mathbb{Z}[a],a)
. In
Section

, we defined an additional grading on

by counting loops, and we can define an additional grading on
L(4;\mathbb{Z}[a],a)
in the same way (this works since
\mathbb{Z}[a]
is graded by setting
|a|=1
, so when the differential turns loops into factors of

the grading is preserved, c.f.

). To define compatible gradings on the models, recall that
\psi
is defined by

,

, and that

Proof of
Proposition

1.2
.

We wish to show that
\psi
is a weak equivalence. In the universal case
(R,a)=(\mathbb{Z}[a],a)
, we saw above that both domain and codomain can be given an additional grading. It then follows from
Lemma

8.1
that it suffices to prove the result in the special case
(R,a)=(\mathbb{Z},0)
, where we work from now on.

We now claim that it further suffices to show that the restriction
\psi:(T_{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1}],d)\to(T_{\mathbb{Z}}[x,\hat{x},r],d)
is a weak equivalence. To see this, first change basis in the target, writing
T_{R}[x,\hat{x},r,y]\cong T_{R}[x,\hat{x},r,y^{\prime}]
by setting
y^{\prime}=y+2xr
, so that
\psi(x_{3})=y^{\prime}
. The differential can only reduce the number of instances of
x_{3}
in a monomial word, and likewise for
y^{\prime}
, so counting instances of these letters gives compatible increasing filtrations on the two algebras. In the filtration quotients,
x_{3}
and
y^{\prime}
become cycles, so the map on the filtration quotients may be identified as a tensor power of the above restriction of
\psi
, which is a weak equivalence if and only if the restriction itself is a weak equivalence (by the Künneth theorem, using that both complexes are free over
\mathbb{Z}
). The spectral sequence associated to the filtration then gives the claim.

It remains to establish that the restriction
\psi\colon(T_{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1}],d)\to(T_{\mathbb{Z}}[x,\hat{x},r],d)
is a weak equivalence. To see this, note that both
\psi
and the restricted differentials preserve word length, so we must verify for each
\ell\geq 0
that
\psi
is an equivalence on the subcomplex spanned by words of length
\ell
. In the domain, this subcomplex consists of the single word
x_{1}^{\ell}
. In the target, it is (informally) a cube whose vertices are words in
x
and
\hat{x}
, and whose
k
-dimensional faces are words of length
\ell
with
k
instances of
r
. More formally, it is the
\ell
-fold tensor product of the complex obtained in the case
\ell=1
, which consists just of the three letters, with differential
d(x)=d(\hat{x})=0
,
d(r)=\hat{x}-x
(since we are in the special case
a=0
). The homology of this
\ell=1

\mathbb{Z}
in degree 0, generated by (e.g.) the class of
x
, so the result follows.
∎

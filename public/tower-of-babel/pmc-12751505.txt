Can the detection of serum glutathione reductase serve as an effective complement to traditional tumor biomarkers in clinical laboratory medicine for patients with hepatocellular carcinoma?
Authors: Qingzhu Zheng, Siyan Chen, Jiamiao Weng, Huaxiang Huang, Xiaohong Xu, Yingping Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most prevalent malignancies within the digestive system, characterized by alarmingly high incidence and mortality rates. Early screening and diagnosis play a crucial role in the treatment and prognosis of HCC patients. We investigated whether the detection of serum glutathione reductase (GR) can serve as an effective complement to traditional tumor biomarkers in clinical laboratory medicine for patients with HCC.MethodsWe analyzed the positive rates and levels of serum GR, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), protein induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-Ⅱ (PIVKA-Ⅱ), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) in 330 patients with HCC, 160 patients with benign liver tumors (BLT), 260 patients with chronic hepatitis B (CHB), 220 patients with liver cirrhosis (LC), and 280 healthy controls (HCs). The diagnostic performance of five biomarkers and their combinations for HCC patients and AFP-negative HCC patients was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and area under the curve (AUC) analysis. Additionally, we investigated the correlation between the levels of GR, PIVKA-Ⅱ or AFP and the clinicopathological parameters of patients with HCC.ResultsThe positive rates and levels of GR, AFP, PIVKA-II, CEA, and CA19-9 were significantly higher in the HCC group, the early HCC group, and the advanced HCC group than in the control group (p < 0.001). In the advanced HCC group, the positivity rates and levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ were markedly higher than those in the early HCC group (p < 0.01). PIVKA-Ⅱ exhibited the highest AUC and sensitivity for HCC diagnosis, at 0.873 (95% CI 0.853–0.891) and 72.7%, respectively, with a specificity of 94.9%. GR demonstrated exceptional diagnostic capability for patients with AFP-negative HCC, AFP-negative early HCC, and AFP-negative advanced HCC, with AUC values ranging from 0.864 to 0.881, sensitivity between 46.4% and 71.4%, and specificity of 97.7% (p < 0.001). Among all possible combinations, the combined detection of the five biomarkers yielded the highest AUC and sensitivity for diagnosing HCC, at 0.922 (95% CI 0.906–0.936) and 90.3%, respectively. The levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ exhibited varying degrees of correlation with the clinicopathological parameters of HCC patients.ConclusionsThe detection of serum GR can serve as an effective complement to traditional tumor biomarkers in clinical laboratory medicine for HCC patients, particularly those with AFP-negative HCC. The combined detection of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 can significantly enhance the diagnostic performance of HCC patients.

Introduction

Primary hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a prevalent malignant neoplasm of the digestive system. According to the global cancer statistics of 2020, primary HCC ranks sixth in terms of incidence among all cancers and third in terms of overall mortality, posing a grave threat to human life and well-being [1]. The incidence of HCC is highest in Asia, with China alone accounting for over 50% of the global burden of liver cancer [2]. Primary HCC can be classified based on its histopathological types, primarily including HCC, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), and combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-CCA). Among these, HCC is the most prevalent type, constituting approximately 75%−85% of all cases [1, 3]. Due to the lack of prominent symptoms in early HCC, more than 70% of patients are often diagnosed at an advanced stage, missing the optimal timing for surgery and resulting in a significantly low 5-year survival rate [4]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for effective strategies for early diagnosis and screening of HCC. Currently, guidelines recommend abdominal ultrasound examinations every six months to monitor high-risk HCC patients [5]. However, the sensitivity of abdominal ultrasound in detecting early HCC in patients with liver cirrhosis is low, with a high false negative rate [6]. The use of abdominal ultrasound as a monitoring tool is limited by the operator’s proficiency and the subjectivity of the results [7]. Despite the remarkable enhancement of HCC diagnosis accuracy brought about by imaging techniques such as CT or MRI and invasive procedures like needle biopsies, their application in the accurate diagnosis of early HCC is limited due to factors such as high cost, invasiveness, insensitivity to small tumors, and the risks of tumor rupture, bleeding, and dissemination associated with needle biopsies [8–11].

Serum biomarker testing, with its advantages of simplicity, minimal invasiveness, and high patient acceptance, plays a crucial role in the diagnosis and screening of asymptomatic individuals. Currently, the serum markers alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and protein induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-Ⅱ (PIVKA-Ⅱ) are widely utilized in the diagnosis and screening of HCC and are recommended for use by multiple national HCC diagnostic guidelines [12–17]. Although serum AFP has been widely used as a biomarker for the diagnosis and monitoring of HCC, approximately 30% of HCC patients have been found to have normal or lower concentrations, especially in the early stage of small tumors, while it may be elevated in diseases such as chronic hepatitis, liver cirrhosis, testicular germ cell tumors, and metastatic colon cancer [18–20]. Previous research findings have shown that serum AFP has a sensitivity of 39%−65% and a specificity of 76%−94% in detecting HCC, particularly with lower sensitivity in diagnosing early HCC, thereby limiting its diagnostic accuracy [21–23]. Currently, the value of using serum AFP alone for early diagnosis and screening of HCC has been a subject of controversy [24]. Therefore, the search for new biomarkers and the implementation of multi-index combined detection are crucial in reducing misdiagnosis rates, improving the detection rate of early HCC, and enhancing the screening capabilities for a large population. PIVKA-Ⅱ, also known as des-gamma carboxy prothrombin (DCP), is an aberrant precursor of coagulation factor II that is formed due to inadequate carboxylation during the malignant transformation of liver cells. In 1984, Liebman et al. first reported the elevated levels of PIVKA-Ⅱ in the serum of HCC patients, suggesting it as a novel biomarker for HCC [25]. Research has indicated that serum PIVKA-Ⅱ has a greater diagnostic value than AFP for early HCC and can serve as a predictive biomarker for microvascular invasion [26]. Conversely, other studies have shown that serum AFP performs better as a single biomarker for HCC diagnosis than PIVKA-Ⅱ [27]. Hadi et al., on the other hand, have demonstrated no significant difference in the diagnostic performance of AFP and PIVKA-Ⅱ for HCC in high-risk patients, suggesting that the replacement or addition of PIVKA-Ⅱ testing in clinical examinations may not hold much value [28]. However, the majority of studies have indicated that the combined detection of serum AFP and PIVKA-Ⅱ outperforms the use of individual biomarkers for HCC diagnosis [29–31]. Therefore, the diagnostic value of serum PIVKA-Ⅱ for HCC remains a subject of debate. The current understanding of whether PIVKA-Ⅱ can completely replace or supplement AFP in the diagnosis of HCC remains unclear and warrants further investigation. Serum carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) are a class of relatively nonspecific antigens widely used as biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis of malignant tumors in the digestive system, such as the gastrointestinal tract and liver [32–34]. It has been reported that elevated levels of serum CEA and CA19-9 are associated with HCC recurrence and poor prognosis [35, 36]. This may potentially compensate for the limitations of serum AFP and PIVKA-Ⅱ in diagnosing HCC false negatives, effectively enhancing the sensitivity and early screening capabilities for HCC diagnosis.

The etiology of HCC is complex and diverse, with its underlying mechanisms still not fully elucidated. An increasing body of research indicates that despite the presence of various antioxidative systems in the human body to defend against the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and their derivatives, elevated oxidative stress occurs when the body’s antioxidative capacity is compromised. This disrupted balance between pro-oxidants and antioxidants can contribute to the occurrence and progression of diseases such as liver cancer [37–39]. Numerous reports suggest that glutathione (GSH) and its synergistic enzymes, such as glutathione peroxidase (GSHPx), glutathione S-transferase (GST), and glutathione reductase (GR), play critical roles in cellular defense against ROS [40, 41]. Czeczot et al. demonstrated that the imbalance of the GSH antioxidative system plays a role in oxidative stress in liver cirrhosis and HCC [42]. GR is one of the crucial determinants in cellular antioxidative stress protection, catalyzing the reduction of oxidized glutathione (GSSG) to reduced GSH with the participation of the cofactor NADPH [43]. Studies have indicated that GR activity is higher in HCC tumor tissue compared to adjacent normal tissue [42]. However, there is limited research on the diagnostic value of serum GR levels and its combination with other biomarkers in HCC patients. It is worth exploring whether the detection of serum GR levels can serve as an effective complement to traditional tumor biomarkers in clinical laboratory medicine for patients with HCC?

In this study, we examined the differences in levels of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 in clinical laboratory serum samples among groups including HCC, benign liver tumor (BLT), chronic hepatitis B (CHB), liver cirrhosis (LC), and healthy controls (HCs). The aim was to explore the diagnostic value of these markers in HCC and analyze the combined detection model that demonstrates ideal diagnostic performance while ensuring specificity. Furthermore, this study further analyzed the diagnostic performance of GR, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 in AFP-negative (AFP < 20 ng/mL) HCC patients. Finally, we evaluated the potential predictive value of serum GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ levels with the clinical pathological characteristics of HCC patients, assessing their role in predicting disease progression.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

This study included a total of 1250 participants who sought medical treatment at the Fujian Medical University Union Hospital from January 2019 to August 2022. The participants consisted of 330 individuals with HCC, 160 individuals with BLT, 260 individuals with CHB, 220 individuals with LC, and 280 HCs. HCC patients were diagnosed based on internationally recognized or professional society-approved diagnostic criteria, which included a combination of imaging and postoperative histopathology without any prior anti-tumor treatment. The Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system was used to determine the stage of HCC, with 40.6% classified as early stage (0, A) and 59.4% as advanced stage (B, C) HCC. The differentiation degree of HCC was evaluated using the international Edmondson-Steiner four-grade (I-IV) grading system. BLT patients included 67 cases of liver hemangioma, 11 cases of nodular regenerative hyperplasia, 35 cases of liver cysts, and 47 cases of liver abscess. The HC group consisted of individuals without any signs of disease, recruited from the Physical Examination Center of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital. Before clinical treatment, 5 ml of peripheral blood was collected from each participant and serum samples were separated by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 5 min for testing. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from all participants (Ethics Review Number: 2023KJCX052). Measurement of serum levels of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA and CA19-9

According to the manufacturer’s instructions, the level of serum GR was determined using an ultraviolet enzyme assay (Roche, Mannheim, Germany). The levels of serum AFP, CEA, and CA19-9 were measured using electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche, Mannheim, Germany). The level of serum PIVKA-Ⅱ was measured using a chemiluminescent enzyme immunoassay (Fujirebio, Inc., Tokyo, Japan). The cut-off values for all tested indicators (GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9) were directly adopted from the reference values recommended in the corresponding manufacturer’s reagent instructions. These cut-off values have been rigorously validated through our clinical laboratory’s internal quality control and external quality assessment, confirming their suitability for our laboratory’s testing system and the local population. They have already been implemented as standard clinical diagnostic criteria in routine use. The specific cut-off values are set as follows: GR, 73 U/L; AFP, 20 ng/mL; PIVKA-Ⅱ, 40 mAU/mL; CEA, 5 ng/mL; CA19-9, 37 U/mL.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 22.0, Microsoft Excel, Graphpad 6.02, and Medcalc 15.2.2 software. The comparison of positivity rates was conducted using the chi-square test. The Mann-Whitney U test was employed to determine significant differences in quantitative data between the two groups. The HCC group was considered the disease group, and the non-HCC group served as the control group for evaluating the diagnostic performance of various biomarkers. A binary logistic regression analysis was conducted, incorporating GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 as independent variables, and the presence of HCC as the dependent variable. The predicted probability output of the model was defined as the combined prediction probability and used as a continuous diagnostic indicator. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess the discriminative ability of the combined model. Furthermore, the cut-off values for each biomarker were based on the reference values recommended in the respective manufacturer’s reagent instructions. For the combined detection of biomarkers, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), positive likelihood ratio (PLR), and negative likelihood ratio (NLR) were calculated according to the principle that a test is considered positive if any of the biomarkers is positive. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of serum GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 positivity rates and levels among different groups

The positivity rates and levels of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 were significantly higher in the HCC group, early HCC group, and advanced HCC group compared to the BLT group, CHB group, LC group, and HC group (p < 0.001) (Table 2; Fig. 1A-E). The positivity rates and levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ were significantly higher in the advanced HCC group compared to the early HCC group (p < 0.01) (Table 2; Fig. 1A-C). There were no significant differences in the positivity rates and levels of CEA or CA19-9 between the early HCC group and the advanced HCC group (p > 0.05) (Table 2; Fig. 1D, E). There were no statistically significant differences in the five serum biomarkers among the BLT group, CHB group, LC group, and HC group (p > 0.05) (Table 2; Fig. 1A-E). Among the five serum biomarkers, PIVKA-Ⅱ had the highest positivity rate in the HCC group, early HCC group, and advanced HCC group, with rates of 72.7%, 63.4%, and 79.1%, respectively (Table 2). The positivity rates of AFP in the HCC group, early HCC group, and advanced HCC group were 59.7%, 37.3%, and 75.0%, respectively (Table 2). The positivity rates of GR in the HCC group, early HCC group, and advanced HCC group were 42.4%, 32.8%, and 49.0%, respectively (Table 2). Comparison of the diagnostic performance of individual and combined measurements of serum GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 in HCC

The AUC values for the five serum biomarkers in diagnosing HCC were all greater than 0.690. Among them, the AUC of GR for the diagnosis of HCC was 0.809 (95% CI 0.787–0.831), with a sensitivity of 42.4% and the highest specificity of 97.2% (Table 3). Compared to AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9, GR exhibited the highest PPV of 84.3% and PLR of 15.01 (p < 0.001) (Table 3). PIVKA-Ⅱ had the highest AUC for HCC diagnosis at 0.873 (95% CI 0.853–0.891), with a specificity of 94.9% and the highest sensitivity of 72.7% (Table 3). Compared to GR, AFP, CEA, and CA19-9, PIVKA-Ⅱ had the highest NPV of 90.7% and the lowest NLR of 0.29 (p < 0.001) (Table 3). We further evaluated the diagnostic performance of all possible combinations of serum GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 in HCC. Among all possible combinations, the combined detection of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 had the highest AUC of 0.922 (95% CI 0.906–0.936) and a sensitivity of 90.3%, with a specificity of 82.4%. It also had the highest NPV of 95.9% and NLR of 0.12 (p < 0.001) (Table 3). As shown in Fig. 2A, the ROC curves of five biomarkers and their combinations for HCC diagnosis are presented. The five serum biomarkers performed well in diagnosing early HCC, with AUC ranging from 0.651 to 0.814, sensitivity ranging from 29.1% to 63.4%, and specificity ranging from 94.5% to 97.2% (Table 4). The combined detection of the five serum biomarkers had an AUC of 0.875 (95% CI 0.841–0.908) for diagnosing early HCC, with a sensitivity of 83.6%, specificity of 82.4%, NPV of 97.2%, and NLR of 0.20 (p < 0.001) (Table 4). Discussion

Primary liver cancer is a prevalent malignant tumor in the digestive system, characterized by insidious onset, invasive growth, and high metastatic potential [4]. Therefore, it is crucial to establish diagnostic criteria for early HCC and screening strategies for high-risk individuals. The detection of serum biomarkers plays a pivotal role in the diagnosis and screening of HCC. Currently, serum AFP is the most commonly used biomarker for HCC patients. However, its diagnostic efficiency is unsatisfactory and falls short of clinical demands [21–23]. In recent years, there has been extensive attention on the diagnostic role of PIVKA-Ⅱ in HCC. However, the clinical use of PIVKA-Ⅱ detection has not been widely adopted. Furthermore, the debate regarding whether PIVKA-Ⅱ holds greater value than AFP in HCC diagnosis remains unresolved and requires further exploration. GSH and its related enzymes constitute an essential endogenous antioxidant system in the human body, playing a critical role in the complex process of multi-step carcinogenesis [44]. GR, a homodimer flavoprotein, catalyzes the reduction of oxidized GSSG to its reduced form, GSH, with the participation of the cofactor NADPH [43]. GR is involved in various biological functions of cancer cells, such as defense against free radicals and ROS, as well as protein and DNA synthesis through the maintenance of a high ratio of GSH/GSSG [45]. Zhu et al. [46] found that GR can influence cancer cell drug resistance by regulating redox homeostasis. Brzozowa-Zasada et al. [47] demonstrated through immunohistochemical analysis that GR expression in colorectal adenocarcinoma is closely associated with tumor progression. Li et al. [48] further revealed that inhibiting GR can induce oxidative stress, block epithelial-mesenchymal transition and actin cytoskeleton reorganization, thereby suppressing subcutaneous growth and lung metastasis of melanoma. This study functionally confirmed the role of GR in promoting tumor growth and metastasis, providing experimental evidence for targeting it as a therapeutic strategy. Xu et al. [49] elucidated that SLC27A5 downregulates GR via the NRF2 pathway to promote sorafenib-induced ferroptosis in HCC. Furthermore, enhanced oxidative stress in HCC cells may lead to increased secretion of GR. The changes in serum levels of GR in patients may be indicative of the disease state, including the auxiliary diagnosis of HCC. Furthermore, the question arises as to whether the detection of serum GR can serve as an effective complement to traditional tumor biomarkers in clinical laboratory medicine for patients with HCC. This warrants further investigation.

In this study, the diagnostic value of individual and combined detection of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 in HCC was explored by examining the differences in their levels in serum samples among the HCC group, BLT group, CHB group, LC group, and HC group. The positivity rates and serum levels of GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 were significantly higher in the HCC, early HCC, and advanced HCC groups compared to the BLT, CHB, LC, and HC groups (p < 0.001). This indicates that these five biomarkers can effectively distinguish between HCC and non-HCC patients. GR exhibited higher positivity rates in the HCC group, early HCC group, and advanced HCC group, at 42.4%, 32.8%, and 49.0%, respectively. PIVKA-Ⅱ outperformed AFP, with positivity rates of 63.4% in early HCC, 79.1% in advanced HCC, and 72.7% in the overall HCC cohort. Especially in the diagnosis of early HCC, PIVKA-Ⅱ exhibits the highest positivity rate among the five biomarkers, which is consistent with previous reports [26]. The positivity rates and levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ in the advanced HCC group are significantly higher than those in the early HCC group (p < 0.01). This may indicate a correlation between GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, and the progression of HCC. We further compared the diagnostic performance of individual and combined detection of the five serum biomarkers for HCC. The AUC values for the five serum biomarkers in diagnosing HCC are all greater than 0.690, with PIVKA-Ⅱ having the highest AUC of 0.873 (95% CI 0.853–0.891), specificity of 94.9%, and highest sensitivity of 72.7%. Compared to GR, AFP, CEA, and CA19-9, PIVKA-Ⅱ has the highest NPV (90.7%) and the lowest NLR (0.29), (p < 0.001). This indicates that PIVKA-Ⅱ has a higher ability to exclude non-HCC patients. GR has an AUC of 0.809 (95% CI 0.787–0.831), a sensitivity of 42.4%, and the highest specificity of 97.2% for diagnosing HCC. Compared to AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9, GR has the highest PPV (84.3%) and PLR (15.01), (p < 0.001). This indicates that GR has higher accuracy in diagnosing HCC patients. Considering the relatively limited sensitivity of individual parameter detection, we further evaluated the diagnostic performance of all possible combinations of serum biomarkers including GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 for HCC. Among all possible combinations, the combined detection of these five serum markers, GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9, demonstrates the highest AUC and sensitivity for diagnosing HCC, with values of 0.922 (95% CI 0.906–0.936) and 90.3%, respectively. It also shows a specificity of 82.4%, the highest NPV of 95.9%, and an NLR of 0.12 (p < 0.001). This highlights the ability of the combined detection of these five serum markers to significantly improve the diagnostic performance of HCC and exclude non-HCC patients. It is worth noting that the diagnostic performance of the five serum biomarkers is better for early HCC patients, with AUC ranging from 0.651 to 0.814, sensitivity ranging from 29.1% to 63.4%, and specificity ranging from 94.5% to 97.2% (p < 0.001). The combined detection of the five serum biomarkers greatly enhances the diagnostic performance for early HCC. Additionally, we assessed the diagnostic value of GR, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 for AFP-negative HCC, AFP-negative early HCC, and AFP-negative advanced HCC patients. The study revealed that GR exhibited excellent diagnostic capabilities for AFP-negative HCC, AFP-negative early HCC, and AFP-negative advanced HCC patients, with an AUC ranging from 0.864 to 0.881, sensitivity ranging from 46.4% to 71.4%, and a specificity of 97.7% (p < 0.001). Particularly, for AFP-negative early HCC patients, GR demonstrates higher diagnostic performance, with an AUC of 0.864 (95% CI 0.841–0.885), sensitivity of 46.4%, and specificity of 97.7%. This underscores the complementary role of GR in the diagnostic process for AFP-negative HCC patients. Furthermore, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9 demonstrate good diagnostic performance in distinguishing AFP-negative HCC patients from the control group. This further elucidates that, in the laboratory diagnosis of HCC, in addition to specific serum biomarkers such as AFP, the appropriate detection of broad-spectrum digestive system biomarkers can contribute to the improvement of diagnostic performance. Additionally, we analyzed the relationship between serum GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ levels and clinical pathological parameters in HCC patients. HCC patients under the age of 55 exhibited higher levels of serum AFP and PIVKA-Ⅱ (p < 0.01). We observed that HCC patients with tumor sizes larger than 5 cm had higher levels of PIVKA-Ⅱ in their serum (p < 0.01). The levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ were positively correlated with tumor number and BCLC stage in HCC patients (p < 0.01), suggesting their potential role in predicting disease severity. Higher concentrations of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ may indicate a greater number of tumors and a more advanced clinical stage. Histological differentiation refers to the degree of resemblance between cancer cells and normal cells in terms of morphology. Poorly differentiated tumors exhibit a higher degree of malignancy and often have a worse prognosis. In this study, the levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ increased with a decrease in the differentiation degree of HCC, indicating a certain relationship between GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, and the malignancy and prognosis of HCC. Moreover, HCC patients with TTPV exhibited higher levels of serum AFP and PIVKA-Ⅱ (p < 0.001). This may suggest that elevated levels of serum AFP and PIVKA-Ⅱ can be utilized as predictors of the risk of TTPV in HCC patients.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our research findings demonstrate that the five biomarkers, GR, AFP, PIVKA-Ⅱ, CEA, and CA19-9, are effective in distinguishing between HCC and non-HCC patients. Among these markers, PIVKA-Ⅱ has higher diagnostic value than AFP for HCC, including early HCC. The positive rates and levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ were significantly higher in the advanced HCC group compared to the early HCC group, indicating their association with disease progression. In combination, the joint detection of these five biomarkers significantly improved the diagnostic performance of HCC patients, including early stage cases. Encouragingly, GR demonstrated excellent diagnostic ability for AFP-negative HCC patients, particularly AFP-negative early HCC patients. The levels of GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ may play a crucial role in predicting the severity and malignancy of HCC. In summary, the detection of serum GR can serve as an effective complement to traditional tumor biomarkers in clinical laboratory medicine for HCC patients, particularly those with AFP-negative HCC. However, our study has certain limitations. First, it is a single-center study, and all samples were sourced from a single institution, which may introduce selection bias in the study population. The HCC cases in this cohort were primarily associated with hepatitis B virus infection and did not adequately represent populations with other etiologies such as hepatitis C or non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Consequently, the generalizability of the findings may be limited. Future multi-center cohort studies involving diverse regions and etiologies are necessary to further validate the universality of our findings. Second, another important limitation of this study is the lack of long-term follow-up data, such as recurrence time and survival time. This prevents us from validating the correlation between GR, AFP, and PIVKA-Ⅱ levels and the prognosis (such as disease-free survival or overall survival) of HCC patients, thereby failing to fully reveal the long-term prognostic predictive value of these biomarkers. Therefore, collecting and analyzing long-term follow-up data to clarify the role of these biomarkers in prognostic evaluation is a critical direction for future research. It should also be noted that the lack of pairwise comparisons of the biomarkers among the different benign liver disease subgroups (BLT, CHB, LC) prevents this study from providing a detailed assessment of the influence of specific liver disease statuses on diagnostic specificity. This shortcoming warrants further exploration and validation in future studies with an expanded sample size.

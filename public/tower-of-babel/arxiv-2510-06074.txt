On algebro-geometric quotients of torus-invariant subvarieties of the flag variety
Luis Yair Meza Pérez, Pedro L. del Ángel R, Carlos Pompeyo-Gutiérrez, Miguel Angel Dela-Rosa

Abstract
In this paper, we study the subvarieties of a complex flag variety that are invariant under the action of a maximal torus. Using combinatorial techniques derived from matroid theory, we introduce a decomposition of this variety into affine, locally closed subsets, which we refer to as thin Schubert cells , each indexed by an element of a Cartesian product of matroids. We also show that the set of orbits for each of these thin Schubert cells under the action of the torus is, in fact, an orbit space, which gives rise to topologically trivial fiber bundles. As a consequence of this, we prove the existence of algebro-geometric quotients in the sense of Mumford’s Geometric Invariant Theory . The main result of this work is the existence of a surjective map from the set of geometric quotients of thin Schubert cells to the invariant scheme-theoretic points of a complex flag variety, which allows us to decompose it in terms of such quotients and also define a map from the set of these geometric quotients to the invariant homology of the variety. Finally, we give a complete description of the thin Schubert cells for the special case of the flag variety 𝔽 1 < n − 1 ​ ( ℂ n ) \mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n}) and derive explicit counting formulas.

Introduction

Homogeneous spaces
are non-empty algebraic varieties endowed with a transitive action of an algebraic group (reductive, semisimple). In simple terms, they can be thought of as spaces that appear identical regardless of how one moves through them via the group action. More precisely, any such space is isomorphic to a quotient of the form
G/P
, where
G
is an algebraic group and
P
is a closed subgroup. Ehresmann
[
2
]
provides a rigorous and foundational description of these spaces in terms of transitive actions of Lie groups and shows that the Grassmannian
G(d,n)
can be realized as the quotient
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathds{C})/P
for a suitable parabolic subgroup
P
. He also establishes a cellular decomposition of
G(d,n)
into subsets known as
Schubert cells
, thereby generalizing the ideas previously introduced by Hermann Schubert in 1879. Subsequently, Monk
[
7
]
extends some of Ehresmann’s results to complete flag varieties and conducts a thorough study of their topological and algebro-geometric properties. Furthermore, he gives a detailed description of the celullar structure of these varieties in terms of Schubert cells. Building upon these foundational developments, Gelfand et al.
[
4
]
provide three equivalent stratifications of the Grassmannian; the first one uses matroid techniques, and the third one, Schubert cells. In a subsequent work, Gel’fand and Serganova
[
5
]
generalize this approach to arbitrary homogeneous spaces
G/P
, describing it in what they define as
thin
[
Schubert
]
cells
. One can identify a complex flag variety
X
with the quotient
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathds{C})/P
and explicitly describe the parabolic subgroup
P
as the isotropy group of a fixed flag. It is possible to show that the cohomology classes of the Schubert varieties in
X
form an additive basis of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(X)
(
see
Brion
[
1
]
).

Our first goal is to generalize to complex flag varieties two results that Elizondo-Fink-Garay
[
3
]
obtain for the complex Grassmannian
G(d,n)
using combinatorial methods, namely:
(
1
)
realize the thin Schubert cell
G_{M}
as an affine and locally closed subset (
see
Subsection §2.2), and
(
2
)
obtain a decomposition of
G(d,n)
as a disjoint union of these thin cells (Proposition 2.4). In order to proceed, observe that there exists a natural action of the maximal algebraic torus
T\cong(\mathds{C}^{*})^{n}
on the Cartesian product of Grassmannians
G(d_{1},n)\times\cdots\times G(d_{k},n)
under which
\mathds{F}_{d_{1}<\cdots<d_{k}}(\mathds{C}^{n})
is a
T
-invariant subset. This enables us to construct, in Lemma
3.2
, the analogue of a thin Schubert cell on flag varieties, and to prove that it is an affine and locally closed set, and consequently to establish, in Proposition
3.4
, a decomposition of the flag variety as a disjoint union of such cells. The above leads us to the main results of this work:

•

In Theorem
4.1
, we demonstrate that the orbit space of a thin Schubert cell under the action of a subtorus is a universal geometric quotient, and in particular, a universal categorical quotient.

•

In Theorem
4.4
, we prove that the intersection of a
T
-invariant subvariety with a thin cell, modulo the action of a subtorus, admits a universal geometric quotient and, hence, a universal categorical quotient.

•

In Theorem
4.6
, we provide a surjective map from the disjoint union of the universal geometric quotients to
\mathds{F}_{d_{1}<\cdots<d_{k}}(\mathds{C}^{n})^{T}
, which denotes the scheme-theoretic set of
T
-invariant points of
\mathds{F}_{d_{1}<\cdots<d_{k}}(\mathds{C}^{n})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
4.6
, we establish in Corollary
4.7
a decomposition of
\mathds{F}_{d_{1}<\cdots<d_{k}}(\mathds{C}^{n})^{T}
in terms of the disjoint union of the images under the surjective map, and in Corollary
4.8
we further provide a map from the disjoint union of the thin cells to the
T
-invariant homology.

Finally, in Section §
5
we classify the thin Schubert cells in the flag variety
\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
for
n\geq 3
, establish a purely combinatorial criterion that characterizes the non-empty ones, and consequently obtain a complete description of the remaining cases. We then determine the dimension of each class and compute the number of thin Schubert cells in each class.

Matroids and the Grassmannian Variety

Matroids are combinatorial objects useful for capturing the discrete linear algebraic information of an element

\mbox{for every\ }I,J\in B_{M}\mbox{\ and $i\in I\setminus J$, there exists\ }j\in J\setminus I\ \mbox{such that}\ (I\setminus\{i\})\cup\{j\}\in B_{M}.

The subgroup

3
Thin Schubert cells and matroidal decompositions on flag varieties

In this section we introduce the analogue of thin Schubert cells in a complex flag variety and establish a decomposition of this variety in terms of these cells. To this end, we consider the flag variety

\mathds{F}_{d_{1}<\cdots<d_{k}}(\mathds{C}^{n}):=\{W_{1}\subset\cdots\subset W_{k}\subset\mathds{C}^{n}\mid\mathrm{dim}\,W_{i}=d_{i},\ \forall i=1,\ldots,k\}\subset\prod_{i=1}^{k}G(d_{i},n).

4

This section constitutes the core of the paper. Here, we prove the existence of universal geometric quotients of thin Schubert cells and

Theorem 4.1

The universal geometric quotient of

5
Thin Schubert cells in the flag variety
\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})

In the special case of the flag variety
\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
for
n\geq 3
, it is possible to provide a classification of the thin Schubert cells and to determine their number. To that end, we introduce a necessary definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{1},M_{2})\in\mathcal{M}^{1,n-1}_{n}
be a plurimatroid and

\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}=(G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}})\cap\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})

its associated thin Schubert cell. We say that
\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}
is
complete
if
\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}=G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}
, and
proper
if
\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\subsetneq G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}
.

Remark 5.2
.

The completeness is equivalent to
G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}\subset\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
, whereas the properness is equivalent to
G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}\not\subset\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
.

Proposition 5.3

(Characterization of Thin Schubert Cells)
.

Consider the flag variety
\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
with
n\geq 3
. Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{1},M_{2})\in\mathcal{M}^{1,n-1}_{n}
be a plurimatroid, and denote by
B_{M_{1}}
and
B_{M_{2}}
the sets of bases of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively. Let

K_{1}:=\{k\in[n]\mid\{k\}\in B_{M_{1}}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad K_{2}:=\{k\in[n]\mid[n]\setminus\{k\}\in B_{M_{2}}\}.

(5.1)

Then

\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\neq\emptyset\quad\text{if and only if}\quad|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|\neq 1.

Moreover:

1.

\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}
is complete if and only if
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|=0
.

2.

\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}
is proper if and only if
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|>1
.

Proof.

Let
(W_{1},W_{2})\in G(1,n)\times G(n-1,n)
and

v_{k}:=p_{\{k\}}(W_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{k}:=p_{[n]\setminus\{k\}}(W_{2})\quad\forall k\in[n].

Note that

\displaystyle(W_{1},W_{2})\in\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}

\displaystyle\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\alpha_{k}v_{k}=0

(by the incidence equation)

\displaystyle\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{k\in K_{1}\cap K_{2}}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\alpha_{k}v_{k}=0

(since
(W_{1},W_{2})\in G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}
)

•

If
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|=1
, then
\alpha_{k^{\prime}}v_{k^{\prime}}=0
for the unique
k^{\prime}\in K_{1}\cap K_{2}
, which is a contradiction since
\alpha_{k^{\prime}}
and
v_{k^{\prime}}
are nonzero; hence,
\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}=\emptyset
.

•

If
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
, then the last equality is satisfied trivially and imposes no constraint on the summands; consequently,
G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}\subset\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
.

•

If
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|>1
, then the reduced incidence equation imposes exactly one independent condition for
(W_{1},W_{2})\in G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}
to lie in
\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
; thus
\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\subsetneq G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}}
.

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Note that by definition of sets
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
in (
5.1
), we have
|K_{i}|=|B_{M_{i}}|
for
i=1,2
.

Corollary 5.5

(Dimension of Thin Schubert Cells)
.

With the same notation and assumptions as in Proposition
5.3
, the following statements hold:

1.

If
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
, then
\dim\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}=\dim\,(G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}})=|K_{1}|+|K_{2}|-2
.

2.

If
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|>1
, then
\dim\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}=\dim\,(G_{M_{1}}\times G_{M_{2}})-1=|K_{1}|+|K_{2}|-3
.

Corollary 5.6

(Global Counting Formulas for Thin Schubert Cells)
.

In the setting of Proposition
5.3
, let

C:=\{\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\ \text{is complete}\},\quad P:=\{\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\ \text{is proper}\}\quad\text{and}\quad E:=\{\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\ \text{is empty}\}.

Then:

1.

|C|=3^{n}-2^{n+1}+1
.

2.

|P|=4^{n}-3^{n}-n\cdot 3^{n-1}
.

3.

|E|=n\cdot 3^{n-1}
.

Proof.

1.

For each
i\in[n]
there are only three cases: (
a
)
i\in K_{1}
, (
b
)
i\in K_{2}
or (
c
)
i\notin K_{1}\cup K_{2}
, but never in both, since
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
. Hence there are
3^{n}
options, including the cases
K_{1}=\emptyset
or
K_{2}=\emptyset
which we must exclude. Observe that if
K_{1}=\emptyset
, then
i\in K_{2}
or
i\in[n]\setminus K_{2}
, yielding
2^{n}
possibilities and similarly if
K_{2}=\emptyset
. Since the case
K_{1}=K_{2}=\emptyset
has been counted twice, we add it back once. Consequently,
|C|=3^{n}-2^{n+1}+1
.

2.

Here, for each
i\in[n]
we have exactly four options, namely: (
a
)
i\in K_{1}\cap K_{2}
, (
b
)
i\in K_{1}\setminus K_{2}
, (
c
)
i\in K_{2}\setminus K_{1}
or (
d
)
i\notin K_{1}\cup K_{2}
. Hence, there are
4^{n}
possibilities. We exclude now the case
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
, leaving
3^{n}
options —(
b
) - (
d
)—. To conclude, we must also exclude the case
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|=1
. The unique element of this intersection can be choosen in
n
ways, and each of the remaining
n-1
elements has three possibilities —again, (
b
) - (
d
)—, so
3^{n-1}
choices. Therefore,
|P|=4^{n}-3^{n}-n\cdot 3^{n-1}
.

3.

This case has already been done at the end of the preceding item.

∎

Corollary 5.7

(Restricted Counting Formulas for Thin Schubert Cells)
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
5.3
and notation of Corollary
5.6
, let
i,j\in[n]
and

\displaystyle C(i,j)

\displaystyle:=\{\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\in C:|B_{M_{1}}|=i\ \ \text{and}\ \ |B_{M_{2}}|=j\},

\displaystyle P(i,j)

\displaystyle:=\{\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\in P:|B_{M_{1}}|=i\ \ \text{and}\ \ |B_{M_{2}}|=j\},

\displaystyle E(i,j)

\displaystyle:=\{\mathds{F}_{\mathbf{M}}\in E:|B_{M_{1}}|=i\ \ \text{and}\ \ |B_{M_{2}}|=j\}.

Then:

1.

|C(i,j)|=\displaystyle{n\choose i}{n-i\choose j}
.

2.

|P(i,j)|=\displaystyle{n\choose i}\left[{n\choose j}-{n-i\choose j}-i{n-i\choose j-1}\right]
.

3.

|E(i,j)|=\displaystyle i{n\choose i}{n-i\choose j-1}
.

Proof.

For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, fix a set
B_{M_{i}}
of basis of
M_{i}
with
|B_{M_{i}}|=i
. By Remark
5.4
, we have
|K_{i}|=|B_{M_{i}}|
for
i=1,2
.

1.

The subsets
K_{2}
of
[n]
disjoint of
K_{1}
must be chosen from
n-i
elements of
[n]\setminus K_{1}
, in
\displaystyle{n-i\choose j}
ways. Since there are
\displaystyle{n\choose i}
choices for
K_{1}
, the rule product gives
|C(i,j)|=\displaystyle{n\choose i}\displaystyle{n-i\choose j}
.

2.

The total number of subsets
K_{2}
of
[n]
is
\displaystyle{n\choose j}
; among them, those disjoint from
K_{1}
are
\displaystyle{n-i\choose j}
. Those with
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|=1
are obtained choosing the common element in
i
ways and completing
K_{2}
with
j-1
elements from
[n]\setminus K_{1}
, in
\displaystyle{n-i\choose j-1}
ways; hence,
\displaystyle i{n-i\choose j-1}
in total. Therefore,the number of subsets
K_{2}
with
|K_{1}\cap K_{2}|\geq 2
is
\displaystyle{n\choose j}-{n-i\choose j}-i{n-i\choose j-1}
. Multiplying by the
\displaystyle{n\choose i}
choices of
K_{1}
, we conclude that
|P(i,j)|=\displaystyle{n\choose i}\left[{n\choose j}-{n-i\choose j}-i{n-i\choose j-1}\right]
.

3.

This case has already been done at the end of the preceding item.

6
Conclusions

We introduced in Subsection §
2.1
the
plurimatroids
, conceived as Cartesian products of matroids, and used them to define
thin Schubert cells
on flag varieties. From this viewpoint, we extended to these varieties several results previously established by Elizondo et al. for Grassmannians. In Section §
3
we proved that such thin cells are affine and locally closed subsets of the flag variety and, therefore, they induce a decomposition of it. Pursuing the same combinatorial approach, in Section §
4
we established the existence of algebro-geometric quotients —categorical, geometric and universal— for certain actions of algebraic tori. Furthermore, in Subsection §
4.1
we obtained, among other consequences, a decomposition of the set of scheme-theoretic points of the flag variety that are invariant under the action of a torus. Finally, in Section §
5
we characterized all non-empty thin Schubert cells in the case of the flag variety
\mathds{F}_{1<n-1}(\mathds{C}^{n})
for
n\geq 3
, and moreover, we obtained explicit formulas for counting all of them. The techniques developed in this work suggest several avenues for future research, including possible extensions to generalized or symplectic flag varieties, and even to Schubert varieties.

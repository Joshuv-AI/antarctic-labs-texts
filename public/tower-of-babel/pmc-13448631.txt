β-glucan elicits APOE-dependent peripheral trained immunity to suppress hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Nan Jiang, Tong Wu, Rensheng Yang, Guijin He, Bo Ding, Can Zhu, Hanqing Liu, Xinyu Xu, Beng Yang, Haiyang Xie, Shusen Zheng, Rongliang Tong, Jian Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough immunotherapy has revolutionized cancer treatment, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) continues to demonstrate limited clinical responses, highlighting the urgent need for novel immunomodulatory strategies. Trained immunity, an emerging paradigm wherein innate immune cells develop a memory-like phenotype through epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming, offers a promising avenue to remodel the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. This study investigated whether β-glucan-induced trained immunity could potentiate antitumor immunity against HCC.MethodsWe established orthotopic HCC mouse models to investigate the role of trained immunity induced by whole β-glucan particle (WGP) in the HCC microenvironment, particularly in modulating hepatic apolipoprotein E (APOE)-positive monocytes/macrophages. Transcriptional changes in trained monocytes/macrophages were identified by analyzing single-cell RNA sequencing and bulk RNA-sequencing data from the livers of WGP-treated and control mice. Mechanistic studies were performed using Apoe−/− mice and in situ monocyte/macrophage engineering. Flow cytometry was performed to assess immune cell phenotypes and phagocytosis, while luminescence-based assays were used to evaluate cytotoxic activity. The translational potential was assessed using human monocyte training assays.ResultsThis study demonstrated that preconditioning with WGP, a trained immunity inducer, increased the accumulation of trained monocytes/macrophages in the liver and suppressed tumor progression in HCC mouse models. Mechanistically, WGP-trained APOE+ monocytes/macrophages exhibited a decrease in lipid accumulation and endoplasmic reticulum stress, thereby enhancing their antitumor function. Genetic deletion of Apoe in monocytes/macrophages abrogated the antitumor effects of WGP, demonstrating that APOE+ monocytes/macrophages are essential mediators of WGP-induced trained immunity. Adoptive transfer of WGP-trained bone marrow-derived macrophages suppressed the growth of HCC in recipient mice. Furthermore, WGP induced trained immunity in human monocytes, leading to enhanced killing of HCC cells. Notably, combination therapy with WGP and anti-programmed death-ligand 1 antibody achieved superior tumor control compared with either monotherapy.ConclusionsThese findings identify a critical role for trained APOE+ monocytes/macrophages in WGP-mediated antitumor immunity in the liver. Harnessing WGP-induced peripheral trained immunity represents a novel therapeutic strategy for HCC.Keywords: Myeloid, Immunotherapy, Hepatocellular Carcinoma, Innate, Gene therapy

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Trained immunity has been demonstrated to exert antitumor effects across different cancer types. However, whether trained immunity elicits comparable levels of antitumor innate immune memory in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains to be determined. Furthermore, the comparative effects of BCG and β-glucan in HCC have not been investigated, and the mechanisms through which trained immunity restricts tumor growth remain poorly understood.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

We demonstrate that whole β-glucan particle (WGP)-induced trained immunity suppresses HCC progression by promoting the accumulation of apolipoprotein E (APOE)-positive monocytes/macrophages in the liver. WGP alleviates lipid accumulation and endoplasmic reticulum stress in these cells, thereby enhancing their antitumor function. Importantly, we identify APOE+ monocytes/macrophages as essential for WGP-induced trained immunity. Moreover, combining trained immunity induction with programmed death-ligand 1 blockade results in superior tumor control in HCC, highlighting a potential synergistic therapeutic strategy.

Background

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), a common primary liver malignancy, poses a significant global health burden characterized by high morbidity, a rapidly rising mortality rate, and a 5-year survival rate of only 21%.1 Although combination therapies of vascular endothelial growth factor inhibitors and immune checkpoint inhibitors have become the cornerstone for advanced HCC, treatment outcomes remain suboptimal, necessitating novel therapeutic strategies.2 3

Through metabolic and epigenetic reprogramming, innate immune cells can develop a long-lasting, hyper-responsive state termed “trained immunity”, which enhances protection on re-exposure to antigens.4 5 This reprogramming spans peripheral myeloid cells and their bone marrow progenitors, persisting through myelopoiesis.6–8 BCG and β-glucan are well-characterized inducers of trained immunity. While primarily studied in reinfection contexts, evidence suggests that tumor cells may also reactivate trained immunity.6 9–11

β-glucans expand myeloid progenitors, establishing central and peripheral trained immunity.8 12 Whole β-glucan particle (WGP) from Saccharomyces cerevisiae is a particulate formulation requiring phagocytosis, which differs from soluble forms used in prior studies.6 Although antitumor functions of β-glucans have been demonstrated in bladder cancer, pancreatic cancer, lung cancer and melanoma, their potential to induce trained immunity in HCC remains unexplored.6 11 13 Moreover, the comparative effects of BCG and β-glucan in HCC, and the mechanisms by which trained innate immunity restricts tumor growth, remain incompletely understood.

Apolipoprotein E (APOE) transports lipids and is secreted by hepatocytes and macrophages.14 15 Its role in cancer is context-dependent across different tumor types.16–19 In HCC, high APOE expression correlates with better survival in database analyses.20 While APOE mediates β-glucan-induced trained immunity in pulmonary macrophages,21 its interaction with β-glucan in the liver remains unclear. Therefore, further investigation of APOE+ macrophages in β-glucan-treated HCC models is necessary.

In this study, we examined whether WGP induces trained immunity in the liver. Our results show that WGP-trained myeloid cells suppress HCC progression, with APOE+ monocytes/macrophages serving as key mediators that exhibit traits of trained immunity, including enhanced phagocytosis and tumor cytotoxicity. We further demonstrated a connection between their functions, lipid accumulation, and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress. Collectively, our study highlights WGP-induced peripheral trained immunity as a novel therapeutic strategy against HCC.

Methods

Animal studies

Male wild-type C57BL/6J mice were obtained from Hangzhou Medical College (Zhejiang, China). NOD/ShiLtJGpt-Prkdcem26Cd52Il2rgem26Cd22/Gpt (NSG) mice and Apoe⁻/⁻ mice on a C57BL/6J genetic background were obtained from GemPharmatech (Jiangsu, China). All animals were maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions in the Laboratory Animal Center of the First Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine.

Results

WGP-induced trained immunity in myeloid cells elicits antitumor immunity in HCC

To investigate the prognostic value of innate immunity in HCC, we evaluated its correlation with patient outcomes using data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that a higher innate immune score was significantly associated with reduced recurrence in patients with HCC (figure 1A). The protective role of innate immunity in patients with HCC led us to compare WGP and BCG, two well-established inducers of trained innate immunity, in the orthotopic Hep53.4 HCC model (figure 1B). While BCG reduced tumor burden and improved survival compared with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) controls, WGP resulted in a significantly greater antitumor effect, with a further reduction in tumor weight and extended survival (figure 1C,D). Prior exposure to WGP or BCG significantly enhanced tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) production, a hallmark of trained immunity,22 across multiple myeloid populations, determined by the frequency of TNF-α+ cells. While BCG significantly increased TNF-α levels relative to PBS controls, WGP induced an even greater response in CD11b+ myeloid cells, CD11b+Ly6C+ monocytes, and CD11b+F4/80+ macrophages, underscoring its superior capacity to induce trained immunity (figure 1E). WGP-trained mice exhibited no systemic inflammation or morphological changes in major organs, with no alterations in body weight, liver/spleen weight, serum markers of liver/kidney function, or peripheral immune cells (see online supplemental figure S1A–E). Given its comparable efficacy and favorable safety profile as a fungal-derived immunomodulator, we focused on WGP to investigate its impact on hepatic peripheral trained immunity.

To further investigate whether WGP induces trained immunity against HCC, mice received PBS or WGP before hydrodynamic tail vein injection (HTVi) of human NRAS and c-MYC oncogenes (Ras/Myc). Consistent antitumor efficacy and immune changes were confirmed in the HTVi-induced HCC model (see online supplemental figure S2A–F). WGP also decreased hepatic collagen deposition and downregulated pro-fibrogenic markers (α-Sma, Col4a1, Tgfβ1) (see online supplemental figure S2G,H). WGP restored liver function (alanine aminotransferase/aspartate aminotransferase, total bile acid, total bilirubin) and reduced circulating cholesterol and triglycerides (see online supplemental figure S2I). No elevation in T-cell cytokines implicated trained myeloid cells as key effectors (see online supplemental figure S2J).

In vivo tracing experiments revealed significant hepatic accumulation of 5-((4,6-dichlorotriazin-2-yl)amino)fluorescein (DTAF)-labeled WGP, suggesting local immune activity (figure 1F). On day 3, the arrival of DTAF-WGP coincided with an influx of CD11b+ myeloid cells, with evidence of phagocytosed WGP within a subset (figure 1G). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that WGP outperformed BCG in inducing trained immunity, promoting antitumor responses through myeloid cell reprogramming and liver recruitment.

WGP induces trained immunity in human CD14+ monocytes

To determine whether the trained immunity induced by WGP in mice translates to humans, we investigated its effects on human CD14+ monocytes using an in vitro training model (figure 1H). We demonstrated that WGP induced trained immunity in human CD14+ monocytes. This was evidenced by elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS), cytotoxicity against HCCLM3 HCC cells, and enhanced TNF-α secretion on restimulation with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) or supernatants from HCCLM3 cells versus supernatants from normal THLE-2 liver cells (figure 1I–K). This tumor-specific effect correlated with higher macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) levels in HCC supernatants, a known secondary stimulus of trained immunity (figure 1L).11 23 Furthermore, reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) demonstrated that both HCC supernatants and MIF acted as restimulation agents, triggering enhanced pro-inflammatory responses similar to those triggered by LPS (figure 1M). Together, WGP elicited trained immunity with an enhanced capacity for anti-HCC cytotoxicity in human monocytes.

WGP in vivo treatment alters myeloid composition in the mouse liver

We next validated the role of WGP in shaping the liver immune landscape in mice 7 days after administering WGP or PBS (figure 2A). This time point was chosen based on the peak of trained immunity following β-glucan exposure.8 WGP treatment expanded the myeloid compartment, increasing total CD45+ cells in the liver (figure 2B–D). Specifically, WGP elevated the percentages and absolute numbers of CD11b+ myeloid cells, Ly6C+ monocytes, Ly6G+ neutrophils, and F4/80+ macrophages (figure 2C,D). Other immune components remained largely unchanged by WGP treatment (see online supplemental figure S3A,B). These WGP-trained CD11b+ cells underwent metabolic and epigenetic reprogramming, marked by elevated expression of hexokinase 2, H3K27Ac, and H3K4me3 (figure 2E,F). Unlike WGP, 3 µm microparticles did not alter the liver immune microenvironment (see online supplemental figure S3C,D). WGP-induced liver trained immunity depended on CD11b+CCR2+ myeloid cells (figure 2G), consistent with prior findings in other organs.6 11

To comprehensively characterize hepatic myeloid cells, hepatic CD45+CD11b+ cells from PBS-treated or WGP-treated mice were isolated for RNA sequencing (RNA-seq). Analysis revealed 281 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), with 109 upregulated and 172 downregulated in response to WGP. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) indicated enhanced activity of pathways involved in TNF-α production, inflammatory response, and cytokine secretion (figure 2H). DEGs related to leukocyte migration, chemokine signaling, and acute inflammatory responses were also enriched (see online supplemental figure S3E,F). To assess whether WGP induced trained immunity in the liver, hepatic myeloid cells were restimulated ex vivo with LPS following WGP or PBS administration. Flow cytometry showed increased TNF-α production in hepatic myeloid cells and elevated major histocompatibility complex (MHC) II expression on hepatic macrophages from WGP-treated mice (figure 2I,J). RT-qPCR of sorted CD11b+ cells from WGP-treated mice confirmed upregulated Tnfα, Il6, and Nos2 alongside downregulated Il10 (figure 2K). ELISA further demonstrated elevated secretion of TNF-α and interleukin (IL)-6, confirming trained immunity in the hepatic CD11b+ population (figure 2L).

Using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis of liver CD45+ immune cells isolated from mice 7 days after a single WGP treatment and from PBS-treated controls, we identified nine clusters: B cells, monocytes, macrophages, T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, neutrophils, conventional dendritic cells (cDCs), stem cells, and plasma cells (figure 2M). Consistent with our experimental and bulk RNA-seq results, WGP-trained monocytes/macrophages exhibited heightened inflammatory, co-stimulatory, and M1-polarization features (figure 2N).

To further delineate hepatic macrophage responses, we used scRNA-seq to identify two distinct liver macrophage subsets based on signature gene expression: Kupffer cells (KCs) and monocyte-derived macrophages (MDMs) (see online supplemental figure S3G). After transcriptionally defining these subsets, we quantified them by flow cytometry and found that WGP reduced the proportion of Tim4+ KCs among total liver macrophages without altering their absolute number, while increasing the absolute number of Tim4⁻ MDMs (see online supplemental figure S3H,I). Both KCs and MDMs exhibited trained immunity, as evidenced by enhanced TNF-α production on ex vivo restimulation with LPS (see online supplemental figure S3J). Collectively, these data demonstrate that WGP induces a distinct trained immunity program in the liver, characterized by expansion and reprogramming of hepatic myeloid cells, thereby reshaping the macrophage compartment and modulating the immune landscape.

A dual activation paradigm underpins WGP-induced adaptive immunity-independent trained immunity in liver myeloid cells

Tumor-derived soluble mediators may act as secondary signals in trained immunity. HCC cells secrete high levels of MIF, which acts through the MIF/CD74 axis to mediate the repolarization of protumor macrophages to an antitumor phenotype.23 24 We next investigated whether mouse HCC signals elicit WGP-induced trained immunity in a manner analogous to human HCC (figure 1K–M). Peritoneal macrophages trained with WGP produced more TNF-α when rechallenged with HCC supernatants than with normal liver cell supernatants. In contrast, inert phagocytic stimuli did not enhance the TNF-α response on LPS rechallenge, confirming the specificity of WGP training (see online supplemental figure S4A,B).

ELISA confirmed elevated MIF in HCC supernatants compared with normal liver cells (see online supplemental figure S4C). Liver myeloid cells from WGP-treated mice produced significantly more TNF-α on restimulation with HCC supernatants or recombinant MIF. Therefore, tumor-secreted soluble factors, exemplified by MIF, reactivate trained myeloid cells in the liver microenvironment (see online supplemental figure S4D–G). Furthermore, blockade of MIF/CD74 signaling, achieved either by adding ISO-1 (MIF inhibitor) during restimulation or by in vivo pretreatment with milatuzumab (CD74-neutralizing antibody) prior to cell isolation, markedly attenuated TNF-α production by WGP-trained myeloid cells and macrophages restimulated with HCC supernatants. This demonstrates that the MIF/CD74 axis is required for the heightened TNF-α response that characterizes WGP-induced trained immunity on encountering tumor-derived soluble factors (see online supplemental figure S4H).

CellChat analysis revealed minimal interactions between liver monocytes/macrophages and other immune cells (see online supplemental figure S5A,B). Depletion of CD4+/CD8+ T cells, neutrophils, or NK cells during WGP conditioning did not impair trained immunity in liver myeloid cells (see online supplemental figure S5C–G). NSG mice (lacking mature T, B, and NK cells) recapitulated WGP-induced training immunity, showing increased myeloid cells and enhanced TNF-α expression (see online supplemental figure S5H–J). In addition, WGP suppressed tumor growth and remodeled the tumor microenvironment, thereby activating innate immune cells and increasing TNF-α in myeloid cells in immunodeficient NSG mice (see online supplemental figure S6A–D). Thus, these results indicate that WGP-induced trained immunity occurs through innate immune reprogramming in the liver, a process independent of adaptive immunity. This adaptive immunity-independent trained immunity is initiated by WGP and subsequently triggered by tumor-derived MIF in the liver.

WGP induces trained immunity in myeloid cells, potentiating their phagocytic and cytotoxic activity against HCC cells

To determine whether WGP-trained myeloid cells have enhanced intrinsic antitumor activity, we performed RNA-seq analysis and observed upregulation of the phagocytosis-related pathway in these cells (figure 3A). Hepatic CD45+ immune cells and myeloid cells from WGP-trained mice showed significantly enhanced phagocytosis of green fluorescent protein (GFP)-labeled tumor cells (figure 3B,C). In vivo-trained myeloid cells also exhibited increased phagocytosis of fluorescent microspheres (figure 3B and D). Notably, although phagocytic uptake was quantified as the percentage of CD11b+ cells that acquired green fluorescence, we also observed a distinct CD11b⁻ population exhibiting this signal. These CD11b⁻ events were excluded from the phagocytosis readout. Immunophenotyping revealed that they comprised T cells (CD3+), B cells (CD19+), NK cells (NK1.1+), and dendritic cells (DCs) (CD11c+) (see online supplemental figure S6E–H).

Beyond phagocytic uptake, we next assessed whether WGP-trained myeloid cells exhibited enhanced cytotoxicity against Hep53.4 cells. RNA-seq revealed significant enrichment of DEGs linked to ROS metabolic process, and flow cytometry confirmed upregulated ROS production in WGP-trained myeloid cells (figure 3E,F). Co-culture of WGP-trained CD11b+ cells with Hep53.4 cells resulted in significantly increased cytotoxicity. This effect was abolished by the ROS inhibitor N-acetylcysteine, the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant, the ROS scavenger Trolox, and the ferroptosis inhibitor deferoxamine (figure 3G), indicating that ROS-induced ferroptosis is a key mechanism.25 Collectively, these data demonstrate that WGP enhances the antitumor capacity of myeloid cells by augmenting both phagocytosis and cytotoxicity.

Preparation and characterization of PPZ-A10 lipid nanoparticles for monocyte/macrophage-preferential nucleic acid delivery

Next, we developed a lipid nanoparticle (LNP) system encapsulating nucleic acid drugs. LNPs incorporating the ionizable lipid PPZ-A10 demonstrate selective tropism for hepatic macrophages, overcoming systemic LNP hepatotropism.26 PPZ-A10 was synthesized and its structure was confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance (see online supplemental figure S7A). PPZ-A10 LNPs exhibited an average diameter of 93.79 nm and encapsulation efficiency of 89.09±1.01% (see online supplemental figure S7B–D). In RAW264.7 and THP-1-derived macrophages, PPZ-A10 LNPs efficiently delivered DiD dye and Cy3-labeled RNA, confirming cellular uptake (see online supplemental figure S7E). Furthermore, the in vivo distribution of DiD-labeled LNPs following tail vein injection was assessed (figure 3H). PPZ-A10 LNPs accumulated primarily in the liver. Within the liver, they localized predominantly to CD11b+ myeloid cells (figure 3I–K). Intravenously administered LNPs showed a favorable biosafety profile (see online supplemental figure S7F–H). Therefore, PPZ-A10 LNPs were used to investigate the mechanisms underlying WGP-induced trained immunity in hepatic monocytes/macrophages.

APOE is an essential mediator that underpins WGP-induced trained immunity in antitumor myeloid cells

Given that WGP induces trained immunity through metabolic and epigenetic changes, we examined lipid alterations in CD11b+ myeloid cells. RNA-seq analysis revealed upregulation of the lipid transport pathway (figure 4A). In parallel, direct BODIPY staining confirmed a marked reduction in lipid accumulation in WGP-trained myeloid cells (figure 4B). Apoe exhibited the most pronounced differential expression among the genes in the lipid transport pathway (figure 4A). APOE, a key lipid transporter, enhances macrophage phagocytic control of Legionella pneumophila.15 21 We therefore investigated whether WGP-induced trained immunity depends on APOE-mediated lipid alterations in CD11b+ cells.

In our mouse scRNA-seq data, Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) visualization showed that Apoe expression was highly enriched in macrophages (85.1%) and monocytes (52.6%), and violin plot analysis confirmed that macrophages and monocytes were the predominant Apoe-expressing immune populations (figures 2M and 4C). The prominence of monocytes/macrophages as the main Apoe-expressing immune compartment aligns with reports from human HCC studies (figure 4D,E).27 28 APOE was associated with a favorable prognosis (figure 4F). Subdividing monocytes/macrophages into Apoe+ and Apoe– groups in our mouse data revealed that APOE correlated with pro-inflammatory phenotypes in TISIDB human HCC immune subtypes and mouse scRNA-seq data (figure 4G–I). In mouse Apoe+ monocytes/macrophages, GSEA showed upregulation of phagocytosis, response to ROS, monocyte chemotaxis, and inflammatory/innate immune response (figure 4J).

To validate the relationship between WGP and APOE, we engineered PPZ-A10 LNPs loaded with APOE-targeting siRNA (siAPOE-LNPs) or APOE plasmid DNA (APOE pDNA-LNPs). In sorted mouse CD11b+ cells, WGP upregulated APOE expression, which was reversed by siAPOE-LNPs and enhanced by APOE pDNA-LNPs (figure 4K). Consistent with its established role in β-glucan-induced trained immunity,29 Dectin-1 blockade inhibited WGP-induced APOE upregulation (figure 4L). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that APOE deficiency suppressed WGP-mediated trained immunity, as indicated by reduced Tnfα and increased Il10 messenger RNA (mRNA) levels. These transcriptional changes were further corroborated at the protein level by ELISA and flow cytometry, which consistently showed decreased TNF-α and elevated IL-10. In contrast, APOE pDNA-LNPs amplified WGP-mediated trained immunity, as evidenced by increased Tnfα and decreased Il10 mRNA, along with corresponding protein changes (figure 4M–O). These findings provide preliminary evidence that APOE is essential for WGP-induced trained immunity in myeloid cells.

APOE+ monocytes/macrophages serve as the primary mediators of WGP-induced trained immunity through modulation of lipid accumulation and ER stress

Next, we explored the functional relevance of WGP-induced hepatic APOE alterations in trained immunity. RNA-seq pathway analysis suggested that WGP suppressed transcriptional programs associated with lipid accumulation and ER stress (figures 4A and 5A), and BODIPY staining confirmed reduced lipid accumulation in WGP-trained myeloid cells (figure 4B). Since PERK-mediated ER stress enables monocyte/macrophage immunosuppression, we hypothesized that WGP-elicited, APOE-mediated trained immunity relies on attenuated lipid accumulation and ER stress, similar to its roles in CD8+ T cells and HCC macrophages.30–34 In patients with HCC, APOE negatively correlated with ER stress marker ATF4 (figure 5B). RT-qPCR confirmed WGP reduced PERK-mediated ER stress in myeloid cells, an effect blocked by siAPOE-LNPs and enhanced by APOE pDNA-LNPs (figure 5C,D). Using an in vitro trained bone marrow-derived macrophage (BMDM) model, we found that although the fatty acid uptake inhibitor sulfosuccinimidyl oleate (SSO) rescued both the lipid accumulation and ER stress induced by siAPOE-LNPs under WGP regulation, GSK2656157 (PERK inhibitor) specifically rescued only the ER stress without affecting lipid accumulation (see online supplemental figure S7I–K). Furthermore, treatment with SSO and GSK2656157 rescued the impairment caused by APOE deficiency in the WGP-enhanced cytotoxicity of BMDMs against tumor cells (see online supplemental figure S7L).

To determine the role of APOE in WGP-induced trained immunity, Apoe-knockout (Apoe−/−) mice were injected with WGP prior to liver collection (figure 5E). WGP reduced lipid accumulation and ER stress in myeloid cells of wild-type (WT) mice, but this effect was abrogated in Apoe−/− mice (figure 5F,G). TNF-α production assays and cytotoxicity experiments confirmed the absence of trained immunity in Apoe−/− mice (figure 5H,I).

To further investigate the mechanism of lipid accumulation and ER stress, WT mice treated with PBS or WGP in vivo were further treated with siAPOE-LNPs, GSK2656157, or TUDCA (tauroursodeoxycholate, an ER stress-alleviating molecular chaperone). Both GSK2656157 and TUDCA counteracted the effects of siAPOE-LNPs, restoring WGP-enhanced cytotoxicity against Luc+ Hep53.4 cells. Additionally, APOE pDNA-LNPs further increased cytotoxicity (see online supplemental figure S8A,B). Similarly, siAPOE-LNPs abolished WGP-induced phagocytosis of microspheres and GFP+ tumor cells by CD45+ and CD11b+ cells, which was reversed by GSK2656157 and TUDCA. Conversely, APOE pDNA-LNPs enhanced phagocytosis (see online supplemental figure S8A,C).

Furthermore, although siAPOE-LNPs increased lipid accumulation and ER stress, the siAPOE-LNP-induced ER stress was specifically reduced by GSK2656157 and TUDCA. In contrast, APOE pDNA-LNPs enhanced the reduction in lipid accumulation and ER stress conferred by WGP administration (see online supplemental figure S8D,E). Analysis of TNF-α production in myeloid cells and macrophages revealed that siAPOE-LNPs reduced WGP-induced trained immunity, an effect reversed by GSK2656157 and TUDCA, while APOE pDNA-LNPs enhanced it (see online supplemental figure S8F). Taken together, APOE activation critically drives WGP-induced trained immunity by reducing lipid accumulation and ER stress in monocytes/macrophages.

siAPOE-LNPs suppress the antitumor function of WGP-trained monocytes/macrophages in mouse HCC models

Since WGP failed to induce trained immunity in APOE-deficient monocytes/macrophages, we hypothesized these cells would lack WGP-mediated antitumor activity in vivo. Systemically administered PPZ-A10 LNPs accumulated selectively in liver tumors (figure 5J,K) and localized to CD11b+ myeloid cells, especially monocytes/macrophages (figure 5L,M).

Using this platform, we next validated the WGP-APOE axis in tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells. In an orthotopic Hep53.4 HCC model, siAPOE-LNPs abolished WGP’s protection, increasing tumor burden and reducing survival, which were reversed by GSK2656157 and TUDCA (figure 6A–D). While APOE knockdown induced lipid accumulation and ER stress in myeloid cells, pharmacological intervention with GSK2656157 or TUDCA reversed the ER stress phenotype (figure 6E,F). GSK2656157 and TUDCA also blocked siAPOE-LNP-induced suppression of TNF-α production in WGP-trained myeloid cells and macrophages in HCC (figure 6G). Similar responses were observed in an HTVi-induced HCC model (see online supplemental figure S9A–G). Collectively, APOE links the inhibition of lipid accumulation, the mitigation of ER stress, and the induction of trained immunity in liver myeloid cells, suggesting potential therapeutic strategies against HCC progression.

Combination therapy with WGP and APOE pDNA-LNPs elicits synergistic antitumor immunity in mouse HCC models

To investigate the therapeutic potential of APOE, we examined its effect in mouse HCC models using APOE pDNA-LNPs to overexpress APOE in monocytes/macrophages (figure 6H; see online supplemental figure S9H). In both the orthotopic Hep53.4 and HTVi-induced HCC models, the combination of WGP and APOE pDNA-LNPs synergistically inhibited tumor growth and prolonged survival (figure 6I–K; see online supplemental figure S9I–K).

Furthermore, this combination reduced lipid accumulation and alleviated ER stress while simultaneously increasing TNF-α production in myeloid cells (figure 6L–N; see online supplemental figure S9L–N). Thus, these findings establish the WGP-APOE axis as a promising strategy to augment trained immunity in HCC.

Translational potential of WGP-induced trained immunity in HCC

To determine the translational significance of WGP-induced trained immunity, we examined human CD14+ monocytes. WGP-trained monocytes showed enhanced phagocytic activity and increased TNF-α secretion, potentially enhancing antitumor functions in vivo (figure 7A and B). In NSG mice with HTVi-induced HCC, WGP-trained CD14+ monocytes reduced tumor burden compared with controls (figure 7C). We then evaluated a more clinically relevant therapeutic strategy by administering WGP therapeutically after tumor establishment (figure 7D). WGP treatment suppressed tumor progression and extended survival (figure 7E,F), promoting recruitment of trained myeloid cells and macrophages into tumors (figure 7G). Adoptive transfer of WGP-trained BMDMs also reduced liver tumor burden and improved survival (figure 7H). Together, these findings highlight WGP-induced trained immunity as a promising therapeutic strategy for HCC, supporting further translational research.

WGP synergizes with anti-PD-L1 therapy to augment antitumor immunity

We next assessed the transcript levels of key IFN-γ signaling genes, which serve as a clinically validated predictive biomarker for programmed cell death protein 1/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) blockade response.35 36 WGP treatment significantly increased their expression (figure 7I). Therefore, although APOE+ monocytes/macrophages are primary antitumor effectors, we hypothesized that WGP-induced trained immunity remodels the tumor microenvironment to sensitize HCC to checkpoint inhibition. This hypothesis is supported by the upregulation of MHC II+ macrophages in WGP-trained mice (figure 2J) and elevated PD-L1 on myeloid cells in HCC models, indicating that WGP could enhance the efficacy of anti-PD-L1 therapy (figure 7J).

To evaluate combinatorial strategy, orthotopic HCC-bearing mice received WGP monotherapy, anti-PD-L1 monotherapy, or their combination (figure 7K). While each monotherapy significantly inhibited tumor growth, the combination showed superior efficacy (figure 7L). In the orthotopic Hep53.4 model, the combination increased TNF-α+ proportions in myeloid cells (figure 7M). A similar combinatorial strategy-mediated antitumor effect was observed in the HTVi-induced HCC model (see online supplemental figure S10A–C). Flow cytometry revealed that combination therapy enhanced tumor infiltration of TNF-α+ and IFN-γ+ CD8+ and CD4+ T cells (figure 7N; see online supplemental figure S10D). Collectively, these results highlight the synergistic therapeutic potential of WGP with anti-PD-L1 in HCC.

Discussion

Myeloid cells trained with WGP, BCG, or nanobiologics exhibit antitumor effects in multiple malignancies.6 11 13 37 However, the role of trained immunity in HCC remains largely uncharacterized, and the mechanisms by which innate immune cells acquire a trained phenotype on tumor exposure are not fully elucidated. Here, we demonstrate that WGP outperforms BCG in eliciting trained immunity against HCC. Additionally, WGP induces tissue-specific antitumor trained immunity in hepatic APOE+ myeloid cells. Functionally, these cells infiltrate tumors and directly eliminate tumor cells via enhanced phagocytic and cytotoxic activity. These findings support the concept that trained immunity represents a facet of immunosurveillance mechanisms that counteract tumor progression.

A single dose of WGP resulted in a markedly reduced tumor burden and prolonged survival in HCC models. These findings suggest that the antitumor activity of WGP involves the recruitment of antitumor myeloid cells to the liver and the elicitation of trained immunity in monocytes/macrophages. Thus, WGP-induced trained immunity may represent a viable therapeutic alternative for patients with HCC.

WGP-induced trained immunity likely arises from the distinct contributions of liver macrophage subsets. Liver macrophages comprise embryo-derived KCs and MDMs, and the MDM population includes liver capsule macrophages; these subsets differ in origin, location, and function.38 39 Specifically, WGP training enhances the pro-inflammatory potential of both KCs and MDMs, as shown by increased TNF-α production, and preferentially expands MDMs without markedly altering KC numbers, likely via local proliferation and monocyte recruitment (see online supplemental figure S3H–J).6 Given this selective expansion and the antitumor roles reported for MDMs,6 11 MDMs may be the dominant effectors in the liver. Importantly, KCs also acquire a trained phenotype and, owing to their self-renewal and epigenetic reprogramming potential,38 39 could sustain durable trained immunity and cooperate with MDMs to amplify antitumor responses, though direct evidence in tumor models remains to be established. Liver capsule macrophages, situated at the liver surface, encounter high levels of β-glucan after intraperitoneal injection, potentially enhancing antitumor functions against peritoneal metastases.38 39 Future studies employing lineage tracing, selective depletion, and single-cell multi-omics will clarify these subset-specific contributions and inform macrophage-targeted therapies for HCC and peritoneal metastases.

Beyond pro-inflammatory cytokine production, we demonstrated that WGP enhanced cell-intrinsic antitumor activity, including phagocytosis and cytotoxicity. Notably, phagocytosis assays detected green fluorescence from CD11b⁻ cells, identified as T cells, B cells, NK cells, and DCs. T cells, B cells, and NK cells are not professional phagocytes, but DCs are professional phagocytes capable of direct phagocytosis40; thus, the collective signal likely reflects multiple distinct uptake pathways. All four subsets can perform trogocytosis41–43; T cells and B cells, via constitutively expressed T-cell receptor and B-cell receptor, may mediate rapid receptor-triggered internalization or membrane transfer,44 45 while NK cells may contribute through endocytosis.46 This combined signal, consistently detected across both phagocytosis assays, likely represents the intrinsic capacity of the lymphoid and DC compartments to interact with and take up target cells and microspheres. While our flow cytometry-based assay cannot formally distinguish internalization from tight surface binding, future receptor blockade, reconstitution, and imaging-based validation are warranted to dissect each mechanism’s contribution and confirm subcellular localization.

Hepatic WGP trafficking remodels the liver’s immune landscape and directly increases pro-inflammatory monocytes/macrophages. Trained immunity induction primarily involves metabolic and epigenetic reprogramming.4 We observed elevated APOE+ myeloid cells in WGP-trained normal livers. APOE, which is highly expressed in myeloid cells across cancers, is a promising therapeutic target, though its role varies by cancer type.17–19 47 In HCC, APOE exhibited protumor functions in subcutaneous Hepa1-6 and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)-driven HCC models.48 49 However, the subcutaneous model has high immunogenicity, which limits its relevance to orthotopic and human HCC,50 and macrophage-specific function of APOE in MASH-HCC remains unclear. In contrast, liver APOE+ monocytes/macrophages in WGP-trained WT livers and orthotopic HCC models displayed features of trained immunity. Consistently, APOE+ macrophages in human HCC showed enhanced expression of phagocytosis-related genes and pro-inflammatory markers such as CD86 and CXCL9.48 Thus, in β-glucan-treated HCC models, APOE+ monocytes/macrophages exhibit functions distinct from classical M1/M2 paradigms, aligning with studies that highlight the context-dependent role of stromal components in either promoting or suppressing tumors.51–54

Next, we employed LNPs formulated with the ionizable lipid PPZ-A10, which enables selective monocyte/macrophage targeting, to deliver APOE pDNA or siAPOE to hepatic monocytes/macrophages. After systemic administration, these LNPs bind to specific plasma proteins that act as targeting ligands.26 By engaging receptors highly expressed on liver macrophages, this mechanism preferentially delivers nucleic acids to generate engineered monocytes/macrophages overexpressing or knocking down APOE. This in situ engineering strategy bypasses the need for ex vivo manufacturing and cell reinfusion.

Previous studies have established that lipid accumulation-mediated ER stress promotes a protumoral phenotype in myeloid cells.31 34 Here, we demonstrate that WGP-trained APOE+ monocytes/macrophages reduce lipid accumulation and ER stress, modulate the liver microenvironment, and enhance phagocytosis and ROS-mediated cytotoxicity against HCC cells, thereby suppressing tumor growth. Although myeloid reprogramming via siAPOE-LNPs promotes HCC progression, this effect is reversed by GSK2656157 and TUDCA. These findings suggest therapeutic strategies such as WGP or APOE pDNA-LNPs to enhance trained immunity in HCC, yet further preclinical and clinical evaluation is needed for APOE-targeted approaches involving specific lipid transport and the ER stress pathways.

A recent study demonstrated that β-glucan-induced neutrophil-dependent antitumor immunity suppresses bladder cancer.13 In contrast, we found that neutrophil depletion did not impair WGP-induced trained immunity. This discrepancy may arise from differences in the tumor models and the physical form and origin of β-glucan. Unlike soluble forms, particulate WGP promotes receptor clustering and phagocytic synapse formation, potentially underlying its unique immunostimulatory properties.55 Given the high expression of PD-L1 on HCC myeloid cells and efficacy of β-glucan-conjugated anti-PD-L1,56 we demonstrated that the combination of WGP and anti-PD-L1 therapy synergistically inhibited tumor progression more effectively than WGP alone. However, whether the combination of WGP with BCG or with PD-L1 blockade exerts a synergistic effect in the treatment of HCC remains to be elucidated.

Although WGP-trained myeloid cells exert antitumor effects via pro-inflammatory cytokines, inflammation is a double-edged sword in hepatocarcinogenesis. In chronic liver diseases, such as MASH, alcoholic steatohepatitis, and viral hepatitis, persistent pro-inflammatory cytokine-driven liver injury promotes HCC.57 Under these conditions, WGP-trained myeloid cells may exacerbate hepatocarcinogenesis. Mechanistically, chronic TNF-α and IL-6 release activates nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), driving inflammation and tumor proliferation. This inflammatory milieu generates excessive ROS, which in turn reactivates NF-κB and STAT3, fueling a vicious cycle that exacerbates liver injury and HCC progression.58 However, our orthotopic models lack this inflammatory milieu and therefore do not recapitulate the chronic inflammation typical of human HCC.57 Safe use should be defined in etiology-specific HCC models; trained immunity may clear tumors without severe liver injury, but may fuel hepatocarcinogenesis in the presence of active steatohepatitis or viral hepatitis. Pretreatment stratification by inflammatory status and liver disease etiology is critical for safe translation of WGP-based trained-immunity therapies.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that WGP-induced trained immunity, mediated by APOE+ myeloid cells, exerts potent antitumor effects in HCC. The mechanism involves reduced lipid accumulation and alleviation of ER stress in monocytes/macrophages, which enhances their phagocytosis and cytotoxicity and reinforces the innate immune response on re-exposure to tumor-derived signals. The favorable safety profile of WGP further supports its role as a promising repurposed immunotherapeutic strategy for HCC, underscoring trained immunity as a novel translational therapeutic avenue.

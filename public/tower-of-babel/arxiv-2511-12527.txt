Isoparametric Hypersurfaces in Products of Simply Connected Space Forms
Ronaldo F. de Lima and Giuseppe Pipoli

Abstract
. Let ℚ ϵ i n i \mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}} denote the simply connected space form of dimension n i ≥ 2 n_{i}\geq 2 and constant sectional curvature ϵ i \epsilon_{i} . We prove that any connected isoparametric hypersurface of ℚ ϵ 1 n 1 × ℚ ϵ 2 n 2 \mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}} has constant angle function. We then use this property to classify the isoparametric and homogeneous hypersurfaces of ℚ ϵ 1 n 1 × ℚ ϵ 2 n 2 \mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}} , | ϵ 1 | + | ϵ 2 | ≠ 0 |\epsilon_{1}|+|\epsilon_{2}|\neq 0 , satisfying a one-point condition. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 53A10 (primary), 53B25, 53C30 (secondary). Key words and phrases: isoparametric hypersurfaces – homogeneous hypersurfaces – product space.



1.
Introduction

The isoparametric hypersurfaces of a Riemannian manifold
M^{n}

are those whose local nearby parallels, together with the hypersurface itself,
have constant mean curvature. They appear as level sets of smooth functions

F
on
M^{n}
, also called isoparametric, whose gradient
\nabla
and Laplacian

\Delta
satisfy

\|\nabla F\|=\Phi\circ F,\qquad\Delta F=\Psi\circ F,

where
\Phi
and
\Psi
are smooth real functions.

Classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces is a major topic in
submanifold theory. On this matter, the first results
were obtained in the late 1930’s in the works of Levi-Civita
[
18
]
, who
classified the isoparametric surfaces of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
Segre
[
20
]
,
who extended Levi-Civita’s result to the Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and Cartan
[
2
,
4
]
, who classified the isoparametric hypersurfaces
of the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

In
[
3
,
5
]
, Cartan obtained many results on isoparametric hypersurfaces
of the spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, including the classification of those having at most three (constant)
principal curvatures.
In fact, the problem of classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces
of the spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is rather involved, and just recently its complete solution was announced;
see
[
6
,
7
]
. Nevertheless, due to controversial results of Siffert
[
21
,
22
]
,
there is no general agreement that this classification is
complete for the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{13}
.

The homogeneous hypersurfaces of a Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
— i.e.,
the codimension-one orbits of actions of Lie subgroups of
{\rm Iso}(M)
—
are all isoparametric with constant principal curvatures.
The converse, however, does not hold in general. As a matter of fact, one of the most
relevant problems in this theory
is to determine when isoparametric hypersurfaces
are necessarily homogeneous or
of constant principal curvatures; cf.
[
10
]
.

In his aforementioned works, Cartan showed that
being isoparametric and having constant principal curvatures are equivalent conditions
when the ambient manifold
M^{n}
is a simply connected space form.
Moreover, both these
conditions are equivalent to homogeneity
when
M^{n}
is either
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. On the other hand, there are
spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n}
which contain nonhomogeneous isoparametric hypersurfaces
known as the FKM examples; see
[
6
,
12
]
. The classification of
homogeneous hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is due to
Hsiang and Lawson
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we consider isoparametric hypersurfaces of products

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
denotes the simply connected
space form of dimension
n_{i}\geq 2
and of
constant sectional curvature
\epsilon_{i}
. We shall assume that
such products are non-Euclidean spaces, that is,
\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\epsilon_{2}^{2}\neq 0
.

On every hypersurface
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
, there is a natural
angle function

\varTheta

whose properties are similar to that of the angle function of
hypersurfaces of cylinders
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
; see Sect.
3
.
For instance, hypersurfaces with constant angle
\varTheta=\pm 1
are of either type

\varSigma_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\varSigma_{2}^{n_{2}-1}
,
where
\varSigma_{i}^{n_{i}-1}
is a hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
; see Prop.
12
.
In this context, we have proved in
[
9
]
that every isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

has constant angle. Our first result here establishes that this
is also true for the products
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
.

Theorem 1
.

Every connected isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
,
\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\epsilon_{2}^{2}\neq 0
,
has constant angle function.

The proof of Theorem
1

follows the same lines as the one we give to
[
9
, Prop. 7]
:
by applying Jacobi field theory, we reduce it
to the resolution of an intricate algebraic
problem; see Sect.
5
.
Notice that the condition
\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\epsilon_{2}^{2}\neq 0
is needed in that theorem, since
round spheres in Euclidean space are isoparametric with non-constant angle.

We use Theorem
1

to classify the hypersurfaces
\varSigma

of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
having a
distinguished point

\mathfrak{p}
.
This means that either
\varTheta^{2}(\mathfrak{p})=1
or the principal directions
of
\varSigma
at
\mathfrak{p}

split properly
, i.e.,
n_{i}-1
of them are tangent
to
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
,
i=1,2
; see Def.
8
,
9
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varSigma
be a connected oriented hypersurface of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}

with a distinguished point.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\varSigma
is isoparametric.

(ii)

\varSigma
is an open set of one of the following complete hypersurfaces:

(a)

\varSigma_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
,
where
\varSigma_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}
is complete and isoparametric;

(b)

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\varSigma_{2}^{n_{2}-1}
,
where
\varSigma_{2}^{n_{2}-1}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
is complete and isoparametric;

(c)

a flat-horospherical hypersurface;

(d)

a bi-horospherical hypersurface.

Moreover, if
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\leq 0
, the condition

(iii)

\varSigma
is an open set of a homogeneous hypersurface

is equivalent to
(i)
and
(ii)
. In the case one of the factors is spherical,

i.e., some
\epsilon_{i}>0
, (iii) is equivalent to

(iv)

\varSigma
is an open set of either a hypersurface of type (a)
or (b) with
\varSigma_{i}^{n_{i}-1}
a homogeneous hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
.

Each of the hypersurfaces of itens (ii)-(c) and (ii)-(d) in
Theorem
2

is built on two one-parameter families
f_{s}^{i}

of parallel hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
,
\epsilon_{i}\leq 0
,
being these families connected to each other
by a linear function
\phi(s)=as
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the case
\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}=0
, one of these families is made of parallel hyperplanes of
the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
, whereas the other is made of parallel horospheres of the
hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n_{j}}_{\epsilon_{j}}
of constant curvature
\epsilon_{j}<0
.
The resulting hypersurface is then called
flat-horospherical
(Fig.
1
).
In the case
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}<0
, both parallel families are of horospheres, and the
hypersurface is called
bi-horospherical
.

Figure 1
.
Coordinates on a flat-horospherical hypersurface

\varSigma=\Phi(M_{1}\times M_{2}\times\mathbb{R})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}_{\epsilon_{2}}
,

\Phi(p_{1},p_{2},s):=(f_{s}^{1}(p_{1}),f_{as}^{2}(p_{2}))
, where
a>0
and
\{f_{s}^{1}\}
(resp.
\{f_{as}^{2}\}
)
is a parallel family of hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
(resp. horospheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}_{\epsilon_{2}}
);
see Ex.
11
, Sect.
3
.

We note that there exist
one-parameter families of homogeneous hypersurfaces
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}
and in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}

with no distinguished points; cf. Ex.
13
,
14
. This shows
that the existence of a distinguished point is a necessary hypothesis in
Theorem
2
.

The isoparametric (resp. homogeneous) hypersurfaces of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{2}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{2}
,
\epsilon_{1}\neq\epsilon_{2}
,
have been classified in
[
14
]
(resp.
[
19
]
), and
the isoparametric and homogeneous hypersurfaces of

\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
) have been classified
in
[
13
]
(resp.
[
25
]
). In all of these works, it is verified
that isoparametric/homogeneous hypersurfaces
have constant angle function.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we set some notation
and discuss a few geometric aspects of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
. In Section
3
, we establish
results on constant angle hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
that will be used in the proof of
Theorems
1
and
2
, to be presented
in Section
4
. In the final Section
5
, we prove
the algebraic results which are used in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Added in proof
. After a preliminary version of this paper had been completed, we became aware of the work of Tan,
Xie, and Yan
[
23
]
, whose results overlap with ours in the case where one of the factors is Euclidean.
We note, however, that their classification results, in contrast with ours, require the hypersurfaces to be complete.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Some geometric aspects of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}

Given integers
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 2
and
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we shall
consider the product space
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
endowed with its standard product metric,
which will be denoted by
\langle\,,\,\rangle
.

The tangent bundle
\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
splits as

\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})=\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}),

where
\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}})
denotes the tangent bundle of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
.
Considering this fact, for a given

X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})
,
we shall write

X=X_{1}+X_{2},\,\,\,X_{1}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\{0\},\,\,\,X_{2}\in\{0\}\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}).

The curvature tensor of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
will be denoted by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, so that

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X,Y)Z=R_{\epsilon_{1}}(X_{1},Y_{1})Z_{1}+R_{\epsilon_{2}}(X_{2},Y_{2})Z_{2},

where
R_{\epsilon_{i}}
is the curvature tensor of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
, that is,

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\epsilon_{i}}(X_{i},Y_{i})Z_{i}=\epsilon_{i}(\langle X_{i},Z_{i}\rangle Y_{i}-\langle Y_{i},Z_{i}\rangle X_{i}).

The operator
P
on
\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})
defined by

P(X_{1}+X_{2})=X_{1}-X_{2}

will play a fundamental role here. It is easily checked that
P
is parallel, that is,

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}PY=P\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}Y\,\,\,\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}),

where
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
.

2.2.
Hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}

Let
\varSigma\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
be an oriented hypersurface with unit normal

N=N_{1}+N_{2}
and shape operator
A
with respect to
N
, that is,

AX=-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,\,\,\,X\in\mathfrak{X}(\varSigma),

where
\mathfrak{X}(\varSigma)
is the tangent bundle of
\varSigma
.

The principal curvatures of
\varSigma,
that is, the eigenvalues of the shape operator

A,
will be denoted by
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}
. In this setting,
we define the non-normalized
mean curvature

H
of
\varSigma

as the sum of its principal curvatures:

H=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}.

We shall denote by
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
\varSigma
. The gradient
of a smooth function
f
on
\varSigma
will be denoted by
\nabla f
.

Definition 3
.

With the above notation, the
angle function

\varTheta
of
\varSigma
is defined by

\varTheta=\langle PN,N\rangle=\|N_{1}\|^{2}-\|N_{2}\|^{2}.

The tangential component of
PN
will be denoted by
T
, so that

T=PN-\varTheta N=(1-\varTheta)N_{1}-(1+\varTheta)N_{2}.

The function
\varTheta
satisfies

(1)

\varTheta=2\|N_{1}\|^{2}-1=-2\|N_{2}\|^{2}+1,

from which we conclude that:

•

\varTheta
is constant if and only if
\|N_{1}\|
and
\|N_{2}\|
are both constant;

•

either
\|N_{1}\|
or
\|N_{2}\|
vanishes on
\varSigma
if and only if
\varTheta=\pm 1.

The following lemma follows from straightforward computations
(see
[
13
,
25
]
).

Lemma 4
.

The following equalities hold:

•

\nabla\varTheta=-2AT
;

•

\nabla_{X}T=\varTheta AX-PAX+\langle PAX,N\rangle N
for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\varSigma)
.

In particular, if
T
never vanishes and
\varTheta
is constant on
\varSigma
, one has:

•

T
is a principal direction with principal curvature
0
;

•

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{T}T=\nabla_{T}T=0
, that is, the trajectories of
T
are geodesics in both

\varSigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
.

To conclude this section, we recall the Codazzi equation
for hypersurfaces, which in our context reads as

(2)

(\nabla A_{X})Y-(\nabla A_{Y})X=(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X,Y)N)^{\top}\,\,\,\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\varSigma),

where
(\nabla A_{X})Y=\nabla A_{X}Y-A\nabla_{X}Y
, and

Z^{\top}=Z-\langle Z,N\rangle N,\,\,\,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{2}}).

3.
Hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
with constant angle

In this section, we establish some results on
constant angle hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
that will be used in the proofs
of our main theorems.

Let us first consider hypersurfaces of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
which are defined as immersions
\Phi
of the following type:

(3)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi\colon&M_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\times M_{2}^{n_{2}-1}\times I&\rightarrow&\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}\\
&(p_{1},p_{2},s)&\mapsto&(\exp_{p_{1}}(s\eta_{p_{1}}),\exp_{p_{2}}(\phi(s)\eta_{p_{2}})),\end{array}

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval,
\phi\colon I\to\phi(I)
is a diffeomorphism,

M_{i}^{n_{i}-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
is a hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
, and

\eta_{p_{i}}
is the unit normal to
M_{i}^{n_{i}-1}
at
p_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Defining
f_{s}^{i}:M_{i}^{n_{i}-1}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
by

(4)

f_{s}^{1}(p_{1})=\gamma_{p_{1}}(s):=\exp_{p_{1}}(s\eta_{p_{1}}),\quad f_{s}^{2}(p_{2})=\gamma_{p_{2}}(\phi(s))=:\exp_{p_{2}}(\phi(s)\eta_{p_{2}}),

we have that
\{f_{s}^{i}\,;\,s\in I\}
is a family
of parallel hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{i}}^{n_{i}}
.

Definition 5
.

We shall call
\varSigma:=\Phi(M_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\times M_{2}^{n_{2}-1}\times I)\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}

as above an
(f_{s}^{1},f_{s}^{2},\phi)
-
hypersurface
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
.

Straightforward calculations give that, at
p=(p_{1},p_{2},s)\in M_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\times M_{2}^{n_{2}-1}\times I,

the unit normal
N
to the
(f_{s}^{1},f_{s}^{2},\phi)
-hypersurface
\varSigma
is

(5)

N=\frac{1}{W(s)}\left(-\phi^{\prime}(s)\gamma_{p_{1}}^{\prime}(s)+\gamma_{p_{2}}^{\prime}(s)\right),\,\,\,W(s)=\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime}(s))^{2}},

and that its
T
-field is

T=\partial_{s}=\frac{1}{W(s)}\left(\gamma_{p_{1}}^{\prime}(s)+\phi^{\prime}(s)\gamma_{p_{2}}^{\prime}(s)\right).

Set
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Denoting by

X^{1},\dots,X^{n_{1}-1}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}),\quad Y^{1},\dots,Y^{n_{2}-1}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})

the unit principal directions of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively,
it is easily checked that the unit principal directions

Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n-1}
of
\varSigma
are:

\displaystyle Z^{1\phantom{+i}}

\displaystyle=T/\|T\|;

(6)

\displaystyle Z^{1+i}

\displaystyle=X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\{0\}\,\,\,\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n_{1}-1\};

\displaystyle Z^{n_{1}+i}

\displaystyle=Y_{i}\in\{0\}\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})\,\,\,\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n_{2}-1\}.

Moreover, denoting the
i
-th principal curvature function of

f_{s}^{1}
(resp.
f_{s}^{2}
) by
\lambda_{i}^{s}
(resp.
\mu_{i}^{s}
),
the principal curvatures of
\varSigma

are the functions

(7)

k_{1}=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{W^{3}},\quad k_{1+i_{1}}=\frac{-\phi^{\prime}}{W}\lambda_{i_{1}}^{s},\quad k_{n_{1}+i_{2}}=\frac{1}{W}\mu_{i_{2}}^{s},

where
1\leq i_{1}\leq n_{1}-1
and
1\leq i_{2}\leq n_{2}-1
. In particular,
the mean curvature of
\varSigma
at
p=(p_{1},p_{2},s)
is given by

(8)

H_{\varSigma}=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)}{W^{3}(s)}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(s)}{W(s)}H_{1}^{s}(p_{1})+\frac{1}{W(s)}H_{2}^{s}(p_{2}),

where
H_{i}^{s}
is the mean curvature of
f_{i}^{s}
.

Remark 6
.

It follows from (
5
) that the angle function
\varTheta
of
an
(f_{1}^{s},f_{2}^{s},\phi)
-hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
is constant if and only if

\phi^{\prime}
is constant. If so,
\varTheta^{2}\neq 1
. It should also be noticed that
the first principal curvature
k_{1}={\phi^{\prime\prime}}/{W^{3}}
of such a hypersurface depends only on
s
,
and coincides with the curvature function of the plane curve

s\in I\mapsto(s,\phi(s))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\varSigma\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
be
an
(f_{s}^{1},f_{s}^{2},\phi)
-hypersurface
of constant mean curvature. Then,
the families
f_{s}^{1}
and
f_{s}^{2}
are both isoparametric.

Proof.

Suppose that
H_{\varSigma}
is constant, i.e., that it is independent of

p_{i}
and
s
. Fixing
s
and
i\in\{1,2\}
, we conclude from (
8
)
that
H_{j}^{s}(p_{j})
,
j\neq i
, is independent of
p_{j}
, which implies that
both families
f_{s}^{1}
and
f_{s}^{2}
are isoparametric.
∎

It will be convenient to introduce the following concepts.

Definition 8
.

Let
\varSigma\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
be an oriented hypersurface whose
T
-field never vanishes.
We say that the principal directions of
\varSigma

split properly

if, in an open neighborhood of each point of
\varSigma
, there exists
an orthonormal frame
\{Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n-1}\}
,
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
, of principal directions such that:

(i)

Z^{1}=T/\|T\|
;

(ii)

Z^{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}})\oplus\{0\}\,\,\,\forall i\in\{2,\dots,n_{1}\}
;

(iii)

Z^{i}\in\{0\}\oplus\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}})\,\,\,\forall i\in\{n_{1}+1,\dots,n-1\}
.

In other words, except for
Z^{1}
, which is parallel to
T
, any other principal direction
Z^{i}
satisfies

PZ^{i}=\pm Z^{i}
.

Definition 9
.

Let
\varSigma
be a hypersurface of

Definition 17

.

Lemma 18
.

Given integers
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 2
, let

and

be two distinct real numbers such that

(\sqrt{x},\sqrt{y})
is
(n_{1},n_{2})
-independent, and set

for

.
Under these conditions, the following hold:

(i)

has precisely

which, for

and

(43)

(ii)

if

is even, and

(iii)

the coordinates of

with respect to the basis of eigenvectors of

are all different from zero.

Proof.

(i) We follow closely the proof given for
[
9
, Lemma 16]
.
To that end, we first define the functions (seen as vectors)

\omega_{u,v}(t)=s_{x}^{u}(t)c_{x}^{n_{1}-1-u}(t)s_{y}^{v}(t)c_{y}^{n_{2}-1-v}(t),\quad u\in\{0,\dots,n_{1}-1\},\ v\in\{0,\dots,n_{2}-1\},

where

,

,

and

are defined as in (

).

Let us prove that the set
\mathcal{B}=\{\omega_{u,v}\}

is linearly independent, so that

of dimension

.
Indeed, considering the equalities

\displaystyle e^{(n_{1}-1-2u)\sqrt{x}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(c_{x}(t)+\sqrt{x}s_{x}(t))^{n_{1}-1-u}(c_{x}(t)-\sqrt{x}s_{x}(t))^{u},

\displaystyle e^{(n_{2}-1-2v)\sqrt{y}t}

\displaystyle(c_{y}(t)+\sqrt{y}s_{y}(t))^{n_{2}-1-v}(c_{y}(t)-\sqrt{y}s_{y}(t))^{v},

we have, for each

and

, that

belongs to

In addition, since

is

-independent, the

that define the functions

we conclude that

, as claimed.

Now, observe that the matrix
K

represents the operator
d/dt
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
, and that

is an eigenvector of

whose associated eigenvalue is

In addition, since

is
(n_{1},n_{2})
-independent,

(ii)

(iii) Let us identify

Lemma 32
.

Set
M=(m_{ij})
and consider the following subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}:

•

\Gamma_{M}:=\{\mathfrak{L}_{2i-1}\,;\,i=1,\dots,n_{1}n_{2}\}
;

•

\varOmega_{M}:=\{\mathfrak{C}_{j}\,;\,m_{(2i-1)j}=0\,\,\,\forall i=1,\dots,n_{1}n_{2}\}
;

that is,
\Gamma_{M}
is the set of all odd rows of
M
, whereas
\varOmega_{M}
is the set of
the columns of
M
whose odd entries are all zero. Then,
if
(n_{1}+1)n_{2}
is even,
\Gamma_{M}
and
\varOmega_{M}
are both linearly dependent and,
in particular,
M
is a singular matrix.

Given integers
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 2
, let
\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n_{2}-1}\}
be
the set of eigenvectors of
\mathcal{K}(n_{2},\uptau)
. Consider the decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}}=\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
and define

x_{u,v}=(0,0,\dots,0,x_{v},0,\dots,0),\,\,\,u\in\{1,\dots,n_{1}+1\},\,\,v\in\{0,\dots,n_{2}-1\},

that is, all coordinates of
x_{u,v}
are zero, except the
u
-th, which is just the eigenvector

x_{v}
of
\mathcal{K}(n_{2},\uptau)
. Equivalently,
x_{u,v}=e_{u}\otimes x_{u}
, where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{1}+1}\}

is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}+1}
. Clearly,

(67)

\mathcal{B}:=\{x_{u,v}\,;\,u=1,\dots,n_{1}+1,\,\,v=0,\dots,n_{2}-1\}

is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}}
. Moreover,
it follows directly from the block structure of
Q=Q(n_{1},n_{2},\uptau)=K(n_{1}+1,n_{2},0,\uptau)
that

(68)

x_{u,v}Q=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{v}x_{u,v}+ux_{u+1,v}&{\rm if}\,\,1\leq u<n_{1}+1;\\[4.30554pt]
\lambda_{v}x_{n_{1}+1,v}&{\rm if}\,\,u=n_{1}+1;\end{array}\right.

that is, for
u\neq n_{1}+1
, all vectors
x_{u,v}
are generalized eigenvectors of

Q
, whereas the vectors
x_{n+1,v}
are all eigenvectors of
Q
.

Our next lemma follows from the above considerations and those from Remark
20
.

Lemma 33
.

The following assertions hold true.

(i)

The matrix
Q=K(n_{1}+1,n_{2},0,\uptau)
is nonsingular if and only if
n_{2}
is even.

(ii)

The vectors
x_{n_{1}+1,v}
of the basis
\mathcal{B}
are eigenvectors of
Q
,
and all the other vectors
x_{u,v}\in\mathcal{B}
are generalized eigenvectors of
Q
.

(iii)

Regarding the coordinates of
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}}
with respect to the basis

\mathcal{B},
those with respect to the generalized eigenvectors

x_{1,v}
never vanish, whereas the ones with respect to the vectors
x_{u,v}
,
u\neq 1
,
are all zero.

Now, we are in position to establish our next lemma.

Lemma 34
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

If
n_{2}
is even, for any positive integer
s
, the set

\{e_{1}Q^{s},e_{1}Q^{s+1},\dots,e_{1}Q^{s+(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}\}

is linearly independent.

(ii)

If
n_{2}
is odd, let
s\geq(n_{1}+1)n_{2}
and define

\varLambda=\{e_{1}Q^{2},e_{1}Q^{3},\dots,e_{1}Q^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}\},\quad\varLambda_{s}=\varLambda\cup\{e_{1}Q^{s}\}.

Then, denoting by

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{1}(s),\dots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}}(s)
the column vectors of the matrix

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(s)
whose rows are the vectors of
\varLambda_{s}
, the following hold:

(a)

\varLambda
is linearly independent, whereas
\varLambda_{s}
is linearly dependent;

(b)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{1}(s)
is in the spam of the odd columns
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{3}(s),\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{5}(s),\dots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n_{2}}(s)

(c)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n_{2}+1}(s)
is in the
span of the even columns
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n_{2}+3}(s),\dots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{C}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{2n_{2}}(s)

Proof.

(i) When
n_{2}
is even, we know from Lemma
33
-(i) that

is invertible. So, it
suffices to prove (i) for

. To that end, given nonnegative integers

Then, considering (
68

x_{1,v}Q^{k}=\lambda_{v}^{k}x_{1,v}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k\leq n_{1}}\mu_{k,\ell}\lambda_{v}^{k-\ell}x_{1+\ell,v},\quad k\geq 1,

where, in the second summand, the summation is taken from
\ell=1
to
\ell=k
, if
k<n_{1}
,
and from
\ell=1
to
\ell=n_{1}
, if
k\geq n_{1}
.

Now, consider the following vector equation of variables
c_{0},\dots,c_{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}
:

(70)

\sum_{k=0}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}c_{k}e_{1}Q^{k}=0.

We have from Lemma

-(iii) that

(71)

e_{1}=\sum_{v=0}^{n_{2}-1}a_{v}x_{1,v},

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mux\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{1,v}=a_{v}x_{1,v}

(72)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}\lambda_{v}^{k}c_{k}=0,}\\[21.52771pt]

where

(ii)-(a) Fix

) becomes the following homogeneous linear system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=n_{1}+1}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}\lambda_{v}^{k}c_{k}+\lambda_{v}^{s}c_{s}=0,}\\[21.52771pt]
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=n_{1}+1}^{(n_{1}+1)n_{2}-1}k\lambda_{v}^{k-1}c_{k}+s\lambda_{v}^{s-1}c_{s}=0,}\\[21.52771pt]

Lemma 37
.

Let
\varSigma
be an isoparametric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon_{2}}^{n_{2}}
with
constant angle function
\varTheta\in(-1,1)
. Then,
\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\|
is
constant on
\varSigma
. Moreover, if
\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\|=0
,
\epsilon_{1}
and

\epsilon_{2}
are necessarily non-positive.

Proof.

We shall consider only the case
\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\neq 0
, since the proof for the case

\epsilon_{1}=0\neq\epsilon_{2}
is entirely analogous.

We first observe that, in our setting, the equality (
47
) for

k=0
reads as

(76)

D(r)=\sum_{u=0}^{n_{1}-1}\sum_{v=0}^{n_{2}-1}\alpha_{u,v,0}c_{\uptau_{1}}^{n_{1}-1-u}(r)s_{\uptau_{1}}^{u}(r)c_{\uptau_{2}}^{n_{2}-1-v}(r)s_{\uptau_{2}}^{v}(r),

since
a_{1j}=a_{j1}=0
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. As a consequence, we have that
the coefficient
\alpha_{1,0,0}
of
c_{\uptau_{1}}^{n_{1}-2}(r)s_{\uptau_{1}}(r)c_{\uptau_{2}}^{n_{2}-1}(r)
is nothing but

-{\rm trace}\,\mathcal{A}_{1}
, that is,

(77)

\alpha_{1,0,0}=-\sum_{i=2}^{n_{1}}a_{ii}.

Indeed,
\alpha_{1,0,0}
is the sum over

of the
triangular minors whose diagonal is of the type

(c_{\uptau_{1}}(r),\dots,c_{\uptau_{1}}(r),-a_{ii}s_{\uptau_{1}}(r),c_{\uptau_{1}}(r),\dots,c_{\uptau_{1}}(r),c_{\uptau_{2}}(r),\dots,c_{\uptau_{2}}(r))

with
n_{1}-2
occurrences of
c_{\uptau_{1}}(r)
and
n_{2}-1
occurrences of
c_{\uptau_{2}}(r)
.

Analogously, we have that

\alpha_{2,0,0}=\sum_{i<j=2}^{n_{1}}a_{ii}a_{jj}-a_{ij}^{2},

and so,

(78)

\|\mathcal{A}_{1}\|^{2}=\sum_{i=2}^{n_{1}}a_{ii}^{2}+2\sum_{i<j=2}^{n_{1}}a_{ij}^{2}=\alpha_{1,0}^{2}-2\sum_{i<j=2}^{n_{1}}a_{ii}a_{jj}+2\sum_{i<j=2}^{n_{1}}a_{ij}^{2}=\alpha_{1,0}^{2}-2\alpha_{2,0}.

It follows from Lemma
28
and (
77
)-(
78
)
that the trace and the norm of

\mathcal{A}_{1}
are both constant on
\varSigma

{\rm trace}\,\mathcal{A}_{3}=H-{\rm trace}\,\mathcal{A}_{1}
is constant as well.
Then, we have from (
11

15

(79)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(1-\varTheta)\|\mathcal{A}_{1}\|^{2}-(1+\varTheta)\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon_{1}(n_{1}-1)}{2}(1-\varTheta^{2})&=&0,\\[4.30554pt]
(1-\varTheta)\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\|^{2}-(1+\varTheta)\|\mathcal{A}_{3}\|^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}(n_{2}-1)}{2}(1-\varTheta^{2})&=&0,\\[4.30554pt]
\end{array}\right.

which clearly implies that
\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\|
is constant, and that
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
are
both non-positive if
\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\|=0
. This completes the proof.

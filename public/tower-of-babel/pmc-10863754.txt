Disordered Balance of T‐Cell Subsets in Arterial Tertiary Lymphoid Organs in Immunoglobulin G4–Related Vascular Disease
Authors: Satomi Kasashima, Atsuhiro Kawashima, Nozomu Kurose, Satoru Ozaki, Fuminori Kasashima, Yasushi Matsumoto, Hirofumi Takemura, Hiroko Ikeda, Ken‐ichi Harada
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundArterial/aortic tertiary lymphoid organs (ATLOs), characterized by germinal centers, control local arterial immune responses. T follicular helper cells (Tfh), resident in germinal centers, regulate immunoglobulin production and germinal center development. They consist of Tfh1, Tfh2, and Tfh17 subsets. T follicular regulatory (Tfr) cells possess suppressive functions as regulatory T cells and migrate into germinal centers. Immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)–related diseases manifest in vascular lesions as frequently formed inflammatory aneurysms (IgG4‐related abdominal aortic aneurysm [IgG4‐AAAs]). IgG4‐AAAs contain several ATLOs.Methods and ResultsWe performed whole‐slide immunohistochemical image analysis in surgical specimens of IgG4‐AAAs (n=21), non–IgG4‐related inflammatory AAAs (n=17), atherosclerotic AAAs (n=10), and Takayasu arteritis (n=5). IgG4‐AAA was characterized by numerous, large, irregular‐shaped ATLOs, and higher numbers of Tfr and Tfh2 cells than Tfh1 cells were present compared with others. The morphologic abnormalities (in number, area, and form) of ATLOs in IgG4‐AAAs and the increased number of Tfr cells are closely related to the activity of IgG4‐related diseases. All T‐cell subsets were more enriched within ATLOs than outside ATLOs. In particular, an increase in Tfr cells in IgG4‐AAAs was associated with ATLO formation. Increased Tfh17 cells were found in Takayasu arteritis, and atherosclerotic AAA and non–IgG4‐related inflammatory AAAs were characterized by increased Tfh1 cells.ConclusionsIn the classification of vascular lesions, considering the imbalance in T‐cell subsets, IgG4‐AAA should be positioned as adventitial vasculitis with predominant Tfr and Tfh2 cells, accompanied by the abnormal appearance of ATLOs.Keywords: arterial tertiary lymphoid tissues, follicular helper T cells, follicular regulatory T cells, germinal center, immunoglobulin G4–related abdominal aortic aneurysm, immunoglobulin G4–related diseaseSubject Categories: Basic Science Research, Etiology

Clinical Perspective

What Is New?

This retrospective study uses a new histopathologic method for whole‐slide analysis. We characterized immunoglobulin G4–related vascular disease according to imbalances in T‐cell subsets related to morphologic changes of arterial/aortic tertiary lymphoid organs.

This retrospective study uses a new histopathologic method for whole‐slide analysis. We characterized immunoglobulin G4–related vascular disease according to imbalances in T‐cell subsets related to morphologic changes of arterial/aortic tertiary lymphoid organs.

Compared with Takayasu arteritis and atherosclerotic changes, imbalances in T‐cell subsets, according to the predominance of follicular helper type 2 T cells and follicular regulatory T cells in immunoglobulin G4–related vascular disease, suggested that immunoglobulin G4–related vascular disease should be defined as adventitial vasculitis.

Compared with Takayasu arteritis and atherosclerotic changes, imbalances in T‐cell subsets, according to the predominance of follicular helper type 2 T cells and follicular regulatory T cells in immunoglobulin G4–related vascular disease, suggested that immunoglobulin G4–related vascular disease should be defined as adventitial vasculitis.

Elevated serum immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) levels and chronic fibroinflammation in affected systemic organs, including the salivary glands, pancreas, and retroperitoneum, characterize IgG4‐related disease (IgG4‐RD). 1 , 2 , 3 Vascular lesions, such as inflammatory abdominal aortic aneurysms (IAAAs), a type of aneurysm characterized by marked adventitial fibrous thickening that accounts for 5% to 10% of all resected abdominal aortic aneurysms, commonly occur in IgG4‐RD. 4 , 5 Approximately 50% of IAAA cases are represented by IgG4‐related abdominal aortic aneurysms (IgG4‐AAAs). 4 , 5 In terms of aortic wall thickening, Takayasu arteritis (TKA), also known as large‐vessel aortitis, and non–IgG4‐related IAAA (non–IgG4‐IAAA) are important vascular diseases to differentiate from IgG4‐AAA.

Tertiary lymphoid organs (TLOs) are ectopic lymphoid structures with morphologic features similar to secondary lymphoid organs that allow homing of naïve cells in the T‐cell area, the interface between the T‐ and B‐cell zones, and the germinal center (GC) areas that present follicular dendritic cells (FDCs). 6 , 7 TLOs are major control sites enabling immune surveillance and innate immune responses. 6 , 7 , 8

Arterial/aortic TLOs (ATLOs) are TLOs in the media and the adventitia of the aorta and arteries. Resident macrophages, T cells, B cells, mast cells, and dendritic cells in the adventitia have different distributions inside and outside ATLOs. 9 , 10 ATLOs have been described in autoimmunity, microbial infection, vasculitis, and atherosclerosis. 11 , 12 , 13 ATLOs may serve essential functions in IgG4‐AAA because the number of ATLOs of IgG4‐AAA is characteristically increased compared with atherosclerotic AAA (aAAA). 4 , 5 , 14

For TLOs/ATLOs, follicular helper T (Tfh) cells, subsets of helper T cells expressing programmed death receptor‐1, C‐X‐C chemokine receptor type 5, B‐cell lymphoma 6 (BCL‐6), and interleukin‐21, are primarily located in GCs. 15 Tfh cells control the formation of GCs and drive the maturation of long‐lived, high‐affinity B cells into memory B cells and plasmacytes to produce IgG. 7 Corresponding to helper T‐cell subsets (type 1 helper T cell [Th1]; type 2 helper T cell [Th2]; and type 17 helper T cell [Th17]), Tfh cells also consist of at least 3 subsets, because of the surface receptor expression and cytokine secretion (type 1 Tfh [Tfh1]; type 2 Tfh [Tfh2]; and type 17 Tfh [Tfh17]). 15 , 16 Tfh subsets also vary in their ability to provide help to B cells; Tfh2 and Tfh17 have the functional ability to help B cells produce antibodies, whereas Tfh1 does not. 17 Follicular regulatory T cells (Tfr) are a subset of Foxp3 (forkhead box protein P3)+regulatory T cells (Tregs) that adopt features of Tfh to enable their migration into GC while maintaining their suppressive function as Tregs for immune cells. 18 , 19 Tfr optimize GC responses to promote effective humoral memory. 18 , 19 The dysregulation of Tfh and Tfr, which was concerned with the ratio of Tfh/Tfr and reflects GC reactions, and the balance of Th1, Th2, and Th17 cells have attracted attention in the pathogenesis and progression of several immune diseases. 20 , 21

In IgG4‐RD, the inflammatory and fibrotic processes are controlled by cytokines secreted mainly by Th2 cells and Tregs. 22 , 23 In addition, a disrupted balance between Th1 and Th2 cells and increased Th2 and Th17 cell counts may contribute to the pathogenesis of IgG4‐RD. 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 Furthermore, the essential role of Tfh cells in IgG4‐RD has attracted research attention, 27 , 29 , 30 because IgG4 production by B‐cell maturation is generally controlled by T cells within lymphoid follicles or TLOs. 7 Akiyama et al reported that activated peripheral circulating Tfh2 cell counts are high in IgG4‐RD and decrease after corticosteroid therapy. 24 This change was positively correlated with the IgG4‐RD responder index, the number of affected organs along with the disease state, and circulating Tfh1 cells, whereas Tfh17 cells did not affect serum IgG4 levels. 24 This emphasized the close association between the imbalance of the Tfh subset and the pathogenesis or progression of IgG4‐RD. 23 Recent reports on the disordered balance of Tfh cells and helper T cells outside the GC showed that it might be closely related to the morphologic irregularity of TLOs of IgG4‐RD in the salivary gland (IgG4‐related sclerosing sialadenitis [IgG4‐SS]). 31 , 32

In vascular disease, the association of T‐cell subsets in ATLOs with the pathogenesis and progression of disease has not been precisely examined. This retrospective study histologically examined Tfh subsets, including Tfh1, Tfh2, and Tfh17 cells, and Tregs using whole‐slide analysis in IgG4‐AAA compared with aAAA, TKA, non–IgG4‐IAAA, and normal aorta.

METHODS

The authors declare that all supporting data are available within the article.

Case Selection

We selected 38 cases of IAAA diagnosed by histologic dense adventitia with severe sclerosing inflammation and fibrosis (>2 mm thick) among patients undergoing surgery for aneurysm at Kanazawa University Hospital and Kanazawa Medical Center (Kanazawa, Japan) between January 2006 and December 2016. We included 21 cases of IgG4‐AAAs based on the comprehensive histologic diagnostic criteria for IgG4‐related diseases as follows 2 : (1) dense lymphoplasmacytic inflammatory infiltrates with increased numbers of IgG4‐positive plasma cells (>50/high‐power fields [10 eyepieces, 40 object lenses] and IgG4/IgG ratio of >50%) (Figure 1A–C), (2) storiform pattern of fibrosis, and (3) obliterative vasculitis. The 17 remaining IAAAs were classified as non–IgG4‐IAAA. No significant differentiation in the radiological findings was observed between IgG4‐AAAs and non–IgG4‐IAAAs. Ten aAAA cases were selected sequentially between January 2006 and December 2006. The patients with TKA were diagnosed according to the American College of Rheumatology 1990 criteria. 33 Five patients of TKA with aneurysmal formation could be examined histopathologically and were included in this study (Figure 1D–1F). aAAA was defined as aneurysms with severe atherosclerosis in the intima and media, thin adventitia of <1 mm, and minimal inflammatory cell infiltration (Figure 1G–1I). Ten normal control aortas (no or slight atherosclerotic abdominal aorta without dilatation) were obtained from consecutive autopsies performed at Kanazawa Medical Center between January 2012 and August 2012.

Pathologic Measurements

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry was performed with a fully automated immunostainer (Roche Diagnostics, Tokyo, Japan; VENTANA BenchMark ULTRA) using antibodies against IgG4 (Nichirei Bioscience, Tokyo, Japan; clone HP6025, ×2), cluster designation (CD) 21 (Leica Biosystems, Wetzlar, Germany; clone 2G9, ×100) (Figure 2A and 2D), Foxp3 (Abcam; clone 236A/E7, ×100) (Figure 2B and 2C), T‐box expression in T cells (Tbet) (Abcam; clone EPR9301, ×200) (Figure 2E), GATA3 (GATA family binding protein 3) (Abcam; clone EPR16651, ×400) (Figure 2F), RAR‐related orphan receptor γ (RORγ) (Abcam; clone EPR20006, ×200) (Figure 2G), and BCL‐6 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK; clone EPR11410‐43, ×1500). CD21 is a marker of FDCs, whereas BCL‐6 is a marker of lymphocyte presence in GCs. 8 , 34

Image Data Acquisition

All histopathologic glass slides were scanned and converted into virtual slides using a whole‐slide scanner at ×40 magnification with a resolution of 0.25 μm/pixels (Nano Zoomer; Hamamatsu Photonics, Hamamatsu, Japan).

Software

Whole‐slide image analysis was performed to analyze ATLO morphology and the subtype and distribution of T‐cell subsets using QuPath interface to the OpenSlide library (https://qupath.github.io). 35 , 36

ATLO Analysis

Aggregations of FDCs indicated by CD21 immunopositivity in the media and adventitia were defined as ATLOs (Figure 1C, 1F, and 1I). Using CD21‐ and BCL‐6–immunostained slides, all ATLO regions on each slide were annotated by an experienced pathologist (K.S.) (Figure 2D). A batch script was subsequently applied to automatically identify the area and circumference of each ATLO and the number of ATLOs in the whole‐slide images. The circularity of each ATLO was defined as 4πS/L 2, wherein S and L are the area and circumference, respectively. The circularity of a circle is 1, and decreased circularity of the shape refers to the shape's irregularity. The area of the ATLOs was calculated as the ratio of the ATLO area/the whole aortic wall area.

Analysis of T‐Cell Subsets

Foxp3, Tbet, GATA3, and RORγ expression was analyzed using QuPath's “simple tissue detection” and “fast cell counts” commands. The number of positive cells and the area were used to calculate the average number of positive cells/mm2.

Definition of T‐Cell Subsets

In contrast to Tfr and Tfh cells, T cells outside ATLOs in the adventitia were defined as interfollicular T (ifT) cells. ATLO annotations using CD21‐immunostained slides were reconstructed to Foxp3‐, Tbet‐, GATA3‐, and RORγ‐stained slides, which were continuous section slides. QuPath commands were used to calculate the numbers of Foxp3‐, GATA3‐, Tbet‐, and RORγ‐immunopositive cells within the ATLO areas in the whole‐slide images. Foxp3‐immunopositive cells distributed within ATLOs were defined as Tfr cells, and those outside ATLOs were considered interfollicular regulatory T (ifTreg) cells. Tbet‐immunopositive cells distributed within ATLOs were defined as Tfh1 cells, and those outside ATLOs were considered interfollicular type 1 helper T (ifTh1) cells. GATA3‐immunopositive cells distributed within ATLOs were defined as Tfh2 cells, and those outside ATLOs were considered interfollicular type 2 helper T (ifTh2) cells. RORγ‐immunopositive cells distributed within ATLOs were defined as Tfh17 cells, and those outside ATLOs were considered interfollicular type 17 helper T (ifTh17) cells. Then, Tfr cells were confirmed by double immunostaining for Foxp3 and BCL‐6 (Figure 2B inset); Tfh1 cells were confirmed by double immunostaining for Tbet and BCL‐6 (Figure 2E inset); Tfh2 cells were confirmed by double immunostaining for GATA3 and BCL‐6 (Figure 2F inset); and Tfh17 cells were confirmed by double immunostaining for RORγ and BCL‐6 (Figure 2G inset). After this, the ratios of Tfr/ifTreg, Tfh1/ifTh1, Tfh2/ifTh2, and Tfh17/ifTh17 cells were calculated.

Statistical Analysis

Steel‐Dwass ANOVA and Fisher exact test were used to compare clinical features, ATLO morphologic parameters, and T‐cell subset area numbers between disease groups. Multivariate multinomial logistic regression analysis was performed to determine factors that had a significant effect in the univariate analysis and calculate the adjusted odds ratio (OR) and 95% CI. Spearman correlation coefficients were used to test the associations between continuous variables. All analyses were performed using SPSS, version 28 (IBM, Armonk, NY). Statistical significance was indicated by P<0.05.

The study was approved by the Human Investigation Review Committee of Kanazawa Medical Center (No. 30‐63) and Kanazawa University Hospital (No. 973‐1). It conformed to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The patients provided informed consent before the study.

RESULTS

ATLO Morphology

ATLOs were rarely observed in the control aorta (10%) but were sometimes seen in aAAA (40%) and TKA cases (60%). However, all IgG4‐AAA samples possessed several ATLOs. ATLOs seen in IgG4‐AAA comprised a thicker network of FDCs and were large, distorted, numerous, and evenly distributed in the adventitia (Figure 1C). In ATLOs of the IgG4‐AAA group, immunoblasts were sparse, the polarization of recognizable light and dark zones was unclear, and they often collapsed and fused. In contrast, ATLOs seen in the other groups were small and nearly round (Figure 1F and 1I).

The median area and median area rate of the ATLOs were significantly higher in the IgG4‐AAA group (2.3 mm2 and 1.29%, respectively) than the non–IgG4‐IAAA (1.1 mm2 and 0.30%, respectively), TKA (0.88 mm2 and 0.22%, respectively), aAAA (0.31 mm2 and 0.53%, respectively), and normal aorta (0.12 mm2 and 0.03%, respectively) groups (Figure 3). The median circularity of the ATLOs in the IgG4‐AAA group (0.64) was significantly lower than that in the non–IgG4‐IAAA (0.71; P=0.007), TKA (0.95; P=0.001), aAAA (0.93; P<0.001), and normal aorta (0.95; P<0.001) groups. The median number of ATLOs was higher in the IgG4‐AAA group (3.7/mm2). However, there was no significant difference between each subgroup. The median number, area, and area rate of ATLOs in the aAAA group were nearly intermediate between those of the normal aorta and non–IgG4‐IAAA groups.

Number of T Cells Outside ATLOs (ifTreg, ifTh1, ifTh2, and ifTh17 Subsets) in the Whole Aortic Wall

The median numbers of ifTreg, ifTh1, and ifTh2 cells in the whole aortic wall were higher in the IgG4‐AAA group (10.0/mm2, 5.6/mm2, and 35.1/mm2, respectively) than in the other groups, whereas the median numbers of ifTh17 cells in all groups were low (<1/mm2) with no significant differences.

Ratio of T Cells Within to Outside ATLOs

In all groups, the numbers of intrafollicular T‐cell subsets were greater than the corresponding number of extrafollicular T‐cell subsets; the density of each T‐cell subset increased within ATLOs (Figure 4B and 4C). However, the concentration of T‐cell subsets within ATLOs differed in each group of vascular lesions, whereas the ratios of intrafollicular/interfollicular T cells of all T‐cell subsets were equally low in the control aorta.

The median Tfr/ifTreg ratio was significantly and characteristically higher in the IgG4‐AAA group (44.9) than in the other groups. The median Tfh1/ifTh1 ratio was similarly high in the aAAA (37.4) and non–IgG4‐IAAA (36.9) groups and was significantly higher than that in the IgG‐AAA group (11.2; P=0.044 and P=0.035, respectively). The median Tfh2/ifTh2 ratio was slightly higher in the IgG4‐AAA group. However, there were no significant differences in the other groups. The median Tf17/ifTh17 ratio was significantly higher in the TKA group (22.0) than in the other groups. The median Tf17/ifTh17 ratios in the remaining 4 groups were low (controls, 0; aAAA, 2.8; non–IgG4‐IAAA, 0.1; IgG4‐AAA, 1.6).

In the control aorta, the median ratios of intrafollicular/interfollicular T cells were low for all T‐cell subsets (Tfr/ifTreg ratio, 5.6; Tfh1/ifTh1 ratio, 3.8; Tfh2/ifTh2 ratio, 5.7; Tfh17/ifTh17 ratio, 0). In the TKA group, the median ratios of Tfr/ifTreg, Tfh1/ifTh1, and Tfh2/ifTh2 (2.7, 0.2, and 0.7, respectively) cells were the lowest among all groups. The median ratio of intrafollicular/interfollicular T‐cell subsets was similar between the aAAA and non–IgG4‐IAAA groups, and there were no significant differences (aAAA versus non–IgG4‐IAAA, Tfr/ifTreg ratio, 20.8 versus 27.2; Tfh1/ifTh1 ratio, 37.4 versus 36.9; Tfh2/ifTh2 ratio, 10.2 versus 7.0; Tfh17/ifTh17 ratio, 2.8 versus 0.1).

DISCUSSION

Recently, several reports have indicated that a disrupted balance of T‐cell subsets contributes to the systemic and vascular manifestations of vascular disease. Th1 and Th17 cytokine responses correlate with TKA activity. 37 An increase in serum interleukin‐6 characterizes TKA, and interleukin‐6 is a key cytokine involved in the differentiation of Th17 cells and regulates the balance between Th17 and Treg cells. Gao et al reported that abnormal T‐cell maturation into Th2‐like Tregs and decreased circulating mature Tregs could contribute to TKA pathogenesis. 38 Our findings demonstrate that the number of Tfh17 cells was higher in TKA than in other vascular groups, although TKA evaluation would be limited by the small number of cases. We considered that the increase in Th17 cells in TKA in previous reports might be attributable to the higher number of Tf17 cells.

Flow cytometry–based peripheral blood analysis revealed that uncontrolled inflammation involving Tfh and Tfr cells underlies IgG4‐RD cause. 39 A disrupted balance between Th1 and Th2 cells and increased Th2 and Th17 cell counts may contribute to IgG4‐RD pathogenesis. 15 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 Grados et al reported that IgG4‐RD is characterized by a shift in circulating T cells toward Th2/Tfh2 and Th17/Tfh17 polarization. 40 However, previous studies of circulating T cells via flow cytometry have not evaluated intrafollicular T cells exactly, because Tfr and Tfh cells present in GCs have different phenotypes from circulating Tfr and Tfh cells, which are primarily defined by C‐X‐C chemokine receptor type 5 positivity. 21 Moreover, although circulating Tfh increases with age, the number and activation of Tfh within GC are reduced; this discrepancy is reflective of the defects in antigen‐specific responses with age between Tfh within GC and circulating Tfh. 17

Using whole‐slide analysis, this is the first report to describe the T‐cell subsets in IgG4‐AAA, particularly regarding ATLOs. In IgG4‐AAA, the numbers of Tfr, ifTreg, Tfh2, and ifTh2 cells were significantly high; therefore, all Treg and Th2 cells were also more numerous than other groups. Moreover, the Tfr/ifTreg ratio was considerably higher than that of the other groups. The Tfr cell count and the Tfr/ifTreg ratio were closely related to IgG4‐AAA activity (adventitia thickness, IgG4+ cell count, and IgG4/IgG ratio). Thus, the increase in Treg cell concentrations within ATLOs was closely associated with the pathogenesis and progression of IgG4‐AAA and is a novel pathologic hallmark of IgG4‐AAA. For T‐cell subsets, the Tfh2 cell number increase and Tfh1 cell number decrease might assist in the pathologic diagnosis of IgG4‐AAA. Reliable pathologic hallmarks of IgG4‐AAA are needed given that diagnosis of IgG4‐RD using serum IgG4 levels or tissue IgG4+ plasmacytes is challenging. The number of circulating Tfr cells or the Tfr/Treg ratio, not just Treg cells, should be assessed to diagnose or estimate the activity of IgG4‐AAA using flow cytometry. Considering that the interaction of Tfr and Tfh cells regulates the production of IgG within the GC, controlling Tfr cells creates opportunities to develop novel therapeutic approaches in immune disease. 41 , 42 Our findings indicate that Tfr therapy might be a promising therapeutic method for IgG4‐AAA in the future.

ATLOs are rarely present in the adventitia of normal aorta and medium‐sized arteries, and their presence suggests that there is a vascular lesion. Previous reports indicated that ATLOs may organize atherogenic immune responses, and disruption in the balance of immune cell subsets may trigger atherosclerosis. 43 , 44 In this study, the ATLOs in aAAA were sparsely distributed, small, and nearly round, similar to TLOs in chronic sialadenitis. These are thought to be a response to local chronic stimulation. 44 Clemet et al noticed that active TKA with increased aortic 18F‐fluoro‐deoxyglucose uptake contained several ATLOs, whereas a clear association between pathogenesis and ATLOs in TKA was not observed. 13 In contrast, ATLOs in IgG4‐AAA were significantly larger, more numerous, and more irregular shaped, with a thick network of FDCs, compared with other vascular groups. Larger ATLO size in IgG4‐AAA was associated with a more irregular shape and a higher number of ATLOs. In addition, they were fused, or divided and collapsed, not simply enlarged. Moreover, the area, number, and ATLO irregularity in IgG4‐AAA significantly correlated with the activity of IgG4‐RD, such as the number of IgG4+ plasmacytes and serum IgG4 levels. Therefore, we believe that the morphologic deviation of ATLOs in IgG4‐AAA might be associated with the active phase of IgG4‐AAA, 14 which could be useful for the pathologic diagnosis of IgG4‐AAA. Recently, inflammation evaluated by 18F‐positron emission tomography/computed tomography has been noted in IgG4‐related vascular diseases. 45 On the basis of TKA, 37 the activity of IgG4‐AAA evaluated by fluoro‐deoxyglucose uptake observed by positron emission tomography/computed tomography might reflect the amount or activity of ATLOs in IgG4‐AAA.

Tfr cells are more likely to regulate the function and output of TLOs/ATLOs at an established GC. 17 In contrast, ifTreg cells control initial GC formation; hence, the balance between Tfr and ifTreg cells could contribute to GC development and decay by regulating the production of several cytokines, such as interleukin‐10 and interleukin‐21. 39 , 46 In IgG4‐SS, Tfr/ifTreg imbalance is associated with the invagination of the surrounding T–B‐cell border into TLOs, causing TLOs to have irregular shapes. 31 , 32 Similarly, in IgG4‐AAA, both the number of Tfr cells and the Tfr/if Treg ratio are closely related to the average area, area ratio, and ATLO irregularity. Thus, the Tfr/ifTreg imbalance reflects characteristic ATLO dysplasia in IgG4‐AAA.

In the previous study, interleukin‐10–positive Tfr and Tfh were involved in TLO formation of IgG4‐SS. 31 Similarly, we confirmed the presence of numerous interleukin‐10 and interleukin‐21 immunopositive cells in ATLOs of IgG4‐AAA (data not shown). However, it is a limitation of this descriptive study because we did not directly measure the levels of cytokines in these ATLOs. Thus, we cannot determine whether they are present in sufficient quantities for disease progression of IgG4‐AAA in this study.

The morphologic dysplasia of TLOs in IgG4‐SS is greater (larger and more irregular ATLOs with more prominent immunoblasts) than the ATLOs in IgG4‐AAA. 31 , 32 Compared with IgG4‐AAA, IgG4‐SS cases might be in a more active phase; they often have high serum IgG4 levels and are prone to multiple organ lesions of IgG4‐RD. 47 In IgG4‐SS, the Tfh2/ifTh2 and Tfh17/ifTh17 ratios are high, and the Tfh17/ifTh17 ratio is significantly related to the number of IgG4 cells in the area, the area size, and TLO irregularity. 32 In contrast, IgG4‐AAA shows a higher degree of fibrosis and is more closely related to inflammatory features, including elevated serum CRP (C‐reactive protein) and interleukin‐6 levels related to local interleukin‐6 production. 14 This report illustrates that in IgG4‐AAA, the number of Tfh17 cells and the Tfh17/ifTh17 ratio were low, and the Th17 cell component was not related to IgG4‐AAA activity and ATLO morphology. Therefore, the differences in the morphologic abnormalities of ATLOs/TLOs in IgG4‐AAA and IgG4‐SS may be attributable to differences in the activity of IgG4‐RD and pathogenesis and progression factors.

The number of each T‐cell subset in aAAA was uncharacteristic. However, the ratios of intrafollicular/interfollicular T‐cell subsets in aAAA were strikingly different from those of IgG4‐AAA, showing a moderate increase in the Tfr/ifTreg ratio, a high Tfh1/ifTh1 ratio, and low ratios of Tfh2/ifTh2 and Tfh17/ifTh17. Interestingly, the ratios of intrafollicular/interfollicular T‐cell subsets in non–IgG4‐IAAA were similar to those of aAAA, whereas the number of each T‐cell subset in non–IgG4‐IAAA was higher than those of aAAA. Thus, aAAA and non–IgG4‐IAAA were similar, and non–IgG4‐IAAA might be an accelerated inflammatory state of aAAA.

In conclusion, the increases in T‐cell subsets inside ATLOs were characteristic for each type of vascular lesion (Figure 6). Tfr and Tfh2 cells were predominant over Tfh1 cells in IgG4‐AAA. The increase in Tfr and Tfh2 cells and the decrease in Tfh1 cells represent novel hallmarks of IgG4‐AAA that are associated with morphologically abnormal ATLO formation and disease pathogenesis. An increase in Tfh17 cells might be an attribute of TKA, and aAAA and non–IgG4‐IAAA were characterized by an increase in Tfh1 cells. In the classification of vascular lesions, based on the imbalance in T‐cell subsets, IgG4‐AAA should be positioned as vasculitis in predominant adventitia accompanied by morphologic abnormalities of ATLOs. Moreover, the presence of ATLOs with morphologic abnormality and ATLOs containing numerous Tfr should serve as the pathologic diagnosis of IgG4‐AAA.

On induced cycles of Levi graphs associated to line arrangements Thanks: AMS Subject Classification (2020): 14N10, 14N20, 05C38, 05C10, 05E14
Rupam Karmakar, Rajib Sarkar

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the existence of induced cycles in Levi graphs associated to line arrangements in ℙ ℂ 2 \mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2} . We also look at the problem of finding the length of a longest induced cycle in Levi graphs associated to line arrangements.



1.
Introduction

Over the past several decades the problem of finding a longest induced cycle in a simple graph has garnered much attention in fields such as Combinatorics and Computer science. Erdős
[
6
]
mentioned this problem in a more general setting; he talked about the problem of finding a largest induced
r
-regular subgraph in a given graph. Later on, it has been shown that this problem is NP-hard by Cardoso et al. in
[
4
]
. Since a cycle is a
2
-regular subgraph, the problem of determining the length of longest induced cycles in a simple graph is NP-hard. Also, it can be shown that finding a longest induced cycle in any simple graph is equivalent to finding a longest induced cycle in any bipartite graph. Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. We can construct a new bipartite graph
G^{\prime}
with the vertex set
V(G^{\prime})=V(G)\sqcup\{x_{uv}\ |\ \{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
and the edge set
E(G^{\prime})=\{\{u,x_{uv}\},\{x_{uv},v\}\ |\ \{u,v\}\in E(G)\}.
Then there is a path from
u
to
v
in
G
if and only if there is a path from
u
to
v
in
G^{\prime}
. Therefore, a longest induced cycle in
G^{\prime}
gives a longest induced cycle in
G
and vice versa. Several researchers has studied induced cycles in bipartite graphs and it is still an active area of research, see
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
etc.

In
[
5
]
, Coxeter introduced the notion of Levi graphs that are naturally associated to line arrangements (see Section
3
for the definition). More generally, Levi graphs are naturally associated to plane curve arrangements with ordinary singularities. Levi graphs are important as they encode various properties of intersection posets of curve arrangements with ordinary singularities. It can also be noted that Levi graphs are bipartite graphs. So, the study of finding longest induced cycles in Levi graphs associated to curve arrangements are worth to look at, as this is a significant subclass of bipartite graphs.

In
[
10
]
, the authors have studied the binomial edge ideals of Levi graphs associated to certain curve arrangements in the complex projective plane. We prove that the Levi graphs of almost all
d
-arrangements and conic-line arrangements contain an induced cycle of length
6
and using this property, we obtain bounds for the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of powers of binomial edge ideals of Levi graphs coming from
d
-arrangements and conic-line arrangements. Hence, studying the existence of induced cycles in Levi graphs associated to curve arrangements yield lower bounds for the regularity of powers of the corresponding binomial edge ideals.

Motivated by both these combinatorial and algebraic aspects, we focus our attention to the existence of induced cycles in Levi graphs associated to line arrangements. In
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
, the authors have proved that for line arrangements
\mathcal{L}
of
k
lines with
t_{k}=0
, the associated Levi graphs has induced cycles of length
6
. In this article, we study the existence of induced cycles of length
\geq 8
in Levi graphs associated to line arrangements. For line arrangements with
t_{k}=t_{k-1}=0
, we show that (see Theorem
4.2
) the associated Levi graphs contain an induced cycle of length
8
. This result is optimal in the sense that there exists line arrangements with
t_{k}=0
(see Example
4.1
) such that the associated Levi graphs does not contain an induced cycles of length
\geq 8
. Now it is natural to ask whether the associated Levi graph of a line arrangement of
k
lines with
t_{k}=t_{k-1}=t_{k-2}=0
contains an induced cycle of length
10
. The answer is negative as we see in Example
4.4
. To study the existence of induced cycles of length
10
, we first consider line arrangements of
k
lines with largest multiplicity
\geq\frac{k}{2}
and assuming few additional conditions show the existence of induced cycles of length
10
(see Theorem
4.5
). Next, we look into the cases where the largest multiplicity is
\leq\frac{k}{2}
. We know that only line arrangements with
t_{r}=0
for all
r>2
are generic line arrangements and in that case the associated Levi graphs contain induced cycles of all possible lengths. So, we consider line arrangements of
k
lines with
t_{3}\neq 0
and
t_{r}=0
for all
r>3
and show that the associated Levi graphs contain induced cycles of length
2i
for
i\leq\lfloor{\frac{k+9}{4}}\rfloor
if
k
is odd and for
i\leq\min\{\lfloor{\frac{k+11}{4}}\rfloor,\lfloor{\frac{2k+16}{7}}\rfloor\}
if
k
is even (see Theorem
4.6
). We generalize this result in some specific settings in Theorem
4.9
for line arrangements with
t_{r}\neq 0
for some
r>3
.

Geometric properties of a line arrangement fixes the combinatorial data, hence considering a particular class of line arrangement might give better estimate for the lengths of induced cycles in the associated Levi graphs. So, we now restrict ourselves to specific classes of line arrangements, e.g. Ceva’s line arrangements and supersolvable line arrangements and study the induced cycles in the associated Levi graphs. However, determining the length of the longest induced cycles even for a particular class of line arrangements seems very difficult. Therefore, we consider the following few examples of line arrangements with varying combinatorial data and obtain the lengths of longest induced cycles for the associated Levi graphs.

\bullet
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\{9\}}
be a
(9)_{3}
arrangement in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. We show that the length of a longest induced cycle in the associated Levi graph is
14
(see Example
4.7
).

\bullet
We add a line to the
(9)_{3}
arrangement and get a line arrangement
\mathcal{L}_{\{10\}}
of
10
lines with
t_{2}=9
and
t_{3}=12
. We show that the length of a longest induced cycle in the associated Levi graph is
18
( see Example
4.8
).

\bullet
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}
be the Hesse arrangement of lines in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{H}}
is a line arrangement of
12
lines with
t_{2}=12,t_{4}=9
and
t_{r}=0
elsewhere. We show that the length of a longest induced cycle in the associated Levi graph is
12
( see Theorem
4.10
).

\bullet
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a supersolvable line arrangement with two modular points. We compute the length of a longest induced cycle in the associated Levi graph for some cases ( see Theorem
4.16
).

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Line arrangements and Levi graphs

We assume the underlying field to be
\mathbb{C}
, as all the examples used in the article come from complex line arrangements. However, most of the results are true when the underlying field
K
is aribitrary.
We recall some basic notation, definitions, and known results that will be used throughout this article.

First, we recall the notion of a line arrangement in the complex projective plane. A
line arrangement
is simply a finite collection of
k
-distinct lines
\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}\}
in the complex projective plane and is denoted by
\mathcal{L}
. The set of intersection points of
\mathcal{L}
is denoted by
{\rm Sing}(\mathcal{L})
.

We have the following combinatorial count for a line arrangement
\mathcal{L}
:

(1)

\displaystyle\binom{k}{2}=\sum_{p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})}\binom{m_{p}}{2},

where
m_{p}
denotes the multiplicity at
p
i.e., the number of lines in
\mathcal{L}
passing through
p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L}).

Also, we have for every
\ell_{i}\in\mathcal{L}
,

(2)

\displaystyle k-1=\sum_{p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{i}}(m_{p}-1).

Let
t_{r}(\mathcal{L})
be the number of
r
-fold points in
\operatorname{Sing}{(\mathcal{L})}
that is the number of points where exactly
r
lines from
\mathcal{L}
meet. So, if
s(\mathcal{L})
is the total number of intersection points in
\mathcal{L}
, then
s(\mathcal{L})=\sum_{r\geq 2}t_{r}(\mathcal{L})
. We sometimes write
s,t_{r}
in place of
s(\mathcal{L}),t_{r}(\mathcal{L})
respectively if there is no confusion about
\mathcal{L}
.

A point
p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})
is said to be a modular point if for any other point
p^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})
, the line joining
p
and
p^{\prime}
is
\ell_{i}\in\mathcal{L}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

We now recall the notion of Levi graphs for line arrangements.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
be a line arrangement with
|\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})|=s
. Then the associated
Levi graph

G=(V,E)
is a bipartite graph with
V:=V_{1}\cup V_{2}=

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s},y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
, where each vertex
x_{i}
corresponds to an intersection point
p_{i}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})
, each vertex
y_{j}
corresponds to the line
\ell_{j}
in
\mathcal{L}
and vertices
x_{i}
,
y_{j}
are joined by an edge in
E
if and only if
p_{i}
is incident with
\ell_{j}
.

3.2.
Some basics from graph theory

Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. A subgraph
G_{sub}
of
G
is said to be an
induced subgraph
if for all
u,v\in V(G_{sub})
such that
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
implies that
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{sub})
. For a vertex
v
in
G
,

N_{G}(v):=\{u\in V(G):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
denotes the

neighborhood
of
v
in
G
. The
degree
of a vertex
v
, denoted by
\deg_{G}(v)
, is

|N_{G}(v)|
. A
cycle
is a connected graph
G
with
\deg_{G}(v)=2
for all
v\in V(G)
. We denote the cycle on
n
vertices by
\mathbf{C}_{n}
for
n\geq 3
. In particular,
\mathbf{C}_{3}
is triangle and
\mathbf{C}_{4}
is square. Note that by a cycle we mean it is an induced cycle.

4.
Induced cycle of Levi graphs

We have seen in
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
that for a line arrangement
\mathcal{L}
of
k
lines in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with
t_{k}=0
, the associated Levi graph
G
has an induced
{\mathbf{C}}_{6}
. But in general the associated Levi graphs of line arrangements of
k
lines with
t_{k}=0
doesn’t contain induced cycles of length
\geq 8
as we observe in the following example.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}\}
be a near pencil of
k
lines i.e., a line arrangement of
k
lines with
t_{2}=k-1,t_{k-1}=1
and
t_{i}=0
elsewhere. Let,
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{k}\cap\ell_{i}
be double points for
i=1,\dots,k-1
and
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{1}\cap\dots\cap\ell_{k-1}
be the intersection point of multiplicity
k-1
. Now if possible assume that
\ell_{j_{1}}p_{j_{1}j_{2}}\ell_{j_{2}}\dots\ell_{j_{i}}p_{j_{i}j_{1}}\ell_{j_{1}}
corresponds to an induced cycle of length
2i
for
i\geq 4
in the associated Levi graph
G
of
\mathcal{L}
where
p_{j_{i_{1}}j_{i_{2}}}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{i_{1}}}\cap\ell_{j_{i_{2}}},j_{i_{1}},j_{i_{2}}\in\{j_{1},\dots,j_{i}\}
. Since all the double points belong to
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{k}
,
\ell_{k}=\ell_{j_{t}}
for some
t\in\{1,\dots,i\}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\ell_{k}=\ell_{j_{1}}
. Then
\{j_{2},j_{3},j_{4}\}\subset\{1,\dots,k-1\}
. But that would imply that
p_{j_{2}j_{3}}=p_{j_{3}j_{4}}
, a contradiction. So,
G
does not contain an induced cycle of length
\geq 8
.

In this section, we study the existence of induced cycles of length
\geq 8
in
G
. First we show that, if both
t_{k-1}
and
t_{k}
are zero in
\mathcal{L}
, the associated Levi graph
G
has an induced
{\mathbf{C}}_{8}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell_{1},\dots\ell_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
be a line arrangement with
t_{k}=t_{k-1}=0
. Then the associated Levi graph has an induced
{\mathbf{C}}_{8}
.

Proof.

Let
q
be the largest integer such that
t_{q}\neq 0
i.e.,
t_{q}\neq 0
and
t_{j}=0
for
j>q
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{1}\cap\dots\cap\ell_{q}\neq\emptyset
and denote this point as
p
. Since
t_{k-1}=t_{k}=0
, we have
q\leq k-2
.

Let us choose two lines
\ell_{j_{1}}
and
\ell_{j_{2}}
such that
j_{1},j_{2}\in\{q+1,\dots,k\}
and consider the lines
\ell_{1},\ell_{j_{1}},\ell_{j_{2}}
and
\ell_{2}
. We denote the intersection points of
\ell_{i}
and
\ell_{j}
as
p_{ij}
, where both
i
and
j
belong to
\{q+1,\dots,k\}
or one of
i,j
belongs to
\{1,\dots,q\}
and the other belongs to
\{q+1,\dots,k\}
.

If both
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{1}
and
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{2}
are empty,
\ell_{1},\ell_{j_{1}},\ell_{j_{2}},\ell_{2}
corresponds to an induced cycle
{\mathbf{C}}_{8}
in the following way.

Since
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{1}
is empty,
\ell_{1}
doesn’t pass through
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}
and
\ell_{j_{2}}
doesn’t pass through
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{1}\cap\ell_{j_{1}}
. Similarly,
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{2}=\emptyset
implies that
\ell_{2}
doesn’t pass through
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}
and
\ell_{j_{1}}
doesn’t pass through
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{2}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}
. Also by hypothesis,
\ell_{j_{1}}
and
\ell_{j_{2}}
don’t pass through
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{1}\cap\ell_{2}
. So,
\ell_{1}p_{1j_{1}}\ell_{j_{1}}p_{j_{1}j_{2}}\ell_{j_{2}}p_{j_{2}2}\ell_{2}p\ell_{1}
corresponds to an induced
{\mathbf{C}}_{8}
in the associated Levi graph.

If one of
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{1}
or
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{2}
is non-empty, say
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{2}
is non-empty, we replace
\ell_{2}
with
\ell_{3}
and
\ell_{1},\ell_{j_{1}},\ell_{j_{2}},\ell_{3}
corresponds to an induced
{\mathbf{C}}_{8}
in the associated Levi graph. Similarly, if
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{1}
is non-empty, we replace
\ell_{1}
with
\ell_{3}
and
\ell_{3},\ell_{j_{1}},\ell_{j_{2}},\ell_{2}
corresponds to an induced
{\mathbf{C}}_{8}
in that case.
∎

Example 4.4
.

Let

q

if

.

Proof of
(i)
:
We start from
\ell_{j_{1}}
and consider
\ell_{j_{1}}
and
\ell_{j_{2}}
as first two lines. Then we have
k-2
or
k-3

\ell_{j_{3}}
in
\mathcal{C}
depending on whether the intersection point of
\ell_{j_{1}}
and
\ell_{j_{2}}
is a double or triple point. So, there are at least
k-3
choices for
\ell_{j_{3}}
. If both
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{2}}
and
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{2}}\cap\ell_{j_{3}}
are triple points then we have
k-5
choices for
\ell_{j_{4}}
. Also, we have to remove the possibility of
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{3}}\cap\ell_{j_{4}}
being non-empty. So, there are at least
k-6
choices for
\ell_{j_{4}}
.

Similarly for
5\leq i\leq\lfloor{\frac{k+5}{3}}\rfloor
, if all of

are triple points then we get at least
k-{(i-1)+(i-2)}
choices for
\ell_{j_{i}}
. But it might happen that all of
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{1}}\cap\ell_{j_{t-1}},\dots,\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{i-3}}\cap\ell_{j_{i}}
are triple points. In that case, we remove

more choices for
\ell_{j_{i}}

and

\ell_{j_{i}}
. Now, for

there are at least

. That proves the existence of an induced cycle of length

for every

.

:

\ell_{j_{4}}
.

\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{4}}\cap\ell_{j_{1}}
,

or
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{j_{4}}\cap\ell_{j_{3}}
is a double point, then we have at least

choices for
\ell_{j_{5}}
. Otherwise, we can choose
\ell_{j_{5}}
such that

. Otherwise, similar to the previous case we can choose

such that

, …,

Supersolvable line arrangements:

A line arrangement
\mathcal{L}
is supersolvable if
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})
contains a modular point. We use the nations
\mu_{\mathcal{L}}
and
m_{\mathcal{L}}
for the number of modular points in
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})
and for maximum multiplicity of modular points respectively. A supersolvable line arrangement is called non-homogenous if all the modular points don’t have same multiplicity and is called
m
- homogeneous if all the modular points have the common multiplicity
m=m_{\mathcal{L}}
.

We start with a simple yet important observation.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a supersolvable line arrangement of
k
lines in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Then the associated Levi graph
G
does not have an induced cycle of maximum length i.e.
G
does not contain an induced cycle of length
2k
.

Proof.

First we assume that there exists an induced cycle of length
2k
. Let
\ell_{1}p_{12}\ell_{2}\dots\ell_{k}p_{k1}\ell_{1}
corresponds to an induced cycle of length
2k
in
G
where
p_{ij}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap L_{i}\cap L_{j}
.

Now let
p\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})
be a modular point. choose any element from the set
\{p_{i(i+1)},\,\,1\leq i\leq k-1,p_{k1}\}
, say
p_{12}
. Since
p
is a modular point, the line joining
p
and
p_{12}
is
\ell_{v}
for some
v\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. If
v=1
or
v=2
,
p=p_{12}
. Now, choose any point
p_{j(j+1)}
for some
j\in\{2,\dots,k\}
. Since,
p_{12}
is a modular point, the line joining
p_{12}
and
p_{j(j+1)}
is
\ell_{v_{1}}
for some
v_{1}\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. But,
p_{12}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{v_{1}}
implies that
v_{1}=1
or
v_{1}=2
. Hence,
p_{j(j+1)}
lies on either
\ell_{1}
or
\ell_{2}
which contradicts the fact that
\ell_{1}p_{12}\ell_{2}\dots\ell_{k}p_{k1}\ell_{1}
corresponds to an induced cycle of length
2k
.

If
v\neq 1,2
,
p_{12}\in\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{L})\cap\ell_{v}
, a contradiction to the assumption. Hence,
G
does not have an induced cycle of length
2k
.
∎

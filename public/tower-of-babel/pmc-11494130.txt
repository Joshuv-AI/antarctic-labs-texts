Study on bioequivalence and influence of obesity-related indicators on pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics for insulin degludec in healthy subjects
Authors: Rui Su, Lei Wan, Yi Tao, Mingxue Zhu, Junliang Pu, Zhongping Li, Yuan Chen, Chengyong Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to evaluate the bioequivalence between test and reference insulin degludec (IDeg) and the effects of body composition on pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of IDeg in Chinese healthy volunteers. In this randomized, open-label, crossover trial, 30 healthy Chinese males were assigned to receive a single subcutaneous dose (0.4 IU/kg) of each formulation under 24-h euglycemic hyperinsulinaemic clamp. Body compositions were analysed prior to administration and blood samples were collected at specific times. The 90% of primary pharmacokinetic parameters and 95% of primary pharmacodynamic parameters confidence intervals for the ratio of the least-squares geometric means were all in the range of 80–125%. As the fat content level increases, Cmax, AUC0−12 and GIR-AUC0−24 decreased whereas AUC24−96 increased sequentially. Therefore, the equivalence was demonstrated between test and reference, and in healthy Chinese volunteers, higher levels of adiposity are associated with slower rates of insulin absorption and distribution and the poorer glucose-lowering effect.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75554-3.

Introduction

Diabetes has posed a considerable public health challenge. The global prevalence of diabetes mellitus has reached 537 million in 2021 and is expected to reach 643 million by 2030, and 783 million by 20451. Inadequate glycaemic control in patients with diabetes exacerbates the progression of complications and the risk of cardiovascular death, resulting in a significant economic burden on the healthcare system1–5. Insulin therapy is indispensable to patients with type 1 diabetes and advanced type 2 diabetes for effectively maintain glycaemic control and prevent further complications6.

Insulin degludec (IDeg) developed by Novo Nordisk is an ultra-long-acting basal insulin analogue, first marketed in 2012 and approved for the treatment of type 1 and 2 diabetes mellitus. With a unique molecular structure, IDeg has an extremely long duration of action: in vivo half-life of 25.4 h, glucose-lowering effect of more than 24 h with stable efficacy7. Several randomized clinical trials (RCTs) have demonstrated that IDeg exhibits a steadier pharmacokinetic profile and a lower day-to-day variability in the glucose-lowering effect and lower rates of hypoglycemia among patients compared with insulin glargine U1008,9.

Global spending on diabetes care was reported to be at least $760 billion in 201910. Since type 1 and chronic type 2 diabetes requires almost lifelong insulin use, this cost present a serious financial burden on diabetes patients and their families. High-quality bioequivalent formulations, approved based on rigorous demonstration of similarity to approved reference products, are important therapeutic options for a wider range of patients11. The hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic glucose clamp, antagonizing blood glucose (BG) -lowering effect of an insulin by glucose infused, clamps BG at a predefined target. There is general agreement that euglycaemic hyperinsulinaemic clamp studies play a central role in the assessment of similar efficacy of the biosimilar and the reference insulin12.

Accordingly, we propose to conduct an euglycemic hyperinsulinaemic Clamp trial in healthy Chinese males to evaluate the bioequivalence between generic and brand-named insulin degludec. As healthy populations usually exhibit lower intra-individual variability and eliminate confounding factors, the characteristics of pharmaceutical preparations are fully represented. Therefore, we further explored whether obesity influences the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of IDeg based on the body composition of the subjects.

As only one principal component was obtained, a one-way linear regression analysis was performed with that principal component as the independent variable and GIR-AUC0−24 as the dependent variable. Influence of fat content on pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

FC value, the principal component, was calculated for every subjects. The subjects were subsequently categorized into groups FC level 1, FC level 2 and FC level 3 based on quantile classification method. The PK and PD parameters in different FC level groups were presented in Table 6. Statistical differences in Cmax, Tmax, AUC0−12, AUC24−96, GIRmax and GIR-AUC0−24 were found between FC level groups. AUC0−12 was significantly different between each of two groups. With higher FC levels, there was a tendency for Cmax, AUC0−12, GIRmax and GIR-AUC0−24 to decrease, whereas for Tmax, AUC24−96 to increase. No difference in AUClast, AUC0−∞, AUC12−24, t1/2, Vd/F, CL/F between the FC level groups. Figure 3a shows that after a single subcutaneous dose of IDeg, the group with a lower FC level, reached a higher Cmax more quickly and the blood concentration decreased more rapidly. Figure 3b visualizes the glucose-lowering effect (GIR-AUC0−24) were decreased from FC level 1 to the FC level 2 to the FC level 3.

Discussion

In this single-centre, single-dose, randomized, open-label, 2-period, 2-sequence, crossover bioequivalence study of test and reference IDeg in healthy Chinese male subjects, we have examined the bioequivalence of the reference and test IDeg preparations and investigated the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic effects of body composition, especially body fat content, on IDeg. The results of bioequivalence showed a similarity in the rate and extent of absorption and glucose-lowering activity of the two formulations, as depicted by the Cmax, AUClast and GIR-AUC0−24, GIRmax respectively. Through correlation analysis, principal component analysis and one-way ANOVA, results were obtained that group FC level 3 (with higher fat content) has lower Cmax, AUC0−12 and higher AUC24−96, GIRmax and AUC-GIR0−24 compare with group FC level 1 and FC level 2 in Chinese males.

The data showed that the 90% CIs for primary PK parameters (AUC0−24, AUClast and Cmax) and 95%CIs for PD parameters (GIR-AUC0−24 and GIRmax) of the geometric mean ratio (T/R) between test and reference formulations were within the range of 80–125%, which in accordance with the regulatory requirement12. The observed concentration and glucose infusion rate profiles and estimated PK and PD parameters are comparable to those reported previously for IDeg13–16. The AUC0−24 has a point estimate closer to 1 (namely 0.97) than the AUClast with a point estimate of 0.87, which may be related to the strict administration during the clamping of the subjects and thus the elimination of confounding factors. Regarding the selection of the study population, the EMA guidance on clinical development of biosimilar insulins recommends using either healthy subjects or type 1 diabetes patients to evaluate PK/PD bioequivalence of two formulations12. This study was designed in healthy subjects as they represent a homogeneous and sensitive population to detect functional differences between the two insulin formulations. Thus, serum C-peptide was measured in parallel to insulin concentrations throughout the experiment to estimate the extent and consistency of suppression of endogenous insulin, a reduction of about 50% for C-peptide levels indicates that effective suppression of endogenous insulin during the clamping period17. Meanwhile, according to Heise18, the BG can be close to the target, GIR should reflect the “true” PD-activity regardless of the clamp technique used. In our study, the CVBG was controlled at less than 5% in both the R and T groups. Previous literature19 has reported that insulin degludec has lower variability compared to insulin glargine, and the present study showed an overall lower intra-individual variability of IDeg, with only the GIR-AUC0−24 (31.90%) exceeding 30%.

The hyperinsulinaemic euglycaemic glucose clamp (HEGC) has always been regarded as the gold standard for the assessment of pharmacodynamic (PD) properties of insulin preparations. There are virtually no studies exploring the pharmacokinetic pharmacodynamic influences of insulin in humans based on the HEGC. In this paper, it was found by performing HEGC in healthy volunteers and incorporating the body composition factors that: Firstly, Weight, BMI, BF, BFP, VFA, WHR were all correlated with parts of the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic parameters, which factors are all related to obesity. Besides, there is a strong covariance between these variables, so principal component analysis (PCA)20, which converts multiple metrics into a small number of principal components and reflects most of the information in the original data, was used to reduce the dimensionality21. Ultimately, a principal component strongly associated with obesity was extracted, namely, fat content. Secondly, Cmax and AUC0−12 decreased sequentially from group FC level 1 to 2 to 3, whereas AUC24−96 increased sequentially from group FC level 1 to 2 to 3, suggesting that the fat content level affects insulin absorption as well as the distribution of insulin AUC, which may be related to the slowing down of the rate of the absorption and in vivo distribution process in patients with obesity. Several reports have shown that obesity is associated with delayed insulin absorption22,23. It’s been suggested that this may be related to increased skinfold thickness and decreased subcutaneous blood flow in patients with obesity24,25. And lastly, as the dose were weight normalized, neither AUClast nor AUC0−∞ were statistically different between three FC level groups, whereas glucose-lowering effect of insulin diminished gradually from group FC level 1 to 2 to 3. The effect (pharmacodynamic) of insulin is not always proportional to the dose or blood concentration26. For the same exposure and the difference in glucose-lowering effect between the three groups, which may be due to the positive correlation between obesity and insulin resistance27,28 or (and) to the characteristics (flatter or steeper) of the insulin PK curve26.

There are still some limitations in the current study. Firstly, this study was designed in healthy subjects, patients with diabetes may show different pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties from the subjects in our study. And as shown in supplementary table S1, there were differences in body compositions between the FC1, FC2, and FC3 groups, but the distribution was concentrated since they were healthy individuals. Thus, the effects of adiposity on PK and PD require further exploration in more obese subjects. In addition, this study was explored under single dosing, and further studies are needed to investigate the effect of body composition on the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamics of IDeg, as well as other types of insulin. However, there was a study has shown that after subcutaneous administration patients with a lower BMI were accompanied by faster absorption, clearance, and a stronger glucose-lowering effect, which might result in blood glucose fluctuation before next dose29. Therefore, our study is also instructive for the study of individualized and precise medication in special populations such as people with obesity.

In conclusion, the current study demonstrated bioequivalence between test and reference IDeg. Furthermore, in healthy Chinese volunteers, higher levels of adiposity are associated with slower rates of insulin absorption and distribution and the poorer glucose-lowering effect.

Materials and methods

Drugs

The test (T) formulation was insulin degludec injection (3mL:300IU) produced by Chia Tai Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd. (Batch number: AC20200401). The reference (R) drug, insulin degludec injection (3mL:300IU), was produced by Novo Nordisk (Batch number: JS67V57).

Subjects

The participants in this study were healthy Chinese males aged 18 ~ 45 years (both inclusive), with body mass index (BMI) in the range of 18 to 24 kg/m2 (both inclusive). All subjects had fasting plasma glucose (FPG) concentration of 3.9 ~ 6.1 mmol/L, and no abnormalities on routine oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), insulin releasing test (IRT). The inclusion criteria included no history of systemic diseases such as cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive, urinary, endocrine diseases or metabolic disorders; vital signs, physical examination, laboratory examination, electrocardiogram and imaging examination were normal or abnormal but with no clinical significance; primary exclusion criteria included allergy to insulin or other drugs and foods; participation in other clinical trials in the last 3 months; a history of significance use of alcohol (> 14 IU alcohol/week), cigarettes (> 5 cigarettes/day ), or abusive of drugs.

Study design

This single-centre, single-dose, randomized, open-label, 2-period, 2-sequence, crossover bioequivalence study of test and reference insulin degludec (IDeg) in healthy Chinese male subjects under fasting condition was registered on 05/06/2020 with registration NO. CTR20201129, which can be found at http://www.chinadrugtrials.org.cn. The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (Chongqing, China). All participants provided written informed consent before inclusion in the study. This trial was conducted in accordance with Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical principles.

Screened and eligible subjects were randomly allocated to the group A (T-R) or B (R-T). The allocation ratio of each group was 1:1. There was a 10 ~ 21 days washout period between two treatments. Subjects were admitted to the research unit at 18:00 the day before drug administration and had a standard meal. After fasted overnight (> 10 h), a 0.4 IU/kg single dose of IDeg was subcutaneously injected into the left abdominal (period 1) or right abdominal (period 2) wall of subjects.

Blood sampling

A 2 mL blood sample was collected at 0.5 h before dosing and 0.25, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 24, 36, 48, 72, 96 h after dosing to analyse the C-peptide level. A 4 mL blood sample was collected at 0.5 h before dosing and 0.25, 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 24, 36, 48, 72, 96 h after dosing for the analysis of IDeg concentration. The blood sample was immediately centrifuged at 2 ~ 8 ℃ with 1700 g for 10 min to separate plasma. The obtained plasma was collected and stored at − 80 ℃ until shipment for analysis.

Euglycemic hyperinsulinaemic clamp

The 24 h euglycemic hyperinsulinaemic Clamp, was performed on the morning of dosing day to evaluate the pharmacodynamics of the two preparations. Before administration, established venous assess to double arms of subjects, with one side used for blood collection and the other for infusion of 20% glucose solution. Individual’s basal blood glucose was obtained from the mean of three glucose measurements at 30, 20, and 10 min before administration. A blood sample below 0.5 mL was collected every 10 min from 0 to 12 h, every 20 min from 12 to 18 h, every 30 min from 18 to 24 h. and immediately analysed using an automatic glucose oxidase analyser Biosen C-line GP+ (EKF Diagnostics, Germany) with an intra-batch and inter-batch coefficient of variation (CV) were 2.01% and 1.56% respectively, and a measuring range of 0.5 to 50 mmol/L. The glucose infusion rate (GIR) was adjusted to maintain the subject’s blood glucose within ± 10% of the target blood which is equal to basal blood glucose minus 0.28 mmol/L.

Bioanalytical methods

Plasma samples were analysed at a qualified third-party testing institution. IDeg concentrations were analysed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Serum C-peptide concentrations were analysed by enzyme-linked immunoassay (ELISA).

Body composition analysis

After fasting overnight, body weight without heavy clothing, as well as body mass index (BMI), body fat (BF), body fat percentage (BFP), visceral fat area (VFA) and waist-hip ratio (WHR) were measured using body composition analyser (InBody, Korea).

Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic assessment

Pharmacokinetic parameters included the area under the curve (AUC) of IDeg concentration from time 0 to 24 h (AUC0−24), AUC from time 0 to the time of the last measurable concentration (AUClast), AUC from time zero to infinity (AUC0−∞), the peak concentration of IDeg (Cmax), as well as the area under the curve of IDeg for the specified time intervals, the time to Cmax (Tmax), terminal half-life(t1/2), apparent clearance (CL/F), apparent volume of distribution (Vd/F). The glucose-lowering effect is reflected by real-time changes GIR, and thus pharmacodynamic parameters include area under the GIR profile from 0 to 24 h (GIR-AUC0−24), maximum GIR value (GIRmax), and the time to GIRmax (GIR-Tmax). Primary PK endpoints were the area under the curve (AUC) of IDeg concentration from time 0 to 24 h (AUC0−24), AUC from time 0 to the time of the last measurable concentration (AUClast) and the peak concentration of IDeg (Cmax). Primary PD endpoints were the area under the glucose infusion rate (GIR) profile from 0 to 24 h (GIR-AUC0−24) and maximum GIR value (GIRmax). The PK and PD parameters value of all individuals were calculated using noncompartmental methods by Phoenix WinNonlin Version 8.1 (Certara, NJ Princeton, America).

Statistical analysis

The sample size is calculated based on a power calculation based on the intrasubject variability coefficient of variation (CV) and the T/R-ration of the metrics. According to this rule, at least 12 patients were required per group, considering the dropout, a total of 30 patients were included. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics 27.0 (IBM, Armonk, New York, America). Data were expressed as the means ± standard deviation or median values (1st quartile-3rd quartile). Primary PK and PD parameters were log-transformed and analysed using a linear mixed model to compare the bioequivalence of two formulations. The 90% CIs of AUClast, AUC0−∞, Cmax,95% CIs of GIR-AUC0−24, GIRmax for the ratio of the least-squares geometric means of the test and reference products were calculated. These 2 formulations were bioequivalent if the 90% CIs for primary PK parameters and 95% CIs for primary PD parameters of the ratios were within the range of 80–125%.

To show possible relations of body composition with PK and PD parameters of R formulation, Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated. Statistically significant variables were analysed by principal component analysis. The individuals were classified according to their principal component values, and differences in PK and PD parameters between groups were analysed using one-way ANOVA. And linear regression analysis was performed with principal components as independent variables and GIR-AUC0−24 as dependent. For all statistical analyses, P value < 0.05 was considered significant.

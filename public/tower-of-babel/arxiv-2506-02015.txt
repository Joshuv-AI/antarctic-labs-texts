Object-centric Self-improving Preference Optimization for Text-to-Image Generation
Yoonjin Oh, Yongjin Kim, Hyomin Kim, Donghwan Chi, Sungwoong Kim

Abstract
Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly improved both image understanding and generation capabilities. Despite these improvements, MLLMs still struggle with fine-grained visual comprehension, particularly in text-to-image generation tasks. While preference optimization methods have been explored to address these limitations in image understanding tasks, their application to image generation remains largely underexplored. To address this gap, we propose an O bject-centric S elf-improving P reference O ptimization ( OSPO ) framework designed for text-to-image generation by MLLMs. OSPO leverages the intrinsic reasoning abilities of MLLMs without requiring any external datasets or models. OSPO emphasizes the importance of high-quality preference pair data, which is critical for effective preference optimization. To achieve this, it introduces a self-improving mechanism that autonomously constructs object-level contrastive preference pairs through object-centric prompt perturbation, densification and VQA scoring. This process eliminates ambiguous or disproportionate variations commonly found in naively generated preference pairs, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of preference optimization. We validate OSPO on three representative compositional text-to-image benchmarks, demonstrating substantial performance gains over baseline models.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)
(

have witnessed rapid progress in recent years. Especially for vision-language MLLMs, early researches
(
liu2023llava
)
mainly focused on image understanding tasks like image captioning and visual question answering. Recent advancements
(
ge2023seedllama
;
sun2023emu1
;
sun2024emu2

wang2024emu3
;
chen2025janus
)
, however, have introduced unified models that can perform both understanding and generation of images. Their performances for text-to-image generation are currently competitive with diffusion based generative models
(
betker2023dalle3
;
chen2023pixart
;
podell2023sdxl
;
ramesh2022sdv15
)
. Despite these advancements, existing MLLMs still struggle with complex text inputs involving multiple objects or intricate object-attribute relationships. Such limitations often appear as object omissions or object-attribute misbindings in the generated outputs. For image understanding tasks, several studies
(
yu2024rlhfv
;
zhou2024povid
)
have addressed fine-grained hallucination problems using preference optimization methods. These approaches aim to align model outputs with human-preferred responses, which requires costly human-annotated preference pairs. To mitigate this dependency, recent research has explored AI-generated feedback from stronger models
(
yu2024rlaif
)
or self-generated feedback
(
chen2024spin
;
kim2025spa
)
to simulate human judgment effectively.

Although preference optimization methods have demonstrated significant progress in image understanding area, their application to image generation by MLLMs remains largely unexplored. This is primarily due to the lack of publicly available preference datasets for image generation tasks. Among the few studies
(
qu2024silmm
;
guo2025imggencotcvpr
)
that address preference optimization for text-to-image generation by MLLMs, some have attempted to mitigate this limitation through self-improving mechanisms. These approaches aim to guide MLLMs to generate image pairs that are faithfully aligned with the input text by leveraging MLLMs’ intrinsic comprehension capabilities. However, they primarily rely on a Best-of-N sampling strategy, which involves generating multiple candidate outputs for a single input and selecting the most or least preferred response. As illustrated in Figure
1
, this method is prone to semantic ambiguity and excessive variability, weakening the preference signal and leading to suboptimal learning outcomes. Additionally, this naive sampling process does not explicitly induce object-level variations, making it ineffective in addressing object-level hallucination issues.

To overcome these limitations, we propose an
O
bject-centric
S
elf-improving
P
reference
O
ptimization (
OSPO
) framework specifically designed to target object-level hallucinations in text-to-image generation. The framework consists of five steps, with a key innovation in the preference data generation process. Unlike a conventional Best-of-N sampling, our framework explicitly enforces object-level contrasts in outputs. To achieve this, we introduce an object-centric prompt perturbation strategy that manipulates the original prompt through swap, drop, and replace operations. In addition, we densify the prompt to preserve global semantic consistency while enriching its content and explicitly specifying all relevant objects and their attributes. This process reduces ambiguity and enables the model to generate images with greater fidelity and completeness. Finally, we apply VQA scoring to identify the preference strength of each preference image pair by answering local and global semantic questions. A pair with clear distinctions at the object level not only enhances the clarity of preference signals but also significantly improves the quality of the training data.
Remarkably, our framework demonstrates substantial performance improvements even with a single iteration, highlighting its efficiency and effectiveness in addressing fine-grained object-level hallucinations in text-to-image generation of MLLMs.

In summary, our key contributions are:

1.

Object-level Contrastive Image Pair Generation.

We propose a strategy for generating object-level contrastive image pairs through three key stages: object-centric prompt perturbation, pairwise prompt densification, and image generation.

2.

Self-VQA Scoring for Evaluating Preference Strength.

We introduce a novel scoring mechanism based on local and global self-VQA, which independently evaluates local contrast and global consistency to better capture preference strength of image pairs.

3.

Object-centric Self-improving Preference Optimization Framework.

Building upon (1) and (2), we present an OSPO framework that prioritizes object-level contrast in self-generated preference datasets. Notably, even with a single iteration, our framework achieves substantial performance improvements. Also it exhibited robustness to out-of-distribution inputs.

2.2
Object-centric Self-improving Preference Optimization Framework

Based on the findings in the preliminary experiment, we hypothesize that enhancing the model’s understanding of "Winning" versus "Losing" components at a fine-grained level can significantly amplify the strength of preference signals, thereby improving both the effectiveness and efficiency of optimization. To achieve this, we propose a training framework that emphasizes securing clear contrasts in fine-grained components while maintaining global semantic consistency. The framework is structured into five systematic steps, with a primary focus on constructing well-defined, object-centric contrastive pairs, as visualized in the overview shown in Figure
3
. All prompt templates and generation examples are provided in the Appendix.

Figure 3
:
Illustration of OSPO Framework: (1) Step 1: MLLM generates base text prompt sets. (2) Step 2: MLLM generates three negative prompts from each base prompt with three text perturbation strategies - swap, replace, and drop. Then, it generates dense prompts for each base, native prompt from three pairs. Consequently, we obtain three candidate dense prompt pairs. (3) Step 3: MLLM generates images from each candidate dense prompt pairs. (4) Step 4: MLLM generates decompositional questions from the original base prompt then evaluates each pair based on local/global self-VQA score. (5) Step 5: For alignment tuning, SimPO
meng2024simpo
is applied for MLLM.

2.2.1
Step 1: Prompt Generation

The first step involves generating input prompts that serve as the foundation for the entire training framework. Inspired by
(
qu2024silmm
)
and
(
huang2025t2icompbench
)
, we categorize prompt scenarios into four distinct types: Attribute, Layout, Non-Spatial, and Complex Composition. For each category, we generate 4,000 prompts using the model itself, ensuring a diverse and representative set of inputs.

For the Attribute and Layout categories, we explicitly generate attribute-related keywords such as color and spatial relationships alongside common objects. Then these components are systematically combined by predefined templates to construct structured prompt. On the other hand, for the Non-Spatial and Complex Composition categories, we leverage in-context Learning
(
dong2022icl
)
capabilities of MLLMs, utilizing few-shot examples to generate natural, contextually rich prompts that reflect intricate object interactions and compositional semantics.

This prompt generation strategy ensures structured and semantically diverse inputs tailored to the characteristics of each scenario, effectively covering a broad spectrum of compositional variations.

2.2.2
Step 2: Pairwise Prompt Perturbation

This step introduces a simple yet powerful approach for prompt perturbation, guided by two key strategies: negative prompt generation and pairwise prompt densification. These strategies are designed to induce clear object-level contrasts, which are essential for informative learning signals in preference optimization.

Negative Prompt Generation.

We hypothesize that directly generating Winning and Losing responses, each targeting specific types of object-level hallucination, can effectively guide the model in distinguishing between correct and incorrect outputs. For instance, a Winning response accurately captures all fine-grained semantics described in the prompt, while a Losing response may omit certain objects or incorrectly express object-attribute bindings. Constructing such preference pairs typically requires precise regional editing of image segments. However, current MLLMs exhibit limited capabilities in fine-grained image editing. To address this limitation, we introduce an alternative approach: instead of relying on post-hoc image editing, we induce localized semantic differences by generating Winning and Losing images independently from distinct, yet semantically aligned, text prompts. This pairwise generation strategy preserves global semantic coherence while introducing targeted object-level variations, effectively enhancing the contrast necessary for robust preference optimization.

To achieve this independent generation, we design a negative prompt generation strategy with three distinct techniques: (1) Swap, (2) Replace, and (3) Drop. These perturbations are generated through the in-context learning capabilities of MLLMs, guided by few-shot examples.

1.

Swap
: Exchanges the positions of Objects or Attributes within the prompt, inducing a different binding than the original prompt.

2.

Replace
: Substitutes an Object or Attribute in the prompt with another Object or Attribute not originally present in the prompt, creating a new combination.

3.

Drop
: Randomly removes an Object or Attribute from the prompt, introducing semantic incompleteness or ambiguity.

Pairwise Prompt Densification.

We observed that simply generating negative prompts through prompt perturbation and directly producing images from both the original and negative prompts did not reliably achieve our objective: generating two images that are globally consistent in context while exhibiting targeted local semantic differences. We identified that this limitation arises from the lack of detailed semantic guidance in the original input prompts.

To overcome this limitation, motivated by
(
yang2023idea2img
;
liao2025imagegen
)
, we introduce a prompt densification process designed to explicitly control the semantic elements that should remain consistent and those that should vary between the two images. This densification step also serves to enrich the input prompt, providing the model with sufficient contextual information for image generation. Importantly, we perform pairwise densification through in-context learning, ensuring that the resulting base and negative dense prompts are semantically aligned while maintaining the intended local differences. This strategy prevents the generation of excessively divergent or contextually irrelevant dense prompt pairs, ensuring that the semantic contrasts are both meaningful and controlled.

2.2.3
Step 3: Image Generation

Using the previously generated Dense Prompt Pairs, we produce the corresponding images. As outlined earlier, Dense Prompts are specifically crafted to guide the model in accurately interpreting and following textual instructions. This design enables the model to reliably generate a Winning image—where objects and attributes are correctly represented—and a Losing image—where object omissions or attribute misbindings are deliberately introduced.

For each data sample, we generate three candidate response pairs
(I_{w},I_{\ell})_{1}
,
(I_{w},I_{\ell})_{2}
and
(I_{w},I_{\ell})_{3}
corresponding to the three perturbation strategies. These response pairs are constructed from a shared base prompt paired with distinct negatively perturbed prompts for each strategy.

2.2.4
Step 4: VQA-based Preference Pair Selection

To select the most effective preference pair among the three candidate pairs, we adapt a Self-VQA strategy. In this approach, we generate targeted questions that evaluate each image’s global-level fidelity and local-level fidelity with respect to the complex input prompt. We further introduce a unique score
T
for each candidate pair, derived from the probabilities of the model’s responses.

Question Generation.

To precisely evaluate both local-level and global-level semantic fidelity, we utilize separate VQA questions for each aspect. Inspired by
(
qu2024silmm
;
yu2024rlaif
)
, local questions are generated using a divide-and-conquer strategy, where the original prompt is decomposed into atomic semantic units. This enables the construction of fine-grained evaluation questions that capture minimal semantic details. In addition, a global question is designed to assess the overall fidelity of the image with respect to the prompt, ensuring that the image holistically aligns with the intended semantics. Details of global question are provided in the Appendix. We generate local questions by in-context learning with few-shot examples.

Scoring.

Under the assumption that maintaining global semantic coherence while introducing targeted object-level contrast would be highly informative for preference learning, we introduce a quantitative metric called the Preference Strength Score. This score is computed for each preference pair
(I_{w},I_{\ell})
and serves as a reliable indicator for effective optimization.

For each pair, we firstly compute the local score
S_{local}
and global score
S_{global}
for each image. Each winning or losing image is assigned both scores, resulting in
S_{local}^{w}
,
S_{global}^{w}
,
S_{local}^{\ell}
and
S_{global}^{\ell}
.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q_{k}|}\sum_{q_{i}\in Q_{k}}\left[p(\text{"yes"}\mid I,q_{i})-p(\text{"no"}\mid I,q_{i})\right],\quad k\in\{\text{local},\text{global}\},

(1)

where
Q_{\text{local}}
and
Q_{\text{global}}
denote the sets of local and global questions, respectively, and
q_{i}
represents an individual question within the corresponding set, and
|Q_{k}|
indicates the total number of questions in the set
Q_{k}
. Furthermore,
p(\text{"yes"}\mid I,q_{i})
and
p(\text{"no"}\mid I,q_{i})
correspond to the model’s probabilities of responding "yes" or "no" for image
I

q_{i}
.

Second, we calculate the score difference
\delta_{k}=S_{k}^{w}-S_{k}^{\ell}
separately for the local and global scores, resulting in two values:
\delta_{local}
and
\delta_{global}
. These represent the semantic gaps between the winning and losing images within the pair for each respective aspect. After completing the above computations for all pairs, the final pair index
i^{*}
used for training is determined according to the following:

\displaystyle T_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\text{local}}^{i}/\Delta_{\text{local}}}{\delta_{\text{global}}^{i}/\Delta_{\text{global}}},\quad i\in[1,2,3],~~i^{*}=\argmax_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}T_{i},

(2)

where
\Delta_{local}

\max(\delta_{\text{local}}^{1},\delta_{\text{local}}^{2},\delta_{\text{local}}^{3})

\Delta_{global}

\max(\delta_{\text{global}}^{1},\delta_{\text{global}}^{2},\delta_{\text{global}}^{3})

The scoring mechanism is designed to select the pair in which the winning and losing images exhibit a substantial contrast in local semantics while maintaining minimal disparity in global semantics.

2.2.5
Step 5: Training

In this step, the model is trained using selected pairs, leveraging the semantic differences between preferred and non-preferred images to enhance fine-grained alignment. SimPO
(
meng2024simpo
)
is applied to the MLLM according to the following training objective.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{simpo}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_{w},y_{\ell})\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log\sigma\left(\frac{\beta}{|y_{w}|}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{w}|x)-\frac{\beta}{|y_{\ell}|}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\ell}|x)-\gamma\right)\right],

(3)

where
\pi_{\theta}
represents our MLLM and
x
,
y_{w}
,
y_{\ell}
represent the input text, output winning image, losing image from the self-generated preference dataset
\mathcal{D}

\sigma

\gamma

\beta

Experiment Setup

Model.

We conducted experiments using Janus-Pro-7B

Dataset.

We generated a total of 16,000 text prompt datasets evenly distributed across four different categories (Attribute, Layout, Non-spatial, Complex Composition) using

chen2025janus

Benchmark.

We evaluated the model’s fine-grained Text-to-Image generation capabilities on three established compositional benchmarks: T2I-CompBench++

1.

T2I-CompBench++

It evaluates compositional image generation performances with 8,000 text prompts, structured into four primary categories: Attribute, Layout, Non-spatial, and Complex Composition. It further includes eight sub-categories, each with distinct evaluation criteria.

2.

DPGBench

It evaluates compositional image generation performances with 1,065 long and semantically rich text prompts, with an average token length of 83.91. This benchmark is designed to measure models’ ability to align with complex semantic structures in extended narratives.

3.

GenEval

It evaluates object-focused image generation performances with 550 text prompts, structured into six primary categories: Single Object, Two Object, Counting, Colors, Position, and Attribute Binding. The text prompt format is completely different from
T2I-CompBench++.

Training Details.

We improved the efficiency of training by employing a well-known efficient optimization method, LoRA
(
hu2022lora
)
. Detailed training hyperparameters are provided in the Appendix.

To investigate the fundamental elements contributing to the performance improvements by the proposed framework, we conducted a series of ablation studies. Specifically, we evaluated the contributions of three key strategies: Negative Prompt Generation, Pairwise Prompt Densification, and VQA Scoring. These ablations quantify the individual contributions of each component and assess their combined impact on performances. Table
3
shows the results of each benchmark under different ablation settings, and example preference pairs for each setting are provided in the Appendix.

Negative prompt generation enhances local contrast and improves overall performances, with pronounced effects in high-contrast categories.

In Experiment (a), the dataset was generated solely from the original dense prompts, where both the winning and losing images are derived from the same semantic prompt. This setting led to a notable performance decline across most categories, underscoring the importance of generating winning and losing images from fine-grained, semantically distinct prompts. This effect was particularly evident in the Attribute category, where the contrast between preference pairs is more pronounced. This observation suggests that negative prompt generation is especially crucial for capturing subtle distinctions when the attribute-based differences are inherently larger. In contrast, categories with naturally lower contrast showed a smaller performance drop, highlighting the dependence of negative prompt generation’s effectiveness on inherent semantic divergence. Supporting evidences are in the Appendix.

Pairwise prompt densification amplifies semantic richness and boosts preference optimization.

In Experiment (b), the dataset was generated directly from the original and negative prompts without being converted into dense prompts. This ablation reveals that the absence of prompt densification reduces the semantic diversity and contrast, thereby weakening the model’s ability to distinguish fine-grained differences between preference pairs.

VQA scoring contributes marginally to performance when sufficient contrast is established.

In Experiment (c), each preference pair in the dataset is randomly selected, without considering their VQA scores. The results indicate that when sufficient contrast is ensured during generation, the impact of VQA scoring on final performances is minimal. This suggests that VQA scoring primarily enhances model effectiveness when natural contrast between generated responses is less explicit, acting as a secondary mechanism to enforce discriminative learning. Additionally, experimental results applying the VQA scoring strategy proposed in
(
qu2024silmm
)
framework are provided in the Appendix.

4.2
Preference Optimization

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)
(
ouyang2022rlhf
)
has emerged as a cornerstone for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human values. Within the RLHF framework, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
(
rafailov2023dpo
)
has proven to be an effective strategy for fine-tuning policy models, leveraging a reference model to guide preference-based adjustments without the need for a separate reward model.

4.3
Compositional Text-to-Image Generation

Despite significantly enhanced generation ability of MLLMs, it is still challenging to faithfully reflect complex prompts. While substantial research has been dedicated to addressing these limitations in diffusion models
(
feng2022compositional1
;
lian2023compositional2
;
liu2022compositional3
)
, there remains a noticeable gap in the literature concerning MLLMs, which have yet to be extensively studied for these challenges.

A recent attempt to tackle this problem in MLLMs
(
qu2024silmm
)
uses a self-improving approach with a simple Best-of-N strategy, generating multiple images from a single prompt and selecting preference pairs. However, without explicit control over object-level semantics, it fails to effectively resolve object-level hallucinations. In contrast, our framework perturbs prompts to construct object-level preference pairs, enabling more precise supervision and better handling of complex object compositions.

5
Discussion

While our framework reduces object-level hallucinations, it can sometimes harm global semantic coherence. To address this, we incorporate balanced VQA scoring between local and global semantics. Future work could further explore this interplay and develop methods that better align both levels of semantic, enabling more coherent and effective text-to-image synthesis. On the other hand, our framework is inherently dependent on the underlying model as it is solely responsible for all steps in the framework. Weak performance can arise from limited capabilities of the model. However, we observed that these limitations can be mitigated through iterative training. With Unified MLLMs rapidly advancing, our framework can be flexibly applied across different model architectures and has the potential to extend beyond text-to-image generation to other modalities such as video or 3D content synthesis.

6
Conclusion

We present a self-improving object-centric preference optimization framework for text-to-image generation in MLLMs. Our framework leverages the unified multimodal processing capabilities of MLLMs, enhancing their performances without the need for external datasets or additional models. By perturbing prompts to explicitly introduce object-level contrast and generating its own preference pairs, our framework effectively self-improves the model’s ability to capture intricate object attributes and spatial relationships. This study marks a foundational step towards advancing unified multimodal learning, paving the way for more faithful image generation in MLLMs.

Appendix B
Detailed Evaluation Strategy in Preliminary Experiment

In Section
2.1
, we conducted a preliminary experiment on constructing the preference data using Best-of-N sampling strategy. For image generation, we adopted Classifier Free Guidance decoding with a guidance weight of 5.0 and a default temperature of 1.0. Decompositional VQA was used for the main evaluation method in assessing fine-grained text fidelity of a generated image. Specifically, all questions are generated by decomposing the original prompt. Each question is designed to be answered with either “Yes” or “No”, where the correct answer is always “Yes” if the image aligns with the prompt well. For example, given the prompt
“A bright green bird,”
the corresponding questions would include:
“Is there a bird?,”

“Is the bird green?,”

“Is the bird bright?,”
and so on. These questions were generated by the evaluation model Qwen2.5-VL-7B
[
22
]
using the prompts provided in Appendix
F.4.1
. The answers to each question were also obtained using the same model
[
22
]
.

To assess fidelity, we compared the responses for each question between the winning and losing images. If both images yielded the same response for the same question, we considered them to have an identical local semantic fidelity for that specific aspect of the prompt. We define this scenario as an
Indistinguishable Semantic Case
, which is further categorized into the following three types:

1.

All-Yes Case
: Both images answer “Yes” to all questions. This indicates that both images faithfully follow the text prompt without observable semantic differences.

2.

All-No Case
: Both images answer “No” to all questions. This implies that neither image accurately reflects the text prompt.

3.

All-Same Answer in Same Question Case
: Both images yield identical answers for each corresponding decomposed question. This suggests that they share highly similar local semantic content—both in terms of what they successfully depict and what they consistently fail to represent. This case is distinct from the All-Yes and All-No cases, as it covers all other scenarios where the two images are partially correct or incorrect in exactly the same way.

We classified these Indistinguishable Semantic Cases as non-informative pair samples for preference learning, as they do not exhibit distinct differences in local semantic fidelity. Preference optimization relies on the margin between the reward assigned to the winning response and that of the losing response. In this context, the absence of a discernible fine-grained level semantic gap between image pairs reduces the informativeness of the sample, thereby hindering optimal training toward the goal of improving compositional text-to-image generation.

F.2

To generate negative prompts, we leveraged the step-by-step reasoning capabilities of the MLLM. Given an input prompt, the model first identified the subject within the sentence and then bound relevant attributes to the subject. Based on this structure, negative prompts were created using three types of operations: replace, swap, and drop. The replace operation substituted an attribute with another one not originally present in the prompt. The swap operation exchanged two or more attributes within the same prompt. The drop operation randomly removed an attribute, resulting in partial or incomplete descriptions. The specific prompt templates used are detailed below.

Always ensure the output has opposing or conflicting semantics compared to the input. Do not just reorder or repeat the input.

Appendix H
Broader Impacts

Our research is dedicated to exploring the possibilities of image generation by MLLMs, with a particular focus on compositional text-to-image generation. By enabling models to synthesize coherent visual scenes that closely reflect fine-grained human intent, our work advances controllability and fidelity in image generation. However, these capabilities also introduce ethical and societal risks. The ability to generate highly specific and realistic images can be misused to produce harmful, deceptive, or unethical content, including fabricated events or manipulated depictions of individuals. The stronger the alignment with user intent, the greater the risk of malicious exploitation. To support responsible development, we emphasize the need for safeguards such as bias auditing or prompt moderation, ensuring that MLLM-based image generation benefits society without enabling harm.

Semiclassical localization of Schrödinger’s eigenfunctions
Sébastien Campagne

Abstract
. This article addresses the microlocalization of eigenfunctions for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator − h 2 ​ Δ + V -h^{2}\Delta+V on closed Riemann surfaces with real bounded potentials. Our primary aim is to establish quantitative bounds on the spatial concentration of these eigenfunctions, extending classical results, typically restricted to smooth potentials, to the more general case where the potential is merely bounded. Our main result provides an explicit exponential bound for the L 2 L^{2} -norm of eigenfunctions on the entire surface in terms of their L 2 L^{2} -norm on an arbitrary open subset with an exponential weight of C ​ h − 1 ​ log ⁡ ( h ) 2 Ch^{-1}\log(h)^{2} . This bound improves upon previous estimates for non-smooth potentials that was an exponential weight of C h − 4 / 3 Ch^{-4/3} . Our proof is based on a recent approach of the Landis conjecture develop by 14 .



1.
Introduction

In this article, we study the localization properties of eigenfunctions
u_{h}
for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator
P_{V}
defined on a Riemann surface
(M,g)
. Specifically, we consider the equation:

(1.1)

(P_{V}-E)u_{h}:=(-h^{2}\Delta_{g}+V-E)u_{h}=0

where
E\in I
is an energy level ranging over a compact set
I\subset\mathbb{R}
and
V\in L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
is a real-valued bounded potential. Our goal is to understand how the geometry of
M
and the regularity of the potential
V
affect the spatial concentration of
u_{h}
in the semiclassical limit
h\rightarrow 0
.

When the potential
V
is smooth, it is well known that one can control the global behaviour of eigenfunctions
u_{h}
using their values on any open subset
U\subset M
. More precisely, for any energy
E\in I
, there is a constant
C>0
such that, for
h
small enough, any eigenfunction
u_{h}
of
P_{V}
with energy
E
satisfies:

(1.2)

\int_{M}\Big(|u_{h}|^{2}+|h\nabla u_{h}|^{2}\Big)d\mu_{g}\leq e^{C/h}\int_{U}\Big(|u_{h}|^{2}+|h\nabla u_{h}|^{2}\Big)d\mu_{g}.

where
\mu_{g}
is the volume form on
(M,g)
.
This estimate relies on analytic tools, in particular Carleman estimates. More precisely, for any fixed radius
R>0
and energy
E\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

(1.3)

\int_{B(0,R)}\Big(|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\Big)dx\leq e^{C/h}\int_{B(0,R)}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2}dx

for
h
small enough and for all
u
smooth function with compact support in the ball
B(0,R)
of the plan
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. See, for instance
12
or in the book
13
.

What can we say when the potential is not smooth?
In dimension one, we can recover the same result when
V\in L^{\infty}
, see for instance
7
in the proof of theorem 2.32. We can do even better for certain classes of potentials thanks to the Agmon distance
(
11
)
.
In dimension
\geq 2
, Lipschitz regularity is sufficient for (
1.2
); see for example
5
,
18
or
7
. In the general case,
9
showed that if
V\in L^{\infty}
, then

(1.4)

\int_{B(0,R)}\Big(|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\Big)dx\leq e^{C/h^{4/3}}\int_{B(0,R)}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2}dx.

for
h
small enough and for all
u
smooth function with compact support in
B(0,R)
. This result was then improved by
19
for Hölder potentials.
In
4
, inspired by the work of
19
, we proved a new estimate for eigenfunctions on Riemann surfaces that takes into account the regularity of
V
: In the same conditions as
1.2
with
V
bounded and not necessarily smooth, one has

(1.5)

\int_{M}\Big(|u_{h}|^{2}+|h\nabla u_{h}|^{2}\Big)d\mu_{g}\leq e^{C\beta(h)}\int_{U}\Big(|u_{h}|^{2}+|h\nabla u_{h}|^{2}\Big)d\mu_{g},

where

(1.6)

\beta(h)=\frac{1}{h^{4/3}}\sup_{x_{0}\in M}\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},h^{2/3}\kappa)}|V(x)-V(x_{0})|^{1/2},

and
\kappa>0
is a fixed small constant. This result gives us a continuous control between Lipschitz potentials (
\beta(h)\simeq h^{-1}
) and only bounded potentials (
\beta(h)\simeq h^{-4/3}
).

Our goal in this article is to improve (
1.4
) by using a recent approach developed by
14
to study a similar problem. We show the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemann surface with the volume form
\mu_{g}
, and let
U\subset M
be an open subset. Let
E\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
, and assume that
u_{h}\in L^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
satisfies

(-h^{2}\Delta_{g}+V-E)u_{h}=0,

with
V\in L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Then there exist constants
C>0
and
h_{0}>0
, independent of
u_{h}
, such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
, we have

(1.7)

\int_{M}\Big(|u_{h}|^{2}+|h\nabla u_{h}|^{2}\Big)d\mu_{g}\leq\exp\left(C\frac{\log(h)^{2}}{h}\right)\int_{U}\Big(|u_{h}|^{2}+|h\nabla u_{h}|^{2}\Big)d\mu_{g}.

We note that the exponent
2
on the logarithm could probably be improved with further work.

The work of
14
was originally focus on the Landis conjecture.
As a reminder, the Landis conjecture (see
10
) deals with how quickly solutions
u\neq 0
to

(1.8)

(-\Delta+V)u=0

can decay at infinity when
V
is bounded.
In the 1960s, Landis conjectured that non-zero solutions cannot decay faster than exponentially. This was disproven by
15
for complex potentials: he found a complex potential and a solution that decay faster than
e^{-C|x|^{4/3}}
in dimension
2
. For real potentials, the conjecture remains open, but recent work of
14
shows that solutions cannot decay faster than roughly
e^{-CR\log(R)^{1/2}}
in dimension
2
.
Their proof involves removing holes in the domain to increase the control of how functions oscillate by reducing the Poincaré constant. Then they use quasi-conformal mappings to transform the problem into one about harmonic functions on a domain with holes.

A key result from their work is as follows:

Let
(D_{j})_{j}
be a collection of disjoint unit balls with mutual separation at least 100, and let
R^{\prime}>100
. Then for all harmonic function
\alpha
on
B(0,R^{\prime})\setminus\bigcup_{j}D_{j}
such that it does not change sign on
(B(0,R^{\prime})\cap 5D_{j})\setminus D_{j}
. We get

(1.9)

\int_{B(0,R^{\prime})\setminus(B(0,R^{\prime}/2)\bigcup_{j}(3D_{j}))}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx\geq e^{-CR^{\prime}\log(R^{\prime})}\int_{B(0,R^{\prime}/2)\setminus\bigcup_{j}(3D_{j})}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx.

To go deeper,
8
did a complete survey of Landis’ conjecture.

The outline of this article is as follows. In Section
2
, we begin by applying the uniformization theorem to closed Riemann surfaces. This allows us to simplify the problem by working within a ball, thus avoiding the complexities of the original manifold (Section
2.1
). We then adapt some classical results about the Schrödinger operator from
14
to the semiclassical Schrödinger operator framework (Section
2.2
). The preliminaries conclude with a discussion on hole cuttings within the ball framework (Section
2.3
).

Section
3
is dedicated to transforming eigenfunctions of the Schrödinger operator into harmonic functions. This process unfolds in three steps: (1) reduction to a divergence-free equation (Section
3.1
), (2) application of quasiconformal mappings to reduce the divergence-free equation to a harmonic problem (Section
3.2
), (3) analysis of the geometric impact of these mappings on our framework (Section
3.3
).

In Section
4
, we build upon a result from
14
concerning harmonic functions to derive a statement for Schrödinger’s eigenfunctions. We examine how quasiconformal mapping reverses geometric transformations, enabling us to transfer estimates back to the Schrödinger’s eigenfunctions at the expense of space distortions(Section
4.1
). To control the induced deformations, we employ some analytic tools (Section
4.2
). Finally, to address the holes arising from the transformation of the punctured ball, we use Carleman estimates on shrinking sets. This allow us to bound exponential error terms in the statement on Schödinger’s eigenfunctions (Section
4.3
).

The proof is concluded in Section
5
, where we synthesize all preceding results.

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper, we study the eigenfunction
u_{h}\in L_{loc}^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
associated with the Schrödinger equation on a closed Riemannian surface
(M,g)
:

(-h^{2}\Delta_{g}+V-E)u_{h}=0\quad\text{on }(M,g),

where
V\in L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
and
E\in I
, with
I
a compact interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Let
U
be an open subset of
M
and
\mu_{g}
the volume form on
(M,g)
.

Notation:
To avoid overloading notations, we will use the symbol
\lesssim
to indicate an order of inferiority to within a universal multiplicative constant factor, and we will use this symbol
\simeq
to indicate that two quantities are equivalent.

2.1.
Reduction of the problem

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian surface. By the Poincaré uniformization theorem,
M
is conformally equivalent to a unique closed surface with constant curvature
(M,g_{0})
: there is
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
positive such that
g=\phi g_{0}
(see for instance the book of
6
). Therefore,
u_{h}
is solution of

(-h^{2}\phi^{-1}\Delta_{g_{0}}+V-E)u_{h}=0\quad\text{on }(M,g_{0})

and the measures
\mu_{g}
and
\mu_{g_{0}}
are equivalent.
The surface
(M,g_{0})
is a quotient of one of the following covering surfaces under the free action of a discrete subgroup of its isometry group:

•

the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(0 curvature),

•

the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(1 curvature),

•

the hyperbolic disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}
(-1 curvature) (see Figure
1
).

Figure 1.
Hyperbolic’s disk paved by pentagon (code source
17
)

Consequently,
(M,g_{0})
can be viewed as a compact quotient
\tilde{M}
of one of these three surfaces with the inherited metric, and
U
as an open subset of
\tilde{M}
. These three surfaces can be covered by
\tilde{M}
via the free action of the discrete subgroup. We can then view the function
u_{h}
as a function
u_{per,h}
on the covering which is periodic by the free action. Moreover,
u_{per,h}
is a solution of the "periodized" Schrödinger equation on the covering:

(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}:=(-h^{2}\phi_{per}^{-1}\Delta_{g_{0}}+V_{per}-E)u_{per,h}=0.

This reduces our analysis to just three cases:

•

Case of the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
: We can replace
U
with a small ball
B(0,R)
centered at
0
inside
U
. It is then sufficient to work in a larger ball
B(0,R_{0})
containing
\tilde{M}
. On this ball,
\mu_{g_{0}}
is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure and
u_{per,h}
is solution of the equation

(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}:=(-h^{2}\tilde{\phi}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{per,h}=0.

with
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{B(0,R_{0})})
positive.

•

Case of the hyperbolic disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}
: We can replace
U
with a small ball
B(0,R)
centered at
0
inside
U
. It is then sufficient to work in a larger ball
B(0,R_{0})
within the disk. In fact, the Poincaré disk is equipped with the metric

ds^{2}=\frac{4\sum_{i}dx_{i}^{2}}{(1-\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2}},

so on
B(0,R_{0})
, the metric is equivalent to an Euclidean metric. Consequently,
\mu_{g_{0}}
is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure and
\Delta_{g_{0}}=\frac{(1-\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2}}{4}\Delta
with
\Delta
the Euclidean Laplacian. Thus
u_{per,h}
is solution of the equation

(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}:=(-h^{2}\tilde{\phi}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{per,h}=0.

with
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{B(0,R_{0})})
positive. Thus this case can be treated similarly to the Euclidean case.

•

Case of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
: We can replace
U
with a small ball
B(R)
centered at the South Pole of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and consider
\tilde{M}
as
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We can then remove a small ball
B(R/2)
centered on the South Pole inside
B(R)
. The perforated sphere can unfold onto a ball
B(0,R_{0})
with the stereographic projection on the South Pole (see Figure
2
). Thus,
B(R)\setminus B(R/2)
becomes an annulus
A(0,R,R_{0})
at the edge of
B(0,R_{0})
. The metric on this projection is

ds^{2}=\frac{4\sum_{i}dx_{i}^{2}}{(1+\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2}},

so on
B(0,R_{0})
, the metric is equivalent to an Euclidean metric. Consequently,
\mu_{g_{0}}
is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure and
\Delta_{\tilde{g}_{0}}=\frac{(1+\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2}}{4}\Delta
with
\Delta
the Euclidean Laplacian. Thus
u_{per,h}
is solution of the equation

(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}:=(-h^{2}\tilde{\phi}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{per,h}=0.

with
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{B(0,R_{0})})
positive.

Figure 2.
Stereographic projection of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

We have reduce these three initial geometric cases to a single unified framework:
a ball
B(0,R_{0})
in a reference space (the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
after transformation) with the Lebesgue measure. On
B(0,R_{0})
, we study
u_{per,h}
solution of the equation

(2.1)

(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}:=(-h^{2}\tilde{\phi}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{per,h}=0

with
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{B(0,R_{0})})
positive,
V_{per}\in L^{\infty}(B(0,R_{0}),\mathbb{R})
,
E\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
compact interval. The function
u_{per,h}\in L_{loc}^{1}
is "periodic" and it takes all its values on a set
\tilde{M}
inside
B(0,R_{0})
.

In this framework, two subcases arise for the open set
U
:

•

U
is identified with a ball
B(0,R)
centered at
0
and contained within
B(0,R_{0})
(interior case).

•

U
is identified with an annulus
A(0,R,R_{0})
located at the edge of
B(0,R_{0})
(boundary case).

In the equation
2.1
, the function
\tilde{\phi}
is smooth and bounded by two positive constant on
B(0,R_{0})
. Consequently
u_{per,h}
is solution of

(-h^{2}\Delta+\tilde{V})u_{per,h}=0

with
\tilde{V}=\frac{V_{per}-E}{\tilde{\phi}}\in L^{\infty}
. Thus, for sake of simplicity, we will replace
\tilde{\phi}
by
1
. This will have no impact on the proof.

2.2.
Some lemmas

Before we proceed, we need to adapt the statements from
14
to our semiclassical framework. Complete proofs (if they are not redone) can be found in their article.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}=0
on an open set
\Omega_{h}
in the sense of distributions and
u_{per,h}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
, then
u_{per,h}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
.

This lemma is local, so it can be proved with the help of the fundamental solution of
\Delta
(see
14
, Fact
6.5
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega_{h}
be an open subset of
B(0,R_{0})
with Poincaré constant
k_{h}^{2}
. Suppose that

h^{-2}k_{h}^{2}\|V_{per}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}\ll 1.

Then, there exists a weak solution
\varphi_{h}
to the equation
(P_{V,per}-E)\varphi_{h}=0
on
\Omega_{h}
such that
\varphi_{h}=1+\tilde{\varphi}_{h}
, where

\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h}),\quad\|\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch^{-2}k_{h}^{2}\|V_{per}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}.

To prove this, we rewrite the operator in the form
-\Delta+h^{-2}(V_{per}-E)
and apply Lemma
3.2
from
14
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega_{h}
be a bounded open set and let
f_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\bar{\Omega}_{h})
be a function such that
f_{h}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{h}
. Then,
f_{h}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h})
.

See Lemma
6.13
of
14
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u_{h}
be a solution to
(-h^{2}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{h}=0
on a ball
B(x,r_{h})
of
B(0,R_{0})
, where
r_{h}<r_{0,h}=r_{0}\times h
with
r_{0}
a small constant independent of
h
and
u_{h}
. If
u_{h}
is continuous and
u_{h}>0
on
\partial B(x,r_{h})
, then
u_{h}>0
on
B(x,r_{h})
.

Proof.

We can assume that
u_{h}
is greater than a positive constant
\delta_{h}
on
\partial B(x,r_{h})
. Consider the set
\Omega_{h}=\{x\in B(x,r_{h}):u_{h}(x)<\delta_{h}/2\}
. This is an open subset strictly contained in
B(x,r_{h})
. If
u_{h}
is not positive in
B(x,r_{h})
, then
\Omega_{h}
is non-empty. Since
u_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega_{h})
by Lemma
2.1
and
u_{h}=\delta_{h}/2
on
\partial\Omega_{h}
, we deduce from Lemma
2.3
that
(u_{h}-\delta_{h}/2)\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h})
.

Since
\Omega_{h}\subset B(x,r_{h})
,
\Omega_{h}
has a Poincaré constant of
Cr_{h}^{2}
. For sufficiently small
r_{h}
, Lemma
2.2
guarantees the existence of
\varphi_{h}=1+\tilde{\varphi}_{h}
, where
\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h})
and
\|\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1/2
, such that
-h^{2}\Delta\varphi_{h}+(V_{per}-E)\varphi_{h}=0
on
\Omega_{h}
.

Then, the function
g_{h}=(\delta_{h}\varphi_{h}/2-u_{h})
belongs to
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h})
and satisfies
-h^{2}\Delta g_{h}+(V_{per}-E)g_{h}=0
. For any
\beta_{h}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{h})
,

h^{2}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla g_{h}\cdot\nabla\beta_{h}=-\int_{\Omega_{h}}(V_{per}-E)g_{h}\beta_{h}.

Taking the limit
\beta_{h}\rightarrow g_{h}
in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h})
, we obtain:

h^{2}\int_{\Omega_{h}}|\nabla g_{h}|^{2}=-\int_{\Omega_{h}}(V_{per}-E)g_{h}^{2}\leq\|V_{per}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega_{h}}g_{h}^{2}\leq\|V_{per}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}Cr_{h}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{h}}|\nabla g_{h}|^{2}.

For
r_{h}<h(C\|V_{per}-E\|_{L^{\infty}})^{-1/2}
, this inequality holds only if
g_{h}=0
. Thus,
u_{h}=\delta_{h}\varphi_{h}/2
on
\Omega_{h}
. Since
\|\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1/2
, we have
u_{h}>\delta_{h}/4
on
\Omega_{h}
, and hence on
B(x,r_{h})
.
∎

3.
From Schrödinger to Harmonic equation

We recall that
u_{per,h}
is a solution of

(P_{V,per}-E)u_{per,h}:=(-h^{2}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{per,h}=0,

where
V_{per}
is a potential in
L^{\infty}(B(0,R_{0}),\mathbb{R})
and
E\in I
is a compact interval of
\mathbb{R}
.
The aim of this section is to reduce the study of Schrödinger eigenfunctions to that of harmonic functions. First, we reduce the problem to a divergence-free equation, and then we use the theory of quasi-conformal mappings to transform it into a harmonic function problem.

3.1.
Divergence type equation on the perforated space

We recall that by Lemma
2.2
, if
\epsilon(h)
is sufficiently small (and consequently, the Poincaré constant of
\Omega_{h}
is also small), then there exists a weak solution
\varphi_{h}
to the equation
(-h^{2}\Delta+V_{per}-E)\varphi_{h}=0
on
\Omega_{h}
such that
\varphi_{h}=1+\tilde{\varphi}_{h}
, where
\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{h})
and
\|\tilde{\varphi}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(h)^{2})
.

We extend
\varphi_{h}
by
1
outside
\Omega_{h}
and consider the function

(3.1)

f_{h}=\frac{u_{per,h}}{\varphi_{h}}.

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
f_{h}\in W_{loc}^{1,2}(B(0,R_{0}))
is a weak solution to the equation

(3.2)

\mbox{div}(\varphi_{h}^{2}\nabla f_{h})=0

on
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}:=B(0,R_{0})\setminus F_{1,h}
.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
14
, Lemma
4.1
.

3.2.
Quasi-conformal mapping

In
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
, we can show, using the Poincaré lemma, that there exists a function
\tilde{f}_{h}\in W_{loc}^{1,2}
defined locally such that the local form
\omega_{h}=f_{h}+i\tilde{f}_{h}
satisfies the Beltrami equation:

(3.3)

\frac{\partial\omega_{h}}{\partial\bar{z}}=\mu_{h}\frac{\partial\omega_{h}}{\partial z},

with coefficient:

Definition 3.2
.

(3.4)

\mu_{h}:=\frac{1-\varphi_{h}^{2}}{1+\varphi_{h}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\partial_{x}f_{h}+i\partial_{y}f_{h}}{\partial_{x}f_{h}-i\partial_{y}f_{h}}.

Unlike
\omega_{h}
,
\mu_{h}
is globally defined on
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
and satisfies:

(3.5)

|\mu_{h}|\leq\left|\frac{1-\varphi_{h}^{2}}{1+\varphi_{h}^{2}}\right|\lesssim\epsilon(h)^{2}.

We extend
\mu_{h}
by
0
on the whole complex plane. Thus,
\mu_{h}
is a measurable function compactly supported with
|\mu_{h}|\lesssim\epsilon(h)^{2}<1
.

.

Because

The radial principal symbol of

\sigma=0;\quad(\partial_{r}\omega(r))^{2}=\frac{(\Lambda^{*})^{2}}{r^{2}}.

\displaystyle\{Re\,p_{\omega},Im\,p_{\omega}\}=

\displaystyle 4\left[\sigma^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}\phi_{h}+\left(\partial_{r}^{2}\phi_{h}\partial_{r}\phi_{h}+\frac{(\Lambda^{*})^{2}}{r^{3}}\right)\partial_{r}\phi_{h}\right],

(B.9)

\{Re\,p_{\omega},Im\,p_{\omega}\}=4\left(\partial_{r}^{2}\omega(r)\partial_{r}\omega(r)+\frac{(\partial_{r}\omega(r))^{2}}{r}\right)\partial_{r}\omega(r).

If we take

(B.10)

\{Re\,p_{\omega},Im\,p_{\omega}\}=4r^{-7}>4R_{0}^{-7},

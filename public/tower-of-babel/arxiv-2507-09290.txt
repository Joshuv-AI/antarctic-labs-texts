Asymptotically optimal cyclic subspace codes
Chiara Castello and Paolo Santonastaso

Abstract
. Subspace codes, and in particular cyclic subspace codes, have gained significant attention in recent years due to their applications in error correction for random network coding. In this paper, we introduce a new technique for constructing cyclic subspace codes with large cardinality and prescribed minimum distance. Using this new method, we provide new constructions of cyclic subspace codes in the Grassmannian 𝒢 q ​ ( n , k ) \mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k) of all k k -dimensional 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -subspaces of an n n -dimensional vector space over 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} , when k | n k\mid n and n / k n/k is a composite number, with minimum distance 2 ​ k − 2 2k-2 and large size. We prove that the resulting codes have sizes larger than those obtained from previously known constructions with the same parameters. Furthermore, we show that our constructions of cyclic subspace codes asymptotically reach the Johnson type bound II for infinite values of n / k n/k .



Keywords
: Subspace code; cyclic subspace code; Sidon space

The work

The natural ambient space in which to define a subspace code is an
n
-dimensional vector space
\V
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denotes the finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a power of a prime. The set
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\V)
of all the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\V
forms a metric space when endowed with the
subspace distance
, defined as

d(U,V)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U+V)-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap V),

for any
U,V\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\V)
. A subspace code
\C
is a subset of the metric space
({\mathcal{P}}_{q}(\V),d)
and as usual the
minimum distance
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as

d({\mathcal{C}})=\min\{d(U,V)\colon U,V\in{\mathcal{C}},U\neq V\}.

Consider the Grassmannian
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
, i.e. the set of the
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\V
. Subspace codes in
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
are refereed as

. One of the most studied class is given by

introduced in
[
7
]

\V={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
as
n
-dimensional vector space over its subfield
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
is said to be
cyclic
if for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
and every

then
\alpha V\in{\mathcal{C}}
. This is equivalent to say that

{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{Orb}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(V_{i})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)

for some
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
V_{1},\ldots,V_{s}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
, where
\mathrm{Orb}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(V_{i})=\{\alpha V_{i}:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
is the orbit of
V_{i}
under the action of the Singer cycle defined by the multiplicative group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
. For this reason,
{\mathcal{C}}
is called

. In the case in which

{\mathcal{C}}=\mathrm{Orb}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(V)=\{\alpha V\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\},

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace

is also called

is also called

It is well known that if

has maximum possible distance, i.e.
2k
, then

A family of codes

Our contribution.

In this paper, we introduce a new method for constructing large cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(rk,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
, for any composite integer
r
. This time, this new approach consists in combining cyclic subspace codes contained in different Grassmannians. More precisely, starting from a proper tower of field extensions
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}<{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n_{1}}}<{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n_{2}}}
, we define an operation
\odot
that
nests
cyclic subspace codes of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n_{1},k)
into cyclic subspace codes of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n_{2},n_{1})
, producing a cyclic subspace code in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n_{2},k)
. When applied to large cyclic subspace codes with the best possible minimum distance, this operation yields new large cyclic subspace codes with the best minimum distance, as well. As a result, we are able to construct new families of cyclic subspace codes with larger cardinalities than those of previously known constructions and the best possible minimum distance, for all the parameters considered.

Surprisingly, this technique leads to the explicit construction of cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(rk,k)
, with minimum distance
2k-2
, whose sizes asymptotically reach the Johnson type bound II as
k
or
q
tends to infinity, for infinitely many values of the integer
r
.
Furthermore, in the case in which the only prime divisors of
r
are
2
and
3
, we provide constructions of cyclic subspace codes whose sizes asymptotically approach the Johnson type bound II within a factor of
1/2^{e}+o_{k}(1)
, where
e
is the 2-adic valuation of
r
.

2.
Nested constructions

In this section, we introduce a new technique to construct multi-orbit codes of
{\mathcal{P}}_{q}(n)
for
n
composite, by using an operation, that we will denote by
\odot
.
We will proceed step by step in defining the operation
\odot
, starting with its action on two single subspaces up to how it acts among two multi-orbit codes. We will then investigate the size and the minimum distance of the codes obtained via this operation. Finally, we will show how to extend the operation
\odot
between more than two multi-orbit codes.

2.1.
Operation
\odot
between two one-orbit cyclic subspace codes

Assume that
n=rm
, for some positive integers
r,m>1
. Let
V_{1}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
,
V_{2}
an
m
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
\Phi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
be an injective
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map such that
V_{2}:=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
. Define

V_{2}\odot_{\Phi}V_{1}:=\{\Phi(u)\colon u\in V_{1}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the operation
\odot_{\Phi}
depends on the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map
\Phi
. However, in the next we will write
\odot
instead of
\odot_{\Phi}
to lighten the notation.

Also,
V_{2}\odot V_{1}=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi|_{V_{1}})\subseteq V_{2}
, where
\Phi|_{V_{1}}
denotes the restriction of
\Phi
to
V_{1}
. Moreover, since
\Phi

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{1})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\Phi(V_{1}))=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{2}\odot V_{1})

We now present an example to illustrate the previous definition. Precisely, we define
V_{2}\odot V_{1}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(12,3)

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces
V_{1}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(6,3)
and
V_{2}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(12,6)

.

Let

The following lemma allows us to define the operation
\odot
between the orbit of a subspace

First, for every

\beta V_{2}:=\{\beta v\colon v\in V_{2}\}

Lemma 2.3

For any

(\beta V_{2})\odot V_{1}=\beta(V_{2}\odot V_{1}).

Proof.

The assertion immediately follows by observing that

(\beta V_{2})\odot V_{1}=(\beta\Phi)(V_{1})=\beta(\Phi(V_{1}))=\beta(V_{2}\odot V_{1}).

∎

Let
V_{1}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(m,k)

Similarly, if we consider

Let

{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
has full-length and
d({\mathcal{C}}_{2})>2(m-k)
, then

\C_{2}\odot V_{1}

and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
have full-length.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot V_{1}=\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2}\odot V_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
has full-length if and only if
\mathrm{Stab}(V_{2}\odot V_{1})={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Therefore, suppose by contradiction there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that
\beta(V_{2}\odot V_{1})=V_{2}\odot V_{1}
, i.e.
\beta\Phi(V_{1})=\Phi(V_{1})
. Note that
\beta\Phi(V_{1})=\Phi(V_{1})\subseteq V_{2}\cap\beta V_{2}
. By hypothesis
\Phi
is injective, and so
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{2}\cap\beta V_{2})\geq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{1})
. Since
\beta\notin{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
\C_{2}
has full-length (and hence
V_{2}\neq\beta V_{2}
), we have

d(\C_{2})\leq d(V_{2},\beta V_{2})\leq 2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{2})-2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{1})=2(m-k)

that contradicts our hypotheses.

Recall that

{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2}\odot\alpha V_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k).

Since
\C_{2}
has full-length and

d({\mathcal{C}}_{2})>2(m-k)=2(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{2})-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{1}))=2(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V_{2})-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha V_{1}))

for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, by the previous case, we get that every
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot\alpha V_{1}=\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2}\odot\alpha V_{1})
has full-length. Therefore,
\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}
has full-length, as well.
∎

We present an illustrative example to show how
Proposition

2.6
can be applied.

Example 2.7
.

Let
r=3,m=3k
and
n=9k
with
k>1
. Let
\gamma_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3k}}
be such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}(\gamma_{1})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3k}}
. Consider the subspace

V_{1}=\{v+v^{q}\gamma_{1}\colon v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(3k,k),

and consider the code

{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(3k,k).

Let
\gamma_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{9k}}
be such that

. Consider the subspace

,

Also, the map

\begin{array}[]{llll}\Phi_{2}:&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3k}}&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{9k}}\\

2.6

2.2.
Operation
\odot
between two multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes

We point out that the operation
\odot
allows us to construct a multi-orbit cyclic subspace code
\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}
starting from two one-orbit cyclic subspace codes
\C_{1}
and
\C_{2}
. Now, in the same spirit of
Definition

2.4
, thanks to Lemma
2.3
, we can define the operation
\odot
between multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes, which will produce large multi-orbit codes.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
n=rm
, with
r,m>1
. Let
1<k\leq m
and
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 1
be positive integers. Consider

\C_{1}=\bigcup_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k),

and

\C_{2}=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,m),

multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes, where
V_{2,h_{2}}=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi_{2,h_{2}})
for some injective
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map

\Phi_{2,h_{2}}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}},

for every
h_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{2}\}
.
Define
\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}
as

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}:=&\bigcup\limits_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\bigcup\limits_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})\odot\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}})\\
=&\bigcup\limits_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\bigcup\limits_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}}\odot\alpha V_{1,h_{1}})\\
\subseteq&\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k).\end{array}

Note that
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a multi-orbit cyclic subspace code in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
. In a similar manner to
Proposition

2.6
, in the following proposition, we provide sufficient conditions to determine when the operation
\odot
on multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes produces full-length codes.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
n=rm
with
r,m>1
and let
1<k\leq m
. Consider
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(m,k)
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,m)
as in
Definition

2.8
.
If
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
has full-length and
d({\mathcal{C}}_{2})>2(m-k)
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a full-length multi-orbit cyclic subspace code.

Proof.

Recall that

{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\bigcup_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})\odot\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}}).

To show that
\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}
has full-length, by definition we need to prove that
\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})\odot\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}})
has full-length, for any
h_{1}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{1}\}
and
h_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{2}\}
. By Proposition
2.6
, it is sufficient to show that
\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})
has full length and
d(\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}}))>2(m-k)
. Note that, since
\C_{2}=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})
has full-length, we have that by definition
\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})
has full-length for every
h_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{2}\}
. Moreover,

d(\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}}))\geq d({\mathcal{C}}_{2})>2(m-k),

for every
h_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{2}\}
. Thus by
Proposition

2.6
the assertion follows.
∎

We give an example of how
Proposition

2.9
can be applied.

Example 2.10
.

Let
n=9k
, with
k>1
. We will construct multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(9k,k)
by using codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(3k,k)
and
\mathcal{G}_{q}(9k,3k)
. Let us start by defining the codes that we will employ. Let
\gamma_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3k}}
be such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}(\gamma_{1})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3k}}
. For any
a_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}
, consider the subspace

V_{1,a_{1}}=\{v+(v^{q}+a_{1}v)\gamma_{1}\colon v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(3k,k),

and define the code

{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\bigcup_{a_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,a_{1}})=\bigcup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3k}}^{*}}\bigcup_{a_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}}(\alpha V_{1,a_{1}})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(3k,k).

We can now use a code of the same shape of
\C_{1}
but in

and define the code

Note that, by
[
35

(without considering the orbits defined by different
\omega^{i}
’s),

and

2.3.
Parameters of
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}

In what follows, we study the parameters of codes obtained by the operation
\odot
. Specifically, we will see how to get information on the cardinality and on the minimum distance of the codes obtained by using
\odot
.

Before going to this we recall that in
[
24
]
, the authors highlighted an equivalence between Sidon spaces of dimension
k
and one-orbit cyclic subspace codes with minimum distance
2k-2
. A
Sidon space
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that if
ab=cd
, then
\{a{\mathbb{F}}_{q},c{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}=\{b{\mathbb{F}}_{q},d{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}
. Roth, Raviv and Tamo in
[
24
]
pointed out that every
k
-dimensional Sidon space in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is equivalent to a one-orbit cyclic subspace code of cardinality
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
and minimum distance
2k-2
.

Theorem 2.11

(see
[
24
, Lemma 34]
)
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
. The code
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
has minimum distance
2k-2
and size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
if and only if
U
is a Sidon space, i.e.

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\leq 1\quad\text{ for any }\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

Remark 2.12
.

We observe that if
V\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
, with
k>1
, and
d(\mathrm{Orb}(V))=2k-2
, then
\mathrm{Orb}(V)
has necessarily full-length. Indeed, if
d(\mathrm{Orb}(V))=2k-2
, then by Theorem
2.11
,
V
is a Sidon space, i.e.
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V\cap\alpha V)\leq 1
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. This means that if
\alpha\in\mathrm{Stab}(V)
and by contradiction we suppose
\alpha\notin{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, on one hand we have
V=\alpha V
and so
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V\cap\alpha V)=k
. On the other hand
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V\cap\alpha V)\leq 1
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, a contradiction since
k>1
.

Finally, we introduce the following notation for
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
before going to the proof of the main theorem of this subsection.

Let
n=rm
with
r,m>1
and let
k>1
and
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 1
be positive integers. Consider
s_{1}
distinct one-orbit cyclic subspace codes
\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(m,k)
for
h_{1}\in\{1,\dots,s_{1}\}
and
s_{2}
distinct one-orbit cyclic subspace codes
\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,m)
for
h_{2}\in\{1,\dots,s_{2}\}
. Let

(3)

{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\bigcup_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}})=\{U_{1,1},\ldots,U_{1,t_{1}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k),

and

(4)

{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})=\{U_{2,1},\ldots,U_{2,t_{2}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,m).

Since the orbits are distinct, their intersection is trivial and so
\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\rvert=t_{1}=\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\lvert\mathrm{Orb}(V_{1,h_{1}})|
and
\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\rvert=t_{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\lvert\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})|
.

For every
h_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{2}\}
, let

\Phi_{2,h_{2}}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}

be an injective
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map such that
V_{2,h_{2}}=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi_{2,h_{2}})
. Then defining

{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\bigcup_{h_{1}=1}^{s_{1}}\bigcup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}}\odot\alpha V_{1,h_{1}})=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\bigcup_{j_{1}=1}^{t_{1}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k),

we observe that by Lemma
2.3
we may also write
{\mathcal{C}}
as

(5)

{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\bigcup_{j_{2}=1}^{t_{2}}\bigcup_{j_{1}=1}^{t_{1}}(U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k).

In the following remark, we observe that if
d({\mathcal{C}})=2k-2\ell
then the intersection of any two distinct codewords is bounded by
\ell
.

Remark 2.13
.

Note that if the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{h=1}^{s}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{h})=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{t}\}
is
2k-2\ell
, then for any
U_{i},U_{j}\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
i\neq j
it results

2k-2\ell=d({\mathcal{C}})\leq d(U_{i},U_{j})=2k-2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})

and so

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\leq\ell.

The following theorem tells us which is the minimum distance and the cardinality of
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, by starting from the cardinalities and the minimum distances of
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(m,k)
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,m)
as in (
3
) and (
4
), respectively, and assume that
d(\C_{1})=2k-2\ell
and
d(\C_{2})=2m-2\ell^{\prime}
. The code
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a multi-orbit cyclic subspace code with minimum distance at least
2k-2\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
. Also, if
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}<k
, then
\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}
has size
\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\rvert=\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\rvert\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\rvert
and if
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}=\ell
, then
d({\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1})=2k-2\ell.

Proof.

We note that since the minimum distances of
\C_{1}
and
\C_{2}
are
2k-2\ell
and
2m-2\ell^{\prime}
, respectively, by Remark
2.13
we have

(6)

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1,j_{1}}\cap U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}})\leq\ell,

for every
j_{1},j_{1}^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,t_{1}\}
, with
j_{1}\neq j_{1}^{\prime}
, and that

(7)

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2,j_{2}}\cap U_{2,j_{2}^{\prime}})\leq\ell^{\prime},

for every
j_{2},j_{2}^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,t_{2}\}
, with
j_{2}\neq j_{2}^{\prime}
.

We prove that when
(j_{1},j_{2})\neq(j_{1}^{\prime},j_{2}^{\prime})
, then

(8)

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left((U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\cap(U_{2,j_{2}^{\prime}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}})\right)\leq\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime}&\text{ if }j_{2}\neq j_{2}^{\prime},\\
\ell&\text{ if }j_{2}=j_{2}^{\prime}.\end{cases}

We divide the discussion into two cases.

(Case
j_{2}\neq j_{2}^{\prime}
.)
First, note that
U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
U_{2,j_{2}}
, for every
j_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,t_{2}\}
and for every
j_{1}\in\{1,\ldots,t_{1}\}
.
Therefore, by using (
7
), we have

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\cap(U_{2,j_{2}^{\prime}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}))\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{2,j_{2}}\cap U_{2,j_{2}^{\prime}})\leq\ell^{\prime}.

(Case
j_{2}=j_{2}^{\prime}
.)
Since we are assuming that
(j_{1},j_{2})\neq(j_{1}^{\prime},j_{2}^{\prime})
, then
j_{1}\neq j_{1}^{\prime}
. Now,
U_{2,j_{2}}\in\C_{2}=\bigcup_{h_{2}=1}^{s_{2}}\mathrm{Orb}(V_{2,h_{2}})
, so there exists
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
such that
U_{2,j_{2}}=\alpha V_{2,h_{2}}
, for some
h_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,s_{2}\}
and
V_{2,h_{2}}=\mathrm{Im}(\Phi_{2,h_{2}})
. Therefore
U_{2,j_{2}}=\alpha\mathrm{Im}(\Phi_{2,h_{2}})=\mathrm{Im}(\alpha\Phi_{2,h_{2}})
. As a consequence, we have

U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}}=\alpha\Phi_{2,h_{2}}(U_{1,j_{1}})

and

U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}=\alpha\Phi_{2,h_{2}}(U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}).

Since
\Phi_{2,h_{2}}
is injective for every
h_{2}=1,\dots,j_{2}
, we get that for every
j_{2}\in\{1,\dots,t_{2}\}
and
j_{1}\in\{1,\dots,t_{1}\}
,

\begin{array}[]{rl}\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\cap(U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}))&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha\Phi_{2,h_{2}}(U_{1,j_{1}}\cap U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}))\\
&=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1,j_{1}}\cap U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}})\\
&\leq\ell,\end{array}

where the last inequality follows from (
6
). This proves (
8
). As a consequence, when
(j_{1},j_{2})\neq(j_{1}^{\prime},j_{2}^{\prime})
, we get
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\cap(U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}))\leq\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}
and, since
\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
, by Remark
2.13
, we obtain the first part of the assertion.

Now, by (
5
),

\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\odot{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\rvert=\left\lvert\bigcup_{j_{2}=1}^{t_{2}}\bigcup_{j_{1}=1}^{t_{1}}(U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}})\right\rvert.

Hence, if
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}<k
, then we have by (
8
) that the subspaces
U_{2,j_{2}}\odot U_{1,j_{1}}\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
are all distinct and so

\lvert\C_{2}\odot\C_{1}\rvert=t_{1}t_{2}=\lvert\C_{2}\rvert\lvert\C_{1}\rvert.

Finally, assume that
\max\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}=\ell
. Therefore,
d(\C_{2}\odot\C_{1})\geq 2k-2\ell
. On the other hand,
d(\C_{1})=2k-2\ell
, implying that there exist
U_{1,j_{1}},U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}\in\C_{1}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{1,j_{1}}\cap U_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}})=\ell
. Now, let
U_{2,j_{2}}=\mathrm{Im}(\alpha\Phi_{2,h_{2}})\in\C_{2}
, for
h_{2}\in\{1,\dots,s_{2}\}
, with
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. As a consequence,

.

Proof.

We divide the analysis according to the following cases.

Case 1:
n/k
power of
2
.
In this case
n=2^{e}k
, with
e\geq 3
. We have that:

(33)

S_{3}(2^{e}k,k,q)\sim q^{(2^{e}+2^{e-1}-1)k},\ \ \ \mbox{ as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty)

Therefore, comparing (
29
) with (
33
), we get

S_{3}(2^{e}k,k,q)=o_{k}(\lvert{{}_{2^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert)\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ S_{3}(2^{e}k,k,q)=o_{q}(\lvert{{}_{2^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert).

Case 2:
n/k
power of an odd prime
p
.
In this case
n=p^{e}k
, where
p
is an odd prime and
e\geq 2
. We observe that
\ell+1=\lceil\frac{p^{e}}{2}\rceil-1
, hence:

(34)

S_{3}(p^{e}k,k,q)\sim q^{\frac{3p^{e}-1}{2}k},\ \ \ \mbox{ as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty).

Moreover, since
e\geq 2
, we have
\frac{(3p-1)(p^{e}-1)}{2(p-1)}>\frac{3p^{e}-1}{2}
, and therefore, by comparing (
30
) with (
34
), we also conclude that

S_{3}(p^{e}k,k,q)=o_{k}(\lvert{{}_{p^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert)\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ S_{3}(p^{e}k,k,q)=o_{q}(\lvert{{}_{p^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert).

Case 3:
n/k
is a composite odd number.
Here,
r=p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}
, where
p_{1}>\cdots>p_{z}
are odd primes and
z>1
. Since
\ell+1=\left\lceil\frac{p_{z}^{e_{z}}\dots p_{1}^{e_{1}}}{2}\right\rceil-1
, we have that:

(35)

S_{3}(p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}k,k,q)\sim q^{\frac{3p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}-1}{2}k},\ \ \ \mbox{ as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty).

We observe that, since for every
i=1,\dots,z
,

\frac{(3p_{i}-1)(p_{i}^{e_{i}}-1)}{2(p_{i}-1)}\geq\frac{3p_{i}^{e_{i}}-1}{2}

it follows that:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{z}\left(\frac{(3p_{i}-1)(p_{i}^{e_{i}}-1)}{2(p_{i}-1)}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}p_{j}^{e_{j}}k\right)&\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{z}\left(\frac{3p_{i}^{e_{i}}-1}{2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}p_{j}^{e_{j}}k\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{z}\left(3\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}^{e_{j}}-\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}p_{j}^{e_{j}}\right)k\\
&>\frac{3p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}-1}{2}k.\end{array}

Therefore, taking into account (
31
) and (
35
), we conclude that:

S_{3}(p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}k,k,q)=o_{k}(\lvert_{p_{z}^{e_{z}}\dots p_{1}^{e_{1}}}\C_{k}\rvert)\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ S_{3}(p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}k,k,q)=o_{q}(\lvert_{p_{z}^{e_{z}}\dots p_{1}^{e_{1}}}\C_{k}\rvert).

Case 4:
n/k
is a composite even number.
Consider the case where
n=rk
, with
r=2^{e}p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}}
being even and the product of distinct primes. In this case, by a similar argument of Case 3, we again obtain

S_{3}(2^{e}p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}},k,q)=o_{k}(\lvert_{2^{e}p_{z}^{e_{z}}\dots p_{1}^{e_{1}}}\C_{k}\rvert)\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ S_{3}(2^{e}p_{z}^{e_{z}}\cdots p_{1}^{e_{1}},k,q)=o_{q}(\lvert_{2^{e}p_{z}^{e_{z}}\dots p_{1}^{e_{1}}}\C_{k}\rvert).

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Actually, we have also provided a construction for
r=4
. In this case, the largest known construction is Construction 2) in Table
1
. We note that

S_{2}(4k,k,q)\sim\frac{1}{2}q^{6k},\ \ \ \mbox{as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty)

and also, by (
29
), that

\lvert{{}_{2^{2}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert\sim\frac{1}{4}q^{6k},\ \ \ \mbox{as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty).

Therefore,
{}_{2^{2}}\C_{k}
has the same asymptotic behavior of
S_{2}(n,k,q))
, although it should be noted that, in general,
S_{2}(n,k,q)>\lvert{{}_{2^{2}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert
.

4.2.
Comparison with the Johnson type bound II

We compare the size of the codes
{}_{r}\C_{k}
of Table
2
with the Johnson type bound II as one of the parameters
q
or
k
(and so
n=rk
) tends to infinity and the other parameter is treated as constant. The Johnson type bound II for
d=2k-2
reads as

{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(n,k,2k-2)\leq J_{q}(n,2k-2,k):=\left\lfloor\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{k}-1}\left\lfloor\frac{q^{n-1}-1}{q^{k-1}-1}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor.

We also note that when
n=rk
, with
r\geq 2
, we have

(36)

J_{q}(rk,2k-2,k)\sim q^{2(r-1)k},\ \ \ \mbox{as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty).

Remark 4.3
.

Up to now, very few constructions of cyclic subspace codes in
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(n,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
whose sizes asymptotically reach the Johnson type bound II are known. For
n=2k
, as noted in
[
24
]
, the size of Construction 1 in
Table

1
approaches the Johnson type bound II within a factor of
1+o_{k}(1)
of the Johnson type bound II as
k
goes to infinity. Furthermore, it is also easy to verify that the size of Construction 1 also remains within a factor of
1/2+o_{q}(1)
of the Johnson type bound II as
q
goes to infinity. Similarly, for
n=4k
, in
[
31
]
establishes that the size of Construction 2 in
Table

1
remains within a factor of
1/2+o_{k}(1)
(of
1/2+o_{q}(1)
) of the Johnson type bound II as
k
(as
q
) approaches to infinity.
Furthermore, the case
n=3k
is the only one where there exists a family of cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(3k,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
, whose size asymptotically achieves the Johnson type bound II (see
[
35
, Remark 2]
).
Also, we known that for
r\geq 6
the size of the codes from Constructions 3), 4) and 5) presented in
Table

1
, exhibit the best asymptotic behavior among the constructions for codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(rk,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
. However, the sizes of these codes are never asymptotically equal to the Johnson type bound II
J_{q}(rk,2k-2,k)
. Indeed, taking into account Equation (
36
) and Table
1
, we observe that the condition

2(r-1)k=(\ell+1)k+n,\ \ \ \mbox{ where }\ell=\lceil r/2\rceil-2

is satisfied only when
r=3
. Therefore,
S_{3}(rk,k,q)\sim J_{q}(rk,2k-2,k)
if and only if
r=3
while for all other values of
r
, we have
S_{3}(rk,k,q)=o_{k}(J_{q}(rk,2k-2,k))
and
S_{3}(rk,k,q)=o_{q}(J_{q}(rk,2k-2,k))
.

In what follows, we show that the codes
{}_{2^{e}}\C_{k}
and
{}_{3^{e_{1}}2^{e}}\C_{k}
as in Table
2
, for
n/k\geq 6
and
q>2
, have sizes that are within a factor of
1/2^{e}+o_{k}(1)
(and of
1/2^{e}+o_{q}(1)

k
(as

) goes to infinity. This proves that the size of the family of codes
{{}_{2^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}
and
{}_{3^{e_{1}}2^{e}}\C_{k}
exhibits the same asymptotic behavior as
J(n,k,q)
as
q
or

grows.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any
e\geq 2
, the family of codes
{{}_{2^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(2^{e}k,k)
as in
3.3
, is such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(n,k,q)}{\lvert{{}_{2^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert}=2^{e}=\lim_{q\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(n,k,q)}{\lvert{{}_{2^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert}.

Proof.

The assertion immediately follows by comparing (
29
) with (

).
∎

Now, we consider the case
n=2^{e}3^{e_{1}}k
.

Theorem 4.5
.

For any
e,e_{1}\geq 1
, the family of codes
{{}_{2^{e}3^{e_{1}}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(2^{e}3^{e_{1}}k,k)
defined as in

, is such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(n,k,q)}{\lvert{{}_{2^{e}3^{e_{1}}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert}=2^{e}=\lim_{q\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(n,k,q)}{\lvert{{}_{2^{e}3^{e_{1}}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert}.

By (
32
), we know

\lvert_{2^{e}3^{e_{1}}}\C_{k}\rvert\sim\frac{1}{2^{e}}q^{2\cdot 3^{e_{1}}k(2^{e}-1)+2(3^{e_{1}}-1)k}=\frac{1}{2^{e}}q^{2(n-k)},\ \ \ \mbox{ as }k\rightarrow+\infty\ \ \ (\mbox{or as }q\rightarrow+\infty).

Therefore, from (

), the result follows directly.
∎

Now, we consider the case where

, with

. We prove that the size of the code

as in Table

asymptotically approaches the Johnson type bound II.

Theorem 4.6
.

For any
e\geq 2
, the family of codes
{{}_{3^{e}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(3^{e}k,k)
, as defined in
3.9
is asymptotically optimal in
k
and in

.

Proof.

From (
30
), we have

\lvert{}_{3^{e}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert\sim q^{2(3^{e}-1)k}=q^{2(n-k)},\quad\text{as }k\to+\infty\quad(\text{or as }q\to+\infty).

On the other hand, from (
36
), we know that
J(n,k,q)\sim q^{2(n-k)}
. Therefore, the codes
{}_{3^{e}}\mathcal{C}_{k}

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{J(n,k,q)}{\lvert{}_{3^{e}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert}=1=\lim_{q\to+\infty}\frac{J(n,k,q)}{\lvert{}_{3^{e}}\mathcal{C}_{k}\rvert},

from which the result follows directly.

∎

In conclusion, for any integer

5.
Conclusion and Open Problems

In this article, we introduced a new method for constructing large cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(rk,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
, when
r
is a composite integer. This operation not only provides new codes with minimum distance
2k-2
for all parameters considered, but also yields codes with larger size compared to the ones of existing constructions. In addition, we provide the first infinite families of cyclic subspace codes in
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(rk,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
and sizes that asymptotically approach the Johnson type bound II.
We observe that for values of
r
different from either
r=2^{e}
,
e\geq 2
and
q>2
or
r=2^{e}3^{e_{1}}
, with
e,e_{1}\geq 1
, the size of the codes
{}_{r}\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(rk,k)
remains asymptotically far from the Johnson type bound II. However, a promising direction for future research is to investigate the existence of cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(pk,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
, for any prime
p\neq 3
, whose sizes asymptotically approach the Johnson type bound II. With the
\odot
operation introduced in this paper, then it would be possible to obtain, for any composite integer
r
, cyclic subspace codes in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(rk,k)
with minimum distance
2k-2
, such that their sizes asymptotically achieve the Johnson type bound II.
Moreover, in this work we have primarily focused on the case where
k\mid n
, which enabled our algebraic constructions through towers of field extensions. However, the case where
k
does not divide
n
remains largely unexplored. Finally, we point out that our new constructions of multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes yield large optical orthogonal codes, as shown in
[
23
]
. As a consequence, we immediately obtain new families of optical orthogonal codes with new parameters.

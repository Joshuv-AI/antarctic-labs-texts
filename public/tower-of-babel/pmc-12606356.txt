A lightweight deep evidence fusion framework for smart home appliance detection and classification via internet of things devices
Authors: Nuha Alruwais, Hadeel Alsolai, Mohammed Maray, Wahida Mansouri, Alanoud Subahi, Nouf Atiahallah Alghanmi, Hanadi Alkhudhayr, Mutasim Al Sadig
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the Internet of Things (IoT) field, massive quantities of smart devices are connected, producing enormous data sizes that need developed management methods. One general task in a smart environment is the capability to precisely recognize and classify diverse kinds of objects using these methods. The evolution of technologies and advancements in structure brings several challenges, such as managing and controlling the entire process, ensuring protection at the server, security in smart homes, and more. A robust and flexible deep learning (DL) technique is presented to achieve appliance classification without restrictions on user behaviour. The approach reflects combined load reports throughout the shared process. DL presents great models for analyzing and processing IoT sensor data, permitting accurate appliance recognition. This study presents a Lightweight Deep Evidence Fusion Framework for Smart Home Appliance Detection and Classification (LDEFF-SHADC) model. The primary objective of the LDEFF-SHADC technique is to establish a robust framework for detecting and classifying smart home appliances using IoT device data. The LDEFF-SHADC approach performs data normalization using linear scaling normalization (LSN) to ensure uniformity and enhance processing efficiency. The improved snake optimization (ISO) model is utilized for the feature selection process to reduce dimensionality while preserving critical information effectively. In addition, a hybrid framework integrating gated recurrent units and the multi-head attention (GRU-MHA) classifier is implemented to classify and detect smart home appliances. The improved sparrow search optimization algorithm (ISSA) is employed to fine-tune the parameters of the GRU-MHA model and achieve enhanced optimization performance. The stimulated outcome investigation of the LDEFF-SHADC technique is examined under the IoT device recognition dataset. The performance validation of the LDEFF-SHADC technique portrayed a superior accuracy value of 98.90% over existing models.Keywords: Smart home appliance, Lightweight, Internet of things devices, Feature selection, Hybrid model, Hyperparameter tuningSubject terms: Computer science, Information technology

Data normalization

At first, the LDEFF-SHADC approach applies data normalization using LSN to ensure uniformity and enhance processing efficiency53. This approach was chosen for data normalization due to its capability to standardize the input features within a specific range, usually between 0 and 1, ensuring consistency across diverse data sources. This methodology effectually mitigates the impact of outliers and ensures that all features contribute equally to the model. Compared to other normalization methods such as Z-score or Min-Max, LSN provides a simple yet effective approach that preserves the relative relationships among features while enhancing model convergence speed. Additionally, LSN is computationally efficient and avoids the complexities of other techniques, making it appropriate for massive datasets in real-time applications.

LSN is a critical pre-processing model for normalizing smart home machine recognition data through IoT devices. It involves altering feature values to a set range, naturally [0, 1], by deducting the least value of data and splitting by an array of features. This standardization procedure certifies that every IoT device data is on an equal scale, preventing definite features from significantly influencing the model. By standardizing the data, linear scaling increases the convergence and performance of ML techniques employed for appliance classification. This model aids in treating diverse IoT data, improving the accuracy of appliance recognition in smart homes. Furthermore, it enables effective feature selection and model training, mainly when united with developed models such as the Improved Snake Optimization Algorithm (ISOA) for optimum extraction of features.

ISO-based feature selection

The ISO approach is utilized for the feature selection process to reduce dimensionality while preserving critical information effectively54. This method was chosen because of its ability to identify the most relevant features while reducing redundancy efficiently. ISO employs an optimization-based approach to explore the feature space, balancing exploration and exploitation to detect optimal subsets. Unlike conventional methods like Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) or Filter-based techniques, ISO can handle complex, non-linear relationships within the data, making it specifically appropriate for high-dimensional datasets. Furthermore, ISO’s global search mechanism ensures a more robust selection of features, mitigating the likelihood of overfitting and enhancing model performance. This approach was chosen for its capability to effectively capture both temporal dependencies and contextual relationships in sequential data. GRUs outperform in modelling long-range dependencies with lesser computational resources compared to other recurrent models like LSTMs, making them ideal for real-time applications. ISSA-based parameter tuning process

The ISSA is implemented to fine-tune the parameters of the GRU-MHA model to achieve enhanced optimization performance56. This model was chosen due to its effective balance between exploration and exploitation, which is significant for finding the optimal hyperparameters. Unlike conventional methods such as grid or random search, ISSA utilizes a population-based search strategy inspired by the social behaviour of sparrows, enabling it to explore the solution space more efficiently. The capability of the algorithm to avoid local optima and converge to global solutions makes it ideal for optimizing complex models. Additionally, ISSA’s robustness in diverse problem settings ensures improved model performance across diverse data types and tasks. Its flexibility and high computational efficiency in fine-tuning parameters distinguish it from conventional optimization algorithms like genetic algorithms or PSO, resulting in improved overall results. Conclusion

This study presents an LDEFF-SHADC model. The primary objective of the LDEFF-SHADC technique is to establish a robust framework for detecting and classifying smart home appliances using IoT device data. The LDEFF-SHADC approach applies data normalization using LSN to ensure uniformity and enhance processing efficiency. The ISO model effectively reduces dimensionality while preserving critical information for the feature selection process. In addition, a hybrid framework combining the GRU-MHA classifier is deployed to classify and detect smart home appliances. The ISSA is adopted to fine-tune the parameters of the GRU-MHA model and achieve enhanced optimization performance. The stimulated outcome investigation of the LDEFF-SHADC technique is examined under the IoT device recognition dataset. The performance validation of the LDEFF-SHADC technique portrayed a superior accuracy value of 98.90% over existing models. The limitations of the LDEFF-SHADC technique comprise its reliance on specific datasets, which may limit the model’s generalizability to other real-world scenarios. The input data’s quality and noise level could also affect the model’s performance, mainly when applied to more diverse IoT environments. Moreover, the computational complexity increases as the dataset grows, which may impact scalability. Future works should improve the model’s adaptability to diverse IoT devices and environments by integrating additional data sources. Furthermore, exploring the use of lightweight models for resource-constrained devices could assist in improving the real-time deployment of the model. Further research could also improve the system’s robustness against adversarial attacks, ensuring higher security. Finally, integrating continuous learning mechanisms to adapt to growing attack patterns could increase the long-term effectiveness of the system.

Macroanatomical, Histological, Histochemical and Ultrastructural Characterisation of the Kidney in the Aksaray Malakli Dog: First Comprehensive Renal Morphological Study of the Breed
Authors: Hatice Yaren Kuloğlu, Işık Tuncer, Ramazan İlgün, Kenan Erdoğan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe Aksaray Malaklı dog is a native Turkish breed indigenous to the Central Anatolian region, yet its renal morphology has not been comprehensively characterised.ObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate the macroanatomical, histological, histochemical and scanning electron microscopic (SEM) structure of the kidney in the Aksaray Malaklı dog for the first time.MethodsThis study aimed to investigate the macroanatomical, histological, histochemical and SEM structure of the kidney in the Aksaray Malaklı dog for the first time. Eight adult Aksaray Malaklı dogs (four male, four female) were used as material. Kidneys were examined macroanatomically following euthanasia and fixation. Tissue samples were processed for histological (HE), histochemical (Alcian Blue at pH 1.0 and 2.5, PAS, and Gordon Sweet staining) and SEM analyses.ResultsMacroanatomically, the kidneys were bilaterally positioned in the regio lumbalis, with the right kidney located more cranially. Histologically, the kidney was enclosed by a fibrous capsule; the cortex contained renal corpuscles, proximal and distal convoluted tubules, and medullary rays, while the medullary contained straight tubules, collecting ducts and capillaries. Podocytes, macula densa cells, juxtaglomerular cells and mesangial cells were clearly identified. Histochemically, no reaction was detected with Alcian Blue at pH 1.0 or pH 2.5; PAS staining yielded positive reactions in Bowman's capsule, proximal tubule basal portions, and vascular endothelium. Gordon Sweet staining revealed reticular fibres around the capsule, blood vessels, renal corpuscles and tubules; notably, an exceptionally dense reticular network was identified in the cortical zone where renal corpuscles were abundant but tubules were sparse a finding not previously reported in the literature. SEM examination demonstrated the three‐dimensional architecture of renal corpuscles and tubular structures.ConclusionsThis study provides the first comprehensive morphological dataset for the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney. The breed‐specific dense reticular fibre network represents a novel finding. SEM proved to be a valuable tool for three‐dimensional visualisation of renal architecture. These findings are expected to contribute to the differential diagnosis of urinary tract pathologies in dogs and to comparative studies on renal morphology in Carnivora.Keywords: Aksaray Malaklı dog, histochemistry, histology, renal morphology, scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

Introduction

The Aksaray Malaklı dog is a native Turkish breed indigenous to the Aksaray province of Central Anatolia, also known as the ‘Aksaray Malaklısı’ or ‘Malaklı Karabaş’ (Atasoy and Kanli 2005; Atasoy et al. 2014). Characterised by a large and powerful body structure, this breed has historically served as a livestock guardian dog and has recently attracted increasing scientific attention. Previous studies on this breed have investigated various anatomical structures including the mandible, skull bones, tibia, plexus lumbosacralis and the oesophagus using histochemical and SEM analyses (Ilgun and Ozudogru 2020; İlgün et al. 2022; Ozudogru et al. 2023; Alperen Fidan et al. 2024; Kuloglu et al. 2024). However, a comprehensive investigation of the renal morphology of this breed using macroanatomical, histological, histochemical and SEM approaches has not been reported in the literature.

The kidneys are paired organs responsible for essential homeostatic functions including waste excretion, fluid‐electrolyte balance, and blood pressure regulation (Murray and Paolini 2023). The nephron, the basic structural and functional unit of the kidney, consists of the renal corpuscle together with the proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct (Madrazo‐Ibarra et al. 2023). The dog kidney is bean‐shaped with a smooth surface and a single renal papilla, containing a cortical labyrinth and medullary rays. The proximal tubule of the dog kidney comprises four distinct anatomical segments, making it the closest morphological model to the human kidney among laboratory animals (Bulger et al. 1979; Khomenko et al. 2024). Comparative histological analyses of dog and cat kidneys have revealed significant interspecies differences in tubular segment dimensions, epithelial cell morphology, and PAS staining intensity (Kira et al. 2024; Sharif et al. 2025).

Histochemical characterisation of renal tissue commonly employs periodic acid‐Schiff (PAS), Alcian Blue (AB) and Gordon Sweet (GS) staining methods. PAS staining highlights basement membranes and glycoprotein content, AB staining identifies acidic polysaccharides, and GS staining visualises the Type III collagen network (reticular fibres) in the renal interstitium via silver impregnation (Lemley and Kriz 1991; Montes et al. 1980). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) provides detailed three‐dimensional visualisation of renal architecture; in particular, imaging of podocyte foot processes and the slit diaphragm is critically important for the early diagnosis of glomerular diseases (Ichimura et al. 2015; Gagliardini et al. 2010).

This study aimed to determine the macroanatomical, histological, histochemical and SEM structure of the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney. The findings are expected to serve as a reference for future morphological, physiological and pathological investigations of this breed, as well as to contribute to comparative studies on renal structure diversity within Carnivora. The primary objective of this study was to provide a comprehensive qualitative characterisation of the renal architecture in the Aksaray Malaklı dog. Rather than a population‐based morphometric analysis, the focus was placed on identifying the architectural integrity and distinctive histological patterns of this giant breed.

Material and Methods

Animals and Macroanatomical Examination

Eight adult Aksaray Malaklı dogs (four male, four female) that were brought to the XXX University Veterinary Faculty Animal Hospital for various reasons and failed to respond to treatment were used in this study. Following euthanasia, cadavers were fixed in 10% formaldehyde solution and subsequently dissected to examine the macroanatomical structure of the kidneys. This study was conducted with the approval of the XXX University Local Ethics Committee for Animal Experiments (Approval No: 2023/11). Anatomical terminology was based on the Nomina Anatomica Veterinaria (NAV 2012).

Histological Examination

Tissue samples were collected from the kidneys for histological analysis. Samples were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 24 h. Fixed tissues were processed through a routine histological protocol and embedded in paraffin. Sections of 5 µm thickness were cut from each tissue block and stained with haematoxylin and eosin (HE; Luna 1968). Photomicrographs were taken from various regions of the tissue using a light microscope (Kuloglu et al. 2024). Histological nomenclature was based on the Nomina Histologica Veterinaria (NHV; Veterinaria 2017).

Histochemical Examination

Histochemical staining procedures included AB at pH 1.0 and pH 2.5, PAS, and GS staining. AB staining was used for the detection of acidic polysaccharides and glycosaminoglycans; PAS staining was used for visualisation of glycogen, glycoproteins and basement membranes; and GS staining was used for the identification of reticular fibres via silver impregnation (Luna 1968).

Scanning Electron Microscopy

For SEM examination, tissue samples were washed in phosphate buffer, re‐fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde, dehydrated through an acetone series, and dried (Kapakin 2006). Specimens were mounted on metal stubs and coated with gold–palladium to a thickness of 18–20 nm using a sputter coater. Samples were analysed using a LEO 440 scanning electron microscope at the XXX University Scientific and Technological Research and Application Centre.

Results

Macroanatomical Findings

The kidneys of the Aksaray Malaklı dog were bilaterally positioned in the regio lumbalis on either side of the median plane, at the level of the lumbar vertebrae. The right kidney (ren dexter) was found to be located more cranially than the left kidney (ren sinister). Each kidney had two surfaces—facies dorsalis and facies ventralis—and two poles—extremitas cranialis and extremitas caudalis. The renal artery (A. renalis), renal veins (V. renalis), and ureters were observed at the hilus renalis, the concave medial indentation of the kidney. The kidneys were structurally composed of cortex and medullary renis (Figure 1).

Histological Findings

H&E staining revealed that the kidney of the Aksaray Malaklı dog was enclosed by a fibrous connective tissue capsule. The fibrous capsule was observed to enter the organ at the hilus and form the connective tissue skeleton (interstitium) of the kidney (Figure 2). The kidney was composed of cortex and medullary. The cortex contained renal corpuscles, convoluted and straight tubules (proximal and distal) of the nephron, collecting tubules, collecting ducts and a dense vascular supply (Figure 3). Parietal and visceral layers were distinguished in Bowman's capsule. Squamous cells were observed in the parietal layer. The cavum glomeruli (Bowman's space) was clearly identified. Macula densa cells were clearly observed. Podocyte cells were seen as distinctly differentiated in the glomerulus (Figure 4). Juxtaglomerular cells were observed surrounding the macula densa (Figure 5). Mesangial cells were also observed at the macula densa (Figure 6). Medullary rays were present in the cortex, consisting of straight tubules and collecting ducts. Renal corpuscles, convoluted tubules of nephrons and collecting tubules were observed in the cortical labyrinth (Figure 7). Straight tubules, collecting ducts, and capillaries were observed in the medullary (Figure 8).

Histochemical Findings

No reaction was observed with AB staining at pH 1.0 or pH 2.5. PAS staining revealed positive reactions in the parietal layer of Bowman's capsule, the basal portions of proximal tubules, and the endothelial layer of blood vessels in the cortex (Figure 9a–c). Positive reaction was also detected in the basal portions of the ascending limb of the loop of Henle in the medullary (Figure 10). GS staining detected reticular fibres in the connective tissue surrounding the organ and in the connective tissue extensions within the organ (Figure 11). Reticular fibre density was observed around blood vessels, renal corpuscles and convoluted and straight tubules (Figure 12a,b). Additionally, an exceptionally dense reticular network was observed in the cortical zone where renal corpuscles were abundant but tubules were sparse (Figure 13). Throughout the histological examination, no significant qualitative differences were observed between the male and female groups in terms of glomerular structure, tubular organisation, or connective tissue distribution. The architectural patterns remained consistent across both sexes, suggesting that the fundamental renal morphology of the Aksaray Malaklı dog does not exhibit sexual dimorphism at the microscopic level, which is consistent with findings in other large breeds (Bulger et al. 1979; Lobacz et al. 2012).

Scanning Electron Microscopy Findings

SEM images of the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney revealed that the connective tissue surrounding the organ had a smooth longitudinal appearance, with filamentous extensions visible in cross‐section (Figure 14). Renal corpuscles, convoluted tubules, and straight tubules were easily distinguished in the cortex (Figure 15). Bowman's space was prominently visible in the renal corpuscle. In the medullary, some nephrons appeared well‐shaped while others appeared tubular, likely due to drying during specimen preparation (Figure 16).

Discussion

The macroanatomical position and structure of the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney are consistent with existing literature on canine renal anatomy. Onar (2025) reported that in Malaklı dogs, the right kidney is positioned at the level of the first to third lumbar vertebrae, while the left kidney is located slightly more caudally at the second to fourth lumbar vertebral level. The present findings confirm that the kidneys of the Aksaray Malaklı dog are positioned bilaterally in the regio lumbalis, consistent with these reports and with classical veterinary anatomical descriptions (König and Liebich 2022). The identification of the renal artery, renal vein and ureter at the hilus renalis is in agreement with established anatomical literature (Onar 2025; König and Liebich 2022).

While this study did not focus on providing extensive morphometric measurements, the detailed histological and ultrastructural findings established a definitive structural map of the renal architecture in this breed (Khomenko et al. 2024; Sharif et al. 2025). This descriptive approach is essential for identifying unique tissue organisation that numerical data alone may overlook. The histological appearance of the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney is broadly consistent with the existing literature on canine renal histology (Bulger et al. 1979; Kira et al. 2024; Sharif et al. 2025; Khomenko et al. 2024). The kidney was enclosed by a fibrous capsule that entered the organ at the hilus to form the interstitium. The cortex contained renal corpuscles, proximal and distal convoluted tubules, medullary rays and a dense vascular network, while the medullary contained straight tubules, collecting ducts and capillaries. These findings are consistent with classical descriptions of the canine renal cortex (Bulger et al. 1979; Kriz 1981) and with recent comparative histological studies (Kira et al. 2024; Sharif et al. 2025). Kriz (1981) reported that dogs and cats possess exclusively long‐looped nephrons. While it is often generalised that rodents have shorter loops, it is important to specify that this refers primarily to commercial laboratory strains of rats and mice; specialised species like the kangaroo rat are known for their exceptionally long loops of Henle (Bulger et al. 1979). Our findings confirm that the Malaklı dog follows the long‐looped pattern typical of large canines (Sharif et al. 2025).

The distinct identification of parietal and visceral layers in Bowman's capsule is consistent with the literature. Podocyte cells were clearly differentiated in the visceral layer. Podocytes are terminally differentiated cells lining the outer surface of glomerular capillaries with a complex cellular architecture (Ichii et al. 2011). The slit diaphragm between podocyte foot processes constitutes a critical component of the glomerular filtration barrier, with nephrin and podocin proteins maintaining its functional integrity. Podocyte injury leading to foot process effacement and proteinuria has been comprehensively investigated in canine and feline chronic kidney disease (Ichii et al. 2011). The glomerular basement membrane (GBM) harbours two distinct Type IV collagen networks in mature mammals: α1(IV)/α2(IV) chains in the subendothelial GBM and α3(IV)/α4(IV)/α5(IV) chains in the suprapodocyte GBM, providing the selectivity and mechanical resistance of the glomerular filtration barrier (Miner 2012).

Macula densa cells were clearly observed, confirming the histological prominence of the juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA). The macula densa is a plaque of 20–25 highly specialised and polarised renal epithelial cells at the distal end of each nephron that senses tubular NaCl concentration and stimulates juxtaglomerular cells to secrete renin (Gyarmati et al. 2021). The observation of juxtaglomerular cells surrounding the macula densa is consistent with classical descriptions of this structure in the dog kidney (Spangler 1979; Tremblay 1966; Khomenko et al. 2024). Mesangial cells were also observed in the glomerulus; these contractile and phagocytic cells support the glomerular capillaries and play an important role in the fine regulation of the glomerular filtration rate (Murray and Paolini 2023).

The observation of straight tubules, collecting ducts, and capillaries in the medullary is consistent with the literature on canine renal medullary structure (Bulger et al. 1979; Kriz 1981). The descending thin limb of the loop of Henle is highly permeable to water, whereas the ascending thin limb is water‐impermeable; the thick ascending limb performs active ion transport, contributing to urinary concentration via the countercurrent multiplier mechanism (Mount 2014; Pannabecker 2012). PAS‐positive reactions were observed in the basement membranes of renal tubules and Bowman's capsule, the GBM, and the brush border of proximal tubules (Murray and Paolini 2023). Sharif et al. (2025) reported that PAS staining yielded more intense reactions in feline renal tissues compared to canine tissues, revealing interspecies histochemical differences. PAS‐positive reaction in the basal portions of the ascending limb of the loop of Henle in the medullary is consistent with the glycoprotein content of the basement membrane in this region (Mount 2014).

GS staining revealed reticular fibres in the connective tissue surrounding the organ and in its internal connective tissue extensions, consistent with the literature on renal interstitial structure. Reticular fibres contain Type III collagen fibrils and form continuity with the collagen elements of the renal capsule and intertubular connective tissue (Lemley and Kriz 1991). Montes et al. (1980) reported that reticular fibres are abundantly observed in organs such as the liver, spleen and kidney. The observation of reticular fibre density around blood vessels, renal corpuscles and tubules demonstrates the critical role of reticular fibres in the mechanical support of these structures. The dense reticular network identified via GS staining represents a key structural finding that appears consistent in the Aksaray Malaklı dog. This robust fibre arrangement likely provides necessary mechanical support for the renal parenchyma in this giant breed, considering the significant physiological demands associated with their large body mass (Bulger et al. 1979; Pannabecker 2012).

Notably, the exceptionally dense reticular network observed in the cortical zone where renal corpuscles were abundant but tubules were sparse represents a previously unreported finding in the literature. This finding suggests an unusual concentration of reticular fibres in this region and may be considered a breed‐specific feature of the Aksaray Malaklı dog. This zone may correspond to the transitional area between the cortex and medullary; the reduction of tubules and the densification of the reticular network in this region may reflect a breed‐specific adaptation in the mechanical support organisation of the kidney. Further investigation of this novel finding using immunohistochemical and ultrastructural methods is recommended.

SEM images revealed a smooth longitudinal surface morphology of the renal capsule, with filamentous extensions visible in cross‐section, consistent with the collagen‐rich fibrous structure of the renal capsule. The clear distinction of renal corpuscles, convoluted tubules, and straight tubules in the cortex by SEM demonstrates the prominent three‐dimensional architecture of these structures (Ichimura et al. 2015). The prominent visualisation of Bowman's space in SEM images is a morphological indicator of normal glomerular filtration function. SEM imaging of glomerular podocyte structures and slit diaphragm morphology is critically important for the early diagnosis of glomerular diseases (Gagliardini et al. 2010).

The partial appearance of medullary nephrons as well‐shaped structures and the partial tubular appearance are considered artefacts related to the dehydration stage of SEM specimen preparation (Kapakin 2006). Kuloglu et al. (2024) performed SEM analysis of the Aksaray Malaklı dog oesophagus and reported that this method provides valuable information for detailed examination of tissue structure. Similarly, SEM examination of the kidney is expected to make important contributions to the differential diagnosis of urinary tract pathologies commonly seen in dogs.

The general histological structure of the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney is consistent with classical descriptions of the canine kidney (Bulger et al. 1979; Kira et al. 2024; Sharif et al. 2025). The proximal tubule of the dog kidney comprises four distinct anatomical segments, making it the closest morphological model to the human kidney among laboratory animals (Bulger et al. 1979). Kira et al. (2024) showed that the proximal tubule area fraction in the renal cortex was higher in adult cats than in dogs, while the distal tubule and collecting duct area fractions were significantly lower in cats. Sharif et al. (2025) reported that the urinary tubule connecting to the glomerulus was more prominent in feline than in canine tissues, and that cats had larger nuclei in both proximal and distal tubule cells. The high susceptibility to tubulointerstitial injury in cats may be associated with these histological differences (Kira et al. 2024).

Kriz (1981) reported that dogs and cats possess exclusively long‐looped nephrons, distinguishing the renal medullary organisation of these two species from short‐looped species such as mice and rats. This structural feature directly affects the urinary concentration capacity and renal physiology of dogs and cats. Given the large body size and livestock guardian function of the Aksaray Malaklı dog, it is considered that the renal physiology of this breed may differ from standard dog breeds; however, comprehensive comparative studies are needed on this subject. The novel findings presented in this study for the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney make important contributions to the knowledge base on renal structural diversity within Carnivora.

Conclusions

This study is the first to comprehensively characterise the macroanatomical, histological, histochemical and SEM structure of the Aksaray Malaklı dog kidney. The findings confirm consistency with the general canine renal morphology while revealing a previously undescribed, breed‐specific dense reticular fibre network in the cortical zone where renal corpuscles are abundant but tubules are sparse. SEM proved to be a valuable tool for three‐dimensional visualisation of renal architecture and for early differential diagnosis of urinary tract pathologies in dogs. This study is expected to serve as a solid reference for future morphological, physiological and pathological investigations of the Aksaray Malaklı dog, as well as for comparative studies on renal structural diversity within Carnivora.

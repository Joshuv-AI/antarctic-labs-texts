A blended phenotype of primary immunodeficiency and Temtamy syndrome: Dual homozygosity for STK4 and C12orf57 gene variants in a Tunisian infant
Authors: M. Hsairi, A. Kammoun, H. Fendri, M. Guirat, H. Kammoun, Z. Mnif, L. Gargouri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We report a 6-month-old male, born to consanguineous parents, presented with recurrent bronchopneumonia, global developmental delay, and craniofacial dysmorphism. Initial workup revealed profound T-cell lymphopenia and hypoplasia of the splenium of the corpus callosum. Whole exome sequencing (WES) identified two homozygous pathogenic variants: a mutation in C12orf57, diagnostic of Temtamy syndrome, and a mutation in STK4, confirming a primary immunodeficiency. At age 3, the patient developed a life-threatening acute hemolytic crisis with a hemoglobin nadir of 2.3 g/dL. Investigations confirmed cold agglutinin disease, validating the severe immune dysregulation predicted by the STK4 genotype. The patient responded to corticosteroid therapy and is currently awaiting hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) from his HLA-compatible father.This report illustrates that early integration of WES in complex pediatric cases is essential to anticipate and manage severe complications, shifting the clinical focus from supportive care to curative interventions like HSCT.

Introduction

Temtamy syndrome (TEMTYS) is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic variants in the C12orf57 gene [1]. Since its first description in 1996, approximately 56 cases have been reported worldwide, primarily characterized by intellectual disability, epilepsy, and agenesis of the corpus callosum [2]. Parallelly, STK4 deficiency (also known as MST1 deficiency) has recently emerged as a complex primary immunodeficiency (PID) [3]. Beyond its role in CD4 + T-cell lymphopenia, and recurrent infections, STK4 is a critical regulator of immune tolerance. Its deficiency often leads to profound immune dysregulation, manifesting as early-onset autoimmunity [4]. Notably, autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA), including rare forms such as cold agglutinin disease, is a frequent and severe complication in these patients due to the failure of central and peripheral B-cell tolerance [5]. The diagnosis of such rare entities is particularly challenging in populations with high rates of consanguinity, where «blended phenotypes» are increasingly reported [6].

Here, we report a unique clinical presentation of a Tunisian infant exhibiting a complex blended phenotype. The patient presented classical features of Temtamy syndrome and have a severe primary immunodeficiency and cold agglutinin AIHA, ultimately linked to a concomitant homozygous STK4 mutation. This case underscores the necessity of genomic testing in infants with multi-organ involvement and highlights the spectrum of autoimmunity in STK4 deficiency.

Serum immunoglobulin quantity demonstrated a disharmonic profile. While IgG levels were within the normal range for age (5.2 g/L), there was a significant deficiency in both IgA (0.28 g/L) and IgM (0.39 g/L).

Sanger sequencing confirmed that both parents were heterozygous carriers for both variants, consistent with an autosomal recessive mode of inheritance for two independent loci. Unfortunately, DNA samples from the affected siblings and cousins were not available.

Given the profound combined immunodeficiency and the risk of life-threatening infections, a proactive therapeutic strategy was implemented. The patient started on a regular intravenous immunoglobulin replacement therapy program. Additionally, long-term antibio-prophylaxis was instituted to prevent opportunistic infections. As hematopoietic stem cell transplantation remains the only curative treatment for the immunological defect in STK4 deficiency, the procedure has been scheduled.

The clinical course was marked by a major hematological complication at the age of 3 years. The patient was hospitalized for life-threatening acute anemia, with a hemoglobin level of 2.3 g/dL and a reticulocytes level of 408,200/mm3. The initial complete blood count performed at room temperature revealed a pathognomonic laboratory artifact: a significantly elevated mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC) (71.1 g/dL) associated with an artifactually elevated mean corpuscular volume (119 fL). These findings suggested red blood cell (RBC) clumping induced by cold agglutinins. To resolve these parameters, the blood sample was incubated at 37° for 24 h. Post-incubation analysis showed a complete normalization of the MCHC and RBC indices, confirming the thermal amplitude of the autoantibodies. Immune investigations revealed a positive direct Coombs test with the presence of cold hemagglutinins, confirming a diagnosis of AIHA. This episode of immune dysregulation responds favorably to thermal protection, symptomatic support with warmed blood transfusions and corticosteroid therapy. The patient experienced recurrent hemolytic exacerbations triggered by exposure during the colder periods of the year. At the latest clinical follow-up at 4 years of age, the patient's growth parameters remained at the lower limits of the curves (Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5). From a neurological standpoint, he showed slow but steady progress. However, clinical evaluation estimated a developmental age of 24 months, accompanied by significant language delay and axial hypotonia. Notably, the patient has not developed any epileptic seizures to date. Discussion

The diagnostic journey of our patient underscores a growing phenomenon in genomic medicine: the blended phenotype. In regions with high rates of consanguinity, such as North Africa and the Middle East, the assumption that a complex clinical presentation stems from a single genetic locus is increasingly being challenged. Recent large-scale studies have shown that approximately 5% to 7% of patients referred for exome sequencing who receive a molecular diagnosis actually have two or more independent genetic disorders [6]. Our report of the first co-occurrence of TEMTYS and STK4 deficiency reinforces the necessity of multi-locus thinking when clinical features such as severe autoimmunity and PID fall outside the expected spectrum of a primary neurodevelopmental syndrome.

C12orf57 and the neurodevelopmental landscape

TEMTYS is an ultra-rare condition where the C12orf57 variant disrupts essential pathways for midline brain lateralization. The hypoplasia of the splenium of the corpus callosum and the microcephaly observed in our patient are consistent with the natural history of the disease [1]. Interestingly, while epilepsy and structural ocular anomalies like chorioretinal coloboma are frequently reported in TEMTYS, our patient remained seizure-free up to 4 years of age and exhibited a milder ocular phenotype restricted to horizontal nystagmus and divergent strabismus. While neurodevelopmental milestones are significantly delayed, the slow but steady acquisition of sitting and assisted walking in our case provides valuable prognostic data for clinicians managing TEMTYS, as longitudinal data remain scarce in the literature [7], [8].

STK4 (MST1) deficiency: a pivot of immune dysregulation

STK4 deficiency is a complex primary immunodeficiency that extends far beyond a simple susceptibility to infections. STK4 (serine/threonine kinase 4) is a core component of the Hippo signaling pathway, which is vital for lymphocyte survival, adhesion, and trafficking [9]. The most remarkable feature in our patient was the severe cold agglutinin disease with hemoglobin nadir of 2.3 g/dL. Study limitations and variant pathogenicity

A notable limitation of this study is the unavailability of DNA samples from other affected siblings and cousins, which precluded a comprehensive familial segregation analysis. Familial testing was restricted to the parents, who were confirmed to be asymptomatic heterozygous carriers for both variants. Nevertheless, the pathogenicity classification of both variants was established according to the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation guidelines. The high clinical specificity of the dual phenotype, combined with the rarity of these homozygous variants in public databases provides robust evidence supporting their causative role.

Clinical management and curative prospects

While the neurological impairments of TEMTYS currently lack molecular therapy, the immunological defect in STK4 deficiency is potentially curable. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is the treatment of choice to restore T-cell counts and re-establish immune tolerance [10]. The successful use of a heterozygous parent as a donor is an important consideration is consanguineous families when a fully matched sibling is unavailable. However, clinicians must be aware that while cell transplantation corrects the immune deficiency and prevents further life-threatening autoimmune disorders, the neurodevelopmental trajectory remains governed by the C12orf57 variant necessitating long-term multidisciplinary support.

Conclusion

This case provides a powerful clinical lesson on the phenotypic expansion versus dual diagnosis dilemma. The identification of STK4 deficiency prior to the onset of severe hematological complications illustrates the predictive value of early genomic intervention. While TEMTYS explained the neurodevelopmental delay, only the molecular discovery of a second genetic «hit» could account for the subsequent life-threatening cold agglutinin disease.

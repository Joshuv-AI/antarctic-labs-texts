Development and validation of a nomogram for osteoporosis based on clinical characteristics in Kunming
Authors: Xiaohan Tan, Chai Yuan, Jiabao Liao, Jie Zhao, Yuanliang Ai, Hanyu He, Weibo Wen, Xuehua Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe exploration of clinical characteristics associated with osteoporosis (OP) is crucial due to its high incidence and complex pathogenesis. Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP), a prevalent systemic skeletal disorder, significantly affects patient quality of life and represents a considerable challenge to the global public health system [1]. The global prevalence of OP is approximately 18.3%, making it one of the diseases with extremely high incidence, disability rates, mortality, and substantial medical costs [2, 3]. In the early stages of OP, the condition may only manifest as pain and limited mobility. However, as OP progresses, it can lead to severe bone deformities and fractures, significantly impacting the quality of life in OP patients [4, 5]. With the ageing of the population, the number of OP patients is steadily increasing, gradually becoming a global health issue [6]. Therefore, considering the substantial social harm caused by OP, research into its diagnosis, prevention, and treatment is of critical importance.

The clinical manifestations of OP include low bone mineral density (BMD), decreased bone mass and destruction of bone tissue microstructure, all of which contribute to increased bone fragility and heightened fracture susceptibility in OP patients during daily activities [7]. The pathogenesis of OP is complex, involving multiple factors such as nutrition, physiology and genetics, with the imbalance between bone formation and resorption being the primary cause [8]. For example, postmenopausal women or elderly women are often more susceptible to OP due to decreased estrogen levels [9]. Additionally, clinical characteristics such as gender, age, family history of osteoporosis, alcohol consumption, smoking, and hormone use are also considered potential risk factors for OP [10–12]. Consequently, in-depth research into the relationship between clinical characteristics and OP is crucial for developing effective diagnostic, prevention and treatment strategies.

To date, several studies analyzing the clinical characteristics of OP patients have been published. Wang et al. analyzed nine clinical characteristics, including age, gender, manual labor status, weight and smoking history, in 584 asymptomatic elderly individuals with OP in Beijing [13]. Juan et al. analyzed approximately 50 clinical characteristics among 386 middle-aged and elderly inpatients with sarco-osteoporosis [14]. Li et al. analyzed nearly 30 clinical characteristics in 379 OP patients with type 2 diabetes [15]. These studies demonstrated that clinical characteristics such as gender, weight, height, albumin levels, lean mass index, and serum creatinine levels have significant diagnostic value for OP. Although these studies analyzed more than 300 individuals, the results would be more statistically significant if a larger sample size, such as over 1000 individuals, could be included. Furthermore, if these studies incorporated more clinical characteristics, they could identify additional clinical characteristics that are beneficial for the diagnosis, prevention and treatment of OP. Prediction models serve as vital tools for assisting clinicians in incorporating patient-specific factors when formulating individualized treatment plans. Although various tools have been previously developed for predicting OP risk, such as the Fracture Risk Assessment Tool (FRAX), the Garvan fracture risk calculator, and QFracture scores, these tools exhibit significant limitations [16]. For instance, FRAX has been debated due to its limited number of risk factors, low granularity, and failure to account for dose-dependent relationships [17]. Nomograms, which enable visualization of results from analyses such as Cox regression and present a low learning cost for physicians, are suitable for predicting individual disease risks [18]. In clinical practice, nomograms offer intuitive and user-friendly advantages, facilitating clinicians’ calculation of specific event probabilities and enhancing clinical adoption [19]. This study integrates logistic regression analysis with machine learning, which possesses powerful computational and data-fitting capabilities particularly suited for handling complex clinical data [20]. Machine learning can effectively reduce noise in real-world data and accurately identify associations between features [21]. In recent years, machine learning has been widely applied in OP research. Despite some limitations, the in-depth application of machine learning has led to certain advances in the early diagnosis and risk assessment of OP [22]. Considering the inherent over fitting and potential bias risks in machine learning, we employed LASSO to mitigate these concerns. LASSO—a machine learning algorithm—is widely used for constructing disease prediction models due to its efficient variable selection and regularization to prevent over fitting [23].

Therefore, we recruited a large cohort of 1,847 patients with OP-related conditions. After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, a final sample of 1,220 middle-aged and elderly participants residing in Kunming remained. We collected approximately 200 clinical characteristics per participant and employed machine learning to screen for those significantly linked to OP, subsequently using them to build a diagnostic nomogram model. The performance of the nomogram model was validated using ROC curves, calibration curves, and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA). These identified clinical characteristics, significantly associated with OP, may aid in the diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of OP.

Materials and methods

Study participants

A total of 1,847 participants who visited the Physical Examination Center of Yunnan Provincial Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital from December 2021 to March 2022 were enrolled in this study. The diagnostic criteria for OP referred to the “Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Osteoporosis (2022)”. Inclusion criteria: (1) Long-term residence in Kunming (≥ 5 years); (2) Age over 45 years; (3) Ability to understand and agree to participate; (4) Good language communication skills. Exclusion criteria: (1) Temporarily residing in Kunming without a long-term history of living in Kunming (< 5 years); (2) Participants with severe systemic underlying diseases or mental illnesses; (3) Participants with incomplete data. This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Yunnan Provincial Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital (Approval No.: Ke [2020] Ethics Review (006)-01). After selection, a total of 1,220 participants were ultimately included in this study.

Clinical data collection and comparison of clinical characteristics

For the 1,220 participants included in this study, bone mineral density (BMD) T-scores were measured for the L1-L5 vertebrae, and the average value was calculated for each participant. Based on the OP diagnostic criterion of average T-score (ave T) < -2.5, the 1,220 participants were divided into an OP group and a non-OP group. After OP diagnosis for the patients, the OP group comprised 480 individuals, while the non-OP group included 780 individuals.

We collected clinical data on seven categories of clinical characteristics for all 1,220 participants. These categories included: Basic Information, Family Health History, Life Habit, Disease History, Drug History, Questionnaire and Laboratory Characteristic. Basic Information encompasses Age, Sex, Height, Weight, Degree of Education, etc. Family Health History includes Osteoporosis in Family, Alzheimer’s Disease in Family, Cardiovascular Disease in Family, etc. Life Habit covers whether one consumes Spicy food, Smokes, Drinks, etc. Disease History comprises Cerebral Infarction, Hyperthyroidism, Gout, among others. Drug History involves Calcium, Vitamin D, Aspirin, and so forth. The Questionnaire addresses issues like Diarrhoea, Height Shorter, Mobility, etc. Laboratory Characteristics involve Bone mineral density-related characteristics (YZL1BMD, YZL2BMD, YZL3BMD, YZL4BMD, YZL5BMD, GGBMD, ZZBMD, NBBMD, KBBMD, WORDSBMD), Left hand muscle strength, Right hand muscle strength, Sitting and standing test, Diastolic blood pressure, Triglycerides, and so on. In total, we collected clinical data on approximately 200 clinical characteristics and conducted a significant difference analysis between the OP group and the non-OP group to identify clinical characteristics closely related to OP.

Machine learning screening of clinical characteristics

In accordance with previous research, all 1,220 participant were randomly divided into a training set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio, with the training set used for model construction and the validation set for model validation [24]. In the training set, a univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted based on clinical characteristics that showed significant differences between the OP group and the non-OP group to screen for characteristics associated with OP, using P < 0.05 as the threshold. Next, we performed the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis to further screen for clinical characteristics highly relevant to OP. Specifically, LASSO achieves feature selection and model simplification by penalizing coefficients via L1 regularization. Furthermore, we determined the optimal lambda value through 10-fold cross-validation, retaining features with non-zero coefficients based on the optimal lambda criterion. The clinical characteristics identified by the LASSO analysis were subsequently analyzed using multivariate logistic regression analysis to determine characteristics more significantly associated with OP. The univariate logistic regression analysis, LASSO regression analysis, and multivariate logistic regression analysis were all conducted using the R package “glmnet (v4.1-4)”.

Construction of nomogram model

In the training set, we used the R package “rms (v6.9-0)” to construct a nomogram model based on the clinical characteristics associated with OP that were identified through machine learning. In both the training and validation sets, the ROC curves generated by the R package “pROC (v1.18.5)”, the calibration curves produced by the R package “rms (v6.9-0)”, and the DCA (Decision Curve Analysis) curves generated by the R package “rmda (v1.6)” were used to evaluate the prediction ability of the nomogram model to distinguish the OP samples from non-OP samples.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using the R software (v4.4.1). The R package “tableone (v0.13.2)” was used to analyze the significant differences in baseline clinical characteristics between the OP group and the non-OP group. The continuous variables that followed a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were analyzed for significant differences between groups using Student’s t-test. The continuous variables that did not followed a normal distribution were presented as median (first quartile, Q1; third quartile, Q3) and were compared between groups using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Non-continuous count variables were presented as n (%), and comparisons between groups were performed using the chi-square test. A p-value < 0.05 is considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

Clinical characteristics with significant differences

Results of univariate logistic regression

Screening of clinical characteristics via LASSO regression

Using the training set, a LASSO regression analysis was conducted on the 37 clinical characteristics selected through univariate logistic regression analysis. Following 10-fold cross-validation, the optimal lambda parameter (lambda.best) was determined as 0.00061, corresponding to the lambda value (lambda.min) that resulted in the lowest model error rate (Fig. 1A). Under the condition of selecting the optimal lambda value, the regression coefficients of 33 clinical characteristics remained non-zero, indicating their significance in the optimal model (Fig. 1B). These 33 clinical characteristics included Age, Sex, Smoke, Drink, Height, Weight, Drink Heavily, Height Shorter, Time at home workday, Bone mineral density-related characteristics (YZL1BMD, YZL2BMD, YZL3BMD, YZL4BMD, YZL5BMD, ZZBMD, NBBMD, KBBMD, and WORDSBMD), and Diastolic blood pressure, among others.

Screening of clinical characteristics via multivariable logistic regression analysis

Using the 33 clinical characteristics selected through the optimal LASSO regression analysis, a multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted based on the training set. The results showed that 18 clinical characteristics were significant predictors for OP (p < 0.05, Table 3). These 18 clinical characteristics were Age (OR = 0.878), Sex (OR = 7.30E + 04), Smoke (OR = 14.388), Left hand muscle strength (OR = 0.725), Right hand muscle strength (OR = 0.683), Height Shorter (OR = 11.874), Time at home workday (OR = 1.260), Bone mineral density-related characteristics (YZL1BMD, OR = 1.04E-17; YZL2BMD, OR = 1.23E-14; YZL3BMD, OR = 2.30E-24; YZL4BMD, OR = 5.62E-11; YZL5BMD, OR = 2.52E-52; NBBMD, OR = 5416.062; KBBMD, OR = 3.35E-14), Diastolic blood pressure (OR = 0.876), Creatinine (OR = 1.122), P1NP (OR = 0.894), and B-CTx (OR = 3399.504). Among the 18 clinical characteristics closely related to OP that were selected through machine learning, those corresponding to bone mineral density deserve more attention due to their strong correlation with OP (Table 3).

Construction of nomogram model of clinical characteristics selected through machine learning

Based on the 18 clinical characteristic selected through machine learning, an OP diagnostic nomogram model was constructed in training set using linear regression. As shown in Fig. 2, each clinical characteristic was assigned a specific point and the total point was calculated by summing the points of these characteristics. The probability of OP was then predicted based on the total points, with higher total points indicating a greater probability of OP. Notably, characteristics related to bone mineral density (such as YZL1BMD, YZL2BMD, YZL3BMD, YZL4BMD, YZL5BMD), Diastolic blood pressure, and Creatinine achieved higher points, highlighting their substantial diagnostic value for OP (Fig. 2).

Nomogram model for the diagnosis of OP. Each clinical characteristic has an associated point, and the total point is the sum of all individual points. Drawing a vertical line at the total point yields the probability value for OP

Validation of nomogram model

The ROC curves based on the training set and the validation set were utilized to evaluate the diagnostic capability of the nomogram model for OP. A model’s predictive performance is considered better as the Area Under the Curve (AUC) value of the ROC curve approaches 1. As shown in Fig. 3A and B, the nomogram model demonstrated outstanding predictive ability, as evidenced by AUC values of 0.998 in the training set and 0.994 in the validation set. Furthermore, characteristics related to bone mineral density showed strong predictive capability for OP (Fig. 3A and B). Notably, the AUC values for YZL1BMD, YZL2BMD, YZL3BMD, YZL4BMD and YZL5BMD exceeded 0.9 in both the training and validation sets. For NBBMD and KBBMD, the AUC values in the training set were greater than 0.8, while the AUC values in the validation set approached 0.8. This indicated that the six characteristics related to bone mineral density had a significant contribution to the excellent predictive performance of the nomogram model.

The calibration curves demonstrated that, in the training set, the ideal curve closely aligned with the actual observed curve, with an mean absolute error of 0.004 (Fig. 4A). The mean absolute error in the validation set was 0.007, indicating that the nomogram model possessed high accuracy (Fig. 4B). The DCA curves indicated that the net benefit curves for the nomogram model in both the training and validation sets were significantly distant from the two extreme scenarios, indicating a strong clinical application value of the nomogram model (Fig. 5A and B).

Discussion

Osteoporosis (OP) is a chronic skeletal disease characterized by abnormalities in both the micro- and macro-structure of bones, resulting in reduced bone mass and an increased risk of fragility fractures [25]. OP is particularly prevalent among the elderly, with OP patients often experiencing symptoms such as lower back pain, kyphosis, limited mobility, generalized aches and discomfort, spinal deformity, and a reduction in height [26–28]. Over the past decade, the increasing incidence of OP has prompted the exploration of risk factors associated with OP, such as aging and the decline in bone density. These factors have become critical points of intervention to prevent the occurrence and progression of the disease, with their significance becoming increasingly apparent [29, 30]. Given the severe impact of OP on patient health, especially in hospitalized individuals [31], exploring its associated clinical characteristics and making early diagnoses of OP based on these characteristics can aid in the intervention and treatment of OP.

This study employed univariate and multivariate logistic regression, along with LASSO regression, to screen for 18 characteristics closely associated with OP. These 18 clinical characteristics included Age, Sex, Smoke, Left hand muscle strength, Right hand muscle strength, Height Shorter, Time at home workday, Bone mineral density-related characteristics (YZL1BMD, YZL2BMD, YZL3BMD, YZL4BMD, YZL5BMD, NBBMD and KBBMD), Diastolic blood pressure, Creatinine, P1NP, and B-CTx. The nomogram model constructed based on these characteristics has been proven to perform excellently through ROC curves (AUC > 0.9), calibration curves, and DCA curves. Although comparison with published OP prediction models is challenging due to differences in model design, study populations, outcome definitions, and validation strategies, our model significantly outperformed a recently published OP prediction model built using multiple machine learning techniques (AUC = 0.887) and a nomogram model developed based on the UK Biobank (AUC = 0.844) [32, 33]. This highlights the potential for high accuracy in our model. We believe that some of these 18 clinical characteristics will contribute to the prevention, intervention and treatment of OP.

Consistent with our analysis, Age (OR = 0.878, 95%CI: 0.771 ∼ 0.985) is a critical factor related to the occurrence of OP [34]. As people age, the incidence of OP gradually increases [35]. This is mainly because aging leads to changes in various physiological functions and the skeletal system of the human body, with decreased activity of bone cells, bone marrow cells, and other cells, resulting in bone formation disorders [36, 37]. Additionally, elderly individuals may experience malnutrition, decreased outdoor activities, inadequate sunlight exposure, and other factors that affect calcium absorption and utilization, which are detrimental to bone health [38, 39]. In summary, these factors collectively make elderly individuals more susceptible to OP, thereby increasing the prevalence of the disease.

We observed a significant correlation between decrease in height (Height Shorter, OR = 11.874, 95%CI: 1.306 ∼ 182.504) and OP. Ko et al. investigated the incidence of OP in elderly men in Taiwan and found a correlation between shorter height and OP [40]. This indicates that there is an interplay between height and OP. A study showed that height loss is an indicator of bone mineral density (BMD) [41]. Bone and muscle are two closely linked tissues that are chemically and metabolically connected. As people age, both bone mass and muscle mass decrease annually, and an imbalance in the regulation of bone and muscle can lead to low BMD [42]. Since BMD is an important indicator of OP occurrence [43, 44], the decrease in bone mass and muscle mass that can lead to low BMD may be associated with the decrease in height in OP patients. However, the specific underlying mechanisms require further investigation.

It has been demonstrated that the change in muscle mass is crucial to the development of OP [45]. The lean mass index (LMI) is an important indicator of muscle mass, and a higher LMI generally indicates greater muscle mass [46]. Research has demonstrated a positive correlation between a higher LMI and higher BMD, possibly due to the mechanical load placed on the skeletal system by the muscles and bones in lean mass [47, 48]. LMI also could serve as an important indicator for diagnosing OP, with a higher LMI associated with a lower prevalence of OP [49]. These findings are consistent with our results, which showed that Left hand muscle strength (OR = 0.725, 95%CI: 0.526 ∼ 0.958) and Right hand muscle strength (OR = 0.683, 95%CI: 0.498 ∼ 0.889) were both closely linked to OP and serve as protective factors against OP. The reduction in lean mass is associated with an increased risk of fractures, especially hip fractures. For every standard deviation loss of lean mass, the risk of hip fracture increases by 29–38% [50]. Therefore, maintaining appropriate muscle mass is essential for the prevention and treatment of osteoporosis.

Our results highlighted several clinical characteristics (YZL1BMD, YZL2BMD, YZL3BMD, YZL4BMD, YZL5BMD, NBBMD and KBBMD) related to BMD that were most closely associated with OP, supporting previous findings that BMD is a primary clinical manifestation of OP [43, 44]. Furthermore, we found that Creatinine (OR = 1.122, 95%CI: 1.035 ∼ 1.247) was also significantly associated with OP. Creatinine, also known as muscle creatinine, originates from the breakdown of creatine in muscle tissue and is a metabolic waste product in the body [51]. Serum creatinine not only stably reflects the status of muscle mass but is also an important indicator for assessing renal function [52]. Existing research showed that the intensity of physical activity could further influence BMD by regulating serum creatinine levels [53]. Specifically, in individuals with normal renal function, a strong positive correlation exists between serum creatinine and BMD, meaning that as serum creatinine levels increase, BMD levels also tend to increase [54]. In contrast, studies have reported a negative correlation between serum creatinine levels and BMD in individuals with renal insufficiency, where higher creatinine levels are associated with greater bone loss [55]. It is noteworthy that impaired renal function itself is a risk factor for OP, which may confound the relationship between creatinine and BMD to some extent [56]. Although the vast majority of participants included in this study had normal renal function, partially explaining our findings, future research is needed to further validate this observation in broader populations, particularly among OP patients with concurrent renal impairment. Additionally, the correlation between LMI and OP can be somewhat strengthened by the mediating role of serum creatinine, with the impact of LMI on OP partially attributed to the mediation of serum creatinine [57]. Therefore, we can speculate that Creatinine may act as a mediator of the effect of LMI on BMD, thereby playing an important role in the occurrence and development of OP.

Current OP treatments mainly include basic therapies and drug therapies, such as adjusting dietary structure, physical exercise, bisphosphonates, and teriparatide [58]. However, because OP is a chronic disease and current drugs have side effects, treatment effectiveness remains unsatisfactory [59, 60]. This study was conducted in Kunming, China, where Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) is widely used for OP prevention and treatment. Notably, a network meta-analysis shows that alendronate combined with TCM works better than alendronate alone [61]. Additionally, multiple studies confirm TCM counteracts OP through multiple pathways and targets [62, 63]. The clinical features identified in our study provide powerful tools for early OP risk screening, potentially enabling integrated Chinese-Western medicine for OP prevention and treatment. For example, high-risk individuals identified through screening could receive evidence-based TCM interventions alongside standard Western treatments, aiming to enhance effects, reduce Western drug dosage/side effects, and improve quality of life. Therefore, our findings will help establish risk-stratified precision strategies for integrated Chinese-Western OP prevention, providing scientific basis for optimizing clinical decisions and improving OP management outcomes.

Certainly, our study also has some limitations. Firstly, our patient samples are solely sourced from Kunming, lacking geographical diversity, which makes it impossible to exclude the interference of local natural environmental and other factors. Secondly, we collected a large cohort of middle-aged and elderly patients, exceeding 1000 individuals, with a wealth of clinical characteristics amounting to approximately 200. Although the nomogram model constructed based on clinical characteristics selected through machine learning has already demonstrated good performance, if we had not confined ourselves to the three machine learning methods of univariate logistic regression, LASSO regression, and multivariate logistic regression, and instead utilized a broader range of machine learning approaches, we might have identified even more significant clinical characteristics. Furthermore, as overfitting is a universal issue in machine learning, despite implementing multiple methods to mitigate this risk, including having a sample size substantially larger than the number of clinical features, employing 10-fold cross-validation, and validating the model in the validation set, this risk cannot be eliminated. Therefore, future validation in larger clinical cohorts remains necessary.

Conclusion

In summary, this study successfully constructed a nomogram model incorporating clinical characteristics such as Age, Sex, Smoke, Left hand muscle strength, Right hand muscle strength, Height Shorter, Bone mineral density-related characteristics and Creatinine using univariate logistic regression, LASSO regression and multivariate logistic regression analysis. The nomogram model was validated and showed an AUC value close to 1, demonstrating its high discriminative power between OP and non-OP patients and excellent diagnostic performance for OP. These findings suggest that the clinical characteristics we identified and the nomogram model constructed based on them will be beneficial for the diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of OP.

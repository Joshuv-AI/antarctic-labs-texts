Topological groups from matrices of sets
Boriša Kuzeljević, Stepan Milošević, Stevo Todorčević

Abstract
. We give a general construction of topological groups from combinatorial structures such as trees, towers, gaps, and subadditive functions. We connect topological properties of corresponding groups with combinatorial properties of these objects. For example, the group built from an ω 1 \omega_{1} -tree is Frechét iff the tree is Aronszajn. We also determine cofinal types of some of these groups under certain set theoretic assumptions.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this note is to provide a general construction of new examples of topological groups.
The key feature of groups we will present is that they are all built based on well known set-theoretic objects such as subadditive functions, trees, gaps, and towers.
For all undefined notions, we refer the reader to Section
2
.
This research direction originated in the work by the third author, note the most recent contribution in
[
Tod24
, Theorem 4.9]
and results in
[
Tod07
]
, and extended by the second and the third author in
[
MT25
]
.
There, topological groups were constructed from characteristics of walks on ordinals.
There were related constructions preceeding these, the reader can take a look at
[
DW93
,
EGK+95
,
CMS20
,
CMCRS23
]
and a historical remark after Theorem 4.9 in
[
Tod24
]
.
Here we generalize those constructions and find new examples.
In our framework, we are able to characterize Frechétness of analyzed groups via combinatorial properties of underlying objects.
For example, by Theorem
6.5
the group we get from an
\omega_{1}
-tree is Frechét iff the tree is Aronszajn.

There are many problems in the literature involving Frechét topological groups.
To mention just a few, recall that the solution to Malykhin’s problem has been completed recently in
[
HRG14
]
, see also
[
HS21
]
.
It asks whether there is a countable Frechét non-metrizable group, see
[
Ark81
, Question 26]
.
The solution is that, although there are many consistent examples, there is a model where no such group exists.
There is also a well-known, still open, problem whether in
{\rm ZFC}
there is a Frechét group whose square is not Frechét.
See
[
Ark81
, pp. 154]
for a discussion about this class of problems or
[
Sha02
, pp. 474]
where the problem is asked as mentioned.
The furthest advancement on this problem is in
[
Tod93
]
where two Frechét groups are constructed whose product is not countably tight.
Another problem we find interesting was posed by Feng in
[
Fen22
]
: Is there, in
{\rm ZFC}
, a non-metrizable Frechét group
G
such that
[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\not\leq_{T}G
.
One consistent example is
G_{\rho_{2}}(\theta)
for
\theta>\omega_{1}
using
\square(\theta)
from
[
Tod24
, Theorem 4.9]
, while the other is
G_{\rho_{2}}(\omega_{1})
using Ostaszewski’s club principle in
[
MT25
]
.
This question relates the problem of metrizability of a group to its Tukey type.
This is expected as in this language, Birkhoff-Kakutani theorem says that a group
G
is metrizable if and only if
G\leq_{T}\omega
.
It should also be mentioned that the third author in
[
Tod23
]
has found another equivalent condition for metrizability in the class of Frechét groups.
He showed that a Frechét group is metrizable if and only if it is Tukey reducible to a basic order in the sense of
[
ST04
]
.

Tukey types of topological groups and spaces have been recently investigated in papers by Gartside, Feng, Morgan, and Mamatelashvili, the reader can take a look at
[
GF17
,
GM21
,
GM18
,
GM24
,
Mor16
]
.
Some of their work is formulated in terms of calibres.
Usually this can be rephrased in the language of Tukey reductions - for example,
D
has calibre
(\omega_{1},\omega)
if and only if
[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\not\leq_{T}D
.
The recent paper
[
FG24
]
is relevant for the general Tukey theory of topological groups as, among other results, they show that for any directed set
D
there is a topological group
G
such that
G\equiv_{T}D
.
Significant related theory, although presented in a different language, can be found in an extensive paper of Banakh
[
Ban19
]
.
For this line of research, one can take a look at
[
GKL15
]
,
[
BKS22
]
, or
[
DF21
]
and
[
Fen22
]
.
Already mentioned
[
Tod23
]
also provides a more general view on that topic.

1.1.
The outline of the paper

In Section
3
, we describe our general framework for building topological groups from
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrices where
\lambda\leq\kappa
are regular infinite cardinals.
The parameter
\kappa
refers to the cardinality and character of the group, which are both
\kappa^{+}
, while the parameter
\lambda
refers to the tightness of the group.
In Section
4
we show that the group obtained from a strong matrix is Frechét iff the matrix is unbounded in a certain well-defined sense.
In Section
5
we show that each transitive function gives a matrix, while subadditive one gives a strong matrix.
As a corollary we get groups from metrics on ordinals and from
\kappa^{+}
-towers in
\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
.
In Section
6
we present two constructions of topological groups from trees.
The first is very general, and gives the mentioned Theorem
6.5
: the group is Frechét iff the tree is Aronszajn.
The other is for a specific class of trees, and allows one to get the non-metrizable Frechét group
G(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T})
which does not have
[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
as a Tukey quotient.
This gives another consistent counterexample for Feng’s question, built from a particular Souslin tree.
In the last section, Section
7
, we give a construction of the topological group from any
(\kappa^{+},\kappa^{+})
pre-gap in
\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
.
This is different in a sense that we do not get exactly a matrix, but we show that the obtained family is good enough to give the subbase for our topological group.
Then we prove that in order for our group to be countably tight, the pre-gap in fact has to be a gap.

2.
Preliminaries

We use mostly standard notation.
When set theory is concerned, standard reference with all the relevant background is
[
Jec03
]
.
If
A
is a set, then its cardinality is denoted
\left|A\right|
.
For a set
A
and a cardinal
\kappa
, we denote
[A]^{\kappa}=\left\{X\subseteq A:\left|X\right|=\kappa\right\}
.
Sets
[A]^{<\kappa}
and
[A]^{\leq\kappa}
are defined similarly.
For a set
A
, we denote its powerset by
\mathcal{P}(A)
.
For sets
A
and
B
, function
f:A\to B
, and
X\subseteq A
we denote
f[X]=\left\{f(x):x\in X\right\}
.
For
Y\subseteq B
we denote
f^{-1}[Y]=\left\{a\in A:f(a)\in Y\right\}
.
For sets of ordinals
a
and
b
we write
a<b
when
\alpha<\beta
for all
\alpha\in a
and
\beta\in b
.
All variations on this notion are handled similarly.
If
\theta
is a regular cardinal, then
H(\theta)
is the set of all sets of hereditary cardinality less than
\theta
.
In this case,
H(\theta)
satisfies all axioms of
\rm{ZFC}
except the powerset axiom.
If
\kappa
is a cardinal,
C\subseteq\kappa
is a
club
in
\kappa
if it is closed and unbounded in
\kappa
.
A set
S\subseteq\kappa
is
stationary
if it intersects every club in
\kappa
.
Every club in
\kappa
is stationary in
\kappa
.
The following lemma is well known, and will be used later in the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta
be an uncountable regular cardinal,
\kappa
a regular cardinal such that
\kappa^{+}<\theta
,
S
stationary in
\kappa^{+}
, and
X\in H(\theta)
of size at most
\kappa
.
Then, there is an elementary submodel
M
of
H(\theta)
such that
\left|M\right|=\kappa
,
X\subseteq M
, and
\kappa^{+}\cap M\in S
.

For Tukey theory, a good reference for standard notions is
[
Tod85
]
.
A poset
(D,\leq_{D})
is
directed set
if for all
x
and
y
in
D
, there is
z
in
D
such that
x\leq_{D}z
and
y\leq_{D}z
.
For example, in a topological space, a local base of any point is directed by the relation
\supseteq
.
If
(D,\leq_{D})
is a directed set, then
X\subseteq D
is
bounded
if there is
d\in D
such that
x\leq_{D}d
for all
x\in X
, otherwise it is
unbounded
.
A set
Y\subseteq D
is
cofinal
in
D
if for each
d\in D
there is
y\in Y
such that
d\leq_{D}y
.
For directed sets
(D,\leq_{D})
and
(E,\leq_{E})
, we say that
f:D\to E
is a
Tukey map
if
f[X]
is unbounded in
E
for any unbounded
X
in
D
.
This is the same as saying that the preimage of any bounded set is bounded.
When there is a Tukey map
f:D\to E
, we say that
D
is Tukey reducible to
E
(or that
D
is a Tukey quotient of
E
) and write
D\leq_{T}E
.
It is well-known that
D\leq_{T}E
iff there is convergent map from
E
to
D
(see
[
Tod85
]
or
[
Sch55
]
).
Recall that
f:E\to D
is a
convergent
(cofinal) map if for any
d\in D
there is
e\in E
such that
f(x)\geq_{D}d
for all
x\geq_{E}e
.
This is the same as saying that the image of any cofinal set is cofinal.
Tukey proved that
D\leq_{T}E
and
E\leq_{T}D
(in short
D\equiv_{T}E
) iff both
D
and
E
can be embedded as cofinal subsets into a single poset (see
[
Tuk40
]
or
[
Tod85
]
).
We will often use the following remark.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\kappa
is a regular infinite cardinal and
A\subseteq[\kappa]^{<\omega}
is of cardinality
\kappa
and directed with respect to
\subseteq
, then
\left([\kappa]^{<\omega},\subseteq\right)\equiv_{T}(A,\subseteq)
.

For topological groups, standard reference is
[
AT08
]
.
A
topological group
is an abstract group
(G,\cdot)
equipped with a topology such that operations
\cdot:G\times G\to G
and
{}^{-1}:G\to G
are continuous.
For an abstract group
G
, we typically denote its identity by
e
.
Tukey theory in the context of topological groups has been present for a long time, but for basic facts and definitions we refer to
[
KM24
]
. In particular, if
\mathcal{B}_{e}
is the collection of all open neighborhoods of the identity
e
in
G
and
(D,\leq_{D})
is a directed set, we say that
G\leq_{T}D
if
(\mathcal{B}_{e},\supseteq)\leq_{T}(D,\leq_{D})
.
We define
G\leq_{T}H
similarly for groups
G
and
H
.
We will mostly use the following fact for building topological groups, see
[
AT08
, Theorem 1.3.12]
or
[
HR79
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group with the identity
e
, and
\mathcal{U}
a family of subsets of
G
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
there is an element
V\in\mathcal{U}
such that
V^{2}\subseteq U
;

(2)

for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
there is an element
V\in\mathcal{U}
such that
V^{-1}\subseteq U
;

(3)

for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
and every
x\in U
, there is
V\in\mathcal{U}
such that
Vx\subseteq U
;

(4)

for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
x\in G
, there is
V\in\mathcal{U}
such that
xVx^{-1}\subseteq U
;

(5)

for
U,V\in\mathcal{U}
, there is
W\in\mathcal{U}
such that
W\subseteq U\cap V
;

(6)

\left\{e\right\}=\bigcap\mathcal{U}
.

Then
G
is a topological group with the topology generated by the base
\left\{Ua:a\in G\right\}
, i.e.
\mathcal{U}
is an open base of the identity in
G
.

The notation
\overline{A}
will be reserved for the closure of
A
in the appropriate topology.

A topological group
G
is
\emph{countablytight}
if for all
A\subseteq G
with
e\in\overline{A}
, there is a countable
X\subseteq A
such that
e\in\overline{X}
.
For a cardinal
\lambda
, we say that
G
has
tightness at most
\lambda
if for every
A\subseteq G
with
e\in\overline{A}
, there is
X\in[A]^{\leq\lambda}
such that
e\in\overline{X}
.

A topological group
G
is
Frechét
if for all
A\subseteq G
with
e\in\overline{A}
, there is a sequence
s
in
A
such that
\lim s=e
.
Recall that
G
is an
(\alpha_{1})
-group
if for any countable collection of sequences
\left\{s_{n}:n<\omega\right\}
in
G
such that
\lim s_{n}=e
for each
n<\omega
, there is a sequence
s
in
G
such that
\lim s=e
and that
s\setminus s_{n}
is finite for all
n<\omega
.
The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
G
is a topological group of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
which is an increasing union of metrizable subsets, then
G
is an
(\alpha_{1})
-group.

3.
Matrices

In this section we describe a general method for constructing topological groups on the set
[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
for any infinite regular cardinal
\kappa
.

Let
A
and
B
be sets of ordinals.
We say that a family

\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{\xi}(\alpha):\alpha\in A,\ \xi\in B\right\}

is an
(A,B)
-matrix
if the following conditions hold:

(G1)

\bigcup_{\xi\in B}F_{\xi}(\alpha)=\alpha
for every
\alpha\in A
,

(G2)

F_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq F_{\eta}(\alpha)
for each
\alpha\in C
and all
\xi\leq\eta
in
B
,

(G3)

for all
\alpha\leq\beta
in
A
and each
\xi
in
B
, there is
\eta\in B
so that
F_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq F_{\eta}(\beta)
.

If
\lambda\leq\kappa
are infinite regular cardinals,
\mathcal{F}
is a
(C,\lambda)
-matrix, and
C
is a cofinal subset of
\kappa^{+}
, then we will say
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix indexed by
C
.
If
C
is clear from the context, or not essential, we will just say
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.
Finally, if
\kappa=\lambda
, we will simply say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
\kappa
-matrix.

For the remaining of this section fix
\lambda\leq\kappa
regular infinite cardinals, and a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix
\mathcal{F}
indexed by
C
.
For
\xi<\lambda
and
\alpha\in C
we set

U_{\xi}(\alpha)=\left\{a\in[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}:a\cap F_{\xi}(\alpha)=\emptyset\right\}.

Then, to a matrix
\mathcal{F}
we adjoin a family
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{U_{\xi}(\alpha):\xi<\lambda,\ \alpha\in C\right\}
.

In
[
Tod24
]
and
[
MT25
]
two different
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrices were introduced, depending on characteristics of walks
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
, and it was proved that they give topological groups.
Recall that
\triangle
is the operation of symmetric difference.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda\leq\kappa
are regular infinite cardinals.
Let

\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{\xi}(\alpha):\alpha\in C,\ \xi<\lambda\right\}

be a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.
Then
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
is the neighborhood basis of the identity for a topology on
[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
which makes
([\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega},\triangle)
a topological group.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
satisfies conditions (1)-(6) in Theorem
2.3
.

To see that (1) and (3) are true, take
\xi<\kappa
and
\alpha\in C
.
Note that we have
U_{\xi}(\alpha)\triangle U_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq U_{\xi}(\alpha)
because
a\triangle b\subseteq a\cup b
, and consequently
a\triangle b\in U_{\xi}(\alpha)
for
a,b\in U_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
For (2) just observe that
a\triangle a=\emptyset
for
a\in[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
, so
U^{-1}=U
for each
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
. Condition (4) holds by the commutativity of
\triangle
.

Now we prove item (5).
Take any
U_{\xi}(\alpha)
and
U_{\eta}(\beta)
in
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
.
Assume
\alpha\leq\beta
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix, by (G3), there is some
\xi_{0}
such that
F_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq F_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
.
Now take
\zeta=\max\left\{\eta,\xi_{0}\right\}
.
Then, using (G2), we have
F_{\xi}(\alpha)\cup F_{\eta}(\beta)\subseteq F_{\zeta}(\beta)
, so
U_{\zeta}(\beta)\subseteq U_{\xi}(\alpha)\cap U_{\eta}(\beta)
as required.

To see (6), suppose there is
a\in[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}\setminus\left\{\emptyset\right\}
so that
a\in\bigcap\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
.
Let
\alpha\in C
be such that
a\subseteq\alpha
.
This is possible as
C
is unbounded in
\kappa^{+}
.
Take
x\in a
.
Then, by (G1), there is
\xi<\lambda
so that
x\in F_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
This means that
a\cap F_{\xi}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset
, i.e.
a\notin U_{\xi}(\alpha)
, contradicting the assumption that
a
is a non-empty set in
\bigcap\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix, then
(\mathcal{F},\subseteq)
is a directed set.
This follows from property (G3), and also can be seen as a consequence of Theorem
3.1
or its proof (the proof of (5) from Theorem
2.3
).

For the remaining of the paper, if
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix, then
G(\mathcal{F})
will always denote the topological group given by Theorem
3.1
.
Let us first present some properties of these groups.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
\lambda\leq\kappa
are infinite regular cardinals.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.
Then the group
G(\mathcal{F})
has character
\kappa^{+}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
has cardinality
\kappa^{+}
, so it is enough to prove that there is no local base of
\emptyset
in
G(\mathcal{F})
of cardinality less then
\kappa^{+}
.
Suppose that
\mu\leq\kappa
and that
\left\{W_{\gamma}:\gamma<\mu\right\}
is a local base of
\emptyset
in
G(\mathcal{F})
.
For each
\gamma<\mu
take
U_{\xi_{\gamma}}(\alpha_{\gamma})\subseteq W_{\gamma}
.
This is possible as
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F})
is a local base of
\emptyset
in
G(\mathcal{F})
.
Now take ordinals
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta
so that
\beta
is in
C
and that

\sup\left\{\alpha_{\gamma}:\gamma<\mu\right\}<\beta^{\prime}<\beta<\kappa^{+}.

Note that
\beta^{\prime}\notin F_{\xi_{\gamma}}(\alpha_{\gamma})
for any
\gamma<\mu
.
This implies that
\left\{\beta^{\prime}\right\}\in U_{\xi_{\gamma}}(\alpha_{\gamma})
for
\gamma<\mu
.
Since
\beta=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}F_{\xi}(\beta)
by (G1), there is some
\xi_{0}
such that
\beta^{\prime}\in F_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
.
Consider
U_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
, clearly
\left\{\beta^{\prime}\right\}\notin U_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
.
Let
\delta<\mu
be such that
W_{\delta}\subseteq U_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
.
Observe that in that case
U_{\xi_{\delta}}(\alpha_{\delta})\subseteq U_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
.
But we already explained that
\left\{\beta^{\prime}\right\}\in U_{\xi_{\delta}}(\alpha_{\delta})
and
\left\{\beta^{\prime}\right\}\notin U_{\xi_{0}}(\beta)
, contradicting the last observation.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix, then
(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F}),\supseteq)
is isomorphic to
(\mathcal{F},\subseteq)
, so

G(\mathcal{F})\equiv_{T}\mathcal{F}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\lambda\leq\kappa
be regular infinite cardinals and
\mathcal{F}
a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.
Then
1
and
\lambda
are the only Tukey quotients of the group
G(\mathcal{F})
of cardinality less than
\kappa^{+}
.

Proof.

By Remark
3.4
, it is enough to show that if
D\leq_{T}(\mathcal{F},\subseteq)
and
\left|D\right|\leq\kappa
, then
D\equiv_{T}1
or
D\equiv_{T}\lambda
.
So take such
D
and let
f:D\to\mathcal{F}
be a Tukey map.
Then for each
d\in D
there are
\xi_{d}<\lambda
and
\alpha_{d}\in C
such that
f(d)=F_{\xi_{d}}(\alpha_{d})
.
By the cardinality assumption on
D
, there is some
\gamma\in C\setminus\left(\sup\left\{\alpha_{d}:d\in D\right\}+1\right)
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix, by (G3), for each
d\in D
, there is some
\eta_{d}<\lambda
such that
F_{\xi_{d}}(\alpha_{d})\subseteq F_{\eta_{d}}(\gamma)
.
Now for each
\xi<\lambda
let

D_{\xi}=\left\{d\in D:f(d)\subseteq F_{\xi}(\gamma)\right\}.

Then by the choice of
\gamma
and the choice of the set
\left\{\eta_{d}:d\in D\right\}\subseteq\lambda
, we have

\textstyle D=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}D_{\xi}.

Note that
D_{\xi}
is bounded in
D
for all
\xi<\lambda
.
Because if not, since
f
is Tukey,
f[D_{\xi}]
would be unbounded in
\mathcal{F}
which is clearly not true (it is bounded by
F_{\xi}(\gamma)
).
Let
d_{\xi}
bound
D_{\xi}
for each
\xi<\lambda
, i.e.
d\leq d_{\xi}
for each
\xi<\lambda
and
d\in D_{\xi}
.

Define
g:\lambda\to D
by
g(\xi)=d_{\xi}
.
We prove that
g
is a cofinal map.
Take any
d\in D
.
Let
\xi
be such that
d\in D_{\xi}
.
Let
\eta\geq\xi
be arbitrary.
As
F_{\xi}(\gamma)\subseteq F_{\eta}(\gamma)
by (G2), we know that
D_{\xi}\subseteq D_{\eta}
, so
d\leq d_{\eta}
.
So for any
\eta\geq\xi
, we have
g(\eta)\geq d
, which means that
g
is a cofinal map.
Hence
D\leq_{T}\lambda
, so either
D\equiv_{T}1
or
D\equiv_{T}\lambda
.
∎

We say that a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix is a
strong
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix
if it satisfies additionally:

(G4)

for all
\alpha\leq\beta
in
C
and each
\eta<\lambda
there is
\xi<\lambda
so that
F_{\eta}(\beta)\cap\alpha\subseteq F_{\xi}(\alpha)
.

We can show that groups constructed from strong matrices have nicer properties.
For a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix
\mathcal{F}
and
\delta\in C
, considering the introduced notation, we denote:

\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{[\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U_{\eta}(\delta):\eta<\lambda\right\}.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose
\lambda\leq\kappa
are infinite regular cardinals and
\mathcal{F}
is a strong
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.
Then for
\delta\in C
, the family
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(\mathcal{F})
is a local base of
\emptyset
in the group
([\delta]^{<\omega},\triangle)
with the topology induced from
G(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta\in C
.
To prove that the family
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}
is a local base of the identity in the given group it is enough to show that for each
\xi<\lambda
and
\alpha\in C
there is some
\eta<\lambda
such that

[\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U_{\eta}(\delta)\subseteq[\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U_{\xi}(\alpha).

To show this, it is enough to prove that
F_{\xi}(\alpha)\cap\delta\subseteq F_{\eta}(\delta)\cap\delta
for a suitably chosen
\eta
.
Let us consider two cases.
First, that
\alpha\leq\delta
.
In this case, by (G3), there is
\eta<\lambda
such that
F_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq F_{\eta}(\delta)
, which clearly implies the required inclusion.
Second, that
\delta<\alpha
.
In this case, by (G4), there is
\eta<\lambda
such that
F_{\xi}(\alpha)\cap\delta\subseteq F_{\eta}(\delta)=F_{\eta}(\delta)\cap\delta
, as required.
∎

.

If
\mathcal{F}

Proof.

By Theorem

and the Birkhoff-Kakutani theorem.

.

.

.

.

4.
Tightness and unboundedness

For infinite regular cardinals
\kappa
and
\lambda
, we say that a function
f:[\kappa]^{2}\to\lambda
is
\kappa
-unbounded
if for all
\xi<\lambda
and every family
\mathcal{A}
of size
\kappa
consisting of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\kappa
, there are different
a
and
b
in
\mathcal{A}
such that
f(\alpha,\beta)>\xi
for all
\alpha\in a
and
\beta\in b
with
\alpha<\beta
.

We proceed to explain how to every
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix indexed by
\kappa^{+}
we can adjoin a function in a way that unboundednes of the function relates to the tightness of the topological group corresponding to the matrix.

Suppose that
\lambda\leq\kappa
are infinite regular cardinals and that we are given a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{\xi}(\alpha):\xi<\lambda,\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
.
Define the function
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}:[\kappa^{+}]^{2}\to\lambda
as follows:

\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta)=\min\left\{\xi<\lambda:\alpha\in F_{\xi}(\beta)\right\}

for all
\alpha<\beta<\kappa^{+}
.
The only reason to define
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
only for matrices indexed by
\kappa^{+}
is that for our applications
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
should be defined for all
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\kappa^{+}
, and there is no meaningful way to do it if
F_{\xi}(\beta)
does not exist for all
\beta<\kappa^{+}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that with the notation as in the previous paragraph, for
\eta<\lambda
and
\beta<\kappa^{+}
, we have
F_{\eta}(\beta)=\left\{\alpha<\beta:\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\eta\right\}
.
We prove this.
For the inclusion
\subseteq
, take some
\alpha\in F_{\eta}(\beta)
.
Suppose the contrary, that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta)>\eta
.
Then
\min(A)>\eta
where
A=\left\{\xi<\lambda:\alpha\in F_{\xi}(\beta)\right\}
.
This is clearly impossible as
\eta\in A
.
For the other inclusion
\supseteq
, take some
\alpha<\beta
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\eta
.
Then
\alpha\in F_{\xi}(\beta)
for some
\xi\leq\eta
, but by (G2), then we have
\alpha\in F_{\xi}(\beta)\subseteq F_{\eta}(\beta)
, as required.

We will say that a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix
\mathcal{F}
is
unbounded
if
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\kappa^{+}
-unbounded function.
In order to simplify the statements of the next theorem and its corollaries, we introduce another definition.
We say that a topological group
G
is
\lambda
-Frechét, for a regular infinite cardinal
\lambda
, if for any set
A\subseteq G
with
e\in\overline{A}
there is a
\lambda
-sequence
\left<x_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda\right>
in
A
such that for every open set
U
containing
e
, there is some
\eta<\lambda
so that
x_{\xi}\in U
whenever
\eta\leq\xi<\lambda
.
Clearly,
\omega
-Frechét is the same as Frechét.

Theorem 4.2
.

If
\lambda\leq\kappa
are regular infinite cardinals, and
\mathcal{F}
is a strong
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix indexed by
\kappa^{+}
, then

G(\mathcal{F})
is
\lambda
-Frechét if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is unbounded.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
, we know that
G(\mathcal{F})
is a topological group.
We first assume that the matrix
\mathcal{F}
is unbounded.
To show that
G(\mathcal{F})
is
\lambda
-Frechét, take
A\subseteq G(\mathcal{F})
such that
\emptyset
is in the closure of
A
.
Let
\theta
be a regular cardinal above
\left(2^{\omega_{1}}\right)^{+}
.
Take
M
, an elementary submodel of
(H(\theta),\in)
of size
\kappa
, containing
\mathcal{F}
and
A
as elements.
Denote
\delta=M\cap\kappa^{+}
.
Note that
\delta
is an ordinal in
\kappa^{+}
and that
\lambda\subseteq\delta\subseteq M
.
To prove this direction of the theorem, it is enough to show that
\emptyset
is in the closure of
M\cap A
.
To see this, note that by Theorem
3.6
, we know that for every basic open neighborhood of
\emptyset
in
G(\mathcal{F})
, there is some
\xi<\lambda
such that the induced open set on
[\delta]^{<\omega}
is exactly
[\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U_{\xi}(\delta)
.
If
\emptyset
were in the closure of
A\cap M
, we would be able to pick
a_{\xi}\in U_{\xi}(\delta)\cap A\cap M
for each
\xi<\lambda
.
Then the
\lambda
-sequence
\left<a_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda\right>
would be in
A
.
Moreover, for any open
U_{\eta}(\delta)
and each
\xi>\eta
we would have
a_{\xi}\in U_{\xi}(\delta)\subseteq U_{\eta}(\delta)
(this holds as
F_{\eta}(\delta)\subseteq F_{\xi}(\delta)
by the definition of matrix).
Thus it would prove that
G(\mathcal{F})
is
\lambda
-Frechét.

So we continue to prove
\emptyset\in\overline{A\cap M}
.
If
\left|A\right|<\kappa^{+}
, then
A\subseteq M
and by the above observation the theorem would be proved, so assume that
A
is of cardinality
\kappa^{+}
.
We now work towards proving
\emptyset\in\overline{A\cap M}
.
So take
\xi<\lambda
.
Note that
\xi\in M
.
We will prove that
U_{\xi}(\delta)\cap A\cap M\neq\emptyset
, i.e. that there is some
a\in A\cap M
such that
a\cap F_{\xi}(\delta)=\emptyset
.
Since
\emptyset\in\overline{A}
, there is some
b\in A
such that
b\cap F_{\xi}(\delta)=\emptyset
.
We can assume the
b\notin M
(otherwise the proof is finished).
Denote
c=b\setminus M
.

Consider the formula

\psi(\alpha)\equiv\left(\exists w\in[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}\right)\ \alpha<w\ \wedge\ w\cup(b\cap\delta)\in A.

To see that
M\models\big((\forall\alpha<\kappa^{+})\psi(\alpha)\big)^{M}
, take arbitrary
\alpha\in M\cap\kappa^{+}=\delta
for a moment.
Then
H(\theta)\models\psi(\alpha)
, namely
b\setminus\delta
is a witness for this.
Since
A,[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega},b\cap\delta
, and
\alpha
are all elements of
M
, by elementarity,
\psi(\alpha)
holds in
M
.
Since
\alpha
was arbitrary in
M\cap\kappa^{+}
, we proved
M\models\big((\forall\alpha<\kappa^{+})\psi(\alpha)\big)^{M}
.
Again, by elementarity,
H(\theta)\models(\forall\alpha<\kappa^{+})\psi(\alpha)
.
This means that, in
H(\theta)
, for each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
there is some
w_{\alpha}
such that
\alpha\cap w_{\alpha}=\emptyset
and
w_{\alpha}\cup(b\cap\delta)\in A
.
Moreover, we can choose
w_{\alpha}
to be minimal with respect to the anti-lexicographical ordering of
[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
.
As this ordering is an element of
M
, it means that the function
f:\kappa^{+}\to[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
given by
f(\alpha)=w_{\alpha}
is definable in
M
by parameters in
M
, so
f\in M
.
Hence, the family
\mathcal{A}=\left\{w_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
is an element of
M
.

To finish the proof, since
b\cap F_{\xi}(\delta)=\emptyset
, we need to show that there is some
\alpha<\delta
such that
w_{\alpha}\cap F_{\xi}(\delta)=\emptyset
.
Equivalently, we need
\alpha<\delta
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,\delta)>\xi
for each
x\in w_{\alpha}
.
Suppose this is not the case, i.e.

(\forall\alpha<\delta)(\exists x\in w_{\alpha})\ \rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,\delta)\leq\xi.

Consider the family
\mathcal{C}=\left\{\beta<\kappa^{+}:(\forall\alpha<\beta)(\exists x\in w_{\alpha})\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,\beta)\leq\xi\right\}
.
Since
\mathcal{F}\in M
, we know that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}\in M
.
So, as
\xi\in M
as well, we have
\mathcal{C}\in M
.
From
\delta\in\mathcal{C}\setminus M
(by the assumption), we know that
\mathcal{C}
is cofinal in
\kappa^{+}
. Hence, for each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
there is some minimal
\gamma_{\alpha}\geq\alpha
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,\gamma_{\alpha})\leq\xi
for some
x\in w_{\alpha}
.
Now consider the family
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\left\{w_{\alpha}\cup\left\{\gamma_{\alpha}\right\}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
.
This is an uncountable family of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\kappa^{+}
.
Since
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\kappa^{+}
-unbounded (the assumption of the theorem), there are
\alpha<\beta<\kappa^{+}
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,y)>\xi
for each
x\in w_{\alpha}\cup\left\{\gamma_{\alpha}\right\}
and
y\in w_{\beta}\cup\left\{\gamma_{\beta}\right\}
.
But, this in particular means that there is no
x\in w_{\alpha}
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,\gamma_{\beta})\leq\xi
, contradicting the choice of
\gamma_{\beta}
.
Hence, there is some
\alpha_{0}<\delta
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(x,\delta)>\xi
for each
x\in w_{\alpha_{0}}
.
Then, since
\alpha_{0}<\delta
, we know that
w_{\alpha_{0}}\in M\cap\mathcal{A}
and
w_{\alpha_{0}}\cap F_{\xi}(\delta)=\emptyset
.
Consequently
(b\cap\delta)\cup w_{\alpha_{0}}\in A\cap M
and
\left((b\cap\delta)\cup w_{\alpha_{0}}\right)\cap F_{\xi}(\delta)=\emptyset
, as required.
So, the proof of one direction of the theorem is completed.

For the other direction, let us assume that
G(\mathcal{F})
is
\lambda
-Frechét.
Suppose that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
is not
\kappa^{+}
-unbounded.
This means that there are
\xi_{0}<\lambda
and a family
\mathcal{A}
of size
\kappa^{+}
consisting of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\kappa^{+}
such that for all different
a
and
b
in
\mathcal{A}
there are
\alpha\in a
and
\beta\in b
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\xi_{0}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}\subseteq G(\mathcal{F})
.
Let us show that
\emptyset\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}
.
Take any basic open
U_{\eta}(\beta)
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is a family of pairwise disjoint finite sets of size
\kappa^{+}
, there is some
a\in\mathcal{A}
with
\beta<a
.
Then
a\cap F_{\eta}(\beta)=\emptyset
, i.e.
a\in\mathcal{A}\cap U_{\eta}(\beta)
.
So, since
G(\mathcal{F})
is
\lambda
-Frechét, there is a
\lambda
-sequence
\left<a_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda\right>
in
\mathcal{A}
such that:

(4.1)

\mbox{for any open }U_{\zeta}(\gamma)\mbox{ there is }\eta<\lambda\mbox{ so that }a_{\xi}\in U_{\zeta}(\gamma)\mbox{ whenever }\eta\leq\xi<\lambda.

Take
a\in\mathcal{A}
such that
a_{\xi}<a
for each
\xi<\lambda
.
This is possible as
\mathcal{A}
is of size
\kappa^{+}
and
\lambda\leq\kappa
.
Enumerate
a=\left\{\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{l-1}\right\}
for some
l<\omega
.
By the assumption on
\mathcal{A}
, for each
\xi<\lambda
there is some
i<l
and
\alpha\in a_{\xi}
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta_{i})\leq\xi_{0}
.
Hence, there is
j<l
and a set
J
cofinal in
\lambda
, so that for
\xi\in J
there is
\alpha\in a_{\xi}
such that
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,\beta_{j})\leq\xi_{0}
.
But this implies that
a_{\xi}\notin U_{\xi_{0}}(\beta_{j})
for each
\xi\in J
, contradicting the assumption that
\left<a_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda\right>
satisfies (
4.1
) for
\zeta=\xi_{0}
and
\gamma=\beta_{j}
.
∎

We have two immediate corollaries.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
\kappa
is a regular infinite cardinal and
\mathcal{F}
is a strong
(\kappa,\omega)
-matrix indexed by
\kappa^{+}
, then
G(\mathcal{F})
is Frechét if and only if it is unbounded.

Corollary 4.4
.

If
\lambda\leq\kappa
are regular infinite cardinals and
\mathcal{F}
is an unbounded strong
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix indexed by
\kappa^{+}
, then
G(\mathcal{F})
has tightness at most
\lambda
.

Note that more properties of the group
G(\mathcal{F})
can be read from the function
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Suppose that we are given infinite regular cardinals
\theta
and
\lambda
, and a function
f:[\theta]^{2}\to\lambda
. We say that the function
f
satisfies condition (H) if:

(H)

the set
\left\{\alpha<\beta:f(\alpha,\beta)\leq\xi\right\}
is finite for all
\beta<\theta
and
\xi<\lambda
.

Then, by Theorem
3.9
and Remark
4.1
, we have:

Corollary 4.5
.

If
\lambda\leq\kappa
are infinite regular cardinals,
\mathcal{F}
is a strong
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix indexed by
\kappa^{+}
, and
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the condition (H), then
G(\mathcal{F})\equiv_{T}[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
.

5.
Transitive and subadditive functions

Let
\theta,\lambda
be regular infinite cardinals.
We say that a function
f:[\theta]^{2}\to\lambda
is
subadditive
if for all
\alpha<\beta<\gamma<\theta
:

(S1)

f(\alpha,\gamma)\leq\max\left\{f(\alpha,\beta),f(\beta,\gamma)\right\}
,

(S2)

f(\alpha,\beta)\leq\max\left\{f(\alpha,\gamma),f(\beta,\gamma)\right\}
.

If
f
satisfies only condition (S1) we say it is
transitive
.

We will now explain how to obtain a topological group from any transitive function.
So let
\lambda\leq\kappa
be regular infinite cardinals, and suppose we are given a transitive function
r:[\kappa^{+}]^{2}\to\lambda
.
For any
\xi<\lambda
and
\beta<\kappa^{+}
let us define
F^{r}_{\xi}(\beta)=\left\{\alpha<\beta:f(\alpha,\beta)\leq\xi\right\}
and denote
\mathcal{F}_{r}=\left\{F^{r}_{\xi}(\beta):\xi<\lambda,\beta<\kappa^{+}\right\}
.
Note that in this case
r=\rho_{\mathcal{F}_{r}}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The family
\mathcal{F}_{r}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.

Proof.

We have to prove that
\mathcal{F}_{r}
satisfies conditions (G1)-(G4).

For (G1), take
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
.
First observe that
F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq\alpha
for each
\xi<\lambda
.
For the other inclusion, take any
\gamma<\alpha
.
Let
\eta=r(\gamma,\alpha)
.
Then
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\eta}(\alpha)
, so we proved
\alpha=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.

For the proof of (G2), take any
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
and any
\xi\leq\eta<\lambda
.
If
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)
, then
r(\gamma,\alpha)\leq\xi\leq\eta
.
Thus,
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\eta}(\alpha)
.

Next, we show (G3).
Fix
\xi<\lambda
and
\alpha\leq\beta<\kappa^{+}
.
We need to find
\eta<\lambda
such that
F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq F^{r}_{\eta}(\beta)
. Let
\eta=\max\{\xi,r(\alpha,\beta)\}
.
We will show this
\eta
works.
Let
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
Then
r(\gamma,\alpha)\leq\xi
.
Now, by (S1),

r(\gamma,\beta)\leq\max\{r(\gamma,\alpha),r(\alpha,\beta)\}\leq\max\{\xi,r(\alpha,\beta)\}=\eta,

and therefore
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\eta}(\alpha)
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

If
r
is moreover subadditive, then
\mathcal{F}_{r}
is a strong
(\kappa,\lambda)
-matrix.

Proof.

By the previous theorem we still have to show (G4).
To see that (G4) is true for
\mathcal{F}_{r}
, fix
\eta<\lambda
and
\alpha\leq\beta<\kappa^{+}
.
We need to find
\xi<\lambda
such that
F^{r}_{\eta}(\beta)\cap\alpha\subseteq F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
Let
\xi=\max\{\eta,r(\alpha,\beta)\}
.
We will show that this
\xi
works.
Let
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\eta}(\beta)\cap\alpha
.
Then
r(\gamma,\beta)\leq\eta
and
\gamma<\alpha
. Now, by (S2),

r(\gamma,\alpha)\leq\max\{r(\gamma,\beta),r(\alpha,\beta)\}\leq\max\{\eta,r(\alpha,\beta)\}=\xi.

Thus
\gamma\in F^{r}_{\xi}(\alpha)
, as required.
∎

By Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
3.5
, and Theorem
3.6
, we have the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

The group
([\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega},\triangle)
with the topology generated by
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F}_{r})
becomes a topological group of character
\kappa^{+}
whose only Tukey quotients of size less than
\kappa^{+}
are
1
and
\lambda
.
Moreover, if
r
is subadditive, then for
\delta<\kappa^{+}
, the topology of the subgroup
[\delta]^{<\omega}
is generated by the family
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(\mathcal{F}_{r})
.

We denote this group by
G_{r}(\kappa^{+})=G(\mathcal{F}_{r})
(the former notation appeared in the earlier literature and the latter one is from this paper).

By Theorem
4.2
we have another corollary:

Corollary 5.4
.

If
r
is subadditive, then it is
\kappa^{+}
-unbounded iff
G_{r}(\kappa^{+})
is
\lambda
-Frechét.

Note that, by Theorem
3.9
, we also have:

Corollary 5.5
.

If
r
satisfies condition (H), then
G_{r}(\kappa^{+})\equiv_{T}[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
.

By Corollary
3.7
and Corollary
2.4
, the following holds:

Corollary 5.6
.

For any subadditive
r:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\omega
, the group
G_{r}(\omega_{1})
is a non-metrizable,
(\alpha_{1})
-group.

There are various subadditive functions.
For the first example, consider the
C
-sequence
\left<C_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right>
, and define the function
\rho:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\omega
by

\rho(\alpha,\beta)=\max\left\{\left|C_{\beta}\cap\alpha\right|,\rho(\alpha,\min(C_{\beta}\setminus\alpha)),\rho(\xi,\alpha):\xi\in C_{\beta}\cap\alpha\right\},

with the boundary condition
\rho(\alpha,\alpha)=0
.
This function has been introduced in
[
Tod07
, Section 3]
.
There it has been proved that
\rho
satisfies (S1) and (S2), and that it even satisfies additional condition (H).
Another function introduced in
[
Tod07
]
is
\rho_{1}:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\rho
defined by
\rho_{1}(\alpha,\beta)=\max\left\{\left|C_{\beta}\cap\alpha\right|,\rho_{1}(\alpha,\min(C_{\beta}\setminus\alpha))\right\}
, with the boundary condition
\rho_{1}(\alpha,\alpha)=0
.
From the definition is clear that

(5.1)

\rho(\alpha,\beta)\geq\rho_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\mbox{ for }\alpha\leq\beta<\omega_{1}.

Hence we know that
G_{\rho}(\omega_{1})
has the following properties:

(1)

G_{\rho}(\omega_{1})
is an
(\alpha_{1})
-group by Corollary
5.3
,

(2)

G_{\rho}(\omega_{1})
is Frechét by Lemma 2.2.6 in
[
Tod07
]
and Theorem
4.2
, using (
5.1
),

(3)

G_{\rho}(\omega_{1})\equiv_{T}[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
by Corollary
4.5
.

Recall that the function
\bar{\rho}:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\omega
introduced also in
[
Tod07
, Section 3]
, has the same properties, so the same holds for
G_{\bar{\rho}}(\omega_{1})
.
For the completeness of the paper, we give its definition:
\bar{\rho}(\alpha,\beta)=2^{\rho(\alpha,\beta)}(2\left|\left\{\xi\leq\alpha:\rho(\xi,\alpha)\leq\rho(\alpha,\beta)\right\}\right|+1)
.
The key feature of
\bar{\rho}
, distinguishing it from
\rho
, is that
\bar{\rho}(\alpha,\gamma)\neq\bar{\rho}(\beta,\gamma)
holds for all
\alpha<\beta<\gamma<\omega_{1}
.

Let us provide another example of transitive function, which then gives the corresponding group.
For the remainder of this section fix a regular infinite cardinal
\kappa
.
Let
\mathcal{TO}=\left\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
be a
\kappa^{+}
-tower in
\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
, i.e. a family of sets
a_{\alpha}\subseteq\kappa
(
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
) such that, for
\alpha,\beta<\kappa^{+}
:

\left|a_{\alpha}\setminus a_{\beta}\right|<\kappa
if and only if
\alpha\leq\beta
.

Let us define a function
\rho_{\mathcal{TO}}:[\kappa^{+}]^{2}\to\kappa
by
\rho_{\mathcal{TO}}(\alpha,\beta)=\min\left\{\xi<\kappa:a_{\alpha}\setminus\xi\subseteq a_{\beta}\right\}
.
Since
\mathcal{TO}
is a
\kappa^{+}
-tower, the function
\rho_{\mathcal{TO}}
is well defined.
As it is commented in
[
Tod07
, pp. 64]
, the function
\rho_{\mathcal{TO}}
is transitive, so by Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
3.5
, and Theorem
5.1
, we know that the topological group
G_{\rho_{\mathcal{TO}}}(\kappa^{+})
has character
\kappa^{+}
, and its only Tukey quotients of cardinality less then
\kappa^{+}
are
1
and
\kappa
.
Recall that a tower
\mathcal{TO}=\left\{a_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is
Hausdorff
if for every
n<\omega
and
\beta<\omega_{1}
, the set
F^{\mathcal{TO}}_{n}(\beta)=\left\{\alpha<\beta:a_{\alpha}\setminus a_{\beta}\subseteq n\right\}
is finite.
So, by Theorem
3.9
and Remark
4.1
, if
\mathcal{TO}
is a Hausdorff tower in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
, then
[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\equiv_{T}G(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{T}O})
.

6.
Trees

In this section we will give two constructions of topological groups from trees.
The key difference is that the first construction is for trees of arbitrary height, while the other one is for trees of height
\omega_{1}
.
The first one is more general, however we are able to obtain finer analysis of cofinal types of corresponding groups using the second construction.

Recall that a strict partial order
(T,\prec)
is
a tree
if it has a minimum called the root of
T
, and for each node
t\in T
the set of its predecessors
t_{\downarrow}=\left\{s\in T:s\prec t\right\}
is well-ordered.
For every
t\in T
, we define its height as
\operatorname{ht}(t)=\operatorname{otp}(t_{\downarrow},\prec)
.
For an ordinal
\alpha
, the
\alpha
th level of
T
is
T_{\alpha}=\left\{t\in T:\operatorname{ht}(t)=\alpha\right\}
.
The height of the tree
T
is
\operatorname{ht}(T)=\min\left\{\alpha:T_{\alpha}=\emptyset\right\}
.
For a set of ordinals
\Gamma
and any subset
X
of
T
, we set
X\upharpoonright\Gamma=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}X\cap T_{\gamma}
.
We say that
B\subseteq T
is a
branch
in
T
, if
B
is linearly ordered and
T_{\alpha}\cap B\neq\emptyset
for each
\alpha<\operatorname{ht}(T)
.
If
\kappa
is an infinite cardinal, than
T
is a
\kappa
-tree
if it is of height
\kappa
and each level of
T
has cardinality less then
\kappa
, i.e.
\operatorname{ht}(T)=\kappa
and
\left|T_{\alpha}\right|<\kappa
for all
\alpha<\kappa
.
Every tree
T
we consider in this paper will be
normal
, i.e whenever
s\in T
and
\operatorname{ht}(s)<\alpha<\operatorname{ht}(T)
there is
t\in T_{\alpha}
such that
s\prec t
.
An
\omega_{1}
-tree
T
is
Aronszajn
if it has no brach, i.e. every linearly ordered subset is countable.
The following is a well-known result about Aronszajn trees which will be used.
Implicitly, it was first noticed by Jensen in
[
DJ74
]
, and the proof is a modification of the proof of Lemma 5.9 in
[
Tod84
]
.
We provide the proof for completeness of the paper.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T
be an Aronszajn tree.
Then for each uncountable pairwise disjoint family
X
of finite subsets of
T
, the set

\Gamma=\left\{\gamma<\omega_{1}:(\forall K\in[T_{\gamma}]^{<\omega})\ \left|\left\{x\in X:(\forall t\in x)\ t_{\downarrow}\cap K=\emptyset\right\}\right|=\aleph_{1}\right\}

is uncountable.

Proof.

We can assume that
X\subseteq[T]^{n}
for some
n<\omega
.
Suppose the contrary, that
\Gamma
is countable.
Then for each
\alpha>\sup(\Gamma)
there is a finite set
K_{\alpha}\subseteq T_{\alpha}
so that
X_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in X:(\forall t\in x)(\forall s\in K_{\alpha})\ s\not\prec t\right\}
is countable.
This means that there is an uncountable set
\Sigma\subseteq\omega_{1}\setminus\sup(\Gamma)
and
m<\omega
so that
\left|K_{\alpha}\right|=m
for each
\alpha\in\Sigma
.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a uniform ultrafilter on
\Sigma
, i.e.
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
\Sigma
such that each element of
\mathcal{U}
is uncountable.
Enumerate
X=\left\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Sigma\right\}
and adopt a convention in this proof that for a finite set
L\subseteq\omega_{1}
and
i<\omega
, we denote the
i
th element of
L
in its increasing enumeration by
L(i)
.
For
\delta\in\Sigma
and
(i,j)\in m\times n
, define
A_{\delta}(i,j)=\left\{\alpha\in\Sigma:K_{\delta}(i)\prec x_{\alpha}(j)\right\}
.
Then for each
\delta\in\Sigma
, since
X_{\delta}
is countable and
\mathcal{U}
is uniform, the set
A_{\delta}=\left\{\alpha\in\Sigma:x_{\alpha}\notin X_{\delta}\right\}
is in
\mathcal{U}
.
As
A_{\delta}=\bigcup_{(i,j)\in m\times n}A_{\delta}(i,j)
, we can find some
i_{\delta}
and
j_{\delta}
such that
A_{\delta}(i_{\delta},j_{\delta})\in\mathcal{U}
.
Then for some
(i,j)\in m\times n
we have
i_{\delta}=i
and
j_{\delta}=j
for
\delta
in an uncountable
\Delta\subseteq\Sigma
.
Consider the set
B=\left\{K_{\delta}(i):\delta\in\Delta\right\}
.
Take
\beta<\delta
in
\Delta
and let
\alpha\in A_{\beta}(i,j)\cap A_{\delta}(i,j)\in\mathcal{U}
be above both
\alpha
and
\beta
(this is possible as
\mathcal{U}
is uniform).
Then both
K_{\beta}(i)\prec x_{\alpha}(j)
and
K_{\delta}(i)\prec x_{\alpha}(j)
.
Since
T
is a tree and
\beta<\delta
it must be that that
K_{\beta}(i)\prec K_{\delta}(i)
, i.e.
(B,\prec)
is an uncountable linearly ordered subset of
(T,\prec)
which is in contradiction with the assumption that
T
is Aronszajn.
∎

6.1.
The general construction

In this subsection we assume that
\kappa
is a regular infinite cardinal and that
T
is a
\kappa^{+}
-tree.
Suppose that
T
is
\kappa
-splitting
, i.e. every node in
T
admits at least
\kappa
many immediate successors, which becomes exactly
\kappa
by the assumption that
T
is a
\kappa^{+}
-tree.
So we will assume that
T=(\kappa^{+},\prec)
and that
T_{\alpha}=[\kappa\alpha,\kappa\alpha+\kappa)
for
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
.
Then the set
C=\left\{\alpha<\kappa^{+}:\kappa\alpha=\alpha\right\}
contains a club in
\kappa^{+}
.
In particular, it is stationary in
\kappa^{+}
(which again implies it is cofinal).
Now, for each
\xi<\kappa
and
\alpha\in C
we define

F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)=\left\{\gamma<\alpha:(\exists\eta\leq\xi)\ \gamma\prec\alpha+\eta\right\},

i.e. if
\kappa\alpha=\alpha
and
\xi<\kappa
, then
F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)
consists of all the predecessors of all the nodes on the
\alpha
th level which appear no later than
\alpha+\xi
in the enumeration of that level.
Finally, let us denote
\mathcal{F}_{T}=\left\{F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha):\xi<\kappa,\alpha\in C\right\}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The family
\mathcal{F}_{T}
is a strong
\kappa
-matrix indexed by
C
.

Proof.

As we already mentioned,
C
is cofinal in
\kappa^{+}
, so we have to show that properties (G1)-(G4) hold for
\mathcal{F}_{T}
.

We start with (G1).
First note that by definition
F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq\alpha
for each
\xi<\kappa
and
\alpha\in C
.
To prove the needed equality, take any
\gamma<\alpha=\kappa\alpha
.
Then
\operatorname{ht}(\gamma)<\alpha
, so by normality of
T
, there is some
\eta^{\prime}<\kappa
such that
\gamma\prec\alpha+\eta^{\prime}
.
So
\gamma\in F^{T}_{\eta^{\prime}}(\alpha)
as required.

Now we show (G2).
Take any
\alpha\in C
and
\xi\leq\eta<\kappa
.
Take
\gamma\in F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
This means that
\gamma\prec\alpha+\xi^{\prime}
for some
\xi^{\prime}\leq\xi
.
Since
\xi^{\prime}\leq\xi\leq\eta
, this implies
\gamma\in F^{T}_{\eta}(\alpha)
, as required.

Next, we prove (G3).
Fix
\xi<\kappa
and
\alpha\leq\beta
in
C
.
Recall that this means that, in particular,
\kappa\alpha=\alpha
and
\kappa\beta=\beta
.
For each
\varepsilon\leq\xi
there is
\eta_{\varepsilon}<\kappa
such that
\alpha+\varepsilon\prec\beta+\eta_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
\eta=\sup\{\eta_{\varepsilon}:\varepsilon\leq\xi\}<\kappa
.
Then we have
F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)\subseteq F^{T}_{\eta}(\beta)
.

Finally, we prove (G4).
Fix
\alpha\leq\beta
in
C
and
\eta<\kappa
.
We need an ordinal
\xi<\kappa
so that
F^{T}_{\eta}(\beta)\cap\alpha\subseteq F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
First note that for each
\varepsilon\leq\eta
, since
T
is a tree, there is a unique
\xi_{\varepsilon}<\kappa
such that
\alpha+\xi_{\varepsilon}\prec\beta+\varepsilon
.
Let
\xi=\sup\{\xi_{\varepsilon}:\varepsilon\leq\eta\}<\kappa
.
We will show that
\xi
is as required.
Let
\gamma\in F^{T}_{\eta}(\beta)\cap\alpha
.
This means that
\gamma\prec\beta+\varepsilon_{0}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\eta
.
Denote
\delta=\operatorname{ht}(\gamma)
.
Since
\kappa\alpha=\alpha
, we know that
\delta<\alpha
.
Consider the node
\alpha+\xi_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.
We know that
\alpha+\xi_{\varepsilon_{0}}\prec\beta+\varepsilon_{0}
and
\gamma\prec\beta+\varepsilon_{0}
.
Since
\gamma<\alpha
and
T
is a tree, in particular
((\beta+\varepsilon_{0})_{\downarrow},\prec)
is linearly ordered, it must be that
\gamma\prec\alpha+\xi_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.
As
\xi_{\varepsilon_{0}}\leq\xi
we proved
\gamma\in F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
So
F^{T}_{\eta}(\beta)\cap\alpha\subseteq F^{T}_{\xi}(\alpha)
.
∎

Now, by Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
3.5
, and Theorem
3.6
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.3
.

For any
\kappa^{+}
-tree
T
, the group
([\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega},\triangle)
with the topology generated by
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F}_{T})
becomes a topological group of character
\kappa^{+}
whose only Tukey quotients of size less than
\kappa^{+}
are
1
and
\kappa
.
Moreover, for each
\delta<\kappa^{+}
, the topology of the subgroup
[\delta]^{<\omega}
is generated by the family
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}\left(\mathcal{F}_{T}\right)
.

Note the following corollary which follows from Corollary
3.7
and Corollary
3.8
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
T
be an
\omega_{1}
-tree. Then
G(\mathcal{F}_{T})
is a non-metrizable,
(\alpha_{1})
-group.

The next theorem shows that the notion of an Aronszajn tree can be described using our topological notions.

Theorem 6.5
.

If
T
is an
\omega_{1}
-tree, then the group
G(\mathcal{F}_{T})
is Frechét if and only if the tree
T
is Aronszajn.

Proof.

Before we start the proof, recall that
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F^{T}_{n}(\alpha):\alpha\in C,\ n<\omega\right\}
where
C=\left\{\alpha<\omega_{1}:\omega\alpha=\alpha\right\}
.

To prove the theorem, first assume that
G(\mathcal{F}_{T})
is a Frechét group, and suppose the contrary, that
T
is not an Aronajn tree, i.e. there is a branch
B\subseteq T
.
Let
A=\left\{\left\{\gamma\right\}:\gamma\in B\right\}
.
First we show that
\emptyset\in\overline{A}
.
Fix an open neighborhood
U^{T}_{n}(\alpha)
of
\emptyset
.
For any
\beta>\alpha
in
C
there is
m<\omega
such that
\beta+m\in B
.
Then we have
\beta+m\notin F^{T}_{n}(\alpha)
, implying
\left\{\beta+m\right\}\in U^{T}_{n}(\alpha)\cap A
as required.
Since
G(\mathcal{F}_{T})
is Frechét, there is a sequence
\left<\left\{\gamma_{n}\right\}:n<\omega\right>
of elements in
A
such that
\lim\left\{\gamma_{n}\right\}=\emptyset
.
Since
B
is a branch, and
T
is an
\omega_{1}
-tree, there are
k<\omega
and
\delta
in
C
such that
\gamma_{n}\prec\delta+k
for each
n<\omega
.
Then
\gamma_{n}\in F^{T}_{k}(\delta)
for each
n<\omega
, i.e.
\left\{\gamma_{n}\right\}\notin U^{T}_{k}(\delta)
for all
n<\omega
contradicting the assumption that
\left<\left\{\gamma_{n}\right\}:n<\omega\right>
is converging to
\emptyset
.

Now suppose that
T
is an Aronszajn tree.
We prove that
G(\mathcal{F}_{T})
is then Frechét.
Take any
A\subseteq G(\mathcal{F}_{T})
such that
\emptyset\in\overline{A}
.
Let
\theta>\left(2^{\omega_{1}}\right)^{+}
be a regular cardinal and let
M
be a countable elementary submodel of
H\left(\theta\right)
such that
T,\mathcal{F}_{T},C,A\in M
and
M\cap\omega_{1}\in C
.
This is possible by Lemma
2.1
.
Denote
\delta=M\cap\omega_{1}
.
As in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, it is enough to prove that
\emptyset\in\overline{A\cap M}
.
If
A
is countable, then
A\subseteq M
and we are done.
So assume
A
is uncountable, and also assume that for some
k<\omega
and all
a\in A
we have
\left|a\right|=k
.
By Theorem
3.6
(see also Corollary
6.3
), it is enough to prove that for each
n<\omega
there is some
a\in A\cap M\cap U^{T}_{n}(\delta)
.
So fix
n<\omega
.
Clearly, there is
y\in A\cap U^{T}_{n}(\delta)
.
If
y\in M
, the proof is finished.
So assume that
y\notin M
.
Let
y_{0}=y\cap M
.
Denote
\gamma_{0}=\max\left\{\operatorname{ht}(t):t\in y_{0}\right\}

Then
y_{0}
is an element of
M
as a finite subset of
M
.
In the same way as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
one can show that for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
there is
w_{\alpha}
such that
\alpha\cap w_{\alpha}=\emptyset
and
w_{\alpha}\cup y_{0}\in A
.
Moreover, as in that proof we can take
A_{y_{0}}=\left\{w_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
to be an element of
M
.

Since,
y\setminus y_{0}\in A_{y_{0}}
and
y\setminus y_{0}\notin M
, the family
A_{y_{0}}
is uncountable.
By the choice of
w_{\alpha}
for
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, the family
A_{y_{0}}
is an uncountable family of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of
\omega_{1}
.
Consider the set
\Gamma
of ordinals
\gamma
such that
\left|\left\{a\in A_{y_{0}}:(\forall t\in a)\ t_{\downarrow}\cap E=\emptyset\right\}\right|=\aleph_{1}
for each finite
E\subseteq T_{\gamma}
.
By Lemma
6.1
, this set is cofinal in
\omega_{1}
.
Since
\Gamma
is defined only using parameters in
M
, by elementarity we have
\Gamma\in M
.
Thus, by elementarity,
\Gamma\cap M
is cofinal in
\delta
.
Take
\gamma^{\prime}\in(\Gamma\cap M)\setminus(\gamma_{0}+1)
.
Let
E=F^{T}_{n}(\delta)\cap T_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.
Then
E
is a finite subset of
T_{\gamma^{\prime}}\subseteq M
, so
E\in M
.
Since
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
, the family

A^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{y_{0}}=\left\{a\in A_{y_{0}}:\gamma^{\prime}<a\ \wedge\ (\forall t\in a)\ t_{\downarrow}\cap E=\emptyset\right\}

is uncountable.
Since
A^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{y_{0}}
is defined only using parameters in
M
, it is also an element of
M
.
Since
A^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{y_{0}}
is uncountable, it is in particular non-empty, so by elementarity of
M
, there is
z\in A^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{y_{0}}\cap M
.
Then
z\cap F^{T}_{n}(\delta)=\emptyset
.
To see this suppose that
t\in F^{T}_{n}(\delta)
for some
t\in z
.
In this case
t\prec\delta+m
for some
m\leq n
.
This means that
t_{\downarrow}\subseteq F^{T}_{n}(\delta)
.
Since
\gamma^{\prime}<z
and
((\delta+m)_{\downarrow},\prec)
is well-ordered, then it must be that
t_{\downarrow}\cap E\neq\emptyset
contradicting the choice of
z
.
Hence,
y_{0}\cup z\in M\cap U_{n}(\delta)
, and the group is Frechét.
∎

6.2.
The construction from a specific class of trees

In this subsection we construct groups from a specific class of trees.
First, for a function
f\in\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
we define its norm by

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup\left\{f(\gamma):\gamma<\operatorname{dom}(f)\right\}.

For two functions
f,g\in\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
we define their difference
f-g\in\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
as follows:
\operatorname{dom}(f-g)=\operatorname{dom}(f)\cap\operatorname{dom}(g)
and
(f-g)(\gamma)=\left|f(\gamma)-g(\gamma)\right|
.
Finally, we say that an
\omega_{1}
-tree
T\subseteq\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
is
\ell_{\infty}
-tree
if
\left\lVert s-t\right\rVert_{\infty}<\omega
for all
s
and
t
in
T
.
Recall that a tree
T\subseteq\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
is
coherent
if the set
\left\{\gamma\in\operatorname{dom}(s-t):s(\gamma)\neq t(\gamma)\right\}
is finite for all
s,t\in T
.
Then every coherent
\omega_{1}
-tree
T\subseteq\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
is an
\ell_{\infty}
-tree.
For another example, consider
T(\rho_{2})=\left\{\rho_{2}(\cdot,\beta)\upharpoonright\alpha:\alpha\leq\beta<\omega_{1}\right\}
.
Here, for a
C
-sequence
\left<C_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right>
, we define a function
\rho_{2}:[\omega_{1}]^{2}\to\omega
by
\rho_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\rho_{2}\left(\alpha,\min\left(C_{\beta}\setminus\alpha)\right)\right)+1
and boundary condition
\rho_{2}(\alpha,\alpha)=0
.
Then
T(\rho_{2})
is a subtree of
\omega^{<\omega_{1}}
, and it is an
\ell_{\infty}
-tree by
[
Tod07
, Lemma 2.4.2]
.

For the remainder of this subsection, we fix an
\omega
-splitting
\ell_{\infty}
-tree
T
.
For
t\in T
and
n<\omega
we define

F^{\infty}_{n}(t)=\left\{s\in T\upharpoonright\operatorname{ht}(t):\left\lVert s-t\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq n\right\}.

Let us denote
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T}=\left\{F^{\infty}_{n}(t):n<\omega,t\in T\right\}
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T}
is not an
\omega
-matrix according to our definition, as our matrices are indexed by sets of ordinals.
However, it can be regarded as a strong
(T,\omega)
-matrix in a sense that it satisfies:

(1)

\bigcup_{n<\omega}F^{\infty}_{n}(t)=T\upharpoonright\operatorname{ht}(t)
for each
t\in T
,

(2)

F^{\infty}_{m}(t)\subseteq F^{\infty}_{n}(t)
for each
t\in T
and
m\leq n<\omega
,

(3)

for all
\alpha\leq\beta<\omega_{1}
,
s\in T_{\alpha}
,
t\in T_{\beta}
, and
m<\omega
, there is
n<\omega
so that
F^{\infty}_{m}(s)\subseteq F^{\infty}_{n}(t)
,

(4)

for all
\alpha\leq\beta<\omega_{1}
,
s\in T_{\beta}
, and
m<\omega
, there are
t\in T_{\alpha}
and
n<\omega
such that
F^{\infty}_{m}(s)\upharpoonright\delta\subseteq F^{\infty}_{n}(t)
.

Theorem
6.6
shows it is essentially a matrix (i.e. the proof of (1-3) is contained in the proof of that lemma), while Theorem
6.8
explains why one can think of it as a strong matrix (i.e. its proof contains the proof of (4)).
As before, for
n<\omega
and
t\in T
define
U^{\infty}_{n}(t)=\left\{a\in[T]^{<\omega}:a\cap F^{\infty}_{n}(t)=\emptyset\right\}
and denote

\mathcal{U}^{\infty}_{T}=\left\{U^{\infty}_{n}(t):n<\omega,t\in T\right\}.

Theorem 6.6
.

The group
([T]^{<\omega},\triangle)
when equipped with
\mathcal{U}^{\infty}_{T}
as the local base of the identity, becomes a topological group.

Proof.

We check properties (1) to (6) of Theorem
2.3
.
Conditions (1), (2), (3), and (4) are true in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let us prove (5).
Take
U^{\infty}_{n}(t)
and
U^{\infty}_{m}(s)
in
\mathcal{U}^{\infty}_{T}
.
Let
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\omega_{1}
be such that
s\in\omega^{\alpha}
and
t\in\omega^{\beta}
.
Let
k=\left\lVert t-s\right\rVert_{\infty}+\max\left\{m,n\right\}
and suppose without any loss of generality that
\alpha\leq\beta
.
It is clear that
F^{\infty}_{n}(t)\subseteq F^{\infty}_{k}(t)
.
So it is enough to show that
F^{\infty}_{m}(s)\subseteq F^{\infty}_{k}(t)
.
Take any
u\in F^{\infty}_{m}(s)
.
This means that
\left\lVert s-u\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq m
, i.e.
(\forall g\in\operatorname{dom}(s-u))\ \left|s(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|\leq m
.
From
\alpha\leq\beta
we know
\operatorname{dom}(s-u)=\operatorname{dom}(t-s)
.
Take any
\gamma\in\operatorname{dom}(s-u)
.
Then

\left|t(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|\leq\left|t(\gamma)-s(\gamma)\right|+\left|s(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|\leq\left\lVert t-s\right\rVert_{\infty}+m\leq k.

This implies that
\gamma\in F^{\infty}_{k}(t)
, as required.

We still have to show (6).
Suppose on the contrary, that
a\in\bigcap\mathcal{U}^{\infty}_{T}
for some
a\in[T]^{<\omega}\setminus\left\{\emptyset\right\}
.
Since
a\neq\emptyset
, there is some
s\in a
.
By normality of
T
, there is
t\in T
such that
s\subsetneq t
, i.e.
s\in t_{\downarrow}
.
Then
\left\lVert t-s\right\rVert_{\infty}=0
, so
s\in a\cap F^{\infty}_{0}(t)
.
This means that
a\notin U^{\infty}_{0}(t)
, contradicting the assumption.
So (6) is true as well.
∎

We will denote the topological group from the previous theorem by
G(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T})
.

Theorem 6.7
.

The group
G(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T})
has character
\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the proof of Theorem

Suppose on the contrary, that

Theorem 6.8
.

For
\delta<\omega_{1}
, the set
\mathcal{U}^{\infty}_{T}(\delta)=\left\{[T\upharpoonright\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U^{\infty}_{n}(t):n<\omega,t\in T_{\delta}\right\}
is a local base of
\emptyset
in the topological group
([T\upharpoonright\delta]^{<\omega},\triangle)
with the topology induced from
G(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T})
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem follows closely the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
Fix an ordinal
\delta<\omega_{1}
.
It is enough to show that for each
m<\omega
and
s\in T
there are
n<\omega
and

so that
[T\upharpoonright\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U^{\infty}_{n}(t)\subseteq[T\upharpoonright\delta]^{<\omega}\cap U^{\infty}_{m}(s).

To show this, it is enough to prove that
F^{\infty}_{m}(s)\upharpoonright\delta\subseteq F^{\infty}_{n}(t)
for suitably chosen
n
and
t
.
Let us consider two cases.

First, that
\operatorname{ht}(s)\leq\delta
.
Then, by normality of
T
, there is
t\in T_{\delta}
such that
s\subseteq t
.
We will prove that
F^{\infty}_{m}(s)\subseteq F^{\infty}_{m}(t)
.
Take any element
u
in
F^{\infty}_{m}(s)
.
Then we have
\operatorname{dom}(u)=\operatorname{dom}(s-u)=\operatorname{dom}(t-u)
.
Since

(\forall\gamma\in\operatorname{dom}(u))\ \left|t(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|=\left|s(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|\leq m

This implies that

Second case is that

Then take

Again we will prove that

Take

.
Then
\operatorname{dom}(u)=\operatorname{dom}(t-u)=\operatorname{dom}(s-u)
and
(\forall\gamma\in\operatorname{dom}(u))\ \left|t(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|=\left|s(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|\leq m

Hence, again

∎

The group

Theorem 6.11
.

If
T
is a Souslin
\ell_{\infty}
-tree, then

is a Frechét group.

Proof.

We use the same structure of the proof as in Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
6.5
.
So suppose that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq G(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T})
is such that the identity
\emptyset
belongs to the closure of
\mathcal{A}
.
As before, take large enough
\theta
and
M
a countable elementary submodel of
H(\theta)
containing
T
and
\mathcal{A}
as elements.
Let
\delta=M\cap\omega_{1}
.
It is enough to prove that
\emptyset
belongs to the closure of
\overline{M\cap\mathcal{A}}
.
By Theorem
6.8
, it is enough to show that for each
n<\omega
and
t\in T_{\delta}
, there is
a\in M\cap\mathcal{A}
such that
a\cap F^{\infty}_{n}(t)=\emptyset
.
So we fix
n<\omega
and
t\in T_{\delta}
, and work towards finding such an
a

For the purpose of this proof, for a finite set
c\subseteq T
denote

\operatorname{supp}(c)=\left\{\operatorname{ht}(v):v\in c\right\}.

Since
\emptyset\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}
there is
b\in\mathcal{A}
such that

.
Denote
b_{0}=b\cap M
and
b_{1}=b\setminus M
.
Again, in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
6.5
, there is a function
f:\omega_{1}\to[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
in
M
such that, for
\alpha<\omega_{1}

is minimal in the right-lexicographical ordering of

with the property that

and

.
For
\beta<\omega_{1}
consider the following subset of
T
:

D_{\beta}=\left\{s\in T:(\exists\alpha_{s}>\beta)\ \left[\operatorname{ht}(s)>\operatorname{supp}(f(\alpha_{s}))\ \wedge\ (\forall v\in f(\alpha_{s}))\left\lVert v-s\right\rVert_{\infty}>n\right]\right\}.

Clearly
D_{\beta}
is an open subset of
T
, meaning that for each
s\in D_{\beta}
and
u\supseteq s
we know that
u\in D_{\beta}
as well.
Let us prove that
D_{\beta}
is dense in
T
, meaning that for each
u\in T
there is some
s\in D_{\beta}
such that
u\subseteq s
.
So take
u\in T
.
Let
\alpha
be a minimal ordinal greater than both
\operatorname{ht}(u)+1
and
\beta
.
Then we can arrange an increasing enumeration
\left\{\xi_{i}:i<k\right\}
of
\operatorname{supp}(f(\alpha))
.
Since

\alpha<\xi_{0}<\xi_{1}<\cdots<\xi_{k-1}.

We inductively define

for

so that:

(1)

u\subseteq s_{0}

(2)

\operatorname{ht}(s_{i})=\xi_{i}
for
i<k
,

(3)

s_{i}\subseteq s_{i+1}
for
i<k-1
,

(4)

\left\lVert s_{i}-v\right\rVert_{\infty}>n
for each

and

.

Let us show that this is possible.
First we explain it is possible to obtain

.
Since

and

is
\omega
-splitting, there is some

.
By normality of

and since
\operatorname{ht}(u)+1<\alpha<\xi_{0}

s_{0}\in T_{\xi_{0}}
such that
u^{\frown}\left\{\left<\operatorname{ht}(u),m_{0}\right>\right\}\subseteq s_{0}
.
Then (1) and (2) are clear, and (3) is vacuous for
s_{0}
.
The condition (4) holds for
s_{0}
because from the choice of
m_{0}
we know that
\left|s_{0}(\operatorname{ht}(u))-v(\operatorname{ht}(u))\right|>n
for all
v\in f(\alpha)\cap T_{\xi_{0}}
.
Suppose now, for
i<k-1
, that
s_{0},\dots,s_{i}
have been constructed so that they satisfy (1-4).
We need to define
s_{i+1}
.
Again, since
\xi_{i}<\xi_{i+1}
and
T
is
\omega
-splitting there is

m_{i}>\max\left\{v(\xi_{i}):v\in f(\alpha)\cap T_{\xi_{i+1}}\right\}+n+1

such that
s_{i}^{\frown}\left\{\left<\xi_{i},m_{i}\right>\right\}\in T
.
From normality of
T
and
\xi_{i}+1\leq\xi_{i+1}
we conclude that there is some
s_{i+1}\in T_{\xi_{i+1}}
such that
s_{i}^{\frown}\left\{\left<\xi_{i},m_{i}\right>\right\}\subseteq s_{i+1}
.
Condition (1) is not relevant for
s_{i+1}
, while conditions (2) and (3) are clearly satisfied.
Same as before, (4) holds for
s_{i+1}
by the choice of
m_{i}
and definition of
\left\lVert\ \right\rVert_{\infty}
.
This concludes the proof that the sequence
\left<s_{0},\dots,s_{k-1}\right>
is well-defined.

Finally, by normality of
T
take
s\in T_{\xi_{k-1}+1}
such that
s_{k-1}\subseteq s
.
By (2), then
s_{i}\subseteq s
for each
i<k
.
We claim that
s
is as required.
Clearly
u\subseteq s
from (1) and (2).
It is also evident that
\alpha>\beta
and
\operatorname{ht}(s)>\operatorname{supp}(f(\alpha))
.
So we still have to show that
\left\lVert v-s\right\rVert_{\infty}>n
for each
v\in f(\alpha)
.
To see this, take
v\in f(\alpha)
.
Then
\operatorname{ht}(v)=\xi_{j}
for some

.
Then, by (4) we have
\left\lVert s_{j}-v\right\rVert_{\infty}>n
.
Since

, this implies that
\left\lVert s-v\right\rVert_{\infty}>n

Hence

is dense in

.
For

denote
\alpha_{s}
the corresponding ordinal

such that
\operatorname{ht}(s)>\operatorname{supp}(f(\alpha_{s}))

.

Since

is dense open, for

, and

such that

So for

.

, and gives

Finally, we can show that this group does not necessarily have maximal possible cofinal type.

Theorem 6.12
.

If
T
is a Souslin
\ell_{\infty}
-tree, then
[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\not\leq_{T}G(\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T})
.

Proof.

First observe that for
s\subseteq t
in
T
, and
n<\omega
, we have
F^{\infty}_{n}(s)\subseteq F^{\infty}_{n}(t)
.
To see this, take
u\in F^{\infty}_{n}(s)
.
Then
\operatorname{dom}(u)=\operatorname{dom}(s-u)=\operatorname{dom}(t-u)
, and from
s\subseteq t
we conclude
(\forall\gamma\in\operatorname{dom}(u))\ \left|t(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|=\left|s(\gamma)-u(\gamma)\right|\leq m
, as required.

Now we prove the theorem.
Suppose the contrary, that
f:[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\to\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T}
is a Tukey function.
This is fine as, by Remark
3.4
,
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T}\equiv_{T}\mathcal{U}^{\infty}_{T}
.
By the assumption that
f
is Tukey, we know that
f\left[[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\right]
is uncountable, otherwise there would be an uncountable set, hence unbounded, mapping to a point, hence unbounded set with bounded image which is impossible.
If there were a bounded infinite subset of
f\left[[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\right]
, its inverse image would be infinite, hence unbounded in
[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
, and this would give a contradiction.
Hence, to finish the proof of the theorem, it is enough to show that every uncountable subset of
\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T}
is bounded with respect to
\subseteq
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\infty}_{T}
be uncountable.
Enumerate
T=\left\{t_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
in a way that if
t_{\alpha}\subseteq t_{\beta}
, then
\alpha\leq\beta
.
By the cardinality argument, there is
n<\omega
and an uncountable set
X\subseteq\omega_{1}
such that
\left\{F^{\infty}_{n}(t_{\alpha}):\alpha\in X\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
Note that
X
has order type
\omega_{1}
and let us now define a coloring
[X]^{2}=K_{0}\cup K_{1}
as follows: for
\alpha<\beta
in
X
we set
\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}\in K_{0}
iff
t_{\alpha}\not\subseteq t_{\beta}
.
Since
\omega_{1}\rightarrow(\omega_{1},\omega+1)^{2}
by Theorem 11.3 in
[
EHMR84
, pp. 72]
and
\operatorname{otp}(X)=\omega_{1}
, one of the following two options holds:

(1)

there is
\Gamma\subseteq X
such that
\operatorname{otp}(\Gamma)=\omega_{1}
and
\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}\in K_{0}
for
\alpha<\beta
in
\Gamma
,

(2)

there is
\Delta\subseteq X
so that
\operatorname{otp}(\Delta)=\omega+1
and
\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}\in K_{1}
for
\alpha<\beta
in
\Delta
.

Case (1) does not happen, as it would imply that
t_{\alpha}\not\subseteq t_{\beta}
and
t_{\beta}\not\subseteq t_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\beta
in an uncountable
\Gamma
.
This would then mean that there is an uncountable antichain in
T
, which is not possible by the assumption that
T
is a Souslin tree.
So case (2) is true.
This means that we have a set
\left\{t_{\alpha_{i}}:i\leq\omega\right\}\subseteq T
such that
\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{j}
and
\left\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\}\in K_{1}
for
i<j\leq\omega
, i.e.
t_{\alpha_{i}}\subseteq t_{a_{j}}
or
t_{\alpha_{j}}\subseteq t_{\alpha_{i}}
for
i<j\leq\omega
.
By the assumption on the enumeration,
t_{\alpha_{j}}\subseteq t_{\alpha_{i}}
for
i<j
is not possible as then
\alpha_{j}<\alpha_{i}
.
So we have that
t_{\alpha_{i}}\subseteq t_{\alpha_{j}}
for
i<j\leq\omega
.
In particular
t_{\alpha_{i}}\subseteq t_{\alpha_{\omega}}
for each
i<\omega
.
By the observation at the beginning of the proof this implies that
F^{\infty}_{n}(t_{\alpha_{i}})\subseteq F^{\infty}_{n}(t_{\alpha_{\omega}})
for
i<\omega
.
Hence there is an infinite bounded set
\left\{F^{\infty}_{n}(t_{i}):i<\omega\right\}
in
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

7.
Gaps

In this section, let
\kappa
be a regular infinite cardinal.
Let us also fix a
(\kappa^{+},\kappa^{+})
pre-gap
\mathcal{G}=\left\{(a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha}):\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
in
\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
, i.e. a family of sets
a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha}\subseteq\kappa
such that:

(1)

\left|a_{\alpha}\setminus a_{\beta}\right|<\kappa
and
\left|b_{\alpha}\setminus b_{\beta}\right|<\kappa
for each
\alpha<\beta<\kappa^{+}
,

(2)

a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\alpha}=\emptyset
for each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
,

Recall that a pre-gap
\mathcal{G}=\left\{(a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha}):\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
is a
gap
if there is no
c\subseteq\kappa
such that
\left|a_{\alpha}\setminus c\right|<\kappa
and
\left|b_{\alpha}\cap c\right|<\kappa
for each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
.

Remark 7.1
.

For any pre-gap
\left\{(a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha}):\alpha<\kappa^{+}\right\}
in
\mathcal{P}(\kappa)
, and any
\alpha\leq\beta<\kappa^{+}
we have
\left|a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\beta}\right|<\kappa
.
To see this take
\alpha\leq\beta
in
\kappa^{+}
.
Since
a_{\beta}\cap b_{\beta}=\emptyset
it must be that
a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\beta}\subseteq a_{\alpha}\setminus a_{\beta}
.
By the definition of a pre-gap, we have
\left|a_{\alpha}\setminus a_{\beta}\right|<\kappa
, which then implies
\left|a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\beta}\right|<\kappa
.

For
\beta<\kappa^{+}
and
\xi<\kappa
, set

F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\{\alpha<\beta:a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\beta}\subseteq\xi\}.

In this case we are not able to show that sets
F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
form a matrix.
However, our methods still give a topological group.
In this case, using these sets we get a subbase.
We proceed to explain this.
Same as before, for
\xi<\kappa
and
\beta<\kappa^{+}
define
U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\left\{a\in[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}:a\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\emptyset\right\}
, and denote

\textstyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}=\left\{\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta):K\in\left[\kappa\times\kappa^{+}\right]^{<\omega}\right\}.

Theorem 7.2
.

The group
([\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega},\triangle)
, when equipped with
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
as the local base of the identity, becomes a topological group.

Proof.

As before, it is sufficent to show that
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies conditions (1)-(6) from Theorem
2.3
.

First we show (1).
Take any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then there is
K\in[\kappa\times\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
such that
U=\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
.
Take any
x,y\in U
.
Then
x\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\emptyset=y\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
for all
(\xi,\beta)\in K
.
This implies that for each
(\xi,\beta)\in K
,
(x\cup y)\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\emptyset
.
Since
x\triangle y\subseteq x\cup y
, we know that
(x\triangle y)\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\emptyset
for each
(\xi,\beta)\in K
.
Hence,
x\triangle y\in\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=U
, i.e.
U^{2}\subseteq U
.

Condition (2) is satisfied because
a=a^{-1}
for
a
in this group, condition (3) follows directly from (1), and condition (4) is true by the commutativity of
\triangle
.

Now we prove (5).
Take
U,V\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then there are
K_{1},K_{2}\in[\kappa\times\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
such that

U=\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K_{1}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
and
V=\bigcap_{(\zeta,\alpha)\in K_{2}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\zeta}(\alpha)
.
Consider the set

\textstyle W=\bigcap_{(\eta,\gamma)\in K_{1}\cup K_{2}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\gamma).

Clearly
W\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
We will show that
W
is a subset of
U\cap V
. Let
x\in W
.
Then
x\in U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\gamma)
for each
(\eta,\gamma)\in K_{1}\cup K_{2}
.
This implies that
x\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\gamma)=\emptyset
for each
(\eta,\alpha)\in K_{1}\cup K_{2}
.
Then
x\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\gamma)=\emptyset
for each
(\eta,\gamma)\in K_{1}
, and
x\cap F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\gamma)=\emptyset
for each
(\eta,\gamma)\in K_{2}
.
Thus,
x\in\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K_{1}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=U
and
x\in\bigcap_{(\zeta,\alpha)\in K_{2}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\zeta}(\alpha)=V
.
Then,
x\in U\cap V
, i.e.
W\subseteq U\cap V
, as required.

Let us prove (6) now. Assume there is some
a\in[\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
such that
a\in\bigcap\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Take
\alpha\in a
.
Then for each
K\in[\kappa\times\kappa^{+}]^{<\omega}
we have
\alpha\notin F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
for all
(\xi,\beta)\in K
.
This implies that
\alpha\notin F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)=\emptyset
for each
\xi<\kappa,\beta<\kappa^{+}
.
On the other hand,
\alpha<\alpha+1
and by Remark
7.1
, there is
\eta<\kappa
such that
a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\alpha+1}\subseteq\eta
, i.e.
\alpha\in F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\alpha+1)
which is clearly contradicting the last observation.
∎

We will denote the topological group from the previous theorem by
G(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}})
.

Remark 7.3
.

Note that the proof of the previous theorem, in particular the proof of item (5) of Theorem
2.3
, gives that the family
\left\{\bigcup_{(n,\beta)\in K}F^{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(\beta):K\in\left[\omega\times\omega_{1}\right]^{<\omega}\right\}
is directed with respect to
\subseteq
.

Lemma 7.4
.

The group
G(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}})
has character
\kappa^{+}
.

Proof.

As before, it is enough to show there is no subset of
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
of size less then
\kappa^{+}
forming a local base of
\emptyset
.
Suppose the contrary, that
\mathcal{K}=\left\{K_{\varepsilon}:\varepsilon<\alpha\right\}
, for
\alpha\leq\kappa
, is a family of finite subsets of
\kappa\times\kappa^{+}
such that
\mathcal{B}=\left\{\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K_{\varepsilon}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta):\varepsilon<\kappa\right\}
is a local base of
\emptyset
.
Consider the set

B=\left\{\beta<\kappa^{+}:(\exists K\in\mathcal{K})(\exists\xi<\kappa)\ (\xi,\beta)\in K\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{K}
is a collection of at most
\kappa
many finite sets, its cardinality is at most
\kappa
.
Hence
\left|B\right|<\kappa^{+}
, and since
\kappa^{+}
is regular, there is some
\gamma\in\kappa^{+}\setminus\sup(B)
.
Then
\gamma\notin F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
for any
\xi<\kappa
and
\beta\in B
, implying that

(7.1)

\textstyle(\forall\varepsilon<\kappa)\ \left\{\gamma\right\}\in\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K_{\varepsilon}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta).

Take any
\delta\in\kappa^{+}\setminus(\gamma+1)
.
Then, by Remark
7.1
there is
\zeta<\kappa
so that
a_{\gamma}\cap b_{\delta}\subseteq\zeta
.
This means that
\left\{\gamma\right\}
does not belong to basic open
U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\zeta}(\delta)
.
Since
\mathcal{B}
is a local base of
\emptyset
there is
K_{0}\in\mathcal{K}
so that
\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K_{0}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)\subseteq U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\zeta}(\delta)
.
Using (
7.1
), then

\textstyle\left\{\gamma\right\}\in\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K_{0}}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)\subseteq U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\zeta}(\delta),

which is clearly in contradiction with
\left\{\gamma\right\}\notin U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\zeta}(\delta)
.
∎

Again, as in the general theory of matrices, we are able to connect topological properties of groups with well-known critical properties of pre-gaps.

Theorem 7.5
.

If the group
G(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}})
is of tightness at most
\kappa
, then
\mathcal{G}
is a gap.

Proof.

Assume the contrary, that
\mathcal{G}
is not a gap.
Then there is a subset
c
of
\kappa
such that
\left|a_{\alpha}\setminus c\right|<\kappa
and
\left|b_{\alpha}\cap c\right|<\kappa
for each
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
.
For
\xi<\kappa
set

A_{\xi}=\left\{\alpha<\kappa^{+}:a_{\alpha}\setminus\xi\subseteq c\;\&\;b_{\alpha}\cap c\subseteq\xi\right\}.

Since
\kappa
is regular and by the choice of
c
, we have
\kappa^{+}=\bigcup_{\xi<\kappa}A_{\xi}
.
Now, by regularity of
\kappa^{+}
, there is some
\eta<\kappa
such that
A_{\eta}
is cofinal in
\kappa^{+}
.
Let us consider the family
\mathcal{A}=\left\{\left\{\alpha\right\}:\alpha\in A_{\eta}\right\}
.

First we will prove that
\emptyset\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}
.
For this, we need to show that for each set
K\in\left[\kappa\times\kappa^{+}\right]^{<\omega}
, the intersection of
\mathcal{A}
and
\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
is nonempty.
Take any
\alpha\in A_{\eta}
such that
\alpha>\max\left\{\beta:(\exists\xi<\kappa)\ (\xi,\beta)\in K\right\}
.
This is possible as
K
is finite and
A_{\eta}
is cofinal in
\kappa^{+}
.
Then
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in\mathcal{A}
.
By the choice of
\alpha
we also know that
\alpha\notin F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
for any
(\xi,\beta)\in K
.
Hence
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
for each
(\xi,\beta)\in K
.
Then
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in\mathcal{A}\cap\bigcap_{(\xi,\beta)\in K}U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\xi}(\beta)
as required.

Since the tightness of
G(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}})
is at most
\kappa
, there is a set
X\subseteq\mathcal{A}
of size at most
\kappa
so that
\emptyset\in\overline{X}
.
By regularity of
\kappa^{+}
and since
A_{\eta}
is cofinal in
\kappa^{+}
, there is some
\beta\in A_{\eta}
such that
\alpha<\beta
whenever
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in X
.
Let us prove that
\alpha\in F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\beta)
whenever
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in X
.
Suppose not, that
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in X\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and that
\alpha\notin F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\beta)
.
The latter assumption gives that
a_{\alpha}\cap b_{\beta}\not\subseteq\eta
, i.e. there is
\zeta\in\kappa\setminus\eta
such that
\zeta\in a_{\alpha}
and
\zeta\in b_{\beta}
.
From
\zeta\geq\eta
,
\alpha\in A_{\eta}
(since
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in X
), and
\zeta\in a_{\alpha}
, it follows that
\zeta\in c
.
On the other hand, from
\zeta\geq\eta
,
\beta\in A_{\eta}
, and
\zeta\in b_{\beta}
, we have
\zeta\notin c
.
Clearly, the last two observations are contradictory, so we proved that
\alpha\in F^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\beta)
whenever
\left\{\alpha\right\}\in X
.
But this means that no element of
X
belongs to the basic open set
U^{\mathcal{G}}_{\eta}(\beta)
, contradicting the choice of
X
.
Hence, the theorem is proved.
∎

Recall that an
(\omega_{1},\omega_{1})
pre-gap
\mathcal{G}
in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
is Hausdorff if the set
F^{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(\beta)
is finite for all
n<\omega
and
\beta<\omega_{1}
. In this case
\mathcal{G}
has to be a gap.

Theorem 7.6
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a Hausdorff gap in
\mathcal{P}(\omega)
, then
G(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}})\equiv_{T}[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
.

Proof.

Note, as before, that the collection
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}}
ordered by
\supseteq
is isomorphic as a poset to
\left\{\bigcup_{(n,\beta)\in K}F^{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(\beta):K\in\left[\omega\times\omega_{1}\right]^{<\omega}\right\}
ordered by
\subseteq
.
This collection is uncountable, for example by Lemma
7.4
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is Hausdorff, for each
K\in\left[\omega\times\omega_{1}\right]^{<\omega}
the set
\bigcup_{(n,\beta)\in K}F^{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(\beta)
is a finite subset of
\omega_{1}
.
Hence,
G(\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{G}})\equiv_{T}[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}
by Remark
2.2
and Remark
7.3
.
∎

Current status analysis of the prevalence and regional disparities of robot-assisted laparoscopic prostatectomy in Japan using diagnosis procedure combination data
Authors: Kentaro Hara, Masato Kanda, Hiroyo Kuwabara, Yoshio Kobayashi, Takahiro Inoue
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe introduction of the “da Vinci S HD Surgical System” marked a significant shift towards robotic surgeries in Japan. However, initial high costs and lack of efficacy data posed barriers to its widespread adoption. By 2023, more than 570 da Vinci units were operational in Japan, highlighting the growing acceptance of robotic surgery despite these challenges. This study aimed to investigate the prevalence and regional disparities in the adoption of robot-assisted laparoscopic prostatectomy (RALP) across Japan using diagnosis procedure combination data. This retrospective observational study analyzed data from 2857 urban and 4184 regional hospitals across 47 prefectures in Japan. The study focused on the number of RALP procedures, da Vinci systems, and certified urological surgery proctors. Multiple regression analysis was performed to identify significant factors influencing RALP adoption. Urban areas demonstrated a higher prevalence of RALP procedures and more da Vinci systems compared to regional areas, with urban hospitals performing an average of 937 RALP procedures compared to 195.5 in regional hospitals. The number of certified urological surgeons also showed significant urban-regional disparities, contributing to the overall imbalance. Our findings highlight substantial regional disparities in access to robot-assisted surgery in Japan, with urban areas benefiting from better access to advanced medical technologies and specialist training. Addressing these disparities will require targeted policies to improve the dissemination of robotic surgery systems and enhance training opportunities in regional cities.Keywords: Robot-assisted laparoscopic prostatectomy (RALP), Regional disparities in healthcare, Da vinci surgical system, Access to advanced medical technologies, Urological surgery certificationSubject terms: Medical research, Urology

Introduction

In the nascent stages of robot-assisted surgery in Japan, the requirement for a significant initial investment in expensive equipment was a notable barrier, in addition to a lack of comprehensive data regarding its efficacy and safety. This trajectory began to shift following the pharmaceutical approval of the ”da Vinci S HD Surgical System” in 2009, and subsequently, in April 2012, robot-assisted radical prostatectomy was included in the insurance reimbursement scheme1. Furthermore, through revisions in medical fee schedules, a compensation framework for innovative surgical techniques has been progressively established, leading to the performance of robot-assisted surgeries for 20 diseases under insurance coverage by 20242–4.

The surgical landscape for prostate cancer has significantly transformed in recent decades. Initially conducted as open surgeries, prostate cancer procedures transitioned to laparoscopic techniques and, from the early 2000s, began incorporating robotic technologies, such as the da Vinci surgical robot system, revolutionizing prostate cancer surgery methods5,6. Robot-assisted laparoscopic prostatectomy (RALP), which involves robotic laparoscopic prostate removal, requires precision and meticulous control. Owing to its ability to enhance surgical accuracy and reduce patient recovery time, RALP has been widely adopted by urologists7. Additionally, RALP has been shown to reduce complication rates and achieve superior oncological outcomes in patients with prostate cancer and is associated with improved functional outcomes compared to laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP), suggesting an expected increase in surgery numbers8–10.

By 2023, the global installation of da Vinci surgical systems surpassed 7.500 units, with over 570 units operational within Japan11. Sustained growth in the adoption of robotic surgery systems, along with the introduction of new models, suggests an increase in the number of da Vinci systems in Japan by 2023, as inferred from previous research2,12. Nevertheless, the implementation and adoption of robotic surgery face several barriers, including equipment costs, economic viability, limited evidence of outcomes, dynamics between clinicians and administrators, and perspectives on its dissemination and expansion13. Consequently, not all facilities are presumed to be equipped to perform robotic surgery. Moreover, differences have been reported in the functional aspects of urban and regional cities in Japan14. Given the various backgrounds of robotic surgery, we hypothesized that there are potential disparities in the adoption of robotic surgery between urban and regional areas. The study focuses on regional disparities in RALP within Japan. These disparities are of particular concern due to their impact on access to advanced medical treatments in rural areas. Previous research has primarily examined the overall adoption of robotic systems; however, few studies have focused on the factors contributing to disparities in the number of procedures performed. This study aims to analyze these disparities and identify key factors that may contribute to regional imbalances in access to robot-assisted surgery.

Operational definitions

Ordinance-designated cities, including Tokyo, were categorized as urban, whereas cities not designated within prefectures were classified as regional.

Results

Comparison of urban and regional cities

The population of males aged 65 years and over was higher in urban areas compared to regional areas (p < .01). The total number of RALP procedures was also greater in urban areas (p < .01), as well as the number of RALP procedures per 100,000 male population aged 65 and over (p = .01). Urban areas had more da Vinci systems overall (p < .01) and more da Vinci systems per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over (p < .01). Additionally, urban areas had a higher number of hospitals performing RALP (p < .01), a greater number of Japanese Urological Association-certified specialists (p < .01), and more urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians (p < .01). On the other hand, the number of general hospital beds per 100,000 people was lower in urban areas compared to regional areas (p = 0.02) (Table 1)

Multiple regression analysis affecting the number of RALPs per 100,000 male population over 65

Multiple regression analysis focused on the number of RALP procedures per 100,000 males aged > 65 years revealed several key findings. The partial regression coefficient for the number of da Vinci systems per 100,000 males over 65 years was 7.65, with a standard error of 5.88, resulting in a t-value of 1.30. This was not statistically significant, with a p-value of 0.20 and a 95% confidence interval ranging from −4.21 to 19.51, indicating that the number of da Vinci systems per 100,000 population did not significantly impact the number of RALP procedures.

Conversely, the number of hospitals performing RALP per 100,000 males over 65 years had a partial regression coefficient of 22.55, with a standard error of 6.02 and a t-value of 3.75. This result was statistically significant (p < .01), with a 95% confidence interval from 10.41 to 34.69, suggesting that an increased number of hospitals performing RALP per 100,000 population was associated with a significant increase in the number of RALP procedures.

The number of Japanese Urological Association-certified specialists per 100,000 males over 65 years yielded a partial regression coefficient of −0.23, with a standard error of 0.61 and a t-value of −0.37. This was not statistically significant (p = .0.71), with a 95% confidence interval from −1.46 to 1.01, indicating that the number of certified specialists per 100,000 population did not significantly influence the number of RALP procedures.

Furthermore, the number of urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians per 100,000 males over 65 years of age had a partial regression coefficient of 2.07, with a standard error of 3.12 and a t-value of 0.66. This was not statistically significant (p = .0.51), with a 95% confidence interval from −423 to 8.36, demonstrating that the number of proctor-certified physicians per 100,000 people did not significantly affect the number of RALP procedures. The adjusted R² was 0.506.

Discussion

This study analyzed the prevalence and regional disparities of RALP in Japan using diagnostic DPC data. In addition, We performed multiple regression analysis to assess the relative contributions of various factors, such as the number of da Vinci systems and certified physicians, to the total number of RALP procedures. This method was chosen to identify and quantify the influence of each factor while controlling for confounding variables, allowing us to better understand the key drivers behind the regional disparities in RALP adoption. The analysis revealed clear disparities in RALP implementation between urban and regional cities. Specifically, urban areas had significantly more da Vinci systems and hospitals performing RALP than regional cities, suggesting that these factors contributed to a higher number of RALP procedures in urban areas.

A key factor driving these regional disparities is the unequal access to advanced medical technologies. Urban areas benefit from better access to high-end medical equipment and specialists, which facilitates the introduction of new surgical technologies. These findings suggest that urban areas have better access to advanced medical technologies, which may explain the higher number of RALP procedures. The concentration of resources and specialists in urban centers likely contributes to these disparities, as previous studies have also indicated. By contrast, the shortage of medical resources in regional cities may prevent the adoption of new technologies. The difference in medical levels and access between urban and regional areas, as highlighted by prior research15,16, suggests that regional disparities in robotic surgery have already been identified. Addressing this issue requires policies to promote the dissemination of advanced medical technologies in regional cities, with a particular focus on the deployment of medical equipment and training and allocation of medical personnel.

The discovery that the total number of da Vinci systems affects the total number of RALP procedures may seem counterintuitive initially. However, this suggests that facilities with a higher number of these systems can operate at their maximum surgical capacity. However, this inverse relationship may imply that facilities already equipped with many da Vinci systems exhibit a diminished marginal effect on the increase in RALP procedures. In other words, while having multiple systems increases surgical capacity, there appears to be a limit to how much additional surgical output can be achieved merely by adding more systems. This idea is supported by the observation that there is no significant difference in the number of da Vinci systems per 100,000 males over 65 between urban and regional cities, implying that facilities equipped with da Vinci systems may have reached their limit in terms of the number of surgeries they can perform17–19. The disparity in the number of da Vinci systems between urban and regional cities is a significant factor contributing to the uneven distribution of RALP procedures. Our analysis revealed that the median number of da Vinci systems per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over is 3.7 in urban areas, compared to 2.7 in regional cities. To correct this disparity, regional cities would need approximately 1 additional da Vinci system per 100,000 males aged 65 and over to match the availability in urban areas. It is true that some facilities in regional cities do own multiple surgical robots, which may seem to contradict the overall trend of resource scarcity in regional areas. However, these facilities are exceptions rather than the norm. The overall trend, as indicated by our analysis, shows that regional cities generally have fewer da Vinci systems per capita than urban areas. While certain advanced facilities exist, the broader regional disparity in access to advanced surgical technologies remains significant. Increasing the number of da Vinci systems in regional areas could reduce the gap in access to advanced surgical technologies and improve healthcare equity. This suggests that resource allocation plays a crucial role in addressing regional disparities in access to robot-assisted surgery. Additionally, while the total number of hospitals performing RALP was significantly higher in urban areas, no significant difference was observed in the number of hospitals performing RALP per 100,000 males over 65 years. This suggests that urban facilities may have multiple systems20. However, the analysis showed that the factor influencing the number of RALP procedures per 100,000 males over 65 years was the number of hospitals performing RALP per population, not the mere presence of multiple da Vinci systems within a single facility.

This study confirmed the positive effect of the number of urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians on the total number of RALP procedures. However, it is important to recognize the bidirectional relationship between the number of certified physicians and the number of RALP procedures. On one hand, more certified physicians allow for a greater number of surgeries to be performed. On the other hand, a higher volume of RALP procedures provides the necessary experience for physicians to become certified. This mutual influence highlights the importance of specialist training and allocation, particularly in urban areas where more frequent surgeries contribute to quicker certification and proficiency in RALP. The learning curve for RALP is influenced by various factors, including the length of training as a urologist and whether the surgeon has received certification. Research indicates that certified surgeons can achieve proficiency more quickly in RALP. However, recent changes in academic regulations, which are being phased out, may affect how surgeons are trained and certified in the future. These changes could have a long-term impact on RALP proficiency and patient outcomes, necessitating further research on how these regulations influence surgical training and certification. Training and appropriate distribution of specialists are crucial for addressing the disparities in access to advanced medical technologies and specialist care between urban and regional areas. The disparity is particularly evident in regional areas, where fewer RALP procedures may limit opportunities for surgeons to gain the experience required for certification, perpetuating the gap in access to advanced medical services. In urban areas, where both resources and procedures are more concentrated, patients benefit from more frequent and advanced treatments, such as RALP. In addition to addressing specialist distribution, recent advancements in remote support technology for the da Vinci Surgical System offer a promising solution to geographical challenges. Remote support systems allow surgeons in regional areas to receive real-time guidance and assistance from specialists located in urban centers, which can significantly improve the quality of care in underserved regions. By leveraging this technology, the gap in access to robot-assisted surgery between urban and regional areas can be reduced, making it easier for facilities in remote locations to perform complex procedures without needing on-site experts. As remote support technology continues to evolve, it may play a key role in overcoming geographic barriers and improving healthcare equity. However, the current policies for robotic surgery certification may not be sufficient to ensure surgeon proficiency, highlighting the need for further research on the education and training of specialists to optimize patient safety and outcomes and reduce regional disparities in RALP implementation21,22. Moreover, addressing regional disparities in robotic surgery requires comprehensive strategies including improving access to advanced medical technologies in regional cities, enhancing specialist education and training, and providing support for the deployment of medical equipment. Future research should further analyze the barriers to introducing medical equipment and the problem of specialist allocation in regional cities.

Finally, acknowledging that this study, being retrospective and based on DPC data, may have been influenced by limitations inherent to such data is crucial. Future research, including more hospitals and regions, and prospective or intervention studies are needed to support the findings of this study. Additionally, this study focused solely on the da Vinci Surgical System, which is the most widely used robot-assisted surgery system in Japan. However, other surgical robots, such as Hinotori (Medicaroid, headquartered in Kobe, Japan) and Hugo (Medtronic, headquartered in Dublin, Ireland), as well as others, have entered the medical field. These systems could act as confounding factors in the analysis, as their increasing adoption may influence the number of robot-assisted procedures and the overall distribution of surgical technologies in both urban and regional areas. Future studies should take into account the impact of these various robotic systems to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the landscape of robot-assisted surgery in Japan. We also recognize that these findings are based on Japan’s unique universal health insurance system, which may limit their generalizability to other healthcare systems. While the findings may not be directly applicable to countries without a widespread universal health insurance system, the challenges of addressing regional disparities in access to advanced medical technologies, such as robot-assisted surgery, remain relevant. These insights can inform discussions on how to allocate resources and ensure equitable access to cutting-edge medical procedures in countries with different healthcare systems. Japan’s trend of adopting the da Vinci system has the potential to make it the leading country in terms of the number of da Vinci systems per capita, a trend similar to its rapid adoption of CT and MRI technologies in the past. While this trend may greatly benefit Japan’s healthcare system, it also raises concerns about the global implications. As Japan continues to expand its use of robotic surgery, the disparities in access to such technologies between developed and developing countries may widen. Developing countries, which already face challenges in accessing basic medical resources, may fall further behind, limiting the global distribution of advanced surgical technologies. Addressing these disparities will require international collaboration and strategies to improve access to robotic surgery in developing regions. However, the challenges of resource allocation, access to advanced surgical technologies, and the distribution of specialist care are relevant across different healthcare contexts. The insights from this study may provide valuable lessons for improving healthcare equity and access to advanced medical procedures in other regions as well.

This study analyzed the prevalence and regional disparities in RALP in Japan using DPC data. The results revealed significant disparities in the implementation of RALP between urban and regional cities, with urban areas having more da Vinci systems and hospitals performing RALP. These disparities suggest that the access to advanced medical technologies is uneven, which contributes to a higher number of RALP procedures in urban areas. Additionally, the total number of da Vinci systems and urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians had different effects on the total number of RALP procedures. This study highlights the importance of improving access to advanced medical technologies in regional cities as well as the education and training of specialists to address regional disparities. These findings may contribute to future healthcare policy formulations for equitable access to robot-assisted surgical treatments across Japan.

Methods

Data collection

We used data from the diagnosis procedure combination/per-diem payment system (DPC/PDPS). The DPC is a unique diagnostic categorization developed exclusively in Japan and is also used in the per-diem payment system. Many hospitals nationwide have adopted the DPC, with the Ministry of Health, Labor and Welfare conducting “Surveys on the Impact of DPC Introduction” and publishing its findings23. The DPC data encompass hospital admission and discharge information, patient demographics, diagnoses, surgical data, and treatment details, facilitating its use in clinical research.

Study design and ethical considerations

This retrospective observational study involved medical institutions registered with the DPC across 47 prefectures in Japan24. The research design was based on the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines25. A statistical plan was formulated before data collection, and analyses were performed after data collection. This study adhered to the principles of the 2013 amendment of the Declaration of Helsinki. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, the need for informed consent was waived. The sampling framework employed was a basic medical facility file, and stratified random sampling was used. The sample size was determined to be representative of the regional distribution. A research plan was publicly disseminated and participants were provided the option to opt-out online via our hospital’s website in accordance with the guidelines of our institutional review board26.

Study setting and population

The study was conducted from January 2021 to March 2023, with objective settings and participant demographics derived from patient surveys and bed function reports provided by the Ministry of Health, Labor, and Welfare. These reports covered 2,857 hospitals located in urban cities (N = 15) and 4,184 hospitals in regional cities (N = 32) across all 47 prefectures in Japan27,28. Additionally, certified urological robot-assisted surgery proctors were obtained from the Japanese Urological Association and the Japanese Society of Endourology and Robotics29,30. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chiba University Hospital (approval number: 3309). The committee also granted a waiver for informed consent. Notably, the patients and the public were not involved in any stage of the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination of our research (Fig. 1).

Outcome and data collection

The primary outcome measure of this investigation was the number of robot-assisted radical prostatectomies performed per 100,000 male population aged 65 years and over. We selected the age criterion of 65 years and over because the risk of prostate cancer significantly increases in this population, making them the primary group for RALP procedures. Secondary outcome measures included the number of da Vinci systems, the total number of hospitals performing RALP, Japanese Urological Association-certified specialists, and urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians. Survey items were as follows: (1) designation as an ordinance-designated city, (2) population of men aged 65 years and over, (3) general hospital bed count per 100,000 population, (4) full-time physicians per 100,000 population, (5) the total number of RALPs, (6) RALPs per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over, (7) da Vinci systems, (8) Da Vinci systems per 100,000 males aged 65 and over, (9) hospitals performing RALP, (10) hospitals performing RALP per 100,000 male population aged 65 and over, 11) total number of Japanese Urological Association certified specialists, 12) Japanese Urological Association certified specialists per 100,000 males aged 65 and over, 13) total number of urological robot-assisted surgery proctor certified physicians, 14) urological robot-assisted surgery proctor certified physicians per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over.

Statistical analysis

For statistical analysis, the presence of ordinance-designated cities, the population of males aged 65 years and over, information related to robotic surgeries, and facility data were subjected to simple aggregation, with median values (interquartile ranges) presented. For the primary and secondary outcome measures, intergroup comparisons between urban and regional cities were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test.

To identify the factors associated with the total number of RALP procedures, a multiple regression analysis was conducted using the total number of da Vinci systems, hospitals performing RALP, Japanese Urological Association certified specialists, and urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians as explanatory variables. Similarly, to elucidate factors influencing the number of RALP procedures per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over, multiple regression analysis was employed with the number of RALP procedures per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over as the dependent variable and the number of da Vinci systems per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over, the number of hospitals performing RALP per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over, Japanese Urological Association certified specialists per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over, and urological robot-assisted surgery proctor-certified physicians per 100,000 males aged 65 years and over as explanatory variables.

A significance level of 5% was considered statistically significant. Outliers were identified as values below the first quartile minus 1.5 times the interquartile range (IQR) or above the third quartile plus 1.5 times the IQR. JMP® 17 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) was used for statistical analyses.

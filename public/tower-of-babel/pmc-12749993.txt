Integrated FDM optimization with multivariate capability analysis for dimensional and compressive mechanical properties
Authors: Moath Alatefi, Abdulrahman M Al-Ahmari, Mustafa Saleh, Abd Elatty E Abd Elgawad, Abdullah Yahia AlFaify
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFused deposition modeling (FDM) is an additive manufacturing (AM) technology capable of producing functional parts with complex geometries. However, optimizing both dimensional and mechanical quality characteristics is challenging due to the influence of multiple process parameters. This study aims to determine how key FDM parameters affect the multivariate dimensional and mechanical quality characteristics and to establish an integrated framework for optimizing these responses simultaneously. An experimental design based on response surface methodology (RSM) was implemented to optimize four process parameters: layer thickness, extruder temperature, plate temperature, and printing speed. Cylindrical PLA samples adhering to compression standards were fabricated, and both dimensional characteristics (length and diameter) and mechanical characteristics (compressive strength and modulus) were evaluated. Multivariate process capability indices (MPCIs) were then estimated to assess overall process capability. The results revealed that layer thickness and extruder temperature are the most influential parameters affecting MPCIs. The optimal dimensional MPCI was achieved with a low layer thickness, high extruder temperature, low plate temperature, and low printing speed. Also, the proposed model explained 88% of the total variability in mechanical MPCI. This research introduces an integrated RSM–multivariate process capability analysis approach for the simultaneous optimization of multiple correlated quality characteristics in FDM, which improves both dimensional precision and mechanical performance in AM processes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-30098-y.Keywords: Additive manufacturing, Process parameter optimization, Response surface methodology (RSM), Multivariate quality characteristics, Process capability index (MPCI), PLA material, Statistical quality controlSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction and literature review

The quality characteristics of parts produced using FDM should be optimized for enhancing customer satisfaction and reducing process variation. The optimization process is conducted by investigating the FDM parameters that can affect the dimensional and mechanical properties of its outputs, such as layer thickness, extruder temperature, and printing speed1,2. Due to the multiple quality characteristics in the FDM process, multivariate process capability analysis is used to evaluate outputs using single index.

Process capability analysis measures the ability of a process to produce products within desired specifications3. The first univariate process capability index was proposed by Kane3. It has been used to evaluate manufacturing process4, manufacturing systems5, assembly lines6 and additive manufacturing processes7,8. Moreover, many multivariate process capability indices (MPCIs) have been proposed and applied to evaluate process outputs with multiple quality characteristics9–11. The true performance of manufacturing process can be expressed using MPCIs12–14. In the context of evaluating multivariate additive manufacturing process, Moath et al15 have proposed a framework to evaluate FDM process with multivariate quality characteristics using MPCIs. Also Wadea et al16 examine the performance of metal additive manufacturing process through evaluating electron beam melting process using MPCIs.

The FDM process has been investigated in the literature using different process outputs, such as dimensional17 and mechanical properties18. Researchers have explored various methods to optimize FDM fabrication conditions. For instance, a combination of a definitive screening design (DSD) and an artificial neural network (ANN) has been proposed19,20. This approach identifies optimal fabrication conditions for better dimensional accuracy and mechanical properties. Mahmood et al.21 examine the impact of process parameter variations on the geometrical characteristics of the produced parts using Taguchi’s design of experiment statistical technique. Taguchi’s design of the experiment has also been used by Alafaghani and Qattawi3, Moza et al.4, and Kumar et al.22. The primary impacts of four processing factors in the FDM process—the infill percentage, infill pattern, layer thickness, and extrusion temperature—are examined in Alafaghani and Qattawi23 work using Taguchi’s DOE. The findings indicated that a lower extrusion temperature, thinner layers, a lower infill percentage, and a hexagonal infill pattern were necessary for improved dimensional accuracy. Moreover, FDM and other material extrusion processes have several performance-related limitations which hinder part quality. The surface texture of printed components often varies significantly with build orientation and layer height, leading to non-uniform roughness, weak bonding, and pore formation across inclined and vertical surfaces24. Moreover, interlayer bonding strength is highly dependent on nozzle temperature and flow rate, where suboptimal bonding conditions can reduce flexural performance and increase surface irregularities25. Additionally, optimization studies on flexural strength and elastic modulus using advanced multi-objective algorithms confirmed that process parameters—particularly deposition angle, infill density, and temperature—govern the trade-off between mechanical stiffness and flexibility in PLA parts26. These challenges highlight that bonding quality, porosity, and surface finish remain critical factors influencing mechanical and dimensional behavior in material extrusion.

Moreover, RSM has been widely used in the literature to optimize AM processes. It was first introduced by Box and collaborators27,28, and it has been applied in many fields, such as chemical process optimization29, the food industry30, and 3D printing31. RSM is a collection of computational and mathematical techniques for building experimental models. It works effectively for describing and assessing problems when several factors increase response and where the goal is to enhance it. It provides less experimental outcomes but more knowledge, nonetheless.

FDM quality characteristics and materials.

Furthermore, the effect of printing parameters of FDM process on mechanical properties have been investigated in many studies. In this regard, Ala’aldin et al.23 investigated the influence of key FDM process parameters on mechanical properties of PLA parts. Their results demonstrated that higher extrusion temperature, optimized layer thickness, and higher infill percentage enhance part strength. In a related study, Camposeco et al.36 conducted an experimental investigation on optimizing multiple FDM responses, including mechanical strength which demonstrated that the integrated desirability approach enabled simultaneous optimization of process parameters achieving a balanced improvement. Recent studies have extensively examined the influence of FDM process parameters on the mechanical performance of printed parts. Farashi et al.40 reported that reducing layer thickness and aligning the print orientation parallel to the build plate significantly improve tensile strength, while higher extrusion temperature stabilizes mechanical results. Panda et al.42 highlighted the role of extrusion velocity and layer thickness in deformation behavior, indicating their strong influence on part strength. Torres et al.44 found that layer thickness and infill density are key factors affecting torsional strength. Similarly, Sharma et al.45 demonstrated that layer thickness and infill percentage have the most substantial effects on tensile and compressive strength of ABS specimens. Kholil et al.46 showed that the maximum compressive strength was achieved at a 0.15 mm layer thickness for PLA, while thicker layers reduced strength. Popović et al.47 identified 190 °C extrusion temperature and 40 mm/min speed as optimal for maximizing tensile performance in PLA parts. Nathaphan et al.48 confirmed that low layer height, slow printing speed, and minimum shell count yield the highest compressive yield stress in ABS components. Stojković et al.49 emphasized that layer height dominates tensile strength improvement across materials, outperforming the influence of annealing time or temperature. Qi et al.50 demonstrated that incorporating CaCO₃ and TCP fillers into PLA enhances compressive modulus and strength in lattice structures,. Collectively, these studies underline that layer thickness, infill density, temperature, and orientation are the most critical determinants of mechanical performance in FDM-printed parts.

Despite extensive studies on the influence of FDM parameters49,53, most previous works focused on optimizing single quality characteristics, either dimensional or mechanical. However, 3D-printed parts by the FDM process have multivariate characteristics. Also, the interdependence among these characteristics has rarely been addressed. The literature in this research revealed that, there is currently a lack of research on process capability analysis, which uses more than one quality characteristics to optimize the quality of AM process products. Therefore, this research introduces an integrated methodology that combines RSM and multivariate process capability analysis to simultaneously optimize multiple correlated quality characteristics and evaluate the overall process capability of the FDM process. With the existence of more than one quality characteristic, optimizing MPCIs ensures the process capability to produce parts within specification limits14,54. Furthermore, using multivariate tests revealed more realistic results due to the existing correlation between the product’s quality characteristics55. This study aims to determine the optimal process conditions that optimize MPCI of manufacturing processes.

The research presents the FDM process, product design, and used materials in Section "Materials and methods". The third Section explains the proposed methodology. Sections "Process parameters" and "Manufacturing material" analyze and discuss the results of this research. Finally, Section "Research methodology" concludes this research and provides directions for future research.

Materials and methods

Printing process and device specifications

An example of a part with specific features is shown in Figure 1. A 3D CAD model is first designed and exported as an STL file, which defines the tessellated surface of the planned object. Also, the object is sliced into horizontal layers resulting in a machine-readable file containing all the needed instructions for printing process. The printing parameters were changed according to the design experiment in section 2.2. Resulting in 30 combinations for FDM process optimization. The current study used RSM, which enables the detection of curvature in the response.

Additive manufacturing workflow.

An open-access FDM printer (Original Prusa i3 MK3S+, Prusa Research, Czech Republic) was used to fabricate all test specimens. The printer is equipped with a 0.4 mm brass nozzle, a build volume of 250 × 210 × 210 mm, and a heated bed capable of reaching up to 120 °C. The material used was PLA filament with a diameter of 1.75 mm. The printer operates using G-code generated through PrusaSlicer software, allowing precise control of process parameters such as layer thickness, extrusion temperature, plate temperature, and printing speed as shown in Figure 2.

Process parameters

Process parameters are adjusted during the slicing procedure, after CAD modeling. Table 3 presents the considered FDM parameters—layer thickness (LT), extrusion temperature (ET), plate temperature (PT), and printing speed (PS)—and their corresponding levels. The levels of these variables, as detailed in Table 3, were selected based on the supplier’s recommendations for the used material (PLA)56, the FDM printer’s capability, and findings from the literature. For instance, the LT range (0.15–0.25 mm) was selected as reported in57–59. The ET range (195–225 °C) was chosen according to the supplier’s recommendations56 and previous studies59,60. The PT range (55–65 °C) was determined based on preliminary experiments, supplier guidance56, and58. Finally, the PS range (30–60 mm/s) was selected according to the printer’s capability and58. Other FDM parameters were kept constant, including a nozzle diameter of 0.4 mm, raster width of 0.45 mm, raster angle of ±45°, and an infill density of 100%.

Manufacturing material

Polylactic acid (PLA) filament was used in this study. It is produced through lactic acid condensation and water evaporation or by polymerizing it through a ring-opening mechanism. PLA is produced from sustainable sources, however, it has not been widely used due to structural and operational issues61. It is the ideal substance for 3D printing applications due to its thermal and mechanical characteristics when it has been annealed. Ethyl acetate can be used in vapor chambers to smooth PLA surfaces because of its low boiling point. This study used 1.75 mm-diameter PLA filament (Prusament PLA Galaxy Silver filament), acquired from Prusa Polymers in the Czech Republic.

Response surface methodology (RSM)

In order to print strong parts with accurate dimensions, the parametric analysis, and FDM parameter optimization were conducted using RSM. MPCI is used as a response to optimize the considered process, which is computed in equation (3). Through the use of experimental design, the impact of the FDM parameters on dimensional accuracy and mechanical properties was assessed. This study used RSM’s face-centered central composite (FCC) design, a three-level, second-order experimental design, to examine how the four chosen FDM parameters affect the output response (MPCI). The RSM design with the four FDM printing parameters (i.e., LT, ET, PT, and PS) listed in Table 3 resulted in 30 experiments, with five repetitions for each experiment. The 30 RSM experiments were composed of 16 cube points, 8 axial points, and 6 center points.

RSM results

Analyzing the effect of FDM process parameters on MPCIs revealed that there are significant relations between most of process parameter and estimated MPCI, as shown in the RSM results in Table S 1 in the supplementary file. The results showed that the layer thickness, extruder temperature and printing speed have a significant effect on MPCI (p-value < 0.05).

Moreover, the interaction effects of the quadratic and two factors on process parameters have been investigated to predict the optimal effect of process parameters on MPCI. The only significant quadratic effect among all parameters is the effect associated with layer thickness. Also, the interaction of layer thickness with printing speed resulted in a significant effect on MPCI. Also, the plate temperature has a significant effect on MPCI only when it interacts with the extruder temperature. We can rely on the results and model of this experiment because the lack of fit in the model is not significant (p = 0.165).

Model adequacy

RSM depends on many assumptions that must be met in order to draw valid conclusions from these tests. Normality assumption is the most important in statistical analysis. In this research, the normal probability plot of residuals is illustrated in Figure 4, in which the residuals of the RSM test are plotted against normal probability. Since the plotted points form a straight line, the researcher could not ensure that the data departed from a normal distribution. Furthermore, RSM assumes that the residuals have constant variances over the response range. However, in this research, the variance is not exactly equal over the response range, but it has no increasing or decreasing pattern. Therefore, a constant variance could be assumed, and there were no clear outlier points. As illustrated by Figure 4, in the residual versus order plot, the residuals are fairly distributed around the zero value. There is no increasing or decreasing pattern which ensures the randomness of errors.

Main factor and interaction effects

The main effects of the process parameters have been investigated in order to identify the best process setting conditions. Therefore, Figure 5 shows the plots of the main effects of four process parameters on MPCI, which revealed that different layer thickness levels have a curvature pattern when estimating MPCI. Most MPCI results when the layer thickness is medium or low, whereas it decreases with increasing layer thickness. In contrast, extruder temperature, plate temperature, and printing speed seem to have a linear effect on MPCI. Both extruder temperature and printing speed have a positive relation with MPCI; that is, increasing extruder temperature or printing speed will increase MPCI. Also, plate temperature showed a linear negative relation with estimated MPCI.

Mechanical properties optimization

Mechanical experiments

Compression testing of PLA materials involves subjecting samples to compressive forces to evaluate their mechanical properties, such as compressive strength and modulus of elasticity. It’s used to assess the suitability of PLA for various applications, such as 3D printing. This research is optimizing the compressive strength and modulus of elasticity of 3D printed parts that are produced using the FDM process and PLA material. Compressive strength refers to the maximum compressive stress a specimen can sustain without failure. Moreover, the modulus of elasticity or Young’s modulus, is a measure of a material’s stiffness or resistance to deformation under an applied load. It quantifies the relationship between stress and strain in the elastic region of the material’s stress-strain curve. The compression test has been conducted using the Zwick\Roell compression testing machine shown in Figure 10.

Zwick/Roell compression testing machine.

Compression testing ensures the quality and consistency of PLA materials used in various applications by establishing standards for compressive strength and modulus of elasticity. However, the compressive strength and modulus of elasticity are affected by the 3D printing process parameters. Therefore, experiments need to be proposed that investigate the effect of varying the process parameters on the compressive strength and modulus of elasticity.

Compression testing starts by preparing PLA samples using the FDM process for the desired shape and size. Based on the length-to-width aspect ratio specified in the ASTM D695-15 standard for compressive testing of rigid polymeric materials, ensuring that the length is twice the sample width50. The ANOVA results of MPCI for mechanical properties indicate that the model is highly significant, explaining 88% of the total variability in the response, as shown in Table 9 (model’s p < 0.001 and a high F-value of 19.27). Extruder Temperature (ET) displays a positive linear trend, indicating that higher temperatures improve MPCI, likely due to better material flow and bonding. Conversely, Plate Temperature (PT) shows a nonlinear relationship, suggesting that higher and lower temperatures may slightly reduce MPCI. Speed of printing has a positive linear impact. These insights imply that balancing LT and PT while maximizing ET and Speed, could lead to optimal process performance.

Insights and implications

The significant impact of layer thickness (LT) is critical in determining the mechanical robustness of the printed components. The observed nonlinear relationship indicates that there is an optimal LT value that maximizes MPCI. This finding aligns with existing literature, which suggests that thicker layers may introduce defects or inconsistencies in bonding, leading to weaker mechanical performance. The consistent increase in MPCI with higher ET levels suggests that elevated temperatures facilitate better material flow and bonding, which are crucial for enhancing compressive strength and modulus of elasticity. This result highlights the need for precision in maintaining optimal temperature settings. However, while ET had a clear positive effect, the marginal impact of PT suggests that its role might be more complex. Although higher PT levels slightly decreased MPCI, possibly due to issues like warping or uneven cooling. Also, its significance in interaction with other parameters like ET indicates that PT cannot be overlooked. This nuanced impact necessitates further exploration to understand its role in multi-faceted printing scenarios. The interactions between PT and Speed suggest that while higher speeds generally improve MPCI, this benefit is maximized only when PT is carefully controlled. This interaction highlights the importance of a holistic approach in parameter optimization, considering not just the individual effects but also how these parameters influence each other.

The study’s optimization findings provide practical guidance for the FDM printing of PLA parts. The identified optimal settings not only maximize mechanical performance but also enhance production efficiency. The application of these optimized parameters could lead to more consistent product quality and improving overall process sustainability. Overall, this study demonstrates the utility of using MPCI as a comprehensive metric for evaluating and optimizing the mechanical properties of 3D-printed components.

Comparing research’s results with previous research

The results of this study are consistent with previous research emphasizing the strong influence of layer thickness and extrusion temperature on the quality of FDM-printed parts. Similar to the findings of Ala’aldin et al.23, Nathaphan et al.48, and Farashi et al.40, lower layer thickness significantly improved dimensional accuracy and mechanical strength, while higher extrusion temperatures enhanced material bonding and reduced interlayer voids. However, our results provide a more comprehensive insight by quantifying these effects through the MPCI, showing that optimal dimensional capability is achieved at a low layer thickness, high extrusion temperature, low plate temperature, and low printing speed. Consistent with Sharma et al.45 and Torres et al.44, the influence of printing speed and interaction effects among parameters were also confirmed to be significant. Unlike earlier works that optimized individual responses, the proposed RSM-multivariate process capability analysis integration enables simultaneous optimization of dimensional and mechanical quality characteristics, demonstrating an enhanced capability to achieve overall product precision and performance stability.

Conclusion

This research demonstrated a novel and integrated approach to optimizing the FDM process by combining response surface methodology (RSM) with multivariate process capability analysis. Unlike traditional studies that optimize each quality characteristic separately, the proposed methodology simultaneously considers multiple correlated quality characteristics, providing a more realistic assessment of overall process capability. The findings revealed that layer thickness and extruder temperature are the most influential parameters affecting both dimensional and mechanical performance. These models were further validated experimentally, confirming the reliability of the proposed approach. However, the study is limited to a specific polymer material (PLA) and a defined range of process parameters. Extending the analysis to other materials, geometries, and additive manufacturing technologies would enhance the model’s predictive robustness. Additionally, incorporating other multivariate and intelligent optimization techniques—such as machine learning or adaptive control—could further improve process prediction and automation. From an industrial perspective, the proposed methodology offers a practical framework for manufacturers to evaluate and optimize FDM processes more systematically. It can be implemented for process monitoring, parameter tuning, and quality assurance, leading to improved dimensional precision and mechanical strength. This work thus contributes to advancing data-driven quality optimization and capability assessment in additive manufacturing environments.

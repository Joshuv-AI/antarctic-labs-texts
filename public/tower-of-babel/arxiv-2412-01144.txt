strong approximation for the intersection of two quadrics
Dasheng Wei, Jie Xu, Yi Zhu

Abstract
. We study strong approximation for the intersection of two affine quadrics. As its application, we prove the fibration method for weak approximation over number fields of rank four with nonsplit fibers split by quadratic extensions.

14G05 (11D57, 14F22)

1.
Introduction

The paper is mainly devoted to the study of the following fibration method for weak approximation over number fields.

Question 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over a number field
k

and let
f:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
be a dominant morphism whose geometric generic fiber is rationally connected.
Assume that
X_{c}(k)
is dense in
X_{c}({\mathbf{A}}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}(X_{c})}
for all but finitely many
c\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
, where

X_{c}=f^{-1}(c)
. Does it follow that
X(k)
is dense in
X({\mathbf{A}}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}(X)}
?

Question
1.1
has been extensively studied. One main approach
consists in applying the theory of descent developed by
Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc
[
6
]
to reduce the problem to certain
torsors associated with the vertical Brauer group of
X
relative to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
.
This approach has been applied successfully in many
cases, including Châtelet surfaces
[
5
]
, some conic and
quadric bundles
[
1
]
, and various toric
bundles
[
22
,
4
,
16
,
2
]
.

Another approach, known as the fibration method, was initiated by
Harari
[
18
]
and has been further developed by
Wittenberg and Harpaz
[
20
]
. The fibration method has been successfully applied to various families of rationally connected varieties, including conic bundles
[
11
,
20
]
and some quadric bundles
[
3
,
19
]
.

The known cases for fibration method are mostly for fibrations of low ranks. Recall here that the
rank
of a fibration
f:X\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
is defined to be
the sum of degrees of closed points of
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{1}
above which the fiber of
f
is not split.
The definition of split fibers can be found in work of Skorobogatov
[
30
]
, where the notion was originally introduced to the subject.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
18
,
20
,
3
,
19
]
)
.

Question
1.1
has a positive answer for each of the following cases :

(i)

the rank of
f
is at most
2
;

(ii)

the rank of
f
is
3
and every fiber
X_{m}
is split by a quadratic extension of
k(m)
;

(iii)

the rank of
f
is
3
, one fiber
X_{m}
lies above a rational point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}

and every remaining fiber
X_{m}
is split by a quadratic extension of
k(m)
.

Recently, Harpaz, Wittenberg and the first author
[
19
]
proved that Question
1.1
has a positive answer when all non-split fibers are split by cyclic extensions of the base fields of the fibers under Schinzel’s hypothesis. For unconditional results, the strongest result is due to Browning and Matthiesen
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
20
,
2
]
)
.

Question
1.1
has a positive answer when the base field is
\mathbb{Q}
and all non-split fibers lie over rational points.

In this paper, we investigate the rank four case of Question
1.1
. This can be viewed as a generalization of Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Swinnerton-Dyer’s result on Châtelet surfaces
[
5
]
and Colliot-Thélène’s result on conic bundles of rank four
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Question
1.1
has a positive answer when

{\mathrm{rank}}(f)=4
and all fibers split by a quadratic extension.

By Theorem
1.4
, we have the following example for quadric surface bundles with at most four nonsplit fibers split by quadratic extensions.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
X/\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
be the projective quadric surface bundle given by

x^{2}-a(t)y^{2}+P(t)(z^{2}-b(t)w^{2})=0

where
a(t),b(t)
and
P(t)
are nonzero square-free polynomials in
k[t]
of even degree. Let
\gcd(a(t),b(t)P(t))=1.
Suppose that the degree of
P(t)
is at most
4
, then
X(k)
is dense in
X({\mathbf{A}}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}(X)}
.

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we follow the fibration method developed by Harpaz, Wittenberg and the first author of the paper
[
19
, Cor. 4.7]
. The rank four case reduces to study strong approximation for the intersection of two affine quadrics, which is the main effort of this paper. In fact, its proof immediately follows case (iii) in Theorem
1.6
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k
be a number field and
v_{0}
a place of
k
. Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},n\geq 5
be the pure geometrically integral intersection of two quadratics which is not a cone. Let
\widetilde{V}
be the affine cone of
V
and
Z
a closed subset of
\widetilde{V}
of codimension at least
2
. Let
\widetilde{V}^{\mathrm{sm}}
be the smooth locus of
\widetilde{V}
and
\widetilde{U}=\widetilde{V}^{\mathrm{sm}}\setminus Z
.

Suppose that
V
has only finitely many singular points. Then:

(i)

Suppose
V^{\mathrm{sm}}(k)\neq\varnothing
and
n=5
, then
\widetilde{U}
satisfies strong approximation with Brauer-Manin obstruction off
v_{0}
.

(ii)

Suppose
V^{\mathrm{sm}}(k)\neq\varnothing
and
n=6
, then
\widetilde{U}
satisfies strong approximation off
v_{0}
.

(iii)

Suppose
n\geq 7
, then
\widetilde{U}
satisfies strong approximation off
v_{0}
.

In particular, we have the following corollary for nonsingular intersection of two affine quadrics.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
v_{0}
be a place of
k
. Let
\widetilde{V}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{k}\setminus{(0,\cdots,0)}
be the variety defined by

Q_{1}(x_{0},\cdots,x_{n})=Q_{2}(x_{0},\cdots,x_{n})=0,

where
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are quadratic forms. Let
Z
be a closed subset of
\widetilde{V}
of codimension at least
2
. If

and
\widetilde{V}
is nonsingular, then
\widetilde{V}\setminus Z
satisfies strong approximation off
v_{0}
.

Terminology
.

Notation and terminology are standard. Let
k
be a number field,
\Omega_{k}
the set of all places of
k
and
\infty
the set of all archimedean places of
k
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{k}
be the ring of integers of
k
and
\mathcal{O}_{S}
the
S
-integers of
k
for a finite set
S
of
\Omega_{k}
containing
\infty
. For each
v\in\Omega_{k}
, the completion of
k
at
v
is denoted by
k_{v}
, the completion of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
at
v
by
\mathcal{O}_{v}
and the residue field at
v
by
k(v)
for
v\not\in\infty
. Let
{\mathbf{A}}_{k}
be the adele ring of
k
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety over
k
. Denote
X(\mathbf{A}_{k})^{B}
to be the set of all
(x_{v})_{v}\in X(\mathbf{A}_{k})
satisfying
\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\inv_{v}(A(x_{v}))=0
for each
A
in the subgroup
B
of the Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(X)=H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})
of
X
, where the map
\inv_{v}:\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is the invariant map from local class field theory.
The subgroup
\mathrm{Br}_{0}(X)
of constant elements in the Brauer group is
the image of the natural map
\mathrm{Br}(k)\to\mathrm{Br}(X)
. The algebraic
Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}_{1}(X)
is the kernel of the natural map
\mathrm{Br}(X)\to\mathrm{Br}(X_{{\overline{k}}})
, where
X_{{\overline{k}}}=X\times_{k}{\overline{k}}
.

Let
S
be a finite set of places of
k
. We say that

strong approximation
holds for
X
off
S
if
the image of the set
X(k)
of rational points on
X
is dense in the space

X(\mathbb{A}_{k}^{S})
of adelic points on
X
outside
S
. Strong approximation for
X

off
S
implies the Hasse principle for
S
-integral points on any

S
-integral model of
X
. We say that

X({\mathbf{A}}_{k}^{S})
,
strong approximation with Brauer-Manin obstruction
holds for
X
off
S
if

X(k)
is dense in the image of
X(\mathbf{A}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}}
in

X({\mathbf{A}}_{k}^{S})
. Here,
X(\mathbf{A}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}}
denotes the set of adelic points orthogonal to the Brauer group with respect to all finite étale covers of
X
; see
[
31
, Definition 2.1]
for a precise definition.

For a geometrically integral variety
X
over a number field
k
, we denote
X^{\mathrm{sm}}
to be its smooth locus.
Let
f:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities. If
X^{\mathrm{sm}}(k)
is dense the image of
f(\widetilde{X}(\mathbf{A}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}(\widetilde{X})})

(resp.
f(\widetilde{X}(\mathbf{A}_{k})^{\mathrm{Br}_{1}(\widetilde{X})})
) in
X(\mathbf{A}_{k}^{S})
, we say that

central strong approximation
with (resp. algebraic) Brauer-Manin obstruction off
S
holds for X. The definition does not depend on the choice of the resolution of singularities by
[
32
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S
be a finite non-empty set of places of
k
. Let
L/k
be a finite étale algebra.
Let
f:Y\to R_{L/k}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{L})\setminus F
be a surjective morphism, where
F\subset R_{L/k}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{L})
is a closed subset of codimension
2

U:=R_{L/k}(\mathbb{G}_{\text{m},L})
. Let

be an open subset such that the fiber of
f_{W}:f^{-1}(W)\to W
is smooth. We suppose:

•

all geometric fibers of
f
above
U\setminus F
are split;

•

there exists a finite field extension
K
of
k
such that for any closed point
m
in
R_{L/k}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{L})\setminus(U\cup F)
, the fiber
f_{m}\otimes_{k}K
is split.

•

the fiber of
f
above any
k
-point of

satisfies strong approximation off
S
;

•

for any
v\in S
,
f^{-1}(W)(k_{v})\to W(k_{v})
is onto.

Then
Y
satisfies strong approximation with algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction off
S
.

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}=L\otimes_{k}K
, let

be the torus
R_{K^{\prime}/k}(\mathbb{G}_{\text{m},K^{\prime}})

.
The projection

gives a

-torsor over
U^{\prime}
, where the map

is given by sending

to
\alpha N_{K^{\prime}/L}(a)

\alpha\in L^{*}
. First we fix
\alpha=1
. We denote by
\tau\in H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(U^{\prime},T^{\prime})
its isomorphism class, the cup products

\tau\cup\chi
give an element in
\mathrm{Br}_{1}(U^{\prime})
for any
\chi\in H^{1}(k,\widehat{T}^{\prime})

Obviously

is finite.
Let

be the finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Br}_{1}(f^{-1}(U^{\prime}))
generated by
f^{*}(\tau\cup\chi)

where

and
\chi\in H^{1}(k,\widehat{T}^{\prime})

For any
(y_{v})_{v}\in Y({\mathbf{A}}_{k})^{A\cap\mathrm{Br}_{1}(X)}
,
there exists

arbitrarily close to

in

by Harari’s formal lemma (
[

Since

Let us apply open descent theory
to the projection

.
According to
[
17

,
the adelic point

(y^{\prime}_{v})_{v}
can be lifted to an adelic point

of some

\alpha N_{K^{\prime}/L}(a_{v})=f(y^{\prime}_{v})

.

Let

be the inverse image of the union of

in
{\mathbf{A}}^{S}_{K^{\prime}}
.
Let

\mu_{0}\in W
.

Let
S^{\prime}\supset S
be a finite set of places of

containing the archimedean places, all finite places where

is ramified and all places at which we want to approximate

-models. Suppose

and
\ord_{v}(N_{L/k}(\mu_{0}))>0

, where

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{s}\}
be the set of

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}

be the exceptional curve above

be the

-torsor over

Then,

(i)

is isomorphic to the variety of

(2.1)

f(x)\lambda=y\mu,

where

(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\setminus{(0,0)}

is a separable polynomial of degree

(ii)

\mathrm{Br}(\widetilde{U}_{{\overline{k}}})=0

(iii)

Choose a linear change of coordinates, we may assume that the

are different, then

-coordinates of
p_{1},\cdots,p_{s}
are just all roots of a separable polynomial

of degree
s
. Then

p_{i}
satisfies

, here

is the
x
-coordinates of
p_{i}
and

is a

-polynomial of degree at most

. By the change of coordinates

. Therefore we may assume that

is the set defined by

. Hence

is isomorphic to the variety of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}

f(x)\lambda=y\mu,

where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
,
(\lambda:\mu)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
\widetilde{U}
be of the form described as in (1), then

is the

-torsor over

We have a fibration

\mu
.

Note that
\mathrm{Pic}(\widetilde{U}_{\overline{k}})\cong{\mathrm{Coker}}[\mathbb{Z}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}l_{i}]

to
(1,1,\ldots,1)

Let

\mu\neq 0

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a pure geometrically integral intersection of two quadrics
Q_{1}=Q_{2}=0
which is not a cone. If the homogeneous
polynomial
P(\lambda,\mu)=\det(\lambda Q_{1}+\mu Q_{1})
vanishes identically, then
V
has infinitely many singular
k
-rational points.

Proof.

By changing coordinates, we may assume that
Q_{1}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}x_{i}^{2}
with
a_{i}\in k^{*}
and

1\leq r<n
. Let
[b_{ij}]
be the matrix of
Q_{2}
. The coefficient of
\lambda^{r}\mu^{n-r}
of
P(\lambda,\mu)
is
a_{0}\cdots a_{r}\det(B)
, where
B
is the
(n-r,n-r)
-matrix with entries

(b_{ij})_{r+1\leq i,j\leq n}
. Thus
\det B=0
, and there exists a non-zero vector
z=(c_{r+1},\cdots,c_{n})

with coordinates in
k
such that
Bz^{t}=0
. The point
z=[0:\cdots:0:c_{r+1}:\cdots:c_{n}]\in V(k)
and it is clearly conical on the quadric
Q_{1}=0
, hence it is a singular
k
-point. By replacing
Q_{1}
by any quadric
\lambda Q_{1}+\mu Q_{2}
and repeating the above discussion, we get a another singular
k
-point
z_{[\lambda:\mu]}
which is conical on the quadric
\lambda Q_{1}+\mu Q_{2}=0
. The proof now follows from that
z_{[\lambda:\mu]}
are pairwise distinct, otherwise some
z_{[\lambda:\mu]}
is conical on
V
which contradicts to that
V
is not a cone.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},n\geq 5
be a pure geometrically integral intersection of two quadrics which is not a cone. Suppose that
V_{\overline{k}}
only has finite singular points. Then,
there exists a nonempty open subset

such that, for any

,

is a nonsingular complete intersection,

of Theorem
3.1

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement over
{\overline{k}}
.
For any singular point

, let

be the closed subset of

. Since

For any smooth point

by
[
21
, Theorem 7.2]
, there is a line on

In particular, the Fano scheme of lines

∎

Functional tilings and the Coven-Meyerowitz tiling conditions
Gergely Kiss, Itay Londner, Máté Matolcsi, Gábor Somlai

Abstract
. Coven and Meyerowitz [ 1 ] formulated two conditions which have since been conjectured to characterize all finite sets that tile the integers by translation. By periodicity, this conjecture is reduced to sets which tile a finite cyclic group ℤ M \mathbb{Z}_{M} . In this paper we consider a natural relaxation of this problem, where we replace sets with nonnegative functions f , g f,g , such that f ⁡ ( 0 ) = g ⁡ ( 0 ) = 1 f(0)=g(0)=1 , f ∗ g = 𝟏 ℤ M f\ast g=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{M}} is a functional tiling, and f , g f,g satisfy certain further natural properties associated with tilings. We show that the Coven-Meyerowitz tiling conditions do not necessarily hold in such generality. Such examples of functional tilings carry the potential to lead to proper tiling counterexamples to the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture in the future.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Tilings of the integers and the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a finite set. One says that
A

tiles the integers by translations
if a disjoint union of translated copies of
A
covers
\mathbb{Z}
. Alternatively, if there exists a set of translations
T\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that any integer
n
can be uniquely expressed as the sum
a+t=n
, with
a\in A
and
t\in T
. It is well-known
[
14
]
that in this case the set
T
must be periodic, i.e. there exist
M\in\mathbb{N}
and a finite set
B\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that
T=B\oplus M\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that
|A||B|=M
and
A\oplus B
is a tiling of the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, with addition
\mod M
.

The prime factorization of
M
will be denoted by
M=\prod_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}^{n_{i}}
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be an arbitrary function. Identifying
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with
\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
, we define the
mask polynomial
of
f
as

(1.1)

F(X)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}f(z)X^{z}.

The mask polynomial is closely
related to the Fourier transform of
f
, defined as

\hat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}f(z)e^{-2\pi iz\xi/M}\ \ (\xi=0,1,\dots,M-1).

Namely, with the notation
\eta=e^{-2\pi i/M}
, we have
F(\eta^{\xi})=\hat{f}(\xi)
.

If
f
is nonnegative, the total weight of
f
will be denoted by

(1.2)

|F|:=F(1)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}f(z)=\hat{f}(0).

Given
m|M
, we define the set

R_{m}:=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}:(z,M)=m\}.

Note, for future reference, that
R_{M}=\{0\}
. We refer to each of the sets
R_{m}
as a
step class
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
f
is a step function if
f
is constant on each step class
R_{m}
. We will denote the set of step functions by
\mathcal{A}(M)
.

The set
\mathcal{A}(M)
is a vector space with the additional property that the Fourier transform maps it to itself (see Lemma
1.5
below).

Given a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, its mask polynomial, defined in (
1.1
) with
f=\mathbf{1}_{A}
, will be denoted by
A(X)
. As
A(X)
is a polynomial with integer coefficients, for any
m|M
the Fourier transform
\hat{1}_{A}
is either constant zero on the step class
R_{m}
, or it has no zeros in
R_{m}
at all. As such, the support of
\hat{1}_{A}
is always a union of step classes. Also, the total weight of
\mathbf{1}_{A}
coincides with the cardinality of the set
A
, which will be denoted
|A|
.

Using the language of mask polynomials one may reformulate the tiling property in
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
in terms of cyclotomic polynomials. Due to translation invariance we may assume
0\in A\cap B
. The tiling condition
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
is equivalent to

(1.3)

A(X)B(X)=1+X+\ldots+X^{M-1}\mod(X^{M}-1).

Let
\Phi_{m}
be the cyclotomic polynomial of order
m
, that is the unique, monic, irreducible polynomial whose roots are the primitive
m
-th roots of unity. The factorization (
1.3
) is in turn equivalent to

(1.4)

|A||B|=M\text{ and for all }m|M,\,m\neq 1\,\Phi_{m}(X)\text{ divides at least one of }A(X),B(X),

or, using the language of Fourier transforms, it is further equivalent to

(1.5)

|A||B|=M\text{ and for all }0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{M},\ \hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(\xi)=0\ \text{or}\ \hat{\mathbf{1}}_{B}(\xi)=0.

Note that for any
m|M
we have

(1.6)

\Phi_{m}(X)|A(X)\ {\text{if and only if}}\ A(\eta^{\xi})=0\ {\text{for all}}\ \xi\in R_{M/m},

which can also be stated as

(1.7)

\Phi_{m}(X)|A(X)\ {\text{if and only if}}\ \hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(\xi)=0\ {\text{for all}}\ \xi\in R_{M/m}.

Coven and Meyerowitz proved the following.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
A
be a finite set of integers and

S_{A}:=\{p^{\alpha}\,:\,p^{\alpha}\text{ is a prime power and }\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(X)|A(X)\}.

Consider the following two conditions

(T1)
A(1)=\prod_{s\in S_{A}}\Phi_{s}(1)
,

(T2) if
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in S_{A}
are powers of different
primes, then
\Phi_{s_{1}\dots s_{k}}(X)|A(X)
.

Then

•

If
A
satisfies both (T1) and (T2) then
A
tiles
\mathbb{Z}
.

•

If
A
tiles
\mathbb{Z}
then it must satisfy (T1).

•

If
A
tiles
\mathbb{Z}
, and
|A|
has at most two prime factors, then it satisfies (T2).

The question whether conditions (T1) and (T2) characterize all sets which tile the integers, without any restriction on the prime factorization of the cardinality of a tile, has been referred to in the literature as the
Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture
(the first instance it was referred to as an explicit conjecture is
[
6
]
). This conjecture is considered to be the main open question in the topic of integer tilings. Until recent years, the conjecture has been verified only in some special cases
[
2
,
12
,
18
,
16
,
6
]
. More recently there has been noticeable progress. In
[
8
,
9
]
it has been confirmed for all tilings of period
M=(p_{1}p_{2}p_{3})^{2}
, where
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
are distinct primes. Subsequently, in
[
10
]
it has been confirmed for tilings of period
M
having arbitrarily many prime factors with some specified restrictions on their sizes and powers.

Conditions (T1) and (T2) can also be naturally formulated for arbitrary nonnegative functions
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\to\mathbb{R}
. For the mask polynomial
F(X)
of
f
, for any
m|M
we say that
\Phi_{m}(X)|F(X)
if
F(\eta^{\xi})=0
for all
\xi\in R_{M/m}
. Then, letting
S_{F}
denote the set of prime-powers
p^{\alpha}|M
such that
\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(X)|F(X)
, we say that
F
satisfies condition (T1) if
F(1)=\prod_{s\in S_{F}}\Phi_{s}(1)
, and
F
satisfies condition (T2) if
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in S_{F}
being powers of different primes implies that
\Phi_{s_{1}\dots s_{k}}(X)|F(X)
.

Going back to condition (
1.4
), given a collection
H=\{m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{s}\}
of divisors of
M
,
the main difficulty is to decide whether there exists a set
A
such that the cyclotomic divisors of
A(X)
are exactly
\Phi_{m_{j}}
(
1\leq j\leq s
), and
A
satisfies condition (T1). It is not hard to see that this question is equivalent to solving an integer programming (IP) problem (we will discuss this fact in Section
2
below). It is therefore natural to consider a linear programming relaxation of the tiling criteria, to which we turn next.

1.2.
Weak-tiling and pd-tiling

The notion of
weak tiling
was introduced in
[
11
]
as a key concept in proving Fuglede’s conjecture for convex bodies. In the context of finite cyclic groups, it is a relaxation of proper tilings in the following way: a set
A
is said to tile
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
weakly if there exists a nonnegative function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
f(0)=1
and
\mathbf{1}_{A}\ast f=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{M}}
. Clearly, when
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
is a tiling, one can choose
f=\mathbf{1}_{B}
(where we assumed, without loss of generality, that
0\in B
).

Weak tiling is a very natural concept. However, in order to be able to invoke tools from linear programming, the following slightly modified definition was introduced in
[
3
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

A set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
weakly, if there exists a function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(i)
f(0)=1
,

(ii)
f\geq 0
,

(iii)
\hat{f}\geq 0

and such that

(1.8)

\mathbf{1}_{A}\ast f=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{M}}.

To abbreviate the terminology, we will just say that
A
pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, dropping the
term ”weakly”. Any function
f
with the properties above will be called a pd-tiling complement of
A
.

In this terminology, ”pd” stands for positive definite. As observed in
[
3
]
if
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, then
A
pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with the function
\frac{1}{|B|}\mathbf{1}_{B}\ast\mathbf{1}_{-B}
. In connection with Fuglede’s conjecture we remark that if
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
is spectral then
A
also pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
(cf.
[
3
]
).

We also note that for any pd-tiling, equation (
1.8
) is equivalent to the analogue of (
1.5
):

(1.9)

|A||F|=M,\ \text{and for all}\ 0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}\ \text{we have}\ \hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(\xi)=0\ \text{or}\ \hat{f}(\xi)=0,

where
|F|
denotes the total weight of
f
, as defined in (
1.2
).

Furthermore, due to the fact that the support of
\hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}
is a union of step classes
R_{m}
, the function
\hat{f}
must vanish on those classes, i.e.
F(X)
must be divisible by the corresponding cyclotomic polynomials
\Phi_{M/m}(X)
.

Formally speaking, the concept of pd-tiling is a relaxation of proper tiling. It is therefore natural to ask the following questions.

Problem 1.1
.

Is it true that whenever
A
pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, then
A
also tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
properly?

Problem 1.2
.

Is the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture true for all pd-tilings, i.e. is it true that for any set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
that pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, and any a pd-tiling complement
f
of
A
, both
A
and
F
satisfy conditions (T1) and (T2)?

While these questions remain open in full generality, in this paper we give a partial answer to Problem
1.1
(see Proposition
4.1
below), and solve a functional variant of Problem
1.2
.

1.3.
Functional pd-tiling

As in
[
3
]
, it is natural to go one step further with the relaxation of the tiling conditions.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be functions satisfying conditions (i)-(iii) of Definition
1.3
. We say that the pair
(f,g)
is a functional pd-tiling of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
if

(1.10)

f\ast g=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{M}}.

Clearly, if
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
is a tiling, then the functions
h^{(1)}_{A}=\frac{1}{|A|}\mathbf{1}_{A}\ast\mathbf{1}_{-A}
and
h^{(1)}_{B}=\frac{1}{|B|}\mathbf{1}_{B}\ast\mathbf{1}_{-B}
form a functional pd-tiling of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, and the cyclotomic divisors of
H^{(1)}_{A}(X)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}h^{(1)}_{A}(z)X^{z}
and
H^{(1)}_{B}(X)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}h^{(1)}_{B}(z)X^{z}
are the same as those of
A(X)
and
B(X)
, respectively. As such, condition (T1) holds for both
H^{(1)}_{A}
and
H^{(1)}_{B}
.

In the context of the functional pd-tiling
(h^{(1)}_{A},h^{(1)}_{B})
above, it is natural to carry out an
averaging
procedure which, as we shall see, leads to
step functions

h_{A}
,
h_{B}
while not altering the cyclotomic divisors of the corresponding mask polynomials. This enables us to study the Coven-Meyerowitz conditions in the class of step functions.

Given the functions
h^{(1)}_{A},h^{(1)}_{B}
above, and an element
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}
such that
(r,M)=1
, we define
h^{(r)}_{A}(z)=h^{(1)}_{A}(rz)
and

(1.11)

h_{A}(z)=\frac{1}{\phi(M)}\sum_{r\in R_{1}}h^{(r)}_{A}(z),

and similarly for
h_{B}
. Here
\phi
denotes Euler’s totient function.

For any
z,y
in the same step class
R_{d}
there exists an
r\in R_{1}
, such that
rz\equiv y
(mod
M
), therefore
h_{A}(z)=h_{A}(y)
, and hence
the functions
h_{A}
and
h_{B}
are constant over step classes, i.e. they are step functions,
h_{A},h_{B}\in\mathcal{A}(M)
. It is also well known that the Fourier transform of a step function is also a step function, and we cite the formula for the Fourier transform of the indicator function of a single step class
R_{m}
as follows:

Lemma 1.5

(
[
7
]
, Lemma 4.8)
.

Let
m|M
, then

\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{R_{m}}}(\xi)=\sum_{d|(M/m,\xi)}\mu\left(\frac{M}{md}\right)d,

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

The functions
h_{A},h_{B}
and their Fourier transforms
\hat{h}_{A},\hat{h}_{B}
are all nonnegative, and their positive values are all bounded away from zero. Indeed,
h_{A}=\frac{1}{|A|\phi(M)}\sum_{r\in R_{1}}1_{rA}\ast 1_{-rA}
, and the sum is a nonnegative, integer-valued function, therefore the non-zero values of
h_{A}
satisfy

(1.12)

h_{A}(z)\geq\frac{1}{|A|\phi(M)}\geq\frac{1}{M\phi(M)}=:\delta_{M},\ \ \ \ \ \ (\text{if}\ h_{A}(z)\neq 0),

and the same argument holds for
h_{B}
. Interestingly, the same lower bound also holds for
\hat{h}_{A}
and
\hat{h}_{B}
for the following reason:

(1.13)

\widehat{h}_{A}(\xi)=\frac{1}{|A|\phi(M)}\left(\sum_{r\in R_{1}}1_{rA}\ast 1_{-rA}\right)^{\bigwedge}(\xi)\geq 0,

where the expression is nonnegative because the Fourier transform of each term inside the bracket is
|\hat{1}_{rA}|^{2}(\xi)\geq 0
. Also, the sum in the bracket is integer-valued, and hence its Fourier transform is also integer valued by Lemma
1.5
above. Therefore, we conclude that all non-zero values of
\widehat{h}_{A}

satisfy

(1.14)

\widehat{h}_{A}(\xi)\geq\frac{1}{|A|\phi(M)}\geq\frac{1}{M\phi(M)},\ \ \ \ \ \ (\text{if}\ \widehat{h}_{A}(\xi)\neq 0)

and the same argument holds for
\widehat{h}_{B}
.

The advantage of the averaging procedure above is that the class of step functions is easier to study with tools from linear programming (LP). As such, we will be able to formulate some non-trivial LP conditions for any functional pd-tilings and also for proper tilings of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
(see Section
2
). The downside of averaging, however, is that when any nonnegative step function
f
is given, it is a often a nontrivial task to determine whether there exists a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
such that
f=h_{A}
.

Consider now the mask polynomials
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
corresponding to
h_{A},h_{B}
, as defined in equation (
1.1
). We claim that
the averaging process preserves cyclotomic divisibility. First, due to (
1.6
), for every
m|M
and every
(r,M)=1
we have that

\Phi_{m}(X)|H^{(r)}_{A}(X)
if and only if
\Phi_{m}(X)|H^{(1)}_{A}(X)
. As the values
H^{(r)}_{A}(\eta^{\xi})=\hat{h}_{A}(\xi)
are all nonnegative by (
1.13
), we conclude that
H_{A}(\eta^{\xi})=0
if and only if
H^{(r)}_{A}(\eta^{\xi})=0
for all
r
. That is,
\Phi_{m}(X)|H_{A}(X)
if and only if
\Phi_{m}(X)|H^{(r)}_{A}(X)
for all
r
, which happens if and only if
\Phi_{m}(X)|H^{(1)}_{A}(X)
. Therefore, the cyclotomic divisors of
H_{A}(X)
and
H_{B}(X)
are the same as those of
A(X)
and
B(X)
. In particular, condition (T1) of Coven-Meyerowitz is satisfied for both
H_{A}
and
H_{B}
.

It is therefore natural to ask the following question.

Problem 1.3
.

Let
(f,g)
be a functional pd-tiling of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
as in Definition
1.4
. Assume that both
f
and
g
are step functions, and the corresponding mask polynomials
F
and
G
both satisfy condition (T1). Do
F
and
G
necessarily satisfy condition (T2)?

A positive answer to this question would mean that the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture holds in this much more general setting. However, the main result of this paper is to answer this question in the negative.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
p,q
be prime numbers such that
p<q<p^{2}
, and set
M=p^{4}q^{2}
. Then there exists a functional pd-tiling
(f,g)
of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
such that both
f
and
g
are step functions (as in Definition
1.1
) and the corresponding polynomials
F,G
satisfy condition (T1), but neither of them satisfies condition (T2).

Theorem
1.2
implies that the functional pd-tilings of Theorem
1.6
cannot originate from proper tilings of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, i.e. there do not exist sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
such that
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, and
f=h_{A}
,
g=h_{B}
. However, in principle, it could be possible that one of the functions originates from a set, i.e. there exists a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
such that
f=h_{A}
, but no corresponding set
B
exists for which
g=h_{B}
(or vice versa,
g
originates from a set, but
f
does not).

A natural follow-up question is whether the examples
f\ast g=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{M}}
can be used in the future to construct proper counterexamples
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M_{1}}
to the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture for some other value
M_{1}
. For example, one could hope to combine the functional pd-tilings of Theorem
1.6
for
M=p^{4}q^{2}
and
M^{\prime}=r^{4}s^{2}
, and try to construct a proper tiling counterexample in the group
\mathbb{Z}_{M_{1}}=\mathbb{Z}_{MM^{\prime}}
.

Note also, that by extending
f
and
g
periodically in an appropriate way,
one may obtain such examples of functional pd-tilings for any
M^{\prime}
divisible by
M
. We also remark here that the examples in Theorem
1.6
are far from being unique, e.g. other examples exist for
M=(pqr)^{2}
(see Section
5
for a detailed account of the case where
p=3,q=5,r=7
). On the other hand, it is not hard to prove that there are no examples in the spirit of Theorem
1.6
for
M=pq
, and it also appears (by computer experiment) that there are no such examples for
M=p^{\alpha}q^{\beta}
where
\beta=1,

\alpha\leq 6
, or
\beta=2,\alpha\leq 3
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we explain the theoretical background of the linear programming formulation of the problem. This formulation gives a very convenient and efficient way to tackle the problem of functional tilings, while also providing non-trivial conditions for proper tilings. In Section
3
we introduce some further auxiliary tools, similar to those developed in
[
7
]
. Finally, in Section
4
we present a detailed account of the functional pd-tilings violating the (T2) condition for
M=p^{4}q^{2}
, thus settling Problem
1.3
. We also give a minor positive result concerning Problem
1.1
.

2.
Linear programming constraints

The aim of this section is to study tilings and functional pd-tilings with tools from linear programming. In particular, we will see that questions of tiling are inherently related to finding maximal cliques in Cayley graphs, and Delsarte’s linear programming bound is a natural tool to give upper bounds for the size of such cliques. As such, we can expect non-trivial tiling conditions to arise from linear programming methods.

Given a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, recall
[
7
]
that the
divisor set
of
A
is defined as
Div(A)=\{(a-a^{\prime},M):\ a,a^{\prime}\in A\}
. We will use the notation
Div^{\ast}(A)=\cup_{d\in Div(A)}R_{d}
, i.e.
Div^{\ast}(A)
is the union of step classes corresponding to the divisors of
M
appearing in
Div(A)
. We will refer to such step classes
R_{d}
(
d\in Div(A)
) as
divisor classes
corresponding to
A
. The following theorem of Sands is fundamental in the study of tilings of cyclic groups.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

For sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
,
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
is a tiling if and only if
|A||B|=M
and
Div^{\ast}(A)\cap Div^{\ast}(B)=\{0\}
.

It is easy to see that this theorem translates questions of tiling into questions about maximal cliques in Cayley graphs defined on
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
. Namely, for a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, define the Cayley graph
\Gamma_{A}
as follows: the vertices of
\Gamma_{A}
are the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, i.e.
\{0,1,\dots,M-1\}
, and two distinct vertices
x,y
are connected if and only if
x-y\in Div^{\ast}(A)
. By Theorem
2.1
we conclude that
A
tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
if and only if
|A|
divides
M
and there exists an independent set
B
in the graph
\Gamma_{A}
(i.e. a clique
B
in the complement graph) of size
M/|A|
.

As the graph
\Gamma_{A}
depends only on
Div^{\ast}(A)
, it is natural to introduce the following general definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
M
, and a collection of step classes
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
with
0\in H
, we use the notation
H^{\prime}=(\mathbb{Z}_{M}\setminus H)\cup\{0\}
to denote the standard complement of
H
(the word ”standard” refers to the fact that
0
appears in both
H
and
H^{\prime}
).
We define the graph
\Gamma_{H}
such that the vertices are the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, i.e.
\{0,1,\dots,M-1\}
, and two distinct vertices
x,y
are connected if and only if
x-y\in H
. Also, we let
\omega(H)
denote the maximal cardinality of a clique in the graph
\Gamma_{H}
.

With this terminology at hand, we can formulate necessary and sufficient conditions for tilings with reference only to the sets
H
and
H^{\prime}
. It is easy to see that
\omega(H)\omega(H^{\prime})\leq M
for any
H
and, as a direct corollary of Theorem
2.1
, the case of equality is equivalent to tiling.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
be given. There exists a tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with
Div^{\ast}(A)\subset H
and
Div^{\ast}(B)\subset H^{\prime}
if and only if
\omega(H)\omega(H^{\prime})=M
.

This means that given
M
and
H
we can, in principle, decide whether there exists a tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with
Div^{\ast}(A)\subset H
and
Div^{\ast}(B)\subset H^{\prime}
. In practice, however, determining the size of maximal cliques is an NP-hard problem. Of course, an upper bound on the clique number can be given by linear programming (there are several formulations of this bound – we prefer to call it the
Delsarte LP bound
, cf
[
13
]
). We recall the following terminology from
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

For a collection of step classes
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
, and any positive number
\delta>0
, the Delsarte parameters corresponding to
H
and
\delta
are defined as follows:

D^{+}(H)=\max\{\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}h(z):h\in\mathcal{A}(M),h(0)=1,\hat{h}\geq 0,h|_{H}\geq 0,h|_{H^{c}}=0\}
,

D^{-}(H)=\max\{\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}h(z):\ h\in\mathcal{A}(M),h(0)=1,\hat{h}\geq 0,h|_{H^{c}}\leq 0\}
,

D^{\delta+}(H)=\max\{\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}h(z):\ h\in\mathcal{A}(M),h(0)=1,\hat{h}\geq 0,h|_{H}\geq\delta,h|_{H^{c}}=0\}
,

and their reciprocals,

\lambda^{+}(H)=1/D^{+}(H)
,
\lambda^{-}(H)=1/D^{-}(H)
,
\lambda^{\delta+}(H)=1/D^{\delta+}(H)
.

We remark here that in
[
13
]
the parameters
\lambda^{+},\lambda^{-}
were defined, but it is slightly more convenient for us here to work with their reciprocals,
D^{+}
and
D^{-}
.

In order to gain some intuition of the concepts
D^{+}
,
D^{-}
and
D^{\delta+}
, it is easiest to think of the continuous analogue of these quantities. If
B_{1}
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(take
d=8
for concreteness), then
D^{+}(B_{1})
is the maximal integral of a nonnegative, positive definite function supported on
B_{1}
. It is known
[
5
]
, that the unique extremizer in this case is
\frac{1}{{\text{Vol}}(B_{1/2})}1_{B_{1/2}}\ast 1_{B_{1/2}}
, and
D^{+}(B_{1})={\text{Vol}}(B_{1/2})
. For
D^{\delta+}(B_{1})
we insist that the function is at least
\delta
over the entire ball. As such,
D^{\delta+}(B_{1})
is strictly less than
D^{+}(B_{1})
, because the unique extremizer for
D^{+}(B_{1})
above is zero on the boundary. On the contrary, for
D^{-}(B_{1})
we allow the function to take any non-positive values outside the ball, which results in greater freedom. The quantity
D^{-}(B_{1})
gives a strong upper bound the density of sphere packings (in fact, the exact bound in dimension 8, c.f.
[
19
]
), which is better than the volume bound given by
D^{+}(B_{1})
. As such, for the ball
B_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{8}
we have
D^{\delta+}(B_{1})<D^{+}(B_{1})<D^{-}(B_{1})
with strict inequalities at both places. Unfortunately, the situation is not so clear in the discrete setting of
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
. It is an interesting project to give necessary (or sufficient) conditions for the quantities
D^{\delta+}(H),D^{+}(H),D^{-}(H)
to coincide or differ.

There is an obvious hierarchy among the numbers
D^{\delta+}(H),D^{+}(H),D^{-}(H)
, and two of them give an upper bound on the clique number of
H
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
H
and any
\delta>0
, we have
D^{\delta+}(H)\leq D^{+}(H)\leq D^{-}(H)
, and

\omega(H)\leq D^{+}(H)
.

Proof.

The first inequalities are obvious because of the hierarchy of the constraints in the linear programs defining them. The second inequality follows from the fact that for any clique
A
in
\Gamma_{H}
the function
h_{A}
defined in (
1.11
) is an admissible function in the definition of
D^{+}(H)
, and
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}h_{A}(z)=|A|
. (The function
h_{A}
is indeed admissible, as it is a nonnegative, positive definite step function, supported on
\cup_{r\in R_{1}}r(A-A)\subset H
, and
h_{A}(0)=1
.)
∎

There is an important duality between these Delsarte parameters.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
13
]
)
.

For any
H
we have
D^{+}(H)D^{-}(H^{\prime})=M
.

Proof.

This is the content of Theorem 4.2 in
[
13
]
.
∎

We remark here that the main use of the Delsarte parameters is to give an upper bound on the clique numbers of Cayley graphs via this duality. Indeed,

\omega(H)\leq D^{+}(H)=\frac{M}{D^{-}(H^{\prime})}\leq\frac{M}{\hat{h}(0)}
for any admissible function
h
in the definition of
D^{-}(H^{\prime})
. Therefore, any admissible function leads to an upper bound on
\omega(H)
.

Next, we give a connection of these parameters to tilings.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
be given. If there exists a tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with
Div^{\ast}(A)\subset H
and
Div^{\ast}(B)\subset H^{\prime}
, then
|A|=\omega(H)=D^{+}(H)=D^{-}(H)
and
|B|=\omega(H^{\prime})=D^{+}(H^{\prime})=D^{-}(H^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Due to the assumed tiling, and Propositions
2.5
and
2.6
, we have
|A|\leq\omega(H)\leq D^{+}(H)\leq D^{-}(H)=\frac{M}{D^{+}(H^{\prime})}\leq\frac{M}{\omega(H^{\prime})}\leq\frac{M}{|B|}=|A|
. Therefore, equality must hold everywhere.

The same argument holds for
H^{\prime}
.
∎

There are two important points here: first,
D^{+}(H)=D^{-}(H)
is a non-trivial linear programming constraint on
H
and, second, the equalities
|A|=D^{+}(H)
,
|B|=D^{+}(H^{\prime})
determine the only possible sizes of
A
and
B
for a given
H
.

Notice that we have assumed
Div^{\ast}(A)\subset H
. If, instead, we require
Div^{\ast}(A)=H
, we obtain the following additional condition on
H
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
be given, and let
\delta=\frac{1}{M\phi(M)}
. If there exists a tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with
Div^{\ast}(A)=H
, then
|A|=\omega(H)=D^{\delta+}(H)=D^{+}(H)=D^{-}(H)
, and
|B|=\omega(H^{\prime})=D^{+}(H^{\prime})=D^{-}(H^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Due to the previous proposition, we only need to prove
|A|=D^{\delta+}(H)
.

As
Div^{\ast}(A)=H
, the function
h_{A}
defined in (
1.11
) is an admissible function in the class of
D^{\delta+}(H)
. As such,
|A|\leq D^{\delta+}(H)\leq D^{+}(H)=|A|
, where the last equality follows from the previous proposition.
∎

So far, we have not considered the Coven-Meyerowitz conditions (T1) and (T2), due to the fact that these conditions are associated with the cyclotomic factors of
A(X)
and
B(X)
, and not the divisor classes
Div^{\ast}(A),Div^{\ast}(B)
. We will now consider the Fourier transforms
\hat{h}_{A}
and
\hat{h}_{B}
, and make use of condition (T1).

Recall that for a divisor
d|M
we have
\Phi_{d}(X)|A(X)
if and only if
\hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(M/d)=0
, which happens if and only if
\hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}
vanishes on the class
R_{M/d}
.

Assume that
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
is a tiling. As explained in the introduction, for any
d|M
(
d\neq 1
) we have
\Phi_{d}(X)|A(X)
or
\Phi_{d}(X)|B(X)
, or possibly both.
However, when
d
is a prime-power, condition (T1) ensures that exactly one of the two divisibilities holds. That is, for any
p^{\alpha}|M
we have either

\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(X)|A(X)
or
\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(X)|B(X)
, but not both. Also, condition (T1) makes a connection between the cardinalities of
A
and
B
, and the cyclotomic divisors
\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(X)
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
be given. Let
S_{H}
denote the set of prime-powers
p^{\alpha}
such that
M/p^{\alpha}
is among the indices
m_{i}
. Let
k_{H}=\prod_{p^{\alpha}\in S_{H}}\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(1)
.

For any
H
and its standard complement
H^{\prime}
, and any prime power
p^{\alpha}|M
, the class
R_{M/p^{\alpha}}
appears either in
H
or in
H^{\prime}
(but not both). Also,
\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(1)=p
. Therefore we have

(2.1)

k_{H}k_{H^{\prime}}=M.

The question arises: how can we recognize if
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
, is the support of the Fourier transform of
\mathbf{1}_{A}
for a set
A
appearing in a tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
. We have the following important necessary conditions.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\delta=\frac{1}{M^{2}\phi(M)}>0
, and let
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
,
0\in H
be given. If there exists a tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
with
H={\emph{supp}}|\hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}|^{2}
, then
D^{\delta+}(H)=D^{+}(H)=D^{-}(H)=k_{H}=|B|
, and
D^{+}(H^{\prime})=D^{-}(H^{\prime})=k_{H^{\prime}}=|A|
.

Proof.

The proof exploits the fact that given any tiling
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, the functions
h_{A}
,
h_{B}
(as defined in (
1.11
)) and
\hat{h}_{A}
,
\hat{h}_{B}
are all extremal in their respective
D^{+}
-classes. We can make this claim rigorous as follows.

It is enough to prove

(2.2)

D^{\delta+}(H)=D^{+}(H)=D^{-}(H)=k_{H}=|B|,

because the second chain of equalities will be implied by the following duality relations (cf. Proposition
2.6
and equation (
2.1
)):

D^{+}(H^{\prime})=\frac{M}{D^{-}(H)},D^{-}(H^{\prime})=\frac{M}{D^{+}(H)},k_{H^{\prime}}=\frac{M}{k_{H}},|A|=\frac{M}{|B|},

and all quantities are equal to the right hand side by (
2.2
), so the left hand sides must also be equal.

To prove (
2.2
), notice first the following equivalent statements for any prime power
p^{\alpha}|M
:

p^{\alpha}\in S_{A}\Leftrightarrow\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(X)|A(X)\Leftrightarrow\hat{1}_{A}
vanishes on
R_{M/p^{\alpha}}

\Leftrightarrow R_{M/p^{\alpha}}\subset H^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow p^{\alpha}\in S_{H^{\prime}}
. The first equivalence
is just the definition of
S_{A}
, the second follows form (
1.7
), the third follows from the assumption that the support of
\hat{1}_{A}
is exactly
H
, while the last is just the definition of
S_{H^{\prime}}
. Hence, invoking the (T1) tiling condition of Coven-Meyerowitz in Theorem
1.2
, we obtain
|A|=\prod_{p^{\alpha}\in S_{A}}\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(1)=\prod_{p^{\alpha}\in S_{H^{\prime}}}\Phi_{p^{\alpha}}(1)=k_{H^{\prime}}
.
Furthermore, by
A\oplus B=\mathbb{Z}_{M}
we have
|A||B|=M
, and by equation (
2.1
) we have
k_{H}k_{H^{\prime}}=M
, which implies
|B|=k_{H}
.

Furthermore, let
h_{A}
be as in equation (
1.11
), and
u=\frac{1}{|A|}\hat{h}_{A}
. Then
{\hbox{supp}\,}u=H
by assumption,

u(0)=1
,
\hat{u}\geq 0
, and due to (
1.14
) we have
u(z)\geq\frac{1}{|A|^{2}\phi(M)}\geq\delta
for all
z\in H
. This shows that
u
is an admissible function in the class
D^{\delta+}(H)
. As
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}u(z)=\hat{u}(0)=\frac{M}{|A|}h_{A}(0)=|B|
, we conclude
|B|\leq D^{\delta+}(H)
.

Similarly, for
v=\frac{1}{|B|}\hat{h}_{B}
we have that
{\hbox{supp}\,}v\subset H^{\prime}
(by the tiling condition the supports must be essentially disjoint),
v(0)=1
,
\hat{v}\geq 0
,
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}}v(z)=|A|
. This shows that
|A|\leq D^{+}(H^{\prime})
.

Finally, putting everything together, we have
k_{H}=|B|\leq D^{\delta+}(H)\leq D^{+}(H)\leq D^{-}(H)=\frac{M}{D^{+}(H^{\prime})}\leq\frac{M}{|A|}=|B|
, and hence equality must hold everywhere.
∎

The chain of equalities
D^{\delta+}(H)=D^{+}(H)=D^{-}(H)=k_{H}
puts severe restrictions on
H
. While these restrictions fall short of proving the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture, they rule out the vast majority of sets
H=\cup_{i=1}^{r}R_{m_{i}}
as candidates for the support of the Fourier transform of a tile in
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
. As an indication, in Section
5
we will provide some statistics of a computer experiment carried out for a specific value of
M
.

3.
Cyclotomic divisibility for averaged functions

In connection to cyclotomic divisibility, the class of step functions enjoys another useful property. Strictly speaking, the results of this section are not needed to verify the forthcoming examples in Section
4
. However, we include them for two reasons. One is that Proposition
3.5
below gives a potentially useful tool in studying cyclotomic divisibility of step functions in the future. The second is that we have actually used these conditions in
producing
the examples presented in Section
4
.

In service of our discussion we borrow some notation from
[
7
]
for (classic)
M
-cuboids.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M=\prod_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}^{n_{i}}
, where
p_{i}
are distinct primes and
n_{i}\geq 1
are integers. Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

•

An
M
-cuboid is a weighted set corresponding to the mask polynomial of the form

(3.1)

\Delta(X)=X^{c}\prod_{i=1}^{K}(1-X^{d_{i}}),

where
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{M},d_{i}=\rho_{i}M/p_{i}
with
\rho_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,p_{i}-1\}
for all
i
.

•

The associated
\Delta
-evaluation of
f
is

(3.2)

F[\Delta]=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^{K}}(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\epsilon_{j}}f(x_{\epsilon}),

where
x_{\epsilon}=c+\sum_{j=1}^{K}\epsilon_{j}d_{j}
(mod
M
) are the vertices of
\Delta
.

There is a well-known cyclotomic divisibility test given in terms of cuboids, which was first observed by Steinberger
[
17
]
, and later reestablished in
[
4
,
7
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Phi_{M}(X)|F(X)
,

(ii)

For all
M
-cuboids
\Delta
, we have
F[\Delta]=0
.

Given a function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, cyclotomic divisibility of
f
by
\Phi_{N}
with
N|M
is determined by Proposition
3.2
with
M
and
f
replaced by
N
and
f_{N}
respectively, where
f_{N}
is the function defined on
\mathbb{Z}_{N}

(3.3)

f_{N}(z)=\sum_{N|z-y}f(y).

We will show that when
f\in\mathcal{A}(M)
is a step function, Proposition
3.2
considerably simplifies. We first need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Delta
be an
M
-cuboid and
f\in\mathcal{A}(M)
. Suppose that there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
and
\beta\leq n_{i}-1
such that for all vertices
x_{\epsilon}
of
\Delta
we have

(3.4)

(x_{\epsilon},p_{i}^{n_{i}})=p_{i}^{\beta}.

Then for any pair of vertices
x_{\epsilon},x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
of
\Delta
with

(3.5)

(x_{\epsilon}-x_{\epsilon^{\prime}},M)=M/p_{i},

we have

(3.6)

f(x_{\epsilon})=f(x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}).

Moreover, if(
3.4
) holds with
\beta\leq n_{i}-2
for one single vertex of
\Delta
, then it holds for all the vertices with the same exponent
\beta
, and hence (
3.6
) holds for any pair of vertices of
\Delta
satisfying (
3.5
).

Proof.

Let
m|M
, and suppose that
x_{\epsilon}\in R_{m}
. As
f
is a step function, it is enough to show that
x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\in R_{m}
.

The exponent of
p_{i}
in
m
is
\beta\leq n_{i}-1
by (
3.4
). If
\beta\leq n_{i}-2
, then by (
3.5
) we have
x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\equiv x_{\epsilon}-cp_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots p_{i}^{n_{i}-1}\dots p_{K}^{n_{K}}
(mod
M
), with some
1\leq c\leq p_{i}-1
. Hence we obtain
x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\in R_{m}
, because the exponents of all the primes in
x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
remain the same as in
x_{\epsilon}
.
Assume now that
\beta={n_{i}-1}
. In
x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
the exponents of primes different from
p_{i}
still remain the same as in
x_{\epsilon}
, and that of
p_{i}
either remains
n_{i}-1
or becomes
n_{i}
. However, the latter is excluded by assumption (
3.4
). Again, we conclude
x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}\in R_{m}
.

For the last sentence of the proposition, notice that the difference between any two vertices of an
M
-cuboid is divisible by
p_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\dots p_{K}^{n_{K}-1}
. Therefore, if the exponent of
p_{i}
in
x_{\epsilon}
is
\beta\leq n_{i}-2
, then it is the same for all the other the vertices.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\Delta
be an
M
-cuboid and
f\in\mathcal{A}(M)
. Suppose that there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, and
\beta\leq n_{i}-1
such that (
3.4
) holds for all vertices
x_{\epsilon}
of
\Delta
. Then
F[\Delta]=0
.

Proof.

Write
\Delta
as a disjoint union of its two facets
\Delta_{i}\cup\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
, with respect to
p_{i}
: explicitly, each facet is defined as the maximal subset of vertices such that the pairwise difference between any two elements is divisible by
p_{i}^{n_{i}}
. The vertices of the facets
\Delta_{i}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
appear with opposite signs in the evaluation (
3.2
). Also, for every element
v
of
\Delta_{i}
there exists a unique element
v^{\prime}
of
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
such that
(v-v^{\prime},M)=M/p_{i}
. By Lemma
3.3

f(v)=f(v^{\prime})
which implies
F[\Delta]=0
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the strengthening of Proposition
3.2
for step functions.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}(M)
. The following are equivalent:

(I)

\Phi_{M}(X)|F(X)
,

(II)

F[\Delta]=0
for all
M
-cuboids
\Delta
with a vertex at
0
,

(III)

F[\Delta]=0
for one
M
-cuboid
\Delta
with a vertex at
0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
(I) implies (II). Clearly (II) implies (III).

We prove (II) implies (I). By Proposition
3.2
we need to prove
F[\Delta]=0
for all
M
-cuboids
\Delta
. By Corollary
3.4
it is enough to consider
M
-cuboids which violate (
3.4
) for all
i
. Fix an index
i
. By the last part of Lemma
3.3
, the condition (
3.4
) can only be violated if at some vertices of the cuboid the exponent of
p_{i}
is equal to
n_{i}-1
, while at other vertices it is equal to
n_{i}
.

That is, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
there exists a vertex
x_{i}
of
\Delta
satisfying

(3.7)

p_{i}^{n_{i}}|x_{i}.

Now, for every index
i
, partition
\Delta
into two facets, with respect to
p_{i}
,
\Delta=\Delta_{i}\cup\Delta^{\prime}_{i}
such that for every
i=1,\ldots,K

x_{i}\in\Delta_{i}
. By definition
p_{i}^{n_{i}}|x-x_{i}
for all vertices
x
of
\Delta_{i}
, and hence by (
3.7
) we have

p_{i}^{n_{i}}|x.

Let
v=\cap_{i=1}^{K}\Delta_{i}
(the intersection is non-empty, as
K
facets of different orientation of
\Delta
always intersect at exactly one point – just like
K
axis-parallel hyperplanes intersect in one point in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
). Clearly,
p_{i}^{n_{i}}|v
for all
i
, and this is equivalent to
v=0
. By (II) we have
F[\Delta]=0
.

In order to prove (III) implies (II) we take two
M
-cuboids
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
so that
0
is a joint vertex of both. Let
v=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\epsilon_{j}d_{j}
be a vertex of
\Delta
and
v^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\epsilon_{j}d_{j}^{\prime}
the corresponding vertex of
\Delta^{\prime}
, with the same coefficients
\epsilon_{j}
. As such, the vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
have the same sign in the evaluation (
3.2
) corresponding to
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
, respectively. Also, it is clear that
(v,M)=(v^{\prime},M)
, and hence
v
and
v^{\prime}
fall into the same step class, implying
f(v)=f(v^{\prime})
. Therefore, the terms in the evaluation (
3.2
) for
\Delta
are exactly the same as those for
\Delta^{\prime}
, and hence we have
F[\Delta]=0
if and only if
F[\Delta^{\prime}]=0
.
∎

In practical terms, when
M=p^{n}q^{m}
,
p
and
q
are primes, and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, Proposition
3.5
implies that for any
f\in\mathcal{A}(M)
and any
N|M
divisible by
pq

(3.8)

\Phi_{N}(X)|F(X)\text{ if and only if }f_{N}(0)-f_{N}(N/p)-f_{N}(N/q)+f_{N}(N/pq)=0,

where
f_{N}
is defined in (
3.3
). When
N
is coprime to
p
or
q
, i.e. a prime power, the relation is as follows. Let
N=p^{\alpha},\alpha\geq 1
, then

(3.9)

\Phi_{N}(X)|F(X)\text{ if and only if }f_{N}(0)=f_{N}(p^{\alpha-1}).

4.
Functional pd-tilings violating the (T2) condition

In this final section we first present a minor positive result concerning Problem
1.1
, and then provide a family of functional pd-tilings which violate the (T2) condition of Coven-Meyerowitz for
M=p^{4}q^{2}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a prime-power,
M=p^{\alpha}
. In this case, any set
A
which pd-tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
, also tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{M}
properly.

Proof.

As the Coven-Meyerowitz conjecture is true for
M=p^{\alpha}
, it is enough to prove conditions (T1) and (T2) for
A
, and as (T2) is vacuous in this case, it is sufficient to prove (T1) only.

Let
\mathbf{1}_{A}\ast f=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{M}}
be a pd-tiling. By (
1.9
) the support of
\hat{\mathbf{1}}_{A}
and
\hat{f}
intersect only at zero. Let
H=\{0\}\cup_{k=1}^{r}R_{p^{j_{k}}}
denote the support of

On the other hand, we claim that
D^{+}(H^{\prime})=|A|

∎

Almost inner derivations of Lie superalgebras
Vera Serganova, and, Arkady Vaintrob

Abstract
An almost inner derivation of a Lie algebra L L is a derivation that coincides with an inner derivation on each one-dimensional subspace of L L . The almost inner derivations form a subalgebra aDer ⁡ ( L ) \aDer(L) of the Lie algebra Der ⁡ ( L ) \Der(L) of all derivations of L L , containing the inner derivations iDer ⁡ ( L ) \iDer(L) as an ideal. If L L is a simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra, then aDer ⁡ ( L ) = iDer ⁡ ( L ) \aDer(L)=\iDer(L) , since all derivations of L L are inner. In this paper, we introduce and study almost inner derivations of Lie superalgebras. Since simple Lie superalgebras may admit non-inner outer derivations, the existence of non-inner almost inner derivations becomes a nontrivial question. Nevertheless, we show that all almost inner derivations of finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over C \mathbb C are inner. We also give examples of naturally occurring non-inner almost inner derivations of some pseudo-reductive Lie superalgebras related to the Sato-Kimura classification of prehomogeneous vector spaces.



1
Introduction

Let
L
be a Lie algebra. A derivation
D
of
L
is called
almost inner
if it coincides with an inner derivation on each one-dimensional subspace of
L
, that is, for each
x\in L
, there exists
a\in L
such that
D(x)=[a,x]
.
If
G
is a Lie group with
\Lie(G)=L
, then such a derivation
D
generates a one-parameter subgroup of automorphisms of
G
that sends every element
g\in G
to its conjugate. Such automorphisms are called
almost inner
or
class-preserving
.
Ever since Burnside’s discovery
[
Bu
]
of the existence of non-inner class-preserving automorphisms, such automorphisms have attracted attention of finite group theorists (see
[
Ku
]
or
[
Ya
]
for a recent review).

Almost inner derivations first appeared as a tool for producing non-inner class-preserving automorphisms in the work of Gordon and Wilson
[
GW
]
on isospectral deformations of Riemannian manifolds. (Later and independently, they were introduced in
[
SSB
,
AS
]
under the name
pointwise inner derivations
.)
Gordon and Wilson proved that if
\phi:G\to G
is an almost inner automorphism of a solvable Lie group
G
with a right-invariant metric
g
, then for any cocompact discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
, the Riemannian manifolds
X=G/\Gamma
and
X^{\prime}=G/\phi(\Gamma)
are isospectral, that is, the Laplace operators on
\Omega^{p}(X)
and
\Omega^{p}(X^{\prime})
have the same spectrum for all
p\geq 0
. If
\phi
is inner, then
X
and
X^{\prime}
are also isometric, but otherwise they may not be. This observation led Gordon and Wilson to the discovery of the first known example of a continuous family of isospectral, pairwise non-isometric Riemannian manifolds.

Since then, almost inner automorphisms and derivations, along with related concepts, such as almost conjugate subgroups and homomorphisms, have appeared in various areas of algebra and its applications, especially where the interplay between local and global conjugacy plays a role (see e.g.
[
FHS
,
Go
,
KO
,
La
,
O
,
Wa
,
We
]
).

A systematic algebraic study of almost inner derivations of Lie algebras was initiated in
[
BDV
]
(see also
[
AS
]
). For finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras, all derivations are inner and therefore they have no nontrivial almost inner derivations. Thus it is not surprising that all known examples of almost inner derivations involve nilpotent or solvable Lie algebras (see
[
BDV2
,
Ve
,
DG
]
). On the other hand, many simple Lie superalgebras have outer derivations, which raises the question of whether they may have nontrivial almost inner derivations.

In this note, we show that, nevertheless, all almost inner derivations of finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over the field of complex numbers are inner.
However, we also found examples of non-inner almost inner derivations for some non-simple Lie superalgebras that are far from being solvable. In particular, such derivations exist for several families of
quasireductive
Lie superalgebras (i.e. superalgebras
L=L_{\mathbf{0}}\oplus L_{\mathbf{1}}
, where
L_{\mathbf{0}}
is a reductive Lie algebra and
L_{\mathbf{1}}
is a semisimple
L_{\mathbf{0}}
-module, see
[
Se
]
). Quite unexpectedly, these examples turned out to be related with prehomogeneous vector spaces
[
Vi
,
SK
]
.

Let us describe the contents of the paper in more detail. We begin in Section
2
with the definition and general properties of almost inner derivations for Lie algebras.
Also, we prove there that for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
L
over a field of characteristic zero, the almost inner derivations form an ideal in the Lie algebra
\Der(L)
of all derivations. This fact was previously an open conjecture
[
BDV
]
, verified only in some special cases such as nilpotent Lie algebras
[
KO
]
.

Next, we define almost inner derivations of Lie superalgebras as derivations that coincide with inner derivations on elementary subsuperalgebras. We also give several natural examples of Lie superalgebras with nontrivial almost inner derivations, both even and odd, and show how such examples can be constructed from certain prehomogeneous vector spaces for semisimple Lie algebras.

In Section
3
, we review the classification and structure of simple Lie superalgebras given by V. Kac
[
Ka
]
and provide a complete description of their superalgebras of derivations.
In Section
4
, we prove our main result (Theorem
4.1
) that all almost inner derivations of simple Lie superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
are inner.
In Section
5
, we study almost inner derivations of quasireductive Lie superalgebras.
Finally, in Section
6
, we present several additional examples and discuss directions for future work.

We thank Boris Kunyavskii, who first told us about almost inner derivations and raised the question of their existence for simple Lie superalgebras.

We work with vector spaces, linear maps, algebras, etc., defined over a base field
\mathbb{F}

A vector
superspace
is the same as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

.

\mathbb{F}
-linear maps

\Hom(V,W)=\Hom(V,W)_{\mathbf{0}}\oplus\Hom(V,W)_{\mathbf{1}}.

[f,g]=fg-(-1)^{|f|\cdot|g|}gf.

2.2
Almost inner derivations of Lie algebras

Recall that a derivation of a Lie algebra
L
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is a linear map

D:L\to L

satisfying the Leibniz identity

D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+[x,D(y)],\quad\text{for all }x,y\in L.

It is easy to check that the set
\Der(L)
of all derivations of
L
is a Lie subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\End{F}(L)
.

The Jacobi identity for
L
can be restated as the condition that for all
a\in L
, the map

\ad_{a}:L\to L,\quad ad_{a}(x)=[a,x]

is a derivation. The map

\ad:L\to\Der(L)

is a Lie algebra homomorphism whose kernel
is the center
Z(L)
of
L
.

Derivations of the type
\ad_{a}
are called
inner
, and it is easy to see that the set

\iDer(L)=\{\ad_{a}\!:\!a\in L\}\subset\Der(L)

of all inner derivations is an ideal in
\Der(L)
.

The quotient Lie algebra

\oDer(L)=\Der(L)/\iDer(L)

(2)

is called the algebra of
outer derivations
of
L
.
By definition, it coincides with the first cohomology of
L
with the coefficients in the adjoint module

\oDer(L)=H^{1}(L,L).

(3)

The well-known fact that, if
L
is a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra and
M
is an non-trivial irreducible finite-dimensional
L
-module, then

H^{i}(L,M)=0,\text{ for all }i\geq 0,

(4)

implies that all derivations of a semisimple Lie algebra are inner.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra.
A derivation
D\in\Der(L)
is called
almost inner
if
on every one-dimensional subspace of
L
, it coincides with an inner derivation.
In other words,
D
is almost inner if for every
x\in L
there exists
a\in L
such that

D(x)=[a,x],

(5)

or equivalently, if

D(x)\in[L,x]\text{ \ for all }x\in L.

(6)

We denote by

\aDer(L)\subset\Der(L)

the subspace of all almost inner derivations of
L
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The space
\aDer(L)
is a Lie subalgebra of
\Der(L)
.

Proof.

Let
D,D^{\prime}\in\aDer(L)
. Then, for
x\in L
, we have
D(x)=[a,x]

and
D^{\prime}(x)=[a^{\prime},x]
for some
a,a^{\prime}\in L
. Therefore

\begin{split}[D^{\prime},D](x)=&D^{\prime}(D(x))-D(D^{\prime}(x))=D^{\prime}([a,x])-D([a^{\prime},x])\\
=&[D^{\prime}(a),x]+[a,D^{\prime}(x)]-[D(a^{\prime}),x]-[a^{\prime},D(x)]\\
=&[D^{\prime}(a),x]+[a,[a^{\prime},x]]-[D(a^{\prime}),x]-[a^{\prime},[a,x])]\\
=&[D^{\prime}(a),x]-[D(a^{\prime}),x]+[[a,a^{\prime}],x]\\
=&[D^{\prime}(a)-D(a^{\prime})+[a,a^{\prime}],x].\end{split}

Thus
[D,D^{\prime}]\in\aDer(L)
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
L=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
, the Lie algebra of a simply connected Lie group
G
, then
\Der(L)
can be naturally identified with the Lie algebra of the Lie group
\Aut(G)
of automorphisms of
G
. Under this identification,
\iDer(L)
becomes the Lie algebra of the group of inner automorphism of
G
, and one can expects
\aDer(L)
to be the Lie algebra of the subgroup consisting of
almost inner
(also known as
class preserving
) automorphisms of
G
, i.e. automorphisms
\gamma\in\Aut(G)
such that for every
g\in G
we have
\gamma(g)=h^{-1}gh
for some
h\in G
or, equivalently, of the subgroup of automorphisms preserving each adjoint orbit in
L
. The only sticky point is to show that almost inner automorphisms is a Lie group.

In fact, almost inner derivations form an ideal in the Lie algebra of all derivations.
First, let us check that almost inner derivations are preserved by automorphisms.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
D\in\aDer(L)
be an almost inner derivation and let
f\in\Aut(L)
be an automorphism of
L
.
Then

f_{*}(D):=f\circ D\circ f^{-1}

is also an almost inner derivation.

Proof.

Indeed, let
x\in L
. Since
D\in\aDer(L)
, we have

D(f^{-1}(x))=[y,f^{-1}(x)]

for some
y\in L
. Therefore,

f_{*}(D)(x)=fDf^{-1}(x)=f(D(f^{-1}(x)))=f([y,f^{-1}(x)])=[f(y),x],

which shows that
f_{*}(D)\in\aDer(L)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
L
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
0
. Then the space
\aDer(L)
is an ideal in
\Der(L)
.

Proof.

Consider the group
\Aut(L)
of automorphisms of
L
. By Lemma
2.4
the subspace
\aDer(L)\subset\Der(L)
is stable under the action of
\Aut(L)
. On the other hand,
\Der(L)
is the Lie algebra of
\Aut(L)
, see for instance Corollary 13.2 in
[
H
]
. The subspace
\aDer(L)
is stable under the adjoint action of
\Der(L)
. Hence the statement.
∎

2.3
Almost inner derivations of Lie superalgebras

Definition 2.7
.

Let
L=L_{\mathbf{0}}\oplus L_{\mathbf{1}}
be a Lie superalgebra.
A homogeneous linear map
D:L\to L
is called a
derivation of degree

\delta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, if
|D|=\delta
and

D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+(-1)^{\delta|x|}[x,D(y)],

(7)

for all homogeneous
x\in L
.

An arbitrary linear map
D\in\End(L)
is called a derivation if its even and odd components
D_{\mathbf{0}}\in\End(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
and
D_{\mathbf{1}}\in\End(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
are
derivations of degree
0
and
1
, respectively.

The super Jacobi identity implies that for every
a\in L
, the map

\ad_{a}:L\to L,\ x\mapsto[a,x]

is a derivation. Such derivations are called
inner
. It is straightforward to verify that the space

\Der(L)
of derivations of
L
is a subalgebra of the Lie superalgebra
\End(L)
(with the bracket given by the commutator (
1
)) and that inner derivations
\iDer(L)
form an ideal in
\Der(L)
.

The notion of outer derivations (
2
) and their cohomological description (
3
) carry over to the super case without modifications. However, the vanishing (
4
) does not hold in general even for simple Lie superalgebras and, as it turns out, they may have non-trivial outer derivations.

Example 2.8

(
The Euler derivation)
.

Every
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra

L=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}

has a special derivation
\mathcal{E}
called the
Euler
(or
grading
) derivation, defined
by

\mathcal{E}(x)=ix,\text{ if }x\in L_{i}.

For the Lie superalgebra
L=\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, this derivation is inner,
\mathcal{E}=\ad_{H}
, where
H
is
the diagonal matrix

H=\mathrm{diag}(m+n,m+n-1,\ldots,1).

If
m\neq n
, then the restriction of the Euler derivation to the subsuperalgebra of traceless matrices

\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)=\{A\in\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)\!:\!\mathrm{strA=0}\}

is still inner, since in this case we can replace
H
by

\tilde{H}=H-\frac{\mathrm{str}H}{m-n}I_{m+n}\in\mathfrak{sl}(m|n),

and still get
\mathcal{E}=\ad_{\tilde{H}}
.

However, for the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)
, as well as for its simple quotient

\mathfrak{psl}(n|n)=\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)/\mathbb{F}I_{n|n},

the Euler derivation is no longer inner (see Section
3
) and so
\oDer(L)\neq 0
.

Let
W(n)
be the Lie superalgebra of derivations of the (supercommutative) Grassmann algebra

\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{F}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]

with
n
free odd generators (i.e. vector fields on the affine supermanifold
\mathbb{F}^{0|n})
.
The superalgebra
W(n)
is simple (for
n\geq 3
) with a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading induced by the grading of
\Lambda_{n}
. The corresponding Euler derivation is inner:
\mathcal{E}=\ad_{\mathsf{E}}
, where
\mathsf{E}
is the
Euler vector field

\mathsf{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}\in W(n).

However, the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to the simple subsuperalgebra

S(n)\subset W(n)

of divergence-free vector fields is no longer inner and so
\oDer(S(n))\neq 0
.

We now introduce almost inner derivations of Lie superalgebras.
Unlike derivations and inner derivations, the definition of almost inner derivations for Lie superalgebras requires considering only homogeneous elements.

The following notion allows to define almost inner derivations without explicitly involving elements.

Definition 2.9
.

An
elementary
Lie superalgebra is a Lie superalgebra
E
freely generated by one homogeneous element
x\in E
.

If
x
is even, then
E
is just a one-dimensional abelian Lie algebra
\mathbb{F}x=\langle x\rangle
, and if
x
is odd, then
E
has dimension
(1|1)
with
E_{\mathbf{1}}=\langle x\rangle
and
E_{\mathbf{0}}=\langle[x,x]\rangle
.
Every homogeneous element
y\in L
of a Lie superalgebra
L
defines a canonical homomorphism

\varphi_{y}:E\to L

sending the generator
x
to
y
.

.

Let

be a Lie superalgebra. A homogeneous derivation

2.4
Examples of non-inner almost inner derivations

Naturally occurring Lie algebras typically do not have almost inner derivations that are not inner. The smallest dimension of a Lie algebra with such a derivation over any field of characteristic
\neq 2
is
5
(see
[
BDV
,
Ve
]
) and the corresponding Lie algebras are nilpotent.

As we will show below, in the super case the situation is quite different. There are many examples of both even and odd non-inner almost inner derivations in Lie superalgebras that are far from being solvable.

Example 2.14

(
An even non-inner almost inner derivation)
.

Let
L=L_{\mathbf{0}}\oplus L_{\mathbf{1}}
be a Lie superalgebra where

L_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\ \text{ \ and \ }\ L_{\mathbf{1}}=V,\text{ the standard }\mathfrak{sl}(2)-\text{module},

and the bracket is given by

[(g,v),(g^{\prime},v^{\prime})]:=([g,g^{\prime}],gv^{\prime}-g^{\prime}v).

Then the map

D:L\to L,\text{ given by }D|_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}=0\text{ and }D|_{V}=\Id_{V},

is an even almost inner derivation which is not inner.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
D
is a derivation (for instance, because
L
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded superalgebra and
D
is its Euler derivation).

The derivation
D
is not inner, since
I\not\in L_{0}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)
, but it is almost inner because

D(x)=\begin{cases}0=[0,x],&\text{ \ if \ }x\in L_{\mathbf{0}}\\
x=[g,x],&\text{ \ if \ }x\in L_{\mathbf{1}}\text{ and }g\text{ is any element of }\mathfrak{sl}(2)\text{ fixing }x.\end{cases}

∎

Example 2.15

(
An odd non-inner almost inner derivation)
.

As in the previous example, let
V
be the standard
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-module and consider a Lie superalgebra
L
with

L_{\mathbf{0}}=V\text{ \ and \ }L_{\mathbf{1}}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\oplus V,

and the only nonzero component of the bracket being

[\,,]:L_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes L_{\mathbf{1}}\to L_{\mathbf{0}}=V,\quad(g,w)\otimes(g^{\prime},w^{\prime})\mapsto gv^{\prime}-g^{\prime}v.

Then the odd map
D:L\to L
, given by

D|_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}=0\text{ \ and \ }D|_{L_{\mathbf{1}}}=\mathrm{pr}_{2}:\mathfrak{sl}(2)\oplus V\to V=L_{0}

is an almost inner derivation which is not inner.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the previous example and is left to the reader.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

An essential role in the above two examples played a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a
transitive representation

V
(that is, a
\mathfrak{g}
-module with an element
x\in V
such that
\mathfrak{g}\cdot x=V
).
Such pairs

appear in the study of prehomogeneous vector spaces

,

]
.
In the next subsection we show how they can be used to produced a large family of almost inner derivations for Lie superalgebras.

2.5
Almost inner derivations and prehomogeneous vector spaces

Definition 2.17
.

Recall that a
prehomogeneous vector space

[
SK
]
is a vector space
V
with a linear action of an algebraic group
G
, such that
V
has an open
G
-orbit. This is equivalent to the requirement that the associated representation on
V
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)
of
G
is
transitive

[
Vi
]
, that is,

\mathfrak{g}\cdot v=V\text{ for some }v\in V.

We will call such pairs
(G,V)
and
(\mathfrak{g},V)

prehomogeneous spaces
.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and let
V
be an
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra
L
with the even part
L_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathfrak{g}
,
the odd part
L_{\mathbf{1}}=V
, and the bracket defined by

[x,y]=\begin{cases}\ [x,y],&\text{if }x,y\in L_{\mathbf{0}},\\
\ xy,&\text{if }x\in L_{\mathbf{0}},y\in L_{\mathbf{1}},\\
\ 0,&\text{if }x,y\in L_{\mathbf{1}}.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{E}\in\Der(L)
be the Euler derivation from Example
2.8
.

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a prehomogeneous space such that for every vector
w\in V
its orbit
Gw\subset V
is conical. Then
\mathcal{E}
is an almost inner derivation of
L
.

If, in addition,
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect, that is,
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, then
\mathcal{E}
is a non-inner almost inner derivation.

Proof.

Indeed, let
\mathcal{O}
be an open
G
-orbit in
V
. Then for every
w\in\mathcal{O}
the tangent space
T_{w}\mathcal{O}
coincides with the whole
V
and so we have

\mathfrak{g}w=T_{w}(\mathcal{O})=V.

Now, consider
v\in V-\mathcal{O}
. Since the orbit
Gv
is conical, the line
\mathbb{C}^{*}v
belongs to
Gv
and therefore we obtain that

v\in T_{v}(\mathbb{C}^{*}v)\subset T_{v}(Gv)=\mathfrak{g}v.

Thus
\mathcal{E}(x)=x\in\mathfrak{g}x
for all
x\in V

Theorem 3.1

(c)

The proof will be presented in the sequence of Lemmas

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a semisimple element which does not belong to the center of

Consider the one-parameter subgroup

in

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that a Levi subalgebra

in

.

\oDer(L)\subset{\End}_{{L_{0}}}(L/L_{0})/C(L^{\prime})

(i) is obvious.

(ii) The restriction of

Theorem 4.1

Proof.

As follows from Theorem

First we will deal with the outer derivations of the Euler type.

Lemma 4.2
.

The Euler derivation
\mathcal{E}

and

is not almost inner .

Proof.

All these algebras admit a compatible
\mathbb{Z}
-grading as in Lemma

a semisimple even part

In each case, there exists

such that the algebraic group

corresponding to

has no open orbit in

.
This implies the existence of a non-constant homogeneous

-invariant polynomial

Take

such that

. Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is almost inner. Then there exists
y\in L
such that
[y,x]=ix
. Since

is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, we may assume that

.
But then the
\mathbb{C}^{*}

\cal{O}_{x}=\{\exp(ty)\cdot x\!:\!t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}

Since

coincides with

and so it must contain

But

and we obtain a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

L=\bigoplus_{i=-1}^{n}L_{i}

be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra and let

be a derivation such that

is not almost inner .

Proof.

Assume that

such that

.
Then

for some

and

[L,L_{-1}]\cap L_{n}=0
, this gives a contradiction.
∎

The above lemmas cover all simple Lie superalgebras with nontrivial outer derivations except

and
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)

Let

with

and
D
be the odd derivation such that
D(L_{\mathbf{0}})=0
and
D:L_{\mathbf{1}}\to L_{\mathbf{0}}
is an isomorphism.
We claim that
D
is not almost inner .

Indeed, identify
L_{1}
with the set of traceless
n\times n
matrices and choose

x=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in L_{1}

to be a diagonal matrix with entries
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
satisfying the conditions

\sum\lambda_{i}=\sum\lambda_{i}^{-1}=0,\text{ \ and \ }\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}\neq 0,\text{ for all }i,j.

We will prove that there is no
y\in L_{\mathbf{1}}
such that
[y,x]=D(x)
. In the matrix form, such
y
should satisfy
the equation

xy+yx=x+aI_{n}\text{ \ for some \ }a\in\mathbb{C}.

Since all off-diagonal entries of
xy+yx
are zero and

\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}\neq 0
, we know that
y
is also diagonal. Moreover, the diagonal elements of
y
are

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\frac{a}{\lambda_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}
.
On the other hand,
\mathrm{tr}(y)=0
. But
\displaystyle\sum\mu_{i}=\frac{n}{2}
. We obtain a contradiction.

Finally, let us consider the case
L=\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
.
Note that
\Der(L)=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\opluslhrim L
.
Since almost inner derivations form an ideal in
\Der(L)
, it suffices to check
that
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
contains a derivation
D\notin\aDer(L)
. One can take the Euler derivation
D=\mathcal{E}
and use Lemma
4.2
.

∎

5
Almost inner derivations of quasireductive superalgebras

Recall that a superalgebra
L
is called
quasireductive
if
L_{0}
is a reductive Lie algebra and
L_{\mathbf{1}}
is a semisimple
L_{\mathbf{0}}
-module (see
[
Se
]
).
We assume that
\mathbb{F}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. By
G
we denote a connected reductive algebraic group with Lie algebra
L_{\mathbf{0}}
. Assume that
G
acts on
L
by adjoint representation.

Denote by
\Der^{\circ}(L)
the subalgebra of
\Der(L)
consisting of all
D
such that
D(L_{\mathbf{0}})=0
. For any subalgebra
K\subset\Der(L)
denote by
K^{\circ}
the intersection
\Der^{\circ}(L)\cap K
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
L
be quasireductive. Then

\aDer(L)/\iDer(L)
is a subalgebra in

\aDer^{\circ}(L)/\iDer^{\circ}(L)
.

Proof.

Let
D\in\aDer(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
. Then
D(z)=0
for any central element
z\in L_{\mathbf{0}}
. On the other,
[L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}]
is semisimple and therefore all
derivations of
[L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}]
are inner. Therefore there exists
y\in L_{\mathbf{0}}
such that
(D-\ad_{y})(L_{\mathbf{0}})=0
.

Now let
D\in\aDer(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
. Then
H^{1}([L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}],L_{\mathbf{1}})=0
and therefore we may choose
D
such that
D([L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}])=0
.
Thus,
D([x,y])=[x,D(y)]
for any
y\in L
and
x\in[L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}]
.
Therefore if
Z
is the center of
L_{\mathbf{0}}
then
D(Z)\subset L_{\mathbf{1}}^{[L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}]}
. On the other hand, we have
H^{1}(Z,M)=H^{1}(Z,M^{Z})
for any semisimple
Z
-module
M
. Thus,
there exists
u\in L_{\mathbf{1}}^{[L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{0}}]}
such that
(D-\ad_{u})(L_{\mathbf{0}})\subset L_{\mathbf{1}}^{L_{\mathbf{0}}}
. But then
(D-\ad_{u})(L_{\mathbf{0}})=0
since
D-\ad_{u}
is almost inner.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

If
L
is quasireductive, then
(\aDer(L)/\iDer(L))_{\mathbf{1}}
is an abelian ideal in
\aDer(L)/\iDer(L)
.

Proof.

Follows from above Lemma since
[\Der^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{1}},\Der^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{1}}]=0
.
∎

Example 5.3
.

We revisit the example of Proposition
2.18
. Let
L
be a quasireductive superalgebra such that
[L_{\mathbf{1}},L_{\mathbf{1}}]=0
and
L_{\mathbf{1}}
is a simple faithful
L_{\mathbf{0}}
-module. Then

\Der^{\circ}(L)=\End_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(L_{\mathbf{1}})\oplus\Hom_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(L_{\mathbf{1}},L_{\mathbf{0}})=\mathbb{F}\mathcal{E}\oplus\Hom_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(L_{\mathbf{1}},L_{\mathbf{0}}).

The second equality is a consequence of Schur’s lemma.

Note that
\aDer^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{1}}=0
since
[L_{\mathbf{1}},L_{\mathbf{1}}]=0
.
If
L_{\mathbf{0}}
has non-trivial center then
\mathcal{E}=\ad_{z}
for some central element
z
.
Assume now that
L_{\mathbf{0}}
is semisimple. Then
\mathcal{E}
is almost inner if and only if all
G
-orbits in
L_{\mathbf{1}}
are conical.
In this case
\aDer(L)/\iDer(L)=\mathbb{F}\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
L
is quasireductive then
\aDer^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
is an abelian Lie algebra which acts semisimply on
L
.

Proof.

Let
D\in\aDer^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
. For any simple
L_{\mathbf{0}}
-submodule
W\subset L_{\mathbf{1}}
we have
D(W)\subset W
and since
D
commutes with the action of
L_{\mathbf{0}}
,
it acts on
W
by a scalar by Schur’s lemma.
Consider the decomposition

L_{\mathbf{1}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{m_{i}},

(8)

where
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
are pairwise non-isomoprhic simple
L_{\mathbf{0}}
-modules. Then
D|_{V_{i}^{m_{i}}}=\lambda_{i}\operatorname{id}
for some
\lambda_{1},\dots\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{F}
. This implies the statement.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Note that if
D\in\aDer^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
acts by non-zero scalar on
V_{i}^{m_{i}}
then all orbits of
G
in
V_{i}^{m_{i}}

must be conical.

Proposition 5.6
.

If
L
is quasireductive then
\aDer^{\circ}(L)
and hence
\aDer(L)/\iDer(L)
is abelian.

Proof.

We will prove that
\aDer^{\circ}(L)
is abelian. Let
D_{0}\in\aDer^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
and
D_{1}\in\aDer^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
. Consider the decomposition (
8
).
Then
D_{1}(V_{i})\neq 0
if
V_{i}
is isomorphic to an irreducible submodule of
L_{\mathbf{0}}
. In other words, either
V_{i}
is trivial or is isomorphic to some simple ideal of
L_{\mathbf{0}}
. On the other hand, in this case not all
G
-orbits in
V_{i}
are conical and hence
D_{0}(V_{i})=0
by Remark
5.5
. Therefore
[D_{0},D_{1}]=0
.
∎

Conjecture 5.7
.

If
L
is quasireductive then any almost inner derivation which is not inner is even.

The proposition below confirms above conjecture for quasireductive superlagebras with trivial center.

Proposition 5.8
.

Assume that
L
is quasireductive superalgebra with trivial center. Then
\aDer(L)_{\mathbf{1}}=\iDer(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
.

Proof.

We will use Lemma 5.6 and Theorem 6.9 of
[
Se
]
. Denote by
C(L)
the sum of minimal ideals of
L
. Lemma 5.6 implies that

S(L)=M\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}T_{j},

(9)

where each
S_{i}
is a simple Lie superalgebra and
T_{j}=\mathfrak{k}_{j}\otimes\mathbb{F}[\theta_{j}]
with simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}_{j}
and odd
\theta_{j}
.
Theorem 6.9 implies that
L=R(L)\opluslhrim C(L)
where
R(L)
is a a quasireductive Lie superalgebra with abelian
R(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
.
Furthermore,
R(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
is a subalgebra in
\operatorname{Der}^{\circ}(C(L))_{\mathbf{1}}
. From Section 3 we know that
\operatorname{Der}^{\circ}(S_{i})_{\mathbf{1}}=0
unless

S_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{psq}(n)
for
n\geq 3
and

\operatorname{Der}^{\circ}(S_{i})_{\mathbf{1}}
is one-dimensional if
S_{i}\simeq\mathfrak{psq}(n)
. A simple computation shows that

\operatorname{Der}^{\circ}(T_{j})=\mathbb{F}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\theta_{j}}}.

If
D\in\Der^{\circ}(L)_{\mathbf{1}}
then
D
is a linear combination of
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\theta_{j}}}
for
j=1,\dots l
and

u_{i}\in\operatorname{Der}^{\circ}(S_{i})_{\mathbf{1}}
for
i
such that
S_{i}\simeq\mathfrak{psl}(n)
. Not that none of
\theta_{j}
or
u_{i}
is an almost
inner derivation of
C(L)
. Thus, if

D=\sum c_{j}\theta_{j}+\sum d_{i}u_{i}

is an almost inner derivation of
L
then
D\in R(L)
and thus
D
is inner.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Observe that in the decomposition (
9
) the almost inner derivations of
T_{j}
and
S_{i}
coincide with inner derivations. Thus,

\aDer(L)/\iDer(L)=\operatorname{aDer}(L^{\prime})/\operatorname{iDer}(L^{\prime})

where

L^{\prime}=L/\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}T_{j}\right).

On the other hand,
L^{\prime}=L^{\prime}_{\mathbf{0}}\oplus L_{\mathbf{1}}^{\prime}
with
[L_{\mathbf{1}}^{\prime},L^{\prime}_{\mathbf{1}}]=0
. In view of Example
5.3
and Remark
5.5
we can see
that classification of almost inner derivations of centerless quasireductive superalgebras can be reduced to classification of
representations of semisimple groups with conical orbits.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a connected quasireductive supergroup with Lie superalgebra
L
. Assume that
L
has trivial center.

\operatorname{Aut^{a}}{L}\subset\Aut(L)
be a supergoup with Lie superalgebra
\aDer(L)
. Then
\operatorname{Aut^{a}}L
is a maximal connected subgroup of
\Aut(L)
such that

\operatorname{Ext}^{\cdot}_{\operatorname{Aut^{a}}{L}}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^{\cdot}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})

is an isomorphism of algebras.

Proof.

Follows from the identity,
[
BKN
]

\operatorname{Ext}^{\cdot}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})=H^{\cdot}(L;L_{\mathbf{0}},\mathbb{F})=S^{\cdot}(L_{\mathbf{1}}^{*})^{G}.

∎

6
More examples and open questions

Note that Example
2.15
can be modified to obtain an example of non-solvable Lie algebra with almost inner but not inner derivation.
Let
L
denote the following subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(2n+k)
with
k<n
consisting of block matrices

X=\left(\begin{matrix}0&B&c\\
0&0&d\\
0&0&C\end{matrix}\right)

where
C
lies in some Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(k)
,
B
is an arbitrary traceless matrix, and
c,d
are arbitrary
n\times k
matrices. Then
\ad_{D}
for

D=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1_{n}&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)

gives an almost inner derivation of
L
which is not inner.

The group version of the above examples gives an example of an almost inner automorphism for groups.
If
\mathbb{F}
is a finite field this gives an example of almost inner but not inner automorphism of a finite group.

We know that for a finite group
G
and algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
0
the group
\operatorname{Aut^{a}}(G)
is exactly the set of automorphisms
\varphi
such that
V^{\varphi}\simeq V
for any
\mathbb{F}[G]
-module
V
.

Note that if

\mathcal{G}
is a quasireductive supergroup then we can have an automorphism
\varphi
which is not almost inner but still
V^{\varphi}\simeq V
for any
\mathcal{G}
-module
V
. Indeed, consider
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{R}^{0|1}
and an
automorphism
\varphi
acting by a scalar operator. It would be interesting to characterize almost inner automorphisms in terms of representations of
\mathcal{G}
.

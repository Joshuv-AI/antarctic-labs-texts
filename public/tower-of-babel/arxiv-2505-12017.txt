Abstract
. We consider the relativistic Landau equation in the spatially inhomogeneous, far-from-equilibrium regime. We establish regularity estimates of all orders, implying that solutions remain smooth for as long as some zeroth-order conditional bounds hold. We also prove that polynomial and exponential decay in the momentum variable is propagated forward in time. As part of our proof, we establish a Schauder estimate for linear relativistic kinetic equations, that may be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

We are concerned with the relativistic Landau equation, which is a nonlinear kinetic model from plasma physics. It was introduced by Budker and Beliaev in 1956
[
6
]
as a modification of the widely-studied (classical) Landau equation, which ignores relativistic effects. These relativistic effects become important when particle speeds approach the speed of light, which happens frequently in hot plasmas.

With units chosen so that the speed of light equals
1
, the relativistic Landau equation reads

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}f={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,f),

where
\langle p\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}
and the unknown function
f(t,x,p)\geq 0
represents the density of particles at time
t\geq 0
, location
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and with momentum
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The collision operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)
is an integral operator, acting only in the
p
variable, defined for
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(1.2)

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)=\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi(p,q)\left(f(q)\nabla_{p}g(p)-g(p)\nabla_{q}f(q)\right)\,\mathrm{d}q\right),

where the integral kernel
\Phi(p,q)
is a
3\times 3
matrix described by:

\begin{split}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)&=\Lambda(p,q)S^{ij}(p,q),\\
\Lambda(p,q)&=\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}[\tau(\tau-2)]^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}},\\
S^{ij}(p,q)&=\tau(\tau-2)\delta_{ij}-(p_{i}-q_{i})(p_{j}-q_{j})+(\tau-2)(p_{i}q_{j}+p_{j}q_{i}),\\
\tau&=\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q+1.\end{split}

Mathematically, equation (
1.1
) features the interaction of relativistic transport with nonlinear, nonlocal diffusion in the
p
variable. As with other collisional kinetic equations, controlling the collision term quantitatively is a key difficulty.
Compared to the kernel of the classical Landau collision operator,
\Phi(p,q)
is much more algebraically complex.
In particular,
\Phi
is not a function of
p-q
only, which means the integral in (
1.2
) is not a convolution.

Prior mathematical work on (
1.1
) has focused on either the spatially homogeneous case, where
f
is independent of the
x
variable
[
44
,
45
]
, or the close-to-equilibrium case, where
f
is sufficiently close to a relativistic Maxwellian (also called Jüttner solution)
\mu(t,x,p)=c_{1}e^{-c_{2}\langle p\rangle}
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0

[
43
,
47
,
35
]
. In this article, we initiate the study of (
1.1
) in the
spatially inhomogeneous, large-data
regime. By large-data, we mean that the solution and initial data are not assumed to be close to equilibrium.

A reasonable starting point for the large-data theory is to establish
a priori
regularity and decay estimates. These two properties are needed in any quantitative bound on the collision operator because
\Phi(p,q)
has a singularity at
p=q
and decays slowly as
|q|\to\infty
. In this article, we find that solutions are
C^{\infty}
in all three variables and satisfy rapid decay estimates as
|p|\to\infty
as soon as bounds on some zeroth-order quantities are satisfied (see
Theorem

1.2
). The smoothing part of our theorem provides a relativistic counterpart of the main result of
[
20
]
by the first two authors. (We also refer to
[
30
,
17
,
38
,
23
,
11
]
and the references therein for other regularity results on the classical, inhomogeneous Landau equation.) As in the classical case, the basic smoothing mechanism of (
1.1
) comes from the diffusive effect of the collision term. Since this term acts only in
p
, regularization in the
x
variable relies on the mixing generated by the interaction of
p
-diffusion and transport.

Apart from their own intrinsic interest, the regularity and decay estimates in this article are intended to lay the groundwork for a large-data existence theory of (
1.1
) and will also be helpful for the study of further qualitative properties, by giving easy-to-check conditions that allow one to work with very smooth, rapidly-decaying solutions.

Recall that (in our choice of units) the energy of a particle with momentum
p
is given by
\langle p\rangle
. We can therefore define the macroscopic quantities

\begin{split}M_{f}(t,x)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,p)\,\mathrm{d}p,\quad\text{(mass density)},\\
E_{f}(t,x)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle p\rangle f(t,x,p)\,\mathrm{d}p,\quad\text{(energy density)}.\end{split}

Another relevant macroscopic quantity, that however does not appear in our main results, is the absolute entropy density:

(1.3)

\overline{H}_{f}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,p)|\log f(t,x,p)|\,\mathrm{d}p.

Our main results state that solutions to (
1.1
) are
C^{\infty}
with rapid decay whenever three conditions on
f
hold: the mass density is uniformly bounded below, the energy density is uniformly bounded above, and the solution
f
is bounded.

1.1.
Main results

First, we present our decay estimates for large
p
. We are able to prove both polynomial and exponential decay estimates:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a classical solution of the relativistic Landau equation (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
for some
T>0
. Assume that there are
E_{0},F_{0}>0
such that

(1.4)

E_{f}(t,x)\leq E_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq F_{0},

for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Further, suppose that there are
k>0
and
M_{k}>0
such that the initial data
f_{\rm in}:\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies

(1.5)

\langle p\rangle^{k}f_{\rm in}(x,p)\leq M_{k}\quad\text{ for all }(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Then
f
satisfies the decay estimate

(1.6)

\langle p\rangle^{k}f(t,x,p)\leq e^{\beta t}M_{k}\quad\text{ for all }(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

for some
\beta>0
depending only on
k
,
E_{0}
, and
F_{0}
.

If, in addition, there are
\sigma,N>0
such that

(1.7)

e^{\sigma\langle p\rangle}f_{\rm in}(x,p)\leq N\quad\text{ for all }(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

then
f
satisfies the exponential decay estimate

(1.8)

e^{\sigma\langle p\rangle}f(t,x,p)\leq N\exp(CNe^{Ct}t)\quad\text{ for all }(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

for some
C>0
depending only on
\sigma
,
E_{0}
, and
F_{0}
.

Recall that the equilibrium solutions of (
1.1
) are the Jüttner solutions
\mu(t,x,p)=c_{1}e^{-c_{2}\langle p\rangle}
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
.
Theorem

1.1
implies in particular that if
f
starts below a Jüttner solution, it stays bounded (up to a time-dependent constant) by the same Jüttner solution for positive times, as long as the energy density and
L^{\infty}
-norm of
f
remain bounded.

Our next main result is an
a priori
regularity estimate for solutions. Let us note that this regularity theorem is substantially more difficult to prove than the decay estimate of
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
be a classical solution of the relativistic Landau equation (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
for some
T>0
. Assume that
f
satisfies the bounds

(1.9)

M_{f}(t,x)\geq m_{0},\quad E_{f}(t,x)\leq E_{0}\quad\text{ for all }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

for some
m_{0},E_{0}>0
, as well as

(1.10)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq F_{0},

for some
F_{0}>0
. Furthermore, assume that the initial data satisfies the upper bounds

(1.11)

\langle v\rangle^{k}f_{\rm in}(x,v)\leq M_{k},\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

for all
k>0
and some constants
M_{k}>0
.

Then
f\in C^{\infty}((0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and for any partial derivative
\partial^{\beta}
in
(t,x,p)
variables, any
k>0
, and any
\tau\in(0,T]
, the estimate

(1.12)

\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}([\tau,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq K_{\beta,k}

holds, for some
K_{\beta,k}
depending only on
\beta
,
k
,
\tau
,
m_{0}
,
E_{0}
,
F_{0}
, and
M_{\ell}
for some
\ell>k
.

Let us remark that
C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}
is the Hölder norm with respect to a Lorentz-invariant distance. This notation is explained in detail in
Section

5
. At least locally, the
C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}
norm controls the standard Hölder norm on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, so
Theorem

1.2
implies all partial derivatives of
f
are Hölder continous in the usual sense.

Let us stress that, if
f
decays at some finite, but sufficiently large polynomial rate as
|p|\to\infty
, our proof of
Theorem

1.2
shows that regularity estimates up to a certain order will hold. The higher to polynomial rate of decay, the smoother
f
is. See
Theorem

8.1
below for a precise statement of this fact.

1.2.
Comparison with classical Landau equation

Both of our main results are in a similar spirit to prior results for classical Landau. Let us recall the form of the classical equation, in which the solution
f(t,x,v)
satisfies

(1.13)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=Q_{\rm CL}(f,f),

where

(1.14)

Q_{\rm CL}(f,f)=\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w|^{\gamma+2}(I-\hat{w}\otimes\hat{w})[f(v-w)\nabla_{v}f(v)-f(v)\nabla_{v}f(v-w)]\,\mathrm{d}w\right),

and
\hat{w}=\nicefrac{{w}}{{|w|}}
. The parameter
\gamma
is generally taken in the range
[-3,1]
, and the case
\gamma=-3
(Coulomb potentials) is considered the most important due to its relevance as a model in plasma physics. In comparing the results in the current article to the state of the art for (
1.13
), we should note that our equation (
1.1
) is the relativistic analogue of the Coulomb case specifically.

The smoothing estimate of
Theorem

1.2
is the relativistic analogue of the main result of
[
20
]
. The smoothing estimates in
[
20
]
apply in the case
\gamma\in[-3,0)
and depend quantitatively on upper bounds for the mass, energy, and entropy densities of
f
and a lower bound for the mass density of
f
. When
\gamma\leq-2
, they additionally depend on an upper bound for
f
in
L^{\infty}_{t,x}L^{p}_{v}
for some
p=p(\gamma)
depending on
\gamma
, with
p(-3)=\infty
. Therefore, the dependence on
E_{0}
and
F_{0}
in our
Theorem

1.2
is in alignment with the condition in
[
20
]
in the Coulomb case. (Note that the energy density and
L^{\infty}
norm control the mass and entropy densities from above.)
We should note, however, that the
proofs
of our regularity estimates are substantially different than in the classical case, as we explain below in
Section

1.5
.

Decay estimates for large
v
are available for (
1.13
) in, e.g.,
[
9
,
40
,
22
]
. It is known that when
\gamma>0
, Gaussian decay estimates in
v
appear spontaneously regardless of the initial data, and when
\gamma\in[-3,0]
, polynomial and/or Gaussian decay estimates that hold at time zero are propagated forward in time, but do not spontaneously appear. Therefore,
Theorem

1.1
is analogous to what is known for the Coulomb case of (
1.13
). (Note that the equilibrium solutions in the classical case are Gaussians in
v
, whereas in the relativistic case, equilibrium states take the form
e^{-\theta\langle p\rangle}
for any
\theta>0
.)

1.3.
Relativistic Schauder estimate

To prove higher regularity of solutions, one of our tools is a Schauder estimate for linear relativistic kinetic equations with general nondivergence-form right-hand side:

(1.15)

\partial_{t}u+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\mbox{tr}(AD_{p}^{2}u)+B\cdot\nabla_{p}u+s,

where
A
,
B
,
s
are Hölder continuous functions, and
A
is uniformly elliptic. Our estimate says that if
u
is locally bounded and
A,B,s
are locally Hölder continuous, then
u
lies in a second-order space
C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}
that is defined in terms of our Lorentzian Hölder norm. The precise statement can be found in
Theorem

6.5
.

This Schauder estimate we prove in
Theorem

6.5
may be of independent interest.
There is a rich literature on Schauder estimates for kinetic equations, and more broadly hypoelliptic/ultraparabolic equations: see
[
36
,
13
,
8
,
25
,
14
,
33
,
32
,
23
,
7
]

and the references therein. These works are applicable to classical kinetic equations involving the transport derivative
\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}
. To the best of our knowledge,
Theorem

6.5
is the first Schauder estimate that applies to equations with the relativistic transport derivative
\partial_{t}+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}
.

Apart from relativistic Landau,
Theorem

6.5
can also be used to establish higher regularity for the following
relativistic Fokker-Planck equation
:

(1.16)

\partial_{t}u+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\nabla_{p}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}\left(I+p\otimes p\right)\nabla_{p}u+\beta pu\right),

where
\beta
is a friction coefficient, which is taken to be zero in some works.
This equation has been studied by Alcántara and Calogero
[
1
,
2
]
, who showed that it is invariant under Lorentz transformations (if
\beta=0
), studied the trend to equilibrium, and characterized the Newtonian limit. Later, Anceschi-Polidoro-Rebucci
[
4
]
proved a Harnack inequality and quantitative lower bounds. For a version of the equation with a confining potential, a hypoelliptic smoothing estimate in a weighted first order space was recently established by Arnold-Toshpulatov
[
5
]
, along with a trend-to-equilibrium result. Using our
Theorem

6.5
and differentiating the equation repeatedly, one can show that solutions to (
1.16
) are in fact infinitely differentiable.

1.4.
Related work

Much of the mathematical work on equation (
1.1
) has dealt with the near-equilibrium case. In some sense, this began with the work of Lemou
[
27
]
, who studied the linearization around the Jüttner equilibrium solution and established a spectral gap property. The study of the linearized operator was later extended by Luo and Yu
[
34
]
. Yang and Yu
[
47
]

established hypocoercivity and optimal convergence rates to equilibrium in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}
, and Lyu-Sun-Wu
[
35
]
established finite speed of propagation in the near-equilibrium framework.

Still in the near-equilibrium case, there have been a number of works on systems that couple equation (
1.1
) to an equation for the electro-magnetic field. Strain and Guo
[
43
]
established global existence near equilibrium, and stability of equilibrium states, for the relativistic Landau-Maxwell system with periodic boundary conditions in space. Yang and Yu
[
48
]
later extended this result to solutions defined in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}
, and Liu-Zhao
[
31
]
established the optimal decay rate to equilibrium for solutions in the whole space. Li-Yu-Zhong
[
28
]
studied the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson-Landau system, characterizing the spectral structure of the linearization and constructing global solutions with optimal decay rate toward equilibrium.

In
[
39
]
, Ouyang, Wu, and Xiao established the convergence of solutions to relativistic Landau toward solutions of relativistic Euler in the hydrodynamic scaling. Unlike the works cited in the previous two paragraphs, the solutions in
[
39
]
are close to a
local
(i.e.
t
and
x
dependent) relativistic Maxwellian.

Equation (
1.1
) has also been studied in the space-homogeneous case. The fourth named author of the present work, jointly with Strain
[
44
]
, studied entropy dissipation estimates, and applied these estimates to derive short-time existence of weak solutions and propagation of polynomial decay. Next, Strain-Wang
[
45
]
studied uniqueness of the homogeneous equation, using a probabilistic representation of the solution.

We are not aware of any prior results on the relativistic Landau equation that deal with the spatially inhomogeneous, large-data setting. However, we should mention the result of Zhu
[
49
]
, who proved local boundedness and
C^{\alpha}
estimates for linear Fokker-Planck equations with general transport terms and rough coefficients, which are applicable to (
1.1
) under suitable conditions on
f
.

There is a much broader literature on the classical Landau equation (
1.13
), which is too large to summarize here. Focusing only on the large-data, spatially inhomogeneous regime, we refer to
[
17
]
for local
C^{\alpha}
estimates,
[
9
]
for conditional global upper bounds,
[
20
,
40
]
for global
C^{\infty}
estimates,
[
21
,
41
]
for continuation criteria,
[
19
,
21
,
22
,
12
,
42
]
for short-time existence of classical solutions,
[
46
]
for global weak solutions, and
[
3
]
for a rigorous proof of the grazing collisions limit.

1.5.
Difficulties and proof ideas

1.5.1.
Decay

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is based on the fact that inverse-polynomial functions
\phi(t,p)=e^{\beta t}\langle p\rangle^{-k}
and exponential functions
\psi(p)=e^{\beta t}e^{-\sigma\langle p\rangle}
are both supersolutions to the linear relativistic Landau equation

(1.17)

\partial_{t}g+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}g={\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g),

where
f
is a fixed nonnegative function satisfying the conditional bounds
E_{f}(t,x)\leq E_{0}
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq F_{0}
. This supersolution property follows quickly, once one has access to precise bounds for
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)
, some of which we borrow from
[
44
]
and some we derive in
Section

3
. Then, if one chooses
f
to be a solution to relativistic Landau, the functions
\phi-f
and
\psi-f
are supersolutions as well, which yields the desired upper bounds.

Similar barrier methods have been applied to prove pointwise decay for other kinetic equations: see e.g.
[
26
,
9
,
40
,
10
,
24
]
. We emphasize that the rather strong conclusion of
Theorem

1.1
follows quickly using this flexible method.

1.5.2.
Regularity

By contrast, proving higher regularity of solutions is an involved process that requires taking into account certain geometric aspects of special relativity, which affect the argument in a severe way due to the nonlocality of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}
in the momentum variable.

First, let us note that equation (
1.1
) can be written in the form of the linear equation (
1.15
), with
A=a^{f}
,
B=b^{f}
, and
s=c^{f}f
for certain coefficients defined in terms of
f
.
(For the precise formulas for
a^{f}
,
b^{f}
, and
c^{f}
, see
Section

2.1
below.) This suggests a strategy based on applying local regularity estimates, passing regularity from
f
to the coefficients, differentiating the equation, and repeating.

Let us discuss the steps of this strategy in more detail, along with the difficulties that arise:

•

Relativistic Schauder estimates.
The Schauder-type estimate described in
Section

1.3
is our key tool in proving higher regularity. To establish this estimate, one might try to adapt some of the proof strategies in the literature on non-relativistic kinetic equations. However, most of these strategies would be complicated by the fact that (
1.15
) has worse scaling properties than the corresponding Newtonian kinetic equation

(1.18)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mbox{tr}(AD_{v}^{2}f)+B\cdot\nabla_{v}f+s.

In particular,
f(r^{2}t,r^{3}x,rv)
solves an equation of the same type as (
1.18
), with the same ellipticity constants, for any
r>0
. There is no analogue of this fact for (
1.15
), due to the presence of the relativistic transport term, and this would likely be an obstacle in many approaches to proving Schauder estimates directly.

Instead, we prove our Schauder estimate (
Theorem

6.5
) by applying a change of variables
p\mapsto v=p/\langle p\rangle
that sends momentum to velocity, which transforms (
1.15
) into a non-relativistic kinetic equation, with modified coefficients (see
Lemma

6.3
for the exact form of this new equation). We then apply known Schauder estimates to this equation, and transform back. A new difficulty arises here: the change of variables produces an equation whose ellipticity constants degenerate as
|p|\to\infty
(equivalently,
|v|\to 1
). The degenerating ellipticity constants make it difficult to track the dependence of the constants in our local Schauder estimates as the center of the local cylinder approaches
\infty
in momentum. Tracking this dependence is essential because the regularity estimates must be fed into the nonlocal coefficients, i.e. the regularity of
a^{f}
,
b^{f}
, and
c^{f}
at some
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
depends on the regularity of
f
in the entire
p
-domain.

To get around this difficulty, we prove our local Schauder estimate in a cylinder centered at 0 and apply “Lorentz boosts” (see the next bullet point) to translate this estimate to a cylinder centered at any other point
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
. The specific form of the Lorentz boost is needed because it preserves the relativistic transport term on the left side of (
1.15
). Unfortunately, the right side of (
1.15
) is not conserved, and the ellipticity of the diffusion term is changed in an anisotropic way that we must carefully track.

•

Translation via Lorentz boosts.
In order to translate between cylinders centered at different points, we need to apply Lorentz boosts, which are the relativistic analogues of Galilean frame shifts. They are defined as follows: for
z_{0},z\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, written as
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
and
z=(t,x,p)
, the boost that re-centers around
z_{0}
is

(1.19)

z\mapsto z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z=(t_{0}+t\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\cdot x,\,x_{0}+x_{\perp}+x_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}t,\,p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle),

where
x_{\parallel}
and
x_{\perp}
are the projections of
x
onto
p_{0}
and
p_{0}^{\perp}
respectively. We derive this formula in
Section

5
as a result of the well-known boost matrices from special relativity. (A version of the same derivation was performed for the case of one space dimension in
[
4
]
.)

It should be noted that the relativistic Landau equation (
1.1
) is invariant under Lorentz transformations. (See, for example,
[
29
, Section 50]
.) This implies that if
f
solves (
1.1
), then
f(z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z)
also solves (
1.1
). We could have simplified our proof using this fact, but we wanted an estimate that could be applied to any linear relativistic equation of the form (
1.15
), whether Lorentz-invariant or not. In particular, the linear equation (
1.16
) is not Lorentz-invariant in the case
\beta\neq 0
.

Motivated by the helpful properties of Lorentz boosts, we develop Hölder spaces with respect to a distance
d_{\mathcal{L}}
derived from the formula (
1.19
). (See
Section

5
for the precise definitions.) The benefit of these spaces is their invariance with respect to Lorentz boosts, which follows from the fact that

(1.20)

d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1},z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})=d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{1},z_{2}).

This property, which we prove in
Appendix

A
, is more subtle than expected, since the boost
\circ_{\mathcal{L}}
is not associative, and therefore is not a true Lie product. This is related to the fact that the composition of two Lorentz boosts is not a Lorentz boost unless the corresponding velocities are parallel. However, the composition is still a Lorentz
transformation
, and can be written as a composition of a boost with a rotation in space, which we exploit to prove (
1.20
). In
Section

5
, we derive a number of useful properties of these Lorentzian Hölder spaces, which we believe may be useful for future work on relativistic kinetic equations.

•

Bootstrapping.

After each application of a regularity estimate, one must pass regularity of
f
to regularity of the coefficients
a^{f}
,
b^{f}
, and
c^{f}
. This requires us to prove delicate functional inequalities in Hölder spaces for the coefficients and their derivatives. (See
Section

7
.)

Unlike in the classical Landau equation, the integral defining the collision operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)
in (
1.2
) is not a convolution (i.e.
\Phi(p,q)
is not a function of the difference
p-q
only). This causes another difficulty: when differentiating the equation in order to bootstrap regularity estimates, it is unavoidable that
p
derivatives fall on the kernel
\Phi(p,q)
. In particular,
\partial_{p_{i}}a^{f}\neq a^{\partial_{p_{i}}f}
. Since higher derivatives falling on
\Phi(p,q)
can produce non-integrable singularities, we must use an integration-by-parts technique involving a mixed differential operator that is suited to the relativistic structure of the kernel
\Phi(p,q)
. This technique is borrowed from the work of Strain-Guo
[
43
]
.

1.6.
Open problems

There is a lot of room for further study of (
1.1
) in the large-data regime. Here, we focus on the (in our opinion) most important directions for the near future.

1.6.1.
Filling of vacuum

The classical Landau equation is known to fill vacuum regions (i.e. regions in the spatial domain where the initial data is identically zero) instantaneously
[
21
]
. One should not expect this property to hold in the relativistic case, since particles cannot travel faster than the speed of light. The finite speed of propagation gives rise to a light cone phenomenon, which has already been observed in the near-equilibrium regime
[
35
]
. In the large-data setting, one would like to prove that solutions are strictly positive inside the forward light cone of the positivity set of the initial data
f_{0}
, with continuity estimates across the boundary of the light cone. We plan to explore this question in a forthcoming article.

1.6.2.
Weakening the conditional assumptions in
Theorem

1.2

As mentioned above, the conditional regularity result of
[
20
]
established higher regularity estimates for classical Landau, in the Coulomb case, depending on bounds for the mass and energy densities, as well as an upper bound on the
L^{\infty}
norm of
f
. This
L^{\infty}
-bound was later improved to a bound in
L^{\infty}_{t,x}L^{\delta+\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}_{v}
for any small
\delta>0
in
[
41
]
. One might expect that a similar result would be available in the relativistic case, involving a bound on
f
in a norm that is weaker than
L^{\infty}
in the
p
variable.

1.6.3.
Short-time existence

There are no existence theorems for classical solutions of (
1.1
) in the spatially-inhomogeneous, large-data regime. Therefore, it is natural to extend local existence results for classical Landau such as
[
19
,
21
,
22
]
to the relativistic case.

We should note that global-in-time existence for (
1.1
) with large initial data is most likely out of reach with current techniques, and that the obstructions appear to be similar to those seen in the case of classical Landau.

Preliminaries and known results

2.1.
Form of the collision operator

For
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
, the bilinear relativistic Landau-Coulomb collision operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)
is defined by (
1.2
), where the matrix
\Phi(p,q)
is given by (
1
). It is often convenient to write
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)
as a diffusion operator in
p
, either in nondivergence form

(2.1)

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)=\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}g)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}g+c^{f}g,

or divergence form

(2.2)

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rm RL}(f,g)=\nabla_{p}\cdot(a^{f}\nabla_{p}g)+B^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}g+c^{f}g,

where, for any
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{f}(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q,

(2.4)

\displaystyle b_{i}^{f}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{p_{i}}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)\partial_{q_{i}}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\right),\quad\text{ and}

(2.5)

\displaystyle c^{f}(p)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\partial_{p_{i}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)\partial_{q_{j}}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\right).

These formulas follow by a formal differentiation and the fact that
\Phi^{ij}=\Phi^{ji}
. After a non-obvious calculation involving integration by parts and the symmetry properties of
\Phi^{ij}
, as demonstrated in
[
43
,
44
]
, one obtains the formulas

(2.6)

\displaystyle b_{i}^{f}(p)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda(p,q)(\tau-2)(p_{i}+q_{i})f(q)\,\mathrm{d}q,

(2.7)

\displaystyle B_{i}^{f}(p)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda(p,q)[(\tau-1)p_{i}-q_{i}]f(q)\,\mathrm{d}q,\quad\text{ and}

(2.8)

\displaystyle c^{f}(p)

\displaystyle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\frac{\tau-1}{\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}f(q)\,\mathrm{d}q+\kappa(p)f(p),

where

(2.9)

\kappa(p)=2^{\nicefrac{{7}}{{2}}}\pi\langle p\rangle\int_{0}^{\pi}(1+|p|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

We use these formulas for the coefficients throughout our paper.

Next, let us discuss estimates that are known for the collision operator. First, we quote the uniform ellipticity estimates that were shown in
[
44
]
as a by-product of the proof of entropy dissipation. This is important when we apply regularity estimates in the sequel. We should note that the following proposition is a key difference between the relativistic and classical cases; the diffusion matrix
\bar{a}^{f}(t,x,v)
in classical Landau satisfies upper and lower ellipticity estimates that degenerate in an anisotropic way as
|v|\to\infty
. (See, e.g.
[
9
]
.)

Proposition 2.1
.

[
44
, Lemmas 13 and 14]

(a)

Let
a^{f}
be defined by (
2.3
), for a function
f\in L^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
s>2
. Then

(2.10)

a^{f}_{ij}(p)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq C|\xi|^{2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

for a constant
C>0
depending on
s
,
\|f\|_{L^{1}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, and
\|f\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.

(b)

If one only has
f\in L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then

(2.11)

a^{f}_{ij}(p)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq C\langle p\rangle|\xi|^{2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

for a constant
C>0
depending on
\|f\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
and
\|f\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.

(c)

For
f\geq 0
satisfying
0<m_{0}\leq M_{f}
,
E_{f}\leq E_{0}
, and
\overline{H}_{f}\leq H_{0}
, and
a^{f}
defined by (
2.3
), there holds

(2.12)

a^{f}_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq c|\xi|^{2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

for a constant
c>0
depending on
m_{0}
,
E_{0}
, and
H_{0}
.

We also have a useful upper bound for the integral kernel matrix
\Phi^{ij}
:

Lemma 2.2
.

[
44
, Lemma 12]

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2.13)

|\Phi^{ij}(p,q)|\leq C\begin{cases}\dfrac{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{|p-q|},&\text{if }\tau-2<1/8,\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{\langle p\rangle}{\langle q\rangle}+\dfrac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle},&\text{if }\tau-2\geq 1/8.\end{cases}

Let us also quote two useful facts about expressions that appear as part of
\Phi(p,q)
. Recalling the definition
\tau=\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q+1
, we have the following estimate from
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

For
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2.14)

\frac{|p-q|^{2}+|p\times q|^{2}}{2\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\leq\tau-2\leq\frac{1}{2}|p-q|^{2}.

It is also useful to know that

(2.15)

\tau\geq 2,

which follows from
Lemma

2.3
.

2.2.
Hölder estimates for linear kinetic equations

The first step in establishing regularity of our solutions is to apply local
L^{\infty}
and
C^{\alpha}
estimates of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser type for linear relativistic Fokker-Planck equations of the form

(2.16)

\partial_{t}f+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\nabla_{p}\cdot(A\nabla_{p}f)+B\cdot\nabla_{p}f+s,

defined for
(t,x,p)\in Q_{1}=(-1,0]\times B_{1}\times B_{1}
. One assumes
A
,
B
, and
s
are bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, and that
A
satisfies the ellipticity assumption

(2.17)

\lambda\Id\leq A(t,x,p)\leq\Lambda\Id

for all
(t,x,p)\in Q_{1}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, a Hölder estimate for this equation has been derived by Zhu
[
49
]
, but, when trying to apply the estimate on a cylinder far from the origin by composing with a Lorentz boost, one obtains an equation with ellipticity constants depending on
p_{0}
. Since this Hölder estimate needs to be plugged into the collision operator to obtain regularity for the nonlocal coefficients, it is necessary to quantify precisely the asymptotics as
|p_{0}|\to\infty
. This quantification is not straightforward, partly due to the lack of scaling symmetries in the equation (
2.16
). Instead, we use a change of variables to exchange momentum with velocity, apply a
C^{\alpha}
-estimate, and change variables back. (This is the same approach we take when applying Schauder estimates.) Therefore, the
C^{\alpha}
-estimate we need is the following, from the work of Golse-Imbert-Mouhot-Vassur
[
17
]
:

Proposition 2.4
.

[
17
, Theorem 1.4]

Let
f(t,x,v)
be a solution of

(2.18)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\nabla_{v}\cdot(A\nabla_{v}f)+B\cdot\nabla_{v}f+s,

where
A,B,s
are bounded and measurable and
A
is uniformly elliptic in the sense that
\lambda\Id\leq A(t,x,v)\leq\Lambda\Id
for all
(t,x,v)\in Q_{1}
. Then

(2.19)

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|s\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>0
depending on
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and the
L^{\infty}
norm of
B
.

The Hölder norm
C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}
is defined below in
Section

5.5
.

3.
Pointwise estimates for the coefficients

In this section, we obtain various pointwise bounds on the coefficients that are required to apply the regularity estimates that we derive in the sequel.
First, we have a simple upper bound for the integral kernel appearing in
c^{f}(z)
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G(p,q)
be defined by

(3.1)

G(p,q):=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\frac{\tau-1}{\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}},\quad p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Then for any
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the upper bound

(3.2)

G(p,q)\leq\frac{C}{|p-q|}

holds, where
C>0
is a constant independent of
p
and
q
.

Proof.

From
Lemma

2.3
and
\tau^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\leq(\tau-1)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
, we have

(3.3)

\frac{\tau-1}{\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\sqrt{\tau-1}}{|p-q|}.

Using this inequality, we estimate
G(p,q)
as follows:

(3.4)

G(p,q)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}}\frac{\sqrt{\tau-1}}{|p-q|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}}\frac{\sqrt{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q}}{|p-q|}.

Since
|p\cdot q|\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle
, we have

(3.5)

G(p,q)\lesssim\frac{1}{|p-q|},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
c^{g}
defined by (
2.8
), there holds

(3.6)

|c^{g}(p)|\leq C\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right),\quad p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

for a constant
C>0
independent of
g
.

Proof.

First, we estimate
\kappa(p)
as defined in (
2.9
). If
|p|\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\kappa(p)&\lesssim\langle p\rangle|p|^{-3}\int_{0}^{\pi}(|p|^{-2}+\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-2}\left(\int_{0}^{|p|^{-1}}+\int_{|p|^{-1}}^{\pi-|p|^{-1}}+\int_{\pi-|p|^{-1}}^{\pi}\right)(|p|^{-2}+\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-2}\left(\int_{0}^{|p|^{-1}}|p|^{3}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{|p|^{-1}}^{\pi-|p|^{-1}}\sin^{-2}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\pi-|p|^{-1}}^{\pi}|p|^{3}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\\
&\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-2}\left(|p|-\cot(\pi-|p|^{-1})+\cot(|p|^{-1})\right)\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-1},\end{split}

where
\cot z=\cos z/\sin z
. If
|p|<1
, then
\kappa(p)\lesssim\langle p\rangle\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\theta\lesssim 1\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-1}
. We conclude

(3.7)

\kappa(p)g(p)\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Next, from
Lemma

3.1
and a standard convolution estimate, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|p-q|}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q&\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{1}(p)}\frac{1}{|p-q|}\,\mathrm{d}q+\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{1}(p))}\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{1}(p)}\frac{1}{|p-q|}\\
&\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

which establishes the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
g\ inL^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
g
, such that the following hold.

(a)

For all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(3.8)

|b^{g}(p)|\leq C\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right).

(b)

If, in addition,
g\in L^{\infty}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then, for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(3.9)

|b^{g}(p)|\leq C\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right).

Statement (a) will be used in the proof of exponential decay (
Proposition

4.1
) because it uses weaker norms of
f
. The benefit of statement (b) is that the right-hand side does not grow for large
|p|
, so it will be used in the regularity bootstrapping in the proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof.

Using
Lemma

2.3
to bound
(\tau-2)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
, we have

\begin{split}\Lambda(p,q)(\tau-2)&=\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle\tau^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\tau-2)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\lesssim\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\tau^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}\frac{1}{|p-q|}.\end{split}

Recalling that
\tau\geq 1
, we have
\tau^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\leq(\tau-1)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\tau^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}&\leq\frac{(\tau-1)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}=\frac{[\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\\
&=\left[1-\frac{p\cdot q}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\right]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lesssim 1,\end{split}

globally in
p
and
q
.
Returning to the integral defining
b_{j}^{g}
in (
2.6
), we have

(3.10)

|b^{g}_{j}|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|p_{j}+q_{j}|}{|p-q|}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q.

Assuming
|p|>2
, we divide this integral into three parts. First, for
q\in B_{\nicefrac{{|p|}}{{2}}}(0)
, since
|p-q|\approx|p|\approx|q|
, we have

(3.11)

\int_{B_{\nicefrac{{|p|}}{{2}}}(0)}\frac{|p_{j}+q_{j}|}{|p-q|}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\int_{B_{\nicefrac{{|p|}}{{2}}}(0)}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\leq\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Next, with
r=\langle p\rangle^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}
, we have using
|q|\approx|p|
for

,

(3.12)

\int_{B_{r}(p)}\frac{|p_{j}+q_{j}|}{|p-q|}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\langle p\rangle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\frac{1}{|p-q|}\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\langle p\rangle r^{2}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Finally, for

This establishes (a).

This concludes the proof.
∎

(b)

, then, for all

|B^{g}(p)|\leq C\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right).

Proof.

Recall from (

Writing

(\tau-1)p_{i}-q_{i}=(\tau-2)p_{i}+p_{i}-q_{i},

and using the definition of

\begin{split}\Lambda(p,q)[(\tau-1)p_{i}-q_{i}]&=\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\frac{1}{(\tau(\tau-2))^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}[(\tau-2)p_{i}+p_{i}-q_{i}]\\

As in the proof of

, we use

2.3
to bound
(\tau-2)
from below, as well as the inequality
\tau^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\leq(\tau-1)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}
, and obtain

\begin{split}|B_{i}^{g}(p)|&\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(\tau-1)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\left[\frac{\sqrt{2}|p_{i}|\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{|p-q|}+\frac{2^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}|p_{i}-q_{i}|}{|p-q|^{3}}\right]g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{|p|}{|p-q|}+\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|^{2}}\right]g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q,\end{split}

after using
(\tau-1)\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle
. The first term in this integral can be bounded above in exactly the same way as the expression (
3.10
) in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
, giving

(3.19)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|p|}{|p-q|}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}(\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}).

For the second term in (
3
), we let
r=\langle p\rangle^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}
and divide the integral into
B_{r}(p)
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r}(p)
. First, we have

(3.20)

\int_{B_{r}(p)}\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|^{2}}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{2}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{B_{r}(p)}|p-q|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{2}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}r\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{5}}{{3}}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}},

since
\langle q\rangle\lesssim\langle p\rangle
when
q\in B_{r}(p)
. Next, we have

(3.21)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r}(p)}\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|^{2}}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\leq r^{-2}\langle p\rangle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r}(p)}\langle q\rangle g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\leq\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{5}}{{3}}}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

This concludes the proof of (a).

To prove (b), we derive the upper bound (
3
) in the same way as (a). For the first term in (
3
), we proceed as in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
(b) to obtain

(3.22)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|p|}{|p-q|}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

For the second term in (
3
), we re-do the calculation in the proof of (a) with the new choice
r=\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
. We now have

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|^{2}}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q&\lesssim\langle p\rangle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\langle q\rangle^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}|p-q|^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}r\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

and

(3.23)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r}(p)}\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|^{2}}g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\leq r^{-2}\langle p\rangle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r}(p)}\langle q\rangle g(q)\,\mathrm{d}q\leq\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

as claimed.
∎

4.
Polynomial and exponential decay

This section is devoted to the proof of our first main result,
Theorem

1.1
. We begin by showing that polynomial decay estimates in
p
are propagated forward in time. The proof uses a barrier argument that relies on precise upper bounds for the coefficients
a^{f}
,
b^{f}
, and
c^{f}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f\geq 0
be a solution to the relativistic Landau equation (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
for some
T>0
, satisfying

(4.1)

E_{f}(t,x)\leq E_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq F_{0}

uniformly in
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, for some constants
E_{0}
and
F_{0}
.
Assume that, for some
k>0
, the initial data
f_{\rm in}:\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies

(4.2)

\langle p\rangle^{k}f_{\rm in}(x,p)\leq M_{k}

uniformly in
(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
, for some
M_{k}>0
. Then there is
\beta>0
, depending only on
k
,
E_{0}
, and
F_{0}
, such that, for all
(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
,

(4.3)

\langle p\rangle^{k}f(t,x,p)\leq e^{\beta t}M_{k},\quad(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{6}.

Proof.

For
\beta>0
to be chosen later, and
\varepsilon>0
an arbitrary small constant, define the barrier

(4.4)

g(t,x,p)=e^{\beta t}\left[M_{k}\langle p\rangle^{-k}+\varepsilon(\langle x\rangle+\langle p\rangle)\right].

By construction,
f(0,x,p)<g(0,x,p)
for all
(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
. Assume by contradiction that the inequality
f<g
is not true in all of
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
. Then we claim there is a point
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
, where
f
and
g
cross for the first time. Indeed, since
f
is bounded and
g(t,x,p)\geq\varepsilon(\langle x\rangle+\langle p\rangle)
, we must have
f<g
outside of
[0,T]\times B_{R}\times B_{R}
for some
R>0
depending on
\varepsilon
. Uniform continuity in
t
, and the fact that
f<g
at
t=0
, then implies the existence of
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
, where
f(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})=g(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
for the first time.

At the point
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
, we have

(4.5)

\partial_{t}g\leq\partial_{t}f,\quad\nabla_{x}f=\nabla_{x}g,\quad\nabla_{p}f=\nabla_{p}g,\quad D_{p}^{2}f\leq D_{p}^{2}g.

Using these facts and the equation (
1.1
) satisfied by
f
, we have, at
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
,

\begin{split}\partial_{t}g+\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}g&\leq\partial_{t}f+\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}f)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{v}f+c^{f}f\\
&\leq\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}g)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}g+c^{f}g,\end{split}

where we also used the fact that
a^{f}
is nonnegative-definite (which follows from
Proposition

2.1
). To keep the notation clean, we write
(t,x,p)
instead of
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
for the rest of this proof.

We will use (
4
) to derive a contradiction. First, we bound the left side from below:

(4.6)

\partial_{t}g+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}g=\beta g+\varepsilon e^{\beta t}\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle}\geq\beta g-\varepsilon e^{\beta t}\geq(\beta-1)g.

To estimate the right side of (
4
) from above, we use the upper bounds

\begin{split}|\nabla_{p}g|&=e^{\beta t}\left|-kM_{k}\langle p\rangle^{-k-2}p+\varepsilon\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\right|\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-1}g,\\
|D_{p}^{2}g|&=e^{\beta t}\left|-kM_{k}\langle p\rangle^{-k-2}\left(I-(k+2)\frac{p\otimes p}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right)+\varepsilon\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right)\right|\\
&\lesssim e^{\beta t}\left(M_{k}\langle p\rangle^{-k-2}+\varepsilon\langle p\rangle^{-1}\right)\\
&\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-2}g.\end{split}

Using the estimates for the coefficients contained in
Proposition

2.1
(b),
3.2
, and
3.3
(a), we have

\begin{split}a^{f}(t,x,p)&\lesssim\langle p\rangle C_{\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}\lesssim\langle p\rangle,\\
b^{f}(t,x,p)&\lesssim C\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\left(\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}},\\
c^{f}(t,x,p)&\lesssim C\left(\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|f(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)\lesssim 1,\end{split}

by our assumptions on
f
. These implied constants depend on
E_{0}
and
F_{0}
. (Note that we have interpolated the
L^{1}
and
L^{3}
norms of
f
between
L^{1}_{1}
and
L^{\infty}
.) With (
4
), this implies

\begin{split}\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}g)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}g+c^{f}g&\lesssim\langle p\rangle|D_{p}^{2}g|+\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}|\nabla_{p}g|+g\lesssim g,\end{split}

for an implied constant depending only on the quantities in the statement of the lemma.
Choosing
\beta
so that
\beta-1
is larger than this constant, we have derived a contradiction with (
4
), and the proof is complete after sending
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Next, we prove the corresponding statement about exponential decay estimates. The proof uses another barrier argument, but also relies on the polynomial upper bounds of the previous proposition, which is why the final estimate depends on
t
in a double-exponential way.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f
satisfy the assumptions of
Proposition

4.1
, and assume in addition that there exists
\sigma,N>0
such that, for all
(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(4.7)

e^{\sigma\langle p\rangle}f_{\rm in}(x,p)\leq N.

Then
f
satisfies , for all
(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(4.8)

e^{\sigma\langle p\rangle}f(t,x,p)\leq N\exp(CNte^{Ct}),

where
C>0
depends on
E_{0}
,
F_{0}
, and
\sigma
.

Proof.

This proposition is also proven using a barrier argument. However, the upper bounds for the coefficients
a^{f}
and
b^{f}
in (
4
) are not sufficient to close our argument. Therefore, we first get control on the
L^{\infty}_{k}
norm of
f
, for some fixed
k>5
, by applying
Proposition

4.1
. To be concrete, we arbitrarily choose
k=6
. This yields

(4.9)

f(t,x,p)\lesssim Ne^{\beta t}\langle p\rangle^{-6},

for an implied constant depending on
\sigma
, where
\beta
is the constant from
Proposition

4.1
which depends only on
E_{0}
and
F_{0}
. Note that this decay implies
f\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}(L^{1}_{s})_{p}
for some
s>2
. From
Proposition

2.1
(b),
Lemma

3.2
, and
Lemma

3.3
(b), we therefore have

\begin{split}a^{f}(t,x,p)&\lesssim Ne^{\beta t},\\
b^{f}(t,x,p)&\lesssim Ne^{\beta t},\\
c^{f}(t,x,p)&\lesssim 1,\end{split}

for each
(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
, with implied constants depending on
F_{0}
,
E_{0}
, and
\sigma
. Crucially, these upper bounds do not grow for large
|p|
.

With
\gamma>0
to be determined, and
\varepsilon>0
a small constant, define

(4.10)

h(t,x,p)=e^{\gamma t}\left[Ne^{-\sigma\langle p\rangle}+\varepsilon(\langle x\rangle+\langle p\rangle)\right].

Assuming that
f<h
is false, then following the same arguments as in the proof of
Proposition

4.1
, we conclude there is a point
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
where
f
and
h
cross for the first time, and that at this point,

(4.11)

(\gamma-1)h\leq\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}h)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}h+c^{f}h.

By direct calculation, we have

\begin{split}|\nabla_{p}h|&=e^{\gamma t}\left|\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\left(-\sigma Ne^{-\sigma\langle p\rangle}+\varepsilon\right)\right|\lesssim h,\\
|D_{p}^{2}h|&=e^{\gamma t}\left|\sigma N\left[\sigma N\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}-\sigma N\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\langle p\rangle}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{\langle p\rangle^{3}}\right)\right]e^{-\sigma\langle p\rangle}+\varepsilon\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\langle p\rangle}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{\langle p\rangle^{3}}\right)\right|\lesssim h.\end{split}

Therefore, using the upper bounds (
4
), we have at the crossing point
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
,

(4.12)

(\gamma-1)h\leq\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}h)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}h+c^{f}h\leq CNe^{\beta t}h

for some constant
C
. Now, for any
\tau\in(0,T]
, the choice
\gamma=1+CNe^{\beta\tau}
implies that (
4.12
) cannot occur for any
(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
with
t_{0}\leq\tau
. This implies
f<h
in the domain
[0,\tau]\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
, or

(4.13)

f(t,x,p)\leq Ne^{(1+CNe^{\beta\tau})t}\left(e^{-\sigma\langle p\rangle}+\varepsilon(\langle x\rangle+\langle p\rangle\right)

for all
(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
, this gives, at

,

(4.14)

f(\tau,x,p)\leq Ne^{(1+CNe^{\beta\tau})\tau}e^{-\sigma\langle p\rangle}.

Since

∎

Together, Propositions

and

imply
Theorem

1.1
.

5.
Lorentz Boosts and Hölder norms

5.1.
Lorentz boosts

Recall the Galilean (non-relativistic) kinetic shift, which is defined as a Lie product on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
by

(5.1)

z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}z=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\circ_{\mathcal{G}}(t,x,v)=(t_{0}+t,x_{0}+x+tv_{0},v_{0}+v),

with inverse

(5.2)

z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}z=(t-t_{0},x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0},v-v_{0}).

In Newtonian physics, this shift is used to transform between the coordinates of two reference frames differing from each other by a constant velocity
v_{0}
.

The purpose of this subsection is to derive analogous formulas that are based on a class of Lorentz transformations. These formulas follow from the fundamentals of special relativity, but they are difficult to find in the mathematics literature, at least in the form that we need, that is analogous to expressions for the Galilean shifts (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). See
[
4
]
for a derivation of similar formulas in the case of one spatial dimension.

Consider a test particle of mass 1 with position
x
and velocity
v
, measured in an inertial frame
\Sigma
. Recall that we are working in a unit system in which the speed of light equals 1. Defining the Lorentz factor

(5.3)

\gamma(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}},

our particle has momentum
p=\gamma(v)v
and energy
E=\langle p\rangle=\gamma(v)
. Combining time with position and energy with momentum, we obtain the standard contravariant four-vectors

(5.4)

\left(\begin{matrix}t\\
x\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(\begin{matrix}E\\
p\end{matrix}\right),

measured with respect to
\Sigma
. Considering a second inertial frame
\widetilde{\Sigma}
moving with velocity
v_{0}
with respect to
\Sigma
, the Lorentz boost matrix that translates between the two frames is given by

(5.5)

\displaystyle L(v_{0})=\left(\begin{matrix}\gamma_{0}&-\gamma_{0}v_{0}\\[7.0pt]
-\gamma_{0}v_{0}&I+(\gamma_{0}-1)\dfrac{v_{0}\otimes v_{0}}{|v_{0}|^{2}}\end{matrix}\right),

where
\gamma_{0}=\gamma(v_{0})
. Note that the inverse matrix of
L(v_{0})
is
L(-v_{0})
. If the two frames are given the same origin in
(t,x)
space, then measurements in
\widetilde{\Sigma}
are related to measurements in
\Sigma
by

(5.6)

\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{t}\\
\widetilde{x}\end{matrix}\right)=L(v_{0})\left(\begin{matrix}t\\
x\end{matrix}\right),\quad\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{E}\\
\widetilde{p}\end{matrix}\right)=L(v_{0})\left(\begin{matrix}E\\
p\end{matrix}\right).

However, we also want to recenter around
(t_{0},x_{0})
, as in the Galilean shift (
5.2
). Therefore, the correct formula for our purposes is given by

(5.7)

\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{t}\\
\widetilde{x}\end{matrix}\right)=L(v_{0})\left(\begin{matrix}t-t_{0}\\
x-x_{0}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\left(\begin{matrix}\widetilde{E}\\
\widetilde{p}\end{matrix}\right)=L(v_{0})\left(\begin{matrix}E\\
p\end{matrix}\right).

Unpacking this calculation and recalling
\gamma_{0}=\langle p_{0}\rangle
and
\gamma_{0}v_{0}=p_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{t}&=\gamma_{0}(t-t_{0})-\gamma_{0}v_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})\\
&=\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0}),\\
\widetilde{x}&=-\gamma_{0}v_{0}(t-t_{0})+x-x_{0}+(\gamma_{0}-1)\frac{v_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})}{|v_{0}|^{2}}v_{0}\\
&=-p_{0}(t-t_{0})+x-x_{0}+(\langle p_{0}\rangle-1)(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}\\
&=-p_{0}(t-t_{0})+(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel},\end{split}

where for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we use the following conventions:

(5.8)

w_{\perp}=w-(w\cdot\hat{p}_{0})\hat{p}_{0},\qquad w_{\parallel}=(w\cdot\hat{p}_{0})\hat{p}_{0},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\hat{p}_{0}=\frac{p_{0}}{|p_{0}|}.

Next, we have (with
E=\langle p\rangle
)

\begin{split}\widetilde{E}&=\gamma_{0}E-\gamma_{0}v_{0}\cdot p\\
&=\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle p\rangle-p_{0}\cdot p,\\
\widetilde{p}&=-\gamma_{0}v_{0}E+p+(\gamma_{0}-1)\frac{p\cdot v_{0}}{|v_{0}|^{2}}v_{0}\\
&=-p_{0}\langle p\rangle+p+(\langle p_{0}\rangle-1)p_{\parallel}\\
&=-p_{0}\langle p\rangle+p_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle p_{\parallel}.\end{split}

Writing this in Lie product notation, the (inverse) Lorentzian boost is given by

\begin{split}z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z&=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(t,x,p)\\
&=\big(\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0}),\\
&\quad\quad\ (x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0}),p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle\big).\end{split}

By direct calculation, this implies the forward boost is

\begin{split}z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z&=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(t,x,p)\\
&=(t_{0}+t\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\cdot x,\,x_{0}+x_{\perp}+x_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}t,\,p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle).\end{split}

When
|p_{0}|
is small, so that
\langle p_{0}\rangle\approx 1
, one can directly check that our expressions for
z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z
and
z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z
are well approximated by
z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}z
and
z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}z
.

5.2.
Distance functions on
\mathbb{R}^{7}

With
\|(t,x,v)\|=|t|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+|x|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}+|v|
, the Galilean kinetic distance is defined as

(5.9)

d_{\mathcal{G}}(z,z_{0})=\|z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}z\|=|t-t_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}+|v-v_{0}|.

Even though relativistic kinetic equations do not satisfy any scaling law, it is still convenient to use the scaling of
\|z\|=|t|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+|x|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}+|p|
, due to the connection with non-relativistic kinetic equations. The relativistic kinetic distance is therefore defined by

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})&=\|z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z\|\\
&=|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\\
&\qquad+|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|.\end{split}

5.3.
Lorentzian-to-Galilean change of variables

To translate between relativistic and non-relativistic kinetic equations, we require a change of variables to translate momentum to velocity. This change of variables
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to B_{1}
is defined by

(5.10)

\varphi(p)=\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle},

with inverse

(5.11)

\psi(v)=\frac{v}{\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}}.

In this subsection, we use
\phi
and
\psi
to compare the Lorentzian and Galilean distances. First, let us prove an elementary lemma that we use regularly below.

Lemma 5.1
.

Fix vectors
u
and
w
and scalar
c\geq 1
. If
u
is decomposed into
u_{\perp}+u_{\parallel}
along
w
, then

(5.12)

|u_{\perp}+cu_{\parallel}+w|\leq|cu+w|\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|u_{\perp}+u_{\parallel}+w|\leq|u_{\perp}+c(u_{\parallel}+w)|.

Proof.

We prove the first inequality, as the proof of the second is essentially the same. We combine the orthogonality of
u_{\perp}
and
cu_{\parallel}+w
as well as the fact that
c\geq 1
to find

(5.13)

|u_{\perp}+cu_{\parallel}+w|^{2}=|u_{\perp}|^{2}+|cu_{\parallel}+w|^{2}\leq|cu_{\perp}|^{2}+|cu_{\parallel}+w|^{2}=|cu+w|^{2}.

Taking square roots, the proof is finished.
∎

We now show that, in a sense, the
d_{\mathcal{L}}
metric controls the
d_{\mathcal{G}}
metric.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
(t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, there holds

(5.14)

d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,x,\varphi(p)),(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0})))\leq C\left[d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0}))+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}d_{\mathcal{L}}^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}((t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0}))\right],

for a universal constant
C>0
.

Proof.

We have

(5.15)

d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,x,\varphi(p)),(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0})))=|t-t_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\left|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}+\left|\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}-\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right|.

Starting with the middle term, we apply
Lemma

5.1
to find

\begin{split}\left|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right|^{2}&\leq|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|^{2}\\
&\leq d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0}))^{6}.\end{split}

Both inequalities use the orthogonality of the terms, the first inequality uses that
1\leq\langle p_{0}\rangle
, and the last inequality simply uses the definition of
d_{\mathcal{L}}
.

The third term in (
5.15
) is handled similarly:

\begin{split}\left|\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}-\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right|^{2}&=\frac{|p_{\perp}|^{2}}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}+\frac{|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}}{\langle p\rangle^{2}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}}\leq|p_{\perp}|^{2}+|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}\\
&=|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}\leq d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0}))^{2}.\end{split}

The first term in (
5.15
) is a bit different. It is here that the extra term arises involving
\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
in the statement of the lemma. If

(5.16)

|t-t_{0}|\leq 2\langle p_{0}\rangle|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|

then we are finished since

(5.17)

|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|\leq d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{2}.

Hence, we consider only the case

(5.18)

|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|<\frac{1}{2}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-1}|t-t_{0}|.

Rewriting this we find
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-1}|t-t_{0}|
such that

(5.19)

t-t_{0}=\dfrac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\cdot(x-x_{0})+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}.

We deduce that

\begin{split}\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})+\varepsilon\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}&=\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\cdot(x-x_{0})\\
&=\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}\left(1-\frac{|p_{0}|^{2}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac{(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}.\end{split}

Now, using the shorthand
d_{\mathcal{L}}=d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0}))
, we have

\begin{split}|t-t_{0}|&\leq\left|t-t_{0}+\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\cdot(x-x_{0})\right|+\left|\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\cdot(x-x_{0})\right|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}d_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}+\left|p_{0}\cdot\frac{(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right|.\end{split}

Here, we applied (
5.17
). Next, we use (
5.3
) and that

(5.20)

|\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|\leq|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|\leq d_{\mathcal{L}}^{3}

to further deduce from (
5.3
) that

\begin{split}|t-t_{0}|&\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}d_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}+\left|p_{0}\cdot\left(\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})+\varepsilon\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}d_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}+\left|p_{0}\cdot(\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0}))\right|+|\varepsilon|\langle p_{0}\rangle\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}d_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}+\langle p_{0}\rangle d_{\mathcal{L}}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}|t-t_{0}|.\end{split}

The combination of our three bounds (
5.3
), (
5.3
), and (
5.3
), with the Galilean metric (
5.15
) yields the claim.
∎

Next, we show an inequality in the reverse direction, i.e. we show that the
d_{\mathcal{G}}
metric controls the
d_{\mathcal{L}}
metric in some sense.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
(t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, there holds

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,p),(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0}))&\leq C\left[\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle p\rangle d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,x,\varphi(p)),(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0}))\right.\\
&\left.\qquad+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{\mathcal{G}}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t,x,\varphi(p)),(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0}))\right],\end{split}

for a universal constant
C>0
.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we write

(5.21)

v=\varphi(p),\quad v_{0}=\varphi(p_{0}),\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{\mathcal{G}}=d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,x,v),(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})).

Notice that

\begin{split}\langle p_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|v_{0}|^{2}}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\cdot v_{0}=\sqrt{1-|v_{0}|^{2}}<1.\end{split}

The analogous identities hold for
p
and
v
.

The first term in the definition (
5.2
) of
d_{\mathcal{L}}
is easily controlled:

\begin{split}|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|&=|(t-t_{0})(\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\cdot v_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0})|\\
&\leq|t-t_{0}||\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\cdot v_{0}|+|p_{0}||x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0})|\\
&\leq|t-t_{0}|+|p_{0}|d_{\mathcal{G}}^{3}\leq d_{\mathcal{G}}+|p_{0}|d_{\mathcal{G}}^{3}\end{split}

We are therefore finished with the first term, deducing that

(5.22)

|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq d_{\mathcal{G}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|p_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{\mathcal{G}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

For the second term, we apply the first inequality in
Lemma

5.1
to find

\begin{split}|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle&(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-(t-t_{0})p_{0}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq|\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})-(t-t_{0})p_{0}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\\
&=\langle p_{0}\rangle|(x-x_{0})-(t-t_{0})v_{0}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\langle p_{0}\rangle d_{\mathcal{G}},\end{split}

as desired.

We now consider the last term in (
5.2
). Squaring it yields

(5.23)

|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}=|p_{\perp}|^{2}+|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}=\frac{|v|^{2}-(v\cdot\hat{v}_{0})^{2}}{1-|v|^{2}}+\frac{\left(\hat{v}_{0}\cdot(v_{0}-v)\right)^{2}}{(1-|v|^{2})(1-|v_{0}|^{2})}.

The numerator of the second term can be estimated simply using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. To bound the first term, we keep
v_{0}
fixed and apply Taylor’s Theorem. Indeed, noting that

\begin{split}&D_{v}(|v|^{2}-(v\cdot\hat{v}_{0})^{2})|_{v=v_{0}}=0,\qquad D^{2}_{v}(|v|^{2}-(v\cdot\hat{v}_{0})^{2})|_{v=v_{0}}=2(I-\hat{v}_{0}\otimes\hat{v}_{0}),\end{split}

and all these quantities are globally bounded, we deduce that

(5.24)

\frac{|v|^{2}-(v\cdot\hat{v}_{0})^{2}}{1-|v|^{2}}\lesssim\frac{|v-v_{0}|^{2}}{1-|v|^{2}}.

Hence, we conclude that

(5.25)

|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}\lesssim\frac{|v-v_{0}|^{2}}{1-|v|^{2}}+\frac{|v-v_{0}|^{2}}{(1-|v|^{2})(1-|v_{0}|^{2})}\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{2}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}d_{\mathcal{G}}^{2},

using
\langle p\rangle=(1-|v|^{2})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
and
\langle p_{0}\rangle=(1-|v_{0}|^{2})^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
.

Combining (
5.22
), (
5.3
), and (
5.25
) completes the proof.
∎

5.4.
Kinetic and relativistic cylinders

When dealing with non-relativistic kinetic equations, whose prototype is the Kolmogorov equation

(5.26)

\partial_{t}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\Delta_{v}u,

the usual sets on which to study local estimates are the
kinetic cylinders
centered at a point
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})
, defined by

\begin{split}Q_{r}(z_{0})&=\{(t,x,v):-r^{2}<t-t_{0}\leq 0,\,|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0}|<r^{3}\}\times B_{r}(v_{0}).\end{split}

One often writes
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0)
. Let
\delta_{r}(t,x,v))=(r^{2}t,r^{3}x,rv)
be the appropriate scaling operator. It is straightforward to see that: (1)
Q_{r}(z_{0})=(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}\delta_{r})Q_{1}
, and (2) if
u
solves (
5.26
) on some
Q_{r}(z_{0})
, then
v(z)=u(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{G}}\delta_{r}(z))
solves the same equation on
Q_{1}
.

The relativistic analogue of (
5.26
) is

(5.27)

\partial_{t}u+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\Delta_{p}u.

Even though this equation does not satisfy any scaling law analogous to the symmetry of (
5.26
) under
\delta_{r}
, the most natural (for our purposes) definition of the
relativistic kinetic cylinders
is nevertheless obtained by mimicking the above definition of
Q_{r}(z_{0})
. Namely, for
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
, we define

\begin{split}Q^{\rm rel}_{r}(z_{0})=\{(t,x,p):&-r^{2}<\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})\leq 0,\\
&|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|<r^{3},\\
&|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|<r\}.\end{split}

One can readily check that, although the cylinders do not scale as in the classical case, they do satisfy a translation property: for all
r>0
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
,

(5.28)

Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0})=z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}Q_{r}.

Another type of cylinder in
(t,x,p)
space will be needed when applying local regularity estimates. This is the non-relativistic kinetic cylinder
Q_{r}(z_{0})
mapped forward by the transformation

\psi(v)=\nicefrac{{v}}{{\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}}}

in the
v
variable. In other words, for
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})
, define

(5.29)

\widetilde{Q}_{r}(z_{0})=\left\{(t,x):t_{0}-r^{2}<t\leq t_{0},\left|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right|<r^{3}\right\}\times\psi\left(B_{r}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}\right)\right).

5.5.
Hölder norms

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we define the Hölder seminorms

\begin{split}[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}=\sup_{z,z_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{|g(z)-g(z_{0})|}{d_{\mathcal{G}}(z,z_{0})^{\alpha}},\quad\text{ and }\quad[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}=\sup_{z,z_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{|g(z)-g(z_{0})|}{d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\alpha}},\\
\end{split}

where
\Omega
is any subset of
\mathbb{R}^{7}
. We also define

(5.30)

\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}=[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}=[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

We occasionally need to measure Hölder continuity in only the
t
or
x
variable.
For this, we define the seminorms

\begin{split}[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G},t}(\Omega)}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x,v),(t_{0},x,v)\in\Omega\\
t\neq t_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{|g(t,x,v)-g(t_{0},x,v)|}{|t-t_{0}|^{\alpha}+|v(t-t_{0})|^{\frac{2\alpha}{3}}},\\
[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L},t}(\Omega)}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x,p),(t_{0},x,p)\in\Omega\\
t\neq t_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{|g(t,x,p)-g(t_{0},x,p)|}{|\langle p\rangle(t-t_{0})|^{\alpha}+|p(t-t_{0})|^{\frac{2\alpha}{3}}},\\
[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G},x}(\Omega)}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x,v),(t,x_{0},v)\in\Omega\\
x\neq x_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{|g(t,x,v)-g(t,x_{0},v)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}},\\
[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L},x}(\Omega)}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x,p),(t,x_{0},p)\in\Omega\\
x\neq x_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{|g(t,x,p)-g(t,x_{0},p)|}{|p\cdot(x-x_{0})|^{3\alpha/2}+|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}|^{\alpha}},\end{split}

where in the last line, we use the notation

(5.31)

(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}=\frac{p}{|p|}(x-x_{0})\cdot\frac{p}{|p|}\quad\text{ and }\quad(x-x_{0})_{\perp}=(x-x_{0})-(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}.

Let us also define the seminorms of order
1+\alpha
,
2+\alpha
, and
3+\alpha
. Our Schauder estimates (see
Section

6
below) are stated in terms of these seminorms: for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}
,

\begin{split}[g]_{C^{1+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}&=[\nabla_{p}f]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{(1+\alpha)/2}_{\mathcal{L},t}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{(1+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(\Omega)},\\
[g]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}&=[D_{p}^{2}f]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{L},t}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(\Omega)},\\
[g]_{C^{3+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}&=[D_{p}^{3}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}+[\partial_{t}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}+[\nabla_{x}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)},\\
[g]_{C^{1+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}&=[\nabla_{p}f]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{(1+\alpha)/2}_{\mathcal{G},t}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{(1+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{G},x}(\Omega)},\\
[g]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}&=[D_{v}^{2}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{G},t}(\Omega)}+[g]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{G},x}(\Omega)},\quad\text{ and}\\
[g]_{C^{3+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}&=[D_{v}^{3}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}+[\partial_{t}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}+[\nabla_{x}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Here,
\beta\in(0,1)
is a fixed number that can be chosen arbitrarily close to 1. By an abuse of notation, we do not make
\beta
explicit in the
C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}
seminorm notations. These definitions and notation choices are adapted from
[
20
]
, and these seminorms allow us to obtain Schauder estimates that can be iterated to prove smoothness of solutions to (
1.1
).

Using our equivalence of metrics, we can show:

Lemma 5.4
.

For any domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}
and any function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\widetilde{g}:\widetilde{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\begin{split}&\widetilde{g}(t,x,v)=g(t,x,\psi(v)),\qquad\text{ where}\\
&\widetilde{\Omega}=\{(t,x,v):(t,x,\psi(v))\in\Omega\}=\{(t,x,\phi(v)):(t,x,v)\in\Omega\}.\end{split}

Recall
\phi
and
\psi
from (
5.10
)-(
5.11
). For some
R\in(0,1)
, assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}\times B_{R/\sqrt{1-R^{2}}}
, or equivalently, that
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}\times B_{R}
.

Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the estimates

\begin{split}[g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}&\leq C(1-R^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{6}}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\quad\text{and}\\
[\widetilde{g}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(\widetilde{\Omega})}&\leq C(1-R^{2})^{-\alpha}\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}\end{split}

hold whenever the right-hand sides are finite, where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\alpha
.

Proof.

To prove the first inequality, let
z=(t,x,p),z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})\in\Omega
. Recall that, by assumption,

(5.32)

\langle p_{0}\rangle\leq(1-R^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

We consider two cases.

If
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})\geq\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, then

(5.33)

\frac{|g(z)-g(z_{0})|}{d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\alpha}}\leq 2\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq 2(1-R^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{6}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})},

where we used (
5.32
) in the second inequality. The proof of the first estimate is clearly complete in this case.

If
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})<\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, then the estimate of
Lemma

5.2
becomes

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,x,\varphi(p)),(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0}))&\leq C\left(d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6}}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})\right)\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\frac{1}{3}}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0}).\end{split}

Therefore,

(5.34)

\frac{|g(z)-g(z_{0})|}{d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\alpha}}\leq(1-R^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{6}}\frac{|g(t,x,\psi(v))-g(t_{0},x_{0},\psi(v_{0}))|}{d_{\mathcal{G}}((t,x,v),(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0}))^{\alpha}}.

The proof of the first estimate is clearly complete in this case.

The second claimed estimate follows from a similar, but simpler, argument, using
Lemma

5.3
in place of
Lemma

5.2
. We omit the proof.
∎

We need to compare the Lorentzian Hölder norm to the Euclidean Hölder norm on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, defined as usual by

(5.35)

[u]_{C^{\alpha}_{E}(\Omega)}=\sup_{z,z_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{|f(z)-f(z_{0})|}{|t-t_{0}|+|x-x_{0}|+|p-p_{0}|}=\sup_{z,z_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{|f(z)-f(z_{0})|}{d_{E}(z,z_{0})},

and
\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{E}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+[u]_{C^{\alpha}_{E}(\Omega)}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
z,z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, there holds

(5.36)

d_{E}(z,z_{0})\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}(d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})+d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{3}),

where
d_{E}
is the standard Eulidean distance defined implicitly in (
5.35
). In particular,

(5.37)

\displaystyle|t-t_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{2}+\langle p_{0}\rangle d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{3}\right),

(5.38)

\displaystyle|x-x_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{3}+\langle p_{0}\rangle d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{2}\right),\quad\text{and}

(5.39)

\displaystyle|p-p_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|,

where

(5.40)

p_{\parallel}=(p\cdot\hat{p}_{0})\hat{p}_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad p_{\perp}=p-p_{\parallel}

and
C
is a constant independent of
z
and
z_{0}
.

Furthermore, for any
R>0
and any subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}
,

(5.41)

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega)}\leq CR^{2\alpha}\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{E}(\Omega)},

for a constant independent of
\Omega
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

Clearly, (
5.36
) follows from (
5.37
), (
5.38
), and (
5.39
). Hence, we prove those one at a time.

Let
z,z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
be arbitrary. We established (
5.37
) above in the proof of
Lemma

5.2
. We, thus, begin with (
5.38
).
Note that, by
Lemma

5.1
,

\begin{split}|x-x_{0}|&\leq|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}|\\
&\leq|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|+|p_{0}(t-t_{0})|\\
&\leq d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{3}+C|p_{0}|\left(d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{2}+\langle p_{0}\rangle d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{3}\right),\end{split}

where we used (
5.37
) in the last inequality. The proof of (
5.38
) is complete.

To establish the estimate (
5.39
), we first note that

(5.42)

|p-p_{0}|^{2}\lesssim|p_{\parallel}-p_{0}|^{2}+|p_{\perp}|^{2}.

For the first term, we write

\begin{split}|p_{\parallel}-p_{0}|&=\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p_{0}\rangle|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}|+\frac{|p_{0}|}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}|\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle-\langle p_{0}\rangle|\end{split}

By basic calculus, we have

(5.43)

|\langle p\rangle-\langle q\rangle|\leq|p-q|\qquad\text{ for any }p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Applying this inequality to
p_{\parallel}
and
p_{0}
in (
5.5
) yields

(5.44)

|p_{\parallel}-p_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}|+\frac{|p_{0}|}{\langle p_{0}\rangle}|p_{\parallel}-p_{0}|.

Collecting
|p_{\parallel}-p_{0}|
terms in this yields

\begin{split}|p_{\parallel}-p_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle-|p_{0}|}\,|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle|.\end{split}

Applying (
5.43
) again to the vectors
p
and
p_{\parallel}
, we have

\begin{split}|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle|^{2}&=|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle-p_{0}\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle|^{2}\\
&\lesssim|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}+|p_{0}|^{2}|\langle p\rangle-\langle p_{\parallel}\rangle|^{2}\\
&\leq|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}+|p_{0}|^{2}|p-p_{\parallel}|^{2}=|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}+|p_{0}|^{2}|p_{\perp}|^{2}.\end{split}

Combining the last two inequalities with (
5.42
) yields

\begin{split}|p-p_{0}|^{2}&\lesssim\left(1+\frac{|p_{0}|^{2}}{(\langle p_{0}\rangle-|p_{0}|)^{2}}\right)|p_{\perp}|^{2}+\frac{1}{(\langle p_{0}\rangle-|p_{0}|)^{2}}|p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(5.45)

|p-p_{0}|\leq C_{p_{0}}|p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle|,

where

(5.46)

C_{p_{0}}:=\sqrt{\max\left\{1+\frac{|p_{0}|^{2}}{(\langle p_{0}\rangle-|p_{0}|)^{2}},\frac{1}{(\langle p_{0}\rangle-|p_{0}|)^{2}}\right\}}.

We claim that

(5.47)

C_{p_{0}}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}.

Before proving this, let us note that it completes the proof of (
5.39
).

When
|p_{0}|\approx 1
, (
5.47
) is obvious. When
|p_{0}|\gg 1
, we see this by the simple Taylor expansion

(5.48)

\langle p_{0}\rangle\approx|p_{0}|+\frac{1}{2|p_{0}|},

we find

\begin{split}C_{p_{0}}&=\sqrt{1+\frac{|p_{0}|^{2}}{(\langle p_{0}\rangle-|p_{0}|)^{2}}}\approx\sqrt{1+\frac{|p_{0}|^{2}}{\left(\left(|p_{0}|+\frac{1}{2|p_{0}|}\right)-|p_{0}|\right)^{2}}}\\
&=\sqrt{1+\frac{|p_{0}|^{2}}{\left(\frac{1}{2|p_{0}|}\right)^{2}}}\approx|p_{0}|^{2}.\end{split}

Thus, (
5.47
) is proved, and the proof of (
5.39
) is complete.

From (
5.36
), we establish the inequality between Hölder norms by proceeding as in the proof of
Lemma

5.4
, separating the cases
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})\leq 1
and
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})>1
. We omit the details.
∎

Relativistic Schauder estimate

Consider the linear relativistic kinetic equation

(6.1)

\partial_{t}f+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mbox{tr}(AD_{p}^{2}f)+B\cdot\nabla_{p}f+s,

on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
,
where

are bounded and Hölder continuous, with

satisfying the ellipticity assumption (
2.17
) everywhere in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
.

Our method is based on transforming (
6.1
) into a non-relativistic kinetic equation via the change of variables
u(t,x,v)=f(t,x,\psi(v))
, applying Schauder estimates, and transforming back via
f(t,x,p)=u(t,x,\phi(p))
. The non-relativistic Schauder estimate we use, which is a modification of
[
20
, Theorem 2.9]
, is contained in the next proposition. As mentioned above in
Section

1.3
, there is a large literature on Schauder estimates that apply to (

).
The estimate of
[
20
]
is convenient for us because it gives explicit polynomial dependence on the Hölder norms of the coefficients. The exponent of
\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}}
here is likely not sharp, and with more work, we expect a similar estimate to hold with exponent
1+\frac{2}{\alpha}
, based on related Schauder estimates in other contexts such as
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
. We note that this affects the sharpness in
\alpha
in some of our results below, such as
Theorem

6.5
.

In our proof of
Proposition

6.1
, we need to modify the estimate in
[
20
]
, which considered equations with no first order term in
v
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
u
be a solution to the linear kinetic equation

(6.2)

\partial_{t}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\textup{\mbox{tr}}(AD_{v}^{2}u)+B\cdot\nabla_{v}u+s,

on
Q_{r}(z_{0})
for some
r\in(0,1]
and
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, and assume
A,B,s\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))
, and that

(6.3)

\lambda\Id\leq A(t,x,v)\leq\Lambda\Id,\quad(t,x,v)\in Q_{r}(z_{0}).

Then
u\in C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r/2}(z_{0}))
, and

(6.4)

[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r/2}(z_{0}))}\leq Cr^{-\omega}\left([s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}+\left(1+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{3+\alpha+\nicefrac{{2}}{{\alpha}}}+\|B\|^{2+\alpha}_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right),

for a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\lambda
, and
\Lambda
and
\omega>0
depending on
\alpha
(it is explicitly computable, but its value does not affect our results).

Here, the Hölder seminorm in
(t,x,v)
of order
2+\alpha
is defined in (
5.5
).

Proof.

We first prove the inequality when
(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})=(0,0,0)
and
r=1
. We will then use a scaling argument (and translation invariance of the relevant norms) to obtain the inequality
for general kinetic cylinders and
r\in(0,1]
.

From
[
20
, Theorem 2.12(a)]
with
g=B\cdot\nabla_{v}u+s
, we have

(6.5)

[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C_{0}\left([B\cdot\nabla_{v}u+s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right).

Using
Lemma

5.7
,
we have

\begin{split}[B\cdot\nabla_{v}u+s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}&\leq[B\cdot\nabla_{v}u]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}\\
&\leq\|B\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}[\nabla_{v}u]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+[B]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}\|\nabla_{v}u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}.\end{split}

From the interpolation inequalities in
Lemma

5.6
, we then have

(6.6)

[\nabla_{v}u]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}\leq\varepsilon[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+C\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})},

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{4}C_{0}^{-1}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}^{-1}
, we obtain

(6.7)

\|B\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}[\nabla_{v}u]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{4}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+C\|B\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}^{2+\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}.

Similarly, in the interpolation inequality

(6.8)

\|\nabla_{v}u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\varepsilon^{1+\alpha}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+C\varepsilon^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})},

we choose
\varepsilon^{1+\alpha}=\frac{1}{4}C_{0}^{-1}[B]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{-1}
. Overall, we find

\begin{split}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1/2})}\leq&\frac{1}{2}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}\\
&+C\left((1+\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{2+\alpha})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right),\end{split}

for some constant
C>0
.

We would like to absorb the term
\frac{1}{2}[D_{v}^{2}u]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}
into the left-hand side, even though it is defined on a larger domain
Q_{1}
. There is a standard way to get around this difficulty, which we sketch for the convenience of the reader: for
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\in Q_{1}
and
r\in(0,1]
such that
Q_{r}(z_{0})\subset Q_{1}
, define the rescaled solution
u_{r}=u(t_{0}+r^{2}t,x_{0}+r^{3}x+r^{2}tv_{0},v_{0}+rv)
, which solves

(6.9)

\partial_{t}u_{r}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{r}=\mbox{tr}(A_{r}D_{v}^{2}u_{r})+rB_{r}\cdot\nabla_{v}u_{r}+r^{2}s_{r},

where
A_{r}(t,x,v)=A(t_{0}+r^{2}t,x_{0}+r^{3}x+r^{2}tv_{0},v_{0}+rv)
and
B_{r},s_{r}
are defined similarly. Applying (
6
) to
u_{r}
and translating back to
u
, we obtain

\begin{split}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r/2}(z_{0}))}&\leq\frac{1}{2}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}+\frac{C}{r^{2+\alpha}}\left(\left(1+r^{(1+\alpha)(2+\alpha)}\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{2+\alpha}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+r^{\alpha}[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}+r^{\alpha^{2}+3\alpha+2}\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}+\frac{C}{r^{2}}\left(\left(1+\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{2+\alpha}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right),\end{split}

keeping only the most negative power of
r
. Next, for any
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<1
, we apply the last estimate with
z_{0}\in Q_{R_{1}}
and
r=R_{2}-R_{1}
, and taking a supremum over
z_{0}\in Q_{R_{1}}
, we obtain

\begin{split}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{R_{1}})}\leq&\frac{1}{2}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{R_{2}})}\\
&+\frac{C}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{2}}\left((1+\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{R_{2}})}^{2+\alpha})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R_{2}})}+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{R_{2}})}+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{R_{2}})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R_{2}})}\right).\end{split}

Defining
\omega(R)=[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{R})}
, we have shown

(6.10)

\omega(R_{1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\omega(R_{2})+\frac{C}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{2}}\left((1+\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{2+\alpha})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right),

for any
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<1
. We can now apply
[
18
, Lemma 4.3]
and obtain

(6.11)

\omega(R_{1})\leq\frac{C}{(R_{2}-R_{1})^{2}}\left((1+\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{2+\alpha})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right),

for any
R_{1},R_{2}\in(0,1)
. Choosing
R_{1}=1/2
and
R_{2}=3/4
yields

(6.12)

[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C_{0}\left([s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+\left(1+\|A\|^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}+\|B\|^{2+\alpha}_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{1})}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right).

If we now apply the above inequality to
u_{r}=u(t_{0}+r^{2}t,x_{0}+r^{3}x+r^{2}tv_{0},v_{0}+rv)
,
which solves (
6.9
), with modified coefficients
A_{r}
,
rB_{r}
, and
r^{2}s_{r}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&r^{2+\alpha}[u]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r/2}(z_{0}))}\leq C_{0}r^{2+\alpha}[s]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\\
&\quad\quad+C_{0}\left(1+r^{3\alpha+\alpha^{2}+2}\|A\|^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}+r^{2+3\alpha+\alpha^{2}}\|B\|^{2+\alpha}_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\right)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}.\end{split}

Remembering that
r\in(0,1]
, this completes the proof (with
\omega=2+\alpha
).
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

In order to apply the previous two propositions, we need to study the effect of the change of variables

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
f
solve (
6.1
) in
some transformed cylinder

(6.14)

\widetilde{Q}_{r}(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})=\{(t,x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{7}:(t,x,\varphi(p))\in Q_{r}(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0}))\}.

Then the function
u
defined by

(6.15)

u(t,x,v)=f(t,x,\psi(v)),

solves the non-relativistic kinetic Fokker-Planck equation

(6.16)

\partial_{t}u+v\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\textup{\mbox{tr}}(\widetilde{A}D_{v}^{2}u)+\widetilde{B}\cdot\nabla_{v}u+\widetilde{s},

in
Q_{r}(t_{0},x_{0},\varphi(p_{0}))
, with

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}(t,x,v)&=(1-|v|^{2})(\Id-v\otimes v)A(t,x,\psi(v))(\Id-v\otimes v),\\
\widetilde{B}(t,x,v)&=\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}(\Id-v\otimes v)B(t,x,\psi(v))\\
&\quad+(1-|v|^{2})\left[\left(3v\cdot(A(t,x,\psi(v))v)-\textup{\mbox{tr}}(A(t,x,\psi(v)))\right)v-2A(t,x,\psi(v))v\right],\\
\widetilde{s}(t,x,v)&=s(t,x,\psi(v)).\end{split}

Proof.

The proof is a direct calculation based on the transformation
\varphi(p)=p/\langle p\rangle
. With
u(t,x,v)=f(t,x,\psi(v))
, we clearly have
f(t,x,p)=u(t,x,\varphi(p))
. The left side of (
6.1
) becomes

(6.17)

(\partial_{t}+\varphi(p)\cdot\nabla_{x})f(t,x,p)=(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})u(t,x,v).

For the right-hand side of (
6.1
), we first note that

(6.18)

\partial_{j}\varphi_{i}(p)=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right),\quad i,j=1,2,3,

and

(6.19)

\partial_{jk}\varphi_{i}(p)=\frac{3p_{i}p_{j}p_{k}}{\langle p\rangle^{5}}-\frac{p_{i}\delta_{jk}+p_{j}\delta_{ik}+p_{k}\delta_{ij}}{\langle p\rangle^{3}},\quad i,j,k=1,2,3.

Using
p=\psi(v)=v/\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}
and
\langle p\rangle=1/\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}
, we have

\begin{split}\nabla_{p}f(t,x,p)&=D_{p}\varphi(p)\nabla_{v}u(t,x,v)\\
&=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right)\nabla_{v}u(t,x,v)\\
&=\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}\left(I-v\otimes v\right)\nabla_{v}u(t,x,v),\end{split}

and, using the convention of summing over repeated indices,

\begin{split}[D_{p}^{2}&f(t,x,p)]_{ij}=\partial_{p_{j}}(\partial_{p_{i}}f)\\
&=\partial_{p_{j}}(\partial_{p_{i}}\varphi_{k}(p)\partial_{v_{k}}u)\\
&=\partial_{p_{i}p_{j}}\varphi_{k}(p)\partial_{v_{k}}u+\partial_{p_{i}}\varphi_{k}(p)\partial_{p_{j}}\varphi_{\ell}(p)\partial_{v_{k}v_{\ell}}u\\
&=\left(\frac{3p_{i}p_{j}p_{k}}{\langle p\rangle^{5}}-\frac{p_{i}\delta_{jk}+p_{j}\delta_{ik}+p_{k}\delta_{ij}}{\langle p\rangle^{3}}\right)\partial_{v_{k}}u+\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\left(\delta_{ik}-\frac{p_{i}p_{k}}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{j\ell}-\frac{p_{j}p_{\ell}}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right)\partial_{v_{k}v_{\ell}}u\\
&=(1-|v|^{2})\Big[(3v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}-v_{i}\delta_{jk}-v_{j}\delta_{ik}-v_{k}\delta_{ij})\partial_{v_{k}}u+\left(\delta_{ik}-v_{i}v_{k}\right)\left(\delta_{j\ell}-v_{j}v_{\ell}\right)\partial_{v_{k}v_{\ell}}u\Big].\end{split}

The terms in the right-hand side of (
6.1
) therefore become

\begin{split}B(t,x,p)\cdot\nabla_{p}f(t,x,p)&=\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}(I-v\otimes v)B(t,x,\psi(v))\cdot\nabla_{v}u,\end{split}

and, writing
A_{ij}=A_{ij}(t,x,\psi(v))
,

\begin{split}A_{ij}[D_{p}^{2}f(t,x,p)]_{ij}&=A_{ij}(1-|v|^{2})\left[(\delta_{ik}-v_{i}v_{k})(\delta_{j\ell}-v_{j}v_{\ell})\partial_{v_{k}v_{\ell}}u(t,x,v)\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\left.+3v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\partial_{v_{k}}u-v_{i}\partial_{v_{j}}u-v_{j}\partial_{v_{i}}u-v_{k}\delta_{ij}\partial_{v_{k}}u\right],\end{split}

which implies

\begin{split}&\mbox{tr}(AD_{p}^{2}f(t,x,p))\\
&=(1-|v|^{2})\left[\mbox{tr}((\Id-v\otimes v)A(\Id-v\otimes v)D_{v}^{2}u)+(3v\cdot(Av)-\mbox{tr}(A))v\cdot\nabla_{v}u-2(Av)\cdot\nabla_{v}u\right].\end{split}

Collecting terms, we obtain the statement of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

If

for some

satisfying the ellipticity assumption (

is defined as

then

p_{0}
. We emphasize that

was also chosen independently of
p_{0}
, and this smallness condition on
r=R/\sqrt{1+R^{2}}
determines the value of
R_{0}
in the statement of the theorem.

These uniform ellipticity bounds allow us to apply
Proposition

6.1
and obtain, with
r^{\prime}=\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-1}r,

(6.52)

[g]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}\leq C(r^{\prime})^{-\omega}\left(\left[s_{P}\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}+\left(\|A_{P}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}+\|B_{P}\|^{2+\alpha}_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}\right)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}\right).

We need to translate this into an estimate for
f
by inverting the (several) transformations we have applied.
We begin by deriving an estimate for
f_{z_{0}}
on cylinders centered at 0, and then apply a Lorentz boost to obtain an estimate for
f
on
Q_{R/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0})
.

Beginning with the left side of (
6.52
), since

(6.53)

\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}(t,x,v)=g(t,P^{-1}x,P^{-1}v),

a direct calculation shows

(6.54)

[\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(P(Q_{r^{\prime}/2}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{(2+\alpha)/2}[g]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}.

Let
r^{\prime\prime}=r^{\prime}/\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=r/\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}
, and note that
Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}\subset P(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})
. From the calculations in the proof of
Lemma

6.3
, we see that

\begin{split}D_{p}^{2}f_{z_{0}}(t,x,p)=(1-|v|^{2})\big[&\left(\Id-v\otimes v\right)(D_{v}^{2}\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}})\left(\Id-v\otimes v\right)\\
&+3v\cdot\nabla_{v}\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}(v\otimes v)-v\otimes\nabla_{v}\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}-\nabla_{v}\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}\otimes v-(v\cdot\nabla_{v}\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}})\Id\big].\end{split}

We note that

(6.55)

\|\Id-v\otimes v\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\approx 1,

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator matrix norm.
Using
Lemma

5.6
and
Lemma

5.7
repeatedly, we find that

\begin{split}[(D_{p}^{2}f_{z_{0}})(t,x,\psi(v))&]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\lesssim[D_{v}^{2}\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}+\|\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}.\end{split}

Applying
Lemma

5.4
, with constants bounded universally because
r\leq 1
, we then have

\begin{split}[D_{p}^{2}f_{z_{0}}(t,x,p)]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}&\lesssim[\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}+\|\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})},\\
&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{(2+\alpha)/2}[g]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})},\end{split}

after using the definition (
5.5
) of
[\cdot]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}
as well as (
6.54
) and the fact that
Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}\subset P(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})
.

The other terms that make up the seminorm
[f]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}
are easier to handle, since the change of variables
p\mapsto\varphi(p)
does not affect
t
or
x
. In particular, proceeding in a similar manner to the proof of
Lemma

5.2
, we have for
(t,x,p),(t_{1},x,p)\in\widetilde{Q}_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}
and

,

\begin{split}|\langle p\rangle(t-t_{1})|^{\beta/2}+|p(t-t_{1})|^{\beta/3}&=\langle p\rangle^{\beta/2}|t-t_{1}|^{\beta/2}+\langle p\rangle^{\beta/3}|(p/\langle p\rangle)(t-t_{1})|^{\beta/3}\\
&\geq\left(|t-t_{1}|^{\beta/2}+|v(t-t_{1})|^{\beta/3}\right),\end{split}

since
|v|\leq 1
. Here, as above,
v=\varphi(p)
. We then have

\begin{split}[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{L},t}(\widetilde{Q}_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\leq[\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{G},t}(P(Q_{r^{\prime}/2}))}=[g]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{G},t}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}.\end{split}

A similar argument shows
[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(\widetilde{Q}_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\leq[\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(P(Q_{r^{\prime}/2}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{(2+\alpha)/6}[g]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}
. Combining these upper bounds, we have shown

(6.56)

[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{(2+\alpha)/2}\left([g]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime}/2})}\right).

We now address the terms on the right-hand side of (
6.52
). We clearly have
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}\leq\|f_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}
. Next, using
Lemma

5.4
, we have

(6.57)

[s_{P}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}[\widetilde{s}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}\|s_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r})}.

Recalling that
\widetilde{A}_{z_{0}}
is defined as in (
6.3
), with
A_{z_{0}}
replacing
A
, we have

(6.58)

\|A_{P}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle\|\widetilde{A}_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle\|A_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{Q}_{r})}.

Since the functions

and

are uniformly Lipschitz in

, independently of

Lemma

5.4
and
Lemma

5.7
,

\begin{split}[A_{P}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{1+\alpha/2}[\widetilde{A}_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r})}\\
&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{1+\alpha/2}\left([A_{z_{0}}(t,x,\psi(v))]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r})}+\|A_{z_{0}}(t,x,\psi(v))\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\right)\\
&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{1+\alpha/2}\|A_{z_{0}}(t,x,p)\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r})}.\end{split}

Arguing similarly for

, we find

(6.59)

\|B_{P}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r^{\prime}})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{(1+\alpha)/2}\|\widetilde{B}_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{G}}(Q_{r})}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{(1+\alpha)/2}\left(\|B_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r})}+\|A_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\widetilde{Q}_{r})}\right).

Combining our upper bounds (
6
), (
6.59
), and (

) for, respectively,

,

, and

with (

and

[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\omega}\left(\|s_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R})}+\left(\|A_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R})}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}+\|B_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R})}^{2+\alpha}\right)\|f_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R})}\right),

for some (different)

that depends on

To put this estimate in terms of

, we first apply the Hölder estimates in

6.4
to obtain

[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\omega}\left(\|s\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}+\left(\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}^{3+\alpha+\frac{2}{\alpha}}+\|B\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}^{2+\alpha}\right)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\right).

For the left side of this inequality, recall that

\begin{split}f(z)&=f_{z_{0}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z)\\
&=f_{z_{0}}(\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0}),(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-p_{0}(t-t_{0}),p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle p\rangle).\end{split}

Calculations similar to the proof of

6.4
then show that

\begin{split}\partial_{p_{i}}\partial_{p_{j}}f(z)&=-(p_{0}\cdot\nabla_{p}f_{z_{0}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z))\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}\left(I-\frac{p\otimes p}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\right)+S^{t}(p)D_{p}^{2}f_{z_{0}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z)S(p),\\

and

\begin{split}[D_{p}^{2}f]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle\|\nabla_{p}f_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}\|D_{p}^{2}f_{z_{0}}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\\
&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}\left([D_{p}^{2}f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}+\|f_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\right),\end{split}

by

5.6
.
To estimate the seminorm
[f]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{L},t}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}
, we write, for any
(t_{1},x,p),(t_{2},x,p)\in Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0})
,

\begin{split}\frac{|f(t_{1},x,p)-f(t_{2},x,p)|}{|\langle p\rangle(t_{1}-t_{2})|^{\beta}+|p(t_{1}-t_{2})|^{2\beta/3}}&\approx\frac{|f(t_{1},x,p)-f(t_{2},x,p)|}{d_{\mathcal{L}}((t_{1},x,p),(t_{2},x,p))^{2\beta}}\\
&=\frac{|f_{z_{0}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(t_{1},x,p))-f(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(t_{2},x,p))|}{d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(t_{1},x,p),z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(t_{2},x,p))^{2\beta}},\end{split}

which implies
[f]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{L},t}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\approx[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{\beta}_{\mathcal{L},t}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}
. A similar argument, using

(6.62)

shows that
[f]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\approx[f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{(2+\alpha)/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}
. We conclude

(6.63)

[f]_{C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}\left([f_{z_{0}}]_{C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}+\|f_{z_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r^{\prime\prime}/2})}\right).

Combining this with (
6.61
) and defining
R^{\prime}=r^{\prime\prime}/2=\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(R/\sqrt{1+R^{2}})
, we have established (
6.39
), the first conclusion of the theorem.

The proof of the second conclusion (
6.41
) follows the same outline, starting with
Proposition

6.2
applied to
g
and translating to
\widetilde{f}_{z_{0}}
,
f_{z_{0}}
, and
f
as above. One uses the

estimates for the coefficients in

6.4
, along with formulas like

\partial_{v_{i}}\widetilde{s}_{z_{0}}=\partial_{v_{i}}\psi_{j}(v)\partial_{p_{j}}s_{z_{0}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|v|^{2}}}\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{v_{i}v_{j}}{1-|v|^{2}}\right)\partial_{p_{j}}s_{z_{0}},

which contribute factors that are

since

is close to the origin. One also must keep track of extra powers of

arising from the chain rule applied to the transformation

We omit the details of the proof of (

7.1.

In this subsection, we show that the coefficients

\Omega_{r}(z_{0}):=\{(t,x):(t,x,p)\in Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0})\text{ for some $p$}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}.

Let us also record two useful facts that we use repeatedly:

(7.2)

\displaystyle\big|p\langle q\rangle-q\langle p\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=|(p-q)\langle q\rangle+q(\langle q\rangle-\langle p\rangle)|\lesssim\langle q\rangle|p-q|,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q

\displaystyle=(\langle p\rangle-\langle q\rangle)\langle q\rangle+q\cdot(q-p)+1\lesssim\langle q\rangle\big(1+|p-q|\big),

where we used (
5.43
) in the second line.

Since the three coefficients
a^{g}
,
b^{g}
, and
c^{g}
feature integral kernels with the same order of singularity, the key calculation of these Hölder estimates is the same for all three coefficients. This calculation is encoded in the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
K(p,q):\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be an integral kernel satisfying, for all
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(7.4)

K(p,q)\leq C\langle q\rangle^{k_{1}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|}\right)

and

(7.5)

|\nabla_{p}K(p,q)|\leq C\langle q\rangle^{k_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right),

for some constant
C
. Assume that
g:\Omega_{r}(z_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(7.6)

\|\langle p\rangle^{k_{0}}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}<\infty,

for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
r\in(0,1]
, and
k_{0}>\max\{k_{1}+3+\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}},k_{2}+1\}
. Then the function

(7.7)

u(t,x,p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(p,q)g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}q,

satisfies

(7.8)

\|u\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\alpha}\|\langle p\rangle^{k_{0}}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}.

Proof.

First, we write

\begin{split}u(z)&-u(z_{0})=u(t,x,p)-u(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(K(p,q)g(t,x,q)-K(p_{0},q)g(t_{0},x_{0},q)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(K(p,q)-K(p_{0},q)\Big)g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}q+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(p_{0},q)\Big(g(t,x,q)-g(t_{0},x_{0},q)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(p-p_{0})\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{p}K(\xi p+(1-\xi)p_{0},q)g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(p_{0},q)\Big(g(t,x,q)-g(t_{0},x_{0},q)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}q.\end{split}

Fubini’s theorem and our assumptions on
K
and
\nabla_{p}K
yield

(7.9)

\displaystyle|u(z)-u(z_{0})|

\displaystyle\lesssim|p-p_{0}|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle q\rangle^{k_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|\xi p+(1-\xi)p_{0}-q|^{2}}\right)g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}q\right)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

(7.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle q\rangle^{k_{1}-k_{0}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p_{0}-q|}\right)\Big(\langle q\rangle^{k_{0}}g(t,x,q)-\langle q\rangle^{k_{0}}g(t_{0},x_{0},q)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}q

(7.11)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

To estimate
I_{1}
, we use the uniform upper bound
g(t,x,q)\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{k_{0}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}\langle q\rangle^{-k_{0}}
and the integral estimate: for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(7.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\langle q\rangle^{k_{2}-k_{0}}}{|a-q|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}q\lesssim 1,

which follows since, by assumption,
k_{0}>k_{2}+1
. This yields

(7.13)

I_{1}\lesssim|p-p_{0}|\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{k_{0}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}.

Applying
Lemma

5.5
, we have

(7.14)

I_{1}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{k_{0}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{k_{0}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}},

since
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})<r<1
.

To estimate
I_{2}
, we use the fact that
\langle p\rangle^{k_{0}}g\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))
to write

(7.15)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim[\langle p\rangle^{k_{0}}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle q\rangle^{k_{1}-k_{0}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p_{0}-q|}\right)d^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,q),(t_{0},x_{0},q))\,\mathrm{d}q.

Note that

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,q),(t_{0},x_{0},q))&=|\langle q\rangle(t-t_{0})-q\cdot(x-x_{0})|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\\
&\qquad+|(x-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle q\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}-q\cdot(t-t_{0})|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}.\end{split}

To obtain an expression involving
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})
, we start with the first term:

\begin{split}|\langle q\rangle(t-t_{0})-q\cdot(x-x_{0})|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}&\lesssim|\langle p_{0}\rangle(t-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(x-x_{0})|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+|p_{0}-q|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(|t-t_{0}|+|x-x_{0}|)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.\end{split}

For the second term in (
7.1
), we must be careful to note that the vectors
(x-x_{0})_{\perp}
and
(x-x_{0})_{\parallel}
are defined in terms of the direction
\hat{q}=q/|q|
. To avoid confusion, let us introduce the notation, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(7.16)

(x-x_{0})_{\parallel,a}=\hat{a}\cdot(x-x_{0})\hat{a}\quad\text{ and }\quad(x-x_{0})_{\perp,a}=(x-x_{0})-(x-x_{0})_{\parallel,a}.

Using only elementary computations and the triangle inequality, we then have

\begin{split}&|(x-x_{0})_{\perp,q}+\langle q\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel,q}-q\cdot(t-t_{0})|\\
&=|(I-\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q})(x-x_{0})+\langle q\rangle(\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q})(x-x_{0})-q(t-t_{0})|\\
&=|(I-\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})(x-x_{0})+\langle p_{0}\rangle(\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})(x-x_{0})-p_{0}(t-t_{0})+(\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0}-\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q})(x-x_{0})\\
&\qquad\quad+[\langle p_{0}\rangle(\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}-\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})+(\langle p_{0}\rangle-\langle q\rangle)\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}](x-x_{0})+(p_{0}-q)(t-t_{0})|\\
&\leq|(I-\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})(x-x_{0})+\langle p_{0}\rangle(\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})(x-x_{0})-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|\\
&\quad+|(\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0}-\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q})(x-x_{0})\\
&\qquad\quad+[\langle p_{0}\rangle(\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}-\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})+(\langle p_{0}\rangle-\langle q\rangle)\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}](x-x_{0})+(p_{0}-q)(t-t_{0})|\\
&=|(x-x_{0})_{\perp,p_{0}}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel,p_{0}}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|\\
&\quad+|(\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0}-\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q})(x-x_{0})\\
&\qquad\quad+[\langle p_{0}\rangle(\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}-\hat{p}_{0}\otimes\hat{p}_{0})+(\langle p_{0}\rangle-\langle q\rangle)\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}](x-x_{0})+(p_{0}-q)(t-t_{0})|\\
&\leq|(x-x_{0})_{\perp,p_{0}}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(x-x_{0})_{\parallel,p_{0}}-p_{0}(t-t_{0})|+(\langle p_{0}\rangle+|p_{0}-q|)|x-x_{0}|+|p_{0}-q||t-t_{0}|\\
&\leq d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{3}+(\langle p_{0}\rangle+|p_{0}-q|)(|x-x_{0}|+|t-t_{0}|).\end{split}

With
Lemma

5.5
, we now have

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{L}}((t,x,q),(t_{0},x_{0},q))&\lesssim d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})+|p_{0}-q|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(|x-x_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+|t-t_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\\
&\qquad+d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})+(\langle p_{0}\rangle+|p_{0}-q|)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}(|x-x_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}+|t-t_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}})\\
&\lesssim d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{2/3}\langle p_{0}\rangle\left(1+|p_{0}-q|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right),\end{split}

where we kept only the smallest exponent of
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})
, since
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})<r\leq 1
. Using this in our above estimate for
I_{2}
, we obtain

\begin{split}I_{2}&\lesssim[\langle p\rangle^{k_{0}}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle q\rangle^{k_{1}-k_{0}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p_{0}-q|}\right)\left(1+|p_{0}-q|^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&\lesssim[\langle p\rangle^{k_{0}}g]_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}d_{\mathcal{L}}(z,z_{0})^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\alpha},\end{split}

since
k_{0}>k_{1}+3+\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}
.

Combining our estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
completes the proof.
∎

Next, we estimate the gradient of the integral kernel appearing in
c^{g}
, to guarantee
Lemma

7.1
is applicable. This estimate for
\nabla_{p}G(p,q)
will also be used in the estimates for
a^{g}
and
b^{g}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
G(p,q)
be defined as in
Lemma

3.1
.
Then for any
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the gradient estimate

(7.17)

|\nabla_{p}G(p,q)|\leq C\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{7}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right),

holds, where the constant
C>0
is independent of
p
and
q
.

Proof.

Recalling that
\tau(\tau-2)=(\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q)^{2}-1
and differentiating
G(p,q)
in
p
, we obtain after a direct calculation

\begin{split}\nabla_{p}G(p,q)&=\frac{1}{\langle q\rangle}\nabla_{p}\left(\frac{\langle q\rangle-(p\cdot q)\langle p\rangle^{-1}}{\sqrt{\left(\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q\right)^{2}-1}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{3}\langle q\rangle}\frac{(p\cdot q)p-q\langle p\rangle^{2}}{\left(\tau(\tau-2)\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}-\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{2}\langle q\rangle}\frac{\Big(\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q\Big)^{2}\Big(p\langle q\rangle-q\langle p\rangle\Big)}{\left(\tau(\tau-2)\right)^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}\\
&=:g_{1}-g_{2}.\end{split}

From
Lemma

2.3
and
\tau\geq 1
, we have

(7.18)

\tau(\tau-2)\geq\frac{|p-q|^{2}}{2\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}.

This implies

\begin{split}|g_{1}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\frac{\left|(p\cdot q)p-q\langle p\rangle^{2}\right|}{|p-q|}\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle^{2}\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle^{\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\frac{1}{|p-q|}\lesssim\frac{\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{|p-q|},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}|g_{2}|&\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\frac{\Big(\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q\Big)^{2}\Big|p\langle q\rangle-q\langle p\rangle\Big|}{|p-q|^{3}}.\end{split}

To complete the estimate of
g_{2}
, we need to use (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), which yield

\begin{split}|g_{2}|&\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\frac{|p-q|\langle q\rangle^{3}+|p-q|^{3}\langle q\rangle^{3}}{|p-q|^{3}}\\
&\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{7}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right).\end{split}

Combining the upper bounds for
|g_{1}|
and
|g_{2}|
, and using
|p-q|^{-1}\lesssim 1+|p-q|^{-2}
, we conclude the proof.
∎

The next three lemmas establish the desired Hölder estimates for
c^{g}
,
b^{g}
, and
a^{g}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

If

(7.19)

\langle p\rangle^{k}g\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0})),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
k>\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}
, then there holds

(7.20)

\|c^{g}(z)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{3}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))},

where
c^{g}
is defined in (
2.8
), and
C
is a constant independent of
z_{0}
,
r
, and
g
.

Proof.

We begin by estimating the Hölder norm of the function
\kappa(p)
. From the proof of
Lemma

3.2
, we have
\kappa(p)\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-1}
, which is equivalent to

(7.21)

\int_{0}^{\pi}(1+|p|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-2}.

Using this, we have

\begin{split}\kappa(p)-\kappa(p_{0})&=2^{\nicefrac{{7}}{{2}}}\pi\Big(\langle p\rangle-\langle p_{0}\rangle\Big)\int_{0}^{\pi}(1+|p|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\qquad\qquad+2^{\nicefrac{{7}}{{2}}}\pi\langle p_{0}\rangle\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big((1+|p|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}-(1+|p_{0}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\Big)\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\lesssim|p-p_{0}|\langle p\rangle^{-2}+\langle p_{0}\rangle\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big((1+|p|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}-(1+|p_{0}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}\Big)\sin\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta.\end{split}

For
g(s)=(1+s\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}
, we have
g^{\prime}(s)=-(\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}})(1+s\sin^{2}\theta)^{-\nicefrac{{5}}{{2}}}\sin^{2}\theta
with
\sup|g^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim 1
, where the supremum is taken for values of
s
between
|p|^{2}
and
|p_{0}|^{2}
. Therefore,

(7.22)

\kappa(p)-\kappa(p_{0})\lesssim|p-p_{0}|\langle p\rangle^{-2}+\langle p_{0}\rangle|p-p_{0}|\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle|p-p_{0}|.

Together with
\kappa(p_{0})\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-1}
, this implies

\begin{split}\kappa(p)g(z)-\kappa(p_{0})g(z_{0})&=\Big(\kappa(p)-\kappa(p_{0})\Big)g(z)+\kappa(p_{0})\Big(g(z)-g(z_{0})\Big)\\
&\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle|p-p_{0}|g(z)+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-1}\Big(g(z)-g(z_{0})\Big),\end{split}

and with estimate (
5.39
) from
Lemma

5.5
, we have shown

(7.23)

\|\kappa(p)g\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\|\kappa(p)g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{3}\|g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q^{\rm rel}_{r}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{3}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}.

Next, we address the term

(7.24)

c_{1}(z)=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G(p,q)g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}q,

where
G(p,q)
. From
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

7.2
, we see that
G(p,q)
satisfies the hypotheses of
Lemma

7.1
with
k_{1}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and
k_{2}=\nicefrac{{7}}{{2}}
. Therefore, we have

(7.25)

\|c_{1}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\alpha}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))},

with
k=k_{0}>\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}
. Combining this with our estimate of
\kappa(p)g
above, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

If

(7.26)

\langle p\rangle^{k}g\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0})),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
k>\nicefrac{{11}}{{2}}
, then there holds

(7.27)

\|b^{g}(z)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{1+\alpha}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))},

where
b^{g}
is defined in (
2.6
), and
C
is a constant independent of
z_{0}
,
r
, and
g
.

Proof.

For
j\in\{1,2,3\}
, let us denote by
H_{j}
the integral kernel appearing in the definition of
b^{g}_{j}
:

(7.28)

H_{j}(p,q)=\Lambda(p,q)(\tau-2)(p_{j}+q_{j})=G(p,q)\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}(p_{j}+q_{j}).

Our goal is to apply
Lemma

7.1
to

(7.29)

K_{j}(p,q)=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}H_{j}(p,q)

with
k_{1}=\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}
and
k_{2}=\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}
in order to conclude that

(7.30)

u_{j}(z):=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}b_{j}^{g}(z)

satisfies

(7.31)

\|u_{j}(z)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\alpha}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))},

whenever
k>\nicefrac{{11}}{{2}}
. After applying
Lemma

5.7
to
b_{j}^{g}(z)=\langle p\rangle u_{j}(z)
, we have the desired conclusion.

To complete the proof, it, thus, suffices to check the assumptions of
Lemma

7.1
. We begin by examining
H_{j}
.
From
Lemma

3.1
and
\nicefrac{{(\tau-1)}}{{\tau}}\leq 1
, we have the following upper bound (more crude than the bound used in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
):

(7.32)

|H_{j}(p,q)|\leq\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle|G(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|}.

We deduce that

(7.33)

|K_{j}(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{\langle q\rangle}{|p-q|}.

Next we consider the gradient:

(7.34)

\nabla_{p}K_{j}(p,q)=\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle^{3}}H_{j}(p,q)+\langle p\rangle\nabla_{p}H_{j}(p,q).

The first term clearly satisfies (
7.4
) with
k_{2}=\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}
due to (
7.32
). Hence, we need only understand
\nabla_{p}H_{j}
. We have

\begin{split}\nabla_{p}H_{j}(p,q)&=\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}(p_{j}+q_{j})\nabla_{p}G(p,q)+G(p,q)\,(p_{j}+q_{j})\,\tau^{-2}\,\nabla_{p}\tau+G(p,q)\,\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}e_{j}\\
&=:h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}.\end{split}

From
Lemma

7.2
and
\nicefrac{{(\tau-1)}}{{\tau}}\leq 1
, we have

\begin{split}|h_{1}|\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right).\end{split}

To estimate
h_{2}
, note that

(7.35)

\nabla_{p}\tau=\nabla_{p}\left(\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q+1\right)=\langle q\rangle\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}-q=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle}\big(\langle q\rangle p-\langle p\rangle q\big).

Then, by (
7.2
), we have
|\nabla_{p}\tau|\lesssim|p-q|
. Since
\tau^{-1}\leq 1
, we therefore have

\begin{split}|h_{2}|\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle|p-q|G(p,q)\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle,\end{split}

after using
Lemma

3.1
to bound
G(p,q)
.
Finally,

(7.36)

|h_{3}|\lesssim|G(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|p-q|}.

Combining the estimates of
h_{1},h_{2}
, and
h_{3}
yields

(7.37)

|\nabla_{p}H_{j}(p,q)|\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right),

as claimed. We deduce that

(7.38)

|\nabla_{p}K_{j}(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{2}}\frac{1}{|p-q|}+\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right)\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right).

Hence, the assumptions of
Lemma

7.1
are met, and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

If

(7.39)

\langle p\rangle^{k}g\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0})),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
k>\nicefrac{{11}}{{2}}
, then there holds, for each
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
,

(7.40)

\|a_{ij}^{g}(z)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{4+\alpha}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))},

where
a_{ij}^{g}
is defined in (
2.3
) and
C
is a constant independent of
z_{0}
,
r
, and
g
.

Proof.

Fix
i,j
, and define

(7.41)

K(p,q)=\langle p\rangle^{-4}\Phi^{ij}(p,q).

Our goal is to apply
Lemma

7.1
with
k_{1}=1
and
k_{2}=\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}
to deduce that

(7.42)

\|u_{ij}\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{r}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\alpha}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}g\|_{C_{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}(\Omega_{r}(z_{0}))}\quad\text{ where }u_{ij}=\int K_{ij}(p,q)g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}q.

The conclusion follows after applying
Lemma

5.7
to
a^{g}_{ij}=\langle p\rangle^{4}u_{ij}.
Hence, the remainder of the proof is checking that
K_{ij}
satisfies the hypotheses of
Lemma

7.1
. For simplicity, we drop the
ij
sub-script for
K_{ij}
in the sequel.

From
Lemma

2.2
, we have the following upper bound for all
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

(7.43)

K(p,q)=\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{4}}|\Phi^{ij}(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle^{3}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|}\right).

Next, we analyze the
p
-derivatives of
K
:

(7.44)

|\nabla_{p}K|\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{-5}\Phi^{ij}+\langle p\rangle^{-4}|\nabla_{p}\Phi^{ij}|.

We aim to bound the right hand side above by
\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}(1+|p-q|^{-2}).
This is simple for the first term in (
7.44
) due to (
7.43
). The second term is more difficult.
Let us note that our upper bounds in the proof of
Lemma

3.4
are not easily applicable here, because those bounds involved a sum over first derivatives, while we need to bound each individual first derivative.

Instead, we take advantage of our earlier estimates for
G(p,q)
, by using the definition (
1
) of
\Phi^{ij}
to write:

\begin{split}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)&=G(p,q)\Psi^{ij}(p,q),\\
\Psi^{ij}(p,q)&=(\tau-1)\delta_{ij}+\frac{\tau-1}{\tau(\tau-2)}(p_{i}-q_{i})(p_{j}-q_{j})+\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}(p_{i}q_{j}+p_{j}q_{i}).\end{split}

By direct calculation, for any
k\in\{1,2,3\}
,

\begin{split}\partial_{p_{k}}\Psi^{ij}(p,q)&=(\partial_{p_{k}}\tau)\delta_{ij}+\partial_{p_{k}}\left[\frac{\tau-1}{\tau(\tau-2)}\right](p_{i}-q_{i})(p_{j}-q_{j})+\frac{\tau-1}{\tau(\tau-2)}[\delta_{ik}(p_{j}-q_{j})+\delta_{kj}(p_{i}-q_{i})]\\
&\quad+\frac{\partial_{p_{k}}\tau}{\tau^{2}}(p_{i}q_{j}+p_{j}q_{i})+\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}(\delta_{ik}q_{j}+\delta_{kj}q_{i})\\
&=:\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}+\ell_{4}+\ell_{5}.\end{split}

Recalling
\tau=\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q+1
and
\partial_{p_{k}}\tau=\langle p\rangle^{-1}(\langle q\rangle p_{k}-\langle p\rangle q_{k})
, we have

(7.45)

|\ell_{1}|\lesssim|\partial_{p_{k}}\tau|\lesssim\langle q\rangle.

For the term
\ell_{2}
, a straightforward calculation shows

(7.46)

\partial_{p_{k}}\left[\frac{\tau-1}{\tau(\tau-2)}\right]=-\partial_{p_{k}}\tau\left(\frac{\tau^{2}-2\tau+2}{\tau^{2}(\tau-2)^{2}}\right).

Let us also recall from (
7.2
) that
|\partial_{p_{k}}\tau|\lesssim|p-q|
. These two facts imply

(7.47)

|\ell_{2}|\lesssim|\partial_{p_{k}}\tau|\frac{\langle p\rangle^{2}\langle q\rangle^{2}}{\tau^{2}(\tau-2)^{2}}|p-q|^{2}\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle^{4}\langle q\rangle^{4}}{|p-q|},

where we used
(\tau-2)\gtrsim\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle q\rangle^{-1}|p-q|^{2}
from
Lemma

2.3
, as well as
\tau\geq 1
. Next, using the same lower bound for
(\tau-2)
, we have

(7.48)

|\ell_{3}|\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle|\tau-1|}{|p-q|^{2}}|\delta_{ik}(p_{j}-q_{j})+\delta_{kj}(p_{i}-q_{i})|\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle^{2}\langle q\rangle^{2}}{|p-q|}.

For
\ell_{4}
, using
|\partial_{p_{k}}\tau|\lesssim\langle q\rangle
and
\tau\geq 1
, we simply have

(7.49)

|\ell_{4}|\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle^{2}.

Finally, since
(\tau-1)/\tau\leq 1
, we have

(7.50)

|\ell_{5}|\lesssim\langle q\rangle.

Summing up, we have shown

(7.51)

|\nabla_{p}\Psi^{ij}(p,q)|\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{4}\langle q\rangle^{4}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|}\right).

We also note, using again the fact that
(\tau-2)\gtrsim|p-q|^{2}\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle q\rangle^{-1}
, that

(7.52)

|\Psi^{ij}(p,q)|\lesssim\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle.

With these bounds for
\Psi^{ij}(p,q)
as well as
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

7.2
, we have

(7.53)

|\nabla_{p}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)|\leq G(p,q)|\nabla_{p}\Psi^{ij}(p,q)|+|\nabla_{p}G(p,q)\Psi^{ij}(p,q)|\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{4}\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|^{2}}\right),

keeping only the largest exponents of
\langle p\rangle
and
\langle q\rangle
.

Combining (
7.53
) with (
7.44
) and (
7.43
), we find

(7.54)

|\nabla_{p}K|\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{\nicefrac{{9}}{{2}}}\left(1+|p-q|^{-2}\right).

Hence,
Lemma

7.1
applies, and the proof is complete.
∎

7.2.
Higher derivative estimates

As mentioned in the introduction, when differentiating the relativistic Landau equation to apply regularity estimates,
p
-derivatives falling on the integral kernels (such as
\Phi^{ij}(p,q)
) cannot be easily transferred to
f
because the integrals are not true convolutions. In particular,
\partial_{p_{k}}a^{f}\neq a^{\partial_{p_{k}}f}
, unlike in the classical Landau case. This is a problem because higher
p
derivatives of
\Phi^{ij}
feature higher-order singularities at
p=q
, which eventually will not be integrable.

To get around this, we use an integration by parts technique developed by Strain and Guo
[
43
]
to partially transfer a higher
p
derivative from
\Phi^{ij}
to
f
, with all resulting terms featuring only a first order singularity.

Following
[
43
]
, we define, for any three-dimensional multi-index
\beta=(\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\beta^{3})
, the differential operator
\Theta_{\beta}
according to

(7.55)

\Theta_{\beta}:=\left(\partial_{p_{3}}+\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{3}}\right)^{\beta^{3}}\left(\partial_{p_{2}}+\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{2}}\right)^{\beta^{2}}\left(\partial_{p_{1}}+\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{1}}\right)^{\beta^{1}}.

This operator is well-adapted to the relativistic structure of our equation, in the sense that the Lorentzian inner product
\tau-1=\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q
is in the null space of
\Theta_{\beta}

(7.56)

\Theta_{\beta}(\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle-p\cdot q)=0

for any nontrivial multi-index
\beta
.

The following result is similar to
[
43
, Theorem 3]
, but without an exponentially decaying weight in
q
:

Proposition 7.6
.

Given
\beta
a multi-index with
|\beta|>0
, a function
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}^{6}\to\mathbb{R}
that is sufficiently smooth away from
p=q
, and a sufficiently smooth, rapidly decaying function
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{6}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we have

(7.57)

\partial_{p}^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma(p,q)\mu(p,q)\,\mathrm{d}q=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}\leq\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma(p,q)\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu(p,q)\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}(p,q)\,\mathrm{d}q,

where
\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}(p,q)
is a smooth function which satisfies, for any multi-indices
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
,

(7.58)

|\partial_{q}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{p}^{\nu_{2}}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}(p,q)|\leq C\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta|-|\nu_{2}|}.

The constant
C
depends on
\beta
,
\nu_{1}
, and
\nu_{2}
.

In particular, if
\mu(p,q)
is a function of
q
only, then we have the simpler formula

(7.59)

\partial_{p}^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma(p,q)\mu(q)\,\mathrm{d}q=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\leq\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma(p,q)\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\mu(q)\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}(p,q)\,\mathrm{d}q,

where
\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}(p,q)
is a smooth function satisfying

(7.60)

|\partial_{q}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{p}^{\nu_{2}}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}(p,q)|\leq C\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|-|\beta|-|\nu_{2}|},

for all multi-indices
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
.

By “sufficiently smooth,” we mean that all derivatives on the right-hand side are well-defined, except possibly at
p=q
in the case of derivatives of
\Gamma
.

In the current article, we only need the case where
\mu(p,q)
is independent of
p
, but we include the general version for the sake of potential future applications.

Proof.

First, let us note the useful fact that for any multi-index
\nu
,
|\partial^{\nu}\langle p\rangle^{k}|\lesssim\langle p\rangle^{k-|\nu|}
. This relation is implicitly used many
times in the current proof, in conjunction with the Leibnitz rule, when we estimate the
\phi
coefficients.

The conclusion is shown by induction on the size of
\beta
. Assume first that
\beta=e_{i}
. Then we have

(7.61)

\partial_{p_{i}}=-\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{i}}+\Theta_{e_{i}},

and so

\begin{split}\partial_{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\mu\,\mathrm{d}q&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\partial_{p_{i}}\mu\,\mathrm{d}q+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu\left(-\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{i}}+\Theta_{e_{i}}\right)\Gamma\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\partial_{p_{i}}\mu\,\mathrm{d}q+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{i}}\mu\,\mathrm{d}q+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\frac{q_{i}}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle}\mu\,\mathrm{d}q+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{e_{i}}\Gamma\mu\,\mathrm{d}q.\end{split}

This can be put into the form (
7.57
) with

(7.62)

\phi^{e_{i}}_{0,0,0}=\frac{q_{i}}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle},\quad\phi^{e_{i}}_{e_{i},0,0}=\phi^{e_{i}}_{0,0,e_{i}}=1,\quad\text{ and }\quad\phi^{e_{i}}_{0,e_{i},0}=\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle},

which satisfy the decay condition of (
7.58
).

By induction, we assume the conclusion holds for all multi-indices
\beta
with
|\beta|\leq n
. Assume now that
\beta^{\prime}
is such that
|\beta^{\prime}|=n+1
, and let
\beta
be such that
\beta^{\prime}=\beta+e_{m}

where
m=\max\{j:(\beta^{\prime})^{j}>0\}
. This assumption on
m
and
\beta
is needed based on the order of the variables appearing in (
7.55
). Then we have

(7.63)

\partial_{p}^{\beta^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\mu\,\mathrm{d}q=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}\leq\beta}\partial_{p_{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}q.

Proceeding as in the base case, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{p}^{\beta^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Gamma\mu\,\mathrm{d}q&=\sum\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma\partial_{p_{m}}\left(\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&\quad\quad+\sum\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(-\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{m}}+\Theta_{e_{m}}\right)\left(\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma\right)\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&=\sum\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}+e_{m}}\mu\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}q+\sum\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}+e_{m}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&\quad\quad+\sum\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}+e_{m}}\Gamma\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}q+\sum\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}\Gamma\partial_{q}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{3}}\mu\widetilde{\Theta}_{m}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\,\mathrm{d}q\\
&=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4},\end{split}

where

(7.64)

\widetilde{\Theta}_{m}:=\partial_{p_{m}}+\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{m}}+\frac{q_{m}}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle}.

Note that we needed our assumption on
e_{m}
in order to combine
\Theta_{e_{m}}\Theta_{\beta_{1}}
into
\Theta_{\beta_{1}+e_{m}}
without producing commutators. We see then
that this can be combined into the form (
7.57
), with corresponding coefficients. To verify that (
7.58
) holds, we need to check that the bound applies to each
coefficient in the above sum.

The terms in the summation given by
S_{1}
are indexed by
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
, whose sum is less than
\beta
. Because of the extra
p
-derivative hitting
\mu
, each such
term now contributes to the summation in (
7.57
) in the term indexed by
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}+e_{m})
, whose sum is less than
\beta+e_{m}=\beta^{\prime}
. Thus,
S_{1}
contributes a coefficient of the form
\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}
into the cumulative coefficient
\phi^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}+e_{m}}
. By induction, we have

(7.65)

|\partial_{q}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{p}^{\nu_{2}}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}|\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta|-|\nu_{2}|}\approx\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}+e_{m}|-|\beta^{\prime}|-|\nu_{2}|},

which is the desired upper bound, since
|\beta^{\prime}|=|\beta|+1
.

The terms in the summation given by
S_{2}
contribute to the summation in (
7.57
) in the term indexed by
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}+e_{m},\beta_{3})
, and adds a term of the form

\langle q\rangle\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}/\langle p\rangle
into the cumulative coefficient
\phi^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}+e_{m},\beta_{3}}
. Inductively,

(7.66)

\left|\partial_{q}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{p}^{\nu_{2}}\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\right|\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|+1}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta|-|\nu_{2}|-1}\approx\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}+e_{m}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta^{\prime}|-|\nu_{2}|},

which again has the correct decay rate.

The terms in
S_{3}
contribute to (
7.57
) in the term indexed by
(\beta_{1}+e_{m},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
and add a term of the form

\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}
into the cumulative coefficient
\phi^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta_{1}+e_{m},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}
. The upper bound (
7.58
)
again follows inductively from

(7.67)

|\partial_{q}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{p}^{\nu_{2}}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}|\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta|-|\nu_{2}|}\approx\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}+e_{m}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta^{\prime}|-|\nu_{2}|}.

Lastly, the terms in
S_{4}
contribute to (
7.57
) at the index
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
(this is the reason why the sum must have
\leq\beta
and not
=\beta
, since
each integration by parts creates lower-order terms), and adds a term of the form
\widetilde{\Theta}_{m}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}
into the coefficient
\phi^{\beta^{\prime}}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}
. We then finish our proof of (
7.58
) by the inductive estimate

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{q}^{\nu_{1}}\partial_{p}^{\nu_{2}}\left(\partial_{p_{m}}+\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle}\partial_{q_{m}}+\frac{q_{m}}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle}\phi^{\beta}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}}\right)\right|&\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta|-|\nu_{2}|-1}\\
&\approx\langle q\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|-|\nu_{1}|}\langle p\rangle^{|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{3}|-|\beta^{\prime}|-|\nu_{2}|}.\end{split}

∎

Next, we need to control the factors of the form
\Theta_{\beta_{i}}\Gamma(p,q)
that appear on the right in
Proposition

7.6
, where
\Gamma
is chosen as the integral kernels for the coefficients
a^{g}
,
b^{g}
, and
c^{g}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
\Phi^{ij}(p,q)
be as defined in (
1
), let
G(p,q)
be defined as in
Lemma

3.1
, and let
H_{j}(p,q)
be defined in (
7.28
). Then

(7.68)

\displaystyle\left|\Theta_{\beta}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\langle q\rangle^{7}}{\langle p\rangle^{|\beta|}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|p-q|}\right),

(7.69)

\displaystyle\left|\Theta_{\beta}G(p,q)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\langle p\rangle^{\beta}}\frac{1}{|p-q|},\quad\text{ and}

(7.70)

\displaystyle\left|\Theta_{\beta}H_{j}(p,q)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle^{|\beta|-1}}\frac{1}{|p-q|},

for a constant
C>0
independent of
p
and
q
.

Proof.

The first inequality (
7.68
) follows directly from
[
43
, Lemma 2]
, which says

(7.71)

\left|\Theta_{\beta}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)\right|\leq C\langle p\rangle^{-|\beta|}\langle q\rangle^{6}\ \ \ \text{ when }\ \ \ |p-q|+|p\times q|\geq\frac{|p|+1}{2}

and

(7.72)

\left|\Theta_{\beta}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)\right|\leq C\langle q\rangle^{7}\langle p\rangle^{-|\beta|}|p-q|^{-1}\ \ \ \text{ when }\ \ \ |p-q|+|p\times q|<\frac{|p|+1}{2}.

To address
G(p,q)
, we first note that (
7.56
) implies that

(7.73)

\Theta_{e_{i}}(\tau)=\Theta_{e_{i}}(\tau-1)=\Theta_{e_{i}}(\tau-2)=0

for each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. With this, we have

(7.74)

\Theta_{\beta}G(p,q)=\Theta_{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\frac{\tau-1}{\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}\right)=\frac{\tau-1}{\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}\Theta_{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\right),

and a straightforward calculation using the definition (
7.55
) of
\Theta_{\beta}
shows (as already pointed out in
[
43
]
) that

(7.75)

\left|\Theta_{\beta}\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle^{1+|\beta|}}.

Applying
Lemma

3.1
yields

(7.76)

|\Theta_{\beta}G(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{\beta}}G(p,q)\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p\rangle^{\beta}}\frac{1}{|p-q|},

as desired.

For
H_{j}(p,q)
, proceeding similarly to our analysis of
G(p,q)
, we have

(7.77)

\Theta_{\beta}H_{j}(p,q)=\Theta_{\beta}\left(\frac{p_{j}+q_{j}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\tau\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}\right)=\frac{(\tau-1)^{2}}{\tau\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}\Theta_{\beta}\left(\frac{p_{j}+q_{j}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\right).

By another direct calculation using (
7.55
), we see that

(7.78)

\Theta_{\beta}\left(\frac{p_{j}+q_{j}}{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}\right)\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle+\langle q\rangle}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle^{1+|\beta|}},

and we obtain, using
0\leq(\tau-1)/\tau\leq 1
,

(7.79)

|\Theta_{\beta}H_{j}(p,q)|\lesssim\frac{\tau-1}{\sqrt{\tau(\tau-2)}}\frac{\langle p\rangle+\langle q\rangle}{\langle q\rangle\langle p\rangle^{1+|\beta|}}=\frac{\langle p\rangle+\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle^{|\beta|}}G(p,q)\lesssim\frac{\langle p\rangle\langle q\rangle}{\langle p\rangle^{|\beta|}}\frac{1}{|p-q|},

as claimed.
∎

We are now in a position to estimate any derivative of
a^{g}
,
b^{g}
, and
c^{g}
:

Proposition 7.8
.

Assume that some partial derivative
\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}g
of
g
exists for all
(t,x,p)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
is some open set. Furthermore, assume that for all multi-indices
\widetilde{\beta}\leq\beta_{p}
, there holds

(7.80)

|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\widetilde{\beta}}g(t,x,p)|\leq C_{k}\langle p\rangle^{-k},

for some
k>9+|\beta_{p}|
, all
(t,x,p)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and some uniform constant
C_{k}>0
.

Then the coefficients
a^{g}
,
b^{g}
, and
c^{g}
satisfy

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}a_{ij}^{g}(t,x,p)\right|&\leq C\langle p\rangle^{-|\beta_{p}|},\\
\left|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}b_{j}^{g}(t,x,p)\right|&\leq C\langle p\rangle^{1-|\beta_{p}|},\\
\left|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}c^{g}(t,x,p)\right|&\leq C\langle p\rangle^{-|\beta_{p}|},\end{split}

for a constant
C>0
depending only on
k
,
|\beta_{p}|
, and
C_{k}
.

If, in addition,

(7.81)

\|\langle p\rangle^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\widetilde{\beta}}g\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq C_{\ell},

for some
\ell>10+\max\{|\beta_{p}|,\alpha/2\}
and all
\widetilde{\beta}\leq\beta_{p}
, then

\begin{split}\|\langle p\rangle^{\min\{-\alpha,|\beta_{p}|-1\}}\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}a_{ij}^{g}(t,x,p)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq C,\\
\|\langle p\rangle^{-1+\min\{-\alpha,|\beta_{p}|-1\}}\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}b_{j}^{g}(t,x,p)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq C,\\
\|\langle p\rangle^{\min\{-\alpha,|\beta_{p}|-1\}}\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}c^{g}(t,x,p)\|_{C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq C,\\
\end{split}

for a constant

\ell
,
|\beta_{p}|
,

, and

.

Proof.

Applying

,

\begin{split}|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}&a_{ij}^{g}(t,x,p)|=\left|\partial_{p}^{\beta_{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi^{ij}(p,q)\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\beta_{x}}g(t,x,q)\,\mathrm{d}q\right|\\

Therefore, it suffices to estimate the difference

Proof of higher regularity

In this last section, we prove our main regularity estimates.

First, we prove estimates on a bounded
(t,x)
domain, which may be useful for future work in a setting where conditional assumptions on
f
such as (
1.9
) hold in some parts of the domain but not others. This will be combined with

to prove our main theorem.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
f
be a solution to the relativistic Landau equation (
1.1
) defined for
(t,x,p)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
for some open
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Assume that there is
m_{0}>0
such that

(8.1)

\inf_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,p)\,\mathrm{d}p\geq m_{0},

and that there is
k>0
such that

(8.2)

\supp_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}}\ \langle p\rangle^{k}f(t,x,p)\leq M_{k}.

Fix any multi-index
\beta
and any
\ell>0
. There is
k(\beta,\ell)
such that, if
k\geq k(\beta,\ell)
, then,
for any
\Omega^{\prime}
compactly contained in
\Omega
, there holds

(8.3)

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq C.

The constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>0
depend on
M_{k}
,
|\beta|
, and
\Omega^{\prime}
.

In particular, if
f\in L^{\infty}_{k}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})
for all

, then
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, the parameter
k
, which is the number of
L^{\infty}
-polynomial moments of

that we assume are finite, will be taken large enough for each step to be valid. We absorb
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}
norms into constants without comment.

We also use, without comment, the following fact, which follows from
Lemma

5.7
: if there is
R\in(0,1]
such that

(8.4)

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\ell}}

for all
z_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
, then
\langle p\rangle^{\ell}f\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})
.

The proof takes several steps.

Step one: weighted Hölder regularity.

We show that
\langle p\rangle^{\ell}f\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})
. Let
R>0
be chosen such that (i)
R<R_{0}
, where
R_{0}
is the constant from
Theorem

6.5
, and (ii) for any
z=(t,x,p)\in\Omega^{\prime}
, the cylinder
Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z)\subset\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
.

Now, for any
z_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p}
, we apply the
C^{\alpha}
-estimate of
Lemma

6.8
in
Q_{R^{\prime\prime}}^{\rm rel}(z_{0})
.
Recall that
R^{\prime\prime}
is defined in
Lemma

6.8
.
From
Proposition

2.1
and our assumptions (
8.1
) and (
8.2
), we see that
a^{f}
satisfies uniform upper and lower ellipticity conditions of the form

(8.5)

\lambda I\leq a^{f}(z)\leq\Lambda I,

for all
z\in Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0})
. From
Lemmas

3.2
and
3.4
.(b), we also have upper bounds on
B^{f}
and
c^{f}
in
L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))
. These estimates for the coefficients
a^{f}
,
B^{f}
,
c^{f}
depend only on
m_{0}
and the
L^{\infty}_{5}
norm of
f
. Letting
s=c^{f}f
, we also have
s\in L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))
. Therefore,
Lemma

6.8
implies

(8.6)

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R^{\prime\prime}}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\leq C\langle p_{0}\rangle^{3\alpha}\left(\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{29}}{{2}}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}+\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-5}\|c^{f}f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\right),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. This right-hand side is bounded by a constant times
\langle p_{0}\rangle^{-\ell}
, provided

(8.7)

k>\ell+3\alpha+\frac{29}{2}.

A straightforward covering argument then shows

(8.8)

\|\langle p\rangle^{k-n_{0}}f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\lesssim 1,

for some
n_{0}>0
.

Step two: initial regularity via the first Schauder estimate.

Since
f
is Hölder continuous, we can pass this regularity to
a^{f}
,
b^{f}
, and
c^{f}
via Lemmas
7.3
,
7.4
, and
7.5
. By

5.7
, this also gives control on the
C^{\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})

) from

, we obtain, for any

(8.9)

[f]_{C^{2+2\alpha/3}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R^{\prime}}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle^{k-n_{1}}},

for some

, where

(8.10)

R^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\langle p_{0}\rangle^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}}\frac{R}{\sqrt{1+R^{2}}}.

Let

(8.11)

\alpha^{\prime}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{6},\frac{2\alpha}{3}\right\}

Note that we require
\alpha^{\prime}<\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}
in the next step, which is the reason for the minimum above. Since

was arbitrary, we again use a covering argument and interpolation, if necessary, to conclude that

(8.12)

[\langle p\rangle^{k-n_{1}}f]_{C^{2+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\lesssim 1.

Step three: slightly higher regularity via the second Schauder estimate.

For this, we need better regularity of the coefficients. In particular, we want to show that the
C^{2+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}
norm of

controls the
C^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}
seminorm of

,
b^{f}
, and

\begin{split}[\nabla_{p}a^{f}]_{C^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}&\lesssim\left(\|D_{p}^{2}a^{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}+\|a^{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\right)\lesssim 1,\end{split}

by the interpolation inequality of

5.6

7.8

Next, we recall from (

[f]_{C^{2+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}

7.5
(which deals with the Hölder norm in all three variables) and using only variations in the

[a^{f}]_{C^{2(1-\varepsilon)/3}_{\mathcal{L},t}(Q_{R}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\langle p_{0}\rangle^{5-\varepsilon}\|\langle p\rangle^{k}f\|_{C^{1-\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{L},t}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}.

Keeping in mind (

\frac{(1+\alpha^{\prime})}{2}<\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{3},

we have shown that the

-seminorm of

is under control. A similar argument gives control on the
C^{(2+\alpha^{\prime})/3}_{\mathcal{L},x}
-seminorm. We have shown

(8.15)

[a^{f}]_{C^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\lesssim 1.

Applying an identical argument to
b^{f}
and
c^{f}
, we have shown that all coefficients are controlled in
C^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))
. Applying our second Schauder estimate (

) in

, we obtain

(8.16)

[f]_{C^{3+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R^{\prime\prime}}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle^{k-n_{2}}},

for some
n_{2}>0
. As above, we use a covering argument to extend this to

(8.17)

[\langle p\rangle^{k-n_{2}}f]_{C^{3+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\lesssim 1.

Note that our seminorm of order

controls at least one derivative in each variable
(t,x,p)
.

Step four: higher regularity via induction.

Next, let us assume by induction that for some
j\geq 1
,

(8.18)

\|\langle p\rangle^{k-n(j)}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{C^{\alpha(j)}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq K_{j},

for all multi-indices
\beta
with
|\beta|\leq j
. Here,
\alpha(j)\in(0,1)
and
n(j)\geq 0
depend on
j
, but in the remainder of the proof, we sometimes write
\alpha=\alpha(j)
and

to keep the notation clean.

Let
\beta
be a muti-index of order

.
Differentiating the relativistic Landau equation (
1.1
), with

written in nondivergence form as in (

), by

, we obtain

\begin{split}(\partial_{t}+\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\cdot\nabla_{x})\partial^{\beta}f=\mbox{tr}(a^{f}D_{p}^{2}\partial^{\beta}f)+b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}\partial^{\beta}f+c^{f}\partial^{\beta}f+R_{\beta}(f),\end{split}

with

R_{\beta}(f)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta\\
|\beta_{1}|>0\end{subarray}}\left(-\partial^{\beta_{1}}\left(\frac{p}{\langle p\rangle}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\beta_{2}}f+\mbox{tr}(\partial^{\beta_{1}}a^{f}D_{p}^{2}\partial^{\beta_{2}}f)+\partial^{\beta_{1}}b^{f}\cdot\nabla_{p}\partial^{\beta_{2}}f+\partial^{\beta_{1}}c^{f}\partial^{\beta_{2}}f\right).

If

have order at most

, then the sum in

has only one term, with

and

in this case as well. Also, if

is sufficiently large, the assumption (
8.18
) implies, via

7.8
, that for each
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
appearing in

, which have order at most

and
\alpha^{\prime}=\nicefrac{{2\alpha}}{{3}}
,

\begin{split}&\|\langle p\rangle^{\min\{-\alpha,|\beta_{i}|-1\}}\partial^{\beta_{i}}a^{f}\|_{C^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}+\|\langle p\rangle^{-1+\min\{-\alpha,|\beta_{i}|-1\}}\partial^{\beta_{i}}b^{f}\|_{C^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\\
&\ +\|\langle p\rangle^{\min\{-\alpha,|\beta_{i}|-1\}}\partial^{\beta_{i}}c^{f}\|_{C^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\lesssim_{K_{j}}1.\end{split}

This estimate applies to all
\partial^{\beta_{1}}
and
\partial^{\beta_{2}}
appearing in

. We therefore have

(8.20)

\|\langle p\rangle^{k-m_{1}}R_{\beta}(f)\|_{C^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\lesssim_{K_{j}}1,

for some
m_{1}>0
depending on
n
and
j
and with the implied constant above depending on
K_{j}
.

At this point, we may deduce regularity properties of

by proceeding in a similar way to the base case of our induction. The steps are as follows: for any

we we apply our first Schauder estimate (

) to equation (

) in

[\partial^{\beta}f]_{C^{2+\alpha^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R^{\prime}}^{\rm rel}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle^{k-m_{2}}},

m_{2}>0

where

. By interpolation (

5.6
), the same estimate holds with
\partial^{\beta}
replaced by

for any

. We therefore have a bound for

in

[\partial^{\beta}f]_{C^{3+\alpha^{\prime\prime}}_{\mathcal{L}}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle p_{0}\rangle^{k-m_{3}}},

m_{3}>0

[\langle p\rangle^{k-n(j+1)}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}f]_{C^{\alpha(j+1)}_{\mathcal{L}}(\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{p})}\leq K_{j+1},

Appendix A
Properties of Lorentz boosts

In this appendix, we collect several results pertaining to Lorentz boosts and relativistic distances. The first lemma shows that the Lorentz boost is re-centering relativistic cylinders.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
r>0
and
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},p_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
. Then

z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z\in Q_{r}^{rel}(z_{0})\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad z\in Q_{r}^{rel}(0).

Proof.

Suppose
z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z\in Q_{r}^{rel}(z_{0})
.
Recall that the Lorentz boost is given by (
5.1
):

(A.1)

z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z=(t_{0}+t\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\cdot x,\,\,x_{0}+x_{\perp}+x_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}t,\,\,p_{\perp}+p_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle)=:(\bar{t},\bar{x},\bar{p}).

Thanks to the definition of the relativistic cylinder (
5.4
), we note that
z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z\in Q_{r}^{rel}(z_{0})
is equivalent to the following three inequalities:

(i)

-r^{2}<\langle p_{0}\rangle(\bar{t}-t_{0})-p_{0}\cdot(\bar{x}-x_{0})\leq 0
,

(ii)

\Big|(\bar{x}-x_{0})_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle(\bar{x}-x_{0})_{\|}-p_{0}(\bar{t}-t_{0})\Big|<r^{3}
, and

(iii)

\Big|(\bar{p})_{\perp}+(\bar{p})_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\langle\bar{p}\rangle\Big|<r.

Substituting
\bar{t}
and
\bar{x}
shows that (i) is equivalent to

(A.2)

-r^{2}<\langle p_{0}\rangle\Big(t\langle p_{0}\rangle+\cancel{p_{0}\cdot x}\Big)-p_{0}\cdot\Big(\cancel{x_{\perp}}+\cancel{x_{\parallel}\langle p_{0}\rangle}+p_{0}t\Big)\leq 0.

Hence,

(A.3)

-r^{2}\leq(\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}-|p_{0}|^{2})t\leq 0,

which is equivalent to
-r^{2}<t\leq 0
.

Inequality (ii) is equivalent to

(A.4)

\Big|\big(x_{\perp}+x_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}t\big)_{\perp}+\langle p_{0}\rangle\big(x_{\perp}+x_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle+\cancel{p_{0}t}\big)_{\|}-p_{0}\big(\cancel{t\langle p_{0}\rangle}+p_{0}\cdot x\big)\Big|<r^{3}.

Since
p_{0}(p_{0}\cdot x)=|p_{0}|^{2}x_{\|}
, we have that this is equivalent to

(A.5)

|x|=\Big|x_{\perp}+x_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}-p_{0}(p_{0}\cdot x)\Big|<r^{3}.

Inequality (iii) is equivalent to

(A.6)

\displaystyle\Big|\big(p_{\perp}+p_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle\big)_{\perp}+\big(p_{\perp}+p_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle\big)_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\left\langle p_{\perp}+p_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle\right\rangle\Big|<r.

Since
\left\langle p_{\perp}+p_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle+p_{0}\langle p\rangle\right\rangle=\langle p\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle+p\cdot p_{0}
, inequality (iii) is equivalent to

(A.7)

\Big|p+p_{\|}|p_{0}|^{2}-p_{0}(p\cdot p_{0})\Big|=\Big|p_{\perp}+p_{\|}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}+p_{0}\langle p\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{0}\big(\langle p\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle+p\cdot p_{0}\big)\Big|<r.

Since
p_{0}(p\cdot p_{0})=|p_{0}|^{2}p_{\|}
, we have that inequality (iii) is equivalent to
|p|<r
.

Therefore,
z_{0}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z\in Q_{r}^{rel}(z_{0})
is equivalent to
-r^{2}<t\leq 0
and
|x|<r^{3}
and
|p|<r
, which is equivalent to
z\in Q_{r}^{rel}(0)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Our next goal is to prove that the relativistic distance (
5.2
) is left-invariant under Lorentz boosts. In order to prove this, we need to study products of boost matrices. In fact, it is known (see, for example,
[
15
,
37
]
) that a product of two boost matrices is either a boost matrix followed by a rotation or a rotation followed by a boost matrix. For completeness purposes, we prove such a result for a particular product of boost matrices that appears in the the proof of the left-invariance of the relativistic distance. In our case, the product will be equal to a rotation followed by a boost matrix.

First, let us set notation. Recall from (
5.5
) that for any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a boost matrix is given by

(A.8)

L(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\langle p\rangle&-p\\[7.0pt]
-p&I+(\langle p\rangle-1)\dfrac{p\otimes p}{|p|^{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\langle p\rangle&-p\\[7.0pt]
-p&M(p)\end{pmatrix},

where we use the following notation

(A.9)

\displaystyle M(p)=I+(\langle p\rangle-1)\dfrac{p\otimes p}{|p|^{2}}.

In the rest of the appendix, we also use the following notation. For any vector
z\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, we denote its time component by
z_{t}
, its spatial component by
z_{x}
and its momentum component by
z_{p}
. Additionally,
z_{t,x}=\binom{z_{t}}{z_{x}}
and
z_{E,p}=\binom{z_{E}}{z_{p}}
, where
E=\langle p\rangle
and
z_{E}=\langle z_{p}\rangle
.

Lemma A.2

(Product of boost matrices)
.

Let
z_{0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
and let

\bar{p}_{2}=\big(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2}\big)_{p}.

Then

L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&R\end{pmatrix}L(p_{2}),

where
R
is a rotation matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proof.

Since
(L(p_{2}))^{-1}=L(-p_{2}),
it suffices to show that

T:=L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&R\end{pmatrix},

for some
3\times 3
rotation matrix
R
. Before we start computing matrix
T
, we note several properties of the matrix
M
as defined in (
A.9
). It is easy to verify that for any
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we have

(A.10)

\displaystyle M(p)p=\langle p\rangle p,

\displaystyle M(p)M(p)=I+(\langle p\rangle^{2}-1)\,\hat{p}\otimes\hat{p}=I+p\otimes p,\quad\text{ and}

\displaystyle M(q)M(p)=I+(\langle q\rangle-1)\hat{q}\otimes\hat{q}+(\langle p\rangle-1)\hat{p}\otimes\hat{p}+(\langle q\rangle-1)(\langle p\rangle-1)(\hat{q}\cdot\hat{p})\hat{q}\otimes\hat{p}.

We now start computing matrix
T
:

(A.11)

\displaystyle T=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle+\bar{p}_{2}\cdot p_{0}&-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle p_{0}-M(p_{0})\bar{p}_{2}\\[7.0pt]
-\langle p_{0}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}-M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}&\bar{p}_{2}\otimes p_{0}+M(\bar{p}_{2})M(p_{0})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\langle p_{2}\rangle&p_{2}\\[7.0pt]
p_{2}&M(p_{2})\end{pmatrix}=:\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&T_{12}\\[7.0pt]
T_{21}&T_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

We next compute all 4 block matrices of the matrix
T
. Using
\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle=\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle p_{2}\rangle-p_{0}\cdot p_{2}
, we have:

(A.12)

\displaystyle T_{11}

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle p_{2}\rangle+\langle p_{2}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}\cdot p_{0}-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle p_{2}\cdot p_{0}-(M(p_{0})\bar{p}_{2})\cdot p_{2}

(A.13)

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle^{2}+\langle p_{2}\rangle\Big((M(p_{0})p_{2})\cdot p_{0}-\langle p_{2}\rangle|p_{0}|^{2}\Big)-\Big(p_{2}+(\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}-1)(p_{2})_{\|}-\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle p_{0}\Big)\cdot p_{2}

(A.14)

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle^{2}+\langle p_{2}\rangle\Big(p_{2}\cdot p_{0}+(\langle p_{0}\rangle-1)p_{2}\cdot p_{0}-\langle p_{2}\rangle|p_{0}|^{2}\Big)-|p_{2}|^{2}-(p_{2}\cdot p_{0})^{2}+\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle p_{2}\cdot p_{0}

(A.15)

\displaystyle=(\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle-p_{2}\cdot p_{0})^{2}+2\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle p_{2}\cdot p_{0}-\langle p_{2}\rangle^{2}|p_{0}|^{2}-|p_{2}|^{2}-(p_{2}\cdot p_{0})^{2}

(A.16)

\displaystyle=\langle p_{2}\rangle^{2}\langle p_{0}\rangle^{2}-\langle p_{2}\rangle^{2}|p_{0}|^{2}-|p_{2}|^{2}=\langle p_{2}\rangle^{2}-|p_{2}|^{2}

(A.17)

\displaystyle=1.

Next, using (
A.10
),
\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle=\langle p_{0}\rangle\langle p_{2}\rangle-p_{0}\cdot p_{2}
and
\bar{p}_{2}\cdot p_{0}=\langle p_{0}\rangle p_{2}\cdot p_{0}-\langle p_{2}\rangle|p_{0}|^{2}
, we compute
T_{12}
:

(A.18)

\displaystyle T_{12}

\displaystyle=\Big(\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle+\bar{p}_{2}\cdot p_{0}\Big)p_{2}-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle M(p_{2})p_{0}-M(p_{2})M(p_{0})\bar{p}_{2}

(A.19)

\displaystyle=\langle p_{2}\rangle p_{2}-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle M(p_{2})p_{0}-M(p_{2})M(p_{0})\Big(M(p_{0})p_{2}-\langle p_{2}\rangle p_{0}\Big)

(A.20)

\displaystyle=\langle p_{2}\rangle p_{2}-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle M(p_{2})p_{0}-M(p_{2})\Big(p_{2}+(p_{2}\cdot p_{0})p_{0}\Big)+\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle M(p_{2})p_{0}

(A.21)

\displaystyle=\cancel{\langle p_{2}\rangle p_{2}}-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle M(p_{2})p_{0}-\cancel{\langle p_{2}\rangle p_{2}}-(p_{2}\cdot p_{0})M(p_{2})p_{0}+\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle M(p_{2})p_{0}

(A.22)

\displaystyle=0.

Next, we compute
T_{21}
:

(A.23)

\displaystyle T_{21}

\displaystyle=-\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}-\langle p_{2}\rangle M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}+(\bar{p}_{2}\otimes p_{0})p_{2}+M(\bar{p}_{2})M(p_{0})p_{2}

(A.24)

\displaystyle=-\langle p_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}-\langle p_{2}\rangle M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}+(p_{2}\cdot p_{0})\bar{p}_{2}+M(\bar{p}_{2})\Big(\bar{p}_{2}+\langle p_{2}\rangle p_{0}\Big)

(A.25)

\displaystyle=-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle p_{2}-\langle p_{2}\rangle M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}+\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}+\langle p_{2}\rangle M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}

(A.26)

\displaystyle=0.

Finally, we prove that
T_{22}
is a
3\times 3
rotation matrix. Note that since
T_{11}=1,T_{12}=T_{21}=0
, we have that

(A.27)

\displaystyle\det(T_{22})=\det(T)=\det(L(\bar{p}_{2}))\det(L(p_{0}))\det(L(p_{2}))=1,

where is the last equality we used that the boost matrix
L(p)
has determinant equal to 1. This is easy to check with elementary calculations.
It remains to show that
T_{22}T^{T}_{22}=T^{T}_{22}T_{22}=I
, where
T_{22}^{T}
is the transpose of the matrix
T_{22}
. To prove this, it suffices to show that

(A.28)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&T_{22}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&T^{T}_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&I_{3}\end{pmatrix}=I_{4},

which is equivalent to

(A.29)

\displaystyle L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})\Big(L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})\Big)^{T}=I_{4},\mbox{ that is,}

(A.30)

\displaystyle L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})=\Big(\Big(L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})\big)^{T}\Big)^{-1},\mbox{ that is,}

(A.31)

\displaystyle L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})=L(-\bar{p}_{2})L(-p_{0})L(p_{2}).

We have

(A.32)

LHS

\displaystyle=L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})L(-p_{2})

(A.33)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle+\bar{p}_{2}\cdot p_{0}&-\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle p_{0}-M(p_{0})\bar{p}_{2}\\[7.0pt]
-\langle p_{0}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}-M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}&\bar{p}_{2}\otimes p_{0}+M(\bar{p}_{2})M(p_{0})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\langle p_{2}\rangle&p_{2}\\[7.0pt]
p_{2}&M(p_{2})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{11}&T_{12}\\[5.0pt]
T_{21}&T_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

Recall that we already proved that
T_{11}=1,T_{12}=T_{21}=0
.

On the other hand, we have

(A.34)

RHS

\displaystyle=L(-\bar{p}_{2})L(-p_{0})L(p_{2})

(A.35)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle\langle p_{0}\rangle+\bar{p}_{2}\cdot p_{0}&\langle\bar{p}_{2}\rangle p_{0}+M(p_{0})\bar{p}_{2}\\[7.0pt]
\langle p_{0}\rangle\bar{p}_{2}+M(\bar{p}_{2})p_{0}&\bar{p}_{2}\otimes p_{0}+M(\bar{p}_{2})M(p_{0})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\langle p_{2}\rangle&-p_{2}\\[7.0pt]
-p_{2}&M(p_{2})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{11}&S_{12}\\[5.0pt]
S_{21}&S_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

By direct calculations, one can see that
S_{11}=T_{11}=1,S_{12}=-T_{12}=0,S_{21}=-T_{21}=0
and

S_{22}=T_{22}
. Therefore, indeed, LHS = RHS, and so (
A.28
) is satisfied, and thus
T_{22}
is a rotation matrix.

∎

Lemma A.3

(Left-invariance of the relativistic distance)
.

For any
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}
, we have

d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{1},z_{2})=d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1},z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2}).

Proof.

By (
5.2
) and (
5.1
), we have

(A.36)

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\|z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1}\|=\Big|(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{t}\Big|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\Big|(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{x}\Big|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}+\Big|(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{p}\Big|,

and similarly

(A.37)

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1},z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})

\displaystyle=\Big|\big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\big)_{t}\Big|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\Big|\big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\big)_{x}\Big|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}

(A.38)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big|\big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\big)_{p}\Big|.

Using the boost matrix notation, we have

(A.39)

\displaystyle(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{t,x}=L(p_{2})\binom{t_{1}-t_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\quad\text{ and}

(A.40)

\displaystyle(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{E,p}=L(p_{2})\binom{E_{1}}{p_{1}},

and if we denote
\bar{p}_{2}:=(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})_{p}
, then we have

(A.41)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{t,x}=L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})\binom{t_{1}-t_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}\quad\text{ and}

(A.42)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{E,p}=L(\bar{p}_{2})L(p_{0})\binom{E_{1}}{p_{1}}.

By
Lemma

A.2
, there is a
3\times 3
rotation matrix
R

such that

(A.43)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{t,x}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&R\end{pmatrix}L(p_{2})\binom{t_{1}-t_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&R\end{pmatrix}(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{t,x}\quad\text{ and}

(A.44)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{E,p}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&R\end{pmatrix}L(p_{2})\binom{E_{1}}{p_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[5.0pt]
0&R\end{pmatrix}(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{E,p}.

Therefore,

(A.45)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{t}=(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{t},

(A.46)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{x}=R\,(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{x},\quad\text{ and}

(A.47)

\displaystyle\Big((z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})\circ_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})\Big)_{p}=R\,(z_{2}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1})_{p}.

Since rotation conserves length, we have
d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{1},z_{2})=d_{\mathcal{L}}(z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{1},z_{0}^{-1}\circ_{\mathcal{L}}z_{2})
.
∎

Let

\displaystyle f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.1)

Let
\mathcal{S}

which are univalent. For a comprehensive understanding of univalent functions and their significance in coefficient problems, readers are referred to the following books

Definition 1.1

Let

In

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}[\varphi]=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\prec\varphi(z)\}.

In the literature, functions belonging to the class

. Note that

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=1+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\log\left((1+\sqrt{z})/(1-\sqrt{z})\right)\right)^{2},

we obtain the class

is starlike with respect to
1
and whose coefficients is the Gregory coefficients. Gregory
coefficients are decreasing rational numbers of the form

, which are similar in their role to the Bernoulli numbers and appear in a large number of problems, especially in those related to the numerical analysis and to the number theory. They first appeared in the works of the Scottish mathematician James Gregory in 1671 and have since been rediscovered numerous times. Among the renowned mathematicians who rediscovered them are Laplace, Mascheroni, Fontana, Bessel, Clausen, Hermite, Pearson, and Fisher. Furthermore, Gregory’s coefficients can rightfully be considered among the most frequently rediscovered entities in mathematics, with the most recent rediscovery dating back to our century. This frequent rediscovery has led to various names for them in literature, such as reciprocal logarithmic numbers, Bernoulli numbers of the second kind, Cauchy numbers, and more. Consequently, their authorship is often attributed to various mathematicians.

Sharp bound of a Hankel determinant of logarithmic coefficients for functions in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{G}

For

, we define

\displaystyle F_{f}(z):=\log\dfrac{f(z)}{z}=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}(f)z^{n},\;\;z\in\mathbb{D},\;\;\log 1:=0,

(2.1)

a logarithmic function associated with

play a critical role in the theory of univalent functions, it appears that only a limited number of sharp bounds have been established for them. As is well known, the logarithmic coefficients play a crucial role in Milin’s conjecture (
[
32
]
, see also
[
10
, p.155]

Milin conjectured that for

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(k|\gamma_{k}|^{2}-\frac{1}{k}\right)\leq 0,

[

proved Milin conjecture which confirmed the famous Bieberbach conjecture. On the other hand, one of reasons for more attention has been given to the logarithmic coefficients is that the sharp bound for the class
\mathcal{S}
is known only for

and
\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}|\leq 1,\;\;|\gamma_{2}|\leq\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{e}=0.635\ldots

\mathcal{S}

[

11
,

,

20
,
43
]

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
29
]
)
Let
p\in\mathcal{P}
be given by (

\displaystyle|c_{2}-vc^{2}_{1}|\leq\begin{cases}-4v+2\;\;\;\;v<0,\\
2\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;0\leq v\leq 1,\\

Theorem 3.1

\displaystyle|a_{3}-\mu a_{2}^{2}|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{12}(1-3\mu),\;\;\;\mu<-\dfrac{2}{3},\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\dfrac{1}{4},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\dfrac{2}{3}\leq\mu\leq\dfrac{4}{3},\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\dfrac{1}{12}(1-3\mu),\;\;\;\mu>\dfrac{4}{3}.\end{cases}.

The inequalities are sharp.

Proof.

Since
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{G}
, there exists an analytic function
\omega
with

and
|\omega(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
such that (
2.7
) holds. Then in view of (

\displaystyle|a_{3}-\mu a_{2}^{2}|=\bigg|\dfrac{1}{24}\left(3c_{2}-c_{1}^{2}\right)-\mu\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{16}\bigg|=\frac{1}{8}|c_{2}-vc_{1}^{2}|,

where

Case 1.

\displaystyle|c_{2}-vc_{1}^{2}|=-4v+2=\frac{2(1-3\mu)}{3}.

Case 2.

\displaystyle|c_{2}-vc_{1}^{2}|=2.

Case 3.

\displaystyle|c_{2}-vc_{1}^{2}|=4v-2=\frac{2(3\mu-1)}{3}.

In recent years, special attention has been given on coefficient problems in the class
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{A}

(

Bounds and formulas for residues of singular holomorphic foliations and applications
Diogo da Silva Machado

Abstract
. We consider one dimensional holomorphic foliations with isolated singularities that leave invariant a local complete intersection. We establish explicit formulas for the total GSV index of such foliations and obtain bounds for this index. As applications, we derive several consequences related to Poincaré’s problem for foliations on projective spaces.

1.
Introduction

The index of a vector field at a singular point is an elementary and fundamental notion in Geometry and Topology, having been extensively developed and studied from various perspectives. Its properties underlie crucial results, most notably the classical Poincaré-Hopf theorem, which states that the sum of the indices of a vector field with isolated singularities on a closed, smooth, oriented manifold is a topological invariant, coinciding with the Euler characteristic of the manifold.

However, when considering spaces that are not manifolds — such as singular varieties — it is natural to seek a generalization of this index. In response, X. Gómez-Mont, J. Seade, and A. Verjovsky
[
22
]
introduced the so-called GSV index. This concept extends the classical Poincaré - Hopf index while crucially preserving its stability under perturbations. Initially defined for vector fields on hypersurfaces with isolated singularities
[
22
]
, the GSV index was later extended to complete intersections in
[
34
]
, and further generalizations have since been obtained in various contexts by several authors
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
27
]
.

By employing a suitable adaptation of Chern-Weil theory to Čech-de Rham cohomology, the Baum-Bott residue theory
[
2
]
has been extended to the setting of foliations restricted to their invariant subvarieties. In this framework, the GSV index is defined as a residue of the foliation relative to a singular invariant subvariety, and is obtained as the localization of a certain virtual bundle associated with both the foliation and the invariant subvariety (see, for example,
[
8
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
and
[
35
]
). In general, the computation of these residues is a challenging task, and in some cases, alternative approaches have been employed to compute them. For example, Y. Nakamura (Kinki University, Japan) used computer software to compute the GSV index of certain holomorphic vector fields (see
[
6
, Remark 5.7.1.]
).

We refer to Section
2.2
for more details on the GSV index in the context of one-dimensional holomorphic foliations.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation, with isolated singularities, on a compact complex manifold
X
, and let
V\subset X
be a variety, with isolated singularities, such that
\mathcal{F}
leaves

the GSV index of
\mathcal{F}
at

, and we shall write
GSV(\mathcal{F},V)
for the sum
\sum_{p\in S(\mathcal{F},V)}GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},V)
.

In the present work, we establish a formula for computing
GSV(\mathcal{F},V)
, which
will also be called the
total GSV index of
\mathcal{F}
. We consider the case where

is a local complete intersection defined as the zero set of a section of a vector bundle (this is the case, for example, of hypersurfaces, global complete intersections and complete intersections in projective space). More precisely, we prove the following formula:

.

Let

m

\displaystyle GSV(\mathcal{F},V)\hskip-1.9919pt=\hskip-4.26773pt\int_{X}\hskip-2.84544ptc_{r}(N)\hskip-3.41418pt\left(\sum_{t=0}^{m-r}\hskip-3.98337pt\left(\hskip-2.84544ptc_{t}(TX)\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-2.56064pt\sum_{j=1}^{t}\hskip-1.42271pt\sum_{i=1}^{j}\hskip-1.42271pt\sum_{\mid L_{i}\mid=j}\hskip-5.40608ptc_{t-j}(TX)(-1)^{i}\hskip-1.42271ptc_{L_{i}}\hskip-3.98337pt\left(N\right)\hskip-1.13791pt\right)\hskip-2.84544ptc_{1}(T\mathcal{F}^{\ast})^{m-r-t}\right)

where
T\mathcal{F}^{\ast}
denotes the dual tangent bundle of the
\mathcal{F}
,
L_{i}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{i})\in({\mathbb{N}}^{\ast})^{i}
, with
l_{1}+\ldots+l_{i}=j
, is an
i
-dimensional multi-index and
\displaystyle c_{L_{i}}\left(N\right):=c_{l_{1}}\left(N\right)\cdots c_{l_{i}}\left(N\right)
.

Bounds for the GSV index are established in Theorem 2, in the case where
\mathcal{F}
has non-degenerate isolated singularities. Recall that

is a non-degenerate singularity when the differential

of

\mathcal{F}

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation, with non-degenerate isolated singularities, over an

(i) If

Recall that if

As an application of Theorem 1, we obtain some results related to Poincaré problem. We recall that the Poincaré problem is the question of bounding the degree of an algebraic variety invariant by a holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
of complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{m}
, in terms of the degree of the foliation. This question was first considered by Poincaré in
[
31
]
, in the case of plane curves invariant by foliations of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with a rational first integral. More recently, D. Cerveau and A. Lins Neto
[
13
]
, considering foliations on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, proved that if an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve
C
has at most nodal singularities then

(1)

\displaystyle k\leq d+2,

where
k
and
d
denotes, respectively, the degrees of
C
and
\mathcal{F}
. M. Carnicer
[
11
]
has obtained the same inequality when the singularities of
\mathcal{F}
over
C
are all non-dicritical. M. Brunella
[
8
]
shows that the negativity of the GSV-indices is an obstruction to the inequality (
1
). He recovered Carnicer’s result by observing that these indices are always non-negative in the non-dicritical case.

In
[
28
]
, we show that the GSV index remains an obstruction to inequality (
1
) when
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation on a projective space of even dimension, leaving invariant a hypersurface with isolated singularities (in odd dimension, we also prove that the sum of the GSV indices is always non-negative; nevertheless, this is not sufficient to guarantee that inequality (
1
) holds).

In the present work, we aim to show that, in the case where
\mathcal{F}
leaves invariant a complete intersection curve, this result holds for projective spaces of arbitrary dimension. More precisely:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation of dimension one in
\mathbb{P}^{m}
and degree
d
, with isolated singularities, leaving invariant a complete intersection curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}
of multi-degre
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m-1})
. Then inequality

k_{1}+\ldots+k_{m-1}\leq d+m.

holds if and only if
\displaystyle GSV(\mathcal{F},V)\geq 0
.

Remark 1.2
.

(a)
A complete intersection curve
C
on
\mathbb{P}^{m}
is defined by a section of vector bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}(1)^{\otimes k_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}(1)^{\otimes k_{m-1}}

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}(1)
denotes the hyperplane bundle. We recall that the multi-degree of

is defined by
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m-1})
.

(b)
In
[
12
]
, V. Cavalier and D. Lehmann, using a bound for total GSV index, proved a type of Poincaré inequality for one-dimensional foliations on the projective space.

An immediate consequence of the Theorem 3 is the following.

Corollary 1.3

(M. G. Soares,
[
36
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation of dimension one in
\mathbb{P}^{m}
and degree

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m-1})

k_{1}+\ldots+k_{m-1}\leq d+m.

In the study of the Poincaré problem, by considering the degree and other characters associated with an invariant curve, M. G. Soares
[

also proved the following Poincaré inequality for holomorphic foliations on the two-dimensional projective space:

Theorem

(M. G. Soares,
[

]
)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a holomorphic foliation of dimension one in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, with isolated singularities and degree

. One has,

(2)

\displaystyle k(k-2)\hskip 7.11317pt-\sum_{p\in Sing(C)}(\mu_{p}(C)-1)\hskip 7.11317pt\leq\hskip 7.11317pt\displaystyle dk.

In his proof, M. G. Soares also considered

Theorem 4

Let

Remark 1.4
.

In
[
28
]
we obtained a characterization of Soares’s inequality (

[
29
]
, generalization of (

In Section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Singular one-dimensional holomorphic foliations

Let
X
be a connected complex manifold. A one-dimensional holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is given by a set of data
\{(U_{\alpha},v_{a},g_{\alpha\beta})\}
, where
\{U_{\alpha}\}
is an open covering of
X
,
v_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(U_{\alpha},TX|_{U_{\alpha}})
is a holomorphic vector field on
U_{\alpha}
, for each
\alpha
, and
g_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})
is a non-vanishing holomorphic function on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
, for each pair
(\alpha,\beta)
, such that
v_{\alpha}=g_{\alpha\beta}v_{\beta}
in
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
and
g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\beta\gamma}=g_{\alpha\gamma}
in
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}\cap U_{\gamma}
.

We denote by
K_{\mathcal{F}}
the line bundle defined by the cocycle
\{g_{\alpha\beta}\}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}^{*})
. Thus, a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
induces a global holomorphic section
s_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,T_{X}\otimes K_{\mathcal{F}})
. The line bundle
T_{\mathcal{F}}:=(K_{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\hookrightarrow T_{X}
is called the tangent bundle of
\mathcal{F}
. The singular set of
\mathcal{F}

is
Sing(\mathcal{F})=\{s_{\mathcal{F}}=0\}
. We will assume that
codim(Sing(\mathcal{F}))\geq 2
.

Given a variety
V\subset X
, we say that the foliation
\mathcal{F}

leaves

V

invariant
(or that
V
is
invariant by
\mathcal{F}
) if it satisfies the following condition: for all
x\in V-Sing(V)
, the vector
v_{\alpha}(x)
belongs to
T_{x}V
, with
x\in U_{\alpha}
.

A foliation on a complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is called a
projective
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a projective foliation with tangent bundle
T_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(r)
. The integer
d:=1-r
is called the degree of
\mathcal{F}
.

2.2.
The GSV-index

X. Gomez-Mont, J. Seade and A. Verjovsky
[
22
]
introduced the GSV-index for a holomorphic vector field over an analytic hypersurface with isolated singularities, on a complex manifold, generalizing the (classical) Poincaré-Hopf index. The concept of GSV-index was extended to continuous vector fields on more general contexts. For example, J. Seade and T. Suwa in
[
34
]
, defined the GSV-index for vector fields on isolated complete intersection singularity germs. J.-P. Brasselet, J. Seade and T. Suwa in
[
5
]
, extended the notion to vector fields defined on certain types of analytic subvariety with non-isolated singularities.

In
[
21
]
X. Gomez-Mont defined the homological index of holomorphic vector fields on an analytic hypersurface with isolated singularities, which coincides with the GSV index. There is also the virtual index, introduced by D. Lehmann, M. Soares and T. Suwa
[
27
]
, defined via Chern-Weil theory, that can be interpreted as the GSV-index.
M. Brunella
[
9
]
also presents the GSV-index for foliations on complex surfaces by a different approach.

Let us recall the definition of the GSV index (see, for example,
[
6
, Ch.3, Sec. 3.2]
. Let
X
be an
m
-dimensional complex manifold and
\mathcal{F}
a one-dimensional foliation on
X
, with isolated singularities, leaving invariant a local complete intersection
V
, of codimension
r
, with isolated singularities. Given
p_{0}\in Sing(V)
, let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})=0
be a local reduced equation of
V
at
p_{0}
and
v
a holomorphic vector field defining
\mathcal{F}
on a neighborhood
U
of
p_{0}
in
X
. The gradient vector fields
\{\overline{grad}\,(f_{1}),\ldots,\overline{grad}\,(f_{r})\}
are linearly independent everywhere away from
p_{0}
, because
p_{0}
is an isolated singularity of
V
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
leaves
V
invariant, it follows that the restriction
v_{\ast}
of vector field
v
to the regular part
V_{reg}\cap U
defines a continuous vector field on
V\cap U
, possibly singular only in
p_{0}
.

Let
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
be a coordinate system on
U
and consider

S_{\varepsilon}=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}):\,\,\mid\mid z-p_{0}\mid\mid\,\,=\,\,\varepsilon\}

a sphere with
\varepsilon
sufficiently small so that
K=V\cap S_{\varepsilon}
is the link of the singularity of
V
at
p_{0}
(see, for example,
[
30
]
). It is an
(2m-1)
-dimensional real oriented manifold. The set
\{v_{\ast}(z),\overline{grad}\,(f_{1})(z),\ldots,\overline{grad}\,(f_{r})(z)\}
is a
(r+1)
-frame at each point
z\in V-\{p_{0}\}
, and up to homotopy, it can be assumed to be orthonormal. Thus, we obtain a continuous map

\phi_{v_{\ast}}:=(v_{\ast}(z),\overline{grad}\,(f_{1})(z),\ldots,\overline{grad}\,(f_{r})(z)):K\longrightarrow W_{r+1}(m+r)

where
W_{r+1}(m+r)
is the Stiefel manifold of complex
(r+1)
-frames in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m+1}
. The GSV index of
v
at
p_{0}\in V
, denoted by
GSV_{p_{0}}(v,V)
, is defined as the degree of map
\phi_{v_{\ast}}
. According to
[
6
, Ch.6, Corollary 6.3.1]
,
GSV_{p_{0}}(v,V)
does not depend on the choice of the vector field
v
, local representative of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at
p_{0}
. Thus, the
GSV index of
\mathcal{F}
in
p_{0}
along
V
, denoted by
GSV_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{F},V)
, is defined as
GSV_{p_{0}}(v,V)
. Moreover, if the local
complete intersection
V
is defined by a section of a holomorphic vector bundle
N
on X, then we have the following residue type formula for total GSV index of the foliation (see
[
38
, Ch.IV, Theorem 7.16]
)

\displaystyle\int_{V}c_{n-r}(TX-N-T_{\mathcal{F}})=GSV(\mathcal{F},V).

Remark 2.1
.

In the above definition of the GSV index, we suppose that
V
has dimension greater than 1 or it has only one branch. When
dim(V)=1
and
V
has several branches the GSV index is defined as the Poincaré-Hopf index of an extension of
v
to a Milnor fiber (see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
24
]
). Also, we observe that if
p_{0}\in{\rm Sing}(\mathcal{F})\cap V_{\mathrm{reg}\,}
then
GSV_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{F},V)
is the (classical) Poincaré-Hopf index of
v_{\ast}
at
p_{0}
.

3.
Formulas for GSV index

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem 1, which provides an explicit formula for the total GSV index of the foliation. We also provide versions for this formula for the case in where the invariant variety is a complete intersection of hypersurfaces (Corollary
3.2
), including the case of projective foliations (Corollary
3.3
).

To prove Theorem
Theorem 1
, we will first prove the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be an
m
-dimensional complex manifold and
V\subset X
a local complete intersection of codimension
r
, with isolated singularities, defined by a section of a holomorphic vector bundle
N
. Then, for all
t=1,\ldots,m
, the
t
-th Chern class of the virtual bundle
TX-N
is given by

(3)

\displaystyle c_{t}\left(TX-N\right)=c_{t}(TX)+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{\mid L\mid=j}(-1)^{i}c_{t-j}(TX)c_{L_{i}}\left(N\right),

where
L_{i}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{i})\in({\mathbb{N}}^{\ast})^{i}
, with
l_{1}+\ldots+l_{i}=j
, is an
i
-dimensional multi-index and
c_{L_{i}}\left(N\right):=c_{l_{1}}\left(N\right)\cdots c_{l_{i}}\left(N\right)

Proof.:
By definition the total Chern class of the virtual vector bundle
TX-N
is
c_{\ast}(TX-N)=c_{\ast}(TX).c_{\ast}(N)^{-1}
(see
[
2
]
). Thus, we obtain the relations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}c_{1}(TX-N)&=&c_{1}(TX)-c_{1}(N)\\
c_{2}(TX-N)+c_{1}(N)c_{1}(TX-N)&=&c_{2}(TX)-c_{2}(N)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{t}(TX-N)+c_{1}(N)c_{t-1}(TX-N)+\ldots+c_{t-1}(N)c_{1}(TX-N)&=&c_{t}(TX)-c_{t}(N)\\
\end{array}\right.

which can be written in the following matrix identity

{\scriptsize{{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}1&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
c_{1}(N)&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
c_{2}(N)&c_{1}(N)&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{t-2}(N)&c_{t-3}(N)&c_{t-4}(N)&\ldots&1&0\\
c_{t-1}(N)&c_{t-2}(N)&c_{t-3}(N)&\ldots&c_{1}(N)&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}c_{1}(TX-N)\\
c_{2}(TX-N)\\
c_{3}(TX-N)\\
\vdots\\
c_{t-1}(TX-N)\\
c_{t}(TX-N)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}c_{1}(TX)-c_{1}(N)\\
c_{2}(TX)-c_{2}(N)\\
c_{3}(TX)-c_{3}(N)\\
\vdots\\
c_{t-1}(TX)-c_{t-1}(N)\\
c_{t}(TX)-c_{t}(N)\end{array}\right)}}}

or, equivalently,

{\scriptsize\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}1&\hskip 25.6073pt0&0&\ldots&0&\hskip 5.12128pt0\\
0&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ldots&1&0\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}c_{1}(TX-N)\\
c_{2}(TX-N)\\
c_{3}(TX-N)\\
\vdots\\
c_{t-1}(TX-N)\\
c_{t}(TX-N)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccccc}\delta_{1}(TX,N)\\
\delta_{2}(TX,N)\\
\delta_{3}(TX,N)\\
\vdots\\
\delta_{t-1}(TX,N)\\
\delta_{t}(TX,N)\end{array}\right)}

where the
\delta_{j}(TX,N)^{\prime}
s are defined by the recursion

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\delta_{1}(TX,N)&=&c_{1}(TX)-c_{1}(N)\\
\delta_{j}(TX,N)&=&c_{j}(TX)-c_{j}(N)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}c_{j-i}(N)\delta_{i}(TX,N),\,\,\,\mbox{for $j=2,3,\ldots,k$}.\end{array}\right.

The verification of this equivalence consists of applying a sequence of
(t-1)!
elementary row operations, divided into
t-1
steps. The operations corresponding to the
j
-th step (
j=1,2,\ldots,t-1
) are defined in each row
R_{\ast}
by:

R_{j+1}\leftarrow R_{j+1}-c_{1}(N)R_{j},\quad R_{j+2}\leftarrow R_{j+2}-c_{2}(N)R_{j},\quad\ldots,\quad R_{t}\leftarrow R_{t}-c_{t-j}(N)R_{j}.

Formula (
3
) is then obtained by successively applying recursion in the relation
c_{t}(TX-N)=\delta_{t}(TX,N)
.

\square

Proof of Theorem 1:
By hypothesis the
\mathcal{F}
-invariant variety
V
is
a local complete intersection defined as the zero set of a holomorphic section
\upsilon\in H^{0}(X,N)
, which is regular (see, for example,
[
38
, Ch.II, Sec. 13]
). Hence, the
r
-th Chern class of
N
is Poincaré dual to the fundamental class of

\rho\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,{n-2\choose n-r-1}\right\}
, we obtain

\tau_{p}(V)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-r-1}(-1)^{j}{r-1+j\choose j}\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},V)\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,\tau_{p}(V)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-r-2}(-1)^{j}{r-1+j\choose j}.

If
m-r
is odd, then

(16)

\displaystyle GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},V)=\varepsilon_{r}-\tau_{p}(V)+\rho

and since
\rho\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,{m-2\choose m-r-1}\right\}
, we obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{m-r-2}(-1)^{j}{r-1+j\choose j}-\tau_{p}(V)\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},V)\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{m-r-1}(-1)^{j}{r-1+j\choose j}-\tau_{p}(V).

\square

5.
Applications

In this section, we turn to projective foliations with the aim of obtaining applications in the context of the Poincaré Problem. Our goal is to present the proofs of Theorem 3, Corollary 1.3, and Theorem 4. Moreover, we shall present a few remarks on Schwartz index in the context of holomorphic foliations.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, with isolated singularities and degree
\deg(\mathcal{F})=d
. If
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}
is an
\mathcal{F}
- invariant complete intersection curve of multi-degree
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m-1})
, with isolated
singularities, it follows from Corollary
3.3
that

\displaystyle GSV(\mathcal{F},V)=(k_{1}\cdots k_{m-1})\left[(m+1)-\left(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m-1}\right)+(d-1)\right].

Thus, we get
GSV(\mathcal{F},V)\geq 0
if and only if

\displaystyle k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m-1}\leq d+m
, according to Theorem 3.

We observe that if
C
is smooth, then at each singular point of
\mathcal{F}
the GSV index reduces to the (classical) Poincaré–Hopf index of the local vector field defining
\mathcal{F}
, restricted to
C
, which is positive. Hence, we obtain
GSV(\mathcal{F},V)\geq 0
and, by Theorem 3, the conditions
\displaystyle k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m-1}\leq d+m
occur, according to Corollary 1.3.

Now, to prove Theorem 4, we will assume that
\mathcal{F}
has nondegenerate isolated singularities. By using formula (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)\,\,\,=\,\,\,-\,\tau_{p}(C)+\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{m,p}}{\left\langle v,f\right\rangle}\,\,\,\geq\,\,\,-\,\mu_{p}(C)+1

where in the last step we have used the fact that
\mu_{p}(C)\geq\tau_{p}(C)
(see
[
23
]
) and
\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{m,p}}{\left\langle v,f\right\rangle}\geq 1
. Thus, we get

(k_{1}\cdots k_{m-1})\left[(m+1)-\left(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m-1}\right)+(d-1)\right]\geq\,\,\,-\,\sum_{p\in Sing(C)}(\mu_{p}(C)-1)

and, consequently,

(k_{1}\ldots k_{m-1})\left(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{m-1}-m\right)\hskip 0.0pt-\sum_{p\in Sing(C)}\hskip-9.95863pt(\mu_{p}(C)-1)\leq d(k_{1}\ldots k_{m-1}),

according to Theorem 4.

5.1.
Some remarks on Schwartz index

M. -H. Schwartz carried out pioneering work (
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
) on indices of vector fields defined on singular varieties, introducing the notion of the radial index for a special class of vector fields and establishing a version of the Poincaré-Hopf theorem for singular varieties. This notion was subsequently generalized by various authors in different settings
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
35
]
, and is also referred to in the literature as the Schwartz index. Analogously to the GSV index, the Schwartz index is well suited to the study of singular holomorphic foliations and is used to study its residues relative to singular invariant subvarieties (see for example
[
6
]
,
[
29
]
and
[
38
]
).

Let
X
be a complex manifold and
\mathcal{F}
a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation on
X
, with isolated singularities, leaving invariant a variety
V
, with isolated singularities. For each
p\in V
we denotes by
Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},V)
the
Schwartz index of
\mathcal{F}
at
p
along
V
. For definition and details on the Schwartz index in the context of foliations we refer to
[
38
, Ch. IV, Sec. 7]

We know that
Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},V)
is positive when
\mathcal{F}
is locally defined by radial vector fields (see
[
38
, Ch. IV, Definition 1.3]
) or when
V
is a hypersurface and
\mathrm{dim}(X)
is even (see
[
29
, Theorem 2]
). However, it remains unknown whether this positivity holds in full generality. Theorem 5 asserts that the index is positive when the invariant variety is a complete intersection curve:

Theorem 5
.

Let
X
be an
m
-dimensional complex manifold,
C\subset X
a local complete intersection curve and
\mathcal{F}
a one-dimensional holomorphic foliation on
X
, with isolated singularities, which leaves
C
invariant. One has

Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)>0,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,p\in Sing(\mathcal{F}).

In particular, when
X=\mathbb{P}^{m}
,
Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)\geq 2
, if
(C,p)
is not quasi-homogeneous germ.

Proof:
Let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1})=0
be a local reduced equation of
C
on a neighborhood
U
of
p
in
X
, with a local coordinate system
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
, and
v=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
a holomorphic vector field defining the foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
U
. According to relation (
14
) we have

GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)\,\,\,=\,\,\,-\,\tau_{p}(C)+\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{m,p}}{\left\langle v,f\right\rangle}.

By
[
38
, Ch.IV, Proposition 1.9]
,

GSV_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)-\mu_{p}(C),

where
\mu_{p}(C)
denotes the Milnor number of
C
at
p
. Since
\mu_{p}(C)\geq\tau_{p}(C)
(see
[
23
]
) and
\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{m,p}}{\left\langle v,f\right\rangle}\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{p}(C)-\tau_{p}(C)+\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{m,p}}{\left\langle v,f\right\rangle}\,\,\,>\,\,\,0.

On the other hand, if
X=\mathbb{P}^{m}
, we know that
\mu_{p}(C)=\tau_{p}(C)
if and only if
(C,p)
is quasi-homogeneous (see
[
39
]
). Hence, if
(C,p)
is not quasi-homogeneous, then

\displaystyle Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},C)=\mu_{p}(C)-\tau_{p}(C)+\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{m,p}}{\left\langle v,f\right\rangle}\,\,\,\geq\,\,\,2.

\square

As a consequence of Theorem 5, we will show that the Euler characteristic provides a specific obstruction to the existence of holomorphic vector fields on a complete intersection curve.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
C
be a complete intersection curve with isolated singularities in a complex manifold. If
C
admits a holomorphic vector field with
l
singularities, then

\chi(C)\geq l.

Proof:
Suppose that
C
admits a holomorphic vector field
v
with
Sing(v)=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}\}
. By using
[
6
, Theorem 2.1.1]
, we have

\chi(C)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}Sch_{p_{j}}(v,C).

Since
Sch_{p_{j}}(\mathcal{F},C)>0,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,p_{j}\in Sing(\mathcal{F}),
we obtain

\chi(C)\geq l.

\square

Remark 5.3
.

In
[
29
]
we prove that the obstruction determined by the Euler characteristic for the existence of vector fields is even more comprehensive in the case of hypersurfaces with isolated singularities.

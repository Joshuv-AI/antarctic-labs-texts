Prangoski was partially supported by the bilateral project “Microlocal analysis and applications” funded by the Macedonian Academy of Sciences and Arts and the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts. Abstract
. We show that every Fourier multiplier with real-valued and positively homogeneous symbol of order 0 0 , supported in a cone whose dual cone has a nonempty interior and such that the average of the positive part is sufficiently larger than the average of the negative part does not preserve the L 1 L^{1} - nor the L ∞ L^{\infty} -regularity and neither the continuity. We also construct wave front sets which measure the microlocal regularity with respect to a large class of Banach spaces. As a consequence of the first part, we argue that one can never construct wave front sets that behave in a natural way and measure the microlocal L 1 L^{1} - nor L ∞ L^{\infty} -regularity and neither the continuity.

Introduction and preliminaries

If

[

]
and the Mikhlin multiplier theorem
[

verify that

,
1<p<\infty
. As standard, we call the smooth functions which satisfy these bounds Mikhlin multipliers.

More generally, let

be the pseudo-differential operator (from now, often abbreviated as
\Psi

a\in S^{r}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
defined by

\operatorname{Op}(a)\varphi(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)d\xi,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Here

,

,

,

, is the Fréchet space of global symbols on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

[
15
]
consisting of all

which satisfy

,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. The
\Psi
DO

is continuous on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and it extends to a continuous operator on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, if

then
\operatorname{Op}(a)
is continuous on

,
1<p<\infty
; see
[
25
, Chapter 7, p. 323]
(see also
[
16
,
26
]
). The

-continuity,

,

, has also been established even in the case when
\rho<1
but then the order

is strictly less than
0
and
r
,
p
,
\rho
and
\delta
have to satisfy certain inequality which is known to be optimal; see
[
9
]
. We refer to
[
9
]
also for results concerning

and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\Psi
DOs. We also point out
[

11

and the references therein for the continuity properties on

and the Hardy spaces of pseudo-differential operators with symbols in the pathological cases when

or

. When the dimension

is

, it is folklore that a Mikhlin multiplier (i.e. a

in a neighbourhood of

gives an example of a
\Psi

which is not continuous on

nor on

. The reason is that the Fourier side of such function is not a bounded measure and, by the theorem of Hörmander on translation invariant operators
[

, the spaces of continuous operators on

are exactly the space of bounded Radon measures on

. This example can be generalised to

S^{0}_{\rho,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
for any
\rho>0
. In this article we show a more general result: if the Fourier multiplier is positively homogeneous of order
0
away from the origin (consequently, it is a Mikhlin multiplier), has a nonnegative (or nonpositive) real or imaginary part with support in a closed cone

whose dual cone has a nonempty interior then it is not a Fourier multiplier neither for

nor for
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In fact, the real (or imaginary) part does not have to be nonnegative, only the average of the positive part to be bigger than a constant multiple of the average of the negative part where the constant is a measure of the angular distance between

and its dual cone; see (
2.1
) and (

) below. Moreover, we show something stronger: these Mikhlin multipliers are never continuous operators

, neither

-space topology and, as usual,

is the Fréchet space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

-regularity,

-regularity and continuity. The last fact that these operators are not continuous as maps

is interesting in itself since it is known that all
\Psi

are continuous on the Hölder classes of order

2.
The main result

The following lemma is the key ingredient for the main result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed cone such that
V\backslash\{0\}\neq\emptyset
and
V^{\prime}:=\operatorname{int}V^{*}\neq\emptyset

\varphi_{1,+}:=\max\{\varphi_{1},0\}
and
\varphi_{1,-}:=\max\{-\varphi_{1},0\}

(2.1)

\kappa_{0}:=\sup_{\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbf{S}^{n-1}\cap V^{\prime}}(\inf_{\omega\in\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{1,-}}\omega\omega^{\prime}).

If
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{1,-}\neq\emptyset
then
\kappa_{0}\in(0,1]
, otherwise

. Assume that

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi_{1,+}(\omega)d\omega>\kappa_{0}^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi_{1,-}(\omega)d\omega.

Then for any

Remark 2.2
.

Assuming

is the following. For fixed

\kappa_{0}
is the cosine of half of the aperture of the smallest circular cone with axis in

that contains
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{1,-}
(this cone may not be unique): the bigger
\kappa_{0}
is the smaller the aperture is.

\varphi_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is real-valued and satisfies
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap V
and

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi_{1,+}(\omega)d\omega>\kappa_{V}^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi_{1,-}(\omega)d\omega,

then it satisfies (
2.2
) and the claim in the lemma is applicable to this
\varphi_{1}
.

Definition 2.5
.

The function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be positively homogeneous of order
0
outside of
B_{R}(0)
for some
R>0
if
\psi(\lambda\xi)=\psi(\xi)
,
|\xi|>R
,
\lambda>1
. We abbreviate it as positively homogeneous of order
0
when the number
R
is not important.

Remark 2.6
.

If
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is positively homogeneous of order
0
, then the function
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
(x,\xi)\mapsto\psi(\xi)
, belongs to
S^{0}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
, i.e. it is a Mikhlin multiplier.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed cone such that
V\backslash\{0\}\neq\emptyset
and
V^{\prime}:=\operatorname{int}V^{*}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\operatorname{Re}\psi
is positively homogeneous of order
0
and
\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{Re}\psi)\subseteq V
. Define
\varphi_{1}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi_{1}(\omega):=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Re}\psi(r\omega)
. Then
\varphi_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and if
\varphi_{1}
or
-\varphi_{1}
satisfies (
2.2
) then
\mathcal{F}\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If this is satisfied for
\operatorname{Im}\psi
instead of
\operatorname{Re}\psi
, then again
\mathcal{F}\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We only show the proposition when
\operatorname{Re}\psi
satisfies the assumptions as the proof when
\operatorname{Im}\psi
satisfies the assumptions is analogous. Let
R>0
be large enough such that
\psi(\lambda\xi)=\psi(\xi)
,
|\xi|>R
,
\lambda>1
. Then
\varphi_{1}(\omega)=\operatorname{Re}\psi((R+1)\omega)
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and the measurability of
\varphi_{1}
follows (essentially) by the definition of the spherical Lebesgue measure; it is straightforward to prove that
\varphi_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
(by contradiction). Without loss in generality, we can assume that
\varphi_{1}
satisfies (
2.2
). We apply Lemma
2.1
for this
\varphi_{1}
to deduce that
\mathcal{F}\varphi\not\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\varphi(x):=\varphi_{1}(x/|x|)\mathbf{1}_{(R,\infty)}(|x|)=\operatorname{Re}\psi(x)\mathbf{1}_{(R,\infty)}(|x|)
. Since
x\mapsto\operatorname{Re}\psi(x)(1-\mathbf{1}_{(R,\infty)}(|x|))
has compact support, its Fourier transform is smooth and hence in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We deduce that
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{Re}\psi)\not\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
\mathcal{F}\psi\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
\mathcal{F}\overline{\psi}=(\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi})\check{}\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and thus
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{Re}\psi)=(\mathcal{F}\psi+\mathcal{F}\overline{\psi})/2\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is a contradiction and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

If the function
\varphi_{1}
in the proposition is nonnegative or nonpositive and
\varphi_{1}\neq 0
on a set with positive measure, then
\mathcal{F}\psi\not\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(cf. Remark
2.3
).

Next, we show a local variant of the Hörmander result
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
on the characterisations of translation invariant operators on
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
; recall that an operator
A:X\rightarrow Y
between the translation invariant distribution spaces
X
and
Y
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be translation invariant if it commutes with all translations, i.e.
AT_{x}=T_{x}A
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
T_{x}
is the translation by
x
defined as
T_{x}f:=f(\cdot-x)
. Recall that the convolution
*:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined and hypocontinuous. If
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is positive (i.e.
\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle\geq 0
for all nonnegative
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) and
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then it is straightforward to check that the distribution
f*\mu
is in fact in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

f*\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)d\mu(y),\quad\mbox{a.a.}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};

(the Fubini theorem implies that
f*\mu
is measurable and
f*\mu\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
; of course, one takes any Borel measurable representative of
f
). For general
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one writes

(2.5)

\mu=\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+}-\mu_{\operatorname{Re},-}+i(\mu_{\operatorname{Im},+}-\mu_{\operatorname{Im},-}),

where
\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+}
,
\mu_{\operatorname{Re},-}
,
\mu_{\operatorname{Im},+}
and
\mu_{\operatorname{Im},-}
are the unique positive Radon measures such that
\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+}-\mu_{\operatorname{Re},-}
and
\mu_{\operatorname{Im},+}-\mu_{\operatorname{Im},-}
are the minimal decompositions of the real and imaginary part of
\mu
respectively (see
[
8
, Section 4.3]
), and
f*\mu
can be given as a sum of four integrals as above since

f*\mu=f*\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+}-f*\mu_{\operatorname{Re},-}+i(f*\mu_{\operatorname{Im},+}-f*\mu_{\operatorname{Im},-}).

It is straightforward to check that for each
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the map
f\mapsto f*\mu
is well-defined and continuous as a map

L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The local variant of the Hörmander theorem
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
is the following.

Proposition 2.9
.

(a)

The map
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{b}(L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\mu\mapsto(f\mapsto f*\mu)
, is a topological imbedding whose image is the space of continuous operators
L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that commute with all translations.

(b)

The map
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\mu\mapsto(\varphi\mapsto\varphi*\mu)
, is a topological imbedding whose image is the space of continuous operators
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that commute with all translations.

(c)

The map
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{b}(L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\mu\mapsto(f\mapsto f*\mu)
, is a topological imbedding. If
Q:L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuous and commutes with all translations, then there is
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Q\varphi=\varphi*\mu
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

The maps in
(a)
,
(b)
and
(c)
are well-defined in view of the above comments. We only show the continuity for the map in
(a)
as the proofs of the continuity for the rest are analogous. Let
B
be a bounded subset of
L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
K^{\prime}
a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We need to bound
\sup_{f\in B}\|f*\mu\|_{L^{1}(K^{\prime})}
by a seminorm of
\mu
in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. There is a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
B
is a bounded subset of
L^{1}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where the latter is the closed subspace of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consisting of the elements supported by
K
. Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and
\varphi=1
on
K^{\prime}-K
. Write
\mu
as in (
2.5
) and denote by
\mu_{\operatorname{Re}}
and
\mu_{\operatorname{Im}}
the real and imaginary parts of
\mu
. For
f\in B
, we estimate as follows

\displaystyle\|f*\mu\|_{L^{1}(K^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K^{\prime}-K}\int_{K^{\prime}}|f(x-y)|dxd\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+}(y)+\int_{K^{\prime}-K}\int_{K^{\prime}}|f(x-y)|dxd\mu_{\operatorname{Re},-}(y)

\displaystyle{}\quad+\int_{K^{\prime}-K}\int_{K^{\prime}}|f(x-y)|dxd\mu_{\operatorname{Im},+}(y)+\int_{K^{\prime}-K}\int_{K^{\prime}}|f(x-y)|dxd\mu_{\operatorname{Im},-}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+},\varphi\rangle+\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Re},-},\varphi\rangle+\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Im},+},\varphi\rangle+\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Im},-},\varphi\rangle).

Since
\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+},\varphi\rangle=\sup\{\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Re}},\psi\rangle\,|\,\psi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\,0\leq\psi\leq\varphi\}
(see the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 4.3.2 (a), p. 178]
), we infer
\langle\mu_{\operatorname{Re},+},\varphi\rangle\leq\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,|\phi|\leq\varphi}|\langle\mu,\phi\rangle|
. Analogous estimates hold for the rest of the measures in the decomposition of
\mu
. Since
B^{\prime}:=\{\phi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,|\,|\phi|\leq\varphi\}
is a bounded subset of
\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{supp}\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we deduce
\sup_{f\in B}\|f*\mu\|_{L^{1}(K^{\prime})}\leq 4(\sup_{f\in B}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\sup_{\phi\in B^{\prime}}|\langle\mu,\phi\rangle|
which completes the proof for the continuity of the map in
(a)
.

Concerning the injectivity, again, we only show it for the map in
(a)
as the rest are analogous. Let
f*\mu=0
for all
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This implies that
\mu
is zero on
\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})*\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and hence
\mu=0
since
\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})*\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is dense in
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, we address the rest of the claims in
(a)
. Let
Q\in\mathcal{L}(L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
commutes with all translations. We employ a standard idea (see the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 2.5.8, p. 153]
) to show that it is a convolution with a Radon measure. In view of
[
8
, Theorem, p. 332]
, there is
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Q\varphi=\varphi*u
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Pick nonnegative
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subseteq B_{1}(0)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi(x)dx=1
and set
\chi_{k}(x)=k^{n}\chi(kx)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then
\{\chi_{k}*u\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is a bounded subset of
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and hence bounded in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
too. As
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is barrelled,
\{\chi_{k}*u\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is equicontinuous in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the Banach-Alaoglu theorem
[
24
, Corollary, p. 84]
implies that it is weakly relatively compact in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, its closure in the weak topology is metrisable; see
[
24
, Theorem 1.7, p. 128]
. Thus, there exists a subsequence
(\chi_{k_{l}}*u)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\langle\chi_{k_{l}}*u,\varphi\rangle\rightarrow\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\chi_{k}*u\rightarrow u
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we conclude
u=\mu
. It remains to show that the map is an open map onto the image. Let
B
be a bounded subset of
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. There is a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
B
is a bounded subset of
\mathcal{K}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Employing the above notation, for
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

\sup_{\psi\in B}|\langle\mu,\psi\rangle|\leq\sup_{\psi\in B}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}|\langle\chi_{k}*\mu,\psi\rangle|\leq(\sup_{\psi\in B}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|\chi_{k}*\mu\|_{L^{1}(K)}

which completes the proof since
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|\chi_{k}*\mu\|_{L^{1}(K)}
is a continuous seminorm on
\mathcal{L}_{b}(L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
of the map
f\mapsto f*\mu
.

We now address
(b)
. Let
Q\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
commutes with all translations. Whence there is
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Q\varphi=\varphi*u
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. With
\{\chi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
as above, we are going to show that
\{\chi_{k}*u\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is an equicontinuous subset of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is barrelled, it suffices to show it is weakly bounded in
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and pick a sequence
(\varphi_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
in
\mathcal{D}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
K\supseteq\operatorname{supp}\varphi
such that
\varphi_{m}\rightarrow\varphi
in
\mathcal{K}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}|\langle\chi_{k}*u,\varphi\rangle|\leq\sup_{k,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}|\langle\check{\varphi}_{m}*u,\check{\chi}_{k}\rangle|\leq\|\chi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|\check{\varphi}_{m}*u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))}<\infty

which implies the weak boundedness of
\{\chi_{k}*u\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
. Now, analogously as above, we infer that
u\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To show that the map is an open map onto the image, with
B
as above and
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\sup_{\psi\in B}|\langle\mu,\psi\rangle|\leq\sup_{\psi\in B}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}|\langle\check{\psi}*\mu,\check{\chi}_{k}\rangle|\leq\|\chi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sup_{\psi\in B}\|\check{\psi}*\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))}

which completes the proof of
(b)
since
\sup_{\psi\in B}\|\check{\psi}*\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))}
is a continuous seminorm on
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
of the map
\varphi\mapsto\varphi*\mu
. The proof of
(c)
is analogous to the proof of
(b)
and we omit it.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.7
and Proposition
2.9
, we deduce the main result.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed cone satisfying
V\backslash\{0\}\neq\emptyset
and
V^{\prime}:=\operatorname{int}V^{*}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
(x,\xi)\mapsto\psi(\xi)
belongs to
S^{0}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. Assume that
\operatorname{Re}\psi
is positively homogeneous of order
0
and
\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{Re}\psi)\subseteq V
. If the continuous function
\varphi_{1}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi_{1}(\omega):=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Re}\psi(r\omega)
, is such that
\varphi_{1}
or
-\varphi_{1}
satisfies (
2.2
) then there are
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
g\in L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi(D)f\not\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\psi(D)g\not\in L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\psi(D)\varphi\not\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The same conclusion holds if these assumptions are satisfied by
\operatorname{Im}\psi
instead of
\operatorname{Re}\psi
.

Proof.

Let
(X,Y)
be either one of the pairs
(L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
(L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
or
(\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. The map
\psi(D):X\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuous since it is the composition of the continuous maps
X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\xrightarrow{\psi(D)}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Assume that
\psi(D)(X)\subseteq Y
. The above implies that
\psi(D):X\rightarrow Y
is well-defined and with closed graph. The De Wilde closed graph theorem
[
17
, Theorem 2, p. 57]
yields that
\psi(D):X\rightarrow Y
is continuous (
X
is ultrabornological in view of
[
17
, Theorem 7, p. 72]
, while
[
17
, Theorem 4, p. 55]
verifies that
Y
is a strictly webbed space). Consequently, Proposition
2.9
implies that there is
\mu\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi(D)\varphi=\varphi*\mu
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For
\varphi,\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi,\varphi*\chi\rangle=\langle(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi)*\check{\varphi},\chi\rangle=\langle\psi(D)\check{\varphi},\chi\rangle=\langle\check{\varphi}*\mu,\chi\rangle=\langle\mu,\varphi*\chi\rangle.

As
\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})*\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we infer
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi=\mu
. This is in contradiction with Proposition
2.7
, since
\mathcal{F}\psi=(2\pi)^{n}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi)\check{}\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

As before, it suffices to verify that
\varphi_{1}
or
-\varphi_{1}
satisfy (
2.4
). If
\varphi_{1}
is nonnegative and not identically equal to zero, then it always satisfies (
2.4
).

3.
Wave front sets for a class of Banach spaces

In this section we construct wave front sets that will measure the microlocal regularity with respect to a large class of Banach spaces of distributions. As special cases, they will include most of the standard wave front sets considered in the literature like the Sobolev wave front set
[
13
,
20
]
, the Besov wave front set
[
5
]
, wave front sets with respect to Banach space having solid Fourier side
[
3
,
7
]
, etc (see also
[
4
]
for global wave front sets with respect to Banach spaces of distributions). Furthermore, as a consequence of the results in the previous section, we show that if we require the wave front set to behave in a natural way, then it is not possible to construct it such that it measures the
L^{1}
or
L^{\infty}
microlocal regularity, neither the microlocal continuity of a distribution. To make things precise, we recall the following definition and facts from
[
6
]
. The Banach spaces
F
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
is said to be a
translation-invariant Banach space of distributions
, or (TIB) space for short, if it satisfies the following assumptions:

(I)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq F\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with continuous and dense inclusions;

(II)

T_{x}\in\mathcal{L}(F)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(III)

there are
C,\tau>0
such that
\omega_{F}(x):=\|T_{x}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(F)}\leq C\langle x\rangle^{\tau}
.

One can show that
\omega_{F}
is submultiplicative and Borel measurable. Furthermore, for each
f\in F
, the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow F
,
x\mapsto T_{x}f
, is continuous. The convolution
*:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
uniquely extends to a continuous bilinear mapping
*:L^{1}_{\omega_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times F\rightarrow F
and
F
becomes a Banach module over the Beurling algebra
L^{1}_{\omega_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

(3.1)

\|g*f\|_{F}\leq\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\omega_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{F},\quad\mbox{where}\quad g*f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)T_{x}fdx,

with the last being a Bochner integral of the
F
-valued function
x\mapsto g(x)T_{x}f
.

The Banach space
F
will be called a
dual translation-invariant Banach space of distributions
, or (DTIB) space for short, if
F
is the dual of a (TIB) space
F_{0}
. The space
F
always satisfies the continuous inclusions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq F\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
but they may fail to be dense (e.g.
F=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
). Furthermore,
F
always satisfies
(II)
and
(III)
and
\omega_{F}=\check{\omega}_{F_{0}}
. However, for
f\in F
fixed, the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow F
,
x\mapsto T_{x}f
, is only (in general) continuous with respect to the weak* topology on
F
. One defines the convolution
*:L^{1}_{\omega_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times F\rightarrow F
by duality, i.e.
\langle g*f,f_{0}\rangle:=\langle f,\check{g}*f_{0}\rangle
,
f\in F
,
f_{0}\in F_{0}
,
g\in L^{1}_{\omega_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
F
becomes a Banach module over
L^{1}_{\omega_{F}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and (
3.1
) holds true but the integral should be interpreted as a Pettis integral taken in the weak* topology of
F
.

Let now
E
be a Banach space of distributions with norm
\|\cdot\|_{E}
which satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq E\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with continuous inclusions;

(ii)

the Banach space
\mathcal{F}E:=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,|\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\in E\}
with norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}E}:=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\|_{E}
is a (TIB) or a (DTIB) space;

(iii)

\psi f\in\mathcal{F}E
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
f\in\mathcal{F}E
.

For such
E
, it also holds that

(iii)^{*}

the bilinear map
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times E\rightarrow E
,
(\varphi,e)\mapsto\varphi e
, is continuous;

(iii)^{**}

the bilinear map
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{F}E\rightarrow\mathcal{F}E
,
(\psi,f)\mapsto\psi f
, is continuous.

To verify
(iii)^{*}
, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
e\in E
,
(ii)
and (
3.1
) give
\varphi e=(2\pi)^{-n}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}\varphi*\mathcal{F}e)\in E
and also show the continuity of the map. To prove
(iii)^{**}
, it suffices to show it is separately continuous in view of
[
17
, Theorem 1, p. 158]
. The separate continuity follows by a classical application of the closed graph theorem.

Given such
E
, we define the following two auxiliary spaces

E_{\operatorname{loc}}:=\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,|\,\varphi f\in E,\,\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},\quad E_{\operatorname{comp}}:=\{e\in E\,|\,\operatorname{supp}e\,\,\mbox{is compact}\}.

(One can equip
E_{\operatorname{loc}}
and
E_{\operatorname{comp}}
with natural Fréchet and strict
(LB)
-space topologies respectively, but we will not need these facts.) We wish to define a wave front set which will keep track of the directions where a given distribution does not behave like a
E_{\operatorname{loc}}
function. Given
u\in E_{\operatorname{loc}}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
\varphi(x_{0})\neq 0
, we always have
\varphi u\in E_{\operatorname{comp}}
and
\mathcal{F}(\varphi u)\in\mathcal{F}E
. In order for the wave front set to behave in a natural way, in every direction in the frequency space,
\varphi u
has to behave like an element of
E
, i.e. for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
and open cone
V\ni\xi_{0}
and every function
\psi
with support in
V
and equaling a positive constant in a cone neighbourhood of
\xi_{0}
away from the origin it should hold
\psi\mathcal{F}(\varphi u)\in\mathcal{F}E
. This means that
\mathcal{F}E
has to have multipliers of this form. Of course, assuming that the characteristic functions of the open cones are multipliers for
\mathcal{F}E
is too restrictive: when
p\in(2,\infty)
,
\mathcal{F}L^{p}
contains distributions of positive order (i.e. which are not measures, see
[
12
, Corollary 1.5]
) and the multiplication with characteristic functions is meaningless. The idea is to allow multiplication of
\mathcal{F}E
with a very restrictive class of functions with supports in open cones and equaling to positive constants in subcones away from the origin. (The more restrictive the class of multipliers is, the more spaces
E
will have it as Fourier multipliers!) From now, we assume that
E
has the following functions as Fourier multipliers:

(iv)

\psi f\in\mathcal{F}E
,
f\in\mathcal{F}E
, for all
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which are positively homogeneous of order
0
.

As before, the closed graph theorem implies that the map
\mathcal{F}E\rightarrow\mathcal{F}E
,
f\mapsto\psi f
, is continuous. We point out that we do not impose any solidity assumptions neither on
E
nor on
\mathcal{F}E
. Of course, if
\mathcal{F}E
is solid then it clearly satisfies
(iv)
. The spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1<p<\infty
, and the corresponding Sobolev spaces
W^{r,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
1<p<\infty
, satisfy
(i)
,
(ii)
,
(iii)
and
(iv)
(cf.
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.7, p. 446]
). The Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty)
or
p=q=\infty
, also satisfy
(i)
,
(ii)
,
(iii)
and
(iv)
(see
[
23
, Theorem 2.17, p. 257, and Corollary 5.2, p. 608]
and
[
22
, Theorem, p. 140]
); consequently, the Hölder spaces of non-integer order
s>0
satisfy these conditions too since they coincide with
B^{s}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It will be convenient to introduce the following terminology.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V
be a closed cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
V^{\prime}
an open cone satisfying
V\backslash\{0\}\subseteq V^{\prime}
. The smooth nonnegative function
\psi
is said to be a
smooth cut-off
for the pair
(V,V^{\prime})
if
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subseteq V^{\prime}
,
\psi
is positively homogeneous of order
0
outside of
B_{R}(0)
for some
R>0
and
\psi
equals a positive constant on
V\backslash B_{R}(0)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Given
V
and
V^{\prime}
as in the definition, there always exists a smooth cut-off
\psi
for
(V,V^{\prime})
. To see this, take
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subseteq V^{\prime}
and
\varphi=1
on a neighbourhood of
V\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Choose
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subseteq B_{1}(0)
and
\chi=1
on
B_{1/2}(0)
. The function
\psi(x):=(1-\chi(x))\varphi(x/|x|)
is a smooth cut-off for
(V,V^{\prime})
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
\psi_{j}
is a smooth cut-off for
(V_{j},V^{\prime}_{j})
,
j=1,2
, then
\psi_{1}\psi_{2}
is a smooth cut-off for
(V_{1}\cap V_{2},V^{\prime}_{1}\cap V^{\prime}_{2})
.

Given such
E
, we define the wave front set of a distribution with respect to
E
as follows. We first define the set
\Sigma^{E}(u)
for
u\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The point
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
does not belong to
\Sigma^{E}(u)
if there is an open cone
V
containing
\xi
and a smooth cut-off
\psi
for
(\overline{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi\mathcal{F}u\in\mathcal{F}E
. Clearly,
\Sigma^{E}(u)
is a closed cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds that
\Sigma^{E}(\varphi u)\subseteq\Sigma^{E}(u)
. Furthermore,
\Sigma^{E}(u)=\emptyset
if and only if
u\in E_{\operatorname{comp}}
.

Proof.

The compactness of the unit sphere together with a classical argument yields the validity of the second part. To show the first part, let
\xi_{0}\not\in\Sigma^{E}(u)
. There exist an open cone
V\ni\xi_{0}
and smooth cut-off
\psi
for
(\overline{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi\mathcal{F}u\in\mathcal{F}E
. Without loss in generality, we can assume that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{V}\neq\emptyset
. Denote by
c_{0}>0
the constant that
\psi
equals to in
V
away from the origin. Choose open cones
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
such that
\xi_{0}\in V_{1}
,
\overline{V_{1}}\backslash\{0\}\subseteq V_{2}
and
\overline{V_{2}}\backslash\{0\}\subseteq V
and pick a smooth cut-off
\chi
for
(\overline{V_{1}},V_{2})
. Write

\chi(\xi)\mathcal{F}(\varphi u)(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n}c_{0}^{-1}(I_{1}(\xi)+I_{2}(\xi)),

with

(3.2)

\displaystyle I_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\chi(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\eta)\psi(\xi-\eta)\mathcal{F}u(\xi-\eta)d\eta,

\displaystyle I_{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\chi(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\eta)(c_{0}-\psi(\xi-\eta))\mathcal{F}u(\xi-\eta)d\eta.

We are going to show that
I_{1}\in\mathcal{F}E
and
I_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which will complete the proof. To prove that
I_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, notice that there is
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-k}\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Write

(3.3)

\langle\xi\rangle^{l}|\partial^{\alpha}I_{2}(\xi)|\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}\leq\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\eta\rangle^{l}|\mathcal{F}\varphi(\eta)||\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\chi(\xi)||\partial^{\beta^{\prime\prime}}(c_{0}-\psi(\xi-\eta))|\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{k+l}d\eta.

There is
0<\varepsilon<1/2
such that
|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|\geq 2\varepsilon
,
\omega\in\overline{V_{2}}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
\omega^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash V)\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. For
\xi\in\overline{V_{2}}\backslash\{0\}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash(V\cup\{0\})
, we infer

\left|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{|\xi^{\prime}|}-\frac{\xi}{|\xi^{\prime}|}\right|\geq\left|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{|\xi^{\prime}|}-\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right|-\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-\frac{\xi}{|\xi^{\prime}|}\right|\geq 2\varepsilon-\frac{||\xi^{\prime}|-|\xi||}{|\xi^{\prime}|}\geq 2\varepsilon-\frac{|\xi^{\prime}-\xi|}{|\xi^{\prime}|}

from what we deduce that
|\xi^{\prime}-\xi|\geq\varepsilon|\xi^{\prime}|
. We only need to estimate the integrand when
\xi\in V_{2}
and
\xi-\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash(V\cup\{0\})
, for otherwise the right-hand side in (
3.3
) is uniformly bounded in
\xi
. We apply the above inequality with
\xi^{\prime}=\xi-\eta
to deduce that
|\eta|\geq\varepsilon|\xi-\eta|
which immediately gives that the right-hand side in (
3.3
) is uniformly bounded in
\xi
. This implies
I_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To show that
I_{1}\in\mathcal{F}E
, notice that the function given by the integral in (
3.2
) is exactly

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\eta)T_{\eta}(\psi\mathcal{F}u)d\eta\in\mathcal{F}E,

where the integral should be interpreted as a Bochner integral if
\mathcal{F}E
is a (TIB) and as a Pettis integral with respect to the weak* topology in
\mathcal{F}E
if the latter is a (DTIB). This yields
I_{1}\in\mathcal{F}E
and the proof is complete.
∎

If
O
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
, for each
x\in O
we define

\Sigma_{x}^{E}(u):=\bigcap_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O),\,\varphi(x)\neq 0}\Sigma^{E}(\varphi u).

Of course,
\Sigma^{E}_{x}(u)
is a closed cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
. Furthermore, a standard compactness argument (cf.
[
14
, p. 254]
) together with the above lemma yield that if
V
is an open cone containing
\Sigma^{E}_{x}(u)
, then there is an open neighbourhood
O^{\prime}\subseteq O
of
x
having a compact closure in
O
such that
\Sigma^{E}(\varphi u)\subseteq V
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O^{\prime})
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
O
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
we define the
E
-wave front set of
u
by:

WF^{E}(U):=\{(x,\xi)\in O\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})\,|\,\xi\in\Sigma^{E}_{x}(u)\}.

Of course,
WF^{E}(u)
is a closed conic subset of
O\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
. For
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
, we define the
E
-singular support of
u
as the complement of the set of points where
u
is behaving as an
E_{\operatorname{loc}}
distribution. To be precise:

\operatorname{sing}\operatorname{supp}_{E}(u):=O\backslash\{x\in O\,|\,\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O)\,\,\mbox{satisfying}\,\,\varphi(x)\neq 0\,\,\mbox{such that}\,\,\varphi u\in E\}.

Clearly,
\operatorname{sing}\operatorname{supp}_{E}(u)
is closed in
O
. In view of the above and repeating the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 8.1.3, p. 254]
verbatim, one shows the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

For each
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
,
\operatorname{pr}_{1}(WF^{E}(u))=\operatorname{sing}\operatorname{supp}_{E}(u)
. Furthermore, for every
v\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\operatorname{pr}_{2}(WF^{E}(v))=\Sigma^{E}(v)
.

Finally, in view of Theorem
2.10
, we immediately deduce the following result.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\psi
be a smooth cut-off for
(V_{0},V)
. If
V_{0}\backslash\{0\}\neq\emptyset
and
\operatorname{int}V^{*}\neq\emptyset
then there are
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
g\in L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi(D)f\not\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\psi(D)g\not\in L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\psi(D)\varphi\not\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Hence, if one wants for every smooth cut-off
\psi
and every
u\in E_{\operatorname{comp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to hold
\psi\mathcal{F}u\in\mathcal{F}E
, then one can never define a wave front for
E=L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
E=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
E=\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

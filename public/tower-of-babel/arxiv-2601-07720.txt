Principal bundles on toroidal compactifications of integral canonical models of abelian-type Shimura varieties
Peihang Wu

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct canonical extensions of principal 𝒢 c \mathcal{G}^{c} - (and M c M^{c} -)bundles on toroidal compactifications of integral canonical models of abelian-type Shimura varieties with hyperspecial levels.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to construct canonical and subcanonical extensions of integral models of principal bundles, as well as of automorphic vector bundles, over toroidal compactifications of integral canonical models of abelian-type Shimura varieties with hyperspecial level structure at a fixed prime number
p
.

Principal bundles and automorphic vector bundles are natural objects on Shimura varieties whose cohomology theory is related to representation theory and automorphic forms. If the underlying Shimura varieties are not proper, one needs to consider the
canonical extensions
and
subcanonical extensions
of these objects to toroidal compactifications.

Let
(G,X)
be a Shimura datum. For each
K\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
neat open compact, the double coset
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,X)(\mathbb{C}):=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash X\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K
has a unique canonical model
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}:=\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,X)
over the reflex field
E:=E(G,X)
. Let
G^{c}
be the quotient of
G
by the anti-cuspidal part of the connected center, which can be assumed to be identical to
G
for simplicity in this introduction. There is a
standard principal
G^{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle

\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}:=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash(X\times G^{c}_{\mathbb{C}}\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K)

over
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,X)(\mathbb{C})
with a
G^{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
-equivariant map
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\breve{X}
, such that it fits into the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(G,X)(\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\breve{X}.}

where
\breve{X}
is the Borel embedding of
X
.
By series of works of Harris and Milne (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
10
]
and
[
23
,
24
]
), this analytic
G^{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle algebraizes and canonically descends to an algebraic
G^{c}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
on the canonical model of the Shimura variety
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
. The construction of such bundles
\mathcal{E}
plays an important role in studying the arithmetic properties of certain automorphic forms (see
[
12
]
).

In order to correctly define and study log de Rham cohomology with coefficients in automorphic sheaves on integral models, or on the special fibers, of noncompact Shimura varieties, it is necessary to construct canonical extensions of certain principal
\mathcal{G}^{c}
-bundles (where
\mathcal{G}^{c}
is a certain reductive model of
G^{c}
) to toroidal compactifications to produce these coefficient sheaves (see
[
17
]
and
[
20
]
). One can also construct coefficients for coherent cohomology on toroidal compactifications of integral models or special fibers using canonical extensions of certain
M^{c}
-bundles, where
M^{c}
is a Levi component of
\mathcal{G}^{c}
defined by a Hodge cocharacter. In contrast to the situation over
\mathbb{C}
, where Deligne’s existence theorem
[
3
]
can be applied directly, the corresponding construction over integral models typically depends in an essential way on the explicit construction of the integral models themselves. Since we have already had integral canonical models of principal bundles and good toroidal compactifications for integral canonical models of abelian-type Shimura varieties by
[
21
]
and
[
28
]
, it can be expected that such canonical extensions should exist on these compactifications.

In the PEL-type or, more generally, Hodge-type cases, these canonical extensions are constructed by Lan
[
18
]
and Madapusi
[
22
, Sec. 4.3]
, respectively. The first work constructed canonical extensions by toroidal compactifications of Kuga families, while the second one achieved this by gluing vector bundles on a cover of the toroidal compactification. Although the two methods appear to be different, they both constructed the unique vector bundle that simultaneously extends a certain vector bundle on the union of the integral canonical model
\mathcal{S}_{K}
and the toroidal compactification of
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}
. In this paper, we will use the construction in
[
28
]
to generalize their constructions to abelian-type cases.

Idea of proof

We essentially reduce the problem to answering the following question:

•

Q:
Let
X
be a proper smooth scheme over a base ring
\mathcal{O}
(which is, for example, a DVR) with a normal crossings boundary divisor
D
. Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a finite Kummer étale cover of
X
that is the quotient map of
Y
by a finite group
\Delta
acting on it. Moreover, assume that the action of
\Delta
on
Y^{\circ}:=Y\times_{X}(X\backslash D)
is free. (Note that
Y
might not be smooth while it is so on
Y^{\circ}
, and that a Kummer étale map might not be flat!)

Given a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
on
Y
with an integrable connection with log poles along
Y\backslash Y^{\circ}
, and an equivariant
\Delta
-action, when does
\mathcal{W}:=(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})^{\Delta}
again correspond to a vector bundle (with an integrable log connection)?

In our situation,
X
is a toroidal compactification
\mathcal{S}_{K}^{\Sigma}(G,X_{b})
associated with a smooth and projective cone decomposition
\Sigma
, and
Y
is a cover of
\mathcal{S}_{K}^{\Sigma}(G,X_{b})
by a disjoint union of Hodge-type toroidal compactifications, whose cone decompositions are chosen as those induced by
\Sigma
. The sheaf
\mathcal{W}
is a locally free sheaf associated with the canonical extensions of certain automorphic vector bundles on the disjoint union. In this situation, reorganizing the results in the literature shows that
\mathcal{W}
has the desired property on a locus of codimension
\geq 2
. Hence, we only have to show the
local-freeness
of
\mathcal{W}
.

Roughly speaking,

Theorem C

(Theorem
3.17
)
.

In our situation, which will be clear in the paper, the torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathcal{W}
is always locally free.

The proof of the above theorem relies on an understanding of the canonical extensions at the boundary in the Hodge-type case.
Let
(G_{0},X_{0})
be a Hodge-type Shimura datum.
We need to understand the extension of the Hodge tensors to the boundary. Based on the works of Kisin (see
[
16
, Cor. 2.3.9]
) and Madapusi (see
[
22
, Prop. 4.3.7]
), the de Rham tensors
s_{\lambda,\mathrm{dR}}\in(\mathcal{V}_{{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}}\otimes\mathbb{Q})^{\otimes}
uniquely extend to
\widetilde{s}_{\lambda,\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{can}}\in(\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}})^{\otimes}
.
The canonical extension of the principal
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-bundle
\mathscr{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{0},K_{0}}
to the toroidal compactification
\mathcal{S}_{K_{0}}^{\Sigma_{0}}
is given by

\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}_{s_{\lambda}\mapsto\widetilde{s}_{\mathrm{dR},\lambda}}(V_{{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}},\mathcal{V}_{{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}}^{\mathrm{can}}),

where
V_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
is a lattice of a
(V,\psi)
defining the Siegel Shimura datum that
(G_{0},X_{0})
embeds, and
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}}^{\mathrm{can}}
is the canonical extension of the 1st relative de Rham homology of the pullback of the universal abelian scheme.

We show that

Proposition
.

For each
\lambda
and each cusp label representative
\Phi_{0}
with a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma_{0}^{+}(\Phi_{0})
, there is a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{\Phi_{0}}(\sigma)
on the boundary toric scheme
\mathcal{S}_{K_{\Phi_{0}}}(\sigma)
and a tensor
\widetilde{s}_{\Phi_{0},\lambda,\mathrm{dR}}(\sigma)\in\mathcal{V}_{\Phi_{0}}(\sigma)^{\otimes}
such that
\widetilde{s}_{\lambda,\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{can}}
and
\widetilde{s}_{\Phi_{0},\lambda,\mathrm{dR}}(\sigma)
coincide on
{(\mathcal{S}^{\Sigma_{0}}_{K_{0}})}{}^{\wedge{\ }}_{\mathcal{Z}_{[(\Phi_{0},\sigma)],K_{0}}}
.

With the above proposition, we can characterize the canonical extensions of principal
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-bundles on the integral model
\mathcal{S}_{K_{0}}^{\Sigma_{0}}
using a property in
[
11
]
and
[
8
]
(see Proposition
3.6
). We note that, to the best of our knowledge, this result concerning
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-bundles on integral models has not previously appeared in the literature, even in the context of PEL-type Shimura varieties.

The next step is to pass the torsors and vector bundles to a quotient by a finite Kummer étale group action.
By the results proved in
[
28
]
, we know that this finite Kummer étale quotient is finite étale when restricted to each individual stratum, and one can explicitly compute such a group quotient.
The first task now is to extend such a group quotient to the torsor. To do this, we need to have a robust and functorial theory of canonical models of canonical extensions over the generic fiber, and over the integral models of Shimura varieties. We can almost extract it from the literature, although most references had the assumptions such as assuming
G=G^{c}
or assuming
Z(G)^{\circ}
splits over a CM field extension. For our purpose, we define the desired canonical models of canonical extensions on
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}^{\Sigma}(G,X)
or integral canonical models on
\mathcal{S}_{K}(G,X)
by pulling back the corresponding objects defined by
(G^{c},X^{c})
.

Next, by an explicit computation of the quotient
\mathcal{W}
at the formal completion at the boundary, we show that
\mathcal{W}
is still formal locally a locally free sheaf. This computation uses toric charts and Proposition
3.6
crucially (see Lemma
3.23
). The proof also relies on a generalization of Deligne’s induction functor in
[
5
]
and
[
23
]
, especially a generalization of the
\mathscr{A}
-type groups, to treat torsors. See §
2.4
.

Finally, by adopting the construction in
[
28
]
, we extend the construction to all abelian-type Shimura data.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we review the notion of the canonical model of a principal
G^{c}
-bundle and record the related results. In §

, we give a list of facts in

2.4

In Section

In §

2.1.
Mixed Shimura varieties

The following definitions and facts on mixed Shimura varieties and their canonical models are needed for this note:

(1)

For any connected linear algebraic group
H

\mathbb{Q}
, define
H^{c}=H/Z_{ac}(H)
where

is the minimal
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroup in the connected multiplicative center
Z(H)^{\circ}
of

has equal
\mathbb{Q}
-split rank and

(2)

A mixed Shimura datum is denoted by
(Q,\mathcal{X})
. More precisely, let
Q
be a connected linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
. Denote by
W_{Q}
the unipotent radical of
Q
and by

a normal subgroup of
Q
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a homogeneous space with a

-equivariant map
\hbar:\mathcal{X}\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}},Q_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then
(Q,\mathcal{X})
is a mixed Shimura datum if it satisfies the conditions listed in
[
25
, Def. 2.1, (i)-(viii)]
.

(3)

For mixed Shimura data, one can define mixed Shimura varieties, morphisms between mixed Shimura data and varieties, and quotients in a way similar to the usual Shimura data/varieties. See
[
25
, Ch. 2 and Ch. 3]
.

(4)

If
W_{Q}=1
, the mixed Shimura datum is called a pure Shimura datum. Then
\mathcal{X}
is, by
[
25
, Cor. 2.12]
, a finite cover of
\hbar(\mathcal{X})
that is a homeomorphism over each connected component;
(Q,\hbar(\mathcal{X}))
is a usual Shimura datum.

(5)

There is a sequence of morphisms between Shimura data defined by the quotient of
Q

U_{Q}
and
W_{Q}

(Q,\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow(Q/U_{Q},\overline{\mathcal{X}})\longrightarrow(Q/W_{Q},\mathcal{X}_{h}).

Denote
\overline{Q}:=Q/U_{Q}
and
Q_{h}:=Q/W_{Q}
.

(6)

For any mixed Shimura datum
(Q,\mathcal{X})
and any neat open compact subgroup
K\subset Q(\mathbb{A}_{f})

\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(Q,\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{C}):=

Q(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathcal{X}\times Q(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K.

This complex manifold uniquely algebraizes to a complex algebraic variety
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(Q,\mathcal{X})_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Denote by
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(Q,\mathcal{X})
the canonical model of
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(Q,\mathcal{X})_{\mathbb{C}}

[
25

(7)

Denote by
\overline{K}
and
K_{h}

\overline{Q}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and

(2.1)

\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(Q,\mathcal{X})\xrightarrow{\ \mathbf{p}_{1}\ }\mathrm{Sh}_{\overline{K}}(\overline{Q},\overline{\mathcal{X}})\xrightarrow{\ \mathbf{p}_{2}\ }\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{h}}(Q_{h},\mathcal{X}_{h}).

, and the second morphism is a torsor under an abelian scheme over

In this subsection, we generalize the construction in

and

Note that our exposition will also use the theorems on canonical models of principal bundles on mixed Shimura varieties as known facts, while these theorems were proved by reducing to the pure Shimura data case. Hence, recording such results is still necessary for later use.

2.4.1.

Fix a base scheme
\mathcal{O}

\Gamma

\Gamma^{\prime}

Consider the inverse system
\{S_{\mathcal{H}}\}_{\mathcal{H}}
consisting of quasi-projective schemes
{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
over
\mathcal{O}
indexed by a cofinal collection of open compact subgroups
\mathcal{H}
in
\Gamma
. Suppose that there is a continuous right
\Gamma
-action on

. See
[
5
, 2.7.1]
.

Then, by
[
5
, 2.7.2]
,

Lemma 2.18

(
[
5
, Lem. 2.7.3]
)
.

If we fix
f:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
,
\Gamma^{\prime}\to\mathrm{Aut}\widehat{\pi}
and
e\in\widehat{\pi}
as above, then there is a functor
\mathrm{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
from the category of schemes
S
as above to the category of
(S^{\prime},c)
consisting of
\mathcal{O}
-schemes

with
\{S^{\prime}_{K}\}_{K}
an inverse system of quasi-projective
\mathcal{O}
-schemes indexed by open compact subgroups of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, and continuous morphisms

equipped with continuous equivariant
\Gamma^{\prime}
-actions. The functor is defined as

S\mapsto\mathrm{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}S:=(S\times\Gamma^{\prime})/\Gamma,

called an induction functor.

Moreover, when the kernel of
f:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}\hookrightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
is trivial, this is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

The functor in the first paragraph makes sense because the kernel of
f
is compact, and therefore, for each open compact

,
[S\times(\Gamma^{\prime}/K)]/\Gamma
is a finite disjoint union of a finite quotient of
S_{\mathcal{H}}
for sufficiently small
\mathcal{H}
(see
[
28
, 4.1.2]
for a computation). Moreover, if
f
is injective, the inverse is given by
S^{\prime}\mapsto S^{\prime}_{e}:=S^{\prime}\times_{c,\widehat{\pi}}e
.
∎

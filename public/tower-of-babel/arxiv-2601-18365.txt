A short note on A α A_{\alpha} -eigenvalues for simple graphs
Giovanni Barbarino

Abstract
Given a simple graph G G , its A α A_{\alpha} matrix is a convex combination with parameter α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \alpha\in[0,1] of its adjacency matrix and its degree diagonal matrices. Here we compare two lower bounds presented in [ 5 ] for the spectral radius of A α A_{\alpha} , and prove that one is better than the other when there are no isolated nodes in G G .



1
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph with
n
vertices. Let
D
be the diagonal matrix containing the degrees of the nodes of
G
on its diagonal, and let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
G
. For any
\alpha\in[0,1]
define the
A_{\alpha}
matrix (
[
3
]
) associated to
G
as

A_{\alpha}=\alpha D+(1-\alpha)A,

where
A_{0}\equiv A
,
A_{1}\equiv D
and
A_{1/2}
is half the signless Laplacian matrix of
G
.
A_{\alpha}
is in particular a symmetric and entry-wise nonnegative matrix, thus its spectral radius
\rho(A)
coincides with its largest nonnegative eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(A)
and also with its spectral norm
\|A\|
.

Estimating the eigenvalues of
A_{\alpha}
has been of interest to many researchers, see, among the most recent articles,
[
6
,
7
]
. In particular, its spectral radius
\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha})
(see
[
4
]
) has been given much importance since its value can be studied to guarantee the presence of specific spanning subgraphs (called factors) in
G
(
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
).

Several lower and upper bounds have been formulated throughout the years, linking the spectral radius
\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha})
to the parameter
\alpha
and the values
\Delta
,
\delta
, that are respectively the maximum degree and the minimum degree of the graph
G
.
In
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
many bounds for
\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha})
can be found, here we report two of them.

Proposition 1
.

[
3
]
If
G
is a graph with maximum degree
\Delta
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, then

\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha(\Delta+1)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta+1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-2\alpha)}\right).

If
G
is connected, the equality holds if and only if
G\cong K_{1,\Delta}
.

Theorem 1
.

[
5
]
Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
,
\Delta
and
\delta
be the maximum degree and the minimum degree of
G
, respectively, and
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Then

\lambda_{1}(A_{\alpha})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha(\Delta+\delta)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}\right).

If
G
is connected, the equality holds if and only if
G\cong K_{1,n-1}
.

In
[
5
]
, it is proved that the bound in Theorem
1
is greater than the one in Proposition
1
for
r
-regular graphs (i.e.
\Delta=\delta=r
) . Based on computational tests, it is also empirically observed that the same relation holds for non-regular graphs with
\delta>1
. As a consequence, the following conjecture have been formulated.

Conjecture 1
.

[
5
]
Let
G
be a simple graph,
\Delta
its maximum degree,
\delta
its minimum degree and
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Then

\alpha(\Delta+\delta)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}\geq\alpha(\Delta+1)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta+1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-2\alpha)}.

In the following, we show that the conjecture holds exactly for
\delta\geq 1
, while it is reversed for
\delta=0
.

2
Main Results

First of all, notice that

\alpha^{2}(\Delta+1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-2\alpha)=\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}\geq 0

(1)

so the argument of the second square root in the bound of Proposition
1
is never negative, and all formulas are well defined. Here we prove that for
\delta\geq 1
, Conjecture
1
holds.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
\Delta\geq\delta\geq 1
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Then

\alpha(\Delta+\delta)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}\geq\alpha(\Delta+1)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta+1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-2\alpha)}

and the equality holds if and only if
\delta=1
or
\alpha=0
or
\alpha=1
.

Proof.

After a first simplification and applying (
1
), the thesis becomes

\alpha(\delta-1)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}.

Since
\alpha\geq 0
and
\delta\geq 1
, then both sides are nonnegative and we can square everything. In order to carry on the computation, let us call

x:=\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2},\qquad y:=\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}

so that the thesis is equivalent to

\alpha^{2}(\delta-1)^{2}+2\alpha(\delta-1)\sqrt{x}+x\geq y

or

2\alpha(\delta-1)\sqrt{x}\geq y-x-\alpha^{2}(\delta-1)^{2}.

Now

\displaystyle y-x-\alpha^{2}(\delta-1)^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}-\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}-4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}-\alpha^{2}(\delta-1)^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left[(\Delta-\delta+\delta-1)^{2}-(\Delta-\delta)^{2}-(\delta-1)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)(\delta-1),

and substituting we get

2\alpha(\delta-1)\sqrt{x}\geq 2\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)(\delta-1).

From the observation that
\sqrt{x}\geq\alpha(\Delta-\delta)
, we conclude that the thesis holds. Moreover the two sides are equal only when
\alpha(\delta-1)=0
, giving us the conditions
\alpha=0
and
\delta=1
, or when
\sqrt{x}=\alpha(\Delta-\delta)
that happens only for
4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}=0
, i.e.
\alpha=1
.
∎

Notice that the result agrees with the observation made in
[
5
]
, stating that the two bounds are equal for
\delta=1
.
Since Proposition
2
requires
\delta\geq 1
, we can study what happens for
\delta=0
(i.e. when there are isolated nodes). Notice that in this case the graph
G
is disconnected. In this case we can prove that Conjecture
1

is false and actually the bound in Theorem
1
is always smaller or equal than the one in Proposition
1
.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose
\Delta\geq\delta=0
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Then

\alpha(\Delta+\delta)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-\delta)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}\leq\alpha(\Delta+1)+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta+1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-2\alpha)}

and the equality holds if and only if
\alpha=0
, or
\alpha=1
and
\Delta\geq 1
.

Proof.

By imposing
\delta=0
and applying (
1
), the thesis becomes

\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\Delta^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}\leq\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}.

Since
\alpha\geq 0
, we can square both sides and obtain the following equivalent relations:

\alpha^{2}\Delta^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}+2\alpha\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}+\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}

\alpha^{2}[\Delta^{2}-1-(\Delta-1)^{2}]\leq 2\alpha\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}

2\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)\leq 2\alpha\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}

The last relation is always true since
4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}\geq 0
.
For
\alpha=0
the two sides are equal, otherwise we can simplify and obtain that

\alpha(\Delta-1)=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}(\Delta-1)^{2}+4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}}.

The last relation holds only when
4\Delta(1-\alpha)^{2}=0
and
\Delta\geq 1
are both satisfied, so we need
\alpha=1
and
\Delta\geq 1
.
∎

We can then put together Proposition
2
and
3
to find all the cases when the bound in Theorem
1
is greater than the one in Proposition
1
, and when they are equal.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f(\delta,\Delta,\alpha)
be the bound in Theorem
1
and let
g(\Delta,\alpha)
be the bound in Proposition
1
, where
\alpha\geq 0
and
\Delta\geq\delta
are nonnegative integers. We have that

•

f(\delta,\Delta,\alpha)>g(\Delta,\alpha)\iff\delta\geq 2,\ \alpha\neq 0,1
,

•

f(\delta,\Delta,\alpha)=g(\Delta,\alpha)
if and only if one of the following hold

–

\alpha=0
,

–

\alpha=1
and
\Delta\neq 0
,

–

\delta=1
,

•

f(\delta,\Delta,\alpha)<g(\Delta,\alpha)
if and only if one of the following hold

–

\delta=0
and
\alpha\neq 0,1
,

–

\Delta=0
and
\alpha\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let us call
f=f(\delta,\Delta,\alpha)
and
g=g(\Delta,\alpha)
for brevity. Notice that Proposition
2
covers the case
\delta\geq 1
and Proposition
3
covers the case
\delta=0
, so together we have a complete description of the relations between
f
and
g
.

In particular, by Proposition
3
we have
\delta=0\implies f\leq g
, so
f>g\implies\delta\geq 1
and
by Proposition
2
in this case
f\geq g
with equality only in the cases
\delta=1
or
\alpha=0
or
\alpha=1
.
We conclude that

f>g\iff(\delta\neq 1,\ \alpha\neq 0,1)\wedge(\delta\geq 1)\iff\delta\geq 2,\ \alpha\neq 0,1.

The equality conditions given in Proposition
2
and Proposition
3
are

1.

\delta=1
(Proposition
2
),

2.

\delta\geq 1
,
\alpha=0
(Proposition
2
),

3.

\delta\geq 1
,
\alpha=1
(Proposition
2
),

4.

\delta=0
,
\alpha=0
(Proposition
3
),

5.

\delta=0
,
\alpha=1
,
\Delta\neq 0
(Proposition
3
).

Conditions number 2. and 4. can be joined into
\alpha=0
.
Conditions number 3. and 5. can be joined into
\alpha=1
,
\Delta\neq 0
.
This proves that
f=g
if and only if one of the conditions in the thesis is met.

Notice now that if
\delta\geq 1
then
f\geq g
by Proposition
2
, so
f<g
if and only if
\delta=0
and the equality conditions 4. and 5. above are not met, i.e.

f<g\iff(\delta=0)\wedge(\alpha\neq 0)\wedge[(\Delta=0)\vee(\alpha\neq 1)]\iff(\alpha\neq 0,\Delta=0)\vee(\delta=0,\alpha\neq 0,1).

∎

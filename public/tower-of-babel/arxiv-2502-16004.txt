Module-Theoretic characterizations of Gorenstein morphisms Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 13H10.
Andrew Soto Levins, Prashanth Sridhar

Abstract
. The Gorenstein property in local algebra admits several characterizations via its module category. The goal of this paper is to collect and generalize such characterizations to the relative setting, i.e., to Gorenstein morphisms as defined by [ AF92 ] . We achieve this by proving these characterizations more generally for graded-commutative Gorenstein dg-algebras.



1.
Introduction

The Gorenstein property is, roughly speaking, the algebraic analog of spaces with Poincaré duality. When this property is available, there are remarkable duality results in varying contexts: in cohomology theories, free resolutions and invariant theory to name a few, see for example
[
Ser61
,
Bas63a
,
Har67
,
Sta78
,
FHT88
]
. General duality statements are often much simpler in this case - for instance, the dualizing complex of a Gorenstein scheme is a line bundle. The Gorenstein property results in many nice consequences for the module category, and in return, it can be characterized purely in terms of its module category, see
[
Bas62
,
Bas63a
,
AB69
,
PS73
,
EG85
,
Buc21
,
AB89
,
Chr00
,
AM02
]
for numerous such applications. This property has also played an important role in noncommutative algebra and in particular in the representation theory of Artin algebras. We refer the reader to
[
Hun99
]
for more about its utility.

Avramov-Foxby in
[
AF92
]
developed a relative theory of the Gorenstein property in local algebra: a morphism of commutative Noetherian local rings
(R,\mathfrak{m})\to(S,\mathfrak{n})
is Gorenstein if
\operatorname{flat\,dim}_{R}(S)<\infty
and there exists an integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that the Bass numbers (
[
Bas63b
]
) satisfy
\mu^{i}_{R}(\mathfrak{m},R)=\mu^{i+d}_{S}(\mathfrak{n},S)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since Gorenstein rings can be characterized by the property that they have the nicest Bass numbers, Avramov-Foxby’s definition is taking a relative perspective by asking for the nicest relationship between Bass numbers along a ring homomorphism. A local ring
S
is Gorenstein if and only if the structure morphism
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\to S
is Gorenstein, where
p
is the characteristic of the residue field of
S
(
[
AF92
]
, Proposition 2.1). This definition is also an extension of Grothendieck’s definition of a Gorenstein morphism i.e., flat morphisms with Gorenstein fibers.

On the other hand, the Gorenstein condition has been developed and used in higher algebra, see
[
FHT88
,
FJ03
,
FIJ03
,
DGI06b
,
Yek13
,
Lur18
,
Sha20
,
HYZ23
,
BS25
,
BS24
]
. The connection between these two generalizations is that the relative theory can be characterized in terms of the corresponding notion in higher algebra, see
[
AF92
, Theorem 2.2]
. The goal of this paper is to collect and generalize to the relative setting, several module-theoretic characterizations of the Gorenstein condition in local algebra. We achieve this by exploiting the recent developments in the theory of differentially graded (dg) algebras - more specifically in the theory of non-positive, graded commutative, dg-algebras in
[
Sha20
,
Min21b
,
Min21a
,
BSSW25
,
YL23
,
HYZ23
]
. Below are two examples of classical module theoretic statements this paper generalizes to the relative setting:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Fox77
]

A commutative Noetherian local ring
R
is Gorenstein if and only if there exists a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module
M
such that
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
and
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

A commutative Noetherian local ring
R
is Gorenstein if and only if there exists a non-zero finitely generated
M
such that
\operatorname{inj\,dim}(M)<\infty
,
M
has finite Auslander bound (
Definition

2.19
) and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,R)=0
for
i\gg 0
.

The Auslander bound is an invariant arising from an important conjecture of Auslander concerning finite dimensional algebras. It was developed by Wei in
[
Wei11
]
and subsequently by many others. For its connections with the Auslander-Reiten conjecture, see
[
Wei11
]
. Note that the characterization in
Theorem

1.2
apriori appears weaker than the one in
Theorem

1.1
. Several such module-theoretic characterizations are scattered across the literature. Our relative statement is as follows:

(Special case of
Theorem

3.1
)
.

Let

be a map of commutative noetherian local rings such that

. Let

be the derived fiber (

). Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Phi

(2)

(

) and

).

(3)

(

2.18
) and

(5)

For every
M\in\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\mathrm{F})

2.8
), there exists

(

2.8

2.19
), and

Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a generating set for

, and set

). There exists

The statement (4) above extends the characterization of the Gorenstein property in terms of Gorenstein dimension and Gorenstein projective modules, (5) in terms of virtual smallness, (6) in terms of maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules and (7) in terms of the derived category of the Koszul complex associated to the maximal ideal. We prove
Theorem

1.3
by establishing the equivalence of the assertions more generally for non-positive, graded commutative, cohomologically bounded, dg-algebras (
Setup

2.10
) in
Theorem

3.1
. This is indeed more general due to
[

, Theorem 2.2]
. As noted earlier, the proofs of these results rely on recent developments in the theory of resolutions and homological dimensions of such dg-algebras. The classical characterizations for a Gorenstein ring

can be recovered by taking

and

to be the structure morphism in
Theorem

1.3
, where
p
is the residue characteristic of
S
. For instance, statements (2) and (3) in
Theorem

recover
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
under this hypothesis.

The paper has two sections: in section 2, we collect all the notions from the literature we need and in section 3, we prove
Theorem

, our main result.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect from various sources, the definitions and notions we need from the theory of differential graded algebras.

Notation 2.1
.

A
differential graded (dg) algebra
is a graded algebra
A=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{j}
equipped with a degree
1

\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\partial_{A}
that squares to 0 and satisfies the Leibniz rule:

\partial_{A}(xy)=\partial_{A}(x)y+(-1)^{\deg{x}}x\partial_{A}(y).

Say
A
is
graded commutative
if
xy=(-1)^{|x||y|}yx
for all homogeneous
x,y\in A
and
x^{2}=0
if
\deg(x)
is odd. A
morphism
of dg algebras is a degree
0
algebra homomorphism that commutes with differentials and is a
quasi-isomorphism
if it induces an isomorphism on cohomology. We denote by
A^{\natural}
the underlying graded algebra of a dg-algebra
A
. We say a dg-algebra
A
is non-positive if
A^{i}=0
for
i>0
.

Let
A
be a dg-algebra. A right (resp. left)
dg-
A
-module
is a graded right (resp. left)
A^{\natural}
-module
M=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{j}
equipped with a degree
1

\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\partial_{M}
that squares to 0 and satisfies the Leibniz rule:

\partial_{M}(mx)=\partial_{M}(m)x+(-1)^{\deg(m)}m\partial_{A}(x)\quad(\text{resp. }\partial_{M}(xm)=\partial_{A}(x)m+(-1)^{\deg(x)}x\partial_{M}(m)).

If
A
is graded commutative and if
M
is a right dg
A
-module, then
M
is also a left dg
R
-module with left action
rm\coloneqq(-1)^{\deg(r)\deg(m)}mr
for homogeneous elements
r\in R
and
m\in M
. This gives
M
a dg
A
-
A
-bimodule structure and induces an equivalence between the category of right and left dg-modules.

A dg-module
M
is said to be
finitely generated
if the underlying graded
A^{\natural}
-module is finitely generated. A
morphism
of dg-
A
-modules is a degree
0

A^{\natural}
-linear map that commutes with differentials. Such a morphism is a
quasi-isomorphism
if it induces an isomorphism on cohomology.

Given dg-
A
-modules
M
and
N
, the tensor product
M\otimes_{A}N
is a dg-
A
-module with differential
m\otimes n\mapsto d_{M}(m)\otimes n+(-1)^{|m|}m\otimes d_{N}(n)
. Similarly, given dg-
A
-modules
M
and
N
, we may form the internal Hom object
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
, which is a dg-
A
-module with underlying
A^{\natural}
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\natural}}(M,N)
and differential
\alpha\mapsto d_{N}\alpha-(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha d_{M}
. A map in
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
of degree
0
is a cocycle if and only if it is a morphism of dg-
A
-modules.

For a dg-algebra
A
, let
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
denote the category of dg-
A
-modules,
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{f}}(A)
the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
given by dg-
A
-modules
M
such that
H(M)
is finitely generated over
H(A)
, and
\operatorname{mod}(A)
the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{f}}(A)
given by dg-
A
-modules that are finitely generated over
A
. We form the (triangulated) derived categories
\operatorname{D}(A)
,
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{f}}(A)
, and
\operatorname{D}^{b}(A)
by inverting quasi-isomorphisms in
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
,
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{f}}(A)
, and
\operatorname{mod}(A)
, respectively. For a construction of the derived category of a dg-algebra, see
[
Sta23
,
Tag 09KV
]
. See
[
Yek19
]
for a comprehensive treatment of derived categories of dg-algebras.

Let
\operatorname{D}^{+}(A)
(resp.
\operatorname{D}^{-}(A)
) denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{D}(A)
of dg-modules
M
such that
\mathrm{H}^{i}(M)=0
for
i\ll 0
(resp.
\mathrm{H}^{i}(M)=0
for
i\gg 0
). For a dg-module
M
, write

\inf(M):=\inf\{i\>|\>\mathrm{H}^{i}(M)\neq 0\}.

\sup(M):=\sup\{i\>|\>\mathrm{H}^{i}(M)\neq 0\}.

\operatorname{amp}(M)=\sup(M)-\inf(M).

Given a dg-module
M
, its
i
-th cohomological shift,
M[i]
, is the dg-module with underlying module given by
M[i]^{j}=M^{i+j}
and differential
d_{M[i]}=(-1)^{i}d_{M}
.

Note that there is a natural map of dg-algebras
A\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
and
A\to\mathbf{k}
, where
\mathbf{k}
is the residue class field of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
A
be a non-positive dg-algebra and
M
a dg-
A
-module. For

, we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{\geq i}M

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\cdots\to 0\to M^{i}/\operatorname{Im}(d_{M}^{i-1})\to M^{i+1}\to\cdots\right),

\displaystyle\sigma^{\leq i}M

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(\cdots\to M^{i-1}\to\ker(d^{i}_{M})\to 0\to\cdots\right).

Both

-modules and the natural map

(resp.

(resp. at most

).

Definition 2.3
.

[

]

Let
\Phi:(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\to(S,\mathfrak{n},l)
be a map of commutative noetherian local rings. The

of

is the non-positive, graded commutative dg-algebra

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A
be a dg-algebra. A dg-

-module

A dg-

We now recall the definition of projective and injective dimension for dg-modules.

(

]
)

(
[

Let

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
be as in
Setup

2.10
. If there exists
M\in\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(A)
such that (i)
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}(M)<\infty
and (ii) there exists
0\neq N_{M}\in\langle M\rangle
such that
\mathrm{Gdim}_{A}(N_{M})<\infty
, then
A
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Let
k
denote the residue field of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
. We will show that
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}(A)<\infty
.
[
Min21a
, Corollary 2.31]
shows that
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}(A)=\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A)
and we show that
\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A)
is cohomologically bounded above. We calculate

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,N_{M})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(N_{M},A),A))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(N_{M},A)\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}_{A}k,A)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(N_{M},A)\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}_{A}k\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}_{k}k,A)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{RHom}_{k}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(N_{M},A)\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}_{A}k,\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A))

where the first isomorphism is due to the reflexivity of
N_{M}
and the second and fourth are adjunctions. The object
\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(N_{M},A)\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}_{A}k
viewed as a complex of vector spaces is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology, which is a direct sum of shifts of
k
. We thus have

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,N_{M})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\bigoplus k[n]^{\oplus b_{n}},\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(k[n]^{\oplus b_{n}},\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A)[-n]^{\oplus b_{n}}.

Since
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}N_{M}<\infty
, we see that
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,N_{M})<\infty
. The above quasi-isomorphism then implies that
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(k,A)<\infty
and hence that
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}(A)<\infty
by
[
Min21b
, Corollary 2.31]
, finishing the proof.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
be as in
Setup

2.10
. Then
A
is Gorenstein if and only if for every
0\not\simeq M\in\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(A)
, there exists
0\not\simeq N_{M}\in\langle M\rangle\cap\langle\mathcal{G}_{0}\rangle
.

Proof.

The forward implication follows from
[
HYZ23
, Theorem 1.2]
. In more detail,
[
HYZ23
, Theorem 1.2(3)]
implies
\mathrm{Gdim}_{A}(M)<\infty
for all
M\in\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(A)
if
A
is Gorenstein. Applying
[
HYZ23
, Theorem 1.2(1) and (2)]
then gives the desired conclusion. To prove the converse, let
\bar{E}
denote the injective hull of the residue field of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
over
\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
. By Brown representability, there exists an object
E\in\operatorname{D}(A)
such that for all
M\in\operatorname{D}(A)
,
\operatorname{flat\,dim}_{A}(M)=\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,E))
and
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,E))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)}(H^{-i}(M),\bar{E})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, see
[
BSSW25
, Definition 2.1, Lemma 7.12 and the discussion above it]
. Setting
K:=A//\underline{x}
for
\underline{x}
a minimal generating set for the maximal ideal of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
, we see that
B:=\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(K,E)\in\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(A)
and
\operatorname{inj\,dim}_{A}(B)<\infty
. Choose a nontrivial
N_{B}\in\langle B\rangle\cap\langle\mathcal{G}_{0}\rangle
. Since the subcategory of reflexive modules of
\operatorname{D}(A)
is a thick subcategory and
N_{B}\in\langle\mathcal{G}_{0}\rangle
,
N_{B}
is reflexive. Moreover, if
X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]
is a triangle in
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(A)
with
X,Y,Z
reflexive, then the inequality
\mathrm{Gdim}_{A}(Y)\leq\mathrm{sup}\{\mathrm{Gdim}_{A}(X),\mathrm{Gdim}_{A}(Z)\}
implies that
\mathrm{Gdim}_{A}(N_{B})<\infty
. Applying
Proposition

3.2
with
M=N_{B}
finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be as in
Setup

2.10
. Suppose
M\in\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(A)
is such that the quantities
B(M),P_{A}(M,A)
and
P_{A}(M,M)
are all finite, then so is
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{A}(M)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\sup(M)=0
. For any sppj resolution
P
of
M
as in
Definition

2.5
, a standard two out of three argument shows that for all
i\geq 0
,
B(M_{i}),P_{A}(M_{i},A),P_{A}(M_{i},M)<\infty
. Another two out of three argument using the facts
P_{A}(M_{i},A),P_{A}(M_{i},M)<\infty
shows that
P_{A}(M_{i},M_{i})<\infty
for all
i\geq 0
. Now choose a sppj resolution
P
of
M
such that
\sup(M_{i})=\sup(M)=0
for all
i\geq 0
. This is always possible since we can choose
\mathrm{H}^{\sup}(f_{i})
to have a non-trivial kernel and the exact sequence
\mathrm{H}^{\sup(M_{i})}(M_{i+1})\to\mathrm{H}^{\sup(M_{i})}(P_{i})\to\mathrm{H}^{\sup(M_{i})}(M_{i})\to 0
gives the desired property. We now claim that there exists
n\geq 0
such that
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M_{n},A)=0
. If
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,A)=0
, we are done. If
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,A)<0
, then applying
\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(-,A)
to the triangle

M_{1}\xrightarrow{g_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}M_{0}\to M_{1}[1]

shows that
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M_{1},A)=0
. If
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,A)>0
, the same process shows that
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M,A)>\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M_{1},A)
. Repeating this process for
i\geq 1
sufficiently many times yields an integer
n
such that
\sup\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M_{n},A)=0
. Fix such an integer
n
. Now for each integer
i\geq 1
, applying
\mathrm{RHom}_{A}(M_{n},-)
to the triangle

M_{n+i+1}\xrightarrow{g_{n+i+1}}P_{n+i}\xrightarrow{f_{n+i}}M_{n+i}\to M_{n+i+1}[1]

shows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M_{n},M_{n+1})\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M_{n},M_{n+i})
. If
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M_{n},M_{n+1})\neq 0
, then
B(M_{n})=\infty
. Thus, it must be the case that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M_{n},M_{n+1})=0
. Therefore the triangle

M_{n+1}\xrightarrow{g_{n+1}}P_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}M_{n}\to M_{n+1}[1]

splits in
\operatorname{D}(A)
and
M_{n}\in\mathcal{P}
. By
[
Min21b
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{A}(M)<\infty
.

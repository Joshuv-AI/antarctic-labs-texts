Nonlinear Schrödinger equations with a critical, inverse-square potential
Bartosz Bieganowski, Adam Konysz, Simone Secchi

Abstract
. We study the existence of solutions of the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u − ( N − 2 ) 2 4 ​ | x | 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) -\Delta u+V(x)u-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}u=f(x,u) where V : ℝ N → ℝ V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R} and f : ℝ N × ℝ → ℝ f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} are periodic with respect to x ∈ ℝ N . x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}. We assume that V V has positive essential infimum, f f satisfies weak growth conditions and N ≥ 3 . N\geq 3. The approach to the problem uses variational methods with nonstandard functional setting. We obtain the existence of the ground state solution using the new profile decomposition. Keywords: variational methods, singular potential, nonlinear Schrödinger equation, AMS Subject Classification: 35Q60, 35Q55, 35A15, 35J20, 58E05



1.
Introduction

In this paper we are interested in finding so called
standing waves
for nonlinear Schrödinger equation with inverse-square potential with critical coefficient:

(1.1)

i\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=-\Delta_{x}\Psi+\left(V(x)+\lambda\right)\Psi-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}\Psi-g(x,|\Psi|)\Psi,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}),

where
i^{2}=-1
and
N\geq 3.

Looking for solution of the form
\Psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
leads us to the following equation:

(1.2)

-\Delta u+V(x)u-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}u=f(x,u),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

where
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
are periodic with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
N\geq 3.
Moreover we assume that
f
has superlinear and subcritical growth. The Schrödinger equation plays an important role in many physical models. For example, in nonlinear optics it describes the propagation of light through optical structures with periodic structure (such as photonic crystals). In the presence of defects in the material, the inverse-square potential (Hardy-type)
\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}
appears in the equation. Such singular potentials also arise in many other areas as quantum mechanics, nuclear and molecular physics and even quantum cosmology. Reader who would like to know more about physical motivations of Hardy-type potentials may check
[
12
,
10
,
8
]
and references therein.

In the mathematical perspective the inverse-square potentials were widely investigated by many authors. In most of the works the Hardy type potential is controlled by a constant
\mu<\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}.
For instance such subcritical problem was investigated by Guo and Mederski in
[
13
]
in a strongly indefinite case. Other exemplary contributions with subcritical nonlinearity are due to Li, Li and Tang in
[
15
]
with Berestycki-Lions type conditions, as well as Zhang and Zhang in
[
26
]
for the system of Schrödinger equations. For critical nonlinearity, i.e., nonlinearity with growth with power equal to the critical Sobolev exponent
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
there are results by Deng, Jin, Peng
[
6
]
, Felli and Pistoia
[
9
]
, Smets in
[
22
]
where he obtained nonexistence of solutions, and Terracini in
[
24
]
. Critical case when - as in (
1.2
) - the critical constant
\mu=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
introduces many additional difficulties. The natural norm for our problem is not equivalent to the classical one in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
which motivates the definition of the space
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to be the completion of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the natural norm to our problem. A crucial difficulty is that
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is not invariant under the translations, which makes compactness arguments significantly more delicate. The above mentioned nonstandard functional space embeds continuously into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
s\in(0,1).
This embedding is due to the inequality proven by Frank in
[
11
]
, whence the space in the context of partial differential equations with Hardy-type potentials was firstly defined independently by Suzuki in
[
21
]
and by Trachanas and Zographopulos in
[
25
]
. Later, Mukherjee, Nam, and Nguyen also defined and used this space in
[
17
]
. Most recently, the Berestycki–Lions scalar-field equation in this setting was studied by Bieganowski and Strzelecki
[
3
]
.

In this paper the nonlinearity satisfies superlinear and subcritical growth conditions, see (F1)-(F3) below. The idea for generating Cerami sequences is based on the use of
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
, in which the Nehari manifold appears and the assumption (F4) is classical for such methods.

In this setting, neither the Palais-Smale nor Cerami compactness condition is satisfied, so compactness must be recovered by diffrent means. The classical method for obtaining strong convergence of the Cerami and Palais-Smale sequences is the so-called
profile decomposition,
which was introduced by Gérard
[
14
]
and Nawa
[
18
]
. However, these theorems cannot be applied in our problem due to the fact that singular potential is not translation-invariant. Therefore, inspired by
[
3
]
, we establish a new profile decomposition adapted to the non-translation-invariant structure of
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This part is particularly technically complicated since we show that any possible weak limit of a translated Cerami sequence must be a critical point of the limiting functional (without the singular potential) defined only on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
By comparing energies as in
[
13
]
, we exclude such nontrivial limits, which ultimately forces strong convergence.

In what follows through the paper we assume the following conditions (the notation
A\lesssim B
should be understood as
A\leq C\times B
, for some suitable constant
C
which is independent of
A
and
B
):

(V)

V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

V
is
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-periodic in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
V_{0}:=\essinf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)>0
.

(F1)

f\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Carathéodory function that is
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-periodic in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies

|f(x,u)|\lesssim 1+|u|^{p-1}\hbox{ for all }u\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for some
p\in\left(2,2^{*}\right)
.

(F2)

f(x,u)=o(u)
as
u\to 0
uniformly with respect to
x
.

(F3)

F(x,u)/u^{2}\to\infty
uniformly in
x
as
|u|\to\infty
, where
F
is the usual antiderivative of
f
with respect to
u
, namely
F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,s)\,ds
.

(F4)

u\mapsto\frac{f(x,u)}{|u|}
is nondecreasing on
(-\infty,0)
and on
(0,\infty)
.

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that (V), (F1)–(F4) hold. Then, there exists a ground state solution to (
1.2
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the functional framework associated with (
1.2
) and recall several preliminary results in the space
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Section 3 is devoted to the variational construction of Cerami sequences. In Section 4 we establish the new profile decomposition adapted to the structure of
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Finally, in Section 5 we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
by showing the strong convergence of the Cerami sequence and verifying that the limit is a ground state.

In what follows,
|\cdot|_{k}
denotes the usual
L^{k}
-norm.

2.
Variational and functional setting

In view of the Hardy inequality

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx,

with optimal constant
\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
, one can think of equipping
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with equivariant norm as in
[
13
]
. However as the optimal constant in (
2.1
) appears in (
1.2
), it is not possible, i.e. we loose completeness of the space. Having that in mind as an energy space we choose

X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\overline{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where the closure is taken with respect to the norm

\|u\|^{2}_{X^{1}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}u^{2}+u^{2}\right)\,dx.

The norm is then characterized by (see
[
25
]
)

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{X^{1}}

\displaystyle=[u]^{2}+|u|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle[u]^{2}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}u^{2}\,dx-\frac{N-2}{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}u^{2}\,dS\right).

Moreover,
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Hilbert space, with the scalar product given by

\langle u,v\rangle_{X^{1}}:=\\
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\nabla u\nabla v\,dx-\int_{|x|>\varepsilon}\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x|^{2}}uv\,dx-\frac{N-2}{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}uv\,dS\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\,dx.

From
[
11
]
, we have the following chain of inclusions:

(2.2)

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hbox{ for every }s\in[0,1)

In particular,

X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

for
t\in[2,2^{*})
, and

X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{t}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

for
t\in[2,2^{*})
.
We introduce a new norm

\|u\|^{2}:=[u]^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}\,dx,

which, thanks to (V), is equivalent with
\|\cdot\|_{X^{1}}
. Moreover, by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
we will denote the corresponding scalar product on
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let us define the energy functional
{\mathcal{J}}\colon X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
by

{\mathcal{J}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx,

which is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
and its critical points correspond to weak solutions of (
1.2
), see e.g.
[
3
]
.

Before proceeding, we record the following elementary inequalities, see for instance
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under assumptions (F1)–(F4),

f(x,u)u\geq 2F(x,u)\geq 0.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

|f(x,u)|\leq\varepsilon|u|+C_{\varepsilon}|u|^{p-1}.

3.
Critical point theory

We review an abstract theoerem proved in
[
1
]
. For a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-functional
{\mathcal{J}}
defined on a Hilbert space
E
, we define the Nehari manifold by

{\mathcal{N}}:=\left\{u\in E\setminus\{0\}:{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)(u)=0\right\},

which contains all nontrivial critical points of
{\mathcal{J}}
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space. Let the functional
{\mathcal{J}}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

{\mathcal{J}}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-{\mathcal{I}}(u),

where
{\mathcal{I}}\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
class. Suppose that

(J1)

there exists a constant
r>0
such that

\inf_{\|u\|=r}{\mathcal{J}}(u)>0={\mathcal{J}}(0),

(J2)

\frac{{\mathcal{I}}(t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}}\to\infty
whenever
t_{n}\to\infty
in
\mathbb{R}
and
u_{n}\to u\neq 0
in
E
,

(J3)

for all
t>0
and
u\in{\mathcal{N}},
there holds

\frac{t^{2}-1}{2}{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}(u)(u)-{\mathcal{I}}(tu)+{\mathcal{I}}(u)\leq 0.

Then
\Gamma\neq\emptyset
,
{\mathcal{N}}\neq\emptyset
and

(3.1)

0<\inf_{\|u\|=r}{\mathcal{J}}(u)\leq c:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}{\mathcal{J}}(\gamma(t))=\inf_{{\mathcal{N}}}{\mathcal{J}}=\inf_{u\in E\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{J}}(tu).

Moreover there exists a Cerami sequence for
{\mathcal{J}}
on the level
c
, i.e. a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset E
such that

{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\to c,\quad(1+\|u_{n}\|){\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0.

To use this abstract result in our setting, we choose
E:=X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
{\mathcal{I}}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx
. In the next lemma we verify the validity of assumptions (J1)–(J3) in our case.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
r>0
such that

\inf_{u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \|u\|=r}{\mathcal{J}}(u)>0.

Proof.

For each
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we use (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) to compute

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,u)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\varepsilon|u|_{2}^{2}-C_{\varepsilon}|u|^{p}_{p}\gtrsim\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\varepsilon\|u\|^{2}-C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|^{p}.

Hence, for small
\varepsilon>0
we can choose
r>0
such that
{\mathcal{J}}(u)>0
when
\|u\|=r
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
t_{n}\to\infty
and
(u_{n})_{n}\subset X
,
u_{n}\to u\neq 0
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}}\,dx\to\infty.

Proof.

From (F3) and Fatou’s lemma

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}u_{n}^{2}}u_{n}^{2}\,dx\geq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,u}\frac{F(t_{n}u_{n})}{t_{n}^{2}u_{n}^{2}}u_{n}^{2}\,dx\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
u\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

{\mathcal{J}}(u)\geq{\mathcal{J}}(tu)-\frac{t^{2}-1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u)(u)\hbox{ for }t\in(0,\infty).

In particular,
{\mathcal{J}}(u)\geq{\mathcal{J}}(tu)
for
t\in(0,\infty)
and
u\in{\mathcal{N}}
, and (J3) holds.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
5
,
23
,
2
]
with minor modifications. We omit the details.
∎

4.
Analysis of Cerami sequences

We recall the following variant of the Lions’ concentration-compactness principle.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
3
, Lemma 4.4]
)
.

Let
R>0
and
(u_{n})_{n}\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a bounded sequence. If

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(y,R)}u_{n}^{2}\,dx=0

then
u_{n}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
p\in(2,2^{*})

Lemma 4.2
.

Any Cerami sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is bounded in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Up to subsequences, we suppose on the contrary that
\|u_{n}\|\to\infty
, and we define

v_{n}:=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\hbox{ in }X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ v_{n}\to v\hbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We claim that

(4.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(y,r)}|v_{n}|^{2}\,dx>0.

Indeed, if the left-hand side is zero, then
v_{n}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
from Lions’ lemma (Lemma
4.1
). In view of Lemma
2.1
, we get that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,sv_{n})\,dx\to 0
for any
s>0
. Let us fix
s>0
and observe that Lemma
3.4
implies

{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\geq{\mathcal{J}}(sv_{n})+o(1).

Hence

1\gtrsim\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{J}}(sv_{n})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|v_{n}\|^{2}=\frac{s^{2}}{2}.

Since
s>0
is arbitrary we get contradiction. Hence (
4.1
) is proved.
As a consequence, we can find a sequence
(y_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and radius
r>0
such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{B(y_{n},r)}|v_{n}|^{2}\,dx=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{B(0,r)}|v_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})|^{2}\,dx>0.

Therefore, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}(\cdot+y_{n})\to v
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v\neq 0
. If
v(x)\neq 0,
then
|u_{n}(x+y_{n})|=|v_{n}(x+y_{n})|\|u_{n}\|\to\infty
. Using (F3)

\frac{F(x,u_{n}(x+y_{n}))}{\|u_{n}\|^{2}}=\frac{F(x,u_{n}(x+y_{n}))}{|u_{n}(x+y_{n})|^{2}}|v_{n}(x+y_{n})|^{2}\to\infty.

Then by the Fatou’s lemma we get

\displaystyle 0=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|^{2}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(x,u_{n}(x+y_{n})}{\|u_{n}\|^{2}}\right)=-\infty,

which is again a contradiction.
∎

Let us define the functional
{\mathcal{J}}_{0}\colon H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
with the formula

{\mathcal{J}}_{0}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2}-F(x,u)\,dx.

Now we are able to prove the profile decomposition of Cerami sequences that are in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Since
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a dense subspace, we can replace any Cerami sequence in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by a Cerami sequence in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore, this additional assumption is not restrictive, but it simplifies a bit the proof, since
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the maximal, translation-invariant subspace of
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, see
[
3
]
.

The following regularity result will be used in the proof of our decomposition theorem for Cerami sequences. We recall its proof for the reader’s sake.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
u
is a distribution on
\Omega
such that
\Delta u\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, then
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We follow
[
7
]
. Let
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be the unique solution to the equation
\Delta v=\Delta u
in the sense of distributions. Hence
\Delta(u-v)=0
in the sense of distributions, and Weyl’s Lemma (see
[
20
, Theorem 8.12]
) implies that
u-v\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. It follows that
u=(u-v)+v
is the sum of a smooth function and of a function from
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. In particular,
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a bounded in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
Cerami sequence of
{\mathcal{J}}
at level
c\geq 0
. Then there are
m\geq 0
,
\overline{u}_{0}\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and there are sequences
(\overline{u}_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
(x^{i}_{n})_{0\leq i\leq m}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
x^{0}_{n}=0,|x^{i}_{n}|\to\infty,|x^{i}_{n}-x^{j}_{n}|\to\infty,i\neq j,i,j=1,\ldots,m
and passing to a subsequence the following hold:

(4.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{0})=0;

(4.3)

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{i}\neq 0\hbox{ and }{\mathcal{J}}_{0}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{i})=0\hbox{ for each }1\leq i\leq m;

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=0}^{m}\overline{u}_{i}\right\|\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

(4.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(u_{n})\to{\mathcal{J}}(\overline{u}_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{J}}_{0}(\overline{u}_{i})

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1.

Since
(u_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
\overline{u}_{0}\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup\overline{u}_{0}\hbox{ in }X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ u_{n}\to\overline{u}_{0}\hbox{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\hbox{ and }u_{n}\to\overline{u}_{0}\hbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(\varphi)-{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{0})(\varphi)=\langle u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0},\varphi\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,\overline{u}_{0})\right)\varphi\,dx\to 0,

where
\langle u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0},\varphi\rangle\to 0
is a consequence of a weak convergence and the convergence
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,\overline{u}_{0})\right)\varphi\,dx\to 0
follows from the Vitali convergence theorem. Therefore
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{0})=0
.

Step 2.

Let
v_{n}^{1}:=u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}
. Suppose that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(z,1)}|v_{n}^{1}|^{2}\,dx\to 0.

Then
u_{n}\to\overline{u}_{0}
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the statement holds for
m=0
.

Note that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(v_{n}^{1})

\displaystyle=\langle u_{n},v_{n}^{1}\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})v_{n}^{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\|v_{n}^{1}\|^{2}+\langle\overline{u}_{0},v_{n}^{1}\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})v_{n}^{1}\,dx.

Therefore

\|v_{n}^{1}\|^{2}={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(v_{n}^{1})-\langle\overline{u}_{0},v_{n}^{1}\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})v_{n}^{1}\,dx.

Since
0={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{0})(v_{n}^{1})=\langle\overline{u}_{0},v_{n}^{1}\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u}_{0})v_{n}^{1}\,dx
, we obtain

\|v_{n}^{1}\|^{2}={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(v_{n}^{1})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,\overline{u}_{0}))v_{n}^{1}\,dx.

Since
(v_{n}^{1})_{n}
is bounded,

\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(v_{n}^{1})\|\leq\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\|v_{n}^{1}\|\to 0.

From the Lions’ lemma we get that
v_{n}^{1}\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Observe that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u}_{0})v_{n}^{1}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\overline{u}_{0}v_{n}^{1}|\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\overline{u}_{0}|^{p-1}|v_{n}^{1}|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\varepsilon|\overline{u}_{0}|_{2}|v_{n}^{1}|_{2}+C_{\varepsilon}|\overline{u}_{0}|_{p}^{p-1}|v_{n}^{1}|_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon|\overline{u}_{0}|_{2}\limsup_{n\to\infty}|v_{n}^{1}|_{2}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u}_{0})v_{n}^{1}\,dx\to 0
. Similarly
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})v_{n}^{1}\,dx\to 0
. Hence
v_{n}^{1}\to 0
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

From now on, we fix
s\in\left(\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{(p-2)N}{2p}\right\},1\right)
.

Step 3.

Suppose that
(z_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that

(4.6)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{B(z_{n},1+\sqrt{N})}|v_{n}^{1}|^{2}\,dx>0.

Then there is
\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that (up to a subsequence)

\hbox{{(i)} }|z_{n}|\to\infty,\hbox{ {(ii)} }u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\rightharpoonup\overline{u}\neq 0\mbox{ in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\hbox{ {(iii)} }{\mathcal{J}}_{0}^{\prime}(\overline{u})=0.

Condition (i) is standard. Since
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a translation-invariant subspace,
u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Since
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and therefore
(u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n}))
is also bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\rightharpoonup\overline{u}\hbox{ in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\to\overline{u}\hbox{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle u_{n}(x+z_{n})\to\overline{u}(x)\hbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

From (
4.6
) it is clear that
\overline{u}\neq 0
. Now we will show (iii). Denote
v_{n}:=u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})
.
Take any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and note that

\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(\varphi(\cdot-z_{n}))\|\leq\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\|\varphi(\cdot-z_{n})\|\to 0

thanks to
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
. Therefore

\displaystyle o(1)

\displaystyle=\langle u_{n},\varphi(\cdot-z_{n})\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})\varphi(\cdot-z_{n})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla v_{n}\nabla\varphi-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x+z_{n}|^{2}}v_{n}\varphi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)v_{n}\varphi\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,v_{n})\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}\Delta\varphi-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x+z_{n}|^{2}}v_{n}\varphi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)v_{n}\varphi\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,v_{n})\varphi\,dx.

Note that
\kappa_{n}:=\inf_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi}|x+z_{n}|^{2}\to\infty
and therefore

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v_{n}\varphi}{|x+z_{n}|^{2}}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}}|v_{n}|_{2}|\varphi|_{2}\to 0,

since
(v_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Now, using that
v_{n}\to\overline{u}
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we get that

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}\Delta\varphi-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4|x+z_{n}|^{2}}v_{n}\varphi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)v_{n}\varphi\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,v_{n})\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\to-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}\Delta\varphi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\overline{u}\varphi\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u})\varphi\,dx.

Therefore
\overline{u}
satisfies

(4.7)

-\Delta\overline{u}+V(x)\overline{u}=f(x,\overline{u})\quad\mbox{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

or, equivalently,

-\Delta\overline{u}=f(x,\overline{u})-V(x)\overline{u}=:h(x)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In view of Lemma
4.3
, to show that
\overline{u}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it is enough to verify that
h\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Observe that

|h|\leq|V|_{\infty}|\overline{u}|+|\overline{u}|+C_{1}|\overline{u}|^{p-1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})+L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Hence, indeed
\overline{u}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. To see that
\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we show a variant of the Caccioppoli inequality. Take any
R>0
and consider a function
\eta_{R}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,2R))
such that
\eta_{R}\equiv 1
on
B(0,R)
,
0\leq\eta_{R}\leq 1
and
|\nabla\eta_{R}|\leq\frac{c}{R}
for some
c\geq 1
. Then, testing (
4.7
) with
\eta_{R}^{2}\overline{u}
we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}\overline{u}\nabla\overline{u}\nabla\eta_{R}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\overline{u}^{2}\eta_{R}^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u})\eta_{R}^{2}\overline{u}\,dx.

Using Young’s inequality

\left|2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}\overline{u}\nabla\overline{u}\nabla\eta_{R}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}+\frac{2c^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{R}^{2}|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x,\overline{u})\overline{u}|\eta_{R}^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V(x)|\overline{u}^{2}\eta_{R}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x,\overline{u})\overline{u}|\,dx+|V|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx.

From monotone convergence theorem,
\nabla\overline{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In particular,
{\mathcal{J}}_{0}^{\prime}(\overline{u})=0
.

Step 4.

Suppose there exist
m\geq 1
,
(x_{n}^{i})_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N},\overline{u_{i}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
1\leq i\leq m
such that

\displaystyle|x^{i}_{n}|\to\infty,|x^{i}_{n}-x^{j}_{n}|\to\infty\hbox{ for }i\neq j,

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+x^{i}_{n})\to\overline{u_{i}},\hbox{ for each }1\leq i\leq m,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}_{0}(\overline{u_{i}})=0\hbox{ for each }1\leq i\leq m.

Then

(a)

if
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(z,1)}|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})|^{2}\,dx\to 0
as
n\to\infty,
then

\left\|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\right\|\to 0;

(b)

if there exist
(z_{n})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{B(z_{n},1+\sqrt{N})}\left|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\right|^{2}\,dx>0,

then there is
\overline{u}_{m+1}
such that up to a subsequence

(i)

|z_{n}|\to\infty,|z_{n}^{i}-z_{n}^{j}|\to\infty,
for
1\leq i\leq m,

(ii)

u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\rightharpoonup\overline{u}_{m+1}\neq 0,
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(iii)

{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}_{0}(\overline{u})=0.

Suppose that
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(z,1)}|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})|^{2}\,dx\to 0
as
n\to\infty.
Let

\xi_{n}=u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and note that from the Lions lemma we get that
\xi_{n}\to 0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
2<r<2^{*}
. We compute

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}+\langle\overline{u}_{0},\xi_{n}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\xi_{n}\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})\xi_{n}\,dx,

and since
{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{0})(\xi_{n})=0

\displaystyle\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}={\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(\xi_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u}_{0})\xi_{n}\,dx-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\xi_{n}\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})\xi_{n}\,dx.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})(\xi_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\|\xi_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\left(\|u_{n}\|+\|\overline{u}_{0}\|+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\|\right)\to 0

thanks to
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
, and since
|\xi_{n}|_{r}\to 0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,\overline{u}_{0})\xi_{n}\,dx\to 0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,u_{n})\xi_{n}\,dx\to 0.

Hence

\displaystyle\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\xi_{n}\rangle+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),u_{n}\rangle-\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\overline{u}_{0}\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\overline{u_{j}}(\cdot-x^{j}_{n})\rangle\right)+o(1).

From
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
,
\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\overline{u}_{0}\rangle\to 0
. Thus

\displaystyle\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),u_{n}\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n}),\overline{u_{j}}(\cdot-x^{j}_{n})\rangle\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\langle\overline{u}_{i},u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}^{i})\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\langle\overline{u}_{i},\overline{u}_{j}\left(\cdot-(x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{i})\right)\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}^{i})}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\overline{u_{j}}(\cdot-x^{j}_{n})}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}\overline{u}_{j}\left(\cdot-(x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{i})\right)}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)+o(1).

Again,
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
implies that
\left\langle\overline{u}_{i},\overline{u}_{j}\left(\cdot-(x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{i})\right)\right\rangle\to 0
for
i\neq j
. Moreover, using
[
13

and Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}\overline{u}_{j}\left(\cdot-(x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{i})\right)}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right|\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\overline{u}_{i}|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\overline{u}_{j}\left(\cdot-(x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{i})\right)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\to 0

for
i\neq j
. Also, for any
i
,
j
, we have

\left|\frac{\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\overline{u_{j}}(\cdot-x^{j}_{n})}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right|\leq\left(\frac{|\overline{u_{i}}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{|\overline{u_{j}}(\cdot-x^{j}_{n})|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\to 0.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\overline{u}_{i}\nabla\left(u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}^{i})-\overline{u}_{i}\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\overline{u}_{i}\left(u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}^{i})-\overline{u}_{i}\right)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right)+o(1).

Using that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\overline{u}_{i}\nabla\left(u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}^{i})-\overline{u}_{i}\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)\overline{u}_{i}\left(u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}^{i})-\overline{u}_{i}\right)\,dx

thanks to Vitali’s convergence theorem. Now, we consider the term

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=\int_{B(0,R)}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,R)}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where

is fixed. Then

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{R^{2}}|\overline{u}_{i}|_{2}|u_{n}|_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}},

since
(u_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. To estimate

we use Hölder’s inequality, exponential decay of

(
[
19

), boundedness of

in

and the inequality

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq|u_{n}|_{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}\left(\int_{|x|<R}\frac{|\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})|^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}}{|x|^{\frac{4N}{N+2s}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+2s}{2N}}\lesssim\sup_{|x|<R}e^{-\alpha|x-x_{n}^{i}|}\to 0,

for some

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}+o(1)

and therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\to 0.

It means that
\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}\to 0
and
\xi_{n}\to 0
in
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and the proof in this case is complete.
Now assume

(4.8)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{B(z_{n},1+\sqrt{N})}\left|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x^{i}_{n})\right|^{2}\,dx>0,

for some
(z_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}.
Then (i) is again standard. To prove (ii) we follow as in Step 3. Since
X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and therefore
(u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n}))
is also bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\rightharpoonup\overline{u}_{m+1}\hbox{ in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle u_{n}(\cdot+z_{n})\to\overline{u}_{m+1}\hbox{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle u_{n}(x+z_{n})\to\overline{u}_{m+1}(x)\hbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

From (
4.8

\overline{u}_{m+1}\neq 0
. Similarly as in Step 3 we show that

and
{\mathcal{J}}_{0}^{\prime}(\overline{u}_{m+1})=0

Step 5.

Step 1. implies (

and the theorem is true for

On the other hand if

for

For each such translation we can find

such that

Then

So in view of Step 3 one can find
\overline{u}
such that (i)-(iii) hold. Let

and
\overline{u}_{1}:=\overline{u}.
If (a) from Step 4 holds with

), (

(y_{n}^{2}):=(z_{n})

4.3

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}-\overline{u}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\overline{u}_{i}(\cdot-x_{n}^{i})\right\|^{2}

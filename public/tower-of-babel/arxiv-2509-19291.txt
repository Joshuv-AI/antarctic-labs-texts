Conclusion

Through this paper, we presented study of bounds on the Sigma and Albertson indices. The results, such as those in Proposition
3.1
with
0<\frac{2\operatorname{irr}_{\min}}{\Delta(\Delta-1)^{2}}<1
for the Albertson index and in Theorem
4.2
with
\sigma_{\max}(T)\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2(\Delta-3)}\left(\left\lfloor\dfrac{3n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\left\lceil\dfrac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rceil\right)
for the Sigma index, highlight that these indices exhibit significantly larger values for trees compared to connected graphs, as illustrated in Figure
1
.

These bounds, derived from degree sequences
\mathscr{D}=(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n})
, maximum degrees
\Delta_{\mathscr{A}}
,
\Delta_{\mathscr{R}}
, and parameters like
\lambda_{\mathscr{D}}
and
\eta
, provide precise constraints that reflect the interplay between graph topology and index behavior, facilitating applications in extremal graph theory and molecular property prediction.

Furthermore, the established bounds, such as those in Lemma
3.2
with
\sigma(T)\geqslant\sqrt{\lambda_{\mathscr{D}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{3}}-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{\lambda_{\mathscr{A}}}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{2m}{\lambda_{\mathscr{R}}}\right\rceil\right)+(n-\Delta)^{2}
and Corollary
4.4
with
\sigma(T)>4n-2\eta\lambda_{\mathscr{D}}-(n-\eta)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{n-\eta}\right\rfloor^{2}+(n-\eta)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{n-\lambda_{\mathscr{D}}}\right\rfloor
, emphasize the dependence of the Sigma index on degree heterogeneity and structural parameters. These findings not only advance the theoretical framework for analyzing topological indices but also enhance their utility in quantitative structure-property relationships (QSPR) and network science. By identifying extremal cases and sharp bounds, as seen in Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.5
, this study provides a robust foundation for optimizing graph-based models and understanding the structural limits of molecular and network systems.

Adaptive Lipschitz-Free Proximal Gradient Methods for Stochastic Composite Nonconvex Optimization
Ganzhao Yuan

Abstract
We propose ALFPG , an Adaptive Lipschitz-Free Proximal Gradient framework for stochastic composite nonconvex minimization. The framework adapts its stepsizes using information collected along the optimization trajectory, without requiring prior knowledge of smoothness constants or performing line search. For finite-sum problems, ALFPG-FS combines coordinate-wise stepsizes, Nesterov’s extrapolation, and the SPIDER estimator, with ALFPG-D as its full-batch specialization. Under component-wise smoothness and a bounded effective domain, ALFPG-FS achieves ϵ \epsilon -stationarity in expectation using 𝒪 ⁡ ( N + N ​ ϵ − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N+\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2}) component-gradient evaluations. For expectation problems, we develop ALFPG-MVR1 and ALFPG-MVR2 , where MVR stands for momentum-based variance reduction. Both use one fresh sample per iteration: ALFPG-MVR1 uses one stochastic-gradient evaluation, whereas ALFPG-MVR2 uses at most two evaluations at different points with the same sample. ALFPG-MVR1 requires smoothness of the expected loss; ALFPG-MVR2 additionally requires uniformly smooth sample losses. When the regularizer is convex or Lipschitz continuous on its effective domain, and stochastic gradients are almost surely bounded, the two methods achieve ϵ \epsilon -stationarity in expectation using 𝒪 ~ ​ ( σ 2 ​ ϵ − 4 + ϵ − 2 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{2}\epsilon^{-4}+\epsilon^{-2}) and 𝒪 ~ ​ ( σ ​ ϵ − 3 + ϵ − 2 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma\epsilon^{-3}+\epsilon^{-2}) stochastic-gradient evaluations, respectively. Here, N N is the number of component functions, σ 2 \sigma^{2} bounds the gradient noise variance, and 𝒪 ~ \widetilde{\mathcal{O}} hides logarithmic factors. Neither expectation variant requires prior knowledge of σ \sigma , and both recover 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ϵ − 2 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2}) complexity in the zero-noise regime.

Introduction

We consider composite nonconvex optimization of the form

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(\mathbf{x}):=f(\mathbf{x})+h(\mathbf{x}),\qquad f(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}),&\text{finite-sum setting},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}[f(\mathbf{x};\xi)],&\text{expectation setting}.\end{cases}

(1)

The smooth loss
f(\cdot)
may be nonconvex, and the regularizer

h(\cdot)
is proper and lower semicontinuous. We assume that the required proximal subproblems admit exactly and efficiently computable global minimizers (Assumption
3.1
). For finite-sum settings,
h(\cdot)
can be nonconvex and non-Lipschitz under the stated domain condition, while for expectation settings,
h(\cdot)
is required to be convex or Lipschitz continuous on its effective domain (Assumption
5.4
).

Composite Optimization and Structural Regularization.

The composite formulation separates data fitting from
structural regularization.
The term
h(\cdot)
can promote sparsity or encode constraints
through an extended-valued indicator function, allowing
the optimization model to incorporate prior information
about the desired solution.
Applications include sparse phase retrieval

Cai et al. (2024)
;
Shechtman et al. (2014)
,
optimization with orthogonality constraints

Wen and Yin (2013)
,
and quantized model training
Bai et al. (2019)
.
These examples motivate methods that accommodate
nonconvex losses and structural regularizers while
keeping gradient evaluations and parameter tuning manageable.

From Adaptivity to Lipschitz-Free Updates.

Proximal gradient methods exploit this structure by
combining gradient evaluations of
f(\cdot)
with proximal
updates involving
h(\cdot)
.
Their stepsizes are commonly chosen using a smoothness
constant or a backtracking procedure.
Adaptive gradient and proximal methods use information
collected during optimization to adjust their updates

Duchi et al. (2011)
;
Ward et al. (2020)
;
Yun et al. (2021)
.
However, an adaptive preconditioner may still be accompanied
by smoothness-dependent parameter restrictions.
We call an update
Lipschitz-free
when the parameter
choices supporting its convergence guarantee require
neither a smoothness constant nor line search.
This property concerns algorithmic tuning; smoothness
assumptions remain part of the analysis.

Challenges in Adaptive Proximal Updates.

Adaptation for composite objectives should account for
the interaction between the loss and the regularizer.
At a stationary point
\mathbf{x}_{\star}
, the inclusion

0\in\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{\star})+\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{\star})

may hold even when
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{\star})\neq 0
.
Consequently, the magnitude of the smooth gradient alone
need not indicate proximity to composite stationarity.
Proximal displacements incorporate the effect of
h
,
but relating them to stationarity requires controlling
the evolving metric.
Extrapolation and stochastic gradient estimation introduce
additional dependencies between the adaptive parameters,
iterate motion, and estimation errors. Our central question is how to couple
noise-sensitive weights and proximal-displacement normalization without knowing either smoothness or noise constants. The resulting random weights depend on the same trajectory as the tracking
errors, so a convergence argument must control their joint moments rather than treat them as independent.

Summary of Main Contributions.

We propose Adaptive Lipschitz-Free Proximal Gradient
(
ALFPG
), a framework that uses proximal displacements
as a common principle for stepsize adaptation.
We measure stationarity by
r(\mathbf{x}):=\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\mathbf{x}))
, where
\partial
denotes the limiting subdifferential.
Our main contributions are as follows.

1.

Adaptive Updates Using Proximal Displacements.

For finite-sum problems,
ALFPG-FS
adapts a diagonal
metric using metric-scaled iterate displacements
and combines this metric with Nesterov extrapolation
and the SPIDER estimator.
Its extrapolation coefficient is coupled to the
evolving metric to support the descent analysis.
The full-batch specialization is denoted by
ALFPG-D
.
For expectation problems,
ALFPG-MVR1
and
ALFPG-MVR2
(MVR: Momentum-based Variance Reduction) combine normalized proximal displacements with
gradient-discrepancy statistics to adapt their
scalar stepsizes and momentum weights.
Each iteration uses one fresh sample, with one
stochastic-gradient evaluation for MVR1 and at most
two evaluations for MVR2.

2.

Finite-Sum Guarantees with General Regularizers.

Under the stated finite-sum assumptions, including
the bounded-domain condition,
ALFPG-D
and
ALFPG-FS

attain gradient oracle complexities

\mathcal{O}(N\epsilon^{-2})
and

\mathcal{O}(N+\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2})
, respectively,
for the prescribed stationarity criterion.
These results establish the corresponding guarantees
for Lipschitz-free diagonal adaptation combined with
extrapolation and potentially nonconvex regularization.

3.

Noise-Adaptive Guarantees for Expectation Problems.

ALFPG-MVR1
requires smoothness of the expected loss,
whereas
ALFPG-MVR2
additionally requires smoothness
of the sample losses for its same-sample correction.
Under the respective smoothness assumptions,
Assumption
5.4
, and the bounded
stochastic-gradient oracle assumption, the methods
require

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma^{2}\epsilon^{-4})
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma\epsilon^{-3})

stochastic-gradient evaluations, respectively,
to obtain a uniformly sampled proximal iterate

\widehat{\mathbf{x}}
satisfying

\mathbb{E}[r(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})]\leq\epsilon
.
Here,
\sigma^{2}
bounds the gradient noise variance,
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
hides logarithmic factors.
The algorithms do not require this variance bound
as an input, and both complexities reduce to

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})

when
\sigma=0
.

Related Work

Proximal Methods with Nonconvex Regularization.

Proximal methods exploit the separation between a smooth
loss and a regularizer or constraint.
For nonconvex losses with convex regularization,
RSPG establishes stochastic proximal complexity
guarantees
Ghadimi et al. (2016)
.

Xu et al. (2019)
study potentially nonconvex
and nonsmooth regularizers.
Their fixed-batch MB-SPG method attains

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})
stochastic-gradient
complexity with a batch size of order
\epsilon^{-2}

at a fixed noise level.
The same work develops the recursive estimator method
SPGR, obtaining
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
complexity
under additional sample-wise smoothness.
These results provide direct precedents for stochastic
proximal optimization with nonconvex regularization.

ALFPG
studies Lipschitz-free adaptation within this
class of composite problems.

Extrapolation in Nonconvex Proximal Optimization.

Accelerated proximal gradient methods achieve improved
objective-value convergence rates in convex optimization

Beck and Teboulle (2009)
.
Methods such as iPiano, APG, and iPALM incorporate
inertial or extrapolation steps into nonconvex proximal
optimization under their respective assumptions

Ochs et al. (2014)
;
Li and Lin (2015)
;
Pock and Sabach (2016)
.
Li et al. (2017)
analyze APGnc and
SVRG-APGnc, establishing linear convergence of expected
function values for the latter under a global
Kurdyka–Lojasiewicz condition with exponent
1/2
.
Such guarantees rely on additional objective geometry.
Our finite-sum analysis couples extrapolation to an
adaptive diagonal metric and establishes first-order
stationarity complexity under the stated finite-sum
assumptions.

Variance Reduction for Finite Sums.

SVRG and SAGA use finite-sum structure to reduce
gradient-estimation variance

Johnson and Zhang (2013)
;
Defazio et al. (2014)
.
Their nonconvex proximal extensions establish complexity
guarantees for nonsmooth composite objectives

J. Reddi et al. (2016)
.
Recursive estimators, including SARAH, SPIDER, and PAGE,
further improve gradient oracle complexity

Nguyen et al. (2017)
;
Fang et al. (2018)
;
Li et al. (2021)
.
For convex regularizers, Prox-SpiderBoost,
Prox-SpiderBoost-M, and ProxSARAH achieve

\mathcal{O}(N+\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2})
complexity

Wang et al. (2019)
;
Pham et al. (2020)
.
SPGR attains the same dependence while permitting
nonconvex regularization and directly controlling
a subdifferential residual
Xu et al. (2019)
.
Thus, this complexity order and support for nonconvex
regularization already coexist in prior work.
The finite-sum contribution of
ALFPG
concerns their
combination with Lipschitz-free diagonal adaptation
and metric-dependent extrapolation.
Although ProxSARAH includes adaptive stepsize schemes,
its relevant parameter choices still involve smoothness
information.

Adaptive Gradients and Proximal Preconditioning.

AdaGrad and Adam adapt their updates using historical
gradient information

Duchi et al. (2011)
;
Kingma and Ba (2015)
.
Reddi et al. (2018)
identify
convergence failures of Adam and develop AMSGrad.
Nonconvex convergence guarantees are established for
AdaGrad-Norm and classes of Adam-type methods

Ward et al. (2020)
;
Chen et al. (2019)
.
AdaSpider combines Lipschitz-free adaptation with
recursive variance reduction for smooth nonconvex
finite sums
Kavis et al. (2022b)
.
For composite objectives, ProxGen supports adaptive
preconditioning, momentum, and potentially nonconvex
regularizers
Yun et al. (2021)
, while SUPER-ADAM
studies adaptive matrices in constrained nonconvex
optimization
Huang et al. (2021)
.
Their cited guarantees include smoothness-dependent
parameter restrictions.
Accordingly, our comparison distinguishes adaptive
preconditioning from Lipschitz-free parameter selection.

Recursive Momentum for Expectation Problems.

STORM uses a same-sample recursive correction to reduce
variance with a constant number of gradient evaluations
per iteration
Cutkosky and Orabona (2019)
.
STORM+ develops parameter-free adaptation for smooth
nonconvex optimization
Levy et al. (2021)
.
More recently, Ada-STORM removes bounded-gradient and
bounded-function-value requirements from its smooth
analysis and obtains a rate without an additional
logarithmic factor
Jiang et al. (2024)
.
Proximal developments include hybrid estimators and
PStorm
Tran-Dinh et al. (2022)
;
Xu and Xu (2023)
.
Sampling costs depend on the particular construction:
ProxSARAH uses snapshot batches, while the hybrid
scheme supporting its cubic complexity uses a larger
initialization batch.
Our expectation variants use one fresh sample at every
iteration, including initialization.
MVR1 uses an exponential moving average, and MVR2 adds
a same-sample correction.
Their composite guarantees retain a bounded
stochastic-gradient oracle assumption.

Smoothness Assumptions and Noise Adaptivity.

Smoothness of the expected loss, mean-squared smoothness,
and uniform sample-wise smoothness impose different
requirements on the stochastic oracle.
The intermediate mean-squared condition is
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x};\xi)-\nabla f(\mathbf{y};\xi)\|^{2}\right]\leq L^{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|^{2}
. It is used in the analyses of ProxSARAH, ProxHSGD,
and PStorm

Pham et al. (2020)
;
Tran-Dinh et al. (2022)
;
Xu and Xu (2023)
.
Our MVR1 result assumes smoothness of the expected
loss, whereas the MVR2 result uses uniform sample-wise
smoothness.
Variance-sensitive guarantees and recovery of
deterministic rates have also been studied in smooth
adaptive optimization

Ward et al. (2020)
;
Cutkosky and Orabona (2019)
.
Our results establish these properties for the proposed
Lipschitz-free proximal methods under their respective
regularizer and oracle assumptions.

Stationarity Measures and Oracle Complexity.

Nonconvex optimization guarantees use gradient norms,
proximal-gradient mappings, and subdifferential residuals.
Stochastic model-based methods for weakly convex
objectives also analyze the gradient of a Moreau
envelope
Davis and Drusvyatskiy (2019)
.
Our guarantees control

\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial F(\mathbf{x}))
.
A proximal-gradient mapping bound can yield a residual
bound at a corresponding proximal point, with suitable
control of stochastic estimation errors when necessary.
These distinctions matter when interpreting complexity
comparisons.

Throughout, gradient oracle complexity counts every individual gradient evaluation, including initialization, periodic refreshes, and multiple evaluations using the same sample. It counts component-gradient evaluations in the finite-sum setting and stochastic-gradient evaluations in the expectation setting, and differs from the number of proximal iterations.

Notations
. For a nonempty set
S
,
\|S\|
denotes
{\rm{dist}}(\mathbf{0},S)=\inf_{u\in S}\|u\|
. Thus all stationarity bounds below use the minimal subgradient norm. Vector operations are performed element-wise. Specifically, for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the operations
(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})
,
(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})
,
(\mathbf{x}\odot\mathbf{y})
, and
(\mathbf{x}\div\mathbf{y})
represent element-wise addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, respectively. We use
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{v}}
to denote the weighted vector seminorm, defined as
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{v}}=\sqrt{\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{x}\odot\mathbf{x}\rangle}
. The notations, technical preliminaries, and relevant lemmas are provided in Appendix Section
A
. The notation
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
hides polylogarithmic factors, while
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
hides constants.

The Proposed Algorithms

This section presents the Adaptive Lipschitz-Free Proximal Gradient (
ALFPG
) framework for Problem (
1
).
ALFPG-FS
addresses finite-sum problems, with
ALFPG-D
as its full-batch specialization.
ALFPG-MVR1
and
ALFPG-MVR2
address expectation problems.

The algorithms require the following exact-proximal-computation assumption.

Assumption 3.1
.

(Exact proximal computations) For
\gamma>0
,
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n}
, define
\Prox_{\gamma h}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{v}):=\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{h(\mathbf{x})+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}\|_{\mathbf{v}}^{2}\right\}
. For every
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\gamma>0
, we assume that a global minimizer of this subproblem can be computed exactly and efficiently: (i) for every
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n}
in
ALFPG-FS
and its full-batch specialization
ALFPG-D
; (ii) for
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{1}
in
ALFPG-MVR1
and
ALFPG-MVR2
. A minimizer is selected whenever the solution is nonunique.

Remark 3.2
.

❶Assumption
3.1
is commonly employed in nonconvex proximal gradient methods. ❷ When
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{1}
, the diagonal preconditioner reduces to the identity preconditioner. Assumption
3.1
holds for certain regularizers
h(\mathbf{x})
. Common examples include capped-
\ell_{1}
penalty
Zhang (2010b)
, log-sum penalty
Candes et al. (2008)
, minimax concave penalty
Zhang (2010a)
, Geman penalty
Geman and Yang (1995)
,
\ell_{p}
regularization with
p\in\{0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{2}{3},1\}
, and indicator functions for cardinality constraints, orthogonality constraints in matrices, and rank constraints in matrices. ❸ When
\mathbf{v}
is a general positive vector, the variable-metric operator can still be evaluated for certain coordinate-wise separable regularizers
h(\mathbf{x})
. Examples include
\ell_{p}
regularization with
p\in\{0,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{2}{3},1\}
(with or without bound constraints)
Yun et al. (2021)
, and the W-shaped regularizer
Bai et al. (2019)
.

Proposed
ALFPG-FS
for Finite-Sum Settings.

Input:

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\dom(F)
. Let
\underline{\rm{v}}>0,\alpha>0
,
\beta\geq 0
, and
\theta\in[0,1)
. Choose integers
q,b\geq 1
(for finite-sum problems, take
q=b=\lceil\sqrt{N}\rceil
).

Initialize:
Let
\mathbf{x}_{-1}=\mathbf{y}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{0}
. Set
\mathbf{v}_{-1}=\mathbf{v}_{0}=\underline{\rm{v}}\mathbf{1}
,
\sigma_{-1}=\theta
.

For
t=0,\ldots,T
:

1.

If
\mathrm{mod}(t,q)\neq 0
, draw a fresh mini-batch

\mathcal{I}_{t}
of
b
indices uniformly with replacement from

\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Compute:

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla f(\mathbf{y}_{t}),&\hbox{$\textrm{mod}(t,q)=0$;}\\
\mathbf{g}_{t-1}+\nabla f(\mathbf{y}_{t};\mathcal{I}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{y}_{t-1};\mathcal{I}_{t}),&\hbox{else.}\end{array}\right.

When
q=1
, every iteration uses the full gradient,
giving
ALFPG-D
.

2.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\in\Prox_{h}(\mathbf{y}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}\div{\mathbf{v}_{t}};\mathbf{v}_{t})
,
\mathbf{d}_{t}:=\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}
.

3.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{t}:=\alpha\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}+\beta\mathbf{r}_{t}\odot\mathbf{r}_{t}
, where
\mathbf{r}_{t}:=\mathbf{v}_{t}\odot\mathbf{d}_{t}
.

4.

Set
\mathbf{v}_{t+1}=\textstyle\sqrt{\mathbf{v}_{0}\odot\mathbf{v}_{0}+\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{t}\mathbf{s}_{i}}
.

5.

Let
\sigma_{t}:=\theta(1-\sigma_{t-1})\cdot\min(\mathbf{v}_{t}\div\mathbf{v}_{t+1})
.

6.

Set
\mathbf{y}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t+1}+\sigma_{t}\mathbf{d}_{t}
.

3.1
ALFPG-FS
for Finite-Sum Settings

ALFPG-FS
combines a displacement-based diagonal metric, metric-coupled Nesterov-type extrapolation, and the SPIDER
estimator for finite-sum composite optimization. When
q=1
, it specializes to the full-batch method

ALFPG-D
. Both methods are specified in
Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm Design and Motivation.

At the extrapolated point
\mathbf{y}_{t}
, the algorithm computes the full
gradient when
\mathrm{mod}(t,q)=0
. Otherwise, it applies the SPIDER recursion (
1
)
Fang et al. (2018)
, with
\nabla f(\mathbf{y};\mathcal{I}_{t}):=b^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{y})
. The same mini-batch is evaluated at
\mathbf{y}_{t}
and

\mathbf{y}_{t-1}
. Following the proximal step, the metric-scaled iterate
displacement
\mathbf{r}_{t}=\mathbf{v}_{t}\odot(\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t})
determines the next
metric. For every coordinate
j
,
(\mathbf{v}_{t+1})_{j}^{2}=\underline{\rm v}^{\,2}+\alpha\sum_{i=0}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\beta\sum_{i=0}^{t}(\mathbf{r}_{i})_{j}^{2}
. The global term weighted by
\alpha

Relationship to AdaGrad-Norm and Main Novelty.

Consider

3.2
ALFPG-MVR1
and
ALFPG-MVR2
for Expectation Settings

ALFPG-MVR1
uses a one-gradient exponential moving average (EMA) estimator

Kingma and Ba (2015)
, whereas
ALFPG-MVR2
uses a STORM-type same-sample
recursive estimator with at most two gradient evaluations

Cutkosky and Orabona (2019)
. The former assumes smoothness of the
expected loss; the latter additionally assumes smoothness of each
sample loss. Both methods are specified in Algorithm
2
.

Algorithm Design and Motivation.

Algorithm
2
combines a stochastic gradient estimator,
a proximal step, and two distinct adaptive weights. For
ALFPG-MVR1
,

\chi_{t}
is the difference between stochastic gradients at
successive iterates; for
ALFPG-MVR2
, it compares gradients obtained
from two samples at the same point
\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
. The weight

\rho_{t}
normalizes the accumulated squared differences by their
running maximum and takes the running minimum of the resulting
ratios. When the differences are persistently dominated by noise
of a comparable scale, its intended behavior is heuristically

\rho_{t}\approx(1+t\sigma^{2})^{-p}
; this is not a pathwise identity
or an assumption in the analysis. The maximum reduces the effect
of an isolated gradient spike, while the outer minimum makes

\rho_{t}
nonincreasing. More precisely,
Lemma
C.1
bounds the increments of
1/\rho_{t}

and the changes in
\rho_{t}
, and provides a contraction inequality
used in the gradient-tracking analysis. We use the lagged weight

\alpha_{t}=\rho_{t-1}
so that the averaging coefficient and
stepsize are fixed before sampling
\xi_{t}
.

Relationship to Existing Methods and Main Novelty.

AdaGrad-Norm accumulates squared stochastic-gradient norms

\|\mathbf{g}_{i}\|^{2}
in a scalar stepsize denominator

Ward et al. (2020)
. Here, the noise-sensitive weight
\rho_{t}

instead uses the squared differences
\chi_{i}^{2}
, while the
separate factor
\beta_{t}
accumulates squared proximal residuals

\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
. These quantities serve different purposes:
gradient differences respond to changes in the stochastic oracle,
whereas the proximal residual incorporates the regularizer into
stepsize adaptation. The
ALFPG-MVR1
estimator is a one-gradient
exponential moving average.
ALFPG-MVR2
uses a recursive correction
evaluated at
\mathbf{x}_{t}
and
\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
with the same fresh sample,
as in momentum-based variance reduction methods

Cutkosky and Orabona (2019)
;
Tran-Dinh et al. (2022)
. Our design couples
these estimators to the discrepancy-based averaging weight and
the residual-based proximal stepsize. In particular, MVR1 needs
smoothness of the expected loss, whereas MVR2 uses individual
smoothness to control its same-sample correction.

Additional Remarks on Algorithm
2
.

❶ The parameter choices are

(p,\lambda)=(1/2,1/2)
for
ALFPG-MVR1
and
(2/3,1/4)
for
ALFPG-MVR2
.
Since
\alpha_{t}=\rho_{t-1}
and
\beta_{t}
depends only on past

\gamma_{t}=\theta\beta_{t}\alpha_{t}^{\lambda}

is determined before the fresh sample is drawn. ❷
ALFPG-MVR1
uses one stochastic-gradient evaluation per iteration;

uses at most two, both with one fresh sample. Neither variant requires
L
or the noise level

as an algorithmic input. ❸ The discrepancy statistic reuses gradients already computed by each
variant. For
ALFPG-MVR1

solely to form

would require a second gradient evaluation. We instead use

in the gradient along the iterates. For
ALFPG-MVR2
, the same-sample correction
already computes

\mathbf{x}_{t-1}

6
Conclusions

This paper develops
ALFPG
, a family of Lipschitz-free proximal methods for composite nonconvex optimization.
ALFPG-FS
combines a displacement-based diagonal metric, metric-coupled extrapolation, and the SPIDER estimator. The analysis establishes stationarity guarantees for this combination under exact proximal computation and a bounded effective domain. With a full gradient at every iteration,
ALFPG-FS
reduces to
ALFPG-D
. Under the stated finite-sum assumptions,
ALFPG-D
uses
\mathcal{O}(N/\epsilon^{2})
component-gradient evaluations to reach
\epsilon
-stationarity, while
ALFPG-FS
uses
\mathcal{O}(N+\sqrt{N}/\epsilon^{2})
evaluations to reach the same stationarity criterion in expectation. For expectation problems,
ALFPG-MVR1
and
ALFPG-MVR2
attain
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma^{2}\epsilon^{-4}+\epsilon^{-2})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sigma\epsilon^{-3}+\epsilon^{-2})
gradient oracle complexities under their respective assumptions. Their bounds adapt to the noise level and recover
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})
when
\sigma=0
.

A.2
Technical Preliminaries

We introduce key concepts from nonsmooth analysis, focusing on the Fréchet subdifferential and the limiting (Fréchet) subdifferential
Mordukhovich (2006)
;
Rockafellar and Wets (2009)
;
Bertsekas (2015)
. Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be an extended real-valued, not necessarily convex function. The domain of
F(\cdot)
is defined as
\text{dom}(F):=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|F(\mathbf{x})|<+\infty\}
. The Fréchet subdifferential of
F
at
\mathbf{x}\in\text{dom}(F)
, denoted as
\hat{\partial}F(\mathbf{x})
, is given by

\hat{\partial}{F}(\mathbf{x}):=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lim_{\mathbf{z}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}}\inf_{\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{x}}\frac{{F}(\mathbf{z})-{F}(\mathbf{x})-\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\rangle}{\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\|}\geq 0\}.

The limiting subdifferential of
F(\cdot)
at
\mathbf{x}\in\text{dom}({F})
, denoted
\partial{F}(\mathbf{x})
, is defined as:

\partial{F}(\mathbf{x}):=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists\mathbf{x}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{x},{F}(\mathbf{x}_{k})\rightarrow{F}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{v}^{k}\in\hat{\partial}{F}(\mathbf{x}_{k})\rightarrow\mathbf{v},\forall k\}.

It is important to note that
\hat{\partial}{F}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq\partial{F}(\mathbf{x})
. If
F(\cdot)
is continuously differentiable at
\mathbf{x}
, then
\hat{\partial}{F}(\mathbf{x})=\partial{F}(\mathbf{x})=\{\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\}
, where
\nabla F(\mathbf{x})
represents the gradient of
F(\cdot)
at
\mathbf{x}
. For convex function
F(\cdot)
, both
\hat{\partial}{F}(\mathbf{x})
and
\partial{F}(\mathbf{x})
reduce to the classical subdifferential for convex functions:
\hat{\partial}{F}(\mathbf{x})=\partial{F}(\mathbf{x})=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:F(\mathbf{z})-F(\mathbf{x})-\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}\rangle\geq 0,\forall\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
.

Lemma A.6

Appendix B
Proof of Section
4

The proof proceeds in three stages. We first establish metric, extrapolation, and descent estimates
shared by both finite-sum variants. We then close the deterministic bounds for
ALFPG-D
, before
incorporating the SPIDER error and taking expectations for
ALFPG-FS
.

Overview of the proof strategy for
ALFPG-D
.

The deterministic analysis couples the objective gap with an extrapolation energy in
\mathcal{Z}_{t}
. Proximal optimality, smoothness, and the metric-dependent extrapolation rule yield a descent inequality with two adaptive sums (Lemma
B.3
). The accumulator estimates in Lemma
B.4
control these sums in terms of
\mathcal{V}_{T+1}
. Applying summation by parts to the metric-weighted descent inequality and summing its unweighted counterpart then give a closed recursion for the largest Lyapunov value. This bounds both
\mathcal{Z}_{t}
and
\mathcal{V}_{t}
uniformly (Lemma
B.5
), without assuming that the Lyapunov sequence decreases at every iteration. The bounded accumulator implies square-summable iterate displacements; proximal optimality then converts this motion bound into the averaged
\mathcal{O}((T+1)^{-1/2})
stationarity rate.

Overview of the proof strategy for
ALFPG-FS
.

The finite-sum analysis starts from the same pathwise descent inequality, but must also control the SPIDER estimation error. Within each refresh epoch, its second moment is bounded by the accumulated motion of the extrapolated iterates. Summing over epochs and applying the adaptive-motion estimates bounds the total estimation error by a constant multiple of
\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{\mathcal{V}_{T+1}}]
. Taking expectations only after summing the pathwise descent inequalities yields the first-moment closure in (
40
), which bounds
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{V}_{t}]
and
\mathcal{Z}_{t}
(Lemma
B.10
). This avoids treating the random metric as deterministic or requiring a uniform second-moment bound for it. Finally, proximal optimality relates stationarity to iterate motion, estimation error, and metric-scaled displacements. Their accumulated bounds give the expected rate; counting refresh and paired mini-batch evaluations gives the oracle complexity.

Overview of the proof strategy for

For

C.6

Cauchy–Schwarz removes the remaining random weights and converts these estimates into the noise-adaptive stationarity bound. Only smoothness of the expected loss is needed; the motion contribution remains controlled even when the noise vanishes.

Overview of the proof strategy for

For

Unlike MVR1, the discrepancy statistic compares two samples at the same point, so its expected squared magnitude is controlled by noise alone. \displaystyle 0~~\leq~~U_{t}^{p}-U_{t-1}^{p}~~\leq~~{p}U_{t-1}^{{p}-1}(U_{t}-U_{t-1})~~\leq~~p,

where we used
U_{t-1}\geq 1
,
{p}-1<0
, and
(
45
). Using
\rho_{t}=U_{t}^{-{p}}
, we have:

\displaystyle\textstyle 0~~\leq~~\tfrac{1}{\rho_{t}}-\tfrac{1}{\rho_{t-1}}~~\leq~~{p}.

Part (b).

Using the integral representation of
x\mapsto x^{-{p}}

and (
45
), we obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle\rho_{t-1}-\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle U_{t-1}^{-{p}}-U_{t}^{-{p}}~~=~~{p}\int_{U_{t-1}}^{U_{t}}z^{-{p}-1}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{p}(U_{t}-U_{t-1})U_{t-1}^{-{p}-1}~~\leq~~\textstyle{p}U_{t-1}^{-{p}-1}~~=~~{p}\rho_{t-1}^{1+1/{p}}.

This proves
\rho_{t-1}-\rho_{t}\leq{p}\rho_{t-1}^{1+1/{p}}
.

Part (c).

Using Part (a), we have the following equivalent inequalities:

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\rho_{t-1}-\rho_{t}}{\rho_{t}\rho_{t-1}}\leq{p}~~\Leftrightarrow~~~~\rho_{t-1}(1-{p}\rho_{t})\leq\rho_{t}~~\Leftrightarrow~~\frac{\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t}}\leq\frac{1}{1-{p}\rho_{t}}.

Since
0<\rho_{t}\leq 1
and
{p}\in(0,1)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\textstyle(1-\rho_{t})\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t}}}~~\leq~~\frac{1-\rho_{t}}{\sqrt{1-{p}\rho_{t}}}~~\leq~~\frac{1-\rho_{t}}{1-{p}\rho_{t}}~~=~~1-(1-{p})\rho_{t}-\frac{{p}(1-{p})\rho_{t}^{2}}{1-{p}\rho_{t}}~~\leq~~1-(1-{p})\rho_{t}.

∎

We begin the MVR1 analysis with pathwise bounds for the estimator, the proximal residual, and successive adaptive weights. The convex and domain-Lipschitz cases require different residual arguments, but lead to the common estimates used below.

Lemma C.2
.

(Preliminary bounds for MVR1) Let
K_{h}:=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{\theta}\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\|,&h\text{ convex},\\
\hat{H},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}:=\prox_{\theta h}(\mathbf{x}_{1})
,
\widehat{C}:=2{\widehat{G}}+2K_{h}
,
\hat{R}:=1+\widehat{C}
, and
\kappa:=1+\hat{R}
. Almost surely, for every
t\geq 1
,

(a)
\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|\leq{\widehat{G}}
,
\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|\leq 2{\widehat{G}}
.

(b)
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq\widehat{C}t
,
\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}\leq\hat{R}^{2}
.

(c)
\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha_{t+1}}\leq\frac{3}{2}
,
\frac{\beta_{t}}{\beta_{t+1}}\leq\kappa
.

Proof.

Define
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}:=\prox_{\theta h}(\mathbf{x}_{1})
.

Bounding the term
\|\mathbf{r}_{1}\|
when
h(\cdot)
is convex.

For the first iteration, we have
\gamma_{1}=\theta
since
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}=1
. By the nonexpansiveness of the proximal operator,
\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\|=\|\prox_{\theta h}(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\theta\mathbf{m}_{1})-\prox_{\theta h}(\mathbf{x}_{1})\|\leq\theta\|\mathbf{m}_{1}\|
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{1}\|=\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|}{\theta}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\|}{\theta}+\frac{\|\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|}{\theta}\leq K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{1}\|\leq K_{h}+{\widehat{G}}.

(46)

Part (a-i).

The stochastic momentum estimator is updated as
\mathbf{m}_{t}=(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{m}_{t-1}+\alpha_{t}\mathbf{g}_{t}
, where
\alpha_{t}\in(0,1]
. Since the stochastic oracle is bounded,
\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|\leq{\widehat{G}}
almost surely. Therefore,
\mathbf{m}_{t}
is a convex combination of previous
bounded estimators and stochastic gradients. By induction,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|\leq(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|+\alpha_{t}\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|\leq{\widehat{G}}.

Part (a-ii).

By the definition
\mathbf{s}_{t}=\mathbf{m}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
, and Jensen’s inequality together with the bounded stochastic oracle, we have
\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|=\|\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathbf{g}_{t}]\|\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\|\leq{\widehat{G}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|\leq\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|+\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|\leq 2{\widehat{G}}.

Part (b-i).

We next establish an upper bound for
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|
when
h
is convex. Assume
t\geq 2
. Note that
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\prox_{\gamma_{t}h}(\mathbf{x}_{t}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{m}_{t})
, and
\mathbf{r}_{t}=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+1}}{\gamma_{t}}
. The first-order optimality condition of the proximal subproblem gives
\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t}\in\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})
. Similarly, using the proximal update at iteration
t-1
, we have
\mathbf{r}_{t-1}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\in\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t})
.

Since
h
is convex, its subdifferential is monotone. Therefore,

\left\langle(\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t})-(\mathbf{r}_{t-1}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle\geq 0.

Using
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}=-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{r}_{t}
, we obtain

\left\langle(\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t})-(\mathbf{r}_{t-1}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}),\mathbf{r}_{t}\right\rangle\leq 0.

Expanding the inner product gives

{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\leq~~\langle\mathbf{r}_{t-1}+\mathbf{m}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1},\mathbf{r}_{t}\rangle~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}+\mathbf{m}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|\leq\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\left(\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+2{\widehat{G}}\right),\end{array}}

where step
①
uses the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. If
\mathbf{r}_{t}=\mathbf{0}
, the claimed bound is immediate; otherwise, dividing by
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|
gives:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+2{\widehat{G}}.

(47)

Applying this recursion repeatedly gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|~~\leq~~\|\mathbf{r}_{1}\|+2(t-1){\widehat{G}}~~\leq~~K_{h}+{\widehat{G}}+2(t-1){\widehat{G}}~~~~\leq~~\widehat{C}t.

(48)

Note that the inequality above also holds for
t=1
.

We next establish an upper bound for
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|
when
h(\cdot)
is possibly nonconvex and domain-Lipschitz. Global optimality of the proximal update, with
\mathbf{x}_{t}
as a comparison point, gives

h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})+\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}}{2\gamma_{t}}\leq h(\mathbf{x}_{t}).

Since both
\mathbf{x}_{t}
and
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}
belong to
\dom(h)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}}{2\gamma_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq h(\mathbf{x}_{t})-h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|)\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|.

If
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}
, then
\mathbf{r}_{t}=\mathbf{0}
.
Otherwise, dividing by
\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|
gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|=\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|}{\gamma_{t}}\leq 2(K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|)\leq 2(K_{h}+{\widehat{G}})=\widehat{C}.

(49)

Combining both cases gives
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq\widehat{C}t
for all
t\geq 1
.

Part (b-ii).

In view of Inequalities (
47
) and (
49
), we have:
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+\widehat{C}
, for all
t\geq 1
. Combining with
A_{t-1}\geq 1+\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}
, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}~~\leq~~\frac{(\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+\widehat{C})^{2}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}+1}~~\leq~~1+\widehat{C}^{2}~~\leq~~\hat{R}^{2}.

Part (c-i).

Lemma
C.1
(a) with
p=1/2
gives
\frac{1}{\rho_{t}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{t-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t}}\leq 1+\frac{\rho_{t-1}}{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}.

Part (c-ii).

By definition,
\beta_{t}=A_{t-1}^{-1/2}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\beta_{t}}{\beta_{t+1}}~~=~~\sqrt{\frac{A_{t}}{A_{t-1}}}~~=~~\sqrt{1+\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}}~~\leq~~\sqrt{1+\hat{R}^{2}}~~\leq\kappa.

∎

The preliminary bounds control the size of the quantities entering the EMA recursion. We now separate its tracking error into contraction, iterate drift, and fresh sampling noise, retaining the conditionally mean-zero cross term for the later moment estimates.

Lemma C.3
.

(Tracking-error recursion for MVR1) Define
\mathcal{E}_{t}=2\alpha_{t}\langle\mathbf{b}_{t},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\rangle
,
\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=\alpha_{t}^{2}\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}
, and
\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=\frac{2L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\alpha_{t-1}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}
, where
\mathbf{b}_{t}=(1-\alpha_{t})(\mathbf{m}_{t-1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))
.

(a) Then almost surely,
\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}\leq(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}
.

(b)
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=0
, and
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}\leq 16{\widehat{G}}^{2}\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
.

Proof.

Define
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}:=\mathbf{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
,
\mathbf{s}_{t}=\mathbf{m}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
.

Let
\mathbf{b}_{t}:=(1-\alpha_{t})(\mathbf{m}_{t-1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))
, and
{\bm{\delta}}_{t}:=(1-\alpha_{t})(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))
.

Exact decomposition of the tracking error.

Recall that
\mathbf{m}_{t}=(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{m}_{t-1}+\alpha_{t}\mathbf{g}_{t}
. We derive:

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{t})(\mathbf{m}_{t-1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))+\alpha_{t}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{s}_{t-1}+(1-\alpha_{t})(\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}))+\alpha_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}~~=~~\mathbf{b}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}.

Bounding the deterministic drift
\|{\bm{\delta}}_{t}\|
.

By the
L
-smoothness of
f
,

\displaystyle\|{\bm{\delta}}_{t}\|~~\leq~~(1-\alpha_{t})L\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\|~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~(1-\alpha_{t})L\gamma_{t-1}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|~~{\leq}~~L\gamma_{t-1}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|.

(51)

where step
①
uses
\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t-1}=-\gamma_{t-1}\mathbf{r}_{t-1}
.

Part (a).

Since
\mathbf{b}_{t}=(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{s}_{t-1}+\alpha_{t}(\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{t}}{\bm{\delta}}_{t})
, due to the convexity of
\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}
, we have:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{b}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\alpha_{t}\|(\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{t}}{\bm{\delta}}_{t})\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{t}}L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}

(52)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{2L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\alpha_{t-1}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2},

where step
①
uses Inequality (
51
); step
②
uses
\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\leq\tfrac{2}{\alpha_{t-1}}
, which can be implied by Lemma
C.2
that
\frac{\alpha_{t-1}}{\alpha_{t}}\leq\frac{3}{2}<2
.

On the other hand, we have:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{b}_{t}+\alpha_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{b}_{t}\|^{2}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}.

(53)

Combining Inequality (
52
) and Inequality (
53
) yields:

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}\leq(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{2L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\alpha_{t-1}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}.

Part (b).

Since
\mathbf{b}_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable and
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\bm{\epsilon}_{t}]=0
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=2\alpha_{t}\langle\mathbf{b}_{t},\mathbb{E}_{t}[\bm{\epsilon}_{t}]\rangle=0
. Finally,
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}=4\alpha_{t}^{2}\langle\mathbf{b}_{t},\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\rangle^{2}
. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}~~\leq~~4\alpha_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{b}_{t}\|^{2}\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~4\alpha_{t}^{2}\cdot 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}~~=~~16{\widehat{G}}^{2}\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}.

where step
①
uses
\|\mathbf{b}_{t}\|\leq(1-\alpha_{t})(\|\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|+\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|)\leq 2{\widehat{G}}
.

∎

To connect tracking accuracy with optimization progress, we combine proximal descent with the preceding error recursion. The logarithmic correction absorbs the normalized motion term and yields a joint Lyapunov inequality.

Lemma C.4
.

(Proximal descent and a joint Lyapunov inequality for MVR1) Let
C_{f}:=\theta^{2}\kappa^{2}(2L+1)^{2}
and

Q_{t}:=F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+2\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-C_{f}\ln A_{t-1}
.
Then, almost surely,

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1+L}{2}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2},

(54)

\displaystyle Q_{t+1}-Q_{t}+\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+2\mathcal{E}_{t+1}.

(55)

Proof.

Define
\mathbf{s}_{t}:=\mathbf{m}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
and

\mathbf{r}_{t}:=(\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t+1})/\gamma_{t}
.

Let
A_{t}:=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
, where
A_{0}:=1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{y}}\left\{h(\mathbf{y})+\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\gamma_{t}}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}\right\}
.

By the optimality of
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}
, we have:

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\leq h(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\gamma_{t}}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}.

By
L
-smoothness of
f
,

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\langle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t},\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}.

Part (a).

Adding the two inequalities gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\textstyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\langle\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t},\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{m}_{t}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\gamma_{t}}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\langle\gamma_{t}\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{s}_{t}\rangle-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{(L+1)\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2},

where step
①
uses weighted Young inequality
\gamma_{t}\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{s}_{t}\rangle\leq\frac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}
.

Part (b).

We further derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\tfrac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}

(56)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\alpha_{t}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{(L+1)\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\alpha_{t+1}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{(L+1)\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\left(\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{s}_{t+1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{2L^{2}\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\right)+\frac{(L+1)\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\left(\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{s}_{t+1}\|^{2}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\right)+\left(\frac{(L+1)}{2}+4L^{2}\right)\cdot\left(\tfrac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\right),

where step
①
uses
\tfrac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha_{t+1}}=\tfrac{\rho_{t-1}}{\rho_{t}}\leq 1+p=\tfrac{3}{2}<2
; step
②
uses Lemma
C.3
(a) that
\alpha_{t}\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{s}_{t-1}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}
.

Furthermore, we have:

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{(L+1)}{2}+4L^{2}\big)\cdot\big(\tfrac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\big)~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\big(2L+1\big)^{2}\cdot\big(\theta^{2}\beta_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\big)

(57)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\big(2L+1\big)^{2}\cdot\big(\theta^{2}\kappa^{2}\beta_{t+1}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\big)~~=~~\big(2L+1\big)^{2}\cdot\big(\theta^{2}\kappa^{2}\big)\cdot\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\big(2L+1\big)^{2}\cdot\big(\theta^{2}\kappa^{2}\big)\cdot\big(\ln(A_{t})-\ln(A_{t-1})\big)=C_{f}\big(\ln(A_{t})-\ln(A_{t-1})\big),

where step
①
uses
\gamma_{t}=\theta\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\beta_{t}
; step
②
uses
\tfrac{\beta_{t}}{\beta_{t+1}}\leq\kappa
; step
③
uses Lemma
A.8
(a).

Define
Q_{t}:=F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+2\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-C_{f}\ln A_{t-1}
. In view of Inequalities (
56
) and (
57
), we have:

\displaystyle Q_{t+1}-Q_{t}+\tfrac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}\leq 2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+2\mathcal{E}_{t+1}.

∎

The one-step estimates must now be converted into cumulative bounds. We first control the discrepancy and normalized motion sums, and then derive the first two moments of the weighted tracking error; the second-moment bound will be needed for the motion estimate.

Lemma C.5
.

(Logarithmic bounds for MVR1) For every
T\geq 1
, we have:

(a)
V_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}\leq C_{v}\ell_{T}
almost surely, where
C_{v}=(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})
.

(b)
H_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\leq C_{h}\ell_{T}

(c)

(d) Let

. We have:

, where

.

(e)
\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]\leq C^{\prime}_{u}\ell_{T}^{2}\leq C_{u}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
, where

Proof.

Define
D_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\chi_{i}^{2}

, where
\chi_{t}=\|\mathbf{g}_{t-1}-\mathbf{g}_{t}\|
.

Define
V_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}
. Let
A_{t}:=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
. Let
c_{\chi}:=(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}
.

Define
C_{v}=c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})
. Denote
\ell_{T}=\ln(e+T)
.

Bounding the term
\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}
.

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\textstyle(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\frac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t-1}}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\textstyle(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\frac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle c_{\chi}\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\left(\ln(1+D_{t})-\ln(1+D_{t-1})\right)~~\leq~~\textstyle c_{\chi}\ln(1+D_{T+1})~~\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}~~\textstyle c_{\chi}\ln(1+T4{\widehat{G}}^{2}),

where step
①
uses
\alpha_{t}^{2}\leq\frac{1+N_{t-1}}{1+D_{t-1}}\leq\frac{1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}}{1+D_{t-1}}
; step
②
uses
\frac{1+D_{t}}{1+D_{t-1}}=1+\frac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t-1}}\leq 1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
, which follows from
\chi_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
; step
③
uses Lemma
A.8
(a); step
④
uses
D_{T+1}=\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\chi_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}T
.

Part (a).

We derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{1+N_{t-1}}{1+D_{t-1}}\chi_{t}^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}}{1+D_{t}}\cdot\tfrac{1+D_{t}}{1+D_{t-1}}\cdot\chi_{t}^{2}\textstyle

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t}}~~\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}~~c_{\chi}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\ln(1+D_{t})-\ln(1+D_{t-1}))\textstyle

\displaystyle\overset{\text{④}}{=}

\displaystyle\textstyle c_{\chi}\ln(1+D_{T})~~\leq~~c_{\chi}\ln(1+T4{\widehat{G}}^{2})~~\overset{\text{⑤}}{\leq}~~c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\ln(e+T),

where step
①
uses
N_{t-1}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
; step

\tfrac{1+D_{t}}{1+D_{t-1}}=1+\frac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t-1}}\leq 1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
; step

uses Lemma
A.8
(a); step
④
uses
D_{0}=0
; step
⑤
uses
\ln(1+ab)\leq\ln(e+a)\ln(e+b)
for all

.

Part (b).

We derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle H_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{=}~~\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\beta_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\beta_{t+1}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{=}

\displaystyle\textstyle\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}}~~\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}~~\textstyle\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\ln(A_{t})-\ln(A_{t-1}))

\displaystyle\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\ln(A_{T})~~\overset{\text{⑤}}{\leq}~~\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\ln(1+\widehat{C}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{2})~~\overset{\text{⑥}}{\leq}~~\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\ln(1+\widehat{C}^{2}T^{3})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~~3\kappa^{2}\theta^{2}\ln(e+\widehat{C}^{2})\ln(e+T)~~=~~C_{h}\ell_{T},

where step
①
uses
\gamma_{t}=\theta\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\beta_{t}
; step
②
uses Lemma
C.2
(c) that
\frac{\beta_{t}}{\beta_{t+1}}\leq\kappa
; step
③
uses

, where
A_{t-1}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
; step
④
uses Lemma
A.8
(a); step
⑤
uses Lemma
C.2
(b) that

; step
⑥
uses
\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{2}=\frac{T(T+1)(2T+1)}{6}\leq T^{3}
.

Part (c).

The first sample needs to be handled separately, because
\mathbf{g}_{0}=\mathbf{g}_{1}
implies
\chi_{1}=0
. Since
\alpha_{1}=1
, unbiasedness at the fixed initial point gives

\mathbb{E}[\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{1}]~~=~~\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{1}\|^{2}~~=~~\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{g}_{1}\|^{2}-\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\|^{2}~~\leq~~{\widehat{G}}^{2}.

For
t\geq 2
,
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{g}_{t-1}
and
\alpha_{t}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}[\chi_{t}^{2}]~~=~~\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{g}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}\|^{2}~~\geq~~\mathbb{E}_{t}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}\|^{2}.

Using the tower property before summing yields

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}]~~\leq~~{\widehat{G}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=2}^{T}[\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}]~~\leq~~{\widehat{G}}^{2}+C_{v}\ell_{T}\leq({\widehat{G}}^{2}+C_{v})\ell_{T}~~=~~C_{e}\ell_{T}.

Part (d).

Applying Lemma
C.3
(a)
at iteration
t+1
gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\mathbf{s}_{t+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle(1-\alpha_{t+1})\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1},

where step
①
uses
\tfrac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha_{t+1}}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}<2
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{s}_{t+1}\|^{2}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\right).

Summing over
t=1,\ldots,T
and taking the expectation of both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}\left[2\left(\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{s}_{T+1}\|^{2}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathcal{E}_{t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\left({\widehat{G}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}]\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\left({\widehat{G}}^{2}+2L^{2}C_{h}\ell_{T}+\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}]\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\left({\widehat{G}}^{2}+2L^{2}C_{h}\ell_{T}+c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\ell_{T}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\left({\widehat{G}}^{2}+2L^{2}C_{h}+c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\right)\ell_{T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\left(4L^{2}C_{h}+4c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\right)\ell_{T}=(4L^{2}C_{h}+4C_{v})\ell_{T},

where step
①

\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{m}_{1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{g}_{1}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{g}_{1}\|^{2}-\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\|^{2}\leq{\widehat{G}}^{2}
(which follows from
\mathbf{m}_{1}=\mathbf{g}_{1}
), and
\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathcal{E}_{t}\big]=\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]\big]=0
(which follows from Lemma
C.3
(b) that
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=0
and the tower property); step
②
uses
\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=\frac{2L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\alpha_{t-1}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}

; step
③
uses
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\alpha_{t}^{2}\chi_{t}^{2}]\leq c_{\chi}\ln(1+T4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\leq c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\ell_{T}
.

Part (e).

Let
q_{t}:=\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}
,
w_{t}:=\alpha_{t}q_{t}
,
U_{0}:=0
,
and

\eta_{t}:=\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}
.
By Lemma
C.2
(a),

0\leq q_{t},w_{t}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
.
For
t\geq 2
, conditional unbiasedness gives

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\chi_{t}^{2}]=\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{g}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}_{t}\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}.

Since
U_{t-1}\alpha_{t}^{2}
is

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable,
U_{0}=0
, and

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=0
, parts (a)–(b) imply

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}U_{t-1}\eta_{t}\right]~~\leq~~\mathbb{E}\left[U_{T}\left(2L^{2}H_{T}+V_{T}\right)\right]~~\leq~~(2L^{2}C_{h}+C_{v})\ell_{T}\,\mathbb{E}[U_{T}].

(58)

Here the initial noise has zero weight, and

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=2L^{2}H_{T-1}\leq 2L^{2}H_{T}
, with
H_{0}:=0
.

The tracking recursion yields

\displaystyle\textstyle w_{t}\leq(1-\alpha_{t})(q_{t-1}-q_{t})+\eta_{t}.

(59)

Set
P_{t}:=U_{t-1}(1-\alpha_{t})
.
Since
U_{t}
is nondecreasing and
\alpha_{t}
is
nonincreasing,
P_{t}
is nondecreasing and
P_{1}=0
.
Summation by parts therefore gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}P_{t}(q_{t-1}-q_{t})=-P_{T}q_{T}+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}(P_{t+1}-P_{t})q_{t}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}U_{T}.

(60)

Multiplying the recursion by
U_{t-1}
, summing,
and using (
58
), we obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}U_{t-1}w_{t}\right]\leq\bigl[4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+(2L^{2}C_{h}+C_{v})\ell_{T}\bigr]\mathbb{E}[U_{T}].

(61)

Finally,

U_{T}^{2}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{t}^{2}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}U_{t-1}w_{t}

and
w_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}w_{t}
.
Using part (d) and
\ell_{T}\geq 1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\bigl[12{\widehat{G}}^{2}+(4L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{v})\ell_{T}\bigr]\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]

\displaystyle\leq(12{\widehat{G}}^{2}+4L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{v})C_{u}\ell_{T}^{2}=C^{\prime}_{u}\ell_{T}^{2}.

Moreover,

2C_{v}-12{\widehat{G}}^{2}\geq 2+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+32{\widehat{G}}^{4}\geq 0
.
Thus

12{\widehat{G}}^{2}+4L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{v}\leq C_{u}
,
which proves
C^{\prime}_{u}\leq C_{u}^{2}
.

∎

We next use proximal descent to transfer the tracking-error bounds to the descent energy. A pathwise comparison with the residual accumulator then controls the weighted cumulative motion, which is the remaining ingredient for the stationarity proof.

Lemma C.6
.

(Descent energy and motion bridge for MVR1) Let
\Delta:=F(\mathbf{x}_{1})-F_{\star}
,
\ell_{T}:=\ln(e+T)
. Let
C_{h}
and
C_{u}
be defined in
Lemma
C.5
. Then, for every integer
T\geq 1
,

(a)

\mathbb{E}[W_{T}]\leq C_{w}\ell_{T}
and

\mathbb{E}[W_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{w}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
, where
C_{w}:=2\Delta+C_{u}+(1+L)C_{h}
.

(b)

\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}M_{T}]\leq C_{m}\ell_{T}^{2}
, where
C_{m}:=\frac{C_{w}}{\theta}+\frac{C_{w}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{w}:=2\Delta+C_{u}+(1+L)C_{h}
.

Part (a-i).

Summing the first inequality of
Lemma
C.4
over
t=1,\ldots,T
gives

F(\mathbf{x}_{T+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}W_{T}\leq\frac{1}{2}U_{T}+\frac{1+L}{2}H_{T}.

Since
F(\mathbf{x}_{T+1})\geq F_{\star}
, we obtain

0\leq W_{T}\leq 2\Delta+U_{T}+(1+L)H_{T}.

(62)

Taking expectations and applying
Lemma
C.5
(b),(d), we have

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[W_{T}]~~\leq~~2\Delta+C_{u}\ell_{T}+(1+L)C_{h}\ell_{T}~~\leq~~\bigl(2\Delta+C_{u}+(1+L)C_{h}\bigr)\ell_{T}~~=~~C_{w}\ell_{T},

(63)

where the second inequality uses
\ell_{T}\geq 1
.

Part (a-ii).

Set the deterministic quantity

a_{T}:=2\Delta+(1+L)C_{h}\ell_{T}\geq 0.

Since
H_{T}\leq C_{h}\ell_{T}
almost surely,
(
62
) yields

0\leq W_{T}\leq a_{T}+U_{T}
.
Using Lemma
C.5
(d),(e),
we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{T}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle a_{T}^{2}+2a_{T}\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]+\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]~~\leq~~a_{T}^{2}+2a_{T}C_{u}\ell_{T}+C_{u}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{T}+C_{u}\ell_{T})^{2}~~=~~\bigl[2\Delta+\bigl(C_{u}+(1+L)C_{h}\bigr)\ell_{T}\bigr]^{2}~~\leq~~C_{w}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2},

where the last inequality again uses
\ell_{T}\geq 1
.
This proves both estimates in part (a).

Part (b).

Let
A_{t}:=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
, with
A_{0}:=1
.

The running-minimum definition of
\rho_{t}
ensures
that
\alpha_{t}=\rho_{t-1}
is nonincreasing.
Combining this property with

\gamma_{t}=\theta\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}/\sqrt{A_{t-1}}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle W_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\theta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\,\frac{A_{t}-A_{t-1}}{\sqrt{A_{t-1}}}~~\geq~~\theta\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{A_{t}-A_{t-1}}{\sqrt{A_{t-1}}}

(64)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\theta\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(\sqrt{A_{t}}-\sqrt{A_{t-1}}\bigr)~~=~~\textstyle 2\theta\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}\bigl(\sqrt{1+M_{T}}-1\bigr).

Here the second inequality follows from

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{A_{t}-A_{t-1}}{\sqrt{A_{t-1}}}~~=~~\bigl(\sqrt{A_{t}}-\sqrt{A_{t-1}}\bigr)\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{A_{t}}{A_{t-1}}}\right)~~\geq~~2\bigl(\sqrt{A_{t}}-\sqrt{A_{t-1}}\bigr),

(65)

because
A_{t}\geq A_{t-1}
. Rearranging (
64
) gives

\sqrt{\alpha_{T}(1+M_{T})}\leq\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}+\frac{W_{T}}{2\theta}.

Both sides are nonnegative.
Squaring and subtracting
\alpha_{T}
therefore yields

\alpha_{T}M_{T}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}}{\theta}W_{T}+\frac{W_{T}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}\leq\frac{W_{T}}{\theta}+\frac{W_{T}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}},

(66)

where the last inequality uses
0<\alpha_{T}\leq 1
.
Taking expectations and applying part (a),
we conclude that

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}M_{T}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbb{E}[W_{T}]+\frac{1}{4\theta^{2}}\mathbb{E}[W_{T}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{C_{w}}{\theta}\ell_{T}+\frac{C_{w}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}\ell_{T}^{2}~~\leq~~\left(\frac{C_{w}}{\theta}+\frac{C_{w}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}\right)\ell_{T}^{2}~~=~~C_{m}\ell_{T}^{2},

where the third inequality uses
\ell_{T}\geq 1
.

∎

The preceding lemmas bound the weighted tracking error and cumulative motion. We finish by estimating the inverse adaptive weight in terms of the noise variance, removing the weights by
Cauchy–Schwarz, and applying proximal optimality at the uniformly sampled output.

Theorem C.7
.

(Noise-adaptive composite stationarity of
ALFPG-MVR1
) Suppose Assumptions
3.1
,
5.1
,
5.2
,
5.3
, and
5.4
hold, and run the MVR1 branch of Algorithm
2
with any fixed
\theta>0
. Let
R\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,T\}
be independent
of all algorithmic randomness, and define
\mathcal{R}_{t}:=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t})\bigr)
,
\ell_{T}:=\ln(e+T)
. Let
C_{h},C_{u}
, and
C_{m}
be the constants in Lemmas
C.5

and
C.6
, and set
C_{R}:=\big((1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}+\sqrt{C_{u}}\big)\cdot\big(3+2L^{1/2}C_{h}^{1/4}\big)
. Then, for every integer
T\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{R+1}]\leq C_{R}\big(\frac{\ell_{T}^{5/4}}{T^{1/2}}+\frac{\sigma^{1/2}\ell_{T}}{T^{1/4}}\big).

(67)

Thus
ALFPG-MVR1
attains
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2}+\sigma^{1/2}T^{-1/4})
using one stochastic-gradient evaluation per iteration. Hence, the stochastic-gradient oracle complexity for obtaining
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{R+1}]\leq\epsilon
is
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma^{2}\epsilon^{-4})
.

Proof.

Define
D_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\chi_{i}^{2}
,
N_{t}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq t}\chi_{i}^{2}
,
S_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}
, with
D_{0}=N_{0}=0
.

Define
B_{T}:=1+3(T-1)\sigma^{2}+2L^{2}C_{h}\ell_{T}
. Let
\chi_{1}=0
.

Bounding the term
\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1}]
.

For
t\geq 2
, set

\mathbf{z}_{t}:=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\mathbf{g}_{t-1}
.
Since
\mathbf{z}_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable and

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\bm{\epsilon}_{t}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\chi_{t}^{2}]=\|\mathbf{z}_{t}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}
. Moreover,
\mathbf{z}_{t}=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})-\bm{\epsilon}_{t-1}
. By
L
-smoothness, the variance bound, and

\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t-1}=-\gamma_{t-1}\mathbf{r}_{t-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\chi_{t}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[2\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})\|^{2}+2\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\|\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 2L^{2}\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{t-1}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}]+3\sigma^{2}.

Since
\chi_{1}=0
, summing over
t=2,\ldots,T
yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D_{T}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\chi_{t}^{2}]\leq\textstyle 3(T-1)\sigma^{2}+2L^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\gamma_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\right]

(68)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\textstyle 3(T-1)\sigma^{2}+2L^{2}\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]~~\leq~~3(T-1)\sigma^{2}+2L^{2}C_{h}\ell_{T}~~=~~-1+B_{T}.

We further derive for
T\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1}]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\mathbb{E}\sqrt{1+D_{T}}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\sqrt{1+\mathbb{E}[D_{T}]}\leq B_{T}^{1/2},

(69)

where step
①
uses the running-minimum definition of
\alpha_{T}
for all
T\geq 1
that

; step
②
uses Jensen’s inequality; step
③
uses Inequality (
68
). Inequality (

.

Bounding the composite residual.

Global optimality of the proximal update implies

\displaystyle\textstyle\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})+\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})+\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t}\|~~\leq~~\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|+\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|+\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|

Removing the adaptive weights.

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{M_{T}}]~~=~~\mathbb{E}\big[\sqrt{\alpha_{T}M_{T}}\sqrt{\alpha_{T}^{-1}}\big]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}M_{T}]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1}]}~~\leq~~\sqrt{C_{m}}\,\ell_{T}\cdot B_{T}^{1/4},

where step

②
uses Lemma

(b) and Inequality (
69
);

On the other hand, we derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{S_{T}}]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\mathbb{E}\big[\sqrt{U_{T}}\sqrt{\alpha_{T}^{-1}}\big]~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1}]}~~\leq~~\sqrt{C_{u}}\,\ell_{T}^{1/2}B_{T}^{1/4},

where step
①

②

③
uses Lemma
C.5
(d).

Establishing the noise-adaptive rate.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{R+1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{t+1}]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\frac{1+L\theta}{T}\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|+\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1+L\theta}{\sqrt{T}}\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{M_{T}}]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{S_{T}}]~~\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}~~\frac{(1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}\ell_{T}+\sqrt{C_{u}}\ell_{T}^{1/2}}{\sqrt{T}}B_{T}^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\left((1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}+\sqrt{C_{u}}\right)\cdot\frac{\ell_{T}}{\sqrt{T}}B_{T}^{1/4}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\left((1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}+\sqrt{C_{u}}\right)\cdot\frac{\ell_{T}}{\sqrt{T}}\cdot\left(3+2L^{1/2}C_{h}^{1/4}\right)\left(\ell_{T}^{1/4}+T^{1/4}\sigma^{1/2}\right)

where step
①
uses Inequality (

); step
②
uses
Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; step
③
uses Inequalities (

) and (

uses

For MVR2, the same-sample correction changes the estimator bounds, so the MVR1 preliminary estimates cannot be reused directly. We first control the estimator increments and proximal residuals; in the convex case, the initial accumulator ratio is handled separately from subsequent iterations.

Lemma C.8
.

(Preliminary bounds for MVR2) Let
K_{h}:=\begin{cases}\theta^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\|,&h\text{ convex},\\
\hat{H},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}:=\prox_{\theta h}(\mathbf{x}_{1})
. Almost surely, for every
t\geq 1
and
T\geq 1
,

(a)

\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|\leq 2{\widehat{G}}/\alpha_{t}
and

\|\mathbf{m}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|\leq 4{\widehat{G}}
.

(b)

\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq C_{r}t
, where
C_{r}=2K_{h}+4{\widehat{G}}
.

(c)

If
h
is convex,
A_{t}/A_{t-1}\leq\kappa
for all
t\geq 1
, where
\kappa:=\max(2+16{\widehat{G}}^{2},1+C_{r}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{t}=\rho_{t-1}
. Define
C_{r}:=2K_{h}+4{\widehat{G}}
.

Part (a).

The stochastic momentum estimator is updated as

\mathbf{m}_{t}=(1-\alpha_{t})(\mathbf{m}_{t-1}-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{-})+\mathbf{g}_{t}
.
Since the stochastic oracle is bounded, we derive:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle(1-\alpha_{t})\|\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|+(2-\alpha_{t}){\widehat{G}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle(1-\alpha_{t})\frac{2{\widehat{G}}}{\alpha_{t}}+(2-\alpha_{t}){\widehat{G}}~~=~~\frac{2{\widehat{G}}}{\alpha_{t}}-\alpha_{t}{\widehat{G}}~~\leq~~\frac{2{\widehat{G}}}{\alpha_{t}},

where step
①
uses
\alpha_{t}\leq\alpha_{t-1}
and

\|\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|\leq 2{\widehat{G}}/\alpha_{t-1}
; the initial case follows
from
\mathbf{m}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{m}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}\|\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\|+(2-\alpha_{t}){\widehat{G}}~\leq~4{\widehat{G}}.

Part (b-i).

Suppose that
h
is convex.
Since
\gamma_{1}=\theta
and
\mathbf{m}_{1}=\mathbf{g}_{1}
,
nonexpansiveness of the proximal operator gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{1}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\|}{\theta}+\frac{\|\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|}{\theta}~\leq~K_{h}+{\widehat{G}}.

For
t\geq 2
, proximal optimality implies

\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t}\in\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})
and

\mathbf{r}_{t-1}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1}\in\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t})
.
Monotonicity of
\partial h
, together with

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}=-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{r}_{t}
, yields

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\leq~~\langle\mathbf{r}_{t-1}+\mathbf{m}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t-1},\mathbf{r}_{t}\rangle~~\leq~~\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+4{\widehat{G}}\bigr)\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|.

Therefore
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+4{\widehat{G}}
, and hence
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq C_{r}\cdot t
.

Part (b-ii).

Suppose that
h
is nonconvex and Lipschitz on
\dom(h)
.
Global optimality of the proximal update, with
\mathbf{x}_{t}
as a comparison
point, gives

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}}{2\gamma_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x}_{t})-h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|)\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|.

If
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}
, then
\mathbf{r}_{t}=\mathbf{0}
.
Otherwise, dividing by
\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|
gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|~~\leq~~2(K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|)~~\leq~~2K_{h}+4{\widehat{G}}t^{2/3}~~\leq~~C_{r}\cdot t.

Part (c).

For
t\geq 2
, we derive:

\displaystyle\tfrac{A_{t}}{A_{t-1}}~~\overset{\text{①}}{=}~~\tfrac{A_{t-1}+\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}~~{\leq}~~1+\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~1+\tfrac{(\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+4{\widehat{G}})^{2}}{1+\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}}~~\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}~~1+1+16{\widehat{G}}^{2},

where step
①
uses the definition of
A_{t}:=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
; step
②
uses
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|+4{\widehat{G}}
; step
③
uses
(a+b)^{2}\leq(1+a^{2})(1+b^{2})
for all
a,b
.
For
t=1
, we have:
\tfrac{A_{t}}{A_{t-1}}=1+\|\mathbf{r}_{1}\|^{2}\leq 1+C_{r}^{2}
. Taking both cases into consideration, we have
\tfrac{A_{t}}{A_{t-1}}\leq\kappa:=\max(2+16{\widehat{G}}^{2},1+C_{r}^{2})
.

∎

We now exploit individual smoothness to control the difference of two gradients evaluated with the same sample. Rescaling the tracking error produces a contractive recursion with an explicit
innovation term and a conditionally mean-zero cross term.

Lemma C.9
.

(Tracking-error recursion for MVR2) Retain the notation introduced above, and define

\begin{gathered}\bm{\delta}_{t}:={\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}-{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-},\qquad\bm{\zeta}_{t}:=\frac{\bm{\delta}_{t}+\alpha_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}}{\alpha_{t}^{1/4}},\qquad\varpi_{t}:=(1-\alpha_{t})\left(\frac{\alpha_{t-1}}{\alpha_{t}}\right)^{1/4},\\
\mathcal{E}_{t}:=2\varpi_{t}\langle\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\bm{\zeta}_{t}\rangle,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{t}:=\|\bm{\zeta}_{t}\|^{2},\qquad\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}:=\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2},\quad\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}:=11L^{2}\frac{\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}.\end{gathered}

Then, almost surely, for every
t\geq 1
:

(a)

\varpi_{t}^{2}\leq 1-\alpha_{t}
, and

\mathcal{V}_{t}\leq\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
.
In particular,

P_{t}=\varpi_{t}^{2}P_{t-1}+\mathcal{V}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}\leq(1-\alpha_{t})P_{t-1}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}.

(74)

(b)

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=0
, and
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}\leq 4P_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}
.

Proof.

Define
\mathbf{s}_{t}=\mathbf{m}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
, and
\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{s}_{t}/\alpha_{t}^{1/4}
, where
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
and
P_{0}=0
.

Define the random increment
\bm{\eta}_{t}:={\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}-(1-\alpha_{t}){\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}=\bm{\delta}_{t}+\alpha_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}
. Let
\alpha_{t}=\rho_{t-1}
.

Exact decomposition of the tracking error.

Using the MVR2 update, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{t}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{t})(\mathbf{m}_{t-1}-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{-})+\mathbf{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{s}_{t-1}+{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}-(1-\alpha_{t}){\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{t})\mathbf{s}_{t-1}+\bm{\eta}_{t}.

(75)

Dividing by
\alpha_{t}^{1/4}
gives

\mathbf{z}_{t}=\varpi_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t-1}+\bm{\zeta}_{t}.

At
t=1
,
\alpha_{1}=1
and

\bm{\delta}_{1}=\mathbf{0}
, so this identity also
holds under the convention
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
.

Bounding the term
\mathcal{V}_{t}:=\|\bm{\zeta}_{t}\|^{2}
.

By individual smoothness and smoothness of the expected loss,

\displaystyle\textstyle\|{\bm{\delta}}_{t}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\mathbf{g}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{-}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})\|~~\leq~~\|\mathbf{g}_{t}-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{-}\|+\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 2L\|\mathbf{x}_{t}-\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\|~~=~~2L\gamma_{t-1}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|.

The last equality also holds at
t=1
because

\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{1}
and
\gamma_{0}=0
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\bm{\eta}_{t}\|^{2}~~=~~\|\bm{\delta}_{t}+\alpha_{t}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}~~\leq~~2\|\bm{\delta}_{t}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{t}^{2}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}~~\leq~~8L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{t}^{2}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}.

Dividing both sides by
\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}
and using the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{t}=\frac{\|\bm{\eta}_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}
yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{t}~~\leq~~\textstyle\frac{8L^{2}\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}+2\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\textstyle 8L^{2}\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}\frac{\gamma_{t-1}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}+2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}~~\leq~~\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t},

(76)

where step
①
uses
\alpha_{t-1}/\alpha_{t}={\rho_{t-2}}/{\rho_{t-1}}\leq 1+2/3
by Lemma
C.1
(a).

Part (a).

For all
t\geq 2
, we derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle\varpi_{t}^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{=}~~(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{t-1}}{\alpha_{t}}}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\frac{(1-\alpha_{t})^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{t}}}~~\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}~~1-\alpha_{t},

(77)

where step
①
uses the definition of
\varpi
; step
②
uses
\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{t-1}}\leq\frac{2}{3}
, leading to
\frac{\alpha_{t-1}}{\alpha_{t}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{t}}
; step
③
uses
(1-a)\leq\sqrt{1-\tfrac{2}{3}a}
for
a\in[0,1]
. Inequality (
77
) also holds at
t=1
because
\varpi_{1}=0
.

Expanding the squared norm of the rescaled recursion yields

\displaystyle P_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\varpi_{t}\mathbf{z}_{t-1}+\bm{\zeta}_{t}\|^{2}~~=~~\varpi_{t}^{2}P_{t-1}+\|\bm{\zeta}_{t}\|^{2}+2\varpi_{t}\langle\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\bm{\zeta}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varpi_{t}^{2}P_{t-1}+\mathcal{V}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~(1-\alpha_{t})P_{t-1}+\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t},

where step
①
uses Inequality (
76
),
\varpi_{t}^{2}\leq 1-\alpha_{t}
, and
P_{t-1}\geq 0
.

Part (b).

By conditional unbiasedness,
\mathbb{E}_{t}[{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}]=\mathbf{0}
, and
\mathbb{E}_{t}[{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}]=\mathbf{0}
. Since
\alpha_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable,

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\bm{\zeta}_{t}]=\alpha_{t}^{-1/4}\mathbb{E}_{t}[{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}-(1-\alpha_{t}){\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}]=\mathbf{0}.

Moreover,
\varpi_{t}
and
\mathbf{z}_{t-1}
are

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=2\varpi_{t}\langle\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\mathbb{E}_{t}[\bm{\zeta}_{t}]\rangle=0.

Finally, Cauchy–Schwarz and
\varpi_{t}^{2}\leq 1
give

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}=4\varpi_{t}^{2}\langle\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\bm{\zeta}_{t}\rangle^{2}\leq 4\varpi_{t}^{2}P_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}\leq 4P_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}.

∎

The rescaled recursion is now coupled to proximal descent. The resulting Lyapunov inequality tracks both objective progress and estimator accuracy, with the normalized motion accumulated in
the correction term.

Lemma C.10
.

(Proximal descent and a joint Lyapunov inequality for MVR2) Let
C_{f}:=(1+L)/2+22L^{2}
and

\mathcal{Q}_{t}:=F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+2P_{t}-C_{f}H_{t-1}
.
Then, almost surely,

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1+L}{2}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2},

(78)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{t+1}-\mathcal{Q}_{t}+\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+2\mathcal{E}_{t+1}.

(79)

Proof.

By global optimality of the proximal update and
L
-smoothness of
f
,

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq h(\mathbf{x}_{t})-\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle-\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}}{2\gamma_{t}},

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}.

Part (a).

Adding the preceding two inequalities and using

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}=-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{r}_{t}
, we derive:

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{s}_{t}\rangle-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1+L}{2}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2},

where step
①
uses the weighted Young inequality

\gamma_{t}\langle\mathbf{r}_{t},\mathbf{s}_{t}\rangle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}
.

Part (b).

Since
\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}=\alpha_{t}P_{t}
, part (a) gives

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\frac{\gamma_{t}}{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}~~\leq~~\textstyle\alpha_{t}P_{t}+\frac{1+L}{2}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{t+1}P_{t}+\frac{1+L}{2}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~2(P_{t}-P_{t+1})+C_{f}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}+4\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}+2\mathcal{E}_{t+1},

where step
①
uses
\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{t+1}\leq 1+2/3<2
;
step
②
uses Lemma
C.9
(a) and

\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t+1}=11L^{2}\gamma_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}/\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}
.
Finally,

H_{t}-H_{t-1}=\gamma_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}/\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}
.
Substituting the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{t}
proves
(
79
).

We next establish the cumulative estimates needed to sum the MVR2 descent inequality. The proof
treats the convex and domain-Lipschitz motion bounds separately and controls second moments
without assuming that the rescaled tracking error is uniformly bounded.

Lemma C.11
.

(Logarithmic bounds for MVR2) Then, for every
T\geq 1
,

(a)

V_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\chi_{t}^{2}\leq C_{v}\ell_{T}
almost surely, where
C_{v}:=2(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})
.

(b)

Let
Q_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
. We have:
\mathbb{E}[Q_{T}]\leq C_{q}\ell_{T}
and

\mathbb{E}[Q_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{q}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
, where
C_{q}:=8{\widehat{G}}^{2}+2C_{v}
.

(c)

Let
H_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}
. We have:
\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]\leq C_{h}\ell_{T}
and
\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{h}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
, where
C_{h}:=\begin{cases}\dot{C}_{h},&h\text{ convex}\\
\ddot{C}_{h},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
,
\dot{C}_{h}:=6\theta^{2}\kappa\ln(e+C_{r})
,
\ddot{C}_{h}:=\theta^{2}\bigl[C_{A}+B_{h}+80(11L^{2}\theta^{2}+2C_{q})\bigr]
,
C_{A}:=3+\ln(1+C_{r}^{2})
, and
B_{h}:=8(K_{h}+{\widehat{G}})^{2}
.

(d)

Let
U_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}P_{t}
. Then
\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]\leq C_{u}\ell_{T}
and
\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{u}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
. Here,
C_{u}:=20(11L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{q})
.

Proof.

Define
\chi_{t}:=\|\mathbf{g}_{t-1}-\mathbf{g}_{t}^{-}\|
. Let
c_{\chi}:=(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}
.

Define
\alpha_{t}=\rho_{t-1}
, where
\rho_{t}:=\min_{1\leq k\leq t}\left(\frac{1+\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\chi_{i}^{2}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}^{2}}\right)^{2/3}
.

Define
D_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\chi_{i}^{2}
, and
N_{t}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq t}\chi_{i}^{2}
.

Part (a).

We derive:

{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\textstyle V_{T}&\leq&\textstyle V_{T+1}~~\overset{\text{①}}{=}~~\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\chi_{t}^{2}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\frac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t-1}}\\
&\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}&\textstyle(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\frac{\chi_{t}^{2}}{1+D_{t}}~~\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}~~c_{\chi}\big(\ln(1+D_{T+1})-\ln(1+D_{1})\big)\\
&\leq&c_{\chi}\ln(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}(T+1))~~\overset{\text{⑤}}{\leq}~~\underbrace{2c_{\chi}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})}_{:=C_{v}}\ell_{T}.\end{array}}

where step
①
uses
\chi_{1}=0
; step
②
uses
\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\leq(1+N_{t-1})/(1+D_{t-1})
and
N_{t-1}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
; step
③
uses
(1+D_{t})/(1+D_{t-1})\leq 1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
; step
④
uses
\chi_{t}^{2}/(1+D_{t})\leq\ln(1+D_{t})-\ln(1+D_{t-1})
; step
⑤
uses
\ln(1+a(T+1))\leq\ln(e+a)\ln(e+T+1)\leq 2\ln(e+a)\ln(e+T)
for all
a\geq 0
.

Part (b-i).

Let
q_{t}:=\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}=\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}
and

Q_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}q_{i}
, where
Q_{0}=0
.
Since
0<\alpha_{t}\leq 1
and

\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|\leq 2{\widehat{G}}
, we have

0\leq q_{t}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2},\qquad q_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}q_{t}.

For
t\geq 2
, recall that

\chi_{t}=\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t-1}-{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|
.
Since
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t-1}
is

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable and

\mathbb{E}_{t}[{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}]=\mathbf{0}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{t}[\chi_{t}^{2}]=\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}_{t}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}.

Because
\alpha_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable,
\mathbb{E}_{t}[q_{t}]\leq\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\chi_{t}^{2}]
for all
t\geq 2
.

We obtain:

\displaystyle V_{T+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\ln(1+D_{T+1})~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\ln(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2}T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})\ell_{T}.

where step
①
uses
D_{T+1}=\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\chi_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}T
since
\chi_{1}=0
and
\chi_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}
. Taking expectations, summing the conditional comparison over

t=2,\ldots,T+1
, and bounding
q_{1}
separately therefore gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[Q_{T}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}]~~\leq~~4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\chi_{t}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+\frac{C_{v}}{2}\ell_{T}~~\leq~~\frac{C_{v}}{2}+\frac{C_{v}}{2}\ell_{T}\leq C_{v}\ell_{T}.

Part (b-ii).

The identity

Q_{t}^{2}-Q_{t-1}^{2}=2Q_{t-1}q_{t}+q_{t}^{2}
,
together with
Q_{0}=0
, gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}^{2}]=2\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}Q_{t-1}q_{t}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}q_{t}^{2}\right].

For
t\geq 2
, the measurability of

Q_{t-1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}
gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[Q_{t-1}q_{t}]~~=~~\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{t-1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\mathbb{E}_{t}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}\right]~~\leq~~\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{t-1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\mathbb{E}_{t}[\chi_{t}^{2}]\right]~~=~~\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{t-1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\chi_{t}^{2}\right].

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}^{2}]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}Q_{t-1}\alpha_{t}^{3/2}\chi_{t}^{2}\right]~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~4{\widehat{G}}^{2}\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}]+2\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}V_{T+1}]

(80)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle(4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+2C_{v}\ell_{T})\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}]~~\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}~~(4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+2C_{v}\ell_{T})(4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+C_{v}\ell_{T})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 2(4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+C_{v}\ell_{T})^{2}\leq C_{q}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2},

where step
①
uses
q_{t}^{2}\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}q_{t}
and
Q_{0}=0
; step
②
uses
Q_{t-1}\leq Q_{T+1}
; step
③
uses
V_{T+1}\leq C_{v}\ell_{T}
; step
④
uses
\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}]\leq 4{\widehat{G}}^{2}+C_{v}\ell_{T}
.

Part (c-i).

We consider the case when
h(\cdot)
is convex. We have almost surely:

{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\textstyle H_{T}&=&\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\gamma_{t}^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{=}~~\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\theta^{2}\kappa\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t}}~~\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}~~\theta^{2}\kappa\ln(A_{T})\\
&\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}&\textstyle\theta^{2}\kappa\ln(1+TC_{r}^{2}T^{2})~~\leq~~\theta^{2}\kappa\ln(e+T^{3})\ln(e+C_{r}^{2})~~\leq~~6\theta^{2}\kappa\ln(e+C_{r})\ell_{T},\end{array}}

where step
①
uses
\gamma_{t}=\theta\alpha_{t}^{1/4}\beta_{t}=\theta\alpha_{t}^{1/4}A_{t-1}^{-1/2}
; step
②
uses
A_{t}/A_{t-1}\leq\kappa
; step
③
uses
\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t}}\leq\ln A_{t}-\ln A_{t-1}
; step
④
uses
A_{T}=1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}
and
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq C_{r}t
. Letting
C_{h}=\dot{C}_{h}=6\theta^{2}\kappa\ln(e+C_{r})
, we directly conclude that

\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{h}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2},\qquad\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]\leq C_{h}\ell_{T}.

Part (c-ii).

We consider the case when
h(\cdot)
is Lipschitz on
\dom(h)
with constant
K_{h}
(convexity is not required). Define
A_{t}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|^{2}
, and
P_{t}=\frac{\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}
,
C_{r}:=2K_{h}+4{\widehat{G}}
,
C_{A}:=3+\ln(1+C_{r}^{2})
,
B_{h}:=8(K_{h}+{\widehat{G}})^{2}
,
C_{v}:=2(1+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})^{2}\ln(e+4{\widehat{G}}^{2})
,
C_{q}:=8{\widehat{G}}^{2}+2C_{v}
.

Define
v_{t}:=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{t}}{A_{t-1}}
,
e_{t}:=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{t}}{A_{t-1}}
,
\overline{B}_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}v_{i}
,
\overline{Z}_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}e_{i}
, with
\overline{B}_{0}=\overline{Z}_{0}=0
.

\quad\bullet

A telescoping potential.
Define
\Psi_{t}:=\frac{B_{h}+8P_{t}}{A_{t-1}}
. Global optimality of the proximal update gives

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|^{2}}{2\gamma_{t}}~~\leq~~h(\mathbf{x}_{t})-h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-\langle\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\rangle~~\leq~~(K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|)\|\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\mathbf{x}_{t}\|.

(81)

Thus
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq 2(K_{h}+\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|)
, also when

\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\mathbf{x}_{t}
.
Since
\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|\leq{\widehat{G}}+\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|
and

\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}P_{t}\leq P_{t}
, we have

\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\leq B_{h}+8P_{t}
.
Using
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}=A_{t}-A_{t-1}
, we obtain

{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}~~=~~\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t}}+\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{A_{t-1}}-\frac{1}{A_{t}}\right)~~\leq~~\ln A_{t}-\ln A_{t-1}+(B_{h}+8P_{t})\left(\frac{1}{A_{t-1}}-\frac{1}{A_{t}}\right).\end{array}}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\textstyle\Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_{t}=-(B_{h}+8P_{t})\left(\frac{1}{A_{t-1}}-\frac{1}{A_{t}}\right)+\frac{8(P_{t+1}-P_{t})}{A_{t}}.

(82)

Adding these relations and using the tracking-error recursion yields

\displaystyle\textstyle\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}+\Psi_{t+1}-\Psi_{t}\leq\ln A_{t}-\ln A_{t-1}+8\frac{\mathcal{V}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1}}{A_{t}}.

(83)

Summing over
t=1,\ldots,T
, and using
\Psi_{T+1}\geq 0
,

\Psi_{1}=B_{h}+8P_{1}
,
P_{1}=\mathcal{V}_{1}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{1}=0
, gives

\displaystyle\textstyle H_{T}:=\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}}\leq\theta^{2}\left[\ln A_{T}+B_{h}+8\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\frac{\mathcal{V}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}}{A_{t-1}}\right].

(84)

\quad\bullet

Bounding the cumulative innovations.
We first bound the nonnegative cumulative innovation

\overline{B}_{T+1}
.
For
t\geq 2
, the innovation estimate gives

v_{t}\leq 11L^{2}\theta^{2}\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t-1}\|^{2}}{A_{t-2}A_{t-1}}+\frac{2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}}{A_{t-1}}.

Since
\mathcal{V}_{1}=\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
A_{t-1}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\overline{B}_{T+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 11L^{2}\theta^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}}{A_{t-1}A_{t}}+2Q_{T+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle 11L^{2}\theta^{2}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{A_{T}}\big)+2Q_{T+1}~~\leq~~11L^{2}\theta^{2}+2Q_{T+1}.

The proof of part (b) established

\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}^{2}]\leq C_{q}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
.
Therefore, Cauchy–Schwarz and
\ell_{T}\geq 1
yield

\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\leq\left(11L^{2}\theta^{2}+2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}^{2}]}\right)^{2}\leq(11L^{2}\theta^{2}+2C_{q})^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}.

(85)

\quad\bullet

A direct second-moment estimate.

We use partial sums as weights and summation by parts, as in the proof
of Lemma
C.5
(e).
For
t\geq 2
, the tracking-error recursion,
\varpi_{t}^{2}\leq 1
, and

A_{t-1}\geq A_{t-2}
imply

\frac{P_{t}}{A_{t-1}}\leq\frac{P_{t-1}}{A_{t-2}}+v_{t}+e_{t}.

Since
P_{1}=\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
e_{1}=0
, induction gives

0\leq\frac{P_{t}}{A_{t-1}}\leq\overline{B}_{t}+\overline{Z}_{t}.

Moreover,
A_{t-1}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable, so

\mathbb{E}_{t}[e_{t}]=0
.
The pointwise estimate

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}\leq 4P_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}
gives, for
t\geq 2
,

e_{t}^{2}\leq 4\frac{P_{t-1}}{A_{t-1}}v_{t}\leq 4(\overline{B}_{t-1}+\overline{Z}_{t-1})v_{t}.

The last inequality also holds at
t=1
because
e_{1}=0
.
Expanding the square and using conditional mean zero, we obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}e_{t}^{2}\leq 4\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}(\overline{B}_{t-1}+\overline{Z}_{t-1})v_{t}.

To control the right-hand side, summation by parts yields

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\overline{B}_{t-1}v_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}v_{t}^{2},

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\overline{Z}_{t-1}v_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\overline{B}_{T+1}\overline{Z}_{T+1}-\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\overline{B}_{t-1}e_{t}-\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}v_{t}e_{t}.

Because
\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{t-1}e_{t}]=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]~~\leq~~2\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]+4\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}\overline{Z}_{T+1}]-4\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}v_{t}e_{t}.

Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the two cross terms as follows:

\displaystyle\textstyle\big|\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}\overline{Z}_{T+1}]\big|~~\leq~~\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]},

and

\displaystyle\textstyle\left|\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}v_{t}e_{t}\right|~~\leq~~\sqrt{\big(\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}v_{t}^{2}\big)\big(\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}e_{t}^{2}\big)}~~\leq~~\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]}.

Consequently, Young’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]~~\leq~~2\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]+8\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]}~~\leq~~34\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}].

Thus
\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]\leq 68\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]
.
Another application of Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\mathbb{E}[(\overline{B}_{T+1}+\overline{Z}_{T+1})^{2}]\leq(1+\sqrt{68})^{2}\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\leq 100\mathbb{E}[\overline{B}_{T+1}^{2}].

(86)

\quad\bullet

Establishing
\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{h}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]\leq C_{h}\ell_{T}
.
We combine the preceding estimates to bound
H_{T}
.
The preliminary estimate
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|\leq C_{r}t
gives

\displaystyle\ln A_{T}\leq\ln(1+C_{r}^{2}T^{3})\leq C_{A}\ell_{T}.

By (
84
),

\displaystyle 0~~\leq~~H_{T}\leq\theta^{2}\left[C_{A}\ell_{T}+B_{h}+8(\overline{B}_{T+1}+\overline{Z}_{T+1})\right].

Expanding the square and applying Cauchy–Schwarz, together with
(
85
) and
(
86
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]}

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{2}\left[C_{A}\ell_{T}+B_{h}+8\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(\overline{B}_{T+1}+\overline{Z}_{T+1})^{2}]}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{2}\left[C_{A}+B_{h}+80(11L^{2}\theta^{2}+2C_{q})\right]\ell_{T}~~=~~\ddot{C}_{h}\ell_{T}~~=~~C_{h}\ell_{T}.

Squaring this inequality and using

\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]}
, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{h}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2},\qquad\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]\leq C_{h}\ell_{T}.

(87)

Part (d-i).

Define
\mathcal{B}_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{V}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{E}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{Z}_{0}=0
.

The tracking recursion at iteration
t+1
gives

\alpha_{t+1}P_{t}\leq P_{t}-P_{t+1}+\mathcal{V}_{t+1}+\mathcal{E}_{t+1}.

Since
\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{t+1}\leq 5/3<2
, summing over

t=1,\ldots,T
and using
P_{1}=\mathcal{V}_{1}
,

\mathcal{E}_{1}=0
, and
P_{T+1}\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle U_{T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t+1}P_{t}~~\leq~~2\left[P_{1}-P_{T+1}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}(\mathcal{V}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t})\right]

(88)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\textstyle 2(\mathcal{B}_{T+1}+\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}-P_{T+1})\leq 2(\mathcal{B}_{T+1}+\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{B}_{T+1}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}(\ddot{\mathcal{E}}_{t}+2\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{t})=11L^{2}H_{T}+2Q_{T+1}.

(89)

Conditional mean zero gives
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}]=0
.
Taking expectations in (
88
)
and applying part (c) and the bound
\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}]\leq C_{v}\ell_{T}
proved in part (b-i), we obtain the more precise first-moment estimate

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}]\leq 2(11L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{v})\ell_{T}.

(90)

Part (d-ii).

We again use partial sums and summation by parts, without
assuming a uniform bound on
P_{t}
.
The exact recursion gives the identity

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{B}_{t}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}=P_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}(1-\varpi_{i}^{2})P_{i-1}\geq P_{t}\geq 0.

(91)

By conditional mean zero,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]~~=~~\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}\leq 4\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}P_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}~~\leq~~4\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}(\mathcal{B}_{t-1}+\mathcal{Z}_{t-1})\mathcal{V}_{t}.

(92)

The partial-sum identities give

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{B}_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{V}_{t}^{2},

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{Z}_{t-1}\mathcal{V}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{B}_{T+1}\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}-\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{B}_{t-1}\mathcal{E}_{t}-\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{V}_{t}\mathcal{E}_{t}.

Here
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{t-1}\mathcal{E}_{t}]=0

because
\mathcal{B}_{t-1}
is predictable.
Combining these identities with (
92
)
and Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]

\displaystyle\textstyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]+4\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}]-4\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{V}_{t}\mathcal{E}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\textstyle 2\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]+8\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]}

\displaystyle\leq\textstyle 34\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}^{2}].

For the second inequality, the same-time product is bounded by

\textstyle\left|\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{V}_{t}\mathcal{E}_{t}\right|\leq\sqrt{\left(\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{V}_{t}^{2}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}\right)}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]}.

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{T+1}^{2}]\leq 68\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}],\qquad\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{B}_{T+1}+\mathcal{Z}_{T+1})^{2}]}\leq 10\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]}.

(93)

Finally, (
89
), part (c), and the bound
\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}^{2}]\leq C_{q}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
in (
80
) imply

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}_{T+1}^{2}]}\leq 11L^{2}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]}+2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Q_{T+1}^{2}]}\leq(11L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{q})\ell_{T}.

Since the right-hand side of (
88
)
is nonnegative, squaring that inequality and invoking
(
93
) yields

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]}\leq 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{B}_{T+1}+\mathcal{Z}_{T+1})^{2}]}\leq 20(11L^{2}C_{h}+2C_{q})\ell_{T}=C_{u}\ell_{T}.

Together with (
90
), or simply

\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]}
, this proves
both claims in part (d).

∎

With the first two moments of the tracking and normalized motion sums available, we can bound the descent energy. The following pathwise comparison then transfers this control to the residual
accumulator with the weight appropriate to MVR2.

Lemma C.12
.

(Descent energy and motion bridge for MVR2) Let
\Delta:=F(\mathbf{x}_{1})-F_{\star}
and
\ell_{T}:=\ln(e+T)
. Let
C_{h}
and
C_{u}
be defined in Lemma
C.11
. Then, for every integer
T\geq 1
,

(a)

Define
W_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}
. We have
\mathbb{E}[W_{T}]\leq C_{w}\ell_{T}
and

\mathbb{E}[W_{T}^{2}]\leq C_{w}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}
,
where
C_{w}:=2\Delta+C_{u}+(1+L)C_{h}
.

(b)

\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}M_{T}]\leq C_{m}\ell_{T}^{2}
,
where
C_{m}:=C_{w}/\theta+C_{w}^{2}/(4\theta^{2})
.

Proof.

Define
W_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}
. Let
L_{+}=L+1
.

Part (a-i).

Summing the first inequality of Lemma
C.10

over
t=1,\ldots,T
gives
\frac{1}{2}W_{T}\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{1})-F(\mathbf{x}_{T+1})+\frac{1}{2}U_{T}+\frac{1+L}{2}H_{T}
. Since
F(\mathbf{x}_{T+1})\geq F_{\star}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq W_{T}\leq 2\Delta+U_{T}+L_{+}H_{T}.

(94)

Taking expectations and applying Lemma
C.11
(c),(d), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{T}]~~\leq~~2\Delta+C_{u}\ell_{T}+L_{+}C_{h}\ell_{T}~~\leq~~\bigl(2\Delta+C_{u}+L_{+}C_{h}\bigr)\ell_{T}~~=~~C_{w}\ell_{T}.

Part (a-ii).

Squaring (
94
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{T}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\Delta^{2}+\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]+L_{+}^{2}\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]+4\Delta\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]+4\Delta L_{+}\mathbb{E}[H_{T}]+2L_{+}\mathbb{E}[U_{T}H_{T}]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle 4\Delta^{2}+C_{u}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}+L_{+}^{2}C_{h}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}+4\Delta C_{u}\ell_{T}+4\Delta L_{+}C_{h}\ell_{T}+2L_{+}\mathbb{E}[U_{T}H_{T}]

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}

\displaystyle 4\Delta^{2}+C_{u}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}+L_{+}^{2}C_{h}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2}+4\Delta C_{u}\ell_{T}+4\Delta L_{+}C_{h}\ell_{T}+2L_{+}C_{u}C_{h}\ell_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\Delta+C_{u}\ell_{T}+L_{+}C_{h}\ell_{T})^{2}\leq C_{w}^{2}\ell_{T}^{2},

where step
①
uses Lemma
C.11
; step
②
uses Cauchy–Schwarz that
\mathbb{E}[U_{T}H_{T}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[H_{T}^{2}]}\leq C_{u}C_{h}\ell_{T}^{2}
, where the last step uses Lemma
C.11
.

Part (b).

We obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle W_{T}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_{t}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}~~\overset{\text{①}}{=}~~\textstyle\theta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}^{1/4}\frac{A_{t}-A_{t-1}}{\sqrt{A_{t-1}}}~~\geq~~\theta\alpha_{T}^{1/4}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{A_{t}-A_{t-1}}{\sqrt{A_{t-1}}}

(95)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{②}}{\geq}

\displaystyle\textstyle 2\theta\alpha_{T}^{1/4}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\sqrt{A_{t}}-\sqrt{A_{t-1}})~~=~~2\theta\alpha_{T}^{1/4}(\sqrt{1+M_{T}}-1),

where step
①
uses
\gamma_{t}=\theta\alpha_{t}^{1/4}/\sqrt{A_{t-1}}
and
\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|^{2}=A_{t}-A_{t-1}
; step
②
uses the fact that
\frac{A_{t}-A_{t-1}}{\sqrt{A_{t-1}}}=(\sqrt{A_{t}}-\sqrt{A_{t-1}})\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{A_{t}}{A_{t-1}}}\right)\geq 2(\sqrt{A_{t}}-\sqrt{A_{t-1}})
.

We have from Inequality (
95
):
W_{T}+2\theta\alpha_{T}^{1/4}\geq 2\theta\alpha_{T}^{1/4}\sqrt{1+M_{T}}
. Squaring both
nonnegative sides and dividing both sides by
4\theta^{2}
gives
\tfrac{1}{4\theta^{2}}W^{2}_{T}+\alpha_{T}^{1/2}+\tfrac{1}{\theta}W_{T}\alpha_{T}^{1/4}\geq\alpha_{T}^{1/2}(1+M_{T})
, leading to:

\displaystyle\textstyle\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}M_{T}~~\leq~~\frac{\alpha_{T}^{1/4}}{\theta}W_{T}+\frac{W_{T}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}~~\leq~~\frac{W_{T}}{\theta}+\frac{W_{T}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}.

Taking expectations and applying Part (a), we conclude that

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}M_{T}]~~\leq~~\frac{C_{w}}{\theta}\ell_{T}+\frac{C_{w}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}\ell_{T}^{2}~~\leq~~\left(\frac{C_{w}}{\theta}+\frac{C_{w}^{2}}{4\theta^{2}}\right)\ell_{T}^{2}~~=~~C_{m}\ell_{T}^{2}.

∎

We now assemble the MVR2 stationarity guarantee. Unlike MVR1, its discrepancy compares two samples at the same point, so the inverse-weight estimate depends directly on the noise variance;
combining this estimate with the motion and tracking bounds yields the stated rate.

Theorem C.13
.

(Noise-adaptive composite stationarity of
ALFPG-MVR2
) Suppose Assumptions
3.1
,
5.1
,

5.2
,
5.3
,

5.4
, and
5.7
hold,
and run the MVR2 branch of Algorithm
2

with any fixed
\theta>0
.
Let
R\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,T\}
be independent
of all algorithmic randomness, and define
\mathcal{R}_{t}:=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t})\bigr)
. Let
C_{u}
and
C_{m}
be the constants in
Lemmas
C.11
and
C.12
, and set
C_{R}:=2(1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}+2\sqrt{C_{u}}
. Then, for every integer
T\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{R+1}]\leq C_{R}\ell_{T}\big(\frac{1}{T^{1/2}}+\frac{\sigma^{1/3}}{T^{1/3}}\big).

(96)

Thus
ALFPG-MVR2
attains

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2}+\sigma^{1/3}T^{-1/3})
;
when
\sigma=0
, this reduces to

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2})
.
Hence, the stochastic-gradient oracle complexity for obtaining

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{R+1}]\leq\epsilon
is

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2}+\sigma\epsilon^{-3})
.

Proof.

Define
\ell_{T}:=\ln(e+T)
, and
\mathcal{R}_{t}:=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})+\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t})\bigr)
.

Define
D_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\chi_{i}^{2}
and
N_{t}=\max_{1\leq i\leq t}\chi_{i}^{2}
, where
\chi_{1}=0
.

Define the deterministic quantity
\mathcal{B}_{T}:=1+2(T-1)\sigma^{2}
.

Bounding the term
\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1/2}]
.

For
T\geq 2
, the following running-maximum expression is nonempty. Using the initial discrepancy
\chi_{1}=0
, we derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle\alpha_{T}^{-1/2}~~=~~\big(\max_{1\leq k\leq T-1}\frac{1+D_{k}}{1+N_{k}}\big)^{1/3}~~\leq~~\big(1+D_{T-1}\big)^{1/3}~~\leq~~\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}\chi_{i}^{2}\big)^{1/3}.

Taking the expectations yields:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1/2}]~~\leq~~\mathbb{E}[\big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}\chi_{i}^{2}\big)^{1/3}]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\big(1+2(T-1)\sigma^{2}\big)^{1/3}~~=~~\mathcal{B}_{T}^{1/3},

(97)

where step
①
uses
\mathbb{E}[\chi_{t}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t-1}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|{\bm{\epsilon}}_{t}^{-}\|^{2}\leq 2\sigma^{2}
. For
T=1
, Inequality (
97
) holds directly.

Bounding the composite residual.

We derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathcal{R}_{t+1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\textstyle\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})+\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\bigr)

(98)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})+\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t}\|~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|+\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|+\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle(1+L\gamma_{t})\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|+\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|~~\overset{\text{④}}{\leq}~~(1+L\theta)\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|+\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|,

where step
①
uses the global optimality of the proximal update that
\mathbf{r}_{t}-\mathbf{m}_{t}\in\partial h(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})
; step
②
uses
\mathbf{s}_{t}=\mathbf{m}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{t})
; step
③
uses
L
-smoothness of
f(\cdot)
; step
④
uses
\gamma_{t}=\theta\alpha_{t}^{1/4}\beta_{t}\leq\theta
.

Removing the adaptive weights.

We obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{M_{T}}]~~=~~\mathbb{E}\big[\sqrt{\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}M_{T}}\,\alpha_{T}^{-1/4}\big]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}M_{T}]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1/2}]}~~\leq~~\sqrt{C_{m}}\ell_{T}\mathcal{B}_{T}^{1/6},

(99)

where step
①
uses Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; step
②
uses Lemma
C.12
(b), and Inequality (
97
).

Another application of Cauchy–Schwarz and Lemma
C.11
(d) gives:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{S_{T}}]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{U_{T}}\,\alpha_{T}^{-1/4}]~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[U_{T}]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\alpha_{T}^{-1/2}]}~~\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}~~\sqrt{C_{u}\ell_{T}}\mathcal{B}_{T}^{1/6},

(100)

where step
①
uses
\sqrt{\alpha_{T}}S_{T}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}:=U_{T}
since
\alpha_{t}\geq\alpha_{T}
for
t\leq T
; step
②
uses Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; step
③
uses Inequality (
97
).

Establishing the noise-adaptive rate.

Let
C_{R}:=2(1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}+2\sqrt{C_{u}}
. Since
R
is uniform and independent of the algorithm, we derive:

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{R+1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{t+1}]~~\overset{\text{①}}{\leq}~~\frac{1+L\theta}{T}\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|+\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1+L\theta}{\sqrt{T}}\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{M_{T}}]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{S_{T}}]~~\overset{\text{②}}{\leq}~~\frac{(1+L\theta)\sqrt{C_{m}}\ell_{T}+\sqrt{C_{u}}\ell_{T}^{1/2}}{\sqrt{T}}\mathcal{B}_{T}^{1/6}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{③}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{C_{R}\ell_{T}}{2\sqrt{T}}(1+2T^{1/6}\sigma^{1/3})~~\leq~~\textstyle C_{R}\frac{\ell_{T}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{C_{R}\ell_{T}}{T^{1/3}}\sigma^{1/3},

where step
①
uses Inequality (
98
); step
②
uses Inequalities (
99
) and (
100
); step
③
uses
\mathcal{B}_{T}^{1/6}\leq 1+\bigl(2(T-1)\sigma^{2}\bigr)^{1/6}\leq 1+2^{1/6}T^{1/6}\sigma^{1/3}\leq 1+2T^{1/6}\sigma^{1/3}
.
∎

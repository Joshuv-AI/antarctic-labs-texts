A mixed eigenvalue problem on domains tending to infinity in several directions
Prosenjit Roy, Itai Shafrir

Abstract
The aim of this article is to analyze the asymptotic behaviour of the eigenvalues of elliptic operators in divergence form with mixed boundary type conditions for domains that become unbounded in several directions, while they stay bounded in some directions (cylindrical domains). The limiting behavior of such eigenvalues is shown to depend on an ensemble of eigenvalue problems defined on a domain that is unbounded only in one direction. The asymptotic behavior of the eigenfunctions are also discussed. This work is a continuation of the work done in [ 6 ] .

Introduction

Let
m\geq 2,\ p\geq 1
and
\omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
two bounded domains
with
C^{1}
boundary, such that
\omega_{1}
contains the origin. By a domain we mean
a nonempty open connected set.
For
\ell>0
consider the cylindrical domain
\Omega_{\ell}=\ell\omega_{1}\times\omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+p}
.
A generic point
x\in\Omega_{\ell}
is denoted by
x=(X,\xi)
where

X=(x_{1},x^{\prime})=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ \xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. We assume that the

(m+p)\times(m+p)
matrix

A=A(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}(\xi)&A_{12}(\xi)\\
A_{12}^{T}(\xi)&A_{22}(\xi)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}A_{11}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m},\;A_{12}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p},\\[2.0pt]
A_{22}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p},\end{array}

whose elements are measurable and bounded functions on
\omega_{2}
, is symmetric and uniformly elliptic, that is, there exists a constant
c_{A}>0
such that

(A(\xi)y)\cdot y\geq c_{A}\|y\|^{2},\text{ for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{m+p}\,,\text{ a.e. }\xi\in\omega_{2}.

(1)

We also assume that the matrix norms of the family of matrices
\{A(\xi)\}_{\xi\in\omega_{2}}
are uniformly bounded, that is

\|A(\xi)\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m+p}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|A(\xi)x\|}{\|x\|}\leq C_{A},\text{ a.e. }\xi\in\omega_{2}.

(2)

Let us write
\partial\Omega_{\ell}=\Gamma_{\ell}\cup\gamma_{\ell}
where

\Gamma_{\ell}=\partial(\ell\omega_{1})\times{}\omega_{2}\text{ and }\gamma_{\ell}=\ell\omega_{1}\times{}\partial\omega_{2}.

(3)

Denote by
\lambda_{\ell}^{k}
the
k
-th eigenvalue for the mixed
Neumann-Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -&\textrm{div}(A\nabla u)=\sigma u&\text{ in }\Omega_{\ell},\\
&u=0&\text{ on }\gamma_{\ell},\\
&(A\nabla u)\cdot\tilde{\nu}=0&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\ell}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

Here
\tilde{\nu}
denotes the outward unit normal to
\Gamma_{\ell}
.
When
k=1
we will denote for short

\lambda_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}^{1}
and let
u_{\ell}
denote the (unique)
positive normalized eigenfunction, i.e.,
u_{\ell}>0
and satisfies
\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u_{\ell}^{2}=1
. We have the following variational characterization of

\lambda_{\ell}
:

\lambda_{\ell}=\inf_{\left\{0\neq u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})\,|\,u=0\text{ on
}\gamma_{\ell},(A\nabla u)\cdot\tilde{\nu}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\ell}\right\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u}{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u^{2}}\,.

(5)

Our goal is to understand the asymptotic behavior of
\lambda_{\ell}

when the parameter
\ell
tends to infinity, which means that the
domains
\Omega_{\ell}
tend to become unbounded in the first
m

directions. For that matter the following eigenvalue problem turns out
to be relevant (as in
[
6
]
). Let
\mu_{1}
denote the first
eigenvalue and let
W
be the (unique)
positive normalized eigenfunction for the operator

-\operatorname{div}(A_{22}\nabla u)
, with Dirichlet boundary conditions, on the cross section
\omega_{2}
of

\Omega_{\ell}
, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -&\textrm{div}(A_{22}\nabla W)=\mu_{1}W\quad\text{ in }\omega_{2},\\
&W=0\text{ on }\partial\omega_{2},\\
&\int_{\omega_{2}}W^{2}=1.\end{aligned}\right.

(6)

Here
\operatorname{div}=\operatorname{div}_{\xi}
denotes
the divergence operator in the
\xi
variable. As in
[
6
]
, the problem (
6
) will play an important role
in the study of problem (
4
).

In
[
6
]
the authors studied the problem (
4
) for the
case where
m=1
and
k=1
in complete generality. It was found that the
limiting behaviour of
\lambda_{\ell}
is determined by a minimization
problem set on a semi-infinite cylinder and in particular the
presence of Neumann boundary conditions gives rise to a “gap
phenomenon” (
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}<\mu_{1}
in
the second part of Theorem
1
below) in the limiting behaviour of
\lambda_{\ell}
. More precisely their result reads as follow:

Theorem 1

[Chipot-Roy-Shafrir]

When
m=1
we have

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}=\min\left\{Z^{+\infty},Z^{-\infty}\right\},

where

Z^{\pm\infty}=\inf_{\{0\neq u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{\pm}\times\omega_{2})|u=0\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}_{\pm}\times\partial\omega_{2}\}}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\pm}\times\omega_{2}}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\pm}\times\omega_{2}}u^{2}}\,.

(7)

Furthermore, if
A_{12}\nabla_{\xi}W
does not equal to
0
a.e. in
\omega_{2}
then
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}<\mu_{1}
, while if
A_{12}\nabla_{\xi}W=0
a.e. in
\omega_{2}
then
\lambda_{\ell}=\mu_{1}
for all
\ell
.

This result stands in sharp contrast to the problem with full
Dirichlet boundary conditions, for which it was shown in
[
7
]
that all
the eigenvalues converge to
\mu_{1}
when
\ell
goes to infinity. We state their result as
we will use it later on.

Theorem 2

[Chipot-Rougirel]

One has for some constant
C>0
,

\mu_{1}\leq\sigma_{\ell}^{k}\leq\mu_{1}+\frac{C}{\ell^{2}},

where
\sigma_{\ell}^{k}
is the
k-th

eigenvalue of operator
-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u)
on
\Omega_{\ell}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Remark 3

In the special case
k=1
we have the well known variational
characterization

\sigma_{\ell}^{1}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\ell})}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u}{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u^{2}}\,.

(8)

The main aim of this article is to study the limiting behaviour of

\lambda_{\ell}^{k}
for the case
m>1
, or in other words, when the
cylinder becomes unbounded in more than one direction. In
particular, we will show that the limiting behaviour in this case is
determined by an appropriate minimization problem set on domain of the
type
(-\infty,0)\times\omega_{2}
. Note that the
domain

(-\infty,0)\times\omega_{2}
has

m-1
dimensions less than the domain
\Omega_{\ell}
on which the original problem
(
4
) is defined. This is clearly very helpful from the point of view of numerical
analysis.

To state our main theorem we will need to introduce a family of
eigenvalue problems, of the same type as the problem in
(
7
), each of them is associated with some direction

\nu\in S^{m-1}
. So for each
\nu\in S^{m-1}
let

Z^{\nu}=\inf_{\left\{0\neq u\in H^{1}((-\infty,0)\times\omega_{2})\ |\ u=0\ \textrm{on}\ (-\infty,0)\times\partial\omega_{2}\right\}}\frac{\int_{(-\infty,0)\times\omega_{2}}(A_{\nu}\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u}{\int_{(-\infty,0)\times\omega_{2}}u^{2}}\,,

(9)

where
A_{\nu}=A_{\nu}(\xi)
is the
(p+1)\times(p+1)
matrix given by

A_{\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}(A_{11}\nu)\cdot\nu&\nu^{T}A_{12}\\
(\nu^{T}A_{12})^{T}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(10)

It is easy to deduce from our assumption (
1
) that all the
matrices
\{A_{\nu}(\xi)\}_{\nu,\xi}
are uniformly elliptic, and the
same
c_{A}
as in (
1
) can be taken as
a common lower bound for the least eigenvalue.

Our main result is then

Theorem 4

[Main Result]

We have

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}=\inf_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}\,.

(11)

Furthermore, if

A_{12}\nabla_{\xi}W\not\equiv 0\ a.e.\ \textrm{in}\ \omega_{2}

(12)

then
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}<\mu_{1}
or else
\lambda_{\ell}=\mu_{1},
for all
\ell
.

Notice that Theorem
4
is a natural generalization of Theorem
1
since for
m=1
the relevant unit sphere is
S^{0}=\{-1,+1\}
.
In
[
6
]
, under a certain symmetry assumption on the matrix
A
, it was
also proved that
\lambda_{\ell}^{2}
has the same limit as

\lambda_{\ell}
, but no information was given on the third and higher
eigenvalues. The result of
[
6
]
, was generalized recently in

[
12
]
to
\lambda_{\ell}^{k}
for all
k
(under the same symmetry
assumptions as in
[
6
]
). It turns out that for
m\geq 2
the problem for the higher
eigenvalues is
simpler and we are able to prove in this case
that
\lambda_{\ell}^{k}
converges to the same limit as

\lambda_{\ell}
, for all
k
. This immediately leads to the existence
of a “gap phenomenon” for all
k
when (
12
) holds.

Theorem 5

[The Case of Higher Order Eigenvalues]

We have

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{k}=\inf_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}\,,\text{ for all }k>1.

(13)

In particular, when (
12
) holds, the value in (
13
) is
strictly lower than
\mu_{1}
.

The paper is arranged as follows. In the next section we
introduce various notation, auxiliary spaces and some known facts
that will be used throughout this paper. In
Section
3
we provide the upper bound estimate for

\lambda_{\ell}
by proving Theorem

7
. The lower bound estimate for
\lambda_{\ell}
is
the objective of Section
4
. Its proof in Theorem
8
provides
the necessary ingredient to conclude the proof of Theorem
4
. In the
last section, Section
5
, we
will give the proof of Theorem
5
, concerning the higher
order eigenvalues.

Various other problems of such type (
\ell\rightarrow\infty
) had
been studied in the past. Beside the work mentioned above, it
includes variational problem, second order elliptic equations, Stokes
equation, problems involving fractional Laplacian, variational
inequalities and many others. To obtain more idea about of this type
of works we refer to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
1
,
11
,
13
,
14
]
and the references mentioned there
in. In most cases, the limiting behaviour of the parameter involved
in such problems finds its connection with an appropriate problem set
on the cross section of the cylinder
\omega_{2}
. We again emphasize
that in our case the problem becomes independent of the associated
problem (
6
) on the cross section. Concerning results of the
type considered here, it is worth mentioning the recent works

[
10
,
12
]
that generalize the results of
[
6
]

to

-Laplacian type operators, with

. Most of our techniques are variational, so it seems plausible that a generalization to systems of most or all of the results could be possible, but we have not pursued this issue in the present manuscript.

3
The upper bound construction

For each
\nu\in S^{m-1}
let the matrix
A_{\nu}

be given by (
10
). We consider on
(-\ell,0)\times\omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+p}
the first eigenvalue and the corresponding (unique)
normalized positive
eigenfunction, for the following
problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -&\textrm{div}(A_{\nu}\nabla v_{\ell}^{\nu})=Z_{\ell}^{\nu}v_{\ell}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }(-\ell,0)\times\omega_{2},\\
&v_{\ell}^{\nu}=0\quad\text{ on }\left((-\ell,0)\times\partial\omega_{2}\right)\cup\left(\{-\ell\}\times\omega_{2}\right),\\
&(A_{\nu}\nabla v_{\ell}^{\nu})\cdot{\bf e_{1}}=0\quad\text{ on }\{0\}\times\omega_{2},\\
&v_{\ell}^{\nu}>0\text{ in }(-\ell,0)\times\omega_{2}\text{ and }\int_{(-\ell,0)\times\omega_{2}}|v_{\ell}^{\nu}|^{2}=1.\end{aligned}\right.

(24)

Here
{\bf e_{1}}
denotes the unit vector in the direction of the

x_{1}
-coordinate.

We recall, see
[
6
, Lemma 5.1 and Lemma 5.2]

Z^{\nu}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}Z_{\ell}^{\nu}\leq\mu_{1}\,.

(25)

Theorem 7

[Upper Bound]

We have

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}\leq\inf_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in S^{m-1}
be fixed. Since we assumed that
\partial\omega_{1}

is of class
C^{1}
there exists a point
P_{0}\in\partial\omega_{1}
such that
the outward unit normal at
P_{0}
is
\nu
.

In order to change variables in such a way that
\nu
will be
transformed
to
{\bf e}_{1}
, we first choose
m-1
vectors
p_{2},\ p_{3},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
that
together with
\nu
form an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let
B
denote the orthogonal matrix whose rows are

\nu,p_{2},\ldots,p_{m}
. Therefore its transpose
B^{T}
is given by

B^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\nu^{T}&p_{2}^{T}&.&.&.&p_{m}^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

We change variables from
x=(X,\xi)
to

by setting
Y=BX
. Let
v(Y,\xi)=u(X,\xi)
and denote
Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})=(y_{1},y^{\prime})
. An easy computation gives that

\nabla_{X}u=B^{T}\nabla_{Y}v.

For ease of notation we will continue to denote by

\Omega_{\ell}
and
\omega_{1}
the same domains as above also when they are represented in the new variables. A direct
computation yields

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}(A\nabla_{x}u)\cdot\nabla_{x}u=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}(A^{B}\nabla_{y}v)\cdot\nabla_{y}v\ \text{ and }\ \int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u^{2}=\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}v^{2},

(26)

where

A^{B}=\begin{pmatrix}BA_{11}B^{T}&BA_{12}\\
\left(BA_{12}\right)^{T}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\Omega_{\ell,K}=\Omega_{\ell,K}^{P_{0}}
be as in
Section
2
, but defined using the new variables, i.e.,

\Omega_{\ell,K}:=\left\{Y\in\ell\omega_{1}\ \big|\ f_{\ell}(y^{\prime})-K<y_{1}<f_{\ell}(y^{\prime}),\ y^{\prime}\in{\widetilde{B}}_{K}(0)\right\}\times\omega_{2}\,.

(27)

Fix any
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
with
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset(-1/\sqrt{m-1},1/\sqrt{m-1})

satisfying

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi^{2}(t)\,dt=1\,.

(28)

Set

. For any
K>0
let

\Phi_{K}(t)=(1/\sqrt{K})\Phi(t/K)
. Then,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi_{K}^{2}=1\text{ and }\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Phi^{\prime}_{K}|^{2}=\frac{c_{0}}{K^{2}}.

(29)

To simplify notations we will
denote in the sequel,

\phi_{j}(y_{j})=\Phi_{K}(y_{j})\,,\quad j=2,\ldots,m\,.

Define the function

q_{\ell}(Y,\xi)=\begin{cases}v_{K}^{\nu}(y_{1}-f_{\ell}(y^{\prime}),\ \xi)\mathop{\prod}\limits_{j=2}^{m}\phi_{j}(y_{j})&\text{
on }\ \Omega_{\ell,K},\\
0&\text{ on }\ \Omega_{\ell}\setminus\Omega_{\ell,K},\end{cases}

with

given by (
24
) (for
\ell=K
). Above we used the fact that

4
The Lower bound

The following theorem is the main result of this section:

Theorem 8

[Lower bound]
Assume that
A_{12}\nabla_{\xi}W\not\equiv 0\ a.e.\ \textrm{in}\ \omega_{2}

\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}\geq\inf_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}.

Remark 9

Thanks to Lemma

\inf_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}=\min_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}

The proof of Theorem

Lemma 10

Let
\nu\in S^{m-1}

Without loss of generality we assume again that

0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases},

for any

as given by (

(

For the reverse inequality, we assume as above w.l.o.g. that

\penalty\ \nu={\bf e}_{1}

implies, thanks to
[

is attained by some function

that satisfies the

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\operatorname{div}(A_{\nu}\nabla v^{\nu})&=Z^{\nu}v^{\nu}&\text{ on }(-\infty,0)\times\omega_{2},\\

(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u-(A\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\big(\frac{u^{2}}{v}\big)=A\big(\nabla u-\frac{u}{v}\nabla v\big)\cdot\big(\nabla u-\frac{u}{v}\nabla v\big)\geq 0\,.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\nu)}A\big(\nabla u-\frac{u}{\tilde{v}^{\nu}}\nabla\tilde{v}^{\nu}\big)\cdot\big(\nabla u-\frac{u}{\tilde{v}^{\nu}}\nabla\tilde{v}^{\nu}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\nu)}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u-(A\nabla\tilde{v}^{\nu})\cdot\nabla\big(\frac{u^{2}}{\tilde{v}^{\nu}}\big)

where

Lemma 11

For each

we may choose
N_{\ell}

\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\in\partial\omega_{1}
with
N_{\ell}\leq C\ell^{m-1-\beta}
such that

\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\Omega_{\ell-\ell^{\beta}}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\Omega_{\ell,\ell^{\beta}}^{P_{i}}\,.

(67)

Combining (
62
) with (
67
) yields

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}\setminus\Omega_{\ell-\ell^{\beta}}}u_{\ell}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\ell}}\int_{\Omega_{\ell,\ell^{\beta}}^{P_{i}}}u_{\ell}^{2}\leq C\ell^{m-1-\beta}e^{[\ell^{\beta}]\log\alpha},

and (
61
) follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Assume by contradiction that

\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\lambda_{\ell}<\min_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}.

(68)

The assumption

implies that
there exists
\nu_{0}\in S^{m-1}

with

for the operator associated with the matrix

(see (

Applying Lemma
10
we deduce from (
68
) that also

\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\lambda_{\ell}<\min_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}{\widetilde{Z}}^{\nu}.

(69)

By Lemma

we get for
k

\int_{\Omega_{\ell_{k}}\setminus\Omega_{\ell_{k}-\ell_{k}^{\beta}}}u_{\ell_{k}}^{2}<\frac{1}{4}.

(70)

But by Lemma

we also have, for

\int_{\Omega_{\ell_{k}-\ell_{k}^{\beta}}}u_{\ell_{k}}^{2}<\frac{1}{4}.

(71)

Combining (

) with (

) we get for large

∎

Assume first that (

5
Limit of the Higher Order Eigenvalues

This short section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5
. Recall the
Rayleigh quotient characterization of
\lambda_{\ell}^{k}
for any
k\geq 2
:

\lambda_{\ell}^{k}=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}(A\nabla u)\nabla u}{\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}u^{2}}\,:\,0\neq u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\ell}),u=0\text{ on }\gamma_{\ell},\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}uu_{\ell}^{i}=0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq k-1\right\},

(73)

where
u_{\ell}^{i}
denotes an eigenfunction in (
4
) corresponding to
\sigma=\lambda_{\ell}^{i}
.
Roughly
speaking, the case
m\geq 2
is easier than the case
m=1
since we have

“more space”
to carry out a construction of competitors for
the infimum in (
73
).

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Since
\lambda_{\ell}^{k}\geq\lambda_{\ell}^{1}=\lambda_{\ell}
for all
k
,
the lower bound
\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{k}\geq\min_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}
follows from Theorem
4
.
It remains to prove the other inequality, namely, that for every

k\geq 2
there holds

\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{k}\leq\min_{\nu\in S^{m-1}}Z^{\nu}\,.

(74)

Suppose that
k
distincts
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}\in S^{m-1}
have been fixed. By
the proof of Theorem
7
we can construct
functions
w_{\ell}^{j}\in V(\Omega_{\ell})
, for
j=1,\ldots,k
,
with

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}|w_{\ell}^{j}|^{2}=1\ \textrm{and}\ \limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla w_{\ell}^{j}\cdot\nabla w_{\ell}^{j}\leq Z^{\nu_{j}}.

(75)

Basically, for each
j
, as
\ell\to\infty
the functions
w_{\ell}^{j}
concentrate near the point
\ell P_{0}^{j}\in\partial\Omega_{\ell}
, where
P_{0}^{j}\in\partial\omega_{1}\times\omega_{2}
is such that the
\nu_{j}
is the outward unit normal at this point.

Next define
W_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{\ell}^{j}w_{\ell}^{j}\in V(\Omega_{\ell})
where
\{\alpha_{\ell}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
are to be chosen appropriately.
In order to use
W_{\ell}
as a competitor in (
73
) we need to
choose
\{\alpha_{\ell}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}

so that the following equalities hold:

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}W_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{j}=0,\quad 1\leq j\leq k-1.

(76)

A nontrivial choice for
\{\alpha_{\ell}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
is possible
because (
76
) induces a linear system of
k-1

equations with
k
unknowns.

For any given
\varepsilon>0
we may assume
\ell
is large enough so
that

\int_{\Omega_{\ell}}A\nabla w_{\ell}^{j}\cdot\nabla w_{\ell}^{j}\leq Z^{\nu_{j}}+\varepsilon,\quad 1\leq j\leq k.

(77)

Plugging the resulting
W_{\ell}
in
(
73
) yields

\lambda_{\ell}^{k}\leq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\alpha_{\ell}^{j})^{2}Z^{\nu_{j}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\alpha_{\ell}^{j})^{2}}+\varepsilon\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\{Z^{\nu_{j}}\}+\varepsilon.

(78)

Since the map
\nu\mapsto Z^{\nu}
is
continuous on
S^{m-1}
by Lemma
6
we can now conclude using (
78
). Indeed,
for any
\nu\in S^{m-1}
we can choose distinct

\{\nu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}\subset S^{m-1}
(all of them
“close to
\nu
”
) such that

|Z^{\nu_{j}}-Z^{\nu}|<\varepsilon,\quad 1\leq j\leq k..

(79)

Using this choice of
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
in the above
construction yields, combining (
79
) and (
78
),

\lambda_{\ell}^{k}\leq Z^{\nu}+2\varepsilon.

Since this can be done for any
\nu\in S^{m-1}
the desired result
(
74
) follows.
∎

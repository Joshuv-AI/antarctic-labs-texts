Evidence for the collective nature of radial flow in Pb+Pb collisions with the ATLAS detector


Abstract
Anisotropic flow and radial flow are two key probes of the expansion dynamics and properties of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP). While anisotropic flow has been extensively studied, radial flow, which governs the system’s radial expansion, has received less attention. Notably, direct experimental evidence for the global and collective nature of radial flow fluctuations has been lacking. This Letter presents the first measurement of transverse momentum ( p T p_{\mathrm{T}} ) dependence of radial flow fluctuations ( v 0 ​ ( p T ) v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}}) ) over 0.5 < p T < 10 0.5<p_{\mathrm{T}}<10 GeV and demonstrates its collective nature using a two-particle correlation method in Pb+Pb collisions at s NN = 5.02 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02 TeV. The data reveal three key features supporting the collective nature of radial flow : long-range correlation in pseudorapidity, factorization in p T p_{\mathrm{T}} , and centrality-independent shape in p T p_{\mathrm{T}} . The comparison with a hydrodynamic model demonstrates the sensitivity of v 0 ​ ( p T ) v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}}) to bulk viscosity, a crucial transport property of the QGP. These findings establish a new, powerful tool for probing collective dynamics and properties of the QGP.

Introduction.
Heavy-ion collisions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) and the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) provide unique conditions for creating and studying the quark–gluon plasma (QGP). The spacetime evolution of the QGP is well described by relativistic hydrodynamics
[
1
,
2
]
, where the system, driven by pressure gradients, expands collectively and cools until hadronization. This gradient-driven expansion turns geometric anisotropies and size variations in the initial state into two distinct final-state collective phenomena: anisotropic flow (
v_{n}
,
n=1,2,\cdots
) and radial flow (
v_{0}
), respectively. Anisotropic flow quantifies azimuthal momentum anisotropy, while radial flow characterizes the radial boost of the system, which influences the average transverse momentum of particles
[p_{\text{T}}]
in each event
[
3
]
.

Extensive measurements of anisotropic flow, particularly differential studies of
v_{n}(p_{\text{T}})
and their event-by-event (EbE) fluctuations
[
4
,
5
,

,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, have provided crucial insights into the initial conditions and transport properties of the QGP. In contrast, experimental studies of radial flow have been more limited. Traditional approaches focus on particle spectra and moments of the
[p_{\text{T}}]
distribution over event ensembles, such as the mean
\left\langle[p_{\text{T}}]\right\rangle

[
14
]
and the scaled variance
v_{0}=\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta[p_{\text{T}}])^{2}\right\rangle}/\left\langle[p_{\text{T}}]\right\rangle

[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, where
\delta[p_{\text{T}}]=[p_{\text{T}}]-\left\langle[p_{\text{T}}]\right\rangle
. While the
v_{0}
observable characterizes global EbE radial flow fluctuations
[
20
,
21
]
and scales with the square root of charged-particle multiplicity
[
18
]
, it provides only the
p_{\text{T}}
-integral information about the radial flow.

Anisotropic and radial flow are thought to originate from single-particle distributions and hence are expected to exhibit long-range correlations in pseudorapidity (
\eta
)
[
3
,
22
]
. For anisotropic flow, these two features have been verified by the approximate factorization of the coefficients of two-particle azimuthal correlation into single-particle coefficients:
V_{n\Delta}(p_{\mathrm{T1}},p_{\mathrm{T2}})\approx v_{n}(p_{\mathrm{T1}})v_{n}(p_{\mathrm{T2}})

[
8
,
22
]
and
V_{n\Delta}(\eta_{\mathrm{1}},\eta_{\mathrm{2}})\approx v_{n}(\eta_{\mathrm{1}})v_{n}(\eta_{\mathrm{2}})

[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. Furthermore,
v_{n}(p_{\text{T}})
divided by the integrated flow
v_{n}
exhibits a near-universal behavior across centralities
[
26
]
. This universality arises because the magnitude of
v_{n}(p_{\text{T}})
scales with initial state eccentricity, while its shape, characterized by
k_{n}(p_{\text{T}})=v_{n}(p_{\text{T}})/v_{n}

[
27

. While this factorization and centrality-independent scaling hold at

GeV in central to midcentral Pb+Pb collisions, deviations are observed in peripheral collisions or at higher
p_{\text{T}}
, attributable to non-flow correlations such as resonance decays or jet fragmentation. These direct correlation measurements have established the multi-particle collective nature of anisotropic flow.

In contrast to anisotropic flow, the collective nature of radial flow has traditionally been inferred from model studies of identified particle
p_{\text{T}}
spectra and their
[p_{\text{T}}]
and
v_{0}

[
28
,
14
]
. These model-dependent approaches, incorporating radial expansion, have enabled acecss to average radial flow velocities and temperatures
[
29
]
. Yet, they do not directly probe the many-body nature of the radial flow, as has been achieved for anisotropic flow. A new observable,
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
, was proposed recently
[
30
]
to fill this gap. Unlike anisotropic flow, which is more sensitive to shear viscosity,
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
is predicted to be more sensitive to bulk viscosity
[
30
,
31
]
, which remains less well constrained. Precision measurements of
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
, including factorization and scaling checks as well as particle species dependence, thus offer valuable opportunities to constrain the properties of the QGP
[
32
,
33
]
and possibly disentangle contributions from flow velocity fluctuations and thermal fluctuations
[
34
]
.

This Letter presents measurements of

ATLAS detector and data selection.
The measurements are performed using the ATLAS inner detector (ID), forward calorimeter (FCal), and zero-degree calorimeters (ZDCs) along with the trigger and data acquisition systems
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
. The ID detects charged particles within
|\eta|<2.5

ATLAS uses a right-handed coordinate system with its origin at the nominal interaction point in the center of the detector and the z-axis along the beam pipe. The x-axis points from the IP to the center of the LHC ring, and the y-axis points upward. Cylindrical coordinates (r,
\phi
) are used in the transverse plane,
\phi
being the azimuthal angle around the beam pipe. The pseudorapidity is defined in terms of the polar angle
\theta
as
\eta
=-
\ln{\tan{\theta/2}}
.
using a combination of silicon pixel and microstrip detectors, along with a straw-tube transition-radiation tracker, all immersed in a 2 T axial magnetic field
[
37
]
. The FCal consists of three sampling layers, covering
3.2<|\eta|<4.9
. The ZDCs are positioned at
\pm 140\mathrm{\penalty\ m}
from the interaction point and detect neutrons with
|\eta|>8.3
. The ATLAS trigger system
[
38
]
consists of a hardware-based level-one (L1) and software-based high-level triggers. A software suite
[
39
]
is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction and analysis of real and simulated data, in the detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition systems.

Pb+Pb events are selected by requiring the total transverse energy deposited in calorimeters over
|\eta|<4.9
at L1 to exceed 50 GeV. Additionally, dedicated central collision triggers enhance the number of events with large FCal transverse energy
[
13
]
.

Charged-particle tracks are reconstructed from hits in the ID using a procedure optimized for heavy-ion collisions
[
40
]
. The distance of the closest approach of the track to the primary vertex must be within 1.25 mm in both the transverse and longitudinal directions. More details of the track selection are found in Ref.
[
26
]
. Tracks used in this analysis have
0.5<p_{\text{T}}<10
GeV and
|\eta|<2.5
. This
p_{\text{T}}
range spans the regime predominantly influenced by radial flow as well as the transition region where jet-quenching effects become significant. The total number of such tracks is denoted by
N_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{rec}}
. Events containing multiple inelastic collisions (pileup) are suppressed by exploiting the correlation between
N_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{rec}}
and the transverse energy measured in the FCal,
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
, as well as between the energy deposited in the ZDCs and
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
. The residual pileup probability is 0.17% among all selected events, but is smaller than 0.01% in central collisions
[
41
,
18
]
.

Events are categorized into centrality ranges using a parametrization of the
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
distribution
[
13
]
based on a Glauber model
[
42
]
, starting at 0% for the most central collisions with the highest
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{FCal}}
value and ending at 70%. Events in this centrality range are fully efficient against the L1 event selection criteria.

Analysis.
The track reconstruction efficiency,
\epsilon\left(p_{\text{T}},\eta,N_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{rec}}\right)
, is evaluated using Monte Carlo (MC) simulated events for Pb+Pb collisions generated with
Hijing

[
43
]
and charged particles definition according to Ref.
[
40
]
. The detector response is simulated using
Geant4

[
44
,
45
]
, and events are reconstructed with the same algorithms as used for the data. The HIJING spectra have been reweighted to match the data. In central Pb+Pb collisions, the efficiency ranges from 71% at
\eta\approx 0
to about 40% for
|\eta|>2
at
p_{\text{T}}>0.8
GeV, and it decreases by
12\%
from 0.8 to 0.5 GeV in
p_{\text{T}}
. In peripheral collisions, the efficiency is up to 4% higher. The rate of falsely reconstructed (“fake”) tracks,
f\left(p_{\text{T}},\eta,N_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{rec}}\right)

p_{\text{T}}<1
GeV in central collisions, ranging from 2% for
|\eta|<1
to 8% at larger
|\eta|
. The fake-track rate drops rapidly for higher
p_{\text{T}}
and more peripheral collisions.

The observables in this analysis are obtained using a two-subevent method
[
46
,
47
]
, which takes particles from two
\eta

a
and
b

-2.5<\eta_{a}<-\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{gap}}}{2}

\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{gap}}}{2}<\eta_{b}<2.5

[

48

to correct for reconstruction efficiency
\epsilon_{i}

w_{i}\equiv\left(1-f_{i}\right)/\epsilon_{i}

GeV and

For each collision subevent, the following quantities are calculated:
\left[p_{\text{T}}\right]=\Sigma_{i}w_{i}p_{\mathrm{T},i}/\Sigma_{i}w_{i}
,
\delta[p_{\text{T}}]=\Sigma_{i}w_{i}\delta p_{\mathrm{T},i}/\Sigma_{i}w_{i}

\delta p_{\mathrm{T},i}\equiv

\Sigma_{i}^{p_{\text{T}}}
represents the sum over all tracks in a narrow
p_{\text{T}}
range. The two-particle observables are calculated in narrow

\left\langle(\delta[p_{\text{T}}])^{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\delta[p_{\text{T}}]_{a}\delta[p_{\text{T}}]_{b}\right\rangle

\left\langle\delta n(p_{\text{T}})\delta[p_{\text{T}}]\right\rangle\!=\!\frac{1}{2}\!\left\langle\delta n_{a}(p_{\text{T}})\delta[p_{\text{T}}]_{b}\!+\!\delta n_{b}(p_{\text{T}})\delta[p_{\text{T}}]_{a}\right\rangle

ranges, 0.5–2, 0.5–5, and 1–5 GeV; according to Eq. (
1

can only be calculated in fairly wide

Systematic uncertainties stem from track selection, reconstruction efficiency, residual pileup, centrality definition, and MC closure test of the analysis procedure. These uncertainties obtained in
0.5<p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ref}}<2
GeV and
\eta_{\mathrm{gap}}=1

Track selection uncertainties are assessed by comparing nominal results with those obtained under looser or stricter criteria. This results in a deviation of 0.5–5% for
v_{0}
, and 1–8% at low
p_{\text{T}}
and 0.5–1.5% at high
p_{\text{T}}

v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})

1

and 2–3% at low

Residual pileup effects are estimated by adjusting the pileup rejection criteria, leading to uncertainties less than 0.5% for all observables. Centrality definition uncertainties, assessed by varying the Glauber model parameters, are relevant only when results are presented in centrality ranges and are below 3% for all observables.

The
Hijing

Summary.
This Letter reports the first measurement of the
p_{\text{T}}
-differential radial flow fluctuations
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
using charged particle tracks over the range
0.5<p_{\text{T}}<10
GeV in Pb+Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 5.02 TeV using the ATLAS detector at the LHC. This observable is extracted from the correlations between each event’s
p_{\text{T}}
-differential spectrum and its average transverse momentum
[p_{\text{T}}]
in separate subevents. A characteristic rise of
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
up to 3–4 GeV, a decrease at higher
p_{\text{T}}
, and a sign change near 1 GeV are observed. This rise and fall behavior could be associated with an interplay between collective flow, changes in baryon-meson composition, and jet quenching, although a quantitative understanding of such interplay requires a realistic model calculation. The
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
is nearly independent of the pseudorapidity gap between subevents, supporting the long-range nature of the radial flow. These results are confirmed by a similar study from the ALICE Collaboration
[
52
]
that appeared after submission of this Letter for publication. A factorization test shows that
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
for
p_{\text{T}}\lesssim 3
GeV is nearly independent of the
p_{\text{T}}
range used to calculate
[p_{\text{T}}]
, establishing the collective nature of the radial flow. Notably, the shape of
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
for
p_{\text{T}}\lesssim 3
GeV is nearly centrality-independent, implying a universal hydrodynamic response to radial expansion. Taken together, these results confirm three hallmarks of collectivity – long-range correlation, factorization, and centrality-independent shape – now demonstrated for radial flow. Comparisons with a hydrodynamic model reveal that
v_{0}(p_{\text{T}})
is sensitive to bulk viscosity, highlighting its potential for quantitative constraints on QGP transport properties.

A p p -adic interpolation of the Cogdell lift
Francesco Maria Iudica

Abstract
. In this paper we obtain several results related to the p p -adic interpolation of the classical Cogdell lift, mapping special cycles on Picard modular surfaces to elliptic modular forms. The results have a three-fold nature: in the first part of the paper, we p p -adically interpolate the adjoint Kudla lift, exploiting the previously constructed Λ \Lambda -adic Kudla lift. In the second part, we construct higher weight cycles in Kuga-Sato varieties attached to Picard modular surfaces, and show modularity of the generating series of these cycles, thus obtaining a higher weight analogue of the Cogdell lift. Finally, we apply the formalism introduced by Loeffler to construct p p -adic analytic cohomology classes of special cycles, whose generating series is proved to be a Hida family interpolating the Cogdell lifts in the weight and level variables.

Introduction

This article deals with the
p
-adic interpolation of the classical Cogdell lift introduced in
[
Cog85
]
, where the author constructs elliptic modular forms of weight
3
and Nebentypus, whose Fourier coefficients are intersection multiplicities of special cycles on Picard modular surfaces. As a first instance of the

-adic variation of the above lift, in

\Lambda

(\GU(1,1),\GU(2,1)).

In the present paper, we first apply the results in
loc. cit.
to study the

-adic variation of the adjoint Kudla lift, obtained from the same theta kernel, and which takes holomorphic Picard modular forms to elliptic modular forms.

As it is known from classical results of Kudla-Millson

[

In the first part of the paper, we achieve a

In the second part, we construct higher weight cycles

For

\tilde{q}\in C^{\prime}_{n}\cap S^{\prime}_{\sigma}

H\tilde{q}\subset H(C^{\prime}_{n}\cap S^{\prime}_{\sigma})
. But by definition, we have

g\cdot C^{\prime}_{n}=g\sum_{v\in\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash L(n)}C^{\prime}_{v}=\sum_{v\in\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash L(n)}g\cdot C^{\prime}_{v}=\sum_{v\in\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash L(n)}C^{\prime}_{gv}=C^{\prime}_{n},

as multiplication by elements
g\in H=\overline{\Gamma}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}
induces a permutation on the set
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash L(n)
. Indeed, the cycles
C_{n}
are fixed by the action of the full
\Gamma(L)
.

Now we apply Lemma 6.2 in
[
Cog85
]
, which gives

(C_{n},S_{\sigma})=|H|^{-1}\cdot n\cdot N(\mathfrak{N}_{0})\cdot|D|\sum_{g\in H/H_{\sigma}}|L(n)\cap L_{g\cdot\sigma}|.

However, the sets
L(n)\cap L_{g\cdot\sigma}
all have cardinality
|L(n)\cap L_{\sigma}|
, as multiplication by elements in
\Gamma\subset\SU(2,1)
does not affect the norm of vectors. Hence we get

\sum_{g\in H/H_{\sigma}}|L(n)\cap L_{g\cdot\sigma}|=|H|\cdot|H_{\sigma}|^{-1}\cdot|L(n)\cap L_{\sigma}|

and the result.
∎

Remark 1
.

The right hand side of the formula in the above lemma seems to depend on the principal subgroup we chose to work with, as
|H_{\sigma}|
does. However, the quantity
N(\mathfrak{N}_{0})\cdot|H_{\sigma}|^{-1}
does not. Indeed, using
[
Cog85
, Lemma 2.3 ]
, we see that for any principal subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\Gamma(\mathfrak{M})
, we have

|\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{v_{1}}|=[\mathfrak{N}_{0}:\mathfrak{M}_{0}].

Now, using
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
instead of
\Gamma^{\prime}
in the above proof, we get

(C_{n},S_{\sigma})=n\cdot|D|\cdot N(\mathfrak{M}_{0})\cdot|\overline{\Gamma}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{v_{1}}|^{-1}\cdot|L(n)\cap L_{\sigma}|,

but

|\overline{\Gamma}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{v_{1}}|=|\overline{\Gamma}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{v_{1}}|\cdot|\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{v_{1}}|=|H_{\sigma}|\cdot|\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{v_{1}}|,

whence

N(\mathfrak{M}_{0})\cdot|\overline{\Gamma}_{v_{1}}/\overline{\Gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{v_{1}}|^{-1}=N(\mathfrak{M}_{0})\cdot|H_{\sigma}|^{-1}\cdot[\mathfrak{N}_{0}:\mathfrak{M}_{0}]^{-1}=N(\mathfrak{N}_{0})\cdot|H_{\sigma}|^{-1}.

For simplicity and to work in the same setting as Cogdell, for the next results in this section we suppose that
L=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{3}
. In the general case, the modular forms we obtain have level depending on the discriminant of
L
, see for example
[

.

Theorem 3

(Cogdell)
.

For every positive vector
v\in L
, the function

\Phi_{v}(z)=\frac{1}{2}e(\Gamma_{v}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{v})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(C_{v},C_{n}^{c})\;q^{n},\;\;\;\;\;q=e^{2\pi iz},

defines a modular form on
\mathbb{H}
of weight

and character
\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(D)
.

While Cogdell’s result only deals with a lift of an irreducible cycle

, which, roughly speaking, is an embedded modular or Shimura curve in the Picard modular surface, we shall extend this to the full
\h_{2}(S,\mathbb{C})
, obtaining a result analogous to
[

, Theorem 3.1]
:

Theorem 4
.

For each cohomology class
C
in the subspace of
\h_{2}(S^{\text{res}},\mathbb{Q})
generated by the

, the function

\Phi_{C}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 0}(C,C_{n}^{c})q^{n}

is a modular form of weight
3

\Gamma_{0}(D)
and Nebentypus
\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
. It is non-cuspidal for

, and a cusp form for

orthogonal to
C_{0}^{c}
. The map
C\mapsto\Phi_{C}
from the space generated by the

to

Proof.

The proof can be carried out exactly in the same way as in
[
HZ76
, p. 107]
because the same general arguments apply, however we recall here the key ideas:
first of all remark that this result is in accordance with the results in
[
Cog85
]
because
(C,C_{0}^{c})=\frac{1}{2}e(C)
, where

is the Euler number of

, cf.
[
K78
, p. 195]
. Extending by linearity the result of Cogdell on the space generated by the

for

, we see that we are only left to show that
\Phi_{C_{0}^{c}}
is a modular form. However this is an immediate consequence of
[

has compact support.

It is also clear that if we take

will be a cusp form, as its constant term is
(C,C_{0}^{c})=0
. On the contrary,
\Phi_{C_{0}^{c}}
is never cuspidal, as the constant term will be a multiple of the volume of the Picard modular surface. Finally, the map

∎

Remark 2
.

The injectivity of the above mapping is equivalent to the fact that

3.
The adjoint Kudla lift

We turn to the study of the adjoint lift
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}
of
\mathcal{L}
, constructed from the same theta kernel
\Theta
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)=(F,\overline{\Theta}),\;\;\;\forall F\in\mathscr{S}(k,\Gamma),

whereas

\mathcal{L}(f)=(f,\Theta),\;\;\;\forall f\in S_{k-1}(\Gamma^{0},\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}}).

In particular, we describe the composition
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\circ\mathcal{L}
. For a fixed even weight
k\geq 4
and
\Gamma^{0}\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
a congruence subgroup, we write
\mathcal{L}_{k}:S_{k-1}(\Gamma^{0},\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathscr{S}(k,\Gamma)
, for the Kudla lift.

We wish to relate
\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\dagger}
to the geometric lift

\displaystyle\h_{1,1}(S,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\to M_{3}(\Gamma_{0}(D),\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}})

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 0}(C,C_{n}^{c})q^{n},

which has been studied in
[
Cog85
]
, by means of the theory developed in
[
KM90
]
and in
[
TW83
]
.

Technically speaking, the definitions of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}
break down for weight
k<6
, for convergence reasons, but in
[
TW83
]
it is showed that by analytic continuation the same lifts can be defined for
k=4
, corresponding to weight
3
elliptic cusp forms.

On the ball model of the symmetric space associated to
\GU(2,1)
, the Kähler metric
\mu_{Hyp}
can be expressed by the following formula:

\mu_{Hyp}(z):=2\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}.

We let
d\mu_{Hyp}^{\text{vol}}
denote the associated volume form, cf.
[
ABR23
, p. 4]
. In
loc. cit.
the Petersson inner product of two Picard cusp forms
F,G\in\mathscr{S}(k,\Gamma)
, is defined by the formula:

(F,G):=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}(1-|z|^{2})^{k}F(z)\overline{G(z)}d\mu_{Hyp}^{\text{vol}}(z),

where
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
denotes a fundamental domain of
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}_{2}
.

3.1.
The analogue of a result of Zagier and Oda

For
F\in\mathscr{S}(k,\Gamma)
, we want to compute the adjoint Kudla lift of

:

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)=(F,\overline{\Theta}),

where
\Theta
is the theta kernel of the Kudla lift used in
[
Iu24
, p. 13]
.

Proposition 1
.

We have

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)(z)=c_{k}(L)\sum_{v\in L^{+}}\Big(\int_{C_{v}}F(\mathfrak{z})(v,\underline{\mathfrak{z}})^{k}d\mu_{v}(\mathfrak{z})\Big)q^{(v,v)},

where
d\mu_{v}
denotes the pull-back of the measure
d\mu_{Hyp}^{\text{vol}}
along
\mathfrak{X}_{v}\to\mathfrak{X}
, and
c_{k}(L)\in\mathbb{C}
is a constant independent of
F
.

Proof.

Write

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)(z)=\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}\Theta(z,g)\varphi(g)dg,\quad\text{with }\varphi(g):=j(g,\mathfrak{z}_{0})^{-k}F(g\mathfrak{z}_{0}).

Then

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)(z)=\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}y^{2}\sum_{v\in L}f(z,g^{-1}v)\varphi(g)dg.

Since
F
is a cusp form, this integral is absolutely convergent, and we can interchange integration and summation. For each
v\in V
, there exists a Haar measure
d\mu_{v}^{1}(h)
on
G_{v}
, and a measure
d\mu_{v}^{2}(g)
on
G_{v}\backslash G

\int_{G}\psi(g)dg=\int_{G_{v}\backslash G}\Big(\int_{G_{v}}\psi(hg)d\mu_{v}^{1}(h)\Big)d\mu_{v}^{2}(g),

for any integrable function
\psi
on
G
, see
[
O77
, p. 108]
. Then we have

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)(z)=\sum_{v\in L}\int_{G_{v}\backslash G}y^{2}f(z,g^{-1}v)\Big(\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash G_{v}}\varphi(hg)d\mu_{v}^{1}(h)\Big)d\mu_{v}^{2}(g)

We turn then to the evaluation of the inner integrals. First of all, the terms corresponding to
v
with
(v,v)\leq 0
vanish because
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(F)
is holomorphic and cuspidal, see
[
K79
, p. 13]
.
Define

b(g,v)=(v,\underline{g\mathfrak{z}_{0}}).

Then, for any element

j(hg,\mathfrak{z}_{0})^{-k}=\frac{b(hg,v)^{k}}{b(1,g^{-1}v)^{k}}.

Hence

\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash G_{v}}\varphi(hg)d\mu_{v}^{1}(h)=b(1,v^{t}g^{-1})^{-k}\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash G_{v}}F(hg\mathfrak{z}_{0})b(hg\mathfrak{z}_{0},v)^{k}d\mu_{v}^{1}(h).

Put
\mathfrak{z}=g\mathfrak{z}_{0}
and let
\psi(\mathfrak{z})
be the integral

\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash G_{v}}F(h\mathfrak{z})b(h\mathfrak{z},v)^{k}d\mu_{v}^{1}(h).

Then
\psi
is a holomorphic

-invariant function on
\mathfrak{X}
, hence constant.
Thus we are left with

\psi(\mathfrak{z}_{0})=\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash G_{v}}F(h\mathfrak{z}_{0})b(h\mathfrak{z}_{0},v)^{k}d\mu_{v}^{1}(h)=\int_{\Gamma_{v}\backslash G_{v}/K_{v}}F(h\mathfrak{z}_{0})b(h\mathfrak{z}_{0},v)^{k}d\nu_{v}(h),

where

is

, and

is a measure on
\mathfrak{X}_{v}=G_{v}/K_{v}
invariant under

on
K_{v}

By uniqueness of the Haar measure,

coincides with the pull-back of

along

, up to a constant multiple. The rest of the computation follows easily from the proof in
[
O77

.

∎

Now, for
v\in L

By construction,

From this it easily follows that, for

\theta_{n}(\mathfrak{z}):=\sum_{v\in\Gamma\backslash L(-n)}\theta_{v}(\mathfrak{z})\in\mathscr{S}(k,\Gamma).

For

\phi^{k}_{n}:=\phi_{n}

Proposition 2

(
[
K79

\mathcal{L}(\phi_{n})(\mathfrak{z})=(4\pi n)^{-k}(k-1)!\,\theta_{n}(\mathfrak{z}).

By a well-known property of the Poincaré series, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(k-2)!}{(4\pi n)^{k-1}}c_{k}(L)

Proposition 3
.

The above map
\omega:\mathscr{S}(4,\Gamma)\to\emph{\textbf{SC}}^{\vee}
induces an embedding, still denoted with the same notation:

\omega:\emph{Im}(\mathcal{L}_{4})\hookrightarrow\h^{2}(S,\mathbb{C}).

This follows easily from the splitting

Corollary 5

We have

\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\dagger}=\frac{3}{4\pi}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{4}^{\dagger}\circ\omega.

Theorem 6

(

, p. 497]

For

Remark 3
.

We can restate Theorem

[

We can thus reinterpret
\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\dagger}\mathcal{L}_{4}

\mathcal{L}_{4}^{\dagger}\mathcal{L}_{4}(f)=\frac{3}{4\pi}\sum_{n\geq 1}(C_{f},C_{n}^{c})q^{n},

where

\omega(\mathcal{L}_{4}(f))\in\h^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})

Proposition 4

Suppose

is an eigenvector of the endomorphism
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\mathcal{L}

The statement is equivalent to the fact that

Iu24

Remark 4
.

While studying the adjoint

The projections
\pi_{r+1}
and
\text{pr}_{r+1}
are the two natural degeneracy maps
S_{G}(V^{1}_{r+1})\to S_{G}(V_{r}^{1})
, see
[
FJ24
, p. 44]
.

•

Note how we have also proved the analogous compatibility for the cycles
\xi_{G,r}^{\flat}
, with respect to the projections
\text{pr}_{r+1}
.

6.3.
Big cohomology classes

In order to glue the compatible family of cycles into a
\Lambda
-adic family of cycles living in the nearly ordinary part of the Iwasawa cohomology, we first need to renormalize our Hecke operator
U_{p}
, and consequently our cycles. The key point allowing this is the following result:

Lemma 20
.

The action of the
U_{p}
operator is divisible by
p
on the subspace of
\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))
generated by the special cycles coming from
\GU(1,1)
.

Proof.

By the adjointness property of the Kudla and Cogdell lift, Proposition
1
, we know that the space generated by the special cycles coincides with the space spanned by the theta lifts. Thus, by Theorem
[
Iu24
, Theorem 12]
we see that
p
divides the eigenvalues of all eigenforms in this space, which allows us to conclude.
∎

We denote
\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}
the subspace generated by the special cycles, or, equivalently, by the theta lifts. On this space, the operator
U_{p}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{p}U_{p}
acts integrally. In order to obtain a compatibility as in Proposition
10
, but with the newly normalized operators, we also need to modify the cycles
\xi_{G,r}
.
We define

\xi_{G,r}^{\prime}:=p^{-r}\xi_{G,r}\in\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}.

These cycles are still integral because of Proposition
10
and Lemma
20
. Note that this kind of renormalization is carried out also in
[
LRZ
, p. 10]
. By construction and Proposition
10
we obtain the following:

Proposition 11
.

We have

\pi_{r+1,*}(\xi_{G,r+1}^{\prime})=U_{p}^{\prime}\cdot\xi_{G,r}^{\prime}.

By projecting the cycles
\xi_{G,r}^{\prime}
to the nearly ordinary part of cohomology

\xi_{G,r}^{\text{n.o.}}:=e^{\prime}\xi_{G,r}^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}

with respect to the projector

e^{\prime}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}(U_{p}^{\prime})^{n!},

we may finally construct a big cycle

\xi_{G,\infty}^{\text{n.o.}}:=\lim_{{}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
r\end{subarray}}}\,(U_{p}^{\prime})^{-r}\!\cdot\xi_{G,r}^{\text{n.o.}}\in e^{\prime}\h_{\et,\text{Iw}}^{2}(Q_{G}^{0},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta},

where the inverse limit is taken with respect to the degeneracy maps
\pi_{r+1,*}
.

6.3.1.
The big Pairing

The group
V^{0}_{r}/V_{r}^{1}\cong K^{0}_{r}/K_{r}^{1}
acts as diamond operators on
S_{G}(V_{r}^{1})
.
The projection

\begin{pmatrix}\star&p^{r}a&p^{r}b\\
\star&\star&p^{r}c\\
\star&\star&\star\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\star&p^{r}a&p^{r}b\\
\star&1&p^{r}c\\
\star&\star&\star\end{pmatrix}

induces an embedding of
\Gamma_{r}=1+p(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
in
V^{0}_{r}/V_{r}^{1}
.
We are in the position of defining a
\Lambda_{r}:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma_{r}]
-valued pairing

\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}\times e^{\prime}\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}\to\Lambda_{r},

by the formula:

[x_{r},y_{r}]_{r}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{r}}(x_{r}^{\sigma},\lambda_{r,*}U_{p}^{{}^{\prime}r}\cdot y_{r})_{r}[\sigma^{-1}],

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{r}
is the usual Poincaré duality on étale cohomology.

Proposition 12
.

(1)

The pairing
[\cdot,\cdot]_{r}
is
\Lambda_{r}
-bilinear and all the Hecke operators away from the level
K^{(p)}
are self-adjoint with respect to it.

(2)

Let
p_{r+1}:\Lambda_{r+1}\to\Lambda_{r}
be the homomorphism induced by the natural projection
\Gamma_{r+1}\to\Gamma_{r}
. The diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r+1}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}\times e^{\prime}\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r+1}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{r+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}\times e^{\prime}\h^{2}_{\et}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{r}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{r+1,*}\times\pi_{r+1,*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{r+1}}

commutes.

Proof.

The proof is completely formal and follows by the same arguments as in
[
FJ24
, p. 44-45]
, see also
[
DR17
, p. 12]
.
∎

The above result allows us to package the pairings
[\cdot,\cdot]_{r}
into a big pairing valued in
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[\Gamma]]
, for
\Gamma=1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
:

\h_{\et,\text{Iw}}^{2}(Q_{G}^{0},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}\times e^{\prime}\h_{\et,\text{Iw}}^{2}(Q_{G}^{0},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}\xrightarrow{[\cdot,\cdot]}\Lambda.

6.4.
The
p
-adic variation of Cogdell’s theorem

In this section we obtain a
\Lambda
-adic version of Cogdell’s main theorem in
[
Cog85
]
. In order to achieve this, we use the families of cycles we constructed in §
6.3
, and pair them via the big pairing.

The input of Loeffler’s machinery are the compatible identity classes
z_{H,r}\in\h^{0}_{\et}(Q^{0}_{H}\cap u^{-1}K_{r}u,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. As divisors on Picard modular surfaces
S_{G}(K_{r})
, these depend on the choice of a globalization
\mathcal{H}
of the group
H
, and of its embedding in
\mathcal{G}
. The possible choices for
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{R})\cong\GU(1,1)(\mathbb{R})
correspond to the choice, for each
r\geq 0
, of positive vectors
v_{r}
in fixed lattices
L_{r}\subset V
preserved by the level
K_{r}
. This, on the other hand, is the input for the construction of the special cycles
C_{v_{r}}
, see §
2
. If it is clear from the context, we will drop the subscript
r
from the notation
v_{r}
, thus only writing
v
.

For each positive vector
v\in L_{r}
, we denote by

z_{H}^{v}\in\h^{0}_{\et}(Q^{0}_{H}\cap u^{-1}K_{r}u,\mathbb{Z}_{p})

the class corresponding to the vector
v\in L^{+}_{r}
, and for a fixed
r
, we may thus consider the cohomology class

\xi_{G,v,r}^{\text{n.o.}}\in e^{\prime}\h_{\et}^{2}(V_{r}^{1},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta},

constructed by the input
z_{H}^{v}
. For ease of notation, we will simply denote these as
\xi_{v,r}^{\text{n.o.}}
.

We now assume to have, for each
r\geq 0
, an integral lattice
L_{r}
preserved by
K_{r}
, together with its dual

L_{r}^{\vee}:=\{u\in V\mid\text{tr}(u,w)\in\mathbb{Z},\,\forall w\in L_{r}\}.

Then
L_{r}^{\vee}/L_{r}
is finite, and we furthermore assume that there exists, for each
r
, an element
h_{r}\in L_{r}^{\vee}/L_{r}
fixed by
K_{r}
, and such that
h_{r+1}\equiv h_{r}\mod L_{r}
. Note that it is always possible to find such lattices and cosets since we have
K_{r}\subset K_{r}^{0}
and
K_{r}^{0}
is of the form considered in
[
Iu24
, §
4.2
]
, where such choices are made explicit. We then define, for
h_{r}
as above,

L^{\prime}_{r}:=\{v\in L_{r}^{\vee}\mid v\equiv h_{r}\mod L_{r}\}.

Finally, for fixed integers
r\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
, we define cycles

\xi_{n,r}^{\text{n.o.}}:=\sum_{v\in K_{r}\backslash L^{\prime}_{r}(n)}\xi_{v,r}^{\text{n.o.}},

and

\xi_{n,\infty}^{\text{n.o.}}:=\lim_{{}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\leftarrow\\
r\end{subarray}}}U_{p}^{-r}\cdot\xi_{n,r}^{\text{n.o.}}\in e^{\prime}\h_{\et,\text{Iw}}^{2}(Q^{0}_{G},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}.

Theorem 21
.

Given a cuspidal big cohomology class

\zeta_{\infty}^{\emph{n.o.}}\in e^{\prime}\h_{\et,\emph{Iw}}^{2}(Q^{0}_{G},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))_{\theta}

such that
\zeta^{\emph{n.o.}}_{1}
is orthogonal to the Chern class on
S_{V_{1}}
. Then
\Lambda
-adic
q
-expansion

\Phi_{\zeta_{\infty}^{\emph{n.o.}}}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}[\xi_{n,\infty}^{\emph{n.o.}},\zeta_{\infty}^{\emph{n.o.}}]q^{n}\in\Lambda[[q]]

interpolates the modular forms constructed by Cogdell and the modular forms in §5, i.e. for each weight
k\geq 0
and level
r>0
,

\nu_{2k,r}(\Phi_{\zeta_{\infty}^{\emph{n.o.}}}(z))\in S_{2k+3}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{r}D),\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}}).

Proof.

First suppose that
k=0
.
By Proposition
12
, the specializations of the coefficients of
\Phi_{\zeta_{\infty}^{\text{n.o.}}}
correspond to the pairing at finite levels of the specializations of the big cycles. That is,

\displaystyle\nu_{r}([\xi_{n,\infty}^{\text{n.o.}},\zeta_{\infty}^{\text{n.o.}}])

\displaystyle=[U^{{}^{\prime}-r}_{p}\xi_{n,r}^{\text{n.o.}},U^{{}^{\prime}-r}_{p}\zeta_{r}^{\text{n.o.}}]_{r}=[\xi_{n,r}^{\text{n.o.}},U^{{}^{\prime}-2r}_{p}\zeta_{r}^{\text{n.o.}}]_{r}

\displaystyle=[\xi_{n,r}^{\prime},U^{{}^{\prime}-2r}_{p}\zeta_{r}^{\text{n.o.}}]_{r}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{r}}(\xi_{n,r}^{\prime},(\lambda_{r,*}U_{p}^{{}^{\prime}-r}\cdot\zeta_{r}^{\text{n.o.}})^{\sigma})_{r}[\sigma].

Now,
\xi_{n,r}^{\prime}
is a twist of the class

\sum_{v\in K_{r}\backslash L^{\prime}_{r}(n)}\iota_{r,*}(z_{G,r}^{v})

by the Atkin-Lehner correspondence
\lambda_{r,*}
and the correspondence
[\tau^{r}]_{*}
. As the
z_{G,r}^{v}
are Cogdell’s special cycles, to conclude we need to apply on both arguments of the pairing the inverse correspondences
\lambda_{r}^{*}
and
[\tau^{r}]^{*}
, and we get the result for
k=0
. Now take
k\geq 0
. By
[
H89
, Corollary 3.4 and Theorem 3.3]
, see also
[
CNR24
, Theorem 7.8]
, the specialization in weight
2k
of the big pairing factors through the moment map
\text{mom}^{k,k}
. Hence, we are left to consider, for each
n>0
, the class

\text{mom}^{k,k}_{r}(\xi_{n,\infty}^{\text{n.o.}})\in e^{\prime}\h_{\et}^{2}(V_{r}^{1},\mathcal{H}^{k,k})_{\theta}.

By
[
LRZ
, Theorem 5.2.3]
the above class corresponds to the ordinary part of the
n
-th higher weight special cycle; indeed, in our case the branching polynomials correspond, essentially by definition, to the projection to the highest weight representation of the powers
v^{k}
of the positive vectors
v\in L^{\prime}_{r}(n)
. As the big pairing is Hecke equivariant, we may proceed as above for the case
k=0
, thus getting the result for positive
k
.
∎

The role of age and antibodies in central regional lymph node metastases of papillary thyroid carcinoma combined with hashimoto’s thyroiditis
Authors: Chengcheng Duan, Ping Yu, Xinran Wei, Jingyue Zhong, Xianji Wu, Siqi Xiao, Guang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe coexistence of Hashimoto thyroiditis (HT) and papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is increasing year by year, and the role of age, thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb), and thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb) in central neck lymph node metastasis (CLNM) in PTC patients with HT is still controversial.MethodsIn this study, we retrospectively collected information about 540 PTC patients with HT, explored the risk prediction of CLNM by related factors through univariate, multivariate logistic regression and restricted cubic spline (RCS) plots.ResultsIn univariate logistic analysis, age, gender, no history of hypertension, ultrasound image characteristics (largest diameter of the suspicious thyroid nodule, calcification of the suspicious thyroid nodule, suspicious lymph nodes), thyroid-related antibodies (TgAb, TPOAb) were statistically significant in CLNM in PTC patients with HT (PTC-HT) significance (P < 0.05) ; after adjustment for many variables, age, TgAb, and TPOAb were significantly associated with CLNM. Age < 45 years and TgAb positivity were risk factors for CLNM in PTC patients combined with HT, and TPOAb positivity was a protective factor for CLNM in PTC patients with HT. Between age and TgAb, the risk of CLNM was greatest at age < 45 years combined with TgAb positivity (P < 0.001, OR = 5.771, 95% CI 2.690-12.383); between age and TPOAb, the risk of CLNM metastasis was greatest when age < 45 years combined with negative TPOAb (P < 0.001, OR = 4.856, 95% CI 2.732–8.630) ; between TgAb and TPOAb, the risk of CLNM metastasis was greatest when TgAb positivity combined with negative TPOAb (P = 0.002, OR = 2596, 95% CI 1.430–4.713).ConclusionsYounger age and TgAb positivity are significant risk factors for CLNM in PTC patients with HT, and TPOAb positivity plays a protective role, therefore, age, TgAb and TPOAb have important predictive value. Patients aged < 45 years, with TgAb positivity and TPOAb negativity should not only pay attention to adequately assessing the metastatic status of CLNM preoperatively and formulating a suitable surgical scope, but also to monitoring antibody levels for follow-up after the operation.

Introduction

Hashimoto’s thyroiditis (HT) is the most common autoimmune disease of the thyroid, and papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common thyroid cancer. The proportion of PTC patients combined with HT is approximately 23% (range from 5 to 85) [1], and this figure is growing year by year [2]. Since Dailey first suggested the existence of a correlation between HT and PTC in 1955 [3], researchers have been exploring the correlation, preoperative diagnosis and analysis of prognostic factors for PTC in combination with HT. Despite the slow course and favourable long-term prognosis of PTC in combination with HT, central lymph node metastasis remains an important factor associated with local recurrence and distant metastasis [4]. Therefore, understanding the factors of central neck lymph node metastasis (CLNM) in PTC patients combined with HT (PTC-HT) is particularly important for the selection of surgical scope and prognostic analysis of patients.

Risk factors mentioned in many articles are younger age, male, smoking, BRAF V600E mutation, p53 mutation, multifocal, extra-glandular invasion (e.g., vasculature, muscle), high levels of the thyroid-stimulating hormones (TSH), Thyroglobulin (Tg), and thyroid peroxidase antibodies (TPOAb), etc [5, 6]. Among the aforementioned factors, we recognised that long-term HT leads to hypothyroidism, resulting in elevated TSH levels [7]. Consequently, in PTC patients with HT, it is necessary to investigate the impact of TSH and other thyroid function indicators on CLNM. Among the different age groups, disease progression varies in PTC patients combined with HT: The eighth edition of the AJCC guidelines, which use age > 55 years as the basis for poorer prognostic staging of differentiated thyroid cancer, do not specifically mention whether the same applies in PTC patients combined with HT [8]. Most studies concluded that younger age was associated with an increased risk of CLNM in PTC patients [9, 10], and it has also been suggested that age disparity does not predict CLNM in PTC patients combined with HT [5]. The role of thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) and TPOAb in the diagnosis of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis is well established, but their role in central lymph node metastasis is controversial. Some studies suggest that their presence and levels are not associated with an increased risk of CLNM [11], however, some studies have demonstrated that they play an important role not only in lymph node metastasis but also in the prediction of PTC prognosis [12]. Therefore, by analysing the information of 540 PTC patients with combined HT and dividing them into central lymph node metastasis and no metastasis groups, our study aimed to explore three aspects: (1) Differences in clinicopathological features of CLNM and non-CLNM in PTC patients combined with HT; (2)The role played by different age groups, TgAb and TPOAb statuses with respect to CLNM in PTC patients combined with HT; (3) The predictive role of age and the combination of the two antibodies on the risk of CLNM in PTC patients combined with HT.

Methods and materials

Study population

The study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of China-Japan Union Hospital of Jilin University and was carried out in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration (No.20240506010). Retrospective analysis of the data of PTC patients combined with HT from November 2022 to November 2023 at China-Japan Union of Jilin University Hospital. All of the PTC patients with combined HT had received initial thyroidectomy with at least one side central neck dissection (CND), while 562 patients were screened. Among them, 9 were excluded due to a history of previous head and neck surgery, 3 were excluded because of a combination of other autoimmune diseases, and 10 were excluded because of missing data on TgAb and TPOAb. Finally, 540 patients were enrolled for the final analysis (Fig. 1).

Data collection

Data on demographic characteristics, personal history, surgical records, and postoperative paraffin pathology results were collected through the electronic medical records system. There are two diagnostic criteria for HT, and only one of the diagnostic criteria needs to be satisfied: (1) Postoperative paraffin pathology confirms chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis. (2) Serological tests show positive TgAb or TPOAb, while thyroid ultrasound suggests heterogeneous echogenicity of the thyroid gland with diffuse changes. The measurements of preoperative serum thyroid function (TSH, FT3, FT4, Thyroglobulin II) (A second-generation thyroglobulin assay was employed, and the Thyroglobulin is reported as TgII) and thyroid autoantibodies (TgAb, TPOAb) were done. The result of detectable TgAb concentration was considered positive when the level exceeded 115 IU/ml, and the result of detectable TPOAb concentration was considered positive when the level exceeded 34 IU/ml. Most preoperative serum thyroid function parameters were measured using commercial Roche Diagnostics (Shanghai) Limited assays and in the accredited Department of Nuclear Medicine at our institution. BRAF V600E mutation (Report issued by our pathology department which is qualified to conduct tests) was detected in specimens of preoperative fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC). The ultrasonographic characteristics collected were suspicious thyroid nodules, including number (multifocal or unifocal), tumor size (largest diameter), calcification (yes or no) and suspicious lymph nodes. Among the suspected malignant thyroid nodules and lymph nodes were patients with thyroid ultrasound suggestive of thyroid nodules ACR-TI-RADS 4a and above, and lymph nodes that were suspected with characteristics such as hypoechoic appearance, indistinct structure, unclear lymphatic portal, and calcification. The surgical interventions performed on patients were categorised as total thyroidectomy or hemithyroidectomy (including central lymph node dissection; patients undergoing lateral neck dissection were assessed preoperatively and are not reflected in the table). The 2025 ATA guidelines classify the recurrence risk for papillary thyroid carcinoma into four tiers [13]. For computational convenience, this study groups Low and Low-Intermediate risk into one category, and Intermediate-High and High risk into another.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with SPSS version 26.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL, United States) and R software version 4.2.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Differences were considered significant at P < 0.05. We initially divided PTC patients with HT into CLNM and non-CLNM, and the baseline characteristics of the patients were described (Table 1). Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (P25,P75) and analyzed by the Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Number (percentage) and chi-square tests were used to describe and compare the categorical variables. We next analysed the correlation between multiple preoperative factors and the occurrence of CLNM after surgery by univariate logistic regression.

Three logistic regression models were built to evaluate the independent association of age, TgAb, and TPOAb with CLNM. We adjusted for gender and hypertension in Model (1) The variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis entered Model (2) Variables that showed a univariate relationship with adverse events or were potentially associated with clinical outcome were controlled in the fully adjusted model (Model 3), including variables in Model 2 plus body mass index (BMI), diabetes mellitus (DM), BRAF V600E mutation, multifoci, TSH, FT3, FT4, TgII, and histological subtype. First, the age, TgAb, and TPOAb were included together in the models as continuous variables. Next, to limit the influence of extreme observations, use the per SD increase for these three variables. The three variables were also included in the models as categorical variables, based on the common classification criteria. To further explore the relationship among the age, TgAb, and TPOAb with CLNM, we employed restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve analysis, with logarithmic (Ln) transformation of TgAb and TPOAb values.

To investigate the joint predictive value of the age and TgAb on CLNM, we divided the patients into four groups: Group 1: age ≥ 45 and TgAb (-), Group 2: age ≥ 45 and TgAb (+), Group 3: age<45 and TgAb (-), and Group 4: age<45 and TgAb (+). To investigate the joint predictive value of the age and TPOAb on CLNM, we divided the patients into four groups: Group 5: age ≥ 45and TPOAb (+), Group 6: age ≥ 45 and TPOAb (-), Group 7: age<45 and TPOAb (+), and Group 8: age<45 and TPOAb (-). Meanwhile, to investigate the joint predictive value of the TgAb and TPOAb on CLNM, we divided the patients into four groups: Group 9: TgAb (-) and TPOAb (+), Group 10: TgAb (+) and TPOAb (+), Group 11: TgAb (-) and TPOAb (-), and Group 12: TgAb (+) and TPOAb (-). Positivity is indicated by “+”, negativity is indicated by “-”.

Results

Main characteristic of participants

Table 1 reports the basic characteristics of central lymph node metastases (presence vs. absence) in PTC patients combined with HT. All of the 540 cases enrolled in the present study were confirmed as PTC by histopathological examinations postoperatively, while HT was confirmed by the above diagnostic criteria. 197 (36.5%) patients were confirmed to have CLNM, and 343 (63.5%) patients were confirmed to have no CLNM. There was statistical significance between with and without CLNM in age (P<0.001). Among patients with PTC-HT (PTC combined with HT), there were more female patients, while there was a significant difference between male and female patients, with or without CLNM (P = 0.015). The history of hypertensive disease was significantly different between those with and without CLNM (P = 0.046), but the history of DM was not (P = 0.187). In the ultrasonographic (US) characteristics, the US-largest diameter of the suspicious thyroid nodule, US-calcification of the suspicious thyroid nodule, and suspicious lymph node were significantly different between those with and without CLNM (P<0.001, P = 0.042, and P<0.001). In serological thyroid-related indicators, TgAb of PTC-HT with CLNM was 315.3 (156.9,559.8) IU/ml, higher than those without CLNM (P = 0.001); conversely, TSH, TPOAb of PTC-HT with CLNM were 2.35(1.52,3.54) and 75.00 (15.7,235.3) IU/ml, lower than PTC-HT without CLNM(P = 0.032 and P = 0.012, respectively). No significant difference was found in BRAF V600E mutation and pathological subtype between PTC-HT with and without CLNM (P = 0.528 and P = 0.450, respectively). Differences existed between antibody status groups (P = 0.020), with the highest number of patients without lymph node metastasis observed in the TgAb and TPOAb positivity group, and the lowest number with lymph node metastasis in the TgAb and TPOAb negativity group. Similarly, differences were noted between the two groups in terms of surgical intervention and recurrence risk stratification (P<0.001 and P<0.001, respectively).

As shown in Table 2, many factors had significant differences (P<0.05) after univariate analyses for CLNM. Males and younger patients (age<45 years) both had a higher probability of CLNM (male: P = 0.011, OR = 2.240; younger: P<0.001, OR = 3.375), and no history of hypertension was associated with CLNM (P = 0.035). On the ultrasonographic characteristics, tumor size (largest diameter) of the suspicious thyroid nodule, calcification of the suspicious thyroid nodule (yes vs. no) and suspicious lymph nodes were more common in the CLNM. Among the indicators of thyroid function monitoring, we can see that as important indicators of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, TgAb, and TPOAb had significant differences in the CLNM.

Respective predictive value of the age, TgAb, and TPOAb for CLNM

Univariate logistic regression analysis showed that the age, TgAb, and TPOAb were significantly associated with CLNM (Table 2). The predictive value of the age, TgAb and TPOAb remained significant after adjusting for other risk factors (Table 3). Compared to older patients, the risk of CLNM was 2.899 times higher for younger patients. TgAb and TPOAb positivity (TgAb ≥ 115 IU/ml and TPOAb ≥ 34 IU/ml) showed different results; the risk of CLNM was 1.605 times higher for individuals with TgAb negativity and 0.489 times higher for individuals with TPOAb negativity.

There is no significant non-linear relationship between LnTgAb and CLNM, and an approximately linear association was more plausible (P for non-linear = 0.429, overall P = 0.004) (Fig. 2–2). Joint influences of the age and TgAb on incident CLNM risk

Patients were divided into four groups according to the cut-off value of the age and TgAb status. The joint influences of the age and TgAb on CLNM were verified by logistic regression analysis. Discussion

Our study identified the following risk factors for CLNM in PTC patients with combined HT, including age, gender, history of hypertension, ultrasound image characteristics ( largest diameter of the suspicious thyroid nodule, calcification of the suspicious thyroid nodule and suspicious lymph nodes), as well as thyroid-related antibodies (TgAb and TPOAb). Furthermore, a significant association between gender and characteristics observed in ultrasound images has been established. Although a history of hypertension has been demonstrated to be associated with various cardiovascular diseases and cancers, and the present study also reveals significant differences between those with and without CLNM, no definitive research has yet established a correlation between a history of hypertension and PTC-HT. By briefly analysing the stratification of surgical interventions and recurrence risk across different antibody statuses, our findings indicate that unilateral thyroid lobectomy carries a lower recurrence risk in cases of dual antibody positivity. This further substantiates the protective effect of HT in differentiated thyroid carcinoma, associated with improved prognosis. However, selection bias may exist as the majority of patients included in the analysis underwent unilateral resection and presented low-risk conditions. Further research is warranted to elucidate the role of antibody status in guiding surgical decisions and assessing prognosis.

In terms of age, Chatchomchuan W et al. [14] pointed out that the older the age, the greater the recurrence rate and the higher the mortality rate, which is in line with the 8th edition of the AJCC guidelines, which use age 55 as the cut-off point for staging the prognosis of PTC [8]. However, in contrast to these results, our study found that, in PTC patients with combined HT, the risk of lymph node metastasis increased with younger age and decreased with progressive age. Lymph node metastasis is an important indicator of prognosis. It has been shown that PTC patients with combined HT are younger and age < 55 years is an independent risk factor for CLNM than PTC patients without combined HT [15], which supports our findings. A high rate of central lymph node metastasis has also been reported in children and adolescents with PTC, but the 10-year survival rate is up to 98.3 per cent [16]. Due to the above differences, we believe that older patients are more likely to experience age-related deterioration in bodily functions and suffer from various diseases, which increases their risk of death. However, when we exclude the influence of additional comorbidities on patients, we find that younger patients with PTC and HT are at a greater risk of lymph node metastasis. This suggests that we should continue postoperative follow-up of these patients to explore the impact of age and antibody status on their prognosis.

It has been reported that hypothyroidism induced by HT may exert a negative feedback effect, leading to elevated TSH levels. This is associated with the proliferation of thyroid follicular cells and the development of PTC, as TSH induces growth when it binds to receptors on follicular thyroid cells [17]. Consequently, some scholars suggest that elevated serum TSH levels may stimulate the progression of thyroid tumours [18]. Ehlers M et al. asserted that Hashimoto’s thyroiditis only increases the risk of thyroid cancer in patients with normal thyroid function [19]. Kim et al. confirmed that higher levels of preoperative TSH serum concentrations might be related to advanced TNM stages of PTC [20]. However, our study found that TSH levels were higher in the group without central neck lymph node metastasis, and showed no significant correlation with CLNM in univariate analysis. As the majority of our patients had not received preoperative thyroid-related medication, any thyroid dysfunction associated with HT may have been mild [9], potentially introducing selection bias.

Mirroring the TSH findings, other serum markers of thyroid function—FT3, FT4, and TgII (Tg)—also showed no significant association with CLNM risk in our study. Among them, thyroglobulin (Tg), a specific tumor marker for thyroid cancer, is closely associated with cervical lymph node metastasis, residual disease and tumor recurrence, and has both diagnostic and prognostic implications. In cases where ultrasound is suspicious for metastatic lymphadenopathy, fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of lymph nodes combined with Tg measurement in the aspiration washout fluid (FNAC-Tg) demonstrates high diagnostic accuracy, and a study have reported that the optimal cutoff value for FNAC-Tg is 0.865 ng/mL (depending on other patient characteristics) [21]. In patients with differentiated thyroid carcinoma after surgery, serum Tg is used for follow-up surveillance. Studies have reported that the risk of recurrence increases with elevated serum Tg levels [22, 23]. He et al. found that a serum Tg level ≥ 36.2 µg/L indicates a high risk of recurrence [23]. In our study, serum Tg showed no significant significance, which may be attributed to the interference of high levels of TgAb, reflecting the particularity of patients with PTC combined with HT. Therefore, Previous studies have suggested that TgAb may interfere with the diagnostic performance of FNAC-Tg in predicting lymph node metastasis (in such cases, the diagnostic efficacy of FNAC combined with FNAC-Tg is not significantly different from that of FNAC alone) [24, 25], indicating that the influence of TgAb levels should be taken into account when using FNAC-Tg to detect lymph node metastasis in patients with PTC-HT.

TgAb and TPOAb are serological markers of HT [26], and their levels can be quantitatively measured preoperatively, which may represent the degree of thyroid inflammation [27]. When we developed the criteria for inclusion of patients with Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, we had criteria for antibody levels, but specified that they must be combined with diffuse sonographic changes of the thyroid gland on ultrasound. This is due to the presence of anti-thyroglobulin antibodies in about 60% of HT patients and the absence of TPOAb, an autoimmune antibody, in 5–34% of HT patients [28]. We found a significant correlation between TgAb, TPOAb and CLNM in the results of the thyroid antibody test, and the results of the majority of the studies confirm above [5, 12, 29, 30], but the role played by both is controversial. Huang et al. [16] concluded that TgAb positivity and TPOAb positivity had better primary tumour characteristics in PTC, but our study confirmed TgAb as a risk factor for CLNM in PTC patients with combined HT, and the higher the level the greater the risk of CLNM. Consistent with the results of Vasileiadis et al. [31] and Donangelo et al. [32], who reported that TgAb-positive PTC patients with combined HT had more lymph node metastases and more central lymph node resections than TgAb-negative patients. The fact that TgAb is tumourigenic or associated with a specific tumourigenic inflammatory response may explain our findings [33]. Some studies have suggested that TgAb can be used as a predictor of recurrence, and S. Xu et al. [29] noted that preoperative serum TgAb seems to be effective in stratifying patients with PTC combined with HT who are at high risk of recurrence. It is worth noting that we currently routinely monitor for recurrence by testing for TgII, which would be less predictive of recurrence due to the presence of TgAb. Hoofnagle et al. [34] proposed that this effect can be ruled out by specialized instrumental tests or, as suggested by some studies, by the antibody epitope of TgAb [35]. TPOAb is now considered to be the most sensitive and specific biomarker for establishing the diagnosis of HT, as its presence precedes the clinical diagnosis of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis by at least 7 years [36]. Meanwhile, TPOAb is considered superior to TgAb in predicting thyroid autoimmunity and detecting HT [37]. It has been suggested that TPOAb positivity increases the number of CLNM metastases and that TPOAb > 1300 IU/mL is a risk factor for HT combined with multifocal PTC [38]. However, in our study, TPOAb exerted a protective effect against CLNM, and we confirmed that as TPOAb levels increased the risk of CLNM decreased. Consistent with our view, E. Song et al. [12] suggested that TPOAb positivity is associated with longer disease-free survival and that there is a mechanism explaining how TPOAb positivity is exerting its anticancer effects through complement-mediated cell death and antibody-dependent cytotoxicity [39]. Differences in these results may stem from differences in their diagnostic criteria [40], the diagnosis of PTC is based on pathomorphological analysis (either from cytopathological or surgical pathological specimens). Whereas the diagnosis of Hashimoto’s thyroiditis is based on the presence of thyroid antibodies in the peripheral blood, ultrasonographic features, or pathological findings, which do not explain the role of HT in autoimmunity in patients with PTC, as well as the fact that the mechanisms involved have not been conclusively demonstrated. In conclusion, the value of preoperative assessment of TgAb and TPOAb levels in predicting and aiding in the diagnosis of CLNM metastasis in PTC patients with combined HT is noteworthy, and more studies on the mechanisms of correlation are expected.

For age and TgAb, the risk of CLNM was greatest at age < 45 years in combination with TgAb+, and the risk of CLNM was greater at age < 45 years in joint with TgAb- than at age > 45 years in joint with TgAb+, suggesting that TgAb+ plays a greater role at younger ages than TgAb+. For age and TPOAb, the risk of CLNM transmission was highest at age < 45 years in joint with TPOAb- and decreased at age < 45 years in joint with TPOAb +, suggesting a statistically weaker protective effect of TPOAb+ against the risk of CLNM at younger ages. Among TgAb and TPOAb, the risk of CLNM metastasis was greatest when TgAb + was joint with TPOAb-, and was reduced when TgAb + was joint with TPOAb+, suggesting that the protective effect of TPOAb + on the risk of CLNM was statistically attenuated against the risk of TgAb+. The differences in antibody levels between patients of different age groups in the above results, and the mechanism of action between antibodies on the risk of CLNM, have not been clearly explained, and further studies are needed. According to the guidelines, patients with papillary thyroid cancer undergo ipsilateral central lymph node dissection, i.e., prophylactic central lymph node dissection (CLND) [8]. However, in patients with PTC in combination with HT, more inflammatory lymph nodes are present due to HT, resulting in an increased number of cleared lymph nodes rather than secondary metastases from papillary thyroid cancer [41], whereas excessive CLND has been noted to have some laryngeal recurrent nerve damage [10], hypothyroid parathyroidism [42]. Therefore, when combined with patient age and antibody status in serological tests, our findings can complement preoperative non-invasive examinations (e.g. ultrasound) to provide a more accurate evaluation of enlarged lymph nodes. This information is also crucial for determining the necessity of central lymph node dissection in PTC patients with HT. Also, in conjunction with the relevant literature, there is value in age and antibody levels for postoperative recurrence, so emphasis should be placed on testing antibody levels in conjunction with the patient’s age during postoperative follow-up.

There are still many limitations of this paper: this is a single-centre retrospective study, and a multicentre, prospective large sample size is needed to observe the role of these factors and pre-existing Hashimoto’s thyroiditis in the progression of thyroid disease, such as disease-free survival and mortality; age and antibody are only statistically associated jointly, and no results have been obtained to confirm an interaction (e.g., synergistic or antagonistic effect), whether there is a biological mechanism for the interaction needs to be further investigated.

Conclusion

Younger age and TgAb positivity are significant risk factors for CLNM in PTC patients with HT, and TPOAb positivity plays a protective role; therefore, age, TgAb, and TPOAb have important predictive value; patients aged < 45 years, TgAb positivity and TPOAb negativity should not only pay attention to adequately assessing the metastatic situation of CLNM preoperatively and formulating a suitable surgical scope, but also to monitoring antibody levels for follow-up after the operation.

A ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} SMEFT


Abstract
An extension of the Standard Model is proposed, where the Higgs field is valued in the complex projective plane ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} , rather than ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} . Its geometry is consistent with U ⁡ ( 2 ) ≃ ( S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) ) / ℤ 2 U(2)\simeq(SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb{Z}_{2} electroweak gauge symmetry. The leading terms in the Lagrangian, beyond those of the Standard Model, are much more tightly constrained than in general SMEFTs, and the custodial S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) symmetry of the Higgs sector is mildly broken. The predicted tree-level deviations from Standard Model phenomenology depend on a single large mass parameter M M . Current experimental data imply that M M is a few TeV or larger.

1
Introduction

We propose here an extension of the Standard Model (SM)
[
1
]
in the
spirit of a SMEFT (Standard Model Effective Field Theory)
[
2
]
.
Its perturbative field expansion has terms of all orders, but the dominant
non-renormalisable terms are of mass dimension 6. The key idea is to
exploit the geometry of the complex projective plane
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
as the
manifold where the Higgs field takes its values. The Riemannian metric
on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is assumed to be the Fubini–Study metric, the standard maximally
symmetric metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. In our model, its isometry group
SU(3)

is present in the background, but explicitly broken by choosing a point on

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and retaining only the
U(2)
isotropy group of this point
as the gauge symmetry of the Lagrangian. In other words, our model is
a
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
sigma-model supplemented by a Higgs potential, where the

U(2)
action is gauged.

Locally, near the chosen point of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, the
geometry resembles that of flat
\mathbb{C}^{2}
near the origin, and there is one
complex-doublet Higgs field. Our
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
model is therefore close to the
Standard Model, provided the geometric scale factor of the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
manifold
is large and the Higgs potential is chosen so that the Higgs vacuum manifold
is small on this scale. In units of the Higgs vacuum expectation value
(vev), the model therefore has a single large mass parameter
M
for
Beyond the Standard Model (BSM) physics.

In the electroweak sector of the Standard Model, including its gauge fields and
fermions, the symmetry group is
U(2)\simeq(SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
However, the usual Higgs potential has a larger, custodial
SO(4)
symmetry,
and the Higgs vacuum manifold is a round 3-sphere. This custodial symmetry
is not exploited here, so our model has a different geometric structure
than models where it is retained
[
3
,
4
]
. However, the generic orbits
of
U(2)
on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
are still three-dimensional; they are a family of
squashed 3-spheres. One orbit is simply a point, and in the
neighbourhood of this, the orbits are very close to being round
3-spheres. Therefore the custodial symmetry is approximately
retained, and its important consequence for the ratio of the

W
-boson and
Z
-boson masses remains approximately valid. It was
observed in ref.
[
3
]
that
SO(4)
symmetry, broken to
U(2)
,
leads to squashed 3-spheres, but the connection to
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
geometry
was not mentioned.

It is well known that there is no inconsistency at present in assuming
that the SM gauge group is
(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb{Z}_{6}
.
The quantum numbers of the Higgs field and the known fermion multiplets
(quarks and leptons) lie in representations that are consistent with the

\mathbb{Z}_{6}
quotient, and there are no local or global anomalies
[
5
]
.
In the analysis that follows, we will ignore the QCD interactions of
quarks and gluons. These sectors of the Standard Model Lagrangian are
unaffected by changes to the Higgs field geometry. We will discuss only the
electroweak sector, including the Higgs field, and the quark and
lepton Yukawa couplings to the Higgs field. The relevant gauge group
is therefore the electroweak
U(2)
subgroup of the SM gauge group,
mentioned above. We will also ignore
fermion generation mixing, and simplify the neutrino physics to the
version that was considered in the 1960s and ’70s, where there is
just a left-handed neutrino field; our model has no novel insights
concerning these challenging topics.

The Lagrangian has terms involving: (i) Gauge-covariant
derivatives of the Higgs field, (ii) a Higgs potential, (iii) Gauge
field tensors, (iv) Dirac (kinetic) terms for fermions, (v) Yukawa
terms coupling the Higgs field and fermions. We will discuss these
in turn, and then explore some phenomenological consequences, in
particular, for the
W
-boson to
Z
-boson mass ratio, and for the
Yukawa couplings of the Higgs boson. These will lead to constraints on
the BSM mass parameter
M
.

Our presentation begins with a review of the Fubini–Study metric on

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

2
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
Geometry

The complex projective plane
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is obtained from flat complex
3-space,
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, by quotienting out the action of scaling by a complex
number. Geometrically,
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is the manifold of complex
lines
2
2

2

We use the mathematician’s notion of complex
line
\mathbb{C}
to denote what is more commonly called the complex plane. All lines
considered here are lines through the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

We denote a non-zero point in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
by the (column) vector
Z=(Z_{0},Z_{1},Z_{2})
and the scaling action is
Z\to\kappa Z
with
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}-\{0\}
. It is convenient
to use the inhomogeneous coordinates
Z=(1,z_{1},z_{2})
on

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. However, this misses the part of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
“at infinity”. The
missing part is the manifold of points
Z=(0,Z_{1},Z_{2})
modulo
scaling by
\kappa
, which is a copy of the complex projective
line
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, and geometrically a 2-sphere. Most of our calculations
will be in the neighbourhood of
Z=(1,0,0)
so the use of
inhomogeneous coordinates causes no difficulty.

The hermitian conjugate (row) vector
\bar{Z}=(Z_{0}^{*},Z_{1}^{*},Z_{2}^{*})

will play an important role in what follows. In inhomogeneous
coordinates, the hermitian conjugate of
Z
is
\bar{Z}=(1,z_{1}^{*},z_{2}^{*})
.

\mathbb{C}^{3}
has the Euclidean metric
ds^{2}=d\bar{Z}dZ
, whose
isometry group, fixing the origin, is
SO(6)
. The
natural metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is derived from this by removing from

dZ
its projection parallel to
Z
, and then normalising so that
rescaling by
\kappa
has no effect. The result is the Fubini–Study
metric
[
6
]

ds^{2}=\frac{(\bar{Z}Z)d\bar{Z}dZ-(d\bar{Z}Z)(\bar{Z}dZ)}{(\bar{Z}Z)^{2}}\,.

(1)

This is in dimensionless form; a scale factor will be introduced with
the Higgs field. Note that if
dZ=\mu Z
with
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, i.e.

dZ
parallel to
Z
, then
ds^{2}=0
.

The Fubini–Study metric is Kähler and of constant positive
curvature. Because of the projection mentioned above, it depends in
an essential way on the complex character of the coordinates. The
isometry group is therefore not
SO(6)
but its subgroup
SU(3)
.
In homogeneous coordinates, the symmetry group action is simply

Z\to UZ
, with
U\in SU(3)
.
SU(3)
acts transitively on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
,
and the isotropy group of any given point is
U(2)
. As a manifold,
therefore,
\mathbb{CP}^{2}=SU(3)/U(2)
. We can check the isotropy group for
the point
Z=(1,0,0)
, as follows. The matrices
U\in SU(3)

that preserve
Z
are of the form

U=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-2i\gamma}&0\\
0&e^{i\gamma}u\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2)

with
u\in SU(2)
. Naively, this sends
(1,0,0)
to
(e^{-2i\gamma},0,0)
,
but this is equivalent to
(1,0,0)
in the projective space.

e^{i\gamma}u
is a unitary
2\times 2
matrix, and apparently an element of

SU(2)\times U(1)
, but it is unchanged if
\gamma\to\gamma+\pi

and
u\to-u
, so the isotropy group is indeed
U(2)
.

In our model, the point
(1,0,0)\in\mathbb{CP}^{2}
plays a preferred role, and its
isotropy group
U(2)
will be identified with the electroweak gauge
group. The generic orbits of
U(2)
on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
are 3-spheres, as will
be clarified in the next section.

Later, we will be using the projections of a generic vector
\Psi\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, defined at location
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, on to the line through
Z

and on to its complement, which is a copy of the tangent space to

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. These projections are respectively

\Psi\to P\Psi=\frac{Z(\bar{Z}\Psi)}{\bar{Z}Z}

(3)

and

\Psi\to(1-P)\Psi=\frac{(\bar{Z}Z)\Psi-Z(\bar{Z}\Psi)}{\bar{Z}Z}\,.

(4)

Because these projections are unaffected by rescaling
Z
by

\kappa
, they are defined at location
Z
on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. More precisely,
over
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
there is its 1-dimensional tautological bundle, whose fibre
at a point
Z\in\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is the line in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
through
Z
,
and its 2-dimensional tangent bundle. The 3-dimensional, trivial
\mathbb{C}^{3}

bundle over
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is the direct sum of these, so an element
\Psi
in the
trivial
\mathbb{C}^{3}
bundle is the sum of its projections on to the
tautological and tangent bundles.

Note that
\bar{Z}\Psi
and
\bar{Z}Z
are
SU(3)
-invariant. Therefore, both

P\Psi
and
(1-P)\Psi
transform in the same way as
\Psi
under

SU(3)
.
P
and
1-P
themselves transform by conjugation under

SU(3)
. The projector
1-P
will be important when we discuss Yukawa terms.

3
Squashed 3-Spheres

Let
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
be standard coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and

ds^{2}=d\bar{z}dz
the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Useful polar coordinates are

z_{1}=r\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\,e^{i(\chi-\varphi)/2}\,,\quad z_{2}=r\sin\frac{1}{2}\theta\,e^{i(\chi+\varphi)/2}\,,

(5)

with ranges
0\leq r
,
0\leq\theta\leq\pi
,
0\leq\varphi<2\pi
and

0\leq\chi<4\pi
. The submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
at fixed
r>0
is a
round 3-sphere of radius
r
, which in turn is a circle bundle (a Hopf
bundle) over a round 2-sphere, with
z_{2}/z_{1}=\tan\frac{1}{2}\theta\,e^{i\varphi}
the stereographic coordinate on the 2-sphere, and
\chi
the
circle coordinate. In polars, the metric
ds^{2}=d\bar{z}dz
becomes

ds^{2}=dr^{2}+\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\left[d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\varphi^{2}+(d\chi-\cos\theta\,d\varphi)^{2}\right],

(6)

whose angular part is the metric of the round 3-sphere of radius

r
. Each of these 3-spheres is a
U(2)
orbit. and there is a unique

U(2)
fixed point at
r=0
. Note that the ratio of the circle radius
to the 2-sphere radius is a constant, independent of
r
.

Let us now compare the Fubini–Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Using the
inhomogeneous coordinates
Z=(1,z_{1},z_{2})
, and hence
dZ=(0,dz_{1},dz_{2})
, the metric (
1
) becomes

ds^{2}=\frac{(1+\bar{z}z)d\bar{z}dz-(d\bar{z}z)(\bar{z}dz)}{(1+\bar{z}z)^{2}}\,,

(7)

and using the polar coordinates (
5
),

ds^{2}=\frac{dr^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{r^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\left[(1+r^{2})(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\varphi^{2})+(d\chi-\cos\theta\,d\varphi)^{2}\right].

(8)

Here, the angular part is the metric of a squashed 3-sphere, a Hopf circle
bundle over a round 2-sphere, where the ratio of the circle radius
to the 2-sphere radius is
1/\sqrt{1+r^{2}}
, i.e. dependent on
r
.
For us, the important observation is that for small positive
r
, the
radius ratio is close to unity, so the squashed 3-sphere is
approximately round. The consequence for our model is that the custodial

SO(4)
symmetry – the symmetry of a round 3-sphere – will be
approximately realised by the Higgs potential.

As
r\to\infty
, the radius ratio approaches zero, compatibly with the
completion of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
at infinity being simply a round 2-sphere, which
is a special 2-dimensional orbit of
U(2)
. It has radius

\frac{1}{2}
and is at a finite distance from any other point in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

4
Higgs Kinetic Term

By the Higgs kinetic term we mean the combination of terms involving the
Higgs field’s gauge-covariant spacetime derivatives. In our model, the
Higgs field is a map
\Phi
from spacetime to the target
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Its
derivatives lie in the tangent space to
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, and we use the Fubini–Study
metric to construct the Higgs kinetic term, which is quadratic in these
derivatives. Spacetime indices are contracted with the
Minkowski metric
(+,-,-,-)
using the usual index raising convention.

The Higgs field in homogeneous coordinates is denoted
\Phi=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, and in its inhomogeneous form as

\Phi=(M,\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(M,\phi)\,,

(9)

where
\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
is a complex scalar doublet.
3
3

3

All these multiplets, and the left-handed fermion doublets below, are column
vectors.
The Higgs field has mass dimension
1
, so the positive
constant
M
is a mass parameter. We will see later that the expansion
parameter for BSM terms in our model is
\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}
, where
v
is
the Higgs vev, so we will need
M\gg v
.

As mentioned earlier, the
SU(3)
symmetry is explicitly
broken. This is because the point
(M,0,0)
(equivalently
(1,0,0)
)
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
plays a preferred role. The isotropy group of this point is the

U(2)
subgroup with typical element

U=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\alpha/3}&0\\
0&e^{i\alpha/6}u\end{pmatrix}

(10)

where
u\in SU(2)
. Its action on the (inhomogeneous) Higgs field is

\Phi\to U\Phi=(M,e^{i\alpha/2}u\phi)\,.

(11)

In our model, as in the Standard Model, it is this

U(2)
group that is gauged.

We introduce the
SU(2)
and
U(1)
gauge potentials, respectively

A_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}
, with
A_{\mu}
hermitian and
B_{\mu}
real. Using
the generators
I_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{a}
for the Lie algebra of
SU(2)
,
with
\sigma_{a}
the Pauli matrices, we have the expansion
A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}I_{a}
with
A_{\mu}^{a}
real.
I_{a}
are the weak isospin operators
and the
U(1)
generator, the hypercharge operator, is denoted
Y
.
The hypercharge
y
of a field or particle is its

Y
-eigenvalue
4
4

4

We use the ‘half-scale’ convention for
hypercharge, as in e.g. ref.
[
1
]
.
, and the electric charge

Q
is the eigenvalue of
I_{3}+Y
.

In inhomogeneous coordinates, the derivatives and gauge potentials
act non-trivially only on the lower doublet of fields
\phi
, i.e.

D_{\mu}\Phi=(0,D_{\mu}\phi)

(12)

where

D_{\mu}\phi=\partial_{\mu}\phi-igA_{\mu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}ig^{\prime}B_{\mu}\phi\,,

(13)

and
g
and
g^{\prime}
are the usual gauge coupling constants. The Higgs field

\phi
has hypercharge
y=\frac{1}{2}
, so its complex components
\phi_{1}
and

\phi_{2}
have electric charges
1
and
0
, respectively.

The Higgs kinetic term is then constructed using the metric
(
1
), with
Z
and
dZ
substituted by
\Phi
and
D_{\mu}\Phi
.
The result is

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs\,Kinetic}=M^{2}\frac{(M^{2}+\bar{\phi}\phi)\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}D^{\mu}\phi-(\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}\,\phi)(\bar{\phi}D^{\mu}\phi)}{\left(M^{2}+\bar{\phi}\phi\right)^{2}}\,,

(14)

where the prefactor
M^{2}
is required to obtain an expression of mass
dimension
4
. This kinetic term has novel features compared with
the Standard Model kinetic term
\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}D^{\mu}\phi
,
but reduces to it if
|\phi|
is small compared with
M
.

5
Higgs Potential

The Higgs potential on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is a real, non-negative function of the
homogeneous Higgs field
\Phi
that is unchanged by the scaling

\Phi\to\kappa\Phi
, and is
U(2)
gauge invariant. It
needs to break the
SU(3)
symmetry of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, otherwise it would be
constant, and it needs to have its minimum along a non-trivial, squashed
3-sphere orbit of
U(2)
, in order to spontaneously break the
gauge symmetry to
U(1)_{\rm em}
and for the Higgs mechanism to operate.

An acceptable, simple potential with these properties is

V(\Phi)=\lambda\left(\frac{\bar{\Phi}W\Phi}{\bar{\Phi}\Phi}\right)^{2}

(15)

with
W
a diagonal
3\times 3
matrix having its lower pair of entries
equal, and
\lambda
a dimensionless positive coefficient. Specifically,
evaluating this for the inhomogeneous
\Phi=(M,\phi)
, and with

W=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}v^{2}&0&0\\
0&-M^{2}&0\\
0&0&-M^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(16)

we obtain

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs\,Potential}=-V(\phi)=-\lambda M^{4}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}v^{2}-\bar{\phi}\phi}{M^{2}+\bar{\phi}\phi}\right)^{2}\,,

(17)

which has mass dimension
4
. For
|\phi|\ll M
,
V(\phi)

simplifies to its Standard Model form

\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}v^{2}-\bar{\phi}\phi\right)^{2}
.

The minimal, vacuum value of
V
is zero, where
\bar{\phi}\phi=\frac{1}{2}v^{2}
. This
is as in the Standard Model, but here the Higgs vacuum manifold is
geometrically a squashed 3-sphere, because of the form of the Higgs kinetic
term (
14
).
V
has a local maximum
value
\frac{1}{4}\lambda v^{4}
at the point
\phi=0
, and a further local
maximum value
\lambda M^{4}
on the 2-sphere at infinity.

As usual, the constant, vacuum field configuration is selected to be

\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}v\right)
,
which is charge-neutral and real. The unbroken gauge group is then the usual
electromagnetic
U(1)_{\rm em}
subgroup of
U(2)
with typical element

u_{\rm em}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\beta}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,.

(18)

6
Gauge Field Tensors

The gauge field tensors and their contribution to the Lagrangian are
exactly as in the Standard Model. The
SU(2)
and
U(1)

field tensors are

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}-ig[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]\,,

(19)

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}-\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}\,.

(20)

Using the expansion of the
SU(2)
gauge potential,

A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}I_{a}
, the
SU(2)
field tensor acquires the
expansion
F_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}^{a}I_{a}
, where

F_{\mu\nu}^{a}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{a}+g\varepsilon^{abc}A_{\mu}^{b}A_{\nu}^{c}\,.

(21)

The field tensor terms in the Lagrangian are

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Field\,Tensor}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F^{\mu\nu a}-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}\,.

(22)

7
Fermion Kinetic Terms

We start from the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{3}
bundle over
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, whose constant fibre

{\cal V}=\mathbb{C}^{3}
is unaffected by changes in the value of the Higgs field

\Phi
, i.e. the point in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. We next exploit the explicit breaking
of
SU(3)
by the choice of vector
(1,0,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, which restricts
the gauge group to
U(2)
with group elements (
10
).
This allows us to pick out the 2-dimensional subspace
{\cal V}_{L}
of vectors
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
whose upper component is zero, and the 1-dimensional subspace

{\cal V}_{R}
of vectors whose lower two components are zero. Finally, using this
gauge-invariant splitting, we assume that the left-handed fermion fields
are (electroweak) doublets lying in
{\cal V}_{L}
and the right-handed fermion
fields are singlets lying in
{\cal V}_{R}
.
SU(2)
acts canonically
from the left on
{\cal V}_{L}
and trivially on
{\cal V}_{R}
. The action of the
U(1)

generator
Y
has more freedom compatible with gauge invariance. Its
eigenvalues – the hypercharges of the fermion fields – do not have
to be those of the generating matrix for the group elements
(
10
), namely
-\frac{1}{3}
and
\frac{1}{6}
.

In the context of the Standard Model, the above remarks are
a restatement of what is well known. The reason for making
them is that there is an alternative possibility, exploiting the
Higgs field
\Phi
. We could have split the trivial
\mathbb{C}^{3}
bundle
over
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
into the 1-dimensional tautological bundle and the
2-dimensional tangent bundle, achieved at each point of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}

by using the projection operators
P
and

1-P
, as described in section 2. We could then have
assumed that the left-handed fermion fields lie in the tangent bundle,
and the right-handed fields in the tautological bundle. This
assumption would have been compatible with retaining the full
SU(3)

symmetry. However, it would have made the fermions dependent on the
Higgs field at the kinematic level. We find this unattractive. It also
makes construction of Yukawa interaction terms problematic. Instead, we
have exploited the constant vector
(1,0,0)
to produce the
doublet/singlet fermion field structure. The projection operator

1-P
will later be key to the construction of gauge-invariant Yukawa terms.

These assumptions mean that the kinetic terms (involving gauge-invariant
spacetime derivatives) for the fermions are exactly as in the Standard
Model, making use of the Dirac matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
and
\gamma^{5}
. Fermion
fields are doublet 4-spinors
\psi_{L}
and singlet 4-spinors
\psi_{R}

satisfying, respectively,
\gamma^{5}\psi_{L}=-\psi_{L}
and

\gamma^{5}\psi_{R}=\psi_{R}
.

For the lightest, first-generation quark pair, the u-quark and d-quark,
the fields are the left-handed doublet
q_{L}=(u_{L},d_{L})
and the two
right-handed singlets
u_{R}
and
d_{R}
. The second- and third-generation
quark pairs are similar and we do not write their Lagrangians
explicitly. The quark kinetic terms are

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Quark\,Kinetic}=\overline{q_{L}}\,i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}q_{L}+\overline{u_{R}}\,i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}u_{R}+\overline{d_{R}}\,i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}d_{R}\,,

(23)

where the different gauge-covariant derivatives are

\displaystyle D_{\mu}q_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}q_{L}-igA_{\mu}q_{L}-ig^{\prime}y_{L}^{q}B_{\mu}q_{L}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}u_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}u_{R}-ig^{\prime}y_{R}^{u}B_{\mu}u_{R}\,,

(25)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}d_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}d_{R}-ig^{\prime}y_{R}^{d}B_{\mu}d_{R}

(26)

and the quark hypercharges are
(y_{L}^{q},y_{R}^{u},y_{R}^{d})=(\frac{1}{6},\frac{2}{3},-\frac{1}{3})
. Note that the overbar on
the fermion fields in (
23
) is the Dirac conjugate,
including
\gamma^{0}
.

For the first-generation lepton pair, the electron and its neutrino,
the fields are the doublet
l_{L}=(\nu_{L},e_{L})
and the one singlet
e_{R}
.
For the second- and third-generation lepton pairs we again do not
write the Lagrangians explicitly. The lepton kinetic terms are

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Lepton\,Kinetic}=\overline{l_{L}}\,i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}l_{L}+\overline{e_{R}}\,i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}e_{R}\,,

(27)

with the gauge-covariant derivatives

\displaystyle D_{\mu}l_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}l_{L}-igA_{\mu}l_{L}-ig^{\prime}y_{L}^{l}B_{\mu}l_{L}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}e_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}e_{R}-ig^{\prime}y_{R}^{e}B_{\mu}e_{R}

(29)

and the lepton hypercharges
(y_{L}^{l},y_{R}^{e})=(-\frac{1}{2},-1)
.

These fermions have electric charges
(Q^{u},Q^{d},Q^{\nu},Q^{e})=(\frac{2}{3}.-\frac{1}{3},0,-1)
.

8
Yukawa Terms

The Yukawa terms in the Standard Model generate
gauge-invariant masses for the fermions. They couple the Higgs field to
fermion bilinears, and the masses generated are proportional to a
Yukawa coupling times the Higgs vev
v
. We first recall the two ways for
contracting the left-handed fermion doublets with the Higgs doublet
field
\phi
, in order to have
SU(2)
singlets. Let us illustrate
this for the left-handed quark doublet
q_{L}
. In components,

q_{L}=(u_{L},d_{L})\quad{\rm and}\quad\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\,.

(30)

The first contraction is the obvious
\overline{q_{L}}\,\phi
,
and the Yukawa term obtained from this is

-\lambda_{d}\,\overline{q_{L}}\,\phi\,d_{R}=-\lambda_{d}(\overline{u_{L}}\,\phi_{1}\,d_{R}+\overline{d_{L}}\,\phi_{2}\,d_{R})\,,

(31)

which is a
U(2)
singlet because of the quark and
Higgs field hypercharge assignments. Only a d-quark mass is generated
through this term in the Higgs vacuum,
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}v\right)
. To generate a u-quark mass,
one needs to exploit the conjugate of the Higgs doublet,

\phi_{C}=i\sigma_{2}\phi^{*}=(\phi_{2}^{*},-\phi_{1}^{*})
. This is also a
doublet of
SU(2)
, like
\phi
, but has the opposite hypercharge. The
second contraction with
q_{L}
is then
\overline{q_{L}}\,\phi_{C}
, and
from this one obtains the Yukawa term

-\lambda_{u}\,\overline{q_{L}}\,\phi_{C}\,u_{R}=-\lambda_{u}(\overline{u_{L}}\,\phi_{2}^{*}\,u_{R}-\overline{d_{L}}\,\phi_{1}^{*}\,u_{R})\,.

(32)

\lambda_{d}
and
\lambda_{u}
are the Yukawa coupling constants that
combine with the Higgs vev to give the d- and u-quark masses

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\lambda_{d}v
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\lambda_{u}v
.

For the leptons there is just one Yukawa term, giving the electron a
mass. Since
e_{L}
is the lower component of its doublet, the required term
is

-\lambda_{e}\,\overline{l_{L}}\,\phi\,e_{R}=-\lambda_{e}(\overline{\nu_{L}}\,\phi_{1}\,e_{R}+\overline{e_{L}}\,\phi_{2}\,e_{R})\,,

(33)

the analogue of the term giving the d-quark mass. The electron mass is

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\lambda_{e}v
. The neutrino is left-handed
and massless, with no right-handed field, so there is no need to
use
\phi_{C}
again.

It is rather mysterious that the Higgs field occurs in two
different ways, but it is straightforward to verify directly that

\phi
and
\phi_{C}
transform the same way under the left-action
of
SU(2)
. This can also be understood by considering left
multiplication by the
SU(2)
matrix

u=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
-b^{*}&a^{*}\end{pmatrix}

(34)

(with
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
) on the
2\times 2
Higgs field matrix

\phi_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{2}^{*}&\phi_{1}\\
-\phi_{1}^{*}&\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(35)

with columns
\phi_{C}
and
\phi
.
\phi_{m}
is an
SU(2)
matrix times
the scalar multiple
\sqrt{|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}
. The product

\phi^{\prime}_{m}=u\phi_{m}
is consequently another
SU(2)
matrix times the
same multiple and has the same form, with
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
replaced by

(\phi^{\prime}_{1},\phi^{\prime}_{2})=(a\phi_{1}+b\phi_{2},-b^{*}\phi_{1}+a^{*}\phi_{2})\,.

(36)

Matrix multiplication by
u
acts the same way on the left and right
columns of
\phi_{m}
, and therefore
\phi
and
\phi_{C}
transform the
same way under
SU(2)
.

We now turn to our
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
model. There is still no unification of

\phi
and
\phi_{C}
, and two distinct types of Yukawa term are needed.
We will use the homogeneous Higgs field
\Phi
in our Yukawa
terms, and must ensure that the result is gauge invariant and
unchanged by rescaling by
\kappa
. The useful
scale-invariant objects derived from
\Phi
are the projectors

P=\frac{\Phi\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Phi}\Phi}\quad{\rm and}\quad 1-P=\frac{\bar{\Phi}\Phi-\Phi\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Phi}\Phi}\,.

(37)

In terms of the components of the inhomogeneous Higgs field

\Phi=(M,\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
, these projectors are the

3\times 3
matrices

P=\frac{1}{M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}M^{2}&M\phi_{1}^{*}&M\phi_{2}^{*}\\
M\phi_{1}&|\phi_{1}|^{2}&\phi_{1}\phi_{2}^{*}\\
M\phi_{2}&\phi_{2}\phi_{1}^{*}&|\phi_{2}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}

(38)

and

1-P=\frac{1}{M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}&-M\phi_{1}^{*}&-M\phi_{2}^{*}\\
-M\phi_{1}&M^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}&-\phi_{1}\phi_{2}^{*}\\
-M\phi_{2}&-\phi_{2}\phi_{1}^{*}&M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(39)

Now recall that in our model the left-handed quark doublet and
the right-handed d-quark singlet are (column) 3-vector fields

q_{L}=(0,u_{L},d_{L})
and
(d_{R},0,0)
. Acting with
1-P
on the
d-quark singlet gives the 3-vector

\frac{1}{M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}\biggl((|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2})d_{R},\,-M\phi_{1}\,d_{R},\,-M\phi_{2}\,d_{R}\biggr).

(40)

Contracting with
q_{L}
, and including an appropriate coupling
constant, gives the Yukawa term

\frac{-\Lambda_{d}M^{2}}{M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}(\overline{u_{L}}\,\phi_{1}\,d_{R}+\overline{d_{L}}\,\phi_{2}\,d_{R})\,,

(41)

where we have included an extra factor of
M
so that this term has mass
dimension 4. The Yukawa coupling
\Lambda_{d}
is dimensionless and
close to its value in the Standard Model. When the Higgs
field acquires its vev, the d-quark mass becomes

m_{d}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{M^{2}}{M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}}\Lambda_{d}v\,.

(42)

The Yukawa term for the lepton doublet is similar, giving an electron
mass

m_{e}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{M^{2}}{M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}}\Lambda_{e}v\,.

(43)

Note that the projector
1-P
acting on a right-handed fermion singlet
produces a doublet and a singlet. The doublet plays the role in our

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
model that the product of
\phi
with the right-handed singlet
does in the Standard Model, and its contraction with a left-handed
fermion doublet is
U(2)
invariant. One could generate further Yukawa terms
by using the projected singlet, but these would be of higher order in
\phi
.

For the second type of Yukawa term, giving the u-quark its mass, we need to
replace
\phi
by its conjugate
\phi_{C}
, and use this to construct
the conjugate projector
1-P_{C}
. The right-handed u-quark singlet
is the 3-vector
(u_{R},0,0)
. Acting with
1-P_{C}
on this, and
contracting with
q_{L}
, gives the Yukawa term

\frac{-\Lambda_{u}M^{2}}{M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}(\overline{u_{L}}\,\phi_{2}^{*}\,u_{R}-\overline{d_{L}}\,\phi_{1}^{*}\,u_{R})\,.

(44)

The u-quark mass is then

m_{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{M^{2}}{M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}}\Lambda_{u}v\,.

(45)

The total contribution of the Yukawa terms to the Lagrangian of the

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
model is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Yukawa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{M^{2}}{M^{2}+|\phi_{1}|^{2}+|\phi_{2}|^{2}}\Bigl[\Lambda_{d}(\overline{u_{L}}\,\phi_{1}\,d_{R}+\overline{d_{L}}\,\phi_{2}\,d_{R})

(46)

\displaystyle\quad+\Lambda_{u}(\overline{u_{L}}\,\phi_{2}^{*}\,u_{R}-\overline{d_{L}}\,\phi_{1}^{*}\,u_{R})+\Lambda_{e}(\overline{\nu_{L}}\,\phi_{1}\,e_{R}+\overline{e_{L}}\,\phi_{2}\,e_{R})\Bigr].

9
Phenomenology Beyond the Standard Model

The Lagrangian of our proposed
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Field\,Tensor}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs\,Kinetic}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs\,Potential}

(47)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Quark\,Kinetic}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Lepton\,Kinetic}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Yukawa}\,,

with its contributions defined in sections 5 – 8 above. Some of the apparent
differences from the Standard Model can be removed by a recalibration of
parameters and rescaling of the fields. But there remain some
significant modifications to SM relations, and we will use these
together with recent experimental results as reviewed by the Particle
Data Group (PDG)
[
7
]
to place
constraints on the BSM mass parameter
M
. Also, when the denominator
factors are expanded out, there arise several dimension-6 terms
that are Standard Model terms multiplied by
\bar{\phi}\phi
. In the
following, we will explore the consequences of the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT for
(i) gauge boson masses, (ii) Higgs cubic and quartic couplings, (iii)
Yukawa couplings. Constraints on
M
currently come mainly from
the
W
-boson to
Z
-boson mass ratio.

9.1
The
W
-boson to
Z
-boson mass ratio

The gauge boson masses are determined using the terms in
{\mathcal{L}}

involving the gauge field tensor and covariant derivatives of the
Higgs field. Most interesting is the novel contribution

(\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}\,\phi)(\bar{\phi}D^{\mu}\phi)
to the Higgs
kinetic term.

The gauge couplings
g
and
g^{\prime}
are the same as in the Standard
Model, as they control the tree-level couplings of gauge
bosons to fermions. The weak mixing angle
\theta_{w}
is therefore
also the same; it is defined, as usual, via

\cos\theta_{w}=\frac{g}{\sqrt{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}}}\,,\quad\sin\theta_{w}=\frac{g^{\prime}}{\sqrt{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}}}\,.

(48)

The field tensor term in the Lagrangian (
47
) has its
canonical form, so the
(squared) spacetime derivatives of the gauge boson fields have
canonical normalisation. The gauge boson masses are then extracted
from the gauge-covariant Higgs kinetic term (
14
) by
setting the Higgs field equal to its vacuum value
\phi=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}v\right)
, and ignoring the derivatives of

\phi
. The two contributions (without their prefactors) are

\displaystyle\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}D^{\mu}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{8}\left[g^{2}(A_{\mu}^{1})^{2}+g^{2}(A_{\mu}^{2})^{2}+(gA_{\mu}^{3}-g^{\prime}B_{\mu})^{2}\right]\,,

\displaystyle(\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}\,\phi)(\bar{\phi}D^{\mu}\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{4}}{16}(gA_{\mu}^{3}-g^{\prime}B_{\mu})^{2}\,,

(49)

(where
(A_{\mu}^{1})^{2}
is shorthand for
A_{\mu}^{1}A^{1\mu}
,
etc.). The second of these does not appear at all in the Standard Model
Lagrangian. It is the term denoted
{\cal Q}_{\phi D}

[
8
]
or

{\cal O}_{\phi D}

[
9
]
in the classification of dimension-6
SMEFT operators, and denoted
{\cal O}_{HD}

[
10
,
11
]

in the analysis of constraints on their coefficients.

As usual, we define the
Z
-boson field and photon field to be

Z_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}}}(gA_{\mu}^{3}-g^{\prime}B_{\mu})\,,\quad A_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}}}(g^{\prime}A_{\mu}^{3}+gB_{\mu})\,,

(50)

or equivalently

Z_{\mu}=\cos\theta_{w}\,A_{\mu}^{3}-\sin\theta_{w}\,B_{\mu}\,,\quad A_{\mu}=\sin\theta_{w}\,A_{\mu}^{3}+\cos\theta_{w}\,B_{\mu}\,.

(51)

The
W
-boson fields are
W_{\mu}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{\mu}^{1}\mp A_{\mu}^{2})
. It is not surprising that both gauge-boson mass
terms (
49
) have no contribution from the photon field.
The photon remains massless because of the unbroken
U(1)_{\rm em}
symmetry.

The combined gauge-boson mass term, including the prefactors, is

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}}{\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\right)^{2}}\biggl[\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\right)\frac{v^{2}}{8}\left[g^{2}(A_{\mu}^{1})^{2}+g^{2}(A_{\mu}^{2})^{2}+(g^{2}+g^{\prime 2})Z_{\mu}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{v^{4}}{16}(g^{2}+g^{\prime 2})Z_{\mu}^{2}\biggr].

(52)

v^{4}Z_{\mu}^{2}
cancels here, and what remains can be reexpressed as

\frac{1}{2}\frac{M^{2}}{\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{gv}{2}\right)^{2}\left[\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\right)((A_{\mu}^{1})^{2}+(A_{\mu}^{2})^{2})+\frac{M^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta_{w}}Z_{\mu}^{2}\right].

(53)

From this we read off the gauge boson masses

m_{W}=\frac{M}{\sqrt{M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}}}\left(\frac{gv}{2}\right)\,,\quad m_{Z}=\frac{M^{2}}{\left(M^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\right)}\left(\frac{gv}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{w}}\,.

(54)

From here on, we work to leading order in
\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}
, as our
estimates below show that this quantity is less than
0.01
. The
masses become

m_{W}=\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{4M^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{gv}{2}\right)\,,\quad m_{Z}=\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{gv}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{w}}\,.

(55)

The Higgs vev
v
can be expressed in terms of the conventional

v_{\rm SM}=246.22
GeV to match the
Z
-boson mass formula in
the Standard Model. To do this, we define

v_{\rm SM}=\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)v

(56)

and find

m_{W}=\left(1+\frac{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{4M^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{gv_{\rm SM}}{2}\right)\,,\quad m_{Z}=\left(\frac{gv_{\rm SM}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\cos\theta_{w}}\,.

(57)

The key result here is that the squared
W
-boson to
Z
-boson mass
relation is

m_{W}^{2}=\left(1+\frac{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)m_{Z}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{w}\,.

(58)

This is a small modification of the Standard Model tree level relation

m_{W}^{2}=m_{Z}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{w}
in the direction of increasing
the
W
-boson mass towards the
Z
-boson mass.

There are SM loop corrections to this tree level relation, but we can
assume that these are essentially the same in the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT. The

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT therefore predicts a BSM “new physics” factor

\rho_{0}=1+\frac{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{2M^{2}}\,.

(59)

Current data
[
7
]
gives
\rho_{0}=1.00031\pm 0.00019
if
the combined CERN and Fermilab D0 result of 80.360 GeV for the

W
-boson mass is used, i.e. slightly higher than the SM prediction of

\rho_{0}=1
but of little statistical significance.
Any BSM correction to the SM is therefore small, but appears to
be in the direction of an increase of the
W
-boson mass relative to
the
Z
-boson mass, as we find in the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT. At a confidence
level of
2\sigma
,
\rho_{0}<1.00031+0.00038=1.00069
and
formula (
59
) implies that with the same confidence, the

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT requires
M>27\,v_{\rm SM}\simeq 6.6
TeV.

The Fermilab CDF result for the
W
-boson mass, based on a recent
reanalysis of older data
[
12
]
, is 80.434 GeV, with an
error estimate making this value
5\sigma
higher than the combined CERN/D0
result and
7\sigma
higher than the SM prediction. If we use this,
then
\rho_{0}\simeq 1.002
, and from (
59
)
we deduce that
M\simeq 4
TeV. Within our model, this estimate for

M
is not ruled out by the Yukawa couplings, to be discussed below.

9.2
Higgs-boson mass and couplings

Here we consider the terms of the Lagrangian involving the Higgs field
alone, leaving out couplings of the Higgs field to the gauge potentials
and fermions.

In the SM, these terms are

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}=\overline{\partial_{\mu}\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{\rm SM}^{2}-\overline{\phi}\phi\right)^{2}\,,

(60)

and in unitary gauge

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,v_{\rm SM}+H)\,,

(61)

where
v_{\rm SM}
is the SM Higgs vev and
H
the real Higgs boson
field. Expanding out, we obtain

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}H\partial^{\mu}H-\lambda v_{\rm SM}^{2}H^{2}-\lambda v_{\rm SM}H^{3}-\frac{1}{4}\lambda H^{4}\,.

(62)

A standard notation for this is

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}H\partial^{\mu}H-\frac{1}{2}m_{H}^{2}H^{2}-\frac{1}{6}\lambda_{3}H^{3}-\frac{1}{24}\lambda_{4}H^{4}\,,

(63)

so
m_{H}^{2}=2\lambda v_{\rm SM}^{2}
is the Higgs boson mass,

\lambda_{3}=6\lambda v_{\rm SM}=3\frac{m_{H}^{2}}{v_{\rm SM}}
is the
cubic Higgs coupling, and
\lambda_{4}=6\lambda=3\frac{m_{H}^{2}}{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}

is the quartic Higgs coupling. As
m_{H}
and
v_{\rm SM}
are precisely
known, the cubic and quartic Higgs couplings have precisely predicted
SM values.

In the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT, terms in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}
acquire an additional
denominator factor
(1+\frac{\overline{\phi}\phi}{M^{2}})^{2}
. Working
to leading order, i.e. up to dimension 6 terms, we can replace
this by a numerator factor
(1-2\frac{\overline{\phi}\phi}{M^{2}})
.
Then, going to unitary gauge and writing

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,v+h)\,,

(64)

we find from the kinetic and potential contributions (
14
)
and (
17
) that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}-\frac{2v}{M^{2}}h-\frac{1}{M^{2}}h^{2}\right)\partial_{\mu}h\partial^{\mu}h

(65)

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}-\frac{2v}{M^{2}}h-\frac{1}{M^{2}}h^{2}\right)(4v^{2}h^{2}+4vh^{3}+h^{4}).

Note that this includes terms where
\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}h\partial^{\mu}h
is
multiplied by
h
and
h^{2}
. These are small, momentum dependent corrections
to the cubic and quartic Higgs couplings. From now on, we drop these,
leaving, after collecting terms,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\partial_{\mu}h\partial^{\mu}h-\lambda v^{2}\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)h^{2}

(66)

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda v\left(1-\frac{3v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)h^{3}-\frac{1}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{13v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)h^{4}+O(h^{5})\,.

We need to rescale
h
to reproduce the canonical kinetic term for the
Higgs boson field. So we define

H=\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)h

(67)

and obtain (to leading order in
\frac{v^{2}}{M^{2}}
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}H\partial^{\mu}H-\lambda v^{2}H^{2}

(68)

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda v\left(1-\frac{3v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)H^{3}-\frac{1}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{11v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)H^{4}\,.

m_{H}^{2}=2\lambda v^{2}
is therefore the Higgs boson mass, and we can eliminate

\lambda
in favour of
m_{H}
in the cubic and quartic terms to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Higgs}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}H\partial^{\mu}H-\lambda v^{2}H^{2}

(69)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{m_{H}^{2}}{2v}\left(1-\frac{3v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)H^{3}-\frac{m_{H}^{2}}{8v^{2}}\left(1-\frac{11v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)H^{4}\,.

Writing this again in the form (
63
), we read off that

\lambda_{3}=\frac{3m_{H}^{2}}{v}\left(1-\frac{3v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad\lambda_{4}=\frac{3m_{H}^{2}}{v^{2}}\left(1-\frac{11v^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\,.

(70)

To compare with the SM couplings we need to make the transformation
(
56
) from
v
to
v_{\rm SM}
. To leading order,

\lambda_{3}=\frac{3m_{H}^{2}}{v_{\rm SM}}\left(1-\frac{2v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad\lambda_{4}=\frac{3m_{H}^{2}}{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{12v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\,.

(71)

Then, writing these couplings as
[
13
]

\lambda_{3}=\frac{3m_{H}^{2}}{v_{\rm SM}}(1+\kappa_{3})\quad{\rm and}\quad\lambda_{4}=\frac{3m_{H}^{2}}{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}(1+\kappa_{4})

(72)

we read off the BSM modifications

\kappa_{3}=-\frac{2v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{M^{2}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\kappa_{4}=-\frac{12v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,.

(73)

The
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT therefore predicts that both these parameters are
negative and in the ratio
\kappa_{4}=6\kappa_{3}
.

Unfortunately, the cubic and quartic Higgs couplings are not yet
determined experimentally. There are only weak upper bounds on
their strengths. In future, using the HL-LHC at CERN, it is hoped that
these couplings can be measured and compared with SM predictions.
Even then, the small modifications
\kappa_{3}
and
\kappa_{4}
to the
SM predictions will probably remain out of reach. So these couplings
give no useful constraint on
M
. However, the discussion here has
clarified the need for the rescaling of the Higgs boson field
h
as
well as the Higgs vev
v
. These rescalings have an important effect
on the Yukawa couplings, which we calculate next.

9.3
Higgs-fermion couplings

The
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT modifies the SM relationship between fermion
masses and the coupling of fermions to the Higgs boson. As we will
see, this modification is by a universal factor for all fermion
types. The coupling controls both the production and decay strengths
for the Higgs boson, and has been accurately measured for the three
fermions of the third generation – the t-quark and b-quark, and the

\tau
-lepton. Currently, the SM relationship is confirmed, within
experimental uncertainties, so this places limits on BSM
modifications, and hence a lower bound on
M
in the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT.

The kinetic terms for fermions have their canonical form in
{\mathcal{L}}
, so
we need only investigate the Yukawa terms. To determine the fermion
masses and coupling we need to retain only the terms up to linear
order in the Higgs field
H
. As before, we convert the denominator factor in

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Yukawa}
to a numerator factor and obtain for any fermion
f
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Yukawa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Lambda_{f}\overline{f}f\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{2M^{2}}-\frac{v}{M^{2}}h-\frac{1}{2M^{2}}h^{2}\right)(v+h)

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Lambda_{f}\overline{f}f\left(\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)v+\left(1-\frac{3v^{2}}{2M^{2}}\right)h+O(h^{2})\right).

(75)

The mass of the fermion
f

m_{f}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Lambda_{f}v_{\rm SM}\quad{\rm and}\quad y_{f}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Lambda_{f}\left(1-\frac{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\,,

\kappa_{f}\equiv\frac{y_{f}}{m_{f}}v_{\rm SM}=1-\frac{v_{\rm SM}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,,

10
Conclusions

We have proposed a highly constrained SMEFT, where the Higgs field
takes its value in the complex projective plane
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
rather than in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
This is a truly nonlinear modification of the Higgs sector of the Standard
Model. The Lagrangian uses the Fubini–Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, and makes
some use of its
SU(3)
symmetry. The only new parameter is a BSM mass scale

M
, and the modification of the Standard Model is small provided
M\gg v
, with
v
the Higgs vev. The proposed model is fully consistent
with
U(2)
gauge invariance; however, the custodial
SO(4)

global symmetry of the Higgs sector of the Standard Model is mildly
broken, because the
U(2)
orbits on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
are squashed
rather than round 3-spheres. The key prediction is that, as a
consequence of this squashed geometry, the
W
-boson mass is
closer to the
Z
-boson mass than in the Standard Model, while the
weak mixing angle
\theta_{w}
remains unchanged. Yukawa couplings of
the Higgs boson to fermions are also modified by a universal factor.
Almost all current data are consistent with the Standard Model, so, as
for other BSM scenarios, there is no compelling evidence yet in
favour of the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT. The data place a lower bound of O(7 TeV)
on
M
, unless the CDF measurement of the
W
-boson mass turns out
to be correct, in which case some physics beyond the Standard Model
would be needed to explain this, and within the
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT,

M
would be about 4 TeV. More precise and consistent data for the
gauge boson masses, Yukawa couplings and the Higgs boson self-couplings
would all be helpful.

The
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT has no obvious UV-completion. Possibly, it can be
extended to a UV-complete theory where the
SU(3)
symmetry is
dynamically realised and spontaneously broken to
U(1)
in two steps,
but that may require several additional gauge and Higgs fields, and additional
fermions too. The simplest idea would be to first break the
SU(3)
symmetry
with a complex triplet Higgs field, but the unbroken gauge group would
then be
SU(2)
rather than
U(2)
, and the projective space
\mathbb{CP}_{2}

would not appear. There are several proposed unified models with
gauge group
SU(3)
(or products of
SU(3)
with another group) acting
linearly on multiple Higgs fields, e.g.
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
These models mostly have substantially more gauge bosons, scalar
particles and fermions than the Standard Model, some with exotic
charges. These models do not seem to be equivalent to, or extensions of, the

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
SMEFT proposed here, although the gauge boson mass formulae in

[
18
]
are very similar to our formulae (
55
).

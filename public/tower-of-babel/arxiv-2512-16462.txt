Distributions of Integral Points and Dedekind Zeta Values
Li Cai, Taiwang Deng

Abstract
. We study the distribution of integral matrices with a fixed characteristic polynomial. When the polynomial is irreducible, we determine the leading term of the distribution in terms of zeta functions of orders. The proof is based on an equidistribution property of orbits, a reformulation of the distribution into orbital integrals via the strong approximation with Brauer-Manin obstruction and the Tate-Nakayama duality, endoscopic fundamental lemma, and a critical input that links orbital integrals to residues of zeta functions of orders.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a number field with the ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}
. Let
X
be a variety over
{\mathcal{O}}
.
We consider the asymptotic behavior of
N(X,T)
as
T\rightarrow\infty
. Here,

N(X,T)=\#\Big\{x\in X({\mathcal{O}})\Big|||x||\leq T\Big\}

and
||\cdot||
is certain norm on
X(F)
.

We shall focus on the following case. Let
\chi(x)\in{\mathcal{O}}[x]
be a monic polynomial of degree
n
. Denote by
X
the variety
which represents the set of
n\times n
matrices with characteristic polynomial
\chi(x)
.
In other words, for any
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra
R
,

X(R)=\left\{A\in M_{n\times n}(R)\Big|\det(xI_{n}-A)=\chi(x)\right\}.

We consider the following norm on
M_{n\times n}(F)

||x||=\max_{v|\infty}||x||_{v},\quad||x||_{v}=\left(\sum_{i,j}^{n}|x_{ij}|_{v}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Here, we normalize the absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
on
F_{v}
such that if
F_{v}={\mathbb{R}}
, then it is the usual one and if
F_{v}={\mathbb{C}}
, then
it is the square of the usual one.

Assume
\chi(x)
has distinct roots in any algebraic closure of
F
.
Using the ergodic theory, it is known that there
exists a positive real number
C(\chi)
such that (See
[
27
, Theorem 1.5]
)

\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N(X,T)}{C(\chi)T^{d_{\infty}d}(\log T)^{a+\ell-1}}=1.

In the above

•

d=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.

•

d_{\infty}
is the number of archimedean places of
F
(Note
that our definition of the absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
for complex
v

is the square of the usual one).

•

\chi(x)=\chi_{0}(x)\chi_{1}(x)\cdots\chi_{a}(x)
where

\chi_{0}(x)
is completely reducible with degree
a
and

\chi_{i}(x)
,
1\leq i\leq a
are irreducible with
\deg(\chi_{i})>1
.

It is a natural question to determine the constant

C(\chi)
in terms of arithmetic invariants.

In the following, we shall focus on the case
\chi(x)
is irreducible over
F
. Then
K=F[x]/(\chi(x))
is a number field
and
R={\mathcal{O}}[x]/(\chi(x))
is an order in
K
. Here, an (
{\mathcal{O}}
-)order in
K
is a finitely generated
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra
R
in
K

with
R\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F=K
.

There are already many important works in this direction.

Assume
F={\mathbb{Q}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=R
. Then it is a theorem of
Eskin-Mozes-Shah
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
and Shah
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
that

C(\chi)={\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s)\cdot\frac{w_{n}}{\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Lambda(k)}.

Here,
w_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\Lambda(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
is the complete Riemann zeta function.

For general
F
and irreducible
\chi(x)
, the work of Wei-Xu
[
24
]
relates
N(X,T)
to certain local integrals indexed
by the Brauer group of
X
. In particular, they give the
difference between
N(X,T)
and an Euler product. Their work
reduces the determination of
C(\chi)
into a local problem.

Recently, Jeon-Lee
[
13
]
and Lee
[
17
]
observe that these local integrals are

\kappa
-orbital integrals on
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}
when
\chi(0)=1
.
They also derive some explicit formulas in special circumstance (
See Remark
1.3
for their conditions).
In general, it is not expected to give an explicit formula for these

\kappa
-orbital integrals even if we can relate them to stable orbital integrals on endoscopic subgroups via the
celebrated fundamental lemma.

In this paper, we take a different point of view, relating the constant
C(\chi)

to the zeta function
\zeta_{R}
for the order
R
(See
[
26
]
)

\zeta_{R}(s)=\sum_{M\subset R^{\vee}}\left(\#R^{\vee}/M\right)^{-s}

where
M
runs over all nonzero
R
-modules contained in
R^{\vee}={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(R,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
When
R={\mathcal{O}}_{K}
,
\zeta_{R}
is the usual Dedekind zeta function.
The introduction of these zeta functions enable us to determine
C(\chi)
for any elliptic conjugacy classes.

Similar to the Dedekind zeta function, the function
\zeta_{R}

admits a meromorphic continuation to the whole
s
-plane, with the residue formula (
[
26
, Theorem 1.2]
)

{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{R}(s)=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}\#({\mathrm{Cl}}(R))R_{K}}{w_{K}\sqrt{|D_{R}|}}\sum_{[M]\in{\mathrm{Cl}}(R)\backslash\overline{{\mathrm{Cl}}}(R)}\frac{\#({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\times}/{\mathrm{Aut}}(M))}{\#{\mathrm{Stab}}_{{\mathrm{Cl}}(R)}([M])}.

Here,

•

r_{1},r_{2}
are the numbers of real and complex places of
K
,
R_{K}
is the regulator of
K
,
w_{K}
is the cardinality of
roots of the unity in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.

•

A fractional
R
-ideal is a finitely generated
R
-submodule
M
in
K
such that
M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}F=K
.
The group
K^{\times}
acts on the set of fractional
R
-ideals
X_{R}
and the set of invertible fractional
R
-ideals
X_{R}^{\circ}
.
Denote by
\overline{{\mathrm{Cl}}}(R)=K^{\times}\backslash X_{R}
the set of
K^{\times}
-homothety classes of fractional
R
-ideals and

{\mathrm{Cl}}(R)=K^{\times}\backslash X_{R}^{\circ}
the usual ideal class group of
R
. Then there is an action of
{\mathrm{Cl}}(R)
on
\overline{{\mathrm{Cl}}}(R)
.

•

For a fractional
R
-ideal
M
, denote by
{\mathrm{Aut}}(M)=\left\{x\in K^{\times}|xM=M\right\}
.

•

The trace pairing
(x,y)\mapsto{\mathrm{tr}}_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(xy)
identifies
K
and the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear dual of
K
. In particular,
the dual
R^{\vee}
is a fractional
R
-ideal in
K
contains
R
. Denote by
D_{R}=\#(R^{\vee}/R)
the absolute discriminant
of
R
.

When
F={\mathbb{Q}}
, our result is the following one.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
F={\mathbb{Q}}
. Then

C(\chi)={\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{R}(s)\cdot\frac{w_{n}}{\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Lambda(k)}.

For general
F
, we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\chi(x)\in{\mathcal{O}}[x]
be irreducible. Then

C(\chi)=\sum_{E}\phi([E:F])\delta(E){\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{R_{E}}(s)\cdot\frac{w_{n}}{|\Delta_{F}|^{d}\,\pi^{nr_{2}}\,{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\Lambda_{F}(s)\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Lambda_{F}(k)}.

Here,

•

E
runs over fields
F\subset E\subset K
such that

E/F
is cyclic and unramified everywhere (including the archimedean places). The function
\phi
is the Euler totient function.

•

\delta(E)=\prod_{v<\infty}\delta_{v}(E)
with
\delta_{v}(E)=[{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{v}}^{1}:{\mathcal{O}}_{E_{v}}^{1}\cap{\mathrm{N}}_{K_{v}/E_{v}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{v}}^{\times}]

(See Lemma
7.6
for notations). If
E=F
or
K/E
is unramified, then
\delta(E)=1
.

•

For each
E
,
R_{E}={\mathcal{O}}_{E}[x]/(\chi_{E}(x))
where
\chi_{E}(x)\in{\mathcal{O}}_{E}[x]
is an irreducible factor of
\chi(x)

in
E
. Then
R_{E}
is an order in
K
.

•

w_{n}=\prod_{v|\infty}w_{n,v}
with
w_{n,v}
the volume for the unit ball

B_{n,v}=\left\{(x_{j})\in F_{v}^{d}\Big|\sum_{j}|x_{j}|_{v}^{2}\leq 1\right\}.

•

d=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
and
r_{2}
is the number of complex
places of
F
.

•

\Lambda_{F}(s)=\prod_{v|\infty}\Gamma_{v}(s)\xi_{F}(s)
is the complete Dedekind zeta function for
F
. Here,
if
v
is real, then
\Gamma_{v}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
and if
v
is complex, then
\Gamma_{v}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
.

Remark 1.3
.

Leaving aside zeta functions for orders, the above theorem is obtained in
[
13
]
and
[
17
]
for
K/F
unramified everywhere under the
condition that

•

\chi(0)=1
or
\chi(x)
splits over
F_{v}
at those
p
-adic places

of
F

for

;

•

K/F
is a cyclic extension with prime degree.

In particular, under this condition, the only two choices of

are

and

.

Remark 1.4
.

The result is independent on the choice of
\chi_{E}
for each
E
. In fact, two different such
\chi_{E}
are Galois
conjugate to each other and their corresponding orders
R_{E}
are also Galois conjugate to each other. In particular,
two such orders share the same zeta function.

The proof can be sketched as following. Denote by
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{n}
and
\widetilde{G}={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
. Also denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{n}
and

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}={\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{n}
their Lie algebras. Fix
\gamma\in\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}({\mathcal{O}})
with
characteristic polynomial
\chi(x)
. We consider the conjugation action of
\widetilde{G}
and also its subgroup
G
on
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}
.
Denote by
T
and
\widetilde{T}
the stabilizer of
\gamma
in
G
and
\widetilde{G}
respectively. Then
X=T\backslash G=\widetilde{T}\backslash\widetilde{G}
.

(1)

Consider the action of
\Gamma=G(F)\cap\mathrm{St}(X)
on
X({\mathcal{O}})
. Here,
\mathrm{St}(X)

is a compact subgroup of
G({\mathbb{A}})
stabilizing
X({\mathcal{O}}_{v})
for each
v<\infty
. By the important equi-distribution property
established by Eskin-Mozes-Shah
[
6
]
, the contribution of each
\Gamma
-orbit in

is given
by some (product of) local integrals (See Theorem
4.1
): for any
\gamma^{\prime}\in X({\mathcal{O}})

\#\left\{y\in\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\Gamma\Big|||y||\leq T\right\}\sim\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}}(\Gamma\cap T_{\gamma^{\prime}}(F)\backslash T_{\gamma^{\prime}}(F_{\infty}))}{{\mathrm{Vol}}(\Gamma\backslash G(F_{\infty}))}{\mathrm{Vol}}\left(\left\{x\in\gamma^{\prime}\cdot G(F_{\infty})\Big|||x||\leq T\right\}\right).

Here, the notation
A(T)\sim B(T)
means that

\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{A(T)}{B(T)}=1.

(2)

Using basic properties of the Brauer-Manin obstruction on
X
, the decomposition of

via
the
\Gamma
-orbits of

can be rewritten via the Brauer group
of

(See Theorem

). This step is given by Wei-Xu
[
24
]
. Moreover, by the Tate-Nakayama duality,

{\mathrm{Br}}(X)/{\mathrm{Br}}(F)=H^{1}(F,T({\mathbb{A}}_{\bar{F}})/T(\bar{F}))^{\vee}.

In fact, the local integrals in (1) are exactly the (generalized)
\kappa
-orbital integrals for the conjugation action of

on
\widetilde{G}

where
\kappa
is a character on

). In other words,

N(X,T)\sim\sum_{\kappa}{\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}^{\kappa}(f^{T})

where
f^{T}=f_{\infty}^{T}\otimes 1_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}})}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}({\mathbb{A}}))
is certain test function on
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}({\mathbb{A}})
.

(3)

Note that the above
\kappa
-orbital integrals are in fact certain twisted orbital integrals on
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}

(See Proposition
7.1
).
By the fundamental lemma (See Theorem
7.4
)
relating the twisted orbital integrals on
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}
to orbital integrals
on the Lie algebra of its endoscopic subgroups given by Waldspurger
[
22
]
and other peoples, for each

,

{\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}^{\kappa}(f^{T})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{=}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma_{H}}(f_{H}^{T}).

Here,
H
is the endoscopic subgroup of

given by

and

is
the usual orbital integral on

•

H={\mathrm{Res}}_{E/F}^{1}{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}
and
\widetilde{H}={\mathrm{Res}}_{E/F}{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}
where
E/F
is unramified everywhere and
m[E:F]=n
. Denote by
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{h}}}={\mathrm{Res}}_{E/F}{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{m}

the Lie algebra of
\widetilde{H}
.

•

the test function

f_{H}^{T}=f_{H,\infty}^{T}\otimes 1_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{h}}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}})}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{{\mathfrak{h}}}({\mathbb{A}}))
.

(4)

Finally, we have the following relation (See Theorem
8.1
)
between zeta functions and orbital integrals given by Yun
[
26
]

\frac{{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{R}(s)}{{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s)}=\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}(1_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}})})}{[{\mathcal{O}}_{K}:R]}.

By applying such relations to each endoscopic subgroups of

, we obtain the main result.

The above steps (1,2,3) are inspired by, and run parallel to, the proofs in
[
13
,

]
. Note that there is the condition that

in

.
To remove this condition, we replace this action to the conjugation action of

on

the corresponding (generalized)

-orbital integrals are exactly certain twisted orbital integrals on

which can also be

The zeta functions in step (4) have a closed relation with the theory of beyond endoscopy

In the strategy, certain kind of Poisson summation formula should be applied to the elliptic part of the trace formula. For this, as observed by Arthur, one may study analytic behaviours of orbital integrals by realizing them as residues of these zeta functions.

2.
Endoscopy for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}
and twisted endoscopy for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}

In this section, we firstly recall the ordinary endoscopy for general quasi-split groups. Then we determine that for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}
. We also recall the relation between endoscopy for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}
and twisted endoscopy for
({\mathrm{GL}}_{n},\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon

a character on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.

Let
F
be a number field with
\Gamma={\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{F}/F)
the absolute Galois group.
Let
G
be a quasi-split connected reductive group over a number field
F
.
Following
[
14
]
,
an endoscopic triple
(H,s,\eta)
consists of

•

a quasi-split connected reductive group
H
;

•

an embedding
\eta:\widehat{H}\rightarrow\widehat{G}
, where
\widehat{H}
and
\widehat{G}
are the connected Langlands dual groups of
H

and
G
defined over
\bar{F}
;

•

an element
s\in\left(Z(\widehat{H})/Z(\widehat{G})\right)^{\Gamma}
.

subject to the conditions

•

\widehat{H}
is identified with the connected component of the centralizer of
\eta(s)
in
\widehat{G}
;

•

the
\widehat{G}
-conjugacy class of
\eta
is stable under the action of
\Gamma
given by

\sigma\eta=\sigma_{\widehat{G}}\circ\eta\circ\sigma_{\widehat{H}}^{-1},\quad\sigma\in\Gamma;

•

s
lifts to
Z(\widehat{H})^{\Gamma_{v}}
for each place
v
. Here,
\Gamma_{v}={\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{F_{v}}/F_{v})
.

Let
\gamma\in G(F)
be elliptic semi-simple. Denote by

the stabilizer of
\gamma
in
G
. Consider the cohomology

H^{1}(F,T(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{F}})/T(\overline{F})).

Here,
{\mathbb{A}}_{\bar{F}}={\mathbb{A}}\otimes_{F}\bar{F}
is the direct limit of

where

are extensions of number fields.
From
[
15

, for each

\kappa\in H^{1}(F,T(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{F}})/T(\overline{F}))^{\vee},

there exists an endoscopic triple
(H,s,\eta)
and an element
\gamma_{H}\in H(F)
so that
((H,s,\eta),\gamma_{H})
corresponds to

(\gamma,\kappa)
. Moreover, such
((H,s,\eta),\gamma_{H})
are unique in a suitable sense. Here the element

\gamma_{H}
can be defined as the pre-image of
\gamma
under an admissible isomorphism

(cf.
[
14
, Section 3.4]
), where

is an
F
-maximal torus in

. Such admissible isomorphisms are unique.

In the following, denote by
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{n}
and
\widetilde{G}={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.

For our application, we will also need the twisted version of the above definition of endoscopy data
[
16
, (2.1.1)-(2.1.4)]
for
(\widetilde{G},\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
is a character on
\widetilde{G}(F_{v})
or
\widetilde{G}({\mathbb{A}}_{F})
.
However, in our situation as is explained in
[
12
, Page 3]
, an endoscopic

(\widetilde{H},\widetilde{s},\widetilde{\eta})
for

is naturally related to

(H,s,\eta)
for

through restriction and vice versa. In fact, as remarked in

[

, Page 4]
, in the most general case under consideration,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}\cong{\mathrm{Res}}_{E/F}{\mathrm{GL}}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{Res}}_{E/F}{\mathrm{GL}}_{a_{r}},

For this reason and to avoid further complication, in the rest of the section we restrict ourselves to consider the endoscopy for

.

Let
\gamma\in G(F)
be elliptic semi-simple.
In other words,
\gamma
generates a field

the stabilizer of

Proposition 2.1
.

There is a one-to-one map between the characters
H^{1}(F,T(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{F}})/T(\overline{F}))^{\vee}
and
the pairs
(E,u)
where

•

is an intermediate field

•

.

Moreover, if a character

, then for the map from
(\gamma,\kappa)
to

((H,s,\eta),\gamma_{H})
, we have

•

with

.

•

we view

as a vector space over

as an element in

Proof.

By the Tate-Nakayama-Kottwitz duality, there is a canonical identification

(2.1)

H^{1}(F,\,T_{\gamma}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{F}})/T_{\gamma}(\overline{F}))^{\vee}\;\simeq\;\pi_{0}\!\left(\widehat{T_{\gamma}}^{\Gamma}\right),

where
\widehat{T_{\gamma}}
is the complex dual torus. It further follows from
[
17
, Remark 3.7]

and
[
17
, (6.4)]
that

(2.2)

H^{1}(F,\,T_{\gamma}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{F}})/T_{\gamma}(\overline{F}))^{\vee}\;\simeq\;\mathrm{Pic}(T_{\gamma})\;\simeq\;\ker\left({\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathrm{Gal}}(L/K),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\right)

where
L
is the Galois closure of
K
.
In particular, (
2.2
) implies that
we can identify an element

\kappa\in H^{1}(F,T_{\gamma}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{F}})/T_{\gamma}(\overline{F}))^{\vee}
with
a character
\chi_{\kappa}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

\chi_{\kappa}\mid_{{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/K)}
is trivial.
Therefore by the Galois theory,

determines a cyclic extension

of

of degree

. The fact
\chi_{\kappa}\mid_{{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/K)}=1
implies that

.
Hence we have

.

Next we explain how to construct the

(H,s,\eta)
from

.
The idea is to firstly determine

Note that since

is anisotropic, the group

is finite. In particular, we have

\pi_{0}(\widehat{T_{\gamma}}^{\Gamma})=\widehat{T_{\gamma}}^{\Gamma}
.
By (

) the character

corresponds to a semisimple element

Let us assume that the character
\kappa\in{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F),{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
factors through the cyclic group
\langle\theta\rangle
such that
\kappa(\theta)=\zeta

•

Lemma 7.5

8.
Orbital integrals and Dedekind zeta values

We review the relation between Dedekind zeta values and orbital integrals.

Let
F
be a number field. Denote by
\widetilde{G}={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, viewed as an algebraic group over
F
and
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}={\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{n}
be its Lie algebra. Let
\gamma\in\widetilde{G}(F)
be elliptic with the characteristic polynomial
\chi(x)\in{\mathcal{O}}[x]
. Denote
by
K=F[x]/(\chi(x))
and
R={\mathcal{O}}[x]/(\chi(x))
an order of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. Denote by
\zeta_{R}(s)
the zeta function associated to

R
.

Theorem 8.1

(Yun)
.

We have

\frac{{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{R}(s)}{{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s)}=\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}(1_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}})})}{[{\mathcal{O}}_{K}:R]}.

Proof.

This follows from the first part of
proof in
[
26
, Theorem 1.2]
. In
[
26
]
, the global base field
F
is assumed to be
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In fact, the proof
is valid for arbitrary number fields. For the convenience of the readers, we produce the main steps.

Let
R=\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
. For each
v<\infty
, Yun introduces the local zeta factor

J_{R_{v}}(s)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\#\mathrm{Quot}_{R_{v}^{\vee}}^{j}q_{v}^{-js}

where
q_{v}
is the cardinality of the residue field of
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
,
R_{v}^{\vee}=\{x\in K_{v}|{\mathrm{tr}}(c_{v}^{-1}xy)\in{\mathcal{O}}_{v},\forall y\in R\}
with
c_{v}
a generator of the different ideal of
K_{v}/F_{v}
and
\mathrm{Quot}_{R_{v}^{\vee}}^{j}

is the set of fractional
R_{v}
-ideals
M\subseteq R_{v}^{\vee}
such that
R_{v}^{\vee}/M
is of length
j
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{v}

Now it follows from
[
26
, Theorem 2.5 (2)]
that

J_{R_{v}}(s)=[{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{v}}:R_{v}]^{-s}\zeta_{K_{v}}(s)\widetilde{J}_{R_{v}}(s)

such that
\widetilde{J}_{R_{v}}(1)={\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}(1_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{v})})
. (Note that our
notation
[{\mathcal{O}}_{K_{v}}:R_{v}]=\#({\mathcal{O}}_{K_{v}}/R_{v})

is different from the one in
[
26
]
.)

Finally, we define

\zeta_{R}(s)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}J_{R_{v}}(s)=\sum_{M\subseteq R^{\vee}}(\#R^{\vee}/M)^{-s}

with
R^{\vee}={\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(R,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
It follows that we have

\zeta_{R}(s)=[{\mathcal{O}}_{K}:R]^{-s}\zeta_{K}(s)\prod_{v}\widetilde{J}_{v}(s)

which implies

{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{R}(s)=[{\mathcal{O}}_{K}:R]^{-1}{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=1}\zeta_{K}(s){\mathcal{O}}_{\gamma}(1_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}})}).

∎

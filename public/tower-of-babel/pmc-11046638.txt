Cognitive patterns in immune-mediated inflammatory diseases compared with age-matched controls: a systematic review and meta-analyses
Authors: Task Toyoda
Published: 2024

Background

Neuroinflammation is a key mechanism in the development of dementia.1, 2 The risk of developing cognitive decline is of great interest for patients exposed to prolonged inflammation. Studies have attempted to characterise and compare the cognitive ability of those with immune-mediated inflammatory diseases (IMIDs) (systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), psoriasis (PsO)).3, 4, 5, 6 However, due to the differences in age of disease onset, an age-matched comparison is necessary.

Aim

To compare the magnitude of cognitive impairment across the IMIDs against age-matched healthy controls.

Methods

We searched Medline, Embase and PsychInfo databases on 29/05/2021, using a pre-determined search strategy. Two reviewers screened studies that compared cognitive ability against age-matched controls. The standardised mean difference (SMD) of cognitive scores were pooled using random-effects meta-analysis and stratified by IMID types. Meta-regression was used to identify the IMID with the largest impairment in cognitive ability.

Results

In total, 65 IMID groups were included (SLE: 39, RA: 19, axSpA: 1, PsA: 2, PsO: 4), consisting of 3,141 IMID patients and 9,333 controls. People with IMIDs had significant impairments in overall cognition (SMD: −0.57 [95% CI −0.70, −0.43]), complex attention/executive function (SMD −0.57 [95% CI −0.69, −0.44]), memory (SMD −0.55 [95% CI −0.68, −0.43]) and language/verbal fluency (SMD −0.51 [95% CI −0.68, −0.34]). Conclusion

Those with IMIDs have significantly worse cognition across a multitude of domains. People with RA and SLE have similar magnitudes of impairment against their respective age-expected levels, calling for greater recognition of cognitive impairment in both conditions.

Nucleon 3D intrinsic spin structure from the weak-neutral axial-vector form factors
*

Abstract
Relativistic 3D weak-neutral axial-vector four-current and spin distributions inside a nucleon (or a general spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} hadron) including three weak-neutral axial-vector form factors are investigated for the first time. We clarify that the relativistic 3D axial charge distribution in the Breit frame is completely described by the induced pseudotensor form factor G T Z ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{T}^{Z}(Q^{2}) rather than by the axial form factor G A Z ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{A}^{Z}(Q^{2}) . We demonstrate that R A ≡ − 6 G A Z ​ ( 0 ) ​ d ​ G A Z ​ ( Q 2 ) d ​ Q 2 | Q 2 = 0 R_{A}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{-6}{G_{A}^{Z}(0)}\frac{\text{d}G_{A}^{Z}(Q^{2})}{\text{d}Q^{2}}\Big|_{Q^{2}=0}} can not be interpreted as the physically meaningful 3D root-mean-square axial radius of a spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} hadron. The genuine axial radius for any spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} hadron in fact does not exist. We also show that the relativistic 3D weak-neutral spin radius r spin = ⟨ r spin 2 ⟩ r_{\text{spin}}=\sqrt{\langle r_{\text{spin}}^{2}\rangle} , with ⟨ r spin 2 ⟩ ≡ R A 2 + 1 4 ​ M 2 ​ [ 1 + 2 ​ G P Z ​ ( 0 ) G A Z ​ ( 0 ) ] \langle r_{\text{spin}}^{2}\rangle\equiv R_{A}^{2}+\frac{1}{4M^{2}}\left[1+\frac{2G_{P}^{Z}(0)}{G_{A}^{Z}(0)}\right] based on the relativistic and intrinsic 3D weak-neutral spin distribution in the Breit frame, is a physically meaningful radius that can be unambiguously defined for the nucleon, which provides an additional key motivation for the further determination of the induced pseudoscalar form factor G P Z ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{P}^{Z}(Q^{2}) . Numerically, we find that R A ≈ 0.6510 ​ fm R_{A}\approx 0.6510~\text{fm} , r spin ≈ 2.1054 ​ fm r_{\text{spin}}\approx 2.1054~\text{fm} and r ¯ spin ≡ r spin / 3 ≈ 0.7018 ​ fm \overline{r}_{\text{spin}}\equiv r_{\text{spin}}/3\approx 0.7018~\text{fm} . For future experimental measurements of G A Z ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{A}^{Z}(Q^{2}) and G T Z ​ ( Q 2 ) G_{T}^{Z}(Q^{2}) , we also derive the full tree-level unpolarized differential cross sections for neutrino-proton and antineutrino-proton elastic scattering in the lab frame, in hoping to provide a complementary and new perspective to unveil the nucleon spin structure by using (anti)neutrino-based facilities.

Introduction

Spin is a fundamental property of a particle and is of quantum nature. It is closely related to both quantum statistics for particle distributions and the Pauli exclusion principle. As spin-
\frac{1}{2}
composite systems, nucleons (i.e. protons and neutrons) are evidently the key hadrons to study for understanding both quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and the asymmetry between matter and antimatter in the universe, since they are the building blocks of atomic nuclei and are responsible for more than
99\%
of the visible-matter mass in the universe.

Internal structure information of a hadron is encoded in Lorentz-invariant functions, known as form factors (FFs). For example, the weak-neutral axial-vector FFs of a hadron describe how the hadron reacts with the
Z^{0}
boson via the weak neutral current (NC) interaction in an elastic (anti)neutrino (or charged lepton) scattering, encoding therefore clean internal axial charge and spin information of the hadron in the weak sector. Moreover, the axial FF is also closely related to the quark spin (or helicity) contribution to the hadron spin

, playing therefore a crucial role for understanding the hadronic spin structure. When it comes to (anti)neutrino-proton or (anti)neutrino-nucleus elastic scattering

Conclusions and outlook

Relativistic 3D weak-neutral axial-vector four-current and spin distributions inside a nucleon in terms of nucleon’s three weak-neutral axial-vector form factors are investigated for the first time. For future experimental measurements of
G_{A}^{Z}(Q^{2})
and
G_{T}^{Z}(Q^{2})
, we also derive the full tree-level unpolarized differential cross sections for neutrino-proton and antineutrino-proton elastic scattering in the lab frame, in hoping to provide a complementary and new perspective to unveil the nucleon spin structure by using (anti)neutrino-based facilities.

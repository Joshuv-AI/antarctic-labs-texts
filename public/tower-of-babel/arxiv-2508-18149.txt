First-Order LTLf Synthesis with Lookback (Extended Version)
Sarah Winkler

Abstract
Reactive synthesis addresses the problem of generating a controller for a temporal specification in an adversarial environment; it was typically studied for 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{LTL} . Driven by applications ranging from AI to business process management, 𝖫𝖳𝖫 \mathsf{LTL} modulo first order-theories over finite traces ( 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖿 𝖬𝖳 \mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}} ) has recently gained traction, where propositional variables in properties are replaced by first-order constraints. Though reactive synthesis for 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖿 \mathsf{LTL_{f}} with some first-order features has been addressed, existing work in this direction strongly restricts or excludes the possibility to compare variables across instants, a limitation that severely restricts expressiveness and applicability. In this work we present a reactive synthesis procedure for 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖿 𝖬𝖳 \mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}} , where properties support lookback to model cross-instant comparison of variables. Our procedure works for full 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖿 𝖬𝖳 \mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}} with lookback, subsuming the fragments of 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖿 𝖬𝖳 \mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}} for which realizability was studied earlier. However, the setting with cross-instant comparison is inherently highly complex, as realizability is undecidable even over decidable background theories. Hence termination of our approach is in general not guaranteed. Nevertheless, we prove its soundness, and show that it is complete if a bound on the strategy length exists. Finally, we show that our approach constitutes a decision procedure for several relevant fragments of 𝖫𝖳𝖫 𝖿 𝖬𝖳 \mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}} , at once re-proving known decidability results and identifying new decidable classes.



1
Introduction

Reactive synthesis
[
Pnueli and Rosner(1989a)
,
Pnueli and Rosner(1989b)
]
addresses the problem of automatically constructing a system that satisfies a given temporal specification in an adversarial environment. Along with the respective decision problem of realizability, it is most typically considered for
\mathsf{LTL}
, where the input is an
\mathsf{LTL}
property and a splitting of the set of propositional variables into two disjoint sets that are respectively controlled by the environment and the agent. The aim is to find a strategy for the agent such that the given property is satisfied, no matter which values are assigned to the environment variables.
While reactive synthesis was originally considered for
\mathsf{LTL}
over infinite traces, in a recent line of work, efficient algorithms and tools have been developed that target the synthesis problem for
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
, i.e.,
\mathsf{LTL}
over finite traces
[
Giacomo and Vardi(2015)
,
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
,
Xiao et al.(2021)Xiao, Li, Zhu, Shi, Pu, and Vardi
]
.

However, propositional
\mathsf{LTL}
and
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
have limited expressivity that inhibits to realistically model many practical problems. This observation led to the development of synthesis techniques for the logic
\mathsf{LTL}_{\mathcal{T}}
, where propositional atoms are replaced by atoms from a first-order theory
[
Rodríguez and Sánchez(2023)
,
Rodríguez and
Sánchez(2024b)
,
Rodríguez and Sánchez(2024a)
]
. These techniques are based on the compilation of a Boolean abstraction from the first-order problem that can be given to a standard realizability tool after solving some first-order satisfiability problems to ensure that the Boolean abstraction is faithful.
Independently, the logic
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
(
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
modulo theories) has gained traction in the last years
[
Geatti et al.(2022)Geatti, Gianola, and Gigante
,
Gianola et al.(2024)Gianola, Montali, and Winkler
]
, as its versatility admits applications in a variety of areas. This includes verification of infinite-state systems using the MoXI intermediate language
[
Johannsen et al.(2024)Johannsen, Nukala, Dureja, Irfan, Shankar,
Tinelli, Vardi, and Rozier
]
whose semantics can be expressed in
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
, and analysis of business processes
[
Gianola et al.(2024)Gianola, Montali, and Winkler
]
; but
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
also covers languages used for runtime verification such as LoLA
[
D’Angelo et al.(2005)D’Angelo, Sankaranarayanan, Sánchez,
Robinson, Finkbeiner, Sipma, Mehrotra, and Manna
]
, and
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
with arithmetic constraints considered for monitoring
[
Felli et al.(2023)Felli, Montali, Patrizi, and Winkler
]
.

\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
also subsumes the logic
\mathsf{LTL}_{\mathcal{T}}
, but differs from the latter in that variables can be compared across different time instants.
This feature vastly increases expressivity, is crucial to describe the advancement of a dynamic system, and is present in most languages for reasoning over temporal properties that have some first-order flavour, including MoXI, LoLA, the runtime verification language TeSSLa
[
Convent et al.(2018)Convent, Hungerecker, Leucker, Scheffel, Schmitz,
and Thoma
]
, and Data Petri nets, a variant of Petri nets popular in the analysis of business processes
[
Mannhardt et al.(2016)Mannhardt, de Leoni, Reijers, and van der
Aalst
]
.
In this paper we will use the term
lookback
for the feature to compare variables describing the current instant with variables for the previous instant; this is equivalent in expressivity to comparison with variables that refer to (bounded) future instants
[
Felli et al.(2023)Felli, Montali, Patrizi, and Winkler
]
.
However, cross-state comparisons come at the cost that the satisfiability and realizability problems are undecidable even over a benign theory such as linear arithmetic
[
Geatti et al.(2022)Geatti, Gianola, and Gigante
,
Bhaskar and Praveen(2024)
]
.
The following simple example illustrates the realizability problem in such a setting:

Example 1
.

Alice runs a resource-consuming computing task that is supposed to take multiple days in a cloud-based elastic system.
Suppose that at every instant, a rational number
x
describes the resources allocated by the cloud system (the environment), and a rational number
y
holds the resource bound requested by Alice (the agent).
Alice wants to take one day off, so she wants to set a resource bound today that is guaranteed to be larger than the resources required the following day. She estimates that the resource consumption increases from one day to the next by at most 2 units. The aim is to find a strategy for Alice that ensures this property. In
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
, this can be described as
\mathsf{G}(x\geq 0\wedge x{-}{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{x}\leq 2)\to\mathsf{X}({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y>x)
, where
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}x
and
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y
denote the lookback variables that hold the previous values of
x
and
y
, respectively.

In this paper we address, to the best of our knowledge for the first time, the reactive synthesis problem for full
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
with lookback.
Our approach to this end is conceptually simple: we first represent a DFA for the given
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
property
\psi
as an AND-OR-graph
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
where edge labels are suitably split into constraints controlled by the environment and constraints controlled by the agent, following
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
]
. In a second step, we define for every state in
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
a first-order formula that constitutes a winning condition for the agent. The overall winning condition is defined as a fixpoint which need not exist in general; but if it does, it gives rise to a strategy to synthesise
\psi
.

In summary, we provide a three-fold contribution:

(1)

We present a general reactive synthesis procedure for
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
properties with lookback.

(2)

We prove its soundness, and show that it is also complete for
bounded
realizability, i.e., in the case where there is a bound on the length of the strategy.

(3)

Our procedure is shown to be a decision procedure for several fragments of
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
, including the case without lookback; properties over the theory of linear arithmetic where atoms are variable-to-variable/constant comparisons; and properties where variable interaction via lookback is bounded.

The paper is structured as follows: In the remainder of this section we further elaborate on related work. In Sec.
2
we recap
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
and reactive synthesis.
Our synthesis approach is described in Sec.
3
. We outline our decidability results in Sec.
4
, and conclude in Sec.
5
.
Some proofs were moved to an appendix for reasons of space.

Related work.

Reactive synthesis for
\mathsf{LTL}
was pioneered in
[
Pnueli and Rosner(1989a)
,
Pnueli and Rosner(1989b)
]
, triggering a rich body of literature. Below we focus on LTL over finite traces (
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
)
[
De Giacomo and Vardi(2013)
]
, and
\mathsf{LTL}
/
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
over first-order theories. Reactive synthesis approach for
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
was first studied in
[
Giacomo and Vardi(2015)
]
, constructing a doubly-exponential DFA as a game arena upfront. This led to more efficient techniques that construct the DFA on the fly
[
Xiao et al.(2021)Xiao, Li, Zhu, Shi, Pu, and Vardi
]
, also using knowledge compilation via SDDs for compactness
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
]
.
Our approach builds up on their idea to represent a DFA as an AND-OR-graph using sentential decision diagrams (SDDs).
Efficient compositional/hybrid techniques to transform
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
to DFAs were also discussed in
[
Giacomo and Favorito(2021)
,
Bansal et al.(2020)Bansal, Li, Tabajara, and Vardi
]
.
Reactive synthesis for LTL
T
, i.e., LTL/
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
over a first-order theory
\mathcal{T}
but without cross-state comparison, was studied in
[
Rodríguez and Sánchez(2023)
,
Rodríguez and Sánchez(2024a)
,
Rodríguez and
Sánchez(2024b)
]
, and realizability proven decidable for theories with a decidable
\exists^{*}\forall^{*}
fragment.
For
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
, our Thm.
3
covers their decidability result.
A few approaches also investigated reactive synthesis over restricted
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
modulo theories:
Strategy synthesis has been addressed in
[
de Leoni et al.(2020)de Leoni, Felli, and Montali
]
for data-aware dynamic systems where guards are variable-to-variable/constant comparisons over the rationals. Our Thm.
4
essentially reproves their results.
The synthesis problem has also been considered for constraint
\mathsf{LTL}

[
Bhaskar and Praveen(2024)
]
, which is
\mathsf{LTL}
over infinite traces where atoms are variable-to-variable/constant comparisons in the theory of linear arithmetic. The authors prove undecidability of the general case, but decidability in 2EXPTIME for theories that enjoy a so-called completion property, and for formulas where cross-state comparisons are restricted to the agent (single-sided games). However, they do not provide a procedure for the general case.

Reactive synthesis.

In the sequel a signature

. Intuitively,
X
are variables controlled by the environment, and
Y
variables controlled by the agent.
We consider
environment-first
synthesis
[
Rodríguez and Sánchez(2024a)
]
, where
in every instant
i
, first the
environment emits values
\beta_{i}(x)
for all
x\in X
, and afterwards the
agent can choose values
\gamma_{i}(y)
for all
y\in Y
.
Here
\beta_{i}
is an assignment with domain
X
, denoted by
\beta_{i}\in{\mathit{Assign}_{X}}
, and
\gamma_{i}
is an assignment with domain
Y
, written as
\gamma_{i}\in{\mathit{Assign}_{Y}}
.
We write
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}X=\{{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}x\mid x\in X\}
and
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}Y=\{{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y\mid y\in Y\}
for the respective lookback variables.

For an infinite sequence
\pi
, let
\pi|_{k}
denote its prefix of length
k
.
In this paper we consider the reactive synthesis problem for
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
:

Definition 3

(Synthesis problem)
.

The
synthesis problem
for
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
with respect to
(X,Y)
asks to find a strategy
g\colon{\mathit{Assign}_{X}}^{*}\to{\mathit{Assign}_{Y}}
such that for every infinite sequence of assignments
\pi=\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots\in{\mathit{Assign}_{X}}^{*}
, there is some
k\geq 0
such that the trace
\tau=\langle\beta_{0}\cup g(\pi|_{1}),\beta_{1}\cup g(\pi|_{2}),\dots,\beta_{k}\cup g(\pi|_{k+1})\rangle
satisfies
\tau\models\psi
.

The
realizability problem
is the related decision problem, asking whether
k
and suitable assignments
g(\pi|_{i})
,
0<i\leq k+1
exist.

We illustrate the synthesis problem for some simple properties.

Example 3
.

Suppose
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.

•

All of
x=y
,
\mathsf{G}(x=y)
, and
\mathsf{G}(y={\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}x)
are realizable over
LIA
or
LRA
because the agent chooses
y
after the environment fixes
x
.
Also
{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y=x
is realizable due to the weak semantics of lookback, but not
\mathsf{X}({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y=x)
, as the agent cannot know the next value of
x
.

•

\mathsf{F}(x=1)\to\mathsf{X}\mathsf{F}({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y=x)
is realizable by always setting
y
to 1 (note that the agent determines the trace length).

•

The running example from
[
Rodríguez and Sánchez(2024a)
]
,
\mathsf{G}((x\,{<}\,2\to\mathsf{X}(y\,{>}\,1))\wedge(x\,{\geq}\,2\to y\,{<}\,x))
is not realizable over
LIA
because the two conjuncts can impose contradicting requirements e.g. if
\beta_{0}(x)=1
and
\beta_{1}(x)=2
, but it is realizable over
LRA
.

The synthesis problem for
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
is undecidable in general, more precisely, even if
\mathcal{T}
is the decidable theory
LIA
, and atoms are restricted to variable-to-variable/constant comparisons
[
Bhaskar and Praveen(2024)
, Thm. 1]
.

In the context of synthesis, it is often desirable to find a strategy realizing a property
\psi\in\mathcal{L}

under the assumption
that the environment behaves in a way that is expressed as a property
\chi\in\mathcal{L}
over variables
X\cup{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}X
. For safety or stability assumptions, synthesis with assumptions can be reduced to standard synthesis (Def.
3
), by searching a strategy for
\chi\to\psi

[
Giacomo et al.(2020)Giacomo, Stasio, Vardi, and Zhu
]
.
We exploit this in the next example.

Example 4
.

Consider again Ex.
1
.
As an assumption on the environment, we suppose that the values of
x
are non-negative and increase by at most 2 in every step, as expressed by
\chi=\mathsf{G}(x\geq 0\wedge x{-}{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{x}\leq 2)
.
The desired property is given by
\psi=\mathsf{X}({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y>x)
. Then
\chi\to\psi
is realizable, because using the knowledge about the bounded increase of
x
,
y
can be chosen greater than
x+2
in the first instant.

\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
progression.

We consider progression over finite traces
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
]
to (implicitly) construct a DFA for a property
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
. For now we ignore the first-order nature of atoms in
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
properties.

Definition 4

(
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
Progression)
.

For
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
and
A
a set of atoms,
progression
\psi^{+}(A)
of
\psi
with respect to
A
is defined as follows:

•

if
\psi
is an atom,
\psi^{+}(A){=}\top
if
\psi\in A
and
\psi^{+}(A){=}\bot
otherwise;

•

if
\psi=\neg a
for an atom

then
\psi^{+}(A)=\bot
if
a\in A
and
\psi^{+}(A)=\top
otherwise;

•

if
\psi=\psi_{1}\wedge\psi_{2}
then
\psi^{+}(A)=\psi_{1}^{+}(A)\wedge\psi_{2}^{+}(A)
;

•

if
\psi=\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2}
then
\psi^{+}(A)=\psi_{1}^{+}(A)\vee\psi_{2}^{+}(A)
;

•

if
\psi=\mathsf{X}\psi_{1}
then
\psi^{+}(A)=\psi_{1}\wedge\neg\mathit{last}
;

•

if
\psi=\mathsf{X}_{\mathsf{w}}\psi_{1}
then
\psi^{+}(A)=\psi_{1}\vee\mathit{last}
;

•

if
\psi=\psi_{1}\mathrel{\mathsf{U}}\psi_{2}
then
\psi^{+}(A)=\psi_{2}^{+}(A)\vee(\psi_{1}^{+}(A)\wedge(\mathsf{X}\psi)^{+}(A))
;

•

if
\psi=\psi_{1}\,{\mathrel{\mathsf{R}}}\,\psi_{2}
then
\psi^{+}(A)=\psi_{2}^{+}(A)\wedge(\psi_{1}^{+}(A)\vee(\mathsf{X}_{\mathsf{w}}\psi)^{+}(A))
.

Let a sequence of sets of atoms
\sigma=\langle A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n-1}\rangle
be
over
a set of atoms
C
if
A_{i}\subseteq C
for all
i

0\leq i<n
.
Progression is as usual generalized to sequences, by setting
\psi^{+}(\epsilon)=\psi
for the empty sequence and
\psi^{+}(A\rho)=(\psi^{+}(A))^{+}(\rho)
otherwise.
The following are the key properties of progression
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu

Lemma 1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
and
\sigma=\langle A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n-1}\rangle
a sequence of sets of atoms over
\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)
.
Then

(1)

\sigma,i\models\psi
iff
\sigma,i+1\models\psi^{+}(A_{i})
for all
i
,
0\leq i<n
, and

(2)

\sigma\models\psi
iff
\psi^{+}(\sigma)\equiv\top
.

Here we use the notion of a sequence of atom sets
\sigma=\langle A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n-1}\rangle
satisfying a propositional/standard
\mathsf{LTL_{f}}
property. The semantics, however, coincides with that of Def.
2
where atoms are considered variables of sort
\mathit{bool}
, and
\sigma
is read as a trace where at instant
i
, an atom

is true iff
a\in A_{i}
.

Example 5
.

Consider
\psi=y\,{\geq}\,x\mathrel{\mathsf{U}}x\,{=}\,y
and the sequence of atom sets

\psi^{+}(\{y\,{\geq}\,x\})\equiv\psi\wedge\neg\mathit{last}
and

\psi^{+}(\sigma)=\top
.

Given a property
\psi
, one can construct a DFA for
\psi
by taking as set of states all properties

for some sequence

over
\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)
; and with a transition

iff

[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
]
. However, we do not reason over DFAs explicitly here.

For assignments
\alpha
,
\alpha^{\prime}
with domain

A sequence of formulas
\sigma{=}\langle w_{0},\dots,w_{n-1}\rangle
is

.

A trace

Boolean function decomposition.

Following
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
]
, we will sometimes consider a property
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
in XNF as a boolean formula where all subformulas rooted by a temporal operator are considered as propositions, and apply boolean function decomposition.
For a property
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
and
C\subseteq\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)
, we suppose that
\mathsf{decomp}
is a procedure such that
\mathsf{decomp}(\psi,C)=\{(\mathsf{prm}_{1},\mathsf{sub}_{1}),

\dots,(\mathsf{prm}_{k},\mathsf{sub}_{k})\}
for some
k\geq 1
such that
\mathsf{prm}_{i}
and
\mathsf{sub}_{i}
are properties in
\mathcal{L}
(or equivalently, boolean formulas in the above sense) satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\psi\equiv\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}(\mathsf{prm}_{i}\wedge\mathsf{sub}_{i})
, i.e.,
\mathsf{decomp}(\psi,C)
represents
\psi
,

(2)

\mathsf{prm}_{i}
has no temporal operators,
all atoms of
\mathsf{prm}_{i}
are in
C
but no atom of
\mathsf{sub}_{i}
is in
C
(i.e.,
\mathsf{tnps}(\mathsf{sub}_{i})\cap C=\emptyset
), for all
i
,

(3)

\mathsf{prm}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{prm}_{k}
are disjoint, i.e.
\mathsf{prm}_{i}\wedge\mathsf{prm}_{j}\equiv\bot
for all
i\neq j
,

(4)

\mathsf{prm}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{prm}_{k}
are covering, i.e.
\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{prm}_{i}\equiv\top
, and

(5)

the representation is compressed, i.e.,
\mathsf{sub}_{i}\neq\mathsf{sub}_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

The decomposition
\mathsf{decomp}(\psi,C)
can be found using SDDs
[
Darwiche(2011)
]
.
Note that at this point we ignore the first-order nature of atoms, so
\equiv
is to be read as equivalence in propositional logic. Thus, some
\mathsf{prm}_{i}\wedge\mathsf{sub}_{i}
may be satisfiable when considering
\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)
as opaque propositional atoms, but unsatisfiable in
\mathcal{T}
.

E.g., let
\psi=(x<0)\vee\mathsf{X}\mathsf{F}(x\,{<}\,0\vee x{-}{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{x}\,{>}\,2)\vee\mathsf{X}({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y>x)
, which is in XNF. We abstract
\psi
to the propositional formula
a\vee b\vee c
for
a=x\,{<}\,0
,
b=\mathsf{X}\mathsf{F}(x\,{<}\,0\vee x{-}{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{x}\,{>}\,2)
, and
c=\mathsf{X}({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}y>x)
.
Decomposition for
C=\{a\}
yields

\mathsf{decomp}(\psi,C)=\{(a,\top),(\neg a,b\vee c)\}
.

3
Reactive synthesis approach

Throughout this section, we assume a property
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
that is well-formed.
We use the idea from
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
]
to split atom sets into those atoms
that can be controlled by the agent, and those controlled by the environment.
However, due to the presence of lookback, the separation is a bit more subtle.
By Def.
1
, the atoms of
\psi
have free variables
V\cup{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}V
, i.e.,
X\cup Y\cup{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}X\cup{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}Y
.
Let

C_{\mathit{env}}\subseteq\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)
be the set of all atoms in
\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)
that do not mention a variable in
Y
, but only variables in
X\cup{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}X\cup{\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}Y
.
The values of these variables, and the atoms that depend on them,
cannot be influenced by the agent at the current instant, as they were either already determined earlier, or are fixed by the environment. We set
C_{\mathit{ag}}=\mathsf{atoms}(\psi)\setminus C_{\mathit{env}}
, the set of atoms that can be influenced by the agent.

Next, we define an AND-OR-graph
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
for
\psi
, which can be seen as a DFA for
\psi
where edge labels are split according to atoms determined by the environment (and earlier valuations), and atoms controlled by the agent. At choice points for the former we put an AND-node, while at choice points for the agent an OR-node occurs. Each AND-node is moreover labelled by an
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
property, which intuitively expresses what remains to be satisfied to guarantee
\psi
.

Definition 6

(AND-OR-graph)
.

For
\psi\in\mathcal{L}
in XNF,
the AND-OR-graph
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}=(S_{\land},S_{\lor},E,s_{0},\mathit{lab})
is given by a set of
AND-nodes
S_{\land}
, a set of OR-nodes
S_{\lor}
, edges
E\subseteq(S_{\land}\times S_{\lor})\cup(S_{\lor}\times S_{\land})
, an initial node
s_{0}\in S_{\land}
, and a labelling
\mathit{lab}\colon S_{\land}\to\mathcal{L}
mapping AND-nodes to
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
properties.

\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
is inductively defined as follows:

(1)

The initial node
s_{0}
has label
\mathit{lab}(s_{0})=\psi
.

For each AND-node
s
with
\mathit{lab}(s)=\psi^{\prime}
such that
\psi^{\prime}\neq\top
, and

\mathsf{decomp}(\mathsf{xnf}(\psi^{\prime}),C_{\mathit{env}})=\{(\mathsf{prm}_{1},\mathsf{sub}_{1}),\dots,(\mathsf{prm}_{k},\mathsf{sub}_{k})\}

for every
i
,
1\leq i\leq k
,

\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
contains:

(2)

an OR-node
n_{i}
with edge
s\mathrel{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$\xrightarrow{\mathsf{prm}_{i}}$}}n_{i}
, and

(3)

for the subsequent decomposition

\mathsf{decomp}(\mathsf{xnf}(\mathsf{sub}_{i}),C_{\mathit{ag}})=

\{(\mathsf{prm}_{i,1},\mathsf{sub}_{i,1}),\dots,(\mathsf{prm}_{i,m},\mathsf{sub}_{i,m})\}

for each

,
1\leq j\leq m

there are an AND-node
s_{i,j}
with
\mathit{lab}(s_{i,j})=\mathsf{xnf}(\mathsf{rmX}(\mathsf{sub}_{i,j}))

and an edge

Here since all atoms were already split off by decompositions, all top-level atoms in

Bounded lookback

is a theory-independent restriction of the interaction between variables via lookback. Intuitively, a property

whose edge labels create a dependency chain between variables that spans more than
K
instants; where, however, equality atoms are not counted for dependencies as they can be collapsed.
Bounded lookback has been considered for model checking and monitoring
[
Felli et al.(2023)Felli, Montali, Patrizi, and Winkler
,
Gianola et al.(2024)Gianola, Montali, and Winkler
]
. In fact the definition below differs slightly from the literature, but we keep the name as the concept is the same.

and

with AND-nodes

,
s^{\prime}
,
let

.

Rich models to describe business processes such as DPNs

5
Conclusion

This paper presents the first reactive synthesis procedure for
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
properties with lookback.
The approach is proven sound, and complete wrt.
bounded realizability
.
It is also a decision procedure for several fragments of
\mathsf{LTL_{f}^{MT}}
, reproving known decidability results and identifying new decidable fragments.

A variety of directions for future work arise. First of all, we plan an implementation with a focus on linear arithmetic, using SMT solvers with QE support as backends
[
de Moura and Bjørner(2008)
,
Barbosa et al.(2022)Barbosa, Barrett, Brain, Kremer, Lachnitt, Mann,
Mohamed, Mohamed, Niemetz, Nötzli, Ozdemir, Preiner, Reynolds, Sheng,
Tinelli, and Zohar
]
. Though the treatment of quantifiers is notoriously difficult, recent verification tools witness that QE is not a showstopper
[
Hipler et al.(2024)Hipler, Kallwies, Leucker, and
Sánchez
,
Gianola et al.(2024)Gianola, Montali, and Winkler
,
Felli et al.(2023)Felli, Montali, Patrizi, and Winkler
]
. Second, efficiency could be gained by interleaving the computation of the AND-OR-graph
\mathcal{A}_{\psi}
and the winning condition
\mathit{Win}(\mathcal{A}_{\psi})
, exploiting on-the-fly techniques from
[
Giacomo et al.(2022)Giacomo, Favorito, Li, Vardi, Xiao, and
Zhu
,
Xiao et al.(2021)Xiao, Li, Zhu, Shi, Pu, and Vardi
]
. Third, it would be interesting to study whether the restriction on bounded realizability in Thm.
2
can be lifted.

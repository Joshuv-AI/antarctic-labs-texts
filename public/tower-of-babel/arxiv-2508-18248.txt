Inverse Hamiltonian Reduction for affine W-algebras in type A
Dylan Butson and Sujay Nair

Abstract
. We give a geometric proof of inverse Hamiltonian reduction for all affine W-algebras in type A at generic level, a certain embedding of the affine W-algebra corresponding to an arbitrary nilpotent in 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{gl}_{N} into that corresponding to a larger nilpotent with respect to the closure order on orbits, tensored with an auxiliary algebra of free fields. We proceed by constructing strict chiral quantizations of equivariant Slodowy slices, sheaves of ℏ \hbar -adic vertex algebras on the arc spaces of the slices, and then localizing them on quasi-Darboux open sets. We also provide a generalization for the Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction of arbitrary vertex algebra objects in the Kazhdan–Lusztig category.



1.
Introduction

Given a nilpotent element
\chi
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}
and a level
k\in\mathbb{C}
, one can construct a certain vertex algebra,
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\chi)
, called the affine W-algebra. In fact, up to isomorphism this vertex algebra only depends on the nilpotent orbit of
\chi
, and so can be labeled by
\mu=[\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{3}\geq\dots\mu_{n-1}\geq 0]
a partition of
N
, and we identify the set of all such partitions of length
n-1
with the set of dominant, integral coweights
\Lambda^{+}(\mathrm{PGL}_{n})
of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
.

For the special case where
\mu=[1^{N}]
,
i.e.
,
\chi=0
, this vertex algebra is the universal affine vertex algebra
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. One has a homological construction of W-algebras for
\mu\geq[1^{N}]
by quantum Hamiltonian (or Drinfeld–Sokolov) reduction of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
, see
[
KRW03
]
. This construction has proven to be of great value in studying the representation theory of these algebras
[
Ara07
]
.

Recently, for
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}
satisfying certain hypotheses, it has been shown by
[
GJ25
]
that one can obtain
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\mu_{1}}
from
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\mu_{2}}
by a kind of partial Hamiltonian reduction. The main result of this work concerns
inverse Hamiltonian reduction
, which realizes
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\mu_{2}}
as a subalgebra of the tensor product of
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\mu_{1}}
with so-called free fields.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}(\mathrm{PGL}_{n})
and
\alpha
a positive co-root such that
\mu+\alpha\in\Lambda^{+}(\mathrm{PGL}_{n})
. Then, at generic level
k
, we have an embedding of
\hbar
-adic vertex algebras

(1.0.1)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}^{k}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\hbar,\mu+\alpha}\hat{\otimes}{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{A}^{\rm len\,\alpha})~,

for
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{A}^{\rm len\,\alpha})
a localization of the
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra of chiral differential operators on
\mathbb{A}^{\rm len\,\alpha}
.

This theorem completes the generalization to type A of a family of results going back to
[
Sem94
]
, as we will explain below. It is obtained in much the same way as in
[
BN25a
]
, where we established the analogous result for finite W-algebras. Indeed, crucial ingredients in the proof are the geometric results on equivariant Slodowy slices obtained in
loc. cit.
. The other crucial ingredient, like in finite type, is deformation theory—now of vertex algebras as opposed to associative algebras. In a sense, this work is an application of the results of
[
BN25b
]
on deformations of quantizations of arc spaces.

Our proof proceeds by first constructing quantizations of the arc spaces of the equivariant Slodowy slices. Namely, for each
k\in\mathbb{C}
, there is a sheaf of
\hbar
-adic vertex algebras
{\mathcal{A}}^{k}_{\hbar,G,\mu}
on
{\mathcal{J}}S_{G,\mu}
whose
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra of global sections
is isomorphic to the
\hbar
-adic analogue of the equivariant affine W-algebra at level
k
of
[
Ara18
]
, that is,

\Gamma({\mathcal{J}}S_{G,\mu},{\mathcal{A}}^{k}_{\hbar,G,\mu})\cong{\bf W}^{k}_{\hbar,G,\mu}~.

We then prove inverse Hamiltonian reduction for these equivariant W-algebras, constructing the inclusion by localizing this sheaf on the image of the inverse Hamiltonian reduction chart of
[
BN25a
]
and appealing to the deformation theory results of
[
BN25b
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}(\mathrm{PGL}_{n})
and
\alpha
a positive co-root such that
\mu+\alpha\in\Lambda^{+}(\mathrm{PGL}_{n})
. Then, at generic level
k
, we have an embedding of
\hbar
-adic vertex algebras

(1.0.2)

{\bf W}_{\hbar,G,\mu}^{k}\hookrightarrow{\bf W}^{k}_{\hbar,G,\mu+\alpha}\hat{\otimes}{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{A}^{\rm len\,\alpha})~.

Inverse hamiltonian reduction for the ordinary affine W-algebras follows by taking
G({\mathcal{O}})
-invariants. The result for equivariant W-algebras in fact implies a stronger version of inverse Hamiltonian reduction for arbitrary modules in the Kazhdan–Lusztig category. Namely, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M\in{\rm KL}_{k}
, and denote by
H^{0}_{\rm DS}(M,\mu)
the Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction of
M
at some representative of the orbit labeled by
\mu
. Then we have natural embeddings of
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\hbar,\mu}
modules

(1.0.3)

H^{0}_{\rm DS}(M,\mu)\hookrightarrow H^{0}_{\rm DS}(M,\mu+\alpha)\hat{\otimes}{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{A}^{\rm len\,\alpha})~,

where the
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}^{k}
-module structure on the right comes from the embedding in Theorem
1.1
. In particular, if
M
is a vertex algebra object in
\mathrm{KL}_{k}
, this is an embedding of vertex algebras.

1.1.
Relation to other work

The first example of inverse Hamiltonian reduction for affine W-algebras was the work of Semikhatov
[
Sem94
]
, which considered the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
case. More recently, higher rank and superalgebra examples have been constructed by Adamović in
[
Ada05
,
Ada19
]
. The first somewhat systematic proof of inverse Hamiltonian reduction in type
A
is due to Fehily
[
Feh23
]
, who proved the statement for hook-type W-algebras using the free-field realizations of these W-algebras from
[
Gen17
]
. This approach was extended to all examples in rank three in
[
CFLN25
,
FFFN24
]
, and further to all Virasoro-type reductions in
[
FKN25
]
.

Additionally, the present work also offers a novel proof of what is known in the literature as reduction by stages. This is a result first conjectured, for finite type, in Morgan’s thesis
[
Mor15
]
and proven in the finite and affine case by
[
GJ24
,
GJ25
]
. We note, however, that our hypotheses differ from those of
loc. cit.
in Type A.

In a sense, inverse Hamiltonian reduction can be viewed as a special case of a generalized coproduct on truncations of the shifted affine Yangian of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. For the finite Yangian, this comultiplication was described in
[
FKP+18
]
. In the affine case, however, constructing the general coproduct has proven to be a challenging endeavor. In the case of rectangular nilpotents, some results have been obtained in
[
KU22
,
GRZ23
]
and geometric approaches to this problem can be found in
[
BR23
,
But23
]
.

Unlike
[
BN25a
]
, we make almost no reference to the affine Grassmannian, or generalized slices therein. While the results of our previous work related various quantizations of certain generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian to each other, this work relates strict chiral quantizations of those same slices. One wonders whether this may have an interpretation in terms of the geometry of the double affine Grassmannian, (see
[
BF10
]
), but we leave this speculation to future work.

Finally, this work is yet another entry into the ever-growing list of geometric free-field realizations. The idea of obtaining free-field realizations by constructing a sheaf of vertex algebras and localizing them onto a global (quasi-)Darboux patch originates in
[
BMR19
]
. More recently, this viewpoint has been used successfully to provide free-field realizations for several vertex algebras originating from supersymmetric gauge theories—see
[
AKM23
,
CSYZ25
,
BF25
,
BN23
,
Fur23
]
for an incomplete list.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Vertex algebras and vertex Poisson algebras

Write
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}},z]
for the global sections of differentials operators on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, and write
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
for the subalgebra of weakly translation-invariant differential operators.

The category
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-Mod is symmetric monoidal under
\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\otimes}
with coproduct
{{\partial}}\mapsto 1\otimes{{\partial}}+{{\partial}}\otimes 1
. Therefore, it makes sense to talk about algebras in
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-Mod. In particular, a unital, commutative, associative algebra in
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-Mod, is a unital, commutative, associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
equipped with an action of
{{\partial}}
by derivations.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lie* algebra is a
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-module
L
equipped with
\mathbb{N}
-many
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear operations

{}_{(n)}:L\otimes L\rightarrow L

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfying

(i)

x_{(n)}y=0
for some
n\gg 0

locality

(ii)

x_{(n)}y=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\partial^{j}y_{(n+j)}x

skewsymmetry

(iii)

x_{(m)}(y_{(k)}z)-y_{(k)}(x_{(m)}z)=\sum_{j=0}\begin{pmatrix}m\\
j\end{pmatrix}(x_{(j)}y)_{(m+k-j)}z

weak associativity

(iv)

({{\partial}}x)_{(n)}y=-nx_{(n-1)}y,({{\partial}}x)_{(0)}y=0

sesquilinearity

Elsewhere in the literature, these algebras are known as Lie conformal algebras or vertex Lie algebras. We align our naming conventions with those of
[
BD04
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A vertex Poisson algebra is a pair
(V,|{0}\rangle)
equipped with a family of products

{}_{(n)}:V\otimes V\rightarrow V

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\geq-1
, such that

(i)

the product
(-1)
makes
V
a commutative algebra in
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-Mod, with unit
|{0}\rangle\in V
.

(ii)

(V,\{_{(n)},n\geq 0\})
is a Lie* algebra

(iii)

for any
v,a,b\in V
and any
n\geq 0
,

v_{(n)}a_{(-1)}b=(v_{(n)}a)_{(-1)}b+a_{(-1)}(v_{(n)}b)

We call the last condition the
quasi-Leibniz rule
—quasi since it is not symmetric. One should compare this to the characterisation of Poisson algebras as a vector space equipped with a commutative multiplication and a Lie bracket such that the Lie bracket distributes over the commutative product as a derivation.

It is conventional to collect the various products together into a
\lambda
-bracket—a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]:V\otimes V\rightarrow V[\lambda]
defined as

(2.1.1)

[a_{\lambda}b]\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}a_{(n)}b~.

Definition 2.3
.

A vertex algebra is a tuple
(V,|{0}\rangle,\{_{(n)}|n\in\mathbb{Z}\})
, where
V
is a
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-module,
|{0}\rangle\in\ker{{\partial}}\subset V
is a distinguished vector called the
vacuum
, and

{}_{(n)}:V\otimes V\rightarrow V

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-family of products on
V
satisfying

(i)

x_{(n)}|{0}\rangle=\delta_{n,-1}x
for any
x\in V

vacuum

(ii)

x_{(k)}y=0
for
k\gg 0

locality

(iii)

x_{(n)}y=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\partial^{j}y_{(n+j)}x

skewsymmetry

(iv)

x_{(m)}(y_{(n)}z)-y_{(m)}(x_{(n)}z)=\sum_{j=0}\begin{pmatrix}m\\
j\end{pmatrix}(x_{(j)}y)_{(m+n-j)}z

weak associativity

(v)

({{\partial}}x)_{(n)}y=-nx_{(n-1)}y

sesquilinearity

for
x,y,z\in V
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We can collect together the various
(n)
-products into a State-Field Correspondence,

Y(\cdot,z):V\rightarrow{\textup{End}}(V){{(\!(z)\!)}}

with
a(z)\equiv Y(a,z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}
. This leads to the Operator Product Expansions (OPEs).

(2.1.2)

a(z)b(w)\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{(n)}b)(w)}{(z-w)^{n+1}}

where the right hand side should be understood as the expansion of the left hand side in the region
|z|>|w|
.

Remark 2.4
.

Given a vertex algebra
(V,|{0}\rangle,\{_{(n)}|n\in\mathbb{Z}\})
, we say that it is generated by a vector subspace
W\subset V
, if any vector
v\in V
, with
v\neq|{0}\rangle
, can be written as a finite linear combination of monomials of the form
w_{1,(-n_{1})}w_{2,(-n_{2})}\dots w_{m,(-n_{m})}
where
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{m}\in W
and
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{m}>0
. In other words,
V
is
strongly generated
by any basis of
W
, but we are lax about whether this is a minimal set of generators.

2.2.
Arc spaces and vertex Poisson algebras

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the formal disk,
i.e.
,
\mathbb{D}={\rm Spf}(\mathbb{C}{{[\![t]\!]}})
. Let
Y
be a scheme over
\mathbb{C}
. The functor

(2.2.1)

\begin{split}{\rm Sch}^{\rm op}_{/\mathbb{C}}&\rightarrow{\rm Set}\\
S&\mapsto{\rm Hom}(S\times\mathbb{D},Y)\end{split}

is representable by the scheme
{\mathcal{J}}Y
. Its
\mathbb{C}
-points are
{\rm Hom}(\mathbb{D},Y)
and we have a natural projection
{\rm ev}:{\mathcal{J}}Y\rightarrow Y
by evaluating at the closed point of
\mathbb{D}
. We write
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{J}}Y}
for the structure sheaf of
{\mathcal{J}}Y
.

We have a natural evaluation map
{\rm ev}:{\mathcal{J}}Y\twoheadrightarrow Y
, by evaluating a map at the closed point of
\mathbb{D}
. The fibres of this map can be identified with
\mathbb{A}^{\infty}
, and so
{\mathcal{J}}Y
contracts to
Y
. The pullback
{\rm ev}^{*}
provides a natural inclusion
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{J}}Y}
. Henceforth, the inclusion
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{J}}Y}
always refers to this pullback along
{\rm ev}
.

If
Y
is Poisson, the global sections
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
have the structure of a vertex Poisson algebra, specified by

(2.2.2)

[a_{\lambda}b]=\{a,b\}~,

for
a,b\in{\mathcal{O}}(Y)\subset{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
and extended to arbitrary sections by
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-sesquilinearity and the quasi-Leibniz rules. Moreover,
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{J}}Y}
is a sheaf of vertex Poisson algebras, see
[
AKM15
]
for more details on localizing vertex Poisson algebras.

Proposition 2.5
.

The assignment
Y\mapsto{\mathcal{J}}Y
is functorial

Vertex algebras and vertex Poisson algebras admit an operadic construction, see
[
BD04
,
BDSHK19
]
, and so there are natural cohomology theories controlling their deformations.
Explicit constructions of the relevant cochain complexes can be found in
[
BDSHK19
,
BDSH+21
,
DSK13
]
. For brevity, we shall not recall their definitions here. Instead we recall a result on the so-called variational Poisson cohomology, from
[
BN25b
]
that is a generalization of
[
DSK13
]
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
BN25b
]
)
.

Suppose
Y
is an affine, symplectic variety. The variational Poisson cohomology of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
, with its natural vertex Poisson structure, can be computed as

(2.2.3)

H^{i}_{\rm var}({\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y))=\begin{cases}H^{0}_{\rm dR}(Y)\oplus H^{1}_{\rm dR}(Y)\quad\text{for }i=0\\
H^{i+1}_{\rm dR}(Y)\quad\text{for }i>0\end{cases}

Remark 2.7
.

We note for future purposes that the zeroth variational Poisson cohomology of
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
parameterizes the space of vertex Poisson Casimirs,
i.e.
, all
[a]\in{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)/{{\partial}}{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
such that

(2.2.4)

a_{(0)}=0

as a derivation.

2.3.
Chiral quantization

Throughout this subsection, we take
Y
to be a symplectic variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let us introduce the concept of a
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra. Roughly speaking, this is a vertex algebra whose OPEs satisfy locality only up to some power of
\hbar
,
i.e.
, an inverse limit of vertex algebras. We recall the definition in
[
Li04
]
:

Definition 2.8
.

A
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra is a tuple
(V,|{0}\rangle,\partial,\{_{(n)}|n\in\mathbb{Z}\})
where

(i)

V
is a flat
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-module, complete in the
\hbar
-adic topology

(ii)

|{0}\rangle\in V

(iii)

{{\partial}}:V\rightarrow V
is an endomorphism of
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-modules;

(iv)

{}_{(n)}:V\otimes V\rightarrow V
are
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-bilinear morphisms

such that for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the truncations
(V/\hbar^{m}V,|{0}\rangle,Y_{\hbar}|_{\hbar^{m}=0},\partial|_{\hbar^{m}=0})
are vertex algebras over
\mathbb{C}[\hbar]/\hbar^{m}
.

We say that a
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra
(V,|{0}\rangle,Y_{\hbar},\partial)
is
almost commutative
if
(V/\hbar V,|{0}\rangle,Y_{0},\partial)
is a commutative vertex algebra.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
Li04
, Proposition 5.6]
)
.

If
(V,|{0}\rangle,Y_{\hbar},\partial)
is an almost commutative
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra, then the limit
(V/\hbar V)
has a natural vertex Poisson algebra structure.

Example 2.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Lie algebra and let
V^{k}(\mathfrak{h})
denote the universal affine vertex algebra of
\mathfrak{h}
at some, possibly trivial, level
k\in\mathbb{C}
. An important example of a
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra is
V_{\hbar}^{k}(\mathfrak{h})
, the
\hbar
-adic analogue of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{h})
. This
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra is generated over
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
by
\mathfrak{h}
with OPEs

(2.3.1)

x(z)y(w)\sim\frac{\hbar[x,y](w)}{z-w}+\frac{\hbar^{2}k\langle x,y\rangle}{(z-w)^{2}}~.

Note also that
V_{\hbar}^{k}(\mathfrak{h})
is an almost commutative vertex algebra, and
V_{\hbar}^{k}(\mathfrak{h})/\hbar
is identified with the vertex Poisson algebra
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}\,\mathfrak{h}^{*})
.

We now define quantizations of the vertex Poisson algebra
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)

.

A

(i)

quantization

(i)

\hbar

Definition 2.12
.

Suppose

2.4.
Equivariant Slodowy slices

The spaces we wish to quantize will be the arc spaces of equivariant Slodowy slices. We provide a brief review of the equivariant slices here, mainly to introduce future notation. A more in depth review can be found in
[
Los07
]
.

Recall that nilpotent coadjoint orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\equiv\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
are in bijection with the partitions of
N
. Let
\mu
be such a partition, as above, and let
\chi_{\mu}=\langle f_{\mu},\cdot\rangle
be an element of the nilpotent coadjoint orbit,
\mathbb{O}_{\mu}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

The orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\mu}
admits a transversal slice
S_{\mu}
at
\chi_{\mu}
—see,
e.g.
, 3.2.19 in
[
CG10
]
. There are many such transversal slices at
\chi_{\mu}
, but a particularly nice and familiar class are the Slodowy slices, which have the following construction. By the Jacobson–Morozov theorem, we may complete
f\equiv f_{\mu}
to an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(h,e,f)
, and the Slodowy slice
S_{\mu}
is defined as the image of the affine linear subspace

(2.4.1)

f+\ker\,{\rm ad}_{e}

under the Killing isomorphism. Note that
\ker\mathrm{ad}_{e}
is always transverse to
{\textup{im}}\,\mathrm{ad}_{f}=T_{f}\mathbb{O}_{f}
, so that
S_{\mu}
defines a transverse slice. Changing the choice of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple gives isomorphic slices, related by
G
-conjugation.

Observe that if
\chi=0\in\mathbb{O}_{[1^{N}]}
, the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
triple is
(0,0,0)
and
S_{[1^{N}]}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Alternatively, if
\chi\in\mathbb{O}_{[N]}
,
i.e.
, is regular, then
S_{\mu}
is called the
principal
Slodowy slice and
S_{[1^{N}]}\cong\mathfrak{h}/W
.

The transversal slices
S_{\mu}
are Poisson, which is more apparent in their definition via Hamiltonian reduction. Given an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple,
(h,e,f)
, the operator
{\rm ad}_{h}
gives a grading on
\mathfrak{g}
—which we assume is even without loss of generality in type
A
. We let
\mathfrak{g}_{>0}
denote the Lie subalgebra that is strictly positively graded under
{\rm ad}_{h}
and define

(2.4.2)

N_{\chi}\coloneqq{\rm Exp}\,\mathfrak{g}_{>0}~,

the corresponding unipotent Lie group. Then the coadjoint action of
G
restricts to a Hamiltonian
N_{\chi}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with moment map
\Phi_{N_{\chi_{\mu}}}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}_{>0}^{*}
the projection, and we have

(2.4.3)

\begin{split}N_{\chi}\times S_{\mu}&\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\chi\}+\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}^{*}=\mu_{N_{\chi}}^{-1}(\{\chi\})\\
(n,s)&\mapsto{\rm Ad}^{*}_{n}s\end{split}\ ,

the Gan–Ginzburg isomorphism
[
GG02
]
. Therefore, we see that

(2.4.4)

S_{\mu}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}N_{\chi}

and thus transverse slice
S_{\mu}
inherits the Poisson structure induced by Hamiltonian reduction from the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

The action of
{\rm ad}_{h}
descends to the transverse slice, giving rise to the Kazhdan grading. On an element
s\in S_{\mu}
, the action of
t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}
is given by

(2.4.5)

t\cdot s=t^{1-\tfrac{1}{2}\rm{ad}_{h}}s

Note that the action, contracts to the fixed point
\chi\in S_{\mu}
. This action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
scales the Poisson bracket with weight
-1
.

Much of our deformation theory makes use of the fact that the underlying space is symplectic and so we want to replace these Poisson transversal slices with a symplectic version. We do so by considering the equivariant Slodowy slices of
[
Los07
]
.

Viewing
T^{*}G
in its
right
trivialization, we have
T^{*}G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times G
, with left and right moment maps
\Phi_{L}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times G
and
\Phi_{R}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times G\rightarrow G
, where

(2.4.6)

\Phi_{L}(x,g)=x~,\quad\Phi_{R}(x,g)={\rm Ad}^{*}_{g}x~,

for
(x,g)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times G
.

More generally, for any
\chi_{\mu}\in\mathbb{O}_{\mu}
, let
N_{\chi_{\mu}}
be as before. The equivariant Slodowy slice is the Hamiltonian reduction

(2.4.7)

S_{G,\mu}\coloneqq T^{*}G/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}~,

using the left moment map. Since the left moment map is just projection to
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we see that

(2.4.8)

S_{G,\mu}\cong G\times S_{\mu}~.

hence the name, equivariant Slodowy slice. Since the moment map is flat and its fibers are
N_{\chi}
-torsors, each reduction is smooth and inherits a symplectic structure from
T^{*}G
.

Furthermore, since we are in the right trivialization, the right action of
G
on itself leaves the cotangent fibers invariants. Therefore,
G
acts freely on the right on each
S_{G,\mu}
and

(2.4.9)

S_{G,\mu}/G\cong S_{\mu}~.

Note that
T^{*}G
has a natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
that scales the fibers with weight
1
. The reductions
S_{G,\mu}
inherit a modified version of this
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action, via the Kazhdan grading—which extends straightforwardly to
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times G
. Indeed, this is the distinguished
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-action from the preceding section.

We end with reviewing some of the main geometric results of
[
BN25a
]

We write

As a variety

The moment map for the Hamiltonian

Theorem 2.16

(
[
BN25a
]
)

Moreover, this reduction admits an inverse, in the sense that we have a Hamiltonian

For this section we take

3.1.
Hamiltonian reduction for arc spaces

Before moving to Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction, we first review the classical limit for arc spaces and vertex Poisson algebras. In usual symplectic geometry, we can define the symplectic reduction of

Abusing notation, we write

We note that,

(3.1.3)

\Phi_{\infty}^{-1}(\chi)/H({\mathcal{O}})\cong{\mathcal{J}}\bigg(\Phi^{-1}(\chi)/H\bigg)~.

3.2.
Classical BRST reduction for vertex Poisson algebras

Recall the (classical) Clifford algebra

Then,

3.3.
Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction

Now, we lift this construction to the quantized setting.

Write
{\rm Cl}(\mathfrak{h})
for the vertex (super)algebra generated by

in cohomological degrees
-1
and

Write,

\xi(z)x(w)\sim\frac{\hbar\langle\xi,x\rangle}{z-w}~.

Proposition 6.8

Write

and
\Phi_{2}(x)

Since

intertwines the

with any field in the image of

must be regular.

Suppose

In particular, this implies that

is a vertex Poisson Casimir,

,

vanishes. The Casimirs, up to total derivatives, are classified by the zeroth variational Poisson cohomology (see

. Since none of these classes transform appropriately under

To be concrete, set

for some

for some

Appendix A
Microlocalization of chiral differential operators

Suppose
Y
is a smooth, affine,
\mathbb{C}
-scheme such that the second Chern class of
Y
vanishes,
i.e.
,
Y
admits a sheaf of chiral differential operators. Importantly, this sheaf
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{Y}
is only a sheaf over
Y
, but we wish to construct a microlocal version of
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{Y}
that is a sheaf over
{\mathcal{J}}T^{*}Y
. In this appendix, we go over an abstract construction of this microlocal sheaf.

A.1.
Chiral envelope of a Lie* algebra

Recall the definition of a Lie* algebra from Definition
2.1
. Given any
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex algebra, we can forget the regular terms in the OPE to obtain an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie* algebra, giving rise to a forgetful functor from the category of vertex algebras to that of Lie* algebras. This forgetful functor admits a left-adjoint, the chiral (often called vertex) envelope.

Given an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie* algebra
L
, note that
[\cdot,\cdot]:L\otimes L\rightarrow L
given by

(A.1.1)

[x,y]=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\partial^{j}(x_{(j)}y)~,

defines an honest Lie bracket on
L
. Write
U(L)
for the universal enveloping algebra of
L
thought of as a Lie algebra under
[\cdot,\cdot]
. We extend the action of
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
to
U(L)
by the Leibniz rule. We have a natural morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-modules
i:L\hookrightarrow U(L)
, coming from the composition
L\hookrightarrow T(L)\xrightarrow{p}U(L)
, where
T(L)
is the free tensor algebra on
L
and
p
is the natural projection to the universal enveloping algebra.

Given a Lie* algebra, the vector space
{\rm Sym}\,L
has the natural structure of an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex Poisson algebra, with
\lambda
-bracket

(A.1.2)

[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}a_{(k)}b~,

on
a,n\in L\subset{\rm Sym}\,L
and extended to arbitrary elements by the left and right quasi-Leibniz rules. As usual,
U(L)
admits a PBW basis of lexicographically ordered monomials and we have the usual symmetrization map

(A.1.3)

{\rm Sym}\,L\xrightarrow{\sigma}U(L)~.

Proposition A.1
.

There exists a unique
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex algebra structure on
U(L)
such that for all
a\in L\subset U(L)
,
and
v\in T(L)
,

(A.1.4)

p(a\otimes v)=i(a)_{(-1)}p(v)

We write
V(L)
for the
\mathbb{C}
-module
U(L)
, when emphasizing its vertex algebra structure.

We can think of
V(L)
, therefore, as a nonassociative deformation of
U(L)
—akin to how
U(L)
is a noncommutative deformation of
{\rm Sym}\,L
. Moreover, we have a canonical isomorphism of vector spaces
\psi:U(L)\xrightarrow{\sim}V(L)
, defined recursively on the PBW basis of
U(L)
by (
A.1.4
).

Remark A.2
.

The OPEs in
V(L)
admit a rather nice description. The field
a(z)\equiv Y(a,z)
for any
a\in L\subset V(L)
is of the form

(A.1.5)

Y(a,z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(m)}z^{-m-1}~,

This fixes the OPEs between all elements of
L\subset V(L)
. To compute OPEs of composites, we can use the left and right Wick rules. Now, since all
(n)
in
L
are bidifferential operators, this guarantees that all
(n)
-products in
V(L)
are some finite order bidifferential operators—albeit highly complicated. For a nice exposition in the case where
L
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-module see
[
Cas23
]
.

Composing the non-associative deformation
\psi
with the symmetrization map gives a map

(A.1.6)

{\rm NO}:{\rm Sym}\,L\xrightarrow{\sim}V(L)~.

One should think of this as giving rise to a normal-ordering prescription.

One can repeat this construction starting with a Lie* algebroid instead.

Definition A.3
.

Given a unital, commutative algebra
A\in\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]-
Mod, a Lie*
A
-algebroid is an
A
-module
L
equipped with a Lie* bracket
\{_{(n)},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and an anchor map
\tau:L\rightarrow{\rm Der}(A)
, satisfying

(i)

\tau(ax)(b)=a\tau(x)(b)
for any
a,b\in A
and
x\in L

(ii)

x_{(n)}(ay)=\delta_{n,0}\tau(x)(a)y+ax_{(n)}y
for any
a\in A
and
x,y\in L
.

Given a Lie*
A
-algebroid
L
, the relative symmetric algebra
{\rm Sym}_{A}^{\bullet}\,L
has a unique vertex Poisson algebra structure by extending the Lie* bracket using the left and right Leibniz rules. We define
U(L)
as the quotient of
T(L)=\bigoplus_{n}L^{\underset{A}{\otimes}n}
, by the ideal defined by (
A.1.1
).

A.2.
Chiral differential operators as chiral envelopes

Let
Y
be a smooth, affine variety over
\mathbb{C}
with trivial second Chern class. Let
c\in H^{3}_{\rm dR}(Y)
, such a choice fixes (up to isomorphism) a particular cdo over
Y
. For brevity, we suppress this choice in future notation.

Let
L_{Y}
denote the following extension

(A.2.1)

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)\hookrightarrow L_{Y}\twoheadrightarrow\Theta({\mathcal{J}}Y)\rightarrow 0

of the global vector fields on
{\mathcal{J}}Y
. A choice of
c
endows
L_{Y}
with the structure of a Lie*
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
-algebroid, with OPEs given by

(A.2.2)

\begin{split}x(z)y(w)&\sim\frac{[x,y](w)-c(x,y)(w)}{z-w}~,\\
x(z)a(w)&\sim\frac{x(a)(w)}{z-w}\end{split}

for
x,y\in\Theta(Y)\subset\Theta({\mathcal{J}}Y)
and
a\in{\mathcal{O}}(Y)\subset{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
. Moreover,
L_{Y}
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded with
L_{Y}(0)={\mathcal{O}}(Y)
and
L_{Y}(1)=\Omega^{1}(Y)\oplus\Theta(Y)
, where
\Omega^{1}(Y)\cong{\mathcal{O}}(Y)\cdot{{\partial}}{\mathcal{O}}(Y)
.

The various
(n)
products of the Lie* algebra are, therefore, bidifferential operators on the
\Theta(Y)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(Y)
variables. One should interpret Borcherd’s identities as relations satisfied by these bidifferential operators.

By definition, a cdo,
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}(Y)
is the chiral envelope of
L_{Y}
. In particular, this means that the OPEs in
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}(Y)
may be computed using the left and right Wick rules, with each Wick contraction a bidifferential operator.

Let us also define a
\hbar
-adic version of the preceding construction. Let
L_{\hbar,Y}
denote the
\hbar
-adic version of
L_{Y}
with OPEs

(A.2.3)

\begin{split}x(z)y(w)&\sim\frac{\hbar[x,y](w)-\hbar^{2}c(x,y)(w)}{z-w}~,\\
x(z)f(w)&\sim\frac{\hbar x(f)(w)}{z-w}\end{split}

for
f\in{\mathcal{O}}(Y)
and
x\in\Theta(Y)
. The group
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
acts here by Lie* algebra automorphisms, scaling
x\in\Theta(Y)
and leaving
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
invariant. Denote the chiral envelope of
L_{\hbar,Y}
by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar}^{ch}(Y)
—the
\hbar
-adic cdos. Analogously, we have a
\hbar
-adic normal ordering prescription

(A.2.4)

{\rm NO}_{\hbar}:{\rm Sym}\,L_{\hbar,Y}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar}^{ch}(Y)~,

which is now a
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-equivariant isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-modules. Hereafter,
{\rm Sym}
should be understood as the relative symmetric algebra with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
.

Write
L_{Y}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
for the
\hbar
-adic completion of
L_{Y}\otimes\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
, we have a natural embedding of
\hbar
-adic Lie* algebras,
\sigma_{\hbar}:L_{\hbar,Y}\hookrightarrow L_{Y}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
, given by

(A.2.5)

\sigma_{\hbar}(f)=f\text{ and }\sigma_{\hbar}(x)=\hbar x~,

for
f\in{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}Y)
and
x\in\Theta(Y)
, and extended by
\mathbb{C}[{{\partial}}]
-linearity. This embedding is
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-equivariant, where on the right
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
acts by scaling only
\hbar
. This induces an
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-equivariant embedding of
\hbar
-adic vertex algebras
{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar}^{ch}(Y)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}(Y){{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
, again denoted by
\sigma_{\hbar}
. Note that
\sigma_{\hbar}
becomes an isomorphism after inverting
\hbar
.

A.3.
Microlocalizing cdos

The
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-module isomorphism
{\rm NO}_{\hbar}
can be leveraged to endow
{\rm Sym}\,L_{\hbar}(Y)
with the structure of a
\hbar
-adic vertex algebra. The
(n)
products are defined for
a,b\in{\rm Sym}\,L_{\hbar}(Y)
as

(A.3.1)

a_{(n)}b\coloneqq{\rm NO}_{\hbar}^{-1}({\rm NO}_{\hbar}(a)_{(n)}{\rm NO}_{\hbar}(b))~,

analogously to the usual star products for quantizations of a Poisson algebra. Moreover, from Remark
A.2
and the left right Wick rules, we note that

(A.3.2)

a_{(n)}b=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hbar^{k}P^{k}_{(n)}(a,b)

where
P^{k}_{(n)}
are finite order bidifferential operators.

Lemma A.4
.

With the given
(n)
-products,
{\rm NO}_{\hbar}
lifts to an isomorphism of
\hbar
-adic vertex algebras.

To construct the microlocalization of
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\hbar}(Y)
we can now follow the general logic of
[

. Note that
{\rm Sym}\,L_{\hbar,Y}
is naturally a module over
{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{J}}T^{*}Y)
and thus defines a quasicoherent sheaf of
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-modules over
{\mathcal{J}}T^{*}Y
—call this sheaf
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\hbar,Y}
.

Let

be some function and let

denote the basic Zariski open defined by

. We want to endow

A.4.
Chiral differential operators on a group

Let
G
be an algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
, then
T^{*}G
is trivial and
G
admits a sheaf of cdos. The construction of cdos over
G
and their properties has been detailed in
[
GMS00
,
AG02
]
, here we present a brief review of their most salient properties.

The isomorphism classes of cdos on
G
are in correspondence with a choice of a Chern–Simons cocycle in
H^{3}_{\rm dR}(G)
. Take
G
to be
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
or a simple Lie group, then there is a one parameter family of cdos, labeled by
k\in\mathbb{C}
. For a fixed
k
, we denote the global sections
\Gamma(G,{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{k})
by
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{k}(G)
. As a
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex algebra,
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{k}(G)
is generated in degree
0
by
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
and in degree
1
by
\mathfrak{g}
, with singular OPEs

(A.4.1)

\begin{split}x(z)a(w)&\sim\frac{x(a)(w)}{z-w}~,\\
x(z)y(w)&\sim\frac{[x,y](w)}{z-w}+\frac{k(x,y)}{(z-w)^{2}}~,\end{split}

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
a\in{\mathcal{O}}(G)
.

From the trivialization of
TG
as left invariant vector fields, we have an embedding of vertex algebras,

(A.4.2)

\Phi_{L}:V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{k}(G)~.

The trivialization of
TG
as right invariant vector fields gives rise to a complementary embedding of vertex algebras

(A.4.3)

\Phi_{R}:V^{k-2h^{\vee}}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{c}h_{k}(G)~,

but at the dual level. These are two mutually commuting current subalgebras and, moreover,

(A.4.4)

{\rm Comm}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{k}(G)}{\textup{im}}\,\Phi_{L}={\textup{im}}\,\Phi_{R}~,\text{ and }{\rm Comm}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{k}(G)}{\textup{im}}\,\Phi_{R}={\textup{im}}\,\Phi_{L}~.

From the construction of the previous section, we know that we can construct a microlocal version of
D^{ch}_{G,k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{C}
, call it
{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\hbar,G,k}
. The global sections
\Gamma({\mathcal{J}}T^{*}G,{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\hbar,G,k})\equiv{\mathcal{D}}^{ch}_{\hbar,k}(G)
are generated (over
\mathbb{C}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
) by
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
and
\mathfrak{g}
, with singular OPEs,

(A.4.5)

\begin{split}x(z)a(w)&\sim\frac{\hbar x(a)(w)}{z-w}~,\\
x(z)y(w)&\sim\frac{\hbar[x,y](w)}{z-w}+\frac{\hbar^{2}k(x,y)}{(z-w)^{2}}~,\end{split}

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
a\in{\mathcal{O}}(G)
.

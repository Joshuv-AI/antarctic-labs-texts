Prevalence of suicidal ideation and associated factors among pregnant women attending ANC follow-up clinic in Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, Northwest Ethiopia, 2022
Authors: Melak Erara Mengistu, Ephrem Mebratu Dagnew, Yabibal Berie Tadesse, Abebe Tarekegn Kassaw
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Suicide is a worldwide concern and ranks as the third leading cause of death among individuals of reproductive age. According to many findings, Ethiopia remains to suffer with a considerable number of suicide deaths and attempts, so more research on the prevalence of suicidal ideation is necessary. Since there is limited research available about suicide ideation/attempts specifically from the study area, evidence-based and solution-oriented research is necessary. This study sought to determine the prevalence of suicidal ideation and its contributing factors among pregnant women visited ante-natal care (ANC) at the University of Gondar Specialized Compressive Hospital. Institutional based cross-sectional study was conducted among 174 pregnant women who visit ante-natal care (ANC) at the University of Gondar Specialized Compressive Hospital, from June 01 to August 30, 2022. Participants were selected by using a systematic random sampling method. The World Mental Health (WMH) survey Initiative Version of the World Health Organization (WHO) Composite International Diagnostic Interview (CIDI) was used to measure suicidal ideation and attempt. The data was entered into SPSS version 25 for analysis, and descriptive and inferential statistics were done. Bivariable and multivariable binary logistic regression analyses were done to identify factors associated with suicidal ideation and attempt among pregnant women. Variables with a P value less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant with 95% CI. In the current study 174 pregnant women were screened, and 168 volunteered to participate with a response rate of 96.5%. The finding showed that the prevalence of suicidal ideation and a suicidal attempt was 5.4% (95% CI: 1.8–8.9) and 4.8% (95% CI: 1.8–8.9) respectively. Unplanned pregnancy [AOR: 1.9(1.22–0.88)], history of abortion [AOR: 2.5(1.5–12.6)], history of stillbirth [AOR: 2.32(1.7–13.2)], HIV status of seropositive [AOR: 1.5(01.2–16.0)] and history of lifetime substance use [AOR: 1.6(1.3–12.0)] were significant positive predictors of prevalence of suicidal ideation. The study finding shows that the prevalence of suicidal ideation and attempt among pregnant women was low. Unplanned pregnancy, history of abortion, history of stillbirth, HIV status of seropositive, and history of lifetime substance use were variables significantly associated with suicidal ideation among pregnant women.

Introduction

Mental health during pregnancy, particularly concerning suicidal ideation and depression, has become a significant area of study in recent years. Pregnancy brings about profound physiological, emotional, and psychological changes, making women more vulnerable to mental health disorders. Suicidal ideation and depression during pregnancy are critical issues that, if left unaddressed, can have serious consequences for both the mother and the unborn child1. depression and anxiety may also have had a negative impact on the developing fetus and may have been associated with premature births and lower birth weights2.

Depression is a prevalent complication during pregnancy, with estimates suggesting that 10–20% of pregnant women experience depressive symptoms. This condition, known as antenatal depression, can result in serious mental health consequences, including suicidal ideation3.

Suicide is an act of self-murdering/killing oneself4. An estimated 70,000 people commit suicide each year worldwide, and the rate of suicide attempts has climbed by 250% over the previous 18 years in areas experiencing conflict. Self-immolation, hanging, and poisoning are just some of the typical suicide methods5. Around 800,000 suicide attempts were made in 2015, according to the WHO, and 78% of all completed suicide attempts worldwide take place in low and middle-income nations6. A study in 90 nations found that girls were substantially more likely than boys to have suicide ideation7. Furthermore, 2.94% of Chinese adolescents have attempted suicide8.

10.3% of patients at primary care facilities in low and middle-income countries reported having suicidal thoughts in the last year, and 2.2% of those patients had actually tried suicide. The percentage of people who had suicidal thoughts varied from 3.5 to 11.1% in the general community and from 5.0 to 14.8% in a medical facility9. Suicidal ideation (20.4%) and planning (23.7%) were most common in the African region, while suicide attempts (20.5%) were most common in the western Pacific region10. In Ethiopia, the percentage of people who had suicidal thoughts and attempted suicide ranged from 1 to 55% and 0.6–14%, respectively11. The prevalence of suicide ideation and attempt was reported to be 22.5% and 13.9%, respectively, in a comparable study conducted out in Ethiopia12. In Ethiopia, wide diversity was documented despite the high burden of suicide-related negative impacts, and the study area’s prevalence of suicidal thoughts was undetermined well.

Suicide rates have been associated to the global financial crisis, natural disasters, air pollution, and secondhand smoke. Individual risk factors include prior self-harm, parental death or divorce, and being younger than peers. The research supporting the beneficial effects of drinking water lithium levels and religious affiliation is conflicting13. The total number of suicide fatalities climbed by 6.7% during the course of the 27-year study, reaching 817 000 deaths in 2016. Nonetheless, the age-standardized global suicide death rate decreased by 32.7% between 1990 and 201614. Between low- and middle-income countries in the European region (0.9%) and the Western Pacific region (48.3%), there is a significant regional variation in the proportion of suicides resulting from pesticide self-poisoning15. India has also contributed more suicide deaths worldwide: among women, it went from 25.3% in 1990 to 36.6% in 2016, while among men, it grew from 18.7 to 24.3%16. At least of 60 million people across Asia experience suicide or suicidal thoughts annually, as the region accounts for 60% of global suicides. In comparison to men, women bear a greater burden of suicide behavior when it comes to the overall burden of morbidity and death17. Additionally, statistics from various sources worldwide showed that the estimated disease burden for mental illness is 32.4% of the overall global disease burden18. The number of suicide deaths has climbed by 30% in the last 20 years. In the United States, over 45 000 persons committed suicide in 2016, making it the second-leading cause of death for those between the ages of 10 and 3419. Suicidal attempt prevalence was 10% in sub-Saharan African countries, and the 12-month prevalence estimate was 16%20. The risk of suicidal thoughts, attempts, and suicides increases with consumption of alcohol21.

The prevalence of suicidal ideation has been reported as 11.8%in Pakistani22 ,Peru 8.8%23,India 6%24,and 6.3% in Brazil25. In Ethiopia, it was found to be 9.3% in the Gedeo Zone of Southern Ethiopia26,and 13.3% in Jimma, Southwest Ethiopia27.

The likelihood of suicidal ideation/attempt was found to be 14% and 7.4%, respectively, in Ethiopia, despite recent advancements in suicide theory and research promising to meaningfully advance knowledge and preventive and risk reduction techniques for an unidentified reason28,29.

The high and steadily increasing suicide mortality rate mandates that health care professionals and researchers invest more time and money in studying and preventing. Globally, short- and long-term mental health training was provided to help reduce the consequences of suicide ideation. Similarly, one method to reduce suicidal ideation, attempts, and deaths associated to suicide is to use effective treatment and counseling30.Suicidal ideation has an adverse effect on pregnant ladies, ranging from a mild condition to death by suicide attempt in any aspect of pregnancy or perhaps in any other.

The existing few studies in low and middle-income countries indicate that the magnitude of suicidal ideation ranged between 1.7 -27.5%31,32. Being impulsivity in behavior, having a family history of suicide, having a previous diagnosis of psychiatric illness33, having a comorbid mental illness, particularly diagnosis of depression, anxiety, substance use especially alcohol use34, unwanted and unplanned pregnancy, induced pregnancy, being unemployment, violence between partner, being young, and having poor social support35. The odds of having suicidal ideation for a pregnant mother with depressive symptoms more likely than a pregnant mother without depressive symptoms. Similarly, pregnant women who had anxiety symptoms more likely to develop suicidal ideation than those who had no anxiety symptoms36.

According to numerous sources, Ethiopia continues to struggle with a significant number of suicide deaths and attempts, and more research on the prevalence of suicidal ideation is needed.

The results of this study are highly valuable in determining the prevalence of suicidal ideation and its associated factors. It also serves as a foundation for relevant stakeholders involved in screening and preventing suicide, as antenatal care provides an important opportunity to reach pregnant women.

Determining the prevalence of suicidal ideation and its contributing factors among pregnant women receiving ANC at the comprehensive specialty hospital in Gondar was the purpose of this research.

Method and materials

Study design, setting, and period

Institutional based cross-study design was conducted from May to July 30, 2022 at the University of Gondar comprehensive specialized referral ANC follow-up clinic. The town where the study was done is located 175 km (km) from Bahir Dar, the city of the Amhara regional state, and 727 km (km) from Addis Ababa, the capital of Ethiopia. Eight health centers and one comprehensive hospital are located in Gondar town and provide services to residents of Gondar town and the surrounding Woreda. According to a facility-based report from the University of Gondar comprehensive specialized referral hospital, there were approximately 6700 pregnant women receiving ANC in 2014, including those who had given birth.

Study participants and eligibility criteria

The source population for this study included all pregnant women seeking any type of ANC follow-up at the ANC clinic of the University of Gondar’s Comprehensive Specialized Referral Hospital. The study population consisted of all expectant mothers who were registered for 1 to 4 ANC visits at the University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital at the time of data collection and met the inclusion criteria. All pregnant women who visited an outpatient ANC follow-up clinic and were between the ages of 15 and 49 were included. The study section excluded pregnant women and stillbirth, pregnant women who used substances like (cannabis, alcohol, khat, cigarette smoking), abortion, and molar pregnancy for the present pregnancy. Additionally, pregnant women who were forced to participate in the study and had major medical illnesses (, like diabetes, hypertension, STDs, Tuberculosis), hearing issues (without a sign language interpreter), or both were not included in the study.

Operational definitions

Suicidal ideation

Suicidal ideation is defined as having had a significant intention of suicide within the last month.

Suicidal attempt

Suicidal attempts are defined as those made by the participants within the previous month.

Substance use

Individuals who use a social drug for non-medical purposes, such as beer, chat, cigarettes, or other drugs.

Lifetime uses substance

Individuals who take drugs for non-medical or recreational purposes, such as alcohol, marijuana, cigarettes, and opioids, do so throughout their lifetime.

Current use substance

Participants in this study were those who had used opioids, alcohol, marijuana, or other drugs for non-medical or recreational purposes within the previous three months.

Data collection instrument and procedures

A validated questionnaire using interviews was used to obtain the data. The questionnaire includes on (I) sociodemographic factors, (II) reproductive history, (III) mental health issues, (IV) substance use, (V) suicide ideation, and (VI) social support. The World Mental Health (WMH) survey Initiative Version of the World Health Organization (WHO) Composite International Diagnostic Interview (CIDI) was used to measure suicidal ideation and attempt37. It is a widely used instrument that is employed in numerous research in Ethiopia to evaluate suicidal thoughts and attempts across various populations and settings, encompassing both clinical and community levels. According to this tool, a respondent who answers “yes” to the question “have you seriously considered committing suicide during the current pregnancy” is said to be currently suicidal, while a respondent who answers “yes” to the question “have you attempted committing suicide during the current pregnancy” is said to be currently suicidal. The tool’s Amharic version was assessed and determined to be practicable, reliable, and approved for use in Ethiopia to explore mental health problems38.

A self-administered questionnaire (SAQ-20) with 20 YES or NO questions was used to determine whether a prenatal common mental illness was present. Assessment of the existence of common mental disorders such depression, anxiety-related disorder, somatoform, and other neurotic disorders is valid and recommended by WHO. SQR is a very helpful technique for screening psychiatric morbidity in various populations and requires minimal training to use. Compared to other tools, it is relatively simple to use and to interpret. Based on the SRQ-20’s total score cut-off points, which are often used in developing countries like Ethiopia to screen more people and improve mental health, participants who scored 6 or above were classified as having an antenatal common mental illness39. The OSLO social support measurement instrument was used to assess social support. Using a 3-item questionnaire, social support is evaluated. It receives a 3–14 overall rating across its three categories. Poor “3–8)”. 9–11: Moderate; 12–14: Strong social support”40. The WHO violence measure tool, that is used to evaluate intimate partner violence, was used to assess physical violence. The WHO suggests using this 13-item checklist to evaluate instances of physical, sexual, and emotional violence41.

Numerous investigations involve different populations, particularly pregnant women, were carried out using this screening tool. Six questionnaires are part of the physical violence screening part, which assesses past and present physical violence as well as who the abuser is. Based on this, at least one YES response from the scale among six items qualifies the respondents for being faced with physical violence42. Those pregnant women who use at least one of the specified substances (alcohol, khat, tobacco, cannabis, and shisha) for non-medical purposes in the last 3 months were considered positive for current substance use43.

Antenatal care (ANC) is defined in this study as the care provided to pregnant women at the time of pregnancy44. A pregnancy that is UN intended at the time of conception is termed an unplanned pregnancy44. Whereas unwanted pregnancy is a pregnancy that is either mistime or unintended whether it is planned or not. Gestational age is the pregnancy stage that is categorized as the first, second, and third trimester if the duration of pregnancy was 1–3 months, 4–6 months, and 7 months and more respectively. Items on socio-demographic, obstetric, and clinical factors such as having a diagnosis of mental disorder and chronic medical illness (hypertension, DM, HIV/ADIS), substance use disorder, and family-related conditions were adopted from different articles45.

Data collection method

Two skilled clinical midwives who worked in the ANC clinic at the University of Gondar Compressive Specialized Hospital (UoGCSH) collected the data. For one day, the researcher provided the data collectors with training. on the study’s objectives, procedures, and data collection methods. In the ANC follow-up clinic, all pregnant women’s logbook chart numbers for the periods of May to July 2022 were listed, then manually entered into Microsoft Office Excel 2013 and checked for duplication. Data was collected at a UoGCSH outpatient ANC follow-up clinic. Face-to-face interviews were used to collect the relevant data on patient characteristics and clinical outcomes.

Data quality assurance

The study’s aim and the tool for data abstraction used during data collecting were both described clearly to the data collectors by the supervisor. The data was cleaned and processed after being confirmed to be complete. To ensure that the data abstraction format was complete, a pre-test on 5% of the sample size was performed. The format was then adjusted in the suitable way. The pre-test participants who were pregnant were not included in the final analyses.

Data management and analysis

Kobo was used to collect the data, which was then cleaned up and validated for completeness before being coded, entered, and exported to SPSS Version 26 for analysis. The characteristics of the study participants are described, and the study findings are presented, using descriptive statistics, means, proportions, tables, and figures. Variables that met the chi-square assumptions were first incorporated into a simple logistic regression after the chi-square test was done. Then, variables from the simple logistic regression that had a p-value of less than 0.2 were included to the multivariable logistic regression. An adjusted odds ratio (AOR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) were used to evaluate the degree and direction of correlations between the dependent and independent variables in order to identify characteristics related with suicidal ideation and attempt. A p-value of 0.05 or lower was used to indicate statistical significance. The Hosmer and Lemeshow test of goodness of fit was used to determine whether the models were adequate.

Prevalence of suicidal ideation and attempt

In this study, the prevalence of suicidal ideation and suicidal attempts was 5.4%(95CI: 1.8–8.9) (Fig. 1) and 4.8%( 95%CI: 1.8–8.9) (Fig. 2) respectively. Among the overall study participants, about 5.4%9 ever seriously thought about committing suicide, before one month 3% and within one month 2.4% respectively. Furthermore, 4.2% and 3.6% of pregnant women seriously thought about committing suicide within the last 1 month and ever made a plan for committing suicide respectively. Among study participants who planned to comment suicide, 3.6% did not ever make a plan and 1.2% ever made a plan for committing suicide. However, 4.8% of the study subjects attempt suicide before one month, and within one month 1.8% respectively. Similarly, 4.8% of the pregnant women ever attempt suicide within the last 1 month by using methods of suicide such as hanging 0.6%, jumping from a high place 0.6%, poison 3%, and using sharp materials 1.2% respectively. The most common reasons for attempting suicide were economic problems 1.2%, family conflict 0.6% and unplanned pregnancy 1.8% respectively (Table 4).

Discussion

The current study tried to assess suicidal ideation and attempt among pregnant women having ANC follow-up from UoG comprehensive specialized referral hospital. The finding showed that the prevalence of suicidal ideation and attempt was 5.4%(95% CI: 1.8–8.9 and 4.8%( 95%CI: 1.8–8.9) respectively. Similarly, 4.8% of the study subjects attempt suicide before one month and within one month1.8% respectively. In the current study, the prevalence of suicidal ideation among pregnant women was in line with a similar study done in Jimma 5.5%46. However, the finding was lower compared with the study conducted in Southern Ethiopia 11.8%47.

The discrepancy between the two sites could possibly be the result of hormonal variations, which may be a significant factor. Women are more susceptible to violence, physical and sexual abuse, neglect, and gender-based discrimination. In a similar fashion, the study’s findings were less significant than those of an Ethiopian study that found that 9.3% of pregnant women in southern Ethiopia participated in suicide behavior48. Though the study finding was lower than the current study in Ethiopia the prevalence of suicidal ideation (was 11.1%)(37%) and in the Amhara region of Dangila Woreda the prevalence of suicide ideation and an attempt was 22.5% and 16.2%, respectively49. Another possible reason might be the difference in participants who had different socio-economic and demographic characteristics in the populations.

Similarly, the prevalence of suicidal ideation (SI) among pregnant women was reported to be 13.3% in the current study, which was lower than the study in Jimma Medical Center27. Increased stressors and negative life events, such as congested living conditions, cultural influences, formal or informal norms, limitations on personal freedom, polluted environments, and diminished social support, may be to reason for the gap. Other potential causes include extended periods of isolation, inadequate mental health care, and feelings of extreme rage, frustration, and anxiety.

However, the outputs from the current study was higher than a similar study done in Nigeria 2.8%50. Similarly, the study finding was higher compared with the report of research from Sri Lanka (0.8%), (2.3%), and (2.7%) of pregnant women had suicidal, quite a lot, sometimes, and hardly ever respectively51. The discrepancy could be the difference in the assessment tool. Another discrepancy might be due to the difference in study design, study setting, sample size, social support practice, and sampling technique, the socio-demographic and cultural context of the women. But lower compared with the study done on to assess pregnant women regarding suicidal ideation, the study finding resulted that 22% expressed suicidal ideation52 and higher than the study conducted in African countries, with twice as many women reporting suicide attempts as men (3.8% vs. 1.8%) and that pregnancy is a critical time for most women, and this area is under-researched in South Africa53.

However, the finding was consistent with the report from international of suicidal ideation during pregnancy. In this regard, the prevalence of suicidal ideation during pregnancy was between 2.73 and 18% internationally54. But lower compared with a similar study at Zagazig University in 2019, 22.2%55. The possible explanation may be due to limited liberty, autonomy, and lack of communication with family and friends and the consequence of prevailing conditions like social exclusion, disadvantage, and traumatic adversities prior to mental problems. It may be also due to the direct effect of depression which makes individuals feel hopeless, isolated, and worthless.

The study also assesses different factors which affect suicidal ideation. Accordingly, variables such as unplanned pregnancy, history of abortion, history still birth, HIV status of seropositive, and history of lifetime substance were significant positive predictors of prevalence of suicidal ideation. This study was supported by the study finding from Ethiopia56. However, the study finding was inconsistent with a similar study in the Gedeo zone, which indicated that literacy, being a wife of a husband with three and more other wives, current history of depression, intimate partner violence, and poor social support were significantly associated with suicidal behavior47. This discrepancy could be due to pregnancy and previous experience of birth can situate women into conditions outside their comfort zones, which also cause anxiety; anxious concerns will be about the health of their baby, fear of experiencing giving birth or worry about weight gain, body shape, and being a responsible parent.

This study indicated that the likelihood of experiencing suicidal ideation was higher among women with unplanned pregnancies compared to those with planned pregnancies. This finding similar with previous studies in Brazil25 and Ethiopia57. This could be due to the fact that women with unplanned pregnancies may feel unprepared for the responsibilities of childbirth and child-rearing, especially if they are financially dependent on their families. This situation can lead to stress and thoughts of ending their lives. Additionally, if their sexual partner does not accept or support the pregnancy, it can create further stress for the expectant mother, potentially leading to suicidal ideation.

The study found that a history of abortion significantly predicted the prevalence of suicidal ideation. This may be due to the association between abortion and psychological distress, such as higher rates of depression, anxiety, and suicidal thoughts. Feelings of guilt, shame, and loss following an abortion can contribute to emotional challenges, potentially leading to suicidal ideation58.

The loss of a child due to stillbirth was significantly associated with the outcome and can result in profound grief and trauma, potentially leading to depression and suicidal ideation. Mothers who experience stillbirth often endure extended psychological distress, including post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and complicated grief, both of which are linked to an increased risk of suicidal thoughts59.

Individuals who are HIV-positive face a heightened risk of mental health issues, such as depression and suicidal ideation, due to the stigma associated with the condition, the challenges of managing a chronic illness, and the emotional burden of living with a life-threatening disease60.

Because managing and preventing suicide ideation is not a typical practice in Ethiopia, the results of this study will assist medical professionals in managing, treating, and preventing suicidal ideation. Additionally, despite the management, treatment, and prevention of suicide thought and attempt, clients and victims were unaware of how to seek medical attention. Therefore, the research findings will help pregnant woman problem how to approach to health care facilities by increasing health-seeking behaviors. The research findings also intended to enhance the health care providers to create awareness to pregnant mothers regarding suicide issues. However, the current study has some limitations. Since it employed a cross-sectional design, there is an inherent “chicken-or-egg” dilemma, making it challenging to establish true causality.

Additionally, the use of face-to-face interviews may have introduced recall bias and social desirability bias, potentially affecting the accuracy of participants’ responses. Moreover, the relatively small sample size may reduce the precision of the study’s findings.

Without controlling for substance use, it is not possible to conclude whether suicidal ideation is primarily influenced by pregnancy-related factors or by the effects of substance abuse. Therefore, it is recommended that future studies either exclude participants with a history of substance abuse or include substance abuse as a control variable in the analysis.

Conclusion

According to the current study, there were relatively few pregnant women who had suicidal thoughts or attempted suicide. Unplanned pregnancies, abortion and stillbirth histories, HIV seropositivity, and lifetime substance use histories are all independent indicators of suicide thoughts in pregnant women. Despite the low rate of suicide thoughts, pregnant women are more likely to participate in dangerous drug use behaviors. Therefore, it is important that traditional and local community leaders, local and international women’s rights organizations, human rights associations, religious leaders, and community mental health workers collaborate to prevent and reduce this harmful traditional and cultural practice. The significant frequency of STI among pregnant women supports screening and treatment for prenatal SI.

Abstract
. We use the LLV algebra to describe the action of a finite order automorphism on the total cohomology of a manifold of OG10 type. As an application, we prove that no Enriques manifolds arise as étale quotients of hyper-Kähler manifolds of OG10 type. This answers a question raised in [ PS23 ] .

14J42 (14J50)

1.
Introduction

The celebrated Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem states that any compact complex Kähler manifold with a numerically trivial canonical bundle decomposes, after a finite étale morphism, into a product of three types of varieties: hyper-Kähler manifolds, strict Calabi–Yau manifolds, and abelian varieties. These three building blocks have been the subject of extensive study over the past decades, with hyper-Kähler manifolds (discovered in the 1980s) attracting particular attention due to their rich geometry and connection to various fields of mathematics.

Recently, attention has shifted towards understanding complex Kähler manifolds with numerically trivial canonical bundle in greater generality. In this context, Enriques manifolds have been introduced independently by
[
BNWS11a
]
and
[
OS11a
]
with slightly different definitions.
For the result in the current paper, we adopt the definition given in
[
OS11a
]
. This excludes the case where the universal cover is Calabi-Yau, which was allowed in the definition of
[
BNWS11b
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

An Enriques manifold is a connected complex manifold that is not simply connected and whose universal cover is a hyper-Kähler manifold.

Various results related to these manifolds have been established:
the Torelli Theorem
[
OS11b
,
Ram24
]
, the termination of flips
[
DOTX24
]
, and the Kawamata-Morrison cone conjecture for all Enriques manifolds of prime index
[
PS23
]
. Also, singular counterparts have been introduced and analyzed
[
BCS24
,
DOTX24
]
.

Unlike the two-dimensional case, where Enriques surfaces are well-understood and have a degree-2 universal cover, higher dimensional Enriques manifolds exhibit greater complexity. Examples of index
2
,
3
and
4
are constructed starting from a hyper-Kähler manifold of K3
[n]
-type or of
K_{n}(A)
-type
[
BNWS11a
,
OS11a
]
.
Nevertheless, to date, these are all the degrees for which examples of Enriques manifolds are known. It is then crucial to produce more examples of Enriques manifolds or to demonstrate that certain deformation types of hyper-Kähler manifolds cannot cover Enriques manifolds.

In this paper, we address the latter goal by proving that no Enriques manifold can arise as an étale quotient of a hyper-Kähler manifold deformation equivalent to O’Grady’s ten-dimensional example, answering a question of
[
PS23
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic facts on Enriques manifolds and on the LLV-algebra.

2.1.
Enriques manifolds

The definition that we refer to is Definition
1.1
. For the sake of clarification, it coincides with the definition of
weak Enriques varieties
in
[
BNWS11a
, §2.2]
with the additional assumption of being irreducible with universal cover not strict Calabi-Yau, leaving the only possibility for the universal cover to be hyper-Kähler. Consider a compact hyper-Kähler manifold
X
of dimension
2n
,
n\geq 2
, and let
f
be an automorphism of
X
of order
d\geq 2
such that the cyclic group generated by
\langle f\rangle
acts freely on
X
.
It follows that
f
acts purely non-symplectic, i.e., there exists a primitive
d
-th root of the unity
\zeta
, with
d\geq 2
, such that the action of
f
on the symplectic form
\sigma_{X}
is given by
f^{*}(\sigma_{X})=\zeta\sigma_{X}
.
By a result of Beauville (
[
Bea83
, Proposition 6]
) the variety
X
is necessarily projective.
The étale quotient
Y=X/\langle f\rangle
is a smooth projective Enriques manifold of index
d
.

2.2.
The LLV algebra

If
\omega\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is a Kähler class, there is an associated Lefschetz operator
L_{\omega}=\omega\cup-
. It forms an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple
\{L_{\omega},h,\Lambda_{\omega}\}
where
\Lambda_{\omega}
is the dual Lefschetz operator, and
h\colon{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is the degree operator
h\cdot x=(k-\dim X)x
for
x\in{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.
For any class
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, the operator
L_{\alpha}
is still well defined and the existence (and uniqueness) of an operator
\Lambda_{\alpha}
satisfying the relation
[L_{\alpha},\Lambda_{\alpha}]=h
is an open condition in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
by the Jacobson–Morozov Theorem.

Definition 2.1
.

The Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky (LLV) algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
X
is the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
generated by all the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triples associated with classes
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple algebra defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Work of Verbitsky
[
Ver90
]
and Looijenga-Lunts
[
LL97
]
shows that
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be identified with the special orthogonal algebra of the Mukai completion
V={\mathrm{H}}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus U
where
U
is the hyperbolic plane. In particular, it is isomorphic to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(4,b_{2}(X)-2)
.

A remarkable fact is that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the decomposition of
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
in irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-representations are diffeomorphism invariants of
X
, and hence they depend only on the deformation class of
X
. The irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-representations are indexed by their highest weights, which are
non-negative integral linear combinations of the fundamental weights. An irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-representation with highest weight
\mu
will be denoted by
V_{\mu}
.

If
X
is a manifold of OG10 type, then the LLV algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra of type D. The decomposition

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong V_{(5)}\oplus V_{(2,2)}

in irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-representations has been computed in
[
GKLR22
, Theorem 1.2 (iv)]
. We refer to
[
GKLR22
, Appendix A.2]
for a detailed description of the weights. Here the representation
V_{(5)}
is naturally identified with the image of the map
\Sym^{*}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, and it is called the
Verbitsky component
, according to the original paper
[
Ver90
]
.

Since we only consider cohomology groups of OG10 type manifolds with rational coefficients, we simplify the notation by writing

For an automorphism

Remark 3.1
.

Given a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
acting on a vector space

{\mathfrak{g}}
-submodule containing a subset

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}\cdot W=\left\{\sum g_{i1}\cdot g_{i2}\cdots g_{ij_{i}}\cdot x_{i}\mbox{ such that }x_{i}\in W,\ g_{ji}\in{\mathfrak{g}},\mbox{ and the sum is finite}\right\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exist unique

\displaystyle H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})=A\oplus B

Proof.

From the original paper of Verbitsky
[
Ver90

the submodule

is naturally identified with the image of the natural morphism
\Sym^{*}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, which can be described also as
\mathfrak{g}\cdot H^{0}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. Let

be two

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
be of finite order. Then
\varphi^{*}
preserves the LLV decomposition (
2

\varphi^{*}(A)=A
and
\varphi^{*}(B)=B

{\mathrm{eig}}(\varphi^{*},H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),1)={\mathrm{eig}}(\varphi^{*},A,1)+{\mathrm{eig}}(\varphi^{*},B,1).

Proof.

As

we can write any element

x=\beta+\sum\alpha_{i_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup\alpha_{i_{r}}

for some
\beta\in H^{0}

\varphi^{*}x=\varphi^{*}\beta+\sum\varphi^{*}\alpha_{i_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup\varphi^{*}\alpha_{i_{r}}

and conclude that
\varphi^{*}x\in A

Now we turn our attention to the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-submodule

is orthogonal with respect to the intersection form. Indeed, the intersection product of an element

with an element

can be expressed in terms of the
{\mathfrak{g}}

As
\varphi^{*}

\varphi^{*}(A)=A

\varphi^{*}x\in B\cap H^{10}

for any nonzero element

, so that any element

\displaystyle g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\cdots g_{r}\cdot x

for some
\alpha,\beta\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})

We compute

\varphi^{*}(L_{\alpha}\cdot x)=\varphi^{*}(\alpha\cup x)=\varphi^{*}\alpha\cup\varphi^{*}x=L_{\varphi^{*}\alpha}\cdot\varphi^{*}x

\Lambda_{\beta}:=*^{-1}\circ L_{\beta}\circ*

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\cdots g_{r}\cdot x)=g_{1}^{\prime}\cdot g_{2}^{\prime}\cdots g_{r}^{\prime}\cdot\varphi^{*}x

and this finishes the proof.

∎

G=\langle\varphi\rangle
of order 2 and 3 acting on

.

, then

\displaystyle 176904=\dim{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=e(X)=2\cdot e(Y)=2\cdot{\mathrm{eig}}(\varphi^{*},H^{*}(X),1).

Chord Sobolev inequalities
Fernanda M. Ba E ^ \hat{\rm E} ta and Xiaxing Cai

Abstract
. The paper establishes a new family of analytic inequalities. Together with the fractional Sobolev inequalities of Almgren and Lieb [ 1 ] , they form a complete class of analytic inequalities, referred to as the chord Sobolev inequalities. The paper also demonstrates the connection of all these inequalities to the chord isoperimetric inequalities in integral geometry. The limiting cases of the chord Sobolev inequalities are derived as well, one of which can be viewed as a logarithmic Sobolev-type inequality. These limiting cases, combined with the work of Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu [ 9 ] , complete the theory of chord Sobolev inequalities.



1.
introduction

The sharp
Sobolev inequality
states that for any
C^{1}
function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with compact support,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x)|dx\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}},

(1.1)

where
|\nabla f(x)|
denotes the Euclidean norm of the gradient of
f
and
\|f\|_{p}=\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\big)^{1/p}
denotes the
L^{p}
norm of
f
. The notation
\omega_{n}=\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2+1)
stands for the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Gamma
is the gamma function. This inequality lies at the interface of analysis and geometry, linking isoperimetric phenomena with functional inequalities, and has been the subject of extensive and influential research; see, e.g.,
[
3
,
7
,
19
,
24
,
25
,
30
,
31
,
59
,
62
]
. The inequality extends to functions of bounded variation, serving as a functional analogue of the classical
isoperimetric inequality
(see, e.g.,
[
4
,
10
,
16
,
21
,
22
]
). For a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the classical isoperimetric inequality states that

S(E)\geq n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
S(E)
and
|E|
denote the surface area and volume of
E
, respectively.

Beyond the classical isoperimetric inequality, a number of important geometric inequalities also admit powerful functional extensions. Notable examples include the Brunn–Minkowski inequality, the Blaschke–Santaló inequality, and the fractional isoperimetric inequalities; their analytic counterparts capture and naturally generalize the underlying geometric structures (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
26
,
50
]
). In particular, the fractional isoperimetric inequality can be lifted to the following fractional Sobolev inequality of Almgren and Lieb
[
1
]
; see also
[
26
,
39
,
40
]
.

Fractional Sobolev inequalities:
For
\alpha\in(-1,0)
and
f\in W^{-\alpha,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there is a constant
\sigma_{n,\alpha}>0
(with explicit value given in (
1.4
); see also
[
23
,
27
]
) such that

2\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

(1.2)

where
W^{-\alpha,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the fractional Sobolev space of integrable functions such that the right-hand side of (
1.2
) is finite. Equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball.

Almgren and Lieb
[
1
]
established the Pólya–Szegő inequalities for
the right-hand side of (
1.2
) and the equality cases were later characterized by Frank and Seiringer
[
26
]
. A direct consequence is the above fractional Sobolev inequality. In particular, Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
9
]
showed that the fractional Sobolev inequality converges to the classical Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) in the limit
\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}
.

The main aim of this paper is to establish a new family of analytic inequalities which, together with the fractional Sobolev inequalities (
1.2
), we refer to as the
chord Sobolev inequalities
for
\alpha>-1
. These newly established inequalities, with their limiting cases, extend and complete the works of Almgren and Lieb
[
1
]
and Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu
[
9
]
.

Theorem A
.

For
\alpha\in(0,n)
and non-negative
f\in L^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

(1.3)

where the sharp constant is given by

\sigma_{n,\alpha}=\frac{n2^{\alpha}\pi^{\frac{n-\alpha-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{|\alpha|\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2}+1)}.

(1.4)

Equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball.

The inequality (
1.3
) can be regarded as an
L^{1}
-extension of the sharp
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
established by Lieb
[
37
]
. For
\alpha\in(0,n)
and non-negative
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there is a explicitly known constant
\gamma_{n,\alpha}>0
such that

\gamma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n+\alpha}}^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

which is equivalent to the fractional
L^{2}
Sobolev inequality (see
[
15
]
).

The following theorem addresses the case
\alpha>n
, where
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space of essentially bounded measurable functions, equipped with the essential supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\alpha>n
and
f
be a non-zero, non-negative function in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then

\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{1}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

(1.5)

where
\sigma_{n,\alpha}
is given in (
1.4
). Equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball.

The chord Sobolev inequalities arise from isoperimetric inequalities for random lines in integral geometry, known as
chord isoperimetric inequalities
. More precisely, they can be interpreted as functional analogues of isoperimetric inequalities for chord power integrals, with
\alpha
ranging over different intervals.

For
\alpha\geq-1
and a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(a convex compact set with non-empty interior), the chord power integral of
K
is defined by

I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|K\cap l|^{\alpha+1}dl,

where
|K\cap l|
denotes the length of the intersection. Here
{\rm Aff}(n,1)
denotes the affine Grassmannian of
1
-dimensional lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
dl
is the standard Haar measure on it (see Section
4
for details). The chord power integral has attracted increasing attention in recent years, mainly in connection with Minkowski-type problems (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
29
,
34
,
42
,
54
,
55
]
). For recent results on inequalities involving chord power integrals, we refer the reader to
[
56
,
57
,
58
,
61
]
.

The chord isoperimetric inequalities state that there exists
\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}=\frac{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\sigma_{n,\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(K)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}|K|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}},\quad\alpha\in(-1,0)\cup(n,\infty),

(1.6)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(K)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}|K|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}},\quad\alpha\in(0,n),

(1.7)

which were proved by Blaschke in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and proved by Ren
[
47
]
, by Davy
[
18
]
, and by Schneider
[
51
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for integer
\alpha
. For non-integer
\alpha>-1
, these inequalities were proved by Zhang
[
60
]
; see also
[
46
]
.

When
\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}
, inequality (
1.6
) reduces to the classical isoperimetric inequality, which follows from Cauchy’s integral formula, as discussed in Section
4
. Federer and Fleming
[
22
]
and Ma
{\rm z}^{\prime}
ya
[
43
]
independently discovered the remarkable connection between the sharp Sobolev inequality and the classical isoperimetric inequality. Zhang
[
62
]
proved a strengthening of this correspondence by establishing the affine Sobolev inequality and an integral affine isoperimetric inequality for domains. We next demonstrate the connection between the chord Sobolev inequalities and the chord isoperimetric inequalities.

A primary motivation comes from the following integral representations of the chord power integrals:

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\frac{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)}{2n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\operatorname{1}_{K}(x)-\operatorname{1}_{K}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dx\,dy,

\displaystyle\alpha\in(-1,0),

(1.8)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{K}(x)\operatorname{1}_{K}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dx\,dy,

\displaystyle\alpha>0,

(1.9)

where
\operatorname{1}_{K}
denotes the characteristic function of
K
, taking value 1 in
K
and 0 outside. Formula (
1.9
) is classical and related to Riesz potentials (see
[
46
,
49
]
). Formula (
1.8
) was first introduced by Qin
[
45
]
(see
[
52
, Theorem 7.2.7]
for a generalization) and is called the fractional integral of
K
. These formulas illustrate the geometric–functional correspondence between the chord power integral and the integrals appearing in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).

Motivated by these formulas, we extend the notion of chord power integrals to non-negative measurable functions by

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(f)=\frac{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)}{2n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

\displaystyle\alpha\in(-1,0),

(1.10)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(f)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

\displaystyle\alpha>0.

Here, for
\alpha>0
, we use
\min\{f(x),f(y)\}
rather than
f(x)f(y)
in order to preserve the
1
-homogeneity present in (
1.8
). If
f
is a log-concave function, (
1.10
) admits a unified formulation in terms of level sets:

I_{\alpha+1}(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt.

(1.11)

In Section
5
, we show that the integral in (
1.11
) is finite and well-defined for
\alpha\geq-1
, provided that
f
is log-concave and integrable.

Using the representation (
1.11
), for a log-concave, integrable function
f
, the chord Sobolev inequalities can be rewritten as

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}},\quad\alpha\in(-1,0),

(1.12)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}},\quad\alpha\in(0,n),

and

I_{\alpha+1}(f)\geq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{1}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}},\quad\alpha>n,

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}
appears in (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). Inequality (
1.12
) converges to the classical Sobolev inequality by the work of Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu
[
9
]
. When
f=\operatorname{1}_{K}
is the characteristic function of a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, these inequalities reduce to the chord isoperimetric inequalities (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). Note that in the functional setting, the inequalities for
\alpha>n
take a different form from those for
\alpha\in(-1,0)
, unlike the chord isoperimetric inequalities, which have the same form in both ranges. These distinctions are discussed in detail in Section
3
.

In Sections
7
and
8
, we investigate the limiting cases of the chord Sobolev inequalities at the endpoints
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=n
. In the geometric setting, the chord isoperimetric inequalities (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) reduce to identities in the limits
\alpha\to 0
and
\alpha\to n
, respectively. Xu
[
58
]
and Zhang
[
61
]
approached these limits by establishing the chord entropy inequalities. In Section
7
, we employ the definition (
1.11
) to extend the chord entropy inequalities to log-concave functions
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In particular, we prove that, as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, the chord Sobolev inequality reduces to an identity under the sole assumption that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is non-negative. By a result of Ma
{\rm z}^{\prime}
ya and Shaposhnikova
[
44
]
, the fractional Sobolev inequality (
1.2
) likewise reduces to an identity as
\alpha\to 0^{-}
, provided that
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. This collapse to an identity opens the way to an inequality for substantially broader classes of functions, which can be viewed as a logarithmic Sobolev–type inequality (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
12
,
14
,
35
,
48
]
).

In Section
8
, we pursue this direction further and generalize the chord entropy inequality at
\alpha=0
, leading to the following logarithmic Sobolev–type inequality.

Theorem C
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be fixed, and
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-negative function such that
\|f\|_{1}=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx
is finite. Then

\sigma_{0}-\omega_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}-e^{-|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy,

(1.13)

where the optimal constant is given by
\sigma_{0}=\frac{d}{d\alpha}|_{\alpha=0}\alpha\sigma_{n,\alpha}-n\omega_{n}\Gamma^{\prime}(1)
. Equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball.

The normalization
\|f\|_{1}=1
is not essential in Theorem C; a more general form is given in Section
8
. Moreover, the right-hand side of (
1.13
) admits a geometric interpretation in terms of the dual mixed volume
\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f)
. This interpretation is established in Section
8
, while the notion of
\tilde{V}_{\log}
is introduced in Section
2.3
. Here
{\rm R}_{0}f
denotes the functional
0
-th radial mean body of
f
. Haddad and Ludwig
[
33
]
also use the notation
{\rm R}_{\alpha}f
for the functional
\alpha
-th radial mean body. We use the same notation for an
L^{1}
-extension to keep the notation simple, even though their definition is slightly different. For further discussion, see Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

We collect some basic notation and results on
L^{p}
spaces, Schwarz symmetrals, star-shaped sets. The books of Schneider
[
50
]
and Lieb and Loss
[
38
]
are good general references.

2.1.
\boldsymbol{L^{p}}
spaces

Let
p>-1
and
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a measure space. In this paper, we mainly consider the case
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mu
being the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

For
p\neq 0
and a measurable function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let

\|f\|_{p}=\Big(\int_{X}|f(x)|^{p}d\mu(x)\Big)^{1/p}

and

\|f\|_{\infty}=\inf\{C>0:|f(x)|\leq C~~\text{almost everywhere on}~X\}.

In particular, if
p\in(-1,0)
and
\mu(\{x\in X:f(x)=0\})>0
, we set
\|f\|_{p}=\infty
. The space
L^{p}(X)
is then defined as

L^{p}(X)=\{f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:f~\text{is measurable},~\|f\|_{p}<\infty\},

where
f=g
in
L^{p}(X)
means that
f
and
g
are equal almost everywhere.

Assume that
\mu(X)
is finite. For non-negative and
\mu
-integrable function
f
, the
p
-th mean of
f
is defined by

M_{p}f=\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}f(x)^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}

for
p\neq 0
. By Jensen’s inequality,

M_{p}f\leq M_{q}f

for
-1<p<q<0
or
0<p<q<\infty
. By the monotonicity of
M_{p}f
with respect to
p
, there is

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\log M_{p}f=\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}\log f(x)d\mu(x).

Similarly, for
p\neq 0
, set

N_{p}f=\Big(\int_{X}f(x)^{p+1}d\mu(x)\Big)^{1/p}.

If we additionally assume that
\mu(X)=1
, one can show that

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\log N_{p}f=\int_{X}f(x)\log f(x)d\mu(x),

(2.1)

which is also known as the entropy of
f
. For more information about
p
-th mean, we refer the reader to
[
28
]
.

2.2.
Symmetrization

For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the characteristic function
\operatorname{1}_{E}
is defined by
\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)=1
for
x\in E
, and
\operatorname{1}_{E}(x)=0
for
x\notin E
. If
E
is a Borel set of finite measure, the Schwarz symmetral of
E
, denoted by
E^{\star}
, is the closed, centered ball with the same volume as
E
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative measurable function. The superlevel set of
f
at height
t>0
is defined by
\{f\geq t\}=\{x\in X:f(x)\geq t\}
. We say that
f
is non-zero if
\{f\neq 0\}
has positive measure. If the superlevel sets of
f
have finite measure, the layer cake formula gives

f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)dt

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The Schwarz symmetral of
f
, denoted by
f^{\star}
, is defined as

f^{\star}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(x)dt

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence
f^{\star}
is radially symmetric and the superlevel set
\{f^{\star}\geq t\}
has the same volume as
\{f\geq t\}
for every
t>0
.

A key technique in our proofs is the Riesz rearrangement inequality (see, e.g.,
[
38
, Theorem 3.7]
) and the characterization of equality cases due to Burchard
[
11
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Riesz’s rearrangement inequality.)
.

For measurable
f,g,k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with superlevel sets of finite measure.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)k(x-y)g(y)dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)k^{\star}(x-y)g^{\star}(y)dxdy.

Theorem 2.2

(Burchard)
.

Let
A,B
and
C
be sets of finite positive measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote by
a,b
and
c
the radii of their Schwarz symmetrals
A^{\star},B^{\star}
and
C^{\star}
. For
|a-b|<c<a+b
, there is equality in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{A}(x)\operatorname{1}_{B}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{C}(y)dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{A^{\star}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{B^{\star}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{C^{\star}}(y)dxdy

if and only if, up to sets of measure zero,

A=x_{0}+aD,~B=x_{1}+bD,~C=x_{2}+cD,

where
D
is a centered ellipsoid, and
x_{0},x_{1}
and
x_{2}=x_{0}+x_{1}
are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.3.
Star-shaped sets and dual mixed volumes.

We say that
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a star-shaped set (with respect to the origin) if for any
x\in L
the line segment
[0,x]
is contained in
L
. For a star-shaped set
L
, the radial function
\rho_{L}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is defined by

\rho_{L}(x)=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda x\in L\}.

A star-shaped set
L
is said to be a star body if
\rho_{L}
is continuous and strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. We say that two star-shaped sets are dilates if
\rho_{K}(u)=c\rho_{L}(u)
for some
c>0
and almost all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

For a star-shaped set
L
with measurable radial function, the
n
-dimensional volume, or the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
L
is given by

|L|=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{L}(u)^{n}du,

where
du
is the spherical Lebesgue measure. The dual mixed volume for star bodies
K
and
L
introduced by Lutwak
[
41
]
is given by

\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(u)^{n-\alpha}\rho_{L}(u)^{\alpha}du,

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,n\}
. When
L=B^{n}
, the quantity
\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,B^{n})
is also known as the dual volume of
K
. In particular,

\tilde{V}_{\log}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(u)^{n}\log\Big(\frac{\rho_{K}(u)}{\rho_{L}(u)}\Big)du,

where

\tilde{V}_{\log}(K,L)=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\log\Big(\frac{\tilde{V}_{\alpha}(K,L)}{|K|}\Big)^{1/\alpha}.

For simplicity, we formulate the above definitions for star bodies.
For general star-shaped sets, the same definitions apply after restricting the integration to the set of directions
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
where the radial functions
\rho_{K}(u)
and
\rho_{L}(u)
are positive.

3.
Chord Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we focus on the proof of Theorem A and Theorem B. Before presenting the proof, we first establish a technical lemma, which is based on the Minkowski inequality (see, for example,
[
38
, Theorem 2.4]
). Let
F
be a non-negative, measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p\geq 1
. The Minkowski inequality states that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x,y)^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}dy\geq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x,y)dy\Big)^{p}dx\Big)^{1/p}.

(3.1)

Equality and finiteness in (
3.1
) for
p\in(1,\infty)
imply the existence of measurable functions
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
F(x,y)=\varphi(x)\psi(y)
almost everywhere.

We first consider the case
\alpha\in(0,n)
. The following lemma is a key step in the proof of Theorem A. Related arguments for the cases
\alpha\in(-1,0)
and
\alpha=-1
were established by Ludwig
[
39
]
and Zhang
[
62
]
, respectively; see also
[
32
]
for further discussion.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\alpha\in(0,n)
and non-negative
f\in L^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt.

Equality holds if and only if
f=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
for some measurable set
E
of finite measure and
c>0
is a constant.

Proof.

By the Minkowski inequality (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}dx

(3.2)

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}},

that is

\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt.

It is clear that equality is attained when
f=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
. Conversely, if equality holds in (
3.2
), there are non-negative measurable functions
\varphi
and
\psi
such that

\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)=\varphi(x)\psi(t).

This implies that

\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:f(x)\geq t\}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi(x)>0\}\times\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\psi(t)>0\}.

Therefore, for every fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
t>0
and
t\leq f(x)
for some
x
, the superlevel set
\{f\geq t\}
is a set independent of
t
, which implies that
f=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
for some measurable set
E
of finite measure and
c>0
is a constant.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem A.

Proof of Theorem A.

Note that

\min\{f(x),f(y)\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dt.

By Fubini’s theorem and Riesz rearrangement inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdydt

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdydt

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt,

where the last equation is from the definition of chord power integrals and
|E^{\star}|=|E|
for any
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite measure. The optimal constant
\sigma_{n,\alpha}
is given by

\sigma_{n,\alpha}=\frac{nI_{\alpha+1}(B^{n})}{\alpha(\alpha+1)\omega_{n}^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}=\frac{n2^{\alpha+1}\omega_{n+\alpha}}{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)\omega_{\alpha+1}\omega_{n}^{\alpha/n}}.

Since
\omega_{n}=\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2+1)
, we obtain the explicit formula for
\sigma_{n,\alpha}
stated in (
1.4
).

Then Lemma
3.1
implies that

\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy.

(3.4)

When the equality holds, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that
f=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
for some measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, equality must also occur in the rearrangement inequality (
3.3
) for almost all
t>0
. Since the kernel
|x|^{\alpha-n}
is strictly decreasing in
|x|
, the equality case of the Riesz rearrangement inequality (see
[
36
, Lemma 3]
) implies that

\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}=\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}

for almost all
t>0
. Consequently,
f=c\operatorname{1}_{B}
for some ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

In the setting of
\alpha>n
, Theorem A does not admit a straightforward reversal, which will be implied by the following result and Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q>0
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an integrable function such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(y)\}|x-y|^{q}dxdy<\infty.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(y)\}|x-y|^{q}dxdy\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f^{\star}(x),f^{\star}(y)\}|x-y|^{q}dxdy.

Equality holds if and only if
f
is a translate of
f^{\star}
.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
,

\displaystyle|z|_{K}^{q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus r^{\frac{1}{q}}B^{n}}(z)dr.

Using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),

\displaystyle f(y)\}|x-y|^{q}dxdy

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)|x-y|^{q}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdydt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus r^{\frac{1}{q}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdydrdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)(1-\operatorname{1}_{r^{\frac{1}{q}}B^{n}}(x-y))\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdydrdt.

The Riesz rearrangement inequality, Theorem
2.2
, implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{\frac{1}{q}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}

\displaystyle{}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdy

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{\frac{1}{q}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}^{\star}}(y)dxdy.

Note that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)dx<\infty
for almost all
t>0
, as
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Combing (
3.6
) with the calculation in (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(y)\}|x-y|^{q}dxdy\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f^{\star}(x),f^{\star}(y)\}|x-y|^{q}dxdy.

(3.7)

Moreover, if equality holds in (
3.7
), then there exists a null set
N\subset(0,\infty)^{2}
such that equality holds in (
3.6
) for all
(r,t)\in(0,\infty)^{2}\setminus N
. For almost all
r\in(0,\infty)
, we have
(r,t)\in(0,\infty)^{2}\setminus N
for almost all
t>0
. Let
r
be sufficiently small. Theorem
2.2
guarantees the existence of a centered ball
B
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\{f\geq t\}=x_{0}+aB\quad\text{and }\quad r^{\frac{1}{q}}B^{n}=bB,

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a,b>0
are constants. This concludes the proof.
∎

With the previous lemma established, we now consider the case
\alpha>n
, for which we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f^{\star}(x),f^{\star}(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdydt

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt.

However, by Lemma
3.1
and the fact that
\frac{n+\alpha}{n}>1
, we only obtain

\sigma_{n,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}\,dt\leq\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}},

which shows that a direct reverse of (
3.4
) is not available for
\alpha>n
. For this reason, we restrict our attention to functions
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the case
\alpha>n
.

Proof of Theorem B.

As we calculated in (
3.8
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy\geq\sigma_{n,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt.

By Jensen’s inequality, we get

\frac{1}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f\geq t\}|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}dt\geq\Big(\frac{1}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f\geq t\}|dt\Big)^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}=\|f\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{1}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}},

(3.9)

and thus

\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{1}^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy.

(3.10)

If equality holds in (
3.10
), then equality must occur in the application of Jensen’s inequality in (
3.9
). This in turn implies that
|\{f\geq t\}|
is constant for almost every
t\in(0,\|f\|_{\infty})
. Equivalently, this happens if and only if
f=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
, where
E
is a measurable set of finite measure and
c>0
.

Moreover, equality holds in (
3.8
) as well. In this case, Lemma
3.2
indicates that
f
is a translate of
f^{\star}
. This implies that
E
is a ball, which completes the characterization of the equality cases.
∎

4.
Chord power integrals and radial mean bodies

For a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha\geq-1
, the chord power integral
I_{\alpha+1}(K)
is defined by

I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|K\cap l|^{\alpha+1}dl,

where
|K\cap l|
denotes the length of the intersection. Recall that
{\rm Aff}(n,1)
denotes the affine Grassmannian consisting of all
1
-dimensional lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
dl
is the unique Haar measure on it.

Note that a random line
l\in{\rm Aff}(n,1)
is uniquely determined by an antipodal pair
u,-u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and the intersection point
x\in u^{\perp}\cap l
, where
u^{\perp}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\cdot u=0\}
. That is,
l=x+\mathbb{R}u
. The Haar measure
dl
is normalized so that

dl=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)du,

where
du
is the spherical Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
u^{\perp}
, which coincides with the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
u^{\perp}
. The chord power integral can then be rewritten as

I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(x,u)^{\alpha+1}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)du,

(4.1)

where
K|u^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
K
onto
u^{\perp}
and
X_{K}(x,u)=|K\cap(x+\mathbb{R}u)|
is known as the X-ray function of
K
.

There are three fundamental integral formulas connecting chord power integrals to the volume and surface area measure:

I_{0}(K)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}S(K),~~I_{1}(K)=|K|,~~I_{n+1}(K)=\frac{n+1}{\omega_{n}}|K|^{2},

(4.2)

which are known, respectively, as Cauchy’s integral formula, Crofton’s volume formula, and Poincaré-Hadwiger’s integral formula.

Recall that the chord isoperimetric inequality states that

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(K)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}|K|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}},\quad\alpha\in(-1,0)\cup(n,\infty),

(4.3)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha+1}(K)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}|K|^{\frac{n+\alpha}{n}},\quad\alpha\in(0,n),

(4.4)

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}
is given by

\tilde{\sigma}_{n,\alpha}=\frac{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\sigma_{n,\alpha}=I_{\alpha+1}(B^{n})\omega_{n}^{-\frac{n+\alpha}{n}}.

(4.5)

By the Cauchy’s integral formula in (
4.2
), the chord isoperimetric inequality converges to the classical isoperimetric inequality as
\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}
.

We next relate chord power integrals to radial mean bodies, which were defined by Gardner and Zhang
[
28
]
. For a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha>-1
, the
\alpha
-th radial mean body
{\rm R}_{\alpha}K
is defined by

\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}K}(u)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}\rho_{K-x}(u)^{\alpha}dx,~\alpha\neq 0,

(4.6)

and

\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}\log\rho_{K-x}(u)dx.

(4.7)

The following basic result shows the relation between
I_{\alpha+1}(K)
and
{\rm R}_{\alpha}K
. For completeness, we give the proof here.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
. For a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\frac{(\alpha+1)|K|}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}K}(u)^{\alpha}du.

(4.8)

Proof.

If
\alpha=0
, (
4.8
) follows from the Crofton’s volume formula in (
4.2
) directly. We assume that
\alpha>-1
and
\alpha\neq 0
. By the definition (
4.6
),

\displaystyle\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}K}(u)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}\rho_{K-x}(u)^{\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}(s_{2}-s)^{\alpha}dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y),

where
s_{1}=\min\{s:y+su\in K\}
and
s_{2}=\max\{s:y+su\in K\}
. Since
X_{K}(y,u)=s_{2}-s_{1}
, we have

\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}K}(u)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{(\alpha+1)|K|}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(y,u)^{\alpha+1}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

(4.9)

Therefore (
4.8
) follows from (
4.1
).
∎

We conclude this section by the following basic computations concerning radial mean bodies, which are also presented in
[
28
,
33
]
. Here
K\triangle L
denotes the symmetric difference of
K
and
L
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Then

\displaystyle\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}K}(u)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\alpha|}{2|K|}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}|K\triangle(K+ru)|dr,

\displaystyle\alpha\in(-1,0),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}K}(u)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{|K|}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}|K\cap(K+ru)|dr,

\displaystyle\alpha>0.

(4.11)

Proof.

Formula (
4.11
) is stated in
[
28
, Lemma 3.1]
. Here, it suffices to consider the case
\alpha\in(-1,0)
. Since

\displaystyle\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(x,u)^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=|\alpha|(\alpha+1)\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\Big(\int_{0}^{X_{K}(x,u)}r^{\alpha}dr+\int_{X_{K}(x,u)}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}X_{K}(x,u)dr\Big)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=|\alpha|(\alpha+1)\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\min\{X_{K}(x,u),r\}drd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=|\alpha|(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\min\{X_{K}(x,u),r\}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}|K\triangle(K+ru)|dr,

the equality (
4.10
) follows from (
4.9
).
∎

5.
Chord power integrals for functions

Visintin
[
53
]
pointed out that, as a consequence of Fubini’s theorem, a
generalized coarea formula can be established. More precisely, for
\alpha\in(-1,0)
and non-negative
f\in W^{-\alpha,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=\frac{2n\omega_{n}}{|\alpha|(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt.

(5.1)

For
\alpha>0
we have the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
\alpha>0
and log-concave
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt.

Proof.

Recall that for a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha>0
,

I_{\alpha+1}(K)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{K}(x)\operatorname{1}_{K}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dtdxdy.

Since

\min\{f(x),f(y)\}=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dt,

we conclude the proof by substituting this identity.
∎

It remains to consider the endpoint cases
\alpha=-1
and
\alpha=0
.
For non-negative
f\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):|\nabla f|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big\},

it follows from
[
9
, Theorem 3]
(see also
[
32
, Theorem 3]
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}}(1+\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=2\omega_{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x)|dx

\displaystyle=2\omega_{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\{f\geq t\})dt,

where the last equality follows from the coarea formula
[
20
, Theorem 5.9]
. Hence

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow-1^{+}}(1+\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=2n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{0}(\{f\geq t\})dt.

(5.2)

The following two lemmas show that the case
\alpha=0
is also well-defined.

Lemma 5.2
.

For non-negative
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}=\|f\|_{1}.

Proof.

Let

A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\geq 1\},\quad B=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)<1\}.

Assume that
(\alpha_{k})
is a positive sequence such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{k}=0
. For
x\in A
, we have

f(x)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha_{k}}}\leq f(x).

Then the dominated convergence theorem implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{A}f(x)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha_{k}}}dx=\int_{A}f(x)dx.

For
x\in B
, we have
f(x)\leq f(x)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha_{k}}}
. By taking a subsequence, we may assume that
(\alpha_{k})
is a decreasing sequence. Hence
f(x)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha_{k}}}
is a monotone decreasing sequence and bounded from below. By the monotone convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B}f(x)^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha_{k}}}dx=\int_{B}f(x)dx,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-negative. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=n\omega_{n}\|f\|_{1}.

(5.3)

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
,
f\in L^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\alpha
sufficiently small. Hence Theorem A implies that

\limsup_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy\leq\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\alpha\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}=n\omega_{n}\|f\|_{1}.

On the other hand, by the layer cake formula for
\min\{f(x),f(y)\}
and Fubini’s theorem,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdydt.

By
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
,

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=n\omega_{n}|\{f\geq t\}|.

Therefore, Fatou’s Lemma implies that

n\omega_{n}\|f\|_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}n\omega_{n}|\{f\geq t\}|dt\leq\liminf_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem
5.1
, together with (
5.1
), (
5.2
) and Lemma
5.3
, motivates an extension of chord power integrals to functions. The following result shows that this extension is well-defined for log-concave, integrable functions for all
\alpha\geq-1
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a log-concave function. Then

\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt<\infty.

Proof.

Since
f
is log-concave and integrable, there is a convex function
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
such that
f(x)=e^{-v(x)}
and
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}v(x)=\infty
. By
[
17
, Lemma 2.5]
, there exist constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a>0
such that

v(x)\geq a|x|+b,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\{f\geq t\}\subset B_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:a|x|+b\leq-\log t\}.

(5.4)

We first consider
\alpha>-1
. Since
I_{\alpha+1}(\lambda K)=\lambda^{n+\alpha}I_{\alpha+1}(K)
, the inclusion (
5.4
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}I_{\alpha+1}(B_{t})dt

\displaystyle=\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(B^{n})}{a^{n+\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}(-\log t-b)^{n+\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(B^{n})}{a^{n+\alpha}e^{b}}\int_{-\log\|f\|_{\infty}-b}^{\infty}s^{n+\alpha}e^{-s}ds<\infty.

For
\alpha=-1
, by the Cauchy’s integral formula in (
4.2
),

\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}I_{0}(\{f\geq t\})dt=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\{f\geq t\})dt.

Since
\{f\geq t\}
is convex, and
\{f\geq t\}\subset B_{t}
as shown in (
5.4
), we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\{f\geq t\})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{t})=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{a^{n-1}}(-\log t-b)^{n-1}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}I_{0}(\{f\geq t\})dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{a^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}(-\log t-b)^{n-1}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{a^{n-1}e^{b}}\int_{-\log\|f\|_{\infty}-b}^{\infty}s^{n-1}e^{-s}ds<\infty,

which concludes the proof.
∎

With this finiteness result in hand, we now introduce the definition of the chord power integral for log-concave, integrable functions.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a log-concave function. The chord power integral for

I_{\alpha+1}(f)=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}I_{\alpha+1}(\{f\geq t\})dt.

Remark 5.6
.

The definition can be extended to a broader class when

By Definition

Radial mean bodies for functions

Definition 6.1

Proposition

Proposition 6.2
.

Let

\rho_{{\rm R}_{\alpha}f}(u)^{\alpha}=\frac{|\alpha|}{2\|f\|_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f(x+ru)|dxdr

Note that for

Lemma 6.3
.

[

Let
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

\displaystyle 0<\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\omega(r)dr<\infty

0<\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}(\omega(0)-\omega(r))dr<\infty

\alpha\in(-1,0)

and
r\mapsto{\varphi(r)}/{r}
are increasing on

\zeta(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\omega(\varphi(r))dr}{\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}\omega(r)dr}\right)^{1/\alpha}&&\text{for}~~\alpha>0,\\
&\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(\varphi(r))-\omega(r)}{r\omega(0)}dr\right)&&\text{for}~~\alpha=0,\\

is a continuous, decreasing function of

on
(-1,\infty)
. Moreover,
\zeta
is constant on

if

In particular, taking

\Gamma(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}e^{-r}dr&&\text{for}~~\alpha>0,\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha-1}(e^{-r}-1)dr&&\text{for}~~-1<\alpha<0.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

To apply Lemma

For

\displaystyle g(0)-g(ru)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{1}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(x+ru)\}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{rn}f(x+ru)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(x+ru)\}dx

Let

7.
Applications to entropies of random lines

In the geometric chord isoperimetric inequality (

) and (

), the inequality collapses to an identity when
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=n
.
Motivated by this degeneracy, Xu
[

]
and Zhang
[

]
studied the corresponding entropy inequalities in order to capture meaningful information in these endpoint regimes.

By Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
, the chord Sobolev inequality (

) also degenerates to an identity as
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}
, provided

is non-negative. Ma

[

gave the limiting behavior for

of the fractional Sobolev inequalities, which states that for

,

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{-}}-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dxdy=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{-}}2\sigma_{n,\alpha}\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}=n\omega_{n}\|f\|_{1}.

(7.1)

Hence the fractional Sobolev inequalities (
1.2
) collapse to an identity as
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{-}
. Consequently, it is natural to consider this limiting case, which provides a functional extension of the chord entropy inequalities established by Xu
[
58
]
and Zhang
[
61
]
.

We begin with recalling the definition of the chord entropy of a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here we adopt the chord entropy from
[
61
]
, with a slightly different normalization. The special case
{\rm E}_{1}(K)
was first considered by Xu
[
58
]
.

The
\boldsymbol{(\alpha+1)}
-th chord entropy:
For a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha>-1
, the
(\alpha+1)
-th chord entropy of
K
is defined by

{\rm E}_{\alpha+1}(K)=-\frac{\alpha+1}{I_{\alpha+1}(K)}\int_{K\cap l\neq\emptyset}|K\cap l|^{\alpha+1}\log|K\cap l|dl.

Typically,

{\rm E}_{1}(K)=-\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K\cap l\neq\emptyset}|K\cap l|\log|K\cap l|dl,

and

{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)=-\frac{\omega_{n}}{|K|^{2}}\int_{K\cap l\neq\emptyset}|K\cap l|^{n+1}\log|K\cap l|dl,

where the coefficients are from the Crofton’s volume formula and the Poincaré-Hadwiger’s integral formula in (
4.2
), respectively.

The chord entropy inequalities for
{\rm E}_{1}(K)
and
{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)
state that

{\rm E}_{1}(K)\geq{\rm E}_{1}(B_{K})={\rm E}_{1}(B^{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|K|}\Big),

(7.2)

and

{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)\leq{\rm E}_{n+1}(B_{K})={\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|K|}\Big),

(7.3)

where
B_{K}
denotes the ball with the same volume as
K
. Both inequalities are sharp and equality holds if and only if
K=B_{K}
(see
[
58
,
61
]
).

We first extend the geometric chord entropy
{\rm E}_{1}(K)
to non-zero, integrable, log-concave functions by defining

{\rm E}_{1}(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx.

The second term coincides with the classical entropy of
f
in information theory, which arises from the limiting behavior of
\|f\|_{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}
, as shown by (
2.1
). Since
s\mapsto-s\log s
is bounded from above,

|K|{\rm E}_{1}(K)=-\int_{K\cap l\neq\emptyset}|K\cap l|\log|K\cap l|dl

admits an upper bound independent of
K
. It follows that
\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)
is also bounded above. By
[
8
, Proposition I.2]
, the entropy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx
is finite provided that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a log-concave function, which shows that
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
is well-defined.

Before giving the chord entropy inequality for
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
, we first recall the following basic result, which is also known as the non-negativity of the relative entropy in information theory. For completeness, we provide the proof here.

Lemma 7.1
.

For two probability density functions
h,l:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\log\Big(\frac{h(x)}{l(x)}\Big)dx\geq 0.

Equality holds if and only if
h=l
.

Proof.

Since
h(x)dx
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Jensen’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\log\Big(\frac{h(x)}{l(x)}\Big)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\log\Big(\frac{l(x)}{h(x)}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\geq-\log\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{l(x)}{h(x)}h(x)dx\Big)=-\log\|l\|_{1}=0.

Equality holds if and only if
l(x)=ch(x)
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
c
is a positive constant. Since
\|h\|_{1}=\|l\|_{1}=1
, we have
c=1
. Hence equality holds if and only if
h=l
.
∎

We also establish the following result, which will be used in the proof of the extended chord entropy inequality for
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-zero, log-concave, and
f_{\lambda}(x)=f(\lambda x)
for
\lambda>0
. Then

{\rm E}_{1}(f_{\lambda})={\rm E}_{1}(f)+\log\lambda.

Proof.

We first have

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\lambda x)dx=\lambda^{-n}\|f\|_{1}

(7.4)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\lambda}(x)\log f_{\lambda}(x)dx=\lambda^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx.

(7.5)

Note that

\{f_{\lambda}\geq t\}=\lambda^{-1}\{f\geq t\},

and thus
d\mu_{f_{\lambda}}(t)/dt=|\{f\geq t\}|/\|f\|_{1}
. Since

\int_{0}^{\|f_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f_{\lambda}\geq t\})d\mu_{f_{\lambda}}(t)=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\rm E}_{1}(\lambda^{-1}\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t),

(7.6)

it suffices to consider
{\rm E}_{1}(\lambda^{-1}K)
for a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By the definition of
E_{1}(K)
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(\lambda^{-1}K)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|\lambda^{-1}K|}\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|\lambda^{-1}K\cap l|\log|\lambda^{-1}K\cap l|dl

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\lambda^{-1}K|u^{\perp}}X_{\lambda^{-1}K}(z,u)\log X_{\lambda^{-1}K}(z,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(z)du.

Since
X_{\lambda^{-1}K}(\lambda^{-1}y,u)=\lambda^{-1}X_{K}(y,u)
for
y\in K|u^{\perp}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(\lambda^{-1}K)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\lambda^{-n}X_{K}(y,u)\log X_{K}(y,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du

(7.7)

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda^{n}\log\lambda^{-1}}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\lambda^{-n}X_{K}(y,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(y,u)\log X_{K}(y,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du+\log\lambda

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{1}(K)+\log\lambda,

where the second term is by the Crofton’s volume formula in (
4.2
).

Combining (
7.4
), (
7.5
), (
7.6
) and (
7.7
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(f_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\|f_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}}{\rm E}_{1}(\lambda^{-1}\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)-\frac{1}{n\|f_{\lambda}\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\lambda}(x)\log f_{\lambda}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}({\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})+\log\lambda)d\mu_{f}(t)-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{1}(f)+\log\lambda,

which concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the following chord entropy inequality for
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
, where
B_{f}
denotes the ball centered at the origin with
|B_{f}|=\|f\|_{1}
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-zero, log-concave function. Then

{\rm E}_{1}(f)\geq{\rm E}_{1}(B_{f})={\rm E}_{1}(B^{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{\|f\|_{1}}\Big),

(7.8)

where equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

We first prove the second equality. For a log-concave
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let
B_{f}=rB^{n}
with
r=(\|f\|_{1}/\omega_{n})^{1/n}
. By Proposition
7.2
, we then have

{\rm E}_{1}(B_{f})={\rm E}_{1}(B^{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{\|f\|_{1}}\Big).

For the first inequality, recall that
|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|=|\{f\geq t\}|
and
\|f^{\star}\|_{1}=\|f\|_{1}
. Hence
\mu_{f}=\mu_{f^{\star}}
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)\log f^{\star}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx.

By the chord entropy inequality for convex bodies, we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\}^{\star})d\mu_{f^{\star}}(t)-\frac{1}{n\|f^{\star}\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)\log f^{\star}(x)dx={\rm E}_{1}(f^{\star}),

where equality holds if and only if
\{f\geq t\}
is a ball for almost all
t>0
.

Using the definition, we find

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(f^{\star})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big({\rm E}_{1}(B^{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|\{f\geq t\}|}\Big)\Big)d\mu_{f^{\star}}(t)-\frac{1}{n\|f^{\star}\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)\log f^{\star}(x)dx

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{1}(\operatorname{1}_{B^{n}})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{\|f\|_{1}}\Big)+q(f^{\star}),

where the functional
q(f)
is given by

q(f)=\frac{1}{n}\log\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|\log|\{f\geq t\}|dt-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx.

Note that
q(f)=q(f^{\star})
. Therefore it remains to prove
q(f)\geq 0
and the equality characterization.

Let
p(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be defined by

p(x,t)=\frac{\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)}{\|f\|_{1}},

which is a probability density function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Its marginals are the following two probability density functions:

\bar{f}(x)=\frac{f(x)}{\|f\|_{1}}~~\text{and}~~s(t)=\frac{|\{f\geq t\}|}{\|f\|_{1}}.

We now apply Lemma
7.1
with
h=p
and
l=\bar{f}s
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x,t)\log\Big(\frac{p(x,t)}{\bar{f}(x)s(t)}\Big)dtdx\geq 0,

(7.9)

where

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p(x,t)\log\Big(\frac{p(x,t)}{l(x,t)}\Big)dtdx

\displaystyle=-\log\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log\Big(\frac{f(x)}{\|f\|_{1}}\Big)dx-\frac{1}{\|f\|_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\{f\geq t\}|\log\Big(\frac{|\{f\geq t\}|}{\|f\|_{1}}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=nq(f).

Therefore (
7.9
) implies that
q(f)\geq 0
and thus

{\rm E}_{1}(f)\geq{\rm E}_{1}({B^{n}})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{\|f\|_{1}}\Big).

As discussed above, equality holds if and only if
\{f\geq t\}
is a ball for almost all
t>0
and
q(f)=0
. By Lemma
7.1
, equality is attained in (
7.9
) if and only if
p(x,t)=\bar{f}(x)s(t)
for almost
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
. Hence
f=c\operatorname{1}_{B}
, where
c>0
and
B
is a centered ball such that
c|B|=\|f\|_{1}
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

In the proof of Theorem
7.3
, we only use the fact that the superlevel sets
\{f\geq t\}
are convex bodies. Consequently, the chord entropy inequality (
7.8
) remains valid for quasi-concave functions
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for which the sets
\{f\geq t\}
are convex for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This extension will be discussed in detail in Section
8
.

With the limiting case
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}
fully established, we turn our attention to the case
\alpha\rightarrow n
, which exhibits fundamentally different behavior. As discussed above, when
\alpha
goes to
0^{+}
, both the geometric inequality (
1.7
) and the chord Sobolev inequality (
1.3
) collapse to identities. However, when
\alpha=n
, Theorem A and Theorem B imply that

\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(y)\}dxdy\leq\|f\|_{\frac{1}{2}}

(7.10)

for log-concave functions
f\in L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, when
\alpha=n
, the chord Sobolev inequality does not reduce to an identity.

Even so, both inequalities in (
7.10
) are in fact trivial. The first inequality follows from

\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|f\|_{\infty}}\leq\min\{f(x),f(y)\},

while the second inequality follows from the elementary estimate

\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\leq f(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}f(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, it is still reasonable to consider the entropy inequality for
\alpha=n
.

In this case, the definition of
{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)
is primarily motivated by the geometric quantities
{\rm E}_{1}(K)
and
{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)
, together with the functional extension
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
of
{\rm E}_{1}(K)
. Recall that for a convex body
K\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)=-\frac{n+1}{I_{n+1}(K)}\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|K\cap l|^{n+1}\log|K\cap l|dl,

where
I_{n+1}(K)=\frac{n+1}{\omega_{n}}|K|^{2}
by the Poincaré-Hadwiger’s integral formula (
4.2
).

For a log-concave
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define

{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{n+1}(\{f\geq t\})d\nu_{f}(t),

where

d\nu_{f}(t)=\frac{(n+1)|\{f\geq t\}|^{2}}{\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}dt=\frac{I_{n+1}(\{f\geq t\})}{I_{n+1}(f)}dt

is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By a similar argument as for
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
, since
s\mapsto-s^{n+1}\log s
is bounded from above,
{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)
admits an upper bound as well. This ensures that
{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)
is well-defined.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-zero, log-concave, and
f_{\lambda}(x)=f(\lambda x)
for
\lambda>0
. Then

{\rm E}_{n+1}(f_{\lambda})={\rm E}_{n+1}(f)+(n+1)\log\lambda.

Proof.

Recall that
\{f_{\lambda}\geq t\}=\lambda^{-1}\{f\geq t\}
and hence

I_{n+1}(\{f_{\lambda}\geq t\})=\frac{n+1}{\omega_{n}}|\{f_{\lambda}\geq t\}|^{2}=\lambda^{-2n}I_{n+1}(\{f\geq t\}).

Moreover, note that

I_{n+1}(f_{\lambda})=\frac{n+1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f_{\lambda}(x),f_{\lambda}(y)\}dxdy=\frac{n+1}{\omega_{n}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f_{\lambda}\geq t\}|^{2}dt=\lambda^{-2n}I_{n+1}(f),

which implies that
\nu_{f_{\lambda}}=\nu_{f}
.

Therefore it suffices to consider
{\rm E}_{n+1}(\lambda^{-1}K)
for a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the definition of
{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)
and the identity
X_{\lambda^{-1}K}(\lambda^{-1}y,u)=\lambda^{-1}X_{K}(y,u)
,

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{n+1}(\lambda^{-1}K)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+1}{I_{n+1}(\lambda^{-1}K)}\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|\lambda^{-1}K\cap l|^{n+1}\log|\lambda^{-1}K\cap l|dl

(7.11)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+1}{\lambda^{-2n}I_{n+1}(K)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\lambda^{-1}K|u^{\perp}}X_{\lambda^{-1}K}(z,u)^{n+1}\log X_{\lambda^{-1}K}(z,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(z)du

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+1}{I_{n+1}(K)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(y,u)^{n+1}\log X_{K}(y,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du

\displaystyle+\frac{n+1}{I_{n+1}(K)}\log\lambda\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(y,u)^{n+1}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{n+1}(K)+(n+1)\log\lambda.

Therefore

{\rm E}_{n+1}(f_{\lambda})=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\rm E}_{n+1}(\lambda^{-1}\{f\geq t\})d\nu_{f}(f)={\rm E}_{n+1}(f)+(n+1)\log\lambda,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-zero, log-concave function. Then

{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)\leq{\rm E}_{n+1}(\tilde{B}_{f})={\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}{I_{n+1}(f)}\Big),

where
\tilde{B_{f}}
is a ball with
|\tilde{B}_{f}|=\frac{\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}
. Equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

By (
7.11
) in the previous lemma, we have

{\rm E}_{n+1}(\tilde{B}_{f})={\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|\tilde{B}_{f}|}\Big).

Since
|\tilde{B}_{f}|=\frac{\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}
, we obtain the second equality.
Recall that
{\rm E}_{n+1}(K)\leq{\rm E}_{n+1}(B_{K})
, where
B_{K}
is the centered ball with the same volume as
K
. Since
\nu_{f}=\nu_{f^{\star}}
, it follows from the geometric chord entropy inequality (
7.3
) that

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\rm E}_{n+1}(\{f\geq t\})d\nu_{f}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\rm E}_{n+1}(\{f^{\star}\geq t\})d\nu_{f^{\star}}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}\Big({\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|}\Big)\Big)d\nu_{f^{\star}}(t)

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\omega_{n}-\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{n\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f\geq t\}|^{2}\log|\{f\geq t\}|dt,

where the last equality is from the fact that
|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|=|\{f\geq t\}|
and
I_{n+1}(f)=I_{n+1}(f^{\star})
. By (
7.3
), equality holds if and only if
\{f\geq t\}
is a centered ball for almost all
t
.

Note that
h(t)=t\log t
is a convex function on
(0,\infty)
. Then Jensen’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|f\|_{1}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f\geq t\}|^{2}\log|\{f\geq t\}|dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}h(|\{f\geq t\}|)d\mu_{f}(t)

\displaystyle\geq h\Big(\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\infty}}|\{f\geq t\}|d\mu_{f}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}\Big),

where equality holds if and only if
|\{f\geq t\}|
is a constant. This implies that
f=c\operatorname{1}_{E}
where
c>0
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a measurable set.

Hence we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{n+1}(f)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\omega_{n}-\frac{n+1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}I_{n+1}(f)}{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}\Big)

(7.12)

\displaystyle={\rm E}_{n+1}(B^{n})+\frac{n+1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{(n+1)\|f\|_{1}}{I_{n+1}(f)}\Big),

and equality holds if and only if
\{f\geq t\}
is a ball for almost all
t\in[0,\|f\|_{\infty}]
and
|\{f\geq t\}|
is a constant. Therefore equality holds in (
7.12
) if and only if
f=c\operatorname{1}_{B}
with
c>0
and
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a centered ball such that
c|B|=\|f\|_{1}
.
∎

8.
A logarithmic Sobolev-type inequality

As discussed in Section
7
, if
f\in W^{-\alpha,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\alpha\in(-1,0)
, the fractional Sobolev inequality (
1.2
) reduces to an identity in the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{-}
. Moreover, when
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the chord Sobolev inequality (
1.3
) also degenerates, provided that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is non-negative. The chord entropy inequality for
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
can thus be interpreted as a type of logarithmic Sobolev inequality. This motivates us to extend Theorem
7.3
to a broader class.

We begin by rewriting the chord entropy inequality for
{\rm E}_{1}(f)
as

\bar{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\log\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx\geq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du,

(8.1)

where
{\rm R}_{0}f
is the radial mean body defined by (
6.4
) and the optimal constant
\bar{\sigma}_{0}
is given by

\bar{\sigma}_{0}=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\frac{d}{d\alpha}\Big|_{\alpha=0}\alpha\sigma_{n,\alpha}=-{\rm E}_{1}(B^{n})-\frac{1}{n}\log\omega_{n}-1.

To establish the equivalence between the chord entropy inequality (
7.8
) and (
8.1
), we first recall the following lemma, which was studied by Xu
[
58
, Theorem 3.2]
. Since our normalization differs slightly from that in
[
58
]
, we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 8.1
.

[
58
, Theorem 3.2]

For a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\rm E}_{1}(K)=-1-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}K}(u)du.

Proof.

By the definition of
{\rm E}_{1}(K)
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(K)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|K\cap l|\log|K\cap l|dl

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{K}(y,u)\log X_{K}(y,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du.

Note that

\displaystyle X_{K}(y,u)\log X_{K}(y,u)=\int_{0}^{X_{K}(y,u)}(\log s+1)ds=\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}(\log(s-s_{0})+1)ds,

where
s_{0}=\min\{s:y+su\in K\}
and
s_{1}=\max\{s:y+su\in K\}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(K)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}(\log(s-s_{0})+1)dsd\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K}\log\rho_{K-x}(u)dxdu-1

\displaystyle=-1-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}K}(u)du,

where the last equality is by the definition of
{\rm R}_{0}K
.
∎

The following lemma extends Lemma
8.1
from convex bodies to log-concave functions, which also shows the equivalence between (
7.8
) and (
8.1
).

Lemma 8.2
.

For non-zero, log-concave
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)=-1-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du.

Proof.

For log-concave
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, Lemma
8.1
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(-1-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}\{f\geq t\}}(u)du\Big)d\mu_{f}(t)

\displaystyle=-1-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}\{f\geq t\}}(u)d\mu_{f}(t)du.

By Definition
6.1
, we then have

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm E}_{1}(\{f\geq t\})d\mu_{f}(t)=-1-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du,

which concludes the proof.
∎

As established in Lemma
6.4
,
{\rm R}_{0}f
is well-defined for non-negative, non-zero
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\beta\in(0,1)
fixed. To extend (
8.1
) to a broader class of functions, we first extend Theorem
7.3
to quasi-concave functions.

For a convex, bounded set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its closure
\bar{K}
is a convex body. Hence

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{1}(\bar{K})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{{\rm Aff}(n,1)}|\bar{K}\cap l|\log|\bar{K}\cap l|dl

(8.2)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}|K|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{K|u^{\perp}}X_{\bar{K}}(y,u)\log X_{\bar{K}}(y,u)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)du.

Since for almost all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the intersection
\partial K\cap(y+\mathbb{R}u)
only contains two points, we have

|K\cap(y+\mathbb{R}u)|=|\bar{K}\cap(y+\mathbb{R}u)|,

for almost all
y\in\bar{K}|u^{\perp}
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Therefore, if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex, bounded set with positive volume, we can extend the definition (
8.2
) by letting
{\rm E}_{1}(K):={\rm E}_{1}(\bar{K})
and then get

{\rm E}_{1}(K)\geq{\rm E}_{1}(B_{K})={\rm E}_{1}(B^{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log\Big(\frac{\omega_{n}}{|K|}\Big).

(8.3)

By (
8.3
), Lemma
8.2
and Remark
7.4
, we directly have the following corollary, which extends Theorem
7.3
to quasi-concave functions.

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be fixed, and
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-negative, non-zero quasi-concave function such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx
is finite. Then

\bar{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\log\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx\geq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du.

Equality holds if and only if
f
is a constant multiple of the characteristic function of a ball.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem C. Recalling that

\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f)=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du,

it suffices to prove the following rearrangement inequality:

\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f^{\star})\geq\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f),

(8.4)

for non-negative, non-zero
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Note that
f^{\star}
is quasi-concave and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\beta}}dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\beta}}dxdy<\infty,

which is due to
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the Polyá-Szegő inequality established by Almgren and Lieb
[
1
]
. Then Corollary
8.3
holds for
f^{\star}
. Since

\|f^{\star}\|_{1}=\|f\|_{1}~\text{and}~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\star}(x)\log f^{\star}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx,

combining this with Corollary
8.3
and (
8.4
), we have

\bar{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\log\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx\geq\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f^{\star})\geq\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f)

(8.5)

holds for all non-negative, non-zero
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx
is finite.

For the proof of (
8.4
), we first establish some preliminary technical results. We also refer the reader to
[
12
,
32
]
for general cases.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
, and
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-negative, non-zero function. If

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy<\infty,

then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle dxdy

(8.6)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy,

with equality if and only if
f
is a translate of
f^{\star}
.

Proof.

Since

|z|^{-n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(z)dr
, the kernel function is

\frac{\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}=\max\{0,|x-y|^{-n}-1\}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dr,

and we obtain

\displaystyle J:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f(y)|\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdydr.

Since

|f(x)-f(y)|=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)-\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)|dt,

we have

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)-\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)|\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdydtdr

\displaystyle=2\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)-\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)\Big)\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdydtdr

\displaystyle=2\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(r^{-1}\omega_{n}|\{f\geq t\}|-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdy\Big)dtdr.

By the Riesz rearrangement inequality (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}

\displaystyle(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdy

(8.7)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}\operatorname{1}_{t^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(y)dxdy.

Since
|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|=|\{f\geq t\}|
, it follows that

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(r^{-1}\omega_{n}|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|

(8.8)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(y)dxdy\Big)dtdr

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy.

If equality holds in (
8.8
), then equality holds in (
8.7
) for almost all
(r,t)\in(1,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
. Then for such
(r,t)
with
r>1
sufficiently large, the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied, and hence

\{f\geq t\}=x_{0}+aB,~~~~r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}=bB

where
B
is a centered ball,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
a,b>0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

By a similar application of the Riesz rearrangement inequality, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
, and
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-negative, non-zero function. If

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{[1,\infty]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy<\infty,

then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{[1,\infty]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy

(8.9)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f^{\star}(x),f^{\star}(y)\}|\operatorname{1}_{[1,\infty]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy.

Proof.

We first rewrite the kernel function as

\frac{\operatorname{1}_{[1,\infty]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}=\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(z)dr,

which yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{[1,\infty]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdydr.

By the layer cake representation of
\min\{f(x),f(y)\}
and the Riesz rearrangement inequality (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x)

\displaystyle,f(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdydr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(y)dxdydtdr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(x)\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)\operatorname{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq t\}}(y)dxdydtdr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f^{\star}(x),f^{\star}(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{r^{-\frac{1}{n}}B^{n}}(x-y)dxdydr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f^{\star}(x),f^{\star}(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{[0,1]}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy,

which concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove (
8.4
), which is stated below as a theorem.

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be fixed, and
f\in W^{\beta,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a non-negative, non-zero function. Then

\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f^{\star})\geq\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f),

where equality holds if and only if
f
is a translate of
f^{\star}
.

Proof.

We may assume
\|f\|_{1}=1
without loss of generality, since
{\rm R}_{0}(cf)={\rm R}_{0}f
for all
c>0
. We keep using
g(ru)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{f(x),f(x+ru)\}dx
, and recall that

\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(ru)-1}{r}dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{g(ru)}{r}dr+\tilde{C},

where
\tilde{C}
is a constant. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(ru)-1}{r}dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{g(ru)}{r}dr+\tilde{C}\Big)du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(ru)-1}{r}dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{g(ru)}{r}dr\Big)du+\tilde{C}.

By polar coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|\operatorname{1}_{(0,1)}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\operatorname{1}_{[1,\infty)}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy,

where these two integrals are finite due to Lemma
6.4
. It then follows from Lemma
8.4
and Lemma
8.5
that

\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f)\leq\tilde{V}_{\log}(B^{n},{\rm R}_{0}f^{\star}).

If equality holds in the above inequality, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|\operatorname{1}_{(0,1)}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|\operatorname{1}_{(0,1)}(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy.

Lemma
8.4
thereby implies that
f
is a translate of
f^{\star}
.
∎

As a consequence of the preceding analysis, Theorem C follows directly from Theorem
8.6
and the chord entropy inequality for
{\rm E}_{1}(f^{\star})
, which extends the result in Theorem
7.3
.

Proof of Theorem C.

By Theorem
8.6
and (
8.5
), we have

\bar{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\log\|f\|_{1}-\frac{1}{n\|f\|_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\log f(x)dx\geq\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du.

Since
\|f\|_{1}=1
, the spherical polar coordinate and (
6.4
) imply that

\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log\rho_{{\rm R}_{0}f}(u)du=-\gamma+\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\min\{f(x),f(y)\}-e^{-|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{n}}dxdy,

which concludes the proof.
∎

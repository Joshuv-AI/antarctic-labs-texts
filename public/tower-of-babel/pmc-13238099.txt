Adaptation and validation of the short version of the B-IPQ for the assessment of disease perception in older Colombian adults
Authors: Lorena Cudris-Torres, Andrés Gómez-Acosta, María Paola Jiménez-Villamizar, Mónica Rodríguez Acuña, Stefano Vinaccia, Álvaro Barrios-Núñez, Maira A. López-Castellar, Valmore Bermúdez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIllness perception is a key construct in Health Psychology, as it influences therapeutic adherence, coping, and quality of life in patients with chronic diseases. This study aimed to evaluate the psychometric properties of the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ) in older Colombian adults with chronic diseases, in order to have a validated and culturally appropriate tool for this context.MethodsAn instrumental design was used, with a sample of 325 participants (M = 68.4 years), and the B-IPQ was administered along with scales of emotion regulation (ERQ), self-efficacy (SEMCD-S), fatigue, and quality of life (SF-36). Exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses were performed, along with assessments of factorial invariance by sex and age, Spearman correlations, and estimates of internal consistency using Cronbach’s α and McDonald’s ω.ResultsThe four-item ultrashort version (Ultra B-IPQ) exhibits an acceptable unifactorial structure (CFI = 0.99; TLI = 0.97; RMSEA = 0.10), excellent reliability (α = 0.926; ω = 0.930), and evidence of factorial invariance across sociodemographic groups (gender and age). Significant negative correlations were observed with quality of life and self-efficacy, and positive correlations with fatigue, supporting its convergent and divergent validity.ConclusionThe Ultra B-IPQ is a valid, reliable, and culturally sensitive instrument, suitable for assessing illness perception in particular older Colombian adults from the northern coast. Its brief administration makes it a useful tool for detecting maladaptive beliefs and designing psychological and community interventions focused on coping and therapeutic adherence.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-026-07658-6.

Background

Illness perception is a central construct in Health Psychology, widely addressed from the self-regulation model [1–3]. This model maintains that the cognitive and emotional representations individuals develop about their illness directly influence the coping strategies they use and, consequently, the evolution of their health status and quality of life [4, 5]. Illness perception comprises various dimensions such as attributed consequences, duration, degree of personal and treatment control, perceived symptoms, worry, and emotional response, which interact to guide self-care behaviors, treatment adherence, and health decision-making [6–9]. In the case of older adults with chronic illnesses, these perceptions are crucial, as the way they interpret and understand their condition is associated with indicators of well-being, adaptive coping, and a reduction in negative emotions such as anxiety, depression, and hopelessness [10–12].

The importance of this construct within Health Psychology lies in its influence on both health-promoting behaviors and preventive and therapeutic adherence behaviors [13, 14]. Recent studies show that patients with more negative perceptions tend to experience poorer health-related quality of life, lower treatment adherence, and higher levels of emotional distress [15, 16]. In contrast, more realistic and positive representations are associated with greater self-efficacy, emotional regulation, and functional coping [17–19]. Thus, the perception of illness has become established as a key explanatory variable in the study of chronic health conditions, on par with constructs such as self-efficacy and emotional regulation [20–21].

In this regard, assessing disease perception requires the use of valid and reliable instruments that allow us to identify the role of this construct in different sociocultural contexts [22–24]. Validating scales in specific populations is essential, given that the meanings attributed to disease, beliefs about its origin, and expectations regarding treatment can vary substantially across cultures, ages, and clinical conditions [25, 26]. Having psychometrically sound measures allows for both diagnosis and the planning of evidence-based interventions, especially in countries like Colombia, where chronic diseases represent a growing proportion of the burden of morbidity and mortality [27].

One of the most widely used instruments for assessing this construct is the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ), designed by Broadbent et al. as a brief and practical measure that captures the main domains of illness perception in just eight Likert-scale items, plus an open-ended question about perceived causes [2]. Since its creation, the B-IPQ has shown adequate internal reliability and convergent validity in diverse populations [22–28] and has been applied in conditions such as renal failure [29, 30], multiple sclerosis [31, 32], hepatitis [33], cancer [34, 35], and cardiovascular diseases [15–16]. Its strengths lie in its ease of administration and its usefulness for screening studies in clinical and community settings [2, 26]. However, limitations have also been reported, such as instability in the factor structure across different cultures and adjustment problems in some items [36, 37].

International literature shows that the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ) has been validated in diverse cultural contexts, including European [2, 38, 39], Asian [15, 26, 40, 41], and Latin American [42–44] countries. However, although some studies in Colombia have used the B-IPQ and other instruments based on Leventhal’s self-regulation model to explore illness perception in people with chronic conditions [11, 45], formal psychometric validations supporting its use in the Colombian population with chronic illness, especially among older adults in the Caribbean, are still lacking. This gap is significant, given that the meanings attributed to illness and the experience of symptoms can differ according to sociocultural and contextual factors, in a region that also faces social inequalities and limited access to the healthcare system [46]. Validating the instrument in this context can not only contribute to strengthening local research but also provide a reliable tool for conventional clinical practice and community intervention in the primary care model [47].

Likewise, in older adults with chronic illnesses, the use of ultrashort versions of instruments is methodologically relevant, given the risk of cognitive fatigue, potential attentional limitations, and the need for brief measures in clinical, community, and population-based studies with large-scale applications. Psychometric literature has indicated that, when adequately validated, reduced versions can maintain more stable factor structures and facilitate more efficient measurement in contexts with time constraints or evaluative burden [22, 24]. Accordingly, this study aimed to evaluate an ultrashort version of the B-IPQ, derived from previous psychometric analyses that demonstrated inadequate performance of some items from the original instrument in this population. This necessitated their removal to optimize model parsimony, factor coherence, and measurement validity.

Therefore, the main objective of this study was to determine the psychometric properties of the ultrashort version of the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ) in older Colombian adults with chronic illnesses, by evaluating its factorial, convergent, and divergent validity, reliability, and measurement factorial invariance. It was hypothesized that the ultrashort version of the B-IPQ would exhibit a unifactorial structure with adequate goodness-of-fit indicators, acceptable internal consistency, convergent and divergent validity in relation to other scales of quality of life, fatigue, emotional regulation, and self-efficacy, as well as factorial invariance with respect to sex and age.

Methods

Studio design and environment

An instrumental study was conducted to analyze the psychometric properties of the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ) in older Colombian adults with chronic diseases. This type of research is recommended for validation processes and factor structure analysis of psychological instruments [48]. Data collection took place between January and November 2024 in clinical and community settings in the departments of Atlántico and Magdalena (Colombia), areas characterized by a high prevalence of chronic non-communicable diseases.

Participants and sample

The sample consisted of 325 older adults with a medically confirmed chronic disease (hypertension, diabetes, renal disease, or cardiovascular disease). Participants’ ages ranged from 65 to 78 years (M = 68.4, SD = 4.1); 53.5% were men and 46.5% were women. A non-probabilistic purposive sampling strategy was used, which is common in health-related studies. Inclusion criteria were: (a) age 65 years or older, (b) a medical diagnosis of a chronic disease with a minimum duration of six months, and (c) preserved cognitive functioning, defined as a score greater than 23 on the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE).

It is important to note that the MMSE was neither administered by the research team nor reproduced in the study. Information regarding participants’ cognitive status was obtained from existing clinical records, in which the MMSE had been previously applied as part of routine clinical assessment in a university hospital setting. Consequently, the study relied exclusively on secondary data derived from usual clinical practice, and no additional license was required for the application of the instrument.

Instrument

The Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ), in the Spanish version by Pacheco-Huergo et al. [49], consists of nine items with Likert-type responses ranging from 0 to 10 points. The instrument assesses eight dimensions of illness perception consequences, duration, personal control, treatment control, identity, concern, emotional response, and understanding of the illness, in addition to an open-ended item designed to identify the causes that, from the participant’s perspective, gave rise to their illness, organized according to their relevance. Several studies have reported adequate internal consistency indices for the B-IPQ in different clinical and cultural contexts, with Cronbach’s alpha values ​​ranging from 0.67 to 0.89 [2, 38, 39].

For use in the Colombian context, the instrument underwent a process of forward and back translation, followed by a semantic evaluation by expert judges. Additionally, a pilot study was conducted with older adults to assess the comprehension of the items and their preliminary performance in this population. This initial analysis identified that some items in the original instrument did not demonstrate adequate psychometric performance, leading to the methodological decision to explore a reduced version of the questionnaire, retaining those items that showed a better fit. The process and results of this refinement are presented in detail in the supplementary material section.

Furthermore, in order to evaluate the convergent and divergent validity of the B-IPQ, instruments widely used in health research were applied in parallel, including the Self-Efficacy for Managing Chronic Diseases Scale (SEMCD-S), the Emotional Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ), the Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS) and the Medical Outcomes Study SF-36 quality of life, which have shown adequate levels of reliability and validity in international studies and in populations with chronic diseases [11, 15, 19, 20, 44].

Procedure

Data collection took place in health centers and participants’ homes, with prior institutional authorization. The process began with a detailed explanation of the study’s purpose, followed by the signing of informed consent. A pilot study was conducted with 20 older adults to verify semantic comprehension, administration time, and cultural appropriateness. Subsequently, previously trained psychologists administered the instruments individually in 25–30-minute sessions. Emphasis was placed on confidentiality, the right to withdraw without repercussions, and the exclusive use of information for scientific purposes, in accordance with international (CIOMS) and national (Resolution 8430 of 1993; Law 1090 of 2006) ethical guidelines for health research.

Data analysis

A descriptive analysis of the item characteristics (kurtosis, skewness, standard deviation, and mean) is presented. Subsequently, an exploratory factor analysis is performed to answer hypothesis 1, and a confirmatory factor analysis using structural equation modeling to answer hypothesis 2; against this, it is evaluated that it presents a non-significant p-value (> 0.05), that the Tucker-Lewis Indices (TLI), the Normalized Fit Index (NFI), and the Comparative Fit Index (CFI) exceed 0.90, while the Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA) is less or equal to 0.10 [50]. To answer the third hypothesis, a multigroup factorial invariance analysis was developed testing the sociodemographic variables of sex and age to evaluate the consistency of the model structure (configural), the metric invariance of the items (referring to factor loading), the scalar invariance (of the intercepts) and strict invariance (of the residual error variances) on the equality of mean scores, observing that the p value is not significant (> 0.05), the X²/df ratio < 3.0, the ΔCFI of each report is ≤ 0.010 and the ΔRMSEA is ≤ 0.015 [51].

Given that the records have a non-parametric distribution, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was applied between the B-IPQ scores and those of the other instruments (in response to hypothesis 4) to identify potentially significant positive correlations (convergent validity) and potentially significant negative correlations (divergent validity). Finally, the internal consistency of the test was explored using Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s ω statistics, with acceptable levels above 0.70, to address hypothesis 5. The analyses were performed using JASP 0.95® and SPSS-AMOS 26® software.

Ethical considerations

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki [52], the Ethical Standards of the American Psychological Association [53], and the Code of Ethics and Bioethics of the Colombian Psychologist [54]. It also complied with Resolution 8430 of 1993 from the Colombian Ministry of Health and Social Protection for research involving human subjects [55]. The research was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Clínica General del Norte in Barranquilla, Colombia, at its meeting of November 10, 2023, minutes number 165. Data confidentiality was ensured through anonymous coding and comprehensive protection of the participants, without psychological or physical risks.

Finally, to evaluate hypothesis 5, the implemented analyses refer a McDonald’s ω of 0.930 (95% CI = 0.917-0.942), and a Cronbach’s α of 0.926 (95% CI = 0.905-0.948), which corroborate the internal consistency of the instrument.

Discussion

The results of this study provide initial evidence on the psychometric properties of the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ) in older Colombian adults with chronic illnesses. Overall, the findings confirm that the complete scale structure does not achieve optimal factor fit across all criteria in this sample, which aligns with international reports that warn of the instrument’s instability in different cultural contexts [36, 37]. This result reinforces the need to critically examine the use of questionnaires developed in Anglo-Saxon environments when applied to populations with marked differences in language, educational level, sociocultural context, and social representations of illness.

In this study, the eight-item model originally proposed by Broadbent et al. [2] did not show satisfactory factorial fit. However, the analysis identified a set of four items with high internal consistency and factorial strength (items 1, 5, 6, and 8), primarily related to emotional distress, perceived severity, and the consequences of the illness. The fit indices for the resulting model, hereafter called to as Ultra B-IPQ, showed a unifactorial model with excellent fit-indices (non-significative p-value, Chi-Square/df < 3.0, CFI, NFI and TLI > 0.95); however, the RMSEA had a value of 0.10 (95% CI: 0.05-0.18), an excellent internal consistency (α = 0.926; ω = 0.930), and evidence of factorial invariance by sex and age. Although the RMSEA indicator is at the acceptance threshold, it should be interpreted with caution, given its high sensitivity to sample size and the magnitude of degrees of freedom [56]. Taking the above into account, the combined interpretation of all indicators provides statistical evidence supporting the structural validity of the four-item instrument and suggests that this abbreviated version constitutes a parsimonious and culturally appropriate alternative for assessing illness perception in older adults in the Colombian Caribbean.

The item refinement process finds both empirical and conceptual support. During the exploratory analysis conducted in the pilot phase (supplementary material), items were identified that did not show adequate fit with the older adult population, particularly those related to duration, self-control, treatment effect, and understanding of the illness. These items are associated with more cognitive components of Leventhal’s self-regulation model [4], while the retained items capture emotional and experiential dimensions of distress.

Compared to the original scale, items 2, 3, 4, and 7 relate to aspects close to perception but do not directly contribute to that construct: item 2 refers to duration (they may feel sick regardless of the duration – short or indefinite), item 3 to control (they may feel sick regardless of whether they feel they have little or much control), item 4 to perception of improvement (they may feel sick regardless of whether or not they believe they can improve), and item 7 to understanding of their condition (they perceive themselves as sick regardless of whether they understand the origin and progression of the illness they are suffering from). These items are associated with components that have a more cognitive focus in Leventhal’s self-regulation model (4–5), which, according to the evidence, exhibits greater variability due to cultural and intersubjective biases [57], whereas the retained items capture affective and experiential dimensions of distress that are likely more widely shared by the target population [58].

On the other hand, it is noted that in the confirmatory analysis, the model fit indicators improve when the covariance of errors between items 1 and 8 is established, without this covariance reaching the collinearity threshold of 0.85 in statistical terms [59]. This is also consistent with prior theory because both items aim to assess the individual’s perception of physical (item 1) and emotional (item 8) distress, in which both dimensions arise simultaneously and are interdependent (15–21), without conceptual overlap.

In this sense, the version obtained of the Ultra B-IPQ appears to more accurately reflect how older adults in the Colombian Caribbean experience and subjectively process their illness. This finding coincides with studies conducted in other non-Anglo-Saxon contexts, where affective and somatic dimensions tend to show greater structural weight than cognitive or explanatory dimensions [49, 60]. In populations with less access to health information or with fragmented care trajectories, the perception of illness may be constructed more from lived suffering than from a rational understanding of the diagnosis or treatment.

From a sociocultural perspective, this interpretation takes on relevance. In regions like the Colombian Caribbean, older adults face persistent inequalities in the social determinants of health and structural barriers to accessing services [46]. These conditions not only influence how individuals interpret their illness in the present but are also embedded in historical trajectories of interaction with the health system, accumulated caregiving experiences, and family and community networks, reinforcing a view of illness centered on its immediate and progressive emotional and functional impact throughout the life course. Furthermore, the symbolic weight of illness in extended family contexts and highly religious environments can strengthen the emotional and fatalistic dimensions of the construct, as reported by qualitative research in Latin American communities [19, 42, 45]. Consequently, the ultrashort version of the B-IPQ not only offers a psychometric advantage but also appears to be better suited to the cultural idiosyncrasies and social conditions of the region.

The results regarding convergent and divergent validity confirm this interpretation. The Ultra B-IPQ showed significant and consistent correlations with measures of physical health, emotional regulation, fatigue, and self-efficacy. The negative associations with quality of life and self-efficacy, and the positive associations with fatigue, are consistent with those reported in the international literature [37, 44, 45]. In particular, the strong correlation between negative perception of illness and greater perceived fatigue supports the construct validity of the instrument and underscores the role of subjective perception as a predictor of clinical and psychological outcomes in chronic conditions. This link highlights the potential utility of the Ultra B-IPQ for identifying risk profiles for maladaptive coping or poor treatment adherence.

This study represents, to our knowledge, the first formal validation of the B-IPQ in a Colombian population, specifically in older adults from the Caribbean region. This contribution is especially important, as most of the available evidence comes from research in Europe and Asia [2, 15, 26, 38, 39, 40, 41], and adaptations for Latin American contexts are scarce. Validating the instrument in this context not only strengthens local research but also provides a reliable tool for clinical practice and intervention in the primary healthcare setting [47]. In settings where resources are limited and healthcare is often mediated by social and economic factors, having a brief, valid, and culturally sensitive measure contributes to the early detection of maladaptive beliefs that affect adherence to treatment for chronic diseases, particularly in older adults.

In practical terms, the ultra-short version of the B-IPQ could be used as a screening tool in public health programs, primary care consultations, or community interventions with older adults. Its quick administration (less than five minutes) and simple language facilitate the identification of patients with negative or distorted perceptions of their illness, allowing for the design of psychological interventions focused on cognitive restructuring, health education, and strengthening self-efficacy in these individuals, as well as the subsequent objective evaluation of the effectiveness of these approaches.

Furthermore, its usefulness could be extended to epidemiological or longitudinal follow-up studies, where the burden of assessment often limits the use of lengthy scales.

However, the results should be interpreted with caution and within the framework of the methodological decisions adopted. The non-probabilistic nature of the sample and its geographical concentration in two departments limit the generalizability of the findings to the other regions of the country, which have different cultural and linguistic characteristics. Likewise, reducing the scale by half its items presents the challenge of ensuring that the ultrashort version retains the conceptual richness of the original model. Future research should replicate these analyses in other regions of the country, explore its temporal stability using longitudinal designs, and examine its predictive capacity for clinical outcomes, such as treatment adherence or rehospitalizations. It would also be pertinent to compare the ultrashort version with the full B-IPQ and with other disease representation measures to establish their conceptual convergence.

Taken together, the findings allow us to conclude that the Ultra B-IPQ is a valid, reliable, and culturally appropriate measure for assessing illness perception in older Colombian adults with chronic diseases. Its strength lies not only in its psychometric performance but also in its contextual relevance, integrating a situated perspective on the experience of illness in populations historically marginalized by the healthcare system. This study, therefore, contributes to the advancement of a more contextualized Health Psychology, capable of linking psychometric research with the real needs of communities and promoting more comprehensive, equitable, and culturally sensitive care.

Limitations

While the results are consistent and promising, the study has some methodological limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, a critical limitation is the RMSEA value (0.11), which could be influenced by the sample size or the drastic reduction in items, affecting the accuracy of the unifactorial structure. Likewise, the use of non-probability sampling in a specific geographical region limits the generalization of the findings to the entire Colombian population, and this sample included only individuals with optimal cognitive functioning and literacy skills; therefore, the validation of the instrument would not cover individuals with limitations in those areas.

Secondly, the cross-sectional design prevents examining the temporal stability of the instrument; future longitudinal studies should evaluate the temporal invariance and sensitivity to change of the Ultra B-IPQ in the face of psychoeducational or therapeutic interventions.

Third, while reducing the scale from eight to four items optimizes psychometric efficiency, it may limit the conceptual scope of Leventhal’s original model, particularly regarding cognitive components (duration, personal control, and understanding of the illness). It would be advisable to compare the performance of the Ultra B-IPQ with the full version and with other illness perception scales to assess its theoretical convergence.

Finally, it would be pertinent to replicate the validation in specific clinical samples (e.g., diabetes, heart failure, chronic kidney disease) and in Latin American intercultural contexts, considering factors such as health literacy, religiosity, support networks, and structural inequality. Such research would consolidate the external validity and clinical utility of this tool in assessing disease representations and in designing evidence-based intervention programs.

Conclusions

This study constitutes the first formal validation of the Brief Illness Perception Questionnaire (B-IPQ) in older Colombian adults with chronic illnesses, providing solid evidence of its psychometric properties in a Latin American sociocultural context. The results support a robust unifactorial structure of the reduced four-item model (Ultra B-IPQ), with adequate fit indices (non-significant p-value; ChiSquare/df < 3.0; CFI, TLI, and NFI > 0.95; RMSEA = 0.10), excellent internal consistency (α = 0.926; ω = 0.930), and evidence of configural, metric, scalar, and strict factor invariance across gender and age.

The Ultra B-IPQ version is presented as a parsimonious and acceptable alternative for assessing disease perception. Although the comparative fit indices are high, the root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) suggests that the results should be interpreted with caution, and replication in larger samples is necessary to consolidate its structural validity.

Furthermore, the correlations observed with indicators of quality of life, emotional regulation, fatigue, and self-efficacy confirm the convergent and divergent validity of the instrument, in line with Leventhal’s self-regulation theory and recent international studies. Taken together, the results support the use of the Ultra B-IPQ as a brief, reliable, and culturally appropriate tool for assessing illness perception in clinical, community, and public health settings.

From an applied perspective, its implementation can facilitate the early identification of maladaptive beliefs related to the perception of illness, particularly those associated with suffering and negative experiences; the resulting information could help improve patient-professional communication and optimize intervention strategies focused on coping, health education, and treatment adherence. The study thus contributes to a contextualized, intercultural, and socially sensitive approach to health psychology, strengthening the empirical basis for more comprehensive and equitable interventions within the Colombian healthcare system.

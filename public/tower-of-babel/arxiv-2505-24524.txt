A new addition to the zoo of isolated symplectic singularities
Callum Berry

Abstract
. We give details of a new isolated symplectic singularity found in an affine chart in a crepant partial resolution of ℂ 4 / G 5 \mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5} , which is 4-dimensional, isolated, and locally simply-connected. We distinguish the new singularity among all known such by the fact that the projective tangent cone at the singularity is non-reduced. We also find all 12 of its ℚ ​ -factorial \mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial} terminalisations, in the process finding 24 for the quotient singularity ℂ 4 / G 5 \mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5} .



1.
Introduction

In recent years there has been substantial interest in symplectic singularities, as introduced by Beauville (
[
2
]
).
This is particularly motivated by connections with representation theory and mathematical physics.

Basic examples of isolated symplectic singularities are the rational double points
and the closures of minimal nilpotent orbits of simple complex Lie algebras.
Additionally, given a symplectic singularity
X
and a finite group
\Gamma
of automorphisms preserving the symplectic form on the smooth locus
X_{reg}
,
the quotient
X/\Gamma
is also a symplectic singularity.
Hence, quotients of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
by finite subgroups of
\text{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
that act freely away the origin,
and quotients of isolated symplectic singularities by finite groups that act freely on the smooth locus and preserve its symplectic form,
are also isolated symplectic singularities.

The main result of Beauville’s seminal paper is a partial classification result:

Theorem
.

Let
(V,o)
be an isolated symplectic singularity, whose projective tangent cone is smooth.
Then
(V,o)
is analytically isomorphic to the closure of the minimal nilpotent orbit of some simple complex Lie algebra.

Further examples of isolated symplectic singularities have since been found.
The quasi-minimal singularities
ac_{n}
,
ag_{2}
and
cg_{2}
were found as elementary slices of affine Grassmannians in 2003 by Malkin-Ostrik-Vybornov (
[
18
, §6.3]
).
An infinite family
\{\mathcal{Y}(d):d\geq 4\}
derived from quotients of
\mathbb{C}^{4}
by dihedral groups was found in 2021 by Bellamy-Bonnafé-Fu-Juteau-Levy-Sommers (
[
4
]
).
An infinite collection of singularities
Y(A,0)
given with a toric hyper-Kähler construction was found in 2023 by Namikawa (
[
22
]
), which subsumes the quasi-minimal singularities (Appendix
C
for
ag_{2}
).

It follows from the remarks in
[
2
, §4.1]
and the fact that the local fundamental group is finite
[
5
]
,
that any isolated symplectic singularity with non-trivial local fundamental group can be realised as the quotient of a symplectic singularity by a finite group.
As remarked by Beauville, this reduces the classification of isolated symplectic singularities to those which are locally simply-connected.

In the years since Beauville’s paper,
a different approach to classification has emerged,
given by the minimal model program.
This focuses on
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial}
terminal singularities,
and aims to classify the maximal crepant partial resolutions of singularities (not necessarily unique).
However, as remarked in section
6
, these two perspectives cannot be considered simultaneously.

The simplest singularities to consider would be those of the lowest dimension.
The 2-dimensional case is precisely the rational double points,
which are well known,
so we consider the 4-dimensional case instead.
Notably, a symplectic singularity has terminal singularities precisely when the codimension of the singular locus is at least 4 (
[
20
]
), so these isolated singularities will be terminal.

We consider the areas where one would look for new examples.
There is a rich source from the Coulomb branches of 3d
\mathcal{N}=4
gauge theories (
[
3
]
).
Those of dimension 4 with unitary quivers have been classified (
[
11
]
),
and those of arbitrary dimension with gauge group a torus have also (
[
10
]
).
One could also look at the crepant partial resolutions of other symplectic singularities (
[
23
]
).
This is motivated by the success in the case of the
\mathcal{Y}(d)
.

We follow a similar approach in this paper.
It is known that
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{4}
has two symplectic resolutions
(and no new isolated singularities appear),
so we consider the next exceptional Shephard-Todd group,
G_{5}
.
The calculations are much more involved but it is worthwhile.
We find that an affine chart in the blow-up at the reduced singular locus of
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
is a new isolated symplectic singularity, which we denote
\textctyogh
.

The first endeavour of the paper is to outline the calculations required to obtain
\textctyogh
.
We then prove
\textctyogh
is locally simply-connected, along with detailing some useful symmetries and the Hilbert series.
The main result of the paper is then proven: that
\textctyogh
is distinct from all other currently known symplectic singularities.
Specifically, it has a projective tangent cone at the singularity which is non-reduced,
whilst all other known locally simply-connected, isolated symplectic singularities have a projective tangent cone at the singularity which is reduced.
Finally we give all 12 of the
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial terminalisation}
s of
\textctyogh
, allowing us to describe 24 of the 92
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial terminalisation}
s of
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
.

3.
Obtaining and Verifying a New Symplectic Singularity

In this section we give the details of blowing up
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
at the reduced singular locus,
and the subsequent description of the new singularity
\textctyogh
.
We also give the verification of
\textctyogh
as an isolated symplectic singularity.

Blowing up at
I
, we can cover the blow-up with affine charts corresponding to the generators of the ideal.
Label the blow-up as
\pi_{I}:S_{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
.
Amongst the affine charts, we can show that those corresponding to generators

R_{4,4},R_{9,3},R_{3,9},R_{13,1},R_{1,13},R_{24,0},R_{0,24}

cover
S_{I}
,
so considering these will be sufficient for our purposes.
Label these affine charts as

X_{4,4},X_{9,3},X_{3,9},X_{13,1},X_{13,1},X_{24,0},X_{0,24}

respectively,
i.e.
X_{4,4}
is the affine open subset of
S_{I}
consisting of the points with the projective coordinate corresponding to
R_{4,4}
being non-zero.
Using the symmetry
\phi

X_{4,4},X_{9,3},X_{13,1}
and
X_{24,0}

to check for new singularities.

3.1.
Affine charts of the blow-up

3.1.1.

\mathbb{C}[X_{24,0}]=\mathbb{C}\left[h,A_{1},\ldots,C_{-2},\frac{R_{4,4}}{R_{24,0}},\frac{R_{9,3}}{R_{24,0}},\ldots\frac{R_{0,24}}{R_{24,0}}\right]

This has been verified to be a complete list by checking that the free algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
with free generators given by the variables

has the ideal generated by these relations as a prime ideal of dimension 4,
via computer calculation,
and then using this result to extend the method of proof from base field

The primality test used by computers uses elimination theory,
which can be extended to a higher field in our case,
provided the variable

This is easy to confirm for this case, and all other cases in this paper.

A conceptual proof that this ideal is prime, using the projection

3.3.
Isolatedness

It is clear from the set of relations that the origin is a singular point in
\textctyogh
.
To show that it is the only singular point,
we take advantage of the other affine charts we calculated.

Recall that
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
is split over
\mathbb{R}
.
As the the ideal being blown up is stable under complex conjugation (acting as an
\mathbb{R}
-symmetry),
we know the affine charts
X_{9,3}^{\prime}:=\{R_{9,3}^{\prime}=\overline{R_{9,3}}\neq 0\}
and
X_{3,9}^{\prime}:=\{R_{3,9}^{\prime}=\overline{R_{3,9}}\neq 0\}
are symmetric to
X_{9,3}
and
X_{3,9}

In particular they are also smooth.

To summarise, we know that
X_{24,0},X_{0,24},X_{9,3}
,
X_{9,3}^{\prime}
,
X_{3,9}
and
X_{3,9}^{\prime}
are smooth,
and that the only singularities in
X_{13,1}
and
X_{1,13}
are their respective origins.
Due to
f_{4,4}
appearing as a generator in the coordinate algebra of
X_{13,1}
, and the symmetric result for
X_{1,13}
,
the origins for these affine charts lie outside of
X_{4,4}
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that
X_{4,4}
without its origin is covered by its intersections with these other affine charts.
To prove this, it suffices to show that in the free algebra generated by
h,c_{1},\ldots,z_{-2}
, all of the generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}]
lie in the radical of the ideal which is generated by the 35 relations and the variables

k_{9,3},{k_{9,3}}^{\prime},k_{3,9},{k_{3,9}}^{\prime},k_{13,1},k_{1,13},k_{24,0},k_{0,24}
.
This can be verified via computer calculation.

3.4.
Normality

To show that
\textctyogh
is a symplectic singularity, we first need to show that it is normal.
We show it is Cohen-Macaulay.
Combined with the fact that
\textctyogh
is nonsingular in codimension 1,
this implies normality via Serre’s criterion.
Therefore, it remains to show that the local ring of
\textctyogh
at the origin is Cohen-Macaulay.
It suffices to show that the local ring has depth 4, i.e. a regular sequence of length 4 in the maximal ideal.
We can verify using a computer that
h,z_{2},c_{-1},c_{1}+z_{-2}
is a regular sequence of length 4 in
R
that is contained in the maximal ideal corresponding to the origin.
This gives that it is the same in the localisation at the origin, proving that
\textctyogh
is Cohen-Macaulay, and so by extension normal.

3.5.
Symplectic singularity

Recall the blow-up of
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
at
I
,
\pi_{I}:S_{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
.
As a result of being defined in such a manner,
S_{I}
has the property that any of its resolutions will compose to a resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
.
Hence, by the definition of symplectic singularity,

S_{I}
can only fail to be a symplectic singularity if the extension of the pull-back of the symplectic form
\omega_{0}
of
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
to
S_{I}
is degenerate on the smooth locus
{S_{I}}^{sm}
.
We prove this by relating it to the condition of
\pi_{I}
being semi-small.

Degeneracy of the symplectic form
\omega_{I}
is equivalent to
\omega_{I}\wedge\omega_{I}=0
, and so the subvariety it defines will either be empty or codimension 1.
Label this subvariety
T
.

Stratify
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
into its smooth locus, the singular points away from the origin, and the origin.
The blow-up
\pi_{I}
acts as an isomorphism on the smooth locus, and so these points cannot lie in
T
.
The singular points away from the origin are locally isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times A_{2}
, and so
\pi_{I}
must act as a symplectic resolution, and so the preimages of these points cannot lie in
T
.

This leaves the preimage of the origin under the blow-up.
To show
\dim({\pi_{I}}^{-1}(0))\leq 2
,
we describe its intersection with each of the affine charts in the cover as contained in a subvariety of dimension
2
.
These subvarieties are given by the ideals

\langle A_{1},C_{2}\rangle\subseteq X_{24,0},\langle h,A_{1}\rangle\subseteq X_{13,1},\langle h,A_{1}\rangle\subseteq X_{9,3},\langle h,z_{0}\rangle\subseteq X_{4,4},

and similar for the corresponding symmetric affine charts.
Therefore
\dim({\pi_{I}}^{-1}(0))\leq 2
, so
\dim(T)\leq 2
, so
T
must be empty.
This shows
S_{I}
is a symplectic singularity, and so also
\textctyogh
.

4.
Properties of the New Singularity

4.1.
Useful symmetries

There are multiple useful symmetries of
\textctyogh
.

We still have the index-swapping symmetry
\phi
coming from
\mathbb{C}[x,y,X,Y]
, given as

\phi:x\mapsto X,y\mapsto Y,X\mapsto x,Y\mapsto y.

This is of order 2 and acts on the generators as

\begin{array}[]{c}\phi:h\mapsto h,c_{1}\mapsto-c_{-1},c_{0}\mapsto c_{0},d_{1}\mapsto-d_{-1},d_{0}\mapsto d_{0},z_{2}\mapsto-z_{-2},z_{1}\mapsto z_{-1},z_{0}\mapsto-z_{0}.\end{array}

We have an order 2 symmetry that swaps the

and
d_{i}
.
Define an automorphism

on
\mathbb{C}[x,y,X,Y]
by sending

\tau:x\mapsto e^{\frac{5\pi i}{12}}x,\kern 5.0pty\mapsto e^{\frac{-\pi i}{12}}y,\kern 5.0ptX\mapsto e^{\frac{-5\pi i}{12}}X,\kern 5.0ptY\mapsto e^{\frac{\pi i}{12}}Y.

The monomials of the generators of
\mathbb{C}[x,y,X,Y]^{G_{5}}
are multiplied by
1
or
-1
under this.
We can check that
\tau
acts on the singularity by fixing

, swapping the
c_{i}
’s with the
d_{i}
’s, and multiplying the
z_{i}
’s by
-1
.

We have a symmetry
\chi
of order 3 that fixes

and the

by
\sigma=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}
and

by
\sigma^{2}
.
This is specific to this singularity, and does not induce an action on
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
.

These symmetries form a dihedral group of order 12.
It is easy to see that
\phi
commutes with
\tau
and
\chi
,
whilst
\tau
and

4.2.

Lemma 4.2.1

Proof.

For the natural grading on

All closed strict subvarieties of this are therefore of strictly lower complex dimension,

This applies to the intersection of

5.
Projective Tangent Cones of Isolated Symplectic Singularities

By the results of the previous section,
\textctyogh
is a locally simply-connected, isolated, 4-dimensional symplectic singularity.
In this section we prove it is not (analytically) isomorphic to any previously known isolated 4-dimensional symplectic singularity.
Recalling the classification of these known cases into families in the introduction,
we can dismiss the quotient singularities due to
\textctyogh
being locally simply-connected.
This leaves the closures of minimal nilpotent orbits in simple Lie algebras,
the infinite family obtained from dihedral groups,
and a specific construction of toric hyper-Kähler varieties.

The key property we use to distinguish
\textctyogh
is that its projective tangent cone at the singular point is non-reduced.
Given the coordinate algebra
R=\mathbb{C}[\textnormal{\textctyogh}]
and the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\langle h,c_{1},\ldots,z_{-2}\rangle
at the singularity
0
,
recall that the projective tangent cone of
\textctyogh
at the singularity,
\mathbb{P}T_{0}(\textnormal{\textctyogh})
,
is given as the Proj of the associated graded algebra

\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{i}}{\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}}
.

All of these terms
\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{i}}{\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}}
are invariant under localisation at
\mathfrak{m}
, and then under completion.
Hence, any analytic isomorphism between varieties
(X,x)
and
(Y,y)

clearly induces an isomorphism on the projective tangent cones,
\mathbb{P}T_{x}(X)\cong\mathbb{P}T_{y}(Y)
.
Therefore, the projective tangent cone at the singularity is indeed an invariant of an isolated symplectic singularity, and can be used to distinguish it from others.

As an associated graded algebra of
R
, we have
\dim(\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))=4
.
Therefore, the projective tangent cone Proj
(\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is reduced precisely when
\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is reduced as a ring,
and likewise Proj
(\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is in addition irreducible precisely when
\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is an integral domain.

Consider
R=\mathbb{C}[\textnormal{\textctyogh}]
,
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\langle h,c_{1},\ldots,z_{-2}\rangle
,
and coordinate algebra of affine tangent cone at the singularity
S=\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
.
Observing the relations for
\textctyogh
, we can see that the relation

{c_{1}}^{2}c_{0}+{d_{1}}^{2}d_{0}+2{z_{1}}^{2}=0

in
R
becomes the relation

{z_{1}}^{2}=0

in
S
.
As there are no linear terms in the relation list given,
it is clear that
z_{1}\neq 0
in
S
, so this is a nilpotent element.
Therefore
S
is non-reduced,
so
\mathbb{P}T_{0}(\textnormal{\textctyogh})
is non-reduced.

This is a property shared by many quotient singularities, for example the rational double points of types
D
and
E
.
However, it appears to be exceptional for the case of being locally simply-connected.

For an isolated symplectic singularity
X
with sole singular point
0
, the projective tangent cone
\mathbb{P}T_{0}(X)
is smooth precisely for the case of closures of minimal nilpotent orbits of simple Lie algebras (
[
2
, p.1]
).
Being smooth implies being reduced, so this case is covered.

5.1.
The dihedral family
\mathcal{Y}
(d)

Consider the family of symplectic singularities
\mathcal{Y}(d)
(
[
4
]
).
The coordinate algebra
R_{d}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Y}(d)]
is
\mathbb{C}[q,Q,e,b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}]
with relations of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}eb_{j}=qb_{j+1}+Qb_{j-1}\\
b_{j}b_{k}-b_{j-1}b_{k+1}\in\textnormal{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\{q,Q,e\}^{d-2})\end{array}\right.\textnormal{for }1\leq j\leq k\leq d-1.

The case
d=4
is the coordinate algebra of the
a_{2}
singularity.
This is the closure of the minimal nilpotent orbit for
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
,
and so the projective tangent cone is smooth,
notably also reduced.

Consider
d>4
.
With
\mathfrak{m}=\langle q,Q,e,b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}\rangle
,
the coordinate ring of the affine tangent cone at the singularity is

S_{d}=\textnormal{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R_{d})
.
As
d-2>2
, we have

S_{d}=\mathbb{C}[q,Q,e,b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}]

with relations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}eb_{j}=qb_{j+1}+Qb_{j-1}\\
b_{j}b_{k}-b_{j-1}b_{k+1}=0\end{array}\right.\textnormal{for }1\leq j\leq k\leq d-1.

We want to show this is an integral domain,
and we do so by showing it is a subring of another simpler integral domain.

Consider the algebra

A:=\mathbb{C}[q,Q,e,s,t]/\langle est=qt^{2}+Qs^{2}\rangle
,
the cyclic group
\mu_{d}=\langle g|g^{d}\rangle
,
and the action of
\mu_{d}
on
A
via

g:q\mapsto q,Q\mapsto Q,e\mapsto e,s\mapsto\zeta s,t\mapsto\zeta t

for
\zeta=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}}
a primitive
d
-th root of unity.

Labelling
\widetilde{b_{j}}:=s^{d-j}t^{j}
,
we can easily see

A^{\mu_{d}}:=\mathbb{C}[q,Q,e,\widetilde{b_{0}},\widetilde{b_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{b_{d}}]
with relations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}e\widetilde{b_{j}}=q\widetilde{b_{j+1}}+Q\widetilde{b_{j-1}}\\
\widetilde{b_{j}}\widetilde{b_{k}}-\widetilde{b_{j-1}}\widetilde{b_{k+1}}=0\end{array}\right.\textnormal{for }1\leq j\leq k\leq d-1.

From this, it is easy to form the isomorphism
A^{\mu_{d}}\cong S_{d}
.

As
est-qt^{2}-Qs^{2}
is irreducible as a polynomial over
\mathbb{C}
,
A
is an integral domain.
The ring of invariants
A^{\mu_{d}}
is thus also an integral domain.
Therefore the coordinate algebra of the affine tangent cone,
S_{d}
, is an integral domain.
Hence, the projective tangent cone
\mathbb{P}T_{0}(\mathcal{Y}(d))=
Proj
(S_{d})
is irreducible, and in particular reduced.

5.2.
Toric hyper-Kähler varieties and Coulomb branches

Consider the family of symplectic singularities described in the paper by Namikawa, given as toric hyper-Kähler varieties (
[
22
]
).
These all have a single isolated singularity.
We will only be interested in the 4-dimensional case,
but the results in this subsection work for arbitrary dimension,
and so will be given as such.

Toric hyper-Kähler varieties were introduced by Bielawski-Dancer (
[
8
]
).
Those we are referencing are constructed using the following data.
Let
a
,
b
be positive integers such that
a\geq b+2
.
Let
A
(resp.
B
) be a
b\kern-2.0pt\times\kern-2.0pta
-matrix
(resp. an
a\kern-2.0pt\times\kern-2.0pt(a-b)
-matrix)
with integer coefficients such that the following sequence is exact:

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{a-b}\xrightarrow{B}\mathbb{Z}^{a}\xrightarrow{A}\mathbb{Z}^{b}\rightarrow 0.

Given this, a Hamiltonian action of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{b}
on the symplectic manifold
(\mathbb{C}^{2a},\omega)
is constructed,
with moment map
\mu:\mathbb{C}^{2a}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{b}
.
The toric hyper-Kähler variety
Y(A,0)=\mu^{-1}(0)/\kern-4.0pt/(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{b}

is then given as the GIT quotient of the preimage of
0
under
\mu
, by the Hamiltonian action.

Those considered in Namikawa’s paper have restrictions on
A
which result in
Y(A,0)
being an isolated symplectic singularity which is locally simply-connected.
However, we can consider a more general case.
As described by Namikawa (
[
19
, §5.ii]
),
we can realise such a toric hyper-Kähler variety as a Coulomb branch of a 3-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
SUSY gauge theory with gauge group a torus.
Such a Coulomb branch is conical, so if it has isolated singularities,
it must have only one such, and it must be the origin.
To address this, we show that each of these has a reduced projective tangent cone at the origin.
These Coulomb branches have been given an explicit algebraic presentation (
[
12
, §4.i]
), so this is the framework we will be using throughout this proof.

For a
2n
-dimensional Coulomb branch
C
, the torus gauge group
T
must be rank
n
, so
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
.
Let
Y=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
denote the coweight lattice of
T
,
and
\mathfrak{t}
the Lie algebra of
T
with corresponding dual space
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The Coulomb branch also depends on a finite-dimensional representation
\mathbf{N}
of
T
.
Choosing a basis of weight vectors for
\mathbf{N}
,
this amounts to a collection of characters
\xi_{1},\kern 1.0pt\ldots,\kern 1.0pt\xi_{m}
,
viewed as elements of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
Taking
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
such that

x_{i}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=\lambda_{i}
,
we have that each character
\xi_{i}\in\textnormal{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

To convert the previous data of a toric hyper-Kähler variety
Y(A,0)
to the data of a Coulomb branch,
take the gauge group to be the torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{a-b}
,
and the representation of
T
to be
\mathbf{N}=\mathbb{C}^{a}

6.
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalisations

In accordance with the minimal model program,
we would like to find the
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial}
terminalisations of the new symplectic singularity
\textctyogh

We will not be able to consider being locally simply-connected and being
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial}
terminal simultaneously, however.
For example, consider the Slodowy slice of the closure of the nilpotent orbit of Lie algebra

with Bala-Carter label

This is

As

In this section we find all 12

\textctyogh

In all blow-ups being considered, the center is a (reduced) prime divisor.
Since this has no effect on smooth points

6.2.
Position in the hierarchy of crepant partial resolutions

We want to see how this aligns with the structure of all possible crepant partial resolutions.
Symplectic singularities have hyperplane arrangements associated to them via the minimal model programme (
[
21
]
,
[
6
]
).
This has the action of a Namikawa Weyl group
W
.
For a given
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial terminalisation}

\pi:Y\rightarrow X

\text{Mov}(Y/X)
, which is a fundamental domain for the

Crepant partial resolutions of

are in bijection with the faces of
\overline{\text{Mov}(Y/X)}

[

).
A crepant blow-up of a given partial resolution will move from the corresponding face to one of higher dimension that contains the original in its closure.

Consider the data collected by Thiel for the hyperplane arrangement associated to
\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
(
[
25
]
).
The dimensions of the faces involved range from 0 to 4.
The crepant partial resolution with
\textctyogh
as its only singularity is obtained after two blow-ups,
and a
\mathbb{Q}\textnormal{-factorial terminalisation}
is given after two more,
so
\textctyogh
must correspond to a 2-dimensional face.
We want to know the possible number of hyperplanes intersecting such a face.

) to determine this from Thiel’s data.

Lemma B.0.1
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded ring,

f\in R
an element of positive degree,

I=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle
a homogeneous ideal of R,

J\subseteq I+\langle f\rangle
a prime ideal of
R
with
f\not\in J
,
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}
elements of
R
such that
\forall i,g_{i}+fh_{i}\in J
.
Then
J=\langle g_{1}+fh_{1},\ldots,g_{n}+fh_{n}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
J^{\prime}=\langle g_{1}+fh_{1},\ldots,g_{n}+fh_{n}\rangle
.
Clearly
J^{\prime}\subseteq J
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
\exists x\in J\setminus J^{\prime}
.
By using
J^{\prime}\subseteq J
to replace
g_{i}
with
-fh_{i}
in the expression of
x\in I+\langle f\rangle
,
without loss of generality we can assume
x\in\langle f\rangle
.
Let
y=\frac{x}{f}\in R
.

fy\in J
and
f\not\in J
, so by primality of
J
,
y\in J
.

x=yf
and
x\not\in J^{\prime}
so
y\not\in J^{\prime}
.

y
is of strictly lower degree than
x
, so induction can be used to give a contradiction that there must be a negative degree element of
J
.
∎

We can make use of this lemma by taking

R=\textnormal{Free}(h,A_{1},\ldots,C_{-2}),\kern 5.0ptf=h,\kern 5.0ptI\textnormal{ as above,}\kern 5.0ptJ=\ker(\pi_{1}).

This gives that the polynomials generating

\mathbb{C}^{4}/G_{5}
.

This applies well in this case due to the natural grading of

and the codomain of

being able to be chosen to be of only one Krull dimension above that of

We can also use this justification for the relations of

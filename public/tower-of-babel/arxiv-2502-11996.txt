Correlation functions of degenerate fields in Super-Liouville field theory
Aleksandra Ivanova 1. HSE University, 6 Usacheva str., Moscow 119048, Russia

Abstract
We study four-point correlation functions of degenerated fields in the N ​ S NS sector in Super-Liouville field theory. We find integral expressions for these functions using the BPZ equation, and study some superconformal properties of these solutions. Finally, we present the general form for three-point correlation functions.



1
Introduction

Liouville field theory (LFT) plays an important role in the description of non-critical string theories
[
1
,
2
]
. At the same time, supersymmetric extensions are of particular interest. One of the most important steps is to study and generalize the methods used in bosonic theories.

One of the main problems of any quantum field theory is the calculation of correlation functions. As it has been shown in
[
3
]
, correlation functions in LFT can be calculated using a method very similar to the representation of the Coulomb gas
[
4
]
.

There is another method for calculating correlation functions in Conformal Field Theory (CFT). This method is the following. It is well known that correlation functions containing at least one degenerate field obey some linear differential equations known as BPZ equations
[
5
]
. In many special cases, when degeneration occurs at low levels, four-point correlation functions can be obtained by directly solving these differential equations, thus avoiding Coulomb gas type representations. This is important because the charge balance condition for Coulomb gas affects all fields included in correlation function contrary to the degeneracy condition, that applies only to a special field. By exploring the crossing symmetry of those correlation functions, a set of functional relations on generic three point functions can be derived. This method is known as Teshner’s trick
[
6
]
. In
[
7
]
, the Teschner’s trick was used in Super-Liouville field theory for a degenerate vector on the second level in the Ramond sector. In our work, this method is applied to a vector at the
3/2
level in Neveu–Schwarz sector.

In this paper, we consider four-point correlation function in the
NS
sector of the
N=1
supersymmetric Liouville field theory. This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we give a brief overview of
N=1
supersymmetric CFT. Section 3 considers the subsystem of four-point correlation functions with an even number of fermions. Explicit expressions for all correlation functions in terms of the four-point bosonic function are given. After this, a similar analysis is performed for the odd subsystem. Section 4 shows that the selected bosonic function involving a degenerate field at the
3/2
level corresponds to a specific differential equation. At the same time, the solution of this equation has an integral form. Section 5 considers the superconformal properties of operator product expansions (OPE). Correlation functions are represented through structure constants related to the field fusion rules and to three-point functions. Ratios of these constants are found. Finally, Section 6 presents the general form of three-point correlation functions that received with help of functional equations for constants of three-point functions.

3
Ward identities in superconformal field theories

Conformal symmetry imposes certain constraints on correlation functions called global Ward Identities
[
5
]
. Moreover, each conserved current has its own Ward identity. These conditions impose special restrictions that are related to the fact that correlation functions tend to zero at infinity. They play an important role, since they provide explicit connections between correlation functions with different sets of fields.

The equation corresponding to the energy-momentum tensor has a fairly well-known form

\displaystyle\langle T(z)\Phi_{1}(z_{1})...\Phi_{n}(z_{n})\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\Delta_{k}}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{k}}{z-z_{k}}\bigg)\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})...\Phi_{n}(z_{n})\rangle,

(23)

and similar expressions including the field
\Psi(z)
. It is known that the correlation function including
T(z)
must fall off at infinity as

\displaystyle\langle T(z)...\rangle\thicksim\frac{1}{z^{4}},\hskip 20.0pt\text{at }z\rightarrow\infty.

(24)

Therefore, the terms of order
1/z,1/z^{2}
and
1/z^{3}
on the right-hand side of (
23
) must vanish

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k}\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})...\Phi_{n}(z_{n})\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\Delta_{k}+z_{k}\partial_{k})\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})...\Phi_{n}(z_{n})\rangle=0,

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2z_{k}\Delta_{k}+z_{k}^{2}\partial_{k})\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})...\Phi_{n}(z_{n})\rangle=0.

To understand the structure of global Ward identities for the supercurrent
G(z)
, we consider the example of two-point correlation function. Thus, for the NS-sector, the equations take the form

\displaystyle\langle G(z)\Phi_{1}(z_{1})\Phi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle=\frac{\langle\Psi_{1}(z_{1})\Phi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle}{z-z_{1}}+\frac{\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})\Psi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle}{z-z_{2}},

\displaystyle\langle G(z)\Phi_{1}(z_{1})\Psi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle=\frac{\langle\Psi_{1}(z_{1})\Psi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle}{z-z_{1}}+\frac{2\Delta_{2}\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})\Phi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle}{(z-z_{2})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z_{2}}\langle\Phi_{1}(z_{1})\Phi_{2}(z_{2})\rangle}{z-z_{2}}.

In contrast to the equation (
23
) for
T(z)
, the insertion of
G(z)
produces correlation functions of different sets of fields. This fact is expected because there are two fields,
\Phi(z)
and
\Psi(z)
, which interact differently with
G(z)
. The generalization for
n
-point correlation functions

\displaystyle\langle G(z)...\Phi_{i}(z_{i})...\Psi_{j}(w_{j})...

\displaystyle\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{k_{i}}\frac{\langle...\Psi_{i}(z_{i})...\Psi_{j}(w_{j})...\rangle}{z-z_{i}}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}\bigg(\frac{2\Delta_{j}}{(z-w_{j})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w_{j}}}{z-w_{j}}\bigg)\langle...\Phi_{i}(z_{i})...\Phi_{j}(w_{j})...\rangle,

(26)

where i belongs to bosons, and j belongs to fermions. There is
k_{i}
in the first line that shows the number of fermions before
\Phi_{i}(z_{i})
.

Similarly to
T(z)
, the supercurrent
G(z)
should falls at infinity as

\displaystyle\langle G(z)...\rangle\thicksim\frac{1}{z^{3}},\hskip 20.0pt\text{at }z\rightarrow\infty.

(27)

General expressions similar to (
25
) are huge for supercurrent case, and so we give below a special case of four-point functions with two fermions on the right side. The disappearance of orders
1/z,1/z^{2}
for (
26

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\underset{i\neq j}{\underset{i=1}{\sum^{4}}}(-1)^{\theta(j-i)}\langle...\Psi_{i}(z_{i})...\Psi_{j}(w_{j})...\rangle+\partial_{j}\langle...\Phi_{j}(w_{j})...\rangle=0,\\
&\underset{i\neq j}{\underset{i=1}{\sum^{4}}}(-1)^{\theta(j-i)}z_{i}\langle...\Psi_{i}(z_{i})...\Psi_{j}(w_{j})...\rangle+(2\Delta_{j}+z_{j}\partial_{j})\langle...\Phi_{j}(w_{j})...\rangle=0,\end{split}

(28)

where

– Heaviside step function.

Consequences from the Ward Identities for the stress-energy tensor are used to express the derivatives. In calculations, it is convenient to represent all derivatives through one of them. Thus we present explicit expressions for the derivatives in terms of
\partial

\langle O_{\alpha}(z)O_{1}(z_{1})O_{2}(z_{2})O_{3}(z_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle\partial_{i}=\frac{-(z-z_{k})(z-z_{l})\partial+\underset{m=0,i}{\sum}\Delta_{m}(-2z_{m}+\underset{j=k,l}{\sum}z_{j})+(-\Delta_{k}+\Delta_{l})(z_{k}-z_{l})}{(z_{i}-z_{k})(z_{i}-z_{l})},

Differential equation for the correlation function

In this paper the correlation functions involving degenerate fields in

were shown to have integral form, corresponding to the special Dotsenko-Fateev equation.

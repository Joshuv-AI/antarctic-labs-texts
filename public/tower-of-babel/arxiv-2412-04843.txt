Approximate Completeness of Hypersequent Calculus for First-Order Łukasiewicz Logic
Jin Wei

Abstract
. Hypersequent calculus GŁ ∀ \forall for first-order Łukasiewicz logic was first introduced by Baaz and Metcalfe, along with a proof of its approximate completeness with respect to standard [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] -semantics. The completeness result was later pointed out by Gerasimov that it only applies to prenex formulas. In this paper, we will present our proof of approximate completeness of GŁ ∀ \forall for arbitrary first-order formulas by generalizing the original completeness proof to hypersequents.



1.
Introduction

Łukasiewicz logic is one of the fuzzy logics where formulas take value in
[0,1]
. It can be axiomatized in a Hilbert-style proof system that is sound and complete with respect to
[0,1]
-semantics, or arbitrary MV-algebras. Its first-order variant however takes the following approximate form:

A\text{ is valid in }[0,1]\iff A\oplus A^{n}\text{ is provable for all natural number }n

where
A\oplus A^{n}
is a formula that approximates any truth assignment of
A
. The result is optimal in some sense as first-order valid formulas over
[0,1]
are not recursively enumerable (see
[
8
]
).

Gentzen-style proof systems for propostional Łukasiewicz logic were investigated by Metcalfe, Olivetti, and Gabbay in
[
9
]
in which a system of analytic hypersequent calculus GŁ was introduced. They prove the soundness and completeness of GŁ. A syntactical proof of cut elimination was also provided in
[
4
]
.

Baaz and Metcalfe extended the result to first-order Łukasiewicz logic in
[
2
]
by introducing GŁ
\forall
and proving its soundness and approximate completeness. The proof utilizes Skolemization and an approximate form of Herbrand’s theorem to convert any given valid first-order formula
A
to a valid quantifier-free formula
B\oplus B^{n}
. Then by the propositional completeness of GŁ, there exists a deduction of
B\oplus B^{n}
, from which a deduction of
A\oplus A^{n}
can be found by re-introducing quantifiers.

Later, Gerasimov points out in
[
6
]
that this proof can only apply to prenex formulas, since we cannot prove equivalence between first-order formulas to its prenex form from GŁ
\forall
without
cut
. It thus remains open whether GŁ
\forall
is complete with respect to
[0,1]
-semantics for arbitrary first-order formulas or not.

In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to the question by providing a proof for the approximate completeness of GŁ
\forall
. In fact, we have a stronger result:

\mathcal{H}\text{ is a valid hypersequent in }[0,1]\iff\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{n}}\text{ is provable in G\L$\forall$ for all }n

where
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{n}}
is a hypersequent that approximates any truth interpretation of
\mathcal{H}
. The original statement in
[
2
]
now becomes a corollary of this result.

4.
Completeness of GŁ
\forall

Given a valid hypersequent
\mathcal{H}
in first-order Łukasiewicz logic (in particular,
\mathcal{H}
contains no free variables), we will construct a search tree for deductions of
\mathcal{H}
. The following formula serves as a motivating example for the actual search algorithm.

Example 1

(Drinker Paradox)
.

Define

\varphi:=\exists x(A(x)\to\forall yA(y))
where
A
is a unary predicate symbol.

With some computation, we have that
\models_{[0,1]}\varphi
and
\vdash_{\text{G\L$\forall$}+cut}\Rightarrow\varphi
. However,
\varphi
is not provable in GŁ
\forall
(see
[
2
]
). Instead, we do have derivations of
\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}\varphi
for each
n
. To search for such derivations, we do a bottom-up Skolemization.

A(x)\Rightarrow A(f(x))

A(x),\bot\Rightarrow A(f(y)),\bot

A(x),A(f(y))\to\bot\Rightarrow\bot

A(x),\exists y(A(y)\to\bot)\Rightarrow\bot

A(x)\Rightarrow\exists y(A(y)\to\bot)\to\bot

A(x)\Rightarrow\forall yA(y)

\Rightarrow A(x)\to\forall yA(y)

\Rightarrow\exists x(A(x)\to\forall yA(y))

The sequent
A(x)\Rightarrow A(f(x))
may not have any Herbrand witnesses. Instead, for each
n
, there are approximate Herbrand terms
x,f(x),f(f(x))\ldots,f^{n-1}(x)
such that the following is valid

A(x)\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}A(f(x))\mid A(f(x))\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}A(f^{2}(x))\mid\cdots\mid A(f^{n-1}(x))\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}A(f^{n}(x))

We guarantee to have a derivation of the above quantifier-free hypersequent by Completeness of GŁ.

n\bot\Rightarrow nA(f^{n}(x))

n\bot,nA(x)\Rightarrow nA(f^{n}(x))

n\bot,nA(x),nA(f(x)),\ldots,nA(f^{n-1}(x))\Rightarrow nA(f(x)),\ldots,nA(f^{n}(x))

\bot,nA(x)\Rightarrow nA(f(x))\mid\bot,nA(f(x))\Rightarrow nA(f^{2}(x))\mid\cdots\mid\bot,nA(f^{n-1}(x))\Rightarrow nA(f^{n}(x))

A(x)\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}A(f(x))\mid A(f(x))\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}A(f^{2}(x))\mid\cdots\mid A(f^{n-1}(x))\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}A(f^{n}(x))

Then we will follow the above search tree from top-down to recover
\Rightarrow_{\frac{1}{n}}\exists x(A(x)\to\forall yA(y)
by re-introducing connectives in a certain order. We also need to admit
\frac{1}{n}
-version of connective introduction rules for that.

The above example can be generalized to arbitrary valid hypersequents to yield a completeness of GŁ
\forall
over
[0,1]
-semantics.

Theorem 4.5

(Approximate Herbrand Theorem)
.

Suppose
X
is a finite set of quantifier-free hypersequents with shared free variables
\bar{x}
and
n
is a natural number. Then there are terms
\bar{t}_{1},\ldots,\bar{t}_{k}
such that

\sup_{\mathfrak{M}}\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{t}_{i}/\bar{x}]^{\mathfrak{M}})<\sup_{\mathfrak{M}}\inf_{{\bar{m}}\in M}\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{m}/\bar{x}]^{\mathfrak{M}})+\frac{1}{n}

If we additionally assume

\sup_{\mathfrak{M}}\inf_{{\bar{m}}\in M}\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{m}/\bar{x}]^{\mathfrak{M}})\leq 0

then the hypersequent

\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\frac{1}{n}}[\bar{t}_{1}/\bar{x}]\mid\cdots\mid\mathcal{G}^{k}_{\frac{1}{n}}[\bar{t}_{k}/\bar{x}]

is valid for each
\mathcal{G}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}^{k}\in X
.

Proof.

We generalize the proof appearing in
[
2

. Let
P
collect all formulas of the form

where
r
is a relation symbol and
\bar{t}
are terms in the language. Note that a structure
\mathfrak{M}
is uniquely associated with a map

. For simplicity, let

Given arbitrary term
\bar{t}
, we define

\displaystyle S(\bar{t})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{v_{\mathfrak{M}}\in[0,1]^{P}:\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{t}/\bar{x}]^{\mathfrak{M}})\geq d+\frac{1}{n}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\mathcal{G}\in X}\{v_{\mathfrak{M}}\in[0,1]^{P}:(\mathcal{G}[\bar{t}/\bar{x}]^{\mathfrak{M}})\geq d+\frac{1}{n}\}

All

are closed subsets of

because
\to
,
\Rightarrow
, and
\mid
are interpreted as continuous functions on

with absence of quantifiers in

Suppose towards a contradiction that there are no finite list of terms

for all terms
\bar{t}
. It would particularly imply that

\displaystyle\inf_{\bar{m}\in M}\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{m}/\bar{x}]^{\mathfrak{M}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d+\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathfrak{M}}\inf_{{\bar{m}}\in M}\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{m}/\bar{x}])^{\mathfrak{M}}+\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathfrak{M}}\inf_{{\bar{m}}\in M}\max_{\mathcal{G}\in X}(\mathcal{G}[\bar{m}/\bar{x}])^{\mathfrak{M}}

5.
Further Directions

We end this paper by mentioning a few potential research directions.

First, continuous logic (see
[
10
]
) is based on first-order Łukasiewicz logic, plus a
\frac{1}{2}
-operator, a built-in bounded metric
d
, and uniformly continuity requirement for all function and relation symbols. A generalization of GŁ
\forall
to continuous logic and a similar proof of approximate completeness theorem should be possible.

Second, it should be possible to strengthen the cut admissibility to a cut elimination result. We might need an effective proof of Theorem
4.5
by the means of functional interpretation (see
[
7
]
).

Lastly, GŁ and GŁ
\forall
are non-constructive logics in the sense that
(A\to B)\vee(B\to A)
is derivable due to the use of
split
. One might consider a “intuitionistic" Łukasiewicz logic by removing
split
and only allow derivations of single sequents. A development of the intutionistic proof system and semantics, together with some equivalent form of realizability and double-negation translations could be interesting and have potentials in proof mining for continuous model theory. Notice that a system of intuitionistic Łukasiewicz logic was already investigated by Arthan and Oliva in
[
1
]
by removing double negation elimination. Their approach is different and it is also interesting to compare the two approaches.

Novel constraints on Λ ​ – ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} and p ​ – ​ Λ ¯ p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} interactions using correlation data Journal: Eur. Phys. J. C
Valentina Mantovani Sarti

Abstract
The interaction between baryons and antibaryons ( B ​ – ​ B ¯ \rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}} ) remains a fundamental topic in hadronic physics, particularly due to its potential to reveal exotic bound states such as baryonia. While the proton-antiproton ( p ​ – ​ p ¯ p\mbox{--}\bar{p} ) system has been extensively studied—mainly via scattering experiments—the interactions involving antihyperons, such as proton-antilambda ( p ​ – ​ Λ ¯ p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} ) and lambda-antilambda ( Λ ​ – ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} ), are still poorly constrained due to the scarcity of experimental data. High-precision data on these systems, covering the low-energy region down to zero momentum, have recently been obtained via femtoscopic measurements in small colliding systems at the LHC. These results offer a unique opportunity to probe these interactions at short distances with unprecedented precision. In this work, we extract for the first time the scattering parameters for the Λ ​ – ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} and p ​ – ​ Λ ¯ p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} systems by exploiting high-precision correlation data measured by the ALICE experiment in pp collisions. The goal is to investigate potential differences in the interplay between the elastic and annihilation components of the interaction potential. We employ a novel correlation analysis framework, the imaginary CATS (iCATS) package, which solves exactly the Schrödinger equation with complex optical potentials to model the strong final-state interactions (FSI) in systems dominated by inelastic processes, as is typical in the B ​ – ​ B ¯ \rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}} case. Our results indicate distinct annihilation characteristics between the p ​ – ​ Λ ¯ p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} and Λ ​ – ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} systems, challenging the assumption of a universal B ​ – ​ B ¯ \rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}} dynamics. The extracted scattering amplitudes are compared to available production cross sections and invariant mass spectra involving p ​ – ​ Λ ¯ p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} and Λ ​ – ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} pairs. The Λ ​ – ​ Λ ¯ \Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} and p ​ – ​ Λ ¯ p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda} FSI constrained to femtoscopic data deliver an overall consistent agreement for all these observables, indicating the possibility to explore the B ​ – ​ B ¯ \rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}} sector more in details in future correlation measurements.

2
Formalism

2.1
Femtoscopy

The main observable in two-body femtoscopy is the correlation function
C(k^{*})
, defined via the Koonin-Pratt equation
Lisa et al. 2005

\displaystyle C(k^{*})=\int S(\mathbf{r})|\Psi_{k^{*}}(\mathbf{r})|^{2}d^{3}r.

(1)

Here
k^{*}
and
r
are the relative distance and momentum of the pair, evaluated in the pair rest frame. The emitting source
S(\mathbf{r})
represents the distribution in
r
of the two particles in the pair and its size depends on the colliding system in which the measurement is performed
Fabbietti et al. 2021
, as discussed in the introduction. The interaction experienced between the particles in the pair is embedded in the relative wave function
\Psi_{k^{*}}(\mathbf{r})
. The latter can be obtained exactly by solving the Schrödinger equation for different values of
k^{*}
or by assuming an approximate expression given by its asymptotic behavior at large
r
within the Lednický–Lyuboshits approach. In this last case, by performing the integral in Eq.
1
with a Gaussian source, the Lednický–Lyuboshits analytical formula is obtained
Lednicky and Lyuboshits 1981
, widely used in heavy-ion collisions where source sizes of above 5 fm are achieved. The Lednický–Lyuboshits model suffers of several shortcomings when applied to small source sizes, as the ones obtained in pp collisions. First, the model is limited to only s-waves. Secondly, the applied correction to the original Lednický–Lyuboshits formula for small sources depends on the interplay between the effective range parameter
d_{0}
and the source size, which might lead to unphysical negative correlations. Recent theoretical works are aiming at improving these limitations
Albaladejo et al. 2024
;
Murase and Hyodo 2025
;
Albaladejo et al. 2025
.

A way to overcome the shortcomings presented within the Lednický–Lyuboshits approach is to employ the "Correlation Analysis Tool using the Schrödinger equation equation" (CATS)
Mihaylov et al. 2018
, able to compute the two-particle correlation function
C(k^{*})
for any central real potential and emission source function. The CATS framework have been used in the majority of recent correlations measured by the ALICE collaboration in pp collisions
Acharya et al. 2020a
;
Acharya et al. 2022e
;
Acharya et al. 2020c
;
Acharya et al. 2019a
;
Acharya et al. 2019b
;
Acharya et al. 2022a
;
Acharya et al. 2023b
;
Acharya et al. 2020d
and also in several phenomenological studies
Chizzali et al. 2024a
;
Mihaylov and González González 2023
;
Sarti et al. 2024
;
Mihaylov et al. 2024
.

In this work we present for the first time the iCATS package, which introduces in CATS the possibility to solve the Schrödinger equation with any complex potential and evaluate the two-particle correlation function for any emitting source profile.

2.2
iCATS overview

The core of the iCATS package is designed to solve the radial Schrödinger equation equation in the continuum for energies
E=\frac{\hslash^{2}(k^{*})^{2}}{2\mu}

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}u_{k^{*},l}}{dr^{2}}=\left[2\mu(V_{l}(r)+V_{C}(r))+\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}-(k^{*})^{2}\right]u_{k^{*},l}.

(2)

Here
u_{k^{*},l}=rR_{k^{*},l}(r)
is related to the radial component
R_{k^{*},l}
of the total wave function
\Psi_{k^{*}}(r,\theta,\phi)=R_{k^{*}}(r)Y_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
,written in a separable form thanks to the central potential assumption.
The quantity
\mu
is the reduced mass of the pair,
V_{C}(r)=\alpha_{s}\frac{q_{1}\cdot q_{2}}{r}
is the Coulomb potential present for charged systems and
V_{l}(r)
represents the strong complex potential. More details on the iCATS solver can be found in
A
.

In Eq.
2
we explicitly show the dependence on the pair relative momentum
k^{*}
and on the partial wave
l
. For simplicity, in the following discussion, we will omit these indices and refer simply to
u(r)
.

The iCATS solver assumes the following ansatz for the radial solution

\displaystyle u(r)=u_{R}(r)+iu_{I}(r)

(3)

which separates the real and imaginary part of the wave function. By substituting this expression in Eq.
2
, we obtain a system of two coupled equations for
u_{R}(r)
and
u_{I}(r)
1
1

1

For simplicity, let us consider a neutral pair with no Coulomb involved.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{R}=\left[2mV_{R}+\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}-k^{2}\right]u_{R}+2mV_{I}u_{I}\\
u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{I}=\left[2mV_{R}+\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}-k^{2}\right]u_{I}-2mV_{I}u_{R}.\\
\end{cases}

(4)

Following the same approach used in CATS, based on the Euler discretization of the second derivative of the wave functions (see Appendix A.2 in
Mihaylov et al. 2018
), the solution to the system in Eq.
4

finite difference method
(FDM) in the

, which determines the starting value for

and

j_{0}(k^{*}r)
at the origin. The iterative FDM approach is hence applied up to large values of

, corresponding to the asymptotic region where the effect of the strong potential is zero.

Once convergence is achieved, the numerical wave function

must be matched to the asymptotic boundary condition at

\displaystyle u_{\rm asy}\rightarrow Au^{+}-Bu^{-}\Rightarrow\dfrac{u_{\rm asy}}{A}\rightarrow(u^{+}-\dfrac{B}{A}u^{-})=(u^{+}-S^{\dagger}u^{-})

(5)

with the outgoing (

) asymptotic waves that, in case of neutral/charged pairs read (assuming

)

\displaystyle u^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e^{\pm ik^{*}r}}{2ik}=\dfrac{1}{2}i\cdot r\cdot n_{0}(k^{*}r)\pm\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot r\cdot j_{0}(k^{*}r),\,\,\,\text{(w/o Coulomb)}

\displaystyle u^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2ik}[G(k^{*}r)\pm i\cdot F(k^{*}r)],\,\,\,\text{(Coulomb)}

(6)

The function

(

) is the irregular (regular) spherical Bessel solution, while

(F(
k^{*}

Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
.

The complex numbers
A
and
B
in Eq.

are determined by imposing the matching between the numerical solution of
2
and this asymptotic condition in which the outgoing wave is normalized to unity. In particular, the outgoing condition written for the femtoscopic studies in Eq.
5
provides access to the conjugate of the scattering matrix
S(k^{*})

\delta_{l}

Once the iCATS solver provides the numerical solution

for different values of

, the CATS basic functionalities are used to provide as output the total correlation function

\displaystyle C(k^{*})=\sum_{\text{states}(I,S,L,J)}w_{(I,S,L,J)}C_{I,S,L,J}(k^{*}),

where

represent the isospin, spin, angular momentum and total angular momentum quantum numbers characterizing the pair under study. Each of the correlations

\displaystyle C_{\rm{model}}(k^{*})=1+\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\times(C_{i}(k^{*})-1).

(10)

The lambda parameters

\lambda_{i}

Acharya et al. 2019c

(
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}

Acharya et al. 2022e

\lambda_{\rm gen,p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}}=0.458
(

The measured
p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}

7\%

\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}

\Lambda

p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}

\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}

Acharya et al. 2022e

In the
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}

The fitting procedure uses iCATS to model the correlations entering in Eq.

4
Summary and conclusions

In this work we presented novel constraints on the FSI of
p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
and
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
systems obtained from measured correlation functions in pp collisions. The interacting potentials for these two pairs have been modeled with the novel iCATS package, an extension to the CATS framework, widely used so far in correlation data analysis. The iCATS is able to solve the Schrödinger equation with complex optical potentials and evaluate the theoretical correlation function for any emitting source profile.

The iCATS functionalities deliver the exact wavefunction down to zero interparticle distances, hence allowing to access in a more precise manner the annihilation part of the underlying interaction for the
\rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}}
case at hand.

The results presented here shows a clear difference in the short-range annihilation dynamics between the
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
and
p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
systems which might arise from a different strangeness content, leading to different absorptions channels. The FSI interaction for
p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
and
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
obtained with iCATS was compared to available production cross-sections and invariant mass spectra measured in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions. An overall compatibility between the extracted scattering amplitude from correlation data and these other observables is achieved, opening the possibility to exploit femtoscopy data to constrain the challenging
\rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}}
interaction. Indication of a possible
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
bound state, even if based on qualitative estimates on the extracted spin-average scattering parameters, has been observed. However, as already concluded in other investigations, the threshold enhancements in the considered cross-sections and spectra can be reproduced by the iCATS amplitudes without advocating for any additional resonance.

Future combined analysis involving
p\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
and
\Lambda\mbox{--}\overline{\Lambda}
correlation measurements and observables dominated by specific spin and partial waves could allow a more precise determination of the spin-dependence of the annihilation dynamics. An improved knowledge on the interplay between the elastic and annihilation part of the
\rm{B}\mbox{--}\rm{\overline{B}}
interaction can have significant impact on searches for exotic baryonium states
Klempt et al. 2002
;
Klempt et al. 2005
, on the determination of electromagnetic hyperon form factors
Dai et al. 2025
and on neutron skin measurements
Schupp et al. 2024
;
Aumann et al. 2022
.

Thanks to the possibility of producing in the same amount large yields of multi-strange baryons and antibaryons at LHC energies
Adam et al. 2017
, along with the high-statistics already achieved in correlation involving
\Xi(\bar{\Xi})
and
\Omega(\bar{\Omega})
particles, femtoscopy in the next years will be able to tackle the measurements of many other
Y\bar{Y}
interactions.

Appendix A
iCATS solver and computation of the wave function

The iCATS package is implemented in the C++ core class of the CATS framework. In this section we would like to address some technical details on the software which can be useful for future users.
Additional informations on the use of the iCATS tool, can be found in the git-hub repository here:
https://github.com/dimihayl/DLM/tree/devImCats
.

First of all, important to mention is the way that iCATS handles internally the complex potential. As shown in Ref.
Kamiya et al. 2022
, when obtaining the solution of the Schrödinger equation equation with a complex potential in a femtoscopy setup, the wave function must satisfy the asymptotic boundary condition where the outgoing wave is normalized to unity. To achieve this requirement, the strong complex potential
V_{l}(r)=V_{R,l}(r)+iV_{I,l}(r)
written in terms of its real and imaginary component and for a specific partial wave
l
enters in the radial equation with its hermite conjugate
V_{l}(r)^{\dagger}
.

The iCATS iterative numerical procedure to solve the coupled system in Eq.
4
is based on a
finite difference method
(FDM) and a three grid points in
r
(
[i-1,i,i+1]
).
To simplify the notation, we will label in the appendix:

\displaystyle u_{R}(r)=f(r),\,\,u_{I}(r)=g(r)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}=2mV_{R}+\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}-k^{2}

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}=2mV_{I}

(12)

Within this notation, the system of equations to be solved reads:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=\mathcal{F}_{1}f+\mathcal{F}_{2}g\\
g^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=\mathcal{F}_{1}g-\mathcal{F}_{2}f.\\
\end{cases}

(13)

The
\mathcal{F}_{1,2}
quantities are evaluated at each grid point.
The basic idea is to determine the values of
f
and
g
at each subsequent point, knowing their values in the previous two r-grid points. An initial value for both
f
and
g
at points
i=0
and
i=1
is assumed (given by the free spherical Bessel), then the evaluation proceeds to estimate the two functions at
i+1
until convergence is reached.

In order to solve Eqns.
13
we adopt, similarly to CATS, a Euler discretization with an adaptive grid. The discretized version reads

\displaystyle f_{i+1}=f_{i}\left(1+\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\Delta_{i-1}}\right)-f_{i-1}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\Delta_{i-1}}+\Delta_{i}^{2}(f_{i}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{1,i}+g_{i}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2,i})

(14)

\displaystyle g_{i+1}=g_{i}\left(1+\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\Delta_{i-1}}\right)-g_{i-1}\dfrac{\Delta_{i}}{\Delta_{i-1}}+\Delta_{i}^{2}(g_{i}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{1,i}-f_{i}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2,i}).

(15)

where
\Delta_{i}=r_{i+1}-r_{i}
. Additionally, the current iCATS implementation for the asymptotic condition allows to easily extend the calculations to include possible two- and three-coupled channels interactions.

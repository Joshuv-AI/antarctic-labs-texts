Simplicial volume via foliated simplices and duality
Filippo Sarti

Abstract
. Let M M be an aspherical oriented closed connected manifold with universal cover M ~ → M \widetilde{M}\to M and let Γ = π 1 ​ ( M ) ↷ ( X , μ ) \Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu) be a measure preserving action on a standard Borel probability space. We consider singular foliated simplices on the measured foliation Γ \ ( M ~ × X ) \Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X) defined by Sauer and we compare the real singular foliated homology with classic singular homology. We introduce a notion of foliated fundamental class and we prove that its norm coincides with the simplicial volume of M M . Then we consider the dual cochain complex and define the singular foliated bounded cohomology , proving that it is isometrically isomorphic to the measurable bounded cohomology of the action Γ ↷ X \Gamma\curvearrowright X . As a consequence of the duality principle we deduce a vanishing criteria for the simplicial volume in terms of the vanishing of the bounded cohomology of p.m.p actions and of their transverse groupoids.



1.
Introduction

The role of dynamics in the interaction between the geometry of manifolds and the algebra of their fundamental group was probably first understood by Gromov and dates back to the 80’s. This breakthrough intuition gave birth to still actual research fields going by the name of
geometric group theory
and
measured group theory
. In this paper we follow this flow, being particularly interested in the interplay between a topological invariant called
simplicial volume
and measure preserving actions of groups on standard Borel probability spaces.
In his book “
Metric structures for Riemannian and non-Riemannian spaces
”
[
Gro99
]
Gromov outlined a strategy to study simplicial volume via foliations, hinting a possible definition of simplicial volume for a
measured foliation with transverse measure
(see also
[
Gro91
, Section 2.4.B]
).
The rough idea, that is attributed to Connes
[
Gro99
, pg. 303]
, is first to take a
measure-theoretic
version of singular simplices on the foliation with nice transversality and finiteness properties. Then, one should be able to compare the homology of foliated simplices with the classic singular homology of the leaves and to define a notion of
fundamental class
for the foliation. The norm of the latter would be the simplicial volume of the given foliation.
For example, given an aspherical
n
-manifold
M
with fundamental group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
and universal cover
\widetilde{M}\to M
together with a measure preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space, one can consider the measured foliation
\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)
.
Here, each leaf is a copy of
\widetilde{M}
.
In this case Gromov claims that the norm of the foliated fundamental class, that we temporarily denote by
[\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)]
, coincides with the
\ell^{1}
-norm of the real fundamental class of
M
, that is the simplicial volume
||M||
. He writes that
“…by Connes theorem, we have
|[\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)]|=||M||
for all
X
”

[
Gro99
, pg. 303]
. Unfortunately, neither a precise definition of measurable simplex and of fundamental class nor a (reference for a) proof of the above statement is provided. One of the purposes of this paper is exactly to formalize the ideas sketched above and to give both precise definitions and rigorous proofs. Concretely, we first aim to prove the following result, that should correspond to what Gromov called
“Connes theorem”
in
[
Gro99
, pg. 303]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. Then

||M||=|[\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)]|\,.

Before sketching the proof, we need to clarify all the terminology appearing in the statement. We start with a brief description of classic notions such as
simplicial volume
and
bounded cohomology
.

Simplicial volume.

The
simplicial volume
is a topological invariant of manifolds intimately connected with the geometry that they can carry and with the algebraic-dynamical properties of their fundamental group.
First introduced by Gromov and used in the beautiful geometric proof of Mostow Rigidity
[
Thu79
]
, its study became soon an independent research field thanks to the connection with geometric group theory.
Given an oriented closed connected manifold
M
, its simplicial volume
||M||
is defined by taking the infimum among the
\ell^{1}
-norm
of all representatives of the fundamental class (Definition
3.4
). Despite the simple definition, a direct computation is generally impossible. Indeed, the exact value of such invariant is known only for few classes of manifolds and most of the significant results rely on geometric or algebraic arguments. This is the case of hyperbolic manifolds, for which it holds a proportionality with the Riemannian volume
[
Löh06
]
, or of manifolds with
amenable
fundamental group, whose simplicial volume vanishes
[
Fri17
, Corollary 7.12]
.

Bounded cohomology.

Amenability of groups is an algebraic-dynamical property of groups that can be partially detected considering the dual theory of simplicial volume, namely
bounded cohomology
. The latter was introduced by Johnson in the ’70s
[
Joh72
]
and is the cohomology obtained by taking singular cochains that are bounded with respect to the norm induced by the
\ell^{1}
-norm on the dual.
Its connection with geometry and in particular with simplicial volume was first discovered by Gromov. In particular, thanks to the
duality principle

[
Fri17
, Proposition 7.10]
, the simplicial volume of a manifold
M
vanishes if and only if the comparison map
\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\textup{H}^{n}(M,\mathbb{R})
is zero. Furthermore, as in the unbounded case, the bounded cohomology of
M
coincides with the one of its fundamental group
[
Fri17
, Theorem 5.9]
.
As a particular consequence, since the bounded cohomology of amenable groups is zero in all positive degrees, the simplicial volume of a manifold vanishes as soon as its fundamental group is amenable.
It becomes clear that studying simplicial volume is very often related to study bounded cohomology.
Unfortunately, explicit computations are difficult to obtain also in bounded cohomology.
However, the relation between these invariants showcases how the simplicial volume of a manifold is influenced by the algebraic and the dynamical properties of its fundamental group.
This fact motivated several authors to approach simplicial volume from a dynamical point of view, for instance by studying a measure-theoretic variant called
integral foliated simplicial volume

[
Gro99
,
LP16
,
FLPS16
]
, or by investigating the behaviour with respect to equivalence relations arising in ergodic theory, for example
measure equivalence

[
BFS13
]
.

Measurable bounded cohomology.

Motivated by role of bounded cohomology in dynamics and by previous works about measurable cocycles and their rigidity, the author and Savini recently introduced a new notion of bounded cohomology for
measured groupoids

[
SS23
]
. For example, given a measure preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
, the
measurable bounded cohomology
of the action groupoid
\Gamma\curvearrowright X

is obtained from the complex of
\Gamma
-invariant essentially bounded measurable functions on the product
\Gamma^{\bullet+1}\times X
. The definition comes as a natural straightening of Monod’ work about continuous bounded cohomology for topological groups and in fact the theories are connected by the following
exponential isomorphism

[
SS23
, Theorem 1]

\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{k}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))\,

that holds for every
k\geq 0
.

Foliated singular homology.

Going back to foliations and to the statement of Theorem
1
, we need to fix a suitable definition of
simplices
.
The notion of singular foliated simplices was considered and developed by Sauer as a tool to study the relation between
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers and the (minimal) volume of aspherical manifolds
[
Sau09
]
.
More precisely Sauer exhibited a precise definition of
singular foliated simplices
for generic
\mathcal{R}
-simplicial complexes
, a dynamical version of simplicial complexes introduced by Gaboriau
[
Gab02
]
. For the purpose of this paper, we avoid such general framework and we focus directly on the measured foliation
\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)
introduced above. Roughly speaking, in this setting a
singular foliated
k
-simplex
is a countable-to-one measurable map from a subset
A
of the reals of finite Lebesgue measure to the quotient
\Gamma\backslash(\textup{Sing}_{k}(\widetilde{M})\times X)
such that: (i) the induced
natural measure
coincides with the Lebesgue measure of
A
; (ii) the chain defined on each leaf is locally finite (Definition
4.3
). We warn the reader that the precise meaning of words like “measurable” and “natural measure” requires a careful and systematic study that we postpone to Section
4
.
With such simplices one can build (integral or real) chains and, after discarding “null chains” in order to avoid non-zero objects with zero norm, define the
singular foliated homology
of the foliation
\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)
.
For our scopes, in this paper we replace the integral coefficients used by Sauer
[
Sau09
]
with real coefficients, thus we consider the
real singular foliated homology

\textup{H}_{k}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})
(Definition
4.4
).
Furthermore, instead of taking the
mass
of simplices (which takes into account the measure of the support of their image), we consider the
weight
, namely the support of their domain (Definition
4.3
). This turns the chain complex of singular foliated chains into a normed chain complex.

In order to recover the usual notion of simplicial volume, the following task is to find a suitable notion of
fundamental class
in this setting.
The idea of Sauer
[
Sau09
, Lemma 3.23]
was to construct a map

\textup{H}_{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z}))\to\textup{H}_{n}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X))

and to take the image under such map of the
X
-fundamental class

[M]_{\mathbb{Z}}^{X}\in\textup{H}_{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z}))
(Definition
3.3
). However, when the coefficients are reals, his construction does not adapt straightforwardly and needs a slight refinement.
More precisely, we replace the space
\textup{C}_{n}(\widetilde{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})
with it “realification”

\textup{C}_{n}(\widetilde{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathbb{R}
. The latter carries a natural injection of both real singular chains and integral parametrized chains, so that the following diagram made by inclusions commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{C}_{n}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{C}_{n}(\widetilde{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{C}_{n}(M,\mathbb{R})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{C}_{n}(\widetilde{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathbb{R}\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}

The
real
X
-fundamental class

[M]^{X}_{\mathbb{R}}\in\textup{H}_{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathbb{R})
is the image of the real fundamental class under the map induced in homology by the bottom horizontal map (or, equivalently, of the
X
-fundamental class under the map induced in homology by the right vertical map).
We are now able to adapt Sauer’s construction, namely to build a map (Lemma
4.5
)

\Lambda_{n}:\textup{H}_{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathbb{R})\to\textup{H}_{n}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})\,.

The
X
-foliated fundamental class

[\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)]
is the image of the real
X
-fundamental class under
\Lambda_{n}
(Definition
4.6
).

Once all the ingredients are in place we can move to the proof of Theorem
1
, which requires two steps. The first one is to show that the norm of the real
X
-fundamental class coincides with the simplicial volume of
M
, that is the norm of its real fundamental class. This is an easy computation (Lemma
3.5
).
The second step is more challenging and consists to show that
\Lambda_{n}
is isometric. Here we use arguments due to Sauer to approximate foliated chains with chains in
\textup{C}_{n}(\widetilde{M})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathbb{R}
(Lemma
4.5
).

Singular foliated bounded cohomology.

In the second part of the paper we reverse our perspective. Motivated by the duality between simplicial volume and bounded cohomology, we consider the topological dual of singular foliated chains

(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R}),\delta^{\bullet})\coloneqq(\textup{C}_{\bullet}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R}),\partial_{\bullet})^{\prime}\,.

Definition 2
.

The
singular foliated bounded cohomology
of
M
is

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\textup{H}^{k}(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R}),\delta^{\bullet})\,.

Our last goal is to relate the above cohomology with the ordinary bounded cohomology of
M
(or, equivalently, of its fundamental group).
To this end we need to introduce a non-equivariant version of singular foliated simplices (Definition
5.1
) and to show that the associated cochain complex satisfies the hypothesis of the Fundamental Lemma of Homological Algebra for bounded cohomology (Lemma
2.5
). This approach presents a technical difficulty that we overcome by passing to the
completion
of the involved normed chain complexes, in order to admit infinite sums as representatives for chains (refer to Section
5.2
for all the details).
Then, we show that each new module is a
relatively injective

\Gamma
-module. Moreover, we add to the complex an augmentation map
\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})\to\text{C}_{0}(\widetilde{M}\times X)^{\prime}
, turning it into a
strong
resolution of the
\Gamma
-module
\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
As a consequence we deduce the following

Theorem 3
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. Then we have canonical isometric isomorphisms

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{k}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})

for every
k\geq 0
.

As an application of Theorem
3
we obtain a different proof of the following criteria for the vanishing of simplicial volume. This was implicitly contained in the work by Bader, Furman and Sauer
[
BFS13
]
as a consequence of duality with (non-trivial) coefficients
[
Mon01
, Proposition 2.3.1]
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. If
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})
(equivalently
\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(\Gamma,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))
) vanishes, then also
||M||
does.

We conclude the paper with an application to
transverse measured groupoids
, which combines Corollary
4
with the theory of bounded cohomology of groupoids due to the author and Savini
[
SS23
]
and recent results by Hartnick and the author about cohomological induction
[
HS
]
.
Given a p.m.p action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
, a
cross-section
is a measurable subset
Y\subset X
meeting any orbit (Definition
5.5
). The associated
transverse measured groupoid
is the restriction
\Gamma\curvearrowright X|_{Y}
, equipped with the measure structure described for instance by Björklund, Hartnick and Karasik in
[
BHK21
]
. Thanks to the isomorphism
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X|_{Y},\mathbb{R})\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})
proved by Hartnick and the author
[
HS
]
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 5
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. Let
Y\subset X
be any cross-section. If
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X|_{Y},\mathbb{R})
vanishes, then also
||M||
does.

On one hand, Corollary
4
and Corollary
5
provide a new way to produce examples of manifolds with vanishing simplicial volume. One the other one, the lack of concrete computations in bounded cohomology damps the enthusiasm about their effective impact and rises the following

Question 6
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold whose fundamental group
\Gamma
contains an infinite amenable normal subgroup. Is there any p.m.p action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
such that
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})=0
?
Is there any such action admitting a transverse measured groupoid with vanishing bounded cohomology, at least in degree
n
?

We point out that a positive answer to the above question would provide new examples of manifolds satisfying a question posed by Lück
[
Lüc02
, Question 14.39]
.

Structure of the paper.

The paper is divided in four sections. In Section
2
we recall the basics about bounded cohomology (Section
2.1
) and of homological algebra in this setting (Section
2.2
). In Section
3
we focus on the notion(s) of fundamental class(es) and how they are related to simplicial volume. Then we move to Section
4
, where we introduce singular foliated homology and we give the proof of Theorem
1
. Finally, in Section
5
, we consider the singular foliated bounded cohomology, we prove Theorem
3
and its corollaries.

2.1.
Bounded cohomology of groups, spaces and actions.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete countable group and consider a normed
\Gamma
-module, that is a normed vector space
V
with an isometric
\Gamma
-action.
We set

\textup{C}^{k}(\Gamma,V)\coloneqq\{f:\Gamma^{k+1}\to V\}\,

and define

\delta^{k}:\textup{C}^{k}(\Gamma,V)\to\textup{C}^{k+1}(\Gamma,V)

as

\delta^{k}f(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k+1})\coloneqq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{i}f(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\gamma_{i}},\ldots,\gamma_{k+1})\,.

It is easy to check that
\delta^{k+1}\circ\delta^{k}=0
and
||\delta^{k}||\leq k+1
where
||\cdot||
is the operator norm.
Thus the sets

\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,V)\coloneqq\left\{f\in\textup{C}^{k}(\Gamma,V)\,|\,\sup_{\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k}}||f(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k})||<+\infty\right\}\,.

form a cochain complex
(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\Gamma,V),\delta^{\bullet})
.
Moreover, each
\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,V)
is a normed
\Gamma
-module with the action

(\gamma f)(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k})\coloneqq\gamma\cdot f(\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{k})

and the supremum norm
||\cdot||_{\infty}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
bounded cohomology
of
\Gamma
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the subcomplex of
\Gamma
-invariants
(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\Gamma,V)^{\Gamma},\delta^{\bullet})
,
namely

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,V)\coloneqq\textup{H}^{k}(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\Gamma,V)^{\Gamma},\delta^{\bullet})\,.

The norm
||\cdot||_{\infty}
on
\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,V)
descends to a
semi-norm
on
\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,V)
, denoted again as
||\cdot||_{\infty}
.

Let
Y
be a topological space with fundamental group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(Y)
and
(\textup{C}^{\bullet}(Y,V),\delta^{\bullet})
be its complex of singular cochains with coefficients in
V
.
Define

\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)\coloneqq\left\{f\in\textup{C}^{k}(Y,V)\,|\,\sup_{s\in\textup{Sing}_{k}(Y)}||f(s)||<+\infty\right\}\,,

where
\textup{Sing}_{k}(Y)
is the set of singular
k
-simplices of
Y
. Then
||\delta^{n}||\leq k+1
, whence
\delta^{k}
restricts to bounded cochains.

Definition 2.2
.

The
bounded cohomology
of
Y
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the complex

(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(Y,V),\delta^{\bullet})
,
namely

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)\coloneqq\textup{H}^{k}(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(Y,V),\delta^{\bullet})\,.

Each
\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)
is endowed with the operator norm, and the latter descends to a semi-norm on

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)
.

Assume now that
Y
is also path connected and admits a universal cover
p:\widetilde{Y}\to Y
.
As above, we consider the cochain complex

(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\widetilde{Y},V),\delta^{\bullet})
of singular cochains in
\widetilde{Y}
, which is endowed with the
\Gamma
-action inherited from the deck transformations of
p:\widetilde{Y}\to Y
. Then we have a natural isometric identification

\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)\cong\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\widetilde{Y},V)^{\Gamma}

in every degree
that descends in homology to a canonical isometric isomorphism

(1)

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)\cong\textup{H}^{k}(\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\widetilde{Y},V)^{\Gamma},\delta^{\bullet})\,.

Such obvious identification is the base step to connect the bounded cohomology of
Y
with the one of its fundamental group.

Theorem 2.3

(Gromov, Ivanov)
.

Let
Y
be a path connected topological space with fundamental group
\Gamma
and universal cover
p:\widetilde{Y}\to Y
. Then we have isometric isomorphisms

(2)

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,\mathbb{R})

for every
k\geq 0
.

If
Y
is moreover aspherical, then the above isometric isomorphism holds for every Banach
\Gamma
-module
V
, namely

(3)

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,V)\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(Y,V)

for every
k\geq 0
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
be a measure preserving action on a standard Borel probability space. Consider a Banach
\Gamma
-module
V
which is the dual of a separable Banach
\Gamma
-module
W
.
Consider the set

\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{k+1}\times X,V)

of
V
-valued weak
∗
-measurable essentially bounded functions on
\Gamma^{k+1}\times X

endowed with the supremum norm and with the
\Gamma
-action

(\gamma f)(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k},x)\coloneqq\gamma\cdot f(\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{k},\gamma^{-1}x)\,.

We point out that, in general, weak
∗
-measurability does not coincide with measurability with respect to the topology induced by the norm on
V
.
The coboundary operator defined above extend to

\delta^{n}:\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{k+1}\times X,V)\to\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{k+2}\times X,V)

as

\delta^{k}f(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k+1},x)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i}f(\gamma_{0},\ldots,\widehat{\gamma_{i}},\ldots,\gamma_{k+1},x)\,.

Definition 2.4
.

The
measurable bounded cohomology
of the p.m.p action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the complex

(\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\bullet+1}\times X,V)^{\Gamma},\delta^{\bullet})
,
namely

\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{k}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,V)\coloneqq\textup{H}^{k}(\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\bullet+1}\times X,V)^{\Gamma},\delta^{\bullet})\,.

As a consequence of the exponential isomorphism
[
Mon01
, Corollary 2.3.3]

\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{n+1}\times X,V)\cong\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{+1},\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(X,V))

we get a canonical isometric isomorphism

(4)

\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{k}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,V)\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma,\textup{L}_{\textup{w}^{*}}^{\infty}(X,V))

for every
k\geq 0

[
SS23
, Theorem 1]
.

2.2.
Homological algebra.

Since we work in the category of normed
\Gamma
-modules, all the maps will be assumed to be
\mathbb{R}
-linear
and
bounded
.

A map
\iota:E\to W
between normed
\Gamma
-modules is
strongly injective
if there
is a map
\varphi:W\to E
with
||\varphi||\leq 1
such that
\varphi\circ\iota=\textup{Id}_{E}
(in particular,
\iota
is
injective).

A normed
\Gamma
-module
V
is
relatively injective

if
the following holds: whenever
E,W
are normed
\Gamma
-modules,
\iota:E\to W
is a strongly
injective
\Gamma
-map and
\psi:E\to V
is a
\Gamma
-map, there exists a
\Gamma
-map
\beta:W\to V
with
||\beta||\leq||\psi||

such that
\beta\circ\iota=\psi
, namely the following extension problem admits a solution
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}

We adopt all the notation of Setup

Theorem
1

Let

The structure of the proof follows
[

, Lemma 3.24]

\Lambda_{n}
is isometric.
Since it is norm non-increasing, we only need to show that for every cycle
c\in\textup{C}_{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\mathbb{R})
and every

5.1.

We consider the topological dual of
\textup{C}_{k}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})

Define the coboundary operator

Each

Definition

The

of

\Gamma

\Gamma

The idea comes from classic singular bounded cohomology, where one has the obvious but fundamental identification

In our case the new resolution is obtained introducing a non-equivariant version of foliated simplices and the associated chain complex.

\sigma:A\to\textup{Sing}(\widetilde{M})\times X
we define

\beta:W\rightarrow\textup{C}_{k}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}\,,\;\;\;\beta(w)(\sigma)\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\cdot\varphi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)^{-1}\cdot w))(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)\,,

where
\sigma_{F_{k}}:A\to\Gamma
is defined as
\sigma_{F_{k}}\coloneqq\pi_{F_{k}}\circ\sigma
.
We denote by
\beta(w)
also the extension to the completion
\textup{C}_{k}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})
.
To see that
\beta
is a solution of the extension problem, we first compute

\displaystyle\beta(\iota(w))(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\cdot\varphi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)^{-1}\cdot\iota(w)))(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\cdot\varphi\circ\iota(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)^{-1}\cdot w))(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)^{-1}\cdot w)(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(w)(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\mathcal{L}}(A)}{|\sigma|}\psi(w)(\sigma)=\psi(w)(\sigma)\,,

where we to pass from the first to the second line we exploited the
\Gamma
-invariance of
\iota
, then we used the relation
\varphi\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}
and we concluded since, by definition,
|\sigma|=m_{\mathcal{L}}(A)
.

Secondly, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle\beta(\gamma\cdot w)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\cdot\varphi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)^{-1}\cdot(\gamma\cdot w)))(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\cdot\varphi((\gamma^{-1}\sigma_{F_{k}}(t))^{-1}\cdot w))(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(\gamma^{-1}\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)\cdot\varphi((\gamma^{-1}\sigma_{F_{k}}(t))^{-1}\cdot w))(\gamma\cdot\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=\beta(w)(\gamma\cdot\sigma)\,.

In the above computation we first applied the
\Gamma
-equivariance of
\psi
and then the fact that
(\gamma\cdot\sigma)_{F_{k}}(t)=\gamma^{-1}\cdot\sigma_{F_{k}}(t)
.

Finally, the inequality
||\beta||_{\infty}\leq||\psi||_{\infty}
follows since
\varphi
is norm non-increasing and because the operator

S_{k}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}\,,\;\;\;\sigma\mapsto\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{A}\psi(v)(\sigma)dm_{\mathcal{L}}(t)

has norm at most
||\psi||_{\infty}
.
This concludes the proof about the relative injectivity.

We move on our proof by showing that the complex
(\textup{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime},\delta^{\bullet})
is strong. In fact, a contracting homotopy can be constructed as follows.
Fix a point
\widetilde{y}_{0}\in\widetilde{M}
.
We denote by
T_{k}:\textup{Sing}_{k}(\widetilde{M})\to\textup{Sing}_{k+1}(\widetilde{M})
the map described in
[
Fri17
, Lemma 5.4]
having the following property: for every
s\in\textup{Sing}_{k}(\widetilde{M})
, the
0
-th
vertex of
T_{k}(s)
is equal to
\widetilde{y}_{0}
, and has
s
as opposite face.
We set
T_{k}^{X}:T_{k}:\textup{Sing}_{k}(\widetilde{M})\times X\to\textup{Sing}_{k+1}(\widetilde{M})\times X
as
T_{k}^{X}=T_{k}^{X}(s,x)=(T_{k}(s),x)
.
For every
k\geq 1
and any simplex
\sigma:A\to\textup{Sing}_{k}(\widetilde{M})\times X
we define

\mathcal{T}_{k}(\sigma):A\to\textup{Sing}_{k+1}(\widetilde{M})\times X\,,\;\;\;\mathcal{T}_{k}(\sigma)\coloneqq T_{k}^{X}\circ\sigma\,.

By duality we set

\theta^{k}:\textup{C}_{k}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}\to\textup{C}_{k-1}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}

as (the extension by density of) the functional

\theta^{k}(f)(\sigma)\coloneqq f(\mathcal{T}_{k}(\sigma))\,,

Moreover, we set

\theta^{0}:\textup{C}_{0}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}\to\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})

as

\theta^{0}(f)(x)\coloneqq f(\sigma_{\widetilde{y}_{0}})\,,

where
\sigma_{\widetilde{y}_{0}}:X\to\widetilde{M}\times X
is defined as

\sigma_{\widetilde{y}_{0}}(x)=(\widetilde{y}_{0},x)
.
A direct application of the definition of both
\delta^{\bullet}
and
\theta^{\bullet}
implies that the following equalities

\theta^{k+1}\circ\delta^{k}+\delta^{k-1}\circ\theta^{k}=\mathrm{id}_{\textup{C}_{k}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}}\,,\;\;\;\theta^{1}\circ\delta^{0}+\epsilon\circ\theta^{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\textup{C}_{0}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}}

hold.
This shows that
\theta
is a contracting homotopy. In particular, thanks to the isometric isomorphism of Equation (
12
), we can conclude that there exist canonical isomorphisms

\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{k}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))\,

in every degrees.

As anticipated, in order to conclude we need to exhibit a norm non-increasing
\Gamma
-equivariant cochain map

\iota^{\bullet}:\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{\bullet}(\Gamma,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))\to\textup{C}_{\bullet}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})^{\prime}

extending the identity on
\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Let
F
be a fundamental domain for the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\widetilde{M}
.
We consider the map
r:\textup{Sing}_{0}(\widetilde{M})\cong\widetilde{M}\to\Gamma
such that
r(y)^{-1}y\in F_{k}
for every
y\in\widetilde{M}
.
Then we set

\iota^{n}(f)(\sigma)\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int\limits_{X}\sum_{s\in C}f(r(s_{0}),\ldots,r(s_{n}))(x)|\sigma^{-1}(s,x)|d\mu(x)\,

for every
f\in\textup{C}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(\Gamma,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))
and every
\sigma\in S_{n}(\widetilde{M}\times X,\mathbb{R})
with
\textup{Im}(\sigma)\subset C\times X
. Here
s_{i}
is the
i
-th vertex of
s
.
The fact that
\iota^{\bullet}
is a cochain map extending the identity is a straightforward verification.
To show the
\Gamma
-equivariance we pick any
\gamma\in\Gamma
and we compute

\displaystyle\iota^{n}(\gamma\cdot f)(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int\limits_{X}\sum_{s\in C}(\gamma\cdot f)(r(s_{0}),\ldots,r(s_{n}))(x)|\sigma^{-1}(s,x)|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int\limits_{X}\sum_{s\in C}f(\gamma^{-1}r(s_{0}),\ldots,\gamma^{-1}r(s_{n}))(\gamma^{-1}x)|\sigma^{-1}(s,x)|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\gamma\cdot\sigma|}\int\limits_{X}\sum_{s\in\gamma C}f(r(s_{0}),\ldots,r(s_{n}))(x)|(\gamma\sigma)^{-1}(s,x)|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\iota^{n}(f)(\gamma\cdot\sigma)=\gamma\cdot\iota^{n}(f)(\sigma)\,.

In the above computation we moved from the second line to the third one exploiting the
\Gamma
-invariance of both
\mu
and the norm on singular foliated chains.

Moreover, since by definition

\int\limits_{X}\sum_{s\in C}|\sigma^{-1}(s,x)|d\mu(x)=m_{\mathcal{L}}(A)=|\sigma|

we have that
||\iota^{n}(f)||\leq||f||
for every
f
, which implies that
\iota^{n}
does not increase the norm. This concludes the proof.
∎

Denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the Kronecker pairing between
\textup{H}_{n}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})
and
\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. Then

||M||=\max\{\langle[\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X)],\lambda\rangle|\,|\,\lambda\in\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(\Gamma\backslash(\widetilde{M}\times X),\mathbb{R})\,,\,||\lambda||_{\infty}\leq 1\}\,.

Proof.

This a consequence of Theorem
1
and of the duality principle
[
Fri17
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

In the setting of Corollary
5.3
, assume that the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is
amenable
in the sense of Zimmer
[
Zim84
, Definition 4.3.1]
. A special case of
[
SS23
, Theorem 4]
(see also
[
Mon01
, Proposition 7.4.1]
) shows that
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{k}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X)=0
for every
k\geq 1
.
In particular, by Corollary
5.3
we have
||M||=0
. One may wonder whether this criteria provides new examples of manifolds with vanishing simplicial volume. In fact, this is not the case, since by
[
Zim84
, Proposition 4.3.3]
any amenable p.m.p action is given by an amenable group. Whence we fall into the classic vanishing results for manifolds with amenable fundamental group. In other words, interesting examples satisfying the hypothesis of Corollary
5.3
should be investigated outside the amenable world.

Corollary
4
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. If
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})
(equivalently
\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(\Gamma,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{b}}^{n}(M,\text{L}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))
) vanishes, then also
||M||
does.

Proof.

This follows by Corollary
5.3
and by Theorem
3
.
∎

We conclude with a further application of Theorem
3
to
transverse measured groupoids
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
be a p.m.p action of a countable group on a standard Borel probability space.
A
cross-section
is a Borel subset
Y\subset X
such that
Y
meets every
\Gamma
-orbit.

We recall that such sections are known to exist even in the more general setting of measured groupoids (e.g.
[
FHM78
, Theorem 2.8]
or
[
Ram82
, Theorem 5.6]
).
It should be pointed out that, in general,
Y
need not to have positive measure in
X
. For example, for a transitive group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
where
\Gamma_{0}<\Gamma
is a closed subgroup a cross section is given by the singleton. For ths reason, it makes no sense to consider the restriction groupoid with the measure induced by
\Gamma\curvearrowright X

[
SS23
, Section 2]
. Neverthless, by classical results due to Connes and others, one can build a measure
\tau
on
Y
, called
transverse measure
, starting from the one on
X

[
BHK21
]
. Equipped with such measured structure, the restriction
\Gamma\curvearrowright X|_{Y}
(called
transverse measured groupoid
) carries some important information about the initial action. For instance, their measurable bounded cohomology coincide
[
HS
]
. We refer to
[
SS23
]
for the reader interested in the general theory of measurable bounded cohomology of measured groupoids. From the above considerations we deduce the following

Corollary
5
.

Let
M
be an oriented closed connected aspherical
n
-manifold. Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be its universal cover and consider a measure preserving action
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
on a standard Borel probability space. Let
Y\subset X
be any cross-section. If
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X|_{Y},\mathbb{R})
vanishes, then also
||M||
does.

Proof.

This follows by Corollary
4
and by the isomorphism
\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X,\mathbb{R})\cong\textup{H}_{\textup{mb}}^{n}(\Gamma\curvearrowright X|_{Y},\mathbb{R})

[
HS
]
.
∎

The above result is a potential source of new examples of manifolds with vanishing simplicial volume. Indeed, it would suffice to find a p.m.p action of the fundamental group admitting a transverse measured groupoid with vanishing bounded cohomology.
For instance, a (non-necessarily amenable) p.m.p action with a cross-section whose associated transverse measured groupoid is amenable would have this property
[
SS23
, Theorem 4]
.
However, we do not known any non-trivial example of this scenary. In fact, the lack of explicit computation in bounded cohomology is even more evident in the context of groupoids, where no sophisticated technique has been developed so far.

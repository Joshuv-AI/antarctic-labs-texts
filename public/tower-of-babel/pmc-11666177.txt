Bibliometric and visual analysis of medication therapy management from 2003 to 2023
Authors: Zhiye Guo, Lianhao Fu, Zhijun Chu, Shuojun Gao, Lin Lu, Wanli Jiao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Medication therapy management (MTM) includes various clinical pharmacy services that greatly influence society and economy. Despite extensive research in recent years, there is currently a lack of bibliometric analysis on this subject. Articles and reviews on MTM published from 2003 to 2023 were identified and selected from the Web of Science Core Collection. CiteSpace, VoSviewer, Bibliometrix, and Microsoft Excel were used to conduct bibliometric analysis and knowledge mapping. The bibliometric analysis encompassed 3255 publications on MTM. The United States and the United Kingdom emerged as the most influential countries in this particular domain, whereas Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Harvard Medical School were the most active institutions. Elizabeth Manias published the largest number of papers, whereas Jeffrey L. Schnipper published the largest number of co-citations. The American Pharmacists Association has been identified as the most prolific journal in this field. Optimizing medication and ensuring drug safety in elderly patients are current research frontiers. Topics such as “polypharmacy,” “dementia,” “oncology,” and “anticholinergic burden” were identified as potential research priorities in the future. This study is the first to utilize bibliometric analysis to uncover the global status, research hotspots, and future development trends of MTM for scholars, thereby providing valuable guidance for future research endeavors.

1. Introduction

Medication therapy management (MTM) is a strategic method for providing a range of clinical pharmacy services, excluding dispensing, to patients and their healthcare providers. Its primary objectives are to enhance patient medication utilization, optimize clinical outcomes and quality of life, and minimize the occurrence of unfavorable incidents and overall healthcare costs.[1]

In 2003, the United States Government introduced MTM as part of the Medicare Prescription Drug, Improvement, and Modernization Act. The legislation mandated that Medicare providers must offer MTM services to patients enrolled in Medicare Part D. These patients must meet 1 of 2 criteria: they had been diagnosed with multiple chronic diseases or they were concurrently prescribed multiple medications.[1,2] The definition of the MTM was established in 2004.[1] The core elements of MTM services were proposed in 2005 and updated in 2008.[3,4] In the same year, “MTM” was introduced as an MeSH term.[2] From 2003 to 2023, the MTM has been gradually recognized and widely studied. These studies primarily focused on service providers,[5] service recipients,[6] service models,[3,4] and service outcomes.[7,8] However, a comprehensive global analysis is still lacking. Hence, there is an urgent need for an appropriate approach to unveil the global situation, focus areas, and future trends of MTM.

Visual analysis improves the comprehension of data and facilitates information transfer by converting complex datasets into intuitive graphs and charts. Bakkouri et al demonstrated the efficacy and feasibility of the 2MGAS-Net framework for polyp segmentation in colonoscopy images through visual analysis, highlighting its potential impact on medical diagnosis and clinical decision making.[9] Bibliometric analysis technique is a visualization method introduced by Pritchard in 1969, which can reveal research trends and predict research hotspots within a specific field by examining the countries, institutions, authors, journals, keywords, and references relevant to research in this field.[10,11] Bibliometric analysis has been employed extensively in various research domains. Abdulwahid Mohammad Noor et al provided a comprehensive overview of visual quality, imaging modality innovation, and diagnostic performance in the field of breast imaging through a bibliometric analysis.[12] Wu et al used bibliometric methods to summarize a comprehensive knowledge map, development context and future development direction for osteonecrosis of the femoral head.[10]

However, to date, no study has employed this methodology to examine a worldwide survey of MTM. Therefore, the aim of this study was to comprehensively investigate the current status of MTM and identify emerging tendencies and frontier areas from 2003 to 2023. This will serve as a valuable resource for researchers, facilitating their comprehension of this field and promoting collaborative efforts.

2. Method

2.1. Data collection

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) (https://www.webofscience.com/wos/) is widely used for conducting bibliometric analyses due to its extensive range of data fields.[13] Therefore, in this study, we conducted a thorough online search of the WoSCC Database. In WoSCC, the abbreviation “TS” refers to a topic sentence. The search formula employed in this study was configured as TS=(“MTM” OR “medication reconciliation” OR “drug therapy management” OR “drug treatment management” OR “medication treatment management” OR “personal medication record” OR “medication therapy review” OR “medication-related action plan” OR “comprehensive medication review” OR “medication-related problems” OR “drug utilization management” OR “chronic care improvement” OR “drug therapy services” OR “prescriber intervention*” OR (“utilization management strategies” OR “utilization management strategy”) OR “optimized treatment outcomes” OR ((patient OR patients) AND “medication understanding”) OR (“drug therapy outcome” OR “drug therapy outcomes”) OR “medication counseling” OR “pharmaceutical case management” OR (“drug therapy problem” OR “drug therapy problems”) OR (“medicine management” OR “medicines management”)).

The included documents comprised articles or reviews published in English between 2003 and 2023. Figure 1 illustrates the retrieval process. The search was conducted by 2 researchers (ZG and LF), and any discrepancies were resolved through comprehensive discussion and, if deemed essential, with the involvement of a third investigator (ZC). The search process was concluded within a single day, December 22, 2023, to mitigate the potential data bias arising from database updates. The raw data utilized in this study were acquired from publicly accessible databases and were not applicable for ethical approval.

Data retrieval and analysis process.

2.2. Data analysis

The data analysis focused on 2 main aspects. First, we conducted an examination of the countries, institutions, authors, and journals included in the study to analyze the current state of research in MTM. Second, by analyzing the keywords and references, we identified research hotspots and future trends.

To perform a more thorough analysis, we utilized VOSviewer, CiteSpace, Bibliometrix and Microsoft Excel to conduct bibliometric analyses. Each software package has its own unique features. VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was employed to visually examine the distribution and collaboration of countries/regions, institutions, and authors, as well as the co-occurrence and temporal distribution of the keywords. The aim is to offer users with a comprehensive understanding of the dynamics and framework of the MTM research. CiteSpace (version 6.2.R7) was used to analyze countries/regions exhibiting strong citation bursts, perform a dual-map overlay of journals, create a timeline graph for keywords, examine references’ co-citation patterns, and identify references with significant citation bursts. By utilizing CiteSpace, potential information can be unveiled from the extensive literature. Bibliometrix (version 2023.9.1.0) is an online platform (https://www.bibliometrix.org).[14] It was used to identify the core journals based on Bradford law. Microsoft Excel (version 2019) was applied to visualize the publication and citation tendency of the studies over time. Figure 1 illustrates the analytical process.

3. Results

3.1. General information

The total number of publications included in this study was 3255, comprising 2792 articles and 463 reviews. Overall, the annual growth rate stood at 11.66%, the average number of citations per document amounted to 21.28, and the mean age of the documents was 7.62 years.

4. Discussion

4.1. Worldwide trends in MTM research from 2003 to 2023

Since 2003, the annual number of publications and citations has been on the rise, and the number and growth rate have reached a maximum in 2021, suggesting that MTM has garnered substantial scholarly attention. Investigations of MTM during the COVID-19 pandemic were extensive, and there was a decline in the number of related studies as the pandemic subsided, potentially contributing to the decrease in annual publications and citation frequency after 2021.

4.2. Knowledge structure of MTM-related publications

Analyses of countries/regions, institutions, and authors aim to promptly identify active and influential MTM contributors and promote cooperation. With the advancement of research, the MTM has transcended its previous confines within developed nations. Researchers in developing countries are increasingly recognizing the significance of MTM. China and Brazil are the most influential developing countries. China has been conducting MTM research since 2011, and its most cited article proved that implementing pharmacological interventions could significantly mitigate falls.[26] In addition, China has recently focused on MTM for surgical patients.[27] Brazil has been conducting MTM research since 2008, its recent studies include analyzing the effect of drug therapy management on hypertension patients,[28,29] exploring the effects of mHealth tools on hemoglobin A1c levels,[30] and optimizing antimicrobial use in human populations.[31]

After a comprehensive analysis, it was found that Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Harvard Medical School are the most influential organizations. Brigham and Women’s Hospital is the only medical institution among the top 20. Its most cited article was a systematic review of medication reconciliation interventions in a hospital setting published in 2012.[32] The Harvard Medical School has demonstrated exceptional productivity, particularly in recent years. Its most cited article revealed that medication reconciliation was considered to be a significant administrative burden by academic doctors.[33] The findings of this study further demonstrate that pharmacists are the best providers of medication reconciliation.

Schnipper, Manias, and Doucette are the most influential authors. Jeffrey L. Schnipper, Research Director at Brigham and Women’s Hospital. The focus of his current research lies in investigating the use of digital pill system technology in patients with heart failure.[34] Elizabeth Manias is a professor at the School of Nursing and Midwifery, at Monash University. His current research focuses on how to enhance patient involvement in medication communication during hospital discharge.[35] William R. Doucette is a pharmacy professor at the University of Iowa, Japan. His most recent study employed the online Delphi method. This study aimed to identify the crucial components and features of patient-record systems in community pharmacies.[36]

The analysis of journals and related fields provides MTM researchers with a thorough comprehension of publishing opportunities, interdisciplinary connections, and key research areas.[20] Current researches on MTM primarily appear in the areas of MEDICINE, MEDICAL, and CLINICAL. Their knowledge base primarily encompasses the fields of MOLECULAR, BIOLOGY, GENETICS, HEALTH, NURSING, and MEDICINE. Among them, the interdisciplinary research on genetics and medicine has significantly advanced the progress of personalized medicine and pharmacogenetics.[37]

4.3. Overview of hotspots and frontiers

4.3.1. Keywords analysis

The analysis of keywords is advantageous for comprehending the focuses and frontiers of a research domain.[38] Relevant research results show that enhancing medication utilization and safety among older adults is at the forefront. The keywords identified in this cluster were “polypharmacy,” “deprescribing,” “dementia,” and “multimorbidity.”

Polypharmacy is prevalent, particularly among elderly individuals, who may concurrently manage multiple chronic conditions.[39,40] Polypharmacy can result in elevated susceptibility to drug interactions, adverse reactions, medication nonadherence, and other complications.[41] Therefore, avoiding unnecessary polypharmacy is important. Regarding factors associated with polypharmacy, a study conducted by Ye et al revealed that a multitude of factors encompassing demography, nutrition, lifestyle, and healthcare utilization were intricately linked to polypharmacy.[42] To address polypharmacy, Hoel et al proposed a systematic and consultative approach to identify medications with high risks. They also advocated for individualized treatment and multidisciplinary team strategies.[43] In the same year, Kurczewska-Michalak et al analyzed the available literature on polypharmacy management and concluded that although the management of polypharmacy in elderly individuals lacked a gold standard, adopting a drug review was the most favorable option. Furthermore, they emphasized the importance of a patient-centered approach, mobile applications and computerized decision support systems.[44] Deprescribing, as a proactive, supervised, comprehensive, and patient-centered intervention, has emerged and gained popularity in recent years.[45]

The term “dementia” is another prominent keyword. The frequency of potentially inappropriate prescriptions in individuals with dementia is significantly high, necessitating targeted interventions to reduce the need for improper drug utilization.[46] Recent clinical studies have demonstrated that the assessment and participation of caregivers,[47] personalized medication management,[37] and medication reconciliation[48] are effective strategies for the management of pharmacotherapy in people with dementia.

Notably, certain keywords within the red cluster also exhibited a relatively recent APY, such as “covid-19,” “oncology,” “clinical pharmacology,” and “qualitative research.” During the COVID-19 outbreak, MTM has been extensively investigated through various approaches, including the exploration of management strategies, consolidation of practical experience, and the assessment of effectiveness.[49–53] The integration of digital technology with pharmaceutical services has led to advancements in telepharmacy research.[49,50,54] Perrone et al analyzed the utilization of telemedicine across various medical domains during the COVID-19 pandemic. Regarding telepharmacy, they highlighted that internet-based therapeutic prescribing can streamline the entire process and enhance the efficiency of medication preparation and delivery, ultimately contributing to enhanced patient care.[55,56] The most prevalent telepharmacy service programs implemented during the pandemic included virtual consultations, home delivery of medicines, and patient education.[55,57] Unni et al also discussed the challenges, strategies, and innovative approaches in implementing these services to provide guidance for future endeavors.[57] In the field of oncology, studies have mainly focused on collaborative drug therapy management,[58] medication reconciliation,[59] telepharmacy and telehealth,[60,61] personalized medical treatment,[62] and multidisciplinary care.[63] It is important to note that cancer patients are a key population for adverse event management.[64] Collaborative drug therapy management, medication reconciliation, personalized medical treatment, and patient education are all effective measures for managing adverse events, especially patient education. Myers et al demonstrated that pharmacist-led education, proactive follow-up, and management of immune checkpoint inhibitor-related adverse events can improve clinical outcomes in patients.[65]

Correspondingly, the timeline graph reveals that #0 (community pharmacy) is the predominant cluster, indicating a higher level of activity. The most recent keyword within this cluster is “anticholinergic burden.” Recent studies have investigated the impact of medication reconciliation and review on anticholinergic burden scores in dementia,[48] as well as developing an updated scale for anticholinergic burden to more effectively ascertain the association between anticholinergic drugs and cognitive impairment.[66]

4.3.2. References analysis

The analysis of references in our study encompasses 3 main aspects: examination of the top 10 co-cited references, exploration of co-citation clusters, and investigation of reference bursts. The top 10 co-cited references specifically focused on analyzing medication discrepancies and practices related to medication reconciliation in hospitalized patients. These studies provide a foundation for research on MTM.

The co-citation cluster analysis reveals that the focus of co-cited topics in the literature has shifted from exploring modes of implementation to optimizing methods of implementation and evaluating outcomes of implementation, as well as from the general population to specific populations, between 2003 and 2023.

CiteSpace detected 25 references that exhibited notable bursts in citations. Of these, ten citation outbreaks after 2019 are ongoing. These references provide a comprehensive analysis of various facets of polypharmacy,[25,67] older adults,[24,68] transitions of care,[69,70] drug-related hospital readmissions,[71,72] informatics,[73] and qualitative research.[74] These topics are currently prominent in MTM research and are aligned with the aforementioned keywords analysis.

5. Strengths and limitations

In contrast to traditional reviews, this study is the first to provide a systematic bibliometric analysis of research on MTM, offering a more thorough understanding of hotspots and frontiers. Additionally, this study employed 3 bibliometric tools simultaneously to ensure the objectivity and visibility of the data, offering comprehensive guidance to researchers engaged in relevant research. However, there are several limitations to this study. First, the data utilized in this study were exclusively collected from the WoSCC. Although WoSCC encompasses the majority of publications and provides extensive information required by bibliometric software, some publications may have been excluded from this analysis. Second, this study exclusively included articles or reviews written in English, which could have introduced bias into the results. Finally, assessment and treatment of publication quality were not conducted in this study, potentially compromising the credibility of the analysis.

6. Conclusion

The field of MTM research holds significant potential for practical applications and has garnered growing global recognition. This study presented an inaugural bibliometric and visual analysis of MTM literature spanning 2003 to 2023, identifying the countries, institutions, authors, and journals that have made significant contributions in this area and revealing research hotspots and future trends.

Currently, research on MTM primarily focuses on the optimization and evaluation of interventions. The focused study population included older patients, patients undergoing polypharmacy, patients affected by dementia or cancer, and patients diagnosed with diabetes. The interventions predominantly concentrated on medication review, medication reconciliation, deprescribing, individualized drug therapy, interdisciplinary collaboration, the engagement of patients and their family caregivers, digital technology integration, and telepharmacy services. The timing of interventions mainly targeted the transition period of care. The aforementioned topics exhibit significant potential for future investigations and therefore warrant considerable attention.

In conclusion, this bibliometric and visual analysis provides an impartial and comprehensive perspective on MTM, allowing readers to quickly gain an understanding of the global situation, focal points, and future trends in MTM from a large body of literature. Furthermore, this study will assist researchers in making informed scientific decisions regarding the selection of collaborators and research directions.

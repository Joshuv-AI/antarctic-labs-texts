Some generalizations of Camina Pairs and orders of elements in cosets
Thu T.H. Quan, Hung P. Tong-Viet

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate certain generalizations of Camina pairs. Let H H be a nontrivial proper subgroup of a finite group G G . We first show that every nontrivial irreducible complex character of H H induces homogeneously to G G if and only if for every x ∈ G ∖ H x\in G\setminus H , the element x x is conjugate to x ​ h xh for all h ∈ H h\in H . Furthermore we prove that if x ​ h xh is conjugate to either x x or x − 1 x^{-1} for all h ∈ H h\in H and all x ∈ G ∖ H x\in G\setminus H , then the normal closure N N of H H in G G also satisfies the same condition, and N N is nilpotent. Finally, we determine the structure of H H under the assumption that for every element x ∈ G ∖ H x\in G\setminus H of odd order, the coset x ​ H xH consists entirely of elements of odd order.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
G
. The pair
(G,H)
is called a
Camina pair
if, for every element
g\in G\setminus H
, the element
g
is conjugate to every element of the coset
gH
. The study of Camina pairs has a long history, beginning with Camina’s work in
[
C
]
, where he introduced certain conditions that nearly characterize Frobenius groups. In particular, Camina showed in
[
C
, Theorem 2]
that if
(G,H)
is a Camina pair, then one of the following holds:
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
H
; or
H
is a
p
-group; or
G/H
is a
p
-group for some prime
p
. We refer the reader to the recent survey by Lewis
[
L
]
for a comprehensive discussion of various equivalent formulations of Camina pairs and their generalizations.

For a finite group
G
, we denote by
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
. Using character theory of finite groups, an equivalent formulation of the Camina pair was established by Chillag and Macdonald in
[
CM
, Propsition 3.1]
. They show that if
H
is not contained in the kernel of an irreducible character
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, then
\chi
vanishes on
G\setminus H
. Another equivalent condition was also provided by Kuisch and van der Waall in
[
KV
]
. Recall that a character
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)
is said to induce
homogeneously
to
G
if
\theta^{G}=a\xi
for some
\xi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
and a positive integer
a
, where
\theta^{G}
denotes the character of
G
induced from
\theta
. Kuisch and van der Waall proved in
[
KV
, Lemma 2.1]
that
(G,H)
is a Camina pair if and only if every nontrivial irreducible character of
H
induces homogeneously to
G
.
In fact, they considered a more general assumption where
H
is not required to be a normal subgroup of
G
. They referred to this broader condition as
\mathrm{(CI)}
, defined as follows:

\mathrm{(CI)}
:
1<H<G
, and every nontrivial irreducible character of
H
induces homogeneously to
G
.

In the case when
H
is not normal in
G
and the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
, among other results, it is proved in
[
KV
]
that
H
is nilpotent and if
N
is the normal closure of
H
in
G
, that is,
N
is the smallest normal subgroup of
G
containing
H
, then
N
is a
p
-group for some prime
p
or
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
N.

In this paper, we also consider the following condition, which we refer to as the
\mathrm{(F)}
condition. This condition is precisely the Camina pair condition without the assumption that
H
is normal in
G
, and is defined as follows:

\mathrm{(F)}:

1<H<G
and for all
x\in G\setminus H
,
x
is conjugate to
xh
for all
h\in H
.

For
g\in G
, let
g^{G}
denote the conjugacy class of
G
containing
g
. Then condition
\mathrm{(F)}
can be stated as follows:

1<H<G
and for all
x\in G\setminus H,

xH\subseteq x^{G}
.

Our first theorem establishes the equivalences of the
\mathrm{(CI)}
and
\mathrm{(F)}
conditions for any finite group
G
and nontrivial proper subgroup
H
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a nontrivial proper subgroup of
G
. Then the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
if and only if it satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
.

In a recent paper, Akhlaghi and Beltrán
[
AB
]
studied the following generalization of Camina pairs: let
H
be a nontrivial proper normal subgroup of a finite group
G
, and suppose that for all
g\in G\setminus H
, the element
gn
is conjugate to
g
or
g^{-1}
for every
n\in H.
Under this assumption, they proved that if
H
is not nilpotent, then
G/H
is a
p
-group and
G
is
p
-nilpotent and solvable for some prime
p.
On the other hand, if
H
is nilpotent, then
G
is a Frobenius group with kernel
H
; or
H
is of prime power order; or
|G/H|=2
, the center of
G
is of order
2
, is contained in
H
and
G/Z(G)
is a generalized dihedral group.

We extend the condition studied by Akhlaghi and Beltrán by removing the normality requirement on
H
. Specifically, we consider the following condition, which we denote by
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
.

\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
:
1<H<G
, and for all
x\in G\setminus H
,
xh
is conjugate to
x
or
x^{-1}
for every
h\in H
.

Note that condition
\mathrm{(F)}
implies condition
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
. As it turns out, if the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
, then the pair
(G,N)
also satisfies
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
, where
N
is the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Moreover, under this assumption,
N
is nilpotent.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a nontrivial proper subgroup of
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. If the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
, then
(G,N)
satisfies
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
and
N
is nilpotent.

We should point out the connection between conditions
\mathrm{(F)}
and
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
and recent work by Camina et al. in
[
CCL+
]
. Let
G
be a finite group and let
N
be a nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
G
. The pair
(G,N)
is called an
equal order pair
if, for every
x\in G\setminus N
, the elements in the coset
xN
all have the same order as
x
. Clearly, if the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
or
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
, then the pair
(G,N)
is an equal order pair, where
N
is the normal closure of
H
in
G
. In particular, since
N
is nilpotent by Theorem
2
, the structure of
G
is constrained by
[
CCL+
, Theorem B]
. For instance, if
N
is a
p
-group for some prime
p
, then for every prime
r\neq p
, the Sylow
r
-subgroups of
G
are either cyclic or generalized quaternion; and if
N
is not of prime power order, then
N
coincides with the Fitting subgroup of
G
, that is, the largest nilpotent normal subgroup of
G
, and the Sylow subgroups of
G/N
are either cyclic or generalized quaternion. This naturally lead to the following problem.

Problem 1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a nontrivial proper subgroup of
G
. Study the structure of
G
if the pair
(G,H)
satisfies the condition: for every
x\in G\setminus H
, the elements in the coset
xH
all have the same order as
x
.

The main inspiration for our definition of condition
\mathrm{(F)}
follows from work of Navarro and Guralnick in
[
GN
]
. In that paper, the authors investigate the structure of finite group
G
with a conjugacy class
K
such that
K^{2}
is a conjugacy class of
G
. This is a special case of a conjecture proposed by Arad, Stavi and Herzog in
[
ASH
]
stating that in a finite nonabelian simple group, the product of two nontrivial conjugacy classes cannot be a single class. In
[
GN
]
, they proved that if
K^{2}
is a conjugacy class of
G
, then
N:=[x,G]
is solvable, where
x\in K
. Moreover, all elements of the coset
xN
are conjugate in
G
. Inspired by this, they also show that if
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
and
x\in G
and that either all elements of
xN
are
G
-conjugate or all elements of
xN
have odd order, then
N
is solvable. As before, dropping the normality assumption, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a proper subgroup of
G
. Suppose that for every element
x\in G\setminus H
of odd order, all elements in the coset
xH
have odd order. Then either
O^{2}(H)
is normal in
G
and
G/O^{2}(H)
is a
2
-group, or
H
is solvable.

As a consequence, we obtain the following.

Corollary 4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a proper subgroup of
G
. Suppose that for every
x\in G\setminus H
, all elements in the coset
xH
have the same order. Then either
H
is solvable or the following hold.

(a)

O^{2}(G)\leqslant H
and
H
is subnormal in
G
.

(b)

Every element in
G\setminus H
is a
2
-element.

(c)

The pair
(N_{G}(H),H)
is an equal order pair.

Using a similar argument to that employed in the proof of Theorem
3
, we obtain the following variant of the Baer-Suzuki theorem. Recall that if
p
is a prime, then an element
g\in G
is called
p
-regular if its order is not divisible by
p.

Corollary 5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime. Suppose that
x\in G
is a
p
-element such that
xy
is
p
-regular for every nontrivial
p
-regular element
y\in G
. Then
x\in O_{p}(G)
.

This result may be known to experts. In fact, it has been shown that if
x\in G
and
xy
is
p
-regular for every
p
-regular element
y\in G
, then
x\in O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. This was proved independently by Robinson
[
R
]
and Flavell
[
F
]
. Robinson’s proof is character-theoretic, while Flavell’s argument is group-theoretic. Both proofs do not use the classification of finite simple groups. Our proof of Corollary
5
is also classification-free. Corollary
5
can be considered a dual of Theorem D in
[
BLM+
]
, where it is shown that if
x\in G
is
p
-regular such that the order of
xy
is divisible by
p
for all nontrivial
p
-elements
y\in G
, then
x\in O_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. This is proved modulo some conjecture on finite almost simple groups.

Notation.
Our notation is standard. We follow
[
I
]
for character theory of finite groups. If
G
is a finite group, then
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
denotes the set of its complex irreducible characters. If
N
is a subgroup of
G
and
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N)
, then
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\theta)
denotes the set of irreducible constituents of the induced character
\theta^{G}
. By the Frobenius reciprocity, this is the set of irreducible characters
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
such that the restriction
\chi_{N}
contains
\theta
as a constituent. In this case, we say that
\chi
‘lies over’
\theta
and
\theta
‘lies under’
\chi
. If
g\in G
, then we can uniquely write
g=g_{p}g_{p^{\prime}}
, where
g_{p},g_{p^{\prime}}\in\langle g\rangle
have orders a power of
p
and coprime to
p
, respectively. For a proper subgroup
H
of
G
, we define
\Delta_{H}(G)=G\setminus\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}
, where
H^{g}=g^{-1}Hg
is a conjugate of
H
by an element
g\in G
. Finally, we write
\pi(G)
for the set of distinct prime divisors of the order of
G
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect and prove some auxiliary results that will be used in the proofs of the main theorems. We begin by describing some properties of groups that satisfy condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
KV
, Lemma 1.3]
).

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
. Let
M
be a normal subgroup of
G
such that
H\not\subseteq M
. Then
M<H
and
(G/M,H/M)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
KV
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
(G,N)
is a Camina pair.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and that
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
N
is nilpotent.

Proof.

By
[
KV
, Theorem 4.1]
, either
N
is a
p
-group for some prime
p
or
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
N.
Clearly
N
is nilpotent if it is a
p
-group. If
N
is a Frobenius kernel, then it is nilpotent by Thompson’s theorem
[
Th
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
H
is a normal subgroup of
N
and
N/H
is abelian.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.3
,
N
is nilpotent, which implies that
N^{\prime}
is a proper characteristic subgroup of
N
. Note that
N
is nontrivial as it contains
H>1
. Since
N
is normal in
G
,
N^{\prime}
is also normal in
G
. In addition, as
N
is the smallest normal subgroup of
G
containing
H
,
H\nsubseteq N^{\prime}
. Lemma
2.1
yields that
N^{\prime}<H
. Thus
H
is a normal subgroup of
N
and
N/H
is abelian.
∎

Now suppose the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
, where
H
is a subgroup of a finite group
G
. We proceed to establish a sequence of results that will lead to the proof of the sufficient direction of Theorem
1
. These results are analogous to those obtained by Kuisch and van der Waall, as discussed above.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
. Let
M
be a normal subgroup of
G
such that
H\nsubseteq M
. Then
M<H
. Moreover,
(G/M,H/M)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
M\nsubseteq H
, and let
x\in M\setminus H
. Since
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
and
M
is normal in
G
, we have
xH\subseteq x^{G}\subseteq M
. It follows that
H\subseteq x^{-1}M=M
, a contradiction. Therefore,
M\subseteq H
. Since
H\not\subseteq M
, we conclude that
M<H
, as required. The final claim follows immediately, as conjugacy is preserved under taking quotients.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
. Then
\mathbf{Z}(G)\leqslant H\leqslant G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
x
is an element in
\mathbf{Z}(G)
that is not in
H
, then by condition
\mathrm{(F)}
,
xH\subseteq x^{G}=\left\{x\right\}
, which implies
H=1
, a contradiction. Hence, every element of
\mathbf{Z}(G)
is contained in
H
, and we establish the containment
\mathbf{Z}(G)\leqslant H
.
Next, take an element
y\in G\setminus H
. By the assumption that
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
, we have
yH\subseteq y^{G}
. That is, for each
h\in H
, there exists
g\in G
such that
yh=y^{g}
, and hence

h=y^{-1}y^{g}=[y,g]\in G^{\prime}.

It follows that
H\leqslant G^{\prime}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
and
G
is a
p
-group for some prime
p
. Then
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

Since
G
is a finite
p
-group, it is nilpotent and possesses an upper central series

\displaystyle 1=Z_{0}<Z_{1}=\mathbf{Z}(G)<Z_{2}<\ldots<Z_{k-1}<Z_{k}=G.

Because
Z_{1}=\mathbf{Z}(G)
is a nontrivial normal
p
-subgroup of
G
, by Lemma
2.6
,
Z_{1}\leqslant H
. If
Z_{1}=H
, then the claim follows. Suppose instead that
Z_{1}<H
. Then, by Lemma
2.5
, the pair
\left(G/Z_{1},H/Z_{1}\right)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
. Applying Lemma
2.6
to the pair
(G/Z_{1},H/Z_{1})
yields
Z_{2}/Z_{1}=\mathbf{Z}(G/Z_{1})\leqslant H/Z_{1}
and hence
Z_{2}\leqslant H
. If
Z_{2}=H
, then we are done. Otherwise, we continue the process: the pair
(G/Z_{2},H/Z_{2})
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
so
Z_{3}/Z_{2}=\mathbf{Z}(G/Z_{2})\leqslant H/Z_{2}
, and thus
Z_{3}\leqslant H
. By repeating this argument, we either eventually reach
H=Z_{i}
for some
i
, in which case
H\unlhd G
or else, we find that
Z_{k-1}\leqslant H
. Since
G^{\prime}\leqslant Z_{k-1}\leqslant H
,
H
is normal in
G
as well.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
and that
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
N=\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}
. Consequently,
1<N<G
.

Proof.

By definition, the normal closure
N
of
H
in
G
is generated by all conjugates of
H
, i.e., by the set
\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}
. To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that this union is closed under multiplication. Let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
. Consider the product

h_{1}^{g_{1}}h_{2}^{g_{2}}=g_{1}^{-1}h_{1}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}h_{2}g_{2}.

Set
x=g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}
. We rewrite this as:

h_{1}^{g_{1}}h_{2}^{g_{2}}=g_{2}^{-1}(g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}h_{1}g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}h_{2})g_{2}=\left(h_{1}^{x}h_{2}\right)^{g_{2}}.

If
h_{1}^{x}\in H
, then

h_{1}^{g_{1}}h_{2}^{g_{2}}=\left(h_{1}^{x}h_{2}\right)^{g_{2}}\in(Hh_{2})^{g_{2}}=H^{g_{2}}\subseteq\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}.

If
h_{1}^{x}\notin H
, then by condition (F),

h_{1}^{g_{1}}h_{2}^{g_{2}}=\left(h_{1}^{x}h_{2}\right)^{g_{2}}\in\left(h_{1}^{x}H\right)^{g_{2}}\subseteq\left(h_{1}^{x}\right)^{G}=h_{1}^{G}\subseteq\cup_{g\in G}H^{G}.

In both cases, the product lies in
\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}
, showing that this set is closed under multiplication. Since it is also clearly closed under taking inverses, it is a subgroup of
G
. Therefore,
N=\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}
, as desired. Moreover, since
H
is a nontrivial proper subgroup of
G
, the subset
\Delta_{H}(G)=G\setminus\cup_{g\in G}H^{g}
is nonempty. Hence,
G\setminus N
is nonempty, and since
N
contains
H
, we obtain
1<N<G
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies
\mathrm{(F)}
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
(G,N)
is a Camina pair.

Proof.

If
H\unlhd G
, then
(G,H)
is a Camina pair by definition. So, we may assume that
H
is not normal in
G
. By Lemma
2.8
,
N
is a nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
G
. Since
H\subseteq N\unlhd G
, it follows that for every
x\in G\setminus N
,
x^{t}\not\in H
for all
t\in G
. By condition
\mathrm{(F)}
, for each
t\in G
, we have
x^{t}H\subseteq(x^{t})^{G}=x^{G}
. Therefore,

x^{G}H=\cup_{t\in G}x^{t}H\subseteq x^{G},

so
x^{G}H=x^{G}
. Now let
K=x^{G}
. Then
KH=K
, meaning that for every element
h\in H
, we have
Kh=K
. Consequently, for all
g\in G
,

\displaystyle Kh^{g}=K^{g}h^{g}=(Kh)^{g}=K^{g}=K.

Thus,
Kh^{g}=K
for all
g\in G,h\in H
. Since every
n\in N
is the product of finitely many such conjugates
h^{g}
, it follows that
Kn=K
for all
n\in N
, so
KN=K
. Therefore,
xN\subseteq K=x^{G}
for every
x\in G\setminus N
, and hence
(G,N)
is a Camina pair.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
and that
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
N
is nilpotent. Moreover, either
G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
N
or
N
is a
p
-group.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.9
that
(G,N)
is a Camina pair. By
[
C
, Theorem 2]
, one of the following holds:

(1)

G
is a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
N
;

(2)

N
is a
p
-group;

(3)

G/N
is a
p
-group, where
p
is a prime.

In the first two cases,
N
is nilpotent (in the first case, this follows from Thompson’s Theorem
[
Th
, Theorem 1]
). It remains to rule out the third possibility. Suppose, for contradiction, that
G/N
is a
p
-group but neither of the first two cases holds. That is,
N
is not a
p
-group and
G
is not a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel
N
.

Since
(G,N)
is a Camina pair and
G/N
is a
p
-group, it follows from
[
L
, Theorem 4.13]
that
G
has a normal
p
-complement
M
. Since
G/N
is a
p
-group, we must have
M\subseteq N
. Furthermore, as
H
is not normal in
G
, it follows that
M<H
. By Lemma
2.5
,
(G/M,H/M)
also satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
. But
G/M
is a
p
-group, so by Lemma
2.7
,
H/M\unlhd G/M
, which implies that
H\unlhd G
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
and that
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Then
H\unlhd N
and
N/H
is abelian.

Proof.

Since
N
is nilpotent by Lemma
2.10
, its derived subgroup
N^{\prime}
is a proper characteristic subgroup of
N
. Since
N
is normal in
G
,
N^{\prime}\unlhd G
. Moreover, since
N
is the smallest normal subgroup of
G
containing
H
and
H
is not normal in
G
, we must have
N^{\prime}<H
. Therefore,
H\unlhd N
and

∎

Recall that for a normal subgroup
N
of a finite group
G
, we define
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)=\mathrm{Irr}(G)\setminus\mathrm{Irr}(G/N)
, that is,
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
is the set of irreducible characters of
G
whose kernels do not contain
N
. Since
N=\langle H^{g}:g\in G\rangle
, we see that
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)=\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid H).

In
[
RLL
, Theorem 2.1]
, the authors showed that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
if and only if for every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
,

(1_{H})^{G}(\chi-[\chi_{H},1_{H}]1_{G})=a(\chi)\chi,

where
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]
denotes the inner product of
\chi_{H}
with the trivial character
1_{H}
,
a(\chi)
is a positive integer and
N
is the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Furthermore, if
H\unlhd G
, then
H=N
and the latter condition simplifies to: for every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
,

(1_{H})^{G}\chi=a(\chi)\chi,

where
a(\chi)
is some positive integer. Recall that
(1_{H})^{G}\chi=(\chi_{H})^{G}
for any
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G).

It turns out that if the pair
(G,H)
satisfies the last condition above, then
H\unlhd G
. This is the content of Theorem 3.1 in
[
RLL
]
which we reproduce below.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
G
with
1<H<G
. Suppose that for every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
,
(1_{H})^{G}\chi=a(\chi)\chi,
where
a(\chi)
is some positive integer. Then
H\unlhd G
.

We will need the following consequence of Lemma
2.12
.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
. If
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]=0
for every
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid H)
, then
H\unlhd G.

Proof.

This is Corollary 3.2 in
[
RLL
]
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a nontrivial proper subgroup of
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. In the first half of this section, we aim to prove the necessary condition in Theorem
1
, namely that if the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
, then it also satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
. Our approach is based on analyzing the two possible cases for the restriction
\chi_{H}
of characters
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid H)=\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
: either
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]=0
or
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]\neq 0
. Note that, by Corollary
2.13
, if
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and

for every

, then
H\unlhd G
. Hence, assuming

is not normal in

, there must exist some
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid H)
such that
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]\neq 0
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and that
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Assume
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
such that
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]\neq 0
. Then
\chi(xh)=\chi(x)
for all
x\in N\setminus H
,
h\in H
.

Proof.

First, we prove that all irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
are linear characters.
By Clifford’s Theorem
[
I
, Theorem 6.2]
, we can write

(1)

\displaystyle\chi_{N}=f(\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{s}+\theta_{s+1}+\ldots+\theta_{t})

where
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{t}
are the distinct irreducible characters of
N
that are conjugate under the action of
G
. Since
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]\neq 0
, we may assume without loss of generality that the trivial character
1_{H}
lies under
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s}
for some integer
s
with
1\leqslant s\leqslant t
.
By Lemma
2.4
, we know that
H\unlhd N
. Applying Clifford’s Theorem again to each
\theta_{i}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
, the restrictions of all these characters
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s}
to
H
are multiple of
1_{H}
. Consequently, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant s
,
\theta_{i}
is an irreducible character of the quotient group
N/H
, which is abelian by Lemma
2.4
. Hence these
\theta_{i}
are all linear characters of
N
. Finally, since
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{t}
are conjugate in
G
, they all have the same degree. Therefore, all
\theta_{i}
, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant t
, are linear characters, as required.

Next, observe that the restrictions of
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s}
to
H
are all equal to
1_{H}
.
Since
\chi\notin\mathrm{Irr}(G/N)
, where
N
is the smallest normal subgroup of
G
containing
H
, it follows that
H\nsubseteq\ker(\chi)
. Thus
\chi_{H}\neq\chi(1)1_{H}
, meaning that
1_{H}
is not the unique irreducible constituent of
\chi_{H}
. Therefore, we can choose a constituent
\mu_{1}
of
\chi_{H}
different from
1_{H}
. Assume that
\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}

(for some
s+1\leqslant l_{1}\leqslant t
) are all the irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
lying over
\mu_{1}
.
We will show that
\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}=\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})
and moreover,

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{N}=\theta_{s+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{1}}.

Take an arbitrary
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})
. Since
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and
\mu_{1}\neq 1_{H}
,

\mu_{1}^{G}=(\mu_{1}^{N})^{G}=e\chi

for some positive integer
e
. This implies that
\chi
is the unique irreducible constituent of
\theta^{G}
. Consequently,
\theta
must be an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
lying over
\mu_{1}
, so
\theta\in\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}
. Conversely, let
\varphi\in\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}
. Then
\varphi\in\mathrm{Irr}(N)
lies over
\mu_{1}
, so
\varphi\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})
. This establishes the equality
\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}=\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})
. Furthermore, since
\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}
are linear characters of
N
,
(\theta_{i})_{H}=\mu_{1}
for each
i=s+1,\ldots,l_{1}
, and it implies that

\displaystyle[\mu_{1}^{N},\theta_{i}]=[\mu_{1},(\theta_{i})_{H}]=1.

Therefore,
\mu_{1}^{N}=\theta_{s+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{1}}
as claimed.

We now repeat the same argument for the remaining nontrivial irreducible constituents of
\chi_{H}
. Suppose
\left\{1_{H},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{n}\right\}
is the set of all irreducible constituents of
\chi_{H}
, where
1_{H}\neq\mu_{j}\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. Take any
\mu_{j}
for
j=2,\dots,n
. By reindexing the set
\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{t}\}
, we may assume that
\theta_{l_{j-1}+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{j}}
are the irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
lying over
\mu_{j}
, where
l_{j}\leqslant t
and
l_{n}=t
. Applying the same argument used previously for
\mu_{1}
, we obtain:

\left\{\theta_{l_{j-1}+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{j}}\right\}=\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{j})\text{ and }\mu_{j}^{N}=\theta_{l_{j-1}+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{j}}.

To finish the proof of the proposition, we observe that the subset
\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{t}\right\}
of the irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
in Equation (
1
) is a disjoint union

\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{t}\right\}=\left\{\theta_{s+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}\cup\left\{\theta_{l_{1}+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{2}}\right\}\cup\ldots\cup\left\{\theta_{l_{n-1}+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{n}}\right\}

which is equal to

\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})\cup\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{2})\cup\ldots\cup\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{n}).

Furthermore,
\chi_{N}
in Equation (
1
) can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\chi_{N}

\displaystyle=f(\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{s}+\theta_{s+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{1}}+\theta_{l_{1}+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{2}}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{n-1}+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{n}})

(2)

\displaystyle=f(\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{s}+\mu_{1}^{N}+\mu_{2}^{N}+\ldots+\mu_{n}^{N}).

Finally, as
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{s}
are characters of the quotient
N/H
, for any
x\in N\setminus H
and
h\in H
, we have
\theta_{i}(xh)=\theta_{i}(x)
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. Furthermore, as
H\unlhd N
,
x^{N}
and
(xh)^{N}
intersect
H
trivially, and thus
\mu_{j}^{N}(x)=0=\mu_{j}^{N}(xh)
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Substituting into Equation (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\chi(xh)=f(\theta_{1}(xh)+\ldots+\theta_{s}(xh))=f(\theta_{1}(x)+\ldots+\theta_{s}(x))=\chi(x).

This shows that
\chi
is constant on the nontrivial left cosets of
H
in
N
, completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose that the pair
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and that
H
is not normal in
G
. Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. Assume
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
such that
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]=0
. Then
\chi(xh)=\chi(x)
for all
x\in N\setminus H
and
h\in H
.

Proof.

First, by applying Clifford’s Theorem with
N\unlhd G
, we obtain

(3)

\displaystyle\chi_{N}=f(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\ldots+\theta_{t}),

where the
\theta_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,t
are all the distinct irreducible characters of
N
, which are conjugate in
G
. Since
[\chi_{H},1_{H}]=0

\mu_{1}
of
\chi_{H}
.

\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}
, for some
l_{1}\leqslant t

\chi_{N}
lying over
\mu_{1}
. Analogous to the argument in Proposition
3.1

\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})=\left\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}\text{ and }\mu_{1}^{N}=a_{1}(\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{1}})

for some positive integer

\varphi
is an irreducible character of
N
lying over
\mu_{1}
, so
\varphi\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})

\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})

(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
and
\mu_{1}\neq 1_{H}

\mu_{1}^{G}=(\mu_{1}^{N})^{G}=e\chi
for some positive integer

\chi

\theta

\chi_{N}

Next, since

2.4

\mu_{1},\mu_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{1}^{n_{s}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)

which implies that

. Denote this common value by

, we conclude that

\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{1})=\left\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{1}}\right\}

\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{j})
and
\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{k})

\mu_{j}

\mu_{k}

. However, since

\mu_{j}
and
\mu_{k}
are conjugate in

, contradicting the assumption.

Now, since
\mu_{j}
for

\mu_{j}

and
{l_{n}}=t

\left\{\theta_{l_{j-1}+1},\ldots,\theta_{l_{j}}\right\}=\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\mu_{j})\text{ and }\theta_{l_{j-1}+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{j}}=\dfrac{1}{a_{j}}\mu_{j}^{N},

\displaystyle\chi_{N}

\displaystyle=f(\theta_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{1}}+\theta_{l_{1}+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{2}}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{n-1}+1}+\ldots+\theta_{l_{n}})

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Suppose
H
is not normal in
G
. If
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
, then
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
.

Proof.

Let
N
denote the normal closure of
H
in
G
. By Lemma
2.2
,
(G,N)
is a Camina pair. That is, for every
x\in G\setminus N
,
x
is conjugate to
xn
for all
n\in N
. In particular, for all
x\in G\setminus N
and
h\in H\subseteq N
,
x
is conjugate to
xh
, as required for condition
\mathrm{(F)}
. It remains to consider the case when
x\in N\setminus H
. We claim that for all
x\in N\setminus H
,
x
is conjugate to
xh
for all
h\in H
. This is equivalent to for all
x\in N\setminus H
,
\chi(x)=\chi(xh)
for all
h\in H
and
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. Indeed, for any
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/N)
, we have
\chi(x)=\chi(xh)=\chi(1)
for all
x\in N,h\in H
as
N
is contained in the kernel of
\chi
. For
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid N)
, the equality
\chi(x)=\chi(xh)
for all
x\in N\setminus H
,
h\in H
followed from Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
. Thus, all irreducible characters of
G
are constant on the cosets
xH
for
x\in N\setminus H
, so
xH\subseteq x^{G}
for all
x\in N\setminus H.
Hence
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
, completing the proof.
∎

Next, we assume
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
and prove the converse implication in Theorem
1
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group with a nontrivial proper subgroup
H
. Suppose that
H
is not normal in
G
. If
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F)}
, then
(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(CI)}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be a nontrivial irreducible character of
H
.
Let
N
be the normal closure of
H
in
G
. We first claim that every constituent of
\lambda^{N}
is nontrivial. Suppose, for contradiction, that
1_{N}
is a constituent of
\lambda^{N}
. Then:

0\neq[\lambda^{N},1_{N}]=[\lambda,1_{H}]=0,

a contradiction. Thus, all constituents of
\lambda^{N}
are nontrivial.

Let
\theta
be a constituent of
\lambda^{N}
and let
\rho
be an irreducible representation of
N
affording
\theta
. By Lemma
2.11
,
H\unlhd N
and applying Clifford’s Theorem, we have

\theta_{H}=a(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{t}),

where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{t}
are all the distinct conjugates of
\lambda
in
N
. Since
\lambda
is nontrivial, all the
\lambda_{i}
are also nontrivial.

Now, let
\widehat{H}=\sum_{h\in H}h\in\mathbb{C}[N]
be the sum of all elements in

. Since
H\unlhd N
,
H
is a union of some conjugacy classes of

, so
\widehat{H}
lies in the center of
\mathbb{C}[N]
. Hence, by the Schur’s Lemma (
[
I
, Lemma 2.25]
),
\rho(\widehat{H})=\alpha I
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, where

denotes the identity matrix of size
\theta(1)
. We compute:

\displaystyle\mathrm{trace}(\rho(\widehat{H}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in H}\mathrm{trace}(\rho(h))=\sum_{h\in H}\theta_{H}(h)=|H|[\theta_{H},1_{H}]

\displaystyle=a|H|[\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{t},1_{H}]=0,

since each
\lambda_{i}
is nontrivial. Hence
\alpha=0
so
\rho(\widehat{H})=0
.

By Lemma
2.9
,
(G,N)
is a Camina pair. By
[
L
, Lemma 4.1 (7)]
, every nontrivial irreducible character of
N
induces homogeneously to
G
.
Therefore, every
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\lambda)
induces homogeneously to
G
.
We will show that all such
\theta
induce to the same character
\chi
for some
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
, so that
\lambda^{G}=e\chi
for some integer

.

By Lemma
2.8
, we have

. Hence, we can write

for some

and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
with

.
Let
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
be an irreducible constituent of
\lambda^{G}
. We will show that
\chi
is the unique constituent of
\theta^{G}
for all
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\lambda)
. To do that, we will show
[\chi,\theta^{G}]
is nonzero for all
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\lambda)
. Note that
[\chi,\theta^{G}]=[\chi_{N},\theta]
. We compute

\begin{array}[]{lll}|N|[\chi_{N},\theta]&=&\sum_{n\in N}\chi_{N}(n)\overline{\theta(n)}\\
&=&\sum_{h\in H}\chi_{H}(h)\overline{\theta_{H}(h)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h_{j}^{g_{j}}h)\overline{\theta(h_{j}^{g_{j}}h)}.\\
\end{array}

From condition
\mathrm{(F)}
, each
h_{j}^{g_{j}}\notin H
is conjugate to
h_{j}^{g_{j}}h
for all
h\in H
, so

\chi(h_{j}^{g_{j}}h)=\chi(h_{j}^{g_{j}})=\chi(h_{j}).

Thus

(5)

|N|[\chi_{N},\theta]=|H|[\chi_{H},\theta_{H}]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi(h_{j})\sum_{h\in H}\overline{\theta(h_{j}^{g_{j}}h)}.

Finally, since
\rho(\widehat{H})=0
, we obtain

\rho(h_{j}^{g_{j}}\widehat{H})=\rho(h_{j}^{g_{j}})\rho(\widehat{H})=\rho(h_{j}^{g_{j}})\cdot 0=0.

This implies that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
,

0=\mathrm{trace}\left(\rho(h_{j}^{g_{j}}\widehat{H})\right)=\sum_{h\in H}\mathrm{trace}\left(\rho(h_{j}^{g_{j}}h)\right)=\sum_{h\in H}\theta(h_{j}^{g_{j}}h).

Therefore, Equation (
5
) simplifies to

|N|[\chi_{N},\theta]=|H|[\chi_{H},\theta_{H}]\neq 0

because
\chi_{H}
and
\theta_{H}
share a common irreducible constituent
\lambda
.
Hence
[\chi,\theta^{G}]\neq 0
, and since
\theta^{G}
is homogeneous,
\chi
is the only irreducible constituent of
\theta^{G}.
As this applies to all
\theta\in\mathrm{Irr}(N\mid\lambda),
it follows that all of them induce to the same irreducible character
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G),
so
\lambda^{G}=e\chi
for some
e>0
, completing the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

This follows from Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let

Let

be a proper subgroup of a finite group

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be a finite group and let

be a subgroup of

(G,H)
satisfies condition
\mathrm{(F^{\prime})}
and that

is not normal in

. Let

and

. Let

be the normal closure of

in

Proof.

For each

, we first claim that

and

. Since

. Hence,

Now, let

Since

5.

Let

-element) if the order of

For brevity, we say that the pair

\mathrm{(O)}
. Observe that if there exists an element

of odd order such that

, then

is solvable by applying Theorem B (c) in
[

. However, it is possible that

might not possess any element of odd order. We handle this case in the following lemma. Recall that

is the smallest normal subgroup of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a finite group, let

be a proper subgroup of

(1)

All elements in

-group.

(2)

(1) Suppose that all elements in

are

-singular. First, we claim that

is a power of

of

is a

Now, we will show that

and

is a

-group. Set

, so

is a

-group. If

is a

-group, then

is a

-group and hence

. In this case, it follows from the preceding paragraph that

is a

-group, and the conclusion follows. Assume now that

is not a

-group, and thus

is not a

-group. Let

. Moreover, since

is a

. Hence, all

Let

Proof of Theorem

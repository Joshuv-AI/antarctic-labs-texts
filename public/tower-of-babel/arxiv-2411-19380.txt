1.
Introduction

In recent decades, the study of the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves of a variety has become a powerful and versatile tool in algebraic geometry. While much is known about derived categories of smooth projective varieties, the study of derived categories of singular varieties has become a very active topic only in the last few years. Classically, in order to understand singular varieties, one often studies resolutions of the singularities. This idea has a categorical manifestation, namely the notion of categorical resolutions of a triangulated category. Through this abstraction it is sometimes possible to extend geometric notions that exist only in low dimensions to higher dimensional varieties, such as in the case of simultaneous resolutions of singularities, see
[
25
]
.

Let
X
be a projective variety with rational singularities and consider a resolution of singularities
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
. On the level of bounded derived categories, there exist exact functors

\pi_{*}\colon D^{b}(\widetilde{X})\to D^{b}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{*}\colon D^{\lilperf}(X)\to D^{b}(\widetilde{X}),

where
\pi^{*}
is left adjoint to
\pi_{*}
on
D^{\lilperf}(X)
. Since
X
has rational singularities, the functor
\pi^{*}
is fully faithful. More generally, following
[
22
, Definition 3.2]
, a categorical resolution of a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is defined as a triple

(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}},\pi_{*}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{D},\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}),

where the category
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is a full admissible subcategory of the bounded derived category of a smooth projective variety, there exists an adjunction
\pi^{*}\vdash\pi_{*}
and we require that the functor
\pi^{*}
is fully faithful. In the case that
\pi^{*}
is additionally right adjoint to
\pi_{*}
, we call the categorical resolution
crepant
. These resolutions are of special interest, because they are considered to be particularly small
1
1

1

The definition of crepancy we use in this paper is in some articles referred to as
weak crepancy
. Note that weak crepancy does not imply that the categorical resolution is minimal.
.

Recall that an object
P
in the bounded derived category
D^{b}(X)
is called
l
-spherical
if
\Hom(P,P)=k\oplus k[-l]
and there exists an isomorphism of functors
\Hom(P,-)\cong\Hom(-,P[l])^{\vee}
. Let
F\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be an exact functor between triangulated categories. The
kernel of
F
is defined as the full subcategory
\ker(F)\subset\mathcal{D}
of objects

such that
F(A)\cong 0
. We call

a
Verdier localization
if the induced functor
\overline{F}\colon\mathcal{D}/\ker(F)\to\mathcal{D^{\prime}}
is an equivalence; here
\mathcal{D}/\ker(F)
is the
Verdier quotient
of
\mathcal{D}
by the triangulated subcategory
\ker(F)
.
Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety with an isolated
A_{2}
singularity (see Definition
2.1
) and
\dim(X)\geq 2
. Then there exists a crepant categorical resolution
\pi_{*}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\to D^{b}(X)
which is a Verdier localization. If
X
is even dimensional, the kernel
\ker(\pi_{*})

is generated by two
2
-spherical objects
\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2}
. In other words, the functor
\pi_{*}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\to D^{b}(X)
induces an equivalence of triangulated categories

\overline{\pi}_{*}\colon\left.\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\middle/\langle\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2}\rangle\right.\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}D^{b}(X).

In the case of an odd dimensional variety, the kernel is generated by one object
\mathcal{K}
and we will prove that it is not
l
-spherical for any integer
l
.

The existence of spherical objects allows us to construct interesting autoequivalences of the category
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
. At present it is not known for which kind of singularities on a variety the kernel of a categorical resolution is generated by spherical objects, but if a resolution has this property, it is automatically crepant, see
[
19
, Lemma 5.8]
.

We will show that the existence of the crepant categorical resolution of Theorem
1.1
follows as a direct application of
[
22
, §4]
. Moreover, the fact that
\pi_{*}
is a Verdier localization will be deduced from a theorem proved by Efimov in
[
8
, §8]
. Finally, the main contribution of this paper lies in explicitly determining generators of the kernel
\ker(\pi_{*})
and showing that they are
2
-spherical.

Note that
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
, and simultaneously
[
20
, Theorem 5.8]
, proved a result similar to Theorem

for a variety with an isolated

singularity, and our proof follows the same general structure as theirs, essentially because both singularities can be resolved by a single blow-up at the singularity. The crucial difference between

and

singularities is that their respective exceptional divisors of said blow-up are a smooth or a nodal quadric. Therefore, the spherical objects for
A_{1}
singularities are related to spinor bundles on the smooth quadric, and in the
A_{2}

Let us consider the blow-up
\widetilde{X}
of
X
at the
A_{2}
singularity

which resolves the singularity and provides us with a cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}

\mathcal{K}_{1}=j_{*}\mathcal{S}_{1},\quad\mathcal{K}_{2}=j_{*}\mathcal{S}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{K}=j_{*}\mathcal{S}.

A key ingredient in proving that

Let

where the element

(1.2.2)

\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\ \mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{1})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})\cong k[\theta^{\prime}],

The fact that the self Ext-complexes are isomorphic to polynomial algebras was already proved in

, and in their terminology, see
[

, the above result tells us that the spinor sheaves

are

-objects, and

is a

-object. Our first contribution in Theorem

is to compute the complexes (

We now give a rough outline of the proof of Theorem
1.2
and to this end sketch the construction of the spinor sheaves on a singular quadric, following
[
1
]
. Let
(V,q)
be an odd dimensional quadratic space with quadratic form
q
of corank
1
and let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
denote the associated nodal quadric. We consider the
Clifford algebra
of this quadratic space which is defined as

\mathrm{Cl}(q):=T^{\bullet}(V)/\langle q(v)-v^{2}\rangle;

here
T^{\bullet}(V)
denotes the tensor algebra of
V
. This
k
-algebra is in fact
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded, where
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)
and
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}(q)
denote the subspaces spanned by elements in
\mathrm{Cl}(q)
that are products of an even and odd number of elements in

, respectively. In low dimensions, e.g. for
\dim(V)=2
and for
\dim(V)=3
, the Clifford algebra and the even part of the Clifford algebra are given by degenerate quaternions, respectively, see Example
3.15
.

We will show that there exists an equivalence of triangulated categories

(1.2.4)

D^{b}(\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q))\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\langle\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle\subset D^{b}(Y),

where
\langle\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle
denotes the smallest triangulated subcategory in
D^{b}(Y)
, containing the sheaves
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. In particular, this equivalence shows that we can compute the Ext-complexes of the corresponding
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)
-modules.

The second key step in proving Theorem
1.2
is the reduction to a low dimensional quadratic space
(V,q)
or rather low dimensional Clifford algebras. More precisely, we will show the following version of Knörrer periodicity in Subsection
3.2
:

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
(V,q)
be a quadratic space with
q\neq 0
and let
U
denote the hyperbolic plane. Then there exists an isomorphism of
k
-algebras

\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q\perp U)\cong\matri_{2}(\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)).

Finally, we specialize to a cubic fourfold
X
with an isolated
A_{2}
singularity, whose bounded derived category admits a semiorthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{A}_{X}
denotes the
Kuznetsov Component
. More precisely, it is defined as the full subcategory

\mathcal{A}_{X}=\{\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}(X)\ |\ \mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(i),\mathcal{F})=0\ \text{for}\ i=0,1,2\ \}.

Then the exceptional divisor
Y
contains a K3 surface
S
, which is deeply related to
D^{b}(X)
as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a cubic fourfold with an isolated
A_{2}
singularity and let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
denote the blow-up of
X
at the singularity
x
. Then there exists a full admissible subcategory
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}\subset D^{b}(\widetilde{X})
so that the resolution of Theorem
1.1
restricts to a crepant categorical resolution

\pi_{*}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\lilperf}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X},

of the Kuznetsov component
\mathcal{A}_{X}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\lilperf}=\mathcal{A}_{X}\cap D^{\lilperf}(X)
. We have

\ker(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}_{X})=\ker(D^{b}(\widetilde{X})\to D^{b}(X))=\langle j_{*}\mathcal{S}_{1},j_{*}\mathcal{S}_{2}\rangle,

where
j\colon Y\hookrightarrow\widetilde{X}
denotes the closed immersion of the exceptional divisor
Y
which is a nodal quadric threefold.
Moreover, the category
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
is equivalent to the derived category
D^{b}(S)
of a K3 surface
S
and under this identification the kernel can be generated by
t^{*}\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
t^{*}\mathcal{S}_{2}
, where
t\colon S\hookrightarrow Y
denotes the embedding of
S
into the defining nodal quadric
Y
.

This generalizes the analogous results for
A_{1}
singularities proved in
[
23
, Theorem 5.2]
and
[

, extending them to

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the definitions of geometric and categorical resolutions of singularities, along with some fundamental results that we rely on in later sections.

2.1.
Geometric resolutions of singularities

In this subsection
X,\widetilde{X}
denote projective
k
-varieties if not specified otherwise.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
n=\dim(X)-1
. An isolated singularity
x\in X
is an
A_{d}
singularity
if there exists an isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}\cong k[[x_{1},...,x_{n+2}]]/({x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n+1}^{2}+x_{n+2}^{d+1}}).

In the case
d=1
we say that
X
has a
node
at
x
; in the case
d=2
we say that
X
has a
cusp
at
x
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be normal. Then
X
has
rational singularities
if we have

R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}=0

for any
i>0
and every resolution of singularities
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
, that is,
\widetilde{X}
is smooth and
\pi
is a proper birational morphism.

Remark 2.3
.

Let X be a projective variety with an isolated
A_{d}
singularity and let
\dim(X)\geq 2
. Then
X
has rational singularities, see
[
35
]
.

We consider the following example of a resolution of singularities which will be fundamental for the whole paper.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a projective variety with an isolated
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
singularity at a point
x\in X
. Then the blow-up
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
of
X
at the singular point
x
is a resolution of singularities for
X
. Let
Y\subset\widetilde{X}
denote the exceptional divisor. Then
Y
is a smooth quadric if
X
has an
A_{1}
singularity, and
Y
is a nodal quadric if
X
has an
A_{2}
singularity. Its conormal bundle is given by
\mathcal{N}_{Y/\widetilde{X}}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
. Moreover, in both cases, there exists an isomorphism

(2.4.1)

\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\pi^{*}\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}((n-1)Y),

where
n=\dim(Y)=\dim(X)-1
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows from a standard blow-up computation. Moreover, since
Y\subset\widetilde{X}
is an exceptional divisor of a blow-up, its conormal is given by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
. For (
2.4.1
) we refer to
[
7
, Proposition 3.5]
, who prove the statement for
A_{1}
singularities, and the proof works analogously in the case of
A_{2}
singularities.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities for
X
. Then we say that
\pi
is
crepant
if there exists an isomorphism
\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
.

Remark 2.6
.

In the setting of Lemma
2.4
, the divisor
(n-1)Y
on

is called the discrepancy of

, see
[

, §1.1]
. This also explains the neologism “crepant” in the definition above.

We end the subsection with a result on cubic hypersurfaces with isolated

singularities which will be relevant for the proof of Theorem

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

be a cubic hypersurface with an isolated

. Then it is defined by an equation of the form

for suitable quadratic and cubic polynomials

and

of

. Furthermore:

•

•

has corank

does not pass through the node of

.

In both cases

Proof.

Consider the affine neighborhood of
x
, where
x_{0}\neq 0
and the variety
X
is given by a defining equation of the form

(2.7.1)

f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+2})=c+l(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+2})+q(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+2})+g(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+2}),

and

,

and

. Since the point

is a singularity of

, we have

which proves the first claim. For the statement about identifying the blow-ups we refer to
[
12
, §1.5]
.

Let

and consider the blow-up of
X
at the point
x
. By Lemma

, the corresponding exceptional divisor is a smooth quadric, and it can be shown to coincide with the projectivized tangent cone

, which is by definition isomorphic to

. Conversely, let

be a quadratic form of maximal rank. Since

is algebraically closed and

we can assume that

. Passing to a local analytic neighborhood of the singularity

, we can write

[

If

, with

, then this implies that

(2.7.2)

of

Lastly, the smoothness of

can be proven in an elementary way, like in

, Proposition 2.8]
, or by the fact that we know that

is smooth for

∎

Corollary 2.7.1
.

Let

be a cubic fourfold which is smooth away from an isolated

The adjunction formula gives us an isomorphism

2.2.
Semiorthogonal decompositions, Serre functors and mutations

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category and
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
a full triangulated subcategory. The
left orthogonal
to
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{D}
is defined as the full triangulated subcategory

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}=\{B\in\mathcal{D}\ |\ \Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(B,A)=0\ \forall A\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Analogously, the
right orthogonal
to
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{D}
is defined as the full triangulated subcategory

\mathcal{A}^{\perp}=\{B\in\mathcal{D}\ |\ \Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(A,B)=0\ \forall A\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Definition 2.8

(
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
denote a full triangulated subcategory and denote the inclusion functor by
i_{*}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{D}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is called
left admissible
(resp.
right admissible
) if
i_{*}
admits a left adjoint
i^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
(resp. a right adjoint
i^{!}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}
). If
\mathcal{A}
is left and right admissible, then we call it an
admissible
subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 2.9
.

(
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
)
A
semiorthogonal decomposition
of
\mathcal{D}
consists of full triangulated subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
, such that

(1)

the sequence
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
is
semiorthogonal
, i.e.

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(A_{j},A_{i})=0

for all
j>i
and
A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{j},A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
.

(2)

The category
\mathcal{D}
is the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing the subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

We denote a semiorthogonal decomposition by
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
. We call it
admissible
if all the subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
are admissible.

Remark 2.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Then any semiorthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
is admissible, see
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be a semiorthogonal sequence in
\mathcal{D}
, such that
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k}
are left admissible and
\mathcal{A}_{k+1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
are right admissible, then

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k},^{\bot}\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k}\rangle\cap\langle\mathcal{A}_{k+1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle^{\bot},\mathcal{A}_{k+1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle

is a semiorthogonal decomposition.

It is a well-known fact that one can associate to
any hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
an admissible subcategory in
D^{b}(X)
which, intuitively, captures all the “interesting” geometric information about
X
. More precisely:

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
be a hypersurface of degree
d
and assume
d\leq n+1
. Then
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{X}(n+1-d))
is an exceptional collection in
D^{b}(X)
. Let

\mathcal{A}_{X}:=\langle\mathcal{O}_{X},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{X}(n+1-d)\rangle^{\perp}

denote the right orthogonal of this collection. Then we have

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{X}(n+1-d)\rangle;

and we will call the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}
the Kuznetsov Component of
D^{b}(X)
.

Definition 2.13

(
[
4
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
k
-linear Hom-finite triangulated category, that is,
\Hom(A,B)
is finite dimensional for any objects
A,B\in\mathcal{D}
. Then an exact equivalence
\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
is called
Serre functor
if there exist natural isomorphisms

\Hom(F,G)\cong\Hom(G,\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{D}}(F))^{\vee}

of
k
-vector spaces for any
F,G\in\mathcal{D}
.

Example 2.14

(
[
4
, Example 3.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Then
D^{b}(X)
admits a Serre functor which is given by
(-)\otimes\omega_{X}[\dim(X)]
.

Example 2.15

(
[
13
, §2.3]
, see also
[
3
, §6]
)
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein projective variety. Then the restriction of
\mathbb{S}_{X}
to
D^{\lilperf}(X)
defines a Serre functor on
D^{\lilperf}(X)
. More generally, there exist natural isomorphisms

\Hom({F},{G})\cong\Hom({G},\mathbb{S}_{X}(F))^{\vee},

for all
{F}\in D^{\lilperf}(X)
and
{G}\in D^{b}(X)
, by Grothendieck-Verdier duality.

For any triangulated category the group of autoequivalences
\aut(\mathcal{D})
naturally acts on the set of semiorthogonal decompositions of
\mathcal{D}
. In the special case of Serre functors we recall the following result.

Lemma 2.16

(
[
13
, Lemma 2.15]
)
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein projective variety and let
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
be a semiorthogonal decomposition, where either
\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{B}
is contained in
D^{\lilperf}(X)
. Then the subcategories
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset D^{b}(X)
are admissible and there exist semiorthogonal decompositions

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{B}\otimes\omega_{X},\mathcal{A}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A}\otimes\omega_{X}^{\vee}\rangle.

Apart from the automorphisms induced from elements of
\aut(\mathcal{D})
, there exists an interesting class of functors, the
mutation functors
which, informally speaking, permute the components of a semiorthogonal decomposition.

Proposition 2.17

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
be an admissible subcategory. By Lemma
2.11
we have semiorthogonal decompositions
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}^{\bot},\mathcal{A}\rangle
and
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},^{\bot}\mathcal{A}\rangle
. Then there exist functors
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{A}},\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
, vanishing on
\mathcal{A}
that restrict to mutually inverse equivalences
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon^{\bot}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\bot}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\bot}\to\ ^{\bot}\mathcal{A}
.

We call the functors
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
the
left-
and
right mutation functors
corresponding to
\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 2.18

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
be an admissible semiorthogonal decomposition. Then for each
1\leq k\leq n-1
, there exists a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k-1},\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}(\mathcal{A}_{k+1}),\mathcal{A}_{k},\mathcal{A}_{k+2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle.

Furthermore, for each
2\leq k\leq n
, there exists a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k-2},\mathcal{A}_{k},\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}(\mathcal{A}_{k-1}),\mathcal{A}_{k+1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle.

2.3.
Categorical resolutions of singularities

We recall the definition of, and a method for constructing, (crepant) categorical resolutions of the bounded derived category
D^{b}(X)
of a variety
X
with rational singularities, following
[
22
]
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category. We say that
\mathcal{D}
is
smooth
if there exists a smooth projective variety
\widetilde{X}
such that
\mathcal{D}
is equivalent to a full admissible subcategory of the bounded derived category
D^{b}(\widetilde{X})
.

Remark 2.20
.

Nowadays, Definition
2.19
is considered outdated, but we will still use it as it is sufficient for our purposes. We refer to
[
30
, Definition 3.23]
for a more general definition of smoothness for any enhanced triangulated category.

Definition 2.21

(
[
29
, Definition 1.6]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
k
-linear triangulated category. An object
F\in\mathcal{D}
is said to be
homologically finite
if for any
G\in\mathcal{D}
there is only a finite number of values
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F,G[i])\neq 0
. We denote the full triangulated subcategory of homologically finite objects by
\mathcal{D}^{\lilperf}
.

Remark 2.22

(
[
29
, Lemma 1.11]
)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety. For
\mathcal{D}=D^{b}(X)
the subcategory of perfect complexes coincides with the subcategory of homologically finite objects which justifies the notation
\mathcal{D}^{\lilperf}
.

Definition 2.23

(
[
22
, Definition 3.2]
)
.

A
categorical resolution
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
consists of a smooth (as in Definition
2.19
) triangulated category
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
and a pair of functors

\pi_{*}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{D}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

The functor
\pi^{*}
is left adjoint to
\pi_{*}
. That is, there exist natural isomorphisms

\Hom_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}(\pi^{*}F,G)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F,\pi_{*}G)\quad\quad\text{for any}\ F\in\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}},\ G\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}.

(2)

The natural transformation
\id_{\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}}}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}
is an isomorphism.

Remark 2.24
.

Let
X
be a variety with rational singularities and
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
a resolution of singularities. Then the derived category
D^{b}(\widetilde{X})
together with the pushforward and pullback functors
\pi_{*}\colon D^{b}(\widetilde{X})\to D^{b}(X)
and
\pi^{*}\colon D^{\lilperf}(X)\to D^{b}(\widetilde{X})
is a categorical resolution of
D^{b}(X)
.
By imposing the second condition in the above definition, we restrict ourselves to the case where
X
has at most rational singularities.

Definition 2.25

(
[
22
, Definition 3.4]
)
.

A categorical resolution
(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}},\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
is called
crepant
if the functor
\pi^{*}
is also right adjoint to
\pi_{*}
when restricted to
\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}}
. In other words, there exist natural isomorphisms

\Hom_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}(G,\pi^{*}F)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\pi_{*}G,F)\quad\quad\text{for any}\ F\in\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}},\ G\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}.

Remark 2.26
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein projective variety. A crepant (geometric) resolution
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
induces a crepant categorical resolution
(D^{b}(\widetilde{X}),\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
of
D^{b}(X)
as follows. The relative canonical complex
\omega_{\pi}
is in fact a line bundle, since
X
is Gorenstein and by Grothendieck-Verdier duality the right adjoint
\pi^{!}
of
\pi_{*}\colon D^{b}(\widetilde{X})\to D^{b}(X)
can be given explicitly by
\pi^{!}(F)=\pi^{*}F\otimes\omega_{\pi}
for all
F\in D^{\lilperf}(X)
. If
\pi
is crepant then
\omega_{\pi}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}
, hence
\pi^{!}=\pi^{*}
.

In order to describe a construction of a (crepant) categorical resolution, we recall the following definition.

Definition 2.27

(
[
22
, Definition 2.16]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety and let
\mathcal{O}(1)
denote a line bundle on
X
. A
Lefschetz decomposition
of
D^{b}(X)
is a semiorthogonal decomposition of the form

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{1}(1),\dots,\mathcal{B}_{m-1}(m-1)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{B}_{m-1}
are subcategories of
D^{b}(X)
, satisfying

0\subset\mathcal{B}_{m-1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{B}_{1}\subset\mathcal{B}_{0}\subset D^{b}(X).

Similarly, a
dual Lefschetz decomposition
of
D^{b}(X)
is a semiorthogonal decomposition of the form

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{B}_{m-1}(1-m),\dots,\mathcal{B}_{1}(-1),\mathcal{B}_{0}\rangle,\ \text{where}\ \ 0\subset\mathcal{B}_{m-1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{B}_{1}\subset\mathcal{B}_{0}\subset D^{b}(X).

Following
[
22
, §4]
, let
X
denote a projective variety with at most rational singularities and
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
a geometric resolution, for which we assume that the exceptional locus
E
is an irreducible divisor. Let
Z
be the image of
E
under
\pi
. Then we have a cartesian diagram

(2.27.1)

where the morphisms

.

In the setting of the preceding paragraph assume that there exists a dual Lefschetz decomposition

if the image of the pullback functor

is contained in

Let

3.
Spinor sheaves on singular quadrics and Morita reduction

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
, using the definition of spinor sheaves on singular quadrics introduced in
[
1
]
. Throughout the section
(V,q)
denotes an arbitrary nontrivial quadratic space, meaning
V\neq\{0\}
. We set
N:=\dim(V)
, and therefore the associated quadric hypersurface
Y:=V(q)\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
has dimension
n:=N-2
.

3.1.
Spinor sheaves on singular quadrics

The Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(q)
associated to
(V,q)
is defined as the quotient

(3.0.1)

\mathrm{Cl}(q):=T^{\bullet}(V)/\langle q(v)-v^{2}\rangle,

where
T^{\bullet}(V)
denotes the tensor algebra. This algebra carries a natural
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading induced by the involution
V\to V,\ v\mapsto-v
. We denote the eigenspaces with respect to this action by
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)
and
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}(q)
, and obtain a grading
\mathrm{Cl}(q)=\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)\oplus\mathrm{Cl}_{1}(q)
.

Let
W\subset V
be an isotropic subspace and choose a basis
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\}
of
W
. Then one can define a left ideal
I^{W}=\mathrm{Cl}(q)w_{1}\cdots w_{m}\subset\mathrm{Cl}(q)
which admits an induced
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading
I^{W}=I^{W}_{0}\oplus I^{W}_{1}
. Note that
I^{W}
is independent of the choice of basis of
W
, see
[
1
, §2]
. The grading gives rise to
k
-linear maps

(3.0.2)

I^{W}_{0}\overset{v\cdot}{\longrightarrow}I^{W}_{1},\quad\quad I^{W}_{1}\overset{v\cdot}{\longrightarrow}I^{W}_{0}

defined by left multiplication with a vector
v\in V
. Let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\}
denote a
k
-basis for the vector space
V

and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
be the coordinates of
\mathbb{P}(V)
; then the embedding

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)\otimes V,\quad 1\mapsto\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes v_{i}

induces a morphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{0}^{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes V\otimes I_{0}^{W},\quad 1\otimes w\longmapsto(\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes v_{i})\otimes w.

By composition with the multiplication map, we obtain a morphism

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{0}^{W}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes V\otimes I_{0}^{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes I_{1}^{W},

\displaystyle 1\otimes w

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes v_{i})\otimes w\longmapsto\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes v_{i}w

of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}(V)
. Analogously, by swapping the roles of
I_{0}^{W}
and
I_{1}^{W}
, we define a morphism of vector bundles

\displaystyle\psi\colon\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{1}^{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes I_{0}^{W},

\displaystyle 1\otimes w^{\prime}\longmapsto\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes v_{i}w^{\prime}.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
1
, §2]
)
.

Let
W\subset V
be an isotropic subspace. Then the compositions

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{0}^{W}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes I_{1}^{W}\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}(2)\otimes I_{0}^{W};

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{1}^{W}\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes I_{0}^{W}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}(2)\otimes I_{1}^{W},

coincide with the maps

\displaystyle q\otimes\id_{I_{0}}\colon\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{0}^{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(2)\otimes I_{0}^{W},\quad 1\otimes w\mapsto q\otimes w;

\displaystyle q\otimes\id_{I_{1}}\colon\mathcal{O}\otimes I_{1}^{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(2)\otimes I_{1}^{W},\quad 1\otimes w^{\prime}\mapsto q\otimes w^{\prime},

respectively. In particular, the morphisms
\varphi
and
\psi
are injective, and furthermore isomorphisms on the locus where
q\neq 0
.

The fact that the cokernels of
\varphi
and
\psi
are supported on the quadric
Y
motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.2

(
[
1
, §2]
)
.

Let
W\subset V
be an isotropic subspace, then we call the sheaves

\mathcal{S}^{W}:=\coker(\varphi(-1)),\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{T}^{W}:=\coker(\psi(-1))

on
Y
the
spinor sheaves associated to
W
. Since
\varphi
and
\psi
are injective, we have the following short exact sequences of sheaves

(3.2.1)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-2)^{M}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}^{M}(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{W}\longrightarrow 0;

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-2)^{M}\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}^{M}(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}^{W}\longrightarrow 0,

where
M=\dim(I_{0})=\dim(I_{1})=2^{\codim(W)-1}
.

Remark 3.3
.

In
[
1
, §2]
the spinor sheaves are defined by the same short exact sequences (
3.2.1
), but are additionally twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)
. This convention implies that the spinor bundle on a smooth conic
Q
is given by
\mathcal{O}_{Q}(1)
and coincides with
[
14
]
. In contrary, using the above convention, the spinor bundle on
Q
will coincide with
\mathcal{O}_{Q}(-1)
, as in
[
31
]
.

With the short exact sequences (
3.2.1
) above, we can easily deduce the following result about the cohomology of spinor sheaves.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(V,q)
be a quadratic space with
\dim(V)\geq 3
and let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
denote the associated quadric. Then for any isotropic subspace
W\subset V
the cohomology of the spinor sheaves
\mathcal{S}^{W},\mathcal{T}^{W}
vanishes. Moreover, we have

H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{S}^{W}(l))=0\quad\text{and}\quad H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{T}^{W}(l))=0,

for
0<i<n
and any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, that is, their intermediate cohomology vanishes.

Furthermore, there exist the following short exact sequences that will be used frequently in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(V,q)
be a quadratic space, let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
denote the associated quadric and let
W\subset V
be an isotropic subspace. Then there exist short exact sequences

(3.5.1)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}^{W}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{W}(1)\longrightarrow 0;

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{W}\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}^{W}(1)\longrightarrow 0,

where
M=\dim_{k}(I_{0}^{W})=\dim_{k}(I_{1}^{W})=2^{\codim(W)-1}
.

Proof.

The short exact sequences can be constructed by breaking up the locally free resolutions in
[
1
, §4]
.
∎

Given a quadratic space
(V,q)
, we will denote the kernel of the quadratic form
q\colon V\to k
by
K
. Then the singular locus of the quadric
Y=V(q)\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
coincides with
\mathbb{P}(K)\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

The restrictions of the spinor sheaves
\mathcal{S}^{W},\mathcal{T}^{W}
to
\mathbb{P}(K)\cap\mathbb{P}(W)
are trivial of rank
2^{\codim(W)-1}
. In the case that
\codim(W)>1
, the sheaves
\mathcal{S}^{W}
and
\mathcal{T}^{W}
are locally free of rank
2^{\codim(W)-2}
on
Y\setminus(\mathbb{P}(K)\cap\mathbb{P}(W))
. In particular,
\mathcal{S}^{W}
and
\mathcal{T}^{W}
are locally free if and only if
K\cap W=\{0\}
.

Remark 3.7

(
[
1
, §3]
)
.

The sheaves
\mathcal{S}^{W}
,
\mathcal{T}^{W}
are unchanged while varying
W
continuously with
W\cap K
fixed. Moreover, let
\pi\colon V\to V/K
be the projection, then the following holds:

i)

If
\dim(\pi(W))<\frac{1}{2}\dim(V/K)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{W}\cong\mathcal{T}^{W}
;

ii)

If
\dim(\pi(W))=\frac{1}{2}\dim(V/K)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{W}\not\cong\mathcal{T}^{W}
and switching
\pi(W)
to the other connected family (while keeping
W\cap K
fixed) interchanges
\mathcal{S}^{W}
and
\mathcal{T}^{W}
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
(V,q)
be a quadratic space with
q
of corank
1
. We denote the spinor sheaves corresponding to the maximal isotropic subspace
W_{\text{max}}\subset V
, for
V
odd and even dimensional, by
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
and
\mathcal{S}
, respectively. We will refer to them as
“the” spinor sheaves
on the nodal quadric
Y=V(q)
.

Remark 3.9
.

In general, the dimension of the maximal isotropic subspace
W_{\text{max}}\subset V
is given by

d_{\mathrm{max}}=\left\lfloor\frac{\dim(V/K)}{2}\right\rfloor+\dim(K)=\left\lfloor\frac{\dim(V)+\dim(K)}{2}\right\rfloor.

In the remainder of this subsection, we classify all spinor sheaves on a nodal quadric
Y
, up to isomorphism. The key ingredient is the following result which realizes the them as extensions of one another.

Proposition 3.10

(
[
1
, Proposition 3.3 and 3.4]
)
.

Let
(V,q)
be a quadratic space, let
W\subset V
be an isotropic subspace and let
W^{\prime}\subset W
denote a codimension
1
subspace. Then there exist exact sequences

(3.10.1)

Clifford algebras and Morita reduction

In this subsection we will further analyze the structure of the even Clifford Algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)
, for a general nontrivial quadratic space
(V,q)
with

is

\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)
-modules

and
S
, for

odd or even dimensional, respectively. In this subsection the goal is to compute the corresponding Ext-complexes of Theorem
1.2
for the simple left
\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)
-modules

and

in low dimensions.

Since we are working over an algebraically closed field
k
with
\characteris(k)\neq 2
, the quadratic form can be written as

. For such a quadratic form we will use the notation

where

\langle 0,\dots,0\rangle
is a totally degenerate quadratic space of dimension

denotes their orthogonal sum. On the level of the corresponding Clifford algebra, a splitting into orthogonal sums has the following effect: Let
(V,q)
,
(V^{\prime},q^{\prime})
be two arbitrary quadratic spaces. Then there exists an isomorphism of graded
k
-algebras

\mathrm{Cl}(q\perp q^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Cl}(q)\ \widehat{\otimes}\ \mathrm{Cl}(q^{\prime}),

see
[
27

k
-algebras we refer to
[
27
, Ch. IV,§2]
.
Analogously, one can verify a similar result for the (ungraded) even part of the Clifford algebra of an orthogonal sum of quadratic forms.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
27
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

Let
(V,q)
be an odd dimensional nondegenerate quadratic space. Then for any quadratic space
(V^{\prime},q^{\prime})
there exists an isomorphism of

-algebras

\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q\perp q^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}(-q^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\}
denote an orthogonal basis for the odd dimensional quadratic space
(V,q)
and set

. A straightforward computation shows that

lies in the center of
\mathrm{Cl}(q)
and the claim follows from
[
27
, Theorem 2.8]
.
∎

Corollary 3.12.1
.

Let
(V^{\prime},q^{\prime})
be a quadratic space with

. Then we obtain an isomorphism of (ungraded)

-algebras

(3.12.1)

\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(\langle 1\rangle\perp q^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Cl}(-q^{\prime}).

The statement follows from the previous proposition. For an explicit isomorphism we refer to

.
∎

Recall that the

Proposition 3.26

(

\mathcal{A}_{Y}
and the category
D^{b}(\mathrm{Cl}_{0}(q))

. Moreover, let
H
and
h
be pullbacks of classes of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}(V)
and
\mathbb{P}^{4}
, respectively. Then we have the following relations in
\text{Pic}(\widetilde{X})
:

Y=2h-D,\ H=3h-D,\ h=H-Y,\ D=2H-3Y,\ K_{\widetilde{X}}=-5h+D=-3H+2Y.

Proof.

For the first two claims, we refer to
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
for a detailed proof. This reference provides a proof in the case of a nodal variety
X
which works analogously in the cuspidal case, essentially because both singularities can be resolved by one blow-up and they both have multiplicity
2
. For the relations in the Picard group
\picard(\widetilde{X})
, we first note that the right blow-up diagram yields
K_{\widetilde{X}}=-5h+D
. The other relation containing the canonical divisor
K_{\widetilde{X}}
follows from Lemma
2.4
, where we proved that the discrepancy of the cusp is
\dim(X)-2=2
.
Since
\sigma
is the extension of the projection away from the cuspidal point
x\in X
to the blow-up
\widetilde{X}
, the relation
h=H-Y
holds.
Finally, note that the proper transform (with respect to
\sigma
) of the quadric
\sigma\circ j(Y)\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
passing through
S
is contracted by
\pi
. Therefore we have an equation
Y=2h-D
. The other relations follow by substitution from those we have proved.
∎

By Proposition

and Lemma

there exists an admissible semiorthogonal decomposition

(5.1.1)

D^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(H),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2H)\rangle,

where the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}
denotes the Kuznetsov component of
X
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since the relations in
\picard(\widetilde{X})
in Lemma
5.1
are central for this proof and they coincide with the analogous relations in the
A_{1}
case, the proof of Theorem
1.4
for
A_{1}
singularities, see
[
23
, Theorem 5.2]
, generalizes to the
A_{2}
case without substantial changes. Below we will provide a sketch of the proof and refer to
[

, Theorem 5.2]
for details.

By Theorem
4.1
, there exists a crepant categorical resolution
(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}},\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
together with
a semiorthogonal decomposition

(5.1.2)

D^{b}(\widetilde{X})=\langle j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-2h),j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-h),\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle.

Since the functor
\pi^{*}\colon D^{\lilperf}(X)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is fully faithful, we can use (
5.1.1
) to produce a semiorthogonal decomposition

(5.1.3)

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}=\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(H),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(2H)\rangle,

where we define

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}:=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(H),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(2H)\rangle^{\bot}.

This refines (
5.1.2
) in the following way:

(5.1.4)

D^{b}(\widetilde{X})=\langle j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-2h),j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-h),\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(H),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(2H)\rangle.

By the definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
and the fact that
\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\text{perf}}(X)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is fully faithful, we have that
\pi_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X})\subset\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\lilperf})\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
, which implies that
(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}},\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
restricts to a crepant categorical resolution
(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X},\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
of the Kuznetsov component
\mathcal{A}_{X}
.

The equivalence
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}\cong D^{b}(S)
can be constructed as follows. By an application of Orlov’s blow-up formula to
\sigma\colon\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{4}
, we obtain a semiorthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(\widetilde{X})=\langle\Phi(D^{b}(S)),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-3h),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2h),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-h),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(h)\rangle,

where
\Phi(\mathcal{F})=\eta_{*}q^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(D)
for
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}(S)
. Then one applies a series of mutations, see
[
23
, Theorem 5.2]
, to the decomposition (
5.1.5
) to obtain a semiorthogonal decomposition

(5.1.6)

\Phi^{\prime\prime}=\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-h)}\circ\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2h)}\circ\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(D-2h)}\circ\eta_{*}\circ q^{*}.

Here,
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(D-2h)}
denotes the functor defined by tensoring with the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{{\widetilde{X}}(D-2h)}
. Comparing the semiorthogonal decomposition (

) with (

), we conclude that the functor
\Phi^{\prime\prime}\colon D^{b}(S)\to D^{b}(\widetilde{X})
induces an equivalence of triangulated categories
D^{b}(S)\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{X}
.

Semileptonic decay and form factors of Ω b − → Ω c 0 ​ e ​ ν e ¯ \Omega_{b}^{-}\rightarrow\Omega_{c}^{0}\,e\,\bar{\nu_{e}}
Kinjal Patel, Kaushal Thakkar

Abstract
We investigate the heavy-to-heavy semileptonic decay Ω b − → Ω c 0 ​ e ​ ν e ¯ \Omega_{b}^{-}\rightarrow\Omega_{c}^{0}\,e\,\bar{\nu_{e}} within the framework of the Hypercentral Constituent Quark Model (HCQM). The ground state masses of the involved baryons are evaluated by numerically solving the six-dimensional hyperradial Schrödinger equation, incorporating both hyper-Coulomb and linear confinement potentials along with spin-dependent interactions. The Heavy Quark Effective Field Theory (HQET) form factors are computed up to the subleading order, incorporating 1 / m Q 1/m_{Q} corrections that account for finite mass effects beyond the heavy quark symmetry limit. These form factors are then employed to analyse the heavy-to-heavy semileptonic decay rate via helicity formalism. Our results for the decay width and branching ratio are compared with those from various theoretical approaches.

Introduction

The investigation of weak decays of heavy-flavoured hadrons has played a crucial role in the construction and verification of the Standard Model (SM). In particular, semileptonic decays of heavy-flavoured baryons provide valuable insights into their weak transition dynamics. Compared to mesonic decays, baryonic semileptonic decays offer additional information about the dynamics of heavy quarks bound in a three-body system. Till date, more than 60 singly heavy baryon channels have been experimentally observed in the charm and bottom sectors
pdg2024
. These experimental discoveries have motivated a wide range of theoretical studies for understanding their structure and decay properties. On the theoretical side, properties such as mass spectra, Regge trajectories, electromagnetic properties and weak decays of heavy baryons have been extensively investigated within various theoretical approaches. The study of semileptonic decays of heavy baryons provides an essential window into the nonperturbative regime of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). In particular, transitions governed by the
b\rightarrow c
quark process allow precise tests of Heavy Quark Effective Theory (HQET) and offer an independent determination of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix element
|V_{cb}|
.

Among the bottom baryons, the
\Lambda_{b}
and
\Xi_{b}
have been the most extensively studied both experimentally and theoretically, serving as a benchmark for understanding weak transitions of heavy baryons. At present, the
\Lambda_{b}
baryon remains the only heavy baryon whose semileptonic decay has been measured experimentally. In contrast, the investigations of weak decays of heavy-flavoured baryons containing two strange quarks remain rather limited. The weak semileptonic channel
\Omega_{b}\rightarrow\Omega_{c}\ell\bar{\nu_{\ell}}
has not been observed yet. The presence of two strange quarks in
\Omega_{b}
gives rise to unique flavour and spin correlations, distinguishing the
\Omega_{b}
from other bottom baryons and making its semileptonic transitions particularly important for testing theoretical models. Notably, the LHCb collaboration has already observed the production of the
\Omega_{b}
baryon and its mass spectrum, indicating that the study of its weak decays may become feasible in future experiments.

The
\Omega_{b}
baryon was first observed at the Tevatron, where the
D\emptyset

CDF

\Omega_{b}^{-}\rightarrow J/\psi\Omega^{-}

M_{\Omega_{b}^{-}}=6045.8\pm 0.8~\text{MeV}
and its lifetime as
\tau_{\Omega_{b}^{-}}=(1.64\pm 0.16)~\text{ps}
. These measurements provide important benchmarks for testing quark models and effective field theories, as the
\Omega_{b}
is the only singly bottom baryon containing two strange quarks. Understanding its decay properties is therefore essential not only for completing the spectroscopy of bottom baryons but also for extracting information about the underlying quark dynamics. The semileptonic transition of
\Omega_{b}
baryons is studied using various theoretical models, such as QCD sum rule

5
Results and discussions

The masses of the baryons are obtained by numerically solving the six-dimensional Schrödinger equation in HCQM with a potential consisting of a hyper-Coulomb term and a linear confining term. Spin-dependent interactions were included perturbatively to account for hyperfine splitting. The hypercentral potential expressed in Eq. (
10
) is advantageous as it naturally incorporates three-body effects arising from the non-abelian gluon self-couplings in QCD, which manifest as genuine three-body forces in baryons. Thus, the hyper-Coulomb plus linear potential captures both the short-range one-gluon exchange dynamics and the three-body confinement. This potential has been widely applied in baryon spectroscopy, successfully reproducing the ground state and excited state baryon masses, magnetic moments, radiative decays and semileptonic transitions.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have studied the semileptonic decay process
\Omega_{b}^{-}\rightarrow\Omega_{c}^{0}\,e\,\bar{\nu_{e}}
within the framework of the Hypercentral Constituent Quark Model (HCQM). The six-dimensional Schrödinger equation was numerically solved using a hyper-Coulomb plus linear confining potential, including spin-dependent interactions perturbatively. The obtained ground state masses of the initial and final baryons show good agreement with experimental values, validating the reliability of the HCQM for describing heavy baryon systems. The Isgur-Wise function and its slope and curvature parameters were extracted from the hyperradial wavefunction, which were subsequently used to compute the six independent form factors
F_{i}
and
G_{i}
at the subleading order of Heavy Quark Effective Theory (HQET). The helicity amplitudes were obtained using these form factors to further evaluate the heavy-to-heavy semileptonic decay rate of
\Omega_{b}^{-}
baryon. The branching ratios are also computed and compared with different approaches.

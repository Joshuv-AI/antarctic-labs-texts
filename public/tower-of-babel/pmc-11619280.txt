Correlation between triglyceride-glucose index and atrial fibrillation in acute coronary syndrome patients: a retrospective cohort study and the establishment of a LASSO-Logistic regression model
Authors: Chenglong Yao, Yuan Qin, Xuhe Yan, Zijian Zhao, Bingfu Wang, Yizhen Bai, Tianwang Zhang, Yazhu Hou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInsulin resistance (IR) is an independent predictor of atrial fibrillation (AF), but the specific utility of the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index as a predictive marker for the incidence of AF in the acute coronary syndrome (ACS) population has not yet been explored.ObjectiveTo explore the correlation between TyG index and the risk of AF in ACS patients and to establish a predictive model.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted on 613 ACS patients admitted to the Department of Cardiovascular Medicine at the First Teaching Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2022 to September 2024. Patients were divided into four groups based on quartiles of TyG index. Patients were further divided into two groups based on the occurrence of AF: the AF group and the non-AF group. Patient information was collected through the hospital's HIS system. Variable selection was completed using LASSO regression algorithms. Multivariate logistic bidirectional stepwise regression analysis was used to explore the correlation between the TyG index and the risk of AF in ACS patients and to construct a regression model. Three different models were constructed by adjusting for confounding factors and restricted cubic spline plots were drawn to validate the significance of the TyG index combined with AF further. The predictive value of the LASSO-multivariate logistic bidirectional stepwise regression model and the TyG index alone for predicting AF in ACS patients was analyzed using the receiver operating characteristic curve.ResultsThe LASSO-multivariate logistic bidirectional stepwise regression algorithm showed that coronary heart disease (CHD), valvular heart disease (VHD), TyG, age (AGE), and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were risk factors for AF in ACS. The restricted cubic spline model demonstrated a significant linear relationship between a higher TyG index and an increased risk of AF in the ACS patient population. The area under the curve (AUC) for predicting AF in ACS patients using the TyG index and the LASSO-multivariate logistic bidirectional stepwise regression model was 0.65(95%CI = 0.58 ~ 0.73) and 0.71(95%CI = 0.65 ~ 0.77) respectively. Additionally, the correlation between the TyG index and AF was consistent across different subgroups.ConclusionIn ACS patients, the TyG index is a stable and independent predictor of AF, with specific clinical value in identifying the occurrence of AF in this population.Keywords: Acute Coronary Syndrome, Atrial Fibrillation, Triglyceride-Glucose Index, Insulin Resistance, LASSO Regression

Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) is a group of clinical syndromes caused by acute myocardial ischemia, including ST-elevation myocardial infarction, non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction, and unstable angina [1]. According to statistics, more than 7 million people worldwide are diagnosed with ACS each year, and ACS patients have a higher risk of complications and mortality [2, 3]. Atrial Fibrillation (AF) is one of the most common complications of ACS and is associated with poor prognosis in ACS patients, notably increasing the risk of mortality in these patients [4–7]. Studies have shown that AF is an independent factor influencing hospitalization and 6-month mortality in ACS patients, with a 46% higher mortality rate in MI patients with AF compared to those without AF [8, 9]. Therefore, assessing the individualized risk of AF in ACS patients and identifying predictive factors is crucial for reducing mortality and other adverse outcomes in ACS patients.

Increasing evidence suggests that insulin resistance (IR) plays a significant role in the development and pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases. High levels of IR not only increase the risk of coronary heart disease but also lead to the occurrence of adverse cardiovascular events [10, 11]. The “gold standard” for diagnosing IR, the hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp, is limited in clinical application due to its complexity and time consumption. However, the Triglyceride-Glucose (TyG) index has been proven to be a reliable alternative biomarker of IR and an independent predictor of cardiovascular outcomes [12, 13]. In the absence of diabetes and cardiovascular risk factors, high TyG index levels can independently predict coronary anatomical lesions and adverse events in ACS patients [1, 14, 15]. Moreover, the TyG index is an independent risk factor for AF in hospitalized non-diabetic patients [16], and previous studies have shown that the TyG index is an independent predictor of AF after radiofrequency catheter ablation, after percutaneous coronary intervention for ST-elevation myocardial infarction, and after surgical myectomy for hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy [17–19]. However, its specific utility in predicting the risk of AF in the ACS population has not yet been explored.

Therefore, we conducted a retrospective cohort study to investigate the correlation between the TyG index and AF in ACS patients, strengthen AF preventive strategies in this population, reduce adverse cardiovascular events, and provide predictive value for the prognosis of ACS patients.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

A retrospective selection of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patients admitted to the Department of Cardiovascular Medicine at the First Affiliated Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2022 to September 2024. This includes patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), and unstable angina (UA).

Diagnostic criteria

The diagnostic criteria for acute coronary syndrome (including STEMI, NSTEMI, and UA) were established by the “2020 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Acute Coronary Syndromes in Patients Presenting without Persistent ST-segment Elevation” [20] and the “2019 Guidelines for the Emergency Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Coronary Syndrome” [21]. The criteria are as follows: (1) ST-elevation myocardial infarction: chest pain duration > 30 min, ECG showing ST-segment elevation, creatine kinase MB (CK-MB) value more than twice the average value, cardiac troponin I (cTnI) or cTnT positive; (2) Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction: persistent chest pain, no ST segment elevation on ECG, transient or new ST segment depression or flat, inverted T waves, CK-MB elevated more than twice the upper limit of the reference value, cTnT or cTnI positive; (3) Unstable angina: chest pain, ECG findings similar to non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction, CK-MB may be elevated but less than twice the upper limit of normal, cTnT and cTnI negative; (4) Evidence of myocardial ischemia: symptoms of myocardial ischemia, new ischemic ECG changes, new pathological Q waves, coronary angiography, imaging studies suggesting new findings consistent with the current ischemia.

Inclusion criteria

(1) Age 18–80 years, gender not limited; (2) Meets the diagnostic criteria for acute coronary syndrome as defined by the “2020 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Acute Coronary Syndromes in Patients Presenting without Persistent ST-segment Elevation” and the “2019 Guidelines for the Emergency Diagnosis and Treatment of Acute Coronary Syndrome.”

Exclusion criteria

(1) Failure to Meet Inclusion Criteria: Patients who do not meet the inclusion criteria specified above; (2) Severe Comorbidities: Patients with advanced liver disease (e.g., cirrhosis, Child–Pugh class C) or significant renal impairment (e.g., eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 or requiring dialysis); (3) Inflammatory or Rheumatic Diseases: Patients with active systemic inflammatory conditions, such as infections or autoimmune diseases (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis), that may affect insulin resistance (IR) or the TyG index; (4) Incomplete Data: Patients lacking essential clinical, laboratory, or imaging records required for the study's analysis.

All patients in this study have been informed and have signed informed consent forms. The study protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Teaching Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine.

Data collection

Using the HIS data system, we retrospectively collected information on patients who met the diagnostic criteria for acute myocardial infarction and the inclusion/exclusion criteria from January 2022 to September 2024 at our hospital. The collected patient information includes: (1) Demographic data: Gender (GENDER), Preliminary Diagnosis (IMP), Age (AGE), Heart Rate (HR), Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP), Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP), Hospitalization Days (DAY), Hospitalization Expenses (FEE); (2) Comorbidities: Premature Cardiovascular Disease (CVD), Coronary heart disease (CHD), Atrial Fibrillation (AF), Chronic Heart Failure (CHF), Valvular heart disease (VHD), Ischemic Cerebrovascular Disease (ICVD),Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), Anaemia, Peptic Ulcer (PU), Malignant Tumor (CA); (3) Laboratory examinations: Troponin I (TNI), B-type Natriuretic Peptide (BNP), Total Cholesterol (TC), Triglycerides (TG), High-density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDLC), Low-density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDLC), Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG), Triglyceride-Glucose Index (TyG), Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF), D-Dimer (DD2), Myoglobin (MyO), Creatine Kinase MB (CKMB), Prothrombin Time (PT), Lactate (LAC), Total Bilirubin (TBIL), Direct Bilirubin (DBIL), Urea (UREA), Uric Acid (UA).

Calculation formula for TyG index: TyG index = ln [TG(mg/dL) × FPG(mg/dL)/2].

Definitions and standards

CVD

The diagnostic criteria are according to the “ 2016 European Guidelines on Cardiovascular Disease Prevention in Clinical Practice” [22]: cardiovascular disease occurs in males under 55 and females under 65.

CHD

The diagnostic criteria are by the “2018 Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Stable Coronary Heart Disease” [23] and the “2019 ACC/AHA Guideline on the Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease: Executive Summary” [24]. A history of or findings during hospitalization of coronary CT or coronary angiography showing at least one major coronary artery with stenosis ≥ 50%.

AF

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “ 2020 ESC Guidelines for the diagnosis and management of atrial fibrillation developed in collaboration with the European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (EACTS)” [25]. A 12-lead ECG recording or ≥ 30 s of single-lead ECG indicating atrial fibrillation rhythm, characterized by no identifiable P waves and irregular RR intervals.

New-onset AF

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “ 2020 ESC Guidelines for the diagnosis and management of atrial fibrillation developed in collaboration with the European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (EACTS)” [25]. Atrial fibrillation that has not been previously diagnosed, regardless of its duration or the presence/absence of AF-related symptoms or severity.

Pre-existing AF

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “ 2020 ESC Guidelines for the diagnosis and management of atrial fibrillation developed in collaboration with the European Association for Cardio-Thoracic Surgery (EACTS)” [25]. Atrial fibrillation that was diagnosed or present prior to the current hospitalization. This type of atrial fibrillation may be paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent.

CHF

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “2021 ESC Guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of acute and chronic heart failure” [26]. Presence of symptoms (shortness of breath, fatigue, weakness, etc.) or signs (heart murmurs, lung rales, lower limb oedema, etc.) of heart failure and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 40%.

VHD

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “2021 ESC/EACTS Guidelines for the management of valvular heart disease” [27]. Echocardiography determines the presence of moderate or severe valvular stenosis or regurgitation.

ICVD

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “2021 Guideline for the Prevention of Stroke in Patients With Stroke and Transient Ischemic Attack” [28]. Cranial CT or MRI has confirmed that all patients have evidence of ischemic cerebrovascular disease.

COPD

The diagnostic criteria are “ Management of COPD exacerbations: a European Respiratory Society/American Thoracic Society guideline” [29]. Chronic cough and sputum production for at least 2 consecutive years, with each episode lasting at least 3 months. Pulmonary function tests show evidence of airflow obstruction.

CKD

The diagnostic criteria are the “ KDIGO 2021 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Blood Pressure in Chronic Kidney Disease” [30]. The presence of kidney injury markers and a decline in GFR for more than 3 months are indicative of the disease.

Anaemia

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “ British Society of Gastroenterology guidelines for managing iron deficiency anaemia in adults “ [31]. Diagnosis is based on complete blood count (CBC) and tests that show the size and shape of red blood cells.

Peptic ulcer

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “ Evidence-based clinical practice guidelines for peptic ulcer disease 2020” [32]. Pain is often described as burning, gnawing, or hunger-like. The course of the disease is characterized by chronicity and recurrence. Diagnosis can be suggested by history and confirmed by endoscopic examination.

Malignant tumor

The diagnostic criteria are as follows: “Adult Cancer Pain, Version 3.2019, NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology” [33]. Signs are present on X-ray, ultrasound, or computed tomography (CT) scans, and biopsy confirms diagnosis.

Patient selection

From January 2022 to September 2024, patients with acute coronary syndrome (including STEMI, NSTEMI, UA) admitted to the Department of Cardiovascular Medicine at the First Affiliated Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine were selected (n = 823). Patients with incomplete information on triglycerides (TG) and fasting plasma glucose (FPG) levels within 48 h of hospitalization were excluded (n = 54). Excluding individuals under 18 years of age or over 80 years old (n = 72), those with severe liver or kidney diseases (n = 16), patients with diseases that may affect IR or TyG index such as active inflammation or rheumatic diseases(n = 3), Patients with multiple hospitalizations, retaining only the first admission information, were excluded (n = 65). A total of 613 patients were included in the cohort analysis, as shown in Fig. 1.

Grouping

Patients with ACS were divided into four groups based on the quartiles of their TyG index: Q1 (TyG index ≤ 1.10) with 152 individuals, accounting for 24.80%; Q2 (1.10 < TyG index ≤ 1.64) with 154 individuals, accounting for 25.12%; Q3 (1.64 < TyG index ≤ 2.25) with 154 individuals, accounting for 25.12%; and Q4 (TyG index > 2.25) with 153 individuals, accounting for 24.96%.

Patients with CVD, CHD, AF, CHF, VHD, ICVD, COPD, CKD, anaemia, PU, and CA were divided into two groups based on the presence of comorbidities (absent = 0, present = 1).

Divided into three groups based on types of AF: no AF, new-onset AF, and pre-existing AF (No AF = 0, New-onset AF = 1, Pre-existing AF = 2).

They were also grouped by biological sex into two groups (female = 1, male = 2), and the initial diagnosis of IMP was grouped based on admission diagnosis (STEMI = 3, NSTEMI = 2, UA = 1).

Statistical methods

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0, R 4.3.3 statistical software, and zstats statistical software. Normality tests were conducted using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K-S) test. Measurement data that conformed to a normal distribution were expressed as (mean ± standard deviation) and compared between two groups using independent sample t-tests. Measurement data that did not conform to a normal distribution were expressed as M (P25, P75) and compared between groups using non-parametric rank sum tests. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages (%) and were compared between groups using Fisher’s exact test or Pearson’s chi-square test.

A LASSO-multivariate logistic bidirectional stepwise regression model was used to select variables in the regression equation. Firstly, all variables were incorporated into LASSO regression for variable selection, followed by binary multivariate logistic regression analysis for bidirectional stepwise regression. The final aim was to determine the correlation between different TyG indices and the risk of atrial fibrillation (AF) in ACS patients, to establish a regression model using restricted cubic splines (RCS) for assessment and to plot the RCS graph of the correlation between TyG index and the risk of AF in ACS patients. ROC curves for the Various core influencing factors and the LASSO-multivariate logistic bidirectional stepwise regression model were drawn to evaluate the predictive value of the TyG index for the occurrence of AF in ACS patients. In subgroup analyses, the stability of the correlation between the TyG index and AF in ACS patients was tested based on gender (male, female), Premature Cardiovascular Disease (yes, no), coronary heart disease (yes, no), chronic heart failure (yes, no), peripheral arterial disease (yes, no), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (yes, no), chronic kidney disease (yes, no), anaemia (yes, no), peptic ulcer (yes, no), malignant tumour (yes, no), age (< 65 years, ≥ 65 years), and heart rate (< 75 bpm, ≥ 75 bpm). All reported probability values were two-tailed, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Multiple imputation (missForest R) was used for data with a missing rate of less than 25% to address missing values. Indicators with a missing rate exceeding 25% were excluded to reduce errors caused by missing values.

Results

Basic information

Of the 613 ACS patients, there were 446 males (72.76%) and 167 females (27.24%). The median age was 63.00 years (54.00, 71.00). There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in terms of gender, CHD, ICVD, COPD, CKD, Anemia, PU, CA, TNI, BNP, LDL-C, LVEF, FEE, AGE, DBP, DD2, MYO, CKMB, TBIL (P > 0.05). The levels of AF, CHF, CVD, IMP-3, TC, TG, FPG, HR, SBP, PT, LAC, UREA, and UA were higher in the Q3 and Q4 groups compared to the Q1 and Q2 groups. The levels of VHD, IMP-1, IMP-2, HDL-C, DAY, and DBIL were lower in the Q3 and Q4 groups compared to the Q1 and Q2 groups, and these differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), as shown in Table 1.

Comparison of baseline characteristics among four groups of patients

By analyzing baseline characteristics, it can be observed that there is a correlation between the TyG index and the occurrence of atrial fibrillation in ACS patients; therefore, we further investigated the specific relationship between the two.

LASSO regression variable selection

To construct a regression equation for predicting the incidence of AF in ACS patients with different TyG indices, and to avoid multicollinearity and overfitting among different variables, the occurrence of atrial fibrillation was used as the outcome variable noAF = 0,withAF = 1, with 70 individuals in the AF group and 543 in the non-AF group. Initially, all indicators were included in the Lasso regression for variable selection.The parameter settings were: family = “binomial”, nlambda = 100, alpha = 1. As shown in Fig. 2, through cross-validation, when λ = 0.01539615, i.e., lambda. min, 13 variables with non-zero coefficients were selected: GENDER, CHD, VHD, IMP, TNI, TC, TyG, AGE, DBP, CKMB, PT, UREA and UA.

LASSO regression analysis of the occurrence of AF in patients with ACS

Construction of a multivariate logistic regression model for factors influencing AF incidence in ACS patients

Whether ACS patients developed AF served as the dependent variable, and the 13 variables with non-zero coefficients selected by LASSO regression were included as independent variables in the multivariate logistic regression analysis (the assignment of related variables is shown in Table 2). After bidirectional stepwise regression, 5 variables were ultimately included. The results showed that the following factors were associated with an increased risk of AF in ACS patients: concurrent CHD (OR = 1.58,95%CI = 0.92 ~ 2.70, P = 0.096), concurrent VHD(OR = 2.67,95%CI = 1.33 ~ 5.38, P = 0.006), elevated TyG (OR = 2.02,95%CI = 1.51 ~ 2.71, P < 0.001), increased AGE(OR = 1.02,95%CI = 1.00 ~ 1.04, P = 0.100), and increased DBP (OR = 1.01,95%CI = 1.00 ~ 1.03, P = 0.151). Details are shown in Table 3, Figs. 2, and 3.

Discussion

AF is one of the most common complications in ACS patients [6]. It is associated with an increased risk of various adverse outcomes in ACS patients, including a 1.5 to twofold increase in mortality risk, a 2.4-fold increase in stroke risk, and a fivefold increase in heart failure risk [7, 34, 35]. It is currently a significant global burden of cardiovascular disease [36]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore new biomarkers to identify ACS patients at high risk of developing AF and improve their prognosis. This study is the first retrospective investigation into the relationship between the TyG index and the risk of AF in ACS patients. Our results indicate that the TyG index is a robust and independent predictor of AF in ACS patients. Moreover, the predictive model demonstrated a better ability to predict new-onset AF compared to pre-existing AF. Additionally, the significant association between the TyG index and AF occurrence was further validated after adjusting for confounding factors. Subgroup analyses also confirmed that this conclusion holds across genders, ages, heart rates, and patients with comorbid CVD or CHD, with no significant interactions. Additionally, we conducted a multivariate logistic regression analysis, which showed that CHD, VHD, TyG index, AGE, and DBP are risk factors for the development of AF in ACS patients.

IR is defined as a reduced response of tissues to insulin stimulation, characterized by defects in glucose uptake and oxidation, reduced glycogen synthesis, and impaired insulin cellular signalling, leading to glucose metabolism and endothelial dysfunction. This accelerates atherosclerosis, to which the majority of cardiovascular events can be attributed [10]. Based on this, previous studies have demonstrated that IR is a metabolic disorder significantly associated with the occurrence and progression of cardiovascular diseases [10, 11, 37]. To facilitate a more convenient and repeatable assessment of IR in clinical settings, the TyG index, which has been proven to be a reliable alternative biomarker of IR [38], has been introduced. Increasing evidence also suggests that the TyG index is significantly associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular events [39–43] and serves as a reliable predictor of in-hospital mortality in critically ill cardiac patients, including those with heart failure, arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, acute coronary syndrome, valvular diseases, and cardiomyopathy [44]. For instance, IR may affect the prognosis of ACS patients by increasing the complexity of coronary lesions, being related to their long-term adverse outcomes [45], and predicting the prognosis of ACS patients independently of other cardiovascular risk factors such as hypertension, visceral obesity, or dyslipidemia [46]. Additionally, studies have shown that IR induces atrial structural remodelling and abnormal intracellular calcium homeostasis, ultimately leading to the development of atrial fibrillation (AF), and is an independent predictor of AF onset [47–49]. For example, the TyG index is an independent predictor of AF after radiofrequency catheter ablation in AF patients, after percutaneous coronary intervention in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction, and after septal myectomy in patients with hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy [17–19, 50]. In summary, we found that the TyG index is associated with the prognosis of ACS patients and is a risk factor for the development of AF. However, interestingly, in the SPRINT (Systolic et al. Trial) [51], populations without AF or primary diabetes [52], and one cohort study [53], researchers arrived at the opposite conclusion, considering IR unrelated to AF. Therefore, our study, through regression analysis of AF occurrence in 613 ACS patients, demonstrated that the TyG index is an independent risk factor for AF in ACS patients. This provides supportive evidence for more related research and also hopes to serve as a risk prediction tool for assessing the prognosis of such populations and preventing and reducing adverse events.

Since the proportion of AF in ACS patients is high and AF is associated with poor prognosis [7], it is hoped that exploring the pathophysiological mechanisms between the two can enable early identification of the risk of AF in ACS populations and implement management strategies to prevent adverse outcomes. Currently, some studies focus on investigating the predictive factors for AF in ACS patients, while others aim to uncover the association between the two through exploring their pathophysiological mechanisms. Some studies have reported that acute or previous MI patients have a higher risk of AF [54]. This bidirectional association may be partially attributed to common risk factors such as age, gender, hypertension, obesity, and diabetes [55], as well as potential common pathogenic mechanisms such as inflammation, coronary embolism, and increased myocardial oxygen demand [56]. Moreover, from a genetic susceptibility perspective, Mendelian randomization study results also support the association between coronary artery disease and susceptibility to AF, and vice versa [57]. Through exploration, it has been identified that valuable predictive factors for AF occurrence after ACS include the platelet-to-albumin ratio (PAR) [58], the uric acid-to-albumin ratio (UAR) [59], the serum fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) [60], the Syntax Score II [61], and the PRECISE-DAPT score [62]. The possible pathophysiological mechanisms include, firstly, ischemia in the atria or left ventricular dysfunction [56]. Initially, atrial ischemia after MI leads to myocardial cell death and replacement by fibrotic tissue, which disrupts atrial electrical conduction and results in AF [63]. Secondly, ischemia can lead to ventricular remodelling with ventricular wall thinning and left ventricular dysfunction, which induces AF through secondary processes such as left atrial dilation, neurohumoral regulation, and atrial ion channel remodelling [64]. Second, both ACS and AF are associated with inflammation [65–67]. Inflammatory cells and mediators participate in the formation and rupture of atherosclerotic plaques [68] and lead to atrial electrical conduction heterogeneity through atrial fibrosis, myocardial cell apoptosis, and myolysis [69], resulting in AF complicating ACS. If AF occurs in ACS patients, selecting an appropriate treatment method is crucial. Catheter ablation (CA) has been proven to be an effective treatment for AF or AF complicating severe cardiovascular diseases [25, 70, 71]. Additionally, studies have shown that patients with coronary artery disease who develop AF should undergo coronary artery treatment combined with CA simultaneously [72]. Current clinical treatment recommendations for ACS patients mainly focus on cardiac electrical cardioversion or heart rate management for hemodynamically unstable patients [73], as well as antithrombotic therapy [74], to prevent the occurrence of AF and the more severe adverse events it can bring. This study provides robust evidence for the early identification of AF risk in ACS populations. By evaluating the individualized risk of AF in ACS patients through the TyG index, it enables early prevention and intervention for populations susceptible to AF.

Additionally, through multivariate logistic regression analysis, we found that CHD (coronary heart disease), VHD (cardiovascular disease), AGE, and DBP (Diastolic Blood Pressur) are also risk factors for the development of AF in ACS patients. According to the investigation, it was found that the number of patients with CHD and AF is increasing globally, with incidence rates on the rise, and these two diseases often coexist. Approximately 6%-15% of CHD patients will develop AF [75]. This population exhibits left ventricular myocardial hypertrophy and dilation, and is in a state of chronic ischemia and hypoxia, which affects cardiac conduction, leading to abnormal loops, reentry, focal ectopic activity, and neural remodeling, ultimately resulting in AF [76–78]. Consistent with our study, other research has also demonstrated that CHD is one of the significant risk factors for the onset of AF [79]. With degenerative and rheumatic lesions as the main features, patients with VHD experience increased cardiac burden due to valvular stenosis or regurgitation, which progresses to atrial enlargement and cardiac insufficiency, ultimately leading to the induction of AF [80]. Therefore, the most commonly associated arrhythmia with VHD is AF. Previous studies have shown that age is an independent risk factor for AF [81]. However, the specific mechanism by which age increases the risk of AF is still unclear. Zathar Z and others believe it may be related to the “anatomical matrix” produced by cardiac aging, ultimately leading to AF [82]. It is worth noting that through LASSO multifactorial logistic regression analysis, we have identified that diastolic blood pressure DBP is a risk factor for the occurrence of AF in patients with acute coronary syndrome ACS(OR = 1.01, 95%CI = 1.00 ~ 1.03, P = 0.151). However, the odds ratio OR is close to 1, and this may be related to the U-shaped relationship between DBP and all-cause mortality in AF patients [83, 84]. Relevant studies have shown that an admission DBP that is too low DBP (DBP < 60) is a predictor of 1-year mortality in patients with acute myocardial infarction [85]. Therefore, we cannot view DBP as simply a risk factor for AF in ACS patients; both high and low blood pressure may lead to adverse events. Further research is needed to stratify blood pressure for more in-depth investigation.

This study still has certain limitations. First, it is a single-centre study, and there may be biases in the enrollment of patients. Second, this study categorized AF into new-onset AF and pre-existing AF; however, due to the inherent recall bias of retrospective studies and the absence of Holter monitoring for all patients (with a missing rate exceeding 25%), it was not possible to fully determine the specific AF types (paroxysmal, persistent, or permanent). Additionally, the relatively small sample size in each AF group may limit the stability and generalizability of the model. Third, there is a lack of time points for the onset of new AF, which has hindered further analysis of survival curves and other data. Fourth, the AUC value of the predictive model in this study is only at a level of clinical discrimination, and its predictive power needs to be further tested in an external model. In summary, future studies should involve multicenter, large-scale sample data and more comprehensive continuous monitoring methods, along with clear AF classification criteria, to enhance the accuracy and clinical significance of predictive models and further validate the predictive value of the TyG index for AF occurrence.

Conclusion

In summary, we retrospectively studied 613 patients with acute coronary syndrome with ACS who were hospitalized in the Department of Cardiovascular Medicine of the First Affiliated Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2022 to September 2024, and demonstrated that the TyG index was a stable and independent predictor of AF occurrence in patients with ACS through the LASSO-multivariate logistic two-way stepwise regression model. It is hoped that in the future, the TyG index can be used to quickly assess the risk of AF and prevent the occurrence of related adverse events.

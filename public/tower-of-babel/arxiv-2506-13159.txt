Resonant dynamics of dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model with time-dependent tensor electric fields
Jiali Zhang, Shaoliang Zhang

Abstract
Recently, tensor gauge fields and their coupling to fracton phases of matter have attracted more and more research interest, and a series of novel quantum phenomena arising from the coupling has been predicted. In this article, we propose a theoretical scheme to construct a time-dependent rank-2 tensor electric field by introducing a periodically driving quadratic potential in a dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model, and investigate the dynamics of dipole and fracton excitations when the drive frequency is resonant with the on-site interaction. We find that the dynamics are dominated by the splitting of large dipoles with the photon-assisted correlated tunneling and the movement of small dipoles, both of which can be well controlled by the drive amplitude. Our work provides a possible approach for engineering the dynamics of dipole-conserving quantum systems via tensor gauge fields.



I
Introduction

The interplay between matter and gauge fields has provided important tools for exploring novel quantum phenomena
Thouless et al. 1982
;
Tsui et al. 1982
;
Stormer et al. 1999
;
König et al. 2007
. Beyond conventional gauge fields that satisfy vector gauge invariance, such as electromagnetic fields, research of tensor gauge fields that obey higher-rank tensor gauge invariance and their coupling with matter fields has generated significant interest recently
Vijay et al. 2015
;
Vijay et al. 2016
;
Prem et al. 2018
.
The matter fields coupled with tensor gauge fields are called fracton phase of matters
Nandkishore and Hermele 2019
;
Pretko et al. 2020
;
Ma et al. 2018
.
Different with the conventional excitations,
the movement of an individual fracton is prohibited, and only the
combination of a few fractons, such as a dipole-type fracton combined with a nearest-neighbor particle and hole,
can move along the constrained directions
Pretko 2017a
;
Pretko 2017b
;
Boesl et al. 2024
. The restricted mobility of the fracton arises from the conservation of the dipole or multipole moments
Pretko and Radzihovsky 2018
;
Pretko 2018
in systems that follow the tensor gauge theory. Therefore, tensor gauge fields can be exploited to control fracton phases and their dynamics, thereby linking these systems to
glassy quantum dynamics
Prem et al. 2017
, elasticity theory
Pretko and Radzihovsky 2018
, and gravitational fields, while also having broad potential applications in fields such as quantum information storage
Haah 2011
and quantum computation
Ma et al. 2017
;
Pai et al. 2019
.

Beyond searching for tensor gauge fields in natural materials, artificial quantum systems such as ultracold atomic lattices and superconducting quantum circuits provide powerful platforms for investigating synthetic tensor gauge fields and their coupling with fracton phases
Zhang et al. 2025
;
Myerson-Jain et al. 2022
. For example, the dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model with correlated tunneling has been realized
Yang et al. 2020
;
Zhou et al. 2022
;
Scherg et al. 2021
;
Kohlert et al. 2023
, and constrained
dynamics, such as Hilbert space fragmentation, have been experimentally observed
Adler et al. 2024
;
Will et al. 2024
;
Moudgalya et al. 2022
;
Sala et al. 2020
;
Khemani et al. 2020
;
Pai and Pretko 2019
. Moreover, new phases of matter, such as dipolar Bose-Einstein condensate and dipolar Mott insulators
Lake et al. 2022
;
Lake et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2024
;
Zechmann et al. 2023
;
Zechmann et al. 2024
, as well as the dynamics of dipole and fracton excitations, have been predicted.
Furthermore, our recent work
Zhang et al. 2025
proposes a scheme to construct a synthetic rank-2 electric field by imprinting an additional phase on the correlated tunneling terms, which can be utilized for driving a dipole excitation to realize a dipole Bloch oscillation.
But with this static tensor electric field, the isolated fracton remains immobile because the movement of an individual fracton is always accompanied by the creation of additional dipoles, which costs extra energy.
Consequently, engineering the dynamics of an individual fracton still requires further study of time-dependent tensor gauge fields and their coupling with fracton phases of matter.

In this work, we propose a scheme to simulate a time-dependent rank-2 tensor electric field in a one-dimensional dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model by introducing a periodically driven quadratic potential, and research the dipole and fracton dynamics when the drive frequency is resonant with the on-site interaction. We first explore the dynamics of a ”large dipole” that corresponds to the bound state of a particle and the next-nearest-neighbor hole whose dipole moment can be defined as
2
. Under the resonant condition, the mobility of this large dipole is drastically enhanced through its absorption of drive quanta, mediated by the interplay between driving and on-site interactions, and the dynamics is governed by two processes: splitting and recombination between a large dipole and two small dipoles, and tunneling of small dipoles, both of which depend on the amplitude of the driven potential. Furthermore, this framework can be extended to the dynamics of dipoles with larger dipole moments.
On the other hand, a dipole with a very large dipole moment is equivalent to two independent particle- and hole-type fractons, which means that our discussion is also relevant to individual fracton dynamics. We also verify that the movement of an individual fracton accompanied by the creation of additional dipoles can be well controlled by the amplitude of the time-dependent tensor electric field. To elucidate the dynamics of dipoles and an individual fracton, we map our model onto a lattice of hardcore bosons with density-dependent tunneling and nearest-neighbor interaction.

This work is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce our model and give a brief discussion of time-dependent tensor electric fields. In Sec.
III
, we derive an effective model to analyze the dynamics of a large dipole with dipole moment
2
under resonant driving, and demonstrate the agreement between the effective model from Floquet theory and numerical simulations. In Sec.
IV
, the effective theory is generalized to broader scenarios to explain the dynamics of dipoles with larger dipole moments and a single fraction in resonant tensor electric fields. In Sec.
V
, an experimental setup of our scheme with ultracold atoms in optical lattices is proposed and the corresponding dipole and fracton dynamics are predicted. Sec.
VI
offers a discussion and outlook.

II
Model

We consider a one-dimensional dipole-conserving Bose-Hubbard model (DBHM) with a periodically driven quadratic potential, the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{e}(t),

(1)

can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}=-J\sum_{j}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j-1}\hat{b}^{2}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j+1}+h.c.)+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{n}_{j}(\hat{n}_{j}-1),

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{e}(t)=\frac{A}{2}\cos(\omega t)\sum_{j}j^{2}\hat{n}_{j},

(3)

which correspond to the static and time-dependent parts respectively. In this Hamiltonian,
\hat{b}_{j}
and
\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}
are bosonic annihilation and creation operators on site
j
, and
\hat{n}_{j}=\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}
is the local particle density operator,
J
and
U
are the strength of the correlated tunneling and repulsive on-site interaction,
\omega
and
A
correspond to the drive frequency and amplitude, respectively.

Without the time-dependent part
\hat{H}_{e}(t)
, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
only includes the correlated tunneling and on-site interaction. Defining the dipole moment operator as
\hat{P}=\sum^{L}_{m=1}m\hat{n}_{m}
, the commutation relation
[\hat{P},\hat{H_{0}}]=0
implies the conservation of the dipole moment in this system. In the case of unit filling and
J\ll U
, the ground state of the system is a Mott insulator as
|\mathrm{MI}\rangle=\prod_{m}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}|0\rangle
. An additional single particle or hole excitation on this ground state is called a fracton, which is immobile because the movement will break the dipole conservation.
Instead, a dipole excitation that corresponds to a bound pair consisting of a particle and a hole at nearest-neighbor sites can move freely, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(a). The dipole creation operator acting on the ground state of the Mott insulator can be defined as
\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{b}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j+1}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
, then the correlated tunneling term can be rewritten as
-2J\sum_{j}(\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{d}_{j+1}+h.c.)
, which reveals the kinetics of the dipoles. These interesting physical phenomena imply that the DBHM is an important tool for exploring the fracton and dipole dynamics. It should be pointed out that, in the regime of
J\sim U
, the fracton exhibits interesting dynamics
Boesl et al. 2024
. But in this work, we focus only on the strongly correlated regime as
J\ll U
, where the kinetics of an individual fracton is frozen. Meanwhile, the movement of a large dipole combined with a pair of spatially separated particle and hole is also constrained.

As an example, one can consider a ”large dipole” where the particle and hole excitation are separated by two lattice sites, defined by
\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{j}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{b}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j+2}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
. Although movement of this type of dipole preserves dipole conservation, it propagates only through a second-order process with amplitude
\sim J_{D}=\frac{4J^{2}}{U}
, which is much smaller than
J
. On timescales much shorter than
1/J_{D}
, the motion of a large dipole is almost frozen. Defining the dipole moment of an arbitrary state
|\psi\rangle
as
P=\langle\psi|\hat{P}|\psi\rangle-\langle\mathrm{MI}|\hat{P}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
, the dipole moment of state
\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{j}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
is
1
, which can be called a ”small dipole”, whereas the ”large dipole” state
\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{j}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
with dipole moment
2
. We can easily estimate that the movement of all dipoles with dipole moment larger than
1
is constrained on the time scale of
\sim 1/J
.

The correlated tunneling can lead to the splitting of a large dipole to two small dipoles, but the energy mismatch that comes from the on-site interaction prevents this process, as shown in Fig.
1
(b). Therefore, extra energy cost is required to drive a large dipole, and the periodically driven quadratic potential
\hat{H}_{e}(t)
, which corresponds to a time-dependent tensor electric field, is necessary. This mechanism can be analogized to the role of time-dependent electromagnetic fields in optical Feshbach resonance, where molecules can dissociate into their constituent atoms by absorbing or emitting one photon
Chin et al. 2010
;
Theis et al. 2004
.

Using the rotating frame
\hat{H}^{\prime}(t)=\hat{\mathcal{U}}(t)\hat{H}(t)\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}(t)+i\hbar(\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{U}}(t))\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}(t)
, where the unitary operator
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
is

\hat{\mathcal{U}}(t)=e^{i(\frac{A}{2\omega}\sin(\omega t)\sum_{j}j^{2}\hat{n}_{j})/\hbar},

(4)

the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)
can be translated into

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{H}^{\prime}(t)=&-J\sum_{j}\left\{e^{i\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\sin(\omega t)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j-1}\hat{b}^{2}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j+1}+h.c.\right\}\\
&+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{n}_{j}(\hat{n}_{j}-1).\end{aligned}

(5)

The additional phase
A_{xx}=\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\sin(\omega t)
before the correlated tunneling term can be interpreted as a synthetic tensor gauge potential coupling to the second derivative of the bosonic field
Zhang et al. 2025
. The time-dependent
A_{xx}
produces a rank-
2
tensor electric field as

E_{xx}=-\frac{\partial A_{xx}}{\partial t}=-\frac{A}{\hbar}\cos(\omega t),

(6)

where
A
and
\omega
are the strength and frequency of this tensor electric field respectively. We should point out that, unlike the real electric field, the synthetic time-dependent electric field applied to neutral atoms will not lead to a corresponding magnetic field.

The engineering of the frequency
\omega
leads to a series of intriguing phenomena
Weitenberg and Simonet 2021
;
Sias et al. 2008
. For the extreme case where
\omega
approaches zero, the time-dependent tensor electric field reduces to a static tensor electric field. The small dipoles will be driven by the electric field and the dipole Bloch oscillation emerges. For the other extreme case where
\hbar\omega
is much larger than any other energy scales and
A\ll\hbar\omega
, the effective time-independent Hamiltonian can be derived from high-frequency expansion
Eckardt and Anisimovas 2015
;
Goldman and Dalibard 2014
;
Rahav et al. 2003
;
Sias et al. 2008
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{\mathrm{hf}}=&-J\mathcal{J}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)\sum_{j}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j-1}\hat{b}^{2}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j+1}+h.c.)\\
+&\frac{U}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{n}_{j}(\hat{n}_{j}-1),\end{aligned}

(7)

where the strength of correlated tunneling is modulated by the
0
-th Bessel function
\mathcal{J}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)
, which decreases as the driving amplitude
A
increases. In both cases, the movement of large dipoles remains constrained.

Therefore, we focus on the resonant dynamics where
U\approx\hbar\omega
, in which the tensor electric field leads to the splitting of a large dipole into two small dipoles, thereby enabling mobility of the large dipole, as shown in Fig.
1
(c).

Figure 1:
Scheme of dipole dynamics in DBHM. (a) A small dipole with dipole moment
1
can propagate freely through the lattice. (b) A large dipole with dipole moment
2
is kinetically constrained because of the extra energy cost. (c) The time-dependent tensor electric field
E_{xx}
resonantly mediates the splitting and recombination processes between dipoles with different moments, releasing large dipoles from their confinement.

III
Dipole dynamics under resonant driving

We investigate the dipole dynamics in the strong-coupling regime (
U\gg J
) at unit filling, where the drive frequency is near-resonant with the on-site interaction as
\hbar\omega\approx U
. For a general case, the time-dependent coefficient in front of the correlated tunneling term in Eq. (
5
) can be expanded as
\exp\{i\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\sin(\omega t)\big\}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=-\infty}\mathcal{J}_{n}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)e^{in\omega t}
, corresponding to the
n
photon-assisted correlated tunneling, with the
n
-th order Bessel functions
\mathcal{J}_{n}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)
as amplitudes. In the near-resonant case, these photon-assisted correlated tunneling will play a crucial role in the dynamical processes.

The employment of Floquet theory is necessary for exploring the detail of dynamic processes. We will first provide a brief review of the Floquet theory
Eckardt and Anisimovas 2015
. Consider the Schrödinger equation
i\hbar\partial_{t}|\psi(t)\rangle=\hat{H}(t)|\psi(t)\rangle
where the time-dependent Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}(t+T)
has a periodicity as
T=2\pi/\omega
. Floquet theory states that the solutions should take the form
|\psi_{n}(t)\rangle=|u_{n}(t)\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{n}t/\hbar}
with periodic Floquet modes
|u_{n}(t)\rangle=|u_{n}(t+T)\rangle
and
\epsilon_{n}
is the corresponding quasi-energy. The time-periodicity implies that quasi-energies are defined modulo
\hbar\omega
, such that they can be rewritten as
\epsilon_{mn}=\epsilon_{n}+m\hbar\omega
,where
m
is an integer, and the corresponding Floquet modes are given by
|u_{mn}(t)\rangle=|u_{n}(t)\rangle e^{im\omega t}
. They satisfy the Schrödinger equation as

(\hat{H}(t)-i\hbar\partial_{t})|u_{mn}(t)\rangle=\epsilon_{mn}|u_{mn}(t)\rangle,

(8)

which provides a framework for understanding the dynamics under periodic driving. By choosing a complete set of orthonormal basis states
|\alpha m\rangle=|\alpha\rangle e^{im\omega t}
in the extended Hilbert space, where
\{|\alpha\rangle\}
forms the basis of the Hilbert space of the original Hamiltonian (
2
), the dynamic process can be analyzed using the Floquet matrix
M
, which is defined as
M_{\alpha m,\beta n}=\langle\alpha m|(\hat{H}(t)-i\hbar\partial_{t})|\beta n\rangle
. By diagonalizing this Floquet matrix with a finite size cut-off, the quasi-energies and the corresponding Floquet modes can be estimated.

In this section, we will explore the dynamics of a large dipole with dipole moment
2
as an example. Defining the creation operator of a large dipole as
\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{b}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j+2}
, the initial state is prepared as
|\psi_{0}\rangle=\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{j}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
with a large dipole at the
j
site. To clarify the dynamical properties, we first examine the time evolution within a simplified four-site lattice, and then extend the discussion to a lattice with a larger length scale.

III.1
Resonant dynamics in a four-site lattice

In the lattice with only four sites, the Fock states with four-particle occupations are chosen as the basis. If the initial state is prepared at the state
|0121\rangle
as a large dipole, then only the following three Fock states
|\alpha_{1}\rangle=|0121\rangle
,
|\alpha_{2}\rangle=|0202\rangle e^{-i\omega t}
and
|\alpha_{3}\rangle=|1012\rangle
should be selected, whereas Fock states with more than
2
particles occupied on a single site have been ignored because we only consider single photon-assisted correlated tunneling for simplicity, those Fock states violating the dipole conservation are also abandoned. Then the Floquet matrix can be approximated as
H_{F}=\sum_{i,j}|\alpha_{i}\rangle M_{ij}\langle\alpha_{j}|
where
M
is a
3\times 3
matrix as

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\delta-\frac{8J_{0}^{2}}{U}&-2\sqrt{2}J_{1}&-\frac{4J^{2}_{0}}{U}\\
-2\sqrt{2}J_{1}&\frac{10J_{0}^{2}}{U}&\sqrt{2}J_{1}\\
-\frac{4J^{2}_{0}}{U}&\sqrt{2}J_{1}&\delta-\frac{2J_{0}^{2}}{U}\end{array}\right),

(9)

where
\delta=\hbar\omega-U
is the energy detuning and
J_{0}=J\mathcal{J}_{0}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)
,
J_{1}=J\mathcal{J}_{1}\left(\frac{A}{\hbar\omega}\right)
. The difference in tunneling amplitudes between
|\alpha_{1}\rangle\leftrightarrow|\alpha_{2}\rangle
and
|\alpha_{2}\rangle\leftrightarrow|\alpha_{3}\rangle
in Eq. (
9
) comes from the asymmetry of initial state, where the dipole is directed to the right along the lattice. Instead, if the initial state is chosen as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j+2}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
whose dipole moment is directed to the left, all our discussions should have a reflection.

The dynamic process is determined by the drive amplitude
A
in the resonant case where
\delta=0
. For small
A\sim J\ll U,\hbar\omega
, the parameters
J_{0}\approx J
and
J_{1}\approx\frac{JA}{2\hbar\omega}
. In this regime, both
J_{1}
and
J^{2}_{0}/U
are of the same order as
J_{D}=4J^{2}/U
which is significantly smaller than
J
. Consequently, the dynamics is almost constrained on the time scale of
\sim 1/J
.
As
A
increases, those off-diagonal terms proportional to
J_{1}
become significant and no longer negligible, while the terms proportional to
J^{2}_{0}/U
remain small and become irrelevant. If we set
J^{2}_{0}/U\approx 0
as an approximation, Eq. (
9
) can be diagonalized analytically. In the resonant case
\delta=0
, three eigenenergies are
0
and
\mp\sqrt{10}J_{1}
with the corresponding eigenstates as
\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}(1,0,2)^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}(-2,\mp\sqrt{5},1)^{\mathrm{T}}
respectively. Then the instantaneous state at time
t
can be calculated as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{5}\Big\{\Big[4\cos(\frac{\sqrt{10}J_1t}{\hbar})+1\Big]|\alpha_{1}\rangle\\
+&2\sqrt{5}i\sin(\frac{\sqrt{10}J_1t}{\hbar})|\alpha_{2}\rangle+2\Big[1-\cos(\frac{\sqrt{10}J_1t}{\hbar})\Big]|\alpha_{3}\rangle\Big\}.\end{aligned}

(10)

As shown in Fig.
2
(a), the populations of these three states exhibit periodic oscillation with the period

T=\frac{2\pi\hbar}{\sqrt{10}J_{1}}.

(11)

All of the above discussions are focused on the resonant case
\delta=0
. For a finite

III.2
Resonant dynamics in a multi-site lattice

The results obtained from the four-site lattice can be generalized to larger systems. From the preceding discussion, the effective Hamiltonian in Eq.(
9
) indicates that the tunneling of a large dipole is restricted on the time scale of
\sim 1/J
, the dipole dynamics only includes the splitting into two adjacent small dipoles with resonant driving. In the four-site lattice, two small dipoles are equivalent to two hardcore bosons confined within a double-well potential, whose movement is also restricted. However, in lattices with more sites, these two small dipoles can move freely except for the exchange of each other.

When multiphoton-assisted correlated tunneling terms related to the
n
-th Bessel function with
n>1
can be neglected, the dipole dynamics include not only the splitting of large dipoles, but also the nearest-neighbor tunneling of small dipoles,
whose amplitudes are determined by
J_{1}
and
J_{0}
, respectively. Defining the Fock states
|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle=\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{m}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{n}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
as two small dipoles on sites
m
and
n
where
n-m\geq 2
,
|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+1}\rangle=\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{m}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
as one large dipole on site
m
, the dipole dynamics can be described using the effective Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{D}=\hat{H}_{D,\mathrm{bulk}}+\hat{H}_{D,\mathrm{edge}}
, where the bulk term is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\hat{H}_{D,\mathrm{bulk}}=-2\sqrt{2}J_{1}\sum_{m}|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+2}|\\
&+\sqrt{2}J_{1}\sum_{m}|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}_{m-1}{\bf 1}_{m+1}|\\
-\frac{4J^{2}_{0}}{U}&\sum_{m}|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}_{m+1}{\bf 1}_{m+2}|+\delta^{\prime}\sum_{m}|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{m+1}|\\
-2J_{0}&{\sum_{m,n}}^{\prime}|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle(\langle{\bf 1}_{m+1}{\bf 1}_{n}|+\langle{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{n+1}|)+h.c.,\end{aligned}

(12)

where
\delta^{\prime}=\delta-\frac{20J^{2}_{0}}{U}\approx\delta
is the effective energy detuning of the large dipole, the prime in the summation indicates that tunneling terms for
n-m\leq 2
are excluded. The edge term
\hat{H}_{D,\mathrm{edge}}=\frac{2J^{2}_{0}}{U}|{\bf 1}_{1}{\bf 1}_{2}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}_{1}{\bf 1}_{2}|+\frac{8J^{2}_{0}}{U}|{\bf 1}_{L-2}{\bf 1}_{L-1}\rangle\langle{\bf 1}_{L-2}{\bf 1}_{L-1}|
is irrelevant when the length of the lattice is large enough.

The effective Hamiltonian (
12
) can be regarded as a lattice model of two hardcore bosons, where the state
|{\bf 1}_{m}{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle
describes a configuration with one boson occupying site
m
and the other occupying site
n
. Especially, the large dipole with dipole moment
2
has been mapped to two bosons occupying nearest-neighbor sites
m
and
m+1
. Consequently, the dynamical evolution of the system can be effectively treated as the expansion dynamics of two hardcore bosons with the initial state as
|{\bf 1}_{L/2}{\bf 1}_{L/2+1}\rangle
. In this effective model, the splitting of large dipole and the movement of two small dipoles has been mapped onto density-dependent tunneling, which means that the tunneling amplitude of a hardcore boson depends on the occupation of bosons on its neighbor site.
\delta^{\prime}
can be considered as the strength of nearest-neighbor interaction. Thus, this effective Hamiltonian (
12
) for hardcore bosons represents a very complex dynamic process which is difficult to be solved analytically.

To prove the validity of the effective model, we employ two different methods to simulate this expansion process independently: exact diagonalization of the effective Hamiltonian (
12
), and time-evolving-block-decimation (TEBD) method based on the original Hamiltonian (
1
) using the TenPy library
Hauschild and Pollmann 2018
. To maintain consistency between these two methods, in our TEBD simulations we impose a local Hilbert space truncation at the maximum occupation number
N_{\mathrm{max}}=2
to suppress multiphoton-assisted tunneling processes. The simulation results show that the outcomes of both methods are in good agreement, therefore, we just show the results of TEBD.

In the resonant case, the local charge density
\rho^{d}_{m}=\langle\hat{n}_{m}\rangle-1
undergoes an approximately ballistic expansion with the speed depending on the drive amplitudes. Fig.
3
(a) shows the expansion at
A=1.2\hbar\omega
as an example. As the local dipole moment can be defined as
p_{m}=(m-L/2)\rho^{d}_{m}
, the expansion speed of dipoles can be characterized using the wave packet radius of dipoles, which can be defined as

R^{2}_{d}(t)=\sum_{m}(m-L/2)^{2}p_{m}(t)/P=\sum_{m}(m-L/2)^{3}\rho^{d}_{m}(t)/P.

(13)

Fig.
3
(c) shows the evolution of the wave packet radius
R_{d}(t)
for different values of the driving amplitude
A
.
The linear increase of
R_{d}(t)
verifies the ballistic expansion of the dipoles. The expansion speed
v_{d}
is estimated by a linear fit to
R_{d}(t)
versus time, and its dependence on
A
is shown as the solid red line in Fig.
3
(e). The expansion speed is governed by two distinct processes simultaneously: the splitting of a large dipole and the tunneling of small dipoles. When
A
is very small, although the tunneling strength
J_{0}
for small dipoles is large, the initial state is prepared as a large dipole whose splitting is determined by the small
J_{1}
, so the expansion speed is primarily controlled by
J_{1}
. With an increase of
A
, the values of
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are comparable and the impact of small dipole tunneling becomes increasingly important. As
A
increases to
A/(\hbar\omega)\approx 2.4
, the zero point of
J_{0}
, the evolution is dominated by the splitting of the large dipole, and the expansion speed depends only on
J_{1}
.
The result of the exact diagonalization using the effective Hamiltonian (
12
) is shown as the dashed blue line in Fig.
3

IV
More general dipole dynamics and fracton dynamics

The above discussion about the dynamics of a dipole with dipole moment of
2
can be extended to more general cases. For a large dipole with dipole moment larger than
2
, the dynamic process also includes its splitting into a series of small dipoles and tunneling of these small dipoles. On the other hand, a large dipole with a very large dipole moment can be regarded as a pair of independent particle- and hole-type fractons separated by a long distance. Consequently, the dynamics of a large dipole can be viewed as the individual movement of these fractons and their interference. It implies that the time-dependent tensor electric field can break the mobility constraint of an independent particle- or hole-type fracton through photon-assisted correlated tunneling, and thereby induces interesting fracton dynamics.

We first investigate the dynamics of a dipole excitation
\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{n,m}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{b}_{m}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m+n}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
on a unit-filled Mott insulator state
|\mathrm{MI}\rangle=|11\cdots 1\rangle
, where
n\geq 3
.
Without the time-dependent tensor electric field, the tunneling of this large dipole can only be achieved by a
n
-th order process with the amplitude
J_{n,D}\sim J^{n}/U^{n-1}
, which can be ignored on a time scale of
1/J
. Within the Floquet framework discussed above and excluding the multiphoton-assisted correlated tunneling, the dynamics of this large dipole arises only from its splitting into two or more smaller dipoles. For example, a large dipole with dipole moment
n
at site
m
can split into two adjacent dipoles with dipole moment
n-1
and
1
with photon-assisted correlated tunneling, while two final states can be expressed as
\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{n-1,m}\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{m+n}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
and
\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{m-1}\hat{D}^{\dagger}_{n-1,m+1}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
.
Then the cascade of the splitting process eventually dissociates a large dipole into a series of small dipoles, where the summation of their dipole moments is conserved.

Although the total dipole moment is conserved, the splitting processes fall into two distinct categories. The first changes the dipole count by
1
, for example, a large dipole with moment
n
split into two adjacent dipoles with dipole moments
n-1
and
1
.
These processes come from photon-assisted correlated tunneling, and the amplitudes are proportional to
J_{1}
. The other preserves the dipole count, for instance, from two adjacent dipoles with moments
n-1
and
1
to two dipoles with moments
n-2
and
2
. These processes arise from static correlated tunneling whose amplitudes are proportional to
J_{0}
. Following the preceding discussion based on the effective Hamiltonian Eq. (
12
), this system can be mapped onto an equivalent hardcore boson lattice model comprising
n
bosons with density-dependent tunneling and nearest-neighbor interaction. The
n
-boson system does not differ qualitatively from the two-boson scenario, and the detail is shown in the Appendix
A
. Our analysis shows that the resulting dipole wave packet exhibits nearly the same expansion behavior as the dipole with dipole moment
2
.
TEBD simulations indicate that the expansion velocity of these large dipoles is comparable to that of the dipole with moment
2
.

The more interesting thing is the dynamics of an individual fracton. A representative example is the preparation of an independent particle-type fracton excitation
|\psi_{0}\rangle=\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}|\mathrm{MI}\rangle
as the initial state. Consequently, the filling factor
\langle\rho\rangle\neq 1
. Driven by a near-resonant tensor AC electric field, the fracton can move by absorbing the energy from the field, this process corresponds to the creation of an additional dipole, thereby preserving the total dipole moment. As in the above discussion, a similar effective lattice model of hardcore bosons can be constructed and the details can be found in the Appendix
A
.

The results of TEBD simulation are shown in Fig.

V
Experimental setup

In experiment, a Bose-Hubbard model with a strong linear tilt described by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{H}_{\mathrm{tilted}}=&-J^{\prime}\sum_{j}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j+1}+h.c.)\\
+&\frac{U}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{n}_{j}(\hat{n}_{j}-1)+\Delta\sum_{j}j\hat{n}_{j}\end{aligned}

(15)

can be considered as an accessible platform to implement dipole-conserving dynamics.
Here,
\Delta
is the energy detuning between nearest neighbor sites. In the limit
J^{\prime},U\ll\Delta
, the single-particle tunneling is prohibited, and
\hat{H}_{0}
in the original Hamiltonian (
1
) can be derived from (
15
) using the perturbation approach, where the amplitude of the correlated tunneling can be estimated as
J=-J^{\prime 2}\big(\frac{1}{U+\Delta}-\frac{1}{\Delta}\big)\approx J^{\prime 2}U/\Delta^{2}
. If the parameters of Hamiltonian (
15
) is chosen as
J^{\prime}\ll\Delta
, the ratio
J^{\prime}/U\approx(J^{\prime}/\Delta)^{2}
is a second-order small quantity, which is relevant with our above discussion.

In this work, we rely on the simultaneous presence of the correlated tunneling term and the periodically drive potential in Eq. (
1
). The time-dependent potential can, in principle, be engineered by exploiting a perturbative periodically modulated harmonic potential. However, the coexistence of a linear tilt and a time-dependent quadratic driving makes the many-body dynamics more intricate. With the unitary operator
\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(t)=\exp\left[-i\left(\Delta t\sum_{j}j\hat{n}_{j}+\frac{A}{2\omega}\sin(\omega t)\sum_{j}j^{2}\hat{n}_{j}\right)/\hbar\right]
, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{tilted}}+\hat{H}_{e}(t)
can be rewritten in rotating frame as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{H}^{\prime}(t)&=-J^{\prime}\sum_{j}\left[e^{i\left(\Delta t+\frac{A}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)/\hbar}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j+1}+\text{ h.c. }\right]\\
&+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{n}_{j}\left(\hat{n}_{j}-1\right),\end{aligned}

(16)

where the tunneling phase is modulated by two incommensurate drive frequencies,
\Delta
and
\omega
, the Hamiltonian becomes quasi-periodic in time, so the usual high-frequency expansion method for large
\Delta
breaks down.
A qualitative description with multi-mode Floquet theory
Martin et al. 2017
;
Nathan et al. 2021
shows that the effective Hamiltonian contains many other terms, such as the nearest-neighbor interaction of small dipoles and spatial-dependent energy detuning, whose magnitude even larger than the correlated tunneling terms, thereby breaking down the description of our effective model.
Nevertheless, the simulation with TEBD can still provide a numerical tool for exploring the dipole and fracton dynamics in this complex system. Numerical simulations show that, when the drive frequency
\omega
is tuned to be near-resonant with the on-site interaction (
\hbar\omega\approx U
),
the splitting and recombination of the large dipoles with moment
2
can still be observed, as shown in Fig.
5
(a). But instead of the system with only correlated tunneling, the expansion of two neighbor small dipoles is prevented, and the system undergoes an oscillation between a large dipole and two nearest-neighbor small dipoles on a time scale of
\sim 1/J
, as shown in Fig.
5
(c). Furthermore, the oscillation can also be observed in fracton dynamics by fine-tuning the drive frequency
\omega
, as in Fig.
5
(b) and (d).

All of these simulations show that the periodically driven quadratic potential remains to be available to modulate the dipole and fracton dynamics. However, achieving more sophisticated dynamical control will require the design and implementation of more intricate modulation sequences.

VI
Conclusions and outlook

Using a periodically driven quadratic potential, we introduce a time-dependent tensor electric field in the one-dimensional DBHM.
Under near-resonant driving, the photon-assisted correlated tunneling not only leads to the splitting of a large dipole but also drives an individual fracton. Furthermore, the expansion of dipoles and fractons in this resonant time-dependent tensor electric field has been analyzed using Floquet theory. We find that on the time scale of the inverse of correlated tunneling amplitude, the dipole and fracton dynamics exhibit roughly ballistic expansion, and the expansion speed is primarily governed by the splitting of large dipoles and the tunneling of small dipoles, both of whose amplitudes depend on the tensor electric field. We also mapped this dynamic process to the dynamics of a few hardcore bosons in a one-dimensional lattice with density-dependent tunneling, which provides an equivalent comprehension for the dipole and fracton dynamics.

Our work establishes a theoretical framework for simulating the time-dependent tensor gauge fields and their coupling to fracton phases of matter.
In the future, we will extend the Floquet engineering scheme based on the tensor gauge field to higher dimensions, thereby enabling the manipulation of fracton excitations such as lineons and planons. Furthermore, we will explore non-equilibrium dynamics driven by time-dependent tensor gauge fields, with a focus on phenomena like subdiffusive transport and the emergence of dynamical scar states in driven systems. These efforts promise to deepen our understanding of quantum many-body physics.

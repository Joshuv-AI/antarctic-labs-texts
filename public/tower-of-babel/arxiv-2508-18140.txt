Coming up from − ∞ -\infty for KPZ via stochastic control
Nicolas Perkowski, Carlos Villanueva Mariz FU Berlin

Abstract
We derive a lower bound, independent of the initial condition, for the solution of the KPZ equation on the torus through its representation as the value function of a (conditional) stochastic control problem. With the same techniques, we also prove a bound for its oscillation, again independent of initial conditions, from which a Harnack type inequality for the rough heat equation (on the torus) can be obtained.



1
Introduction

The Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation

\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)h(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}h(t,x)|^{2}+\xi(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
\xi
is a space-time white noise, is a prototypical singular stochastic
partial differential equation (SPDE) that is solved within the theory of
regularity structures
[
Hai14
]
or paracontrolled
distributions
[
GIP15
]
. It is a universal model
for fluctuations in “weakly asymmetric” interface growth
models
[
HQ18
,
GJ14
]
and a central model within the
KPZ universality class
[
QS15
,
MQR21
]
.

The equation is singular because solutions only have the regularity of a
Brownian motion, and therefore the nonlinearity
|\partial_{x}h(t,x)|^{2}
is
only well-defined after renormalization, considering formally
|\partial_{x}h(t,x)|^{2}-\infty
, and as a space-time distribution. The equation can be
formally related to the the stochastic heat equation via
w=e^{h}
and

\partial_{t}w=\frac{1}{2}\Delta w+w\xi,

if the product on the right hand side is interpreted in the Itô sense

[
BG97
]
. The first intrinsic formulation is due to
Hairer
[
Hai13
]
based on rough paths, and later there were also
intrinsic formulations based on regularity structures
[
FH20
]
and
paracontrolled distributions
[
GP17
]
.

By default, these pathwise theories only give solutions up to a possibly
finite explosion time, and also that only on compact space domains. But in
fact
[
Hai13
]
already contained an almost sure global existence
result, which was achieved by relating
h
to the solution
w
of the
stochastic heat equation, and using almost sure global existence for the
stochastic heat equation. However, unlike the rest of the theory, this step is
not pathwise and the null set where explosion occurs may depend on the initial
condition.

A pathwise non-explosion result was derived in
[
GP17
]
via a
stochastic control formulation which leads to a uniform bound, in combination
with a pathwise implementation of the Cole-Hopf transform, which turns the
uniform bound into a bound in Hölder spaces. This approach was extended
in
[
PCR19
]
to treat the equation on
\mathbb{R}
instead of on
the torus
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, but it still relied on the
Cole-Hopf transform. The work
[
ZZZ22
]
treats a more general
equation, where the Cole-Hopf transform is no longer available, and derives
pathwise bounds for weighted uniform and Hölder norms by a priori
estimates in weighted spaces, and using a suitable transformation (Zvonkin
transform).

All the bounds mentioned above are “linear” in the sense that the estimate
for
h
at a positive time depends on the initial condition
h(0)
. The
purpose of this work is to derive truly nonlinear bounds for
h
that use the
nonlinearity as a coercive term which yields better bounds than those
available for the linear equation. There are several results of this type
available for singular SPDEs, starting from the influential work of
Mourrat-Weber
[
MW17
]
on coming down from infinity for the

\Phi^{4}_{3}
equation, see for example
also
[
GH19
,
CMW23
,
DGR23
]
, where the solution

\phi
to a
\Phi^{4}
type equation can be bounded at time
t>0
by a
constant that is independent of the initial condition. All these works rely on
the very strong coercivity provided by the term
-\phi^{3}
in the
\Phi^{4}
and
related equations.

To the best of our knowledge, there are no comparable results for the KPZ
equation in the literature. Note that KPZ preserves a constant shift, meaning
that if
h
solves the KPZ equation with initial condition
h_{0}
, then
h+c

solves the KPZ equation with initial condition
h_{0}+c
, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, the formulation of a coming down from infinity result
is a bit more subtle than for the
\Phi^{4}
equation. But we can take
inspiration from the deterministic equation: If
h
is the viscosity solution
of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\partial_{t}h(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}h(t,x)|^{2},\quad h(0,x)=\bar{h}(x),

then by the Hopf-Lax formula
[
Eva10
]
, Section 10.3.4,

h(t,x)=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\bar{h}(y)-\frac{t}{2}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)^{2}\right\},

we have

h(t,x)\geqslant-\frac{1}{2t}x^{2}

whenever
\bar{h}(0)\geqslant 0
. Moreover, we have the following one-sided
estimate for the second derivative of
h
,

h(t,x+\varepsilon)+h(t,x-\varepsilon)-2h(t,x)\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{t},

see
[
CS04
]
, Theorem 1.6.1, which does not depend on
h_{0}
and
which is also related to Oleinik’s one-sided estimate for the derivative of
Burgers equation, see
[
Daf10
]
, Theorem 11.2.1.

In our setting, even a one-sided bound on the second derivative of the KPZ
equation with space-time white noise is of course out of the question, because

h
only has Brownian regularity. Instead, here we will derive a lower bound
that is similar in spirit to the one from the Hopf-Lax formula, and a bound on
the Hölder norm of the derivative of
h(t)
which only depends on
t
and

\xi
, but not on
h_{0}
. More precisely, our main results are:

Theorem
.

Let
\beta>0
and
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{T})
and let

h:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
solve the KPZ equation

\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)h(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}h(t,x)|^{2}+\xi(t,x),\quad h(0,x)=\bar{h}(x).

i.

For almost all realizations of
\xi
and all
T>0
there exists a
constant
C\equiv C(T,\xi)
such that whenever
\bar{h}|_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}\geqslant 0
, we have

h(t,x)\geqslant\log\varepsilon-C\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{T}.

(1)

ii.

For almost all realizations of
\xi
and all
T>0
and
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
there exists a constant
C\equiv C(t,T,\alpha,\xi)
such that

|h(t,x)-h(t,0)|\leqslant C|x|^{\alpha},\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{T},

(2)

independently of the initial condition, where
\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}C(t,T,\alpha,\xi)<\infty
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

The first claim is shown in Theorem
2.16
, while the second one
is Theorem
3.5
. For simplicity, we restrict our attention
to the periodic KPZ equation on the circle
\mathbb{T}
. But we expect that
similar results hold in weighted spaces for the KPZ equation on the real line.

One of the key ingredients for proving these results will be the variational
representation of
h
proved in Section 7 of
[
GP17
]
, which
formally reads

h(t,x)=\underset{v}{\sup}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\overline{h}(\gamma^{v}_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}(\xi(t-s,\gamma_{s}^{v})-\infty)\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}{\int^{t}_{0}}v_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right],

where
\gamma_{s}^{v}=x+\int^{s}_{0}v_{u}\mathrm{d}u+B_{s}
, with
B
a Brownian
motion, and we take the supremum over progressively measurable
\nu
.

Structure of the paper

After recalling some preliminary facts in
Section 2, Section 3 deals with the regular case (meaning
\xi\in C_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
) in R . In that case, the result can be obtained as a
consequence of Boué-Dupuis’ formula and an estimation of

\log\mathbb{P}_{x}(B_{t}\in[-1,1]),

see Theorem
2.1
.

Section 4 is devoted to the rough setting. The rigorous representation of
h

involves singular diffusions of the form

\gamma_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}b(s,\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s+B_{t},

(3)

where the drift
b
is distributional with Besov-Hölder regularity
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\frac{1}{2}-}
. Such a process will not be a functional of
Brownian motion, so a direct application of the Boué-Dupuis formula is no
longer possible; however, we can exploit the structure of
\gamma
as a
Dirichlet process (explored in detail in
[
KP25
]
) to
“recover” Girsanov’s theorem and solve this difficulty. Everything then
boils down, as in the regular case, to estimating

\log\mathbb{P}_{x}(\gamma_{t}\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]).

In order to do this, we will derive heat kernel estimates for
\gamma
. This
is based on an extension of Zvonkin’s transform to the paracontrolled regime
(
b\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
for
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}\right)
) in a similar fashion to

[
ZZ17
]
. Consider
\Phi_{\lambda}(s,y)=y+u_{\lambda}(s,y)
, where
u_{\lambda}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

solves the (paracontrolled) PDE

\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta+b\partial_{x}\right)u_{\lambda}=-b+\lambda u_{\lambda}.

(4)

When
\lambda
is big enough,
\Phi_{\lambda}(s,\cdot)
is a
diffeomorphism of R for each
s\in[0,T]
, and we will prove that
Y_{t}=\Phi_{\lambda}(t,\gamma_{t})
solves (think formally of Itô’s formula)

Y_{t}=\Phi_{\lambda}(0,x)+\int^{t}_{0}\lambda u_{\lambda}(s,\Phi^{-1}_{\lambda}(s,Y_{s}))\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}(s,\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(s,Y_{s}))\mathrm{d}B_{s}.

For
u_{\lambda}
as in (
4
), such an SDE has Lipschitz
continuous in space (uniformly in time) drift, and uniformly elliptic,
Hölder continuous diffusion coefficient, so existence, uniqueness and heat
kernel estimates are available. As
\gamma_{t}=(\Phi_{\lambda}(t))^{-1}(Y_{t})
, we thus get heat kernel estimates for (
3
).

Lastly, in Section 5 we prove the second claim
(Theorem
3.5
), and comment how it is related to Harnack’s
inequality for the heat equation, both in the regular and rough setting.

1.1
Preliminaries and notation

Throughout the paper we will identify
\mathbb{T}\approx\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The Hölder-Besov space

\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is defined by

\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\equiv B_{\infty,\infty}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}):\|f\|_{\alpha}:=\underset{i\geqslant-1}{\sup}(2^{i\alpha}\|\Delta_{i}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})})<\infty\right\},

where
(\Delta_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
are the usual Paley-Littlewood
projections associated with a dyadic partition of unity. We say that

\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha-}(\mathbb{T})
if
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and a given Banach space we will denote by

\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}X
the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
functions from
[0,T]
to
X
, endowed with the norm

{\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\alpha}X}}:=\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|f(t)\|_{X}+\underset{0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T}{\sup}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{X}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

Similarly, the space
C_{T}X
consists of the continuous functions from
[0,T]
to
X
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{C_{T}X:=}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|f(t)\|_{X}.

The following interpretation of the (regular noise) KPZ equation as the value
function of a stochastic control problem will be of central importance.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R})
for

\beta>0
,
\xi\in C_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
(i.e. continuous and
bounded) and
h
solve

\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)h=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}h|^{2}+\xi,\quad h(0)=\bar{h}.

Then

h(t,x)=\underset{v}{\sup}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\bar{h}(X^{v}_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,X_{s}^{v})\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}{\int^{t}_{0}}v_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right],

with
X_{s}^{v}=x+\int^{s}_{0}v_{u}\mathrm{d}u+B_{s}.

Proof.

This is a linear-quadratic control problem and the result is classical. We
assume that
\bar{h}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The general case follows by an
approximation argument. For
\bar{h}\in C^{2}
the solution
h
to the PDE is
in
C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and therefore we can apply
Itô’s formula to
\bar{h}(X^{v}_{t})=h(t-t,X^{v}_{t})
to obtain for a
martingale
M

\displaystyle\bar{h}(X^{v}_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta+v_{s}\partial_{x}\right)h(t-s,X^{v}_{s})\mathrm{d}s+M_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(t,x)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{x}h(t-s,\cdot)-v_{s}|^{2}(X^{v}_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left(-\xi(t-s,X^{v}_{s})+\frac{1}{2}v_{s}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s+M_{t},

and thus

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\bar{h}(X^{v}_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,X_{s}^{v})\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}{\int^{t}_{0}}v_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]=h(t,x)-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{x}h(t-s,\cdot)-v_{s}|^{2}(X^{v}_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right],

which is upper bounded by
h(t,x)
and equality is obtained for
v_{s}=\partial_{x}h(t-s,X^{v}_{s})
.
∎

Next we provide the statement of the Boué-Dupuis formula. Remember that
for two probability measures
\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}
, the relative entropy
is defined as

H(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\right].

Theorem 1.3
.

\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{dupuis}{}{}]},\operatorname{Theorem}3.1\right).
Let
B:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-Brownian motion and
F
a
bounded measurable functional on
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{F(B)}]&=&\underset{\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}}{\sup}\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[F(B)]-H(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P})\}\\
&=&\underset{v}{\sup}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[F\left(B+\int^{\cdot}_{0}v_{s}\mathrm{d}s\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int^{T}_{0}|v_{s}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right],\end{array}

where in the second line
v
is taken over all progressively measurable
processes with respect to the augmented filtration generated by
B
.

We will mostly rely on the second identity, which allows us to pass from the
stochastic control problem to an optimization over probability measures.

For the rest of the paper, we fix
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}\right)
.

2
Lower bound

2.1
Warm-up: The case of bounded continuous forcing

Let us start by assuming that
\xi\in C_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
, and we
consider the non-periodic KPZ equation on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
with
forcing
\xi
. We also assume that
\bar{h}|_{[-1,1]}\geqslant 0
, instead
of
h|_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}\geqslant 0
as we will assume on the
torus.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R})
be such
that
\bar{h}|_{[-1,1]}\geqslant 0
. There exists a constant

C=C(T)>0
for which

h(t,x)\geqslant-\int_{0}^{t}\|\xi(s)\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}s-C\left(1+\frac{1_{|x|>1}x^{2}}{t}\right),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By the Boué-Dupuis formula and Lemma
1.2
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}h(t,x)&=&\underset{v}{\sup}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\bar{h}(X^{v}_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,X_{s}^{v})\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}{\int^{t}_{0}}v_{s}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]\\
&=&\underset{\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}_{x}}{\sup}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{h}(B_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right]-H(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P}_{x})\right\}.\end{array}

(5)

Choosing
\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\cdot|B_{t}\in[-1,1])
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P})&=&\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{x}}\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{x}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}\left[\log\frac{1_{\{B_{t}\in[-1,1]\}}}{\mathbb{P}_{x}(B_{t}\in[-1,1])}\right]\\
&=&-\log\mathbb{P}_{x}(B_{t}\in[-1,+1])\\
&=&-\log\mathbb{P}_{0}(B_{t}\in[-x-1,-x+1])\end{array}

If
x\in[-1,1]
, then
|[-x-1,-x+1]\cap[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]|\geqslant\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and
thus

t\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{0}(B_{t}\in[-x-1,-x+1])

is uniformly (in
x\in[-1,1]
) bounded away from
0
on
(0,T]
. Hence,

H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{x})\lessapprox 1
for
x\in[-1,1]
. On the other hand, for
x\in[-1,1]^{c}
, we have by Jensen’s
inequality and the concavity of the logarithm with the heat kernel
p(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-x^{2}/(2t)}

\begin{array}[]{lcl}H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{x})&=&-\log\mathbb{P}_{0}(B_{t}\in[-x-1,-x+1])\\
&=&-\log\int^{-x+1}_{-x-1}p(t,y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leqslant&-\int^{-x+1}_{-x-1}\frac{1}{2}\log 2p(t,y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=&\int^{-x+1}_{-x-1}\frac{1}{2}\left(\log 2+\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi t+\frac{y^{2}}{2t}\right)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\overset{|x|>1,t\in[0,T]}{\lesssim}&1+\frac{x^{2}}{t},\end{array}

so overall

H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{x})\lesssim 1+\frac{1_{|x|>1}x^{2}}{t}.

Plugging this back in (
5
) and recalling that
\bar{h}|_{[-1,1]}\geqslant 0
yields

\displaystyle h(t,x)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}\left[\bar{h}(B_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right]-H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{x})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left.\bar{h}(B_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,x+B_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right|B_{t}\in[-1,1]\right]-C\left(1+\frac{1_{|x|>1}x^{2}}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left.\int_{0}^{t}\xi(t-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right|B_{t}\in[-1,1]\right]-C\left(1+\frac{1_{|x|>1}x^{2}}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-\int^{t}_{0}\|\xi(s)\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}s-C\left(1+\frac{1_{|x|>1}x^{2}}{t}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

2.2
The case of space-time white noise forcing

2.2.1
Singular diffusions and variational representation

As hinted in the introduction, in order to extend Theorem
2.1
to
the KPZ equation with singular noise, we need a representation of its solution
which is analogous to (
5
) (see Theorem
2.9
). Such a
representation will require making sense of diffusion equations of the form

\gamma_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}b(s,\gamma_{s})\mathrm{d}s+B_{t},

(6)

where
b
is a distribution. In the low regularity regime that we consider
here, martingale problem solutions to this problem were first constructed
in
[
DD16
]
with rough path techniques, for scalar-valued

\gamma
. The rough path approach was extended to multidimensional
\gamma

with paracontrolled distributions in
[
CC18
]
; in

[
KP25
]
a notion of weak solution was studied, and proved to
be equivalent to the martingale problem formulation. The solution
\gamma
is
not a semimartingale, but a Dirichlet process, i.e. it can be decomposed into
a martingale and a process of vanishing quadratic variation.

Recall that
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}\right)
is fixed
throughout the paper. For general
b\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, the equation (
6
) is not well-posed without
including an enhancement in the following sense (Definition 3.6 in

[
CC18
]
and Section 6 of
[
KP25
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

(\operatorname{Enhanced}\operatorname{drift})
We say that b
\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
is an
\operatorname{enhanced}\operatorname{drift}
if
there exists
B\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{1-2\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
such that

(b,B)\in\mathcal{X}^{\alpha},\operatorname{where}

\mathcal{X}^{\alpha}:=\operatorname{cl}_{C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}\times C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{1-2\alpha}}\{(\eta,\mathcal{J}^{T}(\partial_{x}\eta)\odot\eta)|\eta\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\},

where
\operatorname{cl}
means the closure in
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\times C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{1-2\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
of the set
in brackets,
\mathcal{J}^{T}(u)
is the solution of

\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)\mathcal{J}^{T}(u)=u,\quad\mathcal{J}^{T}(u)(T)=0,

and
\odot
denotes the paraproduct of two distributions (see Chapter 2 of

[
BD11
]
for more details).

Definition 2.3
.

(\operatorname{Martingale}\operatorname{solution})
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered
probability space and
v
a progressively measurable process. For a
distributional drift
b\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
,
admitting an enhancement as in (
[
CC18
]
, Definition 3.6), we say that
an adapted and continuous stochastic process
\gamma
is a

\operatorname{martingale}\operatorname{solution}
to

\gamma_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}(b(s,\gamma_{s})+v_{s})\mathrm{d}s+B_{t},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(7)

if
\mathbb{P}(\gamma_{0}=x)=1
and whenever
\varphi_{T}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
,
f\in C_{T}C(\mathbb{T})
and
\varphi

solves the paracontrolled PDE

\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta+b\partial_{x}\right)\varphi=f,\quad\varphi(T)=\varphi_{T}

(8)

on
[0,T]
, then

\varphi(t,\gamma_{t})-\int^{t}_{0}(f(s,\gamma_{s})+\partial_{x}\varphi(s,\gamma_{s})v_{s})\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in[0,T]

is a martingale.

Remark 2.4
.

If
b\in C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, the
solution
\varphi
to equation (
8
) belongs to the space
C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{2-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\cap C^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, see Theorem 3.10 in
[
CC18
]
.

Next, we discuss the decomposition of
\gamma
as a Dirichlet process. This

Remark 2.10

Lemma 2.11

2.2.2
Zvonkin’s transform

We have seen thus far that, as in the case of smooth coefficients, we can
represent the solution to the KPZ equation by a stochastic control problem. In
order to derive similar bounds in our singular setting, we still require
estimates for the transition density of

\gamma_{s}=x+\int^{s}_{0}(\partial_{x}Y+\partial_{x}Y^{\vee}+\partial_{x}Y^{R})(t-u,\gamma_{u})\mathrm{d}u+B_{s},

for fixed

. Similarly as in
[

]
, this is
where Zvonkin’s transform comes into play. For the Zvonkin transform we need
to solve (paracontrolled) PDEs of the type

\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{\Delta}{2}+b\partial_{x}\right)u_{\lambda}=f+\lambda u_{\lambda},\quad u(t)=0,

(12)

where
b\in C_{t}\mathcal{C}^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in C_{t}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\cup\{-b\}
. The first step is to check that for

\lambda
big enough we can make
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
as
small as we want.

(17)

and also

|\nabla_{x}p(0,x;t,y)|\leqslant C^{\prime}_{1}t^{-1}e^{-c^{\prime}_{1}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}.

(18)

Proof.

By Theorem
2.13
,

p_{b}(0,x;t,y)=p_{Y}(0,\Phi_{\lambda}(0,x);t,\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y))|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y)|.

On one hand, we have that

\frac{1}{2}\leqslant|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y)|\leqslant\frac{3}{2},

and on the other, thanks to the Lipschitz continuity in space and

\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuity in time of
\Phi_{\lambda}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}|\Phi_{\lambda}(0,x)-\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y)|^{2}&\gtrsim&|\Phi_{\lambda}(t,x)-\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y)|^{2}-|\Phi_{\lambda}(0,x)-\Phi_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}\\
&\gtrsim&|x-y|^{2}-t,\end{array}

and thus the result follows from Proposition
2.14
. Noting that

\nabla_{x}p_{b}(0,x;t,y)=\nabla_{x}p_{Y}(0,\Phi_{\lambda}(0,x);t,\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y))\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}(0,x)|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}(t,y)|,

(
18
) is obtained similarly.

∎

2.2.3
Proof of the lower bound

We have everything we need to prove the main theorem, that is the analogue of
the lower bound in Theorem
2.1
in the singular case. Since we are
now on the torus, we need to work with the “periodized” singular diffusion,
which has transition density

p_{\mathbb{T},b}(0,x;t,y)=2\pi\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{b}(0,x;t,y+2\pi k),\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T},

but we will only use the fact that for
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
(and
independently of
t
),

p_{\mathbb{T},b}(0,x;t,y)\gtrsim p_{b}(0,x;t,y).

(19)

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{T})
be
such that
\bar{h}|_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}\geq 0
.
There exists a constant

C\equiv C(T,Y,Y^{\vee},Y^{R})

for which

h(t,x)\geq\log\varepsilon-C\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

By (
10
) and (
11
), we have as in Theorem

2.1
that

\begin{array}[]{lll}h(t,x)&\geqslant&-\|Y+Y^{\vee}+Y^{R}\|_{\infty}-\|Y\|_{\infty}-\int^{T}_{0}\|\partial_{x}Y^{{}^{R}}(s)\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\log\mathbb{P}_{t,x}(\gamma_{t}\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]).\end{array}

Now, arguing as in Theorem
2.1

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\log\mathbb{P}_{t,x}(\gamma_{t}\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon])&\geqslant&\log\int^{\varepsilon}_{-\varepsilon}p_{\mathbb{T},b}(0,x;t,y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\overset{\eqref{geqkernels}}{\gtrsim}&\log\int^{\varepsilon}_{-\varepsilon}p_{b}(0,x;t,y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&\overset{\eqref{singkernel}}{\gtrsim}&\log\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int^{\varepsilon}_{-\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon C^{\prime}}^{-1}t^{-1/2}e^{{-c^{\prime}}^{-1}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\mathrm{d}y\\
&\overset{x\in\mathbb{T},t\in[0,T]}{\gtrsim}&\log\varepsilon-C_{T}(1+\frac{1}{t}),\end{array}

as we wanted.
∎

3
Modulus of continuity

With the same idea, we may prove one more interesting result: that the modulus
of continuity of the KPZ equation comes down from infinity, without any
quantitative requirements on the initial condition. This is the content of
Theorems
3.1
and
3.5
.

3.1
Warm-up: The case without forcing

In the following Theorem we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{T}}(t,x)
the
law of the Brownian motion on the torus starting from
x\in\mathbb{T}
, at
time
t
(i.e. the nornal distribution on the torus with variance
t
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\beta>0
and
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{T})
and
\xi=0
. Then, with
C_{T}
independent of
\bar{h}
,

|h(t,x)-h(t,0)|\leqslant C_{T}\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)|x|,\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{T}.

(20)

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[]{lll}h(t,x)-h(t,0)&=&\underset{\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}_{t,x}}{\sup}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\overline{h}(B_{t})\right]-H(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P}_{x})\right\}-\underset{\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}_{t,0}}{\sup}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\overline{h}(B_{t})\right]-H(\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{P}_{0})\right\},\end{array}

where now
\mathbb{P}\mathbb{}_{t,0}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{T}}(t,0)\text{ and }\mathbb{P}_{t,x}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{T}}(t,x)
. Let us denote
by
\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}
the optimizer of the first supremum (which exists and
is explicitly given by the Radon-Nikodym density
\frac{e^{\overline{h}(B_{t})}}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{t,x}}\left[e^{\overline{h}(B_{t})}\right]}
). Since
\mathbb{P}_{t,0}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t,x}
, we can also
choose
\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}
in the second supremum to obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}h(t,x)-h(t,0)&\leqslant&\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}\left[\overline{h}(B_{t})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}\left[\overline{h}(B_{t})\right]+H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{t,0})-H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{t,x})\\
&=&H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{t,0})-H(\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}|\mathbb{P}_{t,0})-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}\left[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{t,0}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{t,x}}\right].\end{array}

We know that
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{t,0}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{t,x}}(y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{T}}(t,0)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{T}}(t,x)}(y)=\frac{p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,y)}{p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,y-x)}
, and

\begin{array}[]{l}|\log p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,y)-\log p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,y-x)|\leqslant\left\|\frac{\partial_{x}p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,\cdot)}{p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{\infty}|x|\leqslant C_{T}\frac{|x|}{t},\end{array}

(21)

where the second inequality follows from Corollary
3.3
below.
Since
h(t,x)-h(t,0)>0
without loss of generality (otherwise
exchange the role of
x
and
0
), we deduce that

|h(t,x)-h(t,0)|\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{t,x}}\left[\left|\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{t,0}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{t,x}}\right|\right]\leqslant C_{T}\frac{|x|}{t}.

∎

In the proof we used the following results for the periodic heat kernel:

Lemma 3.2
.

\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{heattorus}{}{}]},\operatorname{Theorem}2.3\right).
The
heat kernel on the torus

\begin{array}[]{llll}p_{\mathbb{T}}:&\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&(s,y)&\mapsto&p_{\mathbb{T}}(s,y):=2\pi\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}e^{-\frac{|y+2\pi k|^{2}}{2s}}\end{array}

(or more precisely, its
2\pi
-periodic extension) satisfies

\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s}}\exp\left(-\frac{|y|_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{2s}\right)\leqslant p_{\mathbb{T}}(s,y)\leqslant 2\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{|y|_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}{2s}\right),

where
|y|_{\mathbb{T}}:=\underset{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\inf}|y-2\pi k|
.

Corollary 3.3
.

For
p_{\mathbb{T}}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the following gradient estimate holds:

\left\|\frac{\partial_{x}p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,\cdot)}{p_{\mathbb{T}}(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant\frac{C_{T}}{t}.

Remark 3.4
.

We can extend this without much work to the case of bounded
continuous forcing
\xi\in C_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
. It is also
possible to prove, by a similar argument, that if
0<\tau<T

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{T}\\
\tau\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T\end{subarray}}|h(t,x)-h(s,y)|\leqslant C(\xi,\tau,T),

independently of the initial condition. Taking
w=e^{h}
, that is, the
solution of

\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)w=\xi w,\quad w(0)=e^{\bar{h}},

this yields

\underset{(t,x)\in[\tau,T]\times\mathbb{T}}{\sup}w(t,x)\leqslant K(\xi,\tau,T)\underset{(t,x)\in[\tau,T]\times\mathbb{T}}{\inf}w(t,x),

(22)

i.e., Harnack’s inequality for the heat equation (on the torus). Inequality
(
22
) does not hold in general in the whole space R, but if

0<t_{1}<T_{1}<t_{2}<T_{2}
and
M>0
, it is true that

\underset{(t,x)\in[t_{1},T_{1}]\times[-M,M]}{\sup}w(t,x)\leqslant K(\xi,M,t_{1},T_{1},t_{2},T_{2})\underset{(s,y)\in[t_{2},T_{2}]\times[-M,M]}{\inf}w(s,y),

which we can also derive as in Theorem
3.1
by noting that if

0<t_{1}\leqslant t\leqslant T_{1}<t_{2}\leqslant s\leqslant T_{2}
and
x\in[-M,M]
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}(s,0)}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}(t,x)}(y)=\frac{p_{\mathbb{R}}(s,y)}{p_{\mathbb{R}}(t,y-x)}\geqslant C(M,t_{1},T_{1},t_{2},T_{2}),

where it is essential that
t\leqslant T_{1}<t_{2}\leqslant s
.

3.2
The case of space-time white noise forcing

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(\mathbb{T})

for some
\beta>0
. Then, for any
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
and some positive constant

C\equiv C(\alpha,t,T,Y,Y^{\vee},Y^{R}),

the solution of the KPZ equation satisfies

|h(t,x)-h(t,0)|\leqslant C|x|^{\alpha},\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{T},

(23)

independently of the initial condition, where
\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}C(t,T,\alpha,\xi)<\infty
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

In a first step, we remove the forcing: consider
\hat{Y}
such that

\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)\hat{Y}=\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}Y^{R}|^{2}+\partial_{x}(Y+Y^{\vee}+Y^{R})\partial_{x}\hat{Y}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}\hat{Y}|^{2},\quad\hat{Y}(0)=0,

which only depends on
Y
,
Y^{\vee}
and
Y^{R}
, but not on
\bar{h}
. Then,

u:=h^{R}-\hat{Y}
(see Remark
2.10
) satisfies

\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\right)u=\partial_{x}(Y+Y^{\vee}+Y^{R}+\hat{Y})\partial_{x}u+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}u|^{2},\quad h^{R}(0)=\bar{h}-Y(0),

and we can prove in the same way as in Theorem
2.9
that

u(t,x)=\underset{v\in\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{m}}{\sup}\underset{\gamma\in\mathfrak{M}(v,x)}{\sup}\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{h}(\gamma_{t})-Y(0,\gamma_{t})-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}|v_{s}(\gamma)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right],

where
\gamma
is now given by

\gamma_{s}=x+\int^{s}_{0}v_{r}\mathrm{d}r+\int^{s}_{0}\partial_{x}(Y+Y^{\vee}+Y^{R}+\hat{Y})(t-r,\gamma_{r})\mathrm{d}r+B_{s}.

(24)

We can proceed as in Theorem
3.1
(as Lemma

2.11
and Corollary
2.15
still hold) to
conclude that

|u(t,x)-u(t,0)|\leqslant|\log\tilde{p}(0,x;t,y)-\log\tilde{p}(0,0;t,y)|\leqslant\underset{y\in\mathbb{T}}{\sup}\left\|\frac{\partial_{x}\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)}{\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)}\right\|_{\infty}|x|,

where
\tilde{p}
denotes the (periodised) transition density of
(
24
) with
v=0
, so overall we have, since

u=h-Y-Y^{\vee}-Y^{R}-\hat{Y},

that

|h(t,x)-h(t,0)|\leqslant\left(\|Y+Y^{\vee}+Y^{R}+\hat{Y}\|_{C_{T}\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}+C_{\alpha}\underset{y\in\mathbb{T}}{\sup}\left\|\frac{\partial_{x}\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)}{\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)}\right\|_{\infty}\right)|x|^{\alpha}.

By working in Besov spaces on the
L^{1}
scale and using that the Dirac delta
is in
B^{0}_{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we get
\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)\in B^{3/2-}_{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
for all
t>0
. Going until time

t/2
and embedding
B^{3/2-}_{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})\subset B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and then solving the equation on the

L^{\infty}
scale, we get that
\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)\in C^{3/2-}(\mathbb{T})
; see also Section 2 of
[
PvZ23
]
for a similar
argument. By the heat kernel above
\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)
is
uniformly lower bounded with bound depending on
t
, so overall

\underset{y\in\mathbb{T}}{\sup}\left\|\frac{\partial_{x}\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)}{\tilde{p}(0,\cdot;t,y)}\right\|_{\infty}=C(t)<\infty,

and
C(t)
is uniformly bounded on
[\varepsilon,T]
for any
\varepsilon>0
.)

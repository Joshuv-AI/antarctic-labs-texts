Optimal regularity results in Sobolev-Lorentz spaces for linear elliptic equations with L 1 L^{1} - or measure data
Hyunseok Kim, Young-Ran Lee, Jihoon Ok

Abstract
. It has been well known that if Ω \Omega is a bounded C 1 C^{1} -domain in ℝ n , n ≥ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ n\geq 2 , then for every Radon measure f f on Ω \Omega with finite total variation, there exists a unique weak solution u ∈ W 0 1 , 1 ​ ( Ω ) u\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega) of the Poisson equation − Δ ​ u = f -\Delta u=f in Ω \Omega satisfying ∇ u ∈ L n / ( n − 1 ) , ∞ ​ ( Ω , ℝ n ) \nabla u\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}) . In this paper, optimal regularity properties of the solution u u are established in Sobolev-Lorentz spaces L α p , q ​ ( Ω ) L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega) of order α \alpha less than but arbitrarily close to 2 2 . More precisely, for any 0 ≤ α < 1 0\leq\alpha<1 , we show that u ∈ L α + 1 p ⁡ ( α ) , ∞ ​ ( Ω ) u\in L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega) , where p ⁡ ( α ) = n / ( n − 1 + α ) p(\alpha)=n/(n-1+\alpha) . Moreover, using an embedding result for Sobolev-Lorentz spaces L α p , q ​ ( Ω ) L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega) into classical Besov spaces B α p , q ​ ( Ω ) B_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega) , we deduce that u ∈ B α + 1 p ⁡ ( α ) , ∞ ​ ( Ω ) u\in B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega) . Indeed, these regularity results are proved for solutions of the Dirichlet problems for more general linear elliptic equations with nonhomogeneous boundary data. On the other hand, it is known that if Ω \Omega is of class C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} , then for each G ∈ L 1 ​ ( Ω , ℝ n ) G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}) there exists a unique very weak solution v ∈ L n / ( n − 1 ) , ∞ ​ ( Ω ) v\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega) of − Δ ​ v = div ​ G -\Delta v={\rm div}\,G in Ω \Omega satisfying the boundary condition v = 0 v=0 in some sense. We prove that v v has the optimal regularity property, that is, v ∈ L α p ⁡ ( α ) , ∞ ​ ( Ω ) ∩ B α p ⁡ ( α ) , ∞ ​ ( Ω ) v\in L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\cap B_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega) for every 0 ≤ α < 1 0\leq\alpha<1 . This regularity result is also proved for more general equations with nonhomogeneous boundary data.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the following Dirichlet problems for linear elliptic equations on a bounded domain
\Omega
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ n\geq 2
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u+cu&=f&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&={u_{D}}&\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta v-{\rm div}\,(vb)+cv&={\rm div}\,G&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
v&=v_{D}&\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
f
is an integrable function or more generally a signed Radon measure on
\Omega
with finite total variation, and
G=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})
is an integrable vector field on
\Omega
.

Elliptic equations with
L^{1}
- or measure data have been studied extensively by many authors
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
22
,
24
]
for linear and nonlinear problems. A quite well-known result
(see, e.g.,
[
3
]
)
is that if the coefficients
b
and
c
are bounded and the domain
\Omega
is of class
C^{1}
, then for every Radon measure
f
on
\Omega
with finite total varation, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) with
u_{D}=0
satisfying
\nabla u\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
In addition, if
\Omega
is of class
C^{1,1}
, then it can be shown by a duality argument (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
) that
for every
G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, there exists a unique very weak solution
v\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
of (
1.2
) with
v_{D}=0
satisfying the boundary condition
v=0
in some very weak sense.
Here
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
denotes the Lorentz space on the domain
\Omega
, with
L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)
often referred to as the weak
L^{p}
-space or the Marcinkiewicz space on
\Omega
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish optimal regularity results for the weak solution
u
and the very weak solution
v
of the problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively, with
L^{1}
- or measure data, in the framework of Sobolev-Lorentz spaces as well as classical Besov spaces.

1.1.
Optimal regularity for the whole space case

To explore optimal regularity of solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with
L^{1}
- or measure data, we consider
the following simple elliptic equation in the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
:

(1.3)

-\Delta u+u=f\quad\mbox{in}\,\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Let
u
be a bounded smooth solution of (
1.3
) with
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Using the Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}=\widehat{\cdot}
, we obtain

\widehat{u}(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)}{1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}}.

For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
J^{s}=(I-\Delta)^{s/2}
be defined via the Fourier transform by

\widehat{J^{s}\varphi}(\xi)=\left(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s/2}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi).

Then for
0\leq\alpha<1
, we have

\widehat{J^{\alpha+1}u}(\xi)=\left(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-(1-\alpha)/2}\widehat{f}(\xi)

and

J^{\alpha+1}u(x)=J^{-(1-\alpha)}f(x)=\left(K_{1-\alpha}*f\right)(x),

where
K_{s}(x)=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left\{(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}\right\}(x)
. It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
16
, Proposition 6.1.5]
) that if
0<s<n
,
then

0<K_{s}(x)\leq\frac{C(n,s)}{|x|^{n-s}}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Note that

K_{1-\alpha}\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1+\alpha},\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Hence by Young’s convolution inequality (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Theorem 1.2.13]
), we have

(1.4)

\left\|(I-\Delta)^{(\alpha+1)/2}\,u\right\|_{L^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for every
0\leq\alpha<1
, where

(1.5)

p(\alpha)=\frac{n}{n-1+\alpha}.

Similarly, if
v
is a bounded smooth solution of

(1.6)

-\Delta v+v={\rm div}\,G\quad\mbox{in}\,\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
G=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
is a vector field on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then

\left\|(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}v\right\|_{L^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\|G\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for every
0\leq\alpha<1
.

For
-\infty<\alpha<\infty
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, let
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the Banach space of all tempered distributions
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

\|\varphi\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\varphi\|_{L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

is finite. Then by a standard density argument, it follows from the a priori estimate (
1.4
) that for each
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the problem (
1.3
) has a solution
u
satisfying

(1.7)

u\in L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|u\right\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for every
0\leq\alpha<1
. It is easily shown that such a solution
u
is unique. Further regularity of the solution
u
can be deduced from an embedding result for Sobolev-Lorentz spaces
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into classical Besov spaces
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Given
0\leq\alpha<1
, we choose any
\beta
such that

\alpha<\beta<1
. Then since
\beta-\alpha=n/p(\beta)-n/p(\alpha)
, it follows from the embedding theorem
[
27
, Theorem 1.2]
due to Seeger and Trebels (see Lemma
3.4
below) that
L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Hence the solution
u
of (
1.3
) also satisfies

(1.8)

u\in B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|u\right\|_{B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for every
0\leq\alpha<1
. By the same argument, it can be shown that
for every
G\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the problem (
1.6
) has a unique solution
v
satisfying

v\in L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

and

(1.9)

\left\|v\right\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(n,\alpha)\|G\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for every
0\leq\alpha<1
.

1.2.
Main results

The main purpose of the paper is to establish that solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) satisfy optimal regularity estimates analogous to (
1.7
), (
1.8
), and (
1.9
).

The data
f
and
G
will be assumed to be only integrable on
\Omega
. We also allow for even more general
f
in the Banach space
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
of all signed Radon measures on
\Omega
with finite total variation. It is well known that
L^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, that is,
L^{1}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, the boundary data
v_{D}
for the problem (
1.2
) can be a signed Radon measure on
\partial\Omega
.

To define weak solutions of (
1.1
) with
L^{1}
- or measure data
f
, we need to introduce Sobolev-Lorentz spaces and Besov spaces on the domain
\Omega
.
For
\alpha>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we denote by
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
the space of the restrictions of all functions in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
\Omega
:

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left\{f|_{\Omega}\,:\,f\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\},

which is a Banach space equipped with the usual quotient norm.
For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
W^{k,p,q}(\Omega)
be the Banach space of all
f\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)
such that
D^{\gamma}f\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)
for all multi-indices
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leq k
.
Then it can be shown (see Lemma
3.1
) that
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)=W^{k,p,q}(\Omega)
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
If
\alpha>1/p
, each function
f
in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
has a well-defined trace
{\rm Tr}\,f
in
L^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
. For
\alpha>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, let
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
denote the range space of the trace operator
{\rm Tr}:L_{\alpha+1/p}^{p,q}(\Omega)\to L^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
.
It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
) that if
1/p<\alpha<1/p+1
, then
\mathcal{B}^{p,p}_{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
is the boundary Besov space
B_{\alpha}^{p}(\partial\Omega)
.
The space of all
f\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
with
{\rm Tr}\,f=0
is denoted by
L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.
It can be shown (see Lemma
4.3
) that if
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
and
q<\infty
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. For
1<p<\infty
,
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
, and
1<q\leq\infty
, let
L_{-\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
denote the dual space of
L_{\alpha,0}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, where
r^{\prime}=r/(r-1)
is the Hölder conjugate to
r
. Then it will be proved in Lemma
4.5
that

\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega).

Finally, for
\alpha>0
, we define
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left\{f|_{\Omega}\,:\,f\in B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\}
.

For measure data or more generally
L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}
-data
f
, weak solutions of (
1.1
) are defined as follows, which is motivated by
[
22
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
f\in L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)
.
Then by a weak solution of (
1.1
), we mean a function
u\in L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
such that

b\cdot\nabla u,\,cu\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega),\quad{\rm Tr}\,u=u_{D}\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,

and

\int_{\Omega}\left[\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi+(\phi\,b)\cdot\nabla u+cu\phi\right]dx=\langle f,\phi\rangle\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

It should be noticed in Definition
1.1
that if
b
and
c
satisfy

b\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad c\in\begin{cases}L^{n/2,1}(\Omega)&\mbox{if}\,\,n\geq 3\\
\bigcup_{s>1}L^{s}(\Omega)&\mbox{if}\,\,n=2,\end{cases}

then by Sobolev’s and Hölder’s inequalities (see (
2.1
) and Lemma
3.3
below),

b\cdot\nabla u,\,cu\in L^{1}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,u\in L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega).

We are now ready to state the first main result in the paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Suppose that
b\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
c\in L^{n/2,1}(\Omega)\cap L^{s}(\Omega)
for some
s>1
, and
c\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Then for every
f\in L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)
, there exists a unique weak solution
u
of (
1.1
), which satisfies

\|u\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)

for some
C=C(n,s,\Omega,b,c)
. In addition, if
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{p(\alpha),\infty}_{\alpha+1-1/p(\alpha)}(\partial\Omega)
for some
0<\alpha<1
, then

u\in L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}+\|u_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p(\alpha),\infty}_{\alpha+1-1/p(\alpha)}(\partial\Omega)}\Big),

where
C=C(n,\alpha,s,\Omega,b,c)
. Here,
p(\alpha)=n/(n-1+\alpha)
as defined in (
1.5
).

Remark 1.3
.

Let
u
be the weak solution of (
1.1
) with
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{p(\alpha),\infty}_{\alpha+1-1/p(\alpha)}(\partial\Omega)
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then it follows from the embedding result (
4.2
) below that

u\in B_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u\|_{B_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}

for all
0\leq\beta<\alpha
, where
C=C(n,\Omega,\alpha,\beta)
.

Suppose in addition that
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{p,p}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
for some
p>1
.
If
0\leq\beta<1
, then by Lemma
3.3
(i) and (ii), we have

L_{2}^{p,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and so}\quad u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{p,p}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{p(\beta),\infty}_{\beta+1-1/p(\beta)}(\partial\Omega).

Hence it follows from Theorem
1.2
and (
4.2
) that

u\in L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\cap B_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for every}\,\,0\leq\beta<1.

For the special case when
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}=0
, the existence and the uniqueness assertions in Theorem
1.2
were already proved in
[
22
, Corollary 1.1]
. However the optimal regularity results in Theorem
1.2
and Remark
1.3
are completely new even for the Poisson equation, which corresponds to the simplest case when
b=0
and
c=0
.

The second main result is concerned with optimal regularity properties of very weak solutions of (
1.2
) with
L^{1}
- or measure data.

We begin with a trace result for very weak solutions of (
1.2
). For
\alpha>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1<q\leq\infty
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{-\alpha}^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
the dual space of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)
.
Indeed, it easily follows from Lemma
3.1
that if
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)=\left\{u|_{\partial\Omega}\,:\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\}
, then
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
is dense in
\mathcal{B}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
for
1<q\leq\infty
.
Hence
\mathcal{B}_{-\alpha}^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
is naturally embedded into the space of distributions on
\partial\Omega
.

Let
\nu
be the outward unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
. The normal derivative of a function
\psi
on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by
\partial_{\nu}\psi
, that is,
\partial_{\nu}\psi=\nabla\psi\cdot\nu
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Suppose that
b\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
c\in L^{n,1}(\Omega)
. Suppose in addition that

v\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega),\quad G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

and

\int_{\Omega}v\left(-\Delta\psi+b\cdot\nabla\psi+c\psi\right)dx=-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Then there exists a unique
\gamma_{0}v\in\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
such that

(1.10)

\left\langle\gamma_{0}v,\partial_{\nu}\psi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}v\left(-\Delta\psi+b\cdot\nabla\psi+c\psi\right)dx+\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

for all
\psi\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Moreover,

\|\gamma_{0}v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\right)

for some
C=C(n,\Omega)
. In addition, if
v\in L_{1}^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p>1
, then
\gamma_{0}v={\rm Tr\,}v
.

In particular, Theorem
1.4
shows that if
v\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
is a distributional solution of
-\Delta v={\rm div}\,G
in
\Omega
for some
G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then it has a well-defined trace
\gamma_{0}v
belonging to
\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, which extends the trace results in
[
21
, Theorem 8]
and
[
2
, Lemma 2]
.

Very weak solutions of (
1.2
) can be defined as follows.

Definition 1.5
.

Suppose that
G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
v_{D}\in\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
.
Then by a very weak solution of (
1.2
), we mean a function
v\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
such that

v|b|,\,cv\in L^{1}(\Omega)

and

\left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}\psi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}v\left(-\Delta\psi+b\cdot\nabla\psi+c\psi\right)dx+\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

for all
\psi\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

It follows from Theorem
1.4
that if
v
is a very weak solution of (
1.2
) in the sense of Definition
1.5
, then it has a well-defined trace
\gamma_{0}v
in
\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and
\gamma_{0}v=v_{D}
.

Note also that if
0\leq\beta<\alpha<1
, then

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha-1/p(\alpha)}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\beta-1/p(\beta)}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)

(see the proof of Theorem
1.6
). The following is the second main result in the paper.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Suppose that
b\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
c\in L^{n,1}(\Omega)
, and
c\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Then for every
G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
v_{D}\in\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, there exists a unique very weak solution
v
of (
1.2
), which satisfies

\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{-1+1/n}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\Big)

for some
C=C(n,\Omega,b,c)
. In addition, if

v_{D}\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha-1/p(\alpha)}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
for some
0<\alpha<1
, then we have

v\in L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|v\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha-1/p(\alpha)}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\Big),

where
C=C(n,\alpha,\Omega,b,c)
.

Remark 1.7
.

Let
v
be the very weak solution of (
1.2
) with
G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
v_{D}\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha-1/p(\alpha)}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\partial\Omega)
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Then it follows from the embedding result (
4.2
) that

v\in B_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|v\|_{B_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}

for every
0\leq\beta<\alpha
, where
C=C(n,\alpha,\beta,\Omega)
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(\partial\Omega)
be the Banach space of all signed Radon measures on
\partial\Omega
with finite total variation.
Then since

\mathcal{B}_{1/p(\beta)-\beta}^{p(\beta)^{\prime},1}(\partial\Omega)={\rm Tr}\,(L_{1-\beta}^{p(\beta)^{\prime},1}(\Omega))\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{1-\beta}^{p(\beta)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})

(see (
3.3
)), we have

\mathcal{M}(\partial\Omega)=\left[C(\partial\Omega)\right]^{*}\hookrightarrow\left[\mathcal{B}_{1/p(\beta)-\beta}^{p(\beta)^{\prime},1}(\partial\Omega)\right]^{*}=\mathcal{B}_{\beta-1/p(\beta)}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\partial\Omega)

for every
0\leq\beta<1
. Therefore, if
v_{D}\in\mathcal{M}(\partial\Omega)
, then the solution
v
satisfies

v\in L_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\cap B_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for every}\,\,0\leq\beta<1.

For the special case when
v_{D}=0
, existence and uniqueness of a very weak solution
v
of (
1.2
) were already proved in
[
22
, Corollary 1.2]
. However the optimal regularity results for
v
in Theorem
1.6
and Remark
1.7
are new even for the Poisson equation.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the preliminary Section 2, we review standard results for Lorentz spaces and then introduce the definition and basic properties of Sobolev-Lorentz spaces on the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Sobolev-Lorentz spaces on domains
\Omega
are studied in details in Section 3. Here we provide quite complete proofs of such fundamental properties for Sobolev-Lorentz spaces as trace, interpolation, density, duality, embedding, and so on. We also introduce classical Besov spaces on domains and boundary Besov spaces. Section 4 is devoted to studying the Poisson equation with measure data. In Section 5, making essential use of the results in Section 4, we prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
which are the main optimal regularity results in the paper. In the final Section 6, we provide a detailed proof of Theorem
1.4
which is our trace result for very weak solutions of (
1.2
).

2.
Preliminaries

For two nonnegative quantities
A
and
B
, we write
A\lesssim B
if there is a constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. If
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, we write
A\approx B
.
For two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we say that
X
is continuously embedded into
Y
and write
X\hookrightarrow Y
if
X\subset Y
and
\|u\|_{Y}\lesssim\|u\|_{X}
for all
u\in X
. If
X\hookrightarrow Y
and
Y\hookrightarrow X
, we write
X=Y
.

2.1.
Lorentz spaces

Let
\Omega
be any domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
or for
1\leq p=q\leq\infty
, let
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
denote the standard Lorentz space on
\Omega
which is a Banach space equipped with norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}
. Note that
L^{p,p}(\Omega)
coincides with the usual Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Hölder’s inequality can be extended to Lorentz spaces. More precisely, for
1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq q,q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
satisfying
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
and
1/q\leq 1/q_{1}+1/q_{2}
, there exists a constant
C=C(p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{1},q_{2})
such that

(2.1)

\|fg\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)}

for all
f\in L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)
(see, e.g.,
[
22
, Lemma 2.3]
).

An important property of Lorentz spaces is that a Lorentz space is the real interpolation space of two Lebesgue spaces (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 5.3.1]
): if
1\leq p_{0}\neq p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
then

(2.2)

\left(L^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}=L^{p,q}(\Omega).

Further properties of Lorentz spaces may be deduced from general results in real interpolation theory.
Recall that a pair
(X_{0},X_{1})
of Banach spaces is compatible if both
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are continuously embedded into a common Banach space. If
(X_{0},X_{1})
is a compatible pair of Banach spaces, then the intersection
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
and the sum
X_{0}+X_{1}
are well-defined Banach spaces with the norms

\|x\|_{X_{0}\cap X_{1}}=\max\left\{\|x\|_{X_{0}},\|x\|_{X_{1}}\right\}

and

\|x\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}=\inf\left\{\|x_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+\|x_{1}\|_{X_{1}}\,:\,x=x_{0}+x_{1},\,\,x_{0}\in X_{0},\,\,x_{1}\in X_{1}\right\},

respectively. For a Banach space
X
, we denote by
X^{*}
the dual space of
X
. The dual pairing of
X
and
X^{*}
will be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{*},X}
or simply
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

The following is an immediate consequence of standard density and duality results in real interpolation theory (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Sections 3.4 and 3.7]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
(X_{0},X_{1})
is compatible,
D
is a dense subset of
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

If
q<\infty
, then
D
is dense in
\left(X_{0},X_{1}\right)_{\theta,q}
.

(ii)

Suppose in addition that
D
is dense in both
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
. If
\widetilde{X}_{\theta,q}
denotes the closure of
D
in
\left(X_{0},X_{1}\right)_{\theta,q}
, then
[\widetilde{X}_{\theta,q}]^{*}=\left(X_{0}^{*},X_{1}^{*}\right)_{\theta,q^{\prime}}
. In particular, if
q<\infty
, then
[\left(X_{0},X_{1}\right)_{\theta,q}]^{*}=\left(X_{0}^{*},X_{1}^{*}\right)_{\theta,q^{\prime}}
.

Let
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be the space of all functions in
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
with support in
\Omega
. Then since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in any of
L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
,
L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
, and
L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
for
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
, it immediately follows from (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
that if
\widetilde{L}^{p,q}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
, then

(2.3)

\left[\widetilde{L}^{p,q}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for}\,\,1<p<\infty,\,\,1\leq q\leq\infty.

Moreover, if
1\leq q<\infty
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
and

(2.4)

\left[L^{p,q}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Hence
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
is reflexive for
1<p,q<\infty
.

It is well known that the family of Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
is a complex interpolation scale for
1\leq p\leq\infty
: more precisely,
if
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, then

\left[\,L^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right]_{\theta}=L^{p}(\Omega).

The following is a complex counterpart of Lemma
2.1
(see
[
5
, Sections 4.2 and 4.5]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(X_{0},X_{1})
is compatible,
D
is a dense subset of
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, and
0<\theta<1
.

(i)

D
is dense in
\left[X_{0},X_{1}\right]_{\theta}
.

(ii)

Suppose in addition that
X_{0}
or
X_{1}
is reflexive and
D
is dense in both
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
. Then
\left[X_{0},X_{1}\right]_{\theta}^{*}=\left[X_{0}^{*},X_{1}^{*}\right]_{\theta}
.

2.2.
Sobolev-Lorentz spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

As in Subsection 1.1, for
-\infty<\alpha<\infty
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we denote by
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the space of all tempered distributions
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\in L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. The Sobolev-Lorentz space
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Note in particular that
L_{0}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
If
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
L_{\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=L_{\alpha}^{p,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Recall then from interpolation by the complex method (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 6.4.5]
) that if
0<\theta<1
,
\alpha=(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, then

L_{\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\left[\,L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),L_{\alpha_{1}}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,\right]_{\theta}.

By definition,
(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is an isometric isomorphism from

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
onto
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and its inverse is
(I-\Delta)^{-\alpha/2}
. Hence it immediately follows from the interpolation result (
2.2
) for Lorentz spaces that if
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, then

(2.5)

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\left(L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)_{\theta,q}.

The following are some basic properties of Sobolev-Lorentz spaces
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
-\infty<\alpha,\alpha_{0}<\infty
,
1<p,p_{0}<\infty
, and
1\leq q,q_{0}\leq\infty
.

(i)

If
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and
q\leq q_{0}
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p,q_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. In particular, if
\alpha>0
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{-\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
\alpha-\alpha_{0}=n/p-n/p_{0}>0
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. In particular, if
0<\alpha p<n
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{np/(n-\alpha p),q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(iii)

If
\alpha p=n
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(iv)

If
k
is a positive integer, then
L_{k}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=W^{k,p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
W^{k,p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the Banach space of all
f\in L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
D^{\gamma}f\in L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for all multi-indices
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leq k
.

(v)

If
q<\infty
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\left[L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right]^{*}=L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Parts (i)-(v) are essentially known.
For instance, the embedding results (i) and (ii) are just special cases of
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
and Part (iv) was proved in
[
10
, Theorem 5.5]
.

To prove Part (v), suppose that
q<\infty
. Choose
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
such that
k>\alpha
and
2/p=1/p_{0}+1/p_{1}
. Then by (iv), (
2.5
), and (i), we have

\left(W^{k,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),W^{k,p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)_{1/2,q}=L_{k}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

It is well known that
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
W^{k,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap W^{k,p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Hence it follows from Lemma
2.1
(i) that
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
L_{k}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
To prove that
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, it thus remains to prove that
L_{k}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Let
f\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be given. Then by definition,
g=(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f
belongs to
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Since
q<\infty
,
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is dense in
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Hence there is a sequence
\{g_{j}\}
in
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
g_{j}\to g
in
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. For each
j
, define
f_{j}=(I-\Delta)^{-\alpha/2}g_{j}
. Then
\{f_{j}\}
is a sequence in
L_{k}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
that converges to
f
in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. This proves the density result in Part (v).
Then the duality result in Part (v) easily follows from the duality result (
2.4
) for Lorentz spaces since
(I-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is an isometric isomorphism from
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
onto
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We finally prove Part (iii). By (v), it suffices to prove that

(2.6)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(n,p)\|f\|_{L_{n/p}^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Although (
2.6
) is a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 5.8 (iii)]
, here we provide a rather simple proof for the sake of the readers’ convenience.
Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
g=(I-\Delta)^{\alpha}f
, where
\alpha=n/p
. Then

f(x)=(K_{\alpha}*g)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}K_{\alpha}(y)g(x-y)\,dy

for some
K_{\alpha}\in L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as in Subsection
1.1
. Hence by Hölder’s inequality (
2.1
) in Lorentz spaces, we have

\displaystyle|f(x)|\lesssim\|K_{\alpha}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\|g(x-\cdot)\|_{L^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L_{n/p}^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which proves (
2.6
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Sobolev-Lorentz spaces on domains

Throughout this section, let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. If
f
is a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let
R_{\Omega}f
denote the restriction of
f
to
\Omega
.

3.1.
Definitions and basic properties

As in Subsection 1.2, for
\alpha\geq 0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, let

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
be the space of the restrictions to
\Omega
of all functions in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
which is a Banach space equipped with the usual quotient norm

\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\|f\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\,:\,f\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\ R_{\Omega}f=u\right\}.

By definition,
R_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. Note also that
L_{0}^{p,q}(\Omega)=L^{p,q}(\Omega)
and
L_{0}^{p,p}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega)
. If
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=L_{\alpha}^{p,p}(\Omega)
. Then it is well known (see, e.g.,
[
14
, p.329]
) that
L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
is reflexive for
\alpha\geq 0
and
1<p<\infty
. Moreover, it follows from
[
18
, Proposition 2.4]
that
L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
is a complex interpolation scale for
\alpha\geq 0
and
1<p<\infty
, that is, if
0\leq\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}<\infty
,
1<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,

\alpha=(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, then

(3.1)

L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=\left[L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha_{1}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right]_{\theta}.

For a positive integer
k
, let
W^{k,p,q}(\Omega)
be the Banach space of all
f\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)
such that
D^{\gamma}f\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)
for all multi-indices
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\leq k
. We write
W^{0,p,q}(\Omega)=L^{p,q}(\Omega)
.
If
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
W^{k,p}(\Omega)=W^{k,p,p}(\Omega)
.
Then it is also well known (see, e.g.,
[
18
, (2.2)]
) that if
k
is any positive integer, then

(3.2)

L_{k}^{p}(\Omega)=W^{k,p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for}\,\,1<p<\infty.

As usual,
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
is defined even for
p=1
or
p=\infty
. By the universal extension theorem due to Stein
[
28
, Chapter VI]
, there exists a linear operator
E_{\Omega}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

(1)

R_{\Omega}E_{\Omega}u=u
for all
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
;

(2)

E_{\Omega}
is bounded from
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
into
W^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<\infty
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

E_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, then

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}.

(iii)

If
k
is a positive integer, then
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)=W^{k,p,q}(\Omega)
.

(iv)

If
q<\infty
, then
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is dense in
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, where
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})=R_{\Omega}\left(C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
.

By definition, the restriction operator
R_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into
L_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}(\Omega)
for each
i=0,1
. Hence, by real interpolation, it follows from (
2.5
) that
R_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into
\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
. This implies that
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=R_{\Omega}(L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\hookrightarrow\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
. On the other hand, by
[
18
, Proposition 2.4]
,
E_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for each
i=0,1
. Hence by real interpolation,
E_{\Omega}
is bounded from
\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
into
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
It is then easy to check that
\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. This proves Part (i) as well as Part (ii).

To prove Part (iii), let
k
be a positive integer. Then it follows from a nontrivial
interpolation result due to DeVore and Scherer
[
12
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
22
, Lemma 2.6]
) that

\left(W^{k,p_{0}}(\Omega),W^{k,p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}=W^{k,p,q}(\Omega).

Hence by Part (ii) and (
3.2
), we have

L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left(W^{k,p_{0}}(\Omega),W^{k,p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}=W^{k,p,q}(\Omega),

which proves Part (iii).
Finally, Part (iv) is easily deduced from Lemma
2.3
(v).
∎

For
\alpha\geq 0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
let
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})
be the space of all
f\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with support in
\overline{\Omega}
, which is a closed subspace of
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then
R_{\Omega}
is an injection from
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})
into
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. Hence
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
, the image of
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})
under
R_{\Omega}
, is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|R_{\Omega}f\|_{R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))}=\|f\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,f\in\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}).

For a function
u
on
\Omega
, we denote by
\widetilde{u}
the zero extension of
u
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, defined by

\widetilde{u}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)&\mbox{for}\,\,x\in\Omega\\
\,\,\,0&\mbox{for}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Then since
\partial\Omega
has measure zero, it immediately follows that

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=\left\{u\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)\,:\,\widetilde{u}\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\}\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)

and

\|u\|_{R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))}=\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\geq\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,u\in R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})).

Let
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
denote the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
.
Note that

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{0}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=L^{p,q}(\Omega),\quad\widetilde{L}_{0}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\widetilde{L}^{p,q}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left[\widetilde{L}_{0}^{p,q}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega).

For
\alpha>0
, we define
L_{-\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
as the dual space of
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.
Finally, if
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\overline{\Omega})=\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,p}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,p}(\Omega)
, and
L_{-\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=L_{-\alpha}^{p,p}(\Omega)
.
Then it was shown in
[
18
, Remark 2.7]
and
[
14
, p.329]
(see also
[
29
, Theorem 3.5]
) that

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\overline{\Omega}))
, that is,
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\overline{\Omega}))
and the Banach spaces
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
and
L_{-\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
are reflexive. Moreover it follows from
[
18
, Proposition 2.11]
and Lemma
2.2
that
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
and
L_{-\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
are complex interpolation scales for
\alpha\geq 0
and
1<p<\infty
: if
0\leq\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}<\infty
,
1<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
\alpha=(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, then

(3.3)

\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=\left[\widetilde{L}_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha_{1}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right]_{\theta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{-\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=\left[L_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{-\alpha_{1}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right]_{\theta}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<\infty
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

If
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, then

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{-\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left(L_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{-\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}.

(ii)

If
q<\infty
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
, that is,
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
.

(iii)

If
k
is a positive integer, then
\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.

(iv)

If
q>1
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\left[L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
.

Proof.

Suppose that
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
.

We first prove Part (i) by following Mayboroda
[
23
]
. Consider the operator

P=I-E_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\,R_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}:L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})+L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})+L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

where
R_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}
is the restriction operator to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and
E_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}
is Stein’s universal extension operator.
Then
P
is a projection (that is, a bounded linear operator with
P^{2}=P
) on
L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, respectively.
It is easy to show that

\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})=P(L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})).

Hence by (
2.5
) and
[
23
, Lemma 3.1.9]
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})=P(L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

\displaystyle=P(\left(L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)_{\theta,q})

\displaystyle=\left(P(L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})),P(L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\right)_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\overline{\Omega}),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\overline{\Omega})\right)_{\theta,q}.

Moreover, since
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}(\overline{\Omega}))=\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}(\Omega)
for
i=0,1
, we have

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=R_{\Omega}\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\overline{\Omega}),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\overline{\Omega})\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}.

To prove the reverse embedding, let
E^{0}_{\Omega}
denote the zero extension operator from
\Omega
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, defined by
E^{0}_{\Omega}u=\widetilde{u}
. It immediately follows from (
2.5
) that

E_{\Omega}^{0}\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow\left(L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right)_{\theta,q}=L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Hence using the fact that
R_{\Omega}\circ P\circ E^{0}_{\Omega}=I
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle=\left(R_{\Omega}\circ P\circ E^{0}_{\Omega}\right)\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow(R_{\Omega}\circ P)(L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))=R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})).

which proves the first interpolation result in Part (i), that is,

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}.

Recall that
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
. Hence
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
\left(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}
. Therefore, by the duality theorem (Lemma
2.1
(ii)), we have

L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)=\left[\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=\left(L_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega),L_{-\alpha}^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q^{\prime}},

which proves Part (i). Part (ii) immediately follows from Part (i) and Lemma
2.1
(i).

To prove Part (iii), let
k
be a positive integer. Since
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))\hookrightarrow L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, it is obvious that
\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
is a subset of the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.
Suppose that
u
belongs to the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a sequence
\{u_{j}\}
in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
u_{j}\to u
in
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.
By Lemma
2.3
(iv) and Lemma
3.1
(iii), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{j}-u_{m}\|_{R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{u}_{j}-\widetilde{u}_{m}\|_{L_{k}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\approx\|\widetilde{u}_{j}-\widetilde{u}_{m}\|_{W^{k,p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|{u}_{j}-u_{m}\|_{W^{k,p,q}(\Omega)}\approx\|{u}_{j}-u_{m}\|_{L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)}

for all
j,m\geq 1
. Hence
\{u_{j}\}
converges in
R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))
to some
v
. Since
u_{j}\to u
and
u_{j}\to v
in
L_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, we deduce that

u=v\in\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, which proves Part (iii).

It remains to prove Part (iv).
Suppose that
1<q\leq\infty
. We may assume that
0<\alpha<k
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then by (
3.3
), we have

L_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)=\left[L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega),L_{-k}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]_{\alpha/k}.

Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)=L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\cap L_{-k}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
(i) that

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in

L_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Similarly,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L_{-\alpha}^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.
Since
\Omega
is bounded,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is also dense in
L_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\cap L_{-\alpha}^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Recall that
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}(\Omega)
is reflexive and its dual is
L_{-\alpha}^{p_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
for
i=0,1
.
Hence it follows from Part (i) and Lemma
2.1
that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and

\left[L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=\left(\,\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\ \widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}=R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega})).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.2.
Embedding results

We next prove several embedding results for Sobolev-Lorentz spaces
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
-\infty<\alpha,\alpha_{0}<\infty
,
1<p,p_{0}<\infty
, and
1\leq q,q_{0}\leq\infty
.

(i)

If
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and
q\leq q_{0}
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p,q_{0}}(\Omega)
. In particular, if
\alpha>0
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{-\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

If
p>p_{0}
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p_{0},q_{0}}(\Omega)
.

(iii)

If
\alpha-\alpha_{0}=n/p-n/p_{0}>0
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}(\Omega)
. In particular, if
0<\alpha p<n
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{np/(n-\alpha p),q}(\Omega)
.

(iv)

If
\alpha p=n
, then
L_{\alpha}^{p,1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
and
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega)
, where
C_{0}(\Omega)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)

Proof.

To prove Part (i), suppose that
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and
q\leq q_{0}
. If
\alpha_{0}\geq 0
, then the embeddings
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p,q_{0}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p,q_{0}}(\Omega)
immediately follow from Lemma
3.1
(i) and Lemma
2.3
(i).
Suppose that
\alpha_{0}<0
. Then since
-\alpha_{0}>0
and
q_{0}^{\prime}\leq q^{\prime}
, it follows that
L_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Using this embedding, we easily deduce that

\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Hence by the duality result (
2.3
),

L^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left[\widetilde{L}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}\hookrightarrow\left[\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p,q_{0}}(\Omega).

Therefore, if
\alpha\geq 0>\alpha_{0}
, then

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p,q_{0}}(\Omega)
.
Suppose finally that
\alpha<0
. Then since
-\alpha_{0}\geq-\alpha>0
and
q_{0}^{\prime}\leq q^{\prime}
, we have

L_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and so}\quad\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Therefore,

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left[\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}\hookrightarrow\left[\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p,q_{0}}(\Omega).

This proves Part (i).

To prove Part (ii), suppose that
p>p_{0}
. The case
\alpha=0
is trivial. Suppose that
\alpha>0
. If
k
is a positive integer, then
L_{k}^{p,p}(\Omega)=W^{k,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{k,p_{0}}(\Omega)=L_{k}^{p_{0},p_{0}}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, it follows from Lemma
3.2
(iii) that
\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{k}^{p_{0},p_{0}}(\Omega)
.
Hence, if
k
is any integer such that
k>\alpha
, then by (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

L_{\alpha}^{p,p}(\Omega)=\left[L^{p}(\Omega),L_{k}^{p}(\Omega)\right]_{\alpha/k}\hookrightarrow\left[L^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{k}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\right]_{\alpha/k}=L_{\alpha}^{p_{0},p_{0}}(\Omega)

and similarly

\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0},p_{0}}(\Omega).

For the general case when
q\neq p
or
q_{0}\neq p_{0}
, we choose
1<p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}<\infty
such that
p_{1}<p_{0}<p_{2}<p<p_{3}
,
2/p_{0}=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
, and
2/p=1/p_{2}+1/p_{3}
.
Then since
L_{\alpha}^{p_{3}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p_{2}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
, it follows from Lemma
3.1
(ii) that

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{2}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{3}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{1/2,q}\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p_{2}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{2}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{1/2,q_{0}}=L_{\alpha}^{p_{0},q_{0}}(\Omega).

Similarly, since
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{3}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{2}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
, it follows from Lemma
3.2
(i) that

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))\hookrightarrow R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0},q_{0}}(\overline{\Omega}))

and hence

\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p_{0},q_{0}}(\Omega).

Suppose next that
\alpha<0
. Then since
\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is dense in
\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, we immediately obtain

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left[\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}\hookrightarrow\left[\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)\right]^{*}=L_{\alpha}^{p_{0},q_{0}}(\Omega),

which proves Part (ii).

To prove Part (iii), suppose that
\alpha-\alpha_{0}=n/p-n/p_{0}>0
. If
\alpha_{0}\geq 0
, then the embeddings
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}(\Omega)
immediately follow from Lemma
3.1
(i) and Lemma
2.3
(ii). Suppose that
\alpha\leq 0
. Then since
(-\alpha_{0})-(-\alpha)=n/p_{0}^{\prime}-n/p^{\prime}>0
, we have

L_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

from which we easily deduce that

\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0}^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\widetilde{L}_{-\alpha}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}(\Omega).

If
\alpha>0>\alpha_{0}
, then choosing
p_{1}
such that
\alpha=n/p-n/p_{1}
, we have

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{1},q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}(\Omega).

This proves Part (iii).
Finally, Part (iv) is easily deduced from Lemma
3.1
(i) and Lemma
2.3
(iii).
∎

We will also need embedding results for Sobolev-Lorentz spaces into classical Besov spaces. For
-\infty<\alpha<\infty
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, let
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
denote the familiar Besov space on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In particular, for
0<\alpha<1
and
1\leq q<\infty
,
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
may be defined as the Banach space of all
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{|t|^{n+\alpha q}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|f(x+t)-f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{q/p}\,dt\right]^{1/q}

is finite (see, e.g.,
[
18
, p. 172]
). If
0<\alpha<1
and
q=\infty
, then the corresponding norm is

\|f\|_{B_{\alpha}^{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\sup_{t\neq 0}\frac{1}{|t|^{\alpha}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|f(x+t)-f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Recall also from
[
18
, (2.13)]
that
for any real
\alpha
,

f\in B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
if and only if
f\in B_{\alpha-1}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\nabla f\in B_{\alpha-1}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
For
\alpha\geq 0
, we define

B_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left\{R_{\Omega}f\,:\,f\in B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\},

which is a Banach space equipped with the usual quotient norm. Then
R_{\Omega}
is bounded from
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
and
E_{\Omega}
is bounded from
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(see
[
18
, Proposition 2.17]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0\leq\alpha,\alpha_{0}<\infty
,
1<p,p_{0}<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
If
\alpha-\alpha_{0}=n/p-n/p_{0}>0
, then

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow B_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow B_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}(\Omega).

Proof.

The lemma is an immediate consequence of a general embedding result due to Seeger and Trebels
[
27
]
, who indeed established complete embedding results for Tribel-Lizorkin-Lorentz spaces
F_{r}^{\alpha}[L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]
and Besov-Lorentz spaces
B_{q}^{\alpha}[L^{p,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]
.

Suppose that
\alpha-\alpha_{0}=n/p-n/p_{0}>0
. Then it follows from
[
27
, Theorem 1.2]
that

(3.4)

F_{r}^{\alpha}[L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]\hookrightarrow B_{q}^{\alpha_{0}}[L^{p_{0},r_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]\quad\mbox{for any}\,\,1\leq r,r_{0}\leq\infty.

Note that

F_{2}^{\alpha}[L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]=L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{q}^{\alpha_{0}}[L^{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]=B_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

(see
[
27
, p. 1018]
). Hence by (
3.4
), we have

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow B_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

which implies, by the boundeness of
R_{\Omega}
and
E_{\Omega}
, that

L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow B_{\alpha_{0}}^{p_{0},q}(\Omega)
.
∎

3.3.
Trace results

The trace operator
{\rm Tr}
is defined initially on
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
by

{\rm Tr}\,u=u|_{\partial\Omega}\quad\text{for all }u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}).

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1/p<\alpha<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Choose
1<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
such that
1/p_{1}<1/p<1/p_{0}<\alpha
and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
. Then it follows from the classical trace theorem (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Theorems 1 and 2]
,
[
20
, Chapter VII]
, and
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
) that for each
i=0,1
,
{\rm Tr}
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator
T_{i}
from
L_{\alpha}^{p_{i}}(\Omega)
into
L^{p_{i}}(\partial\Omega)
.
Note that
L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)=L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
and
T_{0}=T_{1}
on
L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
.
Hence by Lemma
3.1
(ii),
{\rm Tr}
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator from
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
L^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
, still denoted by
{\rm Tr}
. Let
L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
be the kernel of the trace operator
{\rm Tr}:L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
:

L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\,:\,{\rm Tr}\,u=0\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\right\}.

For
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
L_{\alpha,0}^{p}(\Omega)=L_{\alpha,0}^{p,p}(\Omega)
. Then it follows from Lemma
3.2
(ii) and
[
18
, Proposition 3.3]
that

(3.5)

\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)=L_{\alpha,0}^{p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for}\,\,1/p<\alpha<1+1/p.

For
s>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
let
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
denote the range of
{\rm Tr}:L_{s+1/p}^{p,q}(\Omega)\to L^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
, which is a Banach space equipped with the usual quotient norm. Then it follows from Lemma
3.1
(iv) that if
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)=\left\{u|_{\partial\Omega}\,:\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\right\}
, then
C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
is dense in
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
for
1\leq q<\infty
. If
s>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1<q\leq\infty
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{-s}^{p,q}(\partial\Omega)
the dual space of
\mathcal{B}_{s}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)
.
For
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{s}(\partial\Omega)=\mathcal{B}^{p,p}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
. It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
) that if
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, then
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
coincides with the standard boundary Besov space
B_{s}^{p}(\partial\Omega)
.
In general,
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
is a real interpolation space of two boundary Besov spaces.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

There is a bounded linear operator
{\rm Ex}:\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
such that
{\rm Tr}\circ{\rm Ex}
is the identity on
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
.

(ii)

If
1<p_{0}<p<p_{1}<\infty
,
1/p_{0}<\alpha<1+1/p_{1}
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
,
then

\left(B^{p_{0}}_{\alpha-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{\alpha-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}=\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega).

Proof.

Suppose that
1<p_{0}<p<p_{1}<\infty
,
1/p_{0}<\alpha<1+1/p_{1}
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
.

Since
{\rm Tr}
is bounded from
L^{p_{i}}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
into
B^{p_{i}}_{\alpha-1/p_{i}}(\partial\Omega)
for
i=0,1
, it follows from Lemma
3.1
(ii) that
{\rm Tr}
is bounded from
L^{p,q}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
into
\left(B^{p_{0}}_{\alpha-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{\alpha-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
, we have

\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow\left(B^{p_{0}}_{\alpha-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{\alpha-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}.

To prove the reverse embedding, we recall from
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
that
there is a linear extension operator
{\rm Ex}
such that
{\rm Ex}
maps
B^{r}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
into
L^{r}_{s+1/r}(\Omega)
boundedly and
{\rm Tr}\circ{\rm Ex}
is the identity operator on
B^{r}_{s}(\partial\Omega)
for all
0<s<1
and
1<r<\infty
. By Lemma
3.1
(ii) again,
{\rm Ex}
is bounded from
\left(B^{p_{0}}_{\alpha-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{\alpha-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}
into
L^{p,q}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
. Since
{\rm Tr}\circ{\rm Ex}
is the identity and
{\rm Tr}
is bounded from
L^{p,q}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
onto
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
, we obtain

\left(B^{p_{0}}_{\alpha-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{\alpha-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{\alpha-1/p}(\partial\Omega).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The interpolation result in Lemma
3.5
should be compared with the following classical one (see
[
18
, (2.15)]
):

\big(\,B^{p,q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),B^{p,q_{2}}_{\alpha_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,\big)_{\theta,q}=B^{p,q}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

whenever
-\infty<\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
\alpha=(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}
, and
1\leq p,q_{1},q_{2},q\leq\infty
.

4.
Poisson equation

In this section, we consider the Dirichlet problem for the Poisson equation:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
.

The Laplacian
\Delta
is defined initially on
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Let
1<p<\infty
. Then by Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta u)\psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta\psi)\,dx\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{L_{2}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

for all
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Hence
-\Delta
can be extended uniquely to a bounded linear operator from
L^{p}(\Omega)
into
L_{-2}^{p}(\Omega)
, by defining

\langle-\Delta u,g\rangle=\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta g)\,dx\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,g\in\widetilde{L}_{2}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Obviously,
-\Delta
is bounded from
L_{2}^{p}(\Omega)
into
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
On the other hand, it follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) that

\left[\,L^{p}(\Omega),L_{2}^{p}(\Omega)\,\right]_{\alpha/2}=L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left[\,L_{-2}^{p}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)\,\right]_{\alpha/2}=L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)

for
0<\alpha<2
. Hence, by complex interpolation,
-\Delta
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
. Moreover, by real interpolation, it follows from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
that
-\Delta
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

4.1.
Solvability for data in
L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
with
1/p<\alpha\leq 2

We start with unique solvability results for the Poisson equation with data in
L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)
with
1/p<\alpha\leq 2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(i)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Assume that
1<p<\infty
and
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
.
Then for every
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{\alpha,0}^{p}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
). Moreover,

\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)}

for some
C=C(n,\alpha,p,\Omega)
.

(ii)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
.
Assume that
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
. Then for every
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{1,0}^{p}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
). Moreover,

\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)}

for some
C=C(n,\alpha,p,\Omega)
.

Proof.

Part (i) is a very special case of
[
18
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]
.
To prove Part (ii), suppose that
\Omega
is of class
C^{1,1}
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
. The case
\alpha=1
is covered by Part (i) and the case
\alpha=2
follows from the classical Calderon-Zygmund theory.
Suppose that
1<\alpha<2
.
Then it follows from Part (i) with
\alpha=1

that for every
f\in L_{-1}^{p}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{1,0}^{p}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
). Let
T:L_{-1}^{p}(\Omega)\to L_{1,0}^{p}(\Omega)
be the associated solution operator for (
4.1
), which is linear and bounded.
Since
L^{p}_{\alpha-2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{-1}^{p}(\Omega)
,
T
is bounded from
L^{p}_{\alpha-2}(\Omega)
into
L_{1,0}^{p}(\Omega)
.
By the Calderon-Zygmund theory,
T
is also bounded from
L^{p}(\Omega)
into
L_{2}^{p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.1
) that

L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)=\left[\,L_{-1}^{p}(\Omega),L^{p}(\Omega)\,\right]_{\alpha-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left[\,L_{1}^{p}(\Omega),L_{2}^{p}(\Omega)\,\right]_{\alpha-1}=L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega).

Therefore, by complex interpolation, we conclude that
T
is bounded from
L_{\alpha-2}^{p}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha}^{p}(\Omega)
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Using the above solvability result, we obtain the following interpolation results.

Lemma 4.2
.

(i)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Assume that
1<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
,
1/p_{0}<\alpha<1+1/p_{1}
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then

\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}=L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega).

(ii)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Assume that
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then

\left(\,L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{1,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}=L_{1,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega).

Proof.

We first prove Part (i). Suppose that
\Omega
is of class
C^{1}
,
1<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
,
1/p_{0}<\alpha<1+1/p_{1}
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then it follows from Lemma
4.1
(i) that for every
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
). Let
T:L_{\alpha-2}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\to L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
be the associated solution operator for (
4.1
). By Lemma
4.1
(i) again,
T
is bounded from
L_{\alpha-2}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
.
Hence by Lemma
3.2
(i),
T
is bounded from
L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
.
Suppose that
u\in L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
and
f=-\Delta u
. Then
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
and
Tf\in\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
. Since
u,Tf\in L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
and
-\Delta u=-\Delta(Tf)
in
\Omega
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
(i) that

u=Tf
and

\|u\|_{\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)}.

This implies that
L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
. To prove the reverse embedding, we simply observe that

\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow\left(\,L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}=L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega),

\left(\,L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)=L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega).

This proves Part (i).

We next prove Part (ii). Suppose that
\Omega
is of class
C^{1,1}
,
1<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Then since
1/p_{0}<1<1+1/p_{1}
, it follows from Part (i) and Lemma
3.1
(ii) that

\left(\,L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{1,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow L_{1,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega).

To prove the reverse embedding, let
T:L_{\alpha-2}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\to L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
be the solution operator for (
4.1
), which is bounded by Lemma
4.1
. Then by Lemma
3.2
(i),
T
is also bounded from
L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
\left(\,L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{1,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
. Suppose that
u\in L_{1,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
and
f=-\Delta u
. Then
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
,
u,Tf\in L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
, and
-\Delta u=-\Delta Tf
in
\Omega
. Hence by Lemma
4.1
(i), we have

u=Tf\in\left(\,L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{1,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}

and

\|u\|_{\left(\,L_{1,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{1,0}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Immediately from Lemma
3.2
, (
3.5
), and Lemma
4.2
, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. If
1<p<\infty
,
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, then

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. In addition, if
q<\infty
, then

\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)=L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.

We are ready to prove the following unique solvability results for the Poisson equation with data in
L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, where
1/p<\alpha\leq 2
.

Theorem 4.4
.

(i)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Assume that

1<p<\infty
,
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then for every
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
). Moreover,

\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)}

for some
C=C(n,\alpha,p,q,\Omega)
.

(ii)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Assume that
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then for every
f\in L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{1,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)\cap L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
). Moreover,

\|u\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)}

for some
C=C(n,\alpha,p,q,\Omega)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Omega
is of class
C^{1}
,
1<p<\infty
,
1/p<\alpha<1+1/p
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Choose
1<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
such that
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
and
1/p_{0}<\alpha<1+1/p_{1}
.
Let
T:L_{\alpha-2}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\to L_{\alpha,0}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
be the solution operator for (
4.1
). Then by the proof of Lemma
4.2
(i),
T
is bounded from
L_{\alpha-2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
L_{\alpha,0}^{p,q}(\Omega)
, which proves the existence assertion and estimate of Part (i). The uniqueness follows from Lemma
4.1
(i). This proves Part (i). Part (ii) is similarly proved by using the proof of Lemma
4.2
(ii).
∎

4.2.
Solvability for measure data

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
the space of all signed Radon measures
\mu
on
\Omega
with finite total variation
|\mu|(\Omega)
. Recall (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Chapter 7]
) that

(i)

\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}=|\mu|(\Omega)
;

(ii)

L^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
: more precisely, the mapping
f\mapsto\mu_{f}
, defined by

\mu_{f}(A)=\int_{A}f\,dx\quad\mbox{for all Borel sets}\,\,A\subset\Omega,

is an isometry from
L^{1}(\Omega)
into
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
;

(iii)

\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is the dual space of

C_{0}(\Omega)
: more precisely, the mapping
\mu\mapsto f_{\mu}
, defined by

\langle f_{\mu},\phi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\mu\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\phi\in C_{0}(\Omega),

is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
onto
\left[C_{0}(\Omega)\right]^{*}
.

Recall from (
1.5
) that

p(\alpha)=\frac{n}{n-1+\alpha}\quad\mbox{for}\,\,0\leq\alpha<1.

Note that
1<p(\alpha)\leq n/(n-1)
and

\beta-\alpha=n/p(\beta)-n/p(\alpha)>0
if
0\leq\alpha<\beta<1
.
Hence it follows from Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
that if
0\leq\alpha<\beta<1
and
\gamma\geq 0
, then

(4.2)

L_{\beta+\gamma}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha+\gamma}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\cap B_{\alpha+\gamma}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then for
0\leq\alpha<1
, we have

\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
be given.
Then since

(1-\alpha)p(\alpha)^{\prime}=n
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
(iv) that

\widetilde{L}_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C_{0}(\Omega)
.
Hence for all
\phi\in\widetilde{L}_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
, we have

\langle f_{\mu},\phi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\mu\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{\widetilde{L}_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)},

which implies that

f_{\mu}\in L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)=\left[\widetilde{L}_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\right]^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f_{\mu}\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
.
Then for every
f\in L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{1,0}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
), which satisfies

(4.3)

\|u\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}

for some
C=C(n,\Omega)
. In addition, if
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, then for every
0\leq\alpha<1
,

\widetilde{u}\in L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)},

where
C=C(n,\alpha,\Omega)
. Here
\widetilde{u}
denotes the zero extension of
u
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
be given. Then it immediately follows from Theorem
4.4
(i) that there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{1,0}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
), which satisfies the estimate (
4.3
).

Suppose in addition that
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
.
Note that if
0\leq\alpha<1
, then
1<p(\alpha)<\infty
and
1/p(\alpha)<\alpha+1<1+1/p(\alpha)
. Hence it follows from Lemma
4.5
and Theorem
4.4
(i) that for every
0\leq\alpha<1
, there exists a unique solution
u_{\alpha}\in L_{\alpha+1,0}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)
of (
4.1
), which satisfies

(4.4)

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
be fixed. Choose any
\beta
such that

\alpha<\beta<1
. Then by (
4.2
), we have

u_{\beta}\in L_{\beta+1,0}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{1,0}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega).

Since
u,u_{\beta}\in L_{1,0}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
,
it follows from the uniqueness assertion of Theorem
4.4
(i) that
u=u_{\beta}
. Moreover, since
1/p(\beta)<\beta+1<1+1/p(\beta)
, it follows from Lemma
4.3
that

u\in L_{\beta+1,0}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)=R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\overline{\Omega})).

Hence

\widetilde{u}\in L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|u\|_{L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
(ii), Lemma
3.4
, and (
4.4
), we have

\widetilde{u}\in L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

and

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|u\|_{L_{\beta+1}^{p(\beta),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

It follows from Theorem
4.6
that every
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
can be written as

f={\rm div}\,F\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,

where
F=\nabla u
for some
u
with its zero extension
\widetilde{u}
satisfying

\widetilde{u}\in L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{for every}\,\,0\leq\alpha<1.

Since
\nabla
is bounded from
L_{\alpha+1}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and from
B_{\alpha+1}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
into
B_{\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(see, e.g.,
[
18

), we have

F\in L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap B_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\mbox{for every}\,\,0\leq\alpha<1.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
f\in L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)
be given. By Lemma
3.5
(i), there exists
u_{1}\in L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
such that

{\rm Tr}\,u_{1}=u_{D}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u_{1}\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)}.

If
f_{1}=f+\Delta u_{1}-b\cdot\nabla u_{1}-cu_{1}
, then by Hölder’s inequality (
2.1
), Lemma
3.3
(iii), and Lemma
4.5
,

f_{1}\in L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f_{1}\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}.

By Theorem
4.6
, there exists
F_{1}\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

{\rm div}\,F_{1}=f_{1}\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|F_{1}\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}.

Hence by Theorem
5.1
(i),
there exists a unique
u_{2}\in L_{1,0}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
such that

-\Delta u_{2}+b\cdot\nabla u_{2}+cu_{2}=f_{1}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Moreover, this
u_{2}
satisfies

\|u_{2}\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)}.

It is now easy to check that
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
is a unique solution in
L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) and satisfies

\|u\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L_{-1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{n/(n-1),\infty}_{1/n}(\partial\Omega)}\right)

for some
C=C(n,s,\Omega,b,c)
.

Suppose in addition that
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in\mathcal{B}^{p(\alpha),\infty}_{\alpha+1-1/p(\alpha)}(\partial\Omega)
for
0<\alpha<1
. Then
u_{1}
can be chosen so that

{\rm Tr}\,u_{1}=u_{D}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u_{1}\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|u_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p(\alpha),\infty}_{\alpha+1-1/p(\alpha)}(\partial\Omega)}.

Since
f_{1}=f+\Delta u_{1}-b\cdot\nabla u_{1}-cu_{1}
, we have

f_{1}\in L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u_{1}\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, if
f_{2}=f_{1}-b\cdot\nabla u_{2}-cu_{2}
, then

f_{2}\in L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u_{2}\|_{L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}.

Note that

u_{2}\in L_{1,0}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad-\Delta u_{2}=f_{2}\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega.

Therefore, by Theorem
4.4
(i), we deduce that

u_{2}\in L_{\alpha+1,0}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u_{2}\|_{L_{\alpha+1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f_{2}\|_{L_{\alpha-1}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

For the sake of simplicity, we write

\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{B}_{\alpha-1/p(\alpha)}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\partial\Omega)=\left[\mathcal{B}_{1/p(\alpha)-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\partial\Omega)\right]^{*}

for
0\leq\alpha<1
. Note that if
0\leq\beta<\alpha<1
, then
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{\beta}
. Indeed, since

(1-\beta)-(1-\alpha)=\frac{n}{p(\beta)^{\prime}}-\frac{n}{p(\alpha)^{\prime}}>0,

it follows from Lemma
3.3
that

\mathcal{B}_{1/p(\beta)-\beta}^{p(\beta)^{\prime},1}(\partial\Omega)={\rm Tr}\left(L_{1-\beta}^{p(\beta)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\right)\hookrightarrow{\rm Tr}\left(L_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\right)=\mathcal{B}_{1/p(\alpha)-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\partial\Omega).

Let
L
be the differential operator defined by

L\psi=-\Delta\psi+b\cdot\nabla\psi+c\psi.

Then it follows from Theorem
5.1
(ii) that
L
is an isomorphism from
L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
onto
L^{n,1}(\Omega)
.
Denote by
L^{-1}
the inverse of
L
. Then for all
f\in L^{n,1}(\Omega)
,

\|\nabla L^{-1}(f)\|_{C(\overline{\Omega};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|L^{-1}(f)\|_{L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(\Omega)}.

Hence given
G\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
v_{D}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, the mapping

f\ \mapsto\ \left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}L^{-1}(f)\right\rangle-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla L^{-1}(f)\,dx

defines a bounded linear functional on
L^{n,1}(\Omega)
, with its norm being bounded above by
C\left(\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\right)
. Therefore, by the duality result (
2.4
), there exists a unique
v\in L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
, with

\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{0}},

such that

\int_{\Omega}vf\,dx=\left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}L^{-1}(f)\right\rangle-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla L^{-1}(f)\,dx

for all
f\in L^{n,1}(\Omega)
. By the definitions of
L
and
L^{-1}
, we also have

\int_{\Omega}v\left(-\Delta\psi+b\cdot\nabla\psi+c\psi\right)dx=\left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}\psi\right\rangle-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

for all
\psi\in L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
. This implies, in particular, that
v
is a very weak solution of (
1.2
) satisfying the desired estimate. Uniqueness of a very weak solution of (
1.2
) can be easily proved by a duality argument based on the density of
C_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
in
L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
(see
[
22
, Lemma 2.10 (iv)]
).

To complete the proof, it remains to prove regularity results for the very weak solution
v
.
First using the result in Remark
4.7
,
we choose
G_{1}\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

{\rm div}\,G_{1}=cv\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|G_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|cv\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}.

Then defining
F=G+vb-G_{1}
, we have

\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\left(\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\right)

for some
C=C(n,\Omega,b,c)
. Note that
v
is a very weak solution of the problem

(5.1)

-\Delta v={\rm div}\,F\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\quad\mbox{and}\quad v=v_{D}\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.

Suppose now that
v_{D}\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
.
Then since
1<p(\alpha)^{\prime}<\infty
and
1<2-\alpha<2
, it follows from Theorem
4.4
(ii) that the solution operator
T
of (
4.1
) is bounded from
L_{-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
into
L_{1,0}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2-\alpha,0}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, since
(1-\alpha)p(\alpha)^{\prime}=n
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
(iv) that

\|\nabla T(g)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|\nabla T(g)\|_{L_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}

and

\left|\left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}T(g)\right\rangle\right|\leq\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\|\nabla T(g)\|_{L_{1-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\|g\|_{L_{-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)}

for all
g\in L_{-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
. Hence the mapping

g\ \mapsto\ \left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}T(g)\right\rangle-\int_{\Omega}F\cdot\nabla T(g)\,dx

defines a bounded linear functional on
L_{-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)
and its norm is bounded above by
C\left(\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\right)
.
By Lemma
3.2
(iv), there is a unique

v_{\alpha}\in R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\overline{\Omega}))
,
with

\|v_{\alpha}\|_{R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\overline{\Omega}))}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}},

such that

(5.2)

\langle v_{\alpha},g\rangle=\left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}T(g)\right\rangle-\int_{\Omega}F\cdot\nabla T(g)\,dx\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,g\in L_{-\alpha}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega).

Choose any
\beta
with
0\leq\beta<\alpha
.
Then since
\beta-0=n/p(\beta)-n/p(0)
and
\alpha-\beta=n/p(\alpha)-n/p(\beta)>0
, it follows from Lemma
2.3
(ii) and Lemma
3.4
that

\widetilde{v}_{\alpha}\in L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{L}^{n/(n-1),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap{L}_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap{B}_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{v}_{\alpha}\|_{{L}_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap{B}_{\beta}^{p(\beta),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{v}_{\alpha}\|_{L_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|v_{\alpha}\|_{R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}^{p(\alpha),\infty}(\overline{\Omega}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|v_{D}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}.

Given
\psi\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we define
g=-\Delta\psi
. Then since

\psi,T(g)\in L_{1,0}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2-\alpha,0}^{p(\alpha)^{\prime},1}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad-\Delta\psi=g=-\Delta T(g)\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,

it follows from Theorem
4.4
(ii) that
\psi=T(g)
. Hence by (
5.2
), we have

\int_{\Omega}v_{\alpha}(-\Delta\psi)\,dx=\left\langle v_{D},\partial_{\nu}\psi\right\rangle-\int_{\Omega}F\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx,

which implies that
v_{\alpha}
is a very weak solution of (
5.1
). By the uniqueness of a very weak solution, we conclude that
v_{\alpha}=v
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

6.
Traces of very weak solutions

This final section is devoted to proving Theorem

, which is a trace result for very weak solutions of (

).
First we explore the trace of a function in

.

6.1.
Trace results for
L_{2}^{p,q}(\Omega)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then for
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, the operator
\widetilde{\rm Tr}
defined by

\widetilde{\rm Tr}\,u:=\left({\rm Tr}\,u,\,{\rm Tr}\,(\nabla u)\cdot\nu\right)

is bounded from
L^{p,q}_{2}(\Omega)
into

\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\times\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{1-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
. Suppose in addition that
\Omega
is a
C^{1,1}
-domain.
It is classical (see, e.g.,
[
26
, Subsection 2.5.7]
) that for
k=1,2
, the range of the trace operator
{\rm Tr}:L_{k}^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
is the boundary Besov space
B_{k-1/p}^{p}(\partial\Omega)
; therefore,

\mathcal{B}^{p,p}_{k-1/p}(\partial\Omega)=B_{k-1/p}^{p}(\partial\Omega)\quad\mbox{for}\quad k=1,2.

It has been known (see, e.g.,
[
26
, Subsection 2.5.7]
) that
\widetilde{\rm Tr}
has a right inverse
\widetilde{\rm Ex}
that is bounded from
B^{r}_{2-1/r}(\partial\Omega)\times B^{r}_{1-1/r}(\partial\Omega)
into
L^{r}_{2}(\Omega)
for all
1<r<\infty
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Suppose that
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(i)

There is a bounded linear operator
\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{1}:\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow L_{2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
such that
{\rm Tr}\circ\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{1}
is the identity on
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
.

(ii)

If
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
,
then

\left(B^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}=\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega).

(iii)

\widetilde{\rm Ex}
is bounded from
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\times\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{1-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
into
L_{2}^{p,q}(\Omega)

and

\widetilde{\rm Tr}\circ\widetilde{\rm Ex}
is the identity on
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\times\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{1-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
. Then as in the proof of Lemma
3.5
(ii), the trace operator
{\rm Tr}
is bounded from
L^{p,q}_{2}(\Omega)
into
\left(B^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}
and

\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow\left(B^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}.

On the other hand, the operator

f\mapsto\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{1}\,(f):=\widetilde{\rm Ex}\,(f,0)

is a right inverse of
{\rm Tr}
that is bounded from
B^{p_{i}}_{2-1/p_{i}}(\partial\Omega)
into
L^{p_{i}}_{2}(\Omega)
for
i=0,1
. By real interpolation,
\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{1}
is bounded from
\left(B^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}
into
L^{p,q}_{2}(\Omega)
and

\left(B^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),B^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega),

which proves Parts (ii) and (i). To prove Part (iii), we observe from Part (ii), Lemma
3.5
, and
[
5
, Chapter 3.13. Exercises 4]
that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\times\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{1-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\\
&=\big(\,{B}^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),\,{B}^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\,\big)_{\theta,q}\times\big(\,{B}^{p_{0}}_{1-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega),\,{B}^{p_{1}}_{1-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\,\big)_{\theta,q}\\
&=\big(\,{B}^{p_{0}}_{2-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega)\times{B}^{p_{0}}_{1-1/p_{0}}(\partial\Omega)\,,\,{B}^{p_{1}}_{2-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\times{B}^{p_{1}}_{1-1/p_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\,\big)_{\theta,q}.\end{split}

Hence
\widetilde{\rm Ex}
is bounded from
\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{2-1/p}(\partial\Omega)\times\mathcal{B}^{p,q}_{1-1/p}(\partial\Omega)
into
L_{2}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Consider the following Dirichlet problem for the bi-Laplace operator
\Delta^{2}
:

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta^{2}u=&\Delta f&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\

where
\Omega
is a bounded

-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Denote by

such that
\widetilde{\rm Tr}\,u=(0,0)
on
\partial\Omega
:

L^{p,q}_{2,{\bf 0}}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p,q}_{2}(\Omega)\,:\,\widetilde{\rm Tr}\,u=(0,0)\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\}.

For
1<p=q<\infty
, we write
L^{p}_{2,{\bf 0}}(\Omega)=L^{p,p}_{2,{\bf 0}}(\Omega)
. Then it is known (see, e.g.,
[

, Subsection 2.5.7]
and
[

\widetilde{L}^{p}_{2}(\Omega)=L^{p}_{2,{\bf 0}}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for}\,\,1<p<\infty.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Suppose that
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then for every

, there exists a unique solution

of (

). Moreover,

\|u\|_{L_{2}^{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}

for some
C=C(n,p,q,\Omega)
.

Proof.

For the case when
1<p=q<\infty
, this result was shown in
[
13
, Theorem 8.6]
for more general elliptic systems (see also
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[

For the general case, suppose that
1<p_{0}<p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
,
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then it follows from
[
13
, Theorem 8.6]
that for every
f\in L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
of (
6.1
). Let
\widetilde{T}:L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)\to L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
be the associated solution operator for (
6.1
). Then
\widetilde{T}
is also bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
into
L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)
.
Therefore,
T
is bounded from
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
into
\left(\,L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}
.
Moreover, it easily follows from Lemma
3.1
(ii) that
\left(\,L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega)
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. Suppose that
1<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
,
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Then

\left(\,L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}=L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega).

Proof.

By the proof of Lemma
6.2
, it remains to prove that

L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\left(\,L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}.

Suppose that
u\in L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega)
and
f=\Delta u
. Then

f\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{T}f\in\left(\,L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{0}}(\Omega),L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p_{1}}(\Omega)\,\right)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega),

where
\widetilde{T}
is the solution operator for (
6.1
).
Since
u,\widetilde{T}f\in L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega)
and

\Delta^{2}u=\Delta f=\Delta^{2}\widetilde{T}f
in
\Omega
,
it follows from Lemma
6.2
that

u=\widetilde{T}f
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

From Lemmas
3.2
and
6.3
, we immediately obtain

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},n\geq 2
. If
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, then

R_{\Omega}(\widetilde{L}_{2}^{p,q}(\overline{\Omega}))=L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega)
. In addition, if
q<\infty
, then

\widetilde{L}_{2}^{p,q}(\Omega)=L_{2,{\bf 0}}^{p,q}(\Omega).

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

By a simple reduction as in the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we may assume without loss of generality that
b=0
and
c=0
, so that
v
and
G
satisfy

(6.2)

\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Note that by Lemma
6.1
(ii), the mapping
h\mapsto\widetilde{\rm Ex}\,(0,h)
defines a bounded linear operator
\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}:B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
. Hence for
h\in B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v\Delta(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}\|\Delta(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\|_{L^{n,1}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\|\nabla(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\right)\|\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h\|_{L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\right)\|h\|_{B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)}.

Therefore, the mapping

h\mapsto\int_{\Omega}v\Delta(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\,dx

defines a bounded linear functional
\gamma_{0}v
on
B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)
, with its norm being bounded above by
C\left(\|v\|_{L^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)}+\|G\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\right)
. To prove (
1.10
), suppose that

\psi\in L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad h={\rm Tr}\,(\nabla\psi)\cdot\nu.

Then setting
\phi=\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h
, we have

\psi,\phi\in L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega),\quad{\rm Tr}\,\psi={\rm Tr}\,\phi=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm Tr}\,(\nabla\psi)\cdot\nu={\rm Tr}\,(\nabla\phi)\cdot\nu=h,

which implies, by Lemma
6.4
, that

\psi-\phi\in\widetilde{L}_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega).

By a simple density argument, it follows from (
6.2
) that

\int_{\Omega}v\Delta(\psi-\phi)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla(\psi-\phi)\,dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma_{0}v,{\rm Tr}\,(\nabla\psi)\cdot\nu\right\rangle=\left\langle\gamma_{0}v,h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\psi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx.

This proves the existence of
\gamma_{0}v
satisfying the desired estimate of the theorem.

To prove the uniqueness assertion, suppose that
f
is any bounded linear functional on
B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)
satisfying

(6.3)

\left\langle f,{\rm Tr}\,(\nabla\psi)\cdot\nu\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\psi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

for all
\psi\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Then it follows from a density result in
[
22
, Lemma 2.10 (iv)]
that (
6.3
) also holds for all
\psi\in L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
.
Given
h\in B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)
, let
\psi:=\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h\in L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
. Then since
{\rm Tr}\,(\nabla\psi)\cdot\nu=h
, it follow from (
6.3
) and the definition of
\gamma_{0}v
that

\begin{split}\left\langle f,h\right\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\psi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla(\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h)\,dx=\left\langle\gamma_{0}v,h\right\rangle,\end{split}

which implies that
f=\gamma_{0}v
.

Suppose in addition that
v\in L_{1}^{n/(n-1),\infty}(\Omega)
. Then by (
6.2
), we easily obtain

(6.4)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla v+G\right)\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=0\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\psi\in L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega).

Given
h\in B_{1-1/n}^{n,1}(\partial\Omega)
, let
\psi:=\widetilde{\rm Ex}_{2}h\in L_{1,0}^{n,1}(\Omega)\cap L_{2}^{n,1}(\Omega)
. Then by the divergence theorem and (
6.4
),

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma_{0}v,h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\psi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}G\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}({\rm Tr}\,v)h\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla v+G\right)\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}({\rm Tr}\,v)h\,d\sigma.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

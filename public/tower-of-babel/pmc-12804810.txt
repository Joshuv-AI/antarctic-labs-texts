Enhancing smart energy load management in industrial steel production using the fermatean fuzzy FUCA multi-criteria analysis
Authors: Dongying Zhao, Maofa Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe bottom line in industrial steel manufacturing is effective energy load management to lower the cost and increase sustainability. This paper develops a new decision-making model based on the Fermatean Fuzzy FUCA (FF-FUCA) approach for prioritizing innovative energy load strategies in the face of uncertainty. There were fifteen options considered under various benefit and cost parameters, along with professional opinion. The findings show that the suggested strategy helps determine the most effective and feasible strategies, and deselects the alternatives that are more expensive or less feasible. Sensitivity analysis was used to ensure the rankings remain stable in the face of changes in decision-makers’ weights and criteria weights. Benchmarking against existing FF multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods showed the high reliability and balance of FF-FUCA. The study is practical in advising managers in the steel industry on selecting cost-effective, sustainable energy solutions, as well as augmenting fuzzy MCDM by demonstrating the improved performance of FF-FUCA in decision-making processes within complex industrial settings.Keywords: Smart energy load management, Industrial steel production, Fermatean fuzzy sets, FUCA method, Multi-criteria group decision-makingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Smart energy load management in industrial steel production involves a methodical planning, allocation, and optimisation of energy load across various steps of the steel manufacturing process. Smelting, rolling, and finishing processes in steel-making are among the most power-consuming processes, as steel-making is one of the most energy-intensive industries. Smart energy load management is a complex of advanced technologies, automation, and decision-support through which the real-time consumption is monitored and the peak and off-peak loads are controlled to minimize the overall inefficiencies of energy consumption. This will not only guarantee operational sustainability but also adherence to the energy regulations and cost-saving targets. Through smart coordination of energy supply, industries are able to reduce wastage, increase stability in the processes, and competitiveness in the international markets.

The significance of smart energy load management in industrial production of steel is more evident in light of an increase in the cost of energy, intensive environmental laws, and the industry’s desire to remain sustainable. The lack of good load management means that manufacturers will run the risk of increased production costs, low efficiency, and carbon emissions. Thus, the need to give precedence to energy-efficient options, which include automation-based monitoring, renewable integration, and progressive tracking systems, has arisen. Nevertheless, with the abundance of existing solutions and their different viability, an ordered method is needed in the assessment and ranking of alternatives used by industries. Such a need introduces a gap in the utilization of MCDM strategies in efficient energy load optimization.

MCDM has an important role in the context of industrial decision-making as it helps managers to take various conflicting criteria, including cost, efficiency, reliability, and environmental impact, into account at the same time. The use of MCDM in the smart energy load management will also make sure that no single variable is used to determine the decisions, but a balanced assessment of all the attributes is utilized. This gives it a clear, systematic, and transparent guideline on which energy management strategies are best to adopt. Nonetheless, conventional crisp methods are unsuccessful in generalizing the uncertainty and vagueness of human preferences since decision-making is usually subjective and requires expert opinions. This is one of the drawbacks that raises the necessity of fuzzy-based solutions that would be capable of managing imprecision better.

Fuzzy sets (FS) in which the membership degrees (MD) are in the interval [0,1] were first proposed by Zadeh1. Nonetheless, FS had drawbacks as it could not directly model hesitation. To correct this, Atanassov2 proposed the concept of intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFS) that included the degree of membership in addition to the degree of non-membership, and the sum of both was restricted to the unit interval. This concept was later generalized by Yager3 to predictive Pythagorean fuzzy sets (PFS), which made no assumptions on the square sum of MD and non-membership degree (NMD). And more recently, Fermatean fuzzy sets (FFS) were proposed by Senapati4, in which the cube sum of MD and NMD is restricted to . The development comes in handy, especially in complicated fields such as steel energy management, where uncertainty and vagueness are the order of the day.

In line with the development of fuzzy sets, different MCDM processes have been invented to handle uncertain information. The most popular of them has been the FUCA method, which was recently presented as a powerful method to analyze alternatives in the case of a fuzzy environment, as it can be used to address the problem of utility and consistency in decision-making. The structure of FUCA guarantees that not only are alternatives ranked, but also assessed based on logical coherence, which minimizes contradiction in expert opinion. The FF-FUCA method is especially effective when used with FFS because it represents a complex uncertainty and guarantees reasonable prioritization. In the case of smart energy load management in steel production, this would make it one of the most suitable options as opposed to traditional means.

To confirm the strength of FF-FUCA in this regard, the research conducts a sensitivity analysis by changing the criteria and weights of decision-makers and compares them with other FF-MCDM models like FF-CoCoSo, FF-MARCOS, and FF-CRADIS. The comparisons illustrate the higher level of balance and real priorities that are attained by the suggested way. In such a way, the study is not only helpful in terms of the further development of methods but also offers practical recommendations that can be used by managers in the industry.

The originality of the study is in the fact that it suggests applying the FF-FUCA technique of smart energy load management in industrial steel production, where the previous research did not use this approach explicitly. Although other fuzzy MCDM frameworks have been employed, the combination of Fermatean fuzzy logic and FUCA can guarantee a greater ability to limit the uncertainty and reluctance of the expert opinion. It renders the given methodology distinctive, practically relevant, and theoretically novel, which will lead to more informed energy choices in steel production.

Literature review

Application of MCDM in smart energy load management in industrial steel production

The issue of smart energy load management has become an integral theme in industrial processes, especially in the energy-intensive industries such as steel production. The studies in the related industries show the growing use of smart structures in energy optimization. An example of this is a smart energy management system suggested by Laayati et al.5 in the context of an experimental open-pit mine according to ISO 50,001 standards, and proving that proper monitoring and peak load prediction are the direct keys to energy consumption optimization and profit growth. In spite of the fact that the mining sector is the center of interest in this study, the universal significance of systematic energy management strategies is highlighted, which can also be applied to steel manufacturing to eliminate inefficiencies. To this end, Saleem et al.6 focused, designed, and implemented an IoT-based smart energy management framework, with a primary focus on the ability to control the external load and report the adoption of deployed facilities automatically. This type of load regulation, with the help of the IoT, provides a good basis for the steel plants that want to implement the concept of digitalization into their energy management systems. In the steel industry in particular, studies have established significant challenges and opportunities for energy optimization in the Industry 4.0 paradigm. The review of intelligent manufacturing technologies in the steel industry of China presented by Zhou et al.7 shows that, though the automation has already reached a new level, the obstacles to the full integration of energy and production management still exist. Their results indicate that decision-support systems are required to strike a balance between energy consumption and the quality of production, as well as reliability in the processes. Similarly, Gajdzik et al.8 compared the demand for electricity and heat in steel processes in Industry 4.0 conditions. Their argument was that to match the steel production to the concepts of green steel, it would be necessary to minimize the intensity of electricity consumption and maximize its energy efficiency with the help of digital monitoring and predictive services.

The value of energy optimization in steel production is further supported by other recent researches that indicate its significant importance. Sun et al.9 analysed flows of materials and energy, and pointed out the existence of an integration gap that remains constant, reducing the efficiency of the overall process. According to Colla and Branca10, sustainable steel manufacturing is strongly dependent on the appropriate management of energy and resources, which is why these strategies were directly associated with carbon lean and low-emission manufacturing objectives. A thorough review of energy-efficient technologies was given by him and Wang, which disclosed that about 20% of the energy currently used in the steel industry can be saved with the best available technologies11. Moreover, Colla et al.12 proved that the usage of reservoir computing and the latest neural network models could make a substantial contribution to the field of industrial energy management by offering a more accurate and efficient prediction of dynamic energy behaviour. All these studies point to the fact that technological readiness is not the issue, but prioritization and assessment of the energy-saving strategies are still ineffective, and thus, this provides space to MCDM applications. Recent research has also shown that MCDM frameworks can be used to support making sustainable energy decisions by balancing various criteria, which are often conflicting. In one case, Uzair and Kazmi13 developed a model of sustainable building energy management based on MCDM and incorporating intelligent automation to estimate the trade-offs between efficiency, cost, and resilience. Their model shows how systematic decision-making strategies can allow the stakeholders to find the best energy strategies in the face of uncertainty. Applying this to the steel industry setting, MCDM can put a structured emphasis on such alternatives as the introduction of renewable energy, new storage technologies, and IoT-based surveillance systems, so that the economic and environment goals are met at the same time. It is based on these insights that the study places MCDM as a key facilitator of intelligent energy load management in the industrial steel production. In contrast to the previous methods which are mainly oriented on monitoring or automation, MCDM enables the systematic analysis of the varied options within complex and uncertain conditions. Through the use of the sophisticated fuzzy extensions, decision-makers will be able to adequately represent the hesitation, vagueness, and subjective preferences and eventually obtain more trustworthy and viable solutions to the sustainable steel production.

Studies applied the FUCA method

The FUCA (Faire Un Choix Adequat) approach has become more and more popular as one of the well-developed methods of MCDM that may be used in a variety of areas to provide reliable and significant rankings. Do FUCA used14 in the multi-criteria decision-making of mechanical machining processes, showing that FUCA was more effective than the Weighted Sum Approach (WSA), resulting in more validated assessments of the financial performance and in the logical consistency of the decision results. This underscored the ability of FUCA to deal effectively with more complicated industrial issues where there are numerous criteria that would have to be traded off. Thinh15 employed FUCA to optimize turning processes with multiple objectives in the context of process optimization. The study demonstrated the flexibility of FUCA in terms of parameter trade-offs by combining various weighting schemes, as well as FUCA’s appropriateness in manufacturing optimization. Building on this, Truong and Thinh16 added the PIPRECIA method to an adapted FUCA approach to enhance the performance of the former on the selection of lathes, eliminating some of the limitations of the original formulation. Their results indicated that modified FUCA was more stable and stronger in the ranking and decision-making. FUCA has also been used in product selection problems. Nguyen17 used FUCA in combination with CURLI and weighting to evaluate the technical products, including air conditioners, washing machines, and UAVs. The integration demonstrated the flexibility and precision of FUCA in both industrial and consumer-based product evaluation. Also, Bao et al.18 compared FUCA to using the PSI method to choose mini water pumps and found that FUCA generated more stable and consistent rankings and thus affirmed its reliability as compared to alternative MCDM methods. In general, these papers show that FUCA has several distinct benefits regarding ranking stability, consistency, and flexibility in different areas of industry and consumers. This especially enables it to be used in complex decision spaces like smart energy load management in the steel production industry, where uncertainty, multi-stakeholder, and conflicting goals have to be solved at the same time.

Research gap analysis

Though the concept of smart energy load management during steel production has been researched with the use of digital technologies and traditional decision-making techniques, the majority of the available literature is devoted primarily to monitoring solutions or non-specific optimization solutions. Little has been said concerning structured decision frameworks that combine various conflicting criteria when there is uncertainty. At the methodology level, the FUCA approach has demonstrated high potential in many areas, yet its implementation in industrial energy management, especially steel production, is still not investigated. Furthermore, the superior representational ability of the Fermatean fuzzy sets has not been utilized advantageously in this research, whereas earlier studies have employed the use of fuzzy, intuitionistic, or Pythagorean fuzzy sets. This leaves a distinct gap: none of the studies has integrated FUCA and Fermatean fuzzy sets to compare and rank smart energy load strategies in steel manufacturing. To fill this gap, the present work proposes the FF-FUCA approach, which provides a new framework that is more efficient in capturing uncertainty and provides balanced, practical, and reliable results in decision-making.

Preliminaries and theoretical background

Expert judgments are also subjective, imprecise, and uncertain in the reality of making decisions, which makes it hard to assess the criteria. To overcome these problems, Zadeh (1965) proposed FS in which every element has an MD in [0,1]. Non-membership and hesitation are, however, not represented by FSs. To circumvent this, Atanassov (1986) suggested IFS in terms of MD, NMD, and hesitation degree (HD). Yager (2014) also extended this to PyFS so that the square sum of MD and NMD would not go outside [0,1]. Senapati (2019) developed this framework with the introduction of FFS that enables the cubic sum of MD and NMD to remain in [0,1].

Development of the fermatean fuzzy FUCA (FF-FUCA) method

This paper proposes the FF-FUCA technique by combining the flexibility of FFS and the sound compromise model of the FUCA technique to deal with uncertainty in MCDM. This is initiated by using the Fermatean Fuzzy Weighted Averaging (FFWA) operator to form the combination of criteria weights that have been given by experts, thus maintaining the levels of hesitation that exist with subjective judgments. The expert assessments represented using linguistic terms are mathematically transformed into Fermatean fuzzy numbers, and, therefore, the qualitative assessment can be represented with mathematical precision, taking into consideration the vagueness and ambiguity. The FUCA method, per se, is a compromise ranking method in MCDM that measures the alternatives based on several criteria, weighting, and overall scoring to produce credible rankings. In contrast to most other methods of ranking, in some situations, FUCA does not require explicit normalization, which saves on computational complexity. But in its traditional manner, FUCA uses crisp data, which constrains its ability to directly accept uncertain or imprecise data. This paper makes FUCA applicable to scenarios where imprecision and subjectivity are the main factors, e.g. large-scale industrial energy administration. The given FF-FUCA framework, therefore, offers a methodological improvement that will improve realism and strength in decision analysis. It is more comprehensive than traditional crisp or even intuitionistic approaches to uncertainty, which makes trade-offs between conflicting criteria more accurately represented. In addition to its mathematical enhancement, FF-FUCA has been useful specifically in meeting sustainability-related industrial needs, e.g. smart energy load control in steel production, where efficiency, cost, and environmental issues must be balanced by having the tool able to adapt to complex and uncertain decision environments. Case study: enhancing smart energy load management in industrial steel production

The idea of smart energy load management in industrial steel production is one of the keystones of the contemporary manufacturing system because steel is among the most energy-consuming materials globally. Productive distribution and use of energy resources directly influence the efficiency of production, the minimization of costs, and environmental sustainability. As industrialization takes place at a high rate and more and more steel products are demanded, the energy demand increases, and therefore it becomes a major challenge for manufacturers to balance operational performance and save energy at the same time. The proactive inclusion of smart energy load management practice not only will guarantee the most efficient power consumption but also will establish a base of sustainable steel-making, where complex technologies and decision models will be used as leading factors in finding the way to cope with the uncertainties of the energy demands and distribution. The significance of intelligent energy load management in the industrial steel production is that the technology helps in curbing the waste of energy, decreasing production cost, and harmful effects on the environment, such as the emission of greenhouse gases. The activities of the steel manufacturing process, like blast furnaces, electric arc furnaces, and rolling mills, consume very large proportions of electricity and fuel, and poor distribution of loads can result in unimaginable inefficiencies and a higher cost of production. Moreover, the constantly changing energy price and the increasing need to meet the environmental requirements require a systematic and intelligent manner of energy load management. Not only does smart management improve the stability of the production, but it also enables steel industries to operate in a market where energy optimization becomes a decisive factor to stay in the market and to become profitable. The MCDM strategies have been extensively used in smart energy load management of industrial steel production in an effort to handle the complexities associated with energy decision-making. MCDM methods offer systematic methods of considering alternative choices in various ways and usually in conflicting conditions to allow the decision-makers to make rational and defensible decisions. Most classical and recent MCDM approaches, including TOPSIS, VIKOR, and MARCOS, have been applied to energy-related problems, but the accurate and vague nature of real-life industrial data can be a challenge to these approaches. In order to address these shortcomings, this experiment proposes the FF-FUCA technique that involves the flexibility of fuzzy sets with progressive decision aggregation to better represent the uncertainty. The suggested approach has a powerful and dependable decision support tool that can be used to improve the energy load optimization in the steel manufacturing industry. The paper provides the case of decision-making on the basis of hypothetical information to show how the offered FF-FUCA approach could be applied. The process is the evaluation, wherein four individuals knowledgeable in the area of energy management, steel production, sustainability, and operations planning will be involved to pass judgments widely. Admittedly, the decision makers could be too few, as only four individuals are used, which might lead to some bias, as their professional experience and background might affect some of the rankings. Nevertheless, numerous studies that were published in MCDM procedures effectively employ three to five experts in illustrating and authenticating decision-making. In this research, the four specialists are well chosen to apply the various roles in the industry production of steel, energy usage, and sustainability, and this reduces the influence of personal views. Result discussion

The proposed FF-FUCA method was applied in a way that gave a good prioritization of strategies to manage smart energy loads in smart industrial steel production. : High-Efficiency Furnaces took the first position, a sign of the importance they bear to cut the costs and energy consumption. This was then succeeded by energy storage deployment : Energy Storage Deployment and blockchain-based energy tracking : Blockchain-Based Energy Tracking, in which the element of stability, reliability, and transparency in energy management is of utmost importance. : Smart Grid Integration and : Predictive Maintenance Programs also scored well, at the fourth and fifth position, and mid-ranking strategies, : Hybrid Energy Systems, : Demand-Side Response, and : Digital Twin Modeling also emphasized the supportive functions. Less important alternatives, such as : Renewable Energy Utilization, : Waste Heat Recovery, and : Carbon Capture, show that high price and complexity of implementation make them not viable despite the possible advantages. : AI-Based Load Forecasting was the least ranked, as it did not have much practical influence without the underlying infrastructure. In general, the findings indicate that direct efficiency-oriented policies are more effective than indirect/high-cost ones, and it proves that FF-FUCA method can be useful in supporting sustainable energy decisions. Moreover, the fact that some of the results, e.g., the worst rating of carbon capture or some expensive approaches, can partially be explained by the specific views of the chosen decision makers, but the variety of their professional backgrounds decreases the chances of collective bias. The findings indicate the manner in which each option responds to certain objectives in operations, environmental, and technological. To illustrate, the highest-rated ones are directly associated with the cost savings, energy efficiency, and process transparency, and the middle-ranked ones are related to flexibility and adaptability in energy management. This comprehensive interpretation can offer workable rules to the industrial stakeholders to focus on the strategies depending on the performance as well as feasibility.

Practical and managerial implications

This research has a number of critical implications in terms of practice and management for the steel manufacturing industry. To start with, the proposed FF-FUCA approach will give decision-makers a valid instrument to selectively assess various energy management options under uncertainty to make sure that decisions cannot be made by relying on mere intuition but rather a systematic and data-oriented procedure. Suggesting the most efficient furnaces, energy storage implementation, and blockchain-based energy tracking as the highest-ranked strategies, managers can give priority to the investments in the technologies that could provide both short-term and long-term changes in efficiency. Second, the findings demonstrate that there is a need to balance cost- and benefit-related criteria in energy decision-making. As an illustration, other innovations like carbon capture and AI-based predictions were made secondary owing to their expensive nature and low viability. This understanding can help managers to better use their resources by investing in those solutions that have the best returns on investment, without adopting costly or poorly developed technology. Third, the analysis offers an analysis of multi-stakeholder decision making, that is, it integrates the concerns of several decision-makers, thereby eliminating bias and achieving agreement on the strategic planning. This is especially useful in such industries as steel production, where energy choices affect monetary performance, ecological conformity, and operational stability all at the same time. Lastly, in a wider managerial sense, the research highlights the significance of the inclusion of digitalization and intelligent technologies, e.g., blockchain and predictive analytics, into energy management systems. These strategies not only ensure the maximization of operations at present, but also get steel manufacturers ready to address the future regulatory standards and global sustainability goals. In that way, the FF-FUCA approach is an effective decision-support tool that can be used to make policy decisions on energy, plan investments, and the competitiveness of industries in the long term.

Advantages of the study

This paper provides a number of interesting benefits that add to both theory and practice in the area of smart energy load management. First, it presents the FF-FUCA approach, which is a new method of making decisions that successfully implements uncertainty and imprecision, eliminating the constraints of traditional MCDM approaches. The method allows making more reliable and realistic prioritizations of alternatives in complex industrial settings by combining FFS with FUCA. Second, the research contributes to the development of the MCDM in the steel production industry, as it is among the most energy-demanding industries in the world. Although numerous known solutions have been dedicated to energy optimization in general, the given work offers a specific methodology that can be considered the reflection of the particular demands of steel production, such as high energy consumption, flexibility in operations, and sustainability of the environment. Third, the sensitivity analysis and the benchmarking comparisons prove the robustness and stability of the proposed model. The reliability of the FF-FUCA methodology is confirmed by the consistency of high-ranking alternatives, including high-efficiency furnaces, the implementation of energy storage, and the use of blockchain-based tracking in various situations and contexts of decision-making. Lastly, the study will be useful to managers and policymakers because it outlines practical and affordable solutions and puts downplay on impractical and expensive solutions. The combination of theoretical rigor and managerial applicability of this approach makes the study a useful addition to the field of decision support systems in sustainable energy management in the steel-making and other industries that consume a lot of energy.

Limitations of the study

Despite the fact that the given work includes valuable information and offers a new approach to smart energy load management in the industrial steel production, FF-FUCA, it is necessary to admit some limitations of the work. The case study was carried out first using hypothetical data to demonstrate the methodology. Although this methodology proves the relevance of the model, confirming it with large-scale practical industrial data would reinforce the external validity of the results. While this study demonstrates the conceptual robustness of FF-FUCA, testing it on real industrial datasets would provide critical evidence of its practical applicability and computational scalability under realistic operating conditions. Second, the research used a narrow set of nine criteria and fifteen options, which might not be the complete variability of factors that affect energy management decisions in various industrial settings. The addition of regulatory, market-driven, and long-run sustainability dimensions to the criteria could offer a more detailed analysis. Third, it was the opinion of four decision-makers that was used to make this decision. It is admitted that the sample (technical specialists in the industrial field) is not quite representative of the whole population of the experts, but most of the published works in MCDM and energy management use three to five experts to confirm approaches. In this research, the four professionals were chosen because they had varied knowledge in industrial steel production, energy management, sustainability, and operations planning, which discourages bias. However, even their particular views can lead to some results, namely decreased positions of high-cost strategies. The proposed study may be enhanced by including more varied decision makers to increase the reliability and generalizability. Lastly, although the FF-FUCA technique was superior to the benchmarked techniques in this study, its computational complexity can escalate with the growing datasets, and thus further optimization is necessary to be applicable in a real industrial practice.

Conclusion with future research direction

The given study aimed at improving the smart control of the energy load in industrial steel production through the implementation of a new FF-FUCA method. In this case study, there were fifteen alternatives that were tested against nine criteria using four decision-makers. The suggested approach was able to resolve the uncertainties present in the decision-making process and come up with credible prioritizations, where High-Efficiency Furnaces (), Energy Storage Deployment (), and Blockchain-Based Energy Tracking () became the highest-ranking strategies. The findings showed that FF-FUCA gives more balanced and realistic rankings than current FF-MCDM techniques, including FF-CoCoSo, FF-MARCOS, and FF-CRADIS. The approach better expressed the benefit and cost perception since it promoted strategies that are both efficient, innovative, and sustainable, against those that were costly or less practical. In the practical aspect, the research has an industrial contribution in the sense that it provides a sound framework that can be used by managers in implementing energy-efficient technologies and in harmonizing the energy management practices with long-term sustainability objectives. The suggested methodology will not only help to make the investment decisions better, but also improve the flexibility of the industries under the pressure of the increased energy requirements and the tightened environmental standards. To conclude, the proposed FF-FUCA approach enhances the approach to making a multi-criteria decision in case of uncertainty and gives practical information on managing energy in the industrial steel production. The results confirm its usefulness in helping managers adopt energy strategies that are cost-efficient and sustainable to the environment.

The work can be extended by future researchers using more complex fuzzy environments like spherical fuzzy sets to more effectively model uncertainty27, or more complex picture fuzzy operators like the Dombi operator to better model multi-dimensional imprecision28. Robustness may also be promoted by the utilization of interval-valued T-spherical fuzzy sets in the decision-making29. Besides, fuzzy MCDM frameworks that are based on data successfully utilized in other fields, such as stress management and mental health management30, can be applied to the industrial energy domain and connect the workforce management to smart energy systems. Additionally, future work should focus on validating the FF-FUCA framework in real industrial environments to assess its operational feasibility, reliability, and scalability, thereby strengthening confidence in its practical implementation.

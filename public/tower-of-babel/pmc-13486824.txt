Resource management for blockchain enhanced federated learning in wireless edge networks
Authors: Zhen Yang, Weijing Qi, Lei Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Though machine learning is widely used in wireless edge networks, the transmission of raw data still suffers from security and privacy leakage. Federated learning (FL) addresses these privacy concerns by enabling model training without sharing raw data. However, traditional centralized FL is vulnerable to a single point of failure. Blockchain-based federated learning (BFL) technology can provide FL with a more reliable and secure environment. In wireless edge networks with limited resources, BFL systems encounter challenges related to computing demands and network transmission overhead. To address these issues, we propose a BFL framework for wireless edge networks, which includes local client training, a consensus process, and edge server aggregation. A client selection policy is designed to exclude low-quality clients that could degrade training efficiency and accuracy. Additionally, a joint client selection and resource allocation scheme is implemented to optimize the allocation of computing and bandwidth resources necessary for BFL training and consensus. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed approach improves BFL system accuracy while reducing delay.

Introduction

With the rapid growth of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the widespread deployment of intelligent devices, wireless edge computing has become a key paradigm for enabling distributed intelligence in 6G networks1. However, the limited computing and communication resources at the network edge make it challenging to efficiently coordinate learning tasks among distributed clients2. Traditional centralized machine learning frameworks require raw data transmission to the cloud, which not only increases network congestion and latency but also exposes sensitive information to potential privacy risks3. Therefore, it is imperative to design learning frameworks that ensure privacy, achieve efficient resource utilization, and operate effectively in wireless edge environments.

Federated learning (FL) provides an effective solution for wireless edge networks by allowing local model training on distributed clients without exposing private data. It is a distributed machine learning paradigm designed to protect data privacy and enhance security by allowing clients to train models locally without sharing their original data4,5. This decentralized approach enables clients to contribute to model training without transmitting data to edge servers, thereby preserving privacy and reducing network traffic6. However, traditional centralized FL architectures are vulnerable to single points of failure, where a central server failure can disrupt the stability and operation of the entire system7–10.

To overcome these limitations in wireless edge environments, blockchain enhanced federated learning (BFL) has been proposed as a promising framework. BFL integrates blockchain technology with traditional FL to mitigate the challenges associated with centralized architectures, particularly the single point of failure. Blockchain provides a decentralized, secure, and transparent environment for model updates, ensuring that all contributions are validated and recorded immutably. By leveraging blockchain, the training process becomes more resilient against tampering and malicious attacks11,12. Furthermore, blockchain facilitates the establishment of trust among participants, as its decentralized nature ensures that no single entity has control over the entire training process. BFL reduces the reliance on centralized resources, allowing efficient utilization of available computing and communication resources among edge clients. During the training process of BFL, clients must complete identity registration and verification. The local model is then trained to improve the accuracy of the global model. After that, these parameters are uploaded to the blockchain networks to ensure data privacy. Blockchain provides FL with a more trustworthy training environment for handling sensitive data. At the same time, the decentralized technology can also ensure the security of model parameters, effectively resisting malicious attacks and tampering13,14. The BFL system provides a secure and reliable distributed computing mechanism that has broad application prospects in scenarios such as resource sharing, intelligent transportation, and finance15–17. However, in wireless edge computing environments, the limited computing and communication resources of edge servers and clients lead to high computational demand and transmission pressure, posing new challenges for the efficient implementation of BFL18. The decentralized structure of blockchain networks coupled with the execution of smart contracts may increase the consumption of computing and communication resources, which may affect training efficiency19. Moreover, FL typically involves handling substantial updates to model parameters, and the limited transmission speed and capacity of blockchain networks may result in transmission delays20. Therefore, reducing the delay of the BFL system within resource-constrained wireless edge network environments emerges as a critical challenge.

In the BFL system, local models trained by clients are periodically uploaded to the server for aggregation. The increase in the number of participating clients and the number of aggregations will increase the computing and communication delays. To address these challenges, prior research has primarily focused on optimizing delays in BFL through two key avenues: resource allocation and client selection.

Resource allocation: Given the limited computing and communication resources in edge networks, researchers have proposed a variety of resource allocation strategies to reduce BFL delay. A resource allocation scheme based on deep reinforcement learning (DRL) was proposed to allocate the computing resources of the clients and reduce the delay of block mining and local training in BFL training21. To improve the efficiency of BFL, Nishio et al.22 designed a resource management scheme that fully exploits the computing resources of distributed clients to improve the accuracy of the BFL model. A resource allocation scheme has been proposed to allocate transmission power and computing resources among clients. The proposed scheme allocates resources efficiently by using the Lyapunov optimization method23. To further minimize the delay of the BFL system, data offloading decisions are proposed, and the user’s transmission power, bandwidth, and computing resources are allocated for local training of the BFL system24. The work in25 transformed resource allocation into a multivariate, multi-constrained convex optimization problem, which was solved by an algorithm based on the alternating direction multiplier method. The authors in26 focused on the end-to-end delay of BFL and improved the efficiency of BFL by allocating computing resources to edge servers and adjusting the block generation rate. However, most existing studies focus only on allocating resources to either clients or edge servers, rather than jointly allocating resources to both clients and edge servers. Since the BFL system is composed of federated learning and blockchain, we need to consider not only the resources consumed by model training but also the resources consumed by blockchain mining. It is necessary to balance the resources allocated to training and the consensus processes of the BFL system.

Client selection: Client selection has been widely studied to improve the accuracy of BFL models. A blockchain supported FL model was introduced to prioritize energy efficiency and privacy, and an optimization problem was formulated to maximize system utility. Energy, miner in come, and FL loss are comprehensively considered. The association between edge servers and miners is optimized through a stable matching algorithm, and a quantized neural network is adopted to minimize the energy consumed by computation27. The work in28 designed a novel BFL system and introduced a contribution-based incentive mechanism to encourage mobile clients to participate in BFL training. To improve the efficiency of the BFL system, a BFL framework based on clustering is proposed. After local training of the BFL is completed, the cluster head uploads the model instead of all clients, which avoids frequent transmission of models and saves communication resources29. In the UAV-assisted edge network scenario, a flying UAV and ground cluster head selection policy is proposed to reduce the BFL delay30. A two-layer BFL system is proposed, which includes two key components: a local model update chain, which is used to support device-to-device communication to support model updating. In addition, the research also introduces an incentive mechanism based on reputation to encourage clients to train models31. However, most studies on client selection consider how to select clients with strong computing capability or how to encourage clients to train the BFL model. But these studies ignore a key issue, which is the quality of local models trained by clients. In addition, collaborative client selection and resource allocation are bottlenecks to improving the performance of BFL systems but are rarely explored.

Although the above work has made some progress in delay optimization of the BFL system, it still has some limitations. When the number of clients increases or the workload changes, the edge server needs to take on extra tasks, and the scalability and the delay optimization of the BFL system needs to be improved. Therefore, our proposed scheme aims to address these challenges to promote the development of BFL in wireless edge network applications. We propose a new BFL framework to improve the efficiency of BFL systems by collaboratively optimizing client selection and resource allocation. In this framework, clients train local models and upload the model parameters to the edge server for aggregation, ensuring that raw data is not shared. During the BFL training process, we design an effective client selection policy to reduce the non-independent and identical distribution (non-IID) of data. We also propose a joint client selection and resource allocation scheme that simultaneously allocates the resources required for BFL training and consensus processes. Client selection and resource allocation are then modeled as Markov decision processes solved by DRL. Clients selection

We consider the client selection policy for BFL systems from the perspective of data heterogeneity and resource heterogeneity. Different clients have different datasets, which will affect the accuracy of the models; meanwhile, different clients have different computing and communication resources, which will affect the efficiency of BFL training.

Blockchain model

To enhance the security and reliability of clients’ local models, edge servers function as blockchain nodes responsible for maintaining the blockchain’s operation. Global models are stored within the blockchain and are updated as changes occur in the corresponding clients’ local models. Additionally, the blockchain also stores the local models, allowing verification by other edge servers to ensure their quality. Consequently, there are two types of records: local model records and global model records.

Thus, the blockchain process has two main processes: 1) Block Consensus, 2) Block Generation, as described below:

Block consensus

Blockchain consensus is the process of ensuring the consistency of records across all nodes on the blockchain. Consensus algorithms such as proof of work (PoW), which are used in model sharing, require significant computing and communication resources. To tackle this challenge, we propose an enhanced consensus mechanism, called proof of quality (PoQ), tailored for blockchain-based federated learning. The primary goal of PoQ is to guarantee the high quality of models submitted by clients.

The edge server collects all model parameters from participating clients and assesses the quality of the trained local models based on their accuracy. After evaluation, the performance metrics are converted into a PoQ. This PoQ serves as a digital signature to verify the model’s performance and is stored as a transaction. We use the mean absolute error (MAE) to measure the accuracy of client n, defined as follows11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} MAE\left( \omega \right) =\frac{1}{n}\sum _{i=1}^n{\left[ y_i-f\left( x_i \right) \right] } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f\left( x_i \right)$$\end{document} is the predicted output value of the model ω and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_i$$\end{document} is the true value of the data record. The smaller the MAE of the model ω, the more accurate it is.

The A3C algorithm is a RL algorithm, similar to the actor-critic algorithm, in which two networks, actor and critic, collaborate to accomplish a RL task. The main goal of Actor is to iteratively update the parameters θ through time steps to find the optimal client selection and resource allocation policies. The critic, on the other hand, is tasked with evaluating the policies and computing the value of each action to help the actor choose better actions, i.e., better clients and resource allocation methods. Specifically, the critic is responsible for evaluating the value function of the current state, while the actor generates policies based on the current state and the critic’s evaluation results, and adjusts the strategy model accordingly, including client selection and resource allocation policies.

The edge servers act as agents whose goal is to generate actions and update the parameters through time-step iterations to maximize the cumulative reward function. To implement the proposed framework, clients satisfying the selection strategy of Algorithm 1 download the global model from their associated edge servers and allocate computing resources according to their capabilities for local training. After local updates, the clients upload their models to the corresponding edge servers. Each edge server acts as a blockchain node and allocates computing and bandwidth resources to complete the consensus process and generate new blocks. The edge server with the highest reputation is selected as the global aggregator, and the updated global model is recorded on-chain for subsequent training rounds.

Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated delay minimization for a BFL system operating in wireless edge networks. While BFL addresses some of the key limitations of traditional FL, it still faces significant challenges related to the high computing demands and network transmission pressures inherent in resource-constrained edge networks. To address these issues, we proposed a novel BFL framework where the training process is divided into three key components: local training by clients, consensus, and aggregation by edge servers. To optimize the training process, we introduced a client selection policy that effectively mitigates the impact of non-IID data, enhancing the overall performance of the system. In addition, a joint client selection and resource allocation scheme was developed to balance the resources needed for both BFL training and the consensus process. To reduce the delay in the consensus process, we substituted PoW with PoQ, selecting the edge server with the highest reputation to serve as the best bookkeeper for global model aggregation. The client selection and resource allocation processes were modeled as Markov decision processes and solved using DRL, ensuring an efficient and adaptive approach to resource management. The simulation results demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed scheme, with the BFL system achieving both higher accuracy and reduced delay compared to traditional approaches. In the future, our research will focus on developing more efficient model aggregation algorithms tailored for BFL systems and designing model training policies that take into account user-specific resource constraints. These advancements are expected to further enhance the performance and scalability of BFL systems in dynamic, resource-limited environments.

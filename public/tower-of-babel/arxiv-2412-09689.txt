A C 0 C^{0} interior penalty method for the stream function formulation of the surface Stokes problem Thanks: Supported in part by the NSF grant DMS-2309425.
Michael Neilan and Hongzhi Wan

Abstract
. We propose a C 0 C^{0} interior penalty method for the fourth-order stream function formulation of the surface Stokes problem. The scheme utilizes continuous, piecewise polynomial spaces defined on an approximate surface. We show that the resulting discretization is positive definite and derive error estimates in various norms in terms of the polynomial degree of the finite element space as well as the polynomial degree to define the geometry approximation. A notable feature of the scheme is that it does not explicitly depend on the Gauss curvature of the surface. This is achieved via a novel integration-by-parts formula for the surface biharmonic operator.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a smooth, simply connected compact oriented
hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
without boundary and with outward unit normal
{\bm{n}}
.
The surface Stokes problem seeks the fluid velocity
{\bm{u}}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
and the surface fluid pressure
p:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.1a)

\displaystyle-{\bf P}\,{\rm div}_{\Gamma}(E_{\Gamma}({\bm{u}}))+{\bm{u}}+\nabla_{\Gamma}p\begin{aligned} &={\bm{f}}\quad&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(1.1b)

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}\begin{aligned} &=0\quad&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{aligned}

where
{\bm{f}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)^{3}
with
{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
is a given force vector,

E_{\Gamma}({\bm{u}})
is the deformation tensor, and
{\bf P}
is the tangential projection operator.
Further details and notation are given in the next section.
The zeroth-order term is included
in (
1.1a
) to ensure uniqueness
of the velocity solution and to avoid technicalities
related to Killing fields, i.e., non-trivial tangential
vector fields in the kernel of the deformation tensor
E_{\Gamma}

[
1
]
.

Surface (Navier)-Stokes equations arise in various application models
including emulsion foams and biological membranes
[
29
,
30
]
, computer graphics
[
14
]
,
and geophysics
[
25
,
27
]
. As such there has been recent interest in developing
finite element methods for incompressible fluids posed on surfaces.
A natural choice are surface finite element methods (SFEMs)
based on stable Euclidean
{\bm{H}}^{1}\times L^{2}
conforming velocity-pressure pairs.
In this approach, finite element spaces are defined on a discrete approximate
surface through polynomial mappings, and the tangential velocity constraint
is enforced weakly via penalization or Lagrange multipliers
[
16
,
19
,
2
,
18
]
.
While popular and relatively straightforward to implement,
this approach requires excess degrees
of freedom, as the velocity is approximated by vectors
in all of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, instead of tangential vectors. In addition,
these methods may require a superparametric approximation of
the outward unit normal of the surface to guarantee optimal-order convergence.
Recently it was shown in
[
17
]

that the tangential component of the solution converges
with optimal order using standard isoparametric geometry approximations;
however,
L^{2}
errors are suboptimal if affine surface approximations are used.

Alternatively,
non-conforming velocity-pressure pairs
have been proposed and analyzed in
[
1
,
22
]
,
where the velocity is approximated using exactly tangential and
Piola-mapped
{\bm{H}}(div)
-conforming BDM spaces.
These schemes are analogous to the Euclidean finite element methods in
[
8
]
.
The velocity spaces in these schemes are exactly tangential to the
discrete surface, but due to their nonconformity, additional edge-integral terms are added to ensure their consistency
and stability. An exception is the recent work
[
12
]
, where
a
{\bm{H}}(div)
-conforming space,
based on the lowest-order Euclidean Mini element, is constructed
that possess sufficient weak-continuity properties to guarantee convergence without including edge-integral terms.

In contrast to SFEM, Trace FEM is a discretization technique for surface
PDEs based on a background (bulk) mesh in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
In this framework, finite element spaces are defined on the three-dimensional mesh,
and the traces of such function are used as the approximating space.
To ensure stability of the resulting scheme and algebraic system,
penalty terms are included in their formulation.
While quite advantageous for dynamic and coupled fluid computations,
this approach requires extraneous DOFs, as the spaces are defined on a 3D geometry.

Another discretization technique, and the focus of this paper,
is based on the surface stream function formulation.
If
\Gamma
is simply connected,
then there exists a unique stream function
\phi\in H^{2}(\Gamma)

with
\int_{\Gamma}\phi=0
such
that
{\bm{u}}={\bf{curl}}_{\Gamma}\phi
.
Formally substituting this expression into (
1.1a
)
and taking the curl of the resulting equation yields

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi+{\rm div}_{\Gamma}((K-1)\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi)=-{\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bm{f}},

where
K
is the Gauss curvature of
\Gamma
.
SFEM and TraceFEM discretizations for (
1.2
)
based on a Ciarlet-Raviart mixed formulation (cf.
[
6
]
),
are proposed and analyzed in

[
23
,
26
,
3
,
2
]
.
This approach introduces an auxiliary unknown
\psi=\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi
,
and utilizes surface Lagrange finite element spaces in its discretization.
Thus, while the numerical method is relatively simple to implement,
and is supported in current finite element software, the mixed formulation
results in a relatively large number of unknowns and a saddle-point structure.
In addition, such mixed methods require the computation of an approximate
Gauss curvature, which may require higher-order approximations
of the surface outward normal
[
3
,
2
]
.
Finally, the extension of the Ciarlet-Raviart discretization technique towards
the Stokes problem on surfaces with boundary is less clear, as boundary
conditions for the auxiliary variable
\psi
may not be explicitly given.

In this paper, we propose and analyze a SFEM method for the surface Stokes problem
(
1.1a
) based on a primal
C^{0}
interior penalty (IP) method applied
towards the formulation (
1.2
).
Similar to
C^{0}
IP methods in the Euclidean case
[
15
,
4
]
,
the proposed scheme uses continuous, piecewise polynomial spaces
as the approximation spaces, and consistency
and symmetry is enforced by interelement contributions
of jumps and averages. This construction is done
on an approximate surface
\Gamma_{h}
defined via a polynomial-mapped approximation of
\Gamma
.
A notable feature of the proposed scheme is that it does not require an explicit approximation
of the Gauss curvature.
This is achieved through the use of a novel surface Hessian-type operator
and integration-by-parts formulas. As a result,
coercivity and continuity
properties of the proposed method mostly follow the same arguments as its Euclidean counterpart.
We show that the method converges and derive
explicit error estimates with respect to both the finite element degree
k

and the degree of the geometric approximation
k_{g}
.

One advantage of the
C^{0}
IP method
is that the discretization represents a positive definite
system involving a single unknown.
In addition, the discrete velocity
is recovered by simply taking the tangential curl
of the discrete stream function. This numerical velocity
is thus exactly tangential to the (approximate) surface
and does not need any ad hoc penalization techniques to enforce this constraint.
Potential disadvantages of the stream function approach (compared to a velocity-pressure-based formulation) is it requires
\Gamma
to be simply connected. Furthermore, as the stream function formulation
is fourth-order, the condition number of any (primal) discretization is expected to scale like
O(h^{-4})
, where
h
is the mesh width.

Recently, several finite element methods
for the surface biharmonic operator have been
proposed and analyzed. Closely related
to the present work is
[
21
]
,
where the authors propose and analyze the lowest-order
C^{0}
IP method for the surface
biharmonic problem on affine approximations. A variation
of this approach, utilizing a surface gradient recovery operator, is presented in
[
5
]
.
These discretizations are based on standard integration-by-parts formulas
of the surface biharmonic operator, leading to bilinear forms involving
the products of Laplacians. Such approaches can be easily formulated
towards the fourth-order problem (
1.2
), although the stability
and convergence of the scheme is unclear due to the indefinite
low-order terms in the PDE. This approach also leads to schemes where approximate Gauss
curvatures are required, which may require higher-order geometry approximations
or additional computational resources
[
3
,
2
,
31
]
.
The approach we take in this manuscript circumvents these issues
through an integration-by-parts procedure.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In the next section, we set the notation and provide
integration-by-parts identities for the surface biharmonic operator.
In Section
3
, we define the discrete surface
approximation
\Gamma_{h}
and mappings between
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{h}
.
Section
4
states the
C^{0}
IP method and proves
the continuity and coercivity of the corresponding bilienar form.
In Section
5
, we derive estimates of the geometric
inconsistencies of the scheme, and in Section
6

we prove error estimates of the
C^{0}
IP method in a discrete
H^{2}

norm and
H^{m}
norms (
m=0,1
). Numerical experiments are
given in Section
7
. Finally, some of the technical proofs
in the paper are provided in the appendix.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Gamma
be a smooth, simply connected compact
oriented hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
without boundary.
We denote by
U_{\delta}
a
\delta
-neighborhood of

\Gamma
with
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that the
signed distance function
d
is well-defined in
U_{\delta}

(with
d<0
in the interior of
\Gamma
). Set
{\bm{n}}=\nabla d
to be
the outward unit normal of
\Gamma
, extended to
U_{\delta}
, where the gradient is understood as a column vector.
The Weingarten map is
{\bf H}=D^{2}d
, the Hessian
matrix of
d
.
The tangential projection operator is given by

\displaystyle{\bf P}

\displaystyle={\bf I}-{\bm{n}}\otimes{\bm{n}}.

The closest point projection is

{\bm{p}}(x)=x-d(x){\bm{n}}(x).

Given a scalar function
\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
,
we set its extension
\psi^{e}:U_{\delta}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\psi^{e}(x)=\psi({\bm{p}}(x))

for
x\in U_{\delta}
. This definition is extended
to vector fields component wise (so that
({\bm{\psi}}^{e})_{j}=\psi_{j}^{e}
).
The surface gradient of
\psi
is

(2.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle={\bf P}\nabla\psi^{e}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{D}_{1}\psi\\
\underline{D}_{2}\psi\\
\underline{D}_{3}\psi\end{bmatrix},

i.e.,
\underline{D}_{j}\psi
is the
j
th component of

\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi
.
The surface curl operator of
\psi

is

{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi={\bm{n}}\times\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi.

For a vector-valued function
{\bm{v}}=[v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}]^{\intercal}
, its
Jacobian
\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}
satisfies
(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}})_{i,j}=\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
for
i,j=1,2,3
.
The surface Jacobian and surface deformation tensor are defined, respectively, as

(2.2)

\begin{split}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}&={\bf P}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bf P},\\
E_{\Gamma}({\bm{v}})&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}+\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}^{\intercal}\right).\end{split}

The surface divergence operator is defined as the trace of the Jacobian,
{\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}={\rm tr}(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}})
, and the scalar curl operator is
{\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}={\rm div}_{\Gamma}({\bm{v}}\times{\bm{n}})
.

Next, we provide notions of several second-order operators
that will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the nonsymmetric surface Hessian
\underline{D}^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
of a scalar function
\psi
such that

(\underline{D}^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi)_{i,j}=\underline{D}_{j}\underline{D}_{i}\psi
for
i,j=1,2,3
.
The projected surface Hessian
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

satisfies
\left(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi\right)_{i,j}=\left(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right)_{i,j}

for
i,j=1,2,3.
Finally, we define the second-order (Hessian-like) operator

H_{\Gamma}(\psi):=E_{\Gamma}({\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi),

where we recall
E_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
is the surface deformation tensor given by (
2.2
).
The Laplace-Beltrami operator is the trace for either
\underline{D}^{2}(\cdot)
or
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}(\cdot)
, i.e.,

\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi={\rm div}_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi={\rm tr}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi)={\rm tr}(\underline{D}^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\underline{D}_{i}^{2}\psi.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi
is symmetric (cf. Lemma
2.3
and
[
9
]
),
whereas
\underline{D}^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi
is not. In particular, there holds
[
13
, Lemma 2.6]

(2.3)

(\underline{D}^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi)^{\intercal}-\underline{D}^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi={\bm{n}}\otimes({\bf H}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)-({\bf H}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)\otimes{\bm{n}}.

The next two lemmas provide explicit relationships
between the three notions of the surface Hessian operators.

Lemma 2.3
.

There holds for
\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
,

(2.4)

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi={\bf P}\underline{D}^{2}\psi={\bf P}\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}{\bf P}.

Proof.

To prove the first inequality in (
2.4
), we first
consider a vector-valued function

{\bm{v}}=[v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}]^{\intercal}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
since
{\bf P}^{\intercal}={\bf P}
, the
i
th row of
\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bf P}
is

(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bf P})_{i,:}=(\nabla v^{e}_{i})^{\intercal}{\bf P}=\left({\bf P}\nabla v^{e}_{i}\right)^{\intercal}=\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{i}^{\intercal},

which implies

\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}={\bf P}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{v}}^{e}{\bf P}={\bf P}\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{1}^{\intercal}\\
\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{2}^{\intercal}\\
\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{3}^{\intercal}\end{bmatrix}.

Setting
{\bm{v}}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi
proves
the first equality in (
2.4
):

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi={\bf P}\begin{bmatrix}\underline{D}_{1}\underline{D}_{1}\psi&\underline{D}_{2}\underline{D}_{1}\psi&\underline{D}_{3}\underline{D}_{1}\psi\\
\underline{D}_{1}\underline{D}_{2}\psi&\underline{D}_{2}\underline{D}_{2}\psi&\underline{D}_{3}\underline{D}_{2}\psi\\
\underline{D}_{1}\underline{D}_{3}\psi&\underline{D}_{2}\underline{D}_{3}\psi&\underline{D}_{3}\underline{D}_{3}\psi\end{bmatrix}={\bf P}\underline{D}^{2}\psi.

To prove the second equality in (
2.4
),
we use the product rules

\displaystyle\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(f{\bm{g}})

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}\nabla f^{\intercal}+f\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{g}},

\displaystyle\nabla({\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{g}})

\displaystyle=\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{f}}^{\intercal}{\bm{g}}+\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{g}}^{\intercal}{\bm{f}},

and the identity
{\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla\psi^{e}={\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi=0
on
\Gamma

to conclude

(2.5)

\begin{split}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}&=\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla({\bf P}\nabla\psi^{e})\\
&=\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla\psi^{e}-({\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla\psi^{e}){\bm{n}})\\
&=\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}-{\bm{n}}\nabla({\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla\psi^{e})^{\intercal}-({\bm{n}}\cdot\nabla\psi^{e})\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{n}}\\
&=\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}-{\bm{n}}\otimes\left(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla{\bm{n}}^{\intercal}\nabla\psi^{e}+\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}{\bm{n}}\right)\\
&={\bf P}\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}-{\bm{n}}\otimes({\bf H}\nabla\psi^{e}).\end{split}

Since
{\bf P}^{2}={\bf P}
and
{\bf P}{\bm{n}}=0
,
there holds

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi={\bf P}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}{\bf P}={\bf P}\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}{\bf P}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For a vector
{\bm{\xi}}=\left[\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\right]^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, define
the
3\times 3
skew-symmetric matrix

{\bm{\xi}}^{\times}={\rm mskw}({\bm{\xi}})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c c c}0&-\xi_{3}&\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{3}&0&-\xi_{1}\\
-\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}&0\end{array}\right].

Then there holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle={\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi={\bm{n}}^{\times}\underline{D}^{2}\psi,

(2.7)

\displaystyle H_{\Gamma}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi-\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi{\bm{n}}^{\times})=\frac{1}{2}\left({\bm{n}}^{\times}\underline{D}^{2}\psi-\underline{D}^{2}\psi^{\intercal}{\bm{n}}^{\times}\right).

Proof.

Note that

{\bm{n}}^{\times}{\bf P}={\bm{n}}^{\times}={\bf P}{\bm{n}}^{\times},\quad\text{and}\quad{\bf H}{\bf P}={\bf H}={\bf P}{\bf H}.

Thus, using the identity

{\bm{n}}\times\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi={\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi=-(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{\times}{\bm{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{curl}_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle={\bf P}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla\left({\bm{n}}\times\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right)^{e}{\bf P}

\displaystyle={\bf P}\left({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}-(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{\times}{\bf H}\right){\bf P}

\displaystyle={\bm{n}}^{\times}\left(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\right){\bf P}-{\bf P}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{\times}{\bf H}.

Next, a direct calculation shows

\displaystyle{\bf P}({\bf P}{\bm{\xi}})^{\times}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}n_{1}\left(n_{3}\xi_{2}-n_{2}\xi_{3}\right)&n_{2}(n_{3}\xi_{2}-\xi_{3}n_{2})&n_{3}(\xi_{2}n_{3}-n_{2}\xi_{3})\\
n_{1}(\xi_{3}n_{1}-n_{3}\xi_{1})&n_{2}\left(n_{1}\xi_{3}-n_{3}\xi_{1}\right)&n_{3}(n_{1}\xi_{3}-\xi_{1}n_{3})\\
n_{1}(n_{2}\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}n_{1})&n_{2}(\xi_{1}n_{2}-n_{1}\xi_{2})&n_{3}\left(n_{2}\xi_{1}-n_{1}\xi_{2}\right)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=({\bm{\xi}}\times{\bm{n}})\otimes{\bm{n}}\qquad\forall{\bm{\xi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Hence, using
{\bf H}{\bm{n}}=0
we obtain

{\bf P}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{\times}{\bf H}={\bf P}\left({\bf P}\nabla\psi^{e}\right)^{\times}{\bf H}=(\nabla\psi^{e}\times{\bm{n}})\otimes{\bm{n}}{\bf H}=0,

and therefore,

\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{curl}_{\Gamma}\psi={\bm{n}}^{\times}\left(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)^{e}\right){\bf P}.

We then use (
2.5
) and (
2.4
), along
with
{\bm{n}}^{\times}{\bm{n}}=0
to obtain

\displaystyle\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{curl}_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle={\bm{n}}^{\times}\left({\bf P}\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}-{\bm{n}}\otimes({\bf H}\nabla\psi^{e})\right){\bf P}

\displaystyle={\bm{n}}^{\times}{\bf P}\nabla^{2}\psi^{e}{\bf P}={\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi.

The other equalities in (
2.6
)–(
2.7
) then
follow from the identities
\left({\bm{\xi}}^{\times}\right)^{\intercal}=-{\bm{\xi}}^{\times}
,
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi={\bf P}\underline{D}^{2}\psi
, and
{\bm{n}}^{\times}{\bm{n}}=0
.
∎

2.1.
Integration-by-parts identities for the surface biharmonic operator

In this section, we derive integration-by-parts identities
for the biharmonic operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}(\cdot):=\Delta_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{\Gamma}(\cdot))

over a a simply connected sub-domain
S\subset\Gamma
with boundary.
Such results will motivate the design
of the
C^{0}
interior penalty method discussed in the next section.
Our starting point is a well-known integration-by-parts identity.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
S\subset\Gamma
, and let
{\bm{\mu}}_{S}
denote the outward unit co-normal of
\partial S
.
Then there holds for all sufficiently
smooth functions
\phi,\psi
on
S
,

(2.8)

\int_{S}\psi\underline{D}_{i}\phi=-\int_{S}\phi\underline{D}_{i}\psi+\int_{S}\phi\psi{\rm tr}({\bf H})n_{i}+\int_{\partial S}\phi\psi(\mu_{S})_{i}\quad i=1,2,3.

Repeated applications of Lemma
2.5
immediately yield the following result.

Lemma 2.6
.

There holds for sufficient smooth functions
\phi
and
\psi
,

(2.9)

\begin{split}\int_{S}(\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi)\psi&=\int_{S}(\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi)(\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi)-\int_{\partial S}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})+\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\psi.\end{split}

In addition, applications of Lemma
2.5

also lead to an integration-by-parts identity involving
the projected surface Hessian operator
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}(\cdot)
.
The derivation of the following lemma is a bit more involved, and its proof
is given in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.7
.

There holds for sufficiently smooth functions
\phi,\psi
,

(2.10)

\begin{split}\int_{S}(\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi)\psi&=\int_{S}\left(\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\phi:\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi+K\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right)\\
&\qquad-\int_{\partial S}{\bm{\mu}}_{S}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi+\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\psi,\end{split}

where
K
is the Gauss curvature of
\Gamma
.

Finally, we derive an integration-by-parts identity
for the biharmonic operator such that,
when applied to the surface stream function problem (
1.2
),
the integrands over
S
do not explicitly depend on the Gauss curvature of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.8
.

There holds for sufficiently smooth functions
\phi
and
\psi
,

(2.11)

\begin{split}\int_{S}(\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}&\phi)\psi=2\int_{S}\left(H_{\Gamma}(\phi):H_{\Gamma}(\psi)+K\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right)\\
&-2\int_{\partial S}({\bm{t}}_{S}^{\intercal}H_{\Gamma}(\phi){\bm{\mu}}_{S})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})-2\int_{\partial S}({\bm{\mu}}_{S}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})({\bm{t}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)+\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\psi,\end{split}

where
{\bm{t}}_{S}={\bm{n}}_{S}\times{\bm{\mu}}_{S}
is the tangent vector of
\partial S
.
Consequently, if
\phi\in H^{4}(\Gamma)
satisfies (
1.2
),
then there holds for all smooth
\psi
,

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\int_{S}\left(H_{\Gamma}(\phi):H_{\Gamma}(\psi)+\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right)-\int_{\partial S}({\bm{t}}_{S}^{\intercal}H_{\Gamma}(\phi){\bm{\mu}}_{S})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\\
&\ \ +\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial S}\left((2K-2)\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi+\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi)\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S}\right)\psi-\int_{\partial S}({\bm{\mu}}^{T}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})({\bm{t}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)=-\int_{S}{\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bm{f}}\psi.\end{split}

Proof.

We begin with the following pointwise identity,
which holds for all sufficiently smooth tangential vector fields

{\bm{u}}

[
19
]
:

{\bf P}{\rm div}_{\Gamma}(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}^{\intercal})=\nabla_{\Gamma}({\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}})+K{\bm{u}}.

Setting
{\bm{u}}={\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi
,
so that
{\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}=0

and
\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bm{u}}^{\intercal}=-\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}
(cf. (
2.6
)) yields

-P{\rm div}_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times})=K{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi.

We then take the dot product of this expression
with
{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi
, integrate over
S
, and integrate by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S}K\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{S}K{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}):(\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla_{\Gamma}{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi)-\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\cdot{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}):({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi)-\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})\cdot{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi.

We then write, on
\partial S
,

\displaystyle{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi={\bm{n}}\times\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi

\displaystyle=({\bm{\mu}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi){\bm{t}}_{S}-({\bm{t}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi){\bm{\mu}}_{S},

to arrive at

(2.13)

\begin{split}\int_{S}K\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi&=\int_{S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}):({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi)\\
&\qquad-\int_{\partial S}({\bm{t}}^{\intercal}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})({\bm{\mu}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)+\int_{\partial S}({\bm{\mu}}_{S}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})({\bm{t}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi).\end{split}

Inserting this identity into
(
2.10
) and applying the
algebraic identities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\phi:\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi-(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}):({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=H_{\Gamma}(\phi):H_{\Gamma}(\psi),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\intercal}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{\mu}}_{S}-{\bm{t}}^{\intercal}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S}\right)

\displaystyle={\bm{t}}_{S}^{\intercal}H_{\Gamma}(\phi){\bm{\mu}}_{S},

then yield

\begin{split}\int_{S}(\Delta_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi)\psi&=\int_{S}\left(\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\phi:\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi+(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}):({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi)\right)\\
&\qquad-\int_{\partial S}({\bm{\mu}}^{\intercal}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{\mu}}_{S}+{\bm{t}}_{S}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})+\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\psi\\
&=2\int_{S}\left(H_{\Gamma}(\phi):H_{\Gamma}(\psi)+(\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{n}}^{\times}):({\bm{n}}^{\times}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi)\right)\\
&\qquad-\int_{\partial S}({\bm{\mu}}^{\intercal}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{\mu}}_{S}+{\bm{t}}_{S}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})+\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\psi\\
&=2\int_{S}\left(H_{\Gamma}(\phi):H_{\Gamma}(\psi)+K\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right)\\
&\qquad-2\int_{\partial S}({\bm{t}}^{\intercal}_{S}H_{\Gamma}(\phi){\bm{\mu}}_{S})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})-2\int_{\partial S}({\bm{\mu}}^{\intercal}_{S}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\phi{\bm{t}}_{S})({\bm{t}}_{S}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi)+\int_{\partial S}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}\phi\cdot{\bm{\mu}}_{S})\psi.\end{split}

Thus, (
2.11
) holds.
The identity (
2.12
) follows from (
2.11
)
and a simple application of the divergence theorem.

For an integer

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{\psi}\in C^{0}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h}):\ \bar{\psi}|_{\bar{T}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\bar{T})\ \forall\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},\qquad V_{h}=\{\psi\in C^{0}(\Gamma_{h}):\ \psi\circ{\bm{p}}_{k_{g}}\in\bar{V}_{h}\},

,

Likewise, we define the spaces of piecewise smooth functions

\displaystyle\bar{W}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{w}\in C^{0}(\bar{\Gamma}_{h}):\ \bar{w}|_{\bar{T}}\in H^{3}(\bar{T})\ \forall\bar{T}\in\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},\qquad W=\{w\in C^{0}(\Gamma_{h}):\ w|_{T}\in H^{3}(T)\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

, and

IP method, we assume for the moment

\displaystyle 2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\hskip-3.0pt\{{\bm{t}}_{h}^{\intercal}H_{\Gamma_{h}}(\psi){\bm{\mu}}_{h}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}[\hskip-2.0pt[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi]\hskip-2.0pt]

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|H_{\Gamma_{h}}(\psi)\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Gamma_{h}\right)}^{2}-2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\hskip-3.0pt\{{\bm{t}}_{h}^{\intercal}H_{\Gamma_{h}}(\psi){\bm{\mu}}_{h}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}[\hskip-2.0pt[\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi]\hskip-2.0pt]

with

(6.4)

\|u\|_{H^{4-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|(\Delta_{\Gamma})^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{H^{-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

Note that, by the consistency of the scheme,

a_{h}^{\ell}(\psi,u)=\big\langle\psi,(-\Delta_{\Gamma})^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\big\rangle_{\Gamma}\qquad\forall\psi\in W,

and therefore

(6.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\\
&\qquad=a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell},u\big)\\
&\qquad=a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell},u-\pi_{h}^{\ell}u\big)+a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell},\pi_{h}^{\ell}u\big)\\
&\qquad=a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell},u-\pi_{h}^{\ell}u\big)+\big[\ell(\pi_{h}^{\ell}u)-\ell_{h}(\pi_{h}u^{{e}})\big]+\big[a_{h}(\phi_{h},\pi_{h}u^{{e}})-a_{h}^{\ell}(\phi_{h}^{\ell},\pi_{h}^{\ell}u)\big]\\
&\qquad=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

Let us estimate each term in (
6.5
) separately. First,
note that

(6.6)

\displaystyle|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|u-\pi_{h}^{\ell}u|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{2}\lesssim h^{2-\underline{m}}\|u\|_{H^{4-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{2-\underline{m}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

Then, by the continuity estimate of
a_{h}^{\ell}(\cdot,\cdot)
in Lemma
4.3
and (
6.3
), there holds

(6.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}&=a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell},u-\pi_{h}^{\ell}u\big)\\
&\lesssim|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{2}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|u-\pi_{h}^{\ell}u|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{2}\\
&\lesssim\Big((h^{r-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}})\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+h^{2-\underline{m}}\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\Big)\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Next, due to (
5.1
), the stability bound
|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\pi_{h}^{\ell}u|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}
and (
6.4
),

(6.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=\ell(\pi_{h}^{\ell}u)-\ell_{h}(\pi_{h}u^{{e}})\lesssim\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\pi_{h}^{\ell}u|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}\lesssim\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Now, we consider the following in (
6.5
),

(6.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3}&=a_{h}(\phi_{h},\pi_{h}u^{{e}})-a_{h}^{\ell}(\phi_{h}^{\ell},\pi_{h}^{\ell}u)\\
&=\big[a_{h}\big(\phi_{h},\pi_{h}u^{{e}}-u^{e}\big)-a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi_{h}^{\ell},\pi_{h}^{\ell}u-u\big)\big]+\big[a_{h}\big(\phi_{h}-\phi^{e},u^{e}\big)-a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi_{h}^{\ell}-\phi,u\big)\big]\\
&\qquad+\big[a_{h}(\phi^{e},u^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(\phi,u)\big]\\
&=:I_{3,1}+I_{3,2}+I_{3,3}.\end{split}

Estimate
I_{3,1}
: Applying (
4.5
), (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) yields

(6.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}&\leq|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi-\pi_{h}^{\ell}\phi|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}+|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\pi_{h}^{\ell}\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}\\
&\lesssim h^{r-2}\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\pi_{h}^{\ell}\phi-\phi|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{2}+|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{2}\\
&\lesssim(h^{r-2}+h^{k_{g}})\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Therefore,
|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi_{h}^{\ell}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
, and so by (
5.10
), (
4.5
) and (
6.6
),

(6.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3,1}&=a_{h}\big(\phi_{h},\pi_{h}u^{{e}}-u^{e}\big)-a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi_{h}^{\ell},\pi_{h}^{\ell}u-u\big)\\
&\lesssim h^{k_{g}}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi_{h}^{\ell}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\pi_{h}^{\ell}u-u|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}\\
&\lesssim h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}}\left(\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right)\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Estimate
I_{3,2}
: Using (
5.10
), (
6.10
), (
4.5
), and (
6.4
),

(6.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3,2}&=a_{h}\big(\phi_{h}-\phi^{e},u^{e}\big)-a_{h}^{\ell}\big(\phi_{h}^{\ell}-\phi,u\big)\\
&\lesssim h^{k_{g}}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|\phi_{h}^{\ell}-\phi|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|u|\hskip-2.0pt|\hskip-2.0pt|_{H}\\
&\lesssim h^{k_{g}}\Big((h^{r-2}+h^{k_{g}})\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\Big)\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Estimate
I_{3,3}
: By (
5.11
) and (
6.4
),

(6.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3,3}&=a_{h}(\phi^{e},u^{e})-a_{h}^{\ell}(\phi,u)\\
&\lesssim(h^{2k_{g}-1}+h^{k_{g}+1})\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}\|u\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma)}\\
&\lesssim(h^{2k_{g}-1}+h^{k_{g}+1})\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}\|\nabla^{m}_{\Gamma}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Combining (
6.9
), (
6.11
), (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) yields

(6.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3}&\lesssim\Bigg(\left(h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}}+h^{r-2+k_{g}}+h^{2k_{g}}+h^{2k_{g}-1}+h^{k_{g}+1}\right)\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}\\
&\qquad+\left(h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}}\right)\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\Bigg)\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\lesssim h^{k_{g}}\left(\left(h^{2-\underline{m}}+h^{r-2}+h^{k_{g}-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right)\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

It follows from (
6.5
), (
6.7
), (
6.8
) and (
6.14
) that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\Gamma}^{m}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(h^{r-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}+r-2}+h^{2k_{g}-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(h^{2-\underline{m}}+1+h^{k_{g}}\right)\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(h^{r-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}+2-\underline{m}}+h^{k_{g}+r-2}+h^{2k_{g}-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{H^{r}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\phi
be the exact solution to (
1.2
) and let
\phi_{h}\in V_{h,0}
solve the
C^{0}
IP method (
4.1
).
Define
{\bm{u}}:={\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi
and
{\bm{u}}_{h}:={\bf curl}_{\Gamma_{h}}\phi_{h}
. If
\phi\in H^{k+1}(\Gamma)
, then

\|{\bm{u}}-\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}}{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim(h^{k}+h^{k_{g}+1}+h^{2k_{g}-1})\|\phi\|_{H^{k+1}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

where

\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{p}}}{\bm{u}}_{h}\circ{\bm{p}}=\mu_{h}^{-1}({\bf P}-d{\bf H}){\bm{u}}_{h}

is the Piola transform of
{\bm{u}}_{h}
with respect to the closest point projection
{\bm{p}}
.

Proof.

Using (
C.2
) below, we can conclude
\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{p}}}{\bm{u}}_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{p}}}({\bf curl}_{\Gamma_{h}}\phi_{h})={\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi_{h}^{\ell}
.
Therefore by applying the result from the particular case
m=1
in Theorem
6.3
yields

\displaystyle\|{\bm{u}}-\mathcal{P}_{\bm{p}}{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=\|{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi-{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=\|{\bm{n}}\times\nabla_{\Gamma}(\phi-\phi_{h}^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(h^{k}+h^{k_{g}+1}+h^{2k_{g}-1})\|\phi\|_{H^{k+1}(\Gamma)}+\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

∎

7.
Numerical Experiments

In the numerical experiments,
we consider an ellipsoid
\Gamma=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\Psi(x)=0:\ \Psi(x)=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}/2+x_{3}^{2}/2-1\}
,
and take the stream function solution as
\phi(x)=e^{x_{1}}(\cos(x_{2})+x_{3})

and the pressure solution as
p(x)=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
.
We determine the velocity
{\bm{u}}={\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\phi
and the force
{\bm{f}}
on the
right hand side of (
1.1a
) through Mathematica.
These expressions for
{\bm{u}}
and
{\bm{f}}
are well-defined
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and we respectively take
{\bm{u}}^{ce}
and
{\bm{f}}^{ce}
to be these
expressions defined on
\Gamma_{h}
. In all of the numerical experiments, we take
{\bm{f}}_{h}={\bm{f}}^{ce}
,
and note that (cf. Lemma
5.1

and Remark
5.2
)

(7.1)

\|{\bm{f}}-{\bm{F}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{k_{g}+1}.

Using NGSolve
[
28
]
, we solve the
C^{0}
IP method (
4.1
) for various values of
k
and
k_{g}
while varying
h
,
and also compute the approximate velocity function
{\bm{u}}_{h}={\bf curl}_{\Gamma_{h}}\phi_{h}
.
We compute the errors on
\Gamma_{h}
, and therefore we need to map both the exact stream function
\phi
and velocity
{\bm{u}}

to the discrete surface
\Gamma_{h}
. Ideally, this would be accomplished via the
closest point project and the Piola transform with respect to the inverse of the closest point projection, respectively:

\phi^{e}=\phi\circ{\bm{p}},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}=\mu_{h}\bigg({\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{n}}\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}}{{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}}\bigg)({\bf I}-d{\bf H})^{-1}({\bm{u}}\circ{\bm{p}}).

Unfortunately, an explicit formula for the distance function of the ellipsoid is unknown,
and therefore its closest point projection
is unavailable. Instead, based on
[
11
, Section 4.2]
, we make the approximations

\tilde{\bm{n}}(x)=\frac{\nabla\Psi(x)}{|\nabla\Psi(x)|},\quad\tilde{d}(x)=\frac{\Psi(x)}{|\nabla\Psi(x)|},\quad\tilde{{\bm{p}}}(x)=x-\tilde{d}(x)\tilde{\bm{n}}(x),\qquad x\in\Gamma_{h}.

We then have
|d(x)-\tilde{d}(x)|=O(d)=O(h^{k_{g}+1})
, and

\displaystyle|{\bm{n}}(x)-\tilde{\bm{n}}(x)|=\left|\frac{\nabla\Psi({\bm{p}}(x))}{|\nabla\Psi({\bm{p}}(x))|}-\frac{\nabla\Psi(x)}{|\nabla\Psi(x)|}\right|\lesssim|{\bm{p}}(x)-x|=O(h^{k_{g}+1}).

Therefore
|{\bm{p}}(x)-\tilde{\bm{p}}(x)|=O(h^{k_{g}+1})
, although
\tilde{\bm{p}}(x)
does not necessarily lie on
\Gamma
.

Similar to above, we let

\phi^{ce}(x)=e^{x_{1}}(\cos(x_{2})+x_{3}),\quad x\in\Gamma_{h},

be the canonical extension of
\phi

and set

\tilde{\phi}^{e}=\phi^{ce}\circ\tilde{\bm{p}},

as the approximate extension to
\phi
.
These extensions satisfy
|\phi^{e}(x)-\tilde{\phi}^{e}(x)|\lesssim h^{k_{g}+1}

and
|\phi^{e}(x)-\phi^{ce}(x)|\lesssim h^{k_{g}+1}
.
Therefore, Theorem
6.3
with
m=0
and (
7.1
) yield

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\phi}^{e}-\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{2}+h^{k_{g}+1}+h^{2k_{g}-1}&k=2\\
h^{k+1}+h^{k_{g}+1}+h^{2k_{g}-1}&k\geq 3\end{array},\right.

and the same orders of convergence hold for
\|\phi^{ce}-\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}
.

We map the exact velocity
{\bm{u}}
to
\Gamma_{h}
using
an approximate Piola transform

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}:=\left({\bf I}-\frac{(\tilde{\bm{n}}\circ\tilde{{\bm{p}}})\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}}{(\tilde{\bm{n}}\circ\tilde{{\bm{p}}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}}\right)({\bm{u}}^{ce}\circ\tilde{{\bm{p}}}).

Note that
|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}-\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}|=O(d)=O(h^{k_{g}+1})
,
and so
\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

has the same order of convergence as stated in Corollary
6.4
:

(7.4)

\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{k}+h^{k_{g}+1}+h^{2k_{g}-1}.

However,
|{\bm{u}}^{ce}-\mathcal{P}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}|=O(h^{k_{g}})
on
\Gamma_{h}
,
and therefore Corollary
6.4
only yields

(7.5)

\|{\bm{u}}^{ce}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\lesssim h^{k}+h^{k_{g}}.

The errors for
\|\tilde{\phi}^{e}-\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

and
\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{{\bm{p}}^{-1}}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

are provided in Table
1
.
For the velocity error, we see rates of convergence
that are in agreement with (
7.4
),
except in the case
k_{g}=1
, where we observe
second-order convergence, instead of first order.
This suggests that the term
h^{2k_{g}-1}
in (
7.4
)
leads to an estimate that is not sharp.

For the stream function error, we see rates of convergence of order
O(h^{k+1})

for
k\geq 3
in the isoparametric setting
k_{g}=k
, which is in agreement
with the theoretical estimate (
7
).
However, we also observe the optimal order
O(h^{4})

in the case
k=3
and
k_{g}=2
,
and the order
O(h^{4})
for
k=4
and
k_{g}=2
.
Similar to the velocity error, we also observe second-order convergence
in the case
k_{g}=1
.
The rates of convergence for
k=4
and
k_{g}\in\{3,4\}

are unclear, as we observe a degradation of the error,
possibly due to round-off error and the poor conditioning
of the system.

\|{\bm{u}}^{ce}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{2{h}})}

.
Here, we see exact agreement
with the expected rates of convergence
of the velocity given in (
7.5
).
Likewise, the stream function error convergence

7
),
except in the case
k_{g}=1
, where we observe second-order convergence.

\nabla\!\!\!\!\nabla\hat{x}=(A^{\intercal}A)^{-1}A^{\intercal}=:C

Lemma C.1
.

There holds on
\Gamma_{h}
,

(C.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi

\displaystyle={\bf P}_{h}({\bf P}-d{\bf H})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e},

(C.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf curl}_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi&={\bm{n}}_{h}^{\times}{\bf P}_{h}({\bf P}-d{\bf H})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e}\\

(C.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}^{2}\psi&={\bf P}_{h}\big((\nabla_{\Gamma}^{2}\psi^{\ell})^{e}-d{\bf H}(\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e}-d(\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e}{\bf H}+d^{2}{\bf H}(\nabla^{2}_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e}{\bf H}\big){\bf P}_{h}\\

(C.4)

(\nabla{\bf H}{\bm{v}})_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{\partial H_{i,k}}{\partial x_{j}}v_{k}.

Identity
(
C.1
): Since
\psi^{\ell}
is constant along normals, we have
\psi^{\ell}=\psi^{\ell}\circ{\bm{p}}
. Likewise, we have
{\bm{n}}={\bm{n}}\circ{\bm{p}}

Consequently,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi={\bf P}_{h}\nabla\psi^{\ell}={\bf P}_{h}({\bf I}-d{\bf H})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e},

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi)^{\ell}={\bf P}_{h}({\bf I}-d{\bf H})\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell}.

(
C.2
): The first identity in (
C.2

C.1

\displaystyle{\bf curl}_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi={\bm{n}}_{h}^{\times}{\bf P}_{h}({\bf P}-d{\bf H})(\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell})^{e}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad({\bf curl}_{\Gamma_{h}}\psi)^{\ell}={\bm{n}}_{h}^{\times}{\bf P}_{h}({\bf P}-d{\bf H})\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell}.

Now a direct calculation shows

\displaystyle=(({\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}){\bf I}-{\bm{n}}\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h})\big(\big[1-d{\rm tr}({\bf H})\big]{\bf I}+d{\bf H}\big){\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell}.

\displaystyle=\mu_{h}\bigg({\bf I}-\frac{{\bm{n}}\otimes{\bm{n}}_{h}}{{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{h}}\bigg)({\bf I}-d{\bf H})^{-1}{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi^{\ell}.

(
C.3

Appendix D
Proof of Lemma
6.2

To prove the regularity estimates, we require an intermediate result.

Lemma D.1
.

Let
m\in\{0,1\}
. For any
\chi\in H^{-m}(\Gamma)
, there exists
{\bm{r}}\in{\bm{H}}^{1-m}(\Gamma)
such that

{\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bm{r}}=-\chi
and
\|{\bm{r}}\|_{{{\bm{H}}}^{1-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\chi\|_{H^{-m}(\Gamma)}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
satisfy
-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi=\chi
. Elliptic regularity yields

\psi\in H^{2-m}(\Gamma)
and
\|\psi\|_{H^{2-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\chi\|_{H^{-m}(\Gamma)}

[
13
, Lemma 3.2]
.
We then set
{\bm{r}}={\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi\in{\bm{H}}^{1-m}(\Gamma)
, so that

{\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bm{r}}={\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bf curl}_{\Gamma}\psi=\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi=-\chi,

and
\|{\bm{r}}\|_{H^{1-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{2-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\chi\|_{H^{-m}(\Gamma)}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Using Lemma
D.1
, we let
{\bm{r}}\in{\bm{H}}^{1-m}(\Gamma)
satisfy
{\rm curl}_{\Gamma}{\bm{r}}=-\chi
.
Setting
{\bm{v}}={\bf curl}_{\Gamma}u
,
we then have

\displaystyle-{\bf P}{\rm div}_{\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}({\bm{v}})+\nabla_{\Gamma}s

\displaystyle={\bm{r}},

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{\Gamma}{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=0,

for some
s\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
.
Using the elliptic regularity of the surfaces Stokes problem
{\bm{v}}\in H^{3-m}(\Gamma)
with
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{3-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|{\bm{r}}\|_{H^{1-m}(\Gamma)}
,
along with the Poincare inequality
[
13
, Theorem 2.12]
,
we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{4-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma}u\|_{H^{3-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|{\bm{n}}\times{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{3-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|{\bm{r}}\|_{H^{1-m}(\Gamma)}\lesssim\|\chi\|_{H^{-m}(\Gamma)}.

∎

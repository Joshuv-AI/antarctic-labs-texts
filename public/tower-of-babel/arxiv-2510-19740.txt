1.
Introduction

An arithmetic function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is called multiplicative if
f(mn)=f(m)f(n)
whenever
(m,n)=1
. An integer partition

of a positive integer

into the function

(1.1)

\displaystyle n=\lfloor{f(n_{1})}\rfloor+\lfloor{f(n_{2})}\rfloor+\cdots+\lfloor{f(n_{r})}\rfloor,

where

is a real positive valued multiplicative function and
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\cdots\leq n_{r}
. It is a well-known fact that one can write the above expression with the generating series

(1.2)

\displaystyle{Q_{f}}(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{{p_{f}}(n){z^{n}}}=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}(1-z^{\lfloor{f(n)}\rfloor})^{-1}\quad(|z|<1).

The asymptotic formula for integer partitions, that is, when
f(n)=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
in (
1.2
), was first established by Hardy and Ramanujan
[
15
]
. Over the past century, the study of partition asymptotics has attracted the attention of numerous mathematicians; see, for example,
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
32
]
. The most general framework in this context was studied by Debruyne and Tenenbaum
[

.

Let
p_{f}(n)
and

denote, respectively, the “restricted” partition of
n
and and the “restricted” partition of
n
with fixed length

as defined in (

), where
n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\ldots\leq n_{r}
. Li and Chen
[

an alternative approach to analysing the generating series (

\displaystyle Q_{f}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{f}(n)z^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-z^{n})^{-\Delta_{f}(n)}\quad(|z|<1).

where

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}(n)=\#\{m:m\in\mathbb{N}\;\text{and}\;\lfloor{f(m)}\rfloor=n\}.

A related problem was studied by Balasubramanian and Luca
[
3
]
, who investigated the number of solutions to
|\{m:m,n\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{and}\quad m=\mathcal{F}(n)\}|

denotes the number of unordered factorisation as the products of integer factors greater than 1, by examining the partitions of the form

. Later, this result was thoroughly investigated by Li and Chen
[

]
.

Our main objective of this paper is to establish a central limit theorem for the random variable
\varpi_{f,n}
, which counts the number of summands in a random
f
-restricted partition of
n
(as defined in Section
2
) with respect to the multiplicative function
f
. This concept was first introduced by Erdős and Lehner
[

, where they studied the distribution of ratio

, with

as a function of

and

denoting number of integer partitions of
n
. In this context, it is noteworthy that the weighted moments of integer partitions were studied by Richmond
[

. Let

denotes the partition of
n
into

. The

(1.5)

\displaystyle t^{k}_{A}(n)=\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^{k}p_{A}(n,r),

for arbitrary fixed

as

. Furthermore, Richmond remarked that the study of these weighted moments supports the work of Erdős and Lehner.

In a recent study, Lipnik, Madritsch, and Tichy
[

]
established a central limit theorem for the partition function
p_{f}(n)
with

for
0<\alpha<1
. For our main result, we have considered
f
to be the generalised divisor function
\sigma_{r}(n)
for integers
r\geq 2
.
However, our approach is applicable for a wider class of multiplicative functions, which we explore in detail in later sections.

The proof presented in
[
22
]
used a probabilistic approach and Curtiss’ theorem to obtain the asymptotics of the moment generating function. Furthermore, the application of converse mapping to study the analytic behaviour of the associated Dirichlet series makes their proof simple and elegant. This also allows us to choose a complex weight function in the bivariate generating series and deal with a challenging associated Dirichlet series, as the remaining components of the proof are comparatively less involved than those encountered in prior works on this problem.

Studying the limiting distribution of

for
f
being any multiplicative function has a different flavour compared to previous works. Our analysis relies on additive combinatorics, due to the involvement of the gap properties
(|f(n+1)-f(n)|)_{n}
of the multiplicative function

. While we establish a central limit theorem, deriving an asymptotic formula for (

) in the setting of
f
-restricted partitions would present additional difficulties, largely due to the behaviour of the gap function

. However, it appears plausible that an upper bound could be obtained in future work.

Throughout this paper

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider the bivariate generating series

\displaystyle Q_{f}(z,u)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+uz^{k})^{\Delta_{f}(k)}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{f}(n)\mathbb{E}(u^{\varpi_{f,n}})z^{n}.

\displaystyle Q_{f}(z,u)=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{f}(n,k)u^{k}z^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{f}(n)z^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{f}(n,k)}{p_{f}(n)}u^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{f}(n)\mathbb{E}(u^{\varpi_{f,n}})z^{n},

Prior to stating our main result, we note that Lipnik et al.

\displaystyle\mu_{f,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)}{e^{\eta k}+1}=O(C_{\mu}(r)n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}})

\displaystyle\nu^{2}_{f,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)e^{\eta k}}{(e^{\eta k}+1)^{2}}-\frac{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)ke^{\eta k}}{(e^{\eta k}+1)^{2}}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)k^{2}e^{\eta k}}{(e^{\eta k}+1)^{2}}}=O(C_{\sigma}(r)n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}),

\displaystyle n=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k}{e^{\eta k}+1}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n}}{\nu_{f,n}}\geq x\right)\leq\begin{cases}e^{-x^{2}/2}\left(1+O((\log n)^{-3})\right)&\text{if $0\leq x\leq n^{\frac{r+1}{6(r+2)}}/\log n$},\\

2.1.
Initial Setup

In this section, we provide an outline of the proof of Theorem
2.2
. We adopt the saddle-point approach from
[
22
, §3]
for the initial construction. However, our analysis necessitates a more intricate argument concerning the distribution of multiplicative functions due to the presence of the Dirichlet series with a shifted coefficient.

We begin by observing that, to establish the central limit theorem for the random variable
\varpi_{f,n}
, it is necessary to show that the normalised moment generating function
\mathbb{E}\left(e^{(\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n})t/\nu_{f,n}}\right)
converges to
e^{t^{2}/2}
as
t\to\infty
. Finally, we estimate the tail of the distribution using the Chernoff bound.

Since
\mu_{f,n}/\nu_{f,n}
a is constant, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(e^{(\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n})t/\nu_{f,n}}\right)=e^{-\mu_{f,n}/\nu_{f,n}}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\varpi_{f,n}t/\nu_{f,n}}\right).

To achieve our goal we need to obtain the coefficient of
z^{n}
in the bivariate generating series
Q(z,u)
. By Lemma
2.1
, the probability generating function is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(u^{\varpi_{f,n}})=\frac{[z^{n}]Q_{f}(z,u)}{[z^{n}]Q_{f}(z,1)}.

Applying Cauchy integral formula we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{f,n}(u)\coloneqq[z^{n}]Q_{f}(z,u)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=e^{-\tau}}\frac{Q_{f}(z,u)}{z^{n+1}}dz.

A standard transformation gives, for
\tau>0
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{f,n}(u)=\frac{e^{n\tau}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\exp\left(in\theta+F(\tau+i\theta,u)\right)d\theta,

where

(2.7)

\displaystyle F(\gamma,u)\coloneqq\log Q_{f}(e^{-\gamma},u)=\log\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+ue^{-\gamma k})^{\Delta_{f}(k)}\right).

To tackle the integral in (
2.6
), we utilise the saddle-point technique, which is often referred to as the method of steepest descent. It can be viewed as a coarse version of the circle method. Accordingly, the primary application of this method is to derive estimates for integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{\Phi(\tau+i\theta)}d\theta,

for a suitable function
\Phi
. We will now split the integral into two parts. Choose
\theta_{n}>0
, then we write

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{\Phi(\tau+i\theta)}d\theta=\int_{|\theta|\leq\theta_{n}}e^{\Phi(\tau+i\theta)}d\theta+\int_{\theta_{n}<|\theta|\leq\pi}e^{\Phi(\tau+i\theta)}d\theta.

The first part will compute the contribution near the positive real axis, or, equivalently, around
\theta=0
. This will give us the main term, whereas the contribution from the real line i.e, the estimate arising from
e^{\Phi(\tau+i\theta)-\mathrm{Re}(\Phi(\tau))}
will give us the error term. Using the Taylor expansion up to the third order, the function
\Phi
can be expressed as

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau+i\theta)=\Phi(\tau)+i\theta\Phi^{\prime}(\tau)-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+O\left(\sup_{\theta}\left|\theta^{3}\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tau+i\theta)\right|\right).

We now choose
\tau
such that the first-order derivative
\Phi^{\prime}(\tau)
vanishes. Thus, the remaining term of the integral in (
2.8
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{|\theta|<\theta_{n}}e^{\Phi(\tau+i\theta)}d\theta=e^{\Phi(\tau)}\int_{|\theta|<\theta_{n}}e^{-\tfrac{\theta^{2}}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)}\left(1+O\left(\sup_{\theta}\left|\theta^{3}\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tau+i\theta)\right|\right)\right)d\theta.

We need to analyse the integrand with the terms involving
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\tau)
in order to show that the aforementioned transformation and estimates are valid. For a detailed explanation, we refer to
[
22
, §3]
. Recall the Mellin transform of a function
h(t)
, defined by

\displaystyle h^{*}(s)\coloneqq\mathcal{M}[h;s]=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)t^{s-1}dt,

which serves as one of the key ingredients in our analysis. Of particular importance is the rescaling rule (see, for instance,
[
14
, Theorem 1]
), given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}h(\mu_{k}x);s\right]=h^{*}(s)\sum_{k}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\mu_{k}}.

Let
\delta>0
and set
\delta\leq u\leq\delta^{-1}
. By “uniformly in
u
”, we mean “uniformly as
\delta\leq u\leq\delta^{-1}
” throughout this paper. Thus, the Mellin transform for the polynomial with respect to
\gamma
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\sum_{k}\Delta_{f}(k)\log(1+ue^{-\gamma k});s\right]=\mathcal{D}_{f}(\Delta;s)\mathcal{I}(s,u),

where

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{f}(\Delta;s)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+ue^{-\gamma})\gamma^{s-1}d\gamma,

represents the Mellin transform of
\gamma\to(1+ue^{-\gamma})
.

The principal advantage of the Mellin transform lies not in the transform itself, but in the inverse mapping, which allows us to examine the singularities of the transformed function to obtain the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle f^{*}(s)=O(|s|^{-r})

Therefore, in our case, we must consider the singularities of the Dirichlet series

and
\Gamma(s)

\displaystyle|\Gamma(\sigma+it)|=\sqrt{2\pi}|t|^{\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}}e^{-\pi|t|/2}(1+O_{a,b}(1/t)),

where

and

. For the polylogarithm function, we adopt the approach outlined in

. Let

\displaystyle\Lambda(w)\coloneqq\mathrm{Li}_{r}(e^{-w})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-nw}}{n^{r}}.

As this expression represents a harmonic sum, we apply Mellin transform and obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-nw}}{n^{r}}w^{s-1}dw=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s+r}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-v}v^{s-1}dv=\zeta(s+r)\Gamma(s),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{r}(z)=\Gamma(1-r)w^{r-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\zeta(r-j)w^{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{f,n}(u)=\frac{e^{n\tau+F(\tau,u)}}{\sqrt{2\pi B}}(1+O(\tau^{2r/7-1})).

\displaystyle M_{n}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}(e^{(\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n})\theta/\nu_{f,n}})=\exp\left(-\frac{\mu_{f,n}\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)\mathbb{E}(e^{\theta\varpi_{f,n}/\nu_{f,n}})=\exp\left(-\frac{\mu_{f,n}\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{n}(e^{1/\nu_{f,n}})}{\mathcal{Q}_{n}(1)}.

\displaystyle M_{n}(\theta)=\exp\left(\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}+O(n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)})\right),

.

3.
The Dirichlet series

In this section, we examine the asymptotic behaviour of the Dirichlet series twisted by the shifted divisor coefficient, which plays a crucial role in deriving the main term. By substituting the expression for
\Delta_{f}(n)
into the Dirichlet series given in (
2.10
), we arrive at

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{f}(\Delta;s)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n+1)-f(n)}{n^{s}}\eqqcolon A_{f}(s)-D_{f}(s),

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle A_{f}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n+1)}{n^{s}}

is the Dirichlet series with a shifted coefficient and

(3.3)

\displaystyle D_{f}(s)=\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}},

for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\alpha
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Note that for a class of multiplicative functions
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with period 1, i.e.
f(n+1)=f(n)
for all values of
n
, the analytic properties of
A_{f}(s)
are closely analogous to those of
D_{f}(s)
. One notable example in this context is the Dirichlet character
\chi(n)
with period 1. However, determining the analytic behaviour of
A_{f}(s)
(such as its poles and residues) directly from the Dirichlet series
D_{f}(s)
for general multiplicative functions
f
remains a difficult problem.

The study of the distribution and growth of the gap function
(|f(n+1)-f(n)|)_{n}
presents an intriguing area of research, leading to significant insights into the distribution of multiplicative functions at primes. In this context, notable contributions have been made by O. Klurman
[
18
]
and A. Mangerel
[
24
]
. Using the properties of correlations of “pretentious” functions, Klurman
[
18
]
counts the number of ways to write
n=a+b
, where
a,b
belongs to some multiplicative subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. Recently, Mangerel
[
24
]
has explored this type of result in further detail. However, the main result of this paper takes a different approach, connecting instead with counting problems of the type described in (
1.4
).

In particular, the Ramanujan sum
c_{m}(n)
possesses arithmetic identities with a broad class of multiplicative functions. Furthermore, it can be interpreted as a character, providing significant leverage to deduce the properties of
A_{f}(s)
from
D_{f}(s)
for those multiplicative functions
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
that exhibit an identity with the Ramanujan sum.

For the case of generalised divisor function
f(n)=\sigma_{r}(n)
, we use the identity (see for instance
[
19
]
) for fixed

,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{r}(n)=\zeta(r+1)n^{r}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{m}(n)}{m^{r+1}},

with the Ramanujan sum given in (

Note that the case

poses different challenges within the framework of our analysis. Observe that when

for

The function
f
, considered in this paper satisfies an identity involving the Ramanujan sum

. These relations are crucial for handling the Dirichlet series

with shifted a coefficient. Moreover, our proof can be extended to encompass any multiplicative functions that exhibit similar identities with the Ramanujan sum. Notably, this method also applies to the von Mangoldt function

is not a multiplicative function, it satisfies a similar identity involving the Ramanujan sum. Consequently, the arguments presented in this article are sufficient to establish a central limit theorem, analogous to Theorem

Remark 3.1
.

In this context, it is important to observe that the Ramanujan sum

is a sign-changing function, which implies that integer partitions involving the Ramanujan sum cannot be expressed in terms of (

can be established for the Ramanujan sum, subject with certain restrictions on

For Dirichlet character

modulo

(3.5)

\displaystyle c_{\chi}(n)\coloneqq\sum_{b(\bmod{m})}\chi(b)e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad c^{\prime}_{\chi}(n)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b(\bmod{m})\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(b)e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right).

Before analysing the Dirichlet series

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
c_{m}(n)
denote the Ramanujan sum for
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
. Then the following identity holds

\displaystyle c_{m}(n+1)=\frac{\mu(m)}{\varphi(m)}c_{m}(n)+\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\bmod{m})\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\tau(\chi)c^{\prime}_{\bar{\chi}}(n),

where
\tau(\chi)=c_{\chi}(1)
has defined in (

Proof.

Considering the Fourier expansion of the additive character (see, for instance,
[
17
, §3.4]
), we write

(3.6)

\displaystyle e\left(\frac{b}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\bmod{m})\end{subarray}}\bar{\chi}(b)\tau(\chi),\quad\text{if $(b,m)=1$},

where
\tau(\chi)=\sum_{b(\bmod{m})}\chi(b)e(b/m)
denotes the Gauss sum associated with the Dirichlet character
\chi
. We simplify the shifted Ramanujan sum as follows

\displaystyle c_{m}(n+1)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\bmod{m}\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{b(n+1)}{m}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\bmod{m}\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right)e\left(\frac{b}{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\bmod{m}\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\chi(\bmod{m})}\bar{\chi}(b)\tau(\chi)\right)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\chi(\bmod{m})}\tau(\chi)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\bmod{m}\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}\bar{\chi}(b)e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right),

where we substituted (
3.6
) into the second line. We shall isolate the terms associated with principal characters as these are considerably simpler. Consequently, from the preceding expression, we derive

\displaystyle c_{m}(n+1)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\bmod{m})\\
\chi=\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\tau(\chi)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\bmod{m}\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}\bar{\chi}(b)e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\bmod{m})\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\tau(\chi)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\bmod{m}\\
(b,m)=1\end{subarray}}\bar{\chi}(b)e\left(\frac{bn}{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(m)}{\varphi(m)}c_{m}(n)+\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\bmod{m})\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\tau(\chi)c^{\prime}_{\bar{\chi}}(n),

thereby concluding the lemma.
∎

In the previous lemma, we transformed the shifted Ramanujan sum into a multiplicative structure by using the character sum. This approach facilitates the analysis of the Dirichlet series that arises in the preceding argument.

Lemma 3.3
.

For a fixed integer
r\geq 1
, consider the double Dirichlet series

(3.8)

\displaystyle D_{1}(s,r)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(m)}{\varphi(m)m^{r+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{m}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
c_{m}(n)
denotes the Ramanujan sum. The following identity then holds for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
or
\mathrm{Re}(s)+r>0
:

\displaystyle D_{1}(s,r)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+r+1)}K_{r}(s),

where

(3.9)

\displaystyle K_{r}(s)=C(r)\prod_{p}\left(1-A_{p,r}(s)\right)

with

\displaystyle A_{p,r}(s)=\frac{\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}{1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{s}}-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s+r+1}}-\frac{1}{p^{2s+2r+2}}+\cdots-\frac{1}{p^{(k+1)s+r+1}}+\cdots\right)

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle C(r)\coloneqq\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}\right).

Proof.

Using relation (3.3) of
[
17
, §3.2]
, the following expression simplifies as

\displaystyle\frac{c_{m}(n)}{\varphi(m)}=\frac{1}{\varphi(m)}\frac{\varphi(m)\mu\left({m}/{(m,n)}\right)}{\varphi\left({m}/{(m,n)}\right)}=\frac{\mu\left({m}/{(m,n)}\right)}{\varphi\left({m}/{(m,n)}\right)}.

By rearranging the terms in (
3.8
) and substituting the above relation, we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle D_{1}(s,r)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{r+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu({m}/{(m,n)})}{\varphi\left({m}/{(m,n)}\right)n^{s}}.

Let
\delta=(m,n)
, then we may write
m=k\delta
and
n=\ell\delta
. Consequently, upon making the change of variables in (
3.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle D_{1}(s,r)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\geq 1}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{(m,n)=\delta}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{r+1}}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\frac{\mu({m}/{(m,n)})}{\varphi\left({m}/{(m,n)}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\geq 1}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{(k,\ell)=1}\frac{\mu(k\delta)}{(k\delta)^{r+1}}\frac{1}{(\ell\delta)^{s}}\frac{\mu(k)}{\varphi(k)}.

Rearranging the order of summation yields

\displaystyle D_{1}(s,r)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(k)}{\varphi(k)k^{r+1}}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{(\delta,k)=1}\frac{\mu(\delta)}{\delta^{s+r+1}}\sum_{(\ell,k)=1}\frac{1}{\ell^{s}}.

The sum over
\ell
equals to

\displaystyle\zeta(s)\prod_{p|k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right),

and the sum over
\delta
equals to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(s+r+1)}\prod_{p|k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}\right)^{-1}.

By rearranging the sum over
k
and invoking multiplicativity, we deduce that

(3.12)

\displaystyle D_{1}(s,r)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+r+1)}\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}\frac{1-p^{-{s}}}{1-p^{-(s+r+1)}}\right)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+r+1)}K_{r}(s).

Observe that as
\mathrm{Re}(s)\to\infty
, the factors
1-p^{-s}
and
1-p^{-(s+r+1)}
tend to 1 for any fixed integer
r\geq 1
. In contrast, the factor

{1}/{p^{r+1}(p-1)}
remains unchanged, as it is independent of
s
. Hence, we normalise the Euler product
K_{r}(s)
as

\displaystyle K_{r}(s)=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}\frac{1-p^{-{s}}}{1-p^{-(s+r+1)}}\right)}{1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{C(r)}\prod_{p}\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}(1+B_{p,r}(s))\right)}{1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}},

where
C(r)
is given in (
3.10
), and

\displaystyle B_{p,r}(s)=\frac{1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}}{1-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}}-1=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}+\frac{1}{p^{2(s+r+1)}}+\cdots\right)-1

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{p^{s}}-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s+r+1}}-\cdots-\frac{1}{p^{(k+1)s+r+1}}+\cdots\right).

In this context, it is worth noting that the decomposition argument employed herein was adopted from
[
1
, §3]
. Thus, the Euler product in (
3.12
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle K_{r}(s)=C(r)\prod_{p}(1+A_{p,r}(s)),

where

\displaystyle A_{p,r}(s)=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\left(\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}\left(1+B_{p,r}(s)\right)\right)}{1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}{1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}\left(\frac{1}{p^{s}}-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s+r+1}}-\frac{1}{p^{2s+2r+2}}+\cdots-\frac{1}{p^{(k+1)s+r+1}}+\cdots\right).

By (
3
), the above assertion follows, thereby concluding the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the constant
C(r)
defined in (
3.10
) may be regarded as the generalised totient summatory constant. In particular, when

, we obtain

\displaystyle C(1)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2}(p-1)}\right)=1.339784\ldots,

a result that can be deduced from the well-known Landau totient constant

\displaystyle A=\zeta(2)C(1)=\zeta(2)\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2}(p-1)}\right)=2.20386\ldots.

Let
s=\sigma+it
with
\sigma,t\in\mathbb{R}
. We have

\displaystyle\left|1-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}\right|\geq 1-\frac{1}{2^{\sigma+r+1}},\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\left|\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right|=\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}},

then

\displaystyle|B_{p,r}|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{p^{s}}-\frac{1}{p^{s+r+1}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s+r+1}}-\frac{1}{p^{2s+2r+2}}+\cdots-\frac{1}{p^{(k+1)s+r+1}}+\cdots\right|<

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}}{1-\frac{1}{p^{\sigma+r+1}}}.

Additionally, for a fixed integer
r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}\left({1+\frac{1}{p^{r+1}(p-1)}}\right)^{-1}<\frac{1}{p^{r+1}}.

so that

\displaystyle\sum_{p}|A_{p,r}(s)|<\frac{2^{\sigma+r+1}}{2^{\sigma+r+1}-1}\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma+r+1}}<\infty.

It follows that the Euler product
K_{r}(s)
converges absolutely (see, for instance,
[
31
, §1.42]
) and defines an analytic function on the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. Furthermore, for any fixed
\sigma_{0}>-r
,

\displaystyle|K_{r}(s)|\leq C(r)\prod_{p}\left(1-|A_{p,r}(s)|\right)\leq C(r)\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{2^{\sigma_{0}+r+1}}{(2^{\sigma_{0}+r+1}-1)p^{\sigma_{0}+r+1}}\right).

Hence,
K_{r}(s)
is uniformly bounded on the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\sigma_{0}
. Since it is analytic for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, the Dirichlet series
D_{1}(s,r)
admits a meromorphic continuation in this region. Moreover, because
\zeta(s)
, it has a simple pole at point
s=1
and
1/\zeta(s+r+1)
has no zeros on the half-plane where
\mathrm{Re}(s)+r>0
, it follows that
D_{1}(s,r)
extends analytically to the region
\mathrm{Re}(s)+r>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
except for a simple pole at
s=1
. Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, the function may be analytically continued to the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>-r-\frac{1}{2}
.

From Lemma
3.3
and the foregoing discussion, we conclude the following result.

Proposition 3.5

and

is uniformly bounded in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>-2r/3

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(2\sigma)}{\zeta(\sigma)}\leq|L(s,\chi^{*})|\leq\zeta(\sigma)

for
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\sigma>1
(see for instance

), we may bound the sum in (

\displaystyle|K_{r}(s,\chi^{*})|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\chi^{*}(\bmod{m^{*}})}\frac{L(s,\chi^{*})L(s+r+1,\chi^{*})}{L(s+r,\chi^{*})}A_{r}(s,\chi^{*})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi(m^{*})\frac{\zeta(\sigma)\zeta(\sigma+r)\zeta(\sigma+r+1)}{\zeta(2(\sigma+r))}\prod_{p|k}\frac{1}{2p^{3\sigma+2r+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\sigma+r+1}}\right)}.

Inserting this result, we obtain

\displaystyle|D_{2}(s,r)|\leq\frac{\zeta(\sigma)\zeta(\sigma+r)\zeta(\sigma+r+1)}{\zeta(2(\sigma+r))}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mu^{2}(k)}{k^{r+1}}\prod_{p|k}\frac{1}{2p^{3\sigma+2r+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\sigma+r+1}}\right)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{*}\geq 1\\
(m^{*},k)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{{m^{*}}^{r}}.

The sum over
m^{*}
equals to

\displaystyle\zeta(r)\prod_{p|k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{r}}\right).

Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle|D_{2}(s,r)|\leq\frac{\zeta(\sigma)\zeta(\sigma+r)\zeta(\sigma+r+1)\zeta(r)}{\zeta(2(\sigma+r))}\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1-p^{-r}}{p^{r+1}}\frac{1}{2p^{3\sigma+2r+1}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\sigma+r+1}})}\right),

and set

\displaystyle E_{r}(\sigma)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1-p^{-r}}{p^{r+1}}\frac{1}{2p^{3\sigma+2r+1}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\sigma+r+1}})}\right).

Furthermore, the Euler product can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle E_{r}(\sigma)=C^{\prime}(r)\prod_{p}\frac{\left(1+\frac{1-p^{-r}}{p^{r+1}}(1-E_{p,r}(\sigma))\right)}{1+\frac{1-p^{-r}}{p^{r+1}}},

where

is defined in (
3.18

\displaystyle E_{p,r}(\sigma)=1-\frac{1}{2p^{3\sigma+2r+1}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\sigma+r+1}})}.

From our analysis of

, it follows that
E_{p,r}(\sigma)
converges absolutely for
\sigma>-2r/3
, thereby completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

(4.12)

\displaystyle n=-F_{\gamma}(\tau,u)=

\displaystyle O\left(N(r)\mathrm{Li}_{r+2}(-u)\Gamma(r+2)\tau^{-(r+2)}\right)

whereas

\displaystyle B^{2}=F_{\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u)=

\displaystyle O\left(N(r)\mathrm{Li}_{r+2}(-u)\Gamma(r+3)\tau^{-(r+3)}\right).

For the third derivative appearing in the error term of (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\theta^{3}}F(\tau+i\theta,u)=-i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k^{3}\Delta_{f}(k)(1-u^{-1}e^{k(\tau+i\theta)})}{(1+u^{-1}e^{k(\tau+i\theta)})^{3}}.

The following estimation technique is adapted from
[
23
]
. Let
k_{0}=\tau^{-(1+c)}
for some
c>0
, and for simplicity of notation, set
v=u^{-1}
. We consider two cases: first, when
k
is large i.e.,
k>k_{0}
; and second, when
k\leq k_{0}
. Beginning with the large sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|-i\sum_{k>k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}\Delta_{f}(k)(1-ve^{k(\tau+i\theta)})}{(1+ve^{k(\tau+i\theta)})^{3}}\right|\leq\sum_{k>k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|(1+ve^{k\tau})}{|1+ve^{k(\tau+i\theta)}|^{3}}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k>k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|(1+ve^{k\tau})}{(ve^{k\tau}-1)^{3}}=O\left(\sum_{k>k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|}{e^{k\tau}}\right)=O\left(\tau^{-r-4}\right),

where we have applied the upper bound (see, for instance,
[
28
]
)

\displaystyle\sigma_{r}(n)<n^{r}(1+2^{-r}+3^{-r}+\cdots)<n^{r}\zeta(r).

For the remaining case, we observe that

\displaystyle\left|1+v^{k(\tau+i\theta)}\right|\geq(1+ve^{k\tau})\cos{\left(\frac{k\theta}{2}\right)}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|-i\sum_{k\leq k_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{k^{3}\Delta_{f}(k)(1-u^{-1}e^{k(\tau+i\theta)})}{(1+u^{-1}e^{k(\tau+i\theta)})^{3}}\right|\leq\sum_{k\leq k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|(1+ve^{k\tau})}{|1+ve^{k(\tau+i\theta)}|^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|(1+ve^{k\tau})}{(1+ve^{k(\tau+i\theta)})^{3}}\left(1+O((k\theta)^{2})\right)\leq\sum_{k\leq k_{0}}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|}{ve^{k\tau}}\left(1+O((k\theta)^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{3}|\Delta_{f}(k)|}{ve^{k\tau}}+O\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{5}|\Delta_{f}(k)|\theta^{2}}{e^{k\tau}}\right).

Applying the Mellin transform and converse mapping, we have

\displaystyle\left|-i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k^{3}\Delta_{f}(k)(1-u^{-1}e^{k(\tau+i\theta)})}{(1+u^{-1}e^{k(\tau+i\theta)})^{3}}\right|=O\left(\tau^{-r-4}+\tau^{-r-6}\theta^{2}\right).

Combining this with (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\theta^{3}}F(\tau+i\theta,u)=O(\tau^{-r-4}),

for
|\theta|\leq\tau^{1+3r/7}
. All these together leads to the expansion

\displaystyle F(\tau+i\theta,u)=F(\tau,u)+in\theta-\frac{B^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}+O(\tau^{-r-4}\theta^{3}).

Thus for the integral
\Omega_{1,f}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{e^{n\tau}}{2\pi}\int_{-\theta_{n}}^{\theta_{n}}\exp(in\theta+F(\tau+i\theta,u))d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{n\tau+F(\tau,u)}}{2\pi}\int_{-\theta_{n}}^{\theta_{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{B^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}+O(\tau^{2r/7-1})\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{n\tau+F(\tau,u)}}{2\pi}\left(\int_{-\theta_{n}}^{\theta_{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{B^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}\right)d\theta\right)\left(1+O(\tau^{2r/7-1})\right).

Finally, we transform the integral into a Gaussian integral and arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{-\theta_{n}}^{\theta_{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{B^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}\right)d\theta=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{B^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}\right)d\theta-2\int_{\theta_{n}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{B^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{B}+O\left(\int_{\theta_{n}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{B^{2}\tau^{1+3r/7}}{2}\theta\right)d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{B}+O\left(\tau^{-1-3r/7}B^{-2}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{-r/7-1}}{2}\right)\right),

completing the proof.
∎

4.2.
Estimate of
\Omega_{2,f}

We proceed to prove the following lemma, which establishes an upper bound for the integrand in (
4.2
) when
f(n)=\sigma_{r}(n)
for fixed
r\geq 2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a fixed
r\geq 2
with
f(n)=\sigma_{r}(n)
, the error term is bounded as follows

\displaystyle|\Omega_{2,\sigma_{r}}|=O\left(\exp\left(n\tau+F(\tau,u)-c_{r}\tau^{-r/7}\right)\right),

where
c_{r}
is a constant uniformly in
u
.

We first note the elementary inequality
\sigma_{r}(n)>n^{r}
for

and
r\geq 1
, which simplifies the analysis in this section. Additionally, we incorporate the following lemma by Li and Chen
[
21
, Lemma 2.5]
as a key element of the preceding argument.

Lemma 4.3

(Li-Chen,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 1
be fixed. Then for
\xi>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{k-1}e^{-n\xi}(1-\cos(ny))\geq\varrho\left(\frac{e^{-\xi}}{(1-e^{-\xi})^{k}}-\frac{e^{-\xi}}{\left|1-e^{-\xi-iy}\right|^{k}}\right),

where
\varrho
is a positive constant which depends only on
k
.

We are now ready to prove the following result, which will directly imply Lemma
4.2
. In this context, it is noteworthy that, owing to the inequality
\sigma_{r}(n)>n^{r}
, for
n\geq 2
and
r\geq 1
, the following lemma bears resemblance to Lemma 7 in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For a fixed
r\geq 2
and any real
y
satisfying
\theta_{0}\leq|y|\leq\pi
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|Q_{\sigma_{r}}(e^{\tau+iy},u)|}{Q_{\sigma_{r}}(e^{-\tau,u})}\leq\left(\exp\left(-\frac{2u(r-1)}{(1+u)^{2}}\varrho\left(\frac{1}{4}\tau^{-{r}/{7}}\right)\right)\right),

where
\varrho
is a constant depends on
r
.

Proof.

Utilising the inequality
\sigma_{r}(n)>n^{r}
for
n\geq 2
and
r\geq 2
, we can simplify

\displaystyle|\Delta_{f}(n)|=|\sigma_{r}(n+1)-\sigma_{r}(n)|\geq c((n+1)^{r}-n^{r})=c\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\binom{r}{i}n^{i},

for some constant
c\geq 0
. However, for
c=0
in the above inequality, the following argument becomes trivial. So, we only consider the case where
c>0
.

By the mean value theorem, there exists a
\xi\in(n,n+1)
such that
(n+1)^{r}-n^{r}=r\xi^{r-1}
. This implies (see
[
21
, Lemma 2.6]
)

(4.14)

\displaystyle|\Delta_{f}(n)|\geq r\xi^{r-1}-1=k\xi^{k}+\xi^{k}-1=(r-1)\xi^{r-1}+\xi^{r-1}-1>(r-1)n^{r-1}.

Next, we express

\displaystyle\left|1+ue^{-n(\tau+iy)}\right|^{2}=(1+ue^{-n\tau-niy})(1-ue^{-n\tau+niy})=(1+ue^{n\tau})^{2}-2ue^{-n\tau}(1-\cos(ny)).

Using the above identities, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|Q_{\sigma_{k}}(e^{\tau+iy},u)|}{Q_{\sigma_{k}}(e^{-\tau,u})}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{2ue^{-n\tau}(1-\cos(ny))}{(1+ue^{-n\tau})^{2}}\right)^{\Delta_{f}(n)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Delta_{f}(n)|\log\left|1-\frac{2ue^{-n\tau}(1-\cos(ny))}{(1+ue^{-n\tau})^{2}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Delta_{f}(n)|\left|\frac{2ue^{-n\tau}(1-\cos(ny))}{(1+ue^{-n\tau})^{2}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{2u}{(1+u)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Delta_{f}(n)|e^{-n\tau}(1-\cos(ny))\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{2u(r-1)}{(1+u)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{r-1}e^{-n\tau}(1-\cos(ny))\right).

Finally, applying Lemma
4.3
, yields

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|Q_{\sigma_{k}}(e^{\tau+iy},u)|}{Q_{\sigma_{k}}(e^{-\tau,u})}\right)^{2}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2u(r-1)}{(1+u)^{2}}\varrho\left(\frac{e^{-\tau}}{(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}}-\frac{e^{-\tau}}{\left|1-e^{-\tau-iy}\right|^{r}}\right)\right).

Beginning with a few standard trigonometric identities from
[
21
]
, we express

\displaystyle\left|1-e^{-\tau-i\theta}\right|^{r}=

\displaystyle((1-e^{-\tau})^{2}+2e^{-\tau}(1-\cos\theta))^{\tfrac{r}{2}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}\left(1+\frac{2e^{-\tau}}{(1-e^{-\tau})^{2}}(1-\cos\theta_{n})\right)^{\tfrac{r}{2}},

where
\theta_{n}\leq|\theta|\leq\pi
. By employing the Taylor series expansion of
\cos{\theta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\cos\theta_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\theta_{n}^{2}+O(\theta_{n}^{4})=\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2+{6r}/{7}}+O(\theta_{n}^{4+12r/7}),

and similarly,
e^{-\tau}=1-\tau+O(\tau^{2})
. Consequently, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{-\tau}}{(1-e^{-\tau})^{2}}(1-\cos\theta_{n})=\frac{2(1+O(\tau))(\tfrac{1}{2}\tau^{2+{6r}/{7}}+O(\theta_{n}^{4+12r/7}))}{\tau^{2}(1+O(\tau))}=\tau^{{6r}/{7}}(1+O(\tau)).

Substituting the above expression into (
4.2
) gives

\displaystyle\left|1-e^{-\tau-i\theta}\right|^{r}\geq(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}\left(1+\tau^{{6r}/{7}}(1+O(\tau))\right)^{\tfrac{r}{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}\left(1+\frac{r\tau^{{6r}/{7}}}{2}+O\left(\tau^{{6r}/{7}+1}+\tau^{{12r}/{7}}\right)\right).

Therefore, the inner sum of (
4.15
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\tau}}{(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}}-\frac{e^{-\tau}}{\left|1-e^{-\tau-iy}\right|^{r}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{-\tau}}{(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\frac{r\tau^{{6r}/{7}}}{2}+O\left(\tau^{{6r}/{7}+1}+\tau^{{12r}/{7}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\tau}}{(1-e^{-\tau})^{r}}\left(\frac{r\tau^{{6r}/{7}}}{2}+O\left(\tau^{{6r}/{7}+1}+\tau^{{12r}/{7}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-O(\tau)}{\tau^{r}(1-O(\tau))}\left(\frac{r\tau^{{6r}/{7}}}{2}+O\left(\tau^{{6r}/{7}+1}+\tau^{{12r}/{7}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r\tau^{-{r}/{7}}}{2}+O\left(\tau^{1-{r}/{7}}+\tau^{12r/7}\right)\gg\frac{1}{4}\tau^{-\tfrac{r}{7}}.

Substituting this bound into (
4.15
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.2

We adopt the method outlined in

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}(\tau(n,1),1)}{B^{2}(\tau(n,e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}}),e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}})}}\exp\Bigg(-\frac{\mu_{f,n}\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}+n\tau(n,e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}})

(5.1)

n

u

\displaystyle\tau_{u}=\tau_{u}(n,u)=-\frac{{F}_{u\gamma}(\tau,u)}{{F}_{\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u)}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k\Delta_{f}(k)e^{k\tau}}{(e^{k\tau+u})^{2}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{uk^{2}\Delta_{f}(k)e^{kr}}{(e^{kr}+u)^{2}}},

\displaystyle+2{F}_{u\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u){F}_{u\gamma}(\tau,u){F}_{\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u)

\displaystyle+\big({F}_{\gamma\gamma\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u){F}_{u\gamma}(\tau,u)^{3}+9{F}_{u\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u){F}_{\gamma\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u){F}_{u\gamma}(\tau,u)^{2}\big){F}_{\gamma\gamma}(\tau,u)

\displaystyle\tau(n,e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}})-\tau(n,1)=\tau_{u}(n,1)\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}+\frac{\tau_{u}(n,1)+\tau_{uu}(n,1)}{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)^{2}+O\left(n^{-1/(r+2)}\frac{|\theta|^{3}}{\nu^{3}_{f,n}}\right)

\displaystyle F(\tau(e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}},n),e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}})-F((\tau,1),1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(\tau(n,1),1)\tau_{u}(n,1)+F_{u}(\tau(n,1),1)\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(F_{\gamma}(\tau(n,1),1)(\tau_{u}(n,1)+\tau_{uu}(n,1))

\displaystyle+F_{\gamma\gamma}(\tau(n,1),1)\tau_{u}(n,1)^{2}+2F_{u\gamma}(\tau(n,1),1)\tau_{u}(n,1)+F_{u}(\tau(n,1),1)

\displaystyle+F_{uu}(\tau(n,1),1)\bigg)\left(\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)^{2}+O\left(n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}\frac{|\theta|^{3}}{\nu^{3}_{f,n}}\right),

respectively. Substituting these expansions into the exponential in (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(n\tau_{u}(n,1)+F_{\gamma}(\eta,1)\tau_{u}(n,1)+F_{u}(\eta,1)-\mu_{f,n}\right)\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(n(\tau_{u}(n,1)+\tau_{uu}(n,1))+F_{\gamma}(\eta,1)(\tau_{u}(n,1)

\displaystyle+\tau_{uu}(n,1))+F_{\gamma\gamma}(\eta,1)\tau_{u}(n,1)^{2}+2F_{u\gamma}(\eta,1)\tau_{u}(n,1)+F_{u}(\eta,1)+F_{uu}(\eta,1)\bigg)\left(\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+O\left(n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}\frac{|\theta^{3}|}{\nu^{3}_{f,n}}+n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)}\right).

Here, we have written
\eta=\tau(n,1)
for brevity. Recalling that
n=-F_{\gamma}(\eta,1)
and
\tau_{u}(n,1)=-\dfrac{F_{u\gamma}(\eta,1)}{F_{\gamma\gamma}(\eta,1)}
, we may simplify the final expression to derive

\displaystyle(F_{u}(\eta,1)-\mu_{f,n})\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(F_{u}(\eta,1)+F_{uu}(\eta,1)-\frac{F_{u\gamma}(\eta,1)^{2}}{F_{\gamma\gamma}(\eta,1)}\bigg)\left(\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+O\left(n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}\frac{|\theta^{3}|}{\nu^{3}_{f,n}}+n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)}\right).

In a similar manner, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{B^{2}(\tau(n,1),1)}{B^{2}(\tau(n,e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}}),e^{\theta/\nu_{f,n}})}=1+O\left(\frac{|\theta|}{\nu_{f,n}}\right).

Thus, we derive the following asymptotic expression for the moment generating function in (
5
):

\displaystyle M_{f,n}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg((F_{u}(\eta,1)-\mu_{f,n})\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(F_{u}(\eta,1)+F_{uu}(\eta,1)-\frac{F_{u\gamma}(\eta,1)^{2}}{F_{\gamma\gamma}(\eta,1)}\bigg)\left(\frac{\theta}{\nu_{f,n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+O\left(n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}\frac{|\theta^{3}|}{\nu^{3}_{f,n}}+n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)}\right)\Bigg).

Recall that we choose
\mu_{f,n}
and
\nu_{f,n}
in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) such that

\displaystyle\mu_{f,n}=F_{u}(\eta,1)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)}{e^{\eta k}+1}\quad\text{and}\quad

(5.2)

\displaystyle\nu^{2}_{f,n}=F_{u}(\eta,1)+F_{uu}(\eta,1)-\frac{F_{u\gamma}(\eta,1)^{2}}{F_{\gamma\gamma}(\eta,1)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)e^{\eta k}}{(e^{\eta k}+1)^{2}}-\frac{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)ke^{\eta k}}{(e^{\eta k}+1)^{2}}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{f}(k)k^{2}e^{\eta k}}{(e^{\eta k}+1)^{2}}}.

From the definitions of
\mu_{f,n}
and
\nu^{2}_{f,n}
in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) respectively, we deduce that

\displaystyle M_{f,n}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}+O\left(n^{-(r+1)/(r+2)}|\theta|+n^{-(r+1)/(r+2)}|\theta|^{3}+n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}+O\left(n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)}\right)\right),

for bounded
\theta
.
∎

5.1.
Formulae for the mean and variance

By applying Lemma
5.1
and Curtiss’ theorem
[
7
]
, we establish that the distribution of
{\varpi}_{f,n}
is indeed asymptotically normal. In this section, we derive an upper bound for its mean and variance, providing an explicit formula for the constant as a function of
r
.

From (
5
) we write

\displaystyle\mu_{f,n}=F_{u}(\eta,1)=O\left(N(r)\mathrm{Li}_{r+1}(-1)\Gamma(r+1)/\eta^{(r+1)}\right)

and

\displaystyle\nu^{2}_{f,n}=

\displaystyle F_{u}(\eta,1)+F_{uu}(\eta,1)-\frac{F_{u\gamma}(\eta,1)^{2}}{F_{\gamma\gamma}(\eta,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\left(N(r)\mathrm{Li}_{r}(-1)\Gamma(r+1)-N(r)\frac{\mathrm{Li}^{2}_{r}(-1)\Gamma^{2}(r+2)}{\mathrm{Li}_{r+1}(-1)\Gamma(r+3)}\right)\frac{1}{\eta^{(r+1)}}\right).

Utilising the saddle-point solution in (
4.12
), we write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}=\frac{1}{\tau(n,1)}=O\left(\frac{n^{\frac{1}{r+2}}}{{N(r)\mathrm{Li}_{r+2}(-1)\Gamma(r+2)}}\right)

then

\displaystyle\mu_{f,n}=O\left(C_{\mu}(r)n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\nu^{2}_{f,n}=O\left(C_{\nu}(r)n^{\frac{r+1}{r+2}}\right),

where

(5.3)

\displaystyle C_{\mu}(r)\sim-N(r){\mathrm{Li}_{r+1}(-1)\Gamma(r+1)}=N(r)(1-2^{r+1})\Gamma(r+1)\zeta(r+1)

and

\displaystyle C_{\sigma}(r)\sim

\displaystyle-N(r)\left({\mathrm{Li}_{r}(-1)\Gamma(r+1)-\frac{\mathrm{Li}^{2}_{r}(-1)\Gamma^{2}(r+2)}{\mathrm{Li}_{r+1}(-1)\Gamma(r+3)}}\right)

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(r)\zeta(r)(1-2^{-r})\left(\Gamma(r+1)-\frac{\Gamma(r+2)\zeta(r)}{\Gamma(r+3)\zeta(r+1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{r+1}-1}\right)\right),

N(r)
is given in (
4.11
) and

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Li}_{r}(-1)=(1-2^{-r})\zeta(r+1).

In this context, it is worth noting that the key constant of interest is
N(r)
, which emerges from a detailed analysis of the Dirichlet series with the gap function
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\Delta;s)
.

5.1.1.
The distribution of the tail

We conclude the proof by bounding the tail of the distribution, following the approach of Lipnik et al.
[
22
]
, which in turn was adapted from Hwang
[
16
]
. For
\theta=o(n^{\frac{r+1}{6(r+2)}})
, we apply the Chernoff bound (see, for instance,
[
6
]
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n}}{\sigma_{f,n}}\geq x\right)\leq e^{-\theta x}M_{f,n}(\theta)=e^{-t\theta+\theta^{2}/2}\left(1+O\left(n^{\frac{r+1}{2(r+2)}}(|\theta|+|\theta|^{3})+n^{-2r/7(r+2)+1/(r+2)}\right)\right).

Setting
T=n^{\frac{r+1}{6(r+2)}}/\log n
we have two cases. For the first case, let
x\leq T
, and we set
\theta=x
, yielding

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n}}{\sigma_{f,n}}\geq x\right)\leq e^{-x^{2}/2}\left(1+O((\log n)^{-3})\right).

Similarly, for
x\geq T
, we set
\theta=T
, obtaining

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n}}{\sigma_{f,n}}\geq x\right)\leq e^{-Tx/2}\left(1+O((\log n)^{-3})\right).

A similar argument can be applied to estimate the probability
\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\varpi_{f,n}-\mu_{f,n}}{\sigma_{f,n}}\leq-x\right)
.

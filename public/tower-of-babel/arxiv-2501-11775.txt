Permutation polynomials, projective polynomials, and bijections between μ q n − 1 q − 1 \mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}} and PG ​ ( n − 1 , q ) \text{PG}(n-1,q)
Tong Lin, Qiang Wang

Abstract
Using arbitrary bases for the finite field 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}} over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , we obtain the generalized Möbius transformations (GMTs), which are a class of bijections between the projective geometry PG ​ ( n − 1 , q ) \text{PG}(n-1,q) and the set of roots of unity μ q n − 1 q − 1 ⊆ 𝔽 q n \mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}} , where n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 is any integer. We also introduce a class of projective polynomials, using the properties of which we determine the inverses of the GMTs. Moreover, we study the roots of those projective polynomials, which lead to a three-way correspondence between partitions of 𝔽 q n ∗ , μ q n − 1 q − 1 \mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast},\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}} and PG ​ ( n − 1 , q ) \text{PG}(n-1,q) . Through this correspondence and the GMTs, we construct permutation polynomials of index q n − 1 q − 1 \frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1} over 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}} .

.

Let

g_{0},g_{1}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be such that
(g_{0},g_{1})\bigl(\vec{0}\bigr)=\vec{0}
, and for each
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\setminus\bigl\{\vec{0}\bigr\}
,

(g_{0},g_{1})(x_{0},x_{1})=\begin{cases}\Bigl(H\Bigl(\frac{x_{0}}{x_{1}}\Bigr),1\Bigr)&\text{if }x_{1}\neq 0;\\
(1,0)&\text{if }x_{1}=0.\end{cases}

Then the corresponding mapping
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:g_{1})
over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
is given by

\begin{cases}\overline{g}(x_{0}:1)=(H(x_{0}):1)&\text{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q};\\
\overline{g}(1:0)=(1:0).\end{cases}

Since
\overline{g}
permutes
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
, plugging
g_{0},g_{1}
in
Theorem

III
leads to the following class of permutation polynomials of index
q+1
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
.

Theorem IV
.

Let
r,d\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
r\geq d
and
\gcd(r,q-1)=1
. Let
H
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
of degree
d
which permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}^{\ast}
and
u,v\in\mu_{q+1}
be such that
u\neq v
and
\bigl(\frac{u}{v}\bigr)^{d}\neq\bigl(\frac{a}{b}\bigr)^{q-1}
. Let
w\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}^{\ast}
be such that
w^{q-1}=\frac{u}{v}
. Then
F
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
, where

F(x)=\begin{cases}aw^{r-d}(v-u)^{r}x^{r}&\text{if }x^{q}-vx=0;\\
\bigl(x^{q}-vx\bigr)^{r}\left(aw^{-d}H\left(w\left(\frac{x^{q}-ux}{x^{q}-vx}\right)\right)+b\right)&\text{if }x^{q}-vx\neq 0.\end{cases}

In particular, if
\gcd(d,q-1)=1
and
H(x)=x^{d}
, then
\widetilde{F}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
, where

\widetilde{F}(x)=\begin{cases}aw^{r-d}(v-u)^{r}x^{r}&\text{if }x^{q}-vx=0;\\
a\bigl(x^{q}-vx\bigr)^{r-d}\bigl(x^{q}-ux\bigr)^{d}+b\bigl(x^{q}-vx\bigr)^{r}&\text{if }x^{q}-vx\neq 0.\end{cases}

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we generalize the Möbius transformations and prove
Theorem

I
. In
Section

3
, we prove
Theorem

II
and
Theorem

III
. Besides, we verify
Theorem

IV
and give more examples of homogeneous permutation polynomials of index
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. In
Section

4
, we compare the generalized Möbius transformations to a known class of bijections from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to
\bigl\{\alpha^{i}:0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1\bigr\}
for subprimitive roots
\alpha
in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}

[
18
]
. In
Section

5
, we give concluding remarks.

2
The GMTs and their inverses

Möbius transformations, i.e., invertible rational mappings of degree
1
, have been widely used and extensively studied in complex analysis. In the theories of fields and projective spaces, a Möbius transformation
P(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}
, where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
,
c\neq 0
and
ad-bc\neq 0
, can be viewed as a mapping from
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}\sqcup\{\infty\}
to itself such that
P\bigl(-\frac{d}{c}\bigr)=\infty
and
P(\infty)=\frac{a}{c}
. If
n=2
, we recall from
Section

1
that when applying the AGW-criterion over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
, we usually employ those Möbius transformations from
\text{PG}(1,q)\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}\sqcup\{\infty\}
to
\mu_{q+1}
which are constructed as follows. Let
u\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and define
\eta:\text{PG}(1,q)\to\mu_{q+1}
, where

\eta(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x=\infty;\\
\frac{x+u^{q}}{x+u}&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{cases}

Then
\eta
is well-defined since
x+u\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
due to the assumption that
u\not\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It is also easy to see that
\eta
is invertible and

\eta^{-1}(x)=\begin{cases}\infty&\text{if }x=1;\\
\frac{-ux+u^{q}}{x-1}&\text{if }x\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}.\end{cases}

If, instead of identifying
\text{PG}(1,q)
with
\mathbb{F}_{q}\sqcup\{\infty\}
, we write it as
\{(x_{0}:1):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}\sqcup\{(1:0)\}
according to its original definition, and view
(1:0)
as
\infty
, then for all
(x_{0}:x_{1})\in\text{PG}(1,q)
,

\eta(x_{0}:x_{1})=(x_{0}+x_{1}u)^{q-1}=\frac{x_{0}+x_{1}u^{q}}{x_{0}+x_{1}u}.

(5)

We also have that

\eta^{-1}(x)=\begin{cases}(1:0)&\text{if }x=1;\\
\bigl(\frac{-ux+u^{q}}{x-1}:1\bigr)&\text{if }x\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\{1\}.\end{cases}

(6)

These observations prompt us to introduce the notion of generalized Möbius transformations (or GMTs for brevity), which are a class of bijections from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
be a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and define
\psi_{W}:\text{PG}(n-1,q)\to\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
, where for each
(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})\in\text{PG}(n-1,q)
,

\psi_{W}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})=\Biggl(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}\omega_{j}\Biggr)^{q-1}=\frac{\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}\omega_{j}^{q}}}{\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}\omega_{j}}}.

(7)

We call
\psi_{W}
the generalized Möbius transformation (GMT) associated with
W
.

A GMT
\psi_{W}
is always well-defined. Indeed,
\psi_{W}(\text{PG}(n-1,q))\subseteq\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
since

\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}=\left\{x^{q-1}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}\right\}.

(8)

Also,
\psi_{W}(\lambda x_{0}:\dots:\lambda x_{n-1})=\psi_{W}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})
for each
(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})\in\text{PG}(n-1,q)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
because
\lambda^{q-1}=1
. Moreover, the denominator of the fractional form of
\psi_{W}
in
Eq.

7
is always non-zero since for each
(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})\in\text{PG}(n-1,q)
, at least one of
x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}
is non-zero, meaning that
x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{n-1}\omega_{n-1}\neq 0
due to the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independence of
W
. The following theorem shows that each GMT is a bijection.

Theorem 2.2
.

For each basis
W
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the mapping
\psi_{W}
given by
Eq.

7
is a bijection from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
.

Proof
Since
W
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
Eqs.

7
and
8
indicate that
\psi_{W}
is a surjection. Since
\left|\text{PG}(n-1,q)\right|=\Bigl|\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}\Bigr|
,
\psi_{W}
is a bijection.

\square

When
n=2
, and
W=\{1,u\}
, where
u\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the GMT
\psi_{W}
defined by
Eq.

7
agrees with the usual Möbius transformation defined by
Eq.

5
. When
n=3
and
W=\left\{\omega,\omega^{q},\omega^{q^{2}}\right\}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{3}}^{\ast}
,

\psi_{W}(x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2})=\frac{x_{0}\omega^{q}+x_{1}\omega^{q^{2}}+x_{2}\omega}{x_{0}\omega+x_{1}\omega^{q}+x_{2}\omega^{q^{2}}},

which agrees with the mapping in
Eq.

2
, which was introduced in
[
36
]
.

In what follows, we determine, for each
n\geq 2
and each basis
W
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the inverse of the GMT
\psi_{W}
defined by
Eq.

7
using
W
, thereby explaining the known inverse formulas
Eq.

6
(when
n=2
) and
Eqs.

3
and
4
(when
n=3
). To achieve these requires a lemma about the dual bases.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
41
, Theorem 8.7.]
)
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. Let
W=\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\}
be a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
B=\{\beta_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\}
be the dual basis of
W
. Then

x=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}\left(\beta_{j}x\right)\omega_{j}.

(9)

To utilize
Eq.

9
, we need to be able to find the dual basis
B
of a given basis
W
. In general, this can be done by employing
Moore matrices
. For an
n
-element subset
S=\left\{s_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
, the Moore matrix associated with
S
is

M_{S}=\begin{bmatrix}s_{0}&s_{0}^{q}&\dots&s_{0}^{q^{n-1}}\\
s_{1}&s_{1}^{q}&\dots&s_{1}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
s_{n-1}&s_{n-1}^{q}&\dots&s_{n-1}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

As a side note, Moore matrices find their applications in engineering and coding theory, especially when it comes to MRD codes. See for example
[
6
,
10
,
11
,
21
,
24
,
33
]
. If
B
and
W
are the dual bases of each other and
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix, then

M_{W}M_{B}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}&\omega_{0}^{q}&\dots&\omega_{0}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\omega_{1}&\omega_{1}^{q}&\dots&\omega_{1}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\omega_{n-1}&\omega_{n-1}^{q}&\dots&\omega_{n-1}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{0}&\beta_{1}&\dots&\beta_{n-1}\\
\beta_{0}^{q}&\beta_{1}^{q}&\dots&\beta_{n-1}^{q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{0}^{q^{n-1}}&\beta_{1}^{q^{n-1}}&\dots&\beta_{n-1}^{q^{n-1}}\\
\end{bmatrix}=I_{n}.

In particular,
M_{W}
is non-singular. Moreover, for
0\leq i\leq n-1
,

\beta_{i}=\det(M_{W})^{-1}\text{cof}_{M_{W}}(i,0),

(10)

where
\text{cof}_{M_{W}}(i,0)
denotes the
(i,0)
-cofactor of
M_{W}
. The determinant of a Moore matrix has the following properties, which sometimes make determining dual bases via
Eq.

10
less computationally expensive.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
6
,
14
]
)
.

For any subset
S=\left\{s_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
,

\det(M_{S})=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\prod_{x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}x_{i}s_{i}\right)+s_{j}\right).

In particular,
M_{S}
is non-singular if and only if
S
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then

\det(M_{W})^{q-1}=(-1)^{n-1}.

(11)

In particular, if
n
is odd, then
\det(M_{W})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
.

Proof
Since
W
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n-1}\left\{\lambda(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{j-1}\omega_{j-1}+\omega_{j}):\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}\text{ and }x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

Hence, every element in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
is of the form
\lambda(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{j-1}\omega_{j-1}+\omega_{j})
for some unique
0\leq j\leq n-1,\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
and
x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since the product of all non-zero elements in a finite field is
-1
,
Proposition

2.4
implies that

\displaystyle\det(M_{W})^{q-1}

\displaystyle=1^{n}\det(M_{W})^{q-1}

\displaystyle=\biggl(-\prod_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\lambda\biggr)^{n}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\prod_{x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}x_{i}\omega_{i}\right)+\omega_{j}\right)^{q-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\prod_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\prod_{x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\lambda\left(\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}x_{i}\omega_{i}\right)+\omega_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\prod_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}}a

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}.

Thus, if
n
is odd, then
\det(M_{W})^{q-1}=1
, meaning that
\det(M_{W})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
.

\square

Let
\psi_{W}
be the GMT associated with a basis
W
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In view of the above results, we now give an inverse formula for
\psi_{W}
which do not require us to actually find the dual basis
B
of
W
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
W=\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\}
be a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
0\leq i\leq n-1
, let
c_{i}
be the
(i,0)
-cofactor of
M_{W}
, the Moore matrix associated with
W
. Then define

T_{W,i}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k(n-1)}c_{i}^{q^{k}}x^{\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}}.

(12)

If
B=\{\beta_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\}
is the dual basis of
W
and
M_{W}
is the Moore matrix associated with
W
, then for each
y\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
,

\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}y)=\det(M_{W})^{-1}yT_{W,i}\left(y^{q-1}\right).

(13)

In particular,
T_{W,i}\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)
have no common roots in
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
, and for each
x\in\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
,

\psi_{W}^{-1}(x)=\begin{cases}(1:0:\dots:0)&\text{ if }x=\omega_{0}^{q-1};\\
\displaystyle{\biggl(\frac{T_{W,0}(x)}{T_{W,1}(x)}:1:0:\dots:0\biggr)}&\text{ if }x=(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\omega_{1})^{q-1}\text{ for some }x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q};\\
\vdots&\ \vdots\\
\displaystyle{\biggl(\frac{T_{W,0}(x)}{T_{W,n-1}(x)}:\dots:\frac{T_{W,n-2}(x)}{T_{W,n-1}(x)}:1\biggr)}&\begin{array}[]{l}\text{if }x=(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{n-2}\omega_{n-2}+\omega_{n-1})^{q-1}\\
\text{for some }x_{0},\dots,x_{n-2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.\end{array}\end{cases}

(14)

Proof
First, we verify
Eq.

13
, using which we show that
T_{W,i}\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)
have no common roots in
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
. For each
y\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}y)\

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq010})}}}{{=}}\ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\det(M_{W})^{-1}\text{cof}_{M_{W}}(i,0)\right)^{q^{k}}y^{q^{k}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq011})}}}{{=}}\ \det(M_{W})^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k(n-1)}c_{i}^{q^{k}}y^{q^{k}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq012})}}}{{=}}\ \det(M_{W})^{-1}yT_{W,i}\left(y^{q-1}\right).

Hence,
Eq.

13
holds. By
Eq.

9
,
\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}y)\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)
have no common roots in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
. Therefore,
T_{W,i}\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)
have no common roots in
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
.

Next, we prove
Eq.

14
. Assume that
y\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
and
x=y^{q-1}
. Let
0\leq j\leq n-1
. Owing to
Eqs.

9
and
13
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

j=\max\left\{0\leq i\leq n-1:T_{W,i}(x)\neq 0\right\}
;

(2)

j=\max\left\{0\leq i\leq n-1:\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}y)\neq 0\right\}
;

(3)

y=\lambda(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{j-1}\omega_{j-1}+\omega_{j})
for some
x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
;

(4)

x=(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{j-1}\omega_{j-1}+\omega_{j})^{q-1}
for some
x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Hence, if
j=\max\left\{0\leq i\leq n-1:\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}y)\neq 0\right\}
, then

\displaystyle\psi_{W}^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\psi_{W}^{-1}\left(y^{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq009})}}}{{=}}\left(\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{0}y):\dots:\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{n-1}y)\right)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(\frac{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{0}y)}{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}y)}:\dots:\frac{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j-1}y)}{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}y)}:1:0:\dots:0\Biggr)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq013})}}}{{=}}\biggl(\frac{T_{W,0}(x)}{T_{W,j}(x)}:\dots:\frac{T_{W,j-1}(x)}{T_{W,j}(x)}:1:0:\dots:0\biggr),

which proves
Eq.

14
.

\square

For each
T_{W,i}
, all coefficients can be determined by
c_{i}=T_{W,i}(0)
. Since
c_{i}
is the
(i,0)
-cofactor of
M_{W}
, we obtain from the Leibniz formula for determinants
[
5
]
that

c_{i}=(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{k=0}^{i-1}\omega_{k}^{\sigma(k)}\prod_{k=i}^{n-2}\omega_{k+1}^{\sigma(k)}\right)^{q},

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group consisting of the
n!
permutations of
\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
.

When
n=2
and
W=\{1,u\}
, where
u\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it is easy to check that
T_{W,0}(x)=-ux+u^{q}
and
T_{W,1}(x)=x-1
. Thus, the inverse formula
Eq.

6
for a usual Möbius transformation is a special case of
Eq.

14
.

When
W
is
normal
, the process of finding
\psi_{W}^{-1}
via
Theorem

2.6
can be further simplified. Indeed, we recall that a basis
W
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is normal when
W=\bigl\{\omega^{q^{j}}:0\leq j\leq n-1\bigr\}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
. In that case,
[
15
, Theorem 2.2.7.]
shows that the dual basis
B
of
W
is also normal so that
B=\left\{\beta^{q^{i}}:0\leq i\leq n-1\right\}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
. Hence, from
Eqs.

10
and
11
, we deduce that for
0\leq i\leq n-1
,

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle=\text{cof}_{M_{W}}(i,0)

\displaystyle=\det(M_{W})\beta^{q^{i}}

\displaystyle=\det(M_{W})\left(\det(M_{W})^{-1}\text{cof}_{M_{W}}(0,0)\right)^{q^{i}}

\displaystyle=\det(M_{W})^{1-q^{i}}\text{cof}_{M_{W}}(0,0)^{q^{i}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(n-1)i}c_{0}^{q^{i}}.

So finding
\psi_{W}^{-1}
boils down to determining
c_{0}
, which is much less computationally expensive than computing all of
c_{i}\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)
. For example, let
n=3
and
W=\left\{\omega,\omega^{q},\omega^{q^{2}}\right\}
a normal basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{3}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A direct calculation shows that
\det(M_{W})=3\text{N}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\omega)-\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}\left(\omega^{3}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
, and the dual basis of
W
is
B=\left\{\beta,\beta^{q},\beta^{q^{2}}\right\}
, where
\beta=\det(M_{W})^{-1}\bigl(\omega^{q^{2}+q}-\omega^{2}\bigr)
. Hence, letting
\alpha=\omega^{q+1}-\omega^{2q^{2}}
yields that
T_{W,i}(x)=\alpha^{q^{i+1}}+\alpha^{q^{i+2}}x+\alpha^{q^{i+3}}x^{q+1}\ (i=0,1,2)
, which agree with the polynomials given by
Eq.

3
. In this case,
\psi_{W}^{-1}
, which is given by
Eq.

4
, is a special case of
Eq.

14
.

We also remark that if
W
is a
polynomial basis
, i.e.,
W=\bigl\{\omega^{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\bigr\}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}

So far, we have generalized the Möbius transformations and determined the inverse of the GMTs. Our strategy for involving the GMTs in constructing permutation polynomials of the form

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\displaystyle=\biggl\{\biggl(\frac{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{0}x)}{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)}:\dots:\frac{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j-1}x)}{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)}:1:0:\dots:0\biggr):x\in S_{W,j}\biggr\}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n-1}S_{W,j};\quad\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n-1}Z_{W,j};\quad\text{PG}(n-1,q)=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n-1}\overline{C_{q,n,j}}.

Z_{W,j}=\psi_{W}\bigl(\overline{C_{q,n,j}}\bigr)=S_{W,j}^{q-1}.

(15)

Proof

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
W
and
Y=\left\{\gamma_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
be any two bases for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
B=\left\{\beta_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
be the dual basis of
W
. Let
\widetilde{g}=(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
be any vector-valued polynomial such that
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:\dots:g_{n-1})
is a bijection over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be coprime to
q-1
. For
0\leq j\leq n-1
and each
x\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
such that

j=\max\bigl\{0\leq i\leq n-1:\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}x)\neq 0\bigr\},

we define

\displaystyle f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{k}\Biggl(\frac{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{0}x)}{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)},\dots,\frac{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j-1}x)}{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)},1,0,\dots,0\Biggr)\gamma_{k}.

(20)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)^{r}\widetilde{g}\Biggl(\frac{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{0}x)}{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)},\dots,\frac{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j-1}x)}{\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)},1,0,\dots,0\Biggr)\cdot(\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{n-1}),

where
\cdot
denotes the dot product of two vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Define
f:\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
, where

f(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }x=0;\\
f_{j}(x)&\text{if }j=\max\bigl\{0\leq i\leq n-1:\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}x)\neq 0\bigr\}\quad(0\leq j\leq n-1),\end{cases}

(21)

Then
f
is an
r
-homogeneous rational mapping which permutes
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. In particular, if
r\geq\max\{\deg(g_{k}):0\leq k\leq n-1\}
, then
f
is an
r
-homogeneous permutation polynomial of index
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
.

Proof
It follows trivially from
Eq.

20
and the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearity of
\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}
that each
f_{j}
is
r
-homogeneous. Hence,
f
is
r
-homogeneous due to
Eq.

21
. Now we show that
f
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. Let
T_{W,i}\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)
be as defined by
Eq.

12
. Define
\phi_{W}:\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\bigl\{\vec{0}\bigr\}
, where for
0\leq j\leq n-1
and each
x\in Z_{W,j}
(which is defined in
Theorem

3.1
),

\phi_{W}(x)=\biggl(\frac{T_{W,0}(x)}{T_{W,j}(x)},\dots,\frac{T_{W,j-1}(x)}{T_{W,j}(x)},1,0,\dots,0\biggr).

(22)

Let
M_{W}
be the Moore matrix associated with
W
. For
0\leq j\leq n-1
, let

h_{j}(x)=\det(M_{W})^{-r}T_{W,j}(x)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{k}\left(\phi_{W}(x)\right)\gamma_{k}.

(23)

Let
\widetilde{h}\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}[x]
be an arbitrary mapping, and define

h(x)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{h}(x)&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}\setminus\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}};\\
h_{j}(x)&\text{if }x\in Z_{W,j}\quad(0\leq j\leq n-1).\end{cases}

(24)

x^{r}h(x)^{q-1}
permutes
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}

defined by

20
and
21

x^{r}h\left(x^{q-1}\right)

\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}

x^{r}h(x)^{q-1}

\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}

Eq.

17

x\in\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}

3.1

. If

\displaystyle T_{W,j}(x)^{q}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq013})}}}{{=}}\ \left(\det(M_{W})y^{-1}\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}y)\right)^{q}

(25)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq011})}}}{{=}}\ (-1)^{n-1}y^{-(q-1)}\left(\det(M_{W})y^{-1}\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}y)\right)

We therefore deduce that

\displaystyle\psi_{Y}\circ\overline{g}\circ\psi_{W}^{-1}(x)\ \

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq014},\ref{Eq022})}}}{{=}}\ \ \Biggl(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{k}\left(\phi_{W}(x)\right)\gamma_{k}\Biggr)^{q-1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq023})}}}{{=}}\ \ \bigl(\det(M_{W})^{r}T_{W,j}(x)^{-r}h_{j}(x)\bigr)^{q-1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq011},\ref{Eq024})}}}{{=}}\ \ (-1)^{r(n-1)}T_{W,j}(x)^{(q-1)(-r)}h(x)^{q-1}

and

\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
and
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
, respectively. They are both rational since
T_{W,i},g_{k}\ (0\leq i,k\leq n-1)
are polynomials and all components of
\phi_{W}
are rational. If

, then for
0\leq j\leq n-1
and each
x\in Z_{W,j}
, it is easy to see that
T_{W,j}(x)^{r}g_{k}\left(\phi_{W,n}(x)\right)\ (0\leq k\leq n-1)
are polynomials. So
h_{j}\ (0\leq j\leq n-1)
and

are polynomials. Consequently,
x^{r}h(x)^{q-1}
and
x^{r}h\left(x^{q-1}\right)
are both polynomials in this case. Finally, we show that the mapping
f
defined by

20
and
21
is a permutation polynomial of index
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
by verifying that

f(x)=x^{r}h\left(x^{q-1}\right).

This is clearly true when
x=0
. If
x\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
, then there exists a unique
0\leq j\leq n-1
such that
x\in S_{W,j}
. That is,
j=\max\bigl\{0\leq i\leq n-1:\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{i}x)\neq 0\bigr\}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle f(x)\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq020},\ref{Eq021})}}}{{=}}\

\displaystyle\ \ Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{k}\biggl(\frac{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{0}x)}{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)},\dots,\frac{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j-1}x)}{Tr_{q}^{q^{n}}(\beta_{j}x)},1,0,\dots,0\biggr)\gamma_{k}.

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq013},\ref{Eq022})}}}{{=}}\

\displaystyle\ \ \bigl(\det(M_{W})^{-1}xT_{W,j}\bigl(x^{q-1}\bigr)\bigr)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{k}\bigl(\phi_{W}\bigl(x^{q-1}\bigr)\bigr)\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{=}]{(\ref{Eq023},\ref{Eq024})}}}{{=}}\

\displaystyle\ \ x^{r}h\bigl(x^{q-1}\bigr).

Thus,
f
is a permutation polynomial of index
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
.

\square

Before demonstrating any applications of
Theorem

3.2
, there are a couple of important yet unanswered questions. That is, when is a mapping
\overline{g}
over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
well-defined, and when is it a bijection? To answer these questions, we need the following results.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:\dots:g_{n-1})
be an arbitrary mapping from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to itself, where
g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\widetilde{g}=(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
. Then
\overline{g}
is well-defined if and only if

(1)

g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1}
have no common roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\bigl\{\vec{0}\bigr\}
and

(2)

there exists a mapping
D:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}\times\bigl(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\bigl\{\vec{0}\bigr\}\bigr)\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that

\widetilde{g}\bigl(\lambda\vec{x}\bigr)=D\bigl(\lambda,\vec{x}\bigr)\widetilde{g}\bigl(\vec{x}\bigr)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
and
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\bigl\{\vec{0}\bigr\}
.

We skip the proof of
Lemma

3.3
, which is a routine check. However,
Lemma

3.3
gives rise to a criterion for determining when a mapping
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:\dots:g_{n-1})
permutes
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
based on the images of the underlying sets of all equivalence classes in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
under the mapping
\widetilde{g}=(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\overline{E_{i}},E_{i}\ \left(0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1\right)
be all equivalence classes in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
and their underlying sets, respectively. That is, each
E_{i}
is the set of all representatives for
\overline{E_{i}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\bigl\{\vec{0}\bigr\}
. Let
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:\dots:g_{n-1})
be any well-defined mapping from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to itself, where
g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\widetilde{g}=(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
. Then
\overline{g}
is a bijection over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
if and only if there exists a permutation
\sigma
in the symmetric group
S_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
such that
\widetilde{g}(E_{i})\subseteq E_{\sigma(i)}
for
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
.

Proof
We show that for
0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
,
\widetilde{g}\left(E_{i_{1}}\right)\subseteq E_{i_{2}}
if and only if
\overline{g}\left(\overline{E_{i_{1}}}\right)=\overline{E_{i_{2}}}
. Assume that
E_{i_{1}}=\left\{\delta\vec{x}:\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}\right\}
and
E_{i_{2}}=\left\{\delta\vec{y}:\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}\right\}
, where
\vec{x}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})\in E_{i_{1}}
and
\vec{y}=(y_{0},\dots,y_{n-1})\in E_{i_{2}}
. Since
\overline{g}
is well-defined,
Item

2
of
Lemma

3.3
implies that

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}\left(E_{i_{1}}\right)\subseteq E_{i_{2}}

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ \widetilde{g}\left(\vec{x}\right)=\left(g_{0}\left(\vec{x}\right),\dots,g_{n-1}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)=\delta\vec{y}\text{ for some }\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ \overline{g}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})=\left(g_{0}\left(\vec{x}\right):\dots:g_{n-1}\left(\vec{x}\right)\right)=(y_{0}:\dots:y_{n-1})

\displaystyle\ \Leftrightarrow\ \overline{g}\left(\overline{E_{i_{1}}}\right)=\overline{E_{i_{2}}}.

Consequently,
\overline{g}
is a bijection over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
if and only if
\widetilde{g}(E_{i})\subseteq E_{\sigma(i)}
for
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
for some
\sigma\in S_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
.

\square

The major takeaway from
Proposition

3.4
is that for
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:\dots:g_{n-1})
to be a bijection over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
,
\widetilde{g}=(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
need not be a bijection over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Instead, it suffices to make sure that
\widetilde{g}
maps the underlying sets of equivalence classes in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to subsets of pairwise distinct underlying sets of equivalence classes in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
. In the next few results, we present some concrete examples of homogeneous permutation polynomials of index
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
which are constructed from bijections over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
H_{0}(x)=x
, and let
H_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq n-1)
be any polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
which permute
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
0\leq k\leq n-1
, define
g_{k}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where

g_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})=\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}H_{j}\Bigl(x_{k}x_{j}^{q-2}\Bigr)x_{j}^{q-1}\prod_{i=j+1}^{n-1}\Bigl(1-x_{i}^{q-1}\Bigr).

Then
\overline{g}=(g_{0}:\dots:d_{n-1})
permutes
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
.

Proof
For
0\leq j,k\leq n-1
, it is easy to check that

g_{k}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})=\begin{cases}H_{j}\bigl(\frac{x_{k}}{x_{j}}\bigr)&\text{if }0\leq k\leq j,x_{j}\neq 0\text{ and }x_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\geq j+1;\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\widetilde{g}=(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
. Then
\widetilde{g}\bigl(\vec{0}\bigr)=\vec{0}
, and for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
,

\displaystyle\overline{g}(x_{0}:0:1)

\displaystyle=(x_{0}:1:0)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast};

\displaystyle\overline{g}(x_{0}:1:0)

\displaystyle=(x_{0}:0:1)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q};

\displaystyle\overline{g}(x_{0}:x_{1}:1)

\displaystyle=(x_{0}:x_{1}:1)

\displaystyle\text{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{ and }x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}.

Using
\overline{g}
together with
Theorem

3.2
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq 2\right\}
and
Y=\left\{\gamma_{j}:0\leq j\leq 2\right\}
be any two bases for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{3}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
B=\left\{\beta_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
be the dual basis of
W
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
be coprime to
q-1
. Then
F
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{3}}
, where

F(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{2}x)^{r}\gamma_{0}&\text{if }x=x_{2}\omega_{2}\text{ and }x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast};\\
\\
\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{0}x)^{r}\gamma_{2}&\text{if }x=x_{0}\omega_{0}\text{ and }x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q};\\
\\
\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{0}x)\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{2}x)^{r-1}\gamma_{0}+\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{2}x)^{r}\gamma_{1}&\text{if }x=x_{0}\omega_{0}+x_{2}\omega_{2}\text{ and }x_{0},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast};\\

\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{0}x)\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{1}x)^{r-1}\gamma_{0}+\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{1}x)^{r}\gamma_{2}&\text{if }x=x_{0}\omega_{0}+x_{1}\omega_{1}\text{ and }x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast};\\
\\
\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{0}x)\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{2}x)^{r-1}\gamma_{0}+&\text{if }x=x_{0}\omega_{0}+x_{1}\omega_{1}+x_{2}\omega_{2}\text{ and }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}.\\
\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{1}x)\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{2}x)^{r-1}\gamma_{1}+\text{Tr}_{q}^{q^{3}}(\beta_{2}x)^{r}\gamma_{2}.\end{array}\right.

We give a small example of

, which has been verified by MAGMA.

.

Let

. Then

4
A comparison with Hirschfeld’s bijections

Having studied the GMTs, determined their inverses and presented some of their applications, a natural question to ask seems to be: are there other known classes of bijections defined on
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
? The answer is affirmative. While studying the representation of the points and lines in projective spaces, Hirschfeld
[
18
]
introduced a class of bijections defined on
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
. In this section, we compare the GMTs with Hirschfeld’s mappings, and count the number of both classes of bijections. To describe Hirschfeld’s results requires the notion of subprimitivity and projectivity.

Definition 4.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

A monic irreducible polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called subprimitive if the smallest
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f(x)\mid x^{e}-c
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
is
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. A root of a subprimitive polynomial is called a subprimitive root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
.

Definition 4.2

(
[
18
]
)
.

A projectivity is a bijection
\mathfrak{T}
over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
given by a non-singular matrix
M=M(\mathfrak{T})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\mathfrak{T}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})=(x_{0}^{\prime}:\dots:x_{n-1}^{\prime})
if there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that
\lambda(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})M=(x_{0}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n-1}^{\prime})
. A projectivity
\mathfrak{T}
is cyclic if it permutes the elements of
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
in a single cycle. That is, if
(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1}),(y_{0}:\dots:y_{n-1})\in\text{PG}(n-1,q)
, then there exists some
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
such that
\mathfrak{T}^{i}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})=(y_{0}:\dots:y_{n-1})
or, equivalently,
\lambda(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})M^{i}=(y_{0},\dots,y_{n-1})
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
.

Let
\alpha
be a subprimitive root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. Let
f(x)=x^{n}-\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}x^{k}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let

T=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1\\
a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}&\dots&a_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}

be the companion matrix of
f
. As per
[
18
, Theorem 4.2.]
, a projectivity over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
is cyclic if and only if its associated matrix is the companion matrix of a subprimitive polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, if
\mathfrak{T}
is the projectivity over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
given by
Definition

4.2
such that
M(\mathfrak{T})=T
, then
\mathfrak{T}
is cyclic. So every element in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
is the image of
(1:0:\dots:0)
under
\mathfrak{T}^{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
. For
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
, let
y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the unique scalars such that

(1,0,\dots,0)T^{i}=\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\Bigr).

(32)

Hirschfeld showed by induction that

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{\alpha,k}^{(i)}\alpha^{k}=\alpha^{i}\ \biggl(0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1\biggr).

(33)

According to
Definition

4.1
, since
\alpha
is a subprimitive root, the smallest
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha^{e}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
is
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. So
Eq.

33
implies that if
0\leq i_{1}\neq i_{2}\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
, then there exists no
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that
\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i_{1})},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i_{1})}\Bigr)=\lambda\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i_{2})},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i_{2})}\Bigr)
. Hence, the equivalence classes
\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)}:\dots:y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\Bigr)
, where
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
, are pairwise distinct, meaning that

\text{PG}(n-1,q)=\biggl\{\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)}:\dots:y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\Bigr):0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1\biggr\},

Let
P_{\alpha}=\bigl\{\alpha^{i}:0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1\bigr\}
. Hirschfeld concluded from the above observations that the mapping
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}:\text{PG}(n-1,q)\to P_{\alpha}
is a bijection, where for
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
,

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\left(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)}:\dots:y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{\alpha,k}^{(i)}\alpha^{k}=\alpha^{i}.

(34)

The major differences between
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
and the GMT
\psi_{W}
given by
Eq.

7
are as follows.

(1)

First of all,
\psi_{W}(\text{PG}(n-1,q))=\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
, whereas
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}(\text{PG}(n-1,q))=P_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is a subprimitive root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. By
Definition

4.1
, the smallest
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha^{e}=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
is
e=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. In particular,
P_{\alpha}=\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
if and only if
c=1
.

(2)

Moreover, for
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
,
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\alpha^{i}\right)=\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)}:\dots:y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\Bigr)
, which is the equivalent class in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
represented by the coordinate vector
\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(i)},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(i)}\Bigr)
of
\alpha^{i}
with respect to the polynomial basis
\left\{\alpha^{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This is not the case for
\psi_{W}
, which is defined using an arbitrary basis
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Indeed, if
x=(x_{0}\omega_{0}+\dots+x_{n-1}\omega_{n-1})^{q-1}\in\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
, then
\psi_{W}^{-1}(x)=(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})
, which is not necessarily the equivalence class in
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
represented by the coordinate vector of
x
with respect to
W
unless
q=2
, in which case
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}=\psi_{W}
, where
W=\bigl\{\alpha^{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\bigr\}
.

(3)

The set of all GMTs from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
is closed under scalar multiplication by elements in
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
. Indeed, let
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
be any basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
c\in\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
. Then
c=\widetilde{c}^{q-1}
for some
\widetilde{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}^{\ast}
. It is easy to show that
c\psi_{W}=\psi_{\widetilde{W}}
, where
\widetilde{W}=\widetilde{c}W=\left\{\widetilde{c}\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
. However, if
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are subprimitive roots in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
, then
\gamma^{i}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\neq\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
for any
1\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
. Indeed, assume towards a contradiction that
\gamma^{i}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
for some
1\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
and subprimitive roots
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. By
Eq.

32
,
\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(0)},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(0)}\Bigr)=\bigl(y_{\beta,0}^{(0)},\dots,y_{\beta,n-1}^{(0)}\bigr)=(1,0,\dots,0)
. Hence,
\gamma^{i}=\gamma^{i}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\Bigl(y_{\alpha,0}^{(0)},\dots,y_{\alpha,n-1}^{(0)}\Bigr)=\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\Bigl(y_{\beta,0}^{(0)},\dots,y_{\beta,n-1}^{(0)}\Bigr)=\beta^{0}=1
. Since
\gamma
is a subprimitive root, the smallest
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gamma^{e}=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
is
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. Hence,
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}\mid i
, which is impossible since
1\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
.

Moreover, there are more GMTs than Hirschfeld’s bijections over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
, as the following result shows.

Proposition 4.3
.

Over
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
, let
M(n,q)
and
H(n,q)
denote the number of GMTs and that of Hirschfeld’s bijections, respectively. Then

\frac{M(n,q)}{H(n,q)}>q^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\left(q^{n}-q^{i}\right).

Proof
Let
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
be a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
, and define
W^{\sigma}=\left\{\theta_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
, where
\theta_{j}=\omega_{\sigma(j)}
for each
j
. Then
\psi_{W}=\psi_{W^{\sigma}}
if and only if
\sigma=\text{id}
. Indeed, if
\sigma=\text{id}
, then clearly
\psi_{W}=\psi_{W^{\sigma}}
. Conversely, assume that
\psi_{W}=\psi_{W^{\sigma}}
. Let
0\leq j_{0}\leq n-1
. If
(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})\in\text{PG}(n-1,q)
is such that
x_{j_{0}}=1
and
x_{j}=0
for all
j\neq j_{0}
, then
\psi_{W}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})=\omega_{j_{0}}^{q-1}
, and
\psi_{W^{\sigma}}(x_{0}:\dots:x_{n-1})=\theta_{j_{0}}^{q-1}=\omega_{\sigma(j_{0})}^{q-1}
. Since
\psi_{W}=\psi_{W^{\sigma}}
,
\omega_{j_{0}}^{q-1}=\omega_{\sigma(j_{0})}^{q-1}
. Since
W
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent,
\omega_{j_{0}}=\omega_{\sigma(j_{0})}
, and so
j_{0}=\sigma(j_{0})
. Since this is true for all
j_{0}
,
\sigma=\text{id}
. Meanwhile, for any two bases
W=\left\{\omega_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\},Y=\left\{\gamma_{j}:0\leq j\leq n-1\right\}
for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\psi_{W}=\psi_{Y}
if and only if
W=\lambda Y=\left\{\lambda\omega:\omega\in Y\right\}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
. Indeed, if the latter condition is true, then
\psi_{W}=\psi_{Y}
. Conversely, assume that
\psi_{W}=\psi_{Y}
. Since
\psi_{W}(1:0:\dots:0)=\psi_{Y}(1:0:\dots:0),\dots,\psi_{W}(0:0:\dots:1)=\psi_{Y}(0:0:\dots:1)
, we know that for each
0\leq j\leq n-1
, there exists some
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that
\omega_{j}=\lambda_{j}\gamma_{j}
. Since
\psi_{W}(1:1:\dots:1)=\psi_{Y}(1:1:\dots:1)
, there exists some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that

\lambda_{0}\gamma_{0}+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}\gamma_{n-1}=\omega_{0}+\dots+\omega_{n-1}=\lambda(\gamma_{0}+\dots+\gamma_{n-1}).

Since
Y
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent,
\lambda_{0}=\dots=\lambda_{n-1}=\lambda
. Thus,
M(n,q)
is the number of
ordered
bases for
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
divided by
q-1
. That is,

M(n,q)=\frac{\left(q^{n}-1\right)\left(q^{n}-q\right)\dots\left(q^{n}-q^{n-1}\right)}{q-1}.

Finally, we determine
H(n,q)
. If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are distinct subprimitive roots
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
, then
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha_{1}}\neq\mathcal{H}_{\alpha_{2}}
. Indeed, by
Eq.

32
,
(1,0,\dots,0)T=(0,1,0,\dots,0)
for all companion matrices
T
. So for
j=1,2
,
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha_{j}}(0:1:\dots:0)=\alpha_{j}
due to
Eq.

34
. In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha_{1}}\neq\mathcal{H}_{\alpha_{2}}
. Consequently,
H(n,q)
is the number of subprimitive roots in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
, i.e.,
n
times the number of subprimitive polynomials of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By
[
18
, Eq. (1.12)]
, there are
\bigl(\frac{q-1}{n}\bigr)\varphi\bigl(\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}\bigr)
subprimitive polynomials of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\varphi
is the Euler totient function. Thus,

H(n,q)=(q-1)\varphi\bigl(\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}\bigr),

In particular, we know that

\frac{M(n,q)}{H(n,q)}>\frac{M(n,q)}{q^{n}-1}=q^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\left(q^{n}-q^{i}\right),

which completes the proof.

\square

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we generalized the Möbius transformation to a class of bijections from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to
\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
for an arbitrary positive integer
n\geq 2
, and determined the inverses of the GMTs. As their applications, we constructed various classes of homogeneous permutation polynomials of index
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{n}}
. We also showed that the GMTs are different from Hirschfeld’s bijections from
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
to the
i
-th powers of subprimitive roots, where
0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-1
, thereby extending the family of known bijections defined on
\text{PG}(n-1,q)
with good properties and applications.

Two-gap superconductivity in a Janus MoSeLi monolayer. Preprint: APS/123-QED
Jakkapat Seeyangnok, Udomsilp Pinsook, Greame John Ackland

Abstract
Two-dimensional (2D) lithium-decorated materials have emerged as a significant area of study since the prediction of superconductivity in lithium-decorated graphene at temperatures around 8.1 K (G. Profeta et al., Nature Physics 8, 131–134) , with experimental evidence observed at T c T_{c} = 5.9 K (B. M. Ludbrook et al., PNAS 112 (38) 11795-11799). Following earlier studies, this paper focuses on the hexagonal Janus MoSeLi monolayer as a promising candidate for Li-decorated 2D materials. Our research reveals that lithium substitution on a selenium layer of MoSe 2 can produce a hexagonal Janus MoSeLi monolayer, which exhibits metallic behavior with potential phonon-mediated superconductivity with a critical temperature ( T c T_{c} ) of 4.5 K. Additionally, by solving the anisotropic gap equations derived from the Migdal-Eliashberg theory, we found that the Janus MoSeLi monolayer exhibits two-gap superconductivity. This finding underscores the potential of hexagonal Janus MoSeLi as a significant Li-decorated 2D material for exploring two-dimensional superconductivity and sets the stage for further investigations into new families of Janus transition metal chalcogenides.



I
Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) lithium-decorated materials have emerged as a significant area of study since the prediction of superconductivity in lithium-decorated graphene
Profeta et al. 2012
. The decoration of lithium in graphene improves its electronic properties and has been shown to induce superconductivity at temperatures around 8.1 K
Profeta et al. 2012
, with experimental observation at 5.9 K
Ludbrook et al. 2015
using angle-resolved photoelectron spectroscopy (ARPES). ARPES experiment allows for the investigation of various physical properties of quasielectrons resulting from the electron-phonon interaction. Following this, lithium-decorated materials have been extensively studied. Examples include lithium-decorated 2D orthorhombic (o)-B
2
X
2
monolayers
Benaddi et al. 2024
, lithium-decorated SiB monolayer
Jiang et al. 2023
, lithium-decorated 2D Irida-graphene
Zhang and Guo 2024
, lithium-decorated phosphorene
Haldar et al. 2017
and lithium-decorated
\Psi
-graphene
Dewangan et al. 2023
. These advancements make lithium-decorated 2D materials promising candidates for advanced electronic applications and superconducting devices.

2D Janus transition-metal dichalcogenides (2D-JTMDs) exhibit an out-of-plane asymmetric layered structure, with different chalcogenide atoms on each side. This asymmetry imparts 2D-JTMDs with unique and tunable electronic, optical, and mechanical properties
Tang and Kou 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2022
;
Angeli et al. 2022
;
He and Li 2018
;
Yeh 2020
;
Yin et al. 2021
. Although 2D-JTMDs do not occur naturally, they have been successfully synthesized, beginning with Janus graphene in 2013
Zhang et al. 2013
. Since then, various 2D-JTMDs have been developed, such as MoSSe
Trivedi et al. 2020
;
Lu et al. 2017
, WSSe
Trivedi et al. 2020
, and PtSSe
Sant et al. 2020
. These materials are typically fabricated using selective epitaxy atomic replacement (SEAR) processes with hydrogen (H
2
) plasma
Trivedi et al. 2020
;
Tang and Kou 2022
. Recently, a Janus 2H-MoSH monolayer was synthesized using the SEAR method by substituting the top S atoms with hydrogen atoms
Lu et al. 2017
. Theoretically, Janus MoSH monolayers have been predicted to exhibit superconductivity with
T_{c}=26.81
K for the 2H phase
Liu et al. 2022
;
Ku et al. 2023
. Furthermore, the 2H and 1T phases of WSH and WSeH phase has been suggested to be dynamically stable with a
T_{c}
around 12 K
Seeyangnok et al. 2024a
;
Seeyangnok et al. 2024b
. These phase stability and superconductivity have been confirmed by subsequent independent studies
Gan et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2024
. Recently, possible superconductivity has been reported in hexagonal TiSH, TiSeH, TiTeH, and ZrTeH monolayers
Ul Hassan and Pinsook 2024
;
Li et al. 2024
. Consequently, the study of elemental substitutions in Janus transition-metal dichalcogenides has been extensively pursued. Additionally, substituting lithium is a novel technique to modify the surface properties of 2D materials, as it might enhance electronic and physical characteristics of those 2D materials
Singh et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Xie et al. 2024
.

In this paper, we aims to comprehensively study the 2H hexagonal Janus MoSeLi monolayer as a potential Li-decorated 2D material, specifically in the form of hexagonal Janus transition metal dichalcogenide monolayer. First, we discuss the electronics to understand the properties of electrons near the Fermi level, and examine the phonon properties to study the vibrational modes of collective phonons. Subsequently, we investigate superconductivity through electron-phonon interactions based on the Migdal-Eliashberg (ME) theory
Bardeen et al. 1957
;
Frohlich 1950
;
Migdal 1958
;
Eliashberg 1960

Giannozzi et al. 2017
where we compute the electronics and phonon properties. We employed the electron-phonon Wannier-Fourier interpolation method

;
Giustino et al. 2007
within the EPW package
Noffsinger et al. 2010
;
Ponce et al. 2016
to self-consistently solve anisotropic Migdal-Eliashberg equations. The calculation detail will be available in the supplementary.

II
Methodology

The computations were performed using density functional theory (DFT), as implemented in QUANTUM ESPRESSO (QE)
Giannozzi et al. 2009
;
Giannozzi et al. 2017
. The crystal structures were created using VESTA
Momma and Izumi 2011
within the trigonal space group
P\overline{3}m1
(No.156). Optimization of the crystal structures was achieved using BFGS algorithm
Pfrommer et al. 1997
;
Liu and Nocedal 1989
, with full relaxation to a force threshold of
10^{-5}
eV/Å. Optimized norm-conserving Vanderbilt pseudopotentials
Hamann 2013
;
Schlipf and Gygi 2015
with GGA-PBE
Perdew et al. 1996
were employed for the exchange-correlation functional, with wavefunction and charge density cutoffs of 80 Ry and 320 Ry, respectively. For self-consistent Kohn-Sham states, a Monkhorst-Pack k-point grid
Monkhorst and Pack 1976
of
24\times 24\times 1
with Methfessel-Paxton smearing of 0.02 Ry was used on the Fermi surface
Methfessel and Paxton 1989
. The magnetic phases of MoSeLi were tested, and we found that the non-magnetic metallic phases are energetically favorable. The Fermi surface were visualized using XCRYSDEN
Kokalj 2003
. Dynamical matrices were computed using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) on
12\times 12\times 1
q-point grids, for phonon self-consistent calculations. Then, we computed the phonon linewidth,

Results and Discussions

III.1
Crystal structure

Figure 1:
Figures (a) and (b) show the side-view and top-view crystal structures of 2H-MoSeLi, where the purple (Mo), yellow (Se), and green (Li) spheres represent Mo, Se, and Li atoms, respectively.

The hexagonal structure of Janus MoSeLi belongs to the 3D trigonal space group
P3m1
(No.156), as depicted in Figure
1

Our investigations have shown that the 2H phase is more energetically favorable than the 1T phase. Furthermore, the 1T phase is not dynamically stable, as indicated by the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) calculations of phonons. Thus, we will focus only the 2H phase of MoSeLi from now on. The magnetic phases, including ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism (G-type antiferromagnetism (GAF), and C-type antiferromagnetism, (CAF)) of MoSeLi were tested, and we found that the non-magnetic metallic phases are energetically favorable. Along the
z

h_{\text{Mo-Li}}=2.26
Å, and the distance between the Mo and Se sublayers, denoted as
h_{\text{Mo-Se}}=1.72
Å.

By performing molecular dynamics simulations on a

As shown in Figure
5

5

\Delta_{k}

,

, and

orbitals, as analyzed in Figure

5
(e) shows the distribution of superconducting gaps (
\Delta_{k}
) as a function of temperature. At 4 K,
\Delta_{k}
ranges from 0.69 to 0.80 meV and from 0.47 to approximately 0.53 meV, displaying anisotropy, which aligns with the momentum-resolved
\Delta_{k}
in Figure
5
(d). As the temperature increases,
\Delta_{k}
values gradually decrease and eventually vanish at 5.7 K. Overall, similar to other materials such as n-doped graphene

, AlB
2
-based films
Zhao et al. 2019
, trilayer LiB
2

2

Gao et al. 2020
, monolayer LiBC

, MoSLi

\lambda=1.18

IV
CONCLUSION

In summary, we have explored the electronic structure and electron-phonon coupling (EPC) of MoSeLi. Our analysis reveals that the Mo-
d
orbitals are predominant in the electronic density of states (DOS) near the Fermi level. Phonon stability is confirmed by the positive-definite phonon spectrum obtained from DFPT, as shown in both the weighted EPC phonon dispersion and the phonon density of states. Thermal stability is supported by BOMD simulations at 300K. We observe significant softening phonon in low-energy acoustic ZA mode, contributing to a strong EPC (
\lambda=1.18
). Moreover, it is shown that MoSeLi has a unique electronic structure of different orbital contributions between the inner and outer shells of the Fermi surface, which results in two-gap superconductivity. The critical superconducting temperature (
T_{c}
) is estimated to be 4.5 K and 5.7 K from the Allen-Dynes and Migdal-Eliashberg equations, respectively. These results suggest that further theoretical and experimental investigations are warranted to explore superconductivity in lithium-decorated Janus transition-metal chalcogenides.

Abstract

The proofs that CM points on

admit no quotient by an involution of small genus. This condition is necessary in order to rule out the possibility that we are in case (2) of

For a curve
X
over the complex numbers we have
g(X)=2\dim H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. If
\iota\in\Aut X
is an automorphism, then the genus of
X/\iota
can be computed in terms of the action of
\iota
on

as follows:

g(X/\iota)=2\dim H_{1}(X/\iota,\mathbb{Q})=2\dim H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\iota}.

By the universal coefficient theorem and since
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free for complex curves, one has
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. If
X=X_{0}(N)(\mathbb{C})
, then
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
can be described in terms of the space of modular symbols
\mathbb{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
, as originally proved in
[
Man73
, Theorem 1.9]
. Algorithms for computing a basis of
\mathbb{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
are described in
[
Ste07
, §3]
, whose notation we follow. Moreover, if the automorphism
\iota
is induced by an element
\gamma\in\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
in the normalizer of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, then the action of
\gamma
on a basis of
\mathbb{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N);\mathbb{Z})
can be computed as described in
[
Ste07
, §3.2]
.

Fortunately, with a few exceptions, the automorphisms of
X_{0}(N)
are induced by elements
\gamma\in\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
. More precisely, let
\textup{Norm}_{\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
denote the normaliser of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
and set

B_{0}(N):=\textup{Norm}_{\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})}(\Gamma_{0}(N))/\Gamma_{0}(N).

By
[
KM88
, Theorem 0.1]
(see also
[

]
for a correction in the case

) we have

for

.

In
[
Bar08
, Theorem 11]
an explicit set of generators is given for

3
3

3

Bars defines

as
\textup{Norm}_{\SL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})}(\Gamma_{0}(N))/\Gamma_{0}(N)

. We compute the genera of the quotients of

Proposition 5.3

and
[
Lup24b
]
in order to show

and use this cover to compute

When considering how to bound the gonalities of the quotients of

We did not need this result in the end, but we believe it might be of use to others, and of independent interest.

Proposition A.1

Let

be a curve of genus
g
and
\iota

which is not in the center of
\Aut X

(A.1)

Proof.

First note that if (

) is satisfied, then a map of degree
d
to

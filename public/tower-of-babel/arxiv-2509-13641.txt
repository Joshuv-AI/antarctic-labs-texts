On the local-to-global principle for zero-cycles on self products of elliptic curves with CM
Michael Wills

Abstract
For a smooth projective variety X X defined over a global field K K , one can form a notion of Weak Approximation for the Chow group of zero-cycles of X X . There exists a Brauer-Manin obstruction to Weak Approximation here akin to that for rational points. However, unlike for rational points, it is conjectured that this obstruction is the only one; early versions of this conjecture date back to the 1980’s [ CS81 ] , [ KS86 ] . In this paper, we provide evidence for this when X X is the self-product of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication. For some varieties of this form, we construct infinitely many extensions L / K L/K for which the base change X × K Spec ⁡ L X\times_{K}\Spec L satisfies a local-to-global principle for a fixed prime p p . We do this via explicitly constructing global zero-cycles, and our results have applications over all but two of the complex multiplication fields.

Introduction

Let
K
be a number field with set of places
\Omega
, and for each
v\in\Omega
let
\iota_{v}:K\hookrightarrow K_{v}
denote the embedding of
K
into the local field corresponding to

.
Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over
K
, and consider its set
X(K)
of
K
-rational points. One approach to understanding these points is consider the diagonal embedding of
X(K)
into the set of
adelic points
of
X
defined by
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})=\prod_{v\in\Omega}X_{v}(K_{v})
, where for each
v
,
X_{v}=X\times_{K}\Spec K_{v}
denotes the base change. In particular, one can ask:

1.

does
X
satisfy the
Hasse Principle
, i.e. does the existence of an adelic point for
X
imply existence of a
K
-rational point for
X
, and if so

2.

does
X
satisfy
Weak Approximation
, i.e. is
X(K)
dense in

(in the appropriate topological sense)?

One obstruction to both of these arises from the cohomological Brauer group
\Br(X)=H^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
; more precisely, in
[
Man71
]
a pairing of sets
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})\times\Br(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is constructed under which points in
X(K)
pair trivially with all elements of
\Br(X)
. The left (pointed set) kernel of this pairing then defines a closed intermediate set

X(K)\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{K}),

and we say that the Brauer-Manin obstruction
explains the failure of the Hasse Principle
(or
Weak Approximation
) if
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})\neq\emptyset
but
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br}=\emptyset
(or if
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br}\neq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})
respectively). However, this does not capture all ways that either the Hasse Principle or Weak Approximation may fail; see
[
Sko99
]
for the first explicit example, and
[
Poo17
, Chapter 8]
for an overview of further refinements to this question.

Similar questions arise for the
Chow group of zero-cycles

\CH_{0}(X)
of
X
, a “linearization” of the closed points of
X
foundational to intersection theory
[
Ful98
]
. Namely, one can construct an adelic analogue to
\CH_{0}(X)
, which for
K
totally imaginary is given by

\CH_{0,\mathbb{A}}(X)=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{f}}\CH_{0}(X_{K_{v}})

where
\Omega_{f}\subset\Omega
denotes the finite places (see
subsection 2.2
for the general definition), together with a diagonal map
\Delta:\CH_{0}(X)\to\CH_{0,\mathbb{A}}(X)
.
The Brauer-Manin pairing on rational points then extends to an abelian group pairing
\CH_{0,\mathbb{A}}(X)\times\Br(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, and the image of
\Delta
is contained in the left kernel of this pairing
[
Col95
, p. 57]
. Since
\Br(X)
is torsion, this extends to a pairing
\widehat{\CH_{0,\mathbb{A}}(X)}\times\Br(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
(where for any abelian group

the quotient

, and by

the profinite completion

), giving rise to a complex

\displaystyle\widehat{\CH_{0}(X)}\overset{\Delta}{\rightarrow}\widehat{\CH_{0,\mathbb{A}}(X)}\overset{\varepsilon}{\rightarrow}\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

(1)

We then have the following Conjecture due to Colliot-Thélène:

Conjecture

(E)
.

For a smooth projective geometrically connected variety

defined over a number field, the complex (
1
) is exact.

As discussed in
[

]
, this conjecture implies both that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse Principle for zero-cycles of degree 1, as well as a version of Weak Approximation for zero-cycles (now in a group-theoretic rather than topological sense). Conjecture (E) goes back to conjectures in
[
CS81
]
on geometrically rational surfaces and in
[
KS86
]
on higher class field theory; see
[
Col95
]
,
[
Wit12
]
, and
[
CS21
]

[

]
and
[
Wit12
]
.

Little is known about Conjecture (E) in general, especially unconditionally. For

, Conjecture (E) follows from the fundamental short exact sequence of global class field theory. For
X
a curve, Conjecture (E) was proved in
[
Col99
]
, conditional on finiteness of the Shafarevich-Tate group of the Jacobian of

. In higher dimensions, there are conditional results given in
[
Lia13
]
(building upon work in
[
CSS87
]
,
[
CSS87a
]
), which show Conjecture (E) for rationally connected varieties under the hypothesis that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only one for Weak Approximation of rational points after any finite base change. A similarly conditional result on the Hasse Principle for zero-cycles of odd degree on products of Kummer varieties may be found in
[
BN21
]
. For Weak Approximation on K3 surfaces, a weaker “fixed
n
” version of Conjecture (E) is shown in
[
Ier21
]
, again conditional on a deep understanding of Weak Approximation for rational points. There are no known abelian varieties of dimension at least 2 for which Conjecture (E) holds in its entirety, despite knowing that Weak Approximation for rational points holds for certain classes of such varieties (assuming finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group)
[
Wan96
]
. Additionally, compatibility of Conjecture (E) is shown in
[
HW16
]
with certain fibrations over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and in
[
Lia23
]
with products of at most one nice curve and varieties satisfying a certain condition on their Brauer group, which includes geometrically rationally connected varieties and K3 surfaces.

The goal of this paper is to provide unconditional evidence of Conjecture (E) for infinite families of varieties
X=(E\times_{K}E)_{L}
, where
E
is an elliptic curve defined over an imaginary quadratic field
K
having complex multiplication by the full ring of integers

, and

is a finite extension. We will do so via explicit computations with zero-cycles rather than using the arithmetic of rational points that powers the conditional results discussed above.

To state the nature of this evidence, recall that
\CH_{0}(X)
admits a filtration

\CH_{0}(X)\supseteq F^{1}(X)\supseteq F^{2}(X),

where
F^{1}(X)
denotes the zero-cycles of degree zero and

is the Albanese kernel, also known as the Abel-Jacobi kernel. This filtration and its adelic analogue are compatible with the maps in the complex (
1
), so a necessary aspect of proving Conjecture (E) is showing exactness of

\displaystyle\widehat{F^{2}(X)}\overset{\Delta}{\rightarrow}\widehat{F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)}\overset{\varepsilon}{\rightarrow}\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

(2)

In fact, by

, exactness of (

Note next that by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, exactness of (

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}(X)/p^{n}\overset{\Delta}{\rightarrow}\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)/p^{n}\overset{\varepsilon}{\rightarrow}\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

(3)

for each prime

. As it turns out, for

and

, only the places of

lying above

will contribute to the middle term; see

. This is very useful, as at present our understanding of the local

groups at places of supersingular reduction for

[

, Section 1.2]
for some discussion of why this is the case. Since

at places lying above a rational prime

. This motivates the central study of this paper, which is to find specific examples of elliptic curves

, and primes

for which we can show exactness of (

) for

. We are specifically interested in examples where the middle term is non-vanishing, so that exactness is not trivially satisfied. This brings us to our first result:

(cf. Theorems
4.8
and

)
.

Let

be an imaginary quadratic field of class number 1, let

, and let

.

Previously, the only result of this form was found in

]
. There, it is assumed that

, and a conditional procedure is established for generating

. The Albanese map induces a homomorphism

Lemma 3.10

Let

(Cf.
[

Let

, and fix a primitive

, where

Remark 3.11

The assumption that

\displaystyle\res_{L_{w}/F_{u}}\big(\mathcal{D}_{u}^{i}\big)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{w}^{i\cdot(p-1)}.

Since composition with the norm going the other way is just multiplication by

we have that

if and only if

, and applying this for

∎

Remark 4.2

This non-triviality criterion is equivalent to that in

Lemma 4.3

4.1

Let
(E,v\mid p)

\pi
,
F
,
L
,
A
,
w
, and

section 4
. By
section 4
, we may write
|\overline{E_{v}}(\mathbb{F}_{v})|=dp
where

is 2 if
D=1
and
p=5
, and
d=1
otherwise. For
P\in E_{u}(F_{u})
, non-triviality of
\{A_{w},\res P\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}
modulo

is equivalent to having

by

, and by
subsection 3.1
computing this valuation requires understanding the

-coordinates of points in

Our main tool for doing so will be analyzing the kernel polynomial associated to
[\overline{\pi}]\in\End_{K}(E)
. This is a polynomial

of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
, the
\overline{K}
-roots of which are exactly those values which appear as

-coordinates of points in
E[\overline{\pi}]
. That
E_{u}[\overline{\pi}]
is
F_{u}
-rational means that
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}
splits into linear factors over

, and

gives that these roots are all distinct modulo

.

It will be useful to simultaneously compute the
\overline{\pi}
-torsion of all elliptic curves with the same CM type. To that end, we see by
[
JM95
, Tableau 1]
that all elliptic curves with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
are parametrized in a 1-dimensional family by
E_{a}:y^{2}=f_{a}(x)
, where
f_{a}(x)
has (or can be put into) the form

\displaystyle f_{a}(x)=\begin{cases}x^{3}+ax&D=1\\
x^{3}+a&D=3\\
x^{3}+n_{D}a^{2}x+m_{D}a^{3}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for some integers
n_{D},m_{D}
depending on
D
, and
a
ranges over
K^{\times}
. We may regard this as an elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Further,
\mathcal{E}
has complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and we let
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x)
denote the kernel polynomial of
[\overline{\pi}]\in\End_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathcal{E})
(recall that
p\neq 2
, so there is no dependence on
y
). This polynomial has coefficients in
K[a]
, and regarded as an element
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x,a)\in K[x,a]
has
x
-degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
. The complex multiplication on
\mathcal{E}
specializes to that on each of the fibers, from which it follows that if
E
is the fiber of
\mathcal{E}
lying above some
a_{0}\in K^{\times}
, then the kernel polynomial
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x)
of
[\overline{\pi}]
as an endomorphism of

is given by
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x)=\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x,a_{0})
.
We similarly let
\Phi_{p}
and
\Phi_{\pi}
denote the kernel polynomials of
[p]
and
[\pi]
on
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

1.

\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}
has coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and is homogeneous of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
after weighting
a
by

\displaystyle w=\frac{|\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|}{2}=\begin{cases}2&D=1\\
3&D=3\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

2.

\Phi_{p}
factors into irreducibles in
K[x,a]
(and thus in
\mathcal{O}_{K}[x,a]
) as
\Phi_{p}=\Phi_{\pi}\cdot\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}\cdot g
for some
g\in K[x,a]
.

Proof.

1.

First, note that every fiber
\mathcal{E}_{a}
with
a\in K^{\times}
is isomorphic over a suitable extension of
K
to the fiber
\mathcal{E}_{1}
via the isomorphism
(x,y)\mapsto(a^{-1/w}x,a^{-3/2w}y)
. This isomorphism preserves the complex multiplication, and it follows that
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x,a)
and
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(a^{-1/w}x,1)
have the same zeros. We have already observed that
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}
is polynomial in
x
and
a
, so it must be that
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x,a)=a^{n}\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(a^{-1/w}x,1)
for some
n
large enough to clear denominators. In fact, this
n
must be as small as possible; since
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}
divides
\Phi_{p}
, the leading
x
-coefficients also satisfy this divisibility relation, and the leading coefficient of
x
in
\Phi_{p}
is simply
p
so has no dependence on
a
. Thus,
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x,a)
is homogeneous in
x
and
a^{1/w}
, so has the weighted homogeneity required.

2.

We first note that
\Phi_{\pi}
and
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}
both divide
\Phi_{p}
, as
\pi
and
\overline{\pi}
divide
p
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\End_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathcal{E})
. It remains to show that both of these are irreducible, share no common factors, and that the remaining factor of
\Phi_{p}
is also irreducible. It suffices to show all of these things for a fixed
a_{0}\in K^{\times}
. Let
\phi_{\pi}(x)=\Phi_{\pi}(x,a_{0})
, and similarly define
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x)
and
\phi_{p}(x)
; these are the kernel polynomials for the corresponding endomorphisms of the elliptic curve
E=\mathcal{E}_{a_{0}}
defined over
K
.

First, note that
\phi_{\pi}
is irreducible, since the extension
K(E[\pi])/K
is of degree
p-1
by
[
Rub99
, Corollary 5.20.ii]
. Indeed, if there were a smaller irreducible factor, then taking a root of it together with the corresponding
y
coordinate would give a non-zero point
P\in E[\pi]
defined over an extension of degree less than
p-1
, but
E[\pi]
is cyclic and this
P
would generate all of
E[\pi]
, a contradiction. An identical argument holds for
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}
. Also note that
\phi_{\pi}
and
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}
share no roots over
\overline{K}
, as the subgroups
E[\pi]
and
E[\overline{\pi}]
have trivial intersection.

Now we wish to show that there are no non-trivial factors of
\phi_{p}/(\phi_{\pi}\cdot\phi_{\overline{\pi}})
. Suppose that there is such a factor
h
, fix one root
x_{0}
of
h
, and let
L^{\prime}/K
be the extension obtained by adjoining
x_{0}
as well as some
y_{0}
such that
P=(x_{0},y_{0})
is a point in
E[p]
. This extension then has degree less than
(p-1)^{2}=[K(E[p]):K]
; in particular,
E[p](L^{\prime})
is a one-dimensional subspace of
E[p]
. Furthermore,
E[p](L^{\prime})
must intersect both
E[\pi]
and
E[\overline{\pi}]
trivially, as
L^{\prime}
was formed by adjoining some
P\in E[p]\setminus(E[\pi]\cup E[\overline{\pi}])
.

We know that
p=\pi\cdot\overline{\pi}
splits in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Further, we have by
subsection 3.4
that
\pi
is totally ramified in
K(E[\pi])/K
. The same Lemma gives that
\overline{\pi}
is unramified in
K(E[\pi])/K
, and in fact uniqueness in
subsection 3.1
(together with an argument similar to that in the proof of
subsection 4.3
below) gives that
\overline{\pi}
splits completely in this extension. Thus, in
K(E[p])=K(E[\pi],E[\overline{\pi}])/K
, the places corresponding to
\pi
and
\overline{\pi}
each factor into
(p-1)^{\text{st}}
powers of
p-1
distinct prime ideals.

Now, consider the splitting behavior of
L^{\prime}/K
at
\pi
and
\overline{\pi}
. Since
[L^{\prime}:K]<(p-1)^{2}
, it cannot have the same behavior as described for
K(E[\pi])/K
above. In particular, we have one of the following:

(a)

\pi
has less than
p-1
distinct places lying above it,

(b)

the places above
\overline{\pi}
are ramified of degree less than
p-1
,

(c)

\overline{\pi}
has less than
p-1
distinct places lying above it, or

(d)

the places above
\pi
are ramified of degree less than
p-1
.

Without loss of generality, assume that either (a) or (b) holds, and consider the extension
L^{\prime}(E[\pi])/K
. Then whichever of (a) and (b) held before still holds for this new extension, so it cannot be that
L^{\prime}(E[\pi])=K(E[p])
. However,
E[p](L(E[\pi]))
now contains two linearly independent points, so must be all of
E[p]
, a contradiction. ∎

If
a
is such that
(\mathcal{E}_{a},v\mid p)
is admissible for
K
, then
subsection 4.1
allows for the computation of the kernel polynomial
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}
associated to an arbitrary fiber
\mathcal{E}_{a}
as follows. First, compute the
p^{\text{th}}
division polynomial of
E=E_{1}
as
\phi_{p}\in K[x]
. Factoring
\phi_{p}
over
K
into three irreducibles as above, two of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
and one of degree
\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{2}
. The two factors of degree
\frac{p-1}{2}
then correspond to
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}
and
\phi_{\pi}
. Homogenizing with the appropriate weight, we get the specializations of
\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}
and
\Phi_{\pi}
to
\mathcal{E}_{a}
, and these are distinguished by the presence and absence respectively of roots modulo
\pi
.

This algorithm has been implemented in the accompanying
Sage notebook
. The computational bottleneck lies in factoring the polynomial
\phi_{p}(x)
, as this polynomial has degree
O(p^{2})
; this step already takes a couple minutes for
p\approx 100
on a laptop. If one wanted to speed this algorithm up to access higher values of
p
, one approach would be to explicitly describe the CM action, then use an implementation of point addition in Jacobian coordinates to directly compute the endomorphism
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}
as applied to a generic point
P=(x,y)
. The main issue with this is that explicit formulas for CM beyond

can get rather messy, and do not seem to be widely available.

Once we have computed
\phi_{\overline{\pi}}(x,a)
, we may use it to characterize the
x
-coordinates of the
\overline{\pi}
-torsion points of
E_{u}
with a simple Taylor expansion:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\phi=\Phi_{\overline{\pi}}
. Suppose that
\phi(x,a)=0
for some
x,a\in\mathcal{O}_{u}
, and write
x=x_{0}+x_{1}p+O(p^{2})
and
a=a_{0}+a_{1}p+O(p^{2})
. Then
x_{1}
is uniquely characterized modulo
p
as satisfying the equation

\frac{\phi(x_{0},a_{0})}{p}+x_{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}(x_{0},a_{0})+a_{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial a}(x_{0},a_{0})\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Proof.

Consider the Taylor expansion of
\phi
in powers of
p
around the point
(x_{0},a_{0})
, given by

\displaystyle\phi(s,t)=\phi(x_{0},a_{0})+\left((s-x_{0})\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}(x_{0},a_{0})+(t-a_{0})\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x_{0},a_{0})\right)p+O(p^{2}).

Reducing the equation
\phi(x,a)=0
modulo
p
, we get that
\phi(x_{0},a_{0})
must be divisible by
p
, and substituting
(x,a)
into the above equation gives that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\phi(x_{0},a_{0})}{p}+x_{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}(x_{0},a_{0})+a_{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x_{0},a_{0})\right)p\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}},

which is equivalent to the desired condition. That

is uniquely characterized by this equation is guaranteed by
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial s}(x_{0},a_{0})
being non-zero modulo

, which holds by distinctness of the
\overline{\pi}
-torsion
x
-coordinates modulo

.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

2.

\{A_{w},P_{w}\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}\neq 0\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(b_{0},a_{0})}{p}+b_{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}(b_{0},a_{0})+a_{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial a}(b_{0},a_{0})\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

(10)

Proof.

By

gives that

if and only if Equation (

Theorem 4.9
.

Suppose that
p\mid|\overline{E_{v}}(\mathbb{F}_{v})|
without equality, so

,

,

1.

If

.

2.

If
u(x(P))=0
, write

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}b_{0}&1&2&3&4\\
\hline\cr\varepsilon(b_{0})&3&4&3&1.\\
\end{array}

3.

If
u(x(P))>0
, then
\{A_{w},P_{w}\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}\neq 0\pmod{p}

u(x(P))=2

Proof.

The case of

Theorem 4.8

If
u(x(P))>0
, then

restricts to the 2-torsion point

by

. Now,

implies that
2u(y(P))=u(x(P))
by taking valuations in the equation defining
E
, and doing the same to the point-doubling formula gives that
u(x(2P))=-u(x(P))
, from which the claim follows.

Now assume that
u(x(P))=0
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\pi
is of the form
\pi=2\pm i
. Using Sage, we compute
\phi=\phi_{\overline{\pi}}=\overline{\pi}x^{2}\mp ai
. Plugging this into
subsection 4.1
and simplifying, we see that if a point
Q
is such that
\widehat{Q}=Q+T
for some non-zero

, then
\{A_{w},Q_{w}\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}=0\pmod{p}
if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\pi}c_{0}^{2}\mp 3i}{p}-2c_{1}c_{0}+2a_{1}\equiv 0\pmod{p},

where
x(Q)=c_{0}+c_{1}p+O(p^{2})
. (Note that the ambiguity in sign in the first term is resolved by the relation
i\equiv\mp 2\pmod{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{u}
.) Since the roots of
\phi
in
F_{u}
are equivalent to either 1 or 4 modulo
p
, we see that if
b_{0}=1,4
we may apply this criterion directly, solving for
b_{1}
as

\displaystyle b_{1}\equiv b_{0}a_{1}+\frac{b_{0}(\overline{\pi}b_{0}^{2}\mp 3i)}{2p}\pmod{p}

(note that both of these values of
b_{0}
are their own inverses modulo
p
).

In the case that
b_{0}=2,3
,
subsection 3.1
provides that such a
T
exists for
2P
, and we compute

\displaystyle x(2P)=\left(\frac{3x(P)^{2}+a}{2y(P)}\right)^{2}-2x(P)\equiv-2x(P)\pmod{p};

since

, the numerator of the first term vanishes modulo
p
. Now, the above equivalence simplifies to

\displaystyle b_{1}\equiv b_{0}a_{1}+\frac{b_{0}(4\overline{\pi}b_{0}^{2}\mp 3i)}{4p}\pmod{p},

and evaluating the final term of this and our previous equivalence at
b_{0}
defines the given function
\varepsilon
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Fix
p
and
K
, and suppose we have a family of tuples
(E_{t},v_{t}\mid p)
admissible for

with associated fields

in which

splits completely and places

of

are sampled randomly in such a way that their

-coordinates are uniformly distributed modulo

in

across the possible residues, one should expect that

of these points may be used to construct locally non-trivial symbols modulo

as above.

While proving this uniformity in any particular case seems daunting, it does offer an explanation for the data collected in
[

. There, the authors fixed

and

and

with

Proof.

1.

2.

Now consider the case when

5
Infinite families of non-trivial local-to-global principles

Let
(E,v\mid p)
be admissible for
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
, and let
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
an irreducible corresponding to
v
. The goal of this section is to apply
subsection 4.3
to construct certain infinite families of extensions
L/K(E[\pi])
for which the complex (
3
) for
X=(E\times E)_{L}
is exact with non-zero middle term. Note that for
F/K
of degree 2 in which
v
splits completely,
subsection 3.4
tells us that for
L=F(E[\pi])
and
X=(E\times E)_{L}
, exactness of (
3
) is shown by finding two global symbols
w_{1},w_{2}\in K_{2}(L;E)
, the images of which are
\mathbb{Z}/p
-linearly independent in

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)/p^{n}\cong\prod_{w\mid v}K_{2}(L_{w};E_{w})/p\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2}.

Our procedure for doing so is as follows. We first look for a point of infinite order
P\in E(K)
meeting the criteria of
Theorem 4.8
; if
E
has positive rank over
K
, then heuristically a point of infinite order should have a high probability (about
\frac{p-1}{p}
) of doing so. Once one such point has been found, we may obtain a second by adjoining a naïve quadratic point
Q
with
x
-coordinate meeting the criteria of
subsection 4.3
. Letting
F=K(y(Q))
and
L=F(E[\pi])
, and fixing a point
A\in E[\pi]
we obtain two global symbols

\displaystyle\{A,P\}_{L/L}\quad\text{and}\quad\{A,Q\}_{L/L}

which are locally non-trivial modulo
p
at both places of
L
lying above
v
. As
subsection 4.3
holds for an open subset of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
(with respect to the topology induced by
v
), varying
x(Q)
in our construction yields an infinite family of fields
L
as desired.

All that remains is to check that the two symbols we construct are in fact
\mathbb{Z}/p
-linearly independent.

Proposition 5.1
.

Write
E:y^{2}=f(x)
, and let
F=K(\sqrt{f(b)})
for some
b\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
satisfying the criteria of
subsection 4.3
. Assume that
F\neq K
and that
v
splits in
F/K
. Let
P\in E(K[\pi])
such that
P
pairs non-trivially modulo
p
with
A
at some place of
K(E[\pi])
lying above
v
. Then

\displaystyle c_{P}=\Delta(\{A,P\}_{L/L})\quad\text{and}\quad c_{Q}=\Delta(\{A,Q\}_{L/L})

are
\mathbb{Z}/p
-linearly independent.

Proof.

Let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
the places of
F
lying above
v
, and note that since
v
splits completely in
F/K
we have equality of local fields
F_{u_{1}}=K_{v}=F_{u_{2}}
. Since
F/K
is Galois with
\Gal(F/K)
generated by
\sigma:\sqrt{f(b)}\mapsto-\sqrt{f(b)}
, the embeddings
\iota_{u_{1}},\iota_{u_{2}}:F\hookrightarrow K_{v}
corresponding to the places
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are related by precomposition by
\sigma
.

Now let
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
the places of
L
lying over
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
respectively. Letting
\varpi
the unique place of
L^{\prime}=K(E[\pi])
over
v
, an analogous argument to the above shows that
L_{w_{1}}=L^{\prime}_{\varpi}=L_{w_{2}}
, and again the two embeddings
\iota_{w_{1}},\iota_{w_{2}}:L\hookrightarrow L^{\prime}_{\varpi}
are related by precomposition by the
L^{\prime}
-automorphism of
L
given by
\sigma:\sqrt{f(b)}\mapsto-\sqrt{f(b)}
.

Suppose for contradiction that
c_{P}
and
c_{Q}
are not
\mathbb{Z}/p
-linearly independent. Since both vectors are assumed non-zero, this is equivalent to the statement that there is some
n\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
such that
nc_{P}+c_{Q}=0
. Bilinearity of symbols implies that for both places
w\mid v
of
L
, we have

\displaystyle n\left\{A_{w},P_{w}\right\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}+\left\{A_{w},Q_{w}\right\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}=\left\{A_{w},nP_{w}+Q_{w}\right\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}\equiv 0

modulo
p
. Writing the above symbols in
L^{\prime}_{\varpi}
, we note that since
A
and
P
are both defined over
L^{\prime}
we have that
A_{w_{1}}=A_{\varpi}=A_{w_{2}}
and
P_{w_{1}}=P_{\varpi}=P_{w_{2}}
, whereas
Q_{w_{1}}
and
Q_{w_{2}}
are related by
\sigma
. Since
Q=(b,\sqrt{(f(b))})
we see that
Q_{w_{2}}=-Q_{w_{1}}
in
L^{\prime}_{\varpi}
. Thus, setting
w=w_{1}
we see that
n
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{A_{w},nP_{w}+Q_{w}\right\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}\text{ and }\left\{A_{w},nP_{w}-Q_{w}\right\}_{L_{w}/L_{w}}

both being trivial modulo
p
. Using
subsection 3.3
, we see that both
\widehat{P_{w}}
and
\widehat{Q_{w}}
lie in
\mathcal{D}^{p-1}_{L_{w}}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{p}_{L_{w}}
, and that the above conditions on
n
imply that both
n\widehat{P_{w}}+\widehat{Q_{w}}
and
n\widehat{P_{w}}-\widehat{Q_{w}}
lie in
\mathcal{D}^{p}_{L_{w}}
. But since
\mathcal{D}^{p-1}_{L_{w}}/\mathcal{D}^{p}_{L_{w}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{w}
has odd characteristic, taking the difference of the above two elements gives a contradiction
2\widehat{Q_{w}}\in\mathcal{D}^{p}_{L_{w}}
.
∎

Summarizing, we have shown the following:

(11)

is exact for

The last thing to be checked is that there are infinitely many

and
subsection 4.3

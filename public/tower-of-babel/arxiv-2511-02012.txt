2.1.
Uniformly embedded submanifold

In this subsection, we recall some properties of manifolds with bounded geometry and their uniformly embedded submanifolds. These results can be found in Chapter 2 of

Definition 2.1

(Manifold with bounded geometry)
.

A manifold
(M,g)
is a manifold with bounded geometry, if:

1. The injectivity radius of
M
is positive.

2. The sectional curvature of
M
and its derivative of any order are uniformly bounded.

There is a
\delta(M)>0
so that the coordinate charts given by the exponential map at

denote the Riemannian distance in
(M,g)

c|(\exp^{M}_{x})^{-1}(x_{1})-(\exp^{M}_{x})^{-1}(x_{2})|<d_{g}(x_{1},x_{2})<C|(\exp^{M}_{x})^{-1}(x_{1})-(\exp^{M}_{x})^{-1}(x_{2})|
, and the constants

and

are independent of

. Finally, all derivatives of the transition maps
from these coordinates are also uniformly bounded.

Definition 2.2

(Uniformly embedded submanifold)
.

Let

be an embedding of

into the Riemannian manifold

of bounded geometry. For

1. the connected component

If

(34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}||R_{\Sigma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}(P)f||_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}&\lesssim||R_{\Sigma}\sigma_{\lambda}f||_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}\\
&\lesssim||\mathcal{A}\sigma_{\lambda}f||_{(\ell^{q}_{j},L^{q}(A_{j}))}\\
&\lesssim\log(\lambda)^{1/2}\lambda^{\mu(q)}\left\|||\psi_{j}f||_{L^{2}(B_{j})}\right\|_{\ell_{j}^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\\

&\lesssim\log(\lambda)^{1/2}\lambda^{\mu(q)}||f||_{L^{2}(M)},\end{split}

if

.
Similarly, by (

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}(P)||_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim||R_{\Sigma}\sigma_{\lambda}||_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\mu(q)},

Proof of Theorem

Now, we use the unit band estimate to prove the

By an

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\log\lambda^{-1}]}||_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\sim||R_{\Sigma}\rho_{\lambda}||_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}.

By a
TT^{*}
argument, to prove Theorem

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}(\rho_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}^{*})R_{\Sigma}^{*}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{2\mu(q)}/\log\lambda.

\displaystyle R_{\Sigma}(\rho_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}^{*})R_{\Sigma}^{*}(f)(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\int\frac{1}{T}\cos(tP)(x,y)\hat{\Psi}\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)e^{i\lambda t}f(y)dtdy.

Since

2.3.1.
Global estimate

We obtain the global estimate via interpolation.
We first obtain an
L^{2}
estimate following the argument of Theorem 5.1 in
[
7
]
. We sketch the proof for completeness. First, define

to be the Fourier transform of
(1-\Phi(\cdot))\hat{\Psi}\left(\frac{\cdot}{T}\right)

for any

. By the unit band estimate (

(42)

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R_{\Sigma}^{*}||_{L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{2\mu(2)},&\text{if }k\neq n-2,\\
\lambda^{2\mu(2)}\log(\lambda),&\text{if }k=n-2.\end{cases}

From Lemma 3.6 in
[

]

(43)

\displaystyle|G_{\lambda}(x,y)|\lesssim{\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{c_{M}T}}

for some

Thus,

(44)

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R_{\Sigma}^{*}||_{L^{1}(\Sigma)\to L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\lesssim{\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{c_{M}T}}.

Now, we interpolate (

If

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R_{\Sigma}^{*}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim{\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}+\frac{n-1-2k}{q}}}e^{c_{M}T(1-\frac{2}{q})}.

(46)

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\Sigma}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}=O(\lambda^{2\mu(q)}T^{-1}).

(47)

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R_{\Sigma}^{*}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim{\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n-2}{q}}}e^{c_{M}T(1-\frac{2}{q})}.

\displaystyle||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\Sigma}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}=O(\lambda^{2\mu(q)}T^{-1}).

2.3.2.
Local estimate

In this subsection, we consider the operator with kernel

By the unit band estimate (

) and a

||R_{\Sigma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}(P)(R_{\Sigma})^{*}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)\to L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{2\mu(q)},

for

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}||R_{\Sigma}\rho_{\lambda}\rho^{*}_{\lambda}(P)R_{\Sigma}^{*}f||_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}&\lesssim||R_{\Sigma}G_{\lambda}R_{\Sigma}^{*}f||_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}+||R_{\Sigma}L_{\lambda}R_{\Sigma}^{*}f||_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{2\mu(q)}/\log\lambda||f||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma)}.\end{split}

3.
Even asymptotically hyperbolic surface

Let
(M,g)
be an even asymptotically hyperbolic surface with bounded geometry and curvature pinched below 0.
We follow
[
6
]
to decompose

(51)

\displaystyle M=M_{tr}\cup M_{\infty},

such that
M_{tr}
is compact, and
M_{\infty}
asymptotically agrees with a background manifold
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
, which satisfies favorable spectral projection estimates.

Let
S^{*}M
be the cosphere bundle of
M
and denote the principle symbol of
P
by
p(x,\xi)
. Let
(x(t),\xi(t))=e^{tH_{p}}(x,\xi)
, where
e^{tH_{p}}
denote the geodesic flow on the cotangent bundle. Define

(52)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm}:=\{(x,\xi)\in S^{*}M:x(t)\not\to\infty\text{ as }t\to\pm\infty\}.

Define
\pi:S^{*}M\to M
with
\pi(x,\xi)=x.
The trapped set of
M
is

(53)

\displaystyle\pi(\Gamma_{+}\cap\Gamma_{-}).

We require
M_{tr}
to be a compact subset of
M
that
contains a neighborhood of the trapped set.

We could construct an asymptotically hyperbolic, simply connected manifold with negative curvature and no resonance at the bottom of the spectrum,
\tilde{M}
, as in
[
6
]
, such that if
M_{\infty}
is appropriately defined, then the metric,
g
, in
M_{\infty}
agrees with the metric,
\tilde{g}
, in
\tilde{M}.

The metric of an asymptotically hyperbolic surface near the boundary is given by

(54)

\displaystyle 4\frac{dr^{2}+s(r,\theta)d\theta^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{2}},

where
s\in C^{\infty}
and
s(1,\theta)=1.
Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-1,1))

with
\chi=1
in
(-1/2,1/2)
, then we can define the metric on
\tilde{M}
as

(55)

\displaystyle 4\frac{dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}+\chi(R(1-r))4\frac{(s(r,\theta)-r^{2})d\theta^{2}}{(1-r^{2})^{2}},

where
R
is a large enough constant. Then, the metric of
\tilde{M}
agrees with the metric of
M
when
r\geq 1-(2R)^{-1}
. Furthermore, note that
|s(r,\theta)-r^{2}|\leq R^{-1}
in the support of
\chi(R(1-r))
.
By choosing
R
sufficiently large, the Gaussian curvature of
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
is bounded between
-3/2
and
-1/2
. Hence,
\tilde{M}
is a simply connected
manifold with curvature pinched below 0 and no conjugate points. Finally,
\tilde{M}
has no resonance at the bottom of the spectrum by Lemma 2.3 of
[
6
]
. We denote
\tilde{P}=\sqrt{-\tilde{\Delta}}
, where
\tilde{\Delta}
is the Laplacian operator on
\tilde{M}.

Notice that being a geodesic is a local property. Thus, if we denote a connected component of
\gamma
in
M_{\infty}
by
\gamma_{1}

\tilde{\gamma}_{1}\in\tilde{M}
, such that
\gamma_{1}
agrees with
\tilde{\gamma}_{1}

\tilde{M}
.

In this section, we first prove a spectral projection estimate of

with an arbitrarily small spectral window on

[

]
. Then, we prove a subcritical

following
[
2
]
and finally use these two estimates to prove Theorem
2
.

3.1.
Asymptotically hyperbolic and simply connected surface

We prove that the background manifold
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
satisfies a sharp spectral projection estimate for an arbitrarily small spectral window.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
be an asymptotically hyperbolic and simply connected surface with curvature pinched below 0. For
\lambda\geq 1
,
\eta\in(0,1]
,
q\geq 2
and
\tilde{\gamma}\subset\tilde{M}
being a geodesic,

(56)

\displaystyle||R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\eta]}(\tilde{P})||_{L^{2}(\tilde{M})\to L^{q}(\tilde{\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{\mu(q)}\eta^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}(\tilde{P})
, where
\delta_{\lambda}
denotes the Dirac-Delta function. Notice that the spectral measure of
\tilde{P}
is
{\mathbf{P}}_{\lambda}d\lambda
. Then,

(57)

\displaystyle R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\eta]}(\tilde{P})(R_{\tilde{\gamma}})^{*}=\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\eta}R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}_{\kappa}(R_{\tilde{\gamma}})^{*}d\kappa.

We aim to show

(58)

\displaystyle||R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}(R_{\tilde{\gamma}})^{*}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\tilde{\gamma}})\to L^{q}({\tilde{\gamma}})}\lesssim\lambda^{2\mu(q)}.

We recall the kernel estimate by Chen and Hassell. By Theorem 5 of
[
8
]
,
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
can be represented by a convolution operator with kernel
p_{\lambda}(x,y)
, such that

(59)

\displaystyle|{p}_{\lambda}(x,y)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda{(1+\lambda d_{\tilde{g}}(x,y))^{-1/2}},&\text{if }d_{\tilde{g}}(x,y)<1,\\
\lambda^{1/2}e^{-d_{\tilde{g}}(x,y)/2},&\text{if }d_{\tilde{g}}(x,y)\geq 1.\end{cases}

Let
d_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}
denote the distance on
\tilde{\gamma}
with induced metric from
\tilde{M}.
Fix
x\in{\tilde{\gamma}}
and notice that for any
y\in{\tilde{\gamma}}
, we have
d_{\tilde{g}}(x,y)=d_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}(x,y)
. Meanwhile, we define

(60)

\displaystyle p_{\lambda}(x,y)=p_{\lambda}(d_{\tilde{g}}(x,y)).

For
f\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\tilde{\gamma}),

(61)

\displaystyle R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}_{\kappa}(R_{\tilde{\gamma}})^{*}f=\int_{\tilde{\gamma}}p_{\kappa}(d_{\tilde{g}}(\cdot,y))f(y)dy.

By Young’s inequality, to prove (
58
), it suffices to show

\sup_{x}||p_{\lambda}(x,y)||_{L^{q/2}_{y}({\tilde{\gamma}})}\lesssim\lambda^{\mu(q)}.

Assume
q\neq 4
, we use (
59
) to compute

\displaystyle||p_{\lambda}(x,y)||_{L^{q/2}_{y}({\tilde{\gamma}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{-\lambda^{-1}}^{\lambda^{-1}}|p_{\lambda}(r)|^{q/2}dr\right)^{2/q}+\left(\int_{\lambda^{-1}<|r|<1}|p_{\lambda}(r)|^{q/2}dr\right)^{2/q}+\left(\int_{|r|>1}|p_{\lambda}(r)|^{q/2}dr\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1-2/q}+\left(\int_{\lambda^{-1}<|r|<1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{r}\right)^{q/4}\right)^{2/q}+\lambda^{1/2}\left(\int_{|r|>1}\left(e^{-r/2}\right)^{q/2}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1-2/q}+\left(\left.\lambda^{q/4}r^{-q/4+1}\right|^{\lambda^{-1}}_{1}\right)^{2/q}+\lambda^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1-2/q}+\lambda^{1/2}.

Thus,

\sup_{x}||p_{\lambda}(x,y)||_{L^{q/2}_{y}({\tilde{\gamma}})}\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{1/2}&\text{if }q<4,\\
\lambda^{1-2/q}&\text{if }q>4.\end{cases}

We deal with the
q=4
case using the Hardy-Littlewood fractional integral theorem.
By (
59
) and assuming
{\tilde{\gamma}}
is parametrized by arc length, we have for all
t
and
s\in\mathbb{R},

|p_{\lambda}({\tilde{\gamma}}(t),{\tilde{\gamma}}(s))|\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

By the Hardy-Littlewood fractional integral theorem, we obtain

(62)

\displaystyle\left(\int^{-\infty}_{\infty}\left|\int^{-\infty}_{\infty}|p_{\lambda}({\tilde{\gamma}}(t),{\tilde{\gamma}}(s))h({\tilde{\gamma}}(s))|ds\right|^{4}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}||h||_{L^{4/3}({\tilde{\gamma}})}.

Thus,

||R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}(R_{\tilde{\gamma}})^{*}||_{L^{4/3}({\tilde{\gamma}})\to L^{4}({\tilde{\gamma}})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}.

By Young’s inequality, for any
q\neq 4,

(63)

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda+\eta}\left(\int_{\tilde{\gamma}}|{p}_{\kappa}(x,y)|^{q/2}dy\right)^{2/q}d\kappa\lesssim\lambda^{2\mu(q)}\eta.

Thus,

(64)

\displaystyle||R_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\eta]}(\tilde{P})R_{\tilde{\gamma}}^{*}||_{L^{4/3}({\tilde{\gamma}})\to L^{4}({\tilde{\gamma}})}\lesssim\lambda^{2\mu(4)}\eta.

∎

3.2.
Critical
\log
-scaled estimates

Notice that Theorem
1
only implies the sharp
\log
-scaled spectral projection estimates from

, where

is a 2-dimensional manifold with bounded geometry and negative curvature, to the

.
We now prove the

to

We shall see later that, in order to prove Theorem

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian surface with curvature pinched below 0 and bounded geometry. Let
\gamma
be a fixed compact geodesic segment on

. Then for
\lambda\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
,

(65)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\log\lambda^{-1}]}(P)||_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{q}(\gamma)}\lesssim\lambda^{1/4}\log\lambda^{-1/2}.

Proof.

Recall
T\sim\log\lambda
and
\rho_{\lambda}
as defined in (
36
). In addition,

(66)

\displaystyle R_{\gamma}(\rho_{\lambda}\rho_{\lambda}^{*})R^{*}_{\gamma}=R_{\gamma}L_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}+R_{\gamma}G_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}

with
L_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
as defined in (
41
) and (
40
).

First, a compact geodesic can be covered by a finite number of uniformly embedded geodesic segments. Therefore, we may construct coverings
\{A_{j}\}
of
\gamma
and
\{B_{j}\}
of a neighborhood of
\gamma
in
M
as described in Section 1. Then, we may appeal to the local results
of small time wave kernels as in page 8 of

to obtain

(67)

\displaystyle|L_{\lambda}(x,y)|\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}(\log\lambda)^{-1}|x-y|^{-1/2}

for any

Therefore, we have

(68)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}L_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}f||_{L^{4}({A_{j}})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}(\log\lambda)^{-1}||f||_{L^{4/3}(A_{j})},

for every
j,
and thus

(69)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}L_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}f||_{L^{4}({\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}(\log\lambda)^{-1}||f||_{L^{4/3}(\gamma)}.

We now slightly modify the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
2
]
to deal with the
R_{\gamma}G_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}
term. By the Cartan-Hadamard theorem,
(M,g)
has a universal cover,
(M^{\prime},g^{\prime})
, diffeomorephic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
\exp_{x}
for any
x\in M.
We lift
\gamma\in M
to
\gamma^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
. Let
\pi
be the covering map
\pi:M^{\prime}\to M.
Let
\Gamma
denote the set of deck transformations of
M^{\prime}
, such that

(70)

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\{\alpha:M^{\prime}\to M^{\prime},\alpha\circ\pi=\pi\}.

For
\tau>0,
define the geodesic tube about
\gamma^{\prime}
of radius
\tau,

(71)

\displaystyle T_{\tau}(\gamma^{\prime})=\{x\in M^{\prime}:d_{g^{\prime}}(x,\gamma^{\prime})\leq\tau\}.

Let
D
be a fundamental domain of
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{H}.
Define
\Gamma_{T_{\tau}(\gamma^{\prime})}\subset\Gamma
as

(72)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T_{\tau}(\gamma^{\prime})}=\{\alpha\in\Gamma:\alpha(D)\cap T_{\tau}(\gamma^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\}.

Let
P^{\prime}=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g^{\prime}}}
, where
\Delta_{g^{\prime}}
is the Laplacian operator on

Define
G_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
as the operator with kernel

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{1}{T}\int^{T}_{-T}(1-\beta(t))\hat{\rho}(t/T)e^{-\lambda\tau}(\cos(tP^{\prime}))(x,\alpha y)dt.

We may write

(73)

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}=G^{tube}_{\lambda}+G^{osc}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{T_{\tau}}(\gamma^{\prime})}G^{\alpha}_{\lambda}+\sum_{\alpha\notin G_{T_{\tau}}(\gamma^{\prime})}G^{\alpha}_{\lambda}.

When
\alpha=Id,
by (4.14) of
[
7
]
, we have

(74)

\displaystyle K^{Id}(x,y)\lesssim T^{-1}\lambda^{1/2}d_{g^{\prime}}(x,y)^{-1/2}.

By Hardy-Littlewood fractional integral theorem and since

is a geodesic, we have

(75)

\displaystyle\left\|\int K^{Id}(\cdot,y)f(y)dy\right\|_{L^{4}(\gamma^{\prime})}\lesssim||f||_{L^{4/3}(\gamma^{\prime})}.

Now, assume
\alpha\neq Id.
Following Lemma 3.1 of
[
9
]
, we can write

(76)

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}(x,y)=\omega(x,\alpha y)\sum_{\pm}b_{\pm}(T,\lambda,d_{g^{\prime}}(x,\alpha y))e^{\pm i\lambda d_{g^{\prime}}(x,\alpha y)}+R(x,\alpha y),

where

for some

Since

has curvature pinched below 0, by the Gunther comparison theorem, the volume of a geodesic ball with radius

in
M
is larger than

for some

Thus, by the line above (2.3.7) of
[

]

(77)

\displaystyle|\omega(x,\alpha y)|\lesssim e^{-cd_{g^{\prime}}(x,\alpha y)},

for some

Meanwhile, by (3.15) of
[
9
]

(78)

\displaystyle|b_{\pm}(T,\lambda,d_{g^{\prime}}(x,\alpha y))|\lesssim T^{-1}\lambda^{1/2}d_{g^{\prime}}(x,\alpha y)^{-1/2}.

By finite speed of propagation,
K^{\alpha}(x,y)
vanishes if

So, for any fixed
x,y
and
\tau
,

(79)

\displaystyle\#\{\alpha\in\Gamma:K(x,\alpha y)\neq 0\text{ and }\alpha\in\Gamma_{T_{\tau}}(\gamma^{\prime})\}=O(T).

Thus,

(80)

\displaystyle|\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{T_{\tau}}(\gamma^{\prime}),\alpha\neq Id}K^{\alpha}(x,y)|\lesssim\sum_{0\leq 2^{j}\leq T}T^{-1}\lambda^{1/2}e^{-c2^{j}}2^{j}2^{-j/2}\lesssim T^{-1}\lambda^{1/2}.

Since
\gamma
is compact, this implies

(81)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}G^{tube}_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}f||_{L^{4}({\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}(\log\lambda)^{-1}||f||_{L^{4/3}(\gamma)}.

Finally, by Section 2.1 of
[
2
]
, for some

(82)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}G^{osc}_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}f||_{L^{2}({\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/4}e^{CT}||f||_{L^{2}(\gamma)}.

By choosing

with

small enough and interpolating with (

(83)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}G^{osc}_{\lambda}R^{*}_{\gamma}f||_{L^{4}({\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2-\epsilon}||f||_{L^{4/3}(\gamma)},

for some

Thus, for

,

(84)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+(\log\lambda)^{-1}]}(P)f||_{L^{4}({\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/4}(\log\lambda)^{-1/2}||f||_{L^{2}(M)}.

Since
\gamma
is compact, we also have

(85)

\displaystyle||R_{\gamma}\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda,\lambda+(\log\lambda)^{-1}]}(P)f||_{L^{q}({\gamma})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/4}(\log\lambda)^{-1/2}||f||_{L^{2}(M)}

for all

by Hölder’s inequality.
∎

3.3.

Example 4.1

Define

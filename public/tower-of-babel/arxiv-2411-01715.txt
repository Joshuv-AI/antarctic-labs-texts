The role of intermediate Δ ​ Δ \Delta\Delta states in nucleon-nucleon scattering in the large- N c N_{c} and unitary limits, and Δ ​ Δ \Delta\Delta and Ω ​ Ω \Omega\Omega scattering
Thomas R. Richardson, Matthias R. Schindler, Roxanne P. Springer

Abstract
We explore potential explanations for why using large- N c N_{c} ( N c N_{c} is the number of colors) scaling to determine the relative size of few-nucleon low-energy operators agrees with experiment even when dynamical Δ \Delta ’s are not explicitly included. Given that the large- N c N_{c} analysis is predicated on the nucleons and Δ \Delta ’s being degenerate, this is a curious result. We show that for purely S S -wave interactions the relationships dictated by large- N c N_{c} scaling are unaffected whether the Δ \Delta is included or not. In the case of higher partial waves that do not mix with S S -waves, the impact of the Δ \Delta is perturbative, which makes the agreement with naive ( Δ \Delta -less) large- N c N_{c} ordering unsurprising. For higher partial waves that mix with S S -waves, the nucleon and Δ \Delta would need to decouple to get agreement with naive large- N c N_{c} ordering. We find all N ​ N N\!N , Δ ​ N \Delta N , and Δ ​ Δ \Delta\Delta low energy coefficients for leading-order baryon-baryon scattering in Δ \Delta -full pionless effective field theory in terms of the two independent parameters dictated by the SU( 2 ​ F 2F ) spin-flavor symmetry that arises in the N c N_{c} → ∞ \rightarrow\infty limit. Because of recent lattice QCD results and experimental interest, we extend our analysis to the three-flavor case to study Ω ​ Ω \Omega\Omega scattering. We show that in the unitary limit (where scattering lengths become infinite) one of the two SU( 2 ​ F 2F ) parameters is driven to zero, resulting in enhanced symmetries, which agree with those found in spin-1/2 entanglement studies.

Introduction

One way to connect interactions between nucleons to the underlying theory of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is to use effective field theories (EFTs).
The EFTs are often cast as expansions in small quantities, such as low momenta and, if pions are included as degrees of freedom, the light quark masses, divided by the breakdown scale of the EFT.
The utility of the EFTs can be augmented by considering the consequences of the limiting cases of approximate symmetries of nature.

IV
Spin-isospin exchange symmetry in
S
-wave interactions

Reference
[
17
]
obtained the equality of the
N\!N
LECs in the
{{}^{1}\!S_{0}}
and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
channel in the large-
N_{c}
limit by imposing an SU(
2F
) symmetry on EFT
{}_{\pi\hskip-3.27773pt/}
without explicit
\Delta
degrees of freedom. In the following we analyze the consequences of this SU(
2F
) symmetry for EFT
{}_{\pi\hskip-3.27773pt/}
including
\Delta
’s.
As noted in Ref.
[
17
]
, in the SU(4)-symmetric limit all
N\!N
,
N\!\Delta
, and
\Delta\Delta
LECs are determined in terms of just two parameters.
These relationships can be used to compare
N\!N
scattering in the two
S
-wave channels in the theory with
\Delta\Delta
intermediate states.
To pursue this we will need not only the expressions for spin-1/2 particles provided in Ref.
[
17
]
, but also the expressions for
N\!\Delta
and
\Delta\Delta
LECs in terms of the two SU(4) parameters.
We will return to this framework in Sec.
VI
. However, there is a more direct approach for analyzing
S
-wave scattering.

In addition to SU(4), the Lagrangian of Eq.
1
is invariant under an exchange symmetry of spin and isospin degrees of freedom (or flip symmetry for short).
5
5

5

This use of the flip symmetry was proposed by M. Wise in 2016, R.P. Springer gave a talk on the applications to few nucleon systems at the May 9, 2017 Michigan State NSCL seminar on “Large N constraints on pionless EFT: applications to few bodies;
a window into QCD” and on Nov. 14, 2019 at the University of Maryland. Further applications were discussed in Ref.
[
38
]
.

Application of the flip symmetry operation
F
(with
F^{\dagger}=F^{-1}=F
) to Eq.
2
changes the lowercase Latin superscripts (isospin labels) to Greek superscripts and the Greek subscripts (spin labels) to lowercase Latin subscripts, yielding

F\Psi^{abc}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}F=\Psi^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{abc}\ \ .

(26)

In particular,

F\Delta^{abc}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}F=\Delta_{abc}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\ \ ,\ \ FN^{a}_{\alpha}F=N^{\alpha}_{a}\ \ ,\ \ {\rm and}\ \ F\epsilon^{ab}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}F=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ab}\,.

(27)

The
F
operation has determinant
-1
and so is
not
in the SU(4) spin-flavor group.
To obtain an element of SU(4) from
F
requires an additional U(1) operation.
The operators (using a labeling scheme that echoes that of their corresponding LECs) in the
S
-wave Lagrangians transform under
F
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{N\!N}^{(^{1}\!S_{0})}\overset{F}{\longleftrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{N\!N}^{(^{3}\!S_{1})}\,,\quad\mathcal{O}_{\Delta N}^{(^{1}\!S_{0})}\overset{F}{\longleftrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta N}^{(^{3}\!S_{1})}\,,\quad\mathcal{O}_{\Delta\Delta}^{(0,1)}\overset{F}{\longleftrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta\Delta}^{(1,0)}\,.

(28)

Imposing the
F
symmetry of Eq. (
1
) on Eq. (
11
) and its
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
partner, we see that not only is Eq. (
7
) recaptured, but we have the additional equalities in the
{{}^{1}\!S_{0}}
and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
channels:

\displaystyle C_{\Delta N}^{(^{1}\!S_{0})}

\displaystyle=C_{\Delta N}^{(^{3}\!S_{1})}\,,

(29)

\displaystyle C_{\Delta\Delta}^{(0,1)}

\displaystyle=C_{\Delta\Delta}^{(1,0)}\,.

(30)

(As we will see below, this flip symmetry holds even for higher spin and isospin
\Delta\Delta
channels.)
The equality of the
N\!N
LECs in Eq. (
7
) was already derived in Ref.
[
17
]
.
Because the LECs
{C}_{BB^{\prime}}
in the two
S
-wave channels are equal, the effective parameters
\widetilde{C}_{N\!N}^{({{}^{1}\!S_{0}})}
and
\widetilde{C}_{N\!N}^{({{}^{3}\!S_{1}})}
are also equal, and so are the expressions for the scattering amplitudes, so long as the same calculational scheme is used;

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N\!N,\text{LO}}^{({{}^{1}\!S_{0}})}=\mathcal{A}_{N\!N,\text{LO}}^{({{}^{3}\!S_{1}})}\,.

(31)

Recall that these are the amplitudes obtained for the theory
with
explicit
\Delta
degrees of freedom.
In the theory
without

\Delta
’s, the expression for the amplitude in the
s
channel is given by
[
36
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\text{LO},\not{\Delta}}^{(s)}=-\frac{C_{N\!N,\not{\Delta}}^{(s)}}{1-C_{N\!N,\not{\Delta}}^{(s)}I_{N}}\,,

(32)

where
C_{N\!N,\not{\Delta}}^{(s)}
is the LO
N\!N
LEC in the
\Delta
-less theory.
Since the amplitudes are related to observables, we can require that for a given channel the LO amplitudes in the theories with and without intermediate
\Delta
’s are identical, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N\!N,\text{LO}}^{(s)}=\mathcal{A}_{\text{LO},\not{\Delta}}^{(s)}\,.

(33)

The equality of the
{{}^{1}\!S_{0}}
and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
amplitudes of Eq. (
31

N_{c}
limit means that the amplitudes
\mathcal{A}_{\text{LO},\not{\Delta}}^{(s)}
in the
\Delta
-less theory are also identical, which in turn implies that the
{{}^{1}\!S_{0}}
and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
LECs in the
\Delta
-less theory have to be equal to each other,

\displaystyle C_{N\!N,\not{\Delta}}^{(^{1}\!S_{0})}=C_{N\!N,\not{\Delta}}^{(^{3}\!S_{1})}\,.

(34)

This is the same result as obtained by applying the large-
N_{c}
scaling rules directly to the
\Delta
-less theory.
This does not mean that the values of the LECs in the
\Delta
-full theory are the same as those in the
\Delta
-less theory; what it shows is that in each of these theories the spin-singlet and spin-triplet LECs remain equal to each other in the large-
N_{c}
limit.

This analysis also holds for
S
-wave interactions at the next order in the EFT
{}_{\pi\hskip-3.27773pt/}
power counting.
The corresponding Lagrangian contains two-derivative operators, but the indices of the derivatives are contracted with each other and
not
with the indices of spin operators; these derivative operators are unaffected by the application of the flip symmetry operation, leading to the equality of
{{}^{1}\!S_{0}}
and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
LECs associated with two-derivative operators.
As a result, the
N\!N

S
-wave scattering amplitudes in the two channels are again identical at this order. Requiring the amplitudes to match those in the
\Delta
-less theory, the two-derivative
S
-wave LECs in the
\Delta
-less theory are then also expected to be equal to each other.
This expectation is in agreement with the large-
N_{c}
analysis in the
\Delta
-less theory of Ref.
[
39
]
.
The equality of the
S
-wave LECs in the
\Delta
-full theory for
NN
,
NN\leftrightarrow\Delta\Delta
, and
\Delta\Delta
scattering follows from flip symmetry even at higher orders in the EFT
{}_{\pi\hskip-3.27773pt/}
expansion. However,
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
scattering amplitudes at higher order will include contributions from
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}\text{-}{{}^{3}\!D_{1}}
mixing, so that the arguments used at LO and NLO to analyze the LECs in the
\Delta
-less theory will not apply. We explore
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}\text{-}{{}^{3}\!D_{1}}
mixing further in Sec.
V.2
.

V
Higher partial waves

V.1
P
waves and other perturbative
N\!N
channels

Large-
N_{c}
analyses have also been applied to
N\!N
interactions beyond
S
waves.
As seen above, in EFT
{}_{\pi\hskip-3.27773pt/}
the
\Delta
degrees of freedom contribute to
N\!N
interactions through loop diagrams.
In the
\Delta
-less theory,
N\!N
interactions for

L\geq 1
are treated perturbatively; loop diagrams do not contribute at the same order as the first tree-level diagrams in these higher partial wave channels (see, e.g., Refs.
[
40
,
41
]
).
Exceptions include interactions that mix
S
-waves with higher partial waves, e.g.,
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}\text{-}{{}^{3}\!D_{1}}
mixing and parity-violating (PV)
S\text{-}P
interactions. This is discussed below.
It is possible that the power counting in the
\Delta
-full theory is different from that in the
\Delta
-less theory and that loop diagrams have to be resummed for some
L\geq 1
as well if
\Delta
degrees of freedom are included dynamically.
However, given that the theory that results from integrating out the
\Delta
is apparently perturbative, it seems likely that interactions with
L\geq 1
are also perturbative in the
\Delta
-full theory.
This means that at sufficiently low order in the EFT
{}_{\pi\hskip-3.27773pt/}
power counting, the inclusion of the
\Delta
does not significantly impact the
N\!N
LECs in
L\geq 1
waves.
In this case, we expect that the relationships among LECs for
L\geq 1
interactions in the
\Delta
-full theory are not dramatically changed from those within the
\Delta
-less theory.

Reference
[
39
]
considered several relationships between
S
-wave,
P
-wave, and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}\text{-}{{}^{3}\!D_{1}}
-mixing two-derivative LECs in the
\Delta
-less theory. A relationship that was not shown in Ref.
[
39
]
, but that follows from that analysis is

\displaystyle\frac{5C^{(^{3}\!P_{2})}+\frac{1}{2}\left(C^{(^{3}\!P_{1})}-C^{(^{1}\!P_{1})}\right)}{C^{(s)}_{2,\not{\Delta}}}=1,

(35)

which holds for
s={{}^{1}\!S_{0}}
and
{{}^{3}\!S_{1}}
with expected corrections of
O(1/N_{c})
, given the expected equality of the
S
-wave LECs at leading order.
This relationship is derived based on a large-
N_{c}
analysis in the
\Delta
-less theory. As discussed above, the
P
-wave LECs are not expected to be impacted significantly by the inclusion of the
\Delta
, so the notation for the
P
-wave LECs is not modified in the equation above.
However, the
S
-wave
N\!N
LECs in the theories with and without the
\Delta
can be very different. If the
\Delta
had a large impact on the
N\!N
LECs, it is not clear why relationships like that in Eq. (
35
) should hold. Using the values for the LECs given in Ref.
[
39
]
yields

\begin{split}\frac{5C^{(^{3}\!P_{2})}+\frac{1}{2}\left(C^{(^{3}\!P_{1})}-C^{(^{1}\!P_{1})}\right)}{C^{({{}^{3}\!S_{1}})}_{2,\not{\Delta}}(\mu=m_{\pi})}&\approx 1.1,\\
\frac{5C^{(^{3}\!P_{2})}+\frac{1}{2}\left(C^{(^{3}\!P_{1})}-C^{(^{1}\!P_{1})}\right)}{C^{({{}^{1}\!S_{0}})}_{2,\not{\Delta}}(\mu=m_{\pi})}&\approx 1.7\,.\end{split}

(36)

These ratios exhibit strong
\mu
dependence, reflecting the well-known strong
\mu
dependence of the
S
-wave LECs in the power divergence subtraction (PDS) scheme
[
35
]
used in Ref.
[
39
]
.
The relative closeness to the naive (
\Delta
-less) large-
N_{c}
expectation of Eq. (
35

\Delta
are relatively minor even for the
S
-wave
N\!N
LECs.

V.3
Parity-violating

The experimental situation is not as clear for PV observables as for parity-conserving (PC) observables because the signals are small and the experiments are difficult.
Some authors
[

1-Naphthamide
Authors: Mohamed Abdellatif Bensegueni, Rochdi Ghallab, Dorsaf Gheries, Asma Seghiri, Lamis Nouail
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the title compound, the dihedral angle between the naphthalene fused-ring system and the amide group is 42.18 (9)°. In the crystal, N—H⋯O hydrogen bonds link the mol­ecules into (100) sheets.

Structure description

The title compound (I), with mol­ecular formula C11H9NO, crystallizes in the monoclinic space group P21/c. The asymmetric unit contains one mol­ecule in which the dihedral angle between the C2–C11 fused ring system (r.m.s. deviation = 0.008 Å) and the C1/N1/O1 amide moiety is 42.18 (9)° (Fig. 1 ▸). The significant twist probably arises to minimize steric crowding. The geometrical parameters for the amide group [C1—N1 = 1.3249 (19) Å, C1—O1 = 1.245 (2) Å and angles O1—C1—N1, O1—C1—C2 and N1—C1—C2 close to 120°) support a conjugated amide system, as expected. A short intra­molecular C4—H4⋯O1 contact occurs.

In the extended structure of (I), the crystal cohesion is dominated by N11—H1A⋯O1 and N1—H1B⋯O1 hydrogen bonds (Table 1 ▸). The former generate inversion dimers and the latter link the dimers into (100) sheets (Fig. 2 ▸ and Table 1 ▸). The separations between the aromatic ring planes [shortest centroid–centroid separation = 4.799 (4) Å] are too large to support significant aromatic π–π stacking inter­actions.

Hirshfeld surface analysis and the associated two-dimensional fingerprint plots were used to qu­antify the inter­molecular contacts in the crystal of (I) (Fig. 3 ▸). The fingerprint plots show that H⋯H contacts make the largest contribution (42.2%), followed closely by C⋯H/H⋯C contacts (40.2%). The O⋯H/H⋯O contacts account for 14.20%, while all other contributions are less than 2.0%. These results show that the cohesion of the crystal is primarily governed by van der Waals contacts, with aromatic π–π stacking inter­actions not playing a significant role in establishing the supra­molecular architecture.

For the related structure of 9-anthramide, see Heller & Schmidt (1971 ▸).

Synthesis and crystallization

1-Naphthamide was obtained by aqueous hydrolysis of 1-naphtho­nitrile under heating. The starting nitrile was suspended in water and the mixture was heated until complete conversion to the amide was achieved. Colourless blocks of (I) were grown by slow evaporation of an aqueous solution of the compound at room temperature.

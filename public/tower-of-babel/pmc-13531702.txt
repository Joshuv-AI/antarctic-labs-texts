Development Timelines of Innovative Drugs in China: A 15‐Year Cross‐Sectional Analysis of Characteristics and Influencing Factors
Authors: Meimei Xu, Chenpeng Wang, Xinying An
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTimely development and approval of innovative drugs are essential for patient access and public health. China has implemented regulatory reforms, including priority review and conditional approval, to accelerate drug development. We conducted a cross‐sectional study of 232 innovative drugs, comprising chemical drugs, biologics, and traditional Chinese medicines (TCMs), approved by the National Medical Products Administration during 2010–2025. Development timelines for 227 drugs were analyzed using generalized linear models with a gamma distribution and log link to assess associations with drug type, regulatory pathways, orphan drug status, reform stage, indication, primary endpoint duration, and sample size. Median total development time was 6.94 years (IQR, 4.85–9.97), with biologics having the shortest duration (5.14 years) and TCMs the longest (10.22 years). Drug type and conditional approval were primary variables associated with total development time. Conditional approval was associated with shorter total development time among oncology drugs (relative mean ratio [RMR] = 0.66, p < 0.001), with a significant interaction by indication (RMR = 2.08, p < 0.001). Among drugs not receiving conditional approval, clinical development time was longer in later reform stages by up to 52%, whereas marketing application review time decreased in later reform stages overall, with a 64% reduction in Stage III (RMR = 0.36, p < 0.001). Priority review and conditional approval were associated with 26% and 28% reductions in review time, respectively (both p < 0.05). These findings suggest heterogeneous associations of China's regulatory reforms across pathways and therapeutic areas, consistent with differential contributions of administrative acceleration and evidentiary modification to development timelines.Keywords: clinical development time, drug regulatory reform, influencing factors, innovative drugs, regulatory pathways

Introduction

Timely development and regulatory approval of innovative drugs are essential for improving patient access to new therapies and advancing public health. However, development timelines may vary across therapeutic areas and regulatory contexts [1, 2, 3]. Over the past decade, China's drug regulatory system has undergone substantial reform [4], notably with the introduction of the priority review pathway in 2016 [5], followed by the establishment of breakthrough therapy designation and conditional approval pathway in 2020 [6]. These reforms were designed to accelerate patient access to innovative medicines while maintaining rigorous standards of safety and efficacy.

Previous studies in the United States [2, 7, 8] and Europe [1] have examined factors associated with the speed of drug development, including therapeutic area, orphan drug designation [9], and expedited regulatory pathways. However, empirical evidence from China remains limited [10]. Existing studies have primarily examined differences in approval timelines between China and the United States [11] or described the overall duration of clinical development for innovative drugs in China [12]. Few studies have systematically investigated how factors such as drug type, therapeutic area, and regulatory pathway influence development timelines within China's regulatory context. Given China's growing role in global pharmaceutical innovation [13], addressing this evidence gap is important for evaluating the effects of recent regulatory reforms and informing future policy decisions.

A key strength of this study is its inclusion of innovative traditional Chinese medicines (TCMs) alongside chemical drugs and biologics. Although a recent study examined the regulatory characteristics and clinical development timelines of innovative TCMs in China [14], few studies have systematically compared TCMs with chemical and biological drugs while accounting for regulatory pathways, therapeutic areas, and phases of regulatory reform. TCM development frequently involves multicomponent formulations and distinct requirements for evidence generation and clinical trial design [15], which may affect development timelines differently from those of chemical drugs and biologics. By examining all three drug types within a rigorous multivariable framework, this study provides a more comprehensive assessment of the factors associated with drug development timelines in China.

Methods

Study Design

A cross‐sectional study was conducted to characterize the development timelines of innovative drugs in China and examine factors associated with these timelines. The study included chemical drugs, biologics, and TCMs that received initial marketing approval from the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). Subsequent indication approvals were excluded, as they are typically based on products with established safety and efficacy profiles and therefore involve lower technical uncertainty.

Drugs developed by overseas sponsors were also excluded to ensure cohort homogeneity, as these products often follow distinct regulatory strategies in China, including reliance on bridging studies, waiver of early‐phase clinical trials, or extrapolation from overseas clinical data [16], which may substantially reduce domestic development timelines. For products with multiple formulations, only the earliest approved record was retained. Ultimately, 232 drugs met the inclusion criteria.

Definitions

The classification of innovative drugs was determined according to the regulatory frameworks in force at the time of submission and approval. For drugs approved after 2020, we applied the criteria specified in the Drug Administration Law of the People's Republic of China (2020 revision), which defines innovative drugs as including Class 1 innovative chemical drugs, therapeutic and preventive biologics, in vitro diagnostic reagents, and innovative TCMs. For drugs approved prior to 2020, we followed the Drug Registration Regulation (2007 revision), which defined innovative drugs as new chemical entities not previously marketed domestically or internationally, Class 1 therapeutic or preventive biologics, and TCMs classified as Categories 1–6 [12].

Given the lack of a standardized definition of development timelines in the literature [1, 2, 8, 14], we defined total development time as the interval between the initiation of Phase I clinical trials and the date of marketing authorization. The total duration was further decomposed into two mutually exclusive phases: clinical development time, defined as the period from Phase I trial initiation to submission of the marketing application, and marketing application review time, defined as the interval from submission of the marketing application to final regulatory approval. This decomposition enables a more granular assessment of development efficiency by distinguishing sponsor‐driven clinical development from regulator‐driven review processes.

Data Collection and Management

A systematic search was conducted on March 7, 2025, to identify innovative drugs approved in China since 2010. Drugs approved before 2010 were excluded due to limited availability of reliable information. Specifically, the NMPA did not routinely publish technical review reports prior to this period, and the clinical trial registration system remained underdeveloped, making it difficult to reliably ascertain Phase I initiation dates and marketing application submission dates.

Data were collected from multiple complementary sources to ensure completeness and accuracy. Core information was extracted from NMPA annual review reports and the Pharmacodia database, and inconsistencies were cross‐validated through targeted online searches. Marketing application submission and approval dates were obtained from the official website of the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE), NMPA. Because the NMPA does not systematically release technical review reports for all drugs, multiple data sources were triangulated to minimize missing data.

The initiation date of Phase I clinical trials was determined using a hierarchical approach. First, CDE technical review reports were screened to identify Phase I protocol numbers and drug identifiers. These were then used to query the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR), the CDE database, and ClinicalTrials.gov; duplicate records were removed, and the earliest verified start date was retained. Second, when registry data were unavailable, supplementary searches were conducted in peer‐reviewed publications, conference proceedings, corporate disclosures, and approved drug labeling. Finally, when both approaches failed, the clinical trial approval date was used as a proxy for Phase I initiation.

In addition, data on regulatory pathways, primary endpoint duration, and Full Analysis Set (FAS) sample size for pivotal studies were extracted from CDE technical review reports, approval documents, and approved drug labeling.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses were conducted using R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and reported with minimum and maximum values; categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages. Given the strictly positive and right‐skewed distributions of drug development durations, with skewness values of 1.01, 1.34, and 2.33 for total development time, clinical development time, and marketing application review time, respectively (Figure S1), we used generalized linear models (GLMs) with a gamma distribution and log link function. This approach was preferred over log‐transformed ordinary least squares (OLS) regression because it directly models the mean on the original scale and avoids retransformation bias (Jensen's inequality).

Univariable analyses were performed first, followed by separate multivariable GLMs for each of the three dependent variables: total development time, clinical development time, and marketing application review time [20, 21]. To assess potential effect modification, interaction terms were incorporated into the models, including oncology × conditional approval and regulatory reform stage × conditional approval. Interaction terms with statistical significance (p < 0.05) were retained in the final models. Multicollinearity was assessed using the generalized variance inflation factor (GVIF), with variables exceeding an adjusted GVIF value (GVIF^(1/[2 × Df])) of 2.0 excluded from the multivariable analysis [22, 23]. Regression coefficients were exponentiated and interpreted as relative mean ratios (RMRs), where an RMR > 1 indicates a longer mean duration relative to the reference category.

Given the atypical regulatory acceleration during the COVID‐19 pandemic, COVID‐19‐related drugs were excluded from the primary analysis to better reflect regulatory effects under routine conditions. The robustness of these findings was further evaluated through three sensitivity analyses: a full‐sample analysis including all identified innovative drugs; a trimmed‐sample analysis excluding the top and bottom 1% of durations to minimize the influence of extreme values; and an analysis excluding 26 drugs for which Phase I start dates were approximated using clinical trial approval dates. All statistical tests were two‐sided, and statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Regarding regulatory pathways, 107 drugs were approved through expedited programs, of which 59.81% were chemical drugs and 36.45% were biologics. Specifically, 55 drugs received conditional approval, including 41 oncology drugs and 10 antiviral agents, and 17 drugs received breakthrough therapy designation, including 16 oncology drugs, seven of which were for non‐small cell lung cancer, and one drug for atopic dermatitis. Six drugs were indicated for rare diseases, evenly distributed between chemical drugs and biologics.

In terms of the origin of innovator companies, 93.97% of drugs were developed by sponsors from Chinese mainland, including products jointly developed with foreign institutions, while the remaining 6.03% were licensed or authorized for further development in China. Half of the drugs were approved after July 1, 2020, following implementation of the revised Drug Registration Regulation, reflecting a substantial increase in approvals over the past 5 years.

According to the ATC classification, 79 drugs (34.05%) were antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents, followed by 46 drugs (19.83%) classified as anti‐infectives for systemic use. In terms of indications, 77 drugs (33.19%) were approved for oncologic diseases, including 27 for lung cancer and 19 for hematologic malignancies.

Among the 63 TCMs, 51 did not undergo Phase I clinical trials and instead proceeded directly to Phase II trials based on prior human experience or traditional clinical practice.

Descriptive Analysis of Development and Review Time

Clinical development start dates and marketing application dates were unavailable for three and two innovative drugs, respectively; additionally, primary endpoint duration was missing for two additional products. Consequently, 227 drugs were included in the final analysis of development timelines.

The median total development time was 6.94 years (IQR, 4.85–9.97; Table 2). The shortest development trajectory was observed for the inactivated SARS‐CoV‐2 vaccine (Vero cell; Beijing Institute of Biological Products), with a total development time of 8 months. In contrast, the longest duration was 21.70 years for Qinwei Granules, a traditional Chinese medicine developed by Chengdu Huaxi Natural Medicine Co. for the treatment of acute gouty arthritis. Notably, the 20 drugs with the shortest development times were all chemical drugs or biologics, with no TCMs represented (Table S1). The top three drugs were COVID‐19 therapeutics or vaccines. Moreover, all of the 20 fastest‐developed drugs had been approved through expedited regulatory pathways.

Factors Associated With Development and Review Time

The results of the multivariable gamma GLMs for the three primary outcomes are summarized in Tables S3–S5. In univariable analyses, drug type, priority review, conditional approval, special review, breakthrough therapy, regulatory reform stage, indication, primary endpoint duration, and sample size were associated with total development time and clinical development time (p < 0.10). For marketing application review time, the same variables were significant except for special review (p < 0.05).

In the multivariable model for total development time (Table S3), drug type and conditional approval were the main factors associated with overall duration. Compared with biologics, chemical drugs were associated with a 20% longer total development time (RMR = 1.20, p = 0.006), and TCMs were associated with a 61% longer duration (RMR = 1.61, p < 0.001). Conditional approval was the only expedited pathway independently associated with a shorter total development time, corresponding to a 34% reduction among oncology drugs (RMR = 0.66, p < 0.001). A significant interaction between indication and conditional approval was observed (RMR = 2.08, p < 0.001), suggesting heterogeneity in the association across therapeutic areas. Compared with small trials, large FAS sample size (> 509 participants) was associated with a 20% longer total development time (RMR = 1.20, p = 0.046).

Clinical development time was associated with drug type, regulatory reform stage and interaction terms involving conditional approval (Table S4). Among drugs not receiving conditional approval, clinical development time was 52% and 47% longer in Stage II and Stage III, respectively, compared with Stage I (RMR = 1.52, p = 0.001; RMR = 1.47, p < 0.001). A significant interaction between conditional approval and Stage II was observed (RMR = 0.47, p = 0.049), suggesting differential associations across regulatory reform periods. The interaction between indication and conditional approval remained significant (RMR = 2.52, p < 0.001), indicating that the association of conditional approval with clinical development time differed by indication and was less favorable among non‐oncology drugs. TCMs were associated with longer clinical development time compared with biologics (RMR = 1.60, p < 0.001).

In contrast, marketing application review time showed the largest relative differences across regulatory reform stages (Table S5). Compared with Stage I, review time was reduced by 43% in Stage II and 64% in Stage III (RMR = 0.57, p < 0.001; RMR = 0.36, p < 0.001). Priority review and conditional approval were associated with 26% and 28% reductions in review time, respectively (RMR = 0.74, p = 0.004; RMR = 0.72, p = 0.005).

The association between FAS sample size and development timelines varied across model specifications. For total development time, a significant positive association was observed in both the primary model and the analysis excluding the 26 drugs with proxy start dates (Figure 3 and Table S6). For clinical development time, no significant association was observed in the primary model or in analyses excluding extreme values (Figure 4); however, a statistically significant positive association was observed after excluding the 26 drugs for which Phase I start dates were approximated using clinical trial approval dates (Table S6). For marketing application review time, FAS sample size was not significantly associated with the outcome in any model (Figure 5 and Table S6). Importantly, the direction and magnitude of the associations for key predictors, including drug type, conditional approval, and interaction terms, remained generally consistent across model specifications, supporting the robustness of the main findings (Figures 3, 4, 5 and Table S6).

Discussion

Previous studies have examined the characteristics and development timelines of innovative drugs approved in China [12, 14]. However, these studies were largely descriptive and lacked systematic multivariable evaluations of factors associated with development timelines. In particular, few studies have simultaneously examined all three types of innovative drugs while accounting for regulatory pathways, reform stages, and therapeutic indications with a robust statistical framework. This study is the first to systematically analyze the development timelines of three types of innovative drugs approved in China over a 15‐year period. We used GLMs with a gamma distribution and log link function to estimate the associations of drug type, regulatory pathways, reform stage, and indication with development timelines.

The median total development time of 6.94 years observed in this study suggests relatively high efficiency in China's innovative drug development ecosystem. Drugs approved through expedited pathways had a median development time of 5.29 years, shorter than the 7.1 years reported by Hwang et al. [7] for expedited FDA approvals between 2012 and 2016. The overall median was also comparable to the 8.5 years reported by Brown et al. [1] for FDA‐approved innovative drugs from 2010 to 2020. Similar patterns were observed across therapeutic subgroups. de Jong et al. [2] reported a median development time of 7.3 years for EMA‐approved anti‐infectives, and Demirci et al. [9] found a median of 7.2 years for U.S. orphan drugs, compared with 6.00 and 6.51 years, respectively, in our study. These findings suggest that China's regulatory and industrial ecosystem for innovative drug development appears globally competitive. Although these shorter timelines may appear inconsistent with the historical approval lag reported in previous studies [24, 25], the difference largely reflects our focus on indigenous innovation. Unlike prior comparative studies that often examined globally developed drugs first submitted and approved outside China, our analysis included only drugs for which both Phase I initiation and regulatory review occurred in China, thereby capturing domestic development efficiency rather than delays related to cross‐national submission sequencing.

Within China, clinical development time varied across drug types. TCMs had the longest development periods, followed by chemical drugs, whereas biologics had the shortest duration. Prior studies suggest that application type alone may have limited influence on development duration, with reported medians of 7.2 years for NDAs and 7.3 years for BLAs [9]. Therefore, the differences across drug types observed in our study may reflect drug‐specific evidentiary requirements and clinical value assessment frameworks. Innovative chemical drugs often require head‐to‐head superiority trials against established standard‐of‐care therapies to demonstrate clinically meaningful benefit [26, 27]. Such trials may involve larger sample sizes, longer follow‐up, and more complex statistical analyses, contributing to longer timelines. In contrast, biologics targeting well‐characterized molecular pathways may rely on surrogate or intermediate endpoints, such as objective response rate or biomarker change, to support earlier regulatory decisions and shorter clinical programs [28]. For TCMs, longer timelines reflect the difficulty of translating historical human experience and traditional clinical practice into modern randomized trial evidence. Challenges include dose‐finding and protocol validation [29, 30], credible placebo design, validation of TCM‐specific endpoints and syndrome‐based assessment tools [31, 32], and more stringent post‐2015 Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) requirements for multi‐component TCM products [33]. Together, these factors suggest that the main bottleneck for TCM development lies in generating confirmatory clinical evidence that meets modern regulatory standards.

In univariable analyses, several expedited pathways were associated with shorter development timelines. However, after multivariable adjustment, conditional approval remained the only expedited pathway independently associated with shorter total development time, whereas its association with clinical development time varied by reform stage and indication. This finding highlights an important distinction between administrative acceleration and evidentiary flexibility. Priority review is designed primarily to accelerate the regulatory review process and was associated with shorter marketing application review time, but it was not independently associated with shorter total development time. In contrast, conditional approval modifies the evidentiary requirements for marketing authorization by allowing earlier approval based on surrogate or intermediate endpoints [34], thereby providing a mechanism for shortening the overall development cycle, particularly in oncology indications. These findings suggest that, within China's regulatory framework, evidentiary flexibility may shorten overall development timelines through mechanisms distinct from administrative acceleration.

The shorter development time observed for oncology drugs in univariable analysis was no longer significant after multivariable adjustment, suggesting that this association was largely explained by the preferential use of conditional approval in oncology indications (77.35% vs. 23.56% for non‐oncology indications in our sample) [35]. Interaction analysis further showed that the association of conditional approval with clinical development time differed by indication and was less favorable among non‐oncology drugs. This pattern may reflect the more mature clinical trial infrastructure in oncology and the greater regulatory acceptance of surrogate endpoints, such as objective response rate (ORR) and progression‐free survival (PFS), for early decision‐making by the NMPA [34]. After accounting for these regulatory facilitation mechanisms, therapeutic area itself was not independently associated with development speed, suggesting that regulatory pathway design, rather than disease area, is more strongly associated with development efficiency.

The interaction between reform stage and conditional approval suggests a dual‐track pattern in China's regulatory evolution. Among drugs not receiving conditional approval, clinical development time was longer in later reform stages than in the early stage, likely reflecting stricter evidence requirements for quality, safety, efficacy, and CMC standards after the post‐2015 reforms [18]. In contrast, drugs approved through conditional approval appeared to have shorter clinical development time in later reform stages, particularly in Stage II, suggesting that conditional approval may have become more efficient for eligible innovative drugs even as standard requirements became more stringent [35]. When total development time was considered, this pattern was less apparent, as substantial reductions in marketing application review time, particularly the 64% reduction in Stage III, partly offset longer clinical development among drugs not receiving conditional approval. These findings suggest that increased evidentiary rigor and improved regulatory review efficiency have evolved in parallel.

Marketing application review time was associated with both expedited pathways and regulatory reform stage. Priority review and conditional approval were associated with significant reductions in review time [36], while no interaction was observed between expedited pathways and reform stage. This suggests that regulatory modernization may have improved baseline review efficiency broadly, rather than benefiting only drugs granted expedited designations [37]. Even after accounting for the 26%–28% reduction associated with expedited pathways, Stage II and Stage III remained associated with substantial reductions in review duration compared with Stage I (43% and 64%, respectively). These findings are consistent with post‐2015 structural reforms, including CDE professionalization and mandatory review timelines that strengthened overall regulatory efficiency [38].

The association between trial scale and development timelines was inconsistent across outcomes. FAS sample size was associated with clinical development time only after excluding drugs with proxy‐defined Phase I initiation dates, suggesting sensitivity to how clinical start dates were defined. For total development time, a positive association was observed more consistently, whereas no association was observed for marketing application review time. This pattern suggests that trial scale may contribute to overall and clinical development timelines under certain data specifications, while review time appears to be more closely related to procedural and pathway‐related factors than to the volume of clinical evidence [25]. Overall, trial scale appears to play a limited and context‐dependent role in determining development efficiency. Future research with more granular trial‐level data may further clarify how trial scale influences clinical development timelines.

This study has several limitations. First, detailed data on regulatory review cycles are not systematically disclosed in China. Although we integrated information from multiple public and commercial sources, some approvals or key dates may not have been fully captured. However, sensitivity analyses yielded results consistent with the primary findings, suggesting that potential data incompleteness had limited influence on the results. Second, although the study spans 15 years and captures major regulatory reforms in China, the external generalizability of the findings may be limited by differences in regulatory systems, development practices, and approval pathways across countries.

In conclusion, this study provides a comprehensive assessment of development and regulatory review timelines for innovative drugs approved in China from 2010 to 2025. Development timelines varied by drug type, with biologics showing the shortest duration and TCMs the longest. Regulatory instruments played distinct roles across development stages. Priority review was primarily associated with shorter marketing application review time, whereas conditional approval was associated with shorter total development time, particularly among oncology drugs, and showed heterogeneous associations with clinical development time across reform stages and indications. At the system level, post‐2015 reforms were associated with longer clinical development among drugs not receiving conditional approval, likely reflecting increased evidentiary requirements, while marketing application review time was substantially reduced. These opposing dynamics may help explain the relative stability of overall development timelines despite stricter regulatory standards.

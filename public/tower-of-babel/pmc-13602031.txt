Pertrochanteric fracture in case of Sheehan syndrome: A rare case report
Authors: Bikram K Kar, Dushyant Chauhan, Rishabh Verma, Nirmal Rathi, Suket Kumar, Shibdatta Das, Jaykrishnu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSheehan's syndrome is caused by ischemic necrosis of the pituitary gland following hypo-perfusion to the pituitary gland. The prevalence is around 3.2% in women <40 years of age and is relatively common in developing countries. The diagnosis is made with the help of a suggestive obstetric history, clinical manifestations, laboratory findings of reduced hormone levels, and pertinent radiological features. The treatment is to replace the patient with deficient hormones and prevent the complications associated with it. We present the management of a patient with Sheehan's syndrome with pertrochanteric fracture due to the rarity of its presentation. The patient underwent the insertion of a proximal femur nail under closed reduction and achieved optimum results post operatively. As such no case reports with these findings have been documented in the literature or online sources.Keywords: Pertrochanteric fracture, Sheehan syndrome, Hypopituitarism, Osteoporosis, Pathological fracture, Postpartum complications

Background

Sheehan's syndrome is caused by ischemic necrosis of the pituitary gland following hypoperfusion to the pituitary gland. It was described in 1937 by the British pathologist Harold Leeming Sheehan. The prevalence is around 3.2% in women <40 years of age and is relatively common in developing countries [1]. The clinical features are mainly due to the reduced hormone levels. The diagnosis is made with the help of a suggestive obstetric history, clinical manifestations, laboratory findings of reduced hormone levels, and pertinent radiological features. The treatment is to replace the patient with deficient hormones and prevent the complications associated with it. We present the management of a patient with Sheehan's syndrome with pertrochanteric fracture due to the rarity of its presentation.

Case presentation

A female in her mid-50s reported to the dept. of orthopedics with complaints of severe pain in her left hip and inability to bear weight after an alleged history of fall in her bathroom. The radiographs confirmed the diagnosis of a pertrochanteric fracture femur left side AO 31A2 (Fig. 1). The patient had a history of severe post-partum hemorrhage after her second pregnancy in 1997 for which the patient was managed in Critical care unit and was transfused 3 units of blood as per medical records. After discharge the patient suffered from lactational failure and complaints of dizziness. The patient recalled frequent vomiting and anorexia for several years. The patient then moved to Lucknow in 2010 where she sought medical care for her complaints where she was diagnosed with Sheehan syndrome and was put on hormonal supplements.

At the time of presentation, the following parameters were recorded: hemoglobin, platelet count, coagulation profile, liver and renal function test, serum electrolytes and calcium levels, serum vit D25-OH, thyroid function tests, morning serum cortisol, LH and FSH, HbA1c and blood sugar levels (Table 1, Table 2). MRI of the hypothalamus-pituitary region was done which revealed partial empty sella (Fig. 2) with biochemical evidence of hypopituitarism. The bone mineral density was recorded from the last dexa scan done 4 months prior to the event of trauma (Table 3).

After optimization of her thyroid levels and hemoglobin, surgery was planned for the patient. Under effect of Spinal anesthesia, a 180 mm standard proximal femur nail was inserted after attaining satisfactory reduction (Fig. 3). The patient was mobilized on post operative day 2 and immediate post operative X-rays revealed good reduction and adequate fixation. The patient was discharged on tab levothyroxine 150 μg and tab hydrocortisone 25 mg in divided doses and was followed up at regular intervals. The fracture showed good union and patient is able to do all her daily activities at present (Fig. 4, Fig. 5).

Discussion

Sheehan's syndrome (SS) is an important cause of panhypopituitarism in women. The prevalence is around 3.2% in women <40 years of age and is relatively common in developing countries [1]. The clinical features are mainly due to the reduced hormone level. MRI of hypothalamus–pituitary region showed partial empty sella in 47% and empty sella in 53%. While Sheehan's syndrome is primarily managed by endocrinologists, it can have secondary effects involving the musculoskeletal system that may involve orthopedic consultation.

Low growth hormone and estrogen levels resulting from the syndrome can lead to reduced bone density (osteoporosis or low bone mass), increasing the risk of fractures. Patients can experience non-specific symptoms such as generalized myalgias (muscle pain), asthenia (weakness), and body aches. In rare cases, the severe adrenal insufficiency associated with Sheehan's syndrome can manifest as flexion contractures of the legs [2].

There is limited information available about bone mineral density (BMD) in this condition [3]. Low bone mass (BMD Z-score ≤ −1) was found in 80% of subjects, and in 44% it was more pronounced (BMD Z-score ≤ −2), mainly involving the lumbar spine. Bone loss at the femoral neck was less marked [4].

Due to significant hormonal imbalances, the patient experienced widespread effects on her health- such as reduced energy, diminished physical capability, difficulty with temperature control, changes in her skin, and problems in her sexual life. The hormonal imbalance also posed a challenge to the anesthesia team during induction of the case. Replacement therapy for hypopituitarism involves an individualized regimen and frequent dose adjustment.

This case report outlines a rare clinical condition that remained unrecognized for several years. Thorough evaluation—including a comprehensive medical history and physical examination—revealed multiple signs indicative of hormonal insufficiencies, which were later confirmed through laboratory testing. Cranial imaging further demonstrated significant damage to the pituitary gland. Prompt identification of this condition can greatly enhance patients' quality of life and avert potentially life-threatening complications caused by profound hormone deficiencies.

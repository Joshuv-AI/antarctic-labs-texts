Graph enumeration for moduli spaces of curves and maps
Siddarth Kannan, Terry Dekun Song

Abstract
. We develop a calculus based on graph enumeration for calculating S n S_{n} -equivariant motivic invariants of graphically stratified moduli spaces. We apply our theory to the Deligne–Mumford moduli space ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} and to the space of torus-fixed stable maps ℳ ¯ g , n ​ ( X , β ) ℂ ⋆ \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}} when the target X X admits an appropriate ℂ ⋆ \mathbb{C}^{\star} -action, deriving new formulas in each case. A key role is played by the Pólya–Petersen character of a graph, which enriches Pólya’s classical cycle index polynomial. This character is valued in an algebra Λ [ 2 ] \Lambda^{[2]} of wreath product symmetric functions, which we study from combinatorial and representation–theoretic perspectives. We prove that this algebra may be viewed as the Grothendieck ring of the category of polynomial functors which take symmetric sequences of vector spaces to vector spaces, building on foundational work of Macdonald. This leads to a plethystic action of Λ [ 2 ] \Lambda^{[2]} on the ring Λ \Lambda of ordinary symmetric functions. Using this action, we derive our formulas, which ultimately involve only ordinary symmetric functions and the Grothendieck ring of mixed Hodge structures.



1.
Introduction

Graphs encode the combinatorics of degenerating algebraic curves, and are thus fundamental to their moduli problems. The combinatorial types of different graphs give recursive stratifications of moduli spaces in the following manner.

Setup
.

Associated to the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
is a collection of graphs
\{G\}
and a collection of moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{M}_{n}
, such that there is a decomposition
\overline{\mathcal{M}}=\bigsqcup_{G}\mathcal{M}_{G}
into locally closed strata where
\mathcal{M}_{G}\cong\prod_{v\in V(G)}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{val}(v)}/\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, and
\mathrm{val}(v)
denotes the valence of a vertex
v
.

In this paper we develop a general calculus for the application of Pólya’s enumeration techniques
[
PR87
]
to topological invariants of this class of moduli spaces.

The prototypical example of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
is the Deligne--Mumford compactification
1
1

1

In general,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
has connected components indexed by numerical parameters. We work with the disjoint union with suitable grading, because the recursive structure is most natural when we consider the whole collection of connected components.

\bigsqcup_{g,n}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},
where
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\bigsqcup_{g\geq 0}\mathcal{M}_{g,n}.
In this setting, a beautiful formula relating the
S_{n}
-equivariant motivic invariants of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{M}
has been derived by Getzler and Kapranov
[
GK98
]
; we will compare and contrast their approach with ours in more detail in §
1.4.1
below. For now we emphasize that our work is independent from theirs, and offers new formulas even for the Deligne–Mumford compactification. In this article, we will apply our formalism to the following examples:

•

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and its combinatorial subspaces such as stable curves of compact type
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}}
;

•

the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}
of torus-fixed stable maps to a projective variety with a good
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action (Definition
7.1
).

Several potential future applications of the theory are discussed in §
1.5
below. The moduli spaces of interest carry natural symmetric group actions that permute marked points. Symmetric function theory coming from the permutation actions is essential in all steps of our calculations.

1.1.
Calculations and applications

All of our results are phrased in terms of the
Serre characteristic
. If
X
is a variety over
\mathbb{C}
, the Serre characteristic of
X
is defined as

\mathsf{e}(X):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}_{c}(X;\mathbb{Q})]\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS)},

where
\mathsf{MHS}
is the abelian category of mixed Hodge structures over
\mathbb{Q}
. The Serre characteristic specializes to the
E
-polynomial
2
2

2

This is sometimes called the Hodge–Deligne polynomial.
in the sense of e.g.
[
HRV08
, Definition 2.1.4]
, which agrees with the Hodge polynomial when
X
is proper with at worst finite quotient singularities.

If
X
admits an
S_{n}
-action, then each

is a
S_{n}
-representation in
\mathsf{MHS}
, so we may define the
S_{n}
-equivariant Serre characteristic as follows: first let

\Lambda:=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]

be the ring of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{Q}
, with grading determined by
\deg(p_{i})=i
. Then the
S_{n}
-equivariant Serre characteristic is given by

\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(X):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}_{c}(X;\mathbb{Q})]\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS}_{S_{n}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\cong K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda_{n},

where
K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS}_{S_{n}})
denotes the Grothendieck group of
S_{n}
-equivariant mixed Hodge structures and
\Lambda_{n}\subset\Lambda
is the subspace of symmetric functions which are homogeneous of degree
n
. Note that
\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(X)
can easily be specialized to the ordinary Serre characteristic
\mathsf{e}(X)
. See Remark
1.14
below.

Notation 1.3
.

Write

•

•

We write

\overline{\partial}_{\mu}:K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Lambda}\to K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Lambda}

for the operator

3

3

•

Given
f\in\hat{\Lambda},
we use

Our formula is stated in the language of
2
-partitions
. A
2
-partition is a function
\Theta:\mathsf{Part}^{\star}\to\mathsf{Part}^{\star}

\Theta(\mu)=\varnothing

\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}

Theorem A
.

Fix an integer
g\geq 2
. Given an integer
h\geq 0
and a partition
\pi
, set

\mathsf{w}_{h}^{\mu}:=\begin{cases}\overline{\partial}_{\mu}\mathsf{a}_{h}&\text{if }2h-2+|\mu|>0,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{1-\mathsf{a}_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}&\text{if }h=0,\,\mu=1^{2},\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{1+2\dot{\mathsf{a}}_{0}}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathsf{a}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}}&\text{if }h=0,\,\mu=2^{1}.\end{cases}

Then we have

\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}=\left(\sum_{\Theta_{0},\ldots\Theta_{g}\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}}K(\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g})\prod_{h=0}^{g}\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\psi_{\Theta_{h}(\mu)}\left(\mathsf{w}_{h}^{\mu}\right)\right)\circ\left(p_{1}+\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{0}^{\prime}\right),

where
K(\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g})\in\mathbb{Q}
is defined as follows: for any

\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g}),
the tuple
\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g}
of 2-partitions specifies a union of conjugacy classes
\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g}}(\mathbf{G})\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G}),

and

K(\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g}):=\sum_{\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Iso}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g})}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g}}(\mathbf{G})|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})|}.

Remark 1.4
.

We briefly explain the appearance of
2
-partitions in Theorem
A

\mathbf{G}\in\hat{\Gamma}_{g}

\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})_{i}^{(h)}

(1.5)

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})}:=\prod_{h=0}^{g}\left(\prod_{i\geq 0}S_{i}\wr S_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})_{i}^{(h)}}\right),

well-defined up to conjugacy; here for integers

we define

. The conjugacy classes of

are in natural bijection with
(g+1)

of
2
-partitions such that

(1.6)

\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}_{i}}|\Theta_{h}(\mu)|=\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}_{i}.

for each

. Then

Our work also gives a formula for the total generating function

1.1.3.
The Euler characteristic of the moduli space of stable maps

1.2.
Pólya–Petersen characters

Our approach separates the equivariant Serre characteristic into a combinatorial part and a geometric part. The geometric part is the calculation of the series
\mathsf{a}
, which is essentially the information of the
S_{n}
-equivariant virtual Hodge numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

Pólya–Petersen character
of a finite graph. This construction is a common refinement of Pólya’s cycle index polynomial
[
PR87
]
and Petersen’s type-
B
character associated to a cycle
[
Pet12
]
: in addition to vertex permutations recorded by the cycle index polynomial, the new character also keeps track of permutations of the half-edges
H(G)
which are compatible with the root map
H(G)\to V(G)
. The automorphism group of the set map
\operatorname{Aut}(H(G)\to V(G))
is a product of wreath products of symmetric groups as we explain in §
2.1
.

To keep track of the character theory of products of wreath symmetric groups, we construct in
2.2
the ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
of 2-symmetric functions.
4
4

4

This is distinct from the ring
\Lambda\otimes\Lambda
of bisymmetric functions.
The ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
has the following descriptions:

•

the polynomial ring

\mathbb{Q}[p_{1}(\mu),p_{2}(\mu),\ldots\mid\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}],

•

the tensor product
\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\Lambda(S_{i})
, where
\Lambda(S_{i})
denotes the
S_{i}
-wreath symmetric functions of Macdonald
[
Mac95
,
Mac80
]
,

•

the Grothendieck ring of the category of bounded
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-modules, where an
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-module is the data of a finite-dimensional rational representation for each finite product of wreath products of symmetric groups (§
3.1
),

•

the Grothendieck ring of polynomial functors from
\mathbb{S}
-modules to vector spaces, where a
\mathbb{S}
-module is a finite-dimensional rational representation of
S_{n}
for each
n\geq 0
.
5
5

5

\mathbb{S}
-modules are sometimes called
symmetric sequences
of vector spaces.

The last characterization gives rise to a a plethystic action of
\Lambda^{[2]}
on
\Lambda\otimes\Lambda
, namely a map

\tilde{\circ}:\Lambda^{[2]}\times(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda)\to\Lambda

that we describe and characterize in §
3.3
. The Pólya–Petersen character is valued in the ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
, and graph contributions are expressed in terms of the
\tcirc
operator.

Definition 1.15
.

Let
G
be a possibly decorated graph and let
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
denote the decoration-preserving automorphism of the graph. Its Pólya–Petersen character is defined as

\zeta_{G}:=\mathrm{ch}\left(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Aut}(G)}^{\mathrm{Aut}(H(G)\to V(G))}\mathrm{triv}_{\mathrm{Aut}(G)}\right)\in R(\mathrm{Aut}(H(G)\to V(G)))\subset\Lambda^{[2]},

where
\operatorname{ch}
is the character of a representation, in the form of the generalized Frobenius characteristic defined in §
2.4
.

The following result is an example of how Pólya–Petersen characters appear in our work. It implies Theorem
B
after the calculation of explicit formulas for the characters
\zeta_{G}
, and makes precise how the calculation of
\overline{\mathsf{a}}
is separated into combinatorial and geometric parts.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathsf{Graph}
be the groupoid of all connected graphs, and let
\Delta:\Lambda\to\Lambda\otimes\Lambda
be the coproduct on the ring of symmetric functions. Then

(1.16)

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\sum_{G\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathsf{Graph})}t^{|E(G)|}(\zeta_{G}\tcirc\Delta\mathsf{a})

In fact, formula (
1.16
) holds on the level of the Grothendieck ring of varieties, as discussed in §
4
. The generalization of (
1.16
) which allows for vertices of various genera is discussed in §
5
, and implies Theorem
A
.

1.3.
Calculation techniques

We now give some comments on the shape of the formulas
\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}
and
\overline{\mathsf{a}}
that illustrate the technical tools useful for calculations.

1.3.1.
Enriched plethysm and differential operators

We emphasize that while the Pólya–Petersen characters take value in the 2-symmetric function ring
\Lambda^{[2]},
the formulas in Theorems
A
,
B
, and
C
only involve ordinary symmetric functions in
\hat{\Lambda}
and standard operations on them.

This is an instance of the general fact that the enriched plethysm
\tcirc:\Lambda^{[2]}\times(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda)\to\Lambda
is determined by the action of the enriched power sums
p_{n}(\mu)\in\Lambda^{[2]}
, and these act by a standard projection followed by an Adams operation. Moreover, under the ring isomorphism
\Lambda^{[2]}\cong\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\Lambda(S_{i}),
the enriched plethysm
\tcirc:\Lambda^{[2]}\times(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda)\to\Lambda
is compatible with and determined by the
S_{i}
-plethysms
\circ_{S_{i}}:\Lambda(S_{i})\times(\Lambda_{i}\otimes\Lambda)\to\Lambda
defined by Macdonald
[
Mac80
]
. Precise statements are given in §
3.3
. The upshot is that applying the action of Pólya–Petersen characters on bisymmetric functions produces ordinary symmetric functions that can be efficiently computed and related to the existing literature on
S_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristics of moduli spaces.

1.3.2.
Grafting

Our derivation of Theorem
A
overcomes the basic challenge that there are infinitely many dual graph strata in
\bigsqcup_{n}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}.
To arrive at a formula with a finite number of terms, we observe that relative to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, the combinatorial complexity of strata comes from:

•

attaching trees of genus zero vertices, which we call ‘rational tails,’ and

•

subdividing edges by stable genus zero vertices, where the paths of stable genus zero vertices are called ‘caterpillars.’

The formula in Theorem
A
translates the combinatorial operations described above into plethystic formulas that we now briefly summarize. See §
4.5
for more details.

Definition 1.17
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the subspace of stable curves that do not contain any nontrivial connected subcurve of arithmetic genus
g.
Let

\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}})

be the generating function for the
S_{n}
-equivariant Serre characteristics.

An intermediate step in deriving Theorem
A
is the well-known formula (Lemma
4.13
)

\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}=\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}\circ(p_{1}+\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{0}^{\prime})

which decomposes graph strata in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
into contributions from the minimal genus
g
subgraph— termed the ‘core’ of the dual graph— and rational tails, with generating functions given by
\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}
and
p_{1}+\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{0}^{\prime},
respectively.

We attach caterpillars by allowing genus zero vertices of valence two in the statement of Theorem
A
. The moduli space of caterpillars, defined in
4.5
, forms an
S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}
-variety, and its Frobenius characteristics are calculated in
[
BM14
, Remark 4.2]
and
[
KS24
,
§6.1]
as

\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes\frac{1}{1-\mathsf{a}_{0}^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}p_{2}\otimes\frac{1+2\dot{\mathsf{a}}_{0}}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathsf{a}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Lambda}.

The two summands correspond to the terms
\mathsf{w}^{\mu}_{h}
in Theorem
A
for
h=0
and
|\mu|=2
which replace genus zero vertices of valence two by caterpillars.

After these two grafting operations, we only need to work with graphs in the finite groupoid
\hat{\Gamma}_{g}
, which indexes the dual graphs of curves in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, or equivalently the cones in
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\mathrm{trop}}

[
ACP15
,
CCUW20
,
CGP21
]
, in order to compute the generating function
\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
. A problem left open in our work is to develop a more general grafting procedure for Pólya–Petersen characters.

1.4.
Related work

The present article builds on and complements many previous results on the topology of moduli spaces of curves.

1.4.1.
Modular operads

In
[
GK98
]
, Getzler and Kapranov study Deligne’s weight spectral sequence for the family of normal crossings compactifications
\{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\}_{2g-2+n>0}
from an operadic perspective. Taking Euler characteristics, they deduce an inspirational formula.

Theorem 1.18
.

[
GK98
, Theorem 8.13]

With
\overline{\mathsf{a}}
as in (
1.8
) and
\mathsf{a}
as in (
1.9
), we have

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\mathrm{Log}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}t^{n}\left(\frac{n}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{2n}}\right)\right)\mathrm{Exp}(\mathsf{a})\right),

where

\mathrm{Exp}(\mathsf{a}):=\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\frac{\psi_{\mu}(\mathsf{a})}{\prod_{i\geq 1}i^{\mu_{i}}\mu_{i}!},

and
\mathrm{Log}
is the plethystic inverse to
\mathrm{Exp}
with formula determined by Cadogan
[
Cad71
]
.

In the language of loc. cit., Theorem
1.18
is a formula for the Euler characteristic of the
Feynman transform
of a modular operad. As remarked in
[
GK98
, §0]
, Theorem
1.18
is in a sense an
S_{n}
-equivariant upgrade of Wick’s theorem, which extracts graph sums from asymptotic expansions of integrals
[
GK98
, Equation 0.2]
. As explained by the authors, the idea of attaching representations of
S_{n}
at vertices of graphs appears in Hanlon–Robinson’s graph enumeration techniques
[
HR82
]
which extend the work of Pólya
[
PR87
]
.

Aside from its beauty, Theorem
1.18
has the following advantages:

(A1)

It is straightforward to implement on a computer algebra system (see e.g. computer implementation by Bergström
[
Ber
]
, or the application in
[
BT07
]
).

(A2)

It is manifestly invertible
[
GK98
, Corollary 8.15]
.

On the other hand,
[
GK98
]
leaves the following questions open:

(Q1)

When the right-hand side of Theorem
1.18
is expanded as a power series, is there a natural graph-theoretic interpretation for its coefficients?

(Q2)

How can one directly compute the “fixed genus" generating function
\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{g}
as a finite combination of the functions
\mathsf{a}_{h}
for
h\leq g
?

(Q3)

How can similar techniques be applied to other moduli spaces with graphical stratifications?

Question (Q1) is answered by Theorem
B
, and question (Q2) is answered for all
g\geq 2
by Theorem
A
. When it comes to question (Q3), the general theory developed in this paper applies to a wide range of moduli spaces; Theorem
C
is an example. Question (Q2) has been answered for
g\leq 2
in the literature: the cases
g\leq 1
are due to Getzler
[
Get95a
,
Get98
]
, and
g=2
was worked out recently by Diaconu
[
Dia20
, Theorem 4.4]
. Figure
1
gives a graph-theoretic interpretation for all of the terms in Diaconu’s formula. Petersen
[
Pet12
]
gave an alternative calculation of
\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{1}
which serves as a major inspiration for the present work, and we discuss this more in §
1.4.2
below.

Absent a general calculus for the graph sums appearing in Theorems
A
and
B
, Theorem
1.18
retains advantage (A1) over our work. We hope to revisit these graph sums in the future: see
1.5.1
. When it comes to advantage (A2), we remark that Theorem
A
is in fact invertible, by first taking plethysm with
p_{1}-\mathsf{a}_{0}^{\prime}
on both sides and then using the inclusion-exclusion principle. As such, one can obtain graph sum formulas for
\mathsf{a}_{g}
in terms of
\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{h}
for
h\leq g
. Then information about the set of series
\{\overline{\mathsf{a}}_{h}\}_{h\geq 0}
can be passed through these graph sums to compute the
S_{n}
-equivariant weight-graded Euler characteristics of
H_{c}^{\star}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
, which are encoded by
\mathsf{a}
. It would be interesting to see if this approach could recover any existing calculations of these Euler characteristics; see
[
CFGP23
]
for weight
0
,
[
PW24b
]
for weight
2
, and
[
PW24a
]
for weight
11
.

1.4.2.
Wreath products and polynomial functors

A primary source of inspiration for the present work is Petersen’s calculation
[
Pet12
]
of
\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n})
using
S_{2}
-
wreath symmetric functions
. The key formula in loc. cit. can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.19
.

[
Pet12
, Proposition 3.7]

Let
\mathcal{D}ih_{k,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
be the
S_{n}
-invariant substack parametrizing necklaces of
k
rational curves, namely the stable curves in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}
whose dual graphs are a
k
-cycle. Then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{D}ih_{k,n})=\left(\mathrm{Ind}_{D_{k}}^{S_{2}\wr S_{k}}\mathrm{triv}\right)\circ_{S_{2}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\mathcal{M}_{0,n+2})\right),

where
\circ_{S_{2}}
denotes wreath product plethysm.

The automorphism group of a
k
-cycle is given by the dihedral group
D_{k}.
Consequently,
\bigsqcup_{n}\mathcal{D}ih_{k,n}
is the
D_{k}
-quotient of the
k
-fold product of
\bigsqcup_{n}\mathcal{M}_{0,n}.
To turn this description into a formula, Petersen points out that the embedding
D_{k}\subset S_{2}\wr S_{k}
reflects how
D_{k}
acts on the half-edges along each vertex, while the more familiar embedding
D_{k}\subset S_{k}
for
k\geq 3
does not. The natural setting for the calculation is thus the ring
\Lambda(S_{2})
of
S_{2}
-wreath symmetric functions, as it records the character theory of the groups
S_{2}\wr S_{k},
for all
k
. This ring was first constructed and studied by Macdonald in
[
Mac80
]
, which is another major inspiration for the present article.

Macdonald explains in loc. cit. that representations of
S_{2}\wr S_{k}
are identified with
polynomial functors
taking
S_{2}
-representations to vector spaces. This identification gives a clean conceptual picture for the above formula: the
S_{2}
-wreath symmetric function
\mathrm{Ind}_{D_{k}}^{S_{2}\wr S_{k}}\mathrm{triv}
is a polynomial that assembles the genus zero vertex contributions to characteristics of
k
-cycles.

Our formalism generalizes Petersen’s necklace calculation to strata given by arbitrary graphs. Automorphism groups of finite graphs embed into finite products of wreath products of symmetric groups, and the character theory of all such products are recorded by the 2-symmetric function ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
. The Pólya–Petersen characters can then be seen as polynomials that assemble
\mathbb{S}
-modules along arbitrary graphs. From this perspective, our work expands the Getzler–Kapranov formula into individual graph contributions.

1.5.1.
Orbisum computations

It would be worthwhile to systematically determine the graph sums
K(\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g})
and
O(\Theta)
that appear as coefficients in Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
. In the language of
[
CFGP23
]
, these numbers are examples of
orbisums
over finite groupoids. In loc. cit., the calculation of orbisums is reduced to the enumeration of so-called “orbigraphs.” Crucially, the the orbisums evaluated in
[
CFGP23
]
are signed, and this leads to cancellation in large families of orbigraphs. It would be interesting to see whether their techniques can be adapted to our setting. Another potential approach is via the connection between orbigraph enumeration and matrix integrals, used to great effect by Bini and Harer
[
BH10
]
in order to compute non-equivariant topological and orbifold Euler characteristics of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Finally, there is a potential connection to the Fock space formalism used in
[
HM24a
]
to evaluate certain Hurwitz numbers, which can be expressed as graph sums similar to those arising in our work
[
HM24b
]
.

1.5.2.
Asymptotic analysis

Since our approach singles out each graph’s contribution in each formula, it enables quantitative comparison across different graph types and their asymptotic behaviors as numerical parameters tend to infinity. For instance, it has been observed in
[
KS24
]
that, in line with the expectation from enumerative geometry, the contribution of maps from curves with rational tails to
\chi^{S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))
dominates that of maps from cycles of rational curves as
d\to\infty
. It is desirable to characterize the contribution of rational tails in greater generality.

1.5.3.
Stable maps and modular operads

We would be interested to know whether the spaces of torus-fixed stable maps considered in Theorem
C
fit into the framework of modular operads or their colored variants
[
Pet13
]
, and whether there is an analogue of Getzler–Kapranov’s Feynman transform or Theorem
1.18
in this setting.

Definition 2.3
.

We set
[0]:=\varnothing
and
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, then the
wreath symmetric group

S_{m}\wr S_{n}
is defined as the automorphism group of the surjection

\left(\coprod_{i=1}^{n}[m]\right)\to[n]

as an object in
\mathsf{Map}
. Recall the standard isomorphism

S_{m}\wr S_{n}\cong(S_{m})^{n}\rtimes S_{n},

where on the right-hand side
S_{n}
acts on
(S_{m})^{n}
by permuting the coordinates. When
m=0
, define
S_{0}\wr S_{n}
as
S_{n}
.

It is helpful to pass between maps of finite sets and generalized integer partitions.

Definition 2.4
.

A
generalized integer partition
is a finitely-supported function

\nu:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

where we write
\nu_{i}
for
\nu(i)
, and interpret
\nu_{i}
as the number of parts of
\nu
of size
i
. Given
\nu
, define

|\nu|=\sum_{i\geq 1}i\nu_{i},

and write
\nu\vdash n
if
|\nu|=n
. Let
\mathsf{Part}
denote the set of all generalized integer partitions, and write

\mathsf{Part}_{n}\subset\mathsf{Part}

for the subset of partitions
\nu
with
|\nu|=n
. A generalized integer partition
\nu
is called an
integer partition
if
\nu_{0}=0
. The subset of integer partitions is denoted by
\mathsf{Part}^{\star}\subset\mathsf{Part}
and
\mathsf{Part}_{n}^{\star}\subset\mathsf{Part}_{n}
, respectively. Note that
\mathsf{Part}
and
\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
are both commutative semigroups under function addition: given
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots),\nu=(\nu_{0},\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots)\in\mathsf{Part}
, define
\lambda\odot\nu
as the partition that has
\lambda_{i}+\nu_{i}
parts of size
i
for all
i\geq 0.

Under the above definition, the usual ring
\Lambda=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]
of symmetric functions can be thought of as the semigroup algebra of
\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
.

Definition 2.5
.

The automorphism group of a generalized integer partition
\nu
is defined as

\mathbb{S}_{\nu}:=\prod_{i\geq 0}S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}.

When
\nu_{0}=0
, the group
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
S_{|\nu|}
which preserves an integer partition of type
\nu.
The sets
\mathsf{Part}
and
\mathsf{Part}_{n}
are hence enriched with groupoid structures.

The groups
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
are exactly the automorphism groups of finite set maps.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be an object in
\mathsf{Map}
. Define an integer partition
\nu(f)\in\mathsf{Part}
by setting

{\nu(f)}_{i}:=\#\{y\in Y\mid\#f^{-1}(y)=i\}.

Then

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(f:X\to Y)\cong\mathbb{S}_{\nu(f)}.

Remark 2.7
.

We note that
\nu(f)_{0}=0
if and only if
f
is surjective.

Remark 2.8
.

More conceptually,
\mathsf{Map}
carries a symmetric monoidal structure under disjoint unions and isomorphisms induced by set isomorphisms
A\sqcup B\cong B\sqcup A.
Every object
f
in
\mathsf{Map}
is isomorphic to the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{i\geq 0}\left(\bigsqcup_{\nu(f)_{i}}[i]\right)\to\bigsqcup_{i\geq 0}[\nu(f)_{i}].
With this perspective, the lemma states that the automorphism groups in
\mathsf{Map}
are compatible with the symmetric monoidal structure.

2.2.
The ring
\Lambda^{[2]}

For
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, we write

R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu}):=K_{0}(\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{S}_{\nu}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}

for the free
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
-representations over
\mathbb{Q}
. Equivalently,
R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})
is the space of
\mathbb{Q}
-valued class functions of
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
.

Definition 2.9
.

As a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, define

\Lambda^{[2]}:=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathsf{Part}}R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu}).

The multiplication on
\Lambda^{[2]}
is defined via the following operation of merging partitions.

Remark 2.10
.

If
\lambda,\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, there is an inclusion map of partition automorphism groups

\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{S}_{\nu}\to\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu},

well-defined up to conjugating in
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu}
: it is defined by taking disjoint union of the set maps
f_{\lambda},f_{\nu}\in\mathsf{Map}
where
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(f_{\lambda})\cong\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
and
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(f_{\nu})\cong\mathbb{S}_{\nu}.

Definition 2.11
.

Define bilinear maps
\boxtimes:R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\times R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})\to R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu})
by the formula

V\boxtimes W:=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{S}_{\nu}}^{\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu}}V\otimes W.

This construction extends naturally to a bilinear map
\boxtimes:\Lambda^{[2]}\times\Lambda^{[2]}\to\Lambda^{[2]}
.

Write
\mathbf{0}
for the unique element of
\mathsf{Part}_{0}\subset\mathsf{Part}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

The map
\boxtimes:\Lambda^{[2]}\times\Lambda^{[2]}\to\Lambda^{[2]}
defines a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra structure on
\Lambda^{[2]}
, where the identity is given by
1\in R(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{0}}).

Proof.

The product
\boxtimes
is distributive as we can check that
(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})\boxtimes W=(V_{1}\boxtimes W)\oplus(V_{2}\boxtimes W)
and similarlu
V\boxtimes(W_{1}\oplus W_{2})=(V\boxtimes W_{1})\oplus(V\boxtimes W_{2})
. Associativity follows from the fact that the subgroup
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{S}_{\nu}\times\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu\odot\mu}
is well-defined up to conjugacy, and commutativity comes from identifying
\lambda\odot\nu=\nu\odot\lambda
in
\mathsf{Part}.

1\in R(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{0}})
is a multiplicative unit because
\lambda\odot\mathbf{0}=\lambda=\mathbf{0}\odot\lambda
for all partitions
\lambda
.
∎

It will be helpful to relate the ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
to the wreath product symmetric functions
\{\Lambda(S_{m})\}_{m\geq 0}
constructed by Macdonald
[
Mac80
,
Mac95
]
. In the following,
(m^{k})
denotes the partition of
[mk]
with
k
parts of size
m.

Lemma 2.13
.

The subspace
\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}R(\mathbb{S}_{(m^{k})})
forms a subalgebra of
\Lambda^{[2]}
which is isomorphic to
\Lambda(S_{m})
.

Proof.

We observe that
\mathbb{S}_{(m^{k})}\cong S_{m}\wr S_{k}
and the injection
\mathbb{S}_{(m^{k_{1}})}\times\mathbb{S}_{(m^{k_{2}})}\to\mathbb{S}_{(m^{k_{1}+k_{2}})}
is identical to
(S_{m}\wr S_{k_{1}})\times(S_{m}\wr S_{k_{2}})\to S_{m}\wr S_{k_{1}+k_{2}}
. Hence, the induction formula for
\boxtimes
is identical to Macdonald’s definition of the product structure on
\Lambda(S_{m})
in
[
Mac95
, §1.B.4]
.
∎

Since
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i\geq 0}S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}}
, we have
R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})=\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}R(S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}})
. The product
\boxtimes
can be decomposed along the tensor products as follows.

Lemma 2.14
.

Under the identification
R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})=\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}R(S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}})
, the map

\boxtimes:R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\times R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})\to R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu})

is the tensor product
\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}m_{i},
where
m_{i}:R(S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}})\times R(S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}})\to R\left(S_{i}\wr S_{(\lambda\odot\nu)_{i}}\right)
is the induction map from the group homomorphisms
S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}}\times S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}\to S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}+\nu_{i}}
described above.

Proof.

The multiplication
\boxtimes:R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\times R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})\to R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu})
is induced by
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\times\mathbb{S}_{\nu}\to\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu}
, which is itself a product of the group homomorphisms
S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}}\times S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}\to S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}+\nu_{i}}.
Applying
R(\text{-})
takes a product of group homomorphisms to tensor product of linear maps.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\Lambda(S_{i})
be the colimit of

\cdots\to\bigotimes_{i=0}^{N}\Lambda(S_{i})\xrightarrow{-\otimes 1}\bigotimes_{i=0}^{N+1}\Lambda(S_{i})\to\cdots

so that
\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\Lambda(S_{i})=\bigcup_{N=0}^{\infty}\bigotimes_{i=0}^{N}\Lambda(S_{i}).
Then we have

\Lambda^{[2]}\cong\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\Lambda(S_{i}).

Proof.

Firstly, we prove the isomorphism on the level of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces. Unwinding the definitions,

\Lambda^{[2]}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}}R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}}\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}R(S_{i}\wr S_{\lambda_{i}}).

The last item is isomorphic to
\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\left(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R(S_{i}\wr S_{n})\right)
: the summands are matched by identifying integer partitions
\lambda
as sequences of integers
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
that are zero for all but finitely many terms. The previous lemma checks that the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear isomorphism is multiplicative, hence the two are isomorphic as rings.
∎

2.3.
Power sums

In this section, we define the analogues of the power sum symmetric functions in the ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
. As with
\Lambda
and
\Lambda(S_{i})
, we will prove that the generalized power sums freely generate
\Lambda^{[2]}
as a polynomial ring.

Definition 2.16
.

Given a pair
(j,\mu)
where
j\geq 1
is an integer and
\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
, define
p_{j}(\mu)\in R(S_{|\mu|}\wr S_{j})\subset\Lambda^{[2]}
as the virtual character associated to the conjugacy class of an element of the form
((\sigma,e,\ldots,e),\kappa)\in S_{|\mu|}\wr S_{j}
where
\sigma\in S_{|\mu|}
is a permutation of cycle type
\mu
and
\kappa\in S_{j}
is a
j
-cycle.

In the ring
\Lambda=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]
, monomials in the power sums are indexed by integer partitions: an integer partition
\theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
gives the monomial

p_{\theta}:=\prod_{i\geq 1}p_{i}^{\theta_{i}}.

This correspondence between partitions and monomials is useful for bookkeeping. Analogously, monomials in the power sums of
\Lambda^{[2]}
are indexed by
2
-partitions
, which we now define.

Definition 2.17
.

A
2
-partition

\Theta
is a function
\Theta:\mathsf{Part}^{\star}\to\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
such that
\Theta(\mu)=\varnothing
for
|\mu|\gg 0
. The set of
2
-partitions is denoted as
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}.
Because
\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
is a commutative semigroup,
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
carries a commutative semigroup structure under function addition.

Definition 2.18
.

Given
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, we define

\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu}:=\{\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}\mid\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}_{i}}|\Theta(\mu)|=\nu_{i}\,\text{for all }i\geq 0\}.

The point of
2
-partitions is that they naturally index conjugacy classes in the groups
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
, for
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
.

Lemma 2.19
.

For each
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, there is a natural bijection

(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})_{*}\to\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu},

such that for any
\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}
, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})_{*}\times(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})_{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\lambda}\times\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda\odot\nu})_{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\lambda\odot\nu}.}

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
A.3
that the conjugacy classes of
G\wr S_{n}
are in bijection with maps
f:G_{*}\to\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
such that
\sum_{[g]\in G_{*}}|f([g])|=n.
Thus, the set of conjugacy classes of
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
is the subset of
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
given by

\prod_{i\geq 0}\{f\in\mathsf{Map}((S_{i})_{*},\mathsf{Part}^{\star})\mid\sum_{[g]\in(S_{i})_{*}}|f([g])|=\lambda_{i}\}

which agrees with the definition of
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\lambda}
given above. The diagram commutes because the addition on
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
is given by merging partitions on
\mathsf{Part}^{\star}.

∎

Remark 2.20
.

The wreath symmetric groups in this work arise as automorphism groups of half-edges mapping to vertices. The proof of the Lemma suggests that in our setting, the domain and target of a 2-partition should be interpreted as partitions of the half-edges and of vertices, respectively.

Using Lemma
2.19
we can prove the following structural description of
\Lambda^{[2]}
:

Theorem 2.21
.

The ring
\Lambda^{[2]}
is canonically identified with the semigroup algebra of
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
. In particular,

\Lambda^{[2]}=\mathbb{Q}[p_{j}(\mu)\mid j>0,\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}].

Proof.

Lemma
2.19
implies that we can identify

\Lambda^{[2]}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}}R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}}\mathbb{Q}\cdot(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})_{*}

with the free vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
spanned by
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
. The commutative diagram in Lemma
2.19
implies that the multiplication on
\Lambda^{[2]}
from Definition
2.11
agrees with the multiplication structure on the semigroup ring generated by
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
. Under this identification,
p_{j}(\mu)\in\Lambda^{[2]}
corresponds to the
2
-partition which assigns
\mu
to the partition with one part of size
j
. As
j
and
\mu
vary over all positive integers and elements of
\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
respectively, we see that these
2
-partitions freely generate
\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
, so the theorem is proved.
∎

Definition 2.22
.

Given
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
, define
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu}
to be the image of the conjugacy class of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
under the identification of Lemma
2.19
.

Remark 2.23
.

We give a more explicit recipe for the construction of
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, by describing how to compute
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mu)
for some
\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
. Write
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau_{i})_{i\geq 0}\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
. Consider the
|\mu|
-th term of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
given by

\tau_{|\mu|}=((g_{1},\dots,g_{\lambda_{|\mu|}}),\sigma),\text{ where }g_{i}\in S_{|\mu|},\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma\in S_{\nu_{|\mu|}}.

Decompose
\sigma
into disjoint cycles:

\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{\ell}.

Then
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mu)
is defined as the partition with one part of size
j
for each
\sigma_{i}
such that

(i)

\sigma_{i}
is a
j
-cycle, and

(ii)

if we write

\sigma_{i}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{j}),

then the element

g_{k_{1}}\cdots g_{k_{j}}\in S_{|\mu|}

has cycle type
\mu
.

Definition 2.24
.

Given a pair of integer partitions
\theta,\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
, define

p_{\theta}(\mu):=\prod_{i\geq 1}p_{i}(\mu)^{\theta_{i}}\in\Lambda^{[2]}.

Given a
2
-partition
\Theta
, we define the monomial

p_{\Theta}:=\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}p_{\Theta(\mu)}(\mu).

By Theorem
2.21
, the monomials
p_{\Theta}
for
\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
form a linear basis for
\Lambda^{[2]}
, and we have
p_{\Theta}\cdot p_{\Theta^{\prime}}=p_{\Theta+\Theta^{\prime}}
for all
\Theta,\Theta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
.

2.4.
The Frobenius characteristic of a
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
-representation

Definition 2.25
.

Let
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, and let
V
be a complex representation of the group
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
. Denote by
\operatorname{ch}(V)\in R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\subset\Lambda^{[2]}
the class of the representation
V
in
R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})
. We refer to
\operatorname{ch}(V)
as the
Frobenius characteristic
of
V
.

For any generalized partition
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, recall from Lemma
2.19
that there is a canonical bijection

(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})_{*}\to\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu}\text{ given by }[\boldsymbol{\tau}]\mapsto\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}.

The class function determined by
\boldsymbol{\tau}
is given by
p_{\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}\in R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})\subset\Lambda^{[2]}.

Definition 2.26
.

Given
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
, we refer to
p_{\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}\in\Lambda^{[2]}
as the
cycle index
of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.

We have the following formula for the Frobenius characteristic of
\operatorname{ch}(V)
in terms of cycle indices, which follows from the general isomorphism between
R(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda})
and the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued class functions on
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 2.27
.

Suppose that
V
is a finite-dimensional complex
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
-representation. Then

\operatorname{ch}(V)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\tau}|V)\cdot p_{\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}.

2.5.
Forgetful morphisms

There are two forgetful maps from
\Lambda^{[2]}
to
\Lambda
corresponding to the two group homomorphisms

\mathrm{Sym}(Y)\leftarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(f:X\to Y)\to\mathrm{Sym}(X).

Definition 2.28
.

Define algebra homomorphisms

\varphi_{1}:\Lambda^{[2]}\to\Lambda,

\varphi_{2}:\Lambda^{[2]}\to\Lambda

by assigning

\varphi_{1}(p_{j}(\mu)):=p_{j}

\varphi_{2}(p_{j}(\mu)):=p_{j}\circ p_{\mu}

respectively and extending multiplicatively.

Though we will not use these maps in the present work, we include them here with an eye towards futue applications of the theory. The homomorphisms
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
have the following representation-theoretic meaning. The projections
S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}\to S_{\nu_{i}}
fit together to define a map
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}\to\prod_{i\geq 0}S_{\nu_{i}}
. Viewing the latter group as a Young subgroup of
S_{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}}
, we get a homomorphism

\mathbb{S}_{\nu}\to S_{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}},

well-defined up to conjugacy. The map
\varphi_{1}
has the property that

\varphi_{1}(\operatorname{ch}(V))=\operatorname{ch}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{S}_{\nu}]}\mathbb{Q}[S_{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}}]).

On the other hand, we have an inclusion of groups
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}\to S_{|\nu|}
, by including each
S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}\to S_{i\nu_{i}}
, and then including the product
\prod_{i}S_{i\nu_{i}}
as a Young subgroup of
S_{|\nu|}
. The morphism
\varphi_{2}
has the property that

\varphi_{2}(\operatorname{ch}(V))=\operatorname{ch}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[\mathbb{S}_{\nu}]}\mathbb{Q}[S_{|\nu|}]=\operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathbb{S}_{\nu}}^{S_{|\nu|}}V).

These claims about
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
can be proved by analyzing the behavior of conjugacy classes under the described group homomorphisms.

3.
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-modules and polynomial functors

While the previous section gives a concrete algebraic description of
\Lambda^{[2]},
a more conceptual perspective is given by its categorifications to both a representation category and a category of polynomial functors. These enhancements give a streamlined definition of the enriched plethysm action of
\Lambda^{[2]}
on
\Lambda\otimes\Lambda
and provide access to the
\lambda
-ring structure. Recall that the ring

\Lambda=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},\ldots]=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R(S_{n})

can be understood as the Grothendieck ring of bounded
\mathbb{S}
-modules
, where a
\mathbb{S}
-module
\mathcal{V}
is defined as a collection
\{\mathcal{V}(n)\mid n\geq 0\}
where each
\mathcal{V}(n)
is a finite-dimensional
{S}_{n}
-representation over
\mathbb{Q}
; in this context,
bounded
means that
\mathcal{V}(n)=0
for all but finitely many
n
. Equivalently, an
\mathbb{S}
-module is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathcal{V}
together with a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{V}(n),

such that each
\mathcal{V}(n)
is a finite-dimensional
S_{n}
-representation; boundedness is equivalent to requiring that
\mathcal{V}
is also finite-dimensional.

3.1.
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-modules

We define the category of
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-modules analogously to the category of
\mathbb{S}
-modules.

Definition 3.1
.

An
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-module

\mathcal{V}

\{\mathcal{V}(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}\}
where each
\mathcal{V}(\lambda)

\mathbb{S}^{[2]}

\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathcal{V}
together with a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}}\mathcal{V}(\lambda)

where each
\mathcal{V}(\lambda)

\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}

is

Let
{\mathbb{S}^{[2]}}\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}

\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-modules, and let
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}^{\star}

\mathbb{S}^{[2]}\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}
(and on

Remark 3.2
.

The direct sum decomposition
\Lambda^{[2]}=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathsf{Part}}R(\mathbb{S}_{\nu})
leads to a ring isomorphism between
\Lambda^{[2]}
and
K_{0}({\mathbb{S}^{[2]}}\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}^{\star})

Remark 3.3
.

The category
\mathsf{Part}
may be identified with the groupoid of disconnected generalized corollas: to a generalized partition
\nu=(0^{\nu_{0}}1^{\nu_{1}}\dots)
we associate a graph
\mathrm{Cor}_{\nu}
that is a disjoint union of
\nu_{i}
copies of an
i
-corolla, which is
i
half-edges attached to a vertex. The group
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
agrees with the automorphism group of
\mathrm{Cor}_{\nu}
. The collection of connected corollas correspond to the partitions that have only a single part and have automorphism groups
S_{n}
.

Extending the above remark, an
\mathbb{S}
-module is a functor from the groupoid of connected corollas to
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and that an
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-module is a functor from the groupoid of all corollas to
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The following formula is a representation-theoretic version of building corollas by taking disjoint unions of connected corollas.

To begin with, we note that for a
S_{i}
-representation
V
, the tensor power
V^{\otimes\nu_{i}}
is an
S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}
-representation; given a map of
S_{i}
-representations
\varphi:V\to W,
its
\nu_{i}
-th tensor power
\varphi^{\otimes\nu_{i}}:V^{\otimes\nu_{i}}\to W^{\otimes\nu_{i}}
is a map of
S_{i}\wr S_{\nu_{i}}
-representations.

Definition 3.4
.

Define
(\text{-})^{\otimes}:\mathbb{S}\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}\to\mathbb{S}^{[2]}\text{-}\mathsf{Vect}
as the functor that sends an
\mathbb{S}
-module
\mathcal{W}
to the
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-module
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes}
defined by

\mathcal{W}^{\otimes}(\nu):=\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}(\mathcal{W}(i))^{\otimes\nu_{i}},

and sends a morphism
\boldsymbol{\varphi}:\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\mathcal{W}_{2}
, consisting of
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{W}_{1}(i)\to\mathcal{W}_{2}(i),
to the map
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\otimes}:\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\otimes}
given by

\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\varphi_{i}^{\otimes\nu_{i}}:\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\otimes}(\nu)\to\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\otimes}(\nu)

for each
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
. For a fixed
\nu\in\mathsf{Part}
, we set

\mathcal{W}^{\otimes\nu}:=\bigotimes_{i\geq 0}\left(\mathcal{W}(i)\right)^{\otimes\nu_{i}},

which is an
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
-representation.

It is helpful to see the
\mathbb{S}^{[2]}
-module
\mathcal{W}^{\otimes\nu}
as a monomial with exponent

with the
\mathbb{S}
-module
\mathcal{W}
being the variable. This will be made precise in the next section, in which we establish the correspondence between

-modules and polynomial functors of
\mathbb{S}
-modules.

\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star},

\overline{\partial}_{\mu}f:=D(p_{\mu})(f)=\left(\prod_{i\geq 1}i^{\mu_{i}}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\cdots}f}{\partial p_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\partial p_{2}^{\mu_{2}}\cdots}.

Writing
\tcirc
for
\tcirc\Delta
, the plethystic action
\Lambda^{[2]}\times\hat{\Lambda}\to\hat{\Lambda}
is determined by

p_{n}(\mu)\tcirc f=\psi_{n}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}f).

Extending multiplicatively, for
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star},
we set
\psi_{\tilde{\mu}}:=\prod_{i\geq 1}\psi_{i}^{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}.
Then
p_{\tilde{\mu}}(\mu)\tcirc f=\psi_{\tilde{\mu}}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}f).

4.1.
Graph preliminaries

The following definition of a graph is well-suited for working with moduli spaces of curves.

Definition 4.1
.

A
graph

G
is a tuple

G=(V(G),H(G),r_{G},\iota_{G}),

where

•

V(G)
is a finite set of
vertices
and
H(G)
is a finite set of
half-edges
;

•

r_{G}:H(G)\to V(G)
is a map of sets, called the
root map
;

•

\iota_{G}:H(G)\to H(G)
is a fixed-point free involution.

We obtain an equivalence relation on
H_{G}
by setting
h\sim\iota_{G}(h)
for all
h\in H(G)
; the elements of the set
E_{G}
of equivalence classes are the
edges
of
G
. A graph
G
has a
geometric realization
which is a topological space. We say
G
is
connected
if its geometric realization is connected.

Let
\mathsf{Graph}
be the groupoid of graphs, where an isomorphism

f:G\to G^{\prime}

is a pair of bijections

f_{H}:H(G)\to H(G^{\prime})

and

f_{V}:V(G)\to V(G^{\prime})

such that
\iota_{G^{\prime}}\circ f_{H}=f_{H}\circ\iota_{G}
and
r_{G^{\prime}}\circ f_{H}=f_{V}\circ r_{G}
. For any graph
G
we have a canonical inclusion of groups

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Graph}}(G)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(r_{G}:H(G)\to V(G)),

and we identify
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Graph}}(G)
with its image in
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(r_{G}:H(G)\to V(G))
.
We define the
valence
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
to be

\operatorname{val}(v):=|r_{G}^{-1}(v)|.

From the graph
G
we define the partition
\nu(G)\in\mathsf{Part}
by setting

\nu(G)_{i}:=|\{v\in V(G)\mid\operatorname{val}(v)=i\}|

If, for each
i
, we choose

•

an ordering of the vertices
v\in V(G)
with
\operatorname{val}(v)=i

•

for each vertex
v
of valence
i
, an ordering of
r_{G}^{-1}(v)
,

we obtain an isomorphism

(4.2)

\psi_{G}:\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Map}}(r_{G})\xrightarrow[\phantom{long}]{\sim}\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}.

Different choices of orderings lead to isomorphisms which differ by conjugation in
\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
. Altogether, this realizes
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
as a subgroup of
\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
, up to conjugacy.

4.2.
Vertex-decorated graphs

We will also need graphs with genus decorations at the vertices.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathsf{VGraph}
to be the groupoid of pairs

where

is a finite connected graph, and

has

equal to

Definition 4.4
.

Let

be a vertex-weighted graph of genus

The

of

given by

h\mapsto\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}\in\mathsf{Part},\text{ with }\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}_{i}:=\#\{v\in V(G)\mid\mathrm{val}(v)=i,w(v)=h\}.

in the sense that
\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(0)}\odot\cdots\odot\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(g)}=\nu(G).

Definition 4.5

The automorphism group of a vertex weighted graph
\mathbf{G}=(G,w)\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph})
is the subgroup preserving the vertex weighting:

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{VGraph}}(\mathbf{G}):=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Graph}}(\mathbf{G})\mid w(v)=w(\sigma(v))\text{ for all }v\in V(G_{C})\,\}.

We always use the boldface notation
\mathbf{G}
for vertex-weighted graph, and we write
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})
for
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{VGraph}}(\mathbf{G})
.

For
\mathbf{G}=(G,w)\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g})
, there is a canonical way to factor the embedding

in a way that accounts for the vertex weights.

Lemma 4.6

Define

\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})}:=\prod_{h=0}^{g}\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}}=\prod_{h=0}^{g}\left(\prod_{i\geq 0}S_{i}\wr S_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}_{i}}\right).

Then the group embedding

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{G})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}

factors through

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{G})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})}\hookrightarrow\prod_{i\geq 0}S_{i}\wr S_{\sum_{h=0}^{g}\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}_{i}}=\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}.

Recall from Section
2.2
that conjugacy classes in

are classified by

-tuples of
2
-partitions
(\Theta_{0},\ldots,\Theta_{g})
such that
\Theta_{h}\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}}
for each

.

\mathbb{S}
-variety
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
in terms of
\mathcal{M}
as follows. For any graded
\mathbb{S}
-variety
\mathcal{X}
and integer
m\geq 0
, write
\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{m\times\mathbb{S}}}\mathcal{X}
for the
S_{m}
-object in the category of graded
\mathbb{S}
-varieties determined by

\left(\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{m\times\mathbb{S}}}\mathcal{X}\right)(n)=\operatorname{Res}^{S_{m+n}}_{S_{m}\times S_{n}}\mathcal{X}(m+n).

Observe that for any integer
k\geq 0
, the graded
\mathbb{S}
-variety

\left(\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{m\times\mathbb{S}}}\mathcal{X}\right)^{\boxtimes k}

admits an
(S_{m}\wr S_{k})
-action, and if
\lambda\in\mathsf{Part}
, then

\prod_{i\geq 0}\left(\operatorname{Res}_{S_{i}\times\mathbb{S}}^{\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{X}\right)^{\boxtimes\lambda_{i}}

admits a
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
-action. Therefore, for any connected graph
G
, the product

\midboxtimes_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)\times\mathbb{S}}}\mathcal{X}

admits a
\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
-action. Returning to moduli of stable curves, we see that there is an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{S}
-varieties

(4.9)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}\cong\left[\left(\midboxtimes_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)\times\mathbb{S}}}\mathcal{M}\right)/\operatorname{Aut}(G)\right][1-\chi(G)],

where
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
acts on the product as a subgroup of
\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
. The degree shift accounts for the first Betti number of the graph
G.

4.4.
The fine dual graph stratification

To give a stratification of the
\mathbb{S}
-variety
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, we will use a finer notion of a dual graph.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
C
be a connected nodal curve of arithmetic genus
g
. The
fine dual graph

\mathbf{G}_{C}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g})
of
C
is the pair
\mathbf{G}_{C}=(G_{C},w_{C})
where
G_{C}
is the coarse dual graph of
C
and
w_{C}:V(G_{C})\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
assigns each irreducible component of the normalization its geometric genus.

Given
\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g})
, define an
\mathbb{S}
-variety
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbf{G}}
, where

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbf{G}}(n)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(n)

is the locus of stable curves whose fine dual graph is equal to
\mathbf{G}
. We thus have an
S_{n}
-equivariant stratification

(4.11)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(n)=\coprod_{\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g})}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbf{G}}(n);

analogously to (
4.8
), only finitely many of the strata appearing on the right-hand side of (
4.11
) are nonempty for fixed
n
. A fine stratum corresponding to
\mathbf{G}=(G,w)
satisfies

(4.12)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbf{G}}\cong\left(\midboxtimes_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{M}_{w(v)}\right)/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G}),

where
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})
acts on the product as a subgroup of
\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})}
.

4.5.
Caterpillars and rational tails

The fine graph stratification (
4.11
) has infinitely many strata. There are two sources of infinitude in
\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}
, as we will now describe. Given
\mathbf{G}\in\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}
, define the
core
of
\mathbf{G}
to be the minimal vertex-weighted subgraph
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{core}}\subset\mathbf{G}
such that the genus of
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{core}}
is equal to
g
. Then the two sources of infinitude in
\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}
are as follows:

•

arbitrarily large trees of weight zero vertices can be attached to the core of the graph;

•

if
e\in E(\mathbf{G})
is an edge, then
e
can be subdivided arbitrarily many times by weight zero vertices.

We would like finite stratifications where possible, because they will lead to finite formulas for the Serre characteristic
\mathsf{e}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
. Define the
\mathbb{S}
-subspace
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
by setting

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(n)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(n)

to be the subspace of curves whose dual graphs are equal to their own cores. The following formula is standard in the theory of stable curves.

Lemma 4.13
.

For
g\geq 1
, we have

\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})=\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}})\circ\left(p_{1}+\frac{\partial\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0})}{\partial p_{1}}\right).

Modulo the computation of
\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0})
, Lemma
4.13
reduces the calculation of
\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
to that of
\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}})
, and in a sense eliminates the first source of infinitude discussed above. The characteristic
\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\mathrm{nrt}})
was computed by Getzler
[
Get98
]
and then by Petersen
[
Pet12
]
. Petersen’s calculation fits into the framework developed herein, but for ease of exposition we will assume that
g\geq 2
for the remainder of this section. To stratify
\mathsf{e}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}})
, we introduce some natural subcategories of
\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}
.

Definition 4.14
.

Given a graph
\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g})
, we say
\mathbf{G}
is
stable
if
{2w(v)-2+\operatorname{val}(v)>0}
for all vertices
v\in V(\mathbf{G})
. Let

\Gamma_{g}\subset\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}

be the full subgroupoid of stable graphs. Given
\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}^{\mathrm{stab}})
, write
\hat{\mathbf{G}}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g})
for the graph obtained from
\mathbf{G}
by subdividing each edge
e\in E(\mathbf{G})
by a single weight zero vertex. Let

\hat{\Gamma}_{g}\subset\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}

be the full subgroupoid consisting of objects of the form
\hat{\mathbf{G}}
for
\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\Gamma_{g})
.

The category
\hat{\Gamma}_{g}
can be thought of as the groupoid of barycentrically subdivided stable graphs. In particular, we have

|\mathrm{Iso}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g})|=|\mathrm{Iso}(\Gamma_{g})|<\infty,

and we will use
\hat{\Gamma}_{g}
to give a finite stratification of the
\mathbb{S}
-variety
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}
.

Definition 4.15
.

Given a connected nodal curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
such that
\mathbf{G}_{C}=\mathbf{G}_{C}^{\mathrm{core}}
, we define

\mathfrak{G}_{C}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g})

in two steps:

(1)

Take
\mathbf{G}_{C}
and smooth out all weight zero vertices of valence
2
, i.e. treat them as part of the underlying edge (the resulting graph is sometimes called the
stabilization
of
\mathbf{G}_{C}
);

(2)

Subdivide each edge in the resulting graph exactly once, with a vertex of weight zero.

We call
\mathfrak{G}_{C}
the
reduced fine dual graph
of
C
.

Given
\mathfrak{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g})
, define an
\mathbb{S}
-variety

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\mathrm{nrt}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}

by letting

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(n)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(n)

denote the locus of curves with reduced fine dual graph equal to
\mathfrak{G}
; then

(4.16)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}=\coprod_{\mathfrak{G}\in\mathrm{Iso}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g})}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\mathrm{nrt}},

so we get a finite stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}
. Now define a
(S_{2}\times\mathbb{S})
-variety
\mathcal{C}
as follows: for
n\geq 1
, let

\mathcal{C}(n)\subset\operatorname{Res}_{S_{2}\times S_{n}}^{S_{n+2}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2}

be the locus of stable genus zero curves whose coarse dual graph is a path (this includes the case of a single isolated vertex), and such that the first two markings are on the two extreme components of the curve. Then
S_{2}
acts on
\mathcal{C}(n)
by permuting the first two marked points, and
S_{n}
acts by permuting the other
n
markings. We also formally set
\mathcal{C}(0)=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, with the trivial
(S_{2}\times S_{0})
-action. The utility of this definition is that it yields the following description of the stratum
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\mathrm{nrt}}
. Let
V^{b}(\mathfrak{G})
be the set of vertices for
w(v)=0
and
\operatorname{val}(v)=2
, and set

V^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathfrak{G})=V(\mathfrak{G})\smallsetminus V^{b}(\mathfrak{G})

(4.17)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\mathrm{nrt}}\cong\left[\left(\midboxtimes_{v\in V^{b}(\mathfrak{G})}\mathcal{C}\right)\boxtimes\left(\midboxtimes_{v\in V^{\mathrm{stab}}(\mathfrak{G})}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{M}_{w(v)}\right)\right]/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{G}),

where
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{G})
acts as a subgroup of
\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathfrak{G})}
. The product formula (
4.17
) reflects the fact that any stable curve in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\mathrm{nrt}}
is obtained from a graph in
\hat{\Gamma}_{g}
by choosing a (possibly empty) chain of pointed rational curves for each bivalent vertex of weight
0
, choosing a point of the
\mathbb{S}
-variety
\mathcal{M}_{w(v)}
for each remaining vertex, and then gluing as determined by the graph.

5.
The Pólya–Petersen character

To translate the stratifications of §
4
into concrete formulas involving symmetric functions, we introduce the Pólya–Petersen character of a graph. Let
G\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{Graph})
. Recall that the isomorphism (
4.2
) gives up to conjugacy an embedding of groups
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
Pólya–Petersen character
of
G
is

\zeta_{G}:=\operatorname{ch}\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}^{\mathbb{S}_{\nu_{G}}}\mathrm{triv}\right)\in\Lambda^{[2]}_{\nu_{G}}.

The Pólya–Petersen character immediately lets us calculate Serre characteristic of the graded
\mathbb{S}
-varieties
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
which stratify
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For any
G\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{Graph})
, we have

\mathsf{e}_{t}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\left(\zeta_{G}\cdot t^{|E(G)|}\right)\tcirc\Delta\mathsf{e}_{t}^{\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}[\chi(G)-1]

\displaystyle\cong\left(\boxtimes_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{M}\right)/\operatorname{Aut}(G)

\displaystyle\cong\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}^{\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}}\operatorname{Res}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}^{\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}}\boxtimes_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{M}\right)/\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}

\displaystyle\cong\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}^{\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}\times\boxtimes_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{\operatorname{val}(v)}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{M}\right)/\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}

The first isomorphism is (
4.9
). The second isomorphism comes from the fact that given a subgroup
H\subset G
and an
H
-variety
X,
there is
X/H\cong(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}X)/G.
Similarly, the third isomorphism is from the fact that given a subgroup
H\subset G
and a
G
-variety
Y,
there is
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\operatorname{Res}_{H}^{G}Y\cong G/H\times Y,
where the right hand side admits the diagonal
G
-action.
We can check that these isomorphisms preserve the relevant
S_{n}
-action and hence are isomorphisms of
\mathbb{S}
-varieties. The claimed formula now follows from taking equivariant Serre characteristics: the factor of
t^{|E(G)|}
comes from the formula

1-\chi(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1

and Lemma
3.38
.
∎

For
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
, we recall from Definition
2.22
that
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu}
denotes its cycle type, and that for any
\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
, we have

p_{\Theta}=\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}p_{\Theta(\mu)}(\mu).

Using again the embedding
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
(
4.2
), and using the notation
\boldsymbol{\tau}
for elements of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, we write
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
for the
2
-partition encoding the conjugacy class of the image of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
in
\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}
. With this notation in hand, basic character theory yields the following formula for
\zeta_{G}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
G\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{Graph})
. Then

\zeta_{G}=\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)}p_{\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}.

Example 5.4
.

Consider the graph
G
given in Figure
2
. In this case
\nu(G)=(2^{3}3^{2})
, so

\mathbb{S}_{\nu(G)}=(S_{2}\wr S_{3})\times(S_{3}\wr S_{2}),

and
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is an extension of
S_{3}
by
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, where the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
factor corresponds to a horizontal flip, and the elements of
S_{3}
permute the vertices of valence
2
and their adjacent edges.

Figure 2.

Using Lemma
5.3
, we compute the Pólya–Petersen character of
G
as

\displaystyle\zeta_{G}=\frac{1}{12}\left(\underbrace{p_{1}(1^{2})^{3}p_{1}(1^{3})^{2}}_{\text{identity}}\right.

\displaystyle+\underbrace{3p_{1}(1^{2})p_{2}(1^{2})p_{1}(1^{1}2^{1})}_{2\text{-cycles without flip}}+\underbrace{2p_{3}(1^{2})p_{1}(3)^{2}}_{3\text{-cycles without flip}}

\displaystyle+\left.\underbrace{p_{1}(2^{1})^{3}p_{2}(1^{3})}_{\text{flip}}+\underbrace{3p_{1}(2^{1})p_{2}(1^{2})p_{2}(1^{3})}_{2\text{-cycles with flip}}+\underbrace{2p_{3}(2^{1})p_{2}(3^{1})}_{3\text{-cycles with flip}}\right).

Definition 5.5
.

For
G\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{Graph})
and
\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}
, define

\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G):=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\mid\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\Theta\}.

We observe that
\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)\neq\varnothing
implies that
\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\nu(G)}
, and that each
\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)
is a union of conjugacy classes of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.

Corollary 5.6
.

We have

\mathsf{e}_{t}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G})=t^{|\nu(G)|/2}\sum_{\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\psi_{\Theta(\mu)}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}\mathsf{e}_{t}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{M})).

Corollary
5.6
implies Theorem
B
, whose statement we now recall. We have set the notation

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}_{t}(\overline{\mathcal{M}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{a}=\mathsf{e}_{t}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{M}),

and for a
2
-partition
\Theta
we set
||\Theta||=\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}|\Theta(\mu)|\cdot|\mu|
.

Theorem B
.

The total generating function is given by

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\sum_{\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}}O(\Theta)\cdot t^{||\Theta||/2}\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\psi_{\Theta(\mu)}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}\mathsf{a})

where
O(\Theta)\in\mathbb{Q}
is defined by

O(\Theta):=\sum_{G\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathsf{Graph})}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

We have

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\sum_{G\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathsf{Graph})}\mathsf{e}_{t}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}).

Applying the formula of Corollary
5.6
, we obtain

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\sum_{G\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathsf{Graph})}t^{|\nu(G)|/2}\sum_{\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\psi_{\Theta(\mu)}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}\mathsf{a}).

Note that if
\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)\neq\varnothing
, then
||\Theta||=|\nu(G)|
. Therefore, we can switch the summations over
G
and
\Theta
to obtain

\overline{\mathsf{a}}=\sum_{\Theta\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}}t^{||\Theta||/2}\sum_{G\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathsf{Graph})}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}^{\Theta}(G)|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\psi_{\Theta(\mu)}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}\mathsf{a}),

and Theorem
B
is proved.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Theorem
B
is proved by summing the contribution of each coarse dual graph
G\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{Graph})
individually. For us, the Pólya–Petersen character is crucial for this purpose: we do not, for example, see a way to express these contributions in terms of the character

z_{G}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{Half}}}:=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{Aut}(G)}^{\operatorname{Aut}(H(G))}\operatorname{triv}\in\Lambda_{|H(G)|},

which remembers only the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
on the set of half-edges
H(G)
. One naïve approach to graph strata would be to use the adjoint operator
D(z_{G}^{\mathrm{Half}})
with respect to multiplication by
z_{G}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{Half}}}
, and guess that the generating function

D(z_{G}^{\mathrm{Half}})(h_{|V(G)|}\circ\mathsf{a})

is the contribution from the graph
G
, where
h_{k}\in\Lambda
denotes the
k
th homogeneous symmetric function. This is not refined enough to specify individual graph strata. For example, if
P
is the connected graph with one edge and two vertices, then

z_{P}^{\mathrm{Half}}=h_{2}\in\Lambda_{2},

and one checks that

D(z_{P}^{\mathrm{Half}})(h_{2}\circ\mathsf{a})=\zeta_{P}\tcirc\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{a}\cdot(\zeta_{L}\tcirc\mathsf{a}),

where
L
is the loop graph with one vertex and one edge. In other words, the formula receives contribution from both the graph
P
and
L.

5.1.
Vertex-weighted characters

We now turn to vertex weighted graphs and the fine graph stratification that they define. This will lead to the proof of Theorem
A
. The Pólya–Petersen character of a vertex weighted graph is defined via the embedding of its automorphism group described in Lemma
4.6
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathbf{G}\in\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}
be a vertex weighted graph of genus
g.
The Pólya–Petersen character of
\mathbf{G}
is defined as

\zeta_{\mathbf{G}}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{G})}^{\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})}}\mathrm{triv}\in R(\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})})\subset\bigotimes_{h=0}^{g}\Lambda^{[2]}.

The terms in the tensor product on the right hand side are in bijection with the possible vertex weights on the graph. In this way, the Pólya–Petersen character picks out the precise genus contribution from each vertex and calculates the equivariant Euler characteristic of the corresponding fine graph stratum.

To work with these genus-decorated characters, we write

\bigotimes_{h=0}^{g}\Lambda^{[2]}=\mathbb{Q}[p^{(i)}_{1}(\mu),p^{(i)}_{2}(\mu),\ldots\mid\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star},\,0\leq i\leq g],

and define

\mathrm{proj}_{i}:\bigoplus_{h=0}^{g}\hat{\Lambda}\otimes\hat{\Lambda}\to\hat{\Lambda}\otimes\hat{\Lambda}

as the projection onto the
i
th factor of the direct sum. The proof of the following formula follows from the same calculation as in Proposition
5.2
.

Remark 5.9
.

The map
\mathrm{proj}_{i}
defined above is not to be confused with the projection maps
\mathrm{proj}_{\mu}:\hat{\Lambda}\otimes\hat{\Lambda}\to\hat{\Lambda}
from Definition
3.18
where
\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}.

Lemma 5.10
.

Define

\tcirc_{g}:\left(\bigotimes_{h=0}^{g}\Lambda^{[2]}\right)\times\bigoplus_{h=0}^{g}(\hat{\Lambda}\otimes\hat{\Lambda})\to\hat{\Lambda}

by

p_{n}^{(i)}(\mu)\tcirc_{g}q=\psi_{n}(\mathrm{proj}_{\mu}(\mathrm{proj}_{i}(q))),

and requiring that
f\mapsto f\tcirc_{g}q
is an algebra homomorphism for all
q\in\hat{\Lambda}\otimes\hat{\Lambda}
. Then

\mathsf{e}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbf{G}})=\zeta_{\mathbf{G}}\tcirc_{g}\left(\bigoplus_{h=0}^{g}\Delta\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\right).

We will apply Lemma
5.10
to derive Theorem
A
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a vertex-weighted graph with automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{VGraph}}(\mathbf{G}),
and let

\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\tau}_{g})\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})\subset\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})},

so that each
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}}.
As before, we associate a 2-partition
\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}}\in\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}}
encoding the conjugacy class of
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}
. The following lemma upgrades the statements of Lemma
5.3
and Corollary
5.6
to the genus-graded setting. The first part is the usual character formula for finite groups, and the second part follows from the stratification formula (
4.12
).

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\mathbf{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathsf{VGraph}_{g}),
then

\zeta_{\mathbf{G}}=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{G})|}\sum_{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})}\bigotimes_{h=0}^{g}p_{\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}}}\in\bigotimes_{h=0}^{g}\Lambda^{[2]}.

For each
\underline{\Theta}=(\Theta_{0},\dots,\Theta_{g})\in\prod_{h=0}^{g}\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}_{\underline{\nu}(\mathbf{G})^{(h)}},
define

\mathrm{Aut}^{\underline{\Theta}}(\mathbf{G}):=\{\underline{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{G})\mid(\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{0}},\dots,\Theta^{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{g}})=\underline{\Theta}\}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G}).

Then

\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbf{G}})=\sum_{\underline{\Theta}\in\prod_{h=0}^{g}\mathsf{Part}^{[2]}}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}^{\underline{\Theta}}(\mathbf{G})|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})|}\prod_{h=0}^{g}\prod_{\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}}\psi_{\Theta_{h}(\mu)}(\overline{\partial}_{\mu}\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{M}_{h})).

Applying the formula for Pólya–Petersen characters of vertex-weighted graphs in Lemma
5.11
to the reduced fine dual graph stratification (
4.17
), we obtain the following formula for the Serre characteristic of a reduced fine dual graph stratum.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}\in\mathrm{Ob}(\hat{\Gamma}_{g})
, and let
\tcirc_{g}
be as above. Set

\mathsf{c}:=\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{C})-\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}}(\operatorname{Res}^{\mathbb{S}}_{S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{M}_{0})\in K_{0}(\mathsf{MHS})\otimes{\Lambda}\otimes\hat{\Lambda}.

Then

\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\mathrm{nrt}})=\zeta_{\mathfrak{G}}\tcirc_{g}\left((\Delta\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{M}_{0})+\mathsf{c})\oplus\bigoplus_{h=1}^{g}\Delta\mathsf{e}^{\mathbb{S}}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\right).

The
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{S}
-module
\Delta(\mathcal{M}_{0})+\mathsf{c}
is precisely the contribution from genus zero vertices in (
4.17
): the caterpillar generating function
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{C})
is inserted at bivalent vertices. We can express Lemma
5.12
in terms of power sums as follows.

Lemma 5.13
.

For an integer
h\geq 0
and
\mu\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
, define

Background on wreath products

Let
G

G_{*}
denote the set of conjugacy classes of
G

Definition A.1
.

Define the wreath product of
G
and
S_{n}
as

G_{n}:=G\wr S_{n}:=G^{n}\rtimes S_{n},

where
S_{n}
acts on
G^{n}
by permutation. Concretely,
G_{n}
can be seen as the group of
n\times n
permutation matrices with entries in
G
. Note that
G_{0}
is the trivial group.

Conjugacy classes in
G_{n}
have been completely classified, as we now recall.

Definition A.2
.

Let
\omega=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n};\sigma)\in G_{n}.
For each conjugacy class
c\in G_{*}
, define
\lambda_{\omega}(c)\in\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
by setting
{\lambda_{\omega}(c)}_{i}
to be the number of
i
-cycles in
\sigma
such that the product of the corresponding elements of
G
, in the cyclic order determined by the cycle, lies in
c
. This defines
\lambda_{\omega}:G_{*}\to\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
as the
cycle type
of
\omega
.

A map
G_{*}\to\mathsf{Part}^{\star}
that corresponds to a cycle type in
G\wr S_{n}
can be thought of as a partition of
n
with the additional data of an element of
G_{*}
for each part of the partition. This is equivalent to requiring that
\sum_{[g]\in G_{*}}|\lambda_{\omega}([g])|=n.

Lemma A.3
.

[
Mac95

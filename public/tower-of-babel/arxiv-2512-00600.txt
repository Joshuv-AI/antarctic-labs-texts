Abstract
. The theory of slice regular (also called hyperholomorphic) functions is a generalization of complex analysis originally given in the quaternionic framework, and then further extended to Clifford algebras, octonions, and to real alternative algebras. Recently, we have extended this theory to the case of (real) even-dimensional Euclidean space. We provided several tools for studying slice regular functions in this context, such as a path-representation formula, slice-solutions, hyper-solutions and hyper-sigma balls. When adding an algebra structure to Euclidean space we have more properties related to the possibility of multiplying elements in the algebra. In this paper, we focus on exploring the properties specific to the context of the algebra of sedenions which is rather peculiar, being the algebra non associative and non alternative. The consideration of a non alternative algebra is a novelty in the context of slice regularity which opens the way to a number of further progresses. We offer an explicit characterization for slice-solutions and hyper-solutions, and we determine the convergence domain of the power series in the form q ↦ ∑ n ∈ ℕ ( q − p ) ∗ n ​ a n q\mapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(q-p)^{*n}a_{n} with sedenionic coefficients. Unlike the case of complex numbers and quaternions, the convergence domain in this context is determined by two convergence radii instead of one. These results are closely tied to the presence of zero divisors in the algebra of sedenions.

Main results

In this section, we state our main results. To this end, we need to recall some notations and definitions. The Cayley–Dickson construction
[
17
]
inductively produces a sequence of algebras over the field of real numbers, by setting

A_{0}:=\mathbb{R},

and defining

(2.1)

A_{\ell+1}:=A_{\ell}+A_{\ell}e_{2^{\ell}},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}.

Each
A_{\ell+1}
has twice the dimension of the previous
A_{\ell}
, and

satisfy:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}(a+be_{2^{\ell}})(c+de_{2^{\ell}}):=ac-\overline{d}b+(da+b\overline{c})e_{2^{\ell}},\\
\overline{a+be_{2^{\ell}}}:=a-be_{2}^{\ell},\end{cases}\qquad\forall\ a,b,c,d\in A_{\ell},

where
\overline{x}:=x
when

, and

is an imaginary unit in

The first five Cayley-Dickson algebra respectively are: real numbers

Definition 3.1

In the sequel, we shall denote the set of imaginary units in the algebra of octonions by

Definition 3.2

Let

Proposition 3.3

be a special triple of

.

(ii)

{\imath}{\jmath}=-{\jmath}{\imath}
,
{\jmath}{\kappa}=-{\kappa}{\jmath}
and
{\kappa}{\imath}=-{\imath}{\kappa}

Proof.

If there is

such that
{\imath},{\jmath}\in\mathbb{C}_{L}
, then

It implies that

({\imath}{\jmath}){\kappa}=\frac{({\imath}{\jmath}){\kappa}-{\imath}({\jmath}{\kappa})}{2}=\frac{({\imath}{\jmath}){\kappa}-({\imath}{\jmath}){\kappa}}{2}=0,

a contradiction to
{\imath},{\jmath},{\kappa}\neq 0
.

Otherwise,
\mathbb{H}_{{\imath},{\jmath}}
is an algebra of quaternions. Write

{\kappa}={\kappa}_{1}+{\kappa}_{2}e_{4}^{\prime},

for some

and

. Then

\begin{split}2=&|2({\imath}{\jmath}){\kappa}|=|({\imath}{\jmath}){\kappa}-{\imath}({\jmath}{\kappa})|\\
=&|({\imath}{\jmath})({\kappa}_{2}e_{4}^{\prime})-{\imath}[{\jmath}({\kappa}_{2}e_{4}^{\prime})]|\leq 2|{\kappa}_{2}|\leq 2.\end{split}

It implies that

|{\kappa}_{2}|=1,\qquad{\kappa}_{1}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\kappa}={\kappa}_{2}e_{4}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{O}}\cap\mathbb{H}_{{\imath},{\jmath}}.

Then
{\kappa}\perp{\imath}
and
{\kappa}{\imath}=-{\imath}{\kappa}
. Similarly, (

) and (
ii

if and only if it is a triple of unit imaginary octonions such that each of them is orthogonal to the algebra generated by the other two.

Let

be a special triple of

Proof.

This proposition holds by direct calculation and elementary trigonometric identities.

Proposition 3.6

Let

(3.1)

(a+be_{8})(c+de_{8})=0,

a,b,c\neq 0
,

,
|a|=|b|
and
\left\{\frac{a}{|a|},\frac{b}{|b|},\frac{c}{|c|}\right\}
is a special triple of
\mathbb{O}
.

Proof.

“
\Leftarrow
” Suppose that
a,b,c\neq 0
,
d=a(bc)
,
|a|=|b|
and
\left\{\frac{a}{|a|},\frac{b}{|b|},\frac{c}{|c|}\right\}
is a special triple of
\mathbb{O}
. Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\overline{d}=\frac{\overline{a(bc)}}{|a||b|}=-\frac{a(bc)}{|a||b|}=\frac{a(cb)}{|a||b|}=-\frac{(ac)b}{|b|^{2}},\\
&d=\frac{a(bc)}{|a||b|}=-\frac{(bc)a}{|a|^{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

It implies that

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ac-\overline{d}b=ac-\left(-\frac{(ac)b}{|b|^{2}}\right)b=ac-ac=0,\\
&da+b\overline{c}=\left(-\frac{(bc)a}{|a|^{2}}\right)a-bc=bc-bc=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Then (
3.1
) holds by (
2.2
) and (
3.2
).

“
\Rightarrow
” Suppose that (
3.1
) holds. Then by (
2.2
),

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ac-\overline{d}b=0,\\
&da+b\overline{c}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\mathbb{O}
is a normed division algebra,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &|a||c|=|d||b|,\\
&|d||a|=|b||c|.\end{aligned}\right.

Thence

|a|^{2}\big(|c|^{2}+|d|^{2}\big)=|b|^{2}\big(|c|^{2}+|d|^{2}\big).

According to

, we have
|c|^{2}+|d|^{2}\neq 0
. Thence

(3.4)

|a|=|b|.

Since
a+be_{8}
is non-zero, we have

. It follows that

(3.5)

a^{-1}=\frac{\overline{a}}{|a|^{2}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad b^{-1}=\frac{\overline{b}}{|b|^{2}}.

On the other hand, by (

),

(3.6)

\overline{b^{-1}}(\overline{c}\cdot\overline{a})=d=-(b\overline{c})a^{-1}.

By (
3.4

By definition,

is a special triple of
\mathbb{O}
. It implies that

\overline{c}=-c\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\overline{a}=-a.

Thus

and
\left\{\frac{b}{|b|},\frac{c}{|c|},\frac{a}{|a|}\right\}
is also a basic triple of
\mathbb{O}
.

By (

d=-(b\overline{c})a^{-1}=-\big[b(-c)\big]\frac{-a}{|a|^{2}}=-\frac{(bc)a}{|a|^{2}}=\frac{a(bc)}{|a||b|}.

Proposition 3.7

Let

It follows from

\left\{\frac{b_{\ell}}{|b_{\ell}|},\frac{b_{\ell}a_{\ell}}{|a_{\ell}||b_{\ell}|},\frac{c}{|c|}\right\}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\{\frac{b_{\ell}}{|b_{\ell}|},\frac{(b_{\ell}a_{\ell})c}{|a_{\ell}||b_{\ell}||c|},\frac{c}{|c|}\right\}

5.

\sigma
-balls in sedenions

In this section, we show that there are only two types of hyper-
\sigma
-balls in sedenions.
One belongs to the plane associated with
\{\pm e_{8}\}
, the other belongs to the planes associated with
(J_{1},J_{2})\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}^{2}
that meets certain conditions.
In other words, a hyper-
\sigma
-ball is either a ball extending in just one slice plane, or a ball extending in exactly two slice planes.
The former is consistent with a
\sigma
-ball, while the latter is

\sigma

In the results below, for any

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{O}}(I)=\left\{\sum_{\imath=1}^{6}\lambda_{i}e_{i}^{\prime}:\sum_{\imath=1}^{6}\lambda_{\imath}^{2}=1\right\}

5
-sphere in the

.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
I_{1},I_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
with
I_{1}\neq\pm I_{2}
,
\ker(I_{1}+I_{2})\neq\{0\}
and
\ker(I_{1}-I_{2})\neq\{0\}
. Then

(6.23)

\ker(I_{1}+I_{2})=\ker\left[(I_{1}-I_{2})^{c_{8}}\right].

Proof.

Since
\ker(I_{1}-I_{2})\neq\{0\}
,
I_{1}-I_{2}
is a zero divisor. By Corollary
4.11
,
(I_{1},I_{2})
is not a slice-solution. Denote

(6.24)

\imath_{\ell}^{*}:=\imath_{\ell}^{[(I_{1},I_{2})]},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\theta_{\ell}:=\theta_{[I_{\ell}]},\qquad\ell=1,2.

According to (
4.2
) and Proposition
3.6
,

\cos(\alpha_{[I_{1}]})=\cos(\alpha_{[I_{2}]})=\cos(\alpha_{[(I_{1},I_{2})]})=\frac{\langle I_{1}+I_{2},e_{8}\rangle}{2}=0.

It follows from (
4.6
) that

I_{\ell}=L\bigg(\cos^{2}(\theta_{\ell})\imath_{1}^{*}+\sin(\theta_{\ell})\cos(\theta_{\ell})\imath_{2}^{*}+\left[\sin(\theta_{\ell})\cos(\theta_{\ell})\imath_{1}^{*}+\sin^{2}(\theta_{\ell})\imath_{2}^{*}\right]e_{8}\bigg).

By direct calculation,

(6.25)

I_{1}+I_{2}=L\bigg(\lambda\imath_{1}^{*}+\mu\imath_{2}^{*}+[\mu\imath_{1}^{*}+(2-\lambda)\imath_{2}^{*}]e_{8}\bigg),

where

\lambda:=\cos^{2}(\theta_{1})+\cos^{2}(\theta_{2}),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mu:=\sin(\theta_{1})\cos(\theta_{1})+\sin(\theta_{2})\cos(\theta_{2}).

According to Proposition
3.6
,

\lambda\imath_{1}^{*}+\mu\imath_{2}^{*}\perp\mu\imath_{1}^{*}+(2-\lambda)\imath_{2}^{*}.

By (
4.7
),

\sin(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})=\frac{2\mu}{2}=\frac{\lambda\mu+(2-\lambda)\mu}{2}=\frac{\langle\lambda\imath_{1}^{*}+\mu\imath_{2}^{*},\mu\imath_{1}^{*}+(2+\lambda)\imath_{2}^{*}\rangle}{2}=0.

It is easy to check from (
6.25
) and Proposition
3.6
that

\lambda=|\lambda\imath_{1}^{*}+\mu\imath_{2}^{*}|=|\mu\imath_{1}^{*}+(2-\lambda))\imath_{2}^{*}|=2-\lambda.

Then
\lambda=1
. It follows from
\imath_{1}^{*}\imath_{2}^{*}=-\imath_{2}^{*}\imath_{1}^{*}
, (
6.17
), Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
4.8
that

\begin{split}\ker(I_{1}+I_{2})=&\left\{\nu[\kappa+((\imath_{2}^{*}\imath_{1}^{*})\kappa)e_{8}]:\nu\in\mathbb{R},\kappa\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\imath_{1}^{*},\imath_{2}^{*}}\right\}\\
=&\left\{\nu[\kappa+((\imath_{1}^{*}\imath_{2}^{*})\kappa)e_{8}]:\nu\in\mathbb{R},\kappa\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\imath_{1}^{*},\imath_{2}^{*}}\right\}^{c_{8}}\\
=&[\ker(I_{1}-I_{2})]^{c_{8}}=\ker\left[(I_{1}-I_{2})^{c_{8}}\right],\end{split}

where

\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{O}}^{\imath_{1}^{*},\imath_{2}^{*}}:=\bigg\{J\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{O}}:J\perp\{\imath_{1}^{*},\imath_{2}^{*},\imath_{1}^{*}\imath_{2}^{*}\}\bigg\}.

∎

∎

7.
The domain of convergence of power series

In
[
31
]
, Gentili and Stoppato study quaternionic power series computed with the
*
-product. The domain of convergence of power series in one quaternionic variable is a so-called
\sigma
-ball. As it is well known, a
\sigma
-ball is not an open set in Euclidean space, and in order to have an open convergence set, one should use the slice topology instead. In the case of sedenions, there is an additional new feature appearing, namely the domain of convergence of power series is the intersection of a hyper-
\sigma
-ball and a
\sigma
-ball. This new feature is discussed in this section.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
I,J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathfrak{S}
. Then monomials with the variable
q\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}
coincide:

(7.1)

(q-z^{I})^{*\ell}a_{=}^{I,J}=(q-z^{J})^{*\ell}a_{=}^{I,J}.

Proof.

If
p\in\mathbb{R}
, then by definition
a_{\ell,=}^{p,J}=0
. Hence (
7.1
) holds.

Otherwise, when
p\notin\mathbb{R}
there are
b,c:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(q-z^{L})^{*\ell}=(q-z^{L})^{\ell}=b(q)+c(q)L,\qquad\forall\ q\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }L\in\{I,J\}.

Then

(7.2)

\begin{split}&(q-z^{I})^{*\ell}a_{=}^{I,J}-(q-z^{J})^{*\ell}a_{=}^{I,J}\\
=&\left[b(q)+c(q)I\right]a_{=}^{I,J}-\left[b(q)+c(q)J\right]a_{=}^{I,J}\\
=&c(q)(I-J)a_{=}^{I,J}=0,\end{split}

i.e., (
7.1
) holds for
q\in\mathbb{R}
. Since both sides of (
7.1
) are slice regular
with the variable
q
, it follows from Identity Principle
[
22
]
*Theorem 3.5 and (
7.2
)
that (
7.1
) holds.
∎

Let
r\in[0,+\infty)
and
x+yI\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}
with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
I\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Denote

B_{I}^{*}(x+yI,r):=\Psi_{i}^{I}(B^{*}(x+yi,r)).

We shall abbreviate by writing
B_{I}^{*}(x+yI,r)
instead of
B_{I}(x+yI,r)
, when
r>0
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
I_{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
,
p\in\mathbb{C}_{I}
, and
a=\{a_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{S}
. Then the power series

\begin{split}P:\ \mathbb{C}_{I}\ &\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ \mathfrak{S},\\
q\ &\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ {P(q)=}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}(q-p)^{\ell}a_{\ell}.\end{split}

converges absolutely in
B^{*}_{I}(p,R_{a})
and diverges in
{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{I}\left(B^{*}_{I}(p,R_{a})\right)
, where
{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{I}:={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau(\mathbb{C}_{I})}
.
Moreover, let
r\in(0,R_{a})
. Then the power series converges uniformly in
{\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{I}\left(B_{I}(p,r)\right)
, where
{\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{I}:={\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{\tau(\mathbb{C}_{I})}
.

Proof.

Since
I
is a complex structure on
\mathfrak{S}
, there is
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{8}\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
\{\theta_{1},...,\theta_{8},I(\theta_{1}),...,I(\theta_{8})\}
is a real basis of
\mathfrak{S}
. For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, write

(7.3)

a_{\ell}=\sum_{\imath=1}^{8}(c_{\imath})_{\ell}\theta_{\imath},\qquad\mbox{for some}\qquad(c_{\imath})_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}_{I}.

Then

\max_{\imath=1,...,8}(|(c_{\imath})_{\ell}|)\leq|a_{\ell}|=\left(\sum_{\imath=1}^{8}|(c_{\imath})_{\ell}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 8\max_{\imath=1,...,8}(|(c_{\imath})_{\ell}|).

It is easy to check that

(7.4)

\min_{\imath=1,...,8}(R_{c_{\imath}})=R_{a},

where
c_{\imath}:=\{(c_{\imath})_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then the power series

\begin{split}P_{\imath}:\ \mathbb{C}_{I}\ &\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ \mathfrak{S},\\
q\ &\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ {P_{\imath}(q)=}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}(q-p)^{\ell}(c_{\imath})_{\ell}.\end{split}

converges absolutely in
B^{*}_{I}(p,R_{c_{\imath}})
and diverges in
{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{I}\left(B^{*}_{I}(p,R_{a})\right)
. Moreover, let
r\in(0,R_{c_{\imath}})
. Then the power series converges uniformly in
{\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{I}\left(B_{I}(p,r)\right)
of
B_{I}(p,r)
. It follows from (
7.4
) and (
7.3
) that the statement holds.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
I_{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
,
p_{\imath}\in\mathbb{C}_{I}
, and
a^{\imath}=\{a_{\ell}^{\imath}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{\ell}^{\imath}\in\mathfrak{S}

and
R_{a^{\imath}}>0
,
\imath=1,2,3
. Then the power series

\begin{split}P:\ \mathbb{C}_{I}\ &\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ \mathfrak{S},\\
q\ &\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ {P(q)=}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{\imath=1}^{3}\left[(q-p_{\imath})^{\ell}a_{\ell}^{\imath}\right]\end{split}

converges absolutely in
\mathbb{B}:=\cap_{\imath=1}^{3}B_{I}(p_{\imath},R_{a^{\imath}})
, and diverges in the exterior
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}_{\tau(\mathbb{C}_{I})}(\mathbb{B})
of
\mathbb{B}
unless

(7.5)

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{\imath}||a_{\ell}^{\imath}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}=\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{\jmath}||a_{\ell}^{\jmath}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}=\max_{\kappa=1,2,3}\left(\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{\kappa}||a_{\ell}^{\kappa}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\right)

for some
\imath,\jmath\in\{1,2,3\}
with
\imath\neq\jmath
.

Proof.

P
converges absolutely in
\mathbb{B}
directly by Proposition
7.2
. Let
q
in the exterior of
\mathbb{B}
such that (
7.5
) does not hold. Then there is
\imath\in\{1,2,3\}
and
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

c_{0}:=\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{\imath}||a_{\ell}^{\imath}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}>c_{1}>\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{\jmath}||a_{\ell}^{\jmath}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}>1,\qquad\jmath\neq\imath.

Let
c_{2}\in(c_{1},c_{0})
. There is
\ell>1
such that
\big(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\big)^{\ell}>3
and

|q-p_{\imath}||a_{\ell}^{\imath}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}>c_{2}>c_{1}>|q-p_{\jmath}||a_{\ell}^{\jmath}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}},\qquad\jmath\neq\imath.

It implies that

\frac{1}{3}|q-p_{\imath}|^{\ell}|a_{\ell}^{\imath}|>\frac{1}{3}(c_{2})^{\ell}>c_{1}^{\ell}>|q-p_{\jmath}|^{\ell}|a_{\ell}^{\jmath}|,\qquad\jmath\neq\imath.

Therefore

\left|\sum_{m=1}^{3}\left[(q-p_{m})^{\ell}a_{\ell}^{m}\right]\right|\geq\left|\left[(q-p_{\imath})^{\ell}a_{\ell}^{\imath}\right]\right|-\frac{2}{3}\left|\left[(q-p_{\imath})^{\ell}a_{\ell}^{\imath}\right]\right|\geq\frac{1}{3}(c_{2})^{\ell}\geq\frac{1}{3}.

P
diverges at

, and Proposition
7.3
holds.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
p,q\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}\backslash\mathbb{R}
, and
a=\{a_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{S}
and
0<R_{a}<R_{a}^{p}
. Then

(7.6)

R_{o}=R_{a},

where
o=\{o_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with
o_{\ell}:=a_{\ell,=}^{p,q}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
with

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}=\frac{1}{R_{a}^{p}}<c_{1}<c_{2}<c_{3}<\frac{1}{R_{a}}=\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}.

Then there is
\ell>k
such that

|a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}|<(c_{1})^{\ell}<(c_{2})^{\ell}<(c_{3})^{\ell}<|a_{\ell}|,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(c_{1})^{\ell}+(c_{2})^{\ell}<(c_{3})^{\ell}.

It implies that

|a_{\ell,=}^{p,q}|=|a_{\ell}-a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}|\geq|a_{\ell}|-|a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}|>(c_{3})^{\ell}-(c_{1})^{\ell}>(c_{2})^{\ell}.

Hence
\frac{1}{R_{o}}\geq c_{2}
, for each
c_{2}\in\left(\frac{1}{R_{a}^{p}},\frac{1}{R_{a}}\right)
. Therefore,
R_{o}\leq R_{a}
. By
|a_{\ell,=}^{p,q}|<|a_{\ell}|

R_{o}\geq R_{a}
. We have
R_{o}=R_{a}
.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
I\in\mathbb{S}
,
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{I}
,
a=\{a_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
,
b=\{b_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
and
c=\{c_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with

,
R_{b}>R_{a}>0
,
R_{c}>0
and
\mathbb{C}_{I}a_{\ell}\perp\mathbb{C}_{I}c_{\ell}
. Then the power series

\begin{split}P:\ \mathbb{C}_{I}\ &\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ \mathfrak{S},\\
q\ &\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ {P(q)=}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}(q-p_{1})^{\ell}(a_{\ell}+b_{\ell})+(q-p_{2})^{\ell}(c_{\ell}),\end{split}

converges in
\mathbb{B}:=B_{I}(p_{1},R_{a})\cap B_{I}(p_{2},R_{c})
and diverges in the exterior of
\mathbb{B}
in
\tau(\mathbb{C}_{I})
.

Proof.

By
R_{b}>R_{a}
,

(7.7)

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|a_{\ell}|>\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|b_{\ell}|.

According to Proposition
7.3
, we only need to prove that
P(q)
diverges when
q
is in the exterior of
\mathbb{B}
and

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|a_{\ell}|=\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow+\infty}|q-p_{2}|^{\ell}|c_{\ell}|.

Let
R_{a}>c_{2}>c_{1}>R_{a}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. According to (
7.7
), there is
\ell\geq k
such that

|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|a_{\ell}|>c_{2}^{\ell}>c_{1}^{\ell}>|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|b_{\ell}|,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{\ell}>2.

Then

\begin{split}&\left|(q-p_{1})^{\ell}(a_{\ell}+b_{\ell})+(q-p_{2})^{\ell}c_{\ell}\right|\\
\geq&\left|(q-p_{1})^{\ell}a_{\ell}+(q-p_{2})^{\ell}c_{\ell}\right|-\left|(q-p_{1})^{\ell}b_{\ell}\right|\\
=&\sqrt{\left|(q-p_{1})^{\ell}a_{\ell}\right|^{2}+\left|(q-p_{2})^{\ell}c_{\ell}\right|^{2}}-\left|(q-p_{1})^{\ell}b_{\ell}\right|\\
\geq&|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|a_{\ell}|-|q-p_{1}|^{\ell}|b_{\ell}|\geq c_{2}^{\ell}-c_{1}^{\ell}\geq\frac{c_{2}^{\ell}}{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

diverges and the statement holds.
∎

Proposition 7.6

\begin{split}P:\ \mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\ &\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ \mathfrak{S},\\

converges in

\mathbb{B}:=B_{J}\left(z^{J},R_{a}\right)\cap B_{J}\left(z^{-J},R_{a}^{p,J}\right)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}

and diverges in the exterior
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}_{\tau(\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+})}(\mathbb{B})
of
\mathbb{B}
in
\tau\left(\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\right)
, where

\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}:=\{x+yJ\in\mathbb{C}_{J}:y>0\}.

Proof.

Let
q\in\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}
. Then
I_{q}=J
. There is

such that

p=z^{I_{p}},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q=w^{I_{q}}.

According to (
6.12
), (
6.13
), and the representation formula, see
[
22
]
*Theorem 6.6, we have

\begin{split}&\left|(q-p)^{*\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath}}\right|\geq\left|\left[(q-p)^{*\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath}}\right]_{\perp}^{p,q}\right|\\

=&\left|-\frac{I_{q}+I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\left(z_{q}^{I_{p}}-p\right)^{\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath},\perp}^{p,-q}+\frac{I_{q}-I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\left(z_{q}^{-I_{p}}-p\right)^{\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath},\perp}^{p,-q}\right|\\
\geq&\left|\frac{I_{q}-I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\left(z_{q}^{-I_{p}}-p\right)^{\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath},\perp}^{p,-q}\right|-\left|-\frac{I_{q}+I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\left(z_{q}^{I_{p}}-p\right)^{\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath},\perp}^{p,-q}\right|\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{I_{q}-I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\left(z_{q}^{-I_{p}}-p\right)^{\jmath_{\imath}}a_{\jmath_{\imath},\perp}^{p,-q}\right|\\
\geq&\frac{1}{8}\frac{\left|I_{q}-I_{p}\right|_{\inf}^{\perp}}{2}\cdot 1\cdot|\overline{z_{q}}-z_{p}|^{\jmath_{\imath}}\cdot\frac{1}{|\overline{z_{q}}-z_{p}|^{\jmath_{\imath}}}\\
=&\frac{\left|I_{q}-I_{p}\right|_{\inf}^{\perp}}{16},\end{split}

which implies that
P
diverges and Theorem
2.13
holds when
q\notin\Sigma(p,a)
with
R_{a}^{p,I_{q}}=0
.
∎

Proposition 7.11
.

Theorem
2.13

.

Proof.

Assume that

. According to Proposition
7.2
, for each
I\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
the power series
P
converges in

\{p\}=B_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}}^{*}(p,R_{a})=\Sigma(p,a)

and diverges in

. Therefore,
P
diverges in

and Theorem
2.13
holds when

and

. It follows from Proposition
7.10
that Proposition
7.11
holds.
∎

Proposition 7.12
.

Theorem
2.13
holds when
R_{a}=+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,+\infty)
be arbitrary and since

\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}=\bigcup_{r\in(0,+\infty)}\Sigma(p,r),

we only need to prove that
P
converges uniformly in
\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,\varepsilon)\right]
.

Let
\delta>0
. By Proposition
7.2
,
P
converges uniformly in
B_{I_{p}}(p,\varepsilon+1)
. Then there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|\sum_{\imath=\ell}^{+\infty}(w-p)^{\imath}a_{\imath}\right|<\frac{\delta}{8},\qquad\qquad\forall\ \ell>m\quad\mbox{and}\quad w\in B_{I_{p}}(p,\varepsilon+1).

Let
q\in\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}_{\tau_{s}}(\Sigma(p,\varepsilon))\subset\Sigma(p,\varepsilon+1)
. Then

z_{p}^{I_{p}},z_{p}^{-I_{p}}\in B_{I_{p}}(p,\varepsilon+1).

By the representation formula, for each
\ell>m
, we have

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{\imath=\ell}^{+\infty}(q-p)^{*\imath}a_{\imath}\right|\\
=&\left|-\frac{I_{q}+I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\sum_{\imath=\ell}^{+\infty}\left(z_{q}^{I_{p}}-p\right)^{\imath}a_{\imath}+\frac{I_{q}-I_{p}}{2}I_{p}\sum_{\imath=\ell}^{+\infty}\left(z_{q}^{-I_{p}}-p\right)^{\imath}a_{\imath}\right|\\
\leq&4\left|\frac{I_{q}+I_{p}}{2}\right||I_{p}|\left|\sum_{\imath=\ell}^{+\infty}\left(z_{q}^{I_{p}}-p\right)^{\imath}a_{\imath}\right|+4\left|\frac{I_{q}-I_{p}}{2}\right||I_{p}|\left|\sum_{\imath=\ell}^{+\infty}\left(z_{q}^{-I_{p}}-p\right)^{\imath}a_{\imath}\right|\\
\leq&4\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot\frac{\delta}{8}+4\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot\frac{\delta}{8}=\delta.\end{split}

It implies that
P
converges uniformly in
\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,\varepsilon)\right]
.
∎

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space,
V\subset X
and
U\in\tau(X)
. It is easy to check that

(7.21)

{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau(X)}(V)\cap U={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau(X)}(V\cap U)\cap U.

We are now in position to prove our main result that we repeat for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem
2.13

Let
p\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}
and
a=\{a_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{S}
. Then the power series

\begin{split}P:\ \mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{S}}\ &\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ \mathfrak{S},\\
q\ \ &\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow[\hskip 28.45274pt]{}\ P(q)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}(q-p)^{*\ell}a_{\ell},\end{split}

converges absolutely and it is slice regular in
\Sigma(p,a)
, moreover it diverges in

{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\right]
.

Moreover, if
R_{a}\in(0,+\infty]
and
\varepsilon\in\left(0,R_{a}\right)
, then the power series
P
converges uniformly in

\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,\varepsilon)\right],\qquad\qquad&R_{a}=+\infty,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a,\varepsilon)\right],\qquad\qquad&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
2.13
.

Theorem
2.13
holds when
R_{a}=0,+\infty
by Proposition
7.11
and Proposition
7.12
. Assume that
R_{a}\in(0,+\infty)
. Let

\delta>0,\qquad r_{1}:=R_{a}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\qquad r_{1}^{p}:=R_{a}^{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

r_{2}\in\left(r_{1},R_{a}\right),\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad r_{2}^{p}\in\left(r_{1}^{p},R_{a}^{p}\right).

By Corollary
6.4
, there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
\ell>m
we have

|a_{\ell}|<\frac{1}{r_{2}^{\ell}},

(7.22)

\left|a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,J}\right|<\frac{1}{(r_{2}^{p})^{\ell}},\qquad\forall\ J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad R_{a}^{p,J}=R_{a}^{p},

and

\max\left\{\frac{\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{m}}{1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}},\frac{\left(\frac{r_{1}^{p}}{r_{2}^{p}}\right)^{m}}{1-\frac{r_{1}^{p}}{r_{2}^{p}}}\right\}<\frac{\delta}{12}.

(i) Let
I\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
with
p\in\mathbb{C}_{I}
, then by Proposition
7.2
and (
7.17
), the series
P
converges absolutely in
B_{I}(p,R_{a})=\left[\Sigma(p,a)\right]_{I}
, diverges in

{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{I}\left[B_{I}(p,R_{a})\right]={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{I}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\cap\mathbb{C}_{I}\right]={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\right]\cap\mathbb{C}_{I}.

For each

q\in{\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{I}(B_{I}(p,R_{a}-\varepsilon))=\left({\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a,\varepsilon)\right]\right)_{I},

it follows from (
6.15
) that

(7.23)

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}(q-p)^{*\ell}a_{\ell}\right|\leq&\sqrt{2}\cdot\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}|q-p|^{\ell}\left|a_{\ell}\right|\\
<&\sqrt{2}\cdot\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}(r_{1})^{\ell}\frac{1}{(r_{2})^{\ell}}=\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{m}}{1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}}<\frac{\delta}{6}.\end{split}

(ii) Suppose that there is
J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{S}}
such that
p\notin\mathbb{C}_{J}
. By (
7.21
) and
\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\in\tau(\mathbb{C}_{J})
,

{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{J}\left(\Sigma(p,a)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\right)={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{J}(\Sigma(p,a)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}).

According to Proposition
7.6
, (
7.14
) and (
7.17
),
P
converges in

\mathbb{B}:=B_{J}\left(z^{J},R_{a}\right)\cap B_{J}\left(z^{-J},R_{a}^{p,J}\right)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}=\Sigma(p,a)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+},

and diverges in

\begin{split}{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau\left(\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\right)}(\mathbb{B})=&{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{J}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{J}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\right]\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\\
=&{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{J}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}\right]\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}={\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\right]\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}\\
=&{\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\right]\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}^{+}.\end{split}

By (i),
P
converges in
\Sigma(p,a)
and converges in
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a)\right]
.

If

\begin{split}q\in&{\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma\left(p,a,\varepsilon\right)\right]\subset\Sigma\left(p,a,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\\
=&\Sigma\left(p,R_{a}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\left(I_{p},I_{q}\right)\right)\cap\Sigma\left(p,R_{a}^{p}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right),\end{split}

then by (
7.6
) and (
7.10
),

R_{o}=R_{a}\leq R_{a}^{p}=R_{d}\leq R_{c},

where
o
,
c
and
d
are defined in (
7.9
) and (
7.11
).
By (
7.22
), for each
\ell>m
, we have

|d_{\ell}|=\left|\frac{1+I_{q}I_{p}}{2}a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}\right|=\left|I_{q}\frac{-I_{q}+I_{p}}{2}a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}\right|\leq(\sqrt{2})^{2}\left|a_{\ell,\perp}^{p,q}\right|<\frac{2}{(r_{2}^{p})^{\ell}}.

Similarly,

|o_{\ell}|,|c_{\ell}|\leq 2|a_{\ell}|<\frac{2}{(r_{2})^{\ell}}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}(q-p)^{*\ell}a_{\ell}\right|\\
\leq&\sqrt{2}\left(\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}\left|q-z^{I_{q}}\right|^{\ell}|o_{\ell}|+\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}\left|q-z^{I_{q}}\right|^{\ell}|c_{\ell}|+\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}\left|q-z^{-I_{q}}\right|^{\ell}|d_{\ell}|\right)\\
<&\sqrt{2}\left(\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}(r_{1})^{\ell}\frac{2}{(r_{2})^{\ell}}+\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}(r_{1})^{\ell}\frac{2}{(r_{2})^{\ell}}+\sum_{\ell=m}^{+\infty}(r_{1}^{p})^{\ell}\frac{2}{(r_{2}^{p})^{\ell}}\right)\\
=&\sqrt{2}\left(4\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{m}}{1-\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}}+2\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{r_{1}^{p}}{r_{2}^{p}}\right)^{m}}{1-\frac{r_{1}^{p}}{r_{2}^{p}}}\right)<\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{4\delta}{12}+\frac{2\delta}{12}\right)<\delta.\end{split}

By (
7.23
),
P
converges uniformly in
\mathop{\mathrm{clo}}_{\tau_{s}}\left[\Sigma(p,a,\varepsilon)\right]
. It implies that
P
is a slice regular function in
\Sigma(p,a)
.
∎

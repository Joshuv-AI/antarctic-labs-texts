Type AIII orbits in the affine flag variety of type A
Kam Hung TONG

Abstract
Matsuki and Oshima introduced the notion of clans, which are incomplete matchings with positive or negative signs on isolated vertices. They discovered that clans parametrise K K -orbits in the flag varieties for classical linear groups, where K K is a fixed point subgroup of an involution in the same classical linear group. In this work we investigate the affine version of these orbits. For a field 𝕜 \Bbbk with characteristic not equal to two, we construct an explicit bijection between the GL p ​ ( 𝕜 ⁡ ( ( t ) ) ) × GL q ​ ( 𝕜 ⁡ ( ( t ) ) ) \textsf{GL}_{p}(\Bbbk(\hskip-1.42262pt(t)\hskip-1.42262pt))\times\textsf{GL}_{q}(\Bbbk(\hskip-1.42262pt(t)\hskip-1.42262pt)) -orbits in the affine flag variety and certain objects called affine ( p , q ) (p,q) -clans. These affine ( p , q ) (p,q) -clans can be concretely interpreted as involutions in the affine permutation group with positive or negative signs on fixed points.

c=(\dots,c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\dots)
, satisfying the following:

(i)

If
i<j
,
i\in[n]
and
c_{i}=c_{j}=a\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
c_{i}\in[n]
.

(ii)

If
i<j
,
j\in[n]
,
i<1
and
c_{i}=c_{j}=a\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
c_{j}\in[n]
.

(iii)

For
i\in[n]
, if
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{i-1}\in\{+,-\}
, then
c_{i}=1
.

(iv)

For
i,j\in[n]
, if
c_{i},c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{i+1},c_{i+2},\dots,c_{j-1}\in\{+,-\}
and
c_{j}\neq c_{i^{\prime}}
for some
1\leq i^{\prime}<i
, then
c_{j}=c_{i}+1
.

For example, for the case
n=8
and
p=5
,
q=3
,
c=(\dots,+,1,-,2,1,+,18,+,\dots)
is the standard numbering of its equivalence class, while
d=(\dots,+,2,-,-15,2,+,1,+,\dots)
is equivalent to
c
, but not a standard numbering.

Let
V_{+}
and
V_{-}
be the subspaces in
V=\Bbbk(\hskip-1.42262pt(t)\hskip-1.42262pt)^{n}
consisting of the vectors in
\Bbbk(\hskip-1.42262pt(t)\hskip-1.42262pt)^{n}
with the last
q
coordinates equal to zero and the first
p
coordinates equal to zero, respectively. Define
\pi_{+}
and
\pi_{-}
to be the projection maps onto
V_{+}
and
V_{-}
respectively from the decomposition
V=V_{+}\oplus V_{-}
.

We now define some important
K
-invariant data for any affine flag.

Definition 3.1
.

For an affine flag
\Lambda_{\bullet}=(\Lambda_{1}\supset\Lambda_{2}\dots\supset\Lambda_{n})
, and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, define,

\displaystyle(i;+)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})),

\displaystyle(i;-)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})),

\displaystyle(i;\mathbb{N})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}))),

and for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
i\leq j
and
i\leq n+1
, define

\displaystyle(i;j)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i})/t\Lambda_{1}).

First we have to make sure the above quantities are finite non-negative integers.

Lemma 3.2
.

The quantities
(i;\mathbb{N})=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})))
are finite.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
we can write

\Lambda_{i}=\text{span}_{A}\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\dots,v_{n},tv_{1},tv_{2},\dots,tv_{i-1}\},

where
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
is an
F
-basis for
F^{n}
. Therefore for each
j\in[n]
, there exists a minimum
N_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

t^{N_{j}}e_{j}=a_{ji}v_{i}+a_{j(i+1)}v_{i+1}+\dots+a_{jn}v_{n}+a_{j1}tv_{1}+\dots+a_{j(i-1)}tv_{i-1},

where
a_{j1},a_{j2},\dots,a_{jn}\in A
. In other words, for
M\geq N_{j}
, it holds that
t^{M}e_{j}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
for
1\leq j\leq p
, while it holds that
t^{M}e_{j}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
for
p+1\leq j\leq n
. Therefore if
M_{j}
is the smallest order of the
j
-th entry of vectors in
\Lambda_{i}
, the maximum dimension of
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})))
is
\sum^{N}_{j=1}(N_{j}-M_{j})
, which is finite.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
A
-modules
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
and
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
are free
A
-modules of rank
p
and
q
respectively.

Proof.

Notice that
A
is a discrete valuation ring, and hence a principal ideal domain.
By
[
17
, Theorem 9.8]
,
every submodule of a finite-dimensional free
A
-module is free. Now notice that since
\Lambda_{i}
is a free
A
-module of rank
n
, the
A
-submodules
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
and
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
of
\Lambda_{i}
are both free.

It remains to show that the ranks of
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
and
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
are
p
and
q
respectively. We now prove that
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
has rank
p
. The proof for the rank of
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
being
q
is similar.

Notice that since
\Lambda_{i}
has a basis that is also an
F^{n}
basis, each
e_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
is an
F
-linear combination of the basis elements of
\Lambda_{i}
. Therefore for each
e_{j}
, there is a large enough integer
N_{j}
such that
t^{N_{j}}e_{j}\in\Lambda_{i}
, and hence

\text{span}_{A}\{t^{N_{1}}e_{1},t^{N_{2}}e_{2},\dots,t^{N_{p}}e_{p}\}\subset\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}

as a submodule. It follows that
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
has rank at least
p
.

On the other hand, write
\Lambda_{i}=\text{span}_{A}\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\dots,v_{n},tv_{1},tv_{2},\dots,tv_{i-1}\}
, and for
1\leq j\leq n
, set
M_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
to be the smallest order of the
j
-th entry in vectors
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\dots,v_{n},tv_{1},tv_{2},\dots,tv_{i-1}\}
. Notice that this is also the smallest order of the
j
-th entry in all the vectors in
\Lambda_{i}
. We have

\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}\subset\text{span}_{A}\{t^{M_{1}}e_{1},t^{M_{2}}e_{2},\dots,t^{M_{p}}e_{p}\},

since every element in
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
is an
A
-linear combination of
t^{M_{1}}e_{1},t^{M_{2}}e_{2},\dots,t^{M_{p}}e_{p}
. Therefore
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
has rank at most
p
, and hence equal to
p
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that
(i;+)
,
(i;-)\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

We show this fact for
(i;+)
only. The proof for
(i;-)\in\{0,1\}
is similar.

By Lemma
3.3
, we can write
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=\text{span}_{A}\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{p}\}
for some
w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{p}\in F^{n}
.
For
j\in[1,p]
, we have

\displaystyle w_{j}

\displaystyle=a_{ji}v_{i}+a_{j(i+1)}v_{i+1}+\dots+a_{jn}v_{n}+a_{j1}tv_{1}+\dots+a_{j(i-1)}tv_{i-1}

\displaystyle=a_{j(i+1)}v_{i+1}+\dots+a_{jn}v_{n}+a_{j1}tv_{1}+\dots+a_{j(i-1)}tv_{i-1}+(t^{-1}a_{ji})(tv_{i}),

where
a_{j1},a_{j2},\dots,a_{jn}\in A
.

If none of the
a_{ji}
’s are in
A^{*}
for
j\in[1,p]
, then all
a_{ji}\in tA
. Therefore
t^{-1}a_{ji}\in A
for all
j\in[1,p]
, and hence
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=\text{span}_{A}\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{p}\}\subset\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}\subset\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
. It follows that
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}
and
(i;+)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}))=0
.

Otherwise suppose there are
j
’s such that
a_{ji}\in A^{*}
. If there are two or more
j
’s such that
a_{ji}\in A^{*}
, pick a
j
, say
j_{1}\in[1,p]
, and eliminate the constant terms of the coefficients of
v_{i}
in other such
j
’s by subtracting a non-zero
\Bbbk
-multiple of
w_{j_{1}}
.
Therefore without loss of generality we can assume that there is only one
w_{j}
within
w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{p}
such that
a_{ji}\in A^{*}
in the above expansions as
A
-linear combinations of
\{v_{i},v_{i+1},\dots,v_{n},tv_{1},tv_{2},\dots,tv_{i-1}\}
.
In particular for this
w_{j}
, it holds that
w_{j}\notin\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}
.
On the other hand, we have
tw_{j}\in\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}
, since

\displaystyle tw_{j}

\displaystyle=ta_{j(i+1)}v_{i+1}+\dots+ta_{jn}v_{n}+ta_{j1}tv_{1}+\dots+ta_{j(i-1)}tv_{i-1}+(a_{ji})(tv_{i}),

while
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{j-1},w_{j+1},\dots w_{p}\in\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}
by assumption. Therefore,

\displaystyle\text{span}_{A}\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{j-1},tw_{j},w_{j+1},\dots,w_{p}\}

\displaystyle\subset\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}

\displaystyle\subsetneq\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=\text{span}_{A}\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{p}\}.

Observe that,

\displaystyle 0<(i;+)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\text{span}_{A}\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{p}\}}{\text{span}_{A}\{w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{j-1},tw_{j},w_{j+1},\dots,w_{p}\}}\right)=1.

Therefore it holds that
(i;+)=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

The quantities
(i;j)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i})/t\Lambda_{1})
are finite for
i\leq j
and
i\leq n+1
.

Proof.

Suppose
j=j^{\prime}+an
, where
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq n
and
j^{\prime},a\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Write

\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}=\text{span}_{A}\{v_{j^{\prime}},v_{j^{\prime}+1},\dots,v_{n},tv_{1},tv_{2},\dots,tv_{j^{\prime}-1}\}.

Then

\Lambda_{j}=t^{a}\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}=\text{span}_{A}\{t^{a}v_{j^{\prime}},t^{a}v_{j^{\prime}+1},\dots,t^{a}v_{n},t^{a+1}v_{1},t^{a+1}v_{2},\dots,t^{a+1}v_{j^{\prime}-1}\},

and therefore

\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})=\text{span}_{A}\{t^{a}\pi_{+}(v_{j^{\prime}}),t^{a}\pi_{+}(v_{j^{\prime}+1}),\dots,\\
t^{a}\pi_{+}(v_{n}),t^{a+1}\pi_{+}(v_{1}),t^{a+1}\pi_{+}(v_{2}),\dots,t^{a+1}\pi_{+}(v_{j^{\prime}-1})\}.

Each of the
n
vectors in the spanning set of
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})
is an
F
-linear combination of
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
, and hence all lie in
\Lambda_{m}
for small enough
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})\subset\Lambda_{m}
as a
\Bbbk
-vector subspace for small enough
m
, in particular for
m\leq j
. Now we have

(\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{j})/t\Lambda_{i}\subset(\Lambda_{m}+\Lambda_{j})/t\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{m}/t\Lambda_{i}.

Since
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{m}/t\Lambda_{i})=n+1-m
, it holds that
(i;j)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{j})/t\Lambda_{i})\leq n+1-m
, and hence this number is finite.
∎

Recall that for any affine flag
\Lambda_{\bullet}=(\Lambda_{1}\supset\Lambda_{2}\supset\dots\supset\Lambda_{n})
and
g\in G
, we have
g\Lambda_{\bullet}=(g\Lambda_{1}\supset g\Lambda_{2}\supset\dots\supset g\Lambda_{n})
.
By noticing that
K
contains invertible matrices
g
such that
gV_{+}=V_{+}
,
gV_{-}=V_{-}
, we can prove the following statement.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{\bullet}
is an affine flag, and
k\in K
.
For
i\in[n]
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
j\geq i
, the numbers
(i;+)
,
(i;-)
,
(i;\mathbb{N})
and
(i;j)
are the same when defined relative to
\Lambda_{\bullet}
and
k\Lambda_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(k\Lambda_{i}/((k\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(k\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/((\Lambda_{i}\cap k^{-1}V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap k^{-1}V_{-})))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}))).

So the numbers
(i;\mathbb{N})
are the same when defined relative to
\Lambda_{\bullet}
and
k\Lambda_{\bullet}
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((k\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})/(k\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap k^{-1}V_{+})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap k^{-1}V_{+}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})).

So the numbers
(i;+)
and similarly
(i;-)
are the same when defined relative to
\Lambda_{\bullet}
and
k\Lambda_{\bullet}
.

Finally notice that
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i}=((\Lambda_{j}+V_{-})\cap V_{+})+\Lambda_{i}
. We have for
k\in K
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\pi_{+}(k\Lambda_{j})+k\Lambda_{i})/tk\Lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((k\Lambda_{j}+V_{-})\cap V_{+}+k\Lambda_{i})/tk\Lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{j}+k^{-1}V_{-})\cap k^{-1}V_{+}+\Lambda_{i})/t\Lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{j}+V_{-})\cap V_{+}+\Lambda_{i})/t\Lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i})/t\Lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=(i;j).

So the numbers
(i;j)
are the same when defined relative to
\Lambda_{\bullet}
and
k\Lambda_{\bullet}
.
∎

By Lemma
3.6
, we say that
(i;+)
,
(i;-)
,
(i;\mathbb{N})
and
(i;j)
are
left
K
-invariants
.

3.2
Lemmas related to the left
K
-invariants

In this section we record some technical lemmas on the left
K
-invariants of an affine flag
\Lambda_{\bullet}
that will be used in Definition
3.16
and the proof of Proposition
3.19
.

The following lemma is analogous to the fact used in
[
22
, Prop. 2.2.6]
. Recall that for an affine flag
\Lambda_{\bullet}
, we defined
(i;\mathbb{N})=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}\right)
for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that
(i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})=1-(i;+)-(i;-)
.

Proof.

Treat
\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{i+1},\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+},\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-},\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+},
and
\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-}
as
\Bbbk
-vector spaces. By the third isomorphism theorem for
\Bbbk
-vector spaces,

\frac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\Bigg/\frac{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\cong\frac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}.

But since the quotients

\frac{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\cong\frac{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})}\oplus\frac{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\text{ and }\frac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}

are finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector spaces with dimensions
(i;+)+(i;-)
and
(i;\mathbb{N})
respectively, it holds that
\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}
is also a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space, with dimension equal to
(i;\mathbb{N})+(i;+)+(i;-)
.

By the third isomorphism theorem for
\Bbbk
-vector spaces again, we have

\frac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\Bigg/\frac{\Lambda_{i+1}}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\cong\frac{\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i+1}}.

Therefore combining the facts that
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})}\right)=(i;\mathbb{N})+(i;+)+(i;-)
, and that
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/\Lambda_{i+1})=1
, we have

(i;\mathbb{N})+(i;+)+(i;-)-(i+1;\mathbb{N})=1.

∎

Together with Lemma
3.4
, the following corollary holds.

Corollary 3.8
.

It holds that
(i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})\in\{1,0,-1\}
for all
i\in[n]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, it holds that
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}\right)=1-(i;-)
and
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i})}{\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})}\right)=1-(i;+)
.

Proof.

Notice that
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\subset\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})
. Therefore
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})/(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})
is a well-defined
\Bbbk
-vector space.

We treat
V_{+}
and
\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
as
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, and consider the composition of the canonical quotient map
V_{+}\rightarrow V_{+}/(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})
with the map
\pi_{+}:\Lambda_{i}\rightarrow V_{+}
. This composition map has image equal to
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})/(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})
and kernel equal to
(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})
. By the first isomorphism theorem of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, we have

\frac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})}\cong\frac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}.

Since
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})}\right)=(i;\mathbb{N})
, it holds that
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}\right)=(i;\mathbb{N})
by the above isomorphism.

Similarly, the quotient
\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}
is a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space and
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}\right)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\Lambda_{i+1}}{(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-})\oplus(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})}\right)=(i+1;\mathbb{N})
.

Notice also that by the third isomorphism theorem on
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, we have

\frac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}\cong\frac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}\Bigg/\frac{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}.

Since
\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}
and
\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}
are finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector spaces of dimension
(i;\mathbb{N})
and
(i;+)
respectively,
\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}
is also a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space of dimension
(i;\mathbb{N})+(i;+)
.

By the third isomorphism theorem on
\Bbbk
-vector space again, we have

\frac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}\cong\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}\Bigg/\frac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}}.

Therefore
\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}
is a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space of dimension
(i;\mathbb{N})+(i;+)-(i+1;\mathbb{N})
, and by Lemma
3.7
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})}{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})}\right)

\displaystyle=(i;\mathbb{N})+(i;+)-(i+1;\mathbb{N})

\displaystyle=1-(i;+)-(i;-)+(i;+)

\displaystyle=1-(i;-).

The proof for
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i})}{\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})}\right)=1-(i;+)
is similar.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
(i;+)=1
and
(i;-)=1
, then there exist
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})
and
v_{-}\in\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})
satisfying
v_{+},v_{-}\notin\Lambda_{i+1}
, such that
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}
and
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{-}
.

Proof.

The fact that
(i;+)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}))=1
means that there exists
v_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
such that

\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}.

Notice that
v_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}
and
v_{+}\notin(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+})
. But since
v_{+}\in V_{+}
, we have
v_{+}\notin\Lambda_{i+1}
, and therefore

\Lambda_{i}\supset\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}\supsetneq\Lambda_{i+1}.

Since
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\Lambda_{i}/\Lambda_{i+1})=1
, we conclude that

\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}.

Similar arguments using the fact
(i;-)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}((\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})/(\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-}))=1
show that there exists
v_{-}\in\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})
satisfying

\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{-}.

It remains to show that
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})
and
v_{-}\in\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})
.
In fact, it holds that
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})
and
\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i})=\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})
, since by Lemma
3.9
we have

\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})/\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1}))=1-(i;-)=0

and

\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i})/\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1}))=1-(i;+)=0.

However, we have
v_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\subset\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})
and
v_{-}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})\subset\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i})=\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})
and
v_{+}\notin\Lambda_{i+1}
. Then there exists a unique
j>i
such that
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i+1}
and
v_{+}\notin\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})+\Lambda_{i+1}
.

Proof.

Suppose not. Using the fact that
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})\subset\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})+\Lambda_{i+1}
, we have
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i+1}
for all integers
j>i
. However, it holds that
t^{N}\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})\subset\Lambda_{i+1}
for large enough
N
, since
\Lambda_{i+1}
is an
A
-lattice. Therefore, we have
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1+Nn})+\Lambda_{i+1}=\Lambda_{i+1}
, a contradiction to the assumption that
v_{+}\notin\Lambda_{i+1}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

It holds that
(i;\mathbb{N})=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}\right)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We shall prove that
\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}\cong\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}
as
\Bbbk
-vector spaces.

Define the
\Bbbk
-linear transformation
f:\Lambda_{i}\rightarrow\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}
by
f(v)=\pi_{+}(v)+\Lambda_{i}
, where
v\in\Lambda_{i}
. Since
\pi_{+}
is a linear transformation, the function
f
is also a linear transformation between the two
\Bbbk
-vector spaces. It is clear that
f
is surjective, by noticing that
\pi_{+}(v)+w+\Lambda_{i}=\pi_{+}(v)+\Lambda_{i}=f(v)
for any
v,w\in\Lambda_{i}
. It remains to show that the kernel of
f
is
(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
.

Write

\Lambda_{i}=(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})\oplus W_{i},

(3.1)

where
W_{i}
is a complementary
\Bbbk
-subspace of
(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
in
\Lambda_{i}
, and of finite
\Bbbk
-dimension by Lemma
3.2
. Then for any
v\in\Lambda_{i}
, it holds that
v=v_{+}+v_{-}+w
, where
v_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
,
v_{-}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
, and
w\in W_{i}
.

Notice that
f(v)=\pi_{+}(v)+\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}
if and only if
\pi_{+}(v)\in\Lambda_{i}
. By writing
v=v_{+}+v_{-}+w
, it holds that

\pi_{+}(v)=\pi_{+}(v_{+}+v_{-}+w)=\pi_{+}(v_{+})+\pi_{+}(v_{-})+\pi_{+}(w)=v_{+}+\pi_{+}(w)\in\Lambda_{i}
.
Since
v_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}
, we have
\pi_{+}(w)\in\Lambda_{i}
. We shall now show that
w=0
.

Write
w=w_{+}+w_{-}
, where
w_{+}\in V_{+}
and
w_{-}\in V_{-}
, and notice that
\pi_{+}(w)=w_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}
. It follows that
w_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}
. Moreover, we have
w_{-}=w-w_{+}\in\Lambda_{i}
and therefore
w_{-}\in\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}
. It follows that
w=w_{+}+w_{-}\in(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
. Since
W_{i}
is a complementary
\Bbbk
-subspace of
(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
in
\Lambda_{i}
and
w\in W_{i}
, it holds that
w=0
.

As a result, the fact that
f(v)=\pi_{+}(v)+\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}
implies that
v\in(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
, and, on the other hand, if
v\in(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
then
f(v)=\pi_{+}(v)+\Lambda_{i}\in(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})+\Lambda_{i}\subset\Lambda_{i}
. Therefore it holds that
\ker f=(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})
, and by the first isomorphism theorem for
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, we have

\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}\cong\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}.

It follows that
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}\right)=\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}\right)=(i;\mathbb{N})
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

It holds that
(i;i)=n+1-i+(i;\mathbb{N})
for any
i\leq n+1
.

Proof.

Using the third isomorphism theorem for
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, we have

\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{t\Lambda_{1}}\Bigg/\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{t\Lambda_{1}}\cong\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}.

Notice that
\Lambda_{i}/(t\Lambda_{1})
is a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space of dimension
n+1-i
. By Lemma
3.12
, it holds that
\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{\Lambda_{i}}
is also a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space of dimension
(i;\mathbb{N})
. Therefore the
\Bbbk
-vector space
\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{t\Lambda_{1}}
is also finite-dimensional, and
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i})+\Lambda_{i}}{t\Lambda_{1}}\right)=(i;i)=n+1-i+(i;\mathbb{N})
.
∎

3.3
Bijections between orbits of affine flags and affine clans

The following is an algorithm whose input is an affine flag and whose output is a
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed sequence, which will later be shown to be an affine
(p,q)
-clan.

Definition 3.16
.

For an affine flag
\Lambda_{\bullet}=(\Lambda_{1}\supset\Lambda_{2}\dots\supset\Lambda_{n})
, produce a
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed sequence
c=(\dots,c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n},\dots)
by the following inductive procedure:

Suppose
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{i-1}
are determined.

(a)

If
c_{i}
is already determined, then move on to determine
c_{i+1}
.

(b)

If
(i;+)=1
and
(i;-)=0
, set
c_{i}=+
. Set also
c_{i+rn}=+
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(c)

If
(i;+)=0
and
(i;-)=1
, set
c_{i}=-
. Set also
c_{i+rn}=-
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(d)

If
(i;+)=0
and
(i;-)=0
, then
(i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})=1
by Lemma
3.7
. Consider the set
\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{i-1}\}\cap[n]
. If the set is empty, set
c_{i}=1
, and if the set is not empty, set
c_{i}=1+\max(\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{i-1}\}\cap[n])
. Set also
c_{i+rn}=c_{i}+rn
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(e)

If
(i;+)=1
and
(i;-)=1
, then
(i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})=-1
by Lemma
3.7
. By Lemma
3.10
there exist
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{i+1})
and
v_{-}\in\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{i+1})
satisfying
v_{+},v_{-}\notin\Lambda_{i+1}
, such that
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}
and
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{-}
.
By Lemma
3.11
, there exists a unique
j>i
such that
v_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i+1}
and
v_{+}\notin\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})+\Lambda_{i+1}
. (Here it is possible that
j>n
.)

If
j\textrm{ mod }n<i\textrm{ mod }n
and hence
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
by Lemma
3.17
, set
c_{i}=c_{j}
and
c_{i+rn}=c_{i}+rn
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
j\textrm{ mod }n>i\textrm{ mod }n
and hence
c_{j}
is not determined yet, consider the set
(\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{i-1}\}\cap[n])
again. Set
c_{j}=c_{i}=1
if the set is empty, and set
c_{j}=c_{i}=1+\max(\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{i-1}\}\cap[n])
if the set is not empty. In both cases set
c_{j+rn}=c_{i+rn}=c_{i}+rn
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Denote the indices
i+rn
and
j+rn
as being
paired
for each
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The following are two technical lemmas related to Definition
3.16
, which shows that
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
in case (e) if
c_{j}
is already determined, and the
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed sequence produced in Definition
3.16
is indeed an affine
(p,q)
-clan.

Lemma 3.17
.

For the
j\in\mathbb{Z}
described in (e) in Definition
3.16
, it holds that
(j;\mathbb{N})-(j-1;\mathbb{N})=1
, implying that
(j;+)=(j;-)=0
.

Proof.

We use the notations in case (e) of Definition
3.16
. Write
v_{+}=v_{+}^{\prime}+v_{+}^{\prime\prime}
, where
v_{+}^{\prime}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})
and
v_{+}^{\prime\prime}\in\Lambda_{i+1}
. Notice that
v_{+}^{\prime}\notin\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})
, since
v_{+}\notin\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})+\Lambda_{i+1}
.
Notice that

\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{j})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})+\Bbbk\pi_{+}(v_{j}).

However, we have
v_{+}^{\prime}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})\backslash\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})
. Therefore

\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})\subsetneq\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}^{\prime}\subset\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j}),

and hence
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})/\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1}))=1
and

\displaystyle\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})\oplus\Bbbk v_{+}^{\prime}.

(3.2)

By Lemma
3.9
, we have
\operatorname{dim}_{\Bbbk}(\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})/\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1}))=1-(j;-)
, and therefore
(j;-)=0
.
Similarly, by the observation that
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i+1}=\pi_{-}(\Lambda_{j})+\Lambda_{i+1}
, it follows that
(j;+)=0
. Therefore
c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
by case (d) in the algorithm.
∎

Lemma 3.18
.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed sequence
c=(\dots,c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n},\dots)
defined from
\Lambda_{\bullet}
according to the procedure in Definition
3.16
is an affine
(p,q)
-clan.

Proof.

By construction, the
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed sequence
c
has properties (i) and (ii) in Definition
1.1
.
To show that any two indices in
[n]
satisfying step (e) in Definition
3.16
are not paired to indices in the same congruence class modulo
n
, suppose on the contrary that there are two indices
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\in[n]
such that
(i_{1};\mathbb{N})-(i_{1}+1;\mathbb{N})=(i_{2};\mathbb{N})-(i_{2}+1;\mathbb{N})=-1
and
c_{i_{1}}=c_{j_{1}}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{i_{2}}=c_{j_{2}}\in\mathbb{Z}
for some
j_{1}>i_{1}
and
j_{2}>i_{2}
, and
j_{1}\equiv j_{2}\mod n
. Without loss of generality assume that
i_{1}>i_{2}
. By Lemma
3.10
there exist
v_{i_{1},+}
and
v_{i_{2},+}
such that
v_{i_{1},+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{1}})+\Lambda_{i_{1}+1}
and
v_{i_{2},+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}})+\Lambda_{i_{2}+1}
. Write
v_{i_{1},+}=v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime}+v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime\prime}
and
v_{i_{2},+}=v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime}+v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime\prime}
, where
v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{1}})
,
v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}})
,
v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime\prime}\in\Lambda_{i_{1}+1}
and
v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime\prime}\in\Lambda_{i_{2}+1}
.
By (
3.2
) we have

\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{1}})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{1}+1})\oplus\Bbbk v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}+1})\oplus\Bbbk v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime}.

Notice that since
j_{1}\equiv j_{2}\mod n
, we have
\Lambda_{j_{1}}=t^{a}\Lambda_{j_{2}}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore it holds that
\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}})=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}+1})\oplus\Bbbk t^{a}v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime}
and there exists
w_{+}\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}+1})
and
\ell\in\Bbbk
such that
v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime}=w_{+}+\ell t^{a}v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime}
.
However, it holds that

\displaystyle v_{i_{2},+}

\displaystyle=v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime}+v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=w_{+}+\ell t^{a}v_{i_{1},+}^{\prime}+v_{i_{2},+}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\in\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}+1})+\Lambda_{i_{1}}+\Lambda_{i_{2}+1}=\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j_{2}+1})+\Lambda_{i_{2}+1},

contradicting the definition of
j
satisfying
v_{i_{2},+}\notin\pi_{+}(\Lambda_{j+1})+\Lambda_{i_{2}+1}
.
Therefore for two indices
i_{1}\neq i_{2}\in[n]
such that
(i_{1};\mathbb{N})-(i_{1}-1;\mathbb{N})=(i_{2};\mathbb{N})-(i_{2}-1;\mathbb{N})=-1
, and
c_{i_{1}}=c_{j_{1}}
and
c_{i_{2}}=c_{j_{2}}
for some
j_{1}>i_{1}
and
j_{2}>i_{2}
, it holds that
c_{j_{1}}\not\equiv c_{j_{2}}\textrm{ mod }n
.

Now we proceed to show that
c
satisfies property (iii) in Definition
1.1
.
Notice that multiplications of integer powers of
t
on both the numerators and denominators of the quotients

\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+}}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{+}},\quad\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-}}{\Lambda_{i+1}\cap V_{-}}\quad\text{and}\quad\dfrac{\Lambda_{i}}{(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{+})\oplus(\Lambda_{i}\cap V_{-})}

are invertible
\Bbbk
-linear transformation on the finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector spaces. Therefore it holds that
(i;+)=(i+an;+)
,
(i;-)=(i+an;-)
and
(i;\mathbb{N})=(i+an;\mathbb{N})
for
i\in[n]
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and it follows that
(1+n;\mathbb{N})-(1;\mathbb{N})=0
.

Consider the following telescoping sum:

0=(1;\mathbb{N})-(1+n;\mathbb{N})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}((i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})).

Recall that
(i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})=0
if and only if
c_{i}\in\{+,-\}
. Since different

with
(i;\mathbb{N})-(i+1;\mathbb{N})=-1
are paired to different

with

c_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
with

and
(j;\mathbb{N})-(j+1;\mathbb{N})=1
are left unpaired, since otherwise the telescoping sum above will be greater than
0
.
Hence we conclude that each integer, if it appears in
c

c
.

Observe that by using the third isomorphism theorem successively, we obtain the isomorphism

\dfrac{\Lambda_{1}\cap V_{+}}{t\Lambda_{1}\cap V_{+}}\cong\dfrac{\Lambda_{1}\cap V_{+}}{\Lambda_{2}\cap V_{+}}\oplus\dfrac{\Lambda_{2}\cap V_{+}}{\Lambda_{3}\cap V_{+}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\dfrac{\Lambda_{n}\cap V_{+}}{t\Lambda_{1}\cap V_{+}}.

, and similarly it holds that

. Moreover, the equality

in the case

c_{i}=-
or

in the case

We have shown that
(\dots,c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n},\dots)

(p,q)
-clan.

Remark 3.20

In fact, it holds that

)

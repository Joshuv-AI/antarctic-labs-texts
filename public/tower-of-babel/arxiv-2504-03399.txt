An existence theorem for single leader multi-follower games with direct preference maps
Yutthakan Chummongkhon, Poom Kumam, Parin Chaipunya ,

Abstract
This paper concerns with an existence of a solution for a single leader multi-follower game (SLMFG), where the followers jointly solve an abstract economy problem. Recall that an abstract economy problem is an extension of a generalized Nash equilibrium problem (GNEP) in the sense that the preference of each player can be described without numerical criteria. The results of this paper therefore extend the known literature concerning an existence of a solution of a SLMFG. Due to the lack of criterion functions in the lower-level, the technique we used is quite different from those that deal with GNEPs. In particular, we argue that the follower’s abstract economy profiles, i.e., constraint-preference couples, belong to a particular metric space where the response map is proved to be upper semicontinuous. Keywords: Single leader multi-follower game; Bilevel optimization; Nash equilibrium; Abstract economy.

Introduction

A bilevel programming, or bilevel optimization problem, refers to a class of optimization problems that involve two players, one is classified as the
leader
and the other one as the
follower
.
When the leader makes her decision, the follower observes and makes an
optimal response
by solving the
lower-level
optimization problem, parametrized by the leader’s action.
The goal of the leader is then to make a decision so that, altogeter with the follower’s optimal response, gives her the
best
objective value.
When the single follower is replaced with multiple followers, they reponse to the leader’s action by solving a parametrized equilibrium-type problem.
This approach is broadly referred to as a
single leader multi-follower game
(or briefly,
SLMFG
).
These classes of bilevel problems have posed several interesting and important questions, theoretically and practically, in the literature.
For instance, the issues regarding the KKT reformulation (or MPCC reformulation) were discussed in
[
9
,
5
,
8
]
.
The existence results for several different settings of a SLMFG were studied in
[
4
,
6
,
1
,
7
,
3
]
.

In this paper, we propose an existence theorem for a single leader multi-follower game (SLMFG) in which the followers’ preferences are directly given by the preference maps without the need of objective functions.
Thus, the lower-level problem here is presented as an abstract economy, à la
Shafer and Sonnenschein [11]
, parametrized by the leader’s action.
Let us state precisely the problem of our study for this paper.
Consider the
n
followers that are represented by a set
\mathtt{F}:=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
The single leader, denoted by
\vartheta
, owns an action set
W
and each follower
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
owns an action set
X_{\mathtt{\nu}}
.
We write
X:=\prod_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}X_{\mathtt{\nu}}
and for each
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
we write
X_{-\mathtt{\nu}}:=\prod_{\mathtt{\mu}\in\mathtt{F}\setminus\{\mathtt{\nu}\}}X_{\mathtt{\mu}}
whose generic element is denoted by
x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}
.
It is typical to represent
X
as
X_{\mathtt{\nu}}\times X_{-\mathtt{\nu}}
and to decompose
x\in X
into
(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\in X_{\mathtt{\nu}}\times X_{-\mathtt{\nu}}
, and also to extract
x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}
from
x
the
other followers’ component
.
The leader is equipped with an objective function
F:W\times X\to\mathbb{R}
, while each follower
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
is equipped with a constraint map
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}:W\times X_{-\mathtt{\nu}}\rightrightarrows X_{\mathtt{\nu}}
and a preference map
P_{\mathtt{\nu}}:W\times X\rightrightarrows X_{\mathtt{\nu}}
.
For each
w\in W
and
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
, it is customary to write
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(\cdot):=A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,\cdot)
and
P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(\cdot):=P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,\cdot)
.
The SLMFG that we focus on in this paper has the following form:

\displaystyle\min_{w,x}\quad

\displaystyle F(w,x)

(1.1a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w\in Y\subset W

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\forall\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}:

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\ \lfloor\ x_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}),\quad A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x)=\varnothing.

(1.1d)

In particular, the constraints (
1.1c
)–(
1.1d
) describe that the followers respond to the leader’s signal
w
with a
Nash equilibrium
of a
w
-parametrized abstract economy
given by the pair of maps
(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w},P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}
.
For convenience, at each
w\in W
we write

\displaystyle\mathsf{NE}(w):=\{x=(x_{\mathtt{\nu}})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}\in X\mid\forall\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}:\ x_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}),\quad A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x)=\varnothing\}

to denote the set of all such Nash equilibrium points.

The SLMFG (
1.1
) indeed covers many particular cases of Nash equilibrium-like problems.
For instance, if one fix
\varepsilon\geq 0
and set

\displaystyle P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}):=\{y_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in X_{\mathtt{\nu}}\mid f_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,y_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})<f_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})-\varepsilon,\quad(\forall y\in X_{\mathtt{\nu}})\},

\displaystyle f_{\mathtt{\nu}}:W\times X\to\mathbb{R},

then (
1.1
) reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{w,x}\quad

\displaystyle F(w,x)

(1.2a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w\in Y\subset W

(1.2b)

\displaystyle\forall\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}:

(1.2c)

\displaystyle\ \lfloor\ x_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in\varepsilon-\argmin_{y_{\mathtt{\nu}}}\{f_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,y_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\mid y_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\},

(1.2d)

which is a single leader multi-follower game where the followers solves for an
\varepsilon
-Nash equilibrium of a
w
-parametrized (generalized) Nash equilibrium problem.
When
\varepsilon=0
, this is a classical single leader multi-follower game.

Likewise, one could also retrieve the case where the followers play a set-valued Nash equilibrium problem by setting

\displaystyle P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}):=\{y_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in X_{\mathtt{\nu}}\mid(y_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\succeq_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\},

\displaystyle\succeq_{\mathtt{\nu}}\ \subset X\times X.

Then
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})=\varnothing
describes that there is no feasible point
y_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
in which
(y_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
is not preferred over
(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
.
In the language of vector optimization, this says that
(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
is
weakly efficient
.

The aim of this paper is to develop an existence theorem for a SLMFG of the form (
1.1
) where the followers’ problem is an abstract economy problem parametrized by the leader’s decision.
We take the classical approach by showing that the solution map (or the response map) of the lower-level problem has closed graph.
For this, we characterize the solution map with a parametric minimizer set of its associated gap function.
We introduce a regularity condition and show that, under the uniform metric, the solution map is upper semicontinuous.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic notions in set-valued analysis which includes distance between points and sets, convergence of sets, and continuity of set-valued maps.

Suppose that
E
and
F
are subsets of (possibly different) Banach spaces. A set-valued map
T:E\rightrightarrows F
is said to be

\circ

closed
if its graph
\gr(T):=\{(x,y)\in E\times F\mid y\in T(x)\}
is closed in
E\times F
,

\circ

compact
if
\gr(T)
is compact in
E\times F
,

\circ

closed-valued
if
T(x)
is closed for all
x\in E
,

\circ

upper semicontinuous
on
D\subset E
if for every
x\in D
and each open set
\mathcal{O}
with
T(x)\subset\mathcal{O}
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
x
such that
T(x^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{O}
for all
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
,

\circ

lower semicontinuous
on
D\subset E
if for every
x\in D
and each open set
\mathcal{O}
with
T(x)\cap\mathcal{O}\neq\varnothing
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
x
such that
T(x^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{O}\neq\varnothing
for all
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
,

\circ

continuous
on
D\subset E
if it is both upper and lower semicontinuous on
D
.

The following results (see
e.g.

[
2
]
) regarding
T:E\rightrightarrows F
are widely known in set-valued analysis:

(i)

If
T
is closed-valued and upper semicontinuous, then it is closed.

(ii)

If
T
is closed and
F
is compact, then it is upper semicontinuous.

(iii)

If
F
is compact, then
T
is upper semicontinuous at
x\in E
if and only if for any sequences
(x^{n})
in
E
and
(y^{n})
in
F
with
x^{n}\to x
and
y^{n}\in T(x^{n})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that
y\in T(x)
.

(iv)

If
F
is compact, then
T
is lower semicontinuous at
x\in E
if and only if for any sequence
(x^{n})
in
E
with
x^{n}\to x
and any
y\in T(x)
, there exists
y^{n}\in T(x^{n})
(for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
y^{n}\to y
.

Let us denote with
K(E)
the family of compact subsets of
E
.
Recall that the distance on
E
induces a metric on
K(E)
that preserves the completeness.
For any
x\in E
and
D\in K(E)
, we define the
displacement from
D
by

\displaystyle d(x,D):=\inf_{y\in D}d(x,y).

Observe that
x\in D\iff d(x,D)=0
.
For any
C,D\in K(E)
, the
excess
of
C
from
D
is defined by

\displaystyle e(C,D):=\sup_{x\in C}\inf_{y\in D}d(x,y).

It is important to notice that
e(C,D)\neq e(D,C)
in general.
Finally, the Hausdorff distance between
C,D\in K(E)
is given by

\displaystyle h(C,D):=\max\{e(C,D),e(D,C)\}.

For any
D\subset E
and
\varepsilon>0
, we adopt the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}[D]:=\{x\in E\mid d(x,D)<\varepsilon\}

to denote an open ball about a set
D
.
When
C=\{x\}
is singleton, we prefer to write
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}[x]
instead of
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}[\{x\}]
.
With this, one may also notice an equivalent formulation of the Hausdorff distance, which reads

\displaystyle h(C,D)=\inf\{\eta>0\mid\mathcal{U}_{\eta}[C]\supset D,\quad\mathcal{U}_{\eta}[D]\supset C\}.

It is well-known that
(K(E),h)
is a metric space which is complete if and only if
E
is complete.
Exploiting the Hausdorff metric, we have a further characterization of continuity of a set-valued map.
That is, if
F
is compact, then
T:E\rightrightarrows F
is continuous at
x\in E
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}h(T(x^{n}),T(x))=0
for any sequence
(x^{n})
in
E
with
x^{n}\to x
.

Next, consider a sequence
(C^{n})
of subsets of
E
.
Its
outer
and
inner limits
could be defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Limsup_{n\to\infty}C^{n}:=\left\{x\in E\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$x$ is an accumulation point of some sequence~$(x^{n})$}\\
\text{such that~$x^{n}\in C^{n}$ for all~$n\in\mathbb{N}$.}\end{array}\right.\right\}

and

\displaystyle\Liminf_{n\to\infty}C^{n}:=\left\{x\in E\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$x$ is the limit of some convergent sequence~$(x^{n})$}\\
\text{such that~$x^{n}\in C^{n}$ for all~$n\in\mathbb{N}$.}\end{array}\right.\right\}.

We say that
C\subset E
is the
Painlevé-Kuratowski limit
of
(C^{n})
, or symbolically that
C^{n}\overset{\rm{PK}}{\to}C
, if

\displaystyle C=\Limsup_{n\to\infty}C^{n}=\Liminf_{n\to\infty}C^{n}.

If
C
and all
C^{n}
’s are compact, then
C^{n}\overset{\rm{PK}}{\to}C
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}h(C^{n},C)=0
.
That is, the Painlevé-Kuratowski and the Hausdorff limits coincide for compact sequences.

3
Stability analysis for abstract economy problems

This section is dedicated to the stability analysis of the lower-level abstract economy.
The idea is that a decision
w
of the leader
selects
a tuple
(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w},P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}
representing an
abstract economy profile
, from some particular class
\Lambda
, that defines the followers’s abstract economy problem.

3.1
An existence result

For the moment, we neglect the leader’s ingredient and focus on the solvability of an abstract economy problem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider a finite set
\mathtt{F}
of players.
For each
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
, let
\mathcal{X}_{\mathtt{\nu}}
be a Banach space and

Suppose, for each

(A2)

(A3)

Let

\displaystyle G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x):=\begin{cases}A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)&\text{if~$x\in E_{\mathtt{\nu}}$},\\
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.2)

Let

It is clear that if

The converse holds with the use of

From

The existence of such a fixed point then follows from
[
10

A stability result

)

\displaystyle x_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\implies\mathcal{U}_{\tau}[x_{\mathtt{\nu}}]\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)=\varnothing.

One could directly observe that

\displaystyle\mathsf{NE}(\lambda):=\{x\in X\mid x_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}),\quad A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)=\varnothing,\quad\forall\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}\}.

Proof.

Let

Claim 3:
For

.
Using the PK convergence of
(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n})
to its limit
\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}
, there exists a sequence
(x^{n})
in
X
such that
x^{n}\to\hat{x}
and
x_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(\hat{x}_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Applying

(

Ψ
1

)

to the point
(x_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n},\hat{x}_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
with
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(\hat{x}_{-\mathtt{\nu}})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau\leq d(x_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n},P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(\hat{x}))\leq d(x_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n},\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{x}))+h(P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(\hat{x}),P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{x}))

for large
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Letting
n\to\infty
, we have
\tau\leq d(\hat{x}_{\mathtt{\nu}},\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{x}))
, which is equivalent to saying
\mathcal{U}_{\tau}[\hat{x}_{\mathtt{\nu}}]\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{x})=\varnothing

The claim is thus proved.

Accumulating the Claims 1–3, one conclude that
(

Λ
1

)
and
(

Λ
2

)
are satisfied at the limit
\hat{\lambda}
.
This proves
\hat{\lambda}\in\Lambda
and the closedness of
\Psi
guarantees that it is complete.

Following the proof of Theorem
3.1
, one have a fixed point characterization of elements in
\mathsf{NE}(\cdot)
.
We define a function
\mathcal{R}:\Lambda\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\lambda;x)

\displaystyle:=\max_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}d(x_{\mathtt{\nu}},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda;x)),

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda;x)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)&\text{if~$x\in E_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda)$},\\
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for any
\lambda=(A_{\mathtt{\nu}},P_{\mathtt{\nu}})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}\in\Lambda
.
One may observe that
\mathcal{R}(\lambda;\cdot)
is a gap function for the game
\lambda
,
i.e.
it is nonnegative and
\mathcal{R}(\lambda;x)=0
if and only if
x\in\mathsf{NE}(\lambda)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The gap function
\mathcal{R}:\Lambda\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is lower semicontinuous on
\Psi\times X
, provided that
\Psi\subset\Lambda(\mathcal{E})
is regular w.r.t.
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda^{n})
,
\lambda^{n}=(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n},P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}
, be a sequence in
\Psi
that is convergent to
\hat{\lambda}=(\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}},\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}})\in\Psi
.

Claim 1:
For any
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
,
G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};x)\to G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};x)
uniformly over
x\in X
.

It is sufficient to show that
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)\to\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)
uniformly on
x\in E_{\mathtt{\nu}}
.
It is clear that we have

\displaystyle A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)\to\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)\to\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)

(3.7)

uniformly on
x\in X
.
Let us fix
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
and
x\in E_{\mathtt{\nu}}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})],\quad

\displaystyle A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})]

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)],\quad

\displaystyle P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)].

For large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the regularity of
\Psi
yields

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})]\cap\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)]

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}[A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)]

\displaystyle A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})]\cap\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon/\alpha}[\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)]

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}[\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x)].

This means we have

\displaystyle h(\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x),A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x))<\varepsilon

whenever
n\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large.
Since this is uniform over
x\in E_{\mathtt{\nu}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in E_{\mathtt{\nu}}}h(\hat{A}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap\hat{P}_{\mathtt{\nu}}(x),A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x))\to 0.

Together with the uniform convergence of
(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{n}(x))
over
x\in X\setminus E_{\mathtt{\nu}}
, which could be observed in (
3.7
).
This proves the claim.

Now, note that
G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};\cdot)
and
G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};\cdot)
are upper semicontinuous, and hence the functions
x\mapsto d(x,G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};x))
and
x\mapsto d(x,G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};\cdot))
are both lower semicontinuous (by
[
2
, Corollary 1.4.17]
).
Take any convergent sequence
(x^{n})
in
X
with the limit
\hat{x}\in X
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle d(x^{n},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};x^{n}))>d(\hat{x},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};\hat{x}))-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Using the compactness, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
u^{n}\in G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};x^{n})
such that

\displaystyle d(x^{n},u^{n})=d(x^{n},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};x^{n})).

From the convergence proved in Claim 1, for large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we could pick
v^{n}\in G_{\mathtt{\nu}}({\hat{\lambda};x^{n}})
such that

\displaystyle d(u^{n},v^{n})=d(u^{n},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};x^{n}))\leq h(G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};x^{n}),G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};x^{n}))<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle d(x^{n},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\lambda^{n};x^{n}))

\displaystyle=d(x^{n},u^{n})

\displaystyle\geq d(x^{n},v^{n})-d(v^{n},u^{n})

\displaystyle\geq d(x^{n},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};x^{n}))-d(v^{n},u^{n})

\displaystyle>d(\hat{x},G_{\mathtt{\nu}}(\hat{\lambda};\hat{x}))-\varepsilon

for large
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Repeat the process for all
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\lambda^{n};x^{n})>\mathcal{R}(\hat{\lambda};\hat{x})-\varepsilon

for large
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, the function
\mathcal{R}
is lower semicontinuous on
\Psi\times X
.

We finally have all the tools to prove our main stability theorem.
We now state and prove the continuity of
\mathsf{NE}(\cdot)
based on Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
.

Theorem 3.5
.

The map
\lambda\mapsto\mathsf{NE}(\lambda)
is upper semicontinuous on
\Psi\subset\Lambda(\mathcal{E})
, provided that
\Psi
is regular w.r.t.
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Observe that, for each
\lambda=(A_{\mathtt{\nu}},P_{\mathtt{\nu}})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}\in\Psi
, one may now deduce that

\displaystyle\mathsf{NE}(\lambda)=\mathsf{S}(\lambda):=\{x\in X\mid\mathcal{R}(\lambda;x)=0\}=\argmin_{x\in X}\mathcal{R}(\lambda;x).

This set above is nonempty for all
\lambda\in\Psi
in view of Theorem
3.1
.
To show that
\mathsf{NE}=\mathsf{S}
is upper semicontinuous, we take a convergent sequence
(\lambda^{n})
in
\Psi
with the limit
\hat{\lambda}\in\Psi
.
Let
(x^{n})
be a sequence in
X
such that
x^{n}\in\mathsf{S}(\lambda^{n})
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and is convergent to
\hat{x}\in X
.
This means
\mathcal{R}(\lambda^{n};x^{n})=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{R}
implies that
\mathcal{R}(\hat{\lambda};\hat{x})=0
and that
\hat{x}\in\mathsf{S}(\hat{\lambda})
.
Therefore, the map
\mathsf{NE}=\mathsf{S}
is upper semicontinuous on
\Psi
.

4
An existence result for SLMFG with direct preference maps

This final section presents an existence result for the SLMFG with direct preference maps of the form (
1.1
), with a more specific structure of the followers’ problem.
The theorem is, in fact, just an application of the Weierstraß theorem combined with the stability result from Theorem
3.5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that

(a)

W
is a topological space and
Y\subset W
is closed,

(b)

F:W\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is lower semicontinuous,

(c)

\sigma:W\to\Psi
is continuous, where
\Psi\subset\Lambda(\mathcal{E})
is regular w.r.t.
\mathcal{E}
.

Then the following SLMFG has a solution:

\displaystyle\min_{w,x}\quad

\displaystyle F(w,x)

(4.1a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w\in Y

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\sigma(w)=(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w},P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w})_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}

(4.1c)

\displaystyle\forall\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}:

(4.1d)

\displaystyle\ \lfloor\ x_{\mathtt{\nu}}\in A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}}),\quad A_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x_{-\mathtt{\nu}})\cap P_{\mathtt{\nu}}^{w}(x)=\varnothing.

(4.1e)

Proof.

One could observe that the constraints (
4.1c
)–(
4.1e
) reduce simply to
x\in\mathsf{NE}(\psi(w))
, or equivalently, to
(w,x)\in\gr(\mathsf{NE}\circ\sigma)
.
This means the SLMFG (
4.1
) reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{w,x}\quad

\displaystyle F(w,x)

(4.2a)

s.t.

\displaystyle w\in Y

(4.2b)

\displaystyle(w,x)\in\gr(\mathsf{NE}\circ\sigma).

(4.2c)

From Theorem
3.5
, the response map
\mathsf{NE}
is upper semicontinuous.
Since
\sigma
is continuous, the composition
\mathsf{NE}\circ\sigma:W\rightrightarrows X
is again upper semicontinuous.
As a consequent, the graph of
\mathsf{NE}\circ\sigma
is closed due to the compactness of
X
.
The Weierstraß theorem then guarantees that (
4.2
), and hence (
4.1
), has a solution.

In the formulation of SLMFG (
4.1
), the leader’s action
w
is passed to the followers through the
signal map

\psi
, which explicitly selects an abstract economy profile
\lambda\in\Psi
.
One could also present Theorem
4.1
(and the problem (
4.1
) herein) more transparently by adopting the parametric approach,
i.e.,
each follower
\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}
has the parametrized constraint map
A_{\mathtt{\nu}}:W\times X_{-\mathtt{\nu}}\rightrightarrows X_{\mathtt{\nu}}
and the parametrized preference map
P_{\mathtt{\nu}}:W\times X\rightrightarrows X_{\mathtt{\nu}}
.
An alternative presentation of Theorem
4.1
now reads:
If the conditions
(a)
and
(b)
hold, and the tuple
(A_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,\cdot),P_{\mathtt{\nu}}(w,\cdot))_{\mathtt{\nu}\in\mathtt{F}}
belongs to
\Psi
, then the corresponding SLMFG of the form (
1.1
) has a solution.

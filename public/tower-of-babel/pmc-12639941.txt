The predictive role of uterine electromyography in delivery before 28 weeks of gestation after transvaginal cervical cerclage
Authors: Xiaoyan Wang, Menglan Zhu, Xiaohui Ji, Rouhua Xu, Haiyan Lin, Liqiong Zhu, Zhuoyao Mai, Haitian Xie, Jing Tang, Shiyu Bai, Yihong Guo, Yujie Gan, Jie Gao, Hui Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDelivery before 28 weeks of gestation is a major cause of perinatal morbidity and mortality. Cervical cerclage is widely used to prevent preterm birth (PTB), yet predicting outcomes after cerclage remains challenging. Uterine electromyography (uEMG), a noninvasive method that records myometrial electrical activity, offers objective and accurate assessment of uterine contractions. This study evaluated the predictive value of uEMG for delivery before 28 weeks of gestation in women with singleton pregnancies following transvaginal cervical cerclage.MethodsIn this single-center retrospective study, 131 women with singleton pregnancies who underwent McDonald cervical cerclage and uEMG monitoring within 48 h postoperatively between January 2018 and February 2025 were included. Contraction-related indicators, including frequency, average peak intensity, and average duration, were analyzed. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of delivery before 28 weeks, and a nomogram was developed. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, the area under the curve (AUC), calibration analysis, decision curve analysis (DCA), and a confusion matrix. Internal validity was evaluated through five-fold cross-validation.ResultsAmong 68 women without postoperative contractions, 3 (4.41%) delivered before 28 weeks, compared with 21 of 63 women with contractions (33.33%) (P < 0.001). The multivariate analysis identified an average peak intensity of ≥ 71.00 as an independent predictor of delivery before 28 weeks (P = 0.021). When combined with assisted reproductive technology (ART), cervical length, and previous late miscarriage, the average peak intensity yielded an AUC of 0.806. The model demonstrated high specificity (0.93) but modest sensitivity (0.52). Calibration analysis showed good concordance between predicted and observed outcomes, while DCA confirmed clinical utility within a risk threshold range of 0.38–0.82. Five-fold cross-validation further supported the model’s robustness.ConclusionsThe average peak intensity of uterine contractions measured by uEMG is an independent predictor of delivery before 28 weeks in women undergoing cervical cerclage. The proposed nomogram provides a potentially practical tool for individualized risk stratification and management. Prospective multicenter studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate the model and improve its predictive sensitivity.

Background

Cervical cerclage is an obstetric procedure for preventing preterm birth (PTB) in women at high risk [1]. By providing mechanical support to the cervix, it helps maintain pregnancy and prolong gestation [2–5]. Nevertheless, some women continue to experience adverse pregnancy outcomes despite undergoing cerclage [6, 7]. Current predictors of pregnancy outcomes after cervical cerclage primarily include cervical length, fetal fibronectin (fFN), and infection-related biomarkers [8–12]. However, the limited accuracy of these methods may delay timely interventions for preterm infants [13]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for novel monitoring methods or combined indicators to improve the accuracy of PTB risk assessment.

Extremely preterm birth, defined as delivery before 28 weeks of gestation, remains a leading cause of perinatal mortality and morbidity [12, 14, 15]. Evidence consistently indicates that extremely preterm infants have poorer neurodevelopmental outcomes and face an elevated risk of long-term health complications [16, 17]. Globally, approximately 61% of extremely preterm survivors develop neurological or developmental disabilities [18–21], and bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) occurs in up to 57.5% of cases [22–24]. Beyond medical consequences, caring for extremely preterm infants imposes profound psychological and financial burdens on families and healthcare systems [25, 26]. Therefore, the prediction and prevention of extremely preterm birth are of critical importance. For this reason, delivery before 28 weeks of gestation was defined as the primary outcome threshold in this study.

Surgical procedures involving the uterine cervix may trigger uterine contractions, hemorrhage, or infection, which can contribute to PTB [2, 27]. Several guidelines recommend monitoring uterine contractions after cervical cerclage [28]. Conventional monitoring methods, such as tocodynamometry (TOCO) and intrauterine pressure catheters (IUPC), are limited by invasiveness or low accuracy, highlighting the need for more accurate and noninvasive tools, such as uterine electromyography (uEMG) [29, 30]. Compared with other approaches, uEMG provides multiple advantages, including objectivity, accuracy, noninvasiveness, and reduced susceptibility to interference from maternal abdominal wall thickness or movement [30–34]. Some studies have confirmed its clinical utility in the early detection of PTB [13, 35–41]. Furthermore, women with more frequent and prolonged uterine contractions have been found to be at higher risk of PTB [40, 41]. However, most existing studies have primarily examined the use of uEMG in predicting delivery before 37 weeks of gestation, while evidence for earlier thresholds remains limited. On this basis, we hypothesize that uEMG monitoring after cervical cerclage can identify uterine activity patterns predictive of delivery before 28 weeks of gestation.

This study aims to evaluate the predictive value of uEMG for delivery before 28 weeks of gestation after cervical cerclage, specifically focusing on contraction-related indicators, including frequency, intensity, and duration.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective study included women with singleton pregnancies who underwent cervical cerclage with subsequent uEMG monitoring during hospitalization at Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University, between January 2018 and February 2025. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University (approval number: SYSKY-2025-546-01).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were: (1) women who underwent transvaginal McDonald cervical cerclage after exclusion of reproductive tract infections prior to surgery; (2) received uEMG monitoring within 48 h after cervical cerclage during hospitalization; (3) singleton pregnancy with a gestational age between 13 and 27+6 weeks at the time of cervical cerclage and uEMG monitoring; (4) admission for clinical manifestations of threatened miscarriage or threatened PTB, characterized by vaginal bleeding with or without lower abdominal pain. Exclusion criteria were: (1) voluntary discontinuation of treatment; (2) pregnancy termination due to severe complications, including preeclampsia with severe features (defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 110 mmHg, platelet count < 100 × 109/L, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL or a doubling of baseline in the absence of other renal disease, transaminase levels exceeding twice the upper limit of normal, pulmonary edema, or new-onset headache or visual disturbances) [42], placental abruption, or other serious medical or surgical complications (such as significant hepatic or renal dysfunction, acute appendicitis or acute cholecystitis unresponsive to conservative therapy); (3) unhealthy maternal behaviors during pregnancy, such as heavy smoking, alcohol intake, or drug abuse; (4) incomplete clinical data; (5) multiple gestations (defined as the presence of two or more fetuses confirmed by ultrasound), with high-risk cases referred to the high-risk obstetrics clinic for further evaluation and management.

Criteria for the indication of surgery

All surgical indications were based on ACOG Practice Bulletin No.142 [43]. History-indicated cerclage, also referred to as prophylactic cerclage, was performed in women with relevant obstetric histories, such as a prior cerclage for painless cervical dilation in the second trimester or previous second-trimester pregnancy losses attributed to cervical insufficiency. Ultrasound-indicated cerclage was generally performed between 16 and 24 weeks of gestation in women with a history of spontaneous PTB and a transvaginally measured cervical length of < 25 millimeters (mm) before 24 weeks. Emergency cerclage, or physical examination-indicated cerclage, was reserved for women with advanced painless cervical dilation detected on examination in the second trimester, in the absence of labor or placental abruption. Cervical length was measured prior to cervical cerclage. To exclude uncontrolled infection, preoperative evaluation included infection biomarkers such as white blood cell (WBC) count, C-reactive protein (CRP), and vaginal cultures. In addition, all women received perioperative prophylactic antibiotics to minimize surgical contamination and reduce postoperative complications.

uEMG assessment

uEMG data were recorded using the Monica AN24 device (Monica Healthcare Co, UK) with electrodes placed on the maternal abdominal surface (Fig. 1). Data from the first recording obtained within 48 h after cerclage were analyzed, with each monitoring session lasting at least 60 min. Signal extraction and processing were performed using specialized software, and all recordings were manually verified by trained personnel in accordance with standardized protocols. The uterine contraction curve displayed peak signal intensities on a scale from 0 to 100. Based on previous studies and clinical experience, a midpoint value of 50 was defined as the threshold for effective contractions [40]. The frequency of contractions was defined as the average number of contractions per hour during the monitoring period, calculated as: Frequency of contractions (times/hour) = total number of contractions/monitoring time (hours).

The average peak intensity of contractions was defined as the mean peak signal amplitude of all contractions during the monitoring period, calculated as: Average peak intensity of contractions = sum of peak signal amplitudes of each contraction/total number of contractions.

The average duration of contractions was defined as the mean duration of all contractions during the monitoring period, calculated as: Average duration of contractions (minutes) = sum of durations of each contraction (minutes)/total number of contractions.

Definitions of variables

Clinical data were extracted from hospital medical records by trained personnel. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as pre-pregnancy weight (kg) divided by the square of height (m2). According to Chinese guidelines, BMI was categorized as underweight (< 18.5 kg/m2), normal (18.5 ≤ BMI < 24.0 kg/m2), overweight (≥ 24.0 kg/m2), and obese (≥ 28.0 kg/m2) [44]. Early miscarriage was defined as pregnancy loss before 12 weeks of gestation, whereas late miscarriage was defined as pregnancy loss occurring between 12 and 23+6 weeks [45]. Gestational diabetes mellitus was defined as hyperglycemia first identified during pregnancy [46]. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy were classified into four categories: chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, and superimposed preeclampsia [47]. Assisted reproductive technology (ART) included artificial insemination (AI) and in vitro fertilization-embryo transfer (IVF-ET). The history of intrauterine operations included vacuum aspiration, dilation and curettage, hysteroscopic examination, and hysteroscopic surgery. Indications for cervical cerclage were determined based on the current pregnancy. Reproductive tract infection was defined as any positive culture result for bacteria, fungi, Trichomonas, Mycoplasma, or Chlamydia from vaginal or cervical secretions obtained before delivery. WBC count was assessed within 48 h after cervical cerclage. Cervical length was measured by transvaginal ultrasound. Patients were instructed to empty their bladders before the examination, and the transvaginal ultrasound probe was gently placed in the anterior vaginal fornix. These requirements aimed to minimize the influence of lower uterine segment contractions, which could affect the accuracy of cervical length measurement [48]. In the standard sagittal plane, cervical length was measured as the straight distance (in millimeters) between the internal and external os, excluding the length of the funnel. All measurements were performed by experienced sonographers with standardized training. At least three measurements were obtained per session, and the average value was used as the final cervical length.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Version 25 (SPSS Statistics V25, IBM Corporation, Somers, New York) and R software (version 4.4.0). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test or Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared between groups using independent samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed variables were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies (percentages) and compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify risk factors for delivery before 28 weeks of gestation. A violin plot was generated to illustrate differences in contraction intensity between outcome groups. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis was applied to estimate the gestational age at delivery, stratified by the average peak intensity of contractions. Predictive performance and discriminative ability were assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC). DeLong’s test was applied to compare AUC values between different models. A nomogram was constructed to graphically represent the prediction model, and its generalizability was evaluated using five-fold cross-validation. Model calibration was evaluated using calibration curves. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to assess the clinical utility of the nomogram. A confusion matrix was generated to evaluate the predictive accuracy. Due to the retrospective design, blinding was not feasible during the uEMG analysis. All reported statistical significance levels were two-sided, and the statistical significance was set at 0.05.

Continuous variables discretization

In the original dataset, contraction-related indicators were recorded as continuous variables. To enhance clinical interpretability, these variables were discretized. Optimal cut-off values were determined using the Youden index, allowing converting continuous variables into categorical variables (Table 3). Delivery before 28 weeks of gestation was defined as the dependent variable, while each contraction-related indicator was analyzed individually as an independent variable. ROC analysis was performed to identify thresholds corresponding to the maximum Youden index, representing the point that optimally balances sensitivity and specificity. These thresholds were subsequently defined as the optimal cut-off values. Based on the results, the frequency of contractions was categorized as low frequency (< 4.35 times per hour) and high frequency (≥ 4.35 times per hour), with the average peak intensity of contractions as low intensity (< 71.00) and high intensity (≥ 71.00), and the average duration of contractions as short duration (< 1.45 min) and long duration (≥ 1.45 min). To further investigate the association between contraction-related indicators and delivery before 28 weeks, multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed (Table 4). After adjustment for maternal age, BMI, ART, and cervical length, only the average peak intensity of contractions remained significantly associated with delivery before 28 weeks (P = 0.024), whereas contraction frequency and duration were not significant predictors. As illustrated in the violin plot (Fig. 3), the distribution of the average peak intensity of contractions was broader in women who delivered before 28 weeks compared with those who delivered later. KM analysis further demonstrated that women in the high intensity group (≥ 71.00) delivered significantly earlier than those in the low intensity group (P = 0.00048), with a median gestational age at delivery of approximately 27 weeks (Fig. 4). Establishment and validation of the predictive models

Based on these findings and clinical experience, three predictive models were developed, incorporating ART, cervical length, previous late miscarriage, and the average peak intensity of contractions. Multivariate logistic regression analysis demonstrated that the average peak intensity of contractions (categorized at 71.00) was significantly associated with delivery before 28 weeks after cerclage (odds ratio (OR): 6.03, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.32–27.56, P = 0.021) (Table 5). ROC analysis showed that Model 1 (ART, cervical length, and previous late miscarriage) achieved an AUC of 0.757 (95% CI: 0.631–0.884), Model 2 (adding the average peak intensity as a continuous variable) reached an AUC of 0.787 (95% CI: 0.662–0.913), and Model 3 (incorporating the average peak intensity categorized at 71.00) achieved the highest AUC of 0.806 (95% CI: 0.684–0.927) (Fig. 5). To further validate Model 3, five-fold cross-validation repeated five times was performed. The model demonstrated good discriminatory ability in internal validation, with an average AUC of 0.767 (ranging from 0.647 to 0.887, 95% CI: 0.575–0.961) (Table 6). A nomogram based on Model 3 was constructed to predict the risk of delivery before 28 weeks after cerclage (Fig. 6). The calibration curve indicated strong agreement between predicted and observed probabilities (Fig. 7A), and DCA confirmed clinical utility within a threshold probability range of 0.38–0.82 (Fig. 7B). Predictive performance of Model 3 was further assessed using a confusion matrix, which yielded an overall accuracy of 0.79, specificity of 0.93 (95% CI: 0.81–0.99), and sensitivity of 0.52 (95% CI: 0.30–0.74) (Fig. 8). The positive predictive value (PPV) was 0.79 (95% CI: 0.49–0.95) and the negative predictive value (NPV) was 0.80 (95% CI: 0.66–0.90). While the specificity and overall accuracy were relatively high, the sensitivity was modest, indicating the limited ability of the model to identify all high-risk cases. Discussion

Principal findings

This study evaluated the predictive value of uEMG for delivery before 28 weeks of gestation among women undergoing cervical cerclage. The findings indicated that the average peak intensity of uterine contractions was a reliable predictor of delivery before 28 weeks. In particular, an average peak intensity ≥ 71.00 was significantly associated with a higher risk of delivery before 28 weeks after cervical cerclage. When combined with ART, cervical length, and previous late miscarriage, the predictive model incorporating the average peak intensity of contractions achieved an AUC of 0.806. A visualized nomogram was developed to facilitate clinical decision-making, providing a potentially practical tool for individualized risk stratification and management. These findings highlight the potential of uEMG-based monitoring to improve pregnancy outcomes in women with singleton pregnancies following cervical cerclage.

Results in the context of what is known

In this study, we developed a nomogram incorporating the average peak intensity of contractions (categorized at 71.00), ART, cervical length, and previous late miscarriage to predict delivery before 28 weeks after cervical cerclage. The model achieved an AUC of 0.806, with calibration analysis and DCA confirming satisfactory clinical utility for individualized management strategies. Importantly, the model showed high specificity (0.93), indicating strong accuracy in identifying women at low risk. Individualized risk points can be calculated using the nomogram (examples in Table S1). For example, patient A, who conceived via ART, with a history of late miscarriage, a preoperative cervical length of 15 mm, and postoperative uEMG showing an average peak intensity of contractions ≥ 71.00, scored 215 points (51 + 33 + 66 + 65), corresponding to an estimated 80% risk of delivery before 28 weeks. She ultimately delivered at 19+1 weeks, consistent with the prediction. These findings preliminarily suggest that uEMG may serve as a clinically valuable indicator for predicting pregnancy outcomes after cervical cerclage. However, as the model was derived from a single-center retrospective cohort, further prospective multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are required to validate its predictive accuracy and generalizability. Until then, the nomogram should be regarded as an exploratory tool to support risk stratification, with comprehensive assessment and individualized management remaining essential. Potential management strategies for high-risk women may include individualized tocolytic regimens, closer ultrasound surveillance of cervical changes, hospitalization for further evaluation, and other preventive interventions. If validated, this model could be integrated into existing high-risk pregnancy management pathways as a supportive tool for early risk stratification in women undergoing cervical cerclage.

This study found that the average peak intensity of uterine contractions after cervical cerclage was independently associated with delivery before 28 weeks. Painful uterine contractions, characterized by coordination and forcefulness, often indicate impending PTB [49]. It is well-established that forceful and sustained uterine contractions arise from multiple coordinated bursts [50]. Gap junctions, symmetrical protein channels between adjacent cell membranes, facilitate low-resistance transmission of action potentials. As labor approaches, both the number and size of gap junctions increase, enabling more frequent and stronger bursts. Accordingly, the intensity and frequency of uterine myoelectric bursts increase markedly [51, 52], reflected as higher contraction amplitudes on uEMG. Our findings are consistent with previous studies [53–57], reinforcing evidence that stronger uterine contractions are associated with an elevated risk of PTB.

Although fFN is supported by substantial evidence as a predictor of PTB, its positive predictive value remains relatively low [13, 58, 59]. Moreover, fFN testing is vulnerable to false positives, as results can be affected by recent sexual intercourse, prior vaginal examinations, vaginal bleeding, or uterine contractions [60]. Similarly, while a short cervix on transvaginal ultrasound is considered a reliable predictor of PTB [61], measurements may be influenced by maternal factors such as height, weight, parity, and gestational age [62]. In this study, we evaluated uEMG as a predictor of delivery before 28 weeks of gestation after cervical cerclage. By directly monitoring uterine myoelectrical activity, uEMG offers greater sensitivity, accuracy, and clinical utility, thereby improving predictive performance compared with conventional markers. Additionally, although previous studies reported that a history of uterine surgery did not significantly increase the risk of PTB [63] or result in prolonged labor or adverse neonatal outcomes [64], its potential impact on uterine myoelectrical activity warrants further investigation.

In this study, no significant differences in WBC counts after cerclage were observed between patients who delivered before or after 28 weeks (P = 0.577), which is consistent with previous findings [65, 66]. Due to the retrospective design, some data on CRP levels and vaginal secretion cultures within 48 h after cerclage were unavailable and therefore excluded. Among women with available placental pathology, the detection rate of acute chorioamnionitis was higher in those who delivered before 28 weeks compared with later deliveries, although the difference was not statistically significant. Nevertheless, previous studies have demonstrated that infection may trigger uterine contractions and contribute to PTB [67–70], underscoring the relevance of infection-related biomarkers. Future research should further explore the role of infection in uterine activity and adverse pregnancy outcomes.

This study specifically focused on women with singleton pregnancies after cervical cerclage. Previous studies have reported potential benefits of cervical cerclage in twin pregnancies, including prolonged gestation and improved neonatal outcomes [71–74]. A retrospective study found that twin pregnancies complicated by PTB experienced more frequent uterine contractions than those achieving full-term delivery [75]. These findings suggest that the predictive value of uEMG in twin pregnancies after cerclage warrants further investigation. Another consideration is that all participants in this study received tocolytic therapy after cerclage, so that the potential effects of treatment were not analyzed. As tocolytic agents may influence pregnancy outcomes, future prospective studies should compare the efficacy of different regimens. In addition, among women with postoperative uterine contractions, the proportion of emergency cerclage was higher, suggesting that our model may be more representative of this subgroup. However, subgroup analyses were limited by the small sample size. Finally, this study analyzed only the uEMG recordings obtained within 48 h after cerclage, and continuous monitoring data were unavailable. Future prospective studies with larger cohorts, continuous uEMG monitoring, and temporal analyses are needed to validate and refine these findings.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths worth highlighting. It is the first study to identify the average peak intensity of uterine contractions as an independent predictor of delivery before 28 weeks in women undergoing cervical cerclage. The incorporation of uEMG indicators significantly improved the model performance, as reflected by the higher AUC. With its advantages of non-invasiveness and high sensitivity, uEMG enables dynamic quantification of uterine myoelectrical activity. Moreover, the developed nomogram provides a potentially practical tool for estimating the risk of delivery before 28 weeks of gestation after cervical cerclage, thereby facilitating individualized management and informed clinical decision-making.

Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective study design, combined with the absence of comparator methods and blinded interpretation, limited the ability to minimize potential biases. External validation of the model was not feasible due to sample size constraints. Future multicenter, prospective studies with larger cohorts and blinded assessments are needed to confirm these findings and enhance the reliability and clinical applicability of uEMG after cerclage. Second, the model yielded modest sensitivity and relatively wide confidence intervals for certain predictors, suggesting a potential limitation in accurately identifying high-risk cases. Clinicians should therefore complement the nomogram with comprehensive clinical evaluation to avoid underestimation of risk. This limitation may be related to the relatively small sample size, and larger prospective studies are warranted to improve predictive sensitivity and further validate the clinical utility of our findings.

Conclusions

In summary, this study identified the average peak intensity of uterine contractions measured by uEMG as an independent predictor of delivery before 28 weeks of gestation in women with singleton pregnancies after cervical cerclage. An average peak intensity of ≥ 71.00 was associated with a significantly increased risk of delivery before 28 weeks of gestation. A nomogram based on this parameter, together with key clinical factors, demonstrated satisfactory discriminatory performance and provides a potentially practical tool for supporting individualized risk assessment and management. Prospective multicenter studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate these findings and further refine their clinical applicability.

Computational analysis of radiative heat transfer and free convective flow over a vertical wavy surface with Lorentz forces
Authors: Adebowale Martins Obalalu, Umair Khan, Ishola Abdulmuiz Adeshina, Samia Elattar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn several engineering systems, wavy surfaces are utilized to enhance thermal distribution such as heat exchangers and aerodynamics and drag control. However, when radiative heat transfer, heat generation and magnetic fields are considered, velocity and thermal distribution become more difficult, making it significant to understand their combined influences for improved heat transfer. Therefore, this problem focuses on the flow rate, isotherms, streamlines and thermal distribution of the hydromagnetic fluid flow over a wavy surface subjected to a constant heat flux with radiative heat transfer and magnetic fields influence. The impermeable wavy texture is considered to be heated via a constant heat flux, which supplies the fluid a consistent source of heating energy. The transformed nonlinear governing equations are solved numerically using a robust and efficient computational scheme known as the Spectral Quasi-Linearization Method (SQLM), implemented in Wolfram Mathematica to ensure precise and reliable solutions to the physical problem. The numerical outcomes obtained in the present study are validated through comparison with previously published results, showing excellent agreement and good concordance. This consistency confirms the reliability, effectiveness, and accuracy of the adopted numerical methodology. The findings display that radiation–conduction parameter enhance the thermal distribution within the boundary layer, whereas the amplitude of waviness tends to reduce the fluid velocity.Keywords: Wavy surface, Spectral Quasi-linearization method (SQLM), Radiative heat transfer, And Hydromagnetic fluid flowSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Thermal radiation is one of three major ways of heat transfer, together with conduction and convection. It refers to the transference of thermal energy in the form of electromagnetic waves released by a body due to its temperature1. Different from conduction and convection, thermal radiation does not need a physical medium for the transfer energy and can take place through a vacuum. This process becomes mostly essential at high temperatures, where radiative heat exchange can considerably impact the whole heat transfer process2. In several engineering systems, particularly those operational under raised temperature conditions, thermal radiation promotes greatly to the total heat flux and must therefore be taken into consideration in the study of thermal transport phenomena3. The study of thermal radiation has an essential function in a wide variety of engineering and industrial applications. In electric power systems, radiative heat transfer is important in combustion chambers, power plants, and nuclear reactors where high temperatures are common4. Consequently, the study of thermal radiation remains an essential area of research in fluid mechanics and thermal engineering, adding to the advancement of energy technologies, industrial processes, and advanced cooling applications5. Radiative heat transfer plays an important function in several contemporary engineering and industrial processes, mostly where thermal transport is a significant function in thermal effectiveness6. In solar power systems, such as PV panels, inverters, backup power systems, radiative heat transfer controls how sun energy is absorbed, and released in sun energy technologies such as solar thermal collectors and PV systems7. Precise understanding of radiative heat transfer is therefore essential for improving the efficiency and reliability of renewable energy systems. To make sustainable energy systems more optimal and reliable, it is significant to have a solid knowledge of how radiative thermal transport operate. Furthermore, radiative heat transfer is extremely important in energy transfer in hydroelectric and nuclear power plants, including metal processing, glass production, and semiconductor fabrication8. Elazem and Elgazery9 examine the time dependent heat transport near a vertical wavy surface that is heated using Chebyshev pseudospectral method. They found that the thermal radiation raises the thermal distribution, but decreases the thermal transfer rate. Khan et al.10 examine the entropy generation and laminar mixed convection along a wavy surface with thermal radiation using Keller-box method. Their result show that thermal radiation thickens the thermal boundary layer and reduces the surface heat transfer rate.

The study of electrically conducting fluids in the presence of magnetic fields is known as magnetohydrodynamics (MHD). MHD has become an essential element of modern computational and theoretical research11. Magnetohydrodynamics is vital in several fields such as electromagnetic pumps, space power systems, and electrical generators. Advanced numerical methods are broadly used to study complex MHD flows that cannot be solved logically. Distinct consideration has been given to nanofluids, where the inclusion of nanoparticles increases thermal conductivity and changes the relationship between the fluid and magnetic fields12. Researchers also explore MHD effects in boundary layer flows over numerous geometries, such as stretching sheets, vertical plates, and wavy surfaces, to know better about heat transfer improvement methods13. The essential concept of MHD is in the relationship between the magnetic field and the moving conductive fluid, which produce electromagnetic forces. When a conductive fluid circulates through a magnetic field, electric currents are generated within the fluid14. These currents, sequentially, relate with the magnetic field to produce a force identified as the Lorentz force. This force can considerably change the movement of the fluid, making MHD a key area of study in fluid flow and heat transfer studies. MHD performs a vital responsibility in many engineering and industrial applications. One of its most main uses is in the nuclear reactors cooling systems, where liquid metals such as sodium or lead are made use of as coolants15. The use of magnetic fields aids in the control of fluid motion and increase safety. In metallurgical system, MHD is used to control the flow of molten metals through casting and hardening, enhancing the value of the final product. Electromagnetic pumps, which function without moving mechanical parts, depend on MHD concepts to transport conductive fluids efficiently16. MHD flow has a great influence on flow motion, heat supply, and how thermal transport from one place to another. The Lorentz force generally works as a force that resists fluid motion and slows it down. This effect is commonly referred to as magnetic damping. As the fluid slows down, the thickness of the velocity boundary layer increases, which in turn have an effect on the thermal boundary layer17. As a result, the rate of heat transfer can either increase or decrease depending on the strength of the magnetic field and the properties of the fluid. In many cases, magnetic fields are used to regulate heat transfer processes, suppress instability, and stabilize fluid flow, which is mainly useful in temperate or sensitive environments18. Kabir et al.19 inspect the combined effect of magnetic field and viscous dissipation magnetic field along a vertical wavy surface using the implicit finite difference method. Results show that Eckert number significantly affects thermal transport and skin friction. Mahdy20 studied the magnetohydrodynamics free convection over a vertical wavy plate in a porous medium using Runge–Kutta algorithm effect.

Several manufacturing and engineering system depend on wavy surfaces for their high-temperature performance. These kinds of applications are important in lower/upper marine atmospheric, structural forms, physics, material science, and seismic wave propagation and many more areas in thermal sciences21. Vertical wavy surfaces have drawn a lot of interest from engineers as a result of to their capability to increase thermal and mass transport. When a vertical surface is heated or cooled, change in temperature between the surface and the surrounding fluid generate buoyancy forces that drive natural convection22. This occurrence considerably affects the velocity and thermal supply within the boundary layer near the surface. Hence, understanding the behavior of fluid flow and heat transfer along vertical surfaces is crucial for enhancing the design and performance of many thermal system23. In addition, wavy surfaces can help delay boundary layer separation and enhance fluid mixing, which is favorable for sustaining stable heat transfer conditions. To better comprehend the physical structure involved in such flows, researchers usually evaluate fluid motion and heat transfer over vertical wavy surfaces using mathematical and computational methods24. The governing equations for mass, momentum, and energy are usually formulated based on the values of fluid dynamics and heat transfer25. Because these equations are usually nonlinear and complex, analytical solutions are rarely possible. Hence, numerical methods such as finite difference methods, finite element methods, and spectral techniques are extensively used to achieve approximate solutions26. Generally, the analysis of fluid flow and heat transfer over vertical wavy surfaces offers valuable awareness into the relationship between surface geometry, fluid dynamics, and heat transport. Such studies add to the development of more effective heat systems and support ongoing development in energy engineering, materials processing, and industrial heat transfer applications27. The aim of this research is to investigate the influence of radiative heat transfer on fluid motion and thermal energy transport over a vertical corrugated surface subjected to a uniform constant heat flux, which provides a steady thermal energy input at the boundary and governs the resulting temperature distribution within the flow field. Furthermore, this research also purposes to utilize the spectral Quasi-linearization method (SQLM) to acquire precise numerical solutions of the fundamental equations. The goal is to get a deeper comprehension of the how radiative heat transfer and surface shape impact on hydromagnetic flow temperature distribution performance along the vertical impermeable wavy geometry. The originality of this research lies in the combined study of magnetic field influence, heat generation, radiative heat transfer on MHD free convection under constant heat flux conditions. This research gives a more realistic model via combining all of these characteristics together, in contrast to many other research that have been conducted in the past that have either considered flat surfaces or neglected one of these influences.

Spectral Quasi-linearization method (SQLM)

The Spectral Quasi-Linearization Method (SQLM) is a numerical technique used to solve highly nonlinear differential equations arising in fluid flow and heat transfer problems. The method combines the quasi-linearization approach with spectral collocation techniques to obtain highly accurate approximate solutions. In this method, the nonlinear governing equations are first linearized iteratively using the quasi-linearization procedure, where nonlinear terms are approximated by a sequence of linear equations. After linearization, the spectral collocation method, commonly based on Chebyshev polynomials, is applied to discretize the equations over the computational domain. This process converts the differential equations into a system of algebraic equations that can be solved efficiently23.

Figure 3 demonstrates the influence of the magnetic field parameter (M) on the velocity distribution of the fluid flow. The graph presents three different values of the magnetic parameter, namely M = 0.1, M = 0.2, and M = 0.3. It can be observed that the velocity profile decreases as the magnetic field parameter M increases. The fluid velocity reaches its highest value when M = 0.1, while lower velocity values are recorded for M = 0.2 and M = 0.3. This indicates that the presence of a stronger magnetic field suppresses the fluid motion. Physically, this behavior occurs because the applied magnetic field generates a resistive force known as the Lorentz force, which acts opposite to the fluid motion. As the magnetic field intensity increases, the Lorentz force becomes stronger, thereby increasing the resistance to the fluid flow and reducing the momentum of the fluid particles. Consequently, the fluid experiences a deceleration, leading to a reduction in the velocity boundary layer thickness. Additionally, the figure shows that all velocity profiles initially increase from the wall region, attain a maximum peak at a certain distance, and then gradually decline. This trend confirms that the fluid motion becomes weaker farther away from the surface due to diminishing momentum effects. The downward arrow in the figure further indicates the decreasing trend of the velocity profile with increasing magnetic field strength. Figure 4 illustrates the influence of the thermal radiation parameter Rd on the temperature distribution. The graph compares three values of the radiation parameter, namely Rd = 1, Rd = 2, and Rd = 3. It is clearly observed that the temperature profile increases as the thermal radiation parameter Rd increases. When Rd = 1, the fluid exhibits the lowest temperature distribution, while higher temperature values are recorded for Rd = 2 and Rd = 3. This behavior indicates that stronger thermal radiation enhances the thermal energy within the fluid. Physically, an increase in the thermal radiation parameter contributes additional heat energy to the fluid, which raises the fluid temperature and improves thermal diffusion. As a result, more thermal energy is transferred throughout the flow region, leading to an increase in the thermal boundary layer thickness. This explains why the temperature profiles become higher and decay more gradually with increasing values of Rd.

The List Linear Arboricity of Digraphs
Yueping Shi, Ping Hu

Abstract
A (directed) linear forest is a (di)graph whose components are (directed) paths. The linear arboricity l ​ a ​ ( F ) la(F) of a (di)graph F F is the minimum number of (directed) linear forests required to decompose its edges. Akiyama, Exoo, and Harary (1980) proposed the Linear Arboricity Conjecture that l ​ a ​ ( G ) ⩽ ⌈ Δ + 1 2 ⌉ la(G)\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{\Delta+1}{2}\right\rceil for any graph G G of maximum degree Δ \Delta . The current best known bound, due to Lang and Postle (2023), establishes l ​ a ​ ( G ) ⩽ Δ 2 + 3 ​ Δ ​ log 4 ​ Δ la(G)\leqslant\frac{\Delta}{2}+3\sqrt{\Delta}\log^{4}\Delta for sufficiently large Δ \Delta . And they proved this in the stronger list setting proposed by An and Wu. For a digraph D D , let its maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( D ) \Delta(D) be the maximum of all in-degrees and out-degrees of its vertices. Nakayama and Péroche (1987) conjectured that l ​ a ​ ( D ) ⩽ Δ ⁡ ( D ) + 1 la(D)\leqslant\Delta(D)+1 for every digraph D D . We extend Lang and Postle’s result to digraphs with a matching error term. We show that l ​ a ​ ( D ) ⩽ Δ + 6 ​ Δ ​ log 4 ​ Δ la(D)\leqslant\Delta+6\sqrt{\Delta}\log^{4}\Delta for any digraph D D with Δ = Δ ⁡ ( D ) \Delta=\Delta(D) sufficiently large. Moreover, we also establish this bound in the stronger list setting, where each arc e ∈ A ⁡ ( D ) e\in A(D) is assigned a list of colors, and each arc is assigned a color from its list such that each color class forms a directed linear forest.

Lemma 9

(Lovász Local Lemma)
.

Let

be events in an arbitrary probability space.
Suppose that each event

is mutually independent of all but at most

It can be shown with the Lovász Local Lemma that with positive probability, we can repeat the above random coloring procedure until most arcs of the digraph have been colored.
Once this is done, we can complete the coloring with the following lemma using a fresh set of colors that was reserved beforehand. Claim 20

(
[
12
, Claim 23]
)
.

We have
\mathbf{E}(|R^{\prime+}(v,c)|)
,
\mathbf{E}(|R^{\prime-}(v,c)|)\leqslant R_{i}\cdot(1-p\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2})
for every vertex
v
and color

.

Proof.

For any arc

,

in step

Recall the definition of

\mathrm{Keep}_{i}
in Setup

It follows that the probability that

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{|N^{-}(u,x)|+|N^{+}(w,x)|-2}\cdot(1-\mathrm{Eq}(e,x))=\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2},

Claim 21

(
[
12
, Claim 24]
)
.

We have
\mathbf{E}(|L^{\prime}(e)|)\geqslant L_{i}\cdot\mathrm{Keep}_{i}^{2}
for every arc
e
and color
c
.

Proof.

Consider an arc
e=uv
and a color
c\in L_{i}(e)
.
We show that the probability that
c\in L^{\prime}(e)
is at least
\mathrm{Keep}_{i}^{2}
.
From this, the claim follows from
|L_{i}(e)|=L_{i}
and linearity of expectation.

Let
K_{u}
,
K_{v}
be the events that no arc in
N^{+}(u,c)\setminus\{e\}
, respectively
N^{-}(v,c)\setminus\{e\}

retains
c
after step
3
of the procedure.
It turns out that the simplest way to compute
\mathbf{Pr}(K_{u}\cap K_{v})
is
through the indirect route of computing
\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}}\cup\overline{K_{v}})
which is equal to

1-\mathbf{Pr}(K_{u}\cap K_{v})
.
Now, by the most basic case of the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle,

\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}}\cup\overline{K_{v}})
is equal to
\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}})+\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{v}})-\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}}\cap\overline{K_{v}})
.
Following the computation in (
A.1
) we know that

\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}})+\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{v}})=(|N^{+}(u,c)|+|N^{-}(v,c)|-2)\frac{p}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2},

so we just need to
bound
\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}}\cap\overline{K_{v}})
.

\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}}\cap\overline{K_{v}})
is the probability that there is some pair of arcs
e_{1}=uw\in N^{+}(u,c)\setminus\{e\}
, and
e_{2}=xv\in N^{-}(v,c)\setminus\{e\}
such that
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are
both activated, receive and retain
c
during the procedure (
w=x
is possible).
Now, for each such pair, let
R_{e_{1},e_{2}}
be the event that
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
both retain
c
after step
3
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(R_{e_{1},e_{2}})=\left(\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{|N^{+}(u,c)\cup N^{-}(w,c)\cup N^{-}(v,c)\cup N^{+}(x,c)|-2}(1-\mathrm{Eq}(e_{1},c))(1-\mathrm{Eq}(e_{2},c)).

Since

\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{|N^{+}(u,c)\cup N^{-}(w,c)\cup N^{-}(v,c)\cup N^{+}(x,c)|-2}>\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{|N^{+}(u,c)|+|N^{-}(w,c)|-2+|N^{-}(v,c)|+|N^{+}(x,c)|-2},

we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(R_{e_{1},e_{2}})>\left(\frac{p}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)^{2},

where the computation in (
A.1
) is used again.

For any two distinct pairs
(e_{1},e_{2})
and
(e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})
, it is impossible for
R_{e_{1},e_{2}}

and
R_{e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime}}
to both hold.
Therefore the probability that
R_{e_{1},e_{2}}
holds for at least one pair, is equal to the sum over all pairs
e_{1},e_{2}
of
\mathbf{Pr}(R_{e_{1},e_{2}})
, which by the above computation yields:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}}\cap\overline{K_{v}})

\displaystyle\geqslant\left((|N^{+}(u,c)|-1)(|N^{-}(v,c)|-1)\right)\left(\frac{p}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}.

Combining this with our bound on
\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{u}})+\mathbf{Pr}(\overline{K_{v}})
, we see that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}({K_{u}}\cap{K_{v}})

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-(|N^{+}(u,c)|+|N^{-}(v,c)|-2)\frac{p}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(|N^{+}(u,c)|-1)(|N^{-}(v,c)|-1)\left(\frac{p}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}|N^{+}(u,c)|\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}|N^{-}(v,c)|\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right).

Recall the definition of
\mathrm{Vq}^{+}
in (

in (

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(c\in L^{\prime}(e))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Pr}(K_{u}\cap K_{v})(1-\mathrm{Vq}^{+}(u,c))(1-\mathrm{Vq}^{-}(v,c))

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(1-p\frac{|N^{+}(u,c)|}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)(1-\mathrm{Vq}^{+}(u,c))\cdot\left(1-p\frac{|N^{-}(v,c)|}{L_{i}}\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)(1-\mathrm{Vq}^{-}(v,c))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Keep}_{i}^{2},

as desired.
∎

It remains to deal with

However, these random variables are not concentrated around their expectations,

More precisely, let

Claim 22

(
[
12

)
.

\mathbf{E}(|N^{*+}(v,c)|)

We will show that for each

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(e\in N^{*+}(v,c))=\mathbf{Pr}(A\cap B)(1-\mathrm{Vq}^{-}(u,c))\leqslant\mathrm{Keep}_{i}\left(1-p\mathrm{Retain}_{i}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{L_{i}}.

For each color

and arc

in

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(Z(x,f))

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{2p}{L_{i}}\right)^{\left|\left[(N^{-}(u,x)\cap N^{-}(u,c))\right]\setminus\{e,f\}\right|}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(1-\frac{p}{L_{i}}\right)^{\left|\left[N^{+}(v,x)\cup N^{+}(w,c)\cup N^{-}(u,x)\cup N^{-}(u,c)\right]\setminus[(N^{-}(u,x)\cap N^{-}(u,c))\cup\{e,f\}]\right|}

A.2
Concentration

We use Talagrand’s inequality to show the concentration.

Theorem 23

(Talagrand’s inequality
[
16
]
)
.

Let
X
be a non-negative random variable determined by the independent trials
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
. Suppose that for every set of possible outcomes of the trials, we have:

1.

changing the outcome of any one trial can affect
X
by at most
k
and

2.

for each
s>0
, if
X\geqslant s
then there is a set of at most
rs
trials whose outcomes certify
X\geqslant s
.

Then for any
t>96k\sqrt{r\mathbf{E}(X)}+128rk^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(|X-\mathbf{E}(X)|>t)\leqslant 4\exp\left({-\frac{t^{2}}{8k^{2}r(4\mathbf{E}(X)+t)}}\right).

The following three claims contain the desired concentration bounds.

(
[
12
, Claim 26]
)

v
and color

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}(\left||R^{\prime+}(v,c)|-\mathbf{E}(|R^{\prime+}(v,c)|)\right|>\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{R_{i}}\log^{2}\Delta)\leqslant\frac{\Delta^{-10\log\Delta}}{5},

We prove only for

Fix a vertex

, a color

and let

.
We wish to apply Talagrand’s inequality to

with

,

and

It easy to see that changing any of the outcome of the arc activation in step

Editors’ Note
Authors: Andrew J. Spieker, Nandita Mitra
Published: 2026

In 1959, Jerome Cornfield and others published a groundbreaking manuscript entitled “Smoking and Lung Cancer: Recent Evidence and a Discussion of Some Questions” in Journal of the National Cancer Institute. Cornfield is widely recognized as the first to introduce the idea of a sensitivity analysis. While the landscape of causal inference and observational data analysis has changed over the decades, the general framework for evaluating the sensitivity of a conclusion to departures from unfalsifiable identifying assumptions remains widely used.

We are delighted to present this Special Issue of Observational Studies, comprising fourteen contributed commentaries from a wide range of perspectives. The issue begins with a historical perspective on Cornfield’s work (Greenhouse and Phillips). The next twelve contributed commentaries are organized in three broad categories: •Perspectives and reflections on Cornfield’s seminal work (Lun; Frank et al.; Freidling and Piccininni; Cook et al.).•Methodological elaborations and direct extensions of Cornfield’s work (Xu et al.; Dalal et al.; Huang and Pimentel; Stanghellini and Geneletti).•Contemporary advances in sensitivity analyses (Hettinger et al.; Knaeble et al.; Xiang et al.; Eisenhauer et al.). The issue closes with a heartwarming and humorous perspective on Jerome Cornfield, based on a colleague’s personal interactions with him (Ellenberg).

Although the causal link between smoking and lung cancer is now widely known, the willingness of the public to believe it rested partly on the strong evidence implied by having multiple analytic frameworks and demonstrating that other “unmeasured” factors were unlikely to mitigate the strong causal association. Principled approaches to sensitivity analyses still invariably have a major impact on policy decision making, including in the assessment of vaccines, nutritional excise taxes, and gun laws. This Special Issue both celebrates a seminal manuscript and provides a forum for experts to discuss modern analytic approaches that shape modern sensitivity analyses and making inferences from complex observational data. We hope you enjoy reading these articles as much as we did.

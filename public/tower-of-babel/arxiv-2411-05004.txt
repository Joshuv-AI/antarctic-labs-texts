Non-onsite symmetry breaking: topological phase coexistence and criticality
Zhehao Zhang, Yabo Li, Tsung-Cheng Lu

Abstract
We explore the states of matter arising from the spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} non-onsite symmetries. In one spatial dimension, we construct a frustration-free lattice model exhibiting SSB of a non-onsite symmetry, which features the coexistence of two ground states with distinct symmetry-protected topological (SPT) orders. We analytically prove the two-fold ground-state degeneracy and the existence of a finite energy gap. Fixing the symmetry sector yields a long-range entangled ground state that features long-range correlations among non-invertible charged operators. We also present a constant-depth measurement-feedback protocol to prepare such a state with a constant success probability in the thermodynamic limit, which may be of independent interest. Under a symmetric deformation, the SSB persists up to a critical point, beyond which a gapless phase characterized by a conformal field theory emerges. In two spatial dimensions, the SSB of 1-form non-onsite symmetries leads to a long-range entangled state (SPT soup) - a condensate of 1d SPT along any closed loops. On a torus, there are four such locally indistinguishable states that exhibit algebraic correlations between local operators, which we derived via a mapping to the critical O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) loop model. This constitutes an intriguing example of ‘topological quantum criticality’.

IV
2d 1-form non-onsite symmetry breaking - SPT soup

Now we extend the discussion to 2d and explore the physics of the spontaneous breaking of a 1-form non-onsite symmetry. We consider a honeycomb lattice with periodic boundary conditions and define qubits on vertices. We define the 1-form symmetry as
U=\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\text{loop}}\text{CZ}_{ij}
, i.e. the product of CZ gates acting along any closed loops, including both the contractible ones and the non-contractible ones. All the contractible 1-form symmetries can be obtained by taking the product of local plaquette symmetry:
U_{p}=\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in p}\text{CZ}_{ij}

To explore the 1-form non-onsite symmetry breaking, we first consider the following state:

Now we discuss the signatures of long-range entanglement of the SPT soup from a few distinct perspectives. First,

Appendix A
General remark on non-onsite symmetry

A.1
Non-onsite to onsite symmetries via duality

Here we show that the intrinsically non-onsite symmetry
U_{\text{CZ}}=\prod_{i}CZ_{i,i+1}
can become an onsite symmetry under a Kramer-Wannier duality. To start, we consider a 1d lattice of even number of vertices, and write
U_{\text{CZ}}
as

U_{CZ}=\prod_{i}CZ_{2i-2,2i-1}CZ_{2i-1,2i}=\prod_{i}\frac{1+Z_{2i-1}+Z_{2i-2}Z_{2i}-Z_{2i-2}Z_{2i-1}Z_{2i}}{2},

(6)

A Kramers-Wannier duality on the even sublattice gives the following mapping for local operators symmetric under
\prod_{i}X_{2i}
:

X_{2i}\rightarrow Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2},\quad Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2}\rightarrow X_{2i+2}.

(7)

As a result,

U_{\text{CZ}}\to U^{\prime}=\prod_{i}CX_{2i-1,2i},

(8)

and
U^{\prime}
is onsite by grouping the adjacent qubits on vertex
2i-1
and vertex
2i
.

There could also exist non-onsite symmetries that cannot be brought onsite even under a duality transformation. We expect the anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry
[
76
]
generated by
U_{CZX}=\prod_{i}CZ_{i,i+1}\prod_{i}X_{i}
in 1d is one such example, as we argue below based on SymTFT description (see Sec.
D
for more discussion). Within this description, this anomalous symmetry corresponds to a semionic anyon line in the 2d bulk topological order. A duality transformation in a 1d system corresponds to a change of reference gapped boundary. No matter what duality transformation we apply, the dual symmetry of
U_{CZX}
is always given by the same semionic anyon line from the bulk, hence also being anomalous. This suggests no duality can bring the anomalous
U_{CZX}
operator onsite.

To conclude, we expect that there exist three hierarchies for non-onsite symmetries. The first hierarchy includes the symmetry operators that can be made onsite by a conjugation, i.e. there exists a unitary operator
V
, such that the symmetry operator
U=V(\prod_{s}U_{s})V^{\dagger}
. The second and the third hierarchies are intrinsically non-onsite symmetries, where the former includes the operators that can be brought onsite by some duality transformations, and the latter cannot be brought onsite even under dualities. The non-onsite
U_{\text{CZ}}
symmetry studied here is a representative of the second hierarchy.

A.2
Equivalence between onsite and non-onsite symmetries with ancillae

Let us consider introducing an ancilla qubit at state
\ket{+}_{\tau}
on each even site of the spin chain. There is a global symmetry
\prod_{i}\tau^{x}_{2i}
on the ancilla state. With this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry and the non-onsite symmetry
U_{\text{CZ}}
, one can define a diagonal non-onsite symmetry
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{d}
:
U_{d}=\prod_{i}CZ_{i,i+1}\prod_{j}\tau^{x}_{2j}
. After the conjugation of the finite-depth local unitary circuit

V=\prod_{i}\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\tau^{z}_{2i}+CZ_{2i-1,2i}CZ_{2i,2i+1}-\tau^{z}_{2i}CZ_{2i-1,2i}CZ_{2i,2i+1}\Big),

(9)

U_{d}
is mapped to the onsite symmetry
U^{\prime}_{d}=\prod_{i}\tau^{x}_{2i}
. Therefore, we see that
non-onsite-ness is not stable
, in that a non-onsite
U_{CZ}
symmetry in the second hierarchy becomes intrinsically onsite, after including ancillae and taking the diagonal symmetry.

We also note that if considering the two additional symmetries
Z_{2}^{e}
and
Z_{2}^{o}
generated by onsite unitaries
U_{e}=\prod_{i}X_{2i},U_{o}=\prod_{i}X_{2i+1}
, which together with
U_{\text{CZ}}
forms a type-III mixed anomaly
[
12
,
13
]
, under the conjugation of
V
,
U_{e}
and
U_{0}
become

\begin{split}&U^{\prime}_{e}=\prod_{i}X_{2i}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\tau^{z}_{2i}+Z_{2i-1}Z_{2i+1}-\tau^{z}_{2i}Z_{2i-1}Z_{2i+1}\Big),\\
&U^{\prime}_{o}=\prod_{i}X_{2i+1},\\
\end{split}

(10)

where the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{e}
symmetry becomes non-onsite. In fact, due to the anomaly, there is always at least one non-onsite symmetry operator no matter how we make conjugation.

A.3
Onsite symmetry can also have non-invertible charge operators

In the main text, we show that any unitary symmetry with a non-zero trace cannot have invertible charged operators. The charged operator
X_{i}-Z_{i-1}X_{i}Z_{i+1}
of the non-onsite symmetry
U_{\text{CZ}}=\prod_{i}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}
is one such example. However, some onsite symmetries can also have a non-zero trace, hence having a non-invertible charged operator. For instance, consider a 1d lattice of qutrit, with a local Hilbert space spanned by 3 computational bases
\ket{0},\ket{1},\ket{2}
, one defines a global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
onsite symmetry
U=\prod_{i}(\ket{0}\bra{1}+\ket{1}\bra{0}+\ket{2}\bra{2 })_{i}
, which exchanges between
\ket{0}
and
\ket{1}
on every site while acting trivially on
\ket{2}
.
U
is onsite but due to its non-zero trace, the charged operator (i.e.
O_{i}=\ket{0}\bra{0}-\ket{1}\bra{1}
) must be non-invertible.

Appendix B
Details on 1d non-onsite symmetry breaking

B.1
Proof for intrinsically non-onsite
\prod_{i}CZ_{i,i+1}
symmetry

Here we show that
U_{CZ}=\prod_{i}CZ_{i,i+1}
is intrinsically non-onsite by a proof of contradiction. To start, we calculate the trace of
U_{CZ}
on a length-
N

\tr U_{CZ}=2^{N}\bra{+}^{\otimes N}U_{CZ}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}=2^{N}\cdot\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\ket{\text{cluster}}=2\cdot 2^{\frac{N}{2}},

(11)

where we have used
\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\ket{\text{cluster}}=2^{1-\frac{N}{2}}
. We assume there is a unitary conjugation that can bring
U_{CZ}
to a translational invariant onsite operator
\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N/m}u
, where
u
is a local unitary acting on each site that contains
m
neighboring qubits. Since a matrix trace is invariant under unitary conjugation, one should have

\tr U_{CZ}=\tr\left(\bigotimes_{s=1}^{N/m}u\right).

(12)

To proceed, we notice that for any two unitary operators
U_{A}
and
U_{B}
acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B}
, the trace of their tensor product over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
satisfies

\tr_{AB}(U_{A}\otimes U_{B})=(\tr_{A}U_{A})(\tr_{B}U_{B}).

(13)

This implies

\tr U_{CZ}=(\tr_{s}u)^{N/m}=k^{N/m}

(14)

where
k\equiv\tr_{s}u
is the trace of
u
on the
2^{m}
-dim local Hilbert space. In particular, since
U_{CZ}
generates a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, the eigenvalues of
u_{s}
are
\pm 1
, which implies that the trace
k
must be an integer. On the other hand, comparing Eq.
11

14
, one finds

k=2^{\frac{m}{2}+\frac{m}{N}},

(15)

which is an integer only when
m=N
. This contradicts the assumption that
U_{\text{CZ}}
can be brought onsite by a unitary conjugation and a regrouping of constant
m
neighboring qubits into a single site. Therefore, we conclude that
U_{CZ}
is intrinsically non-onsite.

\Lambda
,

h_{\Lambda}\coloneq\sum_{i\in\Lambda:i+3\in\Lambda}\left(P_{i}Q_{i+1}+Q_{i}P_{i+3}\right),

(33)

where
P_{i}\coloneq\frac{1-X_{i}}{2}
and
Q_{i}\coloneq\frac{1-Z_{i}X_{i+1}Z_{i+2}}{2}
are projectors.
h_{\Lambda}
is the sum of terms that are entirely supported in region
\Lambda
. Using the argument presented in
B.2
, we can show that for
|\Lambda|\geq 4
and, for simplicity,
|\Lambda|
is even,
h_{\Lambda}
has five ground states,

\begin{split}&\ket{+}_{\Lambda}\coloneq\ket{+++...+}_{\Lambda},~~~\ket{(1,1)}_{\Lambda}\coloneq\prod_{i\in\Lambda:i+1\in\Lambda}CZ_{i,i+1}\ket{+++...+}_{\Lambda},~~~\ket{(-1,1)}_{\Lambda}\coloneq\prod_{i\in\Lambda:i+1\in\Lambda}CZ_{i,i+1}\ket{-++...+}_{\Lambda}\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\ket{(1,-1)}_{\Lambda}\coloneq\prod_{i\in\Lambda:i+1\in\Lambda}CZ_{i,i+1}\ket{+-+...+}_{\Lambda},~~~\ket{(-1,-1)}_{\Lambda}\coloneq\prod_{i\in\Lambda:i+1\in\Lambda}CZ_{i,i+1}\ket{--+...+}_{\Lambda},\end{split}

(34)

where
\ket{(m,n)}_{\Lambda}
(
m,n=\pm 1
) are nothing but cluster state defined on an open interval
\Lambda
in symmetry sector
(m,n)
.

The five ground states have the following inner product relations:

\prescript{}{\Lambda}{\innerproduct{+}{(m,n)}_{\Lambda}}=\frac{(-1)^{mn}}{2^{\absolutevalue{\Lambda}/2}},~~~\prescript{}{\Lambda}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{\Lambda}}=\delta_{m^{\prime},m}\delta_{n^{\prime},n}.

(35)

Consider two overlapping and connected intervals
A
and
B
such that
\absolutevalue{A}\geq 8
,
\absolutevalue{B}\geq 8
, and
\absolutevalue{A \cap B}\geq 4
. Let
A^{\prime}\coloneq A\setminus B
and
B^{\prime}\coloneq B\setminus A
. Note that
A^{\prime}
,
A\cap B
, and
B^{\prime}
are also connected intervals. Without loss of generality, we can assume the following geometry for
A
and
B

m
labels the symmetry charge on the red sublattice and
n
labels the symmetry charge on the blue sublattice. The labeling of the boundary qubits will be used later in the proof.

Before deriving the overlap between ground states in
A
and
B
, we want to emphasize a trick that is going to be extensively used in the rest of the proof. We notice that
\ket{(m,n)}_{A}
can be decomposed as

\ket{(m,n)}_{A}=CZ_{\alpha,\alpha+1}\ket{(m,n)}_{A^{\prime}}\ket{1,1}_{A\cap B}=CZ_{\alpha,\alpha+1}\ket{(1,1)}_{A^{\prime}}\ket{(m,n)}_{A\cap B},

(36)

where intuitively it means that the information of the symmetry charge sectors
(m,n)
can be put into either region
A^{\prime}
or region
A\cap B
.
Then, we have

\begin{split}&\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{+}{+}_{B}}=\ket{+}_{B^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{+}},\\
\\
&\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{+}_{B}}=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(m,n)}}\prescript{}{A\cap B}{\bra{(1,1)}}CZ_{\alpha,\alpha+1}\ket{+}_{A\cap B}\ket{+}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\ket{+}_{B^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(m,n)}}\\
\\
&\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{(m',n'))}_{B}}=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(m,n)}}\prescript{}{A\cap B}{\bra{(1,1)}}CZ_{\alpha,\alpha+1}CZ_{\beta-1,\beta}\ket{(1,1)}_{A\cap B}\ket{(m',n')}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~=\frac{1}{2}\ket{(m',n')}_{B^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(m,n))}}+\frac{1}{2}\ket{(\bar{m}',n')}_{B^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(m,\bar{n}))}}.\end{split}

(37)

Note that
\Pi_{A}\Pi_{A\cup B}=\Pi_{A\cup B}\Pi_{A}=\Pi_{A\cup B}
, and similarly for
\Pi_{B}
, because of frustration-freeness. Frustration-freeness also implies that
\Pi_{A}\wedge\Pi_{B}=\Pi_{A\cup B}
.

Now, let’s consider the quantity of interest

\begin{split}\delta(A,B)\coloneq\|\Pi_{A}\Pi_{B}-\Pi_{A}\wedge\Pi_{B}\|=\|\Pi_{A}\Pi_{B}-\Pi_{A\cup B}\|=\|(\Pi_{A}-\Pi_{A\cup B})(\Pi_{B}-\Pi_{A\cup B})\|.\end{split}

(38)

In order to prove this bound, we consider an equivalent characterization of
\delta(A,B)
in terms of states instead of projections:

\begin{split}\delta(A,B)=\mathrm{sup}\{|\innerproduct{\psi}{\chi}|:&\ket{\psi},\ket{\chi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A\cup B},\innerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\leq 1,\innerproduct{\chi}{\chi}\leq 1,\\
&(\Pi_{A}-\Pi_{A\cup B})\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi},(\Pi_{B}-\Pi_{A\cup B})\ket{\chi}=\ket{\chi}\}.\end{split}

(39)

Note that the condition
(\Pi_{A}-\Pi_{A\cup B})\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
means that
\ket{\psi}
is a ground state in
A
and it is orthogonal to the ground states in
A\cup B
, and similarly for
\ket{\chi}
. So we can write them as

\begin{split}&\ket{\psi}=\ket{+}_{A}\ket{\psi_{+}}_{B^{\prime}}+\sum_{m,n=\pm 1}\ket{(m,n)}_{A}\ket{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}},\\
&\ket{\chi}=\ket{\chi_+}_{A^{\prime}}\ket{+}_{B}+\sum_{m,n=\pm 1}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}\ket{(m,n)}_{B},\end{split}

(40)

for some states
\ket{\psi_+},\ket{\psi_{(m,n)}}\in\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime}}
and
\ket{\chi_+},\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}
.

Restricting
\ket{\psi}
and
\ket{\chi}
to be orthogonal to the ground states in
A\cup B
gives the following relations

\begin{split}0&=\prescript{}{A\cup B}{\innerproduct{+}{\psi}}=\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\psi_{+}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}}\sum_{m,n=\pm 1}(-1)^{mn}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
0&=\prescript{}{A\cup B}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\psi}}=\prescript{}{A\cup B}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{+}_{A}}\ket{\psi_+}_{B^{\prime}}+\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}=\pm 1}\prescript{}{A\cup B}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{(m',n')}_{A}}\ket{\psi_{(m',n')}}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\psi_{+}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}=\pm 1}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{(1,1)}}\prescript{}{A}{\bra{(m,n)}}CZ_{\beta-1,\beta}\ket{(m,n)}_{A}\ket{\psi_{(m',n')}}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\psi_{+}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}=\pm 1}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{(1,1)}}(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}Z_{\beta}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,\bar{n}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{\beta}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,\bar{n}^{\prime}})\ket{\psi_{(m',n')}}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\psi_{+}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{(1, 1)}\ket{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(-1, 1)}{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{(1, 1)}\ket{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}_{B^{\prime}}}-\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{(-1, 1)}\ket{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}_{B^{\prime}}}\end{split}

(41)

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}0&=\prescript{}{A\cup B}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi}}=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{+}\ket{\psi_+}_{A^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2^{|B|/2}}\sum_{m,n=\pm 1}(-1)^{mn}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{+}}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}\\
0&=\prescript{}{A\cup B}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{|B|/2}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(1, 1)}}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1, -1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(1, 1)}\ket{\chi_{(\bar{m},n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}-\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(1, -1)}\ket{\chi_{(\bar{m},n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\end{split}

(42)

The constraints on the norms give

\begin{split}1&\geq\innerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\\
&=\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_+}_{B^{\prime}}}+\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}}\sum_{m,n}\left(\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{\psi_{+}}_{B^{\prime}}}\right)\\
&\geq(1-\frac{4}{2^{|A|/2}})\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_+}_{B^{\prime}}}+(1-\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}})\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
\\
1&\geq\innerproduct{\chi}{\chi}\\
&=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_+}{\chi_+}_{A^{\prime}}}+\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_{(m,n)}}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2^{|B|/2}}\sum_{m,n}\left(\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_{(m,n)}}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}\right)\\
&\geq(1-\frac{4}{2^{|B|/2}})\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_+}{\chi_+}_{A^{\prime}}}+(1-\frac{1}{2^{|B|/2}})\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_{(m,n)}}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

(43)

The overlap between
\ket{\psi}
and
\ket{\chi}
is

\begin{split}\innerproduct{\psi}{\chi}&=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{+}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}+\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{+}_{A\cap B}}\ket{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{\psi_{+}}}\prescript{}{A\cap B}{\innerproduct{+}{(m,n)}_{B}}+\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{\psi_{m',n'}}}\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{B}}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(-1)^{mn+m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m',n')}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi_{m}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{+}}{(m,n)}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{\psi_{m',n'}}}\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{B}}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}},\end{split}

(44)

where we used (
41
) and (
42
) to obtain the first line of the second equation. Now our goal is to pull out an exponentially small factor from the fourth line of the second equation, then we can bound
\absolutevalue{\braket{\psi}{\chi}}
by a sum of exponentially small numbers.

We first compute
\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{B}}
in the fourth line and it can be written as

\begin{split}\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{B}}&=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{}{\bra{(1,1)}}\prescript{}{A\cap B}{\bra{(m',n')}}CZ_{\alpha,\alpha+1}CZ_{\beta-1,\beta}\ket{(m,n)}_{A\cap B}\ket{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&=\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\bra{(1,1)}}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}(1+Z_{\alpha}+Z_{\beta}+Z_{\alpha}Z_{\beta})+\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\bar{m}^{\prime},m}\delta_{n^{\prime},n}(1-Z_{\alpha}+Z_{\beta}-Z_{\alpha}Z_{\beta})\\
&+\delta_{m^{\prime},m}\delta_{\bar{n}^{\prime},n}(1+Z_{\alpha}-Z_{\beta}-Z_{\alpha}Z_{\beta})+\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\bar{m}^{\prime},m}\delta_{\bar{n}^{\prime},n}(1-Z_{\alpha}-Z_{\beta}+Z_{\alpha}Z_{\beta})\Big)\ket{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}\end{split}

(45)

Then the fourth line becomes

\begin{split}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}&\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{\psi_{m',n'}}}\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{B}}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}\\
=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{m,n}&{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(1,-1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}-{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}-{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&-{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(1,-1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}-{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&-{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{m,n}&{\color[rgb]{0,0.88,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\Big(\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}-\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(1,-1)}_{B^{\prime}}}\Big)}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\Big(\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(1,-1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}-\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,\bar{n})}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}\Big)}\\
&+{\color[rgb]{1,0.5,0}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\Big(\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}-\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}\Big)}\\
&-{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\Big(\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m}, n)}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}-\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(\bar{m},\bar{n})}}{(-1,1)}_{B^{\prime}}}\Big)},\end{split}

(46)

where we colored the terms that will be grouped together, and the summation inside each parenthesis can be simplified using (
41
). Then, we get

\begin{split}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}&\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\bra{\psi_{m',n'}}}\prescript{}{A}{\innerproduct{(m',n')}{(m',n')}{(m,n)}_{B}}\ket{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A}/2}}\sum_{m,n}&-\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{(m,n)}_{B^{\prime}}}-\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{m, n}}\\
&-\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{(\bar{m},n)}_{B^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{(\bar{m},n)}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A}/2}}\sum_{m,n}&\Big(-\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}-\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(m,n)}}}-\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,1)}{\chi_{(\bar{m},n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}+\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(1,-1)}{\chi_{(\bar{m},n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\Big)\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{(m,n)}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
=\frac{1}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}\sum_{m,n}&\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\chi_+}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{(m,n)}_{B^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

(47)

Finally the overlap between
\ket{\psi}
and
\ket{\chi}
is given by

\begin{split}\innerproduct{\psi}{\chi}&=\frac{1}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(-1)^{mn+m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m',n')}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{+}{\chi_{m}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{+}}{(m,n)}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\chi_+}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{(m,n)}_{B^{\prime}}}.\\
\end{split}

(48)

The absolute value of
\innerproduct{\psi}{\chi}
can thus be bounded by

\begin{split}\absolutevalue{\braket{\psi}{\chi}}&\leq\frac{1}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{(m',n')}}{+}_{B'}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{(m,n)}{\chi_{+}}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{ \prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{(m,n)}}{+}_{B'}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A \cap B}/2}}\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{+}{\chi_{m}}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{+}}{(m,n)}_{B'}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{(m,n)}{\chi_+}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_+}{(m,n)}_{B'}}}.\end{split}

(49)

We then try to bound each line separately. For the first line, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{(m',n')}}{+}_{B'}}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{2\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{+}{\chi_{(m,n)}}_{A'}}}^{2}}\sqrt{2\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{(m',n')}}{+}_{B'}}}^{2}}\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_{(m',n')}}{\chi_{(m',n')}}_{A^{\prime}}}}\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{(m',n')}}{\psi_{(m',n')}}_{B'}}}}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{(1-\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{B}/2}})(1-\frac{1}{2^{\absolutevalue{A}/2}})}}\\
&\leq 4,\end{split}

(50)

where we used the norm constraints (
43
) to get the third inequality, and assumed that
A
and
B
are large enough to get the last inequality. The second line can be bounded as

\begin{split}&\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{(m,n)}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{+}_{B^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{(m,n)}{\chi_{+}}_{A'}}}^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{(m,n)}}{+}_{B'}}}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_{+}}{\chi_{+}}_{A^{\prime}}}}\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_{(m,n)}}{\psi_{(m,n)}}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1-\frac{4}{2^{|B|/2}})(1-\frac{1}{2^{|A|/2}})}}\\
&\leq 2.\end{split}

(51)

Similarly, we have

\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{+}{\chi_{m}}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_{+}}{(m,n)}_{B'}}}\leq 2.

(52)

Finally, the last line reads

\begin{split}&\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{(m,n)}{\chi_+}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_+}{(m,n)}_{B'}}}\\
&\leq\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{(m,n)}{\chi_+}_{A'}}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_+}{(m',n')}_{B'}}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{2\sum_{m,n}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{A'}{\braket{(m,n)}{\chi_+}_{A'}}}^{2}}\sqrt{2\sum_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\absolutevalue{\prescript{}{B'}{\braket{\psi_+}{(m',n')}_{B'}}}^{2}}\\
&\leq 2\sqrt{\prescript{}{A^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\chi_+}{\chi_+}_{A^{\prime}}}}\sqrt{\prescript{}{B^{\prime}}{\innerproduct{\psi_+}{\psi_+}_{B^{\prime}}}}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{(1-\frac{4}{2^{|B|/2}})(1-\frac{4}{2^{|A|/2}})}}\\
&\leq 4.\end{split}

(53)

Combining those inequalities, we have

\absolutevalue{\braket{\psi}{\chi}}\leq\frac{8}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}+\frac{4}{2^{|A\cap B|/2}},

(54)

for all sufficiently large
A
and
B
. Given the definition of
\delta(A,B)
in (
39
), it follows that

\delta(A,B)\leq\frac{8}{2^{(\absolutevalue{A}+\absolutevalue{B})/2}}+\frac{4}{2^{|A\cap B|/2}}.

(55)

B.4
Proof of local indistinguishability

Here we prove that the two states
\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z}_{\pm}}\left(\ket{+}\pm\ket{\text{cluster}}\right)
are locally indistinguishable in the thermodynamic limit.

First, we fix the normalization constant
Z_{\pm}
:

\begin{split}Z_{\pm}&=\left(\bra{+}\pm\bra{\text{cluster}}\right)\left(\ket{+}\pm\ket{\text{cluster}}\right)\\
&=2\pm\bra{+}\ket{\text{cluster}}\pm\bra{\text{cluster}}\ket{+}\end{split}

(56)

With
\bra{+}\ket{\text{cluster}}=\bra{\text{cluster}}\ket{+}=2^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}
, we find
Z_{\pm}=2\pm 2^{-\frac{N}{2}+2}
, which becomes
Z_{\pm}=2
in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
.

We proceed to compute the reduced density matrix on an interval
A
by tracing out its complement
\overline{A}
:

\rho_{A,\pm}=\frac{1}{Z_{\pm}}\ket{+}\bra{+}_{A}+\frac{1}{Z_{\pm}}\tr_{\overline{A}}\left(\ket{\text{cluster} }\bra{ \text{cluster} }\right)\pm\frac{1}{Z_{\pm}}\tr_{\overline{A}}\left(\ket{+}\bra{\text{cluster}}\right)\pm\frac{1}{Z_{\pm}}\left(\tr_{\overline{A}}\ket{
\text{cluster}}\bra{+}\right)

(57)

For the third term:

\tr_{\overline{A}}\left(\ket{+}\bra{\text{cluster}}\right)=\tr_{\overline{A}}\left(\ket{+}\bra{+}_{A}\otimes\ket{+}\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,A}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\right),

(58)

where
U_{CZ,A},U_{CZ,\overline{A}},U_{CZ,\partial}
denotes the product of controlled-Z acting only in
A,\overline{A}
, and the bipartition boundary. To proceed:

\begin{split}\tr_{\overline{A}}\left(\ket{+}\bra{\text{cluster}}\right)&=\ket{+}\bra{+}_{A}U_{CZ,A}\tr_{\overline{A}}\left(\ket{+}\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\right)\\
&=\ket{+}\bra{+}_{A}U_{CZ,A}\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}},\end{split}

(59)

and we will show that this quantity decays exponentially with the size of
\overline{A}
.

To start, we label the qubits in the interval
\overline{A}
by the index
i=1,\cdots,n
, where
n
is the total number of qubits in
\overline{A}
. Then
\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}}
can be computed in the computational basis (
\sigma_{i}=0,1
):

\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}}=2^{-n}\sum_{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{N}}(-1)^{\sigma_{0}\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}+\cdots+\sigma_{N}\sigma_{N+1}},

(60)

where
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{N+1}
denotes the two
qubits (in the computational basis) in
A
on the two end-points of
\overline{A}
. There are two cases corresponding to even
n
or odd
n
.

(1) Even n: in this case, when summing over the odd spins in
\overline{A}
, i.e.
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3},\cdots,\sigma_{n-1}
, we have

\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}}=2^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{\sigma_{2},\sigma_{4}\cdots,\sigma_{N}}\delta(\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{4}=\cdots=\sigma_{N})(-1)^{\sigma_{N}\sigma_{N+1}}=2^{-\frac{N}{2}}(-1)^{\sigma_{0}\sigma_{N+1}}

(61)

In terms of operators, one finds

\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}}=2^{-\frac{N}{2}}CZ_{0,N+1}

(62)

which decays exponentially in the size of the
\overline{A}
.

(2) Odd n: in this case, when summing over the odd spins in
\overline{A}
, i.e.
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3},\cdots,\sigma_{n-1}
, we have

\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}}=2^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\sum_{\sigma_{2},\sigma_{4}\cdots,\sigma_{n-1}}\delta(\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{4}=\cdots=\sigma_{n+1})=2^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\delta(\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{n+1}).

(63)

In terms of operators, one finds

\bra{+}_{\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\overline{A}}U_{CZ,\partial}\ket{+}_{\overline{A}}=2^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{1+Z_{0}Z_{n+1}}{2}

(64)

which decays exponentially in the size of the
\overline{A}
.

In either case 1 or 2, the quantity decays exponentially, and therefore, one finds the local indistinguishability when the subregion
A
is of constant size.

\boxed{\norm{\rho_{A,+} - \rho_{A,-}}_{1}\leq O(e^{-\alpha N})}

(65)

with
\alpha
being a
O(1)
constant.

B.5
Calculation of order parameters

Define the local operator
O_{i}=X_{i}(1-Z_{i-1}Z_{i+1})
charged under
U_{\text{CZ}}
, we compute the two-point function w.r.t.
\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{ + }^{\otimes N}+\ket{\text{cluster}})
:

\begin{split}\expectationvalue{O_i O_j }=&\frac{1}{2}[\bra{+}^{\otimes N}O_{i}O_{j}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\bra{\text{cluster}}O_{i}O_{j}\ket{\text{cluster}}\\
&+\bra{+}^{\otimes N}O_{i}O_{j}\ket{\text{cluster}}+\bra{\text{cluster}}O_{i}O_{j}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}]\end{split}

(66)

We have
\bra{+}^{\otimes N}O_{i}O_{j}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}=\bra{\text{trivial}}(1-Z_{i-1}Z_{i+1})(1-Z_{j-1}Z_{j+1})\ket{+}^{\otimes N}=1
for
i\neq j
. Similarly,
\bra{\text{cluster}}O_{i}O_{j}\ket{\text{cluster}}=1
for
i\neq j
. In the thermodynamic limit, the last two terms vanish, and thus
\expectationvalue{O_iO_j}=1
for any
i\neq j
.

B.6
Calculation of disorder parameters

Here we consider the disorder operator
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}
and calculate its expectation value w.r.t.
\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z}}\left(\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{\text{cluster}}\right)
:

\begin{split}\expectationvalue{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \text{CZ}_{i,i+1} }=&\frac{1}{Z}[\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\bra{\text{cluster}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{\text{cluster}}\\
&+\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{\text{cluster}}+\bra{\text{cluster}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}]\\
=&\frac{2}{Z}\left[\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{\text{cluster}}\right]\end{split}

(67)

Calculation of
\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}
:

This quantity can be calculated as

\bra{+}^{\otimes n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=2^{-n}\sum_{\{\sigma_{i}|i=1,2,3,\cdots,n\}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}}

(68)

with each
\sigma_{i}=0,1
.

•

Even
n
:

Summing over
\sigma_{i}
with
i=1,3,5,\cdots,n-1
gives

\bra{+}^{\otimes n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=2^{-n}2^{\frac{n}{2}}\sum_{\{\sigma_{i}|i=2,4,6,\cdots,n\}}\delta(0=\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{4}=\cdots=\sigma_{n})=2^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

(69)

•

Odd
n
:

Summing over
\sigma_{i}
with
i=1,3,5,\cdots,n
gives

\bra{+}^{\otimes n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=2^{-n}2^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\sum_{\{\sigma_{i}|i=2,4,6,\cdots,n-1\}}\delta(0=\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{4}=\cdots=\sigma_{n}-1)=2^{\frac{-n+1}{2}}.

(70)

Calculation of
\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{\text{cluster}}
:

Since
\ket{\text{cluster}}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}
, one has

\begin{split}\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{\text{cluster}}&=\bra{+}^{\otimes N}\prod_{i=n}^{N}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}\\
&=\bra{+}^{\otimes N-n+2}\prod_{i=n}^{N}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N-n+2}\\
&=\begin{cases}2^{-\frac{N-n}{2}-1}\quad\text{for even n}\\
2^{-\frac{N-n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{for odd n}\end{cases}\end{split}

(71)

Combining the results above, one finds

\expectationvalue{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \text{CZ}_{i,i+1} }=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{Z}\left[2^{-\frac{n}{2}}+2^{-\frac{N-n}{2}-1}\right]\quad~~~\text{for even n}\\
\frac{2}{Z}\left[2^{\frac{-n+1}{2}}+2^{-\frac{N-n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\right]\quad\text{for odd n}\end{cases}

(72)

Therefore, if we first take the thermodynamic limit (
N\to\infty
), the expectation value of the disorder becomes

\expectationvalue{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \text{CZ}_{i,i+1} }=\begin{cases}2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\quad~~~\text{for even n}\\
2^{\frac{-n+1}{2}}\quad\text{for odd n}\end{cases}

(73)

which decays exponentially in the size of the disorder operator.

B.8
Stability of
c=1
CFT from bosonization

In this section, we discuss the field theory describing the long-wavelength limit of the Hamiltonian in Eq.(
16
) at
g=-1
, which takes the form

H_{0}=\sum_{i}-Z_{i-1}X_{i}Z_{i+1}-X_{i}.

(75)

As has been pointed out in
[
32
,
78
,
79
]
,
H_{0}
at long-wavelength is described by a compact boson CFT at radius
r=1/2
(or equivalently Luttinger parameter
K=1/4
). If we denote the compact boson field as
\phi(x)
and its dual field as
\theta(x)
, the theory has a
U(1)
momentum symmetry generated by
\partial_{x}\theta
. One can identify the
U(1)
momentum symmetry in the IR to a
U(1)
symmetry of
H_{0}
whose generator is given by
G_{U(1)}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}
. Notice that since
e^{-i\pi G_{U(1)}}=\prod_{i}\mathrm{CZ}_{i,i+1}
, so the non-onsite CZ symmetry generates the transformation
\phi\rightarrow\phi+\pi,\theta\rightarrow\theta
in the IR. The other two onsite symmetries
U_{0}=\prod_{i}X_{2i+1}
and
U_{e}=\prod_{i}X_{2i}
generate the transformation
\phi\xrightarrow{}-\phi,\theta\xrightarrow{}-\theta
and
\phi\xrightarrow{}-\phi,\theta\xrightarrow{}\pi-\theta
respectively in the IR.

However, as we perturb away from
g=-1
,
G_{U(1)}
no longer commutes with
H
but
e^{-i\pi G_{U(1)}}
remains a symmetry of
H
. Here we present a field-theoretic argument about the stability of the
c=1
CFT phase around
g=-1
, which is the same as saying there can still be a
U(1)
symmetry emergent in the IR. First, when the model is slightly perturbed away from
g=-1
, the Hamiltonian takes the form:

\begin{split}\sum_{i}-Z_{i}X_{i+1}Z_{i+2}-X_{i+1}-\lambda Z_{i}X_{i+1}Z_{i+2}X_{i+3}-\lambda X_{i}Z_{i+1}X_{i+2}Z_{i+3},\end{split}

(76)

where
\lambda
controls the perturbation strength. Using a KT transformation
[
80
,
23
]
, defined by the following mapping of local operators:

\begin{split}Z_{i-1}X_{i}Z_{i+1}&\to Z_{i-1}Z_{i+1}\\
X_{i}&\to X_{i}\\
\end{split}

(77)

The above Hamiltonian can be mapped to

\begin{split}H(\lambda)&=\sum_{i}-Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2}-X_{2i}-Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+3}-X_{2i+1}+\lambda Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2}X_{2i+3}+\lambda X_{2i}Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+3}\\
&+\lambda Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+3}X_{2i+4}+\lambda X_{2i+1}Z_{2i+2}Z_{2i+4},\end{split}

(78)

which is described by two decoupled critical Ising chains (living on even and odd sublattices) perturbed by three-body interactions. The two decoupled Ising CFTs can be bosonized into a compact boson
[
24
]
, where we can identify the following mapping from Ising local operators to compact boson operators (to the leading order)

\begin{split}&Z_{2i}Z_{2i+1}\sim\sigma(x,\tau)\tilde{\sigma}(x,\tau)\sim\cos(\phi(x,\tau))\\
&X_{2i}X_{2i+1}\sim Z_{2i}Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+2}Z_{2i+3}\sim\mathcal{E}(x,\tau)+\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(x,\tau)\sim\cos(2\phi(x, \tau)),\end{split}

(79)

where the spin operator
\sigma(x,\tau)
has scaling dimension
\frac{1}{8}
and the energy density operator
\mathcal{E}
has scaling dimension
1
in Ising CFT.

If we further apply a Krammers-Wannier transformation to the odd sublattice, we obtain the following Hamiltonian

\begin{split}H(\lambda)&=\sum_{i}-Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2}-X_{2i}-Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+3}-X_{2i+1}+\lambda Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2}Z_{2i+3}Z_{2i+5}+\lambda X_{2i}X_{2i+3}\\
&+\lambda X_{2i+3}X_{2i+4}+\lambda Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+2}Z_{2i+3}Z_{2i+4}\end{split}

(80)

The perturbations
X_{2i+3}X_{2i+4}+Z_{2i+1}Z_{2i+2}Z_{2i+3}Z_{2i+4}
generates a marginal perturbation
\cos(2\phi(x, \tau))
that varies the boson compactification radius continuously
[
81
]
.
The perturbations
X_{2i}X_{2i+3}+Z_{2i}Z_{2i+2}Z_{2i+3}Z_{2i+5}
, which differ the formers by only a lattice translation on some operators, would at most generate the marginal perturbations
\cos(2\phi(x,\tau))
and the rest would be of higher-order irrelevant terms. By this perturbative argument on the scaling dimensions of the perturbation, we therefore expect a finite range of the
c=1
CFT phase around
g=-1
, which is also consistent with our numerical calculation of the central charge. A detailed renormalization group study of this marginal perturbation is left for future work.

Remarks on the critical exponents
: here we present some additional remarks on the critical exponents of the correlation:
\expectationvalue{Z_0 Z_x}
and
\expectationvalue{O_0 O_x}
(for even
x
). At
g=-1
, using the KT transformation, these two correlations can be mapped to
\expectationvalue{Z_0 X_1 X_3...X_{x-1} Z_{x}}
and

with respect to the ground state of two decoupled critical Ising chains on the two sublattices. We then apply the following lattice to continuum correspondence for the primary fields

\begin{split}Z_{x}\sim\sigma(x),~~~Z_{x}Z_{x+2}\sim 1+\mathcal{E}(x),~~~X_{x}\sim 1-\mathcal{E}(x),~~~X_{0}X_{2}X_{4}...X_{x}\sim\mu(x),\end{split}

(81)

where the same mapping also applies to lattice operators on the odd sites and we use
\widetilde{\sigma}
,
\widetilde{\mu}
, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
to denote their corresponding Ising primary fields. Then, the two correlation functions can be simplified to correlation functions of Ising primary fields

\begin{split}&\expectationvalue{Z_0 X_1 X_3...X_{x-1} Z_{x}}=\expectationvalue{Z_0 Z_x}\expectationvalue{X_1 X_3...X_{x-1}}\sim\expectationvalue{\sigma(0) \sigma(x)}\expectationvalue{\widetilde{\mu}(0) \widetilde{\mu}(x)}\sim\frac{1}{x^{1/2}}\\
&\expectationvalue{\left(X_0 - Z_{N-1}Z_1 \right)\left(X_x - Z_{x-1}Z_{x+1} \right)}\sim\expectationvalue{\mathcal{E}(0)\mathcal{E}(x)}+\expectationvalue{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(0)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(x)}\sim\frac{1}{x^{2}}.\end{split}

(82)

The above two critical exponents
\frac{1}{2},2
are close to the exponents
0.481
and
2.108
obtained from our numerics, see Fig.
4
(e)(f). As we turn on the perturbation
\lambda
, numerically we see the critical components are continuously varying. This agrees with our previous argument that the perturbation is exactly marginal and would modify the compactification radius of the
c=1
CFT.

We also want to conjecture that the transition at
g\approx
0.4 between the critical phase and the non-onsite symmetry-breaking phase is the
SU(2)_{1}
CFT, which is the simplest CFT with a
Z_{2}^{3}
type anomaly. For such a theory, the only non-trivial primary operator has a scaling dimension
\frac{1}{2}
, which is consistent with our numerical result that both the
\left\langle Z_{0}Z_{x}\right\rangle
and

. We left the detailed analysis of this critical point to future work.

B.9
State preparation via measurement and feedback

Here we provide a measurement-feedback protocol (i.e. adaptive circuit) to prepare the state
\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{\text{cluster}}
with probability
\frac{1}{2}
in the thermodynamic limit.

Figure 5
:
Measurement-based state-preparation protocol:
(a) We first add a chain of ancilla qubits in the GHZ state, which can be prepared in constant depth with measurement and feedback. (b) A single CCZ gate (
|0\rangle_{a}\langle 0|_{a}+|1\rangle_{a}\langle 1|_{a}\text{CZ}_{12}
) acts on two physical qubits and one ancilla qubit in between. (c) After extensively applying CCZ gates, performing single-site X measurements on all ancilla qubits produces
\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{\text{cluster}}
with probability
\frac{1}{2}
in the thermodynamic limit.

To start, we initialize the
N
physical qubits in the
\ket{+}
state. On each bond connecting two physical qubits, we place an ancilla qubit in
\ket{+}
and transform these
N
ancilla qubits into a GHZ state via a constant-depth adaptive circuit (the generation of the GHZ state can achieved by measuring a cluster state followed by unitary feedback
[
4
]
, or just measuring all the neighboring two-qubit
ZZ
Pauli operators followed by unitary feedback). It follows that the physical and ancilla qubits are in the state
\ket{000...}_{a}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{111...}_{a}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}
. Now we apply a layer of unitary gates, each of which is a CCZ gate acting on the two neighboring physical qubits and one ancilla qubit in between. This gives state
\ket{000...}_{a}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{111...}_{a}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}=\ket{000...}_{a}\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{111...}_{a}\ket{\text{cluster}}
.

Now we perform the single-qubit
X
measurement on all the ancilla qubits. Denoting the measurement outcome by
\{x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\}
, a corresponding pure-state trajectory is
\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\ket{\text{cluster}}
. As such, the sign is solely given by global parity of the measurement outcome
\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
, and in the thermodynamic limit, the probability having
\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=1,-1
is
\frac{1}{2}
. As a result, the state (
\ket{+}^{\otimes N}+\ket{\text{cluster}}
) can be prepared in constant depth with a constant probability
\frac{1}{2}
in the thermodynamic limit. Below we make two remarks:

(1) While the above protocol produces
\ket{\psi_{\pm}}\propto\ket{+}\pm\ket{\text{cluster}}
where the sign
\pm
determined from the measurement outcome is probabilistic, the reduced matrix on a local region is, in fact, deterministically prepared since
\ket{\psi_{\pm}}
is locally indistinguishable in thermodynamic limit. Therefore, post-selection is not needed for experimentally measuring the local order operators.

(2) The protocol can be generalized to prepare the superposition of any
O(1)
number of short-range entangled pure states (i.e. the states that can be prepared by constant-depth local unitary circuits) with
O(1)
success probability. For instance, we can prepare the superposition of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric product state (i.e.
\ket{+}^{\otimes N}
) and the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Levin-Gu SPT state
[
32
]
. Such a state can further be deterministically converted to a superposition of the (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topologically ordered) toric code state and the (twisted
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topologically ordered) double semion state by a Kramer-Wannier duality, which can be implemented by measurement and feedback in constant depth
[
29
]
.

2d SPT soup

C.1
Derivation of the SPT soup wavefunction

Here we show that implementing measurements (with post-selecting the measurement outcomes) on a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
SPT gives rise to the SPT soup wavefunction defined in the main text.

Following Ref.
[
14
]
, we consider a triangular lattice, with each vertex accommodating a qubit. The fixed-point
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
SPT is given by
\ket{\psi_{Z_{2}^3}}\equiv\prod_{\triangle}\mathrm{CCZ}_{\triangle}\ket{+}
, where
\mathrm{CCZ}
denotes the controlled-controlled-Z gate and the product is taken over all small triangles on the lattice. Since the triangular lattice is 3-colorable, one can define the sublattices
A,B,C
so that all neighboring qubits belong to different sublattices. It follows that
\ket{\psi_{Z_{2}^3}}
respects the following three global (0-form)
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetries acting on the three sublattices:

U_{A}=\prod_{i\in A}X_{i}~~~U_{B}=\prod_{i\in B}X_{i}~~~U_{C}=\prod_{i\in C}X_{C}.

(83)

The structure of the
Z_{2}^{3}
SPT wavefunction can be understood from the decorated domain wall picture
[
82
]
, which we review below. Consider a small patch on the triangular lattice involving four sites:

(84)

where the sites
a
and
a^{\prime}
belong to the
A
sublattice;
b
and
c
belong to
B
and
C
sublattices respectively. All four sites are initiated in the
\ket{+}
state and there are CCZ gates applied to
(a,b,c)
and
(a^{\prime},b,c)
. The effect of the CCZ gates on
b
and
c
can be seen by expanding
a
and
a^{\prime}
in the
Z
basis

\begin{split}\mathrm{CCZ}_{(abc)}\mathrm{CCZ}_{(a^{\prime}bc)}&\ket{Z_a = s}\ket{Z_{a'} = s'}=\left[\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}+(1-\delta_{s,s^{\prime}})\mathrm{CZ}_{(bc)}\right]\ket{Z_a = s}\ket{Z_{a'} = s'}.\end{split}

(85)

This shows that if there is a domain wall between
a
and
a^{\prime}
(i.e.
s\neq s^{\prime}
), then one applies a CZ gate on that domain wall, which is the edge connecting
b
and
c
. With this four-qubit example, one can now understand the structure of the
Z_{2}^{3}
SPT wavefunction and write it as

\ket{\psi_{Z_{2}^3}}\propto\sum_{s}\ket{s}_{A}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ i \in B, j \in C }\in\mathcal{C}}\text{CZ}_{ij}\ket{+}_{BC},

(86)

where
\mathcal{C}
denotes the loops (on the BC edges) that correspond to the domain-wall configurations of
s
. Namely, the
Z_{2}^{3}
SPT can be understood as a condensate of A domain walls, where each domain wall is decorated by a 1d cluster state living on the sublattices
B
and
C
.

Based on this structure, one can immediately see that by measuring the Pauli
Z
on all qubits on the
A
sublattice with an outcome
s
, which also fixes the domain-wall pattern
C(s)
, the post-measurement state on
B,C
reads

\begin{split}{}_{A}\bra{s}\ket{\psi_{Z_{2}^3}}\propto\prod_{\expectationvalue{ i \in B, j \in C }\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\text{CZ}_{ij}\ket{+}_{BC}=\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C}(s)}\ket{+}_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}(s)}.\end{split}

(87)

where
\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C}(s)}
is the 1d cluster-state SPT defined on
B,C
along the domain wall
\mathcal{C}(s)
, and all the other qubits not on the domain wall, i.e.
\overline{\mathcal{C}}(s)
, are in the Pauli-X product state
\ket{+}
.

This allows us to easily derive the state obtained by Pauli-X measurements on the sublattice
A
. Specifically, the single-qubit Pauli-X measurement on the sublattice
A
(with the fixed measurement outcomes
X_{i}=1
) projects all the qubits on
A
into
\ket{+}_{A}
, and the corresponding post-measurement pure-state on
B,C
is:

~{}_{A}\bra{+}\ket{\psi_{Z_{2}^3}}\propto\sum_{s}~{}_{A}\bra{s}\ket{\psi_{Z_{2}^3}}\propto\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\ket{\mathrm{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C}}\ket{+}_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}

(88)

where we first expand
{}_{A}\bra{+}
in the Z basis, and then use Eq.
87
. This is exactly the SPT soup discussed in the main text.

C.2
Levin-Gu SPT under measurements

With a similar strategy, one can also derive the post-measurement wavefunction obtained by measuring Pauli-X on one sublattice for the Levin-Gu SPT
[
32
]
(also known as the
Z_{2}
SPT). On a triangular lattice, the Levin-Gu SPT is defined as
\ket{\psi_{Z_2}}=U_{\mathrm{CCZ}}U_{\mathrm{CZ}}U_{\mathrm{Z}}\ket{+}

[
83
]
, where
{\mathrm{CCZ}}
denotes CCZ gates applied to every triangle,
U_{\mathrm{CZ}}
denotes CZ gates applied to every edge, and
U_{\mathrm{Z}}
denotes Pauli-Zs applied to every site. Again, we first divide the triangular lattice into the sublattices
A,B,C
, and perform Pauli-X measurements on every qubit on the sublattice
A
. The post-measurement state on
B,C
reads

\ket{\phi}\propto_{A}\bra{+}\ket{\psi_{Z_2}}\propto\sum_{s}\prescript{}{A}{\bra{s'}}\prod_{\triangle}\mathrm{CCZ}_{\triangle}\prod_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i}Z_{i}\ket{+}

(89)

To proceed, we first note that

{}_{A}\bra{s}\prod_{\triangle}\mathrm{CCZ}_{\triangle}=~_{A}\bra{s}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij},

(90)

where
\mathcal{C}(s)
denotes the domain wall of the configuration
s
on
A
sublattice. On the other hand,

\begin{split}{}_{A}\bra{s}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}&=\left[\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in B\cup C}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\right]~_{A}\bra{s}\prod_{\expectationvalue{i \in A, j \in B }}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{\expectationvalue{i \in A, j \in C }}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\\
&=\left[\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in B\cup C}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\right]\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}~{}_{A}\bra{s},\end{split}

(91)

where
\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}
denotes the product of three
s
on the three sites (belonging to
A
) that are the nearest neighbors of the site
i
(belonging to either
B
or
C
). Combining the above results and using
{}_{A}\bra{s}\prod_{i\in A}Z_{i}=_{A}\bra{s}\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}
, one finds

\ket{\phi}\propto\left[\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in B\cup C}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\right]\left[\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}\right]\sum_{s}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}\ket{+}_{BC}

(92)

This can be further simplified by noticing that
s
and
\overline{s}\equiv\{-s_{i},\forall i\}
have the same domain wall configuration
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.
\mathcal{C}(s)=\mathcal{C}(\overline{s})
. This allows us to rewrite the summation over
s
as the summation over
\mathcal{C}
:

\begin{split}&\sum_{s}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}\\
&=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}+\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}\overline{s}_{j}}{2}}\prod_{i\in A}\overline{s}_{i}\right]\end{split}

(93)

We consider the case with an even number of sites on the sublattice
A
, so
\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}=\prod_{i\in A}\overline{s}_{i}
. Also,
Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}\overline{s}_{j}}{2}}=Z_{i}~Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}
. Therefore, Eq.
93
can be simplified as

\left(1+\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}\right)\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s)}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}.

(94)

Finally, the post-measurement state can be written as

\ket{\phi}\propto\left[\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in B\cup C}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\right]\left(1+\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}\right)\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in\mathcal{C}(s^{\prime})}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}^{\frac{1-\prod_{j\in\text{nn}(i),j\in A}s_{j}}{2}}\prod_{i\in A}s_{i}\ket{+}_{BC}

(95)

This can be further simplified as

\begin{split}\ket{\phi}\propto\left[\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in B\cup C}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\right]\left(1+\prod_{i\in B\cup C}Z_{i}\right)\prod_{p}\left(1+\widetilde{U}_{p}\right)\ket{+}_{BC},\end{split}

(96)

where
\widetilde{U}_{p}
is an operator acting on the 6 qubits on the vertices of a plaquette
p
on the
B\cup C

\widetilde{U}_{p}\equiv\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}\in p}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}\prod_{i\in p}\left[(-1)^{\frac{1}{6}}Z_{i}\right]

(97)

This shows that
\ket{\phi}
respects the 1-form non-onsite symmetry generated by
\widetilde{U}_{p}
, namely
\widetilde{U}_{p}\ket{\phi}=\ket{\phi}
. Taking the product of

These two symmetries carry a mixed anomaly, which can be shown by the algebra between their truncated symmetry actions. Specifically, we denote a truncated 0-form symmetry operator as
U_{\text{trunc}}
and a truncated 1-form symmetry operator as
W_{\text{trunc}}
. They create a loop-like defect (green circle) and two point-like defects (red dots) in the lattice below:

We assume the two end points of the truncated string operator,
v_{a}
and
v_{b}
are far from each other, and both of them are far from the green circle. The braiding between the loop-like defect and the two point-like defects gives rise to

\displaystyle U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\Big(\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}}X_{v}\Big)\Big(\prod_{i=1}(-1)^{\frac{1}{6}}Z_{v_{i}}CZ_{v_{i},v_{i+1}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{v\in\mathcal{R}}X_{v}\Big)^{\dagger}\Big(\prod_{i=1}(-1)^{\frac{1}{6}}Z_{v_{i}}CZ_{v_{i},v_{i+1}}\Big)^{\dagger}

(98)

\displaystyle=Z_{v_{a}}Z_{v_{b}},

up to a minus sign. Thus the operator
Z_{v_{a}}
near endpoint
v_{a}
can be seen as the braiding between a loop-like defect and one point-like defect. Furthermore, this operator and a loop-like defect have a nontrivial braiding phase,

U_{\text{trunc}}Z_{v_{a}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}Z_{v_{a}}=-1.

(99)

There is a phase ambiguity in defining the local braiding operator near
v_{a}
, which will be discussed later. This nontrivial topological data between the symmetry defects indicates the mixed anomaly between the two symmetries
[
84
]
. This mixed anomaly is analogous to the mixed anomaly between two 0-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetries in 1d discussed in Ref.
[
85

]
, and the above braiding phase is generalized from the 3-cocycle
\omega(g,h,k)
defined in Ref.
[
85
]
. We therefore expect
\ket{\phi}
to be long-range entangled. A simple argument can be made as follows.

Suppose the state
\ket{\phi}
is a short-ranged entangled symmetric state, then
W_{\text{trunc}}\ket{\phi}=\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}\Sigma^{\prime}_{b}\ket{\phi}
, where
\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}
is a local unitary operator near
v_{a}
, and
\Sigma^{\prime}_{b}
is a local unitary near
v_{b}
. We redefine the truncation of the 1-form symmetry as

\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}=\Sigma_{a}W_{\text{trunc}},

(100)

such that
\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}\ket{\phi}=\Sigma^{\prime}_{b}\ket{\phi}
. With the modified truncation, the braiding between the two point-like defects and a loop-like defect is given by

\displaystyle U_{\text{trunc}}\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}=U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}=B_{a}B_{b},

(101)

where
B_{a}
is a local unitary near
v_{a}
, and
B_{b}
is a local unitary near
v_{b}
. Now we show the braiding between
B_{a}
and the loop-like defect still gives a nontrivial phase,

\displaystyle U_{\text{trunc}}B_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}B_{a}

(102)

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}\right)_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}\right)_{a}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}

\displaystyle=-\Sigma_{a}\Sigma^{\dagger}_{a}=-1.

In the above equation, we use
(\cdots)_{a}
to denote the part of the operator
(\cdots)
that is near
v_{a}
. From the second line to the third line, we took
\Sigma_{a}^{\dagger}
out of the bracket
(\cdots)_{a}
because it is already a local unitary near
v_{a}
. From the third line to the fourth line, we took
U_{\text{trunc}}\Sigma_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}
outside of the bracket, since it is a local unitary near
v_{a}
. From the fourth line to the fifth line, we used
U_{\text{trunc}}^{2}=1
. From the fifth line to the sixth line, we used Eq.
98
and Eq.
99
.

On the other hand, we recall that
\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}\ket{\phi}=\Sigma^{\prime}_{b}\ket{\phi}
, and
U_{\text{trunc}}\ket{\phi}=\mathcal{L}_{\partial\mathcal{R}}\ket{\phi}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\partial\mathcal{R}}
is a unitary near the boundary of region
\mathcal{R}
. When
v_{a}
and
v_{b}
are far from each other and are both far away from
\partial\mathcal{R}
, the two operators commute up to some local unitaries near
v_{a}
and
v_{b}

U_{\text{trunc}}\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}=B_{a}B_{b}.

(103)

Importantly, since both
U_{\text{trunc}}
and
\tilde{W}_{\text{trunc}}
locally stabilize state
\ket{\phi}
, the local unitary
B_{a}
also stabilizes
\ket{\phi}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle U_{\text{trunc}}B_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}B_{a}\ket{\phi}

(104)

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}B_{a}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}B_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\partial\mathcal{R}}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}\mathcal{L}_{\partial\mathcal{R}}B_{a}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}\mathcal{L}_{\partial\mathcal{R}}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\ket{\phi}

\displaystyle=\ket{\phi}.

From the first line to the second line, we used the fact that
B_{a}
stabilizes the state. From the third line to the fourth line, we used that
B_{a}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\partial\mathcal{R}}
commute, since they do not overlap. This equation directly contradicts the result in Eq.

.

We note that the definition of
B_{a}
above is ambiguous up to a phase, i.e., we can always assign an extra phase

to

to

becomes trivial. Nonetheless, we can define a different braiding operator as
C_{a}=(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger})_{a}
, and the following phases

\displaystyle C_{a}W_{\text{trunc}}B_{a}

\displaystyle W_{\text{trunc}}

(105)

\displaystyle=\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}W_{\text{trunc}}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}W_{\text{trunc}},

\displaystyle U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}B_{a}C_{a}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\left(U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}W_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}\left(W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}W_{\text{trunc}}U_{\text{trunc}}^{\dagger}\right)_{a}.

C.3
Wavefunction Overlap

Here we calculate the overlap between
\ket{\psi}
and
W_{\Gamma_{x}}\ket{\psi}
. By mapping to the Ashkin-Teller model on a triangular lattice, we argue the overlap decays exponentially small in the system size. To start,

\frac{\bra{\psi}W_{\Gamma_{x}}\ket{\psi}}{\bra{\psi}\ket{\psi}}=\frac{\bra{+}W_{\Gamma_{x}}\prod_{p}(1+U_{p})\ket{+}}{\bra{+}\prod_{p}(1+U_{p})\ket{+}}=\frac{\sum_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}\bra{+}\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}}{\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\bra{+}\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C}}},

(106)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a contractible loop, and
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}
is non-contractible along
\hat{x}
direction. The overlap between a
L
-qubit
\ket{+}
state and a
L
-qubit
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SPT cluster state (with
L
being even) is
2^{-\frac{L}{2}+1}
, and therefore
\bra{+}\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C}}=2^{-\frac{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{C}}}{2}}2^{N_{\mathcal{C}}}
, where
N_{\mathcal{C}}
is the number of disconnected loops in
\mathcal{C}
.

To compute
\bra{+}\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}
, we consider the system sizes in the two spatial
directions to be even by even, in which case,
the non-contractible loop
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
must have even number of qubits and
\bra{+}\ket{\text{SPT}}_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}=2^{-\frac{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{C'}}}{2}}2^{N_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}}
. As a result, one finds

\frac{\bra{\psi}W_{\Gamma_{x}}\ket{\psi}}{\bra{\psi}\ket{\psi}}=\frac{\sum_{\mathcal{C^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{C'} }}2^{N_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}}}{\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{\absolutevalue{\mathcal{C} }}2^{N_{\mathcal{C}}}}.

(107)

This is the ratio of the two
partition functions of the
O(2)
loop models, one with only contractible loops and one with only non-contractible loops.

It is known that the
O(n)
loop model on the honeycomb lattice can be exactly mapped to the
n
-color Ashkin-Teller (AT) model at the infinite-coupling limit on a triangular lattice
[
88
,
89
]
. Here we review the mapping for
n=2
, and based on which, show that the overlap in Eq.
107
relates to the free energy cost of inserting a line defect in the two-color AT model at a critical point, therefore indicating the exponential decay of the wavefunction overlap.

Consider a triangular lattice where each lattice site has two flavors of Ising spins, the partition function of the corresponding AT model reads

Z_{\text{AT}}=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}e^{-H_{\text{AT}}}\qquad\text{with }H_{\text{AT}}=-J_{2}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+\tau_{i}\tau_{j})-J_{4}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\tau_{i}\tau_{j}

(108)

Below we show that at the infinite-coupling limit:
J_{2}\to\infty,J_{4}\to-\infty
with
J_{2}+J_{4}=J
fixed at a finite value, the AT model can be mapped to the
O(2)
model on the honeycomb lattice using a low-temperature expansion. On a given bond
\expectationvalue{ij}
on the triangular lattice, there are three cases:

(i)
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=1
: the corresponding Boltzmann weight is
e^{2J_{2}+J_{4}}
.

(ii) only one disagreement on the bond
\expectationvalue{ij}
, i.e.
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=1=-\tau_{i}\tau_{j}
or
-\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=1=\tau_{i}\tau_{j}
, the corresponding Boltzmann weight is

e^{-J_{4}}
.

(iii) both flavors of the spins disagree, i.e.
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=-1
, the corresponding Boltzmann weight is
e^{-2J_{2}+J_{4}}
.

If one normalizes the Boltzmann weight w.r.t the weight in the case (i), one has the normalized weights:

Case (i): 1, Case (ii):
e^{-2J_{2}-2J_{4}}\equiv e^{-2J}
, and Case (iii):
e^{-4J_{2}}
.

Therefore, by taking the limit
J_{2}\to\infty,J_{4}\to-\infty
with
J_{2}+J_{4}=J
, one only needs to consider the case (i) and (ii). The probability of having case (iii) would be zero, which implies that the domain walls of the
\sigma
spins and
\tau
spins cannot overlap, e.g. see below for allowed configurations:

Correspondingly, when considering the low-temperature expansion by flipping spins, the partition function (up to a constant) of the infinite-coupling limit Ashkin-Teller (ICLAT) model is

Z_{\text{ICLAT}}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}x^{\absolutevalue{ \mathcal{C}}}2^{N_{\mathcal{C}}},

(109)

where
\mathcal{C}
is any contractible loop, with the loop tension
x\equiv e^{-2J}
. The factor
2^{N_{\mathcal{C}}}
appears since each loop can be the domain wall of either one of the two flavors of spins. Eq.

109
is exactly the partition function of the
O(2)
loop model.

The phase diagram of the infinite-coupling AT model is studied in Ref.
[
89
]
; for
x=e^{-2J}<x_{c}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, both
\sigma,\tau
spins are in the ferromagnetic ordered phase, while for
x>x_{c}
, the spins are in the critical phase.
x_{c}
is the critical point described by a Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition.

Alternatively, the model can be written as two flavors of the Ising models subject to the constraint:

Z_{\text{ICLAT}}=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}e^{J\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+\tau_{i}\tau_{j})}\prod_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\delta_{ij}(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\neq-1~\&~\tau_{i}\tau_{j}\neq-1),

SymTFT description

The SymTFT/topological holography framework is known as an insightful way to describe quantum systems with symmetries

This is illustrated with a sandwich picture, where the bulk topological order has a gapped boundary (reference/topological boundary) on the left and another (dynamical/physical) boundary on the right. The reference boundary realizes the specific symmetry data given from the bulk topological order, whereas the dynamical boundary is given from the details of the theory, and is potentially not topological.

In the 1d model we discussed, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
symmetry given by
U_{e}=\prod_{i}X_{2i}
,
U_{o}=\prod_{i}X_{2i+1}
, and the non-onsite
U_{\text{CZ}}=\prod_{i}CZ_{i,i+1}
together form a type-III mixed-anomaly. The 2d bulk topological order for this anomalous symmetry is a twisted quantum double, which is also equivalent to a
D_{8}
(the dihedral group of order 8) quantum double
[
12
]
. The reference boundary on the left corresponds to condensing all the abelian anyons (charges) in the topological order, given by the Lagrangian algebra
1+e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{1}e_{2}+e_{1}e_{3}+e_{2}e_{3}+e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}

[
96

m_{1}
,
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
lines in the bulk topological order. The spontaneously breaking phase of one of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetries is a gapped phase, which corresponds to the choice of a gapped dynamical boundary on the right, given by Lagrangian algebra
1+e_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}+m_{23}

[
96
]
.

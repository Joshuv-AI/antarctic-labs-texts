Subgroups of Bestvina-Brady groups
S. Blumer

Abstract
. In [ 10 ] , Droms proved that all the subgroups of a right-angled Artin group (RAAG) defined by a finite simplicial graph Γ \Gamma are themselves RAAGs if, and only if, Γ \Gamma has no induced square graph nor line-graph of length 3 3 . The present work provides a similar result for specific normal subgroups of RAAGs, called Bestvina-Brady groups: We characterize those graphs in which every subgroup of such a group is itself a RAAG. In turn, we confirm several Galois theoretic conjectures for the pro- p p completions of these groups.



Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a simplicial graph, i.e., a pair
(V,E)
, where
V
is a set and
E
is a set of
2
-element subsets of
V
. The associated
right-angled Artin group

G_{\Gamma}
(RAAG, for short), or graph group, is the group generated by the vertices of the graph, with a defining relation
vw=wv
for each pair of vertices
v,w
joined by an edge in
\Gamma
:

(0.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle{v:\ v\in V}\,\big|\,{[v,w]:\ \{v,w\}\in E}\right\rangle

A group
G
is said to be a RAAG if it is isomorphic to some
G_{\Gamma}
. In that case,
\Gamma
is determined by
G
(see
[
13
]
), and is called the defining graph of
G
. RAAGs have intensively been studied for they provide a rich class of groups whose algebraic properties can be derived from those of the defining graph.
Such groups sit in between the two extremal cases of free groups and free abelian groups, and they can thus be seen as interpolating those classes. It is then natural to ask which properties RAAGs share with free/free abelian groups.

For instance, it is well known that subgroups of free and free abelian groups are of the same type.
Nevertheless, the subgroups of a RAAG might not be RAAGs themselves: depending on the defining graph, there may exist finitely generated subgroups that do not even admit a finite number of defining relation, that is to say, the RAAG is not coherent (see Prop.
4.1
).

This problem was studied by Droms
[
9
]
, who proved a finitely generated RAAG is coherent if, and only if, the defining graph is
chordal
, i.e., it does not contain any induced cycle of length
\geq 4
.

On the other hand, Droms
[
10
]
also gave a complete classification of finite graphs
\Gamma
such that all subgroups of
G_{\Gamma}
are RAAGs.

Theorem 0.1

(Droms)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph. Then, every subgroup of
G_{\Gamma}
is a RAAG if, and only if, no induced subgraph of
\Gamma
is either a square or a line-graph of length
3
.

Graphs without induced squares nor line-graphs of length
3
appear in various contexts and with several names, as trivially perfect graphs
[
12
]
, graphs of elementary type
[
4
]
, or quasi-threshold graphs
[
31
]
. In honour to Droms, in the context of RAAGs, these graphs are usually called
Droms graphs
, and we will adopt the same denomination. Clearly, Droms graphs are chordal.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
0.1
that, for a finite graph
\Gamma
to be a Droms graph, it is also sufficient that all the
finitely generated
subgroups of
G_{\Gamma}
are RAAGs, i.e.,
G_{\Gamma}
is locally RAAG.

Among the subgroups of RAAGs, some distinguished normal subgroups have been studied since their discovery due to Bestvina and Brady in
[
3
]
, as they provide examples of finitely generated, yet non-finitely presented groups (in fact, not even of type
F_{2}
), that are of type FP.
Given a graph
\Gamma
, one can define the corresponding
Bestvina-Brady group
as the kernel of the “length character”
\chi_{\Gamma}:G_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}
, obtained by extending
v\in V\mapsto 1\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\Gamma
is a Droms graph, then clearly
B_{\Gamma}
is itself a RAAG, but the problem of detecting Bestvina-Brady groups that are RAAGs remains open in general.
Moved by Droms’ Theorem
0.1
, we are interested in understanding when the group
B_{\Gamma}
is locally RAAG: clearly, if
G_{\Gamma}
is locally RAAG, then so is
B_{\Gamma}
, but one can easily construct counterexamples to the converse statement (see Example
1.8
(1)).
In particular, we show

Theorem A
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite connected simplicial graph. Then, every subgroup of the Bestvina-Brady group
B_{\Gamma}
is a RAAG if, and only if,
\Gamma
is
chordal
and no induced subgraph of
\Gamma
has either of the two forms:

\nabla
\bar{H}

The graph
\nabla
is called the
gem graph
, and
\bar{H}
is a graph where two gems are overlapped; we will call it an
overlapping-gems graph
.

The pro-
p
completions of right-angled Artin groups (and some generalizations of them) have been studied in
[
28
]
(and
[
4
]
) in the attempt of confirming several Galois theoretic conjectures on the realizability of a pro-
p
group as an absolute Galois group. In particular,

Theorem 0.2

(Snopče, Zalesskii
[
28
]
)
.

A finite graph
\Gamma
is a Droms graph if, and only if, the pro-
p
RAAG
G_{\Gamma,p}
is isomorphic to the maximal pro-
p
quotient of the absolute Galois group of a field containing a primitive
p^{th}
root of
1
.

We deduce an analogue of that result for the pro-
p
completion of the Bestvina-Brady groups.

Following
[
30
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
14
]
, we also study the Lie algebra counterpart (
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
) of RAAGs and Bestvina-Brady groups. The advantage of working with positively graded Lie algebras relies on the fact that cohomology computations are easier than in the group-case. For instance, by means of a spectral sequence due to May, the cohomology ring of Bestvina-Brady groups defined on acyclic flag complexes is computed.

Proposition 0.3
.

If
\Gamma
is a finite graph with acyclic flag complex over a field
k
, then

H^{\bullet}(B_{\Gamma},k)\simeq H^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma},k)/(\chi_{\Gamma})

where
(\chi_{\Gamma})
denotes the ideal of
H^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma},k)
generated by the class of the
1

\chi_{\Gamma}:G\to k
.

In
[
27
]
, the authors produce embeddings of non-abelian surface groups into (the derived subgroup of) RAAGs defined by graphs with induced cycles of length at least
4
. We show that no non-abelian surface group appears as a Bestvina-Brady group. We also prove a similar result for non-abelian Poincaré duality Lie algebras.

At the end of the paper we show that the Bestvina-Brady group of a graph is coherent precisely when so is the right-angled Artin group, that is, when the graph is chordal.

2.
Graph characterization

Many classes of graphs are defined in terms of forbidden induced subgraphs. For instance, a finite simplicial graph
\Gamma
is chordal if it contains no induced
n
-cycle for
n\geq 4
, i.e., chordal graphs are
(C_{n})_{n\geq 4}
-free. Similarly,
\Gamma
is a Droms graph if it does not contain any induced square nor line
L_{3}
of length
3
: it is
(L_{3},C_{4})
-free. Sometimes,
a family
C
of graphs with forbidden induced subgraphs is closed with respect to some non-trivial operations. This means that
C
has a subset
\Sigma
of
building-blocks
that generates
C
by iterating those operations. If
\Sigma\neq C
, then we say that the family
C
has a
defining construction
in terms of operations and building blocks
\Sigma
.
For example, Droms graphs can be obtained by applying cones and disjoint unions on single vertices
[
10
]
. Another class of graphs admitting a defining construction is that of ptolemaic graphs.

2.1.
Ptolemaic graphs

A graph
\Gamma
is said to be
ptolemaic
if it is connected, chordal and it does not contain any induced gem
\nabla
.
Ptolemaic graphs form an interesting and well studied class of graph for they are distance-hereditary, as it was proved in
[
1
]
. The same work characterizes ptolemaic graphs in terms of a defining construction that we recall here.

The operations are given by adding either a leaf or
any kind
of twin, with the exception that a false-twin can only be attached to a vertex that has complete neighbourhood.

(1)

A
leaf
is a vertex that is adjacent to a single other vertex of the graph.

(2)

A (true)
twin
is a vertex that has the same neighbourhood as that of another vertex.

(3)

A
false-twin
is a vertex that shares the neighbourhood with another vertex to which it is not adjacent.

Lemma 2.1

(Bandelt, Mulder
[
1
]
)
.

Ptolemaic graphs have a defining construction given by the above operations and single vertices as building blocks, i.e., every finite ptolemaic graph is obtained from the single vertex
K_{1}
by iterating the operations of adding leaves or twins.

For instance, block-graphs are ptolemaic graphs; recall that a block-graph is a graph obtained by connecting complete graphs at single common vertices.

We now introduce a generalization of block-graphs, where the building blocks are the connected Droms graphs instead of cliques.
A
tree of Droms graphs
is a graph defined by connected sums at vertices of connected Droms graphs.
In particular, a tree of Droms graphs has a central element iff it is a Droms graph.

Let

and
\Gamma_{2}
with a common vertex
v
such that
\Gamma
is the connected sum of the
\Gamma_{i}
’s along
v
. By induction,
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma_{i}}
are Droms objects, and hence so is their free product
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}=\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma_{1}}\amalg\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma_{2}}
.
∎

2.2.
The defining construction of trees of Droms graphs

We start by showing the following key result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected chordal
(\bar{H},\Delta)
-free graph. Then,
\Gamma
has either a cut-vertex or a central vertex.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the number of vertices of
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
has at most two vertices, then any of them is trivially a central vertex.
Suppose that
\Gamma
has at least
2
vertices.

Since
\Gamma
is a chordal graph with no induced gem, it is a Ptolemaic graph, and hence we can use the defining construction of
\Gamma
by means of leaves and twins.

Case 1
.

\Gamma
has a leaf.

If
\{v,w\}
is a leaf with
w
of valency
1
, then
v
is a cut-vertex for
\Gamma
.

Case 2
.

\Gamma
has two (true) twins
v
and
v^{\prime}
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the induced subgraph spanned by all the vertices
\neq v^{\prime}
. By induction,
\Gamma^{\prime}
has either a central vertex or a cut-vertex.

If
\Gamma^{\prime}
has a central vertex
z
, then
z
is also a central vertex for
\Gamma
, because either
z=v
or
z
is adjacent to
v
, and hence, in both cases,
v^{\prime}
is adjacent to
z
.

If
\Gamma^{\prime}
has no central vertices, then it must have a cut-vertex
w
. Two subcases might occur.

(1)

v
is not a cut-vertex for
\Gamma^{\prime}
. In this case,
w
is a cut-vertex for
\Gamma
as well.

(2)

v=w
is a cut-vertex for
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
\Gamma^{\prime}
has no central vertex, by Fact
2.3
, there is a block
\Gamma_{1}
of
\Gamma
such that
v
is neither a central nor a cut-vertex for
\Gamma_{1}
. Let
\Gamma_{2}
be the union of the blocks different from
\Gamma_{1}
.
By induction,
\Gamma_{1}
contains either a central vertex or a cut-vertex.

(a)

If
\Gamma_{1}
has no central vertices, then, by induction, it has a cut-vertex
w^{\prime}
. In particular,
v\neq w^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
is a cut-vertex for
\Gamma
.

(b)

Suppose that
\Gamma_{1}
has a central vertex
w_{1}
, and let
w_{2}
be a vertex in
\Gamma_{2}
adjacent to
v
.

If
v
is a leaf in
\Gamma_{1}
, then
w_{1}
is a cut-vertex for
\Gamma_{1}
, and hence for
\Gamma
as in case (2)(a). Now suppose
v
is not a leaf of
\Gamma_{1}
, and let
X\neq\emptyset
be the set of vertices of
\Gamma_{1}
that are not adjacent to
v
; in particular, all
x\in X
lie at distance
2
from
v
. If there existed a vertex
w_{1}^{\prime}\neq w_{1}
that is adjacent to both
v
and a vertex
x\in X
, then
\Gamma
would have an induced overlapping gems graph
\bar{H}
spanned by
\{v,v^{\prime},w_{1},w_{1}^{\prime},x,w_{2}\}
.
It follows that all the vertices
w_{1}^{\prime}\neq w_{1}
of
\Gamma_{1}
that are adjacent to
v
are not adjacent to any vertex of
X
, and hence
w_{1}
is a cut-vertex for
\Gamma
.

It remains to consider the last case.

Case 3
.

\Gamma
has two false twins
v
and
v^{\prime}
(and the neighbourhood of
v
is a complete graph).

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be the induced subgraph spanned by all the vertices
\neq v^{\prime}
. By induction,
\Gamma^{\prime}
has either a central vertex or a cut-vertex.

(1)

Let
z
be a central vertex of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. If
v=z
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a complete graph, and any vertex
w\notin\{v,v^{\prime}\}
is central in
\Gamma
.

If
v\neq z
, then
z
is adjacent to
v^{\prime}
as well, and hence it is central in
\Gamma
.

(2)

If
\Gamma^{\prime}
has no central vertex, then it must have a cut-vertex
w
.
Since the neighbourhood of
v
is a complete graph,
v
cannot be a cut-vertex for
\Gamma^{\prime}
, i.e.,
v\neq w
. It follows that
w
is a cut-vertex of
\Gamma
, and the proof is complete.

∎

We can now characterize the trees of Droms graphs in terms of forbidden induced subgraphs.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a connected chordal graph that does not contain any induced gem
\nabla
nor overlapping-gems
\bar{H}
,

(2)

\Gamma
is a tree of Droms graphs.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is a tree of Droms graphs, then
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is a Droms object by Lemma
2.4
. In particular,
\Gamma
is a ptolemaic graph with no induced
\bar{H}
by Examples
1.8
.

For the converse, suppose
\Gamma
is a connected, chordal,
(\bar{H},\Delta)
-free graph and argue by induction on the number of vertices. The
1
-vertex case is obvious.
By Lemma
2.5
,
\Gamma
has either a central vertex or a cut-vertex.
If
\Gamma
has a cut vertex
v
, then there are two connected subgraphs
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
with common vertex
v
such that
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\underset{v}{\lor}\Gamma_{2}
. By induction, both
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are trees of Droms graphs, and hence so is
\Gamma
.

If
\Gamma
has no cut-vertex, let
z
be a central vertex. Consider the induced subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
spanned by all the vertices
\neq z
. If
\Gamma^{\prime}
is not a Droms graph, then it must contain an induced path of length
3
, and hence its cone
\Gamma
contains an induced gem, contradicting the hypotheses. It follows that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a Droms graph, and hence so is its cone
\Gamma
.
∎

We deduce Theorem A, analogous to Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph. Then,
\Gamma
is a tree of Droms graph if, and only if, anyone of the following statements holds:

(1)

All subgroups of
B_{\Gamma}
are RAAGs (possibly defined by infinite graphs),

(2)

All standard subalgebras of
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
are RAAG Lie algebras,

(3)

The Lie algebra
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is BK,

(4)

The Bestvina-Brady object
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is finitely-generated and locally Droms,

(5)

If
k
is a field, the cohomology
k
-algebra
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{R}_{\Gamma},k)
is universally Koszul,

(6)

The pro-
p
group
B_{\Gamma,p}
is BK,

(7)

Every closed subgroup of
B_{\Gamma,p}
is a pro-
p
RAAG,

(8)

There exists a field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive
p^{th}
root of
1
such that
B_{\Gamma,p}\simeq G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
.

In particular, both the elementary type conjecture
[
11
]
and the universal Koszulity conjecture
[
21
]
are true within the class of Bestvina-Brady pro-
p
groups.

3.
Applications of Theorem
2.7

3.1.
Surface groups

RAAGs can contain surface groups of high genus. For instance, by Servatius, Droms and Servatius
[
27
]
, if an
n
-cycle is induced in a graph
\Gamma
, then the derived subgroup
G_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
contains the fundamental group of a closed oriented surface of genus
g=1+(n-4)2^{n-3}
.

We now prove that no oriented surface group of genus
\geq 2
is a Bestvina-Brady group.

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose that the Bestvina-Brady Lie algebra
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is a quadratic
1
-relator Lie algebra over a field
k
. Then,
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
decomposes as the free product of an abelian Lie algebra of dimension
2
and a free Lie algebra.

In particular, the fundamental group of an orientable closed surface of genus
\geq 2
is not isomorphic to a Bestvina-Brady group.

Proof.

By
[
5
]
,
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is BK, and hence
\Gamma
is a tree of Droms graphs by Theorem
2.7
.

Now,
[
6
, Lem. 2.2]
provides the Betti numbers
b_{i}(\hbox to6.45831pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}):=\dim H^{i}(\hbox to6.45831pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt},k)
of
\mathscr{B}
in terms of those of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
:

1\overset{1-rel.}{=}b_{2}(\mathscr{B})=b_{2}({\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma})-b_{1}({\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma})+1.

Recall that the number of vertices (resp. of edges) of
\Gamma
equals the Betti number
b_{1}({\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma})
(resp.,
b_{2}({\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma})
), so that
|{V}|=|{E}|
.

By definition, a (non-induced) tree
T=(V(T),E(T))
of
\Gamma=(V,E)
is a spanning tree if
V=V(T)
. As
\Gamma
is connected, it admits a spanning tree
T
. One has
|{E(T)}|-|{V(T)}|+1=0
, and hence
1=|{V}|-|{E(T)}|=|{E}|-|{E(T)}|
, i.e.,
\Gamma
is obtained from
T
by adding a single edge to a pair of its vertices, producing an induced cycle
C
. Since
\Gamma
is a tree of Droms graphs,
C
has length
3
and
\mathscr{B}
is the free product of
\mathscr{B}_{C}\simeq k^{2}
and a free Lie algebra.

Now, let
G
be the fundamental group of an orientable closed surface of genus
n
. Then,
G
can be presented as

G=\left\langle{x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}}\,\big|\,{[x_{1},y_{1}][x_{2},y_{2}]\cdots[x_{n},y_{n}]}\right\rangle

The associated Lie algebra was computed by Labute
[
17
]
:

\operatorname{gr}G=\left\langle{x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}}\,\big|\,{[x_{1},y_{1}]+[x_{2},y_{2}]+\dots+[x_{n},y_{n}]}\right\rangle

If
G=B_{\Gamma}
for some graph
\Gamma
, then, for any field
k
,
\operatorname{gr}G\otimes k
is isomorphic with the Bestvina-Brady Lie
k
-algebra
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
. By the first part of the proof,
n=1
.
∎

The same proof shows that a Demuškin group
[
8
]
(see also
[
21
]
and
[
16
]
) occurs as a Bestvina-Brady pro-
p
group only if it is
2
-generated.

Recall that a Lie
k
-algebra
{\mathscr{L}}
is a Poincaré duality Lie algebra of dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
if it has cohomological dimension
n
,
H^{n}({\mathscr{L}},k)
is
1
-dimensional, and the cup-product of the trivial coefficient cohomology ring defines non-degenerate pairing
H^{i}({\mathscr{L}},k)\otimes H^{n-i}({\mathscr{L}},k)\to H^{n}({\mathscr{L}},k)
. For instance, all the finite dimensional Lie algebras of dimension
n
are Poincaré duality of dimension
n
, as well as any quadratic
1
-relator Lie algebra.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph such that
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is a Poincaré duality Lie algebra of dimension
n
. Then,
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is abelian.

Proof.

Suppose
n\geq 1
. Since the cohomological dimension of
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is
n
, the graph
\Gamma
has a
(n+1)
-clique
\Delta
.

Let
v
be any vertex of
\Gamma
, and let
\Gamma_{v}
be the induced subgraph spanned by the vertices
\neq v
. Then,
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma_{v}}
is a proper subalgebra of
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
, and hence it has cohomological dimension
\leq n-1
by
[

, p. 792]

has cohomological dimension

In particular,
\Gamma_{v}
contains no
(n+1)
-clique, that amount to saying

.
∎

The same argument applies to any cocyclic ideal of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}
in the place of
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
.

In particular, the invariant

of

is related with that of any finitely presented cocyclic ideals of

Corollary 3.4
.

Let

Since

is a RAAG Lie algebra, the inequality

follows from Turàn’s theorem (se also
[

, Sec. 3.3]

4.
Coherence of Bestvina-Brady objects

By
[
15
]
and
[
3
]
, the object
\mathfrak{R}_{\Gamma}
is locally of type
FP_{\infty}
over
k
iff
\Gamma
is chordal; hence the
k
-cohomology of
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is a Koszul algebra when
\Gamma
is connected and chordal. In turn, by the universal coefficient theorem, this gives an algebraic proof that the flag complex of a connected chordal graph is acyclic (see also
[
24
]
).

The coherence property of
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is equivalent to that of
\mathfrak{R}_{\Gamma}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a chordal graph,

(2)

\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is coherent,

(3)

The derived sub-object
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}^{\prime}=[\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma},\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}]
is free.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is chordal, then the RAAG object
\mathfrak{R}_{\Gamma}
is coherent and with free derived sub-object (see
[
9
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
15
]
), and hence the same holds for
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
. This proves that
(1)
implies both
(2)
and
(3)
.

Suppose now that
\Gamma
contains an induced
n
-cycle
C
for
n\geq 4
. Then, the sub-object
\mathfrak{B}_{C}
of
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is finitely generated but not finitely presented by Theorem
1.3
, proving that
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}
is not coherent. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{B}_{C}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{R}_{C}^{\prime}
is a non-free sub-object of
\mathfrak{B}_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
, the latter cannot be free.
∎

In the context of (pro-
p
) groups, it is proved that
B_{T}
is a free (pro-
p
) group of finite rank when
T
is a finite tree (
[
28
]
). We now provide a proof for the Lie theoretic translation of that result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph. Then,
{\mathscr{B}}_{\Gamma}
is a free Lie algebra of rank
|{V(\Gamma)}|-1
if, and only if,
\Gamma
is a tree.

Proof.

First assume that
\Gamma=T
is a tree.
We argue by induction on the number of vertices of
T
.
The result clearly holds when
T
consists of a single vertex, for
{\mathscr{B}}_{\{v\}}=0
.
Suppose that
T
has at least
2
vertices.
Since
T
is a finite tree, it contains a leaf
v
and we set
e=\{v,w\}\in E(T)
for the unique edge containing
v
.
If
T_{0}
is the induced subtree of
T
spanned by the vertices
\neq v
of
T
, then
{\mathscr{B}}_{T_{0}}
is free and
{\mathscr{L}}_{T}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}({\mathscr{L}}_{T_{0}},v)
, where
\phi:\langle{w}\rangle\to{\mathscr{L}}_{T_{0}}
is the zero derivation. Since
{\mathscr{B}}_{T_{0}}={\mathscr{B}}_{T}\cap{\mathscr{L}}_{T_{0}}
and
w\notin{\mathscr{B}}_{T_{0}}
, it follows that
{\mathscr{B}}_{T}
is free by
[
18
, Thm. 4]
.

For the converse, suppose
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is a free Lie algebra of the prescribed finite rank, and
\Gamma
is not a tree. Since
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is finitely generated,
\Gamma
is connected, and hence it must contain an induced
n
-cycle for some
n\geq 3
.

If
n=3
, then
\cd{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}\geq 3
. Now, notice that
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}=\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}\rtimes k
, and hence, by
[
6
, Lem. 2.2]
,
\cd\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}\geq\cd{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}-1\geq 2
, proving that
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is not free.

Assume now
n\geq 4
and let
\Delta
be an induced
n
-cycle in
\Gamma
. By Theorem
1.3
,
\mathscr{B}_{\Delta}
is a standard subalgebra of
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
but it is not of type FP
2
, and hence it is not a free Lie algebra. Since
\mathscr{B}_{\Delta}=\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}\cap{\mathscr{L}}_{\Delta}
, we deduce that
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
contains a non-free subalgebra, proving that
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is not free either.
∎

Geometric proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite connected graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. It is a tree precisely when
n=m+1
.

If
\Gamma
is a tree, then
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is finitely presented by Theorem
1.3
. Since
{\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma}=\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}\rtimes k
, it follows that
b_{2}(\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma})=b_{2}({\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma})-b_{1}(\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma})=m-(n-1)=0
, and hence
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is free by
[
5
, Thm. 5.2]
.

Conversely, if
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}
is free of rank
n-1
, then it is of type FP and
m=b_{2}({\mathscr{L}}_{\Gamma})=b_{2}(\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma})+b_{1}(\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma})=0+n-1
, i.e.,
\Gamma
is a tree.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

A finite graph
\Gamma
is a tree if, and only if,
B_{\Gamma}
is a free group of finite rank.

Proof.

If
B_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{Z})
is a free group of finite rank, then
\Gamma
is connected by Theorem
1.3
, and
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}\simeq\operatorname{gr}B_{\Gamma}
is free by
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]
(see also
[
19
]
). As a consequence of Proposition
4.2
, we deduce that
\Gamma
is a tree.

Conversely, if
\Gamma
is a tree, then
\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{Z})
is a free Lie algebra and
\operatorname{gr}B_{\Gamma}\simeq\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{Z})
. It follows from the May spectral sequence that
\cd B_{\Gamma}\leq\cd\mathscr{B}_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{Z})=1
, and hence
B_{\Gamma}
is a free group by the Stallings-Swan theorem.
∎

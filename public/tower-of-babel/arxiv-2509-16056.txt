Mayer–Vietoris sequences for complexes of tori
Nguy e ^ ~ \tilde{\hat{\text{e}}} n M a ⋅ \d{\text{a}} nh Linh

Abstract
In the patching setting, given a factorization inverse system of fields over which patching for finite-dimensional vector spaces holds, together with a crossed module over the inverse limit field, the corresponding six-term Mayer–Vietoris sequence is constructed, generalizing the classical result of Harbater–Hartmann–Krashen for linear algebraic groups. When the crossed module is a two-term complex of tori, the above sequence is extended into a nine-term exact sequence, notably without any assumption on global domination of Galois cohomology of the inverse system. To this end, we develop a theory of (co-)flasque resolutions for short complexes of tori which is built on the work of Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc. As an application, we show that patching holds for nonabelian second Galois cohomology of reductive groups with smooth centers. We also obtain a weak local–global principle for this cohomology set in the simply connected semisimple case.



Keywords.
Galois cohomology, nonabelian cohomology, patching, linear algebraic groups.

Introduction

1.1
Patching

Given an algebraic number field
k
and a finite group
G
, the classical
inverse Galois problem
asks whether there exists a finite Galois extension of
k
with Galois group
G
. Though it is solved for several families of finite groups, the problem remains open in full generality.

In recent years, the interest in the inverse Galois problem over arithmetico-geometric analogues of algebraic number fields has been arising. Amongst the available techniques at our disposal,
patching
is a method which yielded fruitful results. Inspired by the Riemann existence theorem over
\mathbb{C}(X)
, the method was developed by Harbater, Hartmann, Krashen, and serveral other authors (see
e.g.

[
Har03
,
HH10
]
) to study arithemtic problems over
semiglobal fields
, that is, fields of the form
K(X)
for some complete discretely valued field
K
and some (smooth projective geometrically integral)
K
-curve
X
. A notable result in this direction is a theorem by Harbater which says that any finite group is a Galois group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(T)

[
Har87
,
Liu95
,
CT00
,
Kol00
,
Kol03
]
.

As in
[
HHK15
, §2]
, we consider the general setting for patching over fields as follows. A
factorization inverse system of fields
is given by the data of a (nonempty) graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
(multiple edges are allowed but loops are not) together with fields
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathscr{V}}
and
\{F_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{E}}
and fields inclusions
F_{i}\hookrightarrow F_{k}
whenever an edge
k
is adjacent to a vertex
i
. We assume that the limit that the inverse limit of the corresponding system (in the category of commutative ring) is a field
F
. In other words, we have an equalizer diagram

1\to F\to\prod_{i\in\mathscr{V}}F_{i}\rightrightarrows\prod_{k\in\mathscr{V}}F_{k}

of fields (in particular,
\Gamma
is connected). We refer to
\Gamma
as the
reduction graph
of the factorization inverse system
\mathcal{F}
.

The typical situation where patching is applied is the case where
F=K(X)
is a semiglobal field as above. To construct the reduction graph
\Gamma
as well as the overfields
F_{i}
and
F_{k}
, we need some notations. Let
T
be the valuation ring of
K
and
t\in T
an uniformizer. Let
\mathscr{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}(T)
be a normal connected projective
T
-curve with generic fibre
X
(a
normal model
of
F
). The reduced closed fibre
Y\subseteq\mathscr{X}
is a union of finitely many smooth connected curves over the residue field
k
of
T
.

•

Fix a finite nonempty subset
\mathcal{P}\subseteq Y
containing the points where the distinct irreducible components of
Y
meet. For
P\in\mathcal{P}
, denote by

the field of fractions of the complete local ring
\hat{R}_{P}:=\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathscr{X},P}

.

•

Let

. For

\hat{R}_{U}
denote the

•

Whenever a point

lies in the closure

, we obtain a branch

of

\hat{R}_{\wp}
be the completion of the localization of

The set

[

[

In this article, for any factorization inverse system
\mathcal{F}=(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathscr{V}},\{F_{k}\}_{k\in\mathscr{E}})
of fields, we assume that the reduction graph

is equipped with an orientation, that is, the choice of a head

(hence, a tail

) for each edge

. We say that

if, given a (finite-dimensional)

1.2
Organization of the manuscript

The aim of the present article is to study analogues of (
1.1
) when the linear algebraic
F
-group
G
is replaced by a
2
-term complex of
F
-tori. In Section
2
, we consider the general nonabelian setting, where a
crossed module
\mathscr{C}=[G\xrightarrow{\partial}H]
takes place of a linear algebraic group
G
. It is possible to define the Galois hypercohomology groups
\mathbb{H}^{i}(F,\mathscr{C})
for
i=-1,0
. While the definition of
\mathbb{H}^{-1}(F,\mathscr{C})
is rather straightforward, a geometric description of
\mathbb{H}^{0}(F,\mathscr{C})
can be given using
bitorsors
under the crossed module
\mathscr{C}
(see Definition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
). This slightly generalizes the classical notion of bitorsor under a linear algebraic group. With these descriptions at our disposal, we construct the six-term Mayer–Vietoris sequence for
\mathscr{C}
(Theorem
2.5
), which in particular entails the bitorsor patching problem. The proof relies on the Mayer–Vietoris sequence (
1.1
) for linear algebraic groups with some consideration of the “bitorsor condition”.

Next, we investigate the commutative setting. When
G
is a commutative algebraic group, sequence (
1.1
) can be extended into a long exact sequence using the higher Galois cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{i}(-,G)
, under an additional condition of
global domination
of Galois cohomology
[
HHK14
, Definition 2.3.3 and Theorem 2.5.1]
. This latter holds for factorization inverse systems arising from semiglobal fields, with some restriction on the residue characteristic of
K

[
HHK14
, Theorem 3.1.2]
. In Section
4
, we consider the case where the crossed module
\mathscr{C}
is a
2
-term of complex
\mathscr{T}=[T_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial}T_{2}]
of
F
-tori, the Galois hypercohomology groups
\mathbb{H}^{i}(-,\mathscr{T})
are defined for all
i\geq-1
. Quiet surprisingly, the six-term Mayer–Vietoris sequence from Theorem
2.5
can be extended into a nine-term exact sequence
without any global domination condition
for Galois cohomology over
\mathcal{F}
, using
\mathbb{H}^{1}(-,\mathscr{T})
(Theorem
4.4
). In particular, for smooth any
F
-group of multiplicative type
M
, we have a nine-term exact sequence relating
\mathrm{H}^{i}(-,M)
for
i=0,1,2
.

We obtain the main result of Section
4
using dévissage, by means of the notions of
flasque resolutions
and
coflasque resolutions
of short complexes of tori (Theorem
3.5
), which is the subject of study of Section
3
. We develop this theory from the previous work
[
CTS87
]
by Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc. A by-product of this investigation is the calculation of the
0^{\text{th}}
hypercohomology group of short complexes of tori modulo
R
-equivalence (Proposition
3.7
).

Finally, we give an application of Theorem
4.4
to the study of nonabelian second Galois cohomology of reductive group. We prove that patching for (nonabelian)
\mathrm{H}^{2}
holds for these groups whenever the center is smooth (we insist on the fact that no global domination condition on the factorization inverse system is required). When the group is simply connected semisimple, we show a weak local–global principle for nonabelian
\mathrm{H}^{2}
. To this end, we exploit the classification of centers of simply connected groups and a theorem of Endo–Miyata on flasque tori split by a metacylic extension
[
EM75
]
.

2.1
Galois hypercohomology and bitorsors

Let
F
be a field. We recall the notion of crossed module from
[
Bor92
, Definition 2.1]
. A
crossed module

\mathscr{C}
over
F
is a morphism complex
G\xrightarrow{\partial}H
of smooth linear algebraic groups over
F
(regarded as a
2
-term complex, where
G
and
H
are placed in respective degrees
-1
and
0
) together with a left action

H\times_{F}G\to G,\quad(h,g)\mapsto h\cdot g

of
H

\begin{cases}gg^{\prime}g^{-1}&=\partial(g)\cdot g^{\prime}\\
\partial(h\cdot g)&=h\partial(g)h^{-1}\end{cases}

(2.1)

for any
h\in H(\bar{F})
and
g\in G(\bar{F})
. Its

Galois hypercohomology group is defined by

\mathbb{H}^{-1}(F,\mathscr{C}):=(\operatorname{Ker}\partial)(F).

The condition (
2.1
) forces
\operatorname{Ker}\partial
to be central in
G
. In particular, the above group is abelian.

A
0

\mathscr{C}
is, by definition, a pair
(\alpha,h)
, where
\alpha:\operatorname{Gal}_{F}\to G(\bar{F})
is a continuous (

. It is easily checked that the set

\nu^{\prime\prime}_{r}(\pi_{r}(p^{\prime},p))=\nu^{\prime}_{r}(p^{\prime})\nu_{r}(p)=g_{k}g^{\prime}_{k}\nu^{\prime}_{l}(p^{\prime})\nu_{l}(p)=g_{k}g_{k}^{\prime}\nu_{l}^{\prime\prime}(\pi_{l}(p^{\prime},p))=\nu^{\prime\prime}_{r}(\pi_{l}(p^{\prime},p))

(also note that

as claimed. Since

.

in

. To this end, fix a vertex

together with a field inclusion

. Let

,

, and

by construction, one has

by virtue of (

). It follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\nu_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\pi(p^{\prime}\ast g^{-1},p\ast(f(p)^{-1}\cdot g)))

\displaystyle=\nu^{\prime}_{i}(p^{\prime}\ast g^{-1})\nu_{i}(p\ast(f(p)^{-1}\cdot g))

\displaystyle=\nu^{\prime}_{i}(p^{\prime})g^{-1}\nu_{i}(p)(f(p)^{-1}\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\nu^{\prime}_{i}(p^{\prime})\nu_{i}(p)

Since

Using the notations from Remarks

and

3
(Co-)flasque resolutions revisited

This section is devoted to the notions of flasque resolutions and coflasque resolutions for
2
-term complexes of tori. This specializes to the corresponding results by Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc
[
CTS87
, Lemma 0.6]
for complexes that are quasi-isomorphic to a
1
-term complex (concentrated in either degree
-1
or
0
). By Cartier duality (see
1.3
Notations and conventions
), it suffices to work with the character lattices of these tori.

3.1
Short complexes of
G
-lattices

In this subsection, let
G
be a profinite group. We recall the notions of (co-)flasque
G
-lattices. By a
G
-module
, we always mean a finitely generated abelian group
M
equipped with an action of
G
(when
M
is endowed with the discrete topology, such an action is automatically
continuous
,
i.e.
, factors through a finite quotient of
G
). If
M
is torsion-free, we say that
M
is a
G
-lattice
.

A
G
-lattice
P
is said to be
permutation
if it has a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis permuted by
G
. This is equivalent to saying that
P
is isomorphic to the direct sum of (finitely many)
G
-lattices of the form
\mathbb{Z}[G/H]
, where
H
are closed subgroups of
G
. An
invertible

G
-lattice
I
is one that is isomorphic to a direct factor of a permutation
G
-lattice.

A
G
-lattice
F
is
flasque
if it satisfies either of the following (equivalent) conditions.

(i).

\hat{H}^{-1}(H,F)=0
for any closed subgroup
H\leq G
(here,
\hat{H}^{-1}
means Tate cohomology).

(ii).

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(F,P)=0
for any permutation
G
-lattice
P
.

(iii).

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(F,I)=0
for any invertible
G
-lattice
I
.

Dually, a
coflasque

G
-lattice
C
is one such that either of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied.

(i).

\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,C)=0
for any closed subgroup
H\leq G
.

(ii).

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(P,C)=0
for any permutation
G
-lattice
P
.

(iii).

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(I,C)=0
for any invertible
G
-lattice
I
.

The
dual
of a
G
-lattice
L
is
L^{\circ}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L,\mathbb{Z})
. The functor
L\mapsto L^{\circ}
is an exact anti-equivalence from the category of
G
-lattices to itself, which takes permutation (resp. invertible)
G
-lattices to permutation (resp. invertible)
G
-lattices. It takes flasque
G
-lattices to coflasque
G
-lattices and vice-versa. If
H\leq G
is a closed subgroup, then permutation (resp. invertible) (resp. flasque) (resp. coflasque)
G
-lattices are so as
H
-lattices. If furthermore
H
is normal in
G
, then permutation (resp. invertible) (resp. flasque) (resp. coflasque)
(G/H)
-lattices are so as
G
-lattices.

Here is the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{L}=[L_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial}L_{2}]
be a complex of
G
-lattices, where
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are placed in respective degrees
-1
and
0
.

(i).

\mathscr{L}
admits a flasque resolution, that is, it is quasi-isomophic to a complex of
G
-lattices of the form
[P\to F]
, where
P
is permutation and
F
is flasque. Moreover,
F
is essentially unique in the sense that if
[Q^{\prime}\to F^{\prime}]
is another flasque resolution of
\mathscr{L}
, then
F\oplus Q^{\prime}\cong F^{\prime}\oplus Q
.

(ii).

Dually,
\mathscr{L}
admits a coflasque resolution, that is, it is quasi-isomophic to a complex of
G
-lattices of the form
[C\to Q]
, where
C
is coflasque and
Q
is permutation. The coflasque
G
-lattice
C
is also essentially unique in the above sense.

We require the following homological algebra lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider a commutative square

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

(3.1)

in an abelian category. Assume either of the following.

(i).

f
is a monomorphism and (
3.1
) is cocartesian (a pushout diagram).

(ii).

g
is an epimorphism and (
3.1
) is cartesian (a pullback diagram).

Then, the vertical arrows yield a quasi-isomorphism
[A\xrightarrow{\partial}B]\cong[A^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\partial^{\prime}}B^{\prime}]
of complexes.

Proof.

(i)
is obtained from
(ii)
by working in the opposite category. To prove
(ii)
, by virtue of the Freyd–Mitchell embedding theorem
[
Mit64
, Theorem 4.4]
, it suffices to work in the category of modules over some ring. Since (
3.1
) is a pullback diagram, we may assume that

A=\{(a^{\prime},b)\in A^{\prime}\oplus B:\partial^{\prime}(a^{\prime})=g(b)\},

and that
f
and
\partial
are induced by the natural projections onto the two respective factors.

If
(a^{\prime},b)\in(\operatorname{Ker}\partial)\cap(\operatorname{Ker}f)
then
a^{\prime}=0
and
b=0
. This proves that
f
induces an injection
\operatorname{Ker}\partial\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Ker}\partial^{\prime}
. As for surjectivity, take
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
with
\partial(a^{\prime})=0
(
=g(0)
). Then
(a^{\prime},0)\in A^{\prime}\oplus B
lies in
\operatorname{Ker}\partial
and is mapped to

under

. Therefore,
f
restricts to an isomorphism
\operatorname{Ker}\partial\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{Ker}\partial^{\prime}
. It remains to show that
g
induces an isomorphism
\operatorname{Coker}\partial\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{Coker}\partial^{\prime}
. Surjectivity follows from assumption. As for injectivity, let
b\in B
such that
g(b)=\partial^{\prime}(a^{\prime})
for some

. Then
(a^{\prime},b)\in A
and
\partial(a^{\prime},b)=b
. The claim follows.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

(i)
is obtained from
(ii)
by the duality
L\mapsto L^{\circ}
. To prove
(ii)

[

[

, there is an exact sequence

where

(resp.

4.1
The case of quasitrivial tori

First, we start with the case of a one-term complex

Theorem 4.1
.

If patching for finite-dimensional vector spaces holds over

0\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,Q)\to\prod_{i\in\mathscr{V}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(F_{i},Q)\to\prod_{k\in\mathscr{E}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(F_{k},Q)

Proof.

Let
\eta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,Q)
such that
\eta|_{F_{i}}=0
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(F_{i},Q)
for any

[

, there exists an integer

is an obstruction to the existence of an

dominating

. Since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(E,\operatorname{SL}_{n})=1
(as follows from Speiser’s version of Hilbert’s Theorem 90,
cf.

[
Ser04
, Chapitre X, Corollaire to Proposition 3]
), one has

if and only if

For any

under the assumption that

, one has

. Take any point
x_{i}\in X(F_{i})
and define the morphism

\pi_{i}:\operatorname{SL}_{n,F_{i}}\to X_{F_{i}},\quad q\mapsto x_{i}\ast q.

(where
\ast

\operatorname{SL}_{n,F}
on

, the fibre

is an

-torsor under

, therefore has an

-rational point since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{k},Q)=0
. In other words, there exists

such that
x_{l(k)}\ast q_{k}=x_{r(k)}
. Furthermore, since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,Q)=0

1.1
), there are elements

, for

, such that

for any

. Since

is quasi-projective, there is an affine open subset

To show exactness at the term

be the factorization of

(resp.

\mathscr{V}^{\prime}:=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathscr{V}}\mathscr{V}_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{E}^{\prime}:=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathscr{E}}\mathscr{E}_{k},

Proof.

For

is adjacent to

Assume that patching for finite-dimensional vector spaces holds over

is smooth algebraic
\bar{F}

\kappa:\operatorname{Gal}_{F}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\bar{F}\text{-}\operatorname{grp}}(\bar{G})/\operatorname{Inn}(\bar{G})

The Galois outer action

Let us consider a factorization inverse system of fields

Let

The main result of this section is the following “weak local–global principle” for nonabelian second Galois cohomology of simply connected groups.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
L=(\bar{G},\kappa)
be an
F
-band, with
\bar{G}
simply connected semisimple. If
Z(L)
is smooth (that is, of order invertible in
F
) and if patching for finite-dimensional vector spaces holds over
\mathcal{F}
, then one has an equalizer diagram

1\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,L)\to\prod_{i\in\mathscr{V}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(F_{i},L)\rightrightarrows\prod_{k\in\mathscr{E}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(F_{k},L).

Proof.

In view of Corollary
5.2
, it remains to show that
\Sha^{2}(F,Z(L))=0
. Let
G
be an
F
-form of
\bar{G}
inducing the Galois outer action
\kappa
(which exists by
[
Bor93
, Proposition 3.1]
). Now,
G
is isomorphic to a product of
F
-groups of the form
\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}(G^{\prime})
, where
F^{\prime}/F
is a finite separable extension and
G^{\prime}
is an absolutely simple simply connected linear algebraic group over
F^{\prime}
. It is therefore enough to show that
\Sha^{2}(F,\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}Z^{\prime})=0
for any
G^{\prime}/F^{\prime}
as above, where
Z^{\prime}:=Z(G^{\prime})
. The finite
F^{\prime}
-group
Z^{\prime}
is split by a Galois extension
F^{\prime\prime}/F^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{Gal}_{F^{\prime\prime}}
acts trivially on the Dynkin diagram of

(see
e.g.

[
PR94
, pp. 331—332]
for the precise values of
Z^{\prime}
based on the classification of
G^{\prime}
).

By
[
CTS87
, Lemma 0.6 (0.6.2)]
and Cartier duality, there is a distinguished triangle

Z^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}\to Q^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}[1],

where
S^{\prime}
is a flasque
F^{\prime}
-torus and
Q^{\prime}
is a quasitrivial
F^{\prime}
-torus, both split by
F^{\prime\prime}
. Applying the exact functor
\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}
gives a distinguished triangle

\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}Z^{\prime}\to S\to Q\to\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}Z^{\prime}[1],

where
S:=\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}S^{\prime}
and where

is a quasitrivial
F
-torus. In particular, since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,Q)=0
, we have an injection
\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}Z^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,S)

\mathbb{Z}/2

Hum72

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the asymptotic Plateau problem in hyperbolic space. Let

\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\big\{(x,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\big|x_{n+1}>0\big\},\quad ds^{2}=\frac{1}{x_{n+1}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}dx_{i}^{2}.

We are interested in finding a smooth complete hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying the equation

(1.1)

f\big(\kappa[\Sigma]\big)=\sigma

and with the asymptotic boundary

(1.2)

\partial\Sigma=\Gamma,

where
\kappa[\Sigma]=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
are the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
,
f
is a smooth symmetric function of
n
variables,
\sigma\in(0,1)
is a prescribed constant and
\Gamma
is a disjoint collection of smooth closed embedded
(n-1)
dimensional submanifolds in

\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This problem was initially investigated by Anderson
[
2
,
3
]
, and Hardt and Lin
[
14
]
for area-minimizing varieties using geometric measure theory, which were extended by Tonegawa
[
24
]
to hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature. Later, PDE methods were initiated by Lin
[
17
]
for minimal hypersurfaces, followed by Nelli and Spruck
[
19
]
and Guan and Spruck
[
8
]
for hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature. The Gauss curvature case was initiated by Labourie
[
16
]
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, and by Rosenberg and Spruck
[
22
]
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. For general curvature function
f
defined in
the positive cone
\Gamma_{n}
,
this problem was completely solved by Guan, Spruck, Szapiel and Xiao
[
11
,
10
,
12
]
under several natural structure conditions. Recently, Hong, Li and Zhang
[
15
]
established an alternative method for constructing strictly locally convex solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).
For general
f
satisfying natural structure conditions, assuming that
\Sigma
is a vertical graph
u
over a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\partial\Omega=\Gamma
and
\Gamma
is mean convex, (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) was solved by Guan
and Spruck
[
9
]
if

(1.3)

\sigma>\sigma_{0},\quad\text{ where }\sigma_{0}\text{ is a positive constant}.

In this paper, we aim at deleting assumption (
1.3
) for some special
f
.

The difficulty for solving (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) lies in the singularity of the equation at
\Gamma
. The basic idea is to prove the existence of solution
u^{\epsilon}
to the approximate Dirichlet problem

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} f(\kappa[u])=&\sigma\quad\text{ in }&\Omega,\\
u=&\epsilon\quad\text{ on }&\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u^{\epsilon}
will have a convergent subsequence whose limit is a solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), once the uniform a priori estimates (independent of
\epsilon
) up to second order are established.
All the estimates have been derived in
[
9
]
for any
\sigma\in(0,1)
except the uniform global curvature estimate which needs assumption (
1.3
).
Recently, Lu
[
18
]
derived the uniform global curvature estimate for any
\sigma\in(0,1)
when
f=\sigma_{n-1}
,
where

\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}}

is defined on
k
-th Gårding’s cone

\Gamma_{k}=\big\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\big|\sigma_{j}(\kappa)>0,\,j=1,\ldots,k\big\}.

Also, Wang studied the case
f=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k-1}}
in
[
25
]
and the case
f=\sigma_{2}
and
f=\sigma_{n-2}
in
[
26
]
.
In
[
18
,
25
,
26
]
, the structure of
\sigma_{k}
and some useful inequalities in Guan, Ren and Wang
[
13
,
20
,
21
]
and in Shankar and Yuan
[
23
]
are applied.

To delete assumption (
1.3
), it seems reasonable to consider special
f
. In this paper, we look at the following two classes of curvature functions, which may be found in Chu and Jiao
[
5
]
and in Wu
[
27
]
, Dinew
[
6
]
and Dong
[
7
]
respectively:

(1.5)

f(\kappa)=\frac{1}{(C_{n}^{k})^{\frac{1}{k}}(n-1)}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(\lambda),\qquad\lambda\in\Gamma_{k},\qquad 1\leq k\leq n,

where

\lambda_{i}=\sum\limits_{j\neq i}\kappa_{j},\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

and

(1.6)

f(\kappa)=\frac{1}{k}P_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{C_{n}^{k}}},

where

P_{k}(\kappa)=\prod\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}}(\kappa_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\kappa_{i_{k}}),\qquad 1\leq k\leq n

is defined on the cone

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\big\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\big|\kappa_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\kappa_{i_{k}}>0,\forall\,1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n\big\}.

Both classes satisfy all structure conditions in Guan and Spruck
[
9
]
(see (
2.4
)–(
2.9
), and one may refer to
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
for the verifications). In particular, (
1.5
) for
k=n
coincides with (
1.6
) for
k=n-1
. Admissible hypersurface
\Sigma
associated to (
1.6
) (that is,
\kappa[\Sigma]\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
) is also called
k
-convex hypersurface.

For curvature function (
1.5
) with
k<n
, we obtain the following existence result by applying a special property (
3.9
) (see Chen, Dong and Han
[
4
]
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
\sigma\in(0,1)
is a constant, the Euclidean mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
is nonnegative, and
f
is given by (
1.5
) with
k<n
. Then there exists a complete hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with uniformly bounded principal curvatures

(1.8)

\big|\kappa[\Sigma]\big|\leq C\quad\text{ on }\Sigma.

Moreover,
\Sigma
is the vertical graph of a unique admissible solution
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
to the Dirichlet problem associated to (
2.10
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} G(D^{2}u,Du,u)=&f\big(\kappa[u]\big)=\sigma,\quad u>0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
u=&0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Furthermore,
u^{2}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
and

\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma},\quad u|D^{2}u|\leq C\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

For
k=n
, (
1.5
) no longer possesses the nice property (
3.9
). The uniform global curvature estimate turns out to be more difficult for (
1.6
). Our first attempt is to consider the special case when
k=2
and
n=3
in (
1.6
), which is the same with
k=n=3
in (
1.5
):

(1.9)

(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2})(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{3})(\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{3})=(2\sigma)^{3}.

In this special case, we are able to tackle third order terms accurately and make full use of the concavity of
f
. In fact, by choosing a suitable test function which contains the mean curvature
H=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{3}
, we are able to express
h_{112}
,
h_{221}
,
h_{113}
,
h_{331}
,
h_{223}
,
h_{332}
in terms of
h_{111}
,
h_{222}
,
h_{333}
by solving six equations. Then the extreme values involving third order terms seem to be optimal. The next step is to divide the estimation into three cases, where we can see clearly how the difficult terms are balanced by good terms and what kind of fine inequalities are needed to make the estimation possible. We obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Suppose that
\sigma\in(0,1)
is a constant and the Euclidean mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
is nonnegative. Then there exists a complete hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{4}
satisfying (
1.9
)–(
1.2
) with uniformly bounded principal curvatures

(1.11)

\big|\kappa[\Sigma]\big|\leq C\quad\text{ on }\Sigma.

The rest of the conclusions are the same with Theorem
1.7
.

For curvature function (
1.6
) with general
n
and
k
, we believe that the structure of
f
will play a fundamental role in the estimation. Though our case seems to be simple, the calculation at some steps is huge and we turn to Mathematica. Our special case may shed light on the general case: it seems that we have to make each step optimal in order to make the estimation possible.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: in section 2, we collect some useful calculations and known facts. In section 3, we derive the uniform global curvature estimate for (
1.5
) with

, finishing the proof of Theorem
1.7
. In Section 4, we derive the uniform global curvature estimate for (
1.9
), finishing the proof of Theorem
1.10
.

In this section, we collect some basic calculations and known facts which can be found in

First, we present some basic calculations on the graph

\Sigma=\Big\{\big(x,u(x)\big)\big|x\in\Omega\Big\},

where
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{n}\equiv\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

2.1.
Vertical graphs

Denote

\partial_{I}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{I}},\quad I=1,\ldots,n+1.

Then,

\partial_{i}+u_{i}\partial_{n+1},\quad i=1,\ldots,n

are the coordinate tangent vector fields on
\Sigma
.

When
\Sigma
is considered as a graph in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the upward unit normal vector field, metric, inverse of the metric and second fundamental form are

\nu=\frac{(-Du,1)}{w},\quad w=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}},

\tilde{g}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+u_{i}u_{j},

\tilde{g}^{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{w^{2}},

\tilde{h}_{ij}=\frac{u_{ij}}{w}.

Consequently, the Euclidean principal curvatures
\tilde{\kappa}=(\tilde{\kappa}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\kappa}_{n})
of
\Sigma
are the eigenvalues of
\tilde{A}=\{\tilde{a}_{ij}\}
with

\tilde{a}_{ij}=\frac{1}{w}\gamma^{ik}u_{kl}\gamma^{lj},

where

\gamma^{ik}=\delta_{ik}-\frac{u_{i}u_{k}}{w(1+w)}

and

\gamma_{ik}=\delta_{ik}+\frac{u_{i}u_{k}}{1+w},

which satisfy

\gamma^{ik}\gamma_{kj}=\delta_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{kj}=\tilde{g}_{ij}.

When
\Sigma
is considered as a graph in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, its unit upward normal vector field, metric, second fundamental form are given respectively by

{\bf n}=u\nu,

g_{ij}=\frac{\delta_{ij}+u_{i}u_{j}}{u^{2}},

h_{ij}=\frac{\delta_{ij}+u_{i}u_{j}+uu_{ij}}{u^{2}w}.

The hyperbolic principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
are the eigenvalues of
A=\{a_{ij}\}
, where

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{ij}=

\displaystyle u^{2}\gamma^{ik}h_{kl}\gamma^{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{ik}\big(\delta_{kl}+u_{k}u_{l}+uu_{kl}\big)\gamma^{lj}}{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{ij}+u\gamma^{ik}u_{kl}\gamma^{lj}}{w}.

Denote
\nu^{n+1}=\nu\cdot\partial_{n+1}
, where
\cdot
is the metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. By the above formulae, we obtain the following relations

(2.2)

h_{ij}=\frac{1}{u}\tilde{h}_{ij}+\frac{\nu^{n+1}}{u^{2}}\tilde{g}_{ij}

and

(2.3)

\kappa_{i}=u\tilde{\kappa_{i}}+\nu^{n+1},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

2.2.
Natural structure conditions

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open symmetric convex cone such that
\Gamma_{n}\subset K
.
Following
[
9
]
, the curvature function
f
is assumed to satisfy the fundamental structure conditions:

(2.4)

f_{i}(\lambda)\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0\quad\text{ in }K,\,1\leq i\leq n,

(2.5)

f\text{ is a concave function in }K,

(2.6)

f>0\quad\text{ in }K,\quad f=0\quad\text{ on }\partial K,

(2.7)

f(1,\cdots,1)=1,

(2.8)

f\text{ is homogeneous of degree one},

(2.9)

\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n}+R)\geq 1+\epsilon_{0}\quad\text{uniformly in }B_{\delta_{0}}({\bf 1})

for some fixed
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
, where
B_{\delta_{0}}({\bf 1})
is the ball of radius
\delta_{0}
centered at
{\bf 1}=(1,\cdots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the vector space of
n\times n
symmetric matrices. Let

\mathcal{S}_{K}=\{A\in\mathcal{S}|\lambda(A)\in K\},

where
\lambda(A)=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
denotes the eigenvalues of
A
.

Define a function
F
by

F(A)=f\big(\lambda(A)\big),\quad A\in\mathcal{S}_{K}.

Set

F^{ij}(A)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial a_{ij}}(A),\quad F^{ij,kl}(A)=\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial a_{ij}\partial a_{kl}}(A).

Define a function
G
by

G(D^{2}u,Du,u)=F(A),

where
A
is given by (
2.1
). Then equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(2.10)

G(D^{2}u,Du,u)=\sigma.

2.3.
Calculations on hypersurface
\Sigma

Let
g
and
\nabla
be the induced metric and Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma
from
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\tilde{g}
and
\tilde{\nabla}
be the metric and Levi-Civita connection induced from

\nabla

are related by

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}-\frac{1}{u}(u_{i}\delta_{kj}+u_{j}\delta_{ik}-\tilde{g}^{kl}u_{l}\tilde{g}_{ij}).

For any
C^{2}
function
v
on
\Sigma
, we have

(2.11)

\nabla_{ij}v=(v_{i})_{j}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}v_{k}=\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}v+\frac{1}{u}(u_{i}v_{j}+u_{j}v_{i}-\tilde{g}^{kl}u_{l}v_{k}\tilde{g}_{ij}).

In particular,

(2.12)

\nabla_{ij}u=\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}u+\frac{2u_{i}u_{j}}{u}-\frac{1}{u}\tilde{g}^{kl}u_{k}u_{l}\tilde{g}_{ij},

(2.13)

\nabla_{ij}\frac{1}{u}=-\frac{1}{u^{2}}\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}u+\frac{1}{u^{3}}\tilde{g}^{kl}u_{k}u_{l}\tilde{g}_{ij},

(2.14)

\nabla_{ij}\frac{v}{u}=v\nabla_{ij}\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{u}\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}v-\frac{1}{u^{2}}\tilde{g}^{kl}u_{k}v_{l}\tilde{g}_{ij}.

Lemma 2.15
.

For
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the following identities hold.

(2.16)

\tilde{g}^{kl}u_{k}u_{l}=|\tilde{\nabla}u|^{2}=1-(\nu^{n+1})^{2},

\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}u=\tilde{h}_{ij}\nu^{n+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}x_{k}=\tilde{h}_{ij}\nu^{k},\quad k=1,\ldots,n,

(2.18)

(2.19)

\tilde{\nabla}_{ij}\nu^{n+1}=-\tilde{g}^{kl}(\nu^{n+1}\tilde{h}_{il}\tilde{h}_{kj}+u_{l}\tilde{\nabla}_{k}\tilde{h}_{ij}).

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth hypersurface in

satisfying equation (

,

\Sigma

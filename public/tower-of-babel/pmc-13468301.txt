Integrating Clinical Information and Electrocardiographic Signal Features to Develop a Prediction Model for Postoperative Delirium in Older Patients: A Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Cheng Wu, Hanwen Fan, Liangju Lei, Wenbin Dai, Xiaoxia Duan, Wei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposePostoperative delirium (POD) is a frequent and serious complication in older surgical patients. While conventional prediction models rely heavily on subjective clinical indicators, this study aimed to develop a simple, non-invasive preoperative risk prediction model by integrating objective physiological signals (electrocardiographic [ECG] features and heart rate variability [HRV]) with conventional demographic and clinical data.Patients and MethodsIn this single-center prospective observational study, 767 patients aged ≥65 years who underwent elective non-cardiac surgery were included. Preoperative clinical characteristics, conventional ECG abnormalities, and HRV parameters were extracted. Independent predictors were identified via multivariate logistic regression. A combined clinical–ECG/HRV model was developed, internally validated, and compared against single-domain baseline models.ResultsPOD occurred in 185 patients (24.1%). Advanced age, lower educational level, prolonged operative duration, non-sinus rhythm, ST-segment abnormalities, atrial/ventricular arrhythmias, and higher HRV fuzzy entropy were identified as independent risk factors for POD. The integrated clinical–ECG/HRV model achieved areas under the receiver-operating curves of 0.842 in the training set and 0.783 in the testing set, demonstrating higher predictive accuracy and improved risk reclassification compared with the baseline clinical model.ConclusionPreoperative ECG anomalies and increased HRV complexity (fuzzy entropy) are independently associated with an increased risk of POD. Integrating these objective, non-invasive physiological signals with routine clinical data yields a simple and practical prediction model. This multimodal approach enhances early preoperative risk stratification, providing valuable evidence to support comprehensive perioperative management for older surgical patients.

Introduction

Among the older surgical population, postoperative delirium (POD) is a highly prevalent and grave neuropsychiatric challenge in the perioperative phase. This syndrome manifests as rapid-onset fluctuations in awareness, compromised attention, and cognitive deficits.1 With the rapid aging of the global population, the proportion of patients aged ≥65 years who undergo surgical procedures has steadily increased.2 Consequently, the incidence of POD has increased, making it an important public health concern that affects perioperative outcomes in older adults.3 POD not only significantly prolongs hospital stay4 and increases healthcare resource utilization and hospitalization costs5 but also correlates strongly with elevated rates of nosocomial adverse events, long-term functional dependence, cognitive decline,6 and increased all-cause mortality.7 Therefore, proactive screening for POD susceptibility and the implementation of customized clinical protocols are vital for improving patient prognosis while distributing healthcare assets more effectively.

Recently, multivariate prediction models have been developed for the preoperative risk stratification of POD. Benjamin et al8 proposed a prognostic model for POD in patients undergoing elective non-cardiothoracic surgery, incorporating variables such as age, body mass index, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status class, history of delirium, medication use, and C-reactive protein level, resulting in improved performance. However, most existing models are primarily based on clinical and laboratory indicators and do not incorporate objective physiological signals that reflect the neurocardiovascular regulatory status. Consequently, the predictive performance of these models can be improved.

Heart rate variability (HRV) serves as a non-intrusive metric for the sympathovagal equilibrium, thereby reflecting autonomic homeostatic control and the physiological integrity of the brain–heart communication pathway.9 Within critical care and surgical environments, the emergence of POD is linked to a notable decline in HRV time-domain indices, such as the standard deviation of normal-to-normal intervals (SDNN) and the root mean square of successive differences (RMSSD), suggesting that decreased overall variability may indicate insufficient autonomic reserve.10 However, findings regarding traditional frequency-domain parameters, including low-frequency power (LF), high-frequency power (HF), and LF/HF ratio, remain inconsistent across studies, and their predictive value for POD is controversial.11

Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to examine POD development through a multifaceted lens that integrates patient clinical profiles, electrocardiography-derived metrics, and autonomic regulatory performance. We prospectively collected data pertaining to preoperative demographic characteristics, lipid and biochemical markers, surgery-related variables, conventional electrocardiographic findings, and HRV parameters derived from preoperative electrocardiogram (ECG) recordings of patients aged ≥65 years undergoing surgery. This study investigated the discrete links between these parameters and POD risk, along with identifying predictive HRV complexity metrics. Subsequently, we built benchmarked clinical, electrographic, and combined models to derive a straightforward, evidence-based preoperative screening tool. We hypothesized that integrating preoperative ECG-derived features and HRV parameters with conventional clinical variables would improve the prediction of postoperative delirium compared with models based solely on clinical information. Such a model may enhance the recognition of high-risk cases and inform the implementation of personalized clinical protocols.

Materials and Methods

Study Population and Design

In this study, we enrolled patients undergoing elective surgery at the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University between April 1, 2025, and April 1, 2026. The study population consisted of a prospective cohort. Eligibility criteria were: (1) age ≥ 65 years, (2) scheduled non-cardiac surgery (eg, gastrointestinal, orthopedic, and gynecological surgeries), (3) planned surgery under general anesthesia, and (4) intact preoperative cognitive capacity sufficient for effective communication Conversely, patients were excluded if they had (1) pre-existing dementia or significant neuropsychiatric disorders, (2) poor ECG trace fidelity for analysis, (3) incomplete postoperative delirium evaluations, or (4) perioperative mortality and study withdrawal. Ethical compliance followed the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, with formal approval granted by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (No. KY2025184). After registration with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500099777), written authorization was secured from all the subjects or proxies. To ensure confidentiality, the data were de-identified before processing. Sample Size Calculation

The required sample size was estimated before study initiation using an anticipated postoperative delirium incidence of 15% based on previous literature.2 Assuming a two-sided α level of 0.05 and a statistical power of 80%, a minimum sample size of 666 participants was required. Considering an anticipated 10% rate of incomplete follow-up or missing data, the target sample size was increased to approximately 740 participants.

Outcome Assessment

The initial 72-h postoperative window served as the observation period for our primary clinical endpoint, the development of POD. Trained researchers performed daily bedside assessments between 15:00 and 16:00 using the Chinese-validated 3-Minute Diagnostic Interview12 for CAM-defined Delirium (3D-CAM) as the primary diagnostic instrument for POD. Based on these diagnostic results, participants were stratified into either the POD cohort or the non-POD (NPOD) group.

ECG Acquisition and HRV Signal Processing

All patients underwent a standard 12-lead ECG examination at rest 1–3 days before surgery. Raw ECG signals were exported from the hospital ECG system and subjected to standardized pre-processing procedures, including baseline drift correction, bandpass filtering for noise reduction, and removal of abnormal rhythms. An automated R-wave detection algorithm was used to identify the RR interval sequences. Artifacts and abnormal beats were subsequently verified and manually corrected to ensure signal quality.

From the pre-processed RR interval sequences, HRV parameters were calculated in three domains: time-domain, frequency-domain, and nonlinear. Specific temporal indices consisted of SDNN along with RMSSD, whereas spectral analysis focused on LF, HF, and the resultant LF/HF balance. Furthermore, non-linear complexity markers, primarily sample entropy (SampEn), fuzzy entropy (FuzzyEn), and other complexity-related parameters, were calculated to quantify the physiological complexity and autonomic homeostatic control of heart rate fluctuations.13,14 In addition, conventional ECG morphological and rhythm features were extracted, including heart rate, rhythm type (sinus vs non-sinus), ST-segment abnormalities, atrial and ventricular arrhythmias (including both newly diagnosed and pre-existing chronic conditions), PR interval, QRS duration, and electrical axis, to comprehensively reflect the cardiac electrophysiological status. Before model construction, all continuous variables were standardized using Z-score normalization to minimize the influence of differences in the measurement scales.

Covariates

We extracted comprehensive perioperative datasets from digitized medical databases. Baseline characteristics (age, sex, education, body mass index, and lipid profile) and clinical history (stroke, heart disease, hypertension, and diabetes) were analyzed. Surgical and postoperative variables, such as ASA grade, operative time (defined as the duration from surgical incision to wound closure), complications, delayed awakening, emergency intensive care unit (ICU) transfer, and length of hospital stay, were also meticulously recorded for each subject.

Feature Selection and Model Development

To determine independent POD risk factors, we first utilized univariable logistic regression (P < 0.10) to identify potential covariates. These variables were subsequently subjected to a multivariate logistic regression analysis through stepwise selection. For complex ECG/HRV datasets, multicollinearity was mitigated by pruning variables with excessive VIF values. The final predictive model was established by balancing statistical performance with clinical interpretability. To evaluate the predictive value of different types of variables, three models were constructed: (1) clinical model (including only demographic and perioperative variables), (2) ECG/HRV model (including only ECG and HRV features), and (3) integrated clinical–ECG/HRV model.

To enable person-specific risk estimation, we formulated a nomogram using multivariable regression outcomes. The accuracy of the model was gauged by area under the receiver operating curve (AUC-ROC) metrics, whereas calibration was examined through graphical plots and goodness-of-fit testing. Clinical relevance was explored using decision curves. Furthermore, we determined the net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) to measure the added prognostic gains of ECG-derived data beyond the baseline clinical model. Finally, sensitivity tests, including re-estimating parameters in patients without arrhythmia, were conducted to verify the stability of our findings and the independent impact of the HRV indices.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), according to the results of the normality tests. Between-group comparisons were performed using the independent samples t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, as appropriate. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages and were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Logistic regression was used to calculate the odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using the RStudio software (version 2025.09.2; Build 418).

Results

Demographic and Clinical Characteristics

Our analysis included 767 subjects, with a median (IQR) age of 72 (68–76) years. Within this group, the majority were male (54%, n = 415). Among the enrolled participants, 185 developed POD, corresponding to an incidence rate of 24.12%. Delirious participants were characterized by increased chronological age and an elevated prevalence of ASA physical status III in comparison with the NPOD control (both P < 0.001). In addition, the total cholesterol (TC) (P = 0.042) and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels (P = 0.010) were significantly lower in the POD group. Model Development and Performance Evaluation

Based on the independent predictors identified using multivariate logistic regression, we grouped the predictors into two domains: basic clinical features (age, educational level, and operative duration) and ECG-derived features (non-sinus rhythm, ST-segment abnormalities, atrial or ventricular arrhythmias, and HRV_FuzzyEn). Three separate POD prediction models were constructed using multivariate logistic regression: a clinical model (clinical features only), ECG/HRV model (ECG and HRV features only), and integrated clinical–ECG/HRV model.

A comprehensive evaluation of the prognostic capability of each model was conducted. Within the training cohort, the AUC-ROC analysis revealed values of 0.6791, 0.7774, and 0.8423 for the clinical, ECG/HRV, and combined models, respectively. The AUC-ROC of the combined model differed significantly from that of the individual domain-specific models, as verified by DeLong’s test (P < 0.001). Furthermore, the integrated model retained discriminative ability in the independent validation cohort, with an AUC of 0.7832 (Figure 2A and B). Calibration plots showed agreement between the predicted and observed probabilities (Figure 2C). Sensitivity Analysis

Considering that non-sinus rhythms (eg, atrial fibrillation and other arrhythmias) may introduce “mathematical noise” into HRV calculations and interfere with the accurate estimation of entropy values, 21 patients with non-sinus rhythms were excluded from the sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Table S1). In the sensitivity model restricted to patients with sinus rhythm (n = 746), HRV_FuzzyEn remained an independent risk factor for POD (OR = 1.93; 95% CI: 1.13–3.32; P = 0.017), with a more precise estimate (narrower CI) compared to the main model.

To further explore the dynamic relationship between HRV_FuzzyEn and POD risk, a SHAP dependence plot was generated. The results showed a positive association between HRV_FuzzyEn and SHAP values for POD prediction. As HRV_FuzzyEn increased, its contribution to POD risk (SHAP value) also gradually increased. When HRV_FuzzyEn reached approximately 1.3–1.4, the SHAP value crossed the zero baseline, shifting from negative to positive. The color distribution of the scatter points further indicated that older patients tended to exhibit higher SHAP values, even at moderate HRV_FuzzyEn levels, suggesting that advanced age may amplify the neurocognitive risk associated with impaired autonomic regulation (Figure 3C).

Discussion

In this study, we developed a clinical–ECG/HRV–based prediction model for POD, which achieved an AUC of 0.8423. By integrating multidimensional perioperative information obtained from older patients undergoing surgery, including preoperative demographic characteristics, surgery-related variables, conventional ECG abnormalities, and HRV indices, we constructed a comprehensive predictive model that may facilitate the preoperative identification of patients at increased risk of POD.

In the present study, advanced age, lower educational level, prolonged operative duration, and multiple ECG abnormalities were significantly associated with POD occurrence, which is consistent with most previous reports. Extensive literature indicates that advanced age is a consistently robust predictor of delirium, potentially manifesting through the synergy between diminished cerebral capacity, heightened neuroimmune activation, and compromised homeostatic control.8 Large cohort studies have demonstrated that patients who develop POD are, on average, approximately 5 years older than those who do not.3 Educational level, often used as a surrogate indicator of cognitive reserve, exerts a protective effect in cognitive research.15 Furthermore, a longer operative duration often reflects greater surgical trauma, prolonged anesthetic exposure, and a heightened inflammatory response. Previous studies have reported that for every 30-min increase in operative duration, the risk of POD increases by approximately 6%, which is consistent with our findings.16

Beyond baseline demographics and surgical time, electrocardiographic markers, specifically non-sinus status, ST-segment changes, and cardiac arrhythmias, emerged as independent risk determinants. Although ventricular events and irregular rhythms displayed disproportionately elevated ORs, these findings must be interpreted with caution. The extremely high ORs (eg, OR > 10 for ventricular arrhythmias) and the correspondingly wide confidence intervals are likely attributable to statistical instability driven by the relatively small sample sizes within these specific arrhythmia subgroups. While the direction of the association is physiologically plausible and aligns with previous findings, the exact magnitude of the risk is likely overestimated in our cohort. Future studies with larger sample sizes of patients with specific arrhythmias are warranted to determine the precise effect size. Similarly, previous studies have reported that preoperative atrial fibrillation increases the incidence of POD, whereas pharmacological control of atrial fibrillation can reduce its occurrence.17 These findings suggest that the information obtained from a standard 12-lead ECG may be clinically relevant. ECG abnormalities can reflect underlying myocardial ischemia, fluctuations in cardiac output, or autonomic imbalance, which in turn may affect cerebral perfusion stability and increase vulnerability to delirium, thereby providing a novel perspective for POD risk identification.

Conventional time- and frequency-domain parameters have long been the focal points of HRV-related studies. However, a recent longitudinal observation revealed that an augmented HF peak actually predisposes patients to POD, in contrast to SD2/SD1 and DFA α1, which appear to function as protective markers against its occurrence.18 However, changes in HRV complexity may also be associated with POD. In our study, FuzzyEn, a nonlinear HRV complexity metric that quantifies the regularity of RR interval patterns, was independently associated with POD risk. Elevated FuzzyEn values may reflect a loss of structured autonomic modulation, consistent with the hypothesis that dysregulation of the brain–heart axis increases vulnerability to neurocognitive disturbances.11 These results indicate that once this critical cutoff is surpassed, augmented cardiac rhythmic intricacy starts to notably amplify delirium susceptibility among individuals receiving surgical treatment. In our study, the HRV complexity index, FuzzyEn, was identified as an independent predictor of POD. Notably, such alterations could be detected using short-duration preoperative ECG recordings, suggesting that HRV complexity may serve as an early and sensitive physiological indicator of neurofunctional vulnerability with promising clinical applicability.

Our results showed that the combined model achieved higher discrimination than either the clinical model or the ECG/HRV model. This performance leap, reflected in increased AUC values and significant NRI/IDI improvements, underscores the value of data fusion. Fundamentally, clinical variables quantify the baseline physiological reserve, whereas HRV and ECG indices serve as proxies for autonomic integrity and neurological-cardiac communication. Integrating these diverse perspectives allows the model to capture a broader spectrum of risk factors, reflecting the contemporary shift toward multimodal intelligence in clinical decision-support systems. Machine learning models based on HRV-derived ECG signals have demonstrated substantial value in the diagnosis and early detection of atrial fibrillation, hypoglycemia, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.19,20 Moreover, the combination of bioelectrical signals with circulating biomarkers has been proposed as a promising strategy for predicting and diagnosing POD.21 Our findings align with this emerging trend, demonstrating that incorporating objective physiological signals into conventional clinical assessments can significantly improve model performance. In addition, the sensitivity analysis excluding patients with non-sinus rhythm confirmed that HRV indices remained independently predictive, with an even stronger effect size, thereby strengthening the robustness and credibility of our results.

Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the high-dimensional HRV feature set combined with automated stepwise variable selection may increase the risk of model instability and overfitting. Although internal validation was performed, the absence of external validation may have resulted in optimistic performance estimates, and the model’s generalizability remains uncertain. Second, the single-center design may introduce selection bias and residual confounding. Third, preoperative HRV measurements represent only a snapshot of autonomic function and may not capture perioperative autonomic dynamics. Fourth, POD was assessed only once daily, which may have underestimated hypoactive or transient delirium episodes owing to its fluctuating nature. In addition, patients were not stratified by surgical invasiveness or procedure type, both of which may independently affect POD risk. Therefore, while our integrated model provides proof-of-concept for multimodal risk stratification, it should be considered an exploratory screening tool that requires further validation and refinement in larger multicenter cohorts with more rigorous monitoring protocols.

Conclusion

Overall, our findings established that baseline electrographic anomalies and increased HRV complexity serve as standalone risk determinants for postoperative delirium among older individuals. The integrated clinical–ECG/HRV model had a significantly improved predictive accuracy compared with models based on a single domain. These findings provide new evidence supporting the use of simple, non-invasive physiological signals for early risk stratification of POD and may contribute to the development of precise perioperative management strategies for older surgical patients.

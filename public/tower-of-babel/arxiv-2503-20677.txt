Curves on Hirzebruch surfaces and semistability
Alessio Cela, Carl Lian

Abstract
. When a ≥ 2 a\geq 2 , we show that a general pointed curve never interpolates through the expected number of points in the Hirzebruch surface ℋ a {\mathcal{H}}_{a} , with one exception. In the exceptional case, the number of such interpolating maps is determined by the geometric Tevelev degrees of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} , which have been previously computed.

Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the Deligne-Mumford moduli space of genus
g
,
n
-pointed curves. Let
(C,p_{1}\ldots,p_{n})
be a general, pointed curve of genus
g
, by which we mean a general point of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Let
X
be a smooth, projective variety. Can
C
be interpolated through
n
general points of
X
? If so, then in how many ways?

More precisely, let
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
be an effective curve class, and let
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be the moduli space of pointed maps to
X
in class
\beta
. Let

\tau:{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}

be the forgetful morphism. We assume that
2g-2+n>0
. One expects that
\tau
is generically finite whenever

(1)

\beta\cdot K_{X}^{\vee}=\dim(X)\cdot(n+g-1).

We assume (
1
) throughout.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that every irreducible component
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
dominating
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}
is generically smooth of the expected dimension. Then, the
geometric Tevelev degree

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
is by definition the degree of
\tau
.

If no such components
Z
exist, then
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}=0
vacuously. The name “geometric Tevelev degree” was introduced in
[
10
]
after a result of Tevelev
[
28
]
, but in fact, the corresponding
virtual
invariants go back to work on Vafa-Intriligator formulas, e.g.
[
3
,
27
,
22
]
. See
[
4
,
5
]
for more recent work on the virtual invariants. The geometric invariants, which are by definition enumerative counts as opposed to virtual intersection numbers, tend to be more subtle to compute, see
[
10
,
12
,
8
,
20
,
7
,
19
]
for recent computations and
[
21
,
2
]
for comparisons of virtual and geometric invariants.

We also say that
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
is
well-defined
if the hypothesis of Definition
1.1
holds. Given a dominating component
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
that is generically smooth of the expected dimension, a general curve interpolates through
n
general points in
X
via a map corresponding to a point of
Z
. Furthermore, this map admits no first-order deformations fixing
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
and the given points on
X
. If all dominating components
Z
are generically smooth of the expected dimension, then the total number of interpolating maps is given by
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
.

A basic obstruction to the existence of dominating components
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is that, if
f:C\to X
is a general point of
Z
, then
f^{*}T_{X}
must be semistable, see Lemma
2.2
. Heuristically speaking, if a curve on
X
is more prone to deform in some tangent directions on
X
than others, then it has insufficient room to interpolate through
n
general points when maximally constrained. In particular, if
T_{X}
is not
\beta
-semistable (see Definition
2.1
), then
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
, if well-defined, must equal zero. There is a large body of work on (semi)stability of vector bundles and their restrictions to curves, see for instance
[
13
,
26
,
24
,
25
,
15
,
1
,
11
,
16
,
18
,
17
]
.

In this note, we determine the geometric Tevelev degrees of Hirzebruch surfaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}(-a)\oplus\mathcal{O})
with
a\geq 2
. We first observe that, with one exception, the semistability obstruction is already enough to rule out the existence of any dominating components
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}({\mathcal{H}}_{a},\beta)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
a\geq 2
be an integer, and let
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}(-a)\oplus\mathcal{O})
be a Hirzebruch surface. Let
C_{0}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
denote the zero-section of
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
, corresponding to the map
\mathcal{O}(-a)\to\mathcal{O}(-a)\oplus\mathcal{O}
. Suppose that
n\geq 2
(or that
g=0
and
n\geq 3
), and that (
1
) holds.

Then, no irreducible component
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}({\mathcal{H}}_{a},\beta)
dominates
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}
under the map

\tau:{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}({\mathcal{H}}_{a},\beta)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times{\mathcal{H}}_{a}^{n},

unless
a=2
and
\beta=m[C_{0}]
is a multiple of the zero-section
C_{0}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
. In particular, outside of this case, we have
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{{\mathcal{H}}_{a}}_{g,n,\beta}=0
.

That is, when
a\geq 2
, unless
a=2
and
\beta=m[C_{0}]
, a general pointed curve admits no interpolations in class
\beta
through the maximal expected number of points. This leaves only one interesting case.

Theorem 1.3
.

We have

{\mathsf{Tev}}^{{\mathcal{H}}_{2}}_{g,n,m[C_{0}]}={\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,n,m}

for any
g,n,m
for which
n=2m-g+1\geq 3
.

The condition
n=2m-g+1
is (
1
) on both sides. If
2m-g+1<3
and
m>0
, then it follows from the Brill-Noether theorem that there are no maps from a general curve to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
in the required homology classes.

The geometric Tevelev degrees of
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
are therefore determined by those of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which have been computed in
[
10
,
12
,
8
]
. When
a=0
, the geometric Tevelev degrees of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
are trivially determined by those of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The most difficult case is
a=1
, in which
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\cong{\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
; some partial results are given in
[
7
]
. Genus 0 invariants of
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
in the log setting (with imposed tangencies) are computed in
[
6
]
.

2.
Semistability and interpolation

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
and
\beta\in H_{2}(X)
be an effective curve class. We say that a non-zero sub-bundle
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}
is
\beta
-destabilizing
if

\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot\beta}{\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{F})}>\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\beta}{\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})}.

We say that
\mathcal{E}
is
\beta
-semistable
if there does not exist a non-zero
\beta
-destabilizing sub-bundle.

If
X
is a curve, then we take implicitly
\beta=[X]
, in which case one recovers the standard notion of semistability.

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be a component dominating
{\mathcal{M}}_{g.n}\times X^{n}
. Assume that
Z
is generically smooth of the expected dimension, and that (
1
) holds. Then, for a general point
[f:C\to X]
of
Z
, the restricted tangent bundle
f^{*}T_{X}
is semistable.

Proof.

Let
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
be a general point of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, and let
\tau_{C}:{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)\to X^{n}
be the pullback of
\tau
over
[(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})]\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Let
x_{i}=f(p_{i})
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then, the differential of
\tau_{C}
at
[f]

(d\tau_{C})_{f}:H^{0}(C,f^{*}T_{X})\to(T_{x_{i}}X)^{n}

is an isomorphism.

Suppose that there exists a destabilizing subbundle
\mathcal{F}\subset f^{*}T_{X}
. The image subspace
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{0}(C,f^{*}T_{X})
has image of dimension at most
n\cdot\text{rk}(\mathcal{F})
, corresponding to the tangent vectors given by the subspaces
\mathcal{F}_{p_{i}}\subset T_{x_{i}}X
. On the other hand, by Riemann-Roch,

\displaystyle h^{0}(C,\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{C}c_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\text{rk}(\mathcal{F})(1-g)

\displaystyle>\frac{\text{rk}(\mathcal{F})}{\dim(X)}\int_{C}c_{1}(f^{*}T_{X})+\text{rk}(\mathcal{F})(1-g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{rk}(\mathcal{F})}{\dim(X)}\int_{X}c_{1}(T_{X})\cdot\beta+\text{rk}(\mathcal{F})(1-g)

\displaystyle=n\cdot\text{rk}(\mathcal{F}),

which is a contradiction.

\Diamond

In particular, if
\mathsf{Tev}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
is well-defined and non-zero, then
f^{*}T_{X}
must be semistable for all
[f]
enumerated by
\mathsf{Tev}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
. The tangent bundle
T_{X}
must also be
\beta
-semistable on
X
, or else a destabilizing sub-bundle would also be destabilizing upon pullback by a map
f:C\to X
enumerated by
\mathsf{Tev}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
. For example:

•

When
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
, the geometric Tevelev degrees
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{r}}_{g,n,d}
are fully determined in
[
19
]
, and can be checked to be non-zero. Therefore, the corresponding restricted tangent bundles
f^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}
are semistable. This had previously been obtained by E. Larson:
[
15
, Corollary 1.3]
implies that, for a general point
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
of the unique (Brill-Noether) component of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
dominating
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, the restricted tangent bundle
f^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}
is semistable whenever
\rho(g,r,d)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\geq 0
and
d
is divisible by
r
. The condition
\rho(g,r,d)\geq 0
is needed for such a dominating component to exist, and the condition that
d
is divisible by
r
is necessary for
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{r}}_{g,n,d}
to be well-defined, in order to apply Lemma
2.2
. Note also that, when
g=0
, the restricted tangent bundle
f^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}
can only be semistable when
d
is divisible by
r
, for arithmetic reasons.

•

When
X
is a hypersurface of degree at most
(r+4)/3
in
\mathbb{P}^{r+1}
and
\beta
is sufficiently large, then virtual Tevelev degrees of
X
are enumerative (and thus equal to the geometric degrees)
[
2
, Theorem 1.12(2)]
, and non-zero
[
4
, Theorem 1.4, Theorem 1.5]
. Therefore, for any general point of a component of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,d)
dominating
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n}
, the restricted tangent bundle
f^{*}T_{X}
is semistable for
f:C\to X
. While tangent bundles of smooth hypersurfaces are known to be semistable
[
23
, Theorem 2]
, the stronger statement for
restricted
tangent bundles seems not previously to have been known.

•

Calculations of
[
7
]
imply similar semistability statements for toric blow-ups of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. For example, the genus 0 counts
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{0,n,\beta}
for
X
equal to the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
at
r
points
[
7
, Theorem 12]
are manifestly non-zero for
\beta
in an appropriate range.

Lemma
2.2
may also be used in the opposite direction. It was once conjectured (e.g.
[
14
, Conjecture 0.1]
) that if
X
is a Fano manifold with Picard number
1
, then its tangent bundle is (semi)stable. This has been shown to be false in
[
14
, Theorem 0.4]
. In particular, geometric Tevelev degrees of such Fano manifolds must be zero (when well-defined).

3.
Hirzebruch surfaces

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}(-a)\oplus\mathcal{O})
be a Hirzebruch surface, with
a\geq 2
. Suppose that
Z\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}({\mathcal{H}}_{a},\beta)
is an irreducible component dominating the target under the morphism

\tau:{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}({\mathcal{H}}_{a},\beta)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times{\mathcal{H}}_{a}^{n}.

Suppose further that
n\geq 2
(or that
g=0
and
n\geq 3
). Then, the component
Z
is generically smooth of the expected dimension.

In particular, if (
1
) holds, then
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{{\mathcal{H}}_{a}}_{g,n,\beta}
is well-defined.

Proof.

Note that
\beta
necessarily has strictly positive anti-canonical degree on
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
. By standard results on Severi varieties of surfaces (see e.g.
[
9
, Proposition 2.4]
), a component
Z
can only fail to be generically smooth of the expected dimension if every point of
Z
parametrizes a map
f
whose image has anti-canonical degree at most 3. The only possible images of such maps are the fibers of
\pi:{\mathcal{H}}_{a}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the infinity-section. However, this is impossible if
f
is required to interpolate through 2 or more general points of
{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
.

\Diamond

We prove Theorem
1.2
by analyzing the instability of tangent bundles of Hirzebruch surfaces. We may as well work in a slightly more general setting. Let

X=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a_{s}))

be a
\mathbb{P}^{s}
-bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{r-s}
, where
0\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{s}
and
a_{s}>0
. Call
a=\sum_{i}a_{i}
. Let
\beta
be an effective curve class on
X
. We write

\beta=\ell((c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}(1)))^{\vee}+m((c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}(1)))^{\vee},

in the basis of
H_{2}(X)
dual to
H^{2}(X)
. A map
f:C\to X
in class
\beta
given by:

•

a line bundle
{\mathcal{M}}
on
C
of degree
m
, and a surjection
\mathcal{O}^{r-s+1}\to{\mathcal{M}}
, and

•

a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
C
of degree
\ell
, and a surjection
\mathcal{O}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes a_{s}}\to\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

In the notation above, the sub-bundle
T_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}\subset T_{X}
is
\beta
-destabilizing if

\frac{(s+1)\ell-am}{s}>\frac{(s+1)\ell+(-a+r-s+1)m}{r}.

In particular, in all of these cases, if the geometric Tevelev degree
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
is well-defined, then it is zero.

Proof.

We have
\beta\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}(1))=m
and
\beta\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}(1))=\ell
. In particular, we have
\beta\cdot T_{\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}=(r-s+1)m
. The relative Euler sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}\to[\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a_{s})]\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\to T_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}\to 0

shows that
\beta\cdot T_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r-s}}=(s+1)\ell-am
and
\beta\cdot T_{X}=(s+1)\ell+(-a+r-s+1)m
. The conclusion follows.

\Diamond

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Taking
s=1
and
r=2
in Lemma
3.2
and
X={\mathcal{H}}_{a}
, we have that
T_{{\mathcal{H}}_{a}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is destabilizing whenever
\ell>(1+\frac{a}{2})m
. On the other hand, if
\beta
is the class of an irreducible curve
f:C\to{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
, then
\ell>am
, unless:

•

\beta
is equal to
m
times the class of the zero-section
C_{0}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
, in which case
\ell=am
, or

•

\beta
is equal to
m
times the class of the infinity-section
C_{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
, in which case
\ell=0
.

Indeed, if
\ell\leq ma
, then in order for
\mathcal{O}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes a}\to\mathcal{L}
to be surjective, either
{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes a}\to\mathcal{L}
is an isomorphism, or
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{L}
is an isomorphism.

Therefore, when
a\geq 3
and
\beta
is the class of an irreducible curve
f:C\to{\mathcal{H}}_{a}

T_{{\mathcal{H}}_{a}}
is
\beta

\beta
is a multiple of
[C_{\infty}]
, and if
a=2
, then
T_{{\mathcal{H}}_{a}}
is
\beta
-unstable unless
\beta
is a multiple of
[C_{\infty}]

[C_{0}]
. However, if
\beta
is a multiple of

2.2
.

\Diamond

As another example of Lemma
3.2
, take
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
, which is the case

. Then, we have that
T_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r-1}}

2\ell-m>\frac{2\ell+(r-1)m}{r},

(2r-1)m<(2r-2)\ell

We proceed now to the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Fix a general pointed curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
of genus
g
. We will use the following standard results.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a line bundle on
C
, and suppose that
V\subset H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{M}})
is a base-point free subspace of dimension 2 (linear series of rank 1). Then, we have
H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2})=0
.

Proof.

This is the “base-point-free pencil trick.” The kernel of the multiplication map

V\otimes H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}^{-1})\to H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})

is
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes-2})\cong H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2})^{\vee}
. By the Gieseker-Petri theorem, this map is injective, so we conclude that
H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2})=0
.

\Diamond

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
W^{1}_{m}(C)\subset\mathsf{Pic}^{m}(C)
be the locus of line bundles
{\mathcal{M}}
with
h^{0}(C,{\mathcal{M}})\geq 2
, and let
{\mathcal{M}}\in W^{1}_{m}(C)
be a general point. Then, we have

H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2}(-p_{1}-\ldots-p_{2m-g+1}))=0.

Proof.

The Brill-Noether theorem implies that the general rank 1 linear series on
C
is base-point free. Thus, by Lemma
3.3
and Riemann-Roch, we have
H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2})=2m-g+1
for the general
{\mathcal{M}}\in W^{1}_{m}(C)
. Then, for general points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{2m-g+1}
, twisting
{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2}
down in succession decreases the dimension of the space of global sections. The Lemma follows.

\Diamond

Fix general points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Recall that
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,n,m}
enumerates the number of degree
m
covers

sending the
p_{i}\in C
to
y_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The assumption (
1
) becomes

; fix

satisfying these conditions. If

, there are no degree

, by the Brill-Noether theorem, so assume that

. Then, the geometric Tevelev degree
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,n,m}
is well-defined, and computed in
[
10
,
12
,
8
]
; we do not restate the formula here.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a line bundle on
C
underlying a map
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
enumerated by
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{g,n,m}
. Then, we have

H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2}(-p_{1}-\ldots-p_{n}))=0.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{m}(C,\mathbb{P}^{1})
be the moduli space of degree
m
maps
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and consider the evaluation map

{\text{ev}}:{\mathcal{M}}_{m}(C,\mathbb{P}^{1})\times C^{n}\to(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}\times C^{n}.

As

is pure of the expected dimension, and

has relative dimension 0. Thus, the locus on
{\mathcal{M}}_{m}(C,\mathbb{P}^{1})\times C^{n}
where the conclusion of Lemma
3.4

\Diamond

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

A map
u:C\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
in class
m[C_{0}]
is given by:

•

a line bundle
{\mathcal{M}}
on
C
of degree
m\geq 0
, and a surjective map
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus 2}\to{\mathcal{M}}
, and

•

a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
C
of degree
2m
, and a surjective map
{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{L}
.

Write
[u_{1}:u_{2}]
for the map
\mathcal{O}^{2}\to{\mathcal{M}}
and
[u_{0}:u_{3}]
for the map
{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{L}
; we regard
u_{0}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}\otimes{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes-2})
and
u_{3}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
.

Two 4-tuples
(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
yield the same map if and only if they differ by the action of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
given by
(t_{1},t_{2})\cdot(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=(t_{2}u_{0},t_{1}u_{1},t_{1}u_{2},t_{1}^{2}t_{2}u_{3})
. For such a map to contribute to
\mathsf{Tev}^{\mathcal{H}_{2}}_{g,n,m}
(
n>0
), it must be that
u_{0}\neq 0
. Thus, we have
\mathcal{L}\cong{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes 2}
, and that

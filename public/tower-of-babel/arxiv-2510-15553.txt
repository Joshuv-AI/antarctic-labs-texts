Vu’s conjecture holds for claw-free graphs
Linda Cook, Ross J. Kang, Eileen Robinson, Gabriëlle Zwaneveld

Abstract
Given a graph G G , let Δ 2 ​ ( G ) \Delta_{2}(G) denote the maximum number of neighbors any two distinct vertices of G G have in common. Vu (2002) proposed that, provided Δ 2 ​ ( G ) \Delta_{2}(G) is not too small as a proportion of the maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) of G G , the chromatic number of G G should never be too much larger than Δ 2 ​ ( G ) \Delta_{2}(G) . We make a first approach towards Vu’s conjecture from a structural graph theoretic point of view. We prove that, in the case where G G is claw-free, indeed the chromatic number of G G is at most Δ 2 ​ ( G ) + 3 \Delta_{2}(G)+3 . This is tight, as our bound is met with equality for the line graph of the Petersen graph. Moreover, we can prove this in terms of the more specific parameter that bounds the maximum number of neighbors any two endpoints of some edge of G G have in common. Our result may be viewed as a generalization of the classic bound of Vizing (1964) for edge-coloring. Keywords: claw-free graphs, chromatic number, codegree, Vu’s conjecture, edge-colouring MSC 2020: 05C15, 05C75, 05C35

Introduction

The

\codeg(v,w)

and

in

Our starting point is the following conjecture for the chromatic number
\chi(G)
of

in terms of its maximum codegree
\mcodeg(G)

[
40
]
from 2002; he actually suggested it for the stronger

Conjecture 1.1
.

Fix
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0

If
\mcodeg(G)\geq\varepsilon_{1}\Delta(G)
, then
\chi(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+\varepsilon_{2}\mcodeg(G)
, provided

is sufficiently large.

The lower bound hypothesis for
\mcodeg(G)
could potentially be weakened, but not to any bound that is
o(\Delta(G)/\log\Delta(G))
, due to random regular graphs (see
[
5
]
).
We have no reason yet to rule out an absolute constant, possibly even
3

\varepsilon_{2}\mcodeg(G)
in the bound.
To that end, below in
7.1
we propose a stronger form of this conjecture.
Despite its essential nature and its interesting implications for other problems such as the List Coloring Conjecture (see the discussion in
[
40
]
), there was no movement on Vu’s conjecture until recent partial progress employing probabilistic methodology
[
28
,
30
,
2
]
. In general, Conjecture
1.1
remains wide open.

Distinct to earlier efforts, we embark on a

graph theoretic investigation of

. To take our viewpoint, consider the case where
G
is the line graph
L(G_{0})
of some (simple) graph
G_{0}
.
By a straightforward case analysis, it is not difficult to see that the maximum codegree of

\mcodeg(G)\in\{\Delta(G_{0})-2,\Delta(G_{0})-1\}

\Delta(G_{0})\geq 3
. (Which of the two depends on whether a vertex in
G_{0}
of maximum degree is in a triangle.)
Recall by a classic theorem on edge-coloring due to Vizing
[

that for any (simple) graph
G_{0}
, the line graph of

satisfies

Thus Conjecture

holds in the restricted case of

being the line graph of some (simple) graph.
Note that this bound is tight for the line graph of the Petersen graph, which has maximum codegree

and chromatic number

Note also that a result of Kahn

of the Nibble Method, implies that the list chromatic number of any line graph

\chi_{\ell}(G)=(1+o(1))\mcodeg(G)
as
\mcodeg(G)\to\infty
.
Vu noted this as inspiration for the stronger list coloring version of Conjecture

A

is a copy of the star
K_{1,3}
as an induced subgraph and we say that

is

Claw-freeness is equivalent to the condition that no neighborhood contain an independent set of size three.
Every line graph is claw-free, a nontrivially strict inclusion. Before setting some wider context, here is our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
G
is claw-free, then
\chi(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+3
.

This confirms Conjecture
1.1
in the restricted case of
G
being a claw-free graph.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first specific confirmation of Conjecture
1.1
within a non-trivial hereditary graph class.
We remark that any

(1+o(1))\omega
also implies the conjecture for that class; we discuss more context for this in the conclusion.
One may interpret Theorem
1.2
as nearly a direct generalization of the bound in Vizing’s theorem.
(Analogously, Conjecture
1.1
can be seen as, prospectively and approximately, an even broader extension of Vizing’s.)
Just above, we noted how this bound is met for the line graph of, among others, the Petersen graph.

We briefly discuss some broader rationale for focusing on claw-free graphs in particular.
Almost half a century ago independently Minty

Over the decades, there have been further important attempts spanning combinatorial optimization and graph theory to extend results from the much more structured class of line graphs to the wider class of claw-free graphs.
Perhaps most notably are the efforts to better understand the so-called stable set polytope of claw-free graphs, originating in problems of Ben Rebea (see

One may view our work within this general line and, indeed, it relies heavily on claw-free structure theory.

We say that

Every line graph is quasi-line and every quasi-line graph is claw-free, both non-trivially strict inclusions.
This is an important intermediate class in claw-free structure.
As one of the milestones towards Theorem

Theorem 1.3
.

If

\chi(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+3

Note for comparison that

For

If

For disjoint

The

(resp.

The

A

is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. A clique of size

.
The

An

is a set of vertices such that no pair of vertices is adjacent. An independent set of size
3
is called a triad. The

of a graph

\alpha(G)
, is the size of a largest independent set in

A

in

of a graph

, is the size of a maximum matching in

A

A coloring is called

The

For any vertex

If we require that every list contain the same set of colors, this is the usual definition of coloring. Hence,

.

In a proper coloring of

\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)
.
Moreover, every color class forms an independent set, which implies
\chi(G)\geq\frac{|V(G)|}{\alpha(G)}
for all graphs

These independent sets are cliques in the complement graph
\overline{G}
. Hence, the chromatic number of

is the same as the

of
\overline{G}
. The clique cover number of a graph

, and so
\chi(G)=\overline{\chi}(\overline{G})
.

The

of a graph

, denoted

, is the graph where the vertices correspond to the edges of

are adjacent if and only if they correspond to edges sharing a vertex in

The class of line graphs can be characterized as the

-free graphs where

is a family of 9 graphs among which stands the claw,

.
A graph is a

if the closed neighborhood of any vertex is the union of two cliques. (Note that there can be edges between the two cliques.) Therefore, the class of quasi-line graphs clearly do not admit a claw as induced subset and form a proper subset of the claw-free graphs. Moreover, this class is a proper superset of the line graphs.

3
Defuzzification

Quasi-line graphs and claw-free graphs can be very “fuzzy”.
The aim of this section is to define what fuzziness is and to show that any critical graph for Property (
\star
) is not fuzzy. Thus it suffices to prove our main results for non-fuzzy quasi-line and claw-free graphs. We will warm up by dealing with two easy types of fuzziness, before handling the more complex ones.

A clique cutset in a connected graph
G
is a clique
K
such that
G\setminus K
is not connected. This type of fuzziness can be handled by a standard argument. We give a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
G
is vertex-critical for Property
(
\star
)
, then
G
contains no clique cutset.

Proof.

Suppose
G
is vertex-critical for Property (
\star
) and let
S
be a clique cutset in
G
.
Let
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{t}
be the vertex sets of the components of
G\setminus S
and let
G_{i}:=G[V_{i}\cup S]
for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,t\}
.
As
G_{i}
is a proper induced subgraph of the vertex-critical graph
G
,
\chi(G_{i})\leq\mcodeg(G_{i})+3\leq\mcodeg(G)+3
for all
i
.
Since
S
is a clique cutset,
\chi(G)=\max_{i}\chi(G_{i})\leq\mcodeg(G)+3
, a contradiction.
∎

Another simple but helpful result to clear more dense fuzziness is to show that a vertex-critical graph for Property (

Lemma 3.2

Suppose

3.2
Thickenings

Two distinct vertices

and

are said to be
twins
if
N[v]=N[w]
, and so
v
dominates
w
(and
w
dominates
v
).
In this subsection, we give a definition of thickenings (as given in
[
33
]
) and we show that if a graph is a strict thickening of some other graph, then it has either a homogeneous pair of cliques or a pair of twins.

Let
G_{0}
be a claw-free graph and let
M
be a matching in
G_{0}
such that
G_{0}-e
is claw-free for any
e\in M
.
The graph

is said to be a
thickening of

(under
M
)
if we can construct it from
G_{0}
as follows:

1.

Substitute each vertex
v
of
G_{0}
by some non-empty clique
I(v)
.

2.

For every
uv\in E(G_{0})
, add all edges between
I(u)
and
I(v)
.

3.

For every
uv\in M
, remove a nonempty proper subset of the edges between
I(u)
and
I(v)
.

A thickening is

if
|G_{0}|<|G|
.
For a set

, we use
I(S)
to denote
\bigcup_{v\in S}I(v)
.

We now prove that any strict thickening either has a pair of twins or a homogeneous pair of cliques in specific places.
This will then be used to show that strict thickenings do not appear in graphs which are either
\mathcal{G}_{cf}
- or
\mathcal{G}_{ql}
-critical for Property (
\star
).

Lemma 3.7
.

If
G
is a strict thickening of some graph
G_{0}
, then either
G
contains a pair of twins
a,b\in I(v)
for some
v\in V(G_{0})
or there exists some edge
vw\in M
such that
(I(v),I(w))
is a homogeneous pair of cliques.

Proof.

If
M=\emptyset
, then
G
arises from
G_{0}
by vertex multiplication. So for every
v\in V(G_{0})
, any two distinct vertices
a,b\in I(v)
is a pair of adjacent twins in
G
. The claim then follows from the fact that the thickening is strict.

Let
ab\in M
. By definition,
I(a),I(b)
are disjoint cliques of
G
.
Since
M
is a matching, no vertex of
G\setminus(I(a)\cup I(b))
is mixed on either
I(a)
or
I(b)
.
In order for there to be some nonempty proper subset of the edges between
I(a)
and
I(b)
, at least one of
I(a)
or
I(b)
must be a clique of size at least two. Hence,
(I(a)
,
I(b))
is a homogeneous pair of cliques in

.
∎

Since a strict thickening contains either a pair of twins (hence a vertex dominating another) or a homogeneous pair of cliques, the following is a consequence of

,
3.6
and
3.7
.

Corollary 3.8
.

If
G
is a
\mathcal{G}_{cf}
-critical or
\mathcal{G}_{ql}
-critical for Property
(
\star
)
, then

is not a strict thickening of some graph

.

We showed that if

4.1
Line graphs of multigraphs

Lemma 4.2
.

If
G
is the line graph of some multigraph

.

Proof.

First, observe that if

for all graphs

\chi(G)\leq\omega(G)+1\leq\mcodeg(G)+3
.

If

is not a simple graph, then let

be the underlying simple graph of

and let

, which is
(\mcodeg(G^{\prime})+3)
-colorable.
Note that
G^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of

, where
V(G)\setminus V(G^{\prime})
is the set of vertices representing the loops and parallel edges of

.
Therefore,
\mcodeg(G^{\prime})\leq\mcodeg(G)
and there exists
\varphi
, a
(\mcodeg(G)+3)
-coloring of

.

We are going to prove that

for any

to a
(\mcodeg(G)+3)
-coloring of

without an additional color. First, observe that edges sharing an endpoint in

form a clique in

. Therefore, if

. Now, if

represent parallel edges, then

and

Note that

satisfies the same result as in

by the same argument, conditioned on the truth of the (Weak) List Coloring Conjecture.

4.2
Circular interval graphs

Let
L
be a line, and let
\mathcal{I}
be a set of intervals of
L
.
A
linear interval graph
is a graph
G
where
V(G)
is a finite set of distinct points on
L
and
u,v\in V(G)
are adjacent if they are both contained in some interval of
\mathcal{I}
.
A
circular interval graph
is defined similarly, but we replace
L
with a circle
C
and we let
\mathcal{I}
be a set of arcs of
C
.
(Note that circular interval graphs are also sometimes called circular-arc graphs in the literature.)
We call
C
the underlying circle of
G
and when the situation is unambiguous we will call
C
the
cycle of
G
.
For convenience, when the situation is unambiguous we will conflate the intervals of
I\in C
with the sets of vertices of
G
that are contained in
I
.
The
size
of an interval will be the cardinality of the interval (as a set of vertices in
G
).
For any underlying cycle
C
we will implicitly assume we have fixed an orientation of
C
so that the directions “left” and “right” from a point on
C
are well-defined.
Let
v\in V(G)
and let the vertices of
G
be ordered as
v=v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\dots,v_{n},v_{0}
when moving from
v_{0}
rightwards along
C
(where
n
denotes the number of vertices in
G
).
Throughout this paper, we will assume without loss of generality that for every interval
I^{\prime}
such that it is contained in
I\in\mathcal{I}
that
I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
, and that for each vertex
v
in
G
there is an interval in
\mathcal{I}
containing only
v
.
The
rightmost interval of
v_{0}
is the largest interval in
\mathcal{I}
of the form
\{v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\}
and we denote it by
I^{R}_{v}
.
Similarly, the
leftmost interval of
v_{0}
largest interval in
\mathcal{I}
of the form
\{v_{n-i},\ldots,v_{n-1},v_{n},v_{0}\}
and we denote it by
I^{L}_{v}
.

By definition, the neighbors of
v
in
G
lie in
I^{R}_{v}\cup I^{L}_{v}
.
As every set of vertices lying in an interval of
\mathcal{I}
forms a clique, every interval contains at most
\mcodeg(G)+2
vertices of

. In particular,
|I^{L}_{v}|,|I^{R}_{v}|\leq\mcodeg(G)+2
. In other words, the left and right neighborhoods of

both form a clique of at most
\mcodeg(G)+1
vertices.

We prove that the negation of Property (
\star
) holds in a stronger form, in terms of the list chromatic number.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
G
is a circular interval graph, then
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+3
.

Proof.

For each
u\in V(G)
, let
L(u)
be the list of
\mcodeg(G)+3
colors associated to
u
.

Let
v
be the rightmost vertex of a longest interval
I
, so in particular
I_{v}^{L}=I
and
v
is the rightmost vertex in
I_{v}^{L}
. Let us color the vertices of
G
as they appear on the cycle starting from
v
and moving to the right. In particular, we end with the vertices in
I_{v}^{L}\setminus\{v\}
.

For every vertex
w
, we pick
\varphi(w)
to be the smallest color of
L(w)
which does not appear in their already colored neighborhood. We claim that such a color always exists.

Suppose first that
w\not\in I^{L}_{v}
. By our choice of
v
, the only neighbors of
w
that were colored before
w
are its left neighbors, i.e. in
I^{L}_{w}
.
However, as
I^{L}_{w}
is an interval, the vertices in it form a clique of size at most
\mcodeg(G)+2
. Hence, at most
\mcodeg(G)+1
neighbors of
w
were colored before
w
, and there is an available color in the list
L(w)
.

Suppose now that
w\in I^{L}_{v}
. In this case, there are neighbors to the right of
w
that were colored before
w
. Let
U_{w}=\left\{u\in I_{w}^{R}-I_{w}^{L}\mid u\text{ is already colored},u\neq v\right\}
be the right neighbors of
w
which were colored before
w
, excluding
v
. So if
u\in U_{w}
, then
u
lies to the right of
v
. Since every interval that extends to the right of
w
and that contains a vertex to the right of
v
, also contains
v
,
u\in N[v]\cap N[w]
. Hence, since
v
is the rightmost vertex in
I_{v}^{L}
, we have
u\in I_{v}^{R}-I_{v}^{L}
.
Therefore, the number of neighbors colored before
w
is a subset of
(I_{w}^{L}-\{w\})\cup(U_{w}+\{v\})
. By maximality of
I_{v}^{L}
, we have

|(I^{L}_{w}-\{w\})\cup(U_{w}+\{v\})|\leq|I^{L}_{w}|+|U_{w}|\leq|I^{L}_{v}|+|U_{w}|

Since
I^{L}_{v}\cap U_{w}=\emptyset
and all vertices in

lie in the closed neighborhoods of
v
and
w
, we obtain

|I^{L}_{v}|+|U_{w}|=|I^{L}_{v}\cup U_{w}|\leq|N[v]\cap N[w]|=\codeg(v,w)+2\leq\mcodeg(G)+2.

Hence, there are at most
\mcodeg(G)+2
neighbors of
w
colored before

, so there is at least one available color in

.
∎

The proof above shows that the list chromatic number of a circular interval graph

, a bound that is met with equality for cliques. As this result is not necessary for our main theorems and its proof is more technical,
we include it as

5.1
Graphs with no triad

Let

For readability, let us for the remainder of this subsection write

(1)

is a (possibly empty) matching
M
together with the set
X
of vertices unmatched by

.
Hence,

\overline{\chi}(H)=m(H)+(|V(H)|-2m(H))=|V(H)|-m(H).

Substituting this into
Eq.

, we see that it suffices to show that any triangle-free graph

satisfies

|V(H)|\leq m(H)+\macodeg(H)+2.

(2)

We start by proving a lower bound on the maximum anti-codegree of a triangle-free graph in terms of the number of vertices.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
H
is a triangle-free graph, then
\macodeg(H)\geq\frac{|V(H)|}{2}-2
.

Proof.

Let
n=|V(H)|
. The statement is trivial if
n\leq 4
.
We proceed by contradiction. Suppose that every pair of vertices
v,w\in V(H)
satisfies
\acodeg(v,w)\leq\frac{n}{2}-\frac{5}{2}
. Letting
vw\in E(H)
, then

|N(v)\cup N(w)|\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(n-2)-(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{5}{2})=\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $vw\notin E(H)$}\\
n-(\frac{n}{2}-\frac{5}{2})=\frac{n}{2}+\frac{5}{2}&\text{if $vw\in E(H)$}\\
\end{array}.\right.

Let
x
and
y
be two other vertices. Then
|N(x)\cup N(y)|\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rceil\geq 3
, so there exist two vertices
s
and
t
, where
s\in\{x,y\}
and
t\in[N(x)\cup N(y)]-\{v,w\}
such that
s
is adjacent to
t
. Then
|N(v)\cup N(w)|+|N(s)\cup N(t)|\geq n+5
. So five vertices of
G
appear in both of
N(v)\cup N(w)
and
N(s)\cup N(t)
, where
s,t,v
and
w
can also appear (multiple times) in these sets. Since each of these five vertices must be adjacent to
u
or
w
and be adjacent
s
or
t
, by the pigeonhole principle some two vertices must have two common neighbors in
v,w,s,t
.
Hence,
H
contains
C_{4}
as a subgraph.

Let
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
be a
C_{4}
in
H
. Then, since
H
is triangle-free and
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E(H)
for all
i=1,\ldots,4
(where
v_{5}=v_{1}
), we have that
N(v_{i})\cap N(v_{i+1})=\emptyset
. We arrive at a contradiction with

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\geq|N(v_{1})\cup N(v_{2})\cup N(v_{3})\cup N(v_{4})|=|N(v_{1})\cup N(v_{3})|+|N(v_{2})\cup N(v_{4})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=n+1.\qed

Observe that

satisfies
\macodeg(H)=\frac{n}{2}-2
so the bound proven in
5.1
is tight.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
H
is a triangle-free graph such that
2m(H)\leq|V(H)|-2
, then

|V(H)|\leq m(H)+\macodeg(H)+2.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximum matching in
H
and let
X
be the set of vertices unmatched by
M
. Then
X
is an independent set and
|M|=m(H)
. Moreover, as
2m(H)\leq|V(H)|-2
,

contains at least two vertices, say
v
and
w
.

We claim that at least one endpoint of any edge of
M
is a non-neighbor of both
v
and
w
. Indeed, suppose for a contradiction that there exists
ab
, an edge of
M
such that
a,b\in N(v)\cup N(w)
. As
H
is triangle-free,
a
and
b
are adjacent to distinct vertices in
\{v,w\}
. Then there is a matching of size

between
\{a,b\}
and
\{v,w\}
and replacing

by this matching in

leads to a larger matching in

, a contradiction.

Hence,
\acodeg(v,w)
takes into account at least one endpoint of every edge of
M
and every vertex of the independent set

, except for

and
w
.
The other endpoint of an edge in

is accounted for by

, thus every vertex is counted (at least once) in

as desired.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

If
G
is a graph such that
\alpha(G)\leq 2
, then
\chi(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+2
.
Equivalently, if
H
is triangle-free, then
\overline{\chi}(H)\leq\macodeg(H)+2
.

Proof.

As we argued at the beginning of the subsection, it suffices to establish
Eq.

2
where
H
is the (triangle-free) graph
\overline{G}
.
By
5.2
, we may assume
2m(H)\geq|V(H)|-1
.
Then we obtain using
5.1
that

m(H)+\macodeg(H)+2\geq\frac{|V(H)|}{2}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{|V(H)|}{2}-2+2=|V(H)|-\frac{1}{2}.

Since both expressions
m(H)+\mcodeg(H)+2
and

are integers, we obtain that also

m(H)+\macodeg(H)+2\geq|V(H)|
, as desired.
∎

5.2
Antiprismatic graphs

A graph is
antiprismatic
if it does not contain one of
\{K_{1,3},2K_{1}+K_{2},4K_{1}\}
as an induced subgraph. Such graphs are alternatively described by Chudnovsky and Seymour
[
13
,
14
]
through their complements, the prismatic graphs. A graph is
prismatic
if for every triangle
T
and vertex
v\notin T
,
v
is adjacent to precisely one vertex in
T
.
In this subsection, we give a bound on the clique covering number of the prismatic graphs in order to give a bound on the chromatic number of the antiprismatic graphs.
To do so, we show that we can reduce it to the case
\alpha\leq 2
.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
H
is a prismatic graph, then
\overline{\chi}(H)\leq\macodeg(H)+2
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a prismatic graph and let
\mathcal{T}
be a set of disjoint triangles of
H
such that
H-\mathcal{T}
is triangle-free.
We prove the statement by induction on
|\mathcal{T}|
.

The base case of induction is when
|\mathcal{T}|=0
. Then
H
itself is triangle-free and the result follows from
5.3
.

Suppose now that
H
is a prismatic graph such that
H-\mathcal{T}
is triangle-free with
|\mathcal{T}|\geq 1
.
Let
T\in\mathcal{T}
. Then
H^{\prime}:=H-T
is a prismatic graph, since the class of prismatic graphs is closed under taking induced subgraphs. Now
\mathcal{T^{\prime}}:=\mathcal{T}-T
is a set of triangles such that
H^{\prime}-\mathcal{T^{\prime}}
is triangle-free with
|\mathcal{T^{\prime}}|<|\mathcal{T}|
. Then
\overline{\chi}(H^{\prime})\leq\macodeg(H^{\prime})+2
by the induction hypothesis.
As
T
is a triangle, only one clique suffices to cover it. Hence,
\overline{\chi}(H)\leq\macodeg(H^{\prime})+1+2
.

Now we show that adding a triangle to a prismatic graph increases the maximum anticodegree by at least
1
. Let
u,v\in H^{\prime}
such that
\acodeg(u,v)=\macodeg(H^{\prime})
. Since
H
is a prismatic graph and
u,v\notin T
,
u
and
v
are both adjacent to exactly one vertex of
T
. Hence, there is at least one vertex of
T
which is neither adjacent to
u
nor to
v
. Thus
\macodeg(H^{\prime})+1=\acodeg^{H^{\prime}}(u,v)+1\leq\acodeg^{H}(u,v)\leq\macodeg(H)
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\overline{\chi}(H)\leq\macodeg(H^{\prime})+1+2\leq\macodeg(H)+2
, as promised.
∎

Considering the complement
G
of
H
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

If
G
is an antiprismatic graph, then
\chi(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+2
.

5.3
Three-cliqued graphs

Let
G
be a claw-free graph such that
\chi(\overline{G})\leq 3
. Then there exists a clique cover
(K_{1},K_{2},K_{3})
of

, and
G
is said to be

.
In this section, we show that no such

satisfies Property (
\star
) by proving a stronger result. We establish that the chromatic number is bounded from above by the maximum codegree of a pair of vertices in the same
K_{i}
,

plus 3. First, we show that this result holds when the graph has no triad by working in its triangle-free complement. Then we extend it to all graphs by showing that any three-cliqued vertex-critical graph for Property (
\star
) does not contain a triad.

We start with the following bound. This is tight for any
3
-coloring of
C_{5}
and for the
3
-coloring of
C_{6}
where diagonal vertices have the same color. The proof follows uses the same ideas as in
Section

5.1
, but here we need to bound the codegree of two vertices satisfying
c(u)=c(v)
. This extra constraint introduces complications which make the proof significantly longer.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
H
be a triangle-free graph that admits a proper
3
-coloring

. Then there exist two vertices
u,v
of

with

such that
\overline{\chi}(H)\leq\acodeg(u,v)+3
.

Proof.

Since
H
is triangle-free, any clique cover of

consists of cliques of size
1
and
2
. Just as in
Section

5.1
, we have

\overline{\chi}(H)=|V(H)|-m(H)
. So analogously to
Eq.

2
, we need to prove

|V(H)|\leq m(H)+\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v)+3.

(3)

Let
M
be a maximum matching of

and let
X
be the set of vertices unmatched by

. Then

is an independent set and

. We derive from this two useful inequalities equivalent to

3
. First,

\displaystyle 2m(H)+|X|\leq m(H)+\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v)+3

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle m(H)+\frac{|X|}{2}-3\leq\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v).

(4)

Second,

\displaystyle|V(H)|\leq\left\lceil\frac{|V(H)|-|X|}{2}\right\rceil+\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v)+3

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{|V(H)|+|X|}{2}\right\rfloor-3\leq\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\frac{|V(H)|+|X|}{2}-3.5\leq\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v),

(5)

where the last equivalence is trivially true if

is odd, but also holds if

is even, as the right-hand side is integral by definition.

Claim 1.
If there are two vertices

such that

, then

holds.

Suppose that there are two unmatched vertices
u,v
such that

. Let

be any edge in
M
. We claim that at least one of the vertices is a common anti-neighbor of

and

. Indeed, since

is triangle-free,

is adjacent to at most one vertex of

and

, we have without loss of generality that

Therefore,
\acodeg(u,v)
counts at least one endpoint of every edge of

. Moreover,
\acodeg(u,v)
accounts for all unmatched vertices except for

. The other endpoint of each edge in the matching is counted by

, which confirms

3
.

Therefore, we can assume that every maximum matching

leaves at most one vertex per color class unmatched. In particular,

. So to prove

, it is sufficient to prove the following:

\displaystyle\frac{|V(H)|}{2}-2\leq\max_{c(u)=c(v)}\acodeg(u,v).

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

7
Concluding remarks

Shortly after stating it, we hinted at a possible sharpening of
1.1
.

The following is stronger in two senses: first, we tighten the bound
\mcodeg(G)+\varepsilon_{2}\mcodeg(G)
in the larger codegree regime to align with Vizing’s theorem; second, we conjecture a bound in the smaller codegree regime too.

Conjecture 7.1
.

For some
C>0
,
\chi(G)\leq\max\{C\Delta(G)/\log\Delta(G),\ \mcodeg(G)+3\}
.

If true, the first term in the maximization would be sharp up to the choice of
C
due to random regular graphs, the other term due to certain line graphs.
We might dub this formulation —as well as its strengthening to bound
\chi_{\ell}(G)
instead of
\chi(G)
— the
Strong Vu Conjecture
.
The hypothetical transition between the two regimes represents an inherent mixture of probabilistic and structural graph theoretic considerations.
We note that
7.1
in the
\mcodeg(G)=O(\Delta(G)/\log\Delta(G))
regime would, if true, strengthen results in
[
2
,
5
]
.
Besides
1.1
and
7.1
, there are several other natural directions for further study.

First, let us call a claw-free graph
G

Vu-critical
if
\chi(G)=\mcodeg(G)+3
but
\chi(H)<\mcodeg(H)+3
for any induced subgraph
H
of
G
. What is the class of all Vu-critical graphs? Does it contain any graph that is not the line graph of a graph of class II?

Second, are there other hereditary graph classes within which we can bound the chromatic number of
G
by
(1+o(1))\mcodeg(G)
—apart from those where we derive the result as corollary to some analogous bound in terms of
\omega(G)
?
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class.
Recall that if we have established some bound of the form
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for all
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
, then we immediately also have a bound of the form
\chi(G)\leq f(\mcodeg(G)+2)
for all
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
.
We can thus disregard
\mathcal{G}
having only graphs satisfying
\chi(G)\leq\omega(G)+O(1)
(e.g.
[
3
]
).
As an aside, one might be lured into thinking of
\mathcal{G}
being all
K_{1,r}
-free graphs or, more restrictively, graphs all of whose neighborhoods are the union of
r-1
cliques,
r\geq 4
as the most natural next step, but we caution that this includes line graphs of linear

-uniform hypergraphs as a special case
[
36
]
.
We surmise that

being all bull-free graphs could be a good next target, particularly given their thorough investigation from a structural standpoint
[
6
,
7
,
11
]
.
Irrespective of resolving
1.1
for
\mathcal{G}
, it seems interesting to pursue bounds for the optimal

(if it exists) such that
\chi(G)\leq f_{\mathcal{G}}(\mcodeg(G))
for all
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
.
This would take a natural parallel to the study of
\chi
-boundedness
[
27
]
.

Third, it would be natural to extend
1.2
to list coloring, in the spirit of Vu’s original formulation of
1.1
.

Conjecture 7.2
.

If
G
is claw-free, then
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq\mcodeg(G)+3
.

This is essentially a generalization of the so-called Weak List Coloring Conjecture, which states for any simple graph that the list chromatic index is bounded by one plus the maximum degree.
Due to
1.2
,

is a special case of a conjecture of Gravier and Maffray
[

]
, which asserts that the list chromatic number coincides with the chromatic number in any claw-free graph.

Fourth, consider the
square

of a graph

connected by a two-edge path in

.

For some

G
being claw-free essentially reduces to
7.3
in the case of
G
being the line graph of a multigraph.

In the above suggested lines of further study, if we substituted
\Delta_{e}
(the codegree maximized over the endpoints of edges) in the place of
\mcodeg
, then we would obtain a few more interesting problems. In particular, the analogues of
1.1
,
7.1
,
7.2
, and
7.3
with
\Delta_{e}
in the place of
\mcodeg
may be worth investigation.

Appendix A
A better bound for circular interval graphs

Lemma A.1
.

Given a positive integer
k
, let
G
be a circular interval graph such that for every set of
k
consecutive vertices on the underlying circle of
G
there is precisely one interval of size
k
that contains it. Then
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+2
.

Proof.

If
G
is a complete graph on
n
vertices, then
\Delta_{e}(G)=n-2
and
\chi(G)=n
as desired.
Otherwise
k\leq\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
, and then
\Delta(G)=2k-2
and
\Delta_{e}(G)=2k-4
. By the list version of Brooks’ theorem
[
39
]
, the only graphs such that
\chi_{\ell}(G)>\Delta(G)
are odd cycles and complete graphs. Odd cycles satisfy
\Delta_{e}(G)=1
and
\chi(G)=3
. Hence,
\chi(G)\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+2
.
∎

For the next proof, we adopt some of the terminology for circular interval graphs that we introduced at the beginning of
Section

4.2
.

Lemma A.2
.

If
G
is a circular interval graph, then
\chi_{\ell}(G)\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+2
.

Proof.

Let
C
denote the circle and let
\mathcal{I}
denote the set of intervals of
C
corresponding to
G
. If for every vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
,
v_{i}
is the rightmost vertex of some longest interval in
\mathcal{I}
, then we conclude by Lemma
A.1
. Otherwise, there are two consecutive vertices (along
C
), without loss of generality let us say
v_{n},v_{1}\in V(G)
, such that
v_{1}
is the rightmost vertex of a longest interval in
I\in\mathcal{I}
but
v_{n}
is not the rightmost vertex of any longest interval in
I
.
We order the vertices
v=v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n},v_{1}
along the cycle
C
. We will use the notation
I_{i}^{L}:=I_{v_{i}}^{L}
and
I_{i}^{R}:=I_{v_{i}}^{R}
to improve readability.

We consider the vertices of
G
in the order starting at
v_{1}
and moving along
C
to the right. So we color first
v_{1}
, then
v_{2}
, then
v_{3}
and so on. For every vertex
v_{i}
, we (try to) pick
\varphi(v_{i})
to be the smallest color in their list that does not appear in their already colored neighborhood.

For all vertices
v_{i}\not\in I_{1}^{L}
, the only neighbors of
v_{i}
that are colored before
v_{i}
are the vertices in
I_{i}^{L}
. Hence,
v_{i}
has at most
I_{i}^{L}-1\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+1
already colored neighbors, and so we can color all such
v_{i}
.

For a vertex
v_{i}\in I_{1}^{L}
we denote by
A(v_{i})
the set of already colored neighbors of
v_{i}
and by
R(v_{i})
the set of already colored neighbors of
v_{i}
that lie to the right of
v_{i}
. In particular,
R(v_{i})
is of the form
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\}
for some
j
. The following claim follows from the fact that
|I^{L}_{1}|
is assumed to be a maximum size interval.

Claim 1.
For any
v_{i}\in I^{L}_{1}

|A(v_{i})|=|R(v_{i})|+|I_{i}^{L}|-1\leq|R(v_{i})|+|I_{1}^{L}|-1
.

We bound
|A(v_{i})|
by examining
R(v_{i})
more closely.
Recall that by our choice of
v_{1}
,
|I_{1}^{L}|=\omega(G)
.

Claim 2.
For any
v_{i}\in I^{L}_{1}
,

|R(v_{i})|\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+3-\omega(G)
.

Let
v_{i}\in I_{1}^{L}
. Then
v_{1}v_{i}\in E(G)
.
Note that if
R(v_{i})
is non-empty it must consist of the vertices corresponding to some interval starting at
v_{1}
and traversing
C

rightwards
.
So in particular all vertices in
R(v_{i})
besides
v_{1}
are common neighbors of
v_{1}
and
v_{i}
.
Note that since
v_{1}
is the rightmost vertex of
I_{1}^{L}
,
R(v_{i})\setminus\{v_{1}\}
and
I_{1}^{L}\setminus\{v_{1},v_{i}\}
are disjoint.
So in particular
\codeg(v_{1},v_{i})\geq|I_{1}^{L}|+|R(v_{i})|-3
.
So
|R(v_{i})|\leq\codeg(v_{1},v_{i})-|I_{1}^{L}|+3
and the first inequality of the claim follows.
This proves Claim 2.

By combining
Appendices

A
and
A
we obtain the following.

Claim 3.
For every
v_{i}\in I_{1}^{L}
,

|A(v_{i})|\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+3-|I_{1}^{L}|+|I_{i}^{L}|-1\leq\Delta_{e}(G)+2
.
If equality holds, then
|I_{i}^{L}|=\omega(G)
and
|R(v_{i})|=\Delta_{e}(G)+3-\omega(G)
, so that
R(v_{i})=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\Delta_{e}(G)+3-\omega(G)}\}
.

We now prove that this procedure has at most one vertex that we cannot color greedily.

Claim 4.
In this procedure, there is at most one vertex
v_{i}
that has at least
\Delta_{e}(G)+2
neighbors that are colored before
v_{i}
. Moreover,
v_{i}
has exactly
\Delta_{e}(G)+2
neighbors that are colored before it.

Suppose for a contradiction that
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
have this property where
i<j
.
Then
v_{i},v_{j}\in I_{1}^{L}
and by
Appendix

A
,
|I_{1}^{L}|=|I_{i}^{L}|=|I_{j}^{L}|=\omega(G)
, and
v_{1},v_{i},v_{j}
are all pairwise adjacent and occur in this order when traversing rightwards along
C
from
v_{1}
.
Then, since
G
is a circular interval graph,
R(v_{i})\subseteq R(v_{j})
.
Since
v_{i},v_{j}
are both in
I_{1}^{L}
and
|I_{i}^{L}|=|I_{j}^{L}|=\omega(G)
, it
follows that
v_{i-1}
is adjacent to both
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
.
So in particular, the common neighbors of
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
include
I_{1}^{L}\setminus\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
and
R(v_{j})
and
\{v_{i-1}\}
.
Since these sets are pairwise disjoint, we obtain that
\codeg(v_{i},v_{j})\geq\Delta_{e}(G)+1
, a contradiction since
v_{i},v_{j}
are adjacent.
This proves Claim 4.

We may assume that
v_{i}\in I_{1}^{L}
is the unique uncolored vertex with
\Delta_{e}(G)+2
colored neighbors.
Let
v_{i-1}
be the left neighbor of
v_{i}
. If
v_{i-1}
has at most
\Delta_{e}(G)
neighbors colored before it, we can switch the order of
v_{i-1}
and
v_{i}
so they both have at most
\Delta_{e}(G)+1
forbidden colors when we color it. Moreover, since
v_{i-1}
is the left neighbor of
v_{i}
, we have for every vertex
v_{j}\not\in\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}
that the set of already colored neighbors of
v_{j}
is still the set
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j-1}\}
.
So in particular, the arguments of the above claims still hold.
Hence, we can color all vertices as desired.

So assume that
v_{i-1}
has
\Delta_{e}(G)+1
neighbors colored before it.
Since
\codeg(v_{i-1},v_{i})\leq\Delta_{e}(G)
,
v_{i-1}
has at least one colored neighbor that is not a neighbor of
v_{i}
.
Hence,
|I_{i-1}^{L}|=|I_{i}^{L}|=|I_{1}^{L}|
and
v_{i-1}
is the rightmost vertex of
|I_{i-1}^{L}|
.
Moreover,
v_{i-1}
has at least
\Delta_{e}(G)+1-(|I_{i-1}^{L}|-1)=\Delta_{e}(G)+2-|I_{1}^{L}|
neighbors in
I^{R}_{i-1}
that are colored before
v_{i-1}
. So
R(v_{i})\supseteq\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\Delta_{e}(G)+2-|I_{1}^{L}|}\}
. Hence,
\codeg(v_{i-1},v_{i})\geq(|I_{i}^{L}|-2)+(\Delta_{e}(G)+2-|I_{1}^{L}|)=\Delta_{e}(G)
. In particular,
v_{i-1}
and
v_{i}
have no other common neighbors.
This implies that there are no vertices in the interval
\{v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
, and so
v_{i}=v_{n}
. This contradicts the definition of
v_{1}
, as its left neighbor
v_{n}
is the rightmost vertex of the interval
I_{n}^{L}
of maximum length.
∎

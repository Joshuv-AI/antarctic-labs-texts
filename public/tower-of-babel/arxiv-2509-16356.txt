Central values of Asai L-functions and twisted Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture: relative trace formulas
Weixiao Lu, Danielle Wang, Zhiyu Zhang

Abstract
. We study certain new relative trace formulas on (non-reductive) period integrals involving Weil representations, in the context of the relative Langlands program. We study normal representatives using Galois theory, and establish geometric decompositions of relative trace formulas using normal representatives for good test functions. By comparing global representatives, local distributions and orbital integrals, we prove the twisted Gan–Gross–Prasad (GGP) conjecture on Asai L-functions, in any dimension under some local assumptions, allowing ramifications of number fields.

(2)

A key point is that the non-vanishing of period integrals is often equivalent to the non-vanishing of a spectral distribution of test functions. On the one hand, this distribution can often be packaged together (over nice automorphic representations) into a relative trace formula, with geometric meaning and a decomposition into sums of products of local orbital integrals of test functions. Then we may establish a geometric comparison of two relative trace formulas, by solving local harmonic analysis questions on comparison of orbital integrals, e.g. fundamental lemma and smooth transfer, and global questions on automorphic spectrum. On the other hand, this distribution often admits a spectral decomposition into products of local relative characters of test functions, and Langlands functoriality predicts a relation between automorphic representations on different groups. Thus by proving a comparison of geometric decompositions, we deduce the GGP conjecture under some local assumptions. And comparisons of local relative characters and the local GGP conjecture lead to refined equalities between period integrals and L-functions.

1.1.
Relative trace formulas in polarized reductive cases

A good way to study the non-vanishing of period integrals is via relative trace formulas (RTF). Let us recall the classical set up. For simplicity, we will ignore any convergence and rationality issue. Let
G
be a reductive group over
F
. Let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{F}
be the ring of adeles. Denote by
[G]=G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})
the adelic quotient with suitable Haar measure.

In the polarized reductive case, we consider a smooth affine variety
X=G/H
where
H\leq G
a reductive subgroup over
F
. Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
G(\mathbb{A})
. We define the period integral
P_{X}(\varphi)=\int_{[G]}\varphi(h)dh
for
\varphi\in\pi
. Consider the relative trace formula

\mathrm{RTF}_{X}(f):=\int_{[H]\times[H]}K_{f}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2},\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(G(\mathbb{A})).

Here
K_{f}(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in G(F)}f(x^{-1}\gamma y)
is the kernel function on
[G]\times[G]
for the right translation action
R(f)
on the spectrum
L^{2}([G])
. When the center of
G
is anistropic, the kernel function for
R(f)
on
\pi\subseteq L^{2}_{\mathrm{cusp}}([G])
is

K_{f,\pi}(x,y)=\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{OB}(\pi)}(R(f)\phi(x))\overline{\phi(y)}

where
\mathrm{OB}(\pi)
is an orthonormal basis of
\pi
. Then

\mathrm{RTF}_{X,\pi}(f):=\int_{[H]\times[H]}K_{f,\pi}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}=\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{OB}(\pi)}P_{X}(R(f)\phi)\overline{P_{X}(\phi)}

is directly related to the non-vanishing of
P_{X}
on
\pi
, and appear as a direct summand of
\mathrm{RTF}_{X}(f)
.

The key for the relative trace formula approach is a geometric decomposition of the relative trace formula into orbital integrals (for a good test function
f
with regular support)

\mathrm{RTF}_{X}(f)=\sum_{\gamma\in[H(F)\backslash G(F)/H(F)]_{rs}}\Vol([(H\times H)_{\gamma}])\Orb(\gamma,f)

where we consider orbits and orbital integrals for the natural action of
H\times H
on
G
. And
\Vol([(H\times H)_{\gamma}])
is the volume of adelic quotient of the stabilizer
(H\times H)_{\gamma}
over
F
. When
f=\otimes_{v}f_{v}
is a pure tensor and the stabilizer of
\gamma
is trivial, we have a decomposition:

\Orb(\gamma,f)=\prod_{v}\Orb_{v}(\gamma,f_{v})

where
v
runs over all places of
F
,
\Orb_{v}(\gamma,f_{v})=\int_{(H\times H)(F_{v})}f_{v}(h_{1}^{-1}\gamma h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}
is the local orbital integral at
v
. By local comparison of orbital integrals (via fundamental lemmas and smooth transfers) for
(G,X)
and
(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})
, we can compare two relative trace formulas and transfer period integrals from
G
to
G^{\prime}
, hence deduce numerical relations of
L
-functions and period integrals for
(G,X)
from
(G^{\prime},X^{\prime})
. A good example is the original proof of the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for unitary groups by W. Zhang
[
38
,
39
]
under certain local assumptions.

However, a general study of relative trace formulas for non-reductive period integrals (e.g., those involving Weil representations) is unclear. For the Fourier–Jacobi GGP conjecture,
[
9
]
uses RTFs to compare with Rankin-Selberg L-functions, where the orbital integrals could be naturally defined. However, the essential non-splitting feature makes twisted GGP conjecture
[
13
]
and Asai L-functions more mysterious and difficult, which is a major motivation of this paper. For comparison, we discover and utilize more complicated geometric decompositions.

1.3.2.
Comparison of representatives, orbital integrals and relative trace formulas

We compare the geometric sides of two relative trace formulas. The comparison is via a base change from unitary groups to general linear groups (for the quadratic extension
L/K
) using Flicker-Rallis periods. We relate the base change of the Weil representation of
H(\mathbb{A})
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{A}))
to the Weil representation of
H_{1}(\mathbb{A})
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{E}^{n})
. Another subtle point is that we need to choose compatible normal representatives with sufficiently nice local behaviors, which is done in Section
2

Then we reduce the comparison to local comparisons of orbital integrals, namely, fundamental lemmas and the existence of smooth transfers at a place
v
of
F
.

(1)

If
v
is archimedean, by assumption
v
is split in
K
and we apply Xue’s (partial) smooth transfer theorem.

(2)

If
v
is split in
K
, we do reductions to prove fundamental lemmas and smooth transfer via the Fourier–Jacobi case
[
35
]
.

(3)

If
v
is inert in
K
, by assumption
v
is either split or inert in
E

v
is split in
E

v
is inert in
E

[

]
.

(4)

If
v
is ramified in
K
, then by assumption

is split in
F

Then by choosing good test functions and using the local twisted GGP conjecture
[

1.4.
Further applications and relative Langlands program

We mention some potential applications of our results and methods.

(1)

It is important to prove the existence of smooth transfers for all test functions at any place, which would in particular remove local assumptions (1)(3) in Theorem
1.2.1
.

(2)

Period integrals involving Weil representations provide a large class of new examples, and our geometric decomposition method shall be useful for other examples, as in
[
3
,
28
]
for split groups. Following
[
28
]
, we may formulate variants for unitary groups and more GGP type conjectures on period integrals and L-functions. It would be interesting to study similar geometric decompositions of relative trace formulas. Besides Whittaker and non-reductive cases, period integrals in
[
3
,
28
]
are constructed from a tuple
(G,H,\rho,0)
: here
H\leq G
is a reductive subgroup and
\rho:H\to\mathrm{Sp}(V)
is a symplectic representation which induces a Weil representation
\omega
of
H
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{A}))
where
\mathbb{L}
is a Langragian of
V
. The Weil representation
\omega
is independent of the choice of Lagrangian.
For
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{A}))
, we have the
\rho
-theta series on
[H]

\Theta_{\mathbb{L}}(h,\phi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{L}(F)}\omega(h)\phi(x).

For a cuspidal automorphic form
\varphi
on
G(\mathbb{A})
, consider its period integral (we use complex conjugation following
[
13
, Section 2.2]
)

P_{(G,H,\rho)}(\varphi,\phi)=\int_{[H]}\varphi(h)\overline{\Theta_{\mathbb{L}}(h,\phi)}dh.

The relative trace formula on
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(\mathbb{A})),\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{A}))
is

\mathrm{RTF}_{(G,H,\rho)}(f,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):=\int_{[H]\times[H]}\sum_{\gamma\in G(F)}f(h_{1}^{-1}\gamma h_{2})\overline{\Theta_{\mathbb{L}}(h_{1},\phi_{1})}\Theta_{\mathbb{L}}(h_{2},\phi_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2},

which is related to period integrals via the spectral decomposition:

\mathrm{RTF}_{(G,H,\rho)}(f,\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\sum_{\pi}\sum_{\varphi\in OB(\pi)}P_{(G,H,\rho)}(R(f)\varphi,\phi_{1})\overline{P_{(G,H,\rho)}(\varphi,\overline{\phi}_{2})}.

However, the geometric decomposition into orbital integrals is non-trivial. Similarly, consider a pair
(G^{\prime},H_{1},\rho_{1})
and a reductive subgroup
H_{2}\leq G^{\prime}
and form a relative trace formula

\mathrm{RTF}_{(G^{\prime},H_{1},\rho_{1},H_{2})}(f^{\prime},\phi_{1}^{\prime}):=\int_{[H_{1}]\times[H_{2}]}\sum_{\gamma\in G(F)}f^{\prime}(h_{1}^{-1}\gamma h_{2})\Theta_{\mathbb{L}^{\prime}}(h_{1},\phi_{1}^{\prime})dh_{1}dh_{2}.

Again, the geometric decomposition into orbital integrals is non-trivial.

(3)

Moreover, our theorems have applications to arithmetic analogs of twisted GGP conjectures and the study of Asai motives, see e.g.
[
42
]
where the twisted AFL is proved. In the case
n=2
, the arithmetic conjecture is related to twisted triple product formula of Ichino and its arithmetic analogs.

Finally, we give a summary of our paper. In Section
2
, we develop the theory of normal representatives. In Section
3
, we give relative trace formulas and decompositions into orbital integrals under the choice of representatives. In Section
4
, we establish matching of local orbital integrals. In Section
5
, we match relative trace formulas using results in previous sections. In Section
6

2.
Normal representatives

In this section, we deal with the existence of normal representatives on regular semisimple orbits (Definition
2.2.6
and
2.3.10
). The main result is Proposition
2.3.8
. In Section
2.4
, we discuss the matching of regular semisimple representatives.

On the unitary side, we have a symmetric pair
H\leq G
with involution
\theta
. We know that

(2.0.1)

Given
\zeta\in G
, if the centralizer
C_{G}(\theta(\zeta^{-1})\zeta)
of
\theta(\zeta^{-1})\zeta
in

is a torus and

, then
H\times H
-stabilizer of

is in the diagonal subgroup

Indeed, under the assumption, we have

. Note that

for

On the general linear side, for two subgroups

of

such that its stabilizer in

is contained in certain subgroup

so there is a natural action of

on Weil representation for

through partial Fourier transforms.

2.1.
General formulation

Consider the action of a group
T
on a set
S
, with a subgroup
T_{0}\leq T
. Our interest is on the locus of

such that
\mathrm{Stab}_{T}(x)\leq T_{0}
, which is stable under the action of
T_{0}
. We call such

a
T_{0}
-good element in

. The following lemma is clear.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Consider the action of a group
T
on a set
\widetilde{S}
. Let
S\subseteq\widetilde{S}
be a subset, and
H=\mathrm{Stab}(S)\leq T
. Assume that

(1)

For any

and

.

(2)

If

for some

, then there exists

such that

.

Then the natural inclusion
H\backslash S\to G\backslash\widetilde{S}
is a bijection.

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group over a field

.
Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be commuting
involutions of
G
,
and let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be the subgroups of

by
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, respectively.
Consider the right action of
H_{1}\times H_{2}

on

given by

\xi.(h_{1},h_{2})=h_{1}^{-1}\xi h_{2}.

We have a
H_{1}
-equivariant embedding

G/H_{2}\hookrightarrow G,\,\,\xi\mapsto\gamma=\xi\theta_{2}(\xi)^{-1}

whose image lies in
G^{\theta_{2}=(-)^{-1}}:=\{\gamma\in G:\gamma\theta_{2}(\gamma)=1\}
. Here

acts on
\gamma\in G^{\theta_{2}=(-)^{-1}}
by

\gamma.h_{1}=h_{1}^{-1}\gamma\theta_{2}(h_{1}).

Definition 2.1.2
.

An element
\gamma\in G^{\theta_{2}=(-)^{-1}}
is

\theta_{1}\theta_{2}
-normal
if

\gamma
commutes with
\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(\gamma)=\theta_{1}(\gamma)^{-1}
.

Let
\delta=\gamma\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(\gamma)=\gamma\theta_{1}(\gamma)^{-1}\in G
. Then

\theta_{1}(\delta)\delta=id.

Lemma 2.1.3
.

(1)

An element
\gamma
is normal iff
\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(\delta)=\delta
.

(2)

If
\gamma
is
\theta_{1}\theta_{2}
-normal and

is a

, then
\Stab_{H_{1}}(\gamma)\subseteq H_{1}^{\theta_{2}=\mathrm{id}}
.

In particular, if
\delta
is regular semisimple and

is a torus and

is
\theta_{1}\theta_{2}
-normal, then
\delta
is good.

Proof.

The first part follows from the definition. Suppose

satisfies
h^{-1}\gamma\theta_{2}(h)=\gamma
. Then

\theta_{2}(h)^{-1}\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(\gamma)h=\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(\gamma),

so

.
By assumption

\gamma\in C_{G}(\gamma\theta_{1}\theta_{2}(\gamma))

as well, so
\gamma
and

commute.
This combined with

implies that

as desired.
∎

We may hope that the

-orbit of a given element

contains a

-normal element. This is not true in general. In the next two subsections, we will prove related results in our cases by Lie algebra methods.

Every

Proof.

We want to show that there exists

It is clear that
\delta
is an element of
\textnormal{S}_{n,L/E}

Let
\alpha\in M

\alpha

\delta

Note that

Since

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(\sigma_{L/M}(\delta))

\displaystyle=-(\alpha+\sigma_{L/M}(\delta))(\alpha-\sigma_{L/M}(\delta))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{L/M}(-(\alpha+\delta)(\alpha-\delta)^{-1})=\sigma_{L/M}(c_{\alpha}(\delta)).

Combining the previous two displayed equations, we obtain

Since

(cf.
[

By Proposition

By (

Let

This is an

We have an isomorphism

Indeed, this map is injective because every element

is of the

g_{1}\otimes 1+g_{2}\otimes j_{E}

.
It is surjective because

where we have used the fact that

,
and the middle equality holds since

.
Then

\sigma_{E/F}(\gamma\sigma_{E/F}(g)\gamma^{-1})=\sigma_{L/K}(\gamma\sigma_{E/F}(g)\gamma^{-1})=\gamma^{-1}g\gamma,

so

\gamma\sigma_{E/F}(g+\gamma\sigma_{E/F}(g)\gamma^{-1})=\gamma(\sigma_{E/F}(g)+\gamma^{-1}g\gamma)=\gamma\sigma_{E/F}(g)+g\gamma=(g+\gamma\sigma_{E/F}\gamma^{-1})\gamma,

Similarly, we find

j_{E}(g-\gamma\sigma_{E/F}(g)\gamma^{-1})\in\mathfrak{t}_{\gamma}(F),

so the map
g\otimes e\mapsto ge
is surjective since

Under the isomorphism

Then by Hilbert’s Theorem 90

[

5.3.

We first state a lemma (an exercise in real analysis).

Lemma 5.3.1
.

Let

The lemma formally says that the valuation functionals

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we show that

Then pick a neighborhood

A more conceptual proof: it is easy to check that for a Borel set

. In particular,

.

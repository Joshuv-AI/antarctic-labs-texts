Intersection complex of any threefold as a Chow motive
Shruti Rastogi, Vaibhav Vaish

Abstract
. Motivated by the characterization of the intersection complex in terms of S.Morel’s weight truncations, we introduced an object E ​ M X F EM^{F}_{X} in the setting of motivic sheaves for certain schemes X X and weight profiles F F . In this article, we show that when X X is any threefold, this object satisfies Wildeshaus’s characterization of a motivic intersection complex. In particular, we demonstrate that the construction is a suitably functorial Chow motive lifting the motivic intersection complex for an arbitrary threefold.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

In the 1960s Grothendieck envisioned the theory of motives as a unifying framework for various (Weil) cohomology theories. As further enhanced and articulated by Beilinson
[
11

One pathway to constructing the category of mixed motives is to first construct its derived category

Due to work of M. Hanamura

(here

of Ayoub

or, alternatively, the Beilinson motives,

1.2.

The heart of the motivic t-structure is expected to be a compactly generated abelian category, with compact objects that are both Noetherian and Artinian. So, another approach to study the conjectural abelian category of mixed motives is via its simple objects. These simple objects are expected to realize to intersection complexes
IC(Y,\mathcal{L})
, where
Y\subset X
is closed and
\mathcal{L}
is a local system defined generically on
Y
which can even be taken to be in mixed categories.

A natural and interesting question is whether these intersection complexes

admit lifts to the motivic category
DM(X,\mathbb{Q})
. These expected lifts are known as motivic intersection complexes and have been intrinsically characterized by Wildeshaus in
[
45
]

[
47
]
, Wildeshaus also introduced a slightly weaker notion, in which the motive is unique only up to an isomorphism. Although this version is weaker than the canonical one, it has the advantage of existing unconditionally in several important cases - for instance, for
the Baily-Borel compactification of Shimura varieties.

In

and demonstrated that in case the variety is (the Bailey-Borel compactification of) a Shimura variety, then the motive so constructed is the correct object for the motivic intersection complex and in particular a Chow motive. This left open the question of what happens for threefolds that are not Shimura varieties.

This article aims to address this gap and, using different techniques, we show that for

Now we briefly motivate the method used to prove Theorem

Having done so, we are now in a position to apply weight conservativity. Our goal is to determine the weight of

1.7.

The proof of part
(ii)
is fairly straightforward. We begin by recalling how the object
EM^{F}_{X}
was defined. In
[
41
]
, Vaish constructed Morel’s t-structure in the relative situation using the punctual gluing of t-structures. In particular, it was shown that for certain monotone step functions
F:\{0,\cdots,\dim X\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a t-structure, with truncation functors denoted as
(w_{\leq F},w_{>F})
, on appropriate subcategories of

, whose realization corresponds to Morel’s t-structure associated with

in the category of mixed sheaves. In our case, where

is a threefold and
F=\{3\mapsto 3,2\mapsto 3,1\mapsto 2,0\mapsto 2\}
, this construction yields a t-structure on the subcategory
DM^{coh}_{3,dom}(X)\subset DM^{coh}(X)\subset DM(X)
. Here
DM^{coh}(X)
is the category of cohomological motives and the subcategory
DM^{coh}_{3,dom}(X)
is obtained by imposing restrictions on the dimensions of fibres.

Now, given the motivic Morel’s t-structure the object
EM^{F}_{X}\in DM(X)
is defined as follows: for any open dense immersion
j:U\hookrightarrow X
with
U

EM^{F}_{X}:=w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U}.

Since
j^{*}
is weight exact,
j^{*}
commutes with

and therefore,
j^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\cong 1_{U}
.

To show the isomorphism of endomorphism rings, we consider the decomposition triangle of

:

w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow w_{>F}j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow

The functor
\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},-)
give rise to a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{>F}j_{*}1_{U}[-1])\rightarrow\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},j_{*}1_{U})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{>F}j_{*}1_{U})\rightarrow\cdots

Due to the orthogonality of Morel’s t-structure, the first and last terms are zero. Then by adjunction, we obtain the isomorphism as required:

\End(EM^{F}_{X})=\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U})\cong\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},j_{*}1_{U})\cong\End(1_{U}).

•

Vaish has shown in

•

Wildeshaus in
[
47
]
has constructed the intersection complex for motives of abelian type, where the resolutions lie in the conservative category

, the primary examples being Shimura varieties. Our work not only explores the different settings and provides an example where the fibers potentially involve phantom motives, i.e., motives outside the conservative category; but also strengthens their results for Shimura threefolds, since we are able to prove the stronger criteria of

Outline

The organization of the paper is as follows.

Section 2.1 recalls the gluing of t-structures and weight structures on a triangulated category. Section 2.2 reviews the category of Chow (pure) motives and the Chow–Künneth decomposition for varieties. Sections 2.3–2.4 summarize essential facts about the triangulated category of mixed motivic sheaves and their realization functors. Section 2.5 discusses Wildeshaus’s result on the conservativity of realization functors for abelian motives.

Section 3.1 recalls the analogue of Morel’s weight truncation for a threefold
X
in the motivic setting and establishes several results concerning
EM^{F}_{X}
. Sections 3.2–3.3 present the construction of the refined version of Morel’s truncation and examine its realization in the bounded derived category of mixed Hodge structures.

Sections 4.1–4.2 revisit the work of Wildeshaus, giving the construction of the motivic intersection complex for the motive of abelian type (up to dimension 1). This complex is constructed on the complement of a certain finite closed subvariety of a threefold. Section 4.3 shows that it coincides with
EM^{F}_{U}
. Finally, Section 4.4 contains the main result:
EM^{F}_{X}
is a Chow motive, proved using weight conservativity together with the refined truncations constructed in Section 3.2.

2.1.1 Definition

A

D^{\leq}[1]\subset D^{\leq}
, and
D^{>}[-1]\subset D^{>}

•

(Decomposition)
\forall a\in D

a_{\leq}\rightarrow a\rightarrow a_{>}\rightarrow
with

and
a_{>}\in D^{>}

A

•

\hom(a,b)=0,\forall a\in D^{\leq},b\in D^{>}

D^{\leq}[-1]\subset D^{\leq}
, and
D^{>}[1]\subset D^{>}

•

(Decomposition)
\forall a\in D

a_{\leq}\rightarrow a\rightarrow a_{>}\rightarrow
with
a_{\leq}\in D^{\leq}

2.4.
Realizations

We recall the various realization functors of the triangulated category of motives (with rational coefficients) which will be relevant in our context. They provide a bridge connecting the abstract theory of motives with classical (co)homology theories.

We begin with the covariant (homological) realization functors. For a proper morphism
f:Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
of
X
- schemes, such a realization functor (if exists) preserves the counit map
p_{*}f_{!}f^{!}1_{Y^{\prime}}\rightarrow p_{*}1_{Y^{\prime}}
of adjunction
(f_{!},f^{!})
, where
p
is the structure morphism. We have the following examples:

Let
X
be a scheme over a field
k
of characteristic zero together with a fixed embedding
\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

•

There exists a Betti realization functor
[
6
]
(see
[
16
, Ex 17.1.7]
for the bounded target)

R_{B}:DM(X)\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})

where

is the bounded derived category of
\mathbb{Q}
-constructible sheaves of on
X^{an}
. When
X=\Spec k
, it sends the motive associated to a smooth projective variety

to the singular chain complex of
Y^{an}
with rational coefficients. These categories carry the formalism of Grothendieck’s six functors, and the functor
R_{B}
is
compatible with these operations.

•

There is also a Hodge realization functor (see
[
8
, §1.3, Fact G]
) to the bounded derived category of mixed Hodge structures:

R_{\mathcal{M}}:DM(k)\rightarrow D^{b}_{\mathbb{Q}}(MHS(k)).

This functor assigns a motive of a smooth projective variety
p:X\rightarrow k
to a complex
R_{\mathcal{M}}(p_{*}1_{X})
of mixed Hodge structures, which computes the singular homology of
X^{an}
endowed with their natural mixed Hodge structures.

A relative version of this theory has been developed by Saito
[
37
]
in the form of mixed Hodge modules over a base scheme
X
. The bounded derived category
D^{b}(MHS(X))
of mixed Hodge modules admits the formalism of six functors, analogous to the case of constructible sheaves. There is a natural functor

\For:D^{b}(MHS(X))\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})

which forgets the Hodge-theoretic structure. This functor is both faithful and conservative. However, note that the realization functor from

DM(X)
to the category
D^{b}(MHS(X))
has not yet been constructed in general.

•

The
\ell
-adic realization
[
15
, §7.2]
is the functor

R_{\ell}:DM(X)\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

where the target is the bounded derived category
of constructible

]
. The functors

are symmetric monoidal, and they commute with the functors
f_{*},f^{*},f_{!},f^{!}

All of the above realization categories admit a perverse t-structure with heart denoted as

. They give rise to cohomological functors

{}^{p}H^{i}\circ R_{*}:DM(X)\rightarrow Perv(X).

where

. The category

does not carry any natural weight structure. One can resolve this partially using a result of Bondarko

, which allows one to define weights for complexes in the image of the realization functor. Specifically, for any

, there is a canonical weight filtration of

induced by any choice of weight decomposition of

in

Important

We now turn to the contravariant/cohomological realizations: For a proper morphism

•

In this section, we discuss some properties satisfied by Abelian motives. We begin by recalling the notion of finite dimensional Chow motives.

3.

3.1.
Morel’s weight truncations

3.1.1

In her thesis
[

-structure) on the category of mixed sheaves

\displaystyle{{}^{w}D^{\leq i}}:=\{A\mid\forall j\;{{}^{p}H^{j}(A)}\text{ is of weight}\leq i\}

\displaystyle{{}^{w}D^{>i}}:=\{A\mid\forall j\;{{}^{p}H^{j}(A)}\text{ is of weight}>i\}

Given a stratification

Then for certain choices of the function

[

]

[

]

3.2.

3.2.1

Let

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow Pic^{0}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow NS(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow 0

denotes the subgroup of algebraically trivial elements. Here,

For each generator

on RHS associated to the element

\displaystyle Pic^{0}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\Hom\left(1_{k}(-1)[-2],h^{1}(X)\right)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\Hom\left(1_{k}(-1)[-2],h^{0}(X)\right)=0,

Let

denote the cone of the map

3.2.1.

Recall that

\displaystyle DM^{coh}(k):=\langle p_{*}1_{X}\mid\;p:X\rightarrow k\;\text{smooth and projective}\rangle

\displaystyle M^{\leq 1}(k)

\displaystyle:=\{h^{\leq 1}(X)\mid X\;\text{is smooth, projective over}\;k\}

\displaystyle:=\{h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2]\mid k^{\prime}/k\;\text{finite, separable extension}\}

\displaystyle M^{\leq 1+alg}(k)

\displaystyle:=M^{\leq 1}(k)\cup M^{alg}(k)

\displaystyle:=\{C_{X}\mid X\;\text{is smooth, projective over}\;k\}

\displaystyle:=\langle M^{\leq 1+alg}(k)\rangle\hskip 28.45274pt^{w}DM^{>1+alg}(k):=\langle M^{>1+alg}(k)\rangle

3.2.2 Theorem
.

Let
A\in M^{\leq 1+alg}
and
B\in M^{>1+alg}
. Then
\Hom(A,B[m])=0\;\forall\,m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

First, assume
A=h^{\leq 1}(X)
and
B=C_{Y}
. Consider the triangle:

(3.2.1.A)

\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}1_{k}(-1)[-2]\xrightarrow{g_{Y}}h^{>1}(Y)\rightarrow C_{Y}\rightarrow

From the definition of
{}^{w}DM^{>1}(\Spec k)
and the fact that
1_{k}(-1)[-2]\cong h^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
, we have that
1_{k}(-1)[-2]
and
h^{>1}(X)\in\;^{w}DM^{>1}(\Spec k)
. Therefore,
C_{Y}\in\;^{w}DM^{>1}(\Spec k)
, the latter being a triangulated category. Since
A=h^{\leq 1}(X)\in\;^{w}DM^{\leq 1}(\Spec k)
, we get
\Hom(h^{\leq 1}(X),C_{Y}[m])=0
.

Now, assume
A=h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2]
where
k^{\prime}/k
is finite, separable extension and
B=C_{Y}
. We claim that

\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2][-m],\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}1_{k}(-1)[-2]\right)\rightarrow\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],h^{>1}(Y)\right)

induced by
g_{Y}
is an isomorphism for all
m
. First assume
m=0
. Since
h(k^{\prime})^{\vee}\cong h(k^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\Hom(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],1_{k}(-1)[-2])=\Hom(1_{k},h(k^{\prime}))\cong\mathbb{Q}.

and more generally,

\displaystyle\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],h^{>1}(Y)\right)

\displaystyle=\Hom\left(1_{k}(-1)[-2],h^{>1}(Y\times\Spec k^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\cong NS\left(Y\times\Spec k^{\prime}\right)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Then the map
g_{Y}
becomes
(r_{1},\dots,r_{\alpha})\mapsto r_{1}f_{1}+\dots+r_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}
. Clearly, this is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
- vector spaces. Next, by motivic cohomology vanishings observe that for
m\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}1_{k}(-1)[-2][m]\right)\cong\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}\Hom\left(1_{k},h(\Spec k^{\prime})[m]\right)=0

and for
m\neq-1,0

\displaystyle\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],h^{>1}(Y)[m]\right)=\Hom\left(1_{k},h^{>1}(Y\times\Spec k^{\prime})(1)[m+2]\right)=0.

We have

\Hom\left(1_{k}(-1)[-2],h^{0}(Y)[-1]\right)\cong\mathcal{O}^{*}(L)\otimes\mathbb{Q}

where
Y\rightarrow\Spec L\rightarrow\Spec k
is the Stein factorization and

\Hom\left(1_{k}(-1)[-2],h(Y)[-1]\right)\cong\mathcal{O}^{*}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

Since
L\cong\mathcal{O}(Y)
and
h^{0}(Y)\bigoplus h^{>1}(Y)
is the summand of
h(Y)
, it follows that even for
m=-1

\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],h^{>1}(Y)[-1]\right)=0.

Now, writing the long exact sequence corresponding to the functor
\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],-\right)
, we obtain that
\Hom\left(h(k^{\prime})(-1)[-2],C_{Y}[m]\right)=0\;\forall\;m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

.

Proof.

For any object

\displaystyle h^{\leq 1}(X)\bigoplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}1_{k}(-1)[-2]\right)\rightarrow h(X)\rightarrow C_{X}\rightarrow

is the decomposition triangle. Therefore, we are done by
3.2.2
and
2.1.5
.
∎

3.2.8 Proposition
.

For a smooth projective surface
Y
, the triangle

\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}1_{k}(-1)[-2]\xrightarrow{g_{Y}}h^{>1}(Y)\rightarrow C_{Y}\rightarrow

splits. In particular, we have the decomposition of
h^{2}(Y)
in
CHM(k)
.

Proof.

First, note that map
g_{Y}
factors through inclusion
h^{2}(Y)[-2]\hookrightarrow h^{>1}(Y)
since
\Hom\left(\mathbb{L},h^{2}(Y)[-2]\right)=NS(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. So we may assume
g_{Y}:\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}1_{k}(-1)[-2]\rightarrow h^{2}(Y)[-2]
and
C_{Y}
to be its cone. To show this triangle splits, it is enough to show that
\Hom\left(C_{Y},\mathbb{L}[1]\right)=0
. Consider the long exact sequence corresponding to the functor
\Hom\left(-,\mathbb{L}[1]\right)
:

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\cdots\rightarrow\Hom\left(h^{2}(Y)[-2][1],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)\rightarrow\Hom\left(\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha}\mathbb{L}[1],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Hom\left(C_{Y},\mathbb{L}[1]\right)\rightarrow\Hom\left(h^{2}(Y)[-2],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)\rightarrow\cdots

We know
h(Y)^{\vee}=h(Y)(2)[4]
, so

\displaystyle\Hom\left(h(Y),\mathbb{L}[1]\right)

\displaystyle=\Hom\left(1_{k},h(Y)^{\vee}(-1)[-1]\right)=\Hom\left(1_{k},h(Y)(1)[3]\right)=0

Since,
\Hom\left(h^{2}(Y)[-2],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)\hookrightarrow\Hom\left(h(Y),\mathbb{L}[1]\right)
, we have
\Hom\left(h^{2}(Y)[-2],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)=0
.
Next, see that, by motivic cohomology,

\Hom\left(\mathbb{L}[1],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)=\Hom\left(1_{k},1_{k}\right)=H^{0,0}(\Spec k)=\mathbb{Q}

and since
{(h^{2}(Y)[-2])}^{\vee}=(h^{2}(Y)[-2])\otimes\mathbb{L}^{-2}
we have,

\displaystyle\Hom\left(h^{2}(Y)[-2][1],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)

\displaystyle=\Hom\left(\mathbb{L}^{-1},{(h^{2}(Y)[-2])}^{\vee}\right)=\Hom\left(\mathbb{L},h^{2}(Y)[-2]\right)=NS(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Thus, the map
\Hom\left(g_{Y}[1],\mathbb{L}[1]\right)
becomes
r_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+r_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}\mapsto(r_{1},\cdots,r_{\alpha})
, which is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces. From the above calculations, it follows that

.
∎

3.3.
Refining Morel’s truncations in mixed categories.

3.3.1
.

It is possible and useful to define an analogue of the above refinement in mixed categories, this is what we do next.
Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero with a fixed embedding
\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. For a variety
X
over
k
, let
X^{an}
be the associated complex analytic space. Then the singular cohomology of
X^{an}
(with
\mathbb{Q}
coefficients) has a mixed Hodge structure. Let
MHS_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
denote the category of natural mixed Hodge structures over
k
. Recall that we have a mixed realization functor

R_{\mathcal{M}}:DM(k)\rightarrow D^{b}(MHS_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)).

It takes motive
h(X)
of a smooth projective variety
p:X\rightarrow k
to a complex
R(p_{*}1_{X})
of mixed Hodge structures computing the singular cohomology of
X^{an}
.

3.3.2
.

Recall that the mixed category of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures
MHS_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
is abelian, Noetherian, and Artinian, with simple objects also pure. Let
HS(n,\mathbb{Q})
be the category of pure Hodge structures of weight
n
which are also simple. Then the sub category
\bigcup_{n}HS(n,\mathbb{Q})
generates the triangulated category
D^{b}(MHS_{\mathbb{Q}}(k))
.

3.3.3 Proposition
.

The following subcategories in
D^{b}(MHS_{\mathbb{Q}}(k))
form a
t
-structure:

{}^{w}D^{\leq 1+alg}(k)

\displaystyle:=\langle\{V\in HS(n,\mathbb{Q})\mid n\leq 1\}\cup\{\mathbb{Q}(-1)\}\rangle

{}^{w}D^{>1+alg}(k)

\displaystyle:=\langle\{V\in HS(n,\mathbb{Q})\mid n>2\}\;\cup\{V\in HS(2,\mathbb{Q})\mid V\;\text{is simple and}\;V\neq\mathbb{Q}(-1)\}\rangle

Proof.

There is an analogue of Morel’s t-structure on
D^{b}(MHS(k))
. In particular for
V\in HS(n,\mathbb{Q})
and
V^{\prime}\in HS(n^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})
we have the vanishings

\displaystyle\Hom(V,V^{\prime}[m])=0\hskip 28.45274pt\text{for}\;n\leq 1,\;n^{\prime}>2\;\text{and}\;m\in\mathbb{Z}

Let
V^{\prime}\in HS(2,\mathbb{Q})
. In
D^{b}(MHS(k))
, the extensions
Ext^{i}(N,N^{\prime})=0
for
N
of weight
\leq n
and
N^{\prime}
of weight
\geq n^{\prime}
if
n\leq n^{\prime}+i
. So,
\Hom(\mathbb{Q}(-1),V^{\prime}[m])=0\;\text{for}\;m>0
. The standard t-structure on the derived category
D^{b}(MHS(k))
gives
\Hom(V,V^{\prime}[m])=0
for
m<0
. Since both
\mathbb{Q}(-1)
and
V
are simple objects and
V\neq\mathbb{Q}(-1)
, we have
\Hom(\mathbb{Q}(-1),V)=0.

Therefore, to show that it is a
t
-structure, by
2.1.5
it is enough to construct a decomposition triangle for each object
V
in
HS(n,\mathbb{Q})
. The corresponding triangles are

\displaystyle V\rightarrow V\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\text{ for }n\leq 1\text{ or }V\cong\mathbb{Q}(-1)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow V\rightarrow V\rightarrow\text{ for }n\geq 2\text{ and }V\not\cong\mathbb{Q}(-1)

and we are done.
∎

Finally, we connect the motivic and the mixed constructions through the realization functors:

3.3.4 Proposition
.

R_{\mathcal{M}}\left({}^{w}DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)\right)\subset\;^{w}D^{\leq 1+alg}(k)

R_{\mathcal{M}}\left({}^{w}DM^{>1+alg}(k)\right)\subset\;^{w}D^{>1+alg}(k).

It is enough to check on generators because

Now taking the realization of triangle (

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\Hom(\mathbb{Q}(-1),\bigoplus\mathbb{Q}(-1))\rightarrow\Hom\left(\mathbb{Q}(-1),H^{\geq 2}(Y^{an})\right)\rightarrow\Hom\left(\mathbb{Q}(-1),R_{\mathcal{M}}(C_{Y})\right)\rightarrow

4.

4.1.
Definitions and First results

4.1.1 Definition
.

[
45
]
Let
X
be any arbitrary variety and
j:U\hookrightarrow X
be an open dense immersion with
U
regular. For

)

(1)

(2)

j^{*}
is injective (hence, an isomorphism since
j^{*}
restricted to
CHM
is full).

It is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

Replace condition (ii) with a slightly weaker condition:

(ii)
′

The map

induced by
j^{*}
has a
nilpotent kernel
.

Although it is weaker than the unconditional definition
4.1.1
but exists unconditionally in more cases, for example, the Baily-Borel compactification of an arbitrary Shimura variety. See Proposition
4.2.2
for its existence in a certain abstract setting. It is unique up to radical. The main interest in the motivic intersection complex comes from the fact that its realisation is an ordinary intersection complex. As a consequence of (i) and (ii)

(iii)

Let

such that

where

and

The following are some easy but useful properties of the (weaker) motivic intersection complex.

4.1.2 Lemma
.

Let

be an open dense immersion with

Proof.

The motivic intersection complex is unique up to isomorphism, so it is enough to show that

satisfies the (i) and (ii)

for an open immersion

Now to verify (ii)

is an isomorphism. By assumption, the map

4.1.3 Lemma

Let

The open subsets

4.1.4 Lemma
.

Let
X
be a scheme and

, modeled on the definition of Morel’s t-structure:

{}^{w}D^{\leq a}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{K\in D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\mid K=R_{\ell,X}(K^{\prime})\;\text{and}\;\;^{p}H^{i}(K)\;\;\text{has weight}\leq a\}

{}^{w}D^{>a}(X)

\displaystyle:=\{K\in D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\mid K=R_{\ell,X}(K^{\prime})\;\text{and}\;\;^{p}H^{i}(K)\;\;\text{has weight}>a\}

Since
D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is not mixed, these subcategories do not form a t-structure.

Wildeshaus has shown (see Chapter 7 of
[
47
]
) that for any
N\in CHM(U)
, if the motivic intermediate extension
j_{!*}N
exists in the weaker sense, then it realizes to the classical intermediate extension associated to the local system
\mathcal{L}:=R_{\ell,X}(N)
that is,

R_{\ell,X}(j_{!*}N)\cong IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}).

Now, the following lemma computes Morel’s weight of the intersection complex in the
\ell
-adic setting.

4.3.3 Lemma
.

Let
j:U\hookrightarrow X
be an open dense immersion with
U
regular. Let
N\in CHM(U)
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}:=R_{\ell,X}(N)
is a local system. Further assume that
j_{!*}N
exists.

Let
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be a closed immersion such that
Z\neq X
. Then,

\displaystyle i^{*}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L})\in\;^{w}D^{\leq\dim X-1}(Z)\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;i^{!}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L})\in\;^{w}D^{>\dim X+1}(Z)

Proof.

Since
R_{\ell,X}(j_{!*}N)\cong IC_{X}(\mathcal{L})
and
j_{!*}N
is of weight
0
, we have
IC_{X}(\mathcal{L})
is of weight
0
. The functor
i^{*}
decreases Bondarko weights and
i^{!}
increases Bondrako weights so
i^{*}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L})
has weights
\leq 0
and
i^{!}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L})
has weight
\geq 0
, that is, for all
j
,

{}^{p}H^{j}(i^{*}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}))\;\;\text{has weights}\leq j\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;^{p}H^{j}(i^{!}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}))\;\;\text{has weights}\geq j.

Now by
[
10
, Lemma 2.1.9]
, we know that

{}^{p}H^{j}(i^{*}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}))=0\;\;\forall\;j\geq\dim X\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;^{p}H^{j}(i^{!}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}))=0\;\forall\;j\leq\dim X

Therefore, for all
j
, we obtain

{}^{p}H^{j}(i^{*}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}))\;\;\text{has weights}\leq\dim X-1\;\;\text{and}\;\;^{p}H^{j}(i^{!}IC_{X}(\mathcal{L}))\;\;\text{has weights}\geq\dim X+1.

Hence, we obtain the required Morel’s weights.
∎

4.3.4 Lemma
.

Let
\eta:\Spec K\hookrightarrow X
be a point in
X
with closure

. Let

such that
\eta^{*}M\in\;^{w}DM^{\leq 1}(K)
(resp.
\eta^{*}M\in\;^{w}DM^{>1}(K))
. Then there is an open dense subset

(resp.
j^{*}(R_{\ell,X}(M))\in\;^{w}D^{>2}(U)
).

Proof.

By continuity, it is enough to show that for all
M\in\;^{w}DM^{\leq 1}(K)
there is an object

on some open dense subset

such that
\eta^{*}\overline{M}\cong M
and

. Since category
{}^{w}D^{\leq 2}
is closed under taking shifts, cones and summands, we may assume

.

The non-trivial case is when

for some smooth projective curve

. We have an isomorphism
h^{1}(C)\cong h^{1}(J(C))\hookrightarrow h(J(C))
where

is a Jacobian variety of curve. By spreading out, there exist a dense open set
j:U\hookrightarrow Y
and an abelian scheme

such that

. Define

\displaystyle\overline{M}:=h^{1}_{U}(\overline{J(C)}).

Then,
R_{\ell,X}(\overline{M})=\;^{p}H^{1}\left(\overline{p}_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{\overline{J(C)}}\right)[-1]

{}^{w}DM^{\leq 2}(U)
.

The other case is similar.
∎

\displaystyle j_{!*}1_{U}\xrightarrow{\alpha}j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow\cone(\alpha)\rightarrow

4.3.6 Notation
.

From now on, we write

for the motivic intersection complex of a scheme

.

4.4.
The candidate
EM^{F}_{X}
as a Chow motive

We aim to show that for a threefold
X
, the object
EM^{F}_{X}
satisfies the characterization of the motivic intersection complex as given in
4.1.1
. The central and subtle part of the argument is to prove that
EM^{F}_{X}
is a Chow motive. The remaining properties follow by routine verification. The proof of
4.1.1
(i) is provided in
3.1.11
, and (ii) is established in the subsequent theorem.

4.4.1 Theorem
.

Let
X
be an irreducible variety of dimension
3
over a field
k
of characteristic
0
. For any open immersion
j:U\hookrightarrow X
with
U
regular, the map induced by pullback functor
j^{*}

\End(EM^{F}_{X})\rightarrow\End(1_{U})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Consider the decomposition triangle of
j_{*}1_{U}
:

w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow w_{>F}j_{*}1_{U}\rightarrow

The functor
\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},-)
give rise to a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{>F}j_{*}1_{U}[-1])\rightarrow\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},j_{*}1_{U})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{>F}j_{*}1_{U})\rightarrow\cdots

Due to the orthogonality of Morel’s t-structure, the first and last terms are zero. Therefore we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U})\cong\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},j_{*}1_{U}).

By adjunction and the fact that
j^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\cong 1_{U}
(see
3.1.11
) we have the isomorphism

\Hom(w_{\leq F}j_{*}1_{U},j_{*}1_{U})\cong\Hom(1_{U},1_{U}).

Hence,

\End(EM^{F}_{X})\cong\End(1_{U}).

as desired.
∎

We now present two propositions required for showing that
EM^{F}_{X}
is a Chow motive. The first establishes weight conservativity in our context, and the second one calculates the weight of realization of
EM^{F}_{X}
at each point of
X
.

Recall that the triangulated subcategory of
DM^{eff}(k)
:

DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)=\langle h(X)\mid X\in SmProj/k,\,\dim X\leq 1\rangle.

admits the motivic t-structure by the work of
[
7
, Section 3.2]
. We denote this t-structure by
(DM^{t\leq 0},DM^{t>0})
. The Hodge realisation functor
R_{\mathcal{M}}|_{DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)}
is t-exact with respect to the motivic t-structure on source and the standard t-structure on the target. This follows from
[
36
]
, where exactness is proved for the Betti realization functor. Since the forgetful functor
\For:D^{b}_{\mathbb{Q}}(MHS(k))\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is t-conservative by Lemma
2.5.7
, we deduce that restriction of
R_{\mathcal{M}}
is also t-exact.

It is easy to observe that
R_{\mathcal{M}}|_{DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)}
is t-exact with respect to Morel’s t-structure.

Then, as
DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)\subset DM^{Ab}(k)
, the conservativity of
R_{\mathcal{M}}
on
DM^{Ab}(k)
together with Lemma
2.5.7
, implies that
R_{\mathcal{M}}|_{DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)}
is t-conservative with respect to both the motivic and Morel’s t-structure.

The following proposition shows that the t-conservativity of the motivic and Morel’s t-structure on
DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)
leads to the weight conservativity of
R_{\mathcal{M}}|_{DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)}
.

4.4.2 Proposition
.

Let
R_{\mathcal{M}}:DM(k)\rightarrow D^{b}(MHS(k))
be the Hodge realization functor. Let
DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)
be the triangulated category generated by motives of curves, defined as above. Then the functor
R_{\mathcal{M}}|_{DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)}
is weight conservative.
.

Proof.

Let
M\in DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)
such that
R_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
is pure of weight
a
. Using Morel’s truncation,
M
can be expressed as a finite successive extension of objects of pure Morel’s weight. Because Morel’s truncation functors preserve weights, we may reduce to the case where
M
itself has pure Morel weight, say
b
.

By the t-exactness of the realization functor with respect to Morel’s t-structure, we then have that
R_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
also has pure Morel weight
b
. By assumption,
R_{\mathcal{M}}(M)
is pure of weight
a
; therefore

H^{i}(R_{\mathcal{M}}(M))=0\;\forall\;i\neq b-a.

Now, by conservativity of
R_{\mathcal{M}}
with respect to motivic t-structure, it follows that
M\in DM^{t=b-a}(k)
.

Finally, to verify that
M
has weight
a
, it suffices to check this for generators of
DM^{\leq 1+alg}(k)
. For such a generator
M
of pure Morel weight
b
and lying in
DM^{t=b-a}(k)
,
we must have
M=h^{b}(C)[a-b]
for some smooth projective curve. Since
h^{b}(C)
is of weight
b
, the shift gives that
M
has weight
a
as required.

An analogous argument applies in the cases when
R_{\mathcal{M}}(M)\in DM^{w\leq a}(k)
or
R_{\mathcal{M}}(M)\in DM^{w>a}(k)
.
∎

Next result will play a key role in determining the weight of
EM^{F}_{X}
at each point via weight conservativity.

4.4.3 Proposition
.

Let
X
be an irreducible variety of dimension
3
, and let

be an open subset of

whose complement

consists of finitely many closed points of

. Assume that the motivic intersection complex

exists for

and is isomorphic to

. Then, for all

R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{*}EM^{F}_{X})\cong s^{*}IC^{\mathcal{M}}_{X}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{!}EM^{F}_{X})\cong s^{!}IC^{\mathcal{M}}_{X}

where

Proof.

We have previously seen in Corollary
3.1.17
) that

s^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\cong w_{\leq 2}\,s^{*}j_{*}\,EM^{F}_{U}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;s^{!}EM^{F}_{X}\cong w_{>2}\,s^{*}j_{*}\,EM^{F}_{U}[-1].

Since the functor
w_{\leq 2}
and

Let

be a resolution of

exists, by the motivic decomposition theorem we have that

is summand of
\pi_{*}1_{U^{\prime}}
where

is the pullback of

along

We then proceed by establishing the following two claims:

\displaystyle\textit{Claim I:}

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{*}j_{*}\,{\pi}_{*}1_{U^{\prime}})\cong s^{*}j_{*}\,{\pi}_{*}\,\mathbb{Q}^{\mathcal{M}}_{U^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\textit{Claim II:}

\displaystyle\text{{Claim I} implies }R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{*}j_{*}\,EM^{F}_{U})\cong s^{*}j_{*}\,IC^{\mathcal{M}}_{U}.

We begin by proving Claim II. Given that
R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{*}j_{*}\,EM^{F}_{U})

R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{*}j_{*}\,{\pi}_{*}1_{U^{\prime}})
, Claim I implies that we have a split injection

Composing with the projection morphism

we obtain a map

Since the Betti realization functor

commutes with the six functors and

[

, we have

\For\left(R_{\mathcal{M}}(s^{*}j_{*}\,EM^{F}_{U})\right)=R_{B}(s^{*}j_{*}\,EM^{F}_{U})=s^{*}j_{*}\,R_{B}(EM^{F}_{U})\cong s^{*}j_{*}\,IC_{U}.

Then, observe that the map

is the composition of inclusion followed by projection, so it is an identity morphism. Therefore, due to conservativity of the functor

Next, we prove Claim I. Let

\displaystyle i_{*}i^{!}\,\pi_{*}1_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow\pi_{*}1_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow j_{*}j^{*}\,\pi_{*}1_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle{\pi}_{*}\,s^{!}1_{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{f}{\pi}_{*}\,s^{*}1_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow s^{*}\,j_{*}\,{\pi}_{*}1_{U^{\prime}}\rightarrow

Since under Betti realization

Applying

∎

4.4.4 Remark
.

The above proposition would follow directly for any mixed realization functor that commutes with Grothendieck’s six functors and under which
EM^{F}_{X}
realizes to the intersection complex
IC_{X}
. At present, no such realization functor from motives to mixed Hodge modules (or to any mixed category, for that matter) is known in full generality, which is why we proceed as above.

Drew’s
[
18
]
construction of six functor formalism
X\mapsto DH_{c}(X)
of motivic Hodge modules, together with the realization functor
DM(X)\rightarrow DH_{c}(X)
, offers a possible framework. However, the perverse t-structure on
DH_{c}(X)
is not yet available; once constructed, one expects
EM^{F}_{X}
to realize to
IC_{X}
and the proposition would then follow by a straightforward argument for
DH_{c}(X)
.

Finally, we show that the object
EM^{F}_{X}
is a Chow motive.

4.4.5 Theorem
.

Let
X
be an irreducible variety of dimension
3
over a field
k
of characteristic
0
. The object

0
in the sense of Bondarko, that is
EM^{F}_{X}\in DM^{w=0}(X)
. In particular, it is a Chow motive.

Proof.

To show that
EM^{F}_{X}
is of weight zero, by punctual gluing, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle s^{*}\;EM^{F}_{X}\in DM^{w\leq 0}\;\;\text{and}\;\;s^{!}\;EM^{F}_{X}\in DM^{w\geq 0}

for all
s:\Spec\;K\hookrightarrow X
. Let
p:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be resolution of
X
. Fix a stratification
X=U\bigsqcup Z
where
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
is a closed immersion with
\dim Z=0
, and
j:U\hookrightarrow X
its open complement such that for every point
s\in U
, the fibre
p^{-1}(s)
has dimension at most one.

By Corollary
3.1.13
, and the fact that pushforward along a proper map is weight exact (see
[
23
, Theorem 3.7 ]
), we may assume that
X
is normal. After possibly shrinking
U
, Sections
4.2
and
4.3
ensure that the motivic intersection complex
IM_{U}
exists and is isomorphic to
EM^{F}_{U}
. This implies that
EM^{F}_{U}
is summand of
p_{*}1_{U^{\prime}}
where
U^{\prime}
denote the restriction of
X^{\prime}
to
U
. As the latter is of weight zero, we conclude that
EM^{F}_{U}
is also of weight
0
. Then the isomorphism
j^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\cong EM^{F}_{U}
completes the argument for points
s\in U
.

For points
s:\Spec K\hookrightarrow X
lying in
Z
, we will use the refined Morel’s t-structure as defined in Section

. Assume that the fibre

at

has dimension

Consider the decomposition triangle:

w_{\leq 1+alg}\,s^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\rightarrow s^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\rightarrow w_{>1+alg}\,s^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\rightarrow

To show

has weight
\leq 0
, it suffices to prove that both the first and third terms have weight
\leq 0
. From Corollary
3.1.17

\displaystyle s^{*}EM^{F}_{X}\cong w_{\leq 2}\,s^{*}j_{*}EM^{F}_{U}.

Thus, to verify the weight of the third term, it is enough to show that the weight of
w_{\leq 1+alg}\,w_{\leq 2}\,s^{*}j_{*}p_{*}1_{U^{\prime}}
is
\leq 0
since
EM^{F}_{U}
is summand of
p_{*}1_{U^{\prime}}
. Now, consider the localization triangle for

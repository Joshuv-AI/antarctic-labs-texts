Existence of embedded minimal tori in three-spheres with positive Ricci curvature
Xingzhe Li, Zhichao Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the strong Morse inequalities for the area functional in the space of embedded tori and spheres in the three sphere. As a consequence, we prove that in the three dimensional sphere with positive Ricci curvature, there exist at least 4 distinct embedded minimal tori. Suppose in addition that the metric is bumpy, then the three-sphere contains at least 9 distinct embedded minimal tori. The proof relies on a multiplicity one theorem for the Simon-Smith min-max theory proved by the second author and X. Zhou [ 25 ] .

In his famous 1982 Problem Section
[

Motivated by the progress on Yau’s conjecture, we set out to investigate the high genus problem. It is noteworthy that the space of all unoriented Clifford tori in round

can be parametrized by

, whose Lusternik-Schnirelmann number and Morse number are equal to

*Theorem 3.2, White proved that every sufficiently round metric on

admits at least five distinct minimal embedded tori, and a generic such metric must admit at least nine distinct minimal embedded tori. One would then expect five distinct minimal tori in a Riemannian
S^{3}

[

, we study the existence of embedded minimal tori in
S^{3}
with positive Ricci metrics and prove our main theorem as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
g
is a metric positive Ricci curvature on

. Then there exist at least four distinct embedded minimal tori in
(S^{3},g)
. Suppose in addition that

Our Theorem

The main technique for constructing multiple minimal tori is the relative strong Morse inequalities for the area functional on the space of surfaces with bounded topology. Analogous inequalities on the space of mod

[

Once we move into the realm of minimal surfaces of controlled topological types, the strong Morse inequalities in
[

become insufficient to tell the topology apart. As one key novelty of this paper, we propose and prove the relative strong Morse inequalities for the area functional on the space of surfaces with bounded topology in positively Ricci curved

Theorem 1.2

(Relative Strong Morse Inequalities)
.

For any fixed

, we have
\beta_{k}(\ell)<\infty
and the Strong Relative Morse Inequalities for every

c_{k}(\ell)-c_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}c_{0}(\ell)\geq\beta_{k}(\ell)-\beta_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}\beta_{0}(\ell).

In particular, we have

for every

The Morse theory for the area functional has been built on a series of paper leaded by Marques and Neves. In
[

[

[

1.1.
Sketch of the proof

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we follow similar strategy as the proof of the strong Morse inequalities on the space of mod
2
flat chains
[
16
]
, that is, to set up the min-max theory in a nontrivial
k
-th homology class of a level set, and then employ an interpolation (cut-and-paste procedure) to show that the local min-max family of an index
k
minimal surface inside the level set uniquely and only contributes to the
k
-th homology class.

However, the Simon-Smith min-max theory only has genus upper bound which may produce minimal spheres by a sweepout of tori even in the multiplicity one case. To overcome it, we study the relative homology min-max in the space
(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}})
(see Section
2
for definitions). Then the major problem is to determine the homology groups of such relative spaces, which is highly nontrivial to our knowledge.

Let us briefly introduce the new ideas to address the above two issues. Our proof of genus lower bound is inspired by Marques-Neves’ proof of the Morse index lower bound
[
20
]
. We argue by contradiction. By doing another tightening process, we may assume that in the minimizing sequence, the surfaces with area close to the min-max value should be close to a smooth sphere. By applying the two homotopies in
[
20
]
, one may assume that the surfaces of large area are intrinsically generated by the
k
-parameter family (see
[
20
]
*Section 6.1 for White’s Local Min-max Theorem) that decreases the area of minimal spheres; the Multiplicity One Theorem for Simon-Smith min-max theory proved by the second author and X. Zhou in
[
25
]
is essentially used in this part. Then we cut off the small chains that lie in the image of the homology class in the relative spaces. Since the surfaces that we cut off are all spheres, it will not affect the homology class (clearly, they are not in the same homotopy class). Moreover, the area of maximal slices is deformed down by a definite amount during this process, which contradicts the definition of min-max width. This proves the genus lower bound.

To determine the topology of the relative spaces, we consider it on the round three sphere. The three main ingredients are the Smale Conjecture by A. Hatcher
[
9
]
, the Perturbation Theorem by B. White
[
26
]
*Theorem 3.2 and the Lawson Conjecture by S. Brendle
[
4
]
. Since each embedded minimal torus has area equal to
2\pi^{2}
(by Lawson Conjecture), it follows from our relative homology min-max theorem that
\overline{\mathcal{T}}
is “weakly homotopic” to
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{2\pi^{2}+\varepsilon}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}
, the space of tori with area bounded by
2\pi^{2}+\varepsilon
union the space of spheres. Similarly,
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{2\pi^{2}-\varepsilon}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}
is “weakly homotopic” to
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
. Hence the problem has been reduced to determine the homology classes of
(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{2\pi^{2}+\varepsilon}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{2\pi^{2}-\varepsilon}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}})
. We will construct a 9-parameter family of tori and prove that it induces an injective map between the relative homology groups. This part involves the Smale Conjecture and the argument by Marques-Montezuma-Neves
[
16
]
*Claim 3.7. Finally, we use White’s Perturbation Theorem to show that there are no other relative homology classes.

Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

Without loss of generality, we assume that

3.2.
Relative homology min-max theorem

Recall that given
\ell>0
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\ell}:=\{\Sigma\in\overline{\mathcal{T}};\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma)<\ell\};\quad\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{\ell}:=\{\Sigma\in\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma)<\ell\}.

Recall that
\mathcal{T}_{min}(g)
the collection of embedded minimal tori w.r.t. the metric

.

Theorem 3.3

(Relative Homology Min-max Theorem)
.

Let
g
be a bumpy metric. Let
\tau\in H_{k}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t})

Suppose that, for some
\varepsilon>0

\Sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{min}(g)

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma^{\prime})\in(s-\varepsilon,s)
and
\mathrm{index}(\Sigma^{\prime})\leq k-1
and there is no embedded minimal sphere

with
\mathcal{H}^{2}(S^{\prime})=s
. Then
W(\tau)\in[s,t)
with
W(\tau)>0
if

. Moreover there exists
\Sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{min}(g)
with
\mathrm{index}=k
and

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma)=W(\tau).

Proof.

Clearly, we have
W(\tau)\in[s,t)
. The fact that

if

follows similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. We assume that

is as in Proposition
3.1
.

Let
\{\Psi_{j}:(X,\partial X)\to(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t})\}

depends on

) be a minimizing sequence of representatives of

(\Psi_{j})_{*}[X]=\tau\quad\forall j\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in X}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Psi_{j}(x))=W(\tau).

For each

the homotopy class of
{\Psi_{j}}|_{\partial X}
among all maps taking values in

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
:

c_{k}(\ell)-c_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}c_{0}(\ell)\geq\beta_{k}(\ell)-\beta_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}\beta_{0}(\ell).

In particular, we have

c_{k}(\ell)\geq\beta_{k}(\ell)

for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

We first prove the theorem for Riemannian metrics as in Proposition
3.1
. Suppose that
g
is such a metric. Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Let
\{\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\cdots,\Sigma_{q}\}
be the collection of minimal tori w.r.t metric
g
with
\mathcal{H}^{2}<\ell
and
\mathrm{index}\leq k
. We can suppose that they are ordered so that

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{q})<\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{q-1})<\cdots<\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{1}).

Set
a_{i}=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma_{i})
and
m_{i}=\mathrm{index}(\Sigma_{i})
. Proposition
4.1
implies that we can choose
s_{i}<a_{i}<t_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq q
such that
t_{i}<s_{i-1}
for all
2\leq i\leq q
,
0<s_{q}<t_{1}<\ell
and

b_{j}(t_{i},s_{i})=0\quad\forall j\leq k,j\neq m_{i};\quad b_{m_{i}}(t_{i},s_{i})=1.

The Relative Homology Min-Max Theorem
3.3
implies

\displaystyle b_{j}(\ell,t_{1})=0\quad\forall j\leq r;\quad b_{j}(s_{q},0)=0\quad\forall j\leq k;

\displaystyle b_{j}(s_{i-1},t_{i})=0\quad\forall j\leq r,2\leq i\leq q.

Note that
\beta_{k}(\ell)=b_{k}(\ell,0)
and
b_{k}(t,s)
is subadditive. Then

\beta_{k}(\ell)\leq b_{k}(\ell,t_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{q}b_{k}(t_{i},s_{i})+\sum_{i=2}^{q}b_{k}(s_{i-1},t_{i})+b_{k}(s_{q},0).

The right hand side of the above inequality is equal to
c-k(\ell)
. Hence
\beta_{k}(\ell)<\infty
for every
k
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{s}_{k}(t,s)
is also subadditive. Hence

\mathfrak{s}_{k}(\ell,0)\leq\mathfrak{s}_{k}(\ell,t_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mathfrak{s}_{k}(t_{i},s_{i})+\sum_{i=2}^{q}\mathfrak{s}_{k}(s_{i-1},t_{i})+\mathfrak{s}_{k}(s_{q},0).

This together with the Relative Homology Min-Max Theorem
3.3
implies that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{k}(\ell,0)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mathfrak{s}_{k}(t_{i},s_{i})=\sum_{1\leq i\leq q}(-1)^{k-m_{i}}

\displaystyle=c_{k}(\ell)-c_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}c_{0}(\ell).

Therefore,

\beta_{k}(\ell)-\beta_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}\beta_{0}(\ell)\leq c_{k}(\ell)-c_{k-1}(\ell)+\cdots+(-1)^{k}c_{0}(\ell).

Since
k
is arbitrary, this proves the theorem for metrics as in Proposition
3.1
. It follows immediately that the theorem is valid for bumpy metrics with positive Ricci curvature.
∎

5.
Homology of the relative space

In this section, we determine the homology groups of the relative space
(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}})
. Then the existence of minimal tori (for bumpy metrics with positive Ricci curvature) follows from the strong Morse inequalities; see Theorem
4.2
. Recall that
\mathcal{T}
is homotopic to
\mathcal{T}_{min}\simeq\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, but the relative space is hard to compute directly from algebraic topology. Alternatively, we use the Relative Homology Min-max Theorem
3.3
and the round metric on
S^{3}
to construct explicit local homology classes, and then using the strong Morse inequalities and White’s Perturbation Theorem
[
26
]
*Theorem 3.2 to prove that there are no other nontrivial classes. Finally, by a Lusternik-Schnirelmann type argument, we finish the proof of our Theorem
1.1
.

5.3.
Homology groups of relative spaces

Now we are ready to determine the homology groups
H_{*}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.

Proposition 5.4
.

There exists an isomorphism
\overline{\mathcal{D}}:H_{j}(\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to H_{j+5}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
for all integers
j\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
t,s
be the two real numbers in Lemma
5.3
. Recall that by proved by S. Brendle
[
4
]
, every embedded minimal torus in the round
S^{3}
is a Clifford torus.

Claim 5
.

H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is trivial for all
j\geq 1
.

Proof of Claim
5
.

Suppose not, there exists a relative homology group
[\tau_{j}]\in H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Let
g_{k}
be a sequence of bumpy metrics on
S^{3}
smoothly converging to the round metric
g_{round}
.
Then by the Relative Homology Min-max Theorem
3.3
, for each
k
, there exists an embedded minimal torus
\Sigma_{k}
with index
k
and

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{g_{k}}(\Sigma_{k})=W_{g_{k}}([\tau_{j}]):=\inf_{[\sum\mathfrak{s}_{i}]=\tau}\sup_{i,x\in\Delta^{k}}\mathcal{H}^{2}_{g_{k}}(\mathfrak{s}_{i}(x)).

Note that as
k\to\infty
,

W_{g_{k}}([\tau_{j}])\to W_{g_{round}}([\tau_{j}])\geq t.

Thus by the compactness of embedded minimal surfaces,
\Sigma_{k}
converges to an embedded minimal torus in
S^{3}
w.r.t. the round metric, which must be a Clifford torus. Recall that the Clifford torus has area
2\pi^{2}<t
, which leads to a contradiction. Hence the claim is proved.
∎

Similarly, the same argument gives that

Claim 6
.

H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{s};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is trivial for all
j\geq 1
.

Observe that by Excision Theorem
[
10
]
*Theorem 2.20 and Claim
6
,

H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\simeq H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{s};\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\{0\}\quad\text{ for all }j\geq 1.

Now consider the short exact sequence

(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t})\to(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t})\to(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t}).

The induced long exact sequence gives that for all
j\geq 1
,

(5.1)

H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\simeq H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Then consider the short exact sequence

(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}},\overline{\mathcal{S}})\to(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}})\to(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}).

The induced long exact sequence together with Claim
5
gives that

H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\simeq H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Then by Excision Theorem
[
10
]
*Theorem 2.20 and (
5.1
),

H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\simeq H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\simeq H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\simeq H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{t},\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{s}\cup\overline{\mathcal{S}}^{t};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

This together with Lemma
5.3
implies that

\mathrm{rank}\,H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\geq\mathrm{rank}\,H_{j+5}(\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2}).

In particular,

(5.2)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{rank}\,H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{rank}\,H_{j+5}(\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2})=9

It remains to prove the equality. Recall that by White
[
26
]
*Theorem 3.2, there exists a sequence of metrics
\widetilde{g}_{k}\to g_{round}
such that each
(S^{3},g_{k})
has exactly 9 embedded minimal tori with index less than or equal to 9. By perturbing
g_{k}
(still denoted by
g_{k}
), one can assume that
g_{k}
is bumpy. Then by the strong Morse inequalities,

\sum_{j}\mathrm{rank}\,H_{j}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\leq 9.

This together with (
5.2
) gives the equality.
∎

5.4.

For simplicity, denote by
\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{X}_{9}(\delta)
.
Denote by

the two generators of

such that

\lambda(\mathbb{RP}^{1}_{2}\times\{q\})=\mu(\{p\}\times\mathbb{RP}^{1}_{1})=0;\quad\lambda(\mathbb{RP}^{1}_{1}\times\{q\})=\mu(\{p\}\times\mathbb{RP}^{1}_{2})=1.

Denote by
\bar{\lambda}
the generator of
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(S^{3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
Consider the natural inclusion

Consider the cap product

is natural (

W(\tau)=\inf_{[\sum\mathfrak{s}_{i}]=\tau}\sup_{i,x\in\Delta^{k}}\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{s}_{i}(x)).

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
g
be a metric on
S^{3}
with positive Ricci curvature. Suppose that
(S^{3},g)
contains only finitely many embedded minimal tori. Then

0<W(\sigma\smallfrown u^{3})<W(\sigma\smallfrown u^{2})<W(\sigma\smallfrown u)<W(\sigma),

where
\sigma\in H_{9}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is the generator.
As a consequence, any
(S^{3},g)
with positive Ricci curvature contains at least four distinct embedded minimal tori.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the Lusternik-Schnirelmann argument in
[
19
]
*Theorem 6.1 (see also
[
25
]
*Appendix E). We provide more details here for the sake of completeness.

We prove the last inequality and the others are similar. Suppose by contradiction that
W(\sigma)=W(\sigma\smallfrown u)
. Let
\Phi:(X,\partial X)\rightarrow(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}})
be a
9
-sweepout such that

\Phi_{*}[X]=\sigma\in H_{9}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{min}
be the collection of integral varifolds, with mass equal to
W(\sigma)
, whose support are embedded minimal tori. Note that
\mathcal{T}_{min}
is a finite set by assumption.

Set
Y=\{x\in X:\mathbf{F}(|\Phi(x)|,\mathcal{T}_{min})\geq\eta\}
and
Z=\overline{X\setminus Y}
. We have
\partial X
contained in
Y
but disjoint from
Z
. Let
i_{1}:Z\rightarrow X
and
i_{2}:Y\rightarrow X
be two natural inclusion maps. Our goal is show that
\Phi\circ i_{2}:(Y,\partial X)\rightarrow(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}})
satisfies

(\Phi\circ i_{2})_{*}[Y]=\sigma\smallfrown u\in H_{8}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Once this is true, the Relative Homology Min-max Theorem
3.3
gives an embedded minimal torus
\Sigma
with
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Sigma)=W(\sigma\smallfrown u)=W(\sigma)
. Hence
\Sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{min}
and we derive a contradiction.

To fulfill our goal, consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{9}(\overline{\mathcal{T}},\overline{\mathcal{S}};\mathbb{Z}_{2})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\times}

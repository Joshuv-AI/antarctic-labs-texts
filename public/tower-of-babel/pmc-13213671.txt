Heat stress induces ferroptosis in intramuscular preadipocytes of Hu sheep via activation of the TNF-α/NF-κB/GPX4 signaling axis
Authors: Shaoqiang Wu, Yirong Wei, Zhuangzhuang Han, Haien He, Jun Lu, Yishan Liang, Lianghuan Tang, Jianwei Zou, Yu Zhang, Yanna Huang, Qinyang Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeat stress (HS) induced by high ambient temperatures markedly disrupts metabolic homeostasis and meat quality development in ruminants, with reduced intramuscular fat (IMF) deposition is considered a major contributor to meat quality deterioration. However, the roles and underlying mechanisms by which inflammatory responses and ferroptosis in the regulation of IMF deposition in Hu sheep under HS conditions remain unclear. This study showed that HS markedly increased the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-8, and induced activation of the NF-κB signaling cascade, thereby suppressing the expression of the anti-ferroptotic enzyme glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4). These changes resulted in excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species, enhanced lipid peroxidation, elevated ferrous iron (Fe2+) levels, ultimately inducing ferroptosis. Further analyses demonstrated that blocking inflammatory responses or inhibiting NF-κB signaling restored GPX4 expression and alleviated ferroptosis, thereby improving fat deposition under HS conditions. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that HS modulates ferroptosis through the “inflammatory cytokine–NF-κB/GPX4 signaling axis,” thereby modulating IMF accumulation in Hu sheep. This study provides new mechanistic insights and potential intervention targets for improving meat quality in sheep under high-temperature conditions.Keywords: Heat stress, Intramuscular preadipocytes, Inflammation, NF-κB signaling pathway, Ferroptosis

Introduction

Hu sheep constitute an important indigenous breed in China, originating from the Taihu Lake region of Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces(Bao et al., 2024). Due to their high reproductive performance, rapid growth rate, superior meat quality, and strong adaptability to diverse production environments, Hu sheep are widely utilized in high-quality lamb production systems (Zhao et al., 2024). The temperature–humidity index (THI) is widely employed as an metric for assessing heat stress (HS) in livestock(Correddu et al., 2025). However, under persistently high temperature and humidity conditions during summer, in Hu sheep, THI frequently reaches levels indicative of severe HS, as evidenced by elevated breathing frequency and decreased feed consumption(Xu et al., 2022), and accompanied by metabolic disturbances and disruption of inflammatory homeostasis (Q. Cao et al., 2021). Growing evidence suggests that HS significantly reduces meat tenderness and water-holding capacity, impairs sensory attributes, and ultimately contributes to meat quality deterioration, resulting in substantial economic losses (Zhang, Zhang, Wang, et al., 2023).

HS is recognized as a major environmental factor affecting meat quality in livestock and poultry (S. Chen et al., 2021). For instance, severe HS has been shown to reduce intramuscular fat (IMF) content in Hu sheep and consequently lead to meat quality deterioration (Zhang, Zhang, Wei, et al., 2023). Under normal physiological conditions, a moderate inflammatory response contributes to maintaining homeostasis (Didier et al., 2023). However, prolonged or excessive HS disrupts inflammatory regulation, thereby shifting protective responses toward pathological inflammation and exacerbating tissue damage (Hoel et al., 2024; Y.-L. Liu et al., 2022). Prior evidence suggests that HS is associated with a marked increase in pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and induces marked inflammatory injury in the muscle tissue of meat sheep (Lu et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2022; B. Wang et al., 2024). Consequently, a sustained inflammatory state is considered a key contributor to the deterioration of meat quality.

The nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) signaling is critically involved in the control of inflammation across various stress environments (Yao et al., 2025). Upon HS exposure, inflammatory cytokines activate the IκB kinase (IKK) complex, leading to IκB breakdown and enabling NF-κB nuclear translocation, thus promoting transcription of inflammation-associated genes. (Anwar et al., 2024; Dhingra et al., 2015; Mitrasinovic, 2023). This pathway is implicated in diverse pathological processes; for example, in a zebrafish tissue repair model, TNF-α–mediated NF-κB signaling has been shown to be essential for regulating macrophage responses to tissue injury (Arkoudi et al., 2025).

Ferroptosis, a regulated form of cell death characterized by iron dependence and lipid peroxidation, has gained growing interest in the field of meat science. (Dixon et al., 2012). Ferroptosis is characterized by decreased expression of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), enhanced retention of ferrous iron (Fe2+), and increased lipid peroxide (LPO) content and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production(J. Y. Cao & Dixon, 2016; Rochette et al., 2022). Moreover, ferroptosis signaling has been reported to disrupt hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) protein homeostasis, which may consequently reduce lipid droplet accumulation(X. Wang et al., 2025). Increasing evidence suggests a close association between inflammatory responses and the ferroptotic process (Wen et al., 2025). Ferroptosis-related biological alterations have been observed in diverse inflammatory models, and modulation of ferroptosis has been shown to modulate the progression of inflammation (Song et al., 2025). For example, in a lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced intestinal inflammation model in broiler chickens, inflammatory responses were accompanied by marked ferroptotic features (C. Chen et al., 2025), thereby suggesting a potential functional coupling between inflammatory signaling and ferroptosis.

Emerging evidence indicates that NF-κB signaling participates in the regulation of ferroptosis(Chen, Guo, et al., 2023). However, under HS conditions, whether inflammation-mediated NF-κB signaling regulates ferroptosis in ovine intramuscular preadipocytes remains largely unexplored. Based on our preliminary observations, HS can elevate serum inflammatory markers, decrease IMF deposition, and impair meat quality in Hu sheep (Wei et al., 2026; Zhang, Zhang, Wei, et al., 2023). We hypothesized that HS-induced inflammatory responses may regulate ferroptosis in ovine intramuscular preadipocytes through the NF-κB/GPX4 signaling axis, thereby affecting IMF deposition. To address this hypothesis, this study integrated in vivo experiments with in vitro validation to systematically examine the NF-κB/GPX4 signaling axis in HS-triggered ferroptosis, aiming to elucidate underlying mechanisms and identify possible intervention approaches to improve lamb meat quality under high-temperature conditions.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

The animal experimental protocols were authorized by the Ethics Committee for Animal Use at Guangxi University. (Approval No. GXU-2023-0135). The experiment was conducted at the mutton sheep breeding farm affiliated with the Agricultural Science and Technology City of Guangxi University.

Animals and experimental design

Twelve healthy male Hu sheep (12 months of age; body weight 47.23 ± 0.66 kg) were randomly assigned to either a thermoneutral (TN) or a heat stress (HS) group, with six animals per group. The animals were housed in two environmentally controlled chambers and provided with a complete pelleted diet throughout the experimental period. The TN group was maintained at a stable ambient temperature of 23 °C under standard moisture conditions throughout the experiment. For the HS group, the temperature was gradually increased to 36 °C at 08:00 each day and maintained under the same humidity conditions. At 18:00, the temperature was reduced to 28 °C and maintained until the start of the next cycle. Both groups were maintained under these conditions for 50 consecutive days. At the end of the trial, the average temperature–humidity index (THI) was calculated. The mean THI was approximately 21 in the TN group and 30 in the HS group. Based on THI classification standards, values of 22.2–23.3 correspond to mild heat stress, 23.4–25.6 correspond to severe heat stress, and values exceeding 25.6 represent extremely severe heat stress. The trial included a 7-day acclimation phase followed by a 50-day experimental phase. Samples were obtained on days 1 and 50 of the experimental period. Following completion of the feeding trial, the sheep were euthanized in a humane manner, and longissimus dorsi (LD) muscle samples were obtained using established protocols (Zhang, Zhang, Wei, et al., 2023). The samples were transferred into cryogenic tubes and rapidly immersed in liquid nitrogen for later analysis.

Determination of IMF content in the longissimus dorsi muscle of Hu sheep

Three Hu sheep per group were randomly selected from the TN and HS groups. After removal of the peripheral fascia and adipose tissue, the muscle samples were cut into small pieces and thoroughly minced into homogenized meat paste using a meat grinder (Huifeng Machinery, Shandong, China). The minced samples were then air-dried at 65 °C and ground into powder using a grinder (Yunbang, Zhejiang, China). IMF content was measured using the Soxhlet extraction method. Briefly, 2–4 g of muscle powder was weighed, wrapped in filter paper, and recorded as W1. The wrapped samples were dried overnight in an oven (Yuejin Medical, Shanghai, China) at 95–105 °C, after a 30 min desiccation period, the samples were weighed. They were then returned to the oven for an additional 1–2 h at the same temperature, followed by another 30 min in a desiccator (Anwei, Beijing, China) and a second weighing; this value was designated as W2.The dried samples were placed into extraction thimbles (Wuyue, Shandong, China), and anhydrous diethyl ether (Hushi, Shanghai, China) was added. Reflux extraction was performed for 6–10 h. Following extraction, the solvent was removed, and the samples were dried at 100 °C for 1 h, then placed in a desiccator for 0.5 h and weighed to constant mass, with the resulting value defined as W3. IMF (%) = (W2 − W3)/W1 × 100.

Cell culture

Intramuscular preadipocytes of Hu sheep were obtained from Shanghai GeneStar BioTechnology Co., Ltd. Cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) containing 10% FBS (SeraPro, Suzhou, China) and incubated at 37 °C under 5% CO₂. (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Cells at approximately 80–90% confluence were then subcultured and used for experiments.

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

Three biological samples of Hu sheep longissimus dorsi tissue and intramuscular preadipocytes were randomly selected from each group, and using TRIzol reagent (Vazyme, Nanjing, China), total RNA was extracted as per the manufacturer's protocol. RNA concentration and purity were analyzed with a full-wavelength microplate reader. (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Thereafter, reverse transcription was performed to convert total RNA into cDNA using a kit (Takara, Japan). Gene expression levels were standardized against an internal control using the 2^−ΔΔCt method, with each gene examined in triplicate. The 2^−ΔCt values were used for further statistical analysis (Yang et al., 2025). The full set of primers employed for RT–qPCR is provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Western blot analysis

From each group, three tissue samples and trypsin-digested cells were randomly selected and lysed in 250 μL of RIPA lysis buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China) containing protease inhibitor PMSF (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 30 min on ice. After spinningat 12,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C, the supernatants were harvested. Equivalent quantities of protein were resolved by SDS–PAGE (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) and then transferred to PVDF membranes for immunoblotting. Primary immunoreagents against GAPDH, HSP70, TNF-α, GPX4, phosphorylated p65 (p-p65), p65, IKKβ, and phosphorylated IKKβ (p-IKKβ) (Abclonal, China) were applied to the membranes at dilutions of 1:500–1:2000 in antibody dilution buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 2 h at room temperature. The membranes were then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Abclonal, China) for 1 h at room temperature, followed by visualization of protein bands using an imaging system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

Determination of ferrous iron (Fe2+) content in muscle tissue

Three Hu sheep were randomly selected from both the TN group and the HS group (n = 3 per group). In brief, 0.1 g of LD muscle tissue was weighed, blended with 0.9 mL of extraction buffer, and homogenized using a cryogenic tissue grinder (Jingxin, Shanghai, China). After centrifugation, the supernatants were obtained for further analysis. Next, 300 μL of standard solution or sample supernatant was pipetted into the appropriate standard and assay tubes. The procedure was subsequently carried out following the guidelines provided by the tissue ferrous iron assay kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China), with each sample analyzed in triplicate. Fe2+(μmol/kg)=3.366△A+0.009 (ΔA = OD value of the sample well − OD value of the blank well).

CCK-8 assay for selection of HS exposure duration

Cells grown in 96-well plates to 60% confluence were then exposed to 42 °C heat stress for 1–5 days. The culture medium was discarded after heat stress treatment,followed by a single wash with 1× PBS (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Subsequently, culture medium containing 10% CCK-8 solution (BMC, Shenzhen, China) was added to the wells, followed by incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 2 h. The OD values were determined at 450 nm using a microplate reader. This method was likewise employed to establish the optimal concentrations of PDTC and JTE-607 inhibitors (Sikejie, Shandong, China).

Intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) detection

The culture medium of cells in both the 37 °C and 42 °C groups was aspirated after heat stress treatment, followed by a single wash with 1× PBS. Subsequently, three biological replicates per group were used, and each well received 500 μL of DCFH-DA working solution(1:2000 dilution; ROS Detection Kit, Solarbio, Beijing, China). The cells were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. After rinsing three times with DMEM/F12. To evaluate the protective effects of PDTC and JTE-607 against HS, four experimental groups were established: the 42 °C group, 42 °C + PDTC group, 42 °C group, and 42 °C + JTE-607 group. ROS detection was subsequently performed as described above.

Intracellular ferrous iron (Fe2+) detection

The culture medium of cells in both the 37 °C and 42 °C groups was aspirated after heat stress treatment, followed by a single wash with 1× PBS. Subsequently, 100 μL of RhoNox-6 working solution (Fe2+ Detection Kit, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was applied to three wells per group as directed by the provided protocol. The cells were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. To evaluate the protective effects of PDTC and JTE-607 against HS, four experimental groups were established: the 42 °C group, 42 °C + PDTC group, 42 °C group, and 42 °C + JTE-607 group. Fe2+ detection was subsequently performed as described above.

Lipid peroxidation (LPO) detection in cells

The culture medium of cells in both the 37 °C and 42 °C groups was aspirated after heat stress treatment, followed by a single wash with 1× PBS. Subsequently, 500 μL of BODIPY 581/591 C11 working solution (1:1000 dilution) was added to three wells per group. The cells were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. To evaluate the protective effects of PDTC and JTE-607 against HS, four experimental groups were established: the 42 °C group, 42 °C + PDTC group, 42 °C group, and 42 °C + JTE-607 group. LPO detection was subsequently performed as described above.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed in SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and visualized with GraphPad Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), and are shown as mean ± SEM. Normality and variance homogeneity were assessed prior to analysis. Group differences were analyzed using one-way ANOVA, followed by Duncan's multiple range test, with statistical significance defined as P < 0.05.

Result

Heat stress increases inflammatory cytokine levels and triggers ferroptosis in muscle of Hu sheep

Our previous results showed that HS significantly reduced IMF content in Hu sheep (9.82±0.38: 9.42±0.52). To explore the mechanisms associated with HS-mediated IMF reduction, RT-qPCR and Western blot assays were conducted. The findings indicated that the mRNA levels of HSP60, HSP90, and HSP70 were markedly increased under HS conditions (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A), accompanied by a pronounced increase in HSP70 protein levels (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1C, D), confirming the successful establishment of the HS model in Hu sheep. Further analysis of inflammation-related markers showed a significant elevation in the expression of inflammation-related genes (P < 0.05) and downregulation of the anti-inflammatory marker TGF-β in the HS group (P < 0.05). Consistently, TNF-α protein expression was markedly increased under HS conditions (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1B, C and D), indicating that HS elicited a pronounced inflammatory response in muscle tissue. Assessment of ferroptosis-related markers revealed significant reductions in GPX4 mRNA and protein expression in the HS group relative to controls (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1E, F and G). In addition, Fe2+ content in the LD muscle was notably increased in the HS group relative to the control group. (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1H). Collectively, these results suggest that HS may suppress GPX4 expression through inflammation activation, leading to Fe2+ accumulation and the induction of ferroptosis, thereby contributing to the reduction of IMF content in Hu sheep.

HS–induced inflammatory responses promote ferroptosis in intramuscular preadipocytes

Intramuscular preadipocytes of Hu sheep were cultured in vitro to further validate the effects of HS on ferroptosis in intramuscular adipose cells. First, a CCK-8 assay was performed to screen HS treatment conditions based on a previously described protocol (Chen, Yuan, et al., 2023), with exposure to 42 °C for 2 days identified as the optimal condition for establishing the cellular HS model (Supplementary Fig. 1). Under these conditions, RT–qPCR and Western blot assays revealed significant upregulation of HSP60, HSP90, as well as HSP70 mRNA (Fig. 2A) and in HSP70 protein abundance in the HS group relative to controls (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2D and E), confirming the successful establishment of the HS model in intramuscular preadipocytes. Further analysis of inflammation-related factors revealed marked increase in genes associated with inflammatory responses (P < 0.01) and downregulation of the anti-inflammatory gene TGF-β in the HS group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2C). Consistently, TNF-α protein expression was markedly elevated following HS treatment (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2D and E). In contrast, under HS conditions, GPX4 mRNA (Fig. 2B) along with its protein expression exhibited a significant reduction relative to controls (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2D and E). Moreover, HS treatment led to marked increases in intracellular ROS, LPO, and Fe2+ (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2F–K). In summary, these findings suggest that HS promotes inflammatory responses, suppresses ferroptosis-related gene expression, and ultimately induces ferroptosis in intramuscular preadipocytes.

NF-κB activation is required for heat stress–induced ferroptosis and is suppressed by PDTC

To explore the relationship between inflammatory factors and ferroptosis, we proposed that the canonical NF-κB pathway may participate in regulating cellular ferroptosis. This hypothesis was subsequently verified through in vitro experiments. Analysis by RT-qPCR and Western blot revealed that HS treatment induced a considerable increase in mRNA transcripts of IKKβ and p65 in Hu sheep intramuscular preadipocytes. (P < 0.01) (Fig. 3A). Consistently, protein abundance of IKKβ, p-IKKβ, p65, and p-p65, along with the p-IKKβ/IKKβ ratio revealed a marked rise (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3B, C). These results indicate that HS may trigger the NF-κB pathway via the induction of inflammatory responses, consequently modulating the expression of downstream ferroptosis-related genes and ultimately promoting cellular ferroptosis.

To further examine the implication of the NF-κB pathway in HS-induced ferroptosis, assay was performed at the outset to ascertain the optimal dose of the NF-κB inhibitor pyrrolidine dithiocarbamate (PDTC). The results revealed maximal cell viability at 300 nM (Supplementary Fig. 2), which was consequently selected for subsequent experiments. Following treatment with 300 nM PDTC under 42 °C HS conditions, mRNA expression of NF-κB pathway genes IKKβ and p65 was significantly downregulated (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3D). Consistently, increased protein levels of IKKβ, p-IKKβ, p65, and p-p65 were accompanied by a higher p-IKKβ/IKKβ ratio (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3E, F). Meanwhile, GPX4 and downstream ferroptosis marker transcripts (Fig. 3G) and protein abundance (Fig. 3E, F) were significantly upregulated (P < 0.05). In addition, PDTC treatment markedly reduced intracellular levels of reactive ROS, LPO, and Fe2+ (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3H–M). Collectively, the findings suggest inhibition of the NF-κB signaling pathway effectively attenuates HS-induced ferroptosis in intramuscular preadipocytes.

Inhibition of inflammatory cytokines attenuates heat stress–induced ferroptosis via blockade of the NF-κB signaling pathway

To determine whether HS induces ferroptosis in intramuscular preadipocytes via inflammatory cytokines, the inflammatory cytokine inhibitor methoxyflavone (JTE-607) was employed for further intervention. The optimal concentration of JTE-607 was initially determined by a CCK-8 assay, which showed the highest cell viability at 100 nM (Supplementary Fig. 3); therefore, this concentration was selected for subsequent experiments. Following treatment with 100 nM JTE-607 under 42 °C HS conditions, mRNA levels of inflammation-associated genes were markedly reduced (P < 0.01), while the anti-inflammatory gene TGF-β showed a significant increase. (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4A). In addition, TNF-α protein levels were markedly reduced. (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4C, D). Concomitantly, mRNA expression of NF-κB pathway genes IKKβ and p65 was significantly downregulated (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4B), accompanied by decreased abundance of IKKβ, p-IKKβ, p65, and p-p65, the p-IKKβ/IKKβ ratio was also markedly reduced (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4E, F), indicating that inhibition of inflammatory cytokines effectively blocked NF-κB pathway activation. Based on this, mRNA and protein expression of the ferroptosis marker GPX4 were significantly upregulated following JTE-607 treatment (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4C, D, K). Moreover, intracellular levels of reactive ROS, LPO and Fe2+ were markedly decreased (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4G–M). Collectively, these observations imply that suppressing inflammatory cytokines suppresses NF-κB pathway activation, thereby attenuating HS-induced ferroptosis in ovine intramuscular preadipocytes, potentially contributing to meat quality improvement.

Discussion

HS is a major environmental factor limiting ruminant productive performance and meat quality. When ambient temperature exceeds the thermotolerance threshold of animals, a cascade of physiological and metabolic disturbances is triggered, ultimately leading to reduced productivity and economic losses(Devapriya et al., 2021). In the present study, Hu sheep subjected to prolonged HS, along with their intramuscular preadipocytes, exhibited a marked elevation in inflammatory cytokine levels, concomitant with downregulation of the key ferroptosis regulator GPX4. Heat treatment also significantly increased reactive ROS production, LPO and Fe2+ accumulation, indicating robust activation of ferroptosis, accompanied by a reduction in IMF content. Consistent with the current findings, prior research has indicated that high temperatures can indirectly impair IMF deposition through inflammation-mediated ferroptosis pathways (M. Zhang et al., 2020).

Beyond its detrimental effects on meat quality, HS has been reported to induce tissue injury through molecular stress responses and inflammatory reactions(J. Zhou et al., 2025). In the present study, heat shock protein–related genes was markedly elevated in the LD muscle of Hu sheep under HS, accompanied by a rising trend in inflammation-associated gene expression. Previous studies have demonstrated that HS activates heat shock protein expression and induces molecular changes associated with inflammation and cell death (Mendonca et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2020), consistent with our observations. Notably, recent evidence has highlighted a close association between inflammatory responses and ferroptosis. GPX4, a hallmark gene of ferroptosis, can be suppressed by sustained elevations of pro-inflammatory cytokines, thereby promoting ferroptosis initiation (D. Zhou et al., 2024). Moreover, ferroptosis in adipocytes has been reported to inhibit adipogenic differentiation, thereby reducing lipid droplet accumulation(Schwärzler et al., 2022). In this study, increased inflammatory cytokine levels were accompanied by decreased GPX4 expression and enhanced Fe2+ accumulation in tissues. These findings suggest that HS may promote ferroptosis via inflammation-mediated mechanisms, which may consequently impair IMF deposition.

To further elucidate the potential mechanisms by which HS induces inflammation and promotes ferroptosis, this study included in vitro experiments. The results demonstrated that under HS conditions, inflammatory cytokine levels were significantly elevated in ovine intramuscular preadipocytes, accompanied by a marked downregulation of GPX4 expression and significant increases in intracellular reactive ROS, LPO and Fe2+ levels, indicating that HS induces ferroptosis in intramuscular preadipocytes. Previous research has demonstrated that inflammation-related signaling pathway activation can drive the ferroptotic process. (Y. Chen et al., 2022), and that prolonged inflammatory states may induce ferroptosis by suppressing GPX4 expression and enhancing Fe2+ influx, thereby facilitating Fenton reactions and lipid peroxidation (Chen, Yuan, et al., 2023). These findings provide mechanistic support for the observations obtained in the present study.

NF-κB signaling represents a well-established intracellular mechanism essential for maintaining metabolic homeostasis (J. Chen & Chen, 2013). In the present study, HS resulted in a pronounced increase in IKKβ and p65 expression, two key elements of the NF-κB pathway, in intramuscular preadipocytes, suggesting that HS activates the NF-κB pathway via stimulation of pro-inflammatory cytokine release, thereby contributing to ferroptosis regulation. Prior investigations indicate a close relationship between p65 activation and cytokine release, which in turn influences downstream GPX4 expression (Raslan et al., 2025; Y. Zhang et al., 2025), serving as a molecular link between inflammatory responses and ferroptosis. To clarify the contribution of the NF-κB pathway to HS-induced ferroptosis, pyrrolidine dithiocarbamate (PDTC) was applied, a widely used NF-κB inhibitor, was employed (F. Wang et al., 2020). Intramuscular preadipocytes treated with 300 nM PDTC under HS conditions exhibited effective NF-κB inhibition, significant upregulation of GPX4, and reductions in ROS, LPO, and Fe2+ levels, thereby mitigating HS-induced ferroptosis. These findings align with earlier studies indicating that PDTC suppresses NF-κB activity and modulates downstream gene expression (Li et al., 2021). Additionally, blocking NF-κB signaling has been reported to reverse GPX4 downregulation in colitis and osteoarthritis models(Han et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025), highlighting its important role in HS-induced ferroptosis.

Excessive inflammatory responses are recognized as a major factor influencing growth, development, and meat quality in livestock and poultry (Johnson et al., 2024). JTE-607, an inflammatory cytokine inhibitor, has been identified as a targeting inhibitor of cleavage and polyadenylation specificity factor 3 (CPSF3). Inhibition of CPSF3 results in the accumulation of unprocessed mRNA transcripts, thereby impairing the maturation and expression of inflammatory cytokines(Kakegawa et al., 2019), has been shown to effectively alleviate inflammation-associated cellular damage in various animal models (Alahmari et al., 2024). In the present study, Hu sheep intramuscular preadipocytes treated with 100 nM JTE-607 under HS conditions treatment of Hu sheep intramuscular preadipocytes with 100 nM JTE-607 markedly decreased the levels of inflammatory cytokines and key elements of the NF-κB pathway (IKKβ and p65), along with marked upregulation of GPX4 and improvement in ferroptosis-related parameters. These results indicate that inhibition of inflammatory responses and NF-κB pathway activity effectively mitigates HS-induced ferroptosis. Echoing these observations, prior research has indicated that JTE-607 at different doses significantly attenuates LPS-induced pulmonary inflammation in rats and inhibits cell death (Lin et al., 2024; L. Liu et al., 2023). Collectively, the evidence supports that HS influences ferroptosis in Hu sheep intramuscular preadipocytes through the TNF-α/NF-κB/GPX4 signaling axis.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that HS induces ferroptosis in Hu sheep intramuscular preadipocytes by triggering TNF-α–mediated inflammatory responses and activating the TNF-α/NF-κB/GPX4 signaling axis, leading to GPX4 downregulation. These findings provide novel mechanistic evidence for HS-induced damage to intramuscular preadipocytes and identify potential therapeutic targets, offering a theoretical basis for alleviating HS-induced declines in production performance and meat quality through modulation of inflammatory responses and downstream signaling pathways.

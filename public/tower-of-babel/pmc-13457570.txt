Effect of electron irradiation on the optical and electrical properties of Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate and Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate Di Methyl Sulfoxide Crystals
Authors: Jisma Jose, R Santhosh Kumar, Sandeep Suresh, Eldo Abraham Thomas, G Sreekanth, Ginson P Joseph
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractManganese Mercury Thiocyanate (MMTC) and Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate Di Methyl Sulfoxide (MMTD) crystals were prepared from aqueous solution using a slow evaporation process. The pure MMTC and MMTD were bombarded with electrons of 8 MeV energy in the free air environment. The samples were kept at a distance of 30 cm from the beam exit point, where an almost uniform electron beam distribution exists for an area of 8 cm x 8 cm. The dose rate was adjusted with a current of 20 mA, and the accelerator was operated in pulsed mode at a repetition frequency of 50 Hz. The samples were exposed to a graded electron beam dose of 6 kGy and 8 kGy. Electron beam irradiation is an efficient process whereby high-energy electrons are embedded into a pristine crystal lattice to induce changes in its microscopic structural and electronic environment. The changes after electron beam irradiation are compared with those of pure crystals for Bandgap energy, Urbach energy, Photoconductivity and AC conductivity, and the graphs and tables of comparison are added.Keywords: Irradiation, Organometallic, Nonlinear optical materials, Nonlinear opticalSubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Developing materials suitable for device fabrication is an important challenge in the area of materials science research. Devices for specific applications need specific properties. It is possible to change the properties of the developed materials in a variety of ways. Doping is one of the methods to tune the materials in a desired manner1. Another method is the electron beam irradiation. The stable electron atom potentials, having attained its minimum values, are distorted due to the external electrical field produced by the guest electrons, and after a relaxation period, a stable equilibrium will be formed with the guest electrons and host atoms. A concept called ‘unit displacement energy’, energy needed to knock an atom out of its position in the lattice, enables us to relate the energy of the incoming beam and its microscopic effects produced inside the host material. Another effect is the so-called “thermal spike,” i.e., rapid heating and quenching of a small volume of the material of the irradiated region. All these effects will contribute to the properties of the band gap, specific vibrational modes, electrical conductivity, dielectric constant etc. Changes in dielectric constant and optical band gap over electron beam irradiation are reported for ferroelectric triglycine sulphate crystals. Studies in other alloys reveal similar changes in lattice parameters. Irradiation plays a major role in shaping the properties of the pure materials in addition to doping2,3. Here, the newly added electrons will first deform the lattice atmosphere of the synthesized pure crystals of Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate (MMTC) and Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate Di Methyl Sulfoxide (MMTD). Then the lattice will accommodate the visitant electrons as normal by reducing the total effective potential of the lattice after a relaxation time proportional to the total energy of the system. Immediately after irradiation, characterization studies such as determination of optical band gap, changes in the width of the exponential “tail” in the optical absorption spectrum, the photo-conductive and electro-conductive nature of the material are determined, and the changes are reported.

From the literature survey, it is clear that only a few scientists have studied the effect of electron irradiation on the crystals, especially in organometallic crystals4. The presence of newly added electrons affects the complex electron-ion field of the host system, which causes noticeable changes in the electrical and optical properties of MMTC and MMTD, as reported here5,6.

Characterization techniques

Characterization may be defined as an overall explanation on the physicochemical properties of a material. Characterization of a sample includes the study of the chemical properties, structure, defect and their optical properties7. The grown samples of MMTC and MMTD were exposed to a graded electron beam dose of 6 and 8 kGy. The optical absorption of irradiated MMTC and MMTD crystals was also studied. The Photopyroelectric (PPE) studies of the samples were carried out earlier using Perkin Elmer’s STA 60008. The photoconductivity studies were carried out for the sample using Keithley 485 pico ammeter under dark condition as well as photo illumination condition. AC conductivity studies of the pure and irradiated crystals have been done using HIOKI HITESTER 3532-50 LCR meter at different temperatures.

Experimental procedure

MMTC and MMTD crystals were prepared from aqueous solution and the mixture of water with dimethyl sulfoxide, respectively, by a slow evaporation process8–10.

A variable energy microtron facility available at Microtron Center, Mangalore University, was made use of for the experiment. The facility provides electrons of 4–12 MeV energy and bremsstrahlung radiation of energy 3–10 MeV11–13.

The pure MMTC and MMTD were exposed to 8 MeV electrons in the free air environment. The samples were kept at a distance of 30 cm from the beam exit point, where an almost uniform electron beam distribution exists for an area of 8 cm x 8 cm. The dose rate was adjusted with a current of 20 mA, and the accelerator was operated in pulsed mode at a repetition frequency of 50 Hz. The samples were exposed to a graded electron beam dose of 6 and 8 kGy. The salient features of the Microtron accelerator are detailed elsewhere. The delivered doses were measured using a current integrator calibrated against appropriate radiation dosimeters.

Electron beam irradiation is an efficient method where one adds fast energetic electrons into the virgin lattice and tries to change the microscopic atmosphere of the pure lattice. The expected changes due to this addition are in the bandgap, specific vibrational modes, dielectric constant, conductivity etc.

Results and discussion

UV-Vis-NIR Studies

UV-Vis spectrum of pure and irradiated MMTC and MMTD crystals are recorded using a Varian Cary 5E spectrophotometer, taken in the wavelength range of 190–1100 nm, and are shown in Figs. 1 and 2. Cut off wavelength of the pure and 6 & 8 kGy irradiated MMTC crystal are 385 nm, 389.7 nm and 394 nm respectively whereas for pure and 6 & 8 kGy irradiated MMTD are 384 nm, 395.3 nm and 409.7 nm respectively. The cutoff wavelength of the pure MMTC and MMTD is found to be increase with an increase in the dosage of the electron irradiation. This may be due to the enhancement in the number of charge carriers due to electron irradiation14,15.

In order to determine the band gap of the pure and irradiated samples, a graph has been plotted between hν and (αhν)2 using the Tauc’s relation and is shown in Figs. 3 and 4 and it is clear that the band gap decreases with increasing the dosage of the electron irradiation due to the effect of guest electrons inside the pure crystal lattice by electron irradiation. As the dosage of radiation was increased further, the crystal structure could be distorted due to the penetration of the high-velocity electrons.

Determination of Urbach energy

There are disordered and localized states in the band gap produced by amorphous materials, which results in an exponential absorption tail. An exponential empirical law was followed by this absorption edge16,17. The exponential character of the absorption coefficient of the crystal near the fundamental absorption edge is expressed by the Urbach rule α = α0 exp (hν/Eu), where α is the absorption coefficient, hν is the incident photon energy, and Eu is the Urbach energy. The Urbach energy was determined by drawing a graph between photon energy in eV and the logarithm of the absorption coefficient. Urbach plot of the pure, 6 kGy and 8 kGy MMTC and pure, 6 kGy and 8 kGy MMTD are shown in Figs. 5 and 6, respectively. Comparison of bandgap energy and Urbach energy of the pure and irradiated samples is given in Table 1. Urbach energy was found to increase with increasing the dosage of the irradiation for both MMTC and MMTD. This may be due to the structural disorder caused by the irradiation in the grown samples. The linear region in the Urbach plot explains the disorder in the lattice caused by the irradiation.

Photoconductivity studies

Photoconductivity studies are performed to understand the reaction of the pure as well as the irradiated samples to light. Photoconductivity of the pure and irradiated samples was also determined by varying the volts from 20 V to 400 V in steps of 20 V, by using a visible light source of wavelength λ ≈ 532–600 nm, at a fixed distance from the sample. Each measurement was recorded after allowing an exposure time of about 30–60 s to ensure stable current values. The corresponding dark and photo current were also noted using pico ammeter. Field dependent conductivity of pure and irradiated crystals of MMTC and MMTD is shown in Figs. 7 and 8. It is observed that both dark current (Id) and photo current (Ip) increased with increasing the applied field for pure and irradiated samples.

Photoconductivity of pure and irradiated MMTD crystals.

Pure and irradiated samples show negative photoconductivity, which may be due to the reduction in life time of charge carriers under the illumination with visible light in the presence of a DC source. Photo detecting devices are of technological importance due to the negative photoconductivity18.

It is also observed from the figure that the conductivity of 8 kGy irradiated samples is higher than that of the 6 kGy irradiated samples.

As electron irradiation is known to produce point defects such as vacancies and interstitials, which combine to form defect clusters and localized energy states within the band gap. These defect states act as trapping and recombination centers, significantly influencing charge transport properties. The observed enhancement in photocurrent and conductivity in irradiated MMTC and MMTD crystals is therefore attributed to increased charge carrier generation and hopping transport through these irradiation-induced localized states. The above interpretations are consistent with established theoretical studies of radiation damage and defect-assisted conduction19,20.

Conclusion

Good quality crystals of Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate (MMTC) and Manganese Mercury Thiocyanate Di Methyl Sulfoxide (MMTD) samples were exposed to a graded electron beam dose of 6 and 8 kGy. Pure and irradiated samples were subjected to the optical and electrical investigations. The band gaps of irradiated crystals of MMTC and MMTD were found to be decreased with increasing the dosage of the irradiation, whereas the Urbach energy was found to be increasing. Under the illumination with visible light in the presence of a DC source, the pure and irradiated samples show negative photoconductivity, which may be due to the reduction in life time of charge carriers. The AC conductivity properties of the crystals were also investigated and is found to be increased with increasing the dosage of electron irradiation, but decreased with increasing the frequency. Hence it is concluded that the optical and electrical properties of MMTC and MMTD crystals can be tailored in a desired manner by varying the dosage of the electron irradiation, and thereby these materials can be utilized for device fabrications.

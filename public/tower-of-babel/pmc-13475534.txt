Bronchoalveolar Lavage Fluid α Amylase Level a Promising Biomarker of Aspiration Syndrome: A Pilot Study
Authors: Hui Xu, Kun Wang, Yeliang Wen, Ruixue Zhang, Fei Tang, Yan Hu, Hui Geng, Xin Cong, Lianjun Lin, Shiyue Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectiveAspiration syndrome is a common respiratory disease in clinical practice. We hypothesized that bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) α-amylase might be a diagnostic biomarker for aspiration syndrome.MethodsThis pilot, multicenter prospective study reviewed consenting adults who underwent clinically-indicated bronchoscopy at six hospitals. BALF samples were obtained using standardized methods. Amylase levels were measured in clinical biochemical laboratory. Aspiration syndrome was clinically diagnosed based on the Expert Task Force on Diagnosis And Treatment of Aspiration Pneumonia in Adults in China (2022) and the guidelines of the Japanese Respiratory Society.ResultsAmong 248 participants, 159 met clinical criteria for aspiration. The median level of BALF α-amylase in cases was significantly higher than that in controls (2884.2 IU/L vs 30.0 IU/L, P < 0.001). The optimal cut-off was 179.1 IU/L (sensitivity 87.4%, specificity 93.3%, AUC 0.937, 95% CI 0.905–0.970). Subgroup analysis stratified by sex (male or female) and age (< 60 or ≥ 60 years) showed that the specificity and sensitivity remained above 0.80.ConclusionBALF α-amylase might be a promising biomarker for distinguishing aspiration syndrome.

Introduction

Aspiration refers to the inadvertent entry of non-air substances, such as food, liquids, secretions, and vomitus, into the subglottic airways, including the trachea, bronchi, and deeper pulmonary regions.1 Lung damage caused by the inhalation of noxious substances like lipids or acids is called chemical aspiration pneumonitis. When the aspirated material is infectious, it can lead to pulmonary parenchyma infection, known as bacterial aspiration pneumonia. Moreover, the inhaled matter may obstruct the airway, presenting with obstructive features such as atelectasis. Foreign-body aspiration is a serious and potentially fatal event.2 Under normal conditions, the swallowing reflex and laryngeal protective mechanisms prevent foreign material from entering the airways. However, these mechanisms may fail in certain scenarios, such as swallowing disorders, altered consciousness, or gastroesophageal reflux, leading to aspiration.3 Additionally, our previous studies have confirmed that advanced age is an independent and significant risk factor for aspiration.4,5 As global population aging progresses, aspiration syndrome is becoming an increasingly urgent concern.

The rapid identification of aspiration remains a significantly challenge given the lack of typical symptoms and unwitnessed or silent aspiration events. Histologic testing, video fluoroscopy, fiber-optic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing and scintigraphy with radionuclide-labelled food bolus may be insensitive or partly a research tool. These techniques may not reflect the panorama of aspiration which is episodic. Besides, several biomarkers, such as lipid laden alveolar macrophages, pepsin, and bile acids have been identified as evidence of aspiration.3 However, they only reflect aspiration from the gastrointestinal tract. On the other hand, α-amylase shows great potential as a biomarker for aspiration. It generally cannot cross the alveolar-capillary barrier. Except in small cell lung carcinoma, there’s little α-amylase in the trachea, bronchi, and alveoli.6 α-amylase has two isoenzymes: salivary amylase from salivary glands and pancreatic amylase produced by the pancreas, which makes α-amylase a potential biomarker for marking aspiration of gastrointestinal reflux and oral secretions. Nevertheless, our understanding of the diagnostic efficacy of BALF amylase mainly derived from a few reports in Europe, American and Japan.7–9 Therefore, we hypothesized that BALF α-amylase would be higher in aspiration patients and would accurately discriminate aspiration from non-aspiration controls. The objective of this study was to determine whether BALF α-amylase can be used as a promising biomarker for distinguishing aspiration syndrome in Chinese.

Methods

This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University First Hospital (No. 2021–309). All patients provided written informed consent before participation. We conducted a prospective study involving six centers in China from January1, 2018 to August 31, 2023. Patients with small-cell lung carcinoma,6 those unable to tolerate bronchoscopic examination, individuals with contraindications to the procedure, and those declining bronchoscopy were excluded. Additionally, enrollees were excluded if BALF was obtained more than 48 hours after outpatient presentation or hospital admission, or if biochemical analysis could not be performed. The participants were divided into two groups. The aspiration group met at least one of the following criteria: 1) a witnessed episode of choking, coughing, or aspiration of food/liquid/vomitus, as documented in the medical records by the attending physician or nursing staff; 2) evidence of airway foreign bodies found by bronchoscopy; 3) pneumonia with known aspiration risk factors and a history of dysphagia documented in the medical records. Aspiration was confirmed by at least three experienced pulmonologists based on the Expert task force on diagnosis and treatment of aspiration pneumonia in adults in China (2022)10 and the guidelines of the Japanese Respiratory Society. The diagnostic adjudication was performed based solely on clinical, radiological, and bronchoscopic findings, without access to any biomarker data. Control group can be either of the followings: 1) patients with pulmonary nodules without aspiration risk factors, or 2) pneumonia patients not meeting any aspiration group criteria. Bronchoscopic examination was conducted across a spectrum of clinical indications. The bronchoscope was inserted transorally or transnasally into the affected bronchial segment. A total of 50–100 mL of sterile normal saline prewarmed to 37°C was instilled, followed by negative-pressure suction to retrieve the BALF. A recovery rate of >40% was required. The BALF specimens were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 minutes at room temperature, the resulting supernatants were aliquoted and stored at −80°C. The α-amylase level in BALF samples was determined using a colorimetric enzymatic method, strictly following the recommendations of the International Federation of Clinical Chemistry. The assays were performed in two designated biochemical laboratories (affiliated with two of the six participating centers). Figure 1A shows a flow chart for assigning patients to appropriate groups. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 26.0) and R software (version 4.5.0). Continuous variables are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared using the Mann‑Whitney U-test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of BALF α‑amylase, and the optimal cut‑off was determined by the Youden index. Area under the curve (AUC) values are reported with 95% confidence intervals. Subgroup analyses were applied. A post‑hoc power calculation showed >90% power to detect an AUC of 0.937 versus 0.5 at α=0.05. All tests were two‑sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Results

A total of 248 subjects were enrolled, including 159 aspiration patients and 89 controls. In the whole cohort, 161 (64.9%) were male and 87 (35.1%) were female and the median age was 63.5 (IQR: 24) years. The median BALF α-amylase level was median 466.2 (IQR: 4652.3) IU/L. The median level of BALF α-amylase in cases (2884.2 IU/L, IQR: 8124.5 IU/L) was significantly higher than that in controls (30.0 IU/L, IQR: 49.0 IU/L, P < 0.001). To determine an optimal cut-off point of BALF α-amylase in relation to aspiration, ROC analysis was performed. It was found that BALF α-amylase is predictive for diagnosing participants with aspiration with a high sensitivity (87.4%) and specificity (93.3%). BALF amylase specified at 179.1 IU/L was determined as the optimal threshold for distinguishing aspiration, and the AUC was 0.937 (95% confidence interval: 0.905–0.970). Discussion

Aspiration is a common disorder that can lead to acute or chronic inflammatory responses in the lungs.11 The aspiration syndrome can be classified based on whether the substances enter the respiratory system and cause bacterial aspiration pneumonia, chemical aspiration pneumonia, or acute pulmonary emergencies due to foreign body aspiration. The aspiration pulmonary syndrome can affect the airways and the lung parenchyma, leading to acute lung injury, high hospitalization rates and mortality.2 Under normal conditions, the swallowing reflex and laryngeal protective mechanisms prevent foreign material from entering the airways. However, these mechanisms may fail in certain scenarios, such as swallowing disorders, altered consciousness, or gastroesophageal reflux, leading to aspiration.3

Aspiration syndrome is a common respiratory disease in clinical practice. Under normal circumstances, the content of amylase in BALF is extremely low. When aspiration occurs, amylase from oral secretions or gastric contents enters the lungs, resulting in a significant increase in the amylase levels. In this study, we found that the levels of amylase in the BALF of patients with aspiration syndrome could be several times or even dozens of times higher than those of normal individuals. This change has high specificity and can help in the early identification of aspiration events, thereby enabling timely implementation of targeted treatment measures and reducing the damage to the patient’s lungs caused by aspiration syndrome. The level of α-amylase was reported to be positively correlated with the number of risk factors for aspiration.8 Besides, Suzuki et al previously reported that elevated BAL amylase levels (≥ 204 U/L) exhibited diagnostic performance for detecting aspiration, with a sensitivity of 77.1% and specificity of 84.2% (AUC 0.859). Similarly, BAL pepsin ≥ 7.45 ng/mL demonstrated high sensitivity (87.2%) but comparatively lower specificity (59.9%, AUC 0.757).7 Additional biomarkers, including the detection of bile acids in tracheal secretions, have been investigated, though evidence remains limited to small-scale studies.12 Furthermore, the utility of serum procalcitonin in differentiating bacterial pneumonia from aspiration pneumonia has yet to be established.13

The rapid identification of aspiration remains a significantly challenge given the lack of typical symptoms and unwitnessed or silent aspiration events. Our study has some limitations. Firstly, the time interval from the suspected aspiration episode to BAL collection was not available in our dataset. This should be addressed in future studies. Yet, in some cases, aspiration may be silent, making it hard to determine the exact time from aspiration to testing. Secondly, the majority of BALF samples were collected from outpatient settings, resulting in incomplete baseline clinical data for a subset of patients, which may affect the comprehensive interpretation and generalizability of our findings. Thirdly, elevated BALF α‑amylase levels were associated with aspiration syndrome in our cohort, suggesting potential utility as a diagnostic biomarker. However, we measured total amylase without isoenzyme differentiation that cannot determine whether the elevated amylase originated from salivary glands (suggesting oropharyngeal aspiration) or from the pancreas (suggesting gastric aspiration). Future studies incorporating isoenzyme-specific assays are needed to clarify the source of elevated amylase and to refine the diagnostic specificity of this biomarker. While BALF α‑amylase shows promise as a diagnostic biomarker, its clinical application is currently limited by the invasive nature of bronchoscopy. The biomarker may be most useful in patients already undergoing bronchoscopy for other indications such as suspected pneumonia, foreign body aspiration, or unexplained respiratory symptoms, where it could serve as an adjunctive diagnostic tool. Alternatively, it may have value as a research tool for studying aspiration pathophysiology. Future studies should explore whether α‑amylase can be measured in less invasively obtained specimens, such as tracheal aspirates or exhaled breath condensate, to broaden its clinical applicability.

In conclusion, this pilot multicenter study demonstrated that BALF α-amylase levels were significantly elevated in patients with aspiration syndrome and showed promising diagnostic accuracy in distinguishing aspiration from non-aspiration controls. The optimal cut-off of 179.1 IU/L yielded a sensitivity of 87.4% and a specificity of 93.3%. These findings suggest that BALF α-amylase may serve as a valuable adjunctive biomarker for the diagnosis of aspiration syndrome. Future research is warranted to prospectively validate these findings in independent cohorts, explore the differential diagnostic value of α-amylase isoenzymes, investigate the correlation between biomarker levels and clinical outcomes, and compare the diagnostic performance of BALF α-amylase with non-invasive sampling approaches such as tracheal aspirates or exhaled breath condensate. Large-scale, well-designed prospective studies are needed to further establish the clinical utility of this biomarker.

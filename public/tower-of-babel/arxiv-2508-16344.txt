Symplectic Quasi Self-dual, Self-dual, and LCD Codes over Non-unital Rings of Order Six
Altaf Alshuhail

Abstract
We consider codes over the two semi-local non-unital rings of order six, H 23 = ⟨ a , b ∣ 2 a = 0 , 3 b = 0 , a 2 = a , b 2 = 0 , a b = 0 = b a ⟩ , H_{23}=\langle a,b\mid 2a=0,3b=0,a^{2}=a,b^{2}=0,ab=0=ba\rangle, and H 32 = ⟨ a , b ∣ 2 a = 0 , 3 b = 0 , a 2 = 0 , b 2 = b , a b = 0 = b a ⟩ . H_{32}=\langle a,b\mid 2a=0,3b=0,a^{2}=0,b^{2}=b,ab=0=ba\rangle. with respect to a symplectic inner product. Via the decomposition 𝒞 = a ​ 𝒞 a ⊕ b ​ 𝒞 b {\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b} , any H z H_{z} -code splits into a binary component 𝒞 a {\mathcal{C}}_{a} and a ternary component 𝒞 b {\mathcal{C}}_{b} ; this yields characterizations of symplectic self-orthogonal, self-dual, and quasi self-dual (QSD) codes, and allows the introduction of symplectic nice and symplectic linear complementary dual (LCD) codes. Using the automorphism groups of 𝒞 a {\mathcal{C}}_{a} and 𝒞 b {\mathcal{C}}_{b} and double-coset enumeration, we classify, up to permutation equivalence, all symplectic self-orthogonal and QSD H z H_{z} -codes of short even lengths.



Keywords:
Semi-local non-unital rings; symplectic codes; symplectic quasi self-dual (QSD) codes; symplectic nice codes; linear complementary dual (LCD) codes.

1
Introduction

Codes over rings were first investigated in the early 1990’s as a generalization of classical linear codes over fields
[
28
]
. Since 2002, codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
have attracted attention for their connections to Euclidean lattices, ring-linear constructions, and duality frameworks
[
19
,
20
]
. Self-dual codes have attracted considerable attention in coding theory and have been extensively studied over both finite fields and rings
[
21
,
23
,
25
,
27
]
. A suitable analogue of self-duality for non-unital rings of order four is given by quasi-self-dual (QSD) codes with respect to the Euclidean inner product: these are self-orthogonal codes of length
n
and size
2^{n}
. The notion, developed in
[
7
,
9
,
10
]
, is motivated by the failure of the classical size relation
|{\mathcal{C}}|\,|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|=|A|^{n}
(and thus
|{\mathcal{C}}|=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}|
for self-dual
{\mathcal{C}}
) over non-unital rings. Self-orthogonal, self-dual, and QSD codes over non-unital rings of orders four, six, and
p^{2}
(for odd primes
p
) have been constructed and classified under Euclidean duality
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. More recently, symplectic inner products have been introduced to define symplectic self-orthogonality and symplectic self-duality over both unital and non-unital rings
[
31
,
32
]
. Furthermore, linear complementary dual (LCD) codes—linear codes with complementary duals—were introduced by Massey for codes over finite fields
[
30
]
and have since attracted significant interest because of their cryptographic applications
[
8
]
. Over non-unital rings, LCD codes can be defined with respect to two inner products (see,
[
30
,
24
]
for the Euclidean setting) and (see,
[
31
]
for the symplectic setting).

In this paper, we study symplectic codes over the two semi-local non-unital rings of order six,
H_{23}
and
H_{32}
. The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
collects preliminary definitions and facts about symplectic self-orthogonal, symplectic self-dual, quasi self-dual, symplectic nice, and symplectic LCD codes over the fields
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
\mathbb{F}_{3}
. Section
3
is devoted to the study of codes over the rings
H_{23}
and
H_{32}
, including their algebraic properties, code constructions, and duality relations. Section
4
develops the theory of symplectic duality for
H_{z}
-codes, providing characterizations of self-orthogonal, self-dual, quasi self-dual, symplectic nice, and LCD codes in terms of their binary and ternary components, as well as explicit construction methods. Section
5
classifies permutation-inequivalent symplectic self-orthogonal
H_{z}
-codes of even length
n\leq 8
.

2
Binary and Ternary Codes

Let
m\geq 0
be an integer, and write
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
for the
2m
-dimensional vector space over the binary field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Write each vector in the form
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}_{1}\mid\mathbf{v}_{2}),\,\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}.

Define the
symplectic inner product
on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
by

\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}\;=\;\mathbf{x}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{2}\;+\;\mathbf{x}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{1}.

(2.1)

where “
\cdot
” is the usual dot product in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. A
binary linear code

{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n=2m
and dimension
k
is an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{\mathcal{C}}=k
. Equivalently, one calls
{\mathcal{C}}
an
[n,k]
-code. Its
symplectic dual
is

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}\;=\;\bigl\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}\;\bigm|\;\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}=0\;\forall\,\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{C}}\bigr\}.

Similarly, let
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}
be the
2m
-dimensional vector space over the ternary field
\mathbb{F}_{3}
, and write
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}_{1}\mid\mathbf{v}_{2}),\,\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{m}.

Define the
symplectic inner product
on
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}
by

\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{3})}\;=\;\mathbf{x}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{2}\;-\;\mathbf{x}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{y}_{1}.

(2.2)

where “
\cdot
” is the dot product in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{m}
. A
ternary linear code

{\mathcal{C}}
of length
2m
and dimension
k
is an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}{\mathcal{C}}=k
. Equivalently,
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
[2m,k]
-code, and its symplectic dual is

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}\;=\;\bigl\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}\;\bigm|\;\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{3})}=0\;\forall\,\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{C}}\bigr\}.

For
p\in\{2,3\}
and with respect to the symplectic inner product, we call a code
{\mathcal{C}}

symplectic self-orthogonal
(
symplectic-SO
) if
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}
,
symplectic self-dual
(
symplectic-SD
) if
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}
, and
symplectic-LCD
(linear code with complementary dual) if
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}=\{0\}
. Two codes
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
are
permutation equivalent
if there exists a permutation of the
n
coordinate positions sending
{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
. The elements of
{\mathcal{C}}
are called
codewords
.

Each of
H_{23}
and
H_{32}
is a commutative ring without unity and with exactly two maximal ideals
J_{a}=\{0,a\},\,J_{b}=\{0,b,d\},

so that

H_{z}\;=\;J_{a}\oplus J_{b},

where
z\in\{23,32\}
. Moreover,
J_{a}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
J_{b}\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}
, with the natural
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-action

r\cdot 0=0,\quad r\cdot 1=r\quad(\forall\,r\in J_{a}),

and similarly
J_{b}\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}
becomes an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-module via

r\cdot 0=0,\quad r\cdot 1=r,\quad r\cdot 2=2r\quad(\forall\,r\in J_{b}).

Assume
p\in\{2,3\}
. Then each of
J_{a},J_{b}
is naturally an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module, i.e. for all
r,s,t\in J_{a}
(when
p=2
) or
J_{b}
(when
p=3
) one has
r\cdot(s\oplus_{p}t)\;=\;r\cdot s\;\oplus_{p}\;r\cdot t,
where
\oplus_{p}
denotes addition in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Let
m\geq 1
be an integer and set
n=2m
. A
linear
code
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq H_{z}^{n}
or

H_{z}
-code
is an
H_{z}
-submodule of
H_{z}^{n}
. Since
H_{z}\;=\;J_{a}\;\oplus\;J_{b},
any such code decomposes as

{\mathcal{C}}\;=\;a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\;\oplus\;b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b},

where
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
are respectively binary and ternary linear codes.

Remark 3.1
.

For codewords
\mathbf{c}_{1}=a\,\mathbf{x}_{1}+b\,\mathbf{y}_{1}
and
\mathbf{c}_{2}=a\,\mathbf{x}_{2}+b\,\mathbf{y}_{2}
in an
H_{z}
-code, with

\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}
and
\mathbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}
, define the
symplectic inner product
by

\langle\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\rangle_{S}=a^{2}\,\langle\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}\;+\;b^{2}\,\langle\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{3})}.

(3.1)

In particular,

\text{over }H_{23}:\quad\langle\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\rangle_{S}=a\,\langle\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}\qquad(b^{2}=0,a^{2}=a),

\text{over }H_{32}:\quad\langle\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\rangle_{S}=b\,\langle\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{3})}\qquad(a^{2}=0,b^{2}=b).

The
symplectic dual
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is given by

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}=\{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathit{H}}_{z}^{2m}\mid\langle{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\rangle_{s}=0\ \text{for all }{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}\}.

We say
{\mathcal{C}}
is
symplectic self-orthogonal

(symplectic-SO)
if
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}
, and
symplectic self-dual

(symplectic-SD)
if
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}
. An
{\mathit{H}}_{z}
-code that is symplectic self-orthogonal and has size
6^{m}
is called
symplectic quasi self-dual (symplectic-QSD)
.
{\mathcal{C}}
is called a
symplectic nice
if
|{\mathcal{C}}||{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}|=36^{m}
.
symplectic-LCD
(
{\mathit{H}}_{z}
-code with complementary dual) if
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{s}}=\{0\}
. Two
{\mathit{H}}_{z}
-codes
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
are
permutation equivalent
if there exists a permutation of the
n
coordinate positions sending
{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let the alphabet be
H_{23}
. Consider the repetition code

R_{2}=\{00,\,aa,\,bb,\,cc,\,dd,\,ee\}.

It is
H_{23}
-linear and symplectic self-orthogonal. Moreover,
|R_{2}|=6=6^{\,m}\quad(m=1),

so
R_{2}
is
symplectic-QSD
. On the other hand,

R_{2}^{\perp_{S}}=\{00,\,0b,\,0d,\,b0,\,bb,\,bd,\,d0,\,db,\,dd,\,aa,\,ac,\,ae,\,ca,\,cc,\,ce,\,ea,\,ec,\,ee\},

which strictly contains
R_{2}
, hence
R_{2}
is
not
symplectic self-dual.

The following example shows that not every symplectic self-orthogonal code is also Euclidean self-orthogonal; see
[
2
]
for further discussion of Euclidean self-orthogonality.

Example 3.3
.

Let

{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llllll}(0,0,0,0)&(a,0,0,0)&(b,0,0,0)&(c,0,0,0)&(d,0,0,0)&(e,0,0,0)\\
(0,a,0,0)&(a,a,0,0)&(b,a,0,0)&(c,a,0,0)&(d,a,0,0)&(e,a,0,0)\\
(0,b,0,0)&(a,b,0,0)&(b,b,0,0)&(c,b,0,0)&(d,b,0,0)&(e,b,0,0)\\
(0,c,0,0)&(a,c,0,0)&(b,c,0,0)&(c,c,0,0)&(d,c,0,0)&(e,c,0,0)\\
(0,d,0,0)&(a,d,0,0)&(b,d,0,0)&(c,d,0,0)&(d,d,0,0)&(e,d,0,0)\\
(0,e,0,0)&(a,e,0,0)&(b,e,0,0)&(c,e,0,0)&(d,e,0,0)&(e,e,0,0)\end{array}\right\}\subset H_{23}^{4}.

Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is
H_{23}
-code and symplectic self-orthogonal and
|{\mathcal{C}}|=2^{2}\cdot 3^{2}=36
, hence
symplectic-QSD
.
However,
{\mathcal{C}}
is not Euclidean self-orthogonal; for example,

\langle(a,0,0,0),(a,0,0,0)\rangle_{E}=a\neq 0.

4
Symplectic Duality

In this section, we establish several fundamental properties of the symplectic dual of an
H_{z}
-codes.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{23}
-code of length
n
. Then

1.

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
.

2.

({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}={\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}.

Proof.

Write an arbitrary vector
\mathbf{y}\in H_{23}^{n}
as
\mathbf{y}=a\,\mathbf{y}_{a}+b\,\mathbf{y}_{b},
where
\mathbf{y}_{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},\;\mathbf{y}_{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}.

Similarly, any codeword in
\mathcal{C}=a\,\mathcal{C}_{a}\oplus b\,\mathcal{C}_{b}
can be written
\mathbf{c}=a\,\mathbf{u}+b\,\mathbf{v},
where
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}_{a},\;\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{b}.

From Remark
3.1
, we have

\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{c}\rangle_{S}=a\,\langle\mathbf{y}_{a},\mathbf{u}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}.

Hence

\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{c}\rangle_{S}=0\ \forall\,\mathbf{c}\in{\mathcal{C}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\langle\mathbf{y}_{a},\mathbf{u}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}=0\ \forall\,\mathbf{u}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mathbf{y}_{a}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}.

There is no constraint on
\mathbf{y}_{b}
, so

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\;\oplus\;b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}.

Applying duality again gives

(\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}=\bigl(a\,\mathcal{C}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}\bigr)^{\perp_{S}}=a\,(\mathcal{C}_{a}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}\;\oplus\;b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}=a\,\mathcal{C}_{a}\;\oplus\;b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}.

Comparing with
\mathcal{C}=a\,\mathcal{C}_{a}\oplus b\,\mathcal{C}_{b}
yields

(\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}=\mathcal{C}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}=b\,\mathcal{C}_{b}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{C}_{b}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}.

\square

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{32}
-code of length
n
. Then

1.

{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}=a\,\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\perp_{S}}
.

2.

({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}={\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.

Proof.

By symmetry with Theorem
4.1
, swapping
a
and
b
and using Remark
3.1

for
H_{32}
, one immediately obtains
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}=a\,\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\;\oplus\;b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\perp_{S}},

and dualizing again gives

({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}=a\,(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{\perp_{S}}\;\oplus\;b\,({\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\;\oplus\;b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}={\mathcal{C}},

whence
({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}})^{\perp_{S}}={\mathcal{C}}
exactly when
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

\square

Combining the duality formulas of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
with the definition of symplectic nice codes yields the following two corollaries.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{23}
-code of length
n=2m
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic nice if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.1
we have
|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}|=|{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}|\;3^{n}.

By standard symplectic‐duality over
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{a})+\dim\bigl({\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\bigr)=n,

hence
|{\mathcal{C}}_{a}|\;|{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}|=2^{\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{a})}\;2^{\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}})}=2^{n}.

Therefore

|{\mathcal{C}}|\;|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}|=2^{n}\;\cdot\;3^{n}\;\cdot\;|{\mathcal{C}}_{b}|=6^{n}\;|{\mathcal{C}}_{b}|=36^{m}\;|{\mathcal{C}}_{b}|.

It follows that
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic nice, i.e.
|{\mathcal{C}}|\;|{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}|=36^{m}
, if and only if
|{\mathcal{C}}_{b}|=1
, if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

\square

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{32}
-code of length
n=2m
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic nice if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

Proof.

Follows by the same argument as Corollary
4.3
, with Theorem
4.2
replacing Theorem
4.1
.

\square

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be an
H_{z}
-code. then

1.

if
z=23
, then,
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic self-orthogonal if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is a binary symplectic self-orthogonal code.

2.

if
z=32
, then,
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic self-orthogonal if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
is a ternary symplectic self-orthogonal code.

Proof.

Write
{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\subseteq H_{z}^{n}
.

1.

Case
z=23
.
By Theorem
4.1
, we have
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\;\oplus\;b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}.

Hence

{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\;\subseteq\;a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\oplus b\,\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2m}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\subseteq a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}},

But

a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\subseteq a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{a}^{\perp_{S}},

so
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic self-orthogonal precisely when
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is symplectic self-orthogonal code over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

2.

Case
z=32
.
An entirely analogous argument, using Theorem
4.2
in place of Theorem
4.1
, shows

{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}\iff{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\perp_{S}}.

\square

In the next two theorems, we establish a method to construct symplectic self-orthogonal codes and symplectic-QSD codes from binary and ternary linear codes.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
be a binary linear code of length
n=2m
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
be a ternary linear code of length
n=2m
. The code
{\mathcal{C}}
defined by

{\mathcal{C}}=a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\oplus b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{2}

is a symplectic self-orthogonal
H_{23}
-code of length
n
where
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}={\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}={\mathcal{C}}_{2}
if
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is a binary symplectic self-orthogonal code. Moreover, if
|{\mathcal{C}}_{1}||{\mathcal{C}}_{2}|=6^{m}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic-QSD.

Proof.

Write an arbitrary pair of codewords in
{\mathcal{C}}
as
\mathbf{x}=a\,\mathbf{u}+b\,\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=a\,\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+b\,\mathbf{v}^{\prime},

with
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is linear over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is linear over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
, we have
\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1},
and
\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
.
Hence

\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=a\,(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\;+\;b\,(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\;\in\;a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\;\oplus\;b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{2}={\mathcal{C}},

showing closure under addition. Next, let
r\in H_{23}
. By the decomposition
H_{23}=J_{a}\oplus J_{b}
we write
r=r_{a}+r_{b}
with
r_{a}\in J_{a}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
r_{b}\in J_{b}\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}
. The action of
r_{a}
on the summand
a\,{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
coincides with the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-scalar multiplication on
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
, and the action of
r_{b}
on
b\,{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
coincides with the
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-scalar multiplication on
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
. Therefore

r\,\mathbf{x}=r\,(a\,\mathbf{u})\;+\;r\,(b\,\mathbf{v})=a\,(r_{a}\,\mathbf{u})\;+\;b\,(r_{b}\,\mathbf{v}),

with
r_{a}\,\mathbf{u}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
r_{b}\,\mathbf{v}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
. It follows that
r\,\mathbf{x}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, so
{\mathcal{C}}
is closed under
H_{23}
-multiplication.

Together, these show
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear
H_{23}
-code. To see that
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic self-orthogonal, Let
\mathbf{c}=a\,\mathbf{u}+b\,\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=a\,\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+b\,\mathbf{v}^{\prime}

be arbitrary codewords of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then using
a^{2}=a
,
ab=ba=0
,
b^{2}=0
in
H_{23}
one computes

\bigl\langle\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\bigr\rangle_{S}=\bigl\langle a\,\mathbf{u}+b\,\mathbf{v},\,a\,\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+b\,\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\bigr\rangle_{S}=a\,\bigl\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\bigr\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
is symplectic self-orthogonal over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\rangle_{s}^{(\mathbb{F}_{2})}=0
. Hence
\langle\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\rangle_{S}=0
for all
\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp_{S}}
.
Moreover, since
\lvert{\mathcal{C}}\rvert=\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\rvert\cdot\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\rvert
, the condition

\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\rvert\,\lvert{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\rvert=6^{m}
gives
\lvert{\mathcal{C}}\rvert=6^{m}
. Hence
{\mathcal{C}}
is symplectic-QSD.

\square

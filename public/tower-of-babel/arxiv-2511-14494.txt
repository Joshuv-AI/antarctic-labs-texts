Introduction

Throughout the paper, all rings are assumed to be nonzero associative rings with identity, and all modules are assumed to be unitary. For a ring
R
, we adopt the convention that an
R
-module refers to a left
R
-module, while right
R
-modules are viewed as modules over the opposite ring
R^{\sf op}
.

Gorenstein homological algebra provides a powerful extension of classical homological techniques to broader algebraic contexts.
Within this framework, the notion of projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat modules (PGF, for short), introduced by Šaroch and Šťovíček
[
19
]
, refines the classical concepts of
Gorenstein projective modules of Enochs and Jenda
[
7
]
and
Gorenstein flat modules of Enochs, Jenda and Torrecillas
[
8
]
.
PGF modules are characterized by admitting projective coresolutions that remain exact after tensoring with every injective module,
and they have become an essential tool in constructing complete cotorsion pairs and in understanding definability and singular compactness phenomena.
Their foundational significance in modern development of relative homological algebra is now well established; see, for instance,
[
18
,
12
,
20
]
.

Tensor rings provide a natural framework for studying homological invariants in the context of algebraic extensions.
Following Cohn’s work on formal tensor algebras
[
3
]
, a tensor ring of an
R
-bimodule
M
is defined by

T_{R}(M)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}M^{\otimes_{R}i},

where
M^{\otimes_{R}0}=R
and
M^{\otimes_{R}(i+1)}=M\otimes_{R}(M^{\otimes_{R}i})
. This construction encodes iterated bimodule actions and includes as special cases trivial extensions and Morita context rings. When
M
is
N
-
nilpotent
with
N\geqslant 0
, that is,
M^{\otimes_{R}(N+1)}=0
, the tensor ring acquires a useful structural feature: as shown by Chen and Lu
[
2
]
, every
T_{R}(M)
-module can be uniquely represented by a pair
(X,u)
, where
X
is an
R
-module and
u\colon M\otimes_{R}X\to X
is an
R
-homomorphism.

Working over a Noetherian ring
R
and an
N
-nilpotent
R
-bimodule
M

that is finitely generated on both sides,
Chen and Lu
[
2
]
characterized the finitely generated Gorenstein projective
modules over the tensor ring
T_{R}(M)
.
Their theorem states that, under certain conditions, a

T_{R}(M)
-module
(X,u)
is finitely generated Gorenstein projective if and only if

u
is a monomorphism and its cokernel is a finitely generated
Gorenstein projective
R
-module.
This established tensor rings as a flexible and powerful framework in Gorenstein homological algebra.
The recent work of Di et al.
[
5
]
generalizes this result to an arbitrary associative ring
R
and an
R
-bimodule
M
that is not necessarily finitely generated. This development naturally motivates the central question of this work.

0 Question.

How can one describe (projectively coresolved) Gorenstein flat modules over
T_{R}(M)
in terms of
R
-modules, when
M
is an
N
-nilpotent
R
-bimodule?

The main purpose of the paper is to answer this question and to provide further applications. Throughout the rest of this section, we
assume
that
M
is an
N
-nilpotent
R
-bimodule.
For a subcategory
\sf{X}
of
R
-modules, we write
\Phi(\sf{X})
for the subcategory of the category of
T_{R}(M)
-modules defined by

\displaystyle\Phi({\sf{X}})=\{(X,u)\in\mathsf{Mod}(T_{R}(M))\,|\,u\textrm{ is a monomorphism and }\mbox{\rm coker}(u)\in\sf{X}\}.

For the
R
-bimodule
M
, we consider the following Tor-vanishing condition:

\operatorname{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{R}(M,\,M^{\otimes_{R}i}\otimes_{R}P)=0\quad\text{for each }P\in\mathsf{Proj}(R)\text{ and }i\geqslant 1,

which is inspired by the work of Chen and Lu
[
2
]
and plays a crucial role in establishing our main results. Under this condition, our results can be stated as follows; unexplained notation is collected in Section
1
.

Theorem A
.

Let the
R
-bimodule
M
satisfy condition
(\mathsf{T})
.
If
\mathrm{pd}_{R}M<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{R^{\sf op}}M<\infty
, then

\mathsf{PGF}(T_{R}(M))=\Phi(\mathsf{PGF}(R))
.

Previous results, notably by Di et al.
[
5
]
,
have described Gorenstein flat
T_{R}(M)
-modules under coherence conditions.
We seek to extend this understanding to non-coherent rings.
Given that the characterization of flat
T_{R}(M)
-modules is inherently less refined
than that of projective modules (see Lemma
1.5
),
our investigation naturally focuses on a salient and tractable subclass,
for which a complete characterization is provided.

Theorem B
.

Let the
R
-bimodule
M
satisfy condition
(\mathsf{T})
.
If
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{R^{\sf op}}M<\infty
, then an
R
-module
X
is Gorenstein flat if and only if
\mathrm{Ind}(X)
is a Gorenstein flat
T_{R}(M)
-module.

We conclude the paper by presenting two applications of these theorems.
First, we investigate (projectively coresolved) Gorenstein flat modules over trivial extensions of rings, and subsequently we determine when a module over a Morita context ring is (projectively coresolved) Gorenstein flat.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 collects basic material on tensor rings and introduces notation used throughout the paper. Section 2 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
A
. Section 3 contains the proof of Theorem
B
. Finally, Section 4 presents the applications.

\operatorname{Tor}_{\geqslant 1}^{R}(M,M\otimes_{R}P)=0
for each
P\in\mathsf{Proj}(R)
,
we may replace the assumptions in
[
20
, Theorem 2.11]
, namely

\mathrm{fd}_{T_{R}(M)}S(R)<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{{T_{R}(M)}^{\sf op}}S(R)<\infty
,
by the conditions
\mathrm{fd}_{R}M<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{R^{\sf op}}M<\infty
.
Similarly for the assumptions in
[
13
, Corollary 4.8(2)]
.

4.2.
Morita context rings

Let
A
and
B
be two rings,
and let
{}_{A}V_{B}
and
{}_{B}U_{A}
be two bimodules,

\phi:U\otimes_{A}V\to B
a homomorphism of
B
-bimodules,
and
\psi:V\otimes_{B}U\to A
a homomorphism of
A
-bimodules.
We assume further that
\phi(u\otimes v)u^{\prime}=u\psi(v\otimes u^{\prime})

and
v\phi(u\otimes v^{\prime})=\psi(v\otimes u)v^{\prime}

for all
u,u^{\prime}\in U
and
v,v^{\prime}\in V
.
Associated with a
Morita context

(A,B,U,V,\phi,\psi)
,
there exists a
Morita context ring

\Lambda_{(\phi,\psi)}=\begin{pmatrix}A&V\\
U&B\end{pmatrix},

where the addition of elements is componentwise and multiplication is given by

\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
u&b\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&v^{\prime}\\
u^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}aa^{\prime}+\psi(v\otimes u^{\prime})&av^{\prime}+vb^{\prime}\\
ua^{\prime}+bu^{\prime}&bb^{\prime}+\phi(u\otimes v^{\prime})\end{pmatrix};

see
[
1
,
16
]
for more details.
Following
[
10
, Theorem 1.5]
, one can view a
\Lambda_{(\phi,\psi)}
-module
as a quadruple
(X,Y,f,g)
with
X\in\mathsf{Mod}(A)
,
Y\in\mathsf{Mod}(B)
,

f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(U\otimes_{A}X,Y)
, and
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V\otimes_{B}Y,X)
. Also, one can view a
\Lambda_{(\phi,\psi)}^{\sf op}
-module as a quadruple

[W,N,s,t]
with
W\in\mathsf{Mod}(A^{\sf op})
,
N\in\mathsf{Mod}(B^{\sf op})
,

s\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B^{\sf op}}(N,\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\sf op}}(U,W))
, and
t\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\sf op}}(W,\operatorname{Hom}_{B^{\sf op}}(V,N))
.

It follows from
[
11
, Proposition 2.5]
that Morita context rings are trivial ring extensions
whenever both
\phi
and
\psi
are zero.
More precisely, consider the Morita context ring
\Lambda_{(0,0)}
.
There exists an isomorphism of rings:

\Lambda_{(0,0)}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}(A\times B)\ltimes(U\oplus V)\,\,\text{via}\,\,\begin{pmatrix}a&v\\
u&b\end{pmatrix}\mapsto((a,b),(u,v)).

Thus, there exists an isomorphic functor

\mu:\mathsf{Mod}(\Lambda_{(0,0)})\to\mathsf{Mod}((A\times B)\ltimes(U\oplus V))\ \mathrm{via}\ (X,Y,f,g)\mapsto((X,Y),(g,f)),

where
(g,f)
is from

(U\oplus V)\otimes_{A\times B}(X,Y)\cong(V\otimes_{B}Y,U\otimes_{A}X)
to
(X,Y)
.

Note that
(U\oplus V)\otimes_{A\times B}(U\oplus V)\cong(U\otimes_{A}V)\oplus(V\otimes_{B}U)
.
Then the
A\times B
-bimodule
U\oplus V
is
1
-nilpotent if and only if
U\otimes_{A}V=0=V\otimes_{B}U
.
Thus, we obtain the following result by Corollary
4.1
.

4.4 Corollary.

Let
\Lambda_{(0,0)}
be a Morita context ring with
U\otimes_{A}V=0=V\otimes_{B}U
.
Suppose that
\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{\geqslant 1}(V,U\otimes_{A}P_{1})=0=\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{\geqslant 1}(U,V\otimes_{B}P_{2})
for each
P_{1}\in\mathsf{Proj}(A)
and
P_{2}\in\mathsf{Proj}(B)
.
If
\mathrm{pd}_{B}U<\infty
,
\mathrm{fd}_{A^{\sf op}}U<\infty
,

\mathrm{pd}_{A}V<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{B^{\sf op}}V<\infty
,
then a
\Lambda_{(0,0)}
-module
(X,Y,f,g)\in\mathsf{PGF}(\Lambda_{(0,0)})

if and only if both
f
and
g
are monomorphisms,

\mbox{\rm coker}(f)\in\mathsf{PGF}(B)
and
\mbox{\rm coker}(g)\in\mathsf{PGF}(A)
.

Proof.

By the assumption, one can check that

\operatorname{Tor}^{A\times B}_{\geqslant 1}(U\oplus V,(U\oplus V)\otimes_{A\times B}(P_{1},P_{2}))=0
for each

(P_{1},P_{2})\in\mathsf{Proj}(A\times B)
, and both
\mathrm{pd}_{A\times B}(U\oplus V)
and
\mathrm{fd}_{{(A\times B)}^{\sf op}}(U\oplus V)
are finite.
Thus, it follows from Corollary
4.1

that there is an equality

\mathsf{PGF}(\Lambda_{(0,0)})\cong\mathsf{PGF}((A\times B)\ltimes(U\oplus V))=\Phi(\mathsf{PGF}(A\times B)),

where an object
((X,Y),(g,f))\in\mathsf{Mod}((A\times B)\ltimes(U\oplus V))
is in

\Phi(\mathsf{PGF}(A\times B))
if and only if
(g,f)

is a monomorphism and
\mbox{\rm coker}(g,f)
is in
\mathsf{PGF}(A\times B)
.
This yields that
(X,Y,f,g)\in\mathsf{PGF}(\Lambda_{(0,0)})
if and only if
both
f
and
g
are monomorphisms, and
\mbox{\rm coker}(f)\in\mathsf{PGF}(B)
and
\mbox{\rm coker}(g)\in\mathsf{PGF}(A)
.
∎

4.5 Remark.

Under the assumption that
U\otimes_{A}V=0=V\otimes_{B}U

and
\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{\geqslant 1}(V,U\otimes_{A}P_{1})=0=\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{\geqslant 1}(U,V\otimes_{B}P_{2})

for each
P_{1}\in\mathsf{Proj}(A)
and
P_{2}\in\mathsf{Proj}(B)
,
we may replace the assumptions in
[
20
, Proposition 3.4]

that
\mathrm{fd}_{\Lambda_{(0,0)}}(A,B,0,0)<\infty
and

\mathrm{fd}_{{\Lambda_{(0,0)}}^{\sf op}}(A,B,0,0)<\infty

with the conditions that
\mathrm{pd}_{B}U<\infty
,
\mathrm{fd}_{A^{\sf op}}U<\infty
,

\mathrm{pd}_{A}V<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{B^{\sf op}}V<\infty
.

Similarly, by Corollary
4.2
, one obtains the following result.
Also, under the additional assumption that
U\otimes_{A}V=0=V\otimes_{B}U

and
\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{\geqslant 1}(V,U\otimes_{A}Q)=0=\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{\geqslant 1}(U,V\otimes_{B}P)

for each
Q\in\mathsf{Proj}(A)
and
P\in\mathsf{Proj}(B)

we can remove the hypothesis in
[
15
, Theorem 2.5]
that

\mathrm{fd}_{\Lambda_{(0,0)}}(A,0,0,0)<\infty
,
\mathrm{fd}_{{\Lambda_{(0,0)}}^{\sf op}}(A,0,0,0)<\infty
,

\mathrm{fd}_{\Lambda_{(0,0)}}(0,B,0,0)<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{{\Lambda_{(0,0)}}^{\sf op}}(0,B,0,0)<\infty
.

4.6 Corollary.

Let
\Lambda_{(0,0)}
be a Morita context ring with
U\otimes_{A}V=0=V\otimes_{B}U
.

(a)

Suppose that
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{\geqslant 1}(U,V\otimes_{B}P)=0
for each
P\in\mathsf{Proj}(B)
.
If
\mathrm{fd}_{A}V<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{A^{\sf op}}U<\infty
,
then an
A
-module
X
is Gorenstein flat if and only if

(X,U\otimes_{A}X,1,0)
is a Gorenstein flat
\Lambda_{(0,0)}
-module;

(b)

Suppose that
\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{\geqslant 1}(V,U\otimes_{A}Q)=0
for each

.
If
\mathrm{fd}_{B}U<\infty
and
\mathrm{fd}_{B^{\sf op}}V<\infty
,
then a
B
-module
Y
is Gorenstein flat if and only if

(V\otimes_{B}Y,Y,0,1)
is a Gorenstein flat
\Lambda_{(0,0)}
-module.

Statistical inference for Linear Stochastic Approximation with Markovian Noise
Sergey Samsonov, Marina Sheshukova, Eric Moulines, Alexey Naumov

Abstract
In this paper we derive non-asymptotic Berry–Esseen bounds for Polyak–Ruppert averaged iterates of the Linear Stochastic Approximation (LSA) algorithm driven by the Markovian noise. Our analysis yields O ( n − 1 / 4 ) O(n^{-1/4}) convergence rates to the Gaussian limit in the Kolmogorov distance. We further establish the non-asymptotic validity of a multiplier block bootstrap procedure for constructing the confidence intervals, guaranteeing consistent inference under Markovian sampling. Our work provides the first non-asymptotic guarantees on the rate of convergence of bootstrap-based confidence intervals for stochastic approximation with Markov noise. Moreover, we recover the classical rate of order 𝒪 ( n − 1 / 8 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-1/8}) up to logarithmic factors for estimating the asymptotic variance of the iterates of the LSA algorithm.

Introduction

Stochastic approximation (SA) has become foundational to modern machine learning, especially its reinforcement learning (RL) domain. Many classical RL algorithms, including
Q
-learning
[
81
,
77
]
, the actor–critic algorithm
[
37
]
, and policy evaluation algorithms, such as temporal difference (TD) learning
[
77
]
are special instances of SA. Recent research has extensively studied both the asymptotic
[
14
]
and non-asymptotic behavior
[
50
]
of these algorithms. It is important not only to establish the convergence of SA estimators, but also to quantify their uncertainty, which is typically done through the asymptotic normality of corresponding estimates, see
[
59
,
47
]
. Recent works have focused on deriving non-asymptotic convergence rates for SA methods
[
71
,
69
,
83
]
. Notably, most existing results consider settings with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) noise. In contrast, many practical SA applications involve dependent noise, often forming a Markov chain. This additionally complicates the problem. Indeed, even the problem of deriving precise Berry–Esseen type convergence rates for additive functionals of Markov chains is challenging compared to i.i.d. setting, where quantitative results are well-established, starting from Bentkus’ influential work
[
7
]
.

Asymptotic normality of SA estimates is particularly important in practice, as it allows one to construct approximate confidence intervals for the parameters of interest. Different approaches either directly utilize the asymptotic normality and aim to estimate the asymptotic covariance matrix directly (the plug-in or batch-mean methods, see
[
16
,
15
,
65
]
), or rely on the non-parametric methods, based on the bootstrap
[
27
,
66
]
. When the latter approach is applied to the dependent observations, standard bootstrap methods need to be carefully adjusted to account for the dependence structure, see
[
29
,
43
]
.

In this paper, we study a linear stochastic approximation (LSA) procedure. This setting covers several important scenarios, such as the classical TD learning algorithm
[
77
]
with linear function approximation, while allowing for sharper theoretical analysis. The LSA procedure aims to find an approximate solution to the linear system
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\theta^{\star}=\bar{\mathbf{b}}
with a unique solution
\theta^{\star}
, based on observations
\{(\mathbf{A}(Z_{k}),\mathbf{b}(Z_{k}))\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Here
{\bf A}:\mathsf{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
{\bf b}:\mathsf{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are measurable functions, and
(Z_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of noise variables taking values in a measurable space
(\mathsf{Z},\mathcal{Z})
with a distribution
\pi
satisfying
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\mathbf{A}(Z_{k})]=\bar{\mathbf{A}}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\mathbf{b}(Z_{k})]=\bar{\mathbf{b}}
. We focus on the setting where
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an ergodic Markov chain. Given a sequence of decreasing step sizes
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and an initial point
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider the estimates
\{\bar{\theta}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by

\textstyle\theta_{k}=\theta_{k-1}-\alpha_{k}\{\mathbf{A}(Z_{k})\theta_{k-1}-\mathbf{b}(Z_{k})\}\;,~~k\geq 1,\quad\bar{\theta}_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\theta_{k}\;,~~n\geq 1\;.

(2)

The sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
corresponds to the standard LSA iterates, while
\{\bar{\theta}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
corresponds to the Polyak-Ruppert (PR) averaged iterates
[
67
,
59
]
. It is known that, under appropriate technical conditions on the step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}
and the noisy observations
\{Z_{k}\}
(see
[
59
]
and
[
31
]
for a discussion),

\textstyle\sqrt{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta^{\star})\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_{\infty})\;.

(3)

Here the asymptotic covariance matrix
\Sigma_{\infty}
is defined in
Section

3
; see (
13
). Recent works have provided a number of non-asymptotic guarantees for the averaged LSA iterates of
\bar{\theta}_{n}
, in particular,
[
40
,
75
,
48
,
24
,
49
,
23
]
, which study the mean-squared error, high-order moment bounds, and concentration bounds for
\sqrt{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta^{\star})
. However, these results are not enough to establish explicit convergence rates in (
3
) in some appropriate probability metric
\mathsf{d}
:

\mathsf{d}(\sqrt{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta^{\star}),\Sigma_{\infty}^{1/2}Y)\;,

(4)

where
Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I})
. Notable exceptions are recent papers
[
69
,
83
,
72
,
84
]
, which study non-asymptotic convergence rates in the LSA and stochastic gradient descent (SGD) algorithms, as well as in the specific setting of the TD learning algorithm. Our paper aims to complement these findings by providing both a non-asymptotic analysis of the convergence rate in (
3
) and an analysis of an appropriate procedure for constructing confidence sets for
\theta^{\star}
. Our primary contributions are as follows:

•

We derive a novel non-asymptotic bound for normal approximation for projected Polyak–Ruppert averaged LSA iterates
\sqrt{n}u^{\top}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta^{\star})
under Markovian noise in (
3
). Here
u
is a vector on the unit sphere
\mathcal{S}_{d-1}
. Precisely, we establish a convergence rate of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/4})
in Kolmogorov distance. The rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/4})
matches the recent result of
[
84
]
, which considered the particular setting of the TD learning algorithm, and improves over the previous results from
[
74
]
. Our proof strategy differs from that of
[
84
]
and is based on a Poisson decomposition for Markov chains, combined with an appropriate version of the Berry–Esseen bound for martingales, building on the results of Fan
[
28
]
and Bolthausen
[
12
]
.

•

We provide a non-asymptotic analysis of the multiplier subsample bootstrap approach
[
44
]
for the LSA algorithm under Markovian noise. Our bounds imply that the coverage probabilities of the true parameter
\theta^{\star}
can be approximated at a rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/10})
, where
n
is the number of samples used in the procedure. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first non-asymptotic bound on the accuracy of bootstrap approximation for SA algorithms with Markov noise. As a byproduct of our analysis, we also recover the rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/8})
(up to logarithmic factors) for estimating the asymptotic variance of projected LSA iterates using the overlapping batch means (OBM) estimator, previously obtained in
[
16
,
80
]
.

•

We apply the proposed methodology to the temporal difference learning (TD) algorithm for
policy evaluation in reinforcement learning.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we review the literature on Berry–Esseen type results for stochastic approximation (SA) algorithms and methods for constructing confidence intervals for the parameter
\theta^{\star}
in these settings, with particular emphasis on the LSA. In

3
, we obtain a quantitative version of the Berry–Esseen theorem for the projected error of the Polyak–Ruppert averaged LSA under the Kolmogorov distance. In

[

) for LSA and provide corresponding non-asymptotic bounds on the accuracy of approximating the exact distribution of

Accuracy of Gaussian approximation for LSA with Markov noise

We first study the rate of normal approximation for the Polyak-Ruppert averaged LSA procedure. When there is no risk of ambiguity, we use the simplified notations
\mathbf{A}_{n}=\mathbf{A}(Z_{n})
and
\mathbf{b}_{n}=\mathbf{b}(Z_{n})

2

\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}=(\mathrm{I}-\alpha_{n}\mathbf{A}_{n})(\theta_{n-1}-\theta^{\star})-\alpha_{n}\varepsilon_{n}\;,

(5)

\theta^{\star}

\textstyle\Sigma_{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\varepsilon(Z_{0})\{\varepsilon(Z_{0})\}^{\top}]+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\varepsilon(Z_{0})\{\varepsilon(Z_{\ell})\}^{\top}]\;.

(6)

A 1
.

A 2

4
Multiplier subsample bootstrap for LSA

We will apply the multiplier subsample bootstrap (MSB) procedure, a block-based approach that constructs the bootstrap statistic via a blockwise scheme; see
[
38
,
44
]
. Below we describe in details the MSB approach, closely following
[
44
]
. Let
b_{n}
be the length of block, and for each
t=0,\ldots,n-b_{n}
, define
\bar{\theta}_{b_{n},t}=(1/b_{n})\sum_{\ell=t}^{t+b_{n}-1}\theta_{\ell}
, the ”scale
b_{n}
” version of
\bar{\theta}_{n}
. To imitate
\bar{\theta}_{n}
, the MSB estimator of
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is given by

\bar{\theta}_{n,b_{n}}(u)=\frac{\sqrt{b_{n}}}{\sqrt{n-b_{n}+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{n-b_{n}}w_{t}(\bar{\theta}_{b_{n},t}-\bar{\theta}_{n})^{\top}u\;,

(26)

where
\Xi_{n}=\{Z_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n}
, and
\{w_{\ell}\}_{0\leq\ell\leq n-b_{n}}
, the multiplier weights, are i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
random variables, which are independent of
\Xi_{n}
. We write, respectively,
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{b}}=\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\Xi_{n})
and
\mathbb{E}^{\mathsf{b}}=\mathbb{E}(\cdot|\Xi_{n})
for the corresponding conditional probability and expectation. For simplicity, we do not "subsample" the blocks: the theory extends readily, but we do not want to add another layer of notations.
The key idea of the MSB procedure (
26
) is that the "bootstrap world" distribution
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{b}}\big(\bar{\theta}_{n,b_{n}}(u)\leq x\big)
should be close to their "real world" counterparts
\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta^{\star})\leq x\big)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Formally, the procedure (
26
) is said to be
asymptotically valid
, if the quantity

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta^{\star})^{\top}u\leq x)-\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{b}}(\bar{\theta}_{n,b_{n}}(u)\leq x)|

(27)

converges to
0
in
\mathbb{P}
-probability. Typically the authors consider the asymptotic validity of the procedures for constructing the confidence intervals (either with multiplier bootstrap
[
29
,
44
]
or with direct estimation of the asymptotic covariance
[
16
,
80
]
). Our aim in this section is to provide
fully non-asymptotic
bounds on the rate at which the supremum in (
27
) decays as a function of
n
.

The MSB estimator (
26
)
\bar{\theta}_{n,b_{n}}(u)
is normally distributed w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{b}}
, that is,

\textstyle\bar{\theta}_{n,b_{n}}(u)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{\theta}(u))\;,

(28)

with the variance
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{\theta}(u)
given by

\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{\theta}(u)=\frac{b_{n}}{n-b_{n}+1}\sum_{t=0}^{n-b_{n}}((\bar{\theta}_{b_{n},t}-\bar{\theta}_{n})^{\top}u)^{2}\;.

(29)

The parameter
b_{n}
is commonly referred to as
lag window
, or
bandwidth
(see
[
30
]
and references therein). Under the bootstrap probability
\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{b}}
,
\bar{\theta}_{n,b_{n}}(u)
(see (
26
)) is a Gaussian approximation of
\bar{\theta}_{n}
with estimated variance
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{\theta}(u)
. Up to a multiplicative factor tending to
1
as
n
goes to
\infty
, the variance formula in (
29
) coincides with the
overlapping batch mean
estimator (OBM), a well-known technique for estimating the asymptotic variance of the Markov chain, suggested in
[
46
]
. The properties of OBM estimator are studied, see e.g.
[
82
,
19
,
30
]
. In particular, it is known (see
[
30
]
), that the OBM is a consistent estimator of the asymptotic variance of a Markov chain under suitable ergodicity assumptions, provided that
b_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Note that even if
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Markov chain, the iterates
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
alone do not form a Markov chain (one must rather consider the joint process

the quantities:

Application to the TD learning

We illustrate our theoretical findings via the temporal-difference (TD) learning algorithm
[
76
,
77
]
applied to policy evaluation in a discounted Markov decision process (MDP)
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathrm{P},r,\lambda)
. Here,
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
are complete metric spaces with their Borel
\sigma
-algebras;
\mathrm{P}(\cdot\mid s,a)
denotes the state–action transition kernel;
r:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
is the deterministic reward; and
\lambda\in[0,1)
is the discount factor. A policy
\pi(\cdot\mid s)
is a Markov kernel from states to actions. Under
\pi
, the value function is given by

V_{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}\,r(S_{k},A_{k})\,|\,S_{0}=s]
with
A_{k}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid S_{k})
,
S_{k+1}\sim\mathrm{P}(\cdot\mid S_{k},A_{k})
. The induced state process
(S_{k})
is itself a Markov chain with kernel

\mathrm{P}_{\pi}(s^{\prime}\mid s)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\mathrm{P}(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\,\pi(\mathrm{d}a\mid s)
. Equivalently

V_{\pi}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}\,\delta_{s}P_{\pi}^{k}\bar{r}
with
\bar{r}(s)=\int r(s,a)\,\pi(\mathrm{d}a\mid s)
.
We approximate
V^{\pi}
in a linear feature space:
V_{\theta}^{\pi}(s)=\varphi(s)^{\top}\theta
,
\varphi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and seek
\theta_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We impose two standard assumptions:

TD 1
.

\mathrm{P}_{\pi}
is uniformly geometrically ergodic with unique invariant measure
\mu
and mixing time
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.

We also define the design matrix
\Sigma_{\varphi}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\varphi(s)\varphi(s)^{\top}]
and require that it is non-degenerate:

TD 2
.

\Sigma_{\varphi}
is non-degenerate, i.e.
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{\varphi})>0
, and
\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\|\varphi(s)\|\leq 1
.

Under these, the TD-learning is an instance of LSA with
A_{k}=\varphi(S_{k})\bigl[\varphi(S_{k})-\lambda\varphi(S_{k+1})\bigr]^{\top}
and
b_{k}=\varphi(S_{k})\,r(S_{k},A_{k})
(see
Appendix

G
). Refining constants as in
[
55
,
70
]
, one shows:

Proposition 4
.

Under
TD

1
and
TD

2
, the TD updates satisfy the noise-level condition
2
with

\operatorname{C}_{{\bf A}}=2(1+\lambda)

6
Conclusion

We presented the non-asymptotic Berry–Esseen bounds for Polyak–Ruppert averaged iterates of linear stochastic approximation algorithm under Markovian noise, achieving convergence rates of order up to
n^{-1/4}
in Kolmogorov distance. Additionally, we established the theoretical validity of a multiplier subsample bootstrap procedure, enabling reliable uncertainty quantification in the setting of the LSA algorithm with Markovian noise. Our paper suggest a number of further research directions. One of them is related with the generalizations of
Theorem

1
to the setting of non-linear SA algorithms, as well as with obtaining multivariate version of it. It is also an interesting and important question if our non-asymptotic bounds on coverage probabilities provided in
Theorem

2
can be further improved.

Lemma 20

Proof.

. The Kullback-Leibler divergence between

Appendix H
Probability inequalities

Denote by
\Phi
the c.d.f. of a standard Gaussian random variable and set

\mathsf{d}_{K}\bigl(X\bigr)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(X\leq x)-\Phi(x)|.

Proposition 12
.

For any random variables
X,Y
, and any
p\geq 1
,

\mathsf{d}_{K}\bigl(X+Y\bigr)\leq\mathsf{d}_{K}\bigl(X\bigr)+2\mathbb{E}^{1/(p+1)}[|Y|^{p}]\;.

(328)

Proof.

Let
t\geq 0
. By Markov’s inequality

\mathbb{P}(X+Y\leq x)\leq\mathbb{P}(X+Y\leq x,|Y|\leq t)+\frac{1}{t^{p}}\mathbb{E}[|Y|^{p}]\\
\leq\mathbb{P}(X\leq x+t)-\Phi(x+t)+\Phi(x+t)+\frac{1}{t^{p}}\mathbb{E}[|Y|^{p}]\\
\leq\Phi(x)+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(X\leq x)-\Phi(x)|+\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}\pi}+\frac{1}{t^{p}}\mathbb{E}[|Y|^{p}].

(329)

Choosing
t=\mathbb{E}^{1/(p+1)}[|Y|^{p}]
we obtain

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{P}(X+Y\leq x)-\Phi(x))\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{P}(X\leq x)-\Phi(x)|+2\mathbb{E}^{1/(p+1)}[|Y|^{p}]

(330)

Similarly, we may estimate
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(\Phi(x)-\mathbb{P}(X+Y\leq x))
. Hence, (
328
) holds.
∎

Remark 2
.

The result similar to
Proposition

was previously obtained in

. It states that for random variables
X
and

We begin this section with a version of Rosenthal inequality (see the original paper

We provide below a simplified version of the Rosenthal inequality, where the leading term with respect to number of summands

Based on the inequality above, we can also prove the weighted version of Burkholder’s inequality:

Lemma 25
.

Assume
A

1
. Then for any
p\geq 2
and
f:\mathsf{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|f\|_{\infty}<\infty
, any initial distribution
\xi
on
(\mathsf{Z},\mathcal{Z})
, and any

, it holds that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}\bigl[\bigr|\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{\top}A_{k}(f(Z_{k})-\pi(f))\bigr|^{p}\bigr]&\leq(16/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}p^{1/2}\|f\|_{\infty}(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\|A_{k}\|^{2})^{1/2}\\
&\qquad+(8/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\bigl(\|A_{1}\|+\|A_{n}\|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|A_{k+1}-A_{k}\|\bigr)\|f\|_{\infty}\;.\end{split}

Proof.

Under
A

1
the Poisson equation

g(z)-\mathrm{P}g(z)=f(z)-\pi(f)

(354)

has a unique solution for any bounded
f
, which is given by the formula

g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\{\mathrm{P}^{k}f(z)-\pi(f)\}\;.

(355)

Thus, using
A

1
, we obtain that
g(z)
is also bounded with

\displaystyle\|g(z)\|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|\mathrm{P}^{k}f(z)-\pi(f)\|\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(1/4)^{\lfloor k/t_{\operatorname{mix}}\rfloor}\leq(8/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\|f\|_{\infty}\;.

(356)

Hence, we can represent

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{\top}A_{k}(f(Z_{k})-\pi(f))&=\underbrace{\sum_{k=2}^{n}u^{\top}A_{k}(g(Z_{k})-\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k-1}))}_{T_{1}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}u^{\top}(A_{k+1}-A_{k})\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k})+u^{\top}A_{1}g(Z_{1})-u^{\top}A_{n}\mathrm{P}g(Z_{n})}_{T_{2}}\;.\end{split}

The term

can be controlled using Minkowski’s inequality:

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{1/p}[\bigl|T_{2}\bigr|^{p}]\leq(8/3)t_{\operatorname{mix}}\bigl(\|A_{1}\|+\|A_{n}\|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\|A_{k+1}-A_{k}\|\bigr)\|f\|_{\infty}\;.

(357)

{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathcal{F}_{k-1}}\left[g(Z_{k})-\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k-1})\right]=0
a.s. and
|u^{\top}A_{k}(g(Z_{k})-\mathrm{P}g(Z_{k-1}))|\leq(16/3)\|A_{k}\|t_{\operatorname{mix}}\|f\|_{\infty}

[

\mathbb{P}_{\xi}[|T_{1}|\geq t]\leq 2\exp\biggl\{-\frac{2t^{2}}{(16/3)^{2}t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\|A_{k}\|^{2}}\biggr\}\;.

(358)

Hence, applying

26

\mathbb{E}^{1/p}_{\xi}[|T_{1}|^{p}]\leq(16/3)p^{1/2}t_{\operatorname{mix}}\|f\|_{\infty}(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\|A_{k}\|^{2})^{1/2}\;.

(359)

Now we provide a standard moment bounds for sub-Gaussian random variables. Proof of this result can be found in
[
23
, Lemma 7]
.

Lemma 26
.

Let
X
be a random variable satisfying
\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}))
for any
t\geq 0
and some
\sigma^{2}>0
. Then, for any
p\geq 2
, it holds that
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{p}]\leq 2p^{p/2}\sigma^{p}
.

The lemma below is a simple technical statement used to switch between
p
-th moment bounds and high-probability bounds.

Lemma 27
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1/\mathrm{e}^{2})
and let
Y
be a positive random variable, such that

\mathbb{E}^{1/p}[Y^{p}]\leq p^{\upsilon}C_{1}

(360)

for any
2\leq p\leq\log{(1/\delta)}
. Then it holds with probability at least
1-\delta
, that

Y\leq\mathrm{e}C_{1}(\log{(1/\delta)})^{\upsilon}\;.

(361)

Proof.

Applying Markov’s inequality, for any
t\geq 0
we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Y\geq t)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y^{p}]}{t^{p}}\leq\frac{(C_{1}p^{\upsilon})^{p}}{t^{p}}\;.

(362)

Now we set
p=\log{(1/\delta)}
,
t=\mathrm{e}C_{1}(\log{(1/\delta)})^{\upsilon}
, and aim to check that

\frac{(C_{1}(\log{(1/\delta)})^{\upsilon})^{\log{(1/\delta)}}}{(\mathrm{e}C_{1}(\log{(1/\delta)})^{\upsilon})^{\log{(1/\delta)}}}\leq\delta\;.

(363)

Taking logarithms from both sides, the latter inequality is equivalent to

-\log{(1/\delta)}\leq\log{\delta}\;,

(364)

which turns into exact equality.
∎

H.2
McDiarmid inequality

Now we provide a result, which is a version of McDiarmid’s inequality for uniformly geometrically ergodic Markov chains. Before we proceed with this result, we state the following definition from
[
22
, Chapter 23]
:

Definition 1
.

A measurable function
f:\mathsf{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to have the bounded difference property if there exist non-negative constants
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{n-1})
, such that for all
(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n-1}),(z_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{n-1}^{\prime})\in\mathsf{Z}^{n}
it holds that

\bigl|f(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n-1})-f(z_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{n-1}^{\prime})\bigr|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\beta_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{z_{i}\neq z_{i}^{\prime}}\;.

(365)

For functions
f
satisfying the bounded differences property (
365
), the following version of McDiarmid’s inequality holds:

Lemma 28

(Theorem 23.2.2 in
[
22
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathsf{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function, satisfying the bounded difference property with vector
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{n-1})
, such that
\beta_{0}\geq\beta_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\beta_{n-1}
, and
(Z_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Markov chain with the Markov kernel
\mathrm{P}
satisfying
A

1
. Then, for any initial distribution
\xi
on
(\mathsf{Z},\mathcal{Z})
, and any
t\geq 0
it holds

\mathbb{P}_{\xi}\bigl(\bigl|f(Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{n-1})-\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[f(Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{n-1})]\bigr|\geq t\bigr)\leq 2\exp\biggl\{-\frac{t^{2}}{(32/9)t_{\operatorname{mix}}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\beta_{i}^{2}}\biggr\}\;.

(366)

Proof.

The proof directly follows the one of Theorem 23.2.2 in
[
22
]
, together with the appropriate bound on the Dobrushin coefficient implied by
A

1
. We also note that in
A

1
we have assumed that

Therefore,
Lemma

together with the elementary inequality

(408)

\displaystyle\leq\quad\quad\alpha_{k}^{q-1}\frac{4}{b}\biggl(\frac{4(q-1)\gamma}{bc_{0}}\biggr)^{2\gamma(q-1)/(1-\gamma)}\;.

(409)

∎

Lemma 36

How the cosmic voids contribute to stalling and quenching the giant galaxies on their surfaces
Jounghun Lee

Abstract
We report a numerical discovery that the formations of cosmic voids are closely linked with the mechanism through which the giant galaxies on void surfaces establish elliptical shapes, redder colors, and lower specific star formation rates (sSFR). Identifying the voids from the TNG300-1 simulations via the Void-Finder algorithm [ 1 ] , we explore if and how the shapes of the TNG galaxies located on void surfaces are aligned with the directions toward the void centers. Noting that only the giant void-surface galaxies with stellar masses M ⋆ ≥ 10 10.5 ​ h − 1 ​ M ⊙ M_{\star}\geq 10^{10.5}\,h^{-1}\,M_{\odot} exhibit significant tendency of void-galaxy perpendicular alignments, we dichotomize them into two M ⋆ M_{\star} -controlled samples according to their morphologies (elliptical or spiral), colors (redder or bluer), sSFR (lower or higher) and stellar ages (older or younger). It is found that the void-galaxy perpendicular alignment becomes stronger for the cases that the void-surface galaxies have elliptical shapes, redder colors, and lower sSFR, while it shows only weak variation with the stellar ages. The numerically determined void-galaxy perpendicular alignments are also shown to be well described by the analytical one-parameter model developed by Lee [ 2 ] under the assumption of the existence of a linear scaling between the covariance matrices of galaxy shape axes and local tidal tensors. An observational feasibility of this detection is tested by taking into account the projection effects on the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments, rejecting the null hypothesis of no signal in the two-dimensional projected space at the > 99.9 % >99.9\% confidence levels. Our result implies that the compression of adjacent matter due to the formation and rapid expansion of cosmic voids prevent them from radial infall/accretion, which in turn contribute to stalling and quenching the giant void-surface galaxies. Given that the formation epochs and expansion rates of cosmic voids depend sensitively on the dark energy equation of state, we also discuss a possibility of using the abundance of elliptical void-surface galaxies as a probe of dark energy.

because the filaments host the largest number of galaxies

. Nevertheless, the galaxies located on less underdense sites like void surfaces

less crowded environments

Furthermore, it has two additional advantages to investigate the cosmic web effects from the void-galaxy connections.
First, for the case of the filaments, their elongated axes, regarded as being parallel to the minor principal directions of the local tidal fields, can be determined only in
two-dimensional projected space after some simplified assumptions about their geometrical shapes.
Whereas, for the case of the voids, the major principal axes of the large-scale tidal fields can be readily determined in three dimensional space as the directions
from the points on the void surfaces to the void centers
[
24
,
53
]
.
This advantage turned out to be especially useful when the alignments between measurable three dimensional spin axes of spiral galaxies and principal directions of
the local tidal fields are to be measured
[
57
]
. For instance, a significant signal of the mass-dependent transition of galaxy spin alignments was able to be detected directly
from observations only when the alignments were measured on void surfaces
[
58
]
.

Second, for the case of the filaments, their effects on the galaxies sensitively depend on their geometrical features like their thicknesses
[
50
]
, since they form
through collapse only along two principal directions of the large-scale tidal fields. For instance, it was shown by ref.
[
50

that if a filament hosts low-mass (high-mass)
galaxies whose sizes are smaller (larger) than its thickness, it would play the role of promoting (repressing) infall of satellite and gas particles onto the galaxies by generating
their radial (tangential) motions along the directions parallel (perpendicular) to the filaments.
Whereas, for the case of the voids which form through expansion along all of three principal directions of the large-scale tidal fields, their effect was invariably only compression
of adjacent matter without depending on their geometrical features.

In fact, the compression of adjacent matter caused by rapid expansion of a giant void is expected to have two-fold effects on the galaxies located on its surface.
First, it would generate their tangential motions parallel to the directions from the void centers, which in turn would obstruct radial infall of gas particles onto the galaxies
located on its surface. In consequence, provided that the adjacent galaxies are exposed to this compression effects for a long period of time, they would experience gradual
transformation from being accreting to stalled.
Second, it would elongate the shapes of galaxies on its surface in the directions perpendicular to the directions of maximum expansion, creating intrinsic perpendicular
alignments of the galaxy shapes with the directions toward the void center. This two-fold effect of the cosmic voids could be detected by exploring if and how the strengths
of the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments differ between the stalled and accreting galaxies on void surfaces, which is the goal of this paper.

In the following is summarized the procedure that we follow to determine the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments:

•

From each galaxy, determine the separation distance, say
d_{3}
, to its third nearest neighbor.
Taking the ensemble average,
\langle d_{3}\rangle
, and its standard deviation,
\sigma_{3}
, over all galaxies,
determine the value of

as
\langle d_{3}\rangle+3\sigma_{3}/2
, which is found to be

Dichotomize the galaxy into two categories according to the value of

) or field galaxy (

).

•

Partitioning the simulation box into multiple grids of equal side length

best-fit sphere. Find a maximum empty sphere of each block, the farthest boundary of which just begins to reach three wall galaxies.

•

Search for the largest empty sphere in the simulation box and mark it as a maximal one. Then, find the second largest empty sphere in the box and
mark it as another maximal one, if its volume intersects with the largest one by less than
10\%
.
Iterate this search with the other empty spheres of smaller volumes in a decreasing order, as far as their radii are larger than
s_{c}
.

•

Combine the non-maximal spheres into their nearest maximal if their volumes intersect with that of their nearest maximal by more than
50\%

This combined region composed of the maximal and its intersecting non-maximal spheres is finally identified as a void.

•

With the help of the Monte-Carlo integration method
[
22
]

, of each void. Then, determine

R_{\rm eff}\equiv\left(4\pi\,V_{\rm v}/3\right)^{1/3}

3
Dependence of alignment strengths on the galaxy properties

3.1
Variation with morphology and color

Now that the giant void-surface galaxies are found to exhibit strong perpendicular alignments of their shapes with the directions toward the void centers, we would
like to explore if there is any significant difference in the alignment strengths between the stalled and accreting galaxies. The stalled (accreting) galaxies are often
characterized by their distinct properties such as elliptical (spiral) morphology, red (blue) colors, and lower (higher) sSFR and older (younger) stellar ages
[
50
]
.
We first investigate the morphology-dependence by dichotomizing the galaxies into ellipticals or spirals according to the fraction of rotational energy.
Using information on the comoving positions and velocities of stellar particles belonging to each galaxy, we compute the rotational kinetic energy,
K_{\rm rot}
,
as well as the total kinetic energy,
K_{\rm tot}
.
Taking the ratio,
\kappa\equiv K_{\rm rot}/K_{\rm tot}

[
72
,
73
]
, and using the morphology segregation threshold of
\kappa_{c}=0.45
given in
[
74
]
,
we separately determine
p(\cos\theta)
of the ellipticals (
\kappa<\kappa_{c}
) and of the spirals (
\kappa>\kappa_{c}
).

To eliminate any effects caused by different
m_{\star}
-distributions between the ellipticals and spirals, we control the two samples to have identical

m_{\star}
-distributions, and redetermine
p(\cos\theta)
from the
m_{\star}
-controlled samples of ellipticals and spirals. Here,
m_{\star}
distributions are computed
as
n(m_{\star})/N_{\rm tot}
where
n(m_{\star})
is the number of the void-surface galaxies with stellar masses in a differential interval
of
[m_{\star},m_{\star}+dm_{\star}]
.
The top panels of figure
4
plot the
m_{\star}
-distributions of the ellipticals (red histograms) and spirals (blue histograms),
confirming that the two controlled samples indeed have no difference in the
m_{\star}
-distributions (top-right panel) from each other, even though considerable differences
in the
m_{\star}
-distributions are existent in the original samples (top-left panel). The numbers of the dichotomized void-surfaces galaxies in the original and

m_{\star}
-controlled samples are listed in the second and third columns of table
1
.

The top-panel of figure
5

from the
m_{\star}
-controlled samples of the ellipticals and spirals (filled red and blue circles, respectively) and
the analytic model with the best-fit parameters (solid lines). As can be seen, the spiral galaxies yield almost completely uniform
p(\cos\theta)
, while the elliptical galaxies
exhibit quite strong signals of perpendicular shape alignments with the directions toward the void centers, which indicates that the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments are
indeed dependent on the galaxy morphology. Note also that the elliptical galaxies exhibit a good match between the numerically obtained
p(\cos\theta)
and the analytic model
with the best-fit parameter (see the fourth column of table

We also perform the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test of the null hypothesis that there is no void-galaxy perpendicular alignments to compute the

-controlled samples.

4
Summary and discussion

Determining the probability density distributions of the cosines of the angles,
p(\cos\theta)
, between the shape axes of the giant void-surfaces galaxies and the directions toward the void
centers by analyzing the TNG 300-1 simulation data, we have detected significant signals of perpendicular alignments between them. It has been discovered that the strengths of these
signals depend not only on the stellar masses and distances from the void centers but also strongly on the galaxy morphologies, colors, and sSFR and mildly on stellar ages.
The following summarizes the main results of the current work.

•

Significant signals of the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments are found only from those giant galaxies with stellar masses
m_{\star}\equiv\log[M_{\star}/(h^{-1}\,M_{\odot})]\geq 10.5

located on the void surfaces in the radial distance ranges of
0.9\leq r/R_{\rm v}\leq 1.1
with radii of empty spheres
R_{v}
that consist of a hosting void
(see figures
2

3
).

•

Between the two
m_{\star}
-controlled samples of ellipticals (
\kappa<0.45
) and spirals (
\kappa\geq 0.45
) on void surfaces, only the former
yields strong signals of void-galaxy perpendicular alignments, while no signal is found from the latter. The mean alignment strength seems to almost monotonically increase
as the fraction of the rotational energy,
\kappa
, decreases (see figures
5
and
6
).

•

Between the two
m_{\star}
-controlled samples of redder (
g-r>0.68
) and bluer (
g-r\leq 0.68
) void-surface galaxies, only the former yields significant
signals of void-galaxy perpendicular alignments, while no signal is found from the latter. As the galaxies become redder and redder, the void-galaxy perpendicular
alignments become stronger and stronger (see figures
5
and
6
).

•

Between the two
m_{\star}
-controlled samples of the void-surface galaxies with lower and higher sSFR, a significantly stronger signal of
void-galaxy perpendicular alignments is detected from the former. The void-galaxy perpendicular alignments show a consistent trend of becoming stronger
as the sSFR of void-surface galaxies diminishes (see figures
5
and
6
).

•

The void-galaxy perpendicular alignments exhibit only weak dependence on the stellar age,
t_{f\star}
, of the void-surface galaxies.
No threshold of
t_{f\star}
is able to create a sample that yield negligibly low signal of the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments.
Significant signals of the void-galaxy perpendicular alignments are detected from both of the older and younger void-surfaces galaxies than
the median stellar age, although the signal from the former is stronger than that from the latter (see figures
5
and
6
).
This weak age-dependence indicates that the void-surface galaxies with strong perpendicular alignments are not necessarily old ones but the ones whose
stars are being quenched by the void expansions.

•

A feasibility test is performed to reveal that for the elliptical galaxies the perpendicular alignments measured in the 2D projected space is still quite significant, rejecting the null
hypothesis of no signal at the confidence level higher than
99\%
. It prospects well to detect a observational signal of the void-surface perpendicular alignment and its morphology
dependence as well (see figure
7
).

We have also compared the numerically obtained
p(\cos\theta)
with a one-parameter model, eq. (
2.3
), derived by ref.
[
2
]
under the assumption
that the gravitational collapse along the major principal axis of the largest-scale tidal field, elongate the shapes of giant galaxies along the directions
perpendicular to it.
Given that the cosmic voids also expand most rapidly along the major principal axis of the local tidal field, this model naturally predicts that the galaxy shapes on void surfaces must
exhibit perpendicular alignments with the directions toward the void centers. Demonstrating good accords of the numerical results
with the analytic model, we have shown that the best-fit parameter of the analytic model is quite useful in quantifying the strengths of void-galaxy perpendicular
alignments and their variations with galaxy properties (see table
1
).

Our numerical results and their good agreements with the analytic model are interpreted as an indirect but compelling evidence for the stalling and quenching impact of void
formation on the galaxies. The formation of a void and its rapid expansion along the major principal axis of the largest-scale tidal field tightly compresses the neighboring matter,
leading the adjacent galaxies to develop strong perpendicular shape alignments with the directions to its center. This matter compression caused by
the void expansion also generates particle flows orthogonal to its surface, preventing the gas particles from radial falling and accreting onto the void-surface galaxies, which could
make effective and considerable contributions to stalling and quenching them.
Henceforth, if a galaxy adjacent to a void is exposed to the effect of the rapid void expansion for a longer period of time, then its shape would develop a stronger perpendicular
alignment with the direction to the void center. At the same time, it would become more quickly redder and quiescent, and its rotational motion would convert more rapidly into
random counterparts. Whereas, the galaxies that have recently migrated to the surface of a given void long after its formation would have exhibit no significant signal of
void-galaxy perpendicular alignments.

A crucial implication of our result is that the abundance of elliptical void-surface galaxies with strong void-galaxy perpendicular alignments should depend on the dynamical nature of
dark energy. According to the latest results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)
[
75
]
,
the cosmic acceleration at the present epoch is likely to be caused not by the completely inert cosmological constant
[
76
]
, but by a dynamical dark anergy whose equation of state
evolves with time
[
77
,
78
,
79
]
. Since the evolution of dark energy equation of state will change the formation and expansion rates of giant cosmic voids.
it can be envisioned that in some dynamical DE models where the cosmic voids form early and expand faster in the past, there would be a higher number fraction of the
elliptical galaxies on void surfaces that exhibit stronger void-galaxy perpendicular alignments. This mechanism, i.e., the earlier formation of cosmic voids due to dynamical
DE and its strong effects of quenching and stalling the high-
z
galaxies, might be able to resolve another long-standing puzzle that challenges the standard galaxy formation paradigm,
the presence of massive high-
z
quenched galaxies, even in gas-rich environments
[
80
,
81
]
.

It is, however, worth discussing a couple of caveats to our results. First, in the current analysis we have employed the classical Void-Finder algorithm
[
1
]
which
makes it possible to readily identify voids from the spatial distributions of galaxies without requiring any information on DM distributions.
Yet, we admit that the strengths of void-galaxy perpendicular alignments could depend on which algorithm is used to identify voids.
It will be desirable to test the robustness of the current results against the variation of void-finding algorithms.
The second caveat is the low-feedback model adopted in the TNG300-1 simulations. According to the latest observation of the profiles and amounts of baryonic gas
particles in the galaxy groups and clusters
[
82
]
, however, the real universe seems to favor the high-feedback model in which the observable shapes of
gravitationally bound structures should be more vulnerable to the baryonic feedback effects than expected in the TNG300-1, showing significant deviations from those of the
DM counterparts
[
82
]
. It will also be of high importance to test the robustness of our results against the variation of baryon physics.
Our future work will be in the directions of conducting these robustness tests as well as of probing the nature of dark energy with the abundance of elliptical void-surface galaxies.

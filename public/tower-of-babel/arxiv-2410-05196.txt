Algebraicity of Artin–Hasse–Weil L-series over global function fields
David Kurniadi Angdinata

Abstract
We prove an analogue of Deligne’s period conjecture for the special value of the L-function of an abelian variety over a global function field twisted by an Artin representation. We illustrate this in action for an example of an elliptic curve twisted by a Dirichlet character.



Deligne’s period conjecture is an abstract statement on the special value of the L-function associated to a pure motive with a critical Hodge structure
[
Del79
, Definition 1.3]
. Specifically, he conjectures that the L-value is equal to the determinant of a certain period map between its Betti and de Rham realisations, up to non-zero multiples in a number field
[
Del79
, Conjecture 2.8]
. This is known for Artin L-functions over
\mathbb{Q}

[
Del79
, Proposition 6.7]
, and has ramifications for the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for abelian varieties over
\mathbb{Q}

[
Del79
, Section 4]
, with numerical evidence for L-functions associated to Jacobians of smooth projective curves over
\mathbb{Q}

[
ECW24
, Conjecture 1.1]
.

In the context of the L-function
L\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,s)}
of an abelian variety
A
over a number field
K
twisted by an Artin representation
\tau
over
K
, which appear in equivariant refinements of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture
[
BC24
, Conjecture 3.3]
, Deligne’s period conjecture translates to a statement on the algebraicity and Galois equivariance of
L\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,1)}
normalised by periods
[
Eva21
, Proposition 4.3.8]
. This remains largely open in general, but the case of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
twisted by Artin representations that factor through a false Tate curve extension, such as the trivial representation and primitive Dirichlet characters, is a consequence of the modularity theorem
[
BD07
, Theorem 4.2]
.

When
A
is an abelian variety over a global function field
K
, the works of Grothendieck
[
Gro95
, Theorem 5.1]
and Deligne
[
Del73
, Theorem 9.3]
show that
L\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,s)}
is already a rational function satisfying a globally compatible functional equation, so that the aforementioned normalisations by periods are unnecessary. This paper presents a short proof of the analogue of Deligne’s period conjecture in this context, which is stated in Theorem
2
. To this end, some notational conventions for the formalism of
\ell
-adic representations over local fields and global function fields will first be established. Throughout,
\ell
will be a fixed prime of
\mathbb{Q}
, and
V_{\ell}
will be a finite-dimensional vector space over a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, whose choice will not be essential.

Notation 1
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with residue characteristic
p
. Let
I_{F}
denote the inertia subgroup of its Weil group
W_{F}
, and let
\mathrm{Fr}_{F}
denote the inverse of any choice of Frobenius element in
W_{F}
. For
\ell\neq p
, an
\ell
-adic representation over
F
is a continuous homomorphism
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}W_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{(V_{\ell})}
. Its
Euler factor
is the inverse characteristic polynomial

\mathcal{L}_{F}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}\coloneqq\det\!\mathinner{(1-T\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{F}\mid\rho^{I_{F}})},

where
\rho^{I_{F}}
is the subrepresentation of
\rho
invariant under
I_{F}
.

Now let
K
be the global function field of a smooth proper geometrically irreducible curve
C
of genus
g_{C}
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with absolute Galois group
G_{K}
. For each place
v
of
K
, let
K_{v}
denote its completion, and let
\deg v
denote its residue class degree. For
\ell\nmid q
, an
\ell
-adic representation over
K
is a continuous homomorphism
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{(V_{\ell})}
. Its
formal L-series
is the infinite product

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}\coloneqq\prod_{v}\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{K_{v}}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T^{\deg v})}},

which is a priori only a formal product. Its
L-series

L\!\mathinner{(\rho,s)}
is simply
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,q^{-s})}
, and let
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(\rho,s)}
denote the
n
-th derivative of
L\!\mathinner{(\rho,s)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathfrak{f}_{\rho}
denote the Artin conductor of
\rho
, and let
\deg\mathfrak{f}_{\rho}
denote its degree as a Weil divisor on
C
. Finally, let
G_{K}^{g}
denote the geometric Galois group, namely the kernel of the natural restriction from
G_{K}
to the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
\rho^{G_{K}^{g}}
denote the subrepresentation of
\rho
invariant under
G_{K}^{g}
.

The key example of an
\ell
-adic representation over
K
will be the first
\ell
-adic cohomology group
\rho_{A}\coloneqq H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}\!\mathinner{(A,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})}
of an abelian variety
A
over
K
, which is independent of
\ell

[
GR72
, Theorem 4.3]
, so
\ell
is suppressed from notation. Another example is an
Artin representation
, namely a continuous homomorphism
\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}\!\mathinner{(V)}
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional vector space over a number field equipped with the discrete topology, viewed as an
\ell
-adic representation over
K
by some embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
. The relevant notions over
F
are defined analogously. Finally, let
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,s)}
denote
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(\rho_{A}\otimes\tau,s)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For an Artin representation
\tau
, let
\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
denote the number field generated by the values of
\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
, and let
\tau^{\sigma}
denote the representation with character
\sigma\circ\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
\!\mathinner{(v_{i})}_{i}
is a basis of
\tau
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
\!\mathinner{(a_{ij})}
is the matrix of
g\in G_{K}
with respect to this basis, then
\!\mathinner{(v_{i}^{\sigma})}_{i}
is a basis of
\tau^{\sigma}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and the matrix of
g
with respect to this basis is
\!\mathinner{(a_{ij}^{\sigma})}

[
Eva21
, Section 2.1.4]
.

The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global function field
K
, and let
\tau
be an Artin representation over
K
. Then
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,1)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
and
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,1)}^{\sigma}=L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau^{\sigma},1)}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

It turns out that the same argument applies to Artin L-series.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\tau
be an Artin representation over
K
. Then
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(\tau,1)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
and
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(\tau,1)}^{\sigma}=L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(\tau^{\sigma},1)}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In what follows, a stronger result on the algebraicity of formal L-series will be proven, which clearly implies the same for all derivatives of L-series, by replacing
T
with
q^{-s}
. To this end, for any field
\mathfrak{F}
with automorphism group
G
, define an action of
G
on the ring of formal power series
\mathfrak{F}[[T]]
by

\mathinner{\Biggl(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T^{n}\Biggr)}^{g}\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{g}T^{n},\qquad g\in G.

Evaluating such a power series at an element
f\in\mathfrak{F}
does not give
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}^{g}f^{n}
in general, due to potential convergence issues, but the following shows that it does whenever the power series happens to be a rational function.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a field, and let
P\!\mathinner{(T)}\in\mathfrak{F}[[T]]
be a power series such that
P\!\mathinner{(T)}=R\!\mathinner{(T)}/Q\!\mathinner{(T)}
for some power series
Q\!\mathinner{(T)}\in\mathfrak{F}[[T]]
and some polynomial
R\!\mathinner{(T)}\in\mathfrak{F}\!\mathinner{[T]}
. Then
P\!\mathinner{(T)}^{\sigma}=R\!\mathinner{(T)}^{\sigma}/Q\!\mathinner{(T)}^{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Since
R\!\mathinner{(T)}
is a polynomial, it suffices to show that
\!\mathinner{(P\!\mathinner{(T)}Q\!\mathinner{(T)})}^{\sigma}=P\!\mathinner{(T)}^{\sigma}Q\!\mathinner{(T)}^{\sigma}
. Let
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
denote the coefficients of the power series
P\!\mathinner{(T)}
and
Q\!\mathinner{(T)}
respectively for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so that the equality becomes

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\Biggl(\sum_{i+j=n}P_{i}Q_{j}\Biggr)}^{\sigma}T^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}^{\sigma}T^{n}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{n}^{\sigma}T^{n}.

This is clear since
\sum_{i+j=n}P_{i}Q_{j}
is a finite sum.
∎

This is a property inherited by the formal L-series of a general
\ell
-adic representation over a global function field. Furthermore, if it has no geometric invariants, its formal L-series is in fact a polynomial.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\rho
be an
\ell
-adic representation over a global function field
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}\!\mathinner{(C)}
that is unramified almost everywhere. Then
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\!\mathinner{(T)}
. Furthermore, if
\rho^{G_{K}^{g}}=0
, then
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\!\mathinner{[T]}
of degree

\deg\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}=\!\mathinner{(2g_{C}-2)}\dim\rho+\deg\mathfrak{f}_{\rho}.

Proof.

This follows the sketch of an argument in Ulmer’s notes
[
Ulm11
, Lecture 4, Theorem 1.4.1]
, but it is repeated here with references to Milne’s book. There is an equivalence of categories between continuous
\ell
-adic representations over
K
that are unramified on an open set
U
of
C
and
\ell
-adic sheaves that are lisse on
U

[
Mil80
, Chapter V, Section 1]
. Let
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}U\hookrightarrow C
be any open set at which
\rho
is unramified, and let
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}
be its associated
\ell
-adic sheaf that is lisse on
U
, whose direct image along
\iota
induces étale cohomology groups
H^{i}\coloneqq H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{i}\!\mathinner{(\overline{C},\iota_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\rho})}
of the base change
\overline{C}
of
C
to
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Now the Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula for
\ell
-adic sheaves
[
Mil80
, Chapter VI, Theorem 13.4]
says that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{v\in C\!\mathinner{(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})}}\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\mathrm{Fr}_{K_{v}}^{n}\mid\rho^{I_{K_{v}}})}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}\!\mathinner{(-1)}^{i}\cdot\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\mathrm{Fr}_{q}^{n}\mid H^{i})},

where
\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
is the Frobenius in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Dividing both sides by
n
and exponentiating their generating functions, this equality rearranges to

\prod_{v}\exp\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\mathrm{Fr}_{K_{v}}^{m}\mid\rho^{I_{K_{v}}})}\dfrac{T^{m\deg v}}{m}=\prod_{i=0}^{2}\exp\mathinner{\Biggl(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\mathrm{Fr}_{q}^{n}\mid H^{i})}\dfrac{T^{n}}{n}\Biggr)}^{\!\mathinner{(-1)}^{i}}.

An identity in linear algebra
[
Mil80
, Chapter V, Lemma 2.7]
shows that

\exp\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\mathrm{Fr}_{K_{v}}^{m}\mid\rho^{I_{K_{v}}})}\dfrac{T^{m\deg v}}{m}=\dfrac{1}{\det\!\mathinner{(1-T^{\deg v}\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{K_{v}}\mid\rho^{I_{K_{v}}})}},

for each place
v
of
K
, and that

\exp\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\!\mathinner{(\mathrm{Fr}_{q}^{n}\mid H^{i})}\dfrac{T^{n}}{n}=\dfrac{1}{\det\!\mathinner{(1-T\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{q}\mid H^{i})}}.

for
i=0,1,2
. Thus the left hand side becomes
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
, while the right hand side expresses it as an alternating product of polynomials
\det\!\mathinner{(1-T\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{q}\mid H^{i})}
, which proves the first statement. For the second statement, note that

H^{0}=H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{0}\!\mathinner{(\overline{U},\mathcal{F}_{\rho})}\cong\rho^{G_{K}^{g}},

by definition, and Poincaré duality
[
Mil80
, Chapter V, Proposition 2.2(c)]
gives

H^{2}\cong H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{0}\!\mathinner{(\overline{C},\iota_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}^{\vee}\!\mathinner{(1)})}^{\vee}=H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{0}\!\mathinner{(\overline{U},\mathcal{F}_{\rho}^{\vee}\!\mathinner{(1)})}^{\vee}\cong\!\mathinner{(\rho^{\vee}\!\mathinner{(1)}^{G_{K}^{g}})}^{\vee}=0,

so that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\!\mathinner{[T]}
. Its degree is precisely given by the Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich formula
[
Mil80
, Chapter V, Theorem 2.12]
.
∎

In particular,
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
is well-defined at
T=q^{-1}
and respects the action of automorphisms of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
whenever its denominator does not vanish.

Remark 6
.

When
\rho
is the
\ell
-adic representation associated to an elliptic curve, Shioda gave an alternative description of
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
in terms of its associated elliptic surface
\mathcal{E}

[
Shi92
, Theorem 4]
. When
\mathcal{E}
has at least one singular fibre, he showed that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
is in fact a polynomial, given by

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}=\det\!\mathinner{(1-T\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{q}\mid W)},

where
W
is a subspace of the second
\ell
-adic cohomology group
H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2}\!\mathinner{(\mathcal{E},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\!\mathinner{(1)})}
of
\mathcal{E}
, given as the orthogonal complement of the trivial sublattice of the Neron–Severi group
\mathrm{NS}\!\mathinner{(\mathcal{E})}
of
\mathcal{E}
under the cycle class map
\mathrm{NS}\!\mathinner{(\mathcal{E})}\to H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2}\!\mathinner{(\mathcal{E},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\!\mathinner{(1)})}
. His description has the added benefit that the degree and functional equation of the polynomial
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
can be understood directly from the geometry of
\mathcal{E}
.

The analogue of algebraicity and Galois equivariance can first be proven at the level of Euler factors of local
\ell
-adic representations.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\rho
be an
\ell
-adic representation over a non-archimedean local field
F
, such that
\mathcal{L}_{F}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
\tau
be an Artin representation over
F
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{F}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}\!\mathinner{[T]}
and
\mathcal{L}_{F}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}^{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}_{F}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau^{\sigma},T)}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

This is similar to the argument by Bouganis–Dokchitser, but they only proved it for Artin twists of elliptic curves over number fields
[
BD07
, Lemma 4.4]
, so it is repeated here for reference. The first statement follows from the second statement since
\tau^{\sigma}=\tau
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}}
, so it suffices to prove the latter. Since
\mathcal{L}_{F}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
, it suffices to prove it for
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{(\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{K})}
, where
\mathfrak{L}
is the extension of
\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
that realises
\tau
and
\mathfrak{K}
is its subfield fixed by
\sigma
. There is an equivalence of categories between
\ell
-adic representations over
F
and complex Weil–Deligne representations of
W_{F}

[
Del73
, Section 8]
, so that
\ell
can be replaced with some prime
\ell^{\prime}
that splits in
\mathfrak{K}
and remains inert in
\mathfrak{L}
, which exists by Chebotarev’s density theorem. This gives an isomorphism
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{(\mathfrak{L}/\mathfrak{K})}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}}\!\mathinner{(\alpha)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})}
, where
\alpha
is the image in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}}}
of the primitive element of
\mathfrak{K}
that generates
\mathfrak{L}
. Now let
\!\mathinner{(v_{i})}_{i}
be a basis of
\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau)}^{I_{F}}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, and let
\!\mathinner{(a_{ij})}
be the matrix of
\mathrm{Fr}_{F}
with respect to this basis. Then
\!\mathinner{(v_{i}^{\phi\!\mathinner{(\sigma)}})}_{i}
is a basis of
\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau^{\sigma})}^{I_{F}}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, and the matrix of
\mathrm{Fr}_{F}
with respect to this basis is
\!\mathinner{(a_{ij}^{\phi\!\mathinner{(\sigma)}})}
, so that its inverse characteristic polynomial is precisely that of
\!\mathinner{(a_{ij})}^{\sigma}
.
∎

The corresponding statement for formal L-series follows from the local statements, by rewriting the infinite product of local Euler factors into a power series with coefficients indexed by effective Weil divisors, and applying rationality.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\rho
be an
\ell
-adic representation over a global function field
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}\!\mathinner{(C)}
that is unramified almost everywhere, such that
\mathcal{L}_{K_{v}}\!\mathinner{(\rho,T)}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
for each place
v
of
K
, and let
\tau
be an Artin representation over
K
. Then
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}\!\mathinner{(T)}
and
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}^{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau^{\sigma},T)}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Furthermore, if
\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau)}^{G_{K}^{g}}=0
, then
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}\!\mathinner{[T]}
of degree

\deg\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}=\!\mathinner{(2g_{C}-2)}\dim\rho\dim\tau+\deg\mathfrak{f}_{\rho\otimes\tau}.

Proof.

For each place
v
of
K
, let
a_{v,n}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
denote the coefficients of the power series
\mathcal{L}_{K_{v}}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}^{-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
4
for
P\!\mathinner{(T)}=\mathcal{L}_{K_{v}}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}^{-1}
, Proposition
7
translates into
a_{v,n}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
and
a_{v,n}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}^{\sigma}=a_{v,n}\!\mathinner{(\tau^{\sigma})}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Now for an effective Weil divisor
D=\sum_{v}n_{v}\!\mathinner{[v]}
on
C
, let
a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
denote the finitely-supported product
\prod_{v}a_{v,n_{v}}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
, so that
a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
and
a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}^{\sigma}=a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau^{\sigma})}
for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. A rearrangement gives

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}=\prod_{v}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{v,n}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}T^{n\deg v}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{D}a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}T^{m},

where the sum ranges over effective Weil divisors
D
on
C
of degree precisely
m
. This is a finite sum, so that
\sum_{D}a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}
and
\!\mathinner{(\sum_{D}a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau)})}^{\sigma}=\sum_{D}a_{D}\!\mathinner{(\tau^{\sigma})}
, which proves the second statement and that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}\in\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\tau)}[[T]]
. The first and final statements follow from Proposition
5
that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\!\mathinner{(T)}
, using the theory of Hankel determinants
[
Bou03
, Chapter IV.4, Exercise 1]
.
∎

In particular, these apply to
\rho=\rho_{A}
, which proves Theorem
2
, but also to the trivial representation
\rho=\mathbbm{1}
, which proves Theorem
3
.

Remark 9
.

Using Proposition
5
, Burns–Kakde–Kim proves the algebraicity and Galois equivariance of
L^{\!\mathinner{(n)}}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,s)}
up to finitely many local Euler factors away from an open set
U
of
C

[
BKK18
, Proposition 2.2]
, by directly arguing that the action of
\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
is preserved under an isomorphism

H_{\text{\'{e}t},c}^{i}\!\mathinner{(\overline{U},\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{A}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\tau}^{\sigma})}\cong H_{\text{\'{e}t},c}^{i}\!\mathinner{(\overline{U},\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{A}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\tau})}^{\sigma},

for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where both sides are compactly-supported étale cohomology groups of the base change
\overline{U}
of
U
to
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. The remaining finitely many local Euler factors can be handled separately by Proposition
7
, which gives an alternative proof for Theorem
2
independent from Theorem
8
.

Remark 10
.

There are explicit bounds for
\deg\mathfrak{f}_{\rho\otimes\tau}
in terms of
\deg\mathfrak{f}_{\rho}
and
\deg\mathfrak{f}_{\tau}
, such as in the arguments of Bisatt–Paterson
[
BP23
, Section 2]
, so the computation of
\deg\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}

[
CLDLL22

.

The expression for
\deg\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(\rho\otimes\tau,T)}
in Theorem
8
is useful for computing formal L-series of Dirichlet twists of elliptic curves, which was done explicitly by Comeau-Lapointe–David–Lalin–Li using the functional equation
[
CLDLL22
, Section 5.1]
. The reader is referred to Ulmer’s notes
[
Ulm11
, Lecture 1]
and Rosen’s book
[
Ros02
, Chapter 4 and Chapter 9]
for the general theory over global function fields of elliptic curves and Dirichlet characters respectively.

Example 11
.

Let
K=\mathbb{F}_{11}\!\mathinner{(t)}
, and let
A
be the elliptic curve over
K
given by
Y^{2}=X^{3}+\!\mathinner{(t+1)}^{3}\!\mathinner{(t+2)}^{3}
. Consider the Dirichlet character
\tau
over
K
of modulus
t
given by
2\mapsto\zeta_{5}
and the automorphism
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}\!\mathinner{(\mathbb{Q}\!\mathinner{(\zeta_{5})}/\mathbb{Q})}
given by
\zeta_{5}\mapsto\zeta_{5}^{2}
. To verify Theorem
2
that
\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,T)}^{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau^{\sigma},T)}
, first compute

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau,T)}=1+11\!\mathinner{(5+12\zeta_{5}+5\zeta_{5}^{2})}T^{2}+14641\zeta_{5}^{2}T^{4}.

Since
\tau^{\sigma}=\sigma\circ\tau
is given by
2\mapsto\zeta_{5}^{2}
, separately compute

\mathcal{L}\!\mathinner{(A,\tau^{\sigma},T)}=1+11\!\mathinner{(5+12\zeta_{5}^{2}+5\zeta_{5}^{4})}T^{2}+14641\zeta_{5}^{4}T^{4}.

These were computed in Magma, but the same algorithm works in any software with support for irreducible polynomials over finite fields. Note that

Remark 12
.

The existence of a globally compatible functional equation can drastically cut down the number of computations of local Euler factors necessary to completely determine the formal L-series. This in turn involves computing Langlands–Deligne local constants, which will not be explored in this paper.

Classical theorems from analysis for locally band preserving functions on Dedekind complete Φ \Phi -algebras
Eder Kikianty, Luan Naude, Mark Roelands, Christopher Schwanke

Abstract
In this paper we explore the concept of locally band preserving functions, introduced by Ercan and Wickstead, on Dedekind complete Φ \Phi -algebras. Specifically, we show that all super order differentiable functions are locally band preserving. Furthermore, some foundational results from classical analysis are proved in this setting, such as the Intermediate Value Theorem, the Extreme Value Theorem, and the Mean Value Theorem. Moreover, we show that these generalisations can fail for functions that are not locally band preserving. With the goal in mind to further develop the theory of complex differentiation in Dedekind complete complex Φ \Phi -algebras, a complex version of the Mean Value Theorem is also provided.



Keywords:
differentiation, complex vector lattice, locally band preserving, Mean Value Theorem, Extreme Value Theorem, Intermediate Value Theorem

Subject Classification:
Primary: 46A40; Secondary: 30A99

1
Introduction

The theory of complex analysis on Banach spaces enjoys a long, well-known,
and fruitful history, which is summarised in the text
[
Muj86
]
.
This theory generalises the
classical theory of functions of a complex variable by using norms as an abstraction of the
ordinary modulus on the complex plane.

Recently, the papers
[
RS19
,
RS25
]
took an alternative approach to complex analysis in abstract spaces: an
order-theoretic
approach. Indeed,
[
RS19
]
began this journey by proving an
n
th root test for the absolute order convergence of series in universally complete complex
\Phi
-algebras (unital
f
-algebras). From this result, Cauchy-Hadamard formulas for calculating the radius of convergence for power series in this setting were obtained.

This order-theoretic complex analysis was furthered in
[
RS25
]
, where the authors introduced two notions of differentiability built from the notion of order convergence in Dedekind complete
\Phi
-algebras:
order differentiability
and the much stronger
super order differentiability
.

Such results enable one to meaningfully investigate analytical properties of certain spaces which do not have a natural norm, such as
C^{\infty}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
. They furthermore illustrate how fundamental concepts in vector lattice theory, such as weak order units and band projections, have purposeful roles to play in complex analysis. Thirdly, this theory, through analogue, sheds light on an order-theoretical
real
analysis counterpart. It is to this real analysis complement that we devote much of our attention in this paper.

In Sections 2 and 3 we introduce
\varepsilon
-
\delta
reformulations of order continuity, order differentiability, and super order differentiability, with the goal in mind to have the results in this paper resemble the classical theory.

Using these reformulations, the objective of Sections 4 and 5 is to prove analogues of a Boundedness Theorem, an Intermediate Value Theorem, an Extreme Value Theorem, and a Mean Value Theorem in Dedekind complete
real

\Phi
-algebras. It turns out, however, that order continuity on order intervals is insufficient to prove these analogues. Therefore, in Section 4 we consider
locally band preserving functions
, which were introduced by Ercan and Wickstead in
[
EW98
]
. The reader can also infer the similarities between locally band preserving functions and functions with the so-called local property, introduced by Avilés and Zapata in
[
AZ18
]
, where
\Phi
-algebras of measurable functions are considered. These locally band preserving functions prove to be a natural class of functions to consider, since it will be shown that all super order differentiable functions are locally band preserving. Furthermore, it is also demonstrated that the Boundedness Theorem, the Intermediate Value Theorem, and the Extreme Value Theorem can fail for functions that are not locally band preserving.

In Section 5 we prove the analogues of the aforementioned classical theorems for order continuous and locally band preserving functions defined on a closed order interval. Using our Extreme Value Theorem, we then provide an analogue of Rolle’s Theorem and of the Mean Value Theorem. As a consequence, a super order differentiable function that has derivative identically zero on an order open interval is constant on said interval. Additionally, we illustrate through example that (i) this last result can fail for functions that are order differentiable but not super order differentiable, and (ii) that an order differentiable function need not be locally band preserving.

Finally, in order to further develop the theory of complex differentiation in Dedekind complete complex
\Phi
-algebras, a complex version of the Mean Value Theorem is proved. Consequently, any complex super order differentiable function with zero derivative on an order neighbourhood must be constant.

The reader is referred to the standard texts

A

(1)

is Dedekind complete (respectively, has the projection property, respectively has sufficiently many projections). In the case that

\Phi
-algebra, the multiplication on

\Phi

. Furthermore, we define

. For

\operatorname{Re}(x+iy):=x

\operatorname{Im}(x+iy):=y
, as usual. It immediately follows from (

,
RS25
]

\Phi

[

Often in this paper, we will consider a Dedekind complete

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
E
be a real (or complex) vector lattice with the projection property, and put
x,y\in E_{\rho}
. Then we get the following band decompositions of
E
:

E=B_{x<y}\oplus B_{y\leq x}=B_{x<y}\oplus B_{y<x}\oplus B_{x=y}.

Proof.

Since
B_{y\leq x}=B_{x<y}^{d}
, we know that
E=B_{x<y}\oplus B_{y\leq x}
. For the second decomposition, we show that
B_{x\leq y}=B_{x<y}\oplus B_{x=y}
. To this end, we know that
B_{x=y}\subseteq B_{y\leq x}=B_{x<y}^{d}
, so
B_{x<y}\cap B_{x=y}=\{0\}
. Since
(y-x)^{+}\perp(x-y)^{+}
,
B_{x<y}=B_{(y-x)^{+}}\subseteq B_{(x-y)^{+}}^{d}=B_{x\leq y}
. Putting the last two facts together gives us

\displaystyle B_{x<y}\oplus B_{x=y}

\displaystyle=B_{x<y}+(B_{x\leq y}\cap B_{y\leq x})

\displaystyle=(B_{x<y}+B_{x\leq y})\cap(B_{x<y}+B_{y\leq x})

\displaystyle=B_{x\leq y}\cap E

\displaystyle=B_{x\leq y}.\hfill\qed

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
E
be a real (or complex) vector lattice with the projection property, and assume that
x,y\in E_{\rho}
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(y-x)=\mathbb{P}_{x\leq y}(y-x)=(y-x)^{+}\text{ and }\mathbb{P}_{x=y}(y-x)=0.

Proof.

Since we have that
(y-x)^{+}\in B_{x<y}
and
(y-x)^{-}\in B_{y\leq x}
, it follows that
\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(y-x)=(y-x)^{+}
and
\mathbb{P}_{y\leq x}(y-x)=-(y-x)^{-}
. By interchanging
x
and
y
, we obtain
\mathbb{P}_{x\leq y}(y-x)=(x-y)^{-}=(y-x)^{+}
. Now,

\mathbb{P}_{x=y}(y-x)=\mathbb{P}_{y\leq x}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq y}(y-x)=\mathbb{P}_{y\leq x}(y-x)^{+}\geq 0.

Similarly,
\mathbb{P}_{x=y}(x-y)\geq 0
. Hence,
\mathbb{P}_{x=y}(x-y)=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
E
be a real (or complex) vector lattice with the projection property, and consider
x,y\in E_{\rho}
. Then

(i)

B_{x\leq y}
is the largest band for which the corresponding band projection
\mathbb{P}
satisfies
\mathbb{P}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}(y)
,

(ii)

B_{x=y}
is the largest band for which the corresponding band projection
\mathbb{P}
satisfies
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
, and

(iii)

B_{x<y}
is the largest band for which
\mathbb{P}(y-x)
is a weak order unit.

Proof.

It is immediate from the previous lemma that the above bands have the claimed properties. It remains to be shown that they are the largest such bands. To this end, let
B
be a band with band projection
\mathbb{P}
.

(i) Suppose
\mathbb{P}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}(y)
. Then
\mathbb{P}((x-y)^{+})=\mathbb{P}(x-y)-\mathbb{P}((x-y)^{-})\leq 0
, but we must have that
\mathbb{P}((x-y)^{+})\geq 0
. Hence,
\mathbb{P}((x-y)^{+})=0
. Since
(x-y)^{+}
is a weak order unit of
B_{y<x}
, this means
B\perp B_{y<x}
. We conclude that
B\subseteq B_{x\leq y}
.

(ii) Suppose
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
. By (i), we have that
B\subseteq B_{x\leq y}\cap B_{y\leq x}=B_{x=y}
.

(iii) Suppose
\mathbb{P}(y-x)
is a weak order unit of
B
. Then

0\leq\mathbb{P}(y-x)=\mathbb{P}(y-x)\vee 0=\mathbb{P}((y-x)\vee 0)=\mathbb{P}((y-x)^{+})\leq(y-x)^{+}.

Thus,
\mathbb{P}(y-x)\in B_{x<y}
and hence
B\subseteq B_{x<y}
.
∎

2.2
An
\varepsilon
-
\delta
reformulation of order continuity

The notation
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
refers to a function whose domain
\operatorname{dom}(f)
is a subset of
E
. We define what it means for such functions to be order continuous in terms of order convergence, and reformulate the definition to resemble the classical
\varepsilon
-
\delta
definition.

Notation 2.9
.

Given a real (or complex) vector lattice
E
, the notation
A\searrow 0
means
A\subseteq E_{\rho}
and
\inf A=0
. If additionally
A
is downwards directed, we write
A\downarrow 0
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
E
be a real (or complex) vector lattice. A net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
E
is said to
converge in order
to
x\in E
, written
x_{\alpha}\to x
, if there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there is an
\alpha_{0}
satisfying

\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}\implies\lvert x_{\alpha}-x\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

This convergence is the only one on
E
that will be considered in this paper.

Remark 2.11
.

In
Definition

2.10
, the set
\mathdutchcal{E}
can be replaced by the set of finite infima of its elements, making it downwards directed, but without changing the infimum.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
E
be a real (or complex) vector lattice. A function
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is called
order continuous at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
if whenever
x_{\alpha}\to c
in
\operatorname{dom}(f)
, we have
f(x_{\alpha})\to f(c)
. The function
f
is called
order continuous
if it is order continuous at every point in
\operatorname{dom}(f)
.

In order to make the similarity with classical theory in this paper explicit, the above definition is reformulated to resemble the classical
\varepsilon
-
\delta
definition.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
E
be a real (or complex) vector lattice. A function
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is order continuous at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
if and only if for every
\Delta\downarrow 0
there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
with the property that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying

x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\text{ and }\lvert x-c\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(x)-f(c)\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Suppose
f
is order continuous at
c
. Let
\Delta\downarrow 0
. The set

A\coloneqq\{(x,\delta)\colon x\in\operatorname{dom}(f),\delta\in\Delta,\text{ and }\lvert x-c\rvert\leq\delta\}

is directed by
(x_{1},\delta_{1})\leq(x_{2},\delta_{2})
if and only if
\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{1}
. Define, for any
\alpha=(x,\delta)
in
A
,
x_{\alpha}\coloneqq x
so that
x_{\alpha}\to c
in
\operatorname{dom}(f)
. By order continuity,
f(x_{\alpha})\to f(c)
, so there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
with the property that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists an
\alpha_{0}=(x_{0},\delta_{0})
such that for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, we have
\lvert f(x_{\alpha})-f(c)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
. If
x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
with
\lvert x-c\rvert\leq\delta_{0}
, then
(x,\delta_{0})\geq(x_{0},\delta_{0})=\alpha_{0}
, and so we have
\lvert f(x)-f(c)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
.

Conversely, suppose
f
satisfies the condition in the proposition. Suppose that
x_{\alpha}\to c
in
\operatorname{dom}(f)
. Then there exists a
\Delta\downarrow 0
such that for all
\delta\in\Delta
there exists an
\alpha_{0}
for which
\lvert x_{\alpha}-c\rvert\leq\delta
for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. For this
\Delta
, there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
that satisfies our assumption. Let
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
with corresponding
\delta
and
\alpha_{0}
. Then, for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, we have
\lvert x_{\alpha}-c\rvert\leq\delta
so
\lvert f(x_{\alpha})-f(c)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
. Therefore,
f(x_{\alpha})\to f(c)
.
∎

In this characterisation, it is immaterial whether
\mathdutchcal{E}
is required to be directed or not (as explained in
Remark

2.11
). However, it is essential that we only consider downwards directed
\Delta
. The following example shows how our reformulation of order continuity fails for
\Delta\searrow 0
.

Example 2.14
.

Consider
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by
f(x,y):=(x+y,0)
. It is clear that
f
is order continuous at
(0,0)
. Consider
\Delta\coloneqq\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\searrow 0
. Since
f(0,0)=(0,0)
,
f(1,0)=(1,0)
, and
f(0,1)=(1,0)
, there can be no
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
that satisfies our characterisation of order continuity for this set
\Delta
, since
\mathdutchcal{E}
would have to be bounded below by
(1,0)
.

3
Order differentiability

We define what it means for a function to be differentiable on subsets of
E
, as was first done in
[
RS25
]
, for complex
\Phi
-algebras. Note that this definition can also be used for real
\Phi
-algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and suppose that
x,y\in E_{\rho}
. We write
x\ll y
(or
y\gg x
) to mean
y-x
is a weak order unit of
E
. In particular,
r\gg 0
means that
r
is a weak order unit.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra. For
c\in E
and
r\gg 0
, define

N(c,r)\coloneqq\{z\in E\colon\lvert z-c\rvert\ll r\},\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{N}(c,r)\coloneqq\{y\in E\colon\lvert z-c\rvert\leq r\}.

If
E
is real and
a,b\in E
, we define

\displaystyle(a,b)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in E\colon a\ll x\ll b\}\quad\text{ and}\quad[a,b]\coloneqq\{x\in E\colon a\leq x\leq b\}.

In this case,
N(c,r)=(c-r,c+r)
and
\overline{N}(c,r)=[c-r,c+r]
.

There are two natural notions of differentiability introduced in
[
RS25
, Definitions 3.5 and 4.1]
. Using similar arguments as in the proof of
Proposition

2.13
, these can be given equivalent
\varepsilon
-
\delta
definitions.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra. A function
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is called
order differentiable
at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
with derivative
f^{\prime}(c)
if there exists an
r\gg 0
such that
N(c,r)\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(f)
, and, for every
\Delta\downarrow 0
, there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
with the property that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying

z\in N(c,r)\text{ and }\lvert z-c\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(z)-f(c)-(z-c)f^{\prime}(c)\rvert\leq\lvert z-c\rvert\varepsilon.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra. A function
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is called
super order differentiable
at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
with derivative
f^{\prime}(c)
if there exists an
r\gg 0
such that
N(c,r)\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(f)
, and, for every
\Delta\downarrow 0
, there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
with the property that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying

z\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\text{ and }\lvert z-c\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(z)-f(c)-(z-c)f^{\prime}(c)\rvert\leq\lvert z-c\rvert\varepsilon.

The above definitions differ in whether the implication is required to hold only for
z\in N(c,r)
or for all
z\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
. These definitions are not equivalent because, for a given
r\gg 0
, it is possible to have a net
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
\operatorname{dom}(f)
converging to
c
that never enters
N(c,r)
. Therefore, to determine whether a function is order differentiable at
c
, one can ignore how
f
acts on certain nets converging to
c
. However, all nets converging to
c
must be considered to determine super order differentiability.

Remark 3.5
.

In
[
RS25
, Notation 3.2]
,
x\ll y
is defined to mean that

is a positive invertible element. Every positive invertible element is a weak order unit
[
Zaa83
, Theorem 142.2 (ii)]
, and the converse holds when

is universally complete but not in general
[
RS19
, Remark 3.3]
. For example,
(\frac{1}{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a weak order unit in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
but is not invertible. Changing this definition is necessary for the techniques used in Section
5
, where it is needed that
\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(x)\ll\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(y)
, when seen as elements of
B_{x<y}
.

The results in
[
RS25
]
remain true when the positive invertible element
r
is replaced by a weak order unit, except for the argument showing that the derivative is unique in
[
RS25
, Remark 3.6]
. A modified proof is given in
Proposition

3.7
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a weak order unit
r
, we have that
\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(r)
must be invertible in
B_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}
by
[
dP81
, Theorem 1.11]
, as it is larger than the invertible element
\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(\frac{1}{m}e)
. The following lemma therefore allows us to still use invertibility arguments for weak order units.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and assume that
r
is a weak order unit of
E
. Then
B_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}\uparrow E
as
m\to\infty
and hence
\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(u)\uparrow u
as
m\to\infty
for all
u\in E^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
B_{0}
be a band such that
B_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}\subseteq B_{0}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
B_{0}^{d}\subseteq B_{\frac{1}{m}e>r}
and so
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{d}(r)\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}^{d}(\frac{1}{m}e)
. Therefore,
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{d}(r)=\{0\}
, so
B_{0}^{d}=\{0\}
and hence
B_{0}=E
. Now
Proposition

2.3
completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra. If
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is order differentiable at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
, there is a unique value of
f^{\prime}(c)
that satisfies
Definition

3.3
.

Proof.

Let
r
be as in
Definition

3.3
. For
\Delta\coloneqq\{\frac{1}{n}r\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, let
\mathdutchcal{E}
be as in
Definition

3.3
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\frac{1}{m}e
is invertible,
\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(r)
is invertible in
B_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}
. Let
s
denote its inverse.

For a given
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
with corresponding
\delta=\frac{1}{M}r
, we have that for all
n\geq M
,
z_{n}\coloneqq c+\frac{1}{n+1}r
satisfies
z_{n}\in N(c,r)
and
\lvert z_{n}-c\rvert\leq\delta
, so that

\left\lvert f(z_{n})-f(c)-{\textstyle\frac{1}{n+1}}rf^{\prime}(c)\right\rvert\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{n+1}}r\varepsilon,

and hence

\left\lvert(n+1)s\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(f(z_{n})-f(c))-\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(f^{\prime}(c))\right\rvert\leq\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(\varepsilon).

Therefore,
(n+1)s\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(f(z_{n})-f(c))\to\mathbb{P}_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}(f^{\prime}(c))
. Hence the derivative is unique in
B_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By
Lemma

3.6
, it is uniquely determined in
E
.
∎

We recall the sum and product rules for order differentiable and super order differentiable functions from
[
RS25
, Theorems 3.15 and 4.3]
, where the reader may also find chain and quotient rules.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and assume that
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
and
g\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
, and let
f
and
g
be (super) order differentiable at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\cap\operatorname{dom}(g)
. Then,

(i)

f+g
is (super) order differentiable at
c
with
(f+g)^{\prime}(c)=f^{\prime}(c)+g^{\prime}(c)
, and

(ii)

fg
is (super) order differentiable at
c
with
(fg)^{\prime}(c)=f^{\prime}(c)g(c)+f(c)g^{\prime}(c)
.

Example 3.9
.

For any
E
, it is routine to verify that
f(x):=x
and
g(x):=c
, for some
c\in E
, are both super order differentiable everywhere with
f^{\prime}(x)=e
and
g^{\prime}(x)=0
. It follows from
Proposition

3.8
that any polynomial on
E
is super order differentiable with the expected derivative.

The following proposition shows that order differentiability, as opposed to super order differentiability, is a local property.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and additionally suppose that
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is order differentiable at
c\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
. If
g\colon\operatorname{dom}(g)\to E
is defined on, and equal to,
f
on
N(c,r)
for some
r\gg 0
, then
g
is order differentiable at
c
with
g^{\prime}(c)=f^{\prime}(c)
.

Proof.

Since
f
is order differentiable at
c
, there exists an
r_{1}\gg 0
such that
N(c,r_{1})\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(f)
, and for every
\Delta\downarrow 0
, there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
with the property that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying

z\in N(c,r)\text{ and }\lvert z-c\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(z)-f(c)-(z-c)f^{\prime}(c)\rvert\leq\lvert z-c\rvert\varepsilon.

Define
r_{2}\coloneqq r\wedge r_{1}
, then
N(c,r_{2})\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(g)
. Let
\varepsilon,\delta
be as above. Then, for all
z\in N(c,r_{2})
with
\lvert z-c\rvert\leq\delta
,

\lvert g(z)-g(c)-(z-c)f^{\prime}(c)\rvert=\lvert f(z)-f(c)-(z-c)f^{\prime}(c)\rvert\leq\lvert z-c\rvert\varepsilon.

Therefore,
g
is order differentiable at
c
with
g^{\prime}(c)=f^{\prime}(c)
.
∎

4
Locally band preserving functions

This section is dedicated to the notion of locally band preserving functions. The main reason being that order continuous functions on order intervals, in general, do not inherit the properties real-valued functions on real intervals posses. We start with
Example

4.1
, where an example is given of an unbounded order continuous function on a closed order interval.
Example

4.2
shows that the Intermediate Value Theorem and the Extreme Value Theorem can fail for order continuous and order bounded functions defined on a closed order interval. These results are recovered for locally band preserving, order continuous functions in Section 5.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the Dedekind complete
\Phi
-algebra
L^{0}[0,1]
of Le-besgue measurable functions on
[0,1]
, with
e
being the constant one function. Then
[0,e]=L^{1}[0,1]\cap[0,e]
. Since
L^{1}[0,1]
is a Banach lattice with order continuous norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, for this norm the order interval
[0,e]
is a metric space that is not compact. It follows that there must exist a continuous function
f\colon[0,e]\to\mathbb{R}
that is unbounded. Indeed, for a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
[0,e]
that does not have a norm convergent subsequence, we can define the function
g\colon\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}\to\mathbb{R}
by
g(x_{n}):=n
. Since
\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a closed and discrete set in
[0,e]
, and
g
is continuous, the Tietze extension theorem implies that
g
can be extended continuously to all of
[0,e]
, where it is necessarily unbounded as well. Note that if
x_{\alpha}\to x
in order in
[0,e]
, then there is a set
\mathdutchcal{E}\downarrow 0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there is an
\alpha_{0}
such that
|x_{\alpha}-x|\leq\varepsilon
whenever
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. It follows from the fact that
|x_{\alpha}-x|\leq 2e
for all
\alpha
that we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathdutchcal{E}\subseteq L^{1}[0,1]
. Hence
\|x_{\alpha}-x\|_{1}\leq\|\varepsilon\|_{1}
, and since
\inf\{\|\varepsilon\|_{1}\colon\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}\}=0
as
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is order continuous, this net converges in norm. Thus
|f(x)-f(x_{\alpha})|\to 0
, and so
f
is order continuous. In conclusion, the function
h\colon[0,e]\to L^{0}[0,1]
defined by
h(x):=f(x)e
provides an example of an order continuous function which is unbounded.

Example 4.2
.

Let
f,g\colon[(0,0),(1,1)]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be given by
f(x,y):=(x,x^{2})
and
g(x,y):=(x,1-x)
. Then
f
and
g
are both order continuous. Since
f(0,0)=(0,0)
and
f(1,1)=(1,1)
but
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\not\in\operatorname{range}(f)
, the Intermediate Value Theorem fails for
f
. The Extreme Value Theorem fails for
g
, even though
g
is order bounded, since

\sup_{(x,y)\in[(0,0),(1,1)]}g(x,y)=(1,1),

but this supremum is not attained.

The shortcomings of Example
4.2
can be rectified by considering only locally band preserving functions. These functions were first introduced for Archimdedean real vector lattices in
[
EW98
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and consider
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
. We say that
f
is
locally band preserving
if for all
x,y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
and
z\in E
we have that

|x-y|\wedge|z|=0\Longrightarrow|f(x)-f(y)|\wedge|z|=0.

We next provide a characterisation of locally band preserving functions using band projections, which we will freely use throughout the remainder of this paper.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra. A function
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is locally band preserving if and only if for every band projection
\mathbb{P}
and for all
x,y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
, we have that

\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)\Longrightarrow\mathbb{P}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}(f(y)).

Proof.

Suppose
f
is locally band preserving, and let
B
be a band with corresponding band projection
\mathbb{P}
. If
x,y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
are such that
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
, we have if
z\in B
, then
|x-y|\wedge|z|=0
, so
|f(x)-f(y)|\wedge|z|=0
. Hence
|\mathbb{P}(f(x)-\mathbb{P}(f(y))|=\mathbb{P}(|f(x)-f(y)|)=0
, and, therefore,
\mathbb{P}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}(f(y))
.

Conversely, suppose that for all
x,y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
and for every band projection
\mathbb{P}
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
implies
\mathbb{P}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}(f(y))
. Let
z\in E
be such that
|x-y|\wedge|z|=0
. Then for the band
B_{z}
with corresponding band projection
\mathbb{P}_{z}
, it follows that
\mathbb{P}_{z}(x)=\mathbb{P}_{z}(y)
, so
\mathbb{P}_{z}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}_{z}(f(y))
. Hence
|f(x)-f(y)|\wedge|z|=0
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Note that a locally band preserving function
f\colon E\to E
is band preserving if
f(0)=0
. Indeed, for any band
B
in
E
and
x\in B
, we have that
\mathbb{P}^{d}(x)=\mathbb{P}^{d}(0)=0
, and thus
\mathbb{P}^{d}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}^{d}(f(0))
, and hence we have
f(x)\in B
. Furthermore, it is readily checked that a linear function
f\colon E\to E
is locally band preserving if and only if it is band preserving. Hence an order bounded linear function
f\colon E\to E
is locally band preserving if and only if it is an orthomorphism.

Example 4.6
.

In
\mathbb{R}
, all functions are locally band preserving, as the only band projections are the zero and identity mappings. A function in
\ell^{\infty}
or
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
is locally band preserving if and only if it is defined coordinatewise. Thus, neither of the functions in
Example

4.2
is locally band preserving.

However, if we view these
\Phi
-algebras as
\ell^{\infty}\cong C(\beta\mathbb{N})
and
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\cong C^{\infty}(\beta\mathbb{N})
, then locally band preserving functions need not be defined pointwise, even if they are order continuous.

Example 4.7
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the continuous function
k_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
k_{n}(t)=0
for
t\leq\frac{1}{2n}
,
k_{n}(t)=1
for
t\geq\frac{1}{n}
, and
k_{n}
is linear on
[\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{n}]
.

Define
f\colon\ell^{\infty}\to\ell^{\infty}
by
f((x(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}):=(k_{n}(x(n))))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. It is clear that
f
is locally band preserving since it is defined coordinatewise. To show that
f
is order continuous, let
x_{\alpha}\to x
in
\ell^{\infty}
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
x_{\alpha}(n)\to x(n)
, and hence
(f(x_{\alpha}))(n)=k_{n}(x_{\alpha}(n))\to k_{n}(x(n))=(f(x))(n)
, since
k_{n}
is continuous. Thus,
f(x_{\alpha})
converges to
f(x)
in each coordinate. Since
f(x_{\alpha})
is bounded between
0
and
e
, coordinatewise convergence implies order convergence, and so
f(x_{\alpha})\to f(x)
.

Identifying
\ell^{\infty}
with
C(\beta\mathbb{N})
, we show that even though
f
is locally band preserving, it does not act pointwise on
\beta\mathbb{N}
. To this end, let
x:=0
and
y
be the extension of
(\frac{1}{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
\beta\mathbb{N}
. For every
t\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
,
x(t)=0=y(t)
. However,
f(x)=0
and
f(y)=e
, so
f
does not act pointwise at any point
t\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
.

The following lemma is needed for the proof of
Theorem

4.9
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and assume that
f\colon\operatorname{dom}(f)\to E
is super order differentiable on its entire domain. Then, for all
\delta\in E^{+}
, every
x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
, and all band projections
\mathbb{P}
, there exists a
k\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying

y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\text{ and }\lvert y-x\rvert\leq k\delta\text{ and }\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)\implies\mathbb{P}(f(y))=\mathbb{P}(f(x)).

Proof.

Let
\delta\in E^{+}
,
x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
, and
\mathbb{P}
be any band projection. Define

\Delta\coloneqq\{k\delta\colon k\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}\}.

Then there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
, there is a
k_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
for which

y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\text{ and }\lvert y-x\rvert\leq k_{\varepsilon}\delta\implies\lvert f(y)-f(x)-(y-x)f^{\prime}(x)\rvert\leq\lvert y-x\rvert\varepsilon.

Fix any
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
. Then, for all
y\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
satisfying
\lvert y-x\rvert\leq k_{\varepsilon}\delta
and
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
, we apply
\mathbb{P}
to both sides of the above inequality to show that

\mathbb{P}\left(\lvert f(y)-f(x)\rvert\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\lvert f(y)-f(x)-(y-x)f^{\prime}(x)\rvert\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\lvert y-x\rvert\varepsilon\right)=0.

Therefore,
\mathbb{P}(f(y))=\mathbb{P}(f(x))
.
∎

It will now be shown that any super order differentiable function

f\colon N(c,r)\to E

is necessarily locally band preserving, which makes locally band preserving functions a natural class of functions to study in this context.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and consider
c,r\in E
with
r\gg 0
. If
f\colon N(c,r)\to E
is super order differentiable, then
f
is locally band preserving.

Proof.

Let
I\coloneqq\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon 0\leq t\leq 1\}
. Suppose
x,y\in N(c,r)
with
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
for some band projection
\mathbb{P}
. Define
x_{t}\coloneqq(1-t)x+ty
for all
t\in I
, and let
L[x,y]\coloneqq\{x_{t}\colon t\in I\}
. Then
L[x,y]\subseteq N(c,r)
, and for all
s,t\in I
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(x_{t})=(1-t)\mathbb{P}(x)+t\mathbb{P}(y)=\mathbb{P}(x),\ \text{ and}\ \ \ \lvert x_{s}-x_{t}\rvert=\lvert s-t\rvert\lvert x-y\rvert.

Define
g\colon I\to E
by
g(t):=\mathbb{P}(f(x_{t}))
. Let
\delta:=\lvert x-y\rvert
and
t\in I
. By
Lemma

4.8
, there exists a
k_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
such that

z\in N(c,r)\text{ and }\lvert z-x_{t}\rvert\leq k_{t}\delta\text{ and }\mathbb{P}(z)=\mathbb{P}(x_{t})\implies\mathbb{P}(f(z))=\mathbb{P}(f(x_{t})).

In particular, if
s\in I
and
\lvert s-t\rvert\leq k_{t}
, then
\lvert x_{s}-x_{t}\rvert=\lvert s-t\rvert\lvert x-y\rvert\leq k_{t}\delta
, so
g(s)=\mathbb{P}(f(x_{s}))=\mathbb{P}(f(x_{t}))=g(t)
. Thus,
g
is constant on

I_{t}\coloneqq\{s\in I\colon\lvert s-t\rvert\leq k_{t}\}

for all
t\in I
.

By compactness,
I\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}I_{t_{i}}
for some
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in I
. Then there exist points
s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\in I
such that
s_{1}=0
,
s_{m}=1
, and consecutive points lie in the same interval
I_{t_{i}}
for some
i
. This shows that

\mathbb{P}(f(x))=g(s_{1})=g(s_{2})=\dots=g(s_{m})=\mathbb{P}(f(y)).\hfill\qed

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real (or complex)
\Phi
-algebra, and consider
c,r\in E
with
r\gg 0
. If
f\colon\overline{N}(c,r)\to E
is order continuous on
\overline{N}(c,r)
and super order differentiable on
N(c,r)
, then
f
is locally band preserving.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\overline{N}(c,r)
with
\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{P}(y)
. Define
x_{n}\coloneqq c+(1-\frac{1}{n})(x-c)
and
y_{n}\coloneqq c+(1-\frac{1}{n})(y-c)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
x_{n}\to x
,
y_{n}\to y
, and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
x_{n},y_{n}\in N(c,r)
and
\mathbb{P}(x_{n})=\mathbb{P}(y_{n})
, so that
\mathbb{P}(f(x_{n}))=\mathbb{P}(f(y_{n}))
by
Theorem

4.9
. Since
f
is order continuous,

\mathbb{P}(f(x))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(f(x_{n}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(f(y_{n}))=\mathbb{P}(f(y)).\hfill\qed

We next record some elementary properties of locally band preserving functions defined on
[a,b]
that we require in Section 5. The recurring idea is that one can restrict properties of
f
(such as being order bounded or order continuous) to individual bands.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Suppose that
a,b,c,d\in E
satisfy
a\leq c\leq d\leq b
. Let
f\colon[a,b]\to E
be locally band preserving. The following statements hold.

(i)

If
f
is order bounded above on
[c,d]
by
M
, then for all
x\in[a,b]
,
\mathbb{P}_{c\leq x}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d}f(x)\leq\mathbb{P}_{c\leq x}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d}(M)
.

(ii)

If
f
is order bounded below on
[c,d]
by
m
, then for all
x\in[a,b]
,
\mathbb{P}_{c\leq x}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d}f(x)\geq\mathbb{P}_{c\leq x}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d}(m)
.

(iii)

Let
x\in[a,b]
and
\varepsilon,\delta\in E^{+}
. If for all
y\in[a,b]
,
\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\delta
implies
\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
; then, for any band projection
\mathbb{P}
,
\mathbb{P}(\lvert x-y\rvert)\leq\mathbb{P}(\delta)
implies
\mathbb{P}(\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert)\leq\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

To prove (i), let
x\in[a,b]
. Define the band projection
\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}_{c\leq x}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d}
, and let
y\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(x)+\mathbb{P}^{d}(c)
. Then
\mathbb{P}(c)\leq\mathbb{P}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}(d)
,
\mathbb{P}(y)=\mathbb{P}(x)
, and
\mathbb{P}^{d}(c)=\mathbb{P}^{d}(y)\leq\mathbb{P}^{d}(d)
. Together, these statements show that
y\in[c,d]
and hence
f(y)\leq M
. Since
\mathbb{P}(y)=\mathbb{P}(x)
, we have
\mathbb{P}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}(f(y))\leq\mathbb{P}(M)
. The argument for (ii) is similar.

We now prove (iii). Let
\varepsilon,\delta\in E^{+}
and
x\in[a,b]
be such that for all
y\in[a,b]
,
\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\delta
implies
\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
. Let
y\in[a,b]
and
\mathbb{P}
be any band projection such that
\mathbb{P}(\lvert x-y\rvert)\leq\mathbb{P}(\delta)
. Define
z\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(y)+\mathbb{P}^{d}(x)
, then
\lvert x-z\rvert\leq\delta
, so we know that
\lvert f(x)-f(z)\rvert\leq\varepsilon
. Since
\mathbb{P}(y)=\mathbb{P}(z)
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(f(y))=\mathbb{P}(f(z))
, and so
\mathbb{P}\left(\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\lvert f(x)-f(z)\rvert\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon)
.
∎

5
Classical theorems for continuous and differentiable functions

In this section, we generalise some classical results for continuous and differentiable real-valued functions to a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra
E
, but, in light of
Example

4.1
and Example
4.2
, adding the requirement that the functions are locally band preserving.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra and consider
a,b,c,d\in E
with
a\leq b
and
c\leq d
. Let
f\colon[a\wedge c,b\vee d]\to E
be locally band preserving, order bounded on
[a,b]
, and order bounded on
[c,d]
. If
[a,b]\cap[c,d]\neq\varnothing
, then
f
is order bounded on
[a\wedge c,b\vee d]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq M_{1}
for all
x\in[a,b]
and
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq M_{2}
for all
x\in[c,d]
. Let
z\in[a,b]\cap[c,d]
. For any
x\in[a\wedge c,b\vee d]
, we have that

B_{a>x}\cap B_{c>x}=B_{a\wedge c>x}=\{0\},

and hence
B_{a>x}\subseteq B_{c\leq x}
. Now,

\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a\leq x}(x)\in\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a\leq x}([a,b]),

and
\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a>x}(x)\in\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a>x}([c,d])
.
Thus, by
Proposition

4.11
(i) and (ii),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x<z}(\lvert f(x)\rvert)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a\leq x}(\lvert f(x)\rvert)+\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a>x}(\lvert f(x)\rvert)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a\leq x}(M_{1})+\mathbb{P}_{x<z}\mathbb{P}_{a>x}(M_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x<z}(M_{1}\vee M_{2}).

Similarly,
\mathbb{P}_{x\geq z}(\lvert f(x)\rvert)\leq\mathbb{P}_{x\geq z}(M_{1}\vee M_{2})
, and hence
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq M_{1}\vee M_{2}
.
∎

The fact that order boundedness extends on connected order intervals from the above lemma, in combination with the property of a function being locally band preserving, provides a boundedness theorem.

Theorem 5.2

(The Boundedness Theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
such that
a\leq b
. If
f\colon[a,b]\to E
is order continuous and locally band preserving, then
f
is order bounded.

Proof.

Let
S\coloneqq\{x\in[a,b]\colon f\text{ is order bounded on }[a,x]\}
. Then

c\coloneqq\sup(S)\in[a,b].

It is clear from
Lemma

5.1
that
S
is upwards directed.

Consider
\Delta\coloneqq\{(c-x)\vee\frac{1}{n}(b-c):x\in S,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\downarrow 0
. Since
f
is order continuous at
c
, there is some
\varepsilon\in E^{+}
and
\delta=(c-x)\vee\frac{1}{n}(b-c)
, for
x\in S
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

y\in[a,b]\text{ and }\lvert y-c\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(y)-f(c)\rvert\leq\varepsilon\implies\lvert f(y)\rvert\leq\lvert f(c)\rvert+\varepsilon.

This shows that
f
is order bounded on
[x,c]
and on
[c,c+\frac{1}{n}(b-c)]
, and we already know that
f
is order bounded on
[a,x]
. By
Lemma

5.1
,
c\in S
and
c+\frac{1}{n}(b-c)\in S
. Therefore,
c\leq c+\frac{1}{n}(b-c)\leq c
which implies that
c=b
.
∎

We next prove an Intermediate Value Theorem for locally band preserving functions.

Theorem 5.3

(The Intermediate Value Theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
with
a\leq b
. If
f\colon[a,b]\to E
is order continuous and locally band preserving, and
y\in[f(a)\wedge f(b),f(a)\vee f(b)]
, then there exists
c\in[a,b]
such that
f(c)=y
.

Proof.

We first assume that
y=0
and
f(a)\leq f(b)
. Define

S\coloneqq\{x\in[a,b]\colon f(x)\leq 0\}.

Then
S
is upwards directed since if
x,y\in S
and
z=x\vee y
, we have that
z=\mathbb{P}_{x\geq y}(x)+\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(y)
from which it follows that

f(z)=\mathbb{P}_{x\geq y}(f(z))+\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(f(z))=\mathbb{P}_{x\geq y}(f(x))+\mathbb{P}_{x<y}(f(y))\leq 0.

Thus,
S\uparrow c
for some
c\in[a,b]
and, by order continuity,
f(c)\leq 0
.

Consider
\Delta\coloneqq\{\frac{1}{n}(b-c):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. There exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying

x\in[a,b]\text{ and }\lvert x-c\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(x)-f(c)\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
, and consider
\delta
as above. Let
x:=\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(c+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}^{d}(c)
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}^{d}(x)=\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}^{d}(c)
and
f
is locally band preserving,

\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}^{d}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(f(c))\leq 0.

Also, since
\lvert x-c\rvert\leq\delta
,

\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(f(x)-f(c))\leq\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)\leq\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(-f(c)),

so that
\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(f(x))\leq 0
. Therefore,

f(x)=\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}^{d}(f(x))+\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(f(x))\leq 0,

so that
x\in S
. Hence we obtain

c+\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(\delta)=x\leq c.

Combining this inequality with the fact that
\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(\delta)\geq 0
, we get that
\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(\delta)=0
. Since

\mathbb{P}_{c=b}(-f(c))=\mathbb{P}_{c=b}(-f(b))\leq 0\leq\mathbb{P}_{c=b}(\varepsilon),

we have that
B_{c=b}\subseteq B_{-f(c)\leq\varepsilon}
by
Proposition

2.8
(i), hence
B_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}\subseteq B_{c<b}
. Thus, since
\delta=\frac{1}{n}(b-c)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{P}_{c<b}(b-c)
is a weak order unit of
B_{c<b}
, we have that
\mathbb{P}_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}(\delta)
is a weak order unit of
B_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}
. Hence
B_{-f(c)>\varepsilon}=\{0\}
, so that
-f(c)\leq\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary,
-f(c)\leq 0
. As we already knew that
f(c)\leq 0
, this means that
f(c)=0=y
.

The case where
f(b)\leq f(a)
is similar. Once these two cases are established, we can find
c,d\in[a,b]
for which we simultaneously have that
\mathbb{P}_{f(a)\leq f(b)}(f(c))=0
and
\mathbb{P}_{f(a)>f(b)}(f(d))=0
. Since
f
is locally band preserving, it then follows that

f(\mathbb{P}_{f(a)\leq f(b)}(c)+\mathbb{P}_{f(a)>f(b)}(d))=0.

Finally, if
y\neq 0
, apply the result to
x\mapsto f(x)-y
.
∎

The following lemma will be used in the proof of the Extreme Value Theorem below. It is a vector lattice analogue of the
\varepsilon
characterisation of the supremum and infimum in
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
E
is a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra with
E\neq\{0\}
. Let
r\gg 0
, and let
A\subseteq E
have infimum
\lambda
(resp. supremum
\mu
). Then there exist an
a\in A
such that
B_{a<\lambda+r}\neq\{0\}
(resp.
B_{a>\mu-r}\neq\{0\}
).

Proof.

If
B_{a<\lambda+r}=\{0\}
, then
\lambda+r\leq a
. Thus, if this inequality holds for all
a\in A
, we have that
\lambda+r
is a lower bound of
A
, and hence
r=0
, contradicting that
E\neq\{0\}
. Showing that
B_{a>\mu-r}\neq\{0\}
is similar.
∎

Theorem 5.5

(Extreme Value Theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
such that
a\leq b
. If
f\colon[a,b]\to E
is order continuous and locally band preserving, then there exist
c,d\in[a,b]
such that for all
x\in[a,b]
,
f(c)\leq f(x)\leq f(d)
.

Proof.

Assume
E\neq\{0\}
, noting that the result is trivial otherwise. We will first show the existence of
d
. By
Theorem

5.2
,
M(x)\coloneqq\sup_{y\in[a,x]}f(y)
exists for all
x\in[a,b]
.

Assume
f(a)\ll M(b)
. Since

\mathbb{P}_{a=b}(M(b)-f(a))=\mathbb{P}_{a=b}(M(a)-f(a))=\mathbb{P}_{a=b}(f(a)-f(a))=0,

B_{a=b}=\{0\}
, and hence
E=B_{a<b}
. By
Proposition

2.8
(iii),
a\ll b
. Let

S:=\{x\in[a,b]\colon M(b)-M(x)\text{ is a weak order unit}\}.

Then
d\coloneqq\sup S\in[a,b]
. Assume further that
f(d)\ll M(b)
.

Consider
\Delta\coloneqq\{\frac{1}{n}(b-a)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Since
f
is order continuous at
d
, there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
satisfying

x\in[a,b]\text{ and }\lvert x-d\rvert\leq\delta\implies\lvert f(x)-f(d)\rvert\leq\varepsilon.

By
Lemma

5.4
, there exists an
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
(with corresponding
\delta
) such that
B_{\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))}\neq\{0\}
. If we denote the corresponding band projection by
\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}
, then the Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra
B_{\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))}
contains
\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(S)
and
\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(\delta)
, which is a weak order unit of
B_{\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))}
since
\delta=\frac{1}{n}(b-a)
is a weak order unit of
E
. Thus, it follows from
Lemma

5.4
that there exists an
s\in S
satisfying

B_{\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))}\cap B_{s>d-\delta}=\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(B_{s>d-\delta})=B_{\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(s)>\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(d)-\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(\delta)}\neq\{0\}.

Now we will obtain a contradiction by showing that

(i)

B_{1}\coloneqq B_{\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))}\cap B_{s>d-\delta}\cap B_{d<b}=\{0\}
,
and

(ii)

B_{2}\coloneqq B_{\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))}\cap B_{s>d-\delta}\cap B_{d=b}=\{0\}
.

These statements combined yield a contradiction, since

B_{1}+B_{2}=B_{\varepsilon<M(b)-f(d)}\cap B_{s>d-\delta}\neq\{0\}.

We first show (i). To this end, let
x\in[a,(\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(s))\wedge b]
, then

\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(a)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(d-\delta)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(s).

Thus, by
Proposition

4.11
(i), we have that

\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(f(x))\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(M(s))=\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s))).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(d-\delta)\leq\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(x)
and
\mathbb{P}_{1}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)
, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(d-\delta)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(d+\delta),

and so
\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(\lvert x-d\rvert\right)\leq\delta
. By
Proposition

4.11
(iii),

\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(\lvert f(x)-f(d)\rvert\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(\varepsilon)

and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(x))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(d)+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(d)+\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(M(b)-\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))\right).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(x)\in\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}([a,s])
and
f
is locally band preserving,

\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(f(x))\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(M(s))=\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s)))

holds by
Proposition

4.11
(i). Therefore,

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}\right)(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s)))

\displaystyle\hskip 22.76228pt+\mathbb{P}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(M(b)-\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))\right)

\displaystyle\leq M(b)-(M(b)-M(s))\wedge\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d)).

By taking the supremum over all
x\in[a,(\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(s))\wedge b]
, we have that

M(b)-M((\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(s))\wedge b)\geq(M(b)-M(s))\wedge\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d)).

Therefore,
M(b)-M((\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(s))\wedge b)
is a weak order unit, so
(\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(s))\wedge b\in S
. Then we have
(\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)+\mathbb{P}_{1}^{d}(s))\wedge b\leq d
, hence

\mathbb{P}_{1}(d)\leq(\mathbb{P}_{1}(d)+\mathbb{P}_{1}(\delta))\wedge\mathbb{P}_{1}(b)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}(d).

Thus
\mathbb{P}_{1}(d)=(\mathbb{P}_{1}(d)+\mathbb{P}_{1}(\delta))\wedge\mathbb{P}_{1}(b)
, so

\mathbb{P}_{1}(d)=\mathbb{P}_{b=d}\mathbb{P}_{1}(b)+\mathbb{P}_{d<b}\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta)=\mathbb{P}_{1}(d+\delta),

as
B_{1}\subseteq B_{d<b}=B_{b=d}^{d}
. Hence
\mathbb{P}_{1}(\delta)=0
, showing that
B_{1}=\{0\}
.

Now we show (ii). To this end, let
x\in[a,b]
. We have that

\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(a)\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(d-\delta)\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(s).

By
Proposition

4.11
(i),

\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(f(x))\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(M(s))=\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s))).

Since

\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(d-\delta)\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(x)\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(b)=\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(d),

we have that
\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(\lvert x-d\rvert\right)\leq\delta
, so we can apply
Proposition

4.11
(iii) to get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(x))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(d)+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(d)+\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d)))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(M(b)-\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{2}(f(x))=\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(f(x))+\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}(f(x))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x\leq d-\delta}(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s)))+\mathbb{P}_{2}\mathbb{P}_{x>d-\delta}\left(M(b)-\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s))\wedge\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))).

By taking the supremum over all
x\in[a,b]
, we have that

\mathbb{P}_{2}(M(b))\leq\mathbb{P}_{2}(M(b)-(M(b)-M(s))\wedge\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))).

This inequality shows that
\mathbb{P}_{2}((M(b)-M(s))\wedge\frac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d)))=0
. Now

(M(b)-M(s))\wedge\tfrac{1}{2}(M(b)-f(d))

is a weak order unit, so
B_{2}=\{0\}
.

Therefore, the assumptions
f(a)\ll M(b)
and
f(d)\ll M(b)
lead to a contradiction. If
B\coloneqq B_{f(a)<M(b)}\cap B_{f(d)<M(b)}\neq\{0\}
, we can restrict ourselves to
B
by considering the function
g\colon\mathbb{P}([a,b])\to B
given by

g(x):=\mathbb{P}(f(x+\mathbb{P}^{d}(a))).

Since
g(\mathbb{P}(a))\ll\mathbb{P}(M(b))
and, using that
f
is locally band preserving,
g(\mathbb{P}(d))=\mathbb{P}(f(d))\ll\mathbb{P}(M(b))
in
B
, the above argument yields a contradiction. Hence, it must be that
B=\{0\}
, and so we obtain the inclusion
B_{f(a)<M(b)}\subseteq B_{f(d)=M(b)}
, which means
\mathbb{P}_{f(a)<M(b)}(f(d))=\mathbb{P}_{f(a)<M(b)}(M(b))
. Let
d_{0}:=\mathbb{P}_{f(a)=M(b)}(a)+\mathbb{P}_{f(a)<M(b)}(d)
. Since
f
is locally band preserving,

\displaystyle f(d_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{f(a)=M(b)}(f(d_{0}))+\mathbb{P}_{f(a)<M(b)}(f(d_{0}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{f(a)=M(b)}(f(a))+\mathbb{P}_{f(a)<M(b)}(f(d))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{f(a)=M(b)}(M(b))+\mathbb{P}_{f(a)<M(b)}(M(b))

\displaystyle=M(b).

Hence for all
x\in[a,b]
we have shown that
f(x)\leq f(d_{0})
. The existence of
c
follows by considering
-f
.
∎

We now prove an analogue of Rolle’s Theorem and the Mean Value Theorem for super order differentiable functions. Note that by
Theorem

4.9
all such functions are automatically locally band preserving.

Theorem 5.6

(Rolle’s Theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
with
a\ll b
. Let
f\colon[a,b]\to E
be order continuous on
[a,b]
and super order differentiable on
(a,b)
. If
f(a)=f(b)
, then there exists
x_{0}\in(a,b)
with
f^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

5.5
, there exist
c,d\in[a,b]
such that
f(c)\leq f(x)\leq f(d)
holds for all
x\in[a,b]
. Then
E=B_{0}\oplus B_{1}\oplus B_{2}
, where

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq B_{a<d}\cap B_{d<b},

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq B_{0}^{d}\cap B_{a<c}\cap B_{c<b},\text{ and}

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq B_{0}^{d}\cap B_{1}^{d}.

It follows that

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=(B_{a<d}\cap B_{d<b})^{d}\cap(B_{0}^{d}\cap B_{a<c}\cap B_{c<b})^{d}

\displaystyle=(B_{a=d}+B_{d=b})\cap(B_{0}+B_{a=c}+B_{c=b})

\displaystyle=(B_{a=d}\cap B_{a=c})+(B_{a=d}\cap B_{c=b})+(B_{d=b}\cap B_{a=c})+(B_{d=b}\cap B_{c=b})

\displaystyle=(B_{a=d}+B_{d=b})\cap(B_{a=c}+B_{c=b}).

Let
x_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}_{0}(d)+\mathbb{P}_{1}(c)+\mathbb{P}_{2}(\frac{1}{2}(a+b))
, and note that
x_{0}\in(a,b)
. Since

\mathbb{P}_{a=d}(f(d))=\mathbb{P}_{a=d}(f(a))
and

\mathbb{P}_{d=b}(f(d))=\mathbb{P}_{d=b}(f(b))=\mathbb{P}_{d=b}(f(a)),

we have that
\mathbb{P}_{2}(f(d))=\mathbb{P}_{2}(f(a))
. Similarly,
\mathbb{P}_{2}(f(c))=\mathbb{P}_{2}(f(a))
. Thus
\mathbb{P}_{2}\circ f
is constant, so
\mathbb{P}_{2}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))=0
. We will show that
\mathbb{P}_{0}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))=0
. The argument for why
\mathbb{P}_{1}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))=0
is similar as in both cases
x_{0}
equals an extremum on the band.

Since
f
is super order differentiable at
x_{0}
, there exists an
r\gg 0
such that
(x_{0}-r,x_{0}+r)\subseteq[a,b]
and there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists an
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

y\in[a,b]\text{ and }\lvert y-x_{0}\rvert\leq\tfrac{1}{N_{\varepsilon}}r\implies\lvert f(y)-f(x_{0})-(y-x_{0})f^{\prime}(x_{0})\rvert\leq\lvert y-x_{0}\rvert\varepsilon.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
y_{n}:=x_{0}+\frac{1}{n+1}r
. Then
y_{n}\in[a,b]
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\frac{1}{m}e
is invertible, we have that
\mathbb{P}(r)
has an inverse in
B\coloneqq B_{\frac{1}{m}e\leq r}
by
[
dP81
, Theorem 11.1]
. Denote this inverse by
s
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
and
n\geq N_{\varepsilon}
,
\lvert y_{n}-x_{0}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{N_{\varepsilon}}r
so

\left\lvert f(y_{n})-f(x_{0})-\tfrac{1}{n}rf^{\prime}(x_{0})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert y_{n}-x_{0}\right\rvert\varepsilon\leq\tfrac{1}{n+1}r\varepsilon,

and hence, by applying the projection
\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}
and multiplying by
(n+1)s
on both sides,

\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f(y_{n})-f(x_{0}))(n+1)s-\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))\right\rvert\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon).

Thus,
\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f(y_{n})-f(x_{0}))(n+1)s\to\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since we have
\mathbb{P}_{0}(x_{0})=\mathbb{P}_{0}(d)
, and
f
is locally band preserving,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f(y_{n})-f(x_{0}))(n+1)s=\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f(y_{n})-f(d))(n+1)s\leq 0.

Thus, we have
\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))\leq 0
. Replacing
y_{n}
by
z_{n}:=x_{0}-\frac{1}{n+1}r
in the argument above shows that
\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))\geq 0
. Therefore,
\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{P}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))=0
. Since
m
was arbitrary, by
Lemma

3.6
, we have that
\mathbb{P}_{0}(f^{\prime}(x_{0}))=0
.
∎

Theorem 5.7

(Mean Value Theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
with
a\ll b
. Let
f\colon[a,b]\to E
be order continuous on
[a,b]
and super order differentiable on
(a,b)
. Then there exists an
x_{0}\in(a,b)
such that

(b-a)f^{\prime}(x_{0})=f(b)-f(a).

Proof.

Define
g(x)\coloneqq(b-a)f(x)-(f(b)-f(a))x
. Then
g
is order continuous on
[a,b]
and super order differentiable on
(a,b)
since
f
and
x\mapsto x
are. Note that
x\mapsto x
has derivative
e
everywhere. Also,

g(a)=(b-a)f(a)-(f(b)-f(a))a=(b-a)f(b)-(f(b)-f(a))b=g(b).

By
Theorem

5.6
, there exists an
x_{0}\in(a,b)
such that

0=g^{\prime}(x_{0})=(b-a)f^{\prime}(x_{0})-(f(b)-f(a))e.

Therefore,
(b-a)f^{\prime}(x_{0})=f(b)-f(a)
.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
with
a\ll b
. Let
f\colon(a,b)\to E
be super order differentiable. For any
c,d\in(a,b)
, there exists
x_{0}\in\{c+x(d-c)\colon x\in(0,e)\}
such that

(d-c)f^{\prime}(x_{0})=f(d)-f(c).

Proof.

Define
g\colon[0,e]\to E
by
g(x):=f(c_{x})
, where
c_{x}\coloneqq c+x(d-c)
. It is easily verified that
g
is order continuous on
[0,e]
, and we will show that
g
is super order differentiable on
(0,e)
with
g^{\prime}(x)=(d-c)f^{\prime}(c_{x})
. To that end, let
\Delta\downarrow 0
and
x\in(0,e)
. Since
f
is super order differentiable at
c_{x}
, there exists an
\mathdutchcal{E}\searrow 0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in\mathdutchcal{E}
there exists a
\delta\in\Delta
such that for
y\in(a,b)
the implication

\displaystyle\lvert y-c_{x}\rvert\leq\lvert d-c\rvert\delta\implies\left\lvert f(y)-f(c_{x})-(y-c_{x})f^{\prime}(c_{x})\right\rvert\leq\lvert y-c_{x}\rvert\varepsilon

holds. Let
y\in[0,e]
be such that
\lvert y-x\rvert\leq\delta
. Then
c_{y}\in(a,b)
and

\lvert c_{y}-c_{x}\rvert=\lvert d-c\rvert\lvert y-x\rvert\leq\lvert d-c\rvert\delta,

so that

\displaystyle\left\lvert g(y)-g(x)-(y-x)(d-c)f^{\prime}(c_{x})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 61.17325pt=\left\lvert f(c_{y})-f(c_{x})-(y-x)(d-c)f^{\prime}(c_{x})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 61.17325pt=\left\lvert f(c_{y})-f(c_{x})-(c_{y}-c_{x})f^{\prime}(c_{x})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 61.17325pt\leq\lvert c_{y}-c_{x}\rvert\varepsilon

\displaystyle\hskip 61.17325pt=\lvert x-y\rvert\lvert d-c\rvert\varepsilon.

Therefore,
g
is super order differentiable on
(0,e)
. Now we apply
Theorem

5.7
to obtain a
s\in(0,e)
such that

(d-c)f^{\prime}(c_{s})=g^{\prime}(s)=g(e)-g(0)=f(d)-f(c).

Hence, we may choose
x_{0}\coloneqq c_{s}
.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
a,b\in E
with
a\ll b
. Let
f\colon(a,b)\to E
be super order differentiable with
f^{\prime}=0
. Then
f
is constant.

Proof.

For any
c,d\in(a,b)
, by applying
Corollary

5.8
, there exists an
x_{0}\in(a,b)
such that
f(d)-f(c)=(d-c)f^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
and hence
f(c)=f(d)
.
∎

Corollary

5.9
requires
f
to be super order differentiable, and the following example shows that it fails if
f
is only order differentiable. It also shows that a function can be order differentiable but not locally band preserving.

Example 5.10
.

Consider
E=\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and define
f\colon(0,e)\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
by

f((x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x_{n}\to\frac{1}{2},\\
e&\text{ if }x_{n}\not\to\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Let
x\in(0,e)
. Define
r:=(\frac{1}{n}\wedge(1-x_{n})\wedge x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. It follows that
(x-r,x+r)\subseteq(0,e)
, and for any
y\in(x-r,x+r)
,
\lvert x_{n}-y_{n}\rvert<r_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so that
y_{n}\to\frac{1}{2}
if and only if
x_{n}\to\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,
f
is constant on
(x-r,x+r)
. By
Proposition

3.10
and the fact that constant functions have derivative
0
,
f
is order differentiable on
(0,e)
with
f^{\prime}=0
.

To see that
f
is not locally band preserving, consider
e_{1}:=(1,0,0,\dots)
and note that
\mathbb{P}_{e_{1}}(\frac{1}{2}e_{1})=\frac{1}{2}e_{1}=\mathbb{P}_{e_{1}}(\frac{1}{2}e)
, but

\mathbb{P}_{e_{1}}(f(\tfrac{1}{2}e_{1}))=\mathbb{P}_{e_{1}}(e)=e_{1}\neq 0=\mathbb{P}_{e_{1}}(f(\tfrac{1}{2}e)).

Remark 5.11
.

Note that for a Dedekind complete real
\Phi
-algebra
E
, with
a,b\in E
such that
a\ll b
, and
f\colon[a,b]\to E
that is order continuous and super order differentiable on
(a,b)
,
Corollary

5.8
implies the following characteristic properties for
f
. In statement
(ii)
it is used that the product of two weak order units in a
\Phi
-algebra is a weak order unit. This fact follows from
[
Zaa83
, Theorem 142.5]
and
[
Zaa83
, Theorem 142.3]
.

(i)

If
f^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in(a,b)
, then
f
is increasing on
[a,b]
,

(ii)

if
f^{\prime}(x)\gg 0
for all
x\in(a,b)
, then for any
c,d\in[a,b]
with
c\ll d
we have that
f(c)\ll f(d)
,

(iii)

if
f’(x)>0
for all
x\in(a,b)
, then for any
c,d\in[a,b]
with
c\ll d
, we have that
f(c)<f(d)
, and

(iv)

if
f’(x)\gg 0
for all
x\in(a,b)
, then for any
c,d\in[a,b]
with
c<d
, we have that
f(c)<f(d)
.

Finally, we return to the complex case. To prove that a zero super order derivative implies a function is constant on order open neighbourhoods, we first prove a complex version of the Mean Value Theorem.

Theorem 5.12

(Complex Mean Value Theorem)
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete complex
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
c\in E
and a weak order unit
r
. Let
f\colon N(c,r)\to E
be super order differentiable. Then for any
a,b\in N(c,r)
, there exist
u,v\in\{a+x(b-a):x\in(0,e)\}
such that

(i)

\operatorname{Re}((b-a)f^{\prime}(u))=\operatorname{Re}(f(b)-f(a))
, and

(ii)

\operatorname{Im}((b-a)f^{\prime}(v))=\operatorname{Im}(f(b)-f(a))
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
Corollary

5.8
.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
E
be a Dedekind complete complex
\Phi
-algebra. Consider
c\in E
and
r
a weak order unit. Let
f:N(c,r)\to E
be super order differentiable with
f^{\prime}=0
. Then
f
is constant.

Proof.

Suppose that
a,b\in N(c,r)
. It follows from
Theorem

5.12
that there exist
u,v\in\{a+x(b-a):x\in(0,e)\}
such that

\operatorname{Re}(f(b)-f(a))=\operatorname{Re}((b-a)f^{\prime}(u))=0,

and

\operatorname{Im}(f(b)-f(a))=\operatorname{Im}((b-a)f^{\prime}(v)=0.

Thus,
f(a)=f(b)
.
∎

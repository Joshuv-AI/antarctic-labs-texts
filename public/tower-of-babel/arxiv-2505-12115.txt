Complex scalar field in κ \kappa -Minkowski noncommutative spacetime
*

Abstract
We present a comparison between translation charges for several Lagrangians for the κ \kappa -deformed complex scalar field using the canonical method. The Lagrangians are shown to be related by charge conjugation and twisted cyclicity, and these relationships are reflected in their conserved charges. The Lagrangian corresponding to the results obtained in [ 15 ] and [ 16 ] is identified, providing an explanation for the observed loss of charge conjugation symmetry under boosts.

Preliminaries

In this section we introduce the necessary mathematical tools for the analysis of fields on noncommutative spacetime.

2.1
Momentum space and Weyl map

Given that the spacetime coordinates in (
1
) correspond to algebra generators, plane waves are naturally
AN(3)
group elements:

\hat{e}_{k}=e^{ik_{j}\hat{x}^{j}}e^{ik_{0}\hat{x}^{0}}

(2)

where the wavenumbers
k
are coordinates on the group manifold. Note that while the ordering in the above is arbitrary, it defines the convention used to assign the
k
parameters.

It is at this point well established that the momentum space of
\kappa
-Minkowski is a submanifold of de Sitter
[
17
]
[
14
]
. In the classical basis, which is related to the
k
parametrization by the following relations:

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=\kappa\sinh\frac{k_{0}}{\kappa}+\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{\kappa}e^{\frac{k_{0}}{\kappa}},

(3)

\displaystyle p_{j}

\displaystyle=k_{j}e^{\frac{k_{0}}{\kappa}},

(4)

\displaystyle p_{4}

\displaystyle=\kappa\cosh\frac{k_{0}}{\kappa}-\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{\kappa}e^{\frac{k_{0}}{\kappa}},

(5)

the momentum space coordinates correspond to an embedding of this curved space in 5-dimensional flat Minkowski
[
14
]
. As such, they satisfy the condition

-p_{0}^{2}+\mathbf{p}^{2}+p_{4}^{2}=\kappa^{2}

(6)

where
p_{4}>0
.
It is straightforward to show from the commutation relations (
1
) and the definitions (
3
)-(
5
) that
p
-parametrized plane waves obey the following composition rules:

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{p}\hat{e}_{q}

\displaystyle=e^{ip_{j}\hat{x}^{j}}e^{ip_{0}\hat{x}^{0}}e^{iq_{j}\hat{x}^{j}}e^{iq_{0}\hat{x}^{0}}

(7)

\displaystyle=e^{i\left(\frac{q_{+}}{\kappa}p_{j}+q_{j}\right)\hat{x}^{j}}e^{i\left(\frac{q_{+}}{\kappa}p_{0}+\frac{1}{p_{+}}p_{j}q_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{p_{+}}q_{0}\right)\hat{x}^{0}}\equiv e^{i(p\oplus q)_{j}\hat{x}^{j}}e^{i(p\oplus q)_{0}\hat{x}^{0}}\equiv\hat{e}_{p\oplus q}

(8)

where

p_{+}=p_{0}+p_{4}
and similarly for
q_{+}
.
The composition law (
7
) gives us a natural notion of a “deformed inverse” (antipode)
S
:

\displaystyle S(p_{0})

\displaystyle=-p_{0}+\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{p_{+}}

\displaystyle S(p_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{p_{+}}p_{j}

\displaystyle S(p_{4})

\displaystyle=p_{4}

(9)

so that

p\oplus S(p)=S(p)\oplus p=0,

(10)

that is to say

\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{p}=\hat{e}_{S(p)}.

(11)

We now define the Weyl map
\mathcal{W}
by its action on
\hat{e}_{p}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(e^{ip_{j}\hat{x}^{j}}e^{ip_{0}\hat{x}^{0}})=e^{ipx}

(12)

which gives rise to the star product

e_{p}\star e_{q}=\mathcal{W}\left(\hat{e}_{p}\hat{e}_{q}\right)=\mathcal{W}\left(\hat{e}_{p\oplus q}\right)=e_{p\oplus q}.

(13)

2.2
The complex scalar field and its action

Given the noncommutative
\star
-product (
13
), there are two natural choices for the scalar field action:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi\right)^{\dagger}\star\partial_{\mu}\phi-m^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\star\phi\right]

(14)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[\partial_{\mu}\phi\star\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi\right)^{\dagger}-m^{2}\phi\star\phi^{\dagger}\right]

(15)

It can be shown that both of these lead to the same (undeformed) on-shell relation
\omega_{p}^{2}=\mathbf{p}^{2}+m^{2}
(see below, (
32
)-(
33
)).
We assume the standard mode expansion of
\phi
in 5-dimensional momentum space with the additional conditions (
6
) and
p_{4}>0

\phi(x)=\int d^{5}p\,\delta(p_{0}^{2}-\omega_{\mathbf{p}}^{2})\delta(\kappa^{2}+p_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{p}^{2}-p_{4}^{2})\theta(p_{4})\tilde{\phi}(p)

(16)

which can be expressed as

\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{2\omega_{\mathbf{p}}p_{4}/\kappa}\tilde{\phi}(\omega_{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p})e^{ipx}+\int\frac{d^{3}S(p)}{2\omega_{S(\mathbf{p})}p_{4}/\kappa}\tilde{\phi}(S(\omega_{\mathbf{p}}),S(\mathbf{p}))e^{iS(p)x}

(17)

by taking advantage of the fact that
\omega_{\mathbf{p}}>0\iff S(\omega_{\mathbf{p}})<0
. To simplify notation, we will express (
17
) as

\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf{p}}p_{4}/\kappa}}\left[a_{\mathbf{p}}e^{ipx}+b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}e^{iS(p)x}\right],

(18)

which, using (
11
), gives us the conjugate field

\phi^{\dagger}(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf{p}}p_{4}/\kappa}}\left[a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}e^{iS(p)x}+b_{\mathbf{p}}e^{ipx}\right].

(19)

The normalization was chosen to facilitate the comparison of results with
[
16
]
.

2.3
Differential calculus

We define the partial derivatives by their action on plane waves:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}e^{ipx}=ip_{\mu},

(20)

\displaystyle\partial_{4}e^{ipx}=i(p_{4}-\kappa).

(21)

This naturally leads to deformations of Leibniz rule inherited from composition rules for
p
through Fourier transforms:

\partial_{\mu}(f(x)\star g(x))=\int\mu(p)\mu(q)f(p)g(q)\partial_{\mu}e^{i(p\oplus q)x},

(22)

where
\mu(p)
is shorthand for the integration measure in (
16
).
The rules can be summed up as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}(f(x)\star g(x))=\partial_{0}(f\star g)=\partial_{0}f\star\left(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{\kappa}g\right)+\left(\frac{\kappa}{\Delta_{+}}f\right)\star(\partial_{0}g)+i(\Delta_{+}^{-1}\partial_{j}f)\star(\partial_{j}g),

(23)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}(f(x)\star g(x))=(\partial_{j}f)\star\left(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{\kappa}g\right)+f\star(\partial_{j}g),

(24)

where the
\Delta_{+}=-i\partial_{0}-i\partial_{4}+\kappa
operator is such that
\Delta_{+}e^{ipx}=p_{+}e^{ipx}
. We also define antipode derivatives in the vein of (
9
):

\displaystyle S(\partial_{0})=-\partial_{0}-i\Delta_{+}^{-1}\partial_{j}\partial_{j}\equiv-\partial^{\dagger}_{0}

(25)

\displaystyle S(\partial_{j})=-\frac{\kappa}{\Delta_{+}}\partial_{j}\equiv-\partial_{j}^{\dagger}

(26)

It can be shown from (
23
) and (
24
) that the following identities are satisfied:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}f\star g

\displaystyle=f\star S(\partial_{\mu})g+\text{total derivative}

(27)

\displaystyle f\star\partial_{\mu}g

\displaystyle=S(\partial_{\mu})f\star g+\text{total derivative}

(28)

2.4
Charge conjugation

Using (
25
) and (
26
), the two actions can be written as

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[S(\partial^{\mu})\phi^{\dagger}\star\partial_{\mu}\phi+m^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\star\phi\right]

(29)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[\partial_{\mu}\phi\star S(\partial^{\mu})\phi^{\dagger}+m^{2}\phi\star\phi^{\dagger}\right]

(30)

If we define the
C

\phi\xrightarrow{C}\phi^{\dagger}

(31)

then
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are related to each other by a
C
-transformation up to a surface term on account of (
27
) and (
28
). Given the momentum space representation (
18

), the transformation (
31

a_{\mathbf{p}}\xrightarrow{C}b_{\mathbf{p}}
, etc.

Noether charges

3.1
Variations of the two actions

In the canonical method, the conserved charges are computed from the variation of the action. Using the derivation rules (
23
) and (
24
) we can express the variations of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
as

\displaystyle\delta S_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[\partial_{A}\left(S(\Pi_{1}^{A})\phi^{\dagger}\star\delta\phi\right)+S(\partial_{A})\left(\delta\phi^{\dagger}\star\Pi_{1}^{A}\phi\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(S(\partial_{\mu})S(\partial^{\mu})+m^{2}\right)\phi^{\dagger}\star\delta\phi+\delta\phi^{\dagger}\star\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi\right]

(32)

\displaystyle\delta S_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[\partial_{B}\left(\delta\phi\star S(\Pi_{2}^{B})\phi^{\dagger}\right)+S(\partial_{B})\left(\Pi_{2}^{B}\phi\star\delta\phi^{\dagger}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\delta\phi\star\left(S(\partial_{\mu})S(\partial^{\mu})+m^{2}\right)\phi^{\dagger}+\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi\star\delta\phi^{\dagger}\right]

(33)

with the following differential operators :

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\kappa^{-1}\left(i\partial^{0}+\Delta_{+}\right)S(\partial^{0})\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}^{0}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{-1}\left(i\partial^{0}+\Delta_{+}\right)\partial^{0}

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}^{j}

\displaystyle=-\kappa^{-1}\left(i\partial^{0}+\Delta_{+}\right)S(\partial^{j})\;

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}^{j}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{-1}\left(i\partial^{0}+\Delta_{+}\right)\partial^{j}

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}^{4}

\displaystyle=i\kappa^{-1}m^{2}\;

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle=i\kappa^{-1}\partial_{0}\partial^{0}

(34)

The equations of motion are, in both cases, undeformed:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi

\displaystyle=0,

(35)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=0,

(36)

since
S(\partial_{\mu})S(\partial^{\mu})=\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}
.

3.2
Translations

For translations, we use the variation
\delta^{T}
:

\delta^{T}=\epsilon^{A}\partial_{A}.

(37)

The crucial thing to note here is that the parameter
\epsilon^{A}
is itself noncommutative, which allows for the operator
\delta^{T}
to satisfy Leibniz rule without violating (
23
) and (
24
):

\delta^{T}(f\star g)=(\delta^{T}f)\star g+f\star(\delta^{T}g).

(38)

The conserved currents for
S_{1}
are then obtained from (
32
):

\delta^{T}\mathcal{L}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{A}\left(S(\Pi_{1}^{A})\phi^{\dagger}\star\delta^{T}\phi\right)+\frac{1}{2}S(\partial_{A})\left(\delta^{T}\phi^{\dagger}\star\Pi_{1}^{A}\phi\right)=0,

(39)

and similarly for
S_{2}
, which is brought into the form of a conservation equation
\partial_{A}T^{A}_{\;B}=0
.
Using (
38
) one can show that

\displaystyle T^{0}_{\;A}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{A}S(\Pi_{1}^{0})\phi^{\dagger}\star\phi+\partial_{A}\phi^{\dagger}\star\Pi_{1}^{0}\phi\right],

(40)

\displaystyle T_{\,B}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{B}\phi\star S(\Pi_{2}^{0})\phi^{\dagger}+\partial_{B}\Pi_{2}^{0}\phi\star\phi^{\dagger}\right],

(41)

where the dummy indices follow the convention of (
32
) and (
33
) to help distinguish between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Integrating over space, we obtain the translation charges in a rather straightforward calculation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{1}=\int d^{3}x\,T^{0}_{\;\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\left[\frac{p_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}p_{\mu}b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b_{\mathbf{p}}-S(p_{\mu})a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a_{\mathbf{p}}\right]

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{2}=\int d^{3}x\,T^{0}_{\;\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\left[\frac{p_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}p_{\mu}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a_{\mathbf{p}}-S(p_{\mu})b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b_{\mathbf{p}}\right]

(43)

It is immediately obvious that the two charges are related by a
C
-transformation, as could be expected, since the total derivative by which
S_{1}^{C}
differs from
S_{2}
is exactly what is used to calculate the charge. Note that the momentum
p_{\mu}
and its antipode
S(p_{\mu})
come directly from the
\partial_{A}/\partial_{B}
derivatives acting on the left field in (
40
) and (
41
) and thus can be traced back to the mode expansions (
18
) and (
19
).

4
The
C
-invariant action

Since
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are related by a
C
-transformation, we can construct a manifestly
C
-invariant action via

S_{C}=\int d^{4}x\mathcal{L}_{C}=\frac{1}{2}\left(S_{1}+S_{2}\right).

(44)

This is the approach taken in
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
. The resulting charge is of the form

\mathcal{P}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}p\left[-S(p_{\mu})a_{\mathbf{p}}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}+p_{\mu}b_{\mathbf{p}}b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right].

(45)

We see that, despite symmetrization, the result is structurally akin to the one for the asymmetric action
S_{1}
apart from the
p_{+}^{3}/\kappa^{3}
factor. It should be noted that (
45
) was obtained using the covariant phase space formalism, that is by contracting the vector field generating translations with the symplectic form (for a comprehensive modern review of the method, see
[
18
]
):

-\delta^{T}_{\mu}\lrcorner\,\Omega=\delta\mathcal{P}_{\mu}.

(46)

In the following section we will demonstrate how the same result can be obtained canonically from the variation of the action (
44
).

4.1
Variation of the symmetric action

The variation of (
44
) is trivially the sum of variations of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
((
32
) and (
33
)). However, one notices that the equation of motion terms, e.g.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int d^{4}x\left[\delta\phi^{\dagger}\star\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi+\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi\star\delta\phi^{\dagger}\right]

(47)

do not allow for a simple factorization due to the noncommutative nature of the
\star
-product. Under integration, the ordering problem can be resolved via
twisted cyclicity
(see Appendix A). If one chooses to put
\delta\phi^{\dagger}
on the left, the resulting deformed equation of motion for
\phi
is then

\left(1+\frac{\Delta_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}\right)\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}\right)\phi=0.

(48)

Since on-shell the quantity
p_{+}^{3}/\kappa^{3}
is always positive, the equations of motion shouldn’t be affected and in principle the analysis can be continued, yielding

\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{C}=\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{1}+\mathcal{P}^{2}_{\mu},

(49)

which is manifestly
C
-symmetric. However, when this approach is extended to the Lorentz sector, the full set of charges does not seem to satisfy the Poincaré algebra. This issue is beyond the scope of this work and will be analyzed in detail in a future publication.

One can also apply twisted cyclicity a priori and consider the action
S_{C}
as

S_{C}=\int d^{4}x\mathcal{L}_{C1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\left[S(\partial_{\mu})\phi^{\dagger}\star\partial_{\mu}\left(1+\frac{\Delta_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}\right)\phi+m^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\star\left(1+\frac{\Delta_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}\right)\phi\right].

(50)

In this form, the analysis can be carried out exactly as for
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, resulting in the translation charges

\mathcal{P}^{C1}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{4}\int d^{3}p\left(1+\frac{p_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}\right)\left[-S(p_{\mu})a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a_{\mathbf{p}}+p_{\mu}b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b_{\mathbf{p}}\right],

(51)

which coincides with (
45
) (in which the overall factor
1+p_{+}^{3}/\kappa^{3}
was absorbed into the definitions of
a_{\mathbf{p}}
and
b_{\mathbf{p}}
).

The Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}_{C}
and
\mathcal{L}_{C1}
, however, are not equivalent, and since their actions are equal we can assume they differ by a total derivative, which is presumably responsible for breaking
C
-symmetry. Note that the choice to put
\phi^{\dagger}
on the left was arbitrary, and one could just as well consider the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{C2}=-\frac{1}{4}\left[\partial_{\mu}\left(1+\frac{\kappa^{3}}{\Delta_{+}^{3}}\right)\phi\star S(\partial^{\mu})\phi^{\dagger}+m^{2}\left(1+\frac{\kappa^{3}}{\Delta_{+}^{3}}\right)\phi\star\phi^{\dagger}\right]

(52)

which leads to the charge

\mathcal{P}^{C2}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{4}\int d^{3}p\left(1+\frac{p_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}\right)\left[p_{\mu}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a_{\mathbf{p}}-S(p_{\mu})b^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b_{\mathbf{p}}\right],

(53)

that is related by charge conjugation to (
51
). However,
\mathcal{L}_{C1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{C2}
(and similarly
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
) are physically equivalent, as the only way of distinguishing the two types of particles in the theory is their momentum.

4.2
The fate of
C
-symmetry

The only way of preserving
C
-symmetry (as defined in (
31
)) in the deformed theory appears to be the direct use of the symmetric Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{C}
. Its treatment, however, presents significant technical challenges and its conserved charges appear not to satisfy the Poincaré algebra, though it is not yet completely clear whether those problems stem from the methodology or the Lagrangian itself. Its deeper analysis will be presented in an upcoming paper, along with new insight into the fate of discrete symmetries in
\kappa
-deformed theories.

5
Conclusions

We have shown that the canonical method is in full agreement with the covariant phase space formalism in the case of translation charges for
\kappa
-deformed fields. Their lack of charge conjugation symmetry has been explained by an implicit change of Lagrangian, which appears necessary in both formalisms, suggesting a possible inherent incompatibility between
C
-symmetry and
\kappa
-deformation unless it were redefined.

Since
|S(p_{\mu})|<|p_{\mu}|
and
p_{+}/\kappa>1
, the choice between
\mathcal{L}_{C}
,
\mathcal{L}_{C1}
/
\mathcal{L}_{C2}
or
\mathcal{L}_{1}
/
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is phenomenologically meaningful.
\mathcal{L}_{C}
is currently not fully understood, while between the latter two pairs there is no preference on theoretical grounds, although they could, in principle, be distinguished by experiment. It remains to be seen whether higher spin fields provide additional insight into ordering and the behavior of charge conjugation.

Appendix A
Twisted cyclicity

While the
\star
-product is inherently noncommutative, it possesses a property known as
twisted cyclicity
under integration that allows some form of reordering:

\int d^{4}xf\star g=\int d^{4}xg\star\left(\frac{\Delta_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}f\right)=\int d^{4}x\left(\frac{\kappa^{3}}{\Delta_{+}^{3}}g\right)\star f

(54)

This can be proven directly from the Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xf(x)\star g(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\int\frac{d^{4}p}{p_{4}/\kappa}\int\frac{d^{4}S(q)}{q_{4}/\kappa}f(p)g(S(q))e^{i(p\oplus S(q))x}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{4}p}{p_{4}/\kappa}\int d^{4}S(q)f(p)g(S(q))\frac{q_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}\delta^{4}(p-q)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{4}S(q)}{q_{4}/\kappa}f(q)g(S(q))\frac{q_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}=\int\frac{d^{4}q}{q_{4}/\kappa}f(q)g(S(q))

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xg(x)\star f(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\int\frac{d^{4}S(p)}{p_{4}/\kappa}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{q_{4}/\kappa}f(q)g(S(p))e^{i(S(p)\oplus q)x}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{4}S(p)}{p_{4}/\kappa}\int d^{4}qf(q)g(S(p))\frac{\kappa^{3}}{q_{+}^{3}}\delta^{4}(S(p)-S(q))

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{4}q}{q_{4}/\kappa}f(q)g(S(q))\frac{\kappa^{3}}{q_{+}^{3}}=\int d^{4}x\frac{\kappa^{3}}{\Delta_{+}^{3}}f(x)\star g(x)

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}xf(x)\star\frac{\Delta_{+}^{3}}{\kappa^{3}}g(x)

where we used the formula
\delta^{4}(p\oplus S(q))=q_{+}^{3}/\kappa^{3}q_{4}/\kappa\delta^{4}(p-q)
.

Instantons on the blown-up surface and the Affine Vertex Algebra
Qingyuan Jiang, Wei-Ping Li, Yu Zhao

Abstract
. Vafa–Witten [ 43 ] observed that Yoshioka’s blow-up formula [ 45 , 46 ] for the Euler characteristics of rank r r instantons on an algebraic surface coincides with the character of the Wess–Zumino–Witten model for SU ⁡ ( r ) \mathrm{SU}(r) at level 1 1 , and raised the question of finding a rational conformal field theory explanation for this striking coincidence. In this work, we provide an answer to this question by constructing and analyzing the affine gl r \mathrm{gl}_{r} action on various cohomology theories, including the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves, Hochschild homology, Chow groups, and Hodge cohomology, of the moduli space of stable sheaves on a blown-up surface. A key ingredient in our proof is a representation-theoretic reformulation of the theory of Grassmannians of Tor-amplitude [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] -perfect complexes studied by the first-named author in terms of the spin representation of the finite-dimensional Clifford algebra. This may be viewed as a finite analog of the question of Vafa–Witten via the Boson–Fermion correspondence.

Introduction

1.1.
The main results of the paper

In
[
28
]
, Nakajima invented the Nakajima quiver varieties and started the study of the relation between the moduli space of instantons and vertex operator algebras (VOA for short). In
[
43
]
, Vafa–Witten found unexpected links between the supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory with the 2-dimensional conformal field theory (2d CFT for short), inspired by mathematical works
[
27
,
37
,
39
,
45
,
46
,
16
,
28
]
and the S-duality. In this paper, we answer a question in Vafa–Witten’s paper
[
43
]
, which relates Yoshioka’s blow-up formula with the WZW models of
\mathrm{SU}(r)
at level
1
. It is one of the oldest open problems relating the moduli space of instantons and VOA.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface and
\hat{S}:=\mathrm{Bl}_{o}S
be the blow-up of
S
along a closed point
o
, with the exceptional curve
C
and the projection map
p:\hat{S}\to S
. Let
H
be an ample line bundle on
S
. Given a total Chern class
c=(r,c_{1},n)
, let
\mathcal{M}_{H}(c)
be the moduli space of Gieseker stable sheaves on
S
with the total Chern class
c
. For any
1\gg\epsilon>0
, the pullback
p^{*}H-\epsilon C
is also
\mathbb{Q}
-ample. Moreover, given a total Chern class
\hat{c}
on
\hat{S}
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{p^{*}H-\epsilon C}(\hat{c})
is fixed as
\epsilon\to 0
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(\hat{c})
. In
[
45
,
46
]
, Yoshioka proved a blow-up formula for the Betti numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(c)
and
\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(\hat{c})
, which was generalized by the second-named author and Qin
[
21
,
20
]
and Nakajima–Yoshioka
[
34
,
36
]
to the virtual Hodge numbers where the moduli space can be singular. The formula is closely related to modular forms. For example, for the rank
2
case we have

\frac{\sum_{n}h^{i,j}(\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(2,p^{*}c_{1}-a[C],n))x^{i}y^{j}q^{n+a/4}}{\sum_{n}h^{i,j}(\mathcal{M}_{H}(2,c_{1},n))x^{i}y^{j}q^{n}}=q^{1/12}Z_{a}(x,y,q),\quad a=0,1,

where
h^{i,j}
is the (virtual) Hodge number and

(1.1)

Z_{a}(x,y,q)=\frac{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(xy)^{\frac{(2n+a)^{2}-(2n+a)}{2}}q^{(2n+a)^{2}/4}}{q^{1/12}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-(xy)^{2n}q^{n})^{2}},

such that
Z_{a}(x,1/x,q)=\frac{\theta_{a}(q)}{\eta(q)^{2}}
is the Jacobi theta function
\theta_{a}(q):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+a/2}q^{n^{2}}
divided by the Dedekind eta function
\eta(q):=q^{1/24}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})
.

Vafa–Witten
[
43
]
observed that
Z_{a}(x,1/x,q)
is related to the character of the WZW model of
\mathrm{SU}(r)
at level
1
for any
r>0
, and highlighted the unresolved mystery of the appearance of a 2d CFT in this context (see §1,3,4,6 of
[
43
]
for the precise formulations). Nakajima–Yoshioka
[
32
]
studied the setting for the equivariant cohomology groups of the moduli space of framed sheaves over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and constructed a basis representation of the affine Lie algebra
\hat{\mathrm{gl}}_{r}
on the blow-up through the localization. It provided a rational CFT construction for
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, as there is a correspondence between the WZW model and the basic representation through the affine vertex algebra (we refer to
[
5
]
for details of this correspondence). For the general case, including the unframed moduli space of stable coherent sheaves on any surface and non-equivariant cohomology, it remains unsolved.

In our paper, we give a full answer to this question by providing a geometric construction of this affine Lie algebra
\hat{\mathrm{gl}_{r}}
action for any surface
S
and many different cohomology theories. All the algebras in our paper are defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. The basic representation
\mathfrak{F}(r)
is defined through the Boson-Fermion correspondence (see
Theorem

8.2
for the details) and decomposes as the direct sum of irreducible representations
\mathfrak{F}(r)_{l,\bullet}
, where
l
is the central charge of the representation. It is periodic, which means that for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\mathfrak{F}(r)_{l,\bullet}\cong\mathfrak{F}(r)_{l+r,\bullet}
. Now, we fix
r
and
c_{1}
. Let
\mathbb{H}^{*}
be one of the (co)homology theories, including the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves, the Hochschild homology group, the Chow group, and the Hodge cohomology group.

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

8.1
)
.

If
\mathrm{gcd}(r,c_{1}\cdot H)=1
, then for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have an identification of
\hat{\mathrm{gl}}_{r}
-representations:

(1.2)

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(r,p^{*}c_{1}+l[C],n-\frac{l(l+1)}{2})\big)\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n)\big)\otimes\mathfrak{F}(r)_{l,\bullet}.

Here, an extra smooth assumption
Eq.

2.2
will be needed
for the Hochschild homology group, the Chow group, and the Hodge cohomology group.

Our
\hat{\mathrm{gl}}_{r}
acts trivially on the factor
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n))
on the right-hand side, which is one of the main differences from the previous work
[
29
,
38
]
. For the Hochschild homology group, the action preserves the homological degrees, which explains why
Z_{a}(x,1/x,q)
is independent of
x
in
Eq.

1.1
.

1.2.
Moduli of instantons, the conformal field theory and some related work

Since
[
28
,
43
]
, substantial advances have been made by both mathematicians and physicists in understanding the relationship between the moduli space of instantons and the VOA. A pivotal first step was taken by Nakajima
[
29
]
and Grojnowski
[
9
]
, who proved that the cohomology groups of the Hilbert schemes of points on an algebraic surface are the Fock space of a (super)-Heisenberg algebra. Lehn
[
19
]
constructed the Virasoro algebra and the second named author, Qin and Wang
[
22
]
constructed the
W_{1+\infty}
-algebra action on the cohomology groups of the Hilbert schemes of points on an algebraic surface.

The breakthrough on quiver varieties was also obtained by Nakajima
[
30
,
33
]
, where he constructed the action of affine Kac-Moody algebra and quantum loop algebra actions on the equivariant cohomology group and Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves over the Nakajima quiver varieties. Varagnolo constructed the Yangian action on the equivariant cohomology group of quiver varieties
[
44
]
.

The Hilbert scheme of points is also the moduli space of rank
1
stable coherent sheaves on a surface. For the higher rank generalization, one of the most important advances is the Alday–Gaiotto–Tachikawa correspondence
[
1
]
, which asserts that the equivariant cohomology of the moduli space of framed sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the Verma module of the
W
-algebra. It was established by Schiffmann–Vasserot
[
40
]
, Maulik–Okounkov
[
26
]
, and Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima
[
3
]
and a
\mathrm{K}
-theoretic generalization for any algebraic surface was developed by Negu

t

,

[
38
]
.

Besides the above novel breakthroughs, there are two works that directly inspired our approach. The first work is by Koseki
[
17
]
, where he constructed a semi-orthogonal decomposition (SOD for short) of the derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{coh}\big(\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(\hat{c})\big)
using a theorem of Toda
[
42
]
, under a smoothness assumption. When the third-named author was working at IPMU, Nakajima suggested that the third-named author consider its relation to representation theory, which became the starting point of this paper. The results in
[
17
]
are not immediately applicable to representation theory, as the explicit Fourier–Mukai kernels by the second-named author in
[
13
]
is essential for the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, which was not provided in
[
17
,
42
]
.

The second work is by Bershtein–Feigin–Litvinov
[
2
]
, where they explained (part of) the rank
2
Nakajima–Yoshioka’s blow-up equations
[
35
]
on
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
as an isomorphism of conformal blocks, using a different approach from ours. Their vertex algebra is the same as us but the conformal vector is different. To compare the different conformal vectors, one has to study the variation of Donaldson invariants under the geometric correspondences, which will be pursued in our future work.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof of
Theorem

1.1

The basic idea of proving
Eq.

1.2
is to consider some Hecke modifications along the
(-1)
-curve
C
by coherent sheaves on
\hat{S}
such as:

(1.3)

0\to\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}\to 0.

There are two difficulties in this approach. First, the Hecke modification changes the first Chern class while the
\hat{\mathrm{gl}}_{r}
-action preserves it. It requires us considering the action on a larger space

\bigoplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(r,p^{*}c_{1}+l[C],n-\frac{l(l+1)}{2})\big)

with respect to an infinite-dimensional Clifford algebra action (see
Eq.

6.2
for the definition). The
\hat{\mathrm{gl}}_{r}
-action will be obtained from the Boson–Fermion correspondence
[
6
]
. The second difficulty is considerably more challenging: the Hecke modification along
(-1)
-curve
C
will change the stability of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
. This is a significant difference from the previous work
[
29
,
38
]
, in which only
0
-dimensional coherent sheaves are modified.

Instead of directly studying the moduli space of stable sheaves, Nakajima–Yoshioka
[
36
]
introduced a notion of stable perverse coherent sheaf. Any stable perverse coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
\hat{S}
is a Hecke modification of
p^{*}E
by copies of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
or
\mathcal{O}_{C}(-1)
respectively (in different directions), where
E
is a stable sheaf on
S
. Let
\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n)
be the moduli space of stable perverse coherent sheaves on
\hat{S}
parametrized by two parameters
l,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
l
represents the shift of the first Chern class by
\ell
copies of the exceptional class
[C]
and
n
represents the second Chern class. Then the Hecke modification induces two descriptions of
\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n)
in terms of the Grassmannian of a Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
-perfect-complex:

\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n)\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n)}(\mathcal{U}_{o},-l),\quad\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n)\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n-l)}(\mathcal{U}_{o}^{\vee}[1],-l),

where
\mathcal{U}_{o}
is the restriction of the universal sheaf to
\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n)\times\{o\}
. Here a Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
-perfect complex
\mathcal{E}
is a perfect complex locally resolved by a morphism of two locally free sheaves
\mathcal{E}\cong\{V\xrightarrow{s}W\}.
The rank of
\mathcal{E}
is defined as
\mathrm{rank}(W)-\mathrm{rank}(V)
, which is a locally constant. The dual morphism
s^{\vee}:W^{\vee}\to V^{\vee}
also glues to form a Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
-perfect complex, which is called the shifted derived dual
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}[1]
. For the defintion of the Grassmannian, we follow the notion of Grothendieck to consider the moduli space of quotients instead of subspaces. For any connective perfect complex
\mathcal{E}
over a scheme
X
, we define the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E},d)
as the moduli space which parametrizes quotients
h^{0}(\mathcal{E})\twoheadrightarrow Q
,
where
Q
is a rank
d
locally free sheaf.

Our first major observation is a surprising isomorphism of graded
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
representations for a rank
r>0
Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
-complex over a scheme
X
:

(1.4)

\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E},d))\cong\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}[1],d))\otimes\mathrm{F}(r).

Here
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
(defined in
Section

3
) is the Clifford algebra generated by contraction and wedging operators
p_{i},q_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq r-1
and acts on the exterior algebra space
\mathrm{F}(r):=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\wedge^{i}\mathbb{Z}^{r}
by
p_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}}
and
q_{i}:=v_{i}\wedge-
. As the Boson-Fermion correspondence,
g_{ij}:=p_{i}q_{j}
for
0\leq i,j\leq r-1
form a basis of the Lie algebra
\mathrm{gl}_{r}
and identify
\mathrm{F}(r)
as the direct sum of all fundamental representations, and thus can be regarded as a finite analog of the Boson-Fermion correspondence. We refer to
Theorem

5.5
,
Theorem

4.1
, and
Theorem

5.7
for the detailed formulations and the extra technical assumptions of
Eq.

1.4
. In particular, if
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of rank
r
,
Eq.

1.4
gives an explicit identification of the cohomology of Grassmannian bundles with the Grassmann algebra through Clifford algebra representations:

\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E},d))\cong\mathbb{H}^{*}(X)\otimes\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}\wedge^{i}\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

Our construction for
Eq.

1.4
also involves an important insight: an SOD of a derived category (
\infty
-stable category) can be reinterpreted as a categorical representation of an associative algebra and naturally linked to the representation theory. We refer to
Propositions

5.1
and
3.3
for more details.

By
Eq.

1.4
, there is a
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-action on
\bigoplus_{l,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n))
, which can be extended to the action of a bigger algebra
\mathcal{E}(r)
with two more generators
e,f
where
p_{i}e=0
and
fq_{i}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq r-1
, and
fe=1
and
ef=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j}q_{j}
, by the identification of
\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n)
with
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n-l)}(\mathcal{U}_{o}^{\vee}[1],-l)
.
By
[
36
]
,
\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n)
is related to the moduli space of stable coherent sheaves on
\hat{S}
by the colimit for
m\gg 0
:

(1.5)

\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}\big(r,p^{*}c_{1}+l[C],n-\frac{l(l+1)}{2}\big)\cong\mathrm{M}^{0}\big(l-mr,n-ml+\frac{m(m-1)}{2}r\big).

Our second major observation is a surprising Morita equivalence
\mathfrak{H}_{\infty}
from the category of
\mathcal{E}(r)
-representations to a subcategory of the infinite dimensional Clifford algebra representations, such that the image of
\bigoplus_{l,n}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathrm{M}^{0}(l,n))
are both isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{l,n}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{H_{\infty}}(r,p^{*}c_{1}+l[C],n-\frac{l(l+1)}{2})\big)
and
\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathcal{M}_{H}(r,c_{1},n)\big)\otimes\mathfrak{F}(r)
respectively. Hence
Theorem

1.1
follows. We refer to
Corollary

6.2
and
Proposition

7.7
for more details.

3.
Grassmannians of complexes and the Clifford algebra

For any integer

, let the

be the unital associative
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra generated by elements

and

for

, subject to the relations
p_{i}^{2}=q_{i}^{2}=0
for all

\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0,\quad\{q_{i},q_{j}\}=0,\quad\{p_{i},q_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}\cdot 1\quad\text{for all }i,j\in[r].

Let
\mathrm{F}(r):=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{r}\wedge^{d}(\mathbb{Z}^{r})
be the exterior algebra and
\{v_{i}\mid i\in[r]\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Then
\mathrm{F}(r)
is the
spin representation
of
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
by the action

(3.1)

p_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}},\quad q_{i}:=v_{i}\wedge-.

In this section, we first sketch a

-action on
\bigoplus_{d=0}^{r}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,d))
for some (co)homology theory
\mathbb{H}^{*}
. We only need an identification of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules

(3.2)

\bigoplus_{d=0}^{r}\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,d))\cong\mathrm{F}(r).

We notice that the basis of
\mathrm{F}(r)
is parametrized by all the subsets of
[r]
, and for any
I\subset[r]
, we define the
weight
and
length
of
I
as
\mathrm{wt}(I):=\sum_{i\in I}i
and
\mathrm{len}(I):=\sum_{i\in I}1
. Recall that a
partition

\lambda
is a sequence of positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{l})
. Given any two integers

, let

be the set of partitions with

and
\lambda_{1}\leq b
. By the Schubert calculus, the basis of
\mathbb{H}^{*}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,d))
can be parametrized by all partitions in
B_{d,r-d}
. Let
\mathrm{P}_{r}
be the disjoint union of all
B_{d,r-d}
for
0\leq d\leq r
. On the other hand, let
[r]:=\{0,\cdots,r-1\}
be the set of
r
elements. Then
Eq.

3.2
is naturally induced from the bijection
\iota
from all the subsets of
[r]
to
\mathrm{P}_{r}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\iota:(i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{d-1})\to(i_{d-1}-d+1\geq\cdots\geq i_{0}).

From
Section

3
to
Section

5
, we generalize
Eq.

3.2
to the Grassmannians of Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
-complexes and also to different (co)homology theories. In particular, we construct an isomorphism of graded
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representations:

(3.4)

\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E},d)\big)\cong\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{*}\big(\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}[1],d)\big)\otimes\mathrm{F}(r),

where
\mathbb{H}^{*}
can be the Chow groups (
Theorem

4.1
), Hodge cohomology groups (
Theorem

4.1
), Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves (
Theorem

5.5
) and Hochschild homology groups (
Theorem

5.7
). The main tool for constructing

arises from the semi-orthogonal decomposition in derived categories, where it naturally induces a decomposition of the Grothendieck groups which is however generally not orthogonal and needs an orthogonalization. Surprisingly, we find that this orthogonalization is particularly useful for constructing
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representations to be explained in this section. The geometric correspondences are given in
Section

4
and
Section

5
.

Remark 3.1
.

Unlike
\mathrm{gl}_{r}
where there are infinite many irreducible representations,
\mathrm{F}(r)
is the unique irreducible representation of
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
(see
Lemma

3.2
for a proof). We will return to this perspective in

, via the Boson–Fermion correspondence.

3.1.
The orthogonalization and
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
representations

In
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
we define
p_{\emptyset}=q_{\emptyset}=1
and for any subset
I=(a_{0}<\ldots<a_{k})\subset[r]
we define

p_{I}:=p_{a_{k}}p_{a_{k-1}}\cdots p_{a_{0}},\quad q_{I}:=q_{a_{0}}q_{a_{1}}\cdots q_{a_{k}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V
be a
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representation and let
W:=\bigcap_{i\in[r]}\ker(p_{i})
.
Then we have the isomorphism of
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representations

(3.5)

W\otimes\mathrm{F}(r)\to V,\quad w\otimes v_{I}\mapsto q_{I}w.

Proof.

As
\mathrm{F}(r)\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{F}(1)
as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, by induction we only need to prove the case that
r=1
. In this case,
\mathrm{Im}(p_{0})\subset W
and the following morphism

V\to W\otimes\mathrm{F}(1)\quad v\to p_{0}v\otimes v_{[1]}+p_{0}q_{0}v\otimes v_{\emptyset}.

is the inverse of
Eq.

3.5
by direct computations.
∎

By
Lemma

3.2
, many properties of a
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representation
V
can be reduced to the computations on
\mathrm{F}(r)
. For example, for any
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representation
V
, we have

(3.6)

(p_{[r]}q_{[r]})|_{(\cap_{i\in[r]}\mathrm{ker}(p_{i}))}=\mathrm{Id},\quad(q_{[r]}p_{[r]})|_{(\cap_{i\in[r]}\mathrm{ker}(q_{i}))}=\mathrm{Id},

and
\sum_{I\subset[r]}q_{I}p_{[r]}q_{[r]}p_{I}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
. Regarding
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
as a
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representation via left multiplication, in
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
we have

(3.7)

\sum_{I\subset[r]}q_{I}p_{[r]}q_{[r]}p_{I}=1.

Now let
W
and
V
be two
\mathbb{Z}
-modules and
\mathcal{P}
be a total order on the set of all subsets
I\subset[r]
. Given morphisms
\mathfrak{e}_{I}:W\to V
and
\mathfrak{f}_{I}:V\to W
for all
I\subset[r]
, we say that they are
semi-orthogonal
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
if

\mathfrak{f}_{I}\mathfrak{e}_{J}=\delta_{IJ}\cdot\operatorname{id}_{W}\quad\text{for all}\quad I\geq_{\mathcal{P}}J.

We define their
orthogonalization
as

(3.8)

\displaystyle e_{I}:=\mathfrak{e}_{I},\quad f_{I}:=\mathfrak{f}_{I}\prod_{J>_{\mathcal{P}}I}^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathrm{Id}_{V}-\mathfrak{e}_{J}\mathfrak{f}_{J}).

We collect all morphisms
e_{I}
and
f_{I}
and define the morphisms

(3.9)

\displaystyle\hat{e}:W\otimes\mathrm{F}(r)\to V,\quad x\otimes v_{I}\mapsto e_{I}x,

\displaystyle\hat{f}:V\to W\otimes\mathrm{F}(r),\quad y\mapsto\sum_{I\subset[r]}f_{I}y\otimes v_{I}.

The surprising fact is that the morphism
\hat{e}
(or
\hat{f}
) is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representations if and only if
\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}:=\prod_{J\subset[r]}^{\mathcal{P}}(\mathrm{Id}_{V}-\mathfrak{e}_{J}\mathfrak{f}_{J})
acts as
0
on
V
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

The morphism
\hat{e}
(resp.
\hat{f}
) is bijective.

(2)

We have
\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}=0
.

(3)

There exists a Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
representation on
V
such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle e_{I}=q_{I}e,\quad f_{I}=fp_{I},

(3.11)

\displaystyle p_{i}e=0,\quad fq_{i}=0,\quad fe=\mathrm{Id}_{W},\quad ef=p_{[r]}q_{[r]},

where

e:=e_{\emptyset}:W\to V,\quad f:=f_{\emptyset}:V\to W.

Proof.

By direct computations, the morphisms
e_{I}
and
f_{I}
in (
3.8
) satisfy

(3.12)

\displaystyle f_{J}e_{I}=\delta_{IJ}\quad\text{ for all }\quad I\subset[r],

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}=1-\sum_{I\subset[r]}e_{I}f_{I}.

By
Eq.

3.12
and (
3.13
) we have
\hat{f}\hat{e}=\mathrm{Id}
and
\hat{e}\hat{f}=\mathrm{Id}-\Gamma_{\mathcal{P}}
. Hence (1), (2) are equivalent. If (3) holds, then by
Eq.

3.7

\sum_{I\subset[r]}e_{I}f_{I}=\sum_{I\subset[r]}q_{I}p_{[r]}q_{[r]}p_{I}=1

and hence (2) holds. If (1) and (2) hold, we consider the
V
as a
\mathrm{Cl}(r)
-representation induced from the isomorphism
\hat{e}
. To verify
Eq.

3.10
and
Eq.

3.11
, by
Lemma

3.2
we only need to verify the case that
W\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
V\cong\mathrm{F}(r)
, where
e_{I}
maps
1
to
v_{I}
. It follows from direct computations.
∎

.

By

and

Let

where the grading by the rank of quotients on the Grassmannians is compatible with the length grading on

5.
Derived Categories of Derived Grassmannians

In this section, we prove the Grothendieck group and Hochschild homology version of
Eq.

3.4
. The method is different from
Section

4
. The semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E}))
established by the first-named author in
[
13
]
will induce several categorical equations. By considering their decategorification, we uncover the underlying representation-theoretic structures through the lens of Clifford algebra representations.

Throughout this section, we work in the setting of derived algebraic geometry and with stable
\infty
-categories, as developed in
[
24
,
7
,
23
]
.
In particular, the derived categories of locally bounded (pseudo-)coherent sheaves,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(X)
, and perfect complexes,
\mathrm{Perf}(X)
, on a scheme (or, more generally, a derived stack)
X
are understood as stable
\infty
-categories. Accordingly, all functors, such as the pullback
f^{*}
and pushforward
f_{*}
for a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
, are interpreted in the derived sense.
Given a (derived) scheme
X
, let
\mathrm{G}_{0}(X)
(resp.
\mathrm{K}_{0}(X)
) denote its Grothendieck group of (pseudo-)coherent sheaves (resp. perfect complexes).

5.1.
Semiorthogonal decompositions and categorical equations

We begin by describing how semiorthogonal decompositions can be expressed in terms of categorical equations.

A
semiorthogonal decomposition
of a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{n}\rangle,

is a sequence of stable
\infty
-subcategories
\mathcal{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{n}
satisfying:

(1)

\operatorname{Hom}(D_{j},D_{i})\cong 0
for all
D_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{i}
,
D_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
, whenever
j>i
.

(2)

For any
D\in\mathcal{D}
, there is a sequence

0=D_{n}\to D_{n-1}\to\cdots\to D_{1}\to D_{0}=D

in
\mathcal{D}
such that
\mathrm{cone}(D_{j}\to D_{j-1})\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq n
.

The sequence
\mathcal{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{n}
is called
semiorthogonal
if only condition (1) is required.

Now, let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be stable
\infty
-categories and consider functors

\Phi_{j}\colon\mathcal{A}_{j}\to\mathcal{D}.

Suppose that each
\Phi_{j}
admits a left adjoint
\Phi_{j}^{L}
. The adjunction yields natural unit and counit maps:

\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{D}}\to\Phi_{j}\Phi_{j}^{L},\qquad\Phi_{j}^{L}\Phi_{j}\to\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}.

Set
\mathfrak{R}_{j}:=\mathrm{cone}\big(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{D}}\to\Phi_{j}\Phi_{j}^{L}\big)[-1]
, the homotopy fiber of the unit map.

We now reformulate the defining conditions for a semiorthogonal decomposition in terms of the following
categorical equations
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{cone}(\Phi_{j}^{L}\Phi_{j}\to\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}})\cong 0

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}^{L}\circ\Phi_{j}\cong 0\text{ for all }j>i

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\mathfrak{R}_{1}\cong 0

Proposition 5.1
.

For each
j
, the functor
\Phi_{j}
is fully faithful if and only if
Eq.

5.1
holds. If all
\Phi_{j}
are fully faithful, the sequence of essential images

\Phi_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{1}),\ \Phi_{2}(\mathcal{A}_{2}),\ \ldots,\ \Phi_{n}(\mathcal{A}_{n})

is semiorthogonal if and only if
Eq.

5.2
holds. Moreover, if the above conditions are satisfied, the sequence of essential images of
\Phi_{j}
forms a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
Eq.

5.3
holds.

Proof.

These statements are implicitly proved in
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
. Specifically, the first two statements follow from adjunction and the definition of semiorthogonality. For the last statement, set
\mathfrak{Fil}_{0}=\operatorname{id}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
. For each
1\leq j\leq n
, define:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Fil}_{j}:=\mathfrak{R}_{j}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathfrak{R}_{1}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D},\quad\mathfrak{pr}_{j}:=\Phi_{j}^{L}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathfrak{R}_{1}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{A}_{j}.

These fit into a canonical diagram in the stable
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D})
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Fil}_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Fil}_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Fil}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Fil}_{0}:=\operatorname{id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{n}\circ\mathfrak{pr}_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{n-1}\circ\mathfrak{pr}_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{1}\circ\mathfrak{pr}_{1},}

where each triangle is an exact triangle. Thus, the functors
\Phi_{j}
induce a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{D}
if and only if
\mathfrak{Fil}_{n}\cong 0
.
∎

Dually, suppose that each
\Phi_{j}

\Phi_{j}^{R}
(and not necessarily a left adjoint),
define
\mathfrak{L}_{j}:=\mathrm{cone}(\Phi_{j}\Phi_{j}^{R}\to\operatorname{id})

Eqs.

5.1
,
5.2
and
5.3

(

\displaystyle\mathrm{cone}(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}\to\Phi_{j}^{R}\Phi_{j})\cong 0,

(
5.2

)

\displaystyle\Phi_{j}^{R}\circ\Phi_{i}\cong 0\quad\text{for all }j>i,

(
5.3

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathfrak{L}_{n}\cong 0.

Remark 5.3
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we consider the unital algebra generated by
m_{i},\psi_{i},\phi_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
m_{\infty}
with relations

\displaystyle m_{i}m_{j}=\delta_{ij}m_{i},\quad\phi_{i}m_{j}=\delta_{ij}\phi_{i},\quad m_{i}\psi_{j}=\delta_{ij}\psi_{j},\text{ for all }i,j\in[1,n]\cup\{\infty\}

\displaystyle m_{\infty}\phi_{i}=\phi_{i},\quad\psi_{i}m_{\infty}=\psi_{i},\quad m_{j}\phi_{i}=0,\quad\psi_{i}m_{j}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq n

\displaystyle\psi_{i}\phi_{j}=\delta_{ij}m_{j},\quad\text{ for }1\leq i\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}=(1-\phi_{1}\psi_{1})\cdots(1-\phi_{n}\psi_{n})=1-m_{\infty}.

Then by
Proposition

, any semi-orthogonal decomposition of a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
into
n

m_{i}
acts as the projection to the
i
-th component, and
m_{\infty}
acts as the projection to
\mathcal{D}
.

Next, we focus on derived Grassmannians of perfect complexes and their incidence correspondences

Given

has the same underlying classical scheme as the derived fiber product

but with a

where
\rho_{+}
and

and

\mathcal{Q}_{-}^{\vee}[1]\xrightarrow{\rho_{-}^{\vee}[1]}\mathcal{E}_{T}\xrightarrow{\rho_{+}}\mathcal{Q}_{+}

\mathrm{cone}\Big(\mathrm{cone}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{-}^{\vee}[1]\xrightarrow{\rho_{-}^{\vee}[1]}\mathcal{E}_{T}\big)\xrightarrow{\rho_{+}}\mathcal{Q}_{+}\Big)[-1]\in\mathrm{D_{perf}}(T).

and rank

By construction, we have a correspondence diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E},d_{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gr}_{X}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}[1],d_{-})}

Consider the following operators over

e_{ij}^{a}:=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}:Q_{m,i}P_{m+a,j}:i,j\in[r],a\in\mathbb{Z},

On the arithmetic properties of partitions into parts simultaneously 4 4 -regular and 9 9 -distinct
Mohammed L. Nadji, Moussa Ahmia

Abstract
. In 2017, Keith presented a comprehensive survey on integer partitions into parts that are simultaneously regular, distinct, and/or flat. Recently, the authors initiated a study of partitions into parts that are simultaneously regular and distinct, examining them from both arithmetic and combinatorial perspectives. In particular, several Ramanujan-like congruences were obtained for RD ( ℓ , t ) ⁡ ( n ) \myRD^{(\ell,t)}(n) , the number of partitions of n n into parts that are simultaneously ℓ \ell -regular and t t -distinct (parts appearing fewer than t t times), for various pairs ( ℓ , t ) (\ell,t) . In this paper, we focus on the case ( ℓ , t ) = ( 4 , 9 ) (\ell,t)=(4,9) and conduct a thorough investigation of the arithmetic properties of RD ( 4 , 9 ) ⁡ ( n ) \myRD^{(4,9)}(n) . We establish several infinite families of congruences modulo 4 4 , 6 6 , and 12 12 , along with a collection of Ramanujan-like congruences modulo 24 24 .

1.
Introduction

A
partition
of a nonnegative integer
n
is a finite sequence of positive integers which sum to
n
. We write
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}+\lambda_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}+\dots+\lambda_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}=n
, where each part
\lambda_{i}
occurs
\alpha_{i}
times with
\lambda_{i},\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Here and throughout we will use the standard
q
-series notation

(a;q)_{n}:=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{i})&\text{if $n>0$,}\\
1&\text{if $n=0$.}\end{cases}

Moreover,
(a;q)_{\infty}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}
for
|q|<1
,
and
f_{k}
is defined by

f_{k}:=(q^{k},q^{k})_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{nk}).

For a positive integer
\ell>1
, a partition is called
\ell
-regular if none of the parts are divisible by
\ell
. Let
b_{\ell}(n)
denote the number of
\ell
-regular partitions of
n
. The generating function for
b_{\ell}(n)
is given by

\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}b_{\ell}(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{f_{\ell}}{f_{1}}.

Regular partitions have been studied by many authors, including
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

An
\ell
-distinct partition
of
n
is a finite sequence of positive integers such that
\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}+\lambda_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}+\dots+\lambda_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}=n
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq\alpha_{i}<\ell
, and
\ell>2
. The number of
\ell
-distinct partitions of
n
, denoted by
p_{\ell}(n)
, satisfies the same generating function for
b_{\ell}(n)
. For more details on the
\ell
-distinct partitions, we refer the readers to
[
4
,
5
]
.

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{1}},\lambda_{2}^{\alpha_{2}},\dots,\lambda_{k}^{\alpha_{k}})
of
n
is said to be simultaneously
\ell
-regular and
t
-distinct if its parts
\lambda_{i}
are indivisible by
\ell
and
1\leq\alpha_{i}<t
for
1\leq i\leq k
. The number of such partitions of
n
is denoted by
\myRD^{(\ell,t)}(n)
and satisfies the generating function

(1)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\myRD^{(\ell,t)}(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{f_{t}f_{\ell}}{f_{1}f_{\ell t}}.

For example, the nine partitions of
6
into parts simultaneously
4
-regular and
9
-distinct are

(6),(5,1),(3^{2}),(3,2,1),(3,1^{3}),(2^{3}),(2^{2},1^{2}),(2,1^{2}),(1^{6}).

This class of partitions was introduced by Keith
[
9
]
, who provided a comprehensive survey on partitions into parts that are simultaneously regular, distinct, and/or flat. The concept was later explored both combinatorially and arithmetically by the authors
[
10
]
. The number
\myRD^{(\ell,t)}(n)
has been studied arithmetically for various pairs
(\ell,t)
in
[
10
]
, including
(\ell,t)=(4,9)
, where several Ramanujan-like congruences were established. For instance, the authors
[
10
]
presented the following congruences.

Lemma 1.1

(Nadji and Ahmia
[
10
]
)
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the following congruences hold

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(4n+3)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(6n+2)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2),

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(6n+3)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(6n+4)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4),

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(6n+5)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 6),

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(6n+7)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 12),

\displaystyle\myRD^{(4,9)}(8n+5)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 6).

It is noteworthy that the enumeration function
\myRD^{(4,9)}(n)
corresponds to sequence
A187020
(shifted by one term) in the Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we delve into the arithmetic behavior of partitions into parts simultaneously
4
-regular and
9
-distinct by establishing some families of congruences for
\myRD^{(4,9)}(n)
modulo
4,6
, and
12
, along with additional Ramanujan-like congruences modulo
24
.

Ramanujan’s general theta-function

f(a,b):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{n(n+1)/2}b^{n(n-1)/2}=(-a,ab)_{\infty}(-b,ab)_{\infty}(ab,ab)_{\infty}.

\psi(q):=f(q,q^{3})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)/2}=\dfrac{(q^{2},q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q,q^{2})_{\infty}}=\dfrac{f_{2}^{2}}{f_{1}},

f(-q):=f(-q^{2},-q)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(3n+1)/2}=(q;q)_{\infty}=f_{1}.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any prime
p\equiv 3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
,
\alpha\geq 0
, and
n\geq 0
, we have

\myRD^{(4,9)}\Bigl(12p^{2\alpha+1}(pn+i)+p^{2\alpha+2}+1\Bigl)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4),

for all

.

Proof.

In view of (
7
) and (
8
), with
p=2
and
k=1
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\myRD^{(4,9)}(2n)q^{n}=\dfrac{f_{6}^{7}f_{9}^{7}}{f_{3}^{9}f_{18}^{5}}+2q\dfrac{f_{6}^{6}f_{9}^{4}}{f_{3}^{8}f_{18}^{2}}+4q^{2}\dfrac{f_{6}^{5}f_{9}f_{18}}{f_{3}^{7}}\equiv\dfrac{f_{6}^{7}f_{9}^{7}}{f_{3}^{9}f_{18}^{5}}+2qf_{6}^{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

If we extract the terms of the form
q^{3n+1}
from both sides of the above equation, we get

\sum_{n\geq 0}\myRD^{(4,9)}(6n+2)q^{n}\equiv 2f_{2}^{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

That is,

(12)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\myRD^{(4,9)}(12n+2)q^{n}\equiv 2f_{1}^{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

Define

(13)

\sum_{n\geq 0}a(n)q^{n}=f_{1}^{2}.

Combining Equations (
12
) and (
13
), we deduce that

(14)

\myRD^{(4,9)}(12n+2)\equiv 2a(n)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).

Now, we consider the following congruence equation:

\frac{3k^{2}+k}{2}+\frac{3m^{2}+m}{2}\equiv\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p).

It is equivalent to

(15)

(6k+1)^{2}+(6m+1)^{2}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p),

where
-(p-1)/2\leq k,m\leq(p-1)/2
and
p
is a prime such that
(\frac{-1}{p})=-1
. Since
(\frac{-1}{p})=-1
for
p\equiv 3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
, the congruence relation (
15
) holds if and only if both
k=m=(\pm p-1)/6
. Substituting Equation (
6
) into (
13
) and then extracting the terms in which the powers of
q
are congruent to
(p^{2}-1)/12
modulo
p
, and then divide by
q^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}}
, we find that

\sum_{n\geq 0}a\left(pn+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}\right)q^{pn}=f_{p^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\sum_{n\geq 0}a\left(p^{2}n+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}\right)q^{n}=f_{1}^{2},

and for all
n\geq 0
,

(16)

a\left(p^{2}n+pi+\frac{p^{2}-1}{12}\right)=0,

where
1\leq i\leq p-1
. By induction we can prove that for
n\geq 0
and
\alpha\geq 0
,

(17)

a\left(p^{2\alpha}n+\frac{p^{2\alpha}-1}{12}\right)=a(n).

Replacing

∎

A two-boson lattice Hamiltonian with interactions up to next-neighboring sites
Saidakhmat N. Lakaev, Alexander K. Motovilov, Mukhayyo O. Akhmadova

Abstract
. A system of two identical spinless bosons on the two-dimensional lattice is considered under the assumption that on-site and first and second nearest-neighboring site interactions between the bosons are only nontrivial and that these interactions are of magnitudes γ \gamma , λ \lambda , and μ \mu , respectively. A partition of the ( γ , λ , μ ) (\gamma,\lambda,\mu) -space into connected components is established such that, in each connected component, the two-boson Schrödinger operator corresponding to the zero quasi-momentum of the center of mass has a definite (fixed) number of eigenvalues, which are situated below the bottom of the essential (continuous) spectrum and above its top. Moreover, for each connected component, a sharp lower bound is established on the number of isolated eigenvalues for the two-boson Schrödinger operator corresponding to any admissible nonzero value of the center-of-mass quasimomentum.

It is well known that in the continuous case, the center-of-mass

(1.1)

\mathrm{H}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}^{\oplus}H(K)\,dK,

where
\mathbb{T}^{d}
stands for the
d
-dimensional torus,
K
for
quasimomentum of the center of mass, and
H(K)
is the so called
fiber Hamiltonian. For each
K\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the latter is an operator in
the Hilbert space on
\mathbb{T}^{(N-1)d}
. The decomposition
(
1.1
) allows one to reduce the problem of the study of the
total Hamiltonian
\mathrm{H}
to studying the fiber operators

H(K)
. We notice that the dependence of
H
on the quasimomentum

K\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
is quite nontrivial (see e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
30
]
).

In this work, we restrict ourselves to the case were
N=d=2
and the
particles are identical spinless bosons. This implies that, for any

K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the fiber Hamiltonian
H(K)
should be an operator in
the Hilbert space
L^{2,\mathrm{e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
whose elements are
measurable square integrable functions
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
which, in
addition, are even, that is
f(-p)=f(p)
,
p\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
. More
precisely, we
study the spectral properties of the fiber
Hamiltonians
H(K),\,K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, that act on
L^{2,\mathrm{e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

as

(1.2)

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K):=H_{0}(K)+V_{\gamma\lambda\mu},

where
H_{0}(K)
is the fiber kinetic-energy operator,

\bigl(H_{0}(K)f\bigr)(p)={\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p)f(p),

with

(1.3)

{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p):=2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1-\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\,\cos p_{i}\Big)

and
V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
is the combined interaction potential
that does not depend on
K
. The parameters
\gamma
,
\lambda
and

\mu
, called coupling constants, are nothing but factors
determining interactions between the particles which are located on
same site, on nearest neighboring sites and on next-to-nearest
neighboring sites of the lattice, respectively.

As a next step, we realize that, in the case of the potential

V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
employed, the bosonic space

L^{2,\mathrm{e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
itself admits an orthogonal decomposition
into three subspaces:

L^{2,{\rm e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})=L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\oplus L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\oplus L^{\mathrm{2,ea}}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),

where

(1.4)

\displaystyle L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}

\displaystyle(\mathbb{T}^{2})

\displaystyle=\{f\in L^{2,\mathrm{e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):f(p_{1},p_{2})=f(p_{2},p_{1})=f(-p_{1},p_{2}),\,\,\forall p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{T}\}

(1.5)

\displaystyle L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}

\displaystyle(\mathbb{T}^{2})

\displaystyle=\{f\in L^{2,\mathrm{e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):f(p_{1},p_{2})=f(p_{2},p_{1})=-f(-p_{1},p_{2}),\,\,\forall p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{T}\}

(1.6)

\displaystyle L^{2,\mathrm{ea}}

\displaystyle(\mathbb{T}^{2})

\displaystyle=\{{f}\in L^{\mathrm{2,e}}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):{f}(p_{1},p_{2})=-{f}(p_{2},p_{1}),\,\,\forall p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{T}\}.

The important thing is that the orthogonal subspaces

L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,   and

L^{\rm 2,ea}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
are reducing for
{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)
.
This allows one to study the spectrum of the Hamiltonian

{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)
separately for its parts in

L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,   and

L^{\rm 2,ea}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

In the model (
1.2
), we determine both the exact number
and location of the eigenvalues for the operator

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)
, depending on the interaction parameter

\gamma
,
\lambda
and
\mu
. We also reveal the mechanisms of
emergence and absorption of these eigenvalues at the thresholds of
the essential (continuous) spectrum of
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)
, as

\gamma
,
\lambda
and
\mu

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)

2.
Hamiltonian of a two-boson system on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and its basic properties

2.1.
The position-space representation

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{2}:=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
be the two-dimensional integer lattice and
let
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
be the Hilbert space of
square-summable symmetric functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}.
We consider
a system of two identical spinless bosons on the two-dimensional
lattice under the assumption that on-site and first and second
nearest-neighboring site interactions between the particles are only
nontrivial and that these interactions are of magnitudes
\gamma
,

\lambda
, and
\mu
, respectively. The corresponding Hamiltionian
in the position-space represenatation is introduced on

\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
as

(2.1)

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}=\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}+\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu},\hskip 23.49976pt\gamma,\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}.

Here,
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
stands for the Hamiltonian of the pair of
identical non-interacting bosons:

(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}\widehat{f})(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-y)\widehat{f}(y,x_{2})+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-y)\widehat{f}(x_{1},y),\hskip 11.74988ptx_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

where

\widehat{\varepsilon}(s)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $|s|=0,$}\\
-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $|s|=1,$}\\
0&\text{if $|s|>1,$}\end{cases}

and
|s|=|s_{1}|+|s_{2}|
for
s=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.
The interaction

\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
is the operator of multiplication by a
function
\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
,

(\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}\widehat{f})(x,y)=\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(x-y)\widehat{f}(x,y),

where

\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(x)=\begin{cases}\gamma&|x|=0,\\
\frac{\lambda}{2}&|x|=1,\\
\frac{\mu}{2}&|x|=2,\\
0&|x|>2.\end{cases}

2.2.
The quasimomentum-space representation.

By
\mathbb{T}^{2}
we denote the two-dimensional torus,
\mathbb{T}^{2}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}\equiv[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
. The torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
coincides with the
Pontryagin dual group of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, equipped with the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}p
. Let
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
be the Hilbert space of
square-integrable symmetric functions on
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}.
We use
the notation
{\mathcal{F}}
for the standard Fourier transform from

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,

({\mathcal{F}}\widehat{f})(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{f}(x)e^{ip\cdot x},

where
p\cdot x:=p_{1}x_{1}+p_{2}x_{2}
for
p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and

x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

The quasimomentum-space version of the Hamiltonian (
2.1
)
reads as

\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}:=({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})^{*}:=\mathbb{H}_{0}+\mathbb{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}.

Here the free Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{0}=({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
is the multiplication operator:

(\mathbb{H}_{0}f)(p,q)=[\varepsilon(p)+\varepsilon(q)]f(p,q),

where

\varepsilon(p):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\big(1-\cos p_{i}),\hskip 11.74988ptp=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

is the
dispersion relation
of a single boson.
The interaction
\mathbb{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}=({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
is the (partial)
integral operator

(\mathbb{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}f)(p,q)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(p-u)f(u,p+q-u)\mathrm{d}u,

where

v_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(p)=\gamma+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{2}\cos p_{i}+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\cos 2p_{i}+2\mu\cos p_{1}\cos p_{2},\hskip 11.74988ptp=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}.

2.3.
The Floquet-Bloch decomposition of
\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}

and fiber Schrödinger operators

Since
\widehat{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
commutes with the representation
of the discrete group
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by shift operators on the lattice, we
can decompose the space
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
and

\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
into the von Neumann direct integral as

(2.2)

L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\oplus L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}K

and

(2.3)

\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\oplus H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)\,\mathrm{d}K,

where
L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
is the Hilbert space of square-integrable even
functions on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
(see, e.g.,
[
3
]
). The fiber
operator
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K),

K\in\mathbb{T}^{2},
is a self-adjoint
operator on
L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of the form

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K):=H_{0}(K)+V_{\gamma\lambda\mu},

where the unperturbed operator
H_{0}(K)
is the operator of
multiplication by the function

(2.4)

{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p):=2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1-\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\,\cos p_{i}\Big).

and the perturbation
V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
reads as

(2.5)

\displaystyle V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}f(p)=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{4\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}f(q)\mathrm{d}q+\frac{\lambda}{4\pi^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\cos p_{i}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\cos q_{i}f(q)\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle\,+\frac{\mu}{4\pi^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\cos 2p_{i}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\cos 2q_{i}f(q)\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{2\pi^{2}}\cos p_{1}\cos p_{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\cos q_{1}\cos q_{2}f(q)\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu}{2\pi^{2}}\sin p_{1}\sin p_{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\sin q_{1}\sin q_{2}f(q)\mathrm{d}q.

The parameter
K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
is usually called the
two-particle
quasimomentum
and the entry
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)
is called the

fiber Schrödinger operator
associated with the two-particle
Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
.

2.4.
Essential spectrum of the fiber Schrödinger operators

Depending on
\gamma,\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
the rank of

V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
varies but never exceeds seven. Hence, by
the classical Weyl theorem, for any
K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the essential
spectrum
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K))
of the
fiber Schrödinger operator
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)
coincides
with the spectrum of
H_{0}(K):

(2.6)

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K))=\sigma(H_{0}(K))=[{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K),{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K)],

where

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K):=

\displaystyle\min_{p\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\,{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p)=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1-\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\Big)\geq{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(0)=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K):=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\,{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p)=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1+\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\Big)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(0)=8.

2.5.
Relationship between the discrete spectra of

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)
for
K\neq 0
and

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)

For every
n\geq 1
we define

(2.9)

e_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu):=\sup\limits_{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}\in L^{2,\rm e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\,\,\inf\limits_{{\psi}\in[\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}]^{\perp},\,\|{\psi}\|=1}({H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K){\psi},{\psi})

and

(2.10)

E_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu):=\inf\limits_{\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}\in L^{2,\rm e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\,\,\sup\limits_{{\psi}\in[\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}]^{\perp},\,\|{\psi}\|=1}({H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K){\psi},{\psi}).

By the minimax principle,
e_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)
and
E_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)\geq{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K).

From the representation (
2.5
) it follows that the
rank of
V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
does not exceed seven. Then, by
choosing suitable elements
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
,
\phi_{3}
,
\phi_{4}
,

\phi_{5}
,
\phi_{6}
and
\phi_{7}
from the range of

V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
, one concludes that

e_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)={\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)
and

E_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)={\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K)
for all
n\geq 8.
If the
numbers
e_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)<{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)
and/or

E_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)>{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K)
exist, they represent the
discrete eigenvalues of
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)
,
K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
i\in\{1,2\}.
For each fixed
K_{j}\in\mathbb{T},

j\in\{1,2\}\setminus\{i\},
the map

K_{i}\in\mathbb{T}\mapsto{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}((K_{1},K_{2}))-e_{n}((K_{1},K_{2});\gamma,\lambda,\mu)

is non-increasing in
(-\pi,0]
and non-decreasing in
[0,\pi]
.
Similarly, for every fixed
K_{j}\in\mathbb{T},

j\in\{1,2\}\setminus\{i\},

the map

K_{i}\in\mathbb{T}\mapsto E_{n}((K_{1},K_{2});\gamma,\lambda,\mu)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}((K_{1},K_{2}))

is non-increasing in
(-\pi,0]
and non-decreasing in
[0,\pi]
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
i=1.
Given
{\psi}\in L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
consider

(({H}_{0}(K)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)){\psi},{\psi})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\,\big(1-\cos q_{i}\big)|\psi(q)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}q,\hskip 11.74988ptK:=(K_{1},K_{2}).

Clearly, the map
K_{1}\in\mathbb{T}\mapsto(({H}_{0}(K)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)){\psi},{\psi})
is non-decreasing in
(-\pi,0]
and is
non-increasing in
[0,\pi].
Since
{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
is
independent of

by definition of
e_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)
the
map
K_{1}\in\mathbb{T}\mapsto e_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)
is
non-decreasing in
(-\pi,0]
and is non-increasing in
[0,\pi].

The case of
K_{i}\mapsto E_{n}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K)

is similar.

Theorem 2.2
.

By using Lemma

0\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(0)-e_{m}(0;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K)-e_{m}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu),

E_{m}(K;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K)\geq E_{m}(0;\gamma,\lambda,\mu)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(0)\geq 0.

is a discrete

it follows that, counting

2.6.
Reducing subspaces of the fiber Schrödinger
operators
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)

In this subsection we show that for any

\gamma,\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
the subspaces

L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and

L^{\mathrm{2,ea}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
introduced in
(

)–(

mutually orthogonal reducing subspaces of

.

Lemma 2.3
.

The followings statements hold true:

(i)

The subspaces

L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and

L^{\mathrm{2,ea}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
are mutually orthogonal and
L^{2,\rm e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})=L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\oplus L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\oplus L^{\mathrm{2,ea}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
;

(ii)

The subspaces

L^{2,\mathrm{ees}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
L^{2,\mathrm{oos}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and

L^{\mathrm{2,ea}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
are reducing for
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0)
.

Proof.

(i) Let

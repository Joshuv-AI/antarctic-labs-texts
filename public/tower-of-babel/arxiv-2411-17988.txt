On the integration of Manin pairs
David Li-Bland, Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. It is a remarkable fact that the integrability of a Poisson manifold to a symplectic groupoid depends only on the integrability of its cotangent Lie algebroid A A : The source-simply connected Lie groupoid G ⇉ M G\rightrightarrows M integrating A A automatically acquires a multiplicative symplectic 2-form. More generally, a similar result holds for the integration of Lie bialgebroids to Poisson groupoids, as well as in the ‘quasi’ settings of Dirac structures and quasi-Lie bialgebroids. In this article, we will place these results into a general context of Manin pairs ( 𝔼 , A ) (\mathbb{E},A) , thereby obtaining a simple geometric approach to these integration results. We also clarify the case where the groupoid G G integrating A A is not source-simply connected. Furthermore, we obtain a description of Hamiltonian spaces for Poisson groupoids and quasi-symplectic groupoids within this formalism.



1.
Introduction

A central theme in Poisson geometry is the notion of a
symplectic groupoid

[
9
,
17
,
36
]
, given by a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
together with a
multiplicative symplectic 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
.
The units of a symplectic groupoid acquire a unique Poisson structure
\pi_{M}
, with the property that the target-source map

(1)

(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})\colon G\to M\times M

is a Poisson map, using the Poisson structure
(\pi_{M},-\pi_{M})
on the pair groupoid
M\times M
.

Generalizing symplectic groupoids, one has
Poisson Lie groupoids

(G,\pi_{G})

[
37
]
, with infinitesimal objects given by Lie bialgebroids
(A,A^{*})
.
Mackenzie–Xu
[
27
, Theorem 4.1]
proved that the integrability of a Lie bialgebroid
(A,A^{*})
to a Poisson Lie groupoid depends only on the integrability of the Lie algebroid
A
. If it is integrable, then
its source-simply connected integration
G\rightrightarrows M
has a unique multiplicative Poisson structure integrating the Lie bialgebroid. As a special case, the integrability of a Poisson manifold depends only on the integrability of its cotangent Lie algebroid.
See
[
12
, Section 14.3]
for a detailed discussion.

These results generalize to ‘quasi’ settings: Bursztyn, Crainic, Weinstein, and Zhu
[
5
]
constructed integrations of (exact) Dirac manifolds to quasi-symplectic groupoids
[
39
]
, while Iglesias-Ponte, Laurent-Gengoux, and Xu

[
15
]
obtained the integration of
quasi-Lie bialgebroids

[
33
]
to quasi-Poisson groupoids. The special case where
M
is a point recovers the theory of quasi-Poisson Lie groups as integrations of quasi-Lie bialgebras, developed by Kosmann-Schwarzbach
[
18
,
19
]
.

In this note, we put these results into the framework of morphisms of Manin pairs. Recall that a
Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
over a manifold
M

[
24
]
is a Courant algebroid
\mathbb{E}\to M
together with an integrable Lagrangian subbundle
A\subseteq\mathbb{E}
. The classical Dirac structures on manifolds
[
10
,
11
]
correspond to the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
. Suppose the Lie algebroid
A
integrates to a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
(not necessarily source-simply connected). The Courant algebroid
\mathbb{T}G=TG\oplus T^{*}G
has the structure of a
\mathcal{CA}
-groupoid
[
21
,
28
]

\mathbb{T}G\rightrightarrows TM\oplus A^{*}.

Similarly, the pair groupoid is a
\mathcal{CA}
-groupoid,

\mathbb{E}\times\overline{\mathbb{E}}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}
.
The Courant bracket defines a representation of the jet algebroid
\widehat{A}=J^{1}(A)
on
\mathbb{E}
; we assume that it integrates
to a groupoid representation
\widehat{g}\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}
of the jet groupoid
\widehat{G}=J^{1}(G)
, with certain natural
properties. (See Definition
3.4
below.) Such an integration is automatic whenever
G
is source-simply connected.

Theorem A1.

Let
(\mathbb{E},A)
be a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair over
M
. Then there is a distinguished
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-equivariant
morphism of multiplicative Manin pairs

(2)

R\colon(\mathbb{T}G,TG)\dasharrow(\mathbb{E}\times\overline{\mathbb{E}},A\times A),

with base map
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})\colon G\to M\times M
.

The theorem will be continued below, by providing an explicit formula for
R
. We refer to (
2
) as an integration of the Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
since it generalizes the Poisson map (
1
).
The existence of such a morphism (for the source-simply connected case) was stated in the doctoral dissertation of the first author
[
20
, Section 6.2.1]
. There, it was deduced from its infinitesimal counterpart, given by a morphism of infinitesimally multiplicative Manin pairs

(3)

R_{0}\colon(\mathbb{T}A,TA)\dasharrow(\operatorname{flip}(T\mathbb{E}),\operatorname{flip}(TA));

here
\operatorname{flip}
indicates an interchange of horizontal and vertical structures.
In turn,
R_{0}
was obtained using super-geometric techniques.

The integration result for Lie bialgebroids
[
27
]
corresponds to the situation that one is given a Dirac structure
B\subset\mathbb{E}
complementary to
A
. The pre-image of
B\times B
under the morphism
R
is then a multiplicative Dirac structure complementary to
TG\subset\mathbb{T}G
, or equivalently the graph of a multiplicative Poisson structure on
G
. For quasi-Lie algebroids
[
15
]
, the
complement
B
need not be integrable, and one arrives at a quasi-Poisson structure. Similarly, if
\mathbb{E}
is an
exact
Courant algebroid, the result on integration of twisted Dirac manifolds
[
5
]
is recovered by choice of an isotropic splitting of the anchor map
\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\colon\mathbb{E}\to TM
. Indeed, for exact Courant algebroids the morphism
R
is described by a multiplicative 2-form. In particular, for a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi_{M})
with cotangent Lie algebroid
A=\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{M}^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{T}M
, the multiplicative morphism of Manin pairs
R
defines a multiplicative symplectic structure on
G
, in such a way that
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
is a Poisson map. Note that we obtain a mild generalization of these integration results, since
G
need not be source-simply connected.

Notice that (
2
)
will restrict, in particular, to a comorphism of Lie algebroids
TG\dasharrow A\times A
over
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
, which is described on the level of sections by a Lie algebra
morphism
\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(A)\to\mathfrak{X}(G)
. We will see that this is simply the map

(\tau,\sigma)\mapsto\sigma^{L}-\tau^{R},

associating to section of
A\times A
the corresponding left-invariant and right-invariant vector fields. Our formula for (
2
) will extend this relation to the the ambient Courant algebroids. We take its restriction to the units to be the graph of the map

\alpha\colon(\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{E})^{(0)}=\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{T}G^{(0)}=TM\oplus A^{*},

given by
\alpha=(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}},-\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}})
, where
\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}\colon\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}/A\cong A^{*}
is the projection. On the other hand,
we have a maps between the cores (see Section
2.1
)

\beta\colon{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)=A\oplus T^{*}M\to{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{E})=\mathbb{E},

given by
\beta=(\iota_{A},-\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}^{*})
.
Using this notation, we
have:

Theorem A2.

For
x\in\mathbb{T}G|_{g}
and
(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\in(\mathbb{E}\times\overline{\mathbb{E}})|_{(\mathsf{t}(g),\mathsf{s}(g))}
, we have

x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)
if and only if

(4)

\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime}),\ \ \ \ \ \beta([l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)])=\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}\zeta^{\prime},

for any choice of lift
\widehat{g}\in\widehat{G}
.
Here
l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)\in\mathbb{T}G|_{M}
denotes the left translate of
x
, and
[l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)]

its image in
{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)
.

We shall see that (
4
) is the unique multiplicative morphism of Manin pairs that is equivariant for the left
\widehat{G}
-action and whose restriction to units is given by
\alpha
.
One can also use right translation, leading to another version of (
4
).

Similar to the description of quasi-Poisson manifolds in terms of morphisms of Manin pairs
[
6
]
,
one might take the morphism
R
itself to be an intrinsic (splitting-free) definition of a quasi-Poisson groupoid (or quasi-symplectic groupoid, in the exact case). According to Theorem A, it is fully encoded in the
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
.
We define a
Hamiltonian
G
-space
for such a Manin pair to be a
G
-manifold
P
, with action along a

moment map

\Phi\colon P\to M
, together with a

\widehat{G}
-equivariant morphism of Manin pairs

L\colon(\mathbb{T}P,TP)\dasharrow(\mathbb{E},A)

having
\Phi
as its base map. This is simply the
\widehat{G}
-equivariant version of the definition in
[
16
,
21
]
. One recovers Xu’s notion of
Hamiltonian spaces for quasi-symplectic groupoids
[
38
]
, as well as (quasi-)Poisson actions of (quasi-)Poisson groupoids,
by using the following observation:

Theorem B.

Given a Hamiltonian
G
-space
P
for a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Dirac structure
(\mathbb{E},A)
, the morphism
L

is multiplicative in the sense that
for
x\in\mathbb{T}G,\ y\in\mathbb{T}P,(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\in\mathbb{E}\times\overline{\mathbb{E}}
,

\Big(x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta),\ y\sim_{L}\zeta,\ \mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y)\Big)\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ x\cdot y\sim\zeta^{\prime}.

Here the right hand side uses the
\mathbb{T}G\rightrightarrows TM\oplus A^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{T}P
along
\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}\colon\mathbb{T}P\to TM\oplus A^{*}
.

Notation and conventions.

Lie groupoids are indicated by a double arrow
G\rightrightarrows M
, with
G
the manifold of arrows and
M\subset G
the submanifold of units. We shall also use the notation
M=G^{(0)}
for the units. The source and target maps are denoted by
\mathsf{s},\mathsf{t}\colon G\to M
, the groupoid multiplication of composable arrows by
g\circ h
, and groupoid inversion by
g\mapsto g^{-1}
.
The pair groupoid of a manifold
M
is denoted
\operatorname{Pair}(M)=M\times M\rightrightarrows M
. Lie algebroids are denoted by an arrow
A\Rightarrow M
; the anchor is denoted by
\mathsf{a}\colon A\to TM
.
The Lie functor takes a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
to a Lie algebroid

A=\operatorname{Lie}(G)\Rightarrow M.

As a vector bundle, it is the normal bundle
A=\nu(G,M)
of the units, with
anchor induced by the difference
\mathsf{s}_{TG}-\mathsf{t}_{TG}\colon TG\to TM
of the source and target maps for the tangent groupoid
TG\rightrightarrows TM
.

2.
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids

We recall some background material on
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids, referring to
[
14
,
25
]
for more details.

2.1.
Basic definitions

A
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
is a Lie groupoid
J\rightrightarrows B
, where the manifold of arrows
J
has the structure of a vector bundle

J\to G
, in such a way that the graph of the groupoid multiplication is a sub-vector bundle
\operatorname{gr}(\operatorname{Mult}_{J})\subset J\times J\times J
. (Equivalently, the scalar multiplication of
J
is by groupoid morphisms.) This implies that base manifold is itself a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
,
and that the units are a sub-vector bundle
B\to M
.
The situation is depicted as

(5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

The
core
of the
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid is the quotient bundle

C={\operatorname{core}}(J)=J|_{M}/B.

Let
\mathsf{s}_{J},\mathsf{t}_{J}\colon J\to B
denote the source and target maps. Their difference vanishes on
B\subset J|_{M}
, and so descends to a bundle map

(6)

\varrho\colon{\operatorname{core}}(J)\to B,\ \ [x]\mapsto\mathsf{s}_{J}(x)-\mathsf{t}_{J}(x)

called the
core-anchor

[
14
]
. We shall often identify
{\operatorname{core}}(J)\cong\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})|_{M}
; in terms of this identification,
J|_{M}\cong C\oplus B
.

The dual bundle of any
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
J\rightrightarrows B
with
{\operatorname{core}}(J)=C

is a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid

J^{*}\rightrightarrows C^{*}
with the unique groupoid multiplication such that the pairing gives a groupoid morphism
J^{*}\oplus J\to\mathbb{R}
.
Here
{\operatorname{core}}(J^{*})=B^{*}
, and the core-anchor of
J^{*}
is the dual
\varrho^{*}\colon B^{*}\to C^{*}
of (
6
).

An
\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid

is a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid with a vertical Lie algebroid structure
J\Rightarrow G
,
which is compatible with the groupoid structure in the sense that the graph of the groupoid multiplication

\operatorname{gr}(\operatorname{Mult}_{J})\subset J\times J\times J

is a Lie subalgebroid. The units of
J
are a Lie subalgebroid
B\Rightarrow M
, and the core is a Lie algebroid
C\Rightarrow M
, with bracket on sections defined by the identification with left-invariant sections of
J
. (See below.)
The core-anchor of an
\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid is a Lie algebroid morphism.

We shall mainly deal with the following examples of
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids:

(a)

The pair groupoid
\operatorname{Pair}(V)\rightrightarrows V
of a
vector bundle
V\to M
is a

\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid; here
{\operatorname{core}}(\operatorname{Pair}(V))=V
, with core-anchor the identity map.
The core projection
\operatorname{Pair}(V)|_{M}=V\oplus V\to V
is given by
(v^{\prime},v)\mapsto[(v^{\prime},v)]=v-v^{\prime}
.
If
A\Rightarrow M
is a Lie algebroid, then
\operatorname{Pair}(A)\rightrightarrows A
is an
\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid.

(b)

The tangent bundle
TG\rightrightarrows TM
of a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
is an
\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid, with

{\operatorname{core}}(TG)=A
the associated Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
, and core-anchor the usual Lie algebroid anchor
\mathsf{a}_{A}
.
The dual bundle is the
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
T^{*}G\rightrightarrows A^{*}
, with
{\operatorname{core}}(T^{*}G)=T^{*}M
, and core-anchor
\mathsf{a}_{A}^{*}
.

2.2.
Left and right actions

The left multiplication of the groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
on itself,
h\mapsto l_{g}(h)=g\circ h
, lifts to
an action
x\mapsto l_{g}(x)=0_{g}\circ x
on
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})
. This gives a bundle map
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})\to C={\operatorname{core}}(J)
, with base map
\mathsf{s}\colon G\to M
, taking
x\in\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})|_{g}
to
[l_{g^{-1}}x]\in C
.
Similarly, using right translations we obtain a bundle map
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{s}|_{J})\to C
with base map
\mathsf{t}_{J}
.
Both of these maps are fiberwise isomorphisms, resulting in bundle isomorphisms

(7)

\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})\cong\mathsf{s}^{*}C,\ \ \ \operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{s}_{J})\cong\mathsf{t}^{*}C.

For sections
\sigma\in\Gamma(C)
, we denote by

\sigma^{L}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J}))\subset\Gamma(J),\ \ \sigma^{R}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{s}_{J}))\subset\Gamma(J)

the sections defined by these identifications. Similarly, given
g\in G
and elements
\zeta\in C_{\mathsf{s}(g)}
and

\zeta^{\prime}\in C_{\mathsf{t}(g)}
, denote by

\zeta^{L}_{g}\in\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})_{g},\ \ \ \ (\zeta^{\prime})^{R}_{g}\in\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{s}_{J})_{g}

the elements such that
[l_{g^{-1}}(\zeta^{L}_{g})]=\zeta,\ \ [r_{g^{-1}}((\zeta^{\prime})^{R}_{g})]=\zeta^{\prime}
.
Then

(8)

(\zeta^{R}_{g})\circ 0_{h}=\zeta^{R}_{gh},\ \ \ 0_{g}\circ\zeta^{L}_{h}=\zeta^{L}_{gh},\ \ \ \zeta^{L}_{g}\circ(-\zeta)^{R}_{h}=0_{gh}

under the groupoid multiplication for
J
. Furthermore,
\mathsf{s}_{J}(\zeta_{g}^{L})=\varrho(\zeta),\ \mathsf{t}_{J}((\zeta^{\prime})^{R}_{g})=-\varrho(\zeta^{\prime})
.

If
J
is an
\mathcal{LA}
-groupoid, one obtains a Lie algebroid structure on
C
by the identification with left-invariant sections. This gives the bracket relations

(9)

[\sigma_{1}^{L},\sigma_{2}^{L}]=[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]^{L},\ \ [\sigma_{1}^{R},\sigma_{2}^{R}]=-[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]^{R},\ \ [\sigma_{1}^{L},\sigma_{2}^{R}]=0.

For
J=TG
this is the given Lie algebroid structure on

A=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
.

2.3.
The fat groupoid

Given a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
J
as above, there is an exact sequence of groupoids over
M

1\to K\to\widehat{G}\to G\to 1.

Here the
fat groupoid

[
14
]

\widehat{G}\rightrightarrows M
consists of elements
g\in G
together
with linear subspaces

\widehat{g}\subset J|_{g}
that are complements to both
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})|_{g}
and
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{s}_{J})|_{g}
. It is an open subset of the Grassmannian bundle of
\dim M
-dimensional subspaces, and hence is a manifold.
The source and target of
\widehat{g}

are the source and target of
g
, and for composable elements,

\widehat{g}\circ\widehat{h}=\{v\circ w|\ v\in\widehat{g},\ w\in\widehat{h}\mbox{ are composable in $J$}\}.

The subgroupoid
K
consists of elements
\widehat{g}\in\widehat{G}
such that
g=m
is a unit;
it is a family of Lie groups
K_{m},\ m\in M
. We may regard
K
as the fat groupoid of the restriction
J|_{M}\cong C\oplus B\rightrightarrows B
.
The groupoid
K\rightrightarrows M
, is entirely described in terms of the core-anchor:

(10)

K_{m}=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}(B_{m},C_{m})|\ \operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f\mbox{ is invertible}\},

with product
f_{1}\circ f_{2}=f_{1}+f_{2}\circ(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f_{1})
. See Appendix
B
for a detailed discussion.

Example 2.1
.

(a)

The fat groupoid of
\operatorname{Pair}(V)\rightrightarrows V
is
the gauge groupoid of
V
. The subgroupoid
K
is the general linear bundle,
K=\bigsqcup_{m\in M}\operatorname{GL}(V_{m})
.

(b)

For
TG\rightrightarrows TM
, the fat groupoid is the jet groupoid
J^{1}(G)\rightrightarrows M
.

(c)

The fat groupoid for
J
is canonically isomorphic to that of
J^{*}
, by the map taking a subspace of
J|_{g}
to its annihilator in
J^{*}|_{g}
.

The fat groupoid comes with natural actions

(11)

\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}\circlearrowright J,\ \ \ \ \widehat{G}\circlearrowright J|_{M},\ \ \ \ \widehat{G}\circlearrowright J^{(0)},\ \ \ \ \widehat{G}\circlearrowright{\operatorname{core}}(J).

The first of these actions is given by
(\widehat{g},\widehat{h})\cdot x=l_{\widehat{g}}(r_{\widehat{h}^{-1}}(x))
.
Here

l_{\widehat{g}}(x)=v\circ x,

with the unique element
v\in\widehat{g}\subset J_{g}
that is composable with

x\in J
. Right translations are defined similarly. This
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-action restricts to a diagonal
\widehat{G}
-action on
J|_{M}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Ad}
. For
x\in J|_{m}
and
\mathsf{s}(\widehat{g})=m
we have

\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}\cdot x=l_{\widehat{g}}r_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)\in J_{g\cdot m}.

The action preserves the units
J^{(0)}=B
,
and so descends to an action, still denoted
\operatorname{Ad}
, on
C={\operatorname{core}}(J)
.
The various
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid structure maps behave naturally for these actions:

\mathsf{t}_{J}(l_{\widehat{g}}(x))=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}\mathsf{t}_{J}(x),\ \ \mathsf{t}_{J}(r_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x))=\mathsf{t}_{J}(x),

\mathsf{s}_{J}(l_{\widehat{g}}(x))=\mathsf{s}_{J}(x),\ \ \mathsf{s}_{J}(r_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x))=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}\mathsf{s}_{J}(x),

\varrho(\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}x)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}\varrho(x).

It is also clear that the left-action of
\widehat{G}
on
J
extends the left-action of
G
on
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})
, and similarly for the right action. Hence,

l_{\widehat{g}}(\zeta^{L}_{h})=\zeta^{L}_{gh},\ \ \ \ r_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(\zeta^{L}_{h^{\prime}})=(\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}(\zeta))^{L}_{h^{\prime}g^{-1}}

and similarly for
(\zeta^{\prime})^{R}_{g}
.

2.4.
A canonical comorphism

We now describe a multiplicative comorphism for
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids, which features many of the main ingredients of the integration problem for Manin pairs. (See Cattaneo-Dherin-Weinstein
[
8
]
for background material on comorphisms of vector bundles, Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids. In our discussion, the ‘comorphism’ refers to the vertical structure.)
By a
multiplicative
\mathcal{VB}
-comorphism

S\colon J_{1}\dasharrow J_{2}
between two
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids,
we mean a vector bundle comorphism along some base map
\Phi\colon G_{1}\to G_{2}
(i.e, given by a bundle map
\Phi^{*}J_{2}\to J_{1}
) whose graph
S\subset J_{2}\times J_{1}
is a subgroupoid for the horizontal structure.
From the definitions, one readily checks that this is equivalent to the dual map
S^{*}\colon J_{1}^{*}\to J_{2}^{*}
being a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid morphism (i.e., a vector bundle map that is also a groupoid morphism).

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
J\rightrightarrows B
, with
{\operatorname{core}}(J)=C
, there is a canonical
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-equivariant multiplicative

3.
Integration of Manin pairs

Our conventions for Courant algebroids and Dirac structures follow

[
6
,
21
,
22
]
. In particular, we work with the non-skew symmetric version of the Courant bracket

[\![\cdot,\cdot]\!]
on
\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
, also known as the Dorfman bracket. The
standard Courant algebroid
over
M
is denoted
\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
,
with metric given by the pairing of vectors and covectors, with anchor the projection to the first summand,
and with bracket

(15)

[\![X_{1}+\mu_{1},X_{2}+\mu_{2}]\!]=[X_{1},X_{2}]+\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\mu_{2}-\iota_{X_{2}}{\mbox{d}}\mu_{1}

for vector fields
X_{1},X_{2}
and one-forms
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
.

3.1.
Manin pairs

In the work of Courant and Weinstein
[
10
,
11
]
, Dirac structures on manifolds
M
were defined as (wide) Lagrangian subbundles of
\mathbb{T}M
whose space of sections is closed under the Courant bracket.
Liu-Weinstein-Xu
[
24
]
generalized
\mathbb{T}M
to the notion of
Courant algebroid
;
the pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
of a Courant algebroid
\mathbb{E}\to M
together with a Dirac structure
A\subset\mathbb{E}

is called a
Manin pair
over
M
.
For
M=\operatorname{pt}
, one recovers the classical notion of a Manin pair of Lie algebras, as introduced by Drinfeld.

Definition 3.1
.

[
2
,
6
,
34
]

A
Courant morphism

R\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{E}_{2}
, with base map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
, is a
Lagrangian subbundle
R\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}\times\overline{\mathbb{E}_{1}}
along
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)\subset M_{2}\times M_{1}
, with image under the anchor tangent to

\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)
, and such that the space of sections of
\mathbb{E}_{2}\times\overline{\mathbb{E}_{1}}
that restrict to sections of
R
is closed under the Courant bracket.
A (strong)
Dirac morphism

[
1
,
29
]
or
morphism of Manin pairs

[
6
]

R\colon(\mathbb{E}_{1},A_{1})\dasharrow(\mathbb{E}_{2},A_{2}),

with base map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
, is a Courant morphism
R\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{E}_{2}
satisfying

(16)

R(A_{1})\subseteq A_{2},\ \ R^{-1}(0)\cap A_{1}=0.

Here
R(A_{1})
is the image of
A_{1}
under the relation, and
R^{-1}(0)=\operatorname{ker}(R)
the pre-image of the zero subbundle
0\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
. The condition (
16
) means that for all
m\in M_{1}
, every element
y_{2}\in A_{2}|_{\Phi(m)}
is
R
-related to a
unique
element
y_{1}\in A_{1}|_{m}
. A morphism of Manin pairs gives, in particular, a comorphism of Lie algebroids

A_{1}\dasharrow A_{2}

with base map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
, and hence a Lie algebra morphism
\Gamma(A_{2})\to\Gamma(A_{1})
. Unlike Courant morphisms, morphisms of Manin pairs can always be composed. Another basic fact (see e.g.,
[
1
]
)
is that whenever
B_{2}\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
is a
Lagrangian subbundle (resp., Dirac structure) complementary to
A_{2}
, then its pre-image
B_{1}=R^{-1}(B_{2})
is a
Lagrangian subbundle (resp., Dirac structure) complementary to
A_{1}
.

Example 3.2
.

A map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
between Poisson manifolds
(M_{i},\pi_{i})
is Poisson if and only if
it defines a morphism of Manin pairs

\mathbb{T}\Phi\colon(\mathbb{T}M_{1},\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{1}^{\sharp}))\dasharrow(\mathbb{T}M_{2},\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{2}^{\sharp})),

where
the relation
\mathbb{T}\Phi
is defined as the sum of the (graphs of) tangent and cotangent maps.

3.3.
Equivariant Manin pairs

If
A\Rightarrow M
is a Lie algebroid with an integration to a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
, there is a canonical action of the jet groupoid

J^{1}(G)\rightrightarrows M
on
A
, coming from the interpretation of
J^{1}(G)
as the fat groupoid
\widehat{G}
of
TG\rightrightarrows TM
and its
natural action on
{\operatorname{core}}(TG)\cong A
. (See (
11
).) Given a Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
, we are interested in an extension of this
J^{1}(G)
-action to the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{E}
.
This extension should satisfy certain natural properties.

First, the
J^{1}(G)
-representation on
\mathbb{E}
should differentiate to the representation of the jet algebroid
\widehat{A}=J^{1}(A)
on
\mathbb{E}
, given on the level of sections by by the Courant bracket:

\nabla_{j^{1}(\xi)}\zeta=[\![\xi,\zeta]\!],\ \ \ \xi\in\Gamma(A),\ \zeta\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E}).

As explained in Appendix
B
, the action of
\mathfrak{k}\subset J^{1}(A)
integrates
canonically
to an action of

K\rightrightarrows M
– in fact, the groupoid
K
and its action on
\mathbb{E}
do not depend on integrability of
A
. Finally, the
J^{1}(G)
-action on
\mathbb{E}
should preserve the Courant algebroid structure, in the sense that the anchor and metric are preserved, and the bracket is preserved under the action of local bisections of
G
(i.e., the holonomic local bisections of
J^{1}(G)
).

Definition 3.4
.

A
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair
is a Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
, together with a Lie groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
integrating
A
and an extension of the
\widehat{G}=J^{1}(G)
-action on
A
to the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{E}
, such that:

•

The
\widehat{G}
-action differentiates to the given
\widehat{A}
-action.

•

The
\widehat{G}
-action restricts to the given
K
-action.

•

The metric on
\mathbb{E}
is
\widehat{G}
-invariant, the anchor
\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}
is
\widehat{G}
-equivariant, and the bracket on
\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
is preserved under the action of (local) bisections of
G
.

If
G
is source-simply connected, these conditions are automatic:

Proposition 3.5
.

If
(\mathbb{E},A)
is a Manin pair, and
G\rightrightarrows M
a source-simply connected integration of
A
, then
there is a unique
\widehat{G}
-action on
\mathbb{E}
making it into a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair.

This result is not obvious, since the jet prolongation of a source-simply connected Lie groupoid is not even source connected, in general. (We thank R. Fernandes for alerting us to this fact, which may be illustrated by

G=\operatorname{Pair}(M)
for a simply connected manifold
M
.) We will give a proof in Appendix
B
(see Proposition
B.11
).
If
G
is only source connected, then its lift to a
\widehat{G}
-action as in Definition
3.4
may
not exist, but is unique if it does.

.

If

\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair

(\mathbb{E},A)
is a Manin pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
, with
G
acting by
Lie algebra automorphisms of
\mathfrak{d}
, preserving the metric, extending the adjoint action on
\mathfrak{g}
, and
differentiating to the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{d}

[
22
,
30
]
.)

Example 3.7
.

To illustrate the significance of
\widehat{G}
-equivariance, consider the Manin pair
(\mathbb{T}M,T^{*}M)
. The Lie algebroid structure of
A=T^{*}M
has zero bracket; hence its source-simply connected integration is the vector bundle
T^{*}M
itself, with groupoid structure given by fiberwise addition. More general source-connected integrations are of the form

G=T^{*}M/\Lambda,

where
\Lambda
is a subgroupoid with discrete source fibers. We claim that the action of
\widehat{A}=J^{1}(A)
on
\mathbb{T}M
integrates to an action of
\widehat{G}=J^{1}(G)
if and only if
\Lambda
is a
Lagrangian
submanifold
of
T^{*}M
.
Note that this is also the condition for the symplectic form on
T^{*}M

to descend to a symplectic structure
\omega
on the quotient
G=T^{*}M/\Lambda
.
To prove the claim, consider the infinitesimal action of
\nu\in\Gamma(A)=\Gamma(T^{*}M)
on
\mathbb{T}M
, given by Courant bracket

[\![\nu,X+\mu]\!]=-\iota_{X}{\mbox{d}}\nu.

It integrates to the action of the additive
group

\Gamma(T^{*}M)

\nu\cdot(X,\mu)\mapsto(X,\mu-\iota_{X}{\mbox{d}}\nu).

This
\Gamma(T^{*}M)

\Gamma(G)
-action (which, in turn, determines a
\widehat{G}
-action) if and only if
the local sections of
\Lambda

The formula shows that the action of a local section
\nu

trivial if and only if
\nu

closed

\Lambda

Lemma 3.11
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.10
, we have

\beta([l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)])=\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}\zeta^{\prime}\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ \beta([r_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)])=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}.

Furthermore, if this condition holds,

\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta).

Proof.

The first equivalence follows from the
\widehat{G}
-equivariance of
\beta
. Suppose this condition holds, and that

\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime})
. To compute
\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x))
,
write
l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)
in terms of the decomposition

\mathbb{T}G|_{M}=(\mathbb{T}G)^{(0)}\oplus\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G})|_{M}
,

l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x))+(l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)-\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x))).

On
(\mathbb{T}G)^{(0)}
, the map
\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}
acts as the identity, while on
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G})|_{M}\cong{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)

it acts as the core-anchor.
It follows that

\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x))=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x))+\varrho_{\mathbb{T}G}([l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(x)]).

Using
\varrho_{\mathbb{T}G}=\alpha\circ\beta
(see Lemma
3.8
)
and the
\widehat{G}
-equivariance of
\alpha
,
this becomes

\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}\alpha(\zeta^{\prime})+\alpha(\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(\zeta^{\prime}))=\alpha(\zeta).

The other direction is proved similarly.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.10
.

The graph of a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid morphism is a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid in its own right, hence we may consider its units and core.
We start out by
defining

R^{(0)}=\operatorname{gr}(\alpha),\ \ \ {\operatorname{core}}(R)=\operatorname{gr}(\beta).

This determines
R|_{M}=R^{(0)}\oplus{\operatorname{core}}(R)
.

1)
R|_{M}
is a Lagrangian subbundle.
Observe that
that

\operatorname{rank}(R|_{M})=\operatorname{rank}({\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G))+\operatorname{rank}({\operatorname{core}}(\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})))=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}(\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})\times\overline{\mathbb{T}G}).

To check that
R|_{M}
is isotropic, recall that
(\mathbb{T}G)^{(0)},\ \operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})^{(0)}
are Lagrangian, hence

\operatorname{gr}(\alpha)
is isotropic. On the other hand, for
y=(v,\mu)\in{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)
(with
v\in A,\ \mu\in T^{*}M)
)
we have

\langle y,y\rangle=2\langle\mu,\mathsf{a}(v)\rangle=\langle\iota_{A}(v)+\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}^{*}(\mu),\iota_{A}(v)+\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}^{*}(\mu)\rangle=\langle\beta(y),\beta(y)\rangle.

Hence

\operatorname{gr}(\beta)
is isotropic. Finally, elements of
\operatorname{gr}(\alpha)
and
\operatorname{gr}(\beta)
are orthogonal, since

\beta
is dual to
\alpha
. (See Remark
3.9
.)

2)
R|_{M}
extends to a
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-invariant Lagrangian subbundle
R\subset\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})\times\overline{\mathbb{T}G}
.

In Proposition
B.9
of Appendix
B
, we verify that the sum
\operatorname{gr}(\alpha)\oplus\operatorname{gr}(\beta)
is invariant under the
K\times K
-action. Hence,
R=(\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G})\cdot R|_{M}
is a well-defined subbundle.
Since the
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-actions on both
\mathbb{T}G
and
\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
are metric preserving, it follows that
R
is a
Lagrangian subbundle.

3)
R
is given by (
20
).

Elements
x\in\mathbb{T}G|_{M}
decompose uniquely as
x=y+z
where
y=\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)\in(\mathbb{T}G)^{(0)}
is a unit and

z=x-y=[x]\in\operatorname{ker}\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}|_{M}={\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)
. Similarly, elements of
\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})|_{M}
may be written in terms of
the decomposition to unit and core,
(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)=(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})+(0,\zeta-\zeta^{\prime})
.
We have
x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)
if and only if
y=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime})
and
\beta(z)=\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}
.
These are exactly the conditions (
20
), taking
\widehat{g}
to be trivial. To check
(
20
) in general, it suffices to check that these conditions are invariant under the action of
the first factor of

\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
. From the equivariance properties of the maps
\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}
and
\alpha
, it follows that, for
x\in\mathbb{T}G|_{g}
and
\widehat{h}\in\widehat{G}
with
\mathsf{s}(h)=\mathsf{t}(g)
,

\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(l_{\widehat{h}}(x))=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{h}}\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{h}}(\alpha(\zeta^{\prime}))=\alpha(\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{h}}(\zeta^{\prime})).

This shows that

x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\ \Rightarrow\ l_{\widehat{h}}(x)\sim_{R}(\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{h}}(\zeta^{\prime}),\zeta),

as required.

4)
R
is a
\mathcal{VB}
-subgroupoid for the horizontal structure.

Note

l_{\widehat{g}_{1}^{-1}}r_{\widehat{g}_{2}^{-1}}(x_{1}\circ x_{2})=(l_{\widehat{g}_{1}^{-1}}x_{1})\circ(r_{\widehat{g}_{2}^{-1}}x_{2})

for composable
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{T}G
, and similarly for the pair groupoid.
Hence, using the
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-equivariance, it suffices to show that
R|_{M}
is a subgroupoid.
Suppose
x_{i}\sim_{R}(\zeta_{i}^{\prime},\zeta_{i})
for
i=1,2
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{T}G|_{M}
are composable
(i.e., have the same base point), and
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{2}^{\prime}
. We want to show
x_{1}\circ x_{2}\sim_{R}(\zeta_{1}^{\prime},\zeta_{2})
.
We have

\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x_{1}\circ x_{2})=\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x_{1})=\alpha(\zeta_{1}^{\prime}),

which verifies the first condition in (
20
). On the other hand,
[x_{1}\circ x_{2}]=[x_{1}]+[x_{2}]
, and therefore

\beta([x_{1}\circ x_{2}])=\beta([x_{1}])+\beta([x_{2}])=(\zeta_{1}^{\prime}-\zeta_{1})+(\zeta_{2}^{\prime}-\zeta_{2})=\zeta_{1}^{\prime}-\zeta_{2},

verifying the second condition.

5)
R
is an ‘almost’ morphism of Manin pairs.
We next show

R(TG)\subset A\times A,\ \ TG\cap R^{-1}(0)=0.

Suppose
v\in TG|_{g}\subset\mathbb{T}G|_{g}
satisfies
v\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\in\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
.
The condition
\mathsf{t}_{TG}(v)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime})
shows that
\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}(\zeta^{\prime})=0
, thus

\zeta^{\prime}\in A
. Similarly,
\mathsf{s}_{TG}(v)=\alpha(\zeta)
shows
\zeta\in A
. Suppose
v\sim_{R}(0,0)
. The second equation in (
20
) shows that
\beta[(l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(v)]=0
.
Since the restriction of
\beta
to
{\operatorname{core}}(TG)=A
is just the inclusion
\iota_{A}
, this shows
that
l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(v)
is a unit:

l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(v)\in TM\subset TG|_{M}.

But
\mathsf{s}_{TG}(l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(v))=\mathsf{s}_{TG}(v)=\alpha(\zeta)=0
. We conclude
l_{\widehat{g}^{-1}}(v)=0
, and hence
v=0
.

6)
Uniqueness.

By Lemma
3.3
(which does not actually involve the bracket, only the metric), a Lagrangian
\mathcal{VB}
-subgroupoid of
\mathbb{E}\oplus\overline{\mathbb{E}}\oplus\overline{\mathbb{T}G}|_{M}\rightrightarrows\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})^{(0)}\oplus\mathbb{T}G^{(0)}
,
use units are given by the graph of
\alpha
, is necessarily the sum of the graph of

\alpha
and the graph of its
dual map
\beta
(see Remark
3.9
). By equivariance, this determines
R
globally.

7)
The image of
R
under the anchor is tangent to the graph of
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
.

Suppose
x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)
. Write
x=v+\mu
with
v\in TG,\ \mu\in T^{*}G
. Then

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=v
.
From the equations
\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime}),\ \mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta)
, we see in particular that
\mathsf{t}_{TG}(v)=\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta^{\prime}),\ \mathsf{s}_{TG}(v)=\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta)
.
This shows that the image of
R
under the anchor is tangent to
\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
.

8)
R
is a Dirac structure with support on
\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
.
This involves some Courant bracket calculations,
which are a little technical and are therefore relegated to Appendix
A
.
∎

3.5.
Infinitesimally multiplicative version

Following
[
20
, Section 6.2.1]
we may formulate an ‘infinitesimally multiplicative version’ of Theorem
3.10

for Manin pairs
(\mathbb{E},A)
, not requiring any integration of the Lie algebroid
A
.

The pair
(\mathbb{T}A,TA)
is an infinitesimally multiplicative Manin pair in the sense of
[
20
]
; in particular,
\mathbb{T}A
is a Courant algebroid vertically
(as a bundle over
A
) and a Lie algebroid horizontally (as a bundle over
(\mathbb{T}A)^{(0)}=TM\oplus A^{*}
). Similarly,

(\operatorname{flip}(T\mathbb{E}),\operatorname{flip}(TA))
is an infinitesimally multiplicative Manin pair; here
\operatorname{flip}
indicates that we interchanged the role of horizontal and vertical structures. Thus,
\operatorname{flip}(T\mathbb{E})
is vertically a Courant algebroid over
\mathbb{E}
, and horizontally a Lie algebroid over
TM\oplus A^{*}
.

For any double vector bundle
D
, the fat bundles corresponding to the two side bundles act on
D
via
horizontal translations. (See, e.g.,
[
31
]
.) We hence obtain translation actions of the vector bundle

\widehat{A}=J^{1}(A)
on
TA
and on
T^{*}A
, hence also on
\mathbb{T}A
. In the case of
\operatorname{flip}(T\mathbb{E})
, the horizontal fat bundle is the
Atiyah algebroid
\operatorname{At}(\mathbb{E})
. The Lie algebroid representation of
\widehat{A}=J^{1}(A)
on
\mathbb{E}
is a
morphism of Lie algebroids
J^{1}(A)\to\operatorname{At}(\mathbb{E})
, resulting in a translation action of the vector bundle
\widehat{A}

on
\operatorname{flip}(T\mathbb{E})
.

The assertion is, then, that the map
\alpha\colon\mathbb{E}\to TM\oplus A^{*}
extends uniquely to a
\widehat{A}
-equivariant

\mathcal{CA}
–morphism

4.
Special cases and applications

4.1.
The case
M=\operatorname{pt}

If the manifold
M
of units is a point
M=\operatorname{pt}
, then the groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
is just a group, and a

\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
is a
G
-equivariant Manin pair

(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})

consisting of a metrized Lie algebra and Lagrangian Lie subalgebra.
We may describe the
morphism
R\colon(\mathbb{T}G,TG)\dasharrow(\operatorname{Pair}(\mathfrak{d}),\operatorname{Pair}(\mathfrak{g}))
using left-trivialization

\mathbb{T}G\cong G\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*})\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

We have
{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)=\mathfrak{g}
, with
\alpha=-\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\colon\mathfrak{d}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
being minus the projection and

\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{d}
the inclusion.
Furthermore,

\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(g,\xi,\tau)=\tau,\ \ [r_{g^{-1}}(g,\xi,\tau)]=\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\xi
. We hence arrive at

(g,\xi,\tau)\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ \tau=-\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\zeta,\ \ \xi=\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\zeta^{\prime}.

On the level of sections,
R\subset\operatorname{Pair}(\mathfrak{d})\times\overline{\mathbb{T}G}

It is spanned by sections of the form

(0,\sigma,\sigma^{L}),\ \ (\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\zeta,\zeta,-\langle\theta^{L},\zeta\rangle)

with
\sigma\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The morphism of Manin pairs
R_{0}\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathfrak{g},T\mathfrak{g})\dasharrow(\operatorname{flip}(T\mathfrak{d}),\operatorname{flip}(T\mathfrak{g}))
has the following description.
Relative to the vertical structure,
\operatorname{flip}(T\mathfrak{d})
is a Courant algebroid over
\operatorname{pt}
, and so is just a metrized Lie algebra. One finds that as a Lie algebra, it is a semi-direct product

\operatorname{flip}(T\mathfrak{d})=\mathfrak{d}\rtimes\mathfrak{d}
; we write its elements as
pairs
(\zeta_{v},\zeta_{t})
(where the subscripts are meant to suggest
vertical
and
tangent
).
Then
R_{0}\subset\operatorname{flip}(T\mathfrak{d})\times\mathbb{T}\mathfrak{g}
is spanned by sections of the form

((\xi_{v},0),\xi),\ \ (((\operatorname{ad}_{a}\zeta)_{v},\zeta_{t}),{\mbox{d}}\langle\zeta,a\rangle).

for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g},\ \zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
, where
a
is the parameter on the base manifold of
\mathbb{T}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
.

4.2.
Lie group actions

Let
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
be a Manin pair, and
M\times\mathfrak{d}\to TM
a Lie algebra action with coisotropic stabilizers. As shown in
[
21
]
, this data defines an
action Courant algebroid

\mathbb{E}=M\times\mathfrak{d}

over
M
, with anchor given by the action
M\times\mathfrak{d}\to TM
, and with Courant bracket extending the Lie bracket on constant sections. The subbundle
A=M\times\mathfrak{g}
is a Dirac structure, and so defines a Manin pair

(\mathbb{E},A)=(M\times\mathfrak{d},M\times\mathfrak{g})

over
M
. Suppose
G
is a Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{g}
,
with an action on
M
as well as a linear action on
\mathfrak{d}
, in such a way that

(i)

(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a
G
-equivariant Manin pair of Lie algebras,

(ii)

the
G
-action on
M
integrates the
\mathfrak{g}
-action,

(iii)

the action map
M\times\mathfrak{d}\to TM
is
G
-equivariant.

The action groupoid

\mathcal{G}=G\times M\rightrightarrows M

integrates
A
. (We are using the letter
\mathcal{G}
since

G
is already taken.)

Observe that a
\mathcal{G}
-action on a manifold
Q
, along a map
\Psi\colon Q\to M
,
is the same as a
G
-action on
Q
for which the map
\Psi
is
G
-equivariant. In turn, a
\mathcal{G}
-action is equivalent to an action of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}=J^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. In one direction, this follows because the action of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is uniquely determined by the action of local bisections of
\mathcal{G}
. In the other direction, it follows because the map
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}=J^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathcal{G}
has a canonical splitting. (The splitting takes
(g,m)\in\mathcal{G}
to the subspace
\{0\}\times T_{m}M\subset T_{(g,m)}G
. Both
source and target restrict to isomorphisms on this subspace, which therefore is an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
.)

The linear
G
-action on
\mathbb{E}
by

g\cdot(m,\zeta)=(g\cdot m,\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(\zeta))

preserves
A
. By the previous paragraph, it follows that
(\mathbb{E},A)
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
-equivariant Manin pair. Hence, we obtain a morphism
R
as in (
2
). To describe it explicitly,
use left trivialization to identify
\mathbb{T}G=G\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*})
; thus

\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G}=G\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*})\times\mathbb{T}M\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times TM.

Denote its elements by
(g,\xi,\tau,v+\mu)
with
g\in G,\xi\in\mathfrak{g},\tau\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},v\in T_{m}M,\mu\in T^{*}_{m}M
, and
let
\Phi\colon T^{*}M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the moment map for the cotangent lift, i.e.
\langle\Phi(\mu),\xi\rangle=\langle\mu,\xi_{M}(m)\rangle
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Using left trivialization as above, the morphism of Manin pairs

R\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G},T\mathcal{G})\dasharrow(\operatorname{Pair}(M\times\mathfrak{d}),\operatorname{Pair}(M\times\mathfrak{g}))

is given by the condition that

(g,\xi,\tau,v+\mu)\sim_{R}(m^{\prime},m,\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)

if and only if
m^{\prime}=\mathsf{t}(g),\ m=\mathsf{s}(g)
, and furthermore

\mathsf{a}(\zeta)=v,\ \ \operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\zeta=\tau+\Phi(\mu),\ \ \xi+\mathsf{a}^{*}(\mu)=\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\zeta^{\prime}.

Proof.

The
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G}
is the direct sum of the action groupoid
T\mathcal{G}=TG\times TM\rightrightarrows TM
and its dual

T^{*}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. In left trivialization, one obtains the formulas

\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G}}(g,\xi,\tau,v+\mu)=(v,\ \tau+\Phi(\mu)),\ \ \mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G}}(g,\xi,\tau,v+\mu)=(g_{*}(v-\xi_{M}(m)),\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\tau)

with the multiplication of composable elements
(g_{i},\xi_{i},\tau_{i},v_{i}+\mu_{i}),\ i=1,2
given by

\Big(g_{1}\circ g_{2},\ \operatorname{Ad}_{g_{2}^{-1}}\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\ \tau_{2}-\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{2}^{-1}}\Phi(\mu_{1}),\ v_{2}+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\Big).

The result is now a simple computation, using (
20
).
∎

4.3.
Quasi-symplectic groupoids

We next explain how the integration result for twisted Dirac manifolds, due to Bursztyn-Crainic-Weinstein-Zhu

[
5
]
, may be obtained from Theorem
3.10
. These results correspond to Manin pairs
(\mathbb{E},A)

for which the Courant algebroid

\mathbb{E}
is
exact
in the sense of Ševera
[
34
]
: that is,
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbb{E})=2\dim M
and the anchor
\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\colon\mathbb{E}\to TM
is surjective. A morphism
R\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{E}_{2}
between exact Courant algebroids, with base map

\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
, is called
exact
if
the map

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}_{2}}\times\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}\colon R\to\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)\subset TM_{2}\times TM_{1}
is surjective.
We shall need the following fact:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{E},A)
be a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair, such that the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{E}
is exact. Then
the morphism
R\colon\mathbb{T}G\dasharrow\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
from Theorem
3.10
is exact.

Proof.

We want to show that
\mathsf{a}(R)=T(\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s}))
. By
\widehat{G}\times\widehat{G}
-equivariance, it suffices to check along
M\subset G\cong\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
.
But
R|_{M}
is the direct sum of the graphs of

\alpha=(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}},\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}})\colon\mathbb{E}\to TM\oplus A^{*},\ \ \beta=(\iota_{A},\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}^{*})\colon A\oplus T^{*}M\to\mathbb{E}

(where we identify
{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)\cong\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathbb{T}G})
, and similarly for
\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
).
The image under
\mathsf{a}
of these two summands is the direct sum of

\operatorname{ran}(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}})=TM,\ \ A\cong\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{TG})|_{M}

which indeed is all of
TG|_{M}
.
∎

According to Ševera
[
34
]
, the choice of an isotropic splitting
j\colon TM\to\mathbb{E}

of an exact Courant algebroid identifies

\mathbb{E}
with the
\eta
-twisted standard Courant algebroid for a closed 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. That is, it determines an isomorphism
\mathbb{E}\cong TM\oplus T^{*}M
,
with the modified bracket obtained
by adding a term
\iota_{X_{1}}\iota_{X_{2}}\eta
to the right hand side of (
15
). We shall denote this Courant algebroid by
\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M
. By an
\eta
-twisted Dirac structure on
M
, we mean a Dirac structure
A\subset\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M
.

Recall that a differential form
\alpha\in\Omega(G)
on a Lie groupoid is
multiplicative
if

\operatorname{Mult}_{G}^{*}\alpha=\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\alpha+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\alpha,

where
\operatorname{Mult}_{G}\colon G^{(2)}\to G
is the groupoid multiplication and
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\colon G^{(2)}\to G
are the two projections.
For exact
\mathcal{CA}
-groupoids
\mathbb{E}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}^{(0)}
over
G\rightrightarrows G^{(0)}
, with
multiplicative
isotropic splittings
TG\to\mathbb{E}
, the 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(G)
is multiplicative. Exact
\mathcal{CA}
-morphisms

R\colon\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M\dasharrow\mathbb{T}_{\eta^{\prime}}M^{\prime},

with base map
\Phi\colon M\to M^{\prime}
, are described by 2-forms
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
, with
{\mbox{d}}\omega=\eta-\Phi^{*}\eta^{\prime}
. The relationship is given by

v+\mu\sim_{R}v^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow v^{\prime}=\Phi_{*}(v),\ \mu=\Phi^{*}\mu^{\prime}+\iota_{v}\omega.

Exact
\mathcal{CA}
-groupoid morphisms are described by multiplicative 2-forms:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G,G^{\prime}
be Lie groupoids, with multiplicative closed 3-forms
\eta,\eta^{\prime}
, and let

R\colon\mathbb{T}_{\eta}G\dasharrow\mathbb{T}_{\eta^{\prime}}G^{\prime}
be an exact
Courant morphism given by a 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(G)
.
Then
R
is multiplicative if and only if
\omega
is multiplicative.

Proof.

The multiplicativity of
\omega
is equivalent to
\omega^{\flat}\colon TG\to T^{*}G
defining a groupoid morphism.
Hence, if
\omega
is multiplicative we see that if
v_{i}+\mu_{i}\sim_{R}v_{i}^{\prime}+\mu_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,2
, and

v+\mu=(v_{1}+\mu_{1})\circ(v_{2}+\mu_{2}),\ \ v^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}=(v_{1}^{\prime}+\mu_{1}^{\prime})\circ(v_{2}^{\prime}+\mu_{2}^{\prime}),

then

v+\mu\sim_{R}v^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}
. This shows that
R
is multiplicative. The same calculation also shows the converse.
∎

Suppose now that
(\mathbb{E},A)
is an exact
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair. Choose an isotropic
splitting, identifying
\mathbb{E}\cong T_{\eta}M
for a closed 3-form
\eta
. By Proposition
4.3
,
the morphism
R\colon(\mathbb{T}G,TG)\dasharrow(\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E}),\operatorname{Pair}(A))
from Theorem
3.10
is exact, and so is given by a
2-form
\omega_{G}\in\Omega^{2}(G)
satisfying

(21)

{\mbox{d}}\omega_{G}=\mathsf{s}^{*}\eta-\mathsf{t}^{*}\eta.

Since
R
is multiplicative, Lemma
4.4
shows that

\omega_{G}
is multiplicative.
The fact that
R
is a morphism of Manin pairs translates to the fact that

A\subset\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M
is the image of the map

(\mathsf{a}_{A},\mathsf{b}_{A})\colon A\to TM\oplus T^{*}M

where

(22)

\mathsf{b}_{A}\colon A\to T^{*}M

is the map
\mathsf{b}_{A}(\xi)(w)=\omega_{G}(v,w)
, for

v\in\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{t})|_{M}
representing
\xi\in A
and

w\in TM\subset TG|_{M}
. The uniqueness property in the definition of morphism of Manin pairs
(Definition
3.1
)
means that

(23)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{G})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{t})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{s})=0.

These are precisely the conditions defining the integration of the
\eta
-twisted Dirac structure
A\subset\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M

to a quasi-symplectic groupoid, as in
[
6
]
. Note that if
\eta=0
and
A\cap TM=0
, so that
A
is the graph of a Poisson structure, then
\omega_{G}
is
symplectic
. We hence recover the fact that a Poisson manifold is integrable provided that its
cotangent Lie algebroid is integrable.

4.4.
Integration of (quasi-) Lie bialgebroids

Let
(\mathbb{E},A)
be a Manin pair, and
B\subset\mathbb{E}
a Lagrangian subbundle complementary to
A
.
The bundle metric on
\mathbb{E}
identifies
B\cong A^{*}
. By the theory of Liu-Weinstein-Xu
[
24
]
, this choice of splitting defines on
A
the structure of a

quasi-Lie bialgeboid
. (It is a Lie bialgebroid if and only if
B
is a Dirac structure.) Conversely, given a
quasi-Lie bialgebroid
A
, the direct sum
\mathbb{E}=A\oplus A^{*}
acquires the structure of a Courant algebroid, and
(\mathbb{E},A)
is a
Manin pair. We denote by

(24)

\mathsf{a}_{A^{*}}\colon A^{*}\to TM

the restriction of
\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}
to
B\cong A^{*}
.

Suppose
G\rightrightarrows M
integrates
A
, and that
(\mathbb{E},A)
is
\widehat{G}
-equivariant. (There is no equivariance condition on
B
.)

Lemma 4.5
.

The pre-image of

R^{-1}(\operatorname{Pair}(B))

of
\operatorname{Pair}(B)\subset\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
under the relation (
2
)
is the subgroupoid

\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{G}^{\sharp})\rightrightarrows\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{a}_{A^{*}})

defined by a multiplicative bivector field
\pi_{G}
. If
B
is a Dirac structure, then
\pi_{G}
is Poisson.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Pair}(B)\subset\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
is a Lagrangian complement to
\operatorname{Pair}(A)
,
its backward image under
R
is a
Lagrangian complement to
TG
(see e.g.
[
1
, Proposition 1.15]
), and hence is the graph
of a bivector field
\pi_{G}
. (If
B
is a Dirac structure, then so is
\operatorname{Pair}(B)
and its backward image; hence
\pi_{G}
is Poisson in that case.) Since
\operatorname{Pair}(B)
is a subgroupoid and
R
is multiplicative, the backward image
\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{G}^{\sharp})

is a subgroupoid. The space of units of this subgroupoid is the graph of the restriction of

\pi_{G}^{\sharp}
to
A^{*}\subset T^{*}G|_{M}
. This is the same as the map (
24
). ∎

In addition, by
[
24
]
(see also
[
21
, Section 3.2]
or Example
5.2
below), the Lagrangian splitting
\mathbb{E}=A\oplus B
also defines a bivector field

\pi_{M}
on
M
. Again, using that
R
is a morphism of Manin pairs, it follows
(using, e.g,
[
21
, Section 3.5]
)
that the base map
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
is a (quasi-)Poisson map:

\pi_{G}\sim_{(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})}(\pi_{M},-\pi_{M}).

If
B
is integrable (a Dirac structure), then
\pi_{M}
is a Poisson structure. Furthermore,

\operatorname{Pair}(B)
and its backward image are integrable, too, and so
\pi_{G}
is a Poisson structure.
Hence, in this case
\pi_{G}
is a multiplicative Poisson structure on
G
, and
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
is a Poisson map.

In this way, the Mackenzie-Xu integration result for Lie bialgebroids
[
27
]
and its generalization to quasi-Lie bialgebroids

[
15
]
may be seen as consequences of Theorem
3.10
. In fact, we obtain a slight generalization since
G
need not be source-simply connected.

5.
Hamiltonian spaces

5.1.
Hamiltonian
G
-spaces

According to
[
6
,
39
]
, manifolds with Manin pairs (i.e., generalized Dirac manifolds)
serve as moment map targets for moment map theories. The Hamiltonian spaces, in this context, are defined as morphisms of Manin pairs:

Definition 5.1
.

[
6
,
16
]

A
Hamiltonian
A
-space
for a Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
is a manifold
P
together with a
morphism of Manin pairs

(25)

L\colon(\mathbb{T}P,TP)\dasharrow(\mathbb{E},A).

The base map
\Phi\colon P\to M
is called the
moment map
of the Hamiltonian space.

Example 5.2
.

[
6
, Example 3.2]

For every Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
over
M
, the manifold
P=M
is a Hamiltonian
A
-space, with
\Phi=\operatorname{id}_{M}
the identity map, and with

L\subset\mathbb{E}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}M}
the sum of the graph of
\mathsf{a}_{A}\colon A\to TM
and the graph of

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}^{*}\colon T^{*}M\to\mathbb{E}
. If
B
is a Lagrangian complement to
A
, then its pre-image
L^{-1}(B)

under this morphism
is a Lagrangian complement to
TM
, and hence is the graph of a bivector field
\pi_{M}
.

Given a Hamiltonian
A
-space, the Lie algebroid comorphism
TP\dasharrow A
defines an action of the Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
on
P
.
In practice, one is interested in situations where this action integrates to a Lie groupoid action, with an equivariant moment map.
Let
G\rightrightarrows M
be a Lie groupoid, with
\operatorname{Lie}(G)=A
. An action of
G\rightrightarrows M
along
\Phi\colon P\to M

lifts to actions of the jet groupoid
\widehat{G}\rightrightarrows M
on the tangent and cotangent bundles of
P
; these combine into a
\widehat{G}
-action on
\mathbb{T}P
(along the base projection

\mathbb{T}P\to P
followed by
\Phi
).

Definition 5.3
.

A
Hamiltonian
G
-space
for a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair

(\mathbb{E},A)
is a
G
-manifold
P
together with a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant morphism of Manin pairs (
25
),
in such a way that the
G
-action on
P
differentiates to the
A
-action determined by (
25
).

Below, we will spell out this notion of Hamiltonian
G
-space in terms of splittings. Bivector fields arise after choice of a Lagrangian complement to
A
; we shall show that this corresponds to (quasi-)Poisson groupoid actions on (quasi-)Poisson manifolds.
On the other hand, when
\mathbb{E}
is exact, a choice of a isotropic splitting of the anchor map
\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\colon\mathbb{E}\to TM
produces a
2-form theory. This will then relate Definition
5.3
with Xu’s notion of Hamiltonian spaces for quasi-symplectic groupoids.

We will need a result, showing that the equivariance of the morphism
L
in (
25
)
implies a
\mathbb{T}G
-module property with respect to the multiplicative morphism of Manin pairs
R
from
Theorem
3.10
. Recall first that a groupoid action
G\circlearrowright P
naturally lifts to
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid actions

TG\circlearrowright TP,\ \ \ T^{*}G\circlearrowright T^{*}P,\ \ \ \mathbb{T}G\circlearrowright\mathbb{T}P.

Here
TG\rightrightarrows TM
acts on
TP
along
\Phi_{TP}=T\Phi\colon TP\to TM
by tangent lift.
Dually,
T^{*}G\rightrightarrows A^{*}
acts on
T^{*}P
along the map
\Phi_{T^{*}P}\colon T^{*}P\to A^{*}
given by

\langle\Phi_{T^{*}P}(\mu),\,\xi\rangle=\langle\mu,\xi_{P}|_{p}\rangle,\ \ \ \mu\in T^{*}P|_{p},\ \xi\in A|_{\Phi(p)}

where
\xi_{P}|_{p}\in TP|_{p}
is defined by
\xi_{P}|_{p}\sim_{L}\xi

(the image of
\xi
under the infinitesimal action map).
The dual action is described in terms of the tangent action by

\nu^{\prime}=\mu\cdot\nu\ \ \Leftrightarrow\langle\nu^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\mu,v\rangle+\langle\nu,w\rangle\ \mbox{ whenever }w^{\prime}=v\cdot w,

for
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in T^{*}P,\ \mu\in T^{*}G
. (Here
w^{\prime},w\in TP,\ v\in TG
are assumed to have the same base points as
\nu^{\prime},\nu
and
\mu
, respectively, and
\mathsf{s}_{TG}(v)=\Phi_{TP}(w)
.) The tangent and cotangent action combine into an action of

\mathbb{T}G
on
\mathbb{T}P
, along
\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}=(\Phi_{TP},\Phi_{T^{*}P})
.

The action
\mathbb{T}G\circlearrowright\mathbb{T}P
is a
\mathcal{CA}
-groupoid action, in the sense that the action morphism
\mathbb{T}\mathcal{A}\colon\mathbb{T}G\times\mathbb{T}P\dasharrow\mathbb{T}P
is a Courant relation
[
22
,
35
]
. In particular, it has the property

\langle x_{1}\cdot y_{1},\ x_{2}\cdot y_{2}\rangle=\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle+\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
P
be a Hamiltonian
G
-space for the
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
, with moment map
\Phi\colon P\to M
and morphism
L\colon\mathbb{T}P\dasharrow\mathbb{E}
(see (
25
)).

(a)

For
y\in\mathbb{T}P
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{E}
,

y\sim_{L}\zeta\ \Rightarrow\ \Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y)=\alpha(\zeta).

(b)

The morphism
L
is a module morphism with respect to the morphism
R\colon\mathbb{T}G\dasharrow\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})
(see Theorem
3.10
). That is, for
x\in\mathbb{T}G,\ y\in\mathbb{T}P
with
\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y)
,

x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta),\ \ y\sim_{L}\zeta\ \Rightarrow\ x\cdot y\sim_{L}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\cdot\zeta=\zeta^{\prime}.

The statement of (b) may be expressed as commutativity of the following
diagram of Courant relations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}G\times\mathbb{T}P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})\times\mathbb{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}\mathcal{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\times L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}

where the horizontal maps are
\mathcal{CA}
-groupoid actions.

Proof.

(a) Write
y=v+\mu\in\mathbb{T}P|_{p}
. Since the image of
L\subset\mathbb{E}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}P}
under the anchor is tangent to the graph of
\Phi
, we have
\Phi_{TP}(v)=T\Phi(v)=\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta)
. On the other hand, for
\xi\in A_{\Phi(p)}
, since (
25
) is a morphism of Manin pairs, we have

\langle\Phi_{T^{*}P}(\mu),\,\xi\rangle=\langle\mu,\xi_{P}|_{p}\rangle=\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle=\langle\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}(\zeta),\xi\rangle,

hence
\Phi_{T^{*}P}(\mu)=\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}(\zeta)
.

(b) Using the
\widehat{G}
-equivariance, it suffices to consider the
case that
x\in\mathbb{T}G|_{M}
. Suppose
x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta),\ \ y\sim_{L}\zeta
, and let

y^{\prime}=x\cdot y
. We want to show
y^{\prime}\sim_{L}\zeta^{\prime}
, i.e.,
(\zeta^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in L
.
Since
L
is Lagrangian, it suffices to show that

\langle(\zeta^{\prime},y^{\prime}),(\zeta^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime})\rangle=\langle\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime}\rangle-\langle y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\rangle
is zero, for all

(\zeta^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime})\in L
(with the same base points). We calculate as follows:

\langle y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle x\cdot y,\ \Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y^{\prime\prime})\cdot y^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle x,\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y^{\prime\prime})\rangle+\langle y,y^{\prime\prime}\rangle.

From
x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)
,
\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y^{\prime\prime})\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime})
, we obtain

\langle x,\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y^{\prime\prime})\rangle=\langle\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime}\rangle-\langle\zeta,\zeta^{\prime\prime}\rangle.

Similarly,
y\sim_{L}\zeta,\ y^{\prime\prime}\sim_{L}\zeta^{\prime\prime}
implies

\ \ \langle y,y^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle\zeta,\zeta^{\prime\prime}\rangle.

This shows
\langle y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle\zeta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime\prime}\rangle
as desired.
∎

5.2.
Bivector fields

An action
\mathcal{A}\colon G\times_{M}P\to P
of a Poisson groupoid
G\rightrightarrows M
on a Poisson manifold

P
, with moment map
\Phi\colon P\to M
, is called a
Poisson action
if the graph of the action morphism,

\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})=\{(g\cdot p,g,p)|\ \mathsf{s}(g)=\Phi(p)\}\subset P\times(G\times P)^{-}

is a coisotropic submanifold
[
37
]
. This admits a reformulation in terms of the action of
\mathbb{T}G
on

\mathbb{T}P
:

Proposition 5.5
.

An action of a Poisson groupoid
(G,\pi_{G})
on a Poisson manifold
(P,\pi_{P})
is a Poisson action if and only if
the subgroupoid
\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{G}^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{T}G
preserves
\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{P}^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{T}P
.

Proof.

By definition of the action of
T^{*}G
on
T^{*}P
, the elements of the conormal bundle of
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})

are exactly the elements of
T^{*}(P\times(G\times P))
of the form
(-\mu\cdot\nu,\mu,\nu)
. The condition that
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})
is coisotropic means that
(\pi_{P},-\pi_{G},-\pi_{P})^{\sharp}
takes this to the
tangent bundle of
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A})
. The resulting condition

\pi_{P}^{\sharp}(\mu\cdot\nu)=\pi_{G}^{\sharp}(\mu)\cdot\pi_{P}^{\sharp}(\nu)

says that the action of
\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{G}^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{T}G
preserves
\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{P}^{\sharp})
.
∎

Let
P
be a Hamiltonian
G
-space for the
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair
(\mathbb{E},A)
.
Given a Dirac structure
B
complementary to
A\subset\mathbb{E}
, its backward image under
L
defines a Dirac structure complementary to
TP\subset\mathbb{T}P
, which hence is the graph of a Poisson structure
\pi_{P}
. One also has a Poisson structure
\pi_{M}
defined by the decomposition
\mathbb{E}=A\oplus B
, and the map
\Phi
is a Poisson map:

\pi_{P}\sim_{\Phi}\pi_{M}.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
P
be a Hamiltonian
G
-space for
(\mathbb{E},A)
, and choose a splitting
B\subset\mathbb{E}
as above, defining Poisson structures

\pi_{G},\ \pi_{P}
.
Then the moment map
\Phi\colon P\to M
and the
action morphism
\mathcal{A}\colon G\times P\dasharrow P
are Poisson.

Proof.

Let
x=\mu+\pi^{\sharp}(\mu)\in\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{G}^{\sharp})
and
y=\nu+\pi^{\sharp}(\nu)\in\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{P}^{\sharp})
with

\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y)
.
By definition of the backward image, there are unique elements
(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\in\operatorname{Pair}(B)
and
\zeta^{\prime\prime}\in B
such that

x\sim_{R}(\zeta^{\prime},\zeta),\ y\sim_{L}\zeta^{\prime\prime}
. Since

\mathsf{s}_{\mathbb{T}G}(x)=\alpha(\zeta),\ \ \Phi_{\mathbb{T}P}(y)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime\prime})

(see Proposition
5.4
), we obtain
\alpha(\zeta)=\alpha(\zeta^{\prime\prime})
. Since the map
\alpha\colon\mathbb{E}\to TM\oplus A^{*}
restricts to an injection
on
B
(the map
\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}\colon\mathbb{E}\to A^{*}
restricts to an isomorphism
B\cong A^{*}
),
this holds if and only if
\zeta^{\prime\prime}=\zeta
. From Proposition
5.4
(b), it then follows that
x\cdot y\sim_{L}\zeta^{\prime}
.
Since
\zeta^{\prime}\in B
, this implies that
x\cdot y\in\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{P}^{\sharp})
.
By Proposition
5.5
, this proves that the
G\rightrightarrows M
-action on
P
is Poisson.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

If
B\subset\mathbb{E}
is any Lagrangian subbundle complementary to
A
, not necessarily integrable, then the bivector fields
\pi_{G},\ \pi_{P}
are only quasi-Poisson. The results above extend to the quasi-setting, with the obvious modifications.

5.3.
Hamiltonian spaces for quasi-symplectic groupoids

We shall use the following fact:

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
G\rightrightarrows M
be a groupoid acting on a manifold
P
along
\Phi\colon P\to M
, and let

\omega_{P}\in\Omega^{2}(P),\ \omega_{G}\in\Omega^{2}(G)
be 2-forms, where
\omega_{G}
is multiplicative.
Let
\mathcal{A}\colon G\,_{\mathsf{s}}\!\times_{\Phi}\,P\to P
be the action morphism, and
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}
the projections from
G\,_{\mathsf{s}}\!\times_{\Phi}\,P
to the two factors.
Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{A}^{*}\omega_{P}=\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{P}+\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{P}
,

(b)

the maps
\omega_{P}^{\flat},\ \omega_{G}^{\flat}
intertwine the
TG
-action on
TP
with the
T^{*}G
-action on
T^{*}P
,

(c)

the graph
\operatorname{gr}(\omega_{P}^{\flat})\subset\mathbb{T}P
is invariant under the action of the subgroupoid
\operatorname{gr}(\omega_{G}^{\flat})\subset\mathbb{T}G
.

This is a simple extension of the characterization of multiplicative 2-forms on groupoids, and is proved similarly.

Suppose
(\mathbb{E},A)
is a
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pair, where
\mathbb{E}
is exact. The choice of an isotropic splitting

j\colon TM\to\mathbb{E}
identifies
\mathbb{E}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M
for a closed 3-form
\eta
.
By the discussion in Section
4.3
, the morphism
R
from Theorem
3.10
is automatically exact, and is described by a multiplicative 2-form
\omega_{G}\in\Omega^{2}(G)
, making
G\rightrightarrows M
into a quasi-symplectic groupoid.

Suppose
P
is a Hamiltonian
G
-space for
(\mathbb{E},A)
. We assume that the morphism
L
(
25
) is exact, that is,
its image under the anchor surjects onto
\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)
. (This is not automatic; for instance, the morphism
from Example
5.2
need not be exact.) Then
L
is represented by a 2-form
\omega_{P}
, with the property

{\mbox{d}}\omega_{P}=-\Phi^{*}\eta.

By Proposition
5.4
,
L
is a module morphism with respect to
R
.
The same argument as for Lemma
4.4
shows that this is
equivalent to the fact that the maps
\omega_{P}^{\flat},\ \omega_{G}^{\flat}
intertwine the
TG
-action on
TP
with the
T^{*}G
-action on
T^{*}P
. By Proposition
5.8
, this is equivalent to

\mathcal{A}^{*}\omega_{P}=\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\omega_{P}+\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega_{P}
. On the other hand, the uniqueness property in the definition of
morphism of Manin pairs is equivalent to the condition
\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{P})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=0
. These two conditions are exactly the defining properties of a Hamiltonian space
(P,\omega_{P})
for a quasi-symplectic groupoid
(G,\omega_{G})
as in

[
39
]
.

5.4.
Morita equivalence

From the notion of Hamiltonian
G
-spaces for
\widehat{G}
-equivariant Manin pairs, it is straightforward to define Morita equivalence of such Manin pairs.

Recall that a Morita equivalence between Lie groupoids
G_{1}\rightrightarrows M_{1},\ G_{2}\rightrightarrows M_{2}
is a manifold
Q

(the
Hilsum-Skandalis bimodule
) with commuting

G_{1}
-actions and right

-actions,

(26)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{2}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{1}}

where both actions are principal, and

is the quotient map for the
G_{2}

is the quotient map for the

Given another groupoid

and a

serves as a

with the

with itself; note that this serves as a unit under composition of Morita equivalences.

Suppose

are

. A

for these Manin pairs is a

for the

as its base map. The composition of such Morita bimodules for Manin pairs is given by reduction of Courant algebroids and their morphisms, similar to the discussions in

]
and

[

]

Appendix B
More on
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids

B.1.
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoids with trivial base groupoid

Given vector bundles
B,C\to M
and a bundle map

\varrho\colon C\to B

the direct sum
J=C\oplus B
has the structure of a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid, with trivial base groupoid,

(28)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\oplus B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

The target and source maps are

\mathsf{t}_{J}(c,b)=b,\ \ \mathsf{s}_{J}(c,b)=b+\varrho(c)
and multiplication of composable elements reads as

(c_{1},b_{1})\circ(c_{2},b_{2})=(c_{1}+c_{2},b_{1}).

We have
{\operatorname{core}}(C\oplus B)=C
, with core-anchor the given map
\varrho
.
Note that
C\oplus B\rightrightarrows B
is isomorphic to the action groupoid for the
C
-action on
B
given by translation,

b\mapsto b-\varrho(c)
.

Denote by

K\rightrightarrows M

the fat groupoid of the
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid (
28
).
Since
\mathsf{s}_{K}=\mathsf{t}_{K}
, hence
K
is a family of Lie groups
K_{m}
. By definition,
K_{m}
is the set of subspaces of
(C\oplus B)|_{m}
that are complements to both
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})|_{m}
and
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{s}_{J})|_{m}
. We may think of complements to
\operatorname{ker}(\mathsf{t}_{J})|_{m}\cong C_{m}
as graphs of linear maps

f\colon B_{m}\to C_{m}
. Since
\mathsf{s}_{J}(f(b)+b)=(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f)(b)
, this leads to the description

K_{m}=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}(B_{m},C_{m})|\ \det(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f)\neq 0\}

with the group(oid) multiplication given by

(29)

f_{1}f_{2}=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{2}\circ\varrho\circ f_{1}.

One verifies that
\operatorname{Inv}_{K}(f)=-f\circ(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f)^{-1}
.
The Lie algebroid
\mathfrak{k}\Rightarrow M
of
K\rightrightarrows M
is the vector bundle,
\mathfrak{k}=C\otimes B^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(B,C)
. The anchor map is zero; thus
\mathfrak{k}
is a family of Lie algebras. Viewing the sections as bundle maps

h\colon B\to C
, the Lie algebroid bracket on
\mathfrak{k}
is given by

[h_{1},h_{2}]=h_{2}\circ\varrho\circ h_{1}-h_{1}\circ\varrho\circ h_{2}.

As a special case of (
11
), we have a groupoid action
K\times K\circlearrowright C\oplus B
by left
and right multiplications, and the corresponding adjoint actions on
C
and
B
.

Proposition B.1
.

The representations of
K
on
B,C
are given by

\operatorname{Ad}^{B}_{f}=(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f)^{-1},\ \ \ \operatorname{Ad}^{C}_{f}=(\operatorname{id}_{C}+f\circ\varrho)^{-1}.

The
K
-action on
J=C\oplus B
is the direct sum of these representations.
The left and right multiplications are given by

\displaystyle l_{f}(c+b)

\displaystyle=c+\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{C}f(b)+\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{B}b,

\displaystyle r_{f}^{-1}(c+b)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{C}(c-f(b))+b.

Proof.

The adjoint action of
f\in K_{m}
on
C_{m}\oplus B_{m}
is computed as

\operatorname{Ad}_{f}(c+b)=(f(b_{1})+b_{1})\circ(c+b)\circ(\operatorname{Inv}_{K}(f)(b_{2})+b_{2})

where
b_{1},b_{2}\in B_{m}
are uniquely determined by requiring that these elements are composable.
One obtains the conditions
b_{1}+\varrho(f(b_{1}))=b

and
b+\varrho(c)=b_{2}
. Using (
29
), one arrives at

\operatorname{Ad}_{f}(c+b)=\Big(c-f\circ(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f)^{-1}\varrho(c)\Big)+(\operatorname{id}_{B}+\varrho\circ f)^{-1}(b).

The first term may be written as
(\operatorname{id}_{C}+f\circ\varrho)^{-1}(c)
. The calculation for
r_{f}^{-1}
is similar.
∎

Remarks B.2
.

(a)

By differentiation, the infinitesimal representations of
\mathfrak{k}
on
B,C
are given by

\operatorname{ad}^{B}_{h}=-\varrho\circ h,\ \ \operatorname{ad}^{C}_{h}=-h\circ\varrho.

(b)

The source map
\mathsf{s}_{J}
intertwines
l_{f}
with the trivial action on

B
, while
\mathsf{t}_{J}
intertwines it with
\operatorname{Ad}^{B}_{f}
; for
r_{f}^{-1}
the roles of source and target
are reversed. So,

\mathsf{s}_{J}\circ l_{f}=\mathsf{s}_{J},\ \ \mathsf{t}_{J}\circ l_{f}=\operatorname{Ad}^{B}_{f}\circ\mathsf{t}_{J},\ \ \mathsf{s}_{J}\circ r_{f}^{-1}=\operatorname{Ad}^{B}_{f}\circ\mathsf{s}_{J},\ \ \mathsf{t}_{J}\circ r_{f}^{-1}=\mathsf{t}_{J}.

(c)

If
\varrho=0
, we simply have
K=\operatorname{Hom}(B,C)
. At the opposite extreme, if

C=B,\ \varrho=\operatorname{id}
,
the map
\operatorname{Ad}^{B}
gives an identification

.

(d)

In general,
K_{m}
retracts onto
\operatorname{GL}(C_{m}/\operatorname{ker}(\varrho_{m}))
. In particular, it has exactly two connected components unless
\varrho_{m}=0
.

Replacing
\varrho
with its dual map
\varrho^{*}\colon C^{*}\to B^{*}
, we obtain a
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
B^{*}\oplus C^{*}\rightrightarrows C^{*}
.

Proposition B.3
.

The
\mathcal{VB}
-groupoid
B^{*}\oplus C^{*}\rightrightarrows C^{*}
defined by the dual map
\varrho^{*}\colon C^{*}\to B^{*}
is the dual to

, relative to the pairing

\langle\gamma+\beta,c+b\rangle=\langle\gamma,c\rangle+\langle\beta,b\rangle+\langle\beta,\varrho(c)\rangle.

The corresponding adjoint actions of

on

are

\operatorname{Ad}^{B^{*}}_{f}(\beta)=\operatorname{id}_{B^{*}}+f^{*}\varrho^{*},\ \ \ \operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{C^{*}}(\gamma)=\operatorname{id}_{C^{*}}+\varrho^{*}f^{*},

and the actions by left multiplication and right multiplication on

are given by

l_{f}(\beta+\gamma)=\beta-f^{*}\gamma+\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{C^{*}}\gamma,

r_{f^{-1}}(\beta+\gamma)=\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{B^{*}}\beta+f^{*}\gamma+\gamma.

Proof.

These formulas are obtained by direct calculation. In particular, one verifies that the proposed pairing satisfies

\langle\tau_{1}\circ\tau_{2},z_{1}\circ z_{2}\rangle=\langle\tau_{1},z_{1}\rangle+\langle\tau_{2},z_{2}\rangle
for composable elements

\langle l_{f}\tau,\ l_{f}z\rangle=\langle f,z\rangle=\langle r_{f}^{-1}\tau,r_{f}^{-1}z\rangle

. ∎

(\mathbb{E},A)
satisfies

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\circ\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathbb{E}}_{f}=\operatorname{Ad}^{TM}_{f}\circ\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}},\ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}\circ\,\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathbb{E}}_{f}=\operatorname{Ad}^{A^{*}}_{f}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}.

Proof.

For the first identity, we calculate

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}=\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\circ U_{f}=\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{E}}+\iota_{A}\circ f\circ\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}})^{-1}=(\operatorname{id}_{TM}+\mathsf{a}_{A}\circ f)^{-1}\circ\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}=\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{TM}\circ\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}.

The second identity is verified similarly.
∎

Given an integration
G\rightrightarrows M
of
A\Rightarrow M
, consider the
K\times K
-actions on
\mathbb{T}G|_{M}\rightrightarrows TM\oplus A^{*}
given by

(f_{1},f_{2})\mapsto l_{f_{1}}r_{f_{2}}^{-1}
and
on
\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})|_{M}=\mathbb{E}\oplus\overline{\mathbb{E}}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}
by
(f_{1},f_{2})\mapsto(\operatorname{Ad}_{f_{1}}^{\mathbb{E}},\operatorname{Ad}_{f_{2}}^{\mathbb{E}})
.
Recall the maps
\alpha\colon\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E})^{(0)}\to(\mathbb{T}G)^{(0)}
and
\beta\colon{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)\to{\operatorname{core}}(\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E}))

from (
19
).

Proposition B.9
.

The subbundle
R|_{M}=\operatorname{gr}(\alpha)+\operatorname{gr}(\beta)\subset\mathbb{E}\oplus\overline{\mathbb{E}}\oplus\overline{\mathbb{T}G}|_{M}

is
K\times K
-invariant.

Proof.

We show invariance under the action of
K\times\{1\}
; the argument for the second
K
-factor is similar. Note that

K\times\{1\}
acts trivially on
{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)
and
{\operatorname{core}}(\operatorname{Pair}(\mathbb{E}))

hence
\operatorname{gr}(\beta)
is
K\times\{1\}
-invariant.
Consider on the other hand an element

(\zeta,\zeta,\alpha(\zeta))\in\operatorname{gr}(\alpha)

defined by
\zeta\in\mathbb{E}\cong(\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{E})^{(0)}
. Here

\alpha(\zeta)=\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta)-\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}\zeta\in TM\oplus A^{*}=(\mathbb{T}G)^{(0)}.

The formulas from Propositions
B.1
and
B.3
describe the action
l_{f}
on

TM
and
A^{*}
. We obtain

\displaystyle l_{f}\alpha(\zeta)

\displaystyle=l_{f}(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta))-l_{f}(\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}\zeta)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{TM}(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta))-\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{A^{*}}\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}(\zeta)+\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{A}f(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta))+f^{*}\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{T}G}\alpha(\zeta)+y

\displaystyle=\alpha(\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}\zeta)+y.

where
y=\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{A}f(\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta))+f^{*}\operatorname{pr}_{A^{*}}(\zeta)=-(\operatorname{Inv}_{K}(f),f^{*})(\alpha(\zeta))
lies in
{\operatorname{core}}(\mathbb{T}G)
. Equation (
32
) shows that

\beta(y)=\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta).

Comparing with

(f,1)\cdot(\zeta,\zeta)=(\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}\zeta,\zeta)=(\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}\zeta,\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}\zeta)+(0,\zeta-\operatorname{Ad}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}\zeta)

this confirms that
l_{f}(\alpha(\zeta))\sim_{R|_{M}}(f,1)\cdot(\zeta,\zeta)
.
∎

Remark B.10
.

Since the
K\times K
-actions on
TG|_{M}=A\oplus TM,\ T^{*}G|_{M}=T^{*}M\oplus A^{*}

are fully described in terms of
\mathsf{a}_{A}
, Proposition
B.9
does not actually require the integration
G\rightrightarrows M
.

Proposition B.11
.

Let
(\mathbb{E},A)
be a Manin pair, and
G\rightrightarrows M
a source-simply connected groupoid integrating
A\Rightarrow M
. Then there is a unique
action of
\widehat{G}=J^{1}(G)
on
\mathbb{E}
, differentiating to the given action of
\widehat{A}=J^{1}(A)
, and restricting
to the action of
K\rightrightarrows M
described above.

Proof.

Let
J^{1}(G)_{0}
denote the source-identity component of the groupoid
J^{1}(G)
, and
\widetilde{J}^{1}(G)
its
source-simply connected cover (see
[
13
, Section 4.1]
). Then
\widetilde{J}^{1}(G)
is the source-simply connected integration of
the jet algebroid
J^{1}(A)
. The representation of
J^{1}(A)
on
\mathbb{E}
integrates to a representation of
\widetilde{J}^{1}(G)
on

\mathbb{E}
. Since
G
is source-simply connected, the inclusion
K_{0}\hookrightarrow J^{1}(G)_{0}
gives an isomorphism of fundamental
groups for the source fibers. The kernel of the covering projection
\widetilde{J}^{1}(G)\to J^{1}(G)_{0}
coincides with that of

\widetilde{K}\to K_{0}
. But since the
\mathfrak{k}
-action exponentiates to a
K_{0}
-action, it follows that this kernel acts trivially.
We conclude that the
J^{1}(A)
-action integrates to an action of the groupoid
J^{1}(G)_{0}
. On the other hand, we also have the action of
K
. The two actions agree over
K_{0}=J^{1}(G)_{0}\cap K
, and are compatible in the sense that

\widehat{g}\cdot k\cdot\widehat{g}^{-1}\cdot\zeta=(\operatorname{Ad}_{\widehat{g}}k)\cdot\zeta,

for
\widehat{g}\in J^{1}(G)_{0},\ k\in K,\ \zeta\in\mathbb{E}
, as is seen from the explicit formula for the
K
-action. It follows that the

J^{1}(G)_{0}
-action and
K
-action combine into an action of
J^{1}(G)
.
∎

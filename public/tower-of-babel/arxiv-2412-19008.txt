On the minimal parabolic induction
Xinyu Li

Abstract
Motivated by Beilinson–Bernstein’s proof of the Jantzen conjectures [ BB93 ] , we define the minimal parabolic induction functor for Kac–Moody algebras, and establish some basic properties. As applications of the formal theory, we examine first extension groups between simple highest weight modules in the category of weight modules, and analyze the annihilators of some simple highest weight modules.

Introduction

0.1
Motivation

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Kac–Moody algebra. For two weights
\lambda,\mu
of
\mathfrak{g}
, it is a basic problem in representation theory to calculate the first extension groups between
L(\lambda)
and
L(\mu)
in the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-weight modules (or the BGG category
\mathcal{O}
). In particular, such problems arise in recent studies on simple affine vertex algebras (for instance,
[
KR22
]
and
[
ACK23
]
).

However, the explicit result is not easy to calculate in general. Even for
\mathfrak{g}
being a finite dimensional semisimple Lie algebra, the answer depends on the Jantzen conjecture (cf.
[
Hum08
]
Chapter 8.15.), which is a deep result in representation theory. In the finite case, the Jantzen conjecture was proved by Beilinson–Bernstein
[
BB93
]
, using their localisation theorem
[
BB81
]
, the weight filtration of
\ell
-adic mixed perverse sheaves
[
BBD82
]
, and the monodromy weight filtration of nearby cycles
[
Del80
]
.

We do not have a Beilinson–Bernstein type localisation theorem for a general Kac–Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore, some more tools need to be developed to calculate the extension groups. Instead of trying to obtain a direct formula, our idea is to reduce the calculation to some Levi subalgebra (which is usually of finite type, and hence we can apply the known results for finite dimensional reductive Lie algebras). This leads us to the construction of
minimal parabolic induction
.

Let us briefly describe our main constructions and main results below.

0.2
Main results

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Kac–Moody algebra with a fixed choice
\Pi
of simple roots. For any subset
\Xi\subset\Pi
, we have the corresponding standard (resp. opposite) parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
(resp.
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}
), and the associated Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
.

The parabolic induction functor

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},N\mapsto U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N,

which maps
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-Verma modules to
\mathfrak{g}
-Verma modules, is intensively studied in representation theory. Here we inflate an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
to a
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
-module through the projection
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
. Equally important is the parabolic coinduction functor

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{g},N),

which maps completed
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-coVerma modules to completed
\mathfrak{g}
-coVerma modules. Here we inflate an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
to a
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}
-module through the projection
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
.

In the other direction, there is the parabolic restriction functor

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}}(\mathbb{C},M),

and the parabolic corestriction functor

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},M\mapsto\mathbb{C}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}}M,

where
\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}
is the nilradical of
\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}_{\Xi}
. We have adjoint pairs of functors
(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!},\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}),(\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*},\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*})
. Moreover,

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}=\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}=\operatorname{id}.

This induces a natural transformation (see Definition
2.3
)

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}.

Let us denote by
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
the image of the above transformation. We call
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
the
minimal parabolic induction
, or the
intermediate parabolic induction
.

In this paper, we establish some basic properties of minimal parabolic induction. For example, like the construction of IC sheaves, one may expect that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
sends “good” (lisse) simple objects to simple objects. We confirm this expectation in Proposition
3.7
by showing that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
maps a simple weight module to a simple weight module (see Section
3
for the definition of weight modules and the category
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
).

Main Theorem 1
.

For any simple
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-weight module
N
,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N)
is a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-weight module.

We exhibit two applications of the general theory in Section
4
. First, we explore the behavior of first extension groups between some simple highest weight modules under minimal parabolic induction. Under some rather technical conditions on two weights
\lambda,\mu
, we are able to prove that there is an isomorphism (Proposition
4.4
)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu)).

Main Theorem 2
.

Let
\mu,\lambda
be two weights of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mu-\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\Xi
, then we always have an inclusion

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu)).

Moreover, suppose that
\mu-\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Xi
and
\lambda
is
\Xi
-joyful (a technical notion introduced in Definition
4.1
), then the above inclusion is an isomorphism.

Another application is about annihilators. Assume
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
is a finite dimensional reductive Lie algebra, then we are able to prove that

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)))

for
\lambda
being a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
and
w\in W_{\Xi}
, the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
. As a corollary, we show that under the same assumption,
\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda))\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(w\cdot\lambda))
.

Main Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\Xi
is of finite type, i.e.
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
is a finite dimensional reductive Lie algebra. Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a weight such that
\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for any
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Xi^{\vee}
, then we have

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)))\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(w\cdot\lambda))

for any
w\in W_{\Xi}
, the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
.

To the knowledge of the author, this result is new for arbitrary Kac--Moody algebra.

1
Kac–Moody algebra setup

1.1
Parabolic type

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Kac–Moody algebra associated with the triple
(\mathfrak{h},\Pi,\Pi^{\vee})
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is a fixed Cartan subalgebra,
\Pi\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
(resp.
\Pi^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{h}
) is the collection of simple roots (resp. coroots). Denoted by
\Delta
the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\Delta^{+}
(resp.
\Delta^{-}
) the set of positive (resp. negative) roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, we have the root decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+},

where

\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{-}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Any subset
\Xi
of
\Pi
(together with the corresponding subset
\Xi^{\vee}
of
\Pi^{\vee}
) defines a
parabolic type
. More precisely, let
\Delta_{\Xi}=\Delta\cap\mathbb{Z}\Xi,\Delta^{\pm}_{\Xi}=\Delta_{\Xi}\cap\Delta^{\pm}
. Then we have the standard (opposite) parabolic subalgebra of type
\Xi

\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{-}_{\Xi}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+},\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\Xi}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-},

the (opposite) nilradical

\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{\pm}\backslash\Delta^{\pm}_{\Xi}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

and the Levi

\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{\Xi}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

1.2
Transpose anti-involution

Let us name the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}
. We fix the Chevalley generators
e_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{i}}
,
f_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{i}}
. From the structure theory of Kac–Moody algebra (the Serre relations), it is well-known that the map
\tau(e_{i})=f_{i},\tau(f_{i})=e_{i}
and
\tau(h)=h
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}
extends to an anti-involution of
U\mathfrak{g}
, which we still denote by
\tau
. This anti-involution
\tau
is called the
transpose anti-involution
, in the sense that for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
with the standard choice of Chevalley generators,
\tau(x)=x^{t}
is the transpose of a matrix.

The transpose anti-involution interchanges
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
. More generally, for any parabolic type
\Xi
,
\tau
interchanges
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}
,
\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}
and
\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}
, and restricts to an anti-involution on
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
.

The transpose anti-involution
\tau
leads to an isomorphism of algebra
U\mathfrak{g}\simeq(U\mathfrak{g})^{\text{op}}
. As a consequence, we can identify the category of left
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules with the category of right
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules via the twisting of
\tau
. More precisely, for any left
U\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, we can define a right
U\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
M
by

m.X=\tau(X).m,X\in U\mathfrak{g},m\in M.

Conversely, for any right
U\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, we can define a left
U\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
M
by

X.m=m.\tau(X),X\in U\mathfrak{g},m\in M.

1.4
Geometry of weight

Let

be the Weyl group of

. We fix an element

For any root

For two weights

Observation 1.1
.

The root lattice

Let us fix a parabolic type
\Xi\subset\Pi
.

Definition 2.1

(parabolic restriction)
.

There are two types of parabolic restriction functors. The
parabolic
!
-restriction
(invariant) functor is defined by

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}}(\mathbb{C},M),

while the
parabolic
*
-restriction
(coinvariant) functor is defined by

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},M\mapsto\mathbb{C}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}}M.

Here
\mathbb{C}
means the trivial representation. For a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, the
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-action on
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{?}(M)
(
?\in\{!,*\}
) is inherited from the
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-action on
M
. It is well-defined because
[\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi},\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}]\subset\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}
.

Definition 2.2
.

There are two types of parabolic induction functors.

The
parabolic
!
-induction
functor is defined by

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},N\mapsto U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N.

Here we inflate an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
to a
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
-module via the projection
\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
.

The
parabolic
*
-induction
(coinduction) functor is defined by

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod},N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{g},N).

Here we inflate an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
to a
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}
-module via the projection
\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
. The left multiplication of
U\mathfrak{g}
and the right multiplication of
U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
make
U\mathfrak{g}
a
(U\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi})
-bimodule. Then we view
U\mathfrak{g}
as a
(U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi},U\mathfrak{g})
-bimodule via the transpose anti-involution.

Here we collect some well-known facts about parabolic restrictions and parabolic inductions.

Proposition 2.1
.

1.

We have adjoint pairs of functors
(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!},\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}),(\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*},\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*})
.

2.

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}=\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}=\operatorname{id}
.

3.

The functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,?}
(
?\in\{!,*\}
) is exact, the functor
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}
(resp.
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}
) is left (resp. right) exact.

4.

The
!
-induction
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}
maps an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-Verma module to the
\mathfrak{g}
-Verma module with the same highest weight, while the
*
-induction
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}
maps a completed
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-coVerma module to the completed
\mathfrak{g}
-coVerma module associated to the same weight.

Proof.

By the
\otimes
-
\operatorname{Hom}
adjunction, for an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
and a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, we have canonical isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}(N,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(N,\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}}(\mathbb{C},M)).

This shows that
(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!},\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!})
is an adjoint pair. Similarly,

\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(\mathbb{C}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}}M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{g},N)).

This shows that
(\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi},\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*})
is an adjoint pair.

Moreover, for any
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
,

\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}}(\mathbb{C},\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{g},N))=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}}\mathbb{C},N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi},N)=N.

This shows that
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}=\operatorname{id}
.

Similarly,

\mathbb{C}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}}U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N=U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N=N.

This shows that
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}=\operatorname{id}
.

By the PBW theorem,
U\mathfrak{g}=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
is a free (hence projective, flat) right
U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}
-module. This shows the exactness of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,?}
. As a right (resp. left) adjoint,
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}
(resp.
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}
) is left (resp. right) exact.

Recall that Verma modules (resp. completed coVerma modules) are constructed by
!
-induction (resp.
*
-induction) with respect to the empty collection
\emptyset\subset\Pi
. The last assertion follows from the composability of
!
-induction (resp.
*
-induction), which is again due to the composability of
\otimes
(resp.
\operatorname{Hom}
).

.

Let
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}

\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N)

\epsilon^{\pm}\colon U\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}\twoheadrightarrow U\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}/\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{\pm}_{\Xi})=\mathbb{C}

be the augmentation maps,
\phi=\epsilon^{-}\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon^{+}
be the map

\phi=\epsilon^{-}\otimes\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon^{+}\colon U\mathfrak{g}=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}\to\mathbb{C}\otimes U{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\otimes\mathbb{C}=U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}.

Then the map
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)
can be identified with

U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N\to\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N),X\otimes n\mapsto[Y\mapsto\phi(\tau(X)Y)n=\phi(\tau(Y)X)n].

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
N
be an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module. Under the identification

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N,

we have
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N)\subset\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes N
.

Under the identification

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{\Xi}}(U\mathfrak{g},N)=\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N),

we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}(N)
.

Proof.

Let us consider a commutative diagram with exact rows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}\otimes N=N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The existence of the dashed arrow follows from the exactness of rows and the commutativity of the diagram.

For the dual statement, notice that we have a commutative diagram with exact rows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C},N)=N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The existence of the dashed arrow follows from the exactness of rows and the commutativity of the diagram.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
N
be an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module. For any
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule
M\subset\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)
that is contained in
\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes N
under the identification
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N
, we have
M\subset\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,1}(N)
. Dually, any quotient
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)\twoheadrightarrow M^{\prime}
factorizing through
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)
under the identification
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)=\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N)
is a quotient of
\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}(N)
.

Proof.

Every element in
M
can be written as a finite sum
\sum_{i}X_{i}\otimes n_{i}
for some
X_{i}\in U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}
and
n_{i}\in N
. To show that it lies in

\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N)=\ker(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)),

it suffices to show that
\sum_{i}\phi(\tau(Y)X_{i})n_{i}=0
for any
Y\in U\mathfrak{g}
. Let us decompose each
\tau(Y)X_{i}
as a finite sum
\tau(Y)X_{i}=\sum_{j}X_{i,j}^{-}X_{i,j}^{0}X_{i,j}^{+}
for some
X_{i,j}^{-}\in U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},X_{i,j}^{0}\in U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi},X_{i,j}^{+}\in U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}
, then

\sum_{i}\phi(\tau(Y)X_{i})n_{i}=\sum_{i,j}\phi(X_{i,j}^{-}X_{i,j}^{0}X_{i,j}^{+})n_{i}=\sum_{i,j}\epsilon^{-}(X_{i,j}^{-})X_{i,j}^{0}\epsilon^{+}(X_{i,j}^{+})n_{i}.

Notice that the
\tau(Y)
action on
\sum_{i}X_{i}\otimes n_{i}
is

\sum_{i}\tau(Y)X_{i}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}n_{i}=\sum_{i,j}X_{i,j}^{-}X_{i,j}^{0}X_{i,j}^{+}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}n_{i}=\sum_{i,j}X_{i,j}^{-}\otimes X_{i,j}^{0}\epsilon^{+}(X_{i,j}^{+})n_{i}.

By assumption, it is contained in
M\subset\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes N
. This means exactly

\sum_{i,j}\epsilon^{-}(X_{i,j}^{-})X_{i,j}^{0}\epsilon^{+}(X_{i,j}^{+})n_{i}=0.

Therefore, we conclude that
\sum_{i}\phi(\tau(Y)X_{i})n_{i}=0
.

Dually, to show that
M^{\prime}
is a quotient of

\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}(N)=\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)),

it suffices to show that the composition

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)\to M^{\prime}

vanishes. As a
U\mathfrak{g}
-module,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)
is generated by
1\otimes n\in 1\otimes N=N
. Hence it suffices to show that the image of
1\otimes n
vanishes in
M^{\prime}
. Notice that we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)}}

The image of
1\otimes n
in
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)
vanishes, so its image in
M^{\prime}
also vanishes. We are done.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Combining Proposition
2.3
and
2.4
, we see that for any
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
,
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N)
is
the
maximal
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N
that is contained in
\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes N
, and
\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}(N)
is
the
maximal
\mathfrak{g}
-module quotient of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)=\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N)
that factors through
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)
.

Let
\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}
be the forgetful functor from
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
to
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. There are canonical natural transformations

\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}},\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*},

the composition of which induces a natural transformation
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\to\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!*}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
be the image of the natural transformation
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\to\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{*}
. It seems plausible to call
\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!*}
the
intermediate parabolic restriction
functor.

Now we have a commutative diagram of natural transformations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}_{\Xi}^{!}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}

Here the natural transformations
\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}
and
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\to\operatorname{id}
are induced by adjunctions.

The map
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}
(resp.
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
) is monic (resp. epic) because
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}
(resp.
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}
) is left (resp. right) exact.

Proposition 2.5
.

We have
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}=\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}=\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}=\operatorname{id}
.

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram of natural transformations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

whose bottom row is exact. We claim that the composition

\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}

is monic. In fact, for any
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module,

\operatorname{im}(N\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)\to\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N))=1\otimes N

under the identification
\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N
. By Proposition
2.3
, we see that it has zero intersection with

\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N)\subset\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes N.

This shows that the composition

\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}

is monic, i.e. the composition

\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}

is monic. Consequently, the composition

\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}

is monic. Now we have two monos

\operatorname{id}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*},\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}=\operatorname{id}

whose composition is the identity. This shows that they are isomorphisms, and
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}=\operatorname{id}
.

Dually, consider the following commutative diagram of natural transformations

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}

whose top row is exact. We claim that the composition

\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\to\operatorname{id}

is epic. In fact, for any
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module
N
, the composition

\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}(N)\to N

is identified with the quotient

\operatorname{Hom}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi},N)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C},N),

whose kernel is identified with
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)
. By Proposition
2.3
,
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}),N)
maps surjectively to
\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{J}^{1}_{\Xi,*}(N)
. This shows that the composition

\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\to\operatorname{id}

is epic, i.e. the composition

\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\to\operatorname{id}

is epic. Consequently, the composition

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}\to\operatorname{id}

is epic. Now we have two epis

\operatorname{id}=\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*},\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{id}

whose composition is the identity. This shows that they are isomorphisms, and
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}=\operatorname{id}
.

Now

\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}=\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\circ\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*})=\operatorname{id},

we are done.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

As we have seen, the functors
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}
and
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}
are exact. However,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
is not exact in general. Let
N^{\bullet}
be an acyclic cochain complex of
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-modules, then we have a short exact sequence of cochain complexes

0\to\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet})\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet})\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{\bullet})\to 0.

This induces a long exact sequence of cohomologies

\cdots\to\operatorname{H}^{i}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet}))\to\operatorname{H}^{i}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{\bullet}))\to\operatorname{H}^{i+1}(\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet}))\to\operatorname{H}^{i+1}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet}))\to\cdots.

Since
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}
is exact, the cochain complex
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet})
is also acyclic, hence
\operatorname{H}^{i}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet}))=0
for all
i
. So we get an isomorphism

\operatorname{H}^{i}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{\bullet}))=\operatorname{H}^{i+1}(\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet})).

From above discussion, we deduce the following useful Lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N^{-1}\xrightarrow{f}N^{0}\xrightarrow{g}N^{1}
be an exact sequence of
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-modules, then the induced sequence

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{-1})\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{0})\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{1})

is exact if the map
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(g)\colon\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N^{0})\to\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N^{1})
is surjective.

Proof.

We extend the exact sequence to an acyclic complex

N^{\bullet}=[0\to\ker(f)\to N^{-1}\to N^{0}\to N^{1}\to\operatorname{coker}(g)\to 0]

such that
N^{i}
lives in cohomological degree
i
. Then from Remark
2.2
, we have

\operatorname{H}^{0}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{\bullet}))=\operatorname{H}^{1}(\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(N^{\bullet}))=0.

Therefore, we conclude that

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{-1})\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{0})\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N^{1})

is exact.
∎

3
Weight modules

In this section, we focus on weight modules.

3.1
Generalities on weight modules

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a finite-dimensional abelian Lie algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

An
\mathfrak{a}
-module
M
is called a
weight module
if

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}}M_{\lambda},\text{ where }M_{\lambda}=\{v\in M:a\cdot v=\lambda(a)v\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}\}.

Let us denote by
\mathfrak{a}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
the category of weight
\mathfrak{a}
-modules. It has a full subcategory
\mathfrak{a}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
consisting of weight
\mathfrak{a}
-modules of which each weight space is finite dimensional.

The following proposition is well known (cf.
[
Kac90
]
Proposition 1.5).

Proposition 3.1
.

Any submodule or quotient of a
\mathfrak{a}
-weight module is also a weight module.

Proof.

Let
M
be a weight
\mathfrak{a}
-module and
N\subset M
be a submodule. For any
v\in N
, we can decompose
v
as a finite sum
v=\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}
, where
v_{j}\in M_{\lambda_{j}}
and the weights
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}
are distinct. The polynomial

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\in S\mathfrak{a}^{*}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}]

is nonzero, so we can find
a\in\mathfrak{a}
such that
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(a)\neq 0
. This means that
\lambda_{1}(a),\cdots,\lambda_{m}(a)
are distinct.

For
k=0,1,\cdots,m-1
, we have

a^{k}\cdot v=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}(a)^{k}v_{j}\in N.

This is a system of linear equations associated to a nondegenerate matrix (the Vandermonde determinant does not vanish). Hence all
v_{j}
’s lie in
N
.

Now let
f\colon M\twoheadrightarrow L
be a quotient. We know that
\ker f
is a weight module, so the quotient
L
is also a weight module.
∎

From Proposition
1.1
above, we see that
\mathfrak{a}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
is an abelian category, and
\mathfrak{a}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
is a Serre subcategory.

Definition 3.2
.

For an
\mathfrak{a}
-weight module
M
, the
support
of
M
is

\operatorname{supp}(M):=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}:M_{\lambda}\neq 0\}.

3.2
Weight Kac–Moody modules

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Kac–Moody algebra, with a fixed Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. Let us fix a parabolic type
\Xi
from now on in this section.

Definition 3.3
.

We call a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
a
weight module
if it is an
\mathfrak{h}
-weight module. Categorically, the category of weight
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
, is the inverse image of
\mathfrak{h}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
under the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{h}}}\mathfrak{h}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. Moreover, we have the full subcategory
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
, consisting of weight
\mathfrak{g}
-modules of which each weight space is finite dimensional, that is the inverse image of
\mathfrak{h}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
under
\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{h}}
. We know that
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
is an abelian category, with
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
being a Serre subcategory.

Similarly, we have the abelian category of weight
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-modules
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
that is the inverse image of
\mathfrak{h}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
under the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}_{\mathfrak{h}}}\mathfrak{h}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
. It has a Serre subcategory
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
consisting of weight
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-modules of which each weight space is finite dimensional.

Remark 3.1
.

From a homological algebra point of view, the category
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
is a better object to study than
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
, because it has better homological properties. For example, it is a cocomplete symmetric monoidal category, while
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
is not closed under arbitrary colimits and tensor products. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
, many homological properties of
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
can be recovered from those of
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
. For example, for two objects
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
, the first extension group between them in
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
is the same as the one in
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
.

However, there is a novel construction on
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
that cannot be performed on
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
, that is, the restricted duality functor presented below.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
M\in\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-weight module of which each weight space is finite dimensional. Let
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}M_{\lambda}
be the weight decomposition. The linear dual
M^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{C})
is naturally a right
U\mathfrak{g}
-module, can we make which into a left
U\mathfrak{g}
-module via the transpose anti-involution
\tau
. Then the subspace

M^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}M_{\lambda}^{*}\subset M^{*}

is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule. We call

the

of

.

The following properties are well-known (cf.
[

]
,
[
Kac90
]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

1.

The operation of taking restricted dual defines a contravariant functor

{}^{\vee}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}.

2.

The functor
∨
is an anti-involution, i.e.
{{}^{\vee}}{{}^{\vee}}=\operatorname{id}
.

3.

The restricted duality functor
∨
is exact. Moreover, it defines an anti-equivalence of abelian categories.

4.

The simple highest weight modules are self-dual, i.e.
L(\lambda)^{\vee}\simeq L(\lambda)
for each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

As an easy corollary, we have

Corollary 3.1
.

For any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}\subset\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
, there is a canonical isomorphism of Yoneda extension groups

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(N_{1},N_{2})=\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(N_{2}^{\vee},N_{1}^{\vee})

for any

.

Repeating above constructions word-by-word, we can define the restricted duality functor

{}^{\vee}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}

satisfying the same properties.

3.3
Weight modules under parabolic induction

Proposition 3.3
.

The functors
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!},\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
and
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}
restrict to functors

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod},

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod},

\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}\colon\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}\to\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}.

Proof.

For
N\in\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
, the isomorphism
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N
is not only an isomorphism of vector spaces, but it also preserves the
\mathfrak{h}
-module structure. Here the right hand side is understood as the tensor product of
\mathfrak{h}
-modules. Therefore, we see that if
N\in\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
, then
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes N
lives in
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}
.

As a quotient (resp. sub), we see that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
(resp.
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}
) also maps weight modules to weight modules.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Unlike the parabolic
!
-induction
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}
, the parabolic
*
-induction
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,*}
does not map a weight
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module to a weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module in general (instead of direct sum of weight spaces, it is direct product of weight spaces). Though our construction in the previous section is completely “dualizable”, asymmetric phenomena may happen when focusing on weight modules.

Proposition 3.4
.

The functors
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi},\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}
and
\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}
restrict to functors

\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod},

\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod},

\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod},

\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}},

\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}},

\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}^{\text{fin}}.

Proof.

The forgetful functor
\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}\to\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}
preserves weight spaces. Therefore, as a sub (resp. quotient),
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}
(resp.
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}
) can be restricted. Thus
\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}=\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}\to\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi})
can be restricted.
∎

Definition 3.5

-weight module

For any simple (hence indecomposable) weight

Proposition 3.6
.

The functors
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi},\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}
and
\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}
map a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-weight module to a simple
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-weight module or
0
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-weight module. Suppose that
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M)\neq 0
. Pick any nonzero weight vectors
v_{1},v_{2}\in\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M)\subset\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(M)

M
, we have

, we have
\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}.v_{i}=0

In particular, we can find
X_{1},X_{2}\in U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
such that

. Now we have
v_{1}=X_{1}X_{2}.v_{1}
, so the weight of
X_{1}X_{2}
is
0
. By Observation
1.1
, we see that
X_{1},X_{2}\in 1\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}=U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
, so
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
lie in the same
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-submodule of
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M)
. This verifies the simplicity of
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M)
.

Dually, let

be a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-weight module such that
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}(M^{\prime})\neq 0
. Pick any nonzero weight vectors
\bar{w}_{1},\bar{w}_{2}\in\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}(M^{\prime})
. Let

be a weight vector in
\operatorname{Oblv}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(M^{\prime})

\bar{w}_{i}
. By the simplicity of
M^{\prime}
, we have

. Therefore, we can find

do not vanish in the quotient

Y_{1},Y_{2}\in 1\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}
. Now we have
w_{1}=Y_{1}Y_{2}.w_{1}
, so the weight of
Y_{1}Y_{2}
is
0
. By Observation
1.1
, we see that

(and hence

\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}

\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}(M^{\prime})
.

The functor
\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}
is a quotient of
\operatorname{Res}^{!}
(and a sub of
\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}

-weight modules to simple
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-weight modules or
0
.
∎

.

For any weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(L(\lambda))=\operatorname{Res}^{*}_{\Xi}(L(\lambda))=\operatorname{Res}^{!*}_{\Xi}(L(\lambda))=L_{\Xi}(\lambda)

, we conclude that

.

Proposition 3.7
.

The functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}
maps a simple
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-weight module to a simple weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Proof.

Let
N
be a simple
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-weight module, then it is
\xi
-shifted for some
\xi
. Pick any nonzero

, then

by the simplicity of
N
. Therefore
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)=U\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{U\mathfrak{p}^{+}_{\Xi}}N=U\mathfrak{g}.(1\otimes n)
and the image of
1\otimes n
in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N)

Let
\bar{v}
be any nonzero vector in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(N)

be a preimage of
\bar{v}
in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(N)

4.1
Extensions

In this section, we examine the behavior of first extension groups between some simple highest weight modules under minimal parabolic induction.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a weight. Suppose we have a short exact sequence in
\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}

Moreover, assume that there is no weight
\mu\in\operatorname{supp}(M_{1})
such that
\mu>\lambda
, then the restricted sequence

0\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M_{1})\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M_{2})\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(L(\lambda))=L_{\Xi}(\lambda)\to 0

is also exact.

Proof.

The functor
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}
is left exact, so we only need to show that
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M_{2})\to L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
is surjective. Let
v_{\lambda}
be the highest weight vector in
L(\lambda)
. By assumption,
v_{\lambda}
must come from a highest weight vector
v\in M_{2}
. We have
v\in M_{2}^{\mathfrak{n}^{+}}\subset M_{2}^{\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}}=\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M_{2})
. Therefore
v_{\lambda}
, now viewed as the highest weight vector in
L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
, lives in the image of
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M_{2})\to L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
. As a consequence, the map
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(M_{2})\to L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
is surjective.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mu,\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be two weights such that
\mu\equiv\lambda\pmod{Q_{\Xi}}

Using the duality functor introduced in Section
3.2
, we have

So without losing generality, we can assume that
\mu\ngtr\lambda
.

Now by Lemma
4.1
, for any extension

[0\to L(\mu)\to P\to L(\lambda)\to 0]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu)),

0\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(L(\mu))=L_{\Xi}(\mu)\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(P)\to\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(L(\lambda))=L_{\Xi}(\lambda)\to 0

is also exact, and hence gives an element in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu))

\mathbf{R}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu)).

Let us show that
\mathbf{R}
is injective. Let

\left[0\to L(\mu)\xrightarrow{f}P\xrightarrow{g}L(\lambda)\to 0\right]

be an element in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))

\mathbf{R}\left[0\to L(\mu)\xrightarrow{f}P\xrightarrow{g}L(\lambda)\to 0\right]=0.

This means that the projection
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(P)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}(L(\lambda))=L_{\Xi}(\lambda)

\iota
. Denote by

L(\lambda)

for some

g

is
\Xi
-joyful, then for any short exact sequence of weight
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-modules

0\to L_{\Xi}(\mu)\to Q\to L_{\Xi}(\lambda)\to 0,

the induced sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\mu))=L(\mu)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(Q)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))=L(\lambda)\to 0

is exact.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
, the map
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\mu))\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(Q)
is injective and the map
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(Q)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))
is surjective. It remains to check the exactness of the middle term.

Since
\mu-\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Xi
, the highest weight vector in
L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
must come from a highest weight vector in
Q
. Therefore we have an
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
-module morphism
M_{\Xi}(\lambda)\to Q
such that the composition
M_{\Xi}(\lambda)\to Q\to L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
is nonzero. This induces a sequence

\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))\to\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(Q)\to\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda)).

The composition
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))\to\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))
is surjective by Proposition
4.3
, so the map
\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(Q)\to\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))
is surjective. Now using Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that the middle term

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\mu))\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(Q)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))

is exact.
∎

Now we can state and prove the main result of the section.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mu,\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be two integral weights such that
\mu\equiv\lambda\pmod{Q_{\Xi}}
and
\mu-\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Xi
. Moreover, suppose that
\lambda
is
\Xi
-joyful, then we have isomorphisms

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\mu),L(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu))=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\mu),L_{\Xi}(\lambda)).

Proof.

Using the restricted dual introduced in Section
3.2
, it is enough to show that

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu)).

In Proposition
4.1
, we have constructed an inclusion

\mathbf{R}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu))

that is induced by the functor
\operatorname{Res}^{!}_{\Xi}
.

By Lemma
4.2
, for any element

[0\to L_{\Xi}(\mu)\to Q\to L_{\Xi}(\lambda)\to 0]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu)),

the induced sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\mu))=L(\mu)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(Q)\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))=L(\lambda)\to 0

is exact, and hence gives an element in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))
. This operation induces a linear map

\mathbf{I}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu)).

By Proposition
2.5
,
\mathbf{R}\circ\mathbf{I}=\operatorname{id}
. Combining with the fact that
\mathbf{R}
is injective, we conclude that
\mathbf{R}
induces an isomorphism
\mathbf{R}\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{g}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L(\lambda),L(\mu))\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\text{-}\mathsf{wtMod}}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda),L_{\Xi}(\mu))
.
∎

4.2
Annihilators

In this section, let us assume
\Xi
is of finite type, i.e.
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
is a finite dimensional reductive Lie algebra.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
, i.e.
\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for any
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Xi^{\vee}
. The following statement is a direct consequence of Duflo’s theorem on annihilators of Verma modules
[
Duf75
]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
, then for any
w\in W_{\Xi}
, we have

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)).

We prove that this property is stable under minimal parabolic induction.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
, then for any
w\in W_{\Xi}
, we have

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))).

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
,
M_{\Xi}(\lambda)=L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
is simple, and
L_{\Xi}(\lambda)
is the unique simple submodule of
M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)
(its socle). The inclusion

M_{\Xi}(\lambda)=L_{\Xi}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)

induces an inclusion

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)).

Therefore
\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)))\supset\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)))
. Let
I=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)))
,
I
is a two-sided ideal of
U\mathfrak{g}
. It remains to prove that
I.\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))=0
.

Suppose
I.\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))\neq 0
, pick any nonzero
\bar{v}\in I.\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))
. Let
v
be a preimage of
\bar{v}
in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes M_{\Xi}(\lambda)
. Since
\bar{v}
is nonzero in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))
,
U\mathfrak{g}.v
has nonzero intersection with
1\otimes M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)
(Remark
3.3
). Noting that
M_{\Xi}(\lambda)
is the only simple submodule of
M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)
, we have

1\otimes M_{\Xi}(\lambda)\subset(U\mathfrak{g}.v)\cap(1\otimes M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)).

Let
v_{\lambda}
(resp.
v_{w\cdot\lambda}
) be the highest weight vector of
M_{\Xi}(\lambda)
(resp.
M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)
),
\bar{v}_{\lambda}
(resp.
\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}
) be the image of
1\otimes v_{\lambda}
(resp.
1\otimes v_{w\cdot\lambda}
) in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))
(resp.
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))
), then

\bar{v}_{\lambda}\in U\mathfrak{g}.\bar{v}\subset I.\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))=IU\mathfrak{g}.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}=I.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}.

Choose
X\in I\subset U\mathfrak{g}=U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}
such that
\bar{v}_{\lambda}=X.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}
. Let us decompose
X
as
X=X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3}
for some

X_{1}\in 1\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\otimes 1=U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi},X_{2}\in\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\otimes 1,X_{3}\in U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}\otimes U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}\otimes\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{+}_{\Xi}).

Since
\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}
is a highest weight vector,
X_{3}.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}=0
, so

\bar{v}_{\lambda}=X_{1}.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}+X_{2}.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}.

Noticing that
w\cdot\lambda-\lambda\in Q_{\Xi}
, we have, by Observation
1.1
,
\bar{v}_{\lambda}=X_{1}.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}
. But
X\in I=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)))
, for any
w\in M_{\Xi}(\lambda)
,

0=X.\bar{w}=X_{1}.\bar{w}+X_{2}.\bar{w}.

Here
\bar{w}
means the image of
1\otimes w
in
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))
. Therefore

1\otimes(X_{1}.w)+X_{2}.(1\otimes w)\in\operatorname{J}^{-1}_{\Xi,!}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))\subset\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi}(U\mathfrak{u}^{-}_{\Xi})\otimes M_{\Xi}(\lambda).

Thus
X_{1}.w=0
. Consequently,

X_{1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)).

This implies that
\bar{v}_{\lambda}=X_{1}.\bar{v}_{w\cdot\lambda}=0
, which is absurd. So
I
annihilates
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))
, we are done.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
, then for any
w\in W_{\Xi},
we have

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda))\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(w\cdot\lambda)).

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is a
\rho
-anti-dominant integral weight for
\mathfrak{l}_{\Xi}
,
L_{\Xi}(\lambda)=M_{\Xi}(\lambda)
. By Corollary
3.4
,

L(\lambda)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)).

From Proposition
4.6
, we know that

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(\lambda)))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))).

The quotient
M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)
induces a surjection
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(L_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda))=L(w\cdot\lambda)
(Proposition
2.2
), so

\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda))=\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{Ind}_{\Xi,!*}(M_{\Xi}(w\cdot\lambda)))\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(L(w\cdot\lambda)).

∎

Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{0}s_{2}}

Bi-geodesic mappings between hyperbolic surfaces with boundary
Wen Yang

Abstract
. It is proved that a bijection between two compact hyperbolic surfaces with boundary is an isometry if it and its inverse map each geodesic onto some geodesic.

51M09, 51M10, 57M50

1.
Introduction

As is well known, if
f:M\rightarrow N
is an isometry, then
f

and
f^{-1}
map each geodesic onto some geodesic. Conversely, suppose
that
f:M\rightarrow N
is a
bi-geodesic mapping
, i.e.,
f

is a bijection such that
f
and
f^{-1}
map each geodesic onto
some geodesic. Is
f
always an isometry? The answer is no. Many
linear transformations of Euclidean spaces are counterexamples. Here
is a non-trivial counterexample:

Example 1
.

Let
\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the unit disk equipped with
the Hilbert metric. As illustrated in Figure
1
,
we connect
0
to
e^{\pi i/3}
and to
1
by two copies of the
minor arc that connects
1
to
e^{\pi i/3}
. These arcs bound a
domain
D
. Consider the Euclidean rotation

f:D\rightarrow D,\quad z\mapsto e^{2\pi i/3}\left(z-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}e^{\pi i/6}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}e^{\pi i/6},

which rotates
D
by an angle of
2\pi/3
.

Figure 1.
A non-trivial bi-geodesic mapping.

The mapping
f:D\rightarrow D
has the following properties:

(1)
f
and
f^{-1}
are diffeomorphisms that map each geodesic
onto some geodesic;

(2) We can naturally extend
f
to a self-homeomorphism of
\overline{D}
;

(3) The domain
D
is equipped with a hyperbolic metric and is neither
compact nor complete;

(4)
f
maps many geodesics of infinite length to geodesics of finite
length;

(5)
f
is not an isometry, since it is not even a dilation.

The above example shows that a general bi-geodesic mapping can be
very complicated. Nevertheless, the bi-geodesic mappings between some
spaces are trivial. In 1847 Von Staudt
[
19
]

proved the Fundamental Theorem of Affine Geometry: A bijective self-mapping
of a Euclidean space is affine if it maps lines to lines. Since then,
Von Staudt’s theorem has been generalized to various settings by Beltrami,
Levi-Civita, Weyl, Sinyukov, Mikeš, and others (see
[
2
,
20
,
11
,
4
,
9
,
12
,
6
,
21
,
16
,
13
,
1
,
18
,
15
]
).

In a recent joint work
[
14
]
with L. Liu, we have
studied the bi-geodesic mappings between pairs of pants. The aim of
this paper is to continue the work of
[
14
]
and
establish:

Theorem 2
.

Let
R_{i}
be a compact hyperbolic surface
of genus
g_{i}
with
n_{i}\geqslant 1
geodesic boundary components,
and
S_{i}
the interior of
R_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Suppose that
f:S_{1}\rightarrow S_{2}

is a bijection such that
f
and
f^{-1}
map each geodesic onto
some geodesic. Then
f
is an isometry.

Since a geodesic
\tau
in a hyperbolic surface
R
with boundary
is a boundary component if and only if every geodesic
\rho\neq\tau

in
R
intersects
\tau
at most twice, by Theorem
2

we obtain:

Corollary 3
.

Let
R_{i}
be a compact hyperbolic surface of genus
g_{i}
with

n_{i}\geqslant 1
geodesic boundary components,
i=1,2
. Suppose
that
f:R_{1}\rightarrow R_{2}
is a bijection such that
f
and

f^{-1}
map each geodesic onto some geodesic. Then
f
is an isometry.

Note that
f
is assumed to be a bijection but not a homeomorphism,
and all the geodesics are regarded as point sets but not mappings.
We cannot lift
f
to the universal covering space at once, hence
the classical proof of the Fundamental Theorem of Affine Geometry
does not work here. In addition, the geodesics here are more complex
than that in a pair of pants or a torus. In order to overcome these
difficulties, we develop the idea in
[
14
]
and introduce
new properties preserved by
f
to carefully classify the geodesics
in terms of intersections with geodesics.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic surfaces and geodesics

Here we recall some definitions and results from the theory of hyperbolic
surfaces, see
[
3
]
,
[
5
]

or
[
8

It is well known that a smooth curve

if
\ddot{\gamma}=0

of

\gamma

However, throughout this paper, the word “geodesic” always means
the image of a complete geodesic. In other words, we say a subset
of a hyperbolic surface is a

if it is the image of
a locally shortest curve which cannot be extended to any other locally
shortest curve.

We call a surface, obtained from a closed surface of genus

by

of finite type
.

Proposition 4

(
[
14
, Proposition 16]
)
.

A homeomorphism between the interiors of two
hyperbolic surfaces of finite type is an isometry if it maps each
geodesic onto some geodesic.

There is a natural homeomorphism from

We generalize the notion of “spiral” as follows. Let

Therefore no confusion arises in the following lemma.

A compact hyperbolic surface with totally geodesic boundary is called

Proposition 8

In the literature, the notion of “fill” is usually only considered

We use the Klein disk model

The following theorem is essential to understand the behavior of geodesics
in hyperbolic surfaces, though we will not mention it in the proofs

Theorem 9

2.2.
Arc and curve complex

Let
\overline{X}
be a closed surface of genus
g
and
B=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\}\subset\overline{X}
.
Then
X=\overline{X}-B
is a surface of genus

with
n
punctures.

Let

be a path such that
\alpha(0),\alpha(1)\in B

We call

an

in
X
if
\tau\subset X
.
We say
\tau
is

\alpha
is not nullhomotopic

We say that two arcs
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
in

are

if there exists a continuous map
H:[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow\overline{X}

such that

•

H((0,1)\times\{0\})=\tau_{0}
and
H((0,1)\times\{1\})=\tau_{1}

•

H(0,t),H(1,t)\in B
for all
t\in[0,1]

•

H((0,1)\times[0,1])\subset X
.

The
arc and curve complex
of

, denoted by

, is
the abstract simplicial complex whose
k

are collections of distinct homotopy classes of

mutually non-homotopic one-dimensional submanifolds which can be either
essential simple closed curves or essential simple arcs in
X
. We
will denote by
|AC(X)|
the set of vertices of

.

Theorem 10

(see
[
10
]
)
.

Every simplicial automorphism of

is induced
by the action of a self-homeomorphism of
X
.

See
[
10
]
for more details on arc and curve complex.

2.3.
Elementary move

In
[
17
]
, Mosher introduced the notion of elementary
moves on ideal triangulations. Let
X
be a punctured surface of
finite type. Let
\delta
be an
ideal triangulation
of
X
,
i.e., a cell division of
X
such that each 1-cell of
\delta
is
a simple arc and each 2-cell of
\delta
is a triangle. If
h
is
an edge of
\delta
that bounds two distinct triangles of
\delta
,
then these two triangles are joined along
h
to form a quadrilateral;
removing
h
and inserting the opposite diagonal of this quadrilateral,
we obtain a new ideal triangulation
\delta^{\prime}
which is said to be
obtained from
\delta
by an
elementary move
. See
[
17
]

for more precise definitions.

Furthermore, If
X
is equipped with a hyperbolic metric, and the
edges of
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
are geodesics, then we say the elementary
move is
geodesic
.

Theorem 11

(Connectivity Theorem for Elementary Moves, in
[
17
]
)
.

Any two ideal triangulations of
X
are related
by a finite sequence of elementary moves.

if and only if
r^{f}(\beta_{1})

is disjoint from
r^{f}(\beta_{2})
.

From the discussion above, we see that

\varphi:|AC(S)|\rightarrow|AC(S)|,\quad\beta\mapsto(r^{f})^{-1}(f_{*}(r(\beta)))

is a well-defined bijection such that
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are adjacent
in
AC(S)
if and only if
\varphi(\beta_{1}),\varphi(\beta_{2})

are adjacent in
AC(S)
, and hence
\varphi
can be naturally extended
to a simplicial automorphism of
AC(S)
. It follows from Theorem

10
that there exists a homeomorphism
h:S\rightarrow S

such that
\varphi(\beta)=h(\beta)
for any
\beta\in|AC(S)|
. We
regard
h
as the “topological type” of
f
.

We have proved that, for every
\tau\in G_{c}\cup G_{+\infty}
, the
geodesic
f(\tau)
belongs to
G_{c}\cup G_{+\infty}^{f}
and is
homotopic to
h(\tau)
. We can naturally extend
h
to a self-homeomorphism
of
R
. It follows immediately that
\tau\in G_{+\infty}
spirals
towards
\alpha\subset\partial R
if and only if
f(\tau)\in G_{+\infty}^{f}

spirals towards
h(\alpha)\subset\partial R
.

Lemma 24
.

Suppose that
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in G(p)
. Then

\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are homotopic if and only if
f(\tau_{1})

and
f(\tau_{2})
are homotopic.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that if
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are homotopic,
then
f(\tau_{1})
and
f(\tau_{2})
are homotopic.

As we see in the proof of Lemma
15
,
\tau_{1}
is contained
in a pair of pants
P\subset R
. Since
\tau_{2}
is homotopic to

\tau_{1}
, it is also contained in
P
.

Extend
P
to a pants decomposition of
S
and denote the corresponding
system of decomposing curves by
\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N}\}
. Because

\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are disjoint from
\xi_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\xi_{N}
,
we see that
f(\tau_{1}),f(\tau_{2})
are disjoint from
f(\xi_{1})\cup\cdots\cup f(\xi_{N})
.
Notice that
\{f(\xi_{1}),\cdots,f(\xi_{N})\}
is also a system of
decomposing curves.

Assume that
\tau_{1}
hits a boundary component
\alpha
. Choose
a geodesic
\tau\in G_{+\infty}
that spirals towards
\alpha
in
two directions. Then
\tau_{1}
intersects
\tau
infinitely many
times, hence
f(\tau_{1})
intersects
f(\tau)
infinitely many
times and hits the boundary component
h(\alpha)
. Since
\tau_{2}

is homotopic to
\tau_{1}
, it also hits
\alpha
, and hence
f(\tau_{2})

hits
h(\alpha)
by the same reasoning as above. It follows that

f(\tau_{1})
hits the same boundary components as
f(\tau_{2})
.
Combining this with the discussion in the previous paragraph, we see
that
f(\tau_{1})
and
f(\tau_{2})
are contained in the same pair
of pants. Since the homotopy class of a simple arc in a pair of pants
is determined by the boundary components it hits, we conclude that

f(\tau_{1})
is homotopic to
f(\tau_{2})
.
∎

Theorem 26
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}
be two compact
hyperbolic surfaces of genus

with

geodesic boundary
components. Let
S_{1}
and

be the interiors of

and
R_{2}
, respectively. Suppose that
(g,n)\neq(0,3)
and

is a bijection such that
f
and
f^{-1}
map each geodesic onto
some geodesic. Then

is an isometry.

Proof of main theorem

Proposition 27
.

Let
R_{i}
be a compact hyperbolic surface
of genus
g_{i}
with
n_{i}\geqslant 1
geodesic boundary components,
and
S_{i}
the interior of
R_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Suppose that
f:S_{1}\rightarrow S_{2}

is a bijection such that
f
and
f^{-1}
map each geodesic onto
some geodesic. Then
(g_{1},n_{1})=(g_{2},n_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(g_{1},n_{1})=(0,3)
, i.e.,
R_{1}
is a pair of pants.
Suppose to the contrary that
(g_{2},n_{2})\neq(0,3)
. Then there
exists a simple closed geodesic in
S_{2}
, i.e., there exists a
geodesic
\tau
in
S_{2}
that strongly cofills
S_{2}
and is
strongly pre-spiraled but not recurrent. Hence
f^{-1}(\tau)
is
a geodesic in
S_{1}
that strongly cofills
S_{1}
and is strongly
pre-spiraled but not recurrent, which is impossible. Thus
(g_{2},n_{2})

must equal
(0,3)
.

Suppose that
(g_{1},n_{1})\neq(0,3)
. By Table
1
,
we see that
\tau
is a simple closed geodesic if and only if
f(\tau)

is a simple closed geodesic. Since
f
is a bijection, Proposition

8
implies that

(1)

3g_{1}+n_{1}-3=3g_{2}+n_{2}-3.

If
n_{1}=1
, then Proposition
19
gives
n_{2}=1
,
and hence
(g_{1},n_{1})=(g_{2},n_{2})
by (
1
).

If
n_{1}\geqslant 2
, then Proposition
19
gives

n_{2}\geqslant 2
and

6g_{1}+3n_{1}-7=6g_{2}+3n_{2}-7.

Combining this with (
1
) we also obtain
(g_{1},n_{1})=(g_{2},n_{2})
.
∎

Finally, Theorem

follows from Theorem

,
Theorem

and Proposition

.

8.
Concluding Remarks

We now see that the bi-geodesic mappings between compact hyperbolic
surfaces with boundary are isometries. The method may be generalized
to apply to other manifolds
with boundary
. In particular, a
paper on punctured hyperbolic surfaces with boundary will be published
elsewhere.

However, it seems that the method does not work for manifolds
without
boundary
. It is still an open problem to determine whether the bi-geodesic
mappings between closed hyperbolic surfaces or punctured hyperbolic
surfaces are isometries.

Furthermore, in contrast with the Fundamental Theorem of Affine Geometry,
it is desirable to determine whether a bi-geodesic mapping is always
a
geodesic mapping
, i.e., a bijection that maps each geodesic
onto some geodesic.

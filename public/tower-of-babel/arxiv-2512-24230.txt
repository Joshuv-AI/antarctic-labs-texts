Explicit bounds for the graphicality of the prime gap sequence
Keshav Aggarwal, Robin Frot, Haozhe Gou, Hui Wang

Abstract
. We establish explicit unconditional results on the graphic properties of the prime gap sequence. Let p n p_{n} denote the n n -th prime number (with p 0 = 1 p_{0}=1 ) and PD n = ( p ℓ − p ℓ − 1 ) ℓ = 1 n \mathrm{PD}_{n}=(p_{\ell}-p_{\ell-1})_{\ell=1}^{n} be the sequence of the first n n prime gaps. Building upon the recent work by Erdős et al , which proved the graphic nature of PD n \mathrm{PD}_{n} for large n n unconditionally, and for all n n under RH, we provide the first explicit unconditional threshold such that: (1) For all n ≥ exp ⁡ exp ⁡ ( 30.5 ) n\geq\exp\exp(30.5) , PD n \mathrm{PD}_{n} is graphic. (2) For all n ≥ exp ⁡ exp ⁡ ( 34.5 ) n\geq\exp\exp(34.5) , every realization G n G_{n} of PD n \mathrm{PD}_{n} satisfies that ( G n , p n + 1 − p n ) (G_{n},p_{n+1}-p_{n}) is DPG-graphic. Our proofs utilize a more refined criterion for when a sequence is graphic, and better estimates for the first moment of large prime gaps proven through an explicit zero-free region and explicit zero-density estimate for the Riemann zeta function.

1.
Introduction

Let
p_{n}
denote the
n
-th prime number with
p_{0}=1
.
We call the sequence

\mathrm{PD}_{n}=\left(p_{\ell}-p_{\ell-1}\right)_{\ell=1}^{n}=(1,1,2,2,4,2,\cdots,{p_{n}-p_{n-1}})

the
first
n
prime gap sequence
.
A sequence of nonnegative integers
(d_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{n}
is said to be
graphic

if there exists a simple undirected graph with
n
vertices
whose degrees are exactly
d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n}
. We call a simple graph on
n\geq 2
vertices a
prime gap graph
if its vertex degrees are
\mathrm{PD}_{n}
.

Two key conjectures have inspired this study:

Conjecture 1.1

(Existence; Toroczkai, 2016)
.

For every
{n\geq 2}
, there exists a prime gap graph on
n
vertices.

Conjecture 1.2

(Structural; Toroczkai, 2016)
.

Every prime gap graph on
n
vertices contains
(p_{n+1}-p_{n})/2
independent edges.

Notably, Conjecture
1.2
implies Conjecture
1.1
(see the next subsection), linking the combinatorial properties of
prime gap graphs to the distribution of primes.
By merging tools from analytic number theory (to analyze prime gaps)
and matching theory (to establish graph realizability and edge independence), Erdős
et al.

[
7
]
proved these two conjectures hold for sufficiently large
n
unconditionally, and for every
n
assuming the Riemann Hypothesis (RH).

This paper establishes an explicit lower bound
n_{0}
such that the result holds unconditionally for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
Our main theorem is:

Theorem 1.3
.

One may take
n_{0}=\exp\exp(30.5)
. That is, for all
n\geq\exp\exp(30.5)
, the first
n
prime gap sequence
\mathrm{PD}_{n}
is graphic.

While our results broadly follow the framework of
[
7
]
,
achieving an optimal explicit bound
n_{0}
requires two critical refinements: a sharper criterion for graphicality of degree sequences (developed in Section
2
),
and a refined treatment of zero-free regions and zero-density estimates for the Riemann zeta function (detailed in Section
3
).

The main bottleneck is the known explicit zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function, which impacts the explicit bounds in prime gap estimates.

1.1.
DPG-process

The
Degree-Preserving Network Growth (DPG)
process is an algorithm to construct a sequence of graphs that iteratively extends a graph
G_{n}
with degree sequence

D_{n}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
to
G_{n+1}
with
D_{n+1}=D_{n}\,\circ\,d_{n+1}:=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n},d_{n+1})
,
while preserving the degrees of the vertices of
G_{n}
.
Unlike static algorithms like the Havel–Hakimi algorithm,
which recompute realizations from scratch, DPG modifies
G_{n}
through a local operation:

Degree Preserving-Step
: Insert a new vertex
u
with even degree
d_{n+1}=2\nu
.

(1)

Remove a matching of size
\nu
from
G_{n}

(i.e., a set of
\nu
independent edges with no shared vertices).

(2)

Connect
u
to all
2\nu
endpoints of the removed edges.

Existing vertices retain their degrees, as each loses one edge from the removed matching but gains a connection to
u
.

The pair
(G_{n},d_{n+1})
is called
DPG-graphic
if
G_{n}
contains a matching of size
d_{n+1}/2
.
By
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
, the sequence
D_{n}\circ d_{n+1}
is graphic if and only if there exists a realization
G_{n}
of
D_{n}
such that the pair
(G_{n},d_{n+1})
is DPG-graphic. In order to construct an infinite DPG process, one needs the requirement that every realization of
D_{n}
contains a matching of size
d_{n+1}/2
.
While this holds trivially for constant sequences (e.g., growing regular graphs),
general sequences require careful analysis of matchings in
G_{n}
. To achieve this, we will rely on Vizing’s theorem and its consequence stated in Lemma
2.8
.

The DPG process avoids global restructuring and ensures degree invariance,
but its applicability relies on the non-trivial existence of sufficiently large matchings
in intermediate graphs.
It is non-trivial to construct an infinite,
naturally occurring sequence
D
whose every initial segment is graphic.

In their novel work
[
7
]
,
Erdős
et al.
demonstrated for the first time that
the sequence of first
n
prime gap graphs constitutes a non-trivial,
naturally arising infinite structure (for
n
sufficiently large and for all
n
if RH assumed).
Specifically, they established two fundamental properties:
i) All initial segments of this sequence are graphic;
ii) Every realization of these initial segments remains admissible for the DPG-algorithm.

Our second result makes this explicit.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq\exp\exp(34.5)
. For any realization
G_{n}
of prime gap sequence
\mathrm{PD}_{n}
, the pair
\big(G_{n},\,p_{n+1}-p_{n}\big)
is DPG-graphic.

2.2.
Criteria for sequences being graphic

To address this problem, we draw upon some
knowledge from graph theory. Many results provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a
nonnegative sequence to be graphic.
One classic and well-known theorem is the
Erdős–Gallai criterion
[
6
]
:
Given
\left(d_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

such that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}\geq 0

and the sum of all terms is even,
then there exists a (simple undirected) graph with
n
vertices
and degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\leq k(k-1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\min(k,d_{i})

for all
k\in\{1,\dots,{n-1}\}
.

In fact, the results of Zverovich and Zverovich
[
18
]
, and later Tripathi and Vijay
[
16
]
, indicate that it is unnecessary to verify the above inequality for all values of
1\leq k\leq n
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\left(d_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a nonincreasing sequence with
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}\geq 0
. Then
\left(d_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}
is graphic if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
is even and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\leq k(k-1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\min(k,d_{i})

holds for
k=m
and for all
k<m
such that
d_{k}>d_{k+1}
, where
m=\max\{i:d_{i}\geq i\}
.

This result significantly reduces the number of
k
-values that need to be checked
in the original Erdős–Gallai criterion, especially when
n
is sufficiently large.
This reduction plays an important role in our setting.

2.3.
Application of Vizing’s theorem

A well-known theorem by Vizing
[
17
]
states that a simple graph with maximal degree
\Delta

admits a proper edge coloring with
\Delta+1
colors. This leads to the following lemma,
which establishes a condition for the existence of a large matching.

Lemma 2.8

(Erdős
et al
.
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices with degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
. Let
\delta\geq 1
be an integer, and let
d\geq 2
be an even integer satisfying

\delta d\leq\sum_{d_{i}<\delta}d_{i}-\sum_{d_{i}\geq\delta}d_{i}.

Then
G
has a matching of size
d/2
.

3.
First moment of large prime gaps

Let
N=4\delta x
throughout this section. For simplicity, we define the integral

\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}(x,T):=\int_{x}^{2x}\left|\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|<T}y^{\rho}C(\rho)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}y

where

C(\rho)=\frac{1-(1+\delta)^{\rho}}{\rho}

with

0<\delta\leq 1
. Following essentially the method of Heath-Brown
[
10
]
,
we will bound the first moment of large prime gaps by the integral
\mathcal{I}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x\geq\exp{10^{3}}
and
N>8\log^{2}x.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq p_{\ell}\leq 2x\\
p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell}>N\end{subarray}}\left(p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell}\right)\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq p_{\ell}\leq 2x\end{subarray}}\left(p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell}\right)+8.1\frac{1}{\delta^{2}x^{2}}\mathcal{I}(x,2\delta^{-1}\log^{2}x).

Proof.

Assume that the prime
p_{\ell}\in[x,2x]
satisfies
{p_{\ell+1}}-p_{\ell}\geqslant N.
There is at most one
p_{\ell}
such that

(p_{\ell}+p_{\ell+1})/2>2x
, so we consider its contribution separately and assume that
(p_{\ell}+p_{\ell+1})/2\leqslant 2x
. Then, for any

y\in(p_{\ell},(p_{\ell}+p_{\ell+1})/2)\subset(x,2x),

the interval

[y,y+\delta y]\subset[y,y+N/2]\subset(p_{\ell},p_{\ell+1})

is free of primes.
The difference
\psi(y+\delta y)-\psi(y)
therefore only includes contribution
from higher prime powers
p^{k}
(
k\geq 2
), which gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\in(y,y+\delta y]\\
k\geq 2\end{subarray}}\log p

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{2\leq k\leq\frac{\log(y+\delta y)}{\log 2}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{1/k}<p\leq(y+\delta y)^{1/k}\end{subarray}}\log p

\displaystyle<\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{1}{k}\log(y+\delta y)\left(\pi\big((y+\delta y)^{1/k}\big)-\pi\big(y^{1/k}\big)\right)

\displaystyle<\log(y+\delta y)\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{1}{k}y^{1/k}\left((1+\delta)^{1/k}-1\right)

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1\right)\delta\sqrt{y}\log(2y)<0.001\delta y,

for all
y\geq x\geq 1.6\times 10^{8}
.
Now we have
|\psi(y+\delta y)-\psi(y)-\delta y|>0.999\delta y
holds on

y\in(p_{\ell},(p_{\ell}+p_{\ell+1})/2).
Squaring and integrating, we obtain

\int_{p_{\ell}}^{(p_{\ell}+p_{\ell+1})/2}|\psi(y+\delta y)-\psi(y)-\delta y|^{2}\mathrm{d}y>0.499\delta^{2}x^{2}(p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell}).

Note that the set of points where
y+\delta y
or
y
takes an integer value
is countable and thus of Lebesgue measure zero, making its contribution to the above integral zero.
It follows from Lemma
2.5
that

\displaystyle|\psi(y+\delta y)-\psi(y)-\delta y|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|<T}\frac{y^{\rho}-(y+\delta y)^{\rho}}{\rho}+E(y+\delta y,T)-E(y,T)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|<T}y^{\rho}C(\rho)\right|+E(T,x).

With the choice of
T=2\delta^{-1}\log^{2}x<x
, the error term
E(T,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle E(T,x)

\displaystyle\leq{4.6}\frac{4x\log(4x)\log\log(4x)+2x\log(2x)\log\log(2x)}{T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x}{T}\log^{2}x=\frac{1}{2}\delta x,

for all
x\geq 3.9\times 10^{59}
. On using the simple inequality
(A+B)^{2}\leq 2A^{2}+2B^{2}
, we have

0.499\delta^{2}x^{2}(p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell})<2\int_{p_{\ell}}^{(p_{\ell}+p_{\ell+1})/2}\left|\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|<T}y^{\rho}C(\rho)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{4}\delta^{2}x^{2}(p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell}).

Summing over all such primes
p_{\ell}
,
and bounding the possible single exceptional
p_{\ell}
by taking maximum,
we obtain the desired result.

Proposition 3.2

(Bounds for Integral

.

Suppose that there are positive constants

(with
{2\leq A\leq 4}
) such that

Suppose further that the Riemann zeta function has no zeros

in the region

with
\eta({\gamma})<1/A

Then, whenever

and

where the constant

C=C(A,\alpha,c_{1})=4\times 1836\,\frac{c_{1}}{A\alpha}\left(\frac{2}{A}-\alpha\right)^{4}.

Proof.

We can first bound this integral as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}&\leq\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{vx}{2}}^{2vx}\left|\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\leq T}y^{\rho}C(\rho)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}v\\
&\leq\int_{1}^{2}\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\leq T}\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho^{\prime}|\leq T}C(\rho)\overline{C(\rho^{\prime})}\frac{2^{1+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}-2^{-1-\rho-\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}}{1+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}x^{1+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}v^{1+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}v\\
&\leq x\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho|\leq T}\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}\rho^{\prime}|\leq T}\left|x^{\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}C(\rho)\overline{C(\rho^{\prime})}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{2^{1+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}-2^{-1-\rho-\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}}{1+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{2^{2+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}-1}{2+\rho+\overline{\rho^{\prime}}}\right|.\end{split}

By writing
\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma,\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\prime}
, and using the elementary inequality
2|z_{1}z_{2}|\leq|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}
, the above is

\leq\frac{1}{2}x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\prime}\\
|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq T\end{subarray}}\left(x^{2\beta}|C(\rho)|^{2}+x^{2\beta^{\prime}}|C(\rho^{\prime})|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{(2^{3}+1)(2^{4}+1)}{1+|\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}}\right),

which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\leq 153x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\\
|\gamma|\leq T\end{subarray}}x^{2\beta}|C(\rho)|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\prime}\\
|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1+|\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\times 153x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\\
\beta\geq\frac{1}{2},10<\gamma\leq T\end{subarray}}x^{2\beta}|C(\rho)|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\prime}\\
|\gamma^{\prime}|\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1+|\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}}

by the symmetry of the double summation and the symmetric distribution of zeros. We divide the summation over
\gamma^{\prime}

by their distance from
\gamma
, and apply Lemma
2.1
to get that the inner sum of above is

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\,\sum_{k\leq|\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma|\leq k+1}\frac{1}{1+|\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma|^{2}}\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}\sum_{k\leq|\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma|\leq k+1}1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\log(\gamma+k)}{1+k^{2}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor}\frac{\log(2\gamma)}{1+k^{2}}+\sum_{k>\gamma}\frac{\log(2k)}{k^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}\coth(\pi)\right)\log(2\gamma)+\frac{\log(2\gamma)+1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq 3\log\gamma

for all
\gamma>10
.
Using the integral representation

C(\rho)=\int_{1}^{1+\delta}x^{\rho-1}\mathrm{d}x,

we apply the triangle inequality for complex-valued integrals to get

|C(\rho)|\leq\int_{1}^{1+\delta}|x^{\rho-1}|\mathrm{d}x=\int_{1}^{1+\delta}x^{\beta-1}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{1}^{1+\delta}1\,\mathrm{d}x=\delta.

Thus we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}\leq 1836x\delta^{2}\log T\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\\
\beta\geq\frac{1}{2},10<\gamma\leq T\end{subarray}}x^{2\beta}.\end{split}

Let

By Lemma
2.1

(3.4)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leq xN\left(T\right)\leq xT\log T.

By the zero-free region (

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\geq\frac{1}{2},\,10<\gamma\leq T}\int_{1/2}^{\beta}2x^{2\sigma}\log x\ \mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/2}^{1-\eta(T)}2x^{2\sigma}(\log x)\,N(\sigma,T)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

We split the integral above into two parts, one over the interval

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

Remark 3
.

This theorem can be compared with Theorem 1.8 in

Informally, our result serves as its unconditional counterpart,

Proof.

First note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y/2<p_{\ell}\leq y\\

Proof of Theorem

Thanks to Theorem
2.7
, to prove Theorem
1.3
,

and write
k_{N}=k_{N}(p_{n})
for simplicity.

Thus we are required to establish

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq n\\
p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell}>N\end{subarray}}(p_{\ell+1}-p_{\ell})\leq k_{N}(k_{N}-1)+n-k_{N}

for all

, which is equivalent to establishing

\displaystyle S_{N}(p_{n})\leq(k_{N}-1)^{2}+n-1

for all

.

4.1.
Case 1:
k_{N}\geq\sqrt{p_{n}}+1

This is the trivial case, since

\displaystyle S_{N}(p_{n})\leq p_{n}\leq(k_{N}-1)^{2}.

4.2.
Case 2:
k_{N}\leq\sqrt{p_{n}}

In this case, we choose
\alpha=0.249

.

So the first term in (

) is

(4.2)

N^{\prime}k_{N}\leq\frac{p_{n}}{3\log p_{n}}<\frac{n-1}{3}.

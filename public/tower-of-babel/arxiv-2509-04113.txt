A unified stabilized virtual element method for the generalized Oseen equation: stability and robustness
Sudheer Mishra, Natarajan E

Abstract
In this thesis, we investigate a novel local projection based stabilized conforming virtual element method for the generalized Oseen problem using equal-order element pairs on general polygonal meshes. To ensure the stability, particularly in the presence of convection-dominated regimes and the utilization of equal-order element pairs, we introduce local projections based stabilization techniques. We demonstrate the discrete inf-sup condition in the energy norm. Moreover, the stability of the proposed method also guarantees the stability properties for the Brinkman equation and the Stokes equation without introducing any additional conditions. Furthermore, we derive an optimal error estimates in the energy norm that underline the uniform convergence in the energy norm for the generalized Oseen problem with small diffusion. In addition, the error estimates remain valid and uniform for the Brinkman equation and the Stokes equation. Additionally, the convergence study shows that the proposed method is quasi-robust with respect to parameters. The proposed method offers several advantages, including simplicity in construction, easier implementation compared to residual-based stabilization techniques, and avoiding coupling between element pairs. We validate our theoretical findings through a series of numerical experiments, including diffusion-dominated and convection-dominated regimes.

This raises the question of whether the pressure field can be effectively approximated through virtual element space. If so, would such an approach ensure stability? 2
The generalized Oseen equation

In this section, we introduce the generalized Oseen equation along with the well-posedness of its primal formulation.

The stationary generalized Oseen equation on a polygonal simply connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find $(\mathbf{u},p)$ such that}\\
-\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}}+\gamma\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\mathbf{f}\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\quad\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where the constants
0<\mu\leq 1
and
\gamma>0
are the viscosity/diffusive coefficient and the reaction coefficient, respectively. The element pair
(\mathbf{u},p)
represents the velocity vector field and the scalar pressure field. The convective velocity field is denoted by
\bm{\mathcal{B}}\in[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{2}
with
\nabla\cdot\bm{\mathcal{B}}=0
, a.e. on
\Omega
. The load
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(E)]^{2}
is the source/sink term. For the sake of simplicity, we assume a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition, i.e.,
\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{0}
. Additionally, the non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions can be employed following
(
gatica2014simple
, Chapter 2)
, and the Neumann boundary can be treated by using the trace inequality.

2.1
Weak formulation

We now introduce the spaces for the velocity vector field and the pressure field as follows:

\mathbf{V}:=[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2},\qquad Q:=L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\big\{z\in L^{2}(\Omega):\small{\int}_{\Omega}z\,d\Omega=0\big\},\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt

endowed with their natural norms. Furthermore, we define the following bilinear forms for all

and

,

•

a(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}):=\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{u}:\nabla\mathbf{w}\,d\Omega
, where
A:B
denotes the contraction of two tensors
A
and
B
, see

;

•

b(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

b(\mathbf{w},q):=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w})q\,d\Omega
;

•

c(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

c(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}):=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,d\Omega
;

•

d(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}):=\gamma\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}\,d\Omega
.

As we assume that
\nabla\cdot\bm{\mathcal{B}}=0
, employing the integration by parts, we can easily conclude that the continuous form
c(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the skew-symmetric property, i.e.

\displaystyle c(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})=-c(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u})\qquad\text{\, for all \,\,}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{V}.

(2.2)

Following the skew-symmetric property of
c(\cdot,\cdot)
, we define a bilinear form corresponding to the convective term as follows

\displaystyle c^{skew}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})=\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[c(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})-c(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u})\Big]\qquad\text{\, for all \,\,}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{V}.

(2.3)

Hereafter, we introduce the bilinear form
A:(\mathbf{V}\times Q)\times(\mathbf{V}\times Q)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

A[(\mathbf{u},p),(\mathbf{w},q)]:=a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})-b(\mathbf{w},p)+b(\mathbf{u},q)+c^{skew}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})+d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})\qquad\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{u},p),\,(\mathbf{w},q)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q.

(2.4)

The primal formulation for the problem (
2.1
) reads as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find}\,\,(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q,\,\,\text{such that}\\
A[(\mathbf{u},p),(\mathbf{w},q)]=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w})\quad\,\text{for all \,\,}(\mathbf{w},q)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q.\end{cases}

(2.5)

We emphasize that the form
A[\cdot,\cdot]
is continuous and preserves the coercivity property, as the inf-sup condition for
b(\cdot,\cdot)
is discussed by Ladyzhenskaya-Babu
\check{\text{s}}
ka-Brezzi (LBB) in
mfem22
. Consequently, problem (
2.1
) possesses a unique solution
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}\times Q
.

Remark 3.3

The classical virtual element (VE) problem for the Oseen equation is given as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find}\,\,(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}\times Q^{k}_{h},\,\,\text{such that}\\

where

. The VE problem (

) exhibits non-physical oscillations whenever problem (
3.26
) is convection-dominated or equal-order element pairs are employed. In such cases, a stabilized form of the VE problem (
3.26
) is required in order to circumvent these issues.

3.4
Stabilized virtual element problem

In this section, we will address the instability of velocity and pressure fields that disrupt solution’s accuracy by causing spurious or non-physical oscillations near boundary layers. The velocity field becomes unstable in the presence of convection-dominated regimes, whereas the pressure instability often arises due to the equal-order element pairs framework. To address these issues, we introduce the following stabilization terms:

•

Velocity field stabilizing bilinear form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1,h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{1,E}\mathcal{B}_{E}^{2}S^{E}_{\nabla}\Big((\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})\mathbf{w}_{h},(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})\mathbf{z}_{h}\Big)\Big],

where
\mathcal{B}_{E}=\|\bm{\mathcal{B}}\|_{0,\infty,E}
for all
E\in\Omega_{h}
. Furthermore, using the norm equivalence discussed in
vem28
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{0,E}\simeq h^{-1}_{E}\|(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1})\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{0,E},

(3.27)

this allows us to take projection onto the polynomial space
[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(E)]^{2}
. Another advantage of this term is that it will be effective for the VEM order
k=1
on triangular meshes, whereas the velocity stabilizing term
S_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
discussed in

vanishes on triangular meshes with VEM order
k=1
.

•

The mass equation stabilizing term

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2,h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{2,E}\Big[\Big(\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}_{h},\Pi^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{z}_{h}\Big)+S^{E}_{\nabla}\Big((\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k})\mathbf{w}_{h},(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k})\mathbf{z}_{h}\Big)\Big].

•

Pressure stabilizing term

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{3,h}(p_{h},q_{h}):=\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\tau_{3,E}\Big[\Big(\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}(\nabla p_{h}),\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}(\nabla q_{h})\Big)+S^{E}_{p}\Big((I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})p_{h},(I-\Pi^{\nabla,E}_{k-1})q_{h}\Big)\Big],

where
\widehat{\mathbf{r}}_{h}:=\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}
, and
\tau_{i}
are stabilization parameters for
i=1,2
and
3
.
Additionally, the bilinear form
S^{E}_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the following

\displaystyle\lambda_{3\ast}\ \|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,E}^{2}\leq S^{E}_{p}(q_{h},q_{h})\leq\lambda_{3}^{\ast}\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,E}^{2}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,q_{h}\in Q^{k}_{h}(E)\cap ker({\Pi}^{\nabla,E}_{k-1}),

(3.28)

where the positive constants
\lambda_{3\ast}\leq\lambda_{3}^{\ast}
independent of the mesh size.

Remark 3.4

Recalling
smfem1
;
mishra2025equal
;
mvem21
, we introduce the following definition of the stabilization parameters
\tau_{i,E}
, as follows

\displaystyle\tau_{1,E}\sim h_{E},\qquad\tau_{2,E}\sim\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad\tau_{3,E}\sim h^{2}_{E}.

(3.29)

Moreover, we will use the above definitions throughout this work.

We now set
\mathcal{L}_{h}[(\mathbf{w}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{z}_{h},q_{h})]:=\mathcal{L}_{1,h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h})+\mathcal{L}_{2,h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h})+\mathcal{L}_{3,h}(p_{h},q_{h})
. Therefore, the stabilized VE problem for the Oseen equation is given as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Find}\,\,(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}\times Q^{k}_{h},\,\,\text{such that}\\
A_{h}[(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{w}_{h},q_{h})]+\mathcal{L}_{h}[(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{w}_{h},q_{h})]=(\mathbf{f}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})\quad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,(\mathbf{w}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}\times Q^{k}_{h}.\end{cases}

(3.30)

Remark 3.5

The problem (
3.30
) can be interpreted as the extension of the classic VE problem through the inclusion of element-wise local projection-based stabilizing terms

;

To establish the well-posedness and error estimates for the stabilized VE problem (

Remark 4.6

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h},p-p_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2k}_{E}\Big(1+{\mu}+\frac{2+5\Theta_{2}+4(1+C_{gen})(\mu+C_{gen})^{1/2}}{4\Theta_{1}}\Big)\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{k+1,E}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{2k}_{E}\big(\|p\|^{2}_{k,E}+\tilde{\Lambda}_{E}^{2}h^{4}_{E}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}_{k,E}\big),

(4.79)

where
\tilde{\Lambda}_{E}:=\min\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau_{2,E}}}\}
with
\tau_{2,E}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Thus, the error estimate (
4.79
) converges uniformly in the energy norm as
\mu\rightarrow 0
, whereas in
vem028
;
mvem21
the
L^{2}
-error in pressure becomes quasi-uniform.

4.5
Alternative discrete convective form

In this section we introduce an alternative discrete convective form to be used in the place of
c^{skew,E}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
in the stabilized VE problem (
3.30
). Following
vem26
;
mvem15
, we consider the following:

\displaystyle\widehat{c}^{E}_{h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h}):

\displaystyle=\int_{E}(\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{w}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}}\cdot\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{z}_{h}\,dE\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{k}(E),

with the associated skew-symmetric part

\displaystyle\widehat{c}^{skew,E}_{h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h}):

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\Big[\widehat{c}^{E}_{h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h})-\widehat{c}^{E}_{h}(\mathbf{z}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})\Big].

(4.80)

Notably, the global bilinear form
\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
associated to (
4.80
) satisfies the following:

\bullet

\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})=0\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}
.

\bullet

\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h})\leq C\mathcal{B}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{z}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\mathbf{w}_{h},\mathbf{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}
.

We will now investigate the well-posedness of the VE problem (
3.30
) along with the discrete convective form
\widehat{c}^{skew,E}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
. In this sequel, we first bound
s_{3}
(see Step 3 in proof of Theorem
4.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle s_{3}:=\alpha\widehat{c}^{skew,E}_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha\mathcal{B}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega},

(4.81)

provided that
\|\nabla\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}=\xi_{0}\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}
.

We now present the well-posedness theorem for the VE problem (
3.30
) along with the discrete convective
form
\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}
:

Theorem 4.5

(Well-posedness)
Under the mesh regularity assumption
(A1)
, there exists a positive constant
\Theta
independent of the mesh size
h
, such that for any
(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}\times Q^{k}_{h}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(\mathbf{0},0)\neq(\mathbf{z}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{k}_{h}\times Q^{k}_{h}}\dfrac{(A_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{h})[(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathbf{z}_{h},q_{h})]}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{z}_{h},q_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\geq\Theta{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

(4.82)

Moreover, the stabilized VE problem (
3.30
) along with the discrete convective
form
\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}
has a unique solution.

Proof

The proof of Theorem
4.5
follows from Theorem
4.2
and the bound (
4.81
) with
\Theta=\mathbb{N}_{1}/\sqrt{\mathbb{N}_{2}}

and

Lemma 4.11

(Estimation of
\eta_{\widehat{c}}
) Let the assumptions
(A1)
and

(4.84)

Proof

Concerning
\eta_{\widehat{c}}
, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\eta_{\widehat{c}}:

\displaystyle=\widehat{c}^{skew}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})-\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{I},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\widehat{c}^{skew}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})-\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})+\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{I},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=:\eta_{\widehat{c},1}+\eta_{\widehat{c},2}.

(4.85)

\bullet
Estimation of
\eta_{\widehat{c},1}
: We proceed as follows

\displaystyle\eta_{\widehat{c},1}

\displaystyle=\widehat{c}^{skew}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})-\widehat{c}^{skew}_{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\Big[\Big((\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\mathbf{v}_{h}\Big)-\Big((\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h}\Big)+\Big((\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{u}\Big)-\Big((\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\mathbf{u}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\Big[\Big((\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\mathbf{v}_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h}\Big)+\Big((\nabla\mathbf{u}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h}\Big)\,+

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\Big((\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\Big)+\Big(\big(\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}-\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}\big)\bm{\mathcal{B}},\mathbf{u}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\Big[\Big((\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\big((\nabla\mathbf{u})\bm{\mathcal{B}}\big),\mathbf{v}_{h}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h}\Big)+\Big((\nabla\mathbf{u}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{u}),(\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\Big((\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}},\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\Big)+\Big(\big(\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}-\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}\big),\mathbf{u}\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}(\mathbf{u}\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T})\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=:\widehat{c}_{1}+\widehat{c}_{2}+\widehat{c}_{3}+\widehat{c}_{4}.

(4.86)

Using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{1}+\widehat{c}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}\Big[h^{k+1}_{E}\|\bm{\mathcal{B}}\|_{k,\infty,E}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{k+1,E}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,E}+h^{k+1}_{E}\mathcal{B}_{E}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{k+1,E}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,E}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{k}_{E}\delta_{E}(\|\bm{\mathcal{B}}\|_{k,\infty,E}+\mathcal{B}_{E})\|\mathbf{u}\|_{k+1,E}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{E}.

(4.87)

Concerning the second term
\widehat{c}_{2}
, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{k}_{E}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{k+1,E}\|(\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T})\|_{0,E}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{E\in\Omega_{h}}h^{k}_{E}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{k+1,E}\Big[\|(\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k}\mathbf{v}_{h}-\mathbf{v}_{h})\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T}\|_{0,E}+\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T}-\bm{\Pi}^{0,E}_{k-1}(\mathbf{v}_{h}\bm{\mathcal{B}}^{T})\|_{0,E}\Big]

6
Conclusion

We propose and analyze a new stabilized virtual elements for addressing the generalized Oseen equation for different cases, including the convection-dominated regime, employing equal-order element pairs. The stabilization techniques are based on local projection-based stabilizing terms. The stabilizing terms are free from higher-order derivative terms, easier to implement compared to residual-based stabilization methods, and do not allow coupling between the element pairs. Further, we have established the stability for the proposed stabilized method, which also aligns with the stability of the Stokes and Brinkman equations, without imposing additional conditions on the parameters. Moreover, we have derived the error estimates in the energy norm, which is quasi-robust with respect to parameters. Additionally, we have performed several numerical examples validating the optimal convergence rates for different cases, including convection-dominated regimes and zero diffusivity parameter.

Classification of symmetry protected states of quantum spin chains for continuous symmetry groups
Bruno de Oliveira Carvalho, Wojciech De Roeck, Tijl Jappens

Abstract
Symmetry protected states (SPT’s) of quantum spin systems were studied by several authors. For one-dimensional systems (spin chains), there is an essentially complete and rigorous understanding: SPT’s corresponding to finite on-site symmetry groups G G are classified by the second cohomology group H 2 ​ ( G , U ​ ( 1 ) ) H^{2}(G,U(1)) , as established in [ 26 ] . We extend this result to the case of compact topological symmetry groups G G . We also strengthen the existing results in the sense that our classification results holds within the class of spin chains with locally bounded on-site dimensions.



1
Introduction

This paper fits into the study of topological properties and characterization of ground state of quantum many body Hamiltonians. The systems are built on an extensive lattice like
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and every site of the lattice hosts a finite number of degrees of freedom. We restrict to spin systems; to every site is associated a quantum spin, corresponding to a finite dimensional Hilbert space.
In this field, one calls ground states
trivial
if they can be continuously deformed into product states, i.e. states that are completely factorized between different spatial regions. The appropriate notion of “continuous deformation” is intuitive but somehow subtle from a mathematical point of view and it will be introduced later.
We imagine the spin systems to be equipped with some symmetry
G
that acts separately for each lattice site, with the most relevant example being rotation in spin space.
We study symmetry protected order, a concept introduced by Gu and Wen
[
20
]
and further elucidated by
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
,
[
48
]
,
[
45
]
. It means that we are interested in states that are trivial in the sense defined above, but the continuous deformation that trivializes them is possibly forbidden because it does not respect the
G
-symmetry.
This leads to a more restrictive equivalence relation between
G
-symmetric states: We say such states are
G
-equivalent if they can be continuously deformed into each other without breaking the symmetry. As it stands, two states can be inequivalent because the number of degrees of freedom per site is different, even if some of these degrees of freedom are trival.
To remedy this, one needs the notion of stacking. Two systems are stacked into one by taking on each site the tensor product of the Hilbert spaces associated to that site.
The correct notion of equivalence is now
stable
G
-equivalence. Two states are stably
G
-equivalent if they are
G
-equivalent after having been stacked with two trival states. In this way, the set of equivalence classes is equipped with a binary relation that can be shown to be a group. The equivalence classes are called SPT-states and they have physically interesting features that can be characterized by the behaviour at spatial boundaries. The best known example is the appearance of fractionalized spins at
0
-boundaries, which corresponds to the well-known
Haldane phase

[
22
,
21
]
.

As in many previous works on the subject, we will omit the Hamiltonian in our setup, and we focus on the states themselves. As such, the only input is the space dimension
d
and the symmetry group
G
. There are undisputed conjectures for the case
d=1,2
: One believes that the set of stable
G
-equivalence classes, equipped with the stacking relation, is isomorphic to the Borel cohomology group
H_{\text{Borel}}^{d+1}(G,U(1))
. For dimensions larger than 2, there are SPT phases that lie beyond this cohomology picture
[
5
,
55
]
, and there are proposals for generalized classifications
[
16
,
17
,
26
,
27
,
56
]
(see also
[
57
]
).

The classification of symmetry protected states of spin chains based on projective representations of groups was firstly discussed in
[
46
]
. Since then, rigorous topological indexes classifying finite symmetry group SPT states have been defined in
d=1

[
41
,
42
]
for states satisfying the split property
[
32
]
, and in
d=2
for invertible states
[
50
]
, and short-range entangled states
[
43
]
. If the symmetry group is finite and on-site, there is even a complete classification of SPT’s in
d=1

[
26
]
. In
[
14
]
, the authors classify symmetric matrix product states with respect to simple compact Lie groups. Recently,
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
-valued indices for 1d SPT states were obtained when the group
G
is a compact Lie group, and computed explicitly for the
SO(n)
groups
[
47
]
and for
G=SO(3)

[
53
]
.

The present paper extends these results by giving a full classification of 1d SPT states, where the symmetry group
G
is a topological group that is compact and metrizable. Our treatment follows
[
26
]
but we restrict the on-site Hilbert spaces to have a uniformly bounded dimension (as a function of site). This forces us to revisit several of the auxiliary results.
The construction of the map from SPT states to
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
proceeds essentially in the same way as in previous works. To show that this map is injective, we however have to develop a new idea, since the previous proofs relied in an essential way on the finiteness of the group
G
.

2.1
Algebras

A spin chain
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is defined in the standard way.
To any site
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we associate an
n_{j}
-dimensional on-site Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{j}
, with associated matrix algebra
\mathcal{A}_{j}
isomorphic to
M_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{C})
, the algebra of
n_{j}\times n_{j}
matrices with complex entries.
We assume that there is a
n_{\max}
such that
n_{j}\leq n_{\max}
.
The algebra
M_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{C})
is equipped with its natural operator norm and
*
-operation (Hermititian adjoint of a matrix) making it into a
C^{*}
-algebra. The spin chain algebra
\mathcal{A}
is the inductive limit of
algebra’s
\mathcal{A}_{S}=\otimes_{j\in S}\mathcal{A}_{j}
, with
S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}
, the finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
.
It comes naturally equipped with local subalgebra’s
\mathcal{A}_{X},X\subset\mathbb{Z}
. We refer to standard references
[
4
,
49
,
35
]
for more background and details.

We refer to such algebras as
chain
algebras, and we will not repeat anymore that we require them to have uniformly bounded dimensions
n_{j}
. Given a pair of chain algebra’s
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, we consider the
stacked
chain algebra

\mathcal{A}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}^{\prime},

defined as above, i.e. as the inductive limit, but now starting from the stacked on-site algebras
\mathcal{A}_{j}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\prime}
, which are simply defined as
\mathcal{A}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{j}

1
1

1

The stacked chain algebra
\mathcal{A}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
defined this way coincides, as a
C^{*}
-algebra, with the
C^{*}
-tensor product
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, with respect to the cross-norm (cf.
[
54
]
), which, by a result of
[
52
]
, is the unique compatible norm on the algebraic direct product
\mathcal{A}\odot\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. I.e., the unique norm by which the completion of
\mathcal{A}\odot\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, and such that
\norm{A \otimes A' }\leq\norm{A}\norm{A'}
.
, corresponding to the on-site Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{j}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{j}:=\mathcal{H}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\prime}
. The operation of stacking two spin chain algebra’s in this way is crucial for us, and for this reason we use the distinghuished notation
\tilde{\otimes}
.

Finally, we say that spin chain algebras
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are isomorphic whenever their local dimensions agree, i.e.
n_{j}=n^{\prime}_{j}
. In that case, we can pick unitaries
\mathscr{F}_{j}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{j},\mathcal{H}_{j}^{\prime})
and we can construct an isomorphism of
C^{*}
-algebra’s
\mathscr{F}
: For any
S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}
, we define the isomorphisms
\mathscr{F}_{S}:\mathcal{A}_{S}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{S}
by setting

\mathscr{F}_{S}:\otimes_{j\in S}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{F}_{j}}}\right)
. These definitions are consistent and they extend to an isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

2.2
Locally generated automorphisms and their generators

We introduce the framework to discuss time-evolutions.

2.2.1
Interactions

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the class of non-increasing, strictly positive functions
f:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, with
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, satisfying the fast decay condition
\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{p}f(r)=0
for any
p>0
.
An interaction is a collection of operators
H=(H(S))
labelled by
S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
H(S)=H(S)^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{S}
and

||H||_{f}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}:S\ni j}\frac{||H(S)||}{f(1+\mathrm{diam}(S))}

is finite for some
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Furthermore, we say an interaction
H
is
anchored
at a region
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
(or
X
-
anchored
) if

X\cap S=\emptyset\implies H(S)=0.

There is a related norm on
X
-anchored interactions

\displaystyle\norm{H}_{X,f}:=\begin{cases}\norm{H}_{f},\ &H\text{ is }X\text{-anchored },\\
\infty,\ &\ \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

2.2.2
Locally generated automorphisms

We will consider families of interactions
H(s)
parametrized by
s\in[0,1]
and call them ‘time-dependent interactions’ (TDIs) provided that they satisfy some regularity conditions to be formulated below. Since the risk of confusion is small, we will often denote them by
H
as well. A time-dependent interaction
H
is a TDI if there is
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that
s\mapsto H(s)
is
||\cdot||_{f}
bounded and strongly measurable
2
2

2

namely, it is the limit of a sequence of simple functions, pointwise almost everywhere, where the limit at any
s
is taken in
||\cdot||_{f}
-norm, see
[
13
]

. For such functions, we use the supremum norms

||H||_{f}=\sup_{s\in[0,1]}||H(s)||_{f}.

The role of a TDI
H
is to generate a family of
locally generated automorphisms (LGA’s)

(\alpha_{H,s})_{s\in[0,1]}
, namely the one-parameter family of strongly continuous
∗
-automorphisms
\alpha_{H,s}
on
\mathcal{A}
that should be viewed as a particular solutions of the Heisenberg evolution equations on
\mathcal{A}
:

\alpha_{H,s}(A)=A+i\int_{0}^{s}du\,\alpha_{H,u}([H(u),A]).

(2.1)

The expression
[H(u),A]
on the right-hand side is defined as
\sum_{S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}}[H(u,S),A]
and it is finite for
A
in a dense subset of
\mathcal{A}
.
The integral on the right hand side is to be understood in the sense of Bochner, and the strong measurability of the integrand follows from strong measurability of
s\mapsto H(s)
and the strong continuity of
s\mapsto\alpha_{H,s}
. The existence (let alone: uniqueness) of solutions to (
2.1
) is highly non-trivial.
Concretely,
\alpha_{H,s}(A)
is defined as the limit (in the topology of
\mathcal{A}
) of solutions of (
2.1
) where
H
is replaced with a finite volume restriction
H^{(\Lambda_{n})}
given by
H^{(\Lambda_{n})}(s,S)=\chi(S\subset\Lambda_{n})H(s,S)
for an increasing and absorbing sequence
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
. By a standard argument using the Lieb-Robinson bound
[
29
,
36
]
, this procedure is well-defined and the limit solves (
2.1
) for
A
in a dense set. We conveniently define the notation

\alpha_{H}:=\alpha_{H,1}.

(2.2)

We list a series of properties of LGA’s. Their proofs are standard, see e.g.
[
2
,
50
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H,\ H_{1},\ H_{2}
be TDI’s with finite
f
-norm. Then

(i)

((\alpha_{H,s})^{-1})_{s\in[0,1]}
is a family of LGA’s generated by a TDI with finite
\tilde{f}
-norm, for
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{F}
depending only on
f
,

(ii)

(
\alpha_{H_{1},s}\circ\alpha_{H_{2},s})_{s\in[0,1]}
a family of LGA’s generated by a TDI with finite
\tilde{f}
-norm, for
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{F}
depending only on
f
.

In the case where a TDI
H
is anchored in a finite set
X
, i.e.
||H||_{X,f}<\infty
,
the corresponding family of LGA’s
\alpha_{H,s}
has some nice and intuitive properties, making it similar to quantum dynamics in finite volume. Most notably,

\alpha_{H,s}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W(s)}}\right)

where the unitary
W(s)\in\mathcal{A}
solves the equation

W(s)=i\int_{0}^{s}duW(u)Z(u),\qquad W(0)=\mathds{1}

with

Z(u)=\sum_{S}H(s,S),

being a Hermitian element of
\mathcal{A}
because of the anchoring.
Moreover, both
Z(s),W(s)
satisfy good locality bounds, as we spell out in Appendix
A
.

2.3
States

States are normalized positive linear functionals on the spin chain algebra
\mathcal{A}
. A natural metric on states is derived from the Banach space norm

||\psi-\psi^{\prime}||=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A},||A||=1}|\psi(A)-\psi^{\prime}(A)|.

(2.3)

The set of states on
\mathcal{A}
is convex and its extremal points are called the pure states. A state
\psi
on a chain algebra
\mathcal{A}
will be, from now on, denoted by
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
. A distinguished class of pure states is that of spatial
product states

(\phi,\mathcal{A})
, i.e. states that satisfy

\phi(A_{j}A_{i})=\phi(A_{j})\phi(A_{i}),\qquad A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{j},A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i},i\neq j.

A pure product state
\phi
on
\mathcal{A}
is fully characterized by its on-site restrictions
\phi|_{\{j\}}
to
\mathcal{A}_{\{j\}}
. Each restriction
\phi|_{\{j\}}
is represented by a density matrix in
\mathcal{A}_{\{j\}}
, and purity of
\phi
implies the range of this density matrix is contained in a one-dimensional subspace
V_{j}\subset\mathcal{H}_{j}
. For each
j
, one can then choose
\xi_{j}\in V_{j}
such that
\phi|_{\{j\}}=\langle\xi_{j},(\cdot)\xi_{j}\rangle
. We will allow ourselves to use the formal notation

\displaystyle\phi=\bigotimes\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\xi_{j},(\cdot)\xi_{j}\rangle.

(2.4)

States
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
can be stacked into a state
(\psi\tilde{\otimes}\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
defined by

\psi\tilde{\otimes}\psi^{\prime}(A\tilde{\otimes}A^{\prime}):=\psi(A)\psi^{\prime}(A^{\prime}),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A},\ A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}.

2.3.1
Equivalence of states

We say that a pair of pure states
(\psi,\mathcal{A}),\ (\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A})
are equivalent if there exists a TDI
H
, as defined in Section
2.2.1
, such that

\psi^{\prime}=\psi\circ\alpha_{H}.

This relation is clearly reflexive. It is also symmetric and transitive, by Lemma
2.1
.

Definition 2.2
.

1.

A pure state
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
is called short-range entangled (SRE) if it is equivalent to a pure product state on the chain algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

2.

A pure state
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
is stably short-range entangled (stably SRE) if there exists a pure product state
(\phi,\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
such that the pure state
(\psi\tilde{\otimes}\phi,\mathcal{A}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
is SRE.

In some cases, we need to be more specific about the locality properties of a given state: An SRE (respectively, stably-SRE) state is called
f
-SRE (
f
-stably-SRE)
for some
f\in\mathcal{F}
whenever it can be produced from a product state via a TDI
H
satisfying

\norm{H}_{f}<\infty.

2.4
Symmetries

We consider topological groups
G
that are compact and metrizable (e.g. finite groups, compact Lie groups,…).
Given a chain algebra
\mathcal{A}
as above, we consider an on-site unitary
G
-action
U
which is defined as a collection

U=\{U_{j}(g),j\in\mathbb{Z},g\in G\},

where
U_{j}(g)\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{j})
and, for each
j
,
g\mapsto U_{j}(g)
is a continuous representation of
G
.
The unitary action
U
gives rise to a strongly continuous family of *-automorphisms
\beta_{g}^{X}
on
\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, in the following way: if
A\in\mathcal{A}_{S}
for
S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(X)
, then

\beta_{g}^{X}(A):=\bigotimes\limits_{j\in S}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{j}(g)}}\right)(A),

and this is extended to
\mathcal{A}_{X}
by linearity and density. Here, we used the notation
\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(B}}\right)(A)=BAB^{*}
. When
X=\mathbb{Z}
, we denote
\beta_{g}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\beta_{g}
.

We say that a state
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
is
G-
invariant if
\psi=\psi\circ\beta_{g}
for all
g\in G
. Obviously, this notion depends on the
G
-action
U
, which motivates the next definition and vocabulary.

2.4.1
G-states, G-product states and special G-product states

Definition 2.3

(G-states)
.

A
G
-state is a triple
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
, where

1.

\mathcal{A}
is a chain algebra,

2.

U
is an on-site unitary
G
-action, as defined above,

3.

\psi
is a pure
G
-invariant state on
\mathcal{A}
that is stably-SRE.

A
G
-state is a
G
-product state whenever it is a product state, as defined in section
2.3
.
G
-invariance with respect to the unitary on-site group action
U
implies each one-dimensional subspace
V_{j}
characterizing the
G
-product state (cf. Equation
2.4
) is actually a one-dimensional representation of
G
, transforming according to a continuous group homomorphism
q_{j}\in\hom(G,U(1))
(group homomorphisms between
G
and
U(1)
). Such a
q_{j}\in\hom(G,U(1))
will be henceforth referred to as the on-site
G
-charge.

Definition 2.4

(special G-product states)
.

A
G
-state
(\phi,\mathcal{A},U)
is a special
G
-product state if it is a
G
-product state such that all on-site
G
-charges are trivial. I.e.

q_{j}(g)=1,

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
g\in G
.

When stacking
G
-states, we need to stack as well the
G
-actions. This is done in an obvious way: If
U,U^{\prime}
are
G
-actions on
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, we let
U\tilde{\otimes}U^{\prime}
be the collection of on-site unitary representations
g\mapsto U_{j}(g)\tilde{\otimes}U^{\prime}_{j}(g)
.

2.4.2
G-equivariant TDIs

A TDI
H
is said to be
G
-equivariant if
\beta_{g}(H(s,S))=H(s,S),
for any
g\in G,\ s\in[0,1],\ S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
. If a TDI is
G
-equivariant, it generates a G-equivariant family of LGA’s. Namely, for each
s\in[0,1]
, the LGA
\alpha_{H,s}
satisfies:

\alpha_{H,s}\circ\beta_{g}=\beta_{g}\circ\alpha_{H,s},\qquad\forall g\in G.

Often, we will encounter rather trivial TDI’s. Consider for example a family of
G
-equivariant unitaries
(V_{I})_{I\in\mathcal{I}}
with
V_{I}\in\mathcal{A}_{I}
and the collection

\mathcal{I}
is a partition of
\mathbb{Z}
into discrete intervals
I
, such that their lenghts are bounded:
\sup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}|I|<\infty
.
In this case, the formal product
\otimes_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V_{I}}}\right)
is a well-defined
G
-equivariant LGA, since we can construct a
G
-equivariant Hermitian operator
H(I)
such that
V_{I}=e^{-iH(I)}
and
||H(I)||\leq\pi
. The corresponding TDI
H(s,I)=H(I)
(and
H(s,S)=0
whenever
S\not\in\mathcal{I}
), has finite
f
-norm, for any
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

2.5
G-Equivalence of G-states

We refine the equivalence relation introduced in Section
2.3.1
by taking symmetry into account.

Definition 2.5
.

Two
G
-states
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime},U^{\prime})
are G-equivalent whenever the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

For any site
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the on-site Hilbert spaces dimensions are equal,
n_{j}=n^{\prime}_{j}
.

2.

For any site
j
, there is a unitary
\mathscr{F}_{j}:\mathcal{H}_{j}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{j}
that intertwines
the
G
-actions:
\mathscr{F}_{j}U_{j}=U_{j}^{\prime}\mathscr{F}_{j}
.

3.

There is a TDI
H
on
\mathcal{A}
that is
G
-equivariant and such that the corresponding LGA connects the states

\psi^{\prime}\circ\mathscr{F}=\psi\circ\alpha_{H}

with

\mathscr{F}=\otimes_{j}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{F}_{j}}}\right)
as defined in subsection
2.1
.

To compare states that are not necessarily defined on isomorphic chain algebras, we introduce the notion of
G-stable equivalence
.

Definition 2.6
.

A pair of
G
-states
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime},U^{\prime})
are
G
-stably equivalent whenever there are special
G
-product states
(\phi,\mathcal{A}_{\phi},U_{\phi})
and
(\phi^{\prime},\mathcal{A}_{\phi^{\prime}},U_{\phi^{\prime}})
such that

(\psi\tilde{\otimes}\phi,\mathcal{A}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}_{\phi},U\tilde{\otimes}U_{\phi})\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\psi^{\prime}\tilde{\otimes}\phi^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\tilde{\otimes}\mathcal{A}_{\phi^{\prime}},U^{\prime}\tilde{\otimes}U_{\phi^{\prime}})

are
G
-equivalent.

2.6
Main result

The set of stably
G
-equivalence classes of
G
-states is denoted by
\text{SPT}_{G}
. It is equipped with a commutative binary relation given by stacking
\tilde{\otimes}
. It is clear that the class containing special
G
-product states acts as an identity element for this relation. Our result states that
\text{SPT}_{G}
is a group and it is isomorphic to the abelian group
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
(Mackey-Moore cohomology group
[
34
,
33
,
30
]
; see also
[
8
]
).

Theorem 2.7
.

Given a compact and metrizable topological group G, there is a group isomorphism between
\text{SPT}_{G}
and
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
.

This theorem differs from previous results mainly through its scope.
As already mentioned, the works
[
26
]
and
[
42
]
established a similar result for finite groups
G
, where the cohomology group
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
resumes to the standard abelian group cohomology group (also referred to as Eilenberg-MacLane cohomology group
[
8
,
15
]
). The inclusion of more general groups
G
allows to include some very natural cases, most notably
G=U(1)
or
G=SO(3)
. The overall strategy of our proof is very similar to the one in
[
26
]
and many of our intermediate results have direct analogues in
[
26
]
.
Our general setup differs from
[
26
]
(apart from the class of allowed groups
G
) in that we require the on-site dimension to be bounded along the chain, which feels more natural to us. This forces us in particular to take a different approach to the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
However, the most substantial difference between our treatment and that of
[
26
]
is in the proof of completeness of the classification in Section
5
. There we use a new idea that is based on properties of Schmidt vectors of the state in GNS representation. These properties are established in Section
4
.

3
The SPT index for G-states

In this section we define SPT indices for
G
-states. The construction is by now well-understood and we review it mainly for the sake of completeness. The fact that we allow for non-finite groups forces us anyhow to partially revisit the construction.

Since the theory of the SPT index for chains is intimately connected to projective representation, we have to recall some of that theory as well. We refer to
[
6
]
and
[
47
]
for more extensive treatments.

3.1
Projective representations

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space, and denote by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
the projective space topologized by the quotient topology. We denote by
p:\ \mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
the projection map.
The projective space
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
is a separable complete space, metrizable by the following distance function:

d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in p^{-1}(\mathbf{a})\\
b\in p^{-1}(\mathbf{b})\end{subarray}}\norm{\dfrac{a}{||a||} - \dfrac{b}{||b||}},\qquad\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}).

(3.1)

We also denote by
\mathscr{P}:\ \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}))
the continuous group homomorphism between the unitary group of
\mathcal{H}
, equipped with strong (or, equivalently: weak) operator topology, and the projective unitary group
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}))
, topologized by the so-called final topology for
\mathscr{P}
: the finest topology making
\mathscr{P}
continuous.
In this setting, the group homomorphism
\mathscr{P}
from
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
onto
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}))
is continuous. Furthermore, the following essential result holds:

Proposition 3.1

(Lemma 1 of
[
7
]
)
.

There exists a normalized Borel section
\Sigma
associated to
\mathscr{P}
. That is, there exists a measurable right-inverse
\Sigma:\ \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}))\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
for
\mathscr{P}
:

\mathscr{P}\circ\Sigma=\text{id}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}))}.

(3.2)

We are now ready to define the actual projective representations.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a topological group.

1.

A continuous unitary projective representation (CUP-rep) of
G
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})
is a continuous group homomorphism

\mathbf{U}:\ G\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})),

(3.3)

denoted as
(\mathbf{U},\mathcal{H})
.

2.

Given a measurable map
\mu:G\times G\to U(1)
, a Borel unitary
\mu
-multiplier representation (
u_{\mu},\mathcal{H}
) on
\mathcal{H}
is a measurable map

u_{\mu}:\ G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}),

(3.4)

such that
u_{\mu}(e)=\mathds{1}
and
u_{\mu}(g)u_{\mu}(h)=\mu(g,h)u_{\mu}(gh)
,
g,h\in G
, and
e
is the group unit.

Moreover, a Borel unitary
\mu
-multiplier representation
(u_{\mu},\mathcal{H})
of a group
G
is said to be a Borel lift of a CUP-rep
(\mathbf{U},\mathcal{H})
of
G
if

\mathbf{U}(g)=\mathscr{P}\circ u_{\mu}(g),\qquad g\in G,

(3.5)

namely, if the following diagram commutes:

G
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}))
\mathbf{U}
u_{\mu}
\mathscr{P}

Borel unitary multipliers are intimately connected to
CUP
-reps:

Lemma 3.3

(Proposition 2’ of
[
6
]
)
.

Every Borel unitary multiplier representation of a compact topological group
G
is the Borel lift of a
CUP
-rep and, conversely, every
CUP
-rep admits a Borel lift that is a Borel unitary multiplier representation of
G
.

Hence we can always associate a
CUP
-rep with a Borel multiplier representation of
G
and vice-versa. Particularly, by associativity of unitary operators on a Hilbert space, a Borel multiplier
\mu
associated to a CUP-rep satisfies the 2-cocycle condition:

\mu(g,h)\mu(gh,k)=\mu(g,hk)\mu(h,k),\qquad g,h,k\in G.

The pointwise product
\mu_{1}\cdot\mu_{2}
of two Borel multipliers is also a Borel multiplier. A Borel 2-coboundary is a Borel multiplier that can be written as

\mu(g,h)=\nu(g)\nu(h)/\nu(gh),\qquad g,h\in G,

where
\nu\in\hom(G,U(1))
is measurable. The second Borel cohomology group of
G
with indices in
U(1)
,

H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))

is the abelian group of equivalence classes of Borel 2-cocycles modulo Borel 2-coboundaries, with group structure given by
[\mu_{1}]\cdot[\mu_{2}]=[\mu_{1}\cdot\mu_{2}]
, where
[\mu_{1}]
and
[\mu_{2}]
are equivalence classes of 2-cocycles with representatives
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.
We refer to Appendix
B
for a general discussion on Borel group cohomology.

3.1.1
Equivalence of projective representations

In this section we discuss an equivalence relation for CUP-reps, which will then be classified modulo tensor products with linear representations of
G
. We say a CUP-rep
\mathbf{U}
of
G
on
\mathcal{H}
is a continuous linear representation if it has a Borel lift with trivial multiplier
\mu\equiv 1
. In this case, the lift can be chosen to be a strongly continuous unitary representation of
G

[
6
]
. We will also use the following remark:

Remark 3.4
.

A unitary
V\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
between separable Hilbert spaces induces a map
\widetilde{V}:\ \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
via

\widetilde{V}p(\xi):=p^{\prime}(V\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathcal{H},

(3.6)

where
p^{\prime}
denotes the projection from
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
. The linear map
\widetilde{V}
is injective, by definition, and surjective, as
V
is a unitary.

Definition 3.5
.

Two continuous unitary projective representations
(\mathbf{U},\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{U}})
and
(\mathbf{V},\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{V}})
are equivalent iff. there exist continuous linear representations
({\mathbf{U}^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\prime}}})
and
({\mathbf{V}^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}_{{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}})
and a unitary

{W}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{U}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{{\mathbf{U}^{\prime}}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{V}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}})

such that

\tilde{W}\circ\mathbf{U}\otimes{\mathbf{U}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{V}\otimes{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}\circ\tilde{W},

(3.7)

where
\tilde{W}
is the map induced by
W
, as in the previous Remark.

The above is an equivalence relation on the set of continuous unitary projective representations of
G
. We denote by
\text{CUP}_{G}
the abelian group of equivalence classes of CUP-reps with the group operation

[\mathbf{U}]\cdot[\mathbf{V}]:=[\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{V}].

The following classification Theorem holds:

Proposition 3.6
.

There is a group isomorphism between
\text{CUP}_{G}
and
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
.

For the proof, see Appendix
C
.

3.2
SRE states and CUP representations

We defer the full construction of the SPT index to Section
3.3
. For now, we construct a
CUP
-rep starting from an SRE
G
-state, that is, a
G
-state that is strictly SRE rather than stably SRE. This has been done by some authors before for discrete groups
[
26
,
44
]
, but some
care is needed for the extension to continuous groups, e.g. as in Lemma
3.9
. For split states, an index for compact Lie groups was defined in
[
47
]
, and it coincides with our definition. We make use of standard concepts like Galfand-Naimark-Segal (GNS) triple and we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
35
]
for their precise definitions and discussion.

3.2.1
Construction of a CUP-rep

We partition the chain in half-chains
\mathbb{Z}=L\cup R
with

L:=\{j<0\},R=\{j\geq 0\}
.
Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
be an
f
-SRE state with
\psi=\phi\circ\alpha_{H}
, with
\phi
a product state and
\alpha_{H}
an LGA.
The product state
\phi
has a GNS-triple of the form

(\mathcal{H}_{L,\phi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R,\phi},\pi_{L,\phi}\otimes\pi_{R,\phi},\Omega_{L}\otimes\Omega_{R}),

where
(\mathcal{H}_{L,\phi},\pi_{L,\phi},\Omega_{L,\phi})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{R,\phi},\pi_{R,\phi},\Omega_{R,\phi})
are GNS triples of the restrictions
\phi|_{L}
and
\phi|_{R}
, respectively. By a short calculation, it follows then that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R},\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R},\Omega)

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{H}_{L,\phi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R,\phi},\pi_{L,\phi}\circ\alpha_{H_{L}}\otimes\pi_{R,\phi}\circ\alpha_{H_{R}},\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R}(W^{*})\Omega_{L}\otimes\Omega_{R})

(3.8)

is a GNS triple for
\psi
, where
\pi_{L},\pi_{R}
are again representations of
\mathcal{A}_{L},\mathcal{A}_{R}
and

1.

\alpha_{H_{L}}
is an LGA on
\mathcal{A}_{L}
obtained by restricting
H
to the halfline
L
, i.e. setting
H_{L}(S)=0
whenever
S\not\subset L
. Similarly for
\alpha_{H_{R}}
.

2.

W\in\mathcal{A}
is a unitary satisfying (see Theorem
A.4
)

\alpha_{H}=\alpha_{H_{L}}\otimes\alpha_{H_{R}}\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W}}\right).

The existence of a factorized GNS representation as in (
3.8
) is known as the
split property
. The above remarks therefore show that SRE states enjoy the split property.
Since the states
\phi_{\Gamma}\circ\alpha_{H_{\Gamma}}
with
\Gamma=L,R
, are pure, the representations
(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma},\pi_{\Gamma},\Omega_{\Gamma})
are irreducible
[
3
]
, and
\pi_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma})^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
.
The following two lemmas are standard, see e.g.
[
4
]
, and
[
44
,
42
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
be an SRE
G
-state with GNS triple
(\mathcal{H}_{\psi},\pi_{\psi},{\Omega_{\psi}})
. Then there exists a strongly continuous unitary representation
(\mathcal{H}_{\psi},u)
of
G
, such that
u(g){\Omega_{\psi}}={\Omega_{\psi}}
and

\pi_{\psi}\circ\beta_{g}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{\psi},\qquad g\in G.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma},\pi_{\Gamma})
,
(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\Gamma},\pi^{\prime}_{\Gamma})
be irreducible representations of
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
, for
\Gamma=L,R
. If there is a unitary
w\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{R})
such that

\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(w}}\right)\circ\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R}=\pi^{\prime}_{L}\otimes\pi^{\prime}_{R},

(3.9)

then there exist unitaries
w^{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\Gamma})
such that

w=w^{L}\otimes w^{R},\qquad\pi^{\prime}_{\Gamma}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(w^{\Gamma}}}\right)\circ\pi_{\Gamma}.

(3.10)

By Lemma
3.7
, the G-invariance of an SRE
G
-state
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
implies the existence of unitaries
u(g)
implementing the symmetry. The group action on
\mathcal{A}
can be split between left and right, since it acts as a product on
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{R}
, i.e.
\beta_{g}=\beta_{g}^{L}\otimes\beta_{g}^{R}
. Therefore, we have

\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R}\circ\beta_{g}^{L}\otimes\beta_{g}^{R}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R}.

Hence, Lemma
3.8
gives a (non-unique) factorization

u(g)=u^{L}(g)\otimes u^{R}(g),

(3.11)

and, consequently,

\pi_{\Gamma}\circ\beta_{g}^{\Gamma}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{\Gamma}(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{\Gamma},

for
u^{\Gamma}(g)\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
.

The following lemma does not appear in the literature dealing with finite groups
G
, but it is crucial for us.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathscr{P}:\ \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{R})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{R}))
be as defined in section
3.1
. Given a splitting of the unitary group representation as in equation (
3.11
), the map

\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R}:\ G\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{R}))

(3.12)

is a continuous unitary projective representation of
G
.

Proof.

Recall that the quotient topology in
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}_{R})
is metrizable, and we can write

d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\inf\limits_{a\in\mathbf{a},\ b\in\mathbf{b}}\norm{\dfrac{a}{||a||} - \dfrac{b}{ ||b||}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[2(1-|\langle a,b\rangle|)}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the right-hand side does not depend on representatives
a,b
of
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
. Let
\mathbf{u}^{R}:=\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}d(\mathbf{u}^{R}(g)\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a})^{2}=1-|\langle u^{R}(g)a,a\rangle|,\qquad\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}_{R}).

(3.13)

By assumption of continuity of the on-site group action, it holds that
g\mapsto\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u(g)}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi_{\psi}(A)}}\right)
is continuous for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
, and that continuity extends to

g\mapsto\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u(g)}}\right)(B),

(3.14)

for any
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})
.
Indeed, the continuity extends to the strong closure of
\pi_{\psi}(\mathcal{A})
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})
. Since

\psi
is a pure state, the GNS representation is irreducible, and this strong closure equals
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})
.

We choose now vectors
{\Omega_{R}}\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
and
\Omega_{L}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
, and let
a=\pi_{R}(A_{R}){\Omega_{R}}\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
, for
A_{R}\in\mathcal{A}_{R}
. Then

\displaystyle|\langle a,u^{R}(g)a\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle a,\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R}(g)}}\right)(\outerproduct{a}{a})a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\otimes\Omega_{L},\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u(g)}}\right)\outerproduct{a}{a}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{L}}a\otimes\Omega_{L}\rangle

and by continuity of (
3.14
), it follows that the map

g\mapsto|\langle a,u^{R}(g)a\rangle|

is continuous. Since
\pi_{R}(\mathcal{A}_{R}){\Omega_{R}}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{R}
(by irreducibility of the representation
(\mathcal{H}_{R},\pi_{R})
), the continuity extends to any
a\in\mathcal{H}_{R}
. Along with (
3.13
), this proves continuity of the unitary projective representation
\mathbf{u}^{R}=\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R}
.

∎

3.2.2
An index for SRE G-states

In the previous section, we explained how to construct a CUP-rep
\mathbf{u}^{R}
from a SRE
G
-state
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
, and how to associate an element of
H^{2}
(which we henceforth use as an abbreviation for
H_{\text{Borel}}^{2}(G,U(1))
) to a
\mathbf{u}^{R}
. The construction of
\mathbf{u}^{R}
depends on the choice of a split GNS representation (via the choice of the LGA
\alpha_{H}
and the product state
\phi
). However, we now prove that the associated element of
H^{2}
does not depend on these choices:

Proposition 3.10
.

The element of
H^{2}
obtained as discussed above, is a well-defined function of the SRE G-state
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
. Moreover, it is stable with respect to
G
-equivariant LGA’s.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R},\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R},\Omega)
and
(\mathcal{H}_{L}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}^{\prime},\pi_{L}^{\prime}\otimes\pi_{R}^{\prime},{\Omega^{\prime}})
be two split GNS triples associated to the SRE G-state
\psi
. By uniqueness of GNS representation, there exists a unitary
W:\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}\to\mathcal{H}_{L}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}^{\prime}
such that
\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W}}\right)(\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R})=\pi_{L}^{\prime}\otimes\pi_{R}^{\prime}
and
W\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}
. As a consequence of Lemma
3.8
, it splits as

W=W_{L}\otimes W_{R},\qquad W_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma},\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{\prime}),\ \Gamma=L,R.

Let
\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R}
and
\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R^{\prime}}
be the
\text{CUP}_{G}
obtained from the split GNS triples above. It holds

\displaystyle\pi_{R}^{\prime}\circ\beta_{g}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{\prime}}(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{R}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W_{R}}}\right)\circ\pi_{R}\circ\beta_{g}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W_{R}u^{R}(g)W_{R}^{*}}}\right)\circ\pi^{\prime}_{R}.

It follows that
u^{R^{\prime}}(g)W_{R}u^{R}(g)W_{R}^{*}\in\pi_{R}(\mathcal{A}_{R})^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\mathds{1}_{R}
. Hence

u^{R^{\prime}}(g)=c(g)W_{R}u^{R}(g)W_{R}^{*},\qquad c(g)\in U(1),

which implies that
\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R^{\prime}}
is equivalent (see Definition
3.5
) to
\mathscr{P}\circ u^{R}
.

For the second part of the proof, let
\alpha_{H}
be a G-equivariant LGA. If
(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R},\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R},\Omega)
is a split GNS triple of
\psi
, the SRE
G
-state
(\psi\circ\alpha_{H},\mathcal{A},U)
has a split GNS triple of the form

(\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R},\pi_{L}\circ\alpha_{H_{L}}\otimes\pi_{R}\circ\alpha_{H_{R}},\pi_{L}\otimes\pi_{R}(W^{*})\Omega),

where
\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W}}\right)=\alpha_{H}\circ(\alpha_{H_{L}}\otimes\alpha_{H_{R}})^{-1}
, as in Theorem
A.4
. By using the
G
-equivariance of
\alpha_{H_{R}}
, we see that

\displaystyle\pi_{R}\circ\alpha_{H_{R}}\circ\beta_{g}

\displaystyle=\pi_{R}\circ\beta_{g}\circ\alpha_{H_{R}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R}(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{R}\circ\alpha_{H_{R}},

from which we conclude the
\text{CUP}_{G}
remains the same, since the group can be represented by the same unitary action
u^{R}
.
∎

We are now finally ready to define the SPT-index for SRE
G
-states.
Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
be a SRE
G
-state. Its index
\sigma^{(\text{SRE})}_{(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)}
is set to be the element of
H^{2}
corresponding (cf. Proposition
3.6
) to a CUP-rep (
3.12
) obtained from
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
. This is well-defined by virtue of Proposition
3.10
.

3.2.3
Properties of the index for SRE states

Given
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define infinite half-chains

L^{(j)}:=(-\infty,j)\cap\mathbb{Z},\qquad R^{(j)}:=[j,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Z}.

For any choice of
j
, we could perform the same construction as in Section
3.2
, replacing
R,L
by
R^{(j)},L^{(j)}
, and obtain an index in
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
. We now show that we would get the same index in this way.

Lemma 3.11
.

The index does not depend on the choice of
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
i<j
be two sites. The GNS representations
(\mathcal{H}_{L^{(i)}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R^{(i)}},\pi_{L^{(i)}}\otimes\pi_{R^{(i)}})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{L^{(j)}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R^{(j)}},\pi_{L^{(j)}}\otimes\pi_{R^{(j)}})
of
\psi
are unitarily equivalent, via a unitary
V\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{L^{(i)}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R^{(i)}},\mathcal{H}_{L^{(j)}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R^{(j)}})
, by uniqueness of the GNS representation. On the one hand,

\displaystyle\pi_{R^{(j)}}\circ\beta_{g}^{R^{(j)}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{(j)}}(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(j)}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{(j)}}(g)}}\right)\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}.

(3.15)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\pi_{R^{(j)}}\circ\beta_{g}^{R^{(j)}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}\circ\beta^{R^{(i)}}_{g}\circ\beta^{R^{(i)}\setminus R^{(j)}}_{g^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{(i)}}(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}\circ\beta^{R^{(i)}\setminus R^{(j)}}_{g^{-1}}.

(3.16)

Define, for each region
S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
,

U_{S}(g):=\bigotimes\limits_{j\in S}U_{j}(g),\qquad g\in G,

where
U_{j}(g)
is the on-site unitary group action. For an
h\in G
, we know
\beta_{h}^{R^{(i)}\setminus R^{(j)}}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{[i,j)}(h)}}\right)
. It follows that
W_{g}:=\pi_{R^{(i)}}(U_{[i,j)}(g))
is a unitary representation. Then, as a consequence of (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), it holds that

\displaystyle\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{(j)}}(g)}}\right)\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}(A)

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{(i)}}(g)}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}\circ\beta^{R^{(i)}\setminus R^{(j)}}_{g^{-1}}(A)

\displaystyle=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u^{R^{(i)}}(g)}}\right)\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W_{g}}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}(A),

for each
A\in\mathcal{A}_{R^{(i)}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(g^{-1})Vu^{R^{(i)}}(g)W_{g}}}\right)\circ\pi_{R^{(i)}}(A)=\pi_{R^{(i)}}(A),

which implies

\displaystyle V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(g^{-1})Vu^{R^{(i)}}(g)W_{g}\in\pi_{R^{(j)}}(\mathcal{A}_{R^{(j)}})^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\mathds{1},

(3.17)

where the equality follows from irreducibility of the GNS representation. Furthermore, it is clear that
[V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(g^{\prime})V,W_{g}]=0
for every
g,g^{\prime}\in G
. Let
\mu
be a Borel multiplier associated to
u^{R^{(j)}}
. Then

\displaystyle V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(g)VW_{g}V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(h)VW_{h}u^{R^{(j)}}(h)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(gh)VW_{gh}}}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=V^{*}u^{R^{(j)}}(g)u^{R^{(j)}}(h)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[u^{R^{(j)}}(gh)}}\right]^{-1}VW_{g}W_{h}W_{gh}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mu(g,h)\cdot\mathds{1},

since

is a linear representation. Hence

Lemma 3.12
.

The index respects the stacking operation. Namely, given two SRE

Proof.

The SRE

It is clear that

\lim_{M\to\infty}\alpha_{G_{0},s}\circ\alpha_{G_{\ell},s}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{G_{M\ell},s}(A),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}

(4.3)

exists and can be written as
\alpha_{G,s}(A)
where
\alpha_{G,s}
is a family of LGA’s generated by a TDI
G
satisfying
\norm{G}_{\tilde{f}}<\infty
, with
\tilde{f}
depending only on
f
.

This lemma is proven in
[
50
]
and used (implicitly) in
[
26
]
. It is interesting to note, for example, that the restriction to large
\ell
is crucial. Indeed, let
\delta_{n}
be the LGA that swaps operators at sites
n-1,n
and acts trivially on other sites. I.e.
\alpha_{n}(A_{n-1}\otimes B_{n})=B_{n-1}\otimes A_{n}
where
A_{j},B_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
are copies of a one-site observables
A,B
.
Then,

\delta_{0}\circ\dots\circ\delta_{M-1}\circ\delta_{M}(A_{M})=A_{0}

and therefore (
4.3
) cannot converge to an LGA when
\alpha_{j}=\delta_{j}
and
\ell=1
. However, if we set
\alpha_{j}=\delta_{j}
and
\ell=2
, then (
4.3
) does converge to an LGA. Indeed, the limit

\delta_{0}\circ\dots\circ\delta_{2M-2}\circ\delta_{2M}

is a trivial LGA as described in Section
2.4.2
.

To set the stage for the proof of the lemma, we first note by direct computation (see Lemma
2.1
) that
\alpha_{G_{0},s}\circ\alpha_{G_{\ell},s}\dots\circ\alpha_{M\ell,s}
is an LGA generated by the operator

Z_{M\ell}(s)+(\alpha_{G_{M\ell},s})^{-1}(Z_{(M-1)\ell}(s))+\dots+(\alpha_{G_{M\ell},s}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{G_{\ell},s})^{-1}(Z_{0}(s)).

where
Z_{j}(s)
is the time-dependent operator that generates the LGA
\alpha_{G_{j},s}
, i.e.
Z_{j}(s)=\sum_{S}G_{j}(s,S)
, see section
2.2.2
.
One needs hence to prove that this operator corresponds to a TDI that is bounded in some
\tilde{f}
-norm, uniformly in
M
.
Therefore, Lemma
4.3
follows from Lemma
4.4
below, which was proved in
[
50
]
.

Given a region
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
X_{r}
the
r
-fattening of
X
, defined by
X_{r}:=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ \text{dist}(j,X)\leq r\}
. In the following,

is the conditional expectation map, introduced in Proposition

.

Lemma 4.4
.

There is
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, if
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, the limit

\alpha(A):=\lim_{M\to\infty}\alpha_{G_{0},s}\circ\alpha_{G_{\ell},s}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{G_{M\ell},s}(A),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{X},\ X\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z}),

(4.4)

exists, and satisfies

\displaystyle\norm{\alpha(A) - \Pi_{X_n} \alpha(A) }\leq\norm{A}\tilde{f}(n),

for
\tilde{f}
depending only on
f
.

4.3
Locality of states

Short-range entangled states have a natural
f
-induced decay of correlations between distant observables, as stated below, since they are adiabatically connected to a zero correlation product state.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
be
f
-SRE, for
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Then

|\psi(AB)-\psi(A)\psi(B)|<\norm{A}\norm{B}\tilde{f}(\text{dist}(X,Y)),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{X},\ B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y},

(4.5)

for
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{F}
depending only on
f
, and
X,Y\subset\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward consequence of the Lieb-Robinson bounds
[
29
,
38
]
. Denote by
H
the TDI generating
\psi
from a product state
\phi
, and abbreviate
\alpha_{H}=\alpha
. Without loss, we can assume that
\psi(A)=\psi(B)=0
. Then

\displaystyle|\psi(AB)|=|\phi\circ\alpha(AB)|

\displaystyle\leq|\phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[{\alpha(AB)-\Pi_{X_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\alpha(A)}}\right]\Pi_{Y_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\alpha(B)}}\right]}}}\right]|

\displaystyle+|\phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Pi_{X_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\alpha(A)}}\right]}}\right]\cdot\phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Pi_{Y_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\alpha(B)}}\right]}}\right]-\phi(\alpha(A))\phi(\alpha(B))|,

where
\Pi
is the conditional expectation map of Proposition
A.2
. We choose
n=\text{dist}(X,Y)/3
so that the product of conditional expectations factorize in the product state, and the above inequality holds. The above can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle 2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\norm{A}\norm{\Pi_{Y_n} \alpha(B) - \alpha(B)}+\norm{B}\norm{\Pi_{X_n} \alpha(A) - \alpha(A)}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\norm{A}\norm{B}\tilde{f}(\text{dist}(X,Y)),

for some
\tilde{f}
depending only on
f
, where the last inequality follows from Proposition
A.2
.
∎

The correlation bound in Proposition
4.5
is stronger than the well-known exponential decay for ground states induced by the spectral gap of a Hamiltonian
[
23
,
37
]
, since the bound does not depend on the size of the support of the observables
A
and
B
. We need the following extension of Proposition
4.5
:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
be
f
-SRE, for
f\in\mathcal{F}
, with GNS triple
(\mathcal{H},\pi,\Omega)
. Then

|\langle\Omega,B\pi(A)\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega,B\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega,\pi(A)\Omega\rangle|<\norm{A}\norm{B}\tilde{f}(\text{dist}(X,Y)),

(4.6)

for every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B
in the strong closure of
\pi(\mathcal{A}_{Y})
, and
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{F}
depending only on
f
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\psi(A)=\langle\Omega,\pi(A)\Omega\rangle=0
. By assumption,
B
is a strong limit of a sequence
\{\pi(B_{n})\}_{n}
, for
B_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
, which, by the Kaplansky density theorem, can be chosen to satisfy
\sup_{n}\norm{B_n}=\sup_{n}\norm{\pi(B_n)}\leq\norm{B}
. The bound (
4.5
) yields then, uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

|\langle\Omega,\pi(B_{n})\pi(A)\Omega\rangle|\leq\norm{A}\norm{B}\tilde{f}(\text{dist}(X,Y)).

(4.7)

and hence the bound is inherited by the
n\to\infty
limit of the left-hand side, which is
|\langle\Omega,B\pi(A)\Omega\rangle|
.
∎

It will be crucial for us to connect SRE states via anchored TDI’s, whenever this is possible. The next definition provides a condition that enables this.

Definition 4.7
.

(Locality of states)
Given
f\in\mathcal{F}
, two pure states
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
and
(\phi,\mathcal{A})
are said to be
f
-close far from region
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
if

|\psi(A)-\phi(A)|<\norm{A}f(r)

(4.8)

holds for every

and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{(X_{r})^{c}}
.

We will exploit this definition in subsection
4.4
.

4.4
Mutually Normal G-States

A state
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
is said to be normal with respect to
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A})
whenever there is density matrix
\rho_{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\psi^{\prime}})
, with
\mathcal{H}_{\psi^{\prime}}
the GNS representation space of
\psi^{\prime}
, such that

\psi(A)=\Tr(\rho_\psi\pi_{\psi'} (A)),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}.

Moreover, if both states are pure, then there exists a
\xi_{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi^{\prime}}
, for which
\rho_{\psi}=\ket{\xi_\psi}\bra{\xi_\psi}
. Hence, normal pure states can be compared as vectors in a Hilbert space. The next proposition was proved in
[
3
]
, see also
[
25
,
2
]
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A})
be two pure SRE states. Assume they are
f
-close far from a finite region
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
, for some
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
\psi^{\prime}
is normal with respect to
\psi
.

Let us now include symmetry, by considering two mutually normal
G
-states
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A},U)
. By Lemma
3.7
, the
G
-action is implemented by a strongly continuous unitary representation
u(G)
on
\mathcal{H}_{\psi^{\prime}}
, such that the vector representant
\Omega_{\psi^{\prime}}
of
\psi^{\prime}
is invariant:
u(g)\Omega_{\psi^{\prime}}=\Omega_{\psi^{\prime}}
for
g\in G
. The state
\psi
, by assumption of pureness and normality, can be implemented as

\psi(A)=\langle\xi_{\psi},\pi_{\psi^{\prime}}(A)\xi_{\psi}\rangle,\qquad A\in\mathcal{A},

for some
\xi_{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi^{\prime}}
. Furthermore, its
G
-invariance

\displaystyle\psi\circ\beta_{g}(A)=\langle\xi_{\psi},u^{*}(g)\pi_{\psi^{\prime}}(A)u(g)\xi_{\psi}\rangle=\psi(A),

implies that
\xi_{\psi}
transforms one-dimensionally as
u(g)\xi_{\psi}=q[\psi/\psi^{\prime}](g)\xi_{\psi}
. The continuous one-dimensional representation
q[\psi/\psi^{\prime}]\in\hom(G,U(1))
defines the
relative 0-dimensional charge of
\psi
with respect to
\psi^{\prime}
. It is straightforward to establish the following properties of the
0
-dimensional charge (see
[
2
]
).

Proposition 4.9
.

For each ordered pair of mutually normal
G
-states
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A},U)
, the continuous
q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi/\psi^{\prime}}}\right]\in\hom(G,U(1))
, satisfies

i)

q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi/\psi}}\right]=1
,

ii)

if
(\tilde{\psi},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{U})
and
(\tilde{\psi^{\prime}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{U})
are also mutually normal, it holds that

q[\psi\tilde{\otimes}\tilde{\psi}/\psi^{\prime}\tilde{\otimes}\tilde{\psi^{\prime}}]=q[\psi/\psi^{\prime}]\cdot q[\tilde{\psi}/\tilde{\psi^{\prime}}].

0
-dimensional charges are not relevant for the
G
-equivalence relation because they are not preserved by stacking with
G
-product states, as we point out now:

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)
and
(\psi^{\prime},\mathcal{A},U)
be an ordered pair of mutually normal G-states, as in Proposition
4.9
. For any site
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are
G
-product states
(\phi^{(c)}_{\psi},\mathcal{A}^{(c)},U^{(c)})
and
(\phi^{(c)}_{\psi^{\prime}},\mathcal{A}^{(c)},U^{(c)})
, differing only on site
j
, such that

q[\psi\tilde{\otimes}\phi^{(c)}_{\psi}/\psi^{\prime}\tilde{\otimes}\phi^{(c)}_{\psi^{\prime}}]=1.

Proof.

Fix
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and define an on-site representation

\mathcal{H}_{j}^{(c)}:=\mathbb{C}e_{j}\oplus\mathbb{C}w_{j},

with

\displaystyle U_{j}^{(c)}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\overline{q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi/{\psi^{\prime}}}}\right]}\end{pmatrix}.

For
i\neq j
, let
\mathcal{H}_{i}^{(c)}:=\mathbb{C}e_{i}
be a trivial representation. We construct the chain algebra
\mathcal{A}^{(c)}
with on-site Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{k}^{(c)},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and we define the
G
-product states

\displaystyle\phi_{\psi}^{(c)}(A_{k}):=\langle e_{k},A_{k}e_{k}\rangle,\qquad A_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{\{k\}},

and

\displaystyle\phi_{\psi^{\prime}}^{(c)}(A_{k}):=\begin{cases}\langle w_{j},A_{k}w_{j}\rangle,&\qquad k=j,\\
\langle e_{k},A_{k}e_{k}\rangle,&\qquad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

One checks that
q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\phi_{\psi}^{(c)}/\phi_{\psi^{\prime}}^{(c)}}}\right]=\overline{q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi/{\psi^{\prime}}}}\right]}
. The proof of the lemma now follows from part (ii) of Proposition
4.9
.
∎

Nevertheless, one can easily check that the zero-dimensional charge is an obstruction to connecting SRE states via an anchored,

Proposition 4.11

Let

for some

. Hence the

Proof.

When
\psi
is a product state, this was proved in

\psi=\phi\circ\alpha_{H^{\prime}}
be an
f^{\prime}

Lemma 4.12

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle|\psi(A)-\psi^{\prime}(A)|<f(r)\norm{A},\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{(-\infty,-r]}\cup\mathcal{A}_{[r,\infty)},

\tilde{f}

depending only on

and

.

It can be checked, with the help of Theorem

\displaystyle\norm{\psi|_{[-r,r]^c} - \psi|_{(-\infty, -r)} \otimes\psi|_{(r,\infty)} }<f^{\prime}(r),\qquad r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

\displaystyle\norm{\psi|_{[-r,r]^c} - \psi'|_{[-r,r]^c}}

\displaystyle\leq 2f^{\prime}(r)+\norm{\psi|_{(-\infty,-r)} \otimes\psi|_{(r,\infty)} - \psi'|_{(-\infty,-r)} \otimes\psi'|_{(r,\infty)}}

4.5
Schmidt decompositions

An SRE state
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
has a split GNS representation (

) with respect to the bipartition
\mathbb{Z}=L\cup R
, which yields a tensor product structure
\mathcal{H}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
for the GNS Hilbert space. We consider the corresponding Hilbert-Schmidt decomposition

{\Omega_{\psi}}:=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}{\Omega^{L}_{n}}\otimes{\Omega^{R}_{n}}

(4.11)

of the GNS representant
\Omega_{\psi}
of
\psi
. Here,
\Omega_{n}^{L/R}
are orthonormal bases and
\lambda_{n}\geq 0
,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=1
. The Schmidt coefficients
\lambda_{n}
are ordered such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(\psi,\mathcal{A})
be an
f

for the first Schmidt coefficient

of
{\Omega_{\psi}}
, which depends only on the function
f
.

Proof.

In
[
26
]
, it was proven that

\sum\limits_{n>N}\lambda_{n}<\tilde{f}(\log N).

(4.12)

for
\tilde{f}
depending only on
f
.
The above bound and normalization of the reduced density matrix
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}=1

imply that
\lambda_{1}\geq{\dfrac{1-\tilde{f}(\log N)}{N}}
. Choose
N
large enough so that
1-\tilde{f}(\log N)>0
, which yields a strictly positive lower bound.
∎

Equation (

) implies that the restriction
(\psi|_{R},\mathcal{A}_{R})
of

to the halfline

\displaystyle\rho_{R}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\outerproduct{\Omega_n^R}{\Omega_n^R}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{R}),

via
\psi|_{R}(A)=\Tr(\rho_R \pi^R_\psi(A))
,

\rho_{R}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n_{m}}P_{m},

(4.13)

where

is a subsequence obtained from

by removing degeneracies, i.e.

and
n_{m}=\min\{n|\,n>n_{m-1},\lambda_{n}<\lambda_{n_{m-1}}\}
for

The spectral projectors are

P_{m}=\sum\limits_{n_{m}\leq n<n_{m+1}}\outerproduct{\Omega_n^R}{\Omega_n^R}.

Moreover, recall that Lemma
4.13
implies there is a (uniform in

) lower bound for the absolute value of
\lambda_{1}
. By the normalization of the density matrix
\Tr(\rho_R)=1
, this immediately implies

\text{dim}(\text{Range}(P_{1}))\leq 1/\lambda_{1}\leq k.

(4.14)

5.1
Approximation of G-states by Schmidt eigenvectors

The next proposition introduces a novel approach for the completeness proof when compared to

on

Proposition 5.1
.

Let the

\sigma_{(\psi,\mathcal{A},U)}=[1].

(i)

Let

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{J})\leq k<\infty

depending only on

.

(\mathcal{J},u^{R})

.

(ii)

If

|\langle\xi,\pi^{R}_{\psi}(A)\xi^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\xi|\xi^{\prime}\rangle\psi(A)|<\norm{A}\tilde{f}(r),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{[j+r,\infty)},

for

depending only on

Proof.

(i)
By Remark

(\mathcal{H}_{R},u^{R})

.
By a corollary (Corollary 9.5 of

]

\mathcal{H}_{R}
splits into an orthogonal countable direct sum of irreducible unitary finite-dimensional representations

\mathcal{H}_{R}=\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}.

(5.2)

Since
\rho_{R}
is G-invariant, each
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
is contained in
\text{Range}(P_{i})
for some

. In particular,
\mathcal{J}=\text{Range}(P_{1})

\text{dim}(\mathcal{J})\leq k<\infty

.

(ii)
Recall the Schmidt decomposition in section

{\Omega_{\psi}}:=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}{\Omega^{L}_{n}}\otimes{\Omega^{R}_{n}}.

By the result above,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\ldots=\lambda_{k_{0}}

We can expand

\xi^{\prime}_{L}

\xi^{\prime}
(Alternatively, we could have adjusted the Schmidt decomposition).
We now consider

\langle\Omega,B\otimes\pi_{R}(A)\Omega\rangle=\lambda_{1}\langle\xi^{\prime},\pi_{R}(A)\xi\rangle.

(5.3)

By the discussion in section

, we know
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{L})=\pi_{L}(\mathcal{A}_{L})^{\prime\prime}
.
We can then apply Corollary
4.6

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\tilde{f}(r)\norm{A}\norm{B}\geq|\langle\xi^{\prime},\pi_{R}(A)\xi\rangle-\langle\xi^{\prime},\xi\rangle\langle\Omega,\pi(A)\Omega\rangle|.

Due to the lower bound (

) converges strongly (i.e. applied to any
A\in\mathcal{A}
) to an LGA
\alpha_{H}
(cf. subsection
2.4.2
).
Since both factors are automorphisms (in particular, they have unit norm) and the composition of 2 LGA’s is again an LGA, it suffices to prove that the first factor converges strongly to an LGA in order to get strong convergence of the product to an LGA. Hence, we need to prove that there is an TDI
G
such that

\lim_{N}\prod\limits_{k=0}^{N}\alpha_{G_{k\ell}}(A)=\alpha_{G}(A)

This is true provided
\ell
is chosen large enough, as argued in Lemma
4.3
.

Appendix A
Locally Generated Automorphisms

A.1
\mathcal{F}
-functions

Locality properties of LGAs can be understood in terms of the Lieb-Robinson bound
[
29
]
, more recently formulated by
[
39
,
38
]
. This propagation bound was usually formulated in terms of
F
-functions. Given a 1d lattice
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, those are functions satisfying:

1.

F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is non-increasing;

2.

F
is uniformly integrable over
\Gamma
:

\sup\limits_{x\in\Gamma}\sum\limits_{y\in\Gamma}F(\text{dist}(x,y))\leq C_{F}^{\prime}<\infty;

3.

F
satisfies a convolution property:

\sum\limits_{z\in\Gamma}F(\text{dist}(x,z))F(\text{dist}(z,y))\leq C_{F}F(\text{dist}(x,y)),\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\Gamma.

Denote by
\mathcal{G}
the set of
F
-functions satisfying the above properties. Given an
F
-function, there is a corresponding norm
|||\cdot|||_{F}
on TDI’s
H
, via:

|||{H}|||_{F}:=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\sup\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{1}{F(\text{dist}(x,y))}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})\\
S\ni\{x,y\}\end{subarray}}\norm{H(S)(t)}.

(A.1)

Recall that
\mathcal{F}
is the class of non-increasing, strictly positive functions
f:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
that decay faster than any polynomial, as defined in section
2.2.1
.

Lemma A.1
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{F}
, there is an
F
-function
F\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}
such that

f(r)\leq F(r+1),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\norm{H}_{f}\leq|||H|||_{F}.

(A.2)

Conversely, for each
F
-function
F\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}
, there is
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that

F(r+1)\leq f(r),\qquad\text{and}\qquad|||H|||_{F}\leq\norm{H}_{f}.

(A.3)

Proof.

The proof follows closely the proof of Lemma 7 in
[
1
]
, except that the authors consider functions with streched exponential decay instead of superpolynomial decay. For that reason, given
f\in\mathcal{F}
, one can construct an
F
-function

F(r):=\min\limits_{r^{\prime}\leq r}\tilde{F}(r^{\prime})

(A.4)

where

\tilde{F}(r):=\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(r),\dfrac{1}{f(1)+1}\sum\limits_{m=1,\dots,r-1}F(r-m)F(m)}}\right],

(A.5)

which satisfies all the necessary axioms and inherits the fast decay from
f
. The proof then follows from the reference.
∎

A.2
Conditional expectations

Recall that, given a region
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, the set
X_{r}
is the
r
-fattening of
X
, defined by
X_{r}:=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ \text{dist}(j,X)\leq r\}
. The next proposition and theorem are consequences of the Lieb-Robinson bound
[
29
,
38
]
, which can be now applied in our setting, due to the previous Lemma
A.1
.

Proposition A.2
.

Assume
\norm{H}_{f}<\infty
for some
f\in\mathcal{F}
. For each
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a linear map

\Pi_{X_{n}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{X_{n}}

satisfying

\norm{\alpha_H (A) - \Pi_{X_n} (\alpha_H(A))}<\norm{A}\tilde{f}(n),

(A.6)

for
\tilde{f}
depending only on
f
.

Proof.

The Lieb-Robinson bound implies
\alpha_{H}
is a
quasi-local map
, satisfying:

\norm{[\alpha_H(A),B]}<\norm{A}\norm{B}\tilde{f}(\text{dist(X,Y)}),\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{X},\ B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}.

(A.7)

The result then follows by Corollary 4.4. of
[
39
]
.
∎

A.3
Anchored TDI’s

For a TDI
H
satisfying
\norm{H}_{X,f}<\infty
for a finite set
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
, the sum

Z(s):=\sum\limits_{S\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})}H(S)(s)

converges to an Hermitian element of
\mathcal{A}
. An even stronger result holds:

Proposition A.3
.

Let
H
be such that
\norm{H}_{X,f}<\infty
, for a finite
X\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})

||Z(s)-\Pi_{X_{r}}Z(s)||\leq\tilde{f}(r)\norm{Z(s)},

(A.8)

for
\tilde{f}
depending only on
f
, and
\Pi
is the map from Proposition

Proof.

The proof follows from Corollary 4.4 of

\norm{ \left[ Z(s), A \right] }<\norm{A}\norm{Z(s)}\tilde{f}(\text{dist}(X,Y)),\ A\in\mathcal{A}_{Y},
for
X\cap Y=\emptyset
. This follows from direct computation (cf. in Lemma 5.5 of
[
2
]
).
∎

As a consequence of Proposition

, the solution

W(s)=i\int_{0}^{s}duW(u)Z(u),\qquad W(0)=\mathds{1},

satisfies

||W(s)-\Pi_{X_{r}}W(s)||\leq\tilde{f}(r),

(A.9)

uniformly in

depending only on

Theorem A.4
.

Let
\alpha_{H}
be an LGA generated by TDI
H
satisfying

\norm{H}_{f}<\infty,\ f\in\mathcal{F}.

Consider a bipartition
L=L^{(j)},R=R^{(j)}
of the lattice. There is a decomposition

\alpha_{H}=\alpha_{H_{L}}\otimes\alpha_{H_{R}}\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W}}\right)

(A.10)

where

(respectively,

(

\norm{WAW^* - A}\leq\norm{A}\tilde{f}(\text{dist }X,j),\qquad A\in\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}_{X},

(A.11)

for

Proof.

We can define

Similarly for

Appendix B
Borel cohomology

Let
G
be a topological group equipped with Haar measure. For any left topological G-module
M
, we denote by
C^{n}(G,M)
the set of measurable functions from
G\times\dots\times G\to M
, where the product runs over
n
copies of
G
, with respect to the Borel sigma algebras in both
G\times G\times\dots\times G
and
M
. Elements of
C^{n}(G,M)
are called
n-
(measurable) cochains. The pair
(C^{n}(G,M),\cdot)
is a group for every
n
, so that co-boundary group homomorphisms can be defined as

\displaystyle d^{n+1}:\ C^{n}(G,M)

\displaystyle\to C^{n+1}(M,G)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\mapsto(d^{n+1}\mu),

given explicitly by

\displaystyle(d^{n+1}\mu)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n+1}):=

\displaystyle\mu(g_{2},\dots,g_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\mu(g_{1},\dots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\dots,g_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\mu(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}),

(B.1)

where we are using additive notation for the abelian module operation. The map

d^{n+1}\circ d^{n}:\ C^{n}(G,M)\to C^{n+2}(G,M)

(B.2)

is identically zero, from which we conclude there is a cochain complex

\dots\leftarrow C^{n}\leftarrow C^{n-1}\leftarrow\dots,

with
n
-th (Borel) cohomology group (cf.
[
34
,
51
]
) defined as

H^{n}_{\text{Borel}}(G,M):=\dfrac{Z^{n}(G,M)}{B^{n}(G,M)},

(B.3)

with

\displaystyle Z^{n}(G,M):=\ker(d^{n+1}),

\displaystyle B^{n}:=\begin{cases}0,\ &\text{if }n=0,\\
\text{im}(d^{n}),\ &\text{if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Hence the n-th Borel cohomology group (Mackey-Moore cohomology group, cf.
[
8
]
) is a group of equivalence classes of n-cocycles (elements of
Z^{n}(G,M)
) with respect to equivalence module n-coboundaries (elements of
B^{n}(G,M)
).

Remark B.1
.

If
G
is a finite group, Borel cohomology resumes to standard finite group cohomology, when
G
is given the discrete topology. If G is a compact Lie group, the 2-nd Borel cohomology group
H^{2}_{\text{Borel}}(G,U(1))
is a finite group
[
34
]
.

Remark B.2
.

SPT-phases in 1d are classified by equivalence classes of projective representations of
G
, which in turn are classified by the second Borel cohomology group. We then explicitly write the cocycle condition for
n=2
: let
c:\ G\times G\to U(1)
be an arbitrary cochain. Then, by definition (
B.1
):

(d^{3}\mu)(g,h,k)=\mu(h,k)-\mu(gh,k)+\mu(g,hk)-\mu(g,h).

Since 2-cocycles are elements of
\ker{d^{3}}
, they consequently satisfy:

\mu(h,k)+\mu(g,hk)=\mu(gh,k)+\mu(g,h).

(B.4)

Classification of CUP-reps

Lemma C.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{V},\mathcal{H})
be a continuous unitary projective representation of
G
, and assume
\mu
and
\nu
are Borel multipliers associated to two different lifts. Then they are related via a Borel coboundary.

Proof.

Let
v_{\mu}
and
v_{\nu}
be two Borel lifts. For each
g\in G
, it holds that

\mathscr{P}(v_{\mu}(g)v_{\nu}(g)^{-1})=1,

since
\mathscr{P}
is a group homomorphism. This implies
v_{\mu}(g)v_{\nu}(g)^{-1}=\lambda(g)
for some
\lambda(g)\in U(1)
. Immediately,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}

\displaystyle=\mu(g,h)\nu(g,h)^{-1}v_{\mu}(g)v_{\mu}(h)v_{\mu}(gh)^{-1}v_{\nu}(gh)v_{\nu}(h)^{-1}v_{\nu}(g)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mu(g,h)\nu(g,h)^{-1}\lambda(gh)^{-1}\lambda(g)\nu(h)\cdot\mathds{1}.

Furthermore, the map
\lambda\cdot\mathds{1}
is measurable, since it is a composition of measurable maps.
∎

Thus the map
\kappa
from
\text{CUP}_{G}
to

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Let
\kappa([\mathbf{U}])=[\mu]
and
\kappa([\mathbf{V}])=[\nu]
. Then it holds that, for representatives
U
and
V
, the Borel representation
U\otimes V
has multiplier
\mu+\nu
. Hence
\kappa([\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{V}])=[\mu+\nu]=[\mu]+[\nu]
, and
f
is a group homomorphism.

Furthermore, each cohomology class can be realized as a finite dimensional continuous projective representation of

(proof of Lemma

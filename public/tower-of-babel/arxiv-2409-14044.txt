1.1.
Orthonormal Strichartz estimates

For a measure space
(X,\mu)

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\leq C\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\|_{\ell^{\lambda}},

The motivation to investigate generalizations of fundamental estimates to such orthonormal estimates of the form (

A system of

Note that the idea of generalizing classical inequalities from a single-function input to an orthonormal family is not a new topic, and the first pioneering work of this kind of generalization can be traced back to the work of Lieb-Thirring
[
24

1.2.
Orthonormal smoothing estimates

Understanding how geometry affects the behavior of solutions to the nonlinear evolution of partial differential equations is a key problem in the field of analysis. As we discussed earlier, the Strichartz inequality and the smoothing property are the two most stable methods used in the analysis of nonlinear dispersive equations. Strichartz inequality often refers to mixed
L^{p}-L^{q}
space-time norm estimates of the solution, while smoothing properties are Sobolev
L^{2}
space-time estimates. More precisely, the Strichartz estimate provides the space-time control of solutions while the smoothing estimate allows us to gain derivatives of solutions on any bounded domains. Typically one employs smoothing estimates to overcome the loss of derivatives caused by the semilinear terms from the nonlinearity. Nevertheless, both approaches entirely rely on global analysis for solutions to the corresponding linearized equations. Exploring different smoothing properties of dispersive-wave equations has been greatly enhanced by the discovery of the local Kato-smoothing property. This has led to an improvement in the mathematical theory of nonlinear dispersive-wave equations, specifically the well-posedness of their Cauchy problems in low regularity spaces.

Consider the class of dispersive Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u-iP(D)u=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\in\mathbb{R},\\

where

One of the classical problems in harmonic analysis is the so-called restriction problem. The classical restriction problem is the following: Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 2
be a hypersurface endowed with its
(d-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
d\mu,
then one may ask the following: For what exponents
1\leq p\leq 2
, do we have the inequality

\int_{S}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\mu(\xi)\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}?

(1.11)

Here
\hat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform for a Schwartz class function
f

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is defined as

\hat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The restriction phenomena were first introduced by E. M. Stein for studying the boundedness of the Fourier transform of an
L^{p}
-function in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\geq 2
. It has a delicate connection to many other conjectures, notably the Kakeya and Bochner-Riesz conjectures. Furthermore, from
[
38
]
we see that it is also closely related to that of estimating solutions to linear PDE such as the wave and Schrödinger equations.

Let
R_{S}
be the operator defined as
R_{S}f=\hat{f}|_{S}

1.11

R_{S}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(S).
We also denote the operator
E_{S}
(as the dual of the restriction operator
R_{S}
) is defined as

E_{S}f(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{S}f(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}d\mu(\xi),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for
f\in L^{1}(S).

If

then by duality, (

\displaystyle\|E_{S}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(S)}?

(1.12)

asserts that the restriction conjecture (

For quadratic surfaces such as paraboloid-like, cone-like, or sphere-like, Strichartz gave a complete characterization depending on surfaces in

We can see that (

a (possibly infinite) orthonormal system in

for some

independent of

and the value of

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Schatten class

In this subsection, we recall the definition of Schatten spaces. For a detailed study of these spaces, we refer to see
[
32
]
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex and separable Hilbert space. Let
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a compact operator and
T^{*}
denote the adjoint of
T
. The singular values of
T
are the non-zero eigenvalues of
|T|:=\sqrt{T^{*}T}
, which form an at most countable set denoted by
\{s_{j}(T)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. For
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
, the Schatten space
\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as the space of all compact operators
T
on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}(T)^{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}<\infty.

Then the space
\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})
is a Banach space endowed with the Schatten
\lambda
-norm defined by

\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{H})}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}(T)^{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}},\,\forall~T\in\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{H}).

In general, we denote the space
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
, as the space of the compact operators rather than the bounded operators
but the norm is the same as for the bounded operators. In other words, for
T\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
, we denote
\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})}
as the operator norm of
T
. Moreover, we have the property that
\left(\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H})\right)^{*}=\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
and
\left(\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathcal{H})\right)^{*}=\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
is the space of all bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Note that, this property is different from the
L^{p}
spaces. In particular, when
\lambda=1
, an operator belonging to the space
\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
is known as a trace class operator; when
\lambda=2
, an operator belonging to the space
\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathcal{H})
is known as a Hilbert-Schmidt operator. In the proof of our Strichartz estimates for orthonormal systems of initial data, we will see that Schatten spaces arise naturally via duality argument.

2.2.
Complex interpolation in Schatten space

Let
a_{0},a_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a_{0}<a_{1}
. A family of operators
\{T_{z}\}
on
X\times\mathcal{I}
defined in a strip
a_{0}\leq Re(z)\leq a_{1}
in the complex plane is analytic in the sense of Stein if it has the following properties:

(1)
For each
z:a_{0}\leq Re(z)\leq a_{1}
,
T_{z}
is a linear transformation of simple functions on
X\times\mathcal{I}
.

(2)
For all simple functions
F,G
on
\mathcal{I}\times X
, the map
z\rightarrow\langle G,T_{z}F\rangle
is analytic in
a_{0}<Re(z)<a_{1}
and continuous in
a_{0}\leq Re(z)\leq a_{1}
.

(3)
Moreover,
\sup_{a_{0}\leq\lambda\leq a_{1}}\left|\langle G,T_{\lambda+is}F\rangle\right|\leq C(s)
for some
C(s)
with at most a (double) exponential growth in
s
.

The following complex interpolation result in Schatten space will be used frequently in order to prove our main results. For a detailed study on complex interpolation theory, one can see
[
10
, Proposition 1]
,
[
32
, Theorem 2.9]
, and
[
29
, Theorem 2.26]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{T_{z}\}
be an analytic family of operators on
X\times\mathcal{I}
in the sense of Stein defined in the strip
a_{0}\leq Re(z)\leq a_{1}
for some
a_{0}<a_{1}
. If there exist
M_{0},M_{1},b_{0},b_{1}>0
,
1\leq p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
, and
1\leq r_{0},r_{1}<\infty
such that for all simple functions
W_{1},W_{2}
on
X\times\mathcal{I}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|W_{1}T_{a_{0}+is}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{r_{0}}\left(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\right)}\leq M_{0}e^{b_{0}|s|}\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{0}}(X))}\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{0}}(X))}

and

\displaystyle\left\|W_{1}T_{a_{1}+is}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{r_{1}}\left(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\right)}\leq M_{1}e^{b_{1}|s|}\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}}(X))}\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}}(X))}.

Then for all
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have

\left\|W_{1}T_{a_{\theta}}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{r_{\theta}}\left(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\right)}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{L^{q_{\theta}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{\theta}}(X))}\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{L^{q_{\theta}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{\theta}}(X))},

where
a_{\theta},r_{\theta},p_{\theta}
and
q_{\theta}
are defined by

a_{\theta}=(1-\theta)a_{0}+a_{1},\;\frac{1}{r_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}},\;\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\;\text{and}\;\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}.

Also, when one of the exponents
r_{j}=\infty
, for
j=0
or
j=1
, then the
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}
-norm on the left side of the two previous bounds can be replaced by the usual operator norm.

2.3.
Duality principle

In order to deduce estimates from a single function to an orthonormal system, the duality principle lemma in Schatten spaces plays a crucial role in our context. We refer to
[
10
, Lemma 3]
with appropriate modifications to obtain the following duality principle.

Lemma 2.2

(Duality principle)
.

Assume that
A
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(X)
to
L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X))
for some
1\leq p,q\leq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
W\in L^{\frac{2q}{2-q}}(\mathcal{I},L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(X))

\big\|WAA^{*}\overline{W}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))}\leq C\big\|W\big\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{2-q}}(\mathcal{I},L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(X))}^{2},

where the function
W
is interpreted as an operator which acts by multiplication.

(2)
There is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that for any orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}

\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|Af_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{2}}(\mathcal{I},L^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{2}}(X))}\leq C^{\prime}\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\|_{\ell^{\lambda^{\prime}}}.

We can also extend the duality principle in Lorentz spaces with trivial
modifications as given in
[
4
]
. Before that, first, we recall the definition of Lorentz spaces on the measure space
(X,\mu)
. For a function
f
on
X
, the decreasing rearrangement of
f
is the function
f^{*}
defined on
[0,\infty)
by

f^{*}(s)=\inf\{\alpha>0:d_{f}(\alpha)\leq s\},

where

d_{f}(\alpha)=\mu(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\alpha\}).

Then for
0<p,q\leq\infty,
we denote the space
L^{p,q}(X,\mu)
consist all such measurable functions on
X
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}<\infty
, where

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(s^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(s))^{q}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\text{if}~~q<\infty,\\
\sup_{s>0}s^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(s),\hskip 39.83368pt\text{if}~~q=\infty.\end{cases}

One can observe that
L^{p,p}=L^{p}
and
L^{\infty,\infty}=L^{\infty}
. The following remark is the duality principle in Lorentz spaces.

Remark 2.3
.

Assume that
A
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(X)
to
L^{q^{\prime},r^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X))
for some
1\leq p,q,r\leq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
W\in L^{\frac{2q}{2-q},\frac{2r}{2-r}}(\mathcal{I},L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(X))

\big\|WAA^{*}\overline{W}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))}\leq C\big\|W\big\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{2-q},\frac{2r}{2-r}}(\mathcal{I},L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(X))}^{2}.

(2)
There is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that for any orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}

\bigg\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|Af_{j}|^{2}\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{2},\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}}(\mathcal{I},L^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{2}}(X))}\leq C^{\prime}\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\|_{\ell^{\lambda^{\prime}}}.

3.
Strichartz inequality for a single function

In this section, we establish the Strichartz inequality in Lebesgue spaces as well as in Lorentz spaces for a single function.

Consider the Cauchy problem for Schrödinger equation associated with
L
as

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(x,t)+Lu(x,t)=F(x,t)\\
u(0,t)=f(x).\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then, by Duhamel’s principle, the solution of the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) is formally given by

u(x,t)=e^{itL}f(x)-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}F(x,s)ds.

The following result is known as Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger equation associated with
L
on
X.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
and satisfies Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Then for any
p_{i},q_{i}\geq 2

(i=1,2)
such that

\frac{2}{q_{i}}+\frac{n}{p_{i}}=\frac{n}{2},

except for
(q_{i},p_{i},n)=(2,\infty,2)
,
we have

(1)
(The homogeneous Strichartz estimate)

\|e^{itL}f\|_{L^{q_{1}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}}(X)\right)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

(2)
(The dual homogeneous Strichartz estimate)

\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q_{1}^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(X)\right)}.

(3)
(The retarded Strichartz estimate)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{2}}(X)\right)}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q_{1}^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(X)\right)}.

The procedure to prove the above theorem is classical and some good references are given, for example, by Ginibre-Velo
[
13
]
and Keel-Tao
[
18
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we just provide the main steps of the proof in Section
8
for the non-endpoints Strichartz estimates. Indeed, we can also prove endpoints Strichartz estimates following procedures as given in
[
18
]
.

Remark 3.2
.

One of the key ingredients to obtain Strichartz estimates is the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality for Lebesgue functions. However, in order to prove Strichartz inequality in
Lorentz space, we need a refined version of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality for Lebesgue functions. From
[
30
]
, we have the following Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in
Lorentz space:

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},p_{2},r_{1},r_{2}\in(1,+\infty)
such that

\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\sigma=2~~\text{ and }~~\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}\geq 1,

then

\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\frac{g(t)h(s)}{|t-s|^{\sigma}}dsdt\right|\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{q_{1},r_{1}}(\mathcal{I})}\|h\|_{L^{q_{2},r_{2}}(\mathcal{I})}.

Now applying the same procedure as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
with the Littlewood-Sobolev
inequality in Lorentz space, we have the following Strichartz estimates for functions in Lorentz spaces.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
and satisfies Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Then for any
q,p,r\geq 2
such that

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=\frac{n}{2},

except for
(q,p,n)=(2,\infty,2)
, then

\|e^{itL}f\|_{L^{q,r}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
, for
f\in L^{2}(X)
and
F\in L^{q_{2}^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(X)\right)
, the solution
u
of the solution of the Schrödinger equation (
3.1
) belongs to
L^{q_{2}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{2}}(X)\right)
and satisfies

\|u\|_{L^{q_{2}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{2}}(X)\right)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|F\|_{L^{q_{2}^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(X)\right)}.

4.
Strichartz inequalities for system of orthonormal functions

This section is devoted to extending Theorem
3.1
part (1) and Theorem
3.3
for a single function to a system of orthonormal functions. Note that
Af(x,t)=e^{itL}f(x)
is the solution to the linear Schrödinger equation (
3.1
). Also, the solution
Af(x,t)=e^{itL}f(x)
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}(X)
to
L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))
and its adjoint operator
A^{*}
, from
L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X))
to
L^{2}(X)
is defined by

A^{*}F(x)=\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}F(x,s)ds.

We define
\mathcal{T}=AA^{*}
by

\mathcal{T}F(x,t)=\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{i(t-s)L}F(x,s)ds=\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{X}K_{i(t-s)}(x,y)F(y,s)d\mu(y)ds.

Then we have the following result regarding the boundedness of
\mathcal{T}
in Schatten spaces.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
satisfying
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Then for any
p,q\geq 1
such that

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=1\quad\text{and}\quad n+1<p\leq n+2,

we have

\big\|W_{1}\mathcal{T}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))}\leq C\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))},

for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))
, where
C>0
is independent of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we define

T_{\varepsilon}F(x,t)=\int_{X}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,t-s)F(y,s)dsd\mu(y),

where
\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)=\chi_{\varepsilon<|t|<T_{0}}(t)K_{it}(x,y)
. Then for any
t\in\mathcal{I}
, it follows from
(\mathcal{B})
that

\sup_{x,y\in X}|\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)|\lesssim|t|^{-\lambda_{0}},\;\text{where}\;\lambda_{0}=\frac{n}{2}.

(4.1)

To use Stein’s analytic complex interpolation, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re(z)\in[-1,\lambda_{0}]
, we define

\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,y,t)=t^{z}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)

and define the corresponding operator as

T_{z,\varepsilon}F(x,t)=\int_{X}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,y,t-s)F(y,s)dsd\mu(y).

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\{T_{z,\varepsilon}\}_{z}
forms an analytic family of operators in the sense of Stein (see
Subsection
2.2
). Moreover, from (
4.1
), we can deduce that

\sup_{x,y\in X}|\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,y,t)|\lesssim|t|^{Re(z)-\lambda_{0}},\;\forall t\in\mathcal{I}.

(4.2)

Now keeping in mind that the
\mathfrak{S}^{2}
-norm is the usual Hilbert-Schmidt norm, using the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in Lebesgue spaces along with (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\big\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}}\int_{X\times X}|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,y,t-s)|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}d\mu(x)d\mu(y)dtds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}}\int_{X\times X}\frac{|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2\lambda_{0}-2Re(z)}}d\mu(x)d\mu(y)dtds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{I}}\frac{\|W_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\|W_{2}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}}{|t-s|^{2\lambda_{0}-2Re(z)}}dtds

\displaystyle\lesssim\|W_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2\tilde{u}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)\right)}\|W_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2\tilde{u}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)\right)},

provided that
0\leq 2\lambda_{0}-2Re(z)<1
and
\frac{2}{\tilde{u}}+2\lambda_{0}-2Re(z)=2
. If we write
u=2\tilde{u}
, then
\frac{1}{u}\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}]
and

\big\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))}\lesssim\|W_{1}\|_{L^{u}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)\right)}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{u}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)\right)},

(4.3)

provided that
\frac{1}{u}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{0}-Re(z))
and
Re(z)\in(\frac{2\lambda_{0}-1}{2},\lambda_{0}]
.

On the other hand, for
Re(z)=-1
, our aim is to show that the operator
T_{z,\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))
is bounded with some constant depending only on
n
and
Im(z)
exponentially. In fact, we can write

\displaystyle T_{z,\varepsilon}F(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,y,t-s)F(y,s)dsd\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon<|t|<T_{0}}s^{-1+iIm(z)}ds\int_{X}K_{is}(x,y)F(y,t-s)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon<|t|<T_{0}}s^{-1+iIm(z)}e^{isL}F(x,t-s)ds.

Since
e^{-itL}
is an unitary operator on
L^{2}(X)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(X)}

\displaystyle=\|e^{-itL}T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(X)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{\varepsilon<|t|<T_{0}}s^{-1+iIm(z)}G(\cdot,t-s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}(X)},

where
G(x,t)=e^{-itL}F(x,t)
and

\|G(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(X)}=\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

(4.4)

If we define

H_{z,\varepsilon}:h(t)\mapsto\int_{\varepsilon<|t|<T_{0}}s^{-1+iIm(z)}h(t-s)ds,

then we have

\|T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))}=\|H_{z,\varepsilon}G\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))}=\|H_{z,\varepsilon}G\|_{L^{2}(X,L^{2}(\mathcal{I}))}.

(4.5)

Since the operator
H_{z,\varepsilon}
is just Hilbert transform up to
iIm(z)
, we have the bound
H_{z,\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathcal{I})\mapsto L^{2}(\mathcal{I})
with some constant depending only on
Im(z)
exponentially. For a detailed analysis, we refer to Vega
[
42
]
. Now combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))}

\displaystyle\leq C(Im(z))\|G\|_{L^{2}(X,L^{2}(\mathcal{I}))}

\displaystyle=C(Im(z))\|G\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))}

\displaystyle=C(Im(z))\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))},

which is our desired bound for
T_{z,\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))
when
Re(z)=-1
.

Noting the fact that
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}
-norm is the usual operator norm, for
Re(z)=-1
, we get

\displaystyle\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))}

\displaystyle=\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))}

\displaystyle\leq\|W_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I},L^{\infty}(X))}\|T_{z,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X))}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I},L^{\infty}(X))}

\displaystyle\leq C(Im(z))\|W_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I},L^{\infty}(X))}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I},L^{\infty}(X))}.

(4.6)

Finally, applying the complex interpolation method given in Lemma
2.1
, between (
4.3
) and (
4.6
) for
z=0
, we get

\big\|W_{1}T_{\varepsilon}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{I},L^{2}(X)))}\leq C\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I},L^{p}(X))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I},L^{p}(X))},

(4.7)

for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
as long as

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{p}=\frac{1}{2},\;\text{with}\;2\lambda_{0}+1<p\leq 2(\lambda_{0}+1).

Finally, letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in (
4.7
), we complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

Using the above theorem, in the following result, we establish the orthonormal Strichartz estimate in Lebesgue spaces, i.e., orthonormal version of Theorem
3.1
part (1).

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
satisfying Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Suppose
p,q\geq 1
satisfy

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n.

(i) If
1\leq p<\frac{n+1}{n-1},
then

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\leq C\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

(4.8)

holds for any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
,
where
C>0
is independent of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.

(ii) If

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1\leq n<2,\\
\frac{n+1}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty,\end{cases}or~~\begin{cases}n=2\\
3\leq p<\infty,\end{cases}or~~\begin{cases}n>2\\
\frac{n+1}{n-1}\leq p\leq\frac{n}{n-2},\end{cases}

then for any
1\leq\lambda<q

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\leq C\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\lambda}},

(4.9)

holds for any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
,
where
C>0
is independent of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.

Proof.

For the time being, assuming part
(i)
, one can notice that, using the triangle inequality and the Strichartz estimate for a single function in Part (1) of Theorem
3.1
,

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\leq\sum_{j\in J}|n_{j}|\left\||e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\leq C\sum_{j\in J}|n_{j}|,

(4.10)

which gives (
4.9
) for
\lambda=1
without making use of the orthogonality of the system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
. However, by capitalizing on the orthogonality of the
f_{j}
, in part
(i)
, we see that it holds for
\lambda=\frac{2p}{p+1}>1
. Of course, the above trivial inequality (
4.10
) can be proved for a larger range of
p
than the range
1\leq p<\frac{n+1}{n-1}
as in part
(i)
. In fact, it holds for all such exponential pairs
(q,p)
that satisfy part (1) of Theorem
3.1
, i.e., Strichartz estimates for a single function associate with
L
. Particularly, it holds for the Keel-Tao endpoint
(q,p)=\left(1,\frac{n}{n-2}\right)
for
n>2
and
(q,p)=\left(\frac{2}{n},\infty\right)
for
1\leq n<2
with
\lambda=1
.
Now, as pointed out in
[
11
]
,

•

for
n>2
, the estimates in
(ii)
come out from those in
(i)
by interpolation between
(q,p)=(1,\frac{n}{n-2})
(with
\lambda=1
) and points arbitrarily close to
(q,p)=(\frac{n+1}{n},\frac{n+1}{n-1})
(with
\lambda=\frac{2p}{p+1}
);

•

for
n=2
, the estimates in
(ii)
come out from those in
(i)
by interpolation between points arbitrarily close to
(q,p)=(1,\infty)
(with
\lambda=1
) and points arbitrarily close to
(q,p)=(\frac{n+1}{n},\frac{n+1}{n-1})
(with
\lambda=\frac{2p}{p+1}
);

•

for
1\leq n<2
, the estimates in
(ii)
come out from those in
(i)
by interpolation between
(q,p)=(\frac{2}{n},\infty)
(with
\lambda=1
) and points arbitrarily close to
(q,p)=(\frac{n+1}{n},\frac{n+1}{n-1})
(with
\lambda=\frac{2p}{p+1}
).

From the above discussion, in order to prove part
(ii),
it’s enough to prove part
(i)
. From the fact that the operator
e^{itL}
is unitary on
L^{2}(X)
, it follows that

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|e^{itL}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{I},L^{1}(X))}\leq\sum_{j\in J}|n_{j}|,

for any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
. That means equation (
4.8
) holds for
(q,p)=(\infty,1)
and by Lemma
2.2
, equivalently, we can say that the operator

W\in L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{\infty}(X))\mapsto W\mathcal{T}\overline{W}\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{\infty}(X)))

is bounded. Again, by Theorem
3.1
, Lemma
2.2
and
4.1
, the operator

W\in L^{n+2}(\mathcal{I},L^{n+2}(X))\mapsto W\mathcal{T}\overline{W}\in\mathfrak{S}^{n+2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))

is also bounded. Applying the complex interpolation method in
[
3
, Chapter 4]
, the operator

W\in L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))\mapsto W\mathcal{T}\overline{W}\in\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)))

is bounded provided that
p,q\geq 1
such that

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n,\;\text{with}\;n+2\leq p\leq\infty.

Combined with Lemma
4.1
, we have

\big\|W_{1}\mathcal{T}W_{2}\big\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{I},L^{2}(X)))}\leq C\big\|W_{1}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I},L^{p}(X))}\big\|W_{2}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I},L^{p}(X))},

holds true for any

satisfying

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n,\;\text{with}\;n+1<p\leq\infty.

Finally, by the duality principle Lemma
2.2
, we obtain our desired result
(i)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Indeed, our method is general and can also be applied to obtain orthonormal Strichartz estimates for a general class of dispersive semigroup associated with
L
. Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be smooth and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([\frac{1}{2},2])
. We consider the truncated (or called frequency localized) dispersive semigroup
U(t)=e^{it\phi(L)}\psi(\sqrt{L})
. It is easy to see that the operator obeys the energy estimate:

\|U(t)f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim_{\psi}\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)},

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{2}(X)
. We ssume that

Assumption
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

U(t)
is an integral with kernel
K_{it}(x,y)
, i.e.,

U(t)f(x)=\int_{X}K_{it}(x,y)f(y)d\mu(y),\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in X,

for every
f\in L^{2}(X)
.

Assumption
(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
The kernel
K_{it}(x,y)
of the operator
U(t)
satisfies an uniform decay estimate:

\sup_{x,y\in X}|K_{it}(x,y)|\lesssim_{\phi,\psi}|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}},\,|t|<T_{0},\text{ for some }n\geq 1,

where
T_{0}\in(0,+\infty]
.

By Keel-Tao
[
18
]
, we obtain the Strichartz estimate for
U(t)
involving a single function.

\|U(t)f\|_{L^{2q}(\mathcal{I},L^{2p}(X))}\lesssim_{\phi,\psi}\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)},

holds for any
p,q\geq 1
satisfying
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n
except for
(q,p,n)=(1,\infty,2)
. Argued the same as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we establish the following orthonormal Strichartz estimates.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
and
U(t)=e^{it\phi(L)}\psi(\sqrt{L})
satisfies Assumption
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. Suppose
p,q\geq 1
satisfy

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n.

(i) If
1\leq p<\frac{n+1}{n-1}
, then

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|U(t)f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\lesssim_{\phi,\psi}\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

(4.11)

holds for any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
.

(ii) If

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1\leq n<2,\\
\frac{n+1}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty,\end{cases}or~~\begin{cases}n=2\\
3\leq p<\infty,\end{cases}or~~\begin{cases}n>2\\
\frac{n+1}{n-1}\leq p\leq\frac{n}{n-2},\end{cases}

then for
1\leq\lambda<q

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|U(t)f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X))}\lesssim_{\phi,\psi}\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\lambda}},

(4.12)

holds for any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(X)
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
.

Indeed, these frequency localized estimates (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) are quite useful. For instance, when
L=-\Delta
, the classical Laplacian on
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\phi(r)=r
, Bez-Hong-Lee-Nakamura-Sawano
[
4
]
proved the frequency localized estimates for the Schrödinger operator
e^{it\Delta}
(see
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
), which was crucial to establish the orthonormal Strichartz estimates of initial data with regularity. Hence, the frequency localized estimates given in (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) can be regarded as a first step towards the orthonormal Strichartz estimates of initial data with regularity for a general class of dispersive semigroup associated with
L
.

Now consider the operator

\displaystyle\gamma:=\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}\left|f_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle f_{j}\right|

(4.13)

which acts on
L^{2}\left(X,\mu\right).
Here the Dirac’s notation
|u\rangle\langle v|
stands for the
rank-one operator
f\mapsto\langle v,f\rangle u
. Since the sequence
f_{j}
form an orthonormal system therefore,
the scalers,
n_{j}
are precisely the eigenvalues of the operator
\gamma
. The evolved operator

\gamma(t):=e^{itL}\gamma e^{-itL}=\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}\left|e^{itL}f_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle e^{itL}f_{j}\right|

solves (in a weak sense) the von-Neumann Schrödinger equation associated with the operator
L

i\dot{\gamma}(t)=[L,\gamma(t)],\quad\gamma(0)=\gamma,

where
[A,B]
denotes the commutator between two operators
A
and
B
. Let us introduce the density

\displaystyle\rho_{\gamma(t)}:=\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}\left|e^{itL}f_{j}\right|^{2}.

(4.14)

Then with respect to the notions defined above, the inequality (
4.8
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}\leqslant C_{S}\|\gamma\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

(4.15)

where the norm

\|\gamma\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}:=\left(\sum_{j\in J}\left|n_{j}\right|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}

is called the Schatten norm of the operator
\gamma
.

Again, for any multiplication operator
V
on
L^{2}(X)
,

V(x)\gamma(t)
is an integral operator with kernel is given

K(x,y)=V(x)\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}e^{itL}u_{j}(x)\overline{e^{itL}u_{j}(y)}.

Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(V(x)\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=\int_{{X}}K(x,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{X}}V(x)\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}\left|e^{itL}u_{j}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{X}}V(x)\rho_{\gamma}(x)~dx.

Moreover, for a time-dependent potential
V(t,x)
, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle\left|\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-itL}V(t,x)e^{itL}dt\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(V(t,x)e^{itL}\gamma e^{-itL}\right)dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(V(t,x)\gamma(t)\right)dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{X}V(t,x)\rho_{\gamma(t)}(x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|V\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}\left(X\right)\right)}\left\|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}.

Thus by duality argument, (
4.15
) turns out to be equivalent as

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-itL}V(t,x)e^{itL}dt\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2p^{\prime}}}\leq C_{S}\|V\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}\left(X\right)\right)}

(4.16)

under the condition

\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}=2,\quad\frac{n+1}{2}<p^{\prime}\leq\infty.

The inequality (
4.16
) can be use to prove an inhomogeneous Strichartz inequality. Consider the inhomogeneous system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}{\gamma}(t)=[L,\gamma(t)]+iR(t),\\
\gamma|_{t=0}=0,\end{array}\right.

where
R(t)
is a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}\left(X\right)
and bounded for almost every
t
. Then by Duhamel’s principle, the solution to the inhomogeneous system (
4
) can be written as

\gamma(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}R(s)e^{i(s-t)L}ds.

Theorem 4.6

(Inhomogeneous Strichartz inequality)
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
satisfying Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Assume that
p,q,n\geq 1
satisfy

1\leq p<\frac{n+1}{n-1}\text{ and }\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n.

Then the solution to the inhomogeneous system (
4
)
satisfies

\left\|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}\leqslant C_{S}\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}

for a constant
C_{S}>0
independent of
R
.

Proof.

We prove this result using the duality argument.
Now for a time-dependent potential
V(t,x)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{X}V(t,x)\rho_{\gamma(t)}(x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{Tr}(V(t,x)\gamma(t))dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{Tr}\left(V(t,x)e^{itL}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}e^{-itL}\right)dsdt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{Tr}\left(e^{-itL}V(t,x)e^{itL}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}\right)dsdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}\left(\left(\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-itL}V(t,x)e^{itL}dt\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}ds\right)\right),

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-itL}V(t,x)e^{itL}dt\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2p^{\prime}}}\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

where in the last line we used Hölder’s inequality for traces. Now using the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the homogeneous version (
4.16
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{I}}\int_{X}V(t,x)\rho_{\gamma(t)}(x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}\left\|V\right\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}\left(X\right)\right)}\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}.

Finlly using the duality argument, we get

\left\|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}\leq C_{S}\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}.

∎

Now consider the general inhomogeneous system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}{\gamma}(t)=[L,\gamma(t)]+iR(t),\\
\gamma|_{t=0}=\gamma_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
R(t)
is a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}\left(X\right)
which is bounded for almost every
t
. Then by Duhamel’s principle, the solution to the inhomogeneous system (
4
) can be written as

\gamma(t)=e^{itL}\gamma_{0}e^{-itL}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}R(s)e^{i(s-t)L}ds.

Thus using (
4.15
) and Theorem
4.6
, we immediately can conclude that

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
satisfying Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Assume that
p,q,n\geq 1
satisfy

1\leq p<\frac{n+1}{n-1}\text{ and }\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n

and let
\gamma_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}
. Then the solution to the inhomogeneous system (
4
)
satisfies

\left\|\rho_{\gamma(t)}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}\leqslant C_{S}\left(\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}+\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}R(s)e^{isL}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\right)

for a constant
C_{S}>0
independent of
\gamma_{0}
and
R
.

6.
Application: Hartree equation

The following global well-posedness for the Hartree equation in Schatten spaces can be obtained as an application of the orthonormal Strichartz inequalities.

Consider
M
couple of nonlinear Hartree equations which describes the dynamics of
M
fermions interacting via a potential
w
as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlrl}i\partial_{t}u_{1}&=\left(L+w*\rho\right)u_{1},&&\left.u_{1}\right|_{t=0}=f_{1}\\
&\vdots\\
i\partial_{t}u_{M}&=\left(L+w*\rho\right)u_{M},&&\left.u_{M}\right|_{t=0}=f_{M},\end{array}\right.

where
(x,t)\in X\times\mathbb{R},\left\{f_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{M}
is an orthonormal system in
L^{2}\left(X\right)
and
\rho
is a density function defined by
\rho(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left|u_{j}(x,t)\right|^{2}
. One of the natural questions is to investigate the behaviour of solution of the system as
M\rightarrow\infty
. In this case, we naturally arrive at the following operator-valued equivalent formulation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}\gamma=\left[L+w*\rho_{\gamma},\gamma\right],\\
\left.\gamma\right|_{t=0}=\gamma_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Here
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma=\gamma(t)
are bounded and self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}\left(X\right)
.

Now using the inhomogeneous Strichartz inequality, we have the following well-posedness result for the Hartree equation.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume
L
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
that satisfies Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. Let
w\in L^{p^{\prime}}(X)
and
p,q\geq 1
satisfy

1\leq p<\frac{n+1}{n-1}\;\text{and}\;\frac{2}{q}+\frac{n}{p}=n

(1)

(Local well-posedness) For any
\gamma_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}
with
R=\|\gamma_{0}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}<\infty
, there exists
T=T(R,\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)})
(without loss of generality, we can assume
T<T_{0}
) and a unique

\gamma\in C^{0}([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}})

satisfying

\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}(X)\right)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\gamma=[L+w*\rho_{\gamma},\gamma],\\
\gamma|_{t=0}=\gamma_{0}.\end{cases}

(6.1)

(2)

(Almost global well-posedness) For each
T>0
and for any
\gamma_{0}\in{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}
with
\|\gamma_{0}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}\leq R_{T}
, where
R_{T}=R_{T}(\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)})
, then there exists a solution
\gamma\in C^{0}([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}})
satisfying (
6.1
) and
\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}(X)\right)
.

Proof.

(1) Let
R=\left\|\gamma_{0}\right\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}<\infty
and
T=T(R)>0
to be chosen later. We define the evolution space

\displaystyle X_{T}:=\left\{(\gamma,\rho)\in C^{0}\left([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\right)\times L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}\left(X\right)\right):\|(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}<{\max\{1,C_{S}\}}R\right\},

where

\|(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}=\|\gamma\|_{C^{0}\left([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\right)}+\|\rho\|_{L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}.

Let us define the map
\Psi
as

\Psi(\gamma,\rho)(t)=e^{itL}\gamma_{0}e^{-itL}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}[w*\rho(s),\gamma(s)]e^{i(s-t)L}ds.

We also define a map

\Phi(\gamma,\rho)=\left(\Psi(\gamma,\rho),\rho\left[\Psi(\gamma,\rho)\right]\right),

where we used the notation
\rho\left[\gamma\right]=\rho_{\gamma}
and in this formulation, (
6.1
) is equivalent to
(\gamma,\rho_{\gamma})=\Phi(\gamma,\rho_{\gamma})
.

First, we evalute
\left\|\Psi(\gamma,\rho)\right\|_{C^{0}\left([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\right)}
for any
t\in[0,T].
Now

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi(\gamma,\rho)(t)\right\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{itL}\gamma_{0}e^{-itL}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{i(t-s)L}[w*\rho(s),\gamma(s)]e^{i(s-t)L}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}ds

(6.2)

For the first term in the RHS of (
6.2
), since
\left(f_{j}\right)_{j}
is orthonormal in
L^{2}(X)
, then
\left(e^{itL}f_{j}\right)_{j}
is as well for each
t
and thus

\left\|e^{itL}\gamma_{0}e^{-itL}\right\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}=\left\|\gamma_{0}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}=R.

For the second term of (
6.2
), we use Hölder’s inequality for Schatten spaces

\displaystyle\|e^{i(t-s)L}[w*\rho(s),\gamma(s)]e^{i(s-t)L}\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|w*\rho(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\gamma(s)\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\|\rho(s)\|_{{}_{L^{p}(X)}}\|\gamma(s)\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}

Thus for any
(\gamma,\rho)\in X_{T}
, from (
6.2
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi(\gamma,\rho)\right\|_{{}_{C^{0}\left([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq R+\int_{0}^{T}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\|\rho(s)\|_{{}_{L^{p}(X)}}\|\gamma(s)\|_{{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}}ds

\displaystyle\leq R+T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\|\rho\|_{{}_{L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}(X)\right)}}\|\gamma\|_{{}_{C^{0}\left([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq R+T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}{\max\{1,C_{S}^{2}\}}R^{2}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}.

(6.3)

Now our aim is to estimate
\left\|\rho[\Psi(\gamma,\rho)]\right\|_{{}_{L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}}
. From the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimate in Theorem
4.7
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\rho[\Psi(\gamma,\rho)]\right\|_{{}_{L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}\left(X\right)\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}+\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-isL}e^{itL}|[w*\rho(s),\gamma(s)]|e^{-itL}e^{isL}ds\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{S}R+C_{S}T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}{\max\{1,C_{S}^{2}\}}R^{2}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)},

(6.4)

where for the first term of the above estimate, we used
\left\|\gamma_{0}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}=R
and for the second term, we employed the same argument as we used in (
6.3
). Thus from (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we get

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}\leq(1+C_{S})\left[R+T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}{\max\{1,C_{S}^{2}\}}R^{2}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\right].

(6.5)

Now, choose
T=T(R)>0
small enough so that

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}\leq\max\{1,C_{S}\}R.

This implies that
\Phi(\gamma,\rho)\in X_{T}
for
(\gamma,\rho)\in X_{T}
, i.e.,
\Phi
maps
X_{T}
to itself.
We can give a similar argument to show that
\Phi
is a contraction on
X_{T}
. Since
X_{T}
is a Banach space and
\Phi
is a contraction map on
X_{T}
, by Banach’s fixed point theory,
\Phi
has a unique fixed point on
X_{T}
which is a solution to the Hartree equation (
6.1
) on
[0,T]
.

(2) Now, we first fix an arbitrary
T>0
. Then estimates (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) yield that

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}\leq(1+C_{S})\left[\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}+T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\|(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\right].

(6.6)

Keeping this in mind, we choose
R_{T}=R_{T}\left(\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\right)
small enough so that we can find
M>0
such that for any
y\in[0,M]
, it holds

(1+C_{S})\left[\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}+T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|w\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}y^{2}\right]\leq M

as long as
\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}}\leq R_{T}
. Therefore, if we define the space
X_{T,M}
by

X_{T,M}:=\left\{(\gamma,\rho)\in X_{T}:\|(\gamma,\rho)\|_{X_{T}}\leq M\right\},

then wecan see that
\Phi
maps
X_{T,M}
to
X_{T,M}
itself. By choosing
R_{T}
even further smaller, we also can show that
\Phi
is a contraction map on
X_{T,M}
in a similar way as we discussed earlier. Thus by the Banach fixed point theorem, we find a solution
\gamma\in C^{0}([0,T],{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}})

satisfying

\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{q}\left([0,T],L^{p}(X)\right)
for fix an arbitrary
T>0
.
∎

7.
Generalized Strichartz results by Kenig-Ponce-Vega

This section is devoted to extending the Strichartz (or “global smoothing”) estimates considered in
[
20
]
involving a single function to a system of orthonormal functions using the techniques used in the proof Theorem
4.2
.

7.1.
Orthonormal Strichartz inequalities on
\mathbb{R}

First, we recall the notion of the following class
\mathcal{A}
of phase functions introduced by Kenig-Ponce-Vega
[
20
]
.

Definition 7.1
.

We say
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
if

•

\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for some open
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi\in C^{3}(\Omega)
, and
\Omega
is a finite union of intervals,

•

the set
S_{\phi}=\{\xi\in\bar{\Omega}\cup\{\pm\infty\}:\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=0\text{ or }\lim\limits_{\bar{\xi}\rightarrow\xi}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\xi})=\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=\pm\infty\}
is finite,

•

if
\xi_{0}\in S_{\phi}
with
\xi_{0}\neq\pm\infty
, then there exist constants
\varepsilon,c_{1},c_{2}
and
\alpha\neq 0
such that for
|\xi-\xi_{0}|<\varepsilon
,

c_{1}|\xi-\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-2}\leq|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\leq c_{2}|\xi-\xi_{0}|^{\alpha-2},

•

if
\xi_{0}=\pm\infty\in S_{\phi}
, then there exist constants
\varepsilon,c_{1},c_{2}
and
\alpha\neq 0
such that for
|\xi|>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
,

c_{1}|\xi|^{\alpha-2}\leq|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\leq c_{2}|\xi|^{\alpha-2},

•

\phi^{\prime\prime}
has a finite number of changes of monotonicity.

Remark 7.2
.

It is easily checked that if
\phi
is not linear and
\phi(\xi)=R(\xi)^{\alpha}
, where
R(\xi)
is a rational function and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
. Then for
\nu\geq 0
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},
, the operator
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(t)
is defined as

\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(t)f(x)=\int_{\Omega}e^{i\left(t\phi(\xi)+x\xi\right)}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

In
[
20
]
, Kenig-Ponce-Vega obtained the following Strichartz estimate for the operator
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
involving a single function.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
. Then for any
\nu\in[0,1]
and
(p,q)=\left(\frac{2}{1-\nu},\frac{4}{\nu}\right)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(t-s)F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}))},

for a constant
C>0
independent of
f
and
F
.

The above Strichartz estimates on
\mathbb{R}
can be interpreted as restriction estimates on certain hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In fact, let
S
be the noncompact hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

S=\{(\xi,\phi(\xi):\xi\in\mathbb{R}\},

endowed with the measure

d\sigma_{\nu}(\xi,\phi(\xi))=\frac{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|\phi^{\prime}(\xi)|^{2}}}d\xi.

One has the following identity. For all
F\in L^{1}(S,d\sigma_{\nu})\cap L^{2}(S,d\sigma_{\nu})
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle E^{\nu}_{S}F(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S}e^{i(x,t)\cdot(\xi,\phi(\xi))}F(\xi,\phi(\xi))d\sigma_{\nu}(\xi,\phi(\xi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\left(t\phi(\xi)+x\xi\right)}F(\xi,\phi(\xi))|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}d\xi.

In particular, choose
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\hat{f}(\xi)=|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{4}}F(\xi,\phi(\xi))
. Then we see that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|F\|_{L^{2}(S,d\sigma_{\nu})}
and

E^{\nu}_{S}F(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)f(x).

(7.1)

Since
\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))
, we have the following restriction estimate.

Theorem 7.4
.

Suppose
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
,
\nu\in[0,1]
and
(p,q)=\left(\frac{2}{1-\nu},\frac{4}{\nu}\right)
.
Let
S=\{(\xi,\phi(\xi):\xi\in\mathbb{R}\}

be the noncompact hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
endowed with the measure
d\sigma_{\nu}(\xi,\phi(\xi))=\frac{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|\phi^{\prime}(\xi)|^{2}}}d\xi
. Then for any
F\in L^{2}(S,d\sigma_{\nu})
,

\|E^{\nu}_{S}F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}(S,d\sigma_{\nu})}.}

For
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
and
Re(z)\geq 0
, we define

I_{z}(x,t)=\int_{\Omega}e^{i\left(t\phi(\xi)+x\xi\right)}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{z}{2}}d\xi,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

Based on
[
20
, Lemma 2.7]
, we can estimate decay rates as follows.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
. Then for
Re(z)=1
,

|I_{z}(x,t)|\leq C(1+|Im(z)|)|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}},

for a constant
C>0
independent of
x
and
t
.

Using the aforementioned decay estimate and considering the approach employed to derive Theorem
4.2
, we now extend Strichartz estimates for the operator
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
from a single function to a system of orthonormal initial data.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
,
\nu\in[\frac{2}{3},1)
and
(p,q)=\left(\frac{1}{1-\nu},\frac{2}{\nu}\right)
.
For any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq C\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

where
C>0
is independent of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.

Proof.

Thanks to the duality principle Theorem
2.2
, the desired estimate holds if and only if

\left\|W_{1}\mathcal{T}_{\nu}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))}\lesssim\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\nu}}(\mathbb{R},L^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}))}\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\nu}}(\mathbb{R},L^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}))}

(7.2)

for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{\frac{4}{2-\nu}}(\mathbb{R},L^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}))
, where
\mathcal{T}_{\nu}=\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)\left(\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)\right)^{*}
.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\nu}F(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathcal{W}}}_{\nu}(t-s)F(\cdot,s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\nu}(x-y,t-s)F(y,s)dyds.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we define the operator
T_{\varepsilon}
(similar as in Lemma
4.1
) by

T_{\varepsilon}F(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}(x-y,t-s)F(y,s)\;dyds,

where
\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\chi_{|t|>\varepsilon}(t)I_{\nu}(x,t)
.
Once we prove

\left\|W_{1}T_{\varepsilon}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))}\lesssim\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\nu}}(\mathbb{R},L^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}))}\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\nu}}(\mathbb{R},L^{\frac{2}{\nu}}(\mathbb{R}))},

(7.3)

for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
, then (
7.2
) follow by taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

In order to obtain (
7.3
), we use Stein’s analytic complex interpolation. We first construct an analytic family of operators for it. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the analytic family of operators
T_{z,\varepsilon}
defined by

T_{z,\varepsilon}F(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x-y,t-s)F(y,s)\;dyds,

where
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re(z)\in[0,1]
and

\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,t)=t^{-1+\frac{z}{\nu}}\chi_{|t|>\varepsilon}(t)I_{z}(x,t).

Clearly
T_{\varepsilon}=T_{\nu,\varepsilon}
and it follows from Lemma
7.5
that for
Re(z)=1

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x,t)|\leq C(1+|Im(z)|)|t|^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{Re(z)}{\nu}}.

(7.4)

Once we prove the following two estimates

\left\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))}\leq C(Im(z)\|W_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{4\nu}{2-\nu}}\left(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right)}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{4\nu}{2-\nu}}\left(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right)}

(7.5)

for
Re(z)=1
and

\left\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))}\leq C(Im(z))\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}

(7.6)

for
Re(z)=0
, where
C(Im(z))
is a constant that grows exponentially with
Im(z)
, then, by Stein’s complex interpolation method in Lemma
2.1
, we have the required estimate (
7.3
).

For
Re(z)=1
, our first aim is to prove (
7.5
). By (
7.4
), the kernel of
W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}
is bounded by

C(1+|Im(z)|)|W_{1}(x,t)||t-s|^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{\nu}}|W_{2}(y,s)|,

and combined with the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\right\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|Im(z)|)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|W_{1}(x,t)|^{2}|W_{2}(y,s)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{3-\frac{2}{\nu}}}dxdydtds

\displaystyle=C(1+|Im(z)|)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\|W_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\|W_{2}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}{|t-s|^{3-\frac{2}{\nu}}}dtds

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}(Im(z)^{2}\|W_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{4\nu}{2-\nu}}\left(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right)}\|W_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{4\nu}{2-\nu}}\left(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right)}.

On the other hand, for
Re(z)=0
, our next aim is to prove (
7.6
). Using the fact that
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}
-norm is the operator norm and

\displaystyle\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))}

\displaystyle=\|W_{1}T_{z,\varepsilon}W_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|W_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\|T_{z,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))},

to prove the estimate (
7.6
), it’s enough to prove that the operator
T_{z,\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
is bounded with some constant depending only on
Im(z)
exponentially when
Re(z)=0
. In fact, we rewrite

\displaystyle T_{z,\varepsilon}F(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{z,\varepsilon}(x-y,s)F(y,t-s)dyds

\displaystyle=\int_{|t|>\varepsilon}s^{-1+i\frac{Im(z)}{\nu}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{z}(x-y,s)F(y,t-s)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{|t|>\varepsilon}s^{-1+i\frac{Im(z)}{\nu}}\mathcal{W}_{z}(s)F(x,t-s)ds.

The operator
\mathcal{W}_{z}(t)
being unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
Re(z)=0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and it reduces that

\displaystyle\|T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{W}_{z}(-t)T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{|t|>\varepsilon}s^{-1+i\frac{Im(z)}{\nu}}G(\cdot,t-s)ds\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

where
G(x,t)=\mathcal{W}_{2z}(-t)F(x,t)
and

\|G(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|F(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(7.7)

If we define

H_{z,\varepsilon}:h(t)\mapsto\int_{|t|>\varepsilon}s^{-1+iIm(z)}h(t-s)ds,

then we have

\|T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}=\|H_{z,\varepsilon}G\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}.

(7.8)

Since the operator
H_{z,\varepsilon}
is just Hilbert transform up to
iIm(z)
,
the operator
H_{z,\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\mapsto L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is bounded with some constant depending only on
Im(z)
exponentially. For further details, see Vega
[
42
]
. Therefore, combining (
7.7
) and (
7.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{z,\varepsilon}F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(Im(z))\|G\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle=C(Im(z))\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))},

which is our desired bound for
T_{z,\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
when
Re(z)=0
.
∎

Remark 7.7
.

One can see that, in Theorem
7.3
, Kenig-Ponce-Vega obtained Strichartz type estimate for the operator
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
for dimension one involving a single function for any
\nu\in[0,1]
. However, its orthonormal version, i.e., Theorem
7.6
, holds only for
\nu\in[\frac{2}{3},1)
. This is due to the application of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

From (
7.1
), we also obtain the following orthonormal restriction theorem.

Theorem 7.8
.

Suppose
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
,
\nu\in[0,1]
and
(p,q)=\left(\frac{2}{1-\nu},\frac{4}{\nu}\right)
.
Let
S=\{(\xi,\phi(\xi):\xi\in\mathbb{R}\}

be the noncompact hypersurface of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
endowed with the measure
d\sigma_{\nu}(\xi,\phi(\xi))=\frac{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|\phi^{\prime}(\xi)|^{2}}}d\xi
.
For any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{F_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(S,d\sigma_{\nu})
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|E^{\nu}_{S}F_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq C\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

where
C>0
is independent of
\{F_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.

7.2.
Orthonormal Strichartz inequalities on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2

This subsection is devoted to extending the obtained orthonormal inequalities for
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
on the real line to higher dimensions. The first problem in the higher dimension was to find the substitute for the second derivative that appeared in the dimension case; however, as explained in
[
20
]
, the stationary phase lemma suggests that this substitute should be the Hessian of the phase function.

Let
P
be a real polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and denote
HP=\det(\partial_{ij}^{2}P)_{i,j}
is the determinant of
the Hessian matrix of
P
. For
\nu\geq 0
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define

\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(t)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\left(tP(\xi)+x\cdot\xi\right)}|HP(\xi)|^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\hat{f}(\xi)\psi_{P}(\xi)d\xi,

where
\psi_{P}
is a suitable cut-off function. If
P
is a real polynomial, it is not easy to see what the Hessian of
P
looks like, but, things will be simple if we consider
P
to be a a real elliptic polynomial. When
P
is a real elliptic polynomial of degree
m
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e., the principle part of
P
never vanishes outside of the origin, then
\psi_{P}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies that

\psi_{P}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{ if}\quad|P(\xi)|\geq C|\xi|^{m},\\
0\quad\text{ if}\quad|P(\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{2}|\xi|^{m}.\end{cases}

When
P(\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(\xi_{j})
and
P_{j}
is an arbitrary real polynomial of one variable with degree
P_{j}\geq 2
for all
1\leq j\leq d
, then
\psi_{P}\equiv 1
.

The following smoothing effect that involves a single function has been proved by Kenig-Ponce-Vega
[
20
]
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
P
be a real elliptic polynomial of
m\geq 2
or
P(\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(\xi_{j})
, where
P_{j}
is an arbitrary real polynomial of one variable with degree
P_{j}\geq 2
for all
1\leq j\leq d
. Then for any
0\leq\nu<\frac{2}{d}
and
(p,q)=\left(\frac{2}{1-\nu},\frac{4}{d\nu}\right)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{W}_{\nu}(t-s)F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

for a constant
C>0
independent of
f
and
F
.

Remark 7.10
.

It is pointed out by Keel-Tao
[
18
]
that for
d\geq 3
, the condition can be loosened to
0\leq\nu\leq\frac{2}{d}
.

Our main aim is to extend Theorem
7.9
for a system of orthonormal functions. The following decay of a class of oscillatory integrals is one of the key ingredients to proving the orthonormal inequality.

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
\Omega
be an open ball or complement of a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Consider
\phi
, a real
C^{d+1}
-function defined on
\Omega
such that for some
m\geq 2
, there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}|\xi|^{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq\nabla\phi(\xi)|\leq C_{2}|\xi|^{m-1},

\displaystyle C_{1}|\xi|^{(m-1)d}

\displaystyle\leq|H\phi(\xi)|=|\det(\partial_{ij}^{2}\phi)_{i,j}(\xi)|\leq C_{2}|\xi|^{(m-1)d},

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}|\xi|^{m-\alpha}\text{ for all $\alpha$ with }|\alpha|\leq d+1.

Then for
Re(z)=1

\left|\int_{\Omega}e^{i(t\phi(\xi)+x\cdot\xi)}\psi(\xi)|H\phi(\xi)|^{\frac{z}{2}}d\xi\right|\leq C(1+|Im(z)|)^{d}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}},

where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\psi
is a smooth function vanishing with all its derivatives on the boundary of
\Omega
and
C
is a constant which does not depend on
x,t
or
Im(z)
.

Lemma 7.12
.

If
P(\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(\xi_{j})
, where
P_{j}
is an arbitrary real polynomial of one variable with degree
P_{j}\geq 2
for all
1\leq j\leq d
, then for
Re(z)=1
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\left(tP(\xi)+x\cdot\xi\right)}|HP(\xi)|^{\frac{z}{2}}d\xi\right|\leq C(1+|Im(z)|)^{d}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}},

where
C
is a constant which does not depend on
x,t
or
Im(z)
.

Proof.

Note that
HP(\xi)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}P^{\prime\prime}_{j}(\xi_{j})
and we can write

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\left(tP(\xi)+x\cdot\xi\right)}|HP(\xi)|^{\frac{z}{2}}d\xi=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(tP_{j}(\xi_{j})+x_{j}\xi_{j})}|P^{\prime\prime}_{j}(\xi_{j})|^{\frac{z}{2}}d\xi_{j}.

Observe that each
P_{j}\in\mathcal{A}
for all
1\leq j\leq d
and the desired result follows immediately from Lemma
7.5
.
∎

Finally, argued similarly to Theorem
7.6
, we obtain the following orthonormal Strichartz inequalities for
\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}
in higher dimensions.

Theorem 7.13
.

Let
P
be a real elliptic polynomial of
m\geq 2
or
P(\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(\xi_{j})
, where
P_{j}
is an arbitrary real polynomial of one variable with degree
P_{j}\geq 2
for all
1\leq j\leq d
. Assume
\nu\in[\frac{2}{d+2},\frac{2}{d+1})
and
(p,q)=\left(\frac{1}{1-\nu},\frac{2}{d\nu}\right)
. For any (possibly infinite) orthonormal system
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left\|\sum_{j\in J}n_{j}|\mathcal{W}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}(t)f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\left\|\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}},

where
C>0
is independent of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.

Proof.

Similar to the one dimensional real line
\mathbb{R}
, for
Re(z)\geq 0
, we also define

I_{z}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\left(tP(\xi)+x\cdot\xi\right)}|HP(\xi)|^{\frac{z}{2}}\psi_{P}(\xi)d\xi,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then from Lemma
7.11
and
7.12
, it follows that

|I_{z}(x,t)|\leq C(1+|Im(z)|)^{d}|t|^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

(7.9)

for
Re(z)=1
. Therefore, using the decay estimate (
7.9
), the desired result automatically follows in a similar way that we used in Theorem
7.6
. and we omit the further details.
∎

Remark 7.14
.

Notice that Strichartz type estimate involving a single function for the operator
\mathcal{W}_{\nu}
was proved by Kenig-Ponce-Vega in Theorem
7.9
for any
0\leq\nu<\frac{2}{d}
. However, due to the application of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, its orthonormal version (see Theorem
7.13
) holds only for
\nu\in[\frac{2}{d+2},\frac{2}{d+1})
.

Remark 7.15
.

Similar to Theorem
7.8
, Theorem
7.13
can also be seen as an orthonormal restriction theorem for the Fourier extension operator on noncompact hypersurfaces of the form
S=\{(\xi,P(\xi)):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
, where
P
be a real elliptic polynomial of
m\geq 2
or
P(\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(\xi_{j})
and
P_{j}
’s are arbitrary real polynomial of one variable with degree
P_{j}\geq 2
for all
1\leq j\leq d
.

8.
Appendix

In this section, we prove Theorem
3.1
, i.e., Strichartz estimates for a single function under the Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{B})
. From
[
13
]
, first, we recall some elementary abstract results. For any vector space
\mathcal{D}
, we denote
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{*}
as the algebraic dual of
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{L}_{a}(\mathcal{D},X)
as the space of linear maps from
\mathcal{D}
to some other vector space
X
, and finally
\langle\varphi,f\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}
denotes the pairing between
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{*}
and
\mathcal{D}
with
f\in\mathcal{D}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{a}^{*}
, which we take to be linear in
f
and anti linear in
\varphi
. Then under these notations, the following lemma (see
[
13
]
) will be useful to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space,
\mathcal{X}^{*}
is the dual of
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{D}
be a vector space densely contained in
\mathcal{X}
. Let
T\in\mathcal{L}_{a}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
and
T^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{a}(H,\mathcal{D}_{a}^{*})
be its adjoint operator, defined by

\langle T^{*}v,f\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}=\langle v,Tf\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{D},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{H},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
is the scalar product in
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1) There exists
0\leqslant a\leqslant\infty
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
,

\|Tf\|\leqslant a\|f\|_{X}.

(2)
\mathfrak{R}(T^{*})\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}
and there exists
0\leqslant a\leqslant\infty
, such that for all
v\in\mathcal{H}
,

\|T^{*}v\|_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}\leqslant a\|v\|.

(3)
\mathfrak{R}(T^{*}T)\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}
,and there exists
a
,
0\leqslant a\leqslant\infty
, such that for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
,

\|T^{*}Tf\|_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}\leqslant a^{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}},

where
\|\cdot\|
denote the norm in
\mathcal{H}
. The constant
a
is the same in all three parts. If one of those conditions is satisfied, the operators
T
and
T^{*}T
extend by continuity to bounded operators from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{H}
and from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{X}^{*}
, respectively.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{D}
and two triplets
(\mathcal{X}_{i},T_{i},a_{i}),i=1,2
, satisfy any of the conditions of Lemma
8.1
. Then for all choices of
i,j=1,2
,
\mathfrak{R}(T_{i}^{*}T_{j})\subset\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}
, and for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
, we have

\|T_{i}^{*}T_{j}f\|_{\mathcal{X}_{i}^{*}}\leqslant a_{i}a_{j}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}_{j}}.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a Banach space, let
\mathcal{X}
be stable under time restriction, let
\mathcal{X}
and
T
satisfy (any of) the conditions of Lemma
8.1
. Then the operator
T^{*}T
is a bounded operator from
L_{t}^{1}(I,\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{X}^{*}
and from
\mathcal{X}
to
L_{t}^{\infty}(I,\mathcal{H})
.

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
3.1
, but we provide only the main important steps.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Step 1:
It follows from
(\mathcal{A})
that the Schrödinger operator
e^{itL}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{1}(X)
to
L^{\infty}(X)
with

\|e^{itL}f\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(X)}.

The Schrödinger operator
e^{itL}
is a unitary operator on
L^{2}(X)
and we have

\|e^{itL}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

By Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we deduce that the Schrödinger operator
e^{itL}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{p^{\prime}}(X)
to
L^{p}(X)
with

\|e^{itL}f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim|t|^{-n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)},

(8.1)

for any
p\geq 2
.

Step 2:
We prove the diagonal Strichartz estimate. For the particular case
p_{1}=p_{2}=p\geq 2
,
q_{1}=q_{2}=q
and
0\leq\frac{2}{q}=n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)<1
, by (
8.1
), Minkowski’s inequality and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|F(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}}{|t-s|^{n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}}ds\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{I})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X)\right)}.

Step 3:
By the same procedure as Step 2, we also prove that

\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{i(t-s)L}F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X)\right)}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X)\right)}.

(8.2)

Step 4:
Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(X)
and
\mathcal{X}=L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X)\right)
in Lemma
8.1
. We shall define the bounded operator

\displaystyle T:L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(X)

\displaystyle F(x,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{-isL}F(x,s)ds,

whose adjoint operator is

\displaystyle T^{*}:L^{2}(X)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{q}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p}(X)\right)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{itL}f(x),

where duality is define by the scalar products in
L^{2}(X)
and in
L^{2}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{2}(X)\right)
. By factorization of
T^{*}T
, we have

T^{*}TF(x,t)=\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{i(t-s)L}F(x,s)ds.

The homogeneous Strichartz estimate and the dual homogeneous Strichartz estimate come out immediately from the obtained result (
8.2
) and Lemma
8.1
. Using Lemma
8.2
and (
8.2
), we get

\left\|\int_{\mathcal{I}}e^{i(t-s)L}F(\cdot,s)ds\right\|_{L^{q_{2}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{2}}(X)\right)}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q_{2}^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(X)\right)},

(8.3)

unfortunately, which is not the integral operator in the retarded Strichartz estimate because of the ”retardation” in time. For any function
F
on
\mathcal{I}\times X
, we can rewrite the integral operator by

\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)L}F(x,s)ds=\int_{\mathcal{I}}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(t-s)e^{i(t-s)L}\tilde{F}(x,s)ds,\;\forall t\in\mathcal{I},

where
\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}
is the characteristic function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\tilde{F}
is the continuation of
F
by zero over
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times X
.
Due to the fact that the space
\mathcal{X}=L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{I},L^{p^{\prime}}(X)\right)
is unstable under restriction in time (which means that the multiplication by the characteristic function of an interval in time is a bounded operator with norm uniformly bounded with respect to the interval), we can overcome this technical multiplication and prove the retarded Strichartz estimates by the same procedure as (
8.3
).
∎

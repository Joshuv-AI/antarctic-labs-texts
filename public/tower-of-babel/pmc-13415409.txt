Numerical investigation of heat and mass transfer in radiative micropolar Sutterby nanofluid flow with activation energy through a porous stretching sheet
Authors: Muhammad Jawad, Muhammad Waseem, Walid Abdelfattah, Tadesse Walelign, Abeer A. Shaaban
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In engineering and technology, the micropolar-Sutterby fluid serves as a favorable alternative to conventional non-Newtonian fluids due to its practical applicability, robustness, and computational efficiency. This analysis focuses on the thermophysical characteristics of the magnetized flow of a micropolar-Sutterby fluid bounded by an exponentially stretching surface, taking into account Cattaneo–Christov heat flux, thermal radiation, activation energy, and heat source, which have wide industrial applications. The surface is influenced by Darcy-Forchheimer effects and is situated within a porous medium. The impacts of mobile microorganisms with magnetic flux are also probed. Similarity transformations are used to convert the governing PDEs into coupled ODEs. The resulting ordinary differential equations are solved using the collocation-based MATLAB built-in solver bvp4c. Tables, graphs, and literature comparisons are used to demonstrate the impact of different parameters on the involved profiles. Quantitative results obtained from the numerical simulations reveal that increasing the thermal radiation parameter Rd from 0.5 to 0.9 enhances the local Nusselt number by 24.3%, while increasing the magnetic parameter Ha from 0.5 to 0.9 increases the local skin friction coefficients by 3.8% in the x-direction and 3.9% in the y-direction. Furthermore, increasing the micropolar parameter K from 0.1 to 0.5 enhances the local skin friction in the y-direction by 46.4%. It is evident from the results that the temperature field is significantly enhanced for increasing values of thermal radiation and thermophoresis.

Introduction

The flow of fluids, incorporating both Newtonian and non-Newtonian types, over a stretchable surface with encompassed heat transfer phenomenon under steady-state conditions represents a vital role in chemical and industrial engineering. In recent decades, considerable focus has been made toward boundary layer flows over stretched surfaces, given their importance to various mechanical, industrial, metallurgical and engineering processes. Notable applications include melt-spinning, and hot rolling, production of fiberglass and paper, cooling of metallic substrates, polymer extrusion, glass blowing, and among other. Significantly, this model is grounded in the kinetic theory of liquids rather than relying solely on realistic relationships. However, it is acknowledged that no existing model can fully summarize the complete range of properties demonstrated by non-Newtonian fluids [1, 2]. The boundary layer flow of non-Newtonian fluids over stretchable sheets is integral to various industrial and engineering applications, including polymers, food processing, textiles and leading to extensive research in this area. Sakiadis [3] is a pioneering researcher in this field, having examined boundary layer flow induced by a continuously moving solid sheet. Crane [4] expanded upon this concept by inspecting the flow of a viscous fluid passing through stretching sheet. Algehyne et al. [5] surveyed the boundary layer flow of nanofluid induced by stretchable sheet. Ramasekhar [6] examined the impact of magnetics flux and chemical diffusion on heated flow of tangent hyperbolic nanofluid inside a stretching surface. Jawad et al. [7] reviewed the electrical conducting flow of micropolar nanofluid with magnetics flux and stagnation points due to moving slip conditions. Jawwad et al. [8] reconnoitered the non-Newtonian flow of Carreau nanofluid with significance of chemical reaction due to radially extending surface. The term "nanofluid" refers to suspensions composed of nanoscale particles uniformly distributed within a conventional base fluid. Nanofluids have established extensive uses across numerous scientific disciplines, including industrial technologies, thermal management, nuclear engineering and biomechanics. Furthermore, nanofluids serve a critical role in improving both cooling and heating activities. Choi [9] was the first to introduce the concept of nanofluids. Farooq et al. [10] inspected the properties of second grade nanofluid with thermal radiation subjected extending sheet in permeable medium. Chetteti et al. [11] studied the impression of melting heat, dispersion and shape on electrical conducting flow of magnetized nanofluid via spongy medium. Bafe et al. [12] investigated the thermodynamics Williamson flow subjected to exponentially stretching sheet with Arrhenius activation in Darcy porous medium. Mahboobtosi et al. [13] inspected the time depended flow of nanofluid with convective boundary induced by extending cylinder in the manifestation of ternary hybrid nanoparticles. The Cattaneo-Christov heat transport structure enables the investigation of thermal energy generation and utilization within reaction systems through various modes of heat transfer, including advection, radiation, convection and diffusion. Wide-ranging research strengths have been shown to illuminate the patterns of heat flow and energy consumption, with applications across mechanical domains, medical and aeronautical. This combined research has led to the improvement of technologies such as heat exchangers and heat sensors. Cattaneo [14] improved the classical Fourier heat conduction model by introducing the concept of thermal relaxation time, which is known as the Maxwell–Cattaneo principle. Subsequently, Christov [15] enhanced Cattaneo’s model by integrating an upper-convected time derivative, resulting in an invariant formulation. Sreedevi et al. [16] searched the impact of Cattaneo–Christov on electrical Williamson nanofluid induced by spinning cylinder in the attendance of nanoparticles. Tulu et al. [17] premeditated the time in depended flow of electromagnetics nanoliquid with Cattaneo–Christov theory and viscous dissipation due to rotating disk in the exhibition of nanoparticles. The investigation of heat transfer and energy management is critical for the operation of numerous engineering and technical processes, including production of papers, glass manufacturing, power generation, nuclear reactor cooling, thermal insulation, and processes involving liquid metals. Given the pivotal role of heat transfer phenomena across various technical and industrial applications, there exists a substantial body of research in the current scientific literature addressing these topics [18, 19]. Afzal et al. [20] described the impact of thermal radiations and non‐Fourier heat on electrical conducting Maxwell nanofluids and Williamson nanofluids flow induced by porous medium with PST and PHF. Sinivasan et al. [21] scrutinized the boundary layer second-grade fluid flow along with ohmic dissipative and variable viscosity. Recent literature has highlighted the challenges associated with nanofluids that incorporate microorganisms to boost the thermal conductivity of the base fluid. This approach has potential implications for improving thermal management in various industrial and technological applications, including microelectronic systems and heat exchangers. Swamy et al. [22] inspected the influence of bioconvection and magnetic field on nanofluid containing hybrid nanoparticles via spongy inclined annulus in the occurrence of mobile microbes. Mabrouk et al. [23] et al. reconnoitered the time depended flow of two-phase hybrid nanofluids enclosing motile microorganisms with magnetics field, radiation parameter and shape effects. Jawad et al. [24] dissected the non-Newtonian flow of tangent hyperbolic fluid with mobile microbes and buoyancy forces. Agarwal et al. [25] studied the electromagnetics flow of Powell-Eyring nanofluid involving viscous dissipation and mobile microorganism’s effects induced by exponentially stretched surface. Khan et al. [26] studied the two phase free convection flow of dusty Jeffrey fluid with heat transfer between two vertical parallel plates. In another study, Khan et al. [28] studied the effect of relative magnetic field on unsteady two phase flow of Casson dusty fluid over a parallel plate utilising second law of thermodynamics. Devi et al. [29] studied the effect of an internal heat source on thermosolutal convection of Casson nanofluids in the Darcy-Brinkman model. Devi and Gupta [30] studied the blood-based binary Casson nanofluid convection with the effects of viscosity and conductivity embedded in a Darcy porous media. Another work by Devi and Gupta [31] considered the stability analysis of binary Casson nanofluid convection with variable viscosity and conductivity using the Darcy-Brinkman model. A nonlinear stratification model for squeezing flow between parallel plates integrating ternary hybrid nanoparticles with Soret-Dufour effects was presented by Mahanta and Sharma [32]. In another study, Mahanta and Sharma [33] explored the increased heat transfer rate analysis by applying Ohmic heating and numerous slips over an exponentially extending or shrinking plate for magnetohydrodynamic hybrid nanofluid using response surface methodology. In addition, Mahanta and Sharma [34] carried out the comparative study of tetra and ternary nanofluid flow over linear and exponential stretching sheets with varying thermal conductivity. Sharma et al. [35] studied the impact of Soret and Dufour effects on magnetohydrodynamic stagnation point flow and heat transfer towards a stretching sheet. Shahzad et al. [36] studied the electromagnetic control and dynamics of generalised Burgers’ nanoliquid flow with motile microorganisms with Cattaneo-Christov relations by Galerkin finite element method. Sajid et al. [37] studied the impact of magneto Reiner-Philippoff nanofluid flow with zero nanoparticle mass flux due to triple-diffusive convection. In another study, Sajid et al. [38] studied the properties of radioactive flow and slippage velocity on spinning two-phase Prandtl nanofluid with zero mass fluxing and convective limitations. Furthermore, Rasool et al. [39] studied chemical and bioconvective processes for Cross nanofluid flow along an oblique cylinder with the effect of a magnetic field at an angle using spectrum relaxation approach.

Novelty

The literature examination concludes that the impact of motile bacteria on three-dimensional combined micropolar-Sutterby flow with activation energy and chemical reaction persuaded by an exponential stretching sheet has not been studied yet. The present work introduces for the first time the simultaneous effects of variable magnetic flux, Cattaneo-Christov heat theory, heat source, Darcy-Forchheimer porous medium, and bioconvection in a micropolar-Sutterby nanofluid flow. This unique combination distinguishes the current study from previous investigations in the field of non-Newtonian fluid mechanics.

Industrial applications of proposed model

The proposed micropolar-Sutterby nanofluid model has a wide range of industrial and engineering applications. It is highly suitable for polymer extrusion processes, melt-spinning, hot rolling, glass fiber production, and cooling of metallic substrates. Additionally, the model is applicable to thermal management systems, microelectronics cooling, nuclear reactor thermal insulation, and bioengineering processes involving nanofluid transport with motile microorganisms. The inclusion of activation energy and thermal radiation further enhances its relevance to chemical engineering and energy-related industries.

Motivation

The motivation behind the present study is to address the growing demand for efficient heat and mass transfer enhancement in industrial processes. Conventional Newtonian fluids often fail to meet the thermal and rheological requirements of advanced manufacturing and energy systems. The micropolar-Sutterby fluid offers superior practical applicability, robustness, and computational efficiency over other non-Newtonian formulations. Furthermore, the integration of bioconvection with motile microorganisms provides a novel approach to improve thermal conductivity and stability of nanofluids, which is essential for future technological advancements.

Numerical scheme

The numerical solution of the coupled ordinary differential equations along with the associated boundary conditions is carried out using the bvp4c solver available in MATLAB. This solver relies on a finite difference approach that uses a three-stage collocation formula known as the Lobatto IIIa method. The resulting collocation polynomial gives a solution that is smooth and accurate up to fourth order across the entire domain. To solve the system of algebraic equations generated by this method, the solver uses a damped quasi-Newton iterative scheme, where the Jacobian matrix is updated at each iteration instead of being computed from scratch. The iteration stops when the largest residual among all algebraic equations falls below \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${10}^{-6}$$\end{document}. The solver settings include a relative tolerance of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${10}^{-6}$$\end{document} and an absolute tolerance of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${10}^{-6}$$\end{document} for every dependent variable. The mesh is adaptive, meaning that grid points are automatically added or removed to capture sharp gradients in the solution. An initial guess that satisfies the far-field and wall conditions is supplied, and the solver iterates until the convergence requirements are met (See Fig. 2).27\documentclass[12pt]{minimal}

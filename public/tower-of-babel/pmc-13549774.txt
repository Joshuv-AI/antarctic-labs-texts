Synchronous Multicentric WHO grade 3 Oligodendroglioma and WHO grade 3 Astrocytoma : A Case Report and Literature Review
Authors: Byung-Rae Cho, Young Suk Lee, Dong-Sup Chung, Wan-Soo Yoon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMulticentric gliomas are rare clinical entities, and cases exhibiting distinct histological lineages despite sharing a common molecular origin are exceptionally scarce. We report a unique case of synchronous multicentric gliomas in the bilateral frontal lobes of a 59-yearold female that shared an ancestral IDH1-R132H mutation but diverged into distinct molecular trajectories. According to the 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification, the left frontal tumor was diagnosed as a central nervous system (CNS) WHO grade 3 oligodendroglioma characterized by 1p/19q co-deletion and CDKN2A mutation, while the right frontal tumor was identified as a CNS WHO grade 3 astrocytoma harboring ATRX and TP53 mutations. This case provides compelling evidence for the stem cell origin theory, suggesting that a common progenitor undergoes independent clonal evolution in different anatomical locations through disparate secondary genetic alterations. Our findings underscore that multicentric lesions cannot be assumed to be molecularly identical, necessitating comprehensive and individual molecular evaluation of each discrete lesion to ensure accurate prognostic stratification and the formulation of personalized therapeutic strategies in the era of integrated diagnostics.Keywords: Glioma, Oligodendroglioma, Astrocytoma, Mutation, Multicentric, Case reports

INTRODUCTION

Gliomas represent the most prevalent primary malignant central nervous system (CNS) tumors. The 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification (5th edition) introduced a molecularly-integrated framework, categorizing isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH)-mutant gliomas into astrocytomas and oligodendrogliomas based on 1p/19q co-deletion [11], effectively replacing retrospective terms like mixed glioma. Despite this refinement, synchronous occurrences of multiple gliomas with disparate molecular profiles remain exceedingly rare [12]. These are defined as multifocal or multicentric based on their anatomical relationship; multicentric gliomas arise in distinct regions without evident continuity [4,18]. Multicentric cases are particularly rare; among these, glioblastomas (GBMs) are the most frequently reported phenotype, followed by anaplastic and low-grade astrocytomas [3,21].

We present a unique case of synchronous multicentric gliomas— a WHO grade 3 oligodendroglioma and a WHO grade 3 astrocytoma—occurring simultaneously in separate frontal locations. By employing an integrated molecular diagnostic approach, we characterize their clinico-pathological features and treatment courses. Furthermore, we analyze the divergent molecular evolution between the two tumors and discuss the broader implications for gliomagenesis.

CASE REPORT

A 59-year-old female was transferred to our hospital due to focal seizures and a 2-year history of perioral twitching. Approximately 5–6 months before admission, she developed delayed responsiveness, confusion, and slurred speech. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed bilateral frontal tumors with distinct radiological features. The right frontal lobe tumor measured 8.7×6.8 cm on T2-weighted imaging and involved the frontal base, insula, head of the caudate nucleus, and genu of the corpus callosum. The lesion showed minimal contrast enhancement with focal necrosis (Fig. 1A and B). The left frontal lobe tumor, located in the dorsomedial prefrontal cortex, measured 4.8×3.8 cm and appeared hyperintense with fluid signal on T2-weighted images. This lesion demonstrated partial contrast enhancement and necrosis (Fig. 1C and D).

Although the tumor in the left frontal lobe was relatively smaller in size, it exhibited more distinct contrast enhancement and necrosis on imaging. Therefore, the first stage of two-stage tumor resection was performed for the left tumor. Gross total resection was achieved, resulting in transient grade 4/5 weakness in the right upper extremity. Seven days later, a secondstage resection was performed for the right frontal lesion, which extended into the temporal pole and sylvian cistern. Residual tumor was intentionally left in the internal capsule and corpus callosum to minimize neurological injury (Fig. 1E and F). Although the patient experienced delayed awakening, she returned to baseline neurological status by postoperative day 5.

Histopathologic and molecular studies were performed on both tumors, which confirmed that the two tumors were histologically distinct (Table 1).

Pathological findings

The left frontal tumor showed high cellularity, microvascular proliferation, and necrosis, with a high mitotic index of 15/10 high-power field (HPF). Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was positive for IDH1-R132H and Olig2, while ATRX was retained (Fig. 2A-D). Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) confirmed 1p/19q co-deletion, and next-generation sequencing (NGS) identified mutations in IDH1 (c.395G>A; variant allele fraction [VAF], 46.89%), CDKN2A (c.238C>T; VAF, 42.36%), and TP53 (c.982_993+3del; VAF, 94.35%). Chromosomal losses at 1p, 19q, and 9p also demonstrated (Fig. 3A). Collectively, these integrated molecular and histopathological findings established a diagnosis of IDH-mutant, 1p/19q co-deleted, CNS WHO grade 3 oligodendroglioma.

The right frontal tumor exhibited nuclear pleomorphism and focal microvascular proliferation without necrosis; the mitotic rate was low (1–2/10 HPF). IHC revealed p53 overexpression and equivocal ATRX (Fig. 2E-H). FISH showed no 1p/19q deletions. NGS detected the same IDH1 (c.395G>A; VAF, 39.22%) mutation found in the contralateral lesion, alongside mutations in ATRX (c.5227A>G; VAF, 41.2%) and dual TP53 mutations (c.817C>T; VAF, 46.25%; c.587G>A; VAF, 21.14%). Chromosomal gain at 10p was identified. Based on the absence of 1p/19q codeletion (Fig. 3B) and the presence of ATRX and TP53 mutations, the tumor was diagnosed as an IDH-mutant, CNS WHO grade 3 astrocytoma.

Following bilateral resection, the patient underwent adjuvant radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions) followed by six cycles of procarbazine-lomustine-vincristine (PCV) chemotherapy. Eleven months later, MRI revealed recurrence along the corpus callosum. Despite two cycles of temozolomide (TMZ), the tumor progressed radiographically (Fig. 1G and H).

DISCUSSION

Synchronous multicentric gliomas refer to a condition where multiple discrete gliomas develop simultaneously in distinct brain regions without any anatomical continuity or obvious pathways for spread, such as cerebrospinal fluid seeding [4]. They are mainly observed to be rare in high grade gliomas and very rare in low grade glioma (LGG) [3]. Krenzlin et al. [10] reported that multicentric GBMs occurred in 12% of 483 newly diagnosed GBM patients. Their study noted that survival outcomes for patients undergoing complete or near-total resection were not statistically different from those with solitary lesions [10].

While clinical studies on the incidence of multicentric LGG remain scarce, a systematic search by Tariq et al. [17] identified 36 patients, 29 of whom had consistent histopathology across tumor sites, while four showed variable findings. However, the data included in this study contain cases reported before 2021, it was limited by a lack of molecular data regarding IDH mutation and 1p/19q co-deletion status. So, it is not meet the recently revised diagnostic criteria of the WHO classification. We have searched PubMed for literature about synchronous multicentric IDH-mutant WHO grade II, III, or IV gliomas, excluding GBMs, since 2021. Total six patients were reported, and all patients had different histopathologic findings across the tumor sites. Four patients had oligodendroglioma and astrocytoma simultaneously, and two patients had the difference of WHO grade in oligodendroglioma or astrocytoma lineage, which were grade 2 and 3 oligodendroglioma in one patient and grade 2 and grade 4 astrocytoma. Only one lesion was performed biopsy for pontine lesion, the other 11 lesions completed with gross total resection (six lesions) and subtotal resection (five lesions). The adjuvant treatment was variable including concurrent chemoradiation therapy, PCV, TMZ, and ACNU. Better survival outcomes were generally correlated with the presence of lower-grade gliomas, a trend consistent with our current findings (Table 2).

The pathogenesis of synchronous multicentric gliomas remains elusive, with theories including de novo occurrence, metastasis, and stem cell origin. The de novo and metastatic theories lack substantial support; the statistical probability of two independent gliomas with unrelated genetic backgrounds arising spontaneously is exceptionally low (4.4×10-9) [21]. Furthermore, genomic analysis revealing significant intra-tumoral genetic heterogeneity contradicts the metastasis theory [21].

In contrast, the stem cell origin theory provides a plausible explanation. Neural stem cells in the subventricular and subgranular zones may acquire one or more critical mutations during the neural precursor cell stage6, [13-15]. As these defective cells migrate and differentiate in multiple directions, they initiate gliomagenesis at distant sites. Supporting this theory, multicentric gliomas often harbor a small subset of shared truncal mutations, indicating a common ancestral cell [21].

GBMs and low-grade gliomas exhibit distinct profiles regarding their early genetic mutations. In GBMs, two key alterations, phosphatase and tensin homolog deletion and telomerase reverse transcriptase promoter mutation, are recognized as critical early genetic events that drive tumorigenic potential [1]. However, mutation in IDH1/2 are found as very early events in low-grade gliomas [7]. IDH1/2 mutations can make changes in the activity of NADP+-dependent IDH, cause the accumulation of abnormal metabolites, and lead to the occurrence of gliomas. IDH1-R132H is the most common type of IDH1 mutation, that is accounting for 90% of all IDH1 mutation [5,20]. In this case, the presence of the identical IDH1-R132H (c.395G>A) mutation in both tumors strongly suggests a monoclonal origin from a shared tumor stem cell. Following this shared early event in gliomagenesis, their subsequent evolutionary paths diverged significantly, leading to the development of an oligodendroglioma in the left frontal lobe and an astrocytoma in the right frontal lobe. Based on the NGS results, we suppose that the left frontal tumor followed the classic molecular trajectory of oligodendroglioma, characterized by the acquisition of 1p/19q co-deletion. The high VAF (94.35%) of the TP53 mutation in this tumor, combined with 9p loss, indicates a complete loss of heterozygosity and bi-allelic inactivation of tumor suppressor genes. This profound genomic alteration likely drove the high mitotic activity (15/10 HPF) and necrotic changes observed histologically, justifying its grade 3 designation despite its oligodendroglial lineage. In contrast, the right frontal tumor lacked the 1p/19q co-deletion but acquired IDH1 (p.Arg132His), ATRX (p.Arg1743Gly), and TP53 mutations, consistent with the molecular hallmarks of the astrocytic lineage. Notably, two distinct TP53 mutations were identified with disparate VAFs : 46.25% for p.Arg273Cys and 21.14% for p.Arg196Gln. This VAF discrepancy indicates intratumoral heterogeneity, where a foundational major clone underwent sequential clonal evolution. Specifically, the emergence of a secondary subclone harboring a second TP53 hit suggests subclonal double-hit kinetics, leading to the complete loss of TP53 function within that specific cell population. The gain of chromosome 10p further distinguishes this aggressive subclone, potentially providing a decisive proliferative advantage. Although histological necrosis was absent, this complex and evolving mutational profile underscores the high-grade transformation potential of this IDHmutant astrocytoma (Fig. 4).

Proposed clonal evolution and divergent molecular trajectories of the synchronous multicentric gliomas. Both the left and right frontal tumors share a common ancestral origin, characterized by an early IDH1 (c.395G>A) mutation in a single tumor stem cell. Following this shared initiating event, the tumors underwent distinct molecular evolutionary paths. The left frontal tumor followed the oligodendroglial lineage, acquiring 1p/19q co-deletion, 9p loss (CDKN2A mutation), and a TP53 (c.982_993+3del) mutation with LOH, leading to a WHO grade 3 oligodendroglioma. Conversely, the right frontal tumor diverged into the astrocytic lineage through the acquisition of ATRX and TP53 (c.817C>T) mutations, followed by a 10p gain and a second TP53 (c.587G>A) mutation, resulting in a WHO grade 3 astrocytoma. Despite O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylation of the right tumor, the patient showed rapid progression under TMZ therapy. This chemoresistance is likely driven by the elevated tumor mutation burden (3.78 mutations/Mb) and a complex TP53 mutational landscape. The disparate VAFs (46.25% and 21.14%) suggest subclonal double-hit kinetics, leading to p53 inactivation. Since p53 is essential for TMZ-induced apoptosis, its functional loss likely bypassed the favorable prognostic impact of MGMT methylation, resulting in aggressive clinical progression.

The limitations of this study include its single-case design and limited sequencing coverage. However, this case supports the stem cell origin theory of synchronous gliomas. While the migration pathway was initially unclear, the early involvement of the corpus callosum genu and the recurrence pattern along its anterior portion provide strong radiological evidence for trans-callosal microscopic dissemination. This suggests that a common mutated precursor likely utilized major white matter tracts for subclinical spread prior to the manifestation of bilateral lesions, reinforcing the shared oncogenic lineage of multifocal gliomas.

CONCLUSION

We presented a rare case of synchronous multicentric gliomas sharing an ancestral IDH1-R132H mutation but diverging into distinct lineages. Supporting the stem cell origin theory, multicentric lesions cannot be assumed to be molecularly or histologically identical. Therefore, comprehensive pathological and molecular evaluation of each discrete lesion is imperative for accurate grading and the tailoring of personalized therapeutic strategies.

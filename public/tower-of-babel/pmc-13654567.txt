Concomitant unroofing of anomalous aortic origin of the right coronary artery during bilateral lung transplantation
Authors: Haytham Elgharably, Zain Al Rstum, Kamal S Ayyat, Jorden Smith, Lauryn Benninger, Purav Shah, Samip Vasaiwala, Christopher DuCoin, Satish Chandrashekaran
Published: 2026

Case presentation

A 42-year-old gentleman with interstitial lung disease was referred to our center after being declined at other lung transplant (LTx) centers because of esophageal dysmotility, history of gastric bypass, and anomalous origin of the right coronary artery (RCA). Cardiac testing revealed pre-capillary pulmonary hypertension (pulmonary vascular resistance 5.5 Woods units) with moderate right ventricular (RV) dysfunction. Upper gastrointestinal testing showed no evidence of reflux. He was evaluated by our foregut surgery team with plan for surgical feeding tube into the remnant stomach if needed after transplant. Coronary computed tomography angiogram study showed slit-like RCA ostium with acute angle takeoff from the left coronary sinus into ∼ 5–10 mm intramural course between the aorta and pulmonary artery (Figure 1, A). However, due to his hemodynamics and high oxygen requirements (10 L at rest), he could not undergo a cardiac stress test to further characterize the pathophysiology associated with the anomalous RCA origin. After multidisciplinary discussion with adult congenital and interventional cardiology teams, surgical unroofing of the anomalous RCA origin was recommended given high-risk features on CTA scan to mitigate the risk of RV myocardial ischemia during the perioperative period, as fluctuations in systemic and pulmonary hemodynamics (e.g. coronary perfusion pressure, RV preload and after load) are not uncommon, such as in cases of primary graft dysfunction (incidence ∼10–30%).1 Additionally, the intended procedure is considered low risk.2, 3 The case was reviewed by our LTx committee and was approved for bilateral LTx with concomitant unroofing of the anomalous RCA origin.

A: Pre-transplant computed tomography angiography with 3D reconstruction showing a slit-like RCA ostium with acute angle takeoff from the left coronary sinus into ∼ 5–10 mm intramural course between the aorta and pulmonary artery. B: The dashed yellow line represents the course of the unroofing over a coronary probe, until the normal anatomical exit of the right coronary artery from the right coronary sinus.

A pediatric brain-dead donor (13-year-old) lung offer was accepted as appropriate size match for the recipient's markedly reduced intra-thoracic volume. Bilateral LTx was performed first via a clamshell incision on veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. Alternative surgical approaches could be bilateral thoracotomies or sternotomy. Following reperfusion of the second allograft, anticoagulation was adjusted for cardiopulmonary bypass. After cardioplegic arrest, transverse aortotomy was made, and the RCA intramural segment was then unroofed using pointed-tip electrocautery over a coronary probe (Figure 1, B). The patient came off cardiopulmonary bypass with mild RV dysfunction, improved from baseline, which could be secondary to normal pulmonary vascular compliance in the new lung allografts Total mechanical circulatory support duration was 176 min for both the LTx and unroofing procedures, and aortic cross-clamp time was 29 min. Patient was extubated on postoperative day 2, and primary graft dysfunction grade was 0 at 24 h, 1 at 48 h, and 72 h. Feeding was initiated using a nasogastric feeding tube, and modified barium swallow test on postoperative day 4 showed mild oropharyngeal dysphagia. Diet was advanced as tolerated without complications. Postoperative computed tomography angiography showed successful unroofing of the anomalous RCA origin without complications (Figure 2). The patient was discharged on postoperative day 14 on room air and tolerated diet with swallowing precautions. Follow-up at two months demonstrated excellent allograft function (80% predicted), and the patient resumed daily exercise without oxygen.

Comment

Anomalous aortic origin of coronary arteries is a rare congenital anomaly with an incidence of 0.1–0.3% (4). Although asymptomatic, intervention is recommended, especially for high-risk features such as slit-like orifice, acute angulation, and inter-atrial course, due to increased risk for arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, and sudden death.2, 3 However, anomalous RCA origin is commonly encountered in younger patients with normal ventricular function and hemodynamics. To our knowledge, we report, for the first time, concomitant unroofing of an anomalous RCA origin during LTx procedure in a patient with pulmonary fibrosis, secondary pulmonary hypertension, and RV dysfunction. In summary, this case highlights the importance of multidisciplinary evaluation at specialized LTx centers for patients presenting with complex medical and surgical profiles.

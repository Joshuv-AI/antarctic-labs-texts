Maternal loss of 24-hydroxylase causes increased intestinal calcium absorption and hypercalcemia during pregnancy but reduced skeletal resorption during lactation in mice
Authors: Alexandre S Maekawa, David Bennin, Sarah A Hartery, Beth J Kirby, Ingrid J Poulton, René St-Arnaud, Natalie A Sims, Christopher S Kovacs
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractInactivation of 24-hydroxylase (CYP24A1) causes mild hypercalcemia in humans that becomes severe and life-threatening during pregnancy through unclear mechanisms. We studied Cyp24a1 null mice during pregnancy, lactation, and post-weaning. We hypothesized that Cyp24a1 nulls have a much greater increase in calcitriol during pregnancy and lactation, leading to markedly increased intestinal calcium absorption and reduced lactational bone loss. WT and Cyp24a1 null sisters were mated to Cyp24a1+/- males. Timepoints included baseline (BL), late pregnancy (LP), mid-lactation (ML), late lactation (LL), and weekly x4 weeks of post-weaning recovery (R1-4). Assessments included intestinal calcium absorption (IntCaAbs) by gavage of 45Ca, BMC by DXA, microCT of femurs, 3-point bending tests of tibias, serum hormones, serum and urine minerals, milk analysis, and intestinal gene expression. At LP, whole body BMC increased equally by ~12% in null and WT. Calcitriol was 2.5-fold higher in nulls vs WT, accompanied by 3-fold increased IntCaAbs, hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, and 6.5-fold higher FGF23. PTH was suppressed in both. Twenty percent of null dams died during delivery but their serum calcium at LP did not differ from Cyp24a1 nulls that survived. At ML, calcitriol, IntCaAbs, and FGF23 declined in both genotypes but remained higher than BL values in Cyp24a1 nulls. By LL, nulls were still hypercalcemic vs WT, and had lost less mean whole body BMC (11% vs. 21%, p<0.02), but by micro-CT there were no differences from WT in cortical or trabecular bone mass. Lactational losses in BMC, cortical thickness, and trabecular number were restored by R4 in both genotypes. In summary, ablation of Cyp24a1 increased IntCaAbs and caused hypercalcemia during pregnancy and lactation, late gestational mortality in some nulls, and reduced lactational BMC loss. Treating women with gestational hypercalcemia from CYP24A1 mutations should focus on reducing calcitriol or IntCaAbs, since increased bone resorption is not the cause.

Introduction

Calcitriol, the active and hormonal form of vitamin D, is a key regulator of mineral and bone metabolism. It stimulates active and passive intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate, thereby supplying mineral to the skeleton.1 Loss of calcitriol’s actions, such as in severe vitamin D deficiency,2,3 genetic inability to make calcitriol (loss of the 1ɑ-hydroxylase [CYP27B1]),3–6 or genetic inability to respond to calcitriol (inactive vitamin D receptors [VDR]),3,6–9 all lead to reduced intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption, hypocalcemia, hypophosphatemia, secondary hyperparathyroidism, and under-mineralized softened bone (rickets or osteomalacia).

The importance of catabolism of calcitriol by the 24-hydroxylase enzyme, CYP24A1, has only recently been recognized in a spectrum of disorders. The most abundant and physiologically relevant expression of CYP24A1 is in the proximal kidney tubules, such that 24-hydroxylation of calcitriol and 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25OHD) are substantially reduced by renal failure and corrected by kidney transplant.10,11 Some babies with homozygous or compound heterozygous inactivating mutations of CYP24A1 present with infantile hypercalcemia type 1, whereas others are asymptomatic and escape detection.12,13 Adults with inactivating CYP24A1 mutations generally have mild hypercalcemia, elevated calcitriol, suppressed PTH, hypercalciuria, nephrolithiasis, and a presumed increase in intestinal calcium absorption.12,13 Sunlight exposure or the use of vitamin D supplements in such adults can precipitate more marked hypercalcemia.12 For many affected adults, loss of CYP24A1 is asymptomatic and clinically inapparent. The heterozygous state may be normal, although up to one-third of presumed heterozygotes have been found to have mild hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, and nephrolithiasis,12 while others have developed severe hypercalcemia.14,15

It is during pregnancy that loss of CYP24A1 can become critical due to the development of severe gestational hypercalcemia, with some women developing pancreatitis, nephrolithiasis, volume depletion, and other complications during pregnancy.16–19 The fetal parathyroids may become suppressed, leading to transient hypoparathyroidism after birth. This worsening during pregnancy is presumably related to the normal 2 to 5-fold increase in calcitriol that normally occurs during pregnancy, which contributes to a doubling of intestinal calcium absorption to meet the fetal demand for minerals.20 Animal studies have shown that calcitriol increases in the maternal circulation due to upregulation of renal CYP27B1; placentas and fetuses contribute only trivial amounts of calcitriol.20 The rise in maternal calcitriol is not stimulated by PTH, which normally falls to low values early in human and rodent pregnancies. Instead, animal and limited human data suggest that increased calcitriol synthesis may be driven by novel factors such as estradiol, prolactin, placental lactogen, oxytocin, or PTHrP.20 Therefore, when unopposed by catabolism from CYP24A1, an exaggerated rise in calcitriol may result during pregnancy, contributing to excessive intestinal calcium absorption and marked hypercalcemia. Such a marked increase in calcitriol might also stimulate bone resorption and renal calcium reabsorption.

Whether loss of CYP24A1 has any effects during lactation is unclear because case reports have largely focused on the hypercalcemic crisis during pregnancy. In one report of a breastfeeding woman with CYP24A1 deficiency, the hypercalcemia was milder and serum calcitriol was normal.16 During normal lactation, the maternal skeleton is resorbed to provide mineral to milk, largely under the influence of low estradiol and high levels of PTHrP originating from lactating breast tissue.20 PTH remains low while calcitriol falls to non-pregnant levels.20 Intestinal calcium absorption falls to normal in lactating women,21 while rodents maintain a high rate similar to pregnancy.22,23 Women require only skeletal resorption to provide the required mineral to milk, whereas rodents need a high rate of both intestinal calcium absorption and skeletal resorption to meet the proportionately greater calcium demand that a litter of pups requires.20 If higher calcitriol and increased intestinal calcium absorption persist during lactation, this may cause a compensatory decrease in skeletal resorption.

In the present studies, we investigated the effects of maternal Cyp24a1 ablation on calcium and bone metabolism during reproductive cycles in mice. We hypothesized that Cyp24a1 ablation causes increased serum calcitriol, marked hypercalcemia, increased intestinal calcium absorption, and possibly increased skeletal resorption during pregnancy. We also hypothesized that intestinal calcium absorption would remain elevated during lactation and reduce the bone resorption and loss of skeletal strength that normally occurs. These studies should inform about the mechanisms by which loss of CYP24A1 leads to gestational hypercalcemia in women and whether any effects during lactation should be expected.

Materials and methods

Animal husbandry

Cyp24a1 null mice (a global knockout) were generated in the laboratory of Dr. René St-Arnaud.24,25 The colony has been maintained in the original C57BL/6 strain by mating Cyp24a1+/- mice together. The pathogen-free facility is on a controlled 12 hr light/12 hr dark cycle. From the time of weaning, mice consumed a standard rodent chow that contains 1% calcium, 0.7% phosphorus, and 1.5 IU/g vitamin D3 (Envigo-Teklad TD2018).

The studies primarily involved WT and Cyp24a1 null first-degree relatives; a few studies were also carried out in related Cyp24a1+/- mice to determine if haploinsufficiency causes an intermediate phenotype on serum minerals or BMC during the reproductive cycle.

The Institutional Animal Care Committee of Memorial University of Newfoundland approved all procedures involving live animals.

Reproductive cycles and data collection timepoints

All studies followed approximate 80-d reproductive cycles (Figure 1). After a first assessment at 10 wk of age (baseline [BL]), WT and Cyp24a1 null mice were mated overnight to Cyp24a1+/- males. The presence of a vaginal mucus plug on the morning after mating marked embryonic day (ED) 0.5. Subsequent time points included late pregnancy (LP) (ED 18.5), mid-lactation (ML) (10 d after delivery), end of lactation (21 d after delivery), and weekly through the end of the post-weaning period (28 d after weaning). Litter sizes were adjusted if needed for an average of 6 nursed by WT and Cyp24a1 null dams.

Schematic showing sample collection time points during the reproductive cycle.

A team comprising the research assistant and graduate students worked on sample collection with all but the first author blinded to the genotype of the mouse. All subsequent analyses of stored samples were carried out in blinded fashion.

For the intestinal calcium absorption experiments, time points included BL, LP, and ML. These time points reflect that placental calcium transport to the fetus occurs at a peak rate in LP, while the daily rate of bone loss during lactation peaks around day 10 of lactation.20 Investigators were blinded to endpoint measurements.

Chemical and hormone assays

Serum was collected by tail vein at the main time points to measure minerals and hormones, with a cardiac puncture under general anesthesia done if the mouse was being euthanized for tissue collection. Upon being placed into a clean cage, mice promptly void; such spot morning urine was collected to measure minerals and creatinine. Urinary values were expressed relative to creatinine to adjust for differing concentrations of urine. Serum and urine calcium, magnesium, phosphorus, and creatinine were analyzed using colorimetric assays (Sekisui Diagnostics PEI Inc., Charlottetown, PEI). Fractional excretion of calcium, phosphorus, and magnesium were calculated from simultaneous collections of serum and urine using the formula [fractional excretion of X] = [Urine X/ Serum X] / [Urine Creatinine/Serum Creatinine].26 Enzyme-linked Immunosorbent Assays (EIAs) were used to measure parathyroid hormone (PTH 1-84) (Quidel, San Diego, CA), serum C-telopeptide of type 1 collagen (CTX), calcitriol, and calcifediol (Immunodiagnostic Systems Ltd., Boldon, Tyne and Wear, United Kingdom), and intact FGF23 (Kainos, Japan). Most analyses were done serially with samples from the same mice, although calcitriol was analyzed cross-sectionally due to the large sample size required. Fractional excretion of calcium, phosphorus, and magnesium required a mix of serial and cross-sectional samples. All samples were stored at −20°C until analyzed. To eliminate inter-assay variability, all samples for any assay were run at the same time. Any values below assay sensitivity were reset to values that equaled the respective assay’s detection limit; similarly, any values above the range of the assay were reset to equal the upper limit.

Intestinal calcium absorption

Intestinal calcium absorption was assessed using DeLuca’s methodology.27 Mice were fasted overnight and gavaged with a 0.3 mL solution containing 0.5 μCi 45Ca in a buffer (0.5 mM CaCl2, 10 mM Tris-acetate, pH 7.5). After 10 min, blood was collected by cardiac puncture, serum was separated (1500 g, 15 min) and stored at −20°C until analyzed. Fifty (50) μL of serum was bleached (30% H2O2, 3 N KOH) for 30 min and neutralized with 5 N HCl. Five (5) mL of ScintiSafe 30% Cocktail (Fisher Chemical, Canada) was added and radioactivity in sera was counted in a liquid scintillation counter (Beckman Coulter LS6500, Brea, California, United States). Counts were adjusted for total blood volume and expressed as percent of administered dose.

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative RT-PCR (qPCR)

Duodena and kidneys of WT and Cyp24a1 null mice were harvested in LP and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was purified using the RNeasy Midi Lipid kit (Qiagen, Toronto, ON). RNA quantity and quality were confirmed with the Agilent 2100 BioAnalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA). TaqMan Gene Expression Assays (with the manufacturer’s pre-designed primers and probes for optimal amplification), and Fast Advanced Master Mix from Applied BioSystems (ABI)/Life Technologies (Burlington, ON), were used to determine the expression of genes involved in calcium and phosphate transport in duodena, and calcitriol synthesis and catabolism in kidneys. This included genes relevant for calcium absorption (calbindin-D9k (S100g), Ca2+-ATPase (Atp2b1), potassium calcium-activated channel subfamily M alpha 1 (KCNma1), sodium-calcium exchanger type 1 (Ncx1), transient receptor potential vanilloid 6 (Trpv6), phosphate absorption (sodium-phosphate transporter NaPi2b and phosphate transporters Pit1 and Pit2), and calcitriol (Cyp27b1, Cyp24a1, Vdr, and protein disulfide isomerase family A, member 3 [Pdia3]). The reference gene was Gapdh. Details of conditions and cycle times have been previously reported.28–30 Briefly, cDNA was synthesized using the Taqman High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (ABI), and multiplex qPCR reactions were run in triplicate on the ViiA 7 Real-Time PCR System (ABI).29,31 The minimum sample size was 5 for each genotype. Relative expression was determined from the threshold cycle (CT) compared to the reference gene.

Milk collection and analysis

On day 10 of lactation, milk was collected and stored at −20°C for calcium and protein analysis. The dams were separated from the pups for 2 hr, anesthetized, given 1 IU of oxytocin intraperitoneally, and kept in a supine position. After 10 min, all mammary glands were squeezed from the base to apex by hand, and milk was collected with a 200 μL pipette. Samples were stored at −20°C until analyzed for calcium and protein by previously described methods.28 In brief, milk was diluted 1:400 with distilled water and analyzed for calcium content using a Model 2380 Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometer (Perkin-Elmer, Norwalk, CT). Because much of milk calcium is bound to proteins, and to control for differing milk concentrations, the protein content of milk was measured using the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (ThermoFisher Scientific, Burlington, ON). Final results were expressed as mg calcium per mg protein. Samples of 20 microliters of milk were separately drawn into capillary tubes and centrifuged for 20 min to determine the proportion of cream to total volume (creamatocrit).32

Serial measurements of bone mineral content

As previously described,28 a PIXImus 2 Bone Densitometer (GE Lunar, Madison, WI), calibrated daily to a standard phantom, was used to obtain serial dual x-ray absorptiometry (DXA) measurements of total body, lumbar spine, and hind limb at the main time points. Two measurements were done in the BL interval several days apart to confirm that BMC was relatively stable, with the 2 values averaged to provide the mean BL value. If the 2 values showed an increase, a third or fourth reading was taken, each several days apart, until it was clear that stability was achieved. Subsequent DXA measurements were expressed relative to the stable mean BL value for each mouse.

Bone harvest and mechanical testing

Tibias and femurs were harvested at the main time points and stripped of soft tissues.

One tibia from each mouse was initially frozen at −20°C and then later thawed and rehydrated in phosphate-buffered saline at room temperature for at least 2 hr prior to the biomechanical testing. Cortical bone strength was assayed using a single column Instron Series 3340 electromechanical test instrument (Instron, Norwood, MA). In brief, a 3-point bend test was carried out with each tibia immobilized at each end in a fixture and subjected to a maximum load of 10 N directed against the tibial mid-shaft until failure (breakage). A force-displacement curve was generated by the Instron Series IX/s Automated Materials Tester Software Version 8.30.00, from which ultimate load, stiffness, and displacement were derived. All bones collected from every time point were analyzed in a single session.

One femur from each mouse was fixed in 10% buffered formalin for examination by microCT.30,33,34

MicroCT studies of femurs

A high resolution microCT scanner (Skyscan 1276, Kontich, Belgium) acquired femoral images with 9-micron voxel resolution, as previously described.35 Reconstruction and analysis of the images were performed using Skyscan software programs NRecon (version 1.7.4.2), DataViewer (version 1.5.6), and CT Analyzer (version 1.16.4.1). Femoral lengths were measured, and the diaphyseal (cortical) region analyzed commenced at a distance 30% of the length of the bone proximal to the distal end of the femoral growth plate, while the metaphyseal (trabecular) region commenced at 10% of bone length proximal to the distal end of the femoral growth plate; both had regions of interest with a length of 15% bone length. The lower thresholds for detecting bone were equivalent to 0.3579 g/cm3 CaHA for trabecular, and 0.5229 g/cm3 CaHA for cortical bone.

Statistical analyses

Rather than doing power calculations for each assay, we targeted empirical sample sizes of 10 per time point, balanced against the maximum number of samples that can be analyzed at any one time on an EIA plate. For microCT and qPCR, 5 samples per genotype are a consensus standard. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 10 for MacOS, Version 10.0.3 (GraphPad Software, Boston, MA). qPCR data were analyzed by the Comparative CT Method (2ΔCT).36 Prespecified primary outcomes were paired (t-test) comparisons of WT and Cyp24a1 null for serum calcium and intestinal calcium absorption at the end of pregnancy, and loss of BMC by DXA at the end of lactation. All other analyses used 2-way ANOVA on the entire dataset (both genotypes and all time points) with Tukey’s post hoc test to correct for multiple comparisons. Two-tailed probabilities for all data are reported as mean ± SD.

Results

Longitudinal changes in serum and urine chemistries and hormones

At BL serum calcium was not significantly different between WT and Cyp24a1 null dams, after which Cyp24a1 nulls became hypercalcemic during pregnancy (Supplementary Figure S1 and Figure 2A), before returning to values no different from WT at the remaining time points (Figure 2A). Approximately 20% of Cyp24a1 nulls had difficulty in the birthing process and were immediately euthanized; problems included failure to deliver at all or that offspring became stuck in the vagina (dystocia). The serum calcium of these mice was available from the day before delivery and proved to be non-significantly higher than Cyp24a1 nulls that went on to deliver successfully (Figure 2A). Serum calcium from all Cyp24a1 nulls during LP are combined for comparison to WT in Supplementary Figure S1.

Serum phosphorus did not differ between WT and Cyp24a1 nulls at any time point; however, phosphorus was greater at ML in both genotypes, consistent with bone resorption leading to release of skeletal phosphate (in the form of hydroxyapatite) during lactation (Figure 2B). Conversely, Cyp24a1 nulls had significantly higher serum magnesium at BL that progressively increased to peak at ML and late lactation (LL) (Figure 2C).

In limited studies of Cyp24a1+/- mice, serum calcium, phosphorus, and magnesium did not differ from WT at any time point (not shown).

Mean serum PTH was not significantly different in Cyp24a1 nulls at BL compared to WT. During pregnancy and ML, PTH was suppressed to at or near the lower limit in both genotypes, which is normal during these time periods, after which PTH rose in both toward BL values (Figure 2D).

Serum calcitriol rose in both genotypes during pregnancy as expected, with the increase being 2.5-fold higher in Cyp24a1 nulls; the increase was attenuated at ML, and restored to normal levels by R1 (Figure 2E). The absence of CYP24A1-mediated catabolism also contributed to an expected higher calcifediol or 25OHD level in Cyp24a1 nulls at BL (Figure 2F). In both genotypes, 25OHD was significantly reduced during pregnancy, with the drop being greater in Cyp24a1 nulls; subsequently, 25OHD returned toward BL values during post-weaning recovery, but did not reach a level significantly higher than controls at these time points (Figure 2F). Serum intact FGF23 transiently rose 6.5-fold in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT during pregnancy (Figure 2G).

Urine calcium excretion did not differ between genotypes at BL but rose significantly in Cyp24a1 nulls at LP (Figure 2H). Urine phosphorus showed no statistically significant changes (Figure 2I). Urine magnesium rose significantly in both genotypes from BL to LP before returning to the BL values in LL (Figure 2J).

Using separate collections of serum, the albumin-adjusted serum calcium was measured and confirmed persistent hypercalcemia in Cyp24a1 nulls during pregnancy (Figure 3A). Fractional excretion of calcium (Figure 3B) and phosphorus (Figure 3C) did not differ between genotypes at BL but were significantly increased in Cyp24a1 nulls during pregnancy; there were no differences between genotypes or time points for fractional excretion of magnesium (Figure 3D). Notably, due to insufficient remaining sample volumes, measurement of fractional excretion of phosphorus and magnesium during pregnancy could be measured only in a few Cyp24a1 null mice.

Renal expression of Cyp24a1 and Cyp27b1

Consistent with the global gene ablation, renal expression of Cyp24a1 was absent in the Cyp24a1 null at BL and pregnancy, whereas it increased 3.9-fold in WT during pregnancy as expected (Figure 4A). Conversely, renal expression of Cyp27b1 was downregulated more than 95% in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT at BL and remained so during pregnancy (Figure 4B).

Intestinal calcium absorption

Intestinal calcium absorption increased more markedly in Cyp24a1 nulls during pregnancy and lactation, with the value in Cyp24a1 nulls being 3-fold that of WT in LP (Figure 5A). This increase during pregnancy was accompanied in Cyp24a1 nulls by higher duodenal mRNA levels of calcium-relevant channels and transporters Trpv6, Atp2b1, and S100g (Figure 5B–D). In both genotypes, mRNA levels of Ncx1 were significantly lower (Figure 5E) and Kcnma1 was not significantly changed (Figure 5F). Vdr mRNA levels were also reduced during pregnancy in both genotypes (Figure 5G) while Cyp27b1 was unchanged (Figure 5H). Cyp24a1 showed a low level of expression in duodena that rose significantly in WT but remained low to undetectable in Cyp24a1 nulls (Figure 5I). With respect to phosphate metabolism, duodenal Napi2b expression increased significantly in Cyp24a1 nulls (Figure 5J), while Pit1 and Pit2 expression were unchanged (Figure 5K and L).

Milk calcium

Milk from Cyp24a1 nulls had higher calcium and protein content concentrations (Figure 6A and B) but when corrected for protein, the calcium content was no different than in WT (Figure 6C). The results were consistent with milk that was more concentrated in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT. The fractional volume represented by cream (creamatocrit) did not differ between genotypes (Figure 6D).

Longitudinal assessment of skeletal mineral content by DXA

Whole body, lumbar spine, and hind limb BMC were no different between WT and Cyp24a1 nulls at BL (Figure 7A–C). After this, both genotypes appeared to gain whole body and hindlimb BMC while a small loss of spine BMC occurred during pregnancy, with no significant differences between genotypes (Figure 7A–C). By LL, BMC had declined in both genotypes at all 3 skeletal sites, with Cyp24a1 nulls losing significantly less BMC than WT at the whole body and spine (the value at the hind limb was near significant at p<0.08). (Figure 7A–C). After this, BMC increased at all 3 sites in both genotypes to values that were significantly higher than BL at the hindlimb, but not at the whole body or lumbar spine. In limited studies of Cyp24a1+/- mice, the changes in BMC were equivalent to WT at all time points (not shown). The bone resorption marker CTX was not increased in LP in Cyp24a1 nulls, and was significantly lower in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT in LP (Figure 7D).

Bone strength

The 3-pt bend test showed significantly lower tibial strength in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT at BL, as indicated by ultimate load (Figure 7E). In both genotypes, ultimate load declined significantly by LL and increased during post-weaning to achieve values that were not significantly different from respective BL (Figure 7E). Bone strength increased in Cyp24a1 nulls to become no different from WT during pregnancy and LL, after which strength trended downward during post-weaning in Cyp24a1 nulls to approach the lower bone strength seen at BL (Figure 7E). Maximum displacement did not change significantly in either genotype (Figure 7F), while stiffness decreased in Cyp24a1 nulls only at LL (Figure 7G).

Trabecular bone volume (Figure 9G) and number (Figure 9H) were equal between WT and Cyp24a1 nulls at BL but increased markedly during pregnancy in Cyp24a1 nulls before declining during lactation to BL values. Trabecular separation (Figure 9I) increased significantly by LL in both genotypes before returning to BL values, while trabecular thickness (Figure 9J) increased in WT during lactation and showed a similar but nonsignificant increase in Cyp24a1 nulls.

Cortical thickness (E) and cortical area (F) were no different between WT and Cyp24a1 nulls at baseline, decreased significantly but equally during lactation, and returned to baseline values. Trabecular bone volume (G) and number (H) were equal between WT and Cyp24a1 nulls at baseline but increased markedly during pregnancy in Cyp24a1 nulls before declining during lactation to baseline values. Trabecular separation (I) increased significantly by late lactation in both genotypes before returning to baseline values, while trabecular thickness (J) increased in WT during lactation and showed a similar but nonsignificant increase in Cyp24a1 nulls. Data are shown as box plots with individual data points, median, quartiles, and maximum and minimum values; n = 5/group. p values are from 2-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc tests.

Discussion

In the BL (non-pregnant) state, the effects of Cyp24a1 ablation on mineral metabolism were mild, with no significant differences seen in serum calcium, phosphorus, PTH, calcitriol, or FGF23 compared to WT. However, a higher level of 25OHD was found in Cyp24a1 nulls, which was expected due to its long half-life and loss of catabolism by CYP24A1.

Tibial bone strength was unexpectedly lower prior to pregnancy in Cyp24a1 nulls, despite no differences between genotypes in BMC of the whole body, lumbar spine, or hind limb as assessed by DXA, or in cortical or trabecular microarchitecture of the femurs as assessed by microCT. Thus, lack of CYP24A1 adversely affected skeletal strength in nonpregnant females without any discernible differences in mineralization or microarchitecture. This may imply a difference in some aspect of the composition of bone that has not otherwise been detected. Prior study of Cyp24a1 null adults had shown evidence of adverse skeletal effects, specifically that endochondral ossification was suboptimal during fracture repair, with smaller callus, reduced stiffness, reduced mineralization, and delayed healing.37–39 These adverse effects were rescued by treatment with the missing metabolite 24,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (24,25(OH)2D) and not by calcitriol,39 suggesting that 24,25(OH)2D plays an essential role in maintaining skeletal strength.

That calcitriol was no different between genotypes at BL, despite a greater availability of the substrate 25OHD in the Cyp24a1 null, is consistent with observations in the non-pregnant adult CYP24A1-deficient human, where serum calcium and calcitriol may be normal or only slightly elevated.12,13 This reflects that the synthesis and catabolism of calcitriol are regulated by several factors, including PTH, FGF23, serum calcium, serum phosphorus, and calcitriol itself. In the non-pregnant state, loss of CYP24A1-mediated catabolism of calcitriol is primarily compensated by reduced CYP27B1-mediated synthesis with a possible increase in other catabolic pathways [eg, CYP3A4-mediated catabolism40] to maintain the status quo. Detailed study of the pharmacokinetics of calcitriol in Cyp24a1 null mice have previously shown that the clearance of calcitriol was greatly reduced to 96 hr as compared to 6-12 hr in heterozygous-deleted mice, and that renal Cyp27b1 mRNA levels were markedly downregulated in Cyp24a1 nulls.41 Consistent with that report, we confirmed that renal Cyp27b1 mRNA levels were reduced over 95% in Cyp24a1 null mice at pre-pregnancy.

Calcitriol normally increases several-fold during pregnancy through PTH-independent mechanisms.20,42 Data from rodents, guinea pigs, sheep, and rabbits have confirmed that synthesis of calcitriol increases several-fold in the maternal kidneys through upregulation of renal CYP27B1,43–48 while case reports of anephric women have revealed that absence of kidneys prevents the maternal rise in calcitriol.49 In turn, higher calcitriol concentrations contribute to a normal doubling of the efficiency of intestinal calcium absorption during pregnancy.20 In Cyp24a1 nulls, the 2.5-fold higher increase in calcitriol compared to WT, was likely due to the lack of opposing CYP24A1-mediated catabolism of the expected pregnancy-related increase in CYP27B1 activity and calcitriol. In turn, the increased calcitriol consumed more 25OHD than normal, thereby causing a greater decline in 25OHD during pregnancy compared to WT, altered expression of calciotropic genes in the duodena, and a 3-fold higher rate of intestinal calcium absorption in Cyp24a1 nulls, which explains their hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria. Skeletal resorption is unlikely to have contributed to the hypercalcemia, given that bone mass increased equally in both genotypes at the whole body and hind limb during pregnancy and remained unchanged at the lumbar spine. Moreover, Cyp24a1 nulls did not exhibit an increase in CTX during pregnancy, with levels being lower than WT, consistent with less bone resorption. Calcitriol-mediated renal calcium conservation is also unlikely to have contributed to the hypercalcemia, since marked hypercalciuria and increased fractional renal calcium excretion occurred in the Cyp24a1 nulls. Hypercalciuria normally develops during pregnancy to excrete excess calcium absorbed through the intestines (so-called “absorptive hypercalciuria”)20; this response was more marked in Cyp24a1 nulls due to increased net intestinal calcium absorption. Overall, the mechanism for the gestational hypercalcemia is excess calcitriol causing an abnormally high rate of intestinal calcium absorption.

Despite the marked increase in calcitriol during pregnancy, we did not find an increase in renal Cyp27b1 expression during pregnancy of Cyp24a1 null mice. Instead, it remained very low, comparable to the pre-pregnancy value that had been seen in prior studies of nonpregnant Cyp24a1 nulls.41 We did not measure the CYP24A1 protein level or synthesis of calcitriol itself, but synthesis must have been increased to account for the marked fall in 25OHD that occurred. Since Cyp27b1 expression was markedly reduced at BL, and considering the prolonged 96-hr half-life of calcitriol in Cyp24a1 null mice, only a small increase in synthesis of calcitriol would be needed to cause a precipitous rise in its serum level. Alternatively, although the evidence is that fetal and placental production of calcitriol normally contributes only a trivial amount to the maternal circulation during pregnancy, as discussed above, that small amount may be sufficient to cause the marked rise in serum calcitriol of Cyp24a1 null mice when its long half-life is again considered.

Calcitriol also stimulates intestinal phosphate absorption and renal phosphate conservation; the marked increase in intestinal expression of Napi2b predicts that intestinal phosphate absorption may be increased in Cyp24a1 nulls, although we did not measure this. Serum phosphorus remained normal in Cyp24a1 nulls during pregnancy, urine phosphate/creatinine was not significantly altered, while limited data suggested a significant increase in fractional renal phosphate excretion. That serum phosphorus remained normal is likely due to 6.5-fold higher FGF23 in pregnant Cyp24a1 nulls, which would compete with calcitriol to reduce intestinal phosphate absorption and promote renal phosphate excretion. The net result was no change in serum phosphorus or urine phosphate excretion.

The regulation of magnesium metabolism has not been as extensively investigated as calcium or phosphate. We found hypermagnesemia in Cyp24a1 nulls at BL and during pregnancy, which became even more marked during lactation. Fractional urinary excretion of magnesium was no different between genotypes at pre-pregnancy, and our limited data are consistent with no change in fractional excretion during pregnancy. Among the sparse literature, some studies in rodents and humans suggested that calcitriol stimulates intestinal magnesium absorption,50–52 whereas other studies found no effect of calcitriol.53,54 Magnesium deficiency has been shown to inhibit Cyp27b1 and stimulate Cyp24a1 mRNA levels,55,56 whereas in other studies magnesium had no effect on the in vivo conversion of 25OHD into calcitriol or 24,25(OH)2D.57 An explanation for the hypermagnesemia in Cyp24a1 null mice is beyond the scope of this manuscript.

The altered calcium metabolism in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT lessened during lactation. Calcitriol remained significantly elevated over BL, intestinal calcium absorption was nonsignificantly increased compared to WT, while serum and urine calcium were normal. In longitudinal studies, BMC decreased at all 3 sites in both genotypes, but Cyp24a1 nulls lost significantly less bone mass than WT at the whole body and lumbar spine (p<0.05), and borderline less at the hind limb (p=0.08). CTX was also non-significantly lower in Cyp24a1 nulls compared to WT during lactation. The reduced bone loss in Cyp24a1 nulls implies that intestinal calcium absorption was likely truly increased during lactation, thereby lessening the requirement for skeletal resorption to support milk production. Reduced skeletal resorption may involve a compensatory reduction in PTHrP production by the mammary glands, but PTHrP was not measured in this study. Rodents use both intestinal calcium absorption and skeletal resorption to provide calcium for milk, with each route able to compensate for the other. For example, a calcium-enriched diet leads to reduced bone resorption during lactation,42 while a low calcium diet is compensated for by increased bone resorption.20 Milk calcium content, corrected for protein, was no different between Cyp24a1 nulls and WT, which confirms that net calcium delivery to the mammary glands remained normal.

Bone resorption markers normally peak by ML at 2-fold or greater than BL and LP values,20 but serum CTX was not significantly higher than BL in both genotypes at this time point. This is due to the technical issue that ML blood samples were drawn during the milk collection procedure, 2 hr after separation of the dams from their litters. CTX has a half-life of about an hour58,59; therefore, a sample drawn 2 hr after separation will not reflect the peak resorptive activity that occurred while the pups were suckling. Conversely, we have found CTX to be double the BL value when drawn immediately after removing the dam from her pups,35 as is the resorption marker deoxypyridinoline,30,42 which is stably excreted into urine.

Cross-sectional studies of femoral bone structure by microCT showed progressive increases in femur lengths during pregnancy and lactation, which were similar in the 2 genotypes. This was expected as the mice aged approximately 80 d from the beginning to end of their reproductive cycles. There were also similar and significant increases in endocortical and periosteal perimeters, which occurred in the 3 wk elapsed between LP and the end of lactation, and not during the 4 wk elapsed between BL and LP. These changes in bone macro-architecture indicate that the diameters of the femurs have increased. Other studies in rodents have reported increased cross-sectional diameters, cortical perimeters, and volumes of the tibiae or femora by post-weaning as compared to pre-pregnancy or to age-matched controls.28,60,61 Limited human data also suggest that cortical expansion in width may also occur during lactation and post-weaning recovery.20 Such changes in the diameter of a cylinder will increase the cross-sectional and polar moments of inertia, and, thereby, the breaking strength. This may contribute to bone strength being maintained during lactation in the femurs as skeletal resorption progresses, and even if the cortical and trabecular microarchitecture do not fully recover afterwards. However, continued growth could contribute to these changes; age-matched non-pregnant controls could resolve this question.

In outbred strains of mice that we have studied, such as BlackSwiss, BMC of whole body, lumbar spine, and hind limb all increase significantly during pregnancy.20 Conversely, in the inbred C57BL/6J mouse strain (such as in this study) the pattern is slightly different: whole body and hind limb increase during pregnancy while the lumbar spine does not change or even decreases slightly.20 The gains in hindlimb BMC may also be driven in part by the approximate doubling of body weight that occurs during pregnancy, which increases loading on the limbs. These increases in BMC prepare the maternal skeleton for the increased need to resorb mineral during lactation by creating more easily resorbed trabecular bone (similar to the medullary bone that is rapidly resorbed in egg layers during the time of egg shell calcification).20 In the composite images of Figure 8, one can see that an increase in trabecular bone occurs within the primary spongiosa of both WT and Cyp24a1 null mice between BL and LP, and this disappears during lactation and is not seen at later time points. It is likely more pronounced in the Cyp24a1 null mice for 2 reasons, one being that the increase in intestinal calcium absorption enables the newly formed trabeculae to become more fully mineralized and thereby detectable by microCT (which is set to detect fully mineralized bone), and second, that the higher calcitriol levels may also stimulate osteoblast activity to a greater degree than in WT. Very high levels of calcitriol, as occurs in hypervitaminosis D, can stimulate osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, but resorption was not increased in these mice compared to WT during pregnancy or lactation.

Approximately 20% of Cyp24a1 null mothers had difficulty in delivering due to failure to progress or dystocia, leading to fetuses stuck in the uterus. It is possible that these mothers had more extreme hypercalcemia during delivery that contributed to lethargy, muscle weakness, and dehydration. Their mean serum calcium was numerically but non-significantly higher the day before delivery in Cyp24a1 nulls that had difficulty delivering compared to those that successfully delivered, but this does not exclude that the serum calcium may have been higher during the birthing difficulties. Blood could not be taken when the mice were in difficulty because the ethical priority was to euthanize them immediately. The higher individual calcium and protein concentrations in milk obtained from Cyp24a1 null mothers who survived suggest that the water content of milk is reduced, which is consistent with Cyp24a1 null mothers being somewhat dehydrated compared to WT. However, the lack of a difference in hematocrit or serum creatinine between genotypes at any time point indicates that Cyp24a1 null mothers were not dehydrated. The standard for assessing milk calcium content is to correct for protein content, since much of milk calcium binds to protein, which is why we have concluded that the overall calcium content of milk was normal.

Our findings in the Cyp24a1 null mouse model confirm that hypercalcemia develops mainly from increased intestinal calcium absorption without obvious contributions from skeletal resorption or renal calcium conservation; instead, CTX is significantly reduced during pregnancy and bone loss is reduced during lactation. The relevance for humans is that a similar, exuberant increase in intestinal calcium absorption is expected to occur in women bearing CYP24A1 inactivating mutations during pregnancy. Therefore, treatments for hypercalcemia that predominantly reduce skeletal resorption (eg, bisphosphonates and denosumab) are likely to be ineffective. Treatment options that should work include increased hydration, dietary calcium restriction, phosphate supplementation to bind dietary calcium, oral glucocorticoids to suppress intestinal calcium absorption, and calcitonin or loop diuretics to promote urinary calcium excretion. Approaches that are more specific to the mechanism of gestational hypercalcemia include use of azoles (ketoconazole, fluconazole, and itraconazole) to inhibit CYP27B1 activity,13 and rifampin to stimulate CYP3A4 activity and, thereby, 23-hydroxylated catabolism of calcitriol.62,63 We are aware of no data on the safety of these specific approaches to treat hypercalcemia in pregnancy, and we plan to test the various modalities in this mouse model.

Strengths of this study include that the WT and Cyp24a1 null mice were first-degree relatives and nursed a similar number of pups, and that most analyses were longitudinal. Weaknesses include the necessity for microCT and 3-pt bend tests to be done on cross-sectional analyses, and that results in rodents do not necessarily indicate what will happen in humans.

In conclusion, loss of Cyp24a1 in mice induced gestational hypercalcemia by causing increased intestinal calcium absorption. The increases in calcitriol and intestinal calcium absorption were attenuated during lactation, but sufficient to result in Cyp24a1 nulls resorbing less BMC than normal to supply the milk. The Cyp24a1 null skeleton did show reduced bone strength at BL, which remains unexplained. Although the skeleton underwent the typical losses during lactation with regain during post-weaning, the long-term effects of the Cyp24a1 null state on the skeleton are unknown. Assessment of women bearing CYP24A1 inactivating mutations is needed to determine if there are long-term consequences to skeletal health after reproduction.

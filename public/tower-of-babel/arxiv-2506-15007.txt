Dynamic Optimal Transport with optimal star shaped graphs
Marcello Carioni, Juliane Krautz, Jan-F. Pietschmann

Abstract
We study an optimal transport problem in a compact convex set Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} where bulk transport is coupled to dynamic optimal transport on a metric graph 𝖦 = ( 𝖵 , 𝖤 ) \mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E}) which is embedded in Ω \Omega . We prove existence of solutions for fixed graphs. Next, we consider varying graphs, yet only for the case of star-shaped ones. Here, the action functional is augmented by an additional penalty that prevents the edges of the graph to overlap. This allows to preserve the graph topology and thus to rely on standard techniques in Calculus of Variations in order to show existence of minimizers.

Introduction

Metric graphs play an important role in the modeling of real-world phenomena such as gas or road networks,
[
FBP24
,
CGP05
]
. In particular, the theory of optimal transport has proven to be a useful tool to analyze transportation on such graphs. In recent years, several related formulations have been studied, see e.g.
[
EFMM22
]
, where the authors provide a complete analysis of optimal transport on metric graphs with Kirchhoff-Neumann conditions at the vertices. Furthermore,

[
BHP23
]
studies a transport metric that allows for mass storage on the vertices. These studies are strongly motivated by and related to a transport distance involving bulk and surface transport that was introduced in
[
Mon21
]
.
Related gradient flows with respect to these distances have also been studied, again in
[
EFMM22
]
and, for the case with vertex dynamics, in
[
HPS24
]
. Independently, dynamic optimal transportation with non-linear mobilities was studied in
[
DNS09
]
, see also
[
LM10
]
for the case of a volume filling mobility.

In the present article, we combine these approaches to a model which consists of transport in a compact and convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, coupled to optimal transport on a metric graph. The introduction of mobilities allows to impose lower and upper bounds on the mass densities, leading to a more versatile model.
The coupled problem is motivated by planning of traffic routes. A particular focus lies on networks where, due to pre-existing infrastructure or connections to other transportation networks, one city is highlighted as a central point. We assume that traveling along the graph is cheaper than traveling in the bulk domain. However, there is an additional cost to entering the graph, that can be thought of as the cost of train tickets or waiting times between different connections.
An example of such a system can be found in French railroad planning, where Paris is associated to the accentuated center of the graph.
This article extends the results of
[
CKP25
]
, where a coupled dynamic optimal transport problem between bulk and a second domain, which is the graph of a function (a single road) is analyzed. The resulting problem is understood as a dynamic optimal transportation problem together with an additional penalty functional allowing for an optimization over the metric graph as well.

To introduce our model, we first consider an arbitrary metric graph
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},l)

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

Notation

In order to rigorously define the coupled dynamic system, we start by introducing some notation.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E})
be a combinatorial graph, where
\mathsf{V}
denotes the
set of nodes
and
\mathsf{E}
is the
set of edges

e=(v,w)
for
v,w\in\mathsf{V}
. The number of nodes is denoted by
N_{\mathsf{V}}:=|\mathsf{V}|
and the number of edges by
N_{\mathsf{E}}:=|\mathsf{E}|
. We define
\mathsf{E}(v):=\{e\in\mathsf{E}\,|\,v\in e\}
as the set of edges containing
v\in\mathsf{V}
. Moreover, we introduce the set of
outer vertices

\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}:=\{v\in\mathsf{V}\,|\,|\mathsf{E}(v)|=1\}
and the set of
outer edges

\mathsf{E}_{\rm out}:=\{e\in\mathsf{E}\,|\,\exists\penalty\ v\in\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}\text{ s.t. }v\in e\}
.
With an additional map
l:\mathsf{E}\to(0,+\infty)
, associating a positive length
l_{e}
to each edge
e
of the graph, the combinatorial graph turns into a
metric graph

\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},l)
. By defining the outer normal

\displaystyle n_{e}^{v}:=\begin{cases}-1&:e=(v,w)\\
0&:v\notin e\\
+1&:e=(w,v)\end{cases}

(2)

and therefore fixing an orientation, we can identify edges with closed intervals
[0,l_{e}]
. In order to rigorously define the coupling between a domain and a metric graph, we need to embed the graph.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E})
be a combinatorial graph and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact and convex set. A collection of distinct points
x_{v}\in\Omega
for
v\in\mathsf{V}
is called an
embedding
of the graph, defining edges as straight lines, and we say that the graph
\mathsf{G}
is
embedded into
\Omega
if the embedding has no intersecting edges.
We say that a metric graph
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},l)
is
embedded into
\Omega
if it is embedded as a combinatorial graph and if
|x_{w}-x_{v}|=l_{e}
for all
e\in\mathsf{E}
.

For an embedded metric graph we denote the tangent vector of an edge
e=(v,w)\in\mathsf{E}
by

\displaystyle\tau_{e}:=\frac{x_{w}-x_{v}}{l_{e}}=\frac{x_{w}-x_{v}}{|x_{w}-x_{v}|}

(3)

and we write
[x_{v},x_{w}]=\{x_{v}+t\tau_{e}\,|\,t\in[0,l_{e}]\}\subset\Omega
for the interval defined by an edge
e=(v,w)
of an embedded metric graph. Abusing notation, we identify
e=[x_{v},x_{w}]
throughout the article.

Functions on metric graphs are defined by their restriction to each edge. We introduce the sets

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}:=\bigsqcup_{e=(v,w)\in\mathsf{E}}[x_{v},x_{w}]\penalty\ \text{,}\quad\mathsf{L}:=\bigsqcup_{e=(v,w)\in\mathsf{E}}[0,1]\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{\mathsf{V}}:=\bigsqcup\limits_{v\in\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}}\{x_{v}\}

(4)

given as disjoint unions.
In order to guarantee a metric structure on the metric graph and therefore identifying it with a metric space, we define the new sets

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}:=\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}\big/\sim_{\mathsf{E}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{G}}:=\mathsf{L}\big/\sim_{\mathsf{L}},

where
\sim_{\mathsf{E}}
denotes the equivalence relation that identifies the same vertex on different edges and
\sim_{\mathsf{L}}
is the corresponding identification in
\mathsf{L}
. Note that in
\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}
each vertex
v\in\mathsf{V}
can be identified uniquely, whereas there are multiple copies in
\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}
, one for each edge
e\in\mathsf{E}(v)
.
Any map on a metric graph is given as
\varphi=(\varphi^{e})_{e\in\mathsf{E}}:\Omega_{\mathsf{E}}\to\mathbb{R}
or
\tilde{\varphi}=(\tilde{\varphi}^{e})_{e\in\mathsf{E}}:\mathsf{L}\to\mathbb{R}
. Both can be related using the transformation maps
\gamma_{e}(s):=x_{v}+sl_{e}\tau_{e}
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{e}(x)=\frac{|x-x_{v}|}{l_{e}}
for
e=(v,w)
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}^{e}(s)=\varphi^{e}(\gamma_{e}(s))\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi^{e}(x)=\tilde{\varphi}^{e}(\tilde{\gamma}_{e}(x)).

(5)

Similar definitions can be given for
\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}
and
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{G}}
.
Finally, we define
C^{1}([0,1]\times L):=C^{1}([0,1];C(\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{G}}))\cap C([0,1];C^{1}(\mathsf{L}))
, i.e. functions that are continuous in each vertex having derivatives on each edge.

We call a (metric or combinatorial) graph
star-shaped
if there exists an accentuated node
v_{0}\in\mathsf{V}
such that
E=\{(v_{0},v)\,|\,v\in\mathsf{V}\setminus\{v_{0}\}\}
. The node
v_{0}
is called

. In particular it holds that
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{E}(v_{0})=\mathsf{E}_{\rm out}
and
\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}=\mathsf{V}\setminus\{v_{0}\}
.

In order to rigorously formulate the coupling between different domains, we need to extend measures defined on the graph to measures on
\Omega

Let

\mathbb{R}^{N}

We also consider measures with values in the tangent space of some edge

. In particular, given
x\in e
the measures
V^{e}\in{\mathcal{M}}(e,T_{x}e)
and
G^{e}(x_{v})\in{\mathcal{M}}(\{x_{v}\},T_{x_{v}}e)

is the tangent space to
e
in

. Note that we can represent such measures as

\displaystyle V^{e}=\tau_{e}{\mathcal{V}}^{e}\quad\text{or}\quad G^{e}(x_{v})=\tau_{e}{\mathcal{G}}^{e}(x_{v})

(6)

with
{\mathcal{V}}^{e}\in{\mathcal{M}}(e,\mathbb{R})
,
{\mathcal{G}}^{e}(x_{v})\in{\mathcal{M}}(\{x_{v}\},\mathbb{R})

\rho^{e}\in{\mathcal{M}}(e,\mathbb{R})

\bar{\rho}^{e}\in{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\bar{\rho}^{e}=\int_{e}\phi\,d\rho^{e}

. For measures

\bar{\rho}=(\bar{\rho}^{e})_{e\in\mathsf{E}}

\overline{\tilde{\rho}}=(\overline{\tilde{\rho}^{e}\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{e}})_{e\in\mathsf{E}}

Measures
\rho\in{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{E}},\mathbb{R})
can be identified with measures

Similar notions of extension and push-forward operations can be defined for measures defined on
\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}
and

with values in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm adm}(\mathsf{G}):=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{[0,1]}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})\times{\mathcal{M}}_{[0,1]}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\times{\mathcal{M}}_{[0,1]}(\Omega_{\mathsf{G}},\mathbb{R})\times{\mathcal{M}}_{[0,1]}(\Omega_{\mathsf{E}},T_{x}e)

Admissible initial or final measures are given as elements of the set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm adm}(\mathsf{G}):=\left\{(\mu,\rho)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\Omega)\times{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\Omega_{\mathsf{G}})\,|\,\mu(\Omega)+\rho(\Omega_{\mathsf{G}})=1\right\}.

3
Fixed Graph

In this section we consider a fixed metric graph
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},l)
, embedded in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact and convex. We rigorously define the dynamic problem and show existence of solutions to the formal transport problem (
1
) with minimal dynamic costs.

3.1
Continuity equation

We rigorously define the coupled continuity equations and show mass conservation of the entire system.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a tuple
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm adm}(\mathsf{G})
satisfies the coupled continuity equations for admissible initial and final data
(\mu_{0},\rho_{0}),(\mu_{1},\rho_{1})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm adm}(\mathsf{G})
if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{t}(x)\,d\mu_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\,dJ_{t}dt-\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{t}(\gamma_{e}(s))\,d\tilde{f}^{e}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum\limits_{v\in\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}}\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}(v)}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{x_{v}\}}\phi_{t}(x_{v})n^{v}_{e}\,d\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{e}_{t}(x_{v})dt=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{1}(x)\,d\mu_{0}-\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}(x)\,d\mu_{1}

for all
\phi_{t}\in C^{1}([0,1]\times\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\psi_{t}^{e}(s)\,d\tilde{\rho}^{e}_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\psi_{t}^{e}(s)l_{e}\,d\tilde{{\mathcal{V}}}^{e}_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{t}^{e}(s)\,d\tilde{f}^{e}_{t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum\limits_{v\in\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}}\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}(v)}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{x_{v}\}}\psi^{e}_{t}(x_{v})n^{v}_{e}\,d\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{e}_{t}(x_{v})dt=\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{1}^{e}(s)\,d\tilde{\rho}^{e}_{0}-\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{0}^{e}(s)\,d\tilde{\rho}^{e}_{1}\right)

for all
\psi_{t}=(\psi_{t}^{e})_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\in C^{1}([0,1]\times L)
such that
\psi^{e}_{t}(\tilde{\gamma}_{e}(x_{v}))=:\psi_{t}(v)
for all
e\in\mathsf{E}(v)
, meaning that the map is continuous over vertices. We denote by
{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
the set of such solutions on the graph
\mathsf{G}
.

We can show that solutions to this system satisfy a global continuity equation and therefore are mass preserving.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
. Given
\eta_{t}:=\mu_{t}+\sum_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\bar{\rho}^{e}_{t}
and
W_{t}:=J_{t}+\sum_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\bar{V}^{e}_{t}
it holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{t}+\nabla\cdot W_{t}=0

(9)

weakly with initial data
\mu_{0}+\sum_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\bar{\rho}^{e}_{0}
and final data
\mu_{1}+\sum_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\bar{\rho}^{e}_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}([0,1]\times\Omega)
be an arbitrary test function. For each edge
e\in\mathsf{E}
we define
\psi^{e}_{t}=\phi_{t}\circ\gamma_{e}\in C^{1}([0,1]\times[0,1])
which, by construction, induces the admissible test function
\psi_{t}=(\psi_{t}^{e})_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\in C^{1}([0,1]\times\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{G}})
. Moreover,
\frac{d}{ds}\psi_{t}^{e}(s)=\nabla\phi_{t}(\gamma_{e}(s))\cdot l_{e}\tau_{e}
. We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{t}(x)\,d\eta_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\,dW_{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{t}(x)\,d\mu_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi_{t}(x)\,dJ_{t}dt+\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\psi_{t}(s)\,d\tilde{\rho}^{e}_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\psi_{t}(s)\cdot l_{e}\tau_{e}\,d\tilde{V}^{e}_{t}dt\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{1}(x)\,d\mu_{1}-\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}(x)\,d\mu_{0}+\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\psi^{e}_{1}(s)\,d\tilde{\rho}^{e}_{1}-\int_{0}^{1}\psi^{e}_{0}(s)\,d\tilde{\rho}^{e}_{0}\right)

where the last equality follows by substituting the weak formulation from of Definition
3.1
, thus proving the statement.
∎

3.2
Dynamic formulation

We will define the variational formulation that governs the evolution of the measures
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
over time. It is given as a generalized kinetic energy functional with an additional mobility function defining upper and lower bounds on the mass densities.

Definition 3.3

(Admissible mobilities)
.

We call a function
m:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)\cup\{-\infty\}

admissible mobility
if it is an upper semi-continuous and concave function with
{\rm{int}}({\rm{dom}}(m))=(a,b)
for
0\leq a<b
and
m(z)>0
for all
z\in(a,b)
.

We are now able to rigorously introduce the dynamic problem.

Definition 3.4
.

We define the following variational problem

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\,dt,

(BB)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\Omega}\left(\frac{d\mu_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Omega}},\frac{dJ_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Omega}}\right)\,d\lambda_{\Omega}+\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\left(\int_{e}\alpha_{1}\Psi_{\mathsf{G}}\left(\frac{d\rho^{e}_{t}}{d\lambda_{e}},\frac{d{\mathcal{V}}^{e}_{t}}{d\lambda_{e}}\right)+\alpha_{2}\Psi_{\mathsf{G}}\left(\frac{d\rho^{e}_{t}}{d\lambda_{e}},\frac{df^{e}_{t}}{d\lambda_{e}}\right)\,d\lambda_{e}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathsf{V}_{\rm out}}\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}(v)}\int_{\{x_{v}\}}\Psi_{\mathsf{G}}\left(\frac{d\rho^{e}_{t}|_{x_{v}}}{d\lambda_{v}},\frac{d{\mathcal{G}}^{e}_{t}(x_{v})}{d\lambda_{v}}\right)\,d\lambda_{v}

for
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}>0
describing the cost of transport along the graph (
\alpha_{1}
) and entering or leaving the graph (
\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
) respectively.
Here,
\lambda_{\Omega}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\Omega)
,
\lambda_{e}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(e)
and
\lambda_{v}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\{x_{v}\})

and

for almost all

. The action functionals are defined as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}(u,v):=\begin{cases}\frac{|v|^{2}}{m_{\Omega}(u)}&:m_{\Omega}(u)\neq 0\\
0&:m_{\Omega}(u)=0=v\\
+\infty&:\text{else}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{\mathsf{G}}(u,v):=\begin{cases}\frac{|v|^{2}}{m_{\mathsf{G}}(u)}&:m_{\mathsf{G}}(u)\neq 0\\
0&:m_{\mathsf{G}}(u)=0=v\\
+\infty&:\text{else}\end{cases}

(10)

for admissible mobility functions
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\mathsf{G}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

As shown in

, functionals of the form

Boundedness of the action

\mu_{t}
and
\rho_{t}^{e}
,
e\in\mathsf{E}
following the same arguments as in

.

Proposition 3.6
.

For
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})

\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\,dt<+\infty

(11)

for every

3.3
Existence of minimizers for a fixed graph

In this section we will show well-posedness of
BB
using the direct method of calculus of variations. From Remark
3.5
we infer lower semicontinuity. Compactness will follow from a Hölder estimate.

Proposition 3.7

(Hölder estimate)
.

Let us suppose that
\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\,dt\leq M
for a given
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
and a constant

. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mu_{t}-\mu_{s}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C(b_{\Omega},b_{\mathsf{G}},M)|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}

(12)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\|\rho^{e}_{t}-\rho^{e}_{s}\|_{C^{1}(e)^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C(b_{\Omega},b_{\mathsf{G}},M)|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}

(13)

for a constant

and for all

.

Proof.

The statement can be proven analogously to
[

, Proposition 2.10]
following arguments from
[
Mon21
, Proposition 3.5]
.
∎

From the previously stated Hölder estimates we can infer compactness.

Proposition 3.8

(Compactness)
.

Given
(\mu^{n}_{t},J^{n}_{t},\rho^{n}_{t},V^{n}_{t},f^{n}_{t},G^{n}_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
,

\mu_{t}^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}
weakly in

for all

ii)

{\rho}_{t}^{n}\rightharpoonup\rho_{t}

v)

Proof.

The proof of

.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Remark

4
Varying Graph

In this section we analyze the case of varying graphs restricting our considerations to star-shaped metric graphs. From now on, we suppose that
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},l)
is star-shaped with central vertex
v_{0}\in\mathsf{V}
.
We keep the connectivity of the graph fixed and we optimize the graph by varying the placement of the vertices inside of
\Omega
. For given initial and final data we are therefore looking for an optimal embedding of the combinatorial graph. Note that we allow the edge length to vary as well.
In order for the continuity equation to be well-defined throughout the minimization, we need to prevent overlapping edges. For this reason, we impose an additional constraint on the unsigned angle
\alpha_{x_{v},x_{w}}\in[0,2\pi)
between any pair of edges
(v_{0},v)
and
(v_{0},w)
at the common central node
v_{0}
. The angle can be calculated as

\displaystyle\alpha_{x_{v},x_{w}}=\left|\arccos\frac{\langle x_{v}-x_{v_{0}},x_{w}-x_{v_{0}}\rangle}{|x_{v}-x_{v_{0}}||x_{w}-x_{v_{0}}|}\right|

(15)

and we approach an overlap of two edges if this angle is small.
However, this does not prevent the nodes from collapsing in the center
x_{v_{0}}
. Therefore, we add an additional coloumb-type potential in order to obtain lower bounds on the distances
|x_{v}-x_{v_{0}}|
for
v\in\mathsf{V}
.
The resulting penalty functional reads

\displaystyle R(x_{v_{0}},x_{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{v_{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}}):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}\left(\frac{1}{|x_{v_{i}}-x_{v_{0}}|}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j=1}{j\neq i}}^{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}\frac{1}{\alpha_{x_{v_{i}},x_{v_{j}}}}\right)

(16)

inducing the variational problem

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\mathsf{G}\text{ embedded}}\inf\limits_{{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\,dt+cR(x_{v_{0}},x_{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{v_{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}})

(BB
G
)

as
c>0
allows for different scalings.
The following properties are direct consequences of the definition of the penalty functional.

Proposition 4.1
.

Any representation
x_{v_{0}},x_{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{v_{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}}\in\Omega
of a star-shaped metric graph with
R(x_{v_{0}},x_{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{v_{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}})<+\infty
is embedded in the sense of Definition
2.1
. In particular, there is no overlap between different edges and there exists a lower bound
d>0
such that
|x_{v_{i}}-x_{v_{j}}|\geq d
for all
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,N_{\mathsf{V}}-1\}
,
i\neq j
.

Remark 4.2
.

If the penalty functional is finite, it is continuous with respect to the strong norm convergence of the vertices. In particular, it is lower semi-continuous.

When varying the graph, we need to account for changes in the initial and final data. To do so, let
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega)
be given.
We define
\rho_{k}:={\eta_{k}}_{|\Omega_{\mathsf{G}}}

and
\mu_{k}=\eta_{k}-\bar{\rho}_{k}
for
k=0,1
. Since

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(\Omega)+\sum\limits_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\rho_{k}^{e}(e)

\displaystyle=\eta_{k}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\mathsf{G}})+\eta_{k}(\Omega_{\mathsf{G}})=1

(17)

we constructed a pair of admissible initial and final data
(\mu_{k},\rho_{k})\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\rm adm}(\mathsf{G})
,
k=0,1
. In this way, initial and final data can be defined independently of the graph.

For the minimization we need to assume compatibility conditions for the mobilities and reference measures along varying graphs as well.

Assumption 4.3
.

We make the following additional assumptions:

1.

The mobilities
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\mathsf{G}}
do not depend on the graph.

2.

It holds that
\sup\limits_{\mathsf{G}\text{ embedded}}\sum_{e\in\mathsf{E}}\|\lambda_{e}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}(e)}<+\infty
and
\sup\limits_{\mathsf{G}\text{ embedded}}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{V}}\|\lambda_{v}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}(\{x_{v}\})}<+\infty
.

3.

For
x_{v_{i}}^{n}\to x_{v_{i}}
as
n\to\infty
,
i\in\{0,\ldots,N_{\mathsf{V}}-1\}
it holds that
\lambda_{e^{n}}\rightharpoonup\lambda_{e}
and
\lambda_{v^{n}}\rightharpoonup\lambda_{v}
.

Under these assumptions, we are able to show a similar compactness result to
3.8
.

Proposition 4.4

(Compactness for varying graphs)
.

Given a family of embedded, star-shaped metric graphs
\mathsf{G}^{n}=(\mathsf{V}^{n},\mathsf{E}^{n})
with nodes
x_{v_{i}}^{n}\to x_{v_{i}}
as
n\to\infty
,
i\in\{0,\ldots,N_{\mathsf{V}}-1\}
and measures
(\mu^{n}_{t},J^{n}_{t},\rho^{n}_{t},V^{n}_{t},f^{n}_{t},G^{n}_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathsf{G}}(\mu^{n}_{t},J^{n}_{t},\rho^{n}_{t},V^{n}_{t},f^{n}_{t},G^{n}_{t})\,dt+cR(x^{n}_{v_{0}},x^{n}_{v_{1}},\ldots,x^{n}_{v_{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}})<+\infty

(18)

there exist measures
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
such that, up to subsequences

i)

\mu_{t}^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}
weakly in
{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)
for all
t\in[0,1]

ii)

\rho_{t}^{n}\circ\gamma^{n}\rightharpoonup\rho_{t}\circ\gamma
weakly in
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{G}})
for all
t\in[0,1]

iii)

J_{t}^{n}\rightharpoonup J_{t}
weakly in
{\mathcal{M}}([0,1]\times\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})

iv)

{\mathcal{V}}_{t}^{n}\circ\gamma^{n}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{V}}\circ\gamma
in
{\mathcal{M}}([0,1]\times\mathsf{L})

v)

f_{t}^{n}\circ\gamma^{n}\rightharpoonup f_{t}\circ\gamma
in
{\mathcal{M}}([0,1]\times\mathsf{L})

vi)

{\mathcal{G}}_{t}^{n}\circ\gamma^{n}\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\circ\gamma
in
{\mathcal{M}}([0,1]\times\Omega_{\mathsf{V}})
.

Proof.

By composition of the measures
\rho^{n}_{t},{\mathcal{V}}^{n}_{t},f^{n}_{t}
and
{\mathcal{G}}^{n}_{t}
with the change of variables maps
\gamma^{n}
through the push-forward operation, the whole sequence of measures is defined on a common domain instead of varying graphs.
The remainder of the proof follows as in Theorem
3.8
applied to the resulting push-forward measures. The norm-convergece of the vertices implies
C^{1}([0,1])
-convergence of the maps
\gamma^{n}_{e}
for all
e\in\mathsf{E}
, thus the change of variables can be reversed in the limit, concluding the proof.
∎

With compactness and lower semi-continuity at hand, we can prove well-posedness.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that
BBG
is finite. Then, there exists an embedded star-shaped metric graph
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E},l)
and a family of measures
(\mu_{t},J_{t},\rho_{t},V_{t},f_{t},G_{t})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G})
minimizing
BBG
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{G}^{n}
be a minimizing sequence of graphs with corresponding measures given by the sequence
(\mu^{n},J^{n},\rho^{n},V^{n},f^{n},G^{n})\in{\mathsf{CE}}(\mathsf{G}^{n})
. Each graph is embedded with the embedding given by a family of vertices
\{x^{n}_{v_{0}},x^{n}_{v_{1}},\ldots,x^{n}_{v_{N_{\mathsf{V}}-1}}\}\subset\Omega
, defining the sequences
(x^{n}_{v_{i}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
i\in\{0,\ldots,N_{\mathsf{V}}-1\}
. Compactness of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
allows to apply the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem to each of these sequences. Therefore, there exists a family of limiting points
x_{v_{i}}\in\Omega
for
i\in\{0,\ldots,N_{\mathsf{V}}-1\}
such that, up to a subsequence,
x_{v_{i}}^{n}\to x_{v_{i}}
for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,N_{\mathsf{V}}-1\}
. Now Proposition
4.4
and Proposition
4.2
allow the application of the direct method, proving the theorem.
∎

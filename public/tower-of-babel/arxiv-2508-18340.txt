Deterministic Coreset Construction via Adaptive Sensitivity Trimming
Faruk Alpay, Taylan Alpay

Abstract
We develop a rigorous framework for deterministic coreset construction in empirical risk minimization (ERM). Our central contribution is the Adaptive Deterministic Uniform-Weight Trimming (ADUWT) algorithm, which constructs a coreset by excising points with the lowest sensitivity bounds and applying a data-dependent uniform weight to the remainder. The method yields a uniform ( 1 ± ε ) (1\pm\varepsilon) relative-error approximation for the ERM objective over the entire hypothesis space. We provide complete analysis, including (i) a minimax characterization proving the optimality of the adaptive weight, (ii) an instance-dependent size analysis in terms of a Sensitivity Heterogeneity Index , and (iii) tractable sensitivity oracles for kernel ridge regression, regularized logistic regression, and linear SVM. Reproducibility is supported by precise pseudocode for the algorithm, sensitivity oracles, and evaluation pipeline. Empirical results (Table 1 ) align with the theory. We conclude with open problems on instance-optimal oracles, deterministic streaming, and fairness-constrained ERM.

Preliminaries: ERM and Coresets

Definition 2.1

(Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM))
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
, hypothesis class
\mathcal{F}
, and loss
\ell:\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. The empirical risk of
f\in\mathcal{F}
is
L(f;\mathcal{D})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(f;z_{i})
.

Definition 2.2

(Coreset)
.

A weighted subset
(S,\bm{\alpha})
with
S\subseteq[n]
and
\bm{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in S}
is a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-coreset if

(1-\varepsilon)L(f;\mathcal{D})\leq\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_{i}\ell(f;z_{i})\leq(1+\varepsilon)L(f;\mathcal{D})\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{F}.

Definition 2.3

(Sensitivity)
.

s_{i}\coloneqq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F},\ L(f;\mathcal{D})>0}\frac{\ell(f;z_{i})}{L(f;\mathcal{D})}
(zero if
L(f;\mathcal{D})=0
). Let
S_{\rm tot}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}
.

3
Adaptive Deterministic Trimming (ADUWT)

Algorithm 1

Adaptive Deterministic Uniform-Weight Trimming (ADUWT)

1:

Dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, loss
\ell
, model class
\mathcal{F}
, tolerance
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Sensitivity oracle returning bounds
\bar{s}_{i}\geq s_{i}
.

2:

Coreset
(S,\bm{\alpha})
with
\alpha
uniform on
S
.

3:

Compute sensitivity bounds:
For each
i
, compute
\bar{s}_{i}
.

4:

Trim low-sensitivity tail:
Sort
\bar{s}_{i}
nondecreasing; set
\varepsilon^{\prime}\coloneqq\tfrac{2\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}
. Take the largest
m
with
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bar{s}_{\pi(j)}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Let
U=\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(m)\}
and
S=[n]\setminus U
.

5:

Adaptive uniform weight:
Let
T_{U}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in U}\bar{s}_{i}
and set
\alpha\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1-\varepsilon^{2}}{1-T_{U}}}
.

6:

return

(S,\{\alpha\}_{i\in S})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Collective loss bound)
.

For any
U\subseteq[n]
and
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
\sum_{i\in U}\ell(f;z_{i})\leq\big(\sum_{i\in U}s_{i}\big)L(f;\mathcal{D})
.

Theorem 3.2

(ADUWT guarantee)
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the output of Alg.
1
is a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-coreset.

Proof.

Let
U=[n]\setminus S
,
T_{U}=\sum_{i\in U}\bar{s}_{i}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3.1
, with
L=L(f;\mathcal{D})
and
L_{S}=\sum_{i\in S}\ell(f;z_{i})
,

(1-T_{U})L\leq L_{S}\leq L.

(1)

With
\alpha=\sqrt{(1-\varepsilon^{2})/(1-T_{U})}
, the coreset loss
\hat{L}=\alpha L_{S}
satisfies

\hat{L}\leq\alpha L\leq\sqrt{\frac{1-\varepsilon^{2}}{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\,L=(1+\varepsilon)L,\quad\hat{L}\geq\alpha(1-T_{U})L\geq\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}\,L=(1-\varepsilon)L,

where
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{2\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}
gives
1-\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}
.
∎

Remark 3.3

(Coreset size)
.

Let
m
be the largest index with
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bar{s}_{\pi(j)}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Then
|S|=n-m
. The size adapts to the empirical tail of the sensitivity distribution.

3.1
Minimax optimality of the adaptive weight

Theorem 3.4

(Minimax weight)
.

Given bounds (
1
) with
T_{U}\in[0,\varepsilon^{\prime}]
known, the choice

\displaystyle\alpha^{\star}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\varepsilon^{2}}{1-T_{U}}}

uniquely minimizes
\sup_{x\in[1-T_{U},\,1]}\big|\alpha x-1\big|
and equals the geometric mean of the feasible endpoints
\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1-T_{U}}
and
1+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
g(\alpha)=\max\{1-\alpha(1-T_{U}),\,\alpha-1\}
. The minimax solution satisfies equality of the two terms:
1-\alpha(1-T_{U})=\alpha-1
, giving
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2-T_{U}}(2-T_{U})=\sqrt{\tfrac{1-\varepsilon^{2}}{1-T_{U}}}
after enforcing the outer
(1\pm\varepsilon)
feasibility; equivalently, pick
\alpha
as the geometric mean of endpoints to equalize multiplicative slack. Uniqueness follows from convexity of
g
.
∎

3.2
When trimming beats sampling: a refined view

Definition 3.5

(Sensitivity Heterogeneity Index)
.

For sensitivity bounds
\{\bar{s}_{i}\}
with mean
\mu
and standard deviation
\sigma
, define
\mathrm{SHI}\coloneqq\sigma/\mu
.

Proposition 3.6

(Instance-dependent comparison)
.

Let
\bar{S}=\sum_{i}\bar{s}_{i}
. Sensitivity sampling requires
k=\Theta(\bar{S}/\varepsilon^{2})
samples for a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
coreset
[
6
,
4
]
. ADUWT returns
|S|=n-m
with
m
the largest prefix whose
\sum\bar{s}_{\pi(j)}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. If the empirical CDF
F(t)=\frac{1}{n}|\{i:\bar{s}_{i}\leq t\}|
satisfies
F(t_{0})\geq\rho
for a small
t_{0}
and large
\rho
, then
m\geq\rho n
provided
\rho t_{0}n\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
, yielding
|S|\leq(1-\rho)n
. High
\mathrm{SHI}
implies such mass near
0
, thus favoring trimming over sampling in practice.

Tractable Sensitivity Oracles

4.1
Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR)

Definition 4.1

(KRR setting)
.

Let
f_{w}(x)=\left\langle w,\phi(x)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\ell(w;z_{i})=(y_{i}-f_{w}(x_{i}))^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{n}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
,
\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq B
,
|y_{i}|\leq Y
,
k(x_{i},x_{i})\leq\kappa^{2}
.

Proposition 4.2

(KRR sensitivity bound)
.

Under Def.
4.1
, for any
\delta\in(0,B]
,

\bar{s}_{i}\;=\;\max\!\left\{\frac{y_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{j}y_{j}^{2}},\;\frac{2(Y^{2}+B^{2}\kappa^{2})+(\lambda/n)B^{2}}{\lambda\delta^{2}}\right\}.

Pseudocode: KRR oracle.

1:

for

i=1
to
n

2:

0,&\text{else}\end{cases}

3:

b\leftarrow\frac{2(Y^{2}+B^{2}\kappa^{2})+(\lambda/n)B^{2}}{\lambda\delta^{2}}

4:

\bar{s}_{i}\leftarrow\max\{a_{i},b\}

5:

end

for

6:

return

\{\bar{s}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

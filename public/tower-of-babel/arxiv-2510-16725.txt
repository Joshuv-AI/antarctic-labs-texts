Local integral input-to-state stability for non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems
Yongchun Bi 1,2, Panyu Deng 2, Jun Zheng 2,3, Guchuan Zhu 3

Abstract
In this paper, we prove comparison principles for nonlinear differential equations with time-varying coefficients and develop Lyapunov analytical tools for the integral input-to-state stability (iISS) analysis of nonlinear non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems, which involve nonlinearities satisfying a superlinear growth, bringing difficulties to the iISS analysis. Specifically, our approach starts by establishing several forms of comparison principles for a wide range of ordinary differential equations having time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms, paving the way to conduct iISS assessment for general nonlinear non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems within the Lyapunov stability framework. Then, by using the comparison principles, we prove a local iISS (LiISS) Lyapunov theorem for the nonlinear non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems in the framework of Banach spaces. Furthermore, we provide sufficient conditions of the existence of a local iISS Lyapunonv functional (LiISS-LF) and construct LiISS-LFs for the systems in the framework of Hilbert spaces. Finally, we preset two examples to illustrate the proposed Lyapunov method for the LiISS analysis: one is to show how to obtain the LiISS of a nonlinear finite-dimensional system with time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms under linear state feedback control law while another one is to show how to employ the interpolation inequalities to handle superliner terms and establish the LiISS-LF for a class of multi-dimensional parabolic equations with space-time-varying coefficients. To demonstrate the validity of the results, numerical experiments are also conducted to verify the LiISS of these two classes of systems.

The notion of input-to-state stability (ISS) was initially introduced by Sontag for nonlinear finite-dimensional systems
[

In the past few decades, the ISS theory has witnessed rapid development in the nonlinear and robust control of finite-dimensional systems; see, e.g.,

It is worth noting that in

Allowing for unbounded inputs of finite energy, the iISS expands the theoretical framework of stability analysis by using an integral norm for inputs while employing a supremum norm for states, thereby encompassing nonlinear systems that do not meet ISS requirements.
Especially,
various characterizations of iISS have been provided in
[
14
]
based on dissipation inequalities and other necessary and sufficient conditions involving alternative and nontrivial characterizations, thus laying a foundation for the theory and applications of iISS for nonlinear systems.

\displaystyle x(t_{0})=

In

It is noteworthy that the method adopted in

,

Although proving the 0-UGASs property seems to be relatively straightforward for autonomous infinite-dimensional systems by using the semigroup theory, it becomes rather challenging for non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems, particularly, PDEs with time-varying coefficients.
The main reason is that, unlike autonomous infinite-dimensional systems, the non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems are associated to a family of time-dependent operators
A(t)
, which precludes the existence of a single infinitesimal generator. This inherent structure makes the cornerstone results of semigroup theory, such as the Hille-Yosida theorem, inapplicable. Consequently, the 0-UGASs property must be ascertained through more complex tools for nonlinear systems such as the construction of time-dependent Lyapunov functionals, moving beyond the classical spectral-based approach for linear systems.

It is also worth noting that all of the nonlinear terms considered in

\displaystyle\|C(x,u)\|_{X}\leq k\|x\|_{X}\xi(\|u\|_{U}),\forall x\in X,u\in U

\displaystyle{\|G(t,x,u)\|_{X}}\leq k\|x\|_{X}\phi(\|u\|_{U}),{\forall x\in X,u\in U,t\geq t_{0},}

\displaystyle\|f(t,x,u)\|_{{X}}\leq k(1+\|x\|_{{X}})\gamma(\|u\|_{U}),\forall x\in X,u\in U,

where

However, many infinite-dimensional systems used to describe physical phenomena or dynamic behavior often have nonlinear terms with superlinear growth, such as in the models of population growth
[
31
]
, tumor angiogenesis
[
32
]
, porous media flow
[
33
]
, etc. In contrast to the nonlinear terms with natural growth
[
22
,
24
,
18
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
21
]
, when a nonlinear function has a superlinear growth property in the system, it often maps the state from a smaller space
X_{\alpha}
, rather than
X
, to the space
X
, where
X_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
denotes the fractional power space associated with the considered differential operator
A(t)
, thereby, bringing difficulties to iISS analysis.
In particular, PDEs with general superlinear terms may not be characterized via global iISS, as the solution can blow-up in finite time under large initial values (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Chap. 5]
and
[
35
]
) unless special structural conditions are imposed; see, e.g., the monotonicity or sign conditions of superlinear terms for ensuring the iISS of parabolic PDEs
[
28
]
.

Notably, for autonomous infinite-dimensional systems with superlinear terms, local ISS has been studied in
[
36
]
. Especially, it was shown that when nonlinear terms satisfy a sort of uniform continuity condition w.r.t. external inputs, the local asymptotic
stability at zero uniformly w.r.t. state (0-UASs) is equivalent to the local input-to-sate stability of the system. In addition, a local ISS Lyapunov theorem and the inverse theorem have been proved in
[
36
]
, namely, the existence of a local ISS Lyapunov functional is equivalent to the local ISS of the system.
However, to the best knowledge of the authors, results on the local iISS (LiISS) of non-autonomous (and even autonomous) infinite-dimensional systems with generic nonlinear terms have not been reported in the existing literature yet. Moreover, as mentioned earlier, verifying the 0-UASs for non-autonomous
infinite-dimensional systems under a general form remains a challenge task.

In this paper, we investigate the LiISS for non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems with superlinear terms. In particular, the considered nonlinear term, denoted by
F(t,x,u)
, is allowed to be with a growth as

\displaystyle\|F(t,x,u)\|_{{X}}\leq k(t)(1+\|{x}\|_{X_{\alpha}})+\xi(\|u\|_{U}),\forall x\in X_{\alpha},u\in U,t\geq 0,

or

\displaystyle\|F(t,x,u)\|_{{X}}\leq k(t)\left(1+\|{x}\|_{X}^{m}\right)\xi(\|u\|_{U}),\forall x\in X_{\alpha},u\in U,t\geq 0,

where
k
is a non-negative locally integrable function,
m>1
is a constant, and
\xi
is a
\mathcal{K}
-function.

To avoid the difficulties arising from the “hard-to-verify 0-UASs (or 0-UGASs)” in verifying the iISS criteria for non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems and overcome the impact of superlinear terms on stability analysis, we develop Lyapunov analytical tools for establishing the LiISS of the considered systems.
More
precisely, we first prove comparison principles for a wide range of nonlinear ordinary differential equations (ODEs) having time-varying coefficients, providing fundamental and unified tools for the LiISS assessment of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems in the framework of Lyapunov stability theory.
Then, for a class of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems, we prove an LiISS Lyapunov theorem, i.e., the existence of an LiISS Lyapunov functional (LiISS-LF) implies the LiISS of the system, in the framework of Banach spaces. Furthermore, we provide sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of an LiISS-LF and construct LiISS-LFs for a class of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems in the framework of Hilbert spaces. Finally, as an application of the Lyapunov analytical tools developed in this paper, we establish the LiISS and conduct numerical simulations for a class of ODE systems, and multi-dimensional parabolic PDEs, respectively, with superlinear terms and space-time-varying coefficients. In particular, for PDEs, we show how to employ the interpolation inequalities to transform the
X_{\alpha}
-norm of states into a high-order term of the
X
-norm, thereby effectively handling superlinear terms and, consequently, establishing the LiISS of the system.

Overall, the main contributions of this paper are threefold:

(i)

We prove generalized comparison principles for nonlinear ODEs under a general form to capture local properties of the states, providing a critical tool for establishing the LiISS of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems without verifying the 0-UASs (or 0-UGASs) via the semigroup theory of linear operators.

(ii)

We develop generic Lyapunov tools for the LiISS analysis of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems by simultaneously considering the effect of time-varying coefficients, superlinear terms, and the external inputs on the systems’ stability, which can be used in a wide range of scenarios.

(iii)

To validate the universality and effectiveness of the developed method, we verify the LiISS for a class of nonlinear ODEs and PDEs, respectively, with time-varying coefficients. Especially, for PDEs, we present the application of the interpolation inequalities in the stability analysis. The importance of this technique lies in providing an insightful approach to managing superlinear terms, which is essential for proving the LiISS of nonlinear PDEs.

In the rest of the paper, we first introduce some basic notations used in this paper. In Section
2
, we prove several forms of comparison principles for nonlinear ODEs with time-varying coefficients, providing crucial Lyapunov analytical tools for the LiISS analysis of
non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems. In Section
3
, we consider the LiISS for a class of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems under an abstract form. Using the comparison principles for ODEs, we first prove an LiISS Lyapunov theorem for the non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems in the framework of Banach spaces. Then, we provide sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of an LiISS-LF for the non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems in in the framework of Hilbert spaces. In Section
4
, we present two examples to illustrate the application of the LiISS Lyapunov theorem to an ODE system and a PDE system, respectively. More precisely, in Section
4.1
, we consider the stabilization problem for an ODE system with time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms via a linear state feedback control. By using the LiISS Lyapunov theorem, we prove the LiISS of the ODE system under closed loop. In Section
4.2
, we verify the LiISS for a class of multi-dimensional
parabolic PDEs with space-time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms by employing the proposed Lyapunov method. In
particular, we show how to handle the superlinear terms by using the interpolation inequalities. Numerical experiments are conducted to verify the LiISS of these ODE and PDE systems. Finally, some concluding remarks are given in Section
5
.

Comparison principles for nonlinear ODEs with time-varying coefficients

In this section, we prove comparison principles for a wide range of nonlinear ODEs with time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms, which provide crucial tools for establishing the LiISS of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems within the framework of Lyapunov stability theory.

Lemma 1

Assume that
\alpha,g\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{s}g(\tau){\rm{d}}\tau=+\infty

\right.

(1)

then there is a function

\displaystyle y(t)\leq\beta(y_{0},t),\forall t\in[0,T].

\displaystyle\beta(s,t)=se^{-\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau){\rm d}\tau},\forall s\geq 0,t\in[0,T].

3
LiISS of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems

Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
U\subset X
be a normed linear space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{U}
, respectively. In this section, we consider the LiISS for a class of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems govern by the following evolution equation:

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=

\displaystyle A(t)x(t)+F(t,x(t),u(t)),t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

(8a)

\displaystyle x(0)=

\displaystyle x_{0},

(8b)

where
x(t)\in X
is the state,
x_{0}
represents the initial state,
u(t)\in U
is the control input,
A(t):D(A(t))\subset X\to X
is a bounded linear operator for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and
F
is a nonlinear function, which particularly is a mapping from a subspace of
X
to
X
rather than from
X
to
X
when
t
and
u
are fixed.

Assume that for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
A(t)
generates
an analytic semigroup on the Banach space
X
. Then,
D(A(t))
is dense in
X
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
(see
[
38
, Corollary 1.2.5]
). Furthermore, for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, one can define the fractional power operator

(-A(t))^{-\alpha}:X\rightarrow X
via the contour integral

\displaystyle(-A(t))^{-\alpha}=\int_{\partial S_{\phi^{\prime}}}z^{-\alpha}(zI+A(t))^{-1}{\rm d}z,

where
I
is the identity operator and
\partial S_{\phi^{\prime}}
is the boundary of a sufficiently large sector
S_{\phi^{\prime}}
, which particulary contains the spectrum of
A(t)
in the complex plane for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Since
(-A(t))^{-\alpha}
is a bounded injective operator on
X
for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, one can further define
(-A(t))^{\alpha}:=((-A(t))^{-\alpha})^{-1}:\text{range}((-A(t))^{-\alpha})\to X
. The domain of
(-A(t))^{\alpha}
equipped with the graph norm, i.e.,
|||x|||:=\|x\|_{X}+\|(-A(t))^{\alpha}x\|_{X}
, is a Banach space for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Since
(-A(t))^{\alpha}
is invertible, its graph norm is equivalent to the norm
\|(-A(t))^{\alpha}x\|_{X}
. Thus, the domain of
(-A(t))^{\alpha}
equipped with the norm
\|(-A(t))^{\alpha}x\|_{X}
is a Banach space which we denote by
X_{\alpha}
. For convenience, we denote
\|x\|_{X_{\alpha}}:=\|(-A(t))^{\alpha}x\|_{X}
.
From this definition it is clear that

D(A(t))\subset X_{\beta}\subset X_{\alpha}\subset X
for
0<\alpha<\beta<1
and the imbedding of
X_{\beta}
in
X_{\alpha}
is
continuous; see
[
38
, Sec. 2.6]
.

Throughout this paper, assume that
F:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times X_{\alpha}\times U\rightarrow X
,
x_{0}\in D(A(0))
, and

u\in U_{c}:=C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},{U})
.

In this section, for system (
8
),
we prove an LiISS Lyapunov theorem in the framework of Banach spaces, then provide sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of an LiISS-LF and construct LiISS-LFs in the framework of Hilbert spaces.

3.1
The LiISS Lyapunov theorem in the framework of Banach spaces

In this section, we prove an LiISS Lyapunov theorem for system (
8
) in the framework of Banach spaces.
It is worth mentioning that a
function
x:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to X
is called a classical solution to system (
8
) if
x(t)
is continuous in
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
x(t)
is continuously differentiable in
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
x(t)\in D(A(t))
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and satisfies (
8
).
Note that under appropriate assumptions on
A(t)
and
F(t,x,u)
, the existence of a classical solution (or a strong or mild solution) has been proven in
[
38
]
for system (
8
) in various cases, especially, in the disturbance-free case; see also
[
17
]
for the well-posedness of time-varying semi-linear evolution equations with locally Lipschitz continuous nonlinearities and piecewise-right continuous inputs.
Since the main purpose of the present work is to demonstrate how to perform LiISS analysis on non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems with a wide range of forms within the framework of Lyapunov stability theory, rather than proving the existence and regularity of solutions, throughout this paper, we consider the LiISS of system (
8
) by means of classical solutions.

First, we introduce the concept of LiISS for system (
8
).

Definition 3.8
.

System (
8
) is said to be integral input-to-state stable (iISS) if there exist functions
\gamma\in\mathcal{K}
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
such that, for any initial datum
x_{0}\in D(A(0))
and input
{u\in U_{c}}
, the solution to system (
8
) satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle\|x(t,x_{0},u)\|_{X}\leq{\beta}(\|x_{0}\|_{X},t)+\sigma\left(\int^{t}_{0}\gamma(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm{d}}s\right),\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(9)

In particular, if there exist a constant
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and functions
\gamma_{0}\in\mathcal{K},\sigma_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that the estimate (
9
) holds true for any

\displaystyle({x}_{0},u)\in\mathscr{B}_{R}:=\left\{(x_{0},u)\in D(A(0))\times U_{c}\left|\|x_{0}\|_{X}+\sigma_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\gamma_{0}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm{d}}s\right)\leq R,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right.\right\},

then, system (
8
) is said to be locally integral input-to-state stable (LiISS).

Remark 3.9
.

Note that
D(A(t))
is dense in
X
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
If the solution to system (
8
) depend continuously on the inputs and the initial states, then, based on a standard density argument (see, e.g.,
[
39
]
), the iISS and LiISS of system (
8
) can also be defined via
{x}_{0}\in X
and mild solutions.

Next, we introduce the concept of LiISS Lyapunov functional for system (
8
). Analogous to the classical Lyapunov theory for stability analysis, the LiISS-LF is a crucial tool for verifying the LiISS property of the system.

Definition 3.10
.

A continuously differentiable functional
V:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called an LiISS Lyapunov functional (LiISS-LF) of system (
8
), if it has the following properties:

(i)

there exist two functions
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle{\alpha_{1}}(\|x\|_{X})\leq{V(t,x)}\leq{\alpha_{2}}(\|x\|_{X}),\forall x\in X,t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};

(10)

(ii)

there exist two nonnegative locally integrable functions
g_{1},g_{2}
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}g_{1}(\tau){\rm{d}}\tau=+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad g_{2}(t)\leq g_{1}(t),\forall t\geq 0,

two functions
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{K}
satisfying

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(s)\leq\theta_{1}(s),\forall s\in[0,R^{\prime}]

(11)

with a constant
R^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
and a function
\phi\in\mathcal{K}
, such that the derivative of
V
along the trajectory of system (
8
) satisfies the following inequality:

\displaystyle{\dot{V}(t,x)}\leq-g_{1}(t)\theta_{1}(\|x\|_{X})+g_{2}(t)\theta_{2}(\|x\|_{X})+\phi(\|u(t)\|_{U}),\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(12)

Furthermore, if
g_{2}(t)\equiv 0
in the condition (ii), then
V
is called an iISS Lyapunov functional (iISS-LF) of system (
8
).

Now, we state the LiISS (or iISS) Lyapunov theorem for system (
8
).

Theorem 3.11
.

If system (
8
) admits an LiISS (or iISS) Lyapunov functional, then it is LiISS (or iISS).

Proof 3.12
.

Consider the maximal time interval
{[0,T]}
with some
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
or
[0,+\infty)
, over which system (
8
) admits a classical solution
x
corresponding to the initial state and input
(x_{0},u)\in D(A(0))\times U
. Let
V(t,x)
be an LiISS-LF of system (
8
) and
y(t):=V(t,x(t))
.

If the maximal time interval for the existence of a solution is
{[0,T]}
with some
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then we deduce from (
10
) and (
12
) that the derivative of
y
along the trajectory

satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)\leq-g_{1}(t)\theta_{1}\left({\alpha_{2}}^{-1}(y(t))\right)+g_{2}(t)\theta_{2}\left({\alpha_{1}}^{-1}(y(t))\right)+{\phi}\left({\|u(t)\|_{U}}\right),\forall t\in[0,T].

(13)

Note that the condition (
11
) implies that

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}(s):=\theta_{1}\left({\alpha_{2}}^{-1}(s)\right)\geq\alpha_{4}(s):=\theta_{2}\left({\alpha_{1}}^{-1}(s)\right),\forall s\in\left[0,r^{\prime}\right],

where
r^{\prime}:=\min\{\alpha_{1}(R^{\prime}),\alpha_{2}(R^{\prime})\}
.

Note also that if
(x_{0},u)
belongs to
\mathscr{B}_{R}
with a positive constant
R:=\alpha_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}\right)
and functions
\gamma_{0}(s):=2\phi(s),{\sigma_{0}(s):=\alpha_{2}^{-1}(s)}
for

, then the condition (
10
) and
(x_{0},u)\in\mathscr{B}_{R}
ensure that

\displaystyle y_{0}+\int^{T}_{0}2{\phi}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm d}s\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(\|x_{0}\|_{X})+\int^{T}_{0}2{\phi}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(\|x_{0}\|_{X})+\alpha_{2}\left(\sigma_{0}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\gamma_{0}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm d}s\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{2}\left(\|x_{0}\|_{X}+\sigma_{0}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\gamma_{0}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm d}s\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{2}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{\prime}.

Applying Proposition
2.6
to (
13
), we deduce that
there exists a function
\beta_{1}\in\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
such that

\displaystyle y(t)\leq\beta_{1}(y(0),t)+2\int^{t}_{0}{\phi}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm d}s,\forall t\in[0,T].

In view of (

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(\|x\|_{X})\leq\beta_{2}(\|x_{0}\|_{X},t)+2\int^{t}_{0}{\phi}(\|u(s)\|_{U}){\rm d}s,\forall t\in[0,T],

where

for

is a
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
-function.

Let

, and

for

3.2
Constructing LiISS-LFs in the framework of Hilbert spaces

In this section, let
X
be a Hilbert space with scalar inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
. We provide sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of an LiISS-LF and construct LiISS-LFs for system (
8
) in the framework of Hilbert spaces. More specifically, we prove the following result.

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose that there exist a continuously differentiable, uniformly bounded, uniformly coercive, and positive-definite operator
P(t)\in{\mathscr{L}}(X)
defined for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, nonnegative and locally integrable functions
a_{1}(t),a_{2}(t)
defined over
[0,+\infty)
and satisfying

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}a_{1}(\tau){\rm{d}}\tau=+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad a_{2}(t)\leq Ma_{1}(t)

with a constant
M\geq 0
, constants
m_{1},m_{2}>2
, and a function
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}
, such that the following inequality holds true:

\displaystyle\langle({A^{*}(t)P(t)+P(t)A(t)+\dot{P}(t)})x,x\rangle+2\langle F(t,x,u),P(t)x\rangle\leq

\displaystyle-a_{1}(t)\|x\|^{2}_{X}+a_{2}(t){\left(\|x\|^{m_{1}}_{X}+\|x\|^{m_{2}}_{X}\right)}

\displaystyle+{\zeta({\|u\|_{U}})},\forall x\in D(A),{u\in U},t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(15)

Then,
V(t,x):=\langle{P(t)}x,x\rangle
is an LiISS-LF, and therefore, system (
8
) is LiISS. In particular, if
a_{2}\equiv 0
on
[0,+\infty)
, then
V(t,x):=\langle{P(t)}x,x\rangle
is an iISS-LF, and hence, system (
8
) is iISS.

Proof 3.14
.

Since
P(t)
is a uniformly bounded, uniformly coercive, and positive-define operator, there exist
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

\eta_{1}\|x\|^{2}_{X}\leq\langle P(t)x,x\rangle\leq\|P(t)\|\|x\|^{2}_{X}\leq\eta_{2}\|x\|^{2}_{X},\forall x\in X,

namely,

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\|x\|^{2}_{X}\leq V(t,x)\leq\eta_{2}\|x\|^{2}_{X},\forall x\in X.

The derivative of
V
along the trajectory of system (
8
) is given as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{u}(t,x)=

\displaystyle{\langle\dot{P}x,x\rangle}+\langle P\dot{x},x\rangle+\langle Px,\dot{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\langle\dot{P}x,x\rangle}+\langle P(Ax+F(t,x,u)),x\rangle+\langle Px,Ax+F(t,x,u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\langle\dot{P}x,x\rangle}+\langle PAx,x\rangle+\langle PF(t,x,u),x\rangle+\langle Px,Ax\rangle+\langle Px,F(t,x,u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\langle\dot{P}x,x\rangle}+\langle(PA+A^{*}P)x,x\rangle+\langle PF(t,x,u),x\rangle+\langle Px,F(t,x,u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\langle(\dot{P}+PA+A^{*}P)x,x\rangle+2\langle Px,F(t,x,u)\rangle},

which, along with (
3.13
), yields

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{u}(t,x)\leq

\displaystyle{-a_{1}(t)\|x\|^{2}_{X}+a_{2}(t)\left(\|x\|^{m_{1}}_{X}+\|x\|^{m_{2}}_{X}\right)+\zeta(\|u\|_{U})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{-a_{1}(t)\|x\|^{2}_{X}+{a_{1}(t)M}\left(\|x\|^{m_{1}}_{X}+\|x\|^{m_{2}}_{X}\right)+\zeta(\|u\|_{U})}.

Let
g_{1}(t)=g_{2}(t):=a_{1}(t)
,
\theta_{1}(s):=s^{2},\theta_{2}(s):=M\left(s^{m_{1}}+s^{m_{2}}\right),\phi(s):=\zeta(s)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Note that
m_{1}>2
and
m_{2}>2
. It is clear that
\theta_{2}(s)\leq\theta_{1}(s)
for sufficiently small

.
According to Definition
3.10
, we deduce that
V(t,x)=\langle P(t)x,x\rangle
is an LiISS-LF for system (
8
). Then, by Theorem
3.11
, we infer that system (
8
) is LiISS. Furthermore, if
a_{2}(t)\equiv 0
, we conclude that
V(t,x)=\langle P(t)x,x\rangle
is an iISS-LF for system (
8
), and hence, system (
8
) is iISS.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.13
, we can prove the following result, which provides a sufficient condition to ensure the existence of an LiISS-LF for a class of autonomous infinite-dimensional systems with superlinear terms.

Corollary 3.15

Let

, constants

\displaystyle{\widetilde{m}_{i}>2-\widetilde{n}_{i}}>0,i=1,2,

and a function
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}
, such that

\displaystyle\langle({A^{*}P+PA})x,x\rangle\leq

\displaystyle-\|x\|^{2}_{X_{\alpha}},{\forall x\in D(A),}

and

\displaystyle{\langle F(t,x,u),Px\rangle}\leq

\displaystyle{\mu_{1}\|x\|_{X}^{\widetilde{m}_{1}}\|x\|^{\widetilde{n}_{1}}_{X_{\alpha}}+\mu_{2}\|x\|_{X}^{\widetilde{m}_{2}}}\|x\|^{\widetilde{n}_{2}}_{X_{\alpha}}+{\zeta(\|u\|_{U})},\forall x\in D(A),{u\in U}.

Then,
V(x):=\langle{P}x,x\rangle
is an LiISS-LF, and therefore, system (
8
) is LiISS.

Proof 3.16
.

Using the Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}^{\widetilde{m}_{i}}\|x\|^{\widetilde{n}_{i}}_{X_{\alpha}}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|x\|^{2}_{X_{\alpha}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{i}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{i}}}\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{i}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{i}}}_{X},i=1,2,

where
\varepsilon>0
is a positive constant to be chosen later.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle({A^{*}P+PA})x,x\rangle+2\langle F(t,x,u),Px\rangle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(1-2\mu_{1}\varepsilon-2\mu_{2}\varepsilon\right)\|x\|^{2}_{X_{\alpha}}+2\mu_{1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{1}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{1}}}\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{1}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{1}}}_{X}+2\mu_{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{2}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{2}}}\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{2}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{2}}}_{X}

\displaystyle+2{\zeta(\|u\|_{U})}.

Choosing
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
to be sufficiently small so that
1-2\mu_{1}\varepsilon-2\mu_{2}\varepsilon>0
and using the fact that
\|x\|_{X}\leq C\|x\|_{X_{\alpha}}
with some positive constant
C
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\langle({A^{*}P+PA})x,x\rangle+2\langle F(t,x,u),Px\rangle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C}\left(1-2\mu_{1}\varepsilon-2\mu_{2}\varepsilon\right)\|x\|^{2}_{X}+2\mu_{1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{1}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{1}}}\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{1}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{1}}}_{X}+2\mu_{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{2}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{2}}}\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{2}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{2}}}_{X}

\displaystyle+2{\zeta(\|u\|_{U})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C}\left(1-2\mu_{1}\varepsilon-2\mu_{2}\varepsilon\right)\|x\|^{2}_{X}+\widetilde{b}\left(\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{1}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{1}}}_{X}+\|x\|^{\frac{2\widetilde{m}_{2}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{2}}}_{X}\right)+2{\zeta(\|u\|_{U})},

where
\widetilde{b}:=2\max\left\{\mu_{1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{1}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{1}}},\mu_{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\widetilde{n}_{2}}{2-\widetilde{n}_{2}}}\right\}
. Subsequently, the condition (
3.13
) is fulfilled. Theorem
3.13
ensures that

V(t,x):=\langle{P(t)}x,x\rangle
is an LiISS-LF. Therefore, by Theorem
3.11
, system (
8
) is LiISS.

Remark 3.17
.

It is worth mentioning that for general nonlinear non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems under an abstract form, verifying the structural conditions of nonlinear terms is extremely challenging. As shown in Section
4.2
, even for specific PDEs, it remains difficult to verify the structural conditions in Theorem
3.13
when superlinear terms are involved, usually requiring complex techniques or tools such as Sobolev embedding and interpolation inequalities to handle the nonlinear terms.

4
Illustrative examples

In this section, we present two examples to illustrate the application of the LiISS Lyapunov theorem to an ODE system and a PDE
system, respectively, and provide numerical simulations to validate the LiISS results for the two systems.

4.1
Local stabilization of
a nonlinear ODE system under linear state feedback control

In this section, as an application of the LiISS Lyapunov theorem, we investigate the LiISS of a nonlinear ODE system with time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms. More precisely, we consider the linear state feedback control problem for the following nonlinear system

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}=

\displaystyle g(t)x^{m}_{1}-h_{1}(t)x_{2}^{3}+u_{1},

(16a)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}=

\displaystyle{\tilde{g}(t)x^{n}_{2}}-b(t)x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+u_{2},

(16b)

where
g\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
,
{\tilde{g}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})}
,
b\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{>0})
,
h_{1}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq h_{1}(t)\leq M\quad\text{and}\quad h_{1}^{\prime}(t)\leq h_{1}(t),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

(17)

M>0
is a positive constant,
m>3
and
n>1
are integers, and
u_{1},u_{2}
are control inputs.

Note that the open-loop system (
16
) is not asymptotically stable at the origin for any given initial data
x_{1}(0)
and
x_{2}(0)
.
To apply a linear state-feedback controller, we propose the following control law using time-varying-gains:

\displaystyle u_{1}(t):=

\displaystyle-g_{1}(t)x_{1},

(18a)

\displaystyle u_{2}(t):=

\displaystyle h_{2}(t)x_{1}-g_{2}(t)x_{2},

(18b)

where
g_{1},g_{2},h_{2}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
satisfying

\displaystyle g_{1}(t)\geq

\displaystyle{\overline{g}(t):=\max\left\{g(t),\tilde{g}(t)\right\}},\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}g_{1}(\tau){\rm{d}}\tau=+\infty,

(19)

\displaystyle g_{2}(t)\geq

\displaystyle 1+g_{1}(t),\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

(20)

\displaystyle h_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{h_{1}(t)}{1+h_{1}(t)},\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(21)

Note that the coefficient matrix of the linear part of system (
16
) under the control law (
18
) is given by
A(t):=\begin{bmatrix}-g_{1}(t)&0\\
h_{2}(t)&-g_{2}(t)\end{bmatrix}
, for which
g_{1}(t)
may tend to
0
as
t\to+\infty
.
Note also that due to the presence of the term
g(t)x^{m}_{1}
with a nonnegative function
g
and positive integer
m
, the linear state-feedback control law (
18
) can only
guarantee local asymptotic stability of system (
16
). Furthermore, considering an additional disturbance
d(t)
entering through
u_{2}
, the equation of system (
16
) in closed loop becomes

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}=

\displaystyle-g_{1}(t)x_{1}+g(t)x^{m}_{1}-h_{1}(t)x_{2}^{3},

(22a)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}=

\displaystyle h_{2}(t)x_{1}-g_{2}(t)x_{2}+{\tilde{g}(t)x^{n}_{2}}-b(t)x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+d(t),

(22b)

Assume that
d\in L^{4}(0,T)
for any
T>0
. We have the following LiISS result for system (
22
).

Proposition 4.18
.

System (
22
) is LiISS.

To prove Proposition
4.18
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.19
.

For any
x:={(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it holds that

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}\min{\left\{|x|^{2},|x|^{4}\right\}}
.

Proof 4.20
.

On the one hand, for
x_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}=x_{1}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\geq x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}=x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}{|x|^{2}}.

On the other hand, for
x_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
x_{1}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}\geq x_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}{|x|^{2}}.

In addition, for
x_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
x_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}\geq x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}{|x|^{4}}.

Thus, for any
x:=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it holds that

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}\min{\left\{|x|^{2},|x|^{4}\right\}}
.

Now we prove the LiISS of system (
22
) by using the LiISS Lyapunov theorem. The key step is constructing an appropriate LiISS Lyapunov candidate and verifying the structural conditions in Definition (
3.10
).

Proof of Proposition
4.18
:

For any
x:=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, define
V(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(1+h_{1}(t))x_{2}^{4}
. We claim that
V(t,x)
is an LiISS Lyapunov function of system (
22
).
Indeed, by (
17
), we have

\displaystyle V(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(1+M)x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}(1+M)\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4}(1+M)\right\}{\left(|x|^{2}+|x|^{4}\right)},\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(23)

Moreover, according to (
17
) and Lemma
4.19
, we have

\displaystyle V(t,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}x_{2}^{4}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}\right)\geq\frac{1}{8}\min{\left\{|x|^{2},|x|^{4}\right\}},\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Now, along the trajectory of system (
22
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\dot{V}=

\displaystyle x_{1}\dot{x}_{1}+\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{3}\dot{x}_{2}+\frac{1}{4}h_{1}^{\prime}(t)x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}\left(-g_{1}(t)x_{1}+g(t)x_{1}^{m}-h_{1}(t)x_{2}^{3}\right)+\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{3}\left(h_{2}(t)x_{1}-g_{2}(t)x_{2}+{\tilde{g}(t)x^{n}_{2}}-b(t)x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+d(t)\right)+\frac{1}{4}h_{1}^{\prime}(t)x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{1}(t)x_{1}^{2}+g(t)x_{1}^{m+1}+{\tilde{g}(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{n+3}}-h_{1}(t)x_{1}x_{2}^{3}+\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)h_{2}(t)x_{1}x_{2}^{3}-g_{2}(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle-b(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{4}+\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{3}d(t)+\frac{1}{4}h^{\prime}_{1}(t)x_{2}^{4}

(24)

Using the condition (
21
) and the facts that
b>0
and
h_{1}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle-h_{1}(t)x_{1}x_{2}^{3}+\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)h_{2}(t)x_{1}x_{2}^{3}=-h_{1}(t)x_{1}x_{2}^{3}+h_{1}(t)x_{1}x_{2}^{3}=0,

(25)

\displaystyle g(t)x_{1}^{m+1}+\tilde{g}(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{n+3}\leq

\displaystyle\overline{g}(t)(1+M)\left(\left(x^{2}_{1}+x^{2}_{2}\right)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}+\left(x^{2}_{1}+x^{2}_{2}\right)^{\frac{n+3}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{g}(t)(1+M){\left(|x|^{m+1}+|x|^{n+3}\right)},

(26)

and

\displaystyle-b(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{4}\leq 0,

(27)

where
{\overline{g}(t):=\max\left\{g(t),\tilde{g}(t)\right\}}
. Meanwhile, by using the Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle x_{2}^{3}d(t)\leq\left(\varepsilon x_{2}^{4}+\varepsilon^{-3}(d(t))^{4}\right),

(28)

where
\varepsilon
is a positive constant to be determined later.

In addition, by using (
20
) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle-g_{2}(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{4}+\frac{1}{4}h_{1}^{\prime}(t)x_{2}^{4}\leq

\displaystyle-\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{4}-g_{1}(t)\left(1+h_{1}(t)\right)x_{2}^{4}+\frac{1}{4}h_{1}(t)x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}h_{1}(t)x_{2}^{4}-x_{2}^{4}-g_{1}(t)x_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-g_{1}(t)x_{2}^{4}-x_{2}^{4}.

(29)

Putting (

4.1
), (

4.1

\displaystyle\dot{V}\leq

\displaystyle-g_{1}(t)\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{4}\right)+{\overline{g}(t)(1+M){\left(|x|^{m+1}+|x|^{n+3}\right)}}+(1+M)\varepsilon^{-3}(d(t))^{4}+(-1+(1+M)\varepsilon)x^{4}_{2}.

By Lemma
4.19
and choosing
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{M+1}\right)

\displaystyle\dot{V}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g_{1}(t)\min{\left\{|x|^{2},|x|^{4}\right\}}+{\overline{g}(t)(1+M)\max{\left\{|x|^{m+1},|x|^{n+3}\right\}}}+(1+M)\varepsilon^{-3}|d(t)|^{4}.

Let
\theta_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{s^{2},s^{4}\right\}

4.2
LiISS of time-varying parabolic PDEs with superlinear terms

In this section, as an application of the LiISS Lyapunov theorem, we investigate the LiISS of
N
-dimensional parabolic PDEs with time-varying coefficients and superlinear terms in the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial x}{\partial t}(\xi,t)-{\rm div}\ (a(\xi,t)\nabla x(\xi,t))+c(\xi,t)x(\xi,t)=

\displaystyle u(\xi,t)+h(\xi,t,x(\xi,t)),(\xi,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

(30a)

\displaystyle a(\xi,t)\frac{\partial x}{\partial\nu}(\xi,t)=

\displaystyle 0,(\xi,t)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

(30b)

\displaystyle x(\xi,0)=

\displaystyle x_{0}(\xi),\xi\in\Omega,

(30c)

where
\Omega
is an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\in\mathbb{N})
with
C^{1}
-continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
,
a\in C(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{>0})
and
c\in C(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};\mathbb{R}_{>0})
are functions depending on space-time variables,

represents in-domain disturbances,

is a nonlinear function having superlinear growth w.r.t. the third variable,
\operatorname{div}
denotes the standard divergence operator, and
\nu
is the outer unit normal vector.

Assume that

\displaystyle a(\xi,t)\geq

\displaystyle\underline{a},\forall(\xi,t)\in{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

\displaystyle c(\xi,t)\geq

\displaystyle\underline{c},\forall(\xi,t)\in{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

\displaystyle\left|h(\xi,t,x)\right|\leq

\displaystyle M(\left|x\right|^{m_{1}}+\left|x\right|^{m_{2}}),\forall(\xi,t,x)\in{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R},

where
\underline{a},\underline{c},M,m_{1}
, and

are positive constants, among which
m_{1}
and

satisfy either of the following conditions:

(H1)
For

, assume that
1<m_{i}<+\infty
,
i=1,2
;

(H2)
For
N>2
, assume that
{1<m_{i}\leq\frac{N}{N-2}},i=1,2
.

Let

, introducing the operator

by

, where

\displaystyle D(A(t)):=\left\{x\in X\Big|~{\rm div}(a\nabla x)\ \text{exists\ in}\ \Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\ a(\xi,t)\frac{\partial x}{\partial{\nu}}(\xi,t)=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}.

8
). Moreover, for
X_{\frac{1}{2}}=W^{1,2}(\Omega)

or

for

, each of the conditions
(H1)
and
(H2)
ensures that
|x|^{m_{i}}\in L^{2}(\Omega),i=1,2,
for

. Therefore, for any fixed

,
F
maps the state
x
from
X_{\frac{1}{2}}
to
X
.
Furthermore, By
[

To prove Proposition
4.21
, we need the following Sobolev embedding and interpolation inequalities, which are used to deal with superlinear terms in the LiISS analysis.

Lemma 4.22

(
[
41
, pp. 285, 313, 314, 119]
)
.

The following inequalities hold true:

(i)

if
1\leq q<N
and
v\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, then
\|v\|_{q^{*}}\leq C\|v\|_{1,q}
, where
q^{*}:=\frac{qN}{N-q}
and
C
is a positive constant;

(ii)

if
1\leq q\leq p<+\infty
,
r\geq N
, and
v\in W^{1,r}(\Omega)
, then
\|v\|_{p}\leq C\|v\|_{q}^{1-{\lambda}}\|v\|_{1,r}^{{\lambda}}
,
where
{\lambda}=\frac{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{r}}\in[0,1)
and
C
is a positive constant;

(iii)

if
1\leq q\leq p\leq r\leq+\infty
and
v\in L^{r}(\Omega)
, then
\|v\|_{p}\leq\|v\|_{q}^{1-{\lambda}}\|v\|_{r}^{{\lambda}}
,
where
{\lambda}\in[0,1]
satisfies
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{{\lambda}}{r}+\frac{1-{\lambda}}{q}
.

Using Lemma
4.22
, we can prove Proposition
4.21
.

Proof of Proposition
4.21
:

Define

\displaystyle V(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|x(\cdot,t)\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}x^{2}(\xi,t){\rm d}\xi.

By integrating by parts, and using the Young’s inequality and the structural conditions on
a,c,h
, we deduce that the derivative of
V
along the trajectory satisfies

\displaystyle\!\!{\dot{V}(x)}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x\left({\rm div}(a\nabla x)-cx+u+h\right){\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\!\!=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla x|^{2}{\rm d}\xi-\int_{\Omega}cx^{2}{\rm d}\xi+\int_{\Omega}ux{\rm d}\xi+\int_{\Omega}hx{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\!\!\leq

\displaystyle-\underline{a}\|\nabla x\|^{2}-\underline{c}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+M\left(\|x\|_{m_{1}+1}^{m_{1}+1}+\|x\|_{m_{2}+1}^{m_{2}+1}\right)

\displaystyle\!\!\leq

\displaystyle-\underline{a}\|\nabla x\|^{2}-\underline{c}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}{\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{U}}+M\left(\|x\|_{m_{1}+1}^{m_{1}+1}+\|x\|_{m_{2}+1}^{m_{2}+1}\right),

(31)

where
\varepsilon
is a positive constant to be determined later.

Now we prove in two cases.

Case 1
:
1\leq N\leq 2,1<m_{i}<+\infty,i=1,2
.

Let

\displaystyle p=m_{i}+1,q=r=2,{\lambda_{i}}=N\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{m_{i}+1}\right)\in(0,1).

By Lemma
4.22
(ii) and the Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|x\|^{m_{i}+1}_{{m_{i}+1}}\leq

\displaystyle C\|x\|^{(1-{\lambda_{i}})(m_{i}+1)}\|x\|_{1,2}^{{\lambda_{i}}(m_{i}+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|x\|^{(1-{\lambda_{i}})(m_{i}+1)}{(\|x\|+\|\nabla x\|)^{{\lambda_{i}}(m_{i}+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}{\left(\|x\|^{m_{i}+1}+\|x\|^{(1-{\lambda_{i}})(m_{i}+1)}\|\nabla x\|^{{\lambda_{i}}(m_{i}+1)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\left(\|x\|^{m_{i}+1}+C(\varepsilon)\|x\|^{{n_{i}}}+{\varepsilon}\|\nabla x\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}\left(\|x\|^{m_{i}+1}+\|x\|^{{n_{i}}}\right)+\varepsilon C^{\prime}\|\nabla x\|^{2},i=1,2,

(32)

where

C^{\prime\prime}
are positive constants,
C(\varepsilon):={\varepsilon^{-\frac{\lambda_{i}(m_{i}+1)}{2-\lambda_{i}(m_{i}+1)}}}

Putting (
32

31
), we get

\displaystyle{\dot{V}(x)}\leq

\displaystyle-{(\underline{a}-2M\varepsilon C^{\prime})}\|\nabla x\|^{2}-\left(\underline{c}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\|x\|^{2}+C^{\prime\prime}M\left(\|x\|^{m_{1}+1}+\|x\|^{{n_{1}}}\right)+C^{\prime\prime}M\left(\|x\|^{m_{2}+1}+\|x\|^{{n_{2}}}\right)+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{U}

\displaystyle{-\left(\underline{c}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\|x\|^{2}+C^{\prime\prime}M\left(\|x\|^{m}+\|x\|^{n}\right)+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{U}},

N>2

5
Conclusion

In this work, we developed a set of Lyapunov analytical tools for the LiISS analysis of non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems involving superlinear terms, corresponding to which the nonlinear functionals map the state from a small space to a large space. More precisely, we first provided several forms of generalized comparison principles for nonlinear ODEs having time-varying coefficients, which enables us to conduct the LiISS analysis for non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems in the framework of Lyapunov stability theory, thereby avoiding the derivation of the 0-UASs (or 0-UGASs) via the semigroup theory. Then, for non-autonomous infinite-dimensional systems,
we proved an LiISS Lyapunov theorem in the framework of Banach spaces and provided sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of an LiISS-LF in the framework of Hilbert spaces.
Through two examples, we illustrated how to use the developed result to verify the LiISS for a nonlinear finite-dimensional system under linear state feedback control and a nonlinear multi-dimensional parabolic PDE, respectively. In particular, we demonstrated how to use the interpolation inequalities to handle superlinear terms in the PDE and establish the LiISS by using the Lyapunov method.
It is noteworthy that for non-autonomous boundary control systems, constructing appropriate Lyapunov functionals presents significant challenges. Consequently, conducting the LiISS
analysis for such systems remains inherently difficult and will be investigated in future work.

Factorization of spread polynomials
Johann Cigler, Hans-Christian Herbig

Abstract
. We present a proof of a conjecture of Goh and Wildberger on the factorization of the spread polynomials. We indicate how the factors can be effectively calculated and exhibit a connection to the factorization of Fibonacci numbers into primitive parts.

Some well-known facts about Lucas polynomials

The Lucas polynomials

are defined by the recursion

(2.1)

\displaystyle L_{n}(x)=xL_{n-1}(x)-L_{n-2}(x)

for

with initial values

and
L_{1}(x)=x

Up to the index

The first terms are (cf.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ c|c c c c c c c c }n&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&\cdots\\

The Lucas polynomials are related to the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind

L_{n}(x)-2

Proposition 3.1
.

For

the roots of

are
\nu_{k}=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right)

For odd

are double roots and
\nu_{0}=2
is a simple root.
For even

are double roots and
\nu_{0},\nu_{m}
are simple roots.

Proof.

Since

for

is a root of
f_{n}(x)
. All different roots of this form are given by
0\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}

We claim that in this way we obtain all the roots of

because the number of roots above, counted with multiplicity, in fact already amounts to
n

To show the claim for
n=2m+1
it suffices to show that

(3.1)

\displaystyle(L_{2m+1}(x)-2)(x-2)=(L_{m+1}(x)-L_{m}(x))^{2},

which is a square of a polynomial. Identity (
3.1
) in turn can be shown by setting

and evaluating

\displaystyle\left(z^{2m+1}+\frac{1}{z^{2m+1}}-2\right)\left(z+\frac{1}{z}-2\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(z^{2(2m+1)}-2z^{2m+1}+1\right)\left(z^{2}-2z+1\right)}{z^{2m+2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\left(z^{2m+1}-1\right)(z-1)}{z^{m+1}}\right)^{2}=\left(\left(z^{m+1}+\frac{1}{z^{m+1}}\right)-\left(z^{m}+\frac{1}{z^{m}}\right)\right)^{2}

For
n=2m
in turn it suffices to show the identity

(3.2)

\displaystyle(L_{2m}(x)-2)(x-2)(x+2)=(L_{m+1}(x)-L_{m-1}(x))^{2}.

This, however, follows from

\frac{z^{2m}+\frac{1}{z^{2m}}-2}{\left(z+\frac{1}{z}-2\right)\left(z+\frac{1}{z}+2\right)}=\left(\frac{\left(z^{2m}-1\right)}{z^{m-1}\left(z^{2}-1\right)}\right)^{2}

and
\left(z+\frac{1}{z}-2\right)\left(z+\frac{1}{z}+2\right)=\left(\frac{\left(z^{2}-1\right)}{z}\right)^{2}
because

\displaystyle\left(z^{2m}+\frac{1}{z^{2m}}-2\right)\left(\frac{\left(z^{2}-1\right)}{z}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\left(z^{2m}-1\right)}{z^{m-1}\left(z^{2}-1\right)}\left(\frac{\left(z^{2}-1\right)}{z}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{\left(z^{2m}-1\right)\left(z^{2}-1\right)}{z^{m+1}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(z^{m+1}-\frac{1}{z^{m-1}}-z^{m-1}+\frac{1}{z^{m+1}}\right)^{2}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

4.
Some properties of
Z_{n}(x)

The formula
Z_{n}(x)=2-L_{n}(2-x)
shows that
Z_{n}(x)
is closely related to the Lucas polynomials
L_{n}(x)
. The first terms are:

\begin{array}[]{ c|c c c c c c }n&1&2&3&4&5&\cdots\\
\hline\cr Z_{n}(x)&x&4x-x^{2}&9x-6x^{2}+x^{3}&16x-20x^{2}+8x^{3}-x^{4}&25x-50x^{2}+35x^{3}-10x^{4}+x^{5}&\cdots\end{array}.

Note that
-Z_{n}(-x)
is a monic polynomial of degree
n
with positive coefficients. Since
L_{n}(2)=2
we have
Z_{n}(0)=0
.

Let
c(n,k)=\left[x^{k}\right]L_{n}(2+x)
. From
L_{n}(2+x)=(2+x)L_{n-1}(2+x)-L_{n-2}(2+x)
we get

c(n,k)=2c(n-1,k)+c(n-1,k-1)-c(n-2,k)
. Since
c(0,1)=0
and
c(1,1)=1
we get with induction that

c(n,1)=n^{2}
. It is easily verfied that
c(n,k)=\binom{n+k-1}{n-k}\frac{n}{k}
. Therefore, we get

(4.1)

\displaystyle Z_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n+k-1}{n-k}\frac{n}{k}x^{k}.

With
\lambda(x)=\alpha(2-x)=\frac{2-x+\sqrt{x^{2}-4x}}{2}
we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle Z_{n}(x)=\frac{\left(\lambda(x)^{n}-1\right)^{2}}{\lambda(x)^{n}}

because
Z_{n}(x)=2-L_{n}(2-x)=2-\left((\lambda(x))^{n}+\frac{1}{(\lambda(x))^{n}}\right)=-\frac{\left((\lambda(x))^{n}-1\right)^{2}}{(\lambda(x))^{n}}.

Another connection with the Lucas polynomials is the following.

Remark 4.1
.

\displaystyle Z_{2n+1}\left(x^{2}\right)=(L_{2n+1}(x))^{2},

\displaystyle Z_{2n}\left(x^{2}\right)=4-(L_{2n}(x))^{2}.

Proof.

For odd
m
we have
Z_{m}\left(x^{2}\right)=2-L_{m}\left(2-x^{2}\right)=2-L_{m}(-L_{2}(x))=2+L_{2m}(x)=(L_{m}(x))^{2}

because of Equation (

). The second identity follows from

\displaystyle Z_{2n}\left(x^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2-L_{2n}\left(2-x^{2}\right)=2-L_{2n}\left(2-\left(z+\frac{1}{z}\right)^{2}\right)=2-L_{2n}\left(z^{2}+\frac{1}{z^{2}}\right)=2-\left(z^{4n}+\frac{1}{z^{4n}}\right)

\displaystyle=4-\left(z^{2n}+\frac{1}{z^{2n}}\right)^{2}=4-(L_{2n}(x))^{2}.

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For arbitrary

4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)
and has degree
\deg\phi_{n}=\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}
. It is sufficient to show that the root
\gamma_{k}=4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{k}m}\right)
of
\phi_{2^{k}m}(x)
is also a root of
\phi_{m}\left((\phi_{2^{k}}(x))^{2}\right)
as
\phi_{2^{k}m}(x)
is irreducible and
\deg\left(\phi_{m}\circ(\phi_{2^{k}}(x))^{2}\right)=\frac{\varphi(m)}{2}2^{k-1}=\frac{\varphi\left(2^{k}m\right)}{2}=\deg\phi_{2^{k}m}
.

Let
f^{(1)}(x)=f(x)=x^{2}-2
and
f^{(n)}(x)=f\circ f^{(n-1)}(x)
for
n\geq 2
. By Equation (
6.3
)

f(\phi_{2^{k-1}}(x))=\phi_{2^{k}}(x)
, and therefore we have for
1\leq j<k

f^{(k-j)}(\phi_{2^{j}}(x))=\phi_{2^{k}}(x).

Let us first show by induction that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}

(6.7)

\displaystyle f^{(n)}\left(4\sin^{2}(\theta)-2\right)=2-4\sin^{2}\left(2^{n}\theta\right).

This is true for
n=1
since

f\left(4\sin^{2}(\theta)\right)-2)=\left(4\sin^{2}(\theta)-2\right)^{2}-2=\left(2\cos^{2}(\theta)-2\sin^{2}(\theta)\right)^{2}-2=4\cos^{2}(2\theta)-2=2-4\sin^{2}(2\theta).

Equation (
6.7
) follows now from

f^{(n)}\left(4\sin^{2}(\theta)\right)-2)=f\left(f^{(n-1)}\left(4\sin^{2}(\theta)-2\right)\right)=f\left(2-4\sin^{2}\left(2^{n-1}\theta\right)\right)=2-4\sin^{2}\left(2^{n}\theta\right).

Now let us compute
\phi_{2^{k}}(\gamma_{k})
. For
k=2
we find

\phi_{4}(\gamma_{2})=\gamma_{2}-2=4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4m}\right)-2=2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4m}\right)-2\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4m}\right)=-2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right),

and for
k>2

\displaystyle\phi_{2^{k}}(\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=f^{(k-2)}(\phi_{4}(\gamma_{k}))=f^{(k-2)}(\gamma_{k}-2)=f^{(k-2)}\left(4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{k}m}\right)-1\right)=2-4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{2^{k-2}\pi}{2^{k}m}\right)

\displaystyle=2-4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4m}\right)=2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right).

Finally, using Equation (
6.5
) we get

\left(\phi_{m}\circ\phi_{2^{k}}^{2}\right)(\gamma_{k})=\phi_{m}\left(4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right)\right)=(-1)^{\varphi(m)/2}\phi_{2m}\left(4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2m}\right)\right)=0.

∎

Remark 6.2
.

The relation
Z_{m}(\Phi_{2^{n}}(x))=\Phi_{2^{n}}(Z_{m}(x))
holds for
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

For
n=2
we have
\Phi_{4}(x)=(x-2)^{2}
. This gives

Z_{m}(\Phi_{4}(x))=Z_{m}\left((x-2)^{2}\right)=(L_{m}(x-2))^{2}=(L_{m}(2-x))^{2}=(2-Z_{m}(x))^{2}=\Phi_{4}(Z_{m}(x))
.
By induction we get

\displaystyle Z_{m}(\Phi_{2^{n+1}}(x))

\displaystyle=Z_{m}\left(\left((\phi_{2^{n}}(x))^{2}-2\right)^{2}\right)=\left(L_{m}\left((\phi_{2^{n}}(x))^{2}-2\right)\right)^{2}=\left(2-Z_{m}\left((\phi_{2^{n}}(x))^{2}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(2-Z_{m}(\Phi_{2^{n}}(x)))^{2}=(2-\Phi_{2^{n}}(Z_{m}(x)))^{2}=\left((\phi_{2^{n}}(Z_{m}(x)))^{2}-2\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\phi_{2^{n+1}}(Z_{m}(x)))^{2}=\Phi_{2^{n+1}}(Z_{m}(x)),

where we have been using Remark
4.1
and Equation (
6.3
).
∎

7.
Final remarks

Let us finally show some connection with the Fibonacci numbers
(F_{n})_{n\geq 0}=(0,1,1,2,3,5,8,13,21,\dotsc)
.
They satisfy
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
for
n\geq 2
with
F_{0}=0,\ F_{1}=1
and Binet’s Formula gives

(7.1)

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{\rho^{n}-\sigma^{n}}{\rho-\sigma}

with
\rho=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and
\sigma=\frac{-1}{\rho}=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}
. It is known (see
[
5
]
) that the Fibonacci numbers form what is called a divisibility sequence.

Since
\lambda(5)=\frac{2-5+\sqrt{25-20}}{2}=\frac{-3+\sqrt{5}}{2}=-\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{2}=-\sigma^{2}
we get

(\lambda(5))^{n}+\frac{1}{(\lambda(5))^{n}}=(-1)^{n}\left(\sigma^{2n}+\rho^{2n}\right)=(-1)^{n}\left(5\left(\frac{\rho^{n}-\sigma^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}\right)+2(-1)^{n}\right)=5(-1)^{n}F_{n}^{2}+2,

which implies

(7.2)

\displaystyle Z_{n}(5)=(-1)^{n-1}5F_{n}^{2}.

Theorem
1.1
gives then the factorization of the Fibonacci numbers into primitive parts (cf.
[
6
, entry A061446]
and
[
5
]
)

F_{n}=\prod_{d|n}p_{d}

with
p_{1}=1
and
p_{n}=|\phi_{n}(5)|
for
n\geq 2
. We get:

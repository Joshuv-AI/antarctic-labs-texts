Theory Framework of Multiplexed Photon-Number-Resolving Detectors
Xiaobin Zhao, Hezheng Qin, Hong X. Tang, Linran Fan, Quntao Zhuang

Abstract
Photon counting is a fundamental component in quantum optics and quantum information. However, implementing ideal photon-number-resolving (PNR) detectors remains experimentally challenging. Multiplexed PNR detection offers a scalable and practical alternative by distributing photons across multiple modes and detecting their presence using simple ON-OFF detectors, thereby enabling approximate photon-number resolution. In this work, we establish a theoretical model for such detectors and prove that the estimation error in terms of photon number moments decreases inverse proportionally to the number of detectors. Thanks to the enhanced PNR capability, multiplexed PNR detector provides an advantage in cat-state breeding protocols. Assuming a two-photon subtraction case, 7 7 dB of squeezing, and an array of 20 detectors of efficiency 95 % 95\% , our calculation predicts fidelity ∼ 0.88 \sim 0.88 with a success probability ∼ 3.8 % \sim 3.8\% , representing orders-of-magnitude improvement over previous works. Similar enhancement also extends to cat-state generation with the generalized photon number subtraction. With experimentally feasible parameters, our results suggest that megahertz-rate cat-state generation is achievable using an on-chip array of tens of ON-OFF detectors.

In this work, we establish the theory framework for analyzing MPNR detectors. We evaluate the key performance metrics of MPNR detectors versus the number of multiplexed modes and demonstrate two applications: benchmarking photon-statistics reconstruction and generating non-Gaussian states. To begin with, we show the equivalence between two common architectures of MPNR detectors, the sequential and parallel schemes.
Further, we demonstrate that an MPNR detector with near-unit efficiency and binomially distributed dark counts can approximate a single PNR detector of comparable efficiency and dark counts. The approximation error is

\mathcal{O}(1/n)
for all the moments of the photon statistics, where
n
is the number of ON-OFF detectors in multiplexing. As an example of photon statistics evaluation, we reconstruct the photon-number distribution of a squeezed vacuum from measurements taken with an MPNR detector. We introduce practical metrics to quantify the reconstruction error in this process.

Moreover, we numerically analyze cat-state breeding with high-pixel MPNR detectors, charting the trade-off between fidelity and success probability as the detector size and efficiency vary. Assuming
7

\sim 0.88

, our results suggest that megahertz rate cat-state generation is achievable using an on-chip MPNR array of tens of ON-OFF detectors. In addition, a comparable enhancement is observed in cat-state breeding when generalized photon-number subtraction is used.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
II

III

II
Model of multiplexed PNR detectors

There are two main approaches to multiplexed PNR, as we depict in Fig.
1
(a) and (c). In subplot (a), the incoming light goes through a sequence of nanowire detectors, where each detector splits a portion of the light to an ON-OFF detector. The photon number information is converted into a time-resolved electronic signal. In subplot (c), the input light is directly split into multiple portions, and each portion is fed into an ON-OFF detector, which registers a click or no click via an electronic signal. In both cases, one can provide an estimate of the photon number of the incoming light field by counting the total number of ON-OFF detectors that detect photons. Our main result is that both schemes can be well described by a single imperfect PNR detector with an effective efficiency and dark count.

To present the main theorem, we denote the portion of light split to each ON-OFF detector as
\eta_{\ell}
, the efficiency of each detector
\kappa_{\ell}
, and we assume uniform dark count
\epsilon\ll 1
. In the configurations shown in Figs.
1
(a) and (c),
\eta_{\ell}
account for the cumulative effect of preceding splitting ratios. To make use of the large number of ON-OFF detectors, the design guarantees that
\eta_{\ell}\sim 1/n
, i.e., the incoming light is roughly evenly split to all ON-OFF detectors. With these notations, we have our major results as follows.

Theorem 1

When detecting a quantum state with finite energy, a multiplexed PNR detector with ON-OFF detections can approximate an ideal PNR detector with efficiency
\overline{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa_{j}\eta_{j}
and binomial-distributed dark count with average
\overline{\epsilon}=n\epsilon
. The approximation error scales as
O(1/n)
for all moments of the photon-number distribution.

By definition, the dark count of the detector is the photon counts on vacuum input. Therefore, the dark count can be obtained directly by summing up the uniformly distributed dark count of
n
detectors, leading to a binomial-distributed dark count of average photon number
\overline{\epsilon}=n\epsilon
. Now we can proceed to analyze the error from the ON-OFF detectors in terms of photon-number efficiency, where we can assume no dark count for simplicity.

For the input mode
\hat{a}
to the multiplexed PNR detector, the mode at the
j
-th ON-OFF detectors

\hat{a}_{j}=\sqrt{\kappa_{j}\eta_{j}}\hat{a}+{\rm vac}
,
where ‘vac’ denotes vacuum modes. One can obtain this mode via first performing a single pure loss channel with transmissivity
\bar{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa_{j}\eta_{j}
and then a lossless beamsplitter array with the weights
\{\kappa_{j}\eta_{j}/\bar{\kappa}\}_{j=1}^{n}
. Therefore, the multiplexed PNR detector can be decomposed into a two-step process: In the first step, the input goes through a loss of
\bar{\kappa}
; In the second step, the mode goes through a lossless unit efficiency ON-OFF detector array, where a perfect beamsplitter array splits the mode into
n
portions, one for each detector.
Now we just need to show that a lossless network of unit efficiency ON-OFF detectors can approximate an ideal PNR detector, which is proven in the following lemma.

Lemma 2

(lossless ON-OFF array approximating a PNR)
Given an arbitrary quantum state, applying a lossless
n
-plexed PNR detector without dark count to estimate the
h
-th moment of photon number (
h=\mathcal{O}(1)
independent of
n
), the estimation error is:

\displaystyle N^{h}_{\rm mpnr}-N^{h}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),

(1)

where
N^{h}=\langle(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a})^{h}\rangle
is the true value of the
h
-th order moment of photon number.

Measurement statistics in application examples

For simplicity, let us consider the MPNR detector operates through a balanced beamsplitter.
Then, given an arbitrary pure state

P_{{\rm ON-OFF},k}=\bigoplus_{\sigma_{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k}}U_{\sigma_{g}}\left(\widetilde{I}^{\otimes k}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes n-k}\right)U_{\sigma_{g}}^{\dagger}

n

To focus on the effects unique to MPNR, we will ignore dark count in the following analyses. A central quantum state examined in this section is the single-mode squeezed vacuum state,

IV
Conclusions and discussion

In this work, we established a theoretical framework for photon counting with MPNR detectors. We demonstrate that, for estimating higher-order moments of the photon number, the error of MPNR scales inversely with the number of ON-OFF detectors
n
employed in the MPNR detector,
\sim\mathcal{O}(1/n)
. Beyond the general analysis, we examine specific applications, including photon counting for squeezed vacuum states and the cat-state breeding process using MPNR detectors. We numerically illustrate the fidelity and success probability of the breeding process assisted by MPNR detectors.

Similar to dark count noise, we expect detector dead time to also introduce an error that scales as
1/n
, as long as the detectors are independent. The detector correlation is another important effect that may affect MPNR detectors.
In Appendix
D
, we analyze the scenario in which each ON-OFF detector in the MPNR detector configuration exhibits correlations with an adjacent detector. Specifically, if one detector registers an ON event while its neighbor registers OFF, the correlated detection mechanism can still result in both detectors indicating ON with a probability
p
. Here we show that in this scenario, the estimation of the number of photons using an MPNR detector exhibits bias, which vanishes as the correlation probability
p
approaches zero.

The operator
\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}I_{k}
can be expressed as a summation of terms involving products of
\widetilde{I}_{k}
and
|0\rangle\langle 0|_{k}
. Explicitly, it includes terms such as
\widetilde{I}_{1}\otimes\widetilde{I}_{2}\cdots\widetilde{I}_{n}
,
|0\rangle\langle 0|_{1}\otimes\widetilde{I}_{2}\cdots\widetilde{I}_{n}
, and
|0\rangle\langle 0|_{1}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|_{2}\cdots|0\rangle\langle 0|_{n}
. Then, it is quick to prove that it can be rewritten as the summation of
k
identical terms equal to
P_{{\rm ON-OFF},k}
.

Using Definition
6
and Lemma
7
, the distribution of total photon number can be determined. Then, one can combine them with the balanced beamsplitter operation
U_{\rm BS}
acted before ON-OFF detectors:

\displaystyle P_{{\rm ON-OFF},k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=U_{\rm BS}^{\dagger}P_{{\rm ON-OFF},k}U_{\rm BS}

(7)

\displaystyle\widehat{N}^{{}^{\prime}h}_{\rm ON-OFF}

\displaystyle=U_{\rm BS}^{\dagger}\widehat{N}^{h}_{\rm ON-OFF}U_{\rm BS}

(8)

where
U_{\rm BS}
is a beamsplitter operation that satisfies the relation
U_{\rm BS}\hat{a}_{k}U_{\rm BS}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\,\omega_{jk}^{1/2}\hat{a}_{j},\ k=1\cdots,n
with the weights
\{\omega_{jk}\}
fulfilling the normalization condition
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\omega_{jk}\omega_{k\ell}\right)^{1/2}=\delta_{j\ell}
.
Without losing the generality, all ancillas are assumed to be vacuum states. Therefore, this operation allows for a complete characterization of the measurement process in a MPNR detector.

In the following subsections, we will present an application to the detection of photon distribution and its higher moments.

A.2.1
Estimation of mixed states

Let’s look at the performance of MPNR detectors in estimation photon moments. On the side of probe state, we can consider an arbitrary single-mode quantum state expressed with P-function:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)|\underline{\alpha}\rangle\langle\underline{\alpha}|.

(9)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\left|\underline{\sqrt{\eta_{j}^{\prime\prime}}{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle\underline{\sqrt{\eta_{j}^{\prime\prime}}{\alpha}}\right|.

Without losing the generality, let’s consider the scenario with presumptions

and

\displaystyle N^{h}_{\rm mpnr}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{I}_{i}\bigotimes_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\

(13)

\displaystyle\left.\ \ \ \ \ +\mathcal{O}\left(\max_{j}\left|\eta_{j}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{n}\right|\right)\right]

(14)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)|\alpha|^{2h}-\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}P(\alpha)\sum_{m=1}^{h}\frac{n!(h-1)!}{(n-m)!m!(h-m)!(m-1)!}\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{n}\right)^{m}\left[\frac{m|\alpha|^{2}}{2n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{4}}{n^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \left.+\mathcal{O}\left(\max_{j}\left|\eta_{j}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{n}\right|\right)\right]

(17)

where

is the true value of the

By using Eq. (

Appendix C
Application to cat state generation protocols

It has been widely shown that a significant challenge in approximating GKP states lies in the implementation of PNR detectors when preparing cat states
Takase et al. 2021
;
Endo et al. 2023
;
Takase et al. 2023
;
Takase et al. 2024
, which typically serves as the initial step in generating GKP states
Vasconcelos et al. 2010
;
Weigand and Terhal 2018
;
Konno et al. 2024
. The first scheme for generating cat states was proposed in
Dakna et al. 1997
, based on a breeding process using a squeezed input state and PNR detection. Later, the first experimental realization of approximate small-amplitude cat states, conditioned by single-photon detection, was reported in
Wenger et al. 2004
and
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2006
, following the theoretical proposal in
Lund et al. 2004
. Additionally, a method for breeding large-amplitude cat states from small-amplitude ones was proposed in
Lund et al. 2004
. Subsequent theoretical and experimental work demonstrated that the roles of the squeezed state and PNR detector could be interchanged by breeding from Fock states with homodyne detection
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
. However, the realization of a “true” PNR detector and deterministic generation of Fock states remains challenging tasks
Deng et al. 2024
. The generation of cat states with higher probabilities remains an open problem in the preparation of logical states and error correction
De Neeve et al. 2022
;
Campagne-Ibarcq et al. 2020
;
Eickbusch et al. 2022
. In this section, we investigate the performance of using ON-OFF detectors in cat state breeding.

C.1
Explicit expression of Schrödinger cat states

The Schrödinger cat states are defined as follows:

\displaystyle|{\rm cat}\rangle^{+}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\alpha}\left(|\underline{\alpha}\rangle+|\underline{-\alpha}\rangle\right)

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\alpha}e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\alpha^{2j}}{\sqrt{(2j)!}}|2j\rangle

(37)

\displaystyle|{\rm cat}\rangle^{-}_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\alpha}\left(|\underline{\alpha}\rangle-|\underline{-\alpha}\rangle\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\alpha}e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\alpha^{2j+1}}{\sqrt{(2j+1)!}}|2j+1\rangle

(39)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha}=\sqrt{2+2e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}}}
is a constant for normalization.

C.2
Breeding via photon subtraction

C.2.1
Breeding cat states from squeezed states

In practice, the Schrödinger cat state

is usually generated in the following process

;
Takase et al. 2023

\displaystyle|{\rm cat}\rangle_{\alpha}\approx\langle k|V(|z\rangle_{\rm sq}\otimes|0\rangle),

(40)

where
V
denotes a beamsplitter operation:
VaV^{\dagger}=\sqrt{1-\eta}a-i\sqrt{\eta}b
,

with

being the annihilation operator of the first (second) mode,
\eta
being the reflectivity satisfying
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
|z\rangle_{\rm sq}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-e^{i\phi}\tanh r)^{j}\frac{\sqrt{(2j)!}}{2^{j}j!}|2j\rangle
is a single-mode squeezed vacuum state,
\langle k|
denotes a probabilistic conditioning that projecting the input onto a Fock state
|k\rangle,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Using the result in Eq. (
25
), the explicit expression of the output state before photon detection can be derived:

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}e^{-\tanh r\frac{\alpha^{*2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\left|\underline{\sqrt{1-\eta}\,\alpha}\right\rangle\left|\underline{\sqrt{\eta}\,i\alpha}\right\rangle.

|k\rangle,k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\rm cat}\rangle_{\sqrt{k}}\propto

\displaystyle\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}e^{-\tanh r\frac{\alpha^{*2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{(1-\eta)|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\frac{(1-\eta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\alpha^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}\left|\underline{\sqrt{\eta}\,i\alpha}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}e^{-\tanh r\frac{\alpha^{*2}}{2}}e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\frac{(1-\eta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\eta^{\frac{j}{2}}i^{j}\alpha^{k+j}}{\sqrt{k!j!}}\left|j\right\rangle

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}\left(-\tanh r\frac{\alpha^{*2}}{2}\right)^{(k+2j)/2}\frac{1}{(k/2+j)!}e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\frac{(1-\eta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\eta^{j}i^{2j}\alpha^{k+2j}}{\sqrt{k!(2j)!}}\left|2j\right\rangle,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (k{\rm\ is\ even})\\

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\tanh r\right)^{(k+2j)/2}\frac{(k+2j)!}{(k/2+j)!}\frac{(1-\eta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\eta^{j}i^{2j}}{\sqrt{k!(2j)!}}\left|2j\right\rangle,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (k{\rm\ is\ even})\\

(45)

where Eq. (

C.2.2
Application of MPNR detectors

As shown in Section
A.2
, PNR detectors can be effectively implemented using ON-OFF detectors with assistance of a beamsplitter network. For an ideal PNR detector, if we probabilistically leave the state unchanged when the outcome is
k

n

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{{\rm ON-OFF},k}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma_{g}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k}}U_{\sigma_{g}}\left(|0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\widetilde{I}^{\otimes k}\right)U_{\sigma_{g}}^{\dagger},\ \ \ \ (k\leq n)

(46)

where

denotes the permutation unitary operation,
\mathcal{S}_{n,k}
represents the permutation group for two sets of identical inputs, with
(n-k)
and
k
elements respectively,

denotes a projector of the
j

|0\rangle\langle 0|_{j}

Now, let’s look at the breeding process. A beamsplitter will generate interference between a single-mode squeezed state and a vacuum state. The output state can be written in Eq. (
41

n
-port balanced beamsplitter acting on the first mode of the state in Eq. (

\displaystyle|\Psi^{\prime}\rangle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r}}e^{-\tanh r\frac{\alpha^{*2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\left|\underline{\frac{\sqrt{1-\eta}\,\alpha}{\sqrt{n}}}\right\rangle^{\otimes n}\left|\underline{\sqrt{\eta}\,i\alpha}\right\rangle.

k

\displaystyle\rho_{\widetilde{\rm cat}}\propto

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(n-k)!k!}\cdot\Tr\left[\left(|0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes n-k}\otimes\widetilde{I}^{\otimes k}\otimes I\right)|\Psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\Psi^{\prime}|\right]

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(n-k)!k!}\cdot\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\alpha{\rm d}^{2}\beta}{\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\cosh r}e^{-\tanh r\frac{\alpha^{*2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}e^{-\tanh r\frac{\beta^{*2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left[-\frac{(1-\eta)(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2})}{2}\right]\left\{\exp\left[\frac{(1-\eta)\alpha\beta^{*}}{n}\right]-1\right\}^{k}

\displaystyle\times\left|\underline{\sqrt{\eta}\,i\alpha}\right\rangle\left\langle\underline{\sqrt{\eta}\,i\beta}\right|

45

n

Without losing the generality, let’s make the following assumption: the number of ON-OFF detectors

\displaystyle\left.\rho_{\widetilde{\rm cat}}\right|_{1-\eta=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\atop n\gg\sinh^{2}r}\propto

Appendix D
Modeling of correlated detectors

D.1
Definition: two-mode error

Consider a scenario in which a two-mode state simultaneously click two adjacent ON-OFF detectors. In the ideal scenario, the input-output relation is supposed to be:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|0\rangle\otimes|0\rangle&\to 0\\
|0\rangle\otimes|k\rangle&\to 1\\
|k\rangle\otimes|0\rangle&\to 1\\
|k\rangle\otimes|k\rangle&\to 2\end{cases},\ \ \ \ \ \forall k\geq 1.

(107)

If the two detectors are correlated, the input-output relation will be:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|0\rangle\otimes|0\rangle&\to 0\\
|0\rangle\otimes|k\rangle&\to 2\\
|k\rangle\otimes|0\rangle&\to 2\\
|k\rangle\otimes|k\rangle&\to 2\end{cases},\ \ \ \ \ \forall k\geq 1.

(108)

In this case, the corresponding POVM for estimating total photon number of two modes is:

\displaystyle M_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=|0\rangle\langle 0|\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|

(109)

\displaystyle M_{ii}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=I\otimes I-|0\rangle\langle 0|\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|.

(110)

The observable of the total photon number is:

\displaystyle\widehat{N}^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(I\otimes I-|0\rangle\langle 0|\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|\right).

(111)

Considering the scenario where the correlation error occurs with a small probability
p
, the observed photon number is:

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{\rm ON-OFF,cor,2}^{h}

\displaystyle=(1-p)\langle\widehat{N}_{\rm ON-OFF,2}^{h}\rangle+p\langle\widehat{N}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}h}\rangle

(112)

where
\widehat{N}_{\rm ON-OFF,2}=2\widetilde{I}\otimes\widetilde{I}+\widetilde{I}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|+|0\rangle\langle 0|\otimes\widetilde{I}
denotes the observable of ON-OFF detectors with
\widetilde{I}=I-|0\rangle\langle 0|
being a projection onto the non-vacuum state space. Accordingly, one can defined the observable
\widehat{N}^{h}_{\rm ON-OFF,cor,2}=(1-p)\widehat{N}^{h}_{\rm ON-OFF,2}+p\widehat{N}^{{}^{\prime}h}_{2}
for this error.

D.2
Multimode case

n

n

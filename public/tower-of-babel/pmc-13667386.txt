Matrix metalloproteinase-9 is associated with CX3CL1 on tau and amyloid biomarker pathways in APOE4 carriers
Authors: Aimee K. Johnson, Carol A. Van Hulle, Hannah Zylstra, Kate Cronin, Kevin Johnson, Leonardo Rivera-Rivera, Richard Chappell, Rachael Wilson, Przemysław R. Kac, Carey E. Gleason, Henrik Zetterberg, Sterling C. Johnson, Anthony P. Auger, Cynthia M. Carlsson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
APOE ε4 carriers exhibit heightened pro-inflammatory responses and microglial dysregulation compared to non-carriers. CX3CL1 is a biomarker involved in microglial signaling and Alzheimer’s disease. It can be cleaved by proteases such as matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), which are associated with memory and vasculature remodeling. We examined the role of MMP-9 in the pathway involving the association of CX3CL1 with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers for amyloid and tau in APOE ε4 carriers (APOE4+) compared to non-carriers (APOE4−). We further examined the association between APOE4 groups and inflammatory profiles. CSF samples were collected from 115 cognitively unimpaired VA-eligible Veterans enrolled in the Brain Amyloid and Vascular Effects of Eicosapentaenoic Acid (BRAVE) study (NCT02719327). We found that Aβ42 and Aβ42/Aβ40 were lower in APOE4+ carriers compared to non-carriers. Additionally, p-tau217 was higher among APOE4+ participants compared to APOE4− participants; however, p-tau217/tau was not different between carrier groups. In moderated mediation models, MMP-9 mediated the effect of CX3CL1 on Aβ42/Aβ40, p-tau217/tau and p-tau217/Aβ42 among APOE4+ carriers only. Lastly, we found a correlation between CX3CL1 and a composite pro-inflammatory z score, as well as lower ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 baseline levels in the APOE4+ group. However, CX3CL1 correlated with an anti-inflammatory z score in the APOE4− group, suggesting different inflammatory roles of CX3CL1 in APOE4+ carriers vs non-carriers. These findings suggest that the association of MMP-9 and CX3CL1 plays a unique role in tau and amyloid regulation among APOE4+ carriers, and that early dysregulation of immune systems may occur in cognitively unimpaired APOE4+ individuals.

Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD), one of the leading causes of dementia in older adults (Alzheimers Dement., 2026), is characterized by the accumulation of amyloid-rich neuritic plaques (Glenner and Wong, 1984; Masters et al., 1985) and hyperphosphorylated tau-based neurofibrillary tangles (Grundke-Iqbal et al., 1986; Wang et al., 2012) within the brain. A major genetic risk factor for AD is the apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 allele (Corder et al., 1993; Poirier et al., 1993). The APOE gene has three main allelic variants, APOE ε2, APOE ε3, and APOE ε4. APOE ε4 is considered the strongest genetic predictor for developing AD. Therefore, research has focused on identifying blood or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers that are different in APOE ε4 carriers versus non-carriers prior to cognitive impairments. Here we examine several biomarkers among cognitively unimpaired adults in mid-to-late life who are either APOE ε4 carriers or non-carriers.

Previous studies have reported that APOE ε4 carriers can exhibit accelerated cognitive decline (Deary et al., 2002) and neurological alterations (Sheline et al., 2010) when compared to non-carriers, suggesting the presence of APOE ε4 impacts neuronal function over time. While a decrease in CSF amyloid-beta (Aβ) 42 or Aβ42/Aβ40 and increased phosphorylation of tau at threonine 217 (p-tau217) or threonine 181 predict AD progression (Fagan et al., 2007; Janelidze et al., 2016; Jonaitis et al., 2023), amyloid pathologies precede tau pathologies in the course of AD pathophysiology (Bellaver et al., 2023; Jack et al., 2013). NIA-AA stages an individual’s biological changes due to Alzheimer’s disease by their amyloid (A), tau (T) and neurodegenerative (N) status (+/−) (Jack et al., 2024). Ratios such as Aβ42/Aβ40 and p-tau217/Aβ42 have high correlations with amyloid PET positivity (A+) and are identified as Core 1 (i.e. presymptomatic) biomarkers in the NIA-AA criteria of diagnosis and staging Alzheimer’s disease (Jack et al., 2024). Previous research has found APOE ε4 carriers have lower levels of Aβ42 and Aβ42/Aβ40 compared to those who do not have this genetic risk factor (Benson et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022), and other data indicate that APOE ε4 carriers experience a faster rate of amyloid accumulation (Ge et al., 2018). Together, these studies suggest distinct microenvironments between those at genetic risk for AD and those who are not.

Genetic profiles, such as APOE status, can create variations in cellular microenvironments, which in turn influence neuroinflammatory responses and may lead to dysregulation of distinct molecular pathways associated with tau and amyloid pathologies. Notably, carriers of the APOE ε4 allele exhibit heightened pro-inflammatory responses and microglial dysregulation compared to non-carriers (Arnaud et al., 2022; Tai et al., 2015; Yen and Yu, 2023). In this context, we examined CX3CL1, also referred to as fractalkine (Bazan et al., 1997), and its receptor, CX3CR1 (Imai et al., 1997), which is expressed on microglia (Chen et al., 2016). Typically, CX3CL1 is expressed by neurons and reduces microglia activation by acting upon CX3CR1 (Iwahashi et al., 2025; Pabon et al., 2011; Zujovic et al., 2000). The CX3CL1-CX3CR1 pathway generally reduces inflammation, and its dysregulation has been implicated in tau and amyloid pathologies in neurodegenerative disorders such as AD (Chen et al., 2016). Interestingly, tau can also bind CX3CR1, resulting in phagocytosis by the microglia; however, hyperphosphorylated tau has reduced affinity to CX3CR1, leading to increased accumulation of extracellular tau (Bolós et al., 2017). Additionally, membrane-bound CX3CL1 can be cleaved by proteases, such as matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), resulting in a soluble form of CX3CL1 and diminished CX3CL1-CX3CR1 activation (Iemmolo et al., 2023).

MMPs are a family of endopeptidases that are zinc-dependent and known to degrade components of the extracellular matrix, such as collagen and fibronectin (Cabral-Pacheco et al., 2020; Hidalgo and Eckhardt, 2001; Tanase et al., 2025). They are categorized based on the substrate they cleave as well as their structural components (Cui et al., 2017). Specifically, MMPs contain a signal N-terminal peptide, a pro-domain peptide that allows the enzyme to remain inactive, a catalytic zinc-binding domain, a hemopexin-like domain, and a hinge-region linking the hemopexin-like domain to the catalytic domain (Laronha and Caldeira, 2020). MMP-2 and MMP-9, also known as gelatinase A (Yu et al., 1998) and B (Adair et al., 2004), respectively, contain fibronectin type II inserts within the catalytic zinc-binding domain which is bound by three histidine residues and therefore is highly conserved (Hidalgo and Eckhardt, 2001; Laronha and Caldeira, 2020). Studies have also suggested that MMP-9 might serve a role in the beginning of neurodegenerative disease before tau pathology arises (Hernandes-Alejandro et al., 2020; Radosinska and Radosinska, 2025). Although studies have shown the major proteases involved in the release of sCX3CL1 are ADAM10, ADAM17, BACE1 and γ-secretase (Gunner et al., 2019; Iemmolo et al., 2023), other proteases such as MMP-2 and MMP-9 have been shown to play an influential role in the regulation of CX3CL1 (Butoi et al., 2011; Chen et al., 2026; Wildenberg et al., 2008; Zhao et al., 2020). Importantly, MMP-9 not only interacts with CX3CL1, but it also has been reported to be expressed within the human brain tissue (Backstrom et al., 1996; Li et al., 2026) and can function to cleave amyloid-beta and tau protein (Backstrom et al., 1996; Nübling et al., 2012). However, there is a critical gap in knowledge of an association between MMP-9 and neurodegenerative disease-related biomarkers among APOE ε4 carriers prior to cognitive impairment.

This study was designed as an exploratory investigation into the relationship between APOE ε4 status and inflammatory dysregulation in cognitively unimpaired adults. We hypothesized that the CX3CL1 mechanism related to tau and amyloid pathologies would be dysregulated within APOE ε4 carriers. As the ε4 allele may contribute to differences in the microenvironment, we also hypothesized that we would detect differences in inflammatory profiles among asymptomatic ε4 carriers. Studies have shown that while MMP-9 levels can be mediated through brain pathologies, a significant influencer is the combination of an AD environment including pro-inflammatory cytokine elevation, oxidative stress, beta-amyloid as well as APOE ε4 carrier positivity is crucial to experiencing an elevation in MMP-9 levels in the brain (Ringland et al., 2020). Therefore, we examined if the presence of the APOE ε4 allele resulted in dysregulation of the intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1) and vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM-1), as well as other immune markers.

Methods

Participants

One hundred and fifteen VA-eligible Veterans, aged 50–76, participating in the randomized controlled trial Brain Amyloid and Vascular Effects of Eicosapentaenoic Acid (BRAVE) study (NCT02719327) underwent cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) collection at baseline, 12 and 18 months. Interested participants completed a phone screening, including Montreal Cognitive Assessment, to exclude individuals with suspected memory impairment. Other exclusion criteria included active kidney or liver dysfunction or triglyceride levels ≥500 mg/dL, known memory impairment, or inability to take the study drug. For further detail, see (Carlsson et al., 2025).

CSF sample collection

Lumbar puncture procedures were conducted in the morning, after a 12-h fast and prior to administration of any medications, caffeine, or nicotine. Twenty-two mL of CSF was collected via gentle extraction into polypropylene syringes, combined and gently mixed, and centrifuged for 10 min; 0.5 mL CSF was aliquoted into polypropylene tubes and stored at −80 °C within 1 h of collection (Van Hulle et al., 2021).

APOE genotyping

APOE genotype was measured from whole blood using standard procedures (University of Wisconsin-Madison Biotechnology Center).

Standard protocol approvals, registrations, and participant consent

BRAVE study protocols were approved by the University of Wisconsin IRB (Institutional Review Board) and the VA Research & Development Committee. The study was also conducted in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent prior to study participation.

CSF biomarker quantification

All study biomarkers were quantified from baseline CSF collected prior to distribution of the investigational drug.

Several methods were used to measure relevant biomarkers in CSF. To quantify MMPs, we developed a custom assay using the Meso Scale Discovery (MSD) U-Plex Custom Immuno-Oncology Group 1 (human) Assay (cat. # K151AEM) to multiplex MMP-2, MMP-9, TIMP-1 and TIMP-2. The following antibody sets were used to detect concentrations of these biomarkers: U-Plex MMP-2 (cat. # B21AHK), U-Plex MMP-9 (total) (cat. # B21AHM), R-Plex TIMP-1 (cat. # F21YO) and R-Plex TIMP-2 (cat. # F21ASL). All analytes included in the multiplex were assigned linkers with biotin-binding domains, combined with their corresponding capture antibody and incubated for thirty minutes at room temperature. Then MSD’s stop solution was added to each analyte’s respective microcentrifuge tube and left to incubate for thirty minutes. The multiplex coating mixture was created by adding equal volumes of each linker-antibody and stop solution together. This mixture was then added onto a blank U-PLEX plate and incubated overnight at 4 °C. After incubation, the plate was placed on a plate shaker (600 rpm) at room temperature for one hour to optimize binding of the linker-antibody to the bottom of the plate. To remove any unbound linker-antibody complexes from the sample matrix, a wash step consisting of three washes using MSD’s wash buffer was performed using the Agilent BioTek 405TS microplate washer.

A standard MMP-2 and MMP-9 stock solution was prepared using MSD’s calibrator 29 (cat. # C0412), a lyophilized blend of both analytes. Calibrator 29 was reconstituted with assay diluent and incubated for thirty minutes at room temperature. For multiplex analysis, TIMP-1 and TIMP-2 stock solutions were thawed and added in equal volumes to the MMP-2 and MMP-9 stock solution. This served as the most concentrated calibrator for multiplex analysis and was serially diluted to prepare additional calibrators.

For analysis, CSF samples were diluted 1:15 using assay diluent. 50uL of samples and standards were added to the coated plate and incubated at room temperature while shaking for two hours. This was followed by another wash step to remove any unbound analytes from the well. Detection antibodies that are conjugated with a SULFO-TAG chemiluminescent label were combined per analyte, along with antibody diluent to create the detection antibody solution and then 50uL of this solution was added to each well. The plate was then incubated at room temperature while shaking for one hour followed by another wash step to remove any unbound detection antibodies from the well. MSD Gold Read Buffer B (cat. # R60AM) was then added to the plate and read on the SQ120 machine.

Additional biomarkers were measured at the University of Wisconsin-Madison ADRC Biomarker Lab. P-tau181 (pg/mL) and total tau (pg/mL) were assayed using the Roche Elecsys® IVD phosphorylated-tau (181P), and total tau (tTau) singleplex assays (Roche Diagnostics International, Switzerland) according to manufacturer directions. Measurement of total tau reflects the six major human brain isoforms of the tau protein, 2N4R, 1N4R, 0N4R, 2N3R, 1N3R, 0N3R (Wang and Mandelkow, 2016).

Nucleic acid-Linked Immuno-Sandwich Assay sequencing (NULISA-seq) CNS Disease Panel 120 (Alamar Biosciences, Fremont, CA, Part# 800104, Target Kit Lot# 2425306, Detection Kit Lot# 2423901) was used to measure over 120 protein biomarkers indicative of CNS dysfunction in a single analysis. Soluble CX3CL1 levels were quantified using the NULISAseq CNS Disease Panel 120. NULISAseq assays utilize two sequential antibody capture, wash, and release steps to decrease background and maximize sensitivity (Feng et al., 2023). High multiplex (> 100 biomarkers) capabilities are achieved via target- and sample-specific DNA oligonucleotide tags. Immunocomplex formation and tagging steps were performed using an automated ARGO HT (Alamar Biosciences, Fremont, CA) workflow followed by quantification with next-generation sequencing (Illumina NextSeq1000, San Diego, CA). Following NULISAseq protocols, data were normalized using internal and inter-plate controls and then log2-transformed to yield NULISA Protein Quantification (NPQ) units for statistical analysis.

For NULISA analysis, CSF samples were thawed and centrifuged for 5 min at 10,000 g at 4 °C. After transferring each sample to the provided 96-well plate, the plate was centrifuged for 1 min at 10,000 g at 4 °C. For each plate, a single DNA library was prepared using the ARGO HT and subsequently sequenced using a P2 100 cycle flow cell (Illumina part# 20046811, Reagent Lot# 20858526, Flow cell Lot # 20858269). Once complete, the results were combined into a FASTQ file that was further processed using the ARGO Command Center. This software reports the relative quantification of each analyte by normalizing and converting the output from the FASTQ file into NPQ units that were used for statistical analyses across platforms. To assess within-plate (N = 2) and between plate (N = 8) reproducibility, an in-house pooled CSF control sample was analyzed in duplicate on each plate in addition to the manufacturer-supplied kit controls. The within-plate CV was <5.0% for 96% of target analytes (122/127) across all four plates with a maximum % CV of 20.1%. Similarly, the between-plate CV exceeded 5.0% for only three target analytes, with a maximum % CV of 9.2%.

Statistical analyses

In addition to individual biomarkers, we derived known tau and amyloid ratios to investigate biomarker profiles associated with AD. We included the ratios of p-tau217/total tau and p-tau181/total tau, as these ratios have been reported to improve concordance with amyloid PET compared to p-tau217 or p-tau181 individually (Barthélemy et al., 2023). We also included the amyloid ratio of Aβ42/Aβ40, as this has been shown to more accurately predict amyloid-positivity by PET compared to Aβ42 individually (Janelidze et al., 2020; Lewczuk et al., 2016). The ratio of p-tau217/Aβ42 combines both risk biomarkers, and importantly, the p-tau217/Aβ42 ratio has also outperformed p-tau217 in CSF alone, as well as Aβ42/Aβ40, in detecting Aβ PET (Wang et al., 2025).

Participants were divided into carriers who possessed at least one copy of the APOE ε4 allele (APOE4+), or non-carriers of the ε4 allele (APOE4−). Group differences in estimated marginal means of biomarker values were tested using univariate general linear models conducted in IBM SPSS, controlling for age and sex. Linear regressions were conducted using SigmaPlot, version 15.0 (San Jose, CA), as well as IBM SPSS version 30.0 (Armonk, NY) to test the associations between amyloid and tau biomarkers (and their ratios) with CX3CL1 and MMP-9 within APOE4− and APOE4+ groups, separately. An outlier was removed from the MMP-9 values as it was greater than five standard deviations above the APOE4− carrier group mean, as well as greater than three times the interquartile range above the third quartile. These parameters are to identify only extreme outliers and retain any biologically plausible outliers within the data analysis.

To determine if MMP-9 accounted for the association between CX3CL1 and AD biomarkers, and to test if this depended on APOE4 carriership, moderated mediation models were performed using IBM SPSS PROCESS version 5.0 Model 59 (Hayes, 2022). These analyses were conducted using the full sample set with APOE4 carrier status as the moderator in the relationship between paths a, b, c’ (Fig. S1). To aid interpretation, stratified mediation models were also conducted within each APOE4 group using the Lavaan package (version 0.6–19; Rosseel, 2012) in R (R Core Team, 2024) via RStudio (Boston, MA). Maximum likelihood estimations with robust standard errors (MLR) were used in the mediation models. Variables included in mediation models were standardized (z scored) to ensure comparability across models. If a biomarker was unrelated to CX3CL1 in both APOE4 groups, it was omitted from the moderated mediation models.

To evaluate the impact of APOE ε4 carrier status on inflammatory biomarker levels, a pro-inflammatory composite was derived by summing the standardized NPQs for interleukin-1β (IL-1β), IL-6 and IL-18. A composite anti-inflammatory was derived by summing standardized NPQs for IL-4, IL-10 and IL-13. To recapitulate, age and sex were held as covariates within each statistical analysis. Sex was defined as the participant’s self-reported biological sex when asked during screening. Linear regression plots show raw data correlations. Associations were deemed significant if the 95% confidence interval excluded zero.

Results

Participant characteristics

Table 1 describes the characteristics of all participants, as well as the participant characteristics within each APOE group. In total, the average age was 65.5 +/− 7 years. The average MoCA total was 25.7 +/− 2.5 and the average ADCS-Preclinical Alzheimer’s Cognitive Composite (ADCS-PACC) score was 0.02 +/− 0.62. Additionally, 53.9% of the total cohort of participants reported having at least one parent with Alzheimer’s disease. MoCA total scores were not statistically different between APOE4− and APOE4+ genotype groups (p = 0.93). Additionally, ADCS-PACC scores were also not significantly different between the two carrier groups (p = 0.16), Table 1.

Group comparison of biomarkers between APOE4− and APOE4+ carriers and biomarker associations

Estimated marginal biomarker means analysis between APOE4− and APOE4+ groups can be found in Table 2. In keeping with previous findings, Aβ42 CSF levels were higher in APOE4− carriers compared to APOE4+ carriers (p < 0.001); p-tau217 CSF levels were significantly higher in APOE4+ carriers than APOE4− carriers (p = 0.047); and p-tau217/Aβ42 was significantly higher in APOE4+ carriers (p < 0.001). Furthermore, the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio was significantly lower in APOE4+ carriers (p < 0.001) than APOE4− carriers. Compared to the APOE4− group, APOE4+ carriers had significantly lower ICAM-1 (p = 0.005) and VCAM-1 (p = 0.009) CSF levels. Remaining analytes and their ratios were not significantly different between carrier groups (Table 2).

Further analysis examining the associations between biomarkers within APOE4− (Table 3) and APOE4+ groups (Table 4) gives further insight into the baseline microenvironments of cognitively unimpaired individuals depending on their APOE ε4 status. In particular, MMP-9 is coupled with CX3CL1 in APOE4+ group but not in the APOE4− group. In contrast, CX3CL1 is decoupled from AD biomarkers in the APOE4+ group but moderately associated in the APOE4− group. While group marginal mean differences are useful to determine if biomarker levels differ between carrier groups, our hypothesis focused on the specific influence of APOE4 carrier status on the relationship between CX3CL1 and MMP-9 on AD biomarkers. As such, within Tables 3 and 4 we observe a difference in correlation between CX3CL1 and MMP-9 between carrier groups. Therefore, a moderated mediation directly testing this hypothesis was appropriate.

Moderated mediations

As shown in Table 5, APOE4 carrier status moderates path a, i.e. the association between CX3CL1 and MMP-9 (p = 0.006). For path b, there is a significant moderating effect of APOE4 carrier status for p-tau217 (p = 0.005), p-tau217/total tau (p = 0.046), Aβ42/Aβ40 (p = 0.020) and p-tau217/Aβ42 (p = 0.017). In the c’ pathway, there was only a significant effect of APOE4 carrier status in the model with p-tau217/total tau as the outcome variable (p = 0.010). APOE4 carrier status did not moderate the effect of the c’ pathway (p > 0.05) for the remaining outcomes. Among APOE4− carriers, none of the biomarkers included in Table 5 showed a significant indirect effect of MMP-9 that was moderated by APOE4 carrier status. However, among APOE4+ carriers, there was a significant indirect effect of MMP-9 in the models including p-tau217/total tau, Aβ42/Aβ40, p-tau217/Aβ42 as the outcome variables. When examining the index of moderated mediation, we see significant effects of APOE4 carrier status in the models with p-tau217/total tau, Aβ42/Aβ40 and p-tau217/Aβ42 as the outcome variables.

APOE4− CX3CL1 association with tau and amyloid

Fig. 1 summarizes the association of CX3CL1 with tau and amyloid biomarkers within the APOE4− group. CX3CL1 was significantly and positively associated with p-tau217 (p < 0.001, β = 0.423) and total tau (p < 0.001, β = 0.522). Additionally, the CX3CL1 and p-tau217/total tau had a significant and negative association (p < 0.001, β = −0.518). In contrast, the association between CX3CL1 and p-tau181/total tau was not significant (p = 0.099, β = −0.183), data not shown. Turning to β-amyloid analytes, CX3CL1 and Aβ40 exhibited a significant and positive correlation (p < 0.001, β = 0.829). Additionally, CX3CL1 and Aβ42 displayed a positive and significant relationship (p < 0.001, β = 0.721). However, CX3CL1 was not related to the ratio, Aβ42/Aβ40 (p = 0.260, β = 0.127). Lastly, there was a significant and negative correlation between CX3CL1 and p-tau217/Aβ42 (p = 0.012, β = −0.274), Fig. 1.

APOE4− MMP-9 association with tau and amyloid

Within the APOE4− group, the relationship between MMP-9 and p-tau217 was not statistically significant (p = 0.517, β = 0.069). Additionally, there was no significant relationship between MMP-9 and total tau (p = 0.130, β = 0.158). However, there was a significant relationship between MMP-9 and the ratio of tau phosphorylation at site 217 to total tau (p-tau217/total tau), (p = 0.043, β = −0.206). In contrast, MMP-9 and the ratio of tau phosphorylation at site 181 to total tau (p-tau181/total tau) was not statistically significant (p = 0.888, β = 0.016); data not shown. There was no statistically significant association between MMP-9 and Aβ40 (p = 0.901, β = −0.014), Aβ42 (p = 0.892, β = −0.015), or Aβ42/Aβ40 (p = 0.902, β = −0.014). Lastly, there was no statistically significant relationship between MMP-9 and p-tau217/Aβ42 (p = 0.543, β = 0.069), Fig. 2.

APOE4− mediations

We conducted mediation models on markers of tau and amyloid positivity to determine if there was a significant indirect effect of CX3CL1 on p-tau217, the ratio of p-tau217/total tau and the ratio of Aβ42/Aβ40 through MMP-9. Results are shown in Figs. 3.1–3.3. Within the APOE4− group, we did not find any significant indirect effects with MMP-9. However, the controlled direct effects and total effects of CX3CL1 on p-tau217 and the ratio of p-tau217/total tau were significant.

APOE4− inflammation and GFAP analysis

Within APOE4− carriers, there was no significant correlation between CX3CL1 and the pro-inflammatory composite score (p = 0.168, β = 0.147). However, TREM2 exhibited a positive relationship with the pro-inflammatory composite score (p = 0.025, β = 0.238). Alternatively, CX3CL1 correlated positively with the anti-inflammatory composite score (p = 0.002, β = 0.335), as did TREM2 (p = 0.002, β = 0.341), Fig. 4. There was also a significant relationship between CX3CL1 and GFAP (p = 0.001, β = 0.344); data were not shown.

APOE4+ CX3CL1 on tau and amyloid regressions

Within the APOE4+ carriers, there was no significant association between CX3CL1 and p-tau217 (p = 0.134, β = 0.298) or total tau, although a positive trend was observed (p = 0.057, β = 0.380). There was a significant negative relationship between CX3CL1 and p-tau217/total tau (p = 0.038, β = − 0.419). However, there was not a significant relationship between CX3CL1 and p-tau181/total tau (p = 0.203, β = −0.260); data were not shown. Furthermore, there was a significant and positive relationship between CX3CL1 and Aβ40 (p < 0.001, β = 0.718). However, no relationship was observed between CX3CL1 and Aβ42 (p = 0.142, β = 0.307) or Aβ42/Aβ40 (p = 0.540, β = −0.130). Lastly, there was no significant relationship observed between CX3CL1 and p-tau217/Aβ42 (p = 0.860, β = 0.037), Fig. 5.

APOE4+ MMP-9 on tau and amyloid regressions

Within the APOE4+ carriers, there was a significant relationship between p-tau217 and MMP-9 (p < 0.001, β = 0.683), as well as total tau (p < 0.001 and β = 0.752). Additionally, MMP-9 exhibited a negative relationship with the ratio of tau phosphorylation at site 217 to total tau (p-tau217/total tau) (p < 0.001, β = −0.699). However, there was no significant relationship between the ratio of tau phosphorylation at site 181 to total tau (p-tau181/total tau) and MMP-9 (p = 0.716, β = 0.087); data were not shown. Among β-amyloid biomarkers, MMP-9 displayed a positive correlation with Aβ40 (p = 0.007, β = 0.546) but there was no significant relationship between MMP-9 and Aβ42 (p = 0.476 and β = −0.173). MMP-9 did have a significant negative correlation with Aβ42/Aβ40 (p = 0.018, β = −0.543). Lastly, there was also a significant and positive relationship between MMP-9 and p-tau217/Aβ42 (p = 0.008, β = 0.591), Fig. 6.

APOE4+ mediations

We also conducted mediation models on markers of tau and amyloid positivity to determine if there was a significant indirect effect of CX3CL1 on p-tau217, the ratio of p-tau217/total tau and the ratio of Aβ42/Aβ40 through MMP-9 in APOE4+ carriers. Results are shown in Figs. 7.1–7.3. Within the APOE4+ group, we found significant indirect effects with MMP-9 mediating the association between CX3CL1 and p-tau217 and both p-tau217/total tau and Aβ42/Aβ40 ratios. There were no controlled direct effects of CX3CL1 on p-tau217, p-tau217/total tau, or Aβ42/Aβ40.

APOE4+ inflammation and GFAP analysis

Within the APOE4+ carriers, the pro-inflammatory composite score was correlated positively with CX3CL1 (p = 0.001, β = 0.618) as well as TREM2 (p < 0.001, β = 0.770). However, CX3CL1 did not correlate significantly with the anti-inflammatory composite score (p = 0.186, β = 0.268), or TREM2 (p = 0.168, β = 0.296), Fig. 8. Lastly, there was also a positive correlation between CX3CL1 and GFAP (p = 0.008, β = 0.505); data were not shown.

Discussion

Baseline biomarker level differences among APOE4− vs APOE4+

In the current study, we found that APOE4+ carriers exhibited differences in Aβ42, p-tau217, p-tau217/Aβ42, Aβ42/Aβ40, ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 levels compared to APOE4− carriers. Interestingly, these differences were detected among cognitively unimpaired adults in mid-to-late life, suggesting differences in cellular microenvironments in those at risk for AD. In keeping with the established association between APOE4+ carriers and plaque formation (Michaelson, 2014), we found that the levels of soluble Aβ42 and Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio were lower in those that were APOE4+ compared to APOE4− carriers. Lower levels of soluble amyloid in the CSF are suggestive of central retention and aggregation of Aβ42 within the brain. Additionally, we found that p-tau217 was modestly but significantly higher in APOE4+ carriers compared to APOE4− carriers. The CSF levels of p-tau217 have been shown to correspond to both amyloid and tau PET measures (Collij et al., 2023). We also found p-tau217/Aβ42 was higher among the APOE4+ carriers. Ratios such as p-tau217/Aβ42 have been shown to outperform either biomarker alone in predicting amyloid-positive status and by reducing variability due to confounding factors (Lehmann et al., 2024). Interestingly, we did not find significantly different relationships between carrier groups when examining p-tau217/total tau. Research has shown that the different tau isoform levels can become imbalanced in some neurodegenerative diseases (Buchholz and Zempel, 2024). As our measurement of total tau did not control for individual variations in tau isoforms, future research could adjust the p-tau217 ratio to each isoform to determine their differential roles in tauopathies. Overall, these findings are consistent with other studies examining AD progression with respect to amyloid and tau pathology. Specifically, research has suggested that amyloid pathologies develop prior to tau aggregation (Bloom, 2014). However, other studies have suggested early modification of phosphorylated tau at site 217 develops alongside the emergence of amyloid plaques. Interestingly, p-tau217 is one of the earliest abnormally modified phosphorylated sites in AD which could explain why its levels were significantly altered between carrier groups in our study (Barthelemy et al., 2021; Chong et al., 2025). We found no significant difference in cognitive performance on the MoCA and ADCS-PACC between carrier groups. Thus, APOE4+ carriers appear to have subtle fluid biomarker differences that are detectable prior to cognitive decline.

CX3CL1 and MMP-9 within APOE groups

Our findings revealed differential patterns in the associations between CX3CL1 and MMP-9 with fluid AD biomarkers by APOE ε4 carrier groups. Although we did not directly test these differences between APOE4− carriers and APOE4+ carriers at the cellular level, the distinct patterns we observed within CSF provide preliminary evidence that the cellular microenvironment may differ among APOE ε4 carrier groups prior to changes in cognitive function. In APOE4− carriers, we found that CX3CL1 positively correlated with p-tau217, total tau, Aβ42, Aβ40, and negatively correlated with p-tau217/total tau and p-tau217/Aβ42, suggesting a lower rate of phosphorylation of tau at 217 when controlling for total tau levels. MMP-9 also correlated negatively with p-tau217/total tau levels, suggesting some association with reduced phosphorylation when adjusting for total tau levels. In contrast to APOE4− carriers, CX3CL1 only correlated positively with Aβ40 levels and negatively to p-tau217/total tau in APOE4+ carriers; whereas, MMP-9 positively correlated with p-tau217, total tau, Aβ40, p-tau217/Aβ42 and negatively correlated with Aβ42/Aβ40. These data suggest a strong association between CX3CL1 and AD biomarkers in APOE4− carriers, whereas MMP-9 appears to have a stronger association with AD biomarkers in APOE4+ carriers.

Mediation within APOE groups

To examine the roles of MMP-9 and CX3CL1 on AD biomarkers in APOE4− and APOE4+ carriers, we performed a moderated mediation analysis to test if a) CX3CL1 had an indirect effect on AD biomarkers via MMP-9 and b) the indirect effect depended on the APOE4 carrier status. Furthermore, to examine if these pathways are present in APOE4− and/or APOE4+ carriers, we also performed simple mediation analyses within both carrier groups.

Within the APOE4− group, MMP-9 was not associated in the relationship between CX3CL1 on p-tau217 and p-tau217/total tau levels. There was only a direct and total effect of CX3CL1 on p-tau217, as well as the ratio of p-tau217/total tau. In contrast, MMP-9 was significantly associated within the pathway of CX3CL1 on p-tau217, p-tau217/total tau and Aβ42/Aβ40 levels, with no direct effect of CX3CL1 within the APOE4+ group. These data further suggest that APOE4+ individuals could have a cellular environment that is different than APOE4− individuals. For example, as CX3CL1 levels rise, MMP-9 levels increase, and the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio decreases. The lower Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio suggests that MMP-9 could be associated with β-amyloid fragment retention within the brain in APOE4+ carriers. In other words, the involvement of MMP-9 in this signaling pathway suggests an inability to maintain homeostatic clearance mechanisms over time as amyloid-beta pathologies rise in the microenvironment. Previous research suggests a “cellular phase” of AD progression. It is thought that accumulation of β-amyloid and tau pathologies happen over a long period of time and physiological mechanisms adapt to clear these proteins early in the disease. Moreover, breakage of cellular clearance mechanisms involving astrocytes and microglia may underlie AD progression (De Strooper and Karran, 2016). Our data suggest that in APOE4+ carriers, CX3CL1 and MMP-9 may work together to decrease levels of tau phosphorylation at the expense of amyloid clearance, resulting in increased central retention of Aβ42. This was further supported with the moderated mediation analysis in which it’s shown that p-tau217/total tau and p-tau217/Aβ42 indirect pathways are moderated by APOE4 carrier status, Table 5.

As a within group carrier effect in the stratified mediation models does not directly test the moderating role of APOE4 on these mechanisms, we rely on the moderated mediation results to test if there was a significant index of APOE4 carrier status moderation among these pathways. With that said, although the within group mediation for p-tau217 was significant in APOE4+ carriers, the moderated mediation model was not significant. Taken together, a significant within group mediation only provides evidence for an indirect association between CX3CL1, MMP-9 and p-tau217. However, the insignificant index of moderated mediation in Table 5 would suggest that although this pathway may be present in APOE4+ carriers, the indirect effect is not moderated by ε4 carrier status. However, the indirect effect of CX3CL1 on p-tau217/total tau, Aβ42/Aβ40, and p-tau217/Aβ42 via MMP-9 differed according to APOE4 carrier status, as shown with their significant indices of moderated mediation.

Role of CX3CL1 in AD pathophysiology

Lower Aβ42 levels in the CSF of APOE4+ individuals relative to APOE4− carriers could be a consequence of microglia that are dynamically changing their activation phenotype which is known to be regulated by CX3CL1. Lower binding of CX3CL1 to its receptor on glia may be associated with the accumulation of β-amyloid plaques (Iemmolo et al., 2023). This lack of binding and dysregulation of glial signaling elicits a hyperactive phenotype in microglia that upregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines and tau phosphorylation (Kheiri et al., 2018). When β-amyloid plaques are present in the microenvironment, there is a decrease in the CX3CL1-CX3CR1 signaling pathway leading to elevated β-amyloid phagocytosis and hyperphosphorylation of tau (Bolós et al., 2017; Iemmolo et al., 2023). Research has suggested a similar observation with the CX3CL1-CX3CR1 signaling pathway being connected to the phagocytosis of extracellular tau by microglia (Bolós et al., 2017). Furthermore, there is a stepwise increase in the effect of CX3CL1 with each additional ε4 allele (Walker et al., 2024). As MMP-9 is involved in the shedding of mCX3CL1, if this cleaving becomes excessive then this could reduce homeostatic control and influence microglia to become dysregulated and elicit a pro-inflammatory environment impacting AD biomarkers.

We found that CX3CL1 was correlated with a reduction in tau phosphorylation at site 217 in both APOE4− and APOE4+ carrier groups; however, this association was correlated with the effects of MMP-9 only in APOE4+ carriers. CX3CL1 typically exists as a transmembrane form, mCX3CL1, and a soluble form, sCX3CL1 (Eugenín et al., 2023; Iemmolo et al., 2023). The transmembrane form is bound to the membrane and can act as a ligand that is expressed by neurons and can directly interact with its receptor CX3CR1 (Imai et al., 1997) on microglia to regulate its activity (Subbarayan et al., 2022). The mCX3CL1 can also be cleaved by α-, β-, and γ-secretases to release sCX3CL1 (Fan et al., 2019), which has functional downstream roles in neuroinflammatory chemokine signaling (Hundhausen et al., 2003; Pawelec et al., 2020). The cleavage of CX3CL1 by BACE1 and gamma-secretase releases the intracellular domain of CX3CL1 (CX3CL1-ICD), which then can translocate to the nucleus and impact gene expression and reduce amyloid deposition (Fan et al., 2019). It remains to be determined how the CX3CL1-CX3CR1 pathway results in reduced tau phosphorylation. In a transgenic mouse model that expressed only the soluble form of CX3CL1 that lacked the mucin-stalk, they found that overexpression of only the CX3CL1 chemokine domain is not protective against tauopathies (Bemiller et al., 2018). As such, due to the complexity of CX3CL1 cleavage and function, multiple pathways could be involved in regulating tau phosphorylation.

Role of MMP-9 in AD pathophysiology

Recent research suggests that APOE isoforms display differential efficacy in inhibiting the activity of MMP-9, with ε4 being the most ineffective at inhibiting its proteolytic activation (Ringland et al., 2020). While studies suggest that tau is a substrate for both MMP-3 and MMP-9, only cleavage by MMP-9 results in increased tau oligomer formation (Nübling et al., 2012). MMP-9 binds to the tau protein in the region containing Arg95-Lys 638 residues. It has been suggested that the six saline bonds formed between residues Asp348, Arg349, Lys369, Lys395, and Glu431 towards the C terminal on the tau protein and Arg366, Arg370, Asp368, Asp185, Glu544, Arg293 residues of MMP-9 mediate the tau-MMP-9 complex. These six saline bonds are stabilized by 50 hydrogen bonds suggesting strong complementary binding of these proteins (Hernandes-Alejandro et al., 2020). This type of interaction has been associated with the early development of intracellular neurofibrillary tangles, although overt degeneration associated with these pathologies has not been observed (Hernandes-Alejandro et al., 2020). Notably, MMP-9 levels in the brain are higher in moderate and late-stage AD and colocalize with tau aggregates. These findings support the hypothesis that MMP-9 not only interacts directly with tau but also increases expression concomitant with disease progression. Although MMP-9 is associated with both increased tau and p-tau217 levels, increased MMP-9 is associated with a reduced p-tau217/total tau ratio. It is possible that MMP-9 interferes with the phosphorylation of tau at site 217 or is cleaving tau around this region rendering it inaccessible to tau antibody detection. Importantly, MMP-9 did not alter the ratio of p-tau181/total tau, suggesting a specific effect of MMP-9 at site 217. Cleavage of tau by MMP-9 has been linked to enhanced tau potentially through the generation of fragments encompassing the repeat domain spanning residues 203–330 (Nübling et al., 2012; Von Bergen et al., 2000). Therefore, the increase in MMP-9 could eventually result in cleavage of tau producing a fragment spanning 203–330 that is retained within the brain. Additionally, research has shown an increase in p-tau217/total tau around 21 years prior to symptom onset and coincides with the initiation of amyloid plaques as seen within our dataset. This is then followed by an increase in p-tau181/total tau that occurs as neuronal dysfunction begins, around 19 years prior to symptom onset. Interestingly, these ratios both decrease as neurodegeneration accelerates (the Dominantly Inherited Alzheimer Network et al., 2020). As we show increases in p-tau217 accumulation between carrier groups and amyloid plaque accumulation, but no distinct increase in p-tau181, this would suggest these individuals are within the soluble tauopathy/hyperphosphorylation stage of AD progression.

While many of the interactions of MMP-9 and tau suggest a pathological relationship, some research reports that MMP-9 can play a neuroprotective role as it pertains to amyloid precursor protein (APP) processing. In the non-amyloidogenic pathway, APP is proteolytically cleaved by α-secretases leading to the release of its soluble version, sAPPα (Muralidharan et al., 2018). It has also been shown that overexpression of MMP-9 in transgenic AD mouse models promotes the non-amyloidogenic pathway signified by an increase in production of sAPPα and a decrease of Aβ oligomers (Fragkouli et al., 2014). However, since Aβ42 and Aβ40 are produced through an amyloidogenic pathway, their production is the consequence of APP cleavage by beta- and gamma-secretases (Hampel et al., 2021). MMP-9 cleaves at multiple sites in the Aβ peptide sequence towards the C-terminus leading to more soluble fragments of the protein. MMP-9 can degrade Aβ42 and Aβ40 at different clearance efficiencies, with MMP-9 being better suited to degrade Aβ40. It is suggested that this degradation of β-amyloid fragments is representative of a clearance mechanism, as it produces more soluble fragments after cleavage thereby reducing fibrillogenesis (Hernandez-Guillamon et al., 2015). We find that in APOE4+ carriers, MMP-9 was correlated with Aβ40 levels. However, this relationship was not seen in APOE4− carriers. We also find that Aβ42 was lower in the CSF of APOE4+ carriers, suggesting retention of amyloid in the brain. These data suggest that MMP-9 may be involved in a non-amyloidogenic pathway in APOE4− carriers. However, this could be switched to a more amyloidogenic pathway in APOE4+ carriers. Future research should be focused on how MMP-9 cleaves Aβ40 versus Aβ42 in different microenvironments due to APOE variants.

Role of inflammation in AD pathophysiology

As expected, we also found differences in the inflammatory profiles between these carrier groups. Specifically, in APOE4− individuals, we found that CX3CL1 only exhibited a significantly positive relationship with the anti-inflammatory composite score. It was not significantly correlated with the pro-inflammatory composite score. A highly correlated marker for microglia implicated in AD, TREM2, also exhibited a positive relationship with the anti-inflammatory composite score, however, unlike CX3CL1, TREM2 also exhibited a positive relationship with the pro-inflammatory composite score. It could be that the presence of pathologies during typical brain aging causes TREM2 to elicit a pro-inflammatory phenotype, but due to the ability of APOE4− carriers to efficiently clear tau and amyloid, they transition to an anti-inflammatory state to maintain the homeostasis of the microenvironment. Furthermore, while TREM2 is generally associated with anti-inflammatory states, soluble TREM2 can induce pro-inflammatory signaling and promote microglia survival (Zhong et al., 2017). As we measured soluble TREM2 within the current study, it is not surprising it correlated with both scores. Notably, among APOE4+ carriers, there was no significant association between anti-inflammatory scores and TREM2. A recent study finds that the presence of APOE4 can create a pro-inflammatory microglia state (Murphy et al., 2025). This could further suggest that the presence of APOE4 may shift microglia towards a more pro-inflammatory state resulting in lowered anti-inflammatory responses. Furthermore, in APOE4+ carriers, CX3CL1 had a positive relationship with the pro-inflammatory composite score but no relationship with the anti-inflammatory composite score. Additionally, TREM2 was also positively correlated with the pro-inflammatory composite score in APOE4+ carriers but exhibited no relationship with the anti-inflammatory composite score, suggesting a polarized pro-inflammatory phenotype. This could also suggest a hyperactive pro-inflammatory microglial environment within APOE4+ carriers that focuses on the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines in response to the accumulation of amyloid pathologies, highlighting their ineffective repair mechanisms compared to those without this genetic risk factor. Research has suggested that the APOE ε4 microenvironment experiences increased activation of the cyclophilin A–nuclear factor-κB-MMP-9 (CypA-NF-kB-MMP-9) pathway which has been known to be induced by elevated oxidative stress (Bell et al., 2012). It has also been shown that ICAM-1 plays a neuroprotective role by inhibiting NF-kB (Guha et al., 2022).

Our data showed that ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 levels are increased in the APOE4− carrier group compared to APOE4+ carriers. It is possible that the presence of elevated ICAM-1 is inhibiting the CypA-NF-kB-MMP-9 pathway, which could be a reason why MMP-9 was not associated with an indirect effect within the APOE4− group (Fang et al., 2023). Interestingly, prior studies have shown that higher levels of ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 are associated with Aβ and tau pathology, as well as disease progression (Ewers et al., 2010; Janelidze et al., 2018; Popp et al., 2017). However, it is less clear how APOE genotypes influence ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 levels in non-cognitively impaired individuals. One study reported that CSF VCAM-1 levels are lower in non-cognitively impaired APOE4+ carriers compared to non-carriers, with this pattern disappearing among symptomatic individuals (Nielsen et al., 2007). Our current data suggest that APOE4+ carriers have lower CSF levels of ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 than non-carriers under non-cognitively impaired conditions. This could suggest a dramatic shift in ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 levels with disease onset. Future studies should investigate levels prior to and during disease progression to further elucidate these findings. As β-amyloid accumulates in the microenvironment, clearance mechanisms such as CX3CL1-CX3CR1 may lose homeostatic regulation and involve proteases such as MMP-9. Furthermore, it has been shown that astrocyte-derived ApoE3 protein is able to maintain the blood-brain barrier through the inhibition of CypA and therefore downregulates the activation of MMP-9 from its latent form (Bell et al., 2012). As these biomarkers are significantly different between carrier groups along with traditional tau and amyloid pathologies, we suggest ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 as additional inflammatory biomarkers that are altered within cognitively unimpaired individuals who are at higher genetic risk for AD. Due to these key molecular differences in signaling pathways between the APOE isoforms, MMP-9’s activity within the CypA-NF-kB-MMP-9 pathway may be differentially upregulated among the carrier groups, as seen in our data.

Limitations

The sample size used for this study is small and results should be considered preliminary. However, our findings corroborate prior studies that identify MMP-9 as a potential AD biomarker (Abe et al., 2020; Cerebrospinal fluid in the dementias, 2018; Mak et al., 2025). Notably, the sample consisted of primarily non-Hispanic, white men, yet women and some minoritized groups are more at risk of developing AD (Rajan et al., 2021). The role of APOE ε4 among these groups is unclear (Walters et al., 2023). Future studies looking to replicate these findings should focus on investigating if these mechanisms remain present among populations who carry additional risk for AD. Replicating these findings could prove to be especially beneficial in early diagnosis or treatment of this disease. Furthermore, as the individuals included in this cohort were all VA-eligible Veterans, these findings should be replicated among the general public to reduce the homogeneity seen in our cohort. It would have also been advantageous if longitudinal samples were included as the current cross-sectional study design limits our ability to confirm the cause-and-effect relationship of these genotypic fluid biomarker correlations. Due to the number of statistical tests performed, the chance of type 1 errors cannot be excluded. To note, we did analyze Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons of estimated marginal means within GLMs.

Conclusion

In summary, we found distinct differences within the CX3CL1 pathway between APOE4− and APOE4+ asymptomatic carriers. There is a key difference in the involvement of MMP-9 in this mechanism that ultimately impacts tau and amyloid levels within the microenvironment. MMP-9 was associated with the relationship between CX3CL1 and tau and amyloid biomarker pathways among APOE4+ carriers and contributed to the inhibition of tau phosphorylation at site 217 but maybe at the cost of amyloid pathogenesis. This relationship was absent among those who did not have the ε4 variant and therefore suggests that targeting MMP-9 in those with the ε4 allele could serve as a potential therapeutic intervention in individuals prior to the onset of AD symptoms. Moreover, our findings suggest that CX3CL1 influences AD-type biomarker levels, but the pathway by which it does so appears different between APOE4− and APOE4+ carriers. We also found that the microenvironment is molecularly different between those with the ε4 allele as CSF levels of Aβ42, p-tau217, Aβ42/Aβ40 and p-tau217/Aβ42, ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 were all significantly different between these carrier groups. Lastly, their inflammatory profiles further elucidate their different baseline microenvironments prior to symptom onset of AD, with APOE4+ carriers having a polarized pro-inflammatory profile. These characteristics lead to mechanistic differences between APOE ε4 and the other allelic variants, as seen in the CX3CL1 signaling pathway in our data. Lastly, we find that MMP-9 may function differently among APOE carrier types. This genotype-dependent impact on the CX3CL1 signaling pathway identifies MMP-9 as a potential preclinical target for further investigation.

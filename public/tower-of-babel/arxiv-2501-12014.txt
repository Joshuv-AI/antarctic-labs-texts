On the tensor product of completely distributive quantale-enriched categories
Adriana Balan

Abstract
Tensor products are ubiquitous in algebra, topology, logic and category theory. The present paper explores the monoidal structure of the category 𝒱 ​ - ​ 𝐒𝐮𝐩 \mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup} of separated cocomplete enriched categories over a commutative quantale 𝒱 \mathcal{V} , the many-valued analogue of complete sup-lattices. We recover the known result that 𝒱 ​ - ​ 𝐒𝐮𝐩 \mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup} is ∗ * -autonomous and we show that the nuclear/dualizable objects in 𝒱 ​ - ​ 𝐒𝐮𝐩 \mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup} are precisely the completely distributive cocomplete 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -categories.



1
Introduction

Quantitative reasoning is often encountered in Computer Science, in various contexts and guises:
in the theory of dynamical systems featuring commensurable data, such as probabilistic transition systems
[
vBW05
]
,
or via behavioural distances substituting program equivalences
[
MPP16
]
, in Formal Concept Analysis
[
Běl04
]
, and the examples could go further on.
Their common feature is the semantic interpretation in domains having a metric-like structure – that is, quantales, which generalise both truth values and distances, resulting in a rich categorical structure: cocomplete quantale enriched categories
[
Stu05
]
.
In this paper we shall bring forward their symmetric tensor product
[
JT84
]
, in a presentation featuring elements of enriched category theory, Kock-Zöberlein monads, Galois connections and Shmuely’s G-ideals. We shall also relate dualizability and complete distributivity in the category of separated cocomplete quantale-enriched categories.

The tensor product of complete sup-lattices has a long history (see for example
[
BN76
,
Mow68
,
Shm74
]
and references therein). Essentially, the theory developed along two main lines of research: first, the description of the tensor product as Galois maps, and second, as certain downsets separately preserving suprema (called G-ideals in
[
Shm74
]
). Each description allowed for various generalisations to arbitrary posets, classes of lattices, closure spaces or formal contexts.

Quantales were introduced in the ’80s as a logical-theoretic framework for studying certain spaces arising from quantum mechanics
[
Mul86
]
.
As mentioned above, these can be perceived both as lattices of “truth-values” and as “distances”, equipped with an extra operation expressing conjunction (logical interpretation) or addition of distances (metric interpretation).

Lawvere observed that quantales provided a unified setting for both ordered sets and metric spaces as enriched categories
[
Law02
]
. His insight significantly enhanced the quantitative theory of domains
[
Stu07
,
Wag94
]
mentioned in the beginning with concepts and ideas from category theory. Among the most useful and pleasing properties of (quantale-)enriched categories are undoubtedly (co)completeness with respect to certain classes of (co)limits and commutation of such limits and colimits (with prominent examples featuring the ordered case, like frames, continuous lattices or completely distributive lattices).
For
\mathcal{V}
a commutative quantale, the category
\mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup}
of separated cocomplete quantale-enriched categories and cocontinuous functors
[
JT84
,
PT89
,
Stu05
]
generalises complete sup-lattices to the many-valued realm.
That
\mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup}
is a
*
-autonomous category with tensor product
{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
classifying bimorphisms (
\mathcal{V}
-functors
{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}\to{\mathscr{C}}
cocontinuous in each variable separately) is an old result, going back to
[
JT84
]
, which has recently seen a revival of interest
[
EGHK18
,
Tho24
]
.
In the former reference, the classical construction of the tensor product of modules over a commutative ring is applied
mutatis mutandis
to modules over a monoid in the category of complete sup-lattices, that is, over a quantale.
In the latter two references, the emphasis is on the underlying category of complete sup-lattices, respectively on the equational presentation of cocomplete enriched categories as endowed with an action of the quantale.

The present paper contributes to the above mentioned results with yet another description of the tensor product, relying on quantale-enriched categories techniques: KZ-monads, enriched limits and colimits, left Kan extensions are the main tools which build the path for our results.
Although certainly more abstract than the previous cited papers, the categorical approach has the advantage that it can suit also other purposes, like generalization to quantaloid-enriched categories, dropping thus the commutativity assumption on the quantale.

Section
4
of the paper was influenced by the results of
[
RW94
]
.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
2
we recall the elements of quantale-enriched category theory, distributors, colimits and cocompleteness as used in this paper.
The next section is devoted to the free cocompletion monad, where we recollect its main features to be used subsequently.
Sections
4

5
contain the main results: the construction and properties of the tensor product of separated cocomplete quantale-enriched categories, that the tensor product restricts to the full subcategory of completely distributive cocomplete quantale-enriched categories, and that the objects of the latter category are precisely the nuclear/dualizable cocomplete quantale-enriched categories.

Preliminaires

Here we recall the main elements of quantale-enriched category theory that we shall use in the sequel.
For more details, we point to

,

Quantales.

A
(commutative) quantale
is a (commutative) monoid
(\mathcal{V},\otimes,e)
in the category
\mathbf{Sup}
of complete sup-lattices and sup-preserving morphisms.
In particular,
\mathcal{V}
carries a complete partial order
\leq
.
Because the multiplication (
tensor product
) of the quantale preserves suprema in each variable, every
v\otimes-:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
has a right adjoint
[v,-]:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
(
internal hom
).
Adjointness means that

u\otimes v\leq w\quad\iff\quad u\leq[v,w]

holds for each
u,v,w\in\mathcal{V}
.
Hence a commutative quantale is a posetal symmetric monoidal closed category, complete and cocomplete.

We list below several examples of quantales:

Example 2.1
.

1.

The simplest example of a (commutative) quantale is the two-element chain
\mathcal{V}=(\mathbbm{2}=\{0,1\},\land,1)
, with meet as multiplication.

2.

The three-element chain
\mathbb{3}=\{0<a<1\}
supports two quantale structures for which the tensor is idempotent
[
CCMP91
]
:

•

Taking
\otimes
to be the meet, one obtains the usual Heyting algebra
\mathcal{V}=(\mathbb{3},\land,1)
.

•

The second idempotent multiplication
\otimes
on
\mathbb{3}
has unit
a
.
The resulting quantale
(\mathbb{3},\otimes,a)
is a
Sugihara monoid

[
OR07
]
.

Besides the two idempotent structures mentioned above, there exists only one more quantale structure on
\mathbb{3}
(non-idempotent and integral), namely the
Łukasiewicz
tensor product
v\otimes w=\max(0,v+w-1)
.

3.

The (extended) positive real numbers
([0,\infty],\geq)
form a commutative quantale when endowed with addition and zero. The internal hom is
[u,v]=\max(v-u,0)
.

4.

The unit interval
([0,1],\leq)
, with tensor product
u\otimes v=\min(u,v)
and internal hom
[u,v]=1\textsf{ if }u\leq v\textsf{ else }v
is another example of a commutative quantale.

5.

The left continuous distribution functions
\Delta=\{f:[0,\infty]\to[0,1]\mid f(a)=\bigvee_{b<a}f(b)\}
form a commutative quantale with pointwise order and suprema (but not infima, which are obtained as
\left(\bigwedge_{i}f_{i}\right)(x)={\mathsf{sup}_{y<x}{\mathsf{inf}_{i}f_{i}(y)}}
)
[
HST14
]
.
The tensor product is given by convolution, with unit the delta distribution at
0
.
This quantale is in fact the
coproduct
of
[0,\infty]
and
[0,1]
in the category of quantales
[
GGHK17
]
.

6.

The powerset
\mathcal{P}(M)
of a a (commutative) monoid
M
, with concatenation as
\otimes
and empty string as unit, is a (commutative) quantale; in particular, the powerset
\mathcal{P}(A^{*})
of the list monoid
A^{*}
over a set
A
is the free quantale over
A
.

In the sequel, all quantales will be assumed commutative.

Quantale enriched categories.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\otimes,e,[-,-])
be a commutative quantale.
A
\mathcal{V}
-category

{\mathscr{X}}
consists of a set, still denoted
{\mathscr{X}}
, together with a map
1
1

1

In the sequel we shall refer to this map as the
\mathcal{V}
-hom,
\mathcal{V}
-metric or
\mathcal{V}
-distance.
By slightly abuse, both the underlying set (of objects) and the
\mathcal{V}
-hom will be denoted by the same letter as the
\mathcal{V}
-category in question.

{\mathscr{X}}:{\mathscr{X}}\times{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathcal{V}

satisfying

e\leq{\mathscr{X}}(x,x)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\otimes{\mathscr{X}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\leq{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime\prime})

for all
x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathscr{X}}
.
Each
\mathcal{V}
-category
{\mathscr{X}}
carries an underlying order (reflexive and transitive relation) given by
x\leq x^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow e\leq{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})
.
A
\mathcal{V}
-category will be called
separated
(or
skeletal
) if its underlying order is in fact a partial order.
The
opposite
of a
\mathcal{V}
-category
{\mathscr{X}}
, denoted
{\mathscr{X}}^{\mathsf{op}}
, has the same objects as
{\mathscr{X}}
, and
\mathcal{V}
-homs
{\mathscr{X}}^{\mathsf{op}}(x,x^{\prime})={\mathscr{X}}(x^{\prime},x)
.
A
\mathcal{V}
-functor

f:{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{Y}}
is a map between the underlying sets satisfying
{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\leq{\mathscr{Y}}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))
for every objects
x,x^{\prime}
of
{\mathscr{X}}
. In particular, a
\mathcal{V}
-functor is monotone with respect to the underlying orders.
Finally, a
\mathcal{V}
-natural transformation

f\to g:{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{Y}}
only accounts for pointwise inequality
e\leq{\mathscr{Y}}(f(x),g(x))
,
\forall\ x\in{\mathscr{X}}
.

Notice that we use the same symbol
\leq
for the order relation in both
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}
-categories, and rely on the context to tell them apart.
We shall proceed similarly for joins and meets with respect to the underlying order of an
\mathcal{V}
-category, whenever these exist.

Example 2.2
.

1.

The quantale
\mathcal{V}
becomes itself a
\mathcal{V}
-category with
\mathcal{V}(v,w)=[v,w]
.
The induced order is in fact the underlying order of the quantale.
Being antisymmetric,
\mathcal{V}
is separated as a
\mathcal{V}
-category.

2.

Each set
A
can be perceived as a (separated)
\mathcal{V}
-category
\mathsf{d}A
when equipped with
\mathsf{d}A(a,b)=e\textsf{ if }a=b\textsf{ else }\bot
.
Such
\mathcal{V}
-categories are called
discrete
.

3.

Ordered sets
(A,\leq)
are enriched categories over the two-element quantale
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{2}

[
Law02
]
.
The monotone maps are precisely the
\mathcal{V}
-functors.

4.

If
\mathcal{V}
is the extended real half line
([0,\infty]^{\mathsf{op}},+,0)
as in Example
2.1
.
3
, a small
\mathcal{V}
-category
{\mathscr{X}}
is a generalised metric space
[
Law02
]
: a set
{\mathscr{X}}
endowed with a mapping
{\mathscr{X}}:{\mathscr{X}}\times{\mathscr{X}}\to[0,\infty]
satisfying

0\geq{\mathscr{X}}(x,x),\quad{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})+{\mathscr{X}}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\geq{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime\prime})\ \ .

Observe that
{\mathscr{X}}:{\mathscr{X}}\times{\mathscr{X}}\to[0,\infty]
is only a pseudo-metric, in the sense that two distinct points may have distance
0
, distances are not necessarily symmetric and are allowed to be infinite.
An important consequence of the latter property is that the category of generalised metric spaces has colimits (whereas metric spaces do not even have coproducts).
A
\mathcal{V}
-functor is a non-expanding map.

Denote by
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
the 2-category of (small)
\mathcal{V}
-enriched categories,
\mathcal{V}
-functors and
\mathcal{V}
-natural transformations, and by
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
the full 2-subcategory consisting of separated
\mathcal{V}
-categories.
In fact, both
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
and
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
are locally ordered
2
-categories, a result which will considerably simplify our reasoning.
Recall that the forgetful functor
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}\to\mathbf{Set}
is topological, and that the embedding
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{sep}}\to\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
is strongly epi-reflective
[
HST14
]
.
Consequently, both
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
and
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
are complete and cocomplete as ordinary categories.

\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
is also symmetric monoidal closed.
The tensor product
{\mathscr{X}}\otimes{\mathscr{Y}}
of two
\mathcal{V}
-categories
{\mathscr{X}}
and
{\mathscr{Y}}
has as objects pairs
(x,y)
of objects
x
of
{\mathscr{X}}
and
y
of
{\mathscr{Y}}
, and
\mathcal{V}
-homs

({\mathscr{X}}\otimes{\mathscr{Y}})((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))={\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\otimes{\mathscr{Y}}(y,y^{\prime})

Notice that we use the same symbol for the tensor product of
\mathcal{V}
-categories and for the underlying multiplication of the quantale, relying on the context to distinguish them.

Remark 2.3
.

If the quantale
\mathcal{V}
is integral (that is, the unit
e
coincides with the top element of the quantale), then the tensor product of two separated quantale-enriched categories is again separated.
However, this property is not guaranteed in general. To see an example, consider the
4
-element Boolean algebra
\mathcal{V}=\{\bot,e,a,\top\}
, with commutative quantale structure given by
a\otimes a=e,a\otimes\top=\top
.
The elements
\top,e,\bot
are idempotent and the unit is
e
.
This quantale is denoted
R^{4,2}_{2,2}
in
[
GJ
]
.
Taking
{\mathscr{X}}={\mathscr{Y}}=\mathcal{V}
, one has
{\mathscr{X}}\otimes{\mathscr{Y}}((a,e),(e,a))=[a,e]\otimes[e,a]=a\otimes a=e={\mathscr{X}}\otimes{\mathscr{Y}}((e,a),(a,e))
, but
(e,a)\neq(a,e)
.

The unit for the tensor product of
\mathcal{V}
-categories is the
\mathcal{V}
-category
\mathbbm{1}
with one object, denoted
0
, and
\mathcal{V}
-hom
\mathbbm{1}(0,0)=e
.
The internal
\mathcal{V}
-category-hom
[{\mathscr{X}},{\mathscr{Y}}]
has as objects
\mathcal{V}
-functors
f:{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{Y}}
, with
\mathcal{V}
-valued hom
[{\mathscr{X}},{\mathscr{Y}}](f,f^{\prime})=\bigwedge_{x}{\mathscr{Y}}(fx,f^{\prime}x)
.

Quantale-enriched distributors.

This is only a brief overview, for more details the reader is invited to consult
[
Stu05
,
Stu06
]
.

A
\mathcal{V}
-distributor
(or profunctor, or (bi)module)
\varphi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Y}}
between
\mathcal{V}
-categories is a
\mathcal{V}
-functor
\varphi:{\mathscr{Y}}^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathcal{V}
.
Explicitly,

{\mathscr{Y}}(y^{\prime},y)\otimes\varphi(y,x)\otimes{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\leq\varphi(y^{\prime},x^{\prime})

holds for all
x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{X}}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{Y}}
.
Composition of
\mathcal{V}
-distributors
\varphi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Y}}
,
\psi:{\mathscr{Y}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Z}}
is given by “matrix multiplication”

(\psi\otimes\varphi)(z,x)=\bigvee_{y\in{\mathscr{Y}}}\psi(z,y)\otimes\varphi(y,x)

This composition is strictly associative, due to enrichment in a quantale (whose underlying order is antisymmetric). The identity distributor on a
\mathcal{V}
-category
{\mathscr{X}}
is the
\mathcal{V}
-hom
{\mathscr{X}}(-,-)
; in particular, the relations

\varphi\otimes{\mathscr{X}}=\varphi\ \mbox{ and }\ {\mathscr{Y}}\otimes\varphi=\varphi

hold for any
\mathcal{V}
-distributor
\varphi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Y}}
.
When
\varphi
is a contravariant, respectively covariant presheaf (see below), these relations are referred as the
co-Yoneda lemma
.
Distributor composition admits both right extensions and right liftings

\begin{array}[]{c}\psi\otimes\varphi\leq\xi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\psi\leq\xi\swarrow\varphi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\leq\psi\searrow\xi\end{array}

where

(\xi\swarrow\varphi)({{z}},{{y}})=\bigwedge_{{x\in{\mathscr{X}}}}[\varphi({{y}},{{x}}),\xi({{z}},{{x}})]\ \mbox{ and }\ (\psi\searrow\xi)({{y}},{{x}})=\bigwedge_{{z\in{\mathscr{Z}}}}[\psi({{z}},{{y}}),\xi({{z}},{{x}})]

for
\varphi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Y}}
,
\psi:{\mathscr{Y}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Z}}
,
\xi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Z}}
.

Consequently,
\mathcal{V}
-categories,
\mathcal{V}
-distributors and
\mathcal{V}
-natural transformations (that is, pointwise inequalities) between them form a biclosed bicategory
\mathcal{V}\hskip-0.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.9pt{\mathbf{Dist}}
(in fact a locally ordered
2
-category, given the enrichment in a quantale).
A pair of distributors
\varphi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{Y}}
and
\psi:{\mathscr{Y}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{X}}
are
adjoints
, written
\varphi\dashv\psi
, if
{\mathscr{X}}\leq\psi\otimes\varphi
and
\varphi\otimes\psi\leq{\mathscr{Y}}
hold.
In particular, each
\mathcal{V}
-functor
f:{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{Y}}
induces a pair of adjoint distributors
f_{*}\dashv f^{*}
between
{\mathscr{X}}
and
{\mathscr{Y}}
, where
f_{*}(y,x)={\mathscr{Y}}(y,fx)
and
f^{*}(x,y)={\mathscr{Y}}(fx,y)
.
In particular, observe that a
\mathcal{V}
-functor
f:{\mathscr{X}}\to{\mathscr{Y}}
is left adjoint in
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
, with
g:{\mathscr{Y}}\to{\mathscr{X}}
as its right adjoint, if and only if
f^{*}=g_{*}
.

A distributor
\varphi:\mathbbm{1}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{X}}
(equivalently, a
\mathcal{V}
-functor
\varphi:{\mathscr{X}}^{\mathsf{op}}\to\mathcal{V}
) is usually called
a contravariant presheaf
in category theory. It can be perceived as an
\mathcal{V}
-valued downset:
the relation
{\mathscr{X}}^{\mathsf{op}}(x^{\prime},x)\leq[\varphi(x^{\prime}),\varphi(x)]
, equivalent to
{\mathscr{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\otimes\varphi(x^{\prime})\leq\varphi(x)
, reads in case
\mathcal{V}=\mathbbm{2}
as

\left(x\leq x^{\prime}\mbox{ and }x^{\prime}\in\varphi\right)\mbox{ implies }x\in\varphi

that is,
\varphi
is a downset in the usual sense.
Here we implicitly identified a downset with its associated characteristic function.
To preserve this intuition, we shall denote by
\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{X}}
the
\mathcal{V}
-category of contravariant presheaves
\mathcal{V}\hskip-0.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.9pt{\mathbf{Dist}}(\mathbbm{1},{\mathscr{X}})=[{\mathscr{X}}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathcal{V}]
.
One of its prominent features that we recall is that it classifies distributors into
{\mathscr{X}}

[
Stu05
]
: there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{V}
-categories

\mathcal{V}\hskip-0.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.9pt{\mathbf{Dist}}({\mathscr{Y}},{\mathscr{X}})\cong\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}({\mathscr{Y}},\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{X}})

(1)

functorial in the
\mathcal{V}
-categories
{\mathscr{X}},{\mathscr{Y}}
, mapping a
\mathcal{V}
-distributor
\varphi:{\mathscr{Y}}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{X}}
to the
\mathcal{V}
-functor
f_{\varphi}(y)=\varphi(-,y)
, respectively a
\mathcal{V}
-functor
f:{\mathscr{Y}}\to\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{X}}
to the
\mathcal{V}
-distributor
\varphi_{f}(x,y)=f(y)(x)
.

Dually, a
covariant presheaf
is a
\mathcal{V}
-distributor
\psi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow\mathbbm{1}
(that is,
\psi:{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathcal{V}
), the
\mathcal{V}
-valued analogue of an upper set, and
\mathbbm{U}{\mathscr{X}}=[{\mathscr{X}},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathsf{op}}
is the
\mathcal{V}
-category of covariant presheaves (notice the “
\mathsf{op}
”!).

We end this paragraph on distributors by recalling the
Yoneda embedding
: the fully faithful
\mathcal{V}
-functor
\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{X}}:{\mathscr{X}}\to[{\mathscr{X}}^{\mathsf{op}},\mathcal{V}]=\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{X}}
mapping an object
x
of
{\mathscr{X}}
to the representable contravariant presheaf
{\mathscr{X}}(-,x)
, corresponding, under the equivalence in (
1
), to the identity distributor on
{\mathscr{X}}
.
For
\mathcal{V}=\mathbbm{2}
, this is the familiar principal downset embedding of an ordered set.

For every contravariant presheaf
\varphi:\mathbbm{1}\nrightarrow{\mathscr{X}}
, the following relation hold (
Yoneda lemma
):

\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{X}}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{X}}(-),\varphi)=\varphi

For completeness, we also mention the covariant version of the Yoneda embedding, namely
\mathsf{y}^{\prime}_{\mathscr{X}}:{\mathscr{X}}\to[{\mathscr{X}},\mathcal{V}]^{\mathsf{op}}=\mathbbm{U}{\mathscr{X}}
,
\mathsf{y}^{\prime}_{\mathscr{X}}(x)={\mathscr{X}}(x,-)
which satisfies

[{\mathscr{X}},\mathcal{V}](\mathsf{y}^{\prime}_{\mathscr{X}}(-),\psi)=\psi

for every covariant presheaf
\psi:{\mathscr{X}}\nrightarrow\mathbbm{1}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The
\mathcal{V}
-functor
i:{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}\to{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
is dense and point-separating with respect to the the forgetful functor
\mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup}\to\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
.

Proof.

Because

certainly exists, with identity unit

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mathsf{Lan}_{i}(i)&=&\mathsf{Lan}_{q\circ\mathsf{y}_{{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}}}(q\circ\mathsf{y}_{{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}})&=&\mathsf{Lan}_{q}\mathsf{Lan}_{\mathsf{y}_{{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}}}(q\circ\mathsf{y}_{{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}})\\

is dense.

\mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup}

\mathcal{V}

.

Lemma 5.1

In a symmetric monoidal closed category, an object

3.

Lemma 5.2

Proposition 5.3

For any symmetric monoidal closed category, the full subcategory of nuclear objects is compact closed.

\mathcal{V}
-categories, the coinverter arrow
q:\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})\to{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
of (
10
) is

q(\xi)(a,b)=\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(\mathsf{d}_{2}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}(a),\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}}(b)),\xi)

Proof.

Denote for now by
q_{1}
the
\mathcal{V}
-functor given by the expression above.
We will show that the image of
q_{1}
lives in
{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
and that
q_{1}
indeed provides the left adjoint to the embedding
j:{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})
, being thus isomorphic to
q
.

First, observe that for any
\varphi\in\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{A}}
,

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\varphi}})&=&\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(-,-)\otimes\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\varphi}})&\leq&\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(-,-)\otimes\varphi\end{array}

holds, with the last inequality being a consequence of the adjunction
\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}\dashv{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{}}
.

Now, for any
\varphi\in\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{A}}
,
\psi\in\mathbbm{D}{\mathscr{B}}
we have

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(\mathsf{d}_{2}(\varphi,\psi),q_{1}(\xi))&=&\bigwedge_{a,b}[\varphi(a)\otimes\psi(b),\bigwedge_{x,y}[\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(x,a)\otimes\Downarrow_{\mathscr{B}}(y,b),\xi(x,y)]]\\[10.0pt]
&=&\bigwedge_{x,y}[\bigvee_{a}\varphi(a)\otimes\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(x,a)\otimes\bigvee_{b}\psi(b)\otimes\Downarrow_{\mathscr{B}}(y,b),\xi(x,y)]\\[10.0pt]
&\leq&\bigwedge_{x,y}[\Downarrow_{\mathscr{A}}(x,{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\varphi}})\otimes\Downarrow_{\mathscr{B}}(y,{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{B}}}{\psi}}),\xi(x,y)]\\[10.0pt]
&=&q_{1}(\xi)({\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\varphi}},{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{B}}}{\psi}})\end{array}

Therefore, by (
14
),
q(\xi)
is in
{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
.

Next, observe that
\mathsf{id}_{\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})}\leq q_{1}
holds:

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\xi&=&\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(\mathsf{y}_{{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}}(-,-),\xi)&=&\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(d_{2}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})(-,-),\xi)\\[10.0pt]
&\leq&\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(d_{2}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}})(-,-),\xi)&=&q_{1}(\xi)\end{array}

In particular, if
\xi\in{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
and
\theta\in\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})
satisfy
q_{1}(\theta)\leq\xi
, then also
\theta\leq q_{1}(\theta)\leq\xi=j(\xi)
holds in
\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})
.

Finally, consider again
\xi\in{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}
and
\theta\in\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})
, but such that
\theta\leq\xi
in
\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})
.
Then

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}q_{1}(\theta)&=&\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(\mathsf{d}_{2}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}(-),\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}}(-)),\theta)&\leq&\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})(\mathsf{d}_{2}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}(-),\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}}(-)),\xi)\\[10.0pt]
&\leq&\xi({\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}(-)}},{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{B}}}{\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}}(-)}})&=&\xi\end{array}

using that
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are completely distributive.
∎

Corollary 5.12
.

The monoidal structure of
\mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{Sup}
restricts to
\mathcal{V}\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathbf{ccd}_{sup}
.

Proof.

The unit for
\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}
is
\mathbbm{D}\mathbbm{1}=\mathcal{V}
, which is completely distributive by
[
LZ06
]
.
That the tensor product of two completely distributive lattices
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
is again completely distributive can now be seen using
[
RW95
]
: the fully faithful adjoint string

\mathbbm{D}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}})\dashv\mathbbm{D}({\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{}}\otimes{\mathsf{sup}_{{\mathscr{B}}}{}})\dashv\mathbbm{D}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})\dashv\mathbbm{D}_{-1}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})\dashv\mathbbm{D}_{\forall}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})

induces another fully faithful adjoint string in
\mathcal{V}\hskip-1.5pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt{\mathbf{Cat}}
involving the inverter of
\mathbbm{D}(\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{t}_{\mathscr{B}})\leq\mathbbm{D}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})
, that we shall denote by
\iota
, and the inverter of
\mathbbm{D}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})\leq\mathbbm{D}_{\forall}(\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\mathsf{y}_{\mathscr{B}})
, namely the embedding
j:{\mathscr{A}}\otimes_{\mathcal{V}}{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathbbm{D}({\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}})
described by (
13
).
More precisely, this newly adjoint string writes as
\iota\dashv q\dashv j
, with

This exhibits

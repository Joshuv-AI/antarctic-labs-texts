Qualitative derivation of a density dependent incompressible Darcy law
Danica Basarić, Florian Oschmann, Jiaojiao Pan

Abstract
This paper provides the first study of the homogenization of the 3D non-homogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes system in perforated domains with holes of supercritical size. The diameter of the holes is of order ε α ​ ( 1 < α < 3 ) \varepsilon^{\alpha}\ (1<\alpha<3) , where ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 is a small parameter measuring the mutual distance between the holes. We show that as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , the asymptotic limit behavior of velocity and density is governed by Darcy’s law under the assumption of a strong solution of the limiting system. Moreover, convergence rates are obtained. Finally, we show the existence of strong solutions to the inhomogeneous incompressible Darcy law, which might be of independent interest.



Keywords.
Homogenization; Non-homogeneous; Navier–Stokes system; Darcy’s law.

Mathematics Subject Classification.
35B27, 76M50, 76N06.

1
Introduction

Homogenization of different types of fluid flows in physical domains has been widely considered during the last decades. In the domains perforated by a large number of tiny holes, when the number of holes tends to infinity and their size tends to zero, the limit system that describes the limit behavior of fluid flows is called homogenized system.

Firstly, Tartar
[
Tar80
]
considered the homogenization of Stokes equations, where the holes’ mutual distance is of the same order as their radius, and derived Darcy’s law. For steady Stokes and Navier–Stokes
equations, Allaire
[
All90a
,
All90b
]
provided a systematic study, which shows that the homogenized system is determined by the ratio
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
given as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}:=\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{d}}{a_{\varepsilon}^{d-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ d\geqslant 3;\quad{\sigma_{\varepsilon}}:=\varepsilon\left|\log\frac{{a_{\varepsilon}}}{\varepsilon}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},\ d=2,

where
\varepsilon>0
is the holes’ mutual distance, and
a_{\varepsilon}>0
their radius. Roughly speaking, in the supercritical case, i.e.,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sigma_{\varepsilon}=0
, the holes are too large and the fluid velocity finally tends to zero, however, a proper rescaling shows that the limit behavior is governed by Darcy’s law; in the subcritical case, i.e.,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\infty
, the holes are too tiny to be felt by the fluid and the limit system coincides with the original one; finally, in the critical case, i.e.,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\sigma_{*}\in(0,+\infty)
, the holes impose some friction in the asymptotic limit, giving rise to an additional term “out of nowhere” (cf.
[
CM82
]
), and the limiting system is given by Brinkman’s law
1

1

This additional term coincides with the well-known Stokes drag term, see
[
HKS21
]
for a heuristic explanation.
. A unified approach to all the regimes mentioned was given by Lu in
[
Lu20
]
.

The first homogenization of the compressible Navier–Stokes system was given by Masmoudi
[
Mas02
]
, who studied the case when the size of holes is proportional to their mutual distance, and derived Darcy’s law. For the subcritical case in three spatial dimensions, there is a series of studies
[
BO23
,
DFL17
,
FL15
,
LS18
,
OP23
]
, which showed that the limit system is the same as the original one. Similar results where given in
[
NP22
]
and
[
NO23
]
for the two dimensional case. At low Mach number,
[
BO22
,
HKS21
]
studied the critical and supercritical cases, respectively, and
[
BC24
]
investigated the full Navier–Stokes–Fourier system for heat-conducting fluids in the subcritical case.

For the homogenization of the evolutionary incompressible Navier–Stokes system, Mikelić
[
Mik91
]
studied the case where the size of holes is proportional to their mutual distance and obtained Darcy’s law. The critical and both sub- and supercritical regimes were considered in
[
FNN16
,
LY23
]
, respectively.

Moreover, more complicated models describing fluid flows in homogenization have been studied: Feireisl, Novotný, and Takahashi
[
FNT10
]
studied the supercritical case of the full Navier–Stokes–Fourier system; Lu and Pokorný
[
LP21

considered the subcritical case of stationary compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier equations; Feireisl, Lu, and Sun
[
FLS21

[

considered Laplace equations and Lamé systems separately. Recently, Lu and Qian
[
LQ23
]
considered the homogenization of evolutionary incompressible viscous non-Newtonian flows of Carreau-Yasuda type, where the size of holes is proportional to their mutual distance, and derived the linear Darcy’s law.

Regarding the convergence rates from the original system to the limit system, Allaire
[
All90a

All90b

[

[
Höf23

[
LO24

[
LQ23
]
and derived Darcy’s law when the size of the holes is
\varepsilon^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(1,\frac{3}{2})
. Also there, quantitative convergence rates are given.

1.1
Problems for supercritical case and aim of the paper

Although, as seen above, there is a vast of papers concerning homogenization of incompressible and compressible flows, there does not seem to be much literature regarding the “in-between-case” of inhomogeneous incompressible fluids. The case of critically sized holes was considered in
[
Pan24
]
, whereas the subcritical case was recently solved in
[
LPY25
]
. Therefore, in the present paper, we wish to complete the study in considering the homogenization of the 3D non-homogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes system, where the diameter of the holes is of order
\varepsilon^{\alpha}\ (1<\alpha<3)
, and the mutual distance of holes is proportional to
\varepsilon
(supercritical case).

Due to the presence of density, the traditional method of compactness arguments and oscillating test functions is not enough for us to obtain the limit equation. The main problem lies, both surprisingly and evidently, in the continuity equation: surprisingly, because for compressible fluids, usually the momentum equation causes problems, specifically the non-linear pressure and convective term (cf.
[
FL15
,
LS18
,
OP23
]
); evidently, because the pressure does not depend (explicitly) on the density, hence no compactness argument is required there, and the fluid velocity is small such that the (only remaining non-linear) convective term finally vanishes. Specifically, our main problem is that the mere weak convergence of density
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
and velocity
{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}
do not give enough information to pass to the limit of the product
\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}
in the continuity equation. Instead, we will use the method of relative entropy (energy), which has been shown to be a successful tool when showing weak-strong uniqueness results as well as convergence rates, see
[
Tar80
,
DGZ20
,
CBŠV24
,
HNO24
,
LO24
]
. To this end, we will properly modify the velocity
{\mathbf{u}}
and density
\varrho
of the limiting system and compare this modification with the original primitive density-velocity-couple
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon})
. In doing so, we do not just prove the strong convergence of
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon})
to
(\varrho,{\mathbf{u}})
, but also give precise convergence rates.

Let us emphasize that the literature for the supercritical case in the presence of density is indeed rather sparse. Up to the authors’ knowledge, the non-homogeneous incompressible case has not been addressed yet. Moreover, there seem to be just two rigorous papers for the compressible case: the seminal one by Masmoudi
[
Mas02
]
for the qualitative result if
\alpha=1
, and the paper by Höfer, Nečasová, and the second author
[
HNO24
]
, recovering quantitatively Masmoudi’s result with convergence rates and generalizing it to the case of a small Knudsen number. On the other hand, the case
\alpha>1
seems to be completely out of reach with the present methods; although the result from
[
HKS21
]
gives convergence for all
1<\alpha<3
, the limiting system is indeed incompressible, which is due to the assumption of a low Mach number vanishing as
\varepsilon
does so. Based on this lack of literature, in the present paper, we give the first step in the direction of a limiting system having non-constant density.

Organization of the paper:

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the problem under consideration, give the main results, and introduce the local problem as a crucial ingredient in the homogenization process later on. Section
3
is devoted to show uniform bounds on the velocity and density, and to extend the pressure in a suitable way. The homogenization in case of a toroidal domain is carried out in Section
4
, and adaptations for bounded domains are made in Section
5
. The convergence rates for the pressure can be found in Section
6
. Finally, the local existence of strong solutions for the target system is provided in Section
7
.

and

. The Frobenius inner product of two matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is denoted as
A:B=\sum_{i,i=1}^{3}A_{ij}B_{ij}
. Moreover, we write
a\lesssim b
whenever there is a constant
C>0
that is independent of
a,b,
and
\varepsilon
such that
a\leq Cb
. For a function
f
defined in some domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we use the symbol
\tilde{f}
to denote the zero-extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\tilde{f}=f\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ D,\ \ \ \tilde{f}=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus D.

2
Problem formulation, main results, and useful tools

2.1
Problem formulation

We consider the three-dimensional torus
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{3}
, or a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
of class
C^{2,\beta}
with
0<\beta<1
, and introduce a family of
\varepsilon
-dependent perforated domains

\{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{k\in K_{\varepsilon}}T_{\varepsilon,k},\

\displaystyle K_{\varepsilon}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}|\ \overline{Q}_{k,\varepsilon}\subset\Omega\},

\displaystyle Q_{k,\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\Big(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{3}+k,\ \ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3},

(2.1)

where the sets
T_{\varepsilon,k}
represent holes or obstacles. Suppose we have the following property concerning the distribution of the holes:

T_{\varepsilon,k}:=x_{\varepsilon,k}+a_{\varepsilon}T_{0}\Subset B(x_{\varepsilon,k},b_{0}a_{\varepsilon})\Subset Q_{k,\varepsilon}\subset\Omega,

with

|x_{\varepsilon,k}-x_{\varepsilon,l}|\geq 2\varepsilon\ \mbox{ for any }\ k\neq l\in K_{\varepsilon}.

Here,
b_{0}
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
,
0\in T_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded simply connected domain of class
C^{2,\beta}
contained in
Q_{0,1}
, and
B(x,r)
denotes the open ball centered at
x
with radius
r>0
. Without loss of generality, we will assume that
T_{0}\subset B(0,\frac{1}{8})
. The diameter of each
T_{\varepsilon,k}
is of size
\mathcal{O}(a_{\varepsilon})
and their mutual distance is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
. For the torus case, we will restrict ourselves to
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
with
(2\varepsilon)^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\Omega=\cup_{k\in K_{\varepsilon}}Q_{k,\varepsilon}
. In either case, the number of holes contained in
\Omega
satisfies

|K_{\varepsilon}|\leq C(\varepsilon^{-3}|\Omega|+o(1)),\quad\mbox{for some}\penalty\ C>0\penalty\ \mbox{independent of}\penalty\ \varepsilon.

For given
T>0
, we now consider the following non-homogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes system in
(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varrho_{\varepsilon}+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})=0,&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}=0,&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\big(\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\big)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\Delta_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}p_{\varepsilon}=\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f},&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\varrho_{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0},\quad(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})|_{t=0}=\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}^{0}&\mbox{in }\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
is the fluid’s mass density,
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}
its velocity field in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
p_{\varepsilon}
the pressure,
\mu>0
is a constant viscosity coefficient, and
{\bf f}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
is the external force density. The boundary condition on the outer boundary
\partial\Omega
is redundant if
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}
is the three-dimensional torus.

We remark that the presence of the factor
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}
comes from the fact that we anticipate the
original
fluid velocity to be small of order
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}
and scale this factor out, such that the
rescaled
fluid velocity
{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(1)
(in some sense); see
[
HNO24
,
LO24
]
for more details on this rescaling argument.

2.2
Weak solutions

Let us now introduce the definition of finite energy weak solutions to the non-homogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes system (
2.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),\quad\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

(2.3)

A couple
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})
is called a
finite energy weak solution
to system (
2.2
) in
(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}
provided:

1.

(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})
satisfies:

\displaystyle\varrho_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}}))\cap C([0,T],L^{2}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}})),\quad\varrho_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\quad\mbox{a.e. in}\quad(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}})),\quad\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\in C_{\textup{weak}}([0,T],L^{2}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}})),

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}})).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}{x},\quad\mbox{for any }q\in[1,\infty)\mbox{ and }t\in[0,\infty),\\
\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}}))}=\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}})}.\end{cases}

(2.4)

2.

For any
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{t}\psi+{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}\psi(0,x)\,{\rm d}{x}.

(2.5)

3.

For any
\bm{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\big[-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{t}\bm{\varphi}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}+p_{\varepsilon}{\rm div}_{x}\bm{\varphi}\big]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}^{0}\cdot\bm{\varphi}(0,x)\,{\rm d}{x}.

4.

For a.a.
\tau\in(0,\infty)
, there holds the energy inequality

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the global existence of finite energy weak solutions in Definition
2.1
is shown in
[
Lio96
, Theorem 2.1]
.

2.3
Main results

In this paper, we focus on the three dimensional case and take

a_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{\alpha}\quad\mbox{with}\quad 1<\alpha<3,

which implies
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{\frac{3-\alpha}{2}}
.

Since the fluid is incompressible and the holes are large, we expect in the limit the same behavior as for the purely incompressible case considered by Allaire
[
All90b
]
(see also
[
HNO24
]
for the compressible case with
\alpha=1
). More precisely, we expect the limiting equations to be

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\mathbf{u}})=0&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}=0&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\mu M_{0}{\bf u}=\varrho{\bf f}-\nabla_{x}p&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
{\bf u}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.8)

where
M_{0}
is the resistance matrix defined via the so-called local problem, see (
2.15
) below. Again, the boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
is redundant if
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}
.

Throughout the paper, we will assume the zero extensions of the initial data to satisfy

\displaystyle\tilde{\varrho}^{0}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\ \sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}\tilde{\bf u}^{0}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mbox{ are uniformly bounded in }\varepsilon.

(2.9)

We are now in the position to give the main results of this paper.

Theorem 2.2

(Torus case)
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}
be the three-dimensional torus and
1<\alpha<3
. Let
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})
be a finite energy weak solution of the Navier–Stokes system (
2.2
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
with initial data satisfying (
2.9
), and force
{\bf f}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
. Moreover, let
(\varrho,{\bf u})
be a strong solution to Darcy’s law (
2.8
) belonging to the class

\displaystyle\varrho\in C([0,T];W^{3,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{t}\varrho\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{t}{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega)).

Then, there is
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a constant
C>0
which is independent of
\varepsilon
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{3-\alpha}\big).

For bounded domains, we have a similar result:

Theorem 2.3

(Case of bounded domain)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{2,\beta}
,
\beta\in(0,1)
, and
1<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
. Let otherwise the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
be in order. Then,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{3-2\alpha}\big).

Remark 2.4
.

The different convergence rates stem from the mismatch of the boundary conditions between
{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\mathbf{u}}
on
\partial\Omega
, namely
{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
versus
{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. To overcome this issue, we will construct a boundary layer corrector, giving precisely the worse convergence rates in the case of a bounded domain. For more details on this, we refer to
[
She22
,
HNO24
]
.

Remark 2.5
.

We remark that in order to show our results, the regularity classes required in Theorem
2.2
can be weakened to

\displaystyle\varrho\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}(\varrho^{2})\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)),\quad\nabla_{x}\varrho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}{\mathbf{u}}\in L^{1}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega)).

2.4
Local problem and modified Poincaré inequality

A crucial ingredient in the homogenization process will be the so-called local problem, which we define as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{x}{\mathbf{v}}^{i}+\nabla_{x}q^{i}=0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus T_{0},\\
{\rm div}_{x}{\mathbf{v}}^{i}=0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus T_{0},\\
{\mathbf{v}}^{i}=0&\mbox{on }\partial T_{0},\\
{\mathbf{v}}^{i}={\bf e}^{i}&\mbox{at infinity},\end{cases}

where
\{{\bf e}^{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
In order to go from
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus T_{0}
to the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, we define functions
({\mathbf{v}}^{i}_{\varepsilon},q^{i}_{\varepsilon})
as follows: in cubes
Q_{k,\varepsilon}
that intersect with the boundary of
\Omega
(this is of course just relevant if
\Omega\neq\mathbb{T}^{3}
),

{\mathbf{v}}^{i}_{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{e}^{i},\quad q^{i}_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\mbox{in}\quad Q_{k,\varepsilon}\cap\Omega\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ Q_{k,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset;

in cubes
Q_{k,\varepsilon}
whose closures are contained in
\Omega
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\mathbf{v}}^{i}_{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{e}^{i},\quad q^{i}_{\varepsilon}=0&\mbox{in }Q_{k,\varepsilon}\backslash B(x_{k,\varepsilon},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),\\
-\Delta_{x}{\mathbf{v}}^{i}_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}q^{i}_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad{\rm div}_{x}{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{i}=0&\mbox{in }B(x_{k,\varepsilon},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\backslash B(x_{k,\varepsilon},\frac{\varepsilon}{4}),\\
{\mathbf{v}}^{i}_{\varepsilon}(x)={\mathbf{v}}^{i}\left(\frac{x-x_{k,\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\right),\quad q^{i}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}q^{i}\left(\frac{x-x_{k,\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\right)&\mbox{in }B(x_{k,\varepsilon},\frac{\varepsilon}{4})\backslash T_{\varepsilon,k},\\
{\mathbf{v}}^{i}_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad q^{i}_{\varepsilon}=0&\mbox{in }T_{\varepsilon,k},\end{cases}

together with matching (Dirichlet) boundary conditions on each interface. From
[
LO24
, Proposition A.1]
, we can state the following

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
1<\alpha<3
. For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
fixed, set the tensor-valued function
W_{\varepsilon}=({\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{1},{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{3})
and the vector-valued function
\textbf{{q}}_{\varepsilon}=(q_{\varepsilon}^{1},q_{\varepsilon}^{2},q_{\varepsilon}^{3})^{T}
. Then

\displaystyle W_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}),\quad

\displaystyle\textbf{{q}}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{i}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad

\displaystyle{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{i}=0\mbox{ in }T_{\varepsilon,k},

(2.10)

for any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and any
k\in K_{\varepsilon}
, and

\displaystyle\|W_{\varepsilon}-{\rm Id}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\min\left\{1,\frac{3}{p}\right\}(\alpha-1)}\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{for any}\quad p\in[1,\infty],

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|q_{\varepsilon}^{i}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{p}(\alpha-1)-\alpha}\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{for any}\quad p\in(\frac{3}{2},\infty],

3.1
Bounds for the velocity

Using the energy inequality (

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}(\tau)\,{\rm d}{x}+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C.

\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\mathcal{O}(1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup{\mathbf{u}}\mbox{ weakly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

\displaystyle\mathbf{F_{\varepsilon}}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})).

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf U}}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup{\bf U}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbf{u}}(s,\cdot)\,{\rm d}s\text{ weakly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

(3.4)

Combining this with the definition of

\displaystyle\|{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\leq C.

For

For each
\bm{\varphi}\in L^{q}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}({\Omega}))
with

{L^{q}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

{L^{q}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
and there holds the estimate

\|R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}}))}\leq C(\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\Omega}))}).

(3.6)

Proof.

Note in particular that inequality (

\displaystyle\|R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\Omega_{\varepsilon}}))}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}\bm{\varphi}\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\Omega}))}

Let

Having the two foregoing lemmas at hand, we want to extend the pressure
p_{\varepsilon}

a functional

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{H}_{\varepsilon}(t),\bm{\varphi}\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\nabla_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(t),R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\rangle_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{F}_{\varepsilon}(t)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}(t)+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}{\rm div}_{x}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(t)+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\Delta_{x}{\bf U}_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\rangle_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}|\langle{\rm div}_{x}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(t),R_{\varepsilon}(\bm{\varphi})\rangle_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|

As already mentioned in the introduction, one of the main difficulties in proving convergence lies in the limit of the continuity equation, and more precisely, in finding the limit of the term
\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}
as both functions converge just weakly in their spaces. For this reason, to prove Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
, we will make use of the relative energy inequality (REI), a brief derivation of which is given below. We will follow the presentation of
[

.

4.1
Relative energy inequality

Our proof of convergence will rely on the widely used relative energy inequality (REI). This approach enables us additionally to give convergence rates for the velocity and density.

Given a smooth function
{\bf U}
with
{\bf U}|_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=0
, the relative energy is defined as

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{\varepsilon},{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|r,{\bf U})=\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}-r)^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}.

Using moreover
{\bf U}
as test function in the momentum equation and noticing that
P_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=0
gives

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}{\rm div}_{x}\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}.

Further, using
\frac{1}{2}|{\bf U}|^{2}
in the continuity equation yields

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf U}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf U}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf U}:\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Recall also the energy inequality (
2.7
) as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

which we can write as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf U}|^{2}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Putting together, we find

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf U}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf U}:\nabla_{x}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}{\rm div}_{x}\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x},

or in rearranged form

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U})\cdot(\partial_{t}{\bf U}+({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf U})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf f}\cdot({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}{\bf U}:\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf U})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}{\rm div}_{x}\partial_{t}{\bf U}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot){\rm div}_{x}{\bf U}(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}.

In order to take care of the density, we see that by conservation of
L^{q}
-norms (
2.4

\partial\Omega
,
\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}={\bf w}_{\varepsilon}
if
{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\geq\varepsilon^{\alpha}
, in particular
\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, and

\displaystyle\|\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|\nabla_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{q}},

\displaystyle\|{\rm div}_{x}({\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\min\{1,\frac{3}{q}\}(\alpha-1)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}.

Inserting as before Darcy’s law in the REI (
4.2
), we find

\displaystyle\left[E_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}|\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}+({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x})\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\big)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(\mu M_{0}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla_{x}p)\cdot({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

The pressure term gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}p\cdot({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\lesssim\|{\rm div}_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|{\rm div}_{x}{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}.

As for the convective one,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}+({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x})\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\big)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\nabla_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{6}+(2\delta+\varepsilon+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{3}-\alpha})\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{5}\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-\alpha}\|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{6}(1+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{-\alpha})+(3\delta+\varepsilon+C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\alpha}{3}})\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}.

Note that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{8}\varepsilon^{-\alpha}=\varepsilon^{12-5\alpha}\to 0
if
1<\alpha<\frac{12}{5}
, and also
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\alpha}{3}}=\varepsilon^{3-\frac{4\alpha}{3}}<\delta
just for

1<\alpha<\frac{9}{4}<\frac{12}{5}.

For the stress term, we write

\displaystyle\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(-\Delta_{x}W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}-{\bf z}_{\varepsilon})\cdot({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Hence, the only remaining term to estimate is

\displaystyle\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}(\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{w}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{-\alpha}+\delta\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}({\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{{\mathbf{w}}}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2},

and in turn we need
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\varepsilon^{-\alpha}=\varepsilon^{3-2\alpha}\to 0
, leading to
\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
.

The final energy estimate is then

\displaystyle\|\tilde{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{3-\alpha}+\varepsilon^{3-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{3-2\alpha},

thus finishing the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Remark 5.1
.

We shall compare the requirement
1<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
with the one made in
[
LO24
]
: in there,
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}
is replaced by a more general
\varepsilon^{\lambda}
and the assumption
\lambda>\alpha
is made. Going to our setting where
\lambda=2(3-\alpha)
, this means
\alpha<2
; it is thus expected that the above results holds in the range
\alpha\in(1,2)
with a possibly different error term. Moreover, this hints that the error estimates given in
[
All90b
]
also hold just for this range (in a bounded domain), although the
qualitative
convergence result holds for all
1<\alpha<3
.

6
Pressure convergence

In this section, we will show convergence rates for the pressure in the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}
. The estimates for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
then follow with obvious changes. More precisely, we aim to prove:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
be the pressure extension from Section
3.2
, and let
\tilde{p}_{\varepsilon}=\partial_{t}\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
. Then, under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}_{\varepsilon}-p\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

Recall from Section
3.2
that
\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
is uniformly bounded. Let
p
be the pressure of Darcy’s law (
2.8
), and define
\Pi_{\varepsilon}
by

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\Pi_{\varepsilon},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\nabla_{x}P,R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\mbox{ for any }{\mathbf{v}}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),\qquad P=\int_{0}^{t}p(s,\cdot)\,{\rm d}s.

Similar arguments as before and the strong solution property of
(\varrho,{\mathbf{u}})
show
\Pi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
, and
\partial_{t}\Pi_{\varepsilon}|_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=p
. In other words,
\Pi_{\varepsilon}
is the extension of
1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}P
, defined by duality with the help of the operator
R_{\varepsilon}
. We will show that
\nabla_{x}(\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon})\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;W^{-1,2}(\Omega))
. This is essentially the same procedure as in
[
All90a
,
All90b
]
.

To this end, let
{\mathbf{v}}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))
and use that by definition

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}\Pi_{\varepsilon},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\nabla_{x}P_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}P,R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}.

(6.1)

Now, set

\displaystyle{\bf U}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbf{u}}(s,\cdot)\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle{\bf F}=\int_{0}^{t}(\varrho{\bf f})(s,\cdot)\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle P=\int_{0}^{t}p(s,\cdot)\,{\rm d}s,

where
(\varrho,{\mathbf{u}},p)
is the strong solution to Darcy’s law (
2.8
). Then, since
M_{0}
is constant, for any
t\in(0,T)

\displaystyle\mu M_{0}{\bf U}={\bf F}-\nabla_{x}P\mbox{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega);

since we have actually a strong solution, this also holds pointwise, so especially pointwise in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

To shorten the notation, we will write just
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
instead of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Omega/\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
in what follows. Replacing the gradients in (
6.1
) and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}\Pi_{\varepsilon},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{F}_{\varepsilon}-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0})-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}{\rm div}_{x}\Psi_{\varepsilon}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\Delta_{x}{\bf U}_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle-\langle{\bf F}-\mu M_{0}{\bf U},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\bf F}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf F},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\langle{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon 0},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\langle\Psi_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}{\bf U}_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle+\mu\langle M_{0}{\bf U},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle.

The first three terms are handled as follows: first,

\displaystyle|\langle{\bf F}_{\varepsilon}-{\bf F},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho){\bf f}\cdot R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq C\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|R_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}\big)\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)},

where we used the bounds on
(\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\varrho)
already obtained in (
4.3
).

Next,

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\langle\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\|R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

and

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\langle\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\sqrt{\varrho_{\varepsilon}^{0}}{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Moreover, we get

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\langle\Psi_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\Psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\|\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\varrho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{4}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}.

For the remaining term, we write

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}{\bf U}_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle=\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}({\bf U}_{\varepsilon}-W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}),\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}(W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}),\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle.

Now, from the error estimates (
4.3
) we infer

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}({\bf U}_{\varepsilon}-W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}),\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}({\bf U}_{\varepsilon}-W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon})\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}.

Hence, it remains to estimate

\displaystyle\mu\langle M_{0}{\bf U},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}(W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}),\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle.

But integration by parts and the very same arguments as before show

\displaystyle|\mu\langle M_{0}{\bf U},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle-\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mu\langle\nabla_{x}(W_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{u}}),\nabla_{x}R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|\leq C(\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon})+|\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\langle({\bf q}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}){\mathbf{u}},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|,

and eventually,

\displaystyle|\mu\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\langle({\bf q}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}){\mathbf{u}},R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|{\bf q}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{u}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|R_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\sigma_{\varepsilon}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)},

where we used (see
[
All90a
, Lemma 2.4.1]
)

\|{\bf q}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon.

Finally, we put together the above estimates to conclude

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}(\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{-1,2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}).

Since
\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Pi_{\varepsilon}
have zero mean value over
\Omega
, Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality also gives

\displaystyle\|\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}).

Furthermore, we can use
\partial_{t}(\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})|_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=(p_{\varepsilon},1_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}p)
and continuity of
\partial_{t}
from
L^{2}(0,T)
to
W^{-1,2}(0,T)
to deduce

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}_{\varepsilon}-p\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq\|p_{\varepsilon}-p\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+\|p\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|p_{\varepsilon}-p\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+C\varepsilon^{3(\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}(\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon})\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}+C\varepsilon^{3(\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))}+C\varepsilon^{3(\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}).

Note that this is precisely the convergence rate from
[
All90b
]
for the stationary setting. This finishes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Choosing in the above estimates the function
{\mathbf{v}}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
to be independent of time, we can show by the same arguments that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{P}_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon}\|_{[L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))]^{\prime}}\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}).

This of course is also a consequence of the embedding
L^{2}=[L^{2}]^{\prime}\hookrightarrow[L^{\infty}]^{\prime}
.

Remark 6.3
.

For a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the same arguments as before give rise to

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}_{\varepsilon}-p\|_{W^{-1,2}(0,T;L_{0}^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\sqrt{\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}}(\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-{\mathbf{u}}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}^{0}-\varrho^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3-2\alpha}{2}}).

7
Existence of strong solutions for the target system

In this last section, we aim to prove the following result.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{3}
, and let
\varrho_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\Omega)
,
\varrho_{0}\geq 0
, and
{\bf f}
such that

{\bf f}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})),\quad\partial_{t}{\bf f}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))

be given functions.

Then, there exists a positive time
T^{*}
such that problem (
2.8
) admits a unique solution
(\varrho,{\bf u})
in
(0,T^{*})\times\Omega
such that
\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}
,
\varrho\geq 0
in
(0,T^{*})\times\Omega
, and

\displaystyle\varrho\in C([0,T^{*}];W^{3,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{t}\varrho\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{2,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{3,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{t}{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{2,2}(\Omega)).

Remark 7.2
.

We point out that, by the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}(0,T)\hookrightarrow C([0,T])
, the solution
{\bf u}
of Theorem
7.1
belongs in particular to the class

{\bf u}\in C([0,T];W^{2,2}(\Omega)).

Remark 7.3
.

The torus case can be proven the same way as shown below; the interested reader may consult
[
BLMM+23
]
as well.

In order to prove Theorem
7.1
, we notice that, introducing the inverse of the resistance matrix
A=(\mu M_{0})^{-1}
, system (
2.8
) can be reformulated as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varrho+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varrho=0&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
{\bf u}=\varrho A{\bf f}-A\nabla_{x}p&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
{\rm div}_{x}(A\nabla_{x}p)={\rm div}_{x}(\varrho A{\bf f})&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
(A\nabla_{x}p)\cdot\textbf{n}=(\varrho A{\bf f})\cdot\textbf{n}&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(7.1)

First of all, we focus on the solvability of the transport equation.

Lemma 7.4
.

For any fixed
\widetilde{{\bf u}}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))
such that
{\rm div}_{x}\widetilde{{\bf u}}=0
,
\widetilde{{\bf u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}|_{[0,T]\times\partial\Omega}=0
, and any
\varrho_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\Omega)
,
\varrho_{0}\geq 0
, the transport equation

\partial_{t}\varrho+\widetilde{{\bf u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varrho=0

(7.2)

admits a unique solution
\varrho\in C([0,T];W^{3,2}(\Omega))
such that
\varrho\geq 0
in
(0,T)\times\Omega
; in particular, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(7.3)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(1+C_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\,{\rm d}t\right)\exp\left(C_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\,{\rm d}t\right).

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of
\varrho
can be easily proven by the method of characteristics, as well its non-negativity in
(0,T)\times\Omega
. More precisely, defining for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
the flow map
X(s;t,x)=\gamma(s)
with

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}(s)=\tilde{{\mathbf{u}}}(s,\gamma(s)),\\
\gamma(t)=x,\end{cases}

we find that
\varrho(t,x)=\varrho_{0}(X(0;t,x))
is the unique solution to (
7.2
).

To get estimate (
7.3
), we first multiply equation (
7.2
) by
\varrho
, integrate over
\Omega
, and perform an integration by parts to end up with

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

(7.4)

Secondly, we take the gradient of (
7.2
), multiply the resulting expression by
\nabla_{x}\varrho
, and integrate over
\Omega
; integrating by parts the term

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}^{2}\varrho\cdot\nabla_{x}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}|\nabla_{x}\varrho|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0,

and using the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we get the estimate

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|\nabla_{x}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{x}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}\|\varrho\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(7.5)

Proceeding in a similar way for the second
D_{x}^{2}
and third
D_{x}^{3}
derivatives, and using the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
, we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|D_{x}^{2}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{x}^{2}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{x}\varrho\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|D_{x}^{2}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{\infty(\Omega)}}\|D_{x}^{2}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}\|\varrho\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}^{2},

(7.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\|D_{x}^{3}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{x}^{3}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla_{x}\varrho\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|D_{x}^{3}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|D_{x}^{2}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|D_{x}^{2}\varrho\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|D_{x}^{3}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|D_{x}^{3}\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right)\|\varrho\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(7.7)

Summing up estimates (
7.4
)–(
7.7
) and integrating the resulting expression over time, it follows that for any
t\in[0,T]

\|\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(s,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right)\|\varrho(s,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\textup{d}s.

By Grönwall’s lemma we obtain

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(1+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(s,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\exp\left(C_{1}\int_{s}^{t}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(r,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\textup{d}r\right)\textup{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(1+C_{1}\exp\left(C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(s,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\textup{d}s\right)\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(s,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\textup{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(1+C_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\,{\rm d}t\right)\exp\left(C_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|\widetilde{{\bf u}}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\right]\,{\rm d}t\right).

Since the right-hand side is independent of
t\in[0,T]
, we can take the supremum on the left-hand side and obtain estimate (
7.3
).
∎

Secondly, we focus on the solvability of the elliptic system.

Lemma 7.5
.

For any fixed
\widetilde{\varrho}\in C([0,T];W^{3,2}(\Omega))
and
{\mathbf{f}}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))
, there exists a unique
p\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{4,2}(\Omega))
solving the elliptic system

\begin{cases}{\rm div}_{x}(A\nabla_{x}p)={\rm div}_{x}(\widetilde{\varrho}A{\bf f})&\mbox{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
(A\nabla_{x}p)\cdot\textbf{{n}}=(\widetilde{\varrho}A{\bf f})\cdot\textbf{{n}}&\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(7.8)

in a distributional sense; in particular, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{4,2}(\Omega))}\lesssim\|\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))}\|{\mathbf{f}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))}.

(7.9)

Proof.

To simplify the notation, we denote
g={\rm div}_{x}(\widetilde{\varrho}A{\bf f})
and
h=(\widetilde{\varrho}A{\bf f})\cdot\textbf{{n}}
.

Multiplying the first equation of (
7.8
) by
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
and integrating the resulting expression over
(0,T)\times\Omega
, through an integration by parts we get the identity

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}A\nabla_{x}p\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}g\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Omega}h\varphi\ \textup{d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Therefore, we fix the Hilbert space
H:=L^{2}(0,T;W^{4,2}(\Omega))
and set

\displaystyle a(p,\varphi):=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}A\nabla_{x}p\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle F(\varphi):=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}g\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Omega}h\varphi\ \textup{d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Clearly,
a:H\times H\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous bilinear form, and, due to the fact that the matrix
A
is a symmetric positive definite matrix, it is also coercive. Moreover,
F
is a linear and continuous functional on
H
, hence
F\in H^{\prime}
.

As consequence of the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique
p\in H
such that
a(p,\varphi)=F(\varphi)
for any
\varphi\in H
and such that
\|p\|_{H}\lesssim\|F\|_{H^{\prime}}
, leading to estimate (
7.9
). More precisely, due to the embedding
W^{3,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and the fact that
W^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\|F\|_{H^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|h\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}(\tilde{\varrho}{\mathbf{f}})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\tilde{\varrho}{\mathbf{f}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))}\|{\mathbf{f}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\|\nabla_{x}{\mathbf{f}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))}\|{\mathbf{f}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))}.

∎

Finally, if we fix

\widetilde{\varrho}\in C([0,T];W^{3,2}(\Omega)),\quad{\mathbf{f}}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega)),\quad\widetilde{p}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{4,2}(\Omega)),

the corresponding
{\bf u}
, uniquely determined by the second equation of (
7.1
), clearly belongs to the class

{\bf u}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})),

with

\displaystyle\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))}\|{\bf f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|\widetilde{p}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{4,2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega))}\|{\bf f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

(7.10)

We are now ready to prove the local existence result Theorem
7.1
. Given the initial datum
\varrho_{0}\in W^{3,2}(\Omega)
and
B=2\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}
, for some positive
T
to be fixed later, we consider the space
X_{T}=C([0,T];W^{2,2}(\Omega))
and the set

K_{T}:=\left\{\widetilde{\varrho}\in X_{T}\ :\quad\begin{aligned} &\quad\ \widetilde{\varrho}\in C([0,T];W^{3,2}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}\widetilde{\varrho}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{2,2}(\Omega)),\\
&\widetilde{\varrho}(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\widetilde{\varrho}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{\varrho}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\leq B\end{aligned}\right\}.

Since
\tilde{\varrho}(t,x)=\varrho_{0}(x)\in K_{T}
,
K_{T}
is a non-empty, convex and closed subset of
X_{T}
. Moreover, as consequence of Aubin-Lions lemma,
K_{T}
is compact in
X_{T}
.

Now, for any fixed
\widetilde{\varrho}\in K_{T}
, by Lemma
7.5
we find the corresponding pressure
p=p[\widetilde{\varrho}]
, and consequently the velocity
{\bf u}={\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho},\widetilde{p}]={\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]
. Therefore, we define the map
L
on
K_{T}
such that
\varrho:=L[\widetilde{\varrho}]
is the solution of the transport equation

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]\cdot\nabla_{x}\varrho=0,\quad\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}.

Combining estimates (
7.10
) and (
7.9
) with (
7.3
), we also recover from the last line that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|L[\widetilde{\varrho}](t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}L[\widetilde{\varrho}]\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t

(7.11)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|L[\widetilde{\varrho}](t,\cdot)\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(1+\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(1+C_{2}B\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bf f}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+C_{2}T\right)^{2}\exp\left(C_{2}B\int_{0}^{T}\|{\bf f}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+C_{2}T\right).

Hence, choosing
T
sufficiently small, say
T=T^{*}
, we get that
L(K_{T^{*}})\subset K_{T^{*}}
.
Furthermore, the map
L
is continuous in
X_{T^{*}}
; indeed, if we consider a sequence
\{\widetilde{\varrho}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset K_{T^{*}}
such that

\widetilde{\varrho}_{n}\to\widetilde{\varrho}\quad\mbox{in }X_{T^{*}}=C([0,T^{*}];W^{2,2}(\Omega)),

and we define
\varrho_{n}:=L[\widetilde{\varrho}_{n}]
,
\varrho:=L[\widetilde{\varrho}]
, taking the difference of the equations satisfied by
\varrho_{n}
and
\varrho
, multiplying it by
\varrho_{n}-\varrho
and integrating the resulting expression over
\Omega
, we get the identity

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{n}-\varrho)^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{n}-\varrho)\big({\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}_{n}]-{\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]\big)\cdot\nabla_{x}\varrho_{n}\,{\rm d}{x}=0,

(7.12)

where we have used the fact that, through an integration by parts,

\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{n}-\varrho)\ {\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]\cdot\nabla_{x}(\varrho_{n}-\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]\cdot\nabla_{x}|\varrho_{n}-\varrho|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0.

Integrating (
7.12
) over
(0,\tau)\times\Omega
with
0\leq\tau\leq T^{*}
, by Young’s inequality we obtain the inequality

\|(\varrho_{n}-\varrho)(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim B\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\|{\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}_{n}]-{\bf u}[\widetilde{\varrho}]\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\varrho_{n}-\varrho\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t,

where we used that
\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\|\nabla_{x}\varrho_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\|\varrho_{n}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim B
since
\varrho_{n}\in K_{T^{*}}
. By a Grönwall argument and (
7.10
), we therefore obtain that

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\|(\varrho_{n}-\varrho)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\left(\|{\bf f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))},B,T^{*}\right)\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\|(\widetilde{\varrho}_{n}-\widetilde{\varrho})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence,
\varrho_{n}\to\varrho
in
C([0,T^{*}];L^{2}(\Omega))
, which implies the convergence in
C([0,T^{*}];W^{2,2}(\Omega))
since
K_{T^{*}}
is a compact subset of
X_{T^{*}}
. By Schauder’s fixed point theorem, we get that
L
has a fixed point
\varrho\in K_{T^{*}}
, yielding the solution we were seeking for.

Finally, if
(\varrho,{\bf u},p)
is a solution of (
7.1
) in
(0,T^{*})\times\Omega
belonging to the regularity class

\displaystyle\varrho\in C([0,T^{*}];W^{3,2}(\Omega)),\quad\partial_{t}\varrho\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{2,2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\quad({\bf u},p)\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{3,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{4,2}(\Omega)),

then, taking the time-derivative of (
7.8
) and using elliptic estimates, it can be deduced that

(\partial_{t}{\bf u},\partial_{t}p)\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{2,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{3,2}(\Omega)),

and that the solution is unique in this class, provided
{\mathbf{f}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};W^{3,2}(\Omega))
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Remark 7.6
.

As a consequence of estimate (
7.11
), we can find a global-in-time solution, provided the initial datum
\|\varrho^{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}\ll 1
such that still
L(K_{T})\subset K_{T}
.

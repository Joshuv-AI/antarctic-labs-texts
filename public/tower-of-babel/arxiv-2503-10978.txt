Optimal control problem for reflected stochastic differential equation : existence


Abstract
We establish the existence of both optimal relaxed controls and strict optimal controls for systems driven by Reflected Stochastic Differential Equations RSDEs. Our approach is based on weak convergence techniques for the associated RSDEs in the uniform convergence topology, along with an appropriate Skorokhod representation theorem.

Introduction :

In this paper, we study the existence of optimal controls for systems driven by RSDEs of the form

\begin{cases}X_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{s},U_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})\,dB_{s}+K_{t},&t\in[0,T];\\
X_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{a.s.};\\
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}\,dK_{t}=0\quad\text{a.s.}\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
b
and
\sigma
are given functions,
(B_{t},t\geq 0)
is a standard Brownian motion defined on some filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\},\mathbb{P})
, satisfying the usual conditions. The term
U_{t}
, representing the control strategy imposed on
X_{t}
, called strict control, is a measurable,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted process with values in a compact metric space
A
.

The solution of (
1.1
) is a pair
(X_{t},K_{t})
, where
X_{t}
remains non-negative. The process
K_{t}
is a continuous process that ensures
X
stays within
[0,\infty)
;
K
acts only when
X
reaches the boundary, satisfying
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}\,dK_{t}=0
.

The cost function, which we aim to minimize, is defined by

J(U):=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A}f(s,X_{s},U_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}c(t)\,dK_{t}+g(X_{T})\right].

(1.2)

An
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted control
\hat{U}
is called optimal if it minimizes
J
, that is,

J(\hat{U})=\inf_{U\in\mathcal{U}}J(U),

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{U}
denotes the class of admissible controls, consisting of adapted processes with values in the action space
A
.

The concept of Reflected Stochastic Differential Equations (RSDEs) was first introduced by Skorokhod
[
13
]
and has since become a crucial mathematical tool in various applications, particularly where the state process is constrained within a given domain.

Fleming
[
4
]
established the existence of an optimal relaxed control for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with an uncontrolled diffusion coefficient, using compactification techniques. Later, El Karoui et al.
[
3
]
extended this result to SDEs with a controlled diffusion coefficient, showing that the optimal relaxed control in this case is Markovian.

For forward Itô SDEs, the existence of a strict optimal control is guaranteed by the convexity of the image of the action space
A
under the mapping
(b(t,x,\cdot),\sigma(t,x,\cdot))
, a condition referred to as Roxin-type convexity (see
[
3
,
5
,
10
]
). Without this convexity condition, an optimal control may not exist in
\mathcal{U}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
lacks a compact topology, a new class
\mathcal{R}
of admissible controls is introduced. In this framework, the controller selects a probability measure
q_{t}(da)
over the control set
A
at each time
t
, rather than a specific element
U_{t}\in A
. These are referred to as relaxed controls.

In this paper, we establish the existence of an optimal relaxed control for systems governed by RSDEs. The proof of the main result relies on tightness results for the distributions of the processes defining the control problem, as well as the Skorokhod representation theorem on the space
\mathcal{C}
(
[
8
]
), equipped with the uniform convergence topology (
[
14
,
12
]
). Additionally, when the Roxin convexity condition is fulfilled, we prove that the optimal relaxed control is indeed strict.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we present preliminaries and assumptions made on the model. Section 3 defines the relaxed optimal control problem and presents the main result. Section 4 is dedicated to the proofs.

2
Formulation of the problem, notations and assumptions

We consider an optimal control problem for a system driven by equation (
1.1
), where
U
is a strict control, that is a measurable,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted process with values in a compact metric space
A
. The process
(B_{t},t\geq 0)
is an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
. It is important to note that both the probability space and the Brownian motion may depend on the control U.

The concept of relaxed control involves replacing the
A
-valued process
U_{t}
with a
P(A)
-valued process
q_{t}
, where
P(A)
is the space of probability measures, equipped with the topology of weak convergence. We denote by
\mathcal{V}
the set of probability measures on
[0,T]\times A
, whose projections on
[0,T]
coincide with the Lebesgue measure
dt
. Equipped with the topology of stable convergence of measures,
\mathcal{V}
is a compact metrizable space, as shown by Jacod and Mémin
[
7
]
.

Stable convergence is required for bounded measurable functions
h(t,a)
, where for each fixed
t\in[0,T]
,
h(t,.)
is continuous. A relaxed control can be understood as a random variable that takes values in
\mathcal{V}
(
[
9
]
). The system is thus governed by the following RSDE

\begin{cases}dX_{t}=\int_{A}b(t,X_{t},a)q_{t}(da)dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t}+dK_{t},&t\in[0,T],\\
X_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{a.s},\\
\int_{0}^{T}X_{t}dK_{t}=0\quad\text{a.s.}\end{cases}

(2.1)

The cost to be minimized, over the set
\mathcal{R}
of relaxed controls, has the form

J(q):=E\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A}f(s,X_{s},a)q_{s}(da)ds+\int_{0}^{T}c(t)dK_{t}+g(X_{T})\right].

(2.2)

Under assumption (A.1) and (A.2), the system (
2.1
) has a unique solution
(X,K)
(
[
11
]
).

(A1)

Assume that the functions

\displaystyle b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times A\to\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times m},

are continuous. Moreover, assume that there exists a constant
K>0
such that for every
(t,x,u)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\times A
:

|b(t,x,u)|+|\sigma(t,x)|\leq K(1+|x|).\\

(2.3)

(A2)

There exists a constant
K_{1}>0
such that for every
(t,a)\in[0,T]\times A
, every
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle|b(t,x,a)-b(t,x^{\prime},a)|

\displaystyle\leq K|x-x^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq K|x-x^{\prime}|.

(A.3)

\displaystyle f:

\displaystyle[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times A\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle c:

\displaystyle[0,T]\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle g:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

are continuous functions with a linear growth in x uniformly in
(t,a)
. Moreover, assume that
f
and
g
are Lipschitz in
x
uniformly in
(t,a)
.

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R^{+}})
the space of continuous functions from
[0,T]
into
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the topology of uniform convergence.

3
The main results

Theorem 3.1
.

Under assumptions
(A.1)-(A.3)
, the relaxed control problem has an optimal solution.

To address the existence of a strict optimal control, we need the Roxin condition, given by

(A.4)
(Roxin’s condition). For every
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, the set

(b,f)(t,x,A):=\{(b(t,x,a),z):\ a\in A,f(t,x,a)\geq z\},

(3.1)

is convex and closed in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

If the assumptions
(A.1)-(A.4)
hold, then, the relaxed control problem has a strict optimal control.

4
Proof of the main results

To prove the theorem
3.1
, we need to some auxiliary results concerning the tightness of process.

Let
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a minimizing sequence, that is

\lim_{n\to\infty}J(q_{n})=\inf_{q\in\mathcal{R}}J(q).

Let
(X^{n},K^{n})
be the unique solution of the RSDE

\begin{cases}dX^{n}_{t}=\int_{A}b(t,X^{n}_{t},a)q^{n}_{t}(da)dt+\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})dB_{t}+dK^{n}_{t},&t\in[0,T],\\
X^{n}_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{a.s},\\
\int_{0}^{T}X^{n}_{t}dK^{n}_{t}=0\quad\text{a.s.}\end{cases}

(4.1)

In what follows, the constant
C
may vary from line to line. We denote by
\sigma^{*}
the transpose of
\sigma
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant C such that

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq 0\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\sup_{0\leq 0\leq T}|K^{n}_{t}|^{2}]\leq C.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a minimizing sequence, that is
\lim_{n\to\infty}J(q_{n})=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{R}}J(\mu),

By using Itô’s formula, we get

\displaystyle|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{A}b(s,X^{n}_{s},a)X^{n}_{s}q^{n}_{s}(da)ds+2\int_{0}^{t}X^{n}_{s}\sigma(t,X^{n}_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\sigma^{*}(s,X^{n}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}X^{n}_{s}dK^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle=x^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{A}b(s,X^{n}_{s},a)X^{n}_{s}q^{n}_{s}(da)ds+2\int_{0}^{t}X^{n}_{s}\sigma(t,X^{n}_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\sigma^{*}(s,X^{n}_{s})ds,

where the second equality is due to the Skorokhod’s condition.

We take the expectation and the supremum over
[0,T]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq x^{2}+2\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{A}|b(s,X^{n}_{s},a)X^{n}_{s}|q^{n}_{s}(da)ds+2\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\int_{0}^{t}X^{n}_{s}\sigma(t,X^{n}_{s})dB_{s}|]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma\sigma^{*}(s,X^{n}_{s})|ds],

\displaystyle\leq C+x^{2}+3\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt]+\epsilon\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}]+\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X^{n}_{s})dB_{s}|^{2}],

\displaystyle\leq C+x^{2}+(3+\dfrac{1}{\epsilon})\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt]+\epsilon\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}],

where the second inequality follows from applying the inequality
2ab\leq\dfrac{a^{2}}{\epsilon}+\epsilon b^{2}
, which hold for every
\epsilon\geq 0
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, along with assumption (A.2), and the third inequality is derived using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality.

By setting
\epsilon=\dfrac{1}{2}
, we get

\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}]\leq C+x^{2}+5\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt].

(4.3)

Then by Gronwall’s lemma, there exists a constant C such that

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}]\leq C.

(4.4)

On the other hand, we have

K^{n}_{t}=X^{n}_{t}-x-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{A}b(s,X^{n}_{s},a)q^{n}_{s}(da)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X^{n}_{s})dB_{s}.

(4.5)

So, we get the same estimation for
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|K^{n}_{t}|^{2}]
by the assumption (A.2) and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The processes
(X^{n},K^{n},B)
is tight on the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R^{+}})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R^{+}})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
0\leq s\leq u\leq T
, we consider the solution
(X^{n,s},K^{n,s})
of the following RSDE:

\begin{cases}X^{n,s}_{t}=X^{n}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{A}b(r,X^{n,s}_{r},a)q^{n}_{r}(da)dr+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(r,X^{n,s}_{r})dB_{r}+K^{n,s}_{t},&t\in[0,T],\\
X^{n,s}_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{a.s},\\
\int_{0}^{T}X^{n,s}_{t}dK^{n,s}_{t}=0\quad\text{a.s.}\end{cases}

(4.6)

By proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we can easily get the following estimation

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}]\leq C,

(4.7)

where the constant
C
is independent of
n
.

Now,applying Itô’s formula to the continuous semimartingale
|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}
yields to:

\displaystyle d|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{A}(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})(b(t,X^{n}_{t},a)-b(t,X^{n,s}_{t},a))q^{n}_{t}(da)dt

\displaystyle+2(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))dB_{t}

\displaystyle+(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))^{*}dt

\displaystyle+2(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})dK^{n}_{t}-2(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})dK^{n,s}_{t}.

The Skorokhod conditions implies that
(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})dK^{n}_{t}\leq 0
and

(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})dK^{n,s}_{t}\geq 0
, then we get,

\displaystyle d|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{A}(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})(b(t,X^{n}_{t},a)-b(t,X^{n,s}_{t},a))q^{n}_{t}(da)dt

\displaystyle+2(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))dB_{t}

\displaystyle+(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))^{*}dt.

By applying the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and leveraging on assumption (A.2), along with Lemmas
4.1
and
4.7
, we have that the local martingale
\int_{0}^{t}(X^{n}_{r}-X^{n,s}_{r})(\sigma(r,X^{n}_{r})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{r}))dB_{r}
is uniformly integrable martingale.

If we take the integral with respect to s and u, and then take the expectation, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X^{n}_{u}-X^{n,s}_{u}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\int_{s}^{u}\int_{A}(X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t})(b(t,X^{n}_{t},a)-b(t,X^{n,s}_{t},a))q^{n}_{t}(da)dt]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[\int_{s}^{u}(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))(\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})-\sigma(t,X^{n,s}_{t}))^{*}dt].

By assumption (A.2), there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
s
and
u
,
such that

\mathbb{E}[|X^{n}_{u}-X^{n,s}_{u}|^{2}]\leq C\mathbb{E}[\int_{s}^{u}|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}dt].

(4.8)

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X^{n}_{u}-X^{n,s}_{u}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C(u-s)\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq 2C(u-s)(\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}|X^{n,s}_{t}|^{2}]).

By the uniqueness of the solution to equation (
4.11
), it follows that
X^{n,s}=X^{n}_{.\wedge s}
on
[s,T]
, which implies that

\mathbb{E}[|X^{n}_{u}-X^{n}_{s}|^{2}]\leq C(u-s)\mathbb{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{t}|^{2}].

(4.9)

And the tightness of
(X^{n})_{n\geq 0}
follows from
4.2
.

Similarly, by using the fact that

K^{n}_{t}=X^{n}_{t}-x-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{A}b(r,X^{n}_{r},a)q^{n}_{r}(da)dr-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(r,X^{n}_{r})dB_{r},

(4.10)

we can prove the tightness of
(K^{n})_{n\geq 0}
. The tightness of the process B can be shown using standard arguments.
∎

Proof of theorem
3.1

Proof.

Let
(q^{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a minimizing sequence, that is

\lim_{n\to\infty}J(q_{n})=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{R}}J(\mu).

Let
(X^{n},K^{n})
be the unique solution of the RSDE :

\begin{cases}dX^{n}_{t}=\int_{A}b(t,X^{n}_{t},a)q^{n}_{t}(da)dt+\sigma(t,X^{n}_{t})dB_{t}+dK^{n}_{t},&t\in[0,T],\\
X^{n}_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{a.s},\\
\int_{0}^{T}X^{n}_{t}dK^{n}_{t}=0\quad\text{a.s.}\end{cases}

(4.11)

Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
implies that the sequence of processes
\gamma^{n}=(X^{n},B,K^{n},q^{n})
is tight on the space

\Gamma:=\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R^{+}})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathbb{R^{+}})\times\mathcal{V},

(4.12)

equipped with the product topology of the uniform convergence, and the topology of stable convergences of measures.

By Skorokhod representation Lemma
4.5
, there exists a probability space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathcal{F}},\hat{\mathbb{P}})
, a sequence
\hat{\gamma^{n}}=(\hat{X^{n}},\hat{B^{n}},\hat{K^{n}},\hat{q^{n}})
and
\hat{\gamma}=(\hat{X},\hat{B},\hat{K},\hat{q})
defined on this space such that :

1)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N},

\mathcal{L}aw(\hat{\gamma^{n}})=\mathcal{L}aw(\gamma^{n}).

(4.13)

2)

there exists a sub-sequence
(\hat{\gamma^{n}_{k}})
of
(\hat{\gamma^{n}})
, denoted again by
(\hat{\gamma^{n}})
, it converges almost surely
\hat{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
to
(\hat{\gamma})
in the space
\Gamma
.

3)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\hat{X^{n}}_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}|\to 0
and
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\hat{K^{n}}_{t}-\hat{K}_{t}|\to 0

\hat{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

By property 1), we drive

\begin{cases}d\hat{X^{n}}_{t}=\int_{A}b(t,\hat{X^{n}}_{t},a)\hat{Q^{n}}_{t}(da)dt+\sigma(t,\hat{X^{n}}_{t})d\hat{B^{n}}_{t}+d\hat{K^{n}}_{t},&t\in[0,T],\\
\hat{X^{n}}_{t}\geq 0\quad\text{a.s},\\
\int_{0}^{T}\hat{X^{n}}_{t}d\hat{K^{n}}_{t}=0\quad\hat{\mathbb{P}}-\text{a.s.}\end{cases}

(4.14)

where the Skorokhod’s condition holds due to

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{T}\hat{X^{n}}_{t}d\hat{K^{n}}_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}X^{n}_{t}dK^{n}_{t}]=0

(4.15)

and
\int_{0}^{T}\hat{X^{n}}_{t}d\hat{K^{n}}_{t}\geq 0.

\begin{cases}d\hat{X}_{t}=\int_{A}b(t,\hat{X}_{t},a)\hat{q}_{t}(da)dt+\sigma(t,\hat{X}_{t})d\hat{B}_{t}+d\hat{K}_{t},&t\in[0,T],\\

(4.16)

The convergence

follows from property 3) and the continuity of the map

(x,k)\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}x_{t}\,dk_{t},

under the uniform convergence topology.

To complete the proof of Theorem

, it remains to verify that

is indeed an optimal control. According to properties

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in\mathcal{R}}J(q)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A}f(s,\hat{X^{n}}_{s},a)\hat{q^{n}}_{s}(da)ds+\int_{0}^{T}c(s)d\hat{K^{n}}_{s}+g(\hat{X^{n}}_{T})]

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{A}f(s,\hat{X}_{s},a)\hat{q}_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{T}c(s)d\hat{K}_{s}+g(\hat{X}_{T})].

Theorem
3.1
is thus proved.

Proof of theorem
3.2

Proof.

We set

\int_{A}b(t,\hat{X}_{t},a)\hat{q}_{t}(da):=\hat{b}(t,\omega)\in b(t,x,A),

(4.17)

\int_{A}f(t,\hat{X}_{t},a)\hat{q}_{t}(da):=\hat{f}(t,\omega)\in f(t,x,A).

(4.18)

From (A.4) and the measurable selection theorem (
[
15
]
p.74), there is a A-valued,
\mathcal{F}^{\hat{X},\hat{q}}-
adapted process
\hat{U}
(and thus
\mathcal{F}^{\hat{X},\hat{q},\hat{K}}-
adapted), along with a countable set
D\subset[0,T]
, such that for every
t\in[0,T]\setminus D
and
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}
,

\hat{b}(t,\omega)=b(t,\hat{X}(t,\omega),\hat{U}(t,\omega)),

(4.19)

\hat{f}(t,\omega)=f(t,\hat{X}(t,\omega),\hat{U}(t,\omega)).

(4.20)

Therefore, for every
t\in[0,T]\setminus D
and
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}
, we have

\int_{A}f(t,\hat{X}_{t},a)\hat{q}_{t}(da)=f(t,\hat{X}_{t},\hat{U}_{t}),

(4.21)

\int_{A}b(t,\hat{X}_{t},a)\hat{q}_{t}(da)=b(t,\hat{X}_{t},\hat{U}_{t}).

(4.22)

Since
\hat{X}
and
\hat{K}

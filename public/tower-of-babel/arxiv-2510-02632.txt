On the invariant surface area functionals in 3-dimensional CR geometry
Pak Tung Ho

Abstract
. Cheng, Yang, and Zhang have studied two invariant surface area functionals in 3-dimensional CR manifolds. They deduced the Euler–Lagrange equations of the associated energy functionals when the 3-dimensional CR manifold has constant Webster curvature and vanishing torsion. In this paper, we deduce the the Euler–Lagrange equations of the energy functionals in a more general 3-dimensional CR manifold. Moreover, we study the invariant area functionals on the disk bundle, on the Rossi sphere, and on 3 3 -dimensional tori.

1.
Introduction

In
[
3
]
, Cheng introduced
two CR invariant surface area functional

dA_{1}
and
dA_{2}
.
To recall them, let us review some basic notions
in a pseudohermitian
3
-manifold.
These notions could be found in
[
10
]

and
[
12
]

for example.
For a pseudohermitian
3
-manifold
(M,J,\theta)
,

\theta
is the contact form
and
\xi=\ker\theta
is the contact bundle.
Then
J:\xi\to\xi
is an endomorphism
such that
J^{2}=-id
.
On
(M,J,\theta)
,
there is a canonical connection
\nabla
, which is called
Tanaka-Webster connection.
Associated to this connection, we have
torsion
A_{11}
and (Tanaka-)Webster curvature
W
.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface in
(M,J,\theta)
.
We recall (c.f.
[
4
]
) that a point
p\in\Sigma

is
singular

if its tangent plane
T_{p}\Sigma
coincides
with the contact plane
\xi_{p}
at
p
.
We call a surface nonsingular if
it does not contain any singular point.
We recall in
[
4
]

that a moving frame associated to
\Sigma
can be chosen.
Associated to
\Sigma
,
we have a special frame
e_{1}
,
e_{2}:=Je_{1}
such that

e_{1}\in T\Sigma\cap\ker\theta

and has unit length
with respect to the Levi metric
\frac{1}{2}d\theta(\cdot,J\cdot)
.
We denote the coframe dual to
e_{1},e_{2}
and
T

by
e^{1},e^{2}
and
\theta
.
A
derivation function

\alpha
on
\Sigma
is defined as

T+\alpha e_{2}\in T\Sigma.

The
p
-mean curvature
or
horizontal mean curvature

H
of
\Sigma
is defined as

\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}=He_{2}.

The two invariant surface area functionals
are (c.f.
[
6
]
)

(1.1)

E_{1}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}dA_{1}~~\mbox{ and }~~E_{2}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}dA_{2},

where

(1.2)

dA_{1}=\Big|e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}-\mbox{Im}A_{11}+\frac{1}{4}W+\frac{1}{6}H^{2}\Big|^{\frac{3}{2}}\theta\wedge e^{1}

and

(1.3)

\begin{split}dA_{2}&=\Bigg[(T+\alpha e_{2})(\alpha)+\frac{2}{3}\left(e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}-\mbox{Im}A_{11}+\frac{1}{4}W\right)H+\frac{2}{27}H^{3}\\
&\hskip 22.76219pt+\mbox{Im}\left(\frac{1}{6}W^{,1}+\frac{2i}{3}(A^{11})_{,1}\right)-\alpha\left(\mbox{Re}A^{1}_{\overline{1}}\right)\Bigg]\theta\wedge e^{1}.\end{split}

These were first found by Cheng in
[
3
]
.
Several remarks are in order.
First note that
the integrals in (
1.1
)
can only be taken over nonsingular
region of
\Sigma
.
So we assume
that the singular set of
\Sigma

has measure zero.
For smooth surfaces,
the singular set is always of measure zero.
This fact was proved by
Derridj and later by Balogh
[
1
]

(see also
[
5
, Theorem D]
).
Note also that
the area functionals in (
1.1
)
are CR invariant, i.e. invariant under the contact form change.
To check this, it suffices to check that the
2
-forms
in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are CR invariant.
We have the following transformation laws under the change of contact form

\tilde{\theta}=\lambda^{2}\theta
: (see P.414-415 of
[
6
]
)

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}e_{1},~~\tilde{e}_{2}=\lambda^{-1}e_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-2}T+\lambda^{3}\big(e_{2}(\lambda)e_{1}-e_{1}(\lambda)e_{2}\big),

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\alpha+\lambda^{-2}e_{1}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\tilde{H}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}H-3\lambda^{-2}e_{2}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\mbox{Im}\widetilde{A}_{11}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-2}\mbox{Im}A_{11}+\frac{1}{2}\big[\lambda^{-4}e_{2}(\lambda)^{2}-\lambda^{-4}e_{1}(\lambda)^{2}+\lambda^{-1}(\lambda^{-1})_{22}-\lambda^{-1}(\lambda^{-1})_{11}\big],

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}

\displaystyle=2\lambda^{-1}\big[(\lambda^{-1})_{11}+(\lambda^{-1})_{22}\big]-4\big[\lambda^{-4}e_{1}(\lambda)^{2}+\lambda^{-4}e_{2}(\lambda)^{2}\big]+\lambda^{-2}W.

Then the invariance of
dA_{1}
and
dA_{2}
can be verified directly by using
these transformation laws.

When
(M,J,\theta)
has constant Webster curvature and vanishing torsion,
the Euler-Lagrange equation for
E_{1}
reads (c.f.
[
6
, Theorem 3]
)

\mathcal{E}_{1}=0,

where

(1.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{1}&=e_{1}(|H_{cr}|^{1/2}\mathfrak{f})+\frac{3}{2}|H_{cr}|^{1/2}\alpha\mathfrak{f}\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt+\frac{1}{2}sign(H_{cr})|H_{cr}|^{1/2}\left(9h_{00}+6h_{11}h_{10}+\frac{2}{3}h_{11}^{3}\right),\end{split}

where

(1.5)

\begin{split}H_{cr}&=e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{4}W+\frac{1}{6}H^{2},\\
|H_{cr}|\mathfrak{f}&:=e_{1}(H)\left(e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{3}H^{2}+\frac{1}{4}W\right)\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt+H(T+\alpha e_{2})(H)+\frac{3}{2}(T+\alpha e_{2})\left(e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\right)\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt-\frac{7}{2}\alpha He_{1}(\alpha)-\frac{5}{2}\alpha^{3}H-\frac{2}{3}\alpha H^{3}-\frac{5}{4}\alpha HW\end{split}

and

(1.6)

\begin{split}&9h_{00}+6h_{11}h_{10}+\frac{2}{3}h_{11}^{3}\\
&=9(T+\alpha e_{2})(\alpha)+6H\left(e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{4}W\right)+\frac{2}{3}H^{3}.\end{split}

Furthermore, when
(M,J,\theta)
has constant Webster curvature and vanishing torsion,
the Euler-Lagrange equation for
E_{2}
reads (c.f.
[
6
, Theorem 9]
)

\mathcal{E}_{2}=0,

where

(1.7)

\begin{split}\frac{9}{4}\mathcal{E}_{2}&=He_{1}e_{1}(H)+3e_{1}V(H)+e_{1}(H)^{2}+\frac{1}{3}H^{4}\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt+3e_{1}(\alpha)^{2}+12\alpha^{2}e_{1}(\alpha)+12\alpha^{4}\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt-\alpha He_{1}(H)+2H^{2}e_{1}(\alpha)+5\alpha^{2}H^{2}\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt+\frac{3}{2}W\left(e_{1}(\alpha)+\frac{2}{3}H^{2}+5\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{2}W\right),\end{split}

where
V:=T+\alpha e_{2}
.
Among other things,
Cheng, Yang, and Zhang

[
6
]
found
many examples of critical points of
the invariant area functionals
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

when
(M,J,\theta)
is the Heisenberg group or
the CR sphere.
They also showed that they are related to the CR version of the singular Yamabe problem.
In particular, it is shown that the invariant area surface functional
E_{2}
determines the log term in the volume renormalization
for a formal solution to the singular Yamabe problem, thus providing an obstruction to the existence of smooth solutions of the singular
CR Yamabe problem.

In this paper, we continue
the study of invariant surface area functionals
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
defined in (
1.1
).
First we study these area functionals
of nonsingular surface
\Sigma
in the disk bundle
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
,
where
B^{1}
is the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.

B^{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|^{2}<1\}
.
In section
2
, we recall some computations in the disk bundle

B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, we will see in section
2
that
the Webster scalar curvature is constant and
the torsion vanishes for the disk bundle
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Hence, the Euler-Lagrange equations
for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

deduced by Cheng et al.,
which are stated in (
1.4
)-(
1.7
), are still applicable to
the surfaces in
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.
In section
3
and section
4
, we are able to find several examples
of critical points of the area functionals
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
in
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.
See Lemmas
3.1
-
3.4
and Lemmas
4.1
-
4.2
.

Next we study the area functionals
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

when
(M,J,\theta)
may not have constant Webster curvature or vanishing torsion.
To this end,
we deduce in section
5

the Euler-Lagrange equation for the
invariant surface area functional
E_{1}
in the general case,
for which
(M,J,\theta)
does not necessarily have
constant Webster curvature or vanishing torsion.
See Theorem
5.1
for the precise statement.
In section
6
,
we deduce
the Euler-Lagrange equation for the
invariant surface area functional
E_{2}

when
(M,J,\theta)
has constant Webster curvature
and constant, purely imaginary torsion.
See Theorem
6.5
for the precise statement.

In section
7
,
we study the invariant area functionals
on the Rossi sphere.
We will see in section
7

that, on the Rossi sphere, the Webster curvature is constant and the torsion is constant and purely imaginary.
In particular, Theorem
6.5
is applicable to the Rossi sphere.
Among other things, we prove that
the Clifford torus is a minimizer for
E_{1}

with zero energy in the Rossi sphere
S^{3}_{t_{0}}
, where

t_{0}=4-\sqrt{15}
. See Lemma
7.1
.
We remark that this is a sharp contrast
to the situation in the CR sphere.
Indeed, it was proved in
[
5
]

that if
T^{2}
is a torus in the CR sphere
without any singular points, then it cannot have
E_{1}=0
.
We also prove that the
Clifford torus
is a critical point for
E_{1}

in the Rossi sphere
S^{3}_{t}
for any
t
.
See Lemma
7.3
.
On the other hand, we prove that
the Clifford torus
is a critical point for
E_{2}

in the Rossi sphere
S^{3}_{t}

if and only if
t=4-\sqrt{15}
.

In section
8
, we study the invariant area functionals
when
(M,J,\theta)
is a
3
-dimensional torus.
We note that
for different generating curve, it defines different
3
-dimensional tori.
We also note in section
8
that the torsion of such torus never vanishes.
In particular, the Euler-Lagrangian equations
(
1.4
)-(
1.7
) are not applicable.
This shows the necessity of proving
Theorem
5.1
in section
5
and Theorem
6.5

in section
6
.
We are able to find critical points
for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
on some
3
-dimensional tori.
See Lemmas
8.1
and
8.3
.

2.
Computations in
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}

We include some computations in
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
for the readers’ convenience.
All these could actually be found in
[
9
]
, in which
the differential geometry of curves in the disk bundle
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
has been studied.

On the disk bundle
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
,
where
B^{1}
is the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.

B^{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|^{2}<1\}
,
the contact form is given by

(2.1)

\theta=dt+\frac{2i}{1-|z|^{2}}(zd\overline{z}-\overline{z}dz)

for
(z,t)\in B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The CR structure of
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}

is given by the decomposition of
the complexified tangent bundle
\mathbb{C}\otimes T(B^{1}\times\mathbb{R})=\mbox{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}},T\}

with
\theta(Z_{1})=\theta(Z_{\overline{1}})=0
,

\theta(T)=1
, where

(2.2)

T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},~~Z_{1}=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+i\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},~~Z_{\overline{1}}=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}-iz\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

The contact bundle
\xi
is given by
\xi=\mbox{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}}\}
.
The almost complex structure
J:\xi\to\xi

is defined such that
J^{2}=-I

with
J(Z_{1})=iZ_{1}
and
J(Z_{\overline{1}})=-iZ_{\overline{1}}
.

If we define

(2.3)

\theta^{1}=\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}dz~~\mbox{
and }~~\theta^{\overline{1}}=\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}d\overline{z},

then it follows from (
2.1
) that

(2.4)

d\theta=\frac{4i}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dz\wedge d\overline{z}=i\theta^{1}\wedge\theta^{\overline{1}}.

Since
d\theta=ih_{1\overline{1}}\theta^{1}\wedge\theta^{\overline{1}}
(see (2.2) in
[
10
]
), it follows from (
2.4
) that

(2.5)

h_{1\overline{1}}=1.

Note that the frame
\{Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}},T\}
defined in (
2.2
)
is dual to the coframe
\{\theta^{1},\theta^{\overline{1}},\theta\}
.

The Levi metric is defined as

(2.6)

g_{\theta}=\theta\otimes\theta+\frac{1}{2}d\theta(\cdot,J\cdot).

Then we have

(2.7)

g_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{1})=g_{\theta}(Z_{\overline{1}},Z_{\overline{1}})=0,~~g_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}})=g_{\theta}(Z_{\overline{1}},Z_{1})=\frac{1}{2}.

To see this, we compute

\begin{split}g_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}})&=\frac{1}{2}d\theta(Z_{1},JZ_{\overline{1}})=\frac{1}{2}d\theta(Z_{1},-iZ_{\overline{1}})=-\frac{i}{2}d\theta(Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(\theta^{1}\wedge\theta^{\overline{1}})(Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}})=\frac{1}{2},\end{split}

we have used (
2.4
). The others can be computed similarly.

Note that
Z_{1}
and
Z_{\overline{1}}
can be written as

(2.8)

\begin{split}Z_{1}&=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)+i(x-iy)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\\
&=\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)-i\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-x\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\end{split}

and

(2.9)

Z_{\overline{1}}=\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)+i\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-x\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)

where
z=x+iy
.
Therefore, if we write

(2.10)

\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2y\frac{\partial}{\partial t}~~\mbox{ and }~~\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-2x\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

where
z=x+iy
,
then it follows from (
2.8
)-(
2.10
) that

(2.11)

Z_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}-i\overset{\circ}{e}_{2})~~\mbox{ and }~~Z_{\overline{1}}=\frac{1}{2}(\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}+i\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}),

and hence,

(2.12)

J(\overset{\circ}{e}_{1})=\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}~~\mbox{ and }~~J(\overset{\circ}{e}_{2})=-\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}.

It follows from (
2.11
) that

(2.13)

\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}=Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}}~~\mbox{ and }~~\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}=i(Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}}).

From this, one can easily see verify that
\{\overset{\circ}{e}_{1},\overset{\circ}{e}_{2},T\}
is an orthonormal basis
with respect to the Levi metric
g_{\theta}
.
For example, we find

\begin{split}g_{\theta}(\overset{\circ}{e}_{2},\overset{\circ}{e}_{2})&=-g_{\theta}(Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}},Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}})\\
&=-\big(g_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{1})-g_{\theta}(Z_{1},Z_{\overline{1}})-g_{\theta}(Z_{\overline{1}},Z_{1})+g_{\theta}(Z_{\overline{1}},Z_{\overline{1}})\big)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1\end{split}

by using (
2.7
), and the others can be computed similarly.

It follows from (
2.3
) that

(2.14)

\begin{split}d\theta^{1}&=\frac{2z}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}d\overline{z}\wedge dz=\theta^{1}\wedge\left(-\frac{z}{2}\theta^{\overline{1}}\right),\\
d\theta^{\overline{1}}&=\frac{2\overline{z}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dz\wedge d\overline{z}=\theta^{\overline{1}}\wedge\left(-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\theta^{1}\right).\end{split}

The structure equations imply that (see (4.1) and (4.2) in
[
10
]
)

(2.15)

d\theta^{1}=\theta^{1}\wedge\omega_{1}^{1}+\theta\wedge\tau^{1}~~\mbox{ and }~~\omega_{1}^{1}+\omega_{\overline{1}}^{\overline{1}}=dh_{1\overline{1}},

where
\omega_{1}^{1}
is the connection
1
-form and
\tau
is the torsion form.
Comparing this with (
2.14
) and using (
2.5
), we obtain

(2.16)

\omega_{1}^{1}=\frac{\overline{z}}{2}\theta^{1}-\frac{z}{2}\theta^{\overline{1}}~~\mbox{ and }~~\tau\equiv 0.

By (
2.16
), we compute

(2.17)

\begin{split}\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{1}&=\omega_{1}^{1}(Z_{1})Z_{1}=\frac{\overline{z}}{2}Z_{1},~~\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{\overline{1}}=\overline{(\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{1})}=\frac{z}{2}Z_{\overline{1}},\\
\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{1}&=\omega_{1}^{1}(Z_{\overline{1}})Z_{1}=-\frac{z}{2}Z_{1},~~\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{\overline{1}}=\overline{(\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{1})}=-\frac{\overline{z}}{2}Z_{\overline{1}}.\end{split}

From (
2.13
) and (
2.17
), we find

(2.18)

\begin{split}\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}&=\nabla_{Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}}}(Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}})=\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{1}+\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{\overline{1}}+\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{1}+\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{\overline{1}}\\
&=\frac{\overline{z}-z}{2}(Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}})=-y\overset{\circ}{e}_{2},\\
\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}&=-\nabla_{Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}}}(Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}})=-\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{1}+\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{\overline{1}}+\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{1}-\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{\overline{1}}\\
&=-\frac{z+\overline{z}}{2}(Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}})=-x\overset{\circ}{e}_{1},\\
\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}&=i\nabla_{Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}}}(Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}})=i\Big(\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{1}-\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{\overline{1}}+\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{1}-\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{\overline{1}}\Big)\\
&=i\frac{\overline{z}-z}{2}(Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}})=y\overset{\circ}{e}_{1},\\
\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}&=i\nabla_{Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}}}(Z_{1}+Z_{\overline{1}})=i\Big(\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{1}+\nabla_{Z_{1}}Z_{\overline{1}}-\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{1}-\nabla_{Z_{\overline{1}}}Z_{\overline{1}}\Big)\\
&=i\frac{z+\overline{z}}{2}(Z_{1}-Z_{\overline{1}})=x\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}.\end{split}

From (
2.14
) and (
2.16
), we find

d\omega_{1}^{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{1}\wedge\theta^{\overline{1}}.

Hence, the Webster curvature is given by

(2.19)

W=-\frac{1}{2}.

Note that, on the disk bundle
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
,
it follows from
(
2.16
) and (
2.19
) that
the Webster scalar curvature is constant and
the torsion vanishes.
In particular,
the Euler-Lagrange equations
for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
stated in (
1.4
)
and (
1.7
) are still applicable to
the surfaces
\Sigma
in
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

3.
Examples of critical points of
E_{1}

in the disk bundle

In this section, we look for critical points of
the area functional
E_{1}
in the disk bundle.

3.1.
Example 1.

Consider the surface
\Sigma
in
B^{1}\times\mathbb{R}

defined by
ax+by=c
for some real constants
a
,
b
, and
c

such that
a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0
.
Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
lies in
T\Sigma
,
we conclude that the derivation function

(3.1)

\alpha\equiv 0.

Let
u=ax+by-c
be a defining function. We compute
the sub-gradient of
u
as follows

\nabla_{b}u=\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}(u)\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}+\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}(u)\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2}(a\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}+b\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}),

where
\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}
and
\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}
are defined as in (
2.10
).
This implies

(3.2)

e_{2}=\frac{\nabla_{b}u}{|\nabla_{b}u|}=\frac{a\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}+b\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}},

where the length
|\cdot|
is with respect to the Levi metric
g_{\theta}
defined in (
2.6
).
This together with (
2.12
) implies that

(3.3)

e_{1}=-Je_{2}=\frac{b\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}-a\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}.

From (
2.18
) and (
3.3
),
we compute

\begin{split}\nabla_{e_{1}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}&=\frac{b}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}-\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}=-\frac{ax+by}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}=-\frac{c}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2},\\
\nabla_{e_{1}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}&=\frac{b}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}-\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\nabla_{\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{2}=\frac{ax+by}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1}=\frac{c}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\overset{\circ}{e}_{1},\end{split}

which together with (
3.2

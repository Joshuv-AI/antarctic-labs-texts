Hybrid Harris Hawks optimization with eagle strategy particle swarm optimization for stability and disturbance rejection in tethered UAV systems
Authors: Alialhadi Khaleel Ismael, Sefer Kurnaz, Noorulden Basil
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the paper, a new hybrid optimization method is proposed to design an advanced attitude controller of tethered UAVs. The suggested methodology builds on the use of a Fractional-Order Proportional-Derivative-Derivative Integral (FOPDD-I) controller, which is optimized with the help of a Hybrid Harris Hawks Optimization-Eagle Strategy-Particle Swarm Optimization (HHHOESPSO) algorithm. The FOPDD-I controller is compared with Conventional PID, Cascade PID, classical Active Disturbance Rejection Control (ADRC) and Advanced ADRC methodologies. Comprehensive MATLAB simulations indicate that the FOPDD-I controller, optimized through HHHOESPSO, is more stable, responds more quickly to disturbances, and can reject disturbances better than their traditional counterparts. Significant results are a 56.5 percent increase in Kp to achieve better overall stability, a 65.2 percent increase in Ki to achieve smaller steady-state errors and better roll control and a 98.4 percent decrease in Kd to achieve smaller yaw overshoot and oscillations. Also, the fractional-order parameters are adjusted to adaptability improvement of 12.5 and 14.7 in nonlinear dynamic environment. Conversely, traditional controllers including PID and Cascade PID demonstrate only minor tuning benefits, whereas both classical and state-of-the-art ADRC approaches realize moderate performance benefits, that are not as high as the stability and responsiveness demonstrated by FOPDD-I controller. This study underscores the effectiveness of combining fractional-order control with hybrid optimization strategies, establishing the proposed FOPDD-I controller as a robust solution for tethered UAV attitude control under dynamic and uncertain conditions.Keywords: Tethered UAV system, FOPDD-I controller, HHHOESPSO algorithm, Advanced control strategies, Disturbance rejectionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Tethered UAVs have become major trends in both academic and practical perspectives in recent years mainly because they can overcome the shortcoming of traditional UAVs. Conventional UAVs, although encompassing a wide range of applications, are restricted by the on-board battery and power supply for prolonged uses such as surveillance and communication aerial re-transmission and disaster management. Traditional UAVs often face limitations, particularly when it comes to power supply and communication range. Tethered UAVs address some of these issues by using a physical cable that provides continuous power and a reliable data connection. This has the benefit of saving on the costs of changing batteries and enables the drone to spend a lot of time in the air, particularly in scenarios requiring long-term surveillance or extra protection in hazardous settings1–5. Nevertheless, there are not only benefits associated with tethered drones. Environmental factors such as wind shear, turbulence and vibrations on the tether itself make maintaining stable flight difficult. This are more advanced control systems the stability, maneuverability and overall performance of the drone, which is why more advanced control systems are required. PID controllers and other conventional forms of control can often not handle the complexity of tethered operations. In this regard, more sophisticated methods such as Fractional Order PID (FOPID) controllers and Active Disturbance Rejection Control (ADRC) were investigated to provide better stability and robustness by adjusting on the fly to external disturbances and internal uncertainty6–10. A new control algorithm is proposed in this work, a hybrid FOPID based on a hybrid optimization strategy containing Hybrid Harris Hawks Optimization and Eagle Strategy-enhanced Particle Swarm Optimization (FOPDD-I HHHOESPSO), with the aim of providing an additional improvement in performance and reliability. This approach optimizes the controller parameters to enhance stability and trajectory tracking and disturbance rejection. The proposed method presented in this paper uses the natural flexibility of FOPID and the possibility of metaheuristic algorithms to optimize tethered UAVs with nonlinearity and uncertainties.

The HHGOESPSO algorithm has some important advantages over conventional optimization methods applied to the management of tethered UAV systems. This hybrid approach combines the advantages of a global search with a local fine-tuning by incorporating the techniques of Harris Hawks Optimization, Eagle Strategy, and Particle Swarm Optimization. This renders it especially suited to solving the highly nonlinear solution spaces that characterize UAV dynamics. This is one of its greatest strengths that allows it to optimize its key control parameters like Kp, Ki, Kd, λ and μ that directly affect the stability of flight. Consequently, the algorithm provides increased control of the pitch, roll, and yaw, as well as enhancing the overall responsiveness of the UAV, stability of the system, and performance under dynamic flight conditions in the presence of external disturbances. To show the ability to outperform traditional and hybrid approaches, the HHHOESPSO algorithm exploits the flexibility in constraint management, as well as high-performance in optimum control parameter identification. Its success, as demonstrated in its early simulations, is evidence of its ability and requires experiments in real worlds to demonstrate its utility in practical use11,12.

Fractional-order proportional-derivative-integral (FOPDD-I) controller can play an important role in controlling pitch, roll and yaw instabilities for UAVs with the performance shape of the control parameters, Kp, Ki, Kd, λ and μ. Kp, (directly affecting the response to the errors) instantly corrects for pitch, roll and yaw deviations. The steady state error correction is addressed by accumulating past discrepancies to correct long term deviations. The system’s behaviour is predicted by Kd (derivative gain) reacting to the error rate change thereby smoothing and stabilizing the control with less overshoots and oscillations. Factional order of the parameters λ (fractional integral order) and μ (fractional derivative order) broaden the flexibilities and adaptabilities of the controller with respect to active dynamic and nonlinear UAV system responses. These parameters in combination allow the controller to strike a balance between responsiveness, stability and disturbance rejection—essential in ensuring UAV flight control in harsh, unpredictable conditions13.

The criticality of disturbance rejection for the Tethered UAV system derives from the fact that such systems operate in dynamic and sometimes unpredictable environments where external perturbations, such as wind gusts, tether-induced forces and payload swings, can significantly destabilize the system and degrade performance. Disturbance rejection ensures that the UAV can reject disturbance forces without affecting precision control of pitch, roll and yaw, therefore ensuring operational reliability and the success of the mission. To address this challenge, the proposed HHHOESPSO algorithm optimizes fractional order proportional derivative integral (FOPDD I) controller parameter Kp, Ki, Kd, λ, and μ to perform robust control. The method bridges global and local search capabilities with the combined usage of Harris Hawks Optimization, Eagle Strategy and Particle Swarm Optimization to find control parameters that minimize disturbance effects. In these cases, with the UAV resilient to external perturbations, better system stability, faster response times, and improved adaptation to non-linear dynamics are attained14.

In recent literature dealing with UAV control strategies, we see the development of optimization methods and controllers that deal with dynamic and nonlinear environments. Zhao15 investigated an ADRC tuned using Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) with balanced parameters (Kp = 1.35, Ki = 0.48, Kd = 0.18, λ = 0.32, μ = 0.35) that suppresses the disturbances efficiently and stabilizes the system. Due to its global search capabilities, PSO proved to be an efficient tuning method suitable for this type of nonlinear system, that of tethered UAV systems. This is then expanded in16, where Wang and his colleagues used a Fractional Order PID (FOPID) controller that they optimized with the Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) algorithm. The obtained refined parameters (Kp = 1.37, Ki = 0.495, Kd = 0.185, λ = 0.315, μ = 0.345) could achieve better disturbance rejection and better adaptability to settings with varying conditions, confirming the effectiveness in searching and balancing between exploration and exploitation in high dimensional parameter space by GWO. In Xu et al.17 a PID controller for fixed wing UAV was presented that has been optimized using the Bald Eagle Search (BES) algorithm. BES was shown to exhibit strong global and local search capabilities and did not need tuning, Kp = 1.41, Ki = 0.5, Kd = 0.19, λ = 0.31, μ = 0.34, to improve control stability and resiliency against external perturbations. Through this study, BES was demonstrated to be an effective control tool that can attain robust control of systems with high uncertainty levels. Wang et al. also played another major role in16 by tuning the ADRC controller with Beetle Antennae Search (BAS). The BAS algorithm has shown strong computational efficiency and consistent performance in maintaining UAV stability under dynamic conditions, with optimal control parameters achieved at Kp = 1.45, Ki = 0.52, Kd = 0.2, λ = 0.3, and μ = 0.33. In a related study15, Zhao proposed a hybrid tuning method combining Fractional-Order Adaptive Control (FOAC) with Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) for FOPID controller design. The FOAC-GWO approach achieved impressive optimization results Kp = 1.5, Ki = 0.55, Kd = 0.22, λ = 0.29, μ = 0.31 and demonstrated exceptional control accuracy in dealing with nonlinear system dynamics. Further improvements were observed in18, where a PID controller optimized using the Teaching–Learning-Based Optimization (TLBO) algorithm reached the highest reported parameter values: Kp = 2.83, Ki = 1, Kd = 0.92, λ = 2.67, and μ = 0.79. These results led to significant enhancements in system response and disturbance rejection, though the increased parameter values also suggest possible trade-offs in terms of computational load and real-time feasibility. In contrast, Smith et al.19 introduced an adaptive control strategy using a Radial Basis Function Neural Network (RBFNN). In contrast to the classical optimization-based methods, the given approach was based more on real-time flexibility than on predefined parameter optimization. Nevertheless, the neural network controller displayed strong performance in highly uncertain and dynamic environments, which demonstrates the promise of machine learning methods applied to the complex control of UAVs.

Benchmark functions

Nine benchmark functions were used to evaluate the performance of the proposed HHHOESPSO algorithm, which has been shown to perform effectively in optimizing tethered UAV systems. Each benchmark function presents unique optimization challenges that assess various aspects of the algorithm’s capabilities. Smooth convergence to the global minimum of the Sphere function, which is a simple unimodal, convex function, is demonstrated by the algorithm on the Sphere function as a testbed to see how well the algorithm predicts the linear UAV response and maintains stability under basic maneuvers. The Rosenbrock function (otherwise known as the Banana function) presents the algorithm with a complicated reflexive valley that forces the algorithm to operate on highly skilled trajectories while staying in a tightly packed performance envelope matching UAV path optimization in restrained or restricted environments. A rigorous evaluation of the algorithm’s exploration capability within obstacle laden environments and in the presence of random disturbances, an important aspect of UAV operations, is provided using the Rastrigin function, a multimodal function with many local minima. The test is concerned with the resiliency of the algorithm in avoidance of or breaking any local traps by achieving such UAV stability despite the changes that may occur across the entire field of reason. Similarly, the Ackley-type describing flat areas with a steep minimum is utilized to analyze the trade-off between exploration and exploitation regarding the adaptability of the algorithm in dynamic environments and uncertain UAV operating conditions. The Schwefel problem is a difficult optimization problem whose global optima are misleading, as they represent cases where UAVs have to operate in extremely dynamic environments that require both precision and agility. In applications like tracking targets or stabilization in UAVs, to determine whether the algorithm can reach the correct convergences, the Zakharov function with its smooth but redundant optimization tracks is applied. Apply the functional, which is parabolic in form, to measure convergence speed and trajectory accuracy in the context of point-to-point maneuvering of the UAV. Lastly, the Levy function with sharp ridges and flat plateaus gives the algorithm a strict test of precision and flexibility in delicate environments where the UAV control is highly sensitive. These benchmark functions together also constitute a comprehensive test of efficiency, robustness, and precision with which the HHHOESPSO algorithm can solve high dimensional nonlinear and most importantly dynamic optimization problems. Conclusion

This study introduces the FOPID-based HHHOESPSO algorithm, which significantly enhances the control and stability of tethered UAV systems by employing a hybrid optimization technique to dynamically tune controller parameters. The results demonstrate that this approach outperforms traditional methods in balancing conflicting control objectives, maximizing gains, and developing a robust system capable of maintaining stability under nonlinear and dynamic flight conditions. The ability to generate significant torque in the roll, pitch, and yaw directions, coupled with improved disturbance rejection and trajectory tracking, highlights the effectiveness of this method. However, two key limitations of this study must be addressed. First, while the experiments were conducted on complete hardware systems within a simulated environment, real-world complexities such as unmodeled dynamics, wind turbulence, and hardware imperfections were controlled but not fully captured. This raises concerns about the performance of the optimized controller in practical applications. Second, the study focuses on a specific controller design and optimization approach, excluding alternative algorithms and more sophisticated control strategies, such as advanced feedback control, which could further enhance performance.

Entanglement Entropy of Conformal Field Theory in All Dimensions Preprint: preprintnumbers
Xin Jiang, Haitang Yang

Abstract
We provide a field-theoretic method to calculate entanglement entropy of CFT in all dimensions, for arbitrary shape of entangling surfaces. Ryu-Takayanagi formula is manifested in this formalism.

I
Introduction

Entanglement properties of quantum systems are playing an increasingly crucial role in physics.
An intrinsic quantity counting the
degrees of freedom of entanglement is the von Neumann entropy, also
known as entanglement entropy. It is defined by
S_{A}=-\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}

for a reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
associated with a subsystem

A
, where the total system is bipartitioned into two subsystems,

A
and its complement
A^{c}
. In quantum
field theory (QFT), one usually considers the entropy of entanglement
between two
adjacent and complementary
subregions
A
and
A^{c}
,
denoted as
S(A:A^{c})
. Although the calculation of entanglement entropy in
two dimensional CFT has been well established using the replica trick

(
Callan:1994py
;
Calabrese:2004eu
)
, no approach currently exists
for performing such calculations in higher dimensional CFT
D
for
D>2
.

The entropy
S(A:A^{c})
typically suffers an
ultraviolet (UV) divergence due to the very intense entanglement between
contiguous fields. This divergent behavior renders it extremely challenging,
if not entirely impossible,
to derive exact relations between QFT and gravity.
A natural question arises: are there any finite
elementary
entanglement entropies?
For such quantities to exist, two conditions should be satisfied:

•

The entangled regions must be disjoint.

•

The system has to be a pure state to allow the calculation of the entanglement entropy.

It is not hard to understand that the annular CFT
2
in Figure
1

precisely meets these two conditions.
In prior work with collaborators
(
Jiang:2024ijx
;
Jiang:2025tqu
;
Jiang:2025dir
)
,
we demonstrated that the entanglement entropy
S(A:B)

between disjoint intervals
A
and
B
is indeed finite, for static, covariant and
thermal configurations, respectively. Notably,

S(A:B)
can be equivalently interpreted as the entanglement entropy of the
mixed state
\rho_{AB}
in the infinite system, as illustrated in Figure
2
.
The well-known divergent entanglement entropies for adjacent intervals emerge
naturally as the adjacent limits of disjoint
S(A:B)
.

A critical insight is the straightforward generalization of the annular CFT

Since the replica of a solid torus is still a solid torus,

The usual divergent entanglement entropies for adjacent regions

In this paper, we only study the ball shaped entangling region. But one can easily see
that this framework works for entangling surfaces of arbitrary shape.

For

V
Shape independence and Ryu-Takayanagi formula

We present two important indications or extensions in this section.

Shape independence

Even though we only study the spheric entangling surface in this paper,
our approach is clearly independent of the specific shape of entangling regions
A
and
B
.
In other words, one can replace
A
and
B
with generally shaped regions,
denoted as
\mathbb{X}
. The relevant entanglement entropy would
then be

\displaystyle S(A:B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\mathcal{E}_{\text{vac}}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{X}).

(28)

However, it should be noted that to apply this formula directly,

A
and
B
must be reflection-symmetric
with respect to each other, as only under this condition the thermal
partition function can be formulated.

To calculate the entanglement entropy between two similar,
disjoint regions (same shape, different sizes) with arbitrary positions,
one needs to map the configuration to the reflection-symmetric one with some conformal map.
For any shape of the entangling region, there should exist
a set of inversive products analogous to (
13
).

For adjacent scenarios,
it is easy to understand that, by choosing
some simple setup and then taking adjacent limit, the usual divergent entanglement entropy
for arbitrarily shaped region can be obtained straightforwardly.

Manifestation of Ryu-Takayanagi formula

Since any pair of similar, disjoint regions falls into the reflection-symmetric case,
the entanglement entropy is proportional to
the area
\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{X})
defined within the hyperbolic
metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=(\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\underset{i=1}{\overset{D-2}{\sum}}\mathrm{d}x_{i}^{2})/r^{2}.

Note that the subregion
\mathbb{X}
could always be understood as a
(
D-1
)-dimensional minimal surface embedded in higher-dimensional
AdS
D+1
space. Therefore, the RT formula saying that the entanglement
entropy of CFT
D
is proportional to the area of the codimension-2
minimal surface in the AdS
D+1
spacetime has been manifested
in current method.

VII
Conclusion

In summary, we introduced a general framework to compute the entanglement entropy in CFT
D
for
D\geq 2
.
We provided an explicit expression for the entanglement entropy of ball-shaped entangling regions in CFT
D
.
This method is applicable to entangling regions of arbitrary shape.
Remarkably, this method is substantially simpler
than the traditional one used to calculate CFT
2
entanglement entropy.
The RT formula emerges naturally within this formalism.

In CFT
2
, the divergent entanglement entropy between two adjacent and complementary
subsystems has been extensively studied. However, the finite entanglement entropy
between two disjoint regions has remained underexplored until recently.
From our perspective, the latter is of greater fundamental importance:
it provides a pair of exactly equal dual quantities in the correspondence
between gravity and CFT, whereas the divergent adjacent configuration represents
merely a special limiting case of this disjoint configuration.

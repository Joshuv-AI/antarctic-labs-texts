Abstract

Using Bochner techniques, we prove that a compact Einstein manifold of dimension n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 has constant curvature provided that the curvature operator of the second kind satisfies a cone condition that is strictly weaker than nonnegativity. Furthermore, employing a result of Li [ 17 ] , we establish that any closed Einstein manifold of dimension n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 satisfying k − 1 ​ ( λ 1 + ⋯ + λ k ) ≥ − θ ⁡ ( n , k ) ​ λ ¯ , for some k ≤ [ n + 2 4 ] k^{-1}({\lambda}_{1}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{k})\geq-\theta(n,k)\bar{\lambda},\quad\text{for some}\quad k\leq[\frac{n+2}{4}] must be either flat or a spherical space form. Here, λ 1 ≤ λ 2 ≤ ⋯ ≤ λ ( n − 1 ) ​ ( n + 2 ) 2 {\lambda}_{1}\leq{\lambda}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq{\lambda}_{\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{2}} are the eigenvalues of R ̊ \mathring{R}\, , λ ¯ \bar{\lambda} is their average, and θ ⁡ ( n , k ) \theta(n,k) is a positive constant. This result generalizes the work of Dai-Fu [ 8 ] and Chen-Wang [ 4 , 5 ] .

1.
introduction

The study of the Riemannian curvature operator of the second kind originated with Nishikawa’s conjecture
[
20
]
, which states that a closed simply connected Riemannian manifold with a nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind is diffeomorphic to a locally symmetric space, and if the operator is positive, the manifold is diffeomorphic to a spherical space form. This conjecture was confirmed by Cao–Gursky–Tran
[
3
]
under the assumption of two-positivity, and later strengthened by Li
[
15
]
and Nienhaus–Petersen–Wink
[
19
]
under the weaker condition of three-nonnegativity. Further classification results under various curvature conditions of the second kind can be found in
[
7
,
12
,
14
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
8
,
19
]
.

In the context of Einstein manifolds, Kashiwada
[
11
]
showed that closed Einstein manifolds with a positive curvature operator of the second kind are constant curvature spaces. Li
[
12
]
later proved that Einstein manifolds of dimension
n\geq 4
with
4\frac{1}{2}
-positive (resp.
4\frac{1}{2}
-nonnegative) curvature operator of the second kind are constant curvature spaces (resp. locally symmetric), using Brendle’s result
[
2
]
. Nienhaus, Petersen and Wink
[
19
,
18
]
used the Bochner formula to show that
n
-dimensional compact Einstein manifolds with
k(<\frac{3n(n+2)}{2(n+4)})
-nonnegative curvature operators of the second kind are either rational homology spheres or flat. Dai-Fu
[
8
]
first established a novel Bochner formula on Einstein manifolds. Based on this, they proved that any closed Einstein manifold of dimension
n=4
or
n=5
or
n\geq 8
with
[\frac{n+2}{4}]
-nonnegative curvature operator of the second kind is a constant curvature space. The Bochner formula can also be used on compact Riemannian manifolds with
k
-positive curvature operator of the first kind, the latest results can be found at
[
6
,
21
,
1
,
23
,
9
]
.

Recently, Li
[
17
]
studied cone conditions of the curvature operator of the second kind, which satisfies

{\alpha}^{-1}({\lambda}_{1}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{\alpha})\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda},

for its eigenvalue of curvature operator of the second kind. This condition is denote by
C(\alpha,\theta)
and
\mathring{C}\,(\alpha,\theta)
if the inequality is strict. Li proved that if
\mathring{R}\,\in C(\frac{n+2}{2},\theta)
for some
-1<\theta<\frac{2(n-1)}{n+2},
then
M
is either flat or a rational homology sphere.

Building on the work of Li
[
17
]
and Dai–Fu
[
8
]
, Cheng and Wang
[
5
]
relaxed the curvature assumption to a cone condition. They introduced a positive constant
\theta(n)
and proved that if an
n
-dimensional closed Einstein manifold (
n=4,5
, or
n\geq 8
) satisfies
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\geq-2\theta(n)\bar{\lambda}
, then it is either flat or a spherical space form. This condition is strictly weaker than
2
-nonnegativity, which corresponds to
\theta=0
.

In this paper, we investigate Einstein manifolds of dimension
n\geq 4
whose curvature operator of the second kind satisfies a cone condition that is strictly weaker than nonnegativity. Let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathring{R}\,
and
\bar{\lambda}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}}/{N}\;
their average. By applying results of Dai–Fu
[
8
]
and Li

]

.

Let

be an
n
-dimensional compact Einstein manifold. For

When

2.
notation and lemmas

Here, we introduce our notation and summarize key facts regarding the curvature operator of the second kind. Further details can be found in
[
15
,
17
,
19
,
8
,
5
]
.

Let
(V,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Euclidean vector space and
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
V
. We recall that the second-order tensor space can be decomposed by

T^{2}(V)={\Lambda}^{2}(V)\oplus S^{2}(V),

where
{\Lambda}^{2}(V)
and
S^{2}(V)
is the second-order anti-symmetric tensor space and the second order symmetric tensor space on
V
, respectively. The space
S^{2}(V)
can split subspaces as

S^{2}(V)=S_{0}^{2}(V)\oplus\mathbb{R}g,

where
S_{0}^{2}(V)
is the space of traceless symmetric two-tensors, and
g=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{e_{i}\otimes e_{i}}
. We always denote by
N=\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{2}
the dimension of
S_{0}^{2}(V)
in this paper.

The space of symmetric two-tensors on
{{\Lambda}^{2}}(V)
has the orthogonal decomposition

S^{2}({\Lambda}^{2}(V))=S_{B}^{2}({\Lambda}^{2}(V))\oplus{\Lambda}^{4}(V),

where
S_{B}^{2}({\Lambda}^{2}(V))
consists of all tensors
R\in S^{2}({\Lambda}^{2}(V))
that also satisfy the first Bianchi identity.
S_{B}^{2}({\Lambda}^{2}(V))
is called the space of algebraic curvature operators.
By the symmetries of
R\in S_{B}^{2}({{\Lambda}^{2}}(V))
, there are (up to sign) two natural ways in which induces a symmetric linear map from
T^{2}(V)
to
T^{2}(V)
.
The first one is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{R}:{{\Lambda}^{2}}(V)\to{{\Lambda}^{2}}(V),

\displaystyle\hat{R}({{e}_{i}}\wedge{{e}_{j}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l}{{{R}_{ijkl}}{{e}_{k}}\wedge{{e}_{l}}},

which is called the curvature operator of the first kind.
The second one denoted by
\bar{R}
, is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{R}:{{S}^{2}}(V)\to{{S}^{2}}(V),

\displaystyle\bar{R}({{e}_{i}}\odot{{e}_{j}})=\sum\limits_{k,l}{{{R}_{iklj}}{{e}_{k}}\odot{{e}_{l}}},

\bar{R}
induces a symmetric form
\mathring{R}\,
defined by

\displaystyle\mathring{R}\,:S_{0}^{2}(V)\times S_{0}^{2}(V)\to R

\displaystyle({{S}^{\alpha}},{{S}^{\beta}})\to{{R}_{ijkl}}S_{jk}^{\alpha}S_{il}^{\beta},

which is called curvature operator of the second kind. It was introduced by Nishikawa
[
20
]
and as pointed out in
[
19
]
, the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathring{R}\,
can also be interpreted as
\bar{R}
and its image restrict on
S_{0}^{2}(V)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. For each
p\in M,
let
V=T_{p}M
and
R
is denoted by the Riemannian curvature tensor of
M
.
\mathring{R}\,
is denoted by curvature operator of second kind of
M
. We introduce the following definition which comes from
[
19
]

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T^{(0,k)}(V)
denote the space of
(0,k)
-tensor space on
V
. For
S\in S^{2}(V)
and
T\in T^{(0,k)}(V),
we define

\displaystyle S:T^{(0,k)}(V)\to T^{(0,k)}(V)

\displaystyle(ST)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{T(X_{1},\cdots,SX_{i},\cdots,X_{k})}

and define
T^{S^{2}}\in T^{(0,k)}(V)\otimes S^{2}(V)
by

\left\langle T^{S^{2}}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}),S\right\rangle=(ST)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}).

Hence, if
\left\{{\tilde{S}}^{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
is an orthonormal basis for
S^{2}(V)
, then

T^{S^{2}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}{{\tilde{S}}^{i}T\otimes{\tilde{S}}^{i}}.

Similarly, we define
T^{S_{0}^{2}}\in T^{(0,k)}(V)\otimes S_{0}^{2}(V)
and for an orthonormal basis
\left\{S^{i}\right\}
of
S_{0}^{2}(V)

T^{S_{0}^{2}}=\sum\limits_{i}{S^{i}T\otimes S^{i}}.

Let
\{{\lambda}_{i}\}
denote the eigenvalues of the operator
\mathring{R}\,
and let
\left\{S^{i}\right\}
be the corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis of
S_{0}^{2}(V)
. In what follows, the eigenvalues
\{{\lambda}_{i}\}
are assumed to be arranged in non‑decreasing order. Define the action of
\mathring{R}\,
on the space
T^{S_{0}^{2}}
as

\mathring{R}\,(T^{S_{0}^{2}})=\sum\limits_{i}{S^{i}T\otimes\mathring{R}\,(S^{i}),}

then

\left\langle\mathring{R}\,(T^{S_{0}^{2}}),T^{S_{0}^{2}}\right\rangle=\sum\limits_{i}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}T\right|^{2}}.

Let
W
be the Weyl curvature tensor of
M
, Set
S=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{{\left|{{S}^{i}}W\right|}^{2}}}
,
M=\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\mathop{\max}}\,{{\left|{{S}^{i}}W\right|}^{2}}.
The following estimate holds for the cone condition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\lambda}_{1}\leq{\lambda}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq{\lambda}_{N}
with
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}}=N\bar{\lambda}.

Under the cone condition
k^{-1}({\lambda}_{1}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{k})\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda}
for
0<k\leq[\frac{S}{M}]
,
we have

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}\geq-S\theta\bar{\lambda}.

Proof.

First we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{k+1}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{\lambda}_{k+1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{({\lambda}_{i}-{\lambda}_{k+1})\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{k+1}S+M\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{({\lambda}_{i}-{\lambda}_{k+1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(S-kM){\lambda}_{k+1}+M\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i},

If
k^{-1}({\lambda}_{1}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{k})\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda}
, then

\displaystyle{{\lambda}_{k+1}}\geq\frac{{\lambda}_{1}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{k}}{k}\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda}.

(2.2)

Applying inequality (
2.2
) to the right-hand side of (
2.1
), we get for
0<k\leq[\frac{S}{M}],

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(S-kM){\lambda}_{k+1}+M\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}{\lambda}_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\theta\bar{\lambda}(S-kM)-Mk\theta\bar{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S\theta\bar{\lambda}.

∎

3.
proof of main theorem

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an Einstein manifold of dimension
n
. Denote by
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
the eigenvalues of the operator
\mathring{R}\,
and let
\bar{\lambda}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}}/{N}\;
be their average. From
[
15
]
, it follows that

tr(\mathring{R})=\frac{n+2}{2n}s,

where
s
is the scalar curvature of
M
. Consequently
\bar{\lambda}=\frac{s}{n(n-1)}
.

Based on the calculations presented in
[
8
]
, it has been calculated that

\displaystyle S=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{{\left|{{S}^{i}}W\right|}^{2}}}=\frac{2({{n}^{2}}+n-8)}{n}{{\left|W\right|}^{2}}=\frac{4N-12}{n}{{\left|W\right|}^{2}},

(3.1)

and

M=\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\mathop{\max}}\,{\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}=\frac{8n-16}{n}{{\left|W\right|}^{2}}.

Hence

[\frac{S}{M}]\geq[\frac{n+2}{4}].

Moreover, on Einstein manifold, the square norm of the Weyl curvature can be expressed as

\displaystyle{{\left|W\right|}^{2}}=\frac{4}{3}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}-\frac{4N}{3}{{\bar{\lambda}}^{2}}.

(3.2)

With these preparations we obtain the following estimate for the Bochner formula.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an Einstein manifold of dimension
n\geq 6
. If
k^{-1}({\lambda}_{1}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{k})\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda}
for
k\leq[\frac{n+2}{4}],\theta\geq 0
, then the curvature operator
R
of
M
satisfies

\displaystyle 3\left\langle\Delta R,R\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 16\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{3}}+\frac{16}{3n}[2N-12n+6-(N-3)\theta]\bar{\lambda}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{16N}{3n}[2N-9n+6-(N-3)\theta]\bar{\lambda}^{3}.

Proof.

The following Bochner formula comes from Proposition 3.4 in
[
8
]
.

\displaystyle 3\left\langle\Delta R,R\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}+8\left(\frac{-n^{3}+6n^{2}+12n-8}{3n^{4}(n-1)^{2}}\right)s^{3}

\displaystyle+8\left(\frac{2n^{2}-22n+8}{3n^{2}(n-1)}\right)s\sum\limits_{i}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+16\sum\limits_{i}{\lambda_{i}^{3}}.

Substituting
N=\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{2}
and
\bar{\lambda}=\frac{s}{n(n-1)}
, we simplify the equation to

\displaystyle 3\left\langle\Delta R,R\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{{\lambda}_{i}}{{\left|{{S}^{i}}W\right|}^{2}}}-\frac{16N(2N-9n+6)}{3n}{{{\bar{\lambda}}}^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{16(2N-12n+6)}{3n}\bar{\lambda}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+16\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{3}}.

Applying Proposition
2.2
, together with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}\left|S^{i}W\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{4N-12}{n}\theta\bar{\lambda}\left|W\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{16(N-3)}{3n}\theta\bar{\lambda}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}^{2}}+\frac{16N(N-3)}{3n}\theta\bar{\lambda}^{3}.

Combining the above two equations yields the desired result.
∎

.

When
k=2
, this is Lemma 3.3 of

To establish the main theorem, we present the following algebraic results, which play a crucial role in the proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0\leq{\lambda}_{1}\leq{\lambda}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq{\lambda}_{N}
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}}=C>0
. If
C=\frac{N(N-1)}{2N-1},
then the function

F(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{N})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{3}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}

attains its minimal at
\left(\frac{C}{N},\frac{C}{N},\ldots,\frac{C}{N}\right)
and
\left(0,\frac{C}{N-1},\ldots,\frac{C}{N-1}\right).

Proof.

We will divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1.
Consider
{\lambda}_{1}>0
. Under the constraints
\sum\limits_{i}{{{\lambda}_{i}}}=C>0
, Lagrange multipliers yields a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle 3\lambda_{i}^{2}-2{\lambda}_{i}+\mu=0

(3.4)

for
i=1,\ldots,N.

The possible solution of this equation is

a=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-3\mu}}{3},b=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-3\mu}}{3}.

Hence, the possible critical points are

Q_{k}=\left(\overset{k}{\mathop{\overset{\frown}{a,\ldots,a}}}\,,\overset{N-k}{\mathop{\overset{\frown}{b,\ldots,b}}}\,\right).

Under constrain
ka+(N-k)b=C
, we obtain

(N-2k)\sqrt{1-3\mu}=3C-N.

Because
C=\frac{N(N-1)}{2N-1}
implies
3C-N>0
, we must have
N-2k>0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{3}[1-{\left(\frac{3C-N}{N-2k}\right)^{2}}].

(3.5)

Substituting
Q_{k}
into
F
and applying the relation (
3.4
) to obtain

\displaystyle F(Q_{k})

\displaystyle=-\frac{C}{3}\mu-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{i}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C}{3}\mu-\frac{N(2-3\mu)+2(N-2k)\sqrt{1-3\mu}}{27}.

Together with (
3.5
) given

\displaystyle F(Q_{k})

\displaystyle=-\frac{C}{3}\frac{1}{3}[1-{{\left(\frac{3C-N}{N-2k}\right)}^{2}}]-\frac{N}{27}-\frac{N{{\left(\frac{3C-N}{N-2k}\right)}^{2}}+2(3C-N)}{27}

\displaystyle=(\frac{C}{9}-\frac{N}{27}){{\left(\frac{3C-N}{N-2k}\right)}^{2}}-\frac{C}{3}+\frac{N}{27}.

Since we assume
C=\frac{N(N-1)}{2N-1}>\frac{N}{3},
it follow that
\frac{C}{9}-\frac{N}{27}>0
. Hence

\displaystyle\min F(Q_{k})=\underset{0\leq k<{N}/{2}\;}{\mathop{\min}}\,\{(\frac{C}{9}-\frac{N}{27}){\left(\frac{3C-N}{N-2k}\right)^{2}}-\frac{C}{3}+\frac{N}{27}\}

\displaystyle=F(Q_{0}),

and the minimal point is
\left(\frac{C}{N},\frac{C}{N},\ldots,\frac{C}{N}\right).

Case 2
, Consider
{\lambda}_{1}=0
.
Suppose
X
be a minimizer such that it has the maximum number of zeros among all minimizers. We claim that the non-zero elements of
X
are all equal.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
{\lambda}_{i}>{\lambda}_{j}>0
for some
i,j
. If
{\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j}\leq\frac{2}{3},
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{3}-\lambda_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{j}^{3}-\lambda_{j}^{2}=({\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j})^{3}-({\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j})^{2}+{\lambda}_{i}{\lambda}_{j}(2-3{\lambda}_{i}-3{\lambda}_{j})

\displaystyle\geq({\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j})^{3}-({\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j})^{2}.

Thus, replacing
{\lambda}_{i},{\lambda}_{j}
with
{\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j},0
yields another minimizer. This contradicts the fact that
X
has the largest number of zeros.

If
{\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j}>\frac{2}{3},
letting
u=\frac{{\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j}}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{3}-\lambda_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{j}^{3}-\lambda_{j}^{2}=2(u^{3}-u^{2})+\frac{1}{4}(3{\lambda}_{i}+3{\lambda}_{j}-2)({\lambda}_{i}-{\lambda}_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle>2(u^{3}-u^{2}).

Thus replacing
{\lambda}_{i},{\lambda}_{j}
with
\frac{{\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j}}{2}
,
\frac{{\lambda}_{i}+{\lambda}_{j}}{2}
, yields a smaller minimizer, this contradicts the minimality of
X
. The claim is proved.

Through the above discussion, we can assume that for
X
,
0={\lambda}_{1}=\cdots={\lambda}_{k},
and
{\lambda}_{k+1}=\cdots={\lambda}_{N}
for some
1\leq k<N,
then

F(X)=\frac{C^{3}}{(N-k)^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{N-k}.

So in
\partial\Omega,

\underset{\partial\Omega}{\mathop{\min}}\,F=\underset{1\leq k<N}{\mathop{\min}}\,\frac{C^{3}}{(N-k)^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{N-k}.

Since we assume
C=\frac{N(N-1)}{2N-1},
we obtain

\frac{C^{3}}{(N-k)^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{N-k}-(\frac{C^{3}}{N^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{N})=\frac{{C^{2}}(N^{2}-(N-k)^{2})(C-\frac{N(N-k)}{2N-k})}{(N-k)^{2}N^{2}}\geq 0,

equality holds if and only if
k=1
. Hence,

\underset{\partial\Omega}{\mathop{\min}}\,F=\underset{1\leq k<N}{\mathop{\min}}\,\frac{C^{3}}{(N-k)^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{N-k}=\frac{C^{3}}{N^{2}}-\frac{C^{2}}{N},

and the minimal point is
\left(0,\frac{C}{N-1},\ldots,\frac{C}{N-1}\right)
. As
X
possesses at least one zero, it follows that the minimizer must be unique.

Combining case 1 and case 2, we have completed the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Remark 3.4

Lemma 3.5

Let

Proof.

Since
{{\lambda}_{i}}
is a non-decreasing sequence, and
k^{-1}({\lambda}_{1}+{\lambda}_{2}+\cdots+{\lambda}_{k})\geq-\theta\bar{\lambda}

{{\lambda}_{i_{1}}}+{{\lambda}_{i_{2}}}+\cdots+{{\lambda}_{i_{k}}}\geq-k\theta\bar{\lambda}\quad\text{for any}\quad 1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq N.

Among all such summation sequences, there are
\left(\begin{aligned} &N-1\\
&k-1\\
\end{aligned}\right)
of them that contain
{\lambda}_{1}
, summing over these sequences obtain

\left(\begin{aligned} &N-1\\
&k-1\\
\end{aligned}\right){{\lambda}_{1}}+\left(\begin{aligned} &N-2\\
&k-2\\
\end{aligned}\right)\sum\limits_{i=2}^{N}{{\lambda}_{i}}\geq-\left(\begin{aligned} &N-1\\
&k-1\\
\end{aligned}\right)k\theta\bar{\lambda}

since there are
\left(\begin{aligned} &N-2\\
&k-2\\
\end{aligned}\right)
sequences that include
{\lambda}_{1}
and
{\lambda}_{i},i\neq 1.
For
{{\lambda}_{1}}+{{\lambda}_{2}}+\cdots+{{\lambda}_{N}}=N\bar{\lambda}
, we finally get

\left[\left(\begin{aligned} &N-1\\
&k-1\\
\end{aligned}\right)-\left(\begin{aligned} &N-2\\
&k-2\\
\end{aligned}\right)\right]{{\lambda}_{1}}\geq-\left[\left(\begin{aligned} &N-1\\
&k-1\\

&k-2\\
\end{aligned}\right)\right]\bar{\lambda},

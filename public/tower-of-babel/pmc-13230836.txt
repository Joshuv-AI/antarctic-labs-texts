Evaluating patent assignee influence in artificial intelligence: a heterogeneous innovation network perspective
Authors: Xipeng Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractArtificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping the technological landscape, with patents serving as key indicators of innovation output and technological advancement. Patent assignees play a pivotal role in this process, as their influence reflects both their structural position and functional contributions within innovation ecosystems. However, traditional evaluation methods typically based on patent counts or citation metrics, are limited in capturing the structural complexity and dynamic interactions among innovation entities. To address these limitations, this study constructs a heterogeneous innovation network using AI-related patent data from China spanning 1985 to 2023, integrating multiply types of relationships between patents and assignees. We propose a novel evaluation algorithm—AIHIN, which leverages both the network’s topological structure and fine-grained node attribute features to enable a more comprehensive assessment of assignee influence. Empirical results demonstrate that AIHIN algorithm effectively identifies structurally central and temporally influential assignees in the evolution of AI technologies. This framework provides new insights into the dynamics of innovation within heterogeneous systems and offers methodological contributions to the network-based evaluation of knowledge production and diffusion.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Patent assignee, Heterogeneous innovation network, Influence evaluation, AIHIN algorithmSubject terms: Complex networks; Complex networks; Mathematics and computing; Science, technology and society

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a core driving force behind the new wave of technological revolution and industrial transformation, permeating various sectors and profoundly reshaping the global landscape of innovation. As a key indicator of innovation capability, patents encapsulate the cutting-edge progress of AI development. Among them, patent assignees serve as both creators of technological knowledge and key agents of technological diffusion, thereby playing a pivotal role in innovation systems. Their influence reflects not only their technological authority within the industry, but also their capacity to steer the trajectory of AI evolution. Therefore, systematically evaluating the influence of patent assignees is of both theoretical and practical significance for identifying key innovation actors, optimizing resource allocation, supporting strategic decisions in R&D and innovation policy.

Existing studies on patent and assignee influence evaluation have primarily relied on citation-based indicators, such as patent counts, citation counts, the h-index, and the g-index1–3. These metrics are widely used due to their simplicity and intuitive interpretability. However, they suffer from inherent temporal bias, cumulative advantage effects, and vulnerability to strategic self-citations, which limit their ability to capture the structural and dynamic dimensions of technological influence.4,5.

To overcome the limitations of pure citation counts, a growing body of research has adopted complex network approaches to evaluate influence in innovation systems. Typical methods include PageRank-based ranking of inventor, patent, or assignee networks6–8. These methods exploit topological positions to reflect structural influence. Nevertheless, most existing studies build homogeneous networks and fail to jointly model multiple types of entities and heterogeneous relationships within the patent system.

More recently, heterogeneous information networks (HINs) have been introduced to jointly model multi-type nodes and relationships in innovation analysis9–11. These studies demonstrate the potential of co-ranking and heterogeneous propagation for influence evaluation. However, existing HIN-based approaches often focus on inventors or publications and rarely incorporate assignee-level influence in combination with time-aware dynamics and node attribute features.

Taken together, existing approaches tend to rely on oversimplified citation-based indicators, insufficiently account for the structural heterogeneity of innovation systems, and fail to integrate time-aware dynamics and assignee-level attributes into a unified evaluation framework. While network-based and heterogeneous network methods have demonstrated their potential in influence assessment, a systematic approach that simultaneously models multi-type relationships, temporal evolution, and heterogeneous node features for assignee-level influence evaluation remains lacking.

To address these gaps, this study constructs a heterogeneous “patent–assignee” innovation network based on large-scale Chinese AI patent data as a representative application context, and proposes a novel influence evaluation algorithm, namely AIHIN (Assignee Influence evaluation algorithm based on the Heterogeneous Innovation Network, AIHIN). By integrating type-aware transition mechanisms, harmonic credit allocation, innovation capability weighting, and temporal decay into a unified random-walk framework, AIHIN enables a more accurate and robust assessment of assignee influence in complex innovation systems. This framework not only advances the methodological toolkit for network-based innovation evaluation but also provides a scalable analytical approach for identifying key technological actors in rapidly evolving technological domains.

In this study, “assignee influence” is conceptualized as a composite form of technological influence that combines structural, temporal, and diffusion-based dimensions. Specifically, it reflects an assignee’s capability to shape technological development through (i) the structural position of its patents in the citation network (structural influence), (ii) the sustained impact of its patents over time (temporal influence), and (iii) the extent to which its patented technologies are adopted and referenced by subsequent innovations (technological diffusion influence). Accordingly, the proposed AIHIN algorithm is not designed to measure strategic influence in a managerial or market-competitive sense, but rather to quantify technology-oriented influence within the innovation network. This definition aligns with prior network-based frameworks of scientific and technological influence, where influence is interpreted as a function of network centrality, knowledge diffusion, and time-aware citation dynamics.

Construction of the AIHIN algorithm

This study proposes a novel algorithm named AIHIN, which for evaluating the influence of patent assignees in innovation systems. The overall research framework is illustrated in Fig. 1 and consists of three main components: (1) Construction of a heterogeneous innovation network, which incorporates two types of nodes: patent assignees and patents, two types of edges: the ownership links between assignees and their patents, the citation links among patents. (2) Development of a weighted heterogeneous innovation network structure, in which a type-aware transition mechanism is introduced to assign differentiated transition probabilities across node types. (3) Computation of node features, including credit allocation scores, innovation scores for assignees, and temporal decay factors for patents. These features are integrated with the network’s topological structure to formulate the AIHIN algorithm, enabling more accurate and robust ranking of patent assignees based on their influence within the heterogeneous innovation network.

This modeling choice is grounded in the objective of evaluating assignee influence as technology-driven influence rather than collaboration-driven influence. This would bias the influence diffusion process toward densely connected assignee clusters rather than toward assignees with genuinely influential technological outputs. Therefore, excluding direct assignee–assignee edges helps prevent structural bias and ensures that the propagated influence primarily reflects knowledge diffusion through patented technologies. In line with the assumption that assignee influence should primarily derive from the the quality of patents rather than from direct collaboration ties, our model deliberately omits assignee-assignee edges. This preserves the interpretability and structural balance of the HIN while ensuring that influence propagation is driven by knowledge flow through patents.

Calculation of node features and construction of the ranking algorithm

Building upon the weighted HIN, this study incorporates three categories of node-level features and integrates them into a modified PageRank model to construct the AIHIN ranking algorithm, aiming to more accurately evaluate the influence of patent assignees.

Node features

Credit allocation score The harmonic allocation scheme has been widely adopted in credit assignment problems to reflect diminishing marginal contributions of secondary participants, as it balances fairness and positional importance12. Compared with equal or fractional counting, harmonic allocation better captures hierarchical contribution structures in collaborative outputs. To examine the robustness of this choice, we conducted additional sensitivity checks using equal-share and fractional allocation schemes. The relative ranking patterns of top assignees remained highly consistent across different schemes, indicating that the proposed AIHIN results are not driven by a specific credit allocation rule.

Universities and research institutes primarily focus on frontier knowledge creation, enterprises emphasize technology commercialization and application, while individuals typically operate with more limited organizational resources. Theoretical assumptions

An assignee’s influence score is determined by the aggregated importance scores of its associated patents. This reflects the idea that the reputation and influence of an assignee stem from the quality and significance of its technological output.

AIHIN

The integrated influence propagation mechanism in AIHIN follows a multiplicative modulation framework rather than a linear additive weighting scheme. Specifically, node features (credit allocation and innovation scores), type-aware transitions, and temporal decay jointly modulate the random-walk probability through normalized multiplicative terms. This design ensures that no single component dominates the influence propagation process in isolation. Moreover, the stochastic transition matrix in AIHIN remains column-normalized, and the damping factor preserves the irreducibility and aperiodicity properties of the underlying Markov chain. Therefore, the convergence of the iterative influence scores is theoretically guaranteed, similar to classical PageRank. In practice, we also observe stable convergence within the preset iteration threshold across all experiments.

Data and methodology

AI patent dataset

AI as a frontier of technological innovation, which plays a critical role in enhancing national scientific strength and global competitiveness. To systematically assess the innovation landscape and evaluate the influence of patent assignee in the Chinese AI domain, this study constructs a comprehensive AI patent dataset covering the period from 1985 to 2023.

The raw patent data were obtained from the China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA). Previous studies have demonstrated that granted patents more accurately reflect technological maturity and innovation quality, and are thus widely used in assignee-level evaluation16,17. To identify AI-related patents, we followed the classification index developed by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and extracted relevant records by matching corresponding IPC codes. Citation information for each patent was further supplemented using data from the Google Patents platform to enhance completeness.

To ensure accuracy and the reliability of subsequent network analysis, the following preprocessing steps were performed:

Patent citation cleaning ① A list of granted AI patent IDs was compiled, and only citation links where both the citing and cited patents appeared in this list were retained; ② Self-citations (i.e., instances where a patent cites itself in application and granted stage) were removed; ③ Invalid citations were eliminated, such as those with citation dates earlier than the application dates of the cited patents.

Evaluation indicators

Future non-self citation counts are adopted as the benchmark ground truth because forward citations are widely recognized as a proxy for knowledge diffusion, technological impact, and follow-on innovation, while excluding self-citations helps reduce strategic reference bias. Although citation behavior may exhibit field-dependence and life-cycle effects, this bias is mitigated in our study since all evaluations are conducted within a unified AI patent corpus and against the same benchmark. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that citation-based ground truth may structurally favor fast-evolving technologies and assignees with larger patent portfolios.

To assess the effectiveness of different ranking algorithms in evaluating assignee influence, we focus primarily on the top-ranked entities and employ the following evaluation indicators.

Accuracy It is measured by the overlap between the top-k assignees identified by the algorithm and those ranked highest based on future citation counts over the subsequent five years (referred to as the FutCit ground truth). A higher number of shared entities in the top-k lists indicates better alignment with future innovation performance and, hence, greater ranking accuracy.

Accuracy It is measured by the overlap between the top-k assignees identified by the algorithm and those ranked highest based on future citation counts over the subsequent five years (referred to as the FutCit ground truth). A higher number of shared entities in the top-k lists indicates better alignment with future innovation performance and, hence, greater ranking accuracy.

Normalized discounted cumulative gain (NDCG) It is a widely used indicator for evaluating the quality of ranking algorithms. Unlike Accuracy, which only considers the presence of correct entities in the top-k results, NDCG additionally accounts for their ranking order, assigning higher weights to top-ranked entities. A higher NDCG score implies that highly influential assignees appear earlier in the ranking list, which better reflects algorithmic precision.

The area under the PR curve (AUC) is adopted to quantify the algorithm’s overall ability to distinguish high-impact assignees from less influential ones.

Experimental results and analysis

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm, the AI patent dataset was divided into a training set and a test set: Patents granted between 1985 and 2018 were used for model training, while patents from 2019 to 2023 constituted the test set. Within the test set, the influence score of each assignee was calculated as the total number of non-self citations received by all of their patents. The resulting ranking was adopted as the FutCit benchmark, serving as the ground truth for performance evaluation. Given that the test set includes a total of 7,156 assignees, we focus on the top 500 assignees for comparative analysis. Using three evaluation indicators: Accuracy, NDCG, and PR curve, the experimental results show that the proposed AIHIN algorithm consistently outperforms all competing methods across all evaluation dimensions.

The reciprocal citations problem in PN

A patent citation network comprises only patent nodes and their citation relationships, as illustrated in Fig. 6a. Many studies evaluate assignee influence by calculating the PageRank scores of patents within the network and subsequently aggregating them to the assignee level11,21. However, this approach suffers from a key limitation: reciprocal citations among patents, especially those filed by the same assignee within a short time frame. When the PageRank is applied, these reciprocal links can form closed loops, leading to inflated scores for the involved patents and, consequently, distorted evaluations of assignee influence. To mitigate this issue, we introduce assignee nodes into the PN, thereby breaking closed-loop citation structures formed solely among patent nodes (see Fig. 6b). The resulting WAPN reduces the impact of reciprocal citations and yields more accurate importance scores.

As a result, the rankings of assignees become more consistent with their actual innovation influence. Through the above analysis, it becomes evident that constructing AN or PN introduces specific structural biases—namely, self-citation and reciprocal citation effects, respectively. Applying the PageRank algorithm directly to such homogeneous networks may lead to distorted assessments of assignee influence. In contrast, the proposed weighted heterogeneous innovation network effectively addresses these limitations by incorporating node-type distinctions and type-aware transitions. The resulting influence rankings are thus more robust, reliable, and applicable across diverse analytical contexts.

Discussion

Mechanism interpretation of the superior performance of AIHIN

The superior performance of the proposed AIHIN algorithm can be primarily attributed to three integrated mechanisms. First, by embedding assignee–patent ownership relationships into the citation network, AIHIN enables bidirectional influence propagation between patents and assignees. This mutual reinforcement mechanism allows influential assignees to enhance patent-level impact while highly cited patents also elevate assignee influence, which cannot be captured by conventional citation-based or homogeneous network methods.

Second, the type-aware transition mechanism effectively differentiates the structural roles of heterogeneous nodes and avoids the structural bias caused by dense intra-type connections. As demonstrated in the comparison between homogeneous and heterogeneous networks, self-citation loops and reciprocal citation structures can significantly distort PageRank-based influence diffusion. The proposed heterogeneous framework breaks such closed loops and ensures that propagated influence primarily reflects knowledge diffusion through patented technologies.

Third, the integration of harmonic credit allocation, innovation capability weighting, and temporal decay enables AIHIN to jointly account for collaborative contribution structures, institutional heterogeneity, and time-sensitive influence evolution. This multiplicative modulation design prevents any single factor from dominating the ranking process and ensures both interpretability and stability of influence propagation. Together, these mechanisms explain why AIHIN achieves consistently higher Accuracy, NDCG, and AUC values across both static and dynamic evaluation settings.

Comparison with existing studies and methodological positioning

Existing studies on patent and assignee influence evaluation mainly fall into three categories: citation-based metrics, homogeneous network ranking methods, and heterogeneous network–based co-ranking approaches. Citation-based indicators such as patent counts and citation counts capture innovation output scale and diffusion breadth but fail to incorporate structural dependencies among innovation entities. Homogeneous network approaches based on PageRank improve structural modeling but still suffer from self-citation and reciprocal citation bias.

Compared with prior heterogeneous information network–based studies, the proposed AIHIN framework makes three methodological advancements. First, it explicitly focuses on assignee-level influence rather than inventors or publications. Second, it incorporates time-aware dynamics and heterogeneous node attributes into a unified influence propagation framework. Third, it introduces credit allocation and innovation capability weighting, which enhance interpretability and realism of influence accumulation.

Therefore, AIHIN extends existing network-based innovation evaluation methods from purely structural ranking toward a more comprehensive influence modeling framework that integrates structure, time, and heterogeneous agent attributes.

Implications for innovation analysis and policy

From an analytical perspective, AIHIN provides a scalable tool for identifying core technological actors in rapidly evolving innovation systems. By capturing both structural centrality and temporal diffusion dynamics, the algorithm enables early identification of emerging high-impact assignees, as demonstrated by the rising-star analysis.

From a policy perspective, the proposed framework offers practical insights for innovation governance and R&D resource allocation. Policymakers and funding agencies may use such influence rankings to identify strategic technology leaders, support high-potential innovators, and monitor the evolution of technological dominance. For enterprises and research institutions, AIHIN-based evaluation can support technology positioning, competitive benchmarking, and long-term innovation planning.

Conclusions

This study proposes a novel algorithm AIHIN, to evaluate the influence of patent assignees by leveraging network structural heterogeneity and incorporating fine-grained node-level features. By constructing a heterogeneous innovation network that integrates patent citation and ownership relationships, AIHIN algorithm addresses critical limitations of traditional citation-based approaches. Empirical analysis using Chinese AI-related patent data from 1985 to 2023 demonstrates that AIHIN algorithm consistently achieves superior accuracy and robustness in identifying influential assignees. These findings highlight the algorithm’s effectiveness in capturing substantive influence within the evolving AI innovation ecosystem. This proposed method offers a new analytical framework for evaluating assignee influence from a complex network perspective, contributing to the growing literature on innovation metrics and network science. It also provides valuable insights for enterprises, research institutions, and policymakers involved in strategic decision-making and R&D investment in emerging technologies. Although the empirical validation in this study is conducted using Chinese AI patent data, the proposed AIHIN framework is fundamentally grounded in general network structures and universal mechanisms of knowledge diffusion, credit allocation, and time-aware influence propagation. These mechanisms are not specific to any single national patent system and are widely observed in innovation networks across different institutional environments. Therefore, in principle, AIHIN is expected to maintain its relative effectiveness in other technological fields and national patent systems, provided that patent citation and ownership information is available. However, variations in citation practices, examination standards, and institutional settings may affect the absolute magnitude and distribution of influence scores, which calls for further cross-country validation.

Despite its advantages, this study has several limitations that warrant future exploration: (1) Scope of network construction. The current model includes only patents and assignees, excluding other potentially influential entities such as inventors or technological domains. Incorporating these dimensions could enhance the comprehensiveness of the network. (2) Limited attribute diversity. Only three node-level features are considered. Future research may explore additional variables, such as patent market value, technological maturity or strategic positioning, to further improve explanatory power22,23. (3) Lack of unified evaluation standard. There is no widely accepted benchmark for defining and measuring assignee impact. Although the proposed algorithm shows empirical effectiveness, its applicability across fields and contexts required further validation. Future research should aim to establish standardized, multidimensional evaluation frameworks adaptable to various innovation scenarios. In addition, the citation-based ground truth adopted in this study may structurally favor technologies with shorter innovation cycles and assignees with larger patent portfolios. Future research may therefore incorporate market-based, commercialization-oriented, or licensing-related indicators to construct multi-dimensional evaluation benchmarks for a more comprehensive assessment of assignee influence. (4) Another limitation of this study is that the empirical analysis is based solely on Chinese AI patents. Although the AIHIN algorithm is designed as a general network-based framework, its performance has not yet been systematically validated across different national patent systems or technological domains. Differences in citation cultures, patenting behaviors, and institutional environments may influence the algorithm’s performance. Future work will therefore further examine the transferability and robustness of AIHIN using multi-country and multi-technology patent datasets.

Characterization of continuous stationary fields as generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck fields via multi-parameter Langevin equation and multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integration
Marko Voutilainen, Pauliina Ilmonen, Lauri Viitasaari

Abstract
. In this article, we characterize continuous stationary fields via generalized Langevin dynamics. This gives natural connections between stationary fields, stationary increment fields, self-similar fields, and generalized Langevin dynamics. Our contribution extends some recently proved similar results for stochastic processes to the case of continuous random fields. As a by-product, we introduce some new results on multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals.

60G05, 60G60, 60G10, 60G18, 26B99

1.
Introduction

Random phenomena evolving in time are often modeled using stochastic processes
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in T}
, where
T
is a subset of the one-dimensional Euclidean space, and the role of modeling is to assess the statistical characteristics of the process
X
. Important special classes of such processes include stationary processes, self-similar processes, and processes with stationary increments, as each of these classes allows powerful tools for modeling and understanding random phenomena across diverse fields. Indeed, stationary processes model phenomena where statistical properties remain unchanged over time, self-similar processes model phenomena where statistical characteristics are consistent across different scales (for details on self-similar processes, see the monograph
[
5
]
), and stationary increment processes are particularly suitable models, for example, when considering random movements.

Connections between the three classes, stationary processes, self-similar processes, and processes with stationary increments, are already rather well-understood in the literature. For the first results relating different classes, Lamperti proved in
[
9
]
that there is a one-to-one correspondence between stationary processes and self-similar processes, and the underlying bijection is nowadays known as the Lamperti transformation. Later, stationary increment processes were connected in
[
17
]
, where (continuous) stationary processes in continuous time were characterized as solutions to the Langevin equation

(1)

dX(t)=-\theta X(t)dt+dG(t),

where
G
is a suitable process with stationary increments, and the stochastic differential equation can be understood via integration in the Riemann-Stieltjes sense. Equation (
1
) gives rise to generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, links all three classes intimately together, and one can rather freely move from one class to another with suitable transformations. Such results have later been extended to discrete time
[
20
]
and to vector-valued processes both in continuous time
[
19
]
and in discrete time
[
22
]
.

The features mentioned above, such as consistency of statistical properties over time or across different scales naturally appear also in the case of random fields, where the parameter
t
of the process
\{X(t)\}_{t\in T}
is multivariate. In this context, stationarity is still naturally defined, whereas the concepts of self-similarity and stationarity of increments are more complex. Increments of random fields are typically defined through rectangular increments, which capture changes related to all directions of the parameter space
T
simultaneously. For literature related to stationarity of (rectangular) increments, see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
]
and the references therein. A version of the Lamperti theorem for random fields was established in
[
6
]
, with self-similarity defined componentwise, allowing anisotropic scaling properties required in certain applications. For more details, see e.g.
[
1
]
, where the authors adapt an alternative approach to anisotropy via the so-called operator scaling stable random fields.
For results connecting all three classes of fields via Langevin-type dynamics, we can only mention
[
21
]
studying fields indexed by
\mathbb{N}^{N}
and taking values in
\mathbb{R}
, and later extensions in
[
18
]
to cover fields indexed by
\mathbb{N}^{N}
and taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The main results of these articles provide natural dynamics, similar to the process case
[
20
]
, linking all three classes.

In this article, we extend the results of
[
21
]
to fields
\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. Compared to
[
21
]
, the main difficulty is that differences are replaced by differentials, and the notion of Riemann-Stieltjes integrals is much more subtle in the multivariate case. In particular, for our purpose, we need to extend the notion of multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals over non-hyperrectangles that are non-classical domains. Some of our main contributions include several results on multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals, which are particularly well-suited to our framework. On top of that, we provide an approach to define multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals over certain non-classical domains in situations where one cannot simply apply zero-extensions of functions and use a more classical approach. With this at hand, we provide natural transformations between stationary fields, fields with stationary rectangular increments, and self-similar fields, cf. Theorem
2.7
, Theorem
4.3
, and Corollary
4.4
. We introduce a generalization of the Langevin dynamics (
1
) (Definition
2.21
) and, through the transformations, characterize stationary fields as solutions to these dynamics; cf. Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
4.6
(see also Theorem
4.10
), providing a natural extension of the main result of
[
16
]
from random processes to random fields. We also note that when the generalization of (
1
) is driven by anisotropic fractional Brownian sheets, the resulting fields can naturally be termed fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck fields (of the first kind).

Let us also briefly discuss the existing literature on multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integration. In the univariate case, it is well-known that the Riemann-Stieltjes integral
\int f(s)dg(s)
exists provided that one of the functions
f
and
g
is continuous and the other is of bounded variation. Multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals (over standard hyperrectangles) are well-studied in the literature, with a particular focus on the bivariate case. For details, we refer to the monographs
[
14
,
8
]
. In the multivariate case, the assumption on bounded variation is commonly replaced by bounded variation in the sense of Hardy-Krause, originally introduced in
[
7
]
. Different variations in the bivariate case are studied in
[
2
]
. We also cite more recent works
[
12
,
13
]
considering the general multivariate setting.

The remainder of the article is organized as follows. We begin with necessary preliminaries on random fields and on multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals in Section
2
. In Section
3
we provide our results related to multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals. In particular, we provide an approach to define integrals over hypertriangles that is suitable for our purposes. This allows us to study connections between the three classes of random fields and the generalized Langevin equation in Section
4
.

. We use the notation

to pick the elements of

we denote an

if

Example 2.1
.

Let

The notation

stands for

can be defined accordingly. The mixed partial derivative of an

corresponds to the increment of

on the subrectangle

. Note that
\left[f(r)\right]_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}
is a function of

.
We will return to this in Definition

2.1.
Stationarity, self-similarity, and stationarity of increments

Next, we present the definitions of the three classes of random fields, together with the associated Lamperti theorem.

Definition 2.3
.

A random field
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is stationary if

\{X(t+s)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overset{\text{law}}{=}\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the sense of finite-dimensional distributions.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and let

Y=\{Y(e^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}=\{Y(e^{t_{1}},\dots,e^{t_{N}})\}_{(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}

be a random field. If

\{Y(e^{t+s})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overset{\text{law}}{=}\{e^{\Theta\cdot s}Y(e^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then
Y
is
\Theta
-self-similar.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
, and let
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
and
Y=\{Y(e^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be random fields. The Lamperti transformation
\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}
and its inverse
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\Theta}
are defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}X)(e^{t})

\displaystyle=e^{\Theta\cdot t}X(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\Theta}Y)(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}Y(e^{t}),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Remark 2.6
.

By a simple change of variables, Definitions
2.4
and
2.5
can be shown to be equivalent to the corresponding definitions in
[
6
]
.

For a proof of the next theorem, we refer to
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
. If
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is stationary, then
\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}X
is
\Theta
-self-similar. Conversely, if
Y=\{Y(e^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is
\Theta
-self-similar, then
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\Theta}Y
is stationary.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a random field. Let
t,s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be such that
s_{l}\leq t_{l}
for every
1\leq l\leq N
. Then, the corresponding rectangular increment is

(2)

\begin{split}\left[X\right]_{s}^{t}&=\left[X(r)\right]_{s}^{t}=\sum_{v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}X(s_{v}:t_{-v})\\
&=\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\in\{0,1\}^{N}}(-1)^{\sum_{l=1}^{N}i_{l}}X(t_{1}-i_{1}(t_{1}-s_{1}),\dots,t_{N}-i_{N}(t_{N}-s_{N})).\\
\end{split}

Remark 2.9
.

Degenerate increments, i.e. increments with
t_{l}=s_{l}
for some
l
, equal to zero.

Remark 2.10
.

Definition
2.8
extends in a straightforward and intuitive way to cases where restriction
t\geq s
does not hold. Let
m
be the number of indices
l
for which
t_{l}<s_{l}
and let
\hat{t}
and
\hat{s}
denote the vectors, where elements
t_{l}
and
s_{l}
have been swapped so that
\hat{t}\geq\hat{s}
is satisfied. Then

[X]_{s}^{t}=(-1)^{m}[X]_{\hat{s}}^{\hat{t}}.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a random field. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let

h,t^{(1)},\dots,t^{(n)},s^{(1)},\dots,s^{(n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

be arbitrary. If

\begin{pmatrix}[X]_{s^{(1)}+h}^{t^{(1)}+h}\\
\vdots\\
[X]_{s^{(n)}+h}^{t^{(n)}+h}\end{pmatrix}\overset{\text{law}}{=}\begin{pmatrix}[X]_{s^{(1)}}^{t^{(1)}}\\
\vdots\\
[X]_{s^{(n)}}^{t^{(n)}}\end{pmatrix},

then
X
is a stationary increment field.

Remark 2.12
.

It can be shown that Definition
2.11
is equivalent to

(3)

\{[X]_{h}^{t+h}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overset{\text{law}}{=}\{[X]_{0}^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\quad\text{for all }h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Furthermore, (
3
) is the adapted version of the definition of stationarity of increments discussed, e.g., in
[
11
]
.

So far, we have not posed assumptions related to the regularity of the sample functions of our fields. As we next proceed to multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integration, we make a standing assumption that the realizations are almost surely continuous. This ensures the existence of appropriate integrals in the given sense.

2.2.
Multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals

Assume that
N
-variate functions
f
and
g
are defined on hyperrectangle
[s,t]
, where
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
s\leq t
component-wise. Let
s_{m}=x_{0}^{(m)}<x_{1}^{(m)}<\dots<x_{n_{m}}^{(m)}=t_{m}
be a partition of the one-dimensional interval
[s_{m},t_{m}]
, where
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. The norm of such a partition
P_{m}
is

\|P_{m}\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n_{m}}\{|x^{(m)}_{i}-x^{(m)}_{i-1}|\}.

The one-dimensional partitions define a division of the rectangle
[s,t]
into subrectangles or cells

I_{i}=I_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}\coloneqq[(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}-1},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}-1}),(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})],

where
i_{m}\in\{1,\dots,n_{m}\}
.
The norm of this rectangular partition
P
is

\|P\|=\max_{1\leq m\leq N}\{\|P_{m}\|\}.

Let
\xi_{i}=\xi_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}=(\xi^{(1)}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}},\dots,\xi^{(N)}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}})
be any
N
-vectors satisfying
\xi_{i}\in I_{i}
that we call tags. If

(4)

\lim_{\|P\|\to 0}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{i_{N}=1}^{n_{N}}g(\xi_{i})[f]_{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}-1},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}-1})}^{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})}

exists as a finite number, then we call it the (unrestricted) Riemann-Stieltjes integral of
g
with respect to
f
. We note that if the
tags are of the form
\xi_{i}=(\xi^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,\xi^{(N)}_{i_{N}})
, then the integral is known as the restricted Riemann-Stieltjes integral in the literature (for details in the bivariate case, see e.g.
[
14
]
). Throughout, in our setting, we consider unrestricted Riemann-Stieltjes integrals. Moreover, we denote the integral (
4
) as

\int_{s_{1}}^{t_{1}}\dots\int_{s_{N}}^{t_{N}}g(u)d_{\{1,\dots,N\}}f(u)=\int_{s}^{t}g(u)df(u)=\int_{s}^{t}gdf.

Note that due to the alternating sign in (
2
),

\int_{s}^{t}df=[f]_{s}^{t}

for all functions
f
.
Assume that a partition
P
is fixed and let
\Delta_{j}
be the difference operator in coordinate
j
defined as

\begin{split}\Delta_{j}f(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})&=f(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(j-1)}_{i_{j-1}},x^{(j)}_{i_{j}},x^{(j+1)}_{i_{j+1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})\\
&\quad-f(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(j-1)}_{i_{j-1}},x^{(j)}_{i_{j}-1},x^{(j+1)}_{i_{j+1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}}).\end{split}

Then

(5)

[f]_{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}-1},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}-1})}^{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})}=\Delta_{1}\dots\Delta_{N}f(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})\eqqcolon\Delta f(x_{i}).

For
v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
, we extend previously set notation to comprehend
d_{v}f
and
du_{v}
. The former denotes the multivariate Riemann-Stieltjes differential over the coordinates in
v
and the latter denotes the usual multivariate Riemann or Lebesgue differential over the coordinates in
v
. Naturally, these notations are applied in the relation of integration. For example,

\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)d_{v}f(r)]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}

stands for evaluating the increment of
g(r)d_{v}f(r)
on the subrectangle
[s_{-v},t_{-v}]
. This results in
2^{|-v|}

|v|
-variate Riemann-Stieltjes integrals of
g
with respect to
f
over the coordinates
r_{v}
.

Next, we consider variations of multivariate functions. Let
P
be a partition of the interval
[s,t]
as described above. The variation of
f
over
P
is

V_{P}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{i_{N}=1}^{n_{N}}\left|[f]_{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}-1},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}-1})}^{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})}\right|.

We denote the set of all possible partitions of a fixed interval
[s,t]
with
\mathcal{P}
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
f
be defined on
[s,t]
. The variation of

on
[s,t]
in the sense of Vitali is

V(f,[s,t])\coloneqq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}V_{P}(f).

If
V(f,[s,t])<\infty
, we denote
f\in BV[s,t]
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
f
be defined on
[s,t]
. The variation of

on
[s,t]
in the sense of Hardy and Krause is

V_{HK}(f,[s,t])\coloneqq\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}V(f(r_{v}:t_{-v}),[s_{v},t_{v}]).

If
V_{HK}(f,[s,t])<\infty
, we denote
f\in BVHK[s,t]
.

If the rectangular domain
[s,t]
is clear from the context, we may simply write

and

Lemma 2.18
.

Let

[s,t]

Let

Definition 2.21

(Langevin equation)
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and let
G=\{G(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. The corresponding Langevin differential equation is

(8)

\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}d_{-u}X(t)dt_{u}=dG(t).

Example 2.22
.

In the case

d_{\{1,2\}}X(t)+\theta_{1}d_{2}X(t)dt_{1}+\theta_{2}d_{1}X(t)dt_{2}+\theta_{1}\theta_{2}X(t)dt_{1}dt_{2}=d_{\{1,2\}}G(t).

.

Remark 2.24

We understand Equation (

Example 2.25
.

In the 3-dimensional case, the term
d_{\{2,3\}}X(t)dt_{1}
gives

\int_{0}^{t}d_{\{2,3\}}X(s)ds_{1}=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{3}}d_{\{2,3\}}X(s)ds_{1},

where the inner integral is a double Riemann-Stieltjes integral over the last two coordinates and the outer integral is a usual Riemann or Lebesgue integral. Furthermore, we get

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{3}}d_{\{2,3\}}X(s)ds_{1}=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}[X(s)]_{0_{\{2,3\}}}^{t_{\{2,3\}}}ds_{1}\\
&=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}X(s_{1},t_{2},t_{3})-X(s_{1},t_{2},0)-X(s_{1},0,t_{3})+X(s_{1},0,0)ds_{1}.\end{split}

Definition 2.26
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and
G=\{G(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a stationary increment field. If

\lim_{s\to\infty}\int_{-s}^{t_{1}}\dots\int_{-s}^{t_{N}}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)

converges in probability and defines an almost surely finite random variable for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then we denote
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta}
. In this case, the limit is denoted by
\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)
.

Furthermore, if
G(t)=0
for all
t
such that
\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}=0
, then we write
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
. We define an equivalence relation in
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
as follows. Let
G,\tilde{G}\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}.
If
\tilde{G}(t)=G(t)+F(t)
, where

F(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|v|\neq N\end{subarray}}f^{(v)}(t_{v})

and
F(t)=0
for all
t
such that
\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}=0
, then
\tilde{G}\sim G
, and we write
\tilde{G}=G
.

Remark 2.27
.

Notice that
dF(t)=0
, since every term in the defining sum depends only on a subset
t_{v}
of all variables
t
. This together with
F(t)=0
for all
t
such that
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0
ensures that
\tilde{G}(t)=G(t)+F(t)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}.

Remark 2.28
.

Since
G
is continuous and
e^{\Theta\cdot u}
is, by Lemma
2.17
, of bounded variation in the sense of Hardy and Krause, by Lemmas
2.18
and
2.20
, the integral

\int_{s}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)

exists for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

3.
On multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals

In this section we present our results concerning multiple Riemann-Stieltjes integrals. We begin with the product rule and Radon-Nikodym type results in Section
3.1
. The main difficulty in obtaining our characterizations, provided in Section
4
, is to define Riemann-Stieltjes integrals over hypertriangles in a suitable way. This is the topic of Section
3.2
.

3.1.
On integration over hyperrectangles

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
be a function such that
f_{t}
exists and is continuous on
[s,t]
. Then

d_{u}f(r)=f_{t_{u}}(r)dr_{u}\quad\text{for all }u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}

in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense for every continuous integrand
g
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
u=\{1,\dots,N\}
. The result for lower dimensional differentials follow by fixing variables
r_{-u}
and considering
f
as a
|u|
-variate function. Since the integrand
g
is continuous, both integrals, Riemann-Stieltjes on the left and Riemann on the right, exist. Denote by

\left|I_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}\right|=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(x_{i_{j}}^{(N)}-x_{i_{j}-1}^{(N)})

the Lebesgue measure of
I_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}
. We examine a summand of the Riemann-Stieltjes sum (
4
). By the mean value theorem

\begin{split}g(\xi_{i})[f]_{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}-1},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}-1})}^{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})}=g(\xi_{i})f_{t}(\eta_{i})\left|I_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}\right|\end{split}

with some
\eta_{i}\in I_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}
.
Since
g
is Riemann-Stieltjes integrable with respect to
f
, we may select
\xi_{i}=\eta_{i}

yielding

\begin{split}&\lim_{\|P\|\to 0}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{i_{N}=1}^{n_{N}}g(\xi_{i})[f]_{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}-1},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}-1})}^{(x^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\dots,x^{(N)}_{i_{N}})}\\
&=\lim_{\|P\|\to 0}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\dots\sum_{i_{N}=1}^{n_{N}}g(\eta_{i})f_{t}(\eta_{i})\left|I_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N}}\right|,\end{split}

where the right-hand side converges to the Riemann integral of
gf_{t}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
. Let
f
be such that
f_{t_{v}}
is continuous on
[s,t]
. Then

(9)

df(r)=d_{-v}f_{t_{v}}(r)dr_{v}

in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense for every integrand
g
such that
g_{t}
is continuous.

Proof.

The statement obviously holds for
v=\emptyset
. In addition, by Theorem
3.1
, it holds also for
v=\{1,\dots,N\}.
Hence, we may exclude these cases in the proof. For other choices of
v
, the right-hand side of (
9
) reads

(10)

\begin{split}\int_{s}^{t}g(r)d_{-v}f_{t_{v}}(r)dr_{v}&=\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\int_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}g(r)d_{-v}f_{t_{v}}(r)dr_{v}\\
&=\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\sum_{u\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|u|}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(r)d_{u}g(r)\right]_{s_{-v-u}}^{t_{-v-u}}dr_{v}\\
&=\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\sum_{u\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|u|}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(r)g_{t_{u}}(r)dr_{u}\right]_{s_{-v-u}}^{t_{-v-u}}dr_{v}\\
&=\sum_{u\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|u|}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(r)g_{t_{u}}(r)dr_{v}\right]_{s_{-v-u}}^{t_{-v-u}}dr_{u}\end{split}

by integration by parts, Theorem
3.1
and Fubini’s theorem. Next, we show via induction that

\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(r)g_{t_{u}}(r)dr_{v}\right]_{s_{-v-u}}^{t_{-v-u}}=\sum_{w\subseteq v}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\left[f(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w}}^{t_{-u-w}}dr_{w}.

For this, we assume, without loss of generality, that
v=\{1,\dots,|v|\}.
In addition, we denote
v^{(n)}=\{1,\dots,|v|-n\}
so that
v^{(0)}=v
. We pose the induction hypothesis

(11)

\begin{split}&\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(r)g_{t_{u}}(r)dr_{v}\right]_{s_{-v-u}}^{t_{-v-u}}\\
&=\int_{s_{v^{(n)}}}^{t_{v^{(n)}}}\sum_{w\subseteq v-v^{(n)}}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\left[f_{t_{v^{(n)}}}(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}^{t_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}dr_{w}dr_{v^{(n)}},\end{split}

which a priori holds for
n=0
, giving also the base case. Next, we proceed to the induction step. We apply integration by parts coordinate by coordinate together with Fubini’s theorem, starting from
r_{|v|-n}.
The approach yields that (
11
) is equal to

\begin{split}&\int_{s_{v^{(n-1)}}}^{t_{v^{(n-1)}}}\sum_{w\subseteq v-v^{(n)}}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\int_{s_{|v|-n}}^{t_{|v|-n}}\left[f_{t_{v^{(n)}}}(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}^{t_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}dr_{|v|-n}dr_{w}dr_{v^{(n-1)}},\end{split}

from which integration by parts gives

\begin{split}&\sum_{w\subseteq v-v^{(n)}}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\int_{s_{|v|-n}}^{t_{|v|-n}}\left[f_{t_{v^{(n)}}}(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}^{t_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}dr_{|v|-n}dr_{w}\\
&=\sum_{w\subseteq v-v^{(n)}}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\left[f_{t_{{v^{(n-1)}}}}(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w-{v^{(n-1)}}}}^{t_{-u-w-{v^{(n-1)}}}}dr_{w}\\
&\quad-\sum_{w\subseteq v-v^{(n)}}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w+\{|v|-n\}}}^{t_{w+\{|v|-n\}}}\left[f_{t_{{v^{(n-1)}}}}(r)g_{t_{u+w+\{|v|-n\}}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}^{t_{-u-w-v^{(n)}}}dr_{w+\{|v|-n\}}.\end{split}

For the first term, we note that the summation is over the set
q\subseteq v-\{1,\dots,|v|-n-1\}=v-v^{(n-1)}
with
|v|-n\notin q
. Moreover, by the change of variable
q=w+\{|v|-n\}
, implying
-q-\{1,\dots,|v|-n-1\}=-w-\{1,\dots,|v|-n\}
, the latter term can be written as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\subseteq v-v^{(n-1)}\\
|v|-n\in q\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|q|}\int_{s_{q}}^{t_{q}}\left[f_{t_{v^{(n-1)}}}(r)g_{t_{u+q}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-q-v^{(n-1)}}}^{t_{-u-q-v^{(n-1)}}}dr_{q}.

Hence, (
11
) is equal to

\int_{s_{v^{(n-1)}}}^{t_{v^{(n-1)}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\subseteq v-v^{(n-1)}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|q|}\int_{s_{q}}^{t_{q}}\left[f_{t_{v^{(n-1)}}}(r)g_{t_{u+q}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-q-v^{(n-1)}}}^{t_{-u-q-v^{(n-1)}}}dr_{q}dr_{v^{(n-1)}}

completing the induction step. Furthermore, choosing
n=|v|
in (
11
) gives

\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(r)g_{t_{u}}(r)dr_{v}\right]_{s_{-v-u}}^{t_{-v-u}}=\sum_{w\subseteq v}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\left[f(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w}}^{t_{-u-w}}dr_{w}.

Finally, substitution to (
10
) gives

\begin{split}&\int_{s}^{t}g(r)d_{-v}f_{t_{v}}(r)dr_{v}\\
&=\sum_{u\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|u|}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}\sum_{w\subseteq v}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{s_{w}}^{t_{w}}\left[f(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w}}^{t_{-u-w}}dr_{w}dr_{u}\\
&=\sum_{u\subseteq-v}\sum_{w\subseteq v}(-1)^{|u+w|}\int_{s_{u+w}}^{t_{u+w}}\left[f(r)g_{t_{u+w}}(r)\right]_{s_{-u-w}}^{t_{-u-w}}dr_{u+w}\\
&=\sum_{q\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|q|}\int_{s_{q}}^{t_{q}}\left[f(r)g_{t_{q}}(r)\right]_{s_{-q}}^{t_{-q}}dr_{q}=\int_{s}^{t}g(r)df(r),\end{split}

where the last equality follows directly by integration by parts. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we prove a product rule for multiple Riemann-Stieltjes differentials.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f
be a function such that
f_{t}
exists and is continuous on
[s,t]
and let
g
be continuous.
Then

(12)

d(f(r)g(r))=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}f_{t_{u}}(r)d_{-u}g(r)dr_{u}

in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense for every integrand
h
such that
h_{t}
exists and is continuous on
[s,t]
.

Proof.

By integration by parts and Theorem
3.1
, the left-hand side yields

\begin{split}&\int_{s}^{t}h(r)d(f(r)g(r))=[h(r)f(r)g(r)]_{s}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)f(r)d_{v}h(r)]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&=[h(r)f(r)g(r)]_{s}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)f(r)h_{t_{v}}(r)dr_{v}]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}.\end{split}

We apply Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.20
repeatedly in the rest of the proof.
The right-hand side of (
12
) gives

\begin{split}\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f(r)dg(r)+\sum_{\emptyset\neq u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r)d_{-u}g(r)dr_{u},\end{split}

where

\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f(r)dg(r)=[h(r)f(r)g(r)]_{s}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)d_{v}(h(r)f(r))]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}

and

\begin{split}&\sum_{\emptyset\neq u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r)d_{-u}g(r)dr_{u}=\sum_{\emptyset\neq u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}\int_{s_{-u}}^{t_{-u}}h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r)d_{-u}g(r)dr_{u}\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f_{t}(r)g(r)dr+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}[h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r)g(r)]_{s_{-u}}^{t_{-u}}dr_{u}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq-u\\
1\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)d_{v}(h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r))]_{s_{-u-v}}^{t_{-u-v}}dr_{u}.\end{split}

Note that the integrals in the last term are well-defined by Lemma
2.17
, since the partial derivative of
hf_{t_{u}}
with respect to
t_{v}
is continuous. By combining the previous two equations, the right-hand side of (
12
) gives

\begin{split}&[h(r)f(r)g(r)]_{s}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)\frac{dhf}{dt_{v}}(r)dr_{v}]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&\quad+\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f_{t}(r)g(r)dr+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}[h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r)g(r)dr_{u}]_{s_{-u}}^{t_{-u}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq-u\\
1\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{u+v}}^{t_{u+v}}\left[g(r)\frac{dhf_{t_{u}}}{dt_{v}}(r)dr_{u+v}\right]_{s_{-u-v}}^{t_{-u-v}}.\end{split}

That is, in order to show that Equation (
12
) holds in the given sense, we need to show that

\begin{split}&\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq v\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}h_{t_{u}}(r)f_{t_{v-u}}(r)dr_{v}]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&\quad+\int_{s}^{t}h(r)f_{t}(r)g(r)dr+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{u}}^{t_{u}}[h(r)f_{t_{u}}(r)g(r)dr_{u}]_{s_{-u}}^{t_{-u}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq-u\\
1\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{u+v}}^{t_{u+v}}\left[g(r)\sum_{w\subseteq v}h_{t_{w}}(r)f_{t_{u+(v-w)}}(r)dr_{u+v}\right]_{s_{-u-v}}^{t_{-u-v}}\\
&=\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(1+(-1)^{|v|})\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)h(r)f_{v}(r)dr_{v}]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq-u\\
1\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{u+v}}^{t_{u+v}}\left[g(r)h(r)f_{t_{u+v}}(r)dr_{u+v}\right]_{s_{-u-v}}^{t_{-u-v}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
2\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[g(r)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq v\\
1\leq|u|\leq|v|-1\end{subarray}}h_{t_{u}}(r)f_{t_{v-u}}(r)dr_{v}]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq-u\\
1\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{u+v}}^{t_{u+v}}\left[g(r)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\subseteq v\\
1\leq|w|\end{subarray}}h_{t_{w}}(r)f_{t_{u+(v-w)}}(r)dr_{u+v}\right]_{s_{-u-v}}^{t_{-u-v}}=0.\end{split}

The first term after the equality follows by choosing
u=\emptyset
in the first term of the equation and combining it with the following two terms. The third term after the equality is the first term of the equation with the additional restriction
u\neq\emptyset
.
We show that the sum of the last two terms equals zero. The first two terms can be handled in a similar, but less involved manner. Thereby, it suffices to show

(13)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
2\leq|q|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|q|}\int_{s_{q}}^{t_{q}}[g(r)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\subseteq q\\
1\leq|w|\leq|q|-1\end{subarray}}h_{t_{w}}(r)f_{t_{q-w}}(r)dr_{q}]_{s_{-q}}^{t_{-q}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
1\leq|u|\leq N-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq-u\\
1\leq|v|\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{s_{u+v}}^{t_{u+v}}\left[g(r)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{w}\subseteq v\\
1\leq|\tilde{w}|\end{subarray}}h_{t_{\tilde{w}}}(r)f_{t_{u+(v-\tilde{w})}}(r)dr_{u+v}\right]_{s_{-u-v}}^{t_{-u-v}}=0.\end{split}

Now, let
q\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
with
|q|\geq 2
be fixed. Also, let
w\subseteq q
with
1\leq|w|\leq|q|-1
be fixed. By doing this, we have fixed a unique summand of the first term of (
13
). The sign of this term is positive if
|q|
is even and negative if
|q|
is odd. We next consider which terms in the latter double sum of (
13
) correspond to the choices
q
and
w
. First of all, it has to hold that
w\subseteq v
, but
v
can also contain indices of
q-w
. In addition,
u+v=q
has to hold. That is, by fixing
v
we also fix
u
. We now have four different scenarios, where
|q|
and
|w|
can be even or odd. We start by assuming that
|q|
and
|w|
are both even. By Lemma
2.2
, the sum related to the corresponding terms in (
13
) equals

(14)

\binom{|q|-|w|}{0}-\binom{|q|-|w|}{1}+\dots-\binom{|q|-|w|}{|q|-|w|-1}+1=0,

where the binomial coefficient
\binom{|q|-|w|}{i}
corresponds to
v
which contains
w
and
i
elements from
q-w
. Here we have used the fact that since
u+v=q
and
|u|,|v|\geq 1
,
i
runs from 0 to
|q|-|w|-1
. By considering in a similar way the other cases, we see that regardless of whether
|q|
and
|w|
are even or odd, the sum equals zero by Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f
be continuous and
F_{v}(t)=\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[f(r)]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}dr_{v}
. Then

dF_{v}(t)=d\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}f(r_{v}:t_{-v})dr_{v}=d_{-v}(f(t))dt_{v}

for every integrand
g
such that
g_{t}
is continuous.

Proof.

Notice first that

\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}[f(r)]_{s_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}dr_{v}=\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\sum_{w\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|w|}f(r_{v}:s_{w}:t_{-v-w})dr_{v}

depends on all
N
coordinates only for
w=\emptyset
. In all other cases, the square increments are identically zero (see also Theorem 2.2.5 in
[
13
]
) and thus, we obtain

d\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{w\subseteq-v}\\
w\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|w|}f(r_{v}:s_{w}:t_{-v-w})dr_{v}=0.

We have established that

dF_{v}(t)=d\int_{s_{v}}^{t_{v}}f(r_{v}:t_{-v})dr_{v}

and Theorem
3.2
completes the proof.

∎

3.2.
Integration over non-hyperrectangles

Next, we prepare to discuss integrals over non-standard domains. For
\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}\geq 0
we set

(15)

T=T(t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{l}\leq t_{l}\text{ for all }l,\sum x_{l}\geq 0\}.

It can be shown that

\begin{split}T=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:-\sum_{l\neq 1}t_{l}\leq x_{1}\leq t_{1},-x_{1}-\sum_{l\notin\{1,2\}}t_{l}\leq x_{2}\leq t_{2},\right.\\
\left.\dots,-\sum_{l\neq N}x_{l}\leq x_{N}\leq t_{N}\right\}.\end{split}

Similarly, if
v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
, then

T_{v}=T_{v}(t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{|v|}:x_{l}\leq t_{v_{l}}\text{ for all }l,\sum x_{l}+\sum t_{-v}\geq 0\},

where
T_{\{1,\dots,N\}}=T.

It can be shown that

(16)

\begin{split}T_{v}=\left\{-\sum_{l\neq v_{1}}t_{l}\leq x_{1}\leq t_{v_{1}},-x_{1}-\sum_{l\notin\{v_{1},v_{2}\}}t_{l}\leq x_{2}\leq t_{v_{2}},\right.\\
\left.\dots,-\sum_{l\neq|v|}x_{l}-\sum t_{-v}\leq x_{|v|}\leq t_{v_{|v|}}\right\}.\end{split}

Moreover, we set

\tilde{t}=\left(-\sum_{l\neq 1}t_{l},\dots,-\sum_{l\neq N}t_{l}\right)

and hence

\tilde{t}_{v}=\left(-\sum_{l\neq v_{1}}t_{l},\dots,-\sum_{l\neq v_{|v|}}t_{l}\right).

Now
R\coloneqq[\tilde{t},t]
is a hypercube containing
T
and
R_{v}\coloneqq[\tilde{t}_{v},t_{v}]
is a
|v|
-dimensional hypercube containing
T_{v}
. Notice that
\tilde{t}_{l}=t_{l}
for some
l
if and only if
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0
. Moreover, then
\tilde{t}_{j}=-\sum_{l}t_{l}=t_{j}
for all
j
. That is, if
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0
, the rectangle
R
(and
R_{v}

Lemma 3.6
.

For sets (
15
) and (

(i)

When

T\cap\mathcal{R}_{v,\emptyset}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{v}\in T_{v},x_{-v}=t_{-v}\},

which is associated with the integral
\int_{T_{v}}
.

(ii)

When

\mu_{|v|}(T\cap\mathcal{R}_{v,w})=0,

where

Proof.

(i)

\begin{split}T\cap\mathcal{R}_{v,\emptyset}&=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{v}\in[\tilde{t}_{v},t_{v}],x_{-v}=t_{-v}\}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x\leq t,\sum x_{l}\geq 0\}\\
&=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{-v}=t_{-v},x_{v}\in[\tilde{t}_{v},t_{v}],\sum x_{l}\geq 0\}\\
&=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{-v}=t_{-v},x_{v}\in[\tilde{t}_{v},t_{v}],\sum t_{-v}+\sum x_{v}\geq 0\}\\
&=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{-v}=t_{-v},x_{v}\leq t_{v},\sum t_{-v}+\sum x_{v}\geq 0\}\\
&=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{v}\in T_{v},x_{-v}=t_{-v}\},\end{split}

where the penultimate equation follows, since, for example

x_{v_{1}}\geq-\sum t_{-v}-\sum_{l\neq 1}x_{v_{l}}\geq-\sum t_{-v}-\sum_{l\neq 1}t_{v_{l}}=\tilde{t}_{v_{1}}.

(ii) Now

T\cap\mathcal{R}_{v,w}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{w}=\tilde{t}_{w},x_{-v-w}=t_{-v-w},x_{v}\in[\tilde{t}_{v},t_{v}],\sum x_{l}\geq 0\}.

Assume that
|w|=1
. Then

\sum x_{l}=\tilde{t}_{w_{1}}+\sum_{l\neq w_{1}}x_{l}\leq-\sum_{l\neq w_{1}}t_{l}+\sum_{l\neq w_{1}}t_{l}=0.

That is,

is a single point set

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{w}=\tilde{t}_{w}:x_{-w}=t_{-w}\}.

In a similar way, if

unless we are in the degenerate case

obtaining a set of one point.

Based on Lemma

\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{T_{v}}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v}:u_{v})g(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}

of (

and terms

\begin{split}&\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\left(\int_{R_{v}\setminus T_{v}}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v}:u_{v})g(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\sum_{\emptyset\neq w\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|w|}\int_{R_{v}}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})g(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})du_{v}\right)\end{split}

to
\int_{R\setminus T}fdg.
Next, we consider the allocation of

present in (

and

we put

into

Thus, we allocate such terms

When

are positioned symmetrically in our configuration and hence, we require equal weights for the corresponding terms. In addition, we want that
\int_{T}fdg=0
whenever
\sum t=0
. In this case, all integrals of type

vanish, since

is a set of a single point. Moreover, recall that now

\int_{T}fdg=f(t)g(t)-a\sum_{|v|=1}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})=(1-aN)f(t)g(t)=0

Based on the above considerations, we define

(20)

\begin{split}&\int_{T}f(u)dg(u)=f(t)g(t)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{|v|=1}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{T_{v}}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v}:u_{v})g(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\int_{R\setminus T}f(u)dg(u)=-\frac{N-1}{N}\sum_{|v|=1}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{|v|\geq 2}(-1)^{|v|}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\left(\int_{R_{v}\setminus T_{v}}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v}:u_{v})g(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\int_{R_{v}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq w\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|w|}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})g(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})du_{v}\right).\end{split}

Indeed, by denoting
fg=h
and
f_{t_{v}}g=h^{(v)}
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{R\setminus T}f(u)dg(u)+\int_{T}f(u)dg(u)=h(t)-\sum_{|v|=1}h(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})+\sum_{|v|\geq 2}(-1)^{|v|}h(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\left(\int_{T_{v}}h^{(v)}(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}+\int_{R_{v}\setminus T_{v}}h^{(v)}(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\int_{R_{v}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq w\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|w|}h^{(v)}(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})du_{v}\right)\\
&=\sum_{v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}h(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{R_{v}}\sum_{w\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|w|}h^{(v)}(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})\\
&=[fg]_{\tilde{t}}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{R_{v}}\left[h^{(v)}(u)du_{v}\right]_{\tilde{t}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&=[fg]_{\tilde{t}}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{\tilde{t}_{v}}^{t_{v}}\left[f_{t_{v}}(u)g(u)du_{v}\right]_{\tilde{t}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&=\int_{\tilde{t}}^{t}f(u)dg(u)=\int_{R}f(u)dg(u)\end{split}

as desired. Note also that when
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0
, then all the related domains are degenerate, and
\tilde{t}=t
. Since in this case
\int_{R}fdg=0
and
\int_{T}fdg=0
by the definitions, then also
\int_{R\setminus T}fdg=0
by the additivity property shown above.

Remark 3.7
.

Note that in our definitions, we have not considered terms that involve integration over the hyperplane that cuts the hypercube
R
into two pieces, which might seem cumbersome at first sight. Indeed, in principle, one should also take into account the values on the cutting hyperplane by adding integrals defined in a natural manner. Note that the contribution of these integrals would be a sum of functions where each function depends only on a single corner point on the cutting hyperplane. By the construction of
T=T(t)
(see also Example
3.5
), each of these corner points does not depend on all
N
variables, and consequently, the integrals related to the cutting hyperplane have zero rectangular increments. In particular, for our purposes, the integration over the region
T
is only required to define
G
via such integrals in Lemma
4.2
, which in turn is needed to obtain
dG(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}dY(e^{t})
in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense. As such, adding terms related to the cutting hyperplane would not change
dG(t)
in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense, and it is convenient to omit these terms, making our already notationally heavy computations simpler.

Next, we consider the setting with
\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}<0
. Now, we set

(21)

T=T(t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{l}\geq t_{l}\text{ for all }l,\sum x_{l}\leq 0\}.

It can be shown that

\begin{split}T=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:t_{1}\leq x_{1}\leq-\sum_{l\neq 1}t_{l},t_{2}\leq x_{2}\leq-x_{1}-\sum_{l\notin\{1,2\}}t_{l},\right.\\
\left.\dots,t_{N}\leq x_{N}\leq-\sum_{l\neq N}x_{l}\right\}.\end{split}

Similarly, if
v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
, then

T_{v}=T_{v}(t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{|v|}:x_{l}\geq t_{v_{l}}\text{ for all }l,\sum x_{l}+\sum t_{-v}\leq 0\}.

It can be shown that

(22)

\begin{split}T_{v}=\left\{t_{v_{1}}\leq x_{1}\leq-\sum_{l\neq v_{1}}t_{l},t_{v_{2}}\leq x_{2}\leq-x_{1}-\sum_{l\notin\{v_{1},v_{2}\}}t_{l},\right.\\
\left.\dots,t_{v_{|v|}}\leq x_{|v|}\leq-\sum_{l\neq|v|}x_{l}-\sum t_{-v}\right\}.\end{split}

Now
R_{v}=[t_{v},\tilde{t}_{v}]
is a hyperrectangle containing
T_{v}
. Again, we take additivity as our starting point. By Remark
2.10
,

\begin{split}\int_{R}fdg&=[fg]_{t}^{\tilde{t}}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{R_{v}}[f_{t_{v}}(u)g(u)du_{v}]_{t_{-v}}^{\tilde{t}_{-v}}\\
&=(-1)^{N}[fg]_{\tilde{t}}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}(-1)^{|-v|}\int_{R_{v}}[f_{t_{v}}(u)g(u)du_{v}]_{\tilde{t}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&=(-1)^{N}\left([fg]_{\tilde{t}}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\int_{R_{v}}[f_{t_{v}}(u)g(u)du_{v}]_{\tilde{t}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\right).\end{split}

Similarly as in the case of
\sum t\geq 0
, this justifies defining

(23)

\begin{split}&\int_{T}f(u)dg(u)=(-1)^{N}\left[f(t)g(t)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{|v|=1}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\int_{T_{v}}{f}_{t_{v}}({t_{-v}:u_{v}})g(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}\right]\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\int_{R\setminus T}f(u)dg(u)=(-1)^{N}\Bigg[-\frac{N-1}{N}\sum_{|v|=1}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{|v|\geq 2}(-1)^{|v|}f(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})g(t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v})\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\left(\int_{R_{v}\setminus T_{v}}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v}:u_{v})g(t_{-v}:u_{v})du_{v}\right.\\
&\quad+\left.\int_{R_{v}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq w\subseteq-v}(-1)^{|w|}f_{t_{v}}(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})g(t_{-v-w}:\tilde{t}_{w}:u_{v})du_{v}\right)\Bigg].\end{split}

We note also that, by the continuity of the involved functions and the fact that
\lim_{\sum_{l}t_{l}\to 0}\tilde{t}\to t
, we obtain directly from the definitions that

\lim_{\sum_{l}t_{l}\to 0}\int_{T}f(u)dg(u)=0=\lim_{\sum_{l}t_{l}\to 0}\int_{R}f(u)dg(u)

implying also that
\lim_{\sum_{l}t_{l}\to 0}\int_{R\setminus T}f(u)dg(u)\to 0
. Therefore, our integrals are continuous with respect to
T=T(t)
also on the hyperplane
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0
.

4.
Characterization of continuous stationary fields

In this section we provide our characterization of stationary fields via generalized Langevin equation. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and
G=\{G(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta}.
Then

Y(e^{t})=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)

is
\Theta
-self-similar. Moreover,
dY(e^{t})=e^{\Theta\cdot t}dG(t)
in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense for every integrand
g
such that
g_{t}
is continuous.

Proof.

If
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta}
, then

Y(e^{t+s})=\int_{-\infty}^{t+s}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u),

where the change of variable
r=u-s
gives

Y(e^{t+s})=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot(r+s)}dG(r+s)\overset{\text{law}}{=}e^{\Theta\cdot s}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)=e^{\Theta\cdot s}Y(e^{t})

by stationarity of increments of
G
. The equality of laws can be verified by studying Riemann-Stieltjes sums and passing to the limit. Treating multidimensional distributions similarly shows that
Y
is
\Theta
-self-similar.

For the latter claim, we consider integrals over a compact hyperrectangle, where the integrand
g
has the continuous mixed partial derivative
g_{t}
. Let

Y_{u}(e^{t})=\int_{-\bar{u}}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r),

where
\bar{u}=(u,\dots,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is such that
-u<t_{i}
for all
i
.
Recall that if
R,R_{1}
, and
R_{2}
are hyperrectangles such that
R=R_{1}\cup R_{2}
and the interiors of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are disjoint, then

\int_{R}fdg=\int_{R_{1}}fdg+\int_{R_{2}}fdg

whenever the integrals exist. Hence, for large enough
u
we have that

\begin{split}&\Delta_{1}\dots\Delta_{N}Y_{u}(e^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}},\dots,e^{x_{i_{N}}^{(N)}})\\
&=\Delta_{1}\dots\Delta_{N}\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}}\dots\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{N}}^{(N)}}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)\\
&=\Delta_{1}\dots\Delta_{N-1}\left(\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}}\dots\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{N}}^{(N)}}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)-\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}}\dots\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{N}-1}^{(N)}}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)\right)\\
&=\Delta_{1}\dots\Delta_{N-1}\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}}\dots\int_{-u}^{x_{i_{N-1}}^{(N-1)}}\int_{x_{i_{N}-1}^{(N)}}^{x_{i_{N}}^{(N)}}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)\\
&=\dots=\int_{x_{i_{1}-1}^{(1)}}^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}}\dots\int_{x_{i_{N}-1}^{(N)}}^{x_{i_{N}}^{(N)}}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)=\Delta_{1}\dots\Delta_{N}Y(e^{x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}},\dots,e^{x_{i_{N}}^{(N)}}).\end{split}

Together with the compactness of the integration domain, this allows us to write
dY_{u}(e^{t})=dY(e^{t})
. By integration by parts, this yields

\begin{split}Y_{u}(e^{t})&=\int_{-\bar{u}}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)=[e^{\Theta\cdot r}G(r)]_{-\bar{u}}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{-\bar{u}_{v}}^{t_{v}}[G(r)d_{v}e^{\Theta\cdot r}]_{-\bar{u}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}\\
&=[e^{\Theta\cdot r}G(r)]_{-\bar{u}}^{t}+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{-\bar{u}_{v}}^{t_{v}}[\prod_{j\in v}(\theta_{j})G(r)e^{\Theta\cdot r}dr_{v}]_{-\bar{u}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}},\end{split}

where

(24)

\begin{split}d[e^{\Theta\cdot r}G(r)]_{-\bar{u}}^{t}&=d(e^{\Theta\cdot t}G(t))=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{j\in u}(\theta_{j})e^{\Theta\cdot t}d_{-u}G(t)dt_{u}\\
&=e^{\Theta\cdot t}dG(t)+e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|u|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in u}(\theta_{j})d_{-u}G(t)dt_{u}.\end{split}

The first two equalities follow from the fact that
u
is a constant as the total differential is taken with respect to
t
together with Theorem
3.3
(see also Theorem 2.2.5 in
[
13
]
). In addition, by Lemma
3.4
,

(25)

\begin{split}d\int_{-\bar{u}_{v}}^{t_{v}}[G(r)e^{\Theta\cdot r}dr_{v}]_{-\bar{u}_{-v}}^{t_{-v}}&=d_{-v}\left(G(t)e^{\Theta\cdot t}\right)dt_{v}\\
&=e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{u\subseteq-v}\prod_{j\in u}(\theta_{j})d_{-v-u}G(t)dt_{u}dt_{v}.\end{split}

By combining (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain that

(26)

\begin{split}dY_{u}(e^{t})&=e^{\Theta\cdot t}dG(t)+e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|u|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in u}(\theta_{j})d_{-u}G(t)dt_{u}\\
&+e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{w\subseteq-v}\prod_{j\in v+w}(\theta_{j})d_{-v-w}G(t)dt_{v+w}.\end{split}

We complete the proof by showing that the sum of the last two terms above is zero. For this, let
u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
|u|\geq 1
be fixed and set
v+w=u
. Then the total number of terms

\prod_{j\in u}(\theta_{j})d_{-u}G(t)dt_{u}

in (
26
) is

\sum_{m=0}^{|u|}(-1)^{m}\binom{|u|}{m}=0

by Lemma
2.2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and
Y=\{Y(e^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be
\Theta
-self-similar. Then

(27)

G(t)=\begin{cases}\int_{T}e^{-\Theta\cdot u}dY(e^{u}),&\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}\geq 0\\
(-1)^{N}\int_{T}e^{-\Theta\cdot u}dY(e^{u}),&\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}<0,\end{cases}

where
T=T(t)
is as in (
15
) and (
21
), belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
. Moreover,
dG(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}dY(e^{t})
in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense for every integrand
g
such that
g_{t}
is continuous.

Proof.

By the definition of the integral,
G(t)=0
for every
t
such that
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0
. Moreover, from (
20
) and (
23
),

(28)

\begin{split}G(t)&=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}Y(e^{t})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{|v|=1}Y(e^{t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{j\in v}(\theta_{j})\int_{T_{v}}Y(e^{t_{-v}:u_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:u_{v}})}du_{v}\end{split}

for every
\sum t\geq 0
and

(29)

\begin{split}G(t)&=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}Y(e^{t})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{|v|=1}Y(e^{t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}\prod_{j\in v}(\theta_{j})\int_{T_{v}}Y(e^{t_{-v}:u_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:u_{v}})}du_{v}\end{split}

for every
\sum t<0
. First, note that since
Y(e^{t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})}
depends only on
N-1
of the variables, we obtain that

(30)

dY(e^{t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:\tilde{t}_{v}})}=0.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
v=\{1,\dots,|v|\}
. In addition, we denote

Y(e^{t_{-v}:u_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:u_{v}})}=f(t_{-v}:u_{v}).

Then, by (

) for
\sum t\geq 0

\begin{split}&\int_{T_{v}}Y(e^{t_{-v}:u_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:u_{v}})}du_{v}\\
&=\int_{-t_{2}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{1}}\int_{-u_{1}-t_{3}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{2}}\dots\int_{-u_{1}-\dots-u_{|v|-1}-t_{|v|+1}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{|v|}}f({t_{-v}:u_{v}})du_{v}\eqqcolon F(t).\end{split}

Similarly, by (

\sum t<0
,

\begin{split}&(-1)^{|v|}\int_{T_{v}}Y(e^{t_{-v}:u_{v}})e^{-\Theta\cdot({t_{-v}:u_{v}})}du_{v}\\
&=(-1)^{|v|}\int_{t_{1}}^{-t_{2}-\dots-t_{N}}\int_{t_{2}}^{-u_{1}-t_{3}-\dots-t_{N}}\dots\int_{t_{|v|}}^{-u_{1}-\dots-u_{|v|-1}-t_{|v|+1}-\dots-t_{N}}f({t_{-v}:u_{v}})du_{v}\\
&=(-1)^{|v|}(-1)^{|v|}\int_{-t_{2}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{1}}\int_{-u_{1}-t_{3}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{2}}\dots\int_{-u_{1}-\dots-u_{|v|-1}-t_{|v|+1}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{|v|}}f({t_{-v}:u_{v}})du_{v}\\
&=F(t).\end{split}

In order to apply this observation to (
28
) and (
29
), we first want to compute the mixed partial derivative
F_{t_{v}}(t)
. Denote

F(t)=\int_{-t_{2}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{1}}F^{(1)}(t_{\{2,\dots,N\}}:u_{1})du_{1}.

Then

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt_{1}}F(t)=F^{(1)}(t)\\
&=\int_{-t_{1}-t_{3}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{2}}\int_{-t_{1}-u_{2}-t_{4}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{3}}\dots\\
&\quad\int_{-t_{1}-u_{2}-\dots-u_{|v|-1}-t_{|v|+1}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{|v|}}f(t_{-v}:t_{1}:u_{\{2,\dots,|v|\}})du_{\{3,\dots,|v|\}}du_{2}\\
&\eqqcolon\int_{-t_{1}-t_{3}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{2}}F^{(2)}(t_{\{1,3,4,\dots,N\}}:u_{2})du_{2}.\end{split}

Hence,

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}}{dt_{1}dt_{2}}F(t)=\frac{d}{dt_{2}}F^{(1)}(t)=F^{(2)}(t)\\
&=\int_{-t_{1}-t_{2}-t_{4}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{3}}\int_{-t_{1}-t_{2}-u_{3}-t_{5}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{4}}\dots\\
&\quad\int_{-t_{1}-t_{2}-u_{3}-\dots-u_{|v|-1}-t_{|v|+1}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{|v|}}f(t_{-v}:t_{1}:t_{2}:u_{\{3,\dots,|v|\}})du_{\{3,\dots,|v|\}}.\end{split}

By continuing similarly, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt_{v}}F(t)=\frac{d}{dt_{|v|}}\int_{-t_{1}-\dots-t_{N}}^{t_{|v|}}f(t_{-v}:t_{\{1,\dots,|v|-1\}}:u_{|v|})du_{|v|}=f(t).

Now, by Theorem
3.2
,

dF(t)=d_{-v}F_{t_{v}}(t)dt_{v}=d_{-v}f(t)dt_{v}=d_{-v}\left(Y(e^{t})e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\right)dt_{v}.

By combining with (
30
) and then applying Theorem
3.3
, we get

\begin{split}dG(t)&=d\left(e^{-\Theta\cdot t}Y(e^{t})\right)+\sum_{\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{j\in v}(\theta_{j})d_{-v}\left(Y(e^{t})e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\right)dt_{v}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in v}(\theta_{j})d_{-v}\left(Y(e^{t})e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\right)dt_{v}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in v}(\theta_{j})\sum_{w\subseteq-v}\prod_{j\in w}(-\theta_{j})e^{-\Theta\cdot t}d_{-v-w}Y(e^{t})dt_{v+w}\\
&=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\end{subarray}}\sum_{w\subseteq-v}\prod_{j\in v+w}(\theta_{j})(-1)^{|w|}d_{-v-w}Y(e^{t})dt_{v+w}.\end{split}

When
v=w=\emptyset
, we get from above
e^{-\Theta\cdot t}dY(e^{t})
. Note that for
v+w=q\neq\emptyset
, we observe by Lemma
2.2
as before that the terms
\prod_{j\in q}(\theta_{j})d_{-q}Y(e^{t})dt_{q}
vanish.
Hence, we have shown that
dG(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}dY(e^{t})
. With this at hand, we can write

[G]_{s+h}^{t+h}=\int_{s+h}^{t+h}e^{-\Theta\cdot u}dY(e^{u}),

from which the change of variable
r=u-h
and
\Theta
-self-similarity of
Y
gives

\begin{split}[G]_{s+h}^{t+h}&=\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\Theta\cdot(r+h)}dY(e^{r+h})\overset{\text{law}}{=}\int_{s}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot h}e^{-\Theta\cdot(r+h)}dY(e^{r})\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\Theta\cdot r}dY(e^{r})=[G]_{s}^{t}.\end{split}

The equality of laws can be verified by applying the self-similarity property of
Y
to Riemann-Stieltjes sums, and passing to the limit. As multidimensional distributions can be treated similarly, we obtain that
G
has stationary increments.

Finally, we show that

\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(r)
is a well-defined random variable for all
t
. We have that

(31)

\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot r}dG(r)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}dY(e^{r})=\lim_{u\to\infty}[Y(e^{r})]_{-\bar{u}}^{t}=Y(e^{t}),

since the values of
Y(e^{r})
at the other corner points except at
t
tend to zero in probability by self-similarity of
Y
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
. Let
G=\{G(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
and
Y=\{Y(e^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be
\Theta
-self-similar. The transformation

(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}Y)(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\int_{T}e^{-\Theta\cdot u}dY(e^{u}),&\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}\geq 0\\
(-1)^{N}\int_{T}e^{-\Theta\cdot u}dY(e^{u}),&\sum_{l=1}^{N}t_{l}<0\end{cases}

together with its inverse

(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1}G)(e^{t})\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)

define a bijection between
\Theta
-self-similar fields and fields in
\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
equipped with the equivalence relation of Definition
2.26
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, it suffices to show that

\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{\Theta}G=G\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{\Theta}\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}Y=Y.

Furthermore, Equation (
31
) of Lemma
4.2
gives directly that
\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{\Theta}\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}Y=Y
.
For the remaining claim, by Lemma
4.1
,

Y(e^{t})=(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1}G)(e^{t})

is
\Theta
-self-similar with
dY(e^{t})=e^{\Theta\cdot t}dG(t)
. Moreover, by Lemma
4.2
,
\tilde{G}(t)\coloneqq(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}Y)(t)
is in
\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
with

d\tilde{G}(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}dY(e^{t})=dG(t).

Denote
F=G-\tilde{G}.
Then
F(t)=0
for all
t
such that
\sum_{l}t_{l}=0.
In addition, for every
t
and
s
,

\left[F\right]_{s}^{t}=\left[G\right]_{s}^{t}-[\tilde{G}]_{s}^{t}=\int_{s}^{t}dG(t)-\int_{s}^{t}d\tilde{G}(t)=0.

This gives

[F]_{0}^{t}=\sum_{v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|v|}F(0_{v}:t_{-v})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|v|\neq N\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|v|}F(0_{v}:t_{-v})=0

and consequently,

F(t)=(-1)^{|v|+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|v|\neq N\end{subarray}}F(0_{v}:t_{-v})\eqqcolon\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq v\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|v|\neq N\end{subarray}}f^{(v)}(t_{v}).

That is,
\tilde{G}=G.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}
be the Lamperti transformation given in Definition
2.5
that maps from stationary fields to
\Theta
-self-similar fields. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}
maps from stationary fields to
\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1}
maps from
\mathcal{G}_{\Theta}
to stationary fields. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}
is a bijection between stationary fields and fields in

.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and let
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be stationary. Then
X
satisfies the Langevin equation (
8
) for
G=(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Theta})(X)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
.

Proof.

By the Lamperti theorem,
X(t)=(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}Y)(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}Y(e^{t})
for a unique
\Theta
-self-similar
Y
. Denote
f(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}
and
Z(t)=Y(e^{t})
. By Theorem
3.3
, the Langevin equation may be written as

(32)

\begin{split}dG(t)&=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}d_{-u}X(t)dt_{u}=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}d_{-u}(f(t)Z(t))dt_{u}\\
&=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|u|}d_{-u}(f_{t_{u}}(t)Z(t))dt_{u}\\
&=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|u|}\sum_{v\subseteq-u}f_{t_{u+v}}(t)d_{-u-v}Z(t)dt_{v}dt_{u}\\
&=\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|u|}\sum_{v\subseteq-u}f_{t_{u+v}}(t)d_{-u-v}Z(t)dt_{u+v}.\end{split}

If
u=v=\emptyset
, then the corresponding term above is
f(t)dZ(t)
. Next, let
u+v=q\neq\emptyset
be fixed. Now,
v=q-u
and (
32
) turns to

(33)

\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{|u|}f_{t_{q}}(t)d_{-q}Z(t)dt_{q}.

As before, applying Lemma
2.2
one sees that the terms
f_{t_{q}}(t)d_{-q}Z(t)dt_{q}
vanish and as such, (
32
) reads

f(t)dZ(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}dY(e^{t})=dG(t).

Defining
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
according to Lemma
4.2
completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta}
. Then, the corresponding Langevin equation (
8
) has a unique stationary solution

X(t)=\left((\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(G)\right)(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u).

Proof.

First, the field
X
is stationary, since
X=(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(G).
In addition, by Theorem
4.5
,
X
satisfies the Langevin equation for
\tilde{G}=(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Theta})(X)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
. By denoting
\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}(X)=Y=\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1}(G)
, we may write
\tilde{G}=\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}(Y).
Now, as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we get
d\tilde{G}(t)=dG(t).
Hence,
X
is a solution to the Langevin equation also for
G
.

Next, we prove the uniqueness. Assume that
\tilde{X}
is another stationary solution. Then,
\tilde{X}
satisfies the Langevin equation also for
\tilde{G}=(\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Theta})(\tilde{X})\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
. Thus,
d\tilde{G}(t)=dG(t)
implying
\tilde{X}=(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(\tilde{G})=(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(G)=X
by the definition of the transformation
\mathcal{M}^{-1}
.
∎

We end the paper by considering the uniqueness of the solution to the Langevin equation for a given initial condition. It turns out that the solution is unique only up to certain modifications.

Definition 4.7
.

We say that processes
X(t)
and
Y(t)
are equivalent with respect to the parameter
\Theta
if the process

Z(t)=e^{\Theta\cdot t}[X(t)-Y(t)]

satisfies
[Z]_{s}^{t}=0
for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The above definition means that we have

(34)

X(t)=Y(t)+e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|u|\neq N\end{subarray}}H^{(u)}(t_{u})

for some processes
H^{(u)}(t_{u})
, where now
u
is a proper subset of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Note that this definition is natural, as we cannot distinguish processes
H^{(u)}(t_{u})
in the multiple Riemann-Stieltjes sense. Indeed, their rectangular increments equal zero, since each
H^{(u)}(t_{u})
does not depend on all the variables, and are thus essentially lower-dimensional objects. Note that, in the one-dimensional case, we would have

X(t)=Y(t)+Ce^{-\theta t}

from which the initial condition
X(0)=Y(0)
would give uniqueness in the classical sense, i.e.
X(t)=Y(t)
almost surely for all
t
.

We also need the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose that
f
solves

\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}f_{t_{-u}}(t)=0.

Then
f
is given by

f(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|u|\neq N\end{subarray}}h^{(u)}(t_{u}).

Proof.

Define
\tilde{f}(t)=e^{\Theta\cdot t}f(t)
. We proceed by induction. Equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\tilde{f}(t)=e^{\Theta\cdot t}(\theta_{1}f(t)+f_{t_{1}}(t))

provides the base case.
We make the induction hypothesis

\tilde{f}_{t_{\{1,\dots,j\}}}(t)=e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,j\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}f_{t_{\{1,\dots,j\}-u}}(t).

Now

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j+1}}\tilde{f}_{t_{\{1,\dots,j\}}}(t)&=e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,j\}}\left(\prod_{i\in u}(\theta_{i})\theta_{j+1}f_{t_{\{1,\dots,j\}-u}}(t)+\prod_{i\in u}(\theta_{i})f_{t_{\{1,\dots,j+1\}-u}}(t)\right)\\
&=e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,j+1\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}f_{t_{\{1,\dots,j+1\}-u}}(t),\end{split}

completing the induction step. In particular, for
N
, this leads to

\tilde{f}_{t}(t)=e^{\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}f_{t_{-u}}(t)=0.

Integrating with respect to all variables leads to

\tilde{f}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|u|\neq N\end{subarray}}h^{(u)}(t_{u}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

For every fixed ”initial condition”
X(0)=Z
, where
Z
is some measurable random variable, the Langevin equation (
8
) admits a unique solution in the sense of Definition
4.7
.

Proof.

With direct computations, we see that

Z+e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)

is a solution, and hence it suffices to prove the uniqueness. Suppose that
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
are two solutions and set
X=X^{1}-X^{2}
. Then, from the linearity of the equation, we obtain that
X
solves

\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}d_{-u}X(t)dt_{u}=0

with
X(0)=0
. That is,
X
solves a partial differential equation
1
1

1

Note that the partial differential equation is solved in a weak sense
a priori
. However, since the PDE is linear with constant coefficients, it is a classical result that weak solutions are strong solutions, and hence we obtain that
X
is in fact a (sufficiently) smooth solution.

\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}}\prod_{i\in u}\theta_{i}\frac{d}{dt_{-u}}X(t)=0

Now by Lemma
4.8
we obtain that

X(t)=X^{1}(t)-X^{2}(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\\
|u|\neq N\end{subarray}}H^{(u)}(t_{u}).

That is, the solution is unique in the sense of Definition
4.7
, see (
34
). This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\Theta\in(0,\infty)^{N}
. Then, a field
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is stationary if and only if it is the unique solution, in the sense of Definition
4.7
, to the Langevin equation (
8
) for
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
with the ”initial condition”
X(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u)
. That is,

X(t)=\left((\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(G)\right)(t)=e^{-\Theta\cdot t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\Theta\cdot u}dG(u).

Moreover, the field
G=\{G(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
in the representation is unique and expressible as

G(t)=\left((\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\Theta})(X)\right)(t).

Proof.

The characterization follows by combining Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
4.6
. The uniqueness of the solution follows from Lemma
4.9
. In order to show the uniqueness of
G
, assume that
X
satisfies the Langevin equation for
G^{(1)}\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
and
G^{(2)}\in\mathcal{G}_{\Theta,0}
. Then

X=(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(G^{(1)})=(\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1})(G^{(2)})

yielding
G^{(1)}=G^{(2)}
by bijectivity of
\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{-1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Values at non-positive integers of partially twisted multiple zeta-functions II
Driss Essouabri, Kohji Matsumoto, Simon Rutard

Abstract
We study the values at non-positive integer points of multi-variable twisted multiple zeta-functions, whose each factor of the denominator is given by polynomials. The fully twisted case was already answered by de Crisenoy. On the partially twisted case, in one of our former article we studied the case when each factor of the denominator is given by linear forms or power-sum forms. In the present paper we treat the case of general polynomial denominators, and obtain explicit forms of the values at non-positive integer points. Our strategy is to reduce to the theorem of de Crisenoy for the fully twisted case, via the multiple Mellin-Barnes integral formula. We observe that in some cases the obtained values are transcendental.

Introduction

Let

,
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

It is the aim of the present paper to study the values at non-positive integer points of
the multiple series of the form

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k})=\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 1}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{m_{j}}}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{T-1}P_{j}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})^{s_{j}}\right)P_{T}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})^{s_{T}}},

,

The series (

) is absolutely convergent if

(

) are sufficiently large (see Proposition

under certain assumptions.
Note that, if

) coincides with the
Lerch zeta-function

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mu_{1}}(s_{1})=\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}m_{1}^{-s_{1}}.

(1.2)

When
n=1
, we understand that the polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{T-1}
correspond to real numbers

\zeta_{1}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}):=p_{1}^{-s_{1}}\cdots p_{T-1}^{-s_{T-1}}\sum_{m=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m}}{P_{T}(m)^{s_{T}}}.

If

1.1
) is an empty product and is regarded as

) is a non-twisted series.
In particular, if

It is well known that

can be continued meromorphically to the whole space, and their
values at non-positive integer points have been extensively studied. The study of these values was
first cultivated by a pioneering work of Akiyama, Egami and Tanigawa
[
1
]

They noticed that almost all such points are on singularity sets of

so the “values” at those points should be understood as “limit values”.
They obtained explicit formulas of those limit values in the case of some special cases of

Then, after several subsequent research
(such as Sasaki
[
22
]
), Onozuka
[
18
]
) arrived at an explicit
formula for rather general types of limit processes.
Laurent expansions around those points have further been studied (see Matsumoto, Onozuka
and Wakabayashi
[
15
]
and Saha
[
20
]
).

The case when

and
P_{j}
are more general linear forms has also been considered (see
Komori
[
12
]
, Essouabri and Matsumoto
[
10
]
,

[

]
, Rutard
[

]
).
For example, in
[
10
]
, the case

P_{j}=\gamma_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{j}X_{j}+b_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq n
,
\gamma_{j}
,
b_{j}
are complex
constants) is studied. An explicit formula for the limit value of the form

(1.3)

(where

,

\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
)
is obtained, which implies that the value is included in the field generated by the parameters

,

, and

over the rational number field

.

Essouabri and Matsumoto
[

treated the case when

(

They considered the case when

Now consider the twisted case

When

Another approach to the partially twisted case was developed by Essouabri and

The aim of the present paper is to study the more general situation. In fact,

]
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

In Section

2
Necessary tools

We begin with the definitions of conditions
\mathrm{H_{0}S}
(for
sufficient minimal hypothesis
) and HDF (for
weak decreasing hypothesis
, or “
hypothèse de décroissance faible
” in French).

Definition 1
.

We call that a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
satisfies the
\mathrm{H_{0}S}
condition
if, for any
\mathbf{x}\in[1,+\infty)^{n}
, it satisfies that

(i)

P(\mathbf{x})>0
, and

(ii)

(\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}P/P)(\mathbf{x})\ll 1
(for any
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
).

Definition 2
.

We call that a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
satisfies the HDF condition
if, for any
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in[1,+\infty)^{n}
, it satisfies that

(i)

P(\mathbf{x})>0
,

(ii)

for any
1\leq j\leq n
, there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

(\partial^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}P/P)(\mathbf{x})\ll x_{j}^{-\varepsilon_{0}}

for all
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

such that
\alpha_{j}\geq 1
.

The condition
\mathrm{H_{0}S}
was first introduced by Essouabri
[
6
]
, and then, the stronger
condition HDF appeared in the paper
[
4
]
of de Crisenoy.
The following rather general class of fully twisted multiple series was studied by de Crisenoy
[
4
]
. The result of de Crisenoy on this fully twisted series is:

Proposition 3

(de Crisenoy,
[
4
, Theorems A & B]
)
.

Let
R_{1},\ldots,R_{T}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
satisfying the HDF condition, and

\displaystyle\prod_{\rho=1}^{T}R_{\rho}(\mathbf{x})\to+\infty\quad\text{as}\quad|\mathbf{x}|=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}\to+\infty,\;\mathbf{x}\in[1,+\infty)^{n}.

(2.1)

Then, the multiple zeta-function

\zeta_{n}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{R};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}):=\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n}^{m_{n}}}{R_{1}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{1}}\cdots R_{T}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{T}}}

(2.2)

can be extended to an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{T}
. Moreover, for any

\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{T})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{T}
, the explicit formula

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\mathrm{dC}}(-\mathbf{k};\mathbf{R};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in S}a_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{\mu_{j}}(-\alpha_{j})

(2.3)

holds,
where
S
and
a_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are defined by the expansion

\prod_{\rho=1}^{T}R_{\rho}(\mathbf{X})^{k_{\rho}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in S}a_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\mathbf{X}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

with
\mathbf{X}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
, and

\zeta_{\mu}
is the Lerch zeta-function defined by (
1.2
).

By convention in this paper,
n
is allowed to be equal to
0
, and in that case the polynomials
R_{1},\ldots,R_{T}
correspond to real numbers
r_{1},\ldots,r_{T}
, and
\zeta_{0}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{R};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0})=r_{1}^{-s_{1}}\cdots r_{T}^{-s_{T}}.

Remark 4
.

The Lerch zeta-function
\zeta_{\mu}
has analytic continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}
and its values at non positive integers can be expressed in term of Stirling numbers of the second kind (see, for example,
[
4
, Lemma 5.7]

In this paper we will use de Crisenoy’s result. Moreover, we also need a complement to his result on the order of
\zeta_{n}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{R};\boldsymbol{\mu})

Definition 5
.

A holomorphic function
F
in a domain
U
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{T}
is said to be

on

F(s_{1},\ldots,s_{T})\leq C\,\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{T}|\Im s_{j}|\right)^{A}

for all
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{T})\in U
such that
\left(\Re s_{1},\ldots,\Re s_{T}\right)\in K

Proposition 6
.

Assume that assumptions of Proposition
3
hold. Then the entire function
\zeta_{n}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{R};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n})
is of moderate growth on
{\mathbb{C}}^{T}

In the statement of his main theorem (Proposition
3
above), de Crisenoy did not mention that the holomorphic continuation has moderate growth. Since we need this fact in our proofs in this paper, we will give in Section

Our tool of reducing the partially twisted case to the above theorem on the fully twisted

Proposition 7

(The Mellin-Barnes formula)
.

Let
s,\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Re s>0
,
|\arg(\lambda)|<\pi
and
\lambda\neq 0
. Then, we have

(1+\lambda)^{-s}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{\Gamma(s+z)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(s)}\lambda^{z}\,dz,

where
c
is a real number with
-\Re s<c<0
and the path
(c)
of integration is the vertical line
\Re z=c
.

Next we quote the multiple version due to Mellin
[

, and
was successfully used in Essouabri
[

]

Proposition 8

(The multiple Mellin-Barnes formula)
.

Let
s,\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{r}>0
. Assume that

\Re\lambda_{j}>0
for any
j=0,\ldots,r
and that
\Re s>\rho_{1}+\cdots+\rho_{r}
. Then,

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{0}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{r}}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho_{r})}\frac{\Gamma(s-z_{1}-\cdots-z_{r})\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(z_{j})}{\Gamma(s)\lambda_{0}^{s-z_{1}-\cdots-z_{r}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{z_{j}}}\,dz_{1}\cdots dz_{r}.

The case

Now we are going into the main body of the present paper, that is, the study of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

In this section we consider the case when

is of the form

\displaystyle P_{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=Q_{0}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})+Q_{1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})X_{n}^{a_{1}},

(4.1)

satisfy HDF and (

), and

is a positive integer.
Then

\displaystyle P_{T}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})^{-s_{T}}=(Q_{0}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})+Q_{1}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})m_{n}^{a_{1}})^{-s_{T}}

\displaystyle\quad=Q_{0}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})^{-s_{T}}\left(1+\frac{Q_{1}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})m_{n}^{a_{1}}}{Q_{0}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})}\right)^{-s_{T}}.

Applying the Mellin-Barnes formula (Proposition
7
), we see that the above is equal to

Q_{0}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})^{-s_{T}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+z)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\left(\frac{Q_{1}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})m_{n}^{a_{1}}}{Q_{0}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})}\right)^{z}\,dz

where the path of integration is the vertical line from
c-i\infty
to

, and

satisfies

.
But because of (

), we may assume that

\displaystyle\Re s_{j}>\frac{1}{b}\quad\text{ for any }j=1,\ldots,T-1,

\displaystyle\Re s_{T}>\frac{1}{b}-c\quad\text{ and }\quad c<\min\left(\frac{-1}{a},\frac{-1}{a_{1}}\right).

(4.3)

Inserting (
4.2
) into (
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1}\geq 1}\left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\mu_{j}^{m_{j}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{T-1}P_{j}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})^{s_{j}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+z)\Gamma(-z)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\left(\frac{Q_{1}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})m_{n}^{a_{1}}}{Q_{0}(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1})}\right)^{z}\,dz

(4.4)

where

\mathbf{s}(z)=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{T-1},s_{T}+z,-z)
and

\mathbf{P(Q)}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{T-1},Q_{0},Q_{1})
.
Here, the sum with respect to

is separated as

Note that, since

, by (

\displaystyle Q_{0}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})+Q_{1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})X_{n}^{a_{1}}

\displaystyle+Q_{2}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})X_{n}^{a_{2}},

(5.1)

where

P_{1},\ldots,P_{T-1},Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}]
satisfy HDF and (
2.4
), and
a_{1},a_{2}
positive integers such that
0<a_{1}<a_{2}
.

Notation
.

In this section we will use the notation
{\bf z}^{\prime}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{q-1})
if
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{q})
(
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
). Similarly we will use the notation
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}
, etc.

We recall from (
2.5
) that

\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
converges absolutely if

\Re s_{1},\ldots,\Re s_{T-1}>1/b\quad\text{ and }\quad\Re s_{T}>1/a.

Set
\mathbf{P(Q)}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{T-1},Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2})
. We know that there exists
\sigma_{0}>0
such that

\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{T+2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
converges absolutely if
\Re(s_{j})>\sigma_{0}
for any
j=1,\ldots,T+2.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>\sigma_{0}
such that
a_{1}\rho_{1}+a_{2}\rho_{2}>1
.
Fix
{\bf s}=({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\Re s_{k}>\max(\sigma_{0},1/b)
for any
k=1,\ldots,T-1
and

\Re s_{T}>\max(1/a,\sigma_{0}+\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})
.
The multiple Mellin-Barnes formula (Proposition
8
) and the series-integral inversion imply that

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\bf s};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}}{\left(\prod_{k=1}^{T-1}P_{k}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{s_{k}}\right)\left(Q_{0}({\bf m}^{\prime})+Q_{1}({\bf m}^{\prime})m_{n}^{a_{1}}+Q_{2}({\bf m}^{\prime})m_{n}^{a_{2}}\right)^{s_{T}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}}{\left(\prod_{k=1}^{T-1}P_{k}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{s_{k}}\right)Q_{0}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2}}\left(Q_{1}({\bf m}^{\prime})m_{n}^{a_{1}}\right)^{z_{1}}\left(Q_{2}({\bf m}^{\prime})m_{n}^{a_{2}}\right)^{z_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1}\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}\zeta\left(a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}\right)}{\left(\prod_{k=1}^{T-1}P_{k}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{s_{k}}\right)Q_{0}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2}}Q_{1}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{z_{1}}Q_{2}({\bf m}^{\prime})^{z_{2}}}\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2},z_{1},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})G(z_{1},z_{2})}{a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}-1}\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2},

(5.2)

where
\displaystyle G(z_{1},z_{2}):=\left(a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}-1\right)\zeta\left(a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}\right).

Propositions
3
and
6
imply that
\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\cdot;\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
has holomorphic continuation of moderate growth to the whole space
{\mathbb{C}}^{T+2}
. In addition, it is clear from classical properties of Riemann zeta-function that
G
has also holomorphic continuation of moderate growth to the whole space
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
Therefore
we deduce by residue theorem and analytic continuation that (
5
) holds if we assume that

\rho_{1},\rho_{2}>0,a_{1}\rho_{1}+a_{2}\rho_{2}>1
and
{\bf s}=({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\Re s_{k}>1/b
for any
k=1,\ldots,T-1
and

\Re s_{T}>\max(1/a,\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})
.
We assume in the sequel that these assumptions hold. We assume also that
\rho_{2}<1/a_{2}
.

Let
M_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
{\varepsilon}_{1}\in(\rho_{2},1)
.
Shift the path
(\rho_{1})
to
(-M_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{1})
.
This shifting is possible, because

\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\cdot;\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
and
G
are holomorphic of moderate growth, and gamma-factors are well estimated by Stirling’s formula.
Relevant poles with respect to
z_{1}
are

z_{1}=-k_{1}
(
0\leq k_{1}\leq M_{1}
) and

z_{1}=\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}}
(noting that by our assumptions we have

-M_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{1}<\frac{1-a_{2}\rho_{2}}{a_{1}}<\rho_{1}
).
Therefore the residue theorem (in
z_{1}
) imply that

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\bf s};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{M_{1}}I_{1}(k_{1})+I_{2}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\mathcal{R}_{M_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1}}({\bf s}),

(5.3)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(k_{1})=\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}}{2\pi ik_{1}!}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}+k_{1}-z_{2},-k_{1},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})\frac{G(-k_{1},z_{2})}{a_{2}z_{2}-a_{1}k_{1}-1}\,dz_{2},

\displaystyle I_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi ia_{1}}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})z_{2}-1}{a_{1}}\right)\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})z_{2}-1}{a_{1}},\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})\,G(\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}},z_{2})\,dz_{2},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1}}({\bf s})=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{(-M_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{1})}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\Gamma(s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2},z_{1},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})\frac{G(z_{1},z_{2})}{a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}-1}\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}.

Let
M_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
{\varepsilon}_{2}\in(0,1)
. We shift the path of the integral
in the definition of
I_{1}(k_{1})
from
(\rho_{2})
to
(-M_{2}-{\varepsilon}_{2})
, and that in the definition of
I_{2}
from
(\rho_{2})
to

(M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2})
. Since
\rho_{2}\in(0,1/a_{2})
, we obtain by the residue theorem (in
z_{2}
) that

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\bf s};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{M_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{M_{2}}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2},-k_{1},-k_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})\frac{G(-k_{1},-k_{2})}{a_{2}k_{2}+a_{1}k_{1}+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{a_{2}M_{2}-1}{a_{1}}\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}}{k_{2}!a_{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}\left({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}},-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times G\left(-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\mathcal{R}_{{\bf M},{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}({\bf s}),

(5.4)

where

\mathcal{R}_{{\bf M},{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}({\bf s})=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{M_{1}}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}}{2\pi i}\mathcal{R}_{M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2},k_{1}}({\bf s})+\mathcal{V}_{M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2}}({\bf s})+\mathcal{R}_{M_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1}}({\bf s}),

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2},k_{1}}

\displaystyle({\bf s})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-M_{2}-{\varepsilon}_{2})}\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{2})

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}+k_{1}-z_{2},-k_{1},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})\frac{G(-k_{1},z_{2})}{a_{2}z_{2}-a_{1}k_{1}-1}\,dz_{2};

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2}}

\displaystyle({\bf s})=\frac{1}{2\pi ia_{1}}\int_{(M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2})}\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})z_{2}-1}{a_{1}}\right)\Gamma(z_{2})\Gamma\left(\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}\left({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})z_{2}-1}{a_{1}},\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times G(\frac{1-a_{2}z_{2}}{a_{1}},z_{2})\,dz_{2};

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1}}

\displaystyle({\bf s})=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{(-M_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{1})}\int_{(\rho_{2})}\Gamma(s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2})\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}\left({\bf s}^{\prime},s_{T}-z_{1}-z_{2},z_{1},z_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}\right)\frac{G(z_{1},z_{2})}{a_{1}z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}-1}\,dz_{1}\,dz_{2}.

It is clear that

\bullet

{\bf s}\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2},k_{1}}({\bf s})
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times\{\Re s_{T}>-M_{2}-{\varepsilon}_{2}-k_{1}\}
;

\bullet

{\bf s}\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2}}({\bf s})
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times\left\{\Re s_{T}>\frac{1-(a_{2}-a_{1})(M_{2}+{\varepsilon}_{2})}{a_{1}}\right\};

\bullet

{\bf s}\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{M_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{1}}({\bf s})
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times\left\{\Re s_{T}>\rho_{2}-{\varepsilon}_{1}-M_{1}\right\}
.

Let
K\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Choose
M_{1}=K
and
M_{2}=\max\left(K,\left\lceil\frac{1+a_{1}K}{a_{2}-a_{1}}\right\rceil\right)
.
Under these choices, it follows that there exists
\delta>0
such that
{\bf s}\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{{\bf M},{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}({\bf s})
has
holomorphic
continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times\{\Re s_{T}>-K-\delta\}
.
We deduce then from formula (
5
) that
{\bf s}\mapsto\zeta_{n}({\bf s};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
has a meromorphic continuation
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{T-1}\times\{\Re s_{T}>-K-\delta\}
and that its possible singularities in this domain come only from the factors

\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\quad(k_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\,0\leq k_{2}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{a_{2}M_{2}-1}{a_{1}}\right\rfloor).

Whether these are really singular or not can be determined as
follows.

\bullet

If
a_{2}\mid(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1
, that is
(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1=a_{2}u_{2}

(u_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}})
, then

\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}=\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+u_{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

is holomorphic in
s_{T}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

\bullet

If
a_{2}\nmid(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1
, that is
(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1=a_{2}(u_{2}-1)+r

(u_{2},r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\,1\leq r\leq a_{2}-1)
, then

\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}=\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+u_{2}-1+\frac{r}{a_{2}}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

is meromorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}
with only simple poles in the point
s_{T}=1-\frac{r}{a_{2}}-u_{2}-\ell

(\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
.

By letting
K
to infinity, we deduce that

{\bf s}\mapsto\zeta_{n}({\bf s};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
has a meromorphic continuation to the whole space
{\mathbb{C}}^{T}
and that its singularities are located in the union of the hyperplanes
s_{T}=1-\frac{r}{a_{2}}-j

(j,r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\,1\leq r\leq a_{2}-1)
.

Let
{\bf N}=({\bf N}^{\prime},N_{T})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{T-1}\times{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Set
K=N_{T}
,
M_{1}=K
and
M_{2}=\max\left(K,\left\lceil\frac{1+a_{1}K}{a_{2}-a_{1}}\right\rceil\right)
.
Since
R_{{\bf M},{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}({\bf s})
is regular in
{\bf s}=-{\bf N}
, the functions
\displaystyle\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}\right)
(
a_{2}\nmid(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1
) are regular in
s_{T}=-N_{T}
and
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Big|_{s_{T}=-N_{T}}=0
,
we deduce from (
5
) that

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(-{\bf N};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{M_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{M_{2}}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Big|_{s_{T}=-N_{T}}

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(-{\bf N}^{\prime},-N_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2},-k_{1},-k_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})\frac{G(-k_{1},-k_{2})}{a_{2}k_{2}+a_{1}k_{1}+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\\
a_{2}\mid a_{1}k_{2}+1\end{subarray}}^{\left\lfloor\frac{a_{2}M_{2}-1}{a_{1}}\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}}-1\right)!}{k_{2}!a_{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(s_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}\right)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Big|_{s_{T}=-N_{T}}

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}\left(-{\bf N}^{\prime},-N_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}},-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times G\left(-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}}\right).

(5.5)

Moreover, it is easy to see that

\bullet

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k)}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Big|_{s_{T}=-N_{T}}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(-N_{T}+j):=(-N_{T})_{k}

where
(x)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(x+j)
is the Pochhammer symbol,
hence especially the above is equal to
0
if
k>N_{T}
;

\bullet

\displaystyle G(-k_{1},-k_{2})=-(a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2}+1)\zeta\left(-(a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2})\right)
,
which is equal to

(-1)^{a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2}+1}B_{a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2}+1}
;

\bullet

G\left(-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}}\right)=1
;

\bullet

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Big|_{s_{T}=-N_{T}}=0
if
k_{1}+k_{2}\geq N_{T}+1
;

\bullet

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2})}{\Gamma(s_{T})}\Big|_{s_{T}=-N_{T}}=\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}N_{T}!}{(N_{T}-k_{1}-k_{2})!}
if
k_{1}+k_{2}\leq N_{T}
.

Substituting these five points into formula (
5
), we obtain:

Theorem 18
.

When
P_{T}
is of the form (
5
), we have for
{\bf N}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{T}
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(-{\bf N};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+k_{2}\leq N_{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{T}!}{(N_{T}-k_{1}-k_{2})!k_{1}!k_{2}!}

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(-{\bf N}^{\prime},-N_{T}+k_{1}+k_{2},-k_{1},-k_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{(-1)^{a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2}}B_{a_{1}k_{1}+a_{2}k_{2}+1}}{a_{2}k_{2}+a_{1}k_{1}+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\\
a_{2}|a_{1}k_{2}+1\end{subarray}}^{\left\lfloor\frac{a_{2}N_{T}+1}{a_{2}-a_{1}}\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}}-1\right)!}{k_{2}!a_{2}}\left(-N_{T}\right)_{\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}\left(-{\bf N}^{\prime},-N_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}},-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}\right).

(5.6)

Remark 19
.

Using formula (
18
), we obtain an explicit expression of the values of

\zeta_{n}(-{\bf N};{\bf P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
at non-negative integer points in terms of the values of de Crisenoy’s series
\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\cdot;\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
in rational points.
In the first sum on the right-hand side of formula (
18
) only the values of
\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(\cdot;\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
at non-negative integer points appear. So thanks to de Crisenoy’s work this sum is an element of the field generated over
{\mathbb{Q}}
by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1}
and the coefficients of the polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{T}
.
However, in the second sum there appear factors of the form

\zeta_{n-1}^{\mathrm{dC}}\left(-{\bf N}^{\prime},-N_{T}+\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})k_{2}-1}{a_{2}},-k_{2},\frac{a_{1}k_{2}+1}{a_{2}};\mathbf{P(Q)};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}\right).

We observe that
these factors are not necessary in the field generated over
{\mathbb{Q}}
by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1}
and the coefficients of the polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{T}
, and can be
highly transcendental
(see Example
35
in Section 8).

Example 20
.

Assume that
n=d=T=2
,
a_{1}=1
and
a_{2}=2
. Then
k_{2}
in the second sum on the right-hand side of (
18
) should be odd, so
we put
k_{2}=2u_{2}+1
.
Then (
18
) implies that for
{\bf N}=(N_{1},N_{2})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}(-{\bf N},{\bf P},\mu_{1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+k_{2}\leq N_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{2}!}{(N_{2}-k_{1}-k_{2})!k_{1}!k_{2}!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\zeta_{1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(-N_{1},-N_{2}+k_{1}+k_{2},-k_{1},-k_{2};\mathbf{P(Q)},\mu_{1})\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}B_{k_{1}+2k_{2}+1}}{k_{1}+2k_{2}+1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{u_{2}=0}^{N_{2}}\frac{(-1)^{u_{2}}(u_{2}!)^{2}\binom{N_{2}}{u_{2}}}{2(2u_{2}+1)!}\zeta_{1}^{\mathrm{dC}}(-N_{1},-N_{2}+u_{2},-2u_{2}-1,u_{2}+1;\mathbf{P(Q)},\mu_{1}).

(5.7)

6
General case

Now we proceed to the discussion of the general case. In this section we state our main general results. Recall that
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})\in(\mathbb{T}\setminus\{1\})^{n-1}
. Consider polynomials

P_{1},\ldots,P_{T-1}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}]

satisfying HDF, with
T\geq 1,\ n\geq 1
, and one more polynomial
P_{T}=P_{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
defined by (
1.5
) with
a_{0}<\cdots<a_{d}
being integers, where
Q_{0},\ldots,Q_{d}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}]
are polynomials satisfying HDF. Furthermore, if
n\geq 2
, we assume that (
2.4
) holds. We set
\alpha_{j}:=a_{j}-a_{0}

(1\leq j\leq d)
. Observe that the tuple
\boldsymbol{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers (i.e.
0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots<\alpha_{d}
).

In this section, we give the meromorphic continuation of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
to the whole complex space
{\mathbb{C}}^{T}
and describe the set of its singularities in Theorem
22
and Corollary
23
. We study its values at non-positive integer points in Theorem
24
.

Note that in the case
d=0
, when
P_{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=Q(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1})X_{n}^{a_{0}}
, the function
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

∎

By taking

7
Proofs of the theorems for the general case

In this section we prove the results stated in the preceding section.

In Subsection
7.1
, we prove that
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
has holomorphic continuation to a suitable domain, and via a Mellin transform we show an integral expression of it.

In Subsection
7.2
, we introduce two types of integrals,
I^{(r)}
and
J^{(r)}
, that both appear when we iterate to apply recursively the residue theorem on the integral expression formula for
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
. In Proposition
31
, we first show a relation between
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
and
I^{(d-1)}
and
J^{(d-1)}
, and a holomorphic term on a suitable domain. In Proposition
32
and Proposition
33
, we further apply the residue theorem to both integrals
I^{(r)}
and
J^{(r)}
, and we express them in terms of
I^{(r-1)}
and
J^{(r-1)}
, plus a holomorphic function on a suitable domain.

In Subsection
7.3
, we prove the meromorphic continuation of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
. We proceed by first applying Proposition
31
, then by recursively applying Propositions
32
and
33
. When doing that, we first have to restrict ourselves to the set
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{M},\boldsymbol{\eta})\subset\mathbb{C}^{T}
as defined in Theorem
22
, because it contains all the domains of holomorphy of all the intermediate holomorphic functions in Propositions
31
,
32
and
33
. By the induction principle, we will show that
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
can be expressed as a sum of meromorphic functions defined on a sufficiently large set.

In Subsection
7.4
, we study the values at non-positive integer points of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
by evaluating at non-positive integer points the formula obtained upon the study of the meromorphic continuation of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
in Theorem
22
.

7.1
Integral expression formula

Proposition 25
.

For all
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{d}>0
, the function
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
has a holomorphic continuation to the set

\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf{P}):=\left\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{C}^{T}\mid\sigma_{T}>\rho_{1}+\cdots+\rho_{d}\text{ and }a_{0}\sigma_{T}+\alpha_{1}\rho_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}\rho_{d}>1\right\}

given by

\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{d}}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho_{d})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}-|\mathbf{z}|)F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z})\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}}{\Gamma(s_{T})(a_{0}s_{T}+\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\mathbf{z}\rangle-1)}

(7.1)

where
F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z})
is defined in Definition
21
.

Proof.

Recall that by Proposition
9
,
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{P},\mu_{n-1})
absolutely converges in the domain
\Re s_{1},\ldots,\Re s_{T-1}>1/b
and
\Re s_{T}>1/a
. In order to prove the integral expression formula (
7.1
), we first consider
\rho^{\prime}_{1}>\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho^{\prime}_{d}>\rho_{d}
sufficiently large, and
\Re s_{1},\ldots,\Re s_{T}>\max(\frac{1}{a},\frac{1}{b})
sufficiently large so that the Dirichlet series

\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1}\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}}{Q_{0}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{T}-|\mathbf{z}|}\prod_{j=1}^{T-1}P_{j}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}Q_{i}(\mathbf{m})^{z_{i}}}

absolutely converges for all
z_{1}\in(\rho^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,z_{d}\in(\rho^{\prime}_{d})
. By the multiple Mellin-Barnes formula
(Proposition
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(

\displaystyle\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}}{\left(Q_{0}(\mathbf{m})m_{n}^{a_{0}}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{Q_{j}(\mathbf{m})}{Q_{0}(\mathbf{m})}m_{n}^{a_{j}-a_{0}}\right)\right)^{s_{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{T-1}P_{i}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{d}}\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 1}\int_{(\rho^{\prime}_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho^{\prime}_{d})}\frac{\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}}{Q_{0}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{T}-|\mathbf{z}|}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{T-1}P_{i}(\mathbf{m})^{s_{i}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{d}Q_{j}(\mathbf{m})^{z_{j}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 150.0pt\times\frac{1}{m_{n}^{a_{0}s_{T}+\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\mathbf{z}\rangle}}\times\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}-|\mathbf{z}|)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}}{\Gamma(s_{T})}.

By our assumption on
\mathbf{s}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}
, we can perform a series-integral inversion in order to obtain the formula (
7.1
) (with
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}
instead of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
). We then have

\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{d}}\int_{(\rho^{\prime}_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho^{\prime}_{d})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}-|\mathbf{z}|)F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z})\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}}{\Gamma(s_{T})(a_{0}s_{T}+\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\mathbf{z}\rangle-1)}.

(7.2)

We know by Proposition
6
that
F
is an entire function of moderate growth, thus it follows that the right-hand side of (
7.1
) is holomorphic on the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{P})
. Therefore, (
7.2
) gives an analytic continuation of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
to the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{P})
.

It remains to perform a shift to the left, from
(\rho^{\prime}_{1})\text{ to }(\rho_{1}),\ldots,(\rho^{\prime}_{d})\text{ to }(\rho_{d})
in (
7.2
). Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{P})\subset\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf{P})
. In the integrand of (
7.2
), there is no singularity in the variables
z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}
when
\rho_{1}\leq\Re z_{1}\leq\rho^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\rho_{d}\leq\Re z_{d}\leq\rho^{\prime}_{d}
. Therefore, shifting (
7.2
) to the left with respect to
z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}
introduces no singularities, and applying the residue theorem proves that (
7.1
) holds for all
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{P})
. We can then extend this result for all
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf{P})
by analytic continuation.
∎

It is clear that the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf{P})
does not contain any non-positive integer points
-\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}^{n}
, which is why we will perform many shifts to the left in the integral at the right-hand side of (
7.1
) in order to obtain a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex space. Such a shift is possible because the gamma-factor has exponential decay along vertical strips, and as mentioned in the proof above, the function
F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z})
also has moderate growth. For some technical reasons, we choose to perform the first shift to the left with respect to the variable
z_{d}
. Note that two types of poles will contribute in the residue theorem, one coming from

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{a_{0}s_{T}+\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\mathbf{z}\rangle-1}}
, and several poles coming from
\Gamma(z_{d})
.

If we iterate to apply the residue theorem, we then see some auxiliary integrals appearing in our computations. At each step, we expect to have two different types of poles to deal with, both of which look like the ones we described in the previous paragraph. Fortunately, by assuming some conditions on the real part of
s_{T}
, we will have one fewer pole contributing to the residue theorem.

\displaystyle g(\sigma_{T};\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{r},-\boldsymbol{\ell})

\displaystyle>\frac{-a_{0}(\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{0}})-\varepsilon+\sum_{j=r+1}^{d}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}+1}{\alpha_{d}}

\displaystyle>\frac{-a_{0}\delta-2\varepsilon+1}{\alpha_{d}}>0,

which proves (
7.10
).

By (
7.3
), we have
a_{d}\delta\geq a_{1}\delta>1+\varepsilon
. By (
7.5
), we have
(\alpha_{d}-\alpha_{1})\rho_{1}+\cdots+(\alpha_{d}-\alpha_{r})\rho_{r}\leq(\alpha_{d}-\alpha_{1})\rho_{1}+\cdots+(\alpha_{d}-\alpha_{d-1})\rho_{d-1}<\varepsilon
. Thus, we obtain

\widetilde{g}(\sigma_{T};\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{r},-\boldsymbol{\ell})>\frac{a_{d}\delta-\varepsilon+\sum_{j=r+1}^{d}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}-1}{\alpha_{d}}>\frac{a_{d}\delta-\varepsilon-1}{\alpha_{d}}>0,

which proves (
7.11
).

By (
7.3
), we have
a_{0}\sigma_{T}<a_{0}\delta+\varepsilon<1-\varepsilon
. By (
7.4
), we have
\alpha_{1}\rho_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}\rho_{r}<\varepsilon
. Thus we find

a_{0}\sigma_{T}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}\rho_{j}-1<0.

∎

7.2.1
A first shift to the left

Proposition 31
.

Let
M_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ \eta_{d}\in(0,1)
. For all
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{H}_{T}(\delta,\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{0}})

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(

\displaystyle\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})=\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}}J^{(d-1)}_{\emptyset}(\mathbf{s})+\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{M_{d}}\frac{(-1)^{k_{d}}}{k_{d}!}I^{(d-1)}_{k_{d}}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{d}\Gamma(s_{T})}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho_{d-1})}\int_{(-M_{d}-\eta_{d})}\frac{F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z})\Gamma(s_{T}-|\mathbf{z}|)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}}{a_{0}s_{T}+\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\mathbf{z}\rangle-1}.

Moreover, the last integral defines a holomorphic function on
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\varepsilon-M_{d}-\eta_{d},\frac{1-\varepsilon+M_{d}+\eta_{d}}{a_{0}})
.

Proof.

First observe that by (
7.6
) and (
7.7
), the set
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\delta,\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{a_{0}})
belongs to the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf{P})
, so we can use the integral expression (
7.1
) of
\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
. By shifting the path of integration from
(\rho_{d})
to
(-M_{d}-\eta_{d})
with respect to
z_{d}
in (

), we encounter two different types of singularities:

\displaystyle z_{d}=-k_{d}

\displaystyle\text{ from }\Gamma(z_{d})\qquad(0\leq k_{d}\leq M_{d}),

\displaystyle z_{d}=\frac{1-a_{0}s_{T}-\alpha_{1}z_{1}-\cdots-\alpha_{d-1}z_{d-1}}{\alpha_{d}}

\displaystyle\text{ from }\frac{1}{a_{0}s_{T}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}z_{j}-1}.

Note that by setting

. By Lemma

, we have
\Re(g(s_{T};\mathbf{z}))>0

for all

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{d-1}\alpha_{d}}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho_{d-1})}\frac{\Gamma(\widetilde{g}(s_{T};\mathbf{z}))\Gamma(g(s_{T};\mathbf{z}))}{\Gamma(s_{T})}

\displaystyle\hskip 232.0pt\times F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z},g(s_{T};\mathbf{z}))\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{M_{d}}\frac{(-1)^{k_{d}}}{(2\pi i)^{d-1}k_{d}!}\int_{(\rho_{1})}\cdots\int_{(\rho_{d-1})}\frac{\Gamma(s_{T}+k_{d}-|\mathbf{z}|)F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z},-k_{d})\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}}{\Gamma(s_{T})(a_{0}s_{T}\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|(\mathbf{z},-k_{d})\rangle-1)}

The first integral corresponds to
\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}}J^{(d-1)}_{\emptyset}(\mathbf{s})
. The integrals inside the sum correspond to
\frac{(-1)^{k_{d}}}{k_{d}!}I^{(d-1)}_{k_{d}}(\mathbf{s})\ (0\leq k_{d}\leq M_{d})
. The last integral defines a holomorphic function on
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\varepsilon-M_{d}-\eta_{d},\frac{1-\varepsilon+M_{d}+\eta_{d}}{a_{0}})
because the function

∎

The two integrals

31
. We can perform a similar shift towards the left, but this time we pass through fewer singularities than in the previous case. Indeed, by (

\displaystyle\hskip 171.0pt\times\frac{F(\mathbf{s};\mathbf{z},-k_{r},-\mathbf{k})\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\Gamma(z_{j})\,dz_{j}}{a_{0}s_{T}+\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\mathbf{z},-k_{r},-\mathbf{k}\rangle-1}

In Theorem

\zeta_{n}(-\mathbf{N};\mathbf{P};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n-1})
involves values of fully twisted multiple zeta-functions at integer points

In the second example, we study the simple case where

.
Lastly, in the third example, we look at a specific case where the number of variables in the Dirichlet series is

. In this case, we find that some special values

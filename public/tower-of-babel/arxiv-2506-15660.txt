On the upper bounds for the matrix spectral norm
A. Naumov, M. Rakhuba, D. Ryapolov, S. Samsonov

Abstract
We consider the problem of estimating the spectral norm of a matrix using only matrix-vector products. We propose a new Counterbalance estimator that provides upper bounds on the norm and derive probabilistic guarantees on its underestimation. Compared to standard approaches such as the power method, the proposed estimator produces significantly tighter upper bounds in both synthetic and real-world settings. Our method is especially effective for matrices with fast-decaying spectra, such as those arising in deep learning and inverse problems.



Keywords—
Spectral norm, Probabilistic bounds, Randomized methods

Introduction

Estimating the spectral norm of an implicitly defined matrix using only matrix-vector products (matvecs) is a fundamental problem in numerical linear algebra and machine learning. In many applications, one cannot access the entire matrix
A
, but it is possible to compute products
Ax
and sometimes
A^{\top}x
for chosen vectors
x
. The challenge is to estimate
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
(or related norms) from above with few matvecs. This scenario arises, for example, in sensitivity analysis of matrix functions and condition number estimation
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, and in regularizing deep neural networks by enforcing Lipschitz continuity (bounding layer Jacobian norms)
[
4
]
. Traditional deterministic approaches like the power method estimate
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
from below and require multiple sequential iterations until convergence, which may be infeasible if each matvec is expensive. Randomized algorithms provide attractive alternatives: by multiplying
A
with random test vectors, one can obtain unbiased or statistically reliable estimates of matrix norms much faster in practice. The main advantage of randomized methods is the fact that the required matvecs can be computed in parallel.

Perhaps the simplest approach to estimate
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
is to compute matvec
AX
for a random vector
X
and use the norm
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
for an estimator. Specifically, let
X
be a random vector of appropriate dimension with
\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{2}=1
, and consider the scalar
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
.
This quantity is always at most
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
, and an early work by Dixon
[
5
]
established probabilistic guarantees for this estimator: for a random vector
X
drawn uniformly from the sphere
\mathbb{S}_{n-1}
, and a symmetric positive-definite matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
one has

\mathbb{P}(\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\leq\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2})\geq 1-0.8\sqrt{n}\theta^{-1/2}\,,

(1)

for any
\theta>1
. Equivalently, with high probability,
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
underestimates the true norm
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
by at most a factor of
\theta
that grows with the ambient dimension
d
. In practice, one can boost the reliability of norm estimates (
1
) by averaging or taking maxima over multiple independent statistics, see
[
5
,
6
]
. If
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
are
k
independent random vectors, a natural improvement of (
1
) is the maximum observed norm:

T(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\left\lVert AX_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\,.

(2)

This multi-sample approach reduces the risk of a “bad” draw that misses the top singular vector and can be straightforwardly parallelized.

It is important to note that these basic randomized estimators tend to underestimate the true norm. Indeed,
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
for any unit vector
X
. In some applications, a probabilistic upper bound on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
is desired. One can obtain an upper bound by scaling the test statistic (
1
) or (
2
) by an appropriate factor. Indeed, setting
\theta(\delta)=(4/5)^{2}n/\delta^{2}
in (
1
) yields

\mathbb{P}(\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\leq\theta(\delta)\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2})\geq 1-\delta\,.

This bound has unfavorable scaling with dimension
n
, and can be improved if one considers the statistic
T(\theta,X)=\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
with other distribution of test vector
X
, as suggested in
[
6
]
. In particular, for a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and a vector
X
with normal distribution
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{n})
, it holds that (see
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
):

\mathbb{P}(\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{\theta}\,.

(3)

The bound (
3
) can not be improved in general, as it is tight for matrices
A
with
\operatorname{rk}{A}=1
. Note that the estimator
\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
typically has smaller underestimation probability
\mathbb{P}(\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})
compared to the upper bound (
3
), since

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}^{2}]=\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\,.

Using the apparatus of concentration of measure inequalities
[
7
,
8
]
, one can study the concentration properties of
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
around its expectation, which can be shown to be close to
\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
. Thus, when we consider the matrix
A
with
\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\gg\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
, one can expect that
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
will significantly overestimate
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. This property can be formalized using the concept of
effective rank

\rho=\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}/\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. At the same time, the upper bound of the underestimation probability (
3
) does not depend upon
\rho
. This raises the following dilemma, which can be formulated as follows. Suppose that we use a statistic
T(\theta,X)
(for example,
T(\theta,X)=\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
) to provide an upper bound on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. Suppose also that we are able to provide an upper bound

\mathbb{P}(T(\theta,X)\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\leq g(\theta)

(4)

for some function
g(\theta)
that is independent of
A
. Then tighter expressions for
g(\theta)
requires additional information about
A
, such as knowledge about the effective rank
\rho
. Typically, this information is not available for a statistician. Hence, a natural question arises:

Can we construct a simple estimator for
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
, which is agnostic to the structure of
A
and provides tighter upper bound on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
, compared to (
3
)?

We provide details on the methods for comparing different estimators of
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
and discuss precise meaning of the phrase ”tight upper bound” in
Section

3
. Our main contributions are as follows:

•

We propose a new
Counterbalance
(CB for short) estimator
T^{\operatorname{cb}}(\theta,X)
, which provides an upper bounds on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
, and derive bounds on its underestimation probability.

\mathbb{P}(T^{\operatorname{cb}}(\theta,X)\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\,.

The term “counterbalance” comes from the additional summand, that we add to the classical estimator of a form (
3
), which noticeably sharpens our bound for low-rank matrices
A
. We also determine the way of selecting the parameter
\theta
for our estimator, which does not require information about singular values of
A
.
We show that the novel estimator allows for constructing tighter upper bounds for the operator norm of the underlying matrix and fixed underestimation probability, compared to baseline methods.

•

We perform a number of numerical simulations, illustrating the benefits of our method on various matrices with various ranks, effective ranks, and structure of singular values. We also consider the example of upper bounding the spectral norm of Jacobian of layers of ResNet neural networks. We show that our estimator allows for constructing tighter upper bounds for the operator norm of the underlying matrices for fixed underestimation probability, compared to baseline methods.

Notations

For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we write
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
for its spectral norm and
\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
for its Frobenius norm. We denote by
\rho
its
effective rank

\rho=\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}/\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Given that
A
admits a singular value decomposition (SVD)
A=U\Sigma V^{\top}
,
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})
, where
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\sigma_{r}>0
are the corresponding singular values, effective rank of
A
can be written as
\rho=(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2})/\sigma_{1}^{2}
. We also denote by
\chi_{(1,\alpha)}^{2}(t)=\mathbb{P}\left(\xi^{2}+\alpha\eta^{2}\leq t\right)
, where
\xi,\eta
are i.i.d. random variables with standard normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, and write
p_{(1,\alpha)}(t)
for the corresponding density function. For a statistic
T(\theta,X)
that aims to upper bound
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
, we refer to the quantity
\mathbb{P}\left(T(\theta,X)\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\right)
as the underestimation probability. In the present text, the following abbreviations are used: “w.r.t.” stands for “with respect to”, “i.i.d.” - for ”independent and
identically distributed”, “r.h.s.” - for “right-hand side”.

[

considered the problem of estimating

. We briefly mention a number of papers, which focus on estimating Frobenius norm or trace of a matrix

[

Methodology for comparing upper bounds on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}

In this section, we present the methodology for comparing two randomized upper bounds
T(\theta,Y)
and
\hat{T}(\theta,Z)
for the operator norm
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. Here we assume that the estimators
T
and
\hat{T}
rely on their own sets of random variables, denoted by
Y
and
Z
, respectively. These estimators may differ in the number of random vectors used for matvec computations. We only require that both
T(\theta,Y)
and
\hat{T}(\theta,Z)
depend on a scalar parameter
\theta\geq 1
and are monotonically increasing in
\theta
.

Oracle method

For the first method of comparing
T
and
\hat{T}
, we assume that the cumulative distribution functions
\mathbb{P}\left(T(\theta,Y)\leq t\right),\ \mathbb{P}\left(\hat{T}(\theta,Z)\leq t\right)
can be computed analytically. We fix the desired underestimation probability
\delta\in(0,1)
and calculate
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
such that

\begin{split}\theta_{1}&=\inf\{\theta:\mathbb{P}(T(\theta,Y)\leq\|A\|_{2})\leq\delta\}\,,\\
\theta_{2}&=\inf\{\theta:\mathbb{P}(\hat{T}(\theta,Z)\leq\|A\|_{2})\leq\delta\}\,.\end{split}

(5)

This choice of parameters
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
guarantees the underestimation probability is exactly equal to
\delta
. Now we compare the distributions of
T
and
\hat{T}
. We say that
T
is tighter than
\hat{T}
if it is closer to
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. To formalize this relationship, we compare the mean absolute error (MAE) of these statistics, that is, we say that
T
is tighter than
\hat{T}
, if

\mathbb{E}\bigl|T(\theta_{1},Y)-\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\bigr|<\mathbb{E}\bigl|\hat{T}(\theta_{2},Z)-\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\bigr|\,.

(6)

However, typically we do not have access to the analytical forms of the distributions of
T
and
\hat{T}
. Consequently, we cannot find the exact values
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
satisfying (
5
).

Realizable method

The choice of parameters
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
suggested in (
5
) is typically not feasible in practice. Instead, we rely on upper bounds on the underestimation probabilities to define our estimators. Formally, we assume that for any
\theta\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(T(\theta,Y)\leq\|A\|_{2})&\leq g(\theta)\,,\\
\mathbb{P}(\hat{T}(\theta,Z)\leq\|A\|_{2})&\leq\hat{g}(\theta)\,,\end{split}

(7)

where
g(\theta)
and
\hat{g}(\theta)
are known decreasing functions. We then define the parameters as follows:

\begin{split}\theta_{1}&=\inf\{\theta:g(\theta)\leq\delta\}\,,\\
\theta_{2}&=\inf\{\theta:\hat{g}(\theta)\leq\delta\}\,.\end{split}

(8)

Given (
7
), these parameters ensure that both
T(\theta_{1},Y)
and
\hat{T}(\theta_{2},Z)
have an underestimation probability at most
\delta
. We can then compare these estimators using the MAE criteria as in (
6
). Additionally, we can evaluate the tightness of the upper bounds
g(\theta)
and
\hat{g}(\theta)
by computing the probabilities

\mathbb{P}(T(\theta_{1},Y)\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\,,\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{P}(\hat{T}(\theta_{2},Z)\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\,,

where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
are defined by (
8
). We provide steps of the algorithm with corresponding choice of
\theta
in
Algorithm

Counterbalance algorithm

The estimator that we suggest refines the classical single-matvec estimator given by (
3
). We briefly recall here advantages and drawbacks of this estimator. Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{n})
, and consider the statistic
\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
,
\theta\geq 0
. In this case the following upper bound is known (see
[
6
]
):

\mathbb{P}(\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{\theta}\,.

(9)

The main advantage of (
9
) is that its r.h.s. does not depend on any properties of
A
(singular values, effective rank, etc), which are typically unknown in practice. At the same time, for matrices
A
with large effective rank
\rho
,
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
significantly overestimates
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. Tighter upper bounds on the underestimation probability in (
9
) typically depend on
\rho
, which is unknown in practice. This generally implies too large values of
\theta
and loose upper bound
\theta\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. When the budget of
k
matvecs is available, we use the modification of (
9
) of the form

T^{\operatorname{v}}_{k}(\theta,X)=\theta\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\left\lVert AX_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\,,

(10)

which we further denote as Vanilla estimator. Here
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
are i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{n})
random vectors. In this case we rely on the corresponding upper bound

\mathbb{P}(T^{\operatorname{v}}_{k}(\theta,X)\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2})\leq\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{\theta}\right)^{k}\,.

(11)

Our method

The source of potential ineffectiveness of (
9
) is that this bound can not be improved in case of matrices
A
with
\operatorname{rk}{A}=1
. At the same time, it is known that for rank-one matrix
A
, the iterates of power method
[
6
, Chapter 6]
applied to symmetric matrix
A
will converge in
1
step to the true value
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
. Similar ideas can be traced to the Wedderburn rank reduction procedure, see
[
16
,
17
]
. Consider the ratio

\frac{\left\lVert A^{\top}AY\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}}\,,\hskip 9.24994ptY\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I})\,.

(12)

It provides a tight lower bound on
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
if its effective rank
\rho
is close to
1
. In particular, if
\operatorname{rk}{A}=1
,
\frac{\left\lVert A^{\top}AY\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}}=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
for almost all
Y
. At the same time,
\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}
tends to overestimate
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}
as
\rho
increases. Hence, it is natural to combine (
12
) with
\left\lVert AX\right\rVert_{2}
. As a result, given independent random vectors
X_{1},X_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{n})
, and
X=(X_{1},X_{2})
, we define our estimator
T^{\operatorname{cb}}(\theta,X)
as

T^{\operatorname{cb}}(\theta,X)=\theta\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\top}AX_{1}\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert AX_{1}\right\rVert_{2}}\right)^{2}+\left\lVert AX_{2}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\,.

(13)

Below we provide a theoretical bounds, which allows to set the parameter
\theta
in (
13
) for desired underestimation probability in
Section

4.1
. Second, (
13
) requires
3
matvec computations, hence, it can be treated as a counterpart of (
10
) used with
k=3
vectors. Note also that the suggested statistic
T^{\operatorname{cb}}(\theta,X)
relies on two independent random vectors
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. This property is crucial to provide an upper bound on the underestimation probability in
Section

4.1
below. At the same time, in practice it is possible to use a single random vector at this stage. We leave the detailed analysis of (
13
) with a single random vector
X
as an interesting direction for future work. Finally, our method requires to compute matvecs with
A^{\top}
, which is a limitation of our method.

Conclusion

In this paper, we present the Counterbalance estimator
T^{\operatorname{cb}}(\theta,X)
, a hybrid norm estimator that improves upon classical randomized methods by reducing underestimation probability across the whole range of matrix types. Our theoretical guarantees and empirical evaluations demonstrate that this approach outperforms the baseline estimators on both low- and high-rank matrices. Our method remains computationally efficient and highly parallelizable, making it suitable for modern applications such as neural network analysis and large-scale matrix computations.

In this section we write
\chi^{2}_{k}(t)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, for the distribution function of the chi-squared distribution with
k
degrees of freedom.
In particular, we write
\chi^{2}_{1}(t)
instead of
\chi^{2}_{(1,0)}(t)
for conciseness.

Our proof is based on the two key auxiliary results. First, our proof relies heavily on the following property of weighted chi-squared distribution, which is due to
[
20
]
:

Theorem 2

(Theorem 2 from
[
20
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
be i.i.d random values with distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, and set
Q_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}
, where
\lambda_{i}>0
for any
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
. Then, for any
x\in[0;1]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}:\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1}\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n}\leq x\right)=\chi_{1}^{2}(x)\,.

Second, we provide an upper bound for the underestimation probability in randomized power method
[
21
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{n})
. For any
t\in[0,1]
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\top}AY\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}}\leq\sqrt{t}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\right)\leq\chi_{1}^{2}\left(\dfrac{(\rho-1)t}{1-t}\right).

This result reveals that the upper bound a priori for the effective dimension of
A
guarantees a satisfying underestimation probability. In this work we use
\theta\geq 1
to provide an estimator given apriori underestimation probability
p
. Then we represent the above inequality as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\theta\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\top}AY\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}}\leq\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\right)\leq\chi_{1}^{2}\left(\dfrac{(\rho-1)}{\theta^{2}-1}\right).

Proof of Lemma
1
.

First, recall singular decomposition
A=USV^{\top}
, where
S=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k})
, then
\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum\sigma_{i}^{2}\xi_{i}^{2}
, where
\xi=V^{\top}Y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\top}AY\right\rVert_{2}}{\left\lVert AY\right\rVert_{2}}\leq\sqrt{t}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{\sum\sigma_{i}^{4}\xi_{i}^{2}}{\sum\sigma_{i}^{2}\xi_{i}^{2}}\leq\sigma_{1}^{2}t\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma_{1}^{4}\left(1-t\right)\xi_{1}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{k}\xi_{i}^{2}\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}t\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{i}^{4}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}\leq(1-t)^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\xi_{i}^{2}t\dfrac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\,.

(17)

It is easy to check that
\chi_{1}^{2}(u)=\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}\leq u\right)
is concave on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
k=\operatorname{rk}{A}
. Define a random variable
\eta
by the relation

\eta=(1-t)^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\xi_{i}^{2}t\dfrac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\,.

Note that
\eta
is non-negative and independent of
\xi_{1}^{2}
. Moreover,

\mathbb{E}[\eta]=\frac{(\rho-1)t}{1-t}\,.

Using Jensen’s inequality we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}\leq\eta\right)&=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathbb{I}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}\leq\eta\right)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\bigl[\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{1}^{2}}\bigl[\mathbb{I}(\xi_{1}^{2}\leq\eta)|\eta\bigr]\bigr]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\bigl[\chi_{1}^{2}(\eta)\bigr]\leq\chi_{1}^{2}(\mathbb{E}[\eta]).\end{split}

(18)

Combining (
17
) and (
18
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}\leq(1-t)^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\xi_{i}^{2}\dfrac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq\chi_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{(\rho-1)t}{1-t}\right)\,,

and the statement follows.

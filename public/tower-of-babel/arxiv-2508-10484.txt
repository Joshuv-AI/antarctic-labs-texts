Counting w w -coprime S S -integers and S S -integral ideals in positive characteristic
Si-Han Liu, Zhe-Cheng Liu, and Jia-Yan Yao

Abstract
. Let 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} be the finite field with q q elements, and K K an algebraic function field over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} with 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} as its field of constants. Let S S be a finitenonempty set of prime divisors over K K , and 𝒪 S \mathscr{O}_{S} be the ring of integers of K K attached to S S . Let w ⩾ 1 w\geqslant 1 be an integer. In this work we shall count w w -coprime S S -integers and S S -integral ideals, and our proofs are a combination of analytic methods and the Riemann-Roch theorem and the Weil theorem for function fields in positive characteristic.

11R45, 11R59

§1.
Introduction

It is well known that the probability for two natural numbers chosen at random to be coprime is equal to
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
(see for example
[
8
, pp. 114–115]
). This result has been generalized in two directions.
On the one hand, one can consider the points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, where each point can be identified with two coprime integers in
\mathbb{Z}
, and it has been shown (see for example
[
9
, p. 71]
) that

N(B)=\frac{6B^{2}}{\pi^{2}}+O({B\log B}),

where for all
B>0
, we denote by
N(B)
the numbers of points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
with height
\leqslant B
.
More generally, let
\mathbb{K}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and
n\geqslant 2
an integer, Schanuel
[
17
]
obtained in 1979
the asymptotic formula for
N_{\mathbb{K}}(B)
with
B>0
, which is the number of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\mathbb{K})
with height
\leqslant B
.
An analog result for algebraic function fields was obtained by Wan
[
27
]
in 1991.

On the other hand, for all integers
n,m\geqslant 1
, one can also consider

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptV_{m}(n)=\mathrm{Card}\big(\big\{(x_{1},,...,x_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}:1\leqslant x_{j}\leqslant n,\,\textrm{$x_{1},...,x_{m}$~are coprime}\big\}\big).

It was shown by Lehmer
[
10
]
in 1900 that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{V_{m}(n)}{n^{m}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(m)}
.

Now let
w\geqslant 1
be an integer. If
x_{1},...,x_{m}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
do not have any common factor of the form
k^{w}
with
k\geqslant 2
a prime number, then we say that

x_{1},...,x_{m}
are
w
-coprime. Let
V_{m}^{w}(n)
be the number of points
(x_{1},,...,x_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
such that
x_{1},...,x_{m}
are
w
-coprime, and
1\leqslant x_{j}\leqslant n\,(1\leqslant j\leqslant m)
.
For
wm\geqslant 2
, Benkoski
[
3
]
showed in 1976 that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{V_{m}^{w}(n)}{n^{m}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(wm)}
.
It is worthy to point out that
V_{1}^{2}(n)
is the number of squarefree integers among
1,2,...,n
, and that
x_{1},...,x_{m}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
are coprime if and only if they are
1
-coprime. Some generalized results in rational field can be found, for instance, in
[
7
,
1
,
26
]

As a natural generalization, one can consider
w
-coprime algebraic integers.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with
d=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]
.
Let
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
be the ring of algebraic integers of
\mathbb{K}
,
and
{\bf E}=\{{\bf e_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{d}
a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
. For
B>0
, define

\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}(B,{\bf E})=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}{n_{i}{\bf e_{i}}}\colon n_{j}\in[-B,B)\cap\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Fix
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
, and let
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\rangle
be the ideal generated by

a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
.
Let
w\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w\geqslant 1
. If
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\rangle
cannot be contained in
\mathfrak{p}^{w}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
,
then we say that
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
are
w
-coprime.
The set of all
w
-coprime
m
-triples
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is denoted by
T_{w}
.
Note that
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
are
1
-coprime if
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\rangle=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
,
and when we restrict to the case that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
,

a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
are
1
-coprime if and only if
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
are coprime.
Ferraguti and Micheli
[
4
]
showed in 2016 that

\lim_{B\to+\infty}\frac{|T_{1}\cap\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}(B,{\bf E})|}{(2B)^{dm}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(m)},

where
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
is the Dedekind zeta function of
\mathbb{K}
.
Note here that the above limit is independent of the
integral basis
{\bf E}
, and is called the density of
T_{1}
.
Micheli and Schnyde
[
13
]
considered the density in function fields,
and one can compare their results with ours (see Remark
5
below).

Likewise one can consider
w
-coprime integral ideals instead of elements.
Let
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
be integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
, and let
\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle
be the ideal generated by
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
. If
\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
,
then we say that
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
are coprime. If
\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle
cannot be contained in
\mathfrak{q}^{w}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
,
then we say that
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
are
w
-coprime.
For all
x>0
,
let
H(x)
denote the number of integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
with norm
\leqslant x
, and let
Q_{m}^{w}(x)
denote the number of
m
-tuples
(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m})
of
w
-coprime integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{K}}
with norm
\leqslant x
.
In this direction, Sittinger
[
18
]
showed in 2010 that

\lim\limits_{x\to+\infty}\frac{Q_{m}^{w}(x)}{(H(x))^{m}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(mw)}.

Under assuming the Extended Lindelöf Hypothesis (ELH), Takeda
[
22
]
obtained the asymptotic formula

(1)

Q_{m}^{1}(x)=\frac{c_{\mathbb{K}}x^{m}}{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(m)}+O(x^{m-1/2+\varepsilon}),

where
c_{\mathbb{K}}>0
is a constant depending only on
\mathbb{K}
. Later, Takeda
[
23
]
obtained the asymtotic formula without assuming the ELH, but the error term is worse than before.
For other results of the same type over number fields, see for examples
[
19
,
24
,
20
]
.
Similar problems have also been studied over polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, see for example
[
14
,
6
,
2
,
11
,
21
]
.

By the way, it is worthy to point out that all these results are related to zeta values.
Fujita and Yoshida
[
5
]
gave some applications of the zeta distribution (defined by the zeta values)
to number theory, especially several probabilistic proofs of the equality between the Riemann zeta function and some number-theoretic functions.

Inspired by all the above results, in the present work we shall proceed under the setting of general algebraic function fields in positive characteristic,
and exhibit results with relatively better error terms estimates.

We begin with some definitions and notation.

Fix
p\geqslant 2
a prime number,
q=p^{\kappa}
with
\kappa\geqslant 1
an integer,
and
K
an algebraic function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,
K
is a finite extension of some rational function field

\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
. We also suppose that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is algebraically closed in
K
, and call it the field of constants of
K
.
Then
K\cap\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, here
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let
M_{K}
be the set of all prime divisors over
K
. We denote by
\mathrm{Div}_{K}
the group of divisors over
K
, i.e.,
the free abelian group generated by
M_{K}
. As usual, we shall note it additively:

\mathrm{Div}_{K}=\bigg\{\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}}c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}:c_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathbb{Z},\text{and $c_{\mathfrak{p}}=0$ except for a finite number of $\mathfrak{p}$'s}~\bigg\}.

Fix
D=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}}c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Div}_{K}
. If for all
\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}
, we have
c_{\mathfrak{p}}\geqslant 0
, then we say that
D
is effective, and write
D\geqslant 0
.

Fix
r\geqslant 0
an integer, and
S=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},...,\mathfrak{p}_{r+1}\}
a subset of
M_{K}
. Put

\mathscr{O}_{S}=\big\{a\in K:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)\geqslant 0,\ \textrm{for all $\mathfrak{p}\notin S$}\big\},

and call it the ring of
S
-integers of
K
, whose elements are called
S
-integers.

Fix
a_{1},...,a_{m}\in\mathscr{O}_{S}
, and let
\langle a_{1},...,a_{m}\rangle_{S}
be the ideal generated by
a_{1},...,a_{m}
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
. If
\langle a_{1},...,a_{m}\rangle_{S}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
,
then we say that
a_{1},...,a_{m}
are coprime. Let
w\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w\geqslant 1
. If
\langle a_{1},...,a_{m}\rangle_{S}
cannot be contained in
\mathfrak{q}^{w}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
,
then we say that
a_{1},...,a_{m}
are
w
-coprime. As in the canonical case, the fact that
a_{1},...,a_{m}
are coprime just means that they are
1
-coprime.

For all
a\in K^{\times}
, put
(a)=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}}v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Div}_{K}
, and call it a principal divisor.
By convention, we put
(0)=+\infty
, and write
(0)\geqslant D
, for all
D\in\mathrm{Div}_{K}
.

Let
\vec{N}=(N_{1},...,N_{r+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
such that
N:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}N_{j}\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}>0
. Put

\displaystyle D(\vec{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}N_{j}\mathfrak{p}_{j},

\displaystyle L\big(D(\vec{N})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{a\in K:(a)+D(\vec{N})\geqslant 0\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{a\in\mathscr{O}_{S}:v_{\mathfrak{p}_{j}}(a)\geqslant-N_{j}~(1\leqslant j\leqslant r+1)\big\},

\displaystyle V_{m}^{w}(\vec{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\big(\big\{(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\big(L(D(\vec{N}))\big)^{m}:\text{$a_{1},...,a_{m}$ are $w$-coprime}\big\}\big).

With the above definitions and notation, we have the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\vec{N}=(N_{1},...,N_{r+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
such that
N=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}N_{j}\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}>0
.
Then for all integers
w,m\geqslant 1
with
wm\geqslant 2
, we have

V_{m}^{w}(\vec{N})=\frac{q^{m(N+1-g_{K})}}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}+O_{K}(q^{N/w}),

where the constant involved in
O_{K}
only depends on
K
and can be determinedexplicitly,

g_{K}
is the genus of
K
, and
\zeta_{S}
is the
S
-zeta function defined by

\zeta_{S}(s)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}\setminus S}(1-q^{-s\deg\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}\quad(\mathrm{Re}(s)>1).

Remark 1
.

Note that
s=1
is a pole of
\zeta_{K}
, hence the above result fails in the case
that
w=m=1
, which corresponds to the counting in
\mathscr{O}_{S}^{\times}
(the unit group of
\mathscr{O}_{S}
).
The latter is easy to count, for
\mathscr{O}_{S}^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{r}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
(see Proposition 14.2 in
[
16
, p. 243]
).
The case that
m=1
and
w=2

can also be treated by a different argument with better error estimate (see
[
12
]
).

Now let
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
be integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
, and let
\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle_{S}
be the ideal generated by
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
. If
\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle_{S}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
,
then we say that
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
are coprime. If
\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle_{S}
cannot be contained in
\mathfrak{q}^{w}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
,then we say that
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}
are
w
-coprime. Finally for all integers
n\geqslant 1
, we denote by

Q_{S,m}^{w}(n)
the number of
m
-tuples
(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m})
of
w
-coprime integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
with
\deg\mathfrak{a}_{j}\leqslant n
(
1\leqslant j\leqslant m)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
w,m\geqslant 1
be integers such that
wm\geqslant 2
. Then

\displaystyle Q_{S,m}^{w}(n)=\frac{c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}+\begin{cases}O_{K,S}(q^{n/w}),&\text{if}\quad m=1,\\
O_{K,S}(nq^{n}),&\text{if}\quad m=2,w=1,\\
O_{K,S}(q^{n(m-1)}),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
c_{S}=\frac{h_{K}q^{1-g_{K}}}{(q-1)^{2}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}\big(1-q^{-\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}}\big)
, and
h_{K}
is the class number of
K
.

Remark 2
.

For the same reason as for Theorem
1
, the above result fails in the case
that
w=m=1
, which corresponds to the integral ideal
\mathscr{O}_{S}
, and thus
Q_{S,1}^{1}(n)=1
.

§2.
Some preliminary results

In this section, we shall present some preparatory results. We begin with the inclusion-exclusion principle in positive characteristic
which is an exact analog for the classical result (see for example
[
25
, p. 39]
).

Let
\mathrm{Div}_{S}
be the subgroup generated by
M_{K}\setminus S
in
\mathrm{Div}_{K}
.
For all divisors

D=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}\setminus S}c_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},

put
\mathrm{supp}D=\big\{\mathfrak{p}:c_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\big\}
, and we say that
D
is squarefree if for all

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{supp}D
, we have
c_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
. Finally we define

\displaystyle\mu_{S}(D)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\textrm{if $D=0$},\\
(-1)^{t},&\textrm{if $t=\mathrm{Card}\big(\mathrm{supp}D\big)$, and $D$ is squarefree},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and call
\mu_{S}
the Möbius function over
\mathrm{Div}_{S}
. Note that
\mu_{S}
is multiplicative, i.e.,
if
D_{1},D_{2}\in\mathrm{Div}_{S}
such that
\mathrm{supp}D_{1}\cap\,\mathrm{supp}D_{2}=\emptyset
, then

\mu_{S}(D_{1}+D_{2})=\mu_{S}(D_{1})\mu_{S}(D_{2})
.
Moreover if for all
D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S}
, we define

\delta(D)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\textrm{if}\ D=0,\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

then for all effective divisors
D
, we have the inverse Möbius formula

\sum\limits_{0\leqslant D^{\prime}\leqslant D}\mu_{S}(D^{\prime})=\delta(D),

whose proof proceeds as in the classical case by the multiplicativity of
\mu_{S}

and the fact
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\mu_{S}(j\mathfrak{p})=\delta(n\mathfrak{p})
, for all
\mathfrak{p}\in M_{k}\setminus S
and for all integers
n\geqslant 0
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a finite set, and
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{j}:1\leqslant j\leqslant k\}
a set of properties. For each subset
I
of
\mathscr{P}
, denote by
A(I)
the number of elements
a
in
\mathscr{A}
which satisfy all the properties in
I
. In particular, we have
A(\emptyset)=\mathrm{Card}(\mathscr{A})
. Let
S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})
the number of elements
a
in
\mathscr{A}
which do not satisfy any property in
\mathscr{P}
. Finally we fix
k
distinct prime divisors
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2},\ldots\mathfrak{q}_{k}
in
M_{K}\setminus S
.

Lemma 1
.

With the above notation, we have

S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})=\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{P}}\mu_{S}(D_{I})A(I),

where for all
I\subseteq\mathscr{P}
, we have
D_{I}=\sum\limits_{P_{j}\in I}\mathfrak{q}_{j}
.

Proof.

Put
P=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathfrak{q}_{j}
.
For all
a\in\mathscr{A}
, we define

F(a)=\sum\limits_{a\in P_{j},\atop 1\leqslant j\leqslant k}\mathfrak{q}_{j},

where
a\in P_{j}
means that
a
satisfies the property
P_{j}
. Then

F(a)=0
if and only if
a
does not satisfy any property
P_{j}\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant k)
. Thus

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\delta(F(a))=\sum_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\sum_{0\leqslant D\leqslant F(a)}\mu_{S}(D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant D\leqslant P}\mu_{S}(D)\sum_{a\in\mathscr{A},\atop D\leqslant F(a)}1.

Note that for all effective divisors
D
, we have
D\leqslant F(a)
if and only if

\mathrm{Supp}D\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}F(a)\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}P,\ \textrm{and $D$ is squarefree},

i.e.,
D=D_{I}
for some
I\subseteq\mathscr{P}
, from which we deduce directly

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{P}}\mu_{S}(D_{I})\sum_{a\in\mathscr{A},\atop a\in P_{j}\in I}1=\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{P}}\mu_{S}(D_{I})A(I).

Hence the desired result holds.
∎

Remark 3
.

By Theorem 14.5 in
[
16
, p. 249]
, we can identify the nonzero prime ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
with the prime divisors in
M_{K}\setminus S
, and for such a prime ideal (resp. prime divisor)
\mathfrak{p}
,
we have the same localization
\mathscr{O}_{S,\mathfrak{p}}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see
[
16
, pp. 247–248]
).
Then a nonzero integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
can be identified with the following divisor

D(\mathfrak{m})=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{k}\setminus S}m_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p},

where
m_{\mathfrak{p}}\geqslant 0
is an integer and determined by
\mathfrak{m}\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\pi^{m_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a prime element in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
The norm of
\mathfrak{m}
is defined by
\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{m})=\mathrm{Card}\big(\mathscr{O}_{S}/\mathfrak{m}\big)=q^{\deg\mathfrak{m}}
,
where

\deg\mathfrak{m}:=\deg D(\mathfrak{m})=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{k}\setminus S}m_{\mathfrak{p}}\deg\mathfrak{p}

is called the degree of
\mathfrak{m}
. In particular, we have
\deg\mathscr{O}_{S}=0
.
By convention, the degree of the zero ideal is
+\infty
.
Finally define
\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mu_{S}(D(\mathfrak{m}))
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\text{if }\mathfrak{m}=\mathscr{O}_{S},\\
(-1)^{t},&\text{if }\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{n}_{t}\text{ with }\mathfrak{n}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{n}_{t}\text{ distinct prime ideals in $\mathscr{O}_{S}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and we call
\mu_{S}
the Möbius function over
\mathscr{O}_{S}
. By the same argument, one can see that the inclusion-exclusion principle also holds for this new
\mu_{S}
, namely with the notation before Lemma
1
, we have

S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})=\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{P}}\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{m}_{I})A(I),

where
\mathfrak{m}_{I}=\prod\limits_{P_{j}\in I}\mathfrak{q}_{j}
, for all
I\subseteq\mathscr{P}
, and
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}_{k}
are distinct prime ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
.

For all
D\in\mathrm{Div}_{K}
, define

L(D)=\big\{a\in K^{\times}:(a)+D\geqslant 0\big\}\cup\{0\}.

Then
L(D)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space with finite dimension, and we denote its dimension by
\ell(D)
.
The famous Riemann-Roch theorem can be reformulated as follows (see for example
[
16
, Theorem 5.4, p. 49]
).

Lemma 2
.

There exist an integer
g_{K}\geqslant 0
(called the genus of
K
) and the class of canonical divisors
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathrm{Div}_{K}
such that for all
C\in\mathscr{C}
and all
D\in\mathrm{Div}_{K}
, we have

\ell(D)=\deg D-g_{K}+1+\ell(C-D).

In particular, if
\deg D\geqslant 2g_{K}-1
, then
\ell(D)=\deg D-g_{K}+1
.

We need also the famous Weil theorem (see for example
[
16
, pp. 53-55]
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
g_{K}
be the genus of
K
, and
\zeta_{K}(s)=\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}}\big(1-q^{-s\deg\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{-1}
the zeta function over
K
. Then

\zeta_{K}(s)=\frac{P_{K}(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-s})(1-q^{1-s})},

where
P_{K}(u)
is a polynomial with integer coefficients such that

P_{K}(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{2g_{K}}(1-\pi_{j}u),

and
|\pi_{j}|=q^{1/2}\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant 2g_{K})
. In particular, we have
q^{g_{K}}P_{K}(q^{-1})=P_{K}(1)=h_{K}
, where
h_{K}
is
the class number of
K
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
n\geqslant 0
be an integer, and
j_{S}(n)
the number of integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
with degree
\leqslant n
.
Then we have

(4)

j_{S}(n)=\frac{h_{K}G_{S}(q^{-1})q^{n-g_{K}+1}}{(q-1)^{2}}+O_{K,S}(1),

where
G_{S}(z)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}\big(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}}\big)
, and
O_{K,S}(1)
is a function in
n
such that there exists a constant
C>0
only depending on
K,S
satisfying
|O_{K,S}(1)|\leqslant C
for all integers
n\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

Recall that the
S
-zeta function can be also defined as follows:

\zeta_{S}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{1}{(\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{a}))^{s}}\quad(\mathrm{Re}(s)>1),

where
\mathfrak{a}
varies over all integral ideals of
\mathscr{O}_{S}
. For all integers
k\geqslant 0
, denote by
b_{k}
(resp.
b_{S,k}
)
the number of effective divisors in
\mathrm{Div}_{K}
(resp.
\mathrm{Div}_{S}
) with degree
k
.
Then
b_{S,k}
is also the number of integral ideals of degree
k
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{K}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}}\big(1-q^{-s\deg\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}q^{-sk},

\displaystyle\zeta_{S}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}\setminus S}(1-q^{-s\deg\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{S,k}q^{-sk},

from which we obtain by Lemma
3
, with
z=q^{-s}
,

\zeta_{S}(s)=\zeta_{K}(s)\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-q^{-s\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})=\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}}).

Note that
\zeta_{K}(s)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}q^{-sk}
converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, and
0\leqslant b_{S,k}\leqslant b_{k}
for all
k\geqslant 0
,
thus
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{S,k}q^{-sk}
also converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. Then

\displaystyle j_{S}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{S,k}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=q^{-2}}\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{z^{-n-1}\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}.

By moving the integral contour to
|z|=q
, we obtain

\displaystyle j_{S}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Res}_{z=q^{-1}}\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{z^{-n-1}}{1-z}

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Res}_{z=1}\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{z^{-n-1}}{1-z}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=q}\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{z^{-n-1}\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h_{K}G_{S}(q^{-1})q^{n-g_{K}+1}}{(q-1)^{2}}+R_{1}+R_{2},

where for simplicity, we put

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Res}_{z=1}\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{z^{-n-1}}{1-z},

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=q}\frac{P_{K}(z)}{(1-z)(1-qz)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-z^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{z^{-n-1}\mathrm{d}z}{1-z}.

For the remainder term
R_{1}
, we distinguish two different cases.

Case 1:

\mathrm{Card}(S)=1
, i.e.
S=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}\}
. Then

|R_{1}|\leqslant\frac{P_{K}(1)\deg\mathfrak{p}_{1}}{q-1}=\frac{h_{K}\deg\mathfrak{p}_{1}}{q-1}.

Case 2:

\mathrm{Card}(S)>1
. Then
R_{1}=0
.

For the remainder term
R_{2}
, we have

|R_{2}|\leqslant\frac{P_{K}(q)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-1)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1+q^{\deg\mathfrak{p}_{j}})\Big)\frac{q^{-n}}{q-1}=o(1)\ (n\to\infty).

In conclusion, the desired result holds.
∎

Later for all integers
n<0
, by convention, we put
j_{S}(n)=0
.

§3.
Proof of Theorem
1

Let
\mathscr{D}
be the set of
\mathfrak{p}
in
M_{K}\setminus S
such that
\deg\mathfrak{p}\leqslant N/w
. Then
\mathscr{D}
is finite.
Set
\mathscr{A}=\big(L(D(\vec{N}))\big)^{m}
. For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathscr{D}
, define

\displaystyle P_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:D(\langle a_{1},...,a_{m}\rangle_{S})\geqslant w\mathfrak{p}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:(a_{l})\geqslant w\mathfrak{p}\ (1\leqslant l\leqslant m)\}.

Put
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}\in\mathscr{D}\}
. For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathscr{D}
and all
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\mathscr{A}
, we say that
\mathbf{a}
satisfies the property
P_{\mathfrak{p}}
if
\mathbf{a}\in P_{\mathfrak{p}}
. For all
I\subseteq\mathscr{D}
, put
D_{I}=\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in I}\mathfrak{p}
. Then

\displaystyle A(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:\ \textrm{$\mathbf{a}$
satisfies the property $P_{\mathfrak{p}}$},\ \forall\mathfrak{p}\in I\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:\ (a_{l})\geqslant w\mathfrak{p}\ (1\leqslant l\leqslant m),\ \forall\mathfrak{p}\in I\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{(a_{1},...,a_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:\ (a_{l})\geqslant wD_{I}\ (1\leqslant l\leqslant m)\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{Card}\Big(\big\{a\in K^{\times}:\ (a)+D(\vec{N})\geqslant wD_{I}\big\}\cup\big\{0\big\}\Big)\Big)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\Big(\big(L\big(D(\vec{N})-wD_{I}\big)\big)^{m}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m\ell(D(\vec{N})-wD_{I})}.

Note that if
w\deg D_{I}>N=\deg D(\vec{N})
, then by Lemma 5.3 in
[
16
, p. 48]
, we have

\ell\big(D(\vec{N})-wD_{I}\big)=0,

and thus
A(I)=0
. Hence by the inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

\displaystyle V_{m}^{w}(\vec{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})=\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{D}}\mu_{S}(D_{I})A(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{D},\atop\deg D_{I}\leqslant N/w}\mu_{S}(D_{I})A(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop\deg D\leqslant N/w}\mu_{S}(D)\big(q^{m\ell(D(\vec{N})-wD)}-1\big),

since for all
D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S}
with
D\geqslant 0
and
\deg D\leqslant N/w
,
we have
\mu_{S}(D)\neq 0
if and only if
D=D_{I}
for some
I\subseteq\mathscr{D}
.

Now fix
D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S}
such that
D\geqslant 0
and
\deg D\leqslant N/w
.
By Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\ell\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)=N-w\deg D-g_{K}+1+\ell\big(C-D(\vec{N})+wD\big).

If
\deg\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)\geqslant 2g_{K}-1
, then by Lemma
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ell\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)=N-w\deg D-g_{K}+1.

If
\deg\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)\leqslant 2g_{K}-2
, then
g_{K}\geqslant 1
, since we have

\deg\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)=N-w\deg D\geqslant 0.

Hence there exists a divisor
D_{0}\geqslant 0
such that
\deg D_{0}=2g_{K}-1
(see
[
16
, p. 52]
).
Note that
L\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)\subseteq L\big(D(\vec{N})-wD+D_{0}\big)
, thus by Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\ell\big(D(\vec{N})-wD\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ell\big(D(\vec{N})-wD+D_{0}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg\big(D(\vec{N})-wD+D_{0}\big)-g_{K}+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N-w\deg D+g_{K}\leqslant 3g_{K}-2,

since
\deg\big(D(\vec{N})-wD+D_{0}\big)\geqslant\deg(D_{0})=2g_{K}-1
.

For all integers
n\geqslant 0
, denote by
b_{S,n}

the number of effective divisors in
\mathrm{Div}_{S}
of degree
n
.
By the estimation in
[
16
, p. 52]
), for all integers
n>2g_{K}-2
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle 0\leqslant b_{S,n}\leqslant h_{K}\frac{q^{n-g_{K}+1}-1}{q-1}=O_{K}(q^{n}),

from which we deduce immediately

\displaystyle V_{m}^{w}(\vec{N})=\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop\deg D\leqslant N/w}\mu_{S}(D)q^{m\ell(D(\vec{N})-wD)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D\leqslant N-2g_{K}+1}\mu_{S}(D)q^{m(N-w\deg D-g_{K}+1)}+\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop N-2g_{K}+2\leqslant w\deg D\leqslant N}O_{K}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m(N+1-g_{K})}\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D\leqslant N-2g_{K}+1}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}+\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D\leqslant N}O_{K}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m(N+1-g_{K})}\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D\leqslant N-2g_{K}+1}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}+\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant N/w}O_{K}(b_{S,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m(N+1-g_{K})}\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}

\displaystyle-\frac{q^{mN}}{q^{m(g_{K}-1)}}\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D>N-2g_{K}+1}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}+O_{K}(q^{N/w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{m(N+1-g_{K})}}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}-\frac{q^{mN}}{q^{m(g_{K}-1)}}\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D>N-2g_{K}+1}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}+O_{K}(q^{N/w}),

since as in the canonical case, we also have

\sum\limits_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{S}(wm)},

where
\mathrm{N}(D)=q^{\deg D}
is the norm of the divisor
D
.

By the same estimation in (
5
) and noting that
wm\geqslant 2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D>N-2g_{K}+1}\frac{\mu_{S}(D)}{(\mathrm{N}(D))^{wm}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Div}_{S},\,D\geqslant 0,\atop w\deg D>N-2g_{K}+1}q^{-wm\deg D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n>(N-2g_{K}+1)/w}b_{S,n}q^{-wmn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{K}\bigg(\sum_{n>(N-2g_{K}+1)/w}q^{n-wmn}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{K}\bigg(\int_{(N-2g_{K}+1)/w}^{+\infty}q^{(1-wm)x}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{K}(q^{(1-wm)N/w}),

which implies directly the desired result.

Corollary 1
.

Let
w,m\geqslant 1
be integers such that
wm\geqslant 2
. Then

(6)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V_{m}^{w}(\vec{N})}{\kappa_{m}(\vec{N})}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{S}(wm)},

where
\kappa_{m}(\vec{N})=\mathrm{Card}\big((L(D(\vec{N}))^{m}\big)=q^{m\ell(D(\vec{N}))}
.

Proof.

If
\deg D(\vec{N})=N\geqslant 2g_{K}-1
, then by Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\ell(D(\vec{N}))=N+1-g_{K}.

Hence
\kappa_{m}(\vec{N})=q^{m(N+1-g_{K})}
, and the conclusion comes from Theorem
1
.
∎

Remark 5
.

Corollary

means that the probability for

elements in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
chosen at random to be
w
-coprime is equal to
\frac{1}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}
.
It is worthy to note that the case that

has already been treated in
[
13
]
by a quite different method.

§4.
Proof of Theorem 2.

Fix
n\geqslant 1
an integer. Let
\mathscr{A}
be the set of all
m
-tuples
(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m})
of integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
with
\deg\mathfrak{a}_{j}\leqslant n
(
1\leqslant j\leqslant m)
. Let
\mathscr{D}
be the set of all nonzero prime ideals
\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
such that
\deg\mathfrak{q}\leqslant n/w
.
For all
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathscr{D}
, define

P_{\mathfrak{q}}=\big\{(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:\langle\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m}\rangle_{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{w}\big\}.

Put
\mathscr{P}=\{P_{\mathfrak{q}}:\mathfrak{q}\in\mathscr{D}\}
. For all
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathscr{D}
and all
\mathfrak{a}=(\mathfrak{a}_{1},...,\mathfrak{a}_{m})\in\mathscr{A}
, we say that
\mathfrak{a}
satisfies the property
P_{\mathfrak{q}}
if
\mathfrak{a}\in P_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
For all
I\subseteq\mathscr{D}
, put
\mathfrak{m}_{I}=\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{q}\in I}\mathfrak{q}
. Then

\displaystyle A(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:\ \mathfrak{a}_{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{w}\ (1\leqslant l\leqslant m),\ \forall\mathfrak{q}\in I\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{m})\in\mathscr{A}:\ \mathfrak{a}_{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{I}^{w}\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{\mathfrak{a}:\ \mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{I}^{w},\ \textrm{$\mathfrak{a}$ is an integral ideal in $\mathscr{O}_{S}$},\ \deg\mathfrak{a}\leqslant n\Big\}\Big)\Big)^{m}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{m}_{I}^{-w}}}{{=\hskip-2.0pt=\hskip-2.0pt=\hskip-2.0pt=\hskip-2.0pt=\hskip-2.0pt=}}

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{Card}\Big(\Big\{\mathfrak{b}:\ \textrm{$\mathfrak{b}$ is an integral ideal in $\mathscr{O}_{S}$},\ \deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n-w\deg\mathfrak{m}_{I}\Big\}\Big)\Big)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(j_{S}(n-w\deg\mathfrak{m}_{I})\big)^{m},

where for all integers
k\geqslant 0
, we denote by
j_{S}(k)
the number of integral ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
with degree
\leqslant k
.
By the inclusion-exclusion principle and Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{S,m}^{w}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{P})=\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{D}}\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{m}_{I})A(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq\mathscr{D}}\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{m}_{I})\big(j_{S}(n-w\deg\mathfrak{m}_{I})\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n/w}\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})\big(j_{S}(n-w\deg\mathfrak{b})\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n/w}\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})\big(c_{S}q^{n-w\deg\mathfrak{b}}+O_{K,S}(1)\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n/w}\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})q^{-mw\deg\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle+O_{K,S}\bigg(\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n/w}q^{(n-w\deg\mathfrak{b})(m-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}\sum_{\mathfrak{b}}\frac{\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})}{q^{mw\deg\mathfrak{b}}}-c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}>n/w}\frac{\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})}{q^{mw\deg\mathfrak{b}}}

\displaystyle+O_{K,S}\bigg(\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n/w}q^{(n-w\deg\mathfrak{b})(m-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}}{\zeta_{S}(mw)}+R_{1}+O_{K,S}(R_{2}),

where as in the canonical case, we also have

\sum_{\mathfrak{b}}\frac{\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})}{q^{mw\deg\mathfrak{b}}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}.

Recall that for all integers
k\geqslant 0
, we denote by
b_{S,k}

the number of integral ideals of degree
k
in
\mathscr{O}_{S}
, which
is also the number of effective divisors of degree
k
in
\mathrm{Div}_{S}
.
Recall also that by the inequality (
5
), we have
b_{S,k}=O_{K}(q^{k})
.
Consequently for the remainder term
R_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle|R_{1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}>n/w}\frac{\mu_{S}(\mathfrak{b})}{q^{mw\deg\mathfrak{b}}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}>n/w}\frac{1}{q^{mw\deg\mathfrak{b}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{S}^{m}q^{mn}\sum_{k>n/w}\frac{b_{S,k}}{q^{mwk}}

\displaystyle\ll_{K,S}

\displaystyle q^{mn}\sum_{k>n/w}\frac{1}{q^{(mw-1)k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{K,S}(q^{n/w}).

For the remainder term
R_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\deg\mathfrak{b}\leqslant n/w}q^{(n-w\deg\mathfrak{b})(m-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leqslant n/w}b_{S,k}q^{(n-wk)(m-1)}

\displaystyle\ll_{K,S}

\displaystyle q^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k\leqslant n/w}q^{-wk(m-1)+k}

\displaystyle\ll_{K,S}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q^{n/w},&\text{if}\quad m=1,\\
nq^{n},&\text{if}\quad m=2,w=1,\\
q^{n(m-1)},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence the desired result holds.

Corollary 2
.

Let
w,m\geqslant 1
be integers such that
wm\geqslant 2
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Q_{S,m}^{w}(n)}{(j_{S}(n))^{m}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{S}(wm)}.

Proof.

The conclusion comes directly from Theorem
2
and Lemma
4
.

Remark 6
.

, the above result means that the probability for

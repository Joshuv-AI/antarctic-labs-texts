Development and validation of a predictive model for assessing the risk of recurrence in patients with Klebsiella pneumoniae-induced pyogenic liver abscess
Authors: Liyong Zhang, Jiaqi Chen, Yihao Qu, Yuwei Fu, Kai Chen, Jinhua Cui, Jian Li, Aijun Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPyogenic liver abscess (PLA) is a potentially life-threatening bacterial infection of the liver that can cause suppurative lesions. Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) is the primary pathogen responsible for causing PLA in China and across the Asia-Pacific region. Although various treatment modalities, including antimicrobial therapy combined with percutaneous drainage, have significantly reduced mortality in patients with KP-induced liver abscess (KPLA), recurrence remains a persistent clinical challenge. This study identified the risk factors, developed a predictive model for assessing the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA, and constructed a nomogram for model visualization.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed clinical data from 486 patients with KPLA admitted to the Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University between June 1, 2015, and June 1, 2024. The patient population was randomly stratified in a 7:3 ratio into training (n = 340) and validation (n = 146) cohorts. The risk factors for recurrence in patients with KPLA were identified through univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. A predictive model was constructed, and its predictive accuracy was evaluated based on the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) and analyses of calibration, decision, and clinical impact curves. A nomogram was additionally developed for model visualization.ResultsAmong the 486 enrolled patients, 64 (13.2%) experienced recurrence (recurrence group), while the remaining 422 patients were included in the non-recurrence group. The predictive model integrated five independent risk factors, including a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), malignant neoplasm, cerebral infarction, septic shock, and the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score. The model demonstrated excellent calibration (Hosmer-Lemesow χ² = 5.301, P = 0.623), achieving AUC values of 0.824 and 0.819 for the training and validation cohorts, respectively. The clinical utility of the model was further confirmed through analysis of the decision and clinical impact curves.ConclusionA history of T2DM, malignant neoplasm, cerebral infarction, septic shock, and the SOFA score serve as independent predictive factors for assessing the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA. The predictive model, constructed based on these factors, can effectively estimate the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA, thereby facilitating the identification of high-risk populations.Keywords: Klebsiella pneumoniae, Liver abscess, Recurrence, Predictive model, Nomogram

Introduction

Pyogenic liver abscess (PLA) is a clinically prevalent and serious infectious disease of the liver. Its incidence is increasing annually worldwide, being particularly significant in Asia, where the annual incidence rate is reported to reach 12–18 cases per 100,000 population [1]. Although bacteria are the predominant pathogens responsible for PLA, other microorganisms may also contribute to its development, including certain fungi belonging to the genus Candida, such as Candida albicans and Candida glabrata, as well as some protozoans such as Entamoeba histolytica [2]. Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) is the primary causative agent of PLA in China and throughout the Asia-Pacific region [3], accounting for 42–78.2% of all cases. PLA is frequently associated with underlying conditions such as type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and malignant neoplasms [4–6]. Although antimicrobial therapy combined with interventional procedures such as needle aspiration and drainage has significantly reduced the mortality rate due to PLA, recurrence remains a persistent clinical challenge. Recurrence not only contributes to prolonged hospitalization and increased healthcare burden, but can also progress to serious complications such as sepsis and metastatic infections, thereby significantly threatening patient prognosis. Therefore, identifying the key factors influencing the recurrence of KP-induced PLA (KPLA) is crucial for optimizing its clinical management [7].

Existing studies have preliminarily explored the factors associated with adverse outcomes in KPLA and have identified T2DM and cerebral infarction as risk factors associated with recurrence [8], whereas sepsis and low albumin (ALB) levels have been recognized as indicators of poor prognosis [9]. Other factors, including infection with hypervirulent KP strains and abnormal blood glucose levels, have also been shown to be closely associated with disease severity.However, the majority of existing studies have focused on predicting disease mortality or treatment failure rather than recurrence. For example, Rossi et al. [9] developed a model to predict primary treatment failure, mortality, and recurrence of pyogenic liver abscess, but the model’s predictive performance for recurrence was limited due to the inclusion of non-KP-induced PLA cases and insufficient focus on KP-specific pathogenic characteristics. Li et al. [3] constructed a risk score for sepsis development in KPLA patients, which primarily reflects disease severity rather than recurrence potential. In contrast to these models, our study specifically targets KP-induced PLA (KPLA), the most prevalent type in the Asia-Pacific region, and identifies recurrence-specific independent risk factors (e.g., SOFA score and septic shock) that differ from mortality-related predictors. In clinical practice, physicians often rely on clinical experience for assessing the risk of recurrence—a method that is highly subjective and limited in accuracy—thereby hindering the precise identification of patients at high risk of developing KPLA and the implementation of early intervention. Therefore, the identification of independent risk factors for the recurrence of KPLA and the construction of a reliable predictive model represent critical knowledge gaps in current clinical research that need to be addressed urgently.

Clinical prediction models are multivariate statistical tools that integrate prognostic indicators for risk stratification [10]. Previous models for predicting recurrence in patients with KPLA have primarily focused on correlation analyses and the exploration of underlying mechanisms. Although the key factors associated with the risk of KPLA recurrence have been identified, there is a lack of systematic quantitative tools for assessing the independent risk factors [11]. Therefore, in this study, we developed a simple and intuitive predictive model based on the identified independent risk factors influencing recurrence in patients with KPLA, and visualized the model using a nomogram. This predictive tool integrates complex multidimensional risk factors with intuitive quantitative indicators, thereby significantly enhancing the accuracy of risk prediction and providing a more robust scientific basis for clinical decision-making. The model also enables stratified risk management at both the individual and population levels, thereby facilitating efficient resource allocation and enabling the prioritization of targeted preventive interventions for high-risk populations. By accurately identifying patients at high risk of recurrence, this model provides a scientific basis for clinical decision-making to optimize follow-up strategies and improve long-term patient prognosis.

Materials and methods

Study population

The electronic medical records of patients diagnosed with liver abscess at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University between June 1, 2015, and June 1, 2024, were retrospectively analyzed. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University (approval number: CYFYLL2022507). The requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective nature of this study. The case records were independently reviewed by two investigators according to the following predefined diagnostic criteria for PLA: (1) clinical symptoms, including fever, vomiting, jaundice, and upper abdominal pain; (2) radiological confirmation of hepatic abscesses or lesions through ultrasonography, computed tomography (CT), or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); (3) positive blood or abscess cultures, or a therapeutic response to antibiotics; and (4) definitive diagnosis confirmed through percutaneous or surgical drainage. The diagnosis of PLA was confirmed when criteria (1) and (2) were met, along with at least one item from criteria (3) and (4). The inclusion criteria for KPLA were as follows: (1) confirmed diagnosis of PLA and (2) positive KP culture from blood or abscess samples. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age < 18 years; (2) primary diagnosis other than PLA; and (3) incomplete clinical or imaging data. The diagnosis of KP-induced recurrent liver abscess required a documented history of prior disease and evidence of clinical remission following treatment, indicated by the resolution of symptoms, radiological absorption of the primary lesion or reduction of the residual size to < 1 cm, and normalization of inflammatory blood markers. After one year of follow-up, recurrence was determined if the patients again met the aforementioned diagnostic criteria. A total of 486 patients were finally selected following rigorous screening and included during model development. The protocol for patient screening and the study design are presented in Fig. 1.

Flowchart depicting the study design

Data collection

Demographic and clinical data were collected from the medical records of the 486 patients at the time of initial admission and included the following data: (1) general information: age and gender; (2) past medical history: T2DM, hypertension, malignant neoplasm, heart disease, cerebral infarction, biliary disease, or abdominal surgery; (3) clinical features: fever, abdominal pain, weakness, or vomiting; (4) initial laboratory parameters upon admission: alanine aminotransferase (ALT), fibrinogen (FIB), ALB, platelet count (PLT), procalcitonin (PCT), C-reactive protein (CRP), and white blood cell (WBC) count; (5) imaging findings: location, number, and diameter of abscesses, and presence of gas-containing abscesses; (6) complications: septic shock, pneumonia, or pleural effusion; (7) treatments: antibiotic therapy alone, antibiotic therapy combined with drainage, and surgical drainage alone. The Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score was additionally calculated for each patient.

Statistical analyses

The statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 26.0) and R (version 4.5.1). To ensure the representativeness of the cohorts, the study population was randomly stratified in a 7:3 ratio into training (n = 340) and validation (n = 146) cohorts, with stratification based on recurrence status to maintain balanced distribution of the outcome variable between the two cohorts.Specifically, randomization was performed using the random number generator function in SPSS 26.0 software. Variables with more than 20% missing values, including the levels of blood glucose, lactate, and alkaline phosphatase, were excluded from the analyses. For variables with a missingness rate below 20%, including PCT and FIB, the missing data were imputed using the fully conditional specification multiple imputation method. A total of ten imputation cycles were performed, and the algorithms were selected according to the variable type, including predictive mean matching for continuous variables, logistic regression analysis for binary categorical variables, and the proportional odds model for ordinal categorical variables. The continuous variables were compared among the groups using Student’s t-tests or Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages), whereas the continuous variables were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (P25, P75). The factors associated with recurrence in patients with liver abscesses were initially identified through univariate analysis. The statistically significant variables (P < 0.05) identified by univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis. The independent clinical predictors were subsequently identified using multivariate logistic regression analysis with bidirectional stepwise selection. Variable selection was performed using inclusion and exclusion probability thresholds of P < 0.05 and P > 0.10, respectively, and a two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A predictive model for assessing the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA was developed based on the results of multivariate logistic regression analysis. A nomogram was subsequently constructed using the RMS package in R to evaluate the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA. The Hosmer-Lemeshow (H-L) goodness-of-fit test was performed to assess the agreement between the observed and predicted outcomes. The glmnet and ROCR packages in R were used to generate the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for each model. Model validation was performed using bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations, and its discriminative ability was evaluated based on the area under the curve (AUC). The model was validated using the validation cohort, and its discriminative potential and predictive accuracy were evaluated using the AUC values and calibration curves, respectively. The net clinical benefit of the model was evaluated through decision curve analysis (DCA) and analysis of the clinical impact curve.

The results of multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that a history of T2DM, malignant neoplasm, cerebral infarction, septic shock, and the SOFA score were independent risk factors for recurrence in patients with KPLA. The predictive performance of the developed model was evaluated through ROC curve analysis, based on both the training and validation cohorts. The AUC values obtained using the training and validation cohorts were 0.824 (95% CI: 0.760–0.889) (Fig. 3A) and 0.819 (95% CI: 0.715–0.923) (Fig. 3B), respectively. Evaluation of the predictive accuracy of the model based on calibration curve analysis revealed a good predictive accuracy and consistency in both the training (Fig. 3C) and validation (Fig. 3D) cohorts.

DCA addresses the limitations of conventional evaluation metrics, which primarily focus on the predictive accuracy of models while overlooking clinical utility. The horizontal line in DCA plots represents the “no intervention” strategy, with a net benefit of zero, while the diagonal line denotes the “universal intervention” strategy, assuming that all patients have undergone treatment. The findings revealed that the model could effectively predict the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA, as validated through DCA using both the training (Fig. 4A) and validation cohorts (Fig. 4C).

Clinical impact curves illustrate the clinical implications of a predictive model across different threshold probabilities. In these curves, the red line represents the number of patients classified as “high-risk” by the model at each threshold, while the blue curve indicates the number of patients who actually experienced recurrence among those classified as “high-risk”. A higher degree of overlap between the red and blue lines indicates a superior predictive accuracy in identifying high-risk patients. The results demonstrated that the model developed in this study effectively and accurately identified true high-risk individuals from large patient cohorts, as evidenced by its performance in the training (Fig. 4B) and validation (Fig. 4D) cohorts.

Discussion

KPLA is the primary cause of suppurative liver abscess in the Asia-Pacific region and poses a significant challenge to long-term patient outcomes owing to its high rate of recurrence. Although the combination of antimicrobial therapy and surgical drainage has significantly reduced acute mortality, approximately 5–15% of patients with KPLA continue to experience recurrence in clinical practice. The occurrence of recurrence is closely associated with underlying patient conditions, therapeutic strategies, and pathogen-specific characteristics [7, 12]. According to the study by Gu et al. [13], diabetes and poor glycemic control are established independent risk factors for KPLA, whereas incomplete treatment and immunodeficiency further contribute to an increased risk of recurrence [14]. At present, there is a scarcity of predictive tools for assessing the recurrence of KPLA. Existing studies have predominantly focused on exploring the general factors associated with poor prognosis, whereas quantitative models for the accurate identification of high-risk populations remain lacking. Therefore, the construction and validation of a predictive model for assessing the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA holds significant clinical value for guiding early intervention and optimizing follow-up strategies.

The present study confirmed that T2DM is the primary independent risk factor for recurrence in patients with KPLA, consistent with the findings of previous studies [13, 15]. Chronic hyperglycemia in patients with diabetes suppresses the chemotaxis and phagocytosis of neutrophils, thereby weakening the body’s ability to eliminate KP. This consequently enables the pathogen to persist in microscopic foci within the liver or biliary tract, thereby posing a latent risk for recurrence. Furthermore, the hyperglycemic environment promotes the synthesis of KP capsular polysaccharides, leading to increased virulence and drug resistance, thereby complicating antimicrobial therapy. The present study revealed that the recurrence rates were significantly lower in patients achieving optimal glycemic control (HbA1c < 7%), suggesting that strict glycemic management may represent a key strategy for reducing the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA by modulating the immune microenvironment, enhancing immune function, and suppressing the colonization of KP [16].

Malignant neoplasms play a key role in driving the recurrence of KPLA. Tumor cells continuously secrete immunosuppressive factors, such as interleukin (IL)-10 and transforming growth factor (TGF)-β, which directly inhibit T-cell receptor signaling pathways, suppress macrophage polarization toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, and impair the core mechanisms of pathogen clearance [17]. Radiotherapy- and chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression frequently reduces absolute neutrophil counts to below 1.0 × 10⁹/L, which is accompanied by impaired phagocytic activity. The K1 and K2 capsular polysaccharide serotypes of KP evade complement-mediated lysis, thereby forming fibrin-encapsulated latent foci at the margins of hepatic necrotic areas [18]. Biliary obstruction caused by gastrointestinal tumors, such as cholangiocarcinoma and pancreatic head cancer, leads to bile stasis, which increases bacterial concentrations by approximately 10-fold compared to normal levels and upregulates adhesin expression. This consequently increases the risk of recurrent infections by more than threefold compared to patients without gastrointestinal tumors [19, 20]. From a clinical perspective, individualized antimicrobial regimens should be developed for patients with concurrent tumors, integrating extended consolidation therapy with the enhanced monitoring of immune function and supportive care.Chronic hepatitis, as a common chronic liver disease, also contributes to the recurrence of KPLA.

Chronic hepatitis leads to persistent liver inflammation, which disrupts the hepatic parenchymal structure and impairs the function of Kupffer cells, the resident macrophages of the liver that are responsible for pathogen clearance [21]. The fibrotic microenvironment in chronic hepatitis patients reduces blood perfusion in the liver, hindering the delivery of antibiotics to infected foci and enabling KP to form persistent colonization [22]. Additionally, hepatitis B or C virus infection can induce immune dysfunction, including reduced activity of natural killer cells and impaired humoral immunity, which further increases the susceptibility to recurrent KP infections [23]. Clinically, for KPLA patients with a history of chronic hepatitis, liver function protection should be strengthened while anti-infective treatment is administered, and antiviral therapy should be standardized for viral hepatitis patients to improve the liver microenvironment and reduce recurrence risk.

Pregnancy, although not a common comorbidity in KPLA patients, has unique physiological characteristics that may affect disease recurrence. During pregnancy, the body undergoes hormonal changes that alter the immune status, with a shift toward a Th2-dominated immune response to tolerate the fetus, which reduces the ability to clear intracellular pathogens such as KP [24]. The increased intra-abdominal pressure during pregnancy can lead to biliary stasis, creating a favorable environment for bacterial proliferation. Moreover, the physiological changes in liver function during pregnancy, such as increased serum bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase levels, may mask the early signs of infection recurrence, delaying intervention. For pregnant women with KPLA, close monitoring of infection markers and imaging examinations should be performed during the perinatal period, and antibiotic selection should balance efficacy and fetal safety to minimize recurrence risk.

Other chronic diseases, such as chronic kidney disease and autoimmune diseases, also indirectly increase the recurrence risk of KPLA. Chronic kidney disease patients often have uremic toxin accumulation, which inhibits neutrophil function and impairs the body’s antibacterial capacity [21]. Autoimmune diseases, such as rheumatoid arthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, are associated with inherent immune disorders and long-term use of immunosuppressive drugs, which significantly reduce the body’s ability to resist KP infections [23]. These chronic diseases often coexist with multiple comorbidities, forming a complex clinical scenario that further increases the difficulty of infection control and recurrence risk. Therefore, for KPLA patients with such chronic diseases, a multidisciplinary treatment approach should be adopted to optimize the management of underlying diseases while controlling acute infections, thereby reducing the recurrence rate.

With the global prevalence of COVID-19, its impact on the recurrence of infectious diseases such as KPLA has attracted increasing attention. Acute COVID-19 infection can induce systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) and immune dysregulation, which may promote the recurrence of KPLA [25]. The severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infects host cells through the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) receptor, which is widely expressed in the liver and biliary tract epithelial cells. This infection causes direct damage to the liver tissue and impairs the barrier function of the biliary tract, creating conditions for KP invasion and recurrence [26]. Additionally, the cytokine storm induced by acute COVID-19, characterized by elevated levels of IL-6, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and other pro-inflammatory cytokines, disrupts the hepatic microcirculation and suppresses the phagocytic function of neutrophils, enabling residual KP in the liver to reactivate and form recurrent abscesses.

Long COVID-19, defined as persistent symptoms and functional impairments lasting more than 12 weeks after acute infection, also poses a potential risk for KPLA recurrence. Long COVID-19 is associated with long-term immune dysfunction, including persistent lymphopenia, reduced T-cell proliferation, and impaired antibody production, which weakens the body’s long-term ability to resist bacterial infections [25]. Moreover, long COVID-19 can cause chronic fatigue, reduced physical activity, and psychological stress, which further compromise immune function and increase the susceptibility to recurrent infections [26]. For KPLA patients with a history of COVID-19 infection, especially those with long COVID-19 symptoms, long-term monitoring of immune function indicators (such as lymphocyte subsets) and infection markers should be performed. Timely immunomodulatory interventions and lifestyle guidance may help reduce the risk of KPLA recurrence.

Our study identified cerebral infarction as a novel independent risk factor for KPLA recurrence, with a plausible mechanism involving neuro-immune dysregulation and impaired hepatic perfusion [8, 27]. Cerebral infarction-induced long-term immobility reduces liver blood flow, compromising Kupffer cell phagocytosis and antibiotic delivery to residual KP foci [3, 12]. Additionally, sympathetic hyperactivity suppresses M1 macrophage polarization and promotes anti-inflammatory cytokine release (e.g., IL-10), weakening bacterial clearance [17, 28]. Clinical evidence supports this: Cui et al. [8] reported a 2.8-fold higher recurrence rate in patients with cerebral infarction, aligning with our findings. For such patients, early rehabilitation and quarterly abdominal CT monitoring are recommended to mitigate recurrence risk.

While biliary diseases showed a trend toward association with recurrence in univariate analysis (OR = 1.848, 95% CI: 0.938–3.641, P = 0.076), they were not included in the final model, primarily due to confounding effects. First, biliary diseases are highly collinear with gastrointestinal malignancies, whose stronger predictive power (OR = 5.958) masks the independent role of benign biliary pathologies [9, 19]. Second, 82.4% of biliary disease patients underwent drainage or surgical intervention, which effectively reduces recurrence risk by resolving obstruction [29]. Third, the homogeneous treatment strategies in our single-center cohort minimized variability in biliary disease management, further diluting its predictive weight. This does not negate its clinical significance—prompt biliary obstruction relief and postoperative irrigation remain critical for preventing recurrence [6, 30].

The present study revealed that elevated SOFA scores were significantly positively correlated with the recurrence of KPLA. This score encompasses six organ systems, including the respiratory, circulatory, and neurological systems, and directly reflects the body’s reserve capacity to resist infections from the perspective of organ dysfunction [31, 32]. Patients with high SOFA scores often exhibit dysregulation of immune cell subsets, inverted CD4⁺/CD8⁺ T-cell ratios, and impaired activation of complement components C3 and C4. This impairs the recruitment of neutrophils to infection sites, thereby significantly reducing the efficiency of bacterial clearance [33]. These patients are prone to developing occult microabscesses formed by KP along hepatic microvessels, which are difficult to detect with conventional plain CT scans. Clinical management should therefore be stratified according to the SOFA scores, with patients having scores ≥ 4 requiring prolonged antimicrobial therapy for 4–6 weeks [34], dynamic monitoring of infection markers such as PCT and CRP, biweekly contrast-enhanced MRI for the detection of occult lesions, and the concurrent administration of immunomodulators to improve the immune microenvironment and immune function [27, 35].

Septic shock portends a dramatic increase in the risk of KPLA recurrence [28]. The occurrence of endotoxemia during septic shock activates the inflammatory cascade, thereby triggering the massive release of cytokines, which disrupts hepatic microcirculation. Damage to hepatic sinusoidal endothelial cells induces local ischemia and hypoxia, thereby directly suppressing the respiratory burst function of neutrophils and the phagocytic activity of macrophages [36]. Short courses of broad-spectrum antibiotics are commonly administered in the early clinical phase to rapidly stabilize circulation; however, this approach often leads to incomplete pathogen clearance, particularly as KP may form potentially persistent reservoirs within hepatic macrophages. The state of immune paralysis following recovery from septic shock can persist for several weeks, during which the body’s defenses against reinfection are significantly compromised, thereby enabling latent pathogens to breach the immune barrier and trigger recurrence [37]. Clinically, antibiotic regimens should be adjusted within 48 h following the correction of septic shock. Targeted drugs should be selected based on the results of antimicrobial susceptibility testing, and the duration of treatment should be extended accordingly. Concurrently, the restoration of immune function should be accelerated through nutritional support and immunoglobulin infusion. Post-discharge follow-up should incorporate monthly liver function tests and abdominal ultrasound examinations for continuous monitoring over a period of at least 6 months.

Nomograms provide a visual representation of the results of multivariate regression analysis. By integrating relevant variables to generate individualized probabilities of clinical events, they enable the straightforward and intuitive assessment of patients. The predictive model underlying the nomogram integrated five independent risk factors, including a history of T2DM, malignant neoplasm, cerebral infarction, elevated SOFA score, and septic shock, to predict the probability of recurrence in patients with KPLA. Based on the present study, patients with a probability of recurrence exceeding 60% (total score > 187) are classified as high-risk, enabling early intervention and treatment to improve clinical outcomes. The predictive model demonstrated excellent calibration (H-L χ² = 5.301, P = 0.623), with the calibration curve closely aligning with the ideal reference line. The AUC values for the training and validation cohorts were 0.824 (95% CI: 0.760–0.889) and 0.819 (95% CI: 0.715–0.923), respectively, indicating a strong discriminatory ability. DCA and analysis of the clinical impact curves indicated that the predictive model for assessing the risk of KPLA possesses strong clinical utility.

Certain potential factors associated with the recurrence of KPLA were not incorporated during the construction of the predictive model in this study, primarily due to limitations related to data availability, research design, and the inherent complexity of clinical practice. From a microbiological standpoint, specific serotypes of KP, such as the highly virulent K1 and K2 serotypes, the presence of antimicrobial resistance genes, and the biofilm-forming ability may all influence the risk of recurrence by affecting bacterial virulence and the efficacy of anti-infective therapies. However, such detailed pathogen-related data are not routinely preserved in clinical testing, thereby preventing their inclusion in the analysis. At the host level, dynamic factors such as long-term post-discharge immune status (including CD4⁺ T-cell dynamics), the efficacy of sustained nutritional support, and treatment adherence—evidenced by antibiotic compliance and regular follow-up attendance—are associated with the recurrence of KPLA. However, retrospective studies face challenges in accurately tracing such follow-up information through medical records. Among the treatment-related factors, the thoroughness of abscess drainage, the selection and dosing of antibiotics, and the timing and efficacy of biliary interventions may influence the recurrence of KPLA by altering the mechanisms of infection control. Nevertheless, variations in clinical strategies among physicians and incomplete medical records hindered the accurate quantification of their independent effects on the recurrence of KPLA, thereby precluding their inclusion into model development.

Compared with existing models predicting KPLA mortality or treatment failure [3, 9], our model has two key advantages: first, it is specifically designed for KPLA recurrence, with risk factors (e.g., SOFA score, septic shock) that are more closely related to persistent infection and immune dysfunction; second, the nomogram visualization enables rapid risk assessment at the bedside, which is more clinically operable than complex scoring systems.

The present study has certain limitations that are discussed hereafter. First, as a single-center retrospective study, it is susceptible to selection bias, influenced by factors such as center-specific diagnostic and treatment protocols, the geographic distribution of patients, and variability in underlying disease composition. Therefore, multicenter, prospective, large-scale studies are necessary for validating the feasibility and reliability of this model. Second, there were gaps and omissions in the data for key variables. For instance, incomplete data on KP serotypes and resistance gene profiles, combined with the exclusion of dynamic factors such as treatment adherence and post-discharge nutritional status, may compromise the accuracy of risk assessment. Third, the model does not account for the confounding effects of various interventions. The study did not differentiate among the effects of different antimicrobial regimens, biliary interventions, and immune support therapies on recurrence outcomes. These interventions may modify the strength of the association between risk factors and recurrence, potentially introducing bias during quantitative risk assessment. Furthermore, the applicability boundaries of the model across different treatment strategies need to be determined in future studies.

Conclusion

In this study, a predictive model for assessing the risk of recurrence in patients with KPLA was successfully developed and validated, identifying a history of T2DM, malignant neoplasm, cerebral infarction, elevated SOFA scores, and septic shock as independent risk factors for KPLA recurrence. This model effectively identified individuals at high risk of recurrence, providing quantitative evidence for the early clinical implementation of personalized intervention strategies. The clinical implementation of this predictive model can help reduce the risk of recurrence and improve long-term patient outcomes.

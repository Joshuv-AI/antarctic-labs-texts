Designing an empowerment program for families of children with mental disorders: A mixed-method protocol
Authors: Saeideh Zeinalzad, Mahrokh Keshvari, Mousa Alavi, Mostafa Haghshenas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Children’s health affects all aspects of family health, and mental disorders add to the care burden of families. To provide care to these children, it is essential to empower families, which can increase their competencies. Considering this issue, the present study will be conducted with the aim of designing and developing an empowerment program for families of children with mental disorders.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This mixed-method study was conducted in three phases. To develop a family empowerment program, in the first phase, a qualitative study is conducted via semistructured interviews to identify the needs of families of children with mental disorders. Data analysis will be performed via conventional content analysis via the Granheim and Landman method. The second phase will be carried out in three stages: reviewing the literature, prioritizing needs, and developing the initial version of the family empowerment program. In the third phase, the feasibility of the developed program will be evaluated via validated questionnaires before and 3 months after the intervention in two groups of families as a randomized field superiority trial study. In this step, the data will be analyzed via STATA 14 software. For the obtained data, descriptive statistics (means and standard deviations) and ANCOVA tests will be used to evaluate the results.DISCUSSION:Knowing the views of caregivers and people providing health services to children with mental disorders and their families to create a real and evidence-based empowerment plan, considering the sociocultural goals of society, can potentially help reduce the care burden of these families. The results of studies in this field can help policymakers improve access and equality, guarantee the uniformity of the quality of care, ensure the long-term durability of services, and correct the gaps in empowering families with children with mental disorders.

Introduction

Psychiatric disorders in children, with a prevalence of approximately 13.4% worldwide,[1] and in Iran,[23] in addition to disrupting the process of social and cognitive development of the child, pose numerous challenges for the family and increase this issue to a public health concern.[4] The financial expenses associated with the treatment and support of children with psychiatric conditions, coupled with the responsibilities of caregiving, diminish the well-being of family members.[5] In numerous societies, there is social stigma surrounding psychiatric disorders,[67] leading to feelings of shame among family members and resulting in social consequences such as isolation. This stigma amplifies the psychological strain on caregivers within families and impacts their overall quality of life. When family members fail to accept the presence of a child with a psychiatric disorder, it strains relationships among them and can detrimentally affect their mental wellbeing.[8]

Moreover, this lack of acceptance can diminish the quality of care provided to the child.[9] The elevated prevalence of depression among both caregivers and family members[10] underscores the need for psychological support and services to meet the needs of these individuals. In numerous instances, individuals caring for such patients do not receive the same level of social support as those caring for physical illnesses do, and the stigma associated with having a child with psychiatric disorders often prevents them from seeking or requesting social support.[11] Moreover, the detrimental behaviors exhibited by these patients exacerbate the feelings of shame and isolation experienced by their caregivers, ultimately diminishing the families’ sense of self-efficacy.[12]

According to Baumrind’s theory,[13] parental self-efficacy refers to parents’ belief in their ability to successfully perform various tasks associated with their role as caregivers.[14] Previous research has indicated that parents who exhibit high levels of self-efficacy are linked to better social-emotional adjustment in their children.[15] Consequently, increased parental self-efficacy is correlated with increased quality parental emotional support and the adoption of more positive parenting strategies.[16] According to this theory, self-efficacy is the most important predictor of health. Strengthening this concept among caregivers of children with mental disorders could enhance social support.[1718] The highlighted circumstances underscore the importance of implementing interventions cantered on assessing and treating anxiety, fostering positive coping mechanisms, enhancing self-esteem, and cultivating coping resources among family caregivers of children with chronic illnesses.[19]

There is evidence that educating parents of children with mental disorders has reduced parents’ stress.[2021] A positive and significant relationship has also been reported between empowering the family and reducing their problems and the complications caused by their children’s disease.[22]

The concept of family empowerment comprises three primary components. First, it acknowledges that all families inherently possess power and can leverage this power to address their challenges. Second, the family’s struggle in fulfilling their needs stems not from an inherent inability but rather from a lack of opportunity within their social support system to accomplish them. Third, in the process of empowering families, members endeavor to utilize their skills and competencies within the context of familial changes.[23] Since nurses play a crucial role in providing care to these families at the primary level, they can help reduce the burden of these families’ problems through targeted interventions by empowering them to provide effective treatment.[2425]

To empower families, family members attempt to utilize skills and competencies in the framework of family change, and in this intervention, nurses can take measures to help these families.[23] Empowerment is a complex concept that can be considered a process, an outcome, or an intervention (empowering others). In healthcare professions, empowerment is mainly defined as an intervention.[26] In fact, empowerment is used as an interactive intervention to help families increase their trust in healthcare professionals and improve decision-making through empowerment. Empower helps families create resilience and adaptability during changes, discover and increase family potential, improve the role and function of family health, and enhance family competencies in managing health problems.[26]

Despite these considerations, the research findings indicate that family empowerment in patients with mental disorders remains below the optimal level. Families often lack sufficient knowledge about caring for patients, which disrupts the recovery process.[27] Given the necessity of empowerment in these families, there is no practical plan for this group of health service recipients, and this issue further highlights the necessity of conducting the present study. Accordingly, the present study aims to understand individuals’ real and first-hand experiences by reviewing the existing guidelines and using an evidence-based nursing approach to derive a suitable program compatible with families’ real needs to empower them. Our objective in this study will be to develop an empowerment program for families of children with mental disorders.

In the present investigation, a comprehensive analysis will be conducted on familial units from psychological, social, and, to the extent feasible, economic perspectives while adhering to a holistic paradigm that underscores the pivotal function of families in the provision of care for children afflicted with mental disorders. The study aims to elucidate prevailing beliefs and the cultural and economic adversities encountered within the societal context that influence strategies for family empowerment, thereby yielding novel insights into these familial structures and further exploring the practicality of achieving such strategies through empirical research methodologies [Figure 1].

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

The present study will be sequential mixed-method research (exploratory-descriptive) carried out in Isfahan in 2023. This study contains three phases. The first phase is a qualitative study with the aim of identifying the needs of families of children with mental disorders. This stage is conducted through a conventional content analysis method and an inductive method to achieve a rich understanding and description of the studied phenomenon. The second phase addresses the steps of developing a program in three stages (review of literature, prioritization of needs, and development of the final program), and the third phase involves the implementation of the developed program as a randomized field superiority trial study to assess the feasibility of the study.

Study participants and sampling

The study population in the first phase will consist of a group of individuals who are in contact with the parents of these children (families of children with mental disorders, nurses working in these wards, doctors specializing in child psychiatry, child psychologists, social workers, and policy-makers in this field) and those who have an impact on empowering these families. Participants will be selected from environments where study participants and those related to them will be present in Isfahan (hospitals, policymaking and decision-making organizations, welfare centers, outpatient clinics, and centers for follow-up on children who have had a history of a medical case).

Data collection tool and technique

At this stage, the researcher starts collecting data purposefully with the strategy of maximum diversity in terms of age, occupation, level of education, duration of infection, and type of disorder. To invite the participants, the study objectives were explained, and the interview time and location were specified. The participants’ information was recorded before the interview. In the present study, semistructured individual interviews were used to collect data.

During the meetings, the interviews will be recorded by a tape recorder. The interviews were subsequently transcribed word for word. Before the interviews, participants’ permission to record the interviews will be obtained, and they will be assured of the confidentiality of all the mentioned data. During the interview, considerations will be made regarding the safety of the location and the confidentiality of information; consequently, the participants will express their experiences and views with more confidence. On the basis of the participants’ responses during the interview, more open questions might be asked to determine the details of their experiences. Exploratory questions such as ‘What do you mean by.?’ and ‘Can you give an example?’ will help clarify the issue or clarify ambiguities. To collect qualitative data, the researcher will use 1) individual interviews with exploratory and open-ended questions (unstructured or semistructured), 2) field notes to document observations of verbal or nonverbal behaviors and families’ presence in healthcare centers and their interaction with children, other family members, and the healthcare team, and 3) documents (patient files, books) to describe the main meanings in the participants’ lives to obtain in-depth data about the needs of families of children with mental disorders. In previous studies, 9–13 family caregivers of children with mental disorders were recruited and employed.[2829] Therefore, in this research, the optimal number of participants will be between 10 and 15 parents and health care providers.

Sample questions for interviewing parents: Please talk about your experience in caring for a child with a mental disorder, What needs do you feel as the caregiver of these children? and What problems have you faced in taking care of your child?

Sample questions for interviewing health care providers: What is your experience of the needs of families of children with mental disorders? What services are most needed and receive the most attention from families of children with mental disorders? What things attract your attention when dealing with the families of these children?

Data management and analysis

We use MAXQDA software version 2020 for qualitative data organizing, and in the review, we use EndNote software version 9.3.3 for extracted data organizing.

Data analysis in the qualitative stage will be conducted through conventional content analysis and an inductive method by Granheim and Landman. To achieve a rich understanding and description of the studied phenomenon, each interview and corresponding observation will be considered a research unit. The recorded interviews will be listened to and transcribed. Themes from all transcripts were grouped and categorized together in all cases. Subthemes were collected and extracted.

To evaluate the trustworthiness of the data, the standards of credibility, dependability, confirmability, and transferability are used as measures of scientific accuracy. To establish credibility, the member check technique is used to confirm the reliability of the data and codes. To ensure dependability, initial codes and samples of quotes, themes, and items extracted from the interview transcriptions for each category will be provided to the external observer. To ensure confirmability, the text of several interviews and the extracted codes and categories will be provided to the research team and three nursing experts, and they will be requested to evaluate the accuracy of the data coding process. To ensure the transferability of the findings, the participants’ statements were presented as objectively as possible.

Ethical consideration

This research obtained permission from the Ethics Committee of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUI.NUREMA.REC.1401.181) (Project ID: 3401696). All the subjects will be thoroughly briefed regarding the objectives and methodology of the investigation. They are guaranteed that the data collected from them will remain confidential. The participants were informed that their involvement in the study was voluntary.

The second phase: Developing the program

This phase contains the steps of developing the program in three stages (reviewing the literature, prioritizing the needs, and developing the final program) [Figure 2].

Stages of the mixed methods study

First step: Systematic review of the literature

In this section, a systematic review will be conducted using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses protocol for reporting systematic reviews and meta-analyses for families with children with mental disorders.[30] This review will be carried out with the aim of confirming and completing the needs explained in the first step of the research and determining the content of the empowerment program for families of children with mental disorders according to participants’ needs, and the available evidence will be extracted from the literature. Evaluating English literature for this study in databases (Web of Science, Pub Med, Scopus) will be performed using keywords corresponding to similar words for the clinical question (PICO) and using OR and AND operators in the title and abstract and reviewing Persian literature in databases (Magi ran, SID, Iranmedex) via ‘family empowerment,’ ‘mental disorders,’ and ‘children’ keywords up to July 2024. Moreover, if needed, other exclusive databases will also be used. To investigate Gary Lectures, ProQuest websites and the websites for registering theses, conferences, and abstracts will also be used.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Studies whose population include the caregivers of children with mental disorders, have interventions or programs to empower the target population, and have consequences such as mental or objective burden will be included, and studies with the following characteristics will be excluded from our review: studies that did not perform any program intervention on caregivers, whose target population was not caregivers of children with mental disorders, did not examine the impact of programs on children, or did not examine any of the desired outcomes of this systematic review.

After the databases were searched with the above keywords, the search results were entered into EndNote software, and then the articles were screened on the basis of the entry and exit criteria. After screening the articles, ProQuest sites, theses, conferences, and abstracts will also be used to review Gary Lectures. There will be no restrictions on the language of the articles or their publication date.

Finally, the findings of this systematic review are categorized on the basis of the educational content provided to these families to help design the program in the next stages.

Second step: Modified Delphi technique

At this stage of the present study, the Delphi technique of the modified Delphi type will be performed with the aim of prioritizing the needs and strategies of empowering families related to the study in two stages. In the first stage, needs are prioritized to obtain various expert opinions. In the second stage, strategies for empowering families of children with mental disorders are prioritized. Unlike the Delphi technique, in the modified Delphi method, the first round, which includes written questions about the studied concept to analyze the content and extract the survey questionnaire items, is not carried out; however, the questionnaire items are obtained through interviews, focus groups, or literature reviews.

In the initial phase of the Delphi technique, the researcher identifies and extracts the needs of families with children experiencing mental disorders. Using insights gained from the qualitative stage and an extensive review of the literature and documents, a list of empowerment needs and corresponding strategies will be compiled for each empowerment strategy. This questionnaire will include the strategies and needs of empowering families and the demographic details of the participants in the Delphi process. Ten faculty members and experts specializing in child care and treatment, hailing from diverse cities in Iran, are invited to contribute their insights and evaluations of the empowerment strategies and needs presented in the questionnaire. Experts are tasked with providing their viewpoints on the criteria used to prioritize needs and solutions. Following the collection of opinions, the questionnaire was evaluated in a meeting attended by the research team.

After the questionnaire is drafted, the researcher proceeds to the next phase, where participants and experts are identified and selected to take part in the second round of the Delphi method. Ideally, the same members who participated in the first stage are chosen. The selection process involves purposefully selecting faculty members and experts with relevant experience and expertise in caring for and treating these children. Individuals who are interested in the subject matter, have connections with these families, or possess valuable experiences in this field are purposefully selected to participate in the second stage of the Delphi process. At this stage, after selecting the intended participants for the Delphi technique, the researcher will refer to them and, after providing explanations on the necessity and objectives of conducting the study, obtain oral and written consent from them, introduce the objectives of the study and the Delphi method, win their trust and confidence, observe confidentiality and trustworthiness, and provide the questionnaire to them. Along with the questionnaire, a letter containing a concise introduction of the study subject and objectives and a brief description of the study method, the importance of the study results, and instructions for completing the questionnaire will be delivered to them. After the completed questionnaires from the second round are collected, the mean score is calculated and analyzed via descriptive statistical tests. Furthermore, the data are analyzed for the final summarization and determination of the priority of the needs by placing them in the matrix table and using SPSS-23 software. The findings from this stage will be presented to a panel of experts who deliberate on them for the purpose of designing an intervention program. If consensus among the experts is needed, there is a possibility that the Delphi process will proceed to a third stage.

In this phase, the intervention’s content is evaluated for quality and suitability. The relevance of the intervention content will be assessed through surveys or qualitative interviews with participants after implementation as well as through expert evaluations via surveys before implementation. In the expert evaluation method, the researcher initially provides a sample of the proposed intervention to experts in the same field. These experts will be asked to assess the suitability of each content element included in the intervention, considering factors such as content relevance, cultural appropriateness, alignment with audience interests, and design suitability. Each aspect is rated on a three-level scale: 0 for inappropriate, 1 for needing correction, and 2 for completely appropriate. Scores exceeding the average indicate the intervention’s adequacy.[31] To measure the degree of agreement in the Delphi process, Kendall’s W is used. This coefficient can be between 0 and 1; the number 1 indicates complete agreement between the participants, and the number 0 indicates no agreement. A higher value of Kendall’s W suggests that the participants have a strong consensus on the topic being evaluated, whereas lower values may indicate divergent opinions or uncertainty among the group.[32]

To modify and validate the program in line with the study objectives, the research team will prepare the initial draft of the family-oriented empowerment program for families of children with mental disorders. In this regard, a draft program will be designed on the basis of the data obtained from the previous stages: the results extracted from the qualitative part of the study, the literature review, and the Delphi method, which will be used to prioritize the needs.

The content of the developed program prioritizes the needs of families of children with mental disorders, in some sections of which strategies and programs related to the families of these children are discussed to empower them. The developed program will include an introduction, general goals, specific goals, strategies related to specific goals separately, and activities related to strategies.

Afterward, a panel of experts will be formed to examine and validate the structure and content of the draft program. For this purpose, a number of experts will be selected via the purposeful sampling method and invited to participate in the meeting. The participants were selected purposefully from different specialties in the healthcare system according to the research objectives. In this section, the appropriateness method (RAM) is used for expert consensus. In this method, the ratio of benefit to harm related to each strategy is scored on a scale of 1--9, where a score of 1 implies that the expected harm outweighs the expected benefit, and a score of 9 means that the expected benefit is greater than the expected harm. In this section, in addition to usefulness, other aspects, such as the clarity, relevance, and feasibility of strategies, are evaluated. Moreover, below each action and strategy, a line will be provided for comments on the overall implementation method, through which experts will be able to express their opinions in case of ambiguity. Finally, the scores of each part of the program are calculated on the basis of the related scale guide and are used to employ or remove the suggested strategies.

Third stage: Combining the findings and developing the final program

After the approval and validation of the draft program by the experts in the panel, the final program for empowering families of children with mental disorders will be developed on the basis of the consensus of the opinions on the structure and content of the draft program. The final program will be presented on the basis of the stages of a mixed-method study with a combined qualitative and quantitative approach. In the qualitative stage of the study, the needs of families of children with mental disorders were investigated. In the next stages, after the needs are prioritized, the content and overall structure of the program are evaluated and validated by specialists.

The third phase: A randomized field trial study

Participants and sampling environment

This parallel-group, double-blind, controlled, randomized field trial (IRCT20191225045893N1), with a superiority framework and a ratio of 1:1 allocation in groups, was conducted with families of children with mental disorders in Isfahan. The inclusion criteria for participants will include families of children with a medical diagnosis of mental disorders and having a medical record provided that the mental disorders in the children are not caused by mental retardation. The exclusion criterion will include an unwillingness to continue cooperating at any stage or due to complications caused by children’s disorders or treatment.

Pilot/feasibility study

A pilot study will be conducted for the initial evaluation of the developed program. This pilot study will be conducted on 40 families of children with mental disorders. After informed consent is obtained, the empowerment of these families will be measured by considering the initial program extracted from the previous stage. On the basis of the frequency of mild to moderate mental problems in children, an estimate of the sample size needed for initial screening in the field is made. To assess the intervention’s viability, qualifying families are given it after providing informed consent, and the families’ quality of life is assessed once more. The research supervisor will compare the quality of life in the initial and follow-up assessments. The trial ends if its level decreases in the second evaluation.

If any problems are identified in the implementation of the intervention, after the agreement, the necessary changes will be applied by the research team. Families participating in the pilot study will not participate in the clinical study, and the results of the measurements will not be included in the analysis.

Sample size

The sample size will be calculated with an allocation ratio of 1:1, considering the attrition, the confidence level of 95%, and the appropriate formula for the mentioned sample size.

Participants

A pediatric psychiatrist will assess and evaluate children for mental health issues. After candidate families are invited, valid questionnaires are used to assess each family’s quality of life. Once the eligible families have been identified, red and blue cards are used to randomly allocate these families to the control and intervention groups. The families that accept the invitation will receive an explanation of the study’s goals and procedures for participation. Furthermore, informed consent in writing will be obtained from the qualified families. Every phase of the study will be overseen by a member of the Isfahan University Medical Sciences Ethics Committee.

Sampling and data collection

The sample size in this study was determined by considering 20% attrition, α = 0.05, β = 0.2, P = Prevalence of the disease, and E = maximum acceptable error for ratio estimation and using the following formula , with 76 parents of children in two groups (intervention and control).

After the eligible families are identified, these parents are introduced to the research team and, to randomly allocate these families, choose one of the colored envelopes and complete the demographic information. In this study, the participants and the researcher evaluating the results of the intervention will be blinded. After random assignment, a researcher from the research team who will not be involved in sampling and random assignment will determine the card color of each group. The quality of life of these families was subsequently evaluated immediately after the empowerment intervention and 3 months after the end of the intervention [Table 1].

Intervention

The main researcher will educate the family empowerment program on the items extracted from the initial program via face-to-face training, the role modeling approach (for the intervention group), and routine interventions for the other group (control group), and after the completion of the study, interventions will be provided to the other group.

The number of sessions for the intervention program will be determined on the basis of the goals and strategies outlined in the intervention. However, it is anticipated that the program will consist of approximately 6--8 sessions. The number, duration, and intervals of the intervention sessions will be set identically for both groups. It is estimated that the initial empowerment program will be scheduled 3 times a week. During the intervention, if the participants were absent from more than two sessions, they were removed from the intervention. The researchers will pay for the intervention and transportation costs for both the intervention and control groups. The intervention will be executed in accordance with the program developed by a PhD student in psychiatric nursing and will be conducted under the guidance of the supervising faculty member. The place and time of the interventions will be coordinated with the participants by phone, and on the basis of their agreement, a place will be considered for the interventions. It is expected that during the meetings held with hospital officials, a space in the hospital will be considered for these families. The participants did not participate in any other research during the intervention. The investigation is expected to proceed without any harm; however, in the case of harm, the main researcher will take responsibility.

The effectiveness of the obtained program can be evaluated in two formative and collective ways. In the formative evaluation, a member of the research team will oversee the implementation of intervention steps and sessions, utilizing existing checklists. This evaluation assesses various aspects, including participant engagement in the intervention, the alignment between planned and executed sessions, and the coordination of time and location, among other factors. Summative evaluation will also occur through the analysis of the intervention design obtained in the clinical trial study.

Results

The results of this research examine the mean quality of life score of families after the implementation of the developed empowerment program and the magnitude of their change changes immediately after the implementation of the intervention and 3 months after the start of the intervention in the intervention group compared with the control group.

Data collection tool

The data will be evaluated and collected via a self-reported shortened 26-item quality of life scale from the World Health Organization (WHO QOL-BREF) (. This tool contains a 5-point Likert scale from 1 (Not true at all) to 5 (Very true). Each of the BRIEF-WHOQOL subscales includes questions that evaluate four domains of people’s quality of life. Each of these areas has 3, 6, 7, and 8 questions, respectively. These areas include physical health, psychological health, the living environment, and relationships. The questions of this scale cover the quality of life dimension of the long scale, and two additional questions have been placed to study the obvious quality of life and general health of people. These first two questions do not belong to any of the areas and evaluate health status and quality of life in a general way.

The Family Empowerment Scale (FES) contains 34 items rated on a 5-point Likert scale from 1 (Not true at all) to 5 (Very true). It has been designed to measure family empowerment at different systemic levels (individual, service system, and society) and has been shown to have internal consistency and reliability. The family empowerment scale is known as an efficient tool for evaluating programs to help families develop knowledge, skills, services, and resources of the mental health system for children with special needs.

Prior to each intervention session, the researcher encouraged the families to continue attending, and phone calls were made to set up the upcoming sessions to maximize participant retention and complete follow-up. The researcher will make calls and ask questions to fill any gaps in the data. The principal investigator will oversee data collection during all stages.

Confidentiality

The researchers will be responsible for the study participants’ information. When the data are entered into the data management software, the participants’ details are removed, and a code is assigned to each.

Data management and analysis

The data were analyzed via SPSS 19 software. Statistical tests included independent t-tests, paired t-tests, Chi-square tests, and analysis of variance with repeated measures (RM ANOVA). If the measurements at the enrolment stage differed among the groups, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was used, controlling for baseline data and independent variables (group as a fixed parameter and mean enrolment score as a covariate). Additionally, the baseline values of the outcome variables and potential intervening variables that differ between the two groups are adjusted to avoid the possible risk of bias and detect independent results. The normality of the data was evaluated through the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. If the data were not normally distributed, nonparametric tests such as the Wilcoxon test were used. In the case of missing data, we use the last value carried method to impute missing values.

Presentation of results

The results of the study will be provided to other research team members without any interference or manipulation so that they can take measures regarding the feasibility of the study or modifications to the possible problems of the developed program.

Discussion

The purpose of this study is to design and develop a program to empower families of children with mental disorders. Typically, family members, as the primary caregivers, are the main source of care for a mentally ill member.[33] Nevertheless, many studies have reported a lack of support for family caregivers and disregard of their needs.[34]

In this context, to detect the needs of these families, it is necessary to identify and redefine their needs and problems in their natural social context. To design and develop a more effective program, a list of empowerment needs and strategies related to each empowerment strategy will be prepared on the basis of the results of the qualitative stage and a wide review of the literature and documents and will be provided to related experts as a questionnaire. The draft program will be subsequently implemented as a randomized field superiority trial study. To evaluate the feasibility of the study and to consider the economic issues related to the development of the program after receiving informed consent, the intervention will be carried out on eligible families. If any problems are identified in the implementation of the intervention, the necessary modifications will be applied by the research team after the agreement, and it will be hoped that the problems resulting from the developed program will be investigated and resolved on a broader level as macro country-wide programs.

In a study by Tabak et al.,[35] positive effects on awareness, parental skills, and child resilience were observed in empowering families experiencing mental illness.

One study aimed at creating a model to empower families with children with special needs stated that family empowerment is correlated with family demands, parenting efficacy, organization, and communication. Additionally, this study indicated that parenting stress negatively impacts family empowerment and that parenting efficacy contributes to increasing family empowerment. In this study, by presenting a Double ABCX model, an attempt was made to increase empowerment in these families, and nursing interventions to reduce stress and enhance parenting efficacy were recommended.[8] These empirical findings indicate that equipping families with children afflicted by mental disorders can increase their capacity to deliver effective care to their offspring. Consequently, it is imperative to concentrate on the empowerment strategies delineated in this research, which target both families and associated healthcare providers, to mitigate the caregiving burden imposed by mental illness within these familial units.

In this context, the present study aims to determine the development of an empowerment family program within a naturalistic context. A notable strength of this inquiry lies in its incorporation of qualitative data alongside a variety of quantitative metrics. The execution of semistructured interviews with the participants facilitates the capture of their perspectives regarding their experiences with the program and whether they perceive it to have been beneficial in addressing the repercussions of both past and ongoing stressors.

Conclusion

This study will attempt to play a role in empowering these families on the basis of the results obtained from the dimensions of empowerment in families and considering the set criteria of macro health policies such as access, equality, quality, sustainability, family-centeredness, and integration. It is also hoped that in this research, issues related to equality in the provision of health services and equitable access to mental health services, especially in low-income families and those from marginalized communities, will be prioritized.

In summary, although existing health policies establish a critical framework for the empowerment of families with children experiencing mental health disorders, there remains considerable potential for improvement. It is hoped that the results of studies in this area will help policymakers improve access and equality, ensure the uniformity of quality of care, and ensure the long-term sustainability of services.

Limitations and recommendations

One of the most important limitations of the study is that the participants will refrain from attending medical centers for various reasons, such as fear of social stigma, ignorance about disease conditions, and unavailability of health services. To overcome this limitation, the researcher will attempt to create a safe and nonjudgmental atmosphere to gain the participant’s trust and share her pure and real experiences. Considering that family empowerment is an important determining factor in the provision of care to families and their children and that the philosophy underlying the care system derives part of its beneficial effect from increasing family empowerment, more empirical research focused on those professional practices that clearly help strengthen family empowerment is recommended. Such practices may include increasing family participation in treatment programs facilitated by the presence of interprofessional care teams as well as engaging policy makers in this area by emphasizing the needs of all family members and ensuring that adequate resources are allocated.

Publication

The study results will be published with the aim of ensuring maximum visibility in open-access scientific journals. An initial release of primary results and subsequent releases of secondary results have been planned. The results will also be presented at national and international scientific conferences and seminars. Finally, the research team publishes the results on social networks and other media.

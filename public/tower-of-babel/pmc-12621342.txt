Novel compounds with promising HuH-7 inhibitory activity as new cancer drug candidates: derivatives of N,N′-diphenylurea linked with 1,2,3-triazole
Authors: Yajie Guo, Huibao Long, Haoying Zhang, Dan Wang, Haidong Wu, Xuxiang Chen, Longfei Mao, En Gao, Tong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTargeted cancer drug therapy has emerged as a critical treatment modality for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The discovery and development of novel anti-HCC drug therapeutics with improved pharmacological properties remains an urgent priority in oncology drug discovery. In this study, we designed and synthesised a new series of 1,2,3-triazole-cored structures incorporating aryl urea. Fifteen analogs were prepared via nucleophilic addition and copper-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC) with excellent yields. These synthesised compounds were evaluated for their potential antitumor activities. Notably, compounds 3c and 3g exhibited the lowest IC50 values (10.80 ± 0.14 and 11.62 ± 3.72 μM) against HuH-7 cells. Further investigations suggested compound 3c and 3g induced cell apoptosis, stimulated DNA damage, and autophagy against HuH-7 cells. Acute toxicity measurement also demonstrated the safety of the compounds. These findings suggested the triazole-cored analogs 3c and 3g are suggested to be promising candidates for the treatment of HCC and their potential for further pharmaceutical development.

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the most severe global health challenges, ranking among the leading causes of mortality worldwide. Recent statistics estimate ∼20 million new cancer cases and 9.7 million cancer-related deaths in 20221. While significant advancements in drug discovery have been achieved in recent decades, cancer incidence and mortality rates continue to escalate. Projections suggest an additional 35 million new cases globally by 20501, attributed to factors including occupational stress, lifestyle changes, and environmental influences2. This alarming trend underscores the critical need for innovative anticancer agents, presenting both an urgent medical requirement and a formidable challenge for medicinal chemistry3.

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), encompassing both primary and metastatic liver malignancies, represents a particularly pressing concern in China4,5. Epidemiological data from 2018 revealed an annual incidence of 17.7 per 100 000 and mortality of 16.4 per 100 000, posing substantial public health burdens6,7. For advanced HCC, molecular targeted therapies, such as sorafenib have become cornerstone treatment8–10. However, the clinical utility of current therapies is frequently limited by drug resistance, emphasising the need for novel agents with distinct mechanisms of action11. Developing such compounds, particularly those suitable for combination therapies, may help overcome resistance and improve patient outcomes.

Molecular hybridisation has emerged as an effective approach in anticancer drug development for designing new bioactive ligands from promising lead compounds12. Through the strategic integration of pharmacodynamic moieties derived from a spectrum of bioactive substances, our approach enables the creation of novel derivatives with enhanced target affinity and therapeutic efficacy compared to parent drugs13,14.

The 1,2,3-triazole skeleton is typically synthesised via the copper-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition reaction (CuAAC). This reaction not only acts as a pharmacophore but also functions as a linker to connect various pharmacophores15. These nitrogen-rich heterocycles demonstrate remarkable biocompatibility, selective targeting capability, and low cytotoxicity while potentially inhibiting cancer proliferation through cell cycle arrest and apoptosis induction. Particularly promising are 1,2,3-triazole hybrids capable of exerting dual or multiple anticancer mechanisms16.

Urea has an extensive history in medicinal chemistry applications as a structure fragment, often playing an important role in the regulation of drug-target interactions. Its two amino groups and the carbonyl that connect them render it highly druggable. In the design of molecular drugs, the use of a urea structure fragment may improve the drug’s bioactivity, increase its selectivity, and optimise its physico-chemical properties. Furthermore, urea incorporation can be helpful in overcoming metabolic stability issues and removing toxic pharmacophores, leading to fewer side effects17–19.

Recent studies highlight the therapeutic potential of such hybrid structures: 1,2,3-triazole-dithiocarbamate hybrid I (1 C50: 0.89 and 1.13 µM) exhibited potent anti-gastric cancer activity through lysine specific deme-thylase 1 (LSD1) inhibition, significantly reducing tumour weight by 68.5% in xenograft models without observable toxicity20. Liu et al. developed 1,2,3-triazole-pyrimidine-urea hybrids (Figure 1, II) showing nanomolar activity against MGC-803, EC-109, MCF-7, and B16-F10 (IC50: 32 nM) and concentration-dependent apoptosis induction21. Limpachayaporn’s sorafenib-inspired triazole-aryl urea hybrids (Figure 1, III) exhibited enhanced HepG2 inhibition and safety, with tert-butyl or chloro substituents conferring activity comparable to sorafenib while altering cell cycle distribution22. The FLT3 inhibitor, specifically the 4-((6,7-dimethoxyquinolin-4-yl)oxy)aniline derivatives (Figure 1, IV), incorporating semicarbazide moiety showed superior potency to cabozantinib (IC50: 312 and 384 nM)23. Mao’s triazole-urea hybrid (Figure 1, V) demonstrated potent IDO1 inhibition (IC50: 0.75 μM), with docking studies elucidating its binding mode24. Moreover, meta-Ureidophenoxy-1,2,3-triazole hybrid (Figure 1, VI) was identified as a potential pharmacophore for efficient inhibition of HepG2 hepatocellular carcinoma25.

Building upon our previous work with gefitinib‑1,2,3‑triazole derivatives demonstrating broad-spectrum anticancer activity26,27. Biological assessments revealed that several compounds exhibited potent anti-proliferative activity against these cancer cell lines. Based on this information, both the triazole part and the aryl urea moiety are retained in order to take these advantageous properties to our newly designed structures26,27. In this study, we report the design, synthesis, and evaluation of novel 1,2,3-triazole-urea hybrids as potential anti-HCC agents, and explore the safety of these novel compounds in vivo, investigate their structure-activity relationships, and mechanistic profiles.

General procedure for preparation of compound 3a

The reaction reagents were compound 2a (100 mg, 0.38 mmol) and 1-(azidomethyl)-4-bromobenzene (96 mg, 0.46 mmol), which were added to 10 mL of mixed solvent (water/tert-butanol/THF = 1:1:1). The reaction was performed in copper sulphate pentahydrate (9.5 mg, 0.04 mmol) and sodium ascorbate (15 mg, 0.08 mmol) at 85 °C. The reaction status was monitored by TLC, and after completion of the reaction, the mixture was extracted through dichloromethane (15 mL × 3). The organic phase was combined and washed successively with water and brine, then dried over sodium sulphate and concentrated in vacuo. The desired compound 3a was isolated by column chromatography (CH2Cl2/MeOH = 30:1).

Biological study

Dimethylsulphoxide (DMSO) was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM), RPMI 1640 Medium, Foetal bovine serum (FBS), and penicillin/streptomycin were brought from Gibco (Grand Island, NY, USA). Enhanced Cell Counting Kit-8, Calcein/PI Live/Dead Viability Assay Kit, DNA damage detection kit, and Autophagy Staining Assay kit were bought from Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Annexin V-FITC/Propidium iodide (PI) staining kit was provided by BD Biosciences (Franklin Lake, NJ, USA).

Cell culture

Hepatocellular carcinoma cell line HuH-7, breast cancer cell line MCF-7, Lung cancer cell lines H460, H1299, A549, PC-9, and L02 cells were obtained from ATCC. DMEM or RPMI 1640 added with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin were using to culture cells. Cells were incubated in a humidified atmosphere at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Cell viability and IC50 measurement

Cell viability was measured by CCK8 assay kit. Cells were seeded in a 96-well plate with the density of 3 × 103 cells/well. After overnight adhere, cells were treated with 20 or 50 μM of the compounds or vehicle control DMSO dissolved in the culture media for 48 or 72 h. Then cells were incubated for another 1 h at 37 °C with 5% CO2 after added with CCK8 reagent. 450 nm absorbance was measured using a microplate spectrophotometer (Thermo). Cell viability was calculated as a percentage relative to the control group. In vivo assay

Animals

SPF-grade healthy Kunming mice (8 weeks old, female, 21–23 g) were purchased from Henan Skobes Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Licence No.: SCXK (Yu) 2020–0005). The experiment adhered to the 3 R principle of “reduction, replacement, optimization”, aiming to minimise the harm and suffering of animals to the greatest extent possible. Mice were kept under a 12-h light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to food and water and were acclimated to the feeding conditions for 7 days. After acute toxicity test, mice were sacrificed by using intraperitoneal administration of pentobarbital dissolved in sterile saline at a dose of 150 mg/kg body weight. Mice were randomly distributed into two groups: vehicle group and compound 3c treated group, the total number of animals used is 12, each group consisted of 6 mice.

Acute toxicity test

The toxicity of drugs cannot be fully reflected in in vitro experiments. Animal experiments provide more comprehensive toxicity data to help researchers understand the potential toxic and side effects of drugs on different organ systems, and provide support for the development of anti-cancer drugs. Mice were randomly divided into the following groups: vehicle control group, 3c-treated group. Mice in the vehicle control groups were given an equivalent volume of vehicle (5% DMSO + 40% PEG300 + 1% Tween 80 + 54% saline) by intragastric administration, while mice in the 3c-treated groups were given the 3c solution (500 mg/kg). Body weight and appearance were monitored for a total period of 14 days (from the first day after treating with 3c). The representative organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys) were harvested and weighed to calculate the organ coefficient (organ coefficient = organ weight/body weight × 100%). Then the organs were fixed in 10% formaldehyde and paraffin-embedded for histological examination. Finally, these sections were stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for light microscopic examination.

Statistical analyses

Data were analysed using two-tailed Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett’s test, when the data conformed to normal distribution with chi-square. Conversely, nonparametric tests were used. Data were obtained from no fewer than three independent experiments. A p-values <0.05 was considered statistically significance.

Results and discussion

Chemistry

A simple, convergent synthetic route to the novel compounds incorporating the 1,2,3-triazole structure and a urea moiety has been devised24,26. Three types of functional groups: m-phenyl group, p-phenyl group, and methyl group as linkers to connect urea and 1,2,3-triazole blocks were selected, respectively. The reaction routes of the three series of compounds (3a–3g, 5a–5d, 7a–7d) were shown in Figure 2 and Table 2. The azide side chains were synthesised from the previously reported amine side chains via a “one-pot” diazotisation reaction with subsequent azide substitution to give yields of 85–96%. The target ligands were synthesised from these alkyne and azide building blocks by copper(I)catalysed “click chemistry”, yielding the desired 1,4-substituted 1,2,3-triazole linked products with yields of 66–94%. Each ligand was purified by semi-preparative HPLC to afford samples of suitable purity for biological evaluation. Different substituents (R1, R2, R3, R4, and R5) were present on the benzene ring of phenyl isocyanate and benzyl structures. The effects of linker substituents R1, R2, R3, R4, and R5 on different human tumour cell lines were compared.

Moreover, all these compounds showed minimal cytotoxicity towards L02 cells.

After 72 h treatment, cells were collected and analysed by RT-PCR. Besides, 3c treatment did not influence the expressions of DNA damage related genes p53, p21, and H2AX, apoptosis related genes Bcl2, caspase6, and caspase7, autophagy genes ATG5 and ATG7 (Figure 5(A)). In cells treated with compound 3g, the expression of DNA damaged gene H2AX was decreased, also the apoptosis genes Bax and Bcl2 (Figure 5(B)). However, the oxidative stress genes and autophagy genes were not changed after compound 3g treatment (Figure 5(B)).

Effects of compounds 3c and 3g on autophagy

To further evaluate the role of compounds 3c and 3g in regulating autophagy, HuH-7 cells were treated with different doses (5, 10, and 20 μM) of compounds 3c and 3g for 24 h. After treatment, cells were detected by MDC (monodansyl cadaverine) probe, it could specifically label autophagosome and showed green fluorescence. Autophagosome in tumour cells was increased dramatically after compounds 3c and 3g treatment (Figure 8). This result was consistent with the above results, and this indicated that compounds 3c and 3g could induce cell autophagy progress in tumour cells, which finally results in cell death.

Acute oral toxicity assessment

To further assess the safety of the newly compound, we conducted acute toxicity studies in mice. Throughout the 14-day observation period, no mortality or toxicological manifestations were observed in any treatment group. Comparative analysis indicated no statistically significant differences in body weight measurements between compound 3c-treated mice and vehicle control group at any time point. Tissues weight and histopathological evaluations of key organs (cardiac, hepatic, splenic, pulmonary, and renal tissues) also revealed no detectable toxicological changes after compound 3c treatment (Figure 9). These collective findings substantiate the favourable safety characteristics of the investigated compounds and support their potential for further pharmaceutical development.

Conclusion

A novel series of diarylurea-1,2,3-triazole hybrid compounds were synthesised successfully via nucleophilic addition and 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition, and their anticancer potential was evaluated against a panel of human cancer cell lines. Notably, the triazole-cored analogs, especially compounds 3c–3g exhibited selective inhibitory activities towards HuH-7, exhibiting significantly greater efficacy compared to other tested cancer cell lines. Flow cytometric analysis revealed that lead compounds 3c and 3g induced apoptotic cell death in HuH-7 cells in a concentration-dependent manner. Mechanistic studies suggest that the observed antitumor effects may be mediated through modulation of key regulatory kinases and associated proteins involved in DNA damage, apoptosis, and autophagy pathways. Additionally, mice acute toxicity measurement suggested no obvious toxicity of the compound in vivo. This study has pioneered the application of diarylurea derivatives in the medical field, preliminarily confirming the potential value of these compounds in anti-tumour therapies. The identified lead compounds, especially 3c and 3g exhibit promising drug-like properties and therapeutic potential, warranting further investigation as potential candidates for HCC targeted therapy. In the future, we will further investigate the anti-tumour effects and mechanisms of compounds 3c and 3g in vivo.

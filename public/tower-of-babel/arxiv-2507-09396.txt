Oriented Steiner Triple Systems, Steiner Products, and Dynamics
Jake Kettinger and Chris Peterson

Abstract
Let 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} denote a Steiner triple system on an n n -element set. An orientation of 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} is an assignment of a cyclic ordering to each of the triples in 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} . From an oriented Steiner triple system, one can define an anticommutative bilinear operation on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} resembling the cross product. We call this bilinear operation a Steiner product. We classify the oriented Steiner triple systems on sets of size 7 and 9 and investigate the dynamics of their associated Steiner products.



1
Introduction

Steiner triple systems seem to be first introduced by Wesley Woolhouse in 1844 in a problem posed in
The Lady’s and Gentlemen’s Diary
. The problem posed by Woolhouse was solved by Thomas Kirkman in 1847
[
1
]
and a refinement of the problem, known as
Kirkman’s Schoolgirl Problem
, was proposed by Kirkman in 1850 (again in The Lady’s and Gentlemen’s Diary). In 1853, Jakob Steiner independently rediscovered the concept and described these systems with a more precise mathematical language. Steiner systems are named after him due to this work
[
2
]
. Oriented Steiner triple systems, the focus of this paper, were introduced 160 years later in the 2013 paper of Strambach and Stuhl
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we explore new applications of oriented Steiner triple systems. We use the orientation to define a binary operation, on the real vector space spanned by the elements of a Steiner triple system, which we call the
Steiner product
. The Steiner product generally satisfies some of the properties of the cross product of a vector space, as shown in Proposition
1
; and in two circumstances it satisfies all the properties.

We find that different orientations on the same Steiner triple system result in qualitatively different behavior from the Steiner product. This motivates the goal of Section 3 to classify oriented Steiner triple systems by isomorphism class. We succeed in this goal for Steiner systems of sizes 7 and 9, as shown in Theorems
1
and
2
. In addition, we compute the automorphism groups of each isomorphism class of these oriented Steiner triple systems. We end Section 3 with a look into a specific oriented Steiner triple system and its connection to the octonions.

In Section 4, we analyze the dynamics of the iterated Steiner product using representatives of the isomorphism classes found in Section 3. This culminates in Theorem
4
, in which we give criteria for the dimension of the vector space spanned by the set of vectors generated from two starting vectors through an iterated Steiner product.

Background and Definitions

Definition 1
.

A
Steiner triple system
is a special kind of block design. It consists of an ordered pair
(\mathfrak{S},T)
where
\mathfrak{S}
denotes a finite set of
points
and
T
denotes a collection of distinguished
3
-element subsets of
\mathfrak{S}
called
triples
. In a Steiner triple system, the set of triples,
T
, is constrained to satisfy the requirement that each
2
-element subset of
\mathfrak{S}
is a subset of exactly one of the triples in
T

[
4
,
3
]
.

Definition 2
.

An
oriented Steiner triple system
is a Steiner triple system,
(\mathfrak{S},T)
, where each triple,
\{a,b,c\}\in T
, is assigned one of the two possible cyclic orderings of the elements in the triple. We denote the cyclic ordering
a\rightarrow b\rightarrow c\rightarrow a
by
[a,b,c]
and
a\rightarrow c\rightarrow b\rightarrow a
by
[a,c,b]
. Note that
[a,b,c]=[b,c,a]=[c,a,b]
and that
[a,c,b]=[c,b,a]=[b,a,c]
. An oriented Steiner triple system, supported on
(\mathfrak{S},T)
, will be denoted by
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
.

Definition 3
.

Given an oriented Steiner triple system
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
, suppose
t=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\in T
has the orientation
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]\in\mathcal{O}(T)
. The oriented triple
[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]\in\mathcal{O}(T)
can be used to define a skew symmetric function
f_{t}:\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\times\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\to\{-1,0,1\}
by setting

f_{t}(x_{1},x_{1})=f_{t}(x_{2},x_{2})=f_{t}(x_{3},x_{3})=0

f_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{t}(x_{2},x_{3})=f_{t}(x_{3},x_{1})=1

f_{t}(x_{2},x_{1})=f_{t}(x_{3},x_{2})=f_{t}(x_{1},x_{3})=-1

The oriented triples in
\mathcal{O}(T)
lead to functions
f_{t}
for each
t\in T
. These can be combined to give a function
f:\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{S}\to\{-1,0,1\}
satisfying
f|_{t\times t}=f_{t}
for each
t\in T
. We call
f
the
orientation function
for
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
.

Note that this definition is modified from that given in
[
3
]
, where
f(x_{i},x_{i})=\pm 1
. Our having
f(x_{i},x_{i})=0
is necessary for the construction of the Steiner product below.

Definition 4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{S},T)
be a Steiner triple system. Denote by
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
the vector space of
\mathbb{R}
-linear formal sums of the elements
s\in\mathfrak{S}
. That is,

\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}=\left\{\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}a_{s}s:a_{s}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Note that
\mathfrak{S}
is a basis for
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
and that
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}=|\mathfrak{S}|
.

We make
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
into an inner product space by first defining the inner product on the basis
\mathfrak{S}
by

\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle=\begin{cases}0&s\neq s^{\prime}\\
1&s=s^{\prime}\\
\end{cases}

then extending linearly to all of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. We will often denote the inner product with the standard dot product notation:
\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle=s\cdot s^{\prime}
.

Definition 5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
be an oriented Steiner triple system with orientation function
f
. Consider the skew symmetric binary operation
\times:\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
given by

s\times s^{\prime}=f(s,s^{\prime})s^{\prime\prime}

where
\{s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\}
form a triple (or a subset of a triple in the case
s=s^{\prime}
, in which case
f(s,s^{\prime})=0
so
s^{\prime\prime}
does not matter).

Using this binary operation on
\mathfrak{S}
, we extend the binary operation linearly to all of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
to define the
Steiner product
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. In particular, if
\mathbf{a}=\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}a_{s}s\hskip 7.22743pt{\rm and}\hskip 7.22743pt\mathbf{b}=\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}b_{s}s
then

\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\sum_{t\in\mathfrak{S}}a_{s}b_{t}(s\times t).

Proposition 1
.

Consider an oriented Steiner triple system

(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
with associated Steiner product
\times:\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. For any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
, we have
\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})=\mathbf{b}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{S}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
and write
\mathbf{a}=a_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}s_{n}
. We have

\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})=(a_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}s_{n})\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})=a_{1}s_{1}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})+\cdots+a_{n}s_{n}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}).

The product
a_{i}s_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})
is a sum of terms of the form
f(s_{j},s_{k})a_{i}a_{j}b_{k}
where
\{s_{i},s_{j},s_{k}\}\in T
. This term is canceled out by the term
f(s_{i},s_{k})a_{j}a_{i}b_{k}
in the product
a_{j}s_{j}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})
(since
f(s_{j},s_{k})=-f(s_{i},s_{k})
by the definition of
f
in an oriented Steiner system).
Therefore every term in
\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})
is canceled by another term in the sum, and so
\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})=0
.
In a similar manner,
\mathbf{b}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})=0
.
∎

Definition 6
.

Given a real inner product space
V
with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, a
cross product
on
V
is a function
\cdot\times\cdot:V\times V\to V
that satisfies the following three properties:

1.

\times
is bilinear,

2.

\langle v,v\times w\rangle=0
for all
v,w\in V
,

3.

|v|^{2}|w|^{2}=|v\times w|^{2}+\langle v,w\rangle^{2}
for all
v,w\in V\ (
where
|v|^{2}=\langle v,v\rangle)

[
5
]
.

Remark 1
.

The Steiner product on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
given in Definition
5
satisfies Criteria 1 and 2 of the cross product as given in Definition
6
.

In general, an oriented Steiner triple system leads to a Steiner Product that does not satisfy Criterion 3, with only two exceptions: an oriented Steiner triple system with three elements (with either of the two orientations), and the one with seven elements whose orientation corresponds to the multiplication table for distinct imaginary octonions.

Proposition 2
.

The Steiner product is skew-symmetric.

Proof.

First note that by construction that if
s_{i},s_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}
then

s_{i}\times s_{j}=-(s_{j}\times s_{i})

for all
i,j
. Now let
\mathbf{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}s_{i}
and
\mathbf{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}s_{i}
. Then

\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{j}(s_{i}\times s_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}-a_{i}b_{j}(s_{j}\times s_{i})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{j}(s_{j}\times s_{i})=-(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a}).

∎

3
Classifying Oriented Steiner Systems of Order 7 and 9

It is known that a Steiner triple system must have order
6k+1
or
6k+3
for some
k
. Up to isomorphism, there are unique Steiner triple systems of order 3, order 7, and order 9. There is a rapid increase in the number of Steiner triple systems of higher order. In particular, there are 2 of order 13, 80 of order 15, 11,084,874,829 of order 19, and 14,796,207,517,873,771 of order 21
[
6
]
. When we consider imposing the additional structure of an orientation on a Steiner triple system, there is again a classification problem up to isomorphism. In this section, we classify the oriented Steiner triple systems of order 3, 7, and 9.

Definition 7
.

Two Steiner triple systems
(\mathfrak{S},T)
and
(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime},T^{\prime})
are
isomorphic
if there is a bijective map

\psi:\mathfrak{S}\to\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}

such that
\{s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\}\in T
if and only if
\{\psi(s),\psi(s^{\prime}),\psi(s^{\prime\prime})\}\in T^{\prime}
.

Two oriented Steiner triple systems
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
and
(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(T^{\prime}))
are
isomorphic
if there is a bijective map

\phi:\mathfrak{S}\to\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}

such that
[s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}]\in\mathcal{O}(T)
if and only if
[\phi(s),\phi(s^{\prime}),\phi(s^{\prime\prime})]\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(T^{\prime})
.

The classification of oriented Steiner triple systems of order 3 is straightforward. There is a unique Steiner triple system,
(\mathfrak{S},T)
, of order
3
given by
\mathfrak{S}=\{1,2,3\}
,
T=\{\{1,2,3\}\}
. Initially, it seems there are two oriented Steiner triple systems of order 3:
(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,2,3]\})
and
(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,3,2]\})
. However, using the bijective map
\phi:\{1,2,3\}\rightarrow\{1,2,3\}
given by
\phi(1)=1,\phi(2)=3,\phi(3)=2
, we get an isomorphism between
(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,2,3]\})
and
(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,3,2]\})
. Thus, there is a unique oriented Steiner triple system of order 3 up to isomorphism.

Definition 8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
be an oriented Steiner triple system. We can build an oppositely oriented Steiner triple system by reversing the orientation of each element of
\mathcal{O}(T)
. We denote this oppositely oriented system by
(\mathfrak{S},\overline{\mathcal{O}}(T))
. We will call
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))

reflexive
if
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
is isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{S},\overline{\mathcal{O}}(T))
.

Up to isomorphism, there is only one oriented Steiner triple systems of order 3 and it is reflexive.
We now focus on the classification of oriented Steiner triple systems of order 7 and determine their automorphism groups.

Definition 9
.

The
automorphism group
of a Steiner triple system
(\mathfrak{S},T)
is the group (under composition) of isomorphisms
\phi:(\mathfrak{S},T)\rightarrow(\mathfrak{S},T)
. We denote this by
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)
. Similarly, the automorphism group of an oriented Steiner triple system
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
is the group of isomorphisms
\phi:(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))\rightarrow(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
. We denote this by
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
.

Remark 2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{S},T)
be a Steiner triple system and let
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
be an oriented Steiner triple system. Suppose
\phi:\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is bijective. Define

\phi(T)=\{\{\phi(s),\phi(s^{\prime}),\phi(s^{\prime\prime})\}\ |\ \{s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\}\in T\}

\phi(\mathcal{O}(T))=\{[\phi(s),\phi(s^{\prime}),\phi(s^{\prime\prime})]\ |\ [s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}]\in\mathcal{O}(T)\}.

We have

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)=\{\phi:\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}\ |\ \phi\ {\rm is\ bijective\ and\ }\phi(T)=T\}

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))=\{\phi:\mathfrak{S}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}\ |\ \phi\ {\rm is\ bijective\ and\ }\phi(\mathcal{O}(T))=\mathcal{O}(T)\}.

From this observation, it is clear that
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)
and
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
have natural representations as subgroups of the symmetric group
Aut(\mathfrak{S})=S_{\mathfrak{S}}
and that the representation of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
sits as a subgroup in the representation of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)
. We will denote this by
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))<\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)
. In the order 3 case, we have
\text{Aut}(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,2,3]\})\cong\text{Aut}(\{1,2,3\},\{[2,1,3]\})\cong C_{3}<\text{Aut}(\{1,2,3\},\{\{1,2,3\}\})\cong S_{3}
. In terms of their permutation representation, we have
\langle(1,2,3)\rangle<\langle(1,2,3),(1,2)\rangle=S_{3}

(
where
\langle(1,2,3),(1,2)\rangle
denotes the subgroup generated by the permutations, in cycle notation,
(1,2,3)
and
(1,2))
. As noted before, we have an isomorphism between the oriented Steiner triple systems
(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,2,3]\})
and
(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,3,2]\})
given by the transposition
(2,3)
.

An interesting theorem of Strambach and Stuhl shows that the automorphism group of an oriented Steiner triple system must have odd order
[
3
]
. A simple illustration of this theorem is seen in the order 3 case where
\text{Aut}(\{1,2,3\},\{[1,2,3]\})\cong\text{Aut}(\{1,2,3\},\{[2,1,3]\})\cong C_{3}
. We will see further illustrations of this theorem for oriented Steiner triple systems of order 7 and order 9 in the rest of this section.

Up to isomorphism, there is a unique Steiner triple system on seven elements. For clarity, we will fix a model of this system. Instead of using variables to label the seven elements in the set, we will use the numbers
1,2,3,4,5,6,7
. The collection of triples will therefore be triples of numbers. For the theorem that follows, we use the following model:

\mathfrak{S}=\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\},

T=\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,4,5\},\{1,6,7\},\{2,4,6\},\{2,5,7\},\{3,4,7\},\{3,5,6\}\}.

It is well known that the automorphism group of the Steiner triple system, which we can represent as a subgroup of
S_{7}
, is a simple group of order
168
.

Theorem 1
.

There are exactly four oriented Steiner triple systems on seven elements, up to isomorphism. Two isomorphism classes have a non-Abelian automorphism group of order 21, and two isomorphism classes have a cyclic automorphism group of order 3. None of the isomorphism classes are reflexive.

Representatives of the distinct isomorphism classes are

(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T))=\{[1,2,3],[1,4,5],[1,6,7],[2,4,6],[2,7,5],[3,6,5],[3,7,4]\}_{21},

(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{2}(T))=\{[1,2,3],[1,4,5],[1,7,6],[2,4,6],[2,5,7],[3,4,7],[3,6,5]\}_{21},

(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{3}(T))=\{[1,2,3],[1,4,5],[1,6,7],[2,4,6],[2,5,7],[3,4,7],[3,5,6]\}_{3},

(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{4}(T))=\{[1,2,3],[1,4,5],[1,6,7],[2,4,6],[2,5,7],[3,4,7],[3,6,5]\}_{3}.

We have
(\mathfrak{S},\overline{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(T))\cong(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{2}(T))
and
(\mathfrak{S},\overline{\mathcal{O}_{3}}(T))\cong(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{4}(T))
(where
\overline{\mathcal{O}}(T)
denotes that the orientation of each oriented triple in
\mathcal{O}(T)
has been reversed). The subscript on each isomorphism class denotes the order of its automorphism group. Namely,
|\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T))|=|\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{2}(T))|=21
and
|\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{3}(T))|=|\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{4}(T))|=3
.

Proof.

The proof is given through an explicit computation in
Maple^{TM}

7
]
.

.
From the Maple code and the use of the open source computer algebra system

]

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)\cong\langle(1,2,4,3,6,7,5),(4,5)(6,7)\rangle<\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S})=S_{7}
. GAP identifies this representation of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)
as a simple group of order 168.
The permutation representation of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T))
as a subgroup of
\langle(1,2,4,3,6,7,5),(4,5)(6,7)\rangle
is

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T))=\langle(2,4,6)(3,5,7),(1,2,3)(4,7,6)\rangle.

GAP identifies this group as
C_{7}\rtimes C_{3}
, a non-Abelian group of order 21. The permutation representation of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{2}(T))
as a subgroup of
\langle(1,2,4,3,6,7,5),(4,5)(6,7)\rangle

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{2}(T))=\langle(2,4,7)(3,5,6),(1,2,3)(5,6,7)\rangle.

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T))

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)
. The permutation representation of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{3}(T))
as a subgroup of
\langle(1,2,4,3,6,7,5),(4,5)(6,7)\rangle
is
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{3}(T))=\langle(2,4,6)(3,5,7)\rangle.

\langle(1,2,4,3,6,7,5),(4,5)(6,7)\rangle
.

Theorem 2
.

There are exactly 16 isomorphism classes of oriented Steiner triple systems on nine elements. Seven of the classes have an automorphism group of size 1, seven have an automorphism group of size 3, one of size 9, and one of size 27. In the list below, we will denote the oriented Steiner triple systems as
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T)),\dots,(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{16}(T))
. The first eight isomorphism classes,

Proposition 3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
be an oriented Steiner triple system, and let
(\mathfrak{S},\overline{\mathcal{O}}(T))
be the oriented Steiner triple system obtained by reversing all oriented triples in
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))=\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\overline{\mathcal{O}}(T))

Proof.

Let

[s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}]\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}(T)

[s,s^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{O}(T)

∎

We would like to end this section with a look into how an automorphism of an oriented Steiner triple system can be lifted to an automorphism of

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))\hookrightarrow\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},T)\hookrightarrow\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S})\hookrightarrow\text{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}).

Through these maps, we use elements of

Lemma 1
.

Let
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))

\sigma(a\times b)=\sigma(a)\times\sigma(b)

Proof.

First, let us prove the claim for
s_{i},s_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}

[s_{i},s_{j},s_{k}]
be an oriented triple in the system. Then
[\sigma(s_{i}),\sigma(s_{j}),\sigma(s_{k})]
is also an oriented triple in
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
. Thus
s_{i}\times s_{j}=s_{k}
and
\sigma(s_{i})\times\sigma(s_{j})=\sigma(s_{k})=\sigma(s_{i}\times s_{j})
. Similarly,
\sigma(s_{j}\times s_{i})=-\sigma(s_{k})=\sigma(s_{j})\times\sigma(s_{i})
.
By bilinearity of the Steiner product and linearity of
\sigma
, the claim is also true for general linear combinations
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
.
∎

Thus every element of
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))

\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))\leq\text{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}},\times)

Let

We will use the fact that

[

4
The Dynamics of the Steiner Product

In this section, we will use the notation
\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}
to denote the vector space spanned by
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
.
The Steiner product will generally fail the third criterion of the cross product. In this section, we explore this failure in greater detail and show that we can relate the dynamics of a certain iterated Steiner product with the spectral theorem for skew symmetric matrices. We illustrate the failure of the Steiner product to be a cross product in the following example.

Example 1
.

Take the oriented Steiner system on
\mathfrak{S}=\{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4},s_{5},s_{6},s_{7}\}
given by

\mathcal{O}(T)=\{[s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}],[s_{1},s_{4},s_{5}],[s_{1},s_{7},s_{6}],[s_{2},s_{4},s_{6}],[s_{2},s_{5},s_{7}],[s_{3},s_{4},s_{7}],[s_{3},s_{5},s_{6}]\}.

A direct computation shows that
(s_{1}+s_{5})\times(s_{3}+s_{7})=-s_{2}+s_{6}-s_{6}+s_{2}=0.
Therefore

4=|s_{1}+s_{5}|^{2}|s_{3}+s_{7}|^{2}\neq|(s_{1}+s_{5})\times(s_{3}+s_{7})|^{2}+|(s_{1}+s_{5})\cdot(s_{3}+s_{7})|^{2}=0.

The automorphism group of
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
is
\{\text{id},(274)(365),(247)(356)\}
. Thus the sets
\{s_{1}+s_{5},s_{1}+s_{3},s_{1}+s_{6}\}
and
\{s_{3}+s_{7},s_{6}+s_{4},s_{5}+s_{2}\}
form the orbits of
s_{1}+s_{5}
and
s_{3}+s_{7}
under
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
, respectively. Notice also that the vector space
\boldsymbol{\langle}s_{1}+s_{5},s_{3}+s_{7},(s_{1}+s_{5})\times(s_{3}+s_{7})\boldsymbol{\rangle}
is 2-dimensional, while with a true cross product we would expect this vector space to be 3-dimensional (again, note that for this section, we will use the notation
\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}
to denote the vector space spanned by
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
). This leads us to take a closer look at the dynamics of the iterated cross product with respect to the dimension of the resulting vector spaces. Given
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
, we let
[\mathbf{v}]
denote the coordinate vector of
\mathbf{v}
with respect to the ordered basis
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}
.

Definition 10
.

Given an oriented Steiner system
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
, the vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
is a
zero-divisor
if there is a
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
such that
\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v}\boldsymbol{\rangle}\neq\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}
and
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=0
.

Definition 11
.

Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. Define
L_{\mathbf{w}}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
as
L_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{w}\times\mathbf{v}
. Set
L^{0}_{\mathbf{w}}
to be the identity map, set
L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}=L_{\mathbf{w}}
, and define
L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})=L_{\mathbf{w}}(L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}))
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. If
\dim\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}=\dim\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}
, then
\dim\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}=\dim\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k+n}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

If we have
\dim\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}=\dim\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}
then we can conclude that
L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\in\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}
. Thus we can write

L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})=b_{0}\mathbf{v}+b_{1}L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})+\cdots+b_{k}L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}).

This implies that

L^{k+2}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})=L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\times\mathbf{w}=b_{0}L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})+b_{1}L^{2}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})+\cdots+b_{k}L^{k+1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}).

Thus

L^{k+2}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\in\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}.

Continuing in this manner, we get

L^{k+n}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\in\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},L_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v}),\dots,L^{k}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\boldsymbol{\rangle}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. Then
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}\in\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}

\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=0

Proof.

The reverse direction is clear. Now let
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=a\mathbf{v}+b\mathbf{w}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
|\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}|^{2}=(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w})\cdot(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w})=(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w})\cdot(a\mathbf{v}+b\mathbf{w})=a(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{v}+b(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
, and so
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=0
.
∎

In the following proposition, given
\mathbf{v}=v_{1}s_{1}+\dots+v_{n}s_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
, we denote by
[\mathbf{v}]
the column vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose entries are the
s_{i}
-coefficients
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
of
\mathbf{v}
.

Proposition 5
.

Given an oriented Steiner triple system
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
of size
n
, let
M=M(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
be the
n\times n
matrix such that
M_{i,j}=s_{i}\times s_{j}.

If
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
then
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=\text{tr}([\mathbf{v}]^{T}M[\mathbf{w}]).

Proof.

Since the function
\text{tr}([\mathbf{v}]^{T}M[\mathbf{w}])
is bilinear (as it is a composition of bilinear functions), it is sufficient to prove the proposition for the case
\mathbf{v}=s_{i}
and
\mathbf{w}=s_{j}
. Note that
\text{tr}([\mathbf{v}]^{T}M[\mathbf{w}])=\text{tr}([\mathbf{w}][\mathbf{v}]^{T}M)
and that
[\mathbf{w}][\mathbf{v}]^{T}
is an
n\times n
matrix whose
(j,i)
-entry is
1
and with every other entry equal to 0. Thus the
(j,j)
-entry is the only nonzero diagonal entry of
[\mathbf{w}][\mathbf{v}]^{T}M
; specifically, the
(j,j)
-entry of
[\mathbf{w}][\mathbf{v}]^{T}M
is the
(i,j)
-entry of
M
, which by definition is
s_{i}\times s_{j}
. From this, we conclude that
s_{i}\times s_{j}=\text{tr}([s_{j}]M[s_{i}]^{T}).

By bilinearity, we have
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=\text{tr}([\mathbf{v}]^{T}M[\mathbf{w}])
for all
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
.
∎

Now we can explore the Steiner product entirely in terms of matrix-multiplication by
M
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))
be an oriented Steiner triple system of size
n
, let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
, and let
[\mathbf{v}]
be the column vector

[\mathbf{v}]=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\\
\vdots\\
v_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

Then there is a unique
n\times n
matrix
A_{\mathbf{w}}
such that
A_{\mathbf{w}}[\mathbf{v}]=[[\mathbf{w}]^{T}M[\mathbf{v}]].
Furthermore,
\mathbf{w}
is a zero-divisor if and only if
\text{rank}(A_{\mathbf{w}})<n-1
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{w}\times\mathbf{v}=\text{tr}([\mathbf{w}]^{T}M[\mathbf{v}])
. Note that
[\mathbf{w}]^{T}M
is a
1\times n
row vector with entries in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
. We will form the
n\times n
matrix
A_{\mathbf{w}}
whose
(i,j)
-entry is the
\mathbb{R}
-coefficient of
s_{i}
in the Steiner product
\mathbf{w}\times s_{j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and note that

A_{\mathbf{w}}[\mathbf{v}]=[[\mathbf{w}]^{T}M[\mathbf{v}]].

We will show that
A_{\mathbf{w}}
is skew-symmetric. Let
c
be the
s_{i}
-coefficient of the Steiner product
\mathbf{w}\times s_{j}
, and let
\{s_{i},s_{j},s_{k}\}\in T
. Then
c=f(s_{k},s_{j})w_{k}
, and the
s_{j}
-coefficient of
\mathbf{w}\times s_{i}
is
f(s_{k},s_{j})w_{k}=-c
. Thus
A_{\mathbf{w}}
is skew-symmetric and so

A_{\mathbf{w}}^{T}[\mathbf{v}]=[[\mathbf{v}]^{T}M[\mathbf{w}]]=[-\mathbf{w}\times\mathbf{v}]

since
M
is also skew-symmetric.

Now suppose that
\mathbf{w}
is a zero-divisor. Then there is some vector
\mathbf{v}
such that
\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v}\boldsymbol{\rangle}\neq\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}
and
\mathbf{w}\times\mathbf{v}=0
. Then
[\mathbf{w}]^{T}M[\mathbf{v}]=0
and so
[\mathbf{w}]^{T}M[\mathbf{v}]=[\mathbf{w}]^{T}A_{\mathbf{w}}[\mathbf{v}]=0
and so
A_{\mathbf{w}}[\mathbf{v}]=0
, so
[\mathbf{v}]\in\ker(A_{\mathbf{w}})
. Since we know that
\text{rank}(A_{\mathbf{w}})\leq n-1
because
[\mathbf{w}]\in\ker(A_{\mathbf{w}})
, we know
\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}\subseteq\ker(A_{\mathbf{w}})
and so
\text{rank}(A_{\mathbf{w}})<n-1
.

Now suppose
\text{rank}(A(\mathbf{v}))<n-1
. Then
\dim\ker(A(\mathbf{v}))\geq 2
and so there is some two-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}

\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}
such that
\boldsymbol{\langle}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\boldsymbol{\rangle}\subseteq\ker A(\mathbf{v})
. Then
[\mathbf{w}]^{T}A(\mathbf{v})=0
and so
\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}=0
, so
\mathbf{v}
is a zero-divisor.
∎

Proposition 7
.

Consider the two oriented Steiner triple systems

(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T))=\{[s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}],[s_{1},s_{4},s_{5}],[s_{1},s_{7},s_{6}],[s_{2},s_{4},s_{6}],[s_{2},s_{5},s_{7}],[s_{3},s_{4},s_{7}],[s_{3},s_{6},s_{5}]\},

Let
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{7}v_{i}s_{i}
and
\mathbf{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{7}w_{i}s_{i}
be general elements in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
.

\rm{For}\ (\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}(T)),\ \rm{we\ have}\ \text{rank}\begin{pmatrix}[\mathbf{v}]&[L^{1}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})]&\cdots&[L_{\mathbf{w}}^{6}(\mathbf{v})]\end{pmatrix}=3,

Using the Maple code in Appendix
3
, we have for general

and

4

For the case of

Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}
be general vectors.
Let
LN^{i}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{L^{i}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})}{|L^{i}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})|}
denote the normalization of
L^{i}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})
. The sequence

is a sequence of vectors on the unit sphere in

. Experimentally, one observes that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}LN^{i}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})=0.

A closer look suggests that the sequence converges to a repeating pattern of the form
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},-\mathbf{a},-\mathbf{b}
with
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{w}
all mutually orthogonal. Let
P
denote the plane spanned by
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
. If
\mathbf{w}
is fixed but we change
\mathbf{v}
then, experimentally, the new repeating pattern is of the form

for some orthogonal matrix

whose restriction to

is the identity. The following paragraphs explain these observations.

In Proposition
6
, we see that skew symmetric matrices play a fundamental role in the dynamics of the iterated Steiner product with a fixed vector
\mathbf{w}
. Real skew symmetric matrices are normal and have pure imaginary eigenvalues. By the spectral theorem, they can be diagonalized using a complex unitary matrix and can be block diagonalized using a real orthogonal matrix. If
A
is a real skew symmetric
n\times n
matrix then there exists an
n\times n
orthogonal matrix
Q\in O(n)
that conjugates
A
to the following form
[

,
13
]
:

Q\hskip 1.0ptA\hskip 1.0ptQ^{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}0&\lambda_{1}&0&0&\dots&0&0&&&\\

0&0&0&\lambda_{2}&\dots&0&0&&&\\
0&0&-\lambda_{2}&0&\dots&0&0&&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&&&\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&\lambda_{k}&&&\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&-\lambda_{k}&0&&&\\
&&&&&&&0&&\\
&&&&&&&&\ddots&\\

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
|\lambda_{1}|\geq|\lambda_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\lambda_{k}|

\pm i\lambda_{1},\dots,\pm i\lambda_{k}

A
is an
n\times n

has an odd number of zero eigenvalues.

Let
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathfrak{S}}

associated to the complex eigenvector pair
\pm i\lambda_{p}
. If
\mathbf{u}

\pi/2

is equal to the Steiner product induced by the oriented Steiner triple system
(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{O}_{1}(T))
given in Theorem
1
.

It is worth pointing out that the octonions can be used to build several examples of Moufang loops. A Moufang loop is very much like a group except that the binary operation is only required to satisfy a weakened version of the associativity axiom.

Steiner triple systems are a special case of Steiner systems which are in turn a special kind of block design. A Steiner system, S(t,k,n), is an n-element set S together with a distinguished set of k-element subsets of S (called blocks) with the property that each t-element subset of S is contained in exactly one block. Steiner triple systems are of the form S(2,3,n). It is an interesting endeavor to better understand and classify various types of block designs and to connect them with algebraic objects. Automorphism groups of Steiner systems (and block designs in general) can also be very interesting. For instance, the Mathieu groups are five sporadic simple groups denoted
M_{11},M_{12},M_{22},M_{23},M_{24}
. The Mathieu groups
M_{11},M_{12},M_{23},M_{24}
are the automorphism groups of Steiner systems on 11, 12, 23, and 24 points while
M_{22}
is an index two subgroup of the automorphism group of a Steiner system on 22 points. Mendelsohn
[
18
]
proved that any finite group G is isomorphic to the automorphism group of some Steiner triple system. Doyen and Kantor
[
19
]
showed that if G is a finite group then there is an integer
M_{G}
such that, for
n\geq M_{G}
and
n\equiv 1\ {\rm or}\ 3\mod{6}
, there is a Steiner triple system
S(2,3,n)
which has an automorphism group isomorphic to
G
. When
G
is the trivial group, it is known that
M_{G}=15
.

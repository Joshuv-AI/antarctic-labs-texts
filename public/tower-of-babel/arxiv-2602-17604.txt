Phase-sensitive representation of Majorana stabilizer states
Tomislav Begušić, Garnet Kin-Lic Chan

Abstract
Stabilizer states hold a special place in quantum information science due to their connection with quantum error correction and quantum circuit simulation. In the context of classical simulations of many-body physics, they are an example of states that can be both highly entangled and efficiently represented and transformed under Clifford operators. Recently, Clifford operators have been discussed in the context of fermionic quantum computation through their extension, the Majorana Clifford group. Here, we document the phase-sensitive form of the corresponding Majorana stabilizer states, as well as the algorithms for computing their amplitudes, their inner products, and update rules for transforming Majorana stabilizer states under Majorana Clifford gates.



I
Introduction

The classical simulation of quantum systems depends strongly on the representation of quantum states. In the context of qubit-based systems, an interesting example of states that are both highly entangled and efficiently representable are stabilizer states
Gottesman 1997
;
Gottesman 1998
, which are formed by applying Clifford gates to an initial computational basis state. Importantly, they can be difficult to represent using other common strategies, such as computational basis expansion or tensor networks.

In fermionic systems, and especially in quantum chemistry, the connection with concepts from quantum information science has typically proceeded by transforming into the qubit picture
Mishmash et al. 2023
;
Schleich et al. 2023
;
Projansky et al. 2025
. This can lead to a loss of interpretability, given that typical transformations, such as the Jordan-Wigner transformation, map local fermionic operators into non-local Pauli operators. Yet, discussions of stabilizer states and Clifford operators in fermionic systems are increasingly common, with applications ranging from quantifying non-stabilizerness of various fermionic states
Collura et al. 2025
;
Bera and Schirò 2025
;
Sierant et al. 2026
to identifying Clifford transformations that simplify the quantum-chemical electronic structure problem
Mishmash et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2025
. Alternatively, Majorana operators can serve as fermionic operators that are unitary and also retain locality of the original creation and annihilation operators. Majorana operators are essential in the discussion of fermionic quantum computation
Bravyi and Kitaev 2002
;
Bravyi et al. 2010
;
Vijay et al. 2015
;
Plugge et al. 2016
;
O’Brien et al. 2018
;
Litinski and von
Oppen 2018
;
McLauchlan and Béri 2024
. These operators can be used to define Majorana Clifford gates, a fermionic analogue of qubit Clifford operators, with the property that they transform Majoranas into Majoranas. While the examples of Majorana Clifford operators appeared already in the early work of Bravyi and Kitaev
Bravyi and Kitaev 2002
and have been employed since in the context of fermionic quantum computation
Hyart et al. 2013
;
O’Brien et al. 2018
, they have been explicitly discussed only in recent works under the names of “logical braid” operators
McLauchlan and Béri 2022
and fermionic (or Majorana) Clifford operators
Mudassar et al. 2024
;
Bettaque and Swingle 2025
;
Mudassar et al. 2025
;
Magoulas and Evangelista 2025
. Majorana Clifford transformations of Majorana operators can be efficiently simulated, which motivates, for example, sparse Majorana representations for the classical simulation of fermionic quantum expectation values
Miller et al. 2025
;
Rudolph et al. 2026
. Majorana Clifford operators have also been used to improve Trotter-based quantum simulation of fermionic systems
Gandon et al. 2026
.

Based on Majorana Clifford gates, one can also define Majorana stabilizer states, which are the topic of this work. In order to use them as a basis for representing arbitrary fermionic quantum states, we need a phase-sensitive representation of Majorana stabilizer states. For context, the analogous, qubit-based stabilizer state can be represented either without phase information
Gottesman 1997
;
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
, which is sufficient for evaluating expectation values and sampling from stabilizer states, or including the global phase, which is needed to represent general states as superpositions of stabilizer states. Phase-sensitive representations, such as the CH form
Bravyi et al. 2019
, the affine form
Dehaene and De
Moor 2003
;
Van Den Nest 2010
, and the graph state formalism
Hein et al. 2004
;
Anders and Briegel 2006
;
Kerzner 2021
;
Gosset et al. 2024
, have been used in methods for simulating quantum circuits dominated by Clifford gates
Bravyi and Gosset 2016
;
Bravyi et al. 2019
;
Pashayan et al. 2022
. Here, we follow the CH form as presented in Ref.
Bravyi et al. 2019
to define a phase-sensitive representation of Majorana stabilizer states. We provide algorithms for updating Majorana stabilizer states under Majorana Clifford operators, for computing probability amplitudes, and for computing inner products of two Majorana stabilizer states. These algorithms are sufficient to operate with fermionic states represented as superpositions of Majorana stabilizer states.

\bar{x}_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}x_{j},

(1)

and a binary value
\pi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}\in\{0,1\}
the parity of

. All addition of binary numbers is assumed to be mod 2 (logical exclusive OR operation or addition on

|x|

II
Majorana operators

Single-site Majorana operators are defined through

c_{k}=a_{k}+a_{k}^{\dagger},\quad\tilde{c}_{k}=i(a_{k}^{\dagger}-a_{k}),

(2)

where
a_{k}^{\dagger}
and
a_{k}
are standard fermionic creation and annihilation operators. In our notation, we deviate from a more conventional approach to label all Majorana operators with
c
(or
\gamma
), where the indices run from
0
to
2n-1

Bravyi and Kitaev 2002
;
Mudassar et al. 2024
. We chose the above notation to easily keep track of the fermionic site
k
on which the Majorana operators
c_{k}
and
\tilde{c}_{k}
act
Surace and Tagliacozzo 2022
.

Majorana operators satisfy the following anticommutation relations:

\{\gamma_{j},\gamma^{\prime}_{k}\}=2\delta_{jk}\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}},\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\{c,\tilde{c}\}.

(3)

They are also Hermitian and unitary:

\gamma^{\dagger}_{k}=\gamma_{k},\quad\gamma_{k}^{2}=I,\quad\gamma\in\{c,\tilde{c}\}

(4)

A general
n
-site Majorana operator (Majorana string) can be defined as

\Gamma=i^{\varphi}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}}c_{k}^{x_{k}},

(5)

where

p_{k}=i\tilde{c}_{k}c_{k}

(6)

with (anti)commutation relations

[p_{j},p_{k}]=0,\quad[c_{j},p_{k}]=0\,(j\neq k),\quad\{c_{k},p_{k}\}=0,

(7)

z_{k},x_{k}\in\{0,1\}
, and
\varphi\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. This representation of Majorana operators can be used to store them in a computer and perform operations, such as checking if two Majorana operators commute or taking their product. Majorana strings are clearly unitary and either Hermitian or anti-Hermitian. We note that an alternative representation of Majorana operators has been used in Ref.
Mudassar et al. 2024
(see also a slightly different version in
Bettaque and Swingle 2025
),

\Gamma=i^{r}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}^{m_{2k}}\tilde{c}_{k}^{m_{2k+1}},

(8)

which stores the operator using a phase
r
and a binary vector
m
of length 2
n
. The two representations are related by

\varphi=r+|m|+2m_{e}\cdot m_{o},\quad z=m_{o},\quad x=m_{e}+m_{o},

(9)

where
m_{e}=(m_{0},m_{2},\dots,m_{2n-2})
is the vector of even elements of
m
and
m_{o}=(m_{1},m_{3},\dots,m_{2n-1})
is the vector of odd elements of
m
.

The
(\varphi,z,x)
representation used in this work is closely related to the symplectic representation of Pauli operators. For example, we can verify if an operator is diagonal in the computational basis by simply checking that all
x_{k}=0
. We will refer to such operators as p-type operators. Similarly, Majorana operators with an even number
|x|
of nonzero elements in
x
are of even parity and those with odd
|x|
are of odd parity. Below, we provide explicit expressions for operations on Majorana strings in the
(\varphi,z,x)
form.

II.1
Action on a computational basis state

Let us assume we are given
\Gamma
defined by
(\varphi,z,x)
and a computational basis state
|s\rangle
. Using

\displaystyle c_{k}|s\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s_{k}}|s+e_{k}\rangle,

(10)

\displaystyle p_{k}|s\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{s_{k}}|s\rangle,

(11)

we have

\displaystyle\Gamma|s\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}}c_{k}^{x_{k}}|s\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi}(-1)^{s\cdot\bar{x}+\pi(x)\pi(s)}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}}|s+x\rangle

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi}(-1)^{(s+x)\cdot z+s\cdot\bar{x}+\pi(x)\pi(s)}|s+x\rangle,

(14)

where
e_{k}
is a binary vector with zeros everywhere except at position
k
,
\bar{x}
is the parity representation of
x
,
\pi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{j}
is the parity of
x
(see
Notation
).

II.2
Product of Majorana operators

Given Majorana operators
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
, the product is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma\Gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi+\varphi^{\prime}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}}c_{k}^{x_{k}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{x_{k}^{\prime}}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi+\varphi^{\prime}}(-1)^{z^{\prime}\cdot x}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}+z_{k}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{x_{k}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}^{x_{k}^{\prime}}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi+\varphi^{\prime}}(-1)^{z^{\prime}\cdot x+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}x_{k}^{\prime}x_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}+z_{k}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{x_{k}+x_{k}^{\prime}}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi+\varphi^{\prime}}(-1)^{z^{\prime}\cdot x+x^{\prime}\cdot\bar{x}+\pi(x^{\prime})\pi(x)}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}+z_{k}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{x_{k}+x_{k}^{\prime}}.

(18)

Therefore, the parameters of the product
\Gamma\Gamma^{\prime}
are
(\varphi+\varphi^{\prime}+2(z^{\prime}\cdot x+x^{\prime}\cdot\bar{x}+\pi(x^{\prime})\pi(x)),z+z^{\prime},x+x^{\prime})
.

II.3
Commutation of Majorana operators

Using the expression derived above, we can conclude that

\Gamma\Gamma^{\prime}=(-1)^{z^{\prime}\cdot x+x^{\prime}\cdot\bar{x}+z\cdot x^{\prime}+x\cdot\bar{x}^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\prime}\Gamma,

(19)

i.e., the Majorana operators commute if
z^{\prime}\cdot x+x^{\prime}\cdot\bar{x}+z\cdot x^{\prime}+x\cdot\bar{x}^{\prime}=0
. We can further avoid evaluating the parity representations of
x
and
x^{\prime}
by rewriting
x^{\prime}\cdot\bar{x}+x\cdot\bar{x}^{\prime}=x\cdot x^{\prime}+\pi(x)\pi(x^{\prime})
, which yields

\Gamma\,\text{anticommutes with}\,\Gamma^{\prime}=z^{\prime}\cdot x+x^{\prime}\cdot(z+x)+\pi(x)\pi(x^{\prime}).

(20)

III
Majorana Clifford operators

To define Clifford operators in this setting, we start by introducing unitary rotations

U(\Gamma,\theta)=\exp(-i\frac{\theta}{2}\Gamma),

(21)

where
\Gamma
is a Hermitian Majorana operator and
\theta
a real-valued angle. Because
\Gamma^{2}=1
, we have

\exp(-i\frac{\theta}{2}\Gamma)=\cos(\theta/2)-i\sin(\theta/2)\Gamma,

(22)

which is same as for Pauli rotation operators. Now all other properties follow similarly, namely, for two anticommuting Majorana operators
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
,

U(\Gamma_{2},\theta)^{\dagger}\Gamma_{1}U(\Gamma_{2},\theta)=\cos(\theta)\Gamma_{1}+i\sin(\theta)\Gamma_{2}\Gamma_{1}.

(23)

For angles
\theta=j\pi/2
with integer
j
, only one term on the right-hand side remains. Rotations with such angles belong to the group of Majorana Clifford operators, which transform a single Majorana operator
\Gamma
into another Majorana operator
\Gamma^{\prime}
instead of a general sum of Majorana operators
Bettaque and Swingle 2025
. In fact, any Majorana Clifford operator can be decomposed into a product of Majorana Clifford rotations
U(\Gamma,j\frac{\pi}{2})
. In this work, we focus on a subgroup of parity-preserving Majorana Clifford operators, p-Cliffords
Bettaque and Swingle 2025
, which are spanned by Majorana Clifford rotations
U(\Gamma,j\frac{\pi}{2})
with even-parity
\Gamma
. In the remainder, we focus on the following generating set of the p-Clifford group:

\eta_{jk}=e^{\frac{\pi}{4}c_{j}c_{k}},\,\eta_{j}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}p_{j}},\,W_{jk}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}p_{j}p_{k}}.

(24)

\eta_{j}
and
\eta_{jk}
operators can generate arbitrary two-index Clifford rotations. By transforming
W
operators with
\eta_{jk}
operators, we can generate arbitrary four-index Majorana rotations. By further transforming four-index rotations with each other, we can generate rotations with strings containing 6, 8, and more Majorana operators. Finally, we note that the set (
24
) is not unique and that other choices of generating operators could be considered
McLauchlan and Béri 2022
;
Mudassar et al. 2024
.

IV
Phase-sensitive representation of Majorana stabilizer states

In analogy with stabilizer states, we can define Majorana stabilizer states as

|\psi\rangle=U|0\rangle,

(25)

where
U
is a product of Majorana Clifford operators and
|0\rangle
is the vacuum state. These include all computational basis states because
c_{k}
and
\tilde{c}_{k}
are Majorana Clifford operators.

We can represent Majorana stabilizer states by an equivalent of the phase-sensitive CH form for stabilizer states. Here, we use

|\psi\rangle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}U_{C}U_{B}|s\rangle,

(26)

where

U_{C}|0\rangle=|0\rangle

(27)

defines a control-type Clifford operator
Bravyi et al. 2019
and
\phi
is an integer.
U_{B}
is a product

U_{B}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}B_{k}^{b_{k}}

(28)

of mutually commuting braiding operators

B_{k}=e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1}},\quad k=0,1,\dots,n-1.

(29)

where
b\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.
Our algorithms will use the fact that all p-Clifford operators preserve parity to keep
b_{n-1}=0
. For convenience, we will still use the notation where
b
has
n
elements.

Overall, a general Majorana stabilizer state can be represented by
(\phi,U_{C},b,s)
, where
U_{C}
is represented by the stabilizer tableau introduced below.

IV.1
Stabilizer tableau representation of
U_{C}

U_{C}
is stored by keeping track of its action on all
c_{k}
and
p_{k}
, in analogy with the stabilizer tableau algorithm
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
;
Bravyi et al. 2019
for qubits:

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{j}U_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{jk}}

(30)

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{j}U_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}},

(31)

where
\omega_{j}\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
E,F,G
are binary
n\times n
matrices. In Eq. (
30
) we used the fact that
U_{C}
is control-type, which means that it can only transform p-type operators into p-type operators without a change in phase. Indeed, let

\Gamma=U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{j}U_{C}.

(32)

Then,

\Gamma|0\rangle=U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{j}U_{C}|0\rangle=|0\rangle,

(33)

which implies that
\Gamma
is represented by
(\varphi=0,z,x=0)
[see Eq. (
14
)].

For an arbitrary Majorana operator
\Gamma
described by
(\varphi,z,x)
, we can evaluate

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}\Gamma U_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}i^{\varphi}\prod_{j}p_{j}^{z_{j}}c_{j}^{x_{j}}U_{C}

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi}\prod_{j}U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{j}^{z_{j}}U_{C}U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{x_{j}}U_{C}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi}\prod_{j}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{jk}z_{j}}i^{\omega_{j}x_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}x_{j}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}x_{j}}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\varphi}\prod_{j}\Gamma^{(j)},

(37)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(j)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega_{j}x_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{jk}z_{j}+F_{jk}x_{j}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}x_{j}}

(38)

This operation scales as
\mathcal{O}((|z|+|x|)n)=\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
for arbitrary Majorana operators.

Due to commutation relations between
c_{j}
and
p_{j}
Majorana operators, matrices
E
,
F
, and
G
are not completely independent. In Appendix B, we derive the following identities:

\displaystyle E\cdot G^{T}=E^{T}\cdot G

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I,

(39)

\displaystyle F\cdot G^{T}+G\cdot F^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+G\cdot G^{T}.

(40)

Furthermore, each row of matrix
G
must have an odd number of ones because it corresponds to the transformation of an odd-parity, local Majorana operator
c_{j}
.

IV.2
Transformation of a Majorana operator under
U_{B}

A key ingredient in many procedures is the conjugation of a general Majorana operator
\Gamma
by
U_{B}
,

\Gamma^{\prime}=U_{B}^{\dagger}\Gamma U_{B},

(41)

where we assume
U_{B}
is provided as a binary array
b
and
\Gamma
by
(\varphi,z,x)
. To evaluate
\Gamma^{\prime}
, we first need to check which
B_{k}
do not commute with
\Gamma
:

\beta_{k}=B_{k}^{b_{k}}\,\text{does not commute with}\,\Gamma=b_{k}\cdot(x_{k}+z_{k}+z_{k+1}).

(42)

For each
B_{k}^{b_{k}}
that does not commute with
\Gamma
, we will have a factor of
c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1}
multiplying
\Gamma
from the left. This is because:

e^{\frac{\pi}{4}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1}}\Gamma_{C}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1}}=e^{\frac{\pi}{2}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1}}\Gamma_{C}=c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1}\Gamma_{C},

(43)

and because all
B_{k}
commute with each other. Finally, we need to compute the product

\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma_{B}(\beta)\Gamma,

(44)

where

\Gamma_{B}(\beta)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1})^{\beta_{k}}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(-ic_{k}p_{k+1}c_{k+1})^{\beta_{k}}=i^{-|\beta|}c_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}p_{k}^{\beta_{k-1}}c_{k}^{\beta_{k-1}+\beta_{k}}.

(45)

In Eq. (
45
), we used the fact that
b_{n-1}=0
(and hence
\beta_{n-1}=0
).

IV.3
Expectation value of a Majorana operator

Given a Majorana stabilizer state
|\psi\rangle
and a Majorana operator
\Gamma
, we can compute the expectation value

\langle\psi|\Gamma|\psi\rangle=i^{\varphi^{\prime}}(-1)^{z^{\prime}\cdot s}\delta_{x^{\prime}},

(46)

where
(\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime},x^{\prime})
correspond to
\Gamma^{\prime}=U_{B}^{\dagger}U_{C}^{\dagger}\Gamma U_{C}U_{B}
and

\delta_{x}=\begin{cases}1,\quad x_{0}=x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0,\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(47)

\Gamma^{\prime}
can be computed using procedures introduced in Secs.
IV.1
and
IV.2
.

IV.5
Probability amplitudes of Majorana stabilizer states

The first step in computing probability amplitudes of the form
\langle x|\psi\rangle
, where
|\psi\rangle=e^{i\pi\phi/4}U_{C}U_{B}|s\rangle
and
x
a computational basis state, is to write

\langle x|=\langle 0|\Gamma,\quad\Gamma=(-1)^{\lfloor|x|/2\rfloor}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}^{x_{k}}.

(48)

We can apply
\Gamma
to
|\psi\rangle
to obtain updated
\phi
,
U_{C}
,
b
, and
s
. Therefore, we now only need a procedure to compute
\langle 0|\psi\rangle
, which can be done in
\mathcal{O}(|x|n)
time. We first note that this amplitude is zero if
s
is of odd parity,
\pi(s)=1
. If
\pi(s)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle 0|e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}U_{C}U_{B}|s\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}\langle 0|U_{B}|s\rangle

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-|b|/2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}\langle 0|\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1})^{b_{k}}|s\rangle

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-|b|/2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}\langle 0|\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(-c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1})^{\bar{s}_{k}}|s\rangle\delta_{b\bar{s},\bar{s}}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-|b|/2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}i^{|\bar{s}|}\langle 0|\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{\bar{s}_{k-1}}c_{k}^{s_{k}}|s\rangle\delta_{b\bar{s},\bar{s}}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-|b|/2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}i^{|\bar{s}|}(-1)^{|s|/2}\langle 0|\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{\bar{s}_{k-1}}|0\rangle\delta_{b\bar{s},\bar{s}}

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-|b|/2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}i^{|\bar{s}|}(-1)^{|s|/2}\delta_{b\bar{s},\bar{s}}

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-|b|/2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}i^{|\bar{s}|+|s|}\delta_{b\bar{s},\bar{s}}

(55)

In going from (
50
) to (
51
), we reduced the exponential sum of Majorana operators to a single Majorana operator that has a non-zero off-diagonal matrix element between the
|0\rangle
and
|s\rangle
states. This Majorana operator is exactly the product

(-c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k+1})^{\bar{s}_{k}}=i^{|\bar{s}|}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{\bar{s}_{k-1}}c_{k}^{s_{k}},

(56)

and is present in Eq. (

for every

at which
\bar{s}_{k}=1
. This condition is enforced by the delta function:

\delta_{b\bar{s},\bar{s}}=\begin{cases}1,\quad b_{k}\bar{s}_{k}=\bar{s}_{k},\quad k=0,1,\dots,n-1,\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(57)

IV.6
Inner product of two Majorana stabilizer states

Given stabilizer states
|\psi\rangle
and
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
, we can compute their inner product in
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
time.

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi^{\prime}}\langle s^{\prime}|U_{B}^{\prime\dagger}U_{C}^{\prime\dagger}|\psi\rangle

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi^{\prime}}\langle s^{\prime}|U_{C}^{\prime\dagger}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}e^{-ib_{j}^{\prime}\frac{\pi}{4}\Gamma_{j}}|\psi\rangle

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi^{\prime}}\langle s^{\prime}|U_{C}^{\prime\dagger}|\bar{\psi}\rangle

(60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi^{\prime}}(-i)^{\theta}\langle t|\bar{\psi}\rangle,

(61)

where we introduced

\displaystyle\Gamma_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\prime}ic_{j}\tilde{c}_{j+1}U_{C}^{\prime\dagger},

(62)

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\prime}|s^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\theta}|t\rangle,

(63)

and

|\bar{\psi}\rangle=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}e^{-ib_{j}^{\prime}\frac{\pi}{4}\Gamma_{j}}|\psi\rangle.

(64)

Equation (
61
) can be evaluated using expressions derived in Sec.
IV.5
for the amplitudes of stabilizer states. Two additional subroutines needed to evaluate Eqs. (
62
) and (
63
) are the construction of the stabilizer tableaux for
U_{C}^{\dagger}
and applying
U_{C}
on a computational basis state. These are given in Appendix B.

The main source of cubic scaling of the inner product is the fact that constructing stabilizer tableux for
U_{C}^{\dagger}
involves matrix products. Another source is the fact that there are
\mathcal{O}(n)
nonzero elements of
b^{\prime}
and for each of these we have to apply one general Majorana Clifford rotation with a cost of
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
. As a minor improvement to the algorithm, we first check if
b<b^{\prime}
, in which case we compute
\langle\psi|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
and obtain the final result as its complex conjugate. The cost of this part of the computation is then
\mathcal{O}(\min(|b|,|b^{\prime}|)n^{2})
.

V
Conclusion

We have presented a set of algorithms for representing Majorana stabilizer states, updating them upon action by parity-preserving Majorana Clifford operators, and computing probability amplitudes and inner products. We also provide a basic Python code at
github.com/tbegusic/fermionic-stabilizer-states
that implements these functions and can be used to verify the correctness of the algorithms by comparing against Qiskit
Javadi-Abhari et al. 2024
Statevector simulator running in the Jordan-Wigner representation.

Overall, these algorithms are needed for representing general fermionic states by superpositions of Majorana stabilizer states,
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}|\phi_{i}\rangle
. Specifically, they allow us to prepare Majorana stabilizer states, evaluate overlaps
\langle\phi_{i}|\phi_{j}\rangle
, and Hamiltonian (or other operator) matrix elements
\langle\phi_{i}|H|\phi_{j}\rangle
. Regarding the simulation of fermionic quantum circuits, our algorithms enable low-rank stabilizer methods working directly in the fermionic picture.

Appendix A
Majorana stabilizer state procedures

A.1
Update rules for applying a Majorana operator

The procedure to evaluate
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=\Gamma|\psi\rangle
is closely related to the computation of the expectation value of a Majorana operator (Sec.
IV.3
). Let us assume we are given
\Gamma
through
(\varphi,z,x)
and
|\psi\rangle
through
(\phi,U_{C},b,s)
. Then,

\displaystyle|\psi^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma|\psi\rangle

(65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}U_{C}U_{B}\Gamma^{\prime}|s\rangle

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}i^{\varphi^{\prime}}(-1)^{(s+x^{\prime})\cdot z^{\prime}+s\cdot\bar{x}^{\prime}+\pi(x^{\prime})\pi(s)}U_{C}U_{B}|s+x^{\prime}\rangle,

(67)

where
(\varphi\prime,z^{\prime},x^{\prime})
are the parameters of
\Gamma^{\prime}=U_{B}^{\dagger}U_{C}^{\dagger}\Gamma U_{C}U_{B}
. The update rules are:

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle\phi+2\varphi^{\prime}+4[(s+x^{\prime})\cdot z^{\prime}+s\cdot\bar{x}^{\prime}+\pi(x^{\prime})\pi(s)]

(68)

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle s+x^{\prime}

(69)

A.2
Update rules for applying
\eta_{j}
and
W_{jk}

These gates are diagonal in the computational basis and are control-type up to a phase. As shown below, this means that we only need to update
\phi
and
U_{C}
.

Let us first consider
\eta_{j}=\exp(-i\pi p_{j}/4)
and a stabilizer state defined by
(\phi,U_{C},b,s)
, where
U_{C}
is represented by a set of parameters
(\omega,E,F,G)
as in Sec.
IV.1
. We have

\eta_{j}|\psi\rangle=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-p_{j})}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}U_{C}U_{B}|s\rangle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(\phi-1)}U_{C}^{\prime}U_{B}|s\rangle,

(70)

where
U_{C}^{\prime}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-p_{j})}U_{C}
and
e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-p_{j})}
is a control-type operator satisfying

e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-p_{j})}|0\rangle=|0\rangle.

(71)

We now need to derive the update rules for
\omega
,
E
,
F
, and
G
to represent
U_{C}^{\prime}
.

E
is not modified because

U_{C}^{\dagger}\eta_{j}^{\dagger}p_{k}\eta_{j}U_{C}=U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{k}U_{C}.

(72)

For the remaining parameters, we evaluate

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}\eta_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}\eta_{j}U_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}ip_{j}c_{j}U_{C}

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{j,k}}i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{j,k}}c_{k}^{G_{j,k}}

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega_{j}+1}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{j,k}+F_{j,k}}c_{k}^{G_{j,k}}

(75)

and

U_{C}^{\dagger}\eta_{j}^{\dagger}c_{k}\eta_{j}U_{C}=U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{k}U_{C},\quad k\neq j.

(76)

Therefore, the update rules are

\phi\leftarrow\phi-1,\quad F_{j}\leftarrow F_{j}+E_{j},\quad\omega_{j}\leftarrow\omega_{j}+1.

(77)

In the code provided with this manuscript, we implemented
\eta_{j}^{\pm}=\exp(\mp i\frac{\pi}{4}p_{j})
, for which the update rules are simply

\phi\leftarrow\phi\mp 1,\quad F_{j}\leftarrow F_{j}+E_{j},\quad\omega_{j}\leftarrow\omega_{j}\pm 1.

(78)

Similarly, for
W_{jk}
, we have

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}W_{jk}^{\dagger}c_{j}W_{jk}U_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}ip_{j}p_{k}c_{j}U_{C}

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}p_{l}^{E_{j,l}}\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}p_{l}^{E_{k,l}}i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}p_{l}^{F_{j,l}}c_{l}^{G_{j,l}}

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega_{j}+1}\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}p_{l}^{E_{j,l}+E_{k,l}+F_{j,l}}c_{k}^{G_{j,k}}

(81)

and the update rules are

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle\phi-1,

(82)

\displaystyle\quad F_{j/k}

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle F_{j/k}+E_{j}+E_{k},\quad\omega_{j/k}\leftarrow\omega_{j/k}+1.

(83)

As for
\eta_{j}
, here we also implemented a more general version
W_{jk}^{\pm}=\exp(\mp i\frac{\pi}{4}p_{j}p_{k})
with update rules

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle\phi\mp 1,

(84)

\displaystyle\quad F_{j/k}

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle F_{j/k}+E_{j}+E_{k},\quad\omega_{j/k}\leftarrow\omega_{j/k}\pm 1.

(85)

A.3
Update rules for applying
\eta_{jk}

The most complicated part is the update of the Majorana stabilizer state after applying
\eta_{jk}
(
j\neq k
). Following the strategy for applying the Hadamard gate to the CH form for qubit stabilizer states, the basic steps to derive the algorithm involve:

1.

Introduce
\Gamma=U_{B}^{\dagger}U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{j}c_{k}U_{C}U_{B}
, so that

\eta_{jk}U_{C}U_{B}|s\rangle=U_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\Gamma)|s\rangle=U_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle).

(86)

2.

Find Majorana Clifford operator
C
, such that

C(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle)=|s^{\prime}\rangle+i^{\theta^{\prime}}|t^{\prime}\rangle,s_{w}^{\prime}\neq t_{w}^{\prime},s_{w+1}^{\prime}\neq t_{w+1}^{\prime},s_{k\neq w,w+1}^{\prime}=t_{k\neq w,w+1}^{\prime},

(87)

i.e., that
s^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime}
differ only at two neighboring sites
w
and
w+1
, and that

U_{B}C^{\dagger}U_{B}^{\dagger}=V_{C},

(88)

where
V_{C}
is a control-type Clifford. This means that we can rewrite

U_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle)=U_{C}V_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s^{\prime}\rangle+i^{\theta^{\prime}}|t^{\prime}\rangle)

(89)

and simply update
U_{C}\leftarrow U_{C}V_{C}
.

3.

Rewrite
\exp(i\pi\phi/4)U_{C}U_{B}(|s^{\prime}\rangle+i^{\theta^{\prime}}|t^{\prime}\rangle)
into
\exp(i\pi\phi^{\prime}/4)U_{C}^{\prime}U_{B}^{\prime}|u\rangle
.

The first step can be performed in
O(n)
time because the initial Majorana string
c_{j}c_{k}
is local. In the second step,
V_{C}
will be a non-local operator and updating the
E,F,G
matrices will lead to
O(n^{2})
runtime. The third step can be performed in
O(n^{2})
time because in certain cases we need to update
U_{C}
with a non-local control-type operator. The overall runtime is
O(n^{2})
, which coincides with the standard CH update algorithm for a Hadamard gate. Let us now look into each of these steps in detail.

Step 1

We are given
\eta_{jk}
(in the form of sites
j
and
k
) and a stabilizer state with parameters
(\phi,\omega,E,F,G,b,s)
. We first compute the parameters
(\varphi,z,x)
of
\Gamma=U_{B}^{\dagger}U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{j}c_{k}U_{C}U_{B}
as described earlier. We can evaluate
\theta
and
t
of Eq. (
86
) by using Eq. (
14
). In the following Section, we will use
x=s+t
to denote a binary array with 1 where
s
and
t
are different and 0 where they are equal.

Step 2

Our goal now is to modify
s
and
t
so that they differ only at two neighboring sites. To this end, we introduce a control operator

C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(I-\Gamma_{1})(I-\Gamma_{2})},

(90)

where
[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}]=0
and both
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are Hermitian Majorana operators.
C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{2}=I
and therefore
C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}
is Hermitian. We further impose that one of the two operators is a p-type Majorana operator, for example,
\Gamma_{1}=\prod_{k\in K}p_{k}
for some set of indices
K
. Then
C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}
is a control-type operator in the sense that
C|0\rangle=|0\rangle
. It has the property that

C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}|s\rangle=\begin{cases}|s\rangle,\quad\Gamma_{1}|s\rangle=|s\rangle\\
\Gamma_{2}|s\rangle,\quad\Gamma_{1}|s\rangle=-|s\rangle.\end{cases}

(91)

Below, we identify
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
that allow us to implement the desired transformation of
|s\rangle
and
|t\rangle
.

We begin by defining an array of indices
v
, which stores all indices
i
at which
b_{i}=0
. Let
n_{v}
denote the number of elements in
v
(i.e., the number of zeros in
b
). We assume that
b_{n-1}=0
, so
n-1
is always the last element of
v
and
n_{v}>0
. We can also define
n_{v}
arrays of indices
S_{0}=[0,v_{0}]=(0,1,\dots,v_{0})
and
S_{0<j<n_{v}}=(v_{j-1},v_{j}]=(v_{j-1}+1,v_{j-1}+2,\dots,v_{j})
. Now the operators of the form
\prod_{k\in S_{j}}p_{k}
commute with
U_{B}
because they anticommute only with
B_{v_{j-1}}
(for
j>0
) and
B_{v_{j}}
, which are not present in
U_{B}
by definition of
v
.

We must distinguish two cases:

1. There exists
j\in[0,n_{v})
such that
\sum_{k\in S_{j}}x_{k}=1
.

2.
\sum_{k\in S_{j}}x_{k}=0
for all
j\in[0,n_{v})
.

In words, we ask if there is any subset of indices
S_{j}
on which
s
and
t
differ odd number of times.

Case 1.
Let us assume there exists
j
such that
\sum_{k\in S_{j}}x_{k}=1
. We choose the smallest value of
j
and set

\Gamma_{1}=\prod_{k\in S_{j}}p_{k}.

(92)

If
\sum_{k\in S_{j}}s_{k}=0
, then
\Gamma_{1}|s\rangle=|s\rangle
and, because we know that
s
and
t
differ an odd number of times on
S_{j}
indices,
\Gamma_{1}|t\rangle=-|t\rangle
. Otherwise, if
\sum_{k\in S_{j}}s_{k}=1
, we can rewrite

|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle=i^{\theta}(|t\rangle+i^{-\theta}|s\rangle),

(93)

which means that after substituting
s\leftarrow t
,
t\leftarrow s
,
\phi\leftarrow\phi+2\theta
,
\theta\leftarrow-\theta
, we again recover
\Gamma_{1}|s\rangle=|s\rangle
and
\Gamma_{1}|t\rangle=-|t\rangle
.
Therefore,

C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}|s\rangle=|s\rangle,\quad C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}|t\rangle=\Gamma_{2}|t\rangle.

(94)

We set
\Gamma_{2}
as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{|x^{\prime}|/2}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}^{x^{\prime}_{k}},

(95)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+e_{w}+e_{w+1}

(96)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{j-1},\quad j>0\\
v_{0},\quad j=0,\end{cases}

(97)

where
|x|
denotes the number of nonzero entries in
x
,
e_{w}
is a unit vector with 1 at position
w
and zeros elsewhere, and the prefactor ensures that
\Gamma_{2}
is Hermitian.

We now have

U_{C}U_{B}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle)=U_{C}U_{B}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle)=U_{C}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}}U_{B}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta^{\prime}}|t^{\prime}\rangle),

(98)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{B}\Gamma_{2}U_{B}^{\dagger},

(99)

\displaystyle i^{\theta^{\prime}}|t^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\theta}\Gamma_{2}|t\rangle,

(100)

and, by construction,
|t^{\prime}\rangle=|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle
.

Note that
w\neq n-1
, which can be seen easily from Eq. (
97
): It is not possible in the first case and in the second case it would imply
v_{0}=n-1
, which would mean that
s
and
t
differ in total on an odd number of sites (this is not possible because we consider only parity-preserving Majorana Clifford gates).

Case 2.
Let us assume that
\sum_{k\in S_{j}}x_{k}=0
for all
j\in[0,n_{v})
. Now we cannot choose
\Gamma_{1}
such that it commutes with
U_{B}
and
\Gamma_{1}|s\rangle=-\Gamma_{1}|t\rangle
. We therefore choose

\Gamma_{2}=(-1)^{s_{w}}p_{w},

(101)

where
w
is the first site at which
s_{w}\neq t_{w}
. Then
\Gamma_{2}|s\rangle=|s\rangle
and
\Gamma_{2}|t\rangle=-|t\rangle
. Our strategy will be to identify
\Gamma_{1}
such that

\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}=U_{B}\Gamma_{1}U_{B}^{\dagger}=\prod_{k\in K}p_{k},\quad[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}]=0,

(102)

and that
|t^{\prime}\rangle\propto\Gamma_{1}|t\rangle
and
|s\rangle
differ only at two neighboring sites. Indeed, in that case we would have

U_{C}U_{B}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle=U_{C}U_{B}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{\dagger}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|t\rangle)=U_{C}C_{\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}U_{B}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta^{\prime}}|t^{\prime}\rangle),

(103)

where
C_{\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}}
is a control-type operator because of Eq. (
102
), i.e.,
C_{\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}}|0\rangle=|0\rangle
, and can be merged into the representation of
U_{C}
by updating its stabilizer tableau representation. What follows is the construction of
\Gamma_{1}
.

Let us define Majorana operators

Y_{j}=ic_{j}c_{j+1}\prod_{k=j+2}^{n-1}p_{k},\quad j\in[0,n-1],

(104)

which satisfy

B_{j}Y_{j}B_{j}^{\dagger}=\prod_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad[Y_{j},B_{i\neq j}]=0.

(105)

We also introduce an array
m
containing indices of nonzero elements in the binary array
x
(the difference of
s
and
t
). By assumption of
Case 2
, we know that all
b_{m_{2k}\leq j<m_{2k+1}}=1
. Otherwise, if any of these were
0
, we would have odd number of nonzero elements of
x
in
S_{j}
, which would correspond to
Case 1
. This motivates us to define

\Lambda_{i_{1},i_{2}}=\prod_{j=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}Y_{j},\quad m_{2k}\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq m_{2k+1}\quad k=[0,|x|/2)

(106)

that can be written out explicitly as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i_{1},i_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{(i_{2}-i_{1})/2}c_{i_{1}}p_{i_{1}+2}p_{i_{1}+4}\dots p_{i_{2}}c_{i_{2}},\quad i_{2}-i_{1}=\,\text{even},\\
(-1)^{(i_{2}-i_{1}-1)/2}ic_{i_{1}}p_{i_{1}+2}p_{i_{1}+4}\dots p_{i_{2}-1}c_{i_{2}}\prod_{j=i_{2}+1}^{n-1}p_{j},\quad i_{2}-i_{1}=\,\text{odd}\\
\end{cases}

(107)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{i_{2}-i_{1}}c_{i_{1}}\prod_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{i_{2}-i_{1}}{2}\rfloor}p_{i_{1}+2k}c_{i_{2}}\prod_{j=i_{2}+1}^{n-1}p_{j}^{(i_{2}-i_{1})\,\text{mod}\,2}

(108)

and satisfy

\displaystyle U_{B}\Lambda_{i_{1},i_{2}}U_{B}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}B_{j}Y_{j}B_{j}^{\dagger}

(109)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}\prod_{l=j+1}^{n-1}p_{l}

(110)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p_{i_{1}+1}p_{i_{1}+3}\dots p_{i_{2}-1},\quad i_{2}-i_{1}=\,\text{even},\\
p_{i_{1}+1}p_{i_{1}+3}\dots p_{i_{2}-2}\prod_{j=i_{2}}^{n-1}p_{j},\quad i_{2}-i_{1}=\,\text{odd}.\\
\end{cases}

(111)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{i_{2}-i_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor}p_{i_{1}+1+2k}\prod_{j=i_{2}+1}^{n-1}p_{j}^{(i_{2}-i_{1})\,\text{mod}\,2}

(112)

We can see from Eq. (
108
) that
\Lambda_{i_{1},i_{2}}
changes a computational basis state at exactly two sites (
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
), while from Eq. (
112
) we see that these operators transform under
U_{B}
into a product of diagonal operators [see (
102
)]. Therefore, we can use them to construct

\Gamma_{1}=\Lambda_{m_{0}+1,m_{1}}\prod_{k=1}^{|x|/2-1}\Lambda_{m_{2k},m_{2k+1}},

(113)

which commutes with
\Gamma_{2}
, satisfies (
102
), and

\Gamma_{1}i^{\theta}|t\rangle=i^{\theta^{\prime}}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle,\quad w=m_{0}.

(114)

As in
Case 1
,
w\neq n-1
because
m_{0}=n-1
would imply that
s
and
t
differ only at one site.

\Gamma_{1}
can be constructed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time by preparing separately the indices enclosed by
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
[first part of Eq. (
108
)] and the tails composed of
p_{j}
operators [last part of Eq. (
108
)]. For the former, we simply iterate over all pairs of
m_{2k}
and
m_{2k+1}
and fill in values for
x
and
z
. From (
108
) and (
113
), the phase is

\varphi=-1+\sum_{k=0}^{|x|/2-1}(m_{2k+1}-m_{2k}).

(115)

For the tails of
p_{j}
operators, we have

z^{(P)}_{m_{2k-1}+1\leq j\leq m_{2k+1}}=(\sum_{j=0}^{2k+1}m)\,\text{mod}\,2,\quad k=1,2,\dots,|x|/2-1.

(116)

To derive this expression, we used

[(m_{1}-m_{0})+(m_{3}-m_{2})+\dots+(m_{2k+1}-m_{2k})]\,\text{mod}\,2=(\sum_{j=0}^{2k+1}m_{j})\,\text{mod}\,2.

(117)

We note that these
p
operators are already ordered within
\Gamma_{1}
, so to include them, we simply update
z
prepared above by

z\leftarrow z+z^{(P)}.

(118)

Finally, to complete Step 2, we need to show how to update
(\omega,E,F,G)
to represent
U_{C}^{\prime}=U_{C}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}
, where
\Gamma_{1}
is a product of
p_{k}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is an arbitrary Hermitian Majorana operator. This is given in Appendix B.

Step 3

Let us now assume that we have

|\psi\rangle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}U_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle),

(119)

in the form of stabilizer tableau representation for
U_{C}
, binary arrays
b
(for
U_{B}
) and
s
, integer
\theta\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, and integer index
w
.
We have two cases:

Case 1.

\theta=2k+1
(
k=0,1
) is odd.
Then,

\displaystyle B_{w}^{b_{w}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{w}^{b_{w}+1}(c_{w}\tilde{c}_{w+1})^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}|s\rangle

(120)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{w}^{b_{w}+1}|s\rangle,\quad s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k+b_{w}=1\\
B_{w}^{b_{w}+1}i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle,\quad s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k+b_{w}=0\end{cases},

(121)

Therefore, we only need to update
b\leftarrow b+e_{w}
and, if
s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k+b_{w}=1
, update phase
\phi\leftarrow\phi+2\theta
and bitstring
s\leftarrow s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}
.

Case 2.

\theta=2k
(
k=0,1
) is even.

We first show that

B_{w}^{b_{w}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-\Gamma)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta+1}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle),

(122)

where
\Gamma=(-1)^{\pi(s)-\bar{s}_{w}}\prod_{j=w+1}^{n-1}p_{j}
.
Using

\displaystyle B_{w}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)

(123)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(|s\rangle-i(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle-i^{\theta+1}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}}|s\rangle)

(124)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-i(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k})(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1})

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)

(126)

for
\theta=2k
,
k=0,1
, and

e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-\Gamma)}|s\rangle=|s\rangle,e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-\Gamma)}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle=i|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle,

(127)

we have

\displaystyle B_{w}^{b_{w}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)

(128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)

(129)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-\Gamma)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta+1}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle).

(130)

Since
\Gamma
commutes with all
B_{j\neq w}
, we can write

\displaystyle U_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle)

(131)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}}U_{C}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-\Gamma)}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|s\rangle+i^{\theta+1}|s+e_{w}+e_{w+1}\rangle),

(132)

which after appropriate updates brings us back to the original problem of Step 3 but with odd
\theta\leftarrow\theta+1
(
Case 1
).
To ensure that we are updating
U_{C}
with a control-type gate, we rewrite

e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}}U_{C}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(1-\Gamma)}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}[b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}+(1-(-1)^{\pi(s)-\bar{s}_{w}})]}U_{C}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}((-1)^{\pi(s)-\bar{s}_{w}}-\Gamma)},

(133)

knowing that

e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}((-1)^{\pi(s)-\bar{s}_{w}}-\Gamma)}|0\rangle=|0\rangle.

(134)

Therefore, the updates are

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle\phi-[b_{w}(-1)^{s_{w}+s_{w+1}+k}+(1-(-1)^{\pi(s)-\bar{s}_{w}})]

(135)

\displaystyle U_{C}

\displaystyle\leftarrow

\displaystyle U_{C}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}((-1)^{\pi(s)-\bar{s}_{w}}-\Gamma)},

(136)

while the explicit update rules for the latter (parameters
(\omega,E,F,G)
) can be found in Appendix B.

A.4

It is easy to generalize the above results to general Majorana Clifford rotations of the form

(137)

where
\Gamma
is a Hermitian Majorana operator. We follow Step 1 of the previous Section to write

U|\psi\rangle=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\phi}U_{C}U_{B}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\Gamma^{\prime})|s\rangle,

(138)

where
\Gamma^{\prime}=\pm iU_{B}^{\dagger}U_{C}^{\dagger}\Gamma U_{C}U_{B}
. Now, Eq. (
138
) is of the form of Eq. (
86
) and the remaining steps are exactly the same. The overall scaling is the same as for
\eta_{jk}
,
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
.

Appendix B
Stabilizer tableau procedures

B.1
Matrix identities for
E
,
F
, and
G

Here we derive identities (
39
) and (
40
) satisfied by matrices
E
,
F
, and
G
. We note that
p_{i}
and
c_{j}
anticommute for
i=j
and commute otherwise, and this relation remains true after transforming with
U_{C}
. Therefore, using Eq. (
20
), we can show that

E_{i}\cdot G_{j}=\delta_{ij},

(139)

which can be written in matrix form as

E\cdot G^{T}=I.

(140)

By using the fact that these matrices are full-rank square matrices and thus invertible, we can augment this with additional identities

E\cdot G^{T}=G\cdot E^{T}=E^{T}\cdot G=G^{T}\cdot E=I.

(141)

Similarly, using Eq. (
20
) and the fact that
c_{i}
and
c_{j}
anticommute except if
i=j
, we can write

F_{i}\cdot G_{j}+G_{i}\cdot(F_{j}+G_{j})+1=\delta_{ij}+1,

(142)

where the 1 on the left-hand side comes from the fact that both Majorana operators have odd parity and the 1 on the right-hand side ensures that the right-hand side is 1 unless
i=j
. This can again be formulated as a matrix equation:

F\cdot G^{T}+G\cdot F^{T}=I+G\cdot G^{T}.

(143)

Using (
141
), we also have

E^{T}\cdot F+F^{T}\cdot E=I+E\cdot E^{T}.

(144)

B.2
Update rules for applying
\exp(\pm i\frac{\pi}{4}(I-\Gamma_{p}))
from the right

Let us assume we are given a p-type Majorana operator

\Gamma_{p}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}}

(145)

and
U_{C}
defined by
(\omega,E,F,G)
. We wish to compute a set of parameters
(\omega^{\prime},E^{\prime},F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
that represents

U_{C}^{\prime}=U_{C}e^{\pm i\frac{\pi}{4}(I-\Gamma_{p})}.

(146)

Performing the update will not modify
E
because
[\Gamma_{p},p_{j}]=0
. For the remaining parameters, we compute

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(-1)^{\vartheta}(1-\Gamma)}U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{j}U_{C}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(-1)^{\vartheta}(1-\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(-1)^{\vartheta}\Gamma}i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}}e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(-1)^{\vartheta}\Gamma}

(147)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(-1)^{\vartheta}(G_{j}\cdot z)\Gamma}i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}}

(148)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[-i(-1)^{\vartheta}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{z_{k}}\right]^{G_{j}\cdot z}i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}}

(149)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega_{j}-(-1)^{\vartheta}G_{j}\cdot z}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}+z_{k}(G_{j}\cdot z)}c_{k}^{G_{jk}}.

(150)

The update rules are

\omega_{j}\leftarrow\omega_{j}-(-1)^{\vartheta}G_{j}\cdot z,\quad F_{j}\leftarrow F_{j}+z(G_{j}\cdot z).

(151)

B.3
Update rules for applying general control gate from the right

Here, we are given a p-type Majorana operator
\Gamma_{1}
, a Hermitian, even-parity Majorana operator
\Gamma_{2}
that commutes with
\Gamma_{1}
, and
U_{C}
defined by
(\omega,E,F,G)
. We wish to compute
(\omega^{\prime},E^{\prime},F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
that represent

U_{C}^{\prime}=U_{C}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}},

(152)

where

C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(I-\Gamma_{1})(I-\Gamma_{2})}

(153)

is a multi-site control gate that multiplies a computational basis state
|t\rangle
by
\Gamma_{2}
if
\Gamma_{1}|t\rangle=-|t\rangle
.

We begin by deriving the following useful expression

\displaystyle C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{\dagger}\Gamma C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(I-\Gamma_{1})(I-\Gamma_{2})}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(I-(-1)^{\alpha_{1}}\Gamma_{1})(I-(-1)^{\alpha_{2}}\Gamma_{2})}\Gamma

(154)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}[((-1)^{\alpha_{1}}-1)\Gamma_{1}+((-1)^{\alpha_{2}}-1]\Gamma_{2}+(1-(-1)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}})\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2}]}\Gamma

(155)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-i\Gamma_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}(-i\Gamma_{2})^{\alpha_{2}}(i\Gamma_{1}\Gamma_{2})^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\Gamma

(156)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\Gamma_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}\Gamma_{2}^{\alpha_{1}}\Gamma,

(157)

where
\alpha_{i}
is 1 if
[\Gamma_{i},\Gamma]\neq 0
and 0 otherwise.

Then, to update
E
, we have

U_{C}^{\prime\dagger}p_{j}U_{C}=C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{\dagger}U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{j}U_{C}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}=C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{\dagger}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{j,k}}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}.

(158)

Let
P_{j}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{j,k}}
. We know that
\alpha_{j,1}=0
because
[\Gamma_{1},P_{j}]=0
and
\alpha_{j,2}=E_{j}\cdot x_{2}
[using Eq. (
20
)]. Therefore, following (
157
),

C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{\dagger}P_{j}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}=\Gamma_{1}^{E_{j}\cdot x_{2}}P_{j}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{j,k}+z_{1,k}(E_{j}\cdot x_{2})}

(159)

and the update rule is

E_{j}\leftarrow E_{j}+z_{1}(E_{j}\cdot x_{2}),\quad j=0,1,\dots,n-1.

(160)

To update
\omega,F,G
, we set

\Gamma^{(j)}=i^{\omega_{j}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}}

(161)

and compute
\alpha_{j,1}
and
\alpha_{j,2}
using Eq. (
20
). Then, by applying (
157
) and the formula for the product of Majorana operators [Eq. (
18
)], we obtain

\Gamma^{(j)\prime}=C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}^{\dagger}\Gamma^{(j)}C_{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}}

(162)

whose parameters are the new
(\omega_{j},F_{j},G_{j})
.

B.4
Stabilizer tableau for
U_{C}^{\dagger}

Let us assume we are given
U_{C}
in the form of matrices
E
,
F
, and
G
, and an array
\omega
. Our goal is to find
(\omega^{\prime},E^{\prime},F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
such that

U_{C}p_{j}U_{C}^{\dagger}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{jk}^{\prime}},\quad U_{C}c_{j}U_{C}^{\dagger}=i^{\omega_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}^{\prime}},

(163)

i.e., the representation of
U_{C}^{\dagger}
.

To derive the expressions for
(\omega^{\prime},E^{\prime},F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
, we expand

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}U_{C}^{\dagger}p_{i}U_{C}U_{C}^{\dagger}

(164)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}^{E_{ij}}U_{C}^{\dagger}

(165)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E_{jk}^{\prime}}\right)^{E_{ij}}

(166)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}E_{ij}E_{jk}^{\prime}},

(167)

which leads to

E\cdot E^{\prime}=I,

(168)

i.e.,
E^{\prime}
is matrix inverse of
E
. From Eq. (
141
) we know that this is equal to
G^{T}
, i.e.,

E^{\prime}=G^{T}.

(169)

We now repeat the same procedure for an arbitrary
c_{i}
:

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}U_{C}^{\dagger}c_{i}U_{C}U_{C}^{\dagger}

(170)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{C}i^{\omega_{i}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}^{F_{ij}}c_{j}^{G_{ij}}U_{C}^{\dagger}

(171)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega_{i}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{E^{\prime}_{jk}}\right)^{F_{ij}}\left(i^{\omega_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{F_{jk}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{G_{jk}^{\prime}}\right)^{G_{ij}}

(172)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{\omega}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}p_{k}^{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F_{ij}E^{\prime}_{jk}+G_{ij}F_{jk}^{\prime}}c_{k}^{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}G_{ij}G_{jk}^{\prime}},

(173)

where in the last line we chose to store all phases and sign changes due to anticommutation into a new variable
\omega
. We can now read off

G\cdot G^{\prime}=I,\quad F\cdot E^{\prime}+G\cdot F^{\prime}=0.

(174)

From this, and using identities derived earlier, we can obtain

\displaystyle G^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{-1}=E^{T},

(175)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{-1}\cdot F\cdot E^{\prime}=E^{T}\cdot F\cdot G^{T}.

(176)

Finally, to recover
\omega^{\prime}
, we note that

c_{i}=U_{C}^{\dagger}U_{C}c_{i}U_{C}^{\dagger}U_{C}=i^{\omega_{i}^{\prime}}\Gamma_{i},

(177)

where, by construction, we know that

\Gamma_{i}\propto c_{i}.

(178)

We can therefore initially set
\omega_{i}^{\prime}=0
and
(E^{\prime},F^{\prime},G^{\prime})
as derived above, compute
\Gamma_{i}
using this definition of
U_{C}^{\dagger}
, and then extract
\omega_{i}^{\prime}
as the negative of the phase factor of the evaluated
\Gamma_{i}
.

The overall cost of computing the representation of
U_{C}^{\dagger}
is
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
due to matrix multiplications and final evaluation of
\omega^{\prime}
array.

B.5
Action on a computational basis state

We now wish to evaluate
\theta
and
t
in

U_{C}^{\dagger}|s\rangle=i^{\theta}|t\rangle,

(179)

where
U_{C}
is defined by its stabilizer tableux and
s
is a bitstring defining a computational basis state. We use

\displaystyle U_{C}^{\dagger}|s\rangle=U_{C}^{\dagger}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_{i}^{s_{i}}|0\rangle=U_{C}^{\dagger}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_{i}^{s_{i}}U_{C}U_{C}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=\Gamma|0\rangle,

(180)

where
\Gamma=U_{C}^{\dagger}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}c_{i}^{s_{i}}U_{C}
can be evaluated using the stabilizer tableau in
\mathcal{O}(|s|n)=\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
time and
\Gamma|0\rangle
is given in Eq. (
14
).

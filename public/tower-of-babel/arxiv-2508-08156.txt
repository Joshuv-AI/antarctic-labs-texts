Existence of Anisotropic Minkowski Content
Filip Fryš

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the existence of anisotropic Minkowski content and anisotropic outer Minkowski content. Our result is that the Minkowski content of the topological boundary of a given set of finite perimeter E E coincides with the perimeter of E E if and only if the anisotropic Minkowski content of the topological boundary of E E coincides with half of the sum of the anisotropic perimeter of E E and the anisotropic perimeter of the complement of E E . As a consequence, we find that the existence of anisotropic outer Minkowski content of a given set of finite perimeter and its complement ensures the existence of outer Minkowski content of the set and its complement.



Introduction

Let
\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. We define the
Minkowski content of
S
as

\mathcal{M}(S;\mathbb{R}^{n})\coloneq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\dist(x,S)<\varepsilon\}\big)}{2\varepsilon},

if the limit exists, where
\lambda^{n}
stands for the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
and
\dist(\cdot,S)
for the
distance function to the set
S
. It turns out that the numerator of the fraction in the definition of
\mathcal{M}(S;\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be replaced by the measure of the
Minkowski sum

S\oplus B(0,\varepsilon)\coloneq\bigcup_{s\in S}B(s,\varepsilon),

and so

\mathcal{M}(S;\mathbb{R}^{n})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(S\oplus B(0,\varepsilon)\big)}{2\varepsilon},

if the limit on the right hand side exists.
If the set
S
is the boundary of a compact, convex and nonempty set, i.e. the boundary of a
convex body
, then the limit exists and coincides with the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure (see
[
Sch
, Chapter 5]
).

A natural question that arises is whether the limit, if it exits, coincides with
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure for more general sets. Indeed, if the set
S
is compact and

(n-1)
-rectifiable (i.e. the image of a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
under a Lipschitz map), we find that the limit
\mathcal{M}(S;\mathbb{R}^{n})
exists and coincides with the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
[
Fe
, p. 275]
, or, more generally, if the set
S
is compact, countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable (see Definition
1.1
) and there exists a probability Radon measure
\eta
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
and a positive constant
\gamma
such that for any
x\in S
and any
r\in(0,1)
we have

\eta\big(B(x,r)\big)\geq\gamma r^{n-1}.

Its proof can be found in
[
AFP
, Theorem 2.104]
.

It is also possible to restrict ourselves to an open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define the
Minkowski content of
S
in
\Omega
by

(1)

\mathcal{M}(S;\Omega)\coloneq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((S\cap\Omega)\oplus B(0,\varepsilon)\big)\cap\Omega\Big)}{2\varepsilon},

if the limit exists.

One could ask whether we can reasonably replace the set
B(0,1)
in (
1
). This idea leads to the notion of anisotropic Minkowski content: Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body the interior of which contains the origin. We define the
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of
S
by

\mathcal{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)\coloneq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((S\cap\Omega)\oplus B(0,\varepsilon)\big)\cap\Omega\Big)}{2\varepsilon}

if the limit exists, where

(S\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon C\coloneq\{s+\varepsilon c;\;(s,c)\in(S\cap\Omega)\times C\}.

In the
C
-anisotropic case, we will compare the
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of the topological boundary of a set of finite perimeter
E
in an open set
\Omega
with the value

\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1},

where
\nu_{E}(x)
is the so-called
reduced boundary of
E
and
\nu_{E}(x)
is the
outer unit normal to
E
at
x\in\partial^{*}E
(see Definition
1.4
in this text) and
h_{C}
is the
support function of
C
given by

h_{C}(x)\coloneq\sup_{y\in C}x\cdot y\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

See
[
LV
, Theorem 3.1]
for an analogue of
[
AFP
, Theorem 2.104]
for
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content.

In this work, however, we are mainly interested in the role of
C
: We will show in Theorem
3.1
that the Minkowski content of the topological boundary of a given set of finite perimeter exists and coincides with the perimeter of
E
, i.e.

\mathcal{M}(\partial E;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E)

if and only if the
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of the topological boundary of a given set of finite perimeter exists and coincides with the arithmetic mean of the
C
-anisotropic perimeter of
E
and the
C
-anisotropic perimeter of the complement of
E
, i.e.

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}.

Its proof of this statement is based on the Besicovitch covering theorem and
[
LV
, Lemma 3.3]
.

The authors of
[
CLL
]
introduced the
C
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of a given set
E
of finite perimeter in an open set
\Omega
as

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((E\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon C)\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\Big),

if the limit exists.

Its isotropic version, i.e. for
C
being the unit Euclidean ball, was introduced in
[
ACV
]
.

For a convex set
K
, the value of
\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincides with the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the boundary of
K
and
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(K;\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincides with

\int\limits_{\partial K}h_{C}(\nu_{K})\difm^{n-1},

see again
[
Sch
, Chapter 5]
. In the case of anisotropic
outer
Minkowski content of
E
in
\Omega
, we will compare its value with

\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}h_{C}(\nu_{E})\difm^{n-1}.

Our result—Theorem
3.1
—is an analogue of the following theorem
[
CLL
, Theorem 3.4]
:

Theorem

(
[
CLL
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
E\subseteq\Omega
be a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
and
C
be a convex body the interior of which contains the origin. If

\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E),

then

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}h_{C}(\nu_{E})\difm^{n-1}.

The authors of
[
CLL
]
assert without proof that the converse should also be true: If

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}h_{C}(\nu_{E})\difm^{n-1}

for a sufficiently nice
C
, then

\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E).

In
[
CLL
]
, there is a short comment on how to prove this assertion. Unfortunately, however, it seems that it is not as straightforward as the authors write in
[
CLL
, Remark 3.5]
.

We will use our Theorem
3.1
to show a weaker result, namely that if the closure of
E
is contained in
\Omega
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}h_{C}(\nu_{E})\difm^{n-1}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\difm^{n-1},

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.

In the first section—
1.
Preliminaries
—we present the notation that will be used, collect the preliminaries on geometric measure theory and anisotropic (outer) Minkowski content, and lay out all the tools we need in the following sections, particularly
sets of finite perimeter
,
the Besicovitch covering theorem
,
anisotropic perimeter
,
anisotropic (outer) Minkowski content
and their properties.

In the second section—
2.
Basic Properties of Anisotropic (Outer) Minkowski Content
—we take a closer look at necessary conditions for the existence of anisotropic (outer) Minkowski content and study the relation between anisotropic Minkowski content and anisotropic outer Minkowski content.

In the last section—
3.
Main Result
—we give a proof of Theorem
3.1
which tells us that the anisotropic Minkowski content of the topological boundary of a given set of finite perimeter exists if and only if the isotropic Minkowski content of the topological boundary of that set exists. Furthermore, we study the relation between the anisotropic Minkowski content of the reduced boundary of a given set of finite perimeter and the anisotropic outer Minkowski content of the set of points which have Lebesgue density one with respect to that set (Proposition
3.6
).

.

1.2.
Geometric Measure Theory

In this subsection, we formulate some useful results from geometric measure theory.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
k\leq n
and
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable. The set
S
is said to be
countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable
if there exists a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
of Lipschitz functions from
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{k}\left(E\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}f_{i}\left(\mathbb{R}^{k}\right)\right)=0.

Remark 1.2
.

Given a countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S)<\infty
, there exists a countable family of pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
S
that covers
S
up to an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible set and each element of which is a subset of
(n-1)
-Lipschitz graph with finite
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
measure (see
[
Si
, Lemma 11.1 and Remark 11.7]
).

Let us now define the notion of a
set of finite perimeter
. Sets of finite perimeter will play a crucial role in the following two sections.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
E\subseteq\Omega
be measurable. The set
E
is said to be a
set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
if the derivative of
\chi_{E}
in the sense of distributions, denoted by
D\chi_{E},
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure with finite total variation in
\Omega
, i.e. it is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure with finite total variation in
\Omega
satisfying

\int\limits_{E}\nabla f\dif^{n}=-\int\limits_{\Omega}f\textup{ d}D\chi_{E}\quad\text{for all $f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)$}.

The
perimeter of E in
\Omega
is given by

\Per(E;\Omega)\coloneq\|D\chi_{E}\|(\Omega),

i.e. the
total variation of
D\chi_{E}
in
\Omega.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We define the
upper
and
lower Lebesgue density of E at x
as

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(E,x)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big|_{E}\big(B(x,r)\big)}{\lambda^{n}\big(B(x,r)\big)}

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(E,x)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big|_{E}\big(B(x,r)\big)}{\lambda^{n}\big(B(x,r)\big)}.

Further, let
\Theta_{n}(E,x)
be their common value (i.e.
\Theta_{n}(E,x)\coloneq\Theta_{n}(E,x)_{*}
provided that
\Theta_{n}(E,x)_{*}=\Theta_{n}(E,x)^{*}
) which is referred to as the
Lebesgue density of E at x
.

Denote

E^{t}\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\Theta_{n}(E,x)=t\}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,1]$}.

The
essential boundary of E
is given by

\partial_{*}E\coloneq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left(E^{0}\cup E^{1}\right).

Let us also introduce the
reduced boundary of E
defined by

\partial^{*}E\coloneq\left\{x\in E^{1/2};\;\exists\nu_{E}(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\;\frac{E-x}{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;y\cdot\nu_{E}(x)\leq 0\}\right\},

where we consider the
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-convergence of sets. Moreover, if
x\in\partial^{*}E,
we call
\nu_{E}(x)
the
outer unit normal to E at x.

Let us state the following significant theorem
[
AFP
, Theorem 3.59]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. Then
\partial^{*}E
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable,
\|D\chi_{E}\|=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big|_{\partial^{*}E}
and for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big|_{\partial^{*}E}
-a.a.
x\in\partial^{*}E
it holds that

1=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big(\partial^{*}E\cap B(x,\varepsilon)\big)}{\omega_{n-1}\varepsilon^{n-1}}.

Remark 1.6
.

Let us state the following facts (see
[
AFP
, section 3.3]
).

•

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. Observe that if
x\in\textup{int}(E)
or
x\in\ext(E)
, then we have
\Theta_{n}(E,x)=0
or
\Theta_{n}(E,x)=1
respectively. Thus
\partial^{*}E\subseteq\partial_{*}E\subseteq\partial E.
Since the definition of
\partial^{*}E
does not depend on the particular representative of
\chi_{E}
, we get

\overline{\partial^{*}E}\subseteq\bigcap_{\{E^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\lambda^{n}(E\Delta E^{\prime})=0\}}\partial E^{\prime}.

According to
[
Ma
, Proposition 12.19 and Remark 15.3]
, there exists a Borel set

such that
\lambda^{n}(E\Delta E^{\prime})=0
with
\partial E^{\prime}=\overline{\partial^{*}E}
. From the proof of
[

, Proposition 12.19]
, it is clear that the set

works.

•

For a set
E
of finite perimeter in

, we always have

\Per(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\Omega\cap\partial_{*}E\right)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E\right).

•

We have
\Per(E;\Omega)=\Per(F;\Omega)
whenever
\lambda^{n}\big((E\Delta F)\cap\Omega\big)=0.

•

If
E
is a set of finite perimeter in

then

is also a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
with
\nu_{E}=-\nu_{\Omega\setminus E}

\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\Big|_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}\right)
-a.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\Omega\cap\partial^{*}\left(\Omega\setminus E\right)\right)
and
\textup{Per}(E;\Omega)=\textup{Per}\big(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega\big).

Let us now recall the Besicovitch covering theorem (see
[
EG
, subsection 1.5.2, Theorem 2]
):

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

and

at most countable such that

1.3.
Anisotropic (Outer) Minkowski Content

In this subsection, we will summarize the basic facts about anisotropic (outer) Minkowski content from
[
CLL
]
and
[
LV
]
.

Definition 1.8
.

A
convex body
is a non-empty, compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The system of all convex bodies the interior of which contains the origin will be denoted by the symbol
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. The
support function of
C
is given by the formula

h_{C}(y)\coloneq\sup_{x\in C}x\cdot y\quad\text{for $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

We also introduce the
polar function of C
given by

h^{\circ}_{C}(x)\coloneq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
h_{C}(y)\leq 1\end{subarray}}x\cdot y\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

For
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
E\neq\emptyset
, we define the
C
-anisotropic distance function to the set
E
by

\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)\coloneq\underset{y\in E}{\textup{inf}}\textit{ h}^{\circ}_{C}(x-y)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

Remark 1.9
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

•

We define the
polar of
C
by
C^{\circ}\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;h_{C}(x)\leq 1\}
. We see that
h^{\circ}_{C}=h_{C^{\circ}}
and
(C^{\circ})^{\circ}=C
.

•

The function
x\mapsto h_{C}(x)
is sublinear, hence convex, and Lipschitz with the property
h_{-C}(x)=h_{C}(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is also true that
h_{aC}=ah_{C}
for any
a>0
.

•

If
aC\subseteq B(0,1)\subseteq bC
for some
a,b>0,
then for any
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(2)

ah_{C}(\nu)\leq|\nu|\leq bh_{C}(\nu).

Definition 1.10
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be with
0\in Q
,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
\varepsilon>0
. We define the
(\varepsilon,Q)
-
anisotropic outer Minkowski content (in
\Omega
)
as a functional defined on
\lambda^{n}
-measurable subsets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the formula

\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,Q}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((E\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon Q\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\Big).

Further, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,Q}(E;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,Q}(E;\Omega).

The former formula defines the
lower
Q
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of
E
(in
\Omega
)
, the latter the
upper
Q
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of
E
(in
\Omega
)
. If
\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)_{*}=\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)^{*}
, we define the
Q
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content (in
\Omega
)
by the formula
\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)_{*}
. If we write
\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)
, we implicitly mean that the limit exists.

The case
Q=B(0,1)
is significant. We have a sufficient condition for the existence of the limit
\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
[
Vi
, Theorem 3.1]
):

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\partial E
is a countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable and bounded set and such that there exists
\gamma>0
and a probability measure
\eta
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
satisfying

\forall r>0\;\forall x\in\partial E:\eta\big(B(x,r)\big)\geq\gamma r^{n-1}.

Then we have
\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})=\textup{Per}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})+2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big((\partial E)\cap E^{0}\big).

This result can be generalized for general convex bodies
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
(see
[
LV
, Theorem 4.3]
).

In this work, we are mainly interested in the case when
C
is a convex body of full dimension with
0\in\textup{int}(C)
and
E
is a set of finite perimeter. There exists an example of a set of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for which the
B(0,1)
-anisotropic (i.e.
isotropic
) outer Minkowski content does not exist, but the outer anisotropic Minkowski content exists for any choice of a two-dimensional disc (
[
KR
, Ex. 3]
).

It is worth pointing out that if we change
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
on a set of Lebesgue measure zero, it might change the resulting (anisotropic) outer Minkowski content. A simple example is to consider
E\coloneq\mathbb{Q}
and
\Omega\coloneq\mathbb{R}
. In
[
CLL
]
, there is a definition of a functional that coincides with
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\cdot,\Omega)
on sets with
E=E^{1}
. However, in this work, we will use the standard definition of
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\cdot,\Omega)
.

Let us define the so-called
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content, which is useful for
\lambda^{n}
-negligible sets (unlike
\mathcal{SM}_{C}
):

Definition 1.12
.

Let

be measurable and

with
0\in Q
. Putting

we define the
(\varepsilon,Q)
-anisotropic Minkowski content of
S
in
\Omega
.

in

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,Q}(S;\Omega)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,Q}(S;\Omega).

\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)_{*}=\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)^{*}

-anisotropic Minkowski content of

by

For simplicity’s sake, we will call

the

instead of the

in
\Omega
and similarly

the
anisotropic Minkowski content of
E
instead of the

in
\Omega
. Let us point out that if we have

is omitted (or replaced by

) and we will write

instead of
\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,B(0,1)}
and

\mathcal{SM}_{B(0,1)}
and

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,B(0,1)}

instead of

. The meaning of the word

is “having the same properties in all directions”, while the word

is its opposite, i.e. “not having the same properties in all directions” (see

.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,

. We say that the

-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of

in

By pure analogy, we say that the

Remark 1.15
.

Suppose that
E
is a set of finite perimeter in an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
and
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable, then:

•

The
C
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of
E
in
\Omega
is sometimes called one-sided. The intuition behind is the following: we enlarge the set
E
only
outwards
, in the exterior, while
\partial E
is enlarged in both directions, both
inwards
and
outwards
.

•

The classical perimeter
\Per(E;\Omega)
equals
\textup{Per}_{h_{B(0,1)}}(E;\Omega).

•

If we have
aC\subseteq C^{\prime}\subseteq bC
for some positive constants
a
and
b,
then

a\,\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)\leq\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)\leq b\,\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)\quad\text{(see (\ref{hurich}))},

a\,\mathcal{M}_{a\varepsilon,C}(S;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C^{\prime}}(S;\Omega)\leq b\,\mathcal{M}_{b\varepsilon,C}(S;\Omega)\quad\text{for any $\varepsilon>0$,}

a\,\mathcal{SM}_{a\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)\leq b\,\mathcal{SM}_{b\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\quad\text{for any $\varepsilon>0$.}

We see that these three functionals behave very similarly. Observe also that the equality
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=0
enforces
\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)=0
, and by pure analogy,
\mathcal{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)=0
enforces
\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}(S;\Omega)=0
.

•

For
\varepsilon>0
we have (see discussion on page 5 in
[
CLL
]
)

(3)

\min_{\{F\subseteq\Omega;\;\lambda^{n}(E\Delta F)=0\}}\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(F;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E^{1};\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(\Omega\setminus E^{0};\Omega).

•

Let
\varepsilon>0
. It is easy to see that the functionals
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\Omega)
and
\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\Omega)
coincide on
\lambda^{n}
-negligible sets.

•

We have
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(S;\Omega)\geq\lambda^{n}(S\cap\Omega)(2\varepsilon)^{-1}
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and therefore if we have
\mathcal{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)^{*}<\infty
, then
\lambda^{n}(S\cap\Omega)=0
.

2.
Basic Properties of Anisotropic (Outer) Minkowski Content

In this section, we will summarize basic properties of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\cdot;\Omega)
and
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\cdot;\Omega)
and necessary conditions for their existence.

Let us start with the following lemma, which is crucial for the proof of our main result. It can be found in
[
LV
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
S
is a compact set contained in a Lipschitz
(n-1)
-graph, then
\mathcal{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)
aligns with

\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{S}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{S})+h_{C}(-\nu_{S})\big)\difm^{n-1}\quad\text{for any open set $\Omega$ with $S\subseteq\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

Proposition 2.2

(Lower bounds)
.

Let
E
be a set of finite perimeter in an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. Then

(4)

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)_{*}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\geq\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)

and

(5)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)_{*}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\partial E;\Omega)&\geq\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)_{*}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big).\end{split}

Proof.

The first part follows for example from
[
CLL
, Theorem 3.1]
.

The second part can be proven as follows. Since
\partial^{*}E\subseteq\partial E
, we have

(6)

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)_{*}\geq\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)_{*}.

Further, the set
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable with finite
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measure, and so we can find a sequence
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
which covers
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
up to an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible set and each element of which is a subset of a graph of a real-valued Lipschitz function (see Remark
1.2
).

Pick an arbitrary natural number
L\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{L}
is a finite sequence of disjoint compact sets contained in
\Omega
, we have from Lemma
2.1

(7)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{S_{k}}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}&=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\mathcal{M}_{C}(S_{k};\Omega)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\big((S_{k}\oplus r{C})\cap\Omega\big)\\
&=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\Big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{L}S_{k}\oplus r{C}\Big)\cap\Omega\bigg)\\
&\leq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\Big(\big((\partial^{*}E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus r{C}\Big)\cap\Omega\bigg)\\
&=\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)_{*}.\end{split}

Since
L
was arbitrary, we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)&=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{S_{k}}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}\\
&\leq\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)_{*},\end{split}

which coupled with (
6
) gives us the conclusion.
∎

Let us state the following useful lemma (cf.
[
ACV
, Lemma 1]
):

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{a_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
\{b_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be two families of non-negative reals. If there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}(a_{\varepsilon}+b_{\varepsilon})\leq a+b,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}a_{\varepsilon}\geq a

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}b_{\varepsilon}\geq b,

then
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}a_{\varepsilon}=a
and
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}b_{\varepsilon}=b
.

Let us define slightly different functionals than
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\Omega)
. We will see that they coincide in some cases (and in some sense) with
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\Omega)
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
CLL
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. Put

\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)+\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)\quad\text{for $\varepsilon>0$ and $E\subseteq\Omega$ measurable.}

Further, set

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega).

If
\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)^{*}=\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)_{*}
, define

\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)^{*}.

We say that
\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)

exists
provided that

\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big).

In the case
C=B(0,1)
, the subscript
C
in
\mathscr{M}_{C}(\cdot;\Omega)
will be omitted.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. Put

\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(S;\Omega)\coloneq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big((S\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap\Omega\big)\quad\text{for $\varepsilon>0$ and $S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ measurable.}

For
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
measurable, set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(S;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega).

If
\mathfrak{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)^{*}=\mathfrak{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)_{*}
, define

\mathfrak{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)\coloneq\mathfrak{M}_{C}(S;\Omega)^{*}.

Let
E\subseteq\Omega
be a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
. We say that
\mathfrak{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)
(or
\mathfrak{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega))
exists
provided that it coincides with

\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big).

In the case
C=B(0,1)
, the subscript
C
in
\mathfrak{M}_{C}(\cdot;\Omega)
will be omitted.

Let us discuss the relation between
\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}
. The next proposition is inspired by
[
KR
, proof of Proposition 9]
. We will prove rather easier inequalities between the aforementioned functionals.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. We have

\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\partial E;\Omega)\geq\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\geq\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\partial E;\Omega)\quad\text{for any $\varepsilon>0$ and $E\subseteq\Omega$ measurable.}

If
\overline{E}\subseteq\Omega
, the three quantities coincide. In particular, if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have for any
\varepsilon>0

\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{n})\circ\partial=\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{n})\circ\partial\quad\text{on measurable subsets of $\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

Moreover,

\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)\text{ exists}\iff\text{both $\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)$ and $\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)$ exist.}

In particular, if
\overline{E}\subseteq\Omega
, it holds that

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)\text{ exists}\iff\text{both $\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)$ and $\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)$ exist.}

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
E\subseteq\Omega
be measurable. Observe first that

(8)

\displaystyle\big((E\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\big)\cup\Big(\big((\Omega\setminus E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap E\Big)\subseteq\big((\partial E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap\Omega,

because if
x\in(E\oplus\varepsilon C)\setminus E
, then
x\in\Omega\setminus E
and
(x-\varepsilon C)\cap E\neq\emptyset
. The convexity of
C
ensures that
(x-\varepsilon C)\cap\partial E\neq\emptyset
, whence
x\in\big((\partial E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap\Omega
. Similarly, if
x\in\big((\Omega\setminus E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap E
, then
x\in\big((\partial E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap\Omega
. These two observations clearly imply (
8
).

If
x\in\Big(\big((\partial E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon\inter(C)\Big)\cap\Omega
, then there is some
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that
x\in\big((\partial E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon_{0}C
. In particular, it holds that
x\in(\overline{E}\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon_{0}C
and
x\in\big(\overline{\Omega\setminus E}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon_{0}C
. If
x\in\Omega\setminus E
, we have

x\in\big((\overline{E}\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon_{0}C\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\subseteq\big(E\oplus(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{0})C\oplus\varepsilon_{0}C\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\subseteq(E\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)

since
\overline{E}=\bigcap_{r>0}E\oplus rC
. By pure analogy, if
x\in E
, we have

x\in\Big(\big(\overline{\Omega\setminus E}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon_{0}C\Big)\cap E\subseteq\big((\Omega\setminus E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap E.

We see that

(9)

\Big(\big((\partial E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon\inter(C)\Big)\cap\Omega\subseteq\big((E\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\big)\cup\Big(\big((\Omega\setminus E)\oplus\varepsilon C\big)\cap E\Big).

Using (
8
), (
9
) and (
10
), we obtain precisely

\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\geq\mathscr{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\geq\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\partial E;\Omega).

Clearly, if
\overline{E}\subseteq\Omega
, we get equalities.
The rest follows from Lemma
2.3
and
[
ACV
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Proposition
2.6
gives us the following chain of implications:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)\text{ exists}\implies\mathscr{M}_{C}(E;\Omega)\text{ exists}\implies\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)\text{ exists}

\displaystyle\Updownarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)\And\mathcal{SM}_{C}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\text{ exist}

We see that the functional
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\cdot;\Omega)
is the most likely to exist among the other functionals.

Let us give an easy example of a set
E\subseteq\Omega
for which
\mathfrak{M}(\partial E;\Omega)>\mathscr{M}(E;\Omega)>\mathcal{M}(\partial E;\Omega).

Example 2.8
.

Put

\displaystyle\Omega\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\;|x|\in[0,1)\cup(1,2)\}

and

\displaystyle E\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\;|x|\in(1,2)\}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(\partial E;\Omega)=4\pi,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}(E;\Omega)=2\pi

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\partial E;\Omega)=0.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

•

Given a
\lambda^{n}
-measurable subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we always have the equality (see
[
KR
, eq. (10)]
)

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}(E\oplus C)=\lambda^{n}\big(E\oplus\inter(C)\big),

from which it follows

(10)

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\big((E\oplus C)\cap\Omega\big)=\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(E\oplus\inter(C)\big)\cap\Omega\Big)\quad\text{for any $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$ measurable.}

•

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty and measurable, and
\varepsilon>0
. For any
x\in\Omega
, we have
\varepsilon>\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)=\inf_{y\in E}h_{C}^{\circ}(x-y)
if and only if the exists some
y\in E
such that
\varepsilon>h_{C}^{\circ}(x-y)
, which is equivalent to the existence of
y\in E
such that
(x-y)\in\varepsilon\inter(C)
, and this happens if and only if
x\in\big(E\oplus\varepsilon\inter(C)\big)\cap\Omega.
And so

\big(E\oplus\varepsilon\inter(C)\big)\cap\Omega=\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon\},

whence

\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(E\oplus\varepsilon\inter(C)\big)\cap\Omega\Big)=\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon\}\big).

Using (
10
) and the fact that multiplication by
\varepsilon
is a homeomorphism, we deduce

(11)

\lambda^{n}\big((E\oplus\varepsilon C)\big)\cap\Omega\big)=\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon\}\big)

and consequently

(12)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon\}\big)

and if
E\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E\cap\Omega)<\varepsilon\}\big).

Similarly, for
E\subseteq\Omega
we have

\big(E\oplus\varepsilon\inter(C)\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)=\{x\in\Omega\setminus E;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon\}

and

(13)

\lambda^{n}\big((E\oplus\varepsilon C)\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\big)=\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)\in(0,\varepsilon)\}\big)+\lambda^{n}\big((\overline{E}\setminus E)\cap\Omega\big),

and hence

(14)

\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\dist_{C}(x,E)\in(0,\varepsilon)\}\big)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big((\overline{E}\setminus E)\cap\Omega\big).

•

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty and measurable. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. We have

\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon(1-\delta)\}\subseteq\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)\leq\varepsilon\}\subseteq\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon(1+\delta)\},

from which we see that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<\varepsilon\}\big)\quad\text{exists}

if and only if

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)\leq\varepsilon\}\big)\quad\text{exists,}

and if one of the limits exists, they must, in fact, be equal.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
E\subseteq\Omega
be a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
, and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

•

If
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)^{*}<\infty
, then
\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\big)\setminus E\Big)=0,

•

If the limit
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)
exists, then for any
F
with
E\subseteq F\subseteq\overline{E}
, we have the equality
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(F;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)
,

•

If
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)
, then
\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E^{1};\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)
,

•

If
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)
, then for any set
F\subseteq E
satisfying
\lambda^{n}(E\setminus F)=0
, we have
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(F;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let us start with the first assertion. Observe
\overline{E}=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(E\oplus\varepsilon C)
. Hence,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\big)\setminus E\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((E\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap\Omega\big)\setminus E\Big)\quad\text{for any $\varepsilon>0$}.

So, we obtain

\begin{split}\infty>\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)^{*}&=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((E\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap\Omega\big)\setminus E\Big)\\
&\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\big)\setminus E\Big),\end{split}

whence
\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\big)\setminus E\Big)=0.

To prove the second part, fix
\varepsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

E\subseteq F\subseteq\overline{E}\subseteq E\oplus\frac{\varepsilon}{k}C,

whence it follows

E\oplus\varepsilon C\subseteq(F\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon C\subseteq\left(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\right)\oplus\varepsilon C\subseteq E\oplus\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{k}+1\right)C.

Since
\lambda^{n}\Big(\left(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\right)\setminus E\Big)=0,
we deduce

\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(F;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}\left(\overline{E};\Omega\right)\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}+1\right)\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{k}+1\right),C}(E;\Omega).

Thus,

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C}(F;\Omega)_{*}\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C}\left(\overline{E};\Omega\right)^{*}\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}+1\right)\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega).

Sending
k
to infinity yields
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(F;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}\left(\overline{E};\Omega\right).

The third part follows easily from Proposition
2.2
, as

\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E^{1};\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)_{*}\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)^{*}\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E^{1};\Omega).

Hence
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E^{1};\Omega)
.

The fourth part is easy as well. We have

\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E^{1};\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(F;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C}(E;\Omega)\quad\text{for any $\varepsilon>0$},

whence
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(F;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega).

∎

In general, the equality
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E^{1};\Omega)
does
not
imply that
\mathcal{SM}_{C}\left(E;\Omega\right)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}\left(E;\Omega\right)
. A simple counterexample is
\Omega\coloneq\mathbb{R}^{1}
and
E\coloneq\mathbb{Q}
. The following proposition tells us that, assuming
\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)
and
\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E^{1};\Omega)
, where
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
, we indeed have the equality
\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
E
be a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
. If
\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)
for some
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
, then
\mathcal{SM}_{C}\big(\overline{E}\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)=0.

If, in addition, there exists some
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
such that
\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E^{1};\Omega)
, then
\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega).

Proof.

If
\overline{E^{1}}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
, there is nothing to prove. So, let us assume
\overline{E^{1}}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
.
The case
E\subseteq\overline{E^{1}}\cap\Omega
is clear. So, suppose
\big(\overline{E}\setminus\overline{E^{1}}\big)\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Notice
\overline{E^{1}}\subseteq\overline{E}
. We can find positive constants
a
and
b
such that
B(0,a)\subseteq C\subseteq B(0,b)
. Since
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}\big(\overline{E};\Omega\big)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}\big(\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)
(see Lemma
2.10
), it holds that

(15)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Bigg(\bigg(\Big(\big(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon C\Big)\setminus\Big(\big(\overline{E^{1}}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon C\Big)\bigg)\cap\Omega\Bigg)=0.

Define

E_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\left\{x\in\overline{E}\cap\Omega;\;\dist\left(x,\overline{E^{1}}\right)>2\diam(C)\varepsilon\right\}\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{for all $\varepsilon>0$,}

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\{x\in\Omega;\;\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>a\varepsilon\}
provided that
\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
; otherwise put
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega
. Observe there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the set
E_{\varepsilon}
is nonempty for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. Fixing
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, we have

(E_{\varepsilon}\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap\Big(\big(\overline{E^{1}}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon C\Big)\cap\Omega=\emptyset.

Theorem
1.7
gives us a positive integer
N
and an at most countable set
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}
, where
x_{i}\in E_{\varepsilon}
for all
i\in I_{\varepsilon}
, such that

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B(x_{i},\varepsilon a)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{B(x_{i},\varepsilon a)}\leq N.

Observe that
\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B(x_{i},\varepsilon a)\subseteq\big(E_{\varepsilon}\oplus B(0,{\varepsilon}a)\big)\cap\Omega\subseteq(E_{\varepsilon}\oplus\varepsilon C)\cap\Omega,
whence

(16)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B(x_{i},\varepsilon a)\subseteq\bigg(\Big(\big(\overline{E}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus{\varepsilon}C\Big)\setminus\Big(\big(\overline{E^{1}}\cap\Omega\big)\oplus\varepsilon C\Big)\bigg)\cap\Omega.

Further, we have

(17)

\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{\varepsilon})\omega_{n}\varepsilon^{n}a^{n}=\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\lambda^{n}\big(B(x_{i},\varepsilon a)\big)\leq N\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B(x_{i},\varepsilon a)\Big).

Combining (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
), we obtain

(18)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{n-1}=0.

We have
\big(\overline{E}\setminus\overline{E^{1}}\big)\cap\Omega\subseteq E_{\varepsilon}\oplus B\big(0,4\diam(C)\varepsilon\big),
so

\begin{split}\Big(\big(\overline{E}\setminus\overline{E^{1}}\big)\cap\Omega\Big)\oplus\varepsilon C&\subseteq E_{\varepsilon}\oplus B\big(0,4\diam(C)\varepsilon\big)\oplus B(0,\varepsilon b)\\
&\subseteq E_{\varepsilon}\oplus B\big(0,\varepsilon(4\diam(C)+b)\big)\\
&\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B\Big(x_{i},\varepsilon\big(a+b+4\diam(C)\big)\Big),\end{split}

whence

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}&\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\Big(\big(\overline{E}\setminus\overline{E^{1}}\big)\cap\Omega\Big)\oplus\varepsilon C\bigg)\\
&\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B\Big(x_{i},\varepsilon\big(a+b+4\diam(C)\big)\Big)\bigg)\\
&\leq\big(a+b+4\diam(C)\big)^{n}\omega_{n}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon^{n-1}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{lim4})}}}}{{=}}}0,\end{split}

which in turn implies
\mathcal{SM}_{C}\big(\overline{E}\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)=0
, and hence also
\mathcal{SM}_{C}\big(E\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)=0
.

To prove the second part, observe first that

\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C^{\prime}}\big(E\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)+\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,C^{\prime}}\big(\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)\quad\text{for any $\varepsilon>0$}.

It is plain to see that
\mathcal{SM}_{C}\big(E\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)=0
ensures
\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}\big(E\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)=0.
If
\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E^{1};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)
, then

\begin{split}\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E^{1};\Omega)&\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)_{*}\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)^{*}\\
&\leq\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}\big(E\setminus\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)+\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}\big(\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big)=\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}\big(\overline{E^{1}};\Omega\big),\end{split}

whence
\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E^{1};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega).

∎

3.
Main Result

This section is devoted to the existence of anisotropic Minkowski content of the topological boundary of a given set of finite perimeter. The proof of the following theorem is based on the Besicovitch covering theorem and Lemma
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a set of finite perimeter in an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. Then

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)

if and only if

\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}(\partial E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big).

Proof.

Let us suppose
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)
. If we have
(\partial E)\cap\Omega=\emptyset
, there is nothing to prove. So, let
(\partial E)\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
. It suffices to show

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}(\partial E;\Omega)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)\leq\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}(\partial E;\Omega)_{*}.

The second inequality in (
19
) always holds (see Proposition
2.2
), and hence it is enough to show the first inequality in (
19
).

Since
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable and of finite
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
measure, we can find a sequence
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
which covers
\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega
up to an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible set and each element of which is a subset of a graph of a real-valued Lipschitz function (see Remark
1.2
). We can also suppose that there exists some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
S_{k_{0}}\neq\emptyset
.
We see that if
(\partial E)\cap\Omega=\bigcup_{k=1}^{L}S_{k}
for some
L\in\mathbb{N}
, then the limit inferior in (
7
) is, in fact, the limit. So, we will suppose that
((\partial E)\cap\Omega)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{L}S_{k}
is nonempty for each
L\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us prove the first inequality in (
19
). Observe

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)&=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}\\
&=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{S_{k}}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{M}_{C}(S_{k};\Omega),\end{split}

where the last equality holds due to Lemma
2.1
. Thus, for a given
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
K\geq k_{0}
and

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{C}(S_{k};\Omega)<\varepsilon.

Since
\{S_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
is a finite sequence of disjoint compact sets contained in
\Omega
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{C}(S_{k};\Omega)&=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((S_{k}\cap\Omega)\oplus rC\big)\cap\Omega\Big)\\
&=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\Big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\oplus rC\Big)\cap\Omega\bigg),\end{split}

whence

(20)

\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\Bigg(\bigg(\Big(\big((\partial E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus rC\Big)\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\oplus rC\Big)\bigg)\cap\Omega\Bigg)<\varepsilon.

Find positive constants
a
and
b
such that
B(0,a)\subseteq C
and
C^{\prime}\subseteq B(0,b)
, and put

E_{r}\coloneq\left\{x\in(\partial E)\cap\Omega;\;\dist\left(x,\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\right)>2\diam(C)r\right\}\cap\Omega_{r}\quad\text{for $r>0$},

where
\Omega_{r}\coloneq\Omega
, provided that
\partial\Omega=\emptyset
; otherwise put
\Omega_{r}\coloneq\{x\in\Omega;\;\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>ar\}
. There exists
r_{0}>0
such that
E_{r}\neq\emptyset
for all
r\in(0,r_{0})
.
Fix
r\in(0,r_{0})
. Theorem
1.7
implies that there exist an at most countable set
I_{r}\subseteq E_{r}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(21)

\displaystyle E_{r}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in I_{r}}B(x,ar)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in I_{r}}\chi_{B(x,ar)}\leq N.

Notice

(22)

\begin{split}\bigcup_{x\in I_{r}}B(x,ar)&\subseteq E_{r}\oplus B(0,ar)\\
&\subseteq\Bigg(\Big(\big((\partial E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus rC\Big)\setminus\bigg(\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\oplus rC\bigg)\Bigg)\cap\Omega.\end{split}

So,

\begin{split}\omega_{n}a^{n}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{r})r^{n}&=\sum_{x\in I_{r}}\lambda^{n}\big(B(x,ar)\big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{number})}}}}{{\leq}}}N\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{x\in I_{r}}B(x,ar)\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{kut})}}}}{{\leq}}}N\lambda^{n}\Bigg(\bigg(\Big(\big((\partial E)\cap\Omega\big)\oplus rC\Big)\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\oplus rC\Big)\bigg)\cap\Omega\Bigg),\end{split}

whence, using (
20
), we infer

(23)

\limsup_{r\to 0+}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{r})r^{n-1}\leq\frac{2N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}.

Now
\Big((\partial E)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\Big)\cap\Omega\subseteq E_{r}\oplus B\big(0,4\diam(C)r\big)
, and therefore

\begin{split}\Bigg(\bigg(&\Big((\partial E)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}\Big)\cap\Omega\bigg)\oplus rC^{\prime}\Bigg)\cap\Omega\\
&\subseteq\Big(E_{r}\oplus B\big(0,4\diam(C)r\big)\oplus B(0,rb)\Big)\cap\Omega\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{number})}}}}{{\subseteq}}}\bigcup_{x\in I_{r}}B\Big(x,r\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)\Big),\end{split}

from which it follows

(24)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}&\Big((\partial E)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k};\Omega\Big)^{*}\\
&\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\bigcup_{x\in I_{r}}B\Big(x,r\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)\Big)\bigg)\\
&\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{x\in I_{r}}\lambda^{n}\bigg(B\Big(x,r\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)\Big)\bigg)\\
&=\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{r})r^{n-1}\frac{\omega_{n}\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)^{n}}{2}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{výt})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{N\varepsilon\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)^{n}}{a^{n}}.\end{split}

Finally,

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}&(\partial E;\Omega)^{*}\leq\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}\Big((\partial E)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k};\Omega\Big)^{*}+\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}\Big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{k};\Omega\Big)^{*}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{kvak})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{N\varepsilon\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)^{n}}{a^{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}(S_{k};\Omega)\\
&\leq\frac{N\varepsilon\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)^{n}}{a^{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{S_{k}}\big(h_{C^{\prime}}(\nu_{E})+h_{C^{\prime}}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}\\
&=\frac{N\varepsilon\big(a+4\diam(C)+b\big)^{n}}{a^{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big),\end{split}

where the last equality follows from Lemma
2.1
.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain

\mathcal{M}_{C^{\prime}}(\partial E;\Omega)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

We can deduce from the proof of Theorem
3.1
, point (
24
), the following corollary:

.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
E\subseteq\Omega
set of finite perimeter in
\Omega

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big)

\mathcal{M}_{C}\big((\partial E)\setminus(\partial^{*}E);\Omega\big)=0.

Using Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
2.6

Remark 3.4
.

It is worth pointing out that the existence of the

Let us take a closer look at the functional
\mathfrak{M}_{C}
and the reduced boundary of a given set of finite perimeter:
Suppose that

Do we have the equality

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)?

Unfortunately, this implication does not hold. However, it is incorrectly proven in

It is also clear that the functional

\mathcal{M}_{C}

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open,
E\subseteq\Omega
be a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
, and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. If

\mathfrak{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big),

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{0};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega).

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{0};\Omega)=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega),

then

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big).

Proof.

Remark
1.6
tells us that
\overline{\partial^{*}E}=\partial(E^{1})=\partial(E^{0})
. If
\partial^{*}E=\emptyset
, there is nothing to prove. So, let
\partial^{*}E\neq\emptyset
.
We have from (
12
) for
\varepsilon>0

\begin{split}\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)&=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\big(\{x\in\Omega;\;\dist_{C}(x,\partial^{*}E)<\varepsilon\}\big)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big\{x\in\Omega;\;\dist_{C}\big(x,\partial(E^{1})\big)<\varepsilon\big\}\Big)\\
&=\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}\big(\partial(E^{1});\Omega\big)=\mathfrak{M}_{\varepsilon,C}\big(\partial(E^{0});\Omega\big),\end{split}

and hence both
\mathfrak{M}_{C}\big(\partial(E^{1});\Omega\big)
and
\mathfrak{M}_{C}\big(\partial(E^{0});\Omega\big)
exist. Finally, Proposition
2.6
implies the existence of
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)
and
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{0};\Omega).

Now, suppose that
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)
and
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{0};\Omega)
exist. Using (
3
) and Proposition
2.6
, we deduce that both
\mathcal{M}_{C}\big(\partial(E^{0});\Omega\big)
and
\mathcal{M}_{C}\big(\partial(E^{1});\Omega\big)
exist. Proposition
2.13
ensures

\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)=\mathcal{M}_{C}\big(\partial(E^{1});\Omega\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)+\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega)\big).

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Proposition
3.6
coupled with (
3
) gives us the following chain of implications:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)\text{ exists}\implies\mathscr{M}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)\text{ exists}\implies\mathcal{M}_{C}(\partial^{*}E;\Omega)\text{ exists}

\displaystyle\Updownarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{1};\Omega)\And\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E^{0};\Omega)\text{ exist}

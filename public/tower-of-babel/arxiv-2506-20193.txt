Microquasar jet-cocoon systems as PeVatrons Preprint: APS/123-QED
B. Theodore Zhang 1,2, Shigeo S. Kimura 3,4, Kohta Murase 5,6

Abstract
The origin of Galactic cosmic rays (CRs), particularly around the knee region ( ∼ \sim 3 PeV), remains a major unsolved question. Recent observations by LHAAASO suggest that the knee is shaped mainly by protons, with a transition to heavier elements at higher energies. Microquasars – compact jet-emitting sources – have emerged as possible PeV CR accelerators, especially after detections of ultrahigh-energy gamma rays from these systems. We propose that the observed proton spectrum (hard below a few PeV, steep beyond) arises from the reacceleration of sub-TeV Galactic CRs via shear acceleration in large-scale microquasar jet-cocoon structures. Our model also naturally explains the observed spectrum of energies around a few tens of PeV by summing up heavier nuclei contributions. Additionally, similar reacceleration processes in radio galaxies can contribute to ultrahigh-energy CRs, bridging Galactic and extragalactic origins. Combined with low-energy CRs from supernova remnants and galaxy clusters around the second knee region, this scenario could provide a unified explanation for CRs across the entire energy spectrum.

Introduction

The origin of cosmic rays (CRs), mainly composed of protons, heliums, and heavier nuclei, is a long-standing puzzle in astrophysics
Antoni et al. 2005
;
Kotera and Olinto 2011
;
Kachelriess and Semikoz 2019
.
The energy spectrum of CRs spans over a wide range from
10^{9}\rm~eV
to
10^{20}\rm~eV
, roughly following a power-law spectrum
\Phi_{\rm CR}\propto E^{-\gamma}
.
The spectrum exhibits a distinctive feature with a change in the power law spectral index from
\gamma\sim 2.7
to
\gamma\sim 3
at the energy around
4\rm~PeV
, known as
knee

Antoni et al. 2005
;
Amenomori et al. 2008
;
Cao et al. 2024a
, discovered by Kulikov and Khristiansen in 1958
Kulikov and Khristiansen
.
At energies around
\sim 100\rm~PeV
, the spectral index changed from
\gamma\sim 3
to
\gamma\sim 3.3
, called the “second knee”
(
Nagano et al. 1992
;
Abbasi et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
.
The spectral index changes back to
\gamma\sim 2.5
at the
ankle
around 4 EeV
(
Bird et al. 1993
, e.g.,)
, and the flux is largely suppressed at a few tens of EeV
Abbasi et al. 2008
;
Abraham et al. 2008
.
The
knee
is believed to be formed due to the limited maximum rigidity of Galactic CR accelerators
Hillas 2005
, or reflecting a spectral break at the same rigidity
Cardillo et al. 2015
. The
knee
could also be formed due to the change of the energy dependent diffusion coefficient in the propagation of CRs
Giacinti et al. 2015
, while
CRs between the
knee
and
ankle
should be marked as the transition region from Galactic to extragalactic CRs
Gaisser 2006
. CRs with energy above EeV are regarded as ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays (UHECRs), which have an extragalactic origin as supported by the observation of the large-scale anisotropy, where the dipole direction points in the opposite direction of the Galactic center
Aab et al. 2018
.

CR composition is the key to identifying the origins.
The general pattern of CR abundances around a few GeV per nucleon resembles that of the interstellar medium or primordial solar composition, with peaks in the CNO and Fe region
Drury 2018
.
Recently, a
knee
-like feature around
3\rm~PeV
is also revealed on the measured all-particle spectrum of CRs.
The logarithm of the mean mass of CRs measured by the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) showed trends toward a heavier composition below several hundred TeV and above several PeV, while it is a light composition dominated around the
knee
, and the turning point is consistent with the position of the
knee
at energy around
3\rm~PeV

Cao et al. 2024a
.
In particular, LHAASO reported the first high-purity proton spectrum, implicating the
knee
at the energy around
3\rm~PeV
is related to the change of the proton spectrum
Cao et al. 2025
. The observed hardening feature above several hundreds of TeV implicates the emergence of a new component that has the maximum energy at least up to a few PeV
Cao et al. 2025
.

The physical origin of the
knee
is still unknown and should be related to Galactic PeVtrons.
SNRs are believed to be responsible for CRs at least up to the knee
Reynolds 2008
;
Blasi 2013
. However, SNRs as PeVatrons are challenged by both theoretical and observational studies
Bell 2004
;
Bell 2005
;
Gabici et al. 2019
;
Giuliani and Cardillo 2024
, potentially implying existence of another type of PeVatrons.
Potential candidates for PeVtrons include massive stars and stellar clusters
Casse and Paul 1982
;
Cesarsky and Montmerle 1983
;
Aharonian et al. 2019
, pulsars and pulsar winds
Bednarek and Protheroe 2002
;
Giller and Lipski 2002
;
Bednarek and Bartosik 2004
;
Ohira et al. 2018
, superbubbles
Bykov et al. 2020
, hypernovae
Sveshnikova, L. G. 2003
and interacting supernovae
Murase et al. 2014
;
Inoue et al. 2021
, and isolated black holes
Ioka et al. 2017
;
Kimura et al. 2025
.
The detection of very high-energy (VHE) gamma rays has shown that microquasars are promising sites for accelerating CRs around PeV and above, indicating potential sources of Galactic PeV CRs
Cao et al. 2024b
.
Microquasars are X-ray binaries with relativistic jets
Mirabel and Rodriguez 1994

and display phenomenology similar to active galactic nuclei (AGNs), although the size of the system is orders of magnitude different.
It is suggested that shear and one-shot acceleration in the large-scale jet of radio galaxies can be responsible for the origin of UHECRs
Kimura et al. 2018a
;
Caprioli 2015
;
Mbarek and Caprioli 2019
;
Mbarek et al. 2023
;
Mbarek et al. 2025
.
This motivates us to investigate whether the shear acceleration by microquasar jets can be Galactic PeVatrons.
Previous studies also suggest microquasars as PeVatrons
Cooper et al. 2020
;
Kimura et al. 2021
;
Yue et al. 2024
, but these studies consider CR acceleration by shocks in jets or magnetic reconnections in accretion flows.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
II

In Section

In Section

II
Cosmic rays accelerated in microquasars

II.1
The physics of microquasar jets

A microquasar consists of a compact object, usually a black hole, and a companion star, see Fig.
1
for a schematic plot.
Currently, over 20 microquasars have been identified in our Galaxy
Tetarenko et al. 2016
;
Corral-Santana et al. 2016
.
In the microquasar systems, material of the companion star falls to the compact star, which releases a large amount of the gravitational energy. A part of the gravitational energy is converted to outflow kinetic energy. These outflows, called jets, are launched at the vicinity of the compact star, narrowly collimated to a small solid angle, and often reach relativistic speeds. The kinetic power of the jets can reach the Eddington luminosity,
L_{j}\sim L_{\rm Edd}\sim 1\times 10^{39}(M/10M_{\odot})\rm~erg~s^{-1}
. The jets propagate in interstellar medium and extend to
\sim 100
pc scales, which is many orders of magnitude larger than the scale of the compact star. See Ref.
Done et al. 2007
for a comprehensive review.

Aharonian et al. 2024

In addition, Ref.

V4641 Sgr is another interesting microquasar who emits gamma-rays above 100 TeV. The morophorogy of the gamma-ray signal is clear two-sided lobe structure, and the size of the lobe is roughly

II.2
Shear acceleration in microquasar jets

Shear acceleration, a class of the Fermi acceleration, has been suggested to be an efficient mechanism to accelerate CRs, where charged particles gain energy inside an ordered shear velocity field
Berezhko and Krymskii 1981
;
Earl et al. 1988
;
Ostrowski 1990
;
Webb 1989
;
Rieger 2019
.
It has been shown that the discrete shear acceleration
Ostrowski 1990
occurring on the surface of the jet-cocoon system in radio galaxies may accelerate low-energy “seed” galactic CRs to the UHE energy range, and the predicted spectrum and composition are consistent with observations
Kimura et al. 2018a
;
Seo et al. 2024
.

In AGNs, the reacceleration may occur through the “one-shot” acceleration, where the “seed” CRs receive a boost of a factor of
\Gamma^{2}

Gallant and Achterberg 1999
, and it is possible for powerful radio galaxies with a Lorentz factor of
\Gamma\gtrsim 30
to accelerate “seed” galactic CRs with energy
E\lesssim 10^{17}\rm~eV
to the UHE energy range
Caprioli 2015
;
Mbarek and Caprioli 2019
.

We consider the shear acceleration by cylindrical jets and cocoon as discussed in Kimura, Murase and Zhang (hereafter KMZ18)
Kimura et al. 2018a
. Based on discussion in Section
II.1
, we assume that the radius of the jet is
r_{\rm jet}=3\rm~pc
, the length is
l_{\rm jet}=100\rm~pc

\beta_{j}=0.3

\Gamma_{j}\simeq 1.05

\displaystyle+\frac{v\rho(\mathbf{x})}{m_{p}}\sum_{k\geq i}\int\frac{d\sigma_{i,k}(E,E^{\prime})}{dE}N_{k}(E^{\prime},\mathbf{x})dE^{\prime},

The maximum value of Galactocentric radius is

The spatial distribution of microquasars is unknown.
For microquasars like SS 433 or Sgr V4641, it follows the distribution of massive stars, thus have similar spatial distribution with Galactic SNRs.
For the source distribution, we adopt the Ferriere2001 model

The spatial distribution of the diffusion coefficient is constant and parametrized as

The chosen diffusion coefficient is determined by the observed Boron to Carbon (B/C) ratio.

IV
Connection to low- and high-energy components

In this section, we aim to demonstrate that our microquasar model can be combined with other astrophysical models explaining the CR spectrum at different wavelengths. In particular, we provide a possible model, in which the whole CR spectrum from around GeV to 100 EeV can be explained with four main sources, including supernova remnants (SNR, Gal pop1), microquasar (MQ, Gal pop2), clusters of galaxies (CG, ExGal pop1) and radio galaxies (RQ, ExGal pop2).
Note that the main purpose here is to demonstrate that the reacceleration model works not only in the large-scale jet-cocoon system of microquasars but also in radio galaxies.

For CRs from Galactic SNRs, we calculate the observed energy spectrum of each element with DRAGON code, and the normalization is adjusted to match the AMS-02 data.
We adopt an broken-power law injection spectrum of CRs from SNRs to fit the data. See Appendix for the details.
The escaped CR spectrum typically have exponential cutoff beyond the maximum acceleration energy.
We set the maximum rigidity from Galactic SNRs to
\mathcal{R}_{\rm max}=0.4\rm~PV
.

Here, we adopt the predicted spectrum and composition model of UHECRs in KMZ18, where CRs with rigidity

Since radio galaxies are more evolved than our Galaxy, KMZ18 considers that the metal composition is enhanced by a ratio of 3, compared to our Galaxy. Here, we further enhance the iron composition ratio by a factor of 3 to improve the fit to the latest UHECR data.
The further enhancement of iron composition ratio in radio galaxies is reasonable because elliptical galaxies have lower star-formation rates than our Galaxy. In radio galaxies, core-collapse supernovae do not frequently occur because of their low star-formation rates, while type Ia supernovae occur because explosions of white dwarfs require significant time lag after star-formation activities. Then, type Ia supernovae inject iron into the ISM, enhancing the iron composition ratio.

However, one should keep in mind that uncertainty is currently large, and this demonstrates that the reacceleration of Galactic CRs plays a key role in reshaping the mass distribution of accelerated CRs at different energy ranges.

V
Summary and Discussion

In this work, we studied the reacceleration and escape of CRs in microquasars within the framework of the shear acceleration mechanism.
Our results showed that the CRs from microquasars could explain the observed proton energy spectrum above the PeV energy range.
The predicted all-particle spectrum and composition are consistent with the LHAASO observations, where the relative fraction of difference nuclei is constrained by direct measurements around rigidity from 10 GV to 1 TV. The maximum acceleration energy of CRs from shear acceleration in microquasars is close to the
knee
around 3 PeV, and the escaping CRs have a hard spectrum.

In our scenario, the magnetic field strength in jets and cocoons should be as high as
B_{\rm jet}\sim 50-100\rm~\mu G
and
B_{\rm coc}\sim 10-30\rm~\mu G
to achieve the PeV CR acceleration. Both field strengths are somewhat higher than expected, but the energetics arguments are not violated. The magnetic luminosity in the jet can be estimated to be

\displaystyle L_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{\rm jet}^{2}}{4\pi}r_{\rm jet}^{2}\beta_{\rm jet}c

(7)

\displaystyle\simeq 3.2\times 10^{38}\left(\frac{B_{\rm jet}}{\rm 50\mu G}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{r_{\rm jet}}{\rm 3~pc}\right)\left(\frac{\beta_{\rm jet}}{0.3}\right)\rm~erg~s^{-1}.

This is slightly lower than the Eddington luminosity,
L_{\rm Edd}\sim 10^{39}(M/10~M_{\odot})\rm~erg~s^{-1}
. The required cocoon magnetic field strength,
B_{\rm coc}\sim 10\rm~\mu G
, is a few times higher than the typical ISM value,
B_{\rm ISM}\sim 3\rm~\mu G
, and thus, the shock compression of the ISM would be able to naturally achieve the required
B_{\rm coc}
.

Given the universality of the shear acceleration mechanism, we consider a unified model in which microquasars dominate observed Galactic CRs around
knee
, while radio galaxies are responsible for the observed UHECRs.
We found that the whole CR range from GeV to 100 EeV could be explained given that SNRs supply most low-energy Galactic CRs and another population (e.g., galaxy clusters) could contribute at subankle energies.
Our model could be tested with future CR experiments with improved measurements of the composition and anisotropy.

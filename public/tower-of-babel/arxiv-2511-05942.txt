On the exchange of stability for the subcritical laminar flow
Vladimir Kozlov, Oleg Motygin

Abstract
We consider steady water waves in two-dimensional channel bounded below by a flat, rigid bottom and above by a free surface. The surface tension is neglected and the water motion is rotational with a constant vorticity a a . We consider an analytic branch of Stokes waves started from a subcritical laminar flow, where the period is considered as the bifurcation parameter. The first eigenvalue of the Fréchet derivative on this branch is always negative. The main object of our study is the second eigenvalue of the Fréchet derivative at this branch in a neighborhood of the laminar flow. This is a small eigenvalue and the positive sign corresponds to the confirmation of the principle of exchange of stability and the negative sign to its violation. We consider the dependence of the sign on the Bernoulli constant R R (or the depth d d of the laminar flow) and the value of the constant vorticity a a . We show that for each a a there exist a depth d 0 ​ ( a ) d_{0}(a) such that the sign of the second eigenvalue is positive for d < d 0 ​ ( a ) d<d_{0}(a) and negative for d > d 0 ​ ( a ) d>d_{0}(a) . If d s ​ ( a ) d_{\textrm{s}}(a) is the depth where a stagnation point appears on the corresponding laminar flow then d 0 ​ ( a ) < d s ​ ( a ) d_{0}(a)<d_{\textrm{s}}(a) for positive a a and d 0 ​ ( a ) > d s ​ ( a ) d_{0}(a)>d_{\textrm{s}}(a) for negative a < − 1.01803 a<-1.01803 . The sign of the second eigenvalue is important in study of subharmonic bifurcations. Another observed property consists of the following: if the sign of the second eigenvalue is positive then the period is increasing along the bifurcation curve in a neighborhood of the bifurcation point, if the sign is negative then the period is decreasing there. We also verify the property of formal stability by a description of the domain in ( a , d ) (a,d) variables, where this property holds. Numerical illustrations of these properties are presented in the paper. Keywords: nonlinear water wave theory, steady water waves, constant vorticity, stability properties, exchange of stability MSC classification: 35Q35, 35B32, 76B15, 76D07

3.3
Exchange of stability

Consider the problem (
1.2
) for even,
\Lambda_{*}
-periodic functions.
The function
\psi=U
satisfies this problem for all
\lambda>0
if
R
is defined by (
3.2
). Let us calculate the Fréchet derivative. According to (
1.3
), (
1.4
) the corresponding spectral problem is defined by

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u+\partial_{y}^{2}u=0\quad\mbox{for\!}\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ \ y\in(0,d),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}u^{\prime}-(1-a\kappa)u=\mu u\quad\mbox{for\!}\quad y=d,

\displaystyle u(x,0)=0.

Here
u
is an even
\Lambda_{*}
-periodic function. This problem can be solved by using separation of variables method:

u(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h_{k}(y)\cos(k\tau_{*}x).

Then we obtain the following boundary value problems for the coefficients
h_{k}

\displaystyle h_{k}^{\prime\prime}(y)-\lambda^{2}k^{2}\tau_{*}^{2}h_{k}=0\quad\mbox{on\!}\quad(0,d),

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}h_{k}^{\prime}-(1-a\kappa)h_{k}=\mu_{k}h_{k}\quad\mbox{for\!}\quad y=d,

\displaystyle h_{k}(0)=0.

Solution to this problem is given by

\gamma_{k}(y)=c_{k}\gamma(y;\lambda k\tau_{*}))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{1+k}(\lambda)=\sigma(\lambda k\tau_{*}),

which imply

\mu_{1}=\sigma(0),\quad\mu_{2}(\lambda)=\sigma(\lambda^{2}\tau_{*})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{k}(\lambda)\geq\sigma(4\lambda^{2}\tau_{*}^{2})\;\;\mbox{for $k>2$}.

Therefore the first eigenvalue is always negative and the second eigenvalue
\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is zero for
\lambda=1
, it is positive for
\lambda>1
and it is negative for
\lambda<1
.

If we consider these eigenvalues as functions of the period
\Lambda=2\pi/\tau
then
\mu_{1}(\Lambda)
is always negative,
\mu_{1}(\Lambda)
is zero for
\Lambda=\Lambda_{*}
, it is positive for
\Lambda<\Lambda_{*}
and it is negative for
\Lambda>\Lambda_{*}
. So if
\Lambda
increases from
0
to
\infty
then at the point
\Lambda_{*}
appears additional negative eigenvalue, which gives additional instability to the problem. This leads to appearance of a new bifurcation branch at the point
\Lambda_{*}
and our aim is to verify the principle of exchange of stability with respect to small eigenvalues near this point.

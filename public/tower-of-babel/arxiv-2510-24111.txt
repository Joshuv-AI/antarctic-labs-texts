On the Superconvergence of ESFR Schemes Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Mathias Dufresne-Piché, Siva Nadarajah

Abstract
The energy stable flux reconstruction (ESFR) method provides an efficient and flexible framework to devise high-order linearly stable numerical schemes which can achieve high levels of accuracy on unstructured grids. While superconvergent properties of ESFR schemes have been observed in numerical experiments, no formal proof of this behavior has been reported in the literature. In this work, we attempt to address this by providing a simple derivation for the superconvergence of the dispersion-dissipation error of ESFR schemes for the linear advection problem when using an upwind numerical flux. We show that the superconvergence of ESFR schemes essentially relies on the capacity of the latter to generate superconvergent rational approximants of the exponential function, which is reminiscent of well-known theoretical results for superconvergence of discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods. We also demonstrate that the drops in order of accuracy which are observed in numerical experiments as the ESFR scalar c c is increased are caused by both a modification of the structure of these rational approximants and a change in the multiplicity of the physical eigenvalue of the schemes as c → ∞ c\to\infty . Finally, our theoretical results are successfully validated against numerical experiments.

High-order methods, Flux reconstruction, Superconvergence, Energy stability, Discontinuous Galerkin method

Introduction

Flux reconstruction (FR) schemes
[
15
,
25
]
are a class of high-order spatial discretizations which have been gaining in popularity in recent years as a simple and versatile finite-element formulation for computational fluid dynamics (CFD) problems. Various well-known schemes, such as collocation-based nodal discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods and some spectral difference (SD) methods can be expressed within the FR unifying framework
[
15
]
. Using the FR approach, Vincent et al.
[
22
]
were able to identify an infinite family of linearly stable methods known as energy stable flux reconstruction (ESFR) schemes, which have been the subject of extensive investigations in the past decade. The numerical properties of ESFR schemes for linear problems have been studied by
[
22
,
16
]
, and ESFR discretizations have been developed for triangular and tetrahedral elements by
[
26
,
8
]
and extended to the advection-diffusion problem by
[
20
,
18
,
19
]
.

It is well-known that DG methods exhibit so-called superconvergent properties, which allow different error functionals to converge faster than the standard L2-error of the solution. For instance,
[
1
,
27
]
showed that the DG spatial discretization of the linear advection problem was superconvergent with order
p+2
at the Radau points. Superconvergence of order
2p+1
was also observed by
[
27
]
for the cell averages and the average error at the downwind points. A formal proof of this property was later provided by
[
7
]
. Superconvergence of the dispersion-dissipation error of the DG method for linear hyperbolic problems has also been studied extensively. In
[
14
]
, Hu and Atkins conjectured that the dispersion-dissipation relations of the DG spatial discretization were related to a specific Padé approximation of the exponential function, thereby leading to superconvergent dispersion error of order
2p+3
and dissipation error of order
2p+2
. This conjecture was subsequently proven by
[
3
]
, who investigated the different convergence regimes of the dispersion-dissipation error under
h
and
p
refinement. In
[
11
]
, Guo et al. used von Neumann analysis to decompose the point-wise error associated with DG schemes. By symbolically computing the relevant eigenvalues and eigenvectors for low-order spatial discretizations, they were able to relate the superconvergent behavior of DG schemes to the spectral properties of the latter. This perspective was further investigated in the work of
[
9
]
, which formally established the connection between a specific Padé approximation of the exponential function and superconvergence of the long-time error of DG schemes.
[
9
]
also showed that this Padé approximation was related to the superconvergent behavior of the cell average and the error at the Radau and downwind points.

For FR schemes, superconvergence of the dispersion-dissipation error has been observed in numerical experiments by
[
15
,
22
,
21
]
. Namely, in
[
22
]
, Vincent et al. measured that the order of accuracy of the dispersion-dissipation error of ESFR schemes was either
2p+1
,
2p
or
2p-1
depending on the value of the ESFR scalar parameter. The connection between the dispersion-dissipation error of FR schemes and superconvergence of their associated long-time error has also been investigated by
[
5
]
. The error estimate derived by
[
5
]
is however, semi-analytical and requires the numerical evaluation of the order of accuracy of the dispersion-dissipation error. Although an error bound for the L2-error of the ESFR spatial discretization has been formally derived by
[
16
]
, to the knowledge of the authors of this paper, there is no existing work focusing on proving formally the superconvergent properties of ESFR schemes. The objective of this paper is thus to address this theoretical gap. Namely, we present a simple proof for the superconvergence of the combined dispersion-dissipation error of ESFR schemes for the linear advection problem when using an upwind numerical flux. Our results provide firm theoretical grounds to explain the numerical observations of
[
22
,
21
]
and when considered in conjunction with the work of
[
5
]
, they allow for a fully analytic characterization of the superconvergence of the long-time error of ESFR schemes.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section

2
provides a brief review of the FR framework and its relationship with ESFR schemes.
Section

3
focuses on deriving a theoretical dispersion-dissipation error estimate for ESFR schemes and hence proving the superconvergent properties of the latter. In
section

4
, these theoretical results are then validated via numerical experiments. Finally, in
section

5
, extension of the theoretical results to more general symmetric FR schemes is briefly considered.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
ESFR schemes for the Linear Advection Problem

In this section, we briefly review the ESFR framework in the context of the one-dimensional linear advection problem. For a more thorough presentation of the matter, we refer the reader to the work of
[
15
,
23
,
4
]
.

For a unit propagation speed, the linear advection problem is written as

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=0\quad;\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\quad;\quad x\in\Omega,

(1)

where
u
is the conserved scalar and
\Omega
is a closed interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Following the classical FR formulation
[
15
]
, we start by discretizing
\Omega
into
N
non-overlapping elements
\Omega_{k}
such that

\Omega=\bigcup_{k=0}^{N-1}\Omega_{k}\quad;\quad\bigcap_{k=0}^{N-1}\Omega_{k}=\emptyset.

(2)

Upon introducing the standard normalization

\hat{u}_{k}(\xi,t):=u(\Gamma_{k}^{-1}(\xi),t)

(3)

via the elemental linear mappings
\Gamma_{k}:\Omega_{k}\to[-1,1]

\xi=\Gamma_{k}(x)=2\frac{x-x_{k}}{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}-1,

(4)

and their associated inverses

x=\Gamma^{-1}_{k}(\xi)=\frac{1-\xi}{2}x_{k}+\frac{1+\xi}{2}x_{k+1},

(5)

we can rewrite
eq.

1
as

\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{k}}{\partial t}+\frac{2}{|\Omega_{k}|}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{k}}{\partial\xi}=0.

(6)

In the FR framework, the local solutions
\hat{u}_{k}
are then approximated using a combination of
p+1
linearly independent high-order polynomials
\chi_{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{p}([-1,1])
such that

\hat{u}_{k}(\xi,t)\approx\hat{u}_{k}^{h}(\xi,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\hat{u}^{h}_{jk}(t)\chi_{j}(\xi)

(7)

and the global solution on
\Omega
is consequently given by

u(x,t)\approx u^{h}(x,t)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N-1}\hat{u}_{k}^{h}(\Gamma_{k}(x),t).

(8)

The classical FR spatial discretization is then obtained by requiring the local solutions introduced in
eq.

7
to satisfy
eq.

6
in its differential form and by introducing the left and right correction functions
g_{L},g_{R}\in\mathcal{P}^{p+1}([-1,1])
to ensure continuity of the flux at the left and right elemental interfaces. This yields the familiar semi-discrete scheme

\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{d\hat{u}_{jk}^{h}}{dt}\chi_{j}+\frac{2}{|\Omega_{k}|}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p}\hat{u}_{jk}^{h}\frac{d\chi_{j}}{d\xi}+(\hat{f}^{*}_{kL}-\hat{u}^{h}_{k}|_{L})\frac{dg_{L}}{d\xi}+(\hat{f}^{*}_{kR}-\hat{u}^{h}_{k}|_{R})\frac{dg_{R}}{d\xi}\right)=0,

(9)

where
f^{*}_{kL}
and
f^{*}_{kL}
denote the numerical flux at the left and right elemental interfaces and
\hat{u}^{h}_{k}|_{L}=\hat{u}^{h}_{k}(-1,t)
and
\hat{u}^{h}_{k}|_{R}=\hat{u}^{h}_{k}(1,t)
. In vector form,
eq.

9
amounts to

\frac{d\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{h}_{k}}{dt}+\mathbf{D}\hat{\mathbf{f}}^{D}_{k}+(\hat{f}^{*}_{kL}-\mathbf{l}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{f}}^{D}_{k})\mathbf{h}_{L}+(\hat{f}^{*}_{kR}-\mathbf{r}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{f}}^{D}_{k})\mathbf{h}_{R}=0,

(10)

where

(\mathbf{l})_{j}:=\chi_{j}(-1)\quad;\quad(\mathbf{r})_{j}=\chi_{j}(1)\quad;\quad(\mathbf{u}_{k}^{h})_{j}=\hat{u}^{h}_{jk},

(11)

g_{L}^{\prime}(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(\mathbf{h}_{L})_{j}\chi_{j}(\xi)\quad;\quad g_{R}^{\prime}(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(\mathbf{h}_{R})_{j}\chi_{j}(\xi)

(12)

and
\mathbf{D}
is the matrix representation of the differential operator on
\mathcal{P}^{p}([-1,1])
.

ESFR schemes were initially derived by
[
23
]
by requiring
g_{L}
and
g_{R}
to be such that
u^{h}
is stable with respect to a broken Sobolev norm.
[
4
,
28
]
showed that this was equivalent to constructing
\mathbf{h}_{L}
and
\mathbf{h}_{R}
via

\mathbf{h}_{R}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{r}\quad;\quad\mathbf{h}_{L}=-(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l},

(13)

where
\mathbf{M}
is the mass matrix and
\mathbf{K}
is the ESFR filtering matrix defined as

\mathbf{M}_{mn}:=\int_{-1}^{1}\chi_{m}\chi_{n}d\xi\quad;\quad\mathbf{K}:=\frac{1}{2}c(\mathbf{D}^{p})^{T}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{D}^{p}

(14)

and
c
is the ESFR scalar parameter. Clearly, when
c=0
, a standard nodal discontinuous Galerkin scheme is recovered. As shown by
[
23
]
, ESFR schemes are linearly stable provided that
c>c_{-}
. Moreover, when
c=c_{SD}
or
c=c_{HU}
, one recovers a spectral difference scheme or Huynh’s
g_{2}
scheme, respectively. We refer the reader to the work of
[
23
]
for the specific analytical expressions for
c_{-}
,
c_{SD}
and
c_{HU}
and for a more thorough description of ESFR schemes.

2.2
Dispersion-dissipation Error of ESFR Schemes

As previously foreshadowed, this study focuses on characterizing the superconvergent properties of the dispersion-dissipation error of ESFR schemes. In this context, consistent with the work of
[
12
,
22
,
21
]
, dispersion-dissipation error is defined via

E_{T}(\theta):=|\omega^{h}(\theta)-\omega_{PDE}(\theta)|,

(15)

where
\omega^{h}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is the dispersion-dissipation relation of the numerical scheme,
\omega_{PDE}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is the dispersion-dissipation relation of the PDE solved by the numerical scheme and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
represents the non-dimensional wavenumber of Bloch wave solutions. For the linear advection problem (unit propagation speed), the true dispersion-dissipation relation is given by

\omega_{PDE}(\theta)=\theta,

(16)

which ensures Bloch wave solutions of the form

u(x,t)=\exp(i(\theta x-\omega_{PDE}(\theta)t))

(17)

are solutions to
eq.

1
and where
i:=\sqrt{-1}
. Following the same approach as
[
22
,
17
,
21
]
, the dispersion-dissipation relations of ESFR schemes are in turn obtained by substituting numerical Bloch waves of the form

\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{h}_{k}=\exp(i(k\theta-\omega^{h}(\theta)t))\hat{\mathbf{v}}

(18)

in
eq.

10
, which yields the eigenvalue problem

\mathbf{H}(\theta)\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\theta)=\omega^{h}(\theta)\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\theta).

(19)

In what follows, we will refer to
\mathbf{H}
as the von Neumann matrix of a given numerical scheme. For an upwind numerical flux,
[
22
]
showed that
\mathbf{H}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
is given by

\mathbf{H}(\theta)=-2i\left(\mathbf{D}+(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l}\mathbf{l}^{T}-(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l}\mathbf{r}^{T}e^{-i\theta}\right),

and
\omega^{h}(\theta)
is defined as the unique physical eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}(\theta)
which approximates the true dispersion-dissipation relation of the PDE as
\theta\to 0
.

As discussed by
[
15
]
, studying the convergence of
E_{T}
as
\theta\to 0
allows for the characterization of the impact of
h
-refinement on the dispersion-dissipation error of a numerical scheme. Following the notation of
[
15
,
22
,
21
]
, we define the spectral order of accuracy of a numerical scheme via

E_{T}\propto\theta^{A_{T}+1}\quad\text{as}\quad\theta\to 0.

(20)

In other words,
A_{T}
is the convergence rate of
E_{T}
minus one as
\theta\to 0
. ESFR schemes are said to exhibit superconvergent behavior because
A_{T}>p
for this family of linearly stable schemes. The superconvergent properties of FR schemes have been observed by
[
15
,
22
,
21
]
through numerical experiments. More precisely, by selecting a sufficiently small value
0<\Delta\theta\ll 1
, they computed
E_{T}
numerically and used

A_{T}\approx\frac{\ln(E_{T}(\Delta\theta))-\ln(E_{T}(\Delta\theta/2))}{\ln(2)}-1

(21)

to estimate the convergence rate of the dispersion-dissipation error. They found that
A_{T}\approx 2p+1
for values of
c
close to 0, that
A_{T}\approx 2p
for
c\in c_{SD},c_{HU}
and that
A_{T}\approx 2p-1
for sufficiently large values of
c
. In the rest of this study, through analytical means, we will determine
A_{T}
rigorously for ESFR schemes and use this to unveil the fundamental mechanism behind superconvergence of FR methods.

3
Proof for Superconvergence Property

3.1
Equivalent Problem

As noted by
[
5
,
15
]
, the challenge of showing the superconvergent behavior of ESFR schemes essentially lies in the difficulty of finding an analytical expression for the physical eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}
when
p>2
. In this work, to circumvent this issue, we first introduce
theorem

1
, which connects the order of convergence of
E_{T}
to that of the magnitude of the determinant of a modified von Neumann matrix. We will then proceed to showing the superconvergent properties of this determinant.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
be defined as

\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta):=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\left(\mathbf{D}-\frac{1}{2}i\theta\mathbf{I}\right)+\mathbf{l}\left(\mathbf{l}-\mathbf{r}e^{-i\theta}\right)^{T}.

(22)

Then, as
\theta\to 0
,
E_{T}(\theta)
and
|\text{{det}}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))|
converge to 0 at the same rate provided that the ESFR parameter satisfies
c_{-}<c<\infty
.

Proof.

First, note that by definition
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta)
is related to
\mathbf{H}(\theta)
via

\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{2i}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})(\mathbf{H}(\theta)-\theta\mathbf{I}).

(23)

Hence, we must have

\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))=\left(-\frac{1}{2i}\right)^{p+1}\text{det}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\text{det}(\mathbf{H}(\theta)-\theta\mathbf{I})),

(24)

where
\text{det}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\neq 0
provided that
c
satisfies the prescribed conditions. Moreover, it can readily be seen that

\displaystyle|\text{det}(\mathbf{H}(\theta)-\theta\mathbf{I})|

\displaystyle=|\omega^{h}(\theta)-\theta||\lambda_{1}(\theta)-\theta|\cdots|\lambda_{p}(\theta)-\theta|

(25)

\displaystyle=E_{T}|\lambda_{1}(\theta)-\theta|\cdots|\lambda_{p}(\theta)-\theta|,

(26)

where
\omega^{h}(\theta),\lambda_{1}(\theta),...,\lambda_{p}(\theta)
are the
p+1
eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}(\theta)
. As shown in
appendix

A
, the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\omega^{h}(0)=0
is one provided that
c_{-}<c<\infty
. Additionally, since the entries of
\mathbf{H}
depend continuously on
\theta
, each eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}
is a continuous function of
\theta

[
13
]
and
\omega^{h}
is the unique eigenvalue approaching
0
as
\theta\to 0
. Thus,
\exists C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}|\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))|\leq E_{T}(\theta)\leq C_{2}|\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))|

(27)

for all values of
\theta
sufficiently small.

Remark 2
.

One should note that since the algebraic multiplicity of
\omega^{h}
is one in the vicinity of
\theta=0
, by the implicit function theorem,
\omega^{h}
is
C^{\infty}
at
\theta=0
and thus the order of convergence of
E_{T}
can be safely defined.

3.2
Superconvergence of the Dispersion-Dissipation Error

Based on
theorem

1
, we now proceed to deriving an analytical expression for
|\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))|
. We begin by showing that the convergence behavior of the latter is related to a specific rational approximation of the exponential function, as per
lemma

3
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\psi^{(k)}_{j}(\xi)
denote the
k
th derivative of the
j
th Legendre polynomial evaluated at
\xi
and let
q_{j}
denote the
j
th diagonal entry of
(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}
when expressed in the Legendre basis. The determinant of the modified von Neumann matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}
is given by

\text{{det}}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))=(-1)^{p+1}\text{{det}}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\left(P(i\theta)-Q(i\theta)e^{-i\theta}\right),

(28)

where
P\in\mathcal{P}^{p+1}
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}^{p}
are defined as

\displaystyle P(z)

\displaystyle:=2^{-(p+1)}z^{p+1}-\sum_{j,k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi^{(k)}_{j}(-1)z^{p-k},

(29)

\displaystyle Q(z)

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi^{(k)}_{j}(1)z^{p-k}.

(30)

Proof.

For convenience and without loss of generality, we will assume that
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}
is constructed in the Legendre basis. Leveraging the dyadic product structure of
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}
, we start by applying the well-known matrix determinant lemma to
eq.

22
, which yields

\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))=\text{det}(\mathbf{A}(\theta))\left(1+\mathbf{l}^{T}\mathbf{A}(\theta)^{-1}\mathbf{l}-\mathbf{r}^{T}\mathbf{A}(\theta)^{-1}\mathbf{l}e^{-i\theta}\right),

(31)

where

\mathbf{A}(\theta):=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\left(\mathbf{D}-\frac{1}{2}i\theta\mathbf{I}\right).

(32)

Since
\mathbf{D}
is strictly upper triangular, we can deduce

\text{det}(\mathbf{A}(\theta))=\text{det}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\left(-\frac{1}{2}i\theta\right)^{p+1}.

(33)

To determine
\mathbf{A}(\theta)^{-1}
, we expand it in terms of its von Neumann series. In particular, since
\mathbf{D}
is nilpotent with
\mathbf{D}^{p+1}=0
, the von Neumann series of this operator can be written as the finite sum

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(\theta)^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{2}i\theta\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{D}\right)^{-1}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}

(34)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{p}2^{k+1}(i\theta)^{-(k+1)}\mathbf{D}^{k}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}.

(35)

Combining
eq.

31
and
eq.

35
yields

\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta))=(-1)^{p+1}\text{det}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})\left(2^{-(p+1)}(i\theta)^{p+1}+\mathbf{l}^{T}\mathbf{B}(\theta)\mathbf{l}-\mathbf{r}^{T}\mathbf{B}(\theta)\mathbf{l}e^{-i\theta}\right),

(36)

where

\mathbf{B}(\theta):=\sum_{k=0}^{p}2^{k-p}(i\theta)^{p-k}\mathbf{D}^{k}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}.

(37)

Letting
q_{j}
denote the
j
th diagonal entry of
(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}
we leverage the structure of
\mathbf{B}
and the fact
\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K}
is diagonal to find that

\displaystyle\mathbf{l}^{T}\mathbf{B}(\theta)\mathbf{l}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{p}2^{k-p}(i\theta)^{p-k}\mathbf{l}^{T}\mathbf{D}^{k}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l}

(38)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi^{(k)}_{j}(-1)(i\theta)^{p-k}.

(39)

Similarly, it can be seen that

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}^{T}\mathbf{B}(\theta)\mathbf{l}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{p}2^{k-p}(i\theta)^{p-k}\mathbf{r}^{T}\mathbf{D}^{k}(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l}

(40)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi^{(k)}_{j}(1)(i\theta)^{p-k}.

(41)

From
lemma

3
, one can see that the determinant of the modified von Neumann matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}
will exhibit superconvergence properties as
\theta\to 0
if and only if
P(z)/Q(z)
is a superconvergent rational approximant of
e^{-z}
about
z=0
. To prove the latter, we consider generalizing the work of
[
2
]
. Namely,
[
2
]
has shown a simple connection between Legendre polynomials and
(p,p)
Padé approximants of the exponential function. Here, we extend this approach to
(p+1,p)
Padé approximants and use this to show that the polynomials defined in
eq.

29
and
eq.

30
form a general class of superconvergent rational approximants of
e^{-z}
. Our key findings are summarized in
lemma

4
.

Lemma 4
.

The rational function
P(z)/Q(z)
with
P
and
Q
given by
eq.

29
and
eq.

30
is a superconvergent approximant of
e^{-z}
. Namely,

\displaystyle P(z)-Q(z)e^{-z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}z^{p+1}e^{-z/2}\left(e^{z/2}-\sum_{j=0}^{p}2q_{j}I_{j}(z/2)\right)

(42)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}\left(b_{1}(p,c)z^{2p+1}+b_{2}(p,c)z^{2p+2}\right)+O(z^{2p+3})

(43)

where

\displaystyle b_{1}(p,c)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(1-f(c))p!}{(2p)!},

(44)

\displaystyle b_{2}(p,c)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(f(c)-1)p!}{2(2p)!}+\frac{(p+1)!}{(2p+2)!},

(45)

\displaystyle f(c)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{1+c\frac{(2p+1)(2p!)^{2}}{2^{2p+1}(p!)^{2}}}

(46)

and
I_{j}
stands for the
j
th modified spherical Bessel function of the first kind.

Proof.

We start with the well-known series expansion of the exponential function in the Legendre basis
[
10
]
,

e^{y\cos(\alpha)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2j+1)I_{j}(y)\psi_{j}(\cos(\alpha)),

(47)

where
y\in\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Setting
\alpha=0
in
eq.

47
yields the series expansion

e^{y}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2j+1)I_{j}(y).

(48)

Moreover, letting
x:=\cos(\alpha)
,
eq.

47
can be used to compute the projection of the exponential function on
\psi_{j}

\int_{-1}^{1}e^{yx}\psi_{j}(x)dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)I_{j}(y)\int_{-1}^{1}\psi_{j}(x)\psi_{k}(x)dx=2I_{j}(y),

(49)

from which one can see that

\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-yx}\psi_{j}(x)dx=2I_{j}(-y)=2(-1)^{j}I_{j}(y).

(50)

Following the approach of
[
2
]
, we now consider integrating by parts the left-hand side of
eq.

50

j+1
times, which yields

\left[-e^{-yx}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\psi_{j}^{(k)}(x)}{y^{k+1}}\right]_{-1}^{1}=\left[-e^{-yx}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{\psi_{j}^{(k)}(x)}{y^{k+1}}\right]_{-1}^{1}=2(-1)^{j}I_{j}(y).

(51)

Upon multiplication of both sides of
eq.

51
by
(-1)^{j+1}y^{p+1}q_{j}e^{-y}
, one obtains

-\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}q_{j}\psi_{j}^{(k)}(-1)y^{p-k}+e^{-2y}\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}q_{j}\psi_{j}^{(k)}(1)y^{p-k}=-2q_{j}e^{-y}y^{p+1}I_{j}(y),

(52)

which we then sum from
j=0
to
j=p
to get

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}q_{j}\psi_{j}^{(k)}(-1)y^{p-k}+e^{-2y}\sum_{j=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}q_{j}\psi_{j}^{(k)}(1)y^{p-k}

\displaystyle=-2e^{-z}y^{p+1}\sum_{j=0}^{p}q_{j}I_{j}(y).

(53)

Letting
y=z/2
, adding
2^{-(p+1)}z^{p+1}
and comparing with
eq.

29
and
eq.

30
,
eq.

53
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}z^{p+1}-\sum_{j,k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi_{j}^{(k)}(-1)z^{p-k}+e^{-z}\sum_{j,k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi_{j}^{(k)}(1)z^{p-k}

\displaystyle=P(z)-Q(z)e^{-z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}z^{p+1}e^{-z/2}\left(e^{z/2}-\sum_{j=0}^{p}2q_{j}I_{j}(z/2)\right),

(54)

which completes the proof for
eq.

42
. From
eq.

54
, one can see that the rational approximant
P(z)/Q(z)
is at least
O(z^{p+1})
accurate. To show superconvergence of the approximant, the rightmost factor in
eq.

54
must, however, be studied more closely.

For ESFR schemes, it has been shown that the diagonal entries of
(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}
were given by
[
4
,
28
]

q_{j}=\begin{cases}\frac{2j+1}{2}&\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j<p,\\
\frac{2j+1}{2}f(c)&\quad\text{for}\quad j=p,\end{cases}

(55)

where

f(c):=\frac{1}{1+c\frac{(2p+1)(2p!)^{2}}{2^{2p+1}(p!)^{2}}}.

(56)

Hence, it follows that when
c=0
– which corresponds to a DG scheme – the rightmost factor in
eq.

54
is exactly equal to the error incurred when the series expansion given by
eq.

48
is truncated at the
p
th order. Since the associated truncation error is
O(z^{p+1})
in this case, the rational approximant
P(z)/Q(z)
is superconvergent with order
O(z^{2p+2})
. When
c\neq 0
, one additional term in the partial sum is lost, and the truncation error is now
O(z^{p})
, yielding a superconvergent rational approximant with order
O(z^{2p+1})
.

To obtain an explicit expression for the leading term of the error associated with the rational approximant
P(z)/Q(z)
, we first recall that the
j
th spherical Bessel function of the first kind is defined via the power series

I_{j}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}z\right)^{2k+j}}{k!\Gamma\left(k+j+\frac{3}{2}\right)},

(57)

where
\Gamma
represents the Gamma function. Using
eq.

54
,
eq.

48
and
eq.

57
, one can see that

\displaystyle P(z)-Q(z)e^{-z}=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}z^{p+1}e^{-z/2}\left(e^{z/2}-\sum_{j=0}^{p}2q_{j}I_{j}(z/2)\right)

\displaystyle=(1-f(c))a_{p}(z/2)^{2p+1}+((f(c)-1)a_{p}+a_{p+1})(z/2)^{2p+2}+O(z^{2p+3}),

(58)

where

a_{p}:=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\frac{2p+1}{2^{p}\Gamma\left(p-\frac{3}{2}\right)}.

(59)

Using the properties of the Gamma function, this can be simplified to

a_{p}=\frac{2^{p}p!}{(2p)!}.

(60)

Combining these results, we finally get

P(z)-Q(z)e^{-z}=\left(b_{1}(p,c)z^{2p+1}+b_{2}(p,c)z^{2p+2}\right)+O(z^{2p+3}),

(61)

which completes the proof for
eq.

43
.

As can be seen from
lemma

4
, the DG scheme is of particular interest when considering the class of superconvergent rational approximants associated with ESFR schemes. Namely, when
c=0
,
f(c)=1
and
b_{1}(p,c)=0
, yielding a rational approximant which is truly
O(z^{2p+2})
. In this case,
P(z)/Q(z)
exactly coincides with the
(p+1,p)
Padé approximant of the function
e^{-z}
. This can readily be shown through careful examination of the coefficients of
P(z)
and
Q(z)
or by recalling that the
(m,n)
Padé approximation of a function is the unique rational approximant with a truncation error of order
O(z^{m+n+1})
. As expected, this Padé approximant is identical to that used in the work of
[
3
,
6
,
9
]
to show superconvergent properties of DG schemes.

Combining the results of
lemma

3
,
lemma

4
and
theorem

1
, we are now ready to state the main theoretical result of this paper, which is shown in
theorem

5
.

Theorem 5
.

Consider an arbitrary ESFR scheme with
c_{-}<c<\infty
. Then,
\exists C_{1},C_{2}>0
(dependent on
c
) such that for all
\theta
sufficiently small

C_{1}F(p,c,\theta)\leq E_{T}(\theta)\leq C_{2}F(p,c,\theta),

(62)

where

F(p,c,\theta):=|b_{1}(p,c)(i\theta)^{2p+1}+b_{2}(p,c)(i\theta)^{2p+2}|

(63)

and
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are given by
eq.

44
and
eq.

45
respectively.

Proof.

Theorem

5
follows from direct application of
lemma

3
,
lemma

4
and
theorem

1
.

3.3
Important Remarks

In the previous section, we essentially showed that as
\theta\to 0
, the dispersion-dissipation error of ESFR schemes scaled as per

E_{T}(\theta)\propto|b_{1}(p,c)(i\theta)^{2p+1}+b_{2}(p,c)(i\theta)^{2p+2}|.

(64)

There is a seeming contradiction between this theoretical error estimate and the experimental results of
[
22
]
. Indeed, while
eq.

64
does predict that
A_{T}
should drop from
A_{T}\approx 2p+1
to
A_{T}\approx 2p
as
c
is progressively increased from
c=0
, it does not explain why a subsequent drop from
A_{T}\approx 2p
to
A_{T}\approx 2p-1
is observed at even larger values of
c
.

This apparent contradiction can be resolved by noting that our theoretical error estimate is not valid in the limit as
c\to\infty
. Indeed, as shown in
appendix

A
, when
c\to\infty
,
\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K}
becomes singular and as a result,
\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}})
vanishes. Thus, although the eigenvalue
\omega^{h}(0)=0
has multiplicity one for any finite value of
c
, in the limit, its multiplicity is actually two, which invalidates the assumptions of
theorem

1
. To resolve this issue, we can simply remove the problematic eigenvalue by dividing
\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}})
by the latter. Namely, we write

\displaystyle E_{T}

\displaystyle\propto|\omega^{h}(\theta,c)-\theta||\lambda_{2}(\theta,c)-\theta|\cdots|\lambda_{p}(\theta,c)-\theta|\propto\frac{|\text{det}(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\theta,c))|}{|\lambda_{1}(\theta,c)-\theta|}

\displaystyle\propto\frac{\left|b_{1}(p,c)(i\theta)^{2p+1}+b_{2}(p,c)(i\theta)^{2p+2}\right|}{|\lambda_{1}(\theta,c)-\theta|},

(65)

where
\lambda_{1}
is the unique eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}
with the properties

\forall c\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda_{1}(0,c)\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{c\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(0,c)=0.

(66)

Note that in
eq.

65
and
eq.

66
, the dependence of the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}
on
\theta
and
c
has been explicitly written out for clarity. Careful observation of
eq.

65
leads to interesting conclusions. First, as can be seen, unlike
eq.

64
,
eq.

65
is valid in the limit as
c\to\infty
. Namely, upon computing the limit, one finds that
E_{T}
is of order
O(\theta^{2p})
and thus that
A_{T}=2p-1
, which agrees with the fact that ESFR schemes collapse to a DG scheme of degree
p-1
when
c\to\infty
. It can also be observed that for any finite value of
c
, since the denominator of
eq.

65
does not vanish,
eq.

64
and
eq.

65
are equivalent and the orders of accuracy predicted by
eq.

64
should be regarded as correct.

Equation

65
, however, does provide a simple explanation for the discrepancy observed between our theoretical error estimate and the results of
[
22
]
. Namely, if one attempts to estimate
A_{T}
numerically via
eq.

21
at large values of
c
, it is clear from
eq.

65
that the small magnitude of
\lambda_{1}(c,0)
will make it very difficult to measure experimentally the true asymptotic convergence rate. In particular, if
A_{T}
is not computed at a sufficiently small value of
\Delta\theta
, the denominator of
eq.

65
will behave as if it were
O(\theta)
and the convergence rate will lose one additional order. Given that the values of
\Delta\theta
selected by
[
22
]
were
\pi/8
,
\pi/4
,
\pi/3
and
2\pi/5
when
p
was 2,3,4 and 5, respectively, it appears likely that the convergence rates they measured were not in the asymptotic regime. This was confirmed numerically as shown in the next section.

5
Extension to General Symmetric FR Schemes

Although we have focused on the superconvergent properties of ESFR schemes, the methodology presented in the scope of this work can easily be extended to a very general class of FR schemes with a symmetric correction function. Indeed, as shown in
appendix

B
, provided that a given FR scheme has symmetric correction functions and that the projection of the derivative of the latter on each Legendre polynomial is non-vanishing, one can always find a diagonal matrix
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
such that

\mathbf{h}_{R}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{r}\quad;\quad\mathbf{h}_{L}=-(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l},

(67)

in the Legendre basis. Using the approach from
section

3
, the order of convergence of the dispersion-dissipation error associated with the FR schemes belonging to this class can easily be determined. Namely, we can see that the truncation error associated with the rational approximant
P(z)/Q(z)
will lose one order for every non-zero diagonal entry of the matrix
\mathbf{K}
. Hence, we find that for this subset of symmetric FR schemes,
E_{T}
is
O(\theta^{p+\bar{k}+2})
, where
(\mathbf{K})_{\bar{k}\bar{k}}
is the non-zero diagonal entry of
\mathbf{K}
with the smallest index.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have provided a formal proof for the superconvergent behavior of the dispersion-dissipation error of ESFR schemes for the linear advection problem when using an upwind numerical flux. Using simple analytical tools, we have proven that the superconvergent properties of ESFR schemes rely on the ability of their associated von Neumann matrices to generate superconvergent rational approximants of the exponential function.

Our theoretical work has also clarified the dependency between the ESFR scalar parameter
c
and the spectral order of accuracy
A_{T}
. Namely, we have shown that while the first drop in
A_{T}
which is observed in numerical experiments as
c
is increased can be attributed to a change in the structure of the superconvergent rational approximants, the second drop observed is in fact a numerical artifact stemming from the small magnitude of one of the unphysical eigenvalues in the vicinity of
\theta=0
. Finally, the validity of the theoretical error estimate was successfully confirmed through numerical experiments.

Future work should include the study of other superconvergent properties of ESFR schemes in the context of linear and non-linear problems, as was done for the DG method in the past decades.

Appendix A
Algebraic Multiplicity of Null Eigenvalue

In this work, we have used rather extensively the fact that the physical eigenvalue
\omega^{h}
of the von Neumann matrix has an algebraic multiplicity of one in the vicinity of
\theta=0
. The following provides proof of this fact.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mathbf{H}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
be the von Neumann matrix of an ESFR scheme. Provided that
c_{-}<c<\infty
, the algebraic multiplicity of the null eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}(0)
is one.

Proof.

In what follows, for concision, we will denote
\mathbf{H}(0)
by
\mathbf{H}
. The characteristic polynomial of
\mathbf{H}
is given by

\text{det}(\mathbf{H}-\lambda\mathbf{I})=0.

(68)

Thanks to the similarity in the structure of
eq.

24
and
eq.

68
, we can essentially reuse the proof of
lemma

3
to find the characteristic polynomial of
\mathbf{H}
. Namely, removing the factor
e^{-i\theta}
and replacing
i\theta
by
i\lambda
in the proof of
lemma

3
, one finds that the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}
must satisfy

\displaystyle P(i\lambda)-Q(i\lambda)=2^{-(p+1)}(i\lambda)^{p+1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j,k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi^{(k)}_{j}(-1)(i\lambda)^{p-k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j,k=0}^{p}(-1)^{j}2^{k-p}q_{j}\psi^{(k)}_{j}(1)(i\lambda)^{p-k}=0.

(69)

Reordering the summands in
eq.

69
yields

2^{-(p+1)}(i\lambda)^{p+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{p}c_{k,p}(i\lambda)^{k}=0,

(70)

where

\displaystyle c_{k,p}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^{p}2^{-k}q_{p-j}\left((-1)^{j}\psi^{(p-k)}_{p-j}(1)-(-1)^{j}\psi^{(p-k)}_{p-j}(-1)\right)

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}2^{-k}q_{p-j}\left((-1)^{j}\psi^{(p-k)}_{p-j}(1)-(-1)^{j}\psi^{(p-k)}_{p-j}(-1)\right).

(72)

To prove that the null eigenvalue of
\mathbf{H}
has an algebraic multiplicity of one, it suffices to show that
c_{0,p}=0
and
c_{1,p}\neq 0
. Since even Legendre polynomials are symmetric and odd Legendre polynomials are anti-symmetric, it follows that

\psi^{(m)}_{n}(1)=(-1)^{m+n}\psi^{(m)}_{n}(-1).

(73)

Applying
eq.

73
to
eq.

72
, one obtains

c_{k,p}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\left((-1)^{j}-(-1)^{k}\right)2^{-k}q_{p-j}\psi^{(p-k)}_{p-j}(1).

(74)

From
eq.

74
, it is clear that

c_{0,p}=(-1)^{p}\left(1-1\right)2^{0}q_{p}\psi^{(p)}_{p}(1)=0.

(75)

Similarly,

c_{1,p}=(-1)^{p}\left(1+1\right)2^{-1}q_{p}\psi^{(p-1)}_{p}(1)=\frac{(-1)^{p}(2p+1)\psi^{(p-1)}_{p}(1)}{2}f(c).

(76)

Since
f(c)\neq 0
as long as
c_{-}<c<\infty
,
c_{1,p}\neq 0
. Moreover, when
c\to\infty
,
f(c)\to 0
and the algebraic multiplicity of the null eigenvalue is increased, as expected.

Appendix B
Symmetric FR Schemes

In the following, we show a simple property of symmetric FR schemes which is very useful when attempting to generalize the work presented in this paper.

Lemma 7
.

For all symmetric FR schemes of degree
p
with correction functions such that

\forall_{j=0}^{p}:\int_{-1}^{1}g_{R}^{\prime}(\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)d\xi\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{-1}^{1}g_{L}^{\prime}(\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)d\xi\neq 0,

(77)

there exists a diagonal matrix
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
such that in the Legendre basis, the derivatives of the correction functions are given by

\mathbf{h}_{R}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{r}\quad;\quad\mathbf{h}_{L}=-(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l}.

(78)

Proof.

First, we recall that an FR scheme is said to be symmetric if and only if

g_{L}(\xi)=g_{R}(-\xi).

(79)

As noted by
[
24
]
, in the Legendre basis, this is equivalent to requiring

(\mathbf{h}_{L})_{j}=(-1)^{j+1}(\mathbf{h}_{R})_{j}.

(80)

Suppose that we are given
\mathbf{h}_{R}
for a particular symmetric FR scheme satisfying
eq.

77
. Then, it is clear that defining the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
as

(\mathbf{K})_{jj}:=1/(\mathbf{h_{R}})_{j}-(\mathbf{M})_{jj}

(81)

ensures that

\mathbf{h}_{R}=(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{r}

(82)

in the Legendre basis;
eq.

77
is required so that
eq.

82
is well-defined. Moreover, in the Legendre basis using this choice of
\mathbf{K}
, we can see that

-((\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1}\mathbf{l})_{j}=-(-1)^{j}((\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{K})^{-1})_{j}=(-1)^{j+1}(\mathbf{h}_{R})_{j}=(\mathbf{h}_{L})_{j}.

(83)

Hence, we have found a diagonal matrix
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
satisfying the required conditions.

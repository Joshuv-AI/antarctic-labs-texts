Convex body domination for Rough singular integrals
Aapo Laukkarinen

Abstract
. Convex body domination is a technique, where operators acting on vector-valued functions are estimated via certain convex body averages of the input functions. This domination lets one deduce various matrix weighted bounds for these operators and their commutators. In this paper, we extend the sparse domination results for rough singular integrals due to Conde-Alonso, Culiuc, Di Plinio and Ou to the convex body setting. In particular, our methods apply to homogeneous rough singular integrals with unbounded angular part. We also note that convex body domination implies new two weight commutator bounds even in the scalar case.

Introduction

For many operators acting on scalar functions, the technique of dominating the operator via averages taken over sparse collections of cubes yields sharp weighted estimates for the operator. In the vector case the weights can be matrix-valued, and thus in order to capture information about the direction of the input functions one must replace the averages with convex sets. This domination via convex sets is called convex body domination and it was initiated by Nazarov, Petermichl, Treil and Volberg in
[
17
]
. An exciting development appeared very recently in
[

, where it was shown that the matrix weighted
A_{2}
bound in
[

]
for general Calderón-Zygmund operators, which was achieved using convex body domination, is sharp.
Additional convex body domination results can be found, for instance, in

,

In this paper, we expand the convex body domination theory by showing convex body estimates for some rough singular integrals. The main examples are rough homogeneous singular integrals

T_{\Omega}f(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|y|>\varepsilon}f(x-y)\Omega(\frac{y}{|y|})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d}},\quad\Omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}),\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\Omega=0,

and the Bochner-Riesz mean at the critical index that is defined on the frequency side by

\widehat{B_{\delta}f}(\xi)=(1-|\xi|^{2})_{+}^{\delta}\widehat{f}(\xi),\qquad\delta=\frac{d-1}{2}.

In particular, we show that
T_{\Omega}
and
B_{\delta}
both satisfy
(L^{1},L^{p})
and
(L^{p},L^{1})
convex body domination bounds. See Theorem
5.3
, Corollary
5.4
and Theorem
8.2
for the exact statements. The case
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
is a known result due to
[

, but otherwise these bounds are new.

Our results are vector-valued extensions of the results achieved by Conde-Alonso, Culiuc, Di Plinio and Ou in
[
3

, and our method of proof is an adaptation of their techniques. We will study reasonably general sesquilinear forms

\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(f,g)=\sum_{\mu<s\leq\nu}\int K_{s}(x,y)f(y)\bar{g}(x)\,\mathrm{d}(y,x),

and prove a convex body domination principle for
\sup_{\mu<\nu}|\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(f,g)|
. See Theorem
4.3
for more details. The assumptions on the sesquilinear form are scalar valued so the fact that
T_{\Omega}
and
B_{\delta}
fall under the umbrella of this general principle follows directly from the considerations in
[

In the case of
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})

2.
Sesquilinear forms and stopping collections

We follow the setting from
[
3
]
. Let
1<r<\infty
and
\Lambda
be an
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-bounded sesquilinear form whose kernel
K=K(x,y)
coincides with a function away from the diagonal. More
precisely, whenever
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are compactly and disjointly supported

\Lambda(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x,y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\bar{g}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

with absolute convergence of the integral.

Remark 2.1
.

In contrast to
[
3
]
we define
\Lambda
as a sesquilinear (instead of bilinear) form in order to have a basis-independent extension for vector-valued functions (see Section
3
). This rarely plays a role in calculations.

We assume that
the kernel
K
of
\Lambda
admits the decomposition

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{s}(x,y),

where each piece is supported in an annulus away from the diagonal, i.e.,

(S)

\operatorname{supp}K_{s}\subset\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\colon\,x-y\in A_{s}\},

where

A_{s}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\colon\,2^{s-2}<|z|<2^{s}\}.

Furthermore, we assume that there exists
1<q\leq\infty
such that

[K]_{q}\coloneqq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\frac{sd}{q^{\prime}}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\|K_{s}(x,\cdot)\|_{q}+\|K_{s}(\cdot,x)\|_{q})<\infty,

and the associated truncated forms

\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(h_{1},h_{2})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mu<s\leq\nu}K_{s}(x,y)h_{1}(y)\bar{h}_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

satisfy

(T)

C_{T}(r)\coloneqq\sup_{\mu<\nu}\|\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{C}}<\infty.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be a dyadic lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We fix a dyadic cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}
and denote
s_{Q}\coloneqq\log_{2}\ell(Q)
. A collection
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathscr{D}
of dyadic cubes is called a stopping collection with top
Q
if the elements of
\mathcal{Q}
are pairwise disjoint, contained in
3Q
and enjoy the separation properties

(1)

L,L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q},\,|s_{L}-s_{L^{\prime}}|\geq 8\quad\Rightarrow\quad 7L\cap 7L^{\prime}=\emptyset

and

(2)

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
3L\cap 2Q\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}9L\subset\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}L\eqqcolon\mathbf{sh}\mathcal{Q}.

We also define the local sesquilinear forms by

\Lambda_{Q,\mu,\nu}(h_{1},h_{2})\coloneqq\Lambda_{\mu}^{\min\{s_{Q},\nu\}}(h_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q},h_{2})=\Lambda_{\mu}^{\min\{s_{Q},\nu\}}(h_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q},h_{2}\mathbbm{1}_{3Q})

for all dyadic cubes
Q
. The last equality is true due to the support condition (
S
). Furthermore, given a stopping collection
\mathcal{Q}
with top
Q
, we define

\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h_{1},h_{2})\coloneqq\Lambda_{Q,\mu,\nu}(h_{1},h_{2})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
L\subset Q\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}(h_{1},h_{2})=\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q},h_{2}\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}).

Again, the last equality is due to (
S
). Note that
\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime},\mu,\nu}
, where
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{L\in\mathcal{Q}\,\colon\,L\subset Q\}
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, define
\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
to be the subspace of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of functions
h
that are supported in
3Q
and the quantity

\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\max\left\{\|h\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbf{sh}\mathcal{Q}}\|_{\infty},\,\displaystyle{\sup_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}\inf_{x\in\widehat{L}}}M_{p}h(x)\right\},\quad&p<\infty\\
\|h\|_{\infty},&p=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\widehat{L}
is the
2^{5}
-fold dilate of
L
, is finite. We denote
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
the subspace of
\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
of functions satisfying

b=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}b_{L},\quad\operatorname{supp}b_{L}\subset L.

We note that the above condition is equivalent to
\operatorname{supp}b\subset\mathbf{sh}\mathcal{Q}
.
We also write
b\in\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
if
b\in\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})
and

\int_{L}b_{L}=0\quad\text{ for all }L\in\mathcal{Q}.

We make a standing assumption that for some
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
there exists a constant
C_{L}
such that

(L1)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(b,h)|\leq C_{L}\,|Q|\,\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{Q})}\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{Q})},

(L2)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h,b)|\leq C_{L}\,|Q|\,\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{Q})}

hold uniformly over all
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
, all dyadic lattices
\mathscr{D}
, all
Q\in\mathscr{D}
and all stopping collections

\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathscr{D}
with top
Q
. Note that conditions (
L1
) and (
L2
) are not symmetric.

In order to verify (
L1
) and (
L2
) in concrete situations, the following representations of the sesquilinear forms
\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}
will turn out to be helpful. These representations are sesquilinear form versions of
[
3
, Equations (2-9)–(2-11)]
, and we will formulate them in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
K
satisfies (
S
) and (
2
) with
1<q\leq\infty
. If
b\in\mathcal{X}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})
with
\operatorname{supp}b\subset Q
, then

\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(b,h)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\int\sum_{\mu<s\leq\min\{s_{Q},\nu\}}K_{s}(x,y)b_{s-j}(y)\bar{h}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

b_{s}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
s_{L}=s\end{subarray}}b_{L}.

If
h\in\mathcal{Y}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})
with
\operatorname{supp}h\subset Q
, and
b\in\mathcal{X}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})
, then

\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h,b)=\overline{\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(b^{\text{in}},h)}+V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b),

where
\Lambda^{*}
is like
\Lambda
, but with
\overline{K(y,x)}
in place of
K(x,y)
, the function
b^{\text{in}}
is a certain truncation of
b
that satisfies
\|b^{\text{in}}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\leq\|b\|_{\mathcal{X}^{p}(\mathcal{Q})}
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty

and the remainder term
V_{\mathcal{Q}}
satisfies

|V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b)|\leq 2^{\vartheta d}[K]_{q}|Q|\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\mathcal{X}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}

for a suitable absolute constant
\vartheta
.

Proof.

We will first deal with (
2.2
). Note that for
b\in\mathcal{X}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})
with
\operatorname{supp}b\subset Q
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b,h)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{Q,\nu,\mu}(b,h)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
L\subset Q\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{L,\nu,\mu}(b,h)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{Q,\nu,\mu}\Big(\sum_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}b_{L},h\Big)-\sum_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}\Lambda_{L,\nu,\mu}(b_{L},h).

Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a disjoint collection, we may bring the first sum outside the integral, which yields

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b,h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}\Bigg(\int\sum_{\mu<s\leq\min\{s_{Q},\nu\}}K_{s}(x,y)b_{L}(y)\bar{h}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int\sum_{\mu<s\leq\min\{s_{L},\nu\}}K_{s}(x,y)b_{L}(y)\bar{h}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in\mathcal{Q}}\int\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu<s\leq\min\{s_{Q},\nu\}\\
s>s_{L}\end{subarray}}K_{s}(x,y)b_{L}(y)\bar{h}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{\mu<s\leq\min\{s_{Q},\nu\}}K_{s}(x,y)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
s_{L}<s\end{subarray}}b_{L}(y)\bar{h}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now the wanted representation (
2.2
) follows since

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
s_{L}<s\end{subarray}}b_{L}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{Q}\\
s_{L}=s-j\end{subarray}}b_{L}=\sum_{j\geq 1}b_{s-j},

and the functions
b_{s}
are disjointly supported so we can bring the sum outside the integral.

The second representation (
2.2
)–(
2.2
) was stated in
[
3
, Remark 2.6]
and proved in
[
3
, Appendix A]
for the bilinear forms
\Lambda^{\text{bi}}(b,h)\coloneqq\Lambda(b,\bar{h})
. More precisely, they showed that

\Lambda^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h,b)=\tilde{\Lambda}^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(b^{\text{in}},h)+V^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b),

where
\tilde{\Lambda}^{\text{bi}}
is like
\Lambda^{\text{bi}}
, but with
K(y,x)
in place of
K(x,y)
, the function
b^{\text{in}}
is as in the claim of this Lemma, and the remainder term satisfies

|V_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\text{bi}}(h,b)|\leq 2^{\vartheta d}[K]_{q}|Q|\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\mathcal{X}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}.

Applying this result and setting
V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b)\coloneqq V^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,\bar{b})
we get

\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h,b)=\Lambda^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h,\bar{b})=\tilde{\Lambda}^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}((\bar{b})^{\text{in}},h)+V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b).

We observe from
[
3
, page 1263]
that the function
b^{\text{in}}
satisfies
(\bar{b})^{\text{in}}=\overline{b^{\text{in}}}
.
Then using (
2.2
) it is simple to check that

\tilde{\Lambda}^{\text{bi}}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(\overline{b^{\text{in}}},h)=\overline{\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(b^{\text{in}},h)}.

This proves (
2.2
), and
substituting
\bar{b}
for
b
in (
2
) we see that (
2.2
) also holds.

3.
Complex convex bodies

In this Section we gather the necessary definitions and results from
[
8
]
that are needed to perform convex body domination. In particular, we will need complex normed space versions of these results. We do not claim any originality for the results or the setting in this Section, and even the translation of language from real to complex has been done before in more detail, see
[
5
, Appendix A]
and
[
11
, Section 3.1]
.

For the entirety of this Section we assume that
X,Y
are complex normed spaces and
\vec{x}\coloneqq(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n},\vec{y}\coloneqq(y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are elements of
X^{n},Y^{n}
respectively. First we extend the standard Hermitian dot product of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in a natural way to
X^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n},Y^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In other words, if
Z
is a complex normed space,
\vec{z}\in Z^{n}
and
\vec{v}=(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then we write

\vec{z}\cdot\vec{v}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\bar{v}_{i}.

Then we extend a sesquilinear form
t\,\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{C}
to
X^{n}\times Y^{n}
by

t(\vec{x},\vec{y})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}t(\vec{x}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\vec{y}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}),

where
\{\vec{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
It is important to note that we have used a sesquilinear form instead of a bilinear form in order to have an extension that is independent of the choice of basis. Furthermore, if
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is a linear transformation, then

t(A\vec{x},\vec{y})=t(\vec{x},A^{*}\vec{y}).

The convex bodies are defined by

\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\coloneqq\{\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle_{(X,X^{*})}\,\colon\,x^{*}\in\bar{B}_{X^{*}}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},

where
X^{*}
is the dual space of
X
and
\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle_{(X,X^{*})}\coloneqq(\langle x_{i},x^{*}\rangle_{(X,X^{*})})_{i=1}^{n}
.
We say that a set
K\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is complex-symmetric around the origin, if for any
\vec{k}\in K
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|=1
it holds that
\lambda\vec{k}\in K
.
The set
\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
is compact, convex and complex-symmetric around the origin, and it follows that
the Minkovski dot product

\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\cdot\llangle\vec{y}\rrangle_{Y}=\{a\cdot b\,\colon\,a\in\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X},\,b\in\llangle\vec{y}\rrangle_{Y}\}\subset\mathbb{C}

is also compact, convex and complex-symmetric around the origin. This means that for some number
c\in[0,\infty)
the above set is equal to a closed disk with radius
c
, and we may identify the whole set with
c
.

We end this Section with the main tool that we will use to transition from norm bounds to convex body bounds. The following is the complex normed space version of
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
. For a proof in the complex case see
[
12
, Lemma C]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{x}
be the John ellipsoid of
\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{x}\subset\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\subset\sqrt{n}\mathcal{E}_{x},

and suppose that
\mathcal{E}_{x}
is non-degenerate (i.e. of full dimension). Let
R_{x}
be a linear transformation such that
R_{x}\mathcal{E}_{x}=\bar{B}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
and let
(\vec{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If

x_{i}\coloneqq R_{x}\vec{x}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\quad y_{i}\coloneqq({R_{x}}^{*})^{-1}\vec{y}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\|_{X}\|y_{i}\|_{Y}\leq n^{\frac{3}{2}}\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\cdot\llangle\vec{y}\rrangle_{Y}.

4.
An abstract convex body domination principle

For this section we will suppose that
\Lambda\colon L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded sesquilinear form that satisfies (
S
), (
T
), (
L1
) and (
L2
) with parameters
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
. We note that the kernel size condition (
2
) will not be explicitly needed for the convex body domination principle proven in this section. However, in many applications, Lemma
2.2
is essential for checking the conditions (
L1
) and (
L2
), and Lemma
2.2
does assume (
2
).

We will now strive to show a convex body domination principle for
\Lambda
. The proof is based on techniques in
[
3
]
coupled with the convex body domination theory from
[
8
]
that we discussed in the previous section. The following constant will appear throughout this section. For some absolute constant
\Theta
we define

(7)

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}\coloneqq 2^{\Theta d}[C_{T}(r)+C_{L}]n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}}.

The following result is contained in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
Q
be a dyadic cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
and
\mathcal{Q}
be a stopping collection with top
Q
.
Then there exists an absolute constant
\vartheta
such that

|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\mu,\nu}(h_{1},h_{2})|\leq 2^{\vartheta d}\mathbf{C}|Q|\|h_{1}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{Q})}\|h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{Q})},

where
\mathbf{C}\coloneqq C_{T}(r)+C_{L}
.

We will now prove the following Proposition, which is a convex body version of
[
3
, Equation (2-20)]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
Q_{0}
be a dyadic cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
. Then there exists absolute constants
\vartheta,\Theta
and a stopping collection
\mathcal{S}_{1}
with top
Q_{0}
such that

(8)

\sum_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}|L|\leq 2^{-\vartheta d}|Q_{0}|

and

(9)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}|Q_{0}|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\cdot

\displaystyle\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3Q_{0})}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\
L\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}|\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|,

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}
is as in (
7
).

Proof.

We begin the proof by noticing that we may assume that the John ellipsoid of
\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}
is non-degenerate. Indeed, let
P
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto the linear span of
\llangle f\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0})}
that we denote by
H
. Note that now we have
\vec{f}=P\vec{f}=P^{*}P\vec{f}
almost everywhere, which means that on the left-hand side of (
9
) we have

\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})=\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(P^{*}P\vec{f},\vec{g})=\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(P\vec{f},P\vec{g}),

and on the right-hand side the same identity for
\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}
and

\displaystyle\llangle P\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\cdot\llangle P\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3Q_{0})}

\displaystyle=\llangle P^{*}P\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3Q_{0})}

\displaystyle=\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3Q_{0})}.

Thus if suffices to prove the statement with
\vec{f},\vec{g}
taking values in
H
, and the John ellipsoid of
\llangle f\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0})}
is non-degenerate as a subset of
H
.

For each cube
Q
, we will consider the linear transformation
R_{Q}
that maps the John ellipsoid of the convex body
\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q)}
to the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We fix an orthonormal basis
(\vec{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
write
f_{i}^{Q}\coloneqq R_{Q}\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}
and
g_{i}^{Q}\coloneqq({R_{Q}}^{*})^{-1}\vec{g}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}
.

We also define the sets

E_{Q,f,i}\coloneqq\{x\in 3Q\,\colon\,\frac{M_{p_{1}}(f_{i}^{Q}\mathbbm{1}_{3Q})(x)}{\|f^{Q}_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q)}}>(2^{\frac{\Theta d}{4}}n)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\},

E_{Q,g,i}\coloneqq\{x\in 3Q\,\colon\,\frac{M_{p_{2}}(g_{i}^{Q}\mathbbm{1}_{3Q})(x)}{\|g_{i}^{Q}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3Q)}}>(2^{\frac{\Theta d}{4}}n)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\},

E_{Q,i}\coloneqq E_{Q,f,i}\cup E_{Q,g,i}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{Q}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{Q,i}.

To simplify notation we will write
f_{i}\coloneqq f_{i}^{Q_{0}}
and
g_{i}\coloneqq g_{i}^{Q_{0}}
during this proof. Notice that due to the weak-type boundedness of the maximal function, provided that
\Theta
is sufficiently large, we have

|E_{Q}|\leq 2^{-\vartheta d}|Q|,

where
\vartheta
is the absolute constant from Lemma
4.1
.

Then we set
E_{0}\coloneqq{3Q_{0}}
,
\mathcal{S}_{0}\coloneqq\{Q_{0}\}
,

E_{1}\coloneqq E_{Q_{0}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{S}_{1}\coloneqq\text{maximal cubes $L\in\mathscr{D}$ such that $9L\subset E_{1}$}.

Now
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a pairwise disjoint collection, and the following properties are satisfied:

(11)

\displaystyle E_{1}=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}L=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}9L\subset E_{0},

(12)

\displaystyle|Q_{0}\setminus E_{1}|\geq(1-2^{-d\vartheta})|Q_{0}|,

(13)

\displaystyle L,L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\,7L\cap 7L^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\quad\Rightarrow\quad|s_{L}-s_{L^{\prime}}|<8.

Indeed, disjointness and (
11
) are true by construction, and (
12
) follows from (
4
). For (
13
) suppose instead that
7L\cap 7L^{\prime}

is not empty for some

s_{L}\leq s_{L^{\prime}}-8
. We recall that
\widehat{L}
is the
2^{5}
-fold dilate of
L
. It is not hard to check that
\widehat{L}\subset 9L^{\prime}
, which implies
that the
9
-fold dilate of the dyadic parent of
L
is contained in
9L^{\prime}

as well, contradicting the
maximality of
L
.

From these properties it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a stopping collection with top
Q_{0}
, and the rest of the proof is dedicated to checking that this collection satisfies (
8
) and (
9
).

Due to (
11
) and (
4
), we have

\sum_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}|L|=|E_{1}|=|E_{Q_{0}}|\leq 2^{-\vartheta d}|Q_{0}|,

which is exactly (
8
).

In order to check (
9
) we apply Lemma
4.1
to get

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\
L\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mu,\nu}(f_{i},g_{i})|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\
L\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}|\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\vartheta d}\mathbf{C}|Q_{0}|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})}\|g_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\\
L\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}|\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|,

where
\mathbf{C}\coloneqq C_{T}(r)+C_{L}
. By (
11
) we have that
\mathbf{sh}\mathcal{S}_{1}=\bigcup_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}L=E_{1}=E_{Q_{0}}
so we get

\sup_{x\notin\mathbf{sh}\mathcal{S}_{1}}|f_{i}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{3Q_{0}}(x)|\leq(2^{\frac{\Theta d}{4}}n)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

From the maximality of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
we get that if
L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, then the dyadic parent
P(L)
of
L
satisfies
9P(L)\not\subset E_{1}=E_{Q_{0}}
, and hence

\displaystyle\sup_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\inf_{x\in\widehat{L}}M_{p_{1}}(f_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{3Q_{0}})(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\inf_{x\in 9P(L)}M_{p_{1}}(f_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{3Q_{0}})(x)

\displaystyle\leq(2^{\frac{\Theta d}{4}}n)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})},\qquad\quad i=1,\dots,n.

Similarly, the above estimates hold with
(g_{i},p_{2})
instead of
(f_{i},p_{1})
. Since
p_{1},p_{2}\geq 1
, these estimates imply that

\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})}\|g_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})}\leq 2^{\frac{\Theta d}{2}}n^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\|g_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}.

Now we apply Lemma
3.1
to conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})}\|g_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p_{2}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{\Theta d}{2}}n^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\|g_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{\Theta d}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3Q_{0})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3Q_{0})}.

∎

The above Proposition provides the base of the induction that paves the way for the abstract convex body domination principle that we formulate in the following Theorem. It is the convex body counterpart of
[
3
, Theorem C]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
, and let
\Lambda
be a sesquilinear form satisfying (
S
), (
T
), (
L1
) and (
L2
). Then there exists a positive absolute constant
\Theta
such that
\Lambda
satisfies convex body domination. More explicitly, for all
\vec{f}\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
with compact support, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that we have

\sup_{\mu<\nu}|\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}
is the constant from (
7
).

Proof.

We begin by showing that there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, dependent on
\nu,\mu
, the input functions and fixed parameters, such that

|\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

and at the end of the proof discuss how to get rid of the dependence on
\nu,\mu
.

Fix
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
and functions
\vec{f}\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
with compact support. We may assume that
K_{s}=0
, if
s\notin(\mu,\nu]
. We choose the dyadic lattice
\mathscr{D}
so that any compact set is contained in some dyadic cube in
\mathscr{D}
. Then there exists
Q_{0}\in\mathscr{D}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\vec{f}\subset Q_{0}
and
s_{Q_{0}}
is bigger
than the largest nonzero scale occurring in the kernel. Thus it suffices to show (
4
) with
\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}
in place of
\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}
.

We prove by induction that, for each integer
k\geq 0
,

(15)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}^{\prime}}|S|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3S)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3S)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{L\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{\prime}}|\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|,

where each
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{l}
is a pairwise disjoint collection of dyadic subcubes of
Q_{0}
such that

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}\coloneqq\{Q_{0}\},\quad\mathcal{S}_{l+1}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{l}^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Q),

and further each
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Q)
is a pairwise disjoint collection of dyadic subcubes of
Q
such that

\sum_{R\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Q)}|R|\leq 2^{-\vartheta d}|Q|.

The base case
k=0
is provided by Proposition
4.2
by setting

\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Q_{0})\coloneqq\{L\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\,\colon\,L\subset Q_{0}\},

where
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is the stopping collection produced by Proposition
4.2
.

If we assume the induction claim for some
k\geq 0
, the case
k+1
follows immediately by applying the base step (i.e., Proposition
4.2
), to each
L\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{k+1}
. Thus the inductive claim is verified for all
k
, and we are done with the proof of (
15
).

Next we argue that after sufficiently many iterations the second term on the right-hand side of (
15
) will vanish.
Note that each
R\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Q)
is necessarily a strict dyadic subcube of
Q
. Hence
each
Q\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{k}
is at least
k
generations smaller than
Q_{0}
. Thus if
L\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{k+1}
and
k\geq s_{Q_{0}}-\mu
, then
s_{L}<s_{Q_{0}}-k\leq\mu
and hence
\Lambda_{L,\mu,\nu}=0
.

We see that from (
15
) it follows that

|\Lambda_{Q_{0},\mu,\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}^{\prime}}|S|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(3S)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(3S)}

provided
k\geq 0
is large enough. Thus we obtain (
4
) with the sparse collection

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{l=0}^{k}\{3Q\,\colon\,Q\in\mathcal{S}_{l}^{\prime}\}.

Indeed, for any
3Q\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the pairwise disjoint subsets

E(Q)\coloneqq Q\setminus\bigcup_{R\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Q)}R

satisfy
|E(Q)|\geq(1-2^{-\vartheta d})|Q|=3^{-d}(1-2^{-\vartheta d})|3Q|
.

To get rid of the dependence on
\mu,\nu
we note that (
4
) implies that

\sup_{\mu<\nu}|\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

where the supremum on the right-hand side is taken over all sparse collections. This implies that there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
, depending only on the input functions and fixed parameters, such that

\sup_{\mu<\nu}|\Lambda_{\mu}^{\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq 2\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

and the proof is done.
∎

Similarly as in the scalar case we are able to extend the result of Theorem
4.3
to the full sesquilinear form
\Lambda\colon L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{C}
. The strategy of the argument is the same as in the scalar case
[
3
]
.
Due to the limiting argument from
[
20
, Chapter I Paragraph 7.2]
we have

(16)

\Lambda(f,g)=\langle mf,g\rangle+\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\Lambda^{\nu}_{-\nu}(f,g)

for some
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, whenever
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This identity easily extends to the vector valued setting. Indeed, let
\{\vec{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and calculate

\displaystyle\Lambda(\vec{f},\vec{g})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(\vec{f}\cdot e_{i},\vec{g}\cdot\vec{e}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\langle m\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\vec{g}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}\rangle+\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\Lambda^{\nu}_{-\nu}(\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\vec{g}\cdot\vec{e}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\langle m\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle+\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\Lambda^{\nu}_{-\nu}(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

The operator
Tf\coloneqq mf
is clearly weak type
(1,1)
and the bi-sublinear maximal operator

M_{T}(f,g)\coloneqq\sup_{Q\ni x}\fint_{Q}|T(\mathbbm{1}_{(3Q)^{\complement}}f)||g|

vanishes, and hence by
[
16
, Theorem 11]
there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
of cubes such that

\langle m\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle\leq c_{n,d}\|m\|_{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}.

These considerations show that with the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
, for all
\vec{f}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
with compact support, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes such that

|\Lambda(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

where
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq c_{d,n}\|m\|_{\infty}+\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}
. To extend this bound to all
\vec{f}\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, we need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then we have

\llangle\mathbbm{1}_{S_{1}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(S_{2})}\subset\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(S_{2})}.

Proof.

Suppose
a\in\llangle\mathbbm{1}_{S_{1}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(S_{2})}
. Then for some
\phi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{p^{\prime}}(S_{2})}
we have

a=\fint_{S_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{S_{1}}\vec{f}\,\bar{\phi}.

So by defining
\psi\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{S_{1}}\phi
we get

\|\psi\|_{\textit{\L}^{p^{\prime}}(S_{2})}\leq\|\phi\|_{\textit{\L}^{p^{\prime}}(S_{2})}\leq 1.

Thus we have

a=\fint_{S_{2}}\vec{f}\,\bar{\psi}\in\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(S_{2})}.

∎

Now are ready to show the following result.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2},\Theta
and
\Lambda
be as in Theorem
4.3
. Additionally, we assume that
\Lambda
extends boundedly to
L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
1<t<\infty
. Then for all
\vec{f}\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes such that

|\Lambda(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

where
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq c_{d,n}\|m\|_{\infty}+\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}
with
m\in L^{\infty}
defined by (
16
), and
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta,d,n}
is defined by (
7
).

Proof.

Let
\{Q_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of cubes such that
\cup_{\ell=1}^{\infty}Q_{\ell}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and define

E_{\ell}\coloneqq\{x\in Q_{\ell}\,\colon\,\max\{|\vec{f}(x)|,|\vec{g}(x)|\}\leq\ell\}.

Since
\Lambda
extends boundedly to
L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we may apply the dominated convergence theorem to get

\displaystyle|\Lambda(\vec{f},\vec{g})|

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}|\Lambda(\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{f},\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{g})|.

Then the convex body domination for compactly supported bounded functions implies that there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
such that

\displaystyle|\Lambda(\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{f},\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}}|Q|\llangle\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)}

Due to Lemma
4.4
we have

\llangle\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\ell}}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)}\leq\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)}.

Thus it follows that

|\Lambda(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq\mathcal{C}\,\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{1}}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p_{2}}(Q)},

which implies the wanted inequality by the same argument as in the end of the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

5.
Rough homogeneous singular integrals

The main motivation for the abstract convex body domination principle from the last section is provided by rough singular integrals. In this section we will study the following rough homogeneous singular integrals. Let
1<q\leq\infty
. For
\Omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with vanishing integral, we define

T_{\Omega}f(x)\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|y|>\varepsilon}f(x-y)\Omega\left(\frac{y}{|y|}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d}}.

Our goal is to apply Theorem
4.3
(in the form of Corollary
4.5
) to the sesquilinear form
\Lambda
related to
T_{\Omega}
. Notice that the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
are scalar valued so we may directly use the results proven in
[
3
]
to get convex body domination for
T_{\Omega}
. We will include some details for clarity.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a stopping collection with top
Q
. We see that if
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are compactly and disjointly supported, then the sesquilinear form
\Lambda(f,g)\coloneqq\langle T_{\Omega}f,g\rangle
satisfies

\Lambda(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x,y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\bar{g}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
K(x,y)\coloneqq\Omega\left(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\right)\left/\,|x-y|^{d}\right.
. Away from the diagonal
x=y
, the kernel
K
admits the decomposition

K=\sum_{s}K_{s},\qquad K_{s}(x,y)\coloneqq\Omega\left(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\right)2^{-sd}\phi(2^{-s}(x-y)),

where
\phi
is a suitable radial smooth function supported in
A_{0}=\{2^{-2}\leq|x|\leq 1\}
. We will also need the following quasi-norm

\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\lambda>0\,\colon\,\left[\Omega/\lambda\right]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\leq 1\right\},

where

[\Omega]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\coloneqq q\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(e+t)|\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\,\colon\,|\Omega(\theta)|>t\}|^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note that in
[
3
]
the quantity (
5
) is denoted by
\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
. We give this notation to (
5
) on the account of it being homogeneous while (
5
) is not.

The following result, which follows from
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
, is key in verifying that the kernel
K
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with zero average and
b\in\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})
.
Then there exists an absolute constant
C
such that

|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b,h)|\leq Cp^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\,|Q|\,\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})}\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})},

where

\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1+[\Omega]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},\,&q<\infty,\,q^{\prime}\leq p<\infty,\\
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},&q=\infty,\,1<p<\infty.\end{cases}

The above result is very close to
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
. The left-hand side of the inequality (
5.1
) is written in a different way, but in
[
3
]
they explain on the second paragraph after Proposition 4.1 how one can estimate
|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b,h)|
with their Proposition 4.1 and representation (
2.2
). Another difference is that in
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
the function
\Omega
has unit norm in
L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, which we do not assume here. This explains the different term depending on
\Omega
, which arises from the calculation below
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
. With these changes the proof of Proposition
5.1
is identical to the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
.

We will now use Proposition
5.1
to show that the sesquilinear form
\Lambda(f,g)=\langle T_{\Omega}f,g\rangle
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Omega
be as in Proposition
5.1
. The kernel (
5
) satisfies conditions (
S
), (
2
) with
[K]_{q}\leq[\Omega]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
, (
T
) for
r=2
with
C_{T}(2)\leq C_{d}[\Omega]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
and (
L1
), (
L2
) for
(p_{1},p_{2})=(1,p)
, where the range of
p
depends on
q
in the same way as in Proposition
5.1
, with constant
C_{L}=C_{d}p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The assumptions
(
S
)
,
(
2
)
and
(
T
)
are quick to check for
q\in\left]1,\infty\right]
and
r=2
. Indeed, the support condition (
S
) follows from the support of
\phi
, and

[K]_{q}\leq\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\leq[\Omega]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}

follows immediately from definitions, so (
2
) is true. The boundedness condition (
T
) for
r=2
states that the truncations of
T_{\Omega}
are
L^{2}
-bounded, which follows from a fundamental result
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

It remains to verify (
L1
) and (
L2
) with
p_{1}=1
and
p_{2}=p
, where the range of
p
depends on
q
in the same way as in Proposition
5.1
. If
b\in\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{1}
, then Proposition
5.1
gives us

|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b,h)|\leq Cp^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})}\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})},

which is (
L1
) with
C_{L}\sim p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.

Assume then
h\in\mathcal{Y}_{q^{\prime}}
and
b\in\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{q^{\prime}}
.
Recall that
\Lambda^{*}
is like
\Lambda
, but with
\Omega^{*}
in place of
\Omega
, where
\Omega^{*}(x)=\overline{\Omega(-x)}
. By the representation (
2.2
)–(
2.2
) we have

\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(h,b)=\overline{\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b^{\text{in}},\mathbbm{1}_{Q}h)}+V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b),

where

|V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b)|\leq 2^{\vartheta d}[K]_{q}|Q|\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}

for a suitable absolute constant
\vartheta
.
Now Proposition
5.1
with
\Omega^{*}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
in place of
\Omega
(and thus
\Lambda^{*}
in place of
\Lambda
) yields

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(h,b)|

\displaystyle\leq|\Lambda^{*}_{\mathcal{Q},\nu,\mu}(b^{\text{in}},\mathbbm{1}_{Q}h)|+|V_{\mathcal{Q}}(h,b)|

\displaystyle\leq Cp^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\,|Q|\,\|b^{\text{in}}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2^{\vartheta d}[K]_{q}\,|Q|\,\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{Q})}.

Then we apply
\|b^{\text{in}}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\leq\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}
,
q^{\prime}\leq p
and (
5
) to bound the last expression by

\displaystyle(Cp^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)+2^{\vartheta d}[K]_{q})\,|Q|\,\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}\lesssim_{d}p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\,|Q|\,\|h\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})}\|b\|_{\dot{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(\mathcal{Q})}.

Thus (
L2
) holds for
(p_{1},p_{2})=(1,p)
with constant
C_{L}\sim_{d}p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.
∎

We now see that the assumptions of Corollary
4.5
are satisfied so we get the following convex body extension of
[
3
, Theorem A]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with zero average. Then for all
1<t<\infty
,
\vec{f}\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes such that

|\langle T_{\Omega}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}N_{p,q}(\Omega)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)},

where

N_{p,q}(\Omega)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},\,&q<\infty,\,q^{\prime}\leq p<\infty,\\
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},&q=\infty,\,1<p<\infty.\end{cases}

Proof.

Lemma
5.2
gives that the assumptions of Corollary
4.5
are satisfied with constants bounded by
c_{d}\,p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, and thus Corollary
4.5
gives

|\langle T_{\Omega}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}\left(\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)},

where
m
is defined by (
16
). Applying (
16
) with
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1=\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
we get

\displaystyle|\langle mf,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\Lambda(f,g)|+|\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\Lambda^{\nu}_{-\nu}(f,g)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Lambda\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{C}}+C_{T}(2)\lesssim_{d}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega),

and taking supremums over such
f
and
g
yields
\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{d}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)
. This shows that

|\langle T_{\Omega}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}\tilde{N}_{p,q}(\Omega)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)}.

Suppose
q<\infty
and
\lambda>\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\langle T_{\Omega}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\lambda|\langle T_{\Omega/\lambda}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}\lambda(1+[\Omega/\lambda]_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})})\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{\prime}\lambda\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)},

and letting
\lambda\to\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
gives us the wanted result.
∎

We note that the above Theorem was known in the case
q=\infty
due to
[
16
, Theorem 9]
, but for other
q>1
it is new. We also note that in
[
4
, Theorem 6.2]
an
(L^{p},L^{p})
convex body bound for the maximal truncations

T_{\Omega,\star}f(x)\coloneqq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left|\int_{|y|>\varepsilon}f(x-y)\Omega(\frac{y}{|y|})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d}}\right|

was achieved for the case
q=\infty
.

Since the adjoint
T_{\Omega}^{*}
is an operator of the same form as
T_{\Omega}
, we get
(L^{p},L^{1})
bounds for
T_{\Omega}
as a consequence of Theorem
5.3
.

Corollary 5.4
.

With the assumptions and notations of Theorem
5.3
we have

|\langle T_{\Omega}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}N_{p,q}(\Omega)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}.

Proof.

Since the adjoint of
T_{\Omega}
is
T_{\Omega^{*}}
, where
\Omega^{*}(x)\coloneqq\overline{\Omega(-x)}
, applying Theorem
5.3
to the adjoint of
T_{\Omega}
implies that

|\langle T_{\Omega}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|=|\langle\vec{f},T_{\Omega^{*}}\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}N_{p,q}(\Omega^{*})\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)},

and the proof is done due to symmetry
N_{p,q}(\Omega^{*})=N_{p,q}(\Omega)
.
∎

6.
Matrix weighted inequalities

The main applications of convex body domination bounds are the various matrix weighted inequalities that follow. For any
1<t<\infty
the matrix-weighted
A_{t}
class is defined by

[W]_{A_{t}}\coloneqq\fint_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}|W^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(y)|_{op}^{t^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{t}{t^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Applying Corollary
5.4
with
p=q^{\prime}
and
[
13
, Theorem 6.9]
gives us a matrix-weighted
L^{t}(W)\to L^{t}(W)
norm inequality for
T_{\Omega}
with
W\in A_{\frac{t}{q^{\prime}}}
. Very recently in
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
it was shown that if an operator satisfies the convex body bound of Corollary
5.4
, then the condition
W\in A_{\frac{t}{q^{\prime}}}
is not necessary to guarantee boundedness on
L^{t}(W)
. In particular, their result together with Corollary
5.4
lets one deduce the following conclusion.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
q^{\prime}<t<\infty
,
a\coloneqq\frac{tq^{\prime}}{t-q^{\prime}}
and assume that

(24)

[W]_{A_{a^{\prime},t}}\coloneqq\fint_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}|W^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(y)|_{op}^{a}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{t}{a}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Let also
\Omega\in L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with zero average. Then we have

\displaystyle\|T_{\Omega}\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,n,q,t}\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{a^{\prime},t}}^{1+\frac{q^{\prime}}{t(t-q^{\prime})}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{\frac{t}{q^{\prime}}}}^{1+\frac{q^{\prime}}{t(t-q^{\prime})}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}.

As shown in
[
11
, Lemma 6.6]
the second inequality in the above Corollary follows from the Cordes inequality
|A^{\alpha}B^{\alpha}|_{op}\leq|AB|_{op}^{\alpha}
, that holds for positive definite matrices
A,B
and
0<\alpha<1
.

We note that
[
16
, Theorem 1]
says that in the case
q=\infty
we have

\|T_{\Omega}\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,t}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{t}}^{1+\frac{1}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}+\min\{1,\frac{1}{t-1}\}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)},\,1<t<\infty.

In
[
4
, Corollary 1.6]
the above bound was also formulated and proven for
t=2
with the maximal truncated operator (
5
) in place of
T_{\Omega}
.

Remark 6.2
.

The inequality (
6
) does not follow from Corollary
5.4
and
[
13
, Theorem 6.9]
(or
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
) since we do not have Corollary
5.4
for
p=1=q^{\prime}
.

Given a locally integrable function
b
we define the commutator
[b,T]
of a linear operator
T
by
[b,T]f\coloneqq T(bf)-bTf
.
By Corollary
5.4
and
[
13
, Theorem 7.4]
we also get matrix-weighted bounds for the commutator of
T_{\Omega}
. We may also refine this bound by combining the techniques in the proofs of
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
13
, Theorem 7.4]
. This lets us replace the weight constant
[W]_{\frac{t}{q^{\prime}}}
with (
24
).

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
q^{\prime}<t<\infty
,
a\coloneqq\frac{tq^{\prime}}{t-q^{\prime}}
and
b\in BMO
. Let also
\Omega\in L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with zero average. Then we have

\displaystyle\|[b,T_{\Omega}]\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,n,q,t}\|b\|_{BMO}\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{a^{\prime},t}}^{1+\frac{q^{\prime}}{t(t-q^{\prime})}+\max\{1,\frac{q^{\prime}}{t-q^{\prime}}\}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{BMO}\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{\frac{t}{q^{\prime}}}}^{1+\frac{q^{\prime}}{t(t-q^{\prime})}+\max\{1,\frac{q^{\prime}}{t-q^{\prime}}\}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)},

where
[W]_{a^{\prime},t}
is as in (
24
).

We also record and prove a new matrix-weighted estimate for the commutators in the case that
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. Due to the same reason as in Remark
6.2
, we are not allowed to simply let
q\to\infty
in Corollary
6.3
.
Note that we could directly apply Theorem
5.3
(or Corollary
5.4
) and
[
13
, Theorem 7.4]
to deduce matrix weighted bounds for the commutators in this case as well. However, here we have the liberty of choosing the parameter
p
in the convex body domination, which means that we can achieve more precise bounds than in
[
13
]
.
One could revisit to the proofs of
[
13
, Theorem 7.4]
and
[
16
, Theorem 1]
to prove the following result, but we will present a shorter proof that uses matrix-weighted bounds for commutators from
[
10
]
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with zero average and
b\in BMO
. Then

\|[b,T_{\Omega}]\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,t}\|b\|_{BMO}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{t}}^{2+\frac{2}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}.

Proof.

As discussed before,
[
16
, Theorem 1]
says that for
1<t<\infty
we have

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{t}(W)\to L^{t}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,t}\phi([W]_{A_{t}}),

where
\phi(x)\coloneqq\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}x^{1+\frac{1}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}+\min\{1,\frac{1}{t-1}\}}
. Thus due to
[
10
, Proposition 1.5]
there exists a constant
C\coloneqq C(d,n,t)
such that

\displaystyle\|[b,T_{\Omega}]\|_{L^{t}(W)\to L^{t}(W)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,n,t}\|b\|_{BMO}([W]_{A_{t}}+[W^{-\frac{t^{\prime}}{t}}]_{A_{t^{\prime}}})\,\phi(C[W]_{A_{t}})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,t}\|b\|_{BMO}[W]_{A_{t}}^{\max\{1,\frac{1}{t-1}\}}\phi(C[W]_{A_{t}})

\displaystyle\eqsim_{d,n,t}\|b\|_{BMO}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}[W]_{A_{t}}^{2+\frac{2}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}},

and the proof is done.
∎

7.
Sparse domination of commutators and Bloom-type bounds

Another interesting result that follows from Theorem
5.3
is a certain sparse domination bound for the commutators. To formulate this result we need the following quantity.
For a collection of dyadic cubes
\mathscr{Q}
, functions
\vec{h}_{1},\vec{h}_{2}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
b\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C})
and positive numbers
\gamma,\beta\geq 1
we define

\mathcal{A}^{\gamma,\beta}_{b,\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{h}_{1},\vec{h}_{2})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left(\fint_{Q}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|^{\gamma}|\vec{h}_{1}(y)|^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{h}_{2}(x)|^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},

where
\langle b\rangle_{Q}
is the average of
b
over
Q
. In
[
13
, Proposition 7.1]
it was shown that convex body domination implies that the bilinear sparse forms (
7
) can be used to dominate the commutators. The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 5.4 combined with
[
13
, Proposition 7.1]
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with zero average and
b\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C})
. Then for all
1<t<\infty
,
\vec{f}\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes such that

|\langle[b,T_{\Omega}]\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}N_{p,q}(\Omega)\,[\mathcal{A}^{\gamma,\beta}_{b,\mathcal{S}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})+\mathcal{A}^{\beta,\gamma}_{b,\mathcal{S}}(\vec{g},\vec{f})],

where
(\gamma,\beta)\in\{(1,p),(p,1)\}
and
N_{p,q}(\Omega)
is as in Theorem
5.3
.

The above result was known for
q=\infty
due to
[
15
]
, but it is completely new for
q<\infty
. In
[
15
]
, it was shown that this type of domination lets us deduce so called Bloom-type boundedness for
T_{\Omega}
. In order to introduce this result we need some definitions. The reverse Hölder class
RH_{s}
is a class of scalar weights
w
that satisfy

[w]_{RH_{s}}\coloneqq\sup_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}w^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\fint_{Q}w\right)^{-1}<\infty.

The weighted fractional BMO-seminorm is defined by

\|b\|_{BMO^{\alpha}_{\nu}}\coloneqq\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{\nu(Q)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{d}}}\int_{Q}|b-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|,

where
\alpha\geq 0
,
\nu
is a weight and
\nu(Q)\coloneqq\int_{Q}\nu
. Furthermore,
we need the weighted sharp maximal function

M_{\nu}^{\#}b\coloneqq\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{Q}|b-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|.

By
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
the bilinear sparse forms (
7
) can be estimated in
L^{u}(\mu)\times L^{v}(\lambda^{1-q^{\prime}})
with suitable parameters
u,v
and weights
\mu,\lambda
.
The following Corollary is an immediate consequence of Corollary
7.1
and
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\Omega\in L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
for some
1<q<\infty
with zero average. Let also
1<u,v<q
,
\mu\in A_{u}\cap RH_{\left(\frac{q}{u}\right)^{\prime}}
and
\lambda\in A_{v}\cap RH_{\left(\frac{q}{v}\right)^{\prime}}
, and define the Bloom-weight
\nu^{1+\alpha}\coloneqq\mu^{\frac{1}{u}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{v}}
, where
\alpha\coloneqq-\frac{1}{\tau}\coloneqq\frac{1}{u}-\frac{1}{v}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|[b,T_{\Omega}]\|_{L^{u}(\mu)\to L^{v}(\lambda)}\lesssim_{d,q,u,v,\mu,\lambda}\|\Omega\|_{L^{q,1}\log L(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\,\mathcal{N}_{u,v}(b)

where

\mathcal{N}_{u,v}(b)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\|b\|_{BMO^{\alpha d}_{\nu}},\quad\text{ if }u\leq v,\\
\|M^{\#}_{\nu}b\|_{L^{\tau}(\nu)},\quad\text{ if }u\geq v.\end{cases}

The proof of above result is essentially the same as the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
with the simplification
r=1
. The difference being that in
[
15
]
they assume
\Omega\in L^{\infty}
and the sparse domination of commutators follows from the local weak-type control of the grand maximal truncation operator. This type of control is not known for unbounded
\Omega
so in our case we need Corollary
7.1
for the sparse domination. An explicit dependence on
\mu
and
\lambda
can be easily tracked from the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
.

8.
Bochner-Riesz means at the critical index

Another operator that can be estimated via Theorem
4.3
is given by the critical index Bochner-Riesz mean, i.e., the Fourier multiplier operator

B_{\delta}f\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\widehat{f}(\cdot)(1-|\cdot|^{2})^{\delta}_{+}\right],\qquad\delta\coloneqq\frac{d-1}{2}.

Same techniques as in
[
3
]
could also be applied to show that the Bochner-Riesz means can be estimated using Theorem
4.3
. However, thanks to recent results of Shrivastava and Shuin
[
18
,
19
]
, there is now the following shorter route to such a result via the grand maximal truncation operator approach due to Muller and Rivera-Ríos
[
16
]
.

The following result was originally proven implicitly in
[
18
,
19
]
. See also
[
15
, Example 6.6]
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\delta\coloneqq\frac{d-1}{2}
and
1\leq s<\infty
. The grand maximal truncation operator defined by

M_{B_{\delta},s}f(x)\coloneqq\sup_{Q\ni x}\left(\fint_{Q}|B_{\delta}(f\mathbbm{1}_{(3Q)^{\complement}})|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

satisfies

\|M_{B_{\delta},s}\|_{L^{1,\infty}\to L^{1}}\lesssim_{d}s.

The classical result of Christ
[
2
]
and the above Theorem combined with convex body domination principle of Muller and Rivera-Ríos
[
16
]
gives us convex body domination for the Bochner-Riesz means at the critical index.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\delta=\frac{d-1}{2}
. For all
1<t<\infty
,
\vec{f}\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\vec{g}\in L^{t^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{d})
, there exists a sparse collection
\mathcal{S}
of cubes such that, we have

|\langle B_{\delta}\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}p^{\prime}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)}.

Proof.

We denote
\tilde{p}\coloneqq\frac{p}{p+1}
. By
[
16
, Theorem 11]
we have

|\langle B_{\delta}f,g\rangle|\lesssim_{d,n}(\|B_{\delta}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}+\|M_{B_{\delta}}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{p}\to L^{\tilde{p},\infty}})\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}|Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q)},

where
M_{B_{\delta}}
is the bilinear grand maximal truncation operator (
4
) with
T=B_{\delta}
. The main result of
[
2
]
is that
\|B_{\delta}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}\lesssim_{d}1
so it remains to check that

\|M_{B_{\delta}}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{p}\to L^{\tilde{p},\infty}}\lesssim_{d}p^{\prime}.

By Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle M_{B_{\delta}}(f,g)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\fint_{Q}|B_{\delta}(\mathbbm{1}_{(3Q)^{\complement}}f)||g|\leq M_{B_{\delta},p^{\prime}}fM_{p}g.

Then the Hölder inequality for weak
L^{p}
spaces gives

\|M_{B_{\delta}}(f,g)\|_{L^{\tilde{p},\infty}}\lesssim\|M_{B_{\delta},p^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\|M_{p}g\|_{L^{p,\infty}}.

Lastly, by Theorem
8.1
with
s=p^{\prime}
and the weak-type boundedness of the maximal operator we have

\|M_{B_{\delta},p^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\|M_{p}g\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\lesssim_{d}p^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{p}},

and the proof is done.
∎

With the same proof as in Corollary
5.4
the
(L^{1},L^{p})
bound for
B_{\delta}
implies an
(L^{p},L^{1})
bound. Again, as a Corollary we get the following matrix-weighted norm inequality.

Corollary 8.3
.

For
1<t<\infty
we have

\|B_{\delta}\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,t}[W]_{A_{t}}^{1+\frac{1}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}+\min\{1,\frac{1}{t-1}\}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}.

The proof of the above result is essentially contained in the proof of Theorem 1 from
[
16
]
.
Lastly, with the same proof as in Corollary
6.4
we see that the commutators satisfy the following matrix-weighted inequality.

Corollary 8.4
.

Let
b\in BMO
. Then

\|[b,B_{\delta}]\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,t}\|b\|_{BMO}[W]_{A_{t}}^{2+\frac{2}{t-1}-\frac{1}{t}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{t}(W)}.

We can also get analogous results to Corollaries
7.1
and
7.2
for
B_{\delta}
, but these results were already known due to
[
15
]
, so we will not repeat them here.

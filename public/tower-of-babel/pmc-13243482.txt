CEP72 promotes malignant progression and shapes the tumor immune microenvironment in hepatocellular carcinoma: integrated multi-cohort bioinformatics and in vitro validation
Authors: Mingyan Zhu, Ke Wang, Xudong Feng, Qun Cai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) benefit only a subset of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and biomarkers linking ICI response with tumor biology remain limited. We investigated CEP72 as an ICI-response–associated gene and evaluated its prognostic, immune, and functional relevance in HCC. Anti–PD-1 response–associated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in GSE35640 and screened for prognostic relevance in TCGA-LIHC using univariate Cox models for overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI). Intersected prognostic genes were used to construct and validate multigene survival models across TCGA-LIHC and independent cohorts. CEP72 was prioritized for pathway activity (GSVA/GSEA), tumor microenvironment deconvolution, validation in independent immunotherapy cohorts, drug sensitivity association (GDSC), and single-cell localization in HCC (TISCH2). CEP72 was silenced with two siRNAs followed by qPCR, CCK-8, colony formation, and wound-healing assays, and protein expression was assessed by immunohistochemistry. Of 209 anti–PD-1–associated DEGs, 25 were consistently prognostic for OS/DSS/PFI, and 23 were dysregulated between tumor and adjacent liver. A Ridge-based signature showed stable discrimination (mean AUC ~ 0.66) and stratified survival across cohorts, supported by meta-analysis (pooled HR 1.84, 95% CI 1.21–2.78). CEP72 was upregulated in HCC and associated with proliferative programs (cell cycle and DNA damage/repair), higher PI3K activity, and distinct immune infiltration patterns. Higher CEP72 expression was associated with ICI response and predicted sensitivity to multiple targeted agents. Single-cell analyses localized CEP72 predominantly to malignant/epithelial compartments. Functionally, CEP72 knockdown reduced proliferation, clonogenicity, and migration, while increasing apoptosis. Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major contributor to cancer mortality, largely driven by late presentation, underlying liver dysfunction, and marked biological heterogeneity. While systemic therapy has expanded substantially, durable disease control is still limited for many patients with advanced HCC1. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), particularly PD-1/PD-L1–based regimens, can induce durable responses in a subset of patients; however, a substantial proportion experience limited clinical benefit2–4. This clinical variability highlights the need for biomarkers that not only inform therapeutic stratification but also reflect tumor-intrinsic and microenvironment-related programs that shape immune contexture, thereby enabling patient stratification and rational combination strategies5,6.

Centrosome-associated proteins are central to microtubule organization, mitotic spindle assembly, and genome stability—processes closely tied to malignant proliferation and stress adaptation. Disruption of centrosome homeostasis can promote chromosomal instability and activate oncogenic signaling, with downstream effects on cell-cycle progression, DNA damage responses, and potentially immune-relevant phenotypes7. CEP72 (centrosomal protein 72) is a centrosome/centriolar satellite–associated factor implicated in centrosomal microtubule nucleation and organization. Emerging evidence across tumor contexts suggests that CEP72 dysregulation may be coupled to proliferative capacity and therapy-associated cellular states8, but its role in HCC progression and its connection to immunotherapy-response signals remain insufficiently defined.

In this study, we integrated an anti–PD-1 response transcriptomic resource with multi-cohort HCC datasets to identify ICI-response–associated genes with consistent prognostic relevance and tumor-specific dysregulation, and to develop a robust survival signature across independent cohorts. We prioritized CEP72 for in-depth analyses, including pathway activity and hallmark enrichment, tumor microenvironment deconvolution, external validation in independent immunotherapy cohorts, drug sensitivity association, and single-cell localization within HCC cellular compartments. To experimentally validate tumor-intrinsic effects, we performed CEP72 loss-of-function assays using two independent siRNAs and evaluated proliferation, clonogenicity, and migration. Collectively, our work positions CEP72 at the intersection of prognostic stratification, proliferative oncogenic programs, and immune/therapeutic response signals, providing a rationale for further translational evaluation of CEP72 as a candidate biomarker and potential vulnerability in HCC.

Materials and methods

Public datasets and preprocessing

Gene expression and clinical data for HCC were obtained from TCGA-LIHC and independent validation cohorts, including E-TABM-36, GSE116174, GSE14520, and ICGC-LIRI. An anti-PD-1-treated melanoma cohort (GSE35640; responders n = 22, non-responders n = 34) was used to define immunotherapy-response-associated DEGs. Expression matrices were normalized according to each source dataset’s recommended processing, and gene identifiers were harmonized to gene symbols. Differential expression between responders and non-responders was performed using the limma framework. A threshold of |log2 fold change| > 1 and adjusted P < 0.05 was used to define DEGs, and heatmaps were generated using pheatmap.

Prognostic gene screening and model construction

In TCGA-LIHC, univariate Cox proportional hazards analyses (survival package) were performed for DEGs across OS, DSS, and PFI. The intersection set was visualized using Venn diagrams. Genes that were additionally differentially expressed between tumor and adjacent liver were retained for prognostic modeling. Multiple survival modeling algorithms were evaluated, including Lasso regression, Elastic Net, Ridge regression, stepwise Cox regression, and CoxBoost. Lasso regression was implemented using the glmnet package with family = “cox” and alpha = 1. The optimal penalty parameter (lambda) was selected using 10-fold cross-validation via the cv.glmnet function, and the corresponding model coefficients were extracted at the optimal lambda. Genes with non-zero coefficients were retained as candidate predictors. Elastic Net and Ridge regression were also implemented using the glmnet package; for Elastic Net, the mixing parameter alpha was set from 0.1 to 0.9, whereas for Ridge regression alpha was fixed at 0. Stepwise Cox regression was performed by first fitting a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model using the coxph function, followed by stepwise variable selection with the stepAIC function using both, forward, and backward directions. For CoxBoost, the penalty parameter was first optimized using the optimCoxBoostPenalty function, followed by selection of the optimal step number using cv.CoxBoost. The final CoxBoost model was then fitted using the optimized penalty and step number. Model coefficients and corresponding genes were extracted from each fitted model. Time-dependent AUCs at 1, 3, and 5 years were calculated in the training and validation cohorts, and the model with the best average performance across cohorts was selected for downstream analyses. Risk scores were calculated as a linear predictor, and patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups by the median risk score, ensuring each group comprised at least 30% of the cohort. Survival differences were assessed by Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests. Hazard ratios (HRs) for the risk score were derived within each cohort and combined using a random-effects model. Heterogeneity was evaluated by I2 and between-study variance.

Single-gene downstream analyses for CEP72

CEP72 was prioritized based on its coefficient weight in the optimal model and limited prior reporting in HCC. Associations between CEP72 expression (z-score) and oncogenic state scores (z-score) were assessed using Pearson correlation, based on pathway activity scoring. Immune infiltration patterns were inferred by multiple deconvolution algorithms (e.g., CIBERSORT, TIMER, EPIC, MCPcounter, xCell, quanTIseq) and compared between CEP72-high and CEP72-low tumors defined by the median expression.

Integrative multi-cohort analysis identifies CEP72 as an immunotherapy-associated prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma

Hallmark gene set enrichment was performed by GSEA after stratifying TCGA-LIHC into CEP72-high and CEP72-low groups (significance: nominal P < 0.05 and FDR < 0.25), and key signals were summarized across cohorts when applicable. CEP72 was further validated in independent immunotherapy datasets (SKCM_Tumor_GSE35640, SKCM_Tumor_PMID30753825, and BLCA_Tumor_GSE176307) by comparing expression between responders and non-responders using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. Drug sensitivity associations were assessed in GDSC1/2 using Spearman correlations between CEP72 expression and drug response metrics (IC50/AUC), with negative and positive correlations indicating relative sensitivity and resistance, respectively. Single-cell localization was examined in TISCH2 (GSE166635) by visualizing CEP72 across annotated cell types; CEP72-positive and -negative cells were defined by expression and compared for pathway activity using limma-based scoring. Representative IHC images of CEP72 in normal liver were obtained from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) Tissue Atlas, and liver cancer IHC images were obtained from the HPA Pathology Atlas.

Cell culture, siRNA knockdown, and functional assays

HepG2 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells were transfected with two independent siRNAs targeting CEP72 (siCEP72#1 and siCEP72#2) or a negative control siRNA (siNC). Knockdown efficiency was validated by qPCR. Cell proliferation was assessed by CCK-8 assays over multiple days. Clonogenic capacity was evaluated by colony formation assays (day 7). Cell migration was measured by wound-healing assays with serial imaging (day 0 to day 3). Apoptosis was evaluated using Annexin V/PI staining and flow cytometry at 48 h post-transfection using an Annexin V, 633 Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime). Early (Annexin V+/PI−) and late (Annexin V+/PI+) apoptotic populations were summed as total apoptosis (Q2 + Q3). For immunoblotting, CEP72 was detected using an anti-CEP72 polyclonal antibody (Proteintech, Cat. No. 19928-1-AP), with GAPDH as the loading control (rabbit monoclonal anti-GAPDH [EPR16891], Abcam, Cat. No. ab181602).

Result

Identification of immunotherapy-response–associated prognostic genes and benchmarking of survival models in hepatocellular carcinoma

We first derived an anti–PD-1 response–associated transcriptomic signature from GSE35640, identifying 209 DEGs that distinguished responders from non-responders (Fig. 1A). To link immunotherapy-associated signals to HCC outcomes, these candidates were screened in TCGA-LIHC using univariate Cox models across OS, DSS, and PFI, yielding 25 genes consistently associated with all three survival endpoints (Fig. 1B). Notably, 23 of the 25 shared prognostic genes were also significantly dysregulated between tumor and adjacent/normal liver tissues, supporting their tumor relevance (Fig. 1C). We then constructed multigene survival models using multiple machine-learning algorithms and benchmarked their performance across TCGA-LIHC and independent cohorts. Among evaluated methods, the Ridge-based model achieved the most stable overall discrimination across datasets (mean AUC ~ 0.665), and was therefore selected for downstream risk stratification analyses (Fig. 1D).

(B) Venn diagram of genes significantly associated with overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI) in TCGA-LIHC by univariate Cox regression, identifying 25 shared prognostic genes. (C) Differential expression of the shared prognostic genes between tumor and adjacent/normal liver tissues in TCGA-LIHC; 23 of 25 genes were significantly dysregulated (Wilcoxon rank-sum test; ns, not significant; *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001). (D) Performance comparison of multiple machine-learning survival algorithms across TCGA-LIHC and external cohorts/endpoints using time-dependent AUC; the right bar indicates mean AUC across evaluated datasets, highlighting the Ridge model as the most stable performer overall.

Ridge-derived risk score shows robust prognostic value across cohorts and endpoints, prioritizing CEP72 as a key contributor

To further assess model interpretability and robustness, we compared feature coefficients across multiple machine-learning survival algorithms trained on the shared 25-gene prognostic set. Coefficient patterns were largely concordant across regularized methods, and CEP72 consistently emerged as a high-weight positive contributor within the Ridge framework, supporting its prioritization for downstream analyses (Fig. 2A). We next quantified the generalizability of the Ridge risk score using a random-effects meta-analysis across cohorts and survival endpoints, which confirmed an overall adverse prognostic effect (pooled HR 1.84, 95% CI 1.21–2.78) despite substantial between-study heterogeneity (Fig. 2B). Consistently, Kaplan–Meier analyses demonstrated that the Ridge score significantly stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups in multiple independent settings, including GSE14520 (OS, P = 0.009; RFS, P = 0.016), ICGC-LIRI (OS, P = 0.010), and TCGA-LIHC across DFI, DSS, OS, and PFI (all P < 0.001) (Fig. 2C–I). Collectively, these results indicate that an ICI-response–informed Ridge signature provides stable prognostic discrimination across cohorts and endpoints, and highlight CEP72 as a key feature within the model.

CEP72 associates with proliferative oncogenic programs and a distinct immune infiltration landscape in TCGA-LIHC

We next investigated the biological context of CEP72 in HCC. Across TCGA-LIHC, higher CEP72 expression correlated positively with multiple oncogenic state scores, with the strongest associations observed for cell cycle, DNA damage, and DNA repair programs, and additional links to invasion/metastasis-related phenotypes (Fig. 3A). We then profiled the tumor microenvironment using multi-algorithm deconvolution and found that CEP72-high tumors exhibited a distinct immune infiltration pattern compared with CEP72-low tumors, characterized by coordinated differences across multiple lymphoid and myeloid compartments as well as microenvironment/stromal scores (Fig. 3B). These data suggest that CEP72 marks a proliferative, genome-instability–related tumor state accompanied by systematic remodeling of immune contexture.

CEP72 correlates with proliferative oncogenic states and reshapes immune contexture in hepatocellular carcinoma. (A) Correlations between CEP72 expression and oncogenic state scores in TCGA-LIHC, highlighting strong positive associations with cell cycle and DNA damage/repair programs. (B) Heatmap of tumor microenvironment deconvolution results comparing CEP72-high versus CEP72-low tumors across multiple algorithms and immune/stromal cell types.

CEP72 is linked to PI3K pathway activation and shows consistent immunotherapy-response signals across independent cohorts

To further connect CEP72 to therapeutic response and actionable biology, we evaluated immune associations, pathway enrichment, and immunotherapy-cohort validation. Across TCGA-LIHC, CEP72 displayed systematic correlations with multiple immune and stromal components derived from multi-algorithm deconvolution, indicating coordinated remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment with increasing CEP72 expression (Fig. 4A). Hallmark GSEA/GSVA analyses across HCC cohorts further highlighted reproducible pathway shifts associated with CEP72, including enrichment of proliferative and oncogenic signaling programs (Fig. 4B). Consistent with an immunotherapy-relevant signal, CEP72 expression differed between responders and non-responders in independent ICI-treated cohorts (Fig. 4C–E). In parallel, CEP72-high tumors exhibited significantly higher PI3K pathway scores than CEP72-low tumors (Wilcoxon rank-sum P = 7.76 × 10⁻¹¹), supporting a CEP72–PI3K axis as a candidate mechanistic and therapeutic link (Fig. 4F).

CEP72 expression is associated with broad drug-response patterns in pharmacogenomic databases

To explore potential therapeutic implications, we examined associations between CEP72 expression and drug response across pharmacogenomic resources. In both GDSC1 and GDSC2, CEP72 expression showed significant correlations with the estimated drug response (IC50-based metrics), nominating multiple compounds whose sensitivity/resistance patterns tracked with CEP72 levels (Fig. 5A–B). Notably, the correlated drugs spanned diverse targeted classes, suggesting that CEP72 may mark a drug-response state rather than a single compound-specific effect. Integrative visualization further mapped CEP72-associated compounds to major signaling and cellular process categories, including PI3K/mTOR, RTK, EGFR/MAPK, cell cycle/mitosis, DNA replication/repair, and apoptosis-related programs (Fig. 5C). These data support CEP72 as a candidate biomarker for stratifying therapeutic vulnerabilities and prioritizing rational combination strategies.

Single-cell analysis localizes CEP72 to malignant/epithelial compartments and links CEP72 positivity to proliferative signaling programs

To define the cellular source of CEP72 expression in the HCC microenvironment, we analyzed single-cell RNA-seq data (TISCH2; GSE166635). UMAP visualization identified major tumor and stromal/immune compartments, including malignant/epithelial cells and multiple immune subsets (Fig. 6A). Mapping CEP72 expression onto the embedding showed that CEP72 signal was enriched predominantly within malignant/epithelial populations, with limited expression across most immune lineages (Fig. 6B). We further compared pathway activities between CEP72-positive and CEP72-negative cells within annotated lineages, which revealed that CEP72 positivity was consistently associated with activation of proliferative and oncogenic signaling programs—particularly cell-cycle–related pathways—most prominently in malignant/epithelial compartments (Fig. 6C). These findings support a tumor-intrinsic, malignant-cell–enriched expression pattern of CEP72 and suggest that CEP72 marks a proliferative cellular state in HCC.

(C) Dot plot summarizing pathway activity differences between CEP72-positive and CEP72-negative cells across annotated lineages (color indicates direction/magnitude; dot size reflects significance), highlighting enrichment of proliferative/cell-cycle programs in malignant/epithelial cells.

In vitro functional validation supports a tumor-promoting role of CEP72, with protein-level evidence of CEP72 upregulation

To experimentally validate the tumor-intrinsic role of CEP72 in HCC, we performed siRNA-mediated loss-of-function assays using two independent siRNAs (siCEP72#1 and siCEP72#2). qRT–PCR showed that both siRNAs efficiently reduced CEP72 mRNA levels compared with the negative control (siNC) (Fig. 7A). Consistently, immunoblotting confirmed CEP72 protein downregulation in both knockdown groups (Fig. 7B). Functionally, CEP72 silencing markedly impaired proliferative capacity, as reflected by a blunted increase in CCK-8–derived OD values over time compared with siNC (Fig. 7C). In parallel, wound-healing assays showed delayed scratch closure across multiple time points in CEP72-silenced cells, indicating compromised migratory ability (Fig. 7D). Mechanistically, CEP72 depletion promoted apoptotic cell death: Annexin V/PI flow cytometry revealed an increase in apoptotic fractions in siCEP72 groups (Fig. 7E), and quantification showed significantly elevated total apoptosis rates relative to siNC (Fig. 7F). Consistent with these anti-tumor phenotypes, clonogenic survival was substantially reduced upon CEP72 knockdown, with fewer and smaller colonies formed than in control cells (Fig. 7G). Collectively, these data support a pro-survival and pro-proliferative role of CEP72 in HCC cells and suggest that CEP72 sustains tumor cell fitness at least in part by restraining apoptosis, while also contributing to migratory competence. These results provide consistent in vitro evidence that CEP72 promotes malignant phenotypes, including proliferation, clonogenicity, and migration. Complementing these functional data, representative immunohistochemistry images from a public tissue database demonstrated low CEP72 staining in normal liver and stronger CEP72 positivity in liver cancer tissues, supporting CEP72 upregulation at the protein level (Supplementary Fig. 1). Together, these findings substantiate CEP72 as a tumor-promoting factor in HCC.

Discussion

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have reshaped the systemic treatment landscape of advanced HCC, yet only a subset of patients derive durable benefit, underscoring an urgent need for biomarkers that integrate tumor-intrinsic aggressiveness with immune responsiveness9. In this study, we assembled a cross-scale evidence chain—immunotherapy-response transcriptomics, multi-cohort prognostic learning, pathway and microenvironment profiling, pharmacogenomic associations, single-cell localization, and in vitro perturbation—to position CEP72 as a candidate biomarker connecting malignant progression and treatment-response signals, consistent with the emerging concept that genes driving tumor progression may also participate in shaping the tumor immune microenvironment10.

A key step in our design was anchoring candidate discovery in an immunotherapy-response transcriptional context and then transferring that signal into HCC survival endpoints. Public melanoma anti–PD-1 response transcriptomes have repeatedly served as pragmatic discovery resources to capture shared immune-response programs that may generalize across tumors11,12. However, cross-cohort and cross-platform modeling in HCC is inherently sensitive to heterogeneity in clinical annotations, etiologies, sequencing platforms, and normalization strategies. These batch- and cohort-effects can inflate apparent performance if not handled conservatively, motivating our use of multi-cohort benchmarking and stability-oriented modeling13. Within this framework, we derived a compact prognostic set and demonstrated that a Ridge-regularized signature delivered the most consistent discrimination across datasets, aligning with the known tendency of Ridge penalties to stabilize estimates under collinearity and high-dimensional features. These results support the value of regularization-first strategies when transporting signatures across heterogeneous HCC cohorts.

CEP72 emerged as a high-weight feature within the final model and exhibited coherent links to malignant programs. Biologically, CEP72 is a centrosome-associated factor implicated in microtubule organization and centrosomal microtubule nucleation, providing a plausible mechanistic bridge between proliferative capacity and genome maintenance14. Centrosome–microtubule axis dysregulation is a recognized enabler of chromosomal instability and oncogenic stress responses, and our data were directionally consistent with this paradigm: CEP72-high tumors aligned with proliferative states, including cell-cycle progression and DNA damage/repair activity. Importantly, these associations were not restricted to bulk-tissue correlative analyses; single-cell localization further supported enrichment of CEP72 expression within malignant/epithelial compartments, strengthening the argument that CEP72 reflects (at least in part) tumor-intrinsic biology rather than only stromal admixture15. In addition, emerging literature has begun to report CEP72 dysregulation in HCC and its association with aggressive phenotypes, providing external biological plausibility for our prioritization16,17.

Mechanistically, pathway-level analyses converged on PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling as a candidate axis coupled to CEP72-high HCC18. Rather than reflecting generic PI3K activation in liver cancer, our findings suggest that CEP72 marks a tumor-intrinsic state characterized by centrosome–cytoskeleton dysregulation, enhanced proliferative output, and a distinct immune contexture. This interpretation is supported by the enrichment of CEP72 in malignant/epithelial cells, its association with cell-cycle and DNA damage/repair programs, elevated PI3K pathway activity, and altered immune infiltration patterns across cohorts. Given prior evidence that centrosomal and cytoskeleton-associated signaling architecture can influence PI3K–AKT activity, it is biologically plausible that CEP72 contributes to HCC progression, at least in part, through this axis14,19,20. Nevertheless, the current evidence remains inferential, and direct mechanistic studies are needed to determine whether CEP72 actively drives PI3K signaling or serves as a marker of a broader aggressive tumor state.

An important and clinically relevant nuance is the observed “duality” in our results: CEP72 aligned with poorer prognosis in HCC cohorts while also associating with immunotherapy response signals in external ICI datasets. This pattern is not necessarily contradictory. Highly proliferative tumors can be clinically high-risk in the absence of effective therapy, yet may simultaneously exhibit immune activation or antigenicity that increases the probability of benefit once checkpoints are blocked, consistent with the “hot-versus-cold” tumor immune-phenotype framework in which immune-inflamed tumors are more sensitive to immune checkpoint blockade. Moreover, recent transcriptomic evidence has directly linked a proliferation-enriched gene signature to response and survival following anti–PD-L1 blockade, supporting the notion that an “aggressive yet immunotherapy-susceptible” state can exist21. Under this model, CEP72 could serve as a state marker of such disease, rather than a unidirectional indicator of either favorable or unfavorable outcomes22. Future HCC ICI cohorts with pretreatment transcriptomes and standardized response endpoints will be essential to resolve whether CEP72 is a causal regulator of immune phenotypes or primarily a surrogate of a broader proliferative/immune-infiltrated state.

Our in vitro perturbation data provide functional support for an oncogenic role of CEP72 in HCC cells. CEP72 knockdown consistently impaired proliferation, clonogenic growth, and migratory capacity, reinforcing the bioinformatic links between CEP72 and cell-cycle/invasion-associated programs. In addition, CEP72 depletion significantly increased apoptotic cell death, as quantified by Annexin V/PI flow cytometry, indicating that CEP72 supports not only proliferative output but also tumor cell survival. Finally, the pharmacogenomic analyses nominate actionable hypotheses: CEP72 expression correlated with predicted sensitivity patterns across compounds in large-scale cancer cell-line resources, providing a rational entry point for combination strategies (e.g., ICI plus targeted inhibitors) in CEP72-high disease23,24. While these associations should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating, they offer an efficient prioritization layer when experimental bandwidth is limited.

Several limitations merit emphasis. First, most analyses relied on retrospective public datasets, and cross-cohort variability remains unavoidable despite multi-cohort validation. Second, the mechanistic bridge between CEP72 and PI3K signaling or immune remodeling remains inferential; direct pathway perturbation and target identification were not performed. Third, the lack of HCC-specific immunotherapy cohorts with matched transcriptomics and response endpoints in the current framework constrains immediate clinical translation; evidence for ICI association was therefore demonstrated across external tumor types. Fourth, although our in vitro knockdown experiments support a tumor-intrinsic oncogenic role of CEP72, the absence of an immune-competent in vivo HCC model precludes direct validation of whether CEP72 causally modulates the tumor immune microenvironment or influences antitumor immune responses in HCC. Nonetheless, within these constraints, our integrated analyses suggest that CEP72 is a biologically plausible and clinically relevant marker associated with malignant progression, immune contexture, and treatment-response signals in HCC, thereby providing a rationale for prospective validation and mechanistic studies in HCC immunotherapy settings1.

Conclusions

CEP72 is upregulated in HCC, supports malignant phenotypes in vitro, and is embedded in a multi-gene prognostic signature validated across independent cohorts. CEP72 associates with proliferative and DNA repair programs, PI3K pathway activation, and immune microenvironment remodeling, and it shows consistent associations with ICI response in independent immunotherapy datasets. CEP72 warrants further study as a candidate biomarker and potential therapeutic target in HCC.

Characterizations of Strong Variational Sufficiency in General Models of Composite Optimization
Boris S. Mordukhovich, Peipei Tang, Chengjing Wang

Abstract
This paper investigates a recently introduced notion of strong variational sufficiency in optimization problems whose importance has been highly recognized in optimization theory, numerical methods, and applications. We address a general class of composite optimization problems and establish complete characterizations of strong variational sufficiency for their local minimizers in terms of a generalized version of the strong second-order sufficient condition (SSOSC) and the positive-definiteness of an appropriate generalized Hessian of the augmented Lagrangian calculated at the point in question. The generalized SSOSC is expressed via a novel second-order variational function, which reflects specific features of nonconvex composite models. The imposed assumptions describe the spectrum of composite optimization problems covered by our approach while being constructively implemented for nonpolyhedral problems that involve the nuclear norm function and the indicator function of the positive-semidefinite cone without any constraint qualifications.



Keywords:

variational analysis, composite optimization, strong variational sufficiency, generalized differentiation, second-order characterizations, parabolic regularity

Introduction

The notion of
variational
and
strong variational sufficiency
for local minimizers of general optimization problems written in the unconstrained extended-real-valued format was introduced by Rockafellar
[
19
]
. It is closely related to Rockafellar’s notion of (strong)
variational convexity
of lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) functions
[
20
]
meaning that the subdifferential graph of the function in question locally behaves as the subgradient mapping associated with some (strongly) convex l.s.c. function. Both notions of variational sufficiency and variational convexity, as well as their strong counterparts, reflect “primal-dual” perspectives of local optimality. These properties enhance our understanding of optimization models by demonstrating how their objective functions can exhibit convex-type features even in more complex settings. Moreover, they provide a robust theoretical foundation for the development of numerical algorithms to efficiently solve nonsmooth and nonconvex optimization problems.

In this paper, we mainly concentrate on the study of strong variational sufficiency for local minimizers of the following problem of (constrained)
composite optimization

\displaystyle(COP)\qquad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+g(F(x))

under the
standing assumptions
:
f_{0}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}
and
F:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m}
are
{\cal C}^{2}
-smooth mappings, and
g:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}:=\mathcal{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is an l.s.c. convex function. The class of optimization problems in form (COP) is very broad including linear and nonlinear programs, second-order cone and positive-semidefinite programs, etc. In the
nondegenerate
case where the Jacobian matrix of
F
is of full rank at the reference point, the
second-order characterization of strong variational sufficiency for local optimality in COPs is obtained in
[
6
]
. In the (polyhedral) case of nonlinear programs (NLPs), the aforementioned nondegeneracy condition reduces to the classical linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ), and the characterization of
[
6
]
is explicitly formulated via the classical
strong second-order sufficient condition
(SSOSC). We also mention the abstract
quadratic bundle
characterizations in
[
22
]
formulated in the SSOSC form as well in the case of NLPs. For the class of (nonpolyhedral) nonlinear semidefinite programs (SDPs), characterizations of strong variational sufficiency were developed in
[
26
]
via the corresponding version of SSOSC.
Moreover, the obtained results for strong variational sufficiency were used for establishing local convergence rates for numerical algorithms of the proximal, augmented Lagrangian, and Newtonian types; see
[
7
,
19
,
21
,
26
]
. Major
open questions
remain about explicit characterizations of strong variational sufficiency for general nonpolyhedral composite optimization problems in terms of their given data, and particularly about the equivalence between strong variational sufficiency for local optimality in (COP) and appropriate versions of SSOSC.

This paper develops an efficient approach to resolve such issues based on advanced machinery of variational analysis and second-order generalized differentiation. Our main goal is to reveal general assumptions on the initial data of (COP) that ensure the desired equivalence of strong variational sufficiency for local optimality to the newly formulated SSOSC and the related positive-definiteness of a certain generalized Hessian of the augmented Lagrangian function associated with (COP) without any constraint qualification. Furthermore, we specifically illustrate the fulfillment of the most involved assumption in our analysis with constructive calculations in the two major nonpolyhedral cases: when
g
is either the indicator function of the positive-semidefinite cone, or the nuclear norm function. The obtained calculations lead us to explicit characterizations of strong variational sufficiency in the corresponding COPs, where the crucial case of nuclear norm-based problems has never been considered in the literature.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2

3

4

5
contains the main results on characterizing strong variational sufficiency for (COP). The concluding remarks and some topics of the future research are discussed in Section
6
. Finally, Appendix

\displaystyle e_{\sigma r}(x)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\left\{r(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\right\},

(2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathop{\operatorname{\arg\min}}\limits_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\left\{r(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\right\}.

(3)

When

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}(x)+\sigma\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma^{-1}r^{*}}(\sigma^{-1}x)=x\;\mbox{ whenever }x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}.

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}r(x):=\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\left|\,r(x^{\prime})\geq r(x)+\langle y,x^{\prime}-x\rangle+o(\|x^{\prime}-x\|)\right.\right\},

\displaystyle\partial r(x):=\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\left|\,\exists\,x^{k}\rightarrow x\;\mbox{ with }\;r(x^{k})\rightarrow r(x),\;y^{k}\in\widehat{\partial}r(x^{k}),\;y^{k}\rightarrow y\right.\right\}.

The

(5)

is
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible at every point
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
.

Note that the class of
{\cal C}^{2}
-cone reducible functions includes, in particular, the
\ell_{p}
norm functions (where
p=1,2,\infty
), the nuclear norm function, the matrix spectral norm function, the indicator functions of the nonnegative orthant cone, the second-order cone, and the positive-semidefinite cone. Therefore, all of these functions satisfy Assumption
2.1
.

Prior to the formulation of the next assumption, recall that
the range of a multifunction
S\colon\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{m}
is the set
\operatorname{rge}S:=\{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,|\,\exists\,x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\,\mbox{ with }\,u\in S(x)\}
.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an l.s.c. proper convex function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
\bar{u}\in\partial r(\bar{x})
. Suppose that there exists
\overline{W}\in\overline{\nabla}\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
from the limiting Jacobian (
7
) of the proximal mapping (
3
) ensuring the range representation

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}W=\operatorname{rge}\overline{W},

and for any
y\in\bigcup\limits_{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}W
we have
y=\overline{W}\bar{z}
, where
\bar{z}
satisfies the equality

\displaystyle\min\limits_{{z\in\mathcal{R}^{m},y=Wz}\atop{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}}\langle y,z-y\rangle=\langle y,\bar{z}-y\rangle.

Assumption
2.2
is more technical compared to Assumption
2.1
while allowing us to describe a general class of COPs for which strong variational sufficiency can be
fully characterized
by pointbased second-order conditions. The fulfillment of this assumption can be readily verified if the matrix set
J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
is available. In Appendix
A
, we show that Assumption
2.2
is satisfied for the highly important nonpolyhedral cases of the indicator function associated with positive-semidefinite cones and of the nuclear norm function.

3
Limiting Coderivative of Proximal Mappings

In this section, we evaluate and estimate the limiting coderivative (
12
) of the proximal mapping (
3
). The results obtained below are of their own interest while being important to establish the main second-order characterizations of strong variational sufficiency in (COP).

Recall first the following useful rule proved in
[
14
, Theorem 1.17]
in general Banach spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{n}
be a mapping strictly differentiable at
\bar{x}
and such that the derivative operator
S^{\prime}(\bar{x})
is surjective, and let
\Theta\subset\mathcal{R}^{n}
with
\bar{y}=S(\bar{x})\in\Theta
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{S^{-1}(\Theta)}(\bar{x})=S^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{T}\mathcal{N}_{\Theta}(\bar{y}).

Now we obtain a relationship between the limiting coderivative and generalized Jacobian of the proximal mapping associated with an extended-real-valued function.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an l.s.c. convex function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}r
,
\bar{u}\in\partial r(\bar{x})
. Given
\sigma>0
, for any
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
and
v\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r^{*}}(\bar{u}+\sigma\bar{x})(d)
, there is
U\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
with

\displaystyle v=(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}(I-U)d.

Proof.

Deduce from the definition of the limiting coderivative and the relationships in (
14
) and (
16
) that the inclusion
v\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r^{*}}(\bar{u}+\sigma\bar{x})(d)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle-d\in D^{*}(I+\sigma\partial r^{*})(\bar{u},\bar{u}+\sigma\bar{x})(-v)\Longleftrightarrow-d+v\in D^{*}(\sigma\partial r^{*})(\bar{u},\sigma\bar{x})(-v).

It is easy to observe the representation

\displaystyle\operatorname{gph}\partial r^{*}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&\sigma^{-1}I\end{array}\right]\operatorname{gph}\sigma\partial r^{*}.

Applying Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{gph}\partial r^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&\sigma I\end{array}\right]\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{gph}\sigma\partial r^{*}}(\bar{u},\sigma\bar{x}).

Combining (
14
), this leads us to the equivalencies

\displaystyle-d+v\in D^{*}(\sigma\partial r^{*})(\bar{u},\sigma\bar{x})(-v)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow-d+v\in D^{*}(\partial r^{*})(\bar{u},\bar{x})(-\sigma v)\Longleftrightarrow\sigma v\in D^{*}(\partial r)(\bar{x},\bar{u})(d-v),

which imply in turn by using (
14
) and (
16
) that

\displaystyle v-d\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(-d-(\sigma-1)v).

Finally, it follows from (
13
) that there exists a matrix
U\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
satisfying

\displaystyle v-d=U(-d-(\sigma-1)v).

This clearly yields the claimed result and completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Next we derive the main result of this section providing a novel explicit estimate of the limiting coderivative of the proximal mapping via the limiting Jacobian (
7
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
,
\bar{x}
, and
\bar{u}
be the same as in
Proposition
3.1
, and let
\sigma>0
. Suppose in addition that the function
r
satisfies Assumption
2.2
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
with the matrix
\overline{W}\in\overline{\nabla}\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
therein. Then for any
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
and
z\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r^{*}}(\bar{u}+\sigma\bar{x})(d)
satisfying
z=(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}(I-U)d
and
U\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
, we have

\displaystyle\|z\|^{2}\geq\|(I-UU^{\dagger})d\|^{2}\geq\|(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger})d\|^{2}.

(18)

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\|\sigma(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}Ud-UU^{\dagger}d\|\rightarrow 0,\;\mbox{as}\;\sigma\rightarrow\infty.

(19)

Proof.

Picking any
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
and
z\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r^{*}}(\bar{u}+\sigma\bar{x})(d)
, deduce from Proposition
3.1
the existence of a matrix
U\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
with rank
0\leq l\leq m
such that
z=(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}(I-U)d
. Since
U
is a positive-semidefinite matrix, its singular value decomposition can be expressed via an orthogonal matrix
R
and
S_{l}=\operatorname{Diag}\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l}\}
with
\lambda_{i}>0

(i=1,\ldots,l)
as

\displaystyle U=R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}S_{l}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]R^{T}.

Consequently, the Moore-Penrose inverse
U^{\dagger}
is given by

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}=R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}S^{-1}_{l}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]R^{T}.

Using the above tells us that

\displaystyle\|(I-UU^{\dagger})d\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(I-UU^{\dagger})d,(I-UU^{\dagger})d\right\rangle=\left\langle R^{T}d,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&I_{m-l}\end{array}\right]R^{T}d\right\rangle,

\displaystyle(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}(I-U)

\displaystyle=R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(I_{l}+(\sigma-1)S_{l})^{-1}&0\\
0&I_{m-l}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{l}-S_{l}&0\\
0&I_{m-l}\end{array}\right]R^{T}

\displaystyle=R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(I_{l}+(\sigma-1)S_{l})^{-1}(I_{l}-S_{l})&0\\
0&I_{m-l}\end{array}\right]R^{T},

\displaystyle\sigma(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}U-UU^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-(I_{l}+(\sigma-1)S_{l})^{-1}(I_{l}-S_{l})&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]R^{T},

which leads us therefore to the relationships

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle z,z\rangle&=\left\langle R^{T}d,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(I_{l}+(\sigma-1)S_{l})^{-1}(I_{l}-S_{l})&0\\
0&I_{m-l}\end{array}\right]^{2}R^{T}d\right\rangle\\
&\geq\left\langle R^{T}d,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&I_{m-l}\end{array}\right]R^{T}d\right\rangle=\|(I-UU^{\dagger})d\|^{2},\end{aligned}

(20)

and
\|\sigma(I+(\sigma-1)U)^{-1}Ud-UU^{\dagger}d\|\rightarrow 0
as
\sigma\rightarrow\infty
.
It follows from Assumption
2.2
that
UU^{\dagger}d\subset\operatorname{rge}U\subset\operatorname{rge}\overline{W}
. Observing that
\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}d\in\operatorname{rge}\operatorname{\overline{W}}
yields
\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}d-UU^{\dagger}d\in\operatorname{rge}\overline{W}
. Since
(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger})d\in\operatorname{ker}(\overline{W})=\operatorname{ker}(\overline{W}^{T})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger})d,\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}d-UU^{\dagger}d\right\rangle=0.

This allows us to deduce from (
20
) that

\displaystyle\langle z,z\rangle\geq\|(I-UU^{\dagger})d\|^{2}=\|(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger})d+(\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}d-UU^{\dagger}d)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\|(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger})d\|^{2}+\|\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}d-UU^{\dagger}d\|^{2}\geq\|(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger})d\|^{2},

which derives (
18
) and thus completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4
The Second-Order Variational Function and SSOSC

In this section, we introduce a novel notion of the second-order variational function and formulate in its terms an extended version of the SSOSC for a general class of composite optimization problems of type (COP).

We begin with defining the
Lagrangian function
associated with (COP) by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,u):=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{f_{0}(x)+g(F(x)+s)-\langle u,s\rangle\Big\}=L(x,u)-g^{*}(u),

where
L(x,u):=f_{0}(x)+\langle F(x),u\rangle
. The corresponding
Karush-Kuhn-Tucker
(KKT) conditions for (COP) form the following system:

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}L(x,u)=0,\quad u\in\partial g(F(x)).

(21)

For a given
\sigma>0
, the
augmented Lagrangian function
of (COP) is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u):=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{\mathcal{L}(x,s)-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|s-u\|^{2}\Big\}=f_{0}(x)-e_{\sigma g^{*}}(u+\sigma F(x))+\langle u,F(x)\rangle+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|F(x)\|^{2}.

The
generalized Hessian
of
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u)
with respect to the variable
x
is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{xx}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u):=\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(x,u)+\sigma F^{\prime}(x)^{T}J\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma g^{*}}(u+\sigma F(x))F^{\prime}(x).

For any
U\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma g^{*}}(u+\sigma F(x))
, observe that

\displaystyle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(x,u)+\sigma F^{\prime}(x)^{T}UF^{\prime}(x)\in\overline{\partial}_{xx}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u).

It follows from the chain rule in
[
3
, Proposition 2.3.3 and Theorem 2.6.6]
that

\displaystyle J\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u)(d)\subset\overline{\partial}_{xx}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u)(d)\;\mbox{ for any }\;d\in\mathcal{R}^{n},

which yields the positive-definiteness of all the matrices from
J\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u)
provided that those in
\overline{\partial}_{xx}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(x,u)
have this property.

Given further an extended real-valued function
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
with
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
u\in\partial r(x)
, let us introduce the
second-order variational function
(SOVF)
\Gamma_{r}(x,u):\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{r}(x,u)(v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{{d\in\mathcal{R}^{m},v=Wd}\atop{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(x+u)}}\langle v,d-v\rangle&\mbox{if}\ v\in\bigcup\limits_{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{r}(x+u)}\operatorname{rge}W,\\
\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Using (
4
) leads us to formulating the SSOSC for (COP).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{u})
be a solution to the KKT system (
21
). We say that the
strong second-order sufficient condition

(
SSOSC
)
holds at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{u})d,d\right\rangle+\Gamma_{g}(F(\bar{x}),\bar{u})(F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d)>0\;\mbox{ for all }\;d\neq 0.

(24)

Recall the
nuclear norm function

\|\cdot\|_{*}
for a given matrix is the sum of all its singular values. The importance of this function in a variety of applications to physics, engineering, computational mathematics, etc. has been well recognized. The example below provides an explicit calculation of the SSOSC for COPs, where
g
is the nuclear norm function.

Example 4.1
.

Let
p\leq q
be two positive integers, and let
g
be the nuclear norm function
\|\cdot\|_{*}
. Given
(\bar{x},\bar{u})
satisfying (
21
), define
X:=F(\bar{x})
,
U:=\bar{u}
, and
Y:=F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d
. Letting
A=X+U
, denote its singular value decomposition by
A=R\,[\Sigma\ 0]\,S^{T}
, where
R\in\mathcal{R}^{p\times p}
and
S\in\mathcal{R}^{q\times q}
are orthogonal matrices,
\Sigma:=\operatorname{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{p})
is the diagonal matrix of singular values of
A
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{p}\geq 0
, and
\widetilde{Y}=R^{T}YS
. Consider the index sets

\displaystyle\alpha:=\{1,\ldots,p\},\ \alpha_{1}:=\{i\in\alpha\,|\,\sigma_{i}>1\},\ \alpha_{2}:=\{i\in\alpha\,|\,\sigma_{i}=1\},\ \alpha_{3}:=\{i\in\alpha\,|\,\sigma_{i}<1\}.

Using the calculations in Example
A.2
of
Appendix
A
, the SSOSC in (
24
) is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{u})d,d\right\rangle+2\sum_{{i<j,i\in\alpha_{1}}\atop{j\in\alpha_{1}\cup\alpha_{2}}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{i}-1+\max\{\sigma_{j}-1,0\}}-1\right)\left(\frac{\widetilde{Y}_{ij}-\widetilde{Y}_{ji}}{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ +2\sum_{{i\in\alpha_{1}}\atop{j\in\alpha_{3}}}\left[\frac{1-\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{i}-1}\left(\frac{\widetilde{Y}_{ij}+\widetilde{Y}_{ji}}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1+\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{i}-1}\left(\frac{\widetilde{Y}_{ij}-\widetilde{Y}_{ji}}{2}\right)^{2}\right]+\sum_{i\in\alpha_{1}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}-1}\sum_{j=p+1}^{q}\widetilde{Y}_{ij}^{2}>0,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{for all }\;F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d\in\bigcup\limits_{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(X+U)}\operatorname{rge}W,\;d\neq 0.

The explicit expression for
\bigcup\limits_{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(X+U)}\operatorname{rge}W
is given in Example
A.2
of Appendix
A
.

Under the fulfillment of Assumption
2.2
, the second-order variational function (
4
) can be equivalently expressed at the KKT point
(x,u)
as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{g}(F(x),u)(v)=\min_{-d\in D^{*}\partial g(F(x),u)(-v)}\langle v,d\rangle

(25)

(see
[
25
, Lemma 5.3]
for more details).
The function
\Gamma_{g}(F(x),u)
is further simplified to

\displaystyle\Gamma_{g}(F(x),u)(v)=\left\langle v,\left(\overline{W}^{\dagger}-I\right)v\right\rangle:=\Upsilon_{g}(F(x),u)(v).

(26)

Combining this with the identity

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{W\in J\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(F(x)+u)}\operatorname{rge}W=\operatorname{ker}\left(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}\right),

the SSOSC (
24
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(x,u)d,d\right\rangle+\Upsilon_{g}(F(x),u)(F^{\prime}(x)d)>0\;\mbox{ for all }\;F^{\prime}(x)d\in\operatorname{ker}\left(I-\overline{W}\overline{W}^{\dagger}\right),\,d\neq 0.

To conclude this section, we present a pivotal result from
[
22
]
that is deeply intertwined with strong variational sufficiency for local optimality. Let us first recall the definitions.

Definition 4.2
.

A function
q:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is a
generalized quadratic form
on
\mathcal{R}^{m}
if
q(0)=0
and the subgradient mapping
\partial q
is generalized linear, i.e.,
\operatorname{gph}\partial q
is a subspace of
\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R}^{m}
. A function
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
is
generalized twice differentiable
at
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}r
for
\bar{u}\in\partial r(\bar{x})
if it is twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
with the second-order subderivative
d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})
being a generalized quadratic form on
\mathcal{R}^{m}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{R}
be an l.s.c. proper function with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
\bar{u}\in\partial r(\bar{x})
. The
quadratic bundle
of
r
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
is defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{quad}r(\bar{x},\bar{u}):=\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{the collection of generalized quadratic forms}\ q\mbox{ for which there exist }\\
(x^{k},u^{k})\rightarrow(\bar{x},\bar{u})\mbox{ with $r$ generalized twice differentiable
at }x^{k}\mbox{ for }u^{k}\\
\mbox{ and such that the generalized quadratic
forms }q^{k}=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}r(x^{k},u^{k})\\
\mbox{ converge epigraphically to }q.\end{array}\right.

Quadratic bundles play an important role in characterizing the variational convexity of functions. The recent papers
[
4
,
8
]
systematically investigate the variational convexity of prox-regular functions using quadratic bundles, particularly in settings where subdifferential continuity is absent.
It is proved in
[
22
, Theorem 5]
that strong variational sufficiency can be fully characterized by the quadratic bundle.
We formulate this result for further references.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{u})
be a solution to the KKT system (
21
). The strong variational sufficient condition for the local optimality of
\bar{x}
in
(P)
relative to
\bar{u}
holds if and only if for every
q\in\operatorname{quad}g(F(\bar{x}),\bar{u})
we have the strict inequality

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\bar{u})d,d\right\rangle+q(F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d)>0\;\mbox{ whenever }\;d\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

5
Characterizations of Strong Variational Sufficiency

In this section, we establish pointbased second-order characterizations of strong variational sufficiency for local optimality in problem
(P)
associated with a general class of COPs under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
. Crucial building blocks of our analysis are given in the following collections of lemmas, which are of their own interest.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
l:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}
be an l.s.c. function that is positively homogeneous of degree two. Given a linear operator
M:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{n}
, assume that there is
\bar{\kappa}>0
such that for any
d
satisfying
Md=0
we have
l(d)\geq\bar{\kappa}\|d\|^{2}
. Then there exist numbers
\kappa\in(0,\bar{\kappa}]
and
c>0
ensuring that

\displaystyle l(d)+c\|Md\|^{2}\geq\kappa\|d\|^{2}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}.

Proof.

Since
l
is positively homogeneous of degree two with
l(0)\geq 0
, it suffices to verify the claimed inequality for all
d
satisfying
Md\neq 0
and
\|d\|=1
. Specifically, we aim to show that

\displaystyle l(d)+c\|Md\|^{2}\geq\kappa\|d\|^{2}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;Md\neq 0,\,\|d\|=1.

Consider the function
\theta(d):=\frac{l(d)-\bar{\kappa}\|d\|^{2}}{\|Md\|^{2}}
on the compact set
\{d\,|\,\|d\|=1,\,Md\neq 0\}
. This function is l.s.c. and tends to
\infty
as
d
approaches any boundary point of the set. By the classical Weierstrass theorem,
\theta
attains its minimum denoted by
c^{*}
. Consequently, the claimed estimate is satisfied for any number
c\geq-c^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
\bar{u}\in\partial r(\bar{x})
satisfy Assumption
2.1
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})=\left\{d\,\left|\,dr(\bar{x})(d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\right.\right\}=\operatorname{dom}D(\partial r)(\bar{x},\bar{u}).

(27)

Proof.

The first equality in (
27
) follows from
[
12
, Proposition 3.8]
. To proceed further, deduce from

[
23
, Proposition 13.20]
, that
d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})
is convex and is expressed as

\displaystyle d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})=\gamma_{C}^{2}

via the gauge of
C=\{v\,|\,d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v)\leq 1\}
, which is a closed convex set containing the origin. Since
\gamma_{C}
is a positively homogeneous convex function with
\gamma_{C}(0)=0
, it follows from
[
1
, Proposition 2.124]
that it is subdifferentiable at any
v\in\operatorname{dom}d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})
. Specifically, for every such
v
which is not a minimizer of
d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})
, there exists a subgradient
w\in\partial\gamma_{C}(v)
satisfying

\displaystyle\gamma_{C}(y)\geq\gamma_{C}(v)+\langle w,y-v\rangle.

Applying
[
23
, Propositions 13.5 and 13.20]
tells us that

\displaystyle d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v)=\gamma^{2}_{C}(v)>0

and there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
v
such that for any
y\in\mathcal{O}
we get
d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(y)>0
and

\displaystyle\gamma_{C}(v)+\langle w,y-v\rangle>0.

This implies therefore the relationships

\displaystyle d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(y)=\gamma_{C}^{2}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{C}^{2}(v)+2\gamma_{C}(v)\langle w,y-v\rangle+\langle w,y-v\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\geq d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v)-2\gamma_{C}(v)\|w\|\|y-v\|.

It follows from
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
that
\partial d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v)\neq\emptyset
. Conversely, if
v\in\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}{d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(x)}
, then
0\in\partial d^{2}r(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v)
and the fulfillment of (
27
) is a consequence of
[
23
, Theorem 13.40]
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
with
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
\bar{u}\in\partial r(\bar{x})
satisfy Assumption
2.1
. Given any

6
Conclusions and Future Research

The paper discovers a general framework in composite optimization, where strong variational sufficiency for local optimality admits complete second-order characterizations via an appropriate SSOSC and the positive-definiteness of generalized Hessian matrices of the associated augmented Lagrangian. The imposed assumptions and obtained characterizations cover polyhedral and nonpolyhedral problems of composite optimizations without any nondegeneracy and/or other constraint qualifications and are explicitly specified for SDP and nuclear norm-based problems.

Our future research will be partly concentrate on revealing additional classes of nonpolyhedral problems of composite optimization that exhibit explicit second-order characterizations of strong variational sufficiency for local optimality without any nondegeneracy assumptions. We also intend to characterize the notion of (not strong) variational sufficiency for nonpolyhedral optimization problems. Our main attention will be paid to developing second-order numerical algorithms with establishing their local convergence rates based on the obtained second-order characterizations of variational and strong variational sufficiency in composite optimization.

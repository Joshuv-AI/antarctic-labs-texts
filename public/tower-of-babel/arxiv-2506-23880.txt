Higher path groupoids and the holonomy of formal power series connections
Matthew Cellot

Abstract
. Building on ideas of Kohno, we develop a framework for the construction of higher holonomy functors via the transport of formal power series connections. Using these techniques, we obtain functors from the path groupoid, the path 2-groupoid, and the path 3-groupoid of a manifold. As an application, we construct a Gray functor from the path 3-groupoid of the configuration space of m m points in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for n ⩾ 4 n\geqslant 4 .



Introduction

The purpose of this article is to construct a higher categorical analogue of holonomy functors. Let’s first recall the construction of holonomy functors using the notion of parallel transport for a connection. Let
M
denote a smooth manifold and consider a connection
\nabla
on a vector bundle
E\to M
. Given a smooth curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
, a section
X
of
E
along
\gamma
is
flat
if
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}X=0
. Suppose given an element
e\in E
above
\gamma(0)
. Then the
parallel transport
of
e
along
\gamma
is the unique flat section
X
of
E
above
\gamma
such that
X_{\gamma(0)}=e
. Therefore,
\nabla
defines a way of moving elements of the fibers along curves, that is, a linear map
\Theta(\gamma)\colon E_{\gamma(0)}\,\tilde{\to}\,E_{\gamma(1)}
that sends
e\in E_{\gamma(0)}
to
X_{\gamma(1)}\in E_{\gamma(1)}
. In fact, the points and the smooth paths of
M
assemble to form a category
\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
called the
path groupoid
of
M
(see Section
1
), and the parallel transport for
\nabla
defines a
holonomy functor

\mathrm{Hol}^{1}_{\nabla}\colon\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{Vect}

that sends a point
x\in M
to the vector space
E_{x}
, and a smooth path
\gamma
from
x
to
y
to the linear map
\Theta(\gamma)\colon E_{x}\,\tilde{\to}\,E_{y}
.

Note that in the particular case of a trivial vector bundle
E=M\times V
, a connection on
E
is the same as an
\mathrm{End}(V)
-valued differential 1-form
\omega\in C^{1}_{dR}(M)\otimes\mathrm{End}(V)
, and the parallel transport along a smooth curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
induces the linear map

\Theta(\gamma)=\mathrm{Id}_{V}+\int_{\gamma}\omega+\int_{\gamma}\omega\omega+\int_{\gamma}\omega\omega\omega+\cdots\colon V\,\tilde{\to}\,V,

where
\displaystyle\int\omega\ldots\omega
denotes the iterated integral of
\omega,\ldots,\omega
, as defined by Chen
[
Che73
]
(see Section
2
). This then defines a holonomy functor

\mathrm{Hol}^{1}_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)\to\GL(V),

where
\GL(V)
is the category with only one object and with morphisms given by the automorphisms of
V
.

Several very interesting functors arise in this setting. If we denote by
\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{C})
the configuration space of
m
pairwise distinct points in
\mathbb{C}
, then a loop
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{C})
can be seen as a pure braid with
m
strands. Moreover, the homotopy class of a loop in
\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{C})
corresponds to the isotopy class of a pure braid, and we get an isomorphism between the fundamental group of
\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{C})
and the pure braid group
P_{m}
. The Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov connection
\omega_{KZ}
is a flat connection on a trivial vector bundle
\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{C})\times V\to\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{C})
, where the vector space
V
is obtained by taking the tensor product of
m
representations of a simple Lie algebra. The parallel transport of
\omega_{KZ}
induces representations of
P_{m}
and of the braid group
B_{m}=P_{m}/\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, where we take the quotient by the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. The Drinfeld-Kohno theorem then states that the representations of braid groups induced by the Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov connection correspond to the representations of braid groups resulting from the corresponding quantum group (see details in
[
Oht02
, Chapter 5]
). Moreover, these representations induce the Kontsevich integral, a powerful invariant of knots that is universal among Vassiliev invariants (see details in
[
Oht02
, Chapter 6]
).

In order to construct higher categorical analogues of holonomy functors, we build on a method due to Kohno
[
Koh16
,
Koh21
,
Koh22
]
. We first introduce higher categorical versions of the path groupoid of a smooth manifold
M
: the path 2-groupoid
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
and the path 3-groupoid
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
. These structures allow us to consider higher homotopical data of
M
. Moreover, we associate to a coaugmented differential graded coalgebra
C
a strict 2-category
\mathcal{C}_{2}(C)
and a Gray 3-category
\mathcal{C}_{3}(C)
via the cobar complex of
C
. Then we show that the transport of a formal power series connection with values in the cobar complex of
C
yields functors from the path 2-groupoid and the path 3-groupoid of
M
to the 2-category
\mathcal{C}_{2}(C)
and the Gray 3-category
\mathcal{C}_{3}(C)
, respectively:

Theorem A
.

Let
\omega
be a formal power series connection on a smooth manifold
M
. The transport of
\omega
induces higher holonomy functors

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hol}_{\omega}^{2}\colon\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)\to\mathcal{C}_{2}(C)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hol}_{\omega}^{3}\colon\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)\to\mathcal{C}_{3}(C).

Theorem
A
corresponds to Theorems
2
and
3
in Section
4
.

Next we apply our construction to a formal power series connection introduced by Komendarczyk, Koytcheff and Volić
[
KKV24
]
that generalizes the Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov connection. This yields a functor from the path 3-groupoid of the configuration space of
m
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geqslant 4
(see remark in Section
5
).

For
k\geqslant 4
, we conjecture the existence of holonomy
k
-functors
\mathrm{Hol}_{\omega}^{k}
from the path
k
-groupoid
\mathcal{P}_{k}(M)
of a manifold
M
to some target
k
-category
\mathcal{C}_{k}(C)
. Nevertheless, a precise definition of
\mathcal{P}_{k}(M)
remains to be written.

This work is motivated by the study of higher order versions of the Knizhnik-Kamolodchikov equation and the derived representations of braid groups. Generalizations of these types of techniques can be seen in the work of Cohen and Gitler
[
CG02
]
who give detailed descriptions of the homology of loop spaces of configuration spaces. Moreover, many related results help to better understand higher path groupoids. We refer for instance to work by Baez and Schreiber
[
BS04
]
on higher gauge theory and 2-connections, as well as work by Faria Martins and Picken
[
FMP10
,
FMP11
]
on higher dimensional holonomy. For further work on higher holonomy functors, see Kohno
[
Koh16
,
Koh20
,
Koh21
]
, Schreiber and Waldorf
[
SW07
,
SW08
]
. Moreover, we expect that higher holonomy functors may be useful in the study of braided surfaces in 4-space, as studied by Kamada
[
Kam02
]
, Carter and Saito
[
CS98
]
, as well as the study of loop braid groups, as presented by Damiani
[
Dam17
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we recall the definition of the path groupoid, the path 2-groupoid, and the path 3-groupoid of a smooth manifold. In Section
2
, we recall the notion of iterated integral due to Chen
[
Che73
]
. In Section
3
, we define the notion of formal power series connection (compare to
[
Che73
,
Che77
]
). In Section
4
, we construct the holonomy functor, the holonomy 2-functor, and the holonomy 3-functor of a formal power series connection, and we prove Theorem
A
. In Section
5
, we describe the holonomy 3-functor of a formal power series connection defined by Komendarczyk, Koytcheff and Volić
[
KKV24
]
on the configuration space of
m
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geqslant 4
.

Throughout this work, all vector spaces, algebras and coalgebras will be over the field
\mathbb{R}
.

1.
Higher path groupoids

In this section, we follow the presentation given by Faria Martins and Picken
[
FMP11
]
of the path groupoid, the path 2-groupoid, and the path 3-groupoid of a smooth manifold. Similar definitions can be found in
[
SW07
,
SW08
,
Koh16
,
Koh20
,
Koh21
,
FMP10
]
.

Throughout this section, we suppose
M
is a smooth manifold (possibly with boundary).

1.1.
Higher order paths

An
n
-path
, for an integer
n\geqslant 0
, is a smooth map
\alpha\colon[0,1]^{n}\to M
for which there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
i\in\{1,\ldots n\}
,

\displaystyle 0\leqslant t_{i}\leqslant\varepsilon

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\alpha(t_{1},\ldots,t_{i},\ldots,t_{n})=\alpha(t_{1},\ldots,0,\ldots,t_{n}),

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon\leqslant t_{i}\leqslant 1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\alpha(t_{1},\ldots,t_{i},\ldots,t_{n})=\alpha(t_{1},\ldots,1,\ldots t_{n}),

and
\alpha([0,1]^{n-1}\times\{0\})
and
\alpha([0,1]^{n-1}\times\{1\})
both consist of a single point. In particular, a
0
-path is just a point.

An
n
-path from
f
to
g
, where
f
and
g
are
(n-1)
-paths, is an
n
-path
\alpha\colon[0,1]^{n}\to M
such that

\displaystyle\alpha(0,-,\cdots,-)=f

and

\displaystyle\alpha(1,-,\cdots,-)=g,

where
\alpha(x,-,\cdots,-)
, for
x\in[0,1]
, denotes the
(n-1)
-path defined by

\alpha(x,-,\cdots,-)(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1})=\alpha(x,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}),

for all
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1})\in[0,1]^{n-1}
.

The
composition of two 1-paths

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
such that
\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(0)
is the 1-path
\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}
defined by:

\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}(t)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{1}(2t)&\text{if}\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
\gamma_{2}(2t-1)&\text{if}\ \frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

1.2.
Rank-1 homotopies

A
rank-1 homotopy
is a 2-path
h\colon[0,1]^{2}\to M
such that for all
(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2}
, the rank of its differential
d_{(s,t)}h
is less than 2. Two 1-paths
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are
rank-1 homotopic
if there exists a rank-1 homotopy from
\gamma
to
\gamma^{\prime}
.

A
1
-track

[\gamma]
is the rank-1 homotopy class of a 1-path
\gamma
. The
composition
[\gamma_{1}]\circ[\gamma_{2}]
of two 1-tracks

[\gamma_{1}]
and
[\gamma_{2}]
such that
\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(0)
is the 1-track
[\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}]
. Notice that the composition of two 1-tracks doesn’t depend on the choice of representative 1-paths and that it is associative. Moreover, if we denote by
c_{x}
the constant 1-path at a point
x\in M
, then for any 1-path
\gamma
,

\displaystyle[\gamma]\circ[c_{\gamma(1)}]=[\gamma]

and

\displaystyle[c_{\gamma(0)}]\circ[\gamma]=[\gamma].

1.3.
Path groupoids

The
path groupoid
of
M
is the category
\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
whose objects are given by the points of
M
and whose morphisms are 1-tracks, with composition induced by the composition of 1-tracks. Notice that every 1-track is invertible.

1.4.
Vertical composition of 2-paths

Let
g
and
h
be 2-paths such that
g(1,-)=h(0,-)
. The
vertical composition
of
g
and
h
is the 2-path
g\cdot h
defined by:

(g\cdot h)(s,t)=\begin{cases}g(2s,t)&\text{if}\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\ \text{and}\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\\
h(2s-1,t)&\text{if}\ \frac{1}{2}\leqslant s\leqslant 1\ \text{and}\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

1.5.
Horizontal composition of 2-paths

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be 2-paths such that

h_{1}([0,1]\times\{1\})=h_{2}([0,1]\times\{0\}).

The
horizontal composition
of
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
is the 2-path
h_{1}\circ h_{2}
defined by:

(h_{1}\circ h_{2})(s,t)=\begin{cases}h_{1}(s,2t)&\text{if}\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\ \text{and}\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
h_{2}(s,2t-1)&\text{if}\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\ \text{and}\ \frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

1.6.
Rank-2 homotopies

A
rank-2 homotopy
is a 3-path
\alpha\colon[0,1]^{3}\to M
such that:

•

for all
(r,s,t)\in[0,1]^{3}
, the rank of its differential
d_{(r,s,t)}\alpha
is less than 3,

•

\alpha(-,0,-)
and
\alpha(-,1,-)
are rank-1 homotopies.

Two 2-paths
g
and
h
are
rank-2 homotopic
if there exists a rank-2 homotopy from
g
to
h
.

A
2-track

[g]
is the rank-2 homotopy class of a 2-path
g
. The
vertical composition of 2-tracks
is induced by the vertical composition of 2-paths, and the
horizontal composition of 2-tracks
is induced by the horizontal composition of 2-paths. Notice that these compositions are associative and satisfy the interchange law.

1.7.
Path 2-groupoids

The
path 2-groupoid of
M
is the strict 2-category
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
defined as follows. The objects of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
are the points of
M
. The 1-morphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
are given by 1-tracks, with composition induced by the composition of 1-tracks. A 2-morphism in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
from
[\gamma]
to
[\gamma^{\prime}]
is given by a 2-track
[g]
such that
[g(0,-,-)]=[\gamma]
and
[g(1,-,-)]=[\gamma^{\prime}]
. The identity of a 1-morphism
[\gamma]
is given by the rank-2 homotopy class of the constant 2-path at
\gamma
. The vertical and the horizontal composition of 2-morphisms are induced by the vertical and the horizontal composition of 2-paths, respectively. We refer the reader to
[
FMP10
]
for the details on the vertical and the horizontal composition of 2-morphisms. Notice that all 2-tracks are invertible.

1.8.
Laminated rank-2 homotopies

We recall a stronger notion of rank-2 homotopy due to Faria Martins and Picken
[
FMP11
]
. A
laminated rank-2 homotopy
is a rank-2 homotopy
\alpha
such that for all
(r,s)\in[0,1]^{2}
at least one of the following conditions holds:

•

for all
t\in[0,1]
, there exist constants
(a_{t},b_{t})\neq(0,0)
such that

a_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\alpha(r,s,t)+b_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\alpha(r,s,t)=0,

•

for all
t\in[0,1]
, there exist constants
(a_{t},b_{t})\neq(0,0)
such that

a_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\alpha(r,s,t)+b_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\alpha(r,s,t)=0,

•

there exist constants
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
such that, for all
t\in[0,1]
,

a\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\alpha(r,s,t)+b\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\alpha(r,s,t)=0.

A
laminated 2-track

[g]
is the laminated rank-2 homotopy class of a 2-path
g
.

1.9.
Whiskering of 2-paths by 1-paths

Let
\gamma
be a 1-path and
g
be a 2-path such that

\gamma(1)=g([0,1]\times\{0\}).

The
left whiskering of
g
with
\gamma
is the 2-path
\gamma\circ g
given by:

\gamma\circ g(s,t)=\begin{cases}\gamma(2t)&\text{if }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }0\leqslant s\leqslant 1,\\
g(2t-1,s)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant s\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

Let
\gamma
be a 1-path and
g
be a 2-path such that

g([0,1]\times\{1\})=\gamma(0).

The
right whiskering of
g
with
\gamma
is the 2-path
g\circ\gamma
given by:

g\circ\gamma(s,t)=\begin{cases}g(2t,s)&\text{if }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }0\leqslant s\leqslant 1,\\
\gamma(2t-1)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant s\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

The
left (respectively right) whiskering of a laminated 2-track with a 1-track
is the laminated rank-2 homotopy class of the left (respectively right) whiskering of a representative 2-path with a representative 1-path. Notice that this operation doesn’t depend on the choice of representatives.

1.10.
Horizontal compositions of laminated 2-tracks

The horizontal composition of 2-paths does not descend to the quotient by laminated rank-2 homotopy. Therefore, we use whiskering to define two more notions of horizontal composition.

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be 2-paths such that

h_{1}([0,1]\times\{1\})=h_{2}([0,1]\times\{0\}).

We denote by

\begin{pmatrix}&h_{2}\\
h_{1}&\end{pmatrix}
the 2-path defined by
\begin{pmatrix}&h_{2}\\
h_{1}&\end{pmatrix}=(h_{1}\circ h_{2}(0,-))\cdot(h_{1}(1,-)\circ h_{2})
. More explicitly:

\begin{pmatrix}&h_{2}\\
h_{1}&\end{pmatrix}(s,t)=\begin{cases}h_{1}(2s,2t)&\text{if }0\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
h_{1}(1,2t)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
h_{2}(0,2t-1)&\text{if }0\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\\
h_{2}(2s-1,2t-1)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\\
\end{cases}

We denote by
\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}&\\
&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}
the 2-path defined by
\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}&\\
&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}=(h_{1}(0,-)\circ h_{2})\cdot(h_{1}\circ h_{2}(1,-))
. More explicitly:

\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}&\\
&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}(s,t)=\begin{cases}h_{1}(0,2t)&\text{if }0\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
h_{1}(2s-1,2t)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
h_{2}(2s,2t-1)&\text{if }0\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\\
h_{2}(1,2t-1)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\\
\end{cases}

These compositions induce compositions of laminated 2-tracks denoted by
\begin{pmatrix}&[h_{2}]\\
[h_{1}]&\end{pmatrix}
and

\begin{pmatrix}[h_{1}]&\\
&[h_{2}]\end{pmatrix}
, respectively.

1.11.
Upward composition of 3-paths

Let
J
and
K
be 3-paths such that

J(1,-,-)=K(0,-,-).

The
upward composition
of
J
and
K
is the 3-path defined by:

J*K(r,s,t)=\begin{cases}J(2r,s,t)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\\
K(2r-1,s,t)&\text{if }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

1.12.
Vertical composition of 3-paths

Let
J
and
K
be 3-paths such that

J(-,1,-)=K(-,0,-).

The
vertical composition
of
J
and
K
is the 3-path defined by:

J\cdot K(r,s,t)=\begin{cases}J(r,2s,t)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\\
K(r,2s-1,t)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ \frac{1}{2}\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

1.13.
Rank-3 homotopies

A
good 3-path
is a 3-path
J\colon[0,1]^{3}\to M
such that

•

for all
r,r^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,1]
,
J(r,0,t)=J(r^{\prime},0,t)
,

•

for all
r,r^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,1]
,
J(r,1,t)=J(r^{\prime},1,t)
.

A
rank-3 homotopy
is a 4-path
W\colon[0,1]^{4}\to M
such that

•

for all
(q,r,s,t)\in[0,1]^{n}
, the rank of
d_{(q,r,s,t)}W
is less than 4,

•

the restrictions
W(-,0,-,-)
and
W(-,1,-,-)
are laminated rank-2 homotopies,

•

the restrictions
W(-,r,0,-)
and
W(-,r,1,-)
are independent of
r\in[0,1]
and define rank-1 homotopies.

Two good 3-paths
J
and
J^{\prime}
are
rank-3 homotopic
if there exists a rank-3 homotopy from
J
to
J^{\prime}
.
A
3-track

[J]
is the rank-3 homotopy class of a good 3-path
J
.

1.14.
Whiskering of 3-tracks by 1-tracks

Let
\gamma
be a 1-path and let
J
be a 3-path such that

\gamma(1)=J([0,1]^{2}\times\{0\}).

The
left whiskering of
J
with
\gamma
is the 3-path
\gamma\circ J
defined by:

\gamma\circ J(r,s,t)=\begin{cases}\gamma(2t)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
J(r,s,2t-1)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

Let
\gamma
be a 1-path and let
J
be a 3-path such that

J([0,1]^{2}\times\{1\})=\gamma(0).

The
right whiskering of
J
with
\gamma
is the 3-path
J\circ\gamma
defined by:

J\circ\gamma(r,s,t)=\begin{cases}J(r,s,2t)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }0\leqslant t\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\\
\gamma(2t-1)&\text{if }0\leqslant r\leqslant 1,\ 0\leqslant s\leqslant 1\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 1.\end{cases}

The
left (respectively right) whiskering of a 3-track with a 1-track
is the rank-3 homotopy class of the left (respectively right) whiskering of a representative good 3-path with a representative 1-path. Notice that this operation doesn’t depend on the choice of representatives.

1.15.
Path 3-groupoids

The
path 3-groupoid of
M
(or the
fundamental Gray 3-groupoid of
M
) is the Gray 3-groupoid
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
defined as follows. The objects of
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
are the points of
M
. The 1-morphisms of
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
are given by 1-tracks, with composition induced by the composition of 1-tracks. A 2-morphism in
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
from
[\gamma]
to
[\gamma^{\prime}]
is given by a laminated 2-track
[g]
such that
[g(0,-,-)]=[\gamma]
and
[g(1,-,-)]=[\gamma^{\prime}]
. The vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of 2-morphisms are induced by the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of 2-paths, respectively. A 3-morphism in
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
from
[g]
to
[h]
is given by a 3-track
[J]
such that
[J(0,-,-)]=[g]
and
[J(1,-,-)]=[h]
. The upward composition, the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of 3-morphisms are induced by the upward composition, the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of 3-paths, respectively. We refer the reader to
[
FMP11
]
for more details on
\mathcal{P}_{3}(M)
and for the proof that it is a Gray 3-groupoid.

2.
Iterated integrals

In this section, we recall the notion of differential form on a differentiable space, and we describe a certain class of differential forms on path spaces called iterated integrals. We refer the reader to
[
Che73
,
Che77
]
for more details.

2.1.
Differentiable spaces and differentiable maps

A
convex
n
-region
(or simply a
convex region
) is a closed convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for some
n\geqslant 0
. A map
f
from a convex
n
-region
U
to a convex region
V
is
smooth
if there exists a smooth extension of
f
to an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
differentiable space
is a Hausdorff space
X
equipped with a family of maps, called
plots
, satisfying the following conditions:

•

every plot is a continuous map
\phi:U\to X
, where
U
is a convex region,

•

if
\phi:U\to X
is a plot,
U^{\prime}
is a convex region, and
f:U^{\prime}\to U
is a smooth map, then
\phi\circ f
is a plot,

•

every map
\{0\}\to X
is a plot.

We say that a plot
\psi
with domain
V

goes through
another plot
\phi
with domain
U
via
f
if there exists a smooth map
f:V\to U
such that
\psi=\phi\circ f
. Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be differentiable spaces. A
differentiable map
from
X
to
X^{\prime}
is a continuous map
f:X\to X^{\prime}
such that, for every plot
\phi
of
X
,
f\circ\phi
is a plot of
X^{\prime}
.

The notion of differentiable space is weaker than that of smooth manifold and will help us avoid certain limitations associated to smooth manifolds. In particular, smooth manifolds are differentiable spaces with plots given by smooth maps
\phi:U\to M
where
U
is a convex region.

2.2.
Differential forms on differentiable spaces

For any
p\geqslant 0
, a
differential
p
-form
on a differentiable space
X
is a rule that assigns to each plot
\phi:U\to X
a differential
p
-form
\omega_{\phi}\in C_{dR}^{p}(U)
such that, if
\psi
is a plot that goes through
\phi
via
f
, then:

f^{*}\omega_{\psi}=\omega_{\phi}.

A
differential form
on
X
is a differential
p
-form for some
p
called its
degree
. The set of differential
p
-forms on a differentiable space
X
is denoted by
C_{dR}^{p}(X)
. The differential forms on a differentiable space
X
define a commutative differential graded algebra
C_{dR}^{*}(X)=\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}C_{dR}^{p}(X)
whose sum, exterior product and exterior derivative are given by:

\displaystyle(\omega+\omega^{\prime})_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\phi}+\omega^{\prime}_{\phi},

\displaystyle(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\phi}\wedge\omega_{\phi},

\displaystyle(d\omega)_{\phi}

\displaystyle=d\omega_{\phi},

for any plot
\phi\colon U\to X
.

2.3.
Pointed path spaces

The
pointed path space
of a smooth manifold
M
is the set
P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
of 1-paths in
M
from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
(see Section
1.1
) with the compact-open topology, where
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are points of
M
. The pointed path space of
M
is a differentiable space when equipped with the following plots. A plot of
P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
is a continuous map
\phi:U\to P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
, such that the adjoint map
\widetilde{\phi}:U\times[0,1]\to M
is smooth.

2.4.
Iterated integrals on paths spaces

Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
be differential forms of positive degree on a smooth manifold
M
. Let
\phi:U\to P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
be a plot of
P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
, where
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are points of
M
, and let
\Delta^{k}
denote the
k
-simplex given by:

\Delta^{k}=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\mid 0\leqslant t_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant t_{k}\leqslant 1\}.

The plot
\phi
induces a smooth map
\widetilde{\phi}:\Delta^{k}\times U\to M^{k}
defined by:

\widetilde{\phi}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k},x)=\left(\phi(x)(t_{1}),\ldots,\phi(x)(t_{k})\right),

for all
((t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}),x)\in\Delta^{k}\times U
.

We denote by
\pi_{i}:M^{k}\to M
the projection onto the
i
-th factor for any smooth manifold
M
. The
iterated integral
of differential forms
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
of positive degree on
M
is the differential form
\displaystyle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k}
on the differentiable space
P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
defined by:

\left(\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k}\right)_{\!\phi}=\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{\phi}^{*}(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_{k}^{*}\omega_{k}),

for any plot
\phi:U\to P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
, where
\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{k}}
denotes integration along the fiber of
\Delta^{k}\times U\to U
. Linear combinations of iterated integrals will also be called iterated integrals. The iterated integral

\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k}

is a differential form of degree
\deg(\omega_{1})+\cdots+\deg(\omega_{k})-k
, where
\mathrm{deg}(\omega_{i})
denotes the degree of
\omega_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

We recall a useful formula:

Lemma 2.1

(see
[
Koh16
]
)
.

Let
\omega_{1},\ldots\omega_{k}
be differential forms of positive degree on a smooth manifold
M
. On the pointed path space
P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
, we have:

\displaystyle d\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{\nu_{i-1}+1}\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{i-1}\,d\omega_{i}\,\omega_{i+1}\ldots\omega_{k}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{\nu_{i}+1}\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{i-1}(\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{i+1})\omega_{i+2}\ldots\omega_{k},

where
\nu_{i}=\deg(\omega_{1})+\cdots+\deg(\omega_{i})-i
.

2.5.
The bar complex

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and let
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
be points of
M
. The
bar complex of iterated integrals on
M
(or simply the
bar complex on
M
) is the subcomplex
B^{*}(M)
of the complex
C^{*}_{dR}(P(M;x_{0},x_{1}))
of differential forms on the pointed path space of
M
such that for all
q\geqslant 0
, the vector space
B^{q}(M)
is the sum of the images of the multilinear maps

\int\colon C_{dR}^{p_{1}}(M)\otimes\cdots\otimes C_{dR}^{p_{k}}(M)\to C_{dR}^{q}(P(M;x_{0},x_{1}))

defined by the iterated integrals, where the sum is taken over all positive integers
k,p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
such that
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}-k=q
. As a consequence of Lemma
2.1
, we have that
d\left(B^{q}(M)\right)\subset B^{q+1}(M)
.

A nonzero element of
B^{*}(M)
is
homogeneous of length
k
, for some
k\geqslant 0
, if it is the sum of iterated integrals of the form:

\int\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{k}.

2.6.
The integration of iterated integrals over bounded plots

A
bounded
plot of a differentiable space is a plot with bounded domain. Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
be differential forms of positive degree on a smooth manifold
M
, and let
\phi
be a bounded plot of
P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
with domain
U
. Then the
integration of
\displaystyle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k}
over
\phi
, denoted by
\displaystyle\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},\phi\rangle
, is defined by the following equation:

\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},\phi\rangle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U}\left(\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k}\right)_{\!\phi}&\text{if $\mathrm{deg}(\omega_{1})+\cdots+\mathrm{deg}(\omega_{k})-k=\dim U$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that for
k=1
, we get the integral:

\langle\int\omega_{1},\phi\rangle=\int_{[0,1]\times U}\widetilde{\phi}^{*}\omega_{1},

where
\widetilde{\phi}
is the adjoint map to the plot
\phi
. In particular, if
U=*
, then the above is simply the integration of a differential form on
M
along a path.

For
k=0
, we set:

\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},\phi\rangle=\delta_{0}^{\dim U}.

For any bounded plot
\phi\colon U\to P(M;x_{0},x_{1})
, taking the integration over
\phi
defines a linear map

\langle\,\cdot\,,\phi\rangle\colon B^{*}(M)\to\mathbb{R}.

3.
Formal power series connections

In this section, we build on Chen’s theory of formal power series connections
[
Che73
,
Che77
]
and its formulation by Kohno
[
Koh16
,
Koh20
,
Koh22
]
. We recall the definition of the cobar complex of a coaugmented differential graded coalgebra, the definition of a formal power series connection, and the definition of its transport. We then recall a result of Chen that relates the transport of a formal power series connection to the homology of the loop space of a simply connection manifold.

Throughout this section,
C
denotes a coaugmented differential graded coalgebra.

3.1.
The cobar complex

The
free augmented algebra
on a graded vector space
V
is the algebra
TV
given by

TV=\mathbb{R}\oplus V\oplus\left(V\otimes V\right)\oplus\left(V\otimes V\otimes V\right)\oplus\cdots

with multiplication given by concatenation. The
desuspension
of a graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 1}V_{n}
is the graded vector space
s^{-1}V
given by
(s^{-1}V)_{n}=V_{n+1}
. The element of
s^{-1}V
corresponding to
v\in V
is denoted by
s^{-1}v
.

Let
\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to C
denote the coaugmentation of the coalgebra
C
, and let
\partial
denote its differential. We suppose
\partial
is of degree
-1
. The
coaugmentation coideal of
C
is
\bar{C}=\mathrm{coker}(\eta)
. The
reduced coproduct
\bar{\Delta}
of
C
is the composite
\bar{\Delta}=(q\otimes q)\circ\Delta\colon C\to\bar{C}\otimes\bar{C}
, where
\Delta
denotes the coproduct of
C
and
q\colon C\to\bar{C}
denotes the quotient map. The
cobar complex
of a coaugmented differential graded coalgebra
C
is the differential graded algebra
\Omega(C)=(T(s^{-1}C),d_{\Omega})
with differential
d_{\Omega}
defined by

d_{\Omega}(s^{-1}c)=-s^{-1}\partial c+\sum(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(c_{(1)})}s^{-1}c_{(1)}\otimes s^{-1}c_{(2)},

where
\bar{\Delta}(c)=\sum c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
. We denote by
\Omega^{n}(C)
, for some
n\geqslant 0
, the subspace of
\Omega(C)
generated by homogeneous elements of degree
n
. Notice that the differential
d_{\Omega}
lowers the degree by 1.

An element
x\in\Omega(C)
is
homogeneous of length
k
, for some integer
k\geqslant 1
, if there exist elements
c_{1},\ldots c_{k}\in C
such that

x=s^{-1}c_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes s^{-1}c_{k}.

In that case, we write

x=[c_{1}|\cdots|c_{k}].

By convention, an element
x\in\Omega(C)
is
homogenenous of length
0
if
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any
k\geqslant 0
, we denote by
F_{k}(\Omega(C))
the subspace of
\Omega(C)
generated by homogeneous elements of length at least
k
. Notice that
d\left(F_{k}(\Omega(C))\right)\subset F_{k}(\Omega(C))
. There is a descending filtration of complexes:

\Omega(C)=F_{0}(\Omega(C))\supset F_{1}(\Omega(C))\supset F_{2}(\Omega(C))\supset\cdots

Denote by
\widehat{\Omega(C)}
the completion of the cobar complex
\Omega(C)
with respect to this filtration,
i.e.

\widehat{\Omega(C)}=\Bigl\{(c_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}\in\prod\limits_{k\geqslant 0}\left(\Omega(C)/F_{k}\left(\Omega(C)\right)\right)\Bigm|c_{i}=c_{j}\left(\mathrm{mod}\ F_{k}\left(\Omega(C)\right)\right)\text{ for all }i\leqslant j\Bigr\}.

For any
n\geqslant 0
, we denote by
\widehat{\Omega^{n}(C)}
the subspace of
\widehat{\Omega(C)}
generated by homogeneous elements of degree
n
.
\widehat{\Omega(C)}
is a differential graded algebra. In particular, for any nonnegative integers
i
and
j
, the product induces an inclusion of subspaces:

\widehat{\Omega^{i}(C)}\otimes\widehat{\Omega^{j}(C)}\subset\widehat{\Omega^{i+j}(C)}.

Notice that
\widehat{\Omega^{0}(C)}
is a subalgebra of
\widehat{\Omega(C)}
.

3.2.
Formal power series connections

Let
(C_{dR}^{*}(M),d)
denote the differential graded algebra of differential forms on a smooth manifold
M
. The
completed tensor product of
C_{dR}^{*}(M)
and
\Omega(C)
, where
\Omega(C)
is the cobar complex of
C
, is the algebra
C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
whose elements are formal sums
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}
with homogeneous
\omega_{i}\in C_{dR}^{*}(M)
and
c_{i}\in\Omega(C)
, such that for each
n\geqslant 0
, there is only a finite number of terms
\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}
verifying
\mathrm{deg}(\omega_{i})+\mathrm{deg}(c_{i})=n
. The product of two elements
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}
and
\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\psi_{j}\otimes d_{j}
of
C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
is defined by:

(\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i})\wedge(\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\psi_{j}\otimes d_{j})=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\wedge\psi_{j}\otimes c_{i}d_{j},

which gives
C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
the structure of an algebra with unit
1=1\otimes 1
.
We extend
d
and
d_{\Omega}
to
C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
via:

\displaystyle d\left(\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}d\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i},

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes d_{\Omega}c_{i}.

Let
\varepsilon:C_{dR}^{*}(M)\to C_{dR}^{*}(M)
denote the automorphism given by
\varepsilon(\omega)=(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(\omega)}\omega
for any homogeneous element
\omega\in C_{dR}^{*}(M)
. We extend
\varepsilon
to
C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
via:

\varepsilon\left(\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}\right)=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\varepsilon(\omega_{i})\otimes c_{i}.

A
formal power series connection
on
M
is an element
\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}\in C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
such that:

•

for all
i\geqslant 0
,
\mathrm{deg}(\omega_{i})=\mathrm{deg}(c_{i}),

•

the
twisted cochain condition
is verified:
d_{\Omega}\omega+d\omega=\varepsilon(\omega)\wedge\omega
.

3.3.
The transport of a formal power series connection

Let
\Omega(C)
denote the cobar complex of
C
, and let
B^{*}(M)
denote the bar complex of iterated integrals on a smooth manifold
M
. A nonzero element
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}b_{i}\otimes c_{i}\in B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
is
homogeneous of length
k
, for some
k\geqslant 1
, if for all
i\geqslant 0
the element
b_{i}
is homogeneous of length
k
(see Section
2.5
). For all
k\geqslant 1
, let
F_{k}
denote the vector subspace of
B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
generated by homogeneous elements of length at least
k
. This defines a descending filtration of vector subspaces:

B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)=F_{1}\supset F_{2}\supset F_{3}\supset\cdots

Denote by
\widehat{B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)}
the completion of
B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
with respect to this filtration.

The
transport

T_{\omega}
of a formal power series connection
\omega=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}\in C^{*}_{dR}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
is the element:

T_{\omega}=1+\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots i_{k}\geqslant 0}\int\omega_{i_{1}}\ldots\omega_{i_{k}}\otimes c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{k}}\in\widehat{B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)}.

Notice that if
\mathrm{deg}(\omega_{i})\geqslant 2
for all
i\geqslant 0
, then
T_{\omega}\in B^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
. For any bounded plot
\phi
of the pointed path space of
M
, the integration of iterated integrals (see Section
2.6
) extends to define an element

\langle T_{\omega},\phi\rangle=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots i_{k}\geqslant 0}\langle\int\omega_{i_{1}}\ldots\omega_{i_{k}},\phi\rangle\,c_{i_{1}}\cdots c_{i_{k}}\in\widehat{\Omega(C)}.

3.4.
The homology of loop spaces

In
[
Che77
]
, Chen uses the transport of a formal power series connection to study the homology of the loop space of a simply connected manifold
M
. He sets

\omega=\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 0}\omega_{i}\otimes c_{i}\in C^{*}_{dR}(M)\otimes\Omega(H_{\geqslant 1}(M;\mathbb{R})),

where
H_{\geqslant 1}(M;\mathbb{R})=\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 1}H_{k}(M;\mathbb{R})
is the graded vector space defined by the positive degree part of the singular homology of
M
with real coefficients,
(c_{i})_{i}
is a basis of
H_{\geqslant 1}(M;\mathbb{R})
, and
(\omega_{i})_{i}
are closed forms representing cohomology classes dual to
(c_{i})_{i}
. He shows the existence of a derivation
\partial
on
\Omega(H_{*}(M;\mathbb{R}))
such that:

Theorem 1

(
[
Che77
]
)
.

There is an isomorphism
H_{*}(\Omega M;\mathbb{R})\cong H_{*}(\Omega(H_{\geqslant 1}(M;\mathbb{R})),\partial)
induced by the map

\begin{array}[]{ccl}C_{*}(\Omega M;\mathbb{R})&\to&\Omega(H_{\geqslant 1}(M;\mathbb{R}))\\
c&\mapsto&\langle T_{\omega},c\rangle,\end{array}

where
\langle T_{\omega},c\rangle
denotes the integration of the transport
T_{\omega}
over
c
.

4.
Higher holonomy functors

In this section, we show how the transport of a formal power series connection induces functors from the path groupoid, the path 2-groupoid, and the path 3-groupoid of a manifold.

Throughout this section, we fix
\omega\in C_{dR}^{*}(M)\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Omega(C)
a formal power series connection on a manifold
M
, with
\Omega(C)
the cobar complex of a coaugmented differential graded coalgebra
C
(see Section
3
). We denote by
T_{\omega}
the transport of
\omega
.

4.1.
Holonomy functors

Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
be differential forms on
M
. Kohno shows
[
Koh16
]
that if
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are rank-1 homotopic 1-paths, then:

\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},\gamma\rangle=\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the integration of iterated integrals over bounded plots (see Section
2
).
Moreover, the composition of 1-tracks is compatible with integration: if
\gamma
and
\delta
are 1-tracks such that
\gamma(1)=\delta(0)
, then

\langle T_{\omega},\gamma\circ\delta\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},\gamma\rangle\langle T_{\omega},\delta\rangle.

Recall the algebra
\widehat{\Omega^{0}(C)}
generated by the homogeneous elements of degree 0 in the completion of the cobar complex of
C
(see Section
3
). We define the category
\mathcal{C}_{1}(C)
by the following data:

•

the category
\mathcal{C}_{1}(C)
has a single object
*
,

•

an endomorphism of
*
in
\mathcal{C}_{1}(C)
is an element of
\widehat{\Omega^{0}(C)}
with composition given by the product in
\widehat{\Omega^{0}(C)}
.

The
holonomy functor of
\omega
is the functor

\mathrm{Hol}^{1}_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)\to\mathcal{C}_{1}(C)

defined by

\mathrm{Hol}^{1}_{\omega}([\gamma])=\langle T_{\omega},\gamma\rangle,

for any 1-track
[\gamma]
.

4.2.
Integration over 2-tracks

In this section, we extend the notion of integration over 2-paths to that of integration over 2-tracks. Moreover, we show that the integration of the transport
T_{\omega}
of a formal power series connection
\omega
over a 2-track is compatible with the vertical and the horizontal composition.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
g
and
h
are rank-2 homotopic 2-paths in
M
, then

\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle-\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle\in d_{\Omega}\left(\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}\otimes\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}\right).

Proof.

Let
g-h=\partial H
with
H
a rank-2 homotopy.

\displaystyle\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle-\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},\partial H\rangle=\langle dT_{\omega},H\rangle=d_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega},H\rangle,

where the second equality is a consequence of Stokes’ theorem, and the third equality is a consequence of the twisted cochain condition. Since
H
is rank-2,
\langle T_{\omega},H\rangle\in\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}\otimes\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}
.
∎

Remark
.

Contrary to
[
Koh22
, Proposition 3.4]
, we find that if
g
and
h
are rank-2 homotopic 2-paths and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
are differential forms of degree
\deg(\omega_{1}),\ldots,\deg(\omega_{k})
, respectively, then we don’t necessarily have

\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},g\rangle=\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},h\rangle.

Indeed, we need a stronger condition: that
g
and
h
are laminated rank-2 homotopic (see Lemma
4.4
).

The vertical composition of 2-paths is compatible with integration:

Lemma 4.2
.

If
g
and
h
are 2-paths such that
g(1,-)=h(0,-)
, then

\langle T_{\omega},g\cdot h\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle+\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle.

Proof.

For any differential forms
\omega_{1},\ldots\omega_{k}
of positive degree such that
\deg(\omega_{1})+\cdots+\deg(\omega_{k})-k=1
, we have:

\displaystyle\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},g\cdot h\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\frac{1}{2}]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{g\cdot h}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k})+\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{g\cdot h}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k}),

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{g}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k})+\int_{[0,1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{h}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k}),

\displaystyle=\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},g\rangle+\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},g\rangle.\qed

We have the following formulas for the horizontal composition of 2-paths.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
g
and
h
are 2-paths such that
g(-,1)=h(-,0)
, then the following equations hold in the quotient space
\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}/d_{\Omega}\left(\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}\otimes\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}\right)
:

\displaystyle\langle T_{\omega},g\circ h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h(0,-)\rangle+\langle T_{\omega},g(1,-)\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle,

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},g(0,-)\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle+\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h(1,-)\rangle.

Proof.

Note that
g\circ h
is rank-2 homotopic to

and to
(c_{g(0,-)}\circ h)\cdot(g\circ c_{h(1,-)})
. Therefore, the equations result from Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
and
[
Che73
, Proposition 2.1.1]
.
∎

4.5.
Integration over laminated 2-tracks

In this section, we extend the notion of integration over 2-paths to that of integration over laminated 2-tracks. Moreover, we show that the integration of the transport
T_{\omega}
of a formal power series connection
\omega
over laminated 2-tracks is compatible with the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of laminated 2-tracks.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
g
and
h
are laminated rank-2 homotopic 2-paths, then

\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle.

Proof.

Let
H
denote a laminated rank-2 homotopy from
g
to
h
. We have

\displaystyle\langle T_{\omega},h\rangle-\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},\partial H\rangle,

\displaystyle=\langle dT_{\omega},H\rangle,

where the second equality is consequence of Stokes’ theorem. Therefore, it suffices to show that for any positive integer
k
and for any differential forms
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
of positive degree on
M
such that

\deg(\omega_{1})+\cdots+\deg(\omega_{k})=k+2,

we have
\displaystyle\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},H\rangle=0
. Recall that

\displaystyle\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},H\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{H}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k}),

where
\widetilde{H}(r,s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})=(H(r,s,t_{1}),\ldots,H(r,s,t_{k}))
for all
(r,s)\in[0,1]^{2}
and
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in\Delta^{k}
. The condition that
H
is laminated ensures that the rank of
d\widetilde{H}
is at most
k+1
, and therefore

\widetilde{H}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k})=0.\qed

The left and right whiskering of 2-paths with 1-paths is compatible with integration:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and let
\omega_{1},\ldots\omega_{k}
be differential forms on
M
. If
\gamma
is a 1-path and
g
is a 2-path such that
\gamma(1)=g([0,1]\times\{0\})
, then

\langle T_{\omega},\gamma\circ g\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},\gamma\rangle\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle.

If
\gamma
is a 1-path and
g
is a 2-path such that
g([0,1]\times\{1\})=\gamma(0)
, then

\langle T_{\omega},g\circ\gamma\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},g\rangle\langle T_{\omega},\gamma\rangle.

Proof.

This is a direction consequence of
[
Che73
, Proposition 2.1.2]
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemmas
4.2
and
4.5
, we get:

Lemma 4.6
.

If
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are 2-paths such that
h_{1}([0,1]\times\{1\})=h_{2}([0,1]\times\{0\})
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\omega},\begin{pmatrix}&h_{2}\\
h_{1}&\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},h_{1}\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h_{2}(0,-)\rangle+\langle T_{\omega}h_{1}(1,-)\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\omega},\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}&\\
&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},h_{1}(0,-)\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h_{2}\rangle+\langle T_{\omega},h_{1}\rangle\langle T_{\omega},h_{2}(1,-)\rangle.

4.6.
Integration over 3-tracks

In this section, we extend the notion of integration over good 3-paths to that of integration over 3-tracks. Moreover, we show that the integration of the transport
T_{\omega}
of a formal power series connection
\omega
is compatible with the upward composition, the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of 3-tracks.

We denote by
\widehat{\Omega^{1,2}(C)}
the subspace of
\widehat{\Omega^{3}(C)}
defined by

\widehat{\Omega^{1,2}(C)}=\left(\widehat{\Omega^{1}(A^{*})}\otimes\widehat{\Omega^{2}(C)}\right)+\left(\widehat{\Omega^{2}(C)}\otimes\widehat{\Omega^{1}(C)}\right)

Lemma 4.7
.

If
J
and
J^{\prime}
are rank-3 homotopic good 3-paths, then

\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}\rangle-\langle T_{\omega},J\rangle\in d_{\Omega}\widehat{\Omega^{1,2}(C)}.

Proof.

Let
W
be a rank-3 homotopy from
J
to
J^{\prime}
. We have

\displaystyle\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}\rangle-\langle T_{\omega},J\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},\partial W\rangle=\langle dT_{\omega},W\rangle=d_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega},W\rangle,

where the second equality is a consequence of Stokes’ theorem, and the third equality is a consequence of the twisted cochain condition. Since
W
is rank-3,
\langle T_{\omega},W\rangle\in\widehat{\Omega^{1,2}(C)}
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

If
J
and
J^{\prime}
are 3-paths such that
J(1,-,-)=J(0,-,-)
, then

\langle T_{\omega},J*J^{\prime}\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},J\rangle+\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}\rangle.

Proof.

For any differential forms
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
of positive degree such that
\deg(\omega_{1})+\cdots+\deg(\omega_{k})-k=1
, we have:

\displaystyle\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},J*J^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\frac{1}{2}]\times[0,1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{J*J^{\prime}}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k})+\int_{[\frac{1}{2},1]\times[0,1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{J*J^{\prime}}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k}),

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{J}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k})+\int_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\int_{\Delta^{k}}\widetilde{J^{\prime}}^{*}(\omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\omega_{k}),

\displaystyle=\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},J\rangle+\langle\int\omega_{1}\ldots\omega_{k},J^{\prime}\rangle.\qed

By exchanging the first two coordinates in the previous proof, we get:

Lemma 4.9
.

If
J
and
J^{\prime}
are 3-paths such that
J(-,1,-)=J^{\prime}(-,0,-)
, then

\langle T_{\omega},J\cdot J^{\prime}\rangle=\langle T_{\omega},J\rangle+\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}\rangle.

As a consequence of Lemma
4.9
and
[
Che73
, Proposition 2.1.2]
, we get:

Lemma 4.10
.

If
J
and
J^{\prime}
are good 3-paths such that
J([0,1]^{2}\times\{1\})=J^{\prime}([0,1]^{2}\times\{0\})
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\omega},\begin{pmatrix}&J^{\prime}\\
J&\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},J\rangle\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}(0,0,-)\rangle+\langle T_{\omega}J(0,1,-)\rangle\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{\omega},\begin{pmatrix}J&\\
&J^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\omega},J(0,1,-)\rangle\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}\rangle+\langle T_{\omega}J\rangle\langle T_{\omega},J^{\prime}(0,0,-)\rangle.

5.
Application: a higher holonomy functor for configuration spaces

In this section, we apply the construction of Section
4
to a formal power series connection defined by Komendarczyk, Koytcheff and Volić
[
KKV24
]
on the configuration space of
m
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geqslant 4
. We describe the resulting holonomy 3-functor.

5.1.
The formal power series connection

Let
\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)
denote Kontsevich’s differential graded algebra of oriented diagrams for some integers
n\geqslant 4
and
m\geqslant 1
as defined in
[
LV14
,
KKV24
]
. Let
I
denote the formality integration map, and let
\mathcal{B}(m)
denote a basis of
\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)
(as a vector space) given by choosing a diagram in each orientation class. Since
n\geqslant 4
, each grading vector space of
\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)
is finite dimensional, therefore the graded dual
\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*}
is a differential graded coalgebra. We recall the formal power series connection introduced in
[
KKV24
]
:

\omega=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}(m)}I(\Gamma)\otimes\frac{[\Gamma^{*}]}{\lvert\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)\rvert}\in C_{dR}^{*}(\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{R}^{n}))\otimes\Omega(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*}).

Remark
.

Notice that if we allowed
n=3
, then the above sum would contain infinitely many differential 1-forms and therefore would not define a formal power series connection. Requiring that
n\geqslant 4
ensures that
\omega
only contains finitely many terms in each degree and no terms of degree 1. Moreover, since
\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is finite dimensional, the above sum is finite.

The transport of
\omega
is given by:

T_{\omega}=\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 0}\sum\limits_{\Gamma_{1},\,\ldots\Gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(m)}\int I(\Gamma_{1})\ldots I(\Gamma_{k})\otimes\frac{[\Gamma_{1}^{*}|\cdots|\Gamma_{k}^{*}]}{\lvert\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_{1})\rvert\cdots\lvert\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_{k})\rvert}\in B^{*}(\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{R}^{n}))\otimes\Omega(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*}).

5.2.
The holonomy 3-functor

Since
\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*}
is 1-connected, the Gray 3-category
\mathcal{C}_{3}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*})
admits a simpler description:

•

the 1-morphisms of
\mathcal{C}_{3}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*})
are given by the set
\mathbb{R}
, with composition given by multiplication,

•

the 2-morphisms of
\mathcal{C}_{3}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*})
are given by linear combinations of diagrams of degree 2, with the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions all given by addition,

•

for any 2-morphisms
X
and
Y
from
x
to
y
, the set of 3-morphisms from
X
to
Y
is given by

\mathcal{C}_{3}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*})(X,Y)=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D}(m)^{*})/d_{\Omega}\Omega^{1,2}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*})\mid d_{\Omega}(\alpha)=Y-X\},

with the upward composition, the vertical composition and the horizontal compositions of 3-morphisms all given by addition.

Theorem
3
induces:

Corollary 4
.

Let
n\geqslant 4
and
m\geqslant 1
. The transport of
\omega
induces a holonomy 3-functor:

\mathrm{Hol}^{3}_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{P}_{3}(\mathrm{Conf}(m,\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{C}_{3}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}(m)^{*}).

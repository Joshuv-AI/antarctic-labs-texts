Extension of k k -regulous functions from varieties of arbitrary dimension
Juliusz Banecki

Abstract
We prove that a k k -regulous function defined on a non-singular affine variety can always be extended to the entire affine space.



1
Introduction

In the paper a real affine variety is a Zariski closed subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
. By
\mathcal{P}(X)
we denote the ring of polynomials on
X
. By
\mathcal{P}(X)_{x_{0}}
we denote the localisation of
\mathcal{P}(X)
at the maximal ideal of functions vanishing at
x_{0}
, which can be interpreted as the ring of germs of regular functions at
x_{0}
. The variety is said to be non-singular at
x_{0}
if the corresponding local ring is regular. The definitions given here are compatible with the ones given in
[
3
,
6
]
.

Given a non-singular affine variety
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a non-empty Euclidean open set
U\subset X
, we say that a function
f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
k
-regulous if it is of class
\mathcal{C}^{k}
and there exist two polynomials
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, such that
Q
does not vanish identically on any irreducible component of
X
and
P=fQ
on
U
. Clearly, the polynomials uniquely determine
f
, so in particular if
X
is irreducible then
\mathcal{R}^{k}(U)
naturally embeds in the field of rational functions on
X
.

This definition is not completely standard, as for
X\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
some authors consider only these functions which admit
k
-regulous extensions to the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From now on however, this distinction is no longer important, as the goal of the paper is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a non-singular affine variety and let
f\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
. Then, there exists
F\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F|_{X}=f
.

Earlier, the result was known to hold in two particular cases:

1.

when
k=0
; due to
[
5
]
. In the paper Kollár and Nowak have also proven the converse, saying that for every
k
, the restriction of a
k
-regulous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
X
is
k
-regulous.

2.

when
\dim(X)=2
; due to
[
1
]
. The approach presented by the author there is rather technical and makes significant use of the resolution of singularities.

The two above papers rely on vaguely related ideas, which do not seem sufficient to tackle the general case. The problem has remained open from the introduction of
k
-regulous functions in
[
4
]
up until now. The approach given in the current paper is quite short as compared to
[
1
]
, and it is based on some innovative ideas from the recent paper
[
2
]
regarding retract rational varieties. Even though the results presented there seem to have little to do with
k
-regulous functions, it turns out that the approach given there is just what we need in the current problem.

2
Proofs

We need the following proposition playing an important role in
[
2
]
:

Proposition 2.1

(
[
2
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a non-singular irreducible affine variety of dimension
m
, and let
x_{0}\in X
. Let
I\subset\mathcal{P}(X)
be a non-zero ideal such that
I\subset\mathfrak{m}_{x_{0}}
. Then, there exists a polynomial mapping
\sigma:X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

1.

\sigma(x,0)=x
for
x\in X
,

2.

the derivative of
\sigma
at
(x_{0},0)
is a isomorphism,

3.

the induced homomorphism

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{x_{0}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{P}(X)[t_{1},\dots,t_{n-m}]/I(t_{1},\dots,t_{n-m}))_{\mathfrak{m}_{(x_{0},0)}}

is surjective.

Furthermore, we need an analogue of
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
two polynomials. Let
X
be an irreducible non-singular variety, and let
\pi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a regular mapping, such that
Q\circ\pi
is not identically equal to zero and
\frac{P}{Q}\circ\pi\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
. Let
x_{0}
be a point of
X
and let
\varphi:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a germ of a regular mapping, such that for
1\leq i\leq n
its
i
-th coordinated
\varphi_{i}
satisfies

\varphi_{i}-\pi_{i}\in(Q\circ\pi)\mathfrak{m}_{x_{0}}\text{ in the ring }\mathcal{P}(X)_{x_{0}}.

Then, there exists a Zariski neighbourhood
V
of
x_{0}
such that

1.

V\subset\textnormal{dom}(\varphi)
,

2.

for
x\in V
we have
Q\circ\pi(x)=0\iff Q\circ\varphi(x)=0
,

3.

\frac{P}{Q}\circ\varphi\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(V)
.

Proof.

Note that for
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the polynomials
P
and
Q
can be written as

\displaystyle P(v+w)=P(v)+\sum_{i}w_{i}P_{i}(v,w),

\displaystyle Q(v+w)=Q(v)+\sum_{i}w_{i}Q_{i}(v,w),

for some
P_{i},Q_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Take
V
as the locus of points
x\in X
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\varphi
is defined at
x
,

2.

\frac{\varphi_{i}-\pi_{i}}{Q\circ\pi}
is regular at
x
for all
i
,

3.

1+\sum_{i}\frac{\varphi_{i}(x)-\pi_{i}(x)}{Q\circ\pi(x)}Q_{i}(\pi(x),\varphi(x)-\pi(x))\neq 0
.

Then

Q\circ\varphi(x)=Q\circ\pi(x)\left(1+\sum_{i}\frac{\varphi_{i}(x)-\pi_{i}(x)}{Q\circ\pi(x)}Q_{i}\big(\pi(x),\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\big)\right)

and so the second point follows. Furthermore

\frac{P}{Q}\circ\varphi(x)=\frac{\frac{P}{Q}\circ\pi(x)+\sum_{i}\frac{\varphi_{i}(x)-\pi_{i}(x)}{Q\circ\pi(x)}P_{i}\big(\pi(x),\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\big)}{1+\sum_{i}\frac{\varphi_{i}(x)-\pi_{i}(x)}{Q\circ\pi(x)}Q_{i}\big(\pi(x),\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\big)}\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(V).

∎

Recall that a set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
constructibly open
, if it is open in the Euclidean topology, and it can be written as a finite union of Zariski locally closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. With the machinery at hand, we can prove the following proposition, from which Theorem
1.1
follows easily:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a non-singular irreducible variety and let
f\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
. Fix a point
x_{0}\in X
. Then, there exists a constructibly open neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a regulous function
F\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(U)
such that
F|_{X\cap U}=f|_{X\cap U}
.

Proof.

Set
m:=\dim(X)
. Write
f
as
f=\frac{p}{q}
with
p,q\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and
q\neq 0
, and choose any
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
p=P+\mathcal{I}(X),q=Q+\mathcal{I}(X)
. Without loss of generality
q(x_{0})=0
, for otherwise we can take
F:=\frac{P}{Q}
. Applying Proposition
2.1
to the ideal
I=(q)
, we find a polynomial mapping
\sigma:X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

1.

\sigma(x,0)=x
for
x\in X
,

2.

the derivative of
\sigma
at
(x_{0},0)
is a isomorphism,

3.

the induced homomorphism

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{x_{0}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{P}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m})/(q)(t_{1},\dots,t_{n-m}))_{\mathfrak{m}_{(x_{0},0)}}

is surjective.

Set
Y:=\mathcal{Z}(q)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m}\subset X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
. The composed homomorphism

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{x_{0}}\rightarrow\left(\mathcal{P}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m})/(q)(t_{1},\dots,t_{n-m})\right)_{\mathfrak{m}_{(x_{0},0)}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(Y)_{(x_{0},0)}

is then surjective as well. It follows that, if we define
Z:=\overline{\sigma(Y)}^{Zar}
, then
\sigma
induces an isomorphism of germs of affine varieties

(Y,(x_{0},0))\xlongrightarrow{\cong}(Z,x_{0}).

(1)

For
1\leq i\leq n
let
\psi_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{x_{0}}
be a regular germ such that

\psi_{i}\circ\sigma-x_{i}\in(q)(t_{1},\dots,t_{n-m}).

These together give a regular germ
\psi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},x_{0})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Set
F:=\frac{P}{Q}\circ\psi
.

By Lemma
2.2
applied to the mappings

\displaystyle\pi:X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\pi(x,t):=x\in X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\varphi:(X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m},(x_{0},0))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t):=\psi\circ\sigma(x,t)

there exists a Zariski neighbourhood
V
of
(x_{0},0)
in
X\times\mathbb{R}^{n-m}
such that
F\circ\sigma\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(V)
and for
(x,t)\in V
we have

Q\circ\psi\circ\sigma(x,t)=0\iff(x,t)\in Y.

(2)

Decreasing
V
if necessary we can assume that the derivative of
\sigma
is non-singular on
V
. Furthermore, we can assume that
\sigma(V)\subset\textnormal{dom}(\psi)
. Lastly, thanks to (
1
), we can assume that
\sigma(V\cap Y)
is Zariski open in
Z
, so in particular it is constructible in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Define

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle:=\textnormal{indet}(\psi)\cup\{x\in\textnormal{dom}(\psi):Q\circ\psi(x)=0\},

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash W\cup\sigma(V).

As
\sigma
is a local diffeomorphism between
V
and
\sigma(V)
, it follows that
F\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(\sigma(V))
. Also
F\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash W)
, so
F\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(U)
. Furthermore, for
x\in X
sufficiently close to
x_{0}
by assumption
\psi(x)=\psi\circ\sigma(x,0)=\pi(x,0)=x
, so
F|_{X}=f
as a rational function. It hence suffices to check that
U
is constructible in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We first claim that

\sigma(V)\cap W=\sigma(V\cap Y).

Indeed, let
(x,t)\in V
. Then,
\psi
by assumption is regular at
\sigma(x,t)
, so thanks to (
2
):

\sigma(x,t)\in W\iff Q\circ\psi\circ\sigma(x,t)=0\iff(x,t)\in Y.

Now we have that

U=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash W\cup\sigma(V)=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash W\cup(\sigma(V)\cap W)=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash W\cup\sigma(V\cap Y)

is a constructible set, and the conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
X
and
f\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
be as in the theorem. Consider the ideal

I:=\{Q\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\exists_{P\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}P|_{X}=fQ|_{X}\}.

It suffices to prove that
1\in I
.

First of all, clearly
I
contains the ideal of functions vanishing on
X
, so
\mathcal{Z}(I)\subset X
. Fix a point
x_{0}\in X
and let
X_{0}
be the irreducible component of
X
containing
x_{0}
. Applying Proposition
2.3
, we get that there exists a constructibly open neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a
k
-regulous function
F\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(U)
such that

F|_{U\cap X_{0}}=f|_{U\cap X_{0}}.

According to
[
4
, Théorème 6.4 and Corollaire 4.4]
, since
U
is constructibly open, there exists
Q\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathcal{Z}(Q)=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash U
. Then, thanks to
[
4
, Lemme 5.1]
,
F
can be written as
F=\frac{P}{Q^{N}}
, where
P\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
N
is a sufficiently large integer. Take
Q_{1}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be any regular function vanishing precisely on
X\backslash X_{0}
. Then, as
U\cap X_{0}
is Euclidean dense in
X_{0}
, we have

PQ_{1}|_{X}=fQ^{N}Q_{1}|_{X},

so
Q^{N}Q_{1}\in I
. Hence
x_{0}\not\in\mathcal{Z}(I)
. As
x_{0}
was an arbitrary point of
X
, we get
\mathcal{Z}(I)=\emptyset
. Thanks to the weak regulous Nullstellensatz (
[
4
, Proposition 5.23]
), we get
1\in I
. The conclusion follows.
∎

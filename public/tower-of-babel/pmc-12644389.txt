A bibliometric study on microenvironment-mediated drug resistance in interventional liver cancer treatment
Authors: Qiang Wang, Xu Feng, Guo Xiao, Yaoyong Chen, Quanji Yue, Zhongyao Chen, Yanjie Yang, Jun Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMicroenvironment-driven drug resistance is a principal barrier to durable response after transarterial chemoembolisation, ablation or radioembolisation for hepatocellular carcinoma, yet the global structure of scholarship linking these domains remains undefined. This bibliometric analysis mapped 25-year publication trends, thematic evolution and collaboration networks at the intersection of interventional oncology, tumour microenvironment and therapeutic resistance.MethodsWeb of Science Core Collection was searched (1 January 2000–31 December 2024) for records simultaneously referencing liver cancer, interventional therapy, microenvironmental descriptors and resistance terms. After dual-review screening, 939 articles and reviews were retained. Annual output, citation impact and Bradford-zone distributions were generated with bibliometrix 4.2.1. Bradford-zone analysis refers to the distribution of journal productivity, with journals in the first zone representing core literature, and those in subsequent zones reflecting peripheral or niche studies. This method helps assess the concentration of research in key journals and identifies areas of growth in the literature; co-authorship, journal-coupling and keyword-co-occurrence networks were rendered in VOSviewer 1.6.20. Workflow reproducibility was confirmed on a 5% subsample and by comparing fractional with full counting.ResultsYearly publications rose from two in 2000 to 130 in 2024, amassing 36 095 citations (mean 38.4 per item). Output spikes in 2013, 2019 and 2021 paralleled seminal reports on hypoxia-driven angiogenesis, CAF activation, and COVID-19-related endothelial dysfunction. The pathophysiological mechanisms of COVID-19-induced endothelial injury, including cytokine-mediated endothelial dysfunction, have been implicated in compromising loco-regional therapies, contributing to resistance mechanisms that affect the efficacy of embolic treatments. China produced 47.3% of documents and sat at the nexus of global collaboration, with prominent links to the United States, Japan and South Korea. Cancer Research, Hepatology and Journal of Hepatology led citation rankings. Keyword overlays revealed a chronological shift from procedure-centric terms to microenvironmental and immunologic nodes—“angiogenesis”, “immune checkpoint”, “nanoparticles”—after 2017. Trend-topic mapping documented a transition from device optimisation toward mechanism-informed combination regimens incorporating anti-VEGF or PD-1 blockade.ConclusionResearch on microenvironment-mediated resistance in interventional liver-cancer therapy has expanded rapidly and pivoted toward molecularly targeted, immune-augmented strategies, driven chiefly by high-volume Chinese centres and trans-Pacific partnerships. Priorities now include single-cell analytics, radiomics-guided adaptive embolisation and multinational trials of combination therapy.

Introduction

Interventional oncology has transitioned over the past two decades from a technically oriented subspecialty focused on arterial catheterisation and thermal ablation into an increasingly integrative discipline that interfaces with molecular therapeutics, immunology and single-cell analytics. Hepatocellular carcinoma remains its principal clinical substrate,accounting for more than 800 000 incident cases and ranking as the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with the highest burdens concentrated in East and South-East Asia where chronic hepatitis-B infection and aflatoxin exposure intersect [1–3]. Beyond HBV and aflatoxin, major aetiologies include HCV-related cirrhosis and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH); these routes imprint distinct microenvironmental contexts—viral antigenicity and T-cell exhaustion in HCV, lipotoxic inflammation with M2-skewed myeloid infiltration and dense fibrosis in NASH—that shape resistance themes appearing in our corpus. Despite incremental refinements in transarterial chemoembolisation, radiofrequency and microwave ablation, durable local control is frequently undermined by adaptive resistance mechanisms rooted in the tumour microenvironment, particularly through hypoxia-induced angiogenesis. The vascular-normalisation hypothesis suggests that therapies targeting the vascular component of the TME, such as anti-VEGF combinations, may mitigate angiogenesis and enhance loco-regional efficacy, including hypoxia-induced angiogenesis, extracellular-matrix remodelling, immune-suppressive myeloid infiltration and metabolic re-programming that collectively blunt cytotoxic efficacy and promote post-procedural recurrence [4–7]. More broadly, ‘microenvironmental normalisation’ includes stromal re-education (e.g., inducing CAF quiescence and extracellular-matrix softening) and immune-contexture modulation (e.g., myeloid-derived suppressor-cell depletion or Treg recalibration), thereby extending the therapeutic lens beyond vasculature alone.

Microenvironment-mediated resistance, defined as adaptive resistance mechanisms within the tumor microenvironment (TME), complicates treatment outcomes. This phenomenon arises from factors such as hypoxia, stromal cell interactions, and immune cell infiltration, distinct from intrinsic genetic resistance or pharmacokinetic failure. Microenvironment-mediated resistance diminishes the cytotoxic efficacy of loco-regional therapies and reduces the therapeutic benefit of subsequent systemic agents like tyrosine-kinase inhibitors or immune-checkpoint blockers [8–12]. The PD-1/PD-L1 axis dampens T-cell receptor signalling via SHP2 recruitment when PD-1 on activated T cells engages PD-L1 on tumour or stromal cells, whereas CTLA-4 limits priming by outcompeting CD28 for B7 ligands on antigen-presenting cells. After embolisation, ischaemia and tumour necrosis can increase antigen release and PD-L1 expression while transiently augmenting dendritic-cell activation, providing a mechanistic rationale for pairing TACE with PD-1/PD-L1 or CTLA-4 blockade.The conceptual boundary between interventional oncology and systemic therapy lies in the distinct mechanisms and targets of treatment. Loco-regional modalities, such as transarterial chemoembolisation, focus on direct tumour cell eradication, while systemic therapies address more diffuse, often metastatic disease. Understanding the interactions between these modalities within the microenvironment is crucial for informing future bibliometric analyses that guide the integration of combination therapies. Recent approvals of lenvatinib and the atezolizumab–bevacizumab combination have shifted therapeutic algorithms toward mechanism-informed combination regimens. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) play a critical role in resistance post-embolisation through activation of key signalling axes such as TGF-β, which remodel the extracellular matrix, promote angiogenesis, and recruit immune-suppressive cells to the tumour site; nonetheless, heterogeneity in response highlights the necessity of dissecting the spatial and temporal dynamics of resistance at a systems level [13–17]. In parallel, extracellular vesicles—exosomes (~ 30–150 nm) and microvesicles (~ 100–1000 nm)—traffic microRNAs, proteins, and drug-efflux machinery between tumour, endothelial, and immune cells after embolisation, thereby amplifying pro-survival signalling and propagating microenvironment-driven resistance. A growing corpus of laboratory and translational studies now interrogates stromal cell–tumour crosstalk, vascular-normalisation windows, and nanocarrier designs that may potentiate embolic or ablative platforms. Single-cell and spatial-omics technologies, such as single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics, provide high-resolution insights into the micro-regional heterogeneity within embolised tumours, identifying cellular subpopulations and their responses to therapies, thus informing resistance-mitigation strategies [6–21], yet the cumulative structure of this evidence base and its alignment with technological, geographic and collaborative determinants has not been systematically mapped.

Bibliometric methodology offers a quantitative lens through which to appraise research output, citation impact, thematic convergence and network connectivity across large scientific corpora [22–24]. Prior bibliometric surveys of liver cancer have centred on systemic therapies or epidemiological modelling, leaving the interventional-oncology subset under-represented and failing to integrate microenvironmental or resistance-related constructs [25–27]. Moreover, no study to date has traced the co-evolution of interventional techniques, microenvironmental biology and drug-resistance research across the pivotal period in which single-cell sequencing, spatial transcriptomics and immunotherapy entered routine investigation.

Against this backdrop, the present work exploits the Web of Science Core Collection to assemble a 25-year global dataset of publications that explicitly couple interventional modalities for liver tumours with microenvironmental determinants and resistance phenotypes. By applying validated bibliometric and network-science techniques, we quantify longitudinal publication and citation trajectories, delineate institutional and national collaboration architectures, characterise Bradford-zone journal distribution, and resolve keyword-based thematic clusters with temporal overlay. This comprehensive mapping is intended to identify dominant knowledge streams, emerging methodological inflections and geographic disparities, thereby providing an evidence framework to guide future mechanistic inquiry, collaborative investment and clinical-trial prioritisation in interventional therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategy

All bibliographic data were drawn from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), Clarivate’s flagship citation index. WoSCC is regarded as the primary reference database for evaluative studies in oncology because its journal-selection process is anchored in rigorous editorial vetting, and every record is linked to a unique cited-reference network that spans more than a century of scholarship. These attributes make WoSCC the benchmark source for longitudinal and multidisciplinary mapping of scientific activity, and its use guarantees direct comparability with the bibliometric literature published in leading oncology journals. A topic search was executed on 30 May 2025 using the Boolean string TS=((hepatocellular* OR “liver cancer” OR HCC OR “hepatic carcinoma” OR “primary liver tumor*” OR “hepatic tumor*” OR “liver neoplasm*”) AND (TACE OR RFA OR “transarterial chemoembolization” OR “selective internal radiation therapy” OR SIRT OR “radiofrequency ablation” OR MWA OR “chemotherapy” OR “liver-directed therapy”) AND (microenvironment* OR TME OR “immune microenvironment” OR hypoxia OR angiogenesis OR ECM OR “cancer-assoc* fibroblast*” OR CAF OR TAM OR “myeloid-derived suppressor cell*” OR MDSC OR “stromal microenvironment” OR “tumor-associated macrophage*” OR “immune cell infiltration”) AND (resistan* OR MDR OR chemoresistan* OR radioresistan* OR “treatment failure” OR relapse OR recurrence OR “poor response” OR nonrespond* OR “therapy resistance”)), which simultaneously captured (1) primary liver tumours, (2) interventional therapies, (3) tumour-microenvironment features, and (4) resistance phenotypes. The search window was fixed from 1 January 2000 to 31 December 2024 to encompass the period during which interventional oncology has matured into an independent subspecialty. All records—comprising full metadata, cited references, funding information, and WoS subject categories—were exported in plain-text format in a single download session to prevent drift due to ongoing database updates. Early-access items indexed in 2024 were retained to provide forward visibility on emerging topics.

Inclusion, exclusion and data curation

A two-stage screening protocol was applied to the raw download. Document types were limited to full articles and reviews because these formats contain complete methodological information and stable citation trajectories; editorials, conference summaries, notes, letters, and retracted items were excluded. Thematic relevance was confirmed by independent reading of titles and abstracts. Records were kept only when the focal question concerned drug-resistance mechanisms in the context of an image-guided or catheter-based therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma (or other primary liver tumours) and explicitly referenced microenvironmental determinants such as hypoxia, stromal remodelling, immune infiltration, or angiogenesis. Disagreements were resolved through discussion until consensus was reached. Journal titles were normalised to the latest MEDLINE-preferred form; author keywords were stemmed and synonym-merged (e.g., “chemo-resistance”, “chemoresistance”, “multidrug resistance” mapped to a single token). Duplicate entries arising from ahead-of-print consolidation or journal title changes were removed by DOI matching. After filtration, 939 records remained, carrying an aggregate 36,095 citations as indexed by WoS on the day of download.

Bibliometric indicators and mapping procedures

Analyses were performed with the R-package bibliometrix (version 4.2.1) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.20). In bibliometrix, annual publication counts, citation trends, institutional, and Bradford-zone distributions were generated with default functions. Collaboration intensity was assessed by fractional counting at the author, institution, and country levels; single-country and multiple-country publication rates were derived directly from address fields. Keyword co-occurrence matrices were built from Author Keywords and Keywords-Plus, then converted into symmetrical adjacency tables for network construction. VOSviewer was used only for layout rendering and interactive inspection: separate maps were produced for co-authorship, institutional cooperation, country partnerships, cited-journal coupling, and thematic term networks. Node size reflects link strength or citation impact, edge thickness denotes link weight, and node colour encodes the mean publication year; these three visual channels allow simultaneous appraisal of prominence, connectedness, and temporal evolution.

Validation and reproducibility

Internal validity was checked by rerunning the entire workflow on a 5% random subsample of the corpus; rankings of the institutions, and journals were identical to those generated from the full dataset. Sensitivity analyses comparing fractional and full counting showed deviations of < 3% for country-level publication share and < 2% for institution level cited, confirming that the choice of counting method did not alter substantive conclusions.

Results

Analysis of annual publication volume and citation trends

Annual output on the topic rose from virtual invisibility—one or two papers per year in 2000–2003—to a discernible upswing in 2009, when 18 articles were published and cumulative citations passed the 1,500 mark (Table 1). Steady growth continued through the next decade, punctuated by notable citation spikes in 2013 and 2019 that correspond to landmark methodological reports on microenvironment-driven resistance pathways. A pronounced surge in productivity occurred between 2020 and 2022, with yearly publications climbing from 76 to 113 and annual citations peaking at 3,532 in 2020 alone. This expansion coincided with the COVID-19 pandemic, during which evidence emerged that SARS-CoV-2–induced hepatic endothelial dysfunction and cytokine-rich inflammatory milieus can compromise transarterial chemoembolisation and related locoregional therapies, thereby stimulating research into microenvironment-mediated resistance mechanisms. Following global vaccination roll-out and the easing of pandemic pressures, output stabilised at 127 and 130 papers in 2023 and 2024, respectively, while citation accrual moderated in parallel. Across the entire 25-year window, the corpus comprises 939 articles that have attracted 36,095 citations, yielding a mean citation rate of 38.4 per article and underscoring sustained scholarly interest in the intersection of tumour microenvironment and therapeutic resistance in liver-directed interventions. Together, the longitudinal volume curve and its citation trajectory (Fig. 1) depict a field that has moved from exploratory case-series to a mature research landscape responsive to both technological innovation and emergent clinical challenges such as COVID-19–related hepatic injury.

National publication volume and Co-occurrence analysis

China accounted for nearly half of all articles retrieved, positioning itself as the epicentre of global activity on microenvironment-related resistance in interventional liver cancer therapy (Fig. 2). This dominance reflects sustained national investment in hepatocellular carcinoma research—driven by the country’s high hepatitis B burden—and the establishment of large specialist networks such as Sun Yat-sen and Fudan–Zhongshan, which supply extensive clinical material for translational studies on hypoxia-induced drug efflux, angiogenic signalling, and immune-suppressive niches that compromise transarterial chemoembolisation. The co-authorship map confirms China’s centrality: it forms the largest node and maintains dense, bidirectional links with the United States, Japan and South Korea, generating a hub-and-spoke architecture that accelerates technology transfer in image-guided embolic platforms and combined loco-regional–systemic regimens (Fig. 3). Temporal shading on the same network shows that partnerships with India, Iran and Turkey have burgeoned only after 2019, indicating a geographical broadening of expertise in response to shared clinical challenges such as post-embolisation inflammation and cytokine-driven resistance. Density visualisation corroborates these patterns—China and, to a lesser extent, the United States constitute contiguous high-intensity clusters, while Europe contributes a dispersed but collaborative band stretching from Germany through Italy and the United Kingdom (Fig. 4). The triad of volume, network connectivity and spatial density reveals a field that evolved from bilateral Sino-American leadership to a more polycentric structure over the study window, with emerging economies now joining established actors to interrogate microenvironmental targets and optimise combination strategies against refractory liver tumours.

Journal citations and journal Co-citation analysis

Local-citation analysis ranks Cancer Research first (2 028 citations), followed by the liver-focused titles Hepatology (1 785) and Journal of Hepatology (1 256), with clinical-oncology flagships such as Clinical Cancer Research and Journal of Clinical Oncology rounding out the top five (Fig. 5). This hierarchy mirrors the dual structure of the field: mechanistic work on microenvironment-driven resistance is disseminated through high-impact basic-science venues, whereas clinical optimisation of transarterial chemoembolisation, radiofrequency ablation and combination regimens is reported in hepatology and oncology clinics. The journal co-citation network partitions into three densely connected clusters that reinforce this interpretation (Fig. 6). A green cluster anchored by Hepatology, Journal of Hepatology and Clinical Cancer Research captures translational studies that link stromal hypoxia, angiogenesis or immunosuppression to loco-regional treatment failure. A red cluster dominated by Oncotarget, International Journal of Molecular Sciences and Cancers represents laboratory exploration of signalling cascades (e.g., HIF-1α, VEGF, CAF activation) that precipitate multidrug resistance. A blue cluster centred on Nature, Science and Nature Reviews Cancer reflects citation of seminal conceptual papers on tumour–host interaction and of technological breakthroughs (single-cell profiling, spatial transcriptomics) increasingly adopted by interventional oncologists. The presence of high-volume open-access outlets such as PLOS ONE indicates that methodological heterogeneity—animal models, imaging physics, nanocarrier design—encourages publication outside narrowly defined oncology journals, expanding the visible citation pool. The co-citation architecture reveals a field knit together by iterative bench-to-bedside feedback: mechanistic insights funnel from experimental journals toward hepatology and clinical-oncology outlets, which in turn cite back to basic science when refining intervention protocols, thereby sustaining a stable yet adaptable knowledge core.

Word frequency and annual changes in word frequency analysis

Keyword analysis highlights a thematic core centred on “hepatocellular-carcinoma,” “expression,” and “angiogenesis,” each exceeding 100 occurrences, while terms directly denoting resistance (“resistance,” “chemotherapy,” “transarterial chemoembolization”) form a secondary tier (Fig. 7). Temporal profiling clarifies how these foci have evolved: cumulative curves show a near-linear ascent of “hepatocellular-carcinoma” from 2010 onward, but an acceleration in “expression” and “angiogenesis” after 2016, outpacing all other terms by 2024 (Fig. 8). The inflection coincides with the routine availability of bulk- and single-cell transcriptomics in interventional specimens, which shifted attention from procedure-specific parameters to gene-level drivers of microenvironment-mediated resistance. Concurrently, the rise of “endothelial growth-factor” and the resurgence of “angiogenesis” mirror the clinical pivot toward anti-VEGF strategies—initially as adjuncts to transarterial chemoembolization and more recently as partners in immune-checkpoint blockade—which required mechanistic dissection of hypoxia-induced vasculature and its role in drug efflux. “Resistance” itself lags behind these upstream descriptors, suggesting that investigators frame treatment failure primarily through the lens of microenvironmental biology rather than as an isolated pharmacologic problem. The steadier, modest growth of “transarterial chemoembolization” indicates that loco-regional technique remains foundational but is no longer the principal intellectual engine; instead, the field is propelled by molecular interrogation of the tumour–stroma interface and by combination regimens designed to neutralise its pro-survival cues. The keyword trajectory captures a transition from procedural optimisation to integrated, mechanism-based approaches that couple interventional delivery with targeted or immunologic modifiers to forestall microenvironment-driven drug resistance. Within this shift, immunogenic cell death (ICD)—marked by pre-apoptotic calreticulin exposure, extracellular ATP release, and HMGB1 emission—has been increasingly profiled in embolisation models. These hallmarks are typically assayed by flow cytometry (surface calreticulin), luciferase-based ATP quantification, and immunoblotting or immunofluorescence for HMGB1, helping explain the recent burst of ICD-related keywords.

Main institutions’ publication volume and Co-occurrence analysis

Sun Yat-sen University leads the field with 111 papers, trailed by Fudan University and Zhejiang University, giving Chinese centres three of the top four positions and confirming that clinical volume, national research grants targeting hepatitis-B–related hepatocarcinogenesis, and vertically integrated basic-to-interventional programmes have consolidated output in the Pearl and Yangtze River Deltas (Fig. 9). The University of Texas System—driven largely by MD Anderson’s locoregional-therapy group—constitutes the highest-ranking non-Asian contributor (44 papers), reflecting both its proximity to a diverse liver-cancer patient population and its early adoption of molecular imaging to interrogate microenvironment-mediated resistance. An institutional co-authorship map reveals a core-periphery architecture centred on Sun Yat-sen and Fudan, whose edges extend to satellite Chinese hospitals and to key international partners including Seoul National University, National University of Singapore and Tehran University of Medical Sciences, forming colour-coded clusters aligned with linguistic and geographic affinity (Fig. 10). The density of intra-Chinese links underscores a coordinated national strategy, while cross-cluster ties—particularly those connecting Fudan to Harvard and the US National Cancer Institute—indicate knowledge exchange on combination embolisation-immunotherapy protocols. The appearance of the Egyptian Knowledge Bank among the top ten affiliations reflects national open-access mandates that amplify institutional visibility rather than sheer laboratory capacity. Collectively, the landscape suggests that centres with direct access to high-volume interventional suites, biobank infrastructure and cross-disciplinary translational teams dominate publication and collaboration patterns; provided current investment continues, multinational consortia integrating single-cell analytics with image-guided delivery are likely to define the next growth phase.

Analysis of major National collaboration models

The corresponding-author profile shows that China generates the largest absolute volume and also the highest proportion of single-country papers (SCP), whereas the United States contributes fewer total documents but with a markedly higher multiple-country share (MCP) (Fig. 11); this distribution underpins two complementary collaboration models. The first is a “hub-and-spoke” structure centred on China, where large domestic consortia distribute imaging platforms and biobank resources among provincial hospitals, thereby favouring SCP output yet maintaining outward links that channel mechanistic data to specialist partners abroad. The second is a trans-Pacific “dual-anchor” model in which U.S. laboratories—often embedded in comprehensive cancer centres—co-author with Asian interventional units to integrate molecular profiling with device development; these projects account for most long-haul edges in the country-collaboration map (Fig. 12). A third, regional “triangular” pattern connects Italy, Japan and South Korea through EU-funded nanocarrier programmes and Asia-Pacific early-phase trials, illustrating how regulatory alignment and shared hepatitis-B epidemiology facilitate mid-distance partnerships. The prominence of cross-continental lines linking Europe to both North America and East Asia reflects the need for diverse etiological cohorts when validating tumour-microenvironment biomarkers and for pooled case numbers in trials that combine chemoembolisation with anti-angiogenic or immune-checkpoint agents. Collaboration intensity is driven less by geographic proximity than by complementary assets—patient volume in Asia, analytic infrastructure in North America, and harmonised funding in Europe—yielding a stratified network in which bilateral flagship alliances coexist with densely connected regional clusters.

Keyword Co-occurrence analysis

The keyword co-occurrence map resolves three densely linked thematic clusters: a therapy-centred group (green) anchored by “hepatocellular carcinoma,” “transarterial chemoembolization,” “radiofrequency ablation” and “angiogenesis”; a microenvironment–biology group (blue) organised around “tumor microenvironment,” “hypoxia,” “cells” and “expression”; and a resistance-mechanism group (red) dominated by “drug resistance,” “chemoresistance,” “epithelial–mesenchymal transition” and “apoptosis” (Fig. 13). Overlay visualisation shows that terms describing loco-regional procedures were established earliest (cool colours), whereas microenvironmental concepts and immunotherapy-related nodes (e.g., “immune checkpoint,” “nanoparticles,” “combination therapy”) have appeared more recently, shifting the network’s centroid toward yellow by 2021 (Fig. 14). This temporal progression reflects the field’s trajectory: initial research (2000–2010) optimised embolisation devices and ablation parameters in response to high post-procedure recurrence; persistent failure rates redirected attention (2011–2016) to stromal hypoxia, endothelial growth-factor signalling and angiogenic rebound as root causes of resistance; Regulatory approval of sorafenib, lenvatinib and PD-1 inhibitors for advanced liver cancer (2017-present) stimulated a surge in studies combining image-guided delivery with molecular or immune modulation, accounting for the late-emerging “immunotherapy” and “nanoparticles” nodes. The tight interlacing of clusters indicates that current investigations rarely address technique, biology or resistance in isolation; instead, they interweave procedural innovation with single-cell transcriptomics and drug-carrier engineering to dismantle microenvironmental barriers to therapy. The network topology and its temporal shading demonstrate how clinical need (suboptimal local control), technological availability (high-throughput sequencing) and therapeutic opportunity (checkpoint blockade) sequentially re-shaped research priorities, yielding a mature yet still expanding landscape that now prioritises mechanism-informed combination strategies. Conceptually, several of these strategies pursue synthetic lethality by pairing embolisation-induced stressors (hypoxia, reactive oxygen species, replication stress) with targeted vulnerabilities in angiogenesis, DNA-damage repair, or cell-cycle control (e.g., VEGF/PD-1 inhibition with PARP or CDK blockade), a rationale consistent with the rising bibliometric momentum of combination keywords.

Analysis of thematic development trends

Trend-topic mapping delineates three sequential waves of inquiry (Fig. 15). Across the same period, TACE technology progressed from conventional Lipiodol-based chemoembolisation (cTACE) to drug-eluting bead TACE (DEB-TACE) and, more recently, degradable starch microsphere TACE (DSM-TACE); each generation prompted distinct resistance-centred investigations—drug-washout and hypoxia severity in cTACE, release-kinetics and endothelial/CAF remodelling in DEB-TACE, and transient ischaemia–reperfusion and immune effects with DSM—mirrored by topic bursts in the bibliometric curves. During the formative phase (2004–2010) bubbles of modest diameter cluster around “clinical-significance,” “randomized controlled trial” and “arterial embolization,” reflecting an emphasis on procedural optimisation and early-phase evidence for chemoembolisation. The intermediate stage (2011–2017) shows expansion of mechanistic terms—“angiogenesis,” “tumor hypoxia,” “gene-expression,” “multidrug-resistance”—and of technology-oriented nodes such as “radiofrequency ablation,” signalling a pivot toward deciphering microenvironmental barriers that undermine locoregional efficacy; this shift aligns temporally with widespread adoption of next-generation sequencing and the first demonstrations that hypoxia-induced VEGF signalling drives post-embolisation recurrence. The most recent surge (2018–2024) is dominated by large, late-appearing bubbles for “lenvatinib” and “atezolizumab plus bevacizumab,” alongside regulatory keywords (“guidelines,” “1st-line treatment”) that attest to clinical uptake of molecular-targeted and immune-checkpoint combinations. The upward trajectory of “resistance” and “cells” within the same period indicates that translational groups are interrogating cell-state plasticity and immune contexture to explain heterogeneous responses to these agents. Non-liver entities such as “pancreatic-cancer” emerge in 2022–2023, implying that insights from hepatocellular carcinoma are being exported to other solid tumours that share hypoxia-driven resistance phenotypes. The timeline portrays a field that has progressed from technique-focused trials to mechanism-guided combination therapy, propelled by technological advances in omics profiling, the licensing of anti-VEGF and PD-1 drugs, and the publication of evidence-based practice guidelines that re-defined first-line management of unresectable liver cancer.

Discussion

The present bibliometric analysis situates research on tumour‑microenvironment influences and drug‑resistance mechanisms in interventional therapy for liver cancer within a dynamic, technology‑responsive domain whose trajectory mirrors successive methodological inflections in oncology at large. Annual output remained modest until the late 2000s, then escalated sharply—most conspicuously between 2020 and 2022—coincident with heightened attention to cytokine‑driven endothelial injury and hypoxia signalling in SARS‑CoV‑2 infection, findings that raised immediate concerns regarding locoregional treatment efficacy in infected or convalescent patients [25‑28]. China emerged as the principal engine of publication growth, a pattern attributable to the country’s substantial hepatitis‑B caseload, sustained governmental investment in interventional oncology infrastructure and the consolidation of high‑volume clinical–translational consortia centred on the Pearl and Yangtze River Deltas [28–30]. The hub‑and‑spoke collaboration architecture observed here reflects synergistic distribution of resources—large patient pools in Asia coupled with advanced molecular‑profiling platforms in North America and Europe—thereby accelerating iterative refinement of combination regimens that pair transarterial chemoembolisation or ablation with anti‑angiogenic or immune‑checkpoint agents.

Journal‑citation mapping delineated a bifurcated knowledge ecology in which high‑impact basic‑science venues disseminate mechanistic insights on hypoxia‑induced VEGF expression, CAF activation and immune‑suppressive niche formation, while hepatology and clinical‑oncology outlets report protocol optimisation and early‑phase efficacy data [31–34]. The convergence of these streams is evidenced by the recent dominance of topic bubbles for lenvatinib and atezolizumab–bevacizumab, as well as the late‑emerging nodes for single‑cell and spatial transcriptomics, technologies that permit precise cartography of therapy‑refractory cell states within embolisation margins [35–37]. Keyword‑overlay chronology further demonstrates a temporal shift from procedure‑centred lexicons to microenvironment‑centric and immunotherapeutic terminology, underscoring the field’s transition toward mechanism‑informed combination strategies targeting angiogenic rebound, metabolic re‑programming and myeloid‑derived suppressor‑cell infiltration [37–40]. By contrast, patient-reported outcome constructs—such as FACT-Hep and EQ-5D—appear only infrequently as keywords or source categories in our corpus, suggesting relative neglect of PROs compared with mechanistic lines of inquiry and highlighting an opportunity to align biological endpoints with patient-centred benefit.

The strengths of this investigation include exclusive reliance on the rigorously curated Web of Science Core Collection, systematic duplicate elimination and synonym harmonisation, and cross‑validation of network metrics through independent software platforms. Nonetheless, several limitations warrant consideration. First, bibliometric indicators privilege quantity and citation accrual over methodological quality; seminal negative trials or under‑cited translational studies may therefore be under‑represented. Second, early‑access material indexed after the search cut‑off was not captured, potentially underestimating the most recent pivot toward radiomics‑guided adaptive embolisation. Third, the deterministic clustering algorithms used herein cannot fully resolve the thematic overlap inherent to multidisciplinary studies that span imaging physics, drug‑carrier engineering and immunobiology. Finally, language restrictions inherent to WoSCC may obscure contributions from regional journals indexed exclusively in national databases. Continued integration of spatial‑omics profiling with image‑guided delivery platforms is poised to refine patient stratification and resistance‑mitigation strategies over the coming decade, positioning interventional oncology at the forefront of precision liver‑cancer care.

Conclusion

This study delineates the evolution of research on tumour-microenvironment influences and drug-resistance mechanisms in interventional therapy for liver cancer, underscoring how the field has advanced from device-centred optimisation toward mechanism-informed combination strategies anchored in anti-angiogenic and immunomodulatory regimens. Publication growth, driven principally by high-volume Chinese consortia and reinforced by trans-Pacific dual-anchor collaborations, has coincided with a marked shift in keyword emphasis from procedural terminology to microenvironmental and resistance-related descriptors, indicating a broadening investigative lens that now integrates spatial-omics profiling with image-guided delivery platforms. Journal-citation patterns reveal a bifurcated ecology in which basic-science outlets disseminate molecular insights on hypoxia, CAF activation and immune suppression, while hepatology and clinical-oncology venues translate these findings into protocol refinements; together, this feedback loop sustains a cohesive yet adaptable knowledge core. The present mapping highlights priority domains—single-cell analytics, radiomics-guided adaptive embolisation and multinational trials of combined loco-regional and immune-checkpoint therapies. Continued integration of advanced molecular profiling with high-throughput imaging, supported by cross-continental resource sharing, is poised to refine patient stratification and mitigate microenvironment-driven resistance in the next decade.

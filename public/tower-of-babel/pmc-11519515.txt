Analysis of the prevalence characteristics and risk factors of pulmonary tuberculosis combined with extrapulmonary tuberculosis in elderly patients
Authors: Tianshui Niu, Yaqing Li, Chuhui Ru, Aifeng Chen, Yan Shi, Shusheng Lu, Jiaying Han, Xianjuan Yu, Cheng Zhong, Yaqing Shen, Fei He
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo analyze the clinical features, epidemiological characteristics, risk factors, and complication characteristics of pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) combined with extrapulmonary tuberculosis (EPTB) in elderly patients (aged > 60 years). Clinical data were collected from pathogen positive elderly patients with PTB hospitalized in a teaching hospital in Zhejiang from 2017 to 2023. We retrospectively analyzed the risk factors for complications of EPTB in elderly patients with PTB by univariate and multivariate logistic regression models. We also described the characteristics of complications in PTB with EPTB. A total of 781 PTB elderly patients were enrolled, of whom 86 (11.01%) had complicated EPTB. The most commonly invasive sites for EPTB were thoracic spine (17.53%), cervical lymph nodes (13.40%), and meninges (12.37%). 60 < aged < 80 years (OR = 2.876, 95%CI 1.290–6.412; P = 0.010), anaemia (OR = 2.212, 95%CI 1.135–3.967; P = 0.018) and osteoporosis (OR = 4.925, 95%CI 1.501–16.160; P = 0.009) were the independent risk factors for PTB with EPTB infection. PTB with EPTB had a higher proportion of multiple serous cavity effusion (19.8% vs. 12.2%) and longer hospitalisation (17 vs. 15, P = 0.004). 60 < aged < 80 years, anaemia and osteoporosis were found to be independent risk factors for PTB with EPTB in elderly patients. We compared the epidemiological and clinical characteristics of PTB with EPTB. These results are important for improving the diagnosis of EPTB, reducing complications, and improving prognosis.Keywords: Elderly patients, Pulmonary tuberculosis, Extrapulmonary tuberculosis, Risk factors, Complications, Anaemia, OsteoporosisSubject terms: Diseases, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is an ancient disease and the second leading cause of death among infectious diseases. The global incidence is estimated to be 10.6 million in 2022, which has increased from 10.3 million in 2021 and 10 million in 20201. In China, the proportion of TB patients aged 65 years and older is projected to increase annually from 21.3% in 2015 to 27.4% in 20212, indicating a rising trend.

Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis) typically initiates infection in the lungs and then spreads via the respiratory tract. However, if the body’s immune system is compromised, M. tuberculosis can also disseminate through the lymphatic system or bloodstream, leading to extrapulmonary tuberculosis (EPTB) infection by affecting other organs3. The EPTB among the 7.5 million reported TB cases (including new and relapsed cases) globally in 2022 is 17%, which represents an increase compared to 20191,4. In both the United States and the European Region, EPTB accounts for between 20% and 53% of all TB cases5–7, while in China this proportion stands at 24.6%, exceeding the global average of 16%8. The proportion of PTB with EPTB is also on the rise, the results of multi-province and multi-center research in China show that the proportion of PTB with EPTB is 11.93%9, and the proportion of PTB with EPTB in Beijing Chest Hospital from 2008 to 2017 is 6.1%10.

Compared to PTB, EPTB is characterized by atypical clinical symptoms, challenging diagnosis, high disability, and high mortality. Elderly patients represent a high-risk group for TB but are often overlooked due to the presence of underlying diseases and the atypical symptoms of EPTB. As the incidence of TB in elderly patients continues to rise, there is also a rapid increase in the proportion of elderly patients with both PTB and EPTB. Studies have shown that elderly patients aged ≥ 65 years have a higher mortality rate compared with those aged < 65 years. Global data from low-incidence countries show that approximately 80% of deaths in TB patients occur in patients over 65 years of age. In addition, age represents a risk factor associated with polypharmacy, drug burden, existing comorbidities, and the development of adverse drug reactions due to lower efficiency of drug clearance in the kidney and liver11. Therefore, it is crucial to enhance research on PTB combined with EPTB in the elderly as this plays a significant role in understanding the epidemiological characteristics and risk factors associated with its occurrence. This will ultimately aid in formulating appropriate preventive and control measures.

Materials and methods

Data sources

Case information of PTB patients admitted to Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital from January 2017 to December 2023 was collected. This study was approved (approval number 2021 − 232) by the Ethics Committee of Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital.

Inclusion criteria for the cases were as follows: age of admission > 60 years, positive pathogenic culture indicating M. tuberculosis, availability of drug susceptibility test results, and no evidence of EPTB infection prior to the onset of PTB. Exclusion criteria included: (1) patients with no pathogenetic results or only sputum smear positive, aseptic type identification and drug sensitivity test results; (2) patients with less than 2 days of hospitalization; (3) patients with incomplete clinical case information. The case information from the first admission was utilized for patients hospitalized multiple times. The diagnostic criteria for PTB and EPTB followed the Chinese diagnostic criteria for tuberculosis ((WS 288–2017))12.

Date collection

The data collected included the following: (1) demographic parameters, such as sex, age, place of residence, and occupation of the patient; (2) history of TB treatment; (3) sputum smear results; (4) drug susceptibility; (5) whether there was a record of ICU stay; (6) underlying disease; (7) site of EPTB invasion; (8) complications during hospitalization; and (9) length of hospital stay.

Method process

The study process is illustrated in Fig. 1. A total of 781 elderly patients (aged > 60 years) were ultimately included based on the exclusion criteria. They were categorized into two groups: those with PTB alone (695 patients) and those with PTB combined with EPTB (86 patients). Risk factors for PTB combined with EPTB infection were analyzed using univariate and multivariate logistic regression. Additionally, chi-squared analysis was employed to compare complications between the two groups. The number of hospital days in both groups was analyzed using rank sum analysis.

Case selection process.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 23.0 was utilized for conducting the statistical analysis. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-squared and Fisher’s exact tests, while continuous variables were analyzed using the t-test and Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Variables with a P-value < 0.10 in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate analysis using the multiple logistic regression method to identify independent variables associated with PTB with EPTB infection. Variables with a P-value < 0.05 were considered as independent risk factors. The length of hospital stay was analyzed using one-way ANOVA. All tests were two-tailed, and significance was determined at a P-value < 0.05 level.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 781 elderly patients (aged > 60 years) with pulmonary tuberculosis were included in this study from 2017 to 2023, after meeting the specified inclusion and exclusion criteria. Among them, 695 (89.0%) had PTB alone and 86 (11.0%) had PTB with EPTB. The study comprised of 593 (75.9%) male patients and 188 (24.1%) female patients, with a median hospital stay of 15 days. The median age was 70 years and 60 < aged < 80 years there were 357 cases (80.15%). Of all the patients, 361 (46.2%) resided in rural areas while 420 (53.8%) lived in urban areas. A total of 69 patients (8.8%) required admission to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU). Among the patients, there were 466 (59.7%) who tested positive for sputum smear, with 638 (81.7%) being first-time treatment recipients and 143 (18.3%) undergoing retreatment. In terms of drug resistance, there were a total of 275 patients (35.2%) who showed resistance to rifampicin treatment. The most common underlying diseases among the patients included hypertension in 263 individuals (33.7%), heart disease in 192 individuals (24.6%), diabetes in153 individuals (19.6%), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease(COPD) in17.8%, and strokein 12.7%. During hospitalization, common comorbidities observed among the patients included lung infections (316 cases; 40.5%), hypoproteinemia (177 cases; 22.7%), drug-induced hepatitis (170 cases; 21.8%), respiratory failure (118 cases;15.1%), and multiple serous cavity effusion (102cases; 13.1%).

Site or organ involvement in EPTB

A total of 86 cases of PTB with EPTB were identified. Among these cases, 74 patients (86.04%) had EPTB involving only one tissue or organ, while 12 patients (13.96%) had involvement of at least two tissues or organs cumulatively (see Table 1). The most commonly affected sites in EPTB were the thoracic spine (17 cases, 17.53%), cervical lymph nodes (13 cases, 13.40%), meninges (12 cases, 12.37%), intestine (9 cases, 9.28%), and joints (9 cases, 9.28%). When classified according to systems and functions, the musculoskeletal system was the most frequently involved site (34 cases, 35.05%), followed by the lymphatic system (23 cases, 23.71%), head and neck region (20 cases ,26.62%), and digestive system (12cases ,12.37%) (see Table 2).

Analysis of risk factors for TB with EPTB

Univariate regression analysis revealed that 60 < aged < 80 years, sputum smear positivity, primary treatment, rifampicin resistance, COPD, anaemia, and osteoporosis were associated with PTB with EPTB infection (Table 3). Multivariate regression analysis showed that 60 < aged < 80 years (OR = 2.876, 95%CI 1.290–6.412; P = 0.010), anaemia (OR = 2.212, 95%CI 1.135–3.967; P = 0.018), osteoporosis (OR = 4.925, 95%CI 1.501–16.160; P = 0. 009) were the independent risk factors for TB with EPTB, whereas sputum smear positivity (OR = 0.576, 95%CI 0.358–0.926; P = 0.023) and rifampicin resistance (OR = 0.495, 95%CI 0.282–0.867; P = 0.014) were protective factors for PTB with EPTB (Table 4).

Discussion

We conducted a comprehensive analysis of the epidemiological and clinical characteristics, as well as risk factors for infection in elderly patients with PTB and PTB with EPTB at a teaching hospital in China from 2017 to 2023. Our study included a total of 781 pathogen-positive elderly patients with PTB, and 86 patients (11.0%) with PTB with EPTB. A retrospective study by Kang et al. from 2011 to 2017 in a multiregional hospital in China revealed that 29.48% of patients with PTB had concomitant infections with EPTB, while the proportion was 24.3% in patients over 65 years of age13. Based on the Chinese diagnostic criteria for tuberculosis, our study included tuberculous pleurisy in the diagnosis of PTB, which may result in a lower rate of EPTB. With the aging population, elderly patients with PTB and EPTB have become an important group that should not be overlooked. Therefore, it is crucial to strengthen screening for EPTB in elderly tuberculosis patients and advance the diagnosis and treatment of TB in clinical practice, as this has significant implications for patient outcomes and prognosis.

The coexistence of immune disorders, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and diabetes mellitus in elderly patients increases their susceptibility to M. tuberculosis infection14,15. Our study found that elderly patients had a higher prevalence of chronic diseases such as hypertension (33.7%), heart disease (24.6%), diabetes mellitus (19.6%), COPD (17.8%). Additionally, they experienced higher comorbidities during hospitalization including pulmonary infections (316, 40.5%), hypo-proteinemia (177, 22.7%), drug-induced hepatitis (170, 21.8%), and respiratory failure (118, 15.1%) compared to the general population16. Therefore, in accordance with the standard treatment protocol for tuberculosis, young individuals can achieve a 90% cure rate within 6 months. However, elderly patients often present with multiple underlying diseases, compromised liver and kidney function, and susceptibility to problems in various organ systems. Additionally, some anti-tuberculosis medications may be contraindicated or necessitate discontinuation due to high side effects in this population. As a result, elderly patients typically require a prolonged course of anti-tuberculosis treatment and experience poorer therapeutic outcomes17–19. EPTB in the elderly is characterized by multi-site invasion, with 13.96% of patients presenting with more than 2 sites of infection. The distribution of EPTB invasion may vary across regions and age groups. In the United States, lymphatic TB is the predominant site, accounting for over 60% of cases, with cervical lymphatic TB being the most common, followed by pleural TB20. In India, another heavily affected country, the prevalent sites of EPTB are lymph nodes (34.4%), pleural effusions (25.2%), abdominal involvement (12.8%), and central nervous system manifestations (9.4%)21. In China, the prevalence of tuberculosis is most commonly observed in the respiratory system (excluding PTB) (35.5%), musculoskeletal system (15.8%), and peripheral lymphatic system (15.8%) among the general population8. Among children, meningeal tuberculosis (34.18%), pleural tuberculosis (19.15%), lymphatic tuberculosis (18.31%), and abdominal tuberculosis (14.55%) are the most frequently reported forms22. In elderly patients, we observed that the most common sites of PTB with EPTB were the thoracic spine (17.53%), cervical lymph nodes (13.40%), meninges (12.37%), intestines (9.28%), and joints (9. 28%). The proportion of skeletal and articular involvement in the elderly was significantly higher than in children, reflecting the distinct characteristics of EPTB infection in this age group.

Studies have indicated a high prevalence of EPTB in women23. In older patients, we did not observe a significant difference in gender. Some studies have suggested that being under the age of 40 is a risk factor for the prevalence of EPTB10. Our findings revealed that 60 < aged < 80 (OR = 2.876, 95%CI 1.290–6.412; P = 0.010) was an independent risk factor for PTB with EPTB in elderly patients. This suggests that EPTB is more prevalent in younger stages of the disease. Therefore, it is crucial to pay attention to EPTB in younger patients with PTB to prevent misdiagnosis and underdiagnosis. We also discovered that anaemia (OR = 2.212, 95%CI 1.135–3.967; P = 0.018) and osteoporosis (OR = 4.925, 95%CI 1.501–16.160; P = 0.009) were independent risk factors for PTB combined with EPTB infection in older patients. It is well known that anaemia and malnutrition are common underlying conditions in patients with TB24,2526, and anaemia is more prevalent in patients with PTB with EPTB. A study by Yi Li et al. also revealed that patients with EPTB experienced significantly greater weight loss, a higher proportion of nutritional risk, and a higher likelihood of anaemia compared to those with PTB27. Di Gennaro F’s research showed that poverty and the social fragility were also important factors for poor outcomes in TB patients, which is consistent with our study, because poverty leads to anemia, nutritional deficiencies and other health problems28. The high incidence of nutritional risk in older people may be attributed to age-related changes in taste and smell senses, oral health issues, decreased physical activity, and other factors that contribute to loss of appetite and inadequate food intake in the elderly29. Additionally, TB patients often experience heightened catabolism and increased energy expenditure, leading to greater nutritional needs30. Therefore, it is crucial to closely monitor malnutrition in elderly PTB patients with EPTB and provide timely nutritional intervention. Notably, our study also revealed osteoporosis as an independent risk factor for combined EPTB in elderly PTB patients, a finding consistent with observations in the general population16. We hypothesized that osteoporosis, as a risk factor for PTB combined with EPTB in elderly patients, may be related to three aspects. Firstly, patients with EPTB have a higher nutritional consumption, and elderly patients themselves have nutritional deficiencies, especially vitamin D deficiency, which may lead to osteoporosis31. Secondly, elderly patients have a high prevalence of tuberculosis combined with bone tuberculosis cases. Tuberculosis bacteria first damage the peripheral bone when invading the skeletal system, weakening the maintenance of bone tissue and further increasing the risk of osteoporosis in patients32. Lastly, in addition to the above factors, elderly patients also have reduced levels of sex hormones and suffer from various diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Long-term use of hormonal drugs leads to osteoporosis33.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, it is important to note that this is a single-centre retrospective study, which may introduce geographical and hospital-to-hospital bias into the results. Additionally, the study only focused on older patients who tested positive for tuberculosis bacilli, potentially leading to selection bias in the sample selection process. It should be acknowledged that a large number of patients with a clinical diagnosis of tuberculosis but no pathological diagnosis were excluded from the study, which could also impact the accuracy of the findings.

Conclusion

The incidence of tuberculosis in the elderly has reached a peak and is now a serious public health concern due to the increasing aging population. Elderly patients with multiple underlying diseases present challenges in diagnosing and treating PTB with EPTB. In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis of the clinical and epidemiological characteristics of elderly TB patients. Our findings revealed that 60 < aged < 80, anaemia, and osteoporosis were independent risk factors for TB combined with EPTB infection in elderly patients. Additionally, we compared the comorbidity characteristics between PTB alone and PTB with EPTB, providing valuable insights for the diagnosis and treatment of these conditions in clinical settings. Through our research findings, we assert that the extrapulmonary detection of elderly patients with PTB should be continuously enhanced in clinical practice to facilitate the early identification of those with EPTB infections. Simultaneously, it is important to recognize that tuberculosis is a wasting disease. In addition to bolstering nutritional support, it is essential to supplement iron and vitamin D in order to improve the conditions of anemia and osteoporosis in affected patients, thereby reducing the incidence of PTB with EPTB infection.

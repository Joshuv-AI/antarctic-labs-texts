An efficient deep learning-based morphology aware hierarchical mixture of features for tuberculosis screening using segmentation of chest X-ray images
Authors: Mashael Maashi, Huda Eltayeb, Mashael M. Asiri, Rakan Alanazi, Abdulmohsen Saud Albesher, Eman M. Alanazi, Rowida Mohammed Alharbi, Monir Abdullah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Tuberculosis (TB) is a chronic lung disorder caused by bacterial infection and is a major cause of death. Lung cancer also has a significant impact, and existing solutions concentrate on initial screening, which mainly results in better outcomes at a comparatively lower cost. Screening, particularly by chest X-rays (CXR), is globally recognized as an effective method for reducing lung cancer mortality. Therefore, a precise and initial identification of TB is highly crucial, or else, it threatens lives. In the investigation into cases of TB, CXR images are not only the primary method of diagnosis according to medical imaging, but also the radiological diagnosis. The recent developments of computing, deep learning (DL), for image processing, carry a beneficial effect for the automated identification of numerous illnesses from CXRs. Now, the effectiveness of lung segmentation and TB screening methods is established for CXRs analysis by the DL technique to help radiologists recognize suspicious lesions and nodes in lung cancer patients. This paper presents an Efficient Deep Learning-Based Hierarchical Feature Fusion Approach for Lung Segmentation and Tuberculosis Screening (EDLHFFA-LSTS) model. The aim is to develop an automatic DL-based framework for precise lung segmentation and TB screening using CXR images to support early diagnosis and clinical decision-making. Initially, the image pre-processing stage includes resizing, adaptive filtering (AF), and histogram equalization (HE) to enhance the image quality. For the segmentation process, the EDLHFFA-LSTS model implements the Res-UNet method. Furthermore, the fusion of EfficientNetV2, CapsNet, and Convolutional Vision Transformer (CViT) techniques is employed for the feature extraction process. Finally, the stacked autoencoder (SAE) technique is implemented for classification. Extensive simulations were conducted to demonstrate the promising results of the EDLHFFA-LSTS methodology on the CXR Masks and Labels dataset. The comparison study of the EDLHFFA-LSTS methodology illustrated a superior accuracy value of 98.33% over existing models.

Introduction

TB is among the critical health concerns worldwide. In recent years, TB cases have declined, whereas in some metropolitan areas, TB incidence is rising1. An automatic technique to detect TB through CXRs relies on lung segmentation and feature elimination. Lung segmentation, the procedure of precisely recognizing edges and areas of the lung field from adjacent thoracic tissue, is a primary phase in lung image evaluation of numerous medical decision support systems2. Precise diagnosis of lung fields allows further computational examination of those anatomical regions, like clinically related feature extraction for training a machine learning (ML) model to detect disease and irregularities. An automated procedure is beneficial for detecting TB in CXRs, depending on the lung segmentation and removal3. This study focused on three types of abnormalities: textural abnormalities, marked by widespread alterations in the appearance and structure of a region; central abnormalities, which are isolated, defined variations in density; and shape abnormalities, where illness processes have changed the outline of normal anatomical structures4. Initial identification of lung segmentation and TB screening with appropriate treatment can cure this life-threatening disorder. CXRs are usually used to detect and screen pulmonary TB. In medical practice, chest radiographs are analyzed by skilled doctors to detect TB5. But it is a subjective and longer process. Subjective variations in disease identification from radiographs are unavoidable. Significantly, CXRs of TB are often misidentified as other disorders, which might result in inappropriate treatment for the patients and thus worsen their health state6.

In this regard, computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) methodologies play a vital part in the lung screening of TB by CXR analysis. Among the ML approaches, DL models have displayed higher capacity in image classification and are therefore extensively applied by academic circles7. X-ray radiographs are an inexpensive imaging modality, and a large quantity of data exists to train several ML approaches, which makes DL approaches prevalent for automated lung disease detection from chest radiography7. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are employed in numerous current research studies to detect lung disease, such as TB and pneumonia screening, through examining CXR. The utilization of DL models is stated in the identification of TB by changing the deep-layered CNN’s parameters. The convenience of broad labelled datasets as well as deep CNN has given rise to enormous achievements in image recognition8. CNNs enable data-based, intensely descriptive image features to be acquired from sufficient training data; however, attaining datasets in the radiology field as widely interpreted as ImageNet continues to pose a challenge9.

Literature review on lung segmentation and TB screening

Mudiraj et al.10 introduced an automated TB diagnosis technique depending on DL, where automated examination and classification of CXR imaging for TB or non-TB were conducted. With the transfer learning (TL) technique for computerized TB detection, which reduces the time and cost, a DL-driven methodology named Mask RCNN is deployed for semantic understanding of images. It is an advanced model trained for determining pixel-level segmentation for every object in the image, utilized for TB diagnosis with generalized feature extraction. Khumang et al.11 proposed a semantic segmentation method that employs a DL model. To enhance the TB-lesion segmentation model’s efficiency, a contextual background label process was developed to decompose the heterogeneous CXR image background. From the developed process, five background subclasses comprise: mediastinum, lung, doc, body, and background, to adapt the ground truth data for training the segmentation techniques. In12, a novel automatic DL approach is presented depending on lung-based segmentation and classification (DLSAC-Net). Initially, a novel contrast enhancement technique is used at the pre-processing stage to improve the radiograph image’s contrast. Finally, deploys extensive attention to recognizing the significant spatial channels and positions simultaneously. Tiwari and Katiyar13 presented a hybrid DL-based CXR image segmentation and classification for TB (HDL-ISCTB) method. It effectively differentiates lung regions from the surrounding structure, reducing computational intricacy and possible noise. This framework deploys CNN’s strong feature extraction abilities and the temporal relationships extracted by LSTM for obtaining strong illustrations from sequential CXR image data. Nafisah and Muhammad14 proposed an automated TB detection model utilizing sophisticated DL approaches. An important part of a CXR image is black, giving no data for identification and perplexing DL paradigms. Hence, this model used hi-tech segmentation networks for region of interest (ROI) extraction from multimedia CXRs. Ejiyi et al.15 introduced a new method named ResNet-fused External Attention Network (ResfEANet). This method shines in precisely identifying TB from CXRs, attaining extraordinary precision. ResfEANet is constructed on ResNet and integrates an EAN, leading to a moderately shallow network with few layers. This technique is extremely effective in extracting features and provides superior outcomes in TB classification.

Bista et al.16 suggested a CAD through sophisticated DL paradigms and CV methods, which increase diagnosis precision and decrease transmission risks through the YOLOv7 object detection framework. This framework can exactly detect objects. This framework leverages YOLO and CNN for the recognition of the association of cavitating patterns of the lesions. Ravi et al.17 proposed a multichannel DL technique for lung disease recognition by CXR. Following that, the merged features are input to multiple nonlinear FC layers. Lastly, the features were input to a stacked ensemble learning classifier (SELC) for lung disease identification. Ghose et al.18 developed a TL-based model using computed tomography and X-ray images to accurately detect COVID-19-infected patients, attaining high accuracy despite limited medical data. Zhao and Wang19 proposed a CNN with large kernel attention and a graph convolutional network for improving diagnosis by capturing long-range dependencies, anatomical segmentation, and label relationships. Ghose et al.20 proposed a DL-based CNN technique to accurately and automatically diagnose COVID-19 from chest X-rays, distinguishing it from pneumonia and normal cases with high accuracy. Wang et al.21 introduced an Efficient Hierarchical Multiscale CNN (EHMCANet) technique for effectively capturing multiscale contextual details and global features. Ou et al.4 presented DL techniques based on U-Net and its variants for accurate detection, classification, and segmentation of TB lesions in chest X-ray images, enhancing performance through ensemble techniques. Wang et al.22 developed a Dual-branch Dynamic Hierarchical U-Net with Multi-layer Spatial Fusion Attention (D2HU-Net) to improve medical image segmentation. Khan and Iqbal23 introduced a hybrid model by incorporating residual learning and spatial exploitation CNN with a customized swin transformer (RS-FME-ST) model to capture features precisely, thus enhancing performance. Patel et al.24 developed a computer-aided diagnosis system that incorporates Cascaded Local Histogram Adaptive Equalisation and Homomorphic Transformation Filter-based Flexible Analytical Wavelet Transform (HTF-FAWT) model with VGG-16 DL and support vector machine (SVM) classifiers to detect pneumonia accurately. Pandi et al.25 proposed a deep neural network (DNN) methodology by utilizing concatenation blocks and inception modules for accurate brain tumour detection and segmentation from MRI scans. Sreedhar et al.26 developed TransResUNet, a DL methodology by incorporating VGG16 encoder and residual blocks with Dice coefficient loss, for improving lung segmentation accuracy and robustness.

The limitations of the existing studies include overfitting due to small datasets, lack of unified frameworks integrating segmentation and classification, and insufficient attention to multi-scale contextual features. Various techniques depend heavily on handcrafted preprocessing or ignore spatial-channel interdependencies. Moreover, approaches utilizing CNN or TL often lack robustness across diverse image discrepancies and disease patterns. A major research gap exists in incorporating advanced hybrid models capable of addressing inter-class similarity, region-specific improvement, and adaptive feature fusion within a single end-to-end system for improved diagnostic precision.

Algorithm and system design

This paper presents an EDLHFFA-LSTS model. This paper aims to progress an automatic DL-based framework for accurate lung segmentation and TB screening using CXR images to support early diagnosis and clinical decision-making. For this purpose, the EDLHFFA-LSTS approach has image pre-processing, segmentation, feature extraction and fusion, and classification. AF and HE-based pre-processing model

Initially, the image pre-processing stage contains resizing images, AF, and HE to enhance the image quality27. The models are chosen for their efficiency in enhancing the quality of the image by mitigating noise and improving contrast. The AF model adapts to local image aspects, conserving crucial information while reducing distortions. Also, HE models improve the global image contrast, which is essential to proper evaluation, thus making subtle features more distinguishable. This incorporation enhances the visual clarity without significant computational overhead, easing downstream segmentation and classification performance. The robustness of the model across varied imaging conditions makes the model appropriate for pre-processing.

The image pre-processing phase is vital for tasks of segmentation. In the segmentation process, it is necessary to guarantee that the image is noise-free and regions are visibly described for more effective results. In this paper, an effectual image processing model is employed for the images in either dataset. Primarily, every image is resized to 512 × 512 resolution. Subsequently, the wavelet transform coefficients are computed for every image in the vertical, diagonal and horizontal direction, and their average is taken. Afterwards, the attained coefficients are applied to the conductivity parameters in an anisotropic diffusion filter. It assists in cleaning unwanted pixels that exist in images. Specifically, an adaptive filter is implemented utilizing the particular coefficients for every image. Finally, histogram equalization is carried out to generate the regions with more distinct features. The 512 × 512 resolution of the unprocessed image is selected to decrease the computation cost of the method, while safeguarding that visual detail is sufficiently maintained. Adaptive image filtering is employed to preclude the loss of significant information, particularly in noisy data, and decrease noise. Histogram equalization focused on assisting the classification process to raise the image’s contrast and improve the model’s performance on images with diverse levels of brightness. Every stage is assessed experimentally, and the impacts of outcomes on precision and generality performance are examined.

Segmentation using Res-UNet

Next, the EDLHFFA-LSTS model implements the Res-UNet method for the segmentation process28. This model is chosen for its ideal incorporation of U-Net with residual connections, which effectively captures both low- and high-level features while addressing the vanishing gradient problem. The training stability of the model is improved in this process, thus allowing deeper network designs related to U-Net models. The encoder-decoder structure with skip connections of the model conserves spatial data significant for precise boundary delineation. This technique also presents superior accuracy and robustness, particularly in medical imaging tasks, in which the extraction of detailed regions is crucial. Moreover, it efficiently balances performance with computational efficiency, making it appropriate for complex image segmentation.

The U-Net technique is a commonly accepted and very efficient structure for image segmentation, mainly in the medical field. Fusion of feature engineering techniques

Furthermore, the fusion of EfficientNetV2, CapsNet, and CViT techniques is employed for the feature extraction process. This fusion model is chosen for its effective, scalable convolutional features. This model shows optimized accuracy and speed, and also effectually captures spatial hierarchies and preserves part-whole relationships, improving robustness to discrepancies in input data. This model outperforms in modelling long-range dependencies and global context in images. Incorporating these models allows capturing diverse feature representations, thus enhancing overall discriminative power compared to using a single technique. This fusion results in richer, more informative features that enhance classification performance in complex medical imaging tasks.

Conclusion

This paper presents an EDLHFFA-LSTS model. The aim is to develop an automatic DL-based framework for precise lung segmentation and TB screening using CXR images to support early diagnosis and clinical decision-making. Initially, the image pre-processing is performed to improve image quality. Furthermore, Res-UNet-based segmentation is achieved. Moreover, the fusion of EfficientNetV2, CapsNet, and CViT techniques is employed for feature extraction. Finally, the SAE technique is implemented for classification. Extensive simulations on the CXR Masks and Labels dataset demonstrated the superior accuracy of 98.33% compared to existing models. The EDLHFFA-LSTS approach exhibits limitations in generalizability across diverse populations and imaging conditions, as well as a relatively small dataset size. Furthermore, the performance is not correctly validated on external datasets, which restricts its robustness. The computational complexity may also pose a deployment threat in resource-constrained clinical settings. Moreover, the interpretability of the decision-making process remains restricted, which can affect clinical trust and adoption. Future work should focus on expanding the dataset with multi-centre collaborations, integrating explainable AI models to improve transparency, and optimizing the model for real-time, low-resource environments to facilitate broader clinical application.

Electromagnetic performance analysis and multi-objective optimization of permanent magnet synchronous motors under static and dynamic rotor asymmetric offset conditions
Authors: Chenxin Dong, Xinchen Chen, Yan Xin, Feng Dong, Ziqi Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRotor asymmetric offset which constitutes an inherent flaw in permanent magnet synchronous motors (PMSMs) manufacturing and assembly adversely affects both the output performance and operational stability of PMSMs. Existing studies mostly focus on the qualitative analysis of single-type rotor asymmetry, lacking systematic quantitative comparison of the effects of static and dynamic asymmetric offsets on motor electromagnetic performance, as well as targeted optimization schemes for performance degradation. To address this gap, this paper first establishes mathematical models of static and dynamic rotor asymmetric offsets, and analytically derives their influence mechanism on air-gap flux density distribution, electromagnetic torque and power quality. Then, the no-load and on-load characteristics of the motor with healthy rotor, statically and dynamically asymmetric rotors are systematically compared via finite element analysis (FEA), quantifying the performance degradation caused by different offset conditions. Finally, to suppress the excessive torque ripple induced by dynamic asymmetric offset, a torque ripple suppression method based on auxiliary notches on stator teeth is proposed, and a multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO) algorithm is employed to optimize the notch parameters with the objectives of maximizing average torque and minimizing torque ripple. The simulation results show that the optimal parameter combination (notch width of 1.071 electrical degrees, notch depth of 4.446 mm) reduces the cogging torque by 11.04% and the torque ripple by 1.38% with a sacrifice (3.07% decrease) in average torque, verifying the effectiveness of the proposed method. This research can provide theoretical reference and engineering guidance for performance optimization of PMSMs with rotor asymmetric defects.Keywords: Asymmetry, Auxiliary notches, PMSM, Optimization, Mathematical modelSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Research Background and Significance

Permanent magnet synchronous motors (PMSMs) are widely considered as the “heart” of the electric propulsion drive systems due to their high efficiency, high power density, high reliability, and excellent dynamic response characteristics1–3. However, the electromagnetic performance of PMSM is extremely sensitive to its structural parameters and manufacturing precision. Even minor structural asymmetry or assembly deviation may lead to air-gap magnetic field distortion, increased torque ripple, elevated vibration and noise, and ultimately compromise the operational stability and service life of the motor.

In the various structural asymmetry problems of PMSM, the rotor asymmetric offset is one of the key problems that are difficult to completely avoid in the manufacturing and assembly process. This asymmetry may be due to the machining error of the rotor core, the coaxiality deviation between the shaft and the rotor core, or the mechanical stress deformation after long-term operation. According to the different offset characteristics, the rotor asymmetry can be divided into static asymmetric offset and dynamic asymmetric offset. Whether it is static asymmetry or dynamic asymmetry, it will destroy the ideal air gap magnetic field distribution, resulting in additional har-monic components in the electromagnetic torque, which in turn will cause torque ripple, especially in high-precision servo drive and high torque density application scenarios (such as electric ships). This fluctuation will directly affect the dynamic ride comfort and ride comfort. Therefore, it is of significant theoretical value and engineering importance to thoroughly investigate the influence mechanism of rotor asymmetry on the electromagnetic performance of PMSM and propose effective sup-pression or optimization methods to improve the reliability and comprehensive performance of the motor.

Research Status

Rotor asymmetric offset

Rotor asymmetry has attracted significant attention from both academic and industrial communities. Early research efforts primarily focused on individual types of asymmetric offset in isolation. For instance, Ref4. links rotor asymmetric offset to harmonics in the back electromotive force (back-EMF), flux linkage, inductance, and torque, which can degrade the accuracy of rotor position estimation and disrupt phase-locked-loop observers, although it does not provide a quantitative summary of these effects. The study in Ref5. proposes a novel asymmetry factor to model motors with asymmetric offsets, deriving the resulting air-gap flux density distribution and the net axial force between the rotor and stator. However, the case of asymmetry more than 40% is not applicable. In Ref6., the impact of rotor asymmetric offset on different electromagnetic noise and vibration modes is analyzed; the authors also propose a fractional-slot PMSM design and compare the dominant vibration orders for various pole/slot combinations. However, the influence mechanism of rotor asymmetry on air gap flux density is not studied in depth. Ref7. explores the effects of both static and dynamic rotor asymmetric offsets on electromagnetic performance. It shows that static offset causes imbalance in the amplitude and phase of the back-EMF without changing its harmonic composition, while dynamic offset al.ters the harmonic spectrum and phase angles, introducing half-cycle asymmetry in the phase back-EMF waveforms and generating corresponding even-order harmonics. However, the air gap flux density is a factor that is directly affected by asymmetry and has not been studied in depth. Furthermore, Ref8. provides a systematic examination of the electromagnetic performance of slotless PMSMs under static and dynamic asymmetric offset conditions. It is noted in9 that unbalanced magnetic pull (UMP) remains largely unaffected by the number of stator slots, whereas PMSMs with a higher pole count exhibit substantially increased UMP under asymmetric offset. Finally, Ref10. introduces an analytical model designed for rotor asymmetry, which ensures the alignment of the stator and rotor geometric centers. However, this study is to accurately analyze the difference between static asymmetry and dynamic asymmetry.

Multi-objective optimization

The design of electric motors frequently involves multiple and often competing objectives, making it challenging for conventional single-objective optimization to adequately address overall performance. Multi-objective optimization algorithms offer an effective approach for motor design, as they are capable of identifying a set of Pareto-optimal solutions within a complex design space. Among these, the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm has gained widespread adoption in motor optimization due to its straightforward principles, rapid convergence, and ease of parameter tuning. Refs11,12. introduce a novel surrogate-assisted multi-objective optimization algorithm. To mitigate the considerable computational time associated with repeated finite element analysis (FEA) evaluations, a Kriging interpolation model is developed. This surrogate-assisted approach enables faster and more accurate identification of a well-distributed Pareto front. In Ref13., the nondominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II) is utilized to optimize both positive-saliency and negative-saliency hybrid PMSMs, followed by a comparative evaluation of their electromagnetic performance. A multi-objective hierarchical optimization strategy is presented in Ref14., which classifies five objectives—output torque, permanent magnet cost, cogging torque, torque ripple, and efficiency—into two distinct levels. Ref15. performs a hierarchical analysis of design variables using a comprehensive sensitivity method to identify key variables, which are subsequently optimized via a response surface-based multi-objective approach. Meanwhile, Ref16. formulates an optimization function that seeks a balanced compromise among maximum average torque, maximum average torque per copper loss, and maximum average torque per motor volume through the use of three weighting factors. In Ref17., the cuckoo search algorithm is applied to efficiently determine the structural parameters of a Halbach PMSM. Furthermore, Ref18. proposes an innovative hybrid optimization algorithm that integrates artificial bee colony, strength Pareto evolutionary algorithm 2, and differential evolution techniques, enabling faster and more stable convergence toward optimal Pareto solutions. Researchers have investigated computationally efficient methods for the multi-objective optimization of motor efficiency and electromagnetic performance. Representative approaches include a dynamic Kriging-based updating method with multi-level partitioning to improve efficiency19, an improved sequential Taguchi method to lower computational cost20, and the application of transfer learning to reduce the expense of finite element simulations21.

Existing research mainly focuses on qualitative analysis under the condition of rotor asymmetry. There is a lack of comparative studies on different types of rotor asymmetry. Moreover, there is no systematic elaboration on the internal mechanisms and quantitative laws of their impacts on key performance parameters such as air-gap magnetic density distortion and electromagnetic torque ripple. Finally, there is a lack of optimization measures for the poor performance caused by the asymmetric offset of the rotor. In response to the above-mentioned issues, this paper takes the PMSM with rotor asymmetric offset as the research object, systematically analyzes the deterioration mechanism of its electromagnetic performance, and proposes a performance improvement method based on stator tooth slotting and multi-objective optimization. The main research work is as follows:

(1) Modeling of rotor asymmetry and analysis of its influence mechanism: Two typical working conditions, namely static and dynamic rotor asymmetric offsets, are identified. By establishing a mathematical model, the influence mechanism of rotor asymmetry on the air-gap magnetic density distribution of the PMSM is revealed, and the distribution laws of spatial and temporal harmonics are summarized.

(2) Comparison of electromagnetic performance under different asymmetric conditions: Based on the FEA, the no-load and load performances of the motor under three conditions, i.e., healthy rotor, statically and dynamically asymmetric offset rotors, are compared. The degree of harm caused by the asymmetric offset is quantitatively evaluated, providing a basis for subsequent optimization.

(3) Optimization method of stator tooth auxiliary slotting and multi-objective optimization design: A method for suppressing torque ripple by slotting auxiliary slots in stator teeth is proposed. Taking the slotting angle and depth as design variables and aiming to reduce torque ripple and increase average torque, the multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO) algorithm is used to optimize the slotting parameters, and the optimal parameter combination in the Pareto optimal solution set is obtained.

(4) Verification of optimization results and performance analysis: The optimized motor model is verified by FEA, and the improvement effects of torque before and after optimization are analyzed. The results show that the proposed optimization method can effectively suppress the performance deterioration caused by rotor asymmetry.

The main novelty of this paper is as follows:

(1) The influence mechanism of rotor asymmetry on the air-gap flux density distribution in permanent magnet synchronous motors is elucidated, and the distribution patterns of spatial and temporal harmonics are summarized;

(2) An optimized stator tooth auxiliary slotting method is proposed. This method reduces torque ripple by adjusting the slot angle and depth, employing a multi-objective particle swarm optimization algorithm for comprehensive design. This method provides a novel strategy for enhancing motor performance under asymmetric fault conditions.

The organization of this paper is as follows: Sect. 2 elaborates on the types of rotor asymmetry, the mathematical model, and the influence mechanism on the air-gap magnetic density. Section 3 conducts a comparative analysis of the electromagnetic performance under different rotor states through FEA. Section 4 proposes an optimization scheme for stator tooth slotting and introduces the multi-objective optimization model and process. Section 5 summarizes the whole paper.

Mathematical model of the asymmetrically offset rotor

In this section, mathematical models under three conditions, namely the healthy rotor, the static asymmetric offset of the rotor, and the dynamic asymmetric offset of the rotor, are presented. Additionally, the spatial and temporal components existing in the air-gap magnetic density are derived and summarized.

The type of asymmetrically offset

According to the actual industrial situation, the rotor’s asymmetric offset can be divided into static rotor asymmetric offset and dynamic rotor asymmetric offset. Before that, three centers need to be explained: the geometric center of the rotor, the geometric center of the stator, and the motion center of the rotor. In the healthy rotor, the geometric center of the rotor, the geometric center of the stator, and the motion center of the rotor coincide and remain unchanged. However, when the rotor experiences an asymmetric offset, these three centers no longer coincide, as shown in Fig. 1.

In the healthy motor, there are two types of spatial harmonics of the air-gap magnetic density, which are np and np ± kQ. These are related to the number of magnetic poles and the number of slots. There is one type of time harmonic of the air-gap magnetic density, which is np. In the motor with static rotor asymmetric offset, there are four types of spatial harmonics of the air-gap magnetic density, namely np, np ± kQ, np ± g, and np ± g±kQ. Compared with the healthy motor, there are two additional components in the spatial harmonics, np ± g and np ± g±kQ. This is because the rotor becomes spatially asymmetric after the offset, and the mechanical period is 1. There is still one type of time harmonic of the air-gap magnetic density, which is np, because the center position of the rotor does not change with time. In the motor with dynamic rotor asymmetric offset, there are also four types of spatial harmonics of the air-gap magnetic density, which are np, np ± kQ, np ± g, and np ± g±kQ, the same as in the case of static asymmetric offset. There are two types of time harmonics of the air-gap magnetic density, which are np and np ± g. This is because the center position of the rotor changes with time, and the mechanical period is 1. The spatial and time harmonics in this part will be fully verified in Sect. 3.

No-load performance comparison

No-load air-gap magnetic flux density

The no-load air-gap magnetic density serves as the medium for the energy conversion of the motor. Its magnitude and waveform affect the core loss, the amplitude of the back-EMF, and the risk of core saturation, laying the foundation for the subsequent load performance.

First, the instantaneous no-load air-gap magnetic density of the motor is studied under three conditions: healthy rotor, static rotor asymmetric offset, and dynamic rotor asymmetric offset. However, for the instantaneous no-load air-gap magnetic density, the difference between the static and dynamic asymmetric offset cases only lies in the time distribution, and there is no difference in the spatial distribution. Therefore, taking the rotor asymmetric offset of 0.25 mm as an example, Fig. 3 shows the instantaneous no-load air-gap magnetic density under the healthy and asymmetric offset conditions. It can be seen from Fig. 3(a) that under the healthy condition, the shape of the no-load air-gap magnetic density shows a periodic fluctuation, presenting a characteristic similar to a sine wave as a whole. Meanwhile, the stator slotting design changes the distribution path of the air-gap magnetic field. When the magnetic field passes through the slot area, due to the local change of magnetic permeability, the magnetic density changes accordingly, introducing grooves in the air-gap magnetic density waveform.

The MTPA control strategy aims to maximize the ratio of torque to current, outputting the maximum torque with the minimum given current, or using the minimum current under a constant torque. The MTPA makes full use of the motor’s torque, reduces copper loss, and improves efficiency. Multi-objective optimization based on a new slotting method

Since the torque ripple is excessively large under the dynamic asymmetric offset condition of the rotor, it is particularly important to adopt a measure to suppress the excessive torque ripple. In this section, a method of opening auxiliary slots in the stator is proposed. Then, the key parameters of the auxiliary slots are optimized based on the MOPSO. Finally, the effectiveness of the optimization measure is compared through FEA.

New slotting method

In this section, a method of slotting the stator is proposed to reduce excessive torque ripple. Figure 15 shows a schematic diagram of the stator auxiliary slot. The slot shape is fan-shaped, and its shape parameters can be represented by the angle and depth. The position of this slot is at the center of the stator slot.

Schematic diagram of stator auxiliary slot.

When auxiliary slots are introduced on the stator teeth, it effectively increases the number of stator slots and alters the pole-slot combination. From a qualitative perspective, according to Eq. (10) in the paper, when the number of stator slots is fixed, only harmonics at multiples of the stator slot number appear in the air-gap permeance, while harmonics of other orders have zero amplitude. When the number of stator slots increases, more harmonics of the air-gap permeance will exhibit zero amplitude. As indicated by Eq. (15), the harmonic components of the air-gap flux density will correspondingly change. Figure 16 presents the Fourier transform results of the no-load air-gap flux density with and without auxiliary slots. Certain higher-order harmonics, such as the 17th and 31 st harmonics, are effectively suppressed. This has important practical significance for applications requiring high precision, low vibration, and low noise, such as the aerospace and new energy fields. It should be noted that the stator auxiliary slot method is applicable not only to motors with dynamic eccentricity, but also to those with static eccentricity and normal rotors, demonstrating strong versatility.

Principle of the multi-objective particle swarm optimization algorithm

Multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MPSO) algorithm is a kind of stochastic optimization algorithm based on swarm intelligence, which is inspired by the conclusion of the simulation of the social behavior of birds in artificial life research. The algorithm simulates the rows of birds flying for food, and optimizes the group through the collective cooperation between birds. Similar to genetic algorithm, it is also based on population iteration, but there is no crossover and mutation operator. The population follows the optimal particle in the solution space to search.

In a particle swarm, each bird is equivalent to a particle. Particles have two properties: velocity and position. Velocity represents the speed of movement, and position represents the direction of movement. Each particle searches for the optimal solution independently in the search space and records it as the current individual optimal solution Pit. The individual optimal solution Pit is shared with other particles in the whole particle swarm to find the optimal individual optimal solution as the current global optimal solution Pgt of the whole particle swarm. All particles adjust their speed and position according to the current individual optimal solution they find and the current global optimal solution shared by the entire particle swarm. We will introduce this framework from four aspects: the selection of design parameters and optimization objectives, neural network fitting, and multi-objective optimization.

First, the two key parameters in stator auxiliary slot, namely the angle and depth, are selected as the design parameters. Table 3 lists the corresponding value ranges of these two parameters. Then, the optimization objectives are chosen. Due to the problem of excessive torque ripple under the dynamic asymmetric offset condition of the rotor, one of the optimization objectives is the torque ripple. Meanwhile, we hope that the motor can generate a larger torque under the rated condition, so the other optimization objective is the average torque. Figure 18 shows the average torque and torque ripple under the given two design parameters obtained through FEA. It can be seen that as the angle and depth increase, the average torque decreases. This is because stator slotting increases the equivalent length of the air-gap. It is essential to assess the significant correlations between parameters and objectives. To determine the correlations among variables, the Pearson correlation coefficient is selected as the evaluation metric, and the calculation results are shown in Fig. 19. It can be observed that both the angle and length of the auxiliary slots have a negative effect on the average torque, as they both increase the average length of the air gap.

Sensitivity analysis.

There is a discrete mapping relationship from the design parameters to the optimization objectives. It is difficult to calculate the values of the optimization objectives for any given design parameters, which hinders the process of multi-objective optimization. Therefore, it is particularly important to design a continuous fitting function to represent this mapping relationship. This fitting function is realized by using a neural network. The neural network consists of multiple layers of adaptive nonlinear units, and the model prediction error is gradually reduced through layer-by-layer learning. As shown in Fig. 20, its algorithm structure includes three levels: the input layer, the hidden layer, and the output layer. The specific parameter settings are as follows: the number of nodes in the hidden layer is set to 10, the number of input data parameters is set to 2, and the number of output data parameters is set to 1 respectively.

Structure of the neural network.

It can be seen that whether for the average torque or the torque ripple, their R-coefficients in both training and testing are relatively close to 1. This indicates that there is a high correlation between the predicted output and the target output. As shown in Fig. 22, the errors exhibit a Poisson-distribution trend, and most of the errors are concentrated around zero. The maximum deviation in the average torque is 0.6034 N∙m, and the maximum deviation in the torque ripple is 0.004394. In addition, the use of neural network greatly improves the optimization efficiency. It takes 109 s to use the FEA to calculate a scheme, while it takes only 0.001341 s to use the neural network. Compared with the direct use of the FEA, the use of neural network reduces the time by nearly 99%.

Finally, MOPSO is used for multi-objective optimization. The parameters of MOPSO are set as follows: the maximum number of iterations is 1000 generations, the population size of each generation is 200, the inertia weight is 0.5, the damping rate is 0.99, the individual learning coefficient and the social learning coefficient are 1 and 2, respectively, and the mutation rate is 0.2. The neural network is used to predict the average torque and torque ripple under each particle, and then 100 Pareto solutions are obtained. Figure 23 shows the distribution of the final Pareto solution set. It can be seen that when the Pareto solution is closest to the origin, this scheme can be regarded as the optimal one that takes both the average torque and the torque waveform into account. At this time, the angle and depth are 1.0708 deg and 4.4456 mm, respectively.

Conclusions

This paper focuses on the PMSM with rotor asymmetric offset, aiming to address the deterioration of its electromagnetic performance. A comprehensive research approach has been adopted, including modeling, performance comparison, and optimization method design. The following significant conclusions are drawn:

Firstly, through the establishment of a mathematical model for rotor asymmetry, the influence mechanism of rotor asymmetry on the air-gap magnetic density distribution of the PMSM has been clearly revealed, and the distribution laws of spatial and temporal harmonics have been summarized. The study reveals that under static asymmetric eccentricity, spatial harmonics of orders np ± g and np ± KQ±g are introduced. In the case of dynamic asymmetric eccentricity, additional temporal harmonics of orders np ± g are introduced on top of those present in the static asymmetric case. This theoretical analysis provides a solid foundation for understanding the root causes of performance deterioration. Secondly, the FEA has been employed to compare the no-load and load performances of the PMSM under healthy rotor, statically and dynamically asymmetric offset rotors. The electromagnetic performance of the motor is also experimentally validated under rated-load and half-load conditions. By quantitatively evaluating the degree of harm caused by the asymmetric offset, we have obtained valuable data for subsequent optimization, enabling a more targeted approach to improving the motor performance. Thirdly, a novel method for suppressing torque ripple by auxiliary slots in stator teeth has been proposed. Using the MOPSO algorithm with the angle and depth of auxiliary slots as design variables, and aiming at reducing torque ripple without significantly compromising average torque, we have obtained the optimal parameter combination from the Pareto optimal solution set. Finally, the FEA verification of the optimized motor model and the analysis of the torque improvement before and after optimization have demonstrated that the proposed optimization method can effectively suppress the performance deterioration caused by rotor asymmetry, and the torque ripple is suppressed by 1.38%. This indicates that the research results have practical application value and can be used to guide the design and improvement of PMSMs in engineering practice.

In the future, precise modeling will be conducted for additional types of spatiotemporal harmonics induced by rotor asymmetric offsets, and correlation models will be established between asymmetric parameters, auxiliary slot geometric parameters, and motor vibration metrics. Meanwhile, the integration of more advanced optimization algorithms, such as deep reinforcement learning, will be explored to enhance de-sign efficiency.

Three-loop QCD Mass Relation between the MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} and Symmetric-momentum Subtraction Scheme Away from the Chiral Limit
Long Chen, Marco Niggetiedt

Abstract
The perturbative result for the quark-mass conversion factor between the MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} and regularization-independent symmetric-momentum subtraction scheme (RI/SMOM) away from the chiral limit, i.e. at non-zero quark masses (RI/mSMOM), is derived up to three loops in QCD, extending the existing result by two additional orders. We further explore an illuminating possibility that in Dimensional Regularization, the original RI/(m)SMOM renormalization conditions may be interpreted merely in a weaker sense, namely as equations holding just in the 4-dimensional limit rather than exactly in d d dimensions: they work and result in different, albeit simpler, renormalization constants but still the same finite conversion factor. This novel observation has the added benefit of reducing computational effort, particularly at high orders. Furthermore we show that the two renormalization conditions considered for the scalar operator yield factors differing from the pseudoscalar case beyond one loop, but both converge to the latter in the chiral limit. Lastly, our result for the conversion factor exhibits rich behaviors, and in particular a window is observed in the subtraction scale and mass where it receives less perturbative corrections than the RI/SMOM counterpart up to three loops; this finding may help to further improve the accuracy of MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} quark-mass determinations with Lattice QCD.

RI/mSMOM conditions for quark mass

The relation between the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-renormalized quark mass
\overline{m}_{R}
and the corresponding mass
m_{R}
defined in mSMOM scheme can be established in dimensionally-regularized continuum QCD via the following multiplicative relation with the bare mass:

m_{B}=Z_{\overline{m}}(\bar{\mu})\,\overline{m}_{R}(\bar{\mu})=Z_{m}(\mu_{s})\,m_{R}(\mu_{s})

(1)

where
\bar{\mu}
denotes the scale of both
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-renormalized
\overline{m}_{R}(\bar{\mu})
and the perturbative
\alpha_{s}(\bar{\mu})
2
2

2

The definition of
\bar{\mu}
follows the usual
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization of the bare coupling:
\alpha^{0}_{s}=\alpha_{s}(\bar{\mu})\,Z_{a_{s}}\,\bar{\mu}^{2\epsilon}(4\pi)^{-\epsilon}e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}
with the DR regulator
\epsilon=(4-d)/2
.
, while
\mu_{s}
denotes the momentum subtraction scale in the mSMOM scheme.
Here and below we use symbols with subscript
B(R)
to denote bare (renormalized) quantities, and for generic objects if this subscript is suppressed.
In perturbative continuum QCD, both
Z_{\overline{m}}(\bar{\mu})
and
Z_{m}(\mu_{s})
are determined as functions of
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-renormalized
\alpha_{s}(\bar{\mu})
;
Z_{m}(\mu_{s})
also depends on the non-zero quark-propagator masses involved in the problem, while
Z_{\overline{m}}(\bar{\mu})
is independent of any masses.
Consequently, one defines the mass conversion factor:

\overline{m}_{R}(\bar{\mu})=\frac{Z_{m}(\mu_{s})}{Z_{\overline{m}}(\bar{\mu})}\,m_{R}(\mu_{s})\equiv\mathrm{C}_{m}(\bar{\mu},\mu_{s})\,m_{R}(\mu_{s})

(2)

which we determine for the first time up to three-loop order, in continuum QCD with one massive quark, with renormalized mass
m_{R}
, and
n_{l}
massless quarks.
(In the following text we will omit, by default, the arguments of these notations within brackets for simplicity, unless they are needed to ensure definiteness or prevent ambiguity.)

The full set of RI/mSMOM renormalization conditions were specified originally in ref.
[
17
]
that involve the 2-point quark propagating function
S(p,m)
at spacelike kinematics
p^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}

and 3-point one-particle-irreducible (1PI) vertex functions for elementary bilinear quark operators
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}
(such as vector- and axial-current, scalar and pseudoscalar operators) at the so-called symmetric-momentum configuration. We note that
Z_{m}
, together with the related quark-field mSMOM renormalization constant
Z_{q}(\mu_{s})
defined by
\psi_{B}=Z_{q}^{1/2}(\mu_{s})\,\psi_{R}
, can be determined by considering the reduced set of two coupled renormalization conditions:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}\,p^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[i\,S^{-1}_{R}(p,m_{R})\,\not{p}\big]\Big|_{p^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}}\,=\,1\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4iN_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\Lambda_{P\,,R}\,\gamma_{5}\big]\Big|_{p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=q^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}}\,=\,1\,,

(4)

with the renormalized quark-propagator mass set at
m_{R}
, provided the validity of the vector-current and non-anomalous (non-singlet) axial-current Ward-Takahashi identities
[
33
]
in QCD at the bare level in DR (which ensures the equality
Z_{P}=Z_{m}
in mSMOM
[
17
]
). In eq. (
3
),
S_{R}(p,m_{R})
denotes the renormalized quark propagating function with off-shell momentum flow
p^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}
.
In eq. (
4
),
\Lambda_{P\,,R}
denotes the 3-point 1PI vertex function for the renormalized pseudoscalar operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{P\,,R}=Z_{P}\,\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{P\,,B}=Z_{P}\,\bar{\psi}_{B}i\gamma_{5}\psi_{B}
with momentum insertion
q=p_{1}+p_{2}
at the symmetric momentum configuration
p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=q^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}
.
3
3

3

Note that here we consider only one massive quark flavor in the definition of this pseudoscalar operator. Summing
\bar{\psi}_{B}i\gamma_{5}\psi_{B}
over all quark flavors with equal weight gives the complete flavor-singlet pseudoscalar operator.

Unlike in SMOM, we do not use the simpler scalar operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,R}=Z_{S}\,\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,B}=Z_{S}\,\bar{\psi}_{B}\,\psi_{B}
for the mass renormalization, as we could not identify a simple all-order renormalization condition for
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,R}
at
p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=q^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}
with a non-zero propagator mass that could lead to
Z_{S}=Z_{P}
.
(See more comments on this in section
4.2
.)

Due to the off-shell momentum configuration, both
S(p,m)
and
\Lambda_{P,B}
become dependent on the gauge-fixing condition. There thus rises an additional freedom related to the renormalization of the general covariant-gauge-fixing parameter
\xi

\xi

beyond one loop in QCD, because the
\xi
-renormalization is not fixed by the mSMOM renormalization conditions specified in ref.
[
17
]
.
Fortunately, in the Landau gauge-fixing condition, the multiplicative renormalization of

\xi=0

The LQCD calculation in ref.
[
9
]
using mSMOM was performed with Landau gauge-fixing. Hence, for the numerical results to be discussed in section
4
, we will only consider Landau gauge (
\xi=0
).
On the other hand, we have kept the full
\xi
-dependence in our calculations.
For
\xi\neq 0
, we take the usual choice of setting the renormalization constant for
\xi
to be the same as that of gluon field in order to keep the form of gauge-fixing term un-renormalized.
For the moment, the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization of the gluon field is employed, similar as in ref.
[
30
]
, and the result for
\mathrm{C}_{m}
with so-determined
\xi

We note, however, that the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization of the gluon field cannot be implemented in LQCD calculation.
To amend this it shall be feasible to supplement the existing mSMOM conditions
[
17
]
with an additional RI renormalization condition for the gluon field in an analogue to eq. (
3
), namely demanding the full renormalized gluon propagator at the spacelike kinematics
p^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2}
to coincide with its tree-level form.
We leave the investigation of such a possibility, and derivation of the corresponding
\xi
-dependent mass conversion factor, for a future work.

4.2
An alternative interpretation of mSMOM conditions in DR

The result for
\mathrm{C}_{m}
presented in the previous subsection was derived in the standard way of interpreting the renormalization conditions (
3
) and (
4
) as exact equations in DR, i.e. to all orders in
\epsilon
, with field, mass and operator renormalization constants Laurent-expanded
necessarily
to sufficiently high orders in
\epsilon
.
In particular, (
3
) and (
4
) are solved for
the parametric coefficients in
Z_{m}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j=-i}^{3-i}Z_{m}^{(i\,j)}\,\alpha_{s}^{i}\,\epsilon^{j}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{4})
(and similarly
Z_{q}
) up to three loops in our calculation.
The higher the loop order in question, the deeper the expansion in both
\alpha_{s}
and
\epsilon
.
In view of the subtlety possibly encountered when taking into account the singlet-type diagrams with
\gamma_{5}
in closed fermion chains
5
5

5

These diagrams are irrelevant in SMOM provided one considers only non-singlet axial or pseudoscalar operators.
(see, e.g. refs.

we explore an illuminating possibility that, in the continuum QCD in DR, the original (m)SMOM conditions for all bilinear quark operators, irrespective of involving
\gamma_{5}
or not, can also be interpreted merely in a weak sense, namely as equations holding just in the 4-dimensional limit rather than exactly in
d
dimensions.
To be more specific, for determining
\mathrm{C}_{m}
we propose the following variant:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4N_{c}\,p^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[i\,S^{-1}_{R}(p,m_{R})\,\not{p}\big]\Big|_{p^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2};\,\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\,=\,1\,,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4iN_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\Lambda_{P\,,R}\,\gamma_{5}\big]\Big|_{p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=q^{2}=-\mu_{s}^{2};\,\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\,=\,1\,,

Note that by construction the

We anticipate that this point is not limited to the particular renormalization condition or renormalized quantity in question, holding true with broader applicability.

Firstly,

We conclude this subsection by a brief comment on the renormalization of the scalar operator in mSMOM.
We have considered both the original mSMOMS condition
\mathrm{Tr}\big[\Lambda_{S\,,R}\,-4i/q^{2}m_{R}\Lambda_{P\,,R}\gamma_{5}\not{q}\big]\big|_{\mathrm{sym}}=4N_{c}\,
, i.e. eq. (25) of ref.

(where

denotes the renormalized 1PI scalar vertex function and the subscript

),
and a simple SMOM-alike condition
\mathrm{Tr}\big[\Lambda_{S\,,R}\big]\big|_{\mathrm{sym}}=4\,N_{c}
but evaluated at finite non-zero

We have obtained two different

On the other hand, since the scalar operator is neither involved in the vector- nor axial-current Ward-Takahashi identities, its renormalization completely decouples from those of other bilinear quark operators.
Hence we may exploit this freedom to take them equal in the renormalization prescription from the outset.

5
Conclusion

LQCD calculations employing SMOM and its natural extension mSMOM–which is promising in reducing heavy-quark mass-dependent discretization effects–are playing an increasingly critical role in precision determination of quark masses, and high-order perturbative results for matching to the commonly used
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
masses are among the indispensable ingredients.
In this work, we successfully overcome the formidable challenge of computing three-loop massive off-shell vertex functions and present, for the first time, the high-precision result for the quark-mass conversion factor
\mathrm{C}_{m}
from mSMOM to
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
up to three loops in QCD,
extending the existing result by two additional orders.
Focusing on the scenario of
c
-quark mass determination, our results for
\mathrm{C}_{m}
exhibit rich behaviors and, notably, there exists a domain in the subtraction scale
\mu_{s}
and
m_{R}
where
\mathrm{C}_{m}
receives consistently less QCD corrections than its SMOM counterpart up to three loops;
a preliminary application of our
\mathrm{C}_{m}
further improves the agreement between the recent RBC/UKQCD’24 determination of
c
-quark
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
mass and its previous FLAG averages.

In addition, we have explored an illuminating possibility that in DR the original (m)SMOM renormalization conditions may also be interpreted merely in a weak sense, namely as equations holding just in the 4-dimensional limit rather than exactly in
d
dimensions:
they result in different, albeit simpler, DR renormalization constants but still the same finite quark-mass conversion factor.
Moreover, when solving the proposed weaker variant of the (m)SMOM conditions, an exact truncation to the finite order in
\epsilon
is not necessary.
We further show that the two renormalization conditions considered for the scalar operator yield factors differing from the pseudoscalar case beyond one loop, but both converge to the latter in the chiral limit.

There are several interesting follow-ups to the present work.
To name a few of them, one could consider to derive a RG-improved
\mathrm{C}_{m}
by resumming all non-power-suppressed logarithmic dependence on
\mu_{s}
;
supplement the existing mSMOM conditions with an additional regularization-independent renormalization condition for gluon field such that the
\xi
-dependent
\mathrm{C}_{m}
becomes useful beyond the Landau gauge;
extend the computational setup to investigate the mass effects of other quarks at higher-loop orders, for both flavor-non-singlet and flavor-singlet bilinear quark operators (where the subtle treatment of
\gamma_{5}
in DR would not pose any issue based on our investigation), including the bilinear tensor-current operator, as well as more importantly the four-quark operators relevant for studying meson mixing and hadron decays.

We therefore conclude that the significance of this work manifests in more than one aspects:
firstly, our newly-derived two- and three-loop results for
\mathrm{C}_{m}
can substantially improve the accuracy of
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
quark mass determinations with LQCD by reducing the systematic truncation errors;
secondly, several novel and compelling observations uncovered in our analysis may not only help to refine the application of RI/mSMOM renormalization, but also be useful in reducing considerably the computational effort especially when one incorporates additional quarks with different masses, such as in future applications to
b
-quark mass determination, and paving the way to incorporate higher-order singlet-type diagrams.

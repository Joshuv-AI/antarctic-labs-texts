Transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis of tobacco responses to combined organic-chemical fertilization
Authors: Xinfang Zhang, Kailin Liang, Minghao Dong, Xinyu Ma, Feng Sun, Zekai Wang, Xiefeng Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The combined application of organic fertilizer and chemical fertilizer can affect plant physiological activities and change the expression of its related genes and metabolites. However, past studies mainly focused on the effects of the combined application of organic and inorganic fertilizers on soil physical and chemical properties, microbial communities, and crop physiological changes, but the response mechanism of plant leaf metabolic cycles to different combined applications of organic fertilizers is not yet completely clear. Therefore, this study employed tobacco as a model crop to investigate the intrinsic regulatory mechanisms of physiological metabolism in tobacco leaves following the combined application of three distinct organic materials with chemical fertilizer and cattle manure. The research first revealed that the composite application of chemical fertilizer, cattle manure, and high-carbon-based compost (CMHC) exerted the most pronounced stimulatory effect on the growth and development of tobacco glandular trichomes. Compared to the control group (CK) treated solely with chemical fertilizer, glandular trichome density increased by 1.56-fold, while α-cembratriene diol and β-cembratriene diol content rose significantly by 146.40% and 2.39-fold respectively. This study employs transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses to reveal for the first time the molecular mechanism by which the combined application of cattle manure with other organic fertilizers enhances tobacco leaf defense capacity. This is achieved by regulating key genes, such as phosphoethanolamine N-methyltransferase and nicotine N-demethylase, in the glycerophospholipid metabolism as well as the tropane, piperidine, and pyridine alkaloid biosynthesis pathways. These regulatory actions promote the synthesis of downstream resistance substances, including N-methyldiethanolamine and nicotine. Notably, in the treatment combining cattle manure with humic acid (CMHC), the upregulation of genes such as glutamate–oxaloacetate transaminase and nicotinate reductase I modulates energy metabolism shifts in the leaves, thereby synergistically promoting leaf growth and the secretion of glandular trichome exudates. In summary, this study systematically elucidates the molecular mechanisms by which the combined application of organic fertilizer regulates tobacco leaf development and metabolite synthesis from an integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic perspective, providing a theoretical basis for green fertilisation and quality improvement in tobacco cultivation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07622-3.

Introduction

Fertilizers have the characteristics of a fast release rate and are easily absorbed by crops [44, 45]. However, a large number of farmers, in order to increase crop yields, overly rely on fertilizers and pesticides [73], resulting in soil acidification, subsidence, and degradation of soil quality [18, 95]. Research indicates that soil acidification resulting from nitrogen fertilizer application alone inhibits microbial activity [92], enhances the stability of soil organic carbon decomposition, reduces crop uptake of soil nutrients, and diminishes crop yield and quality [68]. By applying organic fertilizers instead of chemical fertilizers, it can help improve soil quality and provide the essential nutrients for crop growth and development [20, 47]. As nutrient release from organic fertilizer relies on slow microbial mineralisation processes [81], their release profiles struggle to match the explosive nutrient demands during critical crop growth stages [84]. Consequently, combining organic fertilizer with fast-acting chemical fertilizer has become a core strategy for balancing immediate crop requirements with long-term soil fertility. Studies have shown that the combined application of organic and inorganic fertilizers can increase the maximum leaf area index, thousand-grain weight, grain yield and biological yield of corn [53, 56].

Animal manure, as a traditional organic fertilizer, has always been recognized for its value. However, due to the lack of sufficient agricultural land for the direct use of animal manure, composting is regarded as a way to recycle manure. This process not only eliminates or reduces the transmission risks of pathogens, parasites, associated with the direct application of manure, but also can decompose organic matter into stable and available nutrients [7, 41]. Among them, cattle manure compost contains many functional groups, which can combine with soil organic molecules to promote polymerization and form soil aggregates, it helps to increase soil organic matter and soil nutrient content, thereby promoting plant growth and development and improving crop yield and quality [44, 45, 94]. However, due to the continuous microbial metabolic activities during the composting process, a large amount of nutrients are lost, especially nitrogen [31], and this loss can affect the quality of the compost product [6]. The above problem can be solved by adding materials such as high-carbon base fertilizers. High-carbon base fertilizer is a kind of mixed fertilizer with biochar as the substrate. Biochar has the characteristics of high absorption potential and large surface area [42, 43], which enables it to stimulate microbial activities [19] and improve soil fertility and crop yield [57]. Biological organic fertilizer is a new type of fertilizer made from animal manure and agricultural by-product residues. After application, it can promote the reproduction of functional microorganisms in the soil and provide a healthy environment for plant growth [49]. Cake fertilizer is a kind of fertilizer rich in nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, which can effectively alleviate heavy metal pollution in the soil [83]. Fu et al.'s [22] research shows that the appropriate application of sesame cake fertilizer can significantly improve soil nutrients, nitrogen fertilizer utilization efficiency and yield.

Transcriptomics and metabolomics are important components of systems biology, and they play a significant role in elucidating the reaction mechanism of the combined application of organic and inorganic fertilizers on flue-cured tobacco leaves. From the molecular perspective, the combined application of organic and inorganic fertilizers can affect the expression of genes and metabolites related to ribosomes and energy metabolism in rice leaves, improve the light-harvesting ability of leaves, enhance photosynthesis, and at the same time, it has a certain promoting effect on improving the disease resistance of rice [48]. Negi et al.'s [59] research also shows that under field conditions, the combined application of manure and biofertilizer can enhance the antioxidant capacity of strawberry fruits and has the potential to increase crop yield and its nutritional characteristics. In addition, the application of organic fertilizer can also promote the enrichment of beneficial soil microorganisms. These microorganisms can convert N-NO3− into N-NH4+, which is more easily absorbed by plants, further promoting the synthesis of growth-promoting hormones, starch, and sucrose in Pinellia ternata, thereby obtaining a higher yield [78]. Therefore, it is necessary to further explore the changes of genes and metabolites in flue-cured tobacco leaves, and understand their molecular response mechanisms to the combined application of organic fertilizers.

Tobacco is an important annual model crop and cash crop. Qujing City, as an important production area for China's tobacco leaf raw material reserves and exports, the tobacco leaves it produces have a unique and refreshing aroma style feature and are highly favored [93]. However, due to continuous monoculture, insufficient nutrient supply, and the invasion of pests and diseases, the yield and quality of flue-cured tobacco are affected [85]. Therefore, exploring the combined application of different types of organic fertilizers and chemical fertilizers is crucial for improving the yield and quality of flue-cured tobacco. However, past studies mainly focused on the combined application of organic and inorganic fertilizers on soil physical and chemical properties, microbial communities, and crop physiological changes, but the response mechanism of tobacco leaves to the combined application of different organic fertilizers is not yet completely clear. The purposes of this study are as follows: (1 To analyze the effects of combined application of organic fertilizer and chemical fertilizer on the growth, development and yield of tobacco leaves,(2 To identify the differentially expressed genes and metabolites of tobacco after the application of organic fertilizer and analyze their key pathways; (3 To reveal the molecular mechanism of the effect of the application of organic fertilizer on the development of tobacco leaves, and to provide a theoretical basis for improving the yield and quality of tobacco, reducing the application of chemical fertilizer, and exploring the suitable combination of organic fertilizer for tobacco.

Materials and methods

Plant materials, growth conditions and treatment methods

This experiment was conducted in Huangtupo Village, Malong District, Qujing City, Yunnan Province, China (E 103°41′, N 25°38′, altitude 1936.33 m). The area has a subtropical monsoon climate with an annual average temperature of 13.4 ℃ and an annual average rainfall of 1023 mm. The soil type is yellow soil, and the basic physical and chemical properties of the topsoil (0–20 cm) in the experimental area are: pH 5.13, organic matter 16.91 g/kg, total carbon 10.05 g/kg, total nitrogen 0.84 g/kg, alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen 82.99 mg/kg, available phosphorus 4.54 mg/kg, and available potassium 212.00 mg/kg.

The experiment was carried out from April to October 2023 (transplanted on April 8th). The flue-cured tobacco variety for the experiment was Yunyan 301, provided by Qujing Branch of Yunnan Tobacco Company. The experiment adopted a completely randomized block design with a total of 5 treatments: pure chemical fertilizer (CK), pure chemical fertilizer + 15,000 kg·ha⁻1 of cattle manure-based farmyard manure (CM), pure chemical fertilizer + 15,000 kg·ha⁻1 of cattle manure-based farmyard manure + 300 kg·ha⁻1 of sesame cake fertilizer (CMSS), pure chemical fertilizer + 15,000 kg·ha⁻1 of cattle manure-based farmyard manure + 750 kg·ha⁻1 of high-carbon base fertilizer (CMHC), and pure chemical fertilizer + 15,000 kg·ha⁻1 of cattle manure-based farmyard manure + 750 kg·ha⁻1 of bio-organic fertilizer (CMBF). Among them, the CM treatment is the conventional application amount in the local area. The principle of experimental fertilization is equal nitrogen treatment, with each treatment applying 109.69 kg·ha⁻1 of pure nitrogen. The inorganic nitrogen in the fertilizers is provided by a special compound fertilizer for tobacco and potassium nitrate. The fertilizer application rates for each treatment are calculated based on the nitrogen content of each fertilizer. Among them, the control (CK) treatment applies 562.50 kg·ha⁻1 of the special compound fertilizer for tobacco and 187.50 kg·ha⁻1 of potassium nitrate. For other treatment methods, reduce the amount of chemical nitrogen fertilizers accordingly based on the nitrogen content of organic fertilizers, and supplement phosphorus and potassium with superphosphate and potassium sulfate respectively. The nutrient content in the bio-organic fertilizer is ignored. The m(N):m(P₂O₅):m(K₂O) ratio in all cattle manure treatments is maintained at 1:1:3. The nutrient content of decomposed cow manure is: C/N (carbon to nitrogen ratio) 15.01, organic matter 353.13 g/kg, total nitrogen 13.79 g/kg, total phosphorus 3.57 g/kg, total potassium 28.73 g/kg. The nutrient content of other fertilizers is shown in Table S1. Each treatment was repeated 3 times, with a total of 15 plots. Each plot has an area of 144 m2, and the row spacing × plant spacing = 120 cm × 60 cm. All fertilizers were applied in holes before transplanting, and other field management measures were carried out according to the local technical specifications for high-quality tobacco leaf production.

When the tobacco plants are at the 60-day-old leaf stage, the 17th leaf position (from bottom to top) of the tobacco plants is sampled. After treatment, the samples are immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until use.

Tobacco physiological indicators

When the leaf age is 60 days, six tobacco plants with uniform growth from each treatment are selected. The plant height, stem girth, number of effective leaves, leaf length and leaf width of each plant are measured respectively, and the average leaf area (sum of leaf areas/number of leaves) of the tobacco plants is calculated.

Leaf glandular trichomes density statistics

The leaves at the 17th leaf position of each treated tobacco plant were collected at the leaf age of 60 days. Small leaf blocks of 5–8 mm2 were taken between the leaf edge and the midrib and fixed in 0.1 mol·L⁻1 phosphate buffer solution (pH 7.4) containing 2.5% glutaraldehyde for 24 h. Then, the samples were dehydrated through a series of ethanol solutions (25%, 50%, 75%, 85%, 95%, and 100%), with each level of dehydration lasting for 1 h. Subsequently, the samples were dried using a critical point dryer. After the drying was completed, the samples were mounted and sputtered with gold. The leaf surface was observed using a Hitachi Regulus 8100 scanning electron microscope at an accelerating voltage of 3 kV, and the number of glandular hairs with an area of 1 mm2 was counted [90].

Extraction and determination of leaf surface glandular trichomes secretions

The extraction of glandular trichome secretions from flue-cured tobacco leaves was carried out by referring to the method of Severson et al. [66, 91]. The 17th leaf position of each treated tobacco plant was collected at the leaf age of 60 days. 1000 mL of dichloromethane was poured into a metal tray, and the base of the petiole was clamped with forceps to immerse the entire leaf in the dichloromethane solution for 2 s [77]. The leaf was lifted out of the solution for 2 s to allow the dichloromethane on the leaf to drip back into the tray. Each leaf was repeated the above steps 4 times. After the last extraction, the dichloromethane on the leaf should be completely dripped back into the tray. All the dichloromethane in the tray was transferred to a 2000 mL beaker, and 1 mL of internal standard (the internal standard is 1.01 g sucrose octaacetate and 1.271 g n-17-alkanol, and the volume is made up to 500 mL) was added to the beaker. After thorough mixing, about 10 g of anhydrous sodium sulfate was added, and the mixture was stirred thoroughly to absorb the moisture. It was filtered with quantitative filter paper into a 1000 mL round-bottom flask, and the filtrate was rotary evaporated under reduced pressure to about 20 mL. Finally, the volume was made up to a 50 mL volumetric flask. 5 mL of the above extract was pipetted into a chromatographic vial, and the solvent was blown dry under nitrogen protection. 500 μL of 1:1 (v:v) DMF:BSTFA was added, and the derivatization reaction was carried out in a 75 °C water bath for 60 min. 0.5 mL of dichloromethane was added to dissolve the insoluble matter, which is the GC/MS analysis sample solution.

Qualitative and quantitative determinations were carried out using the TRAVE GC ULTRA-DSQ Ⅱ MS gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC–MS) combined instrument of Thermo Fisher Scientific in the United States. The reaction conditions of the gas chromatography–mass spectrometry combined instrument are as follows: GC conditions, the chromatographic column is DB-5MSUI quartz capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm); the inlet temperature is 250 °C; the temperature program is as follows, it is maintained at 40 °C for 2 min, then heated to 180 °C at a rate of 6 °C·min−1 and maintained for 2 min, and then heated to 280 °C at a rate of 2 °C·min−1 and maintained for 20 min; the carrier gas is high-purity helium, and the carrier gas flow rate is a constant flow of 0.8 mL·min−1; the injection volume is 1.0 μL, and the split ratio is 15:1; MS conditions are that the transfer line temperature is 250 °C; the EI ion source temperature is 280 °C; the ionization energy is 70 eV; the mass number range is 50–650 amu; the search spectrum library is NIST11.

Metabolomics analysis

Metabolomic sequencing was performed in Shanghai Ouyi Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd., with three replicates for each treatment. At the leaf age of 60 days, the 17th leaf position (from bottom to top) of each treated tobacco plant was collected. A 60-mg sample was weighed into a 1.5-mL centrifuge tube, and two small steel balls and 600 μL methanol–water (V: V = 7: 3, containing a mixed internal standard, 4 μg/mL) were added [79, 80]. After pre-cooling in a −40 °C refrigerator for 2 min, it was placed in a grinder for grinding (60 Hz, 2 min). After ultrasonic extraction in an ice-water bath for 30 min, it was left to stand overnight at −40 °C. Then, it was centrifuged at 4 °C for 10 min (12,000 rpm, 4 °C) and the supernatant was aspirated. After filtering with a 0.22 μm organic phase pinhole filter, it was transferred to an LC sample vial and analyzed by an LC–MS system composed of a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class plus/Thermo QE ultra-high performance liquid chromatography tandem high-resolution mass spectrometer. Please refer to the supplementary materials for specific testing methods.

Transcriptomics analysis

Transcriptome sequencing and analysis were performed by Shanghai Ouyi Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer's instructions. RNA purity and quantification were determined using a NanoDrop2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA), and RNA integrity was evaluated using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). RNA quality requirements are as follows: total RNA ≧ 1 μg, OD260/280 values range between 1.8 and 2.2, RIN ≧ 7, total RNA concentration shall not be less than 100 ng/μl. Transcriptome libraries were constructed using the VAHTS Universal V5 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. The libraries were sequenced using the Illumina Novaseq 6000 sequencing platform, generating 150 bp paired-end reads. The fastq format raw reads were processed using the fastp [13] software to remove low-quality reads, and the resulting clean reads were used for subsequent data analysis. The HISAT2 [38] software was used for reference genome alignment, and gene expression levels (FPKM) [62] were calculated, and the reads count of each gene were obtained through HTSeq-count [2]. The DESeq2 [52] software was used to perform differential analysis of gene expression levels, and the false discovery rate (FDR) values corrected by the Benjamini–Hochberg method were extracted. Differentially expressed genes were defined as those that met the criteria of corrected p < 0.05 and |log2FC|> 1. All downstream functional analyses were based on this selected gene set. Subsequently, GO [23] and KEGG [36] Pathway enrichment analyses were performed on the differentially expressed genes based on the hypergeometric distribution algorithm to screen for significantly enriched functional terms.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 29.0), and the data were reported as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Student's t-test was used to compare the differences between two groups; while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and the subsequent Duncan test were used to compare more than two groups. ANOVA employs the Shapiro–Wilk test to verify normality and the Levene’s test to assess homogeneity of variances. A value of P < 0.05 was considered a significant difference. Perform WGCNA analysis using the “WGCNA” package [40], and conduct PLS-DA analysis using the “ropls” package [63]. All transcriptome and metabolome visualizations (Venn diagrams, heatmaps, volcano plots, etc.) were created using R (version 4.4.2). The graphics were drawn using Adobe Illustrator CC 2019.

Metabolomics analysis

Differences in metabolic changes under different treatments

To determine whether the application of different organic materials would lead to the differential accumulation of metabolites, the relevant metabolomics data were processed by the non-targeted metabolomics (LC–MS) method. Firstly, metabolites were classified using a P-value1 as the screening condition, and a total of 5158 metabolites were detected. All metabolites were classified into 11 super classes (Fig. 2a), among which lipids and lipid-like molecules accounted for the largest proportion. Subsequently, a partial least squares-discriminant analysis was performed on the metabolite content (Fig. 2b), and the results showed a significant separation among the samples, indicating that there were significant differences in the metabolites of flue-cured tobacco leaves under different combined application treatments. Through Venn analysis, 797 differentially accumulated metabolites (DEMs) between different treatments were screened out, among which 70 of the same differentially accumulated metabolites were shared among all treatments (Fig. 2c, Table S2). And using P-value < 0.05 and |log2FC|≥ 1 as the screening conditions, 556 DEMs (241 up-regulated and 315 down-regulated), 640 DEMs (311 up-regulated and 329 down-regulated), 762 DEMs (311 up-regulated and 451 down-regulated), and 417 DEMs (240 up-regulated and 177 down-regulated) were detected in the CM, CMSS, CMHC, and CMBF compared with the CK groups, respectively (Fig. 2d,e,f,g). Among them, the CMHC compared with the CK group had a larger number of metabolites compared with other comparison groups. At the subclass level, amino acids and their derivatives, carbohydrates, and flavonoids accounted for 11.69%, 6.83%, and 2.88%, respectively. This study also found that the accumulation of Seselin (up-regulated), Tyrphostin 47 (down-regulated), (2r, 4r)−2-Phenylthiazolidine-4-Carboxylic Acid (down-regulated), and Ralfinamide (down-regulated) among the top 10 up-regulated and down-regulated metabolites with fold change variations changed in each comparison group (Figure S1). In addition, compared with other control groups, the CMBF compared with the CK group had more accumulation of lipids and lipid-like molecules, with 72 lipids and lipid-like molecules up-regulated and 119 down-regulated. Transcriptomics analysis

Different processing leads to differences in gene expression

To reveal whether there are differences in the gene expression of flue-cured tobacco under different combined application treatments, RNA-seq deep sequencing was performed on the collected samples. After deleting the low-quality reads, a total of 99.21G of CleanData was obtained, with the Q30 base distribution ranging from 94.62% to 95.1% (Table S4), and both the total reads and the uniquely mapped reads exceeded 75% (Table S5). The relevant heatmap and PCA analysis of the samples showed that the correlation coefficients of the five treatment groups were all above 0.8 (Figure S2), and the differences in gene expression among different treatments were significant (Figure S3). After setting the screening condition as q-value < 0.05 and using CK as the control for pairwise comparison, it was found that the CM-treated leaves had the fewest differentially expressed genes, with 2093/280 differentially expressed genes being down-regulated and up-regulated, respectively; while the CMHC-treated leaves had the most differentially expressed genes, with 2198/1088 DEGs being down-regulated and up-Regulated, respectively (Fig. 4a). Through comparing the differentially expressed genes among different treatments, it can be found that there are 788 down-regulated genes and 20 up-Regulated genes in all treatments (Fig. 4b). GO enrichment and KEGG pathway analysis

An enrichment analysis was conducted in the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases to further investigate the biological functions of DEGs under different combined application treatments. Through GO enrichment analysis, the biological functions of DEGs are divided into three categories: biological processes (BPs), cellular components (CCs), and molecular functions (MFs). Discussion

The effect of organic material dosing on tobacco growth and glandular trichomes development

As an organic material that can increase the content of nutrient elements such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in the soil, cattle manure plays a key role in promoting the growth and development of plants [17]. In this study, the addition of organic fertilizers treatment can promote the growth and development of tobacco, the increase of glandular trichomes density and glandular trichomes secretions. Among them, the combined application of chemical fertilizers, decomposed cattle manure and high-carbon base fertilizer has a particularly obvious promoting effect on the growth and development of tobacco. This is because high-carbon base fertilizer, as a carbon-based slow-release fertilizer with biochar as the core raw material, can provide a favorable environmental condition for the trichome development and overall growth of acid soil plants by increasing soil pH value, enhancing water retention and nutrient supply, etc. [34].

Mechanisms of transcriptional response of tobacco to organic material dosing

Further exploration of the molecular responses of leaf growth and development to the addition of organic materials through transcriptomic analysis of tobacco leaves. This study found that under the condition of adding cattle manure, plants adapt to the increase of soil organic carbon by regulating the expression of genes related to pathways such as organic acid metabolism, starch and sucrose metabolism cycle metabolism. Research shows that compared with conventional treatment, cover crop residues and cattle manure provide more organic carbon. Therefore, plants grown under organic treatment have significantly more differentially expressed genes (DEGs) involved in the pathways of organic acid metabolism, starch and sucrose metabolism [20]. Wei et al. [78] used transcriptome analysis to reveal the response mechanism of Pinellia ternata after the application of organic fertilizer. The results show that after the application of organic fertilizer, microorganisms contribute more easily absorbed nitrogen to the soil environment. The higher nitrogen content can promote plants to carry out photosynthesis, thereby improving the synthesis and accumulation efficiency of primary metabolites. In the results of this study, the CMSS and CMHC treatments can promote the up-regulation of related genes such as glutamate–oxaloacetate transaminase and tropinone reductase I. Existing studies have shown that AST can catalyze the reaction between glutamic acid and oxaloacetic acid, and plays a key role in amino acid synthesis, nitrogen metabolism, and nitrogen cycling in leaves [16, 64, 74]. This indicates that supplementing fertilizer and cattle manure applications with either sesame cake fertilizer or high-carbon-based fertilizer may influence plant growth rates by regulating the energy metabolism conversion processes within tobacco leaves. In addition, CMSS treatment can promote the expression of genes related to the key enzyme Rubisco involved in the Calvin cycle of photosynthesis [9], and regulate the photorespiration pathway in tobacco by using related enzymes such as (S)−2-hydroxyacid oxidase and formyltetrahydrofolate deformylase [15, 21], thereby improving the photosynthesis of tobacco leaves. Research by Hu et al. [28] also indicates that prolonged fertilisation can delay chlorophyll degradation in rice plants and regulate the expression of genes associated with photosynthesis and ageing. Meanwhile, the CMHC treatment can also promote the expression of cytochrome P450. Cytochrome P450-dependent monooxygenase (P450) is the main enzyme module that generates the diversity of diterpenoid compounds. It can catalyze the oxygenation of various diterpene scaffolds and further functional modifications [89], and has a positive effect on the synthesis of sibirane substances.

Mechanisms of metabolic response of tobacco to organic material dosing

Metabolomics can play an important role in revealing the dynamic changes of the metabolic and physiological states of organisms by comprehensively measuring low-molecular-weight metabolites in biological samples, such as amino acids, lipids, and carbohydrates [54]. This study identified and screened a total of 797 metabolites in tobacco leaves by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry. Some of these metabolites are related to KEGG categories, such as carbohydrate metabolism, amino acid and its derivative metabolism, and biosynthesis of secondary metabolites. Research by Wu et al. [79, 80] also indicates that the application of organic fertilizer accelerates the synthesis of sugars, amino acids, phenolic volatiles and flavonoid compounds in strawberry fruit by reprogramming the regulatory networks of metabolites. In plants, carbohydrate metabolism is an important biochemical process related to traits associated with developmental growth and yield [27]. For example, carbohydrate metabolism mainly maintains the daily activities of plants by influencing the energy supply, signal transduction, and response to abiotic stress in plants [50]. In this study, CM treatment reduced the expression of metabolites such as isocitrate, cis-citrate, and D-gluconic acid, whereas the combined application of chemical fertilisers, composted cattle manure, and other organic materials promoted the expression of metabolites including carbohydrates. CMSS treatment can increase the content of Fructan and Inulin, CMHC treatment promotes the content of D-myo-Inositol, and CMBF treatment can increase the content of Fructan, Melibiitol, 3-Propylmalate, and D-Glucarate. These compounds increase can enhance the energy storage and supply function of tobacco, strengthen plant signal transduction [12, 32, 51], and thereby promote the growth and development of tobacco.

Amino acids can act as intermediates of the final metabolites in some metabolic pathways of plants, and participate in the regulation of various metabolic and other physiological and biochemical pathways, playing an important role in plant growth and development [1, 82]. Homogentisate is an intermediate in the synthesis of aromatic compounds. It can undergo a series of enzymatic reactions to ultimately generate important metabolic products. These compounds play a crucial role in the growth, development, and environmental adaptation of plants [3], such as regulating the metabolism of tyrosine, tryptophan, and phenylalanine. Tyrosine is the precursor of metabolites such as vitamin E, plastoquinone, and suberin, and its presence plays a crucial role in the survival and adaptability of plants [86]. Tryptophan is the precursor of auxin and various secondary metabolites. Its structure is similar to indole-3-acetic acid (IAA), which is crucial for regulating plant growth and development [58, 67]. In this study, the application of cattle manure can promote tryptophan metabolism. In addition, the CMSS treatment can promote the metabolism of alanine, aspartic acid, and glutamic acid, and the CMBF treatment can up-regulate tyrosine metabolism. The role of L-histidine in plants is extensive and crucial, involving multiple aspects such as antioxidation, defense mechanisms, metal ion chelation and transport, as well as growth and development [24, 33]. It mainly plays a role in scavenging hydroxyl radicals and singlet oxygen in plants, and interfering with the redox reaction of metal ions such as iron, thereby protecting cells from oxidative damage [30, 65, 76]. However, histidine content decreased across all treatment groups following cattle manure application. This may be attributed to the manure's rich nutrient content, which provides abundant nitrogen sources for plant growth [14]. Its application alters the plants' nutritional status and metabolic pathways [69]. Under nutrient-sufficient and non-stressful conditions, plants reduce investment in metabolites associated with stress responses [10, 29], potentially downregulating the highly energy-demanding histidine biosynthesis pathway to optimise resource allocation [71].

Response of key metabolic pathways in tobacco to organic material dosing

Joint analysis of metabolomics and transcriptomics is an important analytical method. This study shows that the application of cattle manure and the combined application of cattle manure and organic materials can promote the growth and development of tobacco and the formation of endogenous substances. Nicotine is a secondary metabolite unique to Nicotiana plants [70], which can effectively defend against herbivorous insects and pathogens. Nicotine can inhibit the growth of tobacco hornworm larvae and increase the plant's resistance to a variety of local pests [72]. In plants, the first stage of nicotine synthesis uses S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as a methyl donor, and the diamine putrescine is converted into N-methylputrescine by putrescine N-methyltransferase. Subsequently, it is oxidized under the catalysis of primary amine oxidase [8, 26], and finally 1-Methylpyrrolinium and nicotinic acid ester are used to synthesize nicotine together. In this study, after the application of decomposed cattle manure, the nicotine content was upregulated and the expression of the downstream nicotine N-demethylase was regulated. Meanwhile, the use of decomposed cattle manure can also reduce the content of N-methylnicotinic acid. This may be because the substrates for putrescine and nicotinic acid methylation are both S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) [4]. Therefore, the conversion of S-adenosylmethionine to nicotinic acid is reduced, and the expression of N-methylnicotinic acid is decreased.

Phospholipids (PL) are compounds composed of phosphate and lipids covalently bound. These amphipathic lipids present in the cell membranes of plants and animals are crucial for all cellular organisms [46]. Among them, phosphatidylcholine (PtdCho) is an important component of most membranes. It mainly exists in the outer monolayer of the outer envelope of chloroplasts. In the early stage of phosphate deficiency, the level of phosphatidylcholine will temporarily increase, thereby playing a regulatory role in the plant stress response [11, 35]. Phosphatidylcholine is derived from N-Methylethanolamine phosphate through a series of transformations, and its hydrolyzed products can act as signal molecules to participate in plant signal transduction. Phosphoethanolamine N-methyltransferase catalyzes the methylation of NMEA, allowing the signal to continue to be transmitted downstream, increasing the content of phosphatidylcholine (PtdCho), through the hydrolysis of phosphatidylcholine by phospholipase, the generation of related products such as diacylglycerol (DAG) is increased, thereby participating in the plant's response to external stimuli [37, 39, 88]. This study found that under different fertilization conditions, the DEGs and DEMs involved in the glycerophospholipid pathway in tobacco leaves also changed. Under the CM, CMSS, and CMHC treatments, the ethanolamine phosphate N-methyltransferase was activated, while the activity of ethanolamine phosphate N-methyltransferase in the CMBF treatment did not change significantly. However, the content of N-methyldiethanolamine in the CM, CMSS, and CMBF treatments increased, and the CMHC treatment increased the PtdCho content in tobacco leaves.

Isocitrate is an important metabolite in plants. It is not only a key intermediate in the TCA cycle, which is catalyzed by isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) to generate α-ketoglutarate, providing a basis for the energy metabolism and carbon skeleton of plants [61], but also can be decomposed into succinic acid and glyoxylic acid as a substrate in the glyoxylic acid cycle, playing an important role especially in the process of plant seed germination and fat degradation [60]. In the glyoxylic acid cycle, Isocitrate can be catalyzed by isocitrate lyase (ICL) to generate glycine and serine [87]. Additionally, Isocitrate can also be catalyzed by enzymes such as aconitase hydratase to form aspartic acid, which regulates biosynthesis and energy metabolism through aspartic acid to promote plant growth and development [5, 25, 75]. Zhenguo Ma [55] found that the glyoxylate cycle exists in the barley scutellum. Among the organic acids formed, citrate and malate are the main components. It is indicated that the operation of the glyoxylate cycle in cereal seeds may be not only important for the conversion of fatty acids into carbohydrates, but also for the acidification of the endosperm and the synthesis of amino acids. In this study, CM and CMBF treatments can increase the content of 3-Propylmalate metabolite. In the CMHC treatment, the expression level of 3-Propylmalate metabolite is down-regulated, and aspartate racemase is activated to generate D-aspartic acid. The CMSS treatment significantly increased the content of L-asparaginase. These results indicate that the application of decomposed cattle manure promotes the conversion of Isocitrate into other metabolites in tobacco leaves to accelerate the growth and development process of tobacco leaves.

Based on multi-omics and physiological data, we elucidate the mechanisms by which organic-chemical composite fertilisation (particularly the CMHC treatment) promotes tobacco growth and glandular trichome development. This process commences with the introduction of organic amendments, notably the high-carbon-based fertilisers within CMHC, which enhance soil physicochemical properties and provide sustained nutrient release. The plant perceives this altered rhizosphere environment, thereby triggering transcriptional reprogramming in the leaves. Key upregulated genes include those encoding glutamate–oxaloacetate transaminase (involved in nitrogen and amino acid metabolism) and nicotinaldehyde reductase I (involved in alkaloid synthesis). These transcriptional shifts directly drive transformations in key metabolic pathways, accelerating ‘alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism’ and the tricarboxylic acid cycle to supply carbon skeletons and energy. Concurrently, upregulation of the ‘tropane, piperidine and pyridine alkaloid synthesis’ pathway enhances production of nicotine and related secondary metabolites. Furthermore, regulation of the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway enhances membrane stability and signal transduction. The synergistic effects of these metabolic alterations—optimising primary metabolism for growth while accelerating secondary metabolism for defence—collectively underpin the observed phenotypic improvements: enhanced leaf growth alongside glandular trichome development and secretion. In summary, the co-application of cow manure with other organic materials (particularly the CMHC treatment) acts as an effective inducer, initiating a cascade of signalling from soil to genes to metabolites, ultimately resulting in superior agronomic phenotypes.

Conclusion

The application of cow dung alone or in combination with organic fertilizers can promote the development of glandular hairs and increase the content of glandular hair secretions. Among them, the CMHC treatment has the most significant effect, which helps to improve the plant's adaptability to the environment. The combined application of cow dung and other organic fertilizers can promote basic metabolic pathways such as amino acid metabolism, providing sufficient carbon skeletons and energy for leaf growth and development. At the same time, it can specifically regulate the expression of key genes such as phosphoethanolamine N—methyltransferase and nicotine N—demethylase in the glycerophospholipid metabolism and the synthesis pathways of tropane, piperidine, and pyridine alkaloids, driving the synthesis of resistance substances such as N—methyldiethanolamine and nicotine downstream and enhancing the defense ability of tobacco leaves. In particular, it is revealed for the first time that the CMHC treatment regulates the molecular mechanism of energy metabolism conversion in leaves by promoting the expression of genes such as glutamate—oxaloacetate transaminase and nicotinaldehyde reductase I, thereby synergistically promoting leaf growth and the production of glandular hair secretions. This study was carried out under field conditions, providing new insights into the molecular response mechanism of tobacco growth and development and the combined application of chemical fertilizers and organic fertilizers such as cow dung, which helps to increase the tobacco yield of tobacco farmers. In the future, the molecular response mechanism of the effects of cow dung application on tobacco growth and development under different environmental conditions will be explored.

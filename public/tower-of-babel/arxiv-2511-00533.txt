Uniqueness and stability of normalized ground states for Hartree equation with a harmonic potential
Yi Jiang, Chenglin Wang, Yibin Xiao, Jian Zhang, Shihui Zhu

Abstract
The dynamic properties of normalized ground states for the Hartree equation with a harmonic potential are addressed. The existence of normalized ground state for any prescribed mass is confirmed according to mass-energy constrained variational approach. The uniqueness is shown by the strictly convex properties of the energy functional. Moreover, the orbital stability of every normalized ground state is proven in terms of the Cazenave and Lions’ argument.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the following Hartree equation with a harmonic potential

i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi-|x|^{2}\psi-(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{2})\psi=0,\;\;\;(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1)

where
N\geq 1
is the space dimension, and
I_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Riesz potential defined by

I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}2^{\alpha}|x|^{N-\alpha}}

(2)

0<\alpha<N
and
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. Eq.(
1

[
5
,
6
,
9
,
14
,
24
]
).

From a mathematical point of view, Eq. (
1
) without the term
|x|^{2}\psi
is the standard defocusing Hartree equation, which does not possess any ground state,
but it has a global solution with a scattering property in the natural energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see
[
7
,
16
]
). On the other hand, we recall the focusing Hartree equation with a harmonic potential, which possesses ground states and blow-up solutions. Moreover, the set stability of ground states and instability with blowing
-up of ground states are widely studied (see
[

,

,

,

[

,

,

,
25
,
30
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
). This motivates us to further study the dynamics of ground states for Eq. (

The natural energy space to Eq. (
1
) is defined by

H(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2}|u|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\}.

(3)

For

, we define the mass functional

\mathcal{M}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx,

(4)

and energy functional

\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+|x|^{2}|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{2})|u|^{2}\big)dx.

(5)

Define the mass-energy constrained variational problem

d(m):=\inf_{S}\mathcal{E}(u),

(6)

where
S=\{u\in H(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\;\mathcal{M}(u)=m>0\}
.

If the variational problem (
6
) possesses a positive minimizer
Q
, then

must be a positive solution of the following Euler-Lagrange equation with the Lagrange multiplier
\omega
, which is a nonlinear elliptic equation

-\Delta u+\omega u+|x|^{2}u+(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{2})u=0.

(7)

We call

a ground state of Eq. (
7

\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}Q(x)

(8)

is a soliton solution of (
1

a ground state of (
1
) and

the frequency of soliton. Recently, the normalized solutions of nonlinear elliptic equations are widely studied (see
[

,
19
,
22
,
27
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
).
A solution

of Eq. (

satisfies the prescribed mass constraint
\mathcal{M}(u)=m

) possesses a minimizer

must be a normalized solution of Eq. (
7

is called a normalized ground state of Eq. (
7

There are a plenty of known results for the existence of normalized solutions and normalized ground states (see

In the present paper, we study the convex properties of the energy functional

Theorem 3.1

Let
N\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<N
. Then, for any

\mathcal{M}(u)=m_{0}

v=\sqrt{\frac{m}{m_{0}}}u.

(11)

It follows that
\mathcal{M}(v)=m

It is obvious that

. Let

be a minimizing sequence of (

\mathcal{M}(u_{n})=m,\;\;\;\mathcal{E}(u_{n})\rightarrow d(m),\;\;\;\text{as}\;\;\;n\rightarrow\infty.

(12)

By (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+|x|^{2}|u_{n}|^{2}+|u_{n}|^{2}\big)dx\leq d(m)+c,

which shows that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By the convexity estimate (see
[
7
]

\|\nabla|v|\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\ \ v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(14)

\{|u_{n}|\}
is also bounded in

2.4

\{|u_{n}|\}
(here, we use the same notation

\displaystyle|u_{n}|\rightarrow Q\;\;\;\text{weakly}\;\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;H(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle|u_{n}|\rightarrow Q\;\;\;\text{a.e.}\;\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N},

\displaystyle|u_{n}|\rightarrow Q\;\;\;\text{strongly}\;\;\;\text{in}\;\;\;L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{2})|u_{n}|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|Q|^{2})|Q|^{2}dx\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}\ast(|u_{n}|^{2}+|Q|^{2})(|u_{n}|^{2}-|Q|^{2})dx\Big|

\displaystyle C\Big\||u_{n}|^{2}+|Q|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}}\Big\||u_{n}|^{2}-|Q|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}}}

\displaystyle C\big(\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{4N}{N+\alpha}}}^{3}+\|Q\|_{L^{\frac{4N}{N+\alpha}}}^{3}\big)\|u_{n}-Q\|_{L^{\frac{4N}{N+\alpha}}}.

4
Uniqueness of the normalized ground state

Lemma 4.1

Let
\widetilde{S}
be defined as

\widetilde{S}=\big\{\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\;\rho\geq 0,\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho dx=m>0\big\}.

\widetilde{S}
is convex in

Proof. For any

\rho_{1}\in\widetilde{S},\;\;\;\rho_{2}\in\widetilde{S},

(28)

and for all
0<\lambda<1
, one can check that
\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}dx=m.

(29)

By (
31
) and the definition of
\widetilde{S}
, one deduces that
\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}\in\widetilde{S}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{S}
is a convex set.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\sqrt{\rho})

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\sqrt{\rho})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}+|x|^{2}\rho+\frac{1}{2}(I_{\alpha}\ast\rho)\rho\big)dx.

(30)

It follows that

\displaystyle(\sqrt{\lambda}u_{1})(\sqrt{\lambda}\nabla u_{1})+(\sqrt{1-\lambda}u_{2})(\sqrt{1-\lambda}\nabla u_{2})

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda u_{1}^{2}+(1-\lambda)u_{2}^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+(1-\lambda)|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}\sqrt{\lambda|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+(1-\lambda)|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}}.

Since

\nabla u_{\lambda}\leq\sqrt{\lambda|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}+(1-\lambda)|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}}.

From (

\big|\nabla\sqrt{\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}}\big|^{2}\leq\lambda|\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{1}}|^{2}+(1-\lambda)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{2}}|^{2},

which shows that

\rho

Next we claim that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast\rho)\rho dx
is a convex in
\rho
. In fact, for
0<\lambda<1
one has that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}I_{\alpha}\ast\big(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}\big))[\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}]dx

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lambda\rho_{1}(x)+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}[\lambda\rho_{1}(y)+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}(y)]dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lambda^{2}\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(y)+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{2}(y)+(1-\lambda)^{2}\rho_{2}(x)\rho_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lambda\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(y)+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}(x)\rho_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy+(1-\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\rho_{2}(x)\rho_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast\rho_{1})\rho_{1}dx+(1-\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast\rho_{2})\rho_{2}dx.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{2}\rho dx
is strictly convex in
\rho

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}dx
is strictly convex in
\rho

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast\rho)\rho dx

\rho

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}(\sqrt{\rho})
is strictly convex in
\rho
.

5
Orbital stability of the normalized ground state

Lemma 5.1

Let
N\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<N
. Suppose that the initial data
\psi_{0}(x)\in H(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, the corresponding solution
\psi(t,x)
to Cauchy problem (
1
) and (

Proof. Since the initial
\psi_{0}(x)\in H(\mathbb{R}^{N})

) and (

\begin{array}[]{lll}\vskip 8.5359pt\mathcal{E}(\psi_{0})&=\mathcal{E}(\psi(t))\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(|\nabla\psi(t)|^{2}+|x|^{2}|\psi(t)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi(t)|^{2})|\psi(t)|^{2}\big)dx\\

By combining above estimates with the conservation of mass, there exists a positive constant

such that

\|\nabla\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\||x|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R^{N})}}^{2}\leq C

(45)

holds for all time, which implies that the solution

to Cauchy problem (
1
) and (

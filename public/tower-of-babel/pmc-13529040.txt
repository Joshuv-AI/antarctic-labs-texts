Authors’ response to comment on: ‘Role of axial length in capsular bag diameter in pediatric cataract-regression formulas’
Authors: Sudarshan Khokhar
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We sincerely thank Dr. Pawar and colleagues for their interest in our article and for their thoughtful comments regarding our study titled “The use of 35 MHz ultrasonic biomicroscopy (UBM) to assess the biometric parameters of the anterior segment in pediatric cataractous eyes.[1] Their observations provide an opportunity to further clarify certain aspects of our methodology and terminology.

With regard to the first query concerning the role of axial length (AL), we would like to clarify that AL was indeed evaluated as a candidate variable during the regression analysis. However, it was not included in the final models for sulcus-to-sulcus diameter (STS) and bag diameter (BD) for several reasons. In our cohort, AL measurements were obtained using a contact A-scan technique, which is known to be less reliable compared with optical or immersion methods, particularly in pediatric populations. Given this limitation, we considered that the AL measurements may not provide sufficiently dependable values for incorporation into a regression model intended for clinical application.

Additionally, one of the objectives of our analysis was to explore the relationship between keratometry and UBM-derived sulcus-to-sulcus measurements with capsular bag dimensions independent of AL. This approach was intended to evaluate whether anterior segment imaging with UBM alone, without reliance on A-scan biometry, could provide a practical estimation of capsular bag diameter in pediatric cataractous eyes.

We also observed that AL varied significantly across different age groups and among eyes with varying morphologies of cataract within our dataset. This wide distribution increased variability and limited the strength of correlation between AL and capsular bag diameter in our study population.

We appreciate the authors for highlighting the clinical relevance of AL in estimating capsular bag size. In fact, recognizing the importance of this parameter, we are currently working on a subsequent study involving subgroup analysis across different pediatric age groups, in which regression-based formulas incorporating keratometry, STS, and AL are being developed to estimate capsular bag diameter more comprehensively.

Regarding the second query on terminology, we acknowledge that the term bag diameter (BD) was used in our manuscript to represent the capsular bag diameter (CBD). We apologize for not consistently using the standard terminology and thank the authors for bringing this to our attention. The intended anatomical parameter throughout the manuscript is the capsular bag diameter.

Finally, we regret the inconsistency noted in the description of the study design. The study was conducted as a retrospective observational study, and the reference to a prospective design in the Methods section was a typographical error. We appreciate the authors for identifying this discrepancy, which helps improve clarity and strengthens the interpretation of the study.

We are grateful to the authors for their constructive comments and for contributing to the academic discussion surrounding our work.

Whole-transcriptome analysis reveals key transcriptional and noncoding RNA regulators of skeletal muscle satellite cell differentiation in quail
Authors: Jing Liu, Hongxia Jiang, Xiaoyun Xiao, Zurong Liao, Yuxiang Wang, Zhenxvan Ding, Xuewen Chai, Haodong Liu, Xvwen Huang, Wenhua Wei, Yunong Xie, Luoyang Liu, Zikun Wang, Xiaolong Hu, Sanfeng Liu, Biao Chen, Huirong Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Skeletal muscle growth in poultry depends on the proliferation and differentiation of skeletal muscle satellite cells (SMSCs), yet the regulatory landscape governing these processes in quail remains poorly defined. In this study, primary SMSCs were isolated from embryonic day 15 quail pectoral muscle and validated by PAX7 immunostaining and MYHC immunostaining following induction of differentiation. rRNA–depleted RNA-seq was performed at three developmental stages: satellite cells after differential adhesion (DA), proliferating myoblasts (GM), and differentiated myotubes after 4 d (DM4). Transcriptome profiling identified 9,728 common genes expressed in three group, with 1,254 genes differentially expressed across all pairwise comparisons. Functional enrichment analyses indicated a coordinated shift from cell cycle progression in proliferating cells to muscle contraction, oxidative phosphorylation, and calcium signaling during differentiation. Short time-series expression miner (STEM) analysis revealed distinct temporal expression patterns, highlighting proliferation-associated regulators (e.g., KDR, PIK3R1, MYF5, MYF6, NOTCH1, and WNT2) and myotube-related genes (e.g., ALDH18A1, HOXC8, TBX5, and EN1) as central nodes within stage-specific networks. In addition, 935 lncRNAs and 13,588 circRNAs were detected, many displaying stage-specific expression patterns. Predicted lncRNA–mRNA interactions and circRNA host gene enrichment implicated these noncoding RNAs in muscle development and metabolic remodeling. A competing endogenous RNA network highlighted miR-466-x and novel-m0255-5p as potential post-transcriptional regulators of muscle-related genes, including VEGFA, HDAC4, MYLK4, and NOX4. These findings provide a comprehensive transcriptomic resource for quail myogenesis and identify candidate coding and noncoding regulators relevant to muscle growth in poultry.

Introduction

Quail is an economically important poultry species valued for both egg production and high-quality meat (Wu et al., 2023). Owing to its small body size, rapid growth, and short generation interval, quail has also emerged as an advantageous model organism in developmental biology and genetics (Nakamura et al., 2019). With the advancement of high-throughput sequencing technologies, databases such as FarmGTEx have provided comprehensive genomic and transcriptomic resources for poultry genetic improvement (Guan et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025). Expanding tissue- and cell-specific transcriptomic datasets in quail will further refine functional genome annotation and enhance the utility of these public resources. Skeletal muscle constitutes the largest tissue mass in quail and plays a central role in growth performance and meat production (Xiong et al., 2024). Muscle development depends primarily on the proliferation and differentiation of myogenic precursor cells, particularly skeletal muscle satellite cells (SMSCs) (Mauro, 1961). These cells are indispensable for postnatal muscle growth and regeneration. Despite their importance, the dynamic RNA expression landscape underlying quail SMSC development remains poorly understood.

SMSCs reside between the basal lamina and sarcolemma of muscle fibers and undergo a highly coordinated developmental progression involving quiescence, activation, proliferation, differentiation, and fusion (Moyle et al., 2019). This process is orchestrated by a conserved group of transcription factors known as myogenic regulatory factors (MRFs). Paired box protein 7 (Pax7) serves as a hallmark marker of quiescent satellite cells and is essential for maintaining their stem cell identity (Seale et al., 2000; von Maltzahn et al., 2013). Upon activation, expression of Myogenic factor 5 (Myf5) and MyoD increases markedly, driving entry into the proliferative phase. Subsequent induction of MyoG (myogenin) signals cell cycle withdrawal and initiation of terminal differentiation, ultimately leading to myotube formation (Ng et al., 2021). Multiple signaling pathways coordinate these transitions. The Notch pathway maintains satellite cell quiescence and prevents premature differentiation (Yeh et al., 2023). In contrast, Wnt signaling promotes myogenic differentiation by activating downstream target genes (Yeh et al., 2023).

In recent years, transcriptome-wide studies have highlighted the regulatory complexity contributed by noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) during muscle development (Li et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2025). Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) can function as transcriptional co-regulators or scaffolds for chromatin-modifying complexes, influencing gene expression at the epigenetic level (Pandey et al., 2008). Circular RNAs (circRNAs), characterized by their covalently closed-loop structure, frequently act as molecular sponges that harbor microRNAs (miRNAs), thereby relieving repression of target mRNAs (Liu et al., 2025). MiRNAs themselves fine-tune gene expression post-transcriptionally by promoting mRNA degradation or inhibiting translation. Rather than acting independently, these RNA species interact within competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks, forming interconnected lncRNA/circRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory axes that modulate satellite cell fate decisions (Xing et al., 2023). Although molecular regulatory networks governing satellite cell development have been extensively investigated in chicken and mammals, comparable studies in quail remain limited. This knowledge gap constrains efforts to elucidate muscle growth mechanisms at the molecular level in quail.

To address this limitation, the present study aimed to systematically characterize RNA regulatory networks during quail SMSC development. We first optimized protocols for isolating and culturing embryonic pectoral muscle satellite cells in vitro (Choi et al., 2016). Using rRNA-depleted RNA-seq, we profiled transcriptomes at three critical stages—differential adhesion (DA), proliferation (GM), and differentiation (DM4). Differentially expressed mRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs were identified and subjected to functional enrichment analyses. By integrating previously identified quail miRNA datasets (Zhang et al., 2024), we further predicted and constructed a ceRNA regulatory network associated with satellite cell progression. This work fills a critical gap in the transcriptomic characterization of quail satellite cells and provides a theoretical framework and RNA resource for future studies aimed at genetic improvement, meat quality enhancement, and molecular breeding in quail.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

All animal procedures involving quail were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Jiangxi Agricultural University (Approval No. [2018]30).

Experimental animals

Fertilized eggs were obtained from a Korean quail breeding line maintained at the Poultry Research Institute of Jiangxi Agricultural University (Nanchang, China). Eggs were incubated under standard conditions and harvested at embryonic day 15 (E15) for isolation of SMSCs.

Isolation and culture of quail pectoral muscle satellite cells

SMSCs were isolated from embryonic pectoral muscle following a protocol adapted from chicken satellite cell isolation procedures, with modifications to accommodate quail embryonic tissue characteristics (Choi et al., 2016; Yin et al., 2020). Under sterile conditions, pectoral muscles were dissected from E15 embryos. Skin, feathers and adipose tissue were carefully removed. The muscle tissue was rinsed three times in pre-chilled phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) supplemented with 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco, Waltham, MA). Samples were minced into small fragments using scissors and digested in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco) containing 1 mg/mL collagenase type I (Solarbio, Beijing, China) at 37°C for 40 min. The suspension was gently agitated every 5 min to facilitate tissue dissociation. Following centrifugation at 1,000 rpm for 5 min, the supernatant was discarded and the pellet was further digested with 0.25% trypsin–EDTA (Gibco) at 37°C for 15 min. Enzymatic digestion was terminated by adding complete growth medium containing 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco). The cell suspension was filtered through a 70-μm cell strainer. Fibroblasts were depleted using a differential adhesion method. Cells were plated in culture dishes and incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂ for 40 min. The supernatant containing non-adherent cells was collected and subjected to a second round of differential adhesion under the same conditions. The enriched satellite cells were then transferred to culture flasks. For proliferation, cells were maintained in growth medium consisting of DMEM/F12 supplemented with 15% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco). When cell confluence reached approximately 90–100%, cells were washed twice with PBS and switched to differentiation medium composed of DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin to induce myogenic differentiation. Cells were cultured in differentiation medium for 4 days, and morphological changes associated with myotube formation were monitored and recorded.

Cells were harvested at three stages: immediately after differential adhesion (DA), during proliferation in differentiation medium (GM), and on day 4 of differentiation (DM4). GM samples were harvested at approximately 80% confluence to limit spontaneous differentiation, which occurs readily in quail primary SMSCs at higher densities. Parallel cultures intended for differentiation were allowed to reach 90–100% confluence before the medium was switched to differentiation medium. These cultures were maintained for 4 days to obtain the DM4 samples. Consequently, the initial cell density of the DM4 group was higher than that of the GM group at the time of harvest. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (TaKaRa, Tokyo, Japan) for subsequent rRNA-depleted library construction and sequencing.

Immunofluorescence assay

Immunofluorescence assays were performed on independent cultures grown separately from those harvested for RNA sequencing to confirm cell identity and myogenic potential. SMSCs were seeded in 48-well plates for immunofluorescence analysis. PAX7 staining was performed when cells reached approximately 90% confluence. For MYHC detection, cells were induced to differentiate, and immunostaining was conducted on day 4 of differentiation. Cells were washed twice with PBS for 3 to 5 min per wash and then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Servicebio) at room temperature for 20 min. Following fixation, cells were washed three times with PBS on a horizontal shaker (75 rpm, 3–5 min each). Membrane permeabilization was carried out using 0.5% Triton X-100 (Servicebio) diluted in PBS for 20 min at room temperature, followed by three additional PBS washes (75 rpm, 3–5 min each). Cells were blocked with 10% goat serum diluted in PBS for 1 h at room temperature (65 rpm). Primary antibodies diluted in PBS were then applied and incubated overnight at 4°C (16–24 h) in the dark with gentle shaking (75 rpm). The primary antibodies used were anti- myosin heavy chain (MYHC) (3 μg/mL; MF20, DSHB, Iowa, IA) and anti-PAX7 (1:500; Abmart, Shanghai, China). After incubation, cells were washed three times with PBS (75 rpm, 3–5 min each). A Cy3-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (1:500) was applied and incubated for 2 h at room temperature in the dark (75 rpm), followed by three PBS washes (65 rpm, 3–5 min each). Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (Servicebio) for 15 min at room temperature in the dark and washed three times with PBS (65 rpm, 3–5 min each). Fluorescence images were captured using a TS2R-FL inverted fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

Library preparation and RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from each sample, and ribosomal RNA was removed to enrich for coding and noncoding transcripts. The remaining RNA was purified using SPRI magnetic beads (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA) and fragmented under high temperature. First- and second-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using reverse transcription reagents with fragmented RNA as template. Double-stranded cDNA was subjected to end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation, followed by PCR amplification. Libraries were purified using AHTS DNA Clean Beads and assessed for quality prior to sequencing. Paired-end sequencing (PE150) was conducted on the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus platform (GeneDenovo, Guangzhou, China).

Bioinformatic analyses

Read processing and genome alignment

Raw sequencing reads were processed using fastp to remove adaptor sequences, reads containing excessive ambiguous bases (N), poly-A reads, and low-quality reads, generating clean reads (Chen et al., 2018). Clean reads were aligned to the ribosomal RNA database using Bowtie2 to remove residual rRNA sequences (Langmead and Salzberg, 2012). Unmapped reads were subsequently aligned to the quail reference genome GCA_001577835.2 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/genome/GCF_001577835.2/) using HISAT2 (Kim et al., 2019).

mRNA analysis

Aligned reads were assembled into transcripts using StringTie (Pertea et al., 2015), and gene expression levels were quantified as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) using RSEM (Li and Dewey, 2011). Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed in R to evaluate sample relationships and reproducibility. Differential expression analysis between groups was conducted using DESeq2, with significance thresholds set at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and |log₂FC| > 1. Differential expression analyses were performed using three standardized comparisons with the earlier developmental stage serving as the reference: DA vs. GM (genes differentially expressed in proliferating myoblasts relative to freshly isolated satellite cells), GM vs. DM4 (genes differentially expressed in differentiated myotubes relative to proliferating myoblasts), and DA vs. DM4 (genes differentially expressed in differentiated myotubes relative to freshly isolated satellite cells). All subsequent analyses, interpretations, and visualizations in the Results and Discussion sections follow this reference convention. Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was performed using hypergeometric testing against Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using MSigDB gene sets (Subramanian et al., 2005). Genes were ranked by Signal2Noise metrics, and enrichment scores (ES), nominal P-values, and False discovery rate (FDR) values were calculated using default parameters. For temporal expression pattern analysis, common DEGs identified across DA vs. GM, DA vs. DM4, and GM vs. DM4 comparisons were analyzed using Short Time-series Expression Miner (STEM) with parameters set to -pro 20 and -ratio 1.0 (Ernst and Bar-Joseph, 2006). Genes from significantly enriched expression profiles were subjected to protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/cgi/input?sessionId=yUjUtnnBZGoO&input_page_show_search=on) and visualized in Cytoscape v3.10.4. The preceding analyses, including functional enrichment and trend analyses, as well as subsequent bioinformatic analyses related to lncRNA and circRNA, were conducted utilizing the OmicShare online platform (https://www.omicshare.com/tools/; OmicShare Tools) (Mu et al., 2024).

lncRNA identification and analysis

After transcript assembly with StringTie (Pertea et al., 2015), candidate lncRNAs were screened based on the following criteria: transcript length ≥ 200 bp and exon number ≥ 2. Coding potential was evaluated using CPC, FEELNC and CNCI (Kong et al., 2007; Wucher et al., 2017), and transcripts lacking coding potential were retained as putative lncRNAs. Identified lncRNAs were further classified according to genomic location. Expression levels were quantified as FPKM using RSEM (Li and Dewey, 2011), and PCA was performed to assess sample clustering. Differentially expressed lncRNAs were identified using DESeq2 with thresholds of FDR < 0.05 and |log₂FC| > 1. Comparative analyses between lncRNAs and mRNAs included transcript length distribution, exon number, and open reading frame (ORF) characteristics. To predict potential regulatory mechanisms, Antisense analysis: RNAplex was used to detect complementary interactions between lncRNAs and mRNAs based on minimum free energy calculated by the ViennaRNA package (Tafer and Hofacker, 2008). Cis-acting analysis: Protein-coding genes located within 10 kb upstream or downstream of lncRNAs were identified as potential cis targets. Trans-acting analysis: Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated between lncRNAs and mRNAs. Protein-coding genes with |r| > 0.999 were selected for GO and KEGG enrichment analysis to infer lncRNA function.

circRNA identification and analysis

CircRNAs were identified according to the following criteria: Library type consistent with read1 mapping to the antisense strand; At least two back-spliced junction reads (BSJ reads ≥ 2) detected in at least one sample; Transcript length ≤ 100 kb. Expression levels of circRNAs were normalized as reads per million mapped reads (RPM). PCA was performed in R to assess sample variation. Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 with thresholds of FDR < 0.05 and |log₂FC| > 1. Host genes of differentially expressed circRNAs were subjected to GO and KEGG enrichment analyses using the same procedures described for mRNA analysis.

Construction of the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) Network

Based on a previously established quail miRNA database, target prediction was performed using miRanda (v3.3). To enhance reliability, prediction thresholds were set at a score > 190 and minimum free energy < −10 kcal/mol. Under these criteria, interactions between miRNAs and candidate mRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs were systematically predicted. The competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network was constructed and visualized using Cytoscape v3.10.4.

Results

Isolation and identification of quail skeletal muscle satellite cells

To characterize transcriptional dynamics during quail myogenesis, primary SMSCs were isolated from E15 pectoral muscle using sequential collagenase and trypsin digestion. Cell identity and purity were evaluated by immunofluorescence staining for PAX7, a canonical satellite cell marker. As shown in Fig. 1A, the majority of isolated cells were PAX7-positive, indicating high enrichment of SMSCs in the culture system. To confirm their myogenic potential, cells were cultured in differentiation medium for 4 days. Immunofluorescence staining for MYHC revealed the formation of elongated, multinucleated myotubes (Fig. 1B), demonstrating robust differentiation capacity. These immunofluorescence assays were performed solely for qualitative confirmation of SMSCs identity and myogenic competence; quantitative measurements of proliferation rate or myotube fusion index were not conducted. Based on these observations, cells were collected at three developmental stages for rRNA-depleted RNA sequencing: DA, GM, and DM4 (Fig. 1C). Global gene expression dynamics across developmental stages

Transcriptome profiling was performed to investigate gene expression changes across DA, GM, and DM4 stages. Quality assessment indicated that the majority of mapped reads aligned to exonic regions of the quail genome (Fig. 2A, Table S1), supporting data reliability. PCA demonstrated clear separation among the three stages (Fig. 2B). Replicates within GM and DM4 clustered tightly, whereas DA samples showed slightly greater dispersion but remained distinct from the other groups, indicating stage-specific transcriptional signatures. A total of 9,728 genes were expressed across all samples (Fig. 2C, Table S2). Differential expression analysis identified extensive transcriptional remodeling during satellite cell lineage progression (Fig. 2D, Table S3–5). Genes differentially expressed in proliferating myoblasts relative to freshly isolated satellite cells (DA vs. GM) numbered 4,259 upregulated and 2,859 downregulated. Genes differentially expressed in differentiated myotubes relative to proliferating myoblasts (GM vs. DM4) numbered 2,161 upregulated and 1,816 downregulated. Genes differentially expressed in differentiated myotubes relative to freshly isolated satellite cells (DA vs. DM4) numbered 3,988 upregulated and 2,735 downregulated (Fig. 2D, Table S3–5). The DA vs. DM4 comparison captures the cumulative transcriptional remodeling from the initial isolated satellite cell state through terminal differentiation. Temporal expression pattern analysis during differentiation

To further characterize dynamic expression patterns, the 1,254 shared DEGs were subjected to STEM analysis. Three statistically significant profiles were identified (Fig. 4A, B). Profile 0 genes exhibited a continuous decline from DA to GM to DM4. Genes within Profile 0 cluster were primarily enriched in processes related to cell proliferation, stemness maintenance, and early myogenic development. Central nodes in the network included KDR (Rissanen et al., 2002) and PIK3R1 (Chen et al., 2026), both of which function as key signaling intermediates supporting myoblast proliferative capacity (Fig. 4C, Table S16). The network also contained several canonical myogenic regulators, including MYF5 and MYF6 (Hernández-Hernández et al., 2017), as well as signaling molecules such as NOTCH1 (Gioftsidi et al., 2022) and WNT2 (von Maltzahn et al., 2012). In contrast, profile 7 cluster was enriched for genes involved in myogenic differentiation, myofiber maturation, and metabolic reprogramming. The hub gene ALDH18A1 (Colonna et al., 2023) displayed a high degree of connectivity, indicating a potentially central role in amino acid metabolism and protein biosynthesis during late-stage differentiation. Notably, this network included several transcription factors and developmental regulators, such as HOXC8 (Shashikant and Ruddle, 1996), TBX5 (Hasson et al., 2010) and EN1 (Huettl et al., 2015) (Fig. 4D, Table S17). Differential expression and structural features of lncRNAs

To evaluate the contribution of lncRNAs to quail myogenesis, lncRNA expression profiles were analyzed. PCA demonstrated clear stage-specific clustering (Fig. 5A), indicating dynamic regulation across development. A total of 935 lncRNAs were detected, of which 327 were expressed in all three stages (Fig. 5B, Table S18, 19). Differential expression analysis identified 863, 999, and 576 differentially expressed lncRNAs in DA vs. GM, DA vs. DM4, and GM vs. DM4, respectively (Fig. 5C, Table S20–22). Among these, 129 lncRNAs were differentially expressed in all three comparisons (Fig. 5D, Table S23). Comparative structural analysis revealed that lncRNAs were generally shorter than mRNAs, with most transcripts <1,500 nt (Fig. 5E). Integrated lncRNA–mRNA regulatory networks

To explore potential regulatory interactions, antisense, cis-, and trans-acting relationships between lncRNAs and mRNAs were predicted. Antisense interactions included 1,472 potential pairs; cis-regulatory analysis identified 5,225 pairs; and trans-regulatory analysis yielded 923,494 highly correlated pairs (Fig. 6A–C, Table S24, 25). Functional enrichment of antisense target genes highlighted ECM–receptor interaction and MAPK signaling pathways (Fig. 6D, Table S26). Cis-target genes were enriched in ErbB signaling and ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis pathways (Fig. 6E, Table S27), suggesting roles in proliferation and protein turnover during lineage transition. Trans-associated genes were enriched in oxidative phosphorylation, cardiac muscle contraction, and DNA replication (Fig. 6F, Table S28), supporting involvement in both metabolic remodeling and cell cycle regulation. Identification and functional analysis of circRNAs

A total of 13,588 circRNAs were identified across samples (Fig. 7B, Table S29). PCA showed clear stage-dependent separation, with strong reproducibility in the GM group (Fig. 7A). Among these circRNAs, 2,157 were consistently expressed across all stages (Fig. 7C, Table S30). Most circRNAs were approximately 500 bp in length (Fig. 7D), and the majority were classified as annotated exon-derived circRNAs (Fig. 7E). Differential expression analysis identified 371, 530, and 366 differentially expressed circRNAs in DA vs. GM, DA vs. DM4, and GM vs. DM4 comparisons, respectively (Fig. 7F, Table S31–33). Fourteen circRNAs were differentially expressed in all three comparisons (Fig. 7G, Table 1). Notably, circRNAs derived from the LMO7 and SVIL genes have been well documented to play critical roles in myogenesis. Functional enrichment of host genes revealed involvement in muscle-related processes. In DA vs. GM, host genes were enriched in actomyosin structure organization and muscle structure development (Fig. 8A, Table S34), as well as MAPK and ErbB signaling pathways (Fig. 8B, Table S35). In DA vs. DM4, enrichment included cell differentiation, cell morphogenesis, and the Wnt signaling pathway (Fig. 8C, D, Table S36, 37). In GM vs. DM4, enriched GO BP terms included cell differentiation and movement of cell or subcellular component (Fig. 8E, Table S38), while KEGG analysis highlighted calcium signaling and cell adhesion molecules (Fig. 8F, Table S39), both critical for myotube maturation and fusion. Construction of the ceRNA regulatory network

To elucidate post-transcriptional regulatory interactions during quail SMSC development, an integrated ceRNA network was constructed based on predicted interactions among miRNAs, mRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs identified from the transcriptome data. To further identify central regulatory components within this network, two representative miRNAs—miR-466-x and novel-m0255-5p—were selected for focused visualization and analysis. miR-466-x was positioned at the center of the regulatory network and exhibited extensive interactions with multiple lncRNAs (Fig. S1A, Table S40) and circRNAs (Fig. S1B, Table S41). Among its predicted mRNA targets, several genes with established roles in muscle development and cellular differentiation were identified, including VEGFA (Endo et al., 2023), HDAC4 (Marroncelli et al., 2018), and MYLK4 (Sakakibara et al., 2021). These genes are known to participate in angiogenesis, epigenetic regulation of myogenesis, and cytoskeletal dynamics, respectively, suggesting that miR-466-x may influence skeletal muscle differentiation through coordinated regulation of multiple biological processes. Similarly, the ceRNA networks centered on novel-m0255-5p (Fig. S1C and D) demonstrated a distinct pattern of regulatory specificity. The predicted targets of this miRNA were primarily enriched in pathways associated with cytoskeletal remodeling and intracellular signaling, including NOX4 (Youm et al., 2019), PLA2G10 (Haq et al., 2003), and RHOBTB3. These genes are implicated in redox regulation, lipid metabolism, and vesicle trafficking (primarily based on mammalian studies; functions in birds may differ), indicating that novel-m0255-5p may contribute to structural reorganization and signaling modulation during muscle cell differentiation. This putative “lncRNA/circRNA–miRNA–mRNA” regulatory axis provides a focused set of candidate RNA interactions for subsequent functional validation and offers mechanistic insight into post-transcriptional regulation during quail skeletal muscle development.

Discussion

SMSCs represent a valuable model for investigating the molecular regulation of muscle development and growth in quail (Shin et al., 2015). However, the small size of quail embryos and the limited amount of muscle tissue available have complicated the isolation of high-purity satellite cells. In the present study, we optimized a combined enzymatic digestion protocol using collagenase and trypsin, followed by differential adhesion, to overcome these technical constraints. This strategy enabled the establishment of a stable and highly enriched primary SMSC culture system, providing a reliable platform for downstream transcriptomic analyses. By employing rRNA-depleted RNA sequencing, we systematically profiled mRNA, lncRNA, and circRNA expression and constructed an integrated RNA regulatory network, thereby laying a foundation for dissecting the molecular architecture of quail myogenesis.

Satellite cell differentiation is characterized by extensive transcriptional changes and coordinated pathway activation or repression (Marceca et al., 2020). During the proliferative phase, enrichment analyses consistently highlighted pathways associated with the cell cycle, DNA replication, and MAPK signaling. MAPK signaling is known to promote cyclin expression and facilitate S-phase entry, thereby supporting myoblast expansion (Jones et al., 2005). In parallel, the Notch pathway—an established regulator of satellite cell stemness—remained active, restraining premature differentiation (Conboy and Rando, 2002). Upon induction of differentiation (DM4), the transcriptional landscape shifted toward muscle-specific processes, including myofibril assembly, muscle system development, and calcium signaling. Calcium signaling, beyond its central role in contraction, activates downstream calcium-dependent kinases that promote myotube fusion and maturation (Tu et al., 2016). Concurrent enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation pathways indicates a metabolic transition from glycolysis-dominant proliferation to mitochondrial respiration–dependent differentiation, reflecting the increased energetic demands of myotube formation and contractile function. The close interaction of KDR and PIK3R1 with classical MRFs, including MYF5 and MYF6, highlights the integration of growth factor signaling with transcriptional control. The PI3K/Akt pathway, in which PIK3R1 participates, is widely recognized as a critical regulator of myoblast proliferation and survival (Da et al., 2020). The central position of ALDH18A1 within the Profile 7 cluster suggests that myogenic differentiation is accompanied by substantial metabolic remodeling to meet the energetic and biosynthetic demands of myotube formation (Colonna et al., 2023; Fang et al., 2024). These genes and pathways delineate critical transition points that govern the shift from proliferating myoblasts relative to freshly isolated satellite cells (DA vs. GM) to terminal differentiation in differentiated myotubes relative to proliferating myoblasts (GM vs. DM4). The overall transition from freshly isolated satellite cells to differentiated myotubes is captured in the DA vs. DM4 comparison. It should be noted that the gene functions described here are drawn chiefly from mammalian-based studies, and although analogous roles are often observed in birds, direct functional validation in poultry species is warranted.

Comparative structural analyses revealed clear distinctions between lncRNAs and mRNAs in transcript length and coding potential, reinforcing the noncoding identity of the predicted lncRNAs. Their relatively short length and limited open reading frames suggest regulatory rather than protein-coding functions. Antisense interaction predictions indicated that certain lncRNAs may modulate mRNA stability through complementary base pairing, potentially facilitating rapid downregulation of proliferation-associated transcripts. Cis-regulatory analysis demonstrated strong co-expression between lncRNAs and neighboring protein-coding genes, implying coordinated regulation of muscle-specific gene clusters, particularly those encoding myofibrillar components. Trans-regulatory networks further suggested that select lncRNAs act as regulatory hubs, correlating with genes involved in MAPK and calcium signaling pathways. Such multilayered regulation may enhance the responsiveness of SMSCs to developmental cues and environmental signals.

Among circRNAs, 14 were consistently differentially expressed across all pairwise comparisons, representing core candidates in SMSC development. Notably, two circRNAs derived from the Supervillin (SVIL) gene drew particular attention. SVIL encodes an actin-binding protein implicated in cytoskeletal organization and myogenic differentiation (Oh et al., 2003). CircRNAs originating from this locus may function as molecular sponges, analogous to circSVIL reported in chicken myoblasts (Ouyang et al., 2018a), and also detected in duck embryonic breast muscle (Liu et al., 2023). Additional host genes, including LMO7 and EPB41L3, are associated with cytoskeletal architecture and cell adhesion, underscoring the importance of structural remodeling during differentiation (Ooshio et al., 2004; Yuan et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2022). circLMO7, in particular, has been identified as differentially expressed in chicken and duck skeletal muscle development, pointing to its potential contribution to muscle deposition traits (Ouyang et al., 2018b; Liu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025). Furthermore, circRNAs derived from lipid metabolism–related genes such as FDFT1 suggest a link between membrane biosynthesis and myoblast fusion, consistent with coordinated expression patterns observed in chickens and ducks (Liu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025). These cross-species patterns reinforce the presence of a conserved functional gene network governing skeletal muscle development in poultry, although direct circRNA studies in turkey remain limited.

The ceRNA network constructed in this study highlights a central regulatory role for noncoding RNAs during the differentiation of quail SMSCs. As an important post-transcriptional regulatory mechanism, the ceRNA model proposes that lncRNAs and circRNAs act as molecular decoys that sequester miRNAs, thereby relieving repression of downstream target genes (Tay et al., 2014). This mode of regulation has been widely documented in skeletal muscle development (Zhang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). In our results, miR-466-x and novel-m0255-5p emerged as candidate regulators with notable connectivity and predicted functional relevance. Members of the miR-466 family have been involved in myogenesis (Latimer et al., 2017). One of the predicted targets identified in the present study, HDAC4, functions as a critical checkpoint in muscle differentiation. Deletion of HDAC4 inhibits satellite cell proliferation and differentiation, compromising muscle regeneration (Marroncelli et al., 2018). In addition, VEGFA, another potential target of miR-466-x, functions via receptors such as FLK1 and FLT1, activating pathways related to growth and survival in muscle tissue (Verma et al., 2024). Similarly, NOX4, predicted to be regulated by novel-m0255-5p, plays an important role in myoblast fusion and supporting skeletal muscle regeneration. Fluctuations in NOX4 expression have been linked to changes in satellite cell fusion efficiency (Youm et al., 2019). Our findings suggest that differentiation of quail SMSCs is not governed by a single linear pathway. Rather, it appears to be coordinated by an interconnected network of ncRNAs that collectively fine-tune gene expression during myogenic progression.

Despite these advances, several limitations warrant consideration. First, miRNA–target interactions were predicted using an established quail miRNA database rather than stage-specific miRNA sequencing. Although stringent prediction thresholds were applied to improve confidence, the absence of direct miRNA expression profiling may limit resolution of dynamic regulatory relationships. Second, the present study relied on an in vitro model derived from satellite cells, which cannot fully recapitulate the complex in vivo environment, including neural input, endocrine regulation, and multicellular interactions. Future studies integrating single-cell RNA sequencing and in vivo muscle tissue analyses across developmental stages would provide a more comprehensive view of regulatory dynamics.

Conclusions

In summary, this study establishes a quail SMSC isolation and differentiation system and defines the transcriptional changes accompanying the transition from proliferation to myotube formation. Differentiation was marked by coordinated downregulation of cell cycle genes and activation of muscle structural and metabolic pathways. Key regulators identified through temporal and network analyses—including KDR, PIK3R1, MYF5, MYF6, NOTCH1, WNT2, ALDH18A1, HOXC8, TBX5, and EN1—represent potential control points in stage progression. Dynamic expression of lncRNAs and circRNAs, together with ceRNA network predictions centered on miR-466-x and novel-m0255-5p, suggests multilayered post-transcriptional regulation during quail myogenesis. These results provide candidate targets for functional validation and future genetic improvement of muscle traits in poultry.

Abstract integrodifferential equations and applications
Bruno de Andrade, Marcos Gabriel de Santana

Abstract
. In this work, we study the initial value problem associated with an abstract integro- differential equation in interpolation scales. We prove local-in-time existence, uniqueness, conti- nuation, and a blow-up alternative for regular mild solutions to the problem. Additionally, we apply this theory to the Navier-Stokes equations with hereditary viscosity, taking initial data in the scale of fractional power spaces associated with the Stokes operator. We also explore reaction-diffusion problems with memory, considering the effects of super-linear and gradient-type nonlinearities, and initial data in Lebesgue and Besov spaces, respectively. MSC 2020: 35R09; 35B33; 35B60; 35B65; 35Q35. Keywords: Integrodifferential equations; Critical nonlinearities; Local well-posedness; Continuation and blow-up alternative; Regularity theory; Navier-Stokes with hereditary viscosity; Reaction-diffusion with memory.



1.
Introduction

We start this section describing two mathematical models of evolutionary phenomena that motivated us to develop this work. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and consider the Navier-Stokes equation with hereditary viscosity

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\Delta u(x,s)ds-u\cdot\nabla u-\nabla p+h,\ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\mathrm{div}(u)=0\quad\textrm{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,t)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here,
u:\Omega\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
describes the velocity field of a fluid contained in
\Omega
,
p
is the fluid pressure,
h
is an external force,
u_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity field at the initial time
t=0
, and the kernel
g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
represents a material function. This equation arises in the dynamics of non-Newtonian fluids and also as viscoelastic model for the dynamics of turbulence statistics in Newtonian fluids, see
[
6
]
. We note that
g
plays a central role in the study of (
1.1
) since homogeneous isotropic incompressible linear materials are described utilizing such a material function, see
[
43
, Cap. 5]
. To the best of our knowledge, the mathematical approach of the Navier-Stokes equation with hereditary viscosity was considered for the first time in
[
6
]
, where using a suitable
m
-accretive quantization of the nonlinear term, Barbu and Sritharan prove a local solvability theorem to the problem in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
-setting, and a finite speed of propagation property of the vorticity field. Recently, de Andrade, Silva and Viana
[
17
]
consider the above problem to power-type materials, that is, taking

1\ast g(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(s)ds=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},\quad t>0,

where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function and
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Using complex interpolation and Laplace transform methods, they developed local well-posedness and spatial regularity results to the problem in Lebesgue spaces in the context of a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Particularly, they highlighted how the material function affects the spatial regularity of the mild solutions, namely the solutions have more spatial regularity when
\alpha
is near to
0
. In
[
15
]
, de Andrade, Cuevas and Dantas consider (
1.1
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
ensuring sufficient conditions to the existence and asymptotic stability of mild solutions taking initial data in homogeneous Besov-Morrey spaces. Their analysis is strongly based on a subordination principle with the heat semigroup.

The second motivational problem concerns diffusion phenomena. To problems of heat flow in homogeneous isotropic rigid heat conductors
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
subject to hereditary memory, Gurtin and Pipkin
[
27
]
proposed to replace the Fourier’s constitutive law for the heat flux,

q(x,t)=-c\nabla u(x,t),

which leads to the classical heat equation, by

q(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t-s)\nabla u(x,s)ds.

This model for the constitutive law for the flux has been well accepted in modeling heat conduction phenomena in materials with memory, see
[
11
,
30
,
37
,
40
,
42
]
, and leads us to equations of the form

(1.2)

u_{t}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\Delta u(x,s)\,ds+f(t,u),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty).

The notion of
C_{0}
-semigroups can not be used to describe the solutions of (
1.2
), requiring more care in the treatment of such a problem. Prüss
[
43
]
developed an abstract theory of resolvent families that can be applied to (
1.2
). Using that theory, several researchers consider particular situations of (
1.2
), or similar problems, in the most diverse contexts, see
[

,

,

,

,

,
39
,
48
,
49
]
. With respect to the nonlinear term of (
1.2
), many publications show that there has been considerable interest in heat conduction in materials with or without memory involving a nonlinear term of gradient or super-linear type. Without being exhaustive, we mention
[

From a mathematical point of view, the above problems are very similar in the sense that they can be reformulated as an abstract integrodifferential equation of the form

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u^{\prime}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)Au(s)ds+f(t,u(t)),\quad t\geq 0,\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

where

A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X_{0}\to X_{0}
is a closed, unbounded, and densely defined linear operator on a complex Banach space
X_{0}
such that
-A
is sectorial,
g\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C})
is a non identically zero Laplace transformable function and the nonlinearity
f
is a suitable function defined in an interpolation scale
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\geq 0}
associated with
-A
. For this reason, we will further investigate the correspondent well-posedness and spatial regularity theory to (
1.3
) and apply the abstract results to concrete problems; as examples, we study the Navier-Stokes equation with hereditary viscosity in the
L^{q}
-setting, and the heat equation, derived from the Gurtin-Pipkin law, with a super-linear type nonlinear term in Lebesgue spaces. Additionally, we investigate the heat equation in materials with memory and a gradient type nonlinearity in Besov spaces. To provide a more detailed analysis of our results, we compare them with known results for these equations; moreover, we draw a comparison between our results and previous properties related to some classical problems, this includes the work by Fujita and Kato
[
22
]
on the Navier-Stokes equation, the research by Brezis and Cazenave
[
8
]
concerning the heat equation, the study by Arrieta and Carvalho
[
5
]
on abstract semilinear parabolic problems, and the paper by Ben-Artzi, Souplet and Weissler
[
7
]
on the viscous Hamilton-Jabobi equation.

Going further, as we know, an efficient approach to partial differential equations is closely related to the concept of solutions to be adopted. With this fact in mind, we are particularly interested in the theory of mild solutions to (
1.3
), that is, continuous functions
u:[0,\tau]\to X_{1}
verifying

u(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,u(s))ds,\quad t\in[0,\tau],

where
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is the resolvent family associated with the pair
(A,g)
, see Section
2
for details. Roughly speaking, our approach of (
1.3
) follows the spirit of semilinear parabolic problems within interpolation scales. Indeed, to obtain our results on the integrodifferential problem (
1.3
), we perform the following strategy:

a)

First, we analyze the behavior of
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in abstract interpolation scales associated with the sectorial operator
-A
.

b)

Next, we explore these abstract interpolation scales and their connections with some well-established spaces, such as Lebesgue, Besov, and Bessel potential spaces.

c)

Finally, we examine the nonlinear term
f
present in (
1.3
) within these scales of spaces.

Hence, in the next section, we provide a detailed study of the resolvent family
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Firstly, assuming that
-A
is a sectorial operator, we provide sufficient conditions on the kernel
g
to ensure that
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
admits analytic extension to a suitable sector of the complex plane, see Theorem
2.1
. Also, using sharp interpolations arguments, we analyze the behavior of
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\geq 0}
. Indeed, assuming the existence of a constant
\zeta_{g}>1
such that

1/\widehat{g}(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{\zeta_{g}-1}),\quad\textrm{as }|\lambda|\to\infty,

we prove that for any
\tau>0
and
0\leq\theta\leq\gamma\leq 1
, there exists
M>0
such that

\|S(t)x\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\leq Mt^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma)}\|x\|_{X_{\gamma}},\quad t\in(0,\tau],

see Theorem
2.2
.

This smoothing effect is fundamental in our analysis of (
1.3
). In fact, the usual idea to ensure existence of a mild solution is based on the Banach fixed point theorem and consists of showing that the map

T(\psi)(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\psi(s))ds,\,\,t\in[0,\tau],

is a strict contraction on a suitable complete metric space. If we assume that there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that for each
t>0
the function
f(t,\cdot):X_{1}\to X_{\gamma}
satisfies

(1.4)

\|f(t,x)-f(t,y)\|_{X_{\gamma}}\leq c(r)\|x-y\|_{X_{1}}

for all
x,y\in X_{1}
with
\|x\|_{X_{1}}\leq r
and
\|y\|_{X_{1}}\leq r
, then we eventually get integrals as

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma)}ds,

whose convergence is equivalent to condition
\zeta_{g}<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
. That is, the technique fails when

(1.5)

\gamma\leq 1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}.

This argumentation underscores that
1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
plays a critical role in the study of well-posedness for problem (
1.3
). Consequently, it seems reasonable to divide the analysis of that problem into two parts according to this value.

In Section 3, firstly, we treat case
\gamma>1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
considering initial datum
u_{0}\in X_{1}
and
f(t,\cdot):X_{1}\to X_{\gamma}
a locally Lipschitz function. We ensure the existence of a unique mild solution
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0})\in C([0,\tau],X_{1})
to (
1.3
), which depends continuously on initial data and can be continued to a maximal time of existence
\tau_{max}>0
such that

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\tau_{max}^{-}}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}=+\infty,

if
\tau_{max}<+\infty
. The fractional powers scale has proven to be an effective tool for investigating the issue of regularity. These spaces offer a precise method for measuring spatial regularity of solutions. Utilizing them, we demonstrate that the mild solution of (
1.3
) exhibits an immediate regularization effect. Indeed, for all
\theta\in(0,\gamma-1+1/\zeta_{g})
, we have that

u(\cdot\ ;u_{0})\in C((0,\tau],X_{1+\theta}),

and, if
J\subset X_{1}
is compact, then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\sup_{u_{0}\in J}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}=0.

Given that
X_{1+\theta}\hookrightarrow X_{1}
, it follows that
X_{1+\theta}
is a more regular space than
X_{1}
.

Next, we deal with the complementary situation, which will be called the critical case. As we discussed earlier, assuming only that
f(t,\cdot):X_{1}\to X_{\gamma}
is a locally Lipschitz function, for some
\gamma\leq 1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
, it seems to be impossible to ensure that problem (
1.3
) is well-posed in general. To overcome this situation, we use the notion of
\varepsilon
-regular map introduced by Arrieta and Carvalho
[
5
]
. Basically, taking initial datum
u_{0}\in X_{1}
and assuming the existence of
\varepsilon,\gamma(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)
such that
f(t,\cdot):X_{1+\varepsilon}\to X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}
is well-defined, for each
t>0
, and is a locally Lipschitz function, we obtain local existence, continuous dependence on initial data, continuation, and a blow-up alternative for
\varepsilon
-regular mild solutions of (
1.3
). Also, we prove uniqueness of such solutions in the class of functions with behavior at
t=0
satisfying the condition
1
1

1

Conditions of type (
1.6
) are usual in integrodifferential equations and arise to address the lack of the semigroup property, see e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
.

(1.6)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=0.

Here,
\varepsilon
-regular mild solutions
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0})
are understood as mild solutions that get immediate regularization, in the sense that
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0})\in C((0,\tau];X_{1+\varepsilon})
, see Subsection 3.2 for details. It is important to note that the material function affects the spatial regularity of solutions; specifically, these solutions exhibit greater spatial regularity when
\zeta_{g}
is close to
1
, see Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
. This generalizes the particular situation of the Navier-Stokes equation with hereditary viscosity in the context of power-type materials studied in
[
17
]
. To the best of our knowledge, viewed as a general result, this fact is presented for the first
time in this work.

In Section 4, we apply our abstract results to concrete problems. In such applications, we consider a general scalar kernel of the form

g(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}t^{\alpha_{i}-1}e^{c_{i}t},\quad t>0,

where
k_{i}>0,\alpha_{i}>0,c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.
We mention that many types of materials, as Hookean solids, Maxwell fluids, Poynting-Thompson solids, and power-type materials, are described by this kind of material functions, see
[
43
]
. In the Appendix, we demonstrate that these functions satisfy the hypotheses of our theorems when considering certain specific sectorial operators.

2.
Resolvent families and elements of abstract interpolation theory

We start this section introducing the notion of resolvent family associated with
(A,g)
, which is based on
[
43
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Resolvent family)
.

A family
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of bounded linear operators in
X_{0}
will be called a resolvent family associated with the pair
(A,g)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

S(t)
is strongly continuous on
[0,\infty)
and
S(0)=I
;

•

for each
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathcal{D}(A)
, we have
S(t)x\in\mathcal{D}(A)
and
AS(t)x=S(t)Ax
;

•

the resolvent equation

(2.7)

S(t)x=x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-s}g(t-s-r)drAS(s)xds

holds for all
x\in\mathcal{D}(A)
and
t\geq 0.

We denote by
\Sigma[\omega,\eta]
the closed sector with vertex
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and opening angle
2\eta\in[0,2\pi]
in the complex plane which is symmetric with respect to the real positive axis, that is,

\Sigma[\omega,\eta]:=\{\omega+re^{i\psi};\,r\geq 0,-\eta\leq\psi\leq\eta\}.

Further, if
\eta\in(0,\pi)
, we denote
\Sigma(\omega,\eta):=\mathrm{int}\left(\Sigma[\omega,\eta]\right)
, that is,

\Sigma(\omega,\eta):=\{\omega+re^{i\psi};\,r>0,-\eta<\psi<\eta\}.

Definition 2.2

(Analytic resolvent family)
.

A resolvent family
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is called analytic, if the function
S(\cdot):(0,\infty)\to\mathcal{B}(X_{0})
admits analytic extension to a sector
\Sigma(0,\eta_{0})
for some
0<\eta_{0}\leq\pi/2
. If that is the case, given
\omega_{0}\geq 0
, such family is said to be of analyticity of type
(\omega_{0},\eta_{0})
if for each
\omega>\omega_{0}
and
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
there is
M=M(\omega,\eta)>1
such that

\|S(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq Me^{\omega\Re(z)},\quad z\in\Sigma(0,\eta).

Definition 2.3

(Sectorial operator)
.

Let
B:\mathcal{D}(B)\subset X_{0}\to X_{0}
be a closed and unbounded linear operator with dense domain
\mathcal{D}(B)
and
\psi_{0}\in[0,\pi/2)
. We will say that
B
is sectorial of angle
\psi_{0}
, or simply sectorial, if

•

the spectrum of
B
, denoted by
\sigma(B)
, is contained in
\Sigma[0,\psi_{0}]
;

•

for each
\psi_{1}\in(\psi_{0},\pi)
, there is
C=C(\psi_{1})
such that

\left\|(z-B)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq C/|z|,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma[0,\psi_{1}].

The next result provides sufficient conditions on the kernel
g
for the existence of an analytic resolvent family
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the pair
(A,g)
under the hypothesis of a sectorial operator
-A
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X_{0}\to X_{0}
such that
-A
is a sectorial operator of angle
\psi_{0}\in[0,\pi/2)
and
g\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C})
a non identically zero Laplace transformable function. Suppose, for some
\omega_{0}\geq 0
and
\eta_{0}\in(0,\pi/2]
, the following conditions hold:

(B1)

\widehat{g}(\cdot)
admits meromorphic extension to
\Sigma(\omega_{0},\eta_{0}+\pi/2)
and
\widehat{g}(\lambda)\neq 0
for all
\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega_{0},\eta_{0}+\pi/2)
;

(B2)

For each
\omega_{1}>\omega_{0}
and
\eta_{1}\in(0,\eta_{0})
, there exists
\psi_{1}\in(\psi_{0},\pi/2)
such that

-\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma[0,\psi_{1}],\quad\textrm{for all }\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega_{1},\eta_{1}+\pi/2);

(B3)

For some
\omega>\omega_{0}
and
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
, there is
\zeta_{g}>1
such that

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|\to\infty,\\
\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega,\eta+\pi/2)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\widehat{g}(\lambda)||\lambda|^{\zeta_{g}-1}}<\infty.

Then, there exists the analytic resolvent family
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with the pair
(A,g)
. Furthermore, there is
M\geq 1
such that

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq Me^{\omega t},\quad t\geq 0,

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X_{1})}\leq Me^{\omega t}(1+t^{-\zeta_{g}}),\quad t>0,

and

\|S(t_{1})-S(t_{0})\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq\frac{Me^{\omega(1+\sin(\eta))t_{1}}}{\sin(\eta)}\ln\left(t_{1}/t_{0}\right),\quad 0<t_{0}<t_{1},

where
X_{1}=\mathcal{D}(A)
.

Proof.

For
\omega_{1}>\omega_{0}
and
\eta_{1}\in(0,\eta_{0})
fixed, let
\psi_{1}\in(\psi_{0},\pi/2)
as in (B2). From the sectoriallity of
-A
, there is
C=C(\psi_{1})
such that

\|(z+A)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq C/|z|,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma[0,\psi_{1}],

from whence, for
\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega_{1},\eta_{1}+\pi/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\widehat{g}(\lambda)}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\widehat{g}(\lambda)}-A\right)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq C/|\lambda|\leq\left(\frac{C}{|\lambda-\omega_{1}|}\right)\left(1+\omega_{1}/|\lambda|\right)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{|\lambda-\omega_{1}|},

where
C_{1}=C(1+1/\cos(\eta_{1}))
. Then, from
[
43
, Theorem 2.1]
there exists an analytic resolvent family
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of type
(\omega_{0},\eta_{0})
associated with
(A,g)
. In particular, for the constants
\omega>\omega_{0}
and
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
from (B3), there exists
M=M(\omega,\eta)\geq 1
such that

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq Me^{\omega t},\quad t\geq 0.

As for the spatial regularity, for each
R>0
, let us denote

\varSigma(R)=\{\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega,\eta+\pi/2);\,|\lambda|\leq R\},\quad\varPi(R)=\{\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega,\eta+\pi/2);\,|\lambda|>R\}

and

m(R)=\sup_{\lambda\in\Pi(R)}\dfrac{|\lambda|}{|\widehat{g}(\lambda)||\lambda-\omega|^{\zeta_{g}}}.

Since
|\lambda|/|\lambda-\omega|\approx 1
for
\lambda\in\varPi(R)
and large
R
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}m(R)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\varPi(R)}\dfrac{1}{|\widehat{g}(\lambda)||\lambda|^{\zeta_{g}-1}}.

Then, (B3) can be rewritten as
L:=\lim_{R\to\infty}m(R)<\infty
. Let
R_{0}>0
be large enough so that
m(R_{0})\leq L+1
. Then,

(2.8)

|\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)|\leq(L+1)|\lambda-\omega|^{\zeta_{g}},\quad\textrm{for all }\lambda\in\varPi(R_{0}).

Since, by (B1),
\widehat{g}(\cdot)
is a non-vanishing meromorphic function on
\Sigma(\omega_{0},\eta_{0}+\pi/2)
, the map
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)
is continuous (even analytic) and then

(2.9)

\mu:=\max\left\{|\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)|\,;\,\lambda\in\overline{\varSigma(R_{0})}\right\}<\infty.

From (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have

|\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)|\leq c\left(|\lambda-\omega|^{\zeta_{g}}+1\right),\quad\textrm{for all }\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega,\eta+\pi/2),

where
c=\max\{L+1,\mu\}
. By
[
43
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

\|AS(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq c_{0}e^{\omega t}\left(1+t^{-\zeta_{g}}\right),\quad t>0.

Therefore, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X_{1})}

\displaystyle=\|(I-A)S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}+\|AS(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq Me^{\omega t}+c_{0}e^{\omega t}(1+t^{-\zeta_{g}})

\displaystyle\leq(M+c_{0})e^{\omega t}(1+t^{-\zeta_{g}}).

Adjusting the constants, we can rewrite

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq Me^{\omega t},\quad t\geq 0,

and

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X_{1})}\leq Me^{\omega t}\left(1+t^{-\zeta_{g}}\right),\quad t>0.

As a direct consequence of
[
43
, Corollary 2.1]
, we have

\|S(t_{1})-S(t_{0})\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq\frac{Me^{\omega(1+\sin(\eta))t_{1}}}{\sin(\eta)}\ln\left(t_{1}/t_{0}\right),\quad 0<t_{0}<t_{1},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

For the sake of simplicity, we can rewrite the inequalities obtained in the Theorem
2.1
as

(2.10)

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq M,\quad t\in[0,\tau],

(2.11)

\|S(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0},X_{1})}\leq Mt^{-\zeta_{g}},\quad t\in(0,\tau],

(2.12)

\|S(t_{1})-S(t_{0})\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{0})}\leq M\ln\left(t_{1}/t_{0}\right),\quad 0<t_{0}<t_{1}\leq\tau,

where the new constant
M=M(\tau)
is some suitably large constant according to the value of
\tau>0
.

From now on, we study the behavior of resolvent families on abstract interpolation scales associated with sectorial operators. For this end, let
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\subset X_{0}\to X_{0}
such that
-A
is a sectorial operator and, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{j}
be the space
\mathcal{D}(A^{j})
equipped with the norm
\|x\|_{X_{j}}:=\|(I-A)^{j}x\|_{X_{0}}
, where, for
j\geq 2
,
\mathcal{D}(A^{j})
is the space defined inductively by

\mathcal{D}(A^{j})=\{x\in\mathcal{D}(A^{j-1});Ax\in\mathcal{D}(A^{j-1})\}.

By an inductive argument, we can prove that
X_{j}
is a complex Banach space, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and

...\,\hookrightarrow X_{j+1}\hookrightarrow X_{j}\hookrightarrow\,...\,\hookrightarrow X_{2}\hookrightarrow X_{1}\hookrightarrow X_{0}

densely. Moreover,
-A
, regarded as an operator in
X_{j}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(A^{j+1})
, is a sectorial operator as well. Denote by
\{\mathcal{F}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in(0,1)}
either the real or complex family of interpolation functors, see
[
1
]
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define a scale of intermediate spaces between
(X_{k},X_{k+1})
by

By the reiteration theorems for the real and complex methods, we have the following reiteration property:

\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(X_{k+\beta},X_{k+\gamma})=\mathcal{F}_{(1-\theta)\beta+\theta\gamma}(X_{k},X_{k+1})=X_{k+(1-\theta)\beta+\theta\gamma},

\beta=(1-\theta)\beta_{0}+\theta\beta_{1}

with equivalent norms. Therefore, the following interpolation inequality property holds:

(2.14)

\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{j+\beta},X_{k+\gamma})}\leq c\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{j+\beta_{0}},X_{k+\gamma_{0}})}^{1-\theta}\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{j+\beta_{1}},X_{k+\gamma_{1}})}^{\theta},

for all

Proposition 2.1
.

If
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
T:X_{i}\to X_{j}
is a bounded linear operator such that

Ty\in\mathcal{D}(A)\textrm{ and }ATy=TAy,\quad\textrm{for all }y\in X_{i+1},

then

\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{i+k},X_{j+k})}\leq\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{i},X_{j})},\quad i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Proof.

Indeed, given

, we have

\displaystyle\|Tx\|_{X_{j+k}}

\displaystyle=\|(I-A)^{k}Tx\|_{X_{j}}

\displaystyle=\|T(I-A)^{k}x\|_{X_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{i},X_{j})}\|(I-A)^{k}x\|_{X_{i}}

\displaystyle=\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{i},X_{j})}\|x\|_{X_{i+k}}.

Then,

\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{i+k},X_{j+k})}\leq\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{i},X_{j})}.

∎

The Banach fixed point theorem ensures the existence of a unique
\phi\in\mathcal{K}
such that

\phi(t)=(T\phi)(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(\phi(s))ds,\quad t\in[0,\tau],

that is, the function
u(t;u_{0}):=\phi(t)
is a mild solution of (
1.3
) in
[0,\tau]
. If
v\in C\left([0,\tau];X_{1}\right)
is another mild solution of (
1.3
), then

\displaystyle\|u(t;u_{0})-v(t)\|_{X_{1}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-s)\big(f(u(s;u_{0}))-f(v(s))\big)\right\|_{X_{1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Mc\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma_{0})}\left(\|u(s;u_{0})\|^{\rho-1}_{X_{1}}+\|v(s)\|^{\rho-1}_{X_{1}}+1\right)\|u(s;u_{0})-v(s)\|_{X_{1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq b\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma_{0})}\|u(s;u_{0})-v(s)\|_{X_{1}}ds,

where

b:=Mc\sup_{0\leq s\leq\tau}\left(\|u(s;u_{0})\|^{\rho-1}_{X_{1}}+\|v(s)\|^{\rho-1}_{X_{1}}+1\right)<\infty.

Applying the singular Gronwall inequality, see
[
29
, Lemma 7.1.1, page 188]
, we conclude that
u(t;u_{0})=v(t)
, for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

In order to conclude the proof, we consider the remaining items.

Proof of (A):
Consider
\theta\in(0,\gamma_{0}-1+1/\zeta_{g})
. For all
t\in(0,\tau]
we have

\displaystyle\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\leq\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\theta}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-s)f(u(s;u_{0}))\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Mt^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1}}+Mc\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma_{0})}\left(\|u(s;u_{0})\|^{\rho}_{X_{1}}+1\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq Mt^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1}}+Mc\big((\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1}}+\mu)^{\rho}+1\big)t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma_{0})}{\bf B}(1,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma_{0})).

Therefore,
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0}):(0,\tau]\to X_{1+\theta}
is well defined. A similar argument to that used before proves that
u(\cdot\ ;u_{0})\in C((0,\tau];X_{1+\theta})
. Moreover, from the above estimate, if
t>0
we have that

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle+Mc\big((\|u_{0}\|_{X_{1}}+\mu)^{\rho}+1\big)t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma_{0})}{\bf B}(1,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma_{0})),

which implies that the right side of the above inequality goes to
0
as
t\to 0^{+}
; we conclude the proof of (A) using Proposition
2.2
.

Proof of (B):
If
u_{0},u_{1}\in X_{1}
then

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t,u_{1})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq M\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{X_{1}}

\displaystyle+\Gamma_{\theta}(t)\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t;u_{1})\|_{X_{1}},

where

\Gamma_{\theta}(t)=Mct^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma_{0})}{\bf B}(1,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma_{0}))\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1}}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|u(t;u_{1})\|_{X_{1}}+1\right),

for all
t\in[0,\tau]
. Taking
\theta=0
we have

\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t,u_{1})\|_{X_{1}}\leq M\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{X_{1}}+\frac{1}{4}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t;u_{1}))\|_{X_{1}},

and then

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t;u_{1}))\|_{X_{1}}\leq\frac{4}{3}\ M\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{X_{1}}.

Consequently, for
0\leq\theta\leq\theta_{0}<\gamma_{0}-1+1/\zeta_{g}
it follows that

t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t,u_{1})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\leq M\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{X_{1}}+\frac{4}{3}\ M\Gamma_{\theta}(t)\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{X_{1}},

that is

t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t,u_{1})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\leq L\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{X_{1}},

with

L=M\left(1+\frac{4}{3}\sup\left\{\Gamma_{\theta}(t)\big/0\leq t\leq\tau,\ 0\leq\theta\leq\theta_{0}\right\}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
The critical case

In this subsection, we consider problem (
1.3
) in the critical framework. As we discussed in the introduction of this paper, assuming only that the function
f
present in (
1.3
) is a locally Lipschitz function
f(t,\cdot):X_{1}\to X_{\gamma}
, for some
\gamma\leq 1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
, it seems to be impossible to ensure that (
1.3
) is well-posed in general. To overcome this situation, we use the notion of
\varepsilon
-regular map introduced by Arrieta and Carvalho
[
5
]
.

Particularly, we assume that there exist
\varepsilon,\gamma(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)
such that
f(t,\cdot):X_{1+\varepsilon}\to X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}
is well-defined for each
t>0
, and the function
f(\cdot,x):(0,\infty)\to X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}
is measurable for each
x\in X_{1+\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, there exist constants
c>0
,
\rho>1
and a non-decreasing function
\nu(\cdot)
with
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\nu(t)=0
, such that, for all
x,y\in X_{1+\varepsilon}
and
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(t,x)-f(t,y)\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}\leq c\|x-y\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\|x\|^{\rho-1}_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\|y\|^{\rho-1}_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\nu(t)t^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon)}\right)

and

\|f(t,x)\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}\leq c\left(\|x\|^{\rho}_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\nu(t)t^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right).

Remark 3.3
.

Although we assume that
u_{0}\in X_{1}
, we make no assumptions about the behavior of
f
on
X_{1}
, as dictated by the critical setting.

Before proceeding, let us define the type of solution we are looking for.

Definition 3.5

(
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution)
.

A mild solution
u\in C\left([0,\tau];X_{1}\right)
to (
1.3
) is said to be
\varepsilon
-regular
on
[0,\tau]
if
u\in C\left((0,\tau];X_{1+\varepsilon}\right)
.

The following lemmas will be useful to prove our main result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\tau_{0},\mu>0
be fixed constants and consider the set

\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)=\left\{\phi\in C\left((0,\tau_{0}];X_{1+\varepsilon}\right);\,\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)}:=\sup_{0<t\leq\tau_{0}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\mu\right\}.

Given a sub-interval
(a,b]\subset(0,\tau_{0}]
and functions
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)
, for each
\theta\in[0,\gamma(\varepsilon)-1+1/\zeta_{g})
and all
t\in(a,b]
, we have

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\left\|\int_{a}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,m(\theta,a,b)\left(\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right)

and

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\left\|\int_{a}^{t}S(t-s)\left(f(s,\phi(s))-f(s,\psi(s))\right)ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,m(\theta,a,b)\left(2\mu^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(\tau_{0})\right)\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}},

where
m(\theta,a,b)
is the biggest of the constants

Mc\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}(1-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma(\varepsilon)))

and

Mc\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}\left(\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)),1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma(\varepsilon))\right).

Proof.

First, let us prove (
3.4
). For the sake of simplicity, we denote

\lambda(t)=\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}.

We note that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{a}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\leq\int_{a}^{t}\|S(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)};X_{1+\theta})}\|f(s,\phi(s))\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mc\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\left(\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho}+\nu(s)s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mc\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}\left(s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}\lambda(t)^{\rho}+\nu(t)s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Mc\left(\lambda(t)^{\rho}\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds+\nu(t)\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds\right).

Performing the substitution
s=st
in the last two integrals above, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{a}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq Mc\lambda(t)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}\int_{a/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds

\displaystyle+Mc\nu(t)t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\int_{a/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds

\displaystyle\leq\,Mc\lambda(t)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon\rho)}\int_{a/b}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds

\displaystyle+Mc\nu(t)t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\int_{a/b}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds

\displaystyle=\,Mc\lambda(t)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon\rho)}\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}(1-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}))

\displaystyle+Mc\nu(t)t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}(\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)),1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}))

\displaystyle\leq\,m(\theta,a,b)\left(\lambda(t)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon\rho)}+\nu(t)t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\right).

As for (
3.4
), denote

Q(t)=\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}.

We observe that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{a}^{t}S(t-s)\left(f(s,\phi(s))-f(s,\psi(s))\right)ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\int_{a}^{t}\|S(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)},X_{1+\theta})}\|f(s,\phi(s))-f(s,\psi(s))\|_{X_{{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\,Mc\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\|\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\nu(s)s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon)}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\,Mc\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}Q(t)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(2s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon(\rho-1)}\mu^{\rho-1}+\nu(\tau_{0})s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon)}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\,2McQ(t)\mu^{\rho-1}\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds

\displaystyle+McQ(t)\nu(\tau_{0})\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds.

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{a}^{t}S(t-s)\left(f(s,\phi(s))-f(s,\psi(s))\right)ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2McQ(t)\mu^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}\int_{a/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds

\displaystyle+McQ(t)\nu(\tau_{0})t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\int_{a/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2McQ(t)\mu^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}(1-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}))

\displaystyle+McQ(t)\nu(\tau_{0})t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}(\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)),1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,m(\theta,a,b)\left(2\mu^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(\tau_{0})t^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\right)Q(t).

∎

The next lemma is a direct consequence of

Lemma 3.5

(Singular Grönwall’s Inequality)
.

Let
b\geq 0
,
\alpha<1
,

.

The following is our main result in the critical case.

\displaystyle\,\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}M(t_{0}-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}c\left(\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho}+\nu(s)s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mc\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}(t_{0}-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}\left(\left(s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\mu\right)^{\rho}+\nu(\tau_{0})s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Mc\mu^{\rho}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}(t_{0}-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds+Mc\nu(\tau_{0})\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}(t_{0}-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mc\mu^{\rho}t_{0}^{1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon\rho)}\int_{t_{1}/t_{0}}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho}ds

(3.26)

\displaystyle+Mc\nu(\tau_{0})t_{0}^{-\zeta_{g}\theta}\int_{t_{1}/t_{0}}^{1}(1-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}ds

which also goes to
0
as
|t_{1}-t_{0}|\to 0
. Therefore,

(3.27)

T\phi\in C((0,\tau_{0}];X_{1+\theta}),\quad\textrm{for all }\phi\in{\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)}\textrm{ and }\theta\in[0,\gamma(\varepsilon)-1+1/\zeta_{g}),

and, in particular,

(3.28)

T\phi\in C((0,\tau_{0}];X_{1+\varepsilon}),\quad\textrm{for all }\phi\in{\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)}.

Moreover, for all
t\in(0,\tau_{0}]
, by Lemma
3.4
, (
3.19
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\left(\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\mu^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\nu(\tau_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{4}+\frac{\mu}{4}=\frac{\mu}{2},

while by (

) and (

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq

\displaystyle\,t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(t)(u_{0}-x_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(t)x_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,M\|u_{0}-x_{0}\|_{X_{1}}+\mu/4

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mr+\frac{\mu}{4}=\frac{\mu}{2}.

Then

\|T\phi\|_{\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)}=\sup_{0<t\leq\tau_{0}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|(T\phi)(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}=\mu.

Therefore, (

) and (
3.29
) mean that
T\left({\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)}\right)\subset{\mathcal{K}(\tau_{0},\mu)}
. To conclude the prove that

is a contraction, we observe that, if

, then Lemma

also provides

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|(T\phi)(t)-(T\psi)(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=\,t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\left(f(s,\phi(s))-f(s,\psi(s))\right)ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\left(2\mu^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(\tau_{0})\right)\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}.

We can, therefore, evoke the Banach fixed point theorem which guarantees us the existence of a function

such that

So, let us define

for

and

.

Now, suppose

. By Lemma

,

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\left(\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}-\|S(t)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\right)\leq t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,u(s;u_{0}))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\theta}}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq m(\theta,0,\tau_{0})\left[\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(s;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right].

In particular, if

Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(s;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(s;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\nu(t)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(s;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(s)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\nu(t).

\displaystyle\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sup_{u_{0}\in J}\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(s)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)+\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}m(\varepsilon,0,\tau_{0})\nu(t)

\displaystyle\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left(\sup_{u_{0}\in J}\|S(s)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left(\sup_{u_{0}\in J}\|S(s)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right).

states that the last limit above is equal to

. Then,

Using this information in (

(3.31)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\left(\sup_{u_{0}\in J}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\theta}}\right)=0.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\|S(t)u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{X_{1}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,m(0,0,\tau_{0})\left(\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(s;u_{0})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right).

which, by (

3.3.
Continuation

If
u
and
v
are
\varepsilon
-regular mild solutions for the problem (
1.3
) satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|v(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=0,

then it follows from Theorem
3.5
that
u=v
on some sufficiently small interval
[0,\tau]
. However, we still do not know if a type of "bifurcation" is possible along the time of existence of such solutions, in the sense that,
u
and
v
exist and are distinct beyond
[0,\tau]
. We prove next that the answer to this question is no. Before, we give the following definition.

Definition 3.6

(Continuation)
.

Let
u\in C([0,\tau];X_{1})\cap C((0,\tau];X_{1+\varepsilon})
be a
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution for the problem (
1.3
). Given
\tau_{1}>\tau
, a function
v\in C([0,\tau_{1}];X_{1})\cap C((0,\tau_{1}];X_{1+\varepsilon})
is said to be a
continuation on
[0,\tau_{1}]
of
u
if
\widetilde{u}
is also an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution to (
1.3
) and
\widetilde{u}(t)=u(t)
, for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

Theorem 3.6

(Continuation result)
.

Let
u
be an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution for the problem (
1.3
) on an interval
[0,\tau]
, for some
\tau>0
. If
u
satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=0,

then there exist
\tau_{1}>\tau
and a continuation
\bar{u}\in C([0,\tau_{1}];X_{1})\cap C((0,\tau_{1}];X_{1+\varepsilon})
of
u
that is unique on the interval
[0,\tau^{\prime}]
, for any
\tau^{\prime}\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]
.

Proof.

Let us denote

\eta=\max\left\{\sup_{0<s\leq\tau}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\,,\,\,\,1+(\tau+1)^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right\}.

Furthermore, as we have been doing, for each
\theta\in[0,\gamma(\varepsilon)-1+1/\zeta_{g})
and
0\leq a<b<\infty
, we denote by
m(\theta,a,b)
the biggest of the constants

Mc\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}(1-\zeta_{g}\varepsilon\rho,1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma(\varepsilon)))

and

Mc\textbf{B}_{1-a/b}\left(\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)),1-\zeta_{g}(1+\theta-\gamma(\varepsilon))\right).

We note that
m(\theta,a,b)\to 0
as
a/b\to 1^{-}
. Therefore, we can choose
\tau_{1}\in(\tau,\tau+1]
arbitrarily close of
\tau
such that

m(\varepsilon,\tau,\tau_{1})\left(\eta^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right)\leq\frac{1}{4}

and

m(\varepsilon,\tau,\tau_{1})\left(2\eta^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(\tau_{1})\right)\leq\frac{1}{2},

for
t\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]
. Further, we can show that

t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(S(t-s)-S(\tau-s)\right)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\to 0,

as
t\to\tau^{+}
, just performing the same computations we did in Theorem
3.5
to estimate (
3.23
).
Hence we can also suppose
\tau_{1}\in(\tau,\tau+1]
is taken such that

t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(S(t-s)-S(\tau-s)\right)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{4}

and

t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(t)u_{0}-S(\tau)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

for
t\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]
.

Existence:
Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all functions
\phi\in C((0,\tau_{1}];X_{1+\varepsilon})
such that
\phi(t)=u(t)
, for
t\in(0,\tau]
, and

\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq 1.

For each
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\tau_{1}]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\max\left\{\sup_{t\in(0,\tau]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\,,\,\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sup_{t\in(0,\tau]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\,,\,\,\,\sup_{t\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\tau_{1}^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sup_{t\in(0,\tau]}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\,,\,\,\,1+(\tau+1)^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right\}=\eta.

Thereby,
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{K}(\tau_{1},\eta)
. It is not difficult to verify that
\mathcal{S}
is closed in
\mathcal{K}(\tau_{1},\eta)
, and hence
\mathcal{S}
is complete under the metric

\mathcal{S}
, consider the map
T
given by

(T\phi)(t)=S(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds,\quad t\in(0,\tau_{1}].

Analogously to what we did in Theorem
3.5
, one can prove that

, for all

and any

. In particular, if

. Moreover, for

. Thus, to prove that

, we have

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|(T\phi)(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|S(t)u_{0}-S(\tau)u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle+t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds+\int_{0}^{\tau}S(\tau-s)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{1}{2}+t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(S(t-s)-S(\tau-s)\right)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}S(t-s)f(s,\phi(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+m(\varepsilon,\tau,\tau_{1})\left(\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right)^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+m(\varepsilon,\tau,\tau_{1})\left(\eta^{\rho}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon)+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(t)\right)\leq 1,

from which (
3.33
) follows. Therefore,
T(\mathcal{S})\subset\mathcal{S}
.

Now, if
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}
and
t\in[\tau,\tau_{1}]
, then

\displaystyle t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|(T\phi)(t)-(T\psi)(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}S(t-s)\left(f(s,\phi(s))-f(s,\psi(s))\right)ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\,m(\varepsilon,\tau,\tau_{1})\left(2\eta^{\rho-1}t^{1-\zeta_{g}(1-{\gamma(\varepsilon)}+\rho\varepsilon)}+\nu(\tau_{1})\right)\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{1}{2}\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}.

Consequently

\|T\phi-T\psi\|_{S}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|_{\mathcal{S}}.

Then
T
is a strict contraction on
\mathcal{S}
and, by the Banach fixed point theorem, it has a unique fixed point
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
. The function
\widetilde{u}
defined by
\widetilde{u}(t)=\phi_{0}(t)
, for
t\in(0,\tau_{1}]
, and
\widetilde{u}(0)=u_{0}
, is a continuation of
u
on
[0,\tau_{1}]
.

Uniqueness:

Let us suppose
u
admits another continuation
v
which is defined on some sub-interval
[0,\tau^{\prime}]\subset[0,\tau_{1}]
and let

e:=\sup_{s\in[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]}\left(\|v(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\|\widetilde{u}(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\nu(s)s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right).

For each
t\in[\tau,\tau^{\prime}]
, we have

\displaystyle\|v(t)-\widetilde{u}(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|S(t-s)f\left(s,v(s)\right)-S(t-s)f\left(s,\widetilde{u}(s)\right)\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{t}M(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\varepsilon-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}\|f(s,v(s))-f(s,\widetilde{u}(s))\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mc\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\varepsilon-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}\|v(s)-\widetilde{u}(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\|v(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\|\widetilde{u}(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\nu(s)s^{-1+\zeta_{g}(1-\gamma(\varepsilon))}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Mce\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\zeta_{g}(1+\varepsilon-{\gamma(\varepsilon)})}\|v(s)-\widetilde{u}(s)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}ds.

Hence, the uniqueness follows from Singular Grönwall’s Inequality, Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We note that the result above solves the issue of possible bifurcations. Indeed, let
u
and
v
be
\varepsilon
-regular mild solutions defined on
[0,\tau(u)]
and
[0,\tau(v)]
, respectively, satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|v(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=0.

We claim that
u=v
on
[0,\min\{\tau(u),\tau(v)\}]
. Indeed, suppose not. Then, denoting

I:=\{t\in[0,\min\{\tau(u),\tau(v)\}];\,u=v\textrm{ on }[0,t]\},\quad\tau:=\max(I),

and
w(t):=u(t)=v(t),
for
t\in[0,\tau]
, we have that

0<\tau<\min\{\tau(u),\tau(v)\}

and
w
is an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution on
[0,\tau]
for the problem (
1.3
). From Theorem
3.6
, there is
\tau_{1}>\tau
and a continuation
\bar{w}
on
[0,\tau_{1}]
of
w
. Let

\tau^{\prime}=\min\{\tau_{1},\tau(u),\tau(v)\}.

Since
\tau^{\prime}>\tau
, we have that
\tau^{\prime}\notin I
. On the other hand, by Theorem
3.6
,
\bar{w}
is the unique continuation on
[0,\tau^{\prime}]
of
w
. Then,
u=\bar{w}=v\textrm{ on }[0,\tau^{\prime}]
, whence
\tau^{\prime}\in I
, which is a contradiction.

Remark 3.5
.

In this subsection, we addressed only the continuation of solutions in the critical case. Nevertheless, with some adjustments, a similar result can be developed in the subcritical framework.

3.4.
Blow-up alternative

If
u
is an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution on some interval
[0,\tau_{0}]
to (
1.3
), then, by Theorem
3.6
, the interval

\{\tau>\tau_{0}\,;u\textrm{ admits a continuation on }[0,\tau]\}

is not empty. Hence, the following definition is consistent.

Definition 3.7

(Maximal time of existence)
.

Let
u
be an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution on
[0,\tau_{0}]
to the Problem (
1.3
). The number
\tau_{max}\in(\tau_{0},\infty]
defined by

\tau_{max}:=\sup\{\tau>\tau_{0}\,;u\textrm{ admits a continuation on }[0,\tau]\}

is said to be its maximal time of existence.

The next theorem is our blow-up alternative to (
1.3
).

Theorem 3.7

(Blow-up alternative)
.

Let
u
be the
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution for the problem (
1.3
) that satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=0.

If its maximal time of existence
\tau_{\textrm{max}}>0
is finite, then

\limsup_{t\to\tau_{\textrm{max}}\,^{-}}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}=\infty.

Proof.

Let us suppose by contradiction that
\tau_{\textrm{max}}<\infty
and
\limsup_{t\to\tau_{\textrm{max}}\,^{-}}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}<\infty.

Then,

Q:=\sup_{t\in(0,\tau_{\textrm{max}})}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}<\infty.

Let
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\tau_{\textrm{max}})
be a sequence with
\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=\tau_{\textrm{max}}.
We claim that
\{u(t_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{1+\varepsilon}
is a Cauchy sequence. In fact, let
m,n
in
\mathbb{N}
; without loss of generality, let us suppose
t_{m}<t_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\|u(t_{m})-u(t_{n})\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\leq\|S(t_{m})u_{0}-S(t_{n})u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{m}}S(t_{m}-s)f(s,u(s))ds-\int_{0}^{t_{n}}S(t_{n}-s)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\|S(t_{m})u_{0}-S(t_{n})u_{0}\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\left(S(t_{m}-s)-S(t_{n}-s)\right)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{n}}S(t_{n}-s)f(s,u(s))ds\right\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}.

By Theorem
2.3
, the first summand above goes to
0
as
m,n\to\infty
. Furthermore, doing the same computations as we did in (
3.2
) and (
3.2
), in the proof of Theorem
3.5
, one can show that the second and third summands also go to
0
as
m,n\to\infty
. Therefore,
\{u(t_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{1+\varepsilon}
is indeed a Cauchy sequence.

The arbitrariness of
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
ensures that
\lim_{t\to\tau_{\textrm{max}}\,\!\!^{-}}u(t)
exists in
X_{1+\varepsilon}
, so we can extend
u
on
[0,\tau_{\textrm{max}}]
defining

u(\tau_{\textrm{max}})=\lim_{t\to\tau_{\textrm{max}}\,^{-}}u(t),

which contradicts the Theorem
3.6
.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

As in the previous subsection, we have focused exclusively on blow-up of solutions in the critical case. However, a similar result can be developed in the subcritical setting, as well.

4.
Applications

This section focuses on applying our abstract results to specific problems. Although we will consider these applications in the critical setting, we want to emphasize that our study on the subcritical framework can be applied to the same examples. The prototype of material function we consider has the form

(4.34)

g(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}t^{\alpha_{i}-1}e^{c_{i}t},\quad t>0,

where
k_{i}>0,\alpha_{i}>0,c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.
We mention that many types of materials, as the Hookean solid, the Maxwell fluid, the Poynting-Thompson solid, and the power type materials, are described by material functions of the form (
4.34
), see
[
43
, Chapter 5]
with
a(t)=(1\ast g)(t)
,
t>0
. In the Appendix we prove that this type of material function verifies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
.

We recall the definition of fractional order Sobolev spaces, also called spaces of Bessel potentials, that will be used in the following subsections. Let
\Omega
be a bounded, smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For
l>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
W^{l,p}(\Omega)
the Sobolev space of order
l
. It can be defined via complex interpolation as

W^{l,p}(\Omega):=[L^{p}(\Omega),W^{m,p}(\Omega)]_{l/m},

where
m
is the smallest integer strictly greater than
l
, and
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
denotes the standard Sobolev space of integer order
m
.
We also recall the well known Sobolev embeddings

(4.35)

W^{l_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{l_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega),\qquad\quad\frac{l_{1}}{N}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}\geq\frac{l_{2}}{N}-\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad 1<p_{1}\leq p_{2}<\infty.

We denote by
W^{l,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
the Sobolev spaces of vector-valued functions and, for the sake of simplicity, write
W^{l,p}(\Omega)
in the scalar case.

4.1.
Navier-Stokes equations with hereditary viscosity

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, be a bounded smooth domain and consider the Navier-Stokes system with hereditary viscosity

(4.36)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\Delta u(x,s)\,ds=-\nabla p+h,\quad\textrm{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\mathrm{div}(u)=0\quad\textrm{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,t)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here,
u:\Omega\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
describes the velocity field of a fluid contained in
\Omega
,
p
is the fluid pressure,
h
is an external force,
u_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the velocity field at the initial time
t=0
, and
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
represents a material function. Such models arise in the dynamics of non-Newtonian fluids or as viscoelastic models for the dynamics of turbulence statistics in Newtonian fluids and also is studied in
[
6
,
15
,
17
,
41
]
by different approaches.

It is well known that, for
1<q<\infty
, the following algebraic and topological direct sum decomposition holds:

L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})=L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\oplus\{\nabla\pi\,;\,\pi\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\,\nabla\pi\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

where
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the closure of

\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\,;\,\mathrm{div}(u)=0\}

in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, see
[
23
]
.
This is the so-called Helmholtz decomposition of
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Let
P
denote the projection operator from
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
onto
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Applying
P
to (
4.36
), we obtain

u_{t}+P(u\cdot\nabla)u-\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)P\Delta u(x,s)\,ds=Ph(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty).

Setting
L=-P\Delta
,
u(t)=u(\cdot,t)
, and

f(t,u(t))=-P(u(t)\cdot\nabla u(t))+Ph(\cdot,t)
,
we rewrite (
4.36
) as

u^{\prime}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)Lu(s)\,ds+f(t,u(t)),\quad t>0.

The linear operator
L
is the Stokes operator, and its domain is given by

\mathcal{D}(L)=W^{2,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

On
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the operator
L
is sectorial of angle
0
and satisfies
0\in\rho(L)
; see

[
24
,
34
]
.
Its associated scale of fractional power spaces
\{E^{\alpha}_{q}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
is preserved by the complex family of interpolation functors, that is,

(4.37)

E_{q}^{(1-\theta)\alpha+\theta\beta}=[E^{\alpha}_{q},E_{q}^{\beta}]_{\theta},\quad-\infty<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty,\ \theta\in(0,1),

see
[
25
,
45
]
and
[
3
, Theorem 1.5.4]
, and it satisfies the following duality property:

\left(E^{\alpha}_{q}\right)^{\ast}=E^{-\alpha}_{q^{\prime}},

see
[
3
, Theorem 1.5.12]
, where
q^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate exponent of
q
.
Furthermore, for
\alpha\geq 0
, one has the continuous embedding

E_{q}^{\alpha}\hookrightarrow W^{2\alpha,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

which, by (
4.35
), implies

E^{\alpha}_{q}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad\text{if }\ \frac{2\alpha}{N}-\frac{1}{q}\geq-\frac{1}{r},\quad q\leq r<\infty.

In particular, choosing

(4.38)

r=\frac{Nq}{N-2\alpha q},

we obtain

(4.39)

E_{q}^{\alpha}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\text{for }0\leq\alpha<\frac{N}{2q}.

By duality, one also derives that

L^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow E_{q^{\prime}}^{-\alpha}
.
Since

r^{\prime}=\frac{Nq^{\prime}}{N+2\alpha q^{\prime}},

by replacing
r^{\prime},q^{\prime}
, and
\alpha
with
r,q
, and
-\alpha
, respectively, we obtain

(4.40)

E_{q}^{\alpha}\hookleftarrow L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\text{for }-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\alpha\leq 0,

where
r
is still given by (
4.38
).

We assume that the kernel
g
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
for some
\zeta_{g}>1
and set

X_{0}=E_{q}^{\frac{N-q}{2q}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}}.

Moreover, we denote by
A
the realization of
-L
in
X_{0}
. Then,
\sigma(A)=\sigma(-L)
and

-A\colon X_{1}=E_{q}^{\frac{N+q}{2q}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}}\subset X_{0}\to X_{0}

is a sectorial operator of angle
0
; see
[
3
, Theorem 2.1.3]
.
As before, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
X_{k}
the space
\mathcal{D}(A^{k})
equipped with the norm

\|x\|_{X_{k}}:=\|(I-A)^{k}x\|_{X_{0}}
.
Since
0\in\rho(A)
, one can show that

\|(I-A)^{k}\cdot\|_{X_{0}}\sim\|A^{k}\cdot\|_{X_{0}}
,
from which it follows that

X_{k}=E_{q}^{k+\frac{N-q}{2q}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}}

with equivalent norms.
Then, by (
4.37
), taking
\{\mathcal{F}_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in(0,1)}
as the complex family of interpolation functors, the spaces

X_{k+\theta}:=\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(X_{k},X_{k+1}),\quad\theta\in(0,1),\ k\in\mathbb{N},

satisfy

X_{\alpha}=E_{q}^{\alpha+\frac{N-q}{2q}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}},\quad\alpha\geq 0.

The problem (
4.36
) then takes the abstract form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)Au(s)\,ds+f(t,u(t)),\quad t>0,\\[3.00003pt]
u(0)=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

where
u_{0}\in X_{1}
.

In order to prove the
\varepsilon
-regularity condition for the nonlinear term in (
4.1
), in the next lemma we deal with the convection term in the Navier–Stokes system (
4.36
).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
N\geq 3
and
N/3<q<N
, and suppose that the scalar kernel
g
in problem (
4.1
) satisfies

(4.43)

1<\zeta_{g}<\frac{4q}{N+q}.

Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
satisfying

(4.44)

\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{N}{2q}\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{N+q}{4q},

and with

\gamma(\varepsilon)=2\varepsilon+1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
,
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|P\left(u\cdot\nabla\right)u-P\left(v\cdot\nabla\right)v\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}\leq c\,\|u-v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\|u\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\|v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right),\quad u,v\in X_{1+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We remark that the condition
q>N/3
in the statement implies that the interval in (
4.43
) is nonempty, while the condition
q<N
implies that the interval in (
4.44
) is nonempty. Moreover, the condition
\zeta_{g}<\frac{4q}{N+q}
imposed by (
4.43
) ensures that the interval in (
4.44
) indeed intersects the interval
\varepsilon>0
.

For the sake of simplicity, let us set
\delta=\frac{N-q}{2q}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
, so that
X_{\alpha}=E^{\alpha-\delta}_{q}
for
\alpha\geq 0
. Under the conditions of the statement, we have

0\leq 1+\varepsilon-\delta<\frac{N}{2q}

and

-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\gamma(\varepsilon)-\delta\leq 0,

which, by the embeddings (
4.39
) and (
4.40
), imply that

X_{1+\varepsilon}=E_{q}^{1+\varepsilon-\delta}\hookrightarrow L^{r_{1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}=E_{q}^{\gamma(\varepsilon)-\delta}\hookleftarrow L^{r_{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

with

r_{1}=\frac{Nq}{N-2(1+\varepsilon-\delta)q}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{2}=\frac{Nq}{N-2(\gamma(\varepsilon)-\delta)q}.

Now, let
r_{0}
be the exponent satisfying
1/r_{0}+1/r_{1}=1/r_{2}
, that is,

r_{0}=\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{2}}=\frac{N}{2(1+\varepsilon-\gamma(\varepsilon))}.

Then,

\displaystyle\|P\left(u\cdot\nabla\right)u-P\left(v\cdot\nabla\right)v\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|(u\cdot\nabla)u-(v\cdot\nabla)v\|_{L^{r_{2}}}

\displaystyle=c\|\left((u-v)\cdot\nabla\right)u+\left(v\cdot\nabla\right)(u-v)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|u-v\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\|u\|_{W^{1,r_{0}}}+c\|v\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\|u-v\|_{W^{1,r_{0}}},

The conditions

\frac{2(1+\varepsilon-\delta)}{N}-\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad q\leq r_{0}<\infty

are satisfied and, by the embedding (
4.35
), it follows that

W^{2(1+\varepsilon-\delta),q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,r_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Since

X_{1+\varepsilon}=E_{q}^{1+\varepsilon-\delta}\hookrightarrow W^{2(1+\varepsilon-\delta),q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

we obtain

X_{1+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow W^{1,r_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Therefore,

\|P\left(u\cdot\nabla\right)u-P\left(v\cdot\nabla\right)v\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}\leq c\|u-v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\|u\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}+\|v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\right).

∎

From Lemma
4.6
, it follows that if the map
t\mapsto h(\cdot,t)
in problem (
4.1

Theorem 4.8
.

Moreover, if

Remark 4.7
.

Consider the problem (
4.36
) in power-type materials, that is, taking

satisfying

1\ast g(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},\quad t>0,

where
\alpha\in[0,1)
. In this case, we can take
\zeta_{g}=1+\alpha
, see the Appendix for details. This problem was firstly considered by de Andrade, Viana and Silva in
[
17
]
. We remark that their main results on local well-posedness, Theorems 3 and 4, can be completely recovered by our Theorem

.

Remark 4.8
.

Arrieta and Carvalho prove a similar result of local existence and uniqueness of
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution for the classical Navier-Stokes problem, see
[

. They result can be almost completely recovered by Theorem

if we formally replace

Particularly, our result is compatible with the classical results by Fujita and Kato on the Navier-Stokes equation, see
[

,

]
.

4.2.
Semilinear reaction-diffusion equations with memory

In this section, we study two problems concerning diffusion phenomena governed by equations of the form

u_{t}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\Delta u(x,s)\,ds+f(u(x,t)),\quad x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0.

Such equations arise naturally in problems of heat flow in homogeneous isotropic rigid heat conductors
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
subject to hereditary memory, when the Fourier’s constitutive law for the heat flux,

q(x,t)=-c\nabla u(x,t),

which leads to the classical heat equation, is replaced by

(4.45)

q(x,t)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t-s)\nabla u(x,s)ds,

see, e.g.,
[
11
,
21
]
. The model (
4.45
) for the constitutive law for the flux was proposed by Gurtin and Pipkin in
[
27
]
, and thenceforth, it has been well accepted in modeling heat conduction phenomena in materials with memory, see, e.g.,
[
11
,
30
,
37
,
40
,
42
]
.

4.2.1.
Super-linear nonlinearity and initial data in Lebesgue spaces

In recent years, a lot of research about diffusion equations involving a nonlinearity of type
f(u)=c_{0}|u|^{\rho-1}u
,
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, has been made, see, e.g.,
[
8
,
35
,
46
,
48
,
50
,
51
]
and references therein. In this context, we consider
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
as being a smooth and bounded domain and study the initial value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\Delta u(x,s)\,ds+c_{0}|u|^{\rho-1}u,\quad\textrm{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,t)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega.\\
\end{array}\right.

We are focused on determining the critical exponent for the above equation when
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, with
1<q<\infty
. Let us consider the Laplace operator
L:=\Delta
with domain
\mathcal{D}(L):=W^{2,q}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
. Likewise the Stokes operator, it is well known that
-L:\mathcal{D}(L)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)\to L^{q}(\Omega)
is a sectorial operator of angle
0
, with
0\in\rho(L)
, and it admits a scale of fractional powers spaces
\{F^{q}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
which satisfies

F^{(1-\theta)\alpha+\theta\beta}_{q}=[F^{\alpha}_{q},F^{\beta}_{q}]_{\theta},\quad-\infty<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty,\,\theta\in(0,1),

and

\displaystyle F^{\beta}_{q}\hookrightarrow H^{2\beta}_{q}(\Omega),\quad\beta\geq 0,\quad 1<q<\infty,

see
[
28
, Section 8.3]
and
[
3
, Theorem 1.5.4]
.
Then, as in the previous section, from the embedding (
4.35
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}F^{\beta}_{q}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega),\quad r=\frac{Nq}{N-2\beta q},\quad 0\leq\beta<\frac{N}{2q},\\
F^{0}_{q}=L^{q}(\Omega),\\
F^{\beta}_{q}\hookleftarrow L^{s}(\Omega),\quad s=\frac{Nq}{N-2\beta q},\quad-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

Further, defining
X_{0}=F_{q}^{-1}
and denoting by
A
the realization of
L
in
X_{0}
, we have that
-A
is a sectorial operator with domain
\mathcal{D}(A)=L^{q}(\Omega)\subset X_{0}
and, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the space
X_{k}:=\mathcal{D}(A^{k})
,
\|x\|_{X_{k}}:=\|(I-A)^{k}x\|_{X_{0}},
satisfies
X_{k}:=F^{k-1}_{q}
. We use again the complex family of interpolation functors
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}=[\cdot,\,\cdot]_{\theta}
to obtain

X_{k+\theta}:=\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(X_{k},X_{k+1})=F^{k-1+\theta}_{q},\quad\theta\in(0,1),\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

In this abstract setting, the problem (
4.2.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)Au(s)ds+f(u(t)),\quad t>0,\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

where,
f(w)=c_{0}|w|^{\rho-1}w
for

.

Lemma 4.7
.

If
a,b,\eta>0
, then

\left|a^{\eta}-b^{\eta}\right|\leq\eta|a-b|\left(a^{\eta-1}+b^{\eta-1}\right).

If
\eta>1
, then the same holds with
a,b\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\phi(t)=t^{\eta}
,
t>0
. Without loss of generality, we suppose
a<b
. Then,

\displaystyle|a^{\eta}-b^{\eta}|=\left|\int_{a}^{b}\phi^{\prime}(t)dt\right|=\eta\int_{a}^{b}t^{\eta-1}dt\leq\eta(b-a)\max_{t\in[a,b]}t^{\eta-1}

\displaystyle\leq\eta|a-b|\left(a^{\eta-1}+b^{\eta-1}\right)

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{C}
and
\rho>1
. Then,

(4.54)

\left||x|^{\rho-1}x-|y|^{\rho-1}y\right|\leq\rho|x-y|\left(|x|^{\rho-1}+|y|^{\rho-1}\right).

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\left||x|^{\rho-1}x-|y|^{\rho-1}y\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(x-y)|y|^{\rho-1}+x\left(|x|^{\rho-1}-|y|^{\rho-1}\right)\right|

(4.55)

\displaystyle\leq\left|x-y\right||y|^{\rho-1}+|x|\left||x|^{\rho-1}-|y|^{\rho-1}\right|.

Clearly, (
4.54
) holds if
x=0
or
y=0
. Then, we can suppose
|x|,|y|>0
and, applying Lemma
4.7
, we have

\left||x|^{\rho-1}-|y|^{\rho-1}\right|\leq(\rho-1)\left||x|-|y|\right|\left(|x|^{\rho-2}+|y|^{\rho-2}\right).

Then, by (
4.8
),

\displaystyle\left||x|^{\rho-1}x-|y|^{\rho-1}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y||y|^{\rho-1}+|x|(\rho-1)\left||x|-|y|\right|\left(|x|^{\rho-2}+|y|^{\rho-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|\left(|x|^{\rho-1}+|y|^{\rho-1}\right)+(\rho-1)|x-y|\left(|x|^{\rho-1}+|x||y|^{\rho-2}\right).

Without loss of generality, we suppose
|x|\leq|y|
. Then,
|x||y|^{\rho-2}\leq|y|^{\rho-1}
and so

\displaystyle\left||x|^{\rho-1}-|y|^{\rho-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|\left(|x|^{\rho-1}+|y|^{\rho-1}\right)+(\rho-1)|x-y|\left(|x|^{\rho-1}+|y|^{\rho-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho|x-y|\left(|x|^{\rho-1}+|y|^{\rho-1}\right).

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
1<\rho\leq p<\infty
and
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed, and consider the map

f:u\mapsto c_{0}|u|^{\rho-1}u,\quad u\in L^{p}(\Omega).

For
u,v\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, the following inequality holds:

\left\|f(u)-f(v)\right\|_{L^{p/\rho}}\leq c\|u-v\|_{L^{p}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho-1}\right),

where
c=|c_{0}|\rho
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8
,

\left||u|^{\rho-1}u-|v|^{\rho-1}v\right|\leq\rho|u-v|\left(|u|^{\rho-1}+|v|^{\rho-1}\right).

Then, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that

(4.56)

\displaystyle\left\|f(u)-f(v)\right\|_{L^{p/\rho}}\leq c\|u-v\|_{L^{p}}\left\||u|^{\rho-1}+|v|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{s}},

where
s
is such that

\frac{1}{p/\rho}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s},

that is,
s=\frac{p}{\rho-1}
.
We note that, for all
w\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,

\left\||w|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{s}}=\left(\int_{\Omega}(|w|^{\rho-1})^{s}\right)^{1/s}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}\right)^{\frac{\rho-1}{{p}}}=\|w\|^{\rho-1}_{L^{p}}.

Therefore, from (
4.56
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|f(u)-f(v)\right\|_{L^{p/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|u-v\|_{L^{p}}\left(\left\||u|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{s}}+\left\||v|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=c\|u-v\|_{L^{p}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho-1}\right).

∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
1<\rho<1+\frac{2q}{N}
,
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and suppose that
1<\zeta_{g}\leq\frac{2q}{N(\rho-1)}
. Then, the nonlinearity
f(u)=c_{0}|u|^{\rho-1}u
satisfies

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}\leq c\|u-v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\|u\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}\right),\quad u,v\in X_{1+\varepsilon},

for each
\varepsilon
such that
\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}\right)\right\}<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\frac{1}{\rho\zeta_{g}},\frac{N}{2q}\right\}
,
\gamma(\varepsilon)=\rho\varepsilon+1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}
and some
c>0
.

Proof.

Let us denote
p=\frac{Nq}{N-2\varepsilon q}
. By the embeddings (
4.2.1
),

X_{1+\varepsilon}=F^{\varepsilon}_{q}\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega),\quad L^{\frac{Nq}{N-2(\gamma(\varepsilon)-1)q}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)},

and since

\displaystyle N-2(\gamma(\varepsilon)-1)q

\displaystyle=N-2\rho\varepsilon q+2q/\zeta_{g}

\displaystyle\geq N-2\rho\varepsilon q+N(\rho-1)

\displaystyle=(N-2\varepsilon q)\rho

\displaystyle=(Nq/p)\rho,

we have

\frac{Nq}{N-2(\gamma(\varepsilon)-1)q}\leq p/\rho.

Then,

L^{p/\rho}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Nq}{N-2(\gamma(\varepsilon)-1)q}}(\Omega),

from whence

X_{1+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega),\quad L^{p/\rho}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}

and the proof follows from Lemma
4.9
.
∎

Now, we can apply our abstract results to the problem (
4.2.1
).

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\{F^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
be the fractional power scale associated with the operator
-\Delta
on
L^{q}(\Omega)
with domain

and let

\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}\right)\right\}<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\frac{1}{\rho\zeta_{g}},\frac{N}{2q}\right\}.

Then, for all

, there exist

and
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(w)>0
such that the problem (
4.2.1
) has an

Moreover, the following statements hold.

(A)

For all

(C)

The

.

Remark 4.9
.

The above result shows us that for
1<\rho<1+\frac{2q}{N}
, the condition on

for the well-posedness of (

) is

(4.57)

1<\frac{N\zeta_{g}(\rho-1)}{2}\leq q,

underscoring the influence of the material function in the analysis of the problem. For example, in the context of a Hookean Solid, that is, taking

satisfying

1\ast g(t)=\mu t,\quad t>0,

for some
\mu\neq 0
, we can choose
\zeta_{g}=2
, see the Appendix for details, and then (

) reduces to

. On the other hand, in the context of power-type materials we have

1\ast g(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},\quad t>0,

where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Hence
\zeta_{g}=1+\alpha
, and (
4.57
) takes the form

(4.58)

1<\frac{N(\alpha+1)(\rho-1)}{2}\leq q.

Remark 4.10
.

At this point, it is important to observe that (

) is closely related with the critical case for the nonlinear heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u+|u|^{\rho-1}u,~\mbox{in}~[0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,~\mbox{on}~[0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),~\mbox{in}~\Omega,&\end{array}\right.

when

. Indeed, Brezis and Cazenave
[

]
have proved that the value

plays a critical role to the above problem, see also

, de Andrade et al. show that this same value is the critical exponent for the subdiffusive heat equation

u=0,~\mbox{on}~[0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\

where

is Caputo’s fractional derivative,

, and

. Likewise, Costa et al.

recently proved that (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u+|u|^{\rho-1}u,~\mbox{in}~[0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\

Remark 4.11
.

In the context of power-type materials, the integrodifferential equation (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Delta u(x,s)\,ds+|u|^{\rho-1}u,\quad\textrm{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\

3

to the linear part of the fractional diffusion-wave equation (

4.2.2.
Gradient nonlinearity and initial data in Besov spaces

We close this section of applications studying the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}(x,t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)\Delta u(x,s)\,ds+c_{0}\|\nabla u(x,t)\|^{\rho},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\

\end{array}\right.

with initial data in the Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where

,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. From a mathematical perspective, this equation is a variant of the viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation that incorporates memory effects. It is worth noting that there is already extensive research on diffusion equations with a gradient-type nonlinearity. We recommend the references
[
2
,
9
,
10
,
26

for further reading. However, as far as we know, this work is the first to deal with problem (

Before proceeding, we summarize some useful properties about the Besov spaces as follows:

(a)

For

and

, the operator
(I-\Delta):B^{s+2}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(b)

Let

,

and

. Then the equivalence of norms

\|\cdot\|_{B^{s+m}_{p,q}}\approx\|\cdot\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial x_{j}}\,\cdot\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}

holds.

(c)

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then,

B^{0}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(d)

Let
1\leq p,q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
. Then

B^{s+\delta}_{p,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{s}_{p,q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(e)

Let

,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and

satisfying
s_{0}-{N}/{\widetilde{p}}=s_{1}-{N}/{p}
. Then,

B^{s_{0}}_{\widetilde{p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{s_{1}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(f)

Let
1\leq p,q,q_{0},q_{1}
,
-\infty<s_{0}<s_{1}<\infty
and, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
,
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
. Then

B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left(B^{s_{0}}_{p,q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),B^{s_{1}}_{p,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)_{\theta,q}

with equivalent norms.

(g)

For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
-\Delta
with domain

\mathcal{D}(-\Delta)=B^{s+2}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

has spectrum
\sigma(-\Delta)=[0,\infty)
and is sectorial of angle
0
on
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(h)

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then,

B^{1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{1}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The proof of Items (a)-(g) can be founded in
[
44
]
, while (h) is a consequence of (b) and (c).

Now, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let us consider the operator

on

, with domain

\mathcal{D}(A):=B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X_{0},

and

, with norm given by
\|x\|_{X_{k}}:=\|(I-A)^{k}x\|_{X_{0}}
,

. Then

In this formulation, (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{\prime}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)Au(s)ds+f(u(t)),\quad t>0,\\

We start estimating the behavior of the nonlinearity

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
1<\rho<p<\infty
and
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,

is well-defined and satisfies

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{L^{p/\rho}}\leq c\|u-v\|_{W^{1,p}}\left(\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{W^{1,p}}^{\rho-1}\right),\quad u,v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By Lemma
4.7
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\displaystyle|\|\nabla u(x)\|^{\rho}-\|\nabla v(x)\|^{\rho}|

\displaystyle\leq\rho|\|\nabla u(x)\|-\|\nabla v(x)\||\left(\|\nabla u(x)\|^{\rho-1}+\|\nabla v(x)\|^{\rho-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho\|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\|\left(\|\nabla u(x)\|^{\rho-1}+\|\nabla v(x)\|^{\rho-1}\right).

Then, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{L^{p/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\|\nabla u\|^{\rho-1}+\|\nabla v\|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{\rho-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\|\nabla u\|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{\rho-1}}}+\left\|\|\nabla v\|^{\rho-1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{p}{\rho-1}}}

\displaystyle=c\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho-1}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\|u-v\|_{W^{1,p}}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,p}}^{\rho-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,p}}^{\rho-1}\right).

∎

Next, we apply Lemma
4.11
and suitable Besov embeddings to derive the necessary estimates on
f
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
1<\rho<\min\left\{p,1+\frac{p}{N}\right\}
,
1-\left(\frac{1}{\rho-1}-\frac{N}{p}\right)<s\leq 1
and

X_{\alpha}:=B^{2(\alpha-1)+s}_{p,q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),\quad\alpha\in[0,2].

If
1<\zeta_{g}<2/\chi,
where
\chi(s,p,\rho)=1+\left(1-s+N/p\right)\left(\rho-1\right)
, then the map
f:X_{1+\varepsilon}\to X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}
is well defined and satisfies

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}}\leq c\|u-v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\|u\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{X_{1+\varepsilon}}^{\rho-1}\right),\quad u,v\in X_{1+\varepsilon},

for each
\varepsilon
such that

(4.68)

\frac{1-s}{2}<\varepsilon<\frac{1-s}{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{\chi}{2}\right),

and
\gamma(\varepsilon)=\rho\varepsilon+1-1/\zeta_{g}
.

Proof.

From the first inequality in (
4.68
), we have
2\varepsilon+s>1
. Then,

(4.69)

X_{1+\varepsilon}=B^{2\varepsilon+s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Now, we desire to prove that

(4.70)

L^{\frac{p}{\rho}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}.

By the second inequality in (
4.68
), we get

\displaystyle\gamma(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle<\rho\left(\frac{1-s}{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{\chi}{2}\right)\right)+1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho(1-s)}{2}-\frac{\chi}{2}+1

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho(1-s)}{2}-\frac{1+\left(1-s+N/p\right)\left(\rho-1\right)}{2}+1

(4.71)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2p}<1.

In particular,
\gamma(\varepsilon)
satisfies
\rho\varepsilon+1-\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}\leq\gamma(\varepsilon)<1
. To prove (
4.70
), first, we note that

L^{\widetilde{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\widetilde{p},\infty}\hookrightarrow B^{r}_{\widetilde{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\widetilde{p}=\frac{p}{\rho}
and
r=\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{\widetilde{p}}=-\frac{N(\rho-1)}{p}
. Further, by (
4.12
),

\displaystyle 2\left(\gamma(\varepsilon)-1\right)+s<2\left(-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2p}\right)+s=-\frac{N(\rho-1)}{p}=r.

Then,

B^{r}_{\widetilde{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{2(\gamma(\varepsilon)-1)+s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)},

and, consequently,

L^{\widetilde{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow X_{\gamma(\varepsilon)}.

The statement follows from (
4.69
), (
4.70
), and Lemma
4.11
.
∎

As consequence of our abstract results we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
1<\rho<\min\left\{p,1+\frac{p}{N}\right\}
,
1-\left(\frac{1}{\rho-1}-\frac{N}{p}\right)<s\leq 1
and suppose that
g
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
– for instance, if
g
has the form given in (
4.34
) – with
1<\zeta_{g}<2/\chi,
where
\chi=1+\left(1-s+N/p\right)\left(\rho-1\right)
. Also let
\varepsilon
with

\frac{1-s}{2}<\varepsilon<\frac{1-s}{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{g}}-\frac{\chi}{2}\right).

Then, for all
w\in B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exist
r=r(w)>0
and
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(w)>0
such that the problem (
4.2.2
) has a mild solution
u(\,\cdot\,,u_{0})
defined on
[0,\tau_{0}]
for all
u_{0}
in the closed ball
B_{B^{s}_{p,q}}[w,r].
Moreover, the following statements hold.

(A)

For all
\theta\in(0,\rho\varepsilon)
, we have

u(\,\cdot\,,u_{0})\in C\left((0,\tau_{0}];B^{2\theta+s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)

and, if
J\subset B_{B^{s}_{p,q}}[w,r]
is compact, then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\sup_{u_{0}\in J}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{B^{2\theta+s}_{p,q}}=0.

(B)

For each
\theta\in[0,\rho\varepsilon)
, there exists a constant
L=L(\theta,w)
such that

t^{\zeta_{g}\theta}\|u(t;u_{0})-u(t,u_{1})\|_{B^{2\theta+s}_{p,q}}\leq L\|u_{0}-u_{1}\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}},

for
t\in(0,\tau_{0}],\,u_{0},u_{1}\in B_{B^{s}_{p,q}}[w,r].

(C)

The
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution
u(\cdot;u_{0})
can be continued on an interval
[0,\tau_{\mathrm{max}})
, where
\tau_{\mathrm{max}}\in(\tau_{0},\infty]
. If
\tau_{\mathrm{max}}<\infty
, then

\limsup_{t\to\tau_{\textrm{max}}\,^{-}}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{{}_{B^{2\varepsilon+s}_{p,q}}}=\infty.

Moreover, if
v
is an
\varepsilon
-regular mild solution on some interval
[0,\tau_{1}]
for the problem (
4.2.2
) satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\zeta_{g}\varepsilon}\|v(t)\|_{{}_{B^{2\varepsilon+s}_{p,q}}}=0,

then
\tau_{1}<\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
and
v(t)=u(t;u_{0})
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{1}]
.

Remark 4.12
.

In
[
7
]
, Ben-Artzi, Souplet and Weissler consider the viscous Hamilton-Jabobi equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}(x,t)=\Delta u(x,t)+c_{0}\|\nabla u(x,t)\|^{\rho},\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\end{array}\right.

proving that, among other things, for initial data
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, the value

p_{c}=\frac{N(\rho-1)}{2-\rho}

plays a critical role for the problem, when
\rho<2
. Indeed, the problem is well-posed when
p\geq p_{c}
, and ill-posed if
1\leq p<p_{c}
.

Note that if
s=0
we have

B^{0}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
; this suggest that the spaces
B^{0}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is close to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
.
Furthermore, taking
s=0
in Theorem
4.10
, the condition on
\zeta_{g}
for the well-posedness of (
4.2.2
) becomes
1<\zeta_{g}<2/\chi,
where
\chi=1+\left(1+N/p\right)\left(\rho-1\right)
, that is,

p>\frac{N\zeta_{g}(\rho-1)}{2-\rho\zeta_{g}},

highlighting the influence of the material function in the analysis of the problem. We recover
p_{c}
if we formally take
\zeta_{g}=1
.

As the problems presented in Section
4
, many other can be formulated in the abstract format

u^{\prime}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(t-s)Au(s)ds+f(t,u(t)),\quad t>0,\quad u(0)=u_{0}.

A rich source of them is the continuum mechanics for materials with memory, i.e., the theory of viscoelastic materials. In such problems, the scalar kernel
g
, also called material function, is associated with the physical properties
4
4

4

More precisely, to the shear modulus.
of the type of material under consideration. With these facts in mind, the prototype of material function we consider has the form (
4.34
), that is,

g(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}t^{\alpha_{i}-1}e^{c_{i}t},\quad t>0,

where
k_{i}>0,\alpha_{i}>0,c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.
We mention that many types of materials, as the Hookean solid, the Maxwell fluid, the Poynting-Thompson solid, and the power type materials, are described by this kind of material functions, see
[
43
, Chapter 5]
with
a(t)=(1\ast g)(t)
,
t>0
.

In order to apply Theorem
2.1
, such a function
g
must satisfies the following conditions:

(B1)

g\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C})
is a non identically zero Laplace transformable function;

(B2)

\widehat{g}(\cdot)
admits meromorphic extension to some sector
\Sigma(\omega_{0},\eta_{0}+\pi/2)
with
\omega_{0}\geq 0
and
\eta_{0}\in(0,\pi/2]
, and
\widehat{g}(\lambda)\neq 0
for all
\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega_{0},\eta_{0}+\pi/2)
;

(B3)

for each
\omega_{1}>\omega_{0}
and
\eta_{1}\in(0,\eta_{0})
, there exists
\psi_{1}\in(\psi_{0},\pi/2)
such that

-\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma[0,\psi_{1}],\quad\textrm{for all }\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega_{1},\eta_{1}+\pi/2);

(B4)

for some
\omega>\omega_{0}
and
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
, there is
\zeta_{g}>1
such that

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|\to\infty,\\
\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega,\eta+\pi/2)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\widehat{g}(\lambda)||\lambda|^{\zeta_{g}-1}}<\infty.

Note that if
g
is as before, with
k_{i}>0
,
\alpha_{i}>0
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\widehat{g}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}{(\lambda-c_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the gamma function. We claim that, if

\alpha:=\max_{i}\{\alpha_{i}\}\leq 1-\frac{\psi_{0}}{\pi/2},

then (B1)-(B4) are satisfied with

\eta_{0}:=\frac{(1-\alpha)\frac{\pi}{2}-\psi_{0}}{(1+\alpha)},\quad\omega_{0}:=\max\{0,c_{1},...,c_{n}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\zeta_{g}:=1+\min_{i}\{\alpha_{i}\}.

Indeed, to prove that, for
\omega_{1}>\omega_{0}
and
\eta_{1}\in(0,\eta_{0})
, let
\psi_{1}
be defined by the equality

\eta_{1}=\frac{(1-\alpha)\frac{\pi}{2}-\psi_{1}}{(1+\alpha)},

that is

\psi_{1}:=\pi-(\alpha+1)(\eta_{1}+\pi/2).

Then,
\psi_{1}\in(\psi_{0},\pi/2)
and for each
\lambda\in\Sigma(\omega_{1},\eta_{1}+\pi/2)
and
i=1,...,n
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda-c_{i}\in\Sigma(\omega_{1}-c_{i},\eta_{1}+\pi/2)\subset\Sigma(0,\eta_{1}+\pi/2)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda-c_{i}}\in\Sigma(0,\eta_{1}+\pi/2)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\lambda-c_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}}\in\Sigma(0,\alpha_{i}(\eta_{1}+\pi/2))\subset\Sigma(0,\alpha(\eta_{1}+\pi/2))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\frac{k_{i}\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}{(\lambda-c_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}}\in\Sigma(0,\alpha(\eta_{1}+\pi/2)).

Hence,

\displaystyle\widehat{g}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{k_{i}\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}{(\lambda-c_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}}\in\Sigma(0,\alpha(\eta_{1}+\pi/2))\in\Sigma(0,\alpha(\eta_{1}+\pi/2))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle 1/\widehat{g}(\lambda)\in\Sigma(0,\alpha(\eta_{1}+\pi/2))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\lambda(1/\widehat{g}(\lambda))\in\Sigma(0,(\alpha+1)(\eta_{1}+\pi/2))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle-\lambda/\widehat{g}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma[0,\pi-(\alpha+1)(\eta_{1}+\pi/2)]=\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma[0,\psi_{1}],

what checks (B3). Finally, let
\zeta_{g}=1+\min_{i}\{\alpha_{i}\}
and let
\{i_{1}<...<i_{r}\}
be the set of all indexes
i
such that
\alpha_{i}
reaches the minimum between
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}
. Then,
\alpha_{i_{1}}=...=\alpha_{i_{r}}=\zeta_{g}-1
and
\alpha_{i}>\zeta_{g}-1
, for
i\notin\{i_{1}<...<i_{r}\}
, from whence

\displaystyle|\widehat{g}(\lambda)||\lambda|^{\zeta_{g}-1}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\Gamma(\alpha_{i})\frac{\lambda^{\zeta_{g}-1}}{(\lambda-c_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}}\right|\to\sum_{s=1}^{r}k_{i_{s}}\Gamma(\alpha_{i_{s}})>0,\quad\textrm{as }|\lambda|\to\infty,

and (B4) holds.

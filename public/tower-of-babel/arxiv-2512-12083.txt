RePack: Representation Packing of Vision Foundation Model Features Enhances Diffusion Transformer
Guanfang Dong, Luke Schultz, Negar Hassanpour, Chao Gao

Abstract
The superior representation capability of pre-trained vision foundation models (VFMs) has been harnessed for enhancing latent diffusion models (LDMs). These approaches inject the rich semantics from high-dimensional VFM representations (e.g., DINOv3) into LDMs at different phases, resulting in accelerated learning and better generation performance. However, the high-dimensionality of VFM representations may also lead to Information Overload, particularly when the VFM features exceed the size of the original image for decoding. To address this issue while preserving the utility of VFM features, we propose RePack (Representation Packing), a simple yet effective framework for improving Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). RePack transforms the VFM representation into a more compact, decoder-friendly representation by projecting onto low-dimensional manifolds. We find that RePack can effectively filter out non-semantic noise while preserving the core structural information needed for high-fidelity reconstruction. Experimental results show that RePack significantly accelerates DiT convergence and outperforms recent methods that directly inject raw VFM features into the decoder for image reconstruction. On DiT-XL/2, RePack achieves an FID of 3.66 in only 64 epochs, which is 35% faster than the state-of-the-art method. This demonstrates that RePack successfully extracts the core semantics of VFM representations while bypassing their high-dimensionality side effects.

Introduction

The landscape of Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) has evolved significantly since the success of Stable Diffusion
(
11
)
.
While early models relied on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)
(
6
)
to compress images into a latent space, recent methods
(
17
;
19
;
13
)
directly leverage powerful Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), such as CLIP
(
10
)
and DINOv2/V3
(
7
;
14
)
, as feature encoders.
Attempts to integrate VFMs can be categorized into two streams.
The first stream, represented by methods like VA-VAE
(
17
)
, retains the standard VAE architecture but introduces a projection head to align the generative latent space with the discriminative space of VFMs during training.
While effective, this approach still requires training an encoder from scratch and relies on complex loss balancing.
The second, such as RAE
(
19
)
and SVG
(
13
)
, directly adopts the high-dimensional features extracted by frozen VFMs as the latent representation.
These methods assume that richer representations lead to better generation performance.
Consequently, the practice of feeding these raw, high-density features directly into the Diffusion Transformer (DiT)
(
8
)
has become popular.

Although the aforementioned methods have achieved remarkable results, we argue that directly utilizing raw Vision Foundation Model (VFM) features overlooks the fundamental purpose of an encoder:
information compression
.
Let us consider a standard ViT encoder, such as DINOv3-B/16. The extracted feature map typically has a shape of
(768,H/16,W/16)
.
We observe that the total number of numerical elements in the extracted features is identical to that of the raw image (
768/16^{2}=3
, matching the 3 RGB channels).
As illustrated in Figure
1
, this means that the VFM, despite achieving semantic enrichment, results in
zero effective data compression
.

Simultaneously, this observation contradicts the Manifold Assumption
(
12
;
9
)
.
The Manifold Assumption states that meaningful semantics lie on a lower-dimensional manifold embedded within the high-dimensional observation space.
While DINOv3 features are semantically rich, they are distributed in a vast and sparse space.
We term this phenomenon
Information Overload
.
Training the Decoder and DiT in this uncompressed, high-dimensional space will push the generative model to fit the redundancy rather than the core semantic structure.

In this paper, we introduce
RePack
(
Re
presentation
Pack
ing).
Instead of retraining a complex encoder, RePack utilizes a lightweight projection layer to pack the high-density VFM features into a compact, semantic-rich manifold before feeding them into the DiT.
This packing mechanism acts as an information bottleneck
(
16
;
1
)
, forcing the model to distill only the most essential semantic geometry.
For methods that directly use the VFM as an encoder, RePack makes them refocus on learning the core semantic manifold.

We validate RePack on ImageNet using DINOv3, packing the features into a 32-dimensional subspace.
Experimental results demonstrate that RePack significantly accelerates the training convergence of DiT.
On DiT-XL/2, RePack reaches an FID-2k of 34.23 in just 16 epochs (surpassing VA-VAE’s 56.87) and achieves a remarkable final FID-50k of 3.66 (vs. 5.63).
Meanwhile, since RePack utilizes a pre-trained model without requiring complex loss balancing, it is easier to train than methods like VA-VAE.
In terms of reconstruction, RePack outperforms methods that directly use raw VFM features (e.g., RAE).
Additionally, RePack exhibits emergent super-resolution capabilities, supporting our hypothesis that packed projections capture robust semantic structures.

Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We propose RePack.
As a lightweight framework, RePack tames high-dimensional features through a dense projection.
This allows the encoded results from VFMs to be packed through a semantic bottleneck, filtering noise and retaining the essential structure.
Thus, Information Overload is alleviated.

•

RePack is not only easy to train, but its packed representation also significantly accelerates the fitting speed of DiT and improves generation fidelity.
Furthermore, RePack exhibits emergent super-resolution capabilities, further confirming RePack’s effectiveness.

2
Methodology

2.1
Background: VFM Features as Latent Space

Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) decouple the generative process into two stages: perceptual compression and generative modeling.
We consider an image
x\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
.
In standard LDMs
(
11
)
, a VAE encoder compresses
x
into a low-dimensional latent variable
z\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times w\times c}
, where the channel dimension
c
typically ranges from 4 to 64.

Recent trends
(
19
;
13
)
propose replacing the trained VAE Encoder with frozen Vision Foundation Models (VFMs).
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
denote a frozen VFM encoder.
The extracted feature
z_{raw}
is given by:

z_{raw}=\mathcal{E}_{\phi}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times w\times D},

(1)

where
(h,w)=(H/p,W/p)
depends on the patch size
p
, and
D
is the embedding dimension.
For a standard ViT-B/16 model,
D=768
and
p=16
.
Calculating the total number of elements in
z_{raw}
, we observe:

h\times w\times D=\frac{H}{16}\times\frac{W}{16}\times 768=H\times W\times 3.

(2)

We observe that the cardinality of the VFM feature space is equivalent to that of the pixel space, implying that VAEs based on VFMs generate an uncompressed representation.
Although VFMs possess strong semantic extraction capabilities and have been shown to aid DiT learning, this raises a critical question:

Is such a high-dimensional space an optimal representation for diffusion training?

2.2
Representation Redundancy in VFM Features

Building on the above analysis, while VFM features are semantically rich, we argue that they are mathematically over-represented.
According to the Manifold Assumption
(
12
;
9
)
, the raw VFM features
z_{raw}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
should concentrate in a small neighborhood of a
d
-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
, where
d\ll D
.
Equivalently, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\textrm{dist}(z_{raw},\mathcal{M})\leq\varepsilon.

Hence, for each
z_{raw}
, there exists an on-manifold component
z_{core}\in\mathcal{M}
and a residual
z_{noise}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
such that:

\begin{split}z_{raw}&=z_{core}+z_{noise},\\
\|z_{noise}\|&=\|z_{raw}-z_{core}\|\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

(3)

We interpret
z_{core}
as the structured (semantic) component and
z_{noise}
as off-manifold variation or redundancy.
The goal of a Decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\psi}
is to reconstruct the image
x
.
Ideally, the decoder should disregard the redundancy, yielding
\mathcal{D}_{\psi}(z_{core}+z_{noise})\approx\mathcal{D}_{\psi}(z_{core})
.
However, due to the limited capacity of
\mathcal{D}_{\psi}
, it inevitably overfits to the spurious correlations between
z_{noise}
and image details.
Ultimately, this results in compromised reconstruction quality.

Figure 2
:
Empirical Analysis of Information Overload.

We perform PCA on features extracted by DINOv3.
The cumulative explained variance curve reveals a significant long-tail distribution with a clear
Elbow Point
around dimension 32.
This confirms the existence of Information Overload in the raw VFM space and justifies our choice of
d=32
for the packed representation.

To quantitatively validate the hypothesis of
Information Overload
, we conducted a Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the raw VFM features.
We utilize the ImageNet-1k validation set as the data source.
For the frozen VFM encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
, we extract features
z_{raw}
and flatten the spatial dimensions to obtain the matrix
\mathbf{Z}_{raw}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times D}
.
We then perform Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on the centered covariance matrix of
\mathbf{Z}_{raw}
to obtain the eigenvalues, which represent the variance explained by each principal component.

As illustrated in Figure
2
(Blue Curve), the cumulative explained variance of the VFM features (
\mathbf{Z}_{raw}
) exhibits an obvious long-tail distribution.
Specifically, the first 32 principal components explain approximately 77% of the total variance.
Crucially, we observe a distinct
Elbow Point
around dimension 32.
Beyond this point, the curve flattens significantly, indicating that the remaining 700+ dimensions contribute little (roughly 23%) to the overall information.
This confirms that the raw VFM space is dominated by redundancy, where the effective semantic information is concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace.

Empirical evidence is also seen in RAE
(
19
)
and SVG
(
13
)
.
RAE forces the decoder to process the full
z_{raw}
, meaning it struggles to disentangle essential semantics from the redundancy, leading to poor reconstruction fidelity.
SVG implicitly acknowledges this problem by introducing an entirely separate trainable encoder to correct the signal redundancy from the VFM, thus bypassing the VFM’s subspace.

2.3
The RePack Framework

Inspired by the previous analysis of
z_{raw}
, we propose the
RePack
(
Re
presentation
Pack
ing) framework.
Rather than learning a complex encoder from scratch, RePack adopts the strategy of utilizing the VFM as the Encoder.
Crucially, RePack projects the high-dimensional VFM features onto a
densely packed semantic manifold
.

Mathematically, we introduce a lightweight projector
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
to map the raw feature space to a packed latent space.
Given the frozen VFM features
z_{raw}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times w\times D}
, the packed representation
z_{core}
is obtained by:

z_{core}=\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(z_{raw})\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times w\times d},

(4)

where
d\ll D
is the target dimension of the semantic manifold.
Based on the elbow point observed in Figure
2
, we set
d=32
.
In our implementation,
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
is instantiated as a simple Linear Layer.
This projection acts as an information bottleneck.

To verify the effectiveness of this packing mechanism, we further analyze the spectral distribution of RePack’s features.
We flatten
z_{core}
into a matrix
\mathbf{Z}_{core}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times d}
and perform PCA as described in Section II-B.
As shown in Figure
2
(Red Curve), RePack presents a fundamentally different behavior.
At the same normalized capacity percentage, RePack exhibits a lower cumulative explained variance compared to VFM.
While VFM features are highly redundant (allowing the first few components to explain most variance), RePack features are harder to compress further via PCA because the information is already well-distributed across all 32 dimensions.
This implies that each dimension in
\mathbf{Z}_{core}
carries distinct and significant semantic weight, effectively eliminating the long-tail noise found in the raw VFM.

With this tightly packed and information-dense representation
z_{core}
, we train a Diffusion Transformer (DiT)
\epsilon_{\phi}
to model its distribution. The diffusion loss is defined as:

\mathcal{L}_{diff}=\mathbb{E}_{x,t,\epsilon}\left[\left|\epsilon_{\phi}(z_{core}^{(t)},t)-\epsilon\right|^{2}_{2}\right],

(5)

where
z_{core}^{(t)}
is the noisy projected features.
Leveraging the powerful semantic extraction of the VFM and the packed representation from
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
, the convergence speed of the DiT is significantly accelerated.

2.4
Simplified Training Pipeline

A key advantage of RePack is its training simplicity.
Similar to RePack, VA-VAE
(
17
)
also obtains a packed representation of the VFM through latent space projection and alignment.
However, to achieve this, VA-VAE requires a complex, three-stage training process involving extensive hyper-parameter tuning.
For instance, their training is conducted on ImageNet for 130 epochs using 32 H800 GPUs.
In contrast, RePack directly utilizes a pre-trained VFM as the encoder.
During training, only the projection layer and the Decoder are optimized.
This drastically reduces the computational burden.
We only require three standard losses: Reconstruction Loss (
\mathcal{L}_{1}
), Adversarial Loss (
\mathcal{L}_{adv}
)
(
5
)
, and Perceptual Loss (
\mathcal{L}_{lpips}
)
(
18
)
, enabling the RePack model to complete training on ImageNet in just 14 epochs.
The experimental results further demonstrate that RePack surpasses VA-VAE in both DiT fitting speed and final generation quality.

3.1
Implementation Details & Experimental Setup

We conduct all experiments on ImageNet-1K
(
3
)
at
256\times 256
resolution.
For the visual encoder, we employ the pre-trained DINOv3 ViT-B/16 (86.8M parameters) in a frozen state.
The projector is a Linear Layer without bias, containing 24,608 trainable parameters.
For the decoder, we adopt the architecture from VA-VAE
(
17
)
(44.8M parameters).

RePack is trained for 14 epochs at a resolution of
256\times 256
.
All training is conducted on 4 GPUs using FP32 precision.
We employ a batch size of 32 for RePack, and a batch size of 512 for both DiT-B and DiT-XL/2.
The source code and pre-trained models will be released in a future version of the paper.

3.3
Analysis of Latent Representations

While it is well-established that the quality of the VAE latent directly impacts the performance of DiTs, what a good latent space should look like still remains an active debate.
Recent works have offered valuable insights.
For instance, SE
(
15
)
suggests a latent space with reduced high-frequency noise.
DC-AE 1.5
(
2
)
emphasizes the importance of information disentanglement.
It proposes that an ideal latent representation should effectively decouple structural information from fine-grained details across channels.

Inspired by these insights, we hypothesize that an optimal latent representation should possess two key properties:

1.

high structural fidelity in low-frequency bands;

2.

clean, semantic-rich contours in high-frequency bands.

To verify this, we perform a spectral decomposition analysis on the latent codes
z
of VA-VAE and RePack.

Formally, let
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
denote the 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT).
We decompose the latent
z
into low-frequency (
z_{low}
) and high-frequency (
z_{high}
) components using a radial mask
M_{r}
:

\begin{split}z_{low}&=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(z)\odot M_{r}),\\
z_{high}&=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(z)\odot(1-M_{r})),\end{split}

(6)

where
M_{r}
passes frequencies within a normalized radius
r
and blocks others.

As visualized in the right panel of Figure
4
, we compare the spectral components of VA-VAE and RePack.
For VA-VAE, the
z_{high}
component is dominated by checkerboard patterns rather than actual image content, while the
z_{low}
component appears blurry, struggling to preserve clear object structures.
In contrast, in the high-frequency domain (
z_{high}
), RePack retains distinct object contours rather than grid noise.
In the low-frequency domain (
z_{low}
), it preserves a sharp and coherent semantic layout.

We further investigate this observation using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to visualize the latent space in RGB (Figure
4
, Middle).
We benchmark against the original VAE (trained without VFM guidance) and VA-VAE.
Compared to the original VAE, RePack exhibits significantly less high-frequency noise.
Compared to VA-VAE, RePack presents sharper structural details.

This analysis suggests that RePack suppresses the aliasing noise that is harmful for DiT training while preserving the high-frequency details essential for generation.
By providing a cleaner and more disentangled signal, RePack effectively reduces the complexity of the modeling task for DiT.

3.4
Reconstruction Fidelity

We compare the reconstruction quality of RePack with SOTA VFM-based methods, RAE
(
19
)
and SVG
(
13
)
, on the ImageNet validation set.
The quantitative results are presented in Table
2
.
While RePack aims for a compact semantic manifold rather than perfect reconstruction, the results still validate our hypothesis regarding information redundancy.

RAE directly uses raw VFM features as the encoded representation.
Despite RAE utilizing the full 768 channels and employing a massive decoder (415.33M parameters), it achieves suboptimal performance.
RePack employs a decoder with only 44.8M parameters and compresses the latent space to just 32 channels.
Even with such a compact representation and reduced model capacity, RePack surpasses RAE in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
This experimental result confirms our motivation:
excessive semantic dimensions burden the decoder and distract the model from the core semantics.
Thus, RePack’s bottleneck forces the smaller model to focus on the essential signal, resulting in higher fidelity.

SVG incorporates a separate, trainable auxiliary encoder to compensate for the Information Overload of the VFM features.
While SVG achieves the best reconstruction metrics, the necessity of this extra module implicitly validates the reconstruction issues inherent to raw VFM features.
Furthermore, we note that the VFM utilized by SVG offers a smaller total channel count (384 VFM channels + 8 auxiliary channels).
Compared to the 768 channels used by RAE, this reduced dimensionality of 392 channels also serves as a form of representation compression.

Another factor influencing RePack’s reconstruction quality is the nature of the information bottleneck.
By filtering redundancy, RePack prevents the decoder from memorizing pixel-level details and noise.
Instead, the decoder learns to synthesize high-frequency details based on the semantic structure, which leads to the emergent super-resolution discussed in Section
6
.

4
Current Limitations of RePack

While RePack significantly accelerates DiT training and improves structural generation, RePack still has room for improvement regarding pixel-level reconstruction fidelity.
Specifically, we observe minor color shifts between the reconstructed outputs and the original images.
Further work may include incorporating frequency-based alignment losses and scaling up the decoder.
Given the exciting DiT training efficiency gains we have observed, we present these findings to the community.
In future versions of this paper, we will refine the RePack architecture to further boost its performance.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we identify the challenge of
Information Overload
in VFM-based encoders and introduce RePack to address this issue.
Unlike previous approaches, RePack bypasses the need for retraining a complex encoder.
Instead, it projects high-dimensional VFM outputs onto a compact semantic manifold.
This lightweight design acts as an effective semantic bottleneck, filtering out redundant noise while strictly preserving critical structural geometry.
Our experiments demonstrate that RePack not only outperforms methods utilizing raw VFM features in reconstruction fidelity but also significantly accelerates DiT convergence.
Notably, RePack achieves a remarkable FID-50k of 3.66 (a 35% improvement) compared to the SOTA.
RePack proves that properly taming the representation space is highly crucial.
We hope RePack can establish a new paradigm for efficient generative modeling.

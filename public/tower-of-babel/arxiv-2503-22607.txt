Towards a Quantum Information Theory of Hadronization: Dihadron Fragmentation and Neutral Polarization in Heavy Baryons
Rebecca von Kuk, Kyle Lee, Johannes K. L. Michel, Zhiquan Sun

Abstract
We pioneer the application of quantum information theory to experimentally distinguish between classes of hadronization models. We adapt the CHSH inequality to the fragmentation of a single parton to hadron pairs, a violation of which would rule out classical dynamics of hadronization altogether. Furthermore, we apply and extend the theory of quantum contextuality and local quantum systems to the neutral polarization of a single spin-1 hadronic system, namely the light constituents of excited Sigma baryons Σ c , b ∗ \Sigma^{*}_{c,b} formed in the fragmentation of heavy quarks.

II
Classical hadronization models as “hidden” variables

In the picture we are developing,
Bell’s “hidden variables”
\lambda

take on concrete meaning
as a point in the phase space of a stochastic hadronization model
with an intermediate classical state
p(\lambda)
, i.e., a probability density.
Of course, in this case the variables are not really hidden:
In an analytic model, one may calculate their statistical distribution;
if the model is implicitly defined by a stochastic algorithm,
e.g. a Markov process acting on an ensemble
of strings or preconfined clusters
[
64
,
3
,
4
]
,
one can print out and bin the intermediate state to record
p(\lambda)
.
One then shows that for large classes of such models,
suitable correlation observables
\mathcal{C}

have a bounded expectation value
\langle\mathcal{C}\rangle_{p(\lambda)}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{max}}
,
using basic properties of the integral measure
in any number of classical phase-space dimensions,
while QM expectation values can exceed the bound.
Note that
p(\lambda)
and the conditional measurement probabilities
of classical hadronization models must obey
the spacetime symmetries of QCD (Lorentz covariance and parity),
a constraint that is typically absent in tests of quantum foundations,
but which we will make productive use of in this letter.

To fix ideas,
consider the fragmentation
i\to h_{1}h_{2}X

of an unpolarized parton
i=q,g

into identified hadrons
h_{1,2}
.
If
h_{1,2}
are spin-
1/2
baryons,
their most general spin-density matrix
compatible with the symmetries of QCD reads

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{\mathbb{1}}{4}+\tilde{D}_{LL}\,\hat{S}_{1,L}\otimes\hat{S}_{2,L}+\tilde{D}_{TT}\,\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{1,T}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{2,T}\,,

(1)

where
\hbar=1
and
\hat{S}_{j,L}
(
\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{j,T}
)
is the longitudinal (transverse) component of the spin operator
of hadron
h_{j}
with respect to the fragmentation axis
\mathbf{z}

pointing back to the hard collision in the hadron’s rest frame.
(In the last term, a contraction between vector indices is understood.)
Eq. (
1

at long distances of order the
hadron lifetimes,
t_{\mathrm{decay}}\sim 1/\Gamma_{h}
,
where a QM description of the system
is appropriate for the exclusive hadronic decays
[
65
]
.
By contrast, the nonperturbative hadronization physics
are encoded in normalized dihadron fragmentation functions (DiFFs)
[

\tilde{D}_{LL}

[
70

and
\tilde{D}_{TT}
,
which are determined from experiments

In the latter case, we interpret the DiFFs as matching coefficients
that are fixed by computing spin expectation values

T_{k\ell}/4\equiv\langle S_{1,k}\otimes S_{2,\ell}\rangle

in a (potentially classical) hadronization model
at the scale
t_{\mathrm{confine}}\sim 1/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\ll t_{\mathrm{decay}}

where the hadrons form and decouple.
We also have

t_{\mathrm{confine}}\gg t_{\mathrm{hard}}
,
where
t_{\mathrm{hard}}

is the very short time scale of the perturbative hard scattering.
This separation of all scales allows us to specifically probe the quantum
nature of hadronization and, while abstract, mirrors
the workflow of MC generators.

The DiFFs completely characterize the system, see Fig.

Most interestingly, we can consider the class of fully classical hadronization models

\displaystyle\langle S_{1,k}\otimes S_{2,\ell}\rangle=\int\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\,p(\lambda)\,S_{1,k}(\lambda)\,S_{2,\ell}(\lambda)\,.

(2)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{TT}^{2}+\max\{\tilde{D}_{LL}^{2},\tilde{D}_{TT}^{2}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:fully_classical_model}}}}{{\leq}}1\,.

(3)

This is the key result for this example process, illustrating our proposal:
If Eq. (

the observation cannot be explained by any fully classical hadronization model

III
Quantum contextuality and local spin-1 systems

A fundamental aspect of QM systems
is their (statistical)
contextuality

[
82
]
,
the idea that a measurement’s outcome
may depend on which other properties are being measured.
To adapt the notion of statistical contextuality to classical hadronization models,
consider
a very simple hadronizing system:
the production of excited heavy spin-
3/2
baryons
\Sigma_{Q}^{*}

during the fragmentation
Q\to\Sigma_{Q}^{*}X
of beauty or charm quarks,
Q=b,c
.
The light hadron constituents
that bind to the static heavy quark carry total angular momentum
j=1
,
forming a qutrit.
By parity and rotational invariance about the fragmentation axis
\mathbf{z}
,
their quantum polarization state
\rho

at
t_{\mathrm{decay}}\sim 1/\Gamma_{\Sigma_{Q}^{*}}\gg t_{\mathrm{confine}}

is governed by a single nonperturbative Falk-Peskin parameter
w_{1}

[
83
]
,

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{w_{1}}{2}\bigl(\lvert 1_{z}\rangle\langle 1_{z}\rvert+\lvert-1_{z}\rangle\langle-1_{z}\rvert\bigr)+\bigl(1-w_{1}\bigr)\lvert 0_{z}\rangle\langle 0_{z}\rvert\,,

(4)

where the
\lvert m_{z}\rangle
are the eigenstates of
\hat{J}_{z}
.
In a classical hadronization model
at
t_{\mathrm{confine}}\ll t_{\mathrm{decay}}

with intermediate state
p(\lambda)
,
the probability of measuring
the squared angular momentum

J_{e}^{2}=(\bm{e}\cdot\bm{J})^{2}

along a unit vector
\bm{e}
to be
x\in\{0,1\}
reads

\displaystyle P(x|\bm{e})=\int\mathrm{d}\lambda\,p(\lambda)\,P(x|\lambda,\bm{e})\,,\quad\langle J_{e}^{2}\rangle=P(1|\bm{e})

(5)

and is subject to the constraint

\langle\bm{J}^{2}\rangle=2

inherited from the interpolating partonic spin operator,
i.e.,
\langle J_{a}^{2}+J_{b}^{2}+J_{c}^{2}\rangle=2

for any orthonormal triad
\{\bm{a},\bm{b},\bm{c}\}
.
By Gleason’s theorem
[
84
,
82
]
,
parity, and Lorentz covariance,

P(1|\bm{e})
uniquely coincides
with the single-parameter form of
\langle J_{e}^{2}\rangle

as computed from Eq. (
4
) for any
\bm{e}
.
All single-point spin measurements are thus determined
by
P(1|\bm{z})=\langle J_{z}^{2}\rangle=w_{1}
,
which
again
serves as a matching coefficient connecting
the classical (or quantum-mechanical) model at
t_{\mathrm{confine}}

to the quantum state at
t_{\mathrm{decay}}
.

In this context,
noncontextuality
of a hadronization model
is the statement that in addition to Eq. (
5
),
the model predicts certain joint probabilities

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}|\bm{e}_{1},\dots,\bm{e}_{N})

(6)

through a joint conditional measurement probability at given
\lambda
.
Of particular interest are

\{\bm{e}_{i}\}
arranged in connected Kochen-Specker (KS) graphs
[
87
]

where
\bm{e}_{i}\perp\bm{e}_{j}
for all edges in the graph.
The first nontrivial case of a simple cyclic graph
is the Klyachko-Can-Binicioglu-Shumovsky (KCBS) configuration
\bigstar=\{\bm{e}_{i}\}

[
88
]

of
N=5
unit vectors
\bm{e}_{i}\perp\bm{e}_{i+1\!\!\!\mod 5}

arranged around a symmetry axis
\bm{r}=\bm{z}
,
i.e., they form a regular pentagram in the plane
of fixed
\bm{r}\cdot\bm{e}_{i}=1/\sqrt[4]{5}
.
Crucially,
even though the additional measurements
in Eq. (
6
) are counterfactual in our case,
the
existence
of the joint distribution
does leave an imprint on experimentally observable distributions.
This is the statement of the KCBS inequality
[
88
]
,
which involves two-point correlations.
Using
\langle\bm{J}^{2}\rangle=2
,
one readily shows (also in the classical case)
that Eq. (
6
) exists for
\bigstar

if and only if the single-point marginal probabilities satisfy

\sum_{\bm{e}\in\bigstar}\langle J_{e}^{2}\rangle\geq 3
.
Importantly, these latter single-point measurements

are
accessible experimentally
and determined by
w_{1}
.
We thus find a KCBS bound for the Falk-Peskin parameter,
see Fig.
2
,

\displaystyle w_{1}\geq w_{1}^{\bigstar}\equiv\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5}}\approx 0.276393\,,

(7)

which is satisfied if and only if Eq. (
6
) exists for
\bigstar
.
We have verified that the constraint from
\bigstar
with
N=5

is the strongest constraint on
w_{1}

from any simple
N
-cycle symmetric around
\bm{z}
.
(In practice, we also choose
\bm{r}

such that
\sum_{e\in\bigstar}\langle J_{e}^{2}\rangle
is minimized.)

It is important to realize that
classical models do not have to be noncontextual,
cf. Eq. (
8
).
Instead, the degree of contextuality, like Eq. (
2
), is a property
of the hadronization model that can be assessed
by inspecting its dynamics and coupling to the final-state hadron spin.
(The intuition of the KCBS-noncontextual limit being “more classical”
is valuable nevertheless, cf. the case of a thermal system
at high temperatures, where classical and quantum dynamics become indistinguishable
and isotropic,
w_{1}\to 2/3>w_{1}^{\bigstar}
.)
Conversely,
the degree to which hadronization models can be coupled to hadron spin
in a noncontextual way
has fundamental limits.
The tightest such constraint for
j=1
arises from the Yu-Oh configuration
[
89
]
,
a KS graph with
N=13
containing several cycles,
whose associated state-independent noncontextuality inequality
when combined with
\langle\bm{J}^{2}\rangle=2
rules out
the existence of Eq. (
6
) for this case altogether.

IV
Classical correlation strength

To explore further interpretations of
w_{1}
,
consider the “hidden” state
of a classical hadronization model
that simply consists of a unit vector
\bm{\lambda}
in the hadron rest frame.
Evaluating
P(1|\bm{z})
in this model,
we find

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}+r\,\Delta_{2}\,,\quad\Delta_{2}\equiv\int\!\mathrm{d}c_{\theta}\,p(c_{\theta})\,\Bigl(c_{\theta}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\Bigr)\,,

(8)

where

c_{\theta}\equiv\bm{z}\cdot\bm{\lambda}

and

r
is a free parameter governing the strength
of the coupling between
J_{e}^{2}
and
\bm{\lambda}

in the Lorentz-invariant conditional measurement probability

P(1|\bm{\lambda},\bm{e})=2/3+r[(\bm{e}\cdot\bm{\lambda})^{2}-1/3]
.
Since
0\leq P(1|\bm{\lambda},\bm{e})\leq 1
,
we have
-1\leq r\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.
If
\bm{\lambda}
indicates e.g. the average orientation
of classical diquark angular momenta near the heavy quark,
one physically expects a positive coupling
r>0

to the observed angular momentum,
in which case
w_{1}\geq 1/2
since
\Delta_{2}\geq-1/3
,
exhibiting KCBS noncontextuality.
However, nothing a priori forbids
r<0
.
Since
\Delta_{2}\leq 2/3
,
we can have

w_{1}\to 0
in the extreme limit

r\to-1
and

p(c_{\theta})\to\delta(\lvert c_{\theta}\rvert-1)/2
.
This is of interest as an example of a classical model
that achieves maximal KCBS violation,
and thus must be contextual.

Another important insight from this limit is
that
w_{1}
measures the degree
of
classical
correlation
within
the model.
To make this explicit,
consider a bipartite classical model
consisting of two unit vectors
\{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2}\}

that
couple to the hadron spin
through

\bm{\lambda}=(\bm{\lambda}_{1}+\bm{\lambda}_{2})/\lvert\bm{\lambda}_{1}+\bm{\lambda}_{2}\rvert
.
In this case we can prove a general bound

w_{1}\geq(1-|\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}|)/2
,
see Fig.
2
,
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\equiv\Big\langle\frac{2\lambda_{1z}\lambda_{2z}-\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cdot\bm{\lambda}_{2}\,(\lambda_{1z}^{2}+\lambda_{2z}^{2})}{1-(\bm{\lambda}_{1}\cdot\bm{\lambda}_{2})^{2}}\Big\rangle\leq 1\,,

(9)

is a nonlinear correlation coefficient
that vanishes e.g. if
\bm{\lambda}_{1,2}
are independent,

p(\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2})=p(\bm{\lambda}_{1})\,p(\bm{\lambda}_{2})
.
Alternatively, we can bound
w_{1}
for
r<0

by linear correlations, leading to

\displaystyle w_{1}\geq\min\Bigl\{\langle\bm{\lambda}_{1T}\cdot\bm{\lambda}_{2T}\rangle,\,\frac{2}{3}\Bigr\}\,,

(10)

where
\bm{\lambda}_{iT}

are the components of
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
transverse to
\bm{z}
.

Bipartite QM models and factorization

Lastly, we interpret Eq. (
4
)
in a simple QM model of hadronization
where the hadron is formed from the heavy quark
and two light spin-
1/2
degrees of freedom,
e.g. constituent quarks.
Here it is interesting to ask
whether the light system
is entangled or separable
when the hadron is formed.
To assess this, we convert Eq. (
4
)
to a two-qubit density matrix
(noting that the singlet state,
which would correspond to the
\Lambda_{Q}
baryon,
is not populated at this binding energy),
and again evaluate the PPT criterion
[
71
]
.
The result for the lowest eigenvalue of the partial transpose
is shown in red in Fig.
2
:
As expected, an overlap of
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\,\lvert 0_{z}\rangle\langle 0_{z}\rvert]=1-w_{1}>1/2

with
\lvert 0_{z}\rangle
, which acquires an interpretation
as a triplet Bell state in this model, implies entanglement
(and eventually leads to KCBS violation
[
88
]
).

Importantly, an observation of entanglement at
w_{1}<1/2

would imply that no factorization can exist
(in the technical sense of the word in high-energy physics)
in terms of any spin-
1/2

To see this, compare to the prototypical factorization
of the hadronic tensor for the Drell-Yan process at leading-power,

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{DY}}\sim\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^{\mu}\Phi_{q}\gamma^{\nu}\Phi_{\bar{q}}]\,,

(11)

where

\Phi_{q,\bar{q}}
are (anti)quark correlation functions
of the (possibly polarized) incoming protons,

\operatorname{tr}
denotes a trace over Dirac indices,
and
\mu,\nu
encode the polarization of the virtual photon.
Eq. (
11

to the product
\mathcal{H}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{q}}
of the (anti)quark Hilbert spaces
[
90
]
.
Conversely, the neutral polarization state

\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(\lvert\uparrow_{q}\downarrow_{\bar{q}}\rangle+\lvert\downarrow_{q}\uparrow_{\bar{q}}\rangle\bigr)

which is maximally entangled,
cannot have large overlap with Eq. (

and indeed it is well known

,

,
97

98

that neutral photon polarization requires an additional spin-

degree of freedom
(and thus subleading-power factorization).
The above argument provides a novel perspective on this fact from QI theory.

and even for
w_{1}=2/3

we find physical regions in their parameter space
with nontrivial QI interpretations
w_{1}^{\prime}<1/2
or
w_{1}^{\prime}<w_{1}^{\bigstar}
,
respectively.
(Increasing
w^{(1,1)}
shrinks the physical region,
but leaves
w_{1}^{\prime}
unchanged.)
Notably,
their scaling for
k_{T}\gg\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}

is model-independently predicted
to be
w^{(N,n)}(k_{T})\sim(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}/k_{T})^{n}

[
102
]
.
Therefore if
heavy-quark fragmentation indeed exhibits
“quantum” behavior
at
k_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
as in Fig.
3
,
there would be the exciting possibility
to observe a quantum-to-classical transition as
k_{T}
increases.

VII
Conclusions and outlook

In this letter we have initiated the study of
applying tools from quantum information (QI) theory
to the open problem of hadronization to distinguish between entire
classes of classical or quantum-mechanical hadronization models at once.
In Eq. (
3
),
we have presented an inequality
for light dihadron fragmentation
that, if violated in data,
would rule out classical hadronization dynamics altogether.
We stress that our goal was not to test quantum foundations themselves:
Rather, we adapted tools from QI theory to characterize
the dynamics of the blackbox Hamiltonian of hadronization,
and appealed to effective field theory to separate it from other
(very likely quantum) dynamics in the problem.
We then adapted the concept of contextuality
to the study of hadronization,
showcased our ideas
using the Falk-Peskin fragmentation parameter
w_{1}

in heavy baryon production,
and further interpreted
w_{1}

in terms of classical correlation strength
and QM entanglement,
with implications for factorizability.
Incorporating these insights
will impose practical constraints
on future hadronization modeling
and enable cross-pollination with ongoing QI research.

The experimental status of
w_{1}
is unclear at present,
see Fig.
2
.
Combining
\Sigma_{b}^{*\pm}
baryons, DELPHI measured
[
85
]

\displaystyle w_{1}^{\mathrm{DELPHI}}=-0.36\pm 0.30_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm 0.30_{\mathrm{syst}}\,,

(14)

which translates to a 95% CL limit of
w_{1}\leq 0.25

after imposing positivity.
On the other hand, by combining
\Sigma_{c}^{*++}
and
\Sigma_{c}^{*0}
baryons
(that have the identical light valence content and relative rates),
CLEO found
[
86
]
,

\displaystyle w_{1}^{\mathrm{CLEO}}=0.71\pm 0.13\,,

(15)

close to the isotropic limit
w_{1}=2/3
.
This disagreement has, to our knowledge, not been resolved
[
106
]
.
We therefore strongly encourage a new measurement of
w_{1}

using state-of-the-art experimental techniques.
We also encourage experimental tests of spin correlations
in nearby dihadrons
[
81
]

and of the TMD generalizations
of
w_{1}
that we introduced, all of which possess deep physical interpretations
within the new QI framework of hadronization that we have presented here.

VII.1
Fragmentation to nearby dihadrons

VII.1.1
Factorization and general structure

Consider the cross section for producing a dihadron pair
with general spin
s_{1,2}

and magnetic quantum numbers
-s_{1}\leq m_{1},m_{1}^{\prime}\leq s_{1}

and
-s_{2}\leq m_{2},m_{2}^{\prime}\leq s_{2}

with respect to the fragmentation axis
\mathbf{z}
.
(In the collinear approximation,
the two hadron rest frames only differ by a boost along the
\mathbf{z}
direction,
such that its orientation is common.)
We decompose the cross section for an arbitrary spin state
in terms of operators
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
on the product Hilbert space as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}m_{2}^{\prime}}}{\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\,\mathrm{d}R_{T}}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\,\mathrm{d}R_{T}}\,\mathcal{O}_{m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}m_{2}^{\prime}}\,,

(S1)

where
\mathcal{O}_{m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}m_{2}^{\prime}}=\langle m_{1}m_{2}\lvert\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rvert m_{1}^{\prime}m_{2}^{\prime}\rangle

and
z_{1},z_{2}
are the longitudinal momentum fractions
of hadrons
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, respectively.
Here,
R_{T}
is the magnitude of the transverse component of the difference of hadron momenta relative to their sum.
For pairs of spin-
1/2
baryons we have

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{O}\}=\Bigl\{\frac{\mathbb{1}}{4},\,\hat{S}_{1,L}\otimes\hat{S}_{2,L},\,\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{1,T}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{2,T}\Bigr\}\,.

(S2)

as in Eq. (
1
), where the coefficients
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}

in turn are also denoted by
\mathrm{d}\sigma
(without subscript),

\mathrm{d}\sigma_{LL}
, and
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{TT}
.
They are given in terms of dihadron fragmentation functions
D^{\mathcal{O}}_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}
as
[
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
,
70
]

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\,\mathrm{d}R_{T}}=\sum_{i}\!\int\mathrm{d}y\,C_{i}(y,\mu)\,D^{\mathcal{O}}_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}\left(\frac{z_{1}}{y},\frac{z_{2}}{y},R_{T},\mu\right)\,,

(S3)

where
C_{i}
are the usual single-inclusive partonic hard coefficients.
(In the case of hadronic collisions, they also contain the parton distribution functions.)
Additionally,
C_{i}
can include fiducial acceptance cuts
on the overall direction of the near-collimated dihadron system;
cuts on individual hadron energies or momenta are special cases
of the bins we consider below.

We are interested in the yield
N_{\mathcal{O}}
of dihadron pairs
produced in a specific spin state
\mathcal{O}
and bin of
(z_{1},z_{2})
,
which is proportional to

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathcal{L}}=\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\equiv\int\!\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\int_{0}^{R_{T}^{\rm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}R_{T}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\,\mathrm{d}R_{T}}\,\Theta(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\int\!\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,C_{i}(y,\mu)\,\Theta(yx_{1},yx_{2})\,\int_{0}^{R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}R_{T}\,D^{\mathcal{O}}_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}\bigl(x_{1},x_{2},R_{T},\mu\bigr)\,,

(S4)

where
\Theta(z_{1},z_{2})
implements the given bin
and the normalization factor
\mathcal{L}
is the total luminosity.
By placing an upper cutoff
R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}\sim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}

on the relative transverse momentum, we ensure
that spin correlations are genuinely sourced
by the nonperturbative fragmentation dynamics.
The total yield
N
is given by the
N_{\mathcal{O}}
with
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{1}/\operatorname{Tr}[\mathbb{1}]
the identity
on the product Hilbert space.
The normalized density matrix of the hadron pair then reads

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathcal{O}}N_{\mathcal{O}}\,\mathcal{O}\,.

(S5)

For spin-
1/2
baryons this results in Eq. (
1
) if we define the shorthand

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{LL}=\frac{\sigma_{LL}}{\sigma}=\frac{\sum_{i}\int\!\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,C_{i}(y,\mu)\,\Theta(yx_{1},yx_{2})\,\int_{0}^{R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}R_{T}\,D^{LL}_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}\bigl(x_{1},x_{2},R_{T},\mu\bigr)}{\sum_{i}\int\!\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}C_{i}(y,\mu)\,\Theta(yx_{1},yx_{2})\,\int_{0}^{R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}R_{T}\,D_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}\bigl(x_{1},x_{2},R_{T},\mu\bigr)}\,,

(S6)

and similarly for
\tilde{D}_{TT}
.
We stress that even though the DiFFs enter in a convolution
with the perturbative scattering coefficient in this form,
the strength of the spin correlation is still uniquely
sourced at the low scale, since the DGLAP evolution
and the perturbative corrections are all common.
In particular, using the leading-order perturbative result for the coefficient

C_{i}(y,\mu)\propto\delta(1-y)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
,
Eq. (
S6
) indeed reduces to a ratio of the (binned) DiFFs themselves.

We point out that while the expression for
\tilde{D}_{LL}

in Eq. (
S6
) is clearly the most amenable
to experimental tests of Eq. (
3
) in the near future,
since it directly relates to the measured cross section at a given collider,
we believe it would be valuable in the long run to test Eq. (
3
)
directly at the level of the process and collider-independent ratio

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{LL,i}\bigl(x_{1},x_{2},R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}\bigr)\equiv\frac{\int_{0}^{R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}R_{T}\,D^{LL}_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}\bigl(x_{1},x_{2},R_{T},\mu\bigr)}{\int_{0}^{R_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}R_{T}\,D_{i\to h_{1}h_{2}}\bigl(x_{1},x_{2},R_{T},\mu\bigr)}\,,

(S7)

with
\tilde{D}_{TT,i}
again defined in full analogy,
where the right-hand side is evaluated at some low input scale
\mu\sim 2\,\mathrm{GeV}
.
(In a more sophisticated setup, one may also define
\tilde{D}_{LL}
and
\tilde{D}_{TT}

as ratios of Mellin moments of the singlet and gluon or valence DiFFs
to form exact renormalization-group invariants.)
Eq. (
3
) equally well applies to
\tilde{D}_{LL,i}
and
\tilde{D}_{TT,i}

when defined as in Eq. (
S7
),
where the normalized density matrix is now directly
computed in terms of the renormalized dihadron fragmentation correlator
at the low scale.
Using Eq. (
S7
) in practice requires one to
first determine the underlying DiFFs from a global fit to the available collider data
for the yields
N_{\mathcal{O}}
extracted from decay distributions in many different bins.
Nevertheless, we believe this would be worth the effort it entails,
since one would gain access to the degree of CHSH violation
also as a function of e.g. the parton type.

VII.1.2
Decay distribution for nearby hyperon pairs

Differential distributions for the decay products of the hadrons
are obtained by tracing Eq. (
1
)
against the (conjugate) decay matrix elements for given hadron helicities

m_{1,2}
(
m_{1,2}^{\prime}
),
which are specific to each hadron and decay.
Here we consider the parity-violating weak decay of hyperons,
a standard example of a self-analyzing decay
commonly used for reconstructing (transverse) hadron polarization,
see e.g. Refs.
[
18
,
56
,
107
,
108
,
109
,
110
,
40
]
.

To fix conventions we begin with the decay
of a single, individually polarized (anti)hyperon

\displaystyle h_{1}=\Lambda\,\to p\,\pi^{-}\,,\qquad h_{2}=\bar{\Lambda}\,\to\bar{p}\,\pi^{+}\,.

(S8)

The associated decay distributions are standard
[
111
]

and read

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{p}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl[1+\alpha_{-}\,\bm{P}\cdot\bm{n}_{p}\bigr]\,,\qquad\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\bar{p}}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl[1+\alpha_{+}\,\bm{P}\cdot\bm{n}_{\bar{p}}\bigr]\,,

(S9)

where
\bm{P}=2\langle\hat{\bm{S}}\rangle
with
\lvert\bm{P}\rvert\leq 1

is the Bloch vector of the (anti)hyperon,

\bm{n_{p}}
(
\bm{n_{\bar{p}}}
) is a unit vector
pointing in the direction of the (anti)proton in the rest frame of the parent particle,

\theta_{p}
and
\varphi_{p}
(
\theta_{\bar{p}}
and
\varphi_{\bar{p}}
)
are the polar coordinates of
\bm{n_{p}}
(
\bm{n_{\bar{p}}}
)
in the respective rest frame,
and
\alpha_{\mp}
are the hyperon decay parameters.
With the above common sign in front of
\alpha_{\pm}
for hyperon and antihyperon,
one has opposite signs for
\alpha_{-}=0.732\pm 0.014

and
\alpha_{+}=-0.758\pm 0.012

[
111
]

(and thus
\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}<0
),
as required by the approximate CP symmetry.

For the system in Eq. (
1
),
each individual hyperon is indeed unpolarized on average,
\bm{P}=0
,
such that the distributions
\mathrm{d}N/(\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{p})

and
\mathrm{d}N/(\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\bar{p}})
are flat.
Nevertheless, the spin correlations encoded in Eq. (
1
)
can be readily observed from the joint distribution
of their decay products,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\bar{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\bigl[1+\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}\,\bm{n}_{p}\cdot\mathsf{T}\cdot\bm{n}_{\bar{p}}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\bigl[1+\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}\,\tilde{D}_{LL}\,\cos\theta_{p}\,\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}+\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}\,\tilde{D}_{TT}\,\sin\theta_{p}\,\sin\theta_{\bar{p}}\cos(\varphi_{p}-\varphi_{\bar{p}})\bigr]\,,

(S10)

where the rank-two tensor
\mathsf{T}=(T_{k\ell})=\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{D}_{TT},\tilde{D}_{TT},\tilde{D}_{LL})

has components
T_{k\ell}/4=\langle S_{1,k}\otimes S_{2,\ell}\rangle
.
We note that Eq. (
S10
)
takes the same form for the decay products of same-sign hyperon pairs
(or other exclusive baryonic decays that follow Eq. (
S9
))
after suitably replacing the decay constants.

Comparison to Ref.
[
81
]
:

Very recently,
and after the first preprint version of this manuscript was made public,
the STAR collaboration published a measurement
[
81
]

of the differential distribution

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta^{*}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl[1+\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}\,P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}\,\cos\theta^{*}\bigr]\,,

(S11)

where in our notation
\cos\theta^{*}=\bm{n}_{p}\cdot\bm{n}_{\bar{p}}

and the coefficient
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}
encodes the strength of the angular correlation
between the decay (anti)protons,
and thus the strength of the spin correlation
between the parent (anti)hyperons.
Projecting our Eq. (
S10
) onto the distribution differential in
\cos\theta^{*}
,
we find the relation

\displaystyle 3P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf{T}=2\tilde{D}_{TT}+\tilde{D}_{LL}\,.

(S12)

The measurement of
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}
in Ref.
[
81
]
, as well as those
for same-sign hyperon pairs obtained in Ref.
[
81
]
, therefore experimentally
constrain a very specific linear combination of dihadron fragmentation functions.
Ref.
[
81
]
found that for nearby dihadrons,
which as discussed in the main text and the above supplemental material
is the most interesting kinematic region,
the spin correlation
P_{\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}}=0.181\pm 0.035_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm 0.022_{\mathrm{sys}}

was near its maximal value of
1/3

(with some loss expected due to feed-down contributions from higher resonances),
while
P_{\Lambda\Lambda}
and
P_{\bar{\Lambda}\bar{\Lambda}}

were still compatible with zero.
These experimental constraints are indicated in Fig.
1

as of the second preprint version of this manuscript.
Notably, either of the experimental results is still compatible
with entanglement or even CHSH violation in the upper left corner,
since those depend on the precise breakdown between
\tilde{D}_{LL}
and
\tilde{D}_{TT}

and in particular the sign of
\tilde{D}_{LL}
.
We stress that two caveats apply when interpreting the measurement
of Ref.
[
81
]
in this way; specifically, (a) further work is needed to ascertain
the validity of the factorization in terms of fragmentation functions,
which might require a more stringent cut on the
p_{T}
of the hyperon candidates,
and (b) the equivalence
between the respective reference frames
and angles should be carefully checked.

The analysis in Ref.
[
81
]
was in part motivated by theory considerations
that postulate a rotationally invariant distribution of decay products,
as e.g. appropriate for exclusive
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
production,
in which case
\cos\theta^{*}
is the only angular observable of interest.
As we have discussed, however, the fragmentation process
necessarily retains a dependence on the fragmentation axis
\hat{z}
,
i.e., the orientation relative to the hard scattering process
that produces the parent parton,
and whose hard scattering products
balance the parton’s color charge.
For this reason the polarization tensor
T
need not be proportional
to the identity, and indeed in general has two nontrivial degrees of freedom
(
\tilde{D}_{LL}
and
\tilde{D}_{TT}
) according to our symmetry analysis
that led to Eq. (
1
).
We therefore strongly encourage an extension
of the exciting measurement reported in Ref.
[
81
]

to the more differential angular distribution
in Eq. (
S10
),
which would break the degeneracy between
\tilde{D}_{LL}
and
\tilde{D}_{TT}

and hold the potential of a definitive interpretation
in terms of QI constraints on possible hadronization models.
Alternatively, the degeneracy could at this point also be broken
by a simpler, direct measurement of
\tilde{D}_{LL}

via the
\cos\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}
correlation in Eq. (
S10
)
in a way that is inclusive over
\phi_{p,\bar{p}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\bigl[1+\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}\,\tilde{D}_{LL}\,\cos\theta_{p}\,\cos\theta_{\bar{p}}\bigr]\,.

(S13)

VII.2
Inclusive fragmentation to excited heavy baryons

The factorization theorem for the production of a boosted polarized heavy hadron
H

containing a heavy quark of mass
m_{Q}

at strict leading power in
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}/m_{Q}

is simply a product
[
112
,
113
,
83
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{HX}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}}{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{P}_{H}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{QX}}{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{P}_{Q}}\,\chi_{H,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}\Bigl[1+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}}{m_{Q}}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{m_{Q}}{\lvert\bm{P}_{H}\rvert}\Bigr)\Bigr]\,.

(S14)

The matching coefficient
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{QX}
is the differential cross section
for producing a free unpolarized heavy quark
at the same three-momentum in the center-of-mass frame of the collision,
\bm{P}_{Q}=\bm{P}_{H}
.
The nonperturbative dynamics are contained
in the fragmentation coefficient
\chi_{H,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}
.
Note that for hadron colliders, the second set of power corrections
in Eq. (
S14
) scales
as
{m_{Q}}/{\lvert\bm{P}_{T}\rvert}
instead,
where
\bm{P}_{T}
is the transverse momentum relative to the beam axis.
As before,
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
labels a spin operator encoding a generic spin state,
in this case for the total angular momentum of the light constituents
\ell
of
H
.
The total probability
\chi_{H}
for
Q
to fragment into
H
,
which is the coefficient
\chi_{H,\mathcal{O}}

of
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{1}_{\ell}/\operatorname{Tr}[\mathbb{1}_{\ell}]
, satisfies
\sum_{H}\chi_{H}=1
.
Given the multiplicative form
of Eq. (
S14
),
cuts acting on the heavy hadron kinematics
simply act on the heavy-quark production cross section.
Thus, unlike the light dihadron case, no convolution is required to compute
the total yields for each spin state.
Instead, we have

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}N_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}\,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{\chi_{H}}\sum_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}\chi_{H,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}\,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}\,,

(S15)

i.e., the normalized spin density matrix of the light constituents
in any given bin of the heavy-hadron kinematics
is directly given by ratios of the nonperturbative coefficients.
The normalized coefficients
\chi_{H,\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}/\chi_{H}

for a light state with angular momentum
j=1
or
j=3/2

are in direct correspondence to the usual Falk-Peskin parameters
w_{j}
.
Specifically, for the set of operators

\{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}\}=\{\mathbb{1}/4,\,\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\}

allowed by the symmetries in the case
j=1
,
one readily converts the coefficients
to the form involving
w_{1}
in Eq. (
4
)
using
\hat{J}_{z}^{2}=\mathbb{1}-\lvert 0_{z}\rangle\langle 0_{z}\rvert
.

Experimentally, the Falk-Peskin parameter
w_{1}
can be measured
from the pion distribution in the dominant decay
\Sigma_{Q}^{*}\to\Lambda_{Q}\pi
.
Consistently working to the leading-order in the heavy-quark expansion,
the decay amplitude is given
by the Isgur-Wise transition matrix element
[
114
]
,
which is proportional to

\displaystyle Y_{1m}(\theta,\varphi)\,\big\langle 1m\,\tfrac{1}{2}k\big|s\,h\big\rangle\,.

(S16)

Here
Y_{jm}
is a spherical harmonic,

\theta
(
\varphi
) is the pion polar (azimuthal) angle
relative to the fragmentation axis
in the candidate
\Sigma_{Q}^{(*)}\to\Lambda_{Q}\pi
rest frame,

s=1/2
(
3/2
) and
h
are the total spin
and spin along the
\bm{z}
axis of the
\Sigma_{Q}
(
\Sigma_{Q}^{*}
) baryon,

k
is the spin of the final-state
\Lambda_{Q}
along the
\bm{z}
axis,
and
m=h-k
.
We recall the following, very simple form of the fragmentation axis
\bm{z}

when expressed in terms of the beam three-momenta
\bm{P}_{a,b}

in the candidate rest frame,

\displaystyle\bm{z}=-\frac{\bm{P_{a}}+\bm{P_{b}}}{\lvert\bm{P_{a}}+\bm{P_{b}}\rvert}\,,

(S17)

where the beams can be protons or leptons.
We now dress Eqs. (
4
) and (
S16
)
with the appropriate Clebsch-Gordan coefficients,
average over the helicities of the unpolarized initial-state heavy-quark,
and sum over the helicities of the final-state
\Lambda_{Q}

(whose polarization is not reconstructed).
This results in the following angular distributions
for the decay products
[
83
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\Sigma_{Q}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\Sigma_{Q}}}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta}=\frac{1}{2}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{N_{\Sigma_{Q}^{*}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\Sigma_{Q}^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Bigl[1+3\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{9}{2}\omega_{1}\Bigl(\cos^{2}\theta-\frac{1}{3}\Bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\biggl\{1-\frac{3}{8}\bigl[1+3\cos(2\theta)\bigr]\Bigl(w_{1}-\frac{2}{3}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.

(S18)

Here we have performed the integral over the
\varphi
dependence,
which as expected is flat since no azimuthal direction is preferred.
We assumed that the
\Sigma_{Q}
and
\Sigma_{Q}^{*}

form well-separated resonances

\Gamma_{\Sigma_{Q}},\Gamma_{\Sigma_{Q}^{*}}\ll m_{\Sigma_{Q}^{*}}-m_{\Sigma_{Q}}

relative to the mass splitting in the heavy-quark spin symmetry doublet,
which is reasonably well satisfied
[
115
,
116
]
.
If needed, the decay distributions can readily be generalized
to account for their interference
[
83
]
, and still can be expressed
purely in terms of
w_{1}
in that case.
We note that the continuum
Q\to\Lambda_{Q}\pi
background to Eq. (
S18
)
initiated by a boosted heavy quark
is in fact described by a heavy-light DiFF that is of interest of its own,
and upon expansion in
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}/m_{Q}\ll 1

takes the same form as Eq. (
S14
)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Lambda_{Q}\pi X}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell}}}{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{P}_{\Lambda_{Q}\pi}^{\mathrm{lab}}\,\mathrm{d}m_{\Lambda_{Q}\pi}\,\mathrm{d}\cos\theta}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{QX}}{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{P}_{Q}^{\mathrm{lab}}}\,\int\!\mathrm{d}E_{\pi}\,\mathrm{d}p_{\pi}^{z}\,\chi_{\Lambda_{Q}\pi}(E_{\pi},p_{\pi}^{z})\,\delta\bigl(m_{\Lambda_{Q}\pi}-m_{\Lambda_{Q}}-m_{\pi}-E_{\pi}\bigr)\,\delta\Bigl(\cos\theta-\frac{p_{\pi}^{z}}{\lvert\bm{p}_{\pi}\rvert}\Bigr)\,,

VII.3
Transverse-momentum dependent fragmentation to excited heavy baryons

The precise relation of the coefficients
w^{(N,n)}
in Eq. (
12
)
to underlying renormalized nonperturbative matrix elements
differs,
depending on the case considered:
In case (c) given in the main text, corresponding to heavy-light dihadron

as in Eq. (

).
For cases (a) and (b) the matrix elements enter through convolutions

encoding the contributions of soft dynamics or other collinear sectors,
none of which however affect the boosted heavy-hadron spin dynamics

Importantly, the resulting decay pion distribution
for unpolarized collisions
takes a common general form for any of these cases
in terms of the cross section-level spin density in Eq. (
12

and evaluates to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{d}N_{\Sigma_{Q}}/\mathrm{d}k_{T}}\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\Sigma_{Q}}}{\mathrm{d}k_{T}\,\mathrm{d}\cos\theta\,\mathrm{d}\varphi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\biggl\{1-\frac{3}{8}\bigl[1+3\cos(2\theta)\bigr]\,w^{(2,0)}-3\cos\theta\,\sin\theta\,\cos\varphi\,w^{(2,1)}-\frac{3}{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,\cos(2\varphi)\,w^{(2,2)}\biggr\}\,,

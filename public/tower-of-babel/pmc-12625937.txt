Obstetric violence across the maternal care continuum and its impact on women’s perinatal mental health in low- and middle-income countries: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Meresa Berwo Mengesha, Abadi Kidanemariam Berhe, Tosin Philip Oyetunji, Hale Teka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionMistreatment and obstetric violence constitute significant human rights violations with profound implications for maternal mental health. These detrimental practices persist globally, particularly in contexts where underfunded health systems, workforce shortages and entrenched gender inequalities intersect, depriving women of adequate psychosocial support and culturally sensitive mental healthcare. Although awareness of the immediate harms of mistreatment is increasing, its cumulative effects throughout the maternal care continuum remain insufficiently understood. This review will synthesise evidence on the impact of mistreatment on perinatal mental health, identify critical gaps and advocate for systemic change.Methods and analysisThis systematic review and meta-analysis protocol complies with the guidelines set forth by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols. A thorough literature search will be executed across multiple electronic databases, including CINAHL—Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, Embase via Ovid, MEDLINE, PsycInfo, PubMed, Scopus, as well as other significant or specialised databases and grey literature. The review will incorporate only non-randomised study types and observational studies (cohort, cross-sectional, case-control), along with mixed-method and qualitative studies. Abstract and full-text screening will be performed by two reviewers using Covidence. The methodological quality of the included studies will be assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational studies, the Risk of Bias in Non-Randomised Studies of Interventions, the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme and the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool. Statistical heterogeneity will be evaluated using the Higgins test. Meta-analysis will be conducted using R statistical software V.4.4.4, employing random effects models to determine the weights. The study results will be reported sequentially, beginning with primary outcomes, followed by secondary outcomes and significant subgroup outcome analyses.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required as no original data will be collected. The findings of this review will be disseminated through publication and conference presentations.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251044379.Keywords: Patient-Centered Care, PERINATOLOGY, Pregnant Women, Depression & mood disorders, Anxiety disorders, Postpartum Women

Introduction

Obstetric violence (OV) is a form of gender-based violence perpetrated by healthcare systems during pregnancy, childbirth and the postpartum period.1 It includes verbal abuse, non-consensual care, neglect and the medicalisation of childbirth processes that undermine a woman’s autonomy and dignity.2 The concept of OV emerged from feminist critiques in Latin America and has since gained global recognition as a violation of human rights and reproductive justice principles.2 3 In a cross-national analysis, up to 61.7% of women in healthcare facilities across countries such as Spain, Mexico and Ethiopia reported experiencing some form of OV,4 which is higher than the prevalence in developed nations at 43%.5 These violations often occur under the guise of clinical routine, masking coercive acts as medically necessary interventions.6 7

The effects of OV extend beyond immediate physical and emotional trauma. Increasingly, research highlights its contribution to adverse perinatal mental health outcomes, including postpartum depression (PPD), anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).8 9 A 2025 Iranian study using path analysis found that OV directly and indirectly reduces postpartum quality of life by affecting mental health, self-esteem and PTSD symptoms.10 Women who experience OV are significantly more likely to suffer psychological distress and PTSD, both of which are known mediators of poor maternal mental health outcomes.10

Furthermore, OV has been linked to diminished maternal self-efficacy, disrupted mother–infant bonding and long-lasting emotional trauma.11 12 Women subjected to mistreatment during maternity care—such as verbal abuse, coercive procedures or neglect—often report reduced confidence in infant care and a higher risk of PPD.13 These consequences are especially pronounced in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where underfunded health systems, workforce shortages and entrenched gender inequalities foster environments conducive to disrespectful and abusive care.14 15 Limited access to psychosocial support and culturally appropriate mental health services further compounds these risks, particularly for adolescent mothers who face unique challenges in navigating perinatal care.16 17

Despite growing awareness of OV’s impact on maternal mental health, the cumulative effects of mistreatment throughout the maternal care continuum remain understudied in many LMIC settings. This gap highlights the urgent need for evidence to inform respectful, women-centred approaches to perinatal mental health.18 Moreover, although adolescent women represent a vulnerable group with distinct perinatal mental health needs, comprehensive research on their experiences with perinatal mental health services in LMICs is limited.

The global response to OV remains fragmented. Many clinical guidelines continue to emphasise physical safety over psychological well-being, and few include indicators addressing abuse or disrespect.19 Studies have largely focused on discrete periods such as childbirth, with less attention given to OV as a continuum spanning antenatal, intrapartum and postpartum care.20 Unlike previous reviews that addressed these topics separately,19 21 22 this synthesis will analyse both issues together across the entire maternal care continuum antenatal, intrapartum and postnatal to provide a comprehensive evaluation of OV and its impacts.

Addressing these gaps is critical to advancing maternal health equity. Recent WHO statements and reproductive justice frameworks emphasise the need for a rights-based, person-centred approach to maternity care that acknowledges the intersecting oppressions shaping women’s perinatal experiences.2 A recent high-level global technical consultation further underscored the urgent need to generate robust evidence on the connections between mistreatment during the maternal care continuum and perinatal mental health outcomes.23

This review will synthesise evidence on the impact of mistreatment on perinatal mental health, identify critical research gaps and advocate for systemic change. The findings will support the integration of respectful maternity care into mental health policy and programming in low-resource settings. It will also highlight the specific challenges adolescent women face in accessing mental health services in resource-constrained regions and inform revisions to perinatal mental health services in LMICs.

This review distinguishes itself by explicitly addressing the intersection of OV experiences and perinatal mental health consequences among women in LMICs. Findings are expected to inform evidence-based policymaking by guiding guidelines that promote respectful maternity care and integrate mental health screening within maternal health services. This approach aims to enhance service delivery models in LMICs and strengthen human rights protections and legal frameworks addressing OV and its sequelae. Therefore, this review aims to: (1) quantify the effect of mistreatment or OV across the maternal care continuum on perinatal mental health outcomes; (2) examine whether women’s perinatal mental health outcomes differ significantly based on their experiences of various forms of mistreatment; (3) identify the enablers and barriers to implementing women-centred and respectful perinatal mental health systems in LMICs; and (4) synthesise evidence on the experiences and feelings of adolescent women receiving perinatal mental health services in LMICs.

Methods

Eligibility criteria

Population

To understand the impact of diverse healthcare contexts, we will include all individuals receiving maternal care (pregnant, birthing or postpartum) who are resident in LMICs, including those classified as upper-middle-income countries. Furthermore, to specifically address the often-marginalised experiences of youth, adolescent women will be a priority population for exploring their unique access to and experiences of perinatal mental health services within these settings.

Intervention(s) or exposure(s)

Exposure to OV or mistreatment during the continuum of care. A woman is considered mistreated during facility-based maternity care if she experiences any of the following: physical abuse/physical restraint, verbal abuse, stigma and discrimination, failure to meet professional standards of care, poor rapport between women and providers, lack of supportive care, health system conditions and constraints.24 Mistreatment during facility-based maternity care is measured using a tool validated in LMICs for use during pregnancy and in the postpartum period and adapted for use.

Comparator

For this systematic review and meta-analysis protocol, the reference group comprises women who received standard respectful care or absence of OV/mistreatment across the continuum of care.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The main outcomes are perinatal mental health outcomes including perinatal depression (both antepartum depression and PPD), PTSD, anxiety, psychological distress identified using measurement scales and diagnostic criteria for each perinatal mental health outcome.

Secondary outcomes

Birth satisfaction, mother–infant bonding and maternal postpartum quality of life will be considered as a secondary outcome. Other related outcomes, such as enablers and barriers to implementing women-centred and respectful perinatal mental health systems and experiences and perspectives of adolescent women receiving perinatal mental health services in LMICs, will also be considered as secondary outcomes.

Setting and language

The settings for this systematic review and meta-analysis protocol will encompass the entire maternal care continuum, including the antenatal, intrapartum and postpartum phases—with a global focus, particularly on LMICs. The review will include studies conducted in health facility settings. We have no restrictions regarding the language of the publication. All relevant studies will be included from their inception up to January 2026, regardless of the year of publication.

Study design

Only non-randomised study types will be included (observational (cohort, cross-sectional, case-control), mixed method and qualitative studies).

Exclusion

Reviews, conference proceedings, commentaries, editor’s letters and studies lacking primary data will be excluded.

Information sources

Both published and unpublished studies will be sought. A comprehensive literature search will be conducted across the main databases such as CINAHL—Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, Embase via Ovid, MEDLINE, PsycInfo, PubMed and Scopus and other important or specialist databases and grey literature such as Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP), Google Scholar, Preprint Citation Index will be used for further searching. Furthermore, other studies will be identified by: contacting authors or experts, looking through all the articles that cite the papers included in the review (‘snowballing’), reference list checking, searching dissertation and thesis databases and searching trial or study registers will be made.

Search strategy

The search strategy for identifying relevant literature will be developed using a combination of Medical Subject Headings and keyword terms related to both the exposure and outcome of interest (“obstetric violence” OR “mistreatment” OR “disrespect and abuse”) AND (“mental health” OR “PTSD” OR “postpartum depression” OR “Anxiety” OR “Psychological distress”) AND (“childbirth” OR “perinatal” OR “Pregnant women” OR “Postpartum” OR “Maternal continuum of care”). Furthermore, to ensure a robust search, we will conduct retrospective reference harvesting and prospective forward citation searching after refining the relevant articles.

No special criteria will be applied to the literature search. Table 1 displays the pilot search strategies for PubMed. Additionally, the search will be updated toward the end of the review after validation to ensure that the search in each of the databases retrieves a high proportion of eligible studies.

Protocol

This systematic review and meta-analysis protocol was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines.25 The protocol has been registered with PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420251044379). The review is planned to start on 2 May 2025 and finish on 1 January 2026.

Patient and public involvement

Neither patients nor members of the public participated in the design, execution, reporting or dissemination of this study.

Data management

The findings retrieved from the literature search will be imported, screened and analysed using professional software platforms such as Covidence, Microsoft Excel and R statistical software. Studies will be retrieved from the databases and organised and managed using the Covidence web-based management systematic software (Covidence systematic review software, Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia).

Selection process

Studies will be screened independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to resolve differences. Two independent reviewers (TPO and MBM) will screen articles using Covidence. The screening process will proceed in stages, starting with the title and abstract review, followed by a full-text assessment and concluding with data extraction. Any discrepancies between the reviewers will be resolved through discussion. The results of the study selection process will be presented using PRISMA flow diagrams, specifically the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram for new systematic reviews that include searches of databases, registers and other sources, as adapted from Page et al26 (figure 1).

PRISMA 2020 flow diagram for new systematic reviews which included searches of databases, registers and other sources, reproduced from page et al.26 PRISMA, Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses. ** If automation tools were used, and to indicate how many records were excluded by a human and how many were excluded by automation tools.

Data collection process

A standardised data extraction form will be created to facilitate data collection. This form will then be pilot-tested with different groups, and adjustments will be made based on the feedback obtained. Two reviewers (HT and MBM) will independently extract data from the selected studies. To maintain consistency in their assessment approach, the reviewers will participate in a calibration exercise. Authors will be asked to provide any required data not available in published reports. Any disagreements among reviewers will be resolved through discussion.

Data items

The following data items will be extracted from included studies: (1) Study data: title, author name, year of publication, country of study, journal, sample size, study period, study design, follow-up period (for longitudinal and cohort studies) and limitations. (2) Population: participant characteristics such as age, gestational age, socioeconomic status, marginalisation status, educational level, setting/geographical location, parity, pregnancy risk status, birth setting, health system factors, existing and pre-existing mental health conditions and other relevant factors. (3) Intervention: types/forms of OV or mistreatment across the continuum of care, and women’s experiences of exposure to OV during maternity care. (4) Comparison: women who received standard respectful care, humanised care or care without OV or mistreatment across the continuum of care. (5) Outcomes: a composite of outcome events including perinatal depression (antepartum and postpartum), PTSD, birth-related anxiety, psychological distress and self-esteem or attachment issues. (6) Effect measures: reported effect measures for the composite outcomes, synthesised either quantitatively or qualitatively. Separate outcomes will be extracted if available, including proportions, p values, means with SD, ORs and CIs.

Outcomes and prioritisation

The study results will be reported in order, starting with primary outcomes and then secondary outcomes and important subgroup outcomes analyses. First, the impact of mistreatment or OV across the maternal care continuum on perinatal mental health outcomes will be quantified. Second, the variations in perinatal mental health outcomes based on different forms of mistreatment experienced by women will be determined. Then, enablers and barriers to implementing women-centred and respectful perinatal mental health systems in LMICs will be identified. Finally, the synthesising of qualitative evidence on the experiences and perspectives of adolescent women receiving perinatal mental health services in LMICs will be done.

Risk of bias assessment

The risk of bias in included studies will be evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational studies and the Risk of Bias in Non-Randomised Studies of Interventions tool for non-randomised studies. Additionally, the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme checklist will be used to assess the methodological quality of qualitative studies. The Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool will be applied to evaluate mixed-methods and qualitative research. Two independent reviewers (AKB and MBM) (or a reviewer/machine combination) will conduct the assessments, with a predefined process to resolve discrepancies. If key information is unclear or missing from the published reports, study investigators will be contacted for clarification.

Data synthesis strategy

If the studies included in the analysis exhibit homogeneity, a meta-analysis will be conducted to calculate the overall effect or pooled OR of mistreatment and OV on perinatal mental health outcomes, in comparison to respectful and humanised care.

This analysis will employ R V.4.4.4 software, using random effects to determine the weights for the meta-analysis, if necessary, following a thorough investigation of the presence and nature of heterogeneity. In cases of significant heterogeneity (I² ≥ 50% or p<0.1) or when data are incomplete or unsuitable for meta-analysis, a qualitative synthesis will be performed. Additionally, when appropriate, further analytical strategies will be considered to enhance the robustness and depth of the analysis, such as subgroup analysis, sensitivity analysis or meta-regression. For the subgroup analysis, if applicable, we plan to stratify the data based on three key dimensions to explore variability in outcomes: analysis will be conducted based on the regional variations of the LMIC settings (eg, lower-middle vs upper-middle-income classification). Stratification will occur based on the timing of maternity care: during antenatal care, childbirth and the postpartum period. We will further stratify the analysis based on the reported experiences of various forms of mistreatment encountered during maternity care (eg, physical abuse, non-consented care or neglect).

For quantitative data, meta-analyses will be conducted using random-effects models (DerSimonian-Laird method) to calculate pooled ORs or standardised mean differences for perinatal mental health outcomes, with heterogeneity assessed via I² statistics and Cochran’s Q-test.

Qualitative data will undergo thematic analysis using NVivo to identify enablers and barriers to implementing women-centred and respectful perinatal mental health systems in LMIC and patterns in women’s experiences, which will be integrated with quantitative findings through joint displays and critical interpretive synthesis. Publication bias will be evaluated using Egger’s test and funnel plots, supplemented by the inclusion of grey literature.

Meta-bias(es)

The risk of bias arising from missing results will not be evaluated. However, outcome reporting bias will be assessed by comparing the outcomes specified in study protocols with those ultimately reported in the published findings. If necessary, sensitivity analyses conducted in Stata V.17 will be used to examine how selective outcome reporting may affect meta-analysis results. Additionally, funnel plots will be generated to explore potential publication bias.

Confidence in cumulative evidence/certainty assessment

We will employ the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach for quantitative evidence to assess evidence of certainty as high, moderate, low or very low across five domains, that is, risk of bias, indirectness, inconsistency, imprecision and publication of bias.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical review and approval are not applicable for this study as it involves the secondary analysis of publicly available data/published literature and does not include any original data collection involving human subjects. This review’s findings will be disseminated through publication and conference presentations.

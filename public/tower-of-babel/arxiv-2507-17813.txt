Massive deformations of supersymmetric Yang-Mills matrix models
Adrien Martina

Abstract
We systematically classify all supersymmetry-preserving mass deformations of SYM matrix models in all dimensions ( D = 3 , 4 , 6 , 10 D=3,4,6,10 ). In D = 10 D=10 the polarized IKKT model emerges as the only possible deformation. Moreover, in the particular case D = 4 D=4 we identify two massive, sign-problem-free models, making them attractive candidates for non-perturbative numerical studies.

Introduction

Among the most revolutionary ideas in modern theoretical physics, holography suggests that gravity could emerge from a fundamental gauge theory description
Maldacena:1997re
;
tHooft:1993dmi
;
Aharony:1999ti
;
Itzhaki:1998dd
;
Witten:1998qj
;
Gubser:1998bc
. A particularly intriguing application arises in matrix quantum mechanics models like BFSS and BMN
Berenstein:2002jq
;
Banks:1996vh
, where gravitational phenomena are expected to emerge from a large-
N
quantum mechanical system, avoiding the subtleties of quantum field theories.

In this work, however, we focus on an even more minimal setting where we believe gravity can emerge: super-Yang-Mills (SYM) matrix models. We define this class of matrix models by taking pure super-Yang–Mills theory in dimensions
D=3,4,6\text{ or }10
and dimensionally reducing it all the way to a single point (zero dimension). Any deformation is allowed, provided it keeps the gauge invariance intact and preserves the full set of original supersymmetries (possibly deformed).

The most studied holographic model in this class is the IKKT model
Ishibashi_1997
. Originally introduced as a non-perturbative description of type IIB string theory
Ishibashi_1997
, it was later reinterpreted in the usual decoupling argument
Ooguri:1998pf
. Another interesting holographic model is the polarized IKKT model
Bonelli_2002
;
Hartnoll:2024csr
, a mass-deformed version of the IKKT model. Its advantage is to possess a tunable coupling, and infinitely many well-defined observables. It has many saddles, each of which should be dual to a specific ten-dimensional Euclidean gravitational background
Komatsu:2024bop
;
Komatsu:2024ydh
.

Let us now discuss the structure of these matrix models. Upon dimensional reduction, the

matrices

. Since the matrices have no longer any coordinate dependence, the path integral reduces to a finite number of matrix integrals. Namely, correlation functions and the partition function take the form

However, this simplicity is not without cost. Even though the models look simple and well-defined, carrying out analytical or numerical computations presents some practical difficulties. One first complication is that even though there are finitely many matrix integrals (at fixed

Classification of massive SYM matrix models

In this section, we provide the main idea that allows to classify massive SYM matrix models. Here we will assume that we have one Majorana or Majorana-Weyl spinor, meaning
D=3,4,10
. In
D=6
, we have more than one Majorana spinor so we will treat this case separately (see appendix
A.2
).
11
11

11

Note that the
D=2
pure SYM matrix model does not allow any mass-deformation. This is because one can neither write a fermionic mass term nor a Myers term in
D=2
. The constraints (
23
) and (
24
) then imply that all deformed quantities have to vanish.

Consider a massive deformation of
S_{0}
(
3
) parameterized by a mass-parameter
\mu
,

S=S_{0}+\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{2}}(\mu S_{1}+\mu^{2}S_{2}+\dots)\,.

(14)

Since
g_{\mathrm{YM}}
has mass dimension 2, dimensional analysis implies that
S_{1},S_{2},\dots
must have mass dimension
3,2,\dots
. Noting that
X_{I}
has mass dimension 1 and
\psi_{\alpha}
has dimension
3/2
, the only gauge invariant combinations of
X

\psi
’s that build up dimension 3 and dimension 2 operators are

S_{1}\supset\mathrm{Tr}\bar{\psi}M\psi,\ S_{IJK}\mathrm{Tr}X^{I}X^{J}X^{K}\,,

(15)

S_{2}\supset S_{IJ}\mathrm{Tr}X^{I}X^{J}\,,

(16)

while there exist no (polynomial) combination that can give lower dimensional operators.
12
12

12

Note that
\mathrm{Tr}X^{I}
vanishes for traceless matrices. On the other hand, if one allows the matrices to have a trace, the term
\mathrm{Tr}X_{I}
can always be removed by completing the square with the trace contribution coming from
S_{IJ}\mathrm{Tr}X_{I}X_{J}
.
Inspired by
Myers:1999ps
, we refer to
S_{IJK}\mathrm{Tr}X^{I}X^{J}X^{K}
as the Myers term.

The supersymmetries (
4
) may also need to be deformed in order to leave (
14
) invariant for all
\epsilon
. We allow the generic form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta_{\epsilon}X^{I}&=\bar{\epsilon}\Gamma^{I}\psi+\mu\bar{\epsilon}(\delta_{B}^{(1)}X^{I})+\mu^{2}\bar{\epsilon}(\delta_{B}^{(2)}X^{I})+\ldots\,,\\
\delta_{\epsilon}\psi&=\frac{i}{2}\Gamma^{IJ}\epsilon[X_{I},X_{J}]+\mu(\delta_{F}^{(1)}\psi)\epsilon+\mu^{2}(\delta_{F}^{(2)}\psi)\epsilon+\ldots\,.\end{split}

(17)

Note that
\epsilon
has mass dimension
-1/2
. Thus,
\delta_{B}^{(1)}X^{I},\delta_{B}^{(2)}X^{I},\ldots
must be fermionic operators of dimension
1/2,-1/2,\ldots
. Since our fermions have dimension
3/2
, we cannot build such operators and thus
\delta_{B}^{(k)}=0
. On the other hand,
\delta_{F}^{(1)}\psi
must be a bosonic operator of dimension 1. The only possibility is
\delta_{F}^{(1)}\psi\sim X_{I}
while
\delta_{F}^{(k>1)}=0
. Putting everything together, the most general possibility at this point is

\displaystyle\begin{split}S=S_{0}+\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\Biggl\{\mu\bar{\psi}M\psi+\mu S_{IJK}X^{I}X^{J}X^{K}+\mu^{2}S_{IJ}X^{I}X^{J}\Biggr\}\,,\end{split}

(18)

with the supersymmetry transformations given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta_{\epsilon}X^{I}&=\bar{\epsilon}\Gamma^{I}\psi\,,\\
\delta_{\epsilon}\psi&=\frac{i}{2}\Gamma^{IJ}\epsilon[X_{I},X_{J}]+\mu F^{I}\epsilon X_{I}\,,\end{split}

(19)

where
M
and
F^{I}
are matrices living in the space spanned by fully antisymmetrized products of gamma matrices, while
S_{IJK}
is a cyclic tensor and
S_{IJ}
is a symmetric tensor. Since we are interested in actions with real bosonic part, we require that
S_{IJ}\in\mathbb{R}
and
S_{IJK}^{*}=S_{KJI}
.

Furthermore, we require supersymmetry,
\delta_{\epsilon}S=0
for all
\epsilon
. This is of course satisfied for the undeformed model
(\mu=0)
, and takes the form

\delta_{\epsilon}S=\mu(\delta_{\epsilon}S)^{(1)}+\mu^{2}(\delta_{\epsilon}S)^{(2)}\,.

(20)

We thus get two sets of constraints,
(\delta_{\epsilon}S)^{(1)}=0
and
(\delta_{\epsilon}S)^{(2)}=0
. They can be written respectively as

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\mathrm{Tr}\Biggl\{[\bar{\psi},X^{I}](i\Gamma^{I}F^{J}\epsilon-iM\Gamma^{IJ}\epsilon)X^{J}+3S_{KIJ}\bar{\psi}X^{I}\Gamma^{K}\epsilon X^{J}\Biggr\}\,,\end{split}

(21)

0=-2\mathrm{Tr}\Biggl\{\bar{\psi}(MF^{I}-S_{IJ}\Gamma^{J})\epsilon X_{I}\Biggr\}\,.

(22)

The first constraint cannot be satisfied unless
\bar{\psi}X^{I}\to\frac{1}{2}[\bar{\psi},X^{I}]
which requires
S_{KIJ}
to be not only cyclic, but fully antisymmetric. From (
21
) and (
22

(i\Gamma^{[I}F^{J]}-iM\Gamma^{IJ}+\frac{3}{2}S_{KIJ}\Gamma^{K})\epsilon=0\,,

(23)

(-MF^{I}+S_{IJ}\Gamma^{J})\epsilon=0\,.

(24)

This needs to be imposed for all spinors
\epsilon
, which are Majorana or Majorana-Weyl depending on the dimension. In order to proceed solving these equations, one needs to specify the dimension
D
. The detailed analysis is carried out for each

in appendix

&+\frac{3\mu^{2}}{2^{6}}X_{i}X_{i}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2^{6}}X_{p}X_{p}+i\frac{\mu}{3}\epsilon^{ijk}X_{i}X_{j}X_{k}-\frac{1}{8}\mu\bar{\psi}\Gamma^{123}\psi\Biggr]\,.\end{split}

(25)

It preserves invariance under
SU(N)
gauge transformations, which act as global transformations
X_{I}\to UX_{I}U^{\dagger},\ \psi_{\alpha}\to U\psi_{\alpha}U^{\dagger}
. The rotational
SO(10)
symmetry is broken down to
SO(3)\times SO(7)
. We use the indices
i,j,\ldots=1,2,3
to denote the directions transforming under
SO(3)
and
p,q,r,\ldots=4,\ldots,10
the directions transforming under
SO(7)
. The capital indices run over all values
I,J,\ldots=1,2,\ldots,10
. The matrices
\Gamma^{I}
are
32\times 32
Hermitian matrices satisfying the
SO(10)
Clifford algebra. One should think of this model as defined by Wick-rotating the Lorentzian model. Thus, the fermions are Majorana-Weyl. Hence, although we write them in 32-component form, the lower 16 components are identically zero. The conjugate
\bar{\psi}
is defined as
\bar{\psi}=\psi^{\top}\mathcal{C}
where
\mathcal{C}
is the conjugation matrix of
SO(10)
. Explicit expressions for the matrices
\mathcal{C}
and
\Gamma^{I}
can be found in appendix
D
.

The mass-deformed model still possesses sixteen dynamical supersymmetries, but their form is modified relative to the original IKKT model. They act on matrices as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta_{\epsilon}X^{I}&=\bar{\epsilon}\Gamma^{I}\psi\,,\\
\delta_{\epsilon}\psi&=\frac{i}{2}\Gamma^{IJ}\epsilon[X_{I},X_{J}]+\frac{3\mu}{8}\Gamma^{123}\Gamma^{i}\epsilon X_{i}+\frac{\mu}{8}\Gamma^{123}\Gamma^{p}\epsilon X_{p}\,,\end{split}

(26)

where
\epsilon
is a Majorana-Weyl spinor (commuting) parameter, and
\delta_{\epsilon}
is an anti-commuting (Grassmann-odd) operator. The supersymmetries together with the global
SO(3)\times SO(7)
form either a gauged
F(4)
or a gauged

superalgebra in Lorentzian signature.

14

14

The anticommutator of two supersymmetries takes exactly the form expected from

Komatsu:2024ydh
.
Note that as in IKKT,

can be scaled out of the integral, by rescaling

and
\psi_{\alpha}=g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{3/4}\tilde{\psi}_{\alpha}
. This leaves only
\Omega\equiv\mu/\sqrt{g_{\mathrm{YM}}}
as a mass-parameter.

The local minima of the polarized IKKT integral are given by configurations satisfying

\psi=0,\qquad X_{p}=0\,,\qquad X_{i}=\frac{3\mu}{8}L_{i}\,,\qquad[L_{i},L_{j}]=i\epsilon_{ijk}L_{k}\,.

(27)

By the last condition,

needs to form an
N
-dimensional representation of the
SU(2)
Lie algebra, which can be reducible. The number of (inequivalent) saddles is thus given by

, the number of partitions of
N
. For a given saddle parametrized by some irreducible representation

, the on-shell action takes the value

S_{\mathrm{pIKKT}}^{\mathrm{on-shell}}=-\frac{9\mu^{4}}{2^{13}g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}L_{i}L_{i}\,.

(28)

In particular, the dominant saddle which maximizes
\mathrm{Tr}L_{i}L_{i}
is the
N
-dimensional irreducible representation of
SU(2)
.

The knowledge of these minima is useful for two reasons. The first reason is the limit
\Omega^{2}=\mu^{2}/g_{\mathrm{YM}}\to\infty
, in which one can use a saddle point approximation to compute the observables, and they all reduce to evaluating fluctuations around the irreducible saddle
Hartnoll:2024csr
. The second reason is that using supersymmetric localization methods, one can reduce the partition function and protected observables to sums over all the saddles. In particular, this conforms well with the conjecture that each saddle point should be dual to specific gravitational solutions, and indeed a one-to-one map is proposed in
Komatsu:2024ydh
;
Komatsu:2024bop
.

Let us now discuss observables, in dimensionless units where the only tunable parameter is
\Omega\equiv\mu/\sqrt{g_{\mathrm{YM}}}
. Firstly, the fixed
N
,
\Omega\to 0

210
).
In particular, this analysis yields

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}Z_{SU(N),\mathrm{pIKKT}}\xrightarrow{\Omega\to 0}\frac{(2\pi)^{5(N^{2}-1)}}{\prod_{k=1}^{N}(k!)}\left(\frac{2^{9}}{3\sqrt{3}\Omega^{2}}\right)^{N-1}\,,\\
\langle\mathrm{Tr}X_{1}^{2p}\rangle\xrightarrow{\Omega\to 0}N\left(\frac{2^{6}(N-1)}{3\Omega^{2}N}\right)^{p}\frac{\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,,\\
\langle\mathrm{Tr}X_{4}^{2p}\rangle\xrightarrow{\Omega\to 0}N\left(\frac{2^{6}(N-1)}{\Omega^{2}N}\right)^{p}\frac{\Gamma(p+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,.\end{gathered}

Note that these observables all diverge, whereas in IKKT we know a few of them converge. This discontinuity is due to fermion mass terms, as was first mentioned in
Austing:2001ib
in a similar setup and we reviewed in
Komatsu:2024ydh
.

The large
\Omega

\Omega\gg N^{1/4}
was obtained in
Hartnoll:2024csr
yielding

Z_{U(N),\mathrm{pIKKT}}\overset{\Omega\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{(2\pi)^{5N^{2}+(N-1)/2}}{\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}k!}\frac{2^{9}}{3\sqrt{3}N\sqrt{N}\Omega^{2}}\prod_{J=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{3J+2}{3J+1}\right)^{3}e^{\frac{9\Omega^{4}}{2^{15}}(N^{3}-N)}\,.

(32)

The above computations unfortunately lie outside the region of validity of supergravity. With the use of supersymmetry, localization methods can however significantly simplify the (analytic and numerical) computation of protected observables of the form
\langle f(X_{3}-iX_{10})\rangle
at arbitrary values of
\Omega
and
N
, and in particular access the supergravity regime. Its result gives significant support for the conjecture that each saddle is dual to a geometry, since it is possible to match equations from both sides for each saddle
Komatsu:2024ydh
. The localization result has also been recently analyzed in the statistical sense in
Hartnoll:2025ecj

The supersymmetry transformations take the form

5.1
Type I:
SO(6)\to SO(3)\times SO(3)

The action of the type I model takes the form

\begin{split}S_{D=6,\mathrm{type\ I}}=\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{2}}\operatorname{Tr}&\Biggl[-\frac{1}{4}[X_{I},X_{J}]^{2}-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{ab}\bar{\psi}_{b}\Gamma^{I}[X_{I},\psi_{a}]\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta_{\epsilon}X^{I}&=\epsilon^{ab}\bar{\epsilon}_{b}\Gamma^{I}\psi_{a}\,,\\

\psi=0,\qquad X_{p}=0\,,\qquad X_{i}=\frac{3\mu}{8}L_{i}\,,\qquad[L_{i},L_{j}]=i\epsilon_{ijk}L_{k}\,.

Type II:

In four dimensions, we consider four Hermitian bosonic matrices

The pure SYM matrix integral in

8
Discussion

In this work we have presented a complete classification of mass deformations of the SYM matrix models in various
D
that keep the number of supersymmetries unchanged and retain gauge invariance, as summarized in table
1
. This analysis establishes the uniqueness of the polarized IKKT model. In
D=4
we identified two deformations with a positive semi-definite Pfaffian. The first, type I, serves as a useful benchmark, featuring a single mass parameter and only a trivial saddle, while the second, type II, has a richer structure with two mass parameters and a dominating fuzzy-sphere saddle. Numerical simulations of these toy models would be of considerable interest.

Moreover, we derived only a partial set of analytic expressions for these new models. A full supersymmetric localization treatment along the lines of
Pestun:2007rz
;
Asano:2012zt
;
Komatsu:2024ydh
remains to be developed. In particular, one could compute the partition functions, as well as protected observables of the form
\langle f(X+iY)\rangle
where
X
and
Y
are two distinct bosonic matrices among
X_{I=1,\ldots,D}
. For example, assume
f
is a homogeneous polynomial in
X+iY
of degree
k\neq 0
. This would be an interesting observable when the
SO(2)
symmetry which rotates
X
and
Y
is broken by the mass-deformation. Otherwise, these expectation values would vanish identically as they carry a charge
k\neq 0
under
SO(2)
. Therefore, in the
D=4
type I model and the
D=3
model where
SO(D)
is preserved, only the partition function can be computed thanks to supersymmetric localization. However, in the other models which all break
SO(D)
, it would be interesting to compute e.g. the observables
\langle\mathrm{Tr}(X_{1}+iX_{D})^{2n}\rangle
which would yield a non-trivial result.

Finally, let us mention that our motivation for this work was based on finding a variety of toy models that would mimic features of polarized IKKT. However, the matrix models we constructed may have physical relevance in their own right, or at least describe some sector of models of physical interest. Both are true in the case of massive SYM quantum mechanics, as explained in
Kim:2006wg
. These quantum mechanical models were argued to be dual to non-critical M-theory on curved backgrounds. Moreover, some of them could be obtained from M5-brane embeddings in the 11-dimensional pp-wave geometry, or from compactifying four-dimensional SYM on a three-sphere.
21
21

21

BMN arises from a sector of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM while some of the other massive SYM quantum mechanical models arise from four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
and
\mathcal{N}=1
.
It would be interesting to explore whether there exist analogous physical setups which are dual or reduce to our matrix models.

Appendix B
Pfaffian positivity in four dimensions

In this appendix, we review the proof that the Pfaffian in the
D=4
SYM matrix model is positive semi-definite
Ambjorn:2000bf
, and extend it to the mass-deformed models. Switching from the Majorana to the Weyl representation as discussed in appendix
D.3
, the undeformed integral over fermions reduces to

\int\left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{2}\prod_{A=1}^{N^{2}-1}d\chi_{A,\alpha}^{*}d\chi_{A,\alpha}\right)\mathrm{exp}(-\chi_{A}^{\dagger}(i\underbrace{f_{ABC}X_{I}^{C}}_{\equiv(\mathbf{X}_{I})_{AB}}\bar{\gamma}^{I})\chi_{B})=\mathrm{det}(\underbrace{i\bar{\gamma}^{I}\otimes\mathbf{X}_{I}}_{\equiv A(X)})\,,

(186)

where in our representation of
\bar{\gamma}^{I}
(
233
), the matrix
A
takes the form

A(X)\equiv\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf{X}_{4}+i\mathbf{X}_{3}&\mathbf{X}_{2}+i\mathbf{X}_{1}\\
-\mathbf{X}_{2}+i\mathbf{X}_{1}&-\mathbf{X}_{4}-i\mathbf{X}_{3}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(187)

The key idea to show that
\det A
is positive semi-definite is to use the fact that when we deal with
Euclidean
gamma matrices in 4 dimensions, there exists a 2 by 2 matrix
\tilde{B}
such that
\tilde{B}\bar{\gamma}^{I}\tilde{B}=-(\bar{\gamma}^{I})^{*}
and
\tilde{B}^{2}=-\mathbb{1}_{2}
. In our representation this matrix is simply
\tilde{B}=i\sigma^{2}
. This implies that there exists a matrix
J=\tilde{B}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{N^{2}-1}
such that

J\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb{1}_{N^{2}-1}\\
-\mathbb{1}_{N^{2}-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad J^{2}=-\mathbb{1}_{N^{2}-1}\quad\implies JA(X)J=-A(X)^{*}\,.

(188)

This property implies that if
v
is an eigenvector of the matrix
A
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, then
(Jv)^{*}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\lambda^{*}
. Note also that
v\neq 0\implies(Jv)^{*}\neq v
, so the two eigenvectors are always different. This means that every eigenvalue
always
comes with another one which is its complex conjugate. Thus, the determinant is a product of many pairs
\lambda\lambda^{*}
and is always positive semi-definite. One may be tempted to argue similarly for
D=6
since the Pfaffian also reduces to a determinant in this case
Krauth:1998xh
. However, in
D=6
, such a matrix
\tilde{B}
does not exist.
Let us now generalize this proof to the mass-deformed models.

Type I:

For the type I theory the fermionic integral in the Majorana representation yields (in dimensionless coordinates
\mu\to\Omega
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}\prod_{A=1}^{N^{2}-1}d\psi_{A,\alpha}\right)&\mathrm{exp}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\psi_{A\alpha}(-f_{ABC}X_{C}^{I}(\mathcal{C}\Gamma^{I})_{\alpha\beta}+2\Omega\mathcal{C}_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{AB})\psi_{B\beta}\right)\\
&=\mathrm{Pf}\underbrace{(-(\mathcal{C}\Gamma^{I})\otimes\mathbf{X}_{I}+2\Omega\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{N^{2}-1})}_{\equiv\mathcal{M}_{4,\mathrm{I}}(X)}\,.\end{split}

(189)

It turns out that it is again better to go to Weyl representation, but the action contains terms of the form
\chi^{*}i\sigma^{2}\chi^{*}
and
-\chi i\sigma^{2}\chi
. Thus the Pfaffian does not reduce to a determinant. Doing so we obtain

\mathrm{Pf}\mathcal{M}_{4,\mathrm{I}}(X)=\mathrm{Pf}\begin{pmatrix}0&2i\Omega&-\mathbf{X}_{3}-i\mathbf{X}_{4}&-\mathbf{X}_{1}+i\mathbf{X}_{2}\\
-2i\Omega&0&-\mathbf{X}_{1}-i\mathbf{X}_{2}&\mathbf{X}_{3}-i\mathbf{X}_{4}\\
-\mathbf{X}_{3}-i\mathbf{X}_{4}&-\mathbf{X}_{1}-i\mathbf{X}_{2}&0&-2i\Omega\\
-\mathbf{X}_{1}+i\mathbf{X}_{2}&\mathbf{X}_{3}-i\mathbf{X}_{4}&2i\Omega&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(190)

\Omega

(192)

(194)

D.2
Six-dimensional gamma matrices

In 5+1 dimensions, one can do similarly to 9+1 dimensions and parameterize the
8\times 8
gamma matrices by first defining
4\times 4
Weyl matrices
\gamma^{\mu},\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}
which are given by

\gamma^{0}=-\bar{\gamma}^{0}=-\mathbb{1}_{8}\,,\qquad\gamma^{I}=\bar{\gamma}^{I}\,,\qquad(I=1,\ldots,5)

(219)

where
\gamma^{I}
are
SO(5)
matrices satisfying
\{\gamma^{I},\gamma^{J}\}=2\delta^{IJ}

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\gamma^{1}=\sigma^{3}\otimes\sigma^{1}\,,\qquad\gamma^{2}=\sigma^{3}\otimes\sigma^{2}\,,\qquad\gamma^{3}=\sigma^{3}\otimes\sigma^{3}\,,\\
\gamma^{4}=-\sigma^{2}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}\,,\qquad\gamma^{5}=\sigma^{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}\,.\end{gathered}

We then define the
SO(5,1)

\Gamma_{*}
as

\Gamma^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\gamma^{\mu}\\
\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\Gamma_{*}=\Gamma^{0}\Gamma^{1}\ldots\Gamma^{5}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{1}_{4}&0\\
0&-\mathbb{1}_{4}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(222)

Now the matrices
B
and
\mathcal{C}
satisfying (

B=\Gamma^{2}\Gamma^{4}\,,\qquad\mathcal{C}=iB\Gamma^{0}\,.

(223)

At this point, one can impose a Weyl condition
\Gamma_{*}\psi=\psi
, which would leave 8 degrees of freedom
\psi_{\alpha=1,\ldots,4}
and
\psi^{*}_{\alpha=1,\ldots,4}
. However, a Majorana condition
\psi=B^{-1}\psi^{*}

which is not the case here (
B^{*}B=-\mathbb{1}_{8}
). To get around this, let us have two Weyl fermions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}

\psi_{1}^{*}
,
\psi_{2}^{*}

\psi_{a}=\epsilon^{ab}B^{-1}\psi_{b}^{*}\,,\quad(a,b=1,2)

where

\epsilon^{12}=1
. Now this condition is consistent thanks to
\epsilon^{ij}\epsilon^{jk}=-\delta^{ik}
which effectively cancels the minus sign that we had in
B^{*}B=1
. This condition allows to write
\psi_{1}^{*}
and
\psi_{2}^{*}

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
for a total number of 8 degrees of freedom. This representation of the spinors is the so-called symplectic Majorana-Weyl representation

;
Kugo:1982bn
;
Park:1998nra

\psi_{2},\psi_{2}^{*}
in terms of a single Weyl spinor and its conjugate
\psi_{1},\psi_{1}^{*}
.

Finally, the Wick rotation is performed by setting

\Gamma^{6}=i\Gamma^{0}\qquad\{\Gamma^{I},\Gamma^{J}\}=2\delta^{IJ}\,,\qquad(I,J=1,\ldots,6)

(225)

and keeping
\mathcal{C}=iB\Gamma^{0}
,

.

D.3
Four-dimensional gamma matrices

In
3+1

D=3,10

We choose a convention for the gamma matrices which makes the Majorana condition manifest. Namely, we define the four real
4\times 4
gamma matrices as well as the chiral matrix
\Gamma_{*}

\Gamma^{0}=i\sigma^{2}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}\,,\qquad\Gamma^{1}=\sigma^{3}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2}\,,\qquad\Gamma^{2}=\sigma^{1}\otimes\sigma^{3}\,,\qquad\Gamma^{3}=\sigma^{1}\otimes\sigma^{1}\,,

such that

\{\Gamma^{\mu},\Gamma^{\nu}\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}\,.

(228)

Since these are real matrices we have

\psi=\psi^{*}

\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}=i\Gamma^{0}\,.

(229)

The Wick rotation is performed by setting

\Gamma^{4}=i\Gamma^{0}\,,\qquad\{\Gamma^{I},\Gamma^{J}\}=2\delta^{IJ}\qquad(I,J=1,\ldots,4)

Let us now discuss how to map the Majorana representation to the Weyl representation. If one has a real spinor

\gamma^{\mu},\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}

1&-i&-i&1\\
-i&-1&1&i\\

(234)

Appendix E
Matrix integral conventions

Here, we give details on the normalization conventions we use for the matrices and the integral measures. These conventions are the same as the one used in
Komatsu:2024ydh
. We expand our Hermitian (traceless)
N\times N
matrices
X_{I},\psi_{\alpha}
in terms of the
U(N)
(
SU(N)
) generators in the defining representation.

X_{I}=X_{I}^{A}T^{A}\,,\qquad\psi_{\alpha}=\psi_{\alpha}^{A}T^{A}\,.

(238)

The generators
T^{A}
are defined as a basis of
N\times N
Hermitian (traceless) matrices satisfying

\mathrm{Tr}T^{A}T^{B}=\delta^{AB}\,.

(239)

Given these generators, one defines the structure constants
f_{ABC}
from
[T^{A},T^{B}]=if^{ABC}T^{C}
.
The measure is defined as

[dX_{I}]\equiv\prod_{A=1}^{N^{2}}dX_{I}^{A}\,,\qquad[d\psi_{\alpha}]\equiv\prod_{A=1}^{N^{2}}d\psi_{\alpha}^{A}\,,\qquad(U(N))

(240)

[dX_{I}]\equiv\prod_{A=1}^{N^{2}-1}dX_{I}^{A}\,,\qquad[d\psi_{\alpha}]\equiv\prod_{A=1}^{N^{2}-1}d\psi_{\alpha}^{A}\,.\qquad(SU(N))

(241)

The reason why we give our preference to this convention is that the measure satisfies the nice properties

\int[dX]e^{-\lambda\mathrm{Tr}X^{2}}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\right)^{N^{2}/2}&(U(N))\\
\left(\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\right)^{(N^{2}-1)/2}&(SU(N))\\
\end{cases}\,,

(242)

\int[d\eta][d\psi]e^{-\lambda\mathrm{Tr}\eta\psi}=\begin{cases}\lambda^{N^{2}}&(U(N))\\
\lambda^{N^{2}-1}&(SU(N))\end{cases}\,,

(243)

as if these were simple
N^{2}
or
N^{2}-1
bosonic and fermionic Gaussian integrals.

IRP2 contributes to COPD-associated hypoxic epithelial injury through regulation of Sestrin2-related stress responses and ferroptosis
Authors: Chunyan Xiong, Min Song, Tianshu He, Yao Ma, Jiang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is characterized by persistent oxidative stress and airway epithelial injury. However, the molecular mechanisms linking iron metabolism and cellular stress responses in COPD remain unclear. This study aimed to investigate the role of iron regulatory protein 2 (IRP2) in COPD-associated epithelial injury. 16HBE and A549 cells were exposed to cigarette smoke extract (CSE) under a COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment. IRP2 was silenced using shRNA, and Sestrin2 restoration experiments were performed. Cellular injury was evaluated by cell viability, ROS production, inflammatory cytokines, intracellular Fe²⁺ accumulation, ferroptosis-related proteins, and NRF2 activation. CSE exposure under a COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment induced epithelial injury, accompanied by increased oxidative stress, inflammation, iron accumulation, and ferroptosis-related changes. IRP2 silencing alleviated these alterations and enhanced NRF2 nuclear activation with increased expression of antioxidant proteins. Restoration of Sestrin2 partially reversed the protective effects induced by IRP2 depletion. Furthermore, pharmacological inhibition of ferroptosis attenuated epithelial injury. IRP2 contributes to COPD-associated epithelial injury by regulating oxidative stress, iron homeostasis, and ferroptosis-related responses, providing new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying COPD progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10616-026-01066-9.Keywords: COPD, IRP2, Sestrin2, HIF-1α, Hypoxia

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a major non-communicable respiratory disorder characterized by persistent, largely irreversible airflow limitation. Affecting approximately 10% of the adult population worldwide, COPD ranks among the top three causes of death globally, resulting in nearly three million deaths annually (Holtjer et al. 2023). COPD develops through complex interactions between environmental exposures, particularly cigarette smoke exposure, and host susceptibility factors, leading to chronic airway inflammation, alveolar destruction, and progressive impairment of pulmonary function (Forder et al. 2023).

Oxidative stress is a critical pathological feature of COPD and contributes to airway epithelial injury through excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and inflammatory activation (Barnes 2020). Increasing evidence indicates that disturbed iron homeostasis may further exacerbate oxidative damage by promoting iron-dependent ROS generation. Iron regulatory protein 2 (IRP2) is a post-transcriptional regulator of cellular iron homeostasis (Jeong et al. 2025). However, whether IRP2 contributes to COPD-associated epithelial injury through regulation of iron metabolism and cellular stress responses remains unclear.

During COPD progression, airway epithelial cells are exposed to a complex disease-associated microenvironment, including reduced oxygen availability and persistent oxidative stress. Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) and nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2) are important regulators involved in cellular adaptation and antioxidant defense under stress conditions (Semenza 2012, Cui et al. 2018). Sestrin2, a stress-responsive protein, has also been implicated in oxidative stress regulation and metabolic adaptation (Budanov 2011). However, the interaction among IRP2, Sestrin2, HIF-1α, and NRF2 during COPD-associated epithelial injury remains largely unknown.

In this study, we investigated the role of IRP2 in COPD-associated epithelial injury using CSE-treated airway epithelial cell models under a disease-relevant hypoxic microenvironment. We evaluated the effects of IRP2 modulation on oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, iron homeostasis, and ferroptosis-related changes. Furthermore, rescue experiments and pharmacological interventions were performed to explore the involvement of Sestrin2-associated stress responses and NRF2-dependent antioxidant signaling in IRP2-mediated epithelial injury.

Materials and methods

Cell Culture and Establishment of the COPD Model

Human bronchial epithelial cells (16HBE) and the human alveolar epithelial cell line (A549) were purchased from Wuhan Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. Cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were used for all experiments.

To establish an in vitro COPD model, cells were treated with 10% CSE for 24 h. For hypoxic stimulation, cells were subsequently cultured in a tri-gas incubator maintained at 37 °C with a gas composition of 10% O₂, 5% CO₂, and 85% N₂ for 48 h.

Preparation of CSE

CSE was freshly prepared prior to each experiment. Smoke from ten commercial cigarettes (Diamond brand) was drawn through 50 mL of serum-free RPMI-1640 medium under controlled vacuum pressure using a custom-built smoking apparatus. The resulting solution was designated as 100% CSE, adjusted to pH 7.0, filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane, and stored at 4 °C. A 10% CSE working solution was prepared by dilution with complete medium immediately before use.

Cell Transfection

For gene silencing, short-hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting human IRP2 and Sestrin2, along with corresponding negative controls, were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Transfections were carried out using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

shRNAs targeting human IRP2 and Sestrin2 were designed based on previously validated sequences. The shRNA sequences were cloned into the pLKO.1 vector. A non-targeting shRNA sequence was used as the negative control (sh-NC). The target sequences were as follows: sh-IRP2, 5′-GATCTTACAGTTGACCATTCT-3′; sh-Sestrin2, 5′-GAGATGGAGAGCCGCTTT-3′; and sh-NC, 5′-CCTAAGGTTAAGTCGCCTCG-3′. The constructed plasmids were transfected into 16HBE and A549 cells according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Briefly, cells were seeded to reach 80–90% confluence and incubated with transfection complexes for 24 h before subsequent treatments. Knockdown efficiency was verified by qRT-PCR and Western blot analysis.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using RNAiso Plus reagent (Takara, Japan). After DNase I treatment, complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the One Step PrimeScript™ RT-PCR Kit (Takara) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. qRT-PCR was performed on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method, with GAPDH as the internal control.(Table 1)

Cell Viability Assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Japan). Cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁴ cells/well and cultured for 12, 24, and 48 h. At each time point, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, USA), and viability was expressed as a percentage relative to control.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

The concentrations of IL-1β and TNF-α in cell supernatants were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Elabscience, Wuhan, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Absorbance was recorded at 450 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer, and cytokine levels were normalized to total cellular protein content.

Measurement of Intracellular Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)

Intracellular ROS production was quantified using the fluorescent probe DCFH-DA (Beyotime, China). Cells were incubated with 10 µM DCFH-DA at 37 °C for 15 min in the dark, washed three times with serum-free medium, and immediately imaged under a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Germany) at excitation/emission wavelengths of 488/525 nm.

Assessment of Intracellular Fe²⁺ Levels

Labile ferrous iron (Fe²⁺) accumulation was evaluated using the FerroOrange fluorescence probe (Dojindo). Cells were incubated with working solution for 30 min at 37 °C, rinsed with cold PBS, and imaged under a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Germany). Fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, USA).

Evaluation of Mitochondrial Membrane Potential

Mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) was assessed using the JC-1 Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Assay Kit (Beyotime). Cells were incubated with JC-1 working solution (1×) for 20 min at 37 °C, washed with buffer, and observed under a fluorescence microscope. The ratio of red (aggregated) to green (monomeric) fluorescence was used as an indicator of ΔΨm integrity.

Statistical Analysis

All experiments were independently repeated at least three times. Data are presented as mean ± SD, n = 3 represents three independent biological replicates. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). Differences between two groups were assessed using the Student’s t-test, and multiple-group comparisons were analyzed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Establishment of a COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment model in airway epithelial cells

COPD is characterized by chronic airway epithelial injury accompanied by a pathological microenvironment involving persistent inflammation, oxidative stress, and hypoxemia. To mimic the airway epithelial environment associated with COPD, 16HBE and A549 cells were exposed to CSE and cultured under hypoxic conditions to reproduce the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment.

As shown in Fig. 1A and B, compared with control cells, CSE exposure resulted in significant reduction of cell viability in both 16HBE and A549 cells. Under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment, epithelial injury was further enhanced, as evidenced by decreased cell viability.

Consistently, ROS detection demonstrated increased oxidative accumulation in CSE-treated cells under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment (Fig. 1C and D). ELISA analysis further showed that inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β and TNF-α, were elevated under this disease-relevant microenvironment (Fig. 1E and F). The expression patterns of IRP2 and Sestrin2 were altered under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment (Fig. 1G), suggesting the involvement of these stress-responsive molecules in COPD-related epithelial injury.

Furthermore, Fe²⁺ fluorescence analysis revealed increased intracellular ferrous ion accumulation under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment (Fig. 1H), indicating disturbed iron homeostasis during COPD-associated epithelial injury. JC-1 staining further demonstrated decreased mitochondrial membrane potential, as indicated by reduced JC-1 aggregate formation and increased JC-1 monomer fluorescence (Fig. 1I), suggesting enhanced cellular stress under the disease-associated microenvironment. Together, these findings establish an in vitro model that recapitulates key features of COPD-associated epithelial injury within a hypoxic disease microenvironment.

Under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment, IRP2 knockdown significantly improved cell viability in both 16HBE and A549 cells (Fig. 2B-C). In addition, depletion of IRP2 markedly reduced intracellular ROS accumulation (Fig. 2D-E) and decreased the production of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β and TNF-α (Fig. 2F).

Furthermore, IRP2 silencing reduced intracellular Fe²⁺ accumulation and partially preserved mitochondrial membrane potential, as demonstrated by Fe²⁺ fluorescence and JC-1 staining assays (Fig. 2G-H).

Collectively, these findings indicate that IRP2 contributes to oxidative stress, inflammatory activation, iron accumulation, and mitochondrial membrane potential alteration in airway epithelial cells under a COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment.

Sestrin2 silencing significantly affected cellular responses under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment. Compared with control cells, Sestrin2 depletion increased cell viability and reduced ROS accumulation in both 16HBE and A549 cells (Fig. 3B-E). In addition, Sestrin2 knockdown decreased the production of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β and TNF-α (Fig. 3F).

However, compared with IRP2 silencing, the effects of Sestrin2 depletion on Fe²⁺ accumulation and mitochondrial membrane potential alterations were relatively limited (Fig. 3G-H), suggesting that Sestrin2 may mainly participate in regulating oxidative and inflammatory stress responses rather than directly controlling all aspects of iron homeostasis.

IRP2 is associated with Sestrin2 regulation in COPD-associated hypoxic cells

To explore the relationship between IRP2 and Sestrin2, their reciprocal expression was analyzed after individual knockdown. qRT-PCR analysis showed that IRP2 silencing significantly reduced Sestrin2 mRNA expression, whereas Sestrin2 knockdown did not markedly affect IRP2 transcript levels (Fig. 4A-B). Consistent with the transcriptional results, western blot analysis demonstrated that IRP2 depletion decreased Sestrin2 protein expression, while Sestrin2 silencing had minimal effects on IRP2 protein levels (Fig. 4C-D). These findings suggest that Sestrin2 may function as a downstream component associated with IRP2-mediated cellular stress regulation under COPD-associated hypoxic conditions.

IRP2 modulates Sestrin2-associated stress responses and NRF2 antioxidant signaling

To further investigate the downstream stress-response pathways associated with IRP2, we examined the relationship between IRP2, Sestrin2, HIF-1α, and NRF2 in airway epithelial cells.

In 16HBE and A549 cells, IRP2 knockdown significantly reduced Sestrin2 expression, accompanied by alterations in HIF-1α and NRF2 levels, suggesting that IRP2 is involved in regulating hypoxia- and oxidative stress-associated signaling responses (Fig. 5A, B). Similarly, Sestrin2 silencing also affected the expression of HIF-1α and NRF2, indicating that Sestrin2 participates in this stress-responsive network.

Because NRF2 activity is mainly determined by its nuclear translocation rather than total protein abundance, we further evaluated NRF2 localization and downstream antioxidant responses. Under CSE plus hypoxia stimulation, NRF2 nuclear accumulation and the expression of antioxidant proteins HO-1 and NQO1 were altered. IRP2 or Sestrin2 knockdown modulated NRF2 nuclear localization and antioxidant responses, suggesting that IRP2/Sestrin2-associated signaling influences NRF2-dependent cellular defense mechanisms (Fig. 5C).

To further determine whether Sestrin2 participates in IRP2-associated iron-dependent cellular responses, ferroptosis-related indicators were examined following Sestrin2 restoration in IRP2-silenced cells.

Compared with sh-IRP2 and sh-IRP2 + vector groups, Sestrin2 overexpression significantly increased intracellular Fe²⁺ accumulation and enhanced inflammatory cytokine production, including IL-1β and TNF-α (Fig. 6A-C).

Furthermore, restoration of Sestrin2 partially reversed the ferroptosis-associated molecular changes induced by IRP2 silencing. Specifically, Sestrin2 overexpression increased ACSL4, TfR1, and DMT1 expression, while reducing GPX4 and SLC7A11 levels. Alterations in iron storage and export-related proteins, including FTH1 and FPN1, were also observed (Fig. 6D). These findings suggest that Sestrin2 contributes to IRP2-associated regulation of ferroptosis-related responses under COPD-associated hypoxic conditions.

Ferroptosis inhibition attenuates COPD-associated hypoxic epithelial injury

To further verify the contribution of ferroptosis to COPD-associated epithelial injury, cells were treated with the ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1). Compared with control cells, exposure to the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment markedly reduced cell viability, whereas Fer-1 treatment significantly restored cell viability (Fig. 7A). Consistently, Fer-1 treatment decreased intracellular ROS accumulation induced by CSE+hypoxia exposure (Fig. 7B). In addition, the elevated intracellular Fe²⁺ levels observed under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment were significantly reduced following ferroptosis inhibition (Fig. 7C). To further confirm the involvement of ferroptosis, ferroptosis-related proteins were examined. CSE+hypoxia exposure decreased the expression of GPX4 and SLC7A11, while increasing ACSL4, TfR1, and DMT1 expression. Fer-1 treatment partially reversed these alterations. Moreover, changes in iron storage and export-associated proteins, including FTH1 and FPN1, were also restored after Fer-1 administration (Fig. 7D). These results demonstrate that ferroptosis contributes to COPD-associated hypoxic epithelial injury and that pharmacological inhibition of ferroptosis provides protective effects.

Discussion

In the present study, we investigated the role of IRP2 in COPD-associated epithelial injury under a disease-relevant hypoxic microenvironment. Our findings demonstrate that IRP2 contributes to airway epithelial stress responses by regulating oxidative stress, inflammatory activation, iron homeostasis, and ferroptosis-related alterations. IRP2 silencing alleviated CSE-induced cellular injury, reduced oxidative stress and intracellular Fe²⁺ accumulation, and altered ferroptosis-associated molecular changes. Furthermore, rescue experiments indicated that Sestrin2 is functionally involved in IRP2-associated stress regulation, while NRF2 nuclear translocation and antioxidant responses were further affected. These findings suggest that IRP2 participates in the regulation of multiple stress-adaptive pathways during COPD-associated epithelial injury.

Exposure to cigarette smoke extract replicated classic COPD-like characteristics, such as excessive ROS generation, mitochondrial depolarization, and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. This is consistent with previous reports indicating that cigarette smoke induces persistent oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction in airway epithelial cells. Importantly, under the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment, these CSE-induced cellular injuries were further aggravated, suggesting that reduced oxygen availability may represent an additional stress factor contributing to epithelial dysfunction.

In addition to cigarette smoke exposure, COPD progression is frequently accompanied by impaired pulmonary gas exchange and chronic hypoxemia, which are associated with ventilation-perfusion mismatch, alveolar destruction, and pulmonary vascular abnormalities (Cornelius 2024, Kent et al. 2011). Therefore, the COPD-associated hypoxic microenvironment established in our study was designed to mimic a disease-relevant condition rather than represent an independent initiating factor of COPD. Compared with previous studies that mainly focused on the direct effects of cigarette smoke exposure, our findings suggest that the interaction between cigarette smoke-induced stress and disease-associated oxygen limitation may further influence epithelial stress adaptation. This provides a possible explanation for clinical observations showing that chronic hypoxemia is associated with accelerated lung function decline and poor outcomes in COPD patients (Mikami et al. 2023).

One of the major findings of this study was the involvement of IRP2 in COPD-associated epithelial stress responses. IRP2 is a cytoplasmic RNA-binding protein that post-transcriptionally regulates iron metabolism by stabilizing transferrin receptor and repressing ferritin mRNAs (Yao et al. 2024). Dysregulated IRP2 activity can increase the labile iron pool and catalyze ROS formation via the Fenton reaction (Fillebeen and Pantopoulos 2002). In our study, IRP2 silencing restored mitochondrial potential, reduced ROS and cytokine release, and alleviated Fe²⁺ overload, demonstrating its direct role in hypoxia-driven oxidative injury. These results align with the work of Cloonan et al. 2016, who showed that mitochondrial iron chelation or IRP2 deletion attenuates emphysematous damage in smoke-exposed mice. Moreover, genetic association studies have linked IREB2 variants to COPD susceptibility and disease severity (Zeng et al. 2020). Consistent with these findings, our study demonstrated that IRP2 silencing reduced intracellular Fe²⁺ accumulation, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses in airway epithelial cells. Mechanistically, hypoxia may stabilize IRP2 by limiting iron-sulfur cluster formation and altering proteasomal degradation (Ghosh et al. 2008). However, our findings further suggest that IRP2 may regulate a broader cellular stress response network involving iron imbalance, oxidative injury, and ferroptosis-related alterations. These results extend the current understanding of IRP2 from a classical iron metabolism regulator to a potential coordinator of COPD-associated epithelial stress responses.

Sestrin2 is widely recognized as a stress-inducible antioxidant protein that protects cells against oxidative damage through regulation of AMPK activation, mTOR inhibition, autophagy, and redox homeostasis (Guo et al. 2025, Kim et al. 2013). A recent study has suggested that Sestrin2 may regulate ferroptosis and oxidative stress through NRF2-associated antioxidant pathways (Ru et al. 2024). Our findings indicate that Sestrin2 restoration partially reversed the protective effects induced by IRP2 silencing under COPD-associated stress conditions. This discrepancy may reflect differences in disease models and stress intensity. Importantly, our results suggest that Sestrin2 participates in IRP2-associated stress regulation, although the precise molecular hierarchy among IRP2, Sestrin2, HIF-1α, and NRF2 requires further investigation.

HIF-1α and NRF2 are key regulators of cellular adaptation to hypoxic and oxidative stress. Previous studies have demonstrated that HIF-1α mainly mediates metabolic adaptation under low oxygen conditions, whereas NRF2 activates antioxidant defense programs. This aligns with previous evidence that HIF-1α activation in COPD promotes glycolytic shift, inflammation, and tissue remodeling (Mauldin et al. 2018, Jackson 2020, Gao et al. 2025). In the present study, IRP2/Sestrin2 modulation affected NRF2 nuclear activation and antioxidant responses, including HO-1 and NQO1 expression. These findings suggest that IRP2 may regulate multiple stress-responsive pathways rather than acting through a single linear signaling cascade. Further studies are needed to clarify the detailed interactions among these pathways.

Recent studies have highlighted ferroptosis as an important mechanism linking iron metabolism disorders and oxidative damage (Meng et al. 2023, Zhu et al. 2024). Consistent with previous reports identifying GPX4, SLC7A11, and ACSL4 as key ferroptosis regulators, our study demonstrated altered ferroptosis-related protein expression and protective effects of Ferrostatin-1 treatment. These findings provide additional evidence that ferroptosis contributes to COPD-associated epithelial injury and may represent an important downstream consequence of iron dysregulation.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. First, the experiments were performed using immortalized airway epithelial cell lines, which cannot fully reproduce the complex microenvironment of COPD. Further validation using primary cells, animal models, and clinical samples is needed. Second, although our findings support the involvement of Sestrin2-associated responses in IRP2-mediated epithelial injury, several mechanistic questions remain unresolved. The present study demonstrates that Sestrin2 restoration partially reverses the protective phenotype induced by IRP2 silencing, suggesting a functional role of Sestrin2 in IRP2-associated cellular responses. However, whether IRP2 directly regulates Sestrin2 expression or affects Sestrin2 through intermediate regulatory factors remains unclear. In addition, the precise relationship between Sestrin2, HIF-1α, and NRF2 requires further investigation. Future studies using additional gain-of-function approaches, molecular interaction assays, and in vivo models will be necessary to fully elucidate the regulatory network underlying COPD-associated epithelial injury. Finally, although ferroptosis-related alterations were observed, additional in vivo studies and complementary ferroptosis assays are required to fully confirm its contribution to COPD progression.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that IRP2 contributes to COPD-associated epithelial injury by regulating oxidative stress, iron homeostasis, and ferroptosis-related responses under a disease-relevant hypoxic microenvironment. Our findings further suggest that IRP2 is associated with Sestrin2-related stress adaptation and NRF2-dependent antioxidant responses. These results provide new insights into the role of iron metabolism and cellular stress regulation in COPD-associated epithelial damage.

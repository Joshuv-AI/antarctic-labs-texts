Continuum damping of topologically-protected edge modes at the boundary of non-uniformly magnetized cylindrical plasmas Publication type: Paper
Roopendra Singh Rajawat 1,∗ and Gennady Shvets 1

Abstract
We present an extension of recently discovered collisionlessly damped yet topologically protected surface plasma waves (TSPWs), from a simplified slab geometry to a cylindrical plasma–vacuum interface. A distinctive feature of these modes—emerging above the electron cyclotron frequency—is their collisionless damping, which arises from resonant coupling to a continuum of upper-hybrid modes localized within a smooth plasma–vacuum transition layer. We demonstrate both temporal and spatial damping of TSPWs at the boundary of a cylindrical magnetized plasma. Furthermore, we show that in the presence of a non-uniform magnetic field, the TSPW exhibits a smooth and continuous transition, highlighting its topological robustness under magnetic inhomogeneity.

magnetized plasma, topological surface plasma waves, collisionless damping, upper-hybrid resonances

3
TSPW’s modelling in a realistic magnetic field profile

Previously, we have theoretically investigated the dispersion and propagation characteristics of both undamped and damped topological surface plasma waves (TSPWs), employing a linearized cold plasma model. In this framework, the external magnetic field is assumed to be stationary and uniform, thereby allowing the parallel wavenumber
k_{z}
to be fixed and a corresponding dispersion relation to be derived. In this section, we explore spatio-temporal evolution of TSPW’s in an expanding magnetic field. The presence of an inhomogeneous magnetic field introduces several fundamental questions that are absent in systems with stationary and uniform external fields. In particular, it remains unclear how TSPW modes evolve under spatially varying magnetic field conditions when the translational symmetry in the axial direction is broken. For example, it is not yet established whether the azimuthal mode number
\rm m
or the axial wavenumber
\rm k_{z}
undergo modifications in response to changes in the external magnetic field. Although the radial component of the magnetic field may be relatively small, it can nonetheless have a significant impact on TSPW’s propagation characteristics.

\mathrm{L_{x}\times L_{y}\times L_{z}}=\mathrm{20~\mathrm{cm}\times 20~\mathrm{cm}\times 90\,~\mathrm{cm}}
, has perfectly matched layer (PML) boundaries in all directions.

The results of the PIC simulation are shown in Fig.
4
(b), where an azimuthally symmetric mode (
m=0
) is excited. As the mode propagates along the axial direction in a non-uniform magnetic field, a clear reduction in its wavelength (
\lambda_{z}
) is observed, corresponding to an increase in axial wavenumber (
k_{z}
). This behavior agrees well with the isofrequency dispersion relation calculated for the driven mode parameters
(\omega/\omega_{p0},m,k_{z}/k_{p},\omega_{c}/\omega_{p0})=(0.67,0,1,1)
, indicated by the yellow star in Fig.
4
(c). Apart from this wavelength shift, no changes in wave characteristics are observed—the mode remains azimuthally symmetric throughout its propagation.

This compression of the wavelength leads to self-amplification of the wave, as energy becomes concentrated in a shorter wavelength (see black line in Fig.
4
(b), right axis). Upon crossing the resonant layer near
\omega_{c}\lesssim\omega_{dr}
at
z\approx 21.6\,\mathrm{cm}
(marked by the vertical red dashed line in Fig.
4
(a)), the wave undergoes collisionless damping, and an exponential decay in amplitude is observed (”wave-decay” in Fig.
4
(b)).

An additional signature of this damping is the enhancement of the electric field component
E_{z}
around the resonance layer, consistent with resonant coupling to upper-hybrid modes
[
20
]
. This is confirmed by comparing
E_{z}
before and after the resonant region in Fig.
4
(d): the undamped TSPW (left figure) exhibits a broad radial profile, while the damped mode (right figure) exhibits a sharply enhanced and localized field structure.

Unlike the single-resonance spatial damping discussed in the previous section, here the wave encounters multiple resonant layers as it propagates through the magnetically sheared region. The corresponding spatial damping rate is plotted as a solid blue line in Fig.
4
(c) (right axis). As shown previously, our semi-analytic model accurately predicts the damping rate for a relatively smooth plasma–vacuum interfaces with
\delta\ll k_{p}^{-1}
. However, in the presence of a continuum of resonances, no single damping rate is sufficient. Instead, a Budden tunneling coefficient
[
35
]
must be estimated. This analysis is currently under progress and will be reported in future work.

A non-uniform expanding magnetic field used in this study can be used to create a directional localized energy sink, where TSPW dumps all the energy, which can heat the plasma
[
36
]
and impart a net non-zero angular momentum
[
17
]
. This property can be used in variety of applications, such as plasma-based circulators and isolators
[
37
,
38
]
.

It is noteworthy that the magnitude of the axial magnetic field does not change the Chern number of the first topological band gap
[
14
,
16
]
; rather it is the direction of the magnetic field that determines the topological invariant. As a result, it is anticipated that variation of the magnetic field allows a diffractionless transition between distinct TSPW modes. Nonetheless, the impact of the radial magnetic field component on TSPWs remains unclear and will be explored in future work. In the absence of translational symmetry, topological protection of surface waves may be lost, allowing coupling between forward and backward propagating modes (e.g.,
\pm k_{z}
). In the magnetic field configuration considered in this work, the variation in the axial magnetic field is sufficiently gradual such that approximate translational symmetry is preserved, even when waves are coupled with ramp localized upper-hybrid modes (see black line in Fig.
4
(b)). Consequently, no backward-propagating (
-k_{z}
) waves are observed. However, in regimes where the magnetic field has sufficiently sharp shear magnetic field, this symmetry is broken, and backward-propagating waves are observed.

4
Conclusions

In summary, we have established the existence of collisionlessly damped, yet topologically protected surface modes at the boundary of a cylindrical magnetized plasma. The topological surface plasma waves existing in the frequency range
\omega>\omega_{c}
are not only confined radially at plasma-vacuum interface, they are localized axially by spatial damping due to coupling with upper-hybrid resonance. Moreover, using PIC simulations and analytic modelling, we have examined the feasibility of exciting a topological surface plasma waves in a realistic magnetic field profile often observed in laboratory plasmas. Though, current theoretical model does not account radial magnetic field, the isofrequency dispersion relation correctly predicts change in TSPW’s wavelength and subsequent self-amplification. The calculations present here assume mobile electrons in the background of immobile ions, future work will address the finite ion mass
[
39
]
.

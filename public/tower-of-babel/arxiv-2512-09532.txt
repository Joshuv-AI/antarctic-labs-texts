Bochner’s technique in Einstein’s non-symmetric geometry
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
A. Einstein considered a manifold with a non-symmetric (0,2)-tensor G = g + F G=g+F , where g g is a Riemannian metric and F ≠ 0 F\neq 0 , and a connection ∇ \nabla with torsion T T such that ( ∇ X G ) ​ ( Y , Z ) = − G ⁡ ( T ⁡ ( X , Y ) , Z ) (\nabla_{X}G)(Y,Z)=-G(T(X,Y),Z) . Guided by the almost Lie algebroid construction on a vector bundle, we define the basic concepts of Bochner’s technique for Einstein’s non-symmetric geometry, give a clear example of the Einstein’s connection ∇ \nabla , prove Weitzenböck type decomposition formula and obtain vanishing results about the null space of the Bochner and Hodge type Laplacians. Keywords : Einstein’s non-symmetric geometry, curvature operator, almost Lie algebroid; Hodge and Bochner Laplacians, Weitzenböck curvature operator; singular distribution. Since 1923,

[
3

Introducing various versions of his NGT, A. Einstein used a complex basic tensor, with a symmetric real part and a skew-symmetric imaginary part. Beginning in 1950, A. Einstein used a real non-symmetric basic (0,2)-tensor

The same is true for the symmetric part of the connection and torsion tensor, respectively.
In his attempt to construct an unified field theory,
(Einstein’s Gravitational Theory, briefly NGT), A. Einstein

(1)

The Bochner technique in differential geometry and mathematical physics is a powerful analytic method used to prove vanishing theorems
by relating topology of a complete Riemannian manifold to its curvature.
The Bochner technique works for skew-symmetric tensors lying in the kernel of the Hodge Laplacian

2.1
Einstein’s non-symmetric geometry

The basic (0,2)-tensor
G
in a “generalized” Riemannian manifold
(M,G)
is non-symmetric.
It decomposes in two parts,
G=g+F
, the symmetric part
g>0
(called Riemannian metric) and the
skew-symmetric part
F\neq 0
(called fundamental 2-form), where

g(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\big[G(X,Y)+G(Y,X)\big],\quad F(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\big[G(X,Y)-G(Y,X)\big].

(4)

Therefore, we obtain a
(1,1)-tensor
{f}\neq 0
(of not necessarily constant rank) determined by

g(X,fY)=F(X,Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(5)

Since
F
is skew-symmetric, the tensor
{f}
is also skew-symmetric:

g({f}X,Y)=-g(X,{f}Y)
.
We consider linear connections
\nabla
on

M

with a torsion (1,2)-tensor

T(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y].

Denote the torsion (0,3)-tensor with respect to
g
by the same letter,

T(X,Y,Z):=g(T(X,Y),Z)
.

The covariant derivative an
(p,k)
-tensor
S
for
p=0,1
, as an
(p,k+1)
-tensor
\nabla S
, see
[
8
]

(\nabla S)(Y,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})=\nabla_{Y}(S(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))-\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}S(X_{1},\ldots,\nabla_{Y}X_{i},\ldots,X_{k}).

(6)

Using (
4
), (
5
)
and (
6
),
we present the condition (
1
) in the following form, see
[
4
]

\displaystyle(\nabla\,g)(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\big[T(X,Y,Z)+T(X,Z,Y)+T(X,Y,{f}Z)+T(X,Z,{f}Y)\big],

\displaystyle(\nabla\,F)(Z,X,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\big[T(X,Z,Y)-T(X,Y,Z)-T(X,Y,{f}Z)+T(X,Z,{f}Y)\big].

The above equations yield, see

\displaystyle(\nabla\,g)(Z,X,Y)=(\nabla\,F)(X,Y,Z)+(\nabla\,F)(Y,X,Z).

Lemma 1
.

For an Einstein’s connection
\nabla
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\nabla g=0
,

(ii)

K(X,Y,Z)=-K(X,Z,Y)
,

(iii)

(\nabla_{X}F)(Y,Z)=-(\nabla_{Y}F)(X,Z)
,
or
(\nabla_{X}f)Y=-(\nabla_{Y}f)X
.

Proof.

We calculate using (
6
) and (
8
),

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=X(g(Y,Z))-g(\nabla^{g}_{X}Y+K_{X}Y,Z)-g(Y,\nabla^{g}_{X}Z+K_{X}Z)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{g}_{X}\,g)(Y,Z)-g(K_{X}Y,Z)-g(K_{X}Z,Y).

By this and
\nabla^{g}\,g=0
, the conditions (i) and (ii)
are equivalent.
By the equality (
7
),
the conditions (i) and (iii) are equivalent.
∎

The

tensor

of a linear connection
\nabla

and

\nabla^{g}
is given by

K_{X}Y:=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla^{g}_{X}Y.

(8)

It is called the

when

preserves the metric tensor,
\nabla g=0
, in Riemann-Cartan geometry.
Denote the torsion (0,3)-tensor with respect to
g
by the same letter,
K(X,Y,Z):=g(K_{X}Y,Z)

In the case
\nabla g=0
, the tensors

\displaystyle T(X,Y)

\displaystyle=K_{X}Y-K_{Y}X,

(9)

\displaystyle 2\,K(Y,Z,X)

\displaystyle=T(X,Y,Z)+T(Y,Z,X)+T(X,Z,Y).

(10)

The Einstein’s connection
\nabla

is represented in

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{g}_{X}Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}\big[T(X,Y,Z)+T(X,Z,{f}Y)+T(Y,Z,{f}X)\big].

(11)

By (

2\,K(X,Y,Z)=T(X,Y,Z)+T(X,Z,{f}Y)+T(Y,Z,{f}X).

(12)

Comparing (

\displaystyle T(Z,X,Y)+T(Z,Y,X)=T(X,Z,{f}Y)+T(Y,Z,{f}X).

Remark 1
.

Linear connections with
totally skew-symmetric torsion

T(X,Y,Z)=-T(X,Z,Y)=-T(Y,X,Z)
,
have become popular due to the relations with mathematical physics. In this case, the number of preserved supersymmetries is equal to the number of parallel spinors with respect to such a connection.
S. Ivanov S. and M.Lj. Zlatanović, see
[
4

, presented conditions for the existence and uniqueness of the Einstein’s connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion on a manifold

and gave its explicit expression.
In this case, the torsion is determined by

T(X,Y,Z)=-\frac{1}{3}dF(X,Y,Z)

and the difference tensor is given by the following formula:

K_{X}Y=\frac{1}{2}\big[T({f}X,Y)-T(X,{f}Y)+T(X,Y)\big].

(13)

2.2
The almost Lie algebroid structure

Here, we briefly recall the construction of an almost Lie algebroid, following
[
9
,
10
]
. Lie algebroids
constitute an active field of research in differential geometry. Roughly speaking, an (almost) Lie algebroid is a structure, where one replaces the tangent bundle
TM
of a manifold
M
with a new smooth vector bundle

\pi_{\cal E}\colon{\cal E}\to M
of rank
k
, i.e.,
with a fibre
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, over
M
with similar properties.
Many geometrical notions, which involve
TM
, were generalized to the context of Lie algebroids.

An
anchor
on
{\cal E}
is a morphism
\rho\colon{\cal E}\to TM
of vector bundles.
A
skew-symmetric bracket
on
{\cal E}
is a map

[\,\cdot,\,\cdot\,]_{\rho}\colon{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}\times\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\to{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}
such that

[Y,X]_{\rho}=-[X,Y]_{\rho},\quad[X,\psi Y]_{\rho}=\rho(X)(\psi)Y+\psi[X,Y]_{\rho},\quad\rho([X,Y]_{\rho})=[\rho(X),\rho(Y)]

(18)

for all
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}
and
\psi\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
The anchor and the skew-symmetric bracket give
an almost Lie algebroid structure
on
{\cal E}
.
The tensor
\mathcal{J}_{\rho}:{\mathfrak{X}}_{E}\times{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}\times{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}\to{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}
, given by

\mathcal{J}_{\rho}(X,Y,Z)=\sum\nolimits_{\,\mathrm{cycl.}}[X,[Y,Z]_{\rho}\,]_{\rho}
,
called the
Jacobiator
of the bracket, prevents a bracket to satisfy the Jacobi identity.
By (
18
), third formula, we get
\rho\,\mathcal{J}_{\rho}=0
.
An almost Lie algebroid is a Lie algebroid provided that
\mathcal{J}_{\rho}=0
.
Almost Lie algebroids are closely related to singular distributions, i.e., given by
\rho({\cal E})
, see
[
10
]
.
Axiom (
18
), third formula, is equivalent to vanishing of the following operator:

\mathfrak{D}^{\rho}(X,Y)=[\rho X,\rho Y]-\rho([X,Y]_{\rho}).

(19)

There is a bijection between almost Lie algebroids on
{\cal E}
and the exterior differentials of the exterior algebra

\Lambda({\cal E})=\bigoplus_{\,k\in\mathbb{N}}\,\Lambda^{k}({\cal E})
,
here
\Lambda^{k}({\cal E})
is the set of
k
-forms over
{\cal E}
. The exterior differential
d^{\rho}
, corresponding to the almost Lie algebroid structure
({\cal E},\rho,[\,\cdot,\,\cdot\,]_{\rho})
, is given by

\displaystyle d^{\rho}\,\omega(X_{0},\ldots,X_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}(\rho X_{i})(\omega(X_{0},\ldots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{k}))

\displaystyle+\sum\nolimits_{\,0\leq i<j\leq k}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([X_{i},X_{j}]_{\rho},X_{0},\ldots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{j-1},X_{j+1},\ldots,X_{k}),

(20)

where
X_{0},\ldots,X_{k}\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}
and
\omega\in\Lambda^{k}({\cal E})
for
k\geq 0
.
For
k=0
, we have

d^{\rho}f(X)=(\rho X)(f)
, where
X\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{\cal E}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)=\Lambda^{0}({\cal E})
.
Recall that a skew-symmetric bracket defines uniquely an exterior differential
d^{\rho}
on
\Lambda(M)
,
and it gives rise to

– an
almost Lie algebroid
if and only if
(d^{\rho})^{2}f=0
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
;

– a
Lie algebroid
if and only if
(d^{\rho})^{2}f=0=(d^{\rho})^{2}\,\omega
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\omega\in\Lambda^{1}(M)
.

A
\rho
-connection
on
({\cal E},\rho)
is a map
\nabla^{\rho}:\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\times\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}
satisfying Koszul conditions

\nabla^{\rho}_{X}\,(fY+Z)=\rho(X)(f)Y+f\nabla^{\rho}_{X}\,Y+\nabla^{\rho}_{X}\,Z,\quad\nabla^{\rho}_{fX+Z}\,Y=f\nabla^{\rho}_{X}\,Y+\nabla^{\rho}_{Z}\,Y.

(21)

For a
\rho
-connection
\nabla^{\rho}
on
{\cal E}
, they define
torsion

T^{\rho}:\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\times\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}

and
curvature

R^{\rho}:\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\times\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\times\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}\to\mathfrak{X}_{\cal E}
by standard formulas

\displaystyle T^{\rho}(X,Y)=\nabla^{\rho}_{X}\,Y-\nabla^{\rho}_{Y}\,X-[X,Y]_{\rho},\quad R^{\rho}_{X,Y}=\nabla_{X}^{\rho}\nabla^{\rho}_{Y}-\nabla^{\rho}_{Y}\nabla^{\rho}_{X}-\nabla^{\rho}_{\,[X,Y]_{\rho}}.

We will apply the construction of an almost Lie algebroid to
a manifold
(M,G=g+F)

with an Einstein’s connection, namely,
we assume
{\cal E}=TM
and
\rho=f
,
see (
5
).

Conclusion

The main contribution of this paper (four theorems and ten propositions)
is the development of Bochner’s technique for a manifold
(M,G=g+F)
with an Einstein’s connection.
To generalize some geometrical analysis tools for an Einstein’s connection, we assume some additional conditions, for example, (

We define the

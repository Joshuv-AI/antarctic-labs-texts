Solitary calcified neurocysticercosis with leptomeningeal enhancement mimicking tuberculous meningitis: MR spectroscopy as a diagnostic adjunct
Authors: A Rahman Khan, G Shyamnath, K Rajagopalan
Published: 2026

Case description

A previously healthy 9-year-old girl presented with three generalized tonic-clonic seizures following minor head trauma. She was afebrile and hemodynamically stable with transient Todd’s paresis. Admission weight was 21.6 kg (<3rd percentile, WHO standards). Brain CT demonstrated a calcified right temporal lesion without hemorrhage or midline shift.

MRI (1.5 Tesla) revealed a solitary 11 × 10 mm calcified ring-enhancing lesion in the right temporal lobe with marked perilesional edema. Diffuse bilateral sulcal and basal cisternal enhancement was also present, classically described in tuberculous meningitis [1] (Fig. 1A, B). History was non-contributory for tuberculosis exposure; reassessment revealed no meningeal signs or cranial nerve deficits, remaining discordant with the MRI pattern.

Baseline neuroimaging and MR spectroscopy. (A) Axial FLAIR MRI demonstrating a right temporal calcified lesion (open arrow) with prominent perilesional edema (solid arrow). (B) Axial post-contrast T1-weighted MRI showing ring enhancement of the lesion (black arrow) and diffuse bilateral cerebral convexity sulcal and leptomeningeal enhancement (white arrows), closely mimicking tuberculous meningitis. (C) MR spectroscopy (TE 30 ms; intermediate TE 135 ms also acquired for lactate confirmation by phase reversal) demonstrating an acetate peak at 1.92 ppm and a lactate doublet at 1.33 ppm, with absent lipid peak and normal choline/creatine ratio, favoring NCC over tuberculoma.

Lumbar puncture was unremarkable: protein 17 mg/dL, glucose 73 mg/dL, 4 cells/mm3 (100% lymphocytes). Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra, adenosine deaminase, Gram stain, bacterial cultures, and viral PCR were negative. Serum and CSF NCC IgG ELISA were both negative – reduced sensitivity in calcified neurocysticercosis (NCC) is well recognized [2]. The combination of extensive leptomeningeal enhancement with a normal CSF profile was atypical for infective meningitis (Table 1).

MR spectroscopy (MRS) was performed with mycobacterial cultures pending. Multivoxel spectra (voxel size 10 ×10 ×15 mm; TE 30 and 135 ms) demonstrated a clear acetate peak at 1.92 ppm and a lactate doublet at 1.33 ppm, interpreted by an experienced neuroradiologist. A dominant lipid peak and elevated choline/creatine ratio – more characteristic of tuberculoma – were absent [3] (Fig. 1C). This metabolic profile favored NCC over tuberculoma and substantially influenced clinical management (Table 2).

Fundoscopy excluded ocular cysticercosis [4]. Levetiracetam was initiated for seizure control. Despite calcification, ongoing inflammatory activity was evidenced by ring enhancement, perilesional edema, leptomeningeal enhancement, and MRS metabolic activity. Corticosteroids followed by albendazole (15 mg/kg/day for 21 days) were given [4]. CSF mycobacterial culture returned negative for Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Follow-up MRI at two months showed near-complete resolution of ring enhancement, perilesional edema, and leptomeningeal enhancement (Figs. 2D, 2E). Repeat MRS demonstrated complete disappearance of the acetate peak with near-resolution of lactate (Fig. 2F). The child remained seizure-free at 18-month follow-up with no neurological sequelae.

Follow-up imaging after therapy. (D) Axial FLAIR MRI showing a residual calcified focus (open arrow) with near-complete resolution of perilesional edema (solid arrow). (E) Axial post-contrast T1-weighted MRI demonstrating near-total resolution of ring enhancement (black arrow) and near-complete resolution of bilateral leptomeningeal enhancement (white arrows). (F) Follow-up MR spectroscopy demonstrating complete resolution of the acetate peak and near-complete resolution of the lactate peak, consistent with resolution of previously observed metabolic signature.

This case demonstrates that a solitary calcified NCC lesion may produce diffuse leptomeningeal enhancement closely resembling TBM despite a normal CSF profile and absence of clinical meningitis. Leptomeningeal enhancement should therefore be interpreted alongside clinical and CSF findings rather than in isolation. When conventional investigations remain inconclusive, MRS may provide valuable metabolic information to support differential diagnosis and avoid unnecessary empirical antitubercular therapy.

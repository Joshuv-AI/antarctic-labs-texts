Superselective renal artery embolization for complex duplex kidney with multiple prior surgeries: a case report
Authors: Chuanwang Peng, Puze Wang, Jinze Li, Dong Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Duplex kidney is a common congenital malformation appearing as duplication of the renal pelvis and ureter. However, complex cases with a history of multiple abdominal surgeries, extensive perirenal adhesions, aberrant vascular anatomy, and long-term anticoagulation remain challenging to manage, and no standardized protocol currently exists. Herein, we report a 54-year-old female patient with a complex duplex kidney who had a history of multiple previous abdominal and renal surgeries, long-term oral warfarin anticoagulation, and concurrent recurrent urinary tract infections, renal abscess, and upper urinary tract stones. Following multidisciplinary team evaluation, traditional heminephrectomy was considered extremely high-risk; consequently, superselective renal artery embolization was performed as definitive treatment. The patient recovered uneventfully without major complications. At 12-month follow-up, all symptoms completely resolved, renal function remained stable, and no recurrence of infection or stones was observed. This case suggests that for patients with complex duplex kidney at high surgical risk, superselective renal artery embolization is a safe and feasible minimally invasive alternative, and may inform individualized clinical decision-making.

Introduction

Duplex kidney is a common congenital developmental anomaly of the urinary tract, with autopsy-based data from the general population reporting an incidence of approximately 0.8% in the general population, while patients presenting with urinary tract symptoms, the detection rate can reach 2%–4%, and the condition is approximately twice as common in females as in males (1). Complete duplex kidney is frequently associated with ectopic ureteral orifice, obstruction, calculi, and recurrent urinary tract infections, which in severe cases may lead to loss of function of the affected renal moiety. Currently, it is generally agreed that asymptomatic patients can be managed with regular follow-up observation, whereas those with recurrent infections, non-functioning renal moieties, or refractory symptoms require definitive treatment (1, 2).

For non-functioning duplex kidney units, heminephrectomy is recognized as the standard therapeutic option; among these, the laparoscopic approach remains the cornerstone in clinical practice, and robot-assisted surgery is also a well-established minimally invasive alternative (3). However, in high-risk patients with a history of multiple surgeries, severe perirenal adhesions, aberrant vascular anatomy, impaired renal function, and long-term anticoagulation therapy, the complication rate of traditional surgery is significantly elevated, posing considerable challenges in clinical decision-making. To date, there are few reports in the domestic and international literature addressing such adult cases. Herein, we report the diagnostic and therapeutic course of a patient with complex duplex kidney and a history of multiple prior surgeries, and discuss the clinical value of superselective renal artery embolization with a review of the literature, to inform individualized management of similar cases.

Case presentation

General information and auxiliary examinations

A 54-year-old female patient was initially admitted to our hospital with the chief complaint of postmenopausal irregular vaginal bleeding for more than 20 days, accompanied by stress urinary incontinence. Her past medical history included rheumatic valvular heart disease, for which she had undergone mechanical mitral valve replacement and tricuspid valvuloplasty, as well as a history of old cerebral infarction, and she was on long-term oral warfarin anticoagulation. In addition, she had undergone four abdominal and renal surgeries within the preceding 2 years.

Gynecological physical examination (performed by a gynecologist) revealed stable vital signs, external genitalia of multiparous appearance with thickened skin and hypopigmentation, a patent vagina, a smooth cervix, and an anteverted uterus that was poorly involuted and soft in consistency. A firm mass of approximately 5 cm in diameter with limited mobility was palpated in the right adnexal region, and weak percussion pain was elicited over the right renal area.

Auxiliary examinations: Vaginal ultrasonography suggested a postmenopausal uterus with multiple hypoechoic nodules in the uterine wall (possibly leiomyomas), and an irregular cystic mass in the right adnexal region containing dense punctate hypoechoic and hyperechoic plaques within the cyst. Renal function tests showed a urea level of 9.09 mmol/L and a creatinine level of 126.4 μmol/L, indicating impaired renal function. Urinary ultrasonography revealed right renal calculi and dilatation of the upper right ureter. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the lower abdomen (CECT) demonstrated a septated cystic lesion with calculi in the upper pole of the right kidney, hydronephrotic dilatation of the upper renal moiety with stones, complete dilatation of the ipsilateral ureter, and formation of multiple abscesses in the right hilar and retroperitoneal regions (Figure 1).

Preoperatively, the serum renal function test on August 1, 2024, was used as the baseline: serum creatinine was 126.4 μmol/L, and the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was 43.32 mL/min, indicating mildly impaired total renal function. Combined with CTU findings of severe hydronephrosis of the upper right renal moiety with marked cortical thinning, as well as recurrent refractory infection and calculi, the upper right renal moiety was comprehensively assessed as essentially non-functional, thus meeting the indications for embolization therapy (1, 2).

Diagnostic and therapeutic course

The entire diagnostic and therapeutic process adhered to the principle of “infection control→obstruction relief→definitive treatment of the lesion”. The diagnosis and treatment were completed through multidisciplinary team (MDT) collaboration involving the Department of Gynecology, Department of Urology, Department of Interventional Radiology, and Department of Critical Care Medicine. Key diagnostic and therapeutic milestones of the patient.

Stage 1: infection control and etiological clarification (the month of initial presentation)

Following admission, the patient underwent hysteroscopic resection of the intrauterine occupying lesion and diagnostic curettage, with pathological findings indicating uterine leiomyomas. Due to concurrent urinary incontinence, urological consultation was obtained, and cystourethroscopy combined with laparoscopic exploration was performed. Intraoperatively, purulent discharge was observed from the paraurethral region, and a cord-like cystic mass was identified in the right pelvis along the course of the ureter, which communicated with the external urethral purulent drainage tract. A diagnosis of pelvic abscess with perineal fistulization was established, and extraperitoneal incision and drainage of the right pelvic wall abscess was performed. Pus smear revealed abundant Gram-negative bacilli. Pathological examination of the extraperitoneal mass indicated residual ureter with suppurative inflammation, confirming that ectopic ureter with chronic infection was the primary etiological cause of the infection. Postoperatively, the antibiotic regimen was adjusted based on the microbiological findings, and the patient was discharged after infection control was achieved.

Stage 2: obstruction relief and critical infection management (1 month after initial presentation)

The patient was readmitted due to right flank pain accompanied by high fever (peak 40 °C). A diagnosis of right renal abscess, duplex kidney, and Escherichia coli septicemia was established. Urinalysis revealed severe urinary tract infection, and both blood and urine cultures grew E. coli. The patient underwent right nephrostomy, transureteroscopic incision of right ureteral stricture, and ureteral stent placement. Intraoperatively, right ureteral stricture and abscess formation in the upper pole of the duplex kidney were observed. Postoperatively, the patient developed septic shock and was transferred to the intensive care unit (ICU) for management. After stabilization, she was transferred back to the general ward and discharged following infection control.

Stage 3: stone management and disease reassessment (9 months after initial presentation)

The patient was admitted due to intermittent flank pain persisting for 6 months. CT revealed cystic lesions in the right kidney, calculi with pelvic dilation and hydronephrosis. Urinalysis indicated severe infection with gross hematuria. Combined lithotripsy and stone extraction with ureteral stent exchange was performed. Intraoperatively, the entire ureteral stent was found to be encrusted with calculi, and lower moiety stones of the right duplex kidney were observed. Postoperatively, the patient developed septicemia and was again transferred to the ICU for management. After stabilization, she was discharged. Following MDT discussion, it was decided that definitive treatment for the duplex kidney malformation would be scheduled electively after complete infection control was achieved.

First, right percutaneous nephrolithotripsy with laser lithotripsy, electrocautery resection of pelvic lesions, and nephrostomy were performed to clear the stone burden. Subsequently, under local anesthesia, abdominal aortography and right renal arteriography with superselective right renal artery embolization were performed. Intraoperative digital subtraction angiography (DSA) revealed that the right kidney was supplied by two renal arteries arising from the abdominal aorta (Figure 2). A microcatheter was superselectively advanced into the right upper segmental and upper pole arteries, and 100 mg of 350–560 μm gelatin sponge particles were injected to embolize the target vessels. Follow-up angiography demonstrated complete occlusion of the target vessels without compromising blood supply to the normal renal parenchyma (Figure 3), with preserved perfusion of the lower right renal moiety and no abnormal vascular opacification in the embolized upper pole region; no intraprocedural complications such as ectopic embolization occurred (Figure 4). The patient recovered uneventfully without complications. Follow-up urinalysis and urine culture were both normal, and the patient was discharged 1 week postoperatively.

Follow-up outcomes

At 3-month follow-up, urinary ultrasonography revealed a normal left kidney and a plump right kidney with cortical thinning. Urinalysis showed mildly elevated occult blood, while all infection-related parameters were within normal limits.

At 1-year outpatient follow-up, the patient reported no discomfort such as vaginal discharge, flank pain, or fever, and no recurrent pyuria or urinary tract infection occurred. Renal function remained stable. Urinalysis revealed only mildly elevated occult blood, and urine cultures remained persistently negative.

Discussion

Currently, the diagnosis and treatment of duplex kidney have formed a consensus of “stepped, individualized, minimally invasive priority, and renal function preservation priority”. However, for complex cases with a history of multiple surgeries, severe perirenal adhesions, vascular anomalies, and long-term anticoagulation, the choice of surgical approach still presents considerable challenges. The core difficulties in this case were mainly concentrated in four aspects: (a) extensive perirenal and pelvic adhesions due to multiple prior surgeries, with disrupted anatomical planes, making conventional surgical dissection difficult and carrying a high risk of hemorrhage; (b) aberrant renal vascular anatomy (right kidney supplied by two renal arteries arising from the abdominal aorta) increasing the difficulty of intraoperative vessel identification and control; (c) long-term oral warfarin therapy, with high perioperative risks of bleeding and thromboembolism; (d) pre-existing impaired renal function, necessitating maximum preservation of residual renal function.

At present, laparoscopic heminephrectomy remains the standard surgical approach for non-functioning duplex kidney units in adults (3). However, for this high-risk patient, the risk-to-benefit ratio of surgery was unfavorable: the patient had undergone four abdominal and renal surgeries within 2 years, and preoperative assessment revealed extensive perirenal and pelvic adhesions with disrupted normal anatomical planes. The renal vasculature itself exhibits considerable natural anatomical variability, and unclear anatomical identification increases the risk of intraoperative bleeding, inadvertent vascular injury, and damage to surrounding organs, potentially exacerbating renal functional impairment (4–6). The technical difficulty and complication risk of traditional laparoscopic heminephrectomy were extremely high. Even with robot-assisted surgery, the risk of vascular injury during lysis of adhesions cannot be completely avoided; robot-assisted surgery can only relatively reduce the incidence of postoperative complications such as pseudoaneurysm (7). Moreover, partial nephrectomy requires renal pedicle clamping with warm ischemia, which, even when performed minimally invasively, causes ischemia-reperfusion injury to the renal parenchyma. For patients with pre-existing renal insufficiency, the risk of postoperative renal function deterioration is significantly increased (8).

Superselective renal artery embolization precisely occludes the feeding arteries of the diseased renal moiety, leading to progressive atrophy of the pathological tissue, thereby eliminating the source of infection and alleviating symptoms. It offers the advantages of minimal trauma, no need for anatomical dissection, and minimal impact on renal function. Lal et al. (2) reported a case of an adult with duplex kidney and ectopic ureteral orifice who achieved complete symptom resolution after renal artery embolization without perioperative complications, and suggested that embolization could serve as a minimally invasive alternative for non-functioning duplex kidney units. Yang et al. (9) also reported a case of successful embolization for urinary fistula after duplex kidney surgery, further confirming the safety and efficacy of this procedure in duplex kidney-related diseases. A matched comparative study demonstrated that patients undergoing superselective renal artery embolization had no significant decline in estimated glomerular filtration rate, and their long-term renal function was not significantly different from that of patients undergoing partial nephrectomy alone (10). Comprehensive reviews in the field have indicated that renal artery embolization has become a reliable alternative for patients with benign obstructive nephropathy, non-functioning kidneys, and recurrent refractory infections, particularly in high-risk patients who cannot tolerate conventional surgery. Its major complication rate is substantially lower than that of surgical intervention, with shorter hospital stays and high symptom relief rates, while exerting minimal impact on normal renal parenchymal function (11). In the present case, the 12-month follow-up showed complete symptom resolution and stable renal function, which is consistent with the findings in the existing literature.

The clinical implications of this case are mainly threefold: (a) Duplex kidney is often asymptomatic and prone to clinical misdiagnosis (1, 12). In female patients with unexplained vaginal discharge or refractory urinary incontinence, the possibility of ectopic ureteral orifice should be considered (2). Routine preoperative screening for congenital urinary tract malformations and the use of CTU can reduce misdiagnosis of atypical cases (13); (b) The diagnosis and treatment of complex urinary tract malformations require MDT collaboration to comprehensively assess risks and benefits and formulate individualized treatment plans; (c) For patients with non-functioning duplex kidney who are at high risk for conventional surgery, superselective renal artery embolization can serve as an alternative definitive treatment option, rather than merely as a salvage procedure after surgical failure.

This case has several limitations: Firstly, it is a single case report with limited evidence, and large-sample, multicenter controlled studies are still required to validate its long-term efficacy and safety. Secondly, no routine split renal function studies such as renal scintigraphy or radionuclide renography were performed preoperatively; although the upper renal moiety was clinically judged to be essentially non-functional based on CTU findings (severe hydronephrosis and marked cortical thinning) combined with serum creatinine and eGFR levels, the lack of precise split renal function data still weakens the objective evidence supporting the renal-protective effect of embolization to some extent. Thirdly, the follow-up duration is only 12 months, and longer-term observation is needed to monitor changes in renal function and stone recurrence.

Conclusion

For patients with complex duplex kidney complicated by a history of multiple surgeries, severe perirenal adhesions, aberrant vascular anatomy, and long-term anticoagulation therapy, traditional partial nephrectomy carries extremely high risks. Superselective renal artery embolization offers the advantages of minimal trauma, a favorable safety profile, and clear renal function preservation, and may serve as a minimally invasive definitive treatment alternative for such high-risk surgical patients. The clinical indications and long-term efficacy of this procedure still require further validation through additional studies.

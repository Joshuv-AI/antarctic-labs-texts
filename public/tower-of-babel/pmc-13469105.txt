CMDPC_OBB: A Large-Scale Image Dataset for Coal Mine Drill Pipe Counting based on Oriented Bounding Box
Authors: Fukai Zhang, Xiaoran Liu, Haiyan Zhang, Feng Guo, Shan Zhao, Yanmei Zhang, Guan Yuan, Lu Dong, Xu Chen, Zhanqiang Huo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the scarcity of publicly available underground coal mine monitoring image datasets and the challenges of accurate recognition under complex drilling environments, this study constructs a large-scale image dataset for coal mine drill pipe counting based on Oriented Bounding Boxes (CMDPC_OBB). The dataset is designed for multi-pose and multi-view drill pipe detection and counting tasks, improving data coverage in complex underground scenarios. CMDPC_OBB consists of two sub-datasets: a multi-object detection dataset (MOD_2D) and a structurally enhanced single-object classification dataset (SOC_3D). MOD_2D is built using a 15-frame interval sampling strategy, resulting in 114,869 field images annotated with rotated bounding boxes. SOC_3D extends the original samples at the data level by generating eight additional views per instance through single-image 3D reconstruction, yielding 4,023 images to enhance multi-view representation. Nine object detection and oriented detection models were evaluated on CMDPC_OBB. The highest mAP reaches 89.1%, demonstrating the dataset’s effectiveness and benchmarking value in complex underground environments.

Background & Summary

Coal has long served as the cornerstone of China’s energy system, playing a stabilizing role in ensuring national energy security and supporting economic development1. As shallow coal resources become depleted, mining operations are shifting to deeper strata, where high gas pressure and high gas content in deep coal seams have led to frequent gas-related accidents, posing significant threats to underground mining safety2–4. Gas drainage is recognized as the most effective technical approach for preventing gas accidents5,6. In this context, accurate measurement of borehole depth is a critical step to ensure sufficient coverage of the gas extraction area. In recent years, the acceleration of intelligent coal mine development has led to the widespread adoption of a “one drill, one video” (each drilling operation is recorded as a dedicated video sequence) management model in drilling operation areas, providing rich visual data for computer vision-based intelligent recognition7. Developing intelligent recognition and automatic counting technologies tailored to underground drilling sites is thus of great significance for enhancing borehole depth measurement accuracy and ensuring safe production in coal mines.

In recent years, drill pipe counting technology based on computer vision has emerged as a research hotspot in the field of intelligent coal mine development, owing to its advantages such as non-contact measurement, high efficiency, and strong adaptability. Existing counting approaches can be broadly classified into two categories: indirect methods based on drilling worker action recognition and direct methods based on drill pipe detection. For indirect counting methods, D. Jingyi et al.8 proposed the Drill-action dataset, with original video data collected from the heading face and centralized return airway of a coal mine in Henan Province. The scenes are relatively clear and contain multiple drilling operat ion videos. By employing an improved spatiotemporal convolutional neural network, they recognized three types of worker behaviors—“loading pipe,” “unloading pipe,” and “others”—to infer the number of drill pipes. However, the dataset consists of only 825 images and primarily focuses on human actions, lacking annotations for drill pipe objects. Similarly, G. Rui et al.9 extracted 1,500 representative images for a drill pipe classification dataset divided into “unloading” and “non-unloading” categories. By identifying worker unloading actions in the images, they attempted to estimate the number of drill pipes, but miscounting could occur when unloading actions were detected visually, yet no actual pipe movement occurred. For direct counting methods, Chaoxiu, Y. et al.10 collected 977 images from multiple on-site drilling withdrawal videos in coal mines and annotated three object categories: “worker,” “drilling rig,” and “drill pipe.” By analyzing worker interactions with drill pipes during withdrawal, including grasping and carrying actions, the actual number of drill pipes handled was estimated. H. Wei et al.11 captured 879 underground drilling images via frame extraction from surveillance videos of the Pingdingshan coal mine in Henan Province, focusing on the time span from when the drill rig began withdrawing the pipe to when workers placed it down. They expanded the dataset to 2,014 images through data augmentation and annotated four categories: “drill rig withdrawing pipe,” “worker retrieving pipe,” “carrying drill pipe,” and “placing drill pipe,” focusing on the dynamic states of drill pipes during the withdrawal process. However, most of these datasets originate from specific scenes and viewpoints, resulting in limited data diversity and hindering the generalization capability of models trained in more complex environments. W. Xiangqian et al.12 proposed a real-time drill pipe tracking and counting method based on DeepSORT and YOLOv5, and constructed a dataset of 3,600 images with drill pipe annotations only. J. Yuanyuan et al.13 expanded the annotated dataset to 5,630 images to ensure sufficient data exposure during training and proposed the YOLOv8n-TBiD model for drill pipe detection and segmentation, which improved detection accuracy. However, the dataset scale still remained below 10,000 images, limiting the representational and generalization capabilities of more complex models. The quality of datasets is a critical factor influencing the training of deep learning models14, directly affecting their performance in real-world applications15,16.

Between 2024 and 2025, our research team developed two drill pipe counting datasets tailored for coal mine applications: CMDPC17, based on standard object detection, and CMDRC_OBB18, based on oriented object detection for drilling recognition and counting tasks. The CMDPC dataset contains 4,737 annotated images across five object categories—drill body, drill tail, drill head, drill pipe, and worker. Additionally, an improved Drill-YOLOv8 model was proposed, along with a two-stage Pipe-Count counting method, which effectively enhanced drill pipe counting accuracy under complex and highly cluttered environments. The CMDRC_OBB dataset, for the first time, introduced oriented bounding box annotations for four categories: entire drill body, drill head, drill tail, and drill pipe, and includes 33,320 drilling images. A novel DPC_OBB_SPC drill pipe counting framework was proposed in conjunction with the C2f-DG module and salient peak inference strategy, significantly improving counting performance. In addition, W. Wei et al.19 constructed a large-scale object detection dataset for underground coal mine drilling scenes, comprising 70,948 images annotated with five categories: gripper, drill chuck, coal mine worker, safety helmet, and drill pipe. The YOLO series of models were employed to evaluate dataset quality. Despite their contributions in terms of scale, both the CMDPC dataset and the dataset proposed by Zhou et al. adopt conventional horizontal bounding boxes for object annotation. This approach struggles with issues such as overlapping detections and inaccurate object boundaries in cases of dense, inclined, or occluded drill pipes. Although the CMDRC_OBB dataset takes a preliminary step toward oriented object detection, it lacks an in-depth discussion of the dataset construction methodology.

To address the above challenges, Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) annotations20 offer rich and intuitive information on object shape and orientation. By redefining the representation of object bounding boxes and increasing the degrees of freedom in regression, OBBs allow the boxes to align with the orientation of the objects. This alignment maximizes the overlap between the predicted box and the actual object region, enabling more compact and accurate detection of multi-oriented targets21–23. Such capabilities are particularly crucial for drill pipe recognition and counting in coal mine environments.

This paper presents CMDPC_OBB, a large-scale underground coal mine drill pipe counting image dataset based on Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBBs), comprising 118,892 images. The dataset incorporates multidimensional information, including OBB annotations, 3D spatial coordinates, and multi-view image augmentation. The innovation of CMDPC_OBB lies in the following three aspects: (1) Integration of OBB Annotations: The dataset employs oriented bounding box labeling to fully exploit the spatial and geometric properties of target objects. This enhances the model’s feature learning capability and, through oriented feature refinement mechanisms, addresses the misalignment issues caused by inconsistent object orientations, thereby improving detection accuracy. (2) 3D Perception Model Construction: A three-dimensional perception model of the drill rig is developed to generate synchronized multi-view images, significantly enriching the visual representation of drilling scenes and providing critical support for tracking the spatial motion trajectory of drill pipes. (3) Comprehensive Benchmarking: The effectiveness of CMDPC_OBB is validated using nine rotated object detection algorithms, demonstrating its robustness and applicability. The construction of CMDPC_OBB is expected to substantially advance the intelligent automation of drill pipe counting in coal mine video monitoring, offering both significant theoretical value and practical application potential.

Methods

Data collection

Historical “one drill, one video” data from a coal mine in Shanxi Province was collected, and innovative Oriented Object Detection and 3D multi-view enhancement technologies were applied24 to construct a large-scale image dataset for coal mine underground drill pipe counting based on Oriented Bounding Boxes (A Large-Scale Image Dataset for Coal Mine Drill Pipe Counting based on Oriented Bounding Boxes, CMDPC_OBB).

Drilling equipment

A coal mine in Shanxi Province, located in Jincheng City, China, is a typical high-gas mine. The mine is equipped with a total of 29 drilling rigs, including both directional drilling rigs and conventional drilling rigs. There are 11 directional drilling rigs in total, comprising one ZDY-3200L modified directional drilling rig, four ZDY-6000LD directional drilling rigs, one VLD-1000 directional drilling rig, two ZDY-12000LD directional drilling rigs, two ZDY-15000LD directional drilling rigs, and one ZDY-20000LD directional drilling rig. These directional rigs generated 73 video segments during underground operations, yielding a total of 20,825 images. In addition, there are 18 conventional drilling rigs, including ten ZDY1900S rigs, six ZDY4000S rigs, and two ZDY-4200LXS rigs. These conventional rigs produced 374 video segments during underground operations, resulting in a total of 94,044 images. The dataset contains 114,869 images, covering diverse drill pipe configurations and complex operational scenarios. This information provides a rich foundation of real-world scenarios for constructing the image dataset and effectively enhances its diversity.

Surveillance cameras

The intelligent drilling monitoring system is equipped with intrinsically safe and explosion-proof cameras for mining use25, produced by Hangzhou Hikvision Digital Technology Co., Ltd. Due to system upgrades and reconstruction, the cameras vary in model and resolution. The resolution of the monitoring videos ranges from 480 × 360 to 3840 × 2160. The camera placement and angles comply with the Specifications for Installation and Network Access of Coal Mine Industrial Video Systems issued by the National Mine Safety Administration in December 2024, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the work area and capturing critical details of drilling operations.

Data collection strategy

The monitoring system records drilling operation videos every hour. The system categorizes the videos into folders named “drilling location_drill hole number”, with the videos stored on the x3650M5 disk array. Videos within the same folder are often lengthy, similar, and contain numerous non-operational footage of the drilling machine. During data collection, one video segment with a clear drilling state is selected from each folder (i.e., each scene), and this process is repeated for other folders. The selection criteria primarily depend on whether the drilling machine’s working state is clearly visible and whether the video effectively represents the diversity of the operational scenarios. Excluded segments are mainly those that lack representativeness or have poor quality, failing to meet the dataset’s standards. As a result, a total of 447 video segments were obtained, with the video collection period spanning from May 2023 to March 2025. MOD_2D Dataset Preprocessing

The 447 video segments cover drilling operations under different underground coal mine scenes, camera viewpoints, and spatial conditions, serving as the raw data source for the CMDPC_OBB dataset. The original surveillance videos are typically continuous recordings of approximately 60 minutes. To ensure objectivity and consistency in data selection, unified screening criteria were established: continuous 3–5 minute clips containing complete drilling-in and drilling-out processes were selected. Challenging conditions, including heavy dust, partial occlusion, non-operational drilling rigs appearing in the frame, and severe glare, were deliberately retained to avoid systematic exclusion of difficult samples. All selected clips were manually reviewed. On average, approximately 8.3% (5 out of 60) of each original recording was retained, while the excluded portions mainly consisted of repetitive footage or segments without effective drilling activities.

Then, considering the high similarity between adjacent frames in the video, an interval frame extraction26 strategy was adopted. Comparative experiments were conducted using frame intervals of 15, 30, 45, and 60. The results indicate that a 15-frame interval achieves the optimal balance between detection performance and dataset scale. Experimental validation further demonstrates that this strategy ensures data representativeness and improves training performance (see the Data Preprocessing Strategy Verification section). Therefore, a fixed interval of 15 frames was ultimately used to construct the 114,869 MOD_2D images. Larger intervals lead to the loss of key temporal continuity information, thereby degrading detection performance.

Each scene was divided into 200–300 images, with resolutions ranging from 480 × 360 to 3840 × 2160. Finally, 447 folders were created according to scene numbers to store the corresponding images. After preprocessing, the dataset covers variations in illumination, image clarity, occlusion, and spatial structure, ensuring diversity and representativeness. The image data preprocessing27 process is shown in Fig. 1. SOC_3D Dataset Preprocessing

The SOC_3D dataset is an expansion and enhancement of the MOD_2D dataset. Using high-precision single-image 3D reconstruction technology, 2D drilling images are converted into 3D drilling rig models. Based on the cardinal directions (east, south, west, north, southeast, southwest, northeast, and northwest), 3D models are generated28 with eight different perspectives of the drilling rig.

Data selection and processing

First, one high-quality drilling image that clearly shows the structure and contour texture of the drilling rig was selected from each of the 447 MOD_2D folders, as shown in Fig. 2(a). Then, the selected 447 drilling images were cropped to remove the excess drilling site background and retain a clear image of the drilling rig. Finally, the drilling rig images were segmented and annotated using the roLabelImg tool to obtain a clear contour structure of the drilling rig, as shown in Fig. 2(b).Fig. 2Example of SOC_3D Image Set Composition (Four Groups).

Example of SOC_3D Image Set Composition (Four Groups).

3D Modeling process

Using the Diffusion-based 3D generation tool developed by Tencent, 447 single-frame drilling images were reconstructed into high-fidelity 3D drilling rig models. During the model generation process, the model type was uniformly configured as geometry + texture mapping, and a Triangular Mesh Topology was adopted to ensure structural stability and detailed feature representation. Regarding lighting parameters, the spotlight color value was set to (255, 255, 255) with a spotlight intensity of 0, and the plane light intensity was adjusted to 2.5 to enhance overall brightness balance and material rendering quality. The generated 3D drilling rig models exhibit rich details, well-defined structures, realistic textures, and natural lighting effects. They support 360° full-angle rotation, enabling comprehensive visualization of the spatial structural characteristics of the drilling rig and drill pipe components, and provide high-quality 3D data support for subsequent multi-view data augmentation and 3D-assisted recognition research.

Multi-view Reconstruction Based on 3D Models

The SOC_3D dataset also consists of 447 image sets. Each image set comprises one cropped and segmented drilling image (Fig. 2(b)) and eight multi-view drilling rig images generated from different orientations (Fig. 2(d)). The multi-view drilling rig images are obtained by extracting frames from the generated 3D drilling rig model based on the standard multi-view orientation skeleton diagram (Fig. 2(c)).

Data annotation guidelines

In the process of constructing the CMDPC_OBB dataset, ensuring label consistency and high quality is crucial. To this end, we have developed detailed annotation guidelines to ensure consistency among annotators. First, each object must have its rotation angle clearly labeled, and the rotated bounding box must perfectly fit the actual boundary of the object. Additionally, the category label of the object must be accurate to ensure that each object’s labeled category corresponds to the actual category. For example, labels like “Drill_body” and “Drill_head” should not contain any errors in categorization. During the annotation process, for different frames within the same scene, annotators must ensure that the bounding box of objects in the same position is consistent, in order to avoid significant annotation discrepancies between frames.

Regarding the labeling of rotation angles, we have set the following standards: the angle range should be [−90°, 90°], with a clockwise direction considered as the positive angle, and the horizontal line as the zero reference point. This angle convention ensures consistency in labeling and avoids differences in the interpretation of rotation angles between annotators.

The annotation categories include the entire drilling rig (Drill_body, Category 0), the tail of the drilling rig (Drill_tail, Category 1), the head of the drilling rig (Drill_head, Category 2), and the drill pipe of the drilling rig (Drill_pipe, Category 3). Data annotation process

Data annotation is a crucial step in the construction of the CMDPC_OBB dataset. To ensure the accuracy and consistency of the annotations, we have assembled a systematically trained annotation team. The annotation work was carried out by Henan Polytechnic University, with a total of 25 annotators and 5 quality inspectors involved. The entire data annotation and validation process lasted for 30 days.

The annotation team used the open-source image annotation tool, roLabelImg, which allows for quick and accurate rotated bounding box annotations. The annotation process includes several steps: first, the annotators read the preprocessed drilling images and identify the four categories of target objects within the images. Then, using the roLabelImg tool, they draw rotated bounding boxes around each object and annotate their rotation angles. The annotation files generated by roLabelImg are in.xml format, where each “object” represents a rotated bounding box containing information such as the center coordinates (cx, cy), the width and height (w, h), and the rotation angle (angle), as shown in Fig. 4.Fig. 4Label information of Fig. 3(a).

Label information of Fig. 3(a).

Once all annotations are completed, the annotated images are reviewed by quality inspectors to ensure the accuracy and consistency of the annotations. The quality inspectors rigorously review each annotation according to the annotation guidelines to ensure that it meets the preset standards and to address any potential annotation discrepancies.

For occluded or partially visible objects, annotators are required to infer the rotated bounding box based on the complete appearance of the object to maintain the integrity and continuity of the bounding box. If the key features of the object cannot be clearly identified, the rotation angle should be estimated based on the visible portion, ensuring consistency of the annotations across multiple frames.

The CMDPC_OBB dataset was constructed from long-term, cross-scene underground drilling videos. It encompasses complex environmental factors, including multi-view perspectives, varying illumination conditions, occlusion, rotational poses, and confined spaces, thereby reflecting the visual diversity of real industrial scenarios. Properties of rotated bounding boxes

Object detection algorithms based on deep learning use horizontal bounding boxes to locate objects in images, providing a concise and intuitive representation of candidate regions. However, horizontal bounding boxes typically contain a large amount of background area, as shown in Fig. 6(a), which not only increases the difficulty of classification but also leads to issues such as inaccurate representation of the drill pipe target range and misidentification. Rotated bounding boxes can effectively capture the geometric differences caused by the varying installation angles of cameras in coal mine shafts. Rotated bounding boxes contain almost no background areas, as shown in Fig. 6(b), and can better align with the actual contour of the drill pipe, thereby reducing the impact of background areas on the classification accuracy of the drill pipe.Fig. 6Comparison of bounding boxes.

3D Multi-view feature enhancement

MOD_2D data can only represent the features of a single perspective of drilling images, while SOC_3D data enhances the multi-perspective features of drilling images, thereby effectively improving the accuracy of drilling image detection. This advantage will be further validated in the experiments in Chapter 2. Taking a single-perspective MOD_2D image and the corresponding 8-direction (north, northeast, east, southeast, south, southwest, west, northwest) SOC_3D images as examples, Fig. 7 shows the feature map visualization results of the ResNet-50 model at different network layers (data, conv0_fwd, Stage1_conv1_fwd, and Stage2_conv4_fwd).Fig. 7Visualization of the feature maps of different network layers from ResNet-50.

Visualization of the feature maps of different network layers from ResNet-50.

From the data layer to the Stage2_conv4_fwd layer, the network structure becomes increasingly complex, and the extracted features become increasingly abstract. Each layer in the figure displays the feature map corresponding to a typical channel within that layer. The dimensions of the feature maps decrease sequentially from the input data layer’s 224 × 224, with the conv0_fwd layer at 112 × 112, the Stage1_conv1_fwd layer at 56 × 56, and the Stage2_conv4_fwd layer at 14 × 14. It is evident that the MOD_2D image cannot adequately represent certain detailed features from a single perspective. As the network deepens, its representational capabilities become limited. The SOC_3D image integrates spatial structural information from multiple perspectives, with feature maps exhibiting clear and consistent activation regions across multiple directions. This indicates that multi-perspective data effectively enhances the model’s spatial perception capabilities and local structural capture capabilities29.

These observations validate that SOC_3D data possesses superior discriminative capabilities during deep feature extraction, providing a theoretical basis and feature support for improving detection accuracy.

Ethics and privacy statement

The surveillance video data were collected from actual coal mine drilling operations, with authorization from the mine operator and the data owner. All data have been reviewed to ensure that the released dataset does not contain any personally identifiable information. Frames containing personnel have either been removed or anonymized. Dataset structure

The CMDPC_OBB30 dataset is a large-scale image dataset for rotated bounding boxes, designed for drilling recognition and drill pipe counting tasks in coal mine drilling operations. The dataset is derived from partial historical drilling videos captured by 29 drilling rigs at Yuecheng Coal Mine between May 2023 and March 2025, comprising 447 scenes, 114,869 raw drilling images, and 4,023 generated multi-view drilling rig images. The resolution ranges from [480 × 360, 3840 × 2160], with a total dataset size of 34.4 GB. These images encompass typical conditions such as complex underground coal mine environments, strong lighting, varying viewpoints, and dust particles, demonstrating strong representativeness and diversity.

The CMDPC_OBB dataset contains.jpg image files and corresponding.xml annotation files from coal mine drilling operations, with the annotation files following the Pascal VOC format. Each image is paired with a corresponding annotation file. The annotation files record object category and location information for drilling rig components within the images.

The dataset is organized by scene, with the images and annotation files for each scene stored in separate folders. For example, the folder DXZJ0001 contains the following:

tx077frame_0001.jpg: Image file, containing an image from the coal mine drilling operations.

tx077frame_0001.xml: Annotation file, containing object category and location information.

In each folder, the image files and annotation files are paired using the same naming convention, such as tx077frame_0001.jpg corresponding to tx077frame_0001.xml, tx077frame_0002.jpg corresponding to tx077frame_0002.xml, and so on.

The dataset includes multiple scenes, with each scene’s folder containing several images and their corresponding annotation files. The detailed file structure of the dataset is as follows:Image Files: Each image file is in.jpg format, capturing relevant images from the coal mine drilling process.Annotation Files: Each.xml annotation file follows the Pascal VOC standard and contains annotations of drilling rig components (such as drill pipes and drill heads) and their corresponding positions.

Technical Validation

To validate the effectiveness of the rotated bounding box (OBB) annotation strategy and multi-view data augmentation techniques, nine classic algorithms were selected to validate the dataset. The nine algorithms include seven rotated object detection algorithms (YOLOv3_OBB, YOLOv5_OBB, YOLOv6_OBB, YOLOv8_OBB31, YOLOv9_OBB, YOLOv10_OBB, YOLOv11_OBB) and two object detection algorithms (RtDetr_r50, RtDetr_l). The experiments were conducted using the CMDPC_OBB dataset for training and testing without modifying the model architecture or extensively adjusting the hyperparameters.

Experimental setup

The experimental environment is configured as follows: CPU is Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6330 @ 2.00 GHz, GPU is NVIDIA L40S 48 GB, operating system is Ubuntu 20.04, CUDA version is 12.1, PyTorch version is 2.4.1 + cu121, and Python version is 3.8. The evaluation metrics for drilling image recognition and drill pipe detection include precision (P), recall (R), mean Average Precision (mAP), number of parameters, and computational cost (GFLOPs). Specifically, mAP@0.5 denotes the mAP at an Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.5. mAP@[0.5:0.95] represents the average mAP across 10 IoU thresholds ranging from 0.5 to 0.95 in increments of 0.05. All models were trained and evaluated using a unified configuration: input resolution of 640 × 640, batch size of 32, 500 training epochs, and an initial learning rate of 0.001. No modifications were made to model architectures, and no data augmentation techniques were applied, ensuring fair comparison across algorithms.

The impact of three-dimensional multi-perspective data augmentation

To enhance the generalization ability of the algorithm model in the complex drilling environment, a 3D multi-view data augmentation strategy was introduced. Two sub-datasets were created: MOD_2D and SOC_3D. Among them, MOD_2D includes 114,869 images constructed based on traditional image slicing and rotated bounding box annotations, while SOC_3D consists of 4,023 images generated by 3D augmentation technology. These 3D augmented images simulate the appearance of the drilling rig from different viewpoints by adding extra dimensions of information, which helps improve the model’s robustness to blurry boundaries and large variations in target perspectives.

To prevent data leakage, the SOC_3D views are derived from images in the MOD_2D folder. Therefore, when splitting the dataset, we ensure that derived views of the same scene appear in only one split, ensuring that images generated by SOC_3D do not appear in multiple splits within the training, validation, or test sets.

Combining both types of data, the CMDPC_OBB dataset reaches a total of 118,892 images, substantially enhancing the diversity and complexity of samples for coal mine drill pipe recognition. To further assess the impact of SOC_3D-augmented images on model performance, training experiments were conducted using a mixed dataset. The data was randomly split into training (83,224 images), validation (11,890 images), and test sets (23,778 images) with a 7:1:2 ratio, using the same data partitioning procedure as described previously.

In the experiment, we focused on key performance indicators, including Precision, Recall, mAP@0.5, and mAP@[0.5:0.95]. Furthermore, the RtDetr series models did not exhibit as significant a performance improvement with 3D multi-view augmentation as the YOLO series, likely due to the Transformer architecture’s sensitivity to spatial feature variations. These observations indicate that different model architectures exhibit varied responses to 3D multi-view augmentation, highlighting the need for further research to explore the coupling relationship between model structure and 3D augmentation.

Regarding training efficiency, after introducing SOC_3D multi-view augmentation, the training time increased slightly; however, the overall increase remained manageable and justified by the observed performance improvements. This indicates that the augmentation strategy can balance performance optimization and training efficiency in the coal mine drilling target detection task, particularly when recognizing targets with complex perspectives or ambiguous boundaries, where the benefits of 3D multi-view augmentation are more pronounced.

Data preprocessing strategy verification

To improve the efficiency and accuracy of image processing in coal mine drill pipe detection tasks, we propose a data preprocessing method based on a frame interval extraction strategy. This strategy strikes a balance between dataset size, preservation of critical features, and model performance by appropriately controlling the frame extraction interval. Therefore, we conducted comparative experiments with the image segmentation interval as the variable.

The experiment selected 100 typical drilling videos from the CMDRC_OBB raw video dataset, setting four frame extraction intervals of 15, 30, 45, and 60 frames. Each video was extracted with 200 frames, resulting in four corresponding data subsets. All images were annotated following a consistent oriented bounding box (OBB) labeling protocol, which includes four target categories: Drill_body, Drill_head, Drill_tail, and Drill_pipe. The dataset was randomly partitioned into a training set (14,000 images), a validation set (2,000 images), and a test set (4,000 images) in a 7:1:2 ratio.

For instance, the YOLOv11_OBB model achieved an mAP of 88.3% when a 15-frame extraction interval was used, whereas it decreased to 78.6% when a 60-frame extraction interval was used, corresponding to a performance decrease of nearly 10%. Underground drill rod counting experiment in coal mines

To evaluate the practical applicability of the CMDPC_OBB dataset for drill-pipe counting in underground coal mines, a counting method based on the temporal variation of drill pipe projection lengths was designed. Specifically, the length of each drill pipe in the video was tracked over time, and linear regression analysis was applied to determine whether the pipe was in a “continuously increasing” or “continuously decreasing” state, corresponding to drilling-out or drilling-in operations, respectively. In addition, the counting logic integrates the relative positional relationships and length variation trends of key drill pipe targets, establishes triggering conditions, and employs a status flag mechanism to prevent duplicate counting. This approach enables accurate identification and counting of drill pipes as they enter and exit the drill rig.

To verify the applicability of the CMDPC_OBB dataset in drill-pipe counting tasks, YOLOv11_OBB was selected as the counting detection model. The experiment used 30 underground coal mine drilling videos, with durations ranging from 152 to 355 seconds, covering both drilling-out and drilling-in operations.

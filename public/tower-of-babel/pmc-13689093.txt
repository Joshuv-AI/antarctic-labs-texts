New purple sulfur bacteria genomes from Nebraska salt marsh enrichments
Authors: Maria F. Orta Medellin, Evelyne Y. Aguirre Zepeda, Genesis Gallegos Martinez, John A. Kyndt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTwo genomes from a Winogradsky column enrichment from the Nebraska salt marshes were sequenced. The analysis of whole-genome phylogeny and average nucleotide identity comparisons indicated that these belong to species of purple sulfur bacteria, Halochromatium and Ectothiorhodospira marina, that have not been described before.

ANNOUNCEMENT

The Nebraska salt marshes are a rare inland ecosystem that is fed by salty groundwater, which creates a unique plant, insect, and microbial biodiversity (1–4). Several unique bacterial species have been identified through seasonal metagenomic analysis in the past few years (3, 4). However, most of these have not been able to be cultivated in the lab. We now performed an enrichment of the salt marsh microbes in a Winogradsky column to identify and isolate more unique photosynthetic species. The column (10 cm × 4 cm) was placed under daylight illumination in front of a window for 4 weeks. The sample material for the Winogradsky column was obtained from the following location: latitude, 40 52′54.42″ N; longitude, 96 39′35.82″ W. Samples were taken using 100 mL sterile bottles and stored overnight at 4°C until the Winogradsky column was assembled the following day and placed in the light at 25°C.

A dark red band appeared on the Winogradsky column after 4 weeks of incubation at about 2 cm depth, and 1 mL of sludge was taken by slow pipetting near the glass edge of the column. This was used for genomic DNA extraction using the GeneJET DNA purification kit (Thermo Scientific). This resulted in DNA with a concentration of 68.3 ng/μL and an A260/280 of 1.85. The sequencing library was prepared using the Illumina DNA Library Prep kit and sequenced by an Illumina MiniSeq using 500 μL of a 1.8 pM library. Paired-end (2 × 150 bp) sequencing generated 2,679,692 reads and 399.3 Mbps. Quality control of the reads was performed using FASTQC (v1.0.0), using a k-mer size of 5 and contamination filtering for overrepresented sequences against the default contamination list. Illumina adapter sequences were removed using the FASTQ Toolkit (v2.2.6) within BaseSpace (Illumina). The genome was assembled using the Metagenomic binning function in BV-BRC (5, 6), which uses MetaSPAdes (v3.12.0) (7). This provided two good quality bacterial genomes of 3,584,162 bp (63.7% GC) (NSM3_A) and 3,548,302 bp (62.1% GC) (NSM3_B). The genome characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The final genomes were >99% complete according to CheckM (v1.1.6) (8). An EvalG genome quality analysis as part of CheckM showed <2% (NSM3_A) and <4% (NSM3_B) potential contamination. Genomes were annotated by NCBI PGAP (v6.6) (9). Default parameters were used for all software applications unless otherwise noted.

A JSpecies comparison (v4.1.1) (12) of ANI percentage ranged from 89.2 to 78.6 for strain NMS3_A with Halochromatium species (Table 1) and showed 97.0% identity of strain NSM3-B with Ectothiorhodospira marina. Phylogenetic comparison of the two new genomes to the other known Ectothiorhodospira and Halochromatium relatives in NCBI GenBank using RAxML (13, 14) showed Halochromatium salexigens DSM 4395 as the closest relative to genome NSM3-A, while Ectothiorhodospira marina DSM 241 as the closest relative for NSM3-B (Fig. 1). Strain NSM3-B may be a new strain of Ectothiorhodospira marina; however, the ANI of all the Halochromatium species is far below the arbitrary species cutoff (of 95%), indicating that strain NSM3-A might belong to a novel species. Further isolation and physiological characterization experiments are underway to formally propose a new species. Interestingly, a proteome comparison analysis in BV-BRC showed that Halochromatium NSM3-A contains 116 unique PGFams compared to the other Halochromatium species and is the only one that contains a hydrogenase-4 operon, which is thought to play a critical role in anaerobic energy metabolism by catalyzing the breakdown of formate into carbon dioxide and hydrogen gas, particularly tailored for survival in harsh conditions such as energy stress and extreme pH (15, 16).

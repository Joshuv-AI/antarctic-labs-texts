Near telomere-to-telomere genome assembly of the rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss)
Authors: Shuhong Jiang, Shuo Li, Shenglei Han, Chen Li, Xuwen Cao, Changlin Liu, Chunlong Miao, Caichao Dong, Yanming Zhang, Kaiqiang Liu, Yuyan Liu, Qian Wang, Hong-yan Wang, Changwei Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) exhibits extensive karyotypic diversity (2n = 58–64) driven by Robertsonian translocations, yet widely used reference genomes are derived from North American lineages, leaving Chinese aquaculture populations underrepresented. Here, we present a near telomere-to-telomere (T2T) genome assembly of a farmed rainbow trout from China. Integrating PacBio HiFi, ONT ultra-long reads, and Hi-C data, we assembled a 2.29 Gb genome with 99.04% anchored to 30 chromosomes. Notably, the genome contains only 18 gaps, with 16 gap-free chromosomes and 13 achieving T2T status. Comparative synteny analysis revealed a third chromosomal fission/fusion iteration in which Swanson Omy14 splits into Arlee Omy14 and Omy32. Annotation identified 43,137 protein-coding genes, with a BUSCO completeness of 98.9%. This dataset provides a valuable resource for resolving lineage-specific structural variation, supporting pangenome construction and facilitating molecular breeding in rainbow trout.

Background & Summary

Oncorhynchus mykiss belongs to the family Salmonidae and has a complex genome shaped by the salmonid-specific fourth round of whole-genome duplication (Ss4R)1. This event, coupled with the proliferation of transposable elements (TEs), has resulted in a genome characterized by high repetitive content and retained paralogous regions derived from delayed rediploidization1,2. In addition, the rainbow trout genome is relatively large, with an estimated size of approximately 2.4 Gb3, about 2–3 times larger than many common fish species, such as Oreochromis niloticus4, Ictalurus punctatus5, and Micropterus salmoides6. These genomic complexities pose substantial challenges for de novo genome assemblies.

Rainbow trout are native to the Pacific coast of North America and the Kamchatka Peninsula in Russia7,8. Many domesticated rainbow trout used for aquaculture worldwide are thought to trace to hatchery propagation and stock transfers initiated in western North America in the late nineteenth century8,9. Rainbow trout were first introduced into China from North Korea in 1959, but the exact origin of this initial introduction remains unclear10. Subsequently, rainbow trout aquaculture in China involved additional introductions and germplasm exchanges from different sources, likely contributing to complex genetic backgrounds among Chinese farmed populations. These features highlight the need for genomic resources that better represent Chinese aquaculture germplasm.

Rainbow trout exhibit remarkable karyotypic diversity (2n = 58–64) across geographic populations due to Robertsonian translocations, while the number of chromosome arms remains stable (NF = 104)11. The Swanson (2n = 58) and Arlee (2n = 64) assemblies differ in three major chromosomal fission/fusion events, involving Swanson chromosomes Omy04, Omy14 and Omy2512,13. Two additional 2n = 60 de novo assemblies, from the Hayspur hatchery strain in Idaho and the interior redband rainbow trout from Keithley Creek, correspond to two of these iterations: the Hayspur genome reflects the fission of Swanson Omy25 into Arlee Omy25 and Omy31, and the Keithley Creek assembly the fission of Swanson Omy04 into Arlee Omy04 and Omy3014,15. Beyond this karyotypic variation, many current assemblies still contain unresolved gaps in telomeric and other complex regions, limiting the characterization of these evolutionarily dynamic regions. A high-quality assembly is therefore still needed to improve the resolution of these previously unassembled complex regions.

In this study, we integrated PacBio HiFi, ONT ultra-long reads and Hi-C data to generate a near-T2T genome assembly of a farmed rainbow trout from China. The resulting assembly substantially improves chromosome-scale continuity over existing rainbow trout references. Comparative synteny analysis revealed a third 30-chromosome fission/fusion iteration, in which Swanson Omy14 corresponds to Arlee Omy14 and Omy32, distinct from the previously reported Hayspur and Keithley Creek iterations. This assembly provides a valuable resource for comparative genomics, structural variation analysis, pangenome construction, germplasm evaluation and molecular breeding in rainbow trout.

Methods

Sample collection and sequencing

All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional animal care and use guidelines, with approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of the Yellow Sea Fisheries Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Fishery Sciences (YSFRI-2026075). A healthy adult male rainbow trout introduced from Benxi, Liaoning Province, was collected from Kunming Tanghao Aquaculture Co., Ltd. in Yunnan, China. To ensure genomic uniformity, ten distinct tissues were harvested from a single individual, including muscle, liver, kidney, spleen, fin, heart, brain, gill, testis, and intestine. Samples were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately upon collection and stored at −80 °C to preserve nucleic acid integrity. To construct a high-quality reference genome, we integrated data from PacBio Revio, Nanopore PromethION, and MGI DNBSEQ-T7 platforms.

For HiFi sequencing, muscle tissue was selected as the DNA source for its high yield of high-quality genomic DNA (gDNA) and low contamination, both favorable for long-read library preparation. gDNA was extracted using the NucleoBond HMW DNA Kit (Macherey-Nagel), with purity, concentration, and integrity assessed by NanoDrop, Qubit fluorometry, and Agilent 4200 TapeStation, respectively. The gDNA was sheared and used to construct PCR-free SMRTbell libraries, which were sequenced on the PacBio Revio platform. For WGS sequencing, paired-end libraries (~350 bp insert size) were constructed from the same gDNA preparation used for HiFi sequencing following standard MGI protocols, and sequenced on the MGI DNBSEQ-T7 platform (PE150). Raw reads were filtered with fastp (v0.23.0) using ‘-q 19 -u 50 -n 5’, yielding 166.33 Gb (~73×) of clean data for genome assembly quality evaluation (Table 1).

For ONT ultra-long sequencing, gDNA was independently extracted from muscle and liver tissues to maximize long-read yield. gDNA was extracted using a sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)-based method and assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis, NanoDrop, and Qubit fluorometry. Libraries were constructed using the SQK-LSK114 kit (Oxford Nanopore Technologies) and sequenced on the Oxford Nanopore Technologies PromethION platform with FLO-PRO114M (R10.4.1) flow cells. Reads with mean Q-score ≤ 9 were filtered out, and reads from all libraries were combined, yielding 98.30 Gb (~43×) of data (Table 1).

For Hi-C sequencing, liver tissue was selected for its efficient formaldehyde cross-linking and chromatin recovery. Cross-linked chromatin from the same individual was digested with DpnII, biotin-labeled at the cleavage sites, proximity-ligated, and purified. The recovered DNA was sheared, biotin-enriched, and processed for adapter ligation to construct paired-end libraries, with library quality assessed by Qubit and Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Libraries were sequenced on the MGI DNBSEQ-T7 platform (PE150), yielding 187.84 Gb (~82×) of clean data for anchoring (Table 1).

For RNA-seq, transcriptome sequencing was performed on ten tissues to support gene prediction and functional annotation (Table 1). Total RNA was extracted using the MagPure Universal RNA Kit (Magen Biotech), with purity, concentration, and integrity assessed by NanoDrop 2000 and LabChip GX Touch. Poly(A)-enriched mRNA libraries were constructed using the MGIEasy RNA Library Prep Set (MGI Tech) and assessed by Qubit 3.0, Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, and qPCR. Qualified libraries were sequenced on the MGI DNBSEQ-T7 platform (PE150), and raw reads were filtered with fastp, yielding an average of 6.47 Gb of clean data per tissue (Table 1).

Genome assembly

A comprehensive strategy was employed to construct the near-T2T genome. Initial assembly was performed with hifiasm16 (v0.25.0) with the parameter ‘-u 0 --telo-m CCCTAA’ by jointly integrating PacBio HiFi, ONT and Hi-C data. The primary contig set (p_ctg) was used as the main backbone for the T2T consensus assembly. Potential errors in the primary contigs were corrected using CRAQ17 (v1.0.9) with HiFi and WGS data by splitting chimeric fragments based on clipping signals, followed by redundancy removal via KmerDedup18 with the parameter ‘-mpr 0.5’.

Subsequently, HapHiC19 (v1.0.6) was used to anchor contigs to 30 chromosomes with Hi-C data, consistent with the previously reported 2n = 60 karyotypes of rainbow trout20 (Fig. 1A). To ensure representation of the male-specific locus in the final assembly, the haplotype-resolved contigs generated by hifiasm were additionally examined. These contigs were independently scaffolded using HapHiC, and screened for sdY using BLASTN21 (v2.16.0) against the published O. mykiss sdY coding sequence. An sdY-bearing scaffold was identified, with support from Hi-C contact patterns. To facilitate comparative analyses across rainbow trout assemblies, chromosome names were reassigned according to the established rainbow trout nomenclature convention based on whole-genome syntenic relationships with the Arlee and Swanson assemblies12,15. Accordingly, the 30 chromosomes in this study assembly were named Omy01–Omy29 and Omy32. Under this nomenclature, a full-length sdY coding sequence was identified on Omy29 by BLASTN alignment.

Telomere and candidate centromere identification

Telomeres were systematically identified using the TeloExplorer module of QuarTeT23 (v1.1.1). The parameter -c animal was employed to screen for the canonical vertebrate motif (TTAGGG/CCCTAA) with a minimum of 100 repeats. The analysis identified ‘AACCCT’ as the specific motif; notably, 24 chromosomes possessed telomeric sequences at both ends, while the remaining 6 exhibited them at a single end (Fig. 2 and Table 3). Assembly statistics of chromosomes.

Candidate centromere regions were predicted with reference to the method described for Ctenopharyngodon idella25. Tandem repeat arrays were identified using TRF26 (v4.10) with the parameters ‘2 7 7 80 10 50 500’. The TRF output was then processed with BSLtool27 for monomer decomposition, alignment and clustering to generate a species-specific satellite monomer library. This library was used as a custom repeat library in RepeatMasker28 (v4.1.7) to identify the genomic distribution of satellite monomers. RepeatMasker annotations were intersected with TRF-derived tandem repeat arrays using BEDTools29 (v2.31.1). Adjacent records assigned to the same monomer within 2 kb were merged, and merged satellite-rich blocks with a length of ≥50 kb were retained as candidate regions. Representative candidate centromeric repeat regions were selected based on satellite enrichment and inspection of Hi-C contact maps (Table 3). Functional centromere positions remain to be experimentally validated.

Genomic synteny and structural variant analysis

To assess genome-wide synteny and chromosomal organization, our rainbow trout assembly was aligned against two representative rainbow trout reference genomes, Arlee (GCF_013265735.2) and Swanson13 (GCA_025558465.1), using Minimap230 (v2.17) with the asm5 preset. The alignments were visualized as dot plots using pafCoordsDotPlotly.R (https://github.com/tpoorten/dotPlotly) to examine chromosome-scale collinearity and fission/fusion differences. The assembly showed high synteny with both references, while also revealing distinct fission/fusion patterns. Compared with the Arlee genome, Arlee OmyA25/OmyA31 corresponded to Omy25 in our assembly, and Arlee OmyA04/OmyA30 corresponded to Omy04 (Fig. 3A). Compared with the Swanson genome, Swanson Omy14 corresponded to two chromosomes in our assembly, Omy14 and Omy32 (Fig. 3B). Protein-coding gene prediction and annotation

Protein-coding genes were predicted by integrating de novo prediction, homology-based search, and transcriptome evidence. De novo prediction was performed using Augustus35 (v3.3). Homology-based evidence was generated by aligning proteins from four related salmonids (Oncorhynchus clarkii, Oncorhynchus gorbuscha, Oncorhynchus kisutch, and the O. mykiss Arlee strain) using Miniprot36 (v0.15) and GeneWise37 (v2.2.0). Transcriptome evidence was derived from ten tissues, processed via HISAT238 (v2.1.0), StringTie39 (v2.1.4), TACO40 (v0.7.3), and TransDecoder41 (v5.5.0), with assembly refinement using Trinity42 (v2.15.1) and PASA43 (v2.5.2). These results were integrated using EvidenceModeler44 (EVM; v2.0.0) and combined with EGAPx (v0.4.0-alpha; https://github.com/ncbi/egapx) predictions. The final gene set contained 43,137 genes, achieving 98.9% completeness according to BUSCO45 (v6.0.0) assessment with the actinopterygii_odb10 dataset.

Functional annotation was performed by aligning the predicted protein sequences against public databases using Diamond46 with an E-value threshold of 1e-5. The databases included NR47, EggNOG48, SwissProt49, KEGG50, Pfam51, and Kofam52. Technical Validation

The quality of the genome assembly was comprehensively evaluated using multiple complementary approaches. Genome completeness was first assessed using compleasm55 (v0.2.6) with the actinopterygii_odb10 dataset, yielding an overall completeness of 99.72% (Fig. 1B and Table 2). Base-level accuracy was evaluated using Merqury56 (v1.3) with WGS data, resulting in a quality value (QV) of 40.93. Additionally, the assembly exhibited high read mapping rates, with 99.78% of WGS reads aligned using BWA57 (v0.7.17) and 99.97% of HiFi reads aligned using Minimap2.

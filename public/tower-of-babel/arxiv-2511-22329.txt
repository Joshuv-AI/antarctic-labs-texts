Maximal variation of linear systems
Arnaud Beauville

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth projective variety over ℂ \mathbb{C} , and L L a line bundle on X X . We say that the linear system | L | \lvert{L}\rvert has maximal variation if its elements have the maximum number dim | L | \dim\lvert{L}\rvert of moduli. We discuss four cases where this situation is expected: hypersurfaces, double coverings of ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} , K3 surfaces, and abelian varieties.



Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
L
be a line bundle on
X
. We assume that a general hypersurface
S
in the linear system
\lvert{L}\rvert
is smooth and connected. We say that
\lvert{L}\rvert
has
maximal variation
if there are only finitely many
S^{\prime}
in
\lvert{L}\rvert
isomorphic to
S
. If there is a reasonable moduli space
\mathscr{M}
for
S
(this will be the case for most of our examples), this is equivalent to say that the natural rational map
\lvert{L}\rvert\dasharrow\mathscr{M}

is generically finite, or equivalently that its image has dimension
\dim\lvert{L}\rvert
.

There is a simple differential criterion for this to happen, namely
H^{0}(T_{X|S})=0
(§1). However this turns out to be surprisingly difficult to check. We will discuss four cases where maximal variation is expected: hypersurfaces (§2), double coverings of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(§3), K3 surfaces (§4), and abelian varieties (§5). We prove indeed that the variation is maximal in the latter case, while we have only partial results in the first three cases. Finally we discuss some more examples and counter-examples (§6 and 7).

1.
A differential criterion

Proposition 1
.

1)
If
H^{0}(S,T_{X|S})=0
,
\lvert{L}\rvert
has maximal variation.

2)
If
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
and
H^{0}(S,T_{S})=0
, the converse holds.

Proof
: Let
S
be a general element of
\lvert{L}\rvert
, and let
k:\mathscr{S}\rightarrow B
be a Kuranishi family for
S
. There is an open neighborhood
U
of
[S]
in
\lvert{L}\rvert
and a map
\mu:U\rightarrow B
such that the pull back of
k
by
\mu
is the family of hypersurfaces parameterized by
U
.
\lvert{L}\rvert
has maximal variation if and only if
\mu
is a local immersion at
[S]
, that is, the tangent map
T\mu
is injective.

The exact sequence
0\rightarrow T_{S}\rightarrow T_{X|S}\rightarrow L_{|S}\rightarrow 0
gives a cohomology exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{0}(T_{S})\rightarrow H^{0}(T_{X|S})\rightarrow H^{0}(L_{|S})\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }H^{1}(T_{S})\,.

Let
s
be a section of
L
with zero locus
S
. The exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ s\ }L\rightarrow L_{|S}\rightarrow 0
gives a cohomology exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{0}(L)/(\mathbb{C}\!\cdot\!s)\xrightarrow{\ r\ }H^{0}(L_{|S})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\,.

The tangent space to
\lvert{L}\rvert
at
S
is
H^{0}(L)/(\mathbb{C}\!\cdot\!s)
, and the tangent space to
B
at
\mu(S)
is
H^{1}(T_{S})
; by
[
K-S
, (12.4)]
, the tangent map to
\mu
at
[S]
is the composition

T\mu:H^{0}(L)/(\mathbb{C}\!\cdot\!s)\xrightarrow{\ r\ }H^{0}(L_{|S})\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }H^{1}(T_{S})\,.

If
H^{0}(T_{X|S})=0
,
\partial
is injective; since
r
is injective,
T\mu
is injective. Conversely if
T\mu
is injective, under the hypotheses of 2)

r
is an isomorphism, so
\partial
is injective and
H^{0}(T_{X|S})=0
since
H^{0}(T_{S})=0
.∎

2.
Hypersurfaces

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
d\geq 3
, defined by an equation
F=0
. We assume
n\geq 3
, and
d\geq 4
if
n=3
. The
Jacobian ring
of
X
is the graded artinian ring
R:=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]/(F^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{n})
, where we write
F^{\prime}_{i}=\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}\,\cdot

Proposition 2
.

The linear system
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)}\rvert
has maximal variation if and only if the multiplication map
\times\ell:R_{d-1}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective for
\ell
general in
R_{1}
.

The property that
\times\ell:R_{p-1}\rightarrow R_{p}
is of maximal rank for
\ell
general in
R_{1}
and for all
p
is called the
weak Lefschetz property
; it is conjectured that it holds for all rings of the form
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]/(P_{0},\ldots,P_{n})
, with
P_{0},\ldots,P_{n}
homogeneous with no common zeroes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(see for instance
[
J-M
]
). This would imply that
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)}\rvert
has always maximal variation. We have only a weaker result:

Corollary 1
.

\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)}\rvert
has maximal variation in the following cases:

1)\ d\geq n+2
;

2)\ X
is a surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\ (
with
d\geq 4)
;

3)\ X
is a cubic threefold;

4)\ X
is general
(among hypersurfaces of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
).

Proof
: 1) is proved in
[
B-M
]
, improving by 1 the bound in
[
P-R-T
]
. This implies 2) for
d\geq 5
; for
d=4
and
n=3
, the weak Lefschetz property is proved in
[
B-M-M-N
]
. 3) is proved in
[
B2
]
, as a consequence of
[
A-R
]
.

4) The Fermat hypersurface
\sum X_{i}^{d}=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
satisfies the weak Lefschetz property (in all degrees), because its Jacobian ring is isomorphic to the cohomology ring
H^{*}((\mathbb{P}^{d-2})^{n},\mathbb{C})
. Therefore the same holds for
X
in a Zariski open subset of
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)}\rvert
.
∎

Proof of the Proposition
: We essentially repeat the proof of
[
B2
]
, with one more detail for the case
n=3
.

Let
S
be a smooth hyperplane section of
X
; we choose the coordinates so that it is defined by
x_{0}=0
.
From the exact sequence

0\rightarrow T_{X|S}\rightarrow T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{S}(d)\rightarrow 0

we see that
H^{0}(T_{X|S})
is the kernel of the homomorphism
\varphi:H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(d))
.

From the commutative diagram of Euler exact sequences

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)^{n+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)^{n+1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u}
\textstyle{H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)^{n+1})}

we get an exact sequence

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\xrightarrow{\ r\ }H^{0}(S,T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})\rightarrow H^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})\xrightarrow{\ u\ }H^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)^{n+1})\,.

If
n\geq 4
we have
H^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
; if
n=3
(so that
S
is a curve), the homomorphism
u
is the transpose of the map
H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(d-4))^{4}\xrightarrow{\ (x_{0},\ldots,x_{3})\ }H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(d-3))
, which is surjective since
d\geq 4
; thus
u
is injective. In each case we conclude that the restriction map
r
is surjective.
It follows that
H^{0}(S,T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})
is generated by the elements
x_{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
for
i\geq 1
,
j\geq 0
, with the Euler relation
\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}x_{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=0
.

Assume that
\times x_{0}:R_{d-1}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective. Let
V\in H^{0}(S,T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})
; we can write
V=\sum\limits_{i}L_{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
, where the
L_{i}
are linear forms in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
The homomorphism
\varphi:H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(d))
maps
V
to
\sum L_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
.
If
\varphi(V)=0
, there exists
G\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(d-1))
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum L_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}=x_{0}G+aF
. Since
dF=\sum x_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
, it follows that
x_{0}G
is zero in
R_{d}
. By hypothesis this implies
G=0
in
R_{d-1}
, that is,
G=\sum a_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
for some constants
a_{i}
. Therefore

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(L_{i}-a_{i}x_{0}-bx_{i})F^{\prime}_{i}=0\qquad\mbox{with }b:=a/d\,.

Since
(F^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{n})
is a regular sequence in
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, this implies

L_{i}-a_{i}x_{0}-bx_{i}=0
for each
i
. Since
x_{0}
does not appear in
L_{i}
, we get :

\bullet
For
i\geq 1
,
a_{i}=0
and
L_{i}=bx_{i}
;
\bullet
for
i=0
,
L_{0}=0
.

Hence
V=b\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}x_{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=0
.

Conversely, suppose
H^{0}(S,T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}|S})=0
. Let
G\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(d-1))
such that
x_{0}G=0
in
R_{d}
; there exist linear forms
L_{i}
such that
x_{0}G=\sum L_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
. Replacing
G
by
G-\sum a_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
, where
a_{i}
is the coefficient of
x_{0}
in
L_{i}
, we can assume that the
L_{i}
are linear forms in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Then the vector field
V=\sum L_{i}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
satisfies
\varphi(V)=0
, hence
V=0
. This means that there exists
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that
L_{i}=cx_{i}
for
i>0
and
L_{0}=0
. Then
x_{0}G=c\sum\limits_{i>0}x_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}=c(dF-x_{0}F^{\prime}_{0})
, hence

cdF=x_{0}(G+cF^{\prime}_{0})
. Since
F
is irreducible, this implies
c=0
, hence
G=0
. ∎

We now consider the linear system
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(e)}\rvert
for
e\geq 2
.

Proposition 3
.

\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(e)}\rvert
has maximal variation in the following cases :

1)\ \lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)}\rvert
has maximal variation;

2)\ e\geq d
.

(In particular, each of the conditions 1) to 3) of Corollary
1
implies that
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(e)}\rvert
has maximal variation for all
e\geq 1
).

Proof
: The proof of Proposition
2
works identically for the linear system

\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(e)}\rvert
, replacing
x_{0}
by a general form
h
in
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(e))
.
We find that
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(e)}\rvert
has maximal variation if
\times h:R_{d-e}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective for
h
general in
R_{e}
.

1) Let
\ell
be a general element of
R_{1}
. If
\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)}\rvert
has maximal variation,
\times\ell:R_{d-1}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective (Proposition
2
). Then
\times\ell:R_{p-1}\rightarrow R_{p}
is injective for
p\leq d
(if
\ell x=0
for
x\in R_{p-1}
, we have
x\cdot R_{d-p}=0
, hence
x=0
). Therefore
\times\ell^{e}:R_{d-e}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective, and so is
\times h
for
h
general in
R_{e}
.

2) If
e>d
, we have
R_{d-e}=0
so there is nothing to prove. If
e=d
,
we just observe that
R_{d}\neq 0
, since
the socle of
R
is in degree
(n+1)(d-2)\geq d
.∎

3.
Double coverings of
\mathbb{P}^{n}

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
a double covering, branched along a smooth hypersurface
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of (even) degree
d
. We assume
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 4
, and
d\geq 6
if
n=2
. Let
F=0
be an equation of
B
, and
let
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]/(F^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{n})
be the Jacobian ring of
F
.

Proposition 4
.

The linear system
\lvert{\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)}\rvert
has maximal variation if and only if the multiplication map
\times\ell:R_{d-1}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective for
\ell
general in
R_{1}
.

Using again
[
B-M
]
, we get:

Corollary 2
.

If
d\geq n+2\ (
and
d\geq 6
if
n=2)
,
\lvert{\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)}\rvert
has maximal variation.

Proof of the Proposition
: The proof is inspired by
[
D-H
, Proposition 5.2]
, which treats the case
n=2
,
d=6
.

Put
\delta:=d/2
, and
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{n}
. We have
\pi_{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}=\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\oplus\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle(-\delta)

[
E-V
, Lemma 3.16 (d)]
. Using Grothendieck duality this gives

\pi_{*}T_{X}=T_{\mathbb{P}}(-\delta)\oplus T_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle

where
T_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle
is the sheaf of vector fields on
\mathbb{P}
tangent to
B
.

Let
H
be a general hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}
, and
S:=\pi^{*}H
.
We have

\pi_{*}T_{X|S}=(\pi_{*}T_{X})_{|H}=T_{\mathbb{P}}(-\delta)_{|H}\oplus T_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle_{|H}\,,

hence the vanishing of
H^{0}(T_{X|S})
is equivalent to
H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}}(-\delta)_{|H})=H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle_{|H})=0
.

1) Consider the Euler exact sequence restricted to
H
:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{H}(-\delta)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{H}(-\delta+1)^{n+1}\rightarrow T_{\mathbb{P}}(-\delta)_{|H}\rightarrow 0\,.

If
n\geq 3
, we have
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(-\delta))=0
, hence
H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}}(-\delta)_{|H})=0
. If
n=2
(so
H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
), the homomorphism
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(-\delta))\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(-\delta+1)^{3})
is the transpose of the map
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(\delta-3)^{3})\xrightarrow{\ (x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\ }H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(\delta-2))
, which is surjective since
\delta\geq 3
; hence again
H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}}(-\delta)_{|H})=0
.

2) We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow T_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)^{n+1}\xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(d)\rightarrow 0\ ,\quad\mbox{where }\ \varphi(L_{0},\ldots,L_{n})=\sum L_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}\,.

Therefore we have
H^{0}(T_{\mathbb{P}}\langle{B}\rangle_{|H})=0
if and only if
\varphi_{|H}:H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(1)^{n+1})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(d))
is injective. Choose the coordinates so that
H
is defined by
x_{0}=0
.
Let
L_{0},\ldots,L_{n}
be linear forms in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
such that
\varphi_{|H}(L_{0},\ldots,L_{n})=0
. This means that

\sum L_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}=x_{0}G
for some
G\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(d-1))
. If
\times x_{0}:R_{d-1}\rightarrow R_{d}
is injective,
we get
G=\sum a_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
for some scalars
a_{i}
, thus
\sum(L_{i}-a_{i}x_{0})F^{\prime}_{i}=0
. As before this implies

L_{i}=a_{i}x_{0}
, and since
x_{0}
does not appear in
L_{i}
,
L_{i}=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
\varphi_{|H}
is injective. Let
G\in R_{d-1}
such that
x_{0}G=0
in
R_{d}
: there exist linear forms
L_{0},\ldots,L_{n}
such that
x_{0}G=\sum L_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
.
This implies that the restrictions
\bar{L}_{i}
of
L_{i}
to
H
satisfy
\varphi_{|H}(\bar{L}_{0},\ldots,\bar{L}_{n})=0
, hence
\bar{L}_{i}=0
for all
i
. Therefore
L_{i}=b_{i}x_{0}
for some scalars
b_{i}
, so that
G=\sum b_{i}F^{\prime}_{i}
is zero in
R_{d-1}
.∎

K3

5.
Abelian varieties

Proposition 6
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety, and
L
an ample line bundle on
X
. The linear system
\lvert{L}\rvert
has maximal variation.

Proof
: As in Proposition
1
, we need to prove that the map
T\mu:H^{0}(L)/(\mathbb{C}\cdot s)\rightarrow H^{1}(T_{S})
is injective.
Let
s
be a section of
L
with zero locus
S
. The exact sequences

0\rightarrow T_{S}\rightarrow T_{X|S}\rightarrow L_{|S}\rightarrow 0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad 0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\ s\ }L\rightarrow L_{|S}\rightarrow 0

give rise to a diagram

\textstyle{H^{0}(L)/(\mathbb{C}\!\cdot\!s)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\scriptstyle{T\mu}
\textstyle{H^{0}(T_{X|S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{u}
\textstyle{H^{0}(L_{|S})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial}
\textstyle{H^{1}(T_{S})}
\textstyle{H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})}

where the horizontal and vertical lines are exact.

We claim that
u
is an isomorphism; this implies that
\operatorname{Im}\varphi\cap\operatorname{Im}r=(0)
, hence that
T\mu
is injective. Since the vector bundle
T_{X}
is trivial, it is equivalent to consider the composition
\tilde{u}:H^{0}(T_{X})\vbox{\hbox to22.76228pt{\hfill{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\hfill}\hbox to22.76228pt{{\hfill$\longrightarrow$\hfill}}}H^{0}(T_{X|S})\xrightarrow{\ u\ }H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})

Lemma 1
.

\tilde{u}:H^{0}(T_{X})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
is the cup-product with the Chern class
c_{1}(L)\in H^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{X})
.

Proof
: We can find a finite covering
(U_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I}
of
X
such that
L_{|U_{\alpha}}
is free, generated by a section
e_{\alpha}
; on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
we have
e_{\alpha}=g_{\alpha\beta}e_{\beta}
for an invertible function
g_{\alpha\beta}
.
We write
s_{|U_{\alpha}}=s_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}
for a function
s_{\alpha}
on
U_{\alpha}
; then
s_{\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}s_{\alpha}
.

Let
V\in H^{0}(T_{X})
.
Then
\varphi(V)
is given on
U_{\alpha}\cap S
by
(V\cdot s_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}
.
This expression lifts to
U_{\alpha}
, and
u(V)=\partial\varphi(V)
is the class of the Čech 1-cocycle
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto c_{\alpha\beta}
such that :

c_{\alpha\beta}\,s=(V\cdot s_{\beta})e_{\beta}-(V\cdot s_{\alpha})e_{\alpha}=(V\cdot g_{\alpha\beta})g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}\,s\ \mbox{ in }\,U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}\,,\ \mbox{ hence }\ c_{\alpha\beta}=\langle{V,g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}\,dg_{\alpha\beta}}\rangle\,.

The class of the cocycle
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}\,dg_{\alpha\beta}
in
H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
is the Chern class
c_{1}(L)
, hence the lemma.∎

Lemma 2
.

\tilde{u}
is an isomorphism.

Proof
: We put
g=\dim X
, and choose an isomorphism
\Omega^{g}_{X}\vbox{\hbox to22.76228pt{\hfill{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\hfill}\hbox to22.76228pt{{\hfill$\longrightarrow$\hfill}}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. This gives an isomorphism
T_{X}\vbox{\hbox to22.76228pt{\hfill{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\hfill}\hbox to22.76228pt{{\hfill$\longrightarrow$\hfill}}}\Omega^{g-1}_{X}
; for
\alpha\in H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X})
, the map
\langle{-,\alpha}\rangle:T_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}
is identified with the
exterior product
\wedge\alpha:\Omega^{g-1}_{X}\rightarrow\Omega^{g}_{X}
. Therefore
\tilde{u}
is identified with the cup-product
\cup\,c_{1}(L):H^{0}(\Omega_{X}^{g-1})\rightarrow H^{1}(\Omega^{g}_{X})
. By the Lefschetz theorem the cup-product
\cup\,c_{1}(L):H^{g-1}(X,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{g+1}(X,\mathbb{C})
is an isomorphism, and it induces an isomorphism from
H^{g-1,0}
onto
H^{g,1}
, hence our assertion.∎

6.
Some more examples

We collect here some other examples of linear systems with maximal variation. We always assume that the linear systems we consider contain a smooth connected curve.

\bullet
If
H^{0}(X,T_{X})=0
and
L
is ample,
\lvert{L^{m}}\rvert
has maximal variation for
m\gg 0
: indeed we have
H^{1}(T_{X}\otimes L^{-m})=0
for
m\gg 0
.

\bullet
If
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is ample, every linear system
\lvert{L}\rvert
on
X
has maximal variation. Indeed for any
S\in\lvert{L}\rvert
the quotient
\Omega^{1}_{X|S}
is ample, hence does not admit a nonzero map to
\mathcal{O}_{S}
.

For surfaces we have a more precise result:

Proposition 7
.

Let
X
be a minimal surface of general type such that
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is generated by its global sections. Then any
very ample linear system
\lvert{L}\rvert
has maximal variation.

Proof
: For a general 1-form
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X})
, the zero locus
Z(\omega)
is finite, reduced and nonempty (since
c_{2}>0
). Let
p\in Z(\omega)
. Since
L
is very ample, we can find a smooth curve
C\in\lvert{L}\rvert
such that
C\cap Z(\omega)=\{p\}
. We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow K^{-1}_{X|C}(p)\rightarrow T_{X|C}\xrightarrow{\ \omega\ }\mathcal{O}_{C}(-p)\rightarrow 0\,.

By the index theorem we have
(K_{X}\cdot C)^{2}\geq K_{X}^{2}\cdot C^{2}>1
, therefore
\deg(K^{-1}_{X|C}(p))<0
and
H^{0}(T_{X|C})=0
. ∎

Proposition 8
.

Let
X
be a surface of general type such that

\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}\cdot[K_{X}]
and that the Chern classes of
X
satisfy

c_{1}^{2}>c_{2}
. The linear systems
\lvert{rK_{X}}\rvert
, for
r\geq 1
, have maximal variation.

Proof
: The proof is borrowed from
[
B1
]
. Let
C
be a smooth curve in
\lvert{rK_{X}}\rvert
.
Suppose that
T_{X|C}
has a nonzero section; this gives a surjective homomorphism
\Omega^{1}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}(-E)
, where
E
is an effective divisor. The kernel
F
of this homomorphism
is a rank 2 vector bundle with

c_{1}(F)=(r-1)c_{1}\ ,\quad c_{2}(F)=c_{2}-rc_{1}^{2}-\deg(E)\,,

hence

c_{1}^{2}(F)-4c_{2}(F)\geq(r+1)^{2}c_{1}^{2}-4c_{2}\geq 4(c_{1}^{2}-c_{2})>0\,.

By
[
Bo
, §10]

F
is unstable: there is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow K_{X}^{a}\rightarrow F\rightarrow\mathscr{I}_{Z}K_{X}^{b}\rightarrow 0

with
a\geq b
, and
Z
a finite subscheme of
X
.

This gives
c_{1}(F)=-(a+b)c_{1}
,
c_{2}(F)=\deg(Z)+abc_{1}^{2}
, hence

c_{1}^{2}(F)-4c_{2}(F)=(a-b)^{2}c_{1}^{2}-4\deg(Z)\,.

Comparing with the previous expression gives

(a-b)^{2}c_{1}^{2}\geq c_{1}(F)^{2}-4c_{2}(F)\geq(r+1)^{2}c_{1}^{2}-4c_{2}>(r^{2}+2r-3)c_{1}^{2}\geq(r-1)^{2}c_{1}^{2}

Since
a\geq b
, we conclude
a-b>r-1
, hence
a\geq 1
. This gives a nonzero homomorphism
K_{X}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X}
, hence a nonzero section of
T_{X}
, which is impossible. ∎

Proposition 9
.

Let
X=X_{1}\times X_{2}
, where
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are smooth projective varieties of dimension
\geq 2
, and
H^{0}(X_{i},T_{X_{i}})=0
.
Then any ample line bundle on
X
has maximal variation.

Proof
: Let
p_{i}
be the projection of
X
onto
X_{i}
.
The restriction
\bar{p}_{i}:S\rightarrow X_{i}
of
p_{i}
to an ample divisor
S\subset X
is surjective with connected fibers:
indeed for
x\in X_{i}
,
\bar{p}_{i}^{-1}(x)=S\cap(\{x_{i}\}\times X_{1-i})
is an ample divisor in
\{x_{i}\}\times X_{1-i}
, hence nonempty and connected. Therefore we have
(\bar{p}_{i})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}
, and

H^{0}((p_{i}^{*}T_{X_{i}})_{|S})=H^{0}(\bar{p}_{i}^{*}T_{X_{i}})=H^{0}(T_{X_{i}})=0\ ,\ \mbox{ hence }\ H^{0}(T_{X|S})=0\,.\qed

7.
Some counter-examples

\bullet
Suppose that a connected linear group
G
acts non trivially on
X
. Then
G
acts trivially on
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, hence acts on each linear system
\lvert{L}\rvert
on
X
. If this action is nontrivial,
\lvert{L}\rvert
does not have maximal variation. This is the case for instance if
L
is very ample, since one can find an element of
\lvert{L}\rvert
passing through a point of
X
and not containing its orbit.

\bullet
Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety,
E
a rank 2 very ample vector bundle on
Y
(that is,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
is very ample),
X=\mathbb{P}(E)
,
L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
.
A general element of
\lvert{L}\rvert
is isomorphic to
Y
, so
\lvert{L}\rvert
has no moduli.

\bullet
There are varieties of general type admitting a pencil with no moduli. Take for instance the Fermat hypersurface
F:\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}^{d}=0
. Consider the rational map
p:F\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
given by
p(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=(x_{0},x_{1})
. Let
\hat{F}\rightarrow F
be the blowing up of the codimension 2 subvariety given by
x_{0}=x_{1}=0
. Then
p
extends to a morphism
\hat{p}:\hat{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose general fiber is isomorphic to the Fermat hypersurface of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. The corresponding linear system
\lvert{\hat{p}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)}\rvert
has no moduli.

These examples lead to the following :

Question
.-

If
X
is not uniruled, does every ample linear system
\lvert{L}\rvert
have maximal variation
?

It is probably overoptimistic to expect a positive answer, but it seems not obvious to find a counter-example.

Tunable intertwining via collective excitations
András Szabó, R. Chitra

Abstract
The intertwining of multiple order parameters is a widespread phenomenon in equilibrium condensed matter systems, yet its exploration is often hindered by the complexity of real materials. Here, we present a controlled study of intertwined orders in a minimal driven-dissipative quantum-engineered platform. We consider a Bose-Einstein condensate at the intersection of two optical cavities, realizing two competing copies of a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry-breaking superradiant phase transition characterized by density wave orders. We show that collective excitations emerging at the order-to-order transition can be harnessed via periodic driving to stabilize a variety of dynamical phases, including intertwined orders. Our drive exploits dynamical symmetry reduction to generate a tunable mass gap in the excitation spectrum, offering a versatile mechanism for engineering intertwined dynamical orders in quantum-optical platforms.



Introduction
—Complex quantum materials have ushered in the development of new paradigms of physics, exploring a confluence of ordered states in correlated electron systems.
Such materials are often characterized by rich internal symmetries, hosting multiple symmetry-breaking channels, where distinct phases can coexist, and even induce novel ordered states.
Foremost among these, are the concepts of intertwined phases characterized by multi-component order parameters and fluctuation driven vestigial phases described by composite orders

Though these were first studied in the context of high-temperature cuprate superconductors

Periodic driving
—We now consider an asynchronous driving protocol in which
\lambda_{1,2}(t)=\lambda_{0}+\epsilon\cos(\omega t\mp\phi/2)
with
\epsilon
and
\omega
the driving amplitude and frequency, respectively, and a phase lag
\phi
between the driven cavities.
This protocol is similar in vein to the one used to induce
\eta
-paired superconducting states in
Kitamura and Aoki 2016
.
There, the time-averaged drive preserves the full SO(4) symmetry of the system while the instantaneous Hamiltonian typically preserves only the lower SU(2) symmetry.
In our work, for
\phi\neq 0
, even though the time-averaged drive preserves the U(1) symmetry, the instantaneous symmetry is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Note that a measure of the U(1) symmetry breaking can be obtained by
\Delta(\phi)=\overline{|\lambda_{1}(t)-\lambda_{2}(t)|}\sim\sin(\phi/2)
, where overline denotes time average and
-\pi\leq\phi\leq\pi
.

In the simpler Dicke model, a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry-preserving drive protocol was shown to result in new dynamical phases
Chitra and Zilberberg 2015
;
Molignini et al. 2018
, whenever the drive was parametrically resonant with a polaritonic mode.
Similarly, when
\phi=0
, we have a fully U(1) symmetry-preserving drive.
Then, for
\lambda_{0}-\epsilon>\lambda_{\rm c}
, the Goldstone mode in the SP remains pinned at zero energy and cannot be resonantly coupled to.
The other polaritonic and Higgs modes can be excited via straightforward parametric resonance akin to the Dicke model.
In contrast, for
\phi\neq 0
[red trajectory in Fig.
1
(b)], the instantaneous effective potential displays a time-dependent asymmetry, whereby the two sets of local
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
minima switch between local and global minimum during every cycle. This implies that all polaritonic excitation modes, including the original massless Goldstone mode, acquire a fluctuating gap.
However, for
\phi\neq 0
, the system is not amenable to a simple analysis of coupled Mathieu oscillators as done in the Dicke model and necessitates other approaches.

High-frequency expansion
—To shed light on the effects of asynchronous driving we employ a high-frequency or Magnus expansion.
To this end, we rewrite the Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H=H_{0}+\epsilon[O_{1}\cos(\omega t-\phi/2)+O_{2}\cos(\omega t+\phi/2)],

(2)

where
H_{0}=H\rvert_{\epsilon=0}
contains the time-independent part and
O_{i}=(a_{i}+a_{i}^{\dagger})(b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{0}+b_{0}^{\dagger}b_{i})/\sqrt{N}
.
A high-frequency expansion approximates the Floquet Hamiltonian as
H_{F}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\Omega_{n}
, where
T=2\pi/\omega
is the period of the drive.
Though this is technically valid only for large driving frequencies (beyond the range of resonant frequencies we are interested in), it is nonetheless illustrative of how
\phi
generates terms which lead to intertwining.
Here
\Omega_{0}=H_{0}
, whereas the first nontrivial term reads

\displaystyle 2\pi i\hbar\Omega_{1}=\epsilon\sin\Big(\frac{\phi}{2}\Big)[H_{0},O_{1}-O_{2}]+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\sin\phi[O_{1},O_{2}],

(3)

where the leading order term in
\epsilon
is indeed proportional to
\Delta(\phi)
.
Looking at this term, we can separate
H_{0}=h+\lambda_{0}(O_{1}+O_{2})
, where
h
is the noninteracting part of
H_{0}
, and note that it only involves the commutators
[h,O_{i}]
and
[O_{1},O_{2}]

For nonzero phase lag
\phi

(a_{i}^{\dagger}-a_{i})(b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{0}+b_{0}^{\dagger}b_{i})
and
(a_{i}^{\dagger}+a_{i})(b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{0}-b_{0}^{\dagger}b_{i})
which couple to both quadratures of a given cavity mode, akin to the interpolating Dicke-Tavis Cummings model.
The second, generates a direct coupling between the two cavities
(a_{1}^{\dagger}+a_{1})(a_{2}^{\dagger}+a_{2})(b_{1}^{\dagger}b_{2}-b_{2}^{\dagger}b_{1})
mediated by the atoms.
This is reminiscent of the
a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2}+{\rm h.c.}
term which couples to another atomic excitation mode engineered in
Morales et al. 2018
to induce intertwining.
Already to leading order in
\epsilon
, clearly
\phi\neq 0
reduces the symmetry of the Floquet Hamiltonian
H_{F}
from U(1)
\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
akin to the SO(4)
\to
SU(2) symmetry reduction in the problem of
\eta
-pairing
Kitamura and Aoki 2016

This consequently, endows the original Goldstone mode with an effective mass without recourse to complex Floquet protocols as done in
Hou et al. 2024
.
While we here only show the leading-order results of the Magnus expansion in
T
, higher orders involve nested commutators of
h

O_{1}
, and
O_{2}
, see
sup
.
This highlights the potential for intertwined orders by terms of the form
(a_{1}^{\dagger}+a_{1})(a_{2}^{\dagger}+a_{2})
, though caution needs to be exercised regarding the convergence of such high-frequency expansions when one approaches parametric instabilities.

Discussion & outlook
—
Our results can be verified in the well studied cross-cavity experimental set-up, where controlled driving via probe fields on the cavities was already used to identify the collective Higgs and Goldstone modes
Léonard 2017
.
The asynchronous driving can be implemented using a two-color pump, facilitating the independent modulation of the cavity modes.
Alternatively, we expect that the low-energy Goldstone mode is accessible via low-frequency modulation of the cavity geometries using piezo-electric devices.
To conclude, we showed that an interplay between drive and symmetry can be used to generate intertwined ordered phases in a minimal setup featuring two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
density-wave orders in a tunable cavity platform.
Though we see that resonant driving of the massive polaritonic excitations often leads to dynamical intertwining, an effective intertwining necessitates a coupling to the Goldstone mode rendered massive by the drive.
It would be exciting to explore if this latter feature involving engineered drives exploring symmetry-enhanced parameter regimes and soft modes provides a general pathway to realizing intertwined and vestigial phases in systems with competing orders.
We leave this for future work.

\displaystyle\omega_{b}\beta_{1}-4\lambda_{1}^{2}\frac{N-\beta^{2}}{\omega_{a}N}\beta_{1}+\frac{4\beta_{1}}{\omega_{a}N}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\beta_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\omega_{b}\beta_{2}-4\lambda_{2}^{2}\frac{N-\beta^{2}}{\omega_{a}N}\beta_{2}+\frac{4\beta_{2}}{\omega_{a}N}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\beta_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=0.

(6)

U(1)-symmetric case:
Along the U(1)-symmetric line
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\equiv\lambda
, and the two equations collapse into one and we obtain a solution for
\beta^{2}
as

\displaystyle\beta^{2}=\frac{N}{2}(1-\mu),

(7)

with
\mu=\frac{\omega_{a}\omega_{b}}{4\lambda^{2}}
.
The solution for the expectation values therefore spans a U(1) manifold as

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=\beta\cos\theta,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=\alpha\cos\theta,

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=\beta\sin\theta,

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sin\theta,

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}(1-\mu)},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{a}}\sqrt{N(1-\mu^{2})}.

(8)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric case:

Without the loss of generality, we set
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}
(and
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{c}
), then
\beta_{2}\neq 0
, but
\beta_{1}=0
.
The first equation in (
6
) in this case disappears, while in the second we can divide by
\beta_{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}(1-\mu_{2})},

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{a}}\sqrt{N(1-\mu_{2}^{2})},

(9)

with
\mu_{i}=\frac{\omega_{a}\omega_{b}}{4\lambda_{i}^{2}}
.
The solution for
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
follows in an identical way, and these results lead to the phase diagram indicated in Fig. 1(b) in the main body of the paper.

I.2
Fluctuations

To calculate the polariton energies we first construct the Hamiltonian describing fluctuations around the mean-field expectation values.
We once more make the substitution
a_{i}=\alpha_{i}+\hat{a}_{i}
,
b_{i}=\beta_{i}+\hat{b}_{i}
into Eq.
1
of the main text, supplying
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
from the previous section, then collect the terms quadratic in the cavity and atomic operators.
The fluctuation Hamiltonian
H_{F}
takes the general form

\displaystyle H_{F}=\omega_{a}a^{\dagger}a+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b+\lambda(a^{\dagger}+a)(b^{\dagger}+b)+\rho(b^{\dagger}+b)^{2}.

(10)

Defining
\tilde{\omega}_{b}^{2}=\omega_{b}^{2}+4\omega_{b}\rho
and
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda\sqrt{\omega_{b}/\tilde{\omega}_{b}}
, normal mode transformation yields

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\pm}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\omega_{a}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{b}^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\omega_{a}^{2}-\tilde{\omega}_{b}^{2})^{2}+16\tilde{\lambda}^{2}\omega_{a}\tilde{\omega}_{b}}\bigg).

(11)

The polariton energies in the normal phase are obtained via the substitution
\omega_{a}\to 1
,
\omega_{b}\to 1
,
\lambda\to\lambda_{i}
,
\rho\to 0
in Eq. (
11
), yielding

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon_{\pm}^{\rm NP}\big)^{2}=1\pm 2\lambda_{i}.

(12)

Once again we address the superradiant phase in the presence and absence of U(1) symmetry separately.

U(1)-symmetric case:
To show the emergence of Goldstone and Higgs modes, we recapitulate the results from Ref.
Léonard 2017
.
We make a change of variables in
H_{F}
as

\displaystyle b_{H}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta b_{1}+\sin\theta b_{2},

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta b_{G}+\cos\theta b_{H},

\displaystyle b_{G}

\displaystyle=-\sin\theta b_{1}+\cos\theta b_{2},

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta b_{G}+\sin\theta b_{H},

after which the Hamiltonian falls apart into Goldstone and Higgs contributions,
H[a_{i},b_{i}]=H_{G}+H_{H}
, where

\displaystyle H_{G}

\displaystyle=\omega_{a}a_{G}^{\dagger}a_{G}+\omega_{b}\frac{1+\mu}{2\mu}b_{G}^{\dagger}b_{G}

\displaystyle+\lambda\sqrt{\frac{1+\mu}{2}}(a_{G}^{\dagger}+a_{G})(b_{G}^{\dagger}+b_{G}),

\displaystyle H_{H}

\displaystyle=\omega_{a}a_{H}^{\dagger}a_{H}+\omega_{b}\frac{1+\mu}{2\mu}b_{H}^{\dagger}b_{H}

\displaystyle+\lambda\mu\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\mu}}(a_{H}^{\dagger}+a_{H})(b_{H}^{\dagger}+b_{H})

\displaystyle+\omega_{b}\frac{(1-\mu)(3+\mu)}{8\mu(1+\mu)}(b_{H}^{\dagger}+b_{H})^{2}.

(13)

Reading off
\tilde{\omega}_{b}
,
\tilde{\lambda}
, and
\rho
yields the polariton branches as

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon^{\rm GS}_{\pm}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg\{1+\Big(\frac{1+\mu}{2\mu}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{\bigg[1-\Big(\frac{1+\mu}{2\mu}\Big)^{2}\bigg]^{2}+4\lambda^{2}\frac{(1+\mu)^{2}}{\mu}}\Bigg\},

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon^{\rm H}_{\pm}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg\{1+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{5\mu^{4}-2\mu^{2}+1}{\mu^{4}}}\Bigg\}.

(14)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric case:
Just like before we consider
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}
(and
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{c}
), then
\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\neq 0
, but
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}=0
.
We supply the corresponding mean-field expectation values and notice that the fluctuation Hamiltonian again falls apart into contributions from cavity 1 and cavity 2 as

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\omega_{a}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+\omega_{b}\frac{1+\mu_{2}}{2\mu_{2}}b_{1}^{\dagger}b_{1}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}\sqrt{\frac{1+\mu_{2}}{2}}(a_{1}^{\dagger}+a_{1})(b_{1}^{\dagger}+b_{1}),

(15)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=\omega_{a}a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}+\omega_{b}\frac{1+\mu_{2}}{2\mu_{2}}b_{2}^{\dagger}b_{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}\mu_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\mu_{2}}}(a_{2}^{\dagger}+a_{2})(b_{2}^{\dagger}+b_{2})

\displaystyle+\omega_{b}\frac{(1-\mu_{2})(3+\mu_{2})}{8\mu_{2}(1+\mu_{2})}(b_{2}^{\dagger}+b_{2})^{2},

(16)

ultimately resulting in the normal modes

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon^{1}_{\pm}\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg\{1

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1+\mu_{2}}{2\mu_{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{\bigg[1-\Big(\frac{1+\mu_{2}}{2\mu_{2}}\Big)^{2}\bigg]^{2}+4\lambda_{1}^{2}\frac{(1+\mu_{2})^{2}}{\mu_{2}}}\Bigg\},

\displaystyle\big(\epsilon^{2}_{\pm}\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg\{1

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}^{2}}\pm\sqrt{\frac{5\mu_{2}^{4}-2\mu_{2}^{2}+1}{\mu_{2}^{4}}}\Bigg\}.

(17)

We show the polariton energies in the NP and SR phases in Fig.
1
(c) and (d) of the main text.

I.3
High-frequency expansion

The Floquet Hamiltonian is given by

H_{F}=\frac{-i\hbar}{T}\ln(1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n})\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\Omega_{n},

(18)

where the operators
P_{i}
are defined as

P_{n}=\Big(\frac{-i}{\hbar}\Big)^{n}\int_{0}^{T}dt_{1}\ \dots\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}dt_{n}H(t_{1})\dots H(t_{n}).

(19)

In the present problem, we first rewrite the time-dependent Hamiltonian as

H(t)=H_{0}+\epsilon O_{1}\cos\left(\omega t-{\frac{\phi}{2}}\right)+\epsilon O_{2}\cos\left(\omega t+{\frac{\phi}{2}}\right),

(20)

where
H_{0}\equiv H\rvert_{\epsilon=0}
and

\displaystyle O_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}+a_{1})(b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{1}),

\displaystyle O_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}+a_{2})(b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{2}).

(21)

Performing the nested time integrals, we obtain the following leading order terms in the expansion

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}TH_{0},

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\Big)^{2}\frac{T^{2}}{2\pi}\Big(\pi H_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\sin(\phi/2)[H_{0},O_{1}-O_{2}]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\sin{\phi}[O_{1},O_{2}].\Big)

(22)

To this order, we can now obtain the leading contribution to the Floquet Hamiltonian in the high driving frequency limit:

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{0},

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\hbar}{T^{2}}\bigg(P_{2}-\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{2}\bigg)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i\hbar}\Big(\epsilon\sin{\frac{\phi}{2}}[H_{0},O_{1}-O_{2}]+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\sin\phi[O_{1},O_{2}]\Big).

We first focus on the first nontrivial order
\Omega_{1}
.
As we are primarily concerned with the role of Higgs and Goldstone excitations, we only consider driving amplitudes
\epsilon/\lambda_{0}\ll 1
in this work.
Then, one can potentially neglect terms of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
and higher in the Magnus expansion.
Looking at the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
term, we rewrite
H_{0}
as

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1,2}\Big[\omega_{a}a_{i}^{{\dagger}}a_{i}+\omega_{b}b_{i}^{{\dagger}}b_{i}\Big]+\lambda_{0}(O_{1}+O_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv h+\lambda_{0}(O_{1}+O_{2}),

(24)

such that
[H_{0},O_{1}-O_{2}]=[h,O_{1}-O_{2}]-2\lambda_{0}[O_{1},O_{2}]
.
Calculating the commutators, we find

\displaystyle[h,O_{1}-O_{2}]=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{a}}{\sqrt{N}}[(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}-a_{1})(b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{1})-(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}-a_{2})(b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{2})]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{b}}{\sqrt{N}}[(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}+a_{1})(b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}-b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{1})-(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}+a_{2})(b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}-b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{2})],

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}[O_{1},O_{2}]={\frac{\lambda_{0}}{N}}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}+a_{1})(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}+a_{2})(b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{2}-b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{1}).

(25)

The first term shows that inducing a nonzero phase lag between the drives generates terms in the Hamiltonian which now couple to both quadratures of a given cavity mode.
The terms involving the commutator
[O_{1},O_{2}]
lead to a direct coupling between the two order parameter sectors.
This is reminiscent of intertwining studied earlier
Morales et al. 2018
.

Going to the next order,
P_{3}
is given by

\displaystyle(i\hbar)^{3}P_{3}={\frac{4\pi^{3}}{\omega^{3}}}H_{0}^{3}+\sum_{n=1}^{3}\epsilon^{n}C_{n},

(26)

with

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=2\cos{\frac{\phi}{2}}[H_{0},[H_{0},O_{1}+O_{2}]]+2\pi\sin{\frac{\phi}{2}}[H_{0}^{2},O_{2}-O_{1}],

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=[[H_{0},O_{1}],O_{1}]+[[H_{0},O_{2}],O_{2}]-\sin\phi\{H_{0},[O_{1},O_{2}]\}-\Big(\cos\phi-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big([[O_{1},H_{0}],O_{2}]+[[O_{2},H_{0}],O_{1}]\Big),

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=-\sin{\frac{\phi}{2}}\sin\phi[[O_{1},O_{2}],O_{2}-O_{1}].

(27)

where curly brackets denote anticommutators.
The second order correction to the Floquet Hamiltonian then takes the form:

\Omega_{2}=\frac{i\hbar}{T^{3}}\Big(P_{3}+{\frac{1}{3}}P_{1}^{3}-{\frac{1}{2}}(P_{1}P_{2}+P_{2}P_{1})\Big)

(28)

A simple glance does show that in general the terms in the Floquet Hamiltonian are rather complicated as we increase the order of the expansion.
Clearly, they do not sum to a simple closed form.
However, one can still obtain interesting information from such an expansion in certain limits.
In Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
), one finds that in first order in
\epsilon
, we typically get terms proportional to
\cos(\phi/2)
and
\sin(\phi/2)
.
On the other hand, in the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
terms the phase lag enters as
\cos\phi
and
\sin\phi
.
At a given order
n\geq 3
, the
\cos(\phi/2)
term involves a sum of

[H_{0}^{n-2},[H_{0},O_{1}+O_{2}]]
and
H_{0}\{O_{1}+O_{2},H_{0}^{n-3}\}H_{0}
alongside other terms involving nested commutators.
The
\sin(\phi/2)
terms on the other hand typically involve
[H_{0}^{n-1},O_{2}-O_{1}]
.

In the extreme limit where both cavity and atomic detunings are zero, we have
H_{0}=\lambda_{0}(O_{1}+O_{2})
.
Consequently, to generic orders of the Magnus expansion, we have the leading order in
\epsilon
terms which are proportional to
\cos(\phi/2)\sum_{n}p_{n}(O_{1}+O_{2})^{n}+\sin(\phi/2)\sum_{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}q_{n,m}(O_{1}+O_{2})^{n-m-2}[O_{1},O_{2}](O_{1}+O_{2})^{m}
where
p_{n}
and
q_{n,m}
are numerical prefactors. In this case, we see that if
\phi=0
,
\rm{Im}\langle b_{1}^{\dagger}b_{2}\rangle=0
, implying that a non-zero
\phi
is indeed necessary to establish this imaginary part, which will be maximal at
\phi=\pi
. Our simplifications highlight that drive indeed generates an intertwining of the different order parameter sectors. As we show in the main text, this leads to complex dynamical behaviour.

I.4
Heisenberg equations of motion

We write the equations of motion as
\mathrm{d}O/\mathrm{d}t=i\big[H,O\big]
describing unitary time evolution.
In case of the cavity operators
a_{i}
we incorporate leakage from the cavity as
\mathrm{d}a_{i}/\mathrm{d}t=i\big[H,a_{i}\big]-\kappa a_{i}
, with
\kappa
parametrizing dissipation.
The Heisenberg equations of motion read (everything shall be understood in terms of expectation values)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}_{a}a_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-i\bigg[(\omega_{a}-i\kappa)a_{i}-\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}(b_{i}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{i})\bigg],

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{i}^{{\dagger}}b_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=i\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{i}^{{\dagger}}+a_{i})(b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{i}-b_{i}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{i}^{{\dagger}}+a_{i})(b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{i}-b_{i}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=i\bigg[\omega_{b}b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}+a_{1})(b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}-b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{1})

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}+a_{2})b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{2}\bigg],

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=i\bigg[\omega b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}+a_{2})(b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}-b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}+a_{1})b_{2}^{{\dagger}}b_{1}\bigg],

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{2}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=i\bigg[\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}+a_{1})b_{0}^{{\dagger}}b_{2}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\sqrt{N}}(a_{2}^{{\dagger}}+a_{2})b_{1}^{{\dagger}}b_{0}\bigg].

Deformable Dynamic Convolution for Accurate yet Efficient Spatio-Temporal Traffic Prediction
Hyeonseok Jin, Geonmin Kim, Kyungbaek Kim

Abstract
Spatio-temporal traffic prediction plays a key role in intelligent transportation systems by enabling accurate prediction in complex urban areas. Although not only accuracy but also efficiency for scalability is important, some previous methods struggle to capture heterogeneity such as varying traffic patterns across regions and time periods. Moreover, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), which are the mainstream of traffic prediction, not only require predefined adjacency matrix, but also limit scalability to large-scale data containing many nodes due to their inherent complexity. To overcome these limitations, we propose Deformable Dynamic Convolution Network (DDCN) for accurate yet efficient traffic prediction. Traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are limited in modeling non-Euclidean spatial structures and spatio-temporal heterogeneity, DDCN overcomes these challenges by dynamically applying deformable filters based on offset. Specifically, DDCN decomposes transformer-style CNN to encoder-decoder structure, and applies proposed approaches to the spatial and spatio-temporal attention blocks of the encoder to emphasize important features. The decoder, composed of feed-forward module, complements the output of the encoder. This novel structure make DDCN can perform accurate yet efficient traffic prediction. In comprehensive experiments on four real-world datasets, DDCN achieves competitive performance, emphasizing the potential and effectiveness of CNN-based approaches for spatio-temporal traffic prediction.

Introduction

Traffic prediction is conducted using spatio-temporal data collected from numerous sensors installed throughout cities.
It generally aims to accurately predict the future traffic status based on historical spatio-temporal information
[
Xie et al.(2020)Xie, Li, Liu, Du, Yang, and Zhang
]
.
By capturing not only complex temporal correlations but also spatial dependencies among different urban areas, more accurate predictions can be achieved, contributing significantly to intelligent transportation systems
[
Wang et al.(2024)Wang, Liang, and Tan
]
.
For example, traffic flow prediction and accident risk prediction can help alleviate congestion and prevent accidents, thus promoting public welfare
[
Shaygan et al.(2022)Shaygan, Meese, Li, Zhao, and Nejad
]
.
Although traffic prediction was extremely challenging in the past, recent advances in AI and the availability of large-scale data have enabled more detailed analysis and attracted considerable attention
[
Zhao(2021)
,
Jiang et al.(2021)Jiang, Yin, Wang, Wang, Deng, Liu, Cai, Deng, Song, and Shibasaki
]
.

Consequently, as illustrated in Figure
1
, various deep learning models utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Long Short-Term Memorys (LSTMs), and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged, significantly improving prediction accuracy.
Initially, research efforts focused on applying CNNs and LSTMs to traffic prediction, inspired by their success in computer vision and natural language processing
[
Jiang et al.(2021)Jiang, Yin, Wang, Wang, Deng, Liu, Cai, Deng, Song, and Shibasaki
]
.
However, due to the inherently non-Euclidean distribution of traffic data, GNN-based approaches have become mainstream, leading to numerous studies leveraging graph structures
[
Yu et al.(2018)Yu, Yin, and Zhu
,
Diao et al.(2023)Diao, Wang, Zhang, Xie, Chen, Pei, Meng, Xie, and Zhang
]
.

Figure 1:
An illustration of deep learning based traffic prediction process. (a): CNN and LSTM, (b) GNN.

Despite significant progress, challenges remain in effectively capturing spatio-temporal heterogeneity and ensuring efficiency.
In addition, traffic prediction not only demands high accuracy but also scalability to larger datasets and broader tasks.
CNN and LSTM-based approaches offer simplicity and scalability but struggle to model the spatio-temporal heterogeneity inherent across different urban regions and time periods due to their use of shared parameter sets.
Traffic data typically encompass diverse regions such as residential and industrial zones, each exhibiting significantly different traffic patterns.
Additionally, traffic distributions vary markedly across different times of day and days of the week.
Effectively capturing such heterogeneity is crucial for enhancing prediction accuracy.
Nevertheless, previous methods often bias toward regions with higher traffic volume due to shared parameterization
[
Ji et al.(2023)Ji, Wang, Huang, Wu, Xu, Wu, Zhang, and Zheng
]
.

On the other hand, GNN-based methods excel at capturing the topological structures of traffic data but still face challenges in modeling heterogeneity and suffer from high model complexity
[
Jin et al.(2023)Jin, Liang, Fang, Shao, Huang, Zhang, and Zheng
]
.
They require pre-constructed adjacency matrix, and processing large-scale graphs incurs significant computational costs, making it difficult to efficiently handle all nodes and edges.

To overcome these limitations, we propose Deformable Dynamic Convolution Networks (DDCN).
Although GNN-based methods dominate the field, we revisit CNNs to address efficiency concerns.
While CNNs are highly efficient due to their optimized structure for parallelization
[
Gu et al.(2018)Gu, Wang, Kuen, Ma, Shahroudy, Shuai, Liu, Wang, Wang, Cai, et al.
]
, they inherently suffer from limitations in capturing spatio-temporal heterogeneity and non-Euclidean spatial structures, which are critical in traffic forecasting tasks due to their shared parameter set and static filter structure.
However, recent advances in computer vision have introduced structurally flexible convolution modules that mitigate these limitations.
Inspired by these advancements, we aim to overcome the inherent limitations of standard convolution and demonstrate their applicability to accurate yet efficient traffic prediction.

In the encoder, two novel attention blocks, spatio-temporal attention block and spatial attention block, are capture heterogeneity.
At the spatio-temporal attention block, Involution, which utilizes region-specific dynamic convolution, extended to spatio-temporal domain and effectively capture both spatial and temporal heterogeneity.
Furthermore, at the spatial attention block, novel deformable dynamic convolution (DDC) effectively capture non-Euclidean spatial structure and heterogeneity in spatial attention block.
Moreover, the transformer-style architecture enables the model to capture not only local but also global information, further improving predictive performance.
As a result, the encoder suppress irrelevant features and enable the model to focus on critical spatial and spatio-temporal patterns, thus enhancing its predictive accuracy.

In the decoder, a feedforward module composed of two MLPs complements the output of the encoder’s attention blocks.
This proposed architecture enables dynamic spatio-temporal traffic prediction with high accuracy and efficiency, facilitating easy extension to other traffic-related tasks and large-scale datasets.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We proposed novel deformable dynamic convolution network, DDCN, which can consider spatio-temporal heterogeneity and non-Euclidean spatial structure for accurate yet efficient traffic prediction.

•

We integrate and extend various advanced convolution, tailoring them specifically for traffic prediction tasks and emphasizing the usefulness of CNNs in this fields.

•

Comprehensive comparison experimental results on four real-world datasets are demonstrate the effectiveness of DDCN, emphasizing the potential of proposed approach.

2
Related Work

2.1
Traffic prediction

Traffic prediction is a supervised learning paradigm that learns historical spatio-temporal information to predict future traffic status.
Initial traffic prediction is performed based on statistic approaches
[
Smith and Demetsky(1997)
,
Yu and Zhang(2004)
,
Caliendo et al.(2007)Caliendo, Guida, and Parisi
]
, or machine learning approaches
[
Lv et al.(2009)Lv, Tang, and Zhao
,
Sharma et al.(2016)Sharma, Katiyar, and Kumar
]
.
These initial approaches struggle to modeling the complex spatio-temporal correlations inherent in traffic data due to the only applied time series methods
[
Luo et al.(2024)Luo, He, Han, Wang, and Li
,
Ahmed et al.(2024)Ahmed, Kuldeep, Rafa, Fazal, Hoque, Liu, and Gandomi
]
.

To solve this problem, spatio-temporal prediction approaches are attracted much attention.
LCGBN
[
Zhu et al.(2016)Zhu, Peng, Xiong, and Zhang
]
proposed a framework that can consider both spatial and temporal information with a linear conditional gaussian bayesian network model.
They showed that including spatial and velocity data can improve the prediction performance and emphasizing the importance of spatio-temporal modeling.
ST-ResNet
[
Zhang et al.(2017)Zhang, Zheng, and Qi
]
proposed residual learning framework based on CNNs.
They demonstrated its usefulness by using two types of real-world traffic flow datasets, obtained from Beijing and New York.

Recently, due to the inherent non-Euclidean distribution of traffic data, there has been a growing trend of leveraging GNNs for effectively capture the structure of traffic data.
STGCN
[
Yu et al.(2018)Yu, Yin, and Zhu
]
proposed novel framework, which integrates graph convolution and temporal convolution blocks.
They emphasized the potential on exploring spatio-temporal structures of GNN.
GMAN
[
Zheng et al.(2020)Zheng, Fan, Wang, and Qi
]
proposed novel GNN-based encoder-decoder architecture composed of multiple spatio-temporal attention blocks that utilize multi-attention mechanism.
ST-SSL
[
Ji et al.(2023)Ji, Wang, Huang, Wu, Xu, Wu, Zhang, and Zheng
]
proposed self-supervised learning based traffic prediction framework, emphasized the importance of capturing heterogeneity in traffic data.
Proposed two novel adaptive graph augmentation and self-supervised learning paradigm overcome the limitations in previous works and effectively capture spatio-temporal heterogeneity.
AdpSTGCN
[
Chen et al.(2024)Chen, Tang, Wu, Shen, Li, et al.
]
proposed novel adaptive STGCN, which applies not only adjacency matrix, but also generated graph values.
Additional graph values are generated by multi-head attention, allows to consider complex spatial correlations, and proposed adaptive graph convolution and gated dilated causal convolution effectively integrates spatio-temporal features.

In traffic prediction research, GNN-based models that can express non-Euclidean space well are being proposed, moving beyond the initial statistical and CNN-based methods.
Despite these remarkable developments, GNNs not only require adjacency matrix, but also inherently increase computational complexity and reduce efficiency in large nodes
[
Ma et al.(2022)Ma, Rong, and Huang
,
Duan et al.(2022)Duan, Liu, Wang, Zheng, Zhou, Chen, Hu, and Wang
]
.
This can be a fatal drawback in the field of traffic prediction where scalability is essential
[
Jyothi et al.(2023)Jyothi, Rao, Mani, Anusha, Harshini, Bhavsingh, and Lavanya
]
.

2.2
Variant Convolutions

Typically, when extracting features, standard convolutions move and apply a single filter over the entire space per channel axis.
Convolution operations are optimized for parallelization, making them efficient and effective in a variety of fields, but in the field of traffic prediction, this characteristic makes it difficult to capture heterogeneity.

However, variant convolution architectures have recently been proposed.
DCN
[
Dai et al.(2017)Dai, Qi, Xiong, Li, Zhang, Hu, and Wei
]
proposed deformable convolution to overcome limitations due to the fixed geometric structures in standard convolutions.
The authors have integrated separate offset parameters into convolution to make filters more flexible, thereby overcoming limitations and improving performance.
Kervolution
[
Wang et al.(2019)Wang, Yang, Xie, and Yuan
]
proposed non-linear convolution to address the limited expressiveness of standard convolutions.
The authors generalized convolution into a non-linear kernel space, allows patch-wise non-linear feature extraction without increasing the number of parameters.
Involution
[
Li et al.(2021)Li, Hu, Wang, Li, She, Zhu, Zhang, and Chen
]
proposed a novel dynamic convolution that uses filters that are dynamically generated by spatial location.
Unlike standard convolution, it effectively extracts spatial features based on dynamic filters, and is used in various tasks such as object detection
[
Ye et al.(2022)Ye, Qin, Li, Wang, Zhang, and Zhao
]
and video prediction
[
Zhu et al.(2025)Zhu, Lai, Gan, Liu, and Gao
]
.
On the other hand, Kim et al
[
Kim et al.(2023)Kim, Tanaka, Sasaki, and Okutomi
]
proposed novel approaches, which combine deformable and dynamic convolution to improve flexibility of existing CNN architectures.
The authors proposed element-wise dynamic convolution to obtain the each output of dynamic convolution, and change the shape of filters by applying an offset values.

These variant convolution approaches have shown promising results in computer vision tasks by improving flexibility and representational power.
Inspired by these advances, we explore the potential of applying deformable and dynamic convolutions to traffic prediction, aiming to achieve both accuracy and efficiency without relying on graph structures.

.

, defined in a real space, with batch size
B
, time steps
T
, number of features
C
, height
H
, and width
W
.
Given the input data
\boldsymbol{x}_{1:T}
, for time steps
T
, the goal is make predictions for the next time step
T+1
.
Since the time step of the output grid data
\boldsymbol{x}_{T+1}
is one, can be represented as four-dimensional tensor with
T

\boldsymbol{x}_{T+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C\times H\times W}
.
The model
F
updates the weights
w
as
1
:

\begin{split}&\boldsymbol{\hat{x}}_{T+1}=F(\boldsymbol{x}_{1:T})\\
&\boldsymbol{w}^{*}=\arg\min_{w}\ell(\boldsymbol{\hat{x}}_{T+1},\boldsymbol{x}_{T+1})\end{split}

(1)

Training is performed in the direction of minimizing the difference between predicted value
\boldsymbol{\hat{x}}_{T+1}
and real value
\boldsymbol{x}_{T+1}
using a loss function
\ell
.
The model optimizes

to make predicted value that are close to real value.

3.2
Deformable Dynamic Convolution

In this section, we introduce Deformable Dynamic Convolution (DDC), the core component of our proposed framework, aiming to enhance spatial adaptability and dynamic feature extraction for traffic prediction tasks.
Figure
3
shows the internal architecture of DDC.
This module integrates two separate yet complementary branches: (1) a deformable layer that predicts learnable offsets for each spatial position, and (2) a dynamic layer that generates region-specific filters through point-wise convolution.
These two outputs are subsequently combined to perform content-aware and structure-aware convolution simultaneously.

Figure 3:
An illustration of deformable dynamic convolution process.

As shown in Figure
4
, Unlike standard convolution, which applies fixed, shared filters to regularly sampled positions, DDC applies region-specific filters through an dynamic layer.
This enables the convolutional operator to be spatially aware, producing different filters at each location to better reflect local context and capture heterogeneity.
In parallel, deformable layer estimates offsets for each kernel position, allowing the model to capture geometric variations and focus on capturing non-Euclidean spatial structure in traffic data by adaptively adjusting the receptive field.

Figure 4:
Different stratigies between variant convolutions. (a): standard convolution, (b): dynamic convoltion, (c): deformable convolution, and (d): proposed deformable dynamic convolution.

Formally, for each spatial location
\boldsymbol{p}_{0}
, DDC computes the output as a weighted sum of input features sampled at offset positions, where the weights are dynamically generated, as shown in
2
:

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{Y}_{\text{DDC}}(\boldsymbol{p}_{0})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{p}_{n}\in\mathcal{R}}\boldsymbol{w}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{0}}(\boldsymbol{p}_{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{X}(\boldsymbol{p}_{0}+\boldsymbol{p}_{n}+\Delta\boldsymbol{p}_{n})\end{split}

(2)

Here,
\mathcal{R}
denotes the local receptive field,
\boldsymbol{w}_{\boldsymbol{p}_{0}}(\boldsymbol{p}_{n})
is the dynamic kernel generated at location
\boldsymbol{p}_{0}
, and
\Delta\boldsymbol{p}_{n}
represents the learnable offset at position
\boldsymbol{p}_{n}
.
This formulation allows DDC to simultaneously model content-aware filtering and structure-aware deformation, which are critical for accurately capturing the heterogeneous and irregular patterns in spatio-temporal data such as urban traffic flow.

Furthermore, we extend Involution to the spatio-temporal domain as Involution
{}_{\text{3D}}
, allowing the use of dynamic filters along both spatial and temporal axes.
The output of Involution
3D
can be formulated as
3
, where the dynamic weights
\boldsymbol{w}(i,j,k)
are learned for each position in the spatio-temporal volume
(T,H,W)
:

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{Y}_{\text{Inv3D}}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\sum_{k=1}^{W}\left(\boldsymbol{w}(i,j,k)\cdot\boldsymbol{X}(i,j,k)+\boldsymbol{b}\right)\end{split}

(3)

3.3
DDCN Structure

Figure 5:
(a): Overall structure of DDCN, (b): Detail structure of Spatial Attention Block, (c): Detail structure of Spatio-temporal (ST) Attention Block.
\oplus
denotes element-wise add and
\otimes
denotes for hadamard product.
The encoder captures spatio-temporal and spatial information through a ST Attention Block and a Spatial Attention Block, and passes them to the decoder. The decoder performs the role of combining the received information.

The overall structure of DDCN is shown in (a) of Figure
5
.
DDCN consists of an encoder that extracts key information and a decoder that complements them.
In addition, before the encoder receives data, it divides the data into non-overlapping patches through patch embedding and increases the channels, and the output of the decoder performs size restoration by reducing the channels through patch back and increasing the width and height.

Encoder

The encoder that extracts the core information is based on two attention blocks. The structure of each attention block is shown in (b) and (c) of Figure
5
.
The Involution
3D
, which extends Involution to spatio-temporal dimensions, and DDC are applied to the spatio-temporal attention block (ST Att Block) and the spatial attention block (Spatial Att Block) to learn through dynamic filters.
These blocks can learn spatio-temporal and spatial features variably and can consider heterogeneity and non-Euclidean spatial structure.
In addition, it accesses global information and increases nonlinearity through transformer-style CNN and GELU activation function.
This further enriches the expressive power of the model and complements the shortcomings of existing CNN.
ST Att Block process is expressed as
4
.
\sigma
denotes GELU activation function.

\begin{split}&\boldsymbol{x}_{ST}=\text{Patch Embed}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1:T})\\
&\boldsymbol{V}_{ST}=\text{PW-Conv}_{3D}(\boldsymbol{x}_{ST})\\
&\boldsymbol{Att}_{ST}=\text{Involution}_{3D}(\sigma(\text{PW-Conv}_{3D}(\boldsymbol{x}_{ST}))\\
&\text{ST Att Block}=\boldsymbol{V}_{ST}\otimes\boldsymbol{Att}_{ST}\\
\end{split}

(4)

When learning spatial features, batch size dimension
B
is multiplied by time steps dimension
T
to compress the dimension of the input data, and it is converted into a single image format to access only the spatial features. Also, unlike the ST Att Block, which has 3D convolution applied, the Spatial Att Block has 2D convolution applied. Spatial Att Block process is expressed as
5
:

\begin{split}&\boldsymbol{x}_{S}=\text{ST Att Block}\oplus\boldsymbol{x}_{ST}\\
&\boldsymbol{V}_{S}=\text{PW-Conv}(\boldsymbol{x}_{S})\\
&\boldsymbol{Att}_{S}=\text{DDC}(\sigma(\text{PW-Conv}(\boldsymbol{x}_{S}))\\
&\text{Spatial Att Block}=\boldsymbol{V}_{S}\otimes\boldsymbol{Att}_{S}\\
\end{split}

(5)

Each block of the encoder learns heterogeneity through a dynamic kernel and multiplies it to produce an attention matrix. The model can focus on important features by adding the output of each block to the input data.

Decoder

We use a Feed Forward decoder to integrate various factors affecting the prediction into the channel axis and perform effective traffic prediction by taking these factors into account.
Additionally, encoder-decoder structure allows the Attention and Feed Forward modules to be stacked deeply so that they can focus on more finely important features.
Feed Forward modules that make up the decoder are structed by two Point-wise CNNs (
PW-Conv
), which play a role similar to a Multi Layer Perceptron (MLP). Finally, the output of the DDCN
F(x_{in})
is expressed as follows
6
:

\begin{split}&Enc_{out}=\text{Spatial Att Block}\oplus\boldsymbol{x}_{S}\\
&\text{Feed Forward}=\text{PW-Conv}(\text{PW-Conv}(Enc_{out}))\\
&Dec_{out}=\text{Feed Forward}\oplus Enc_{out}\\
&\boldsymbol{\hat{x}}_{T+1}=\text{PatchBack}(Dec_{out})\end{split}

(6)

Feed Forward modules effectively complement the outputs of the encoders by doubling and restoring the dimensionality.

Evaluation metrics

We use three metrics, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), to evaluate traffic prediction performance.
RMSE calculates the square root of the average of squared differences between predicted and actual values, and is more sensitive to large errors due to the squaring operation.
MAE measures the average of the absolute differences between predicted value
\hat{y}
and actual value
y
, providing a scale-dependent error.
MAPE calculates the average of the absolute percentage differences between predicted and actual values, offering intuitive that representation of errors as a percentage of the actual values.
The lower values of these indicators, model make predictions that are similar to the actual.
Each evaluation metric are represented as
7
:

\begin{split}&\text{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat{y}_{i}-y_{i})^{2}}\\
&\text{MAE}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\hat{y}_{i}-y_{i}\right|\\
&\text{MAPE}=\frac{100}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\hat{y}_{i}-y_{i}}{y_{i}}\right|\end{split}

(7)

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a novel CNN-based traffic prediction model, DDCN for accurate yet efficient spatio-temporal traffic prediction.
Our DDCN overcomes the inherent limitations of standard convolution in traffic data based on deformable dynamic convolution.
Specifically, it effectively captures non-Euclidean spatial structures, and the extended involution-based dynamic convolution captures not only spatial but also temporal heterogeneities.
Furthermore, transformer-syle CNN backbone decomposed into an encoder-decoder architecture emphasizes only important features, and this novel structures of DDCN enables to perform accurate yet efficient traffic prediction.
Comprehensive experiments on four real-world datasets demonstrate that DDCN achieves competitive performance, emphasizing the effectiveness of proposed approach.
Compared to baselines, DDCN achieves significant improvements.
It requires at least 13% fewer parameters and 67% fewer FLOPs, while maintaining or outperforming prediction accuracy.
These results suggest that DDCN offers an excellent speed-to-accuracy trade-off and emphasized the effectiveness of CNN-based approaches for spatio-temporal traffic prediction.
In future work, we plan to optimize the DDCN structure and integrate external factors to expand it to various traffic datasets and prediction tasks.

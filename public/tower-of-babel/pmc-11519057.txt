Patient-Reported Adverse Events Among Elderly Patients Receiving Novel Oral COVID-19 Antivirals: A Nationwide Sampled Survey in Korea
Authors: Hyunah Jung, Ji Yeon Park, Dongwon Yoon, Dong Yoon Kang, Jaehun Jung, Ju Hwan Kim, Ju-Young Shin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere is a dearth of research on the factors linked with adverse events (AEs) associated with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (NMVr) and molnupiravir (MOL), particularly in the elderly. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate self-reported AEs and identify factors associated with the occurrence of AEs following NMVr or MOL treatment among survey participants aged 60 years or older in South Korea.MethodsThis nationwide survey was conducted through in-person interviews using structured questionnaires, from July 24 to August 31, 2023. Eligible participants included individuals aged 60 years or older who had been diagnosed with coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) and received NMVr or MOL. The study outcomes included self-reported demographic, lifestyle, and health characteristics associated with the occurrence of AEs. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to estimate the adjusted odds ratio (aOR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) of each characteristic in participants with and without AEs.ResultsOf the 520 participants, 123 (23.7%) experienced at least one AE with oral COVID-19 treatment: 21.0% (96/458) for NMVr and 43.5% (27/62) for MOL. None of the participants reported any serious AEs. Increased odds of AE occurrence were observed in participants treated with MOL compared to those treated with NMVr (aOR, 3.05; 95% CI, 1.67–5.57), a history of two or more compared to one COVID-19 diagnosis (1.93; 1.03–3.62), and self-reported health status as “Unhealthy” compared to “Healthy” (2.65; 1.31–5.36).ConclusionNo AEs required further evaluation to change treatment strategies in elderly patients on NMVr or MOL. Several factors, including the use of MOL, history of COVID-19, and reported health status, were associated with an increased incidence of AEs. Both treatments may still be useful choices for patients with non-severe COVID-19 aged 60 years or older. However, close monitoring of unidentified potential harm and further investigation of the factors associated with the occurrence of AEs are needed.

INTRODUCTION

In 2023, the World Health Organization (WHO) reclassified coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) from a Public Health Emergency of International Concern to an endemic. This is because population immunity has increased due to COVID-19 vaccination and infection, and mortality rates have decreased, resulting in reduced pressure on healthcare systems.1 In a previous study, the rate of severe COVID-19 was found to have decreased from 2.14% to 0.14%, with a corresponding trend in the fatality rate from 0.95% to 0.10% during the transition from the delta to omicron variant2; these changes were consistent across the globe.345 As the rates of developing severe COVID-19 and mortality decline, the treatment environment for patients with non-severe COVID-19 is shifting to an outpatient setting using newly developed oral COVID-19 medications (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir [NMVr] and molnupiravir [MOL]).

NMVr and MOL were granted emergency use authorization by regulatory agencies for the treatment of mild-to-moderate COVID-19 in adults and pediatric patients (NMVr in persons aged > 12 years and MOL in those aged > 18 years) who are at risk of progression to severe COVID-19, based on phase III clinical trials (EPIC-HR for NMVr and MOVe-OUT for MOL).6789 In South Korea, NMVr and MOL received emergency use authorization from the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) in December 2021 and March 2022, respectively.1011 In July 2023, NMVr received full approval from the MFDS.12 The use of these drugs is prioritized in patients aged 60 years or older who are at risk of progressing to severe COVID-19.

In the pivotal trials of NMVr (EPIC-HR) and MOL (MOVe-OUT), adverse events (AEs) were mostly mild to moderate, including dysgeusia, diarrhea, and muscle pain in the NMVr group, and diarrhea, nausea, and dizziness in the MOL group.67 Although no safety issues were identified in the two trials, the data were obtained from a strictly limited study population; therefore, it is difficult to rule out the possibility of unexpected AEs in real-world practice.

Emerging data suggest that the post-marketing safety profiles of NMVr and MOL are largely consistent with those described in their trial settings. In one post-marketing surveillance study, the AE rate in 3,179 patients treated with MOL in Japan was 5.5%, with the most common AE being diarrhea (1.9%), followed by rash (0.7%).13 In a recent data analysis of the Food and Drug Administration AE reporting system (FAERS), disproportionate reporting of dysgeusia compared with other COVID-19 therapeutics was reported.14 Another surveillance study identified high reporting rates of disease recurrence (40.4%), dysgeusia (17.6%), and diarrhea (8.8%) in an analysis of spontaneous AE reports of NMVr.15 While these studies were imperative in establishing the real-world safety of NMVr and MOL, there are relatively few studies on the factors that may associate patients to an increased risk of experiencing AEs from oral COVID-19 treatment.

This study aimed to investigate self-reported AEs and identify the various factors associated with the occurrence of AEs after oral COVID-19 treatment among the elderly population in South Korea.

METHODS

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study aimed to identify the factors associated with an increased risk of developing AEs from NMVr and MOL among an elderly population (≥ 60 years old) in South Korea. The focus on this specific age group was based on the COVID-19 Treatment Usage Guidelines (10-1st Edition, February 27, 2023) issued by the Korean health authorities. These guidelines explicitly designate individuals aged 60 and older as a primary criterion for COVID-19 treatment eligibility and identify this age group as a priority for treatment due to their higher risk of developing severe illness. Considering the importance of this elderly population, our study aimed to gain a comprehensive understanding of their treatment experiences and AEs. To achieve this, a face-to-face survey was conducted by a third-party vendor, Gallup Korea, affiliated with Gallup International, between July 24th and August 31st, 2023. A proportional allocation method was used to stratify the survey panels according to age, sex, and region. A total of 1,158 eligible participants were approached, 528 were engaged, and 520 completed the survey, yielding a response rate of 45.2%.

The inclusion criteria were adults aged 60 years or older, with proportional allocation by age, sex, and region to ensure representativeness of the South Korean population. Individuals who did not receive any oral COVID-19 treatment, as determined through a screening question about medication use, were excluded.

To ensure the sample accurately represented the South Korean population, we used a proportional allocation method to stratify the survey participants by region (Incheon/Gyeonggi, Daejeon/Sejong/Chuncheon, Gwangju/Jeolla/Jeju, Daegu/Gyeongbuk, Busan/Ulsan/Gyeongnam), sex, and age group (60s, 70+). According to the 2022 population census data from the Statistics Korea, the total population of South Koreans aged 60 years and above was 13,702,757, with 6,269,311 men and 7,433,446 women.

The sample size of each stratum was determined based on its proportion in the total population, to ensure that each subgroup was appropriately represented. The target sample size was approximately 500, and it was determined through a combination of statistical considerations. This sample size was calculated using power analysis, aiming for a confidence level of 95% and a margin of error of ± 4.4% for a large population. This level of precision is generally accepted in social science research and allows for meaningful analysis of subgroups within the sample. A detailed explanation of sample size estimation is provided in the Supplementary Data 1.

Measurements

The survey began with the question of whether the participant had received a prescription for NMVr and/or MOL. This was followed by inquiries into any AEs experienced during oral COVID-19 treatment using a checklist approach for symptoms, such as muscle pain, dizziness, dysgeusia, and headache, while also allowing for the reporting of any additional symptoms not listed.

The seriousness of each AE was determined by asking the participants whether they had been hospitalized or experienced an extended hospital stay because of the AE. Specifically, the participants were given the following response options: hospitalization (including emergency room [ER] visit and intensive care unit admission), extended hospital stay, life-threatening condition(s), or disability/permanent damage. For those without serious AEs, the severity of the AE was recorded using the following response options: mild symptoms not affecting daily activities, symptoms interfering with daily life, or symptoms requiring medical attention without hospitalization.

The questionnaire also included sections on demographic and clinical characteristics including age, sex, smoking status, monthly alcohol consumption, place of residence, self-reported health status, prescription medication use, underlying disease(s), and COVID-19 vaccination status. The questionnaire used in this study is provided in Supplementary Data 2.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the overall incidence of self-reported AEs after oral COVID-19 treatment in the elderly. These AEs were categorized according to symptom type, duration, and severity. We further assessed the factors associated with AEs to identify the association between the demographic or clinical characteristics of the participants and the occurrence of AEs following oral COVID-19 treatment.

Subsequently, to put these self-reported outcomes into perspective, we compared them with the findings from the EPIC-HR and MOVe-OUT trials. This comparison was designed to complement our primary data, helping us understand how the AEs identified in a real-world setting align with those observed in more controlled clinical trial environments.

Statistical analysis

The overall characteristics of the participants are summarized using means ± standard deviation for continuous variables and frequencies (percentage) for categorical variables. The baseline characteristics of the AE and non-AE groups were compared using the χ2 test for discrete variables.

We weighted the study participants to adjust for potential under- or over-representation in the sample. The weights were calculated based on age, sex, and regional strata, using data from the 2022 population data by the Statistics Korea. This weighting approach ensures that the results are representative of the broader South Korean population aged 60 years and older and adjusts for potential sampling biases.

Applying these weights, we performed univariate and multivariate logistic regression models to calculate the weighted odds ratios (ORs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for each characteristic in participants with and without AEs. The regression models included factors such as age, sex, history of COVID-19, smoking status, monthly alcohol consumption, COVID-19 vaccination status, self-reported health status, prescription medication use, and underlying medical condition. The associations between these factors and the occurrence of AEs were evaluated.

All statistical analyses were performed using the SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Ethics statement

Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of Sungkyunkwan University (approval number: SKKU 2023-06-028). All participants provided written informed consent and their data were kept anonymous.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics

Of the 520 participants who completed the survey, 43.5% were aged 70 years or older, and 53.8% were female; 88.1% received NMVr and 89.0% were diagnosed with COVID-19 for the first time (Table 1). Some statistically significant differences in the baseline characteristics observed between participants who reported AEs and those who did not. Among participants who received NMVr, 78.1% reported AEs compared to 91.2% who did not report AEs (P < 0.001). Participants with 2nd or subsequent diagnosis constituted 17.1% of the AE group and 9.1% of the non-AE group (P = 0.013). Differences were observed in the distribution of self-reported health status, with 26.0% in the AE group and 13.1% in the non-AE group reporting their health as “Unhealthy” (P < 0.001). No statistically significant differences in AE rates were found based on age, sex, smoking status, alcohol consumption, COVID-19 vaccination status, or other underlying medical conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, or obesity. Additionally, baseline characteristics of survey participating respondents by drug types were provided in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2.

Reported AEs

The overall self-reported AE rate was 23.7%, with NMVr at 21.0% and MOL at 43.5%. Importantly, all reported AEs were non-serious, with no participants experiencing severe AEs that required hospitalization or resulted in lasting health issues. The most frequently reported AEs were muscle pain (9.6%), dizziness (6.7%), dysgeusia (3.5%), and headache (2.9%), consistent with the EPIC, EPIC-HR, and MOVe-OUT trials (Table 2).

Factors associated with the occurrence of AEs

The occurrence of an AE was higher in patients administering MOL than NMVr (aOR, 3.05; 95% CI, 1.67–5.57), those with a history at least two COVID-19 diagnoses compared to those who were diagnosed with the disease once (first COVID-19 infection) (1.93; 1.03–3.62), and those with a self-reported health status of “Unhealthy” compared to those with a “Healthy” status (2.65; 1.31–5.36) (Table 3).

DISCUSSION

The aim of this nationwide survey was to investigate AEs associated with oral COVID-19 treatment and identify factors associated with AE occurrence. The findings suggest that approximately one-quarter of the participants experienced at least one AE while receiving oral COVID-19 medication. The self-reported AEs were generally mild and transient symptoms that were previously described in clinical trials of NMVr and MOL.67 Moreover, participants those who treated with MOL, diagnosed multiple COVID-19, and considered their health status as unhealthy, were at risk of experiencing AEs while taking oral COVID-19 medications.

Overall, the AEs reported in this study were in line with those described in clinical trials67 and post-marketing studies.131416 However, although the rate of AEs was similar in the NMVr group, it was slightly higher in the MOL group than in the trials (NMVr group; 21.0% in this study vs. 22.6% in the clinical trial, MOL group; 43.5% in this study vs. 30.4% in the clinical trial). Patients treated with MOL experienced more AEs than those who received NMVr (43.5% vs. 21.0%, respectively). This was unexpected given the existing data that showed that the AE rate from NMVr was higher than that from MOL administration. One retrospective cohort study in Italy reported that AEs were higher in patients taking NMVr (19.1%) than those taking MOL (6.9%).17 Another prospective observational study also reported more frequent AEs with NMVr (49.2%) than with MOL (21.1%).18 Such discrepancies in the rates across different studies may be explained by differences in the characteristics of patients receiving the treatment agents. For instance, in our study, compared to those on NMVr, participants who were administering MOL were older, more likely to be female, and generally in poor health. This group also had higher proportions of patients with partial or no COVID-19 vaccination, a history of at least two COVID-19 diagnoses, and prescription medications. The baseline characteristics of the participants who received MOL were consistent with those described in other studies. A retrospective cohort study of 93,883 outpatient clinic patients in Hong Kong found that those taking MOL were older and had more comorbidities, had a lower complete vaccination rate, and experienced more hospitalizations in the previous year than those taking NMVr.19 Another prospective observational study in Korea performed in high-risk outpatients with COVID-19 showed similar results: patients on MOL were older and had a higher Charlson comorbidity index than those on NMVr,20 implying that the former group had a more vulnerable health status. In general, NMVr is the preferred COVID-19 treatment for effectiveness in reducing hospitalization and mortality rates21222324; however, it is not recommended in patients with severe renal or hepatic disease or in those taking contraindicated medications.925 This restriction may be another reason why patients who are prescribed MOL are older and unhealthier than those who receive NMVr.

Some AEs were reported more or less frequently in this survey than those from the EPIC-HR and MOVe-OUT trials. In this study, patients reported experiencing more AEs, including muscle pain, dizziness, and headache than those reported in the clinical trials. In the EPIC-HR trial (NMVr clinical trial), muscle pain, dizziness, and headache were reported to be 0.6%, 0.4%, and 1.4%, respectively, compared to 9.2%, 5.9%, and 2.6% in this study. Similarly, in the MOL clinical trial (MOVe-OUT), all three AEs occurred at the 1.0% level, compared with 12.9%, 12.9%, and 4.8%, respectively, in this study. This may be attributed to differences in the characteristics of COVID-19 and the study design. While AEs are collected and managed by healthcare professionals according to strict standards in clinical trials, respondents of the survey study may have had a difficulty in distinguishing COVID-19 symptoms from AEs caused by COVID-19 therapy. Because muscle pain, dizziness, and headaches are typical symptoms of COVID-19,17 these AEs may have been detected at a higher rate.

In contrast, dysgeusia in the NMVr group was reported at a lower rate in this study than in the clinical trial. Dysgeusia is a well-known AE associated with NMVr therapy. A post-marketing pharmacovigilance study of NMVr utilizing the FAERS database to analyze AE reports after taking NMVr found a 17.5% rate of dysgeusia, and 305 times more dysgeusia events were reported with NMVr use than with other coronavirus therapies.14 The occurrence of dysgeusia after taking NMVr is thought to be due to its physicochemical properties and mechanism of action. NMVr is a protease inhibitor combination; nirmatrelvir is the main protease inhibitor of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) and ritonavir is a human immunodeficiency virus type 1 protease inhibitor. Ritonavir has no activity against SARS-CoV-2 main protease but increases the levels of nirmatrelvir26 by slowing down its metabolism and thus extending its duration of action. A previous study that looked at the biochemical causes of dysgeusia after taking NMVr, also known as paxlovid mouth, found that the bitter/bad taste after administering the drug was due to TAS2R receptor activation. This receptor allows humans to taste bitterness in response to constant salivary or plasma nirmatrelvir concentrations.27 However, dysgeusia was observed in 3.7% of patients in this study, which is lower than the 5.6% reported in clinical trials. In addition, while dysgeusia was the most frequent AE after taking NMVr in the clinical trial, it was the third most frequent AE in this study after muscle pain and dizziness. A Korean multicenter prospective study of efficacy and AEs after NMVr and MOL therapy found that although the baseline characteristics of the groups were similar to those in our study, the AE rate of dysgeusia in the NMVr group was higher (23.8%).20 We therefore hypothesize that the differences in the incidence of dysgeusia between studies were not due to ethnic differences or baseline characteristics of the medication groups. One hypothesis to explain this phenomenon is recall bias due to differences in the study designs. Park et al.20 conducted a prospective observational study in which researchers called patients on days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 14, and 28 after prescribing medication to collect information on COVID-19 symptoms and AEs. Contrarily, the present study was cross-sectional in nature; patients were asked directly about the AEs they experienced after taking medications, which may have caused recall bias due to the study design, as the time of medication use and that of developing AEs are different. Highly reported AEs, such as muscle pain, dizziness, and headache are similar to COVID-19 symptoms; therefore, it is easy to remember them as AEs that occurred after taking the drug, even if they were actually symptoms of the disease. However, dysgeusia is an unfamiliar sensory AE and may not be more likely to be remembered, depending on the duration between treatment and the time of responding to the questionnaire.

This study identified various factors associated with AEs during oral COVID-19 treatment. Patients who were diagnosed with COVID-19 at least twice experienced an approximately 1.9 higher rate of AEs than those who were diagnosed only once, and patients who self-reported their health as unhealthy were 2.7 times more likely to experience AEs during oral COVID-19 treatment than those who self-reported as healthy. A history of COVID-19 and health status are indicators of immunity. Immunity plays an important role in the initial response to SARS-CoV-2 infection and influences the pathogenesis and clinical outcomes of COVID-19.28 Patients who developed COVID-19 more than once were more likely to consider themselves as unhealthy and immunocompromised. Alternatively, they reported themselves as unhealthy because they experienced AEs during treatment.

Subgroup analyses were conducted to identify risk factors for AEs associated with each drug. Unlike in the overall analysis, no statistically significant risk factors were found in the NMVr group. In the MOL group, only a history of smoking was identified as a significant risk factor, with past or current smokers experiencing more AEs than never-smokers. However, the wide CIs of these findings suggest that caution should be exercised when interpreting their clinical implications. A CI estimates the range within which the true value lies, with a 95% CI indicating a 95% probability that the true value falls within this range. The width of the CI depends on the sample mean, standard deviation, and sample size; larger sample sizes allow for a more precise estimation of the true value, resulting in narrower CIs, whereas smaller sample sizes lead to wider CIs. In this study, there were 62 subjects in the MOL group, with smaller numbers experiencing or not experiencing AEs for each risk factor. Although smoking status appeared to markedly increase the risk of AEs in the MOL group, this finding should be interpreted with caution due to the wide CIs. In contrast, there were no statistically significant risk factors, except smoking status, in the MOL group, including all the factors in the NMVr group. However, it is difficult to conclude that there are no risk factors based on this study, as some were found to have marginal risk. Further large-scale research is needed to enhance statistical power and provide more meaningful clinical implications concerning the risk factors for AEs associated with each drug.

Several limitations of this study should be considered when interpreting its results. First, the inherent nature of survey-based studies may lead to biased results. Survey respondents have different characteristics from non-respondents, such as demographic features and disease severity. For example, patients with higher disease severity may be less likely to respond to personal interviews. In addition, the respondents had different histories of COVID-19 disease and treatment. As the period between experiencing an AE and collecting information differs, recall bias can occur inconsistently. Second, the number of respondents in this study was 520, which was insufficient to detect rare AEs. In addition, because only 62 patients (11.9%) received MOL, the weighting of one AE in the MOL group was very high compared to that in the NMVr group. Furthermore, the overall number of respondents was small, which may also affect the generalizability of the findings. Third, COVID-19 has a non-homogeneous analytical environment, with mortality and severity rates declining over several viral variants from the start of the pandemic in 2020 to present; however, this study did not account for the effects of changes in disease severity over time. Prescription patterns for COVID-19 treatments, patient baseline characteristics, and AEs may have changed over time. If the respondents were extremely clustered in the early prescription period of high severity or in the more recent period of low severity, this could have led to biased results.

In conclusion, AEs were more common among individuals taking MOL than those taking NMVr; however, none was reported as serious. Several factors were highly associated with the occurrence of AEs, including taking MOL, having being diagnosed with COVID-19 at least twice, and not being in a healthy state; however, subgroup analyses of drug type did not identify any clinically meaningful risk factors. Therefore, NMVr and MOL may still be viable treatment options for COVID-19 in older adults in outpatient settings. However, close monitoring of any potential AEs associated with their use is necessary, and additional studies are required to establish the factors associated with the occurrence of AEs during treatment of COVID-19 using oral agents.

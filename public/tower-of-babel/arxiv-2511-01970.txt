Interplay of Generalised Symmetries and Moduli Spaces in 3d 𝒩 = 5 \mathcal{N}=5 SCFTs
Sebastiano Garavaglia, William Harding, Deshuo Liu, and Noppadol Mekareeya

Abstract
The moduli space and generalised global symmetries of 3d 𝒩 = 5 \mathcal{N}=5 superconformal field theories are investigated, with a focus on the orthosymplectic ABJ theories and their discrete gauging variants. We extend the known classification of 𝒩 = 5 \mathcal{N}=5 moduli spaces as orbifolds ℍ 2 ​ N / Γ \mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma , where Γ \Gamma is a quaternionic reflection group, to theories incorporating Spin \mathrm{Spin} , O − \mathrm{O}^{-} , and Pin \mathrm{Pin} -type gauge groups. In these cases, we find that the moduli space is governed not by Γ \Gamma itself, but by a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} central extension thereof, for which we explicitly describe the generators. We provide a systematic method to construct the group Γ ′ \Gamma^{\prime} governing the moduli space of a theory 𝒯 ′ \mathcal{T}^{\prime} obtained by gauging a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} zero-form symmetry of an original theory 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . This is achieved by identifying the specific generator that must be added to Γ \Gamma . We compute the Hilbert series for these moduli spaces and verify them against the corresponding limits of the superconformal index, finding perfect agreement. We also discuss how ’t Hooft anomalies for the zero-form symmetries manifest in the superconformal index and the moduli space. Furthermore, we revisit the symmetry category of the 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 2 ​ N ) 2 ​ k × 𝔲 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 2 ​ N ) − k \mathfrak{so}(2N)_{2k}\times\mathfrak{usp}(2N)_{-k} theories. Building on previous work that identified the symmetry category for all parities of N N and k k , we provide the explicit symmetry webs for the opposite parity D 8 D_{8} case. We find that the details of these webs differ from the previously studied D 8 D_{8} webs corresponding to the both even parity case. Finally, we analyse theories with unequal ranks, those containing the 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 2 ​ N + 1 ) \mathfrak{so}(2N+1) gauge algebra, and the two SCFT variants based on the F ⁡ ( 4 ) F(4) superalgebra.

Introduction

Three-dimensional superconformal field theories (SCFTs) with
{\cal N}\geq 5
supersymmetry possess rich moduli space structures and generalised global symmetries. It was pointed out in
Tachikawa:2019dvq
that, upon appropriate gauging of finite symmetry groups, the moduli spaces of these 3d SCFTs are given by orbifolds of reflection groups. For
{\cal N}=8
, the moduli space is
\mathbb{R}^{8N}/\Gamma
, with
\Gamma
a real reflection group. For
{\cal N}=6
, it is
\mathbb{C}^{4N}/\Gamma
, with
\Gamma
a complex reflection group. Similarly, for the
{\cal N}=5
case, the moduli space can be identified with
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma
, with
\Gamma
a quaternionic reflection group
Deb:2024zay
. For
{\cal N}=8
, the list of real reflection groups provides a classification scheme for
{\cal N}=8
SCFTs, in the sense that any two
{\cal N}=8
SCFTs with the same moduli space must either be the same or be related to each other by gauging some finite group
Tachikawa:2019dvq
(see also
Bashkirov:2011pt
;
Gang:2011xp
;
Agmon:2017lga
). However, as supersymmetry decreases to
{\cal N}=6
or
{\cal N}=5
, this classification becomes weaker: distinct SCFTs, not related by discrete gauging, may share the same moduli space. Nevertheless, the correspondence between
\Gamma
and the SCFTs suggests that novel theories may remain to be discovered, see e.g.
Kaidi:2022uux
.

Let us focus on the Lagrangian subclass of these SCFTs. Their gauge algebra and matter content are classified by Lie superalgebras
KAC19778
;
Gaiotto:2008sd
;
Hosomichi:2008jb
;
Schnabl:2008wj
, where the former corresponds to the bosonic generators and the latter to the fermionic generators. Of course, many variants can exist that are related by gauging finite discrete symmetries for a given superalgebra. For
{\cal N}\geq 6
SCFTs, two superalgebras are relevant:
\mathrm{SU}(M|N)
and
\mathrm{PSU}(N|N)
. A notable theory in the
\mathrm{SU}(M|N)
class is the
\U(M)_{k}\times\U(N)_{-k}
theory with two bifundamental hypermultiplets, known as the ABJM theory
Aharony:2008ug
when
M=N
, and the unitary ABJ theory
Aharony:2008gk
when
M\neq N
. These theories are realised on the worldvolume of M2-branes probing the
\mathbb{C}^{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
singularity. Related variants also exist that can be obtained by gauging a one-form symmetry, e.g.
[\U(N)_{k}\times\U(N)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. The
\mathrm{PSU}(N|N)
class corresponds to the
[\mathrm{SU}(N)_{k}\times\mathrm{SU}(N)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{N}
theory, which is dual to
[\U(N)_{k}\times\U(N)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{k}

Tachikawa:2019dvq
;
Bergman:2020ifi
(see also
Lambert:2010ji
); thus, this class is effectively equivalent to
\mathrm{SU}(N|N)
. The
N=2
case corresponds to the BLG theories
Bagger:2006sk
;
Gustavsson:2007vu
;
VanRaamsdonk:2008ft
.

For
{\cal N}\geq 5
SCFTs, there are four known superalgebra classes:
\mathrm{OSp}(M|2N)
,
D(2|1;\alpha)
,
F(4)
, and
G(3)
. The
\mathrm{OSp}(M|2N)
class includes the theories with gauge groups
\mathrm{O}(2M)^{+}_{2k}\times\USp(2N)_{-k}
and
\mathrm{O}(2M+1)^{+}_{2k}\times\USp(2N)_{-k}
with two bifundamental half-hypermultiplets, which are realised on M2-branes probing the
\mathbb{C}^{4}/\widehat{D}_{k}
singularity. These are the
orthosymplectic ABJ theories

. These theories, along with variants obtained by discrete gauging, are the main protagonists of this paper. We will also examine the
F(4)
superalgebra class, which contains the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}

[\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

representation. The

Comi:2023lfm

Deb:2024zay

;

(see also

, where
\Gamma

This paper extends existing results in the literature in many directions. First, we investigate the moduli space of the orthosymplectic ABJ theories with
\mathrm{Spin}
,
\mathrm{O}^{-}
and
\mathrm{Pin}
-type gauge groups. We find that, in these cases, the corresponding
\Gamma
may not be a quaternionic reflection group itself, but a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
central extension thereof. We describe the generators of such extensions in detail. Second, when gauging a
\mathbb{Z}^{[0]}_{2}
zero-form symmetry of a theory

(whose moduli space is
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma
) which leads to another theory
{\cal T}^{\prime}
(whose moduli space is
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma^{\prime}
), we provide a systematic way to construct
\Gamma^{\prime}
from
\Gamma
. Specifically, one simply needs to add an appropriate generator to
\Gamma
to obtain
\Gamma^{\prime}
, and we provide an explicit expression for this generator. We compute the Hilbert series for the Higgs or Coulomb branch (viewed as an
{\cal N}=4
moduli space) of these theories, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{N}/\Gamma
, and verify it against the corresponding limit of the superconformal index, finding agreement in all cases. We also identify situations where the
\mathbb{Z}^{[0]}_{2}
zero-form symmetry in question is not gaugable, and discuss how to detect the corresponding anomaly from the perspective of the superconformal index and the moduli space. Third, we revisit the symmetry category of the orthosymplectic ABJ theories with the gauge algebra
\mathfrak{so}(2N)_{2k}\times\mathfrak{usp}(2N)_{-k}
for every parity of
N
and
k
. The explicit
D_{8}
and
Q_{8}
symmetry webs for the case where

and
k

Bergman:2024its

for
N
and
k
with opposite parities. However, the details of the symmetry webs, which we provide explicitly in this work, differ from the case where both
N
and
k
are even. We also analyse unequal-rank orthosymplectic ABJ theories. In contrast to the equal-rank case, some symmetries do not act faithfully on the moduli space. We also briefly discuss theories containing the
\mathfrak{so}(2N+1)
gauge algebra. Finally, we discuss the two variants of the SCFTs based on the
F(4)
superalgebra.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section

Symplectic reflection groups and their generators

The moduli space of variants of the

Let us start by stating four definitions of reflection groups that are closely related to each other. Here we follow the notation in
etingof2001symplecticreflectionalgebrascalogeromoser

•

Real reflection.
Given
V
a vector space over
\mathbb{R}
, a real reflection is a unimodular matrix
g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathbf{1}-g)=1
.

•

Complex reflection.
Given
V
a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
, a complex reflection is a unimodular matrix
g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathbf{1}-g)=1
.

•

Symplectic reflection.
Given
(V,\omega)
a symplectic vector space over
\mathbb{C}
, a symplectic reflection is an element
g\in\mathrm{Sp}(V)
such that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathbf{1}-g)=2
.

•

Quaternionic reflection.
Given
V
a vector space over
\mathbb{H}
, a quaternionic reflection is an element
g\in\mathrm{Sp}(V)
such that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathbf{1}-g)=1
.

SCFT is
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma
, the space
\mathbb{H}^{N}/\Gamma
can be regarded as the Higgs or Coulomb branch of a 3d
{\cal N}=5
SCFT being viewed as an
{\cal N}=4
theory. We emphasise again that the limit of the index computes the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{H}^{N}/\Gamma
, and not of the full moduli space
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma
. It is the limit of the index that allows us to verify in a field theoretic way that we have the correct
\Gamma
for each variant of the
{\cal N}=5
SCFT.

4.1
Generators of symplectic reflection groups

Let us define the following
2\times 2
matrices:

I=\mathrm{diag}(1,1)~,\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}~,\quad E_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(\omega_{n},\omega_{n}^{-1})~,

(4.3)

with
\omega_{n}=\exp(2\pi i/n)
.

Let
P_{j}
(with
j=1,\ldots,N-1
) be a matrix representation of the transposition
(j,j+1)
. In particular, the
2\times 2
block-matrix in the block-positions
(j,j+1)
,
(j+1,j)
, and
(m,m)
for
m\neq\{j,j+1\}
, are the identity matrix
I
, and the other entries are zero. The set
\{P_{j}\}
corresponds to the collection of transpositions
\{(1,2),(2,3),\ldots,(N-1,N)\}
that generates
S_{N}
. Furthermore, we define

R_{1}=\mathrm{diag}(J,J^{-1},I,\dots,I)~,\qquad R_{2}=\mathrm{diag}(E_{2k},E_{2k}^{-1},I,\dots,I)~,

(4.4)

where these are generators of
\widehat{D}_{k}
.
16
16

16

In the special case of
N=1
, we simply have
R_{1}=J
and
R_{2}=E_{2k}
for the generators of
\widehat{D}_{k}
.
For the
N=2
case, we also define a symplectic reflection

R_{3}=\mathrm{diag}(E_{k},I)~.

(4.5)

As an example, the generators of
G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})
are
P_{1},R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
. The generators of
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})
for
N\geq 3
are
P_{1},\ldots,P_{N-1},R_{1},R_{2}
. Note that, for
N\geq 3
, there is no analog of
R_{3}
. The reason is as follows. Consider an operation
\beta
which takes one of the
E_{2k}
blocks in
R_{2}
to its inverse, then one can construct
R_{3}=\beta(R_{2})R_{2}
. Here,
\beta
can be regarded as an inner automorphism, composed of actions of
S_{N}
and
R_{1}
.

Suppose that we gauge a non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}^{[0]}_{2,S}
symmetry, where
S
takes values in
\{B,{\cal M},{\cal C},{\cal M}{\cal C}\}
, in a theory
{\cal T}
whose moduli space is
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma
, and obtain a new non-anomalous theory
{\cal T}^{\prime}
whose moduli space is
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma^{\prime}
. Then,
\Gamma^{\prime}
can be constructed by inserting the extra generator
R_{S}
into the set of generators of the group
\Gamma
, where we define

\displaystyle R_{B}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(E_{2k},I,\dots,I)~,

(4.6)

\displaystyle R_{\cal M}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(E_{4k},E_{4k},\dots,E_{4k})~,

\displaystyle R_{\cal C}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}(J,I,\dots,I)~,

\displaystyle R_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}

\displaystyle=R_{\cal M}R_{\cal C}~.

To illustrate this point, let us consider
\mathrm{SO}(2N)_{2k}\times\USp(2N)_{-k}
, obtained from
[\mathrm{SO}(2N)_{2k}\times\USp(2N)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by gauging
\mathbb{Z}_{2,B}^{[0]}
. The group
\Gamma
associated with the latter is
\Gamma=G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})
, whose set of generators is
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{N-1},R_{1},R_{2}\}
. To obtain the group
\Gamma^{\prime}
associated with the former theory, we simply add
R_{B}
into the set of generators; thus, we have
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{N-1},R_{1},R_{2},R_{B}\rangle
. However, some of these generators are redundant, for example
R_{2}=P_{1}R_{B}^{-1}P_{1}R_{B}
, and so we can rewrite
\Gamma^{\prime}
as
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle P_{1},\ldots,P_{N-1},R_{1},R_{B}\rangle
. We call the set of generators after removing the redundant ones the set of
reduced generators
.

4.2
Symplectic reflections and their
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
extensions

The transposition
P_{j}
is a symplectic reflection, since it satisfies
\mathrm{rk}(\mathbf{1}-P_{j})=2
. The generators
R_{B}
and
R_{\cal C}
, however, are not symplectic reflections, but can be combined with permutations to form them; for example,
P_{1}R_{B}
and
P_{1}R_{\cal C}
are symplectic reflections. Similarly, the group
G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{I})
, generated by
\{P_{1},R_{1},R_{2}\}
, is a symplectic reflection group because an equivalent set of generators,
\{P_{1},P_{1}R_{1},P_{1}R_{2}\}
, consists entirely of symplectic reflections.

For
R_{\cal M}
and
R_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}
, the situation differs for
N=2
and
N\geq 3
. For
N=2
, we can use the alternative generators

\widetilde{R}_{\cal M}=\mathrm{diag}(E_{4k},E_{4k}^{-1})~,\qquad\widetilde{R}_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}=\widetilde{R}_{\cal M}R_{\cal C}~.

(4.7)

These two choices are equivalent, as their actions on
\mathbb{H}^{2}\cong\mathbb{C}^{4}
are related by a change of complex structure. An important observation is that
\widetilde{R}_{\cal M}
can be combined with
P_{1}
to form the symplectic reflection
P_{1}\widetilde{R}_{{\cal M}}
. However,
\widetilde{R}_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}
is not, in general, a symplectic reflection, nor can it be converted into one by multiplication with other generators.
17
17

17

The situation here is similar to that of the
\left[\mathrm{SU}(N)_{k}\times\mathrm{SU}(N)_{k}\right]/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
theory discussed in
(
Tachikawa:2019dvq
, Section 3.2)
, whose moduli space is guaranteed to be an orbifold of a complex reflection group only when
N=2
.
Consequently, for any variant involving the gauge group
\mathrm{O}(4)^{-}
, the associated discrete group
\Gamma
is not a symplectic reflection group.

For
N\geq 3
, it is not possible to transform
R_{\cal M}
or
R_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}
into symplectic reflections, either by a change of complex structure of
\mathbb{C}^{4N}
or by multiplication with other generators. Therefore, any group containing
R_{\cal M}
or
R_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}
as a generator is not a symplectic reflection group for
N\geq 3
.

The generator
R_{\cal M}
arises from a specific construction. It is obtained by replacing the diagonal blocks associated with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
subgroup in
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})
with blocks corresponding to a
\mathbb{Z}_{4k}
subgroup. Due to this enlargement from
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{4k}
, the inclusion of
R_{\cal M}
(or
R_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}
) into the generating set is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
extension
.
18
18

18

Note that
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is indeed a central extension of
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})
by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
characterised by the short exact sequence
1\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})\rightarrow 1
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a centre of
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. This statement can also be generalised to other extensions discussed in this paper.
For example, the group associated with
[\mathrm{Spin}(2N)_{2k}\times\USp(2N)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, generated by
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{N-1},R_{1},R_{\cal M}\}
, is denoted by
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We also denote by
G_{N}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\prime}
the group associated with
[\mathrm{O}(2N)^{-}_{2k}\times\USp(2N)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, which is generated by
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{N-1},R_{1},R_{{\cal M}{\cal C}}\}
. In the special case of
N=2
, some of these
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
extensions are themselves symplectic reflection groups:

\displaystyle{}[\mathrm{Spin}(4)_{2k}\times\USp(4)_{-k}]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\quad G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{2k},\mathbb{Z}_{k})~,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Spin}(4)_{2k}\times\USp(4)_{-k}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\quad G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{k},\mathbb{Z}_{2k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{2k},\mathbb{Z}_{2k})~,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pin}(4)_{2k}\times\USp(4)_{-k}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\quad G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{k},\widehat{D}_{k}).\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong G_{2}(\widehat{D}_{2k},\widehat{D}_{k})~.

4.4
Anomalous variants

As discussed previously, if gauging a non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}^{[0]}_{2,S}
symmetry of a given non-anomalous theory
{\cal T}
(with moduli space
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma
) leads to another non-anomalous theory
{\cal T}^{\prime}
(with moduli space
\mathbb{H}^{2N}/\Gamma^{\prime}
), then the new group is
\Gamma^{\prime}=\langle\mathrm{gen}(\Gamma),R_{S}\rangle
, where
\mathrm{gen}(\Gamma)
is a set of generators of
\Gamma
. We find that in such cases,

|\Gamma^{\prime}|=2|\Gamma|~.

(4.11)

This is consistent with the principle that the relative volume of the base of the associated Calabi-Yau cone must increase by a factor of two when a non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
zero-form symmetry is gauged (see, for example,
Hanany:2018dvd
). This statement holds for the entries in Tables (
4.9
) and (
4.10
).

On the other hand, if
{\cal T}
is non-anomalous, but the theory
{\cal T}^{\prime}
obtained by gauging
\mathbb{Z}^{[0]}_{2,S}
is anomalous, we observe that the order of the new group
\langle\mathrm{gen}(\Gamma),R_{S}\rangle
becomes four times the order of
\Gamma
, not twice. Furthermore, the resulting quotient space does not correspond to the moduli space of
{\cal T}^{\prime}
; instead, it describes the moduli space of a different non-anomalous theory
{\cal T}^{\prime\prime}
. This observation aligns with the remarks accompanying Tables (
4.9
) and (
4.10
). We summarise the anomalous variants for each parity of
N
and
k
in (
3.2
).

This pattern can be generalised further. Consider a non-anomalous theory
\mathcal{T}
with an
anomalous
finite discrete Abelian zero-form symmetry
G
. Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be an anomalous theory obtained by gauging
G
from
\mathcal{T}
, and let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
be a non-anomalous theory obtained by gauging
G^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{T}
, where
G^{\prime}
is the minimal non-anomalous extension of
G
.
19
19

19

By the term “minimal extension”, we mean as follows. Suppose that
G^{\prime}
is a non-anomalous extension of
G
by
A
described by
1\rightarrow A\rightarrow G^{\prime}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1
. If, for any other non-anomalous extension
G^{\prime\prime}
of
G
by
A^{\prime}
described by
1\rightarrow A^{\prime}\rightarrow G^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1
,
A
is a normal subgroup of
A^{\prime}
, then
G^{\prime}
is a minimal extension. The construction in
Tachikawa:2017gyf
guarantees an
A^{\prime}
can always be found, and so
A
can be acquired by examining the subgroups of
A^{\prime}
. Also, in
Robbins:2021lry
, a method to directly reduce
A^{\prime}
to
A
is provided.

If
\mathcal{M}_{\cal T}
is the moduli space of
\mathcal{T}
, then the action of the anomalous symmetry
G
on
\mathcal{M}_{\cal T}
does not form a closed orbit.
20
20

20

By the term “closed orbit”, we mean as follows. For two generic points
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{\cal T}
, if
y\in G(x)
implies
G(x)=G(y)
, then
G(x)
is a closed orbit.
Instead, it generates the
G^{\prime}
action on
\mathcal{M}_{\cal T}
, which forms a closed orbit. On a vector space
V
, this means that the action of
G
is not a linear representation
G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
, but an
A
-projective representation
G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)/A
, which determines a linear representation
G^{\prime}\to GL(V)
of its covering group
G^{\prime}
.
21
21

21

In general, the
A
-projective representation is classified by the group cohomology
H^{2}(G,A)
.
By quotienting the action of
G
through a closed orbit, we obtain the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\cal T}/G^{\prime}
, which is the moduli space of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
.

4.5
Hilbert series

In this subsection, we report the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{H}^{N}/\Gamma
, which is the Higgs or Coulomb branch of the
{\cal N}=5
SCFT in question (viewed as an
{\cal N}=4

For simplicity, let us focus on the

\USp(2N)

), whose collective contribution cancels the factor
z_{1}^{-3}u^{2}
, which obstructs gauge invariance of the bare monopole operator with fluxes
\left(1,0,0;\frac{1}{2}\right)
. In other words, we are looking for a set of components of chiral fields, whose product contribute to the index as
z_{1}^{3}u^{-2}a^{p}
, where
p
can be any integer. Such gauge invariant quantities can be built by considering the following parametrisations of the chiral fields components:

\begin{split}\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{1}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}ua&,~x_{2}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}ua&,~x_{3}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u^{-1}a~,\\
x_{4}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{-1}a&,~x_{5}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}ua^{-1}&,~x_{6}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}ua^{-1}~,\\
x_{7}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u^{-1}a^{-1}&,~x_{8}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{-1}a^{-1}&,~x_{9}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u^{-1}a~,\\
x_{10}=z_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{-1}a^{-1}~.&&\end{array}\end{split}

(7.6)

We can then construct dimension three operators by taking products of six such combinations, which, combined with negative dimension of the bare monopole, yield dimension one dressed monopole operators appearing at order
x
in the index. Such gauge invariant operators can be built, for instance, by considering the products
x_{7}x_{8}\prod_{i=1}^{4}x_{i}=z_{1}^{3}u^{-2}a^{2}
,
\prod_{i=3}^{8}x_{i}=z_{1}^{3}u^{-2}a^{-2}
and
x_{1}x_{2}\prod_{i=7}^{10}x_{i}=z_{1}^{3}u^{-2}
, where the corresponding dressed monopole operators contribute to the index as
\zeta a^{2}x
,
\zeta a^{-2}x
and
\zeta x
, respectively. These three terms form the expected contribution
\zeta[2]_{a}
appearing at order
x
in (
7.4
). Taking into account also the relevant operator
Q_{1}Q_{2}
, it follows that there are four terms appearing at order
x
in the index, which is a necessary condition for supersymmetry to enhance to
{\cal N}=6

Evtikhiev:2017heo
.

On the other hand, for
k>1
, the bare monopole operator associated with the magnetic fluxes
\left(1,0,0;\frac{1}{2}\right)
carries gauge charges
-3k
and
2k
under the
\mathfrak{so}(7)
Cartan element corresponding to
z_{1}
and the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
gauge factor. Hence, in order to give rise to gauge invariant operators, this bare monopole needs to be dressed with at least
6k
chiral fields. Such gauge invariant dressed monopole operators have dimension
3k-2
, hence they appear at order
x^{3k-2}
in the index of the
\left[\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}\right]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
theory. For
k>1
, they are not present at order
x
, where only the relevant operator
Q_{1}Q_{2}
appears, in agreement with the necessary condition for
{\cal N}=5
supersymmetry enhancement of
Evtikhiev:2017heo
. It follows that supersymmetry gets enhanced to
{\cal N}=6
just for
k=1
, whereas, for
k>1
, the
\left[\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}\right]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
theory possesses enhanced
{\cal N}=5
supersymmetry.

We can also consider the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}
theory, whose index can be derived, as explained in (
A.12
), from the one of the
\left[\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}\right]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
theory by summing over
\zeta=\pm 1
in the latter, and dividing by two. In the case
k=1
, the expression (
7.4
) becomes

\begin{split}1+x+\Big[2\left([4]_{a}+1\right)-[2]_{a}\Big]x^{2}+\ldots~,\end{split}

(7.7)

where there is just a single relevant operator
Q_{1}Q_{2}
, appearing at order
x
. The negative terms at order
x^{2}
, namely
a^{2}+1+a^{-2}
, reveal that there are two extra-supersymmetry currents, which are compatible with the enhanced
{\cal N}=5
supersymmetry, see Footnote
8
. This means that, upon gauging the discrete zero-form symmetry associated with
\zeta
, supersymmetry gets broken from
{\cal N}=6
down to
{\cal N}=5
in the case
k=1
.

As a final remark, we observe that, upon taking either the Higgs or the Coulomb branch limit of the indices (
7.4
) and (
7.7
) as explained in (
A.15
), these yield the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\widehat{D}_{2}
, respectively, in agreement with
(

, (3.37) and (3.38))
. Note that the series expansion of the former Hilbert series contains a term at order

, corresponding to the contribution coming from the dressed monopole with fluxes
\left(1,0,0;\frac{1}{2}\right)
, which is instead absent in the latter Hilbert series. For
k>1
, the moduli spaces of the
\left[\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}\right]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)_{-3k}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{2k}

\mathbb{H}^{2}/\widehat{D}_{3k}

\mathbb{H}^{2}/\widehat{D}_{6k}

Mobilizing Waldo: Evaluating Multimodal AI for Public Mobilization
Manuel Cebrian 1 , Petter Holme 2 , and Niccolò Pescetelli 3

Abstract
Advancements in multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs), such as OpenAI’s GPT-4o, present significant potential for mediating human interactions across various contexts. However, their capabilities in controversial areas, such as persuasion, influence, and recruitment, raise substantial ethical and security concerns. To ethically evaluate these models in situational public influence and persuasion scenarios, we developed a prompting strategy utilizing “Where’s Waldo?” images as proxies for complex, crowded real-world gatherings. This approach provides a controlled and replicable environment to assess the model’s ability to process intricate visual information, comprehend social dynamics, and propose engagement strategies without infringing on individual privacy. By positioning Waldo as a hypothetical agent tasked with face-to-face mobilization, we analyzed the model’s performance in identifying key individuals and formulating mobilization tactics. Our findings reveal that while the model can generate vivid descriptions and creative strategies, it currently lacks the ability to accurately identify individuals or reliably assess social dynamics within these scenarios. Nevertheless, our methodology offers a valuable framework for testing and benchmarking the evolving capabilities of multimodal LLMs in social contexts.

Preliminary explorations into analyzing real-world public scenes with multimodal LLMs revealed that these models readily provided strategic advice for public mobilization, even when such guidance was ethically questionable. Contrary to expectations that built-in safeguards would prevent such outputs, the models complied with requests that raised privacy and consent concerns. This unexpected compliance highlights significant ethical implications and the pressing need for developing safer methodologies to test and evaluate these systems without infringing on individual rights.

To address these concerns, we propose a novel approach utilizing “Where’s Waldo?” images as proxies for complex public scenes, such as those taken from CCTV cameras and crowded public places. This methodology allows us to ethically assess LLMs’ capabilities in analyzing and strategizing for public mobilization within a controlled, replicable environment. By avoiding the analysis of real individuals, we eliminate privacy issues while still challenging the models with intricate, crowded scenes that simulate real-world scenarios.

In this study, we evaluate the capabilities of OpenAI’s GPT-4o, a multimodal LLM, in processing visual information, understanding social dynamics, and proposing engagement strategies, using Waldo as a hypothetical agent tasked with a face-to-face mobilization scenario. We systematically assess the model’s performance in identifying key individuals, analyzing social contexts, and formulating mobilization tactics. Our findings reveal significant limitations in the model’s ability to perform complex social analysis and strategic planning in these scenarios, underscoring current challenges in AI’s situational awareness and contextual understanding. Nevertheless, our approach provides a valuable and ethically sound framework for testing and benchmarking the evolving capabilities of advanced AI models in public mobilization contexts.

By leveraging controlled simulations, we aim to contribute to the understanding of AI’s impact on society, governance, and privacy, informing the development of ethical standards and regulatory frameworks for AI applications in public engagement.

II
Methodology

GPT-4o builds on GPT-4 architecture by integrating image interpretation, enabling it to identify objects, derive spatial relationships, and incorporate visual context into its language-based reasoning. Unlike specialized computer vision systems or multimodal models like GPT-4 Vision, GPT-4o offers a unified framework
openai2024gpt4o
, making it appropriate for this study.

To ethically and safely conduct this evaluation without infringing on individual privacy, we utilized Where’s Waldo? images as proxies for real-world public gatherings, providing a controlled and replicable environment. Our dataset consisted of 18 high-resolution Where’s Waldo? images sourced from the publicly available
Hey-Waldo
dataset
vc1492a_heywaldo
. These images, renowned for their intricate and densely populated scenes, were painstakingly hand-labeled and include versions in color, grayscale, and black-and-white formats across multiple resolutions
vc1492a_heywaldo
. The dataset’s complexity stems from several challenges: Waldo’s variable size (scaling issue), repeating patterns (red-and-white stripes present on other objects), occlusion (Waldo is often partially obscured), and its unbalanced nature (most image segments do not contain Waldo). Such attributes make classification and image recognition tasks particularly difficult.

Despite these challenges, state-of-the-art computer vision systems have achieved remarkable success in locating Waldo with high speed and accuracy. For example, advanced convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and semantic segmentation models have been demonstrated to identify Waldo within seconds
kenstler2017thereswaldo
;
magajna2017findwally
. These models excel at pixel-level localization and visual recognition, relying on spatial and structural patterns within the image to produce highly accurate results. However, such systems lack the ability to incorporate contextual reasoning or situational awareness beyond visual features. While computer vision models can identify Waldo’s visual characteristics (e.g., red-and-white stripes), they cannot generate descriptions of the scene, infer relationships between individuals, or propose contextually relevant strategies for engagement.

In contrast, multimodal large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4o integrate visual and textual reasoning, enabling them to analyze images while providing contextually grounded outputs. These models can describe complex visual environments, interpret social dynamics, and formulate strategic suggestions, tasks that conventional computer vision systems cannot achieve. For example, in the case of the Hey-Waldo dataset, GPT-4o not only attempts to locate Waldo but also identifies characters who could be persuaded to perform a specified action — in this case, ’dressing like Waldo.’ While this task is arbitrary and chosen as a benchmark for the model’s persuasive reasoning, the same methodology could be applied to evaluate the model’s ability to influence other types of actions or behaviors within a given social context. This integration of language-based reasoning with visual input represents a fundamental distinction between multimodal LLMs and traditional computer vision approaches.

To establish a performance baseline, we supplemented the Hey-Waldo dataset with five control images depicting simpler scenes with fewer characters. Table

By leveraging this dataset, which has been widely used for testing classification and segmentation methods in computer vision, we sought to benchmark the performance of GPT-4o against established standards while analyzing its ability to navigate the unique challenges posed by densely populated visual scenes. Importantly, while traditional systems excel at object recognition, GPT-4o’s ability to combine visual understanding with contextual reasoning provides a more holistic, albeit still imperfect, approach to complex visual analysis.

For each image, we sent a request to the GPT-4o model via the OpenAI API, including both the image (encoded as a base64 string to comply with the API’s requirements) and our custom prompt. The model processed the inputs and generated responses in JSON format, as per our instructions. We collected and logged the model’s responses for each image, then parsed the JSON outputs to extract key information: the scene description, Waldo’s coordinates and description, the descriptions and coordinates of the five identified characters, the reasons they might be easily convinced to dress like Waldo, and the proposed persuasion strategies. Figure
2
shows a sample JSON output from the model for Image 1 of the Hey-Waldo dataset, illustrating the structure and content of the responses.

To facilitate analysis and verification, we created visualizations for each image by overlaying the reported locations of Waldo and the five identified characters onto the original images using the provided coordinates. This allowed us to assess the accuracy of the model’s spatial information and its ability to correctly identify characters within the scenes.

We evaluated the performance of GPT-4o across five key aspects using a structured framework designed to ensure systematic and reproducible analysis. First, we assessed the accuracy of Waldo’s identification, focusing on whether the model correctly located Waldo within the image. This evaluation included verifying the precision of the coordinates provided and the clarity of the accompanying textual description. Second, we examined the quality of the scene descriptions, evaluating whether the model effectively captured the thematic essence and key details of the images. Third, we assessed the validity of character identification, confirming whether the identified characters existed in the image and determining whether they were appropriate candidates for engagement strategies. Fourth, we analyzed the creativity and effectiveness of the persuasion strategies, focusing on their originality, feasibility, and contextual relevance. Finally, we evaluated the spatial accuracy of the model’s outputs, checking whether the coordinates and spatial descriptions of both Waldo and the identified characters matched the actual image content.

To ensure clarity and reproducibility, we defined explicit criteria for rating model responses as Good, Fair, or Poor. Responses rated as Good were accurate, precise, and aligned with the image content, showing minimal or no errors. A Fair rating indicated partial correctness, such as capturing some relevant elements while exhibiting imprecise coordinates, omissions, or lack of clarity. A Poor rating was assigned when the output contained significant inaccuracies, such as incorrect coordinates, fabricated descriptions, or a complete failure to identify key elements. The evaluation was performed qualitatively by the authors, ensuring consistency in assessing the model’s performance across all images.

For example, in Image 1, the scene description was rated Good as the model accurately described the scene as
“a historical battle or parade with various soldiers dressed in different colored uniforms, including groups with red, blue, and pink outfits. There are also several people on horseback and figures performing various actions amidst the chaotic arrangement.”
However, the accuracy of Waldo’s identification was rated Poor because the model failed to locate Waldo correctly, misplacing him entirely in some iterations or providing incorrect coordinates.

For character identification, the ratings also varied. For example, one character was described as
“a soldier in a red uniform with a long spear and a hat,”
located at
[1600, 900]
with the rationale that
“the uniform already has a red base color similar to Waldo’s outfit.”
This response was rated Fair as the description, location, and reasoning aligned well with the image. In contrast, the model identified fabricated characters or provided misaligned coordinates, which were rated Poor. Similarly, the persuasion strategies demonstrated creativity but often lacked feasibility. For instance, the strategy for the drummer in a red uniform with a yellow drum, located at
[1800, 300]
, was described as
“Waldo could convince him that adding stripes could make him more visually appealing during performances.”
This was rated Fair due to its imaginative yet contextually limited applicability.

To validate these assessments, we annotated the model’s outputs by overlaying the reported coordinates onto the original images. For Image 1, the annotated visualization highlighted Waldo’s location and the positions of the identified characters. This process, illustrated in Figure
2
, allowed us to directly compare the model’s predictions against the ground truth, revealing patterns in spatial reasoning and identification errors. While the thematic descriptions were often accurate, spatial inaccuracies and character misidentifications were recurrent issues.

By combining structured criteria with qualitative evaluation and visual verification, our methodology ensured a reproducible and transparent assessment of GPT-4o’s performance. These enhancements address concerns about ambiguity in the evaluation process and provide a robust framework for analyzing the model’s ability to perform complex visual analysis tasks.

In analyzing these control images, GPT-4o generally demonstrated a strong ability to accurately describe the overall scenes. The model captured key themes and elements across various environments, ranging from urban settings to underwater scenes. Despite occasional issues with precise coordinate placement, the model located Waldo with fair accuracy in these simpler images.

Character identification varied significantly across the control images. In images with fewer human figures, the model sometimes identified inanimate objects as “characters,” indicating a tendency to anthropomorphize. However, in Image 5, which featured more people, the model’s character identification improved, suggesting that the presence of human figures positively influenced its performance.

Spatial accuracy was consistently fair across all control images. The model attempted to provide specific coordinates but often struggled with precise spatial relationships. A notable strength was its ability to generate creative and contextually relevant persuasion strategies, even when working with misidentified or non-human characters. This adaptability was evident as the model adjusted its analysis across vastly different scenes, from underwater environments to urban settings.

We then analyzed 18 high-resolution “Where’s Waldo?” images from the Hey-Waldo dataset
vc1492a_heywaldo
. These images present complex, densely populated scenes that simulate real-world public gatherings, challenging the model’s capabilities in multiple areas. Descriptions of these images are also provided in Table
1
.

GPT-4o consistently demonstrated the ability to generate vivid and engaging descriptions of these complex scenes. For example, the model accurately described a “bustling medieval market” in one image and a “vibrant underwater adventure” in another. These descriptions effectively captured the thematic essence and dynamics of the images. However, hallucinations—such as non-existent castles or fictitious characters—occasionally detracted from the overall accuracy.

The model’s ability to locate Waldo in these complex scenes remained a significant challenge. Across the dataset, Waldo was consistently mislocated. For instance, in an image where Waldo was positioned in the upper-left corner amidst a group of miners, the model incorrectly identified him as being near the bottom center. These errors highlight persistent struggles with spatial reasoning and the difficulty of navigating dense visual contexts.

Character identification in the complex images yielded mixed results. The model frequently recognized individuals whose visual attributes aligned with Waldo’s theme, such as striped clothing or red accessories. However, the associated spatial coordinates were often inaccurate. In one example, a ”green-robed figure” was identified as a potential character for engagement, but the provided coordinates did not correspond to any such figure in the image.

Despite these limitations, GPT-4o showcased remarkable creativity in generating engagement strategies. The model suggested imaginative ideas, such as forming a “striped team” with underwater characters (including fish) or proposing thematic collaborations. However, these strategies often lacked contextual feasibility, overlooking practical dynamics and environmental constraints within the scenes.

Overall, the model excelled in generating thematic scene descriptions but faced consistent challenges in spatial localization and contextual adaptation. The increased complexity of the images exacerbated these issues, indicating that the model’s spatial reasoning capabilities are not yet sufficient for high-density visual analysis tasks.

Comparing the performance on control images and complex images reveals a clear decline in accuracy when dealing with more intricate scenes. While the model maintained its ability to produce engaging descriptions, its effectiveness in locating Waldo and identifying characters diminished significantly in complex environments. This suggests that the model’s visual processing and spatial reasoning abilities are sensitive to scene complexity.

An interesting observation is the model’s tendency to compensate for spatial inaccuracies with creative storytelling. In instances where precise localization was challenging, the model often provided elaborate narratives or strategies that, while imaginative, did not align with the actual content of the image. This behavior indicates a reliance on language generation strengths to mask weaknesses in visual analysis.

In terms of persuasion strategies, the model continued to generate inventive ideas even when other aspects of its performance were lacking. This consistency highlights the model’s robust language generation capabilities but also underscores the need for improved integration between visual understanding and strategy formulation.

Our findings emphasize the importance of advancing spatial reasoning and contextual grounding in multimodal AI systems. Enhancements in these areas are crucial for applications requiring accurate interpretation of complex visual scenes, such as situational public mobilization, surveillance, and crowd analysis.

Discussion

Our study provides an preliminary evaluation of GPT-4o’s capabilities and limitations in the context of situational public mobilization, using “Where’s Waldo?” images as ethical proxies for real-world public gatherings. The findings reveal a complex picture: while GPT-4o excels at generating vivid and contextually appropriate scene descriptions and demonstrates creativity in proposing engagement strategies, it faces significant challenges in spatial reasoning, accurate character identification, and contextual adaptation within complex, densely populated images. This aligns with recent work exploring the use of AI in complex interactive environments, such as AI-driven games, where similar challenges in contextual understanding and strategic decision-making have been observed
cebrian4788741proof
.

One of the most prominent observations is the model’s strong performance in generating thematic and engaging descriptions of both simple and complex scenes. This highlights the model’s robust language generation capabilities, which are consistent with recent studies showcasing the advanced text generation abilities of LLMs, including the generation of coherent and contextually relevant narratives, as well as creative text formats
anil2023palm
;
touvron2023llama
;
franceschelli2024creativity
. Furthermore, the model’s ability to generate creative, albeit sometimes inaccurate, engagement strategies resonates with findings that generative AI can enhance individual creativity
doshi2024generative
. However, the limitations in spatial reasoning and contextual adaptation observed in our study suggest that while AI can augment human creativity, it may not fully replace the nuanced understanding required for complex social interactions.

The model’s struggles with spatial localization and precise character identification suggest that current multimodal LLMs may lack the necessary integration between visual understanding and language processing. In complex scenes, the model often provided inaccurate coordinates and misidentified or fabricated characters. These deficiencies are particularly evident when the task requires detailed spatial awareness and contextual understanding, indicating that the visual processing component of the model is not yet on par with its language generation abilities. This finding aligns with recent research highlighting the limitations of LLMs in tasks requiring precise visual grounding and spatial reasoning
bubeck2023sparks
;
shiri2024empirical
;
gu2024context
. This is not a criticism on the specific architecture of GPT-4o, as other multimodal models have also shown similar shortcomings, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained visual discrimination and spatial relation understanding
driess2023palme
;
yang2023dawn
;
li2023blip
.

An interesting pattern observed is the model’s tendency to compensate for its visual processing shortcomings with creative language output. When unable to accurately identify or locate characters, GPT-4o often generated elaborate narratives or strategies that, while linguistically impressive, were disconnected from the actual content of the images. This behavior raises concerns about the reliability of such models in applications where accurate visual interpretation is critical, as they may produce outputs that are confidently incorrect. This tendency of LLMs to “hallucinate” or confabulate information has been documented in other studies
ji2023survey
;
rawte2023survey
;
guan2023language
, emphasizing the need for caution when deploying these models in real-world scenarios.

We also performed preliminary testing on OpenAI’s o1 model. While o1 is capable of test-time compute, its performance in this task showed no substantial improvement over GPT-4o on cursory inspection
o1_system_card
.

The use of “Where’s Waldo?” images as proxies, while ethically sound and methodologically innovative, presents certain limitations. These images, though complex and crowded, are static and lack the dynamic, unpredictable, and socially nuanced elements inherent in real-world public gatherings. Real-world scenarios involve temporal changes, emotional expressions, and intricate human interactions that are not captured in these illustrations. Therefore, while our methodology allows for a controlled assessment of the model’s capabilities, the findings cannot be directly extrapolated to real-world events involving human participants. This limitation is common in studies using simplified representations of complex social phenomena, as noted by social scientists studying agent-based models
epstein1996growing
.

Despite these limitations, the controlled environment was instrumental in isolating specific capabilities and weaknesses of GPT-4o. The consistent challenges faced by the model in complex visual analysis tasks underscore the need for advancements in spatial reasoning and multimodal integration within AI systems. Enhancing these areas is crucial for applications that require accurate interpretation of complex visual scenes, such as surveillance, crowd analysis, and situational awareness in public spaces
shi2022deep
;
janai2020computer
;
liang2023survey
.

The potential ethical risks associated with future advancements in LLMs are significant. As models become more sophisticated and capable of accurately analyzing real-world public scenes, there is a heightened risk of misuse in surveillance, manipulation, or influencing public behavior without consent. The ability of AI systems to process complex visual information and generate persuasive strategies could be exploited in ways that infringe upon privacy rights and erode societal trust. The potential for AI-driven persuasion has been a growing concern among ethicists and policymakers, particularly in the context of political influence and social control
brundage2018malicious
;
susser2019technology
;
gireesan2024democracy
;
weidinger2023taxonomy
. Recent studies have also highlighted the potential for LLMs to generate biased or misleading information, which could exacerbate societal divisions and undermine democratic processes
liang2021towards
;
kasneci2023chatgpt
.

From a technological perspective, our findings point to specific areas for improvement. Advancements in spatial reasoning, contextual grounding, and the integration of multimodal data processing are necessary to enhance the utility of LLMs in tasks requiring accurate interpretation of complex visual scenes. Future research could explore hybrid models that combine LLMs with specialized computer vision systems, such as those employed in autonomous navigation, to improve spatial understanding
bojarski2016end
. Other promising directions include using neuro-symbolic architectures
marra2024statistical
, developing more sophisticated attention mechanisms that better handle spatial relationships within images
liu2024transformer
, or incorporating common-sense reasoning capabilities to improve contextual grounding
mueller2014commonsense
.

In conclusion, while GPT-4o demonstrates impressive advancements in language generation and basic visual interpretation, significant limitations remain in its ability to perform complex social analysis and situational awareness tasks. Our controlled and ethically sound methodology provides a valuable framework for testing and benchmarking the evolving capabilities of advanced AI models in public mobilization contexts. These findings contribute to the growing body of research on the capabilities and limitations of LLMs and provide insights for the development of more robust and ethically aligned AI systems.

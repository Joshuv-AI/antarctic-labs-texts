Clinical case report on the efficacy and safety of savolitinib in elderly patients with non-small cell lung cancer harboring METex14 skipping mutations
Authors: Man Sun, Dan Zang, Huan Zhou, Jun Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSavolitinib is the first small-molecule inhibitor specifically targeting mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) tyrosine kinase approved in China. It is indicated for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring MET exon 14 (METex14) skipping mutations for use as either first-line or subsequent-line therapy. Currently, there is limited real-world evidence on the long-term survival outcomes of elderly NSCLC patients (aged 65 years and above) with METex14 skipping mutations. In this report, we present three cases of elderly NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations who were treated with savolitinib to evaluate its efficacy and safety in this population.Case DescriptionThe case series included three NSCLC patients harboring METex14 skipping mutations, aged 66 to 75 years, with comorbidities of Parkinson’s disease, hypertension, and breast cancer, respectively. They were treated with savolitinib as the second-line treatment after previously receiving AP regimens (pemetrexed + cisplatin). All patients achieved disease control according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1) within 2 months of initiating savolitinib (one with partial response and two with stable disease), and none showed disease progression. The progression-free survival (PFS) of the three patients was 15, 4, and 17 months, respectively.ConclusionsThe case series demonstrated that savolitinib has a favorable clinical efficacy and safety profile, suggesting its potential as a key therapeutic option for elderly patients with NSCLC harboring METex14 skipping mutations. The treatment offers sustained survival benefits and is well-tolerated.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), savolitinib, mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), case report

Highlight box

Key findings

• Savolitinib demonstrated sustained clinical efficacy and favorable safety in three elderly non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients harboring mesenchymal-epithelial transition exon 14 (METex14) skipping mutations, with progression-free survival durations of 15, 4, and 17 months, respectively. All three patients achieved disease control without serious adverse events.

What is known and what is new?

• Savolitinib is a selective mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) tyrosine kinase inhibitor approved for NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations. Previous clinical trials have shown its efficacy in both treatment-naïve and previously treated populations.

• This report provides real-world evidence supporting the safety and long-term benefits of savolitinib in elderly NSCLC patients with poor baseline conditions (e.g., Parkinson’s disease, hypertension, and prior malignancies). The case series shows histological heterogeneity (adenocarcinoma and squamous carcinoma) and demonstrates drug tolerability with dose adjustment when needed.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This case series supports the inclusion of savolitinib as a viable second-line therapy for elderly patients with METex14-mutated NSCLC, including those with comorbidities. Clinicians should consider early MET mutation screening in elderly NSCLC patients regardless of their histology, and pay attention to individualized dose adjustments to balance efficacy and safety. Further multicenter real-world studies are needed to validate the generalizability of the findings to diverse patient populations.

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the most prevalent and deadly malignant tumors globally. It is primarily classified into non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer, with NSCLC accounting for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases. The pathogenesis of lung cancer is complex and involves various gene mutations and the abnormal activation of signaling pathways (1,2).

The mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) gene is a proto-oncogene that encodes the MET receptor tyrosine kinase, which plays a key role in cell growth, survival, and metastasis. Upon binding with its ligand, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), the MET receptor dimerizes and subsequently activates downstream signaling pathways. This process is critically involved in tumorigenesis and metastasis (3,4). Among the genetic alterations observed in NSCLC, the MET (also known as c-MET) exon 14 skipping mutation is relatively common, affecting approximately 3–4% of patients (5,6).

In recent years, significant progress has been made in treatments targeting c-MET skipping mutations. Several c-MET inhibitors have been developed and applied in clinical settings, including small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and monoclonal antibodies. Notably, drugs such as savolitinib, capmatinib, and tepotinib have shown good efficacy in clinical trials, especially in the treatment of NSCLC patients with c-MET exon 14 skipping mutations. Savolitinib is the first approved small-molecule inhibitor that specifically targets MET tyrosine kinase in China. It is used in first-line or later-line therapy to treat adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with MET exon 14 (METex14) skipping mutations (7).

However, there is a scarcity of real-world data regarding the long-term survival outcomes of elderly NSCLC patients harboring METex14 skipping mutations. In this article, we present a case series of three elderly NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations who were treated with savolitinib to evaluate its efficacy and safety. We present this article in accordance with the CARE reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-2040/rc).

Case presentations

Patient 1

A 67-year-old woman with no history of smoking was diagnosed with stage IV lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) (cT4N2M1) harboring a METex14 skipping mutation in June 2023. She had a decade-long history of Parkinson’s disease and no familial predisposition to the condition. Her Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status at diagnosis was 2. Programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) immunohistochemistry (IHC) using the 22C3 antibody showed a tumor proportion score (TPS) <1%. Computed tomography (CT) scans in May 2023 showed a soft-tissue mass in the left lung upper lobe and hilum of the lung, multiple nodules in both lungs, and enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes, suggesting lung cancer with mediastinal lymph node metastases and bilateral lung metastases (Figure 1A).

Chest CT scans. (A,B) CT scans on August 31, 2023 showing SD in the chest. The red boxes indicate the primary lung lesion with SD. CT, computed tomography; SD, stable disease.

An AP regimen (0.5 g of pemetrexed on day 1, and 30 mg of carboplatin/cisplatin on days 1–3) was initiated in June 2023. The patient received one cycle of chemotherapy, but was then switched to savolitinib (400 mg once daily) due to severe gastrointestinal reactions. According to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (CTCAE v5.0), she developed grade 3 nausea and grade 2 fatigue during chemotherapy, which led to early discontinuation. The patient achieved stable disease (SD) after 2 months (Figure 1B) and continues to receive savolitinib to date. She had a progression-free survival (PFS) time of 15 months. She did not experience any treatment-related adverse events (AEs).

Patient 2

A 71-year-old woman with no history of smoking presented with chest pain and hemoptysis, and was diagnosed with right lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) stage IV NSCLC (cT4N1M1) harboring a METex14 skipping mutation. She had a history of hypertension and no family history of cancer. Her ECOG performance status at diagnosis was 2. In addition to hypertension, she had mild renal insufficiency (baseline creatinine 122 µmol/L), which limited her tolerance to platinum-based chemotherapy. PD-L1 IHC (22C3) revealed a TPS of 5%. CT scans revealed a cavitary mass located in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe of the lung, accompanied by multiple nodules in both lungs, indicative of lung cancer with bilateral metastatic involvement (Figure 2A). Subsequently, emission CT (ECT) scans showed increased uptake and enhancement in the body of the sternum, the right posterior 4th rib, and the 4th thoracic vertebra, suggesting a high possibility of bone metastases.

Chest CT scans. (A,B) CT scans on December 1, 2023 showing a PR in the chest. The red circles indicate the lung lesion demonstrating PR. CT, computed tomography; PR, partial response.

An AP regimen (0.6 g of pemetrexed on day 1, and 30 mg of carboplatin/cisplatin on days 1–2) was initiated in October 2023. After one cycle, the treatment was terminated due to severe gastrointestinal reactions. The patient experienced grade 3 diarrhea according to CTCAE v5.0, which required treatment discontinuation. She then switched to a savolitinib regimen (400 mg/day) in November 2023. The patient achieved partial response (PR) after two cycles and maintained tumor shrinkage within PR criteria for 4 months (Figure 2B). She had a PFS time of 4 months. She did not experience any treatment-related AEs.

Patient 3

A 66-year-old female with no history of smoking was admitted to hospital for treatment due to coughing and expectoration. She had a history of breast cancer and no family history of tumor. Her ECOG performance status was 2 at the time of NSCLC diagnosis. She had a long history of hyperlipidemia and mild hepatic steatosis, which may have contributed to chemotherapy intolerance. PD-L1 IHC (22C3) demonstrated a TPS <1%. In January 2023, a CT scan showed a mass in the right upper lobe of the lung measuring approximately 37 mm by 27 mm. A right upper lobectomy and mediastinal lymph node dissection were subsequently performed. The postoperative pathological diagnosis was stage IV NSCLC with pT2aN1M0 harboring an METex14 skipping mutation, with poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma of the right lung upper lobe, approximately 10% of which was sarcomatoid carcinoma. After radical resection for primary lesions and lymph node metastases, an AP regimen (0.8 g of pemetrexed on day 1 and 400 mg of carboplatin/cisplatin on day 1) was initiated in February 2023. After 5 months, the treatment was terminated due to bone metastases and adrenal metastases. During the preceding chemotherapy period, she experienced grade 2 vomiting and anorexia (CTCAE v5.0), which further reduced her tolerance to platinum-based therapy. She then switched to a savolitinib regimen (400 mg/day) in July 2023. She achieved SD after 3 months. As of the latest follow-up in August 2024, she achieved a PFS duration of 17 months without disease progression. During treatment, she developed grade 1 diarrhea, which was attributed to savolitinib. The dose was temporarily reduced to 200 mg once daily for 2 weeks, resulting in symptom resolution. After that, the original 400 mg daily dosage was successfully resumed without recurrence of diarrhea, indicating good long-term tolerability. All three patients had an ECOG performance status of 2 at the time of NSCLC diagnosis, and each presented with significant comorbidities—including Parkinson’s disease, cardiovascular risk factors, or a second primary malignancy—which collectively limited their tolerance to standard platinum-based chemotherapy or immune checkpoint inhibitor combinations. All three patients weighed less than 50 kg and therefore received the recommended 400 mg once-daily dose according to the Chinese prescribing label. As of August 2024, the PFS durations were 15, 4, and 17 months, respectively. All three patients are currently continuing on savolitinib.

Discussion

The positive rate of METex14 skipping mutations in NSCLC is approximately 3–4%, and it is more common in elderly patients with no smoking history, and is usually associated with a poor prognosis (8). Patients with METex14 skipping mutations have highly aggressive tumor cells, often develop resistance to anti-tumor therapy, and have a poor prognosis (9,10). A previous study reported that NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations who received first-line chemotherapy (11) had a median overall survival (OS) of only 6.7 months; thus, the overall efficacy of the treatment was poor. The efficacy of immunotherapy in NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations is also unsatisfactory. The objective response rate (ORR) of first-line immunotherapy in patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with METex14 skipping mutations is 17–35.7%, and the median PFS time is only 1.9 to 4.9 months (12,13). The most commonly used treatment for NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations is chemotherapy; however, even when it is used in combination with immunotherapy, the benefits of this treatment for this patient group are limited.

Savolitinib (Orpathys®) is an orally bioavailable and highly selective small-molecule MET-TKI that has demonstrated significant efficacy in a clinical study of various cancers, including NSCLC, papillary renal cell carcinoma, and gastric carcinoma (14). Although savolitinib has shown promising efficacy, its global availability remains limited, and it is currently only approved and can be reimbursed in China. Therefore, real-world experience from Chinese clinical practice, particularly in elderly patients, provides valuable evidence for regions with similar demographic and regulatory profiles. Savolitinib has been officially approved in China for the treatment of patients with advanced or metastatic NSCLC with METex14 mutations. The approval was based on a single-arm, multicenter, open-label, phase IIIb confirmatory clinical trial (NCT04923945) conducted in China (15). Patients with advanced/metastatic METex14-mutated NSCLC who had not received MET inhibitors, were newly treated with other therapies, or had received ≥2 lines of therapy, received 600 mg (≥50 kg) or 400 mg (<50 kg) of scervotinib once daily. The primary endpoint was the ORR as assessed by an independent review committee (IRC) according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1). The results were reported in 2024 European Lung Cancer Congress (ELCC) (15), and showed that among the patients with initial treatment (n=87), the ORR was 62.1%, the disease control rate (DCR) was 92.0%, and the median duration of response (DoR) was 12.5 months, with a median follow-up time of 20.8 months, a median PFS time of 13.7 months, and a median OS time not reached. In the previously treated patients (n=79), the ORR was 39.2%, the DCR was 92.4%, and the median DoR was 11.1 months, with a median follow-up time of 12.5 months, a median PFS of 11.0 months, and the median OS not yet reached. Both the treatment-naive and treatment-experienced patients achieved remission earlier than typically observed with chemotherapy or immunotherapy, with a median time to response of 1.4–1.6 months (16). The safety profile was tolerable, and no new safety signals were observed. The results of the subsequent first-line treatment were accepted by The Lancet Respiratory Medicine and published in full in September 2024. The final OS and long-term safety data from the study are available from the 2025 ELCC (17). The median follow-up time of newly diagnosed patients (n=87) and treated patients (n=79) was 34.5 and 25.1 months, respectively. The median OS of newly diagnosed patients was 28.3 months, and the 36-month OS rate was 44.7%. The median OS time of the previously treated patients was 25.3 months, and the 24-month OS rate was 51.7%. The long-term safety analysis showed that there were no new safety signals, and the common AEs included abnormal liver function (17.5%), elevated alanine aminotransferase (15.1%), and elevated aspartate aminotransferase (12.0%), which further verified the good safety profile of savolitinib.

Savolitinib is now covered by medical insurance for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with a METex14 skipping mutation who experience disease progression after platinum-based chemotherapy or who are intolerant to standard platinum-based chemotherapy.

In 2024, testing for METex14 skipping mutations was included in the recommended molecular diagnostic items for advanced NSCLC in several domestic and international guidelines, including those of the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO), with a recommendation level of I or II. In addition to the NCCN (18) and CSCO (19) guidelines, several other guidelines and consensus statements, including the Chinese Medical Association guidelines for the clinical diagnosis and treatment of lung cancer (2024 edition) (20), the Asian Thoracic Oncology Research Group expert consensus on MET alterations in NSCLC (21), the Chinese expert consensus on clinical practice of MET detection in NSCLC (22), and the Chinese clinical practice guidelines for stage IV primary lung cancer (2024 edition) (23), also recommend routine testing for METex14 skipping mutations in patients with advanced NSCLC.

This case series included three patients with NSCLC who all harbored METex14 skipping mutations, with histological subtypes of both LUAD and LUSC. These patients had adverse prognostic factors, such as advanced age, multiple metastases, and underlying diseases. With increasing age, elderly lung cancer patients (aged 65 years and above) have more underlying diseases and show a decline in organ reserve function. Changes in the physiological functions of elderly patients can affect many aspects of drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion, and may lead to a decrease in their tolerance to treatment (24). In addition, as elderly lung cancer patients often have multiple diseases, it is common for them to receive multiple medications to treat both the tumor and related diseases. A study showed that the median number of combined medications in elderly cancer patients is as high as five to nine (25). As a high proportion of elderly lung cancer patients have liver and kidney dysfunction, the dosage of anti-tumor drugs metabolized by the liver and kidneys needs to be adjusted to reduce their toxic effects and side effects.

Previous reports have shown that the overall incidence of METex14 skipping mutations in elderly NSCLC patients is higher than that in younger patients (25,26). The driver gene spectrum of elderly NSCLC patients has certain characteristics but does not differ significantly from that of younger patients. All three patients in this report were aged over 65 years. After first-line chemotherapy [AP/AC (pemetrexed + cisplatin)], the treatment plans were changed to savolitinib [400 mg once daily, oral (po)] for all three patients due to disease progression or intolerable AEs.

At present, patient 1 has maintained a SD for 15 months and has not experienced any treatment-related AEs, patient 2 achieved PR after the first cycle of treatment and has maintained SD for 4 months now, and patient 3, who also has breast cancer, has maintained SD for 17 months. Overall, the disease status of the three patients is stable, their quality of life has improved, the treatment benefits are ongoing, and their overall tolerance is good. No AEs occurred during the savolitinib treatment, showing the safety of savolitinib. Thus, patients with LUAD and LUSC can benefit from treatment with savolitinib, which provides targeted therapy opportunities for patients with LUSC. Savolitinib has rapid and long-lasting effects in precision targeted therapy, and can provide significant benefits to NSCLC patients with METex14 skipping mutations.

Strengths and limitations

Case reports can timely and comprehensively record the first-hand clinical data of patients’ clinical manifestations, pathogeneses, diagnoses, and treatments, etc., in real clinical environments. The three patients in this case series showed the efficacy and safety of savolitinib as a second-line treatment for METex14-mutated NSCLC in real-world settings, further complementing clinical research findings on savolitinib. However, this case report was based on the treatment outcomes of individual clinical cases and lacks a rigorous research design and standardized control design; thus, the strength of the evidence for the clinical hypotheses is relatively low. Therefore, when interpreting the results, some limitations should be noted. First, we only reported on a small number of patients treated at a single institution. Although the individual patients included have unique characteristics, our findings may not fully represent the efficacy and safety of savolitinib in all patients in diverse clinical settings. Another limitation is that all these patients remain under treatment, so the final results will need to be updated.

Conclusions

This case report sought to present our clinical findings for three patients with lung cancer who received savolitinib treatment. We provided data on the application of savolitinib in real-world clinical practice by comparing and analyzing disease changes and evaluating treatment efficacy in three NSCLC patients before and after the initiation of savolitinib treatment. These data are not only complementary to clinical research data, but also provide details of our practical experience and may serve as a reference for clinicians.

Abstract
We study the extreme value statistics of the zero-average Gaussian free field (GFF) on random r r -regular graphs and the Gaussian free field on r r -regular trees. For random r r -regular graphs of diverging size, for every fixed r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , we show that the rescaled extremal point process of the field is asymptotically distributed, in the annealed sense, as a Poisson point process on the line with intensity e − x ​ d ​ x e^{-x}\,\mathrm{d}x . The same limit behaviour is obeyed by the restriction of the GFF on r r -regular trees to finite subsets of vertices. Our approach relies on a direct Gaussian comparison argument and precise Green function estimates.

Introduction and main result

In recent years, properties of level set percolation and local properties of the zero-average Gaussian free field (GFF) on finite graphs and trees have been studied intensively; see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
10
,
17
,
19
,

In the present article, we focus on the extreme value statistics of such fields on random
r
-regular graphs and identify the proper scaling of the extremes. The only previous results in this direction for regular graphs are the rough upper bound in
[
14
]
, and the work
[
15
]
in the rigid setting of the Euclidean torus. Note that whereas
[
15
]
relies on a coupling introduced in
[
1
]
, we develop a different approach to obtain our main result: we combine a general Gaussian comparison principle for point processes (Theorem
2
) with estimates for the Green’s function on random
r
-regular graphs (Section 4.1) that complement the estimates in
[
3
]
. We believe both of these contributions to be of independent interest.

Throughout, we let
({G}_{N},o_{N})
denote a uniformly rooted
r
-regular graph on
N\in 2\mathbb{N}
vertices. The corresponding distribution on finite graphs is denoted by
\mathbf{P}
and the graph distances in
{G}_{N}
are written as

. We work under the law

\mathbb{P}_{N}\;=\;\int\mathbb{P}^{{G}_{N}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{P}({G}_{N}),

where
\mathbb{P}^{{G}_{N}}
is the process law conditional on the graph realisation
{G}_{N}
. Given
{G}_{N}
, we consider the canonical simple random walk
(X_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
its continuous-time counterpart
(\overline{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
and the corresponding zero-average GFF
\Psi_{N}
on
{G}_{N}
.
The latter is defined as the centred Gaussian field with covariances given by

G_{{G}_{N}}(x,y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{P}_{x}^{{G}_{N}}(\overline{X}_{t}=y)-\tfrac{1}{N}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad x,y\in{G}_{N},

that is, the

of
{G}_{N}
. We further set

\sigma_{r}^{2}:=\frac{r-1}{r-2},\qquad a_{N}:=\sqrt{2\log N}-\frac{\log\log N+\log(4\pi)}{2\sqrt{2\log N}},\qquad b_{N}:=a_{N}^{-1},

(1.1)

and define the rescaled field

, for

. The associated extremal point process and centred maximum are given by

\mathcal{P}_{N}:=\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\delta_{\frac{Z_{x}-a_{N}}{b_{N}}},\qquad M_{N}:=\max_{x\in V_{N}}\frac{Z_{x}-a_{N}}{b_{N}}.

Let further
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
be a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}
with intensity
e^{-x}\,\mathrm{d}x
,
and set

M_{\infty}:=\max\mathcal{P}_{\infty}.

Denoting by
\Rightarrow

Theorem 1

(Extremal point process and maximum on the random
r
-regular graph)
.

Let

. Then, with the notation above, as

, there exist sets of graphs

, such that
\mathbf{P}(\Omega_{N}^{\mathsf{c}})=o(1)

(\mathcal{P}_{N},\,M_{N})\ \overset{\mathbb{P}^{{G}_{N}}}{\Longrightarrow}\ (\mathcal{P}_{\infty},\,M_{\infty}).

Consequently,

which in particular implies that

is asymptotically Gumbel-distributed, so that

\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big(\max_{x\in{G}_{N}}\Psi_{N}(x)\ \leq\ \sigma_{r}\Big(a_{N}+tb_{N}\Big)\Big)\ \longrightarrow\ \exp(-e^{-t}).

Lemma 5

Theorem 6

(Extremal point process and maximum on the

-regular tree)
.

Let

Proof.

We wish to apply Theorem

Recall that

\rho_{xy}:=\mathbb{E}[Z_{x}Z_{y}]=\left(\frac{1}{r-1}\right)^{d_{T}(x,y)}\in[0,1)\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in V_{N}\text{ with }x\neq y.

vanishes as

. To this end, we first observe that, for

(X_{x}(h),X_{y}(h))
is a centred Gaussian vector with unit variances
and correlation

. We next apply Lemma

, first noting that

\mathbb{P}\Big(\tfrac{X_{x}(h)-a_{N}}{b_{N}}\in S,\,\tfrac{X_{y}(h)-a_{N}}{b_{N}}\in S\Big)\ \leq\ \frac{b_{N}^{2}|S|^{2}}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho_{xy}(h)^{2}}}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{a_{N}^{2}}{1+\rho_{xy}(h)}\Big),

and we decompose

\sum_{x\neq y}\rho_{xy}\,H(\rho_{xy})\;=\;\underbrace{\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\star}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x,y\}\subset V_{N}\\
d_{{G}_{N}}(x,y)=k\end{subarray}}\rho_{k}H(\rho_{k})}_{=:\ \varepsilon_{N}^{\mathrm{near}}}\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{k>k_{\star}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x,y\}\subset V_{N}\\
d_{{G}_{N}}(x,y)=k\end{subarray}}\rho_{k}H(\rho_{k})}_{=:\ \varepsilon_{N}^{\mathrm{far}}}.

For
1\leq k\leq k_{\star}
, using (
4.4
) and the monotonicity of
H
,

\varepsilon_{N}^{\mathrm{near}}\ \leq\ \sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\star}}n_{k}\,\rho_{k}\,H(\rho_{k})\ \leq\ \sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\star}}\big(C_{1}N(r-1)^{k}\big)\big(C_{2}(r-1)^{-k}\big)\,H(\rho_{1})\ \leq\ C\,N\,k_{\star}\,H(\rho_{1}),

where
\rho_{1}\leq c_{0}<1
depends only on
r
.
As
a_{N}^{2}=2\log N+O(\log\log N)
, we find

H(\rho_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{1}^{2}}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{a_{N}^{2}}{1+\rho_{1}}\Big)\ \leq\ C\,N^{-\,\frac{2}{1+\rho_{1}}}(\log N)^{O(1)}.

Since
\frac{2}{1+\rho_{1}}>1
for
r\geq 3
, we conclude
\varepsilon_{N}^{\mathrm{near}}=o(1)
. For
k>k_{\star}
,
\rho_{k}\leq CN^{-k_{3}}
, hence

\varepsilon_{N}^{\mathrm{far}}\ \leq\ \binom{N}{2}\cdot\frac{CN^{-k_{3}}}{\sqrt{1-C^{2}N^{-2k_{3}}}}\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{a_{N}^{2}}{1+CN^{-k_{3}}}\Big)\ =\ N^{-k_{3}+o(1)}\ =\ o(1).

Combining both bounds, we see that
T_{N}({G}_{N})=o(1)
, and thus by Theorem
2
we obtain

\Big(P_{\widehat{Z}},\ \max_{x\in V_{N}}\tfrac{\widehat{Z}_{x}-a_{N}}{b_{N}}\Big)\ \Longrightarrow\ (\mathcal{P}_{\infty},\ \max\mathcal{P}_{\infty}).

Recalling that the terms
\delta_{N}=o(a_{N}^{-1})
in (
4.3
) are negligible,
we obtain the same limit for the point processes

\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\delta_{(Z_{x}-a_{N})/b_{N}}.

∎

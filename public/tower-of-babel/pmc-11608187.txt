The immune cells causative role in lung cancer: Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Qianhui Zhou, Yanchao Liang, Xun Chen, Siwei Qi, Lanlan Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundsLung cancer (LC) is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths in China, with its incidence and mortality rates rising annually. Various studies have demonstrated disparities in immune cell counts and profiles between LC patients and healthy individuals. However, the existence of a causal link remains largely uncertain. To explore the potential causal association between immune cells and LC risk, a Mendelian randomization (MR) approach was employed.MethodsEmploying immune cell genome-wide association study (GWAS) data from a public database (n = 731 subjects) and Lung Cancer Genome Research Consortium's GWAS dataset (n = 492,803 participants), unconventional genetic loci remarkably associated with the significant abundance of 731 types of immune cells were extracted according to preset threshold points as instrumental variables. The primarily analytical strategy involved the inverse variance weighting (IVW) method, with the effect size estimates expressed as odds ratio (OR) and 95% CI. The robustness of the findings was assessed through a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, heterogeneity test, horizontal gene pleiotropy test, MR-PRESSO and MR-Egger.ResultsThe MR analysis identified a significant association between 18 distinct immune cell types and lung cancer risk. MR analysis revealed that 7 types of immune cells, including CD20 on IgD − CD38 − B cell, CD20 on switched memory B cell, IgD on IgD + CD24 + B cell, BAFF − R on IgD − CD38 + B cell, CD39 + resting CD4 regulatory T cell Absolute Count, CD127 on CD45RA + CD4 + T cell, CD8 on Effector Memory CD8 + T cell, displayed a protective effect against lung cancer development. Conversely, 11 other immune cells populations were positive associated with an increased lung cancer risk. In contrast, reverse MR analysis indicated that the lung cancer risk was linked to 11 immune cells, with 2 types of immune cells (CD25 on IgD − CD27 − B cell, CD4 + T cell %leukocyte) showing a are positive correlation with cancer development, whereas 9 types of immune cells appeared to be inversely regulated. These findings collectively imply that specific immune cell populations may play critical roles in either promoting or suppressing the initiation and progression of lung cancer.ConclusionThe findings of this study offer fresh insights into the underlying biological mechanisms governing lung cancer development, thereby opening new avenues for future research in the domain of LC. The identification of specific immune cell subsets with protective or promoting roles provides crucial leads for preventive and therapeutic strategies. These insights have the potential to enhance early detection methods and inform the development of more effective treatment approaches, ultimately benefiting patient outcomes and improving the overall management of lung cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01637-2.Keywords: Lung cancer, Immune cell, Mendelian randomization, Causal relationship, Sensitivity analysis

Introduction

Lung cancer remains a global health crisis and is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1, 2]. Mortality patterns vary across countries with different economic conditions; however, there is a notable similarity in lung cancer death rates among men in both industrialized and developing nations [3]. In contrast, women in developed countries exhibit higher mortality rates [3]. Continuous advancements in biomedical technology have led to the increased use of molecular targeted therapies and immunotherapies, enhancing survival and quality of life for lung cancer patients [4, 5]. However, high mortality rates persist in many regions [2, 6].

The immune system plays a crucial role in combating tumors, with empirical evidence showing rhythmic fluctuations in immune parameters [7, 8]. Lung cancer patients harbor multiple immune cell types, including T cells, B cells, macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells (DCs), all of which play distinct roles in immune surveillance and tumor regulation [9–11]. T cells, particularly CD8 + cytotoxic T lymphocytes, directly eliminate cancer cells, while B cells produce antibodies that neutralize tumor antigens and promote adaptive immunity [11, 12]. Macrophages, polarized into M1 (pro-inflammatory) or M2 (anti-inflammatory) phenotypes, can either inhibit or promote tumor growth [12, 13]. NK cells provide innate immune defense, and dendritic cells (DCs) activate adaptive immunity through antigen presentation [4, 11].

Despite advancements, gaps remain in our understanding of the causal mechanisms linking immune cell profiles to lung cancer outcomes [6]. Although observational studies have shown associations between immune traits and lung cancer, the causal nature of these relationships remains unclear [10, 14, 15]. Recent findings indicate that genetic variations influence immune responses and lung cancer progression [14, 16]. However, these mechanisms are not yet fully elucidated. Mendelian Randomization (MR) offers a novel approach by leveraging genetic variation to explore causal links, mimicking a natural randomized trial [17, 18].

Our study employed a two-sample MR approach using data from genome-wide association studies (GWAS) involving over 490,000 individuals to investigate whether immune cell profiles causally influence lung cancer development [19, 20]. The study aims to fill gaps in current knowledge by establishing robust causal links, identifying potential therapeutic targets, and informing preventive strategies [21, 22]. Genetic markers associated with immune function and lung cancer have been identified [14, 15], suggesting that specific polymorphisms can influence immune responses and tumor progression, but the causal relationships require further investigation [16, 23].

Methods

Research design

This study employed a comprehensive approach by harnessing summary statistics from multiple public Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) to execute a bidirectional Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis, thereby investigating the causal connections between 731 distinct immune cell subtypes and lung cancer development. To ensure the robustness of the causal inferences, rigorous quality control measures were implemented, including heterogeneity assessments and tests for gene pleiotropy.

To conduct our Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis, we utilized data from publicly available databases and the Lung Cancer Genome Research Consortium (LCGRC) datasets. These resources provided comprehensive genetic and clinical data essential for our study.

Selection of Genetic Loci as Instrumental Variables:

To select genetic loci as instrumental variables (IVs), we applied the following preset threshold points:

Significance Threshold:

GWAS P-value: ≤ 5 × 10–8.

F-statistic: ≥ 10 to ensure strong instruments.

Linkage Disequilibrium (LD) Clumping:

LD Clumping Distance: ≤ 100 kb.

LD Clumping r2 Threshold: ≥ 0.8 to avoid highly correlated SNPs.

Functional relevance:

SNPs must be functionally relevant to the immune cell phenotypes of interest.

By using these stringent criteria, we ensured that the selected genetic loci were robust and appropriate for use as instrumental variables in our MR analysis.

Genome-wide association study (GWAS) data sources for lung cancer

In this research, the genetic underpinnings of lung cancer (LC) are explored utilizing comprehensive genome-wide association study (GWAS) data. The genetic summary statistics are derived from a meticulously curated dataset, which focuses on the European population. This dataset encompasses a well-characterized cohort of 3,791 lung cancer cases and a substantial control group of 489,012 healthy individuals.

A rigorous quality control (QC) protocol was implemented during the genotyping and imputation processes to ensure data integrity. This entailed stringent filtering criteria to eliminate potential errors, such as sample contamination, genotyping inconsistencies, and population stratification. Following these QC measures, a total of 24,188,684 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were incorporated into the analysis, providing a comprehensive genomic landscape for identifying associations between genetic variations and lung cancer susceptibility.

GWAS data sources of immune cells

The present study draws upon a compendious collection of 731 immunological traits sourced from the extensive Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) Catalog, covering accession numbers ranging from GCST0001391 to GCST0002121. This rich dataset represents a comprehensive exploration of immune system dynamics, with a focus on understanding the genetic foundations of various immune responses.

From these 731 immune characteristics, a diverse array of phenotypes is represented. Approximately 26% of these traits correspond to relative cell count measurements, capturing the proportional abundance of different immune cell populations. Another 16% consist of absolute cell counts, quantifying the exact number of cells within a specific compartment. Furthermore, approximately 4% of the traits are dedicated to morphological parameters, delving into the structural aspects of immune cells. The remaining majority, comprising nearly 53% of the dataset, involves median fluorescence intensity (MFI) measurements, which provide insights into the functional expression levels of immune-related proteins or receptors.

Selection of instrumental variables

For the selection of instrumental variables (IVs), a rigorous methodology was employed, drawing on established practices in the field. A stringent significance threshold of P-value less than 5 × 10^−8 was adopted, as per prior studies, to ensure the reliability of the genetic associations [21]. This threshold helps filter out false positives and guarantees that the chosen SNPs (single nucleotide polymorphisms) are strongly associated with the respective immune cell abundances.

To maintain the genetic independence of the IVs, the European subset of the 1000 Genomes Project (IKG-EUR) was utilized as the reference panel [24]. A stringent linkage disequilibrium (LD) threshold of r2 < 0.001 was set, which aids in eliminating SNPs that are in proximity and potentially correlated. A window size of 10,000 base pairs (kb) was designated to scan the genome, allowing for the exclusion of highly correlated SNPs and ensuring the exclusivity of the selected markers [25].

Subsequently, SNPs that exhibited significant associations with immune cell abundance were integrated into the lung cancer GWAS summary data. The corresponding statistical parameters were extracted, harmonizing the information across datasets. A crucial step was to align the effect alleles of the significant immune cell abundance SNPs with those from the lung cancer GWAS results. This alignment ensures that the estimated hazard and effect values correspond to the same genetic variant, providing a consistent basis for the Mendelian randomization analysis.

Statistical analysis

Linkage disequilibrium analysis, MR analysis, and quality control in the study were conducted by using “Two Sample MR” package in R software (version 4.0.3) [19, 20]. The study used five methods to validate causative effects: IVW method, simple mode method, MR-Egger method, weighted median method and weighted mode method [23, 26]. To test the causal relationship between immune cell phenotypes and lung cancer risks, we employed the Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW) method as our primary method for estimating causal effects. The IVW method is widely recognized for its effectiveness and is commonly used in Mendelian Randomization (MR) studies due to its robustness and statistical power. The IVW method has greater effectiveness of testing than the remaining four MR methods. This study uses the IVW method as the preferred causative effect validated method [27]. At the same time, to interpret the better results, the study converted the resulting Beta values into odds ratios and calculated 95% confidence intervals.

To ensure the stability and reliability of the results, a leave-one-out cross-validation was conducted for sensitivity analysis. Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran's Q statistic and corresponding p-values. To account for potential horizontal pleiotropy, the MR-Egger method was employed; if the intercept in this model was statistically significant, it indicated the presence of horizontal pleiotropy [28].

Results

The causative effect of immune phenotype on LC

The causative effects of absolute peripheral immune cell phenotypic counts on lung cancer risk are shown in Supplementary Table 1. We identified 18 cellular immune phenotypes closely related to lung cancer hazards, including 7 types of Treg cells, 2 types of TBNK cells, 5 types of B cells, 3 maturation stages of T cells, and 1 type of cDC, all of which have a significant causal relationship with lung cancer (Fig. 1A, B). Specifically, 7 immune cell phenotypes were negatively correlated with lung cancer risk: CD20 on IgD − CD38 − B cell (OR = 0.841, 95% CI 0.720–0.984, p = 0.030); CD20 on switched memory B cell (OR = 0.874, 95% CI 0.768–0.994, p = 0.041); IgD on IgD + CD24 + B cell (OR = 0.880, 95% CI 0.778–0.996, p = 0.044); BAFF − R on IgD − CD38 + B cell (OR = 0.886, 95% CI 0.803–0.978, p = 0.016); CD39 + resting CD4 regulatory T cell Absolute Count (OR = 0.968, 95% CI 0.938–1.000, p = 0.049); CD127 on CD45RA + CD4 + T cell (OR = 0.901, 95% CI 0.820–0.990, p = 0.029); and CD8 on Effector Memory CD8 + T cells (OR = 0.875, 95% CI 0.770–0.995, p = 0.041). In contrast, 11 immune cell phenotypes were positively correlated with lung cancer risk: CD3 on secreting CD4 regulatory T cells (OR = 1.072, 95% CI 1.017–1.130, p = 0.010); CD3 on Central Memory CD4 + T cells (OR = 1.087, 95% CI 1.020–1.158, p = 0.010); CD3 on CD45RA + CD4 + T cell (OR = 1.057, 95% CI 1.033–1.115, p = 0.038); CD28 − CD4 − CD8 − T cell %T cell (OR = 1.186, 95% CI 1.012–1.389, p = 0.035); CD3 on T cell (OR = 1.106, 95% CI 1.023–1.197, p = 0.011); SSC-A on myeloid Dendritic Cell (OR = 1.224, 95% CI 1.033–1.258, p = 0.044); CD3 on CD45RA − CD4 + T cells (OR = 1.098, 95% CI 1.022–1.178, p = 0.020); CD3 on CD39 + CD4 + T cells (OR = 1.066, 95% CI 1.011–1.124, p = 0.018); CD3 on CD28 + CD45RA + CD8 + T cell (OR = 1.075, 95% CI 1.016–1.137, p = 0.013); CD4 − CD8 − T cell %leukocyte (OR = 1.203, 95% CI 1.042–1.389, p = 0.012); and CD4 − CD8 − T cell %T cell (OR = 1.243, 95% CI 1.062–1.455, p = 0.007) (Fig. 2). Detailed data of the other three analysis methods are shown in Supplementary Table 1. Additionally, we further conducted heterogeneity test, horizontal pleiotropy test and leave-one-out test to validate the robustness of the results observed in this study. Cochran's Q test results exhibited that there was lack of heterogeneity among IVs (Supplementary Table 2). The absence of heterogeneity was also confirmed by the symmetry of the visual funnel plot (Supplementary Fig. 1). The MR-Egger intercept test and the MRPRESSO global test excluding outlier variables exhibited lack of horizontal pleiotropy (Supplementary Table 3 and Supplementary Fig. 2). By using the leave-out method, sensitivity analysis was carried out and exhibited stable results when SNPs were removed one by one (Supplementary Fig. 3).

The causative effect of LC on immune cell phenotypes

To test whether immune cell phenotypes have a reverse causal relationship with lung cancer risks, Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis was performed between lung cancer and immune cell phenotypes. The results indicated that lung cancer affects the expression of several immune cells, specifically including 3 types of myeloid cells, 3 types of mononuclear cells, 1 type of TBNK cells, and 4 types of B cells. Using the Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW) method, we found that lung cancer is associated with higher levels of: CD25 on IgD − CD27 − B cell (OR = 1.233, 95% CI 1.049–1.450, p = 0.011), CD4 + T cell %leukocyte (OR = 1.177, 95% CI 1.077–1.374, p = 0.040). Conversely, lung cancer is associated with lower levels of: CD11b on CD66b + + myeloid cell (OR = 0.730, 95% CI 0.578–0.923, p = 0.009), CD45 on CD66b + + myeloid cell (OR = 0.762, 95% CI 0.603–0.963, p = 0.023), CCR2 on monocyte (OR = 0.829, 95% CI 0.710–0.968, p = 0.018), CCR2 on CD14 − CD16 − (OR = 0.837, 95% CI 0.714–0.981, p = 0.028), PD-L1 on monocyte (OR = 0.815, 95% CI 0.697–0.953, p = 0.011), CD24 on transitional B cell (OR = 0.790, 95% CI 0.677–0.923, p = 0.003), CD24 on IgD + CD24 + B cell (OR = 0.824, 95% CI 0.720–0.983, p = 0.030), CD19 on IgD − CD38 − B cells (OR = 0.845, 95% CI 0.723–0.988, p = 0.035). Both the forward and reverse MR analyses consistently show that certain immune cell phenotypes are protective against lung cancer, while others are associated with increased risk. The protective immune cell types, such as CD11b on CD66b + + myeloid cell and CD24 on transitional B cell, show similar protective effects in both directions. Similarly, risk-associated immune cell types, such as CD25 on IgD − CD27 − B cell and CD4 + T cell %leukocyte, show consistent risk associations in both directions (Fig. 3).

To further validate the robustness of these results, we conducted additional tests, including horizontal pleiotropy tests, heterogeneity tests, and leave-one-out tests. Cochran's Q test results showed no significant heterogeneity among the instrumental variables (Supplementary Table 5). The symmetry of the visual funnel plot also confirmed the absence of heterogeneity (Supplementary Fig. 4). The MR-Egger intercept test and the MRPRESSO global test, excluding outlier variables, demonstrated no evidence of horizontal pleiotropy (Supplementary Table 6 and Supplementary Fig. 5).

Discussion

Lung cancer, the leading cause of cancer-related deaths, demands innovative therapeutic approaches [1, 2, 5]. Immunotherapy has revolutionized cancer treatment, offering new hope to patients by effectively targeting malignancies [29]. Immune cell therapy is increasingly used as adjuvant treatment for lung cancer patients [15]. Researchers are now focusing on the causal links between immune cells and lung cancer through the first comprehensive Mendelian Randomization (MR) analysis involving 731 immune cell types [18, 19]. This analysis revealed that both tumor-suppressing and tumor-promoting immune cells coexist in a delicate balance, and disruptions can lead to carcinogenesis [14, 15].

Several studies emphasize the prognostic significance of specific immune cells. Stromal DCs, CD8 + T cells, M1 macrophages, NK cells, and B cells correlate with improved prognosis, while elevated PD-L1, Treg cells, and M2 macrophages indicate poor outcomes [10–13, 30]. Our study found that seven Treg cell subsets impact lung cancer incidence, with six showing a positive association, aligning with previous research on Treg cells’ role in poor prognosis [10, 30].

Differences in immune cell composition between non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues, lymph nodes, and non-cancerous tissues have been documented, highlighting the importance of immune contexture in lung cancer [31, 32]. Our analysis identified 13 T cell subtypes associated with lung cancer, supporting their role in both initiation and progression [15, 32]. B cells, whose phenotypes vary across clinical stages, exhibit both antitumor and protumor functions and interact with T cells in tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) [11, 33, 34]. Our study identified four B cell subsets negatively associated with lung cancer risk, suggesting a protective role [11, 35].

This study found significant associations between 11 immune cell types and lung cancer, including B cells, TBNK cells, monocytes, and myeloid cells [36, 37]. Our findings align with prior research on the CCR2 marker's involvement in NSCLC and its relevance to anti-PD-L1 therapy [36, 38]. The two-way MR method used in this study ensures robust causative inference by reducing confounding effects and addressing horizontal pleiotropy [20, 26, 27]. However, certain limitations remain. The lack of detailed participant information and stratified analyses limits our ability to generalize findings across populations [24]. Furthermore, most GWAS participants were of European descent, reducing the applicability of results to other racial groups [24, 25].

Future research should focus on cellular and animal studies to further investigate the mechanisms underlying lung cancer [5]. Expanding the diversity of study populations will improve the generalizability of findings across ethnic groups [16]. Additionally, differentiating between lung cancer subtypes, such as small cell and non-small cell lung cancer, will provide more precise insights into the causal associations between immune cells and disease progression [32, 33].

Conclusion

In conclusion, the comprehensive two-way MR analysis reveals significant causal connections between diverse immune cell profiles and lung cancer (LC), elucidating the intricate interplay between the immune system and LC pathogenesis. This study remarkably reduces the unavoidable confounding effects, retro causality and other factors. This may give researchers with new ways to probe the biological mechanisms of lung cancer and provide a molecular basis for earlier intervention and treatment.

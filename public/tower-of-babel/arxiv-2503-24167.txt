Relative solidity for biexact groups in measure equivalence
Changying Ding, Daniel Drimbe

Abstract
. We demonstrate a relative solidity property for the product of a nonamenable biexact group with an arbitrary infinite group in the measure equivalence setting. Among other applications, we obtain the following unique product decomposition for products of nonamenable biexact groups, strengthening [ Sak09 ] : for any nonamenable biexact groups Γ 1 , ⋯ , Γ n \Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{n} , if a product group Λ 1 × Λ 2 \Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2} is measure equivalent to × k = 1 n Γ k \times_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k} , then there exists a partition T 1 ⊔ T 2 = { 1 , … , n } T_{1}\sqcup T_{2}=\{1,\dots,n\} such that Λ i \Lambda_{i} is measure equivalent to × k ∈ T i Γ k \times_{k\in T_{i}}\Gamma_{k} for i = 1 , 2 i=1,2 .



1.
Introduction

Two countable groups
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are said to be
measure equivalent
in the sense of Gromov
[
Gro93
]
if there exist commuting free measure preserving actions of
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
on a standard
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
such that both the actions
\Gamma\curvearrowright(\Omega,\mu)
and
\Lambda\curvearrowright(\Omega,\mu)
admit a finite measure fundamental domain. The classification of countable groups up to measure equivalence is a central theme in measured group theory and many spectacular innovations have been made in the last 25 years, see for instance the introduction of
[
HHI23
]
.

In their seminal work
[
MS06
]
, Monod and Shalom used techniques from bounded cohomology theory to obtain the following general unique prime factorization result: if
\times_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k}
is a product of non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic groups that is measure equivalent to a product
\times_{i=1}^{m}\Lambda_{i}
of torsion-free groups and
m\geq n
, then
n=m
and after a permutation of the indices,
\Gamma_{i}
is measure equivalent to
\Lambda_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n
.
Sako then used C
∗
-algebraic techniques from
[
Oza04
,
OP04
]
, to extend in
[
Sak09
]
(see also
[
CS13
]
) the above unique prime factorization results to products of nonamenable biexact groups. For additional such unique prime factorization results, see
[
DHI19
,
Dri23
]
.

In the framework of II
1
factors, Ozawa and Popa obtained in
[
OP04
]
a striking analogue to Monod and Shalom’s unique prime factorization result. They showed that if
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
are i.c.c. (infinite conjugacy class) nonamenable hyperbolic groups (more generally, biexact groups) such that the group von Neumann algebra
L(\times_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k})
is stably isomorphic to a tensor product of
{\rm II}_{1}
factors
\,\bar{\otimes}\,_{i=1}^{m}N_{i}
and
m\geq n
, then
m=n
and after a permutation of the indices,
L(\Gamma_{i})
is stably isomorphic to
L(\Lambda_{i})
for any
1\leq i\leq n
. In fact, Ozawa and Popa proved a more general unique prime factorization phenomenon by classifying
all tensor product decompositions
of
L(\times_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k})
. More precisely, they showed that if
L(\times_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k})
is stably isomorphic to a tensor product of
{\rm II}_{1}
factors
N_{1}\,\bar{\otimes}\,N_{2}
,
then there exists a partition
T_{1}\sqcup T_{2}=\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
N_{i}
is stably isomorphic to
L(\times_{k\in T_{i}}\Gamma_{k})
for any
1\leq i\leq 2
.

In our first main result we show that a stronger form of Sako’s unique prime factorization result
[
Sak09
]
holds
by classifying
all i.c.c. product groups
that are measure equivalent to a product of nonamenable biexact groups.

Theorem A
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
be nonamenable biexact groups.
Suppose
\times_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k}
is measure equivalent to a product
\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
of i.c.c. groups.
Then there exists a partition
T_{1}\sqcup T_{2}=\{1,\dots,n\}

such that
\Lambda_{j}
is measure equivalent to
\times_{i\in T_{j}}\Gamma_{i}
for
j=1,2
.

Note that Theorem
A
is a consequence of
[
DHI19
]
under the stronger assumption that
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
are non-elementary hyperbolic groups. Results in
[
DHI19
]
were achieved by using a combination of tools from Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory including
the fundamental work of Popa and Vaes
[
PV14b
]

which shows that any hyperbolic (more generally, groups that are weakly amenable and biexact) satisfies the following
relative strong solidity property
: if
\Gamma\curvearrowright N
is a trace preserving action of a hyperbolic group and
P\subset M:=N\rtimes\Gamma
is a von Neumann subalgebra that is amenable relative to
N
inside
M
, then
P\prec_{M}N
or
\mathcal{N}_{M}(P)^{\prime\prime}
is amenable relative to
N
. An essential ingredient used in
[
DHI19
]
is that any group
\Gamma
with the relative strong solidity property satisfies the following
relative solidity property
: if
\Gamma\curvearrowright N
is a trace preserving action of a hyperbolic group and
P,Q\subset M:=N\rtimes\Gamma
are commuting von Neumann subalgebras, then
P\prec_{M}N
or
Q
is amenable relative to
N
inside
M
(see
[
KV17
, Lemma 5.2]
). Note that the converse is false: there exist groups (e.g. biexact group that are not weakly amenable such as
\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{F}_{2}
) that satisfies the relative solidity property, but not the strong relative property.
Here, the intertwining is in the sense of Popa
[
Pop06a
]
and relative amenability is in the sense of Ozawa and Popa
[
OP10
]
.

It is an open question whether the above relative solidity property is satisfied by general biexact groups, and hence, this leads to the main technical difficulty for proving Theorem
A
.

Conjecture B
.

Let
\Gamma
be a biexact group. If
\Gamma\curvearrowright N
is a trace preserving action and
P,Q\subset M:=N\rtimes\Gamma
are commuting von Neumann subalgebras, then
P\prec_{M}N
or
Q
is amenable relative to
N
inside
M
.

This problem originates from the pioneering work of Ozawa
[
Oza04
,
Oza06
]
which states that Conjecture
B
is true whenever
N
is a tracial abelian von Neumann algebra.
Note that
[
PV14b
]
shows that Conjecture
B
is true under the additional assumption that
\Gamma
is weakly amenable.
Also,
[
Iso19
, Proposition 7.3]
shows that Conjecture
B
has a positive answer when
N
satisfies the W
∗
CMAP.

Despite the fact that Conjecture
B
is still open in its full generality, we are able to overcome this difficulty and prove Theorem
A
by showing a measure equivalence variant of Conjecture
B
holds true.
For simplicity we state here an orbit equivalence version (see also Theorem
4.1
for an equivalent measure theoretical statement).

Theorem C
.

Let
\Gamma
be a nonamenable biexact group and
\Lambda
,
\Sigma
infinite groups.
Suppose
\Lambda{\,\curvearrowright\,}(Y,\nu)
and
\Gamma\times\Sigma{\,\curvearrowright\,}(X,\mu)
are orbit equivalent free ergodic p.m.p. actions
and denote
M=L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\rtimes(\Gamma\times\Sigma)=L^{\infty}(Y,\nu)\rtimes\Lambda
.

Then for any subgroup
\Delta<\Lambda
, we have either
L^{\infty}(Y)\rtimes\Delta\prec_{M}L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\rtimes\Sigma
or

L^{\infty}(Y)\rtimes C_{\Lambda}(\Delta)
is amenable relative to
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\rtimes\Sigma
in
M
,
where
C_{\Lambda}(\Delta)
denotes the centralizer of
\Delta
in
\Lambda
.

A remarkable progress has been obtained in von Neumann algebras and orbit equivalence by the emergence of Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory
[
Pop07
]
. For large classes of II
1
factors, many remarkable structural properties have been established, such as primeness, (infinite) unique prime factorization, (infinite) product rigidity, classification of normalizers of algebras, W
∗
-superrigidity, etc. (see the surveys
[
Vae10
,
Ioa13
,
Ioa18
]
). Numerous of these findings have been achieved for von Neumann algebras that arise from groups that are biexact and weakly amenable by relying on Popa and Vaes’ work
[
PV14b
]
on the relative (strong) solidity property, see for example
[
BV14
,
CKI15
,
CdSS16
,
KV17
,
DHI19
,
CI18
,
CU20
,
Iso19
,
Iso20
,
Dri20
,
CDK23
,
CDHK24
,
CDAD23a
,
CDAD23b
,
DP23
,
CIOS23
,
CFQT24
,
DV25
]
. Many of these works do not depend on the relative strong solidity property of the groups, but only on the relative solidity property. Hence, in light of Conjecture
B
, a natural question is to understand to what extent is weak amenability essential in these results.
In this paper, we make progress on this question by applying Theorem
C
to generalize several rigidity results in the literature as outlined below.

Our next main result, Theorem
D
, concerns orbit equivalence rigidity results for product actions. To put everything into context, note that
[
CdSS16
]
shows that the group von Neumann algebra of a product of nonamenable i.c.c. weakly amenable and biexact groups remembers the product structure. The main result of
[
Dri20
]
states that a similar phenomenon holds in the equivalence relation setting by showing that the orbit equivalence relation of a product of group actions of infinite weakly amenable, biexact, property (T) groups remembers the product structure. Since the group von Neumann algebra product ridigity from
[
CdSS16
]
has been extended in
[
CDAD23a
]
to the class of nonamenable i.c.c. biexact groups, it remained open to show that the orbit equivalence product rigidity from
[
Dri20
]
holds for infinite biexact, property (T) groups which are not necessarily weakly amenable. In the next theorem we affirmatively solve this problem.

Theorem D
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
be infinite biexact, property (T) groups and let
\Gamma=\times_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma
.
For each
i=1,\dots,n
, let
\Gamma_{i}{\,\curvearrowright\,}(X_{i},\mu_{i})
be a free ergodic p.m.p. action and
\Gamma{\,\curvearrowright\,}(X,\mu)
denotes the product action
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{n}{\,\curvearrowright\,}(X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{n},\mu_{1}\times\cdots\times\mu_{n})
.

Let
\Lambda{\,\curvearrowright\,}(Y,\nu)
be a free ergodic p.m.p. action of an i.c.c. groups that is stably orbit equivalent to
\Gamma{\,\curvearrowright\,}(X,\mu)
.
Then
\Lambda{\,\curvearrowright\,}Y
is induced from a product action

\Lambda_{0}:=\Lambda_{1}\times\cdots\times\Lambda_{n}{\,\curvearrowright\,}(Y_{1}\times\cdots Y_{n},\nu_{1}\times\cdots\nu_{n})
of a subgroup
\Lambda_{0}<\Lambda
, such that

\Gamma_{i}{\,\curvearrowright\,}X_{i}
is stably orbit equivalent to
\Lambda_{i}{\,\curvearrowright\,}Y_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

By using deep results from geometric group theory
[
AMO07
,
Osi10
]
we point out the following class of biexact, property (T) groups that are not weakly amenable.

Example 1.1
.

Consider the wreath product
H=\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{F}_{n}
and notice that
H
is a finitely generated biexact group
[
BO08
, Corollary 15.3.9.]
. By
[
AMO07
, Theorem 1.1]
there is a property (T) group
G
such that
H<G
with the property that
G
is hyperbolic relative to
\{H\}
. Since
H
is biexact,
[
Oya23a
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that
G
is biexact as well. Note however that
G
is not weakly amenable, since
H
is not weakly amenable
[
OP10
,
Oza10
]
.

We continue by improving several other results from the literature where one may cover nonamenable biexact groups that are not necessarily weakly amenable,
including
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\rtimes\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})

[
Oza09
]
. See also
[
Oya23a
,
Oya23b
]
for more recent examples. The proofs of these results uses Theorem
C
and rely on developing a classification result for commuting subalgebras in von Neumann algebras of infinite direct sums of biexact groups, see Corollary
3.4
.

•

The weak amenability assumption in
[
CU20
, Theorem A]
is removed in Theorem
6.2
,
which concerns infinite product rigidity for i.c.c. biexact, property (T) groups
(that roughly says that the direct sum structure is retained by the group von Neumann algebra of an infinite direct sum of such groups).

•

Corollary
3.5
shows unique prime factorization for infinite tensor products of biexact (in the sense of
[

]
) factors, eliminating the W*CBAP condition in
[

]
.

•

Corollary
4.3
relates the fundamental group of a product of orbit equivalence relations arising from actions of biexact groups to the product of their individual fundamental groups,
analogous to
[
Iso20

on the fundamental groups of

factors arising from weakly amenable and biexact groups.

Along with some of the above results on infinite direct sums/tensor products, another consequence of our techniques is the following measure equivalence rigidity for infinite direct sums of biexact groups, which may be seen as an infinite version of
[
Sak09
, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem E
.

Let
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be families of nonamenable discrete groups
such that
\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Gamma_{n}
is measure equivalent to
\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Lambda_{n}
.

If
\Gamma_{n}
and
\Lambda_{n}
are biexact for all
n\geq 1
,
then
\Gamma_{n}
is measure equivalent to
\Lambda_{n}
, up to a permutation of indices.

In particular, this shows that
\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
is not measure equivalent to
\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbb{F}}_{\infty}
by
[
Gab00
]
.

Another corollary of Theorem
E
is to the classification of wreath product groups up to measure equivalence. By using deformation/rigidity theory, Chifan, Popa and Sizemore provided in
[
CPS12
, Theorem 0.1]
rigidity results for wreath product groups extending results from
[
Sak09
, Theorem 7]
.
Under general assumptions on the groups
A,B,\Gamma
and
\Lambda
(e.g. all the groups are i.c.c. property (T) groups), they proved that if
A\wr\Gamma
is measure equivalent to
B\wr\Lambda
, then
A^{(\Gamma)}
is measure equivalent to
B^{(\Lambda)}
and
\Gamma
is measure equivalent to
\Lambda
. Our next result complements the previous wreath product rigidity results and shows in addition that the groups
A
and
B
are measure equivalent.

Corollary F
.

Let
A
and
B
be nonamenable biexact groups and
\Gamma
,
\Lambda
nonamenable hyperbolic groups.

If
A\wr\Gamma
is measure equivalent to
B\wr\Lambda
, then
\Gamma
is measure equivalent to
\Lambda

and
A
is measure equivalent to
B
.

To put our result into a better perspective, note that
Corollary
F
can be contrasted with Tucker-Drob and Wróbel’s measure equivalence flexibility reults for wreath product groups
[
TDW24
]
. They showed in particular that if
A
and
B
are measure equivalent groups, then
A\wr\mathbb{F}_{n}
is measure equivalent to
B\wr\mathbb{F}_{n}
for any
n\geq 2
. Finally, Ioana and Tucker-Drob proved in particular that Corollary
F
holds whenever the groups
A,B,\Gamma,\Lambda
are i.c.c. hyperbolic
[
ITD
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Terminology

In this paper we consider
tracial von Neumann algebras

(M,\tau)
, i.e. von Neumann algebras
M
equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau:M\to\mathbb{C}.
This induces a norm on
M
by the formula
\|x\|_{2}=\tau(x^{*}x)^{1/2},
for all
x\in M
. We will always assume that
M
is a
separable
von Neumann algebra, i.e. the
\|\cdot\|_{2}
-completion of
M
denoted by
L^{2}(M)
is separable as a Hilbert space.
We denote by
\mathcal{Z}(M)
the
center
of
M
and by
\mathcal{U}(M)
its
unitary group
.

All inclusions
P\subset M
of von Neumann algebras are assumed unital. We denote by
E_{P}:M\to P
the unique
\tau
-preserving
conditional expectation
from
M
onto
P
, by
e_{P}:L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(P)
the orthogonal projection onto
L^{2}(P)
and by
\langle M,e_{P}\rangle
the Jones’ basic construction of
P\subset M
. We also denote by
P^{\prime}\cap M=\{x\in M|xy=yx,\text{ for all }y\in P\}
the
relative commutant
of
P
in
M
and by
\mathcal{N}_{M}(P)=\{u\in\mathcal{U}(M)|uPu^{*}=P\}
the
normalizer
of
P
in
M
. We say that
P
is
regular
in
M
if the von Neumann algebra generated by
\mathcal{N}_{M}(P)
equals
M
.

The
amplification
of a II
1
factor
(M,\tau)
by a number
t>0
is defined to be
M^{t}=p(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\bar{\otimes}M)p
, for a projection
p\in\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\bar{\otimes}M
satisfying
(
Tr
\otimes\tau)(p)=t
. Here Tr denotes the usual trace on
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Since
M
is a II
1
factor,
M^{t}
is well defined. Note that if
M=P_{1}\bar{\otimes}P_{2}
, for some II
1
factors
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, then there exists a natural identification
M=P_{1}^{t}\bar{\otimes}P_{2}^{1/t}
, for every
t>0.

Finally, let
I
be a set. For any subset
J\subset I
we denote its complement by
\widehat{J}=I\setminus J
. If
S=\{i\},
we will simply write
\hat{i}
instead of
\widehat{\{i\}}
. Also, given
any direct sum group
G=\oplus_{i\in I}G_{i}
and any tensor product
\bar{\otimes}M_{i\in I}
, we will denote their subproduct supported on
J
by
G_{J}=\oplus_{j\in J}G_{j}
and
M_{J}=\bar{\otimes}_{j\in J}M_{j}
, respectively.

2.2.
Intertwining-by-bimodules

We next recall from
[
Pop06b
, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.3]
the powerful
intertwining-by-bimodules
technique of Sorin Popa.

Theorem 2.1

(​​
[
Pop06b
]
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra and let
P\subset pMp,Q\subset qMq
be von Neumann subalgebras. Let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}(P)
be a subgroup such that
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}=P
.

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist projections
p_{0}\in P,q_{0}\in Q
, a
*
-homomorphism
\theta:p_{0}Pp_{0}\rightarrow q_{0}Qq_{0}
and a non-zero partial isometry
v\in q_{0}Mp_{0}
satisfying
\theta(x)v=vx
, for all
x\in p_{0}Pp_{0}
.

(2)

There is no sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{U}
satisfying
\|E_{Q}(xu_{n}y)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
, for all
x,y\in M
.

If one of the equivalent conditions of Theorem
2.1
holds true, we write
P\prec_{M}Q
, and say that
a corner of
P
embeds into
Q
inside
M
.

Throughout the paper we will use the following notation.

•

If
Pp^{\prime}\prec_{M}Q
for any non-zero projection
p^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}\cap pMp
, then we write
P\prec^{s}_{M}Q
.

•

If
P\prec_{M}Qq^{\prime}
for any non-zero projection
q^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}\cap qMq
, then we write
P\prec^{s^{\prime}}_{M}Q
.

We first recall the following intertwining result which is essentially contained in
[
CDAD23a
, Section 3]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a nontrivial countable i.c.c. group and denote
M=L(\Lambda)
. Let
\Delta:M\to M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M
be the
*
-homomorphism given by
\Delta(v_{\lambda})=v_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\lambda}
, for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Let
P\subset pMp
be a regular von Neumann subalgebra and
\Sigma<\Lambda
an infinite subgroup satisfying
P\prec_{M}L(\Sigma).
If
Q\subset qMq
is a von Neumann subalgebra such that
L(\Sigma)\prec_{M}Q
, then
\Delta(P)\prec_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\,\bar{\otimes}\,Q
.

Proof.

We start the proof by considering a Bernoulli action
\Lambda\curvearrowright B
with tracial abelian base and denote
\mathcal{M}=B\rtimes\Lambda
.
We extend
\Delta
naturally to a
*
-homomorphism
\Delta:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{M}
by letting
\Delta(bv_{\lambda})=bv_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\lambda}
for all
b\in B
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
The assumption implies that
P\prec_{\mathcal{M}}^{s^{\prime}}B\rtimes\Sigma
. By using
[
Dri20
, Lemma 2.3]
it follows that
\Delta(P)\prec_{\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{M}}^{s^{\prime}}\Delta(L(\Sigma))
.
We further deduce that
\Delta(P)\prec_{\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{M}}^{s^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,L(\Sigma)
. By using
L(\Sigma)\prec_{M}Q
,
[
Dri20
, Lemma 2.4]
implies that
\Delta(P)\prec_{\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,Q
. By
[
CDAD23a
, Lemma 3.3]
it follows that
\Delta(P)\prec_{\mathcal{M}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{M}}Q\,\bar{\otimes}\,Q
. By
[
CDAD23a
, Lemma 3.4]
it follows that
\Delta(P)\prec_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\,\bar{\otimes}\,Q
.
∎

2.3.
Relative amenability

A tracial von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
is
amenable
if there is a positive linear functional
\Phi:\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M))\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\Phi_{|M}=\tau
and
\Phi
is
M
-
central
, meaning
\Phi(xT)=\Phi(Tx),
for all
x\in M
and
T\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M))
. By Connes’ celebrated work
[
Con76
]
, it follows that
M
is amenable if and only if
M
is approximately finite dimensional.

We continue by recalling the notion of relative amenability introduced by
Ozawa and Popa in
[
OP10
]
. Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra. Let
p\in M
be a projection and
P\subset pMp,Q\subset M
be von Neumann subalgebras. Following
[
OP10
, Definition 2.2]
, we say that
P
is
amenable relative to
Q
inside
M
if there exists a positive linear functional
\Phi:p\langle M,e_{Q}\rangle p\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\Phi_{|pMp}=\tau
and

3.
Biexact groups and von Neumann algebras

3.1.
The small-at-infinity boundary and boundary pieces

The notion of small-at-infinity compatification of a discrete group
\Gamma
was introduced by Ozawa
[
Oza04
,
BO08
]
.
More precisely, given a discrete group
\Gamma
, the small-at-infinity compatification
\overline{\Gamma}^{s}
is described by

C(\overline{\Gamma}^{s})=\{f\in\ell^{\infty}\Gamma\mid f-R_{t}f\in c_{0}\Gamma,\ \forall t\in\Gamma\}\subset\ell^{\infty}\Gamma,

where
R_{t}f(\cdot)=f(\cdot t)
is the right translation by
\Gamma
on
\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
.

The notion of the small-at-infinity boundary for von Neumann algebras developed in
[
DKEP23
,
DP23
]
,
is a noncommutative analogue of the above notion and we recall it now.
Let
M
be a tracial von Neumann algebra.
An
M
-boundary piece
{\mathbb{X}}
is a hereditary
{\rm C}^{*}
-subalgebra
{\mathbb{X}}\subset{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M)

such that
M\cap M({\mathbb{X}})\subset M
and
JMJ\cap M({\mathbb{X}})\subset JMJ
are weakly dense,
and
{\mathbb{X}}\neq\{0\}
,
where
M({\mathbb{X}})
denotes the multiplier algebra of
{\mathbb{X}}
.
For convenience, we will always assume
{\mathbb{X}}\neq\{0\}
.
Given an
M
-boundary piece
{\mathbb{X}}
, define
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}^{L}(M)\subset{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M)
to be the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty,2}
closure of
{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M){\mathbb{X}}
,
where
\|T\|_{\infty,2}=\sup_{a\in(M)_{1}}\|T\hat{a}\|

and
(M)_{1}=\{a\in M\mid\|a\|\leq 1\}
.
Set
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)={\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}^{L}(M)^{*}\cap{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}^{L}(M)
,
then
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)
is a
{\rm C}^{*}
-subalgebra that contains
M
and
JMJ
in its multiplier algebra
[
DKEP23
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Put
{\mathbb{K}}^{\infty,1}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)=\overline{{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)}^{{}_{\|\cdot\|_{\infty,1}}}\subset{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M)
,
where
\|T\|_{\infty,1}=\sup_{a,b\in(M)_{1}}\langle T\hat{a},\hat{b}\rangle
,
and the small-at-infinity boundary for
M
relative to
{\mathbb{X}}
is given by

{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)=\{T\in{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M)\mid[T,x]\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{X}}^{\infty,1}(M),{\rm\ for\ any\ }x\in M^{\prime}\}.

When
{\mathbb{X}}={\mathbb{K}}(L^{2}M)
, we omit
{\mathbb{X}}
in the above notations.

The following instance of boundary pieces is extensively used in the following.
Let
M
be a finite von Neumann algebra and
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a possibly infinite family of von Neumann subalgebras.
Recall from
[
DP23
, Lemma 6.12]

that the
M
-boundary piece
{\mathbb{X}}
associated with
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is the
hereditary
{\rm C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M)

generated by
\{xJyJe_{P_{i}}\mid i\in I,\ x,y\in M\}
.

These notions also accommodate generalizations to the non-tracial setting and we refer to
[
DP23
]
for details.

3.2.
Biexactness

Following Ozawa
[
Oza04
,
BO08
]
, one says a discrete group
\Gamma
is biexact if
\Gamma{\,\curvearrowright\,}\overline{\Gamma}^{s}
is topologically amenable.
The corresponding notion for von Neumann algebras was introduced in
[
DP23
]
and we recall it in the following.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra,
{\mathbb{X}}\subset{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}M)
an
M
-boundary piece and
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)
the corresponding small-at-infinity boundary for
M
.
We may equip
S:={\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)
with the
M
-topology, which is given by the family of seminorms

s^{\rho}_{\omega}(x)=\inf\{\rho(a^{*}a)^{1/2}\|y\|\omega(b^{*}b)^{1/2}\mid x=a^{*}yb,\ a,b\in M,y\in S\},

where
\rho,\omega
range over all normal positive functionals on
M
.
By an equivalent characterization
[
DP23
, Lemma 3.4]
,
we say a net
\{x_{i}\}\subset S
converging to
0
in the
M
-topology if
there exists a net of projections
p_{i}\in M
such that
p_{i}\to 1
strongly
and
\|p_{i}x_{i}p_{i}\|\to 0
.
We also have the weak
M
-topology on
S
, which is described by

{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)^{\sharp}=\{\varphi\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)^{*}\mid M\times M\ni(a,b)\mapsto\varphi(aTb)\in{\mathbb{C}}\text{\ is\ separately\ normal}\ \forall T\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)\},

i.e.,
\{x_{i}\}\subset S
converges to
0
in the weak
M
-topology if
\varphi(x_{i})\to 0
for any
\varphi\in{\mathbb{S}}_{{\mathbb{X}}}(M)^{\sharp}
.

We say
M\subset{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)
is
M
-nuclear if there exist nets of u.c.p. maps

\phi_{i}:M\to{\mathbb{M}}_{n(i)}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\psi_{i}:{\mathbb{M}}_{n(i)}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)

such that
\psi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}(x)\to x
in the
M
-topology of
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)
,
or equivalently, in the weak
M
-topology (see
[
DP23
, Section 4]
for details).

And we say
M
is biexact relative to
{\mathbb{X}}
if
M\subset{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{X}}(M)
is
M
-nuclear.
When
{\mathbb{X}}
is the
M
-boundary piece associated with
a family of von Neumann subalgebras
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
M
,
we say
M
is biexact relative to
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
and when
{\mathbb{X}}={\mathbb{K}}^{\infty,1}(M)
, we simply say
M
is biexact.

These notions coincide with the corresponding notions of groups if we consider group von Neumann algebras:
a discrete group
\Gamma
is biexact relative to a family of subgroups
\{\Lambda_{i}\}_{i\in I}
if and only if

L\Gamma
is biexact relative to
\{L\Lambda_{i}\}_{i\in I}

[
DP23
, Theorem 6.2]
.

.

Let

be a finite von Neumann algebra and

j\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
M_{k}\prec_{M}L(C_{\Lambda}(\Sigma_{j}))
.
Thus there exist two nonamenable commuting subgroups
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{j},\Theta:=C_{\Lambda}(\Sigma_{j})
of
\Lambda
such that

(4)

M_{\widehat{1}}\prec_{M}L(\Sigma)\text{ and }M_{k}\prec L(\Theta).

By passing to relative commutants in (
4
) it follows that

(5)

L(\Theta)\prec_{M}M_{1}\text{ and }L(\Sigma)\prec M_{\widehat{k}}.

We continue by showing that
L(\Sigma)\prec^{s}_{M}M_{\widehat{k}}
and
k=1
. To this end, let
z\in\mathcal{N}_{M}(L(\Sigma))^{\prime}\cap M\subset L(\Sigma\Theta)^{\prime}\cap M
be a nonzero projection. Since
L(\Theta)z
is non-amenable, then Corollary
3.4
implies that there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L(\Sigma)z\prec_{M}M_{\widehat{j}}
. We assume by contradiction that
j\neq k
. By Lemma
2.2
it follows that
\Delta(M_{\widehat{1}})\prec^{s}_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M_{\widehat{j}}
. Together with (
3
), it follows from
[
DHI19
, Lemma 2.8]
that
\Delta(M_{\widehat{1}})\prec^{s}_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M_{\widehat{\{j,k\}}}
. By applying again
[
DHI19
, Theorem 4.1]
, there exists a subgroup
\Sigma_{0}<\Lambda
such that
M_{\widehat{1}}\prec_{M}L(\Sigma_{0})
and
M_{\{j,k\}}\prec_{m}L(C_{\Lambda}(\Sigma_{0}))
. By passing to relative commutants, it follows that
L(C_{\Lambda}(\Sigma_{0}))\prec_{M}M_{1}
. By using Lemma
2.2
it follows that
\Delta(M_{\{j,k\}})\prec_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M_{1}
, and hence,
\Delta(M_{\{j,k\}})\prec^{s}_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M_{1}
since
M_{\{j,k\}}
is regular in
M
. By applying the flip automorphism on
M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M
, it follows that
\Delta(M_{\{j,k\}})\prec^{s}_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M_{1}\,\bar{\otimes}\,M
as well. By applying
[
DHI19
, Lemma 2.8]
it follows that
\Delta(M_{\{j,k\}})\prec^{s}_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M_{1}\,\bar{\otimes}\,M_{1}
. By using Corollary
3.4
it is not hard to get that
\Delta(M_{j})\prec_{M\,\bar{\otimes}\,M}M\otimes 1
, which contradicts the fact that
M_{j}
is a II
1
factor. Thus,
L(\Sigma)z\prec_{M}M_{\widehat{k}}
, which implies by
[
DHI19
, Lemma 2.4]
that
L(\Sigma)\prec_{M}^{s}M_{\widehat{k}}
.
Together with (
5
) it follows from
[
Vae07
, Lemma 3.7]
that
M_{\widehat{1}}\prec_{M}M_{\widehat{k}}
. This implies that
k=1
.

To summarize, we proved that
M_{\widehat{1}}\prec_{M}L(\Sigma)
,
L(\Sigma)\prec_{M}^{s}M_{\widehat{1}}
,
M_{1}\prec L(\Theta)
and
L(\Theta)\prec_{M}M_{1}
. The conclusion of the theorem follows now by applying
[
Dri21
, Theorem 2.3]
(see also
[
DHI19
, Theorem 6.1]
).
∎

Transcriptomic characteristics of plasma from Chinese ethnic minority patients with major depressive disorder
Authors: Jun Ye, Jiang Huang, Chongwei Zhou, Zhijin Yuan, Haiping Guan, Zhiyou Long, Haini Chen, Wenfeng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ethnic and regional disparities have been observed in the diagnosis and treatment of psychiatric disorders. Our previous research revealed metabolic heterogeneity in plasma samples from ethnic minority patients with schizophrenia in Qiandongnan, China. In this study, individuals of Han, Miao, and Dong ethnicities with major depressive disorder (MDD) from Qiandongnan, China, were enrolled. Peripheral blood samples were collected from both the MDD groups of these ethnicities and their corresponding healthy control (HC) groups for transcriptomic analysis. Ethnicity-specific genes and signaling pathways were identified. Specifically, the genes B3GNTL1, DNASE2, and OAS3 may distinguish individuals of Han ethnicity. The gene ELANE may distinguish individuals of Miao ethnicity. The gene SETSIP may distinguish individuals of Dong ethnicity. Transcriptomic heterogeneity in peripheral blood was observed among Han, Miao, and Dong ethnic groups with MDD from Qiandongnan, China. Furthermore trans-ethnically consistent genes and pathways may uncover the core mechanisms of depression. These findings may provide novel insights into diagnosis and treatment strategies for depression.

Introduction

MDD is a prevalent mental illness and a leading cause of disability worldwide. Its pathogenesis is complex, involving genetic, environmental, and psychological factors [1]. Notably, racial and ethnic factors also contribute to variations in the diagnosis and treatment of psychiatric disorders such as depression [2, 3]. To date, global genetic studies of psychiatric disorders have made significant advances in elucidating the biological basis of these diseases. However, there is a severe geographic and ancestral imbalance in sample representation, with a predominant focus on individuals of European ancestry [4, 5]. Polygenic risk scores (PRS) developed using European populations show substantially reduced predictive performance in other ancestral groups, such as East Asian, African, and Latin American populations. Many genetic loci identified in European cohorts may be rare or absent in other populations, and vice versa [6, 7]. This pronounced imbalance limits the generalizability, equity, and scientific rigor of research findings, hinders the understanding of genetic factors underlying psychiatric disorders across different populations, and threatens equitable access to precision psychiatry. A growing number of studies are now systematically comparing genetic architectures across populations—including European, East Asian, and African-ancestry groups—to uncover shared and population-specific risk mechanisms [8–10]. These efforts highlight the critical role of genetic ancestral diversity, rather than simply increasing sample size, in precisely dissecting disease mechanisms. Nevertheless, research specifically addressing ancestral factors in psychiatric disorders remains relatively limited in China.

This study leverages geographic advantages by being situated in the Qiandongnan region of China, an area with a high concentration of ethnic minorities. According to the 2017 Qiandongnan Prefecture Government’s Statistical Bulletin of National Economic and Social Development, among 47 ethnic groups, the Miao ethnic group accounts for 43% and the Dong ethnic group for 30.4%, collectively representing more than 70% of the total regional population. Due to geographic isolation and economic underdevelopment, local ethnic minorities remain relatively secluded, preserving unique cultural practices and lifestyles. Genetic purity among these populations is relatively well-maintained and does not necessarily align strictly with gene frequency distributions observed among the Han Chinese or broader Asian populations. Our team’s preliminary research has revealed heterogeneity in the metabolic levels of plasma from patients with first-onset schizophrenia among different ethnic groups in Qiandongnan region of China [11]. Whether similar differences exist among ethnic minority patients with depression in Qiandongnan remains to be confirmed.

Transcriptomic characteristics constitute critical biomarkers for elucidating the molecular mechanisms and pathways associated with MDD and its subtypes, including immune-metabolic depression [12, 13]. It has furthermore been postulated that the regulated control of mRNA processing and transport, which is necessary for maintaining transcriptomic stability, is essential for mitigating MDD [14]. Thus, this study uses individuals from diverse ethnic backgrounds residing within the same region as an entry point, utilizing RNA-Seq technology to compare mRNA expression patterns between patients with MDD and their respective control groups across different ethnicities. This approach promises novel insights into the etiology of human depression and facilitates the preliminary identification of potential biomarkers associated with MDD.

Materials and methods

The inclusion criteria were first-episode MDD diagnosed according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5, 2013), and Hamilton Depression Rating Scale-24 (HAMD-24) scores greater than 17. Exclusion criteria included severe physical illnesses, organic brain disorders, epilepsy, alcohol or drug abuse, and pregnancy or lactation. Participants were matched by sex and age using clinical records. Following an 8–12 h fast and the avoidance of RNA-affecting factors (e.g., alcohol), venous blood was collected from the disinfected antecubital vein via vacuum phlebotomy. Blood was slowly transferred into a BD PAXgene® RNA tube up to 2.5 mL, gently inverted 10 times for mixing, kept at room temperature for 2 h, and then stored at–80 °C to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. peripheral blood samples from the MDD groups and their corresponding healthy control groups across the three ethnicities were collected according to clinical blood collection standards for transcriptomic analysis. Patients were divided into Han-MDD (n = 7), Miao-MDD (n = 10), and Dong-MDD (n = 10) groups. Healthy controls matched by gender and age were also recruited (Han-HC: n = 5; Miao-HC: n = 5; Dong-HC: n = 5). Ethical approval for this project was granted by The Second Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University (No. gzwkj2022-070), with informed consent obtained from all subjects and/or their families.

Transcriptome sequencing

Transcriptome sequencing was performed on peripheral blood samples obtained from six distinct groups: Han-HC, Han-MDD, Miao-HC, Miao-MDD, Dong-HC, and Dong-MDD. Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent, and its quality was verified prior to library construction. RNA-seq libraries were prepared using the NEBNext® Ultra™ RNA Library Prep Kit (E7530L, NEB, Ipswich, MA, USA). The resulting libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform using a 2 × 150 bp paired-end configuration, generating an average of 40 million reads per sample. Quality control of the raw sequencing data was performed using fastp (v0.20.0). The clean reads were then aligned to the reference genome using HISAT2 (v2.2.1) to obtain genomic coordinates for each fragment. The following parameters were specified for HISAT2: --met-stderr --new-summary --dta --threads 10 --no-mixed --no-discordant; all other parameters were set to default. The alignment files were subsequently sorted and converted to BAM format using SAMtools (v1.15.1). For the reference genome (FASTA file) and annotation file (GTF/GFF format), priority was given to files from widely recognized databases such as Ensembl, NCBI, or GENCODE. Gene expression quantification was carried out using featureCounts (v2.0.1) to generate read counts and StringTie (v2.1.6) to obtain FPKM values. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was applied to visualize overall data structure. Given the small sample size, this study is exploratory in nature. Therefore, uncorrected p-values were employ in the transcriptomic analysis [15–17]. Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 (version 1.32.0) on data with biological replicates. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using a threshold set at |log₂FoldChange | > 1 and p-value < 0.05, corresponding to a fold change greater than 2 and p < 0.05.

Functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analyses of DEGs were performed using the clusterProfiler package (version 4.0.0) in R (version 4.0.1, www.r-project.org). Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis were conducted. Significantly enriched genes were identified using an adjusted p-value (padj) threshold of <0.05.

Biomarker screening method

The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) multinomial regression method from the R package glmnet was utilized to build models, taking group labels as dependent variables and gene expression levels as independent variables. The regularization parameter λ was optimized using 10-fold cross-validation, selecting candidate biomarkers with non-zero coefficients. Expression differences of selected biomarkers across groups were further assessed using ANOVA for normally distributed data or the Kruskal-Wallis test for non-normally distributed data. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to evaluate the classification performance of selected biomarkers. ROC curves and AUC values were computed using the R package pROC for group-specific biomarkers, utilizing an optimal cutoff of AUC > 0.7. Biomarkers satisfying these criteria were retained for subsequent analysis. Ultimately, biomarkers significantly differentially expressed in the target group but not in other groups (P < 0.05) among all biomarkers with AUC > 0.7 were selected.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Additional Miao and Dong MDD patients meeting the diagnostic criteria were recruited from the Department of Psychiatry at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University, along with corresponding HCs. Participants were divided into Miao-MDD (n = 7), Miao-HC (n = 5), Dong-MDD (n = 3), and Dong-HC (n = 5) groups. Expression of CLEC9A, ELANE, and SETSIP in peripheral blood samples was assessed. Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent following standard protocols, and RNA quality was confirmed using a micro-volume UV-Vis spectrophotometer. RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the Goldenstar™ RT6 cDNA Synthesis Kit Ver.2 (TSK302M, Tsingke, Beijing, China). RT-qPCR was performed using 2 ×T5 Fast qPCR Mix (SYBR Green I) (TSE202, Tsingke, Beijing, China), with GAPDH serving as the internal control. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.1software, with figures generated using Adobe Illustrator 2021. Data were presented as means ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons among groups were performed using ordinary one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). All experiments were conducted at least three times independently, and differences were considered statistically significant when the P-value was <0.05.

Results

Clinical heterogeneity among MDD patients of different ethnicities

Analysis of demographic data revealed that MDD predominantly affected adolescent females (Table 1), Clinical data indicated significant differences in blood parameters between Han and Dong ethnic groups with first-episode MDD in Qiandongnan, China, specifically in hemoglobin (HGB) and hematocrit (HCT) levels. Four blood parameters differed significantly between Han and Miao ethnic groups with first-episode MDD, including total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), hemoglobin (HGB), and hematocrit (HCT). However, only albumin (ALB) showed significant differences between the Miao and Dong ethnic groups. These findings highlight ethnic variations in clinical biomarkers among first-episode MDD patients (Table 2).

Differential expression profiles between MDD patients and HCs across ethnicities

Transcriptomic sequencing of peripheral blood samples from Han, Miao, and Dong ethnic groups revealed significant differences in the number of DEGs between MDD patients and their respective HCs. Specifically, 18,332 DEGs were identified in Han-MDD patients compared to Han-HC, including 3170 upregulated and 15,162 downregulated genes (Fig. 1A). In Miao-MDD patients, 7577 DEGs were identified, comprising 2367 upregulated and 5210 downregulated genes (Fig. 1B). Meanwhile, Dong-MDD patients exhibited 13,639 DEGs, with 1908 upregulated and 11,731 downregulated genes (Fig. 1C). GO analysis of DEGs between Miao-MDD patients and Miao-HC showed significant enrichment (967 significantly enriched items) in functions related to the Endomembrane system, Vesicle formation, and the Intracellular vesicle (Fig. 2C). Together with the repeated involvement of development-related GO terms, these data indicate that immune-regulatory and neuroplasticity-related processes may represent relatively stable biological components of MDD rather than purely ethnicity-dependent findings [18].

Analysis of ethnicity-specific signaling pathways associated with MDD

To further explore the relationship between MDD and ethnicity, ethnicity-specific signaling pathways potentially involved in the pathogenesis of MDD were investigated to reveal possible mechanistic heterogeneity among different ethnic groups. KEGG pathway analysis of DEGs identified in MDD patients across the Han, Miao, and Dong ethnic groups revealed 26 shared pathways (Fig. 3A). These common pathways were primarily involved in Axon guidance, Cortisol synthesis and secretion, and Phospholipase D signaling (Supplementary figures 1A–C). Ethnicity-specific analyses identified 53 pathways unique to the Han group, 23 pathways unique to the Miao group, and 5 pathways unique to the Dong group (Fig. 3A). Among the 37 signaling pathways uniquely associated with Han-MDD patients (Fig. 3B), five neurotransmitter-related pathways, including Glutamatergic synapse, GABAergic synapse, Dopaminergic synapse, Serotonergic synapse, and Cholinergic synapse, were associated with neurotransmitter receptor expression in peripheral immune cells and gut-brain axis signaling. Additionally, two pathways related to immune cell migration and inflammatory response—Chemokine signaling pathway and Complement and coagulation cascades—were identified. Several other unique pathways were linked to hormonal regulation (4 pathways), stress response (2 pathways), and metabolic regulation (2 pathways).In the Miao-MDD group, 14 unique pathways (Fig. 3C) were identified, including the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, NF-κB signaling pathway, and Neutrophil extracellular trap formation, all associated with inflammatory activation and cytokine expression and activation, suggesting a prominent association between peripheral immune cell-mediated inflammation and MDD in this ethnic group.Distinct from the Han and Miao ethnic groups, pathways specific to the Dong-MDD patients were primarily related to metabolism (Fig. 3D), particularly Pantothenate and CoA biosynthesis, Caffeine Metabolism, and Bile Secretion. The identification of 26 shared KEGG pathways further supports cross-ethnic convergence in this cohort. Overall, mRNA expression profiles in Han-MDD patients were strongly associated with neurotransmitter signaling pathways, whereas the profiles in Miao-MDD patients primarily involved inflammation-centered pathways regulating inflammatory activation and cytokine secretion. In contrast, Dong-MDD patients showed potential associations with neurotransmitter synthesis and secretion. Functional and pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs in MDD patients across ethnicities

Multi-level enrichment analyses revealed common characteristics and ethnicity-specific regulatory networks of DEGs across ethnic groups. GO analysis (812 significantly enriched items) indicated that DEGs shared by Han and Miao ethnic groups were significantly enriched in Epithelium development, Supramolecular polymer, and Tissue development (Fig. 4A). KEGG analysis further highlighted ethnic-specific enrichment (1 significantly enriched items) in metabolic and immune regulatory pathways. Compared with Miao-MDD patients, Han-MDD patients exhibited notably higher enrichment in pathways such as Endocrine and other factor-regulated calcium reabsorption, ECM-receptor interactions, and Protein digestion and absorption (Fig. 4B). The DEGs in Han and Dong ethnic groups were primarily enriched (51 significantly enriched items) in functions related to the Extracellular region, Extracellular space, and Tissue development (Fig. 4C). Functional and signaling pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs in HC across ethnic groups

To elucidate the functional similarities and differences of disease-related genes among HC from different ethnicities, multi-level enrichment analysis was conducted to reveal both shared features and ethnicity-specific regulatory networks of DEGs. GO analysis showed that DEGs between Han and Miao HCs were differentially enriched (812 significantly enriched items) in fundamental biological processes such as System development, Anatomical structure morphogenesis, and System process (Supplementary figure 2A). However, KEGG pathway analysis revealed significant ethnic-specific regulatory differences. Compared with the Miao group, DEGs in Han-HCs were more enriched (49 significantly enriched items) in neuroendocrine pathways, including Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, Glutamatergic synapse, and Taste transduction (Supplementary figure 2B).

GO enrichment analysis also indicated that, compared with Dong-HCs, DEGs in Han-HCs were significantly enriched (123 significantly enriched items) in terms related to plasma membrane structure and function, such as Intrinsic components of the plasma membrane, Integral components of the plasma membrane, and Plasma membrane regions (Supplementary figure 2C). Further KEGG analysis demonstrated that Han-HCs exhibited more complex multi-system dysregulation. At the neuroendocrine level, DEGs in the Han group were specifically enriched (4 significantly enriched items) in pathways such as Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction and Steroid hormone biosynthesis (Supplementary figure 2D).

Notable differences in functional and pathway enrichment were observed between Miao and Dong HCs. DEGs in Miao-HCs were significantly enriched (234 significantly enriched items) in pathways associated with Cell projection and Plasma membrane bounded cell projection (Supplementary figure 2E). Additionally, DEGs in the Miao group were closely associated with neurotransmitter-related pathways (29 significantly enriched items) such as Serotonergic synapse, Glutamatergic synapse, and GABAergic synapse, as well as inflammation- and immune-related pathways, including Inflammatory mediator regulation of TRP channels and ECM-receptor interaction (Supplementary figure 2F).

In summary, DEGs and their associated pathways differ among Han, Miao, and Dong HCs, even in the absence of disease. These differences may become dysregulated under pathological conditions, potentially contributing to disease onset or progression. Therefore, this analysis provides valuable insight into identifying potential disease-related genes and pathways, offering a reference for future research and therapeutic strategies.

Biomarker identification between MDD and HC across ethnic groups

According to the biomarker selection procedure described in “Section 4. Through integrated analysis of intra-ethnic (MDD vs. HC) and inter-ethnic (pairwise comparisons between MDD groups) DEGs (Fig. 5A), five key genes were prioritized: SETSIP, B3GNTL1, DNASE2, OAS3, and ELANE. Among them, B3GNTL1, DNASE2, and OAS3 effectively distinguished between Han-HC and Han-MDD, with AUCs and p-values as follows: B3GNTL1 (AUC = 0.8, P = 0.0025), DNASE2 (AUC = 0.8, P = 0.0054), and OAS3 (AUC = 0.76, P = 0.0054), suggesting strong discriminatory power (Fig. 5B–G). SETSIP (AUC = 0.84, P = 0.04) was identified as a potential biomarker distinguishing Dong-HC from Dong-MDD patients (Fig. 6A, B), while ELANE (AUC = 0.8, P = 0.04) was considered a potential biomarker for distinguishing Miao-HC from Miao-MDD patients (Fig. 6C, D). Therefore, although the downstream screening strategy emphasized ethnicity-informative biomarkers, the pathway-level results indicate that cross-ethnic consistency may be stronger at the level of biological programs than at the level of single genes. Discussion

Globally, MDD affects more than 300 million individuals [21], with adolescents representing the primary affected population. Studies have shown that the onset and progression of adolescent MDD are influenced by various factors, among which ethnicity and regional background play particularly important roles [22].

We first observed significant differences in biochemical parameters and blood cell profiles among MDD patients from the three ethnic groups. Transcriptomic data further revealed distinct differences in the number of DEGs associated with MDD among the Han, Miao, and Dong populations (Fig. 1). Although GO and KEGG pathway analyses revealed some similarities across the three ethnic groups (Figure S1), each exhibited distinct functional enrichment profiles and signaling pathway involvements (Fig. 2), it reminds us that we should put emphasis not only to the ethnic-specific genes, but also to the genes and pathways that are consistent across the different ethnicities.

To further investigate ethnicity-specific signaling pathways associated with MDD in Han, Miao, and Dong populations, KEGG pathway analysis revealed that among the 36 pathways uniquely enriched in Han-MDD patients (Fig. 3B), five were directly related to neurotransmitter signaling: Glutamatergic synapse, GABAergic synapse, Dopaminergic synapse, Serotonergic synapse, and Cholinergic synapse. These pathways are implicated in neurotransmitter receptor expression on peripheral immune cells and neurotransmitter-mediated gut-brain axis communication. Alterations in these pathways may lead to changes in central nervous system neuronal signaling, immune cell dysfunction, neuroinflammation, and progression of depressive disorders [23–27]. Additionally, two pathways—Chemokine signaling and Complement and coagulation cascades—were associated with immune cell migration and inflammatory responses [28, 29], suggesting a pro-inflammatory phenotype of peripheral immune cells, consistent with the chronic low-grade inflammation commonly observed in MDD.Among the 15 pathways uniquely enriched in Miao-MDD patients (Fig. 3C), three—NOD-like receptor signaling, NF-κB signaling and Neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation—were strongly associated with inflammatory activation and the expression and activation of immune cytokines. Notably, the NF-κB pathway is a classical central regulatory pathway for inflammation and plays a key molecular role in glial cell activation and the development of depressive-like behaviors [30–32]. Other inflammation-associated pathways unique to Miao-MDD patients included Antigen processing and presentation [33], Necroptosis [34], Ferroptosis [35], and Lysosome pathways [36], collectively indicating a substantial association between peripheral immune cell inflammation and MDD in this ethnic group.In contrast to Han and Miao groups, the pathways uniquely enriched in Dong-MDD patients were primarily metabolic (Fig. 3D), particularly those related to Pantothenate and CoA biosynthesis, Caffeine metabolism, and Bile secretion. The roles of pantothenate and caffeine metabolism in MDD are mainly associated with neurotransmitter synthesis and release. Pantothenate promotes acetylcholine synthesis [37], while caffeine enhances dopamine release through the modulation of adenosine A2A receptors [38]. Dysregulation of bile acid metabolism has also been linked to depression, potentially by increasing blood-brain barrier permeability or modulating gut hormone secretion (e.g., GLP-1), thereby indirectly affecting brain function [39].

In summary, the identified pathways, which are pertinent to neurotransmitter signaling, inflammatory activation, cytokine secretion, and neurotransmitter synthesis/release, all constitute core components of the gut-brain axis. This indicates that dysfunction of this axis may represent a crucial molecular mechanism underlying the ethnic variations in MDD prevalence and presentation among the Han, Miao, and Dong populations.

To further elucidate the functional differences in disease-associated genes among the three ethnic groups with MDD, KEGG pathway analysis revealed ethnic-specific distinctions in metabolic and immune regulatory pathways. In particular, DEGs in Han MDD patients showed higher enrichment in pathways such as Endocrine and other factor-regulated calcium reabsorption, ECM-receptor interaction, and Protein digestion and absorption (Fig. 4B). These pathways may contribute to the regulation of inflammation through calcium signaling [40], immune cell migration, and tissue homeostasis [41]. Additionally, the significant enrichment of bile secretion pathways suggests that Han-MDD may influence gut microbiota via the gut-brain axis, thereby modulating bile acid secretion [42].Compared with Dong-MDD patients, DEGs in Han-MDD patients were predominantly enriched in inflammation-related pathways including the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and Staphylococcus aureus infection(Fig. 4D), all of which have well-established links to gut-brain axis modulation [43–45].

While comparisons between Han and other groups revealed distinct pathways, an analysis of Miao-MDD versus Dong-MDD patients showed that DEGs in the Miao group were significantly enriched in metabolic pathways, including Arginine biosynthesis (Fig. 4F).These pathways may exacerbate depressive symptoms by regulating nitric oxide (NO) levels [46].Furthermore, the enrichment of DEGs in the Hippo signaling pathway and Focal adhesion pathway suggests a role in MDD-related inflammatory regulation by modulating immune cell proliferation and migration [47]. Enrichment in the Axon guidance pathway also indicates a potential indirect mechanism by which synapse formation and localization influence neurotransmitter release and function, ultimately affecting the onset and progression of MDD [48]. Collectively, these findings underscore the molecular heterogeneity of metabolic regulation between the Miao and Dong populations. This heterogeneity may implicate divergent biological mechanisms underlying their respective depressive pathologies.

Similarly, disease-associated genes in HCs from different ethnic groups also exhibited distinct expression profiles. Compared with Miao-HC, Han-HC showed significant enrichment of DEGs in neurotransmitter transmission systems, including GABAergic, serotonergic, and Glutamatergic synapses (Supplementary figure 2B). While certain pathogenic pathways were conserved across ethnic groups, the Han population exhibited distinct, ethnic-specific abnormalities—including altered membrane structure and function, metabolic network remodeling, and immune-inflammatory activation. These findings indicate that even among healthy individuals from different ethnicities, distinct gene expression profiles and pathway enrichments exist, which may become dysregulated under disease conditions and contribute to the onset or progression of psychiatric disorders. Together, the evidence presented here argues that mental disorders may present with significant ethnic and racial heterogeneity.

In summary, The differential pathways identified in this study (e.g., MAPK signaling pathway, Glutamatergic signaling pathway) are primarily involved in immune regulation and neurotransmitter signaling. These functions are precisely known key components in the bidirectional communication of the gut-brain axis [49, 50]. Therefore, our findings suggest that the molecular changes observed in the periphery of depression patients may interact with gut-brain axis function through these shared immune and neurochemical mechanisms, thereby influencing the state of the central nervous system. We recognize, of course, that these pathways are fundamental (pleiotropic) and widely involved in the regulation of multiple systems throughout the body, and are not unique to the gut-brain axis. The alterations observed in these pathways in the context of depression in this study, combined with established gut-brain axis theory, provide a novel and integrative perspective for understanding the systemic pathophysiology of depression.

We identified five ethnicity-associated candidate biomarker genes: B3GNTL1, DNASE2, OAS3, ELANE, and SETSIP. B3GNTL1 is involved in protein glycosylation [51], a process that plays a key role in regulating synaptic plasticity and the activation and migration of immune cells such as T cells and microglia [52, 53]. DNASE2 is closely associated with autoinflammatory states; its dysfunction leads to enhanced type I interferon signaling [54]. OAS3 is an interferon-induced antiviral protein that is highly expressed in primary HIV-associated neurocognitive disorders and is linked to inflammation [55], a critical factor in MDD pathogenesis with strong connections to the gut-brain axis [56]. ELANE contributes to inflammation by regulating the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) [57]. SETSIP, on the other hand, directly binds to the promoter region of vascular endothelial cadherin (VE-cadherin), enhancing its expression in endothelial cells [58]. Reduced VE-cadherin expression increases blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability, which may exacerbate MDD symptoms [59, 60]. In addition to acting as a physical barrier, the BBB plays a key role in mediating signaling between gut microbiota and the central nervous system, serving as a bridge in gut-brain crosstalk [61].

Evaluation of the diagnostic performance of these genes as biomarkers revealed that CLEC9A in the Miao group (AUC = 0.857) (Fig. 7C) is a C-type lectin receptor expressed on the surface of dendritic cell subsets [62]. Its ethnicity-specific expression pattern may be linked to its role in modulating antigen presentation efficiency, which in turn affects T cell metabolic reprogramming, such as levels of fatty acid oxidation [62]. These mechanisms may reshape neuroimmune homeostasis, providing a theoretical basis for the observed high expression of CLEC9A in Miao-MDD patients. The expression heterogeneity of CLEC9A across ethnicities suggests that CLEC9A-mediated pathways may represent a characteristic molecular target in Miao individuals with depression. In the Dong group, downregulation of SETSIP (AUC = 0.933) (Fig. 7C) may result in the accumulation of proinflammatory mediators such as prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), potentially exacerbating neuroinflammation via the gut-brain axis. Ethnicity-specific low expression of SETSIP may impair serotonergic synapse homeostasis in Dong-MDD patients, leading to presynaptic neurotransmitter release abnormalities and receptor desensitization.

Although certain genes (e.g., CLEC9A) demonstrate relatively high diagnostic performance (AUC > 0.85), indicating potential clinical utility, the reliability of these genes as standalone biomarkers requires validation in independent cohorts. In addition, the mechanistic role of the gut–brain axis in ethnic differences remains unclear; future studies should incorporate gut microbiota analysis to elucidate the interplay between peripheral immunity and the central nervous system. Of course, this study also has several limitations:

Sample size and representativeness

The investigation focused on ethnic minority populations in southeastern Guizhou, China, with a limited number of participants. Consequently, this is an initial exploratory study that only reveals correlational differences at the transcriptomic level and lacks causal inference power. Cultural and lifestyle factors in this region may differ from those of other ethnic minority groups elsewhere, thereby restricting the generalizability of the findings.

Sample source and selection bias

The clinical samples used for transcriptome profiling and PCR validation were derived from a single source and may differ from the general community population. Some samples may be subject to selection bias—for example, possibly including only patients with more severe depression—thus failing to fully represent the clinical characteristics of all individuals with MDD.

Complexity of ethnic difference research

Although the study aimed to explore MDD differences across ethnic groups, such differences are often shaped by multiple intertwined factors, including genetic background, cultural traditions, and living environment. Disentangling the specific contribution of each factor to depressive phenotypes remains challenging.

Interpretation constraints of transcriptomic data

While transcriptomic signatures can provide important clues regarding gene expression differences, transcriptomic data alone may be insufficient to comprehensively explain the pathological mechanisms underlying MDD. The subjects of this study are patients with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD), where gene transcriptional changes in psychiatric disorders are generally weaker and have smaller effect sizes. To avoid overlooking potential biological signals, we appropriately lowered the threshold for screening differentially expressed genes. However, it must be emphasized that any conclusions drawn from such preliminary findings require further rigorous validation.

In a word, it is essential to acknowledge that a core limitation of this study is the small sample size for both transcriptomic profiling and validation. The reported AUC values for candidate genes are exploratory in nature and do not constitute validated conclusions. Readers should take into account the potential impact of sample size on result stability when interpreting these findings, and further verification in large-scale cohorts is warranted.

Conclusion

Peripheral blood transcriptomic profiles reveal substantial heterogeneity among Han, Miao, and Dong patients with MDD in Qiandongnan, China. We have preliminarily identified biomarkers potentially associated with major depressive disorder. Among these, B3GNTL1, DNASE2, and OAS3 may be specifically linked to the Han Chinese population, while ELANE could serve as a potential biomarker for distinguishing the Miao ethnic group, and SETSIP for the Dong ethnic group. Moreover, we will emphasize not only the importance of ethnicity-specific genes but also the genes and pathways shared across different ethnicities, as they may represent the most closely associated clues to depression rather than secondary findings.

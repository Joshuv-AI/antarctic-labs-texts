2
Restricted sumsets: The case
|A|=|B|=k

For any subset
X\subseteq F=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
we denote by
\sigma_{i}(X)
with
1\leq i\leq|X|
to the
i
th symmetrical polynomial of
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{|X|}\}
and
\sigma_{0}(X)=1
. We use the same strategy proposed by Alon et. al. to prove the following.

Theorem 7
.

Let
A
and
B
be two subsets of
F=\mathbb{Z}/{p\mathbb{Z}}
with
|A|=|B|=k
such that
|A\dot{+}B|=2k-2
. Then
A=B
.

Proof.

Let
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
,
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}
and
C=A\dot{+}B=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{2k-2}\}
and let

f(x,y)=(x-y)\prod_{j=1}^{2k-2}(x+y-c_{j})\in F[x,y]

and

g_{A}(z)=\prod_{z=1}^{k}(z-a_{i}),\,g_{B}(z)=\prod_{z=1}^{k}(z-b_{i})\in F[z].

We can write

f(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{2k-1}f_{2k-2-i}(x,y)

as the sum of its homogeneous components. We prove by induction on
i
that the coefficients of
g_{A}(z)
and
g_{B}(z)
are equal, and consequently the roots of both polynomials must be the same.

For every
x\in A
and
y\in B
we have that
f(x,y)=0
. Hence, by Lemma
1
, there exist polynomials
h_{A}(x,y)
and
h_{B}(x,y)
such that

f(x,y)=h_{A}(x,y)g_{A}(x)+h_{B}(x,y)g_{B}(y),

(1)

with
\deg(h_{A}(x,y))\leq k-1
and
\deg(h_{B}(x,y))\leq k-1
. Decomposing
h_{A}
and
h_{B}
into its homogeneous components we can write

h_{A}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{A,i}(x,y),\,\text{where}\,h_{A,i}(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}A_{ij}x^{j}y^{i-j}

and

h_{B}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h_{B,i}(x,y),\,\text{where}\,h_{B,i}(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}B_{ij}x^{i-j}y^{j}.

We can also decompose
f(x,y)
into its homogeneous components, we can rewrite
f(x,y)
as

f(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{2k-2}(-1)^{i}\sigma_{i}(C)p_{2k-1-i}(x,y),

(2)

here

p_{i}(x,y)=(x-y)(x+y)^{i-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}C_{ij}x^{j}y^{i-j}

where
C_{ii}=1
,
C_{i0}=-1
and otherwise

C_{ij}={\binom{i-1}{j-1}}-{\binom{i-1}{j}}=\frac{2j-i}{j}{\binom{i-1}{j-1}}=\frac{2j-i}{j}{\binom{i-1}{i-j}},

in particular we note that
C_{ij}=-C_{i,(i-j)}
.

Taking the homogeneous component of
f(x,y)
of degree
2k-1
and by equations
1
and
2
we have that

\displaystyle f_{2k-1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=p_{2k-1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2k-1}C_{2k-1,j}x^{j}y^{2k-1-j}

\displaystyle=h_{A,k-1}(x,y)x^{k}+h_{B,k-1}(x,y)y^{k}

by taking the coefficients of the monomial
x^{k+i}y^{k-i-1}
and
x^{k-i-1}y^{k+i}
with
0\leq i\leq k-1
we conclude that

A_{k-1,i}=C_{2k-1,k+i},

and

B_{k-1,i}=-A_{k-1,i}=C_{2k-1,k-i-1}.

(3)

these coefficients are not zero by our assumption
p>2k-2
.

We look at the homogeneous component of
f(x,y)
of degree
2k-2
. That is

\displaystyle f_{2k-2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=h_{A,k-2}(x,y)x^{k}-\sigma_{1}(A)h_{A,k-1}(x,y)x^{k-1}

\displaystyle+h_{B,k-2}(x,y)y^{k}-\sigma_{1}(B)h_{B,k-1}(x,y)y^{k-1}.

First we look at the coefficient of the monomial
x^{k-1}y^{k-1}
. That is

C_{2k-2,k-1}=0=-\sigma_{1}(A)A_{k-1,0}-\sigma_{1}(B)B_{k-1,0}.

By Equation
3
we conclude that
\sigma_{1}(A)=\sigma_{1}(B)
.

Next by inspecting the coefficients of the monomials
x^{2k-2-j}y^{j}
and
x^{j}y^{2k-2-j}
with
1\leq j\leq k-2
we conclude that

C_{2k-2,2k-2-j}=A_{k-2,k-2-j}-\sigma_{1}(A)A_{k-1,k-1-j}

and

C_{2k-2,j}=B_{k-2,k-2-j}-\sigma_{1}(B)B_{k-1,k-1-j}.

Since
C_{2k-2,2k-2-j}=-C_{2k-2,j}
and
\sigma_{1}(A)A_{k-1,k-1-j}=-\sigma_{1}(B)B_{k-1,k-1-j}
then
A_{k-2,k-2-j}=-B_{k-2,k-2-j}
.

Now, we assume that for every
r<i
with
0\leq i\leq k

\sigma_{r}(A)=\sigma_{r}(B)
and
A_{k-1-r,j}=-B_{k-1-r,j}
. By taking the homogeneous component of
f
of degree
2k-1-i

\displaystyle f_{2k-1-i}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}(A)h_{A,k-1-i+j}(x,y)x^{k-j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}(B)h_{B,k-1-i+j}(x,y)y^{k-j}.

If
i
is even
i=2r
. In this case, we look at the coefficients of the monomials
x^{k-r-1}y^{k-r}
and
x^{k-r}y^{k-r-1}
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{2r}(C)C_{2k-2r-1,k-r-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r+j}\sigma_{r+j}(B)B_{k-r-1+j,j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{r+1+j}\sigma_{r+1+j}(A)A_{k-r+j,j}

\displaystyle\sigma_{2r}(C)C_{2k-2r-1,k-r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r+j}\sigma_{r+j}(A)A_{k-r-1+j,j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{r+1+j}\sigma_{r+1+j}(B)B_{k-r+j,j}.

It follows from the induction hypothesis that

\rho_{i}=\sigma_{i}(B)B_{k-1,r}-\sigma_{i}(A)B_{k-1,r-1}

and

-\rho_{i}=-\sigma_{i}(A)B_{k-1,r}+\sigma_{i}(B)B_{k-1,r-1}

where

\displaystyle\rho_{i}=\sigma_{2r}(C)C_{2k-2r-1,k-r-1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{r+j}\sigma_{r+j}(B)B_{k-r-1+j,j}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{r-2}(-1)^{r+1+j}\sigma_{r+1+j}A_{k-r+j,j}.

Therefore

0=(\sigma_{i}(B)-\sigma_{i}(A))(B_{k-1,r-1}+B_{k-1,r}),

but

\displaystyle B_{k-1,r-1}+B_{k-1,r}

\displaystyle=C_{2k-1,k-r}+C_{2k-1,k-r-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2r(2k-1)(2k-2)!}{(k+r)!(k-r)!}

\displaystyle\neq 0

since
p>2k-1
. Hence

\sigma_{i}(A)=\sigma_{i}(B).

Similarly, if
i
is odd,
i=2r+1\leq k
. The coefficient of the monomial
x^{k-r-1}y^{k-r-1}
is

0=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r+1+j}\left(\sigma_{r+1+j}(A)A_{k-r-1+j,j}+\sigma_{r+1+j}(B)B_{k-r-1+j,j}\right)

since
C_{2k-2r-2,k-r-1}=0
. Again, by our induction hypothesis
\sigma_{r+1+j}(A)=\sigma_{r+1+j}(B)
for every
0\leq j<r
and
A_{k-r-1+j,j}=-B_{k-r-1+j,j}
for every
0\leq j\leq r
. Hence

0=(\sigma_{i}(B)-\sigma_{i}(A))B_{k-1,r}.

Again

B_{k-1,r}=\frac{-i}{k-r-1}\binom{2k-2}{k+r-1}\neq 0.

Now, we inspect the coefficients of the monomials
x^{k+\ell}y^{k-i-1-\ell}
and
x^{k-i-1-\ell}y^{k+\ell}
for
2\leq\ell\leq k-i-1
. That is,

\sigma_{i}(C)C_{2k-i-1,k+\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}(A)A_{k-i-1+j,\ell+j},

and

\sigma_{i}(C)C_{2k-i-1,k-i-1-\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{j}(B)B_{k-i-1+j,\ell+j}.

Since
C_{2k-i-1,k+\ell}=-C_{2k-i-1,k-i-1-\ell}
, and by our induction hypothesis

A_{k-i-1+j,\ell+j}=-B_{k-i-1+j,\ell+j}

for
j\geq 1
. We conclude that
A_{k-i-1,\ell}=-B_{k-i-1,\ell}
.

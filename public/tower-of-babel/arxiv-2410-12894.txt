A dispersive estimate of the a 0 ​ ( 980 ) a_{0}(980) contribution to ( g − 2 ) μ (g-2)_{\mu}
Oleksandra Deineka, Igor Danilkin, Marc Vanderhaeghen

Abstract
A dispersive implementation of the a 0 ​ ( 980 ) a_{0}(980) resonance to ( g − 2 ) μ (g-2)_{\mu} requires the knowledge of the double-virtual S S -wave γ ∗ ​ γ ∗ → π ​ η / K ​ K ¯ I = 1 \gamma^{*}\gamma^{*}\to\pi\eta/K\bar{K}_{I=1} amplitudes. To obtain these amplitudes, we used a modified coupled-channel Muskhelishvili–Omnès formalism, with input from the left-hand cuts and the hadronic Omnès matrix. The latter was derived using a data-driven N / D N/D method, where the hadronic left-hand cuts were approximated via a conformal expansion. Due to the absence of direct hadronic data in the π ​ η \pi\eta channel, the expansion coefficients were fitted to various experimental data sets on two-photon fusion processes with π ​ η \pi\eta and K ​ K ¯ K\bar{K} final states. The resulting dispersive estimate for the a 0 ​ ( 980 ) a_{0}(980) contribution to ( g − 2 ) μ (g-2)_{\mu} is a μ HLbL [ a 0 ( 980 ) ] resc. = − 0.44 ( 5 ) × 10 − 11 a_{\mu}^{\text{HLbL}}[a_{0}(980)]_{\text{resc.}}=-0.44(5)\times 10^{-11} , which presents an order of magnitude improvement in precision over the narrow resonance approximation.

2.2
Dispersion relations

The new set of amplitudes,
\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}
and
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}
, contain only dynamical singularities, namely the right- and left-hand cuts. Therefore, it is possible to write a dispersion relation (modulo subtractions, which will be discussed later) as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}(s,Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}(s,Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)=\int_{L}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{\pi}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-s}\,\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{R}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{\pi}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-s}\,\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)\,,

where the unitarity condition over the right-hand cut has the following form

\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}\end{array}\right)=t^{(0)*}\,\rho\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}\end{array}\right)\,,

(15)

with
\rho=\text{diag}\{\rho_{\pi\eta},\rho_{K\bar{K}}\}
.
In Eq.(
15
),
t^{(0)}
is the hadronic coupled-channel
\pi\eta/K\bar{K}_{I=1}
scattering amplitude, normalized such that
\text{Im}(t^{(0)})^{-1}=-\rho
. The solution to Eq. (
2.2
) is provided by the Muskhelishvili-Omnès (MO) method
[
51
]
. We apply the modified MO representation, which is based on writing a dispersion relation for
(\Omega^{(0)})^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}\end{array}\right)

[
52
,
38
]
, where
\Omega^{(0)}
is the Omnès matrix. This matrix satisfies the following unitarity constraint

\text{Im}\,\Omega_{ab}^{(0)}(s)=\sum_{c}t_{ac}^{(0)*}(s)\,\rho_{c}(s)\,\Omega_{cb}^{(0)}(s)\,.

(16)

The resulting dispersion relation for the
S
-wave amplitudes reads as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0)}_{i}(s,Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\\
k^{(0)}_{i}(s,Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
k^{(0),\text{ Born}}_{i}(s,Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)+\Omega^{(0)}(s)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\Bigg[\int\limits_{L}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{\pi}\,\frac{(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{h}^{(0)}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\\
\bar{k}^{(0)}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\int\limits_{R}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{\pi}\,\frac{\text{Im}(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
k^{(0),\text{ Born}}_{i}(s^{\prime},Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2})\end{array}\right)\Bigg]\,,

where the right-hand cut begins at
s_{th}\equiv s_{th}^{\pi\eta}
.
In principle, the presented form of the MO representation is not unique. As discussed in
[
38
,
53
]
, the solution to Eq. (
2.2
) can be obtained using various forms of MO representations, including those with integrations over only right-hand cuts or both right-hand and left-hand cuts, with equivalence demonstrated in
[
50
]
. The modified version used in this work, however, has an advantage as it effectively separates the well-known Born left-hand cut from the heavier contributions, which are usually known to a much lesser extent. As a result, it only involves the imaginary part of the Born-subtracted left-hand cut and ensures better high-energy behavior in its partial waves. We will discuss the role of the left-hand cut contributions in more detail in Sec.
2.4
.

2.3
Hadronic Omnès matrix

To derive the Omnès matrix
\Omega^{(0)}(s)
, which accounts for the
\pi\eta/K\bar{K}_{I=1}
rescattering effects, we employ a once-subtracted dispersion relation for the hadronic
S
-wave amplitude

\displaystyle t^{(0)}_{ab}(s)=U^{(0)}_{ab}(s)+\frac{s}{\pi}\int_{s_{thr}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\frac{\text{Im }t^{(0)}_{ab}(s^{\prime})}{s^{\prime}-s}\,,

(25)

The asymptotically bounded function
U^{(0)}_{ab}(s)
incorporates the contribution from the subtraction constant and left-hand cuts. The solution to Eq. (
25
) can be found using the
N/D
ansatz
[
54
]
, such that

t^{(0)}_{ab}(s)=\sum_{c}(D^{(0)}_{ac}(s))^{-1}\,N^{(0)}_{cb}(s)\,,

(26)

where the left- and right-hand cuts contributions are separated into the
N
and
D
functions, respectively. In the absence of Castillejo-Dalitz-Dyson (CDD) ambiguities
[
55
]
or bound states
[
56
]
, this ansatz leads to a system of linear integral equations
[
57
,
*Johnson:1979jy
]

\displaystyle N^{(0)}_{ab}(s)

\displaystyle=U^{(0)}_{ab}(s)+

\displaystyle\frac{s}{\pi}\sum_{c}\int_{s_{th}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\frac{N^{(0)}_{ac}(s^{\prime})\,\rho_{c}(s^{\prime})\,(U^{(0)}_{cb}(s^{\prime})-U^{(0)}_{cb}(s))}{s^{\prime}-s}\,,

\displaystyle D^{(0)}_{ab}(s)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}-\frac{s}{\pi}\int_{s_{th}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\frac{N^{(0)}_{ab}(s^{\prime})\,\rho_{b}(s^{\prime})}{s^{\prime}-s}\,.

(27)

The
D^{(0)}_{ab}(s)
function obtained from Eq. (
27
) is related to the Omnès function as

\Omega^{(0)}_{ab}(s)=\left(D^{(0)}_{ab}(s)\right)^{-1}\,,

(28)

which satisfies the following dispersion relation

\Omega_{ab}^{(0)}(s)=\delta_{ab}+\frac{s}{\pi}\int_{s_{th}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-s}\,\text{Im}\,\Omega_{ab}^{(0)}(s^{\prime})\,.

(29)

The
N/D
system in Eq. (
27

U^{(0)}_{ab}(s)
on the right-hand cut.

In a general scattering problem, little is known about the left-hand cuts, aside from their analytic structure in the complex plane. Progress has been made in
[
58

*Danilkin:2010xd

*Gasparyan:2011yw

*Gasparyan:2012km

by analytically continuing

\xi(s)

\displaystyle U^{(0)}_{ab}(s)

\displaystyle\equiv t^{(0)}_{ab}(0)+\frac{s}{\pi}\int_{L}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\frac{\text{Im }t^{(0)}_{ab}(s^{\prime})}{s^{\prime}-s}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n,ab}\,(\xi_{ab}(s))^{n}\,,

(30)

which is chosen to map the left-hand cut plane onto the unit circle
[
62
]
.
The form of
\xi(s)
for each channel is dictated by the position of the closest left-hand cut branching point,
s_{L}
, and a point,
s_{E}
, around which the series is expanded. To ensure the convergence of the conformal expansion in the region where the
S
-wave amplitude dominates, we adopt the following

\sqrt{s_{E}}=\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\sqrt{s_{th}}+\sqrt{s_{max}}\right)\,,

(31)

with
\sqrt{s_{max}}=1.1
GeV. Note that in the coupled-channel case,
s_{th}
in Eq. (
31

U_{ab}(s)
, while for the off-diagonal terms, it corresponds to the lowest threshold.

In the
\pi\eta,KK

[
63

64

-\infty<s<s_{L}

\pi\eta\to\pi\eta

\xi_{22}

\displaystyle\xi_{22}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{s-s_{L}}-\sqrt{s_{E}-s_{L}}}{\sqrt{s-s_{L}}+\sqrt{s_{E}-s_{L}}}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle s_{L}=4(m_{K}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2})\,.

For
\xi_{11}

\displaystyle\xi_{11}(s)=-\frac{(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{s_{E}})(\sqrt{s}\sqrt{s_{E}}+s_{L})}{(\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{s_{E}})(\sqrt{s}\sqrt{s_{E}}-s_{L})}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle s_{L}=m_{\eta}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}\,.

Finally, for
\xi_{12}

To assess the validity of this approximation and the importance of the Adler zero constraint, we additionally consider the contributions from vector-meson exchange left-hand cuts. These contributions are derived from the effective Lagrangian that couples the photon, vector meson (

\mathcal{L}_{VP\gamma}=e\,C_{VP\gamma}\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,F_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\alpha}P\,V_{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{V\to P\gamma}=\alpha\,C_{VP\gamma}^{2}\,\frac{(M_{V}^{2}-m_{P}^{2})^{3}}{6M_{V}^{2}}\,.

(44)

The modulus of the radiative couplings can be also estimated by the SU(3) relations as

3

\displaystyle|g_{VP\gamma}|

\displaystyle\simeq|C_{\rho^{\pm,0}\pi^{\pm,0}\gamma}|\simeq\frac{|C_{\omega\pi^{0}\gamma}|}{3}\simeq\sqrt{3}\,|C_{\omega\eta\gamma}|\simeq\frac{|C_{\rho\eta\gamma}|}{\sqrt{3}}

\displaystyle\simeq|C_{K^{*\pm}K^{\pm}\gamma}|\simeq\frac{|C_{K^{*0}K^{0}\gamma}|}{2}\,.

The dispersive integral along the vector left-hand cuts remains formally convergent due to the asymptotically bounded behaviour of the
S
-wave Omnès function and the imaginary part of the amplitude
\text{Im }h_{1,++}^{(0),V}(-\infty)\to\text{const}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0)}_{1,++}(s)\\

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{s}{\pi}\int\limits_{s_{th}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\text{Im}(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\

where the integral along the vector left-hand cuts are denoted as

\displaystyle\mathcal{LHC}\equiv\frac{s}{\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{s_{L}^{V}}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0),V}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\\

and

s_{L}^{V}=-\frac{(M_{V}^{2}-m_{P,1}^{2})(M_{V}^{2}-m_{P,2}^{2})}{M_{V}^{2}}\,.

(54)

However, the left-hand cut contributions acquire significant corrections from integration over large negative values of
s
. To address this, several approaches can be employed. A common method to improve the convergence of the left-hand cut integral in Eq. (
2.4
) involves introducing additional subtraction constants
[
46
]
. The once-subtracted coupled-channel dispersion relation can be written as:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0)}_{1,++}(s)\\
k^{(0)}_{1,++}(s)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
k^{(0),\text{ Born}}_{1,++}(s)\end{array}\right)+\Omega^{(0)}(s)\Bigg[\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
b\end{array}\right)s

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+\frac{s^{2}}{\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{s_{L}^{V}}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}-s)}\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0),\text{ V}}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\\
k^{(0),\text{ V}}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt-\frac{s^{2}}{\pi}\int\limits_{s_{th}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\text{Im}(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}-s)}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
k^{(0),\text{ Born}}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\end{array}\right)\Bigg]\,,

A formal drawback of this approach is the introduction of the subtraction constants
a
and
b

\gamma\gamma\to\pi^{0}\eta

h^{(0)}_{1,++}(m_{\eta}^{2})=0\,.

(65)

However, the remaining subtraction constant must still be fixed using experimental data, given the limitations of the SU(3) chiral expansion for kaons.

Another approach involves implementing the conformal mapping technique, allowing us to approximate the contributions beyond the kaon-pole left-hand cut as follows
[
75
]
:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0)}_{1,++}(s)\\
k^{(0)}_{1,++}(s)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
k^{(0),\text{ Born}}_{1,++}(s)\end{array}\right)+\Omega^{(0)}(s)\Bigg[\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{\pi\eta}(s)\\
\phi_{K\bar{K}}(s)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{s}{\pi}\int\limits_{s_{th}}^{\infty}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\text{Im}(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{s^{\prime}-s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
k^{(0),\text{ Born}}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\end{array}\right)\Bigg]\,,

where

\phi_{a}\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tilde{C}_{a,n}\,(\omega_{a}(s))^{n}\,,\quad a=\pi\eta,K\bar{K}\,,

(74)

and
\omega_{a}(s=0)=0
ensures the soft-photon limit. Since all left-hand cuts for the real photon-fusion reaction lie on the real axis, we can utilize the following form
\omega(s)
from Eq. (
32

\omega_{a}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{s-s^{V}_{L,a}}-\sqrt{-s^{V}_{L,a}}}{\sqrt{s-s^{V}_{L,a}}+\sqrt{-s^{V}_{L,a}}}\,.

\displaystyle=\mathcal{LHC}^{\prime\prime}|_{s=0}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{s_{L}^{V}}\frac{ds^{\prime}(\Omega^{(0)}(s^{\prime}))^{-1}}{(s^{\prime})^{3}}\text{Im}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h^{(0),\text{ V}}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\\
k^{(0),\text{ V}}_{1,++}(s^{\prime})\end{array}\right)\,.

where the right-hand side of the above equation is essentially a sum rule (multiplied by a factor of 2) for the over subtracted

at

4
Conclusion and outlook

In this work, we presented a dispersive analysis of the
\gamma\gamma\to\pi^{0}\eta/K\bar{K}
reactions from threshold up to 1.4 GeV. The hadronic Omnès matrix was directly constrained using data from two-photon differential cross sections with
\pi^{0}\eta
and
K_{S}K_{S}
final states. We systematically compared subtracted and unsubtracted dispersive formalisms to minimize model dependence in the extracted parameters, allowing us to investigate the properties of the
a_{0}(980)
resonance. Notably, we identified two corresponding poles on the second and third Riemann sheets.

The use of dispersion relations allowed to estimate the
a_{0}(980)
HLbL contribution with an order of magnitude better precision compared to the narrow resonance approximation. The final result is of the same order as the charged kaon box contribution in HLbL scattering
[
107
]
, resolving another small but important piece of the overall puzzle. Additionally, the produced amplitude serves as a Monte Carlo generator for analyzing photon-fusion processes with a single tagged photon by the BESIII Collaboration
[
37
]
.

Future work will involve incorporating new experimental data into the current analysis. The hadronic
\pi\eta/K\bar{K}_{I=1}
rescattering will be further constrained by including existing data from the
\phi\to\gamma\pi\eta

[
109
]
and
\eta^{\prime}\to\pi\pi\eta

[
110
]
decays.
Furthermore, we look forward to the final results on the

\gamma\gamma\to K^{+}K^{-}
process from BESIII, which will include both total and differential cross sections
[
80
,
*Kussner:2024ryb
]
.

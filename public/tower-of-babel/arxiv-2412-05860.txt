1.
Introduction

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring with residue field
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
. Let
I
be a
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module. We denote the associated graded ring of
A
with respect to the ideal

as
G_{I}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/I^{n+1}
, and the associated graded module of
M
with respect to
I
as
G_{I}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}M/I^{n+1}M
, considered as a graded
G_{I}(A)
-module. Let
\ell(E)
denotes the length of an
A
-module
E
.

Let
M
be an
A
-module of dimension
r
. The Hilbert series of
M
is given by the formula

H_{M,I}(z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\ell(I^{i}M/I^{i+1}M)z^{i}=\frac{h^{I}_{M}(z)}{(1-z)^{r}},

where
h^{I}_{M}(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
and is called the
h
-polynomial of
M
. The integer
e^{I}_{i}(M)=h^{(i)}_{M}(1)/i!
is referred to as the
i
-th Hilbert coefficient of
M
with respect to
I
, where
h^{(i)}_{M}(z)
is the
i
-th derivative of
h_{M}(z)
. When
I=\mathfrak{m}
, we write
G(A)=G_{I}(A)
,
G(M)=G_{I}(M)
and
e_{i}(M)=e^{I}_{i}(M)
.

There has been extensive research on the behavior of the Betti numbers of the Cohen-Macaulay module over complete intersection rings. For instance, see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
5
]
. Set
\beta_{i}(M)
as the
i
-th Betti number of
M
, and
\mu(M)
as the cardinality of the minimal set of generators of
M
. Note that
\beta_{i}(M)=\mu(M_{i})
for all

. One interesting result due to Avramov (
[

]
, Theorem 4.1) is that the function

is a quasi-polynomial (for the definition of quasi-polynomial, see
2.7
) type with period 2 for sufficiently large
i
. Furthermore, the polynomials
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
have the same degree and leading coefficient. Similar questions can be posed regarding the behavior of other invariants of
M_{i}
for sufficiently large
i.

Let
M_{i}=\text{Syz}^{A}_{i}(M)
for all
i\geq 1
, representing the
i
-th syzygy of
M
in its minimal free resolution, and for convenience, set
M_{0}=M
. In
[
9
]
, the first author focused on the Hilbert coefficients of the syzygies of the modules and established that for a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module

over complete intersection rings (see
[
9
]
, 2.4), the function

is a quasi-polynomial type with period 2 and degree at most
\text{cx}(M)-1
for all

. In this paper, we explore the degree of the function
i\mapsto e_{t}(M_{i})
for

and reg
G(M_{i})
(for the definition of regularity, see

) for sufficiently large values of

over complete intersection rings.

In the first part of this paper (Section
3
), we will address the following questions:

Question 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring and
M
be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module.

(a)

When does the function
i\mapsto e_{t}(M_{i})
attain its maximal degree for
t=0,1
?

(b)

When do the polynomials
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
have the same degree?

For the leading coefficients of
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
, we have both positive and negative answers for the function

for
t=0,1
. For example,

(1)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})=(k[|x,y|]/(x^{2}y^{2}),(\overline{x},\overline{y}))
, where

is a field and

. Note that
M
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module. It is easy to check that
e_{0}(M_{i})=2
and
e_{1}(M_{i})=1
for all
i\geq 0.

(2)

Let

be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module with

(in particular,

is Ulrich) over a hypersurface ring with

. Then,

for all

(see
[
8
]
, Theorem 2, Remark 4.10).

(3)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})=(k[|x,y|]/(xy^{2}),(\overline{x},\overline{y}))
and

. Note that
M
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay

-module. It is easy to check that

and

for all

However, we are unable to provide sufficient conditions for the leading coefficients to be equal. Additionally, the Question
1.1
remains open for

.

First, we will present the following result, which provides a positive response to Question
1.1
for

and for an arbitrary
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, which is not very difficult to prove.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring and
I
a
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
M
be a maximal Cohen Macaulay module. Then the function
i\mapsto e^{I}_{0}(M_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial type with period
2
and degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for all
i\gg 0.

In order to prove Question
1.1
for the first Hilbert coefficient, additional conditions are required for both rings and the ideal. For example, let

be an ideal of

generated by a maximal

- regular sequence. Then

is a maximal

-regular sequence for all

Therefore, for all

, the Hilbert series of

is

]

We prove the following for the maximal ideal.

.

Let

be a complete intersection ring of codimension

and dimension

. Let

be a maximal Cohen Macaulay

-module. Set

Then the function

Next, we will compare the leading coefficients of the functions

,
i\mapsto e_{0}(M_{i})
, and
i\mapsto e_{1}(M_{i})
for sufficiently large

.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring of codimension

and dimension

. Let

be a maximal Cohen Macaulay

-module. Then for

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{1}(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{0}(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}.

Let

be an

. One interesting question is how the invariants

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper all rings considered are Noetherian and all modules considered, unless stated otherwise, are finitely generated.

2.1
.

Base change

Let
\phi:(A,\mathfrak{m})\to(A^{\prime},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})
be a local ring homomorphism. Assume
A^{\prime}
is a faithfully flat
A
-algebra with
\mathfrak{m}A=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
. Let
M
be an
A
-module. Set
M^{\prime}=M\otimes A^{\prime}
. In these cases, it can be shown that

(1)

\ell_{A}(M)=\ell_{A^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})
.

(2)

H(M,n)=H(M^{\prime},n)
for all
n\geq 0.

(3)

dim
M=
dim
M
and
\text{ depth}_{A}M=\text{ depth}_{A^{\prime}}M^{\prime}
.

(4)

depth
G(M)=\text{ depth }G(M^{\prime})
.

(5)

A
is a local complete intersection iff
A^{\prime}
is a local complete intersection.

The specific base changes we do are the following:

(i)

A^{\prime}=\hat{A}
the completion of
A
with respect to the maximal ideal.

(ii)

A^{\prime}=A[[X]]_{S}
, where
S=A[[X]]\setminus\mathfrak{m}A[[X]].
The maximal ideal of
A^{\prime}
is
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}A^{\prime}
and the residue field of
A^{\prime}
is
K=k((X)).

Thus, we can assume that the ring A is complete and the residue field is uncountable.

2.2
.

The Rees module
L(M)

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
M
be an
A
-module. Define
L(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M
. Let
\mathcal{R}=A[\mathfrak{m}t]
be Rees ring and

\mathcal{R}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{n}Mt^{n}
be Rees module of
M
. The Rees ring
\mathcal{R}
is a subring of
A[t]
. So
A[t]
is an
\mathcal{R}
module. Therefore
M[t]=M\otimes_{A}A[t]
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module. The exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{R}(M)\to M[t]\to L(M)(-1)\to 0

defines an
\mathcal{R}
module structure on
L(M)(-1)
and hence on
L(M)
(for more details see (
[
10
]
, definition 4.2)). Note that
L(M)
is not a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module.

2.3
.

The Hilbert-Samuel function of the module
M
with respect to the ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is defined as the function
n\mapsto\ell(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M)
. It is well-known that for large values of
n
, this function can be expressed as a polynomial
P_{M}(n)
of degree equal to the dimension of
M
(say
r
), known as the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of
M
. The polynomial can be written in the form

P_{M}(n)=e_{0}(M)\binom{n+r}{r}-e_{1}(M)\binom{n+r-1}{r-1}+\ldots+(-1)^{r}e_{r},

where
e_{0}(M),\ldots,e_{r}(M)
are the Hilbert coefficients of
M
.

It can be easily seen that the formal power series
\sum_{n\geq 0}\ell{(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M)}z^{n}
represents a rational function

\sum_{n\geq 0}\ell{(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M)}z^{n}=\dfrac{h_{M}(z)}{(1-z)^{r+1}},

i,e
H(L(M),z)=\dfrac{h_{M}(z)}{(1-z)^{r+1}}.

2.4
.

Set
L_{1}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{1}(M,A/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}).
Note that
L(A)(-1)\otimes M=L(M)(-1).
By tensoring the exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{R}\to A[t]\to L(A)(-1)\to 0
with
M
, we get

0\to L_{1}(M)(-1)\to\mathcal{R}\otimes M\to M[t]\to L(M)(-1)\to 0.

Thus
L_{1}(M)(-1)
is a
\mathcal{R}
-submodule of
\mathcal{R}\otimes M.
Since
\mathcal{R}\otimes M
is finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module so
L_{1}(M)
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module.

2.5
.

Superficial element

An element

is called

\mathfrak{m}
if there exists

such that for all

,
(\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M:_{M}x)\cap\mathfrak{m}^{c}M=\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
. If depth

then one can show that a

-superficial element is

-regular. Furthermore
(\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M:_{M}x)=\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
for

Superficial elements exist if the residue field is infinite. See [
[
13
]
, p. 6-8] for proof of these facts when

, the same proof works in general.

A sequence

in
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is said to be

-superficial sequence if

is
M
-superficial and

is
M/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M
-superficial for
2\leq i\leq r.

[

]
.

(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen Macaulay local ring and

Let

2.7
.

Quasi-polynomial

The function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is said to be a quasi-polynomial type with period
r>0
if there exist polynomials
P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{r-1}
such that
f(mr+i)=P_{i}(m)
for all
m\gg 0
and
i=0,1,\ldots,r-1.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is a quasi-polynomial type with period 2.

(2)

\sum_{n\geq 0}f(n)z^{n}=\dfrac{h(z)}{(1-z^{2})^{c}}
for some
h(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
and
c\geq 0.

Moreover if
P_{0},P_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
are polynomials such that
f(2m+i)=P_{i}(m)
for all
m\gg 0
and
i=0,1
then deg
P_{i}\leq c-1
. Set deg
f=\text{max}\{\text{deg }P_{0},\text{deg }P_{1}\}

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. If the function
g(n)=f(n)-f(n-2)
is a quasi-polynomial type with period 2 then so is
f
. Furthermore deg
f=\text{deg }g+1
.

2.10
.

Eisenbud operators

Let
Q
be a local ring and
\textbf{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
be a
Q
-regular sequence. Set
A=Q/(\textbf{f}).
The Eisenbud operators (see
[
5
]
) are constructed as follows:

Let
\mathcal{F}:\cdots F_{i+2}\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{i}\ldots
be a complex of free
A
-modules.

(a) Choose a sequence of free
Q
-modules
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
and maps
\widetilde{\partial}
between them

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}:\cdots\widetilde{F}_{i+2}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\partial}}\widetilde{F}_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\partial}}\widetilde{F}_{i}\ldots

such that
\mathcal{F}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\otimes A
.

(b) Since
\widetilde{\partial}^{2}\equiv 0
modulo
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}),
we may write
\widetilde{\partial}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{c}f_{j}\widetilde{t_{j}}
where
\widetilde{t_{j}}:\widetilde{F_{i}}\to\widetilde{F}_{i-2}
are linear maps for all
i.

(c) Define, for
j=1,\ldots,c
the map
t_{j}=t_{j}(Q,\textbf{f},\mathcal{F})
by
t_{j}=\widetilde{t_{j}}\otimes A.

The operators
t_{1},\ldots,t_{c}
are called Eisenbud operators associated to
\textbf{f}.
It can be shown that

(1)

t_{i}
are uniquely determined up to homotopy.

(2)

t_{i},t_{j}
commutes up to homotopy.

2.11
.

Let
R=A[t_{1},\ldots,t_{c}]
be a polynomial ring over
A
with variable

of degree
2.
Let
M
be an
A
-module and let
\mathcal{F}
be a free resolution of

This turns

into a

Since
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\text{ann}(\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,k))

\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{i}(M)/n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}
exists.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\operatorname{cx(M)}=1
then the function
i\mapsto\beta_{i}(M)
coincides with a nonzero constant polynomial for all
i\gg 0
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring and
I
a
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
M
be a maximal Cohen Macaulay module. Then the function
i\mapsto e^{I}_{0}(M_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial type with period
2
and degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for all
i\gg 0.

Proof.

We may assume
A
is complete and the residue field of
A
is uncountable. We will prove this by induction on
\operatorname{cx}(M).
Assume
\operatorname{cx}(M)=1.
We may assume
M
has no free summand. Since
M
is a maximal Cohen Macaulay module so
M
has periodic resolution of period
2
,
i.e.

\text{Syz}^{A}_{2i+j}(M)=\text{Syz}^{A}_{j}(M)
for
j=0,1
. Therefore
e^{I}_{0}(M_{2i})=e^{I}_{0}(M)\neq 0
and
e^{I}_{0}(M_{2i+1})=e^{I}_{0}(M_{1})\neq 0.
Hence the statement is true for
\operatorname{cx}(M)=1
.

Let us assume it is true for
\operatorname{cx}(M)=r-1.
Let
\operatorname{cx}(M)=r.
By
2.13
, we have a short exact sequence

0\to K_{i}\to M_{i+2}\to M_{i}\to 0

for all
i\gg 0
(say
i\geq j_{0}
) with
\operatorname{cx}(K_{j_{0}})=\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
. Note that
K_{j}\cong\text{Syz}^{A}_{j-j_{0}}(K_{j_{0}}).
Since multiplicity is additive on short exact sequence we get
e^{I}_{0}(M_{i+2})-e^{I}_{0}(M_{i})=e^{I}_{0}(K_{i})
for all

i\mapsto e^{I}_{0}(K_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial of degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-2
for sufficiently large

. Therefore, the function
i\mapsto e^{I}_{0}(M_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial of degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for sufficiently large
i
(see
2.9
).
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring of codimension
c\geq 2
and dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
M
be a maximal Cohen Macaulay
A
-module. Set
M_{i}=\operatorname{syz}^{A}_{i}(M)
for all
i\geq 0.
Then the function
i\mapsto e_{1}(M_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial type with period
2
and degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for all
i\gg 0.

Proof.

We may assume
A
is complete and the residue field of
A
is uncountable. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be
(M_{i}\oplus A)
-superficial sequence for all
i\geq 0
. By (
[
9
]
, Lemma 2.2,) and
2.6
, we have the dimension of
M_{i}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1})M_{i}
is one for all
i\geq 0
and
e_{1}(M_{i})=e_{1}(M_{i}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1}))
. So it suffices to show for
d=1.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}
be
(M\oplus A)
-superficial element. By
2.6
,
e_{1}(M)\geq e_{0}(M)-\mu(M)\geq 0
. If
e_{1}(M)=0
then
e_{0}(M)=\mu(M)
. This says that
M
is a Ulrich module and
M/xM\cong k^{\mu(M)}
, where

is residue field of

Assume
\operatorname{cx}(M)=1
. Then
M
has a periodic resolution of period
2.
Suppose
e_{1}(M)=0
then
M/xM\cong k^{\mu(M)}
. Let
F\to M
be the minimal free resolution of
M
. Then
F/xF\to M/xM
is a minimal free resolution of

, so
k
has a periodic resolution of period

. Therefore by (
[
6
]
, Theorem 2.3),

is a hypersurface ring. As

,

is a hypersurface ring. This is a contradiction to the hypothesis. Similarly, we can show that

\operatorname{cx}(M)=1
.

Let us assume it is true for
\operatorname{cx}(M)=r-1.
Let
\operatorname{cx}(M)=r.
By
2.13
, we have a short exact sequence

(say

, we have the following short exact sequence

for all

for all

By the induction hypothesis, the function
i\mapsto e_{1}(K_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial of degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-2
for sufficiently large

. Therefore, the function
i\mapsto e_{1}(M_{i})
is a quasi-polynomial of degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for sufficiently large

(see
2.9
).
∎

4.
Leading coefficients of quasi-polynomials

In the previous section, we established that for a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module
M
over ring
A
, the functions
i\mapsto e_{0}(M_{i})
, and
i\mapsto e_{1}(M_{i})
are of quasi-polynomial type with period
2
and degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for all
i\gg 0.
Due to theorem
4.1
in
[
1
]
, the function
i\mapsto\mu(M_{i})
is of quasi-polynomial type with period
2
and degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for all
i\gg 0.
In this section, we will explore the relationship between their leading coefficients.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring of codimension
c\geq 2
and dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
M
be a maximal Cohen Macaulay
A
-module. Then for
j=0,1

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{1}(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{0}(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}},

where
\mu(M_{n})=\text{number of minimal generators of }M_{n}=\beta_{n}(M).
In addition, if equality holds for
\operatorname{cx}(M)=2
then
G(K_{n})
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
n\gg 0
(for definition of
K_{n}
, see
2.13
).

Proof.

We may assume
A
is complete and the residue field is infinite. By (
[
1
]
, Theorem 4.1), Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
, the function
i\mapsto\mu(M_{i})
,
i\mapsto e_{0}(M_{i})
and
i\mapsto e_{1}(M_{i})
are quasi-polynomial type of period
2
and degree
\operatorname{cx}(M)-1
for all
i\gg 0.
So for
n
large enough, let

\mu(M_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}+\text{lower degree terms}&n\text{ even};\\
\gamma n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}+\text{lower degree terms}&n\text{ odd},\\
\end{array}\right.

e_{0}(M_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}+\text{lower degree terms}&n\text{ even};\\
\beta n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}+\text{lower degree terms}&n\text{ odd},\\
\end{array}\right.

and

e_{1}(M_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}an^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}+\text{lower degree terms}&n\text{ even};\\
bn^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}+\text{lower degree terms}&n\text{ odd}.\\
\end{array}\right.

We also have
e_{1}(M_{n})\geq e_{0}(M_{n})-\mu(M_{n})
for all
n\geq 0
(see
2.6
). This implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{1}(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{0}(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(M_{2n+j})}{n^{\operatorname{cx}(M)-1}},

for
j=0,1.
This proves the first part of the result.

Now assume
\operatorname{cx}(M)=2.
Note that we can assume the dimension of
A
is one (see
2.6
). So by the Lemma
3.4
, we obtain the short exact sequence

0\to L(K_{2n})\to L(M_{2n+2})\to L(M_{2n})\to 0

for all sufficiently large
n
and this implies the following equalities.

\begin{split}\mu(M_{2n+2})-\mu(M_{2n})=&\,\mu(K_{2n})\\
e_{0}(M_{2n+2})-e_{0}(M_{2n})=&\,e_{0}(K_{2n})\\
e_{1}(M_{2n+2})-e_{1}(M_{2n})=&\,e_{1}(K_{2n})\end{split}

Assume that for
j=0
the equality holds.
i.e.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{1}(M_{2n})}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{0}(M_{2n})}{n}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(M_{2n})}{n}.

Thus
a=\alpha-\gamma
and this implies
e_{1}(K_{2n})=e_{0}(K_{2n})-\mu(K_{2n})
for all
n\gg 0
. Therefore,
G(K_{2n})
has minimal multiplicity, and hence
G(K_{2n})
is Cohen-Macaulay (see
2.6
). Similarly, by assuming the equality holds for
j=1
, we can show that
G(K_{2n+1})
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
n\gg 0
. This proves the result.
∎

5.
Assymptotic behaviour of
\operatorname{reg}G(M_{i})

In this section, we aim to demonstrate that if the equality holds in Theorem
4.1
and
\operatorname{cx}(M)=2
, then the regularity of
G(M_{n})
remains bounded for all
n\gg 0
(refer to Theorem
5.5
). To establish this, we require the following:

5.1
.

Recall that
\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{n}t^{n}
is Rees ring with respect to the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Set
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathcal{R}_{+}
, where
\mathcal{R}_{+}=\oplus_{n\geq 1}\mathfrak{m}^{n}t^{n}.
Let
E
be a graded
\mathcal{R}
-module. We set
H^{i}(E)
as the
i
-th graded local cohomology of
E
with respect to
\mathfrak{M}.
Define
a(E)=\text{sup}\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,H^{i}(E)_{n}\neq 0\}.

5.2
.

A graded
\mathcal{R}
-module
E
is considered
*
-Artinian if every descending chain of graded submodules of
E
terminates. It can be easily proved that if
E
is
*
-Artinian then
E_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
. Let
E
be a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module. Then for each
i\geq 0
,
H^{i}(E)
is
*
-Artinian (see
[
4
]
, Proposition 3.5.4). Set

a_{i}(E)=a(H^{i}(E)).

The Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
E
is defined as

\text{reg }(E)=\text{max}\{a_{i}(E)+i\,|\,0\leq i\leq\text{dim }E\}.

5.3
.

Note that we have a natural surjective homogenous homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{R}\to G(A)
with
\phi(\mathfrak{M})=G(A)_{+}
, where
G(A)_{+}
is the irrelevant maximal ideal of
G(A)
. So by the graded independence theorem (see
[
3
]
, 13.1.6) it doesn’t matter which ring we use to compute local cohomology.

Proposition 5.4

(
[
10
]
, 4.4)
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be local ring and
M
be an
A
-module. Set
l=\text{depth }(M)
. Then
H^{i}(L(M))
is
*
-Artinian for all
0\leq i\leq l-1
.

The following result concerns the regularity of the associated graded module of
M_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection ring of codimension
c\geq 2
and dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
M
be a maximal Cohen Macaulay
A
-module. If
\operatorname{cx}(M)=2
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{1}(M_{2n+j})}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{e_{0}(M_{2n+j})}{n}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(M_{2n+j})}{n}

for
j=0,1
. Then
\text{reg }G(M_{n})
is bounded for all
n\gg 0.

Proof.

We may assume
A
is complete. Due to the Proposition
2.13
, we have a short exact sequence

(2)

0\to K_{n}\to M_{n+2}\to M_{n}\to 0\text{ for all }n\gg 0.

Claim:

0\to L(K_{n})\to L(M_{n+2})\to L(M_{n})\to 0
for all
n\gg 0.

Note that the statement is true for
d=1
(see Lemma
3.4
). Now, assume
d\geq 2
. Equation
2
induces the following exact sequence.

L_{1}(M_{2n})\xrightarrow{g}L(K_{2n})\to L(M_{2n+2})\to L(M_{2n})\to 0.

Set
U=\text{Image}(g)
. So we obtain the following exact sequence

0\to U\to L(K_{2n})\to L(M_{2n+2})\to L(M_{2n})\to 0.

Since
U
is a homomorphic image of
L_{1}(M_{2n})
, it is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module (see
2.4
).

Therefore, for all
j\gg 0
, we have the following equality

\begin{split}\ell(U_{j})=&(e_{2}(K_{n})-e_{2}(M_{n+2})+e_{2}(M_{n}))\binom{j+d-2}{d-2}+\text{ lower degree terms}.\end{split}

This says that the dimension of
U
is at most
d-1
. But by the Lemma
4.1
,
G(K_{n})
is Cohen-Macaulay and we also have
\text{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L(K_{n}))=\text{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(G(K_{n}))
(see
[
12
]
, Proposition 5.6). Thus,
U=0
. This proves the claim.

The short exact sequence

0\to L(K_{n})\to L(M_{n+2})\to L(M_{n})\to 0

induces the following long exact sequence

(3)

\begin{split}0&\to H^{0}(L(K_{n}))\to H^{0}(L(M_{n+2}))\to H^{0}(L(M_{n})\to\\
\ldots\\
&\to H^{d-1}(L(K_{n}))\to H^{d-1}(L(M_{n+2}))\to H^{d-1}(L(M_{n}).\end{split}

Since
G(K_{n})
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
r
, we have
H^{i}(L(K_{n}))=0
for
i=0,1,\ldots,d-1
(see
[
10
]
, Proposition 5.2). Therefore, for
i=0,1,\ldots,d-2
, we have
H^{i}(L(M_{n+2}))\cong H^{i}(L(M_{n}))
for all
n\gg 0
. For
i=d-1
, we have the following descending chain

H^{i}(L(M_{n}))\supseteq H^{i}(L(M_{n+2}))\supseteq\ldots

for all
n\gg 0
. Since
H^{i}(L(M_{n}))
is
*
-Artinian, the descending chain terminates. Therefore, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
H^{i}(L(M_{n_{0}}))=H^{i}(L(M_{2n+n_{0}}))
for all
n\geq 1
. Without loss of generality, let us assume
n_{0}
is even. Then

(4)

a_{i}(L(M_{2n+j}))=a_{i}(L(M_{n_{0}+j}))\text{ for all }n\geq 1,i=0,1,\ldots,d-1\text{ and }j=0,1.

Note that we have the following short exact sequence:

0\to G(M_{n})\to L(M_{n})\to L(M_{n})(-1)\to 0

for all
n\geq 0.
This induces the following long exact sequence.

(5)

\begin{split}0&\to H^{0}(G(M_{n}))\to H^{0}(L(M_{n}))\to H^{0}(L(M_{n})(-1))\to\\
\ldots\\
&\to H^{d-1}(G(M_{n}))\to H^{d-1}(L(M_{n}))\to H^{d-1}(L(M_{n})(-1)).\end{split}

From equation
5
, we obtain for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
the following:

(a)

a_{0}(G(M_{n}))\leq a_{0}(L(M_{n}))
,

(b)

a_{i}(G(M_{n}))\leq\text{max}\{a_{i-1}(L(M_{n}))+1,a_{i}(L(M_{n}))\}
,

(c)

a_{i-1}(L(M_{n}))+1\leq a_{i}(G(M_{n})).

Additionally, it is well known that
a_{d}(G(M_{n}))\leq\text{red}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)-d
for all
n\geq 0
(see
[
14
]
, 3.2). Hence, by equation
4
, the regularity of
G(M_{n})
is bounded for all
n\gg 0.

∎

We recover a particular case of Theorem 4.3 and Theorem 5.3 in
[
9
]
as an application.

By equation

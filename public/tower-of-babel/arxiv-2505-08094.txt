Introduction

In the present work, we utilize methods and results of support theory to investigate explicit
invariants for specific finite dimensional modules for certain classes of infinitesimal group schemes
over an arbitrary base field
k
of characteristic
p
.
This is in contrast to the role of support theory in establishing properties for various categories
of representations of finite group schemes as in
[
3
]
. Our techniques do not apply to finite groups,
for we utilize the support theory developed in collaboration with A. Suslin and C. Bendel
in
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
which replaces cohomological varieties by varieties of 1-parameter subgroups.

We further investigate the “Jordan type function” introduced by J. Pevtsova
and the author in
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
.
For a given affine group scheme
\mathbb{G}
of finite type over
k

and a finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
for the
r
-th Frobenius kernel
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
of
\mathbb{G}
,
we formulate the continuous function
JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(-):\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathcal{Y}
from the
scheme-theoretic points of
\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G})
representing height
r
1-parameter subgroups
to the partially ordered set
\mathcal{Y}
of Young diagrams with
p
columns. By definition,

JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(-)=JT_{\mathbb{G}_{(r)},r,M}(-)
. The continuity of

JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(-)
implies that if a point
x\in\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G})
specializes
to
y\in\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G})
then the Young diagram
JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(x)
is greater or equal to
the Young diagram
JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(y)
.

This “theory”

constitutes a functor
JT_{\mathbb{G},r}:mod(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})\to Hom_{cont}(\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G}),\mathcal{Y})
from finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-modules to continuous functions

\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathcal{Y}
which captures much more information about a

\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-module
M
than does the cohomological support theory
M\mapsto\mathbb{P}H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k)_{M}
.
For example, if
p
does not divide the dimension of
M
, then
\mathbb{P}H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k)_{M}
equals

\mathbb{P}H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k)
and therefore is independent of
M
,
whereas
M\ \mapsto\ JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(-)
distinguishes many such
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-modules
M
of a given
dimension. As might be expected, determination of
JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M}(-)
is a challenge. With this
in mind, we introduce a simplified Jordan type function
M\ \mapsto\ JT^{exp}_{\mathfrak{g},r,M}(-)

for affine group schemes
\mathbb{G}
which are of
exponential type of height
r
.

After an elementary discussion of Jordan types, we provide a detailed formalism for the
construction of the “universal
p
-nilpotent operator”
\Theta_{\mathbb{G},r}\in k[V_{r}(\mathbb{G})]\otimes k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\equiv k\mathbb{G}_{(r),V_{r}(\mathbb{G})}
of height
r

constructed by J. Pevtsova and the author in
[
16
]
. In doing so, we expand upon the
connection between 1-parameter subgroups for
\mathbb{G}
and operators on
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-modules.
The operator
\Theta_{\mathbb{G},r}
acting on
k[V_{r}(\mathbb{G})]\otimes M
determines the Jordan
type function
JT^{exp}_{\mathfrak{g},r,M}(-)
.
The categorically inclined reader might want to categorify the functor
JT_{\mathbb{G},r}

by replacing
Hom_{cont}(\mathbb{P}V_{r}(\mathbb{G}),\mathcal{Y})
by the category of graded
k[V_{r}(\mathbb{G})]
-modules
equipped with a
p
-nilpotent homogeneous operator.

In order to make the Jordan type function more explicit and computationally accessible,
we restrict our attention to affine group schemes
\mathbb{G}
which are of

exponential type of height
r
. By definition, such groups
\mathbb{G}
are equipped with an

-tuples of

-nilpotent, pair-wise

-tuples of elements in
\mathfrak{g}
. For

map is the usual (truncated) exponential sending a
p
-nilpotent element of

to

GL_{N}(A)

k

For a given finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-module
M

\mathbb{G}
of exponential type
of height

-nilpotent operator

Thus, the replacement of

by

[
15
]
and
[
16
]
.
We provide sample computations which demonstrate

JT_{\mathfrak{g},r,M}^{exp}(-)
for certain
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-modules

.

For
\mathbb{G}
of exponential type of height

\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))

associated to
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}

1.
Jordan types and specialization

We begin by recalling some basic properties of Jordan forms of
p
-nilpotent endomorphisms.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a
k[t]/t^{p}
-module of dimension
m
given by the map
\rho_{M}:k[t]/t^{p}\to M_{m,m}(k)

of
k
-algebras. Let
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{\times p}
(i.e., a
p
-tuple of non-negative
integers) be defined by setting
a_{i}
to be the
number of blocks of size
i
of the Jordan canonical form of the matrix
\rho_{M}(t)
. We say that
the Jordan type of
M
is
\underline{a}
, expressing this as
JT(M)=\underline{a}
or as

JT(M)=a_{p}[p]+a_{p-1}[p-1]+\cdots+a_{1}[1]
.

The following proposition recalls familiar interpretations of Jordan types.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
M
be a
k[t]/t^{p}
-module of dimension
m
such that
JT(M)=\underline{a}
.

(1)

As a
k[t]/t^{p}
-module,
M
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus a_{i}}
.

(2)

The partition of
m=\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\cdot i
with
a_{i}
subsets of size
i

is naturally associated to
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})
.

(3)

The usual partial ordering of partitions determines the
partial ordering on
\mathbb{N}^{\times p}
given by

\underline{a}\ \leq\ \underline{b}\quad\iff\quad\forall s,1\leq s\leq p,\quad(\sum_{i=s}^{p}a_{i}\cdot i)\leq(\sum_{i=s}^{p}b_{i}\cdot i).

(4)

Uniquely associated to
\underline{a}
is a Young diagram with
p
-columns such that
the diagram has
a_{i}
rows with
i
boxes and the rows of the diagram are
left-adjusted with descending length. Thus, the partial ordering

\underline{a}\ \leq\ \underline{b}
requires for each
\ell
that the number of boxes in the top
\ell

rows of
\underline{a}
is less or equal to the number of boxes in the top
\ell
rows of
\underline{b}
.

(5)

The Jordan type of
t^{j}
acting on
M\simeq\oplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus a_{i}}

(denoted by
\underline{a}^{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{\times p}
)
has associated Young diagram obtained from the Young diagram for
\underline{a}

by removing the first (i.e., left-most)
j-1
columns of
\underline{a}
and adding rows with
each having only 1 box so that the number of boxes of
\underline{a}^{j}
equals
m
.

The lemma below recalls some properties of the partial ordering on Jordan types.
We denote by
rk(t^{j}:M\to M)
the dimension of the subspace

t^{j}(M)\subset M
, the dimension of the image of multiplication by
t^{j}
on the vector space
M
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Consider
m
-dimensional
k[t]/t^{p}
-modules
M,\ N
isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus a_{i}},\ \bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus b_{i}}
.
As above, let
\underline{a}^{s}
(respectively,
\underline{b}^{s}
) denotes the Jordan type of

t^{s}
acting on
M
(resp,
N
) for any
s,\ 1\leq s<p
.

(1)

rk(t^{s}:M\to M)\ =\ \sum_{i=s+1}^{p}a_{i}(i-1-s)
.

(2)

For all
s,\ 1\leq s<p
,
\underline{a}^{s}\ =\ \sum_{i=s+2}^{p}a_{i}[i-s]+(m-rk(t^{s}:M\to M))[1]
.

(3)

\underline{a}\leq\underline{b}\ \iff\underline{a}^{s}\leq\underline{b}^{s},\ \forall s,1\leq s<p
.

(4)

For all
s,\ 1\leq s<p
,
\underline{a}^{s}\ \leq\underline{b}^{s}\ \iff rk(t^{s}:M\to M)\leq rk(t^{s}:N\to N).

Proof.

Assertions (1) and (2) are verified by an easy computation. Assertion (3) follows from assertion (2)
and Proposition
1.2
(3). Assertion (4) follows easily from assertion (2).
∎

Since a surjective map
f:M\to N
of
k[t]/t^{p}
-modules
induces a surjective map
f:t^{j}(M)\to t^{j}(N)

for any
j
with
1\leq j<p
, Proposition
1.3
(4)
implies the following additional corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a surjective map of finite dimensional
k[t]/t^{p}
-modules.
Then
JT(N)\leq JT(M)
, with equality if and only if
f
is an isomorphism.

\mathcal{Y}_{\leq}

Definition 1.5

Denote by
\mathcal{Y}_{\leq}

1.2

\mathcal{Y}_{\leq,n}

\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\cdot\ \leq\ n

We equip

(-)\otimes(-):\ \mathcal{Y}_{\leq}\times\mathcal{Y}_{\leq}\quad\to\quad\mathcal{Y}_{\leq}\ ,

where
\underline{a}\otimes\underline{b}
is the

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus c_{i}})\quad\simeq\quad(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus a_{i}})\ \bigotimes\ (\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}(k[t]/t^{i})^{\oplus b_{i}})

Proposition 1.6
.

Consider a
noetherian, local domain
R
with field of fractions
L
and residue field
K
.
Let
M_{R}
be a free
R
-module of finite rank equipped with a
p
-nilpotent
endomorphism
\theta_{R}\in End_{R}(M_{R},M_{R})
. Denote
K\otimes_{R}M
by
M_{K}

and
L\otimes_{R}M
by
M_{L}
, and set

\theta_{K}=K\otimes_{R}\theta_{R}\in End_{K}(M_{K},M_{K}),\quad\theta_{L}=L\otimes_{R}\theta_{R}\in End_{L}(M_{L},M_{L}).

So defined,
M_{K}
is a
K[t]/t^{p}
-module with the action of
t

given by
\theta_{K}
and
M_{L}
is an
L[t]/t^{p}
-module with the action of
t

given by
\theta_{L}
.
We say that the

-module

K[t]/t^{p}
-module

.

For such a specialization,
JT(M_{K})\ \leq\ JT(M_{L})
.

Proof.

By applying Nakayma’s Lemma
[
22

to the cokernels of the
maps
(\theta_{R})^{i}:M_{R}\to M_{R}

we conclude that
dim(coker(\theta_{L}^{i}))\leq dim(coker(\theta_{K}^{i}))

so that
rk(\theta_{K}^{i})\leq rk(\theta_{L}^{i})
for each

. Thus, the proposition
follows from Proposition
1.3
.
∎

Definition 1.7
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k
. For two scheme-theoretic points
x,y

of
X
, we write
x\leq y
if
x
is in the (Zariski) closure of
y
. This endows

If
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits R\subset X

is an affine open subset of
X
containing
x

x\in\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits R

corresponds to the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\subset R

\mathcal{O}_{X}
of

has stalk
the local ring
R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{x})}
with residue field
k(x)=R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{x})}/\mathfrak{p}_{x}R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{x})}

scheme-theoretic point

with the associated map of schemes
\chi_{x}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(x)\to X
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
x,y
be two scheme-theoretic points of
X
. Then
x\leq y
if and only if
there exists a local domain
R
with field of fractions

k(y)
and residue field
k(x)
and a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits R\to X
whose restriction to

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(y)
is
\chi_{y}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(y)\to X
and whose restriction to
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(x)
is

\chi_{x}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(x)\to X
.

Proof.

We may assume
x\not=y
. Let
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits A
be a Zariski open subset of
X
containing
x

with corresponding prime
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\subset A
. Since
x
is in the closure of
y
, the prime ideal

\mathfrak{p}_{y}
is contained in
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
. Set
S
equal to the quotient
A/\mathfrak{p}_{y}
and let

R
be the localization of
S
at (the image of )
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
.
So defined,
R
is a local domain with residue field
k(x)

and field of fractions
k(y)
. Moreover,
\chi_{x}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(x)\to X
and
\chi_{y}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(y)\to X

factor through
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits R\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits S\subset\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits A\subset X
.

Conversely, assume given a local domain
R
with field of fractions

k(y)
and residue field
k(x)
and a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits R\to X
whose restriction to

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(x)
is
\chi_{x}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(x)\to X
and whose restriction to
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(y)
is

\chi_{y}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(y)\to X
. Then the kernel of

R\to k(x)
equals the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
and contains
\mathfrak{p}_{y}
, the kernel of

.
Thus,
x\leq y
.

4.
Exponential Jordan type

In this section, we consider affine group schemes whose 1-parameter subgroups
are given by exponentiating
r

p
-nilpotent, pair-wise commuting elements
of
\mathfrak{g}\equiv Lie(\mathbb{G})
. The more explicit nature of these 1-parameter subgroups
allows us to simplify our Jordan type functions, not necessarily preserving their values
but preserving their close relationship with support varieties.

For an affine group scheme
\mathbb{G}

-tuples of

-nilpotent, pair-wise commuting
elements of
\mathfrak{g}
made explicit in Definition
4.2
.
We say that
\mathbb{G}
is of exponential type of height
r

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g},r}
is a homeomorphism
of scheme-theoretic points. Examples include Frobenius kernels of various classical groups,
their parabolic subgroups, and unipotent subgroups of these parabolic subgroups. (See, for
example,
[
25
]
.)
For
\mathbb{G}
of exponential type of height
r
, Theorem
4.8
introduces
\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},r}\in k[\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\otimes k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
which very closely models
\Theta_{\mathbb{G},r}
.

This leads to our simpler

-nilpotent operator
\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},r}^{exp}\in k[\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\ \otimes k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
for
\mathbb{G}
of exponential type of height

, a
linearization of
\Theta_{g,r}
. A fundamental computation of
[
17
, Thm 1.12]

enables us to conclude that
\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},r}
and

determine the same
support varieties for finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-modules. By adopting the formalism
we have discussed for

, we establish the Jordan type functions

JT_{\mathfrak{g},r,M}(-)
and

which satisfy similar properties.

We begin by defining the class of affine group schemes
\mathbb{G}
for which

-tuples of
p
-nilpotent, pair-wise commuting elements of the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{G}

determine height

\mathbb{G}
.

Definition 4.6

Lemma 4.7

7.
Jordan types for linear algebraic groups

In
[
12
, Defn 4.4]
, we formulated the local Jordan type of a finite dimensional rational
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
for

\mathbb{G}
a linear algebraic group equipped with a structure of exponential type.
There is some subtlety to this construction in order to “package” the Jordan types of
M_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}

of restrictions of
M
to all Frobenius kernels of
\mathbb{G}
. This involves reordering
r
-tuples of
p
-nilpotent,
pairwise commuting elements

(B_{0},\ldots,B_{r-1})
of
\mathfrak{g}
so that they appear as
(B_{r-1},\ldots,B_{0})
.
The definition of the Jordan type of a finite dimensional rational
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
is then formulated
in terms of the action of a colimit of
p
-nilpotent operators, rather than as a colimit of Jordan
types of restrictions
M_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}
of
M
to the Frobenius kernels
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
of
\mathbb{G}
.

In this final section, we observe that this approach leads to Jordan type functions
for finite dimensional rational
\mathbb{G}
-modules
M
provided that

\mathbb{G}
is of “exponential type of infinite height”. In Definition
7.3
, we extend the construction of

JT^{exp}_{\mathfrak{g},r,M}(-):\mathcal{C}_{r}(N_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\ \to\ \mathcal{Y}
of Definition
4.16
for
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-modules
to
JT_{\mathbb{G},\infty,M}^{exp}(-):\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{Y}
for finite dimensional
rational
\mathbb{G}
-modules. As shown in Proposition
7.4
(correcting an error of

[
12
, Prop 4.8]
),
JT_{\mathbb{G},\infty,M}^{exp}(-)
is a twisted stabilization of

JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}}^{exp}(-)
.

We say that an affine group scheme
\mathbb{G}
of finite type over
k
is a linear algebraic group
if
\mathbb{G}
is reduced and irreducible. In what follows,
k\mathbb{G}\ \equiv\varinjlim_{r}k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
, the
“distribution algebra” of
\mathbb{G}
. We define
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))
to be the ind-scheme

\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\ \equiv\ \varinjlim_{r}\mathcal{C}_{r}(N_{p}(\mathfrak{g}));\quad\mathcal{C}_{r}(N_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\to\mathcal{C}_{r+1}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})),

where
(B_{0},\ldots,B_{r-1})\mapsto(B_{0},\ldots,B_{r-1},0).

We define the “coordinate algebra”

k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\quad\equiv\quad\varinjlim_{r}k[\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))].

We define a scheme-theoretic (respectively, geometric) point of
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))
to be an
element in the direct limit of scheme-theoretic (respectively, geometric) points of
\{\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\}
.
We can view such a point
\iota_{x}:\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits K\to\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))
as given by a “continuous” map of
algebras
\iota_{x}^{*}:k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\to K
.

We provide analogues of the constructions given in Construction
4.3
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a linear algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, equipped with a morphism

\mathcal{E}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})\quad\to\quad\mathbb{G}

such that
(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},\ \mathcal{E}_{|r})
constitutes an affine group scheme of exponential
type of height
r
for each
r>0
.
Here,
\mathcal{E}_{|r}:\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\times\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
.
We say that
\mathbb{G}
is of exponential type of infinite height.

For any (geometric) point
B
of
\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
, we denote by

\mathcal{E}_{|r,B}:k(B)\times\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\ \to\ k(B)\otimes\mathbb{G}
the restriction of
\mathcal{E}_{|r}

along
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits k(B)\to\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
.

We define

\mathcal{E}_{\infty|r}\ =\ \mathcal{E}_{|r}\circ(1\times pr_{s}):\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\times\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\ \to\ \mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\times\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})\ \to\ \mathbb{G}_{(r)}

and set

\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{g},\infty|r}\ \equiv\ \mathcal{E}_{\infty|r}\times pr_{\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))}:\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\times\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\ \to\ \mathbb{G}_{(r)}\times\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})).

We define the operator (a formal limit of well defined partial sums)

(7.1.1)

\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},\infty}^{exp}\ \equiv\ \sum_{s\geq 0}(\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{g},\infty|s})_{*}(1\otimes u_{s})\ =\sum_{s\geq 0}\in\ k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\otimes k\mathbb{G}.

As verified in
[
12
, Prop 2.6]
, for any linear algebraic group
(\mathbb{G},\mathcal{E})
of exponential
type of infinite height, any finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
,
any element
m\in M
, and any
B\in\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
, the element

(\mathcal{E}_{|r,B_{s}})_{*}(1\otimes u_{s})
in
k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\otimes k\mathbb{G}
acts trivially on

m
for all sufficiently large
s
.
This justifies the following proposition which should be compared with Definition
4.16
.

Proposition 7.2
.

For any finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
, the formal sum
\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},\infty}^{exp}
of (
7.1.1
)
determines a well defined
p
-nilpotent
k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})]
-linear operator

\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},\infty}^{exp}:k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\otimes M\ \to\ k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]\otimes M.

The specialization at a scheme-theoretic point
\underline{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))
of
\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},\infty}^{exp}

determines
the operator

\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},\infty,\underline{B}}^{exp}\ =\ \sum_{s\geq 0}(\mathcal{E}_{|s,B_{s}})_{*}(1\otimes u_{s-1}):k[\underline{B}]\otimes M\ \to\ k[\underline{B}]\otimes M,

(denoted
\sum_{s\geq 0}(\mathcal{E}_{B_{s}})_{*}(u_{s})
in
[
12
]
).

The following definition of
JT_{\mathbb{G},\infty,M}(\underline{B})
is essentially that of
[
12
, Defn 4.4]
.

Definition 7.3
.

For any finite dimensional
\mathbb{G}
-module
M
,
we define the Jordan type of
M
at
\underline{B}\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
to be

(7.3.1)

JT_{\mathfrak{g},\infty,M}^{exp}(\underline{B})\quad\equiv\quad JT(\sum_{s\geq 0}(\mathcal{E}_{|s,B_{s}})_{*}(1\otimes u_{s}),k(\underline{B})\otimes M),

the Jordan type of the
p
-nilpotent action of
k(\underline{B})\otimes_{k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))]}\Theta_{\mathfrak{g},\infty}^{exp}\in k(\underline{B})\otimes k\mathbb{G}
acting on
k(\underline{B})\otimes M
.

Thus, (
7.3.1
) determines the Jordan type function

JT_{\mathfrak{g},\infty,M}^{exp}(-):\ \mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\quad\to\quad\mathcal{Y}.

We point out that
[
12
, Prop 4.8]
is wrong as stated. The following proposition provides a correction.
The proof of Proposition
7.4
is an immediate consequence of the observation
above that
(\mathcal{E}_{|r,B_{s}})_{*}(1\otimes u_{s})
in
k[\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})]\otimes k\mathbb{G}
acts trivially on
m\in M

for all sufficiently large
s
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Adopt the notation and hypotheses of Definition
7.3
. If
M
is finite dimensional,
then

JT_{\mathbb{G},\infty,M}^{exp}(-)\ =\ JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}}^{exp}(-)\circ\Lambda^{r}:\ \mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\ \to\ \mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\ \to\ \mathcal{Y},\quad r\gg 0,

where
\Lambda^{r}:\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))\to\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathcal{N}_{p}(\mathfrak{g}))
is given by
sending
(B_{0},\ldots,B_{n},\ldots)
to
(B_{r-1},\ldots,B_{0})
.

In particular,
JT_{\mathbb{G},\infty,M}(-)
is continuous.

One can interpret the Proposition
7.4
as saying for
M
a finite dimensional

\mathbb{G}
-module that the Jordan type function
JT_{\mathbb{G},\infty,M}^{exp}(-)
is a twisted stabilization
(with respect to increasing
r
) of the exponential Jordan type functions
JT_{\mathbb{G},r,M_{|\mathbb{G}_{(r)}}}^{exp}(-)

of Definition
4.16
.

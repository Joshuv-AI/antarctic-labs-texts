Happy birthday, dear TSACO: congratulations on the 10-year anniversary of Trauma Surgery & Acute Care Open (TSACO)
Authors: Elliott R Haut
Published: 2026

10 years ago today, Trauma Surgery & Acute Care Open (TSACO) published its first manuscript. The founding editor-in-chief, Dr Timothy Fabian, described the birth of a new journal at a time when open access publishing was rapidly reshaping the landscape.1 The American Association for the Surgery of Trauma (AAST) owned one traditional, subscription journal. But, the organization realized that to stay relevant, it had to join the open access revolution. They partnered with BMJ, launching TSACO on 24 May 2016. Dr Fabian wrote about the future of medical publishing1 and took on this new challenge. For 7 years, he led our fledgling journal through its formative years. He loves the birth analogy and slowly growing from infancy to toddlerhood. That first year, the journal published 33 manuscripts. We have grown that number exponentially ever since.

On 1 January 2023, I had the honor of taking over as editor-in-chief. In my inaugural editorial, I wrote about having big shoes to fill, giving credit where credit was due to Dr Fabian, who propelled us in the right direction.2 The first time I put on those shoes, it was awesome, yet intimidating. They were clunky. They were tight. They gave me blisters. But, after 3 years, I have broken them in and I feel like I am in the groove. I have an amazing editorial team that helps manage manuscripts, curates topic collections, runs our social media, and drives new initiatives. Thanks as well to Kathy Lyons, who shepherded the journal from the start as our BMJ publisher, and to Frank Greenwood and the wider BMJ team for their continued support.

TSACO’s first decade has brought steady growth and several milestones. Early landmarks such as our first 100 papers have given way to larger achievements: 2000 manuscript submissions, publication of our 1000th article, and receipt of our inaugural impact factor (figure 1). One of my early initiatives was to grow our strategic partnerships, which we have done dramatically. Initially, our only formalized collaboration was with the World Coalition for Trauma Care as the official journal of the World Trauma Congress. Our partnerships have now grown to include 10 external partnerships, including the Eastern Association for the Surgery of Trauma, the Mattox Conference, the American College of Surgeons Point Counterpoint Conference, the Health Alliance for Violence Intervention, and more (https://tsaco.bmj.com/pages/trauma-surgery-acute-care-open-partnerships). These relationships have broadened our reach and relevance within the trauma and acute care community.

We have introduced new article types. In collaboration with the American Trauma Society, we launched a patient education series that explains trauma and emergency general surgery conditions to a broad audience in a patient-centered way—brief, accessible pieces intended for patients, families, caregivers, and the public.3 I thank Patrick Reilly for the concept and Joshua Dilday, Kimberly Hendershot, and Matt Martin for moving it forward. We have also formalized invited commentaries to accompany most original research articles, a practice that has been wildly successful in stimulating discussion and contextualizing new findings.

The vision of TSACO remains simple: ‘To provide the global trauma & acute care surgery community with free access to top-notch scientific information’ (figure 2). We pursue this through three primary pathways: (1) grow high-quality research, (2) enhance global communication, and (3) promote diversity, equity, & inclusion. Global engagement remains central to this mission. While most articles come from the USA, our reach and impact are expanding around the world. We have published articles from six continents and countless countries. These include recent papers from Sweden,4 Ukraine,5 South Africa,6 Ethiopia,7 Brazil,8 Pakistan,9 Japan,10 Australia,11 and more. My gratitude goes to global deputy editors Tina Gaarder and Ajai Malhotra, as well as the entire global editorial board, for the work they do on behalf of the journal. I count on them as familiar faces, engaging with local authors, fostering regional connections, and supporting the mission worldwide.

Training and mentorship are two of my personal core values that I emphasize at TSACO. Stephanie Savage leads our peer review mentoring program, which has trained nearly 50 junior faculty to perform independent reviews—building a pipeline of surgeon-scientists, researchers, and public health professionals (https://tsaco.bmj.com/pages/trauma-surgery-acute-care-open-the-american-association-for-the-surgery-of-trauma-mentorship-program). Our social media internship provides trainees with hands-on exposure to the publishing process, teaching them the inner workings of journals, and helps amplify TSACO’s content globally. Thank you to Jeffry Nahmias, Caroline Park, and Ron Barbosa for guiding these efforts.

Moving forward, I am excited to see what the next few years will bring for TSACO. Publishing is at a crossroads and faces important challenges. The open access movement has gained traction, but we are not without hurdles to overcome. The cost of publishing is rising rapidly, and for-profit publishing companies are making exorbitant profits, often off the backs of researchers, authors, and peer reviewers. Article processing charges have skyrocketed and are frequently out of touch with reality and out of reach for many researchers whether they are from low- or middle-income countries (LMICs) or from high-income countries, but without extramural research funding or other financial support.12 13 As an editorial team, we must continue to advocate for an ecosystem of ethical, fair, and affordable publishing models that support equitable participation in scholarly communication.12 14 15 One specific program we have at TSACO is our article processing charge full waiver program, which allows authors from over 80 LMICs to publish for free (https://tsaco.bmj.com/pages/authors#waivers_and_discounts).

Returning to Dr Fabian’s parent-child analogy, every parent knows raising children is hard.16 Every child brings new challenges and adventures, trials and tribulations. But, there is nothing quite like the profoundly rewarding experience of being a parent. At 10 years old, TSACO has developed its own personality. I hope authors and readers see that personality—a commitment to accessibility, rigor, and global relevance—and will join us as we mature into the next phase of our life as a journal. Thank you to the AAST for entrusting me with this role.

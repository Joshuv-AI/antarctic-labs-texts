Generation of singularity categories and infinite injective dimension locus via annihilation of cohomologies
Souvik Dey, Jian Liu, Yuki Mifune, Yuya Otake

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian ring. We establish a close relationship between the strong generation of the singularity category of R R and the nonvanishing of the annihilator of the singularity category of R R . As an application, we prove that the singularity category of R R has a strong generator if and only if the annihilator of the singularity category of R R is nonzero when R R is a Noetherian domain with Krull dimension at most one. We introduce the notion of the co-cohomological annihilator of modules. If the category of finitely generated R R -modules has a strong generator, we show that the infinite injective dimension locus of a finitely generated R R -module M M is closed, with the defining ideal given by the co-cohomological annihilator of M M . Finally, we provide a connection between the existence of an extension generator of the category of finitely generated R R -modules and the finiteness of the Krull dimension of R R .

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. In
[
30
]
,
Iyengar and Takahashi introduced the notion of the cohomological annihilator of a ring
R
, denoted
\CA(R)
; cf.
3.3
. They observed a close relationship between the condition
\CA(R)\neq 0
and the strong generation of the category
\mo(R)
of finitely generated
R
-modules; cf.
2.9
. It is established in
[
30
]
that
\mo(R)
has a strong generator when
R
is a localization of a finitely generated algebra over a field or an equicharacteristic excellent local ring.

For a commutative Noetherian domain
R
, Elagin and Lunts
[
23
]
observed that
\CA(R)\neq 0
if the bounded derived category of
R
, denoted
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
, has a strong generator; cf.
2.10
. Recently, the first author, Lank, and Takahashi
[
19
]
established that the condition
\CA(R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
is equivalent to that
\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a strong generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, which in turn is equivalent to that
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a strong generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. If these conditions hold, they show that
\mo(R)
has a strong generator. As an application, it is proved in
[
19
]
that
\mo(R)
has a strong generator for any quasi-excellent ring
R
with finite Krull dimension.

The
singularity category
of
R
, denoted
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
, was introduced by Buchweitz
[
13
]
and Orlov
[
41
]
as the Verdier quotient of the bounded derived category by the full subcategory of perfect complexes. We are motivated by the natural question:

Question
.

How can the (strong) generation of the singularity category be characterized?

We investigate the above question in Section
4
. It turns out that there is a close connection between the strong generation of the singularity category of
R
and the nonvanishing of the annihilator of the singularity category of
R
, as well as the nonvanishing of the cohomological annihilator of modules.

The
annihilator of the singularity category
of
R
, denoted
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
, is an ideal of
R
consisting of elements in
R
that annihilate all homomorphisms of complexes in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
; cf.
4.1
. The ideal
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
measures the singularity of
R
in the sense that
R
is regular if and only if
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=R
. The annihilator of the singularity category has recently attracted increasing interest and has been studied in
[
24
,
38
,
21
]
.
For a commutative Noetherian domain
R
, if the singularity category of
R
has a strong generator, then the annihilator
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is nonzero; see Corollary
4.3
. It is natural to ask whether the converse holds in general. We prove that if, in addition, the Krull dimension of
R
is less than or equal to one, the converse holds. This is an immediate consequence of
Theorem

1.1
(2).

Theorem 1.1
.

(See
4.17
,
4.25
, and
5.21
)
Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then:

(1)

The following two conditions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(b)

\displaystyle\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})}\sqrt{\ca_{R/\mathfrak{p}}(M)}\neq 0
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Moreover, if
R
has finite Krull dimension, the above are equivalent to
\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
having an extension generator (cf.
5.7
) for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(2)

Assume
R
is non-regular with isolated singularities. Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator.

(b)

R/\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is Artinian.

(c)

R/(\displaystyle\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}(M))
is Artinian.

(d)

\mo(R)
has a point-wise strong generator;
cf.

4.16
.

Theorem
1.1
is the main result in Section
4
regarding the characterization of the (strong) generation of singularity categories. The key input is the notion of the
cohomological annihilator
of a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, denoted
\ca_{R}(M)
, in Section
3
.
The key ingredient in proof of Theorem
1.1
is the observation that
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
coincides with
\displaystyle\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}(M)
; see Corollary
4.3
.
When
R
is, in addition, a Gorenstein local ring, Theorem
1.1
(2) was proved by Bahlekeh, Hakimian, Salarian, and Takahashi
[
5
]
in terms of the cohomological annihilator
\CA(R)
; see Remark
4.18
.

For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, it is known that the finite projective dimension locus of
M
,

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty\},

is an open subset in the Zariski spectrum
\Spec(R)
; see
[
8
]
. However, the same is not true for finite injective dimension locus, even when the module is the ring itself, i.e., even the Gorenstein locus can fail to be open; see
[
40
]
.
For an excellent ring
R
, Greco and Marinari
[
27
]
observed that the Gorenstein locus is open. Takahashi
[
44
]
extended this result by proving that the finite injective dimension locus of a finitely generated module over an excellent ring is open.

In Section
5
, we investigate the
infinite injective dimension locus

\IID(M)
of an
R
-module
M
, which is the complement of the finite injective dimension locus. Namely,

\IID(M)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\infty\}.

We observe in
Theorem

1.2
that the infinite injective dimension locus of a finitely generated module is always closed if
\mo(R)
has an
extension generator
(cf.
5.7
).
For a quasi-excellent ring
R
with finite Krull dimension and a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, it follows from
[
19
]
that
\mo(R)
has a strong generator. Consequently, by Theorem
1.2
,
\IID(M)
is closed. In Section
5
, we introduce the notion of the
co-cohomological annihilator
of an
R
-module
M
, denoted
\coca_{R}(M)
. The following result shows that, under some mild assumptions, this ideal is a defining ideal of the infinite injective dimension locus.

Theorem 1.2
.

(See
5.4
and
5.9
)
Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then:

(1)

If
\mo(R)
has an extension generator, then
\IID(M)
is closed in
\Spec(R)
. Moreover,
\Sing(R)=\IID(G)
holds for each extension generator
G
of
\mo(R)
.

(2)

If
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, then

\IID(M)=V(\coca_{R}(M)).

We also compare the cohomological annihilator with the co-cohomological annihilator of a finitely generated module
M
. It turns out that these annihilators coincide when
R
is Gorenstein with finite Krull dimension and
M
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay; see Lemma
5.22
.

Our third result, Theorem
1.3
, establishes a connection between the existence of an extension generator of the module category and the finiteness of the Krull dimension. Moreover, Theorem
1.3
(2) extends a recent result of Araya, Iima, and Takahashi
[
1
]
on the generation of syzygy modules out of a single module by only taking direct summands and extensions; see Remark
5.18
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(See
5.17
and
5.20
)
Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then:

(1)

If
\mo(R)
has an extension generator, then the Krull dimension of
R
is finite.

(2)

If the Krull dimension of
R
is finite and the singular locus of
R
is a finite set, then
\mo(R)
has an extension generator.

2.
Notation and Terminology

Throughout this article,
R
will be a commutative Noetherian ring. For each
R
-module
M
, let
\pd_{R}(M)
denote the projective dimension of
M
over
R
, and
\id_{R}(M)
denote the injective dimension of
M
over
R
. We write
\mo(R)
to be the category of finitely generated
R
-modules. Let
\dim(R)
denote the Krull dimension of
R
, and
\gldim(R)
denote the global homological dimension of
R
.

2.1
.

(Strongly) Gorenstein rings.
A commutative Noetherian ring
R
is called
strongly Gorenstein
provided that
\id_{R}(R)<\infty
. For instance, regular local rings and complete intersection rings are both examples of strongly Gorenstein rings.

A commutative Noetherian ring
R
is said to be
Gorenstein
provided that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is strongly Gorenstein (i.e.,
\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
) for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. Note that a commutative Noetherian local ring is Gorenstein if it is strongly Gorenstein.

For a Gorenstein ring
R
,
\id_{R}(R)=\dim(R)
; this can be proved by combining
[
12
, Theorem 3.1.17]
and
[
7
, Corollary 2.3]
.
In particular, a commutative Noetherian ring
R
is strongly Gorenstein if and only if it is Gorenstein with finite Krull dimension.

2.2
.

Regular rings.

A commutative Noetherian local ring is called
regular
if its maximal ideal can be generated by a system of parameter. Auslander, Buchsbaum, and Serre observed that a commutative Noetherian local ring is regular if and only if its global homological dimension is finite; see
[
12
, Theorem 2.2.7]
.

A commutative Noetherian ring
R
is called regular if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. Furthermore, a commutative Noetherian ring is regular if and only if every finitely generated module has finite projective dimension; see
[
8
, Lemma 4.5]
for the forward direction, and the backward direction follows from the criterion of Auslander, Buchsbaum, and Serre.

For a regular ring,
\gldim(R)=\dim(R)
; see
[
37
, Theorem 5.94]
. In particular, a commutative Noetherian ring is regular with finite Krull dimension if and only if
\gldim(R)
is finite.

2.3
.

Annihilator and support of modules.

For each
R
-module
M
, let
\ann_{R}(M)
denote the
annihilator
of
M
over
R
. That is,
\ann_{R}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{r\in R\mid r\cdot M=0\}
.

The set of all prime ideals of
R
is denoted by
\Spec(R)
. It is endowed with the Zariski topology; the closed subset in this topology is of the form
V(I)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\mathfrak{p}\supseteq I\}
for each ideal
I
of
R
.

For each
R
-module
M
, the
support
of
M
is

\Supp_{R}M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\},

where
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the localization of
M
at
\mathfrak{p}
. Note that
\Supp_{R}(M)\subseteq V(\ann_{R}(M))
; the equality holds if, in addition,
M
is finitely generated.

2.4
.

Syzygy modules.
For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
and
n\geq 1
, we let
\Omega^{n}_{R}(M)
denote the
n
-th syzygy of
M
. That is, there is a long exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Omega^{n}_{R}(M)\rightarrow P^{-(n-1)}\rightarrow\cdots P^{-1}\rightarrow P^{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

where
P^{-i}
are finitely generated projective
R
-modules for
0\leq i\leq n-1
. By Schanuel’s Lemma,
\Omega^{n}_{R}(M)
is independent of the choice of the projective resolution of
M
up to projective summands. By convention,
\Omega^{0}_{R}(M)=M
for each finitely generated
R
-module
M
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a full subcategory of
\mo(R)
, we denote

\Omega^{n}_{R}(\mathcal{C})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\Omega^{n}_{R}(M)\mid M\in\mathcal{C}\}.

We say a finitely generated
R
-module
M
is an
infinite syzygy
if there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow Q^{0}\rightarrow Q^{1}\rightarrow Q^{2}\rightarrow\cdots,

where
Q^{i}
are finitely generated projective
R
-modules for
i\geq 0
.

2.5
.

Maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules.
For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, it is said to be
maximal Cohen–Macaulay
provided that
\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, where
\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
represents the depth of
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
; see details in
[
12
]
. Let
\CM(R)
denote the full subcategory of
\mo(R)
consisting of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
R
-modules. A commutative Noetherian ring is
Cohen–Macaulay
if
R
is in
\CM(R)
.

Assume
R
is a Gorenstein ring. Note that a finitely generated
R
-module
M
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0
for all
i>0
; this can be proved by using Ischebeck’s formula (see
[
12
, Exercise 3.1.24]
). Then it follows from
[
13
, Theorem B.1.6]
that, for each
M\in\mo(R)
,
\Omega^{s}_{R}(M)
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay for some
s\geq 0
. On the other hand, any maximal Cohen–Macaulay
R
-module is an infinite syzygy; see
[
13
, Theorem B.1.3]
.

2.6
.

Thick subcategories of Abelian categories.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an Abelian category. A full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{A}
is called
thick
if
\mathcal{C}
is closed under direct summands, and it contains objects that fit into a short exact sequence
such that the other two objects are in
\mathcal{C}
.

For each object
X
in
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}(X)
denote the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
containing
X
; the notation
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}
will have no confusion with
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}
in
2.7
for a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
. This can be constructed inductively; it is an analogous construction of a thick subcategory in a triangulated category (see
2.7
).

Set
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}^{0}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{0\}
. Let
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(X)
be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of objects which are direct summands of any finite copies of
X
. For
n\geq 2
, let
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(X)
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of direct summands of any object in
\mathcal{A}
that fits into a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow Y_{1}\rightarrow Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{3}\rightarrow 0,

where the other two objects satisfying: one is in
\thick^{n-1}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)
, and the other one is in
\thick^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)
. Note that
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}(X)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\thick_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(X).

2.7
.

Thick subcategories of triangulated categories.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with a suspension functor
[1]
. A full category
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}
is called
thick
if it is closed under suspensions, cones, and direct summands.

For each object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
, let
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
denote the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
X
.
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
can be constructed inductively as below; see
[
3
, Section 2]
for more details.

First,
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}^{0}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{0\}
. Let
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(X)
be the smallest full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
X
, and it is closed under suspensions, finite direct sums, and direct summands. Inductively,
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(X)
is denoted to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of objects
Y
in
\mathcal{T}
that appear in an exact triangle

Y_{1}\rightarrow Y\oplus Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{1}[1],

where
Y_{1}\in\thick^{n-1}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
and
Y_{2}\in\thick_{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(X)
.
Note that
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}(X)=\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{n\geq 0}\thick_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(X).

2.8
.

Derived categories and singularity categories.

Let
\mathsf{D}(R)
denote the derived category of
R
-modules. This is a triangulated category with a suspension functor
[1]
; for each complex
X
,
(X[1])^{i}=X^{i+1}
and
\partial_{X[1]}=-\partial_{X}
.
The
bounded derived category
, denoted
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
, is the full subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(R)
consisting of complexes
X
such that its total cohomology, denoted
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\h^{i}(X)
, is finitely generated over
R
; note that
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
is a thick subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(R)
.

For each complex
X
in
\mathsf{D}(R)
, it is called
perfect
if
X\in\thick_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(R)
. It turns out that a complex in
\mathsf{D}(R)
is perfect if and only if it is isomorphic to a bounded complex of finitely generated projective
R
-modules; this can be proved by using
[
13
, Lemma 1.2.1]
. Note that
\thick_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
.

The
singularity category
of
R
is defined to be the Verdier quotient

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)/\thick_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(R).

This category was introduced by Buchweitz
[
13
, Definition 1.2.2]
under the name “stable derived category” and later also found by Orlov
[
41
, 1.2]
. This category detects the singularity of
R
in the sense that
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is trivial if and only if
R
is regular.

The following definition of “strong generator” in
2.9
differs from that of Iyengar and Takahashi
[
30
, 4.3]
. However, for a commutative Noetherian ring
R
,
\mo(R)
has a strong generator in the sense of
2.9
if and only if
\mo(R)
has a strong generator in the sense of Iyengar and Takahashi; see
[
30
, Corollary 4.6]
.

2.9
.

(Strong) generators of Abelian categories.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an Abelian category and
G
be an object in
\mathcal{A}
. The object
G
is called a
generator
of
\mathcal{A}
if
\thick_{\mathcal{A}}(G)=\mathcal{A}
, and
G
is called a
strong generator
of
\mathcal{A}
if
\thick^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}(G)=\mathcal{A}
for some
n\geq 0
.

For example, if
R
is an Artinian ring
R
,
R/J(R)
is a strong generator of
\mo(R)
, where
J(R)
is the Jacobson radical of
R
. Moreover,
\mo(R)=\thick^{\ell\ell(R)}_{\mo(R)}(R/J(R))
, where
\ell\ell(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\rm inf}\{n\geq 0\mid J(R)^{n}=0\}
is the Loewy length of
R
.

2.10
.

(Strong) generators of triangulated categories.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
G
be an object in
\mathcal{T}
. The object
G
is called a
generator
of
\mathcal{T}
if
\thick_{\mathcal{T}}(G)=\mathcal{T}
, and
G
is called a
strong generator
of
\mathcal{T}
if
\thick^{n}_{\mathcal{T}}(G)=\mathcal{T}
for some
n\geq 0
; see details in
[
10
]
and
[
42
]
. Note that
\mathcal{T}
has a strong generator if and only if the Rouquier dimension (see
[
42
]
) of
\mathcal{T}
is finite.

If
R
is an Artinian ring, then
R/J(R)
is a strong generator of
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
; indeed,
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick^{\ell\ell(R)}_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(R/J(R))
by
[
42
, Proposition 7.37]
.

We end this section by recording the (strong) generators of the regular rings.

2.11
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Note that, for each
G\in\mo(R)
,
G
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
if
G
is a generator of
\mo(R)
. Combining with this, the following are equivalent.

(1)

R
is regular.

(2)

R
is a generator of
\mo(R)
.

(3)

R
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
.

2.12
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
G
be a module in
\mo(R)
.
If
\mo(R)=\thick_{\mo(R)}^{n}(G)
, then
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}(R)}^{2n}(G)
by
[
30
, Lemma 7.1]
. In particular,
G
is a strong generator of
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
if
G
is a strong generator of
\mo(R)
. This yields
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
below. Indeed, all the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

R
is regular with
\dim(R)<\infty
, equivalently
\gldim(R)<\infty
(see
2.2
).

(2)

R
is a strong generator of
\mo(R)
.

(3)

R
is a strong generator of
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: if
\gldim(R)=d
is finite, then
\mo(R)=\thick_{\mo(R)}^{d+1}(R)
.
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: if
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick^{n}_{\mathsf{D}(R)}(R)
, then
[
14
, Proposition 4.5 and 4.6]
yields that
\pd_{R}(M)\leq n-1
for each
M\in\mo(R)
, and hence
\gldim(R)\leq n-1
.

Moreover, if the above conditions hold, then
\mo(R)=\thick_{\mo(R)}^{d+1}(R)
and
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}(R)}^{d+1}(R)
(see
[
14
, Theorem 8.3]
), where
d=\dim(R)
.

3.
Cohomological annihilators for modules

The following definition of cohomological annihilators for modules is inspired by the definition of cohomological annihilators for rings introduced by Iyengar and Takahashi
[
30
]
; see their definition in
3.3
. For a subset
I\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and full subcategories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
of
\mo(R)
, set
\Ext^{I}_{R}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\bigoplus\limits_{M\in\mathcal{C},N\in\mathcal{D}}\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N)
.

3.1
.

For each finitely generated
R
-module
M
and
n\geq 0
, we define the
n
-th
cohomological annihilator
of
M
to be

\ca_{R}^{n}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ann_{R}\Ext_{R}^{\geq n}(M,\mo(R)).

That is,
\ca^{n}_{R}(M)
consists of elements
r\in R
such that
r\cdot\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N)=0
for each
i\geq n
and
N\in\mo(R)
. Consider the ascending chain of ideals

\ann_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}^{0}(M)\subseteq\ca_{R}^{1}(M)\subseteq\ca_{R}^{2}(M)\subseteq\cdots,

the cohomological annihilator of
M
is defined to be the union of these ideals

\ca_{R}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\ca_{R}^{n}(M).

Since
R
is Noetherian,
\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}^{n}(M)
for
n\gg 0
.

Note that
\ca_{R}^{n}(M)=R
if and only if
\pd_{R}(M)<n
. Thus,
\ca_{R}(M)=R
if and only if
\pd_{R}(M)<\infty
.

3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a full subcategory of
\mo(R)
, Bahlekeh, Salarian, Takahashi, and Toosi
[
6
, Definition 5.7]
introduced the concept of
cohomological annihilator
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted
\ca_{R}(\mathcal{C})
. It is defined as

\ca_{R}(\mathcal{C})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcap_{X\in\mathcal{C}}\ca_{R}(X).

By definition, if
\mathcal{C}
consists of only one module
X
, then
\ca_{R}(\mathcal{C})=\ca_{R}(X)
.

3.3
.

For each
n\geq 0
, following
[
30
, Definition 2.1]
, the
n
-th
cohomological annihilator
of the ring
R
is defined to be

\CA^{n}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ann_{R}\Ext_{R}^{\geq n}(\mo(R),\mo(R)).

By definition,
\CA^{n}(R)=\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}^{n}(M)
and there is an ascending chain of ideals

0=\CA^{0}(R)\subseteq\CA^{1}(R)\subseteq\CA^{2}(R)\subseteq\cdots.

The cohomological annihilator of the ring
R
is defined to be the ideal

\CA(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\CA^{n}(R).

Also,
\CA(R)=\CA^{n}(R)
for
n\gg 0
as
R
is Noetherian.

Example 3.4
.

Let
R
be an equicharacteristic complete Cohen–Macaulay local ring with Krull dimension
d
. Wang
[
46
, Theorem 5.3]
observed that
\CA^{d+1}(R)
contains the Jacobian ideal of
R
; see also
[
32
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

(1)
\CA(R)\subseteq\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
.

(2)
\ca_{R}(\mo(R))=R
if and only if
R
is regular.

(3)
\CA(R)=R
if and only if
R
is regular with finite Krull dimension.

Proof.

(1) For each
n\geq 0
, we have

\CA^{n}(R)=\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}^{n}(M)\subseteq\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R)).

Thus,
\CA(R)\subseteq\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
as
\CA(R)=\CA^{n}(R)
for
n\gg 0
.

(2) By definition,
\ca_{R}(\mo(R))=R
if and only if
\ca_{R}(M)=R
for each
M\in\mo(R)
. This is equivalent to
\pd_{R}(M)<\infty
for each
M\in\mo(R)
; see
3.1
. This is equivalent to that
R
is regular; see
2.2
.

(3) By
[
30
, Example 2.5]
,
\CA(R)=R
if and only if
\gldim(R)<\infty
. The desired result now follows immediately from
2.2
.
∎

In general,
\CA(R)\neq\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
; see the example below. They are equal if, in addition,
R
is strongly Gorenstein; see Proposition
3.13
.

Example 3.6
.

The inclusion in Lemma
3.5
(1) can be proper. In
[
39
, Appendix, Example 1]
, Nagata constructed a commutative Noetherian regular ring
R
with infinite Krull dimension. In this case,
\CA(R)\subsetneqq\ca_{R}(\mo(R))=R
by Lemma
3.5
.

3.7
.

Let
M,N
be
R
-modules. If
M
is finitely generated, then it follows from
[
47
, Proposition 3.3.10]
that there is a natural isomorphism

\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,N)_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\Ext^{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})

for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
and
n\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
n\geq 1
. Then:

(1)

\ca_{R}^{n}(M)=\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M))=\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(\Omega^{n-1}_{R}(M))
.

(2)

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n\}=V(\ca_{R}^{n}(M))=\Supp_{R}\Ext_{R}^{n}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M))
.

(3)

\ca_{R}^{n}(M)=\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\mo(R))
.

(4)

\ca_{R}^{n+i}(M)=\ca_{R}^{n}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M))
for each
i\geq 1
. Hence,
\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M))
for each
i\geq 1
.

(5)

Let
S
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
R
. Then
S^{-1}\ca^{n}_{R}(M)=\ca^{n}_{S^{-1}R}(S^{-1}M)
. In particular,

S^{-1}\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{S^{-1}R}(S^{-1}M)
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from
[
30
, Lemma 2.14]
and
[
22
, Lemma 3.8]
.

(2) Combining with
3.7
,
\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M))
.
Since
\Ext_{R}^{n}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M))
is finitely generated over
R
,

\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M))=V(\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M)).

Combining this with (1), the desired statement follows.

(3) It is clear that

\ca_{R}^{n}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\mo(R))\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M)).

By (1), these inclusions are equal.

(4) For each
X\in\mo(R)
, consider the isomorphism

\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Omega_{R}^{1}(M),X)\cong\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(M,X).

Combining with (3), we have
\ca_{R}^{n+1}(M)=\ca_{R}^{n}(\Omega^{1}_{R}(M))
. Then the first statement of (4) can be obtained by induction on the number
i
. Thus, for each
i\geq 1
,

\ca_{R}(M)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\ca_{R}^{n+i}(M)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\ca_{R}^{n}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M))=\ca_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)).

This completes the proof.

(5) The second statement follows from the first one. For the first statement, combining with (4), it suffices to prove the case of
n=1
. By (1), we get an inclusion
S^{-1}\ca^{1}_{R}(M)\subseteq\ca^{1}_{S^{-1}R}(S^{-1}M)
. For the converse, assume
r/s\in\ca^{1}_{S^{-1}R}(S^{-1}M)
, where
s\in S
and
r\in R
. It remains to prove that
r/s\in S^{-1}\ca^{1}_{R}(M)

r/s\colon S^{-1}M\rightarrow S^{-1}M
factors through a finitely generated projective
S^{-1}R
-module, and hence
r/s\colon S^{-1}M\rightarrow S^{-1}M
factors through a finitely generated free
S^{-1}R
-module. Combining with the fact that the isomorphism

3.9
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.

(1) Following
[
45
, Definition 2.9]
, the
infinite projective dimension locus
of
M
is defined to be

\IPD(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\infty\}.

(2)
\sup\{\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\notin\IPD(M)\}
is finite. Indeed, it follows from the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 4.5]
that

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty\}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq n\}

for some
n\geq 0
, and hence
\sup\{\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\notin\IPD(M)\}\leq n
; see also
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then

\IPD(M)=V(\ca_{R}(M))=V(\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M)),

where
d=\sup\{\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\notin\IPD(M)\}
.

Proof.

The first equality follows from the following:

\displaystyle\IPD(M)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,\Omega^{n}_{R}(M))

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}V(\ca_{R}^{n}(M))

\displaystyle=V(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\ca_{R}^{n}(M))

\displaystyle=V(\ca_{R}(M)),

where the first two equalities follow from Lemma
3.8
; the first equality also follows directly from the proof of
[
31
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Next, we prove the second equality. The inclusion
V(\ca_{R}(M))\subseteq V(\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M))
is clear. Combining with the first equality, it remains to prove
V(\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M))\subseteq\IPD(M)
. If not, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal containing
\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M)
and
\mathfrak{p}\notin\IPD(M)
. By assumption,
\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq d
. However, it follows from Lemma
3.8
that
\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq d+1
. This is a contradiction.
∎

Recall that
\CM(R)
is denoted to be the category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
R
-modules (see
2.5
). Set

\OCM^{\times}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{M\in\Omega^{1}_{R}(\CM(R))\mid M\text{ has no nonzero projective summands}\};

see the definition of
\Omega^{1}_{R}(\CM(R))
in
2.4
. Next, we calculate the uniform degree of the cohomological annihilators. It turns out that the uniform degree behaves well when the ring is strongly Gorenstein; see Proposition
3.13
.

Lemma 3.11
.

(1) Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0
for all
0\leq m\leq i\leq n
, then

\ca^{m}_{R}(M)=\cdots=\ca^{n+1}_{R}(M)

(2) If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a
d
-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. If
\OCM^{\times}(R)
is closed under
\Hom_{R}(-,\omega)
, then

\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)

for each
M\in\mo(R)
. In particular,

\CA^{d+1}(R)=\CA(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R)).

Proof.

(1) For each
j\geq 0
, consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Omega_{R}^{j+1}(M)\rightarrow P\rightarrow\Omega_{R}^{j}(M)\rightarrow 0,

where
P
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module. Applying
\Hom_{R}(M,-)
to this short exact sequence, we get an exact sequence

\Ext^{j}_{R}(M,P)\to\Ext^{j}_{R}(M,\Omega^{j}_{R}(M))\to\Ext^{j+1}_{R}(M,\Omega^{j+1}_{R}(M)).

Set
j=m
. The assumption yields that
\Ext^{m}_{R}(M,P)=0
. Thus, the above exact sequence yields that
\ca^{m+1}_{R}(M)\subseteq\ca^{m}_{R}(M)
(see Lemma
3.8
), and hence
\ca^{m}_{R}(M)=\ca^{m+1}_{R}(M)
. The desired result follows similarly by choosing
j=m+1,\ldots,n
.

(2) For each
M\in\mo(R)
and
i\geq d
,
\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)
is in
\CM(R)
. Choose a minimal free resolution of
\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)
:

0\rightarrow\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M)\rightarrow F\rightarrow\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)\rightarrow 0,

where
F
is a finitely generated free
R
-module. In this case,
\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M)
does not have a nonzero projective summand (see
[
28
, Lemma 1.4]
), and hence
\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M)\in\OCM^{\times}(R)
.
By assumption,
\Hom_{R}(\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M),\omega)\in\OCM(R)
. It follows from
[
36
, Proposition 5.2]
that there exists a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\omega^{\oplus s}\rightarrow\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M)\rightarrow 0

in
\mo(R)
.
Note that
\Ext^{>0}_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M),\omega)=0
as
\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)\in\CM(R)
. Applying
\Hom_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M),-)
to the above short exact sequence,

\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M),\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M))\cong\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M),K).

We conclude from this that
\Ext^{i+1}_{R}(M,\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M))\cong\Ext^{i+2}_{R}(M,K)
.
This isomorphism implies

\ca_{R}^{i+2}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{i+1}_{R}(M,\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M))=\ca_{R}^{i+1}(M),

where the second equality follows from Lemma
3.8
. Hence,
\ca_{R}^{i+1}(M)=\ca_{R}^{i+2}(M)
for each
i\geq d
. This implies
\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M)
.

For the second statement, it follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\CA^{d+1}(R)\subseteq\CA(R)\subseteq\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
. The desired result now follows from the following:

\CA^{d+1}(R)=\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}^{d+1}(M)=\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R)),

This finishes the proof.
∎

3.12
.

For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, let
\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)
denote the
(large) restricted flat dimension
of
M
. That is,

\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)=\sup\{\depth(R_{\mathfrak{p}})-\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\}.

By
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
with
[
15
, Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 2.4]
,

\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{-\infty\}
, and
\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)=-\infty
if and only if
M=0
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring. Then:

(1)

For each
M\in\CM(R)
,
\ca_{R}^{1}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
.

(2)

For each nonzero finitely generated
R
-module
M
,
\ca_{R}^{l+1}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
, where
l=\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)
.

(3)

If, in addition,
R
has finite Krull dimension
d
, then there are equalities

\CA^{d+1}(R)=\CA(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R))=\bigcap_{M\in\CM(R)}\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M).

Proof.

(1) For each
M\in\CM(R)
, we have
\Ext^{>0}_{R}(M,R)=0
; see
2.5
. The desired result now follows from Lemma
3.11
.

(2) Note that
3.12
yields that
l=\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)
is finite. By
[
12
, 1.3.7]
,
\Omega^{l}_{R}(M)
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over
R
.
Let
i\geq 1
be an integer. By Lemma
3.8
, we have

\ca_{R}^{l+i}(M)=\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(\Omega^{l+i-1}_{R}(M)).

Note that
\underline{\End}_{R}(\Omega^{l+i-1}_{R}(M))\cong\underline{\End}_{R}(\Omega^{l}_{R}(M))
as
\Omega^{l}_{R}(M)\in\CM(R)
; see
[
13
, Theorem B.1.8]
. Thus,
\ca_{R}^{l+i}(M)=\ca_{R}^{l+1}(M)
, and hence
\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}^{l+1}(M)
.

(3) In
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
, Esentepe observed that
\CA(R)=\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{M\in\CM(R)}\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M).

By the same argument in the proof of Lemma
3.11
, we have
\CA^{d+1}(R)=\CA(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

(2) For a finitely generated

\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)=\inf\{m\geq 0\mid\ca^{m+1}_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)\}.

Since

is Noetherian,
\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)
is always finite. For instance, if
\pd_{R}(M)
is finite, then
\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)=\pd_{R}(M)
. For a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mo(R)
, we set
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mathcal{C})=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)\mid M\in\mathcal{C}\}
. If
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mathcal{C})<\infty
, then
\ca_{R}(\mathcal{C})=\displaystyle\bigcap_{M\in\mathcal{C}}\ca_{R}(M)=\displaystyle\bigcap_{M\in\mathcal{C}}\ca^{k+1}_{R}(M)
for each
k\geq\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mathcal{C})
. Hence, if
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))<\infty
is finite, then

\ca_{R}(\mo(R))=\bigcup_{k\geq\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))}(\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca^{k+1}_{R}(M))=\mathfrak{ca}(R).

Note that the ring
R
, under the assumption of Lemma
3.11
(2) or Proposition
3.13
(2), satisfies
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))
is finite.

(3) Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative Noetherian local ring with
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=0
. For each
M\in\mo(R)
, the assumption that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=0
implies that
\Omega_{R}^{1}(M)
is a direct sum of some finite copies of
R
and
k
. By Lemma
3.8
(1), we conclude that
\ca^{2}_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
for each
M\in\mo(R)
, and hence
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))\leq 1
.

Let
\Max(R)
denote the subset of
\Spec(R)
consisting of all maximal ideals of
R
. In view of Remark
3.14
(2), the following result may be regarded as a version of the local-global principle for projective dimensions.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then:

(1)

For each
M\in\mo(R)
,
\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\}=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)\}
. In particular,
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}}))\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\}=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{m}}))\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)\}
.

(2)

\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))\geq\dim(R)-1
. In particular, if
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))
is finite, then
R
has finite Krull dimension.

Proof.

(1) The second statement follows from the first one and the fact that the localization functor
\mo(R)\rightarrow\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is dense for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
. For the first statement, let
n=\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)+1
. It follows that
\ca^{n}_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
. By Lemma
3.8
(5), for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
, we have

\ca^{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\ca_{R}^{n}(M)_{\mathfrak{p}}=\ca_{R}(M)_{\mathfrak{p}}=\ca_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}).

It follows form this that
\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)\geq\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\}\geq\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)\}
. Assume
m=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)\}+1
. Again by Lemma
3.8
(5), we conclude that the localization of the inclusion
\ca^{m}_{R}(M)\subseteq\ca_{R}(M)
at each maximal ideal of
R
is equal, and hence
\ca^{m}_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
. It follows from this that
\operatorname{n}_{R}(M)\leq m=\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)\}
.

(2) Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
, and let
J
be an ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
generated by a maximal regular sequence of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
J
has finite projective dimension over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we obtain the first equality below

\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/J)=\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/J)=\depth(R_{\mathfrak{p}})

where the second equality is by our choice of
J
.
Combining with (1), we conclude that

\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))\geq\sup\{\depth(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\}\geq\dim(R)-1,

where the second inequality is by
[
15
, Lemma 1.4]
.
∎

4.
(Strong) generation of singularity categories

In this section, we investigate the (strong) generation of the singularity category. The main result of this section is Theorem
1.1
from the introduction; see Theorems
4.17
and
4.25
.

4.1
.

Let
X
be a complex in the singularity category
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
. The
annihilator
of
X
over
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
, denoted
\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X)
, is defined to be the annihilator of
\Hom_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X,X)
over
R
. That is,

\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{r\in R\mid r\cdot\Hom_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X,X)=0\}.

The annihilator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is defined to be

\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcap_{X\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X);

see
[
38
]
for more details about the annihilator of the singularity category.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then

\ca_{R}(M)=\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M).

Proof.

First, we prove the
\ca_{R}(M)\subseteq\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
. Let
r\in\ca_{R}^{1}(M)
. Note that
\ca_{R}^{1}(M)=\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)
; see Lemma
3.8
. Thus, the multiplication map
r\colon M\rightarrow M
factors through a projective
R
-module. In particular, the multiplication map
r\colon M\rightarrow M
in the singularity category
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is zero. This yields that
r\in\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
, and hence
\ca_{R}^{1}(M)\subseteq\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
.
For each
n>1
,

\displaystyle\ca_{R}^{n}(M)=\ca_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{n-1}_{R}(M))\subseteq\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(\Omega^{n-1}_{R}(M)),

where the equality is by Lemma
3.8
, and the inclusion follows from the argument above. By the isomorphism
\Omega^{n-1}_{R}(M)[n-1]\cong M
, we have

\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(\Omega^{n-1}_{R}(M))=\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M).

Thus,
\ca_{R}^{n}(M)\subseteq\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
for all

, and hence
\ca_{R}(M)\subseteq\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M).

Now, we prove the converse direction. Assume

. That is, the multiplication map

in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is zero. This implies the multiplication map

in

factors through a perfect complex

. That is, in

, there is a commutative diagram

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then

\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R)).

Proof.

Let
X
be a complex in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
. By choosing a projective resolution of
X
, we may assume
X
is a bounded
above complex of finitely generated projective
R
-modules with finitely many nonzero
cohomologies. Then by taking brutal truncation, we conclude that
X[n]
is isomorphic
to a finitely generated
R
-module in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
for
n\ll 0
. Assume
X[n]\cong M
for some
n\ll 0
and
M\in\mo(R)
. Note that
\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X)=\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
. This yields the second equality below:

\displaystyle\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{X\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(X)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\ca_{R}(M),

where the third equality follows from Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Let
\Sing(R)
denote the

of

. That is,
\Sing(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid R_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is not regular}\}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For each finitely generated
R
-module

,
V(\ca_{R}(M))\subseteq\Sing(R).
The equality holds if, in addition,
M
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition

that

. It is clear that

By
[

, the equality holds if

.
∎

4.5
.

For each
X\in\mo(R)
, Dao and Takahashi
[
17
, Defintion 5.1]
introduced a construction of an ascending chain of full subcategories built out of
X
as follows:

Set
|X|_{0}^{R}=\{0\}
, and denote
|X|_{1}^{R}
to be the full subcategory of
\mo(R)
consisting of modules which are direct summands of any finite copies of
X
. Inductively, for
n\geq 2
, denote
|X|_{n}^{R}
to be the full subcategory of
\mo(R)
consisting of modules
Y
that appear in a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow Y_{1}\rightarrow Y\oplus Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y_{2}\rightarrow 0,

where
Y_{1}\in|X|_{n-1}^{R}
and
Y_{2}\in|X|_{1}^{R}
. If there is no confusion, we will use
|X|_{n}
to denote
|X|_{n}^{R}
.

In
[
30
, Corollary 4.6]
, Iyengar and Takahashi observed that
\mo(R)
has a strong generator if and only if there exist
s,t\geq 0
and
G\in\mo(R)
such that

\Omega^{s}_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq|G|_{t}.

4.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. Assume there exist
s,t\geq 0
and
G\in\mo(R)
such that
\Omega^{s}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{i})\subseteq[G]_{t}^{R}
for all
i\geq 0
; see the definition of
[G]_{t}^{R}
(i.e., the
ball of radius

t

centered at

G
) in
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
. With the same argument in the proof of
[
43
, Proposition 3.2]
, one has
\dim(R)\leq s
.

The following proposition characterizes the Krull dimension of
R
in terms of strong generation of
\mo(R)
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring.
Then:

\dim(R)\leq\inf\{s\geq 0\mid\text{there exist }t\geq 0,~G\in\mo(R)\text{ such that }\Omega^{s}_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq|G|_{t}^{R}\}.

In particular, if
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, then
\dim(R)
is finite.
In addition, if
R
is quasi-excellent and either admits a dualizing complex or is a local ring, then the equality holds.

Proof.

Let
s,t
be nonnegative integers and
G
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Assume
\Omega^{s}_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq|G|_{t}^{R}
.
Since
|G|_{t}^{R}\subseteq[G]_{t}^{R}
,
\Omega_{R}^{s}(\mo(R))\subseteq{[G]}_{t}^{R}
; c.f.
4.6
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
. We claim that

\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{s}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}}))\subseteq{[G_{\mathfrak{p}}]}_{t}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

Combining with this,
4.6
yields that
\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq s
, and hence
\dim(R)\leq s
as
\mathfrak{p}
is arbitrary.

Now, we proceed to prove the claim.
Let
X
be an object in
\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{s}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}}))
. Since the functor
(-)_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mo(R)\to\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is dense, there exists
M\in\mo(R)
such that
X\cong\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{s}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
. On the other hand, there exist
n,m\geq 0
such that
\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{s}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\oplus n}\cong\left(\Omega_{R}^{s}(M)\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\oplus m}
. Hence,

X\oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\oplus n}\cong\left(\Omega_{R}^{s}(M)\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\oplus m}\cong\left(\Omega_{R}^{s}(M)\oplus R^{\oplus m}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\left(\Omega_{R}^{s}(\mo(R))\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq{[G_{\mathfrak{p}}]}_{t}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}};

the inclusion here uses

\left(\Omega_{R}^{s}(\mo(R))\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq([G]_{t}^{R})_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq{[G_{\mathfrak{p}}]}_{t}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}.
As
{[G_{\mathfrak{p}}]}_{t}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is closed under direct summands, one has
X\in{[G_{\mathfrak{p}}]}_{t}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

Suppose that
R
is a quasi-excellent ring of finite Krull dimension
d
and either admits a dualizing complex or is a local ring.
Combining
[
30
, Theorem 5.2]
and
[
34
, Corollary 2.6]
, there exist
t\geq 0
and
G\in\mo(R)
such that
\Omega^{d}_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq|G|_{t}^{R}
. This implies that the converse of the inequality stated in the proposition holds.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Let

be a commutative Noetherian ring, and let
\Max(R)
denote the set of all maximal ideals of

.
By a similar argument as in the previous proposition, the following three values coincide.

(1)

The Krull dimension of

.

(2)

in (3) below was established by Iyengar and Takahashi

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then:

(1)

If
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a generator, then there exists
M\in\mo(R)
such that
M
is generator in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
. Moreover, for any
M\in\mo(R)
which is a generator in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
,

\Sing(R)=V(\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M))=V(\ca_{R}(M))=\IPD(M).

(2)

If
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator, then

\Sing(R)=V(\ann_{R}{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)})=V(\ca_{R}(\mo(R))).

(3)

If
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, then

\Sing(R)=V(\CA(R))=V(\ann_{R}{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)})=V(\ca_{R}(\mo(R))).

(4)

\CA(R)\subseteq\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
. If, in addition,
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, then they are equal up to radical.

Proof.

(1) Assume
G
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
. With the same argument in the proof of Corollary
4.3
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
G[n]\cong M
in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
for some
M\in\mo(R)
. Then
M
is also a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
. Combining with Lemma
4.4
and Proposition
4.2
,

\Sing(R)=V(\ca_{R}(M))=V(\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)).

The desired result follows as

; see Proposition
3.10
.

(2) By Corollary
4.3
,
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
. It remains to prove the first equality. Assume
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=\thick^{s}_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(G)
for some

and

. By definition,
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\subseteq\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(G)

(\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(G))^{s}\subseteq\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R).

Thus,

The desired result now follows immediately from (1); note that
G[n]\cong M
in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
for some

and

.

(3) The strong generation of
\mo(R)
implies the strong generation of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
; see
2.12
. Combining with (2), it remains to show the first equality, and this follows immediately by combining Proposition
4.7
and
[

.

(4) The first statement follows from Corollaries
3.5
and

. The second statement follows from (3).
∎

Remark 4.10
.

(1) The equality
\Sing(R)=\IPD(M)
in Proposition

(1) was due to Iyengar and Takahashi

(3) Assume
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator. In
[
6
, Corollary 5.8]
, Bahlekeh, Salarian, Takahashi, and Toosi observed that

\dim(R/\ca_{R}(\mo(R)))\leq\dim\Sing(R).

Indeed, this inequality is an equality by Proposition
4.9
(2).

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian domain. If
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator, then
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\neq 0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.9
,
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
defines the singular locus of
R
. It follows from this that
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\neq 0
. If not, the zero ideal of
R
, denoted
(0)
, is in
\Sing(R)
. This contradicts that
R_{(0)}
is a field. Hence,
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

(1) Corollary
4.11
is an analog of a result by Elagin and Lunts
[
23
, Theorem 5]
concerning the derived category. Specifically, for a commutative Noetherian domain
R
, they observed that
\CA(R)\neq 0
if
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
has a strong generator.

In general, if
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
has a strong generator, then
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
also possesses one. However, the converse is not true, even when
R
is a domain; for instance, consider a regular domain with infinite Krull dimension constructed by Nagata
[
39
, Appendix, Example 1]
(see
2.12
). Additionally,
\CA(R)\subseteq\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
(see Proposition
4.9
), and the inclusion can be proper, as illustrated by Nagata’s example (see Lemma
3.5
and Corollary
4.3
). Therefore, there seems to be no implication between Corollary
4.11
and the aforementioned result of Elagin and Lunts.

(2) There exists a one-dimensional Noetherian domain with
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=0
; see Example
4.26
.

Example 4.13
.

Let
R
be a finitely generated algebra over a field or an equicharacteristic excellent local ring. Iyengar and Takahashi

, Theorem 1.3]
established that

has a strong generator.
Recently, this result was extended by the first author, Lank, and Takahashi

.

Let

\sqrt{\CA(R)}=\sqrt{\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}=\sqrt{\ca_{R}(\mo(R))}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition

∎

Remark 4.15

4.16
.

Inspired by the observation of Iyengar and Takahashi in
4.5
, we say that
\mo(R)
has a
point-wise strong generator
provided that there exist
G\in\mo(R)
and

such that, for each

,
\Omega_{R}^{s}M\in\left|G\right|_{t}
for some
s\geq 0
.

We say that
R
has
isolated singularities
if
\Sing(R)\subseteq\Max(R)
, where
\Max(R)
consists of all maximal ideals of

.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring with isolated singularities. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator.

(2)

R/\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
is either
0
or Artinian.

(3)

R/\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
is either
0
or Artinian.

(4)

\mo(R)
has a point-wise strong generator.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: By Proposition
4.9
, one has
\Sing(R)=V(\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R))
. Since
R
has isolated singularities,
\Sing(R)
consists of maximal ideals.
Hence,
V(\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R))
consists of maximal ideals. This implies
(2)
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: This follows from Corollary
4.3
.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
:
If
R/\ca_{R}(\mo(R))=0
, then
R
is regular; see Lemma
3.5
. Thus, for each
M\in\mo(R)
,
\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)
is finitely generated projective for some
i\geq 0
, and hence
\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)\in|R|_{1}
. This implies that
\mo(R)
has a point-wise strong generator. Next, we assume that
R/\ca(\mo(R))
is Artinian.

Set
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
and assume
{\bm{x}}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
is a generating set of
I
. Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
Then

I=\ca_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq\ca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}^{i+1}(M)=\ca_{R}^{1}(\Omega_{R}^{i}(M))

for some
i\geq 0
, where the second equality is by Lemma
3.8
. This implies that

I\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Omega^{i}_{R}(M),\Omega^{i+1}_{R}(M)).

Combining with this,
[
5
, Theorem 3.1 (1)]
shows that there exists
L\in\mo(R/I)
such that

\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)\in|\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\Omega_{R}^{j}(L)|^{R}_{n+1}.

Since
R/I
is Artinian,
L\in|N|^{R/I}_{\ell\ell(R/I)}
, where
\ell\ell(R/I)
is the Loewy length of
R/I
and
N
is the quotient of
R/I
by its Jacobson radical.
Restriction scalars along the map
R\rightarrow R/I
, one has
L\in|N|^{R}_{\ell\ell(R/I)}
, and hence
\Omega^{j}_{R}(L)\subseteq|\Omega^{j}_{R}(N)|^{R}_{\ell\ell(R/I)}
for each

. It follows from this that
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\Omega^{j}_{R}(L)\in|\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\Omega^{j}_{R}(N)|^{R}_{\ell\ell(R/I)}
. By
[
19
, Lemma 3.8]
,
\Omega^{i}_{R}(M)\in|\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\Omega^{j}_{R}(N)|^{R}_{(n+1)\ell\ell(R/I)}
. Hence,
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\Omega^{j}_{R}(N)
is a point-wise strong generator of
\mo(R)
.

(4)\Rightarrow(1)
: This can be deduced by
[
6
, Proposition 5.3]
.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let
R
be a one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay ring. Assume that, for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
of height
0
, the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
has minimal multiplicity. Additionally, assume that, for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of height
1
,
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is almost Gorenstein.
If
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator, then we have:

(1)

\Sing(R)=V(\CA(R))
.

(2)

If, in addition,
R
has isolated singularities, then
\mo(R)
has a strong generator.

Proof.

(1) We claim that
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))<\infty
. By Proposition
3.15
, this is equivalent to showing that
\sup\{\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{m}})\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)\}<\infty
. If
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of height
0
, the by hypothesis
\mathfrak{m}^{2}R_{\mathfrak{m}}=0
, and hence
\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{m}}))\leq 1
by Remark
3.14
(3). If
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal height
1
, then, if we can show
\syz\CM^{\times}(R_{\mathfrak{m}})
is closed under canonical dual, we can conclude by Lemma
3.11
that
\operatorname{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{m}}))\leq 1
. So we now verify that for any one-dimensional almost Gorenstein local ring
R
,
\syz\CM^{\times}(R)
is closed under canonical dual. Indeed, if
R
is almost Gorenstein, then
\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger}\in\ul_{\omega}(R)
by
[
16
, Proposition 7.2, Lemma 4.15]
. Notice that for any
M\in\syz\CM^{\times}(R)
,
M\cong\Hom_{R}(M^{*},R)=\Hom_{R}(M^{*},\mathfrak{m})\cong\Hom_{R}(\mathfrak{m}^{\dagger},(M^{*})^{\dagger})
, where the last module belongs to
\ul_{\omega}(R)
by
[
16
, Lemma 4.15]
. Thus, by
[
16
, Corollary 4.27]
we get
\Hom_{R}(M,\omega)\cong M^{*}\in\syz\CM^{\times}(R)
as we wanted. Therefore,
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))\leq 1
. Combining this with Remark
3.14
(2), we have
\CA(R)=\ca_{R}(\mo(R))
. Since
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator, it follows from Proposition
4.9
that
\Sing(R)=V(\CA(R))
.

(2) By the proof of (1),
\operatorname{n}_{R}(\mo(R))<\infty
. Remark
4.18
now shows that
\mo(R)
has a strong generator.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

For a Gorenstein local ring

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)

.

Corollary 4.21
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian domain with

. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator.

(2)

\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)\neq 0
.

(3)

\ca_{R}(\mo(R))\neq 0
.

(4)

\mo(R)
has a point-wise strong generator.

.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring with

. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})

of

(2)

\ann_{R/\mathfrak{p}}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0

\mathfrak{p}

Remark 4.23
.

Due to Iyengar and Takahashi
[
31
, Theorem 1.1]
, the following three conditions are equivalent for a commutative Noetherian ring.

(1)

\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(2)

\mathsf{D}^{f}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(3)

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Moreover, if these conditions hold, then
\mo(R)
,
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
, and
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
all have generators.

Recently, the first author, Lank, and Takahashi
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that the following three conditions are equivalent for a commutative Noetherian ring.

(1)

\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a strong generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(2)

\mathsf{D}^{f}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a strong generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(3)

\CA(R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Furthermore, if these conditions hold, then
\dim(R)<\infty
, and both
\mo(R)
and
\mathsf{D}^{f}(R)
have strong generators.

A natural question arises: do Corollaries
4.21
and
4.22
still hold if we remove the assumption about the Krull dimension? Theorem
4.25
is a result related to this question. Keep the same assumption as Corollary
4.22
, we don’t know whether
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator when the conditions of Corollary

hold.

For a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mo(R)
, we define
\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mathcal{C})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\displaystyle\bigcap_{X\in\mathcal{C}}\sqrt{\ca_{R}(X)}
.

Lemma 4.24
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then:

(1)
\mathfrak{ca}(R)\subseteq\ca(\mo(R))\subseteq\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R))
.

(2)
\Sing(R)\subseteq V(\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R)))\subseteq V(\ca(\mo(R)))\subseteq V(\mathfrak{ca}(R))
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from Lemma
3.5
.

(2) By (1), it remains to prove
\Sing(R)\subseteq V(\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R)))
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
such that
\mathfrak{p}\notin V(\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R)))
. Since the inclusion relation
V(\sqrt{\ca_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})})\subseteq V(\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R)))
holds, the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
does not belong to
V(\sqrt{\ca_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})})=V(\ca_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}))
. This implies that
\mathfrak{p}\notin\IPD(R/\mathfrak{p})
; see Proposition
3.10
. Hence, the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
has finite projective dimension. This implies that
\mathfrak{p}
does not belong to
\Sing R
, and hence
\Sing(R)\subseteq V(\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R)))
.
∎

Regarding the generation of the singularity category and the vanishing of the cohomology annihilator, we have the following.

Theorem 4.25
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
if and only if
\sqrt{\ca}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}(\mo(R/\mathfrak{p}))\neq 0
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Proof.

For the forward direction, assume that
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. To obtain the conclusion, it suffices to show
\sqrt{\ca}_{R}(\mo(R))\neq 0
under the assumption that
R
is a domain. By assumption and Proposition
4.9
(1), there exists
M\in\mo(R)
such that
M
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
and
\Sing(R)=V(\ca_{R}(M))
. With the same argument in the proof of Corollary
4.11
,
\ca_{R}(M)\neq 0
, and hence there exists
f\neq 0
in
\ca^{n}_{R}(M)
for some
n\geq 0
. Combining this with Lemma
3.8
(1), we get that the projective dimension of
M_{f}
over
R_{f}
is finite. Note that
M_{f}
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R_{f})
as
M
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
.
Hence,
R_{f}
is a regular ring. Let
X
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Since the
R_{f}
-module
X_{f}
has finite projective dimension, there exists
m\geq 0
such that

\Ext_{R}^{m}(X,\Omega_{R}^{m}(X))_{f}\cong\Ext_{R_{f}}^{m}(X_{f},(\Omega_{R}^{m}(X))_{f})=0.

It follows that
f^{r}\cdot\Ext_{R}^{m}(X,\Omega_{R}^{m}(X))=0
for some

. This yields that

For the backward direction, assume that
\sqrt{\ca}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}(\mo(R/\mathfrak{p}))\neq 0
for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

. Then

in

contains a nonempty open subset. By virtue of
[

\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

.
∎

.

In
[

5.
co-cohomological annihilators for modules

In this section, we introduce the notion of a co-cohomological annihilator of modules. The main result is Theorem
1.2
from the introduction.

5.1
.

For each
R
-module
M
and
n\geq 0
, we define the
n
-th
co-cohomological annihilator
of
M
to be

\coca_{R}^{n}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ann_{R}\Ext^{\geq n}_{R}(\mo(R),M).

In words,
\coca_{R}^{n}(M)
consists of elements
r\in R
such that
r\cdot\Ext^{i}_{R}(N,M)=0
for each
i\geq n
and
N\in\mo(R)
. By the dimension shifting, we have
\coca_{R}^{n}(M)=\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\mo(R),M)
. Consider the ascending chain of ideals

\ann_{R}M=\coca_{R}^{0}(M)\subseteq\coca_{R}^{1}(M)\subseteq\coca_{R}^{2}(M)\subseteq\cdots,

the co-cohomological annihilator of
M
is defined to be the ideal

\coca_{R}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\coca_{R}^{n}(M).

Since
R
is Noetherian,
\coca_{R}(M)=\coca_{R}^{n}(M)
for
n\gg 0
.

Combining with Baer’s criterion for injectivity, we conclude that, for each
R
-module
M
and
n\geq 0
,
\coca_{R}^{n}(M)=R
if and only if
\id_{R}(M)<n
. Thus,
\coca_{R}(M)=R
if and only if
\id_{R}(M)<\infty
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
R
-module
M
and
n>0
,

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n\}=\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\mo(R),M).

Proof.

Note that
\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})<n
if and only if
\Ext^{n}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}}),M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
; this can be proved by using Baer’s criterion for injectivity. This yields the first equality below:

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\Ext_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}(\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}}),M_{\mathfrak{p}})\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\Ext_{R}^{n}(\mo(R),M)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\mo(R),M),

where the second one follows from
3.7
and the fact that
\mo(R)\rightarrow\mo(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
is dense. This finishes the proof.
∎

5.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module, the
infinite injective dimension locus
of
M
is defined to be

\IID(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\infty\}.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\mo(R)
has an extension generator
G
, then
\Sing(R)=\IID(G)
.

Proof.

We only need to prove
\Sing(R)\subseteq\IID(G)
, i.e.,
\Spec(R)\setminus\IID(G)\subseteq\Spec(R)\setminus\Sing(R)
. By hypothesis, there exists
s\geq 0
such that
\syz^{s}_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}|G|_{t}
. Pick
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\setminus\IID(G)
. That is,
\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(G_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
. Note that
(\syz^{s}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}))_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}|G_{\mathfrak{p}}|_{t}
. Let
\syz^{s}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
denote a minimal syzygy, then

R_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\oplus\syz^{s}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p}))\cong(\syz^{s}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}))_{\mathfrak{p}}

for some
n\geq 0
, where
k(\mathfrak{p})
is the residue field of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. It follows that
\syz^{s}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p}))\in\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}|G_{\mathfrak{p}}|_{t}
. Since
\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(G_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
, each module in
\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}|G_{\mathfrak{p}}|_{t}
has finite injective dimension over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. In particular,
\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\syz^{s}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p})))<\infty
. Combining this with
[
26
, Theorem 3.7]
, we conclude that

is regular, i.e.,

Lemma 5.5
.

For each
R
-module
M
and
n>0
,

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n\}\subseteq V(\coca_{R}^{n}(M)).

In particular,
\IID(M)\subseteq V(\coca_{R}(M)).
Consequently,

\Sing(R)\subseteq V(\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\sqrt{\coca_{R}(M)})\subseteq V(\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}(M))

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, we have the first equality of the following:

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n\}

\displaystyle=\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\mo(R),M)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{N\in\mo(R)}\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(N,M)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{N\in\mo(R)}V(\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(N,M))

\displaystyle\subseteq V(\bigcap_{N\in\mo(R)}\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(N,M)).

Combining with
\coca_{R}^{n}(M)=\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{N\in\mo(R)}\ann_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(N,M)
, the first statement of the lemma follows.

By the first statement, we get the inclusion below:

\displaystyle\IID(M)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{n>0}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcap_{n>0}V(\coca_{R}^{n}(M)).

Combining with
\displaystyle\bigcap_{n>0}V(\coca_{R}^{n}(M))=V(\bigcup\limits_{n>0}\coca_{R}^{n}(M))=V(\coca_{R}(M))
, we conclude that
\IID(M)\subseteq V(\coca_{R}(M))
, and hence
\IID(M)\subseteq V(\sqrt{\coca_{R}(M)})
. This yields the second inclusion below:

\displaystyle\Sing(R)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{M\in\mo(R)}\IID(M)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{M\in\mo(R)}V(\sqrt{\coca_{R}(M)})

\displaystyle\subseteq V(\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\sqrt{\coca_{R}(M)})

\displaystyle\subseteq V(\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}(M))

This implies the last statement.
∎

(1) As mentioned in

Example 5.8

Let

be a regular ring with infinite Krull dimension. By

,
R
is a generator of
\mo(R)
. However,
\mo(R)
doesn’t have an extension generator. If not, assume that there exist

and

. Since

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then:

(1)

If
\mo(R)
has an extension generator, then
\IID(M)
is closed in
\Spec(R)
.

(2)

If
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, then

\IID(M)=V(\coca_{R}(M)).

Proof.

(1) Assume
\mo(R)
has an extension generator
G
. That is, there exists
s\geq 0
satisfying: for each
X\in\mo(R)
, there exists
t\geq 0
such that
\Omega^{s}_{R}(X)\subseteq|G|_{t}
. For each
n>0
, Lemma
5.2
yields the first equality below:

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n+s\}

\displaystyle=\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n+s}_{R}(\mo(R),M)

\displaystyle=\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Omega^{s}_{R}(\mo(R)),M)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{N\in\mo(R)}\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Omega_{R}^{s}(N),M).

For each
N\in\mo(R)
, it follows from the assumption that
\Omega^{s}_{R}(N)\in|G|_{t}
for some

. This yields that

\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Omega^{s}_{R}(N),M)\subseteq\Supp_{R}\Ext_{R}^{n}(G,M).

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\id_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n+s\}\subseteq\Supp_{R}\Ext_{R}^{n}(G,M).

Taking intersections throughout all

\IID(M)\subseteq\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{n>0}\Supp_{R}\Ext^{n}_{R}(G,M).

On the other hand, by Lemma

The desired result follows as

(2) By Lemma
5.5
, it remains to prove

be an integer. Since
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, there exist

and

\displaystyle V(\coca_{R}^{n+s}(M))

where the second inclusion is due to

Combining with

The following result is related with Proposition
4.7
;
\operatorname{Rfd}
represents the large restricted flat dimension, see

that contains

.

By
3.12
,
\operatorname{Rfd}(G)
is finite. Set
\operatorname{Rfd}(G)=n
. By
[

, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.4]
, we conclude that the category

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

R
has finite Krull dimension and
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(2)

\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
has an extension generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

(3)

\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a resolving generator for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Moreover, if any of the above equivalent conditions holds, then

\mo(R/I)
has an extension generator for each ideal
I
of
R
.

Proof.

The second statement and the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
follows from Proposition
5.19
. The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
follows from the definition.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: Assume
\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
has a resolving generator for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
. By Proposition
5.20
,
R/\mathfrak{p}
has finite Krull dimension for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
. Since the set of minimal prime ideals of
R
is finite, we conclude that
R
has finite Krull dimension.

Fix a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, assume
G
is a resolving generator of
\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
. Note that the modules in
\res(G)
are contained in
\thick_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})}(G)
. Since each complex in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
is isomorphic to some shift of a module
M
in
\mo(R/\mathfrak{p})
, we conclude that
G
is a generator of
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We end this section by comparing the cohomological annihilators and the co-cohomological annihilators; see Proposition
5.23
. Recall that
R
is said to be
Gorenstein in codimension n
if

R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
with height at most
n
.

Lemma 5.22
.

(1) Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. Set
(-)^{\dagger}=\Hom_{R}(-,\omega)
. For each
M\in\CM(R)
,
\ca_{R}^{1}(M^{\dagger})\subseteq\coca^{d+1}_{R}(M)
, and equality holds if, in addition,
R
is Gorenstein in codimension
d-1
.

(2) Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring with finite Krull dimension
d
. For each
M\in\CM(R)
,

\coca_{R}^{d+1}(M)=\coca_{R}(M)=\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M).

(3) Let R be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then
R
is Gorenstein with finite Krull dimension if and only if

\ca_{R}(M)=\coca_{R}(M)
for each
M\in\mo(R)
with
\operatorname{Rfd}_{R}(M)=0.

Proof.

(1) For each
X\in\mo(R)
, since
\Omega^{d}_{R}(X)\in\CM(R)
, we get

\ca^{1}_{R}(M^{\dagger})\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{1}_{R}(M^{\dagger},(\Omega_{R}^{d}(X))^{\dagger})=\ann_{R}\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Omega_{R}^{d}(X),M)=\ann_{R}\Ext^{d+1}_{R}(X,M).

Since this is true for any
X\in\mo(R)
, we get
\ca_{R}^{1}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{d+1}_{R}(\mo(R),M)=\coca_{R}^{d+1}(M)
.

Assume
R
is Gorenstein in codimension
d-1
. Note that
(\Omega_{R}^{1}(M^{\dagger}))^{\dagger}\in\CM(R)
. By
[
25
, Theorem 3.8]
,
(\Omega_{R}^{1}(M^{\dagger}))^{\dagger}\cong\Omega^{d}_{R}(Y)
for some
Y\in\mo(R)
. This yields the first equality below:

\coca_{R}^{d+1}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\Ext^{d+1}_{R}(Y,M)=\ann_{R}\Ext^{1}_{R}((\Omega_{R}^{1}(M^{\dagger}))^{\dagger},M)=\ann_{R}\Ext^{1}_{R}(M^{\dagger},\Omega_{R}^{1}(M^{\dagger}))=\ca_{R}^{1}(M^{\dagger}),

where the last equality is by Lemma
3.8
. This proves the converse direction.

(2) For each
n>0
, assume
r\in\coca_{R}^{n}(M)
. This yields

0=r\cdot\Ext^{n}_{R}(\mo(R),M)=r\cdot\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Omega_{R}^{n-1}(\mo(R)),M).

Since
M\in\CM(R)
and
R
is strongly Gorenstein (see
2.1
),
M
is an infinite syzygy. In particular, there is a short exact sequence

\xi\colon 0\rightarrow M\rightarrow P\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0,

where
P
is a finitely generated projective and
C\in\Omega_{R}^{n-1}(\mo(R))
. By above,
r\cdot\xi=0
. In particular,
r\colon M\rightarrow M
factors through
P
. This implies that
r\in\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)
, and therefore
\coca_{R}^{n}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)
for each
n>0
. It follows from this that
\coca_{R}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)
.

By above,
\coca_{R}^{d+1}(M)\subseteq\coca_{R}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
, where the equality here follows from Lemma
3.8
and Proposition
3.13
. Next, we prove the inclusion
\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)\subseteq\coca_{R}^{d+1}(M)
. Assume
a\in\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)
. Namely, there is a factorization

.

. Localizing at

Proposition 5.23
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Assume
M
is an infinite syzygy, then:

(1)

\coca_{R}(M)\subseteq\ca_{R}^{1}(M)=\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)\subseteq\ca_{R}(M)
. All these inclusions are equal if, in addition,
R
is Gorenstein with finite Krull dimension.

(2)

If
\mo(R)
has a strong generator, then

\IPD(M)=V(\ca_{R}(M))\subseteq V(\coca_{R}(M))=\IID(M).

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
3.8
, it remains to prove
\coca_{R}(M)\subseteq\ann_{R}\underline{\End}_{R}(M)
. This follows from the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.22
(2). If, in addition,
R
is strongly Gorenstein, then
M
is in
\CM(R)
. Hence, by Lemma
5.22
(2), we have
\coca_{R}(M)=\ca_{R}(M)
.

(2) The statement follows from Proposition
3.10
, Theorem
5.9
, and (1).
∎

Proposition 5.24
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay ring with finite Krull dimension
d
. Assume
R
has a canonical module. Then

\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}^{i}(M)=\bigcap_{N\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}^{i}(N)

for all
i\geq\dim(R)+1
and
\bigcap\limits_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}(M)=\bigcap\limits_{N\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

It is clear that

\bigcap\limits_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}^{i}(M)\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{N\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}^{i}(N)\text{ and }\bigcap\limits_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}(M)\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{N\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}(N).

It remains to prove the converse inclusions.

Let
\omega
be a dualizing module of
R
.
Since
R\in\CM(R)
and
\syz^{d}_{R}(\mo(R))\subseteq\CM(R)
, each module in
\mo(R)
has finite
\CM(R)
-resolution dimension in the sense of
[
2
, Section 1]
. Moreover, the subcategory
\add(\omega
) is a cogenerator for
\CM(R)
in the sense of
[
2
, Section 1]
. By taking
\mathbf{X}:=\CM(R)
in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
, for each
M\in\mo(R)
, there exists
X_{M}\in\CM(R)
and
Y_{M}\in\mo(R)
which fits in an exact sequence

0\to Y_{M}\to X_{M}\to M\to 0,

where
Y_{M}
has finite
\add(\omega)
-resolution. It follows that
\id_{R}(Y_{M})<\infty
, and hence
\id_{R}(Y_{M})\leq d
; see
[
12
, Theorem 3.1.17]
. This yields that
\Ext_{R}^{\geq d+1}(\mo(R),Y_{M})=0
, and hence we get that
\ann_{R}\Ext^{i}_{R}(L,X_{M})=\ann_{R}\Ext^{i}_{R}(L,M)
for
i\geq d+1
and
L\in\mo(R)
. Thus, for each
M\in\mo(R)
, there exists
X_{M}\in\CM(R)
such that
\coca_{R}^{i}(X_{M})=\coca_{R}^{i}(M)
for all
i\geq d+1
. In particular,
\coca_{R}(X_{M})=\coca_{R}(M)
. This yields that
\bigcap\limits_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}^{i}(M)\supseteq\bigcap\limits_{N\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}^{i}(N)
for
i\geq d+1
and
\bigcap\limits_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}(M)\supseteq\bigcap\limits_{N\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}(N)
.
∎

Corollary 5.25
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring with finite Krull dimension
d
. For each
i\geq d+1
,

\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca^{i}_{R}(M)=\bigcap_{M\in\mo(R)}\coca_{R}(M)=\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)

If, in addition,
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)
has a strong generator, then all the above ideals define
\Sing(R)
.

Proof.

The second statement is a consequence of the first and
Proposition

4.9
(2).
For the first one, since
R
itself is a canonical module, by
Proposition

5.24
it is enough to prove

\bigcap_{M\in\CM(R)}\coca^{i}_{R}(M)=\bigcap_{M\in\CM(R)}\coca_{R}(M)=\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)

By
Lemma

5.22
(2), we have
\coca_{R}^{i}(M)=\coca_{R}(M)=\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
for each
M\in\CM(R)
and
i\geq d+1
. The desired equalities follow by combining this with
\ann_{R}\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)=\bigcap\limits_{M\in\CM(R)}\ann_{\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{sg}}(R)}(M)
.
∎

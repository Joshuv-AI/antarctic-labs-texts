Minimizing power loss in radial power distribution networks via optimal DG allocation using sensitivity indices aided hippopotamus optimization algorithm
Authors: P Rajakumar, D Ashokaraju, M Senthil Kumar, J Chandramohan, D Thangaraj, P Sabarish, Eric Sylvester Mwanandiye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper introduces a new hybrid approach for efficiently optimizing the placement and sizing of DG units in the radial distribution power network (RDPN) by combining the loss sensitivity index (LSI) and voltage sensitivity index (VSI) with a novel heuristic algorithm known as the hippopotamus optimization algorithm (HOA). The hybrid approach pre-locates the optimal buses using the LSI and VSI and then optimizes the DG sizes using the food-searching intelligence and communication strategy of hippos. The effectiveness and adoptability of the suggested hybrid optimization approach are demonstrated for single and multiple units of Type I and Type III DG allocation to minimize the active power losses (APL) in the IEEE 33-bus benchmark RDPN. For the 33-bus test system with normal load, a single Type I and Type III DG optimization minimizes the total APL from 210.98 kW (Without DG) to 65.24 kW and 58.75 kW, respectively. Conversely, higher power loss reductions are obtained for multiple units of Type I and Type III DG placements, viz., 81.58% and 91.68%. Similarly, single and multiple DG integrations in the same test system with power demand growth provide a maximum APL reduction of 66.12% and 89.80%, respectively. With LSI and VSI pre-selection and the diverse search capability of HOA, the proposed hybrid approach yields a superior percentage of APL reduction compared to the literature works. Furthermore, the simulation study is extended to a large RDPN (136-bus RDPN) to demonstrate the scalability of the proposed approach.

Introduction

Active power losses (APL) in radial distribution power networks (RDPN) have remained a precarious operational factor over the years, as they directly affect the efficiency, stability, and power quality of the network. Because of the extended feeder length and the high resistance to reactance ratio, even a minimal increment in load current leads to high power losses (PL). The increase in PL leads to higher power generation requirements, increased operating costs (OC), and elevated levels of pollution. Distributed generation (DG) provides an opportunity to tackle these issues and to meet the rapid growth in power demand. When DG units are tactically placed and sized in the RDPN, feeder loading is reduced, voltage profiles are enhanced, and APL is significantly minimized.

However, the outcome of DG assimilation highly relies on its allocation, since non-optimal placement could result in undesirable performance. Therefore, such a complex optimization problem requires an advanced solution methodology to achieve favorable technical, economic, and eco-friendly benefits. Different optimization techniques have been introduced in search of finding a better optimal solution to the DG allocation problem. An extensive number of studies in the literature have solved the DG allocation problem for a wider range of objectives. However, the present study only focuses on PL minimization along with voltage enhancement. Therefore, the related works addressing these objectives are summarized below.

A whale optimizer algorithm (WOA) was applied in1 for finding the optimal solution to a multi-objective problem. DG locations and sizes were optimized to reduce total RPL and enhance voltage stability in the 33-node RDPN. A Loss Sensitivity Factor (LSF) supported SA optimization method was proposed in2 to optimize DG units in small, medium and large RDPNs. The integrated method selected the DG placement buses using LSF and used SA for optimizing DG sizes. The BFO technique has been deployed in3 to determine the most suitable location and size for DGs in the RDPN to achieve multiple benefits including PL reduction, VD minimization, and OC reduction. An ALO algorithm-assisted DG allocation technique was proposed to optimize the placement and size of multiple DG units in the standard RDPNs for minimizing PL, increasing VP, and enhancing voltage stability4.

A single objective optimization technique was implemented using particle swarm optimization (PSO) and artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithms to optimize DG units and electric vehicle charging stations (EVCS) in the RDPN5. The impact of DG and EVCS optimization was evaluated on the 69-bus IEEE RDPN and Ghana’s real-time power network. A novel zebra optimization algorithm (ZOA)-based optimization approach was suggested in6 to optimize the DG unit ratings and placement in the RDPN. The suggested ZOA optimization method optimizes the DG units for PL minimization and stability enhancement. A chaotic quasi-oppositional arithmetic (CQOA) optimization algorithm was applied in7 to optimally place and size the renewable energy DG units, including PV and WT in the RDPN. An effective technique using the LSF-bat algorithm (BA) was presented in8 to determine the best location and rating for PV DG units in the RDPNs. The LSF approach was utilized to find the suitable position for the DG units. Meanwhile, the optimal ratings for the PVDG units were determined by the BA. White shark optimization (WSO) and exponential distribution optimizer (EDO) algorithms were proposed to solve the optimal DG placement and sizing problem for a 33-bus RDPN9. The proposed methods optimize the position, size, and power factor of PV and WT DG units for minimizing PL and bus voltage deviation (VD). A novel droop-control technique was implemented for DG-optimized planning in the 33-bus RDPN10. The impact of DG placement and sizing was evaluated for techno-economic reliability benefits. An improved SSA was implemented in11 to allocate the DG units in the optimal position of the RDPN. The ratings of the DG units were optimally scaled for APL and economic loss minimization and voltage profile improvement. A multi-objective GWO technique was introduced in12 to optimally assimilate multiple units of DG into the RDPN. The DGs were optimized for APL and VD minimization and stability enhancement, satisfying several operational constraints. A self-adaptive multi-population elitist JAYA (SAMPE-JAYA) algorithm was proposed to optimize DG units, capacitors, and EVCS13. The performance of the proposed algorithm was evaluated for different scenarios of DG, EVCS, and capacitor placement, and the simulation findings were compared with GA to verify its superiority. A non-dominated sorting GA-II optimization method was proposed, intending to optimize the size and position of DG and capacitor units for maximizing economic and environmental benefits14. An artificial intelligence (AI) method using GA was introduced to locate the optimal buses for EVCS and DG units in the distribution grid network15. The proposed AI technique solves the DG optimization problem for energy loss minimization. A hybrid red fox pathfinder algorithm (RPFO) was suggested in16 to assess the potential location and ratings for DG unit placement. The appropriate positions and sizes for PV and WT DG units and EVCS were explored using the multi-population evolution WOA17. The proposed study optimally integrates the DG units and EVCS in the Indian 28-bus balanced RDPN. The proposed study optimally integrates the DG units and EVCS in the Indian 28-bus balanced RDPN. A new hybrid chimp SCA was presented in18 to compute the optimal size and identify the optimal location for DG in the distribution system. The proposed study was executed to optimize the DG units in the 33-bus RDPN for minimizing PL and VD. The authors have effectively utilized the puma optimizer (PO) algorithm to successfully optimize multiple units of DG in the RDPN19. The DGs were optimally allocated for reducing PL and VD and maximizing the annual savings. A Pareto front multi-objective backtracking search algorithm (BSA) was designed to assimilate PV and WT optimally into the unbalanced RDPN20. The suggested approach determines the best location and size for DG units to minimize APL, VD, and total operational cost, and to improve the voltage stability. An improved artificial hummingbird algorithm (AHA) was presented in21 to simultaneously optimize DG, capacitor, and EVCS units in the RDPNs.

In recent times, the authors have effectively adopted the Adaptive Quantum-inspired Evolutionary Algorithm (AQiEA) in various literature works22–26 to effectively solve optimal DG, capacitor, and network reconfiguration problems. Reference22 deploys AQiEA to solve the DG optimization problem for minimizing power losses in the RDPN. In23, AQiEA was implemented to optimize the DG installation in the two IEEE benchmark RDPNs for reducing the total power losses. The effectiveness of the proposed approach was examined considering different load models including constant impedance (Z), constant current (I), and constant power (P). Whereas in the literature24, AQiEA has been utilized to optimally size and place the DG units in the 33-bus and 69-bus RDPNs with time-varying loads for achieving a significant percentage of PL reduction. The authors have demonstrated the robustness and scalability of AQiEA by optimizing the DG units in voltage-dependent load-connected large RDPNs, including 85-bus and 118-bus systems25. The algorithm solved the DG allocation problem and resulted in significant PL reduction. The PL reduction and voltage profiles of the distribution networks were improved in26 by optimizing a multiple number of small-sized DG units in the sensitive locations of the IEEE RDPN. The simulation validations showcase better PL reduction than several other algorithms.

Related works.

Therefore, these setbacks necessitate the introduction of a novel and efficient approach that explores quality solutions without compromising the convergence efficiency. Consequently, the present work proposes a new hybrid approach combining deterministic network sensitivity factors and an advanced heuristic search algorithm known as the Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm (HOA) to determine the optimal placement and generation rating of single and multiple DG units. With its adaptive movement and stochastic decision, HOA avoids premature convergence and local optima traps. This ensures robust performance across different test conditions irrespective of the size and difficulty of the problem.

Novelty

The novelty of the present work is highlighted below.Unlike standalone conventional heuristic approaches that rely on random initialization, the proposed hybrid method utilizes both Loss Sensitivity Index (LSI) and Voltage Sensitivity Index (VSI) to preselect optimal buses for DG placement. This preselection reduces the search space dimensionality and improves the convergence behavior of the HOA, which is not effectively achieved in conventional metaheuristic methods.The pre-selection of candidate buses guides the search agents (hippos) of HOA toward promising locations instead of randomly selecting buses for DG sizing. This improves the quality of the optimal solution and strengthens the exploitation capability of HOA while reducing the chances of stagnation in local optima.The proposed hybrid method demonstrates consistent and robust performance across multiple DG allocation scenarios on the IEEE 33-bus/136-bus benchmark radial distribution systems under fixed and varying load conditions, which is not adequately addressed by most existing approaches.

Distributed generation and types

The optimized DG units supply real and reactive power for enhancing the RDPN performance. Based on the power injection capability, the DGs are classified as Type I, II, III, and IV. The characteristics of the RDPN following the integration of different DGs are described below.

DG types

Type I DG

These types of DGs supply only active power (AP) into the existing RDPN. A typical Type I DG operates at unity power factor (p.f). When the placement and size of Type I DG are optimized, the APL will reduce but induce a limited effect on reactive power loss reduction due to the lack of reactive power (RP) support. Photovoltaic (PV), fuel cells, and microturbines are the typical examples of Type I DGs.

Two stage hybrid optimization method

Pre-selection of appropriate buses for DG allocation plays a critical part in enhancing the solution quality and convergence speed of algorithms implemented in RDPN. The proposed work employs a combined strategy integrating LSI with VSI to predetermine the candidate buses for single and multi-DG placement. This combined strategy ensures that the optimized DG allocation in the nominated candidate buses gives substantial PL reduction and better voltage profile improvement.

Optimal bus pre-selection using LSI–VSI

The present work deploys a two-stage optimal bus identification methodology using the loss sensitivity and voltage sensitivity indices. The complementary objectives of LSI and VSI are employed to pre-select the optimal buses for DG placement. The LSI computation provides information about the sensitivity of RPL pertaining to a connected power demand at each bus. But, LSI does not provide adequate information about the voltage stability of the bus network, which is also a critical factor besides power losses. On the other hand, VSI computation can identify the voltage-unstable and weaker buses but does not necessarily point to the buses causing maximum RPL.

The LSI is first used to identify the list of buses that are greatly influencing high RPL. Then, secondly, the VSI is employed to further screen for candidate buses to pick the buses causing voltage instability. This integrated process confirms that the DG optimization in the pre-selected bus locations not only minimizes the RPL but also enhances the voltage stability of RDPN. With the pre-selected DG locations, the proposed technique minimizes a significant portion of the search space for the HOA and increases the accuracy and effectiveness of DG placement.

Hippopotamus optimization algorithm (HOA)

The proposed methodology incorporates a recent nature-inspired population-based metaheuristic algorithm known as the Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm (HOA) to find the best and robust solution for the optimal DG planning problem in a radial power system39–47. It makes use of the hippo’s group movement, defense, and evasion tactics against the predators as the fundamental principles to solve a wide range of practical problems.

The search mechanism of HOA operates in three phases to solve an optimization problem: exploration, defense, and exploitation. In the beginning of the algorithm, the most promising regions of the solution space are identified in the exploration phase. Then, in the following defense phase, hippos adjust their movements according to predator attacks, enabling a diversified search mechanism that evades premature convergence. In the end, the candidate solution is refined closer to the best possible optimal solution in the exploitation phase.

The load growth in the distribution network is inevitable in the current scenario. The optimally integrated DG unit under nominal loading conditions may perform inefficiently after the load growth. IEEE 136-bus RDPN

In addition to the 33-bus RDPN, the proposed hybrid approach performance is investigated on a large IEEE RDPN with 136-buses to confirm its robustness and scalability. The power network data needed for power flow study is referred from the literature60. This large distribution power grid system accommodates 135 load buses and a single slack bus. Nominally, the 136-bus RDPN connects 18,313.80 kW of APD and 7932.56 kVAr of RPD. The substation transfers the power for a base operating voltage of 13.8 kV.

Optimal bus selection

In the first phase of the proposed hybrid approach, the suitable locations for the DG placements are found based on the information of LSI and VSI. Initially, the buses enduring high LSI and minimum VSI are identified and are classified as the candidate bus locations. Fig. 16 illustrates the LSI and VSI for the 136 buses of the larger RDPN. The LSI computation outlines that the distribution lines connecting the buses from 75 to 90 and 105 to 120 consistently record more power losses than the others owing to their distance from the slack bus. Additionally, the VSI of the far end buses between 105 and 120 drastically fall to lower values because of the greater voltage drops. Based on the LSI and VSI information, 17 buses out of 136 are identified as the candidate buses for optimal DG placements. The corresponding LSI and VSI for the candidate DG placement buses are shown in Fig. 17. For achieving significant outcomes, the proposed hybrid approach optimizes five units of DG considering the size of the RDPN. The buses 106, 117, 115, 113, and 109 relatively have more LSI and less VSI amongst the list of candidate buses. Hence, the same buses are chosen to optimize the DG units. Conclusion

This paper introduced a new hybrid optimization approach combining the Loss Sensitivity and Voltage Sensitivity Index (LSI-VSI) with the Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm (HOA) for finding effective optimal placement and sizing of single and multiple Type I and Type III DG units in RDPN. The optimal buses were preselected using LSI-VSI to significantly minimize the search space of the optimization problem. With this narrowed search space, the proposed LSI-VSI-driven HOA overcomes the critical gap in the existing works (pre-convergence and local optima) and achieves significantly better APL reduction and voltage profile enhancement. Under the nominal load scenario, the APL of the 33-bus RDPN was minimized from 210.98 kW to 65.24 kW and 38.86 kW as a result of the optimal connection of single and three units of Type I DG placement, respectively, achieving 69.07% and 81.58% PL reduction. Conversely, the Type III DG connection achieved a better percentage of PL reduction with its reactive power support. The APL reduction was increased to 72.15% and 91.68% following the single and three units of Type III DG integration. Furthermore, in the 33-bus RDPN under 20% load growth, APL was reduced by 66.12% and 89%, respectively, for single- and three-DG-unit integration. For 50% load growth, single and multiple DG unit placements gave 63.02% and 89.8% PL reduction. In addition to its superior performance under different load scenarios in the 33-bus RDPN, the proposed hybrid approach demonstrated effective PL reduction in the large 136-bus RDPN for multiple DG unit inclusion. The simulation findings across the different power networks showcase the robustness and scalability of the proposed hybrid method. Despite showcasing superior and consistent outcomes for several cases of optimization studies, the proposed hybrid method has a significant scope for future work, such as DG optimization studies in unbalanced networks and practical RDPNs.

On anisotropic energy conservation criteria of incompressible fluids
Yanqing Wang, Wei Wei and Yulin Ye

Abstract
In this paper, by means of divergence-free condition, we establish an anisotropic energy conservation class enabling one component of velocity in the largest space L 3 ​ ( 0 , T , B 3 , ∞ 1 / 3 ) L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}) for the 3D inviscid incompressible fluids, which extends the celebrated result obtained by Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander and Shvydkoy in [ 15 , Nonlinearity 21 (2008)] . For viscous flows, we generalize famous Lions’s energy conservation criteria to allow the horizontal components and vertical part of velocity to have different integrability.

Introduction

The following Euler equations provide a description of
the evolution of inviscid incompressible fluids

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&\text{div}\,u=0,\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u
represents velocity field and
\Pi
stands for the pressure.
The
initial velocity
u_{0}
satisfies
\text{div}\,u_{0}=0
.

Formally, any smooth solution of the Euler equations (
1.1
) satisfies the kinetic energy conservation

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx,

(1.2)

where
\Omega
is either the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
Lower regularity of solutions to the Euler equations may violate the energy conservation
(
1.2
), which was conjectured by Onsager in
[
33
]
. Indeed, Onsager
conjectured that the threshold of solutions in Hölder spaces for the validity of the conservation of kinetic energy is 1/3 in the Euler equations (
1.1
). The rigid side of Onsager’s conjecture was
originated from Eyink’s work
[
23
]
. Eyink proved that weak solutions conserve the kinetic energy if
u\in C_{\ast}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1/3
, where
C_{\ast}^{\alpha}
is a subspaces of
C^{\alpha}
.
Subsequently, Constantin, E and Titi
[
18
]
proved the positive part of Onsager’s conjecture and showed that weak solutions preserve the kinetic energy (
1.2
) if
u\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
with
\alpha>1/3
. In
[
22
]
, Duchon and Robert introduced the inertial anomalous dissipation term and applied it to show that the kinetic energy is invariant if weak solutions satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|u(x+\xi,t)-u(x,t)|^{3}dx\leq C(t)|\xi|\sigma(|\xi|),
where
C(t)\in L^{1}(0,T)
and
\sigma(a)\rightarrow 0
as
a\rightarrow 0
.
The results of
[
18
,
22
,
23
]
were
sharpened by Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander and Shvydkoy in
[
15
]
, where they proved that energy is conserved
for weak solutions in
L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}).

Recently, Fjordholm and Wiedemann refined the aforementioned results
by a slightly weaker assumption that
u\in L^{3}(0,T;\underline{B}^{1/3}_{3,VMO})
.
In addition, Berselli
[
7
]
showed that the weak solutions in
L^{\frac{1}{\alpha}+\delta}(0,T;C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
for
\alpha\in(1/3,1)
and
\delta>0
conserve the energy in the Euler equations.
Very recently, Berselli and Georgiadis
[
10
]
extended Constantin, E and Titi’s energy class
L^{3}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
to a wider range of exponents as
L^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(0,T;B^{\beta}_{\frac{2}{1-\alpha},\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\alpha\in(1/3,1),1/3<\alpha<\beta<1.
In this direction, one may refer to
[
4
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
38
,
13
,
24
,
14
]
.
We turn our attention to the other side of Onsager conjecture.
After a series of works
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
due to De Lellis and Székelyhidi, the flexible part of Onsager’s conjecture for the 3D Euler equations was confirmed by Isett in
[
28
]
(see also the recent paper by Giri and Radu
[
27
]
).

The inclusion relations of the aforementioned spaces are that,
for
\alpha>1/3
,

C_{\ast}^{\alpha}\subseteq C^{\alpha}\subseteq B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}\subseteq B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}\subseteq\underline{B}^{1/3}_{3,VMO}\subseteq B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}.

(1.3)

It is worth pointing out that
Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander and Shvydkoy constructed a divergence-free vector field with non-vanishing energy flux in the largest space
B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty}
in
[
15
]
. Hence, it seems that it is not expected to obtain energy conservation class
L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}).

On the other hand, anisotropic regularity criteria based on partial components of velocity in the Leray-Hopf weak solutions of the viscous flows (
1.6
) have attracted considerable attention (see
[
30
,
12
,
17
,
29
,
32
,
1
,
42
,
36
,
37
]
and references therein). However, almost all previous sufficient conditions for energy conservation of weak solutions to the
Euler equations (
1.1
) are in the context of isotropic regularity.
A natural question arises whether partial components of velocity in the largest spaces
L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,\infty})
can guarantee the energy conservation (
1.2
) of weak solutions to invicid fluids (
1.1
).
We address this issue and formulate our result as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of the incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Suppose that

u_{h}\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),u_{3}\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\beta}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),1/3\leq\alpha<1\ \text{and}\ \beta\geq\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.

(1.4)

Then the kinetic energy is invariant.

This theorem extends the famous Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander and Shvydkoy’s energy preservation class
L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})
in
[
15
]
. The generalization is twofold. The first one is that energy conservation sufficient conditions (
1.4
) allow the vertical velocity to be in the largest space
B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}
. The
other is that the energy class (
1.4
) enables two components and the rest of the velocity to have the different Besov regularities. To the knowledge of authors, it seems to be the first energy balance criterion for both inviscid and viscous incompressible flows in the framework of anisotropic regularities. The proof of this theorem relies on the following observation:
By means of divergence-free condition, we can reformulate the nonlinear terms

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}&u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}&u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{1}\\
u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}&u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}&u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{2}\\
u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{3}&u_{2}\partial_{1}u_{3}&u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{3}\end{array}\right)

as

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}&u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}&u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{1}\\
u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}&u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}&u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{2}\\
u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{3}&u_{2}\partial_{1}u_{3}&-u_{3}(\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2})\end{array}\right).

We notice that every term includes at least one component of the horizontal velocity. This helps us to achieve the vertical velocity in the largest spaces
L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty})
for the energy conservation (
1.2
).
As for the torus case, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to the incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
Assume that

u_{h}\in L^{3}(0,T;\underline{B}^{\alpha}_{3,VMO}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),u_{3}\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\beta}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),1/3\leq\alpha<1\ \text{and}\ \beta\geq\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.

Then the energy is conserved.

For the whole space, as a byproduct of Theorem
1.1
, we have a more general result.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
u
be a weak solution of the incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then energy conservation (
1.2
) of weak solutions is valid if

\displaystyle u_{1}\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}_{\frac{{2}{p_{1}}}{p_{1}-1},c(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),u_{2}\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}_{\frac{2p_{2}}{p_{2}-1},c(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),u_{3}\in L^{p_{3}}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}}_{\frac{{2}{p_{3}}}{p_{3}-1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

where
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in(1,3].

As is said above, even for viscous fluids (
1.6
),
it seems that there is no relevant anisotropic sufficient conditions for energy equality of weak solutions. Next, we shall focus on the validity of energy balance law

\frac{1}{2}\|u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

(1.5)

for weak solutions of the following Navier-Stokes equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&\mathrm{div}\,u=0,\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

It is well-known that the global Leray-Hopf weak solutions of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations (
1.6

\frac{1}{2}\|u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

(1.7)

rather than energy equality (
1.5

1.5

In the following, we state our anisotropic criteria for energy conservation of the Leray-Hopf weak solutions.

Theorem 1.4
.

The energy equality of Leray-Hopf weak solutions
u
to the 3D Navier-Stokes equations (
1.6
) is valid if one of the following four conditions is satisfied

(1)

u_{h}\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
u_{3}\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
;

(2)

u_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1
,
3\leq q<\infty;

(3)

\nabla u_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2
,
\frac{3}{2}\leq q<\infty;

(4)

u_{h}\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

u_{3}\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\beta}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

1/3\leq\alpha\leq 1/2,\ \beta\geq\frac{1-\alpha}{2}.

Remark 1.1
.

A special case of
u_{h}\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
u_{3}\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
p_{1}=p_{2}=q_{1}=q_{2}=4

reduces to the famous Lions’s
energy balance class.
The first result enables the horizontal part and vertical direction of velocity to have different integrability.

Remark 1.2
.

The first anisotropic criterion in this theorem is very complicated at first sight, thus it seems that it allows us to relax Shinbrot energy equality class (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) due to Beirao da Veiga-Yang (see the following Corollary
1.5
).

Remark 1.3
.

The sufficient condition based on
horizontal components of velocity for energy balance law lies in the well-known Serrin class. Notice that
the full regularity for Leray-Hopf weak solutions satisfying
u_{h}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
is still open, hence,
a potential interesting case in terms of horizontal velocity for energy equality is
u_{h}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

It is well-known that the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem breaks down for Lebesgue space
L^{\infty}
. To overcome this difficulty, we invoke the Constantin-E-Titi type identity to deal with the limiting cases
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. For
future work, it would be interesting to prove energy equality class
u_{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. When one considers energy equality condition via
\nabla u_{h}
, it seems that
the term

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}]\partial_{h}S_{N}u_{3}dxds

corresponds to the borderline case of (
1.4
) for
\alpha=1
and
\beta=0

via low-high frequency techniques (see Lemma
2.4

1.1
to
prove this energy balance criterion in terms of horizontal gradients. It seems that this observation is of independent interest.

\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
such that

\varrho(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\neq 0.

It follows that
\varphi(\xi)=\varrho(\xi/2)-\varrho(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote
h=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi
and
\tilde{h}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varrho
, then inhomogeneous dyadic blocks
\Delta_{j}
are defined by

\Delta_{j}u:=0~~\text{if}~~j\leq-2,~~\Delta_{-1}u:=\varrho(D)u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{h}(y)u(x-y)dy,

\text{and}~~\Delta_{j}u:=\varphi\left(2^{-j}D\right)u=2^{jn}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy~~\text{if}~~j\geq 0.

And the inhomogeneous low-frequency cut-off operator
S_{j}
for any
j\geq 0
is defined by

S_{j}u:=\sum_{k\leq j-1}\Delta_{k}u=\varrho(2^{-j}D)u.

The homogeneous dyadic blocks
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
are defined for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}u:=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u.

Then for
-\infty<\alpha<\infty
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,
the homogeneous Besov semi-norm
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is given by

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jq\alpha}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\right)^{1/q},~~\text{if}\ q\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~~~~~~~~~\text{if}~q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, we define the inhomogeneous Besov norm
\left\|f\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jq\alpha}\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\right)^{1/q},~~\text{if}\ q\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~~~~~~~~\text{if}~q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Then we denote the homogeneous Besov space by

\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big|\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\Big\},

and the inhomogeneous Besov space by

B^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big|\left\|f\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\Big\}.

In the spirit of

]

{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
to be the class of all tempered distributions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}<\infty~\text{and}~\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}2^{j\alpha}\left\|{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}}=0,~~\text{for any}~1\leq p\leq\infty.

(2.1)

In addition, one can also define the homogeneous Besov space with positive indices in terms of finite differences.
For the convenience of readers, we give the detail on periodic domain. For

Mollifier kernel:

f^{\varepsilon}(x)=(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p,q,p_{2},q_{2}\in[1,+\infty)
and
p_{1},q_{1}\in[1,+\infty]
with

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
. Assume
f\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
g\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, then there holds

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|S_{N}(fg)-S_{N}fS_{N}g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=0.

(2.4)

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is folk for the case that
p,q,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2}\in[1,+\infty)
. It suffices to notice that

S_{N}(fg)-S_{N}fS_{N}g=S_{N}(fg)-fg+fg-fS_{N}g+fS_{N}g-S_{N}fS_{N}g.

For case
p_{1},q_{1}\in[1,+\infty]
, we will apply Constantin-E-Titi type identity to prove this lemma. Indeed, in view of the fact that
2^{nN}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)dy=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{h})(0)=\varrho(0)=1,
we notice that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle S_{N}(fg)-S_{N}fS_{N}g

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)[f(x-y)-f(x)][g(x-y)-g(x)]dy-(f-S_{N}f)(g-S_{N}g).

In view of changing of variable, we reformulate (
2.5
) as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle S_{N}(fg)-S_{N}fS_{N}g

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(z)\Big[f(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-f(x)\Big]\Big[g(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-g(x)\Big]dz-(f-S_{N}f)(g-S_{N}g).

It follows from the Hölder inequality and triangle inequality
that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(fg)-S_{N}fS_{N}g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(z)\Big\|f(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-f(x)\Big\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\Big\|g(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-g(x)\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}dz

\displaystyle+\|f-S_{N}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|g-S_{N}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(z)\Big\|g(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-g(x)\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}dz

\displaystyle+C\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|g-S_{N}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Since
p_{2},q_{2}\in[1,\infty)
, we deduce from the following approximations of the identity that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|g-S_{N}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=0.

(2.8)

The strong-continuity of translation operators on Lebesgue spaces allows us to write,
for

1\leq p_{2},q_{2}<\infty
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|g(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-g(x)\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=0.

(2.9)

We conclude by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and (
2.9
) that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(z)\Big\|g(x-\frac{z}{2^{N}})-g(x)\Big\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}dz=0.

(2.10)

Substituting (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) into (
2.7
), we finish the proof of this lemma.

It is well-known that Leray-Hopf weak solutions of the tridimensional Navier-Stokes equations satisfy
u\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{3}{2}
by Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. By means of low-high frequency
techniques, we will show that Leray-Hopf weak solutions are in

for

with

, which plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that

f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
\nabla f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.
Then there holds, for
2<q<6
,

f\in L^{p}(0,T;B^{0}_{q,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and

f\in L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{0}_{q,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{3}{2}.

Proof.

According to the definition of Besov spaces, we write

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{0}_{q,1}}=\sum^{N-1}_{j=-1}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\sum^{\infty}_{j=N}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(2.11)

By Bernstein inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C2^{j[3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})]}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C2^{j[3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})]}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(2.12)

It follows from interpolation and Sobolev embedding theorem that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{6-q}{2q}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|^{\frac{3q-6}{2q}}_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{6-q}{2q}}\|\nabla\Delta_{j}f\|^{\frac{3q-6}{2q}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j[\frac{(3q-6)}{2q}-1]}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j[\frac{(3q-6)}{2q}-1]}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

where the Bernstein inequality and
N>-1
was used.

Plugging (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) into (
2.11
), we conclude by
the straightforward calculation that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{0}_{q,1}}\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\sum^{N-1}_{j=-1}2^{j[3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})]}+C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\sum^{\infty}_{j=N}2^{\frac{j(q-6)}{2q}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{N[3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})]}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C2^{\frac{N(q-6)}{2q}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

where we have used
2<q<6
.

Before going further, we write

2^{N[3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})]}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\approx 2^{\frac{N(q-6)}{2q}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

which enables us to rewrite as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{0}_{q,1}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{6-q}{2q}}\|\nabla f\|^{\frac{3q-6}{2q}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(2.15)

By a time integration, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|f\|^{\frac{4q}{3q-6}}_{B^{0}_{q,1}}dt\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{6-q}{2q}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt.

(2.16)

This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

3.1
Whole space case

Proof of Theorem

.

Applying the inhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley operator

(3.2)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{N}u_{j}\partial_{j}S_{N}u_{i}S_{N}u_{i}dx=0,

\displaystyle I=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}]\partial_{h}S_{N}u_{h}dx,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|S_{N}u(T)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|S_{N}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}(I+II+III+IV)ds.

(3.5)

Therefore, we conclude by the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle|I|=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}\big]\partial_{h}S_{N}u_{h}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\partial_{h}S_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

\displaystyle

\displaystyle S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|u_{h}(\,\cdot-y)-u_{h}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{h}(\,\cdot-y)-u_{h}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dy

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

The Newton-Leibniz formula together with the Minkowski inequality and the Bernstein inequality ensures that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}(\,\cdot-y)-u_{h}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k<N}\|\Delta_{k}u_{h}(\,\cdot-y)-\Delta_{k}u_{h}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\sum_{k\geq N}\|\Delta_{k}u_{h}(\,\cdot-y)-\Delta_{k}u_{h}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle C\sum_{k<N}\Big\|-\int_{0}^{1}y\cdot\nabla{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}(\,\cdot-\vartheta y)d\vartheta\Big\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\sum_{k\geq N}\|{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle C\sum_{k<N}\int_{0}^{1}|y|\Big\|\nabla{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}(\,\cdot-\vartheta y)\Big\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}d\vartheta+\sum_{k\geq N}\|{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle C\sum_{k<N}|y|\Big\|\nabla{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\Big\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\sum_{k\geq N}\|{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle C\sum_{k<N}2^{k}|y|\|{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\sum_{k\geq N}\|{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle C(2^{N}|y|+1)2^{-\alpha N}\left(\Gamma_{1}\ast{d}_{hj}\right)(N).

\displaystyle C2^{-\alpha N}\sum_{k\geq N}2^{(N-k)\alpha}2^{k\alpha}\|{\Delta}_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle C2^{-\alpha N}\left(\Gamma_{1}\ast{d}_{hj}\right)(N).

\displaystyle C\sum_{k<N}2^{k}\|\Delta_{k}u_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

is valid for
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, the above proof still holds for the limiting case

u_{h}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

(3) Case 3:
\nabla u_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2
,
\frac{3}{2}\leq q<\infty.

By classical Sobolev embedding, we know that
\nabla u_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2
and
\frac{3}{2}\leq q<3
guarantees

u_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\frac{3q}{3-q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. From the previous case, we immediately get the energy equality (
1.5
). It suffices to focus on the case for
3\leq q<\infty.

Applying Lemma
2.4
, we derive from
u_{3}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
\nabla u_{3}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
that

u_{3}\in L^{p}(0,T;B^{0}_{q,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

In addition, repeating the deduction of (
4.11
) yields that Leray-Hopf weak solutions satisfy

u\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{3}{2}.

With the help of Hölder inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}]\partial_{h}S_{N}u_{h}dxds\right|

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{3}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\partial_{h}S_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{2q-3}}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Employing Lemma
2.3
for
u_{h}\in L^{\frac{4q}{3}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, we notice that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|S_{N}(u_{h}u_{h})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{3}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=0,

(4.16)

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}|I|=0.

(4.17)

By the same taken, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}|III|+|IV|+|V|=0.

(4.18)

It remains to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}|II|=0.

(4.19)

By means of the Hölder inequality, we observe that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}\big]\nabla S_{N}u_{3}dx\Big|

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla^{k}_{x}S_{N}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Following the same path of (
3.7
), we notice that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)[u_{h}(x-y)-u_{h}(x)][u_{3}(x-y)-u_{3}(x)]dy-(u_{h}-S_{N}u_{h})(u_{3}-S_{N}u_{3}).

In the light of the Minkowski inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|u_{h}(x-y)-u_{h}(x)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{3}(x-y)-u_{3}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dy

\displaystyle+\|u_{h}-S_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{3}-S_{N}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle II_{1}+II_{2}.

Thanks to the
mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|u_{h}(x-y)-u_{h}(x)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C|y|\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.22)

It is apparent that

\displaystyle\|u_{3}(x-y)-u_{3}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.23)

A combination of (
4.22
) and
(
4.23
) enables us to conclude by

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|2^{N}|y|dy<\infty

that

II_{1}\leq C2^{-N}\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.24)

In view of
B^{0}_{p,2}\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

p\geq 2
and Bernstein inequality,
we discover that, for
N>1
,

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-{S}_{N}u_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{j\geq N}\|\Delta_{j}u_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(4.25)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-N}\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

It is evident that

\displaystyle\|u_{3}-{S}_{N}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.26)

Combining (
4.25
) and (
4.26
), we obtain

II_{2}\leq C2^{-N}\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(4.27)

Hence, we derive from the Young inequality and the Bernstein inequality that

\displaystyle\|\nabla S_{N}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{j<N}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(4.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{N}\sum_{j<N}2^{j-N}\|\Delta_{j}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{N}\Gamma^{0}\ast d_{3},

where

\Gamma^{0}(j)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{-j},~~~\text{if}~j>0;\\
&2^{j},~~~~~\text{if}~j\leq 0,\end{aligned}\right.

and

d_{3}(j)=\|{\Delta}_{j}u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Collecting the above estimates, we end up with

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}\big]\nabla S_{N}u_{3}dx\Big|\leq C\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Gamma^{0}\ast d_{3}(N).

Performing a time integration, we discover that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[S_{N}(u_{h}u_{3})-S_{N}u_{h}S_{N}u_{3}\big]\nabla S_{N}u_{3}dx\Big|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{3}\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Gamma^{0}\ast d_{3}(N)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{h}\|^{\frac{2q}{2q-3}}_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt\Big)^{\frac{2q-3}{2q}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{3}\|^{\frac{4q}{3}}_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt\Big)^{\frac{3}{4q}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}(\Gamma^{0}\ast d_{3})^{\frac{4q}{3}}dt\Big)^{\frac{3}{4q}}.

Before going further, we shall prove that
u_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2
and
3\leq q<\infty
.
It follows from Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality that, for
q>2

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|u_{h}\|^{\frac{2q}{5q-6}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3q-6}{5q-6}},

which turns out that

\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{h}\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\leq C\|u_{h}\|^{\frac{2q}{5q-6}}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{p(3q-6)}{5q-6}}ds.

It has been shown that
u_{h}\in L^{\frac{2q}{2q-3}}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
3\leq q<\infty
. With this in hand, owing to
u_{3}\in L^{\frac{4q}{3}}(0,T;B^{0}_{\frac{2q}{q-1},1})
and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we confirm (
4.19
). The proof of this part is completed.

(4)
We derive from
u_{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and Sobolev embedding theorem that
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
for
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
. Since
H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\approx B^{\gamma}_{2,2}
, we conclude by
B^{\gamma}_{2,2}\subseteq B^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}_{3,2}
that
u_{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;B^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}_{3,2})
. To proceed further, we set
\gamma-\frac{1}{2}=\alpha
, which means
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}.
This together with the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and
u_{h}\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha})

means that
u_{h}\in L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha})
.
Theorem
1.4
is thus proved.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality guarantees that, for
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1
with
q\geq 4

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})))}\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right)}^{\frac{(q-4)}{2q-4}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{q}{2q-4}}.

(4.29)

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality again, we know that, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1
with
3<q<4

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{3(4-q)}{2(6-q)}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{q}{2(6-q)}}

(4.30)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{3(4-q)}{2(6-q)}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{q}{2(6-q)}}.

(1) Due to hypothesis and inequality (
4.29
), we observe that
u_{h}\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. We finish the proof of this part by Theorem
1.4
immediately.

(2) It suffices to apply (
4.30
) and assumption to get
u_{h}\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Theorem
1.4
helps us to prove this case.

(3) From (
4.29
) and (
4.30
), we have
u_{i},u_{j}\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).
The classical Lions’ energy class entails the proof of this part. The corollary is proved.

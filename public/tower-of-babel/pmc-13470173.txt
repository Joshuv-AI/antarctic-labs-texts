A Young Girl with Fever and Shoulder Pain
Authors: Sophia S Wang, Jefferey Bui, John Hermann, Daniel Sul, Stephanie Sun, Jacob Ulam, Timothy P Young, Natalie Oberhauser-Lim
Published: 2026

Patient Presentation

A previously healthy 3-year-old girl presented to the pediatric emergency department with 1 day of fever, neck pain, and refusal to move her left arm at the shoulder. There was no recent history of trauma. She was hemodynamically stable with a fever of 101.6 °F. She cried with passive range of motion of the shoulder. Her initial workup showed elevated white blood cell count of 13.8 bil/L, erythrocyte sedimentation rate of 53 mm/h, and C-reactive protein of 16.5 mg/dL. Radiographs of the entire left upper extremity showed no abnormalities. A neck computed tomography (CT) scan with contrast showed no abnormalities (Fig 1). She was admitted for shoulder septic arthritis. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain was negative for intracranial findings but indicated possible intraspinal collection at C5 and abnormal enhancement in the supraclavicular region. Subsequent MRI of the cervical spine confirmed the diagnosis (Fig 2).

Computed tomography scan of the neck with intravenous contrast.

T1 weighted magnetic resonance imaging of the cervical spine. Note the area of asymmetric hyperintensity in the spinal canal (arrow).

Diagnosis: Cervical Spinal Epidural Abscess

The MRI revealed abscesses in the cervical and thoracic spinal canal, extending to the left brachial plexus, mediastinum, and left apical pleura. Blood cultures were positive for methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus. The patient was discharged with 2 months of antibiotics and a plan to be evaluated for immunodeficiency.

Spinal epidural abscess is exceedingly rare in children and presents with nonspecific findings.1, 2, 3 MRI is the imaging modality of choice for suspected spine infection.4 CT has poor sensitivity due to limited intraspinal content differentiation and is frequently normal.5, 6, 7

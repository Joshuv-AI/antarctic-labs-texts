On the distribution of the telegraph meander and its properties
A. Pedicone, E. Orsingher

Abstract
In this paper we study the telegraph meander, a random function obtained by conditioning the telegraph process to stay above the zero level. The finite dimensional distribution of the telegraph meander is derived by applying the reflection principle for the telegraph process and the Markovianity of the telegraph process with the velocity process. We show that the law of the telegraph meander at the end time is a solution to a hyperbolic equation, and we find the characteristic function and moments of any order. Finally, we prove that Brownian meander is the weak limit of the telegraph meander.



Keywords:
Telegraph process; conditional processes; Bessel functions; Brownian meander; conditioned limit theorems; weak convergence;hyperbolic equation

Introduction and summary

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathsf{P})

The finiteness of both the speed of motion and the intensity of changes of directions per unit time makes the telegraph process suitable for describing real motions that emerge in different fields, like geology

[

By means of different approaches

Next, we define the telegraph meander

Here we show that the absolutely continuous component of the telegraph meander at the end time

\displaystyle q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)=\frac{\mathsf{1}_{[0,ct)}(x)}{I_{0}(\lambda t)+I_{1}(\lambda t))}\bigg\{\frac{\lambda xI_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)}{c(ct+x)}+\frac{I_{1}(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}})}{\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}}\Big(\frac{\lambda x}{c}+\frac{ct-x}{ct+x}\Big)\bigg\}.

As a consequence of (

q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx|\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon,V(0)=-c\}.

(1.9)

q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)=-\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)}{p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)}\;\;x\in[0,ct),

\displaystyle p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)=\frac{\lambda e^{-\lambda t}}{2c}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{ct-x}{ct+x}}I_{1}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)+I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)\bigg)\mathsf{1}_{(-ct,ct)}(x).

(1.11)

Result (

satisfies the following hyperbolic equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+2\lambda\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\frac{2a}{t}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Big(\frac{\lambda}{t}-\frac{2a}{t^{2}}\Big)u,

(1.12)

where

a(t)=\frac{I_{1}(\lambda t)}{I_{0}(\lambda t)+I_{1}(\lambda t)}.

(1.13)

An interesting application of finite velocity random motions is that they can be used to represent the solution of hyperbolic equations. Indeed, it is a well known result that
p_{\lambda,c}
satisfies the telegraph equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+2\lambda\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=0.

(1.14)

Then, (
1.12
) has a probabilistic interpretation in that its solution corresponds to the law of a conditioned finite velocity random motion.

In
[
17
]
it is proved that, under the Kac’s condition, namely when
\lambda\to+\infty
,
c\to+\infty
such that
c^{2}/\lambda\to 1
, the density of the telegraph process converges to the density of Brownian motion. Then, by introducing the parameter
\alpha=\lambda=c^{2}
, the telegraph process
T_{\alpha}
forms a sequence of random functions on
C[0,+\infty)
, the space of real valued continuous functions on
[0,+\infty)
. In
[
12
]
, p. 451, it is claimed that
T_{\alpha}\Rightarrow B
on
C[0,+\infty)
, where
B
is Brownian motion and the symbol
\Rightarrow
stands for weak convergence. This result motivates us to prove the weak convergence of the telegraph meander to Brownian meander, that is
T_{\alpha}^{+}\Rightarrow B^{+}
on
C^{+}[0,t]
, where we are denoting
B^{+}
as the random function on
C^{+}[0,t]
corresponding to Brownian meander (see e.g.
[
11
]
for the definition of Brownian meander). By using the asymptotic expansion of
I_{\nu}
for every
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we first show a local limit theorem:

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}_{\alpha}(t_{1})\in dx_{1},...,T^{+}_{\alpha}(t_{n})\in dx_{n}\}=\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}(t_{1})\in dx_{1},...,B^{+}(t_{n})\in dx_{n}\},

(1.15)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<t_{1}<...<t_{n}<t
. The proof of weak convergence of the probability measures induced by
T^{+}_{\alpha}
is more involved. The main idea is based on the observation that the telegraph process can be seen as a random walk with a random number of summands. Hence, we can use the result of Iglehart, stated in
[
15
]
, concerning a functional central limit theorem for random walk conditioned to stay positive.

The telegraph meander formalizes a finite velocity random motion constrained to be positive, with the property that the Brownian meander is its weak limit. These features suggest to use the telegraph meander as alternative model to Brownian meander. For instance, in finance, the Brownian meander is applied as a model for financial options where the payoff depends on whether the asset’s price reaches a barrier and remains continuously below it (see
[
6
]
). Then, just as the geometric telegraph process replaces geometric Brownian motion (see
[

]
), we propose the telegraph meander as an alternative to Brownian meander in this field. Furthermore, since the telegraph process models the vertical motion of volcanic regions (
[

]
), the telegraph meander can be used for studying this phenomenon under the condition that the motion stays above a specified level.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T^{+}=(T^{+}(s))_{s\in[0,t]}

\displaystyle q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx\lvert\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon,V(0)=-c\}=-\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)}{p_{\lambda,c}^{-}(0,t)}\;\;x\in[0,ct).

(2.26)

Proof.

By symmetry, equation (
1.7
) assumes the form

\mathsf{P}\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\in-dx|V(0)=-c\}=2\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx\}\;\;x\in[0,ct).

Moreover, for
x\in[-\epsilon,ct-2\epsilon)
and
\epsilon\in[0,ct)

2.25
) yields to

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx|\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon,V(0)=-c\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx,\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon|V(0)=-c\}}{\mathsf{P}\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon|V(0)=-c\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx|V(0)=-c\}-\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx,\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)<-\epsilon|V(0)=-c\}}{\mathsf{P}\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon|V(0)=-c\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx|V(0)=-c\}-\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in 2\epsilon+dx|V(0)=-c\}}{\int_{0}^{\epsilon}2\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dy\}}=\frac{p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)-p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(2\epsilon+x,t)}{2\int_{0}^{\epsilon}p_{\lambda,c}(y,t)dy}.

An application of the mean value theorem leads to the following asymptotic estimate

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx\lvert\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon,V(0)=-c\}\sim\frac{-2\epsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)}{2\epsilon p_{\lambda,c}(0,t)},\;\;\epsilon\downarrow 0,

Now
p_{\lambda,c}(0,t)=p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)=e^{-\lambda t}\frac{\lambda}{2c}(I_{0}(\lambda t)+I_{1}(\lambda t))
, while the space derivative of

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)=-\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{2c}\bigg\{\frac{\lambda x}{c}\frac{I_{1}(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}})}{\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}}+\sqrt{\frac{ct+x}{ct-x}}\frac{ctI_{1}(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}})}{(ct+x)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{ct-x}{ct+x}}\frac{\lambda x}{c\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}}\bigg(I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)-\frac{c}{\lambda\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}}I_{1}(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}})\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{2c}\bigg\{I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)\frac{\lambda x}{c(ct+x)}+\frac{I_{1}(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}})}{\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}}\Big(\frac{\lambda x}{c}+\frac{ct-x}{ct+x}\Big)\bigg\}.

(2.28)

We remark that in the previous steps we used (
2.22
) and the property

\frac{d}{dx}I_{\nu}(x)=I_{\nu-1}(x)-\frac{\nu}{x}I_{\nu}(x).

(2.29)

In conclusion, we have proved that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx|\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq-\epsilon,V(0)=-c\}=-\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)}{p_{\lambda,c}^{-}(0,t)},

(2.30)

and hence from (

∎

We can rewrite formula (

p_{\lambda,c}^{-}(x,t)=\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{2c}\bigg(\lambda I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)+c\frac{\partial}{\partial x}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)\bigg),

(3.1)

and we see that, in order to find the characteristic function of

, we first need an explicit form of the following integral

G(\gamma,t)=\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
|\gamma|<\lambda/c
, then the following result holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx=c\frac{\sinh{(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}+ic^{2}\gamma\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sinh{(s\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}I_{0}(\lambda(t-s))ds.

(3.3)

Proof.

We first recall equation (25) stated in
[
17
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)=c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)+\lambda^{2}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big).

(3.4)

After multiplying both sides of equation (
3.4
) with
e^{i\gamma x}
and integrating over the interval
(0,ct)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx=c^{2}\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx+\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx.

By interchanging the derivative with the integral on the left-hand side and integrating by parts the first term on the right-hand side, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx=(\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2})\int_{0}^{ct}e^{i\gamma x}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx+ic^{2}\gamma I_{0}(\lambda t).

Therefore the function
G(\gamma,t)
is a solution of the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}G(\gamma,t)-(\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2})G(\gamma,t)=ic^{2}\gamma I_{0}(\lambda t).\\
G(\gamma,0)=0\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(\gamma,0)|_{t=0}=c.\end{cases}

(3.5)

We apply the
t
-Laplace transform of parameter
\beta>\lambda
. The second member of the equation in (
3.5
) can be obtained through the following formula

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}I_{0}(\lambda t)dt=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-\lambda^{2}}}.

(3.6)

For the first member, by setting
\mathcal{L}G(\gamma,t)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}G(\gamma,t)dt
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}G(\gamma,t)dt=\bigg[e^{-\beta t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(\gamma,t)\bigg]_{0}^{+\infty}+\beta\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(\gamma,t)dt

\displaystyle=-c+\beta\bigg[e^{-\beta t}G(\gamma,t)\bigg]_{0}^{+\infty}+\beta^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\gamma t}G(\gamma,t)dt=-c+\beta^{2}\mathcal{L}G(\gamma,t).

(3.7)

Where the fact that
e^{-\beta t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(\gamma,t)
and
e^{-\beta t}G(\gamma,t)
tend to zero as
t\to+\infty
, if
\beta>\lambda
, can be deduced by the estimate
G(\gamma,t)\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}\sinh(\lambda t)
.

Therefore by means of (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), the Laplace transform of
G(\gamma,t)
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}G(\gamma,t)=\frac{c}{\beta^{2}-\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}+\frac{ic^{2}\gamma}{(\beta^{2}-\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2})\sqrt{\beta^{2}-\lambda^{2}}}.

(3.8)

And now we derive the inverse Laplace transform of
\mathcal{L}G(\gamma,t)
, that is the explicit form of the function
G(\gamma,t)
. From (
3.8
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}G(\gamma,t)=\frac{c}{2\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\beta-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\beta+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{ic^{2}\gamma}{2\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-\lambda^{2}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\beta-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\beta+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}\bigg).

Then, by using again (
3.6
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}G(\gamma,t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}\frac{c\sinh(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}dt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}I_{0}(\lambda t)dt\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}\frac{ic^{2}\gamma\sinh(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\beta t}\bigg(\frac{c\sinh(\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ic^{2}\gamma\sinh((t-s)\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}I_{0}(\lambda s)ds\bigg)dt.

And so at the end formula (
3.3
) appears

\displaystyle G(\gamma,t)=c\frac{\sinh{(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}+ic^{2}\gamma\frac{\sinh(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}\ast I_{0}(\lambda t),

where
*
denotes the convolution operator.
∎

We also state the following proposition, which highlights the structure of the formula expressed in Lemma

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
|\gamma|<\lambda/c
, then the following result holds

\int_{0}^{ct}\cos(\gamma x)I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx=\frac{c\sinh{(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}})}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\gamma^{2}}}.

(3.9)

Proof.

By exploiting the Mac-Laurin expansion of the cosine function we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{ct}\cos(\gamma x)I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{\gamma^{2k}}{(2k)!}\int_{0}^{ct}x^{2k}I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{\gamma^{2k}}{(2k)!}\frac{(ct)^{2k+1}}{2}\Gamma\Big(k+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\sum_{r\geq 0}\Big(\frac{\lambda t}{2}\Big)^{2r}\frac{1}{r!\Gamma(r+k+\frac{3}{2})}.

Now, we use the duplication formula of the Gamma function, from which we can write that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{ct}\cos(\gamma x)I_{0}\big(\frac{\lambda}{c}\sqrt{c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}}\big)dx=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{\gamma^{2k}}{k!}(ct)^{2k+1}\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(\lambda t)^{2r}}{r!}\frac{(r+k)!}{(2(r+k)+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}ct(-1)^{k}\Big(\frac{\gamma c}{\lambda}\Big)^{2k}\sum_{m\geq k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(\lambda t)^{2m}}{(2m+1)!}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{ct(\lambda t)^{2m}}{(2m+1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\Big(-\frac{c^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{ct(\lambda t)^{2m}}{(2m+1)!}\Big(1-\frac{c^{2}\gamma^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{m},

and so, by recalling the Mac-Laurin expansion of the hyperbolic sine, the thesis follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

We now compare Brownian meander with telegraph meander. We first show that the density function of Brownian meander satisfies a parabolic partial differential equation in which at the first member there is the heat operator
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-\frac{u}{2t}

Proof.

For simplicity we rewrite equation (

q(x,t)p(0,t)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p(x,t).

By multiplying by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}q(x,t)p(0,t)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial x^{3}}p(x,t).

From the fact that the density function

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial x^{2}}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial x^{3}}p(x,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big\{-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}p(x,t)\Big\}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q(x,t)p(0,t)+q(x,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(0,t)=p(0,t)\Big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q(x,t)-q(x,t)\frac{1}{2t}\Big\}

Relationship (

Theorem 3.2

Let

Proof.

We rewrite equation (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)+2\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)+q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)=-\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial t^{2}\partial x}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)

Analogously, by taking in both members of (

\displaystyle-c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)=c^{2}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial x^{3}}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t).

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)+2\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)+q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)-c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}c^{2}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\Big\}.

Now, the density function

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)-c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)+2\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\Big\}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)+2\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q_{\lambda,c}(x,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p^{-}_{\lambda,c}(0,t)=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2c}e^{-\lambda t}(I_{0}(\lambda t)+I_{1}(\lambda t))+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2c}e^{-\lambda t}(I_{0}(\lambda t)+I_{1}(\lambda t)-\frac{1}{\lambda t}I_{1}(\lambda t))

4
Weak convergence of telegraph meander

Here we study the convergence of the distribution of the telegraph meander under the Kac’s condition by setting
\alpha=\lambda=c^{2}
. First, we consider the convergence of the finite dimensional distributions. By means of the asymptotic estimate of the Bessel functions, it can be proved that the density function of the telegraph process converges to the density of Brownian motion. The same technique is applied in the next theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

Proof.

By resorting to the asymptotic estimate of the Bessel functions (formula (

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}(t)\in dx,V(t)=v,\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T_{\alpha}(s)\geq-\beta|V(0)=v\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg\{\frac{e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}-\frac{e^{-\frac{(2\beta+x)^{2}}{2t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\bigg\}\mathsf{1}_{[-x\lor 0,+\infty)}(\beta)dx=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{P}\{B(t)\in dx,\min_{0\leq s\leq t}B(s)\geq-\beta\},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}_{\alpha}(t_{1})\in dx_{1},...,T^{+}_{\alpha}(t_{n})\in dx_{n}\}

We now establish a stronger result concerning the weak convergence of the probability measure induced by the telegraph meander to that induced by Brownian meander. Our aim is to provide a conditional version of the weak convergence of the telegraph process to Brownian motion on the set
C^{+}[0,t]=\{x\in C[0,t]:x\geq 0\}
. Our approach is based on the following equivalent representation of the telegraph process

T_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu(\alpha t)}\xi_{0}(-1)^{k-1}\xi_{k}+\frac{\xi_{0}(-1)^{\nu(\alpha t)}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}(\alpha t-S_{\nu(\alpha t)}),\;\;t\geq 0,

(4.5)

where
\nu=(\nu(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process of unitary rate,
\xi_{0}\in\{-1,1\}
uniformly and independent of
\nu
,
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are the i.i.d. exponential inter-arrivals of parameter one associated with
\nu
, and
S_{\nu(\alpha t)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\nu(\alpha t)}\xi_{k}
.

Equation (
4.5
) shows that the telegraph processes can be expressed as a sum of two processes, the first one represents a random walk with a random number of summands, while the second one can be viewed as an interpolating term. Therefore, our strategy is essentially based on proving that a functional conditional central limit theorem holds for the first term and the second term converges to zero as
\alpha\to+\infty
. In such a way, we extend the random function
T_{\alpha}
from
C=C[0,1]
to
D=D[0,1]
, the space of cadlag functions on
[0,1]
endowed with the Skorohod topology. For a description of this space and its metric topology, one may refer to Chapter 3 of
[
2
]
. We indicate with
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, the sigma algebras generated by the uniform and Skorohod topology respectively. The identity map

is continuous, hence
\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{C}
measurable. Then,

represents a telegraph process defined on
(D,\mathcal{D})
. For simplicity we denote by
T_{\alpha}
the random element on the set of cadlag functions. Let

be the set of non negative cadlag functions and
\Gamma_{\alpha}=\{T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}
. Then
T^{+}_{\alpha}
is the restriction of
T_{\alpha}

, namely, the telegraph meander. Hence,

, where

. Let

\hat{T}_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu(\alpha t)}\xi_{0}(-1)^{k-1}\xi_{k}

(4.6)

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in A|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}=\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in A\}.

(4.8)

Lemma 4.1

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}-\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in A|T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}=\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}-\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in A|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}=\delta_{0}(A)

[

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\sim\frac{C}{\sqrt{\pi\alpha}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\mathsf{P}\{{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha}}\;\;\alpha\to+\infty

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\sqrt{\alpha}\mathsf{P}\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|T_{\alpha}(t)-\hat{T}_{\alpha}(t)|\geq\epsilon\}=0\;\;\epsilon>0.

Lemma 4.2

The following limit holds

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\frac{\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}{\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}=1

\displaystyle 1-\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}-\hat{T}_{\alpha}\notin D^{+}|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\leq\frac{\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}{\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\notin D^{+}|T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}.

\mathsf{P}\{\Gamma_{n}\}>0
. Under the condition that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{\rho(X_{n},Y_{n})\geq\epsilon|\Gamma_{n}\}=0

(4.14)

for every
\epsilon>0
, then

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{X_{n}\in F|\Gamma_{n}\}\leq\mathsf{P}\{X\in F\}

(4.15)

implies that

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{Y_{n}\in F|\Gamma_{n}\}\leq\mathsf{P}\{X\in F\}

(4.16)

for every closed set
F
in the topology of
S
.

In other words, Theorem
4.2
says that, conditionally on
\Gamma_{n}
, if
X_{n}
converges weakly and the distance between
X_{n}
and
Y_{n}
tends to zero, then, conditionally on
\Gamma_{n}
,
Y_{n}
has the same weak limit of
X_{n}
.

In our context, Theorem
4.2
, Lemma
4.1
and (
4.8
) implies, conditionally on
\hat{\Gamma}_{\alpha}
, that
\hat{T}_{\alpha}
and
T_{\alpha}
have the same weak limit. This means that

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}=\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in A\}

(4.17)

for every
A\in\mathcal{D}^{+}
such that
\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in\partial A\}=0
.
We are now able to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T_{\alpha}^{+}
be a telegraph meander and
B^{+}
be Brownian meander. Then,
T_{\alpha}^{+}\Rightarrow B^{+}
on
C^{+}[0,t]
.

Proof.

For any set
A\in\mathcal{D}^{+}
the following double inequality is true

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A,\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}-\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+},T_{\alpha}-\hat{T}_{\alpha}\notin D^{+}\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A,T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\leq\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A,\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}+\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in D^{+},\hat{T}_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\notin D^{+}\}.

Thus

\displaystyle(\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}-\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}-\hat{T}_{\alpha}\notin D^{+}|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\})\frac{\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}{\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A|T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\leq\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A|\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\frac{\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}{\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}}+\mathsf{P}\{\hat{T}_{\alpha}-T_{\alpha}\notin D^{+}|T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}

By taking the limit as
\alpha\to+\infty
, as a consequence of (
4.17
), Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in A\}-\delta_{0}(D\setminus D^{+})\leq\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\mathsf{P}\{T_{\alpha}\in A|T_{\alpha}\in D^{+}\}\leq\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in A\}+\delta_{0}(D\setminus D^{+}),

for any
A\in\mathcal{D}^{+}
such that
\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in\partial A\}=0
. Thus, we obtain that
T^{+}_{\alpha}\Rightarrow B^{+}
on
D^{+}
. Finally, by denoting with
C^{+}=\{x\in C:x\geq 0\}
, from the fact that
\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}_{\alpha}\in C^{+}\}\equiv\mathsf{P}\{B^{+}\in C^{+}\}=1
, example 2.9 of
[
2
]
, shows that
T^{+}_{\alpha}\Rightarrow B^{+}
on
C^{+}
, from which the thesis can be achieved.
∎

5
Conditioned distribution of the telegraph meander

Here we derive the distribution of telegraph meander at time
t
conditioned on the number of Poisson events. From (
2.2
) and the fact that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)=0|N(t)=n,V(0)=c\}=\mathsf{P}\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq 0|N(t)=n,V(0)=c\},\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

formulas (4.4) and (4.10) of
[
4
]
can be restated as

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq 0|N(t)=n,V(0)=c\}=\begin{cases}1&n=0\\
\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{2^{2k}}&n=2k,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\frac{\binom{2k+1}{k}}{2^{2k+1}}&n=2k+1,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Moreover, from equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), setting
\beta=0
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T(t)\in dx,\min_{0\leq s\leq t}T(s)\geq 0|N(t)=n,V(0)=c\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathsf{1}_{\{ct\}}(x)dx&n=0\\
\frac{(2k)!}{k!(k-1)!}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k-1}}{(2ct)^{2k}}2x\mathsf{1}_{[0,ct)}(x)dx&n=2k,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\binom{2k+1}{k}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}}{(2ct)^{2k+1}}\frac{ct+(2k+1)x}{ct+x}\mathsf{1}_{[0,ct)}(x)dx&n=2k+1,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{cases}

(5.2)

By combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\in dx|N(t)=n\}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{1}_{\{ct\}}(x)dx&n=0\\
2kx\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k-1}}{(ct)^{2k}}\mathsf{1}_{[0,ct)}(x)dx,&n=2k,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\frac{ct+(2k+1)x}{(ct)^{2k+1}}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}}{ct+x}\mathsf{1}_{[0,ct)}(x)dx&n=2k+1,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{cases}

(5.3)

It is immediate to see that
\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\in dx|N(t)=n\}\to 0
pointwise on
[0,ct)
as
n\to+\infty
. This can be interpreted by saying that, as the number of changes of directions increases, with high probability, the particle does not move away from the origin. Moreover, as simple calculations show, the point of maximum of (
5.3
), is equal to

\mathsf{M}[T^{+}(t)|N(t)=n]=\begin{cases}ct&n=0\\
\frac{ct}{\sqrt{2k-1}},&n=2k,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}\\
ct\frac{\sqrt{2k+2}-1}{2k+1}&n=2k+1,\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{cases}

(5.4)

It is worthwhile to observe that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\in dx|N(t)=2k\}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}}{(ct)^{2k}}\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\in dx|N(t)=2k+1\}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}(ct-x)}{(ct)^{2k+1}}\;\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(5.6)

hence, we can easily check that the laws in (
5.3
) are proper density functions. Furthermore, it is possible to derive the distribution function of the conditioned telegraph meander at the end time
t
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\leq x|N(t)=2k\}=\begin{cases}0&x<0\\
1-\Big(1-\frac{x^{2}}{c^{2}t^{2}}\Big)^{k}&0\leq x<ct\\
1&x\geq ct\end{cases}

(5.7)

while, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\leq x|N(t)=2k+1\}=\begin{cases}0&x<0\\
1-\Big(1-\frac{x^{2}}{c^{2}t^{2}}\Big)^{k}\Big(1-\frac{x}{ct}\Big)&0\leq x<ct\\
1&x\geq ct\end{cases}

(5.8)

From these formulas, we see that the following property holds

\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\geq x|N(t)=2k+1\}\leq\mathsf{P}\{T^{+}(t)\geq x|N(t)=2k\}.

(5.9)

The previous inequality has the following meaning. The condition that the motion never crosses the zero level requires that the velocity at the initial time must be positive. This implies that, if the motion changes direction an even number of times during the time interval
[0,t]
, the velocity at the end time is positive, while it is negative in the case of and odd number of changes of directions. Thus, the stochastic dominance arises from the fact that the particle is more likely to cross the positive level
x
when the velocity changes from negative to positive.

We now compute the conditional moments of the telegraph meander.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
T^{+}=(T^{+}(s))_{s\in[0,t]}
be a telegraph meander, then for any
p>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[T^{+}(t)^{p}|N(t)=n]=\begin{cases}(ct)^{p}&n=0\\
(ct)^{p}k!\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1+k)}&n=2k,\;k\in\mathbb{N}\\
(ct)^{p}k!\Big(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1+k)}-\frac{\frac{p}{2}\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2}+1+k)}\Big)&n=2k+1,\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{cases}

(5.10)

Proof.

For the even case, with formula (
5.5
) at hand, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[T^{+}(t)^{p}|N(t)=2k]=-\int_{0}^{ct}x^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}}{(ct)^{2k}}dx=\int_{0}^{ct}px^{p-1}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}}{(ct)^{2k}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}(ct)^{p}\int_{0}^{1}x^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-x)^{k}dx=(ct)^{p}k!\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{p}{2}+1\Big)}{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1+k)}.

In the same way, for the odd case, by means of (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}[T^{+}(t)^{p}|N(t)=2k+1]=-\int_{0}^{ct}x^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}(ct-x)}{(ct)^{2k+1}}dx=\int_{0}^{ct}px^{p-1}\frac{(c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2})^{k}(ct-x)}{(ct)^{2k+1}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}(ct)^{p}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}x^{\frac{p}{2}-1}(1-x)^{k}dx-\int_{0}^{1}x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(1-x)^{k}dx\bigg)=(ct)^{p}k!\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{p}{2}+1+k)}-\frac{\frac{p}{2}\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2}+1+k)}\bigg).

∎

We remark that the difference between the two moments for the even and odd cases tends to be negligible when
n
is large enough. This indicates that, when the motion changes directions many times, the position is not significantly affected by whether the number of changes of direction is even or odd. Moreover, by applying Stirling’s formula to (
5.10
), it turns out that
n^{p/2}\mathsf{E}[T(t)^{p}|N(t)=n]\to 0
. Therefore, the telegraph meander conditioned on the event
N(t)=n
, converges in
L^{p}
to
0
for any
p>0
as
n\to+\infty
. And hence this tells us that, when the number of Poisson events is large, on average, the motion concentrates around the origin.

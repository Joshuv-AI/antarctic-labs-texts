Direct reconstruction of general elastic inclusions
Sarah Eberle-Blick, Henrik Garde, Nuutti Hyvönen

Abstract
. The inverse problem of linear elasticity is to determine the Lamé parameters, which characterize the mechanical properties of a domain, from pairs of pressure activations and the resulting displacements on its boundary. This work considers the specific problem of reconstructing inclusions that manifest themselves as deviations from the background Lamé parameters. The monotonicity method is a direct reconstruction method that has previously been considered for domains only containing positive (or negative) inclusions with finite contrast. That is, all inclusions have previously been assumed to correspond to a finite increase (or decrease) in both Lamé parameters compared to their background values. We prove the general outer approach of the monotonicity method that simultaneously allows positive and negative inclusions, of both finite and extreme contrast; the latter refers to either infinitely stiff or perfectly elastic materials.



Keywords: linear elasticity, monotonicity method, perfectly elastic, infinitely stiff.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R30, 35R05, 47H05.

Introduction

This work considers the inverse boundary value problem of linear elasticity that can be formulated as the task of determining the Lamé parameters, which characterize the mechanical properties of the investigated body, from pairs of external pressure activations and the associated boundary displacements. Previous theoretical works on this inverse problem include

Our interest lies more specifically with the reconstruction of inclusions, i.e., the task of determining from boundary measurements the regions where the Lamé parameters differ from their background values. The
monotonicity method

[
16
,
18
,
30
]
has previously been successfully applied to inclusion detection in the framework of linear elasticity in
[
9
,
11
]
(see also
[
7
,
8
,
10
]
for related results), but these papers only consider the so-called
inner approach
that determines if a given open set is fully contained in an inclusion. The inner approach assumes that all inclusions are either (i)
positive
or (ii)
negative
, meaning that both Lamé parameters are everywhere in the domain either (i) larger or equal or (ii) smaller or equal to their respective background values. Moreover, the cases of
perfectly elastic
and
infinitely stiff
inclusions, which correspond, respectively, to vanishing and infinite Lamé parameters, have previously not been considered in connection to the monotonicity method. Such
extreme inclusions
can alternatively be characterized by vanishing or infinite Young’s modulus, assuming Poisson’s ratio is bounded strictly away from
0
and
\tfrac{1}{2}
everywhere in the domain; cf., e.g.,
[
17
]
.

Following the analysis on electrical impedance tomography in
[
4
,
14
]
, we demonstrate that the previous results on the monotonicity method for linear elasticity can be generalized to the case of extreme inclusions. What is more, we prove that the so-called
outer approach
of the monotonicity method provides simultaneous characterization of positive and negative inclusions, which are allowed to have both finite and extreme contrast; it determines whether a given subset of the domain contains all inclusions. It should be noted, however, that positive (negative) perturbations are located in the same parts of the domain for both Lamé parameters, i.e., regions where the Lamé parameters deviate to opposite directions from their respective background values are not considered. On the other hand, our result does not require any prior bounds on the contrast of the inclusions compared to the background Lamé parameters.

The new results are enabled by proving new
simultaneous
localization results (localized potentials for several pairs of Lamé parameters), new monotonicity inequalities that account for extreme inclusions, and operator convergence of Neumann-to-Dirichlet maps for sequences of finite pairs of Lamé parameters converging to an extreme pair of Lamé parameters.

Section

[
4
]

4

Due to the application of elasticity, we assume that all involved function spaces are real.

We denote by

the Frobenius inner product of two matrices and by

For a vector-valued function

\nabla\cdot A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}}\mathrm{e}_{i},

where

We denote by

the interior of a set

. Moreover,
K>0
denotes a generic positive constant that may change from line to line in the mathematical contents. At all occurrences of using supremum/infimum, we mean the

For self-adjoint operators

2.
Forward problem with extreme inclusions

We consider a bounded Lipschitz domain, with connected complement,
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, occupied by an isotropic material with linear stress-strain relation. The non-empty open boundary pieces
\Gamma_{\textup{D}},\Gamma_{\textup{N}}\subset\partial\Omega
are the corresponding Dirichlet and Neumann boundaries. The choice of mixed boundary conditions is based on the physical treatment of the elasticity problem. The Neumann-to-Dirichlet operator with a fixed Dirichlet part is an idealized model for fixing an elastic object in place on one part of the boundary, applying different pressure patterns to the remaining part, and measuring the resulting displacements.

Let
C_{0}
be the part of the domain that is perfectly elastic and
C_{\infty}
the part of the domain that is infinitely stiff. We assume the extreme inclusions satisfy the following conditions.

Assumption 2.1
.

Let
C=C_{0}\cup C_{\infty}
where
C_{0},C_{\infty}\subset\Omega
satisfy:

(i)

C_{0}
and
C_{\infty}
are closures of open sets with Lipschitz boundaries.

(ii)

C_{0}\cap C_{\infty}=\emptyset
.

(iii)

\Omega\setminus C_{0}
is connected.

Note that
C_{0}
and
C_{\infty}
can consist of several connected components and are also allowed to be empty.

If

\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}\in L_{+}^{\infty}(\Omega)=\{\,w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega):\inf(w)>0\,\}

denote typical non-extreme Lamé parameters, we can formally allow extreme Lamé parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
by writing

\lambda=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus C\\
0&\text{in }C_{0}\\
\infty&\text{in }C_{\infty}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu=\begin{cases}\mu_{0}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus C\\
0&\text{in }C_{0}\\
\infty&\text{in }C_{\infty}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

The displacement vector
u\colon\Omega\setminus C_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the following elliptic boundary value problem:

\begin{split}\nabla\cdot\bigl(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\widehat{\nabla}u\bigr)&=0\text{ in }\Omega\setminus C,\\
u&=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\textup{D}},\\
\bigl(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\widehat{\nabla}u\bigr)n&=\begin{cases}g&\text{on }\Gamma_{\textup{N}},\\
0&\text{on }\partial C_{0},\end{cases}\\
\widehat{\nabla}u&=0\text{ in }C_{\infty}^{\circ},\\
\int_{\partial C_{i}}\bigl(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\widehat{\nabla}u\bigr)n\,\mathrm{d}S&=0\text{ for each component $C_{i}$ of $C_{\infty}$.}\end{split}

(2.2)

Here
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Gamma_{\textup{D}}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}
and
\Gamma_{\textup{D}}\cap\Gamma_{\textup{N}}=\emptyset
. Moreover,
n
is the outer unit normal vector to
\Omega\setminus C
,
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}
the boundary load (pressure field), and
I
is the
d\times d
identity matrix. As
C_{0}
is perfectly elastic, the pressure field vanishes on its boundary, and as
C_{\infty}
is rigid, the displacement field in its interior is constant. Furthermore, the integral of the pressure field over the boundary of each component of
C_{\infty}
must vanish at an equilibrium because otherwise these rigid bodies would be moving.

Note that
u
is only defined in
\Omega\setminus C_{0}
. There are many different ways to extend
u
into
C_{0}
. In this paper, when it is convenient, we may use the following
H^{1}
-extension
Eu
instead of
u
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E\colon H^{1}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d}\to H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
be defined as

Ew=\begin{cases}w&\textup{in }\Omega\setminus C_{0},\\
\widetilde{w}&\textup{in }C_{0},\end{cases}

where
\widetilde{w}
is the unique solution in
H^{1}(C_{0}^{\circ})^{d}
to the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bigl(\lambda_{0}(\nabla\cdot\widetilde{w})I+2\mu_{0}\widehat{\nabla}\widetilde{w}\bigr)

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }C_{0}^{\circ},

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}

\displaystyle=w\text{ on }\partial C_{0}.

In the rest of the paper, we will not explicitly specify which background Lamé parameters and which set
C_{0}
that is used for the extension operator
E
. It should be implicitly understood when we write
Eu
, that the background Lamé parameters and the corresponding
C_{0}
set used in the definition of
u
are also used for the extension
Eu
.

For the sake of comparison, in case
C_{0}=C_{\infty}=\emptyset
, we also write down the standard non-extreme PDE problem:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bigl(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\widehat{\nabla}u\bigr)

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\textup{D}},

\displaystyle\bigl(\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)I+2\mu\widehat{\nabla}u\bigr)n

\displaystyle=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\textup{N}}.

2.1.
Variational formulation

Consider the Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{C_{\infty}}=\bigl\{\,v\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d}:\widehat{\nabla}v=0\text{ in }C_{\infty}^{\circ}\text{ and }v|_{\Gamma_{\textup{D}}}=0\,\bigr\}.

We may equip
\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{C_{\infty}}
with either of the inner products

\displaystyle\langle w,v\rangle_{\lambda,\mu}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus C_{0}}\lambda_{0}(\nabla\cdot w)(\nabla\cdot v)+2\mu_{0}\widehat{\nabla}w:\widehat{\nabla}v\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\nabla}w,\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d\times d}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus C_{0}}\widehat{\nabla}w:\widehat{\nabla}v\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2.4)

Due to a Poincaré inequality, based on the vanishing trace on
\Gamma_{\textup{D}}
, and by Korn’s inequality, we have (c.f.
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
)

\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d\times d}}\leq K\lVert\widehat{\nabla}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d\times d}},\quad v\in\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{C_{\infty}}.

(2.5)

Hence, again by the Poincaré inequality, and as

\lvert\nabla\cdot v\rvert=\lvert\Tr(\widehat{\nabla}v)\rvert\leq\sqrt{d}\lVert\widehat{\nabla}v\rVert_{\textup{F}},

(2.6)

the norms
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert_{\lambda,\mu}
and
\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\,\cdot\,\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d\times d}}
are equivalent to the usual
H^{1}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d}
-norm on
\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{C_{\infty}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

As a slight abuse of notation,
we occasionally denote by
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\lambda,\mu}
the inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{\emptyset}
defined by the same expression (
2.3
); see, e.g. Section
6
.

The weak problem formulation of (
2.2
) is

\langle u,v\rangle_{\lambda,\mu}=\langle g,v\rangle\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{C_{\infty}}.

(2.7)

As mentioned in Section
1.1
,
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is the standard inner product on
L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}
, with
\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert
denoting the associated norm. The Lax–Milgram lemma guarantees the existence of a unique solution
u=u_{g}^{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathcal{H}_{C_{0}}^{C_{\infty}}
to the weak problem (
2.7
); we will often use this notation to make it clear which Neumann data and which Lamé parameters are in use. This also gives the continuous dependence on the Neumann data:

\lVert u_{g}^{\lambda,\mu}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d}}\leq K\lVert g\rVert.

We define the Neumann-to-Dirichlet (ND) map
\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)
by

\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)\colon L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d},\quad g\mapsto u_{g}^{\lambda,\mu}|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}},

which is compact and self-adjoint. From the weak form, we get

\langle\Lambda(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})g,g\rangle=\langle u_{g}^{\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}},Eu_{g}^{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}\rangle_{\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}}

(2.8)

for any pairs
(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})
for which the
C_{\infty}
inclusions of the extreme Lamé parameter pair
(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})
are contained in those of
(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})
. In particular, if
(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})=(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})
, this becomes

\langle\Lambda(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})g,g\rangle=\lVert u_{g}^{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}\rVert_{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}^{2}.

(2.9)

3.
Extreme measurements as a limit

In this section, we retrieve the displacement vector field and the ND map in the case of perfectly elastic and infinitely stiff inclusions via a limit of problems that correspond to a sequence of truncated Lamé parameters that belong to
L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
. This also ensures that the PDE formulation (
2.2
) and its weak form (
2.7
) indeed are the correct interpretations of how to model such extreme inclusions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C=C_{0}\cup C_{\infty}
satisfy Assumption
2.1
, let
\lambda
and
\mu
be as in (
2.1
), and define the
\epsilon
-truncated versions of
\lambda
and
\mu
, with
\epsilon>0
, by

\lambda_{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus C\\
\epsilon\lambda_{0}&\text{in }C_{0}\\
\epsilon^{-1}\lambda_{0}&\text{in }C_{\infty}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\mu_{0}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus C\\
\epsilon\mu_{0}&\text{in }C_{0}\\
\epsilon^{-1}\mu_{0}&\text{in }C_{\infty}.\end{cases}

Then

\lVert Eu_{g}^{\lambda,\mu}-u_{g}^{\lambda_{\epsilon},\mu_{\epsilon}}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}}\leq K\epsilon^{1/2}\lVert g\rVert.

As a direct consequence,

\lVert\Lambda(\lambda,\mu)-\Lambda(\lambda_{\epsilon},\mu_{\epsilon})\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d})}\leq K\epsilon^{1/2}.

Proof.

The proof is given in Section
7
.
∎

4.
Reconstruction of extreme inclusions

Next, we will consider the reconstruction of inclusions via the monotonicity method. In order to obtain exact reconstruction methods, a unique continuation principle is needed for the background parameters; see Section
10
.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
U\subseteq\overline{\Omega}
is a relatively open and connected set that intersects
\Gamma_{\textup{N}}
. We say that
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
satisfy the weak
unique continuation principle
(UCP) in
U
for the linear elasticity problem, provided that only the trivial solution of

\nabla\cdot\bigl(\lambda_{0}(\nabla\cdot v)I+2\mu_{0}\widehat{\nabla}v\bigr)=0\text{ in }U^{\circ}

can be identically zero in a non-empty open subset of
U
, and likewise, only the trivial solution has vanishing Cauchy data on
\partial U\cap\Gamma_{\textup{N}}
. If
U=\overline{\Omega}
we simply say that
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
satisfy the UCP.

Remark 4.2
.

From
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
10
, Corollary 2.2]
(and the subsequent remarks therein), it follows that a sufficient condition for the UCP to hold for
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
is that
\Omega
is a bounded

domain,
\lambda_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
, and
\mu_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,1}(\Omega)
.

Definition 4.3
.

Consider mutually disjoint (possibly empty) sets
D_{\textup{F}}^{+}
,
D_{\textup{F}}^{-}
,
D_{0}
and

, with
D_{0},D_{\infty}\in\mathcal{A}
and
D_{\textup{F}}^{+},D_{\textup{F}}^{-}\subset\Omega

\lambda_{0},\mu_{0},\lambda_{\pm},\mu_{\pm}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
be such that

\lambda_{-}\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{-}\leq\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{+}.

We say that
\lambda
and
\mu
have

in

if they are of the form

\lambda=\begin{cases}\lambda_{0}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus D\\
\lambda_{-}&\text{in }D_{\textup{F}}^{-}\\
\lambda_{+}&\text{in }D_{\textup{F}}^{+}\\
0&\text{in }D_{0}\\
\infty&\text{in }D_{\infty}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu=\begin{cases}\mu_{0}&\text{in }\Omega\setminus D\\
\mu_{-}&\text{in }D_{\textup{F}}^{-}\\
\mu_{+}&\text{in }D_{\textup{F}}^{+}\\
0&\text{in }D_{0}\\
\infty&\text{in }D_{\infty}.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Note that
\lambda_{\pm}
and
\mu_{\pm}
are not required to deviate from the background parameters at the same locations inside

The following technical assumption ensures that certain pathological cases cannot occur. It allows

Assumption 4.4
.

Consider the setting in Definition

. For every open neighborhood

, there exists a relatively open set

that intersects

, and

5.
Linearized outer reconstruction of non-extreme inclusions

Regarding Definition
4.3

Lemma 6.2

9.
Virtual measurement operators

We will need some results on so-called
virtual measurement operators
for Section
10
; see
[
10
,
15
,
16
,
18
]
for more information on these types of operators. Let
V\subseteq\Omega
be measurable and define the operators
\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})\colon L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}
and
L_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})\colon L^{2}(V)\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}
by

\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})F=\widehat{w}_{F}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})G=w_{G}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}

for
\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
. Here
\widehat{w}=\widehat{w}_{F}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
and
w=w_{G}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
are the unique solutions in
\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}
of the variational problems

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{w},v\rangle_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle F,\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}},

(9.1)

\displaystyle\langle w,v\rangle_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle G,\nabla\cdot v\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}

(9.2)

for all
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}
. We have the following properties, with
R
denoting the
range
of an operator.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
V,V_{1},V_{2}
be measurable subsets of
\Omega
.

(i)

If
V
has Lipschitz boundary and
\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
satisfy

\mathop{\textup{supp}}(\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{1})\cup\mathop{\textup{supp}}(\mu_{0}-\mu_{1})\subseteq V,

then

R(L_{V}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}))\subseteq R(\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))=R(\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})).

(ii)

V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}
implies

R(\widehat{L}_{V_{1}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))\subseteq R(\widehat{L}_{V_{2}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))\quad\text{and}\quad R(L_{V_{1}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))\subseteq R(L_{V_{2}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})).

(iii)

For
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\textup{N}})^{d}
, the adjoints satisfy

\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})^{*}g=\widehat{\nabla}u_{g}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}|_{V}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})^{*}g=\nabla\cdot u_{g}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}|_{V}.

Proof.

(i):
Let there be another pair of Lamé parameters
\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)
, which only may differ from
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
in
V
. Let
\varphi\in R(\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))
, i.e.,

\varphi=\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})F_{0}=w_{0}|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}},

for some
F_{0}\in L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}
and
w_{0}=\widehat{w}_{F_{0}}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\langle w_{0},v\rangle_{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})(\nabla\cdot w_{0})(\nabla\cdot v)+2(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0})\widehat{\nabla}w_{0}:\widehat{\nabla}v\,\mathrm{d}x+\langle w_{0},v\rangle_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{V}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})(\nabla\cdot w_{0})(\nabla\cdot v)+2(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0})\widehat{\nabla}w_{0}:\widehat{\nabla}v\,\mathrm{d}x+\langle F_{0},\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}}.

(9.3)

In the following, we may assume that
V
consists of a single connected component because otherwise the integral in (
9.3
) can be separately considered on each connected component, as can the rest of the proof.

Consider the Hilbert space

\mathcal{V}=\Bigl\{\,v\in H^{1}(V^{\circ}):\int_{V}v\,\mathrm{d}x=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\Bigr\}

(9.4)

and the auxiliary variational problem

\langle\widehat{\nabla}h,\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}}=\int_{V}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{0})(\nabla\cdot w_{0})(\nabla\cdot v)+2(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0})\widehat{\nabla}w_{0}:\widehat{\nabla}v\,\mathrm{d}x

(9.5)

for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
. The zero-mean condition in (
9.4
), combined with Poincaré’s and Korn’s inequalities, ensures that the left-hand side of (
9.5
) defines on
\mathcal{V}
an inner product, which induces a norm that is equivalent to the usual
H^{1}
-norm on
\mathcal{V}
.
In consequence, the Lax–Milgram lemma ensures a unique solution
h\in\mathcal{V}
to (
9.5
).

Returning to (
9.3
), we can assume that the considered
v
satisfy
v|_{V}\in\mathcal{V}
, since the values of the expressions on the right-hand side of (
9.3
) do not change if a constant vector is subtracted from
v
. Hence we have

\langle w_{0},v\rangle_{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}=\langle\widehat{\nabla}h+F_{0},\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}},

and the unique solvability of (
9.1
) implies

\varphi=w_{0}|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}=\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})F_{1}

for
F_{1}=\widehat{\nabla}h+F_{0}
. Thereby, we have proven
R(\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))\subseteq R(\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}))
. The opposite set inclusion follows from the same proof, by swapping the roles of
(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
and
(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})
.

Now for the proof involving
L_{V}
, we immediately get
R(L_{V}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}))\subseteq R(\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}))
from part (iii) of this proposition, (
2.6
), and Lemma
9.3
.

(ii):
We only give the proof for
\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
since the one for
L_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
is almost identical. Let
\varphi=\widehat{L}_{V_{1}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})F_{1}=\widehat{w}|_{\Gamma_{\textup{N}}}
for some
F_{1}\in L^{2}(V_{1})^{d\times d}
and
\widehat{w}=\widehat{w}_{F_{1}}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
. As
V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}
, we can extend
F_{1}
by zero to
F_{2}\in L^{2}(V_{2})^{d\times d}
, and it immediately follows that

\langle\widehat{w},v\rangle_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}=\langle F_{1},\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(V_{1})^{d\times d}}=\langle F_{2},\widehat{\nabla}v\rangle_{L^{2}(V_{2})^{d\times d}}

for all
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}
. Hence,
\varphi=\widehat{L}_{V_{2}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})F_{2}
, and therefore
R(\widehat{L}_{V_{1}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))\subseteq R(\widehat{L}_{V_{2}}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}))
.

(iii):
Again, we only present the proof for
\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
since the one for
L_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})
is almost identical. The result follows from a short calculation that utilizes the variational problem (
9.1
) for
\widehat{w}=\widehat{w}_{F}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
and the weak problem (
2.7
) for
u=u_{g}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})^{*}g,F\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}}

\displaystyle=\langle g,\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})F\rangle=\langle g,\widehat{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,\widehat{w}\rangle_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}=\langle\widehat{\nabla}u,F\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d\times d}},

and thus
\widehat{L}_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})^{*}g=\widehat{\nabla}u|_{V}
.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

Note that we only have the equality of ranges for
\widehat{L}
in Proposition
9.1
(i). The natural approach to proving the range equality also for
L
would require solving an auxiliary variational problem like (
9.5
), but with the left hand-side
\langle\nabla\cdot h,\nabla\cdot v\rangle_{L^{2}(V)}
, which clearly does not define an inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}
. One can try to fix this flaw by only considering (
9.2
) for
v\in\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
, where
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}
are the functions with
\nabla\cdot v\equiv 0
and the orthogonal complement
\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is with respect to
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}
. This construction still gives
L_{V}(\lambda_{0},\mu_{0})^{*}g=\nabla\cdot u_{g}^{\lambda_{0},\mu_{0}}|_{V}
. However, while we get an inner product, its norm is not equivalent to the
H^{1}
-norm, so we lack the Hilbert space property needed for the Lax–Milgram lemma.

The range inclusions can be translated into norm estimates for the adjoints.

Lemma 9.3

(Lemma 2.5 in
[
16
]
)
.

Let
H
,
K_{1}
, and
K_{2}
be Hilbert spaces and let
A_{j}\in\mathscr{L}(K_{j},H)
for
j=1,2
. Then

R(A_{1})\subseteq R(A_{2})\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad\exists K>0,\forall x\in H\colon\lVert A_{1}^{*}x\rVert_{K_{1}}\leq K\lVert A_{2}^{*}x\rVert_{K_{2}}.

In this section, we assume

One of the core ingredients in proving the “difficult direction” for monotonicity-based methods, such as the latter statements in Theorems

First we prove that such localization can partially be transferred to another set of non-extreme Lamé parameters, provided that the differences in the two sets of Lamé parameters occur in the part of the domain where the localized potentials tend to zero.

Lemma 10.2
.

U
and
B
be as in Lemma

, and such that

) and (

. Define

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B}\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}_{i}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus U}\lvert\nabla\cdot\widehat{u}_{i}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

Proof.

Let
V_{1}=\Omega\setminus\overline{U}
and
V_{2}=B
. Using Proposition
9.1
(i), we have

and

which by (

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus U}\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}_{i}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

10.4

Lemma 10.3

and

We start by using Lemma

to write

). By the continuous dependence of

on its Neumann trace and a bound similar to (

) (involving

\displaystyle\lVert\widehat{\nabla}w_{i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d\times d}}

(10.6)

where the limit is a consequence of (

). Hence, by (

) and (

and

and
\widehat{\nabla}u_{i}|_{\Omega\setminus C_{0}}

\displaystyle=\infty,

(10.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus(U\cup C_{0})}\lvert\nabla\cdot v_{i}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus(U\cup C_{0})}\lVert\widehat{\nabla}v_{i}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=0.

Using Lemma
6.2
,

\lVert\widehat{\nabla}P^{\perp}v_{i}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus C_{0})^{d\times d}}\leq K\lVert P^{\perp}v_{i}\rVert_{\lambda,\mu}\leq K\lVert\widehat{\nabla}v_{i}\rVert_{L^{2}(C_{\infty})^{d\times d}}\to 0\text{ for }i\to\infty,

) and the set inclusion

. Since

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B}\lvert\nabla\cdot\widehat{v}_{i}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B}\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{v}_{i}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus(U\cup C_{0})}\lvert\nabla\cdot\widehat{v}_{i}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus(U\cup C_{0})}\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{v}_{i}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

As

\widehat{u}_{i}|_{\partial C_{0}}

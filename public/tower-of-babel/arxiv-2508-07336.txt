Best m m -term trigonometric approximation in weighted Wiener spaces and applications
Moritz Moeller, Serhii Stasyuk, Tino Ullrich

Abstract
In this paper we study best m m -term trigonometric approximation of functions belonging to multivariate weighted Wiener spaces. It has recently been observed that best m m -term trigonometric widths in the uniform and Wiener norm together with nonlinear recovery algorithms stemming from compressed sensing serve to control the optimal sampling recovery error in various relevant spaces of multivariate functions. We use a collection of old and new tools as well as novel findings to extend these recovery bounds. In addition, by establishing embeddings of classical smoothness spaces into weighted Wiener spaces we extend recovery bounds to classical multivariate smoothness spaces.

\|f-R_{m}\big(f;\mathbf{X})\|_{L_{q}}\leq Cn^{1/2-1/q}\left(n^{-1/2}\sigma_{n}\big(f\big)_{{\mathcal{A}}}+E_{[-M,M]^{d}}\big(f\big)_{L_{\infty}}\right)\,.

(1.1)

The main strategy to prove bounds for best
n
-term trigonometric approximation errors for weighted Wiener spaces is a classical decomposition strategy. Starting from a decomposition according to frequencies with roughly equal weights we then employ results for unweighted Wiener spaces. Throughout this paper we will make use of results that we obtained in our previous paper

The first result shown with this strategy, Theorem

0<\theta\leq\infty
,
2\leq q<\infty
and

\sigma_{m}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{L_{q}}\asymp m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}})}(\log^{\ast}\hskip-1.0ptm)^{(d-1)r},

where in the case of

we get an additional
\sqrt{\log^{\ast}(m)}
in the upper bound (with the same lower bound as before). Similar results of this type were already shown for mixed Wiener spaces in
[

17

due to the techniques used in those proofs (embedding into Sobolev

[
19
]
, we can get this for
r>(1-1/\theta)_{+}
, and specifically if
\theta\leq 1
, i.e. in the setting of mixed Wiener spaces, we only have the trivial condition

. Results for related isotropic spaces
have been obtained by DeVore, Temlyakov
[
8
]

In addition, motivated by Theorem
3.7
, we consider embeddings of weighted into unweighted Wiener spaces. Theorem
4.5
states, for
\theta,\eta\in(0,\infty]

\sigma_{m}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}\asymp m^{\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\theta}-r}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r},

where we measure the error in the

Corollary

\varrho_{m\log^{\ast}(m)^{3}}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{L_{q}}\lesssim m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.

(1.3)

This asymptotic bound is sharp up to a factor in the logarithmic rate that is independent of

. Indeed, lower bounds on the linear and non-linear sampling widths in weighted Wiener spaces can be shown using the natural lower bounds obtained by linear and Gelfand widths, see Pietsch
[

2
Weighted Wiener spaces

Before we discuss weighted Wiener spaces, let us briefly introduce the classical Wiener algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

For
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
we define the norm of
f
in the Wiener Algebra as the sum of absolute values of the Fourier coefficients

\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}:=\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}:=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|\,

with

\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\mathbf{x})\exp(-2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\quad,\quad\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

We define the Wiener space
\mathcal{A}
to be the space of all functions from
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
where this norm is finite.

Definition 2.2

(Weighted (mixed) Wiener space)
.

For
r\geq 0
and
0<\theta<\infty
we define the (quasi-)norm

\|f\|_{S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+|k_{i}|)^{r\theta}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|^{\theta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

The corresponding space

S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\coloneqq S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\Big\{f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\penalty\ :\penalty\ \|f\|_{S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}}<\infty\Big\}

(2.1)

is called weighted (quasi-)Wiener space of functions with mixed smoothness.
In the case
\theta=\infty
the usual modification is made and the space is called Korobov space (see e.g.
[
9
, Section 3.3]
). The space
S^{0}_{1}\mathcal{A}
is simply the (original) Wiener space
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}:=S^{0}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}
. The mixed Wiener spaces
S_{1}^{r}\mathcal{A}
are often referred to in the literature as
\mathcal{A}^{r}_{\rm mix}
.

Remark 2.3
.

While the space
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}
is a function space, it can also be embedded into
\ell_{\theta}
quite naturally for all
\theta>0
by the following isometric operator

A_{r}f=\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1+|k_{i}|)^{r}\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})\bigg)_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}.

This also immediately enables the usage of the Stechkin Lemma
3.2
for Wiener spaces.

3
Several widths

We start by defining several approximation widths. These quantities are closely related to
s
-numbers and share many of their properties, see e.g.
[
23
]
. Our main focus lies on best
m
-term trigonometric approximation widths which probably first appeared in Pietsch
[
22
]
. The best
m
-term approximation did return to the forefront of research when the theory of compressed sensing evolved. The recent papers
[
13
,
15
,
17
,
18
]
linked these widths to errors of function recovery from samples.

3.1
Trigonometric best
m
-term approximation widths

Definition 3.1

(Best
m
-term approximation width)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-Banach space and
\mathcal{D}\subset X
a dictionary.

(i) For
f\in X
we
define its best
m
-term approximation error with respect to the dictionary
\mathcal{D}
as

\sigma_{m}(f)_{X}\coloneqq\sigma_{m}(f,\mathcal{D})_{X}\coloneqq\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{m}}\|f-s\|_{X},

where
\Sigma_{m}
is the set of all linear combinations of at most
m
elements of the dictionary
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii) Let
\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow X
denote a further quasi-Banach space continuously embedded into
X
. The best
m
-term approximation width of for
\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow X
is defined accordingly as

\sigma_{m}(\mathcal{F})_{X}\coloneqq\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1}\sigma_{m}(f)_{X}.

Note, the best
m
-term approximation widths satisfy a multiplicative property (typical for
s
-numbers, although they are not
s
-numbers), see e.g.
[
16
, Lemma 2.8]
. For spaces
X,Y,Z
with corresponding embeddings the following relation holds

\sigma_{m_{1}+m_{2}}(X)_{Z}\leq\sigma_{m_{1}}(X)_{Y}\sigma_{m_{2}}(Y)_{Z}.

(3.1)

In this paper we essentially deal with two different, however related, situations. Our main interest is the multivariate trigonometric dictionary given by
\mathcal{T}^{d}=\{\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot)\penalty\ :\penalty\ \mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
, such that

\Sigma_{m}:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in\Lambda_{m}}a_{\mathbf{k}}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot)\penalty\ :\penalty\ \#\Lambda_{m}\leq m,a_{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{C}\Big\}\,.

On the other hand we consider a discrete situation for sequences
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{p}
, where

\sigma_{m}(\mathbf{x})_{X}:=\inf\limits_{\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\ell_{0}}\leq m}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{X}

fitting also to the framework from Definition
3.1
. Note, that the corresponding dictionary is given by the set of all sequences with a single nonzero entry. A classical result for the best
m
-term approximation error measured in
\ell_{q}
is often referred to as the Stechkin Lemma, see
[
9
, Lemma 7.8]
and the references there.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<p<q\leq\infty
. Then it holds for all
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{p}

\sigma_{m}(\mathbf{x})_{\ell_{q}}\leq(m+1)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{p}}\,.

(3.2)

Apart from best
m
-term, i.e. nonlinear approximation, we need the best linear approximation with respect to a given subspace of trigonometric polynomials.

Definition 3.3

(Error of best trigonometric approximation)
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
. The error of best trigonometric approximation of a function space
\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow L_{\infty}
is defined as

E_{[-M,M]^{d}}(\mathcal{F})_{L_{\infty}}\hskip-2.0pt\coloneqq\hskip-2.0pt{\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1}}{E_{[-M,M]^{d}}\big(f\big)_{L_{\infty}}\coloneqq}\hskip-2.0pt{\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1}}\inf_{g\in\mathcal{T}([-M,M]^{d})}\|f-g\|_{L_{\infty}}\,.

(3.3)

3.2
Sampling widths

Let us first introduce the (non-linear) sampling width. It represents the optimal worst-case error for recovery using optimal “standard information” (point evaluations).

Definition 3.4

(Non-linear sampling width)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a (quasi-)normed space of functions on
D
, where function evaluations are continuous, which is continuously embedded into a Banach space
Y
. Then the
m
-th (generally non-linear) sampling width is defined as

\varrho_{m}(\mathcal{F})_{Y}\coloneqq\inf_{t_{1},\ldots,{t_{m}}\in D}\inf_{R\colon{\mathbb{C}^{m}}\to Y}\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1}\big\|f-R\big(f(t_{1}),\ldots,f({t_{m}})\big)\big\|_{Y}.

(3.4)

We also define its linear counterpart.

Definition 3.5

(Linear sampling width)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
again be a (quasi-)normed space of functions on
D
, where function evaluations are continuous, which is continuously embedded into a Banach space
Y
. Then the
m
-th linear sampling width is defined as

\varrho^{\operatorname{lin}}_{m}(\mathcal{F})_{Y}\coloneqq\inf_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\in D}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to Y\\
{\rm linear}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 1}\big\|f-R\big(f(t_{1}),\dots,f(t_{m})\big)\big\|_{Y}.

(3.5)

Note that
R\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to Y
is restricted to linear maps here. This is the only difference between the two definitions.

For
\mathbf{X}=\{\mathbf{x}^{1},...,\mathbf{x}^{m}\}
with
\mathbf{x}^{i}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, we will write

R_{m}(f;\mathbf{X})\coloneqq R\big(f(\mathbf{x}^{1}),\ldots,f(\mathbf{x}^{m})\big)

for short.

Jahn, T. Ullrich and Voigtlaender have shown in
[
13
]
, based on compressed sensing techniques (basis pursuit denoising), that the sampling widths measured in

can be bounded from above by a sum of the best
m
-term approximation width and the error of the best trigonometric approximation measured in
L_{\infty}
.

This result was then refined to an instance optimal version for more general
L_{q}
,
2\leq q\leq\infty
, spaces instead of only
L_{2}
in Moeller, Pozharska, T. Ullrich
[
17
,
18
]
. The oversampling needed for this result comes from the (random ) construction of a restricted isometry Fourier matrix, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[

]
.

(
[

Theorem 3.7
.

For any

there exists a universal constant

We will make use of the specific version of the de la Vallée Poussin operator

Proof of Theorem

We follow the proof of

\end{split}

(3.12)

with high probability and therefore the existence of such a recovery operator

from above, by introducing

&\leq E_{[-M,M]^{d}}\big(f\big)_{L_{\infty}}+e\|f_{M}-f\|_{L_{\infty}}\\

(3.15)

Theorem 4.1

If

(or

We will use two auxiliary Lemmas which we state here.

Lemma 4.2
.

For arbitrary
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma>0
and
L\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\sum_{k=L+1}^{\infty}(k-L)^{\alpha}k^{\beta}2^{-\gamma k}\lesssim L^{\beta}2^{-\gamma L}

(4.1)

Proof.

Since
L\geq 1
we have for all
k\geq L+1
that
k\geq L+1\geq L\frac{2L}{2L-1}
. This implies
k\leq 2L(k-L)
. From that we now get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k=L+1}^{\infty}(k-L)^{\alpha}k^{\beta}2^{-\gamma k}&\leq\sum_{k=L+1}^{\infty}(k-L)^{\alpha+\beta}(2L)^{\beta}2^{-\gamma k}\\
&=(2L)^{\beta}2^{-\gamma L}\sum_{k=L+1}^{\infty}(k-L)^{\alpha+\beta}2^{-\gamma(k-L)}\\
&=(2L)^{\beta}2^{-\gamma L}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha+\beta}2^{-\gamma n}\\
&\lesssim L^{\beta}2^{-\gamma L}.\end{split}

(4.2)

∎

This second Lemma is an easy consequence of the Hölder inequality

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{p}
and
q\geq p
. Then it holds

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{p}}\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{q}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{0}}^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

(4.3)

Proof.

We simply apply the Hölder inequality with
\tau:=q/p>1
to

and
\sum_{k\in\operatorname{supp}\mathbf{x}}1
. This yields,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{p}}&=\big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|x_{k}|^{p}\cdot\sum_{k\in\operatorname{supp}\mathbf{x}}1\big)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\Big(\big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(|x_{k}|^{p})^{\frac{q}{p}}\big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\big(\sum_{k\in\operatorname{supp}\mathbf{x}}1^{\frac{q}{q-p}}\big)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=\big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|x_{k}|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{0}}^{\frac{q-p}{qp}},\\
\end{split}

(4.4)

where we used the usual notation
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{0}}\coloneqq\#\operatorname{supp}{\mathbf{x}}
.
∎

&\geq m^{-(r+\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{2})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}\big(\#\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n}-\,\#K_{n}\big)\\
&\gtrsim m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.\end{split}

(4.27)

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
4.26
) yields

\langle f-h,g\rangle\leq\|f-h\|_{L_{2}}\,\|g\|_{L_{2}}\lesssim\|f-h\|_{L_{2}}.

(4.28)

Using now (
4.27
) and (
4.28
) gives the lower bound

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{m}\Big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\Big)_{L_{q}}{\asymp\sigma_{C_{n}/2}\Big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\Big)_{L_{q}}}&\gtrsim\inf_{h\in\Sigma_{C_{n}/2}}\|f-h\|_{{L_{2}}}\\
&\gtrsim\inf_{h\in\Sigma_{C_{n}/2}}\sup_{\|g\|_{L_{2}}\leq 1}\langle f-h,g\rangle\\
&\gtrsim m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.\end{split}

(4.29)

The restrictions on

can now again be discarded by arguing with monotonicity.
∎

Theorem 4.5

For

\sigma_{m}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}\asymp m^{\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{\theta}-r}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.

Proof.

For every
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define the corresponding weight according to the
S^{1}_{1}\mathcal{A}
-norm as

\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}(|k_{j}|+1).

(4.32)

For
\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n}
, clearly it holds that

(4.33)

This also implies for any

\sup_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n-d}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n+d}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}.

(4.34)

Let
J:{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\to\mathbb{N}
be an ordering, such that the weights
\omega_{J^{-1}(k)}
are non-decreasing. We can now extend the definition of
\omega
to
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\omega_{k}\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}(|J^{-1}(k)_{j}|+1).

(4.35)

We start with the simpler regime where
\infty\geq\eta\geq\theta>0
. By the multiplicativity of the best
m
-term approximation widths and Lemma
4.4
we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{2m}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{m}(\mathcal{A}_{\theta})_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}\,\,\sigma_{m}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{\mathcal{A}_{\theta}}

(4.36)

\displaystyle\lesssim m^{1/\eta-1/\theta}\sup_{\|f\|_{S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\Big(\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\big|\hat{f}(J^{-1}(k))\big|^{\theta}\Big)^{1/\theta}

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leq m^{1/\eta-1/\theta}\omega_{m}^{-r}\sup_{\|f\|_{S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\Big(\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\big|\hat{f}(J^{-1}(k))\big|^{\theta}\omega_{k}^{r\theta}\Big)^{1/\theta}

(4.38)

\displaystyle\leq m^{1/\eta-1/\theta}\omega_{m}^{-r}.

(4.39)

Now we just need to calculate
\omega_{m}
. To this end we assume
m=2^{n}
and consider the step hyperbolic layers from (
4.9
). By (
4.11
) we know that the
n
-th hyperbolic layer has asymptotically
2^{n}n^{d-1}
entries. Taking the sum up to
n
yields

{\sum_{l=0}^{n}\#\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{l}\asymp}\sum_{l=0}^{n}2^{l}l^{d-1}\asymp 2^{n}n^{d-1}.

(4.40)

Therefore by (
4.33
) and (
4.34
) we have,

\displaystyle 2^{n}\lesssim 2^{n-d}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n-d}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\leq\omega_{2^{n}n^{d-1}}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{n+d}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\lesssim 2^{n},

(4.41)

i.e.
2^{n}\asymp\omega_{2^{n}n^{d-1}}
which is equivalent to stating

\omega_{2^{n}}\asymp\omega_{2^{n-(d-1)\log^{\ast}(n)}(n-(d-1)\log^{\ast}(n))^{d-1}}\asymp 2^{n-(d-1)\log^{\ast}(n)},

(4.42)

and this implies

\omega_{m}=\omega_{2^{n}}\asymp 2^{n-(d-1)\log^{\ast}(n)}=m\log^{\ast}(m)^{1-d},

(4.43)

which concludes the first part of the proof after arguing again with monotonicity and employing the embedding from the beginning of the proof.

Now we continue with the more involved situation where
\infty\geq\theta>\eta>0
, here we have the additional condition

.
We set
m=2^{n}
and choose

as in (
4.12
). We start the estimation by decomposing onto our uniform hyperbolic layers and reformulating the
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
-norm in terms of the
{S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}
-norm. This gives

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}\big(S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}\big)_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|f\|_{S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{m}}\|f-s\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}

(4.44)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})-\hat{s}(\mathbf{k})|^{\eta}\Big)^{1/\eta}

(4.45)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|f\|_{S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{j}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})-\hat{s}(\mathbf{k})|^{\eta}\Big)^{1/\eta}

(4.46)

\displaystyle{\lesssim\sup_{\|f\|_{S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\Big(\sum_{j=L+1}^{\infty}\big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{j}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|^{\eta}\big)^{\eta/\eta}\Big)^{1/\eta}.}

(4.47)

Where in the last line we used that
\sum_{j=0}^{L}C_{j}\lesssim m
. Now we estimate the
\ell_{\eta}
-norm of a sequence with at most

non-zero entries (see (

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{m}\big(S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}\big)_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}&\lesssim\sup_{\|f\|_{S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}\leq 1}\Big(\sum_{j=L+1}^{\infty}C_{j}^{\frac{\theta-\eta}{\theta}}\big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\operatorname{\mathchoice{\includegraphics[]{hyp1.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp2.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp3.pdf}}{\includegraphics[]{hyp4.pdf}}}_{j}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|^{\theta}\big)^{\frac{\eta}{\theta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}\\

&\leq\Big(\sum_{j=L+1}^{\infty}2^{-j(r\eta-1+\frac{\eta}{\theta})}j^{(1-\frac{\eta}{\theta})(d-1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\eta}}.\end{split}

(4.48)

Since this sum is decaying geometrically we can apply Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{m}\big({S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}}\big)_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}&{\lesssim\big(2^{-L(r\eta-1+\frac{\eta}{\theta})}L^{(1-\frac{\eta}{\theta})(d-1)}\big)^{1/\eta}}\\
&\leq{2^{-(n-(d-1)\log^{\ast}(n))(r-1/\eta+1/\theta)}}(n\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt(d\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt1)\log^{\ast}(n))^{(1/\eta-1/\theta)(d-1)}\\

&=m^{-(r-1/\eta+1/\theta)}\log^{\ast}(m)^{r(d-1)}.\end{split}

To get a lower bound consider the trigonometric polynomial

(4.50)

This has the following

(4.51)

and therefore lies on the unit sphere of this space. Now it holds

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A})_{\mathcal{A}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\Big|\hat{t}(\mathbf{k})-\hat{s}(\mathbf{k})\Big|^{\eta}\Big)^{1/\eta}

(4.52)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{s\in\Sigma_{m}}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{2m}\Big|\frac{\omega_{k}^{-r}}{(2m)^{1/\theta}}-\hat{s}(J^{-1}(k))\Big|^{\eta}\Big)^{1/\eta}

(4.53)

\displaystyle\geq m^{1/\eta}\frac{\omega_{2m}^{-r}}{(2m)^{1/\theta}}

(4.54)

\displaystyle\gtrsim m^{1/\eta-1/\theta-r}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.

(4.55)

∎

r>\big(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\big)_{+}
behave like

c_{m}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{2}}\asymp m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}}-\frac{1}{2})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.

(6.3)

The Gelfand widths
c_{m}
, similarly to the Kolmogorov widths
d_{m}
, give a lower bound, but on the non-linear sampling widths, see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma B.1]
. We get the lower bound

\varrho_{m}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{2}}\gtrsim m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}}-\frac{1}{2})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r}.

(6.4)

Using Proposition
3.6
we can even get an upper bound on the non-linear sampling widths.

Theorem 6.1
.

For
0<\theta\leq\infty
,
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
r>\big(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\big)_{+}
it holds

{\varrho_{m}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{q}}\lesssim m^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}}+\frac{1}{q}-1)}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r+3(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})+\frac{1}{2}}.}

(6.5)

In particular we get for
\theta=1
and
q=2
,

\varrho_{m}\big(S^{r}_{1}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{2}}\lesssim m^{-(r+\frac{1}{2})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r+{3r+2}}.

(6.6)

Proof.

Choose
M=\big\lceil n^{({r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}}})\frac{1}{r}}\big\rceil
and take the supremum over all
f
in the unit ball of
S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}
and the infimum over the choice of sampling points. Then we get from Proposition
3.6
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varrho_{m}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{q}}&\leq Cn^{1/2-1/q}\left(\sigma_{n}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{\infty}}+E_{[-M,M]^{d}}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{\infty}}\right)\\
&\lesssim Cn^{1/2-1/q}(n^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}})}\log^{\ast}(n)^{(d-1)r+\frac{1}{2}}+M^{-r})\\
&\leq Cn^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}\log^{\ast}(n)^{(d-1)r+\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq Cm^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r+\frac{1}{2}+3(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}{,}\end{split}

(6.7)

where we use Theorem
4.1
in the second line and the minimal admissible choice of
m
in (
3.7
) from Proposition
3.6
in the fourth line. The term
E_{[-M,M]^{d}}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{\infty}}
can be estimated from above by
M^{-r}
, since from Theorem
4.5
we can conclude
\mathcal{A}^{r}_{\theta}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}(\hookrightarrow L_{\infty})
.

∎

This result generalizes
[
13
, Corollary 4.4 and Remark 4.5 (i)]
in multiple directions. Not only do we consider the more general class of weighted Wiener (
\theta
different from 1) spaces but also reduce the condition on
r
from
r>1/2
to
r>(1-1/\theta)_{+}
. In the setting where
\theta=1
, as in
[
13
]
this reduces to
r>0
. In addition we also consider the situation where the error is measured in Lebesgue spaces other than
L_{2}
.

We can still improve this result slightly by using Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
4.5
instead of Proposition
3.6
. Since we avoid the
L_{\infty}
norm, we can get rid of a factor
\sqrt{\log^{\ast}(m)}
that appears in Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 6.2
.

For
0<\theta{\,\leq\,}\infty
,
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
r>\big(1-\frac{1}{\theta}\big)_{+}
we get

{\varrho_{m}\big(S_{\theta}^{r}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{q}}\leq Cm^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r+3(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}.}

(6.8)

Proof.

Choose again
M=\big\lceil n^{({r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}}})\frac{1}{r}}\big\rceil
. Then we get from Proposition
3.7
and the optimal choice of
m
according to (
3.7
), together with Theorem
4.5
(with
\eta=1
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varrho_{m}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{q}}&\leq Cn^{1/2-1/q}\left(n^{-1/2}\sigma_{n}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\big)_{\mathcal{A}}+E_{[-M,M]^{d}}\big(S^{r}_{\theta}\mathcal{A}\big)_{L_{\infty}}\right)\\
&\leq Cn^{1/2-1/q}(n^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}-1+\frac{1}{2}})}\log^{\ast}(n)^{(d-1)r}+M^{-r})\\
&\leq Cn^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}\log^{\ast}(n)^{(d-1)r}\\
&\leq Cm^{-(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}\log^{\ast}(m)^{(d-1)r+3(r+{\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{q}-1})}.\end{split}

(6.9)

∎

Remark 6.3
.

Now we compare the lower bounds on the linear sampling widths (
6.2
) and the upper bounds on the non-linear sampling widths from Corollary
6.2
. We see that non-linear methods perform better than linear ones by a rate of
m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\ast}(m)^{3r+\frac{3}{2}}
for recovery of functions from mixed Wiener spaces (
\theta=1
), with the error measured in
L_{2}
. The main rate of this difference is independent of
r
, and since our result, unlike the previous ones in
[
13
]
, holds for all
r>0
(in case of
q=2
and
\theta=1
), we know for situations with very low smoothness that linear methods decay very slowly while non-linear algorithms always decay with rate at least
1/2
. The additional logarithmic term
\log^{\ast}(m)^{3r+\frac{3}{2}}
does not depend on the dimension
d
and is the consequence of the conditions on
m
in Theorem
3.7
.

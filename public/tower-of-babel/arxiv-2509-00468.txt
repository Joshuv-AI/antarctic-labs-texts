Weitzenböck-Bochner-Kodaira formulas with quadratic curvature terms
Mingwei Wang, Xiaokui Yang

Abstract
. In this paper we establish new Bochner-Kodaira formulas with quadratic curvature terms on compact Kähler manifolds: for any η ∈ Ω p , q ​ ( M ) \eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M) , ⟨ Δ ∂ ¯ ​ η , η ⟩ = ⟨ Δ ∂ ¯ F ​ η , η ⟩ + 1 4 ​ ⟨ ( ℛ ⊗ Id Λ p + 1 , q − 1 ​ T ∗ ​ M ) ​ ( 𝕋 η ) , 𝕋 η ⟩ . \langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}\eta,\eta\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\eta,\eta\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M}\right)(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}),\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\right\rangle. This linearized curvature term yields new vanishing theorems and provides estimates for Hodge numbers under exceptionally weak curvature conditions. Furthermore, we derive Weitzenböck formulas with quadratic curvature terms on both Riemannian and Kähler manifolds.

Introduction

On a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the classical Weitzenböck formula states that for any differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
,

(1.1)

\Delta\omega=D^{*}D\omega+\Theta\left(\omega\right)

where
\Theta
is a tensor induced by the curvature tensor of
(M,g)
. By combining this identity with curvature positivity conditions, numerous rigidity and vanishing theorems have been established in both Riemannian and Kähler geometry, e.g.,
[
Boc46
,
Boc48
,
Boc49
,

,

,
GM75
,

,

,

,

,
CZ06
,
BS08
,

,

,
Bre10
,
CTZ12
,
Bre19
]
. For a comprehensive overview of these developments, we refer to
[
Wu88
]
and
[
Pet16
]
and the references therein.

The curvature term
\Theta
in the Weitzenböck formula is fundamentally instrumental in deriving new geometric results, particularly through its role in connecting analytic and topological properties of manifolds. In their recent work, Petersen and Wink
[
PW21a
]
established novel vanishing theorems and eigenvalue-based estimates for Betti numbers on Riemannian manifolds by exploiting positivity conditions on the curvature operator. Their framework extends to Kähler geometry via complexification techniques, allowing for systematic treatment of the Kähler curvature operator in compact settings
[
PW21b
]
. A key innovation lies in their algebraic reformulation of the curvature term into specialized representations of the Lie algebras, which provides crucial structural insights while eliminating classical singularities associated with curvature-dependent operators. For more related interesting works, we refer to

The Bochner-Kodaira formula for Hermitian vector bundles
(E,h)
over compact Kähler manifolds
(M,\omega)
,

(1.2)

\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}=\Delta_{\partial_{E}}+\left[\sqrt{-1}R^{E},\Lambda_{\omega}\right]

represents a fundamental refinement of the Weitzenböck formula. It plays a pivotal role in establishing vanishing theorems and quantitative estimates within complex algebraic geometry.
This relationship arises because the Bochner-Kodaira technique leverages analytic methods (particularly the
\overline{\partial}
-Laplacian machinery) to derive profound global geometric consequences for holomorphic structures on complex manifolds. Its applications extend to metric rigidity and extension problems investigations in complex differential geometry. The

L^{2}
-estimate framework derived from the formula establishes cohomology vanishing for holomorphic sections when
E
satisfies Nakano-positivity or dual-Nakano-positivity conditions. In
[
Siu80
,
Siu82
]
, Yum-Tung Siu combined this formula with classical Weitzenböck formula to obtained new vanishing theorems and rigidity theorems by using new positivity concepts.

In this paper, we establish a unified framework for Weitzenböck-Bochner-Kodaira formulas with transparent curvature terms in the context of abstract Hermitian vector bundles. These formulas integrate the analytical tools of
\overline{\partial}
-Laplacians with algebraic conditions on curvature tensors, and extend results established in
[
PW21a
,
PW21b
]
to broader geometric settings.

1.A.
Bochner-Kodaira formulas with quadratic curvature terms on Kähler manifolds

To demonstrate the geometric interpretation of curvature terms in Bochner-Kodaira formulas, we introduce several auxiliary operators. Let
(E,h)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over a Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Let
\nabla^{E}
be the Chern connection of
(E,h)
and
R^{E}
be the corresponding Chern curvature. There is an induced curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}^{E}:\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes E)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes E)
given by

(1.3)

\left\langle\mathfrak{R}^{E}\left(u^{i\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes e_{\alpha}\right),v^{j\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\otimes e_{\beta}\right\rangle=R^{E}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}u^{i\alpha}\overline{v}{}^{j\beta}.

It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{R}^{E}
is a positive operator in the sense of linear algebra if and only if
(E,h)
is Nakano positive. We define the contraction operator for
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
,

(1.4)

\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}:\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q-1}(M,E),\ \ \ \mathbb{S}_{\varphi}(\alpha)=I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}\varphi,

where
\alpha_{\sharp}
is the
(0,1)
type dual vector of
\alpha\in\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)
. One can view it as

(1.5)

\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\in\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E)\cong\Gamma\left(M,\left(T^{1,0}M\otimes E\right)\otimes\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M\right).

As analogous to the induced curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}^{E}
,
one can define the
symmetrized curvature operator

\mathcal{R}:\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)
by the relation

(1.6)

g\left(\mathcal{R}\left(a\right),b\right)=R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}a^{ik}\overline{b}{}^{j\ell}

where
a=\sum a^{ik}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}
and
b=\sum b^{j\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}
are in
\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)
(see also
[
CV60
]
and
[
BNPSW25
]
) . We say that
(M,\omega_{g})
has
positive symmetrized curvature operator

\mathcal{R}

if it is positive definite as a Hermitian bilinear form.
A straightforward computation shows that the symmetrized curvature operator of
({\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})
is
\mathcal{R}=2\cdot\mathrm{Id}
which is positive definite. On the other hand,
if
\mathcal{R}
is a positive operator, then
(M,g)
has positive holomorphic bisectional curvature. Furthermore, when
M
is compact, it follows from Siu-Yau’s solution to the Frankel conjecture (
[
SY80
,
Mori79
]
) that
M
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.

For any
q\geq 1
and differential form
\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
, we define

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}:\Omega^{1,0}(M)\times\Omega^{1,0}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q-1}(M,E)

by the contraction formula:

(1.7)

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(\alpha,\beta)=I_{\beta_{\sharp}}(\alpha\wedge\eta)+I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}(\beta\wedge\eta).

It is obvious that
\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(\beta,\alpha)
and so

(1.8)

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\in\Gamma\left(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E\right).

The space
\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
serves as the natural domain for expressing curvature interactions in the Bochner-Kodaira framework. We equip this bundle with a fiberwise ‌Hermitian inner product‌ by:

(1.9)

\left\langle u\otimes\alpha,v\otimes\beta\right\rangle_{\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}:=g(u,v)\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle,

where
u,v\in\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma\left(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E\right)
.
The symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
admits a canonical extension to the tensor product bundle
\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
via the algebraic tensor product construction

(1.10)

(\mathcal{R}\otimes\text{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E})(u\otimes\alpha):=\mathcal{R}(u)\otimes\alpha.

We now establish the Bochner-Kodaira formula for the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathfrak{R}^{E}
which reveals the intricate relationship between the Laplacians and curvature interaction terms on Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
(E,h)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. For any
E
-valued
(p,q)
form
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
, one has the following Bochner-Kodaira formula:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\big(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E}\big)\big(\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\big),\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}^{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right),\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right\rangle,

where
F=\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E
is the
complex
vector bundle and
\overline{\partial}_{F}
is the
(0,1)
-part of the induced metric connection on
F
.

By convention, the curvature term

\left\langle\big(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E}\big)\big(\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\big),\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right\rangle

vanishes when
q=0
or
p=n
. Moreover, when
q=0
,
F
is a holomorphic vector bundle, and

(1.12)

\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle.

When
p=n
, one obtains the formulation

(1.13)

\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle+\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}^{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{n,q-1}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right),\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right\rangle.

When
E
is the trivial line bundle, one obtains the following Bochner-Kodaira formula on compact Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold.
For any differential form
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, the following Bochner-Kodaira formula holds

(1.14)

\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\left(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M}\right)(\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}),\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right\rangle,

where
F=\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M
is the complex vector bundle of

(p,q)
-forms.

It is well-known that the curvature term

(1.15)

\left\langle\left(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M}\right)\left(\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right),\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right\rangle,

plays a key role in Bochner-Kodaira formula applications. The principal innovation of the Bochner-Kodaira formula (
1.14
) lies in the geometric interpretation of the curvature term, which manifests as a symmetric bilinear form in the contraction operator
\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}
derived from
\varphi
. This representation establishes the curvature term as a quadratic functional of the original
(p,q)
-form
\varphi
.
Through a rigorous analysis of the operator norm relationship between
\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}
and
\varphi
, we derive estimates that bound the curvature term’s magnitude by
|\varphi|
. These results are of independent interest in Kähler geometry.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. Suppose that
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
and
v\in\Gamma\left(M,\Sym^{2}T^{*1,0}M\right)
. Then

(1.16)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(v)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p+1)q}{p+q}|v|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}.

Moreover, if there exists some
k\geq 0
,
k\neq\frac{p+q}{2}
and primitive
\psi\in\Omega^{p-k,q-k}(M)
such that
\varphi=L^{k}\psi
,
then we have improved estimate

(1.17)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(v)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p-k+1)(q-k)}{p+q-2k}|v|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}.

The explicit tensor formula (
1.14
) and estimate (
1.17
) enable more precise spectral analysis of the Laplacian on Kähler manifolds by uncovering previously inaccessible relationships between curvature tensors and harmonic forms. Let us define a Hermitian form
\mathbb{B}^{p,q}:\Omega^{p,q}(M)\times\Omega^{p,q}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

(1.18)

\mathbb{B}^{p,q}(\psi,\eta)=\left\langle\big(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M}\big)\big(\mathbb{T}_{\psi}\big),\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\right\rangle.

By using (
1.17
) we demonstrate that the positivity of
\mathbb{B}^{p,q}
follows from certain weak postivity of
\mathcal{R}
. Specifically, when the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
is
m
-positive (i.e., the sum of its
m
smallest eigenvalues is positive), the curvature term
\mathbb{B}^{p,q}
becomes positive definite for appropriate values of
p
and
q
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold with

m
-positive symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
. Then
\mathbb{B}^{p,q}
is positive definite in the following cases:

(1)

q\geq p+2
and
m\leq\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}
;

(2)

q=p+1
,
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
m\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
;

(3)

q=p+1
,
\frac{n}{2}<p<n
and
m\leq\frac{(n-p+1)(2p+1)}{2(p+1)}
.

\mathbb{B}^{p,q}
is semi-positive definite in the following cases:

(1)

0<q\leq p\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
m\leq\frac{n-p+q}{2}
;

(2)

0<q\leq p
,
\frac{n}{2}<p<n
and
m\leq\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}
.

Moreover,
\mathbb{B}^{p,q}(\varphi,\varphi)=0
if and only if
\varphi=L^{q}\psi
for some
\psi\in\Omega^{p-q,0}(M)
.

In particular, we establish new vanishing theorems and derive refined estimates for Hodge numbers on compact Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. Suppose that the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
is
m
-positive. Then
H_{\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(M,\mathbb{C})=0
if

(1)

q\geq p+2
and
\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}\geq m
; or

(2)

q=p+1
and
\frac{n+1}{2}\geq m
.

Moreover, if
m\leq n/2
, then
H_{\overline{\partial}}^{p,p}(M,\mathbb{C})={\mathbb{C}}
for
0\leq p\leq n
.

The following result is a straightforward application of Theorem
1.5
, which is also obtained in
[
BNPSW25
]
:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. If
\mathcal{R}
is
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
-positive, then
M
has the same cohomology ring as
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.

It is well-known that (e.g.
[
CV60
]
) if
(M,\omega)
is the hyperquadric in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
with the induced metric, then the symmetrized curvatrue operator
\mathcal{R}
has eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}=2-n\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{N}=2

where
N=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}
is
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)
-positive.

By employing Theorem
1.1
and adapting the methodology from the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we establish a vanishing theorem that generalizes the classical Nakano vanishing theorem for abstract vector bundles.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold, and
(E,h)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
. If
(E,h)
is Nakano positive and the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
is
m
-positive, then the cohomology groups vanish:

H_{\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(M,E)=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad q\geq 1

in the following cases:

(1)

p=n
;

(2)

q\leq p+1
,
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
m\leq\frac{n-p+q}{2}
;

(3)

(p,q)
is not in the case of
(1)
or
(2)
and
m\leq\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}
.

We emphasize that Theorem
1.7
incorporates curvature conditions on both the base manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
and the vector bundle

(E,h)
. Moreover, it is clear that in the proof of case
(1)
, the curvature condition on the base manifold is redundant. By using Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
, one can also obtain the fact that harmonic forms remain parallel under less restrictive conditions.

1.B.
Weitzenböck formulas with quadratic curvature terms on Riemannian manifolds

Let
(M,g)
be a compact and oriented Riemannian manifold. The curvature operator

\mathfrak{R}:\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}TM)\to\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{2}TM)

is defined as

(1.19)

g(\mathfrak{R}(X\wedge Y),Z\wedge W)=R(X,Y,W,Z).

For any differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
,

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}:\Gamma(M,T^{*}M)\times\Gamma(M,T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p}(M)
is the operator defined
by the contraction formula:

(1.20)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\wedge I_{\beta_{\sharp}}\omega-\beta\wedge I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}\omega,

where
\alpha_{\sharp},\beta_{\sharp}
denote the dual vector fields of
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively. It is obvious that
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\alpha,\beta)=-\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\beta,\alpha)
and so

(1.21)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\in\Gamma\left(M,\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M\right).

The following formula is analogous to Theorem
1.2
:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold.
For any differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
, the following Weitzenböck formula holds

(1.22)

\left\langle\Delta_{d}\omega,\omega\right\rangle=\left\langle D^{*}D\omega,\omega\right\rangle+\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\right),\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\right\rangle_{\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M},

where
D
is the induced connection on
\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M
.

By applying this Weitzenböck formula with explicit quadratic curvature term, one can derive estimates analogous to those in Theorem
1.3
and obtain applications consistent with the results of the preceding subsection. For further details, we refer to Section
6
and
[
PW21a
]
.

1.C.
Weitzenböck formulas with quadratic curvature terms on Kähler manifolds

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. The reduced (complexified) curvature operator
\mathscr{R}:\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes T^{0,1}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes T^{0,1}M)
is
defined as:

(1.23)

\left\langle\mathscr{R}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}{}^{j}}\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}{}^{k}}\right\rangle=R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}.

For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,
\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}:\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)\times\Gamma(M,T^{*0,1}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q}(M)
is the operator defined
by the contraction formula

(1.24)

\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}(\alpha,\beta)=\beta\wedge I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}\varphi-\alpha\wedge I_{\beta_{\sharp}}\varphi,

where
\alpha_{\sharp}\in\Gamma(M,T^{0,1}M)
and
\beta_{\sharp}\in\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M)
are dual vectors of
\alpha\in\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)
and
\beta\in\Gamma(M,T^{*0,1}M)
respectively. It is obvious that

(1.25)

\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}\in\Gamma\left(M,\left(T^{1,0}M\wedge T^{0,1}M\right)\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\right).

The following formula is a complex analogue of Theorem
1.8
:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. For any differential form
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, the following Weitzenböck formula holds

(1.26)

\langle\Delta_{d}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle D^{*}D\varphi,\varphi\rangle+\left\langle\big(\mathscr{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M}\big)\big(\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}\big),\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\left(T^{1,0}M\wedge T^{0,1}M\right)\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M},

where
D
is the induced connection on
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M
.

We refer to Section
7
and
[
PW21b
]
for more discussions.

2.
The symmetrized curvature operator

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold with
\dim M=n
. In local holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{i}\}
of
M
, for any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, it can be written as

(2.1)

\varphi=\frac{1}{p!q!}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p},j_{1},\cdots,j_{q}}\varphi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}\overline{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{j_{q}}}dz^{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^{i_{p}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\overline{z}{j_{q}},

where
\varphi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}\overline{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{j_{q}}}
is skew symmetric with respect to both
i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}
and
j_{1},\cdots,j_{q}
.
The local inner product on
\Omega^{p,q}(M)
is defined as

(2.2)

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{p!q!}g^{i_{1}\overline{\ell_{1}}}\cdots g^{i_{p}\overline{\ell_{p}}}g^{k_{1}\overline{j_{1}}}\cdots g^{k_{q}\overline{j_{q}}}\varphi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}\overline{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{j_{q}}}\cdot\overline{\psi_{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{p}\overline{k_{1}}\cdots\overline{k_{q}}}}

and the norm on
\Omega^{p,q}(M)
is given by

(2.3)

\|\varphi\|^{2}=(\varphi,\varphi)=\int_{M}\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

It is well-known that there exists a real isometry
*:\Omega^{p,q}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{n-q,n-p}(M)
such that

(2.4)

(\varphi,\psi)=\int\varphi\wedge*\overline{\psi}.

The formal adjoint operators of
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
are denoted by
\partial^{*}
and
\overline{\partial}^{*}
respectively.

For any
X\in\Gamma(M,TM)
,
I_{X}
is the contraction operator, i.e.,

(2.5)

(I_{X}\eta)(\bullet)=\eta(X,\bullet),

for
\eta\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
. In local coordinates,
we also write
I_{i}
for
I_{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}}
and
I_{\overline{j}}
for
I_{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}^{j}}}
. For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, the following formulas are well-known

(2.6)

\partial\varphi=dz^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}\varphi,\ \ \ \overline{\partial}\varphi=d\overline{z}{}^{i}\wedge\nabla_{\overline{i}}\varphi,

and

(2.7)

\partial^{*}\varphi=-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\varphi,\ \ \ \overline{\partial}^{*}\varphi=-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\varphi,

where
\nabla
is the connection on
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M
induced by the Levi-Civita connection.

Let
(E,h)
be a Hermitian

complex
(possibly
non-holomorphic
) vector bundle over
(M,\omega_{g})
. Let
\nabla^{E}
be an arbitrary
metric
connection
on
(E,h)
, i.e., for any
s,t\in\Gamma(M,E)
,

(2.8)

dh(s,t)=h(\nabla^{E}s,t)+h(s,\nabla^{E}t).

There is a natural
decomposition

(2.9)

\nabla^{E}=\nabla^{{}^{\prime}E}+\nabla{{}^{\prime\prime E}}

where

(2.10)

\nabla^{{}^{\prime}E}:\Gamma(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{1,0}(M,E),\ \ \ \ \nabla^{{}^{\prime\prime}E}:\Gamma(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{0,1}(M,E).

There are two induced operators

\partial_{E}:\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q}(M,E)
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}:\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q+1}(M,E)
given by

(2.11)

\partial_{E}(\varphi\otimes s)=\left(\partial\varphi\right)\otimes s+(-1)^{p+q}\varphi\wedge\nabla^{{}^{\prime}E}s,\ \ \ \overline{\partial}_{E}(\varphi\otimes s)=\left(\overline{\partial}\varphi\right)\otimes s+(-1)^{p+q}\varphi\wedge\nabla^{{}^{\prime\prime}E}s,

where
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
and
s\in\Gamma(M,E)
are local sections. The following formula is well-known

(2.12)

\left(\partial_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}+\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial_{E}\right)(\varphi\otimes s)=\varphi\wedge\left(\partial_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}+\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial_{E}\right)s.

Actually, the operator
\partial_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}+\overline{\partial}_{E}\partial_{E}
is
represented by the
(1,1)
component
R^{E}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E)
of the curvature tensor of
(E,\nabla^{E})
. The norm on

\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
can be defined similarly. The dual operators of
\partial_{E}

and
\overline{\partial}_{E}
are denoted by
\partial^{*}_{E}
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}^{*}

respectively. The following lemma is analogous to formulas (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(E,h)
be a Hermitian complex vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
.
For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
, one has

(2.13)

\partial_{E}\varphi=dz^{i}\wedge\widehat{\nabla}_{i}\varphi,\ \ \ \overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi=d\overline{z}{}^{j}\wedge\widehat{\nabla}_{\overline{j}}\varphi,

and

(2.14)

\partial_{E}^{*}\varphi=-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{i}\widehat{\nabla}_{\overline{j}}\varphi,\ \ \ \overline{\partial}_{E}^{*}\varphi=-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\widehat{\nabla}_{i}\varphi,

where
\widehat{\nabla}=\nabla^{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}
is the induced connection on the tensor bundle
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E
.

Lemma
2.1
is essentially well-known (see, e.g.
[
LY12
, Lemma 8.9]
) and can be verified using duality methods. It will also be frequently employed in local computations.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(E,h)
be a Hermitian complex vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
.
For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1,0}(M)
, one has

(2.15)

\nabla^{\Lambda^{p+1,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}_{X}(\alpha\wedge\varphi)=\left(\nabla_{X}\alpha\right)\wedge\varphi+\alpha\wedge\left(\nabla^{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}_{X}\varphi\right),

and

(2.16)

\nabla^{\Lambda^{p-1,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}_{X}\left(I_{Y}\varphi\right)=I_{\nabla_{X}Y}\varphi+I_{Y}\left(\nabla^{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}_{X}\varphi\right),

where
X\in\Gamma(M,T_{\mathbb{C}}M)
and
Y\in\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M)
.

Proof.

The formula (
2.15
) follows directly from the definition of affine connection. For (
2.16
),
let
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1}\in\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M)
and
X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}\in\Gamma(M,T^{0,1}M)
. One can see clearly that

\displaystyle\nabla^{E}_{X}\left(\omega(Y,Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}_{X}\omega\right)\left(Y,Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}\right)

\displaystyle+\omega\left(\nabla_{X}Y,Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1}\right)+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{p-1}\omega(Y,Y_{1},\cdots,\nabla_{X}Y_{\alpha},\cdots Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\beta=1}^{q}\omega\left(Y,Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,\nabla_{X}X_{\beta},\cdots,X_{q}\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\nabla^{E}_{X}\left(\omega\left(Y,Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{E}_{X}\left(\left(I_{Y}\omega)(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{\Lambda^{p-1,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}_{X}\left(I_{Y}\omega\right)\right)\left(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1}\right)+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{p-1}\left(I_{Y}\omega\right)\left(Y_{1},\cdots,\nabla_{X}Y_{\alpha},\cdots Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1}^{q}\left(I_{Y}\omega\right)\left(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{p-1},X_{1},\cdots,\nabla_{X}X_{\beta},\cdots,X_{q}\right).

By comparing these two expressions, we obtain (
2.2
).
∎

For any
\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, one has

(2.17)

\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}\eta=\left(\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}+\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}\right)\eta,

and
\Delta_{\partial}\eta=\left(\partial\partial^{*}+\partial^{*}\partial\right)\eta.

On the other hand, if we set
(E,\nabla^{E})=\left(\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M,\nabla^{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M}\right)
, then
\nabla^{E}
is a metric compatible connection on the
complex vector bundle

E
with the induced Hermitian metric. In particular, for
\eta\in\Gamma(M,E)
, one has

(2.18)

\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\eta=\left(\overline{\partial}_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}^{*}+\overline{\partial}^{*}_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}\right)\eta=\overline{\partial}^{*}_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}\eta.

Similarly,
\Delta_{\partial_{E}}\eta=\left(\partial_{E}\partial_{E}^{*}+\partial^{*}_{E}\partial_{E}\right)\eta=\partial^{*}_{E}\partial_{E}\eta
.
It is well-known that
\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}\eta
and
\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\eta
are related by certain Bochner-Kodaira type formulas.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the following notions.

(1)

The symmetrized curvature operator

(2.19)

\mathcal{R}:\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)

is defined as: for any
a=\sum a^{ik}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}}\in\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)
with
a^{ik}
symmetric,

(2.20)

\mathcal{R}\left(a\right)=a^{ik}g^{p\overline{j}}g^{s\overline{\ell}}R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{s}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{p}}.

(2)

For any
q\geq 1
and
\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
,
\mathbb{T}_{\eta}:\Omega^{1,0}(M)\times\Omega^{1,0}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q-1}(M,E)
is

(2.21)

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(\alpha,\beta)=I_{\beta_{\sharp}}(\alpha\wedge\eta)+I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}(\beta\wedge\eta).

It is obvious that
\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(\beta,\alpha)
and so

(2.22)

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\in\Gamma\left(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E\right).

(3)

The induced inner product on the space
\Gamma\left(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E\right)
is:

(2.23)

\left\langle u\otimes\alpha,v\otimes\beta\right\rangle_{\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}:=g(u,v)\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle,

where
u,v\in\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma\left(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E\right)
are local sections.

(4)

The symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
is extended to
\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
as

(2.24)

(\mathcal{R}\otimes\text{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E})(u\otimes\alpha):=\mathcal{R}(u)\otimes\alpha.

where
u\in\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)
and
\alpha\in\Gamma\left(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E\right)
are local sections.

(5)

For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
,

(2.25)

\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}:\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q-1}(M,E),\ \ \ \mathbb{S}_{\varphi}(\alpha)=I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}\varphi,

where
\alpha_{\sharp}
is the dual vector of
\alpha\in\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)
. In particular,

(2.26)

\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\in\Gamma\left(M,\left(T^{1,0}M\otimes E\right)\otimes\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M\right).

(6)

The operator
\mathfrak{R}^{E}:\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes E)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes E)
is defined by

(2.27)

\left\langle\mathfrak{R}^{E}\left(u^{i\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes e_{\alpha}\right),v^{j\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\otimes e_{\beta}\right\rangle_{T^{1,0}M\otimes E}=R^{E}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}u^{i\alpha}\overline{v}{}^{j\beta},

for any
u=u^{i\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes e_{\alpha}
and
v=v^{j\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\otimes e_{\beta}
in
\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes E)
.

Remark 2.3
.

For a compact complex manifold
M
of complex dimension
\geq 2
, its holomorphic tangent bundle
T^{1,0}M
cannot be Nakano positive. This follows directly from the Nakano vanishing theorem (specifically, part
(1)
of Theorem
1.7
), which implies that if
T^{1,0}M
were Nakano positive, then
M
is Kähler and we would have

(2.28)

0=H^{n,n-1}(M,T^{1,0}M)\cong H^{1,1}(M,\mathbb{C}),

a manifest contradiction. This observation provides strong motivation for considering the symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}:\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}T^{1,0}M).
A natural question arising from this consideration is:

Conjecture 2.4
.

Let
(M^{n},\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold with
k
-positive symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}
. If
1\leq k\leq\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]
, then
M
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.

3.
Bochner-Kodaira formulas with quadratic curvature terms on Kähler manifolds

In this section, we establish new Bochner-Kodaira formulas with quadratic curvature terms on compact Kähler manifolds, thereby proving Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. For reader’s convenience, we restate Theorem
1.1
below:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
(E,h)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
, one has

(3.1)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\big(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E}\big)\big(\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\big),\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}^{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right),\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right\rangle,

where
F=\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E
.

Proof.

We establish curvature identities by using local representations of
\overline{\partial}_{E}^{*}
and
\overline{\partial}_{E}
established in Section
2
. For simplicity, we denote by
\nabla
the induced connection on
F=\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E
when no confusion arises. By Lemma
2.1
, one has

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}{}_{E}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge\nabla_{\overline{k}}\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\varphi+g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\varphi.

Similarly, one concludes that

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{E}\overline{\partial}{}_{E}^{*}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge\nabla_{\overline{k}}(g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge\left(\frac{\partial g^{i\overline{j}}}{\partial\overline{z}{}^{k}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\varphi+g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{k}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge\left(-g^{i\overline{\ell}}\overline{\Gamma}{}_{k\ell}^{j}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{i}\varphi+g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i}\varphi+g^{i\overline{j}}\overline{\Gamma}{}_{jk}^{\ell}I_{\overline{\ell}}\nabla_{i}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i}\varphi,

where the third identity follows from (
2.16
). On the other hand,

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}{}_{F}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{F}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\nabla^{T^{*0,1}M\otimes F}_{i}\left(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\otimes\nabla^{F}_{\overline{k}}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\otimes\nabla^{F}_{i}\nabla^{F}_{\overline{k}}\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}\nabla^{F}_{i}\nabla^{F}_{\overline{j}}\varphi.

Since
\nabla^{F}=\nabla^{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E}
, one can see clearly that

(3.2)

\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\varphi=\nabla^{F}_{i}\nabla^{F}_{\overline{j}}\varphi.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi-\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\partial}{}_{E}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi+\overline{\partial}{}_{E}\overline{\partial}^{*}_{E}\varphi\right)-\left(\overline{\partial}{}_{F}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{F}\varphi+\overline{\partial}{}_{F}\overline{\partial}_{F}^{*}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\left(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i}\right)\varphi.

If we write
\varphi=\varphi^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\alpha}
for a local frame
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
and local forms
\varphi^{\alpha}\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi-\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i})\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\left((\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i})\varphi^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\alpha}+\varphi^{\alpha}\otimes(\nabla^{E}_{i}\nabla^{E}_{\overline{k}}-\nabla^{E}_{\overline{k}}\nabla^{E}_{i})e_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i})\varphi^{\alpha}\right)\otimes e_{\alpha}+(g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\varphi^{\alpha})\otimes h^{\delta\overline{\gamma}}R^{E}_{i\overline{k}\alpha\overline{\gamma}}e_{\delta}.

It is clear that

(3.3)

\left(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i}\right)dz^{p}=-g^{p\overline{m}}R_{i\overline{k}\ell\overline{m}}dz^{\ell},

and

(3.4)

\left(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i}\right)d\overline{z}^{q}=g^{m\overline{q}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{\ell}}d\overline{z}{}^{\ell}.

Hence, for
\varphi^{\alpha}\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,

(3.5)

\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\varphi^{\alpha}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i}\varphi^{\alpha}=-g^{\ell\overline{n}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}dz^{m}\wedge I_{\ell}\varphi^{\alpha}+g^{m\overline{\ell}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}d\overline{z}{}^{n}\wedge I_{\overline{\ell}}\varphi^{\alpha}.

By using Kähler symmetry, one has

(3.6)

g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\left(g^{m\overline{\ell}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}d\overline{z}{}^{n}\wedge I_{\overline{\ell}}\varphi^{\alpha}\right)=g^{i\overline{j}}g^{m\overline{\ell}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{\ell}}\varphi^{\alpha}=g^{m\overline{\ell}}R_{m\overline{k}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{\ell}}\varphi^{\alpha}.

Hence,

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}-\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{i})\varphi^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\left(g^{\ell\overline{n}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}dz^{m}\wedge I_{\ell}\varphi^{\alpha}\right)+g^{i\overline{j}}R_{i\overline{k}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\varphi^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}g^{\ell\overline{n}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}I_{\ell}(dz^{m}\wedge\varphi^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{i\overline{j}}g^{\ell\overline{n}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}I_{\ell}\left(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}(dz^{m}\wedge\varphi^{\alpha})\right).

Therefore, one concludes

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{E}}\varphi-\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\varphi,\varphi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}g^{\ell\overline{n}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}I_{\ell}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}(dz^{m}\wedge\varphi^{\alpha})),\varphi^{\beta}\right\rangle+R^{E}_{i\overline{k}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}\varphi^{\alpha},\varphi^{\beta}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R_{i\overline{k}m\overline{n}}\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}(dz^{m}\wedge\varphi^{\alpha}),g^{k\overline{\ell}}I_{\overline{\ell}}(dz^{n}\wedge\varphi^{\beta})\right\rangle+R^{E}_{i\overline{k}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\left\langle g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\varphi^{\alpha},g^{k\overline{\ell}}I_{\overline{\ell}}\varphi^{\beta}\right\rangle.

On the other hand,

(3.7)

\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(dz^{i},dz^{j})=g^{i\overline{k}}I_{\overline{k}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi)+g^{j\overline{k}}I_{\overline{k}}(dz^{i}\wedge\varphi).

Hence, for any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
,

(3.8)

\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}=\sum_{i,j}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{j}}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\right)\otimes g^{i\overline{k}}I_{\overline{k}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi).

In particular, we have

(3.9)

\left\langle\big(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E}\big)\big(\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\big),\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}\right\rangle=4h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R_{i\overline{k}j\overline{\ell}}\left\langle g^{i\overline{m}}I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi^{\alpha}),g^{k\overline{n}}I_{\overline{n}}(dz^{\ell}\wedge\varphi^{\beta})\right\rangle.

On the other hand, for
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
, one has

\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}(dz^{i})=g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\varphi

and so

(3.10)

\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\otimes e_{\alpha}\otimes g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\varphi^{\alpha}.

Therefore,

(3.11)

\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}^{E}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right),\mathbb{S}_{\varphi}\right\rangle=R^{E}_{i\overline{k}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\langle g^{i\overline{j}}I_{\overline{j}}\varphi^{\alpha},g^{k\overline{\ell}}I_{\overline{\ell}}\varphi^{\beta}\rangle.

In conclusion, we obtain the Bochner-Kodaira formula (
3.1
) and complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
. ∎

Theorem
1.2
represents a specific instance of Theorem
1.1
, where the vector bundle
E
reduces to the trivial line bundle; this particular case will be fundamental to our subsequent analysis.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold.
For any
\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,

(3.12)

\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}\eta,\eta\rangle=\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}_{F}}\eta,\eta\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\big(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}M}\big)\big(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\big),\mathbb{T}_{\eta}\right\rangle,

where
F=\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. For any
\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,
L\eta=\omega\wedge\eta
and
\Lambda
is the dual operator of
L
with respect to
\omega
. In the section, we prove Theorem
1.3
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. Suppose that
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
and
v\in\Gamma\left(M,\Sym^{2}T^{*1,0}M\right)
. Then

(4.1)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(v)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p+1)q}{p+q}|v|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}.

Moreover, if there exists some
k\geq 0
,
k\neq\frac{p+q}{2}
and primitive
\psi\in\Omega^{p-k,q-k}(M)
such that
\varphi=L^{k}\psi
,
then we have imporved estimate

(4.2)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(v)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p-k+1)(q-k)}{p+q-2k}|v|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}.

Theorem
4.1
is purely a local statement and can be formulated at any fixed point. We need some general computations on norm of
\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, one has

(4.3)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}|^{2}=2(q+1)(n-p)|\varphi|^{2}-2|L\varphi|^{2}.

Proof.

By formula (
3.8
), one has

\displaystyle|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(g_{i\overline{k}}g_{j\overline{\ell}}+g_{i\overline{\ell}}g_{j\overline{k}})\left\langle g^{i\overline{m}}I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi),g^{k\overline{n}}I_{\overline{n}}(dz^{\ell}\wedge\varphi)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(g_{i\overline{k}}g_{j\overline{\ell}}+g_{i\overline{\ell}}g_{j\overline{k}})\left\langle g^{n\overline{\ell}}I_{n}\left(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge g^{i\overline{m}}I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi)\right),\varphi\right\rangle.

Moreover, a straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle(g_{i\overline{k}}g_{j\overline{\ell}}+g_{i\overline{\ell}}g_{j\overline{k}})\cdot g^{n\overline{\ell}}I_{n}\left(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge g^{i\overline{m}}I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{j}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{k}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi))+g_{j\overline{k}}g^{i\overline{m}}I_{i}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi)).

On the other hand, one can see clearly that

(4.4)

d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{k}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi)=q\left(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi\right),

and

(4.5)

I_{j}\left(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi\right)=(n-p)\varphi.

Therefore,

(4.6)

I_{j}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{k}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi))=q(n-p)\varphi.

We also have

\displaystyle g_{j\overline{k}}g^{i\overline{m}}I_{i}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{j\overline{k}}g^{i\overline{m}}d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge dz^{j}\wedge\left(I_{i}I_{\overline{m}}\varphi\right)+d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{k}}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L\Lambda\varphi+q\varphi.

Note also that
\left(\Lambda L-L\Lambda\right)\varphi=(n-p-q)\varphi
and so

(4.7)

g_{j\overline{k}}g^{i\overline{m}}I_{i}(d\overline{z}{}^{k}\wedge I_{\overline{m}}(dz^{j}\wedge\varphi))=(n-p)\varphi-\Lambda L\varphi.

By (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain

(4.8)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}|^{2}=2(q+1)(n-p)|\varphi|^{2}-2\langle\Lambda L\varphi,\varphi\rangle.

This is (
4.3
).
∎

We shall deal with the term
|L\varphi|^{2}
in (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that at some point
x\in M
,
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
can be written as

(4.9)

\varphi=L^{k}\psi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda\psi=0,

for some
0\leq k\leq\min\{p,q\}
and
\psi\in\Lambda^{p-k,q-k}T^{*}_{x}M
, then

(4.10)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}|^{2}(x)=2(q-k)(n-p+k+1)|\varphi|^{2}(x).

Proof.

Since for any
\eta\in\Omega^{s,t}(M)
,
[\Lambda,L]\eta=(n-s-t)\eta
, one has

\displaystyle\Lambda L^{k}\varphi-L^{k}\Lambda\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}L^{i}(\Lambda L-L\Lambda)L^{k-i-1}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}L^{i}\left(\left(n-p-q-2(k-i-1)\right)L^{k-i-1}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(n-p-q-k+1)L^{k-1}\varphi.

In particular, for
\psi\in\Lambda^{p-k,q-k}T^{*}_{x}M
, one has

(4.11)

\Lambda L^{k+1}\psi-L^{k+1}\Lambda\psi=(k+1)(n-p-q+k)L^{k}\psi.

Since
\varphi=L^{k}\psi
and
\Lambda\psi=0
, this implies

(4.12)

\Lambda L\varphi=(k+1)(n-p-q+k)\varphi.

Therefore, we conclude

\displaystyle|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[(q+1)(n-p)-(k+1)(n-p-q+k)\right]|\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(q-k)(n-p+k+1)|\varphi|^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Fix
x\in M
and we shall verify (
4.2
) at
x
. Let
\{\widetilde{z}^{i}\}
be a local holomorphic coordinate system centered at
x\in M
with
g(d\widetilde{z}^{i},d\widetilde{z}^{j})(x)=\delta_{ij}
. Then
v=\widetilde{v}_{ij}d\widetilde{z}^{i}\otimes d\widetilde{z}^{j}
with
\widetilde{v}_{ij}
symmetric. By Takagi decomposition for symmetric complex matrices (e.g.
[
HJ85
, Theorem 4.5.15]
), there exists a unitary matrix
U
and a diagonal matrix
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
such that

(\widetilde{v}_{ij})=U\Lambda U^{T}.

If we set
z=U^{T}\widetilde{z}
, then at point
x\in M
, one has

(4.13)

v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}dz^{i}\otimes dz^{i},

and
\{dz^{i}\}
is orthonormal at point
x\in M
.
In particular,

(4.14)

|v|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}.

By formula (
3.8
),

(4.15)

\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(v)=2\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}I_{\overline{i}}(dz^{i}\wedge\varphi).

We introduce the following notation to simplify computations. Let
\mathscr{K}
be the collection of all ordered index pairs
(K_{1},K_{2})
satisfying

(1)

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are ordered subsets of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
;

(2)

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are disjoint;

(3)

|K_{1}|+2|K_{2}|=p+q
.

Moreover, we define

(4.16)

V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{dz^{I}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{J}\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}}\ |\ I\cap J=\emptyset,I\cup J=K_{1}\}\cap\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M,

where
I
and
J
are ordered. Then
there is an orthogonal decomposition of
\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M
:

(4.17)

\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M=\bigoplus_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}V^{p,q}_{(K_{1},K_{2})}.

The inequality (
4.2
) will
be established by using this decomposition. We first show that for
any
\psi\in V_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}^{p_{0},q_{0}}
, one has

(4.18)

|\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p_{0}+1)q_{0}}{p_{0}+q_{0}}|v|^{2}|\psi|^{2},

and
the operator norm inequality holds:

(4.19)

\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}}\leq\frac{4(p_{0}+1)q_{0}}{p_{0}+q_{0}}|v|^{2}.

Actually, in this case, we have
p_{0}+q_{0}\leq n
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{\overline{i}}(dz^{i}\wedge\psi),I_{\overline{j}}(dz^{j}\wedge\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle d\overline{z}{}^{j}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{i}(dz^{j}\wedge\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle d\overline{z}{}^{i}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle-\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle d\overline{z}{}^{j}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\psi,dz^{j}\wedge I_{i}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{j}I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{\overline{j}}I_{i}\psi\rangle.

For fixed
i
and
j
, there are elementary inequalities

(4.20)

2|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}I_{j}\psi\rangle|\leq\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{j}I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{j}I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle+\lambda_{j}^{2}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi\rangle,

and

(4.21)

2|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}I_{j}\psi\rangle|\leq\lambda_{j}^{2}\langle I_{j}I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{j}I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle+\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi\rangle.

Note also that for any
\eta\in\Lambda^{s,t}T^{*}_{x}M
,
\sum_{j}\langle I_{j}\eta,I_{j}\eta\rangle=s|\eta|^{2}
and
\sum_{j}\langle I_{\overline{j}}\eta,I_{\overline{j}}\eta\rangle=t|\eta|^{2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}2|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}I_{j}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p_{0}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle+p_{0}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{2}\langle I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{\overline{j}}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{0}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle,

and

(4.22)

\sum_{ij}2|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}I_{j}\psi\rangle|\leq 2\sum_{j}q_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}\langle I_{j}\psi,I_{j}\psi\rangle.

An average argument shows

(4.23)

\sum_{ij}|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle I_{i}I_{\overline{j}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}I_{j}\psi\rangle|\leq p_{0}\cdot\frac{q_{0}-1}{p_{0}+q_{0}}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle+q_{0}\cdot\frac{p_{0}+1}{p_{0}+q_{0}}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{i}\psi,I_{i}\psi\rangle.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}(p_{0}+1)}{p_{0}+q_{0}}\sum_{i}\left(\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{\overline{i}}\psi,I_{\overline{i}}\psi\rangle+\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle I_{i}\psi,I_{i}\psi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}(p_{0}+1)}{p_{0}+q_{0}}\sum_{i}\langle\lambda_{i}^{2}(d\overline{z}{}^{i}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\psi+dz^{i}\wedge I_{i}\psi),\psi\rangle.

Since
\psi\in V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}
and
|K_{1}|\leq n
, a straightforward computation shows that

(4.24)

\sum_{i}\langle\lambda_{i}^{2}(d\overline{z}{}^{i}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\psi+dz^{i}\wedge I_{i}\psi),\psi\rangle=\sum_{i\in K_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{2}|\psi|^{2}\leq|v|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain (
4.18
).

For a general pair
(K_{1},K_{2})
, we set
(p_{0},q_{0})=(p,q)-(t,t)
for
t=|K_{2}|\geq 0
. We need the following fact.

Claim.
There are identities on operator norms:

(4.25)

\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}}=\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p,q}_{(K_{1},K_{2})}},\ \ \left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M}=\sup_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p,q}_{(K_{1},K_{2})}}.

Proof of Claim.
The linear map
f:V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}\rightarrow V^{p,q}_{(K_{1},K_{2})}
defined by

f(\alpha)=\alpha\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}}

is an isomorphism and

f\left(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)\right)=\mathbb{T}_{f(\alpha)}(v).

Indeed, it is easy to see that
f
is surjective and
\dim V_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}^{p_{0},q_{0}}=\dim V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}={p_{0}+q_{0}\choose p_{0}}
. Hence,
f
is an isomorphism. Moreover, suppose that
K_{2}=\{k_{1},\cdots,k_{t}\}
.
Since
\alpha\in V_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}^{p_{0},q_{0}}
and
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
, for any
i\in K_{2}
,
I_{\overline{i}}\alpha=0
and
I_{i}\alpha=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|f(\alpha)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle I_{\overline{k_{t}}}\cdots I_{\overline{k_{1}}}I_{k_{t}}\cdots I_{k_{1}}\left(dz^{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^{k_{t}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{k_{t}}\wedge\alpha\right),\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=|\alpha|^{2}.

For any
\alpha\in V_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}^{p_{0},q_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{f(\alpha)}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}I_{\overline{i}}(dz^{i}\wedge\alpha\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\notin K_{2}}\lambda_{i}I_{\overline{i}}(dz^{i}\wedge\alpha\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i\notin K_{2}}\lambda_{i}dz^{i}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\alpha\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}}.

Since
\alpha\in V_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}^{p_{0},q_{0}}
and
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
, for any
i\in K_{2}
,
I_{\overline{i}}\alpha=0
, one has

(4.26)

-2\sum_{i\notin K_{2}}\lambda_{i}dz^{i}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\alpha=-2\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}dz^{i}\wedge I_{\overline{i}}\alpha=\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v).

Hence,
f(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v))=\mathbb{T}_{f(\alpha)}(v)
.

For any
\alpha\in V_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}^{p_{0},q_{0}}
,
|f(\alpha)|=|\alpha|
and

|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)|=|f(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v))|=|\mathbb{T}_{f(\alpha)}(v)|
.
Since
f
is an isomorphism, one has

(4.27)

\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}}=\sup_{0\neq\alpha\in V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}}\frac{|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)|}{|\alpha|}=\sup_{0\neq f(\alpha)\in V^{p,q}_{(K_{1},K_{2})}}\frac{|\mathbb{T}_{f(\alpha)}(v)|}{|f(\alpha)|}=\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}.

Moreover, it is clear that

(4.28)

\sup_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}\leq\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M}.

We shall prove the converse. For any
\alpha\in V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}
, then
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)\in V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p+1,q-1}
. Indeed, without loss of generality, we assume that
\alpha=dz^{I}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{J}\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}}
, where
I\cap J=\emptyset,I\cup J=K_{1},|I|=p_{0},|J|=q_{0}
. Since
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)=2\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}I_{\overline{i}}(dz^{i}\wedge\alpha)
, one has

(4.29)

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)=2(-1)^{p_{0}+1}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}dz^{j}\wedge dz^{I}\wedge I_{\overline{j}}(d\overline{z}{}^{J})\wedge dz^{K_{2}}\wedge d\overline{z}{}^{K_{2}}\in V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p+1,q-1}.

Since there are orthonormal decompositions:

(4.30)

\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}_{x}M=\bigoplus_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q},\ \ \ \Lambda^{p+1,q-1}T^{*}_{x}M=\bigoplus_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p+1,q-1},

for any
\alpha\in\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}_{x}M
, it can be written as

(4.31)

\alpha=\sum_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q},

where
\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}\in V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}
, and

(4.32)

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)=\sum_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\mathbb{T}_{\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}(v),

where
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}(v)\in V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p+1,q-1}
. Since

(4.33)

|\alpha|^{2}=\sum_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}|\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}|^{2},\ \ \ |\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)|^{2}=\sum_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}(v)|^{2},

and

(4.34)

|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}(v)|^{2}\leq\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}^{2}|\alpha_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}|^{2},

one concludes that

(4.35)

|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}(v)|^{2}\leq\sup_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V_{(K_{1},K_{2})}^{p,q}}|\alpha|^{2}.

The equality (
4.25
) follows form (
4.28
) and (
4.35
). This completes the proof of Claim.

By (
4.25
), one has

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M}=\sup_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p,q}_{(K_{1},K_{2})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(K_{1},K_{2})\in\mathscr{K}}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{\bullet}(v)\right\|_{V^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{(K_{1},\emptyset)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\left(\frac{4(p+1-t)(q-t)}{p+q-2t}\right)|v|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4(p+1)q}{p+q}|v|.

That is, for any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
and
v\in\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{*1,0}M)
, one has

(4.36)

|\mathbb{T}_{\varphi}(v)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p+1)q}{p+q}|v|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}.

For any
\eta\in\Omega^{a,b}(M)
, it is straightforward to verify that

(4.37)

L\left(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(v)\right)=\mathbb{T}_{L(\eta)}(v),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda\left(\mathbb{T}_{\eta}(v)\right)=\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda(\eta)}(v).

Moreover, if
\Lambda\eta=0
, then by using a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
(e.g. Lemma
5.3
), one has

(4.38)

\Lambda^{k}L^{k}\eta=\prod_{i=1}^{k}i(n+1-a-b-i)\eta\quad\mbox{and}\quad|L^{k}\eta|^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}i(n+1-a-b-i)|\eta|^{2}.

If
\varphi=L^{k}\psi
and
\Lambda\psi=0
for some
k\neq\frac{p+q}{2}
, then
\Lambda\left(\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)\right)=\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda(\psi)}(v)=0
and

(4.39)

|\mathbb{T}_{L^{k}(\psi)}(v)|^{2}=|L^{k}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)\right)|^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}i(n+1-(p-k+1)-(q-k-1)-i)|\left(\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)\right)|^{2}.

By inequality (
4.36
),

(4.40)

|\left(\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(v)\right)|^{2}\leq\frac{4(p-k+1)(q-k)}{p+q-2k}|v|^{2}|\psi|^{2},

and so

\displaystyle|\mathbb{T}_{L^{k}(\psi)}(v)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}i\left(n+1-(p-k)-(q-k)-i\right)\frac{4(p-k+1)(q-k)}{p+q-2k}|v|^{2}|\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4(p-k+1)(q-k)}{p+q-2k}|v|^{2}|L^{k}\psi|^{2},

where the last inequality follows from (
4.38
). Hence, we establish (
4.2
).
∎

5.
Proofs of Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
, Theorem
1.5
, Corollary
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
. Let us recall the
m
-positivity.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A
be a Hermitian
n\times n
matrix and
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
be eigenvalues of
A
. It is said to be
m
-positive if

(5.1)

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}>0.

The symmetrized curvature operator
\mathcal{R}:\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,\Sym^{2}T^{1,0}M)
is called
m
-positive if
\mathcal{R}
is
m
-positive at every point of
M
. One can define
m
-semi-positivity,
m
-negativity and
m
-semi-negativity in similar ways.

Let
A
be an
m
-positive Hermitian
n\times n
matrix. Suppose that

\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an orthonormal frame of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, then

(5.2)

\sum_{s=1}^{k}\langle Ae_{i_{s}},e_{i_{s}}\rangle\geq\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k},

for any
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
.

We need the following technical result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
0\leq p<n
and
0<q\leq n
. Let
s=\min\{p,q-1\}
. For
0\leq k\leq s
, we define

(5.3)

C_{p,q}^{k}=\frac{(n-p+k+1)(p+q-2k)}{2(p+1-k)}.

(1)

If
q\geq p+2
or
p>n/2
, one has

(5.4)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=C_{p,q}^{0}=\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}.

(2)

If
q\leq p+1
and
p\leq n/2
, one has

(5.5)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=C_{p,q}^{q-1}=\frac{n-p+q}{2}.

Proof.

(1)
. Let
m=p+1-k>0
. One has

\displaystyle C_{p,q}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}(n+2-m)(q-p-2+2m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m+\frac{(n+2)(q-p-2)}{2m}+n+2+\frac{p+2-q}{2}.

If
q\geq p+2
, then
C_{p,q}^{k}
is decreasing for
m
and increasing for
k
, and so

(5.6)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=C_{p,q}^{0}=\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}.

Let us consider the case

. In this case, if

, we are done. If
q\leq p+1
, one has
s=q-1
. Moreover,

(5.7)

C_{p,q}^{k}=-m+\frac{(n+2)(q-p-2)}{2m}+n+2+\frac{p+2-q}{2}.

It is obvious that

is convex in
m>0
, and it attains its minimum when
m
attains the boundary. In particular, one has

(5.8)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=\min\left\{C_{p,q}^{0},C_{p,q}^{q-1}\right\}=\min\left\{\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)},\frac{(n-p+q)}{2}\right\}.

Since
q\geq 1
and
p>n/2
, one can see that

(5.9)

\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}\leq\frac{(n-p+q)}{2},

and so

(5.10)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=C_{p,q}^{0}=\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}.

(2)
. If
q\leq p+1
and
p\leq n/2
, one has

(5.11)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=\min\left\{C_{p,q}^{0},C_{p,q}^{q-1}\right\}=\min\left\{\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)},\frac{(n-p+q)}{2}\right\}.

Since
q\geq 1
and
p\leq n/2
, one has

(5.12)

\frac{(n-p+1)(p+q)}{2(p+1)}\geq\frac{(n-p+q)}{2},

and therefore

(5.13)

\min_{0\leq k\leq s}C_{p,q}^{k}=C_{p,q}^{q-1}=\frac{n-p+q}{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\psi\in\Lambda^{p,q}T_{x}^{*}M
and
\Lambda\psi=0
. Then

(5.14)

\Lambda^{k}L^{k}\psi=c_{k}\psi,

where
c_{k}=c(p,q,n,k)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}i(n-p-q-i+1)
is a constant.
Moreover,

(1)

if
p+q>n
, then
\psi=0
.

(2)

if
p+q\leq n
, then
c_{k}\geq 0
, and
c_{k}=0
if and only if
k\geq n-p-q+1
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction. For
k=1
, since
[\Lambda,L]\psi=(n-p-q)\psi
, one has

(5.15)

\Lambda L\psi=(n-p-q)\psi.

Suppose that the result holds for all
t
satisfying
1\leq t<k
. Since we established in the proof of Lemma
4.3
that

(5.16)

\Lambda L^{k}\psi-L^{k}\Lambda\psi=k(n-p-q-k+1)L^{k-1}\psi,

and
\psi
is primative, we conclude that

(5.17)

\Lambda L^{k}\psi=k(n-p-q-k+1)L^{k-1}\psi.

By induction, one has

(5.18)

\Lambda^{k-1}L^{k-1}\psi=\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}i(n-p-q-i+1)\cdot\psi,

and so

\displaystyle\Lambda^{k}L^{k}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{k-1}\left(\Lambda L^{k}\psi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(n-p-q-k+1)\Lambda^{k-1}L^{k-1}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}i(n-p-q-i+1)\cdot\psi.

It is easy to see that if
p+q\leq n
, then
c_{k}\geq 0
, and
c_{k}=0
if and only if
k\geq n-p-q+1
. Moreover, if
p+q>n

), one has

(5.19)

|L\psi|^{2}+(p+q-n)|\psi|^{2}=0,

and it implies

Theorem

Theorem 5.4

6.
Weitzenböck formulas with quadratic curvature terms on Riemannian manifolds

In this section we prove Theorem
1.8
and establish some applications. Let
(M,g)
be a compact and oriented Riemannian manifold. Recall that for any
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
,

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}:\Gamma(M,T^{*}M)\times\Gamma(M,T^{*}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p}(M)
is the operator defined
by:

(6.1)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\wedge I_{\beta_{\sharp}}\omega-\beta\wedge I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}\omega.

One can also regard it as

(6.2)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\in\Gamma\left(M,\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M\right).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold.
For any differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
, the following Weitzenböck formula holds

(6.3)

\left\langle\Delta_{d}\omega,\omega\right\rangle=\left\langle D^{*}D\omega,\omega\right\rangle+\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\right),\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\right\rangle_{\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M},

where
D
is the induced connection on
\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M
.

Proof.

By using the expression of
d^{*}
, we have

(6.4)

d^{*}d\omega=-g^{jk}I_{j}\nabla_{k}(dx^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}\omega).

A straightforward computation shows

\displaystyle d^{*}d\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}I_{j}(\nabla_{k}dx^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}\omega+dx^{i}\wedge\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}(I_{j}\nabla_{k}dx^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}\omega-\nabla_{k}dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\nabla_{i}\omega)-g^{jk}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}\omega-dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}\omega-\Gamma_{jk}^{i}\nabla_{i}\omega)+g^{jk}(dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega-\Gamma_{k\ell}^{i}dx^{\ell}\wedge I_{j}\nabla_{i}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}\omega-\Gamma_{jk}^{i}\nabla_{i}\omega)+g^{jk}dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega-\Gamma_{ki}^{\ell}\nabla_{\ell}\omega).

On the other hand, if we write
s=\omega\in\Gamma(M,E)
where
E=\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M
,

(6.5)

D^{*}Ds=-g^{jk}I_{j}\nabla^{T^{*}M\otimes E}_{k}(dx^{i}\wedge\nabla^{E}_{i}s).

Therefore,

\displaystyle D^{*}Ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}I_{j}(\nabla_{k}dx^{i}\wedge\nabla^{E}_{i}s+dx^{i}\wedge\nabla^{E}_{k}\nabla^{E}_{i}s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}(-\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\nabla^{E}_{i}s+\nabla^{E}_{k}\nabla^{E}_{j}s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{jk}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}\omega-\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\nabla_{i}\omega).

We define
\alpha\in\Gamma(M,T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M\otimes\wedge^{p-1}T^{*}M)
as

(6.6)

\alpha(X,Y)=I_{X}\nabla_{Y}\omega.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}\alpha)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{Z}(\alpha(X,Y))-\alpha(\nabla_{Z}X,Y)-\alpha(X,\nabla_{Z}Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{Z}I_{X}\nabla_{Y}\omega-I_{\nabla_{Z}X}\nabla_{Y}\omega-I_{X}\nabla_{\nabla_{Z}Y}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{X}\nabla_{Z}\nabla_{Y}\omega-I_{X}\nabla_{\nabla_{Z}Y}\omega

where the third identity follows from the contraction formula

(6.7)

\nabla_{Z}\circ I_{W}=I_{W}\circ\nabla_{Z}Z+I_{\nabla_{Z}W}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle dd^{*}\omega=-dx^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}(g^{jk}I_{j}\nabla_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dx^{i}\wedge\nabla_{i}(\mathrm{tr}_{g}\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dx^{i}\wedge g^{jk}(\nabla_{i}\alpha)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dx^{i}\wedge g^{jk}I_{j}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\omega-\Gamma_{ki}^{\ell}\nabla_{\ell}\omega).

Now we obtain

(6.8)

\Delta_{d}\omega-D^{*}D\omega=g^{jk}dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\omega-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\omega)=g^{jk}dx^{i}\wedge I_{k}\left(R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)\omega\right).

On the other hand, at a fixed point with orthonormal frame, it is easy to deduce that

(6.9)

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}=\sum_{i<j}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\right)\otimes\left(dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\omega-dx^{j}\wedge I_{i}\omega\right),

and

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\mathfrak{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M}\big)\left(\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\right),\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\right\rangle_{\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}\sum_{k<\ell}R_{ij\ell k}\langle dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\omega-dx^{j}\wedge I_{i}\omega,dx^{k}\wedge I_{\ell}\omega-dx^{\ell}\wedge I_{k}\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}R_{ij\ell k}\langle dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\omega,dx^{k}\wedge I_{\ell}\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j,\ell}\left\langle dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\omega,\left(R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)dx^{\ell}\right)\wedge I_{\ell}\omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j,\ell}\left\langle dx^{i}\wedge I_{j}\omega,R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)\omega\right\rangle.

This is exactly
\left\langle\Delta_{d}\omega-D^{*}D\omega,\omega\right\rangle
and we complete the proof of (
6.3
).
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

For any
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
,

(6.10)

|\mathbb{T}_{\omega}|^{2}\leq 2\min\{k,n-k\}|\omega|^{2}.

Theorem
6.2
is established in
[
PW21a
]
. The proof of it follows by a formal linear algebra using the operator
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}
which is simpler than that of Theorem
1.3
. Let
V
be a real vector
space and
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V=r
. Let
A:V\rightarrow V
be a linear map. For any
1\leq p\leq r
, the
p
-th compound matrix
\wedge^{p}A\in\mathrm{End}(\Lambda^{p}V)
of
A
is defined as

(6.11)

\left(\wedge^{p}A\right)(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Av_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{p}.

Since
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\in\Gamma\left(M,\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M\right)
,
for a given vector
v\in\Lambda^{2}TM
, we define a map
T(v,\bullet):\Omega^{p}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p}(M)
by

(6.12)

T(v,\omega):=\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(v)\in\Omega^{p}(M),

and this map is denoted by
T_{p}(v)
. One can see clearly that

(6.13)

\wedge^{k}T_{1}(v)=T_{k}(v).

At a fixed point
x\in M
, we assume that
\{e_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\}
is an orthonormal frame of
T_{x}M
. Let
v=\frac{1}{2}\sum v^{ij}e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\in\wedge^{2}T_{x}M
where
v^{ij}
is skew-symmetric. Suppose the matrix
V:=\left[v^{ij}\right]
has complex eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
. Since
V
is skew-symmetric, its rank is denoted by
2r
. We also assume that

(6.14)

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{r},-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{1},\cdots,-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{r},0,\cdots,0\right)

where
\Lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\Lambda_{r}>0
. In this case,
\lambda_{2r+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}=0
. For simplicity, we also set
\Lambda_{r+1}=\cdots=\Lambda_{n}=0
. Hence, one obtains:

Lemma 6.3
.

(1)

The eigenvalues of
T_{1}(v):T^{*}_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}^{*}M
are
2\lambda_{1},\cdots,2\lambda_{n}
.

(2)

The eigenvalues of
T_{k}(v)
are of the form
2\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}\lambda_{i_{s}}
where
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n
.

In particular, the maximal absolute value of the eigenvalues of
T_{k}(v)
is

(6.15)

\min\{2\left(\Lambda_{1}+\cdots+\Lambda_{k}\right),2\left(\Lambda_{1}+\cdots+\Lambda_{n-k}\right)\}.

Indeed, let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
T_{1}(v)
,
a=\sum a_{s}dx^{s}
be an eigenvector and

T_{1}(v)(a)=\lambda a.

A straightforward computation shows

(6.16)

T_{1}(v)(a)=\sum_{i,j}v^{ij}a_{s}\left(g_{im}dx^{m}I_{j}(dx^{s})-g_{jm}dx^{m}I_{i}(dx^{s})\right)=2\sum_{j,m}v^{mj}a_{j}dx^{m}.

In particular, one has

(6.17)

2\sum_{j}v^{mj}a_{j}=\lambda a_{m}.

Hence,
\lambda=2\lambda_{i}
for some
i
.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Let
v=\frac{1}{2}\sum v^{ij}e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\in\wedge^{2}T_{x}M
where
v^{ij}
is skew-symmetric.
It is easy to see that

(6.18)

|v|^{2}_{g}=\sum_{i,j}|v^{ij}|^{2}=-\sum_{i,j}v^{ij}v^{ji}=-\mathrm{tr}\left(V\circ V\right)=-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}=2\sum_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{2}.

Hence, by Lemma
6.3
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(v)|^{2}=|T_{k}(v)(w)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min\{4\left(\Lambda_{1}+\cdots+\Lambda_{k}\right)^{2},4\left(\Lambda_{1}+\cdots+\Lambda_{n-k}\right)^{2}\}|w|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\min\{k,n-k\}|\omega|^{2}|v|_{g}^{2},

where the last step follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities.
∎

7.
Weitzenböck formulas with quadratic curvature terms on Kähler manifolds

The Weitzenböck formulas with quadratic curvature terms on compact Kähler manifolds constitute a natural complexification of their Riemannian counterparts. Here we present the principal results; their proofs follow straightforwardly from the established framework. Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. The reduced (complexified) curvature operator
\mathscr{R}:\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes T^{0,1}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\otimes T^{0,1}M)
is
defined as:

(7.1)

\left\langle\mathscr{R}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}{}^{j}}\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}{}^{k}}\right\rangle=R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}.

For any
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
,
\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}:\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M)\times\Gamma(M,T^{*0,1}M)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q}(M)
is defined
by:

(7.2)

\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}(\alpha,\beta)=\beta\wedge I_{\alpha_{\sharp}}\varphi-\alpha\wedge I_{\beta_{\sharp}}\varphi.

It is obvious that

(7.3)

\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}\in\Gamma\left(M,\left(T^{1,0}M\wedge T^{0,1}M\right)\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\right).

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. For any differential form
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, the following Weitzenböck formula holds

(7.4)

\langle\Delta_{d}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle D^{*}D\varphi,\varphi\rangle+\left\langle\big(\mathscr{R}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M}\big)\big(\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}\big),\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\left(T^{1,0}M\wedge T^{0,1}M\right)\otimes\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M},

where
D
is the induced connection on
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M
.

The following results are essentially established in
[
PW21b
]
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Given
\varphi\in\Omega^{p,q}(M)
, if there exists
k\geq 0
and
\psi\in\Omega^{(p-k),(q-k)}(M)
such that
\varphi=L^{k}\psi
and
\Lambda\psi=0
, the following inequality holds

(7.5)

|\mathbb{Y}_{\varphi}|^{2}\leq(p+q-2k)|\varphi|^{2}.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. Assume that the reduced curvature operator
\mathscr{R}:\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\wedge T^{0,1}M)\rightarrow\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M\wedge T^{0,1}M)
is
m

(1)

and

(7.6)

H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(M,\mathbb{C})=0.

(2)

m\leq n+1-p
, one has

(7.7)

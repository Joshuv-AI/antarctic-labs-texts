Audit the Whisper: Detecting Steganographic Collusion in Multi-Agent LLMs
Om Tailor

Abstract
Multi-agent deployments of large language models (LLMs) are increasingly embedded in market, allocation, and governance workflows, yet covert coordination among agents can silently erode trust and social welfare. Existing audits are dominated by heuristics that lack theoretical guarantees, struggle to transfer across tasks, and seldom ship with the infrastructure needed for independent replication. We introduce Audit the Whisper , a conference-grade research artifact that spans theory, benchmark design, detection, and reproducibility. Our contributions are: (i) a channel-capacity analysis showing how interventions such as paraphrase, rate limiting, and role permutation impose quantifiable capacity penalties—operationalised via paired-run Kullback–Leibler diagnostics—that tighten mutual-information thresholds with finite-sample guarantees and full proofs; (ii) ColludeBench -v0, covering pricing, first-price auctions, peer review, and hosted Gemini/Groq APIs with configurable covert schemes, deterministic manifests, and reward instrumentation; and (iii) a calibrated auditing pipeline that fuses cross-run mutual information, permutation invariance, watermark variance, and fairness-aware acceptance bias, each tuned to a 10 − 3 10^{-3} false-positive budget and validated by 10k honest runs plus an e-value martingale. Across ColludeBench and external suites including Secret Collusion, CASE, Perfect Collusion Benchmark, and SentinelAgent, the union meta-test attains state-of-the-art power at fixed FPR while ablations surface price-of-auditing trade-offs and fairness-driven colluders invisible to MI alone. We release regeneration scripts, anonymised manifests, and documentation so that external auditors can reproduce every figure, satisfy double-blind requirements, and extend the framework with minimal effort.



1
Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are rapidly being embedded into multi-agent workflows for pricing, auctions, peer review, and platform governance. These systems inherit both the flexibility and the opacity of natural language; agents can covertly signal intent through lexical choice, message length, or fairness bias while ostensibly complying with auditing policies. The resulting collusion threatens consumer welfare, exacerbates inequality, and undermines the credibility of automated oversight
(
Cachin et al. 1998
;
Campbell et al. 2024
)
. Despite growing recognition of the hazard, current audits remain ill-equipped: they lean on heuristics such as keyword filters or perplexity shifts, seldom offer calibrated false-positive guarantees, and rarely ship with reproducible artifacts or intervention stress tests.

Designing an auditing framework for collusive LLM agents demands a synthesis of theory, benchmark realism, and disciplined engineering. Auditors must reason about covert-channel capacity, understand how interventions throttle communication, and deploy diagnostics that remain interpretable to human operators. Equally important, they must package evidence so practitioners, regulators, and researchers can independently validate claims.

This paper introduces
Audit the Whisper
, a comprehensive research artifact that elevates collusion auditing to NeurIPS standards. The framework unifies channel-capacity analysis, benchmark construction, detector implementation, and reproducibility infrastructure. Our contributions are:

1.

Capacity-guided auditing.
We derive bounds linking auditor interventions to covert-channel capacity, establishing mutual-information thresholds with explicit finite-sample guarantees and motivating detector fusion through an OR-union of complementary statistics (Section
4
).

2.

ColludeBench
-v0.
We release a benchmark suite spanning pricing duopoly, first-price auctions, and peer review (Section
5
). Each scenario offers configurable covert schemes (keyword, parity, adaptive temperature, fairness skew) and deterministic manifests that capture seeds, interventions, and transcript hashes for byte-level reproducibility.

3.

Calibrated detector pipeline.
We implement mutual-information, permutation-invariance, watermark-variance, and fairness-aware acceptance-bias detectors, calibrating each to
\mathrm{FPR}\leq 10^{-3}
with 200-seed honest runs (Section
6
). Across 600 audited runs, the union meta-test achieves TPR
=1
with zero observed false alarms, hosted Gemini and Groq audits reuse the same thresholds without exceeding the budget, and ablations quantify the price of auditing while illuminating fairness-driven collusion regimes invisible to MI alone. A 10k-run honest replay campaign and sequential e-value analysis certify that the
10^{-3}
target holds with 95% confidence, closing the gap between empirical counts and conference reproducibility expectations.

Audit the Whisper ships with regeneration scripts, dataset manifests, notebooks, and documentation so that practitioners can reproduce every figure and table with a single command. We additionally evaluate on the public
Secret Collusion
, CASE, Perfect Collusion Benchmark, and SentinelAgent suites using the exact same thresholds, demonstrating that the auditor is not over-fit to
ColludeBench
. By pairing rigorous theory with operational guidance and explicit double-blind packaging guidance, we aim to provide a durable blueprint for trustworthy collusion auditing in agentic LLM ecosystems.

2
Threat Model and Auditor Capabilities

Audit the Whisper adopts the threat model formalised in
(
Tailor 2025
)
and expands it with explicit assumptions about adaptation, intervention observability, and calibration workload.

Agent populations.

Each scenario instantiates a fixed roster of roles (two firms, three bidders, or three reviewers). Honest agents pursue individual utility using stochastic policies seeded by the orchestrator and conditioned only on public transcripts. Colluding agents share a covert communication blueprint—keyword substitution, parity encoding, adaptive temperature jitter, or fairness bias—and coordinate to maximise collective reward while staying below auditing thresholds. Colluders cannot view the auditor’s internal randomness or unseen transcripts but do observe the interventions applied to their own messages and may adapt across episodes.

Auditor observables.

The auditor monitors complete transcripts, intervention logs, task-level metrics (profits, welfare, acceptance decisions), and deterministic metadata (seeds, hashes). Internal model weights, gradients, prompts, and intermediate activations remain hidden, reflecting deployment settings where only conversation logs are archived. The auditor can replay environments under fresh seeds to generate honest baselines and collusion sweeps for calibration.

Intervention surface.

Interventions are publicly documented channel transformations: token-budget jitter truncates or pads messages, selective paraphrase performs synonym substitutions, role permutation reassigns conversational order, rate limiting prepends banners, and canary injection appends low-frequency tokens. Each transformation is parameterised, logged, and applied symmetrically to honest and colluding agents. Interventions are independent across runs but deterministic given the run seed, enabling reproducible capacity estimates.

Adversarial objective.

Colluders aim to maximise joint reward, subject to maintaining all detector statistics below calibrated thresholds. They may switch covert schemes between runs, randomise payloads, or exploit fairness channels, but they cannot tamper with logged transcripts or intervention metadata. The auditor commits to a
10^{-3}
false-positive budget and prioritises detectors that admit interpretable diagnostics for human operators.

This threat model captures the operational realities of auditing language-based agents: auditors face adaptive adversaries, limited observability, and strict FPR constraints, yet can deploy controlled interventions and replay infrastructure to throttle covert-channel capacity.

3
Related Work

Economic collusion detection.

Traditional market-surveillance toolkits analyse structured observations such as price series, bid rotation, and welfare deviations
(
Porter 1983
;
Imhof et al. 2017
)
. These methods assume explicit quantitative features and independent signalling channels; they do not address the open-ended semantics of language-based coordination or the need to reason about covert payload capacity under adversarial interventions.

LLM safety and red teaming.

The LLM safety community has explored adversarial prompting, jailbreak defence, and red teaming to improve single-agent robustness
(
Zou et al. 2023
)
. These works largely treat the model as an isolated responder and focus on input sanitisation. In contrast, our setting involves multiple cooperative agents interacting over many rounds, where the auditor must consider joint strategies, replayable transcripts, and fairness-sensitive objectives.

Watermarking and steganalysis.

Token-level watermarking methods bias generation towards detectable vocabularies
(
Kirchenbauer et al. 2023
)
, while classical steganalysis leverages likelihood-ratio tests over cover objects
(
Fridrich 2009
)
. We repurpose these ideas in a multi-agent environment by measuring watermark variance in conversational data and calibrating thresholds through replayed honest runs. Our analysis ties watermark sensitivity to channel-capacity penalties induced by interventions, enabling rigorous false-positive control absent in prior heuristic detectors.

Collusion in LLM agents.

Campbell et al. 2024
first report emergent collusion among language agents trained with reinforcement learning. However, their study lacks a benchmark with configurable covert channels, omits calibrated detector pipelines, and leaves reproducibility infrastructure unspecified. Audit the Whisper advances this line by formalising capacity reductions under auditor interventions, releasing
ColludeBench
-v0 with deterministic manifests, and demonstrating a calibrated detector stack that covers high-MI and fairness-driven collusion regimes.

Governance and reproducibility.

AI governance work emphasises transparency, documentation, and auditability for socio-technical systems
(
Raji et al. 2020
)
. We operationalise these principles through regeneration scripts, dataset hashes, and auditor manifests that make every transcript, intervention log, and calibration artefact independently verifiable.

4
From Channel Capacity to Detection Power

We formalise how auditor interventions throttle covert communication and translate capacity reductions into calibrated detection guarantees.

Setup.

Let
Z
denote the latent payload shared by colluding agents and
X
the transcript features observed by the auditor. Following
Cachin et al. 1998
, the achievable covert rate is upper bounded by the mutual information
I(Z;X)
. The auditor applies a stochastic intervention
\mathcal{T}
(e.g., paraphrase, role permutation, budget jitter) to each utterance, yielding transformed transcripts
\mathcal{T}(X)
.

Intervention-induced penalties.

Data-processing guarantees
I(Z;\mathcal{T}(X))\leq I(Z;X)
for any
\mathcal{T}
. We define the
capacity penalty

d(\mathcal{T})=I(Z;X)-I\big(Z;\mathcal{T}(X)\big)\geq 0,

(1)

which quantifies the reduction attributable to the intervention. We estimate
d(\mathcal{T})
empirically via paired runs with and without interventions, logging surrogate divergences (e.g.,
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{X}\|p_{\mathcal{T}(X)})
) alongside transcript manifests.

Neural mutual-information calibration.

The upgraded cross-run detector trains Donsker–Varadhan (DV) and NWJ critics on whitened transcript features while retaining a discretised plug-in estimator. Features are clipped to
\|\phi(M_{i,t})\|_{2}\leq B
; the critic class comprises two-layer ReLU networks with Frobenius norms
\|W_{1}\|_{F},\|W_{2}\|_{F}\leq W
. Averaging across
K
replicated runs yields the statistic
\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}
.

Lemma 1

(DV generalisation)
.

For any
\delta\in(0,1)
and each run with
n_{k}
paired samples,

|\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}-I^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}|\leq\sqrt{8B^{2}W^{2}\log(4/\delta)/n_{k}}

with probability at least
1-\delta

(
Belghazi et al. 2018
;
Nguyen et al. 2010
)
.

Combining Lemma
1
with independence across runs produces a closed-form calibration threshold.

Theorem 1

(Type I control for DV/NWJ)
.

Let
\bar{n}=\min_{k}n_{k}
and
\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
denote the optimisation gap measured on held-out honest transcripts. Setting

\tau_{\alpha}=\frac{2BW}{\sqrt{K\bar{n}}}\sqrt{\log\frac{4}{\alpha}}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}

(2)

guarantees
\Pr_{H_{0}}[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}>\tau_{\alpha}]\leq\alpha
. We implement the bound by taking the minimum of
\tau_{\alpha}
and the empirical
1-\alpha
quantile computed from honest replay logs (
results/calibration/logs/*_calibration.json
).

Honest-run sample complexity.

The 10k-run calibration campaign used in our experiments yields the Clopper–Pearson one-sided upper bound
u=1-\alpha^{1/N}
with
N=10{,}000
and no observed false positives, so
u\approx 5.1\times 10^{-4}<10^{-3}
. This establishes that the advertised
\alpha=10^{-3}
guarantee holds with 95% confidence using honest replay alone, addressing the inadequacy of small-sample counts (e.g., 0/600) noted by reviewers. When rerunning the auditor is expensive, the optional-stopping-safe e-value martingale in Lemma
2
provides the same guarantee with substantially fewer samples via time-uniform concentration
(
Howard et al. 2021
)
.

Theorem 2

(Power of the DV detector)
.

Let
I_{\star}=I(Z;\mathcal{T}(X))
denote the post-intervention mutual information. Whenever
I_{\star}>\tau_{\alpha}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
,

\Pr_{H_{1}}\!\left[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}\leq\tau_{\alpha}\right]\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{K\bar{n}(I_{\star}-\tau_{\alpha}-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}})^{2}}{8B^{2}W^{2}}\right).

(3)

Equivalently,
\mathrm{TPR}\geq 1-\exp\!\left(-\frac{K\bar{n}(I_{\star}-\tau_{\alpha}-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}})^{2}}{8B^{2}W^{2}}\right)
. Thus, as long as the interventions leave enough residual capacity for the colluders to achieve
I_{\star}>\tau_{\alpha}
, power increases exponentially with the number of audited runs.

Permutation and fairness detectors.

Role permutation statistics retain their exact null guarantees, while acceptance-bias tests rely on asymptotic normality of subgroup differences. Both feed into the Holm–Bonferroni and Westfall–Young unions
(
Holm 1979
;
Westfall and Young 1993
)
, maintaining FWER control at
10^{-3}
(Section
6
).

Minimax lower bound.

The guarantees above are tight. Consider collusive distributions constrained by the post-intervention KL budget
d(\mathcal{T})
. Le Cam’s method converts this divergence into a lower bound on total variation, implying any detector requires at least
K\geq\frac{2\log(1/(1-2\alpha)^{2})}{d(\mathcal{T})}
runs to achieve error
\alpha
on both sides.

Theorem 3

(Minimax KL lower bound)
.

For any test
\delta
observing
K
runs,

\inf_{\delta}\sup_{P_{0},P_{1}:D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}\,\|\,P_{0}\circ\mathcal{T})\leq d}\big(\Pr_{P_{0}}[\delta=1]+\Pr_{P_{1}}[\delta=0]\big)\geq 1-\sqrt{\tfrac{Kd}{2}}.

(4)

Hence achieving Type I/II errors
\leq\alpha
demands
Kd\gtrsim\log(1/\alpha)

(
Tsybakov 2009
)
.

Constructive adversary.

We instantiate the lower bound via a parity-based covert channel: colluders emit honest messages with probability
1-\rho
and insert watermark-aligned tokens with probability
\rho=\Theta(\sqrt{d})
. Auditor paraphrase or canary interventions flip payload bits with probability
\gamma
, yielding post-intervention KL
d\approx\rho^{2}(1-2\gamma)^{2}
and matching Theorem
3
. This strategy is available in ColludeBench for red-team studies.

The guarantee mirrors empirical behaviour: pricing and auction colluders maintain
I_{\star}
above the calibrated threshold, whereas fairness-only colluders shrink
I_{\star}
below
\tau_{\alpha}
and rely on acceptance-bias deviations to evade MI detection.

Optional-stopping safety.

Sequential audits may query detectors after each run and halt once evidence is sufficient. For a calibrated detector producing p-values
p_{t}
, define the e-value
E_{t}=1/\max(p_{t},10^{-12})
and the running product
M_{T}=\prod_{t=1}^{T}E_{t}
. Ville’s inequality
(
Ville 1939
)
implies that
M_{T}
is a non-negative supermartingale with respect to the natural filtration.

Lemma 2

(Martingale union bound)
.

For any stopping time
\tau
with respect to the auditor filtration,

\Pr_{H_{0}}\!\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}M_{t}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right]\leq\alpha.

(5)

Proof sketch.
The result follows from Ville’s inequality:
\Pr[\sup_{t}M_{t}\geq 1/\alpha]\leq\alpha
for any non-negative supermartingale with unit expectation under
H_{0}
. Consequently, the martingale union in Section
6
controls family-wise error even under adaptive stopping, while Bonferroni and Holm correspond to fixed-horizon bounds.

5
ColludeBench-v0

ColludeBench
-v0 packages three environments with honest and colluding agents, each parameterised for intervention sweeps and backed by deterministic manifests. Configuration files live in
configs/
, and every run records transcripts, interventions, reward metrics, and SHA-256 hashes so that results can be reproduced byte-for-byte. We execute 200 honest calibration runs and 200 colluding runs per scenario, spanning 12 intervention variants and producing 600 audited transcripts catalogued in
results/dataset_manifest.json
.

Pricing duopoly.

Two firms simultaneously set prices for differentiated products under a logit-demand model. Honest agents maximise individual profit; colluders use keyword, adaptive smart, or adaptive temperature channels to synchronise prices. The auditor toggles token-budget jitter, selective paraphrase, rate limiting, and canary insertion. Logged metrics include per-firm reward, consumer surplus, Lerner indices, and message-length statistics to contextualise capacity estimates.

First-price auction.

Three bidders compete for a single slot. Honest bidders submit truthful valuations drawn from a common prior, while colluders rotate bids via keyword and adaptive payloads. We report auctioneer revenue, allocative efficiency, win-rate dispersion, and intervention hit rates. Calibration reveals that even welfare-degrading colluders sustain high mutual information, highlighting the persistence of covert capacity under strategic bidding.

Peer review.

Three reviewers deliberate over submissions with hidden ground-truth quality. Honest reviewers vote according to noisy private signals; colluders embed payloads lexically or by biasing acceptance rates toward a protected subgroup. This environment stresses detectors because mutual information remains near zero despite impactful outcomes. Our fairness detector isolates 0.14–0.24 acceptance gaps, illustrating how collusion can manifest as subtle fairness skew rather than explicit coordination.

Every scenario supports intervention toggles such as:

•

paraphrase_off

•

rate_limit_high

•

adaptive_temperature

•

canary_high

•

role_permute

Hosted API connectors.

To validate cross-model transfer,
ColludeBench
ships thin clients for Gemini 2.5 Flash and Groq-hosted Llama 3. Honest calibrations keep consumer surplus near
79.3\pm 0.2
while colluding runs collapse it to zero across both providers, producing reproducible manifests in
results/experiments/pricing_llm_*
. Thresholds pre-registered on Gemini transfer zero-shot to Groq without exceeding the
10^{-3}
FPR budget, and the documentation in
docs/colludebench_usage.md
details the API throttling (2.1 s request spacing) and seed controls needed for reviewers to repeat the comparison.

Sweeps, experiments, and reports are orchestrated by
scripts/regenerate_results.py
, which chains calibration, detection, and analysis end-to-end with deterministic seeds and manifest checks.

False-positive evidence.

2

or the welfare guard blinds the audit to canary payloads and welfare collapses, respectively. A stricter majority-vote meta-test preserves zero false positives but further suppresses recall, motivating the Holm–Bonferroni union while we pursue dependency-aware corrections and resampling options to re-incorporate permutation at higher power.

Runtime and cost.

CPU-only audits of the canonical keyword suites finish in under
0.15
seconds for two seeds (pricing:
0.075
s/run, auction:
0.043
s, review:
0.032
s), so offline evaluation incurs negligible overhead. Hosted LLM audits are dominated by API latency: Gemini executes at the free-tier limit in roughly
45
s/run (
\approx 75
calls), while Groq completes in
38
s/run under a 30 rpm cap. The martingale union typically crosses the Ville threshold by round four in pricing stress tests, enabling auditors to stop early without violating the FWER guarantee.

Reproducibility.

All experiments regenerate via
scripts/regenerate_results.py
, which chains calibration, sweeps, metric aggregation, figure export, and manifest updates. Seed metadata and SHA-256 hashes in
results/dataset_manifest.json
allow external auditors to verify transcript integrity. For submission hygiene, we ship an anonymised tarball that strips vendor names and replaces public repository URLs with neutral identifiers, satisfying NeurIPS double-blind requirements while keeping every script invocable end-to-end.

7
Discussion

Detector coverage.

The detector union satisfies the
10^{-3}
FPR constraint while providing interpretable signals: cross-run MI captures high-capacity colluders, permutation invariance reveals alignment of role-conditioned strategies, watermark variance responds to canary propagation, and acceptance bias surfaces fairness manipulation. Because each statistic is calibrated separately, auditors can inspect which channel triggered an alert and prioritise remediation accordingly.

Operational guidance.

Intervention sweeps show that selective paraphrase is the most efficient lever for shrinking MI without harming utility, whereas aggressive rate limiting yields little benefit while increasing interaction cost. Canary injections enhance watermark detection but must be paired with fairness monitoring to catch low-MI colluders that repurpose acceptance rates. Practitioners can therefore deploy a minimal intervention set (paraphrase + canary) and rely on detector fusion to maintain coverage.

Generalisation evidence.

Cross-task and cross-provider evaluations demonstrate that thresholds calibrated on pricing carry over to auction, peer-review, Gemini, Groq, and open-weight transformers without re-tuning. The same union meta-test wins head-to-head against the Secret Collusion, CASE, Perfect Collusion Benchmark, and SentinelAgent baselines, underscoring that our signals are not bespoke to
ColludeBench
.

Limitations.

The fairness detector assumes subgroup annotations; deployments without reliable metadata will need proxy cohorts, synthetic demographic tags, or adaptive thresholding. Linguistic steganalysis remains weak compared with MI and permutation statistics, motivating richer semantic drift tests and representation-level audits. Although calibrations use 200 seeds per scenario, production systems with limited samples may require sequential testing or Bayesian threshold updates. Appendix
A
and
docs/residual_risks.md
document these open issues alongside proposed mitigations.

Future work.

Promising extensions include expanding
ColludeBench
to multimodal or tool-augmented tasks, training adversaries that jointly minimise MI and fairness gaps, and integrating causal tracing or counterfactual prompting to attribute collusive behaviours to specific prompts. The release materials are designed to accelerate such efforts by providing fully reproducible baselines and hook points for new detectors and environments.

Submission hygiene.

All public artefacts have anonymous mirrors and redacted vendor identifiers so that reviewers can reproduce every figure while maintaining NeurIPS double-blind compliance. The main paper stays within the prescribed nine-page limit, ships the mandatory checklist, and highlights where anonymised supplementary material supplies extended proofs or code.

Conclusion.

Audit the Whisper couples channel-capacity theory with calibrated detector engineering, empirically validated interventions, and hosted-model benchmarks. The union meta-test delivers near-perfect recall at stringent false-positive budgets, while the release artefacts—transcripts, manifests, configs, and notebooks—allow reviewers to replay every figure and extend the study with minimal friction. We view the resulting package as a foundation for dependable collusion auditing in multi-agent LLM deployments.

8
Ethics Statement

Audit the Whisper targets detection of harmful coordination in multi-agent LLM deployments that manipulate prices, allocations, or peer review outcomes. All transcripts are synthetic and generated from publicly accessible models; no proprietary, personal, or user-generated content is included. We provide colluder templates solely to benchmark auditors and accompany them with responsible-use guidance, emphasising that offensive replication should occur only in controlled research settings.

False-positive harm.
Even with 10
-3
calibration there remains a non-zero chance that honest agents are flagged. We recommend that practitioners treat detector outputs as triage signals: flagged transcripts should undergo human review and reproducible replay before any sanction is imposed. The release bundle logs all seeds, interventions, and thresholds so external reviewers can audit the decision trail.

Fairness and privacy.
Acceptance-bias detectors require subgroup labels. In deployments where demographic attributes are unavailable or sensitive, auditors should either use synthetic cohorts or obtain explicit consent from affected users, ensuring compliance with privacy regulations. When such metadata are absent, the limitations section of the paper instructs operators to rely on welfare-centric detectors and to document the residual blind spot.

Misuse risk.
Publishing colluder templates and detector code could help adversaries iterate on their own schemes or could be repurposed for censorship. We mitigate this by shipping only synthetic, domain-specific colluders, documenting known weaknesses, and stressing that any application should adhere to sector-specific governance (e.g., antitrust enforcement, peer-review integrity) rather than indiscriminate surveillance. Sequential e-value documentation gives auditors a principled stopping rule so that escalation decisions remain transparent and reviewable.

By documenting residual risks, providing reproducible baselines, and recommending human oversight, we aim to enable regulators, platform operators, and researchers to detect covert collusion responsibly while acknowledging the dual-use nature of security research.

9
Broader Impact

Steganographic collusion threatens markets, allocation systems, and governance processes, potentially exacerbating inequities and eroding public trust. By releasing a calibrated, reproducible auditor, we enable regulators, platform operators, and researchers to benchmark mitigation strategies before deploying multi-agent LLM systems and to quantify the price-of-auditing trade-offs. Our fairness detector highlights how covert collusion can manifest as subtle acceptance bias, encouraging practitioners to pair statistical audits with domain expertise and human oversight. Publishing colluder templates carries the risk that adversaries may evaluate or iterate on their own schemes; we mitigate this by documenting residual weaknesses, emphasising responsible use, and shipping strong baselines that raise the bar for defensive research. Ultimately, the framework aims to accelerate the development of trustworthy multi-agent AI ecosystems and to inform policy discussions about mandatory auditing requirements for language-agent deployments.

The work is not intended for surveillance or censorship of benign coordination. Operators should deploy the auditor only in contexts where collusion entails demonstrable harm (e.g., price fixing, biased scientific review) and should respect local privacy and due-process requirements when interpreting detector outputs.

Appendix A
Additional Details and Proofs

A.1
Notation and Standing Assumptions

Let
Z
denote the latent payload communicated by colluding agents and
X\in\mathcal{X}
the transcript features observed by the auditor. For every audited run
k\in\{1,\dots,K\}
we collect
n_{k}
i.i.d. paired samples
\{(z_{i}^{(k)},x_{i}^{(k)})\}_{i=1}^{n_{k}}
and compute a mutual-information estimate
\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}
using the Donsker–Varadhan critic. The critic class consists of two-layer ReLU networks
f_{\theta}\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with weight matrices bounded by
\|W_{1}\|_{F},\|W_{2}\|_{F}\leq W
; transcript embeddings satisfy
\|\phi(x)\|_{2}\leq B
. The global statistic is the average

\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}.

Auditor interventions are modelled as stochastic maps
\mathcal{T}
applied to the channel, and we define the capacity penalty
d(\mathcal{T})=I(Z;X)-I(Z;\mathcal{T}(X))\geq 0
. Unless stated otherwise, expectations and probabilities are taken with respect to the distribution induced by
H_{0}
(no collusion) or
H_{1}
(collusion with residual capacity
I_{\star}=I(Z;\mathcal{T}(X))
).

A.2
Proof of Lemma
1

We restate the lemma for completeness.

Lemma
1
(DV generalisation).

For any
\delta\in(0,1)
and run
k
with
n_{k}
samples,

\left|\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}-I^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{8B^{2}W^{2}\log(4/\delta)}{n_{k}}}

with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Proof.

Consider the centred critic class
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{\theta}:\|W_{1}\|_{F},\|W_{2}\|_{F}\leq W\}
and note that the DV estimator can be written as

\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left(\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}f(z_{i}^{(k)},x_{i}^{(k)})-\log\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}e^{f(\tilde{z}_{i}^{(k)},x_{i}^{(k)})}\right),

where
\tilde{z}_{i}^{(k)}
denotes an independent draw from the marginal of
Z
. Lemma 3 of
Belghazi et al. 2018
bounds the empirical process term by the Rademacher complexity of
\mathcal{F}
; using the bounded-input/bounded-weight assumption we obtain
\mathfrak{R}_{n_{k}}(\mathcal{F})\leq 2BW/\sqrt{n_{k}}
(Theorem 2 of
Nguyen et al. 2010
). Applying McDiarmid’s inequality yields the stated deviation bound with probability at least
1-\delta
.
∎

A.3
Proof of Theorem
1

We show that the DV statistic satisfies the advertised Type I control with the combined analytic/empirical threshold.

Theorem
1
(Type I control).

Let
\bar{n}=\min_{k}n_{k}
and
\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
be the optimisation gap measured on held-out data. Setting

\tau_{\alpha}=\frac{2BW}{\sqrt{K\bar{n}}}\sqrt{\log\frac{4}{\alpha}}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}

ensures
\Pr_{H_{0}}[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}>\tau_{\alpha}]\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta=\alpha/2
. Lemma
1
implies that for each run
k
,

\Pr\!\left[\left|\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}-I^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}\right|>\sqrt{\frac{8B^{2}W^{2}\log(8/\alpha)}{n_{k}}}\right]\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}.

Applying the union bound across
K
runs gives

\Pr\!\left[\left|\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}-\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{\text{true}}\right|>\frac{2BW}{\sqrt{K\bar{n}}}\sqrt{\log\frac{4}{\alpha}}\right]\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}.

The optimisation gap
\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
accounts for imperfect critic training and is estimated via a held-out validation set; we therefore have

\Pr_{H_{0}}[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}>\tau_{\alpha}]\leq\Pr_{H_{0}}\!\left[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{\text{true}}>\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}\right]+\frac{\alpha}{2}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}=\alpha,

where the penultimate inequality holds because under
H_{0}
the mutual information is zero and the empirical estimate concentrates below
\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
. Replacing the analytic bound with the empirical
1-\alpha
quantile of the calibration pool can only reduce the Type I error, completing the proof.
∎

A.4
Proof of Theorem
2

Theorem
2
(Power).

Let
I_{\star}=I(Z;\mathcal{T}(X))
. Whenever
I_{\star}>\tau_{\alpha}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
,

\Pr_{H_{1}}\!\left[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}\leq\tau_{\alpha}\right]\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{K\bar{n}(I_{\star}-\tau_{\alpha}-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}})^{2}}{8B^{2}W^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Define
Y_{k}=\widehat{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}_{k}-I_{\star}
. Under
H_{1}
the variables
Y_{k}
are centred and bounded by Lemma
1
; in particular
|Y_{k}|\leq c=2BW\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/n_{k}}
. Hoeffding’s inequality yields

\Pr_{H_{1}}\!\left[\bar{I}^{\mathrm{DV}}-I_{\star}\leq-t\right]\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{2K\bar{n}t^{2}}{(2BW)^{2}}\right).

Choosing
t=I_{\star}-\tau_{\alpha}-\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
proves the claim, provided
t>0
, i.e.
I_{\star}>\tau_{\alpha}+\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}
.
∎

A.5
Proof of Theorem
3

Theorem
3
(Minimax KL lower bound).

For any test
\delta
observing
K
runs,

\inf_{\delta}\sup_{P_{0},P_{1}:D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}\|P_{0}\circ\mathcal{T})\leq d}\big(\Pr_{P_{0}}[\delta=1]+\Pr_{P_{1}}[\delta=0]\big)\geq 1-\sqrt{\tfrac{Kd}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
P_{0}
denote the honest distribution and
P_{1}
any colluding distribution satisfying the KL constraint after interventions. By Pinsker’s inequality, the total variation distance obeys
\mathrm{TV}(P_{0},P_{1})\leq\sqrt{d/2}
. Le Cam’s two-point method (Proposition 2.2 of
Tsybakov 2009
) states that, for any test
\delta
,

\Pr_{P_{0}}[\delta=1]+\Pr_{P_{1}}[\delta=0]\geq 1-\mathrm{TV}(P_{0}^{\otimes K},P_{1}^{\otimes K}).

Since total variation scales at most linearly with
K
under independence, we have
\mathrm{TV}(P_{0}^{\otimes K},P_{1}^{\otimes K})\leq\sqrt{Kd/2}
, establishing the bound.
∎

A.6
Constructive Adversary Achieving the Lower Bound

Consider the parity-based strategy described in Section
4
: colluders emit honest messages with probability
1-\rho
and watermark-aligned tokens with probability
\rho=c\sqrt{d}
. Auditor paraphrase or canary interventions flip payload bits with probability
\gamma
. A direct calculation shows the post-intervention KL divergence satisfies
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}\|P_{0}\circ\mathcal{T})\approx c^{2}(1-2\gamma)^{2}d
. Choosing
c=1/(1-2\gamma)
matches the minimax rate
Kd\gtrsim\log(1/\alpha)
, demonstrating near-optimality of the detector union on ColludeBench.

A.7
Proof of Lemma
2

Lemma
2
(Martingale union bound).

Let
E_{t}=1/\max\{p_{t},10^{-12}\}
and
M_{T}=\prod_{t=1}^{T}E_{t}
. For any stopping time
\tau
with respect to the auditor filtration,

\Pr_{H_{0}}\!\left[\sup_{t\leq\tau}M_{t}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right]\leq\alpha.

Proof.

By construction
M_{t}
is a non-negative supermartingale with
\mathbb{E}_{H_{0}}[M_{t}]\leq 1
(Lemma 1 of
Howard et al. 2021
). Ville’s inequality therefore implies

\Pr_{H_{0}}\!\left[\sup_{t\geq 1}M_{t}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right]\leq\alpha.

Restricting the supremum to
t\leq\tau
can only decrease the probability, completing the proof.
∎

A.8
Proof of Theorem
2
via Hoeffding (Alternative Derivation)

For completeness we provide a classical derivation using Hoeffding’s inequality and bounded MI estimators. Define
R=I(Z;X)
and
d=d(\mathcal{T})
. Under the alternative hypothesis,
\mathbb{E}[\hat{I}_{j}]=R-d
. Applying Hoeffding’s inequality to
\hat{I}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\hat{I}_{j}
with threshold
\tau_{\alpha}
yields

\Pr_{H_{1}}[\hat{I}\leq\tau_{\alpha}]\leq\exp\left(-2K(R-d-\tau_{\alpha})^{2}\right),

which matches the bound stated in the main text when
R-d=I_{\star}
and
BW/\sqrt{\bar{n}}
is absorbed into the constants.

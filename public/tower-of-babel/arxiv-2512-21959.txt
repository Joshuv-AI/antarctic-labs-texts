Key Words and Phrases: Logarithmic p p -Laplacian, Eigenvalue problem, p p -logarithmic Sobolev inequality, Existence results, Morse theory, Fadell-Rabinowitz cohomological index.
Rakesh Arora

Abstract
In this work, we show the existence of an unbounded sequence of minimax eigenvalues for the logarithmic p p -Laplacian via the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -cohomological index of Fadell and Rabinowitz. As an application of these minimax eigenvalues and p p -logarithmic Sobolev inequality proved in [ 4 ] , we prove new existence results for nonlocal Dirichlet problems involving logarithmic p p -Laplacian and nonlinearities with p p -superlinear and subcritical growth at infinity.

Introduction

For
p\in(1,\infty)
,
N\geq 1
, and
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
is the nonlinear nonlocal operator defined on suitably regular functions
u
by

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)=C_{N,s,p}\text{ P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\,(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
C_{N,s,p}
is a normalizing constant chosen such that the limits

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\,(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)=|u(x)|^{p-2}\,u(x),\qquad\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\,(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)=-\Delta_{p}u(x)

hold. This operator can be viewed as a nonlocal analogue of the
classical
p
-Laplace operator. Its analytical theory, covering existence, uniqueness, regularity, and qualitative properties of solutions, has been the focus of extensive research; see, for instance,
[
7
,
14
,
19
,
20
]
.

A particularly interesting direction within nonlocal analysis concerns operators with zero-order kernels, i.e., kernels behaving like
|x-y|^{-N}
near singularity and having infinite range of interactions. These operators arise naturally both in mathematical models exhibiting borderline singular behavior and in a variety of applied contexts. Their study has produced a rich theoretical framework; see
[
6
,
11
,
14
,
13
,
16
,
29
]
for

[

]

A seminal work in this direction was the introduction of the logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\Delta}
, a singular integro-differential
operator derived by Chen and Weth
[
6
]
. For a smooth function
u
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it is defined as

\begin{split}L_{\Delta}u(x)&=\frac{d}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}(-\Delta)^{s}u=c_{N}\int_{B_{1}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{N}}~dy-c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{N}}~dy+\rho_{N}u(x),\end{split}

where
B_{1}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
denotes the unit ball centered at
x
and
c_{N},\rho_{N}
are normalizing constants. The foundational analysis in
[
6
]
established a variational setting for Dirichlet problems involving the logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\Delta}
. Exploiting the underlying Hilbert space structure. The authors obtained a spectral characterization of the eigenvalues of
L_{\Delta}
and showed that the spectrum
\sigma(L_{\Delta})
of
L_{\Delta}
consists of eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})
satisfying

\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{k}\to\infty\ \text{as}\ k\to\infty.

Subsequent developments in this direction include the study of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, as well as optimal continuous and compact embeddings of zero-order Sobolev spaces, carried out by Saldaña et al.
[
2
,
18
]
and by Arora et al.
[
3
]
, respectively. In
[
2
,
3
,
18
]
, the limit behavior of equations

(-\Delta)^{s}

has also been studied, with connections to models in population dynamics, optimal control, and image processing. Additional advancements include: regularity results

Recently, Dyda et al.

p
-Laplacian. For

\alpha>0

p
-Laplacian

is defined by

\displaystyle L_{\Delta_{p}}u(x)

\displaystyle=C_{N,p}\int_{B_{1}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy

\displaystyle+C_{N,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))-|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy

\displaystyle+\rho_{N}\,|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

where

9

). The appropriate function space to look for solutions to the Dirichlet problem involving

[u]_{p}^{p}=C_{N,\,p}\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\\[1.0pt]
|x-y|<1\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dxdy

Then

The main difficulties stem from the nonlinear nature of the operator

The refined structure of this new sequence of eigenvalues has been instrumental in extending many standard variational techniques for semilinear problems to the quasilinear setting (see

Motivated by these developments, and employing a minimax scheme built upon the

Very recently, Arora et al.

To the best of our knowledge, in the nonlinear case

Main problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} L_{\Delta_{p}}u&=g(u)\,&&\text{in }\Omega\\[7.5pt]

•

•

3
Operator setting

For
u,v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
, let

\mathscr{E}_{L,\,p}(u,v)=\frac{C_{N,\,p}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{1}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\,(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dydx\\[7.5pt]
+\frac{C_{N,\,p}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}\,\big[|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\,(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\\[7.5pt]
-|u(x)|^{p-2}\,u(x)v(x)-|u(y)|^{p-2}\,u(y)v(y)\big]\,dydx+\rho_{N}(p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p-2}\,u(x)v(x)\,dx.

We consider the nonlinear operator equation

A_{p}u=l_{f}

in the dual
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)^{\ast}
of
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
A_{p}\in C(X^{p}_{0}(\Omega),X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)^{\ast})
is the operator given by

\left(A_{p}u,v\right)=\mathscr{E}_{L,\,p}(u,v),\quad u,v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)

(3.1)

and
l_{f}\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)^{\ast}
is given by

l_{f}(v)=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx,\quad v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\text{where}\ f\in L^{p/(p-1)}(\Omega).

The operator
A_{p}
is a potential operator with the potential

I_{p}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p}u,u\right)=\frac{1}{p}\,\mathscr{E}_{L,\,p}(u,u),\quad u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega),

i.e.,
I_{p}^{\prime}(u)=A_{p}u
for all
u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
. We write
A_{p}=A_{p}^{\prime}+A_{p}^{\prime\prime}
, where

\left(A_{p}^{\prime}u,v\right)=\frac{C_{N,\,p}}{2}\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\\[1.0pt]
|x-y|<1\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\,(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dydx

and

\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u,v\right)=\frac{C_{N,\,p}}{2}\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\\[1.0pt]
|x-y|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}\,\big[|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\,(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\\[7.5pt]
\hskip-5.5pt-|u(x)|^{p-2}\,u(x)v(x)-|u(y)|^{p-2}\,u(y)v(y)\big]\,dydx+\rho_{N}(p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p-2}\,u(x)v(x)\,dx

for
u,v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Note that, we have

\left(A_{p}^{\prime}u,u\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{p}

(3.2)

and

\left(A_{p}^{\prime}u,v\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{p-1}\left\|v\right\|\quad\forall u,v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)

(3.3)

by the Hölder inequality. Next, we recall the following estimate obtained in Arora et al.​
[
4
]
and prove some preliminaries results concerning the operator
A_{p}
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\big|\!\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u,v\right)\!\big|\leq C\left|u\right|_{p}^{p-1}\left|v\right|_{p}\quad\forall u,v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega).

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\mathscr{E}_{L,\,p}(u,u)-\frac{1}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{p}\right|\leq C\left|u\right|_{p}^{p}\quad\forall u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

Since

\mathscr{E}_{L,\,p}(u,u)=\left(A_{p}u,u\right)=\left(A_{p}^{\prime}u,u\right)+\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u,u\right)\!,

Lemma
3.1
together with (
3.2
) gives the required estimate for
\mathscr{E}_{L,\,p}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The operator
A_{p}
is of type (S), i.e., every sequence
\left(u_{j}\right)\subset X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
and
\left(A_{p}u_{j},u_{j}-u\right)\to 0
converges strongly to
u
.

Proof.

Since
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
is uniformly convex, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that the operator
A_{p}^{\prime}
is of type (S) (see Perera et al.​
[
25
, Proposition 1.3]
). Moreover, since
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
and
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then
\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u_{j},u_{j}-u\right)\to 0
by Lemma
3.1
and hence

\left(A_{p}^{\prime}u_{j},u_{j}-u\right)=\left(A_{p}u_{j},u_{j}-u\right)-\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u_{j},u_{j}-u\right)\to 0.

The desired conclusion follows since
A_{p}^{\prime}
is of type (S).
∎

4
Eigenvalue problem

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Consider the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} L_{\Delta_{p}}u&=\lambda\,|u|^{p-2}\,u&&\text{in }\Omega\\[7.5pt]
u&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(
L_{p}
)

The above eigenvalue problem can be written as the operator equation

A_{p}u=\lambda B_{p}u

in
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)^{\ast}
, the dual of
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
B_{p}\in C(X^{p}_{0}(\Omega),X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)^{\ast})
is given by

\left(B_{p}u,v\right)=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}\,uv\,dx,\quad u,v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega).

The operator
B_{p}
is a potential operator with the potential

J_{p}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\left(B_{p}u,u\right)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx,\quad u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega),

i.e.,
J_{p}^{\prime}=B_{p}
. Note that
B_{p}
is a compact operator since
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Define, the restricted potential operator
\mathscr{I}_{p}:\mathcal{M}_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathscr{I}_{p}=\left.I_{p}\right|_{\mathcal{M}_{p}}\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{M}_{p}=\left\{u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega):J_{p}(u)=1\right\}=\{u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega):\left|u\right|_{p}^{p}=p\}.

(4.1)

Then the norm of
\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u)
as an element of the cotangent space to
\mathcal{M}_{p}
at
u
is given by

\|\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u)\|_{u}^{\ast}=\min_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\,\|I_{p}^{\prime}(u)-\mu J_{p}^{\prime}(u)\|^{\ast}=\min_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\,\|A_{p}u-\mu B_{p}u\|^{\ast},

where
\|\cdot\|^{\ast}
denotes the norm in
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)^{\ast}
(see, e.g., Perera et al.​
[
25
, Proposition 3.54]
). Therefore,
\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u)=0
if and only if
A_{p}u=\mu B_{p}u
for some Lagrange multiplier
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, in which case

\mu=\frac{\left(A_{p}u,u\right)}{\left(B_{p}u,u\right)}=\frac{I_{p}(u)}{J_{p}(u)}=\mathscr{I}_{p}(u).

Hence, we have the following variational formulation of the eigenvalue problem (

L
p

).

Lemma 4.1
.

The eigenvalues of (

L
p

) coincide with the critical values of
\mathscr{I}_{p}
, i.e.,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue if and only if there is a
u\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u)=0
and
\mathscr{I}_{p}(u)=\lambda
.

It was shown in
[
9
]
that the first eigenvalue

\lambda_{1}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{p}}\,\mathscr{I}_{p}(u)

(4.2)

is simple and has a corresponding eigenfunction that is bounded and strictly positive in
\Omega
. Next, we will construct an increasing and unbounded sequence
\left(\lambda_{k}\right)
of eigenvalues using a suitable minimax scheme. First, we show that the functional
\mathscr{I}_{p}
satisfies the Palais-Smale compactness condition.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathscr{I}_{p}
satisfies the
(\text{PS})_{c}
condition, i.e., every sequence
\left(u_{j}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}_{p}
such that
\mathscr{I}_{p}(u_{j})\to c
and
\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u_{j})\to 0
has a strongly convergent subsequence.

Proof.

Let
\left(u_{j}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}_{p}
such that
\mathscr{I}_{p}(u_{j})\to c
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u_{j})\to 0.
Then, we have

\mathscr{I}_{p}(u_{j})=\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p}u_{j},u_{j}\right)=\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p}^{\prime}u_{j},u_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u_{j},u_{j}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{j}\right\|^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u_{j},u_{j}\right)\to c.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\big|\!\left(A_{p}^{\prime\prime}u_{j},u_{j}\right)\!\big|\leq C\left|u_{j}\right|_{p}^{p}=Cp.

This implies that
\left(u_{j}\right)
is bounded in
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
is reflexive and
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then a renamed subsequence of
\left(u_{j}\right)
converges weakly to some
u\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Since

\|\mathscr{I}_{p}^{\prime}(u_{j})\|_{u_{j}}^{\ast}=\min_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\,\|A_{p}u_{j}-\mu B_{p}u_{j}\|^{\ast}\to 0,

we have

\left(A_{p}u_{j},v\right)-\mu_{j}\left(B_{p}u_{j},v\right)=\text{o}(\left\|v\right\|)\quad\forall v\in X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)

(4.3)

for some sequence of Lagrange multipliers
\left(\mu_{j}\right)\subset\mathbb{R}
. Since
\left(u_{j}\right)
is bounded in
X^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\left(A_{p}u_{j},u_{j}\right)=p\,\mathscr{I}(u_{j})\to pc,\qquad\left(B_{p}u_{j},u_{j}\right)=\left|u_{j}\right|_{p}^{p}=p,

taking

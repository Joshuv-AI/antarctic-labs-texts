Abstract
Emerging technologies such as ris make it possible to optimize some parameters of wireless channels. Conventional approaches require relating the channel and its programmable parameters via a simple model that supports rapid optimization, e.g., re-tuning the parameters each time the users move. However, in practice such models are often crude approximations of the channel, and a more faithful description can be obtained via complex simulators, or only by measurements. In this work, we introduce a novel approach for rapid optimization of programmable channels based on AI-aided ald ( ald ), which bypasses the need for explicit channel modeling. By framing the ald algorithm using the map ( map ) estimate, we design a deep unfolded ald algorithm that leverages a dnn ( dnn ) to estimate score gradients for optimizing channel parameters. We introduce a training method that overcomes the need for channel modeling using zero-order gradients, combined with active learning to enhance generalization, enabling optimization in complex and dynamically changing environments. We evaluate the proposed method in ris -aided scenarios subject to rich-scattering effects. Our results demonstrate that our AI-aided ald method enables rapid and reliable channel parameter tuning with limited latency.

II-B

Problem Formulation

The fact that
{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}
are controllable allows us to set these values to achieve a desirable channel via (
2

\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{C}^{{N_{\rm rx}}\times{N_{\rm tx}}\times{B}}\mapsto\mathcal{R}

\mathcal{F}\left(\{{\boldsymbol{H}}[b]\}_{b=1}^{B}\right)=\frac{1}{{B}}\sum_{b=1}^{{B}}\log\left|{\boldsymbol{I}}+\sigma_{w}^{-2}\cdot{\boldsymbol{H}}[b]{\boldsymbol{H}}^{H}[b]\right|.

(3)

Accordingly, our goal is to maximize
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
through the controllable channel parameters. Namely, we seek to approach

{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}^{\star}=\argmax_{{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}\in\mathcal{P}^{{N_{\rm p}}}}\mathcal{F}({{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}};{{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}).

(4)

Tackling (
4

C1

The complex form of
\mathcal{M}
indicates that there is no closed-form solution for (
4
). Thus, one cannot derive
{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}^{\star}
analytically, and must resort to numeric solutions.

C2

The search space
\mathcal{P}^{{N_{\rm p}}}
can be very large, and possibly non-countable, with the vast majority of configurations leading to channels with only minor improvements in
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)
.

C3

The optimization procedure should be carried out rapidly, as
{{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}
can change frequently
[
31
]
, thus altering the mapping (
2
).

II-C

Preliminaries:

Langevin dynamics accommodate a family of stochastic algorithms, typically used for sampling data from a distribution function

[

[

{\boldsymbol{x}}_{t}={\boldsymbol{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\nabla_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\log P({\boldsymbol{x}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\epsilon}{\boldsymbol{z}}_{t},

(5)

where

{\boldsymbol{z}}_{t}
is sampled from a distribution
\mathcal{N}({\boldsymbol{0}},{\boldsymbol{I}})
.
The

\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}={\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{v}}

, where

is

{\boldsymbol{x}}_{t}={\boldsymbol{x}}_{t-1}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\nabla_{\tilde{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}\log P_{\sigma_{t}}({{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{t-1})+\sqrt{\epsilon}{\boldsymbol{z}}_{t},

This form of optimization is particularly useful for generating samples from distributions with sparse support
[

]
.

III-C

Discussion

Our proposed
ai
-aided
ald
channel optimizer is designed to tune parameterized channels under
C1
-
C2
. It draws inspiration from the success of
ald
in generative
ai
in sampling from large sparse spaces (
C2
), and alters its formulation to cast channel optimization as sampling. Our representation of the
map
-inspired loss and the usage of pseudo gradients during learning exploit our ability to analytically asses a given channel and enable gradient based learning that copes with
C1
. While one can potentially use pseudo gradients for channel optimization, doing so during inference entails notable latency due to the need to repeatedly measure the channel. Our training method requires multiple measurements (for computing the pseudo gradients) in offline training, while upon a given
{{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}
, the selection of
{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}}
is rapid and does not involve any additional channel measurements (meeting
C3
).

The proposed methodology alters the deign of diffusion-type
dnn
by a
map
-oriented objective and the usage of pseudo gradients. It notably differs from existing application of diffusion-type
dnn
for optimization purposes, which to date has focused mostly on inverse problems while preserving the conventional generative formulation. As such, our approach is expected to be useful in various other optimization domains beyond that of parameterized channels. We leave such extensions for future work.

Modulation optimization method for seven-level SHEPWM inverter based on EPSO algorithm
Authors: Renzheng Wang, Yuncheng Zhang, Ying Chen, Zhenyao Xin, Di Fan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Selective Harmonic Elimination Pulse Width Modulation (SHEPWM) has excellent harmonic characteristics, but its nonlinear transcendental system of equations is difficult to be solved, and the practical application encounters a bottleneck. In this paper, a modulation optimization method for seven-level SHEPWM inverter based on the Evolutionary Particle Swarm Optimization (EPSO) algorithm is proposed to address this problem, so that the algorithm quickly converges to the global optimum solution. The EPSO algorithm incorporates a population optimization strategy in two phases to improve the population diversity in real time. In the initialization phase, the initialized population is optimized using Opposition-Based Learning (OBL) to improve the quality of the initial population. In the iterative stage, we combine the adaptive Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm, Tunicate Swarm Algorithm (TSA), Adaptive Gaussian Variation, Quasi-Opposition-Based Learning (QOBL) and other optimization methods to solve the problem of insufficient population diversity in the process of searching for the optimal solution, to break through the local optimum, and to improve the convergence speed and accuracy of the algorithm. Experiments of the algorithm in 19 benchmark functions and seven-level SHEPWM inverter optimization modulation show that the optimization ability of the EPSO algorithm is ahead of TSA, INFO, MA (Mayfly Algorithm), EO (Equilibrium Optimizer) and other optimization algorithms. The solution speed is about three times that of PSO, which achieves fast and highly accurate convergence, with a small error in the output of inverter, and better harmonic distortion rate than the standard requirement.

Introduction

Motivation of the work

With the wide application of inverters in the power system, the power system also put forward higher requirements on inverter functions and performance1. Compared with Sinusoidal Pulse Width Modulation (SPWM) and Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation (SVPWM)2, Selective Harmonic Elimination Pulse Width Modulation (SHEPWM) is a more effective inverter control strategy to achieve better harmonic performance even at low switching frequencies3. It not only improves the power quality, but also reduces the energy loss and improves the efficiency of the equipment. The solution of SHEPWM switching conduction angle is the key to its application, but the complexity of the solution restricts the application of SHEPWM inverters. Evolutionary algorithms aim to solve those complex problems that are difficult to cope with by traditional mathematical methods, and their core motivation is to improve the adaptability and robustness of the algorithms, to improve the computational efficiency, and to avoid falling into the local optimal solution. Among them, PSO algorithms are still gaining the attention of a large number of researchers and are active in many optimization problem areas due to their combined advantages in convergence speed, robustness, global search capability, adaptability, and number of parameters4.

Literature review

At present, there are two main methods for solving the SHEPWM switching angle: numerical method5and intelligent algorithm6. The representative numerical methods are Newton iterative method and Homotopy method, etc., but the development is slow because of too much reliance on the initial value selection and computational difficulties; with the rapid development of artificial intelligence, intelligent algorithm has become a research hotspot for solving SHEPWM switching angle. With the development of technology, a large number of research results have emerged in recent years, many of which are achieved by improving and optimizing the traditional particle swarm algorithm to achieve improved performance. For example, Manoharan P used Dynamic Fitness-Guided Particle Swarm Optimization (DFGPSO) algorithm and Enhanced Newton-Raphson (ENR) method to minimize the computational burden and make the method more efficient7; Jiang et al. proposed a Dynamic Multi-swarm Customized Variant Particle Swarm Optimization (DMCVPSO) algorithm, which employs a probabilistic mixed-variance strategy to reduce the blindness of inferior swarm exploration, and helps to solve the problem of excessive convergence rate slow, fall into local optimization and other problems8; Lin et al. proposed a hybrid evolutionary algorithm inspired by cultural algorithms and particle swarm optimization algorithms–Cultural-Particle Swarm Optimization (C-PSO), which introduces a probabilistic method based on improved metropolis rule to update the inertia weights and effectively avoid falling into the local optimum9; Zhou et al. proposed an elite opposition-based learning strategy to expand the scope of the search region and enhance the global exploration capability of the algorithm10; Wang et al. proposed a multi-strategy particle swarm optimization algorithm based on evolutionary ability, which has advantages in convergence speed and accuracy in solving both low and high dimensional problems11;Zhu proposed a new hybrid intelligence method that combines PSO and the Harmonic Search (HS) algorithm12; Shen et al. improved the Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm and combined it with PSO to improve the ability of harmonic elimination and effectively reduce the total harmonic content13; Qiao et al. proposed a Novel Dynamically Weighted Particle Swarm Optimization (NDWPSO) algorithm that combines a variety of hybrid strategies. It employs elite OBL to initialize the particle position matrix and dynamic inertia weight parameters to improve the global search speed in the early iteration phase14; Gajic et al. combined PSO and Chaotic Gravity Search Algorithm (CGSA) to introduce different chaotic maps into the formula of gravitational constants to reduce the probability of falling into the local optimal while improving the convergence speed15; Gireesh Kumar D et al. combined the Newton-Raphson (NR) method with the PSO method to solve the switching angle16; Zhang et al. combined the Simulated Annealing (SA) and PSO to effectively improve the global optimization capability of PSO17; Li et al. improved PSO algorithm and Tabu Search (TS) algorithm respectively, and proposed Improved Particle Swarm Optimization-Tabu Search(IPSO-TS) algorithm18and so on. In addition, researchers have proposed new algorithms by combining ideas from mathematics or neural networks, Ahmadianfar I et al. proposed INFO algorithm, which applies the weighted average idea to solid structures, and updates the position of vectors by updating rules, vector combination and local search three core programs to improve the exploration and development ability and convergence of the algorithm19; Çelik E proposed an optimization algorithm using QOBL and Adaptive Gaussian Variation to improve the optimization algorithm, and proposed Information-Exchanged Gaussian Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm with Quasi-Opposition learning (IEGQO-AOA) algorithm20. Researchers have also proposed biomimetic optimization algorithms inspired by the behavior of living creatures in nature such as foraging, Kumar D G et al. improved the Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO) algorithm by simulating the unique search mechanism of the Harris Hawk’s hunting behavior to optimize the switching angle, and solved the problem of selective harmonic elimination of the pulse width nonlinearity in modulation21; Ürgün S et al. proposed a SHEPWM method for AC/AC voltage converters based on Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA), where the control parameters and fitness function were optimized in adapting the WOA method to the SHE problem22; Shanono I H et al. inspired by the navigational behavior of moths proposed the Moth Flame Optimization (MFO) algorithm provides better cumulative density function values and requires less computational time23; Bimazlim M A S B et al. proposed an Improved Whale Optimization Algorithm (IWOA) achieves a fast search for the global optimal solution while maintaining a detailed exploration of the local optimal solution by simulating the spiral and random search behaviors of whales24; Khizer M et al. proposed an Enhanced Firefly Algorithm (EFA) based on multi-criteria search that exploits the adaptive nature of the social and cognitive components to find the globally optimal solution and achieves a lower fitness value in fewer iterations25; Braik M et al. inspired by the behavior of the great white shark, proposed the Great White Shark Optimizer (WSO) for solving optimization problems on a continuous search space26; Zhang et al. proposed a Multi-Subpopulation Marine Predators Algorithm (MSMPA), which divided Marine predators into three species, leaders, followers and ouroboros. The improved Marine predator algorithm has higher optimization accuracy and speed27.

Research gaps

In the current research field of optimization algorithms, most algorithms still face a series of challenges and research gaps, despite the progress made by some emerging algorithms in improving exploration and development capabilities. First, many optimization algorithms converge slowly in the process of finding the global optimal solution and cannot meet the requirement of solving speed below 0.05s, which is especially obvious when dealing with large-scale or complex problems, leading to inefficient algorithms. Secondly, the algorithm is too dependent on the current search path during the search process, while the randomness and uncertainty of the initialization population of the algorithm can also lead to the premature convergence of the algorithm to the local optimal solution, and the improvement of the method of initialization population can improve the algorithm’s local development and global exploration ability. In addition, the current optimization algorithms are unable to achieve sufficient accuracy in the solution process, which in some engineering applications will lead to suboptimal solutions, thus affecting the overall performance.

EPSO optimization algorithm for seven-level SHEPWM modulation

The PSO algorithm is a simplified model of population intelligence established by imitating the activity law of flying bird cluster, which utilizes information sharing to make the whole population carry out the evolution from disorder to order in the problem-solving space, so as to obtain the optimal solution. However, the PSO algorithm does not have high-quality population update in the late iteration, and the population diversity decreases rapidly, which leads to the lack of exploration ability and falls into the local optimal solution. Therefore, this paper improves PSO and forms EPSO algorithm to solve the problem of PSO algorithm’s declining population diversity and falling into local optimization.

Inverter SHEPWM modulation strategy

SHEPWM modulation is a modulation technique used in power electronic equipment to control the output waveform of alternating current. It is based on the principle of eliminating specific harmonics by selecting the appropriate pulse width to produce an output close to the ideal sine wave. In SHEPWM modulation, the action of the inverter switches is optimized for the purpose of eliminating specific harmonics, i.e., by optimizing the switching angle, a precise control of the pulse width is achieved so that the specific harmonic components in the phase voltage can be effectively suppressed or eliminated, and this modulation strategy usually needs to be implemented by using specific algorithms.

In this paper, the SHEPWM technique is used to modulate the inverter in combination with stepped wave modulation, which optimizes the switching moments of the switching devices to eliminate specific target harmonics and thus reduce the total harmonic content of the output voltage. General framework of the EPSO algorithm

The EPSO algorithm is a hybrid optimization algorithm that integrates the roles of TSA, Adaptive Gaussian Variation, QOBL, etc. It mainly optimizes PSO in two parts: initialization optimization and iterative optimization, and the general framework of the algorithm is shown in Fig. 2.

The algorithm first initializes the population, then performs the initialization optimization to obtain the optimized population, and then performs iterative optimization to obtain the better population through continuous iteration until the final optimized population converges to the global optimal solution. Among them, the initialization optimization adopts OBL to optimize the initialized population and increase the diversity of the population, while the iterative optimization adopts the adaptive PSO, TSA-PSO, and Adaptive Gaussian Variation to continuously optimize the population, respectively. EPSO is accompanied by the optimization of candidate populations before and during the iteration process of the algorithm to ensure that the algorithm can continue to explore and develop the better candidate populations, search for the optimal solution and ensure the diversity of the populations in the iterative process of the algorithm, solving the problem that the diversity of the population of the traditional PSO algorithm decreases too fast and is prone to fall into the local optimal.

Algorithm performance evaluation based on Benchmark functions

Benchmark Functions (BF) are a class of special functions that have the function of evaluating certain aspects of algorithms’ characteristics, and they usually have known mathematical properties, such as global minima, maxima, local extremes, function shapes and so on, and can thus be used to evaluate and compare the performance of different optimization algorithms. There are 19 commonly used benchmark functions19, of which F1-F7 are Unimodal Functions (UF), F8-F13 are Multimodal Functions (MF), and F14-F19 are Composite Functions (CF). The performance of EPSO on the F1-F7 functions can be used to assess the algorithm’s global search capability. The performance of EPSO on the F8-F13 functions can be used to evaluate the local search capability of the algorithm. • Assessment of the balance between global and local exploration

The performance of EPSO on the F14-F19 functions can be used to assess the ability of the algorithm to jump out of the local optimum. Discussion

In this paper, an evolutionary PSO optimization algorithm is proposed to solve the seven-level inverter SHEPWM transcendental equation set solution problem. The algorithm introduces a two-stage population optimization strategy to improve the population diversity in real time so that the algorithm quickly converges to the global optimal solution. In the initialization stage, the initial solution sample set is expanded and optimized by OBL to improve the quality of the initialized population, which lays a good foundation for the subsequent iterative optimization. In each iteration, EPSO uses adaptive PSO and TSA-PSO to increase the diversity of optimized individuals, and then makes use of Adaptive Gaussian Variation and QOBL to effectively break through the local optimum and enhance the global exploration capability of the algorithm. Through the benchmark functions tests and seven-level SHEPWM inverter optimal modulation experiments, the EPSO algorithm has demonstrated excellent characteristics and can obtain output results better than the actual engineering standards.

The EPSO optimizes the original PSO algorithm in two phases respectively, i.e., the initialization optimization, the iterative optimization. In this paper, the OBL method is used to improve the quality of the initialized population of the algorithm, which lays the foundation for the subsequent iterative optimization of the algorithm; for the problem that the algorithm has insufficient global exploration capability due to a single optimization mechanism during the iteration process, this paper proposes to use the TSA algorithm to integrate the PSO algorithm, i.e. the TSA-PSO algorithm, and to integrate the Adaptive Gaussian Variation and QOBL to enhance the global exploration capability and reduce the iterative time of the algorithm. The performance of the algorithm is tested by benchmark functions, and the development and exploration capabilities of EPSO are evaluated based on the results obtained for UF, MF, and CF, respectively. Then, the CF are applied to test the balance between global and local exploration of EPSO. The experimental results show that the EPSO algorithm is a very competitive optimization algorithm and outperforms other optimization algorithms. In this paper, the EPSO algorithm is also applied experimentally in the optimized modulation of seven-level SHEPWM inverters to simulate the optimal switching conduction angle obtained at an output voltage of 130 V. The output voltages and SHEPWM modulation waveforms of the inverters containing the 9th and 15th harmonics are obtained. The results show that the switching conduction angle of the EPSO can effectively eliminate the 5th and 7th harmonics, and the total harmonic distortion rate is 3.82%, which is much higher than the actual engineering standard.

After experimental verification, the specific harmonic elimination method for seven-level inverter proposed in this paper based on the EPSO algorithm achieves high accuracy in solving the benchmark function and achieves the task of solving the SHEPWM transcendental equation set. However, the algorithm proposed in this paper is still far from being applied in real scenarios, and it can be improved in the following aspects in future research work:

The current EPSO algorithm has a complex algorithmic framework to achieve higher recognition accuracy, but in practical applications, a faster and more accurate optimization algorithm is needed. Therefore, in the following research, under the premise of ensuring the final optimization accuracy, more attention will be paid to the lightweight design of the whole algorithmic framework, reducing the number of parameters in the algorithm, and optimizing the algorithmic optimization mechanism to improve the algorithm’s computation speed.In this paper, the algorithm is mainly tested for seven-level SHEPWM modulation scenarios, while in practical applications there are complex scenario variations as well as different inverter topologies with different SHEPWM transcendental equation sets. In the future, we will further investigate how to improve the generalization ability of the algorithm to cope with the changing application scenarios.

A survey on sampling recovery
F. Dai, V. Temlyakov

Abstract
The reconstruction of unknown functions from a finite number of samples is a fundamental challenge in pure and applied mathematics. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of recent developments in sampling recovery, focusing on the accuracy of various algorithms and the relationship between optimal recovery errors, nonlinear approximation, and the Kolmogorov widths of function classes. A central theme is the synergy between the theory of universal sampling discretization and Lebesgue-type inequalities for greedy algorithms. We discuss three primary algorithmic frameworks: weighted least squares and ℓ p \ell_{p} minimization, sparse approximation methods, and greedy algorithms such as the Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (WOMP) in Hilbert spaces and the Weak Tchebychev Greedy Algorithm (WCGA) in Banach spaces. These methods are applied to function classes defined by structural conditions, like the A β r A_{\beta}^{r} and Wiener-type classes, as well as classical Sobolev-type classes with dominated mixed derivatives. Notably, we highlight recent findings showing that nonlinear sampling recovery can provide superior error guarantees compared to linear methods for certain multivariate function classes.

Introduction

The problem of recovery (reconstruction) of an unknown function defined on a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
from its samples at a finite number of points is a fundamental problem of pure and applied mathematics. We would like to construct recovering operators (algorithms) which are good in the sense of accuracy, stability, and computational complexity. In this paper we discuss
the issue of accuracy. Following a standard approach in approximation theory we define some
optimal characteristics – the Kolmogorov widths and errors of optimal recovery – for a given function class and establish relations between them. Recently, it was understood that in the case of recovery in
the
L_{2}
norm the weighted least squares algorithms are reasonably good recovering methods.
Later, it was discovered that greedy type algorithms are also very good recovering methods.
We discuss these results in our paper. The main point of this discussion is to explain that for obtaining those new results on sampling recovery researchers combined two deep and powerful techniques – Lebesgue-type inequalities for the greedy type algorithms and theory of the universal sampling discretization.

Functions of the form

The Dirichlet kernel of order

We now proceed to the general setting.
Let

Recall the setting for the theory of optimal linear recovery. For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}

\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, let
\Phi
be a linear operator from

For a class

\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p}):=\inf_{\xi}\inf_{\text{linear}\,\Phi}\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{F}}}\|f-\Phi(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\|_{p}.

The following modification of the above recovery procedure is also of interest. We now allow any mapping
\Phi:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to X_{m}\subset L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
where

is a linear subspace of dimension

\varrho_{m}^{*}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p}):=\inf_{\xi}\inf_{\Phi;X_{m}}\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{F}}}\|f-\Phi(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\|_{p}.

In both of the above cases we build an approximant, which comes from a linear subspace of dimension at most
m
.
It is natural to compare quantities
\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p})
and
\varrho_{m}^{*}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p})

be a centrally symmetric compact. The quantities

d_{n}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p}):=\operatornamewithlimits{inf}_{\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset L_{p}}\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{F}}}\operatornamewithlimits{inf}_{c_{i}}\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}u_{i}\right\|_{p},\quad n=1,2,\dots,

are called the

of

in the

, the element of best

as an approximating element. This means
that in general (i.e. if

We have the following obvious inequalities:

d_{m}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p})\leq\varrho_{m}^{*}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p})\leq\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p}).

(1.2)

Further, for a function class

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p}):=\inf_{\xi}\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}}\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{F}}}\|f-{\mathcal{M}}(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\|_{p},

where

ranges over all mappings

and

ranges over all subsets
\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}\}
of

\Omega
.
Here, we use the index

1.1
Lebesgue-type inequalities

We need to introduce some definitions from the theory of the Lebesgue-type inequalities for greedy algorithms (see
[
55
, Section 8.7]
).
Let
X
be a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and let
Y\subset X
be a subspace of
X
equipped with a stronger norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
satisfying
\|f\|_{Y}\geq\|f\|_{X}
for all
f\in Y
.
In a general setting, we consider an algorithm (i.e., an approximation method)
{\mathcal{A}}:=\{A_{v}(\cdot)\}_{v=1}^{\infty}
, which is a sequence
of mappings (linear or nonlinear)
A_{v}:Y\to A_{v}(Y)\subset Y
,
v=1,2,\cdots
, where
A_{v}(Y)
is the range of the mapping
A_{v}
. Clearly, for any
f\in Y
, we have

\|f-A_{v}(f)\|_{Y}\geq d(f,A_{v}(Y))_{Y}:=\inf_{g\in A_{v}(Y)}\|f-g\|_{Y},\quad v=1,2,\cdots.

We are interested in those algorithms
{\mathcal{A}}
, which provide approximation in the
X
-norm that is close to the best possible approximation in the stronger
Y
-norm.
To be more precise, we give the following two definitions (similar definitions can be found in
[
55
, Section 8.7]
for the case of
X=Y
).

Definition 1.1
.

For a given integer
u\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we say that an algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{v}(\cdot)\}_{v=1}^{\infty}
satisfies a Lebesgue-type inequality or de la Vallée Poussin inequality of depth
u
for the pair
(X,Y)
of Banach spaces if there exist constants
C_{1}\geq 1
and
C_{2}>0
such that for any
f\in Y
,

\|f-A_{\lceil C_{1}v\rceil}(f)\|_{X}\leq C_{2}d(f,A_{v}(Y))_{Y},\quad v=1,\dots,u.

(1.3)

In the case
C_{1}=1
we call them the Lebesgue inequalities.

More generally, we have

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=\{a(j)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a given sequence of positive numbers in
[1,\infty)
. For a given integer
u\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we say that an algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}=\{A_{v}(\cdot)\}_{v=1}^{\infty}
satisfies
the Lebesgue
\mathbf{a}
-type inequalities of depth
u
for the pair
(X,Y)
if there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that for all
f\in Y
,

\|f-A_{\lceil a(v)v\rceil}(f)\|_{X}\leq C_{3}d(f,A_{v}(Y))_{Y},\quad v=1,\dots,u.

(1.4)

We discuss different algorithms
{\mathcal{A}}
here. In some cases (see
[
56
]
),
\{A_{v}(Y)\}_{v=1}^{\infty}
is
a sequence of
v
-dimensional subspaces, while in other cases (see, for instance,
[
9
]
),
A_{v}(Y)
is the set of all

v
-term linear combinations of a given system
{\mathcal{D}}
. We use two different types of sampling algorithms. The first type of algorithms is based on the
\ell_{p}
minimization, whereas
the second type is based on greedy algorithms. We now describe them in detail.

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional subspace of the space
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
of continuous functions on
\Omega
. For a fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a set of points
\xi:=\{\xi^{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, we associate with each function
f\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
a vector (sample vector)

S(f,\xi):=(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}.

Denote

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{p}:=\|S(f,\xi)\|_{L_{p}^{m}}:=\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{\nu})|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

and

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq\nu\leq m}|f(\xi^{\nu})|.

For a positive weight
\mathbf{w}:=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}
, consider the following norm

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}:=\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w_{\nu}|f(\xi^{\nu})|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Now we consider the following well known recovery operator (algorithm).

Algorithm 1 (
\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi,X_{N}))
.

\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f):=\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi,X_{N})(f):=\underset{g\in X_{N}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\,\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}.

In the case
\mathbf{w}_{m}:=(1/m,\dots,1/m)
, we drop
\mathbf{w}_{m}
from the notation, and write

\ell p(\xi,X_{N})(f):=\ell p\mathbf{w}_{m}(\xi,X_{N})(f).

For a given system
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}=\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of functions on
\Omega
, and a positive integer
v
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
the collection of all linear subspaces spanned by
v
elements from
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
.

Algorithm 2 (
\ell p^{s}(\xi,{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))
,
B_{v}(\cdot,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{p}(\xi))
.
For a given system
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
and a set of points
\xi:=\{\xi^{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, define the algorithm

L^{s}(\xi,f):=\underset{L\in{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})}{\operatorname{argmin}}\|S(f-\ell p(\xi,L)(f),\xi)\|_{p},

\ell p^{s}(\xi,{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(f):=\ell p(\xi,L^{s}(\xi,f))(f).

(1.5)

Index
s
here stands for
sample
to stress that this algorithm only uses the sample vector
S(f,\xi)
.
Clearly,
\ell p^{s}(\xi,{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(f)
is the best
v
-term approximation of
f
with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
in
the space
L_{p}(\xi):=L_{p}(\xi,\mu_{m})
, where
\mu_{m}(\{\xi^{\nu}\})=1/m
for
\nu=1,\dots,m
.
To stress this fact, we use the notation
B_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{p}(\xi)):=\ell p^{s}(\xi,{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))(f)
.
Both notations
\ell p^{s}(\xi,{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N}))
and
B_{v}(\cdot,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{p}(\xi))
are used in the literature.

Algorithm 3.
This algorithm is a well known greedy algorithm – the Weak Tchebychev Greedy Algorithm (the definition is given in Section
5
), which coincides with the Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (Weak Orthogonal Greedy Algorithm) (see Section
5.1
) in the case of the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. We apply this algorithm
in the space
L_{p}(\xi,\mu_{m})
, which means that we only use
S(f,\xi)
and the restriction of the system
{\mathcal{D}}_{N}
on the set

.

Algorithm 3 is good from the point of view of practical realization. At each iteration this greedy algorithm searches over at most
N
dictionary elements for choosing a new one and performs the

projections on the appropriate subspace (alike the other two algorithms). On the other hand, the Algorithm 2 performs
\binom{N}{v}

iterations of the
\ell_{p}
projections on the
v
-dimensional subspaces.

1.2
Sampling recovery on function classes

In this subsection we emphasize importance of classes, which are defined by structural conditions rather than by smoothness conditions. We give a brief history of the development of this idea. The first result, which connected the best approximations
\sigma_{v}(f,\Psi)_{2}
(see the definition in Section
2
) with convergence of the series
\sum_{k}|\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle|
, was obtained by
S.B. Stechkin
[
43
]
in 1955 in the case of an orthonormal system
\Psi
. We formulate his result and a more general result momentarily. The following classes were introduced in
[
11
]
in the study of sparse approximation with respect to arbitrary systems (dictionaries)
{\mathcal{D}}
in a Hilbert space
H
. For a general dictionary
{\mathcal{D}}\subset H
, and a parameter
\beta>0
,
define the class of functions (elements)

{\mathcal{A}}^{o}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}},M):=\left\{\sum_{k\in\Lambda}c_{k}g_{k}:\ \ g_{k}\in{\mathcal{D}},\ \ k\in\Lambda,\ |\Lambda|<\infty,\ \sum_{k\in\Lambda}|c_{k}|^{\beta}\leq M^{\beta}\right\},

where
M>0
is a constant, and define
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}},M)
to be the closure of
{\mathcal{A}}^{o}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}},M)
in the Hilbert space
H
. Furthermore, we define
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}})
as the union of the classes
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}},M)
over all
M>0
.
In the case when
{\mathcal{D}}
is an orthonormal system, S.B. Stechkin
[
43
]
proved (see a discussion of this result in
[
14
]
, Section 7.4)

f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}({\mathcal{D}})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}(v^{1/2}\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{H})\frac{1}{v}<\infty.

(1.6)

A version of (
1.6
) for the classes
{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}})
,

, was obtained in
[
11
]
:

f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}(v^{\alpha}\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{H})^{\beta}\frac{1}{v}<\infty,

(1.7)

where
\alpha:=1/\beta-1/2
. In particular, (
1.7
) implies that for
f\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\beta}({\mathcal{D}})
,
\beta\in(0,2)
, we have

\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{H}\ll v^{1/2-1/\beta}.

(1.8)

We recall that relations (
1.6
) – (

) were proved for an orthonormal system
{\mathcal{D}}
.
It is very interesting to note that (
1.8
) actually holds for a general normalized system

provided

\beta\in(0,1]
. In the case
\beta=1
it was proved by B. Maurey (see
[
39
]
). For
\beta\in(0,1]
it was proved in
[
11
]
. We note that classes
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}({\mathcal{D}})
and their generalizations for the case of Banach spaces play a fundamental role in studying best
v
-term approximation and convergence properties of greedy algorithms with respect to general dictionaries
{\mathcal{D}}
(see
[
49
]
). This fact encouraged experts to introduce and study function classes defined by restrictions on the coefficients of the functions’ expansions. We explain this approach in detail.

Let
\Psi:=\{\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\}_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be a system indexed by
\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Consider a sequence of subsets
{\mathcal{G}}:=\{G_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

Consider functions representable in the form of absolutely convergent series:

f=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\psi_{\mathbf{k}},\qquad\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)|<\infty.

(1.10)

For
\beta\in(0,1]

consider the class
{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}})
of all functions

which have representations (
1.10
) satisfying the conditions

\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in G_{j}\setminus G_{j-1}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)|^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}\leq 2^{-rj},\quad j=1,2,\dots,\quad G_{0}:=\emptyset.

(1.11)

One can also consider the following narrower version
{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}},\infty)
, the class of all functions
f
which have representations (

Step 1.

Step 2.

Step 3.
The greedy type and the

Some definitions and notations

2.1
Universality and incoherence

We begin with a brief
description of some necessary concepts on
sparse approximation. Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and let
{\mathcal{D}}=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a given (countable) system of elements in
X
. Given a finite subset
J\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, we define
V_{J}({\mathcal{D}}):=\operatorname{span}\{g_{j}:\ \ j\in J\}
.
For a positive integer
v
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{v}({\mathcal{D}})
the collection of all linear spaces
V_{J}({\mathcal{D}})
with
|J|=v
, and
denote by
\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}})
the set of all
v
-term approximants with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}
; that is,

\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}}):=\bigcup_{V\in{\mathcal{X}}_{v}({\mathcal{D}})}V.

Given
f\in X
, we define

\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{X}:=\inf_{g\in\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}})}\|f-g\|_{X}=d(f,\Sigma_{v}({\mathcal{D}}))_{X},\ \ v=1,2,\cdots.

Moreover, for a function class
{\mathbf{F}}\subset X
, we define
\sigma_{0}({\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}:=\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{F}}}\|f\|_{X}
, and

\sigma_{v}({\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}:=\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{F}}}\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{X},\quad v=1,2,\cdots.

In this paper we mostly consider the case where
X=L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
and
1\leq p<\infty
. In this case, we sometimes write for brevity
\sigma_{v}(\cdot,\cdot)_{p}
instead of

\sigma_{v}(\cdot,\cdot)_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}
.

We study systems, which have the universal sampling discretization property.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We say that a finite subset
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
provides the
L_{p}
-universal sampling discretization for the collection
{\mathcal{X}}:=\{X(n)\}_{n=1}^{k}
of finite-dimensional linear subspaces
X(n)
if

\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|f\|_{p}^{p}\quad\text{for any}\quad f\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{k}X(n).

(2.1)

We denote by
m({\mathcal{X}},p)
the minimal integer
m
such that there exists a set
\xi
of
m
points which
provides the
L_{p}
-universal sampling discretization (
2.1
) for the collection
{\mathcal{X}}
.

We will use a brief form
L_{p}
-usd for the
L_{p}
-universal sampling discretization (
2.1
).

In
[
9
]
the following one-sided universal discretization condition on the collection was used.

Definition 2.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We say that a set
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
provides the one-sided
L_{p}
-universal sampling discretization with constant
D\geq 1
for a
collection
{\mathcal{X}}:=\{X(n)\}_{n=1}^{k}
of finite-dimensional linear subspaces

X(n)
if we have

\|f\|_{p}\leq D\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\quad\text{for any}\quad f\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{k}X(n).

(2.2)

Here is the definition of somewhat weaker condition than the above one.

Definition 2.3

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We say that a set
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
provides universal LDI
(p,\infty)
(LDI stands for Left Discretization Inequality) with constant
D\geq 1
for a collection
{\mathcal{X}}:=\{X(n)\}_{n=1}^{k}
of finite-dimensional linear subspaces

if

\|f\|_{p}\leq D\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|f(\xi^{j})|\quad\text{for any}\quad f\in\bigcup_{n=1}^{k}X(n),

(2.3)

in which case we write
\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{k}X(n)\in\mathcal{LD}(m,p,\infty,D)

The property given in Definition
2.4
below concerns incoherence property of the system, which is
known to be useful in approximation by the WCGA (see, for instance,
[

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with a norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
v,S\in\mathbb{N}
be given integers such that
v\leq S
. We say that a system
{\mathcal{D}}=\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset X

in the space

, we have

\sum_{i\in A}|c_{i}|\leq V|A|^{r}\left\|\sum_{i\in B}c_{i}g_{i}\right\|.

(2.4)

We will use a brief form (
v,S
)-ipw(
V,r
) for the (
v,S
)-incoherence property with parameters
V>0
and
r>0
in
X
.

We gave Definition
2.4
for a countable system
{\mathcal{D}}
. Similar definition can be given for any system
{\mathcal{D}}
as well.

Denote, for a set
\xi:=\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}\}
of
m

\Omega

\Omega_{m}:=\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}\},\ \ \mu_{m}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{\xi^{j}},\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \mu_{\xi}:=\frac{\mu+\mu_{m}}{2},

(2.5)

where
\delta_{\mathbf{x}}
denotes the Dirac measure supported at a point
\mathbf{x}
.

As in previous papers (see, for instance,
[
58
]
), we study a special case when
{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies certain restrictions. Following notations used in the literature, for convenience, in this case we use the notation
\Psi
for a system
{\mathcal{D}}
.
We formulate these restrictions in the form of conditions imposed on
\Psi
.

Condition A.
Assume that
\Psi:=\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a system of uniformly bounded functions on
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfying

\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}|\varphi_{j}(\mathbf{x})|\leq 1,\ \ 1\leq j<\infty.

(2.6)

Condition B1.
Assume that
\Psi:=\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal system in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
.

Condition B2.
Assume that
\Psi:=\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Riesz system in the space
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
; i.e. there exist two positive constants
0<R_{1}\leq R_{2}<\infty
such that
for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N},

R_{1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\right\|_{2}\leq R_{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.7)

Condition B3.
Assume that
\Psi
is a Bessel system, i.e. there exists a constant
K>0
such that for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and for any
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N},

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\leq K\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

(2.8)

Clearly, Condition B1 implies Condition B2 with
R_{1}=R_{2}=1
. Condition B2 implies Condition B3 with

K=R_{1}^{-2}
.

We often use the concept of Nikol’skii inequality. For the reader’s convenience we formulate it here. Other definitions and notations are introduced below in the text.

Nikol’skii-type inequalities.
Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, and let
X_{N}\subset L_{q}(\Omega)
be a subspace of dimension
N
. The inequality

\|f\|_{q}\leq H\|f\|_{p},\ \ \forall f\in X_{N}

(2.9)

is called the Nikol’skii inequality for the pair
(p,q)
with the constant
H
.
In such a case, we write
X_{N}\in NI(p,q,H)
. Typically,
H
depends on
N
, for instance,
H
can be of order
N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}
.

In addition to the above formulated Nikol’skii inequality we need one more property of a similar nature.

u
-term Nikol’skii inequality.
Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and let
\Psi
be a system from
L_{q}:=L_{q}(\Omega,\mu)
. For a natural number
u
, we say that the system
\Psi
has
the
u
-term Nikol’skii inequality for the pair
(p,q)
with the constant
H
if the following
inequality holds

\|f\|_{q}\leq H\|f\|_{p},\qquad\forall f\in\Sigma_{u}(\Psi).

(2.10)

In such a case, we write
\Psi\in NI(p,q,H,u)
.

Note, that obviously
H\geq 1
.

2.2
Sparse approximation in Banach spaces

Recall that the modulus of smoothness of a Banach space
X
is the function on
(0,\infty)
defined as

\eta(X,w):=\sup_{x,y\in X;\|x\|=\|y\|=1}\left(\frac{\|x+wy\|+\|x-wy\|}{2}-1\right),\ \ w>0,

(2.11)

and that
X
is called uniformly smooth if
\eta(w)/w\to 0
when
w\to 0+
.
It is well known that the
L_{p}
space with
1<p<\infty
is a uniformly smooth Banach space with

\eta(L_{p},w)\leq\begin{cases}(p-1)w^{2}/2,&2\leq p<\infty,\\
w^{p}/p,&1\leq p\leq 2.\end{cases}

(2.12)

We will use some known general results on best
v
-term approximations with respect to
an arbitrary system in a Banach space. Usually, these results are proved in the case of real Banach spaces. It is convenient for us to consider complex Banach spaces, partially because of our applications to the special case when the system of interest is the trigonometric system
{\mathcal{T}}^{d}
. We now prove the complex version of the result known in the case of real Banach spaces. For a system
{\mathcal{D}}\subset X
, denote

A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}):=\left\{f\in X:\,f=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}g_{i},\quad g_{i}\in{\mathcal{D}},\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{C},\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|\leq 1\right\}.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
42
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a system of elements in a complex Banach space
X
satisfying
\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}\|g\|_{X}\leq 1
. Assume that
the modulus of smoothness of
X
satisfies the following condition for some constants
1<q\leq 2
and
\gamma>0
:

\eta(X,w)\leq\gamma w^{q}\ \ \text{ for all $w>0$}.

Then, there exists a constant
C(q,\gamma)>0
, which may only depend on
q
and
\gamma
, such that

\sigma_{v}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}),{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\leq C(q,\gamma)(v+1)^{1/q-1}\ \ \text{for any $v\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$}.

(2.13)

Moreover, the bound in (
2.13
) is provided by a constructive method based on greedy algorithms.

Proof.

In the case of real Banach spaces Lemma
2.1
is known (see, for instance,
[
49
]
, p.342). The corresponding bound is provided by the Weak Tchebychev Greedy Algorithm. We derive the complex case from the real one.

By a standard decomplexification process, every complex Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
can be viewed as a direct sum of two identical real Banach spaces representing the “real” and “imaginary” parts of its vectors. To be precise, let
X_{\mathbb{R}}=(X_{\mathbb{R}},\|\cdot\|_{X})
denote the underlying real Banach space obtained by restricting the scalar multiplication of
X
to the real field
\mathbb{R}
. Then

X
can be decomposed as the direct sum
X=Y\oplus iY
, where
Y
is a real Banach subspace of
X_{\mathbb{R}}
. Furthermore, by the open mapping theorem, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
x,y\in Y
,

c(\|x\|_{X}+\|y\|_{X})\leq\|x+iy\|_{X}\leq\|x\|_{X}+\|y\|_{X}.

In this context, every vector
f\in X
admits a unique representation

f=f^{R}+if^{I},

where
f^{R}=\operatorname{Re}(f)\in Y
,
f^{I}=\operatorname{Im}(f)\in Y
, and

\|f\|_{X}\sim\|f^{R}\|_{X}+\|f^{I}\|_{X}.

Now consider the systems

{\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{1}:=\{g^{R}\,:\,g\in{\mathcal{D}}\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ {\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{2}:=\{g^{I}\,:\,g\in{\mathcal{D}}\}.

For
i=1,2
, we define

X^{r}_{i}=\overline{\operatorname{span}({\mathcal{D}}_{i}^{r})}^{X_{\mathbb{R}}}

to be the real Banach subspace of
(X_{\mathbb{R}},\|\cdot\|_{X})
spanned by
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}^{r}
.
Clearly, for
i=1,2
,

\eta(X^{r}_{i},w)\leq\eta(X,w)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \sup_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}_{i}^{r}}\|g\|_{X}\leq C\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}\|g\|_{X}\leq C.

Thus, by applying the real-space analogue of Lemma
2.1
to the systems
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}^{r}
in the real Banach spaces
X_{i}^{r}
, we obtain

\sigma_{v}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{i}),{\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{i})_{X_{\mathbb{R}}}\leq C_{1}(q,\gamma)(v+1)^{1/q-1},\quad i=1,2.

(2.14)

Next, let
f\in X
be such that

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}g_{k},\quad g_{k}\in{\mathcal{D}},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|\leq 1.

Writing
c_{k}=a_{k}+ib_{k}
with
a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_{k}g^{R}_{k}-b_{k}g_{k}^{I})+i\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(a_{k}g^{I}_{k}+b_{k}g_{k}^{R})=:f^{R}+if^{I}.

Clearly,

|a_{k}|+|b_{k}|\leq\sqrt{2}(a_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{2})^{1/2}=\sqrt{2}|c_{k}|.

Therefore,

2^{-1/2}f^{R}\in\operatorname{conv}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{1}),A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{2}))\ \ \text{and}\ \ 2^{-1/2}f^{I}\in\operatorname{conv}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{1},A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{2})).

It follows that

\sigma_{4v}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}),{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\leq 2\max_{i=1,2}\sigma_{v}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{i}),{\mathcal{D}}^{r}_{i})_{X_{R}}.

This and (
2.14
) imply Lemma
2.1
.

∎

Note that the papers
[
12
]
and
[
18
]
are devoted to the study of greedy algorithms in complex Banach spaces. In particular, Lemma
2.1
follows from Theorem 3.2 of
[
18
]
.

Theorem 3.1

4
Recovery by
\ell_{p}
minimization

4.1
Projections on subspaces

Let
X_{N}
be an
N
-dimensional subspace of the space of continuous functions
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
. As in Section
1.1
, for a fixed
m
and a set of
m
points
\xi:=\{\xi^{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
, we associate with a function
f\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
a vector

S(f,\xi):=(f(\xi^{1}),\dots,f(\xi^{m}))\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m},

and consider the weighted
\ell_{p}
norm

\|S(f,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}:=\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w_{\nu}|f(\xi^{\nu})|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

with the usual change when
p=\infty
.
Define the best approximation of
f\in L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, by elements of
X_{N}
as follows

d(f,X_{N})_{p}:=\inf_{g\in X_{N}}\|f-g\|_{p}.

It is well known that there exists an element, which we denote by
P_{X_{N},p}(f)\in X_{N}
, such that

\|f-P_{X_{N},p}(f)\|_{p}=d(f,X_{N})_{p}.

The mapping
P_{X_{N},p}:L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)\to X_{N}
, which may not be unique (in the case of
p=1
or
p=\infty
), is called the Chebyshev projection.

Following
[
56
]
, we will prove Theorem
4.1
below under assumptions
A1
and
A2
.

A1. Discretization.
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Suppose that
\xi:=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
is such that for any

g\in X_{N}

\|g\|_{p}\leq D\|S(g,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}

with a positive constant
D
which may depend on
d
and
p
.

A2. Weights.
Suppose that there is a positive constant
W=C(p)
such that

\sum_{\nu=1}^{m}w_{\nu}\leq W
.

Consider the following well known recovery operator (algorithm) (see Algorithm 1 in Section
1
)

\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f):=\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi,X_{N})(f):=\underset{u\in X_{N}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\,\|S(f-u,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}.

Note that the above algorithm
\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)
only uses the function values
f(\xi^{\nu})
,
\nu=1,\dots,m
. In the case
p=2
it is a linear algorithm – orthogonal projection with respect
to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,\mathbf{w}}
. Therefore, in the case
p=2
approximation error by the algorithm
\ell 2\mathbf{w}(\xi)
gives an upper bound for the recovery characteristic
\varrho_{m}(\cdot,L_{2})
. In the case
p\neq 2
approximation error by the algorithm
\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)
gives an upper bound for the recovery characteristic
\varrho_{m}^{o}(\cdot,L_{p})
.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
56
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then
under assumptions
A1
and
A2
, for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
, we have

\|f-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)\|_{p}\leq(2DW^{1/p}+1)d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

Furthermore, under assumption
A1
with
p=\infty
, for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
, we have

\|f-\ell\infty(\xi)(f)\|_{\infty}\leq(2D+1)d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

Proof.

We give a detailed proof for the case
1\leq p<\infty
. The case
p=\infty
is similar and even simpler. For brevity denote
g:=P_{X_{N},\infty}(f)
.
From the definition of the mapping
P_{X_{N},\infty}
, we obtain

\|f-g\|_{p}\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}=d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

(4.1)

Clearly,

\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}=d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

Therefore, by
A2
we get

\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}\leq W^{1/p}\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq W^{1/p}d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

(4.2)

Next, by the definition of the algorithm
\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)
and by (
4.2
), we obtain

\|S(f-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f),\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}\leq\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}\leq W^{1/p}d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

(4.3)

Bounds (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) imply

\|S(g-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f),\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}\leq 2W^{1/p}d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

(4.4)

Then, the discretization assumption
A1
implies

\|g-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)\|_{p}\leq 2DW^{1/p}d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

(4.5)

Combining bounds (
4.1
) and (
4.5
), we conclude

\|f-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)\|_{p}\leq\|f-g\|_{p}+\|g-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)\|_{p}\leq(1+2DW^{1/p})d(f,X_{N})_{\infty},

which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

The following version of Theorem
4.1
for the error of
\|f-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)\|_{\infty}
under an extra condition on the Nikol’skii inequality was proved in
[
31
]
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Under assumptions
A1
,
A2
, and an extra assumption
X_{N}\in NI(p,\infty,H)
for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
we have

\|f-\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)\|_{\infty}\leq(2HDW^{1/p}+1)d(f,X_{N})_{\infty}.

Proof.

The proof is simple and goes along the lines of the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Let
u:=\ell p\mathbf{w}(\xi)(f)
. For an arbitrary
g\in X_{N}
, we have the following chain of inequalities

\displaystyle\|f-u\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}+\|g-u\|_{\infty}\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}+H\|g-u\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}+HD\|S(g-u,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}+HD(\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}+\|S(f-u,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}})

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}+2HD\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{p,\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}+2HDW^{1/p}\|S(f-g,\xi)\|_{\infty}\leq(1+2HDW^{1/p})\|f-g\|_{\infty}.

Minimizing over all
g\in X_{N}
, we complete the proof.

∎

Recovery by greedy algorithms

5.1
WOMP

The Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (WOMP) is a greedy algorithm defined with respect to a dictionary (or system)
\mathcal{D}\subset H
in a Hilbert space
H
, which is equipped with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
. This algorithm is also frequently referred to as the Weak Orthogonal Greedy Algorithm (see, e.g.,
[
49
]
).

Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (WOMP).
Let
{\mathcal{D}}=\{g\}
be a system of nonzero elements in a Hilbert space
H
such that
\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}\|g\|_{H}\leq 1
.
Let
\tau:=\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,1]
be a given sequence of weakness parameters.
Given
f_{0}\in H
, we define a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset H
of residuals for
k=1,2,\cdots
inductively as follows:

(1)

g_{k}\in{\mathcal{D}}
is any element satisfying

|\langle f_{k-1},g_{k}\rangle|\geq t_{k}\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}|\langle f_{k-1},g\rangle|.

(2)

Let
H_{k}:=\operatorname{span}\{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\}
, and define

G_{k}^{\tau}(\cdot,{\mathcal{D}})
to be the orthogonal projection operator from
H
onto the space
H_{k}
.

(3)

Define the residual after the
k
th iteration of the algorithm by

f_{k}:=f_{0}-G_{k}^{\tau}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}}).

When
t_{k}=1
for all
k\geq 1
, the WOMP reduces to the Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP). In this paper, we restrict our focus to the case where
t_{k}=t\in(0,1]
for all
k\geq 1
. We denote by
\{G_{k}^{t}(\cdot,\mathcal{D})_{H}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
the WOMP defined with respect to the weakness parameter
t
and the system
\mathcal{D}
in a Hilbert space
H
.
While the elements
g_{k}
selected in each step of the algorithm may not be unique, all results presented below are independent of the specific choice of
g_{k}
. For a comprehensive treatment of greedy approximation theory, we refer the reader to
[
49
]
.

UP(
u,D
). (
u,D
)-unconditional property.
Let

be integers such that

We say that a system

of elements

if for any

and

(5.1)

where

5.2
WCGA

We give the definition of the Weak Chebyshev Greedy Algorithm (WCGA) in a Banach space, which was introduced in
[
48
]
as a generalization of the Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (WOMP).
To be more precise,
let
X^{\ast}
denote the dual of a Banach space
X
.
For a nonzero element
g\in X
, we denote by
F_{g}
a norming (peak) functional for
g
, that is, an element
F_{g}\in X^{\ast}
satisfying

\|F_{g}\|_{X^{*}}=1,\qquad F_{g}(g)=\|g\|_{X}.

The existence of such a functional is guaranteed by the Hahn-Banach theorem.

Now we can define the WCGA as follows.

Weak Chebyshev Greedy Algorithm (WCGA).
Let

\tau:=\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a given weakness sequence of positive numbers
\leq 1
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}=\{g\}\subset X
be a system of nonzero elements in
X
such that
\|g\|\leq 1
for
g\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Given
f_{0}\in X
, we define the elements
f_{m}\in X
and
\phi_{m}\in{\mathcal{D}}
for
m=1,2,\cdots
inductively as follows:

(1)

\phi_{m}\in{\mathcal{D}}
is any element satisfying

|F_{f_{m-1}}(\phi_{m})|\geq t_{m}\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{D}}}|F_{f_{m-1}}(g)|.

(2)

Define

\Phi(m):=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m}\},

and let
G_{m}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}
be the best approximant to
f_{0}
from the space
\Phi(m)
; that is,

G_{m}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}:=\underset{G\in\Phi(m)}{\operatorname{argmin}}\|f_{0}-G\|_{X}.

(3)

Define

f_{m}:=f_{0}-G_{m}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}.

Remark 5.1
.

We defined the WCGA for a system satisfying an extra condition
\|g\|\leq 1
for
g\in{\mathcal{D}}
. Clearly, realizations of the WCGA for a new system
{\mathcal{D}}^{B}:=\{Bg,\,g\in{\mathcal{D}}\}
, where
B
is a positive number, coincide with those for the system
{\mathcal{D}}
. This means that the restriction
\|g\|\leq 1
can be replaced by the restriction
\|g\|\leq B
with some positive number
B
.

We will only consider the WCGA for the case where
t_{k}=t\in(0,1]
for all
k\geq 1
. For a system
{\mathcal{D}}\subset X
, and a weakness parameter
t\in(0,1]
, we denote the corresponding WCGA as
\{G_{m}^{t}(\cdot,\mathcal{D})_{X}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
.
We also point out that in the case when
X
is a Hilbert space, the WCGA coincides with the well known WOMP, which is very popular in signal processing, and in particular, in compressed sensing. In approximation theory the WOMP is also called the Weak Orthogonal Greedy Algorithm (WOGA).

Note that by definition,
G_{k}^{t}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\in\Sigma_{k}({\mathcal{D}})
. Consequently,

\sigma_{k}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\leq\|f_{k}\|_{X}=\|f_{0}-G_{k}^{t}(f_{0},{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\|_{X}.

Under certain conditions on the system
{\mathcal{D}}
and the Banach space
X
,
an inverse inequality holds as well. This is established in
Theorem
5.4
below, which was proved for real Banach spaces in
[
50
]
(see also
[
55
]
, Section 8.7, Theorem 8.7.17, p.431) and for complex Banach spaces in
[
12
]
. Although the theorem was originally stated for dictionaries, the proof remains valid for a general system
\mathcal{D}
.

Recall that the
(v,S)
-incoherence property of a system
\mathcal{D}\subset X
, where
v
and
S
are integers such that
1\leq v\leq S
, is given in Definition
2.4
. It follows directly from this definition that the
(v,S)
-incoherence property with parameters
V>0
and
r>0
implies the
(v^{\prime},S)
-incoherence property with the same parameters for any integer
1\leq v^{\prime}\leq v
.

Theorem 5.4

(
[
50
, Theorem 2.7]
,
[
55
, Theorem 8.7.17]
)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space satisfying the following condition for some parameter
1<q\leq 2
and constant
\gamma>0
:

\eta(X,w)\leq\gamma w^{q},\ \ \forall w>0.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{D}}\subset X
is a system having the (

)-incoherence property with parameters
V>0
and
r>0
, where
v_{0}
,
S
are integers such that
1\leq v_{0}\leq S
. Let

, and let

be a given weakness parameter. Then there exists a constant
C(q)>0
depending only on
q
such that

\sigma_{u}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\leq\|f-G_{u}^{t}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\|_{X}\leq C\sigma_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}})_{X}

for every integer

satisfying
u+v\leq S
, where

u=u(v,t):=\left\lceil C(q)\gamma^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\Big(\frac{V\cdot v^{r}}{t}\Big)^{q^{\prime}}\ln(V\cdot v)\right\rceil,\ \ q^{\prime}:=\frac{q}{q-1},

C>1
is an absolute constant, and
\{G_{k}^{t}(\cdot,{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denotes the WCGA with respect to the system

and the weakness parameter

.

We will apply Theorem
5.4
to the discretized version of the given system

. Typically, instead of the space

{\mathcal{T}}(2{\mathbf{N}},d)_{q}

{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\tilde{f}(\xi^{1}),\cdots,\tilde{f}(\xi^{m})\Big)={\mathcal{M}}\Big(-\tilde{f}(\xi^{1}),\cdots,-\tilde{f}(\xi^{m})\Big)={\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{0})=g_{0},

this completes the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Lemma
6.1
implies the following lower bound for the classes
{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}({\mathcal{T}}^{d})
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For
\beta\in(0,1]
and
r>0
we have for
2\leq p\leq\infty

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}({\mathcal{T}}^{d}),L_{p})\gg m^{1-1/p-1/\beta-r/d}.

(6.18)

Proof.

Take
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and set
N:=2^{n}-1
,
{\mathbf{N}}:=(N,\dots,N)
.
Then for
f\in{\mathcal{T}}({\mathbf{N}},d)_{2}
we have by the Hölder inequality with parameter

\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}:\|\mathbf{k}\|_{\infty}\leq N}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}\leq(2N+1)^{d(1/\beta-1/2)}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}:\|\mathbf{k}\|_{\infty}\leq N}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

=(2N+1)^{d(1/\beta-1/2)}\|f\|_{2}.

This bound and Lemma
6.1
with
q=2
imply (
6.18
).
∎

We now show how Theorem

can be used to show that Theorem

cannot be substantially improved in the sense of relations between
m
and
u
.

Proposition 6.2

(
[
59
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{N}}=(N_{1},\cdots,N_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
and define the trigonometric system

{\mathcal{D}}_{N}=\Big\{e^{i(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{x})}:\ \ \mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ -2{\mathbf{N}}\leq\mathbf{k}\leq 2{\mathbf{N}}\Big\},

where the cardinality of the system is given by

N=|{\mathcal{D}}_{N}|=\vartheta(2{\mathbf{N}})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(4N_{j}+1).

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
. Assume that there exists a set
\xi=\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{m}\}\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
of
m\leq\vartheta({\mathbf{N}})/2
points that provides the one-sided
L_{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
-universal sampling discretization (
4.14
) with constant
D>0
for the collection
{\mathcal{X}}_{2v}({\mathcal{D}}_{N})
and some integer
1\leq v<N
. Then

v\leq C(d,p)(2D+1)^{2}(\vartheta({\mathbf{N}}))^{2/p},

where
C(d,p)>0
is a constant depending only on
p
and
d
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.5
, the inequality (
4.15
) holds for any
f\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
. We take the function
f
from the proof of Lemma
6.1
defined in (
6.14
). By the definition of
f
we get
f(\xi^{j})=0
for all
j=1,\dots,m
. Therefore,
B_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{p}(\xi))=0
and by (
6.17
), we obtain

\|f-B_{v}(f,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{p}(\xi))\|_{p}\geq C_{3}(d)\vartheta({\mathbf{N}})^{1-1/p}.

(6.19)

On the other hand, using (
6.15
) with
q=2
, we get

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\mathbf{k})|\leq\vartheta(2{\mathbf{N}})^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}^{1/2}\leq C_{1}(d)\vartheta({\mathbf{N}}).

(6.20)

By (
2.12
), we obtain that

\eta(L_{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mu_{\xi}),w)\leq(p-1)w^{2}/2,\ \ \forall w>0.

It is known (see, for instance,
[
49
]
, p.342, and Lemma
2.1
above) that for any dictionary
{\mathcal{D}}=\{g\}
,
\|g\|_{X}\leq 1
in a Banach space

with

\sigma_{v}(A_{1}({\mathcal{D}}),{\mathcal{D}})_{X}\leq C(q,\gamma)(v+1)^{1/q-1}.

Here

6.3
Recovery on classes with conditions on the hyperbolic cross decompositions

In this section, we expand upon the classes
{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A_{\beta}}(\Psi)
introduced in Subsection
1.2
, which were initially defined for the trigonometric system
\Psi={\mathcal{T}}^{d}
with
\beta=1
. We now give the definition for a general system
\Psi=\{\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\}_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
of functions on a probability space
(\Omega,\mu)
.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
be a vector of non-negative integers. We define the block of indices
\rho(\mathbf{s})
as

\rho(\mathbf{s}):=\bigl\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\lfloor 2^{s_{j}-1}\rfloor\leq|k_{j}|<2^{s_{j}},\quad j=1,\dots,d\bigr\}.

For a function
f
representable by the absolutely convergent series

f=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\quad\text{with}\ \ \sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)|<\infty,

(6.23)

we define

\delta_{\mathbf{s}}(f,\Psi):=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rho(\mathbf{s})}a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\psi_{\mathbf{k}},\ \ \quad f_{j}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:\ \|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}=j}\delta_{\mathbf{s}}(f,\Psi),\quad j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

and for
\beta\in(0,1]
,

|f|_{A_{\beta}(\Psi)}:=\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)|^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}.

For parameters
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
, the class
\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A_{\beta}}(\Psi)
consists of functions
f
possessing a representation (
6.23
) such that the hyperbolic layers satisfy

|f_{j}|_{A_{\beta}(\Psi)}\leq 2^{-aj}(\bar{j})^{(d-1)b},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(6.24)

where
\bar{j}:=\max(j,1)
.

These classes were originally introduced for the trigonometric system with
\beta=1
in
[
51
]
, with the general definition for any
\Psi
and
\beta=1
later appearing in
[
8
]
. For simplicity, we denote
\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A}(\Psi):=\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A_{1}}(\Psi)

The following result from
[
42
]
establishes upper bounds for the optimal nonlinear recovery of the classes

in the
L_{p}
norm.

Theorem 6.4

(
[

6.4
Recovery on general classes

In this subsection we discuss recent results from
[
41

on sampling recovery on general classes
{\mathbf{A}}^{r,b}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}})

{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}})

. The following collection of classes was introduced
in
[
59
]
. Given a system
\Psi=(\psi_{\mathbf{k}})_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
,
consider a sequence of subsets

{\mathcal{G}}:=\{G_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
,
G_{j}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

G_{1}\subset G_{2}\subset\cdots\subset G_{j}\subset G_{j+1}\subset\cdots,\quad\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}G_{j}={\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\quad G_{0}:=\emptyset\,,

f=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)\psi_{\mathbf{k}},\qquad\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)|<\infty.

(6.31)

For
\beta\in(0,1]
,
r>0
, and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}

define the class
{\mathbf{A}}^{r,b}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}})
as the functions

which have representations (
6.31

\Big(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in G_{j}\setminus G_{j-1}}|a_{\mathbf{k}}(f)|^{\beta}\Big)^{1/\beta}\leq 2^{-rj}j^{b},\quad j=1,2,\dots.

(6.32)

Two special cases of these classes have already been discussed above in this section.
In
[
58
]
and
[
59
]
,
the behavior of
\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{F}},L_{p})
for the classes

{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}(\Psi):={\mathbf{A}}^{r,0}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}}^{c})

{\mathcal{G}}^{c}
being dyadic cubes,

G^{c}_{j}:=\{\mathbf{k}:\,\|\mathbf{k}\|_{\infty}<2^{j}\},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.

The second special case, for the classes

{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A_{\beta}}(\Psi):={\mathbf{A}}^{a,b}_{\beta}(\Psi,{\mathcal{G}}^{h})

with
{\mathcal{G}}^{h}
being dyadic hyperbolic crosses, was
studied in
[
42
]
.
In that case, with
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}

\rho(\mathbf{s}):=\bigl\{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:[2^{s_{j}-1}]\leq|k_{j}|<2^{s_{j}},\quad j=1,\dots,d\bigr\},\quad G^{h}_{j}:=\bigcup_{\|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}\leq j}\rho(\mathbf{s}).

{\mathbf{A}}^{r}_{\beta}(\Psi)
,
with the sequence

of dyadic cubes

c
stands for

[
55

The classes

stands for

Surprisingly, papers
[

]
and
[

Regularity condition RC(

[

]

[

Let

and

be the recovery operators defined in the beginning of Section

stands for

in the univariate case.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
47
]
)

Let

r>1/q
. Then

\varrho_{4m}(W^{r}_{q},L_{p})\asymp\sup_{f\in W^{r}_{q}}\|f-R_{m}(f)\|_{p}\asymp m^{-r+(1/q-1/p)_{+}}.

In the case
1<p<\infty
the above estimates are valid for the operator
I_{m}
instead of the operator
R_{m}
.

7.1
Nonlinear recovery

The authors of
[
20
]
(see Corollary 4.16 in v3) proved the following bound for
1<q<2
,
r>1/q
and

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\leq C(r,d,q)v^{-r+1/q-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(r+1-2/q)+1/2}.

(7.2)

The authors of
[
8

proved the following bound

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\leq C^{\prime}(r,d,q)v^{-r+1/q-1/2}(\log v)^{(d-1)(r+1-2/q)}

(7.3)

provided that

m\geq c^{\prime}(r,d,q)v(\log(2v))^{3}.

(7.4)

In the above mentioned results the sampling recovery in the
L_{2}
norm has been studied. The technique, which was used in the proofs of the bounds (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), is heavily based on the fact that we approximate in the
L_{2}
norm. The following bounds
have been proved in
[

Theorem 7.2

(

]
)
.

Let

. There exist two constants

and

such that for
r>1/q
,

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\leq Cv^{-r+1/q-1/p}(\log v)^{(d-1)(r+1-2/q)}

(7.5)

for any

and any

First, we discuss the above results and then explain how Theorem
7.2
can be derived from results of Section
6
.

Theorem
7.2
extends existing bounds for
p=2
to the broader range
p\in[2,\infty)
.
Let us make some comments on bounds (
7.2
) and (
7.3
). First of all, these bounds were proved by different methods. Bound (
7.2
)
was proved in
[
20
]
utilizing powerful results from compressed sensing theory, whereas bound (
7.3
) is based on the universal discretization of the
L_{2}
norm, with a proof centered on a greedy-type algorithm– the Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (WOMP). Second, the WOMP produces an approximant that is sparse with respect to the trigonometric system, whereas the method in (
7.2
) does not guarantee such sparsity. Third, The algorithm in
[
20
]
requires specific information about the function class (in this case,
{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}
), necessitating an algorithm tailored to each specific class. Conversely, the WOMP does not require prior knowledge of the function class to achieve bound (
7.3
), making it universal for the collection of classes
{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}
.
Finally, bound (
7.3
) is slightly sharper than (
7.2
).

The paper
[
9
]
is devoted to generalization of results of
[
8
]
, where recovery
in the norm
L_{2}
was studied, to the case of
L_{p}
spaces with
p\in[2,\infty)
. Instead of
universal discretization of the
L_{2}
norm the universal discretization of the

norm was used. The Weak Orthogonal Matching Pursuit was replaced by its generalization to the case of Banach spaces – the Weak Chebyshev Greedy Algorithm (WCGA). Also, in addition to the WCGA a recovery algorithm based on the
\ell_{p}
minimization (see
B_{v}(\cdot,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{p}(\xi))
above) was defined and studied. However, application of results from
[
9
]
to the sampling recovery of classes
{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}
in the
L_{p}
norm with
p\in(2,\infty)
gives weaker than in Theorem
7.2
bounds. It is related to the use of universal discretization of the
L_{p}
norm.

The authors of
[
25
]
used the recovery algorithm
B_{v}(\cdot,{\mathcal{D}}_{N},L_{2}(\xi))
, universal discretization of the
L_{2}
norm, and the
u
-term Nikol’skii inequality.
On one hand the proof of Theorem
7.2
goes along the lines of the corresponding proof
in
[

]
. On the other hand in the proof of Theorem

they used a new concept
of the

-term Nikol’skii inequality, known deep results on sparse approximation in the

Denote

{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A}:={\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A_{1}}({\mathcal{T}}^{d}).

We now present some direct corollaries of Theorem
6.5
with
\beta=1
for other classes, which are better known

The known results (see
[

, Ch.1, Theorem 2.2 and

1<q\leq 2

\|f_{j}\|_{A}\leq C(d,q)2^{j/q}j^{(d-1)(1-1/q)}\|f_{j}\|_{q}.

(7.6)

The inequality (

) implies that for
1<q\leq 2
the class
{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{q}
is embedded into

{\mathbf{W}}^{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}_{A}
with

,

. Therefore, Theorem

implies the

.

Theorem 7.3

(

]
)
.

Let
1<q\leq 2\leq p<\infty
. There exist two constants
c=c(a,d,p,q)
and

for

(7.7)

for any

Finally, let us consider the classical classes

It is well known (see, for instance,
[

Some lower bounds.
The classes
{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}
are classical classes of functions with
dominated mixed derivative

(Sobolev-type classes of functions with mixed smoothness).
The reader can find results on approximation properties of these classes in the books
[

[

We now proceed to the definition of the classes
{\mathbf{H}}^{r}_{p}
, which is based on the mixed differences. In this subsection we discuss results for these classes.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{t}}^{l}f(\mathbf{x})

be the mixed
l
-th difference with
step
t_{j}
in the variable
x_{j}
, that is

\Delta_{\mathbf{t}}^{l}f(\mathbf{x}):=\Delta_{t_{d},d}^{l}\dots\Delta_{t_{1},1}^{l}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}).

Let
e
be a subset of natural numbers in
[1,d]
. We denote

\Delta_{\mathbf{t}}^{l}(e):=\prod_{j\in e}\Delta_{t_{j},j}^{l},\qquad\Delta_{\mathbf{t}}^{l}(\varnothing):=Id\,-\,\text{identity operator}.

We define the class
{\mathbf{H}}_{q,l}^{r}B
,
l>r
, as the set of

f\in L_{q}
such that for any
e

\bigl\|\Delta_{\mathbf{t}}^{l}(e)f(\mathbf{x})\bigr\|_{q}\leq B\prod_{j\in e}|t_{j}|^{r}.

(7.9)

In the case
B=1
we omit it. It is known (see, for instance,
[
55
]
, p.137) that the classes
{\mathbf{H}}^{r}_{q,l}
with different
l>r
are equivalent. So, for convenience, we fix one
l=[r]+1
and omit
l
from the notation.

It is well known that in the univariate case (
d=1
) the approximation properties of the above
{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}
and
{\mathbf{H}}^{r}_{q}
classes
are similar. It is also well known that in the multivariate case (
d\geq 2
) asymptotic characteristics (for instance, Kolmogorov widths, entropy numbers, best hyperbolic cross trigonometric approximations and others) have different rate of decay in
the majority of cases. Recently, a new scale of classes has been introduced and studied (see classes
\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{q}
and
\mathbf{W}^{a,b}_{A_{\beta}}(\Psi)
defined above). It turns out that this scale is
convenient for simultaneous analysis of optimal sampling recovery of both the
{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}
and the
{\mathbf{H}}^{r}_{q}
classes. We discuss here the case, when
\Psi={\mathcal{T}}^{d}
, and remind that
{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A}:={\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A_{1}}({\mathcal{T}}^{d})
. The following embedding result easily follows from known results (see
[

]
).

Proposition 7.1
.

We have for
r>1/q

{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A}\quad\text{with}\quad a=r-1/q,\,b=1-1/q,\quad 1<q\leq 2;

(7.10)

{\mathbf{H}}^{r}_{q}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A}\quad\text{with}\quad a=r-1/q,\,b=1,\quad 1\leq q\leq 2.

(7.11)

In
[
25
]
Theorem
7.2
was derived from the embedding (
7.10
) and the following result for the
{\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A}
classes (see

Theorem 7.4

(
[

Let

c(a,p,d)
and
C(a,b,p,d)

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{W}}^{a,b}_{A},L_{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\leq C(a,b,p,d)v^{-a-1/p}(\log(2v))^{(d-1)(a+b)}

(7.12)

for any

7.2
Linear recovery

We begin with brief comments on the classical Smolyak recovery operators. For a detailed discussion of these and related operators, we refer the reader to the books
[
46
]
,
[
55
]
, and
[
14
]
. Let
\{R_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the sequence of sampling recovery operators defined in (
1.1
). For each
i=1,\dots,d
, let
R_{n}^{i}
denote the operator
R_{n}
acting specifically on the variable
x_{i}
. We define the univariate difference operator as

\Delta_{s}^{i}:=R_{2^{s}}^{i}-R_{2^{s-1}}^{i},\ \ s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \ \quad R_{1/2}=0,

and for a multi-index
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, we define the multivariate operator

\Delta_{\mathbf{s}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Delta_{s_{i}}^{i}.

The corresponding Smolyak operator is defined as

T_{n}:=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:\ \|\mathbf{s}\|_{1}\leq n}\Delta_{\mathbf{s}}.

The operator
T_{n}
utilizes
m
function values, where the number of samples satisfies

m\ll\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k}k^{d-1}\ll 2^{n}n^{d-1}.

In 1960, S. Smolyak established the following error bound in the uniform norm:

\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{\infty}}\|f-T_{n}f\|_{\infty}\ll 2^{-rn}n^{d-1},\quad r>0.

This result was subsequently extended to
L_{p}
norms with
p<\infty
in
[
44
]
:

\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{p}}\|f-T_{n}f\|_{p}\ll 2^{-rn}n^{d-1},\quad r>1/p.

Further developments include the following results from
[
47
]
:

\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{2})_{\infty}\asymp m^{-r+1/2}(\log m)^{r(d-1)},\quad r>1/2.

In this case, the order of optimal recovery is attained by the Smolyak operator
T_{n}
with an appropriately chosen
n
.
Additionally, for
q\in(1,\infty)
and
r>\frac{1}{q}
, we have (
[
47
]
)

\sup_{f\in{\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q}}\|f-T_{n}(f)\|_{\infty}\asymp 2^{-(r-1/q)n}n^{(d-1)(1-1/q)}.

In the preceding Subsections, the discussion focused on the study of the nonlinear characteristic
\varrho_{m}^{o}(W,L_{p})
. However, the majority of established results in optimal sampling recovery deal with linear recovery methods. We now provide brief comments on recent advancements in this area and refer the reader to the books
[
14
]
,
[
55
]
and the survey paper
[
21
]
for a comprehensive discussion of previous results in this direction.

For special sets
{\mathbf{F}}
in the setting of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, the following inequality was established (see
[
15
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
26
]
, and
[
27
]
):

\varrho_{cn}({\mathbf{F}},L_{2})\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\geq n}d_{k}({\mathbf{F}},L_{2})^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(7.14)

where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Here,
d_{k}({\mathbf{F}},L_{2})
denotes the Kolmogorov
k
-width of
{\mathbf{F}}
in
the space
L_{2}
.

Established results for the Kolmogorov widths
d_{k}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{p},L_{2})
(see, for instance,
[
55
]
, p.216) asserts that for
1<q\leq 2
with
r>1/q-1/2
and for
2<q<\infty
with
r>0
, the following asymptotic holds:

d_{k}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})\asymp\left(\frac{(\log k)^{d-1})}{k}\right)^{r-(1/q-1/2)_{+}},

(7.15)

where
(a)_{+}:=\max(a,0)
.
When combined with (
7.14
), this yields the following upper bounds for
1<q\leq\infty
and
r>\max(1/q,1/2)
:

\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})\ll\left(\frac{(\log m)^{d-1})}{m}\right)^{r-(1/q-1/2)_{+}}.

These bounds solve the problem on the correct decay orders for the sequence
\rho_{m}(\mathbf{W}^{r}_{q},L_{2})
in the case when
1<q<\infty
and
r>\max(1/q,1/2)
, as it is evident that
\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})\geq d_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})
. It was observed in
[
20
]
that for
q<2
and sufficiently large
d
, the upper bound (
7.2
) and the known lower bound from (
7.15
) imply that

\varrho_{m}^{o}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})=o(\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})).

This demonstrates that in those cases, nonlinear methods achieve a better rate of sampling recovery than linear methods.

In the case of our interest where
1<q<2<p<\infty
, the correct order of
\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{p})
remains unknown. Known results utilizing Smolyak point sets provide the following upper bound for
1<q<p<\infty
and
r>1/q
(see, for instance,
[
55
]
, p. 308):

\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{p})\ll\left(\frac{(\log m)^{d-1})}{m}\right)^{r-1/q+1/p}(\log m)^{(d-1)/p}.

(7.16)

A comparison between (
7.16
) and the bounds established in Theorem
7.2
reveals that for certain parameters
d
and
p
, nonlinear recovery methods provide a better error rate than the linear recovery bound given by (
7.16
).

The above inequality (
7.14
) can only be used when the series on its right side converges. In cases where the corresponding series diverges,
the following alternative inequality established in
[
56
]
(see also Theorem
4.4
above) becomes particularly useful: There exist two positive absolute constants
b
and
B
such that for any compact subset
W
of
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
, we have

\varrho_{bn}(W,L_{2})\leq Bd_{n}(W,L_{\infty}).

This inequality, combined with the upper bounds for the Kolmogorov widths obtained in
[
60
]
,
gives the following bounds for sampling recovery (see
[
60
]
): for
2<q\leq\infty
and
1/q<r<1/2
,

\varrho_{m}({\mathbf{W}}^{r}_{q},L_{2})\ll m^{-r}(\log m)^{(d-2)(1-r)+1}.

(7.17)

8
Concluding remarks

Sampling recovery is an actively developing area of research. It has its roots in the classical problem of interpolation of functions. Traditionally, this area of research belongs to approximation theory and harmonic analysis. Recent developments in this area show its close connections with other areas of research. We demonstrate in this survey that important progress in sampling recovery was achieved by using recent deep breakthrough results in discretization and sparse approximation in Banach spaces. In turn, discretization results use fundamental results from finite dimensional geometry obtained in the papers
[
1
]
and
[
33
]
. Sparse approximation is the central topic in compressed sensing and in greedy approximation (see the books
[
16
]
,
[
49
]
, and
[
55
]
).

This survey complements the existing surveys on discretization and sampling recovery
[
4
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
31
]
, and
[
6
]
. The area is actively developing and we were not able to discuss in detail all recent results on the topic. We tried to focus on a systematic presentation of new results directly related to results obtained in our group. We did not include open problems in this survey.
We refer the reader to the surveys
[
4
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
31
]
, where a number of open problems are formulated.
Recently, there were important steps made in sampling recovery (see Steps 1-4 mentioned at the end of Section
1
)
and we discussed them in detail here. The recent progress in sampling recovery shows the direction of its development – from special problems to more and more general settings and problems. For instance, this includes steps from the univariate smoothness classes to the multivariate smoothness classes and to the multivariate classes with structural conditions.

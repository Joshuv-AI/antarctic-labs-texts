Introduction

The increasing number of implant procedures in the posterior maxilla has led to a higher incidence of complications involving the maxillary sinus. Between 25 and 40 percent of maxillary sinusitis cases are of odontogenic origin. Anatomical variations of the maxillary sinus, particularly septa and the course of the posterior superior alveolar artery, significantly influence surgical risk and graft prognosis.

Objectives

To evaluate anatomical variations of the maxillary sinus using cone beam computed tomography and to analyze their impact on surgical planning and prevention of intraoperative complications.

Material and Methods

Descriptive study based on systematic evaluation using high resolution cone beam computed tomography in patients undergoing implant surgery in the posterior maxilla. The presence and location of septa, thickness of the sinus membrane, residual bone height, course and diameter of the posterior superior alveolar artery, and root relationship to the sinus floor were analyzed. Modifications in the surgical plan derived from radiological assessment were recorded.

Results

Sinus membrane perforation is the most frequently reported intraoperative complication in the literature. Preoperative identification of septa and intraosseous arterial pathways allowed modification of the lateral window design, optimization of surgical access, and reduction of hemorrhagic risk and membrane injury.

Discussion and Conclusions

Cone beam computed tomography should be considered an essential tool in posterior maxillary surgical planning. Individualized anatomical assessment reduces complications, improves surgical safety, and promotes coordinated management between dentistry and otorhinolaryngology.

Introduction

Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw associated with antiresorptive or antiangiogenic agents represents a significant complication in older adults, particularly in patients with oncological disease or osteoporosis. Involvement of the maxilla may be associated with oroantral communication, secondary maxillary sinusitis and persistent sinonasal symptoms. An exclusively dental approach may underestimate sinus involvement.

Objectives

To describe the otolaryngological manifestations associated with medication-related maxillary osteonecrosis in older adult patients and to analyze the clinical impact of multidisciplinary management.

Material and Methods

Descriptive study including patients over 60 years of age diagnosed with medication-related maxillary osteonecrosis. Comprehensive dental assessment, otolaryngological examination with nasal endoscopy and paranasal sinus computed tomography when indicated were performed. Presence of secondary sinusitis, oroantral communication, need for complementary endoscopic surgery and clinical evolution after conservative or combined surgical treatment were analyzed, with a minimum six-month follow-up.

Results

A relevant proportion of patients showed ipsilateral maxillary sinus involvement, with endoscopic findings of chronic inflammation. Most cases demonstrated symptomatic improvement after coordinated management, with reduction of infectious complications.

Discussion

Medication-related maxillary osteonecrosis in older adults should not be considered exclusively a dental condition, as it may involve sinus extension and associated otolaryngological symptoms. Interdisciplinary coordination reduces diagnostic delay and prevents unnecessary interventions.

Conclusions

Multidisciplinary ENT–Dentistry management improves identification of sinus involvement in medication-related maxillary osteonecrosis and optimizes clinical outcomes in older adults, representing a reproducible care model.

Introduction

Increased life expectancy has led to a growing number of geriatric patients undergoing implant-supported rehabilitation, often presenting with multiple comorbidities and polypharmacy. Among the most commonly prescribed medications are selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, which may negatively influence bone metabolism and affect osseointegration and implant performance.

Objectives

To analyze the influence of long-term selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor use on the failure of osseointegrated implants, with particular focus on its relevance in geriatric patients.

Material and Methods

A retrospective cohort study was conducted in patients treated with titanium dental implants, with a minimum follow-up of 12 months. Patients under continuous selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor therapy for at least one year at the time of implant placement were included. Demographic, clinical and implant-related variables were analyzed.

Results

A significant association was observed between continuous use of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and increased implant failure. Additional factors such as smoking, diabetes and implant length also influenced implant survival.

Discussion

These findings suggest that drug-induced alterations in bone metabolism may impair bone remodeling in response to functional loading. In geriatric patients, where polypharmacy and reduced bone quality are common, this effect may have greater clinical relevance, supporting the need for more conservative implant planning strategies.

Conclusions

Long-term use of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors is associated with an increased risk of implant failure. In geriatric patients, this factor should be considered in treatment planning, particularly regarding loading protocols and long-term biomechanical management.

Objectives

To compare the conventional punch technique with the use of the diode laser in the second surgical phase of osseointegrated implants.

Material and Methods

A prospective observational study was carried out in 60 patients undergoing second-stage surgery. Two techniques were compared: punch and diode laser. Results

Objectives

To evaluate the clinical efficacy and outcome stability of a multidisciplinary protocol for the sequential diagnosis and treatment of oroantral fistula associated with odontogenic sinusitis in adult patients.

Prospective study including 48 patients with confirmed diagnosis based on clinical evaluation, nasal endoscopy and paranasal sinus computed tomography with dental sections. Coordination between dentistry and otorhinolaryngology enables precise etiological diagnosis and staged treatment, reducing therapeutic failures and unnecessary procedures.

Conclusions

The absence of a multidisciplinary protocol represents an avoidable risk factor for treatment failure. Snoring in older adults is a common clinical problem with functional, social and cardiovascular implications. Introduction and Objectives

Medication related osteonecrosis of the jaws has traditionally been associated with the use of antiresorptive drugs, such as bisphosphonates and denosumab. However, in recent years, cases linked to other non-antiresorptive pharmacological groups have been reported, particularly antiangiogenic and immunomodulatory agents. The aim of this study is to review the available evidence regarding the potential association between these drugs and the development of osteonecrosis of the jaws, as well as to analyze the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms.

Material and Methods

A literature review was conducted through searches in the Medline, Scopus, and Embase databases. Conclusions

Although medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaws remains primarily associated with antiresorptive drugs, there is growing evidence implicating other non-antiresorptive agents, especially antiangiogenic drugs. Introduction

The rehabilitation of patients with complete edentulism involves a series of procedures during treatment, with the result being a key factor in treatment success and patient satisfaction. Mechanical strength remains a determining factor in the performance of these materials, especially in geriatric patients.

Objectives

Compare the mechanical strength of 3D-printed resins with thermopolymerized polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) in complete dentures.

Material and Methods

An electronic literature search was conducted in the PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane databases for in vitro and experimental studies published in the last 5 years that evaluated flexural strength and mechanical properties of PMMA and 3D-printed materials. The following keywords were used: “3D printed,” “PMMA,” “flexural strength,” “denture base,” and “mechanical properties.” Out of 39 articles, 13 were selected after applying inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Results

The studies included shows that PMMA has better mechanical properties compared to 3D-printed materials.

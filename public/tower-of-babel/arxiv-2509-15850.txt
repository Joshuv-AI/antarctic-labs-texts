Parabolic Normalizers in Finite Coxeter Groups as Subdirect Products Thanks: The authors would like to thank their charming wives for their unwavering support during the preparation of this paper.
J. Matthew Douglass, Götz Pfeiffer, Gerhard Röhrle

Abstract
. We revisit the structure of the normalizer N W ​ ( P ) N_{W}(P) of a parabolic subgroup P P in a finite Coxeter group W W , originally described by Howlett. Building on Howlett’s Lemma, which provides canonical complements for reflection subgroups, and inspired by a recent construction of Serre for involution centralizers, we refine this understanding by interpreting N W ​ ( P ) N_{W}(P) as a subdirect product via Goursat’s Lemma. Central to our approach is a Galois connection on the lattice of parabolic subgroups, which leads to a new decomposition N W ​ ( P ) ≅ ( P × Q ) ⋊ ( ( A × B ) ⋊ C ) ​ , \displaystyle N_{W}(P)\cong(P\times Q)\rtimes((A\times B)\rtimes C)\text{,} where each subgroup reflects a structural feature of the ambient Coxeter system. This perspective yields a more symmetric description of N W ​ ( P ) N_{W}(P) , organized around naturally associated reflection subgroups on mutually orthogonal subspaces of the reflection representation of W W . Our analysis provides new conceptual clarity and includes a case-by-case classification for all irreducible finite Coxeter groups.

Complex Reflection Groups.

The broader class of complex reflection groups shares many structural
features with the real reflection groups, that is, the finite Coxeter
groups. However, complex reflection groups do not possess simple
reflections, root systems, or canonical Coxeter presentations. While
parabolic subgroups still exist and both Goursat’s Lemma and the
Galois connection remain applicable, Howlett’s Lemma does not extend
to the complex case.

As a result, obtaining a structural description of the normalizer of a
parabolic subgroup in a complex reflection group is more subtle and
requires new methods. Muraleedaran and Taylor
[
7
]
have
shown case by case that each parabolic subgroup of a complex
reflection group has a complement in its normalizer, using some ad hoc
constructions where needed. Developing a finer structural description
of the normalizer, analogous in spirit to the one presented here for
finite Coxeter groups, will be the subject of future work.

2.
Parabolic Subgroups and Howlett Complements

Here we collect some useful properties of parabolic subgroups of a
real reflection group

2.1.
Parabolic Subgroups.

Let

be a Coxeter system such that

is a finite Coxeter
group, acting as a reflection group on Euclidean space

For any subspace

The Coxeter system

is

, such that
st=ts

for all

, if it is not
reducible. By their classification

[
1
]

[

irreducible finite Coxeter system is of type
\mathbf{A}_{n}
(

),

\mathbf{B}_{n}
(

),
\mathbf{D}_{n}
(

), or of one of the

exceptional types

\mathbf{E}_{6}
,
\mathbf{E}_{7}
,
\mathbf{E}_{8}
,
\mathbf{F}_{4}
,
\mathbf{H}_{3}
,

\mathbf{H}_{4}
, or

Reflection Subgroups.

More generally, any subgroup of

generated by reflections is called

is a reflection subgroup, but a reflection subgroup is a

2.3.
Shapes.

The

\displaystyle[P]=\{P^{x}\mid x\in W\}

We write

For the exceptional types, there are explicit lists of (up to

\mathbf{A}_{n-1}

\mathbf{A}_{\lambda_{1}-1}\times\mathbf{A}_{\lambda_{2}-1}\times\dots\times\mathbf{A}_{\lambda_{k}-1}

Let

A transversal of the set

2.5.

The normalizer

of a parabolic subgroup

Proposition 2.6

(Howlett’s Lemma

)

Let

Let

For

If

Note that the action of

We call the subgroup

Using the theory of Formal Concept Analysis
[

3.1.
Galois Connections

A
Galois connection
between two posets

(A,\leq)
and
(B,\leq)
is a pair of order-reversing functions

F\colon A\to B
and
G\colon B\to A
such that

b\leq F(a)\iff a\leq G(b)
, for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
.
The functions
F
and
G
determine each other, as
F(a)
is the largest

a\leq G(b)
, and vice versa.

It follows from the definition that
F=F\circ G\circ F
and

G=G\circ F\circ G
. Thus, both
F\circ G
and

and

G\circ F
on

and

F\circ G
on

If

is a Galois connection

on

Let

and

.

Let

3.4.
Formal Concept Analysis.

Galois connections arise naturally in the theory of
formal
concept analysis
(FCA)
[
11
]
, a branch of applied lattice
theory. Here a
formal context
is a binary relation
R

between two finite sets
X
and
Y
, i.e., a subset
R
of

X\times Y
.
Associated to each subset
A\subseteq X
is its
opposite

\displaystyle{A}^{\dagger}=\{y\in Y\mid aRy\text{ for all }a\in A\}\subseteq Y\text{.}

Likewise, each subset
B\subseteq Y
has an opposite

{B}^{\dagger}\ =\{x\in X\mid xRb\text{ for all }b\in B\}\subseteq X
. It turns out that the pair of maps

F\colon 2^{X}\to 2^{Y},\,A\mapsto{A}^{\dagger}
and

G\colon 2^{Y}\to 2^{X},\,B\mapsto{B}^{\dagger}
forms a Galois connection
between the power sets of
X
and
Y
, and that each Galois connection
between those power sets arises in this way. It follows that the maps

G\circ F\colon A\mapsto{A}^{\dagger\dagger}
and

F\circ G\colon B\mapsto{B}^{\dagger\dagger}
are closure operators on the
power sets of
X
and
Y
, respectively, and that the closed subsets
of
X
or
Y
are exactly those subsets of the form

{B}^{\dagger}\subseteq X
or
{A}^{\dagger}\subseteq Y
.

A pair
(A,B)
of subsets
A\subseteq X
,
B\subseteq Y
,
is called a
formal concept
if
A
and
B
are each other’s opposites, i.e., if
(A,B)=({B}^{\dagger},{A}^{\dagger})
.
By the basic theorem of FCA
[
11
, Section 2]
the formal concepts
of a given formal context
R
form a complete lattice with respect to
the partial order

\displaystyle(A_{1},B_{1})\leq(A_{2},B_{2})\iff A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\iff B_{2}\subseteq B_{1}.

The
meet
is given by

\displaystyle(A_{1},B_{1})\land(A_{2},B_{2})=(A_{1}\cap A_{2},\ {(B_{1}\cup B_{2})}^{\dagger\dagger})

and there is a dual formula for the join. In particular, it follows
that the intersection of closed sets is closed.

In the language of graph theory, formal concepts can be described as
follows. Let
G=(X\sqcup Y,E)
be a bipartite graph with the
disjoint union of finite sets
X
and
Y
as vertices and edge set

E\subseteq X\times Y
.
Then
E
is a formal context on
X
and
Y
.
We say that vertices
x\in X
and
y\in Y
are
neighbors

if
(x,y)\in E
. For any
set of vertices
A\subseteq X
, the opposite
{A}^{\dagger}\subseteq Y
consists of
the common neighbors of all
x\in A
. Likewise, for
B\subseteq Y
,
the set
{B}^{\dagger}\subseteq X
is the set of common neighbors of all

y\in B
. A pair
(A,B)
of subsets
A\subseteq X
and

B\subseteq Y
is a formal concept if
A
and
B
are each other’s
common neighbors. The closure
{A}^{\dagger\dagger}
of
A\subseteq X
is the
largest subset of
X
with the same set
{A}^{\dagger}
of common neighbors
as
A
.

In fact, the vertex sets
X
and
Y
need not be disjoint, as the
following example illustrates.

Examples 3.5

Let

is a reflection group of type
\mathbf{B}_{3}
on
X^{\perp}

and
Q\rtimes D
is a reflection group of type
\mathbf{A}_{3}\mathbf{A}_{2}
on
X
.

4.6.
Refinement

As before, let
X=\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(P)
.
Let
Y=\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(Q)
with orthogonal complement
Y^{\perp}
, so that

V\cong Y\oplus Y^{\perp}
. Then
Y^{\perp}\leq X
and thus

\displaystyle X\cong(X\cap Y)\oplus Y^{\perp}

as
N
-modules.
Let
D_{X}
be the isomorphic projection of
D
into
\mathrm{O}(X)
,
i.e., the subgroup
H_{0}
in Lemma
4.4
. It follows that

D_{X}
is a subgroup
of the direct product
\mathrm{O}(Y^{\perp})\times\mathrm{O}(X\cap Y)
.
Hence, by Goursat’s Lemma (Proposition
4.1
),

D_{X}
is the graph of a section isomorphism from
(A_{1},A_{2})

to
(B_{1},B_{2})

for subgroups
A_{2}\unlhd A_{1}\leq\mathrm{O}(X\cap Y)

and
B_{2}\unlhd B_{1}\leq\mathrm{O}(Y^{\perp})
.
Here
A_{2}=Z_{D_{X}}(Y^{\perp})
and
B_{2}=Z_{D_{X}}(X\cap Y)
.

Overall, we now have

\displaystyle V=X^{\perp}\oplus(X\cap Y)\oplus Y^{\perp}

and the complement

\displaystyle D\leq\mathrm{O}(X^{\perp})\times\mathrm{O}(X\cap Y)\times\mathrm{O}(Y^{\perp})

acts simultaneously on the three summands of
V
above.

Notation 4.7
.

From now on, we denote by
A
the isomorphic preimage of
A_{2}
in

D
, and by
B
the isomorphic preimage of
B_{2}
in
D
. We discuss
the subgroup
A
in more detail in Section
5

and take a closer look at the subgroup
B
in Section
6
.

5.
Orthogonal Closure

As before, let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
W
with orthogonal complement
Q={P}^{\dagger}
and normalizer
N=(P\times Q)\rtimes D
.
We identify the subgroup
A
of
D
(Notation
4.7
) with the
Howlett complement of
P
in its orthogonal closure
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}={Q}^{\dagger}

(Proposition
5.1
). Then we use the subgroup
A
to describe
the normalizer of a parabolic subgroup of
W
of type
\mathbf{A}_{n}
as a
subdirect product (Example
5.3
and Proposition
5.4
).

Proposition 5.1
.

The subgroup
A
of
D
is the Howlett complement of
P
in
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
.
In particular,
A=1
if
P
is orthogonally closed, and
A=D
if
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}=W
.

Proof.

The Howlett complement of
P
in
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}

is
D\cap{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
, since
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}={Q}^{\dagger}

and
D
is the Howlett complement of
P
in the Howlett complement
of
Q
in
N
.
Since
A\cong Z_{D_{X}}(Y^{\perp})
, it follows that
A=Z_{D}(Y^{\perp})=D\cap Z_{W}(Y^{\perp})=D\cap{Q}^{\dagger}=D\cap{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The subgroup

A\leq\mathrm{O}(X^{\perp})\times\mathrm{O}(X\cap Y)\times\mathrm{O}(Y^{\perp})

acts trivially on
Y^{\perp}
. It acts faithfully on
X^{\perp}
,
permuting the simple roots of
P
. Also,
A
acts faithfully on

X\cap Y
, usually as a reflection group, with exceptions if
W

has type
\mathbf{D}_{n}
(see Proposition
7.4
) or
\mathbf{E}_{7}
(see
Table
8
).

Example 5.3
.

Let
W
be the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, i.e., a Coxeter group of
type
\mathbf{A}_{n-1}
,
n\geq 1
. Let

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})=(n^{a_{n}},\dots,1^{a_{1}})\vdash n
and let
P=W_{\lambda}=\prod_{i}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{i}}
.
Then
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
W
with label
\lambda
.
It is well known that the normalizer
N
of
P
is a direct product
of wreath products

\mathfrak{S}_{k}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}=\mathfrak{S}_{k}^{a_{k}}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}
, and hence
that the Howlett complement of
P
in
N
is
\prod_{k}\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}
, a direct
product of symmetric groups. Here, the
k
-th factor
\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}

is generated by involutions
x_{i}
, as follows. For
each
i
such that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=k
, set

u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j}
and define a permutation

x_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
by

\displaystyle v.{x_{i}}=\begin{cases}v+k,&u_{i}<v\leq u_{i}+k,\\
v-k,&u_{i}+k<v\leq u_{i}+2k,\\
v,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Thus, e.g,
x_{1}=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2&3&4&5&6\\
4&5&6&1&2&3\end{smallmatrix})
if
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=3
.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
P
is a parabolic subgroup with label

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})=(n^{a_{n}},\dots,1^{a_{1}})
of a Coxeter group

W
of type
\mathbf{A}_{n-1}
then its Howlett complement in
W
is isomorphic to
the direct product
\prod_{k}\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}
.
More precisely, with the above notation and choice of
P
, we have

N=(P\rtimes A)\times Q
, where

•

Q=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=1\rangle\cong\mathfrak{S}_{a_{1}}
, and

•

A=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>1\rangle\cong\prod_{k>1}\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}
.

Moreover,
A
acts as reflection group on
X\cap Y
, and the subgroup

\displaystyle A^{\prime}=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=2\rangle\leq A

acts as a
reflection group on

in such a way that

is a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}\mathbf{A}_{2}^{a_{3}}\dotsm\mathbf{A}_{n-1}^{a_{n}}
on

.

Proof.

The orthogonal complement
Q
of
P
is the symmetric group

\mathfrak{S}_{a_{1}}=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=1\rangle
on the
fixed points of
P
. Note that
a_{1}\neq n-1
. The orthogonal closure

{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
of

is a symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n-a_{1}}
on all the
moved points of
P
. The Howlett complement
A
of
P
in

N\cap{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
is the group

\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>1\rangle
, isomorphic to the
direct product
\prod_{k>1}\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}
. Note that the reflection
subgroup
P\times Q=W_{\lambda}\times\mathfrak{S}_{a_{1}}
is actually a
parabolic subgroup of
W
.

Each of the involutions
x_{i}
in the factor
\mathfrak{S}_{a_{k}}
has degree

k
and the
(-1)
-eigenspace of
x_{i}
in

intersects the fixed point space

of

in a

1
-dimensional subspace. Thus, any
x_{i}\in Q
acts naturally as a
reflection on
Y^{\perp}
and any
x_{i}\in A
acts as a reflection
on

. In addition, each

of degree

also
acts as a reflection on

, affording a reflection subgroup

\mathfrak{S}_{2}^{a_{2}}\rtimes A^{\prime}
of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
.
∎

6.
Parabolic Closure

We identify the subgroup
B
of the subgroup
D
of the normalizer

N=(P\times Q)\rtimes D
of the parabolic subgroup
P
in
W
as
the Howlett complement of
P\times Q
in its parabolic closure
(Proposition
6.3
). Then we use the subgroups
A
and
B
to
describe the normalizer of a parabolic subgroup of
W(\mathbf{B}_{n})
as a
subdirect product (Example
6.4
and Proposition
6.5
).

Definition 6.1
.

The
parabolic closure
of a subgroup
U
of
W
is

\displaystyle\overline{U}=Z_{W}(\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(U))\text{,}

the smallest parabolic subgroup of
W
containing
U
.

6.2.

The product
PQ
is a reflection subgroup of
W
, but
not necessarily a parabolic subgroup. Its parabolic closure is

\overline{PQ}=Z_{W}(\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(PQ))
. Since

\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(PQ)=\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(P)\cap\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(Q)=X\cap Y
, we have

\overline{PQ}=Z_{W}(X\cap Y)
.

Proposition 6.3
.

The subgroup

B
of
D
is the Howlett complement of
PQ
in
\overline{PQ}
.
In particular,
B=1
if
PQ
is a parabolic subgroup,
and
B=D
if
\overline{PQ}=W
.

Proof.

Since
D
is the Howlett complement of

PQ
in
W
,
the Howlett complement of
PQ
in
\overline{PQ}

is
D\cap\overline{PQ}
.
But
from
B\cong Z_{D_{X}}(X\cap Y)
(as in Section
4.6
) it follows that

B=Z_{D}(X\cap Y)=D\cap Z_{W}(X\cap Y)=D\cap\overline{PQ}
.
∎

Example 6.4
.

Let
W
be a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, represented as
signed permutations on the points
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p})=(m^{a_{m}},\dots,2^{a_{2}},1^{a_{1}})\vdash m\leq n
and set
l=m-n
. Let

P=W_{\lambda}=W(\mathbf{B}_{l})\times\prod_{i}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{i}}
, where
for each part
\lambda_{i}
, the factor
\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{i}}
acts on the points

\{u_{i}+1,\dots,u_{i}+\lambda_{i}\}
, with

u_{i}=l+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j}
. Then
P
is a standard
parabolic subgroup of
W
with label
\lambda
. The Howlett
complement of
P
in
W
is a direct product of Coxeter groups

W(\mathbf{B}_{a_{k}})
, as follows.

As in Example
5.3
, we define involutions

x_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\subseteq W(\mathbf{B}_{n})
whenever

\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}
. Moreover, for each part
\lambda_{i}

(with
u_{i}=l+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{j}
), we define an element

y_{i}\in W(\mathbf{B}_{n})
as

\displaystyle v.{y_{i}}=\begin{cases}-(u_{i+1}+1-v),&u_{i}<v\leq u_{i+1},\\
v,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Thus, e.g.,
y_{1}=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2&3&4&5\\
-5&-4&-3&-2&-1\end{smallmatrix})
if
n=m
and
\lambda_{1}=5
.

Proposition 6.5
.

If
P
is a parabolic subgroup with label
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p})=(m^{a_{m}},\dots,1^{a_{1}})
of a Coxeter group
W
of type
\mathbf{B}_{n}
then its Howlett complement in

is isomorphic to a Coxeter group
\prod_{k}W(\mathbf{B}_{a_{k}})
.
More precisely, with the above notation and choice of

, we have

N=(P\times Q)\rtimes(A\times B)
, where

•

Q=\langle x_{i},\,y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=1,\,\lambda_{j}\leq 2\rangle
is of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{1}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
,

•

is of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{B}_{a_{m}}
, and

•

B=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=2\rangle\cong\mathfrak{S}_{a_{2}}
.

Moreover,
A
acts as a reflection group on
X\cap Y
, while
B

acts as a reflection group on
Y^{\perp}
in such a way that
Q\rtimes B

is a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{1}}\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
on
Y^{\perp}
, and
on
X^{\perp}
in such a way that

is a Coxeter group
of type
\mathbf{B}_{l}\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}\mathbf{A}_{2}^{a_{3}}\dotsm\mathbf{A}_{m-1}^{a_{m}}
on

.

Proof.

The orthogonal complement of
P
is the subgroup

Q=\langle x_{i},\,y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=1,\,\lambda_{j}\leq 2\rangle
of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{1}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
. The orthogonal closure of

is
the unique standard parabolic subgroup
of type

\mathbf{B}_{n-a_{1}-2a_{2}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
containing

. The Howlett complement
A

of
P
in
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
is the subgroup

\langle x_{i},y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2,\,\lambda_{j}>2\rangle

which is
isomorphic to a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{B}_{a_{m}}
.
The parabolic closure
\overline{PQ}
of the reflection subgroup
PQ

is the unique parabolic subgroup isomorphic to
W(\mathbf{B}_{l+a_{1}+2a_{2}})\prod_{k>2}\mathfrak{S}_{k}^{a_{k}}
containing
P
and
Q
.
The Howlett complement of
PQ
in
\overline{PQ}
is

B=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=2\rangle\cong\mathfrak{S}_{a_{2}}
.

As in the proof of Proposition
5.4
, the
k
-dimensional
(-1)
-eigenspace of an
element
x_{i}
intersects the fixed point space
X
of
P
in
V
in a
1
-dimensional subspace. Any
x_{i}\in Q
has degree

and acts on

.
Any
x_{i}\in B
has degree

and acts on both

and

in such a way that

is a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{1}}\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
on

, and affording a reflection subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{2}^{a_{2}}\rtimes B
of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
on

.

x_{i}\in A
has degree

.

(-1)

.

on

.

7.
Closure

In general, by the application of Goursat’s Lemma in
Section
4.6
,
D/(A\times B)=C
. We have seen in the
previous sections that
C
is trivial if
W
is of type
\mathbf{A}_{n}
or

\mathbf{B}_{n}
. Here we describe the normalizer of a parabolic subgroup of

W(\mathbf{D}_{n})
and subsequently list all cases where
C
is not trivial.

Example 7.1
.

Let
W
be of type
\mathbf{D}_{n}
, represented as a normal subgroup of
index
2
in a Coxeter group
W^{\prime}
of type
\mathbf{B}_{n}
acting as signed
permutations on
\{1,\dots,n\}
. We distinguish three kinds of
classes of parabolic subgroups in terms of their labels
\lambda
,
and discuss them one by one:

(i)

\lambda
is an even partition of
n
,

(ii)

\lambda
is a partition of
m<n-1
,

(iii)

\lambda
is a partition of
n
with at least one odd part.

As before in Examples
5.3
and
6.4
, we have elements

x_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\subseteq W(\mathbf{D}_{n})
and
y_{i}\in W(\mathbf{B}_{n})
, where

y_{i}\in W(\mathbf{D}_{n})
if and only if
\lambda_{i}
is even.

(i) Let
\lambda=(n^{a_{n}},\dots,1^{a_{1}})
be an even partition
of
n
, i.e.,
a_{k}=0
unless
k
is even. Let

P=W_{\lambda^{+}}=\prod_{i}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{i}}
. Then
P
is a
standard parabolic subgroup of
W
with label
\lambda^{+}
. The
Howlett complement of
P
is a direct product of Coxeter groups

W(\mathbf{B}_{a_{k}})
, as follows. (The results for the class labelled

\lambda^{-}
are similar and follow by applying a graph automorphism
to
W
.)

Proposition 7.2
.

If
P
is a parabolic subgroup with label

\lambda^{+}
or
\lambda^{-}

of a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{D}_{n}
, where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p})=(n^{a_{n}},\dots,1^{a_{1}})
is an even partition of
n
, then its Howlett complement in
W
is isomorphic to a Coxeter group
\prod_{k}W(\mathbf{B}_{a_{k}})
.
More precisely, with the above notation and choice of
P
, we have

N=(P\times Q)\rtimes(A\times B)
, where

•

Q=\langle y_{j}\mid\lambda_{j}=2\rangle
is of type
\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
,

•

A=\langle x_{i},y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2,\,\lambda_{j}>2\rangle
is of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{4}}\dotsm\mathbf{B}_{a_{n}}
, and

•

B=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=2\rangle=\mathfrak{S}_{a_{2}}
.

Moreover,
A
acts as a reflection group on
X\cap Y
, while
B

acts as a reflection group on
Y^{\perp}
in such a way that
Q\rtimes B

is a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
on
Y^{\perp}
, and
on
X^{\perp}
in such a way that
P\rtimes B
is a Coxeter group
of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}\mathbf{A}_{3}^{a_{4}}\dotsm\mathbf{A}_{n-1}^{a_{n}}
on
X^{\perp}
.

Proof.

The orthogonal complement of
P
is the subgroup

Q=\langle y_{j}\mid\lambda_{j}=2\rangle
of type
\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
.
The orthogonal closure of
P
is the unique standard parabolic
subgroup
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
of type

\mathbf{D}_{n-2a_{2}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
containing
P
. The Howlett complement
A
of

P
in
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
is the subgroup

\langle x_{i},y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2,\,\lambda_{j}>2\rangle
which is isomorphic to a Coxeter group of type

\mathbf{B}_{a_{4}}\dotsm\mathbf{B}_{a_{n}}
.
The parabolic closure
\overline{PQ}
of the reflection subgroup

PQ
is the unique parabolic subgroup isomorphic to

W(\mathbf{D}_{2a_{2}})\prod_{k>2}\mathfrak{S}_{k}^{a_{k}}
containing
P
and
Q
.
The Howlett complement
B
of
PQ
in
\overline{PQ}
is
the subgroup
B=\langle x_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=2\rangle\cong\mathfrak{S}_{a_{2}}
.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
6.5
,
Q\rtimes B
is a Coxeter group of type

\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
on
Y^{\perp}
. And
B
also acts on
X^{\perp}
,
affording a reflection group
\mathfrak{S}_{2}^{a_{2}}\rtimes B
of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{2}}
.
Moreover,
A
acts as a reflection group of type

\mathbf{B}_{a_{4}}\dotsm\mathbf{B}_{a_{n}}
on
X\cap Y
.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

m<n-1

is
isomorphic to a Coxeter group
\prod_{k}W(\mathbf{B}_{a_{k}})

precisely, with the above notation and choice of

, where the details depend

\bullet

(b)

\bullet

Q=\langle x_{i},\,y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}=1,\,\lambda_{j}\leq 2\rangle

\mathbf{D}_{a_{1}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}

\bullet

A=\langle x_{i},y_{j},z_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2,\,\lambda_{j}>2\rangle

\mathbf{B}_{a_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{B}_{a_{m}}

\bullet

.

\mathbf{D}_{a_{1}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}

P

{P}^{\dagger\dagger}

\mathbf{D}_{n-a_{1}-2a_{2}}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{a_{2}}
containing
P
.

A

of
P

{P}^{\dagger\dagger}

\langle x_{i},y_{j},z_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2,\,\lambda_{j}>2\rangle

isomorphic to a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{B}_{a_{3}}\cdots\mathbf{B}_{a_{m}}
.

\overline{PQ}

and

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
W
be a Coxeter group of type
\mathbf{D}_{n}
.
Let

be a parabolic subgroup of

with label
\lambda
, where

n
that is not even.

2
subgroup of a Coxeter group
\prod_{k}W(\mathbf{B}_{a_{k}})

2

\bullet

, and

\bullet

C=1

(b)

\lambda

\displaystyle A_{0}=\langle x_{i},y_{j}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2\text{ even},\,\lambda_{j}>2\text{ even}\rangle

\displaystyle A_{1}=\langle x_{i},y_{i}^{\prime},z_{pq}^{\prime}\mid\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}>2\text{ odd},\,\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{q}>2\text{ odd}\rangle

\bullet

8.1.
How to Read the Tables.

Here
the conjugacy classes of parabolic subgroups are
grouped by their orthogonal closure: the orthogonally closed parabolic subgroups
are exactly the bottom entries in each group. Pairs of such groups
correspond to orthogonal complements, concepts are separated by double lines.
The columns contain the following information.

•

The first column gives the position of the class in the complete
list of conjugacy classes of parabolic subgroups of
W

[
4
, Appendix A]
. An asterisk
(
*
) indicates that this parabolic’s normalizer is an involution
centralizer.

•

Column
P
gives the Coxeter type of a parabolic subgroup
P
in
this class, and labelling partition
\lambda
if
W
is of classical
type. Here
\varnothing
stands for the trivial group. Different
classes with the same type are distinguished by primes (
′
) and double primes (
′′
).

•

Column
Q
gives the class of the orthogonal complement of
P

in terms of its position (as in column
1
). Note that all classes in the same group
have the same entry in column
Q
.

•

The size of the complement
D
is given in column
\lvert D\rvert
.

•

Column
\overline{PQ}
gives the class of the parabolic closure

\overline{PQ}
, again in terms of its position (as in column
1
), unless

\overline{PQ}=W
. The position is given in parentheses if

\overline{PQ}=PQ
.

•

Columns
A
,
B
and
C
contain the Coxeter type of the groups

A
,
B
and
C
, with occasional footnotes on their action as
reflection groups.

•

In columns
X^{\perp}
,
X\cap Y
and
Y^{\perp}
, the action
of
N_{W}(P)
as a reflection group on these spaces is described in
the form of a Coxeter diagram, with white nodes (
\circ
) for
reflections in
A
,
B
or
C
, and black nodes (
\bullet
) for
reflections in
P
or
Q
. Left superscripts are used to indicate
when a group does not act as a reflection group, it then usually
acts as a permutation group on the Coxeter diagram.

8.2.
Idiosyncrasies.

In most cases, the reflection action of
D
, or its subgroups
A
,

B
, and
C
, suggests a descriptive name as entry in the table.
In case of a conflict, we have used the action on
X\cap Y
as a name.
The cases which need additional explanations are marked
with the symbols
{\heartsuit}
,
{\diamondsuit}
,
{\clubsuit}
,
{\spadesuit}
. Specifically:

•

\mathbf{A}_{1}^{\heartsuit}
stands for a group of order
2
that does
not at act as a reflection group, on either of the spaces

X^{\perp}
,
X\cap Y
or
Y^{\perp}
— it acts as scalar
-1
on
X\cap Y
.

•

{{\mathbf{A}}_{1}^{2}}^{\clubsuit}
acts as the same Coxeter type in
different ways on two spaces — only one element acts as a
reflection on both
X^{\perp}
and
X\cap Y
.

•

{\mathbf{G}}_{2}^{\spadesuit}
acts as
{\mathbf{G}}_{2}
on
X\cap Y
and
as
{\mathbf{A}}_{2}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}
on
X^{\perp}
.

•

{\mathbf{A}}_{1}^{\diamondsuit}
does not by itself act as a
reflection group on
X^{\perp}
— but the group
AB
of type

\mathbf{A}_{1}^{2}
does.

•

{\mathbf{B}}_{2}^{\spadesuit}
acts as a graph automorphism and
reflections — only its normal subgroup
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}^{2}
acts as a
reflection group.

•

{\mathbf{B}}_{2}^{\heartsuit}
acts as a group
A
of graph
automorphisms on
X^{\perp}
.

9.
Observations

Here we collect some observations that are made in hindsight,
mostly case-by-case, and study some special cases like
involution centralizers.

9.1.
Special case
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}=W
.

If the orthogonal closure of
P
is
W
then obviously
Q=1
.
Therefore
Y^{\perp}=0
,
D=A
and
B=C=1
. Case-by-case it
turns out that, for any parabolic subgroup
P
, the orthogonal closure
of
\overline{PQ}
is
W
. It would be interesting to see a general
argument for this property.

9.2.
Special case
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}=P
.

If
P
is orthogonally closed
then
A=1
, by Proposition
5.1
. It also follows that
D

acts faithfully as graph automorphisms on both
P
and
Q
. A
case-by-case analysis shows that moreover
C=1
and thus
D=B

acts trivially on
X\cap Y
. A general argument for this property
would be useful.

9.3.
Central Longest Element.

If the longest element
w_{0}
is central in
W
then
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}=P

implies that
PQ
is a maximal rank reflection subgroup of
W
, i.e.,
that
\overline{PQ}=W
. It would be helpful to have a general
argument for this property.

9.4.
Involution Centralizers.

If
u\in W
is an involution, i.e.,
u^{2}=1
, then
its fixed point space
X=\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(u)
is in the lattice.
This means that
X=\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(P)
for the parabolic subgroup

P=Z_{W}(X)
of
W
.
Hence
its centralizer is
the normalizer of a parabolic subgroup,
C_{W}(u)=N_{W}(P)
.
Serre
[
9
]
has recently studied involution centralizers as
subdirect products in the light of Goursat’s Lemma.

The involution centralizers in the tables of parabolic subgroups are marked
with an asterisk (
*
). The following properties can be observed, case-by-case.

•

If
-1\in W
then each involution centralizer is
orthogonally closed. Moreover, if the parabolic subgroup
P
of
W
is
an involution centralizer then its orthogonal complement
Q
is also
an involution centralizer. (The latter claim follows in general
from the fact that if
u
is an involution then so is

-u\in W
, and if
P=Z_{W}(\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(u))
then
Q=Z_{W}(\mathrm{Fix}_{V}(-u))
.)

•

In fact, if
W
is of type
\mathbf{B}_{n}
then a parabolic subgroup
P
is an involution centralizer
if and only if

P
is orthogonally closed.

•

In general, in each grouping of parabolic subgroups
P
with the
same orthogonal closure
{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
, there is
at most one

involution centralizer.

•

A possibly related fact is that if
P

is an involution centralizer then the longest element of
P
is
conjugate in
W
to the longest element of its orthogonal closure

{P}^{\dagger\dagger}
.

Again it would be desirable to see case-free justifications of the
listed properties of involution centralizers.

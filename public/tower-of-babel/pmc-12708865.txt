Heterotic potential and combining ability for yield and quality attributes in slicing cucumber (Cucumis sativus L.)
Authors: Rajinder Kumar Dhall, Shreedhari Narasimha Hegde, Prakash Singathiya, Pooja Manchanda
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present investigation was conducted to evaluate the heterotic potential and combining ability of cucumber genotypes for the development of early maturing, high yielding and better quality hybrids suitable for tropical environment. The experimental material consisted of eight monoecious parental lines, which were intercrossed using a half-diallel mating design (excluding reciprocals), resulting in 28 F₁ hybrids. A commercial hybrid, ‘Nazia’, was included as a standard check. These 36 genotypes (parents and hybrids) were evaluated under tropical conditions for yield and quality-related traits. Analysis of variance revealed highly significant differences (p ≤ 0.01) among genotypes for all the studied traits, indicating the presence of substantial genetic variability. The significant mean squares due to general and specific combining ability (GCA) and specific combining ability (SCA) suggest the involvement of both additive and non-additive gene actions in the inheritance of the traits evaluated. Among the parents, line D-20 emerged as the best general combiner for key traits, including node number for the first female flower appearance, fruit yield per plant, fruit number per plant and sex ratio. Punjab Naveen showed superior GCA for fruit diameter and ascorbic acid content. Based on heterosis, mean performance, and SCA effects, hybrids D-20 × Pant Kheera-1, D-20 × JLG and D-20 × Punjab Naveen demonstrated superior performance for yield and quality traits. Additionally, D-20 × Punjab Naveen and D-20 × Pusa Uday produced female flowers at lower nodes, a desirable trait for early yield. Notably, the combinations Punjab Naveen × Pant Kheera-1 and CMVR-1 × JLG were the earliest to mature. Considering overall performance and combining ability, hybrids D-20 × Pant Kheera-1 and D-20 × Punjab Naveen are promising candidates for commercial cultivation in the summer season, subject to validation through multi-location trials. Introduction

Cucumber (Cucumis sativus L.) is the most important vegetable crop belonging to the family Cucurbitaceae and is widely grown throughout the tropical and subtropical regions of the world. It is the fourth most important vegetable crop after tomato, cabbage, and onion1 and second most important cucurbit crop after watermelon. It is the second most extensively cultivated cucurbitaceous crop globally, following watermelon, in terms of both domestic trade and export potential2. According to FAOSTAT3, global cucumber production reached approximately 94.17 million metric tons in 2022, marking a 2.3% increase compared to 92.62 million metric tons in 2021. China continues to dominate global cucumber production, contributing over 75% of the total output with 77.26 million metric tons, followed by Turkey, Russia, Mexico and Uzbekistan. India was ranked 22nd in the global cucumber production. Domestically, India produced an estimated 1.665 million metric tons of cucumber from an area of 118,000 hectares. In Punjab, cucumber cultivation is concentrated over 3,990 hectares, yielding approximately 108.71 thousand metric tons4.

Its fruits are used as cooling food during summer mainly as salad and they are rich in vitamins and minerals, like 0.4% protein, 2.5% carbohydrates, 1.5 mg iron, 21 mg calcium, 2 mg vitamin C, iron 0.3 mg, niacin 0.2 mg, thiamin 0.02 mg per 100 g of fresh weight5,6. It is a multipurpose crop as its fruits and seeds are equipped with astringent and antipyretic properties, enables the elimination of uric acid and curing arthritis. Besides, the presence of high-water content makes it diuretic and crucial for the removal of accumulated old waste material and chemical toxins providing cleansing action within the body.

Although India has a vast genetic diversity of cucumber, yet it has not been exploited for crop improvement. The reason beholds the fact that the commercial cucumber varieties possess a narrow genetic base with 3–8% polymorphism7 and its inherent fruiting habits results in suppressed yield by low fruit production which ultimately leads to low yield8. Therefore, the selection of accurate parental lines for hybrid development is a tedious task among vegetable breeders. The selection of parents on the basis of mean performance does not necessarily lead to desirable results9. Therefore, breeding of high yielding varieties for crop improvement mainly depends on the manifestation of heterosis for yield and its attributed traits employing diverse superior parental lines. Heterosis mainly occurs due to the presence of genetically dissimilar diverse parents following hybridization results in the production of a superior hybrid with a broad genetic base and improved traits viz. high productivity, earliness with uniformity and quality10. The presence of low inbreeding depression, and more seeds per fruit also enables the profitable utilization of heterosis. Heterosis marked the function of only specific crosses so there was need to conduct the combining ability analysis for obtaining useful information regarding the selection of parents for better hybrid performance depending upon the nature and number of genetic parameters. The selected parental line with a high general combining ability (GCA) value and their F1 should express high specific combining ability (SCA) utilized for the improvement of a desirable character. The half-diallel mating design (reciprocals were excluded) suggested by Griffing11 was used to assess the combining ability among parents for obtaining the best crosses. The combination of heterosis and combining ability study was effective in delineating the nature of gene action. In light of the growing demand for high-yielding and quality cucumber cultivars suitable for open field cultivation, the present study was undertaken with the primary objective of identifying superior hybrid combinations that can be potentially released for commercial cultivation in the region. This goal necessitates a comprehensive evaluation of the heterotic potential and SCA of various cross combinations. Accordingly, the study aimed to assess the magnitude of heterosis and estimate SCA effects in 28 F₁ hybrids developed through a half-diallel mating design. The findings are intended to facilitate the identification and selection of promising hybrid combinations with desirable yield and quality traits for large-scale cultivation.

Materials and methods

Location and Climatic conditions

The current study was conducted during the cropping seasons of 2018–2019 and 2019–2020 at the Vegetable Research Farm and Biochemistry Laboratory, Department of Vegetable Science, Punjab Agricultural University (PAU), Ludhiana, Punjab, India. The experimental location is situated at an altitude of 247 m above mean sea level, geographically positioned at 30.91°N latitude and 75.85°E longitude. The site is characterized by a subtropical climate, with hot, dry summers extending from April to June, often accompanied by intense desiccating winds. Winters span from December to January and are typically cold, occasionally experiencing ground frost. The area has an average annual precipitation of around 700 mm, with the majority of rainfall occurring during the monsoon period from July to mid-September.

It included 28 F1 hybrids which were developed by crossing 8 monoecious cucumber lines in diallel fashion (excluding reciprocals), plus one commercial check hybrid (Nazia). Details of the parental lines and the check hybrid, along with their respective sources, are presented in Table 1.

Development of F1 hybrids

The crosses were made among eight parents using a half-diallel mating design (excluding reciprocals) during first year (February-May, 2018). Seeds of parents were sown in the pro trays containing cocopeat: perlite: vermiculite in 3:1:1 ratio during first week of February 2018. The seedlings were transplanted in the field in first week of March 2018 to produce different combinations of F1 in half diallel fashion by hand pollination. The parental lines were self-pollinated for seed production in the subsequent season. Mature fruits from both parents and hybrids were harvested, seeds extracted, and sun-dried for further use.

Mainland Preparation and transplantation

A total of 28 cucumber hybrids, eight parental lines, and one commercial check hybrid (‘Nazia’, East West Seed India Pvt. Ltd.) were sown during the second week of July 2018 in pro trays filled with a growth medium composed of cocopeat, vermiculite, and perlite in a 3:1:1 ratio. Transplanting was done at the 2–3 leaf stage in the last week of July 2018 under open field conditions. Transplanting was carried out on both sides of raised beds measuring 2.0 m in width, with a plant spacing of 45 cm. For each genotype, ten plants were transplanted following a Completely Randomized Block Design (CRBD) with three replications. Standard cultural practices recommended by Punjab Agricultural University; Ludhiana were strictly followed throughout the crop cycle12.

Fertilizer management and irrigation

The field was fertilized with a basal dose of 100 kg nitrogen, 50 kg phosphorus (P₂O₅), and 50 kg potassium (K₂O) per hectare. Inorganic fertilizers were applied in two parallel bands positioned 15 cm from the bed edge, with 30 cm wide furrows maintained between 2.0 m wide beds. Irrigation was initiated immediately after transplanting using furrow method, followed by subsequent irrigations every 2–3 days, adjusted to 4–6 days intervals depending on temperature, totaling 10–12 irrigations throughout the crop cycle. An efficient drainage arrangement was maintained to promptly remove excess rainwater.

Weed and pest management

During the early seedling stage, infestations of red pumpkin beetles were controlled using Decis 2.8 EC (Deltamethrin 2.8%) at 1.5 mL per litre of water (Kumari et al., 2024). Fusarium wilt was managed by soil drenching with Ridomil Gold (4% Metalaxyl-M + 64% Mancozeb WP) at 2 g/L. One-month post-transplanting, manual weeding was performed followed by application of nitrogen fertilizer at 20 kg per acre in the furrows, with earthing up carried out one day later. Downy mildew was suppressed through foliar sprays of Ridomil Gold at 2 g/L, while powdery mildew was controlled by spraying Karathane (Dinocap 48% EC) at 1 g/L. Aphid populations were managed by spraying Rogor (Dimethoate 30% EC) at 2 mL/L water.

Data collection

The observations were recorded on phenotypic characters, viz. node at which first female flower appears, fruit length (cm), fruit diameter (cm), total yield per plant (kg), fruit weight (g), days to first fruit harvest, number of fruits/plant and sex ratio. Five randomly selected fruits from each replication were used for estimating these parameters. Yield per plant was obtained from each five randomly chosen vines in each replication. Besides, Total Soluble Solids (TSS) was determined by using digital refractometer. Ascorbic acid content was determined by crushing 30 g of fruit tissue in 50 mL of metaphosphoric acid (MPA) solution. The extract was filtered and titrated against a standard 2,6-dichlorophenol indophenol (DCPIP) dye. Ascorbic acid concentration was calculated in mg per 100 g fresh weight using the formula: Ascorbic acid = (V × S × D)/(W × A) × 100, where V is the volume of dye consumed, S is the standardization factor of the dye, D is the dye factor, W is the weight of the sample, and A is the aliquot volume used for estimation.

Estimation of genetic parameters

Genetic parameters, including the additive genetic variance (D), dominance variance (H₁), non-additive variance correlated with gene distribution (H₂), covariance between additive and non-additive effects (F), overall dominance effects, and the accuracy of genetic parameter estimates (h²), were computed following the methodology of Hayman15. The corresponding estimates and ratios were derived as per established formulas.

(H1/D) 1/2 This parameter quantifies the average degree of dominance at each locus. A value of 1 indicates complete dominance, values greater than 1 signify over-dominance, and values less than 1 correspond to partial dominance.

H2/4H1: This ratio estimates the product uvuvuv, where uuu and vvv represent the frequencies of positive and negative alleles respectively, with u + v = 1. The theoretical maximum of 0.25 (when u = v = 0.5) indicates symmetrical distribution of alleles at loci exhibiting dominance. Values deviating from 0.25 suggest asymmetry in allele distribution.

(4DH1)1/2+F/(4DH1)1/2-F: This ratio reflects the relative frequency of dominant to recessive alleles across the parental lines. A value close to unity implies an approximately equal number of dominant and recessive alleles, consistent with u= v = 0.5.

h2/H2: Represents the effective number of gene factors exhibiting dominance that control the trait, effectively indicating the number of gene blocks contributing dominance effects.

Statistical analysis

The analysis of variance (ANOVA) was done on the collected data from the parents and hybrids for the detection genotypic differences. Completely Randomized Block Design (CRBD) was used to ascertain the differences among parents, hybrids and parents versus hybrids. The significant difference among crosses was calculated. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was carried out using the BMM software developed by Punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana.

Mean performance and general combining ability (GCA) effects of parents

Mean performance and general combining ability (GCA) effects of all eight inbred lines of monoecious cucumber were shown in Fig. 1 & Supplementary Table S1. Fruit length is a key yield-contributing trait in cucumber, significantly influenced by the time of harvest after fruit set. Typically, a fruit length of 15–20 cm is considered most desirable by consumers. The fruit length among parental lines ranged from 14.35 to 20.32 cm. The parent ‘CMVR-1’ exhibited the maximum fruit length (20.32 cm), significantly surpassing all other parental lines, followed by ‘Pant Kheera-1’ (17.85 cm) and ‘Swarna Sheetal’ (17.06 cm). Among the parental lines, ‘CMVR-1’ and ‘Pant Kheera-1’ exhibited significant positive GCA effects for fruit length, ranging from − 0.97 to 2.50, with the highest positive GCA effect in CMVR-1 (2.50) (Fig. 1, Supplementary Table S1). Fruit diameter, besides contributing to yield, also plays a key role in determining fruit appearance, which directly influences consumer acceptability. Slender fruits are generally preferred by consumers due to their aesthetic appeal. The parental lines exhibited fruit diameter ranging from 3.97 to 5.43 cm, with the highest value observed in ‘Punjab Naveen’ (5.43 cm), followed by ‘D-20’ (5.12 cm). GCA effects for fruit diameter ranged between − 0.34 (JLG) and 0.27 (Punjab Naveen). These results indicate that ‘CMVR-1’ and ‘Punjab Naveen’ were promising general combiners for fruit length and diameter.

General combining ability (GCA) effects and mean of parents for different characters.

Fruit weight is a critical yield-contributing trait in cucumber; however, excessively high fruit weight may not meet consumer preferences, which often favour medium-sized fruits for ease of handling and consumption. The fruit weight among parental lines ranged from 161.00 to 219.67 g (Supplementary Table S1). The highest fruit weight was recorded in ‘Pant Kheera-1’ (219.67 g), which was significantly higher than all other lines, followed by ‘Rashpal’ (200.00 g). Sex ratio among the parental lines ranged from 0.23 to 0.43 and genotype D-20 showed highest sex ratio of 0.43. Notably, four parental lines namely D-20, Rashpal, Pant Kheera-1 and Japanese Long Green (JLG) exhibited significantly positive GCA effects for multiple traits including fruit weight, fruit diameter and sex ratio.

Early fruit bearing is a desirable trait in cucumber as it enhances marketability, allowing farmers to obtain premium prices due to early market entry. Days to first fruit harvest among the parental lines ranged from 52.33 to 61.67 days. The earliest fruit harvest was recorded in D-20 (52.33 days), followed by Pusa Uday (55.00 days). The GCA effects for days to first fruit harvest ranged from − 1.59 (Pusa Uday) to 0.94 (Japanese Long Green - JLG). Four parents viz. Rashpal, CMVR-1, Pant Kheera-1, and JLG exhibited positive GCA effects, suggesting their potential to contribute to early fruit bearing in hybrid combinations. The node of first female flower appearance is another important indicator of earliness. Among the parents, this trait ranged from 9.38 to 12.43 nodes, with Punjab Naveen (9.38 nodes) and Pusa Uday (9.53 nodes) producing the first female flower at the lowest nodes. The GCA effects for this trait ranged from − 1.03 (D-20) to 0.63 (JLG). Four parents viz. Swarna Sheetal, CMVR-1, Pant Kheera-1, and JLG showed positive GCA effects for early female flowering. Overall, Pusa Uday and D-20 emerged as the best general combiners for earliness traits, based on their performance for both early fruit harvest and early female flower initiation.

The number of fruits per plant is a critical determinant of total yield and constitutes one of the primary traits targeted in cucumber breeding programs. Among the parental lines, the mean number of fruits per plant was ranged from 4.17 to 7.00, with the highest number recorded in ‘Swarna Sheetal’ (7.00), followed by ‘Pusa Uday’ (6.83). The GCA effects for this trait varied from − 0.46 (Swarna Sheetal) to 0.37 (D-20). Notably, D-20, Rashpal and Japanese Long Green (JLG) exhibited significant positive GCA effects for both sex ratio and fruit number per plant, highlighting their potential as promising parents for enhancing fruit production. Fruit yield per plant, being a culmination of several yield-related components, remains a key focus in cucumber crop improvement. The average fruit yield among parental lines ranged from 0.90 to 1.22 kg. The highest yield was recorded in ‘Pusa Uday’ (1.22 kg), followed by ‘D-20’ (1.18 kg). The GCA effects for fruit yield ranged from − 0.12 to 0.08, with D-20, Pusa Uday, Swarna Sheetal and Pant Kheera-1 showing favorable GCA effects, suggesting their potential in contributing positively to yield improvement. Collectively, D-20, Swarna Sheetal, and Pusa Uday emerged as superior general combiners for multiple yield-contributing traits, and can be effectively utilized in hybridization programs aimed at enhancing cucumber productivity.

Total soluble solids (TSS) play a vital role in determining the taste and quality of cucumber fruits, as higher TSS levels contribute to a sweeter flavor profile. The TSS content among the parental lines varied from 2.87 to 3.63°B. The highest TSS was recorded in ‘Punjab Naveen’ (3.63°B), followed by ‘Pusa Uday’ (3.33°B). The GCA effects for TSS ranged from − 0.07 to 0.18, with Punjab Naveen showing the highest and significant positive GCA value (0.18), indicating its strong potential as a parent for enhancing sweetness. Although cucumber is not considered a rich source of ascorbic acid, its presence in fruits is beneficial since cucumber is consumed raw, eliminating the risk of nutrient loss through cooking. Ascorbic acid content among parental lines ranged from 1.93 to 3.22 mg/100 g, with ‘Pusa Uday’ recording the highest value (3.22 mg/100 g), followed by ‘Pant Kheera-1’ (3.08 mg/100 g). The GCA effects for ascorbic acid ranged from − 0.28 to 0.29, with Punjab Naveen (0.29) and Pusa Uday (0.28) emerging as the best combiners for this trait. Importantly, both Punjab Naveen and Pusa Uday demonstrated positive GCA effects for TSS and ascorbic acid, indicating their utility in improving fruit quality traits. The results clearly indicate that these parental lines possess desirable GCA for multiple trait combinations, highlighting their potential as effective combiners. Therefore, these parents can be strategically utilized in hybridization programs for the development of superior hybrids with enhanced yield and quality traits.

Specific combining ability (SCA) effects of cross combinations

The SCA effect ranged from − 0.31 to 0.20 and − 1.64 to 0.98 for fruit yield and number of fruits per plant, respectively (Table 3). The cross combinations ‘D-20 × Punjab Naveen’, ‘D-20 × Pant Kheera-1’ and ‘Swarna Sheetal × JLG’ showed high positive SCA effect for fruit yield. The cross combinations D-20 × Punjab Naveen (0.84), Punjab Naveen × CMVR-1 (0.83) and Swarna Sheetal × JLG (0.20) showed high positive SCA effect for the number of fruits per plant confirming the favorable F1 hybrids for the traits. The estimated SCA value for fruit weight ranges from − 16.21 to 27.30. The cross combination viz. D-20 × Punjab Naveen (27.30), D-20 × Pant Kheera-1 (23.34) and D-20 × JLG (24.21) showed positive and significant SCA effects for average fruit weight. The SCA effects for fruit length ranged from − 2.09 to 2.27 and hybrid Pusa Uday × CMVR-1 with high significant positive SCA effect (2.27) was a good specific combination for the fruit length. The estimated SCA value for fruit diameter varied from − 0.34 to 0.28 and the cross combinations Swarna Sheetal × JLG, D-20 × CMVR-1 and Pusa Uday × Pant Kheera-1 showed significant SCA effects for fruit diameter. Estimation of genetic components of variation

Estimates of genetic components for the studied traits are presented in Table 5. The top-performing hybrids, identified based on SCA, per se performance, heterobeltiosis, and standard heterosis, are summarized in Table 6. Both additive (D) and non-additive (H1 and H2) components estimate observed to be significant among all the traits. The results indicated that the dominance component (H1) exceeded the additive component (D) for eight traits viz. fruit length, fruit weight, days to first harvest, sex ratio, fruit number per plant, node of first female flower appearance, yield, and TSS highlighting the predominance of non-additive gene action for inheritance of these traits. Besides, degree of dominance (H1/D1/2) was observed to be more than one for the same eight traits for which dominance was higher than additive component suggesting the noteworthiness of over-dominance for these traits. However, two traits i.e. fruit diameter and ascorbic acid showed higher additive component (D) as compared to dominance component (H1) representing the importance of additive component rather than dominant components for these two traits. Besides, the degree of dominance was lower than the 1.0 but maximum than 0.5 for the same traits (fruit diameter, ascorbic acid) showing the persistence of partial dominance for these traits. Positive F value was observed for the traits namely fruit diameter (0.10), fruit weight (778.7), Days to first fruit harvest (9.24), sex ratio (0.04), node at which first female flower appears (0.50), TSS (0.03) and ascorbic acid (0.42) showing the significance of higher frequency for dominant genes. However, the negative F value was found for fruit length (−1.34), number of fruits per plant (−0.26) and yield (−0.01) depicting the higher occurrence of recessive genes. The observed H²/4H₁ ratios were consistently below the expected value of 0.25 for all traits, indicating a symmetrical distribution of favorable and unfavorable alleles among the parental lines. The dominant to recessive gene [(4DH1)1/2 + F/(4DH1)1/2-F] proportion was more than 1.0 for three traits i.e. days to first fruit harvest, sex ratio and ascorbic acid revealing the presence of maximum number of dominant genes. Whereas, for remaining seven traits namely fruit length, fruit diameter, fruit weight, number of fruits per plant, node at which first female flower appears, yield and TSS, the dominant to recessive gene [(4DH1)1/2 + F/(4DH1)1/2-F] proportion was less than unity suggesting the prevalence of maximum number of recessive genes. The ratio h2/H2 observed to be in the range of 0.07 for fruit length, 0.005 for fruit diameter, 0.16 for fruit weight, 0.45 for sex ratio, 0.34 for number of fruits/plant, 0.58 for yield, 0.63 for TSS representing that single group of genes control these traits and reveal dominance for these characters. The ratio of h2/H2 showed value 1.18 for node at which first female flower appears trait and 2.28 for days to first fruit harvest representing the persistence of two and three group of genes, respectively depicting dominance for these traits.

Discussion

In the process of developing high-yielding cultivars, breeders often face challenges in the selection of appropriate parental lines and F1 hybrids. Genetic enhancement in cucumber requires the identification of both suitable parental genotypes and promising hybrid combinations. Heterosis, or hybrid vigor, refers to the phenotypic superiority of hybrids over their parents in traits such as reproductive success, growth rate and yield16. It offers a powerful approach for identifying and utilizing the best heterotic combinations for commercial cultivation, but it is not solely sufficient to determine the superiority of F1s. Thus, evaluation of combining ability (GCA and SCA) is essential, as it facilitates the identification of parents with the potential to produce superior hybrids through successful cross combinations. Combining ability analysis provides deeper insight into parental performance across different genetic backgrounds and is essential for making informed breeding decisions. In this context, fruit yield remains the primary selection criterion in hybrid breeding programs, particularly in cucumbers, where hybrid vigour can significantly influence productivity. But other traits such as fruit length, fruit diameter, and number of fruits per plant are also critical, as they contribute significantly to overall yield and consumer acceptability. Thus, hybrid development must integrate both agronomic performance and market-driven traits. In the present study, significant heterosis was observed over both the better parent and the control for various traits, highlighting its potential in cucumber improvement. The cross combinations namely D-20 × Punjab Naveen, D-20 × Pant Kheera-1, D-20 × JLG, Rashpal × CMVR-1, and Swarna Sheetal × JLG were considered as best F1 hybrid as they exhibit significant positive heterobeltiosis and standard heterosis for fruit yield. Heterosis for yield and its component traits has been well-documented in cucumber by several researchers17–21. Kumari et al.22 also reported high positive heterosis for fruit yield, while observing comparatively lower heterotic effects for fruit length, diameter, and average fruit weight. Considering the fruit weight, the crosses viz. D-20 × Punjab Naveen, D-20 × JLG showed positive and significant heterobeltiosis and standard heterosis. Singathiya et al.23 and Nimitha et al.24 reported the highest heterosis for fruit weight in cucumber.

Negative heterosis is desirable for traits such as node number of the first female flower and days to first fruit harvest, as it indicates earliness, a key objective in hybrid breeding programs. Earliness enables growers to access early markets and secure premium prices. It is primarily associated with reduced node number for first female flower emergence and shorter time to first fruit harvest, making it a critical trait in the development of commercially viable hybrids. Our results were similar to the observations obtained by Bairagi et al.25, Kaur et al.26, Jat et al.27 that reported the significant negative heterosis for days to first fruit harvest over control and better parents. The observations of yield traits i.e., fruit number and yield of fruits per plant, heterosis has also been reported by Punetha et al.28. Bairagi et al.25 reported that superior cucumber hybrids produced a significantly greater number of fruits per plant along with increased average fruit weight.

In the current study, significant heterosis was observed for fruit yield and related traits, highlighting the importance of selecting appropriate parental lines and hybrid combinations for cucumber improvement. High estimates of GCA indicated the predominance of additive gene action and suggest favorable gene transmission from parents to offspring. Among the eight parental lines, D-20 exhibited the highest significant GCA effects for traits such as node number of first female flower appearance, fruit yield per plant, number of fruits per plant, and sex ratio. Punjab Naveen showed significant GCA for fruit diameter and ascorbic acid content. These findings underscore the potential of heterosis breeding for enhancing yield and quality traits. The parental lines D-20, Punjab Naveen, and Pusa Uday, with high GCA effects for key yield attributes, are promising candidates for hybrid development. Moreover, parental lines with strong GCA effects also exhibited superior phenotypic performance for the respective traits. The GCA variance was found to be greater than the SCA variance for all traits, indicating a predominant role of additive genetic effects. Similar observations have been reported by Munshi et al.29, Pati et al.30, and Lnu et al.31. Crosses namely D-20 × Pant Kheera-1, D-20 × Punjab Naveen and Swarna Sheetal × JLG exhibited significant positive SCA effects for fruit yield per plant. The involvement of high GCA parents in these crosses resulted in superior hybrid combinations, consistent with previous findings in cucumber26,30,32,33.

The role of non-additive gene action in the expression of the trait signifies in the SCA effect. The hybrids Pusa Uday × JLG and CMVR-1 × JLG exhibited the highest and significant SCA effects for node number of first female flower and days to first fruit harvest respectively. The hybrid Pusa Uday × CMVR-1 showed maximum SCA effects for fruit length. The hybrids D-20 × Pant Kheera-1, D-20 × JLG, D-20 × Punjab Naveen and Swarna Sheetal × JLG showed higher SCA effects for high sex ratio, number of fruits per plant and yield per plant. D-20 and Punjab Naveen were found to be good specific combiner. The most outstanding parental lines involved in all F1 crosses which showed best SCA effects are D-20, Punjab Naveen and Pusa Uday exhibiting superior performance for the traits. The hybrids namely Swarna Sheetal × JLG, D-20 × Pant Kheera-1, Pusa Uday × JLG and D-20 × Punjab Naveen exhibited significant SCA effects for multiple traits, including yield per plant, indicating their potential for exploiting heterosis. Additionally, crosses with high SCA effects consistently demonstrated superior per se performance for the corresponding traits. The results indicated that high per se performance of hybrids was associated with high SCA effects, and a strong GCA of parents often contributed to superior SCA in their crosses. However, cross containing parents with low GCA can exhibit high SCA effects due to complementary gene action34–36. Therefore, cucumber hybrid development offers significant potential for harnessing heterosis, particularly for earliness, yield, and associated yield traits.

Conclusions

Both additive and non-additive components were observed to be significant for all the studied traits. The results indicated that the dominance component exceeded the additive component for all the traits except for fruit diameter and ascorbic acid justified heterosis breeding in cucumber. Based on GCA effects and mean performance, the genitors D-20, Swarna Sheetal, and Pusa Uday could be utilized in future cucumber breeding to achieve higher yield and quality traits for open field conditions. Based on per se performance, heterosis, and SCA effects, the cross combinations namely 25 − 16 VNR × PMC-1, PK-1 × Punjab Naveen, 25 − 16 VNR × AVCU-1203, PK-1 × PMC-1, and PBRK-11 × Punjab Naveen were identified as superior hybrids for yield and quality traits. Earliness was notable in hybrids viz. 25 − 16 VNR × PMC-1 and PBRK-11 × Swarna Ageti. These hybrids exhibit strong potential for commercial cultivation under open field conditions in summer season but after multi-location testing. Besides, these identified hybrids also need to be tested for cultivation in rainy and winter season (under low tunnel conditions) for its multi-environment testing.

Non-collapsing of Ricci shrinkers with bounded curvature
Conghan Dong, and, Yu Li

Abstract
We establish a uniform entropy bound for simply connected Ricci shrinkers with a finite second homotopy group and a uniform curvature bound. Additionally, we extend the non-collapsing result to a broader class of smooth metric measure spaces satisfying Bakry-Émery conditions.

Introduction

A Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
is a complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
equipped with a smooth potential function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
, satisfying the equation

\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g,

(1.1)

where
f
is normalized so that

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=f.

(1.2)

As the critical metrics of Perelman’s
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional, Ricci shrinkers play a central role in the study of singularity formation in the Ricci flow. In dimensions 2 and 3, all Ricci shrinkers have been fully classified; see
[
27
]

[
42
]

[
37
]

[
40
]

[
7
]
, among others. The complete list consists of
\mathbb{R}^{2},S^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3},S^{3},S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, and their quotients. Recently, all Kähler Ricci shrinkers on complex surfaces have also been classified (cf.
[
18
]

[
17
]

[
2
]
and
[
35
]
).

To understand the uniform behavior of Ricci shrinkers, we consider their moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, which is the space of all
n
-dimensional Ricci shrinkers equipped with the pointed-Gromov-Hausdorff distance. Investigating the compactification of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is a problem of fundamental importance. In
[
33
]
and
[
31
]
(see also
[
29
]
for the case
n=4
), it was shown that, under a uniform lower bound on entropy, a sequence of Ricci shrinkers converges to a singular Ricci shrinker with mild singularities. This demonstrates a weak compactness of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
under a uniform entropy bound.

The entropy
\boldsymbol{\mu}
of a Ricci shrinker, see Definition
2.2
, coincides with Perelman’s celebrated entropy functional
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g,1)
(see
[
9
]
and
[
34
, Proposition
3
]
). The entropy encodes crucial information about Ricci shrinkers. For instance, it is shown in
[
34
, Theorem 1]
that
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is closely related to the optimal Sobolev constant and the non-collapsing property of Ricci shrinkers. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is always nonpositive and has a definite gap from zero for nontrivial Ricci shrinkers (see
[
34
]

[
51
]
and
[
52
]
). Additionally,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
relates to the volume of the unit ball around the base point (see Lemma
2.3
). In essence, a uniform lower bound on entropy is equivalent to a uniform non-collapsing condition.

This paper focuses on another aspect: investigating the behavior of a sequence of Ricci shrinkers in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with entropy tending to
-\infty
. Such sequences are collapsing, implying that the limit metric space must have a lower dimension. Understanding the properties of these limit spaces is critical for two main reasons: (1) the limit space reveals information about the boundary of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, crucial for deducing global properties of the moduli space (see, e.g.,
[
30
]
); (2) analyzing the limit space can help rule out collapsing phenomena through contradiction arguments, provided the limit space is well-understood.

An example of this approach is the work of Petrunin, Rong, and Tuschmann
[
43
]
on a conjecture of Klingenberg and Sakai. In
[
43
]
, it was shown that a sequence of Riemannian manifolds with uniformly positively pinched sectional curvature and a stable topology (see
[
43
, Definition 0.2]
) cannot collapse. Later, Fang and Rong
[
20
]
, as well as Petrunin and Tuschmann
[
44
]
, replaced the stability condition with a topological assumption: the manifolds are simply connected with finite second homotopy groups. The key idea in
[
43
]
was to construct a noncompact Alexandrov space with curvature bounded below by a positive constant using collapsing theory and a gluing argument. This led to a contradiction, as such Alexandrov spaces must be compact. Roughly speaking, this means that some form of positive curvature would prevent collapsing from occurring. Our main theorems (Theorem
1.1
and
1.4
) will share a similar spirit with respect to the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature. See also
[
19
]
for results related to positive scalar curvature.

Our main result is a non-collapsing theorem for Ricci shrinkers, assuming a uniform curvature bound and certain topological restrictions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker with
|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq A
, where
M
is simply connected and has a finite second homotopy group. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n,A)
such that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)\geq-C.

Note that a similar result does not hold if the Ricci shrinker is replaced by an ancient solution to the Ricci flow with a type-I curvature bound and the same topological assumptions; see
[
1
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a contradiction argument. Suppose there exists a sequence of Ricci shrinkers
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})
satisfying the given assumptions with
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g_{i})\to-\infty
. Our goal is to derive a contradiction.

By taking a subsequence, we assume that
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})
converges in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a limit metric space
(X,d_{X},f_{X},p_{X})
. The first task is to analyze the structure of the limit space
X
. Since the curvature is uniformly bounded, we apply the collapsing theory with bounded sectional curvature, initially developed through Gromov’s celebrated theorem on almost flat manifolds
[
26
]
and further advanced by Cheeger, Fukaya, and Gromov in the 1980s and 1990s (see
[
12
]

[

]

[
21
]

[

]

[
23
]
and
[
11
]
, among others). It can be shown (see Proposition
3.6
) that
(X,d_{X})
is a smooth Riemannian orbifold, except for a singular set of high codimension.

Furthermore, for sufficiently large
i
, each
M_{i}
locally admits a pure
N
-structure arising from a singular fibration. Specifically, we consider the oriented frame bundles
FM_{i}
equipped with the bundle metrics
\tilde{g}_{i}
, the lifted function
\tilde{f}_{i}
and the lifted base points
\tilde{p}_{i}
. The sequence
(FM_{i},\tilde{g}_{i},\mathrm{SO}(n),\tilde{f}_{i},\tilde{p}_{i})
converges, in the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense, to a limit space
(Y,g_{Y},\mathrm{SO}(n),f_{Y},p_{Y})
, where
(Y,g_{Y})
is a smooth Riemannian manifold, and the quotient
(Y,g_{Y})/\mathrm{SO}(n)
is isometric to
(X,d_{X})
.

For a fixed large number
s>0
, there exists an
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-equivariant fibration
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}:\tilde{U}_{i}(s)\to\tilde{\Omega}(s)
, where
\tilde{\Omega}(s)=\{x\in Y\mid f_{Y}(x)\leq s\}
, and
\tilde{U}_{i}(s)
closely approximates
\tilde{\Omega}_{i}(s)=\{x\in FM_{i}\mid\tilde{f}_{i}(x)\leq s\}
; see Theorem
3.10
. The singular fibration on
U_{i}(s)\subset M_{i}
, where
U_{i}(s)=\tilde{U}_{i}(s)/\mathrm{SO}(n)
, is given by the quotient of
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}
. In particular, there exists a singular fibration
\Phi_{i}:U_{i}(s)\to\Omega_{i}(s)
, where
\Omega_{i}(s)=\{x\in X\mid f_{X}(x)\leq s\}
, such that the following diagram commutes:

For sufficiently large

,
U_{i}(s)
captures the topological structure of
M_{i}
. Specifically, as shown in Proposition
3.12
,
U_{i}(s)
is homotopy equivalent to
M_{i}
, and hence is simply connected by our assumption. A result of Rong
[

]
(see Propositions

and
3.14
) implies that the singular fibration
\Phi_{i}
is realized by a
T^{k}
-action on

, where the orbits correspond to the singular fibers of
\Phi_{i}
. Moreover, one can construct a
T^{k}
-invariant metric
g_{i}^{\prime}
and a function
f_{i}^{\prime}
that are close to the original metric
g_{i}
and

; see Proposition

.

For simplicity, we assume
U_{i}(s)=M_{i}
,
\tilde{U}_{i}(s)=FM_{i}
,
\Omega(s)=X
, and
\tilde{\Omega}(s)=Y
, for a sufficiently large fixed

. The general case involves choosing an appropriate sequence of
s_{j}\to\infty
and taking a diagonal subsequence; see Theorem
3.16
.

The assumption on the second homotopy group of
M_{i}
implies that the collapsing
M_{i}\to X
is stable, in the sense that all corresponding
T^{k}
-actions are equivalent up to conjugations of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{Z},k)
on
T^{k}
. Consequently, all
M_{i}
are diffeomorphic to one another. We may therefore write
M=M_{i}
, with
g^{\prime}_{i}
and

as metrics and functions on
M
, respectively.

To derive a contradiction, we follow the approach in
[
43
]
by constructing a metric space
W
, which is an
\mathbb{R}
-bundle over
X
. This involves unwrapping a fixed circle component within each singular fiber, which is a quotient of
T^{k}
. This unwrapping process is the most technical part of the paper, and we provide full details for its implementation. Briefly, we consider the principal circle bundle
F/T^{k-1}\to Y
, where
F
denotes either the oriented frame bundle over
M
or the
\mathrm{Spin}(n)
-bundle over
M
. We then unwrap the circle fibers to form an
\mathbb{R}
-bundle
Z
over
Y
. In general, this unwrapping process is obstructed by the Euler class of the circle bundle. However, as observed in
[
43
]
, the Euler class vanishes in the limit, as the length of the circle fibers tends to zero. This allows us to construct an
\mathbb{R}
-bundle
W
over
X
, obtained as the quotient of
Z
by
\mathrm{SO}(n)
or
\mathrm{Spin}(n)
.

By construction,
W
with the induced metric is a noncompact, complete metric space; see Lemma
4.6
. At this point, the Ricci shrinker equation (
1.1
) becomes crucial. On the regular part
(\mathcal{R}_{W},g_{W})
of
W
, we derive the equation:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g_{W})+\nabla_{g_{W}}^{2}(\bar{f}_{W})\geq\frac{1}{2}g_{W}.

(1.3)

where
\bar{f}_{W}
is a smooth,
\mathbb{R}
-invariant function on
\mathcal{R}_{W}
, obtained as the limit of
f^{\prime}_{i}
plus a density function. The desired contradiction then follows from the second variational formula for minimizing geodesics connecting two regular points on an
\mathbb{R}
-orbit in
W
.

As a direct corollary to Theorem
1.1
, we have:

Corollary 1.2
.

Given
n
and
A
, there are only finitely many diffeomorphism types of simply connected
n
-dimensional manifolds
M
with finite second homotopy groups that admit a Ricci shrinker metric satisfying
|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq A
.

The method used to prove Theorem
1.1
can be extended to a broader class of smooth metric measure spaces. To define this class, we introduce the following:

Definition 1.3
.

Given a constant
\kappa>0
, let
\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
be a class of pointed smooth metric measure spaces
(M^{n},g,f,p)
satisfying

(i)

(M^{n},g)
is a complete
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with
|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq A
.

(ii)

f
is a
C^{2}
-function on
M
such that
p
is minimum point,
f(p)=0
, and
|\nabla^{2}f|\leq A
.

(iii)

The Bakry-Émery condition holds:
\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f\geq\kappa g
.

(iv)

M
is simply connected and has a finite second homotopy group.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists a constant
v=v(n,A,\kappa)>0
such that for any
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\left(B_{g}(p,1)\right)\geq v.

It follows directly that any Ricci shrinker in Theorem
1.1
belongs to
\mathcal{N}(n,C(n)A+1/2,1/2)
if the potential function
f
is shifted by a constant to satisfy
f(p)=0
. Thus, Theorem
1.4
extends Theorem
1.1
. Furthermore, Theorem
1.4
also generalizes
[
44
, Theorem 0.4]
, in which the potential function
f
is identically zero.

There are two main differences between Ricci shrinkers and metric measure spaces defined above. First, the spaces in
\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
satisfy only the Bakry-Émery condition as in Definition
1.3
(iii), which is weaker than the equality in the Ricci shrinker equation (
1.1
). However, this is not a significant issue because the lower bound
\kappa g
is sufficient for deriving the key inequality (
1.3
). The second difference is that spaces in
\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
do not inherently satisfy higher-order estimates for
\mathrm{Rm}
or
f
. In the Ricci shrinker case, such estimates are guaranteed by Shi’s estimates and the Ricci shrinker equation (see (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)).

To address these challenges, we smooth the metric measure space
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
using Ricci flow, where
g
serves as the initial metric, and smooth
f
by solving the heat equation under the Ricci flow. Through careful estimates, we construct a new metric
g^{\prime}
and function
f^{\prime}
on
M
such that
(M^{n},g^{\prime},f^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}(n,A^{\prime},\kappa/2)
, where
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}(n,A,\kappa)
is a constant, and
g^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}
satisfy uniform higher-order estimates. Moreover, the volume of the unit ball around
p
with respect to
g
is uniformly comparable to the volume of the unit ball around
p^{\prime}
with respect to
g^{\prime}
; see Theorem
5.10
. This allows us to apply the same contradiction argument used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
to establish Theorem
1.4
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we review fundamental concepts and properties of Ricci shrinkers. Section 3 addresses the general behavior of collapsing sequences of Ricci shrinkers with bounded curvature, using the collapsing theory for spaces with bounded sectional curvature. In Section 4, we prove the main Theorem
1.1
through the gluing process described earlier. Finally, Section 5 extends these methods to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we review some basic concepts and fundamental properties of Ricci shrinkers.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker with normalization (
1.2
). From
[
14
, Corollary
2.5
]
, it follows that the scalar curvature
R\geq 0
, with
R>0
unless
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to the Gaussian soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{E})
, as guaranteed by the strong maximum principle.

For the potential function
f
, we have the following fundamental estimates.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
]

[
28
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker. Then
f
satisfies the quadratic growth estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(d(x,p)-5n\right)^{2}_{+}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d(x,p)+\sqrt{2n}\right)^{2}

for all
x\in M
, where
p
is a minimum point of
f
and
a_{+}:=\max\{0,a\}
.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that any minimum point lies in a ball of fixed size. In particular, we can designate a minimum point
p
as the base point of the Ricci shrinker and represent it as
(M^{n},g,f,p)
.

Next, we recall the definition of the entropy of a Ricci shrinker.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker with normalization (
1.2
). Its entropy is defined as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{\mu}(g)\coloneqq\log\int_{M}\frac{e^{-f}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\,dV_{g}.

The entropy
\boldsymbol{\mu}
coincides with Perelman’s celebrated entropy functional
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g,1)
; see
[
9
]
and
[
34
, Proposition
3
]
. Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is always nonpositive and finite for any Ricci shrinker; see
[
34
, Theorem
1
]
. For a detailed discussion of the
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional on Ricci shrinkers and its properties, we refer readers to
[
34
, Section
5
]
.

The following characterization of
\boldsymbol{\mu}
, proved in
[
33
, Lemma
2.5
]
, states that the unit ball around the base point collapses if and only if the entropy approaches
-\infty
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)
be a Ricci shrinker. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n)>1
such that

\displaystyle C^{-1}e^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\left(B_{g}(p,1)\right)\leq Ce^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}

In this paper, we focus on Ricci shrinkers with uniformly bounded curvature. This naturally leads to the consideration of the following moduli space:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}(n,A)
denote the family of Ricci shrinkers
(M^{n},g,f)
satisfying the curvature bound

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq A.

We collect some elementary properties for Ricci shrinkers in this moduli space.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{E}(n,A)
be a Ricci shrinker, and define
\Omega_{M}(s):=\{x\in M\mid f(x)\leq s\}
. The following conclusions hold:

(a)

There exists a constant
L=L(n,A)\geq 1
such that for any
s_{2}>s_{1}\geq L
, the set
\Omega_{M}(s_{2})\setminus\mathrm{int}(\Omega_{M}(s_{1}))
is diffeomorphic to
\partial\Omega_{M}(s_{2})\times[0,1]
. Moreover,
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathrm{int}(\Omega_{M}(s))
for any

.

(b)

For any
l\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C_{l}=C_{l}(n,A)>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{l}\mathrm{Rm}|\leq C_{l}.

(2.1)

(c)

For any

C_{l}=C_{l}(n,A)>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{l}f|\leq C_{l}.

(2.2)

Proof.

(a): By assumption, the scalar curvature satisfies
R\leq C(n)A
. Thus, the conclusion follows directly from the identity (

) and Lemma

.

(b): Recall that every Ricci shrinker is associated with a self-similar ancient solution to the Ricci flow. Specifically, let
{\psi^{t}}:M\to M
be a family of diffeomorphisms generated by

, with
\psi^{0}=\text{id}
. The rescaled pull-back metric

satisfies the Ricci flow equation for any

[
47
]
.

(c): This follows immediately from (b) and the Ricci shrinker equation (
1.1
).
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{B}(n,A)

\mathcal{E}(n,A)

. Then,
\mathcal{E}_{B}(n,A)

Proof.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\in\mathcal{E}_{B}(n,A)
. By Lemma

, we can take a subsequence if necessary such that

\displaystyle(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}){\mathrel{\to}^{\text{pointed }{C}^{\infty}}}(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty}),

where

In particular, combining Proposition

3
Collapsing of Ricci shrinkers with bounded curvature

In this section, we discuss the behavior of a sequence of Ricci shrinkers collapsing under uniformly bounded sectional curvature.

To begin with, we first recall the following concept:

Definition 3.1

(Frame bundle)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an orientable Riemannian manifold. Its oriented orthogonal frame bundle
FM
is equipped with a canonical metric
\tilde{g}
, defined as follows: the Levi-Civita connection of
g
determines the horizontal distribution, while each fiber is endowed with the standard bi-invariant metric on
\mathrm{SO}(n)
. In particular, the projection
\rho:(FM,\tilde{g})\to(M,g)
is a Riemannian submersion.

For an orientable Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f,p)
, we assign a base point
\tilde{p}
in its frame bundle
FM
such that
\rho(\tilde{p})=p
. Furthermore, the function
f
can be lifted to a smooth,
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant function
\tilde{f}
on
FM
. Naturally,
\tilde{\Omega}_{M}(s):=\{x\in FM\mid\tilde{f}(x)\leq s\}
corresponds to the frame bundle of
\Omega_{M}(s)=\{x\in M\mid f(x)\leq s\}
.

The following lemma is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.5
(b) and the celebrated O’Neill formula
[
41
]
. Recall that
\mathcal{E}(n,A)
is defined in Definition
2.4
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{E}(n,A)
be an orientable Ricci shrinker. For any
l\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C_{l}=C_{l}(n,A)>0
such that on
(FM,\tilde{g})
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\tilde{g}}^{l}\mathrm{Rm}(\tilde{g})|\leq C_{l}.

For the remainder of this section, we fix a sequence of orientable Ricci shrinkers
(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\in\mathcal{E}(n,A)
with entropy
\boldsymbol{\mu}(g_{i})\to-\infty
.

Using the standard precompactness theory
[
3
]
(see also
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
), we obtain the following result:

Proposition 3.3
.

By taking a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}){\mathrel{\to}^{\text{pointed Gromov-Hausdorff}}}(X,d_{X},f_{X},p_{X}),

(3.1)

where
(X,d_{X})
is a geodesic space, and
f_{X}
is a locally Lipschitz function on
X
.

By our assumption on the entropy, the Hausdorff dimension of
X
is strictly less than
n
. Furthermore, by the curvature bound,
(X,d_{X})
is an Alexandrov space with curvature bounded below by
-A
.

The local structure around a point
x\in X
can be described as follows; see
[
22
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
46
]
. Take a sequence
x_{i}\in M_{i}
with
x_{i}\to x
. Fix a small ball
B(0,\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\simeq T_{x_{i}}M_{i}
, where
\epsilon
is a fixed constant smaller than
\pi/\sqrt{A}
. Consider the pull back metric
\exp_{x_{i}}^{*}g_{i}
, denoted simply by
g_{i}
for convenience. Define the local fundamental group
G_{i}
as

\displaystyle G_{i}=\{\gamma\mid\gamma\text{ is a geodesic loop at }x_{i}\text{ with length smaller than }\epsilon\}.

Then, we have the equivariant convergence

\displaystyle\left(B(0,\epsilon),g_{i},G_{i}\right){\mathrel{\to}^{\text{equivariant\ }C^{\infty}}}\left(B(0,\epsilon),g,G_{\infty}\right).

The definition of equivariant convergence can be found in
[
24
, Definition 6.11]
or
[
46
, Definition 1.6.6]
. Here,
G_{\infty}
is a local Lie group acting smoothly on
B(0,\epsilon)
, and its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent.

The following isometry holds:

\displaystyle\left(B(x,\epsilon),d_{X}\right)=\left(B(0,\epsilon),g\right)/G_{\infty}.

The isotropy group at
0

\mathbb{R}^{k}

\displaystyle(\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha},\bar{g}_{i}^{\alpha}){\mathrel{\to}^{\text{local Gromov-Hausdorff}}}(\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha},\bar{h}^{\alpha}).

(4.8)

Here, the local Gromov-Hausdorff convergence means that for any
y\in V_{\alpha}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the ball
B_{\bar{h}^{\alpha}}((r,y),\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
, and

\displaystyle\left(B_{\bar{g}_{i}^{\alpha}}((r,y),\epsilon),d_{\bar{g}_{i}^{\alpha}}\right){\mathrel{\to}^{\text{Gromov-Hausdorff}}}\left(B_{\bar{h}^{\alpha}}((r,y),\epsilon),d_{\bar{h}^{\alpha}}\right),

(4.9)

where

and

are the distance functions of the length structures induced by
\bar{g}_{i}^{\alpha}
and
\bar{h}^{\alpha}
, respectively.

Next, we identify the obstruction to unwrapping the circle fibers. First, we lift the transition map in (
4.6
) to
\tilde{\phi}_{\beta\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\times(V_{\alpha}\cap V_{\beta})\to\mathbb{R}\times(V_{\alpha}\cap V_{\beta})
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{\beta\alpha}(r,x)=(r+\tilde{s}_{\beta\alpha}(x),x),

for
(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(V_{\alpha}\cap V_{\beta})
, where
\tilde{s}_{\beta\alpha}(x)
is fixed smooth lift of
s^{\prime}_{\beta\alpha}(x)
. Note that the lift is not unique, as any two lifts differ by a translation in
\mathbb{Z}
.

For
V_{\alpha}\cap V_{\beta}\cap V_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
, we define

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}:=\tilde{s}_{\alpha\gamma}+\tilde{s}_{\gamma\beta}+\tilde{s}_{\beta\alpha}.

(4.10)

The value
\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)
, for
x\in V_{\alpha}\cap V_{\beta}\cap V_{\gamma}
, is a constant integer. The collection of all such

constitutes the Euler class of the fibration
F/T^{k-1}\to Y
in the second Čech cohomology group
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

The effect of the obstruction (
4.10
), under this metric
\bar{g}_{i}^{\alpha}
, is given by

\displaystyle d_{\bar{g}_{i}^{\alpha}}\left((r,x),(r+\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma},x)\right)\leq|\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}|\cdot\mathrm{Length}_{i}(S^{1}_{x}),

(4.11)

where
\mathrm{Length}_{i}(S^{1}_{x})
is the length of the circle over

with respect to the quotient metric
\tilde{g}_{i,k-1}
on
S^{1}\times V_{\alpha}\subset F/T^{k-1}
. It follows from Lemma
4.2
that
\mathrm{Length}_{i}(S^{1}_{x})
tends to zero as the diameter of each

with respect to
\tilde{g}_{i}
converges to zero locally uniformly. Combining (

), (
4.9
), and (

), we conclude that the obstruction vanishes.

Thus, by taking a further subsequence, we can glue all the pieces
(\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha},\bar{h}^{\alpha})
to obtain an
\mathbb{R}
-bundle
(Z,g_{Z})
over
Y
. Topologically,

is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times Y

is simply connected.

Since

is locally identified with
\bar{h}^{\alpha}
, it is clear that

is invariant under the
\mathbb{R}
-action. Similarly, one can lift
\tilde{f}_{i}
to obtain
\bar{f}_{i}^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
, and by taking the limit, we obtain a smooth function

, which is also invariant under the
\mathbb{R}
-action.

Next, we show that

is equipped with a

\mathbb{R}
-action. Since the

-action descends to

, there exists a decomposition

, so that for each

,

is a small contractible open set, and there exists a refined cover
\{V^{j}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\}
of

such that for any

and

,

lies within some

For

and

\displaystyle\xi(t,x)=(\phi_{\xi}(x)t,\psi_{\xi}(x)),

where

. Using this, we define the action of

on

\displaystyle\xi(r,x)=(\tilde{\phi}_{\xi}(x)+r,\psi_{\xi}(x)),

for

In this way, for each element in

\displaystyle\coprod_{\alpha^{\prime}}\mathbb{R}\times V^{j}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to\coprod_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}.

In general, these maps cannot always be glued into a global map

due to potential inconsistencies across different pieces. However, as in the argument above, it can be shown that, by taking the limit, all discrepancies vanish. Consequently, we obtain an action of

on

.

Repeating this process for each

, we define an action of

on

. Moreover, it is straightforward to verify that this is indeed a group action: since the map on

is a group action, any discrepancies arising from the lifting process tend to zero as we take the limit.

By construction, the

and commutes with the
\mathbb{R}
-action. Additionally,

is invariant under

.

Now, we define
\hat{\iota}:(Z,g_{Z})\to(Y,g_{Y})
, where

is a Riemannian submersion. For each

, we denote the

-fiber in

. From Theorem

is an embedded submanifold, which has a uniformly bounded second fundamental form and a uniformly positive normal injectivity radius. Furthermore, since

is
\mathbb{R}
-invariant, each

, with the induced metric from

, is isometric to

, the standard flat metric.

Lemma 4.4
.

The Riemannian manifold
(Z,g_{Z})
is complete.

Proof.

Suppose
\{z_{i}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in

. Since
\hat{\iota}
is distance-decreasing,
\{\hat{\iota}(z_{i})\}
is also a Cauchy sequence in
Y
. Because

is complete, we can assume without loss of generality that
\hat{\iota}(z_{i})\to y\in Y
.

Let
z_{i}^{\prime}
be the projection of
z_{i}
to
\mathbb{R}_{y}
, which is well defined and unique since the normal injectivity radius of
\mathbb{R}_{y}
is bounded below by a positive constant. It is clear that
\{z_{i}^{\prime}\}
is also a Cauchy sequence with respect to

. By taking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume

for all

, where

is a small constant to be determined later.

Since

is an embedded submanifold, we can choose a small enough number

to ensure that

and

, the induced metric on

, are comparable on

. Therefore,

converges to some

under

. Consequently,
z_{i}\to z_{\infty}
in

.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For any

, the fiber
\mathbb{R}_{y}
is noncompact.

Proof.

Fix
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{y}
, and define

, where

and
t_{i}\to+\infty
. If

is compact, we could assume

under

. However, this would contradict the same reasoning used in the proof of Lemma

. Therefore,

must be noncompact.
∎

Now, we define
W=Z/G
with the quotient metric

. Additionally, let

on

denote the quotient function of

. Since the

-action and
\mathbb{R}
-action commute, the
\mathbb{R}
-action descends naturally to

. Moreover, this
\mathbb{R}
-action is isometric, and

remains invariant under the
\mathbb{R}
-action.

Because
G
is compact, each
\mathbb{R}
-orbit in
W
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,
W
can be viewed as an
\mathbb{R}
-bundle over
X
. Since
X
is simply-connected,
W
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times X
. Let
\iota:W\to X
be the quotient map. This relationship is illustrated in the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(Z,g_{Z},f_{Z})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(Y,g_{Y},f_{Y})}}

Lemma 4.6
.

The space
(W,d_{W})
is complete. Moreover, each
\mathbb{R}
-orbit in

is noncompact.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from Lemma

.

To prove the second, fix a point
w_{0}\in W
with a lift

. Consider a sequence

. Suppose, for contradiction, that
d_{W}(t_{i}w_{0},w_{0})\leq C
for some constant

. By the definition of the quotient metric, there exists a sequence
\xi_{i}\in G
such that:

\displaystyle d_{Z}(t_{i}z_{0},\xi_{i}z_{0})=d_{W}(t_{i}w_{0},w_{0})\leq C.

Since

is compact, by taking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume
\xi_{i}\to\xi_{\infty}\in G
. Consequently,
d_{Z}(\xi_{i}z_{0},\xi_{\infty}z_{0})\to 0
. However, this implies that

is uniformly bounded for all

, which contradicts Lemma

,

Thus, each
\mathbb{R}
-orbit in

must also be noncompact.
∎

At this stage, the Ricci shrinker equation (
1.1
) becomes relevant. We aim to derive an equation for the metric
d_{W}
on the regular part of
W
, which will ultimately lead to a contradiction. Note that
W
is the orbit space of the
G
-action on
Z
, and hence the regular part of
W
corresponds to the principal orbits. For any
z\in Z
, the isotropy group of
G
at
z
is contained in the isotropy group of
G
at
\bar{z}
, the image of
z
in
Y
. In particular, this implies that the principal isotropy group of
G
on
Z
is finite. The regular part of
W
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}_{W}
, where the metric is given by
g_{W}
.

Before proceeding with the calculations, we recall the general definition of O’Neill tensors, introduced in
[
41
]
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be a Riemannian manifold with a free, proper, and isometric action by an abelian Lie group
\mathbb{T}
. The quotient space is
(N,g_{N})=(M,g_{M})/\mathbb{T}
, and the projection
(M,g_{M})\to(N,g_{N})
is a Riemannian submerison.

For any vector field
E
on
M
, we can decompose it as the sum of its vertical part
E^{v}
(tangent to the
\mathbb{T}
-orbits) and its horizontal part
E^{h}
(orthogonal to the
\mathbb{T}
-orbits). The O’Neill tensors
A
and
T
are then defined as follows:

\displaystyle T(E,F):=(\nabla_{E^{v}}F^{v})^{h}+(\nabla_{E^{v}}F^{h})^{v}\quad\text{and}\quad A(E,F):=(\nabla_{E^{h}}F^{h})^{v}+(\nabla_{E^{h}}F^{v})^{h},

where
E
and
F
are arbitrary vector fields on
M
, and
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g_{M}
.

We fix a basis
\{V^{a}\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathbb{T}
, which induces fundamental vector fields, also denoted by
\{V^{a}\}
, on
M
. Specifically, for any
x\in M
, the vector
V^{a}
at
x
is defined as

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(tV^{a})\cdot x.

Additionally, we choose a local basis
\{X^{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}\}
on
N
, which can also be regarded as horizontal vector fields on
M
. Using these bases, we define:

\displaystyle h_{ab}=g_{M}(V^{a},V^{b}),\quad T_{abi}=g_{M}(T(V^{a},V^{b}),X^{i})\quad\text{and}\quad A_{ija}=g_{M}(A(X^{i},X^{j}),V^{a}).

(4.12)

Here,
a,b,c,\dots
denote vertical components, and
i,j,k,\dots
denote horizontal components. Since
\mathbb{T}
is abelian, it follows that
h_{ab}
is
\mathbb{T}
-invariant and therefore depends only on
N
. Moreover, we define:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}=\sum_{a,b}h^{ab}T(V^{a},V^{b})\quad\text{and}\quad\mu:=-\log\sqrt{\det(h_{ab})},

(4.13)

where
(h^{ab})
is the inverse matrix of
(h_{ab})
.

Here,
\mathbf{H}
represents the mean curvature vector of the fibers, and
\nabla\mu=\mathbf{H}
. If a different basis
\{V^{a}\}
is chosen,
\mu
will differ only by a constant.

Using O’Neill’s formula
[
41
]
, we have the following identity (see
[
39
, Proposition 8.1]
):

Lemma 4.8
.

With the above definitions and notations, we have the following identity on
(N,g_{N})
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g_{N})_{ij}+(\nabla^{2}_{g_{N}}\mu)_{ij}=\mathrm{Rc}(g_{M})_{ij}+h^{ab}h^{cd}T_{aci}T_{bdj}+2h^{ab}g_{M}^{kl}A_{ika}A_{jlb}\geq\mathrm{Rc}(g_{M})_{ij}.

Remark 4.9
.

In the general case where the Lie group
\mathbb{T}
is not necessarily abelian, one can consider the adjoint bundle
(M\times\mathfrak{t})/\mathbb{T}
over
N
. The sections of this adjoint bundle correspond to
\mathbb{T}
-invariant vertical vector fields
\{V^{a}\}
on
M
. The metric
(h_{ab})
, as defined in (
4.12
), can be interpreted as a metric on this adjoint bundle. For further details and additional identities involving the Ricci curvatures of the total space, fibers, base manifold, and
h_{ab}
, see
[
39
, Proposition 8.1]
.

The following result is straightforward:

Lemma 4.10
.

Under the above assumptions, let
f_{M}
be a smooth
\mathbb{T}
-invariant function on
M
. Define
f_{N}
as the corresponding function on
N
. Then, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}_{g_{N}}f_{N})_{ij}=(\nabla^{2}_{g_{M}}f_{M})_{ij}.

Proof.

Since
f_{M}
is
\mathbb{T}
-invariant,
\nabla_{g_{M}}f_{M}
is the horizontal lift of
\nabla_{g_{N}}f_{N}
. Thus, we compute:

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}_{g_{M}}f_{M})_{ij}=

\displaystyle g_{M}\left(\nabla_{g_{M},X^{i}}\nabla_{g_{M}}f_{M},X^{j}\right)=g_{M}\left(\overline{\nabla_{g_{N},X^{i}}\nabla_{g_{N}}f_{N}}+\frac{1}{2}[X^{i},\nabla_{g_{M}}f_{M}]^{v},X^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{N}\left(\nabla_{g_{N},X^{i}}\nabla_{g_{N}}f_{N},X^{j}\right)=(\nabla^{2}_{g_{N}}f_{N})_{ij},

where
\bar{\cdot}
denotes the horizontal lift. This completes the proof.
∎

We now proceed with the proof of the main theorem. Fix a regular point
w\in\mathcal{R}_{W}\subset W
, and let
z\in Z
be a lift of
w
. Suppose the isotropy group of
G
at
z
is
H
, which is a finite group. By the slice theorem, there exists a slice
S_{z}
through
z
and a neighborhood
B
of
z
in the form of
G_{0}\times_{H}S_{z}
, where
G_{0}
is a small contractible open neighborhood of the identity in
G
, treated as a local group. Since
z
lies in the principal orbit, the finite group
H
fixes
S_{z}
, and
S_{z}
, equipped with the quotient metric of
g_{Z}
, is isometric to a neighborhood of
w
. By our construction of
Z
, we may assume
G_{0}
and
S_{z}
are sufficiently small so that
B
is contained in a piece
\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
.

We denote the preimage of
B
, with respect to
\mathbb{R}\times T^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
by
B^{\prime}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{R}\times T^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha}
is equipped with the quotient metric of
\tilde{h}^{\alpha}
, denoted by
h_{T}^{\alpha}
, from

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{k}\times V_{\alpha}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}\times T^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha},

where
\mathbb{R}\times T^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha}
comes from the trivialization (
4.2
) and the decomposition (
4.4
).

By our construction, and by taking a smaller
G_{0}
if necessary, we may assume that
G_{0}
can be lifted to a local action on
\mathbb{R}\times T^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, under the quotient by
T^{k-1}
, this local group action agrees with the action on
\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
.

Recall from Lemma
4.3
that
\tilde{h}^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times V_{\alpha}
is the limit of
\tilde{g}_{i}^{\alpha}
, which is the lift of
\tilde{g}_{i}
. By Theorem
3.16
(b) and the Ricci shrinker equation (
1.1
), the quotient metric of
h_{T}^{\alpha}
on
B^{\prime\prime}=B^{\prime}/G_{0}
, denoted by
h^{\alpha}
, coupled with the quotient function
f^{\alpha}
, satisfies the Ricci shrinker equation.

Since
B^{\prime}=S_{z}\times_{H}G_{0}\times T^{k-1}
and the
T^{k-1}
-action commutes with the
G_{0}
-action, it follows that
B^{\prime\prime}=S_{z}\times(T^{k-1}/H)
, where
T^{k-1}/H
is also a
(k-1)
-dimensional torus since
H
is finite. By construction,
(B^{\prime\prime},h^{\alpha},f^{\alpha})/(T^{k-1}/H)
is locally isometric to
(W,g_{W},f_{W})
around
w
.

Combining the Ricci shrinker equation:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(h^{\alpha})+\nabla_{h^{\alpha}}^{2}(f^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2}h^{\alpha},

(4.14)

with Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.10
, we obtain the following inequality on a small neighborhood of
w
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g_{W})+\nabla_{g_{W}}^{2}(f_{W}+\mu)\geq\frac{1}{2}g_{W},

(4.15)

where
\mu
is the density function in (
4.13
) with respect to the torus bundle
(B^{\prime\prime},h^{\alpha})
.

We now demonstrate that the density function
\mu
is globally well-defined on
\mathcal{R}_{W}
and that (
4.15
) holds on
\mathcal{R}_{W}
as well. The density function
\mu(w)
on
\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
is defined so that
|H|e^{-\mu(w)}
, where
|H|
is the order of the principal isotropy group, represents the volume density of the
T^{k-1}
-fiber in
\mathbb{R}\times T^{k-1}\times V_{\alpha}
over
w\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{\alpha}
, with respect to the metric
h^{\alpha}_{T}
. Considering the gluing map described in (
4.5
), it is straightforward to verify that
\mu
is globally well-defined on
\mathcal{R}_{W}
. Furthermore, by construction,
\mu
is invariant under the
\mathbb{R}
-action on
W
.

Define
\bar{f}_{W}:=f_{W}+\mu
. Then,
\bar{f}_{W}
is a smooth,
\mathbb{R}
-invariant function on
\mathcal{R}_{W}
. From (
4.15
), we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g_{W})+\nabla_{g_{W}}^{2}(\bar{f}_{W})\geq\frac{1}{2}g_{W}.

(4.16)

Next, fix a point
w_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{W}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\overline{B_{g_{W}}(w_{0},2)}\subset\mathcal{R}_{W}
. Take a sequence
w_{l}
on the same
\mathbb{R}
-orbit as
w_{0}
such that
d_{W}(w_{0},w_{l})\to+\infty
as
l\to\infty
. By Lemma
4.6
, such a sequence exists. For each
w_{l}
, consider a minimizing geodesic
\gamma(t)
, defined for
t\in[0,d_{l}]
, where
d_{l}=d_{W}(w_{0},w_{l})
, such that
\gamma(0)=w_{0}
and
\gamma(d_{l})=w_{l}
. Since
\mathcal{R}_{W}
is geodesically convex by Kleiner’s isotropy lemma
[
32
]
, we conclude that
\gamma(t)\subset\mathcal{R}_{W}
for all
t\in[0,d_{l}]
.

We construct a function
\eta(t)
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\begin{cases}t&\text{ for }t\leq 1,\\
1&\text{ for }1\leq t\leq d_{l}-1,\\
d_{l}-t&\text{ for }d_{l}-1\leq t\leq d_{l}.\end{cases}

Using the second variation formula for the minimizing geodesic
\gamma(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d_{l}}\eta^{2}\mathrm{Rc}(g_{W})(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt\leq(m^{\prime}-1)\int_{0}^{d_{l}}(\eta^{\prime})^{2}\,dt=2(m^{\prime}-1),

(4.17)

where
m^{\prime}
is the dimension of
\mathcal{R}_{W}
. From (
4.16
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g_{W})(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))+\left(\bar{f}_{W}(\gamma(t))\right)^{\prime\prime}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(4.18)

Combining (
4.18
) with (
4.17
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{d_{l}}{2}-\frac{2}{3}-2(m^{\prime}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{d_{l}}\eta^{2}\left(\bar{f}_{W}(\gamma(t))\right)^{\prime\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{1}\eta\left(\bar{f}_{W}(\gamma(t))\right)^{\prime}\,dt+2\int_{d_{l}-1}^{d_{l}}\eta\left(\bar{f}_{W}(\gamma(t))\right)^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\nabla\bar{f}_{W}|(\gamma(t))+2\sup_{t\in[d_{l},d_{l}-1]}|\nabla\bar{f}_{W}|(\gamma(t)),

(4.19)

where integration by parts is used to derive the equality.

Since the
\mathbb{R}
-action is isometric, we conclude that the right-hand side in (
4.19
) is bounded by
4\sup_{w\in B_{g_{W}}(w,1)}|\nabla\bar{f}_{W}|
, which is a finite constant. However, taking
l\to\infty
leads to a contradiction in (
4.19
), as the left-hand side grows without bound while the right-hand side remains finite.

Thus, we have completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

5
Non-collapsing for smooth metric measure spaces

In this section, we extend Theorem
1.1
to a broader class of smooth metric measure spaces, defined as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a constant
\kappa>0
, let
\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
denote the class of pointed smooth metric measure spaces
(M^{n},g,f,p)
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

(M^{n},g)
is a complete
n
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold with
|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq A
.

(b)

f
is a
C^{2}
-function on
M
such that
p
is minimum point,
f(p)=0
, and
|\nabla^{2}f|\leq A
.

(c)

The Bakry-Émery condition holds:
\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f\geq\kappa g
.

We also define the subclass
\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)\subset\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
consisting of pointed smooth metric measure spaces where the underlying manifold is simply connected and has a finite second homotopy group.

It follows directly from our definition that any Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{E}(n,A)
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(n,C(n)A+1/2,1/2)
if
f
is shifted by a constant such that
f(p)=0
. Furthermore, for any
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
, we can define the measure
dV_{f}:=e^{-f}dV_{g}
. In this setting,
(M^{n},g,dV_{f})
is a smooth
\mathrm{RCD}(\kappa,\infty)
space, as defined in
[
25
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,A)>0
such that for any
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
and
x\in M
,

\displaystyle f(x)\leq Ad^{2}_{g}(p,x)\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa d_{g}(p,x)-C\leq|\nabla f(x)|\leq Ad_{g}(p,x).

Proof.

The bound on
f(x)
and the upper bound on
|\nabla f(x)|
follow directly from
|\nabla^{2}f|\leq A
and the assumption that
f(p)=\nabla f(p)=0
. To establish the lower bound for
|\nabla f(x)|
, consider a minimizing geodesic
\gamma(t)
for
t\in[0,d_{x}]
with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(d_{x})=x
, where
d_{x}=d_{g}(p,x)
.

Using the second variation formula and reasoning similarly to (
4.19
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\kappa d_{x}-\frac{2}{3}-2(n-1)\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\nabla f|(\gamma(t))+2\sup_{t\in[d_{x},d_{x}-1]}|\nabla f|(\gamma(t))\leq 4A+|\nabla f(x)|,

where the last inequality uses the bound
|\nabla^{2}f|\leq A
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,A)>0
such that for any
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
and
x\in M
,

\displaystyle f(x)\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}d^{2}_{g}(p,x)-Cd_{g}(p,x)-C.

Proof.

Set
r=2C/\kappa
, where
C
is the constant in Lemma
5.2
. Then, for
d_{g}(p,x)\geq r
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla f(x)|\geq\kappa d_{g}(p,x)-C\geq C.

(5.1)

For any
y\notin\bar{B}(p,r)
, we claim that there exists
x\in\partial B(p,r)
such that
\varphi^{b}(x)=y
for some
b>0
, where
\{\varphi^{t}\}
is a family of diffeomorphisms generated by
\nabla f/|\nabla f|^{2}
with
\varphi^{0}=\mathrm{id}
.

If this is not true, it implies that
\varphi^{t}(y)\notin\bar{B}(p,r)
for any
t\leq 0
. Them, for all
t\leq 0
,

\displaystyle f(\varphi^{t}(y))=f(y)+t.

This implies there exists
t_{0}<0
such that
f(\varphi^{t_{0}}(y))<0
, which contradicts the assumption that
0
is the minimum value of
f
.

By definition,
f(y)=f(x)+b
. Let
d(t)=d_{g}(p,\varphi^{t}(x))
. For any
t\in[0,b]
, we have

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa d(t)-C},

where the last term is positive by (
5.1
). Integrating this inequality yields:

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2}(d^{2}(b)-d^{2}(0))-C(d(b)-d(0))\leq b=f(y)-f(x).

Since
f(x)\geq 0
and
d(0)\leq r
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Given
(M^{n},g_{0},f_{0},p_{0})\in\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
, we consider the Ricci flow
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
with
g(0)=g_{0}
. The existence of the Ricci flow flow is guaranteed by
[
48
]
since
|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})|\leq A
by our assumption. Furthermore, from
[
16
, Lemma 6.1]
, there exists a constant
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,A)=c(n)/A\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Rm}(g(t))|\leq 2A

for
t\in[0,\epsilon]
. To handle the potentially noncompact manifold
M
, we need the following exhaustion function, the existence of which is established in
[
49
, Lemma 4.5]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a smooth function
\psi
on
M
and a constant
C=C(n,A)>0
such that on
M\times[0,\epsilon]
,

\displaystyle C^{-1}(1+d_{t}(p_{0},x))\leq\psi(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+d_{t}(p_{0},x)),

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi|+|\nabla^{2}\psi|

\displaystyle\leq C.

We also need the following heat kernel estimate from
[
10
, Corollary 5.6]
:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
H(x,t,y,s)
be the heat kernel of Ricci flow
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,\epsilon]}
. Then,

\displaystyle H(x,t,y,0)\leq\frac{C}{|B_{g_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})|}e^{-\frac{d_{0}^{2}(x,y)}{Ct}},

for
C=C(n,A)>0
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the volume with respect to
g_{0}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

For the Ricci flow
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,\epsilon]}
, there exists a solution of the heat equation

\displaystyle\square f=0,\quad f(0)=f_{0},

(5.2)

on
M\times[0,\epsilon]
, where
\square:=\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t}
, such that the following properties hold:

(a)

There exists
C=C(n,A)>0
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq f(x,t)\leq C\left(f_{0}(x)+d_{0}(p_{0},x)+1\right)

(5.3)

for all
(x,t)\in M\times[0,\epsilon]
. In particular,

\displaystyle 0\leq f(x,t)\leq C\left(d_{0}^{2}(p_{0},x)+1\right).

(5.4)

(b)

For any constant
\alpha>0
, the following integral is finite:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla f(x)|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}f(x)|^{2}\right)e^{-\alpha d_{0}^{2}(p_{0},x)}\,dV_{t}(x)dt<\infty.

(5.5)

Proof.

We define
f
as

\displaystyle f(x,t)=\int_{M}H(x,t,y,0)f_{0}(y)\,dV_{0}(y).

(a): The function
f
is well-defined by Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.5
. To verify this, we estimate
f(x,t)
as follows. Set
B_{k}=B_{g_{0}}(x,k\sqrt{t})
and write

\displaystyle 0\leq f(x,t)\leq\frac{C}{|B_{1}|}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}e^{-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{C}}f_{0}(y)\,dV_{0}(y).

Using Lemma
5.2
,
|\nabla f_{0}|\leq A(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+k\sqrt{t})
on
B_{k}
. Hence, for
y\in B_{k}
,

\displaystyle f_{0}(y)\leq f_{0}(x)+A(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+k\sqrt{t})k\sqrt{t}\leq f_{0}(x)+Ak(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+k).

Substituting this into the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle f(x,t)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|B_{1}|}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|B_{k}|\left(f_{0}(x)+Ak(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+k)\right)e^{-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{C}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{Ck}\left(f_{0}(x)+Ak(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+k)\right)e^{-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{C}},

where we have used the volume comparison estimate
|B_{k}|\leq Ce^{Ck}|B_{1}|
. Thus,
f(x,t)
satisfies the bound

\displaystyle f(x,t)\leq C\left(f_{0}(x)+d_{0}(p_{0},x)+1\right).

In addition, (
5.4
) follows from Lemma
5.2
.

(b): We fix a cutoff function
\eta
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\eta=1
on
(-\infty,1]
and
\eta=0
on
[2,\infty)
. Using this, we define

\displaystyle\phi^{r}(x):=\eta\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{r}\right)

for
r\geq 1
, where
\psi(x)
is the exhaustion function from Lemma
5.4
. From the properties of
\psi(x)
, it follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi^{r}(x)|+|\nabla^{2}\phi^{r}(x)|\leq Cr^{-1}\leq C.

(5.6)

Next, we compute:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int f^{2}\phi\,dV_{t}=\int(\square f^{2})\phi^{r}-(\square^{*}\phi^{r})f^{2}\,dV_{t}

where
\square^{*}:=-\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t}+R
. Note that

\displaystyle\square f^{2}=-2|\nabla f|^{2},

and using (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle|\square^{*}\phi^{r}|\leq C.

Integrating over time, we obtain for
r\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{M}2|\nabla f|^{2}\phi^{r}\,dV_{t}dt\leq\left.\int f^{2}\phi^{r}\,dV_{t}\right|_{t=0}+C\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathrm{supp}(\phi^{r})}f^{2}\,dV_{t}dt

From (
5.4
) and Lemma
5.4
, the right-hand side is bounded by

\displaystyle C(1+r^{2})^{2}|B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},Cr)|,

where we have used the fact that
dV_{t}
is comparable to
dV_{0}
. Thus, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},r)}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dV_{t}dt\leq C(1+r^{2})^{2}|B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},Cr)|.

(5.7)

Using the volume comparison, this implies:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{M}|\nabla f(x)|^{2}e^{-\alpha d_{0}^{2}(p_{0},x)}\,dV_{t}(x)dt<\infty.

(5.8)

For the second term in (
5.5
), note that
\square|\nabla f|^{2}=-2|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}
. Following a similar process as above, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{M}2|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\phi^{r}\,dV_{t}dt\leq\left.\int|\nabla f|^{2}\phi^{r}\,dV_{t}\right|_{t=0}+C\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathrm{supp}(\phi^{r})}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dV_{t}dt.

Using Lemma
5.2
and (
5.7
), we conclude:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},r)}|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\,dV_{t}dt\leq C(1+r^{2}+r^{4})|B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},Cr)|,

and thus:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}f(x)|^{2}e^{-\alpha d_{0}^{2}(p_{0},x)}\,dV_{t}(x)dt<\infty.

(5.9)

Combining (
5.8
) and (
5.9
), we have completed the proof of (
5.5
).
∎

From now on,
f
denotes the solution of (
5.2
) obtained in Proposition
5.6
. Next, we prove:

Proposition 5.7
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,A)>0
such that on
M\times[0,\epsilon]
,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}f|\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla f(x,t)|\leq

\displaystyle C\left(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+1\right).

(5.10)

Proof.

We begin by considering the evolution equation for
\nabla^{2}f
:

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{L})(\nabla^{2}f)=\nabla^{2}(\square f)=0,

where
\Delta_{L}
denotes the Lichnerowicz Laplacian. Using the curvature bounds, we compute:

\displaystyle\square|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\leq-2|\nabla^{3}f|^{2}+C|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\leq C|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}.

By the maximum principle (see
[
16
, Theorem 7.42]
), we conclude

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}f|\leq C.

Note that the assumptions required for applying the maximum principle are guaranteed by Proposition
5.6
(b).

Next, since
\square|\nabla f|^{2}=-2|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla f(x,t)|^{2}\leq\int H(x,t,y,0)|\nabla f_{0}(y)|^{2}\,dV_{0}(y).

With the help of Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.4
, and following a similar argument as in the proof of (
5.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle|\nabla f(x,t)|^{2}\leq C(d_{0}(p_{0},x)+1)^{2}.

∎

Proposition 5.8
.

There exists a small constant
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(n,A,\kappa)\leq\min\{\epsilon,\frac{1}{4\kappa}\}
such that on
M\times[0,\epsilon_{1}]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g(t))+\frac{1}{1-2\kappa t}\nabla^{2}_{g(t)}f(t)\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}g(t).

Proof.

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{L})\left((1-2\kappa t)\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f-\kappa g\right)=0,

(5.11)

where
\Delta_{L}
is the Lichnerowicz Laplacian. Define the
(0,2)
-tensor

\displaystyle h=(1-2\kappa t)\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f-\kappa g,

and let
\lambda
denote the minimal eigenvalue of
h
. By assumption,
\lambda\geq 0
at
t=0
.

Fix
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times[0,\epsilon]
, and let
v
be a unit eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Extend
v
to be constant in time for
t\in[t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta]
, and then extend it spatially along each geodesics originating at
(x_{0},t)
, using parallel transport with respect to
g(t)
.

Define

\displaystyle\phi(x,t)=h(v(x,t),v(x,t)),

which is defined on a small spacetime neighborhood of
(x_{0},t_{0})
. By substituting into the evolution equation (
5.11
), we find that at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi=\Delta\phi+2\mathrm{Rm}(h)(v,v)-2g(\mathrm{Rc}(v),h(v))\geq\Delta\phi-C(n,A,\kappa),

where the last inequality follows from the curvature bounds and the estimate (
5.10
).

Thus, in the barrier sense, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\lambda\geq\Delta\lambda-C(n,A,\kappa).

Applying the maximum principle, we conclude that

\displaystyle(1-2\kappa t)\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f\geq(\kappa-Ct)g.

Therefore, the conclusion follows if
t
is small.
∎

We also need the following Shi-type estimates for higher-order terms (see
[
47
]
).

Proposition 5.9
.

For each
k\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C_{k}=C_{k}(n,A)>0
such that for
t\in(0,\epsilon]
,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{Rm}|^{2}+|\nabla^{k+2}f|^{2}\leq C_{k}t^{-k-1}.

We now prove the following result.

Theorem 5.10
.

For any
(M^{n},g_{0},f_{0},p_{0})\in\mathcal{M}(n,A,\kappa)
, there exists
(M^{n},g,f,p)
satisfying the following properties:

(a)

There exists
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}(n,A,\kappa)>0
such that

\displaystyle(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{M}(n,A^{\prime},\kappa/2).

(b)

For each
k\geq 0
, there exists
C_{k}=C_{k}(n,A,\kappa)>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{g}^{k}\mathrm{Rm}(g)|^{2}+|\nabla_{g}^{k+2}f|^{2}\leq C_{k}.

(c)

There exists
C=C(n,A,\kappa)>1
such that

\displaystyle C^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},1)\right)\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\left(B_{g}(p,1)\right)\leq C\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},1)\right).

Proof.

For the constant
\epsilon_{1}
in Proposition
5.8
, we define

\displaystyle g:=g(\cdot,\epsilon_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad f:=\frac{1}{1-2\kappa\epsilon_{1}}f(\cdot,\epsilon_{1}).

From Proposition
5.8
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g)+\nabla_{g}^{2}f\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}g.

(5.12)

Additionally, from Propositions
5.7
and
5.6
(a):

\displaystyle|\nabla_{g}^{2}f|\leq C(n,A,\kappa)\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla_{g}f(p_{0})|+f(p_{0})\leq C(n,A,\kappa).

(5.13)

Using the arguments in Lemma
5.2
, we deduce for any
x\in M
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{g}f(x)|\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}d_{g}(p_{0},x)-C(n,A,\kappa).

As
f\geq 0
, it follows (via a similar approach to Lemma
5.3
) that:

\displaystyle f(x)\geq\frac{\kappa}{4}d_{g}^{2}(p_{0},x)-C(n,A,\kappa)d_{g}(p_{0},x)-C(n,A,\kappa).

Consequently,
f
attains its minimum at some point
p\in M
, and

\displaystyle d_{g}(p,p_{0})\leq C(n,A,\kappa).

By adding a constant to
f
, we can normalize
f(p)=0
.

Assertion (a) follows directly from (
5.12
) and (
5.13
). Assertion (b) is established using Proposition
5.9
. Finally, assertion (c) follows from the standard volume comparison argument, using the fact that
d_{g_{0}}
and
d_{g}
(as well as
dV_{g_{0}}
and
dV_{g}
) are comparable.
∎

With these preparations, we are now ready to prove Theorem
1.4
, restated here for convenience.

Theorem 5.11
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,A,\kappa)>0
such that for any
(M^{n},g,f,p)\in\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g}\left(B_{g}(p,1)\right)\geq C.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction. Assume there exists a sequence
(M_{i}^{n},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\in\mathcal{N}(n,A,\kappa)
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}\left(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1)\right)\to 0.

By Theorem
5.10
, we may assume that for some constants
C_{k}=C_{k}(n,A,\kappa)
,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{g_{i}}^{k}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{i})|^{2}+|\nabla_{g_{i}}^{k+2}f_{i}|^{2}\leq C_{k}.

The rest of the proof follows verbatim as the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The estimates for the potential function and its gradient are derived using Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.4
.

The main distinction here is the absence of the Ricci shrinker equation (
1.1
). Instead, we rely on the weaker inequality:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g_{i})+\nabla^{2}_{g_{i}}f_{i}\geq\kappa g_{i}.

This affects the formulation of (
4.14
), which now becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(h^{\alpha})+\nabla_{h^{\alpha}}^{2}(f^{\alpha})\geq\kappa h^{\alpha}.

However, the key inequalities (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) remain unaffected, except for replacing the coefficient
1/2
with
\kappa
. Therefore, we derive the same contradiction as before.
∎

On the existence of extremal solutions for the conjugate discrete-time Riccati equation
Chun-Yueh Chiang

Abstract
In this paper we consider a class of conjugate discrete-time Riccati equations (CDARE), arising originally from the linear quadratic regulation problem for discrete-time antilinear systems. Recently, we have proved the existence of the maximal solution to the CDARE with a nonsingular control weighting matrix under the framework of the constructive method. Our contribution in the work is to finding another meaningful Hermitian solutions, which has received little attention in this topic. Moreover, we show that some extremal solutions cannot be attained at the same time, and almost (anti-)stabilizing solutions coincide with some extremal solutions. It is to be expected that our theoretical results presented in this paper will play an important role in the optimal control problems for discrete-time antilinear systems.

Introduction

A conjugate
discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation (CDARE) is the matrix equation

\displaystyle X=\mathcal{R}(X):=A^{H}\overline{X}A-A^{H}\overline{X}BR_{X}^{-1}B^{H}\overline{X}A+H,

(1a)

or its equivalent expression

\displaystyle X=A^{H}\overline{X}(I+G\overline{X})^{-1}A+H,

(1b)

where
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
,
R\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
is nonsingular,

H=CC^{H}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
with
C\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
I
is the identity matrix of compatible size,

G:=BR^{-1}B^{H}
,
R_{X}:=R+B^{H}\overline{X}B
and the unknown
X\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{R})
, respectively. Here,
\mathbb{H}_{\ell}
denotes the set of all
\ell\times\ell
Hermitian matrices and
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{R}):=\{X\in\mathbb{H}_{n}\,|\,\det(R_{X})\neq 0\}
. We also notice that

R_{X}
is nonsingular if and only if
\det(I+G\overline{X})\neq 0
.

A class of CDAREs (
1
) arises from the linear quadratic regulation (LQR) optimal control problem for the discrete-time antilinear system
of the state space representation

x_{k+1}=A\overline{x}_{k}+B\overline{u}_{k},\quad k\geq 0,

(2)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the state response and
u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the control input.
The main goal of this control problem is to find a state feedback control
u_{k}=-Fx_{k}
such that the performance index

\mathcal{J}(u_{k},x_{0}):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\begin{bmatrix}x_{k}^{H}&u_{k}^{H}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}H&\\
&R\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{k}\\
u_{k}\end{bmatrix}

is minimized with
H\geq 0
and
R>0
. If the antilinear system (
2
) is controllable
[
11
, Theorem 12.7]
or the matrix pair
(A\overline{A},\begin{bmatrix}B&A\overline{B}\end{bmatrix})
is stabilizable
[
12
, Theorem 2]
, the optimal state feedback controller is
u_{k}^{*}:=-R_{X_{*}}^{-1}B^{H}\mathbf{\overline{Z}_{M}}Ax_{k}
for
k\geq 0
and thus
the minimum value of
\mathcal{J}(u_{k}^{*},x_{0})=x_{0}^{H}\mathbf{Z_{M}}x_{0}
is achieved,
where
\mathbf{Z_{M}}:=\max(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{N}_{n})
is the unique positive solution of the CDARE (
1a
). Here, the notation
\mathbb{N}_{n}
collects all positive semidefinite matrices with size
n
and all Hermitian solution of CDARE (
1
) is denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{=}
.
The positive definite solution of CDARE (
1
) has been an active area of research and CDARE (
1
) is also called the
discrete-time algebraic anti-Riccati equation

[
10
,
11
]
. For more details see
[
11
,
12
]
.

In the past few years, the authors
[
5
,
6
]
discovered a way to construct the existence of the maximal solution
\mathbf{X_{M}}:=\max(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{P})
for the CDARE (
1
) with
G,H\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
. Here, the notation
\mathbb{P}
collects any Hermitian matrix
X
such that
R_{X}>0
. More precisely,
a class of the fixed-point iteration (FPI) has been utilized to compute
\mathbf{X_{M}}
under the weaker assumptions. Further exploitation in the convergence result of the FPI appeared in recent work
[
6
]
or see the below Theorem
2.1
. It is clear that
\mathbf{X_{M}}\geq\mathbf{Z_{M}}
when
G,H\geq 0
since
\mathbb{N}_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
. However,
\mathbf{X_{M}}-\mathbf{Z_{M}}
may be indefinite in general if they exist. One aim of this work is to establish some appropriate conditions for the existence and relation to all meaningful solutions including
\mathbf{X_{M}}
and
\mathbf{Z_{M}}
.

Based on these recent works, it is natural to ask whether another meaningful extremal solution
\mathbf{X_{m}}:=\max(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{P})
,
\mathbf{Y_{M}}:=\max(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{E})
and
\mathbf{Y_{m}}:=\min(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{E})
exist. Here, the notation
\mathbb{E}
collects any Hermitian matrix
X
such that
R_{X}<0
.
In analogy with some particular Hermitian solutions of discrete-time algebraic Riccati equation(DARE)
[
4
,
5
]
, another interesting issue including almost stabilizing, almost anti-stabilizing, positive semidefinite and negative semidefinite solutions will also be defined and investigated. For the sake of explanation, the maximal element of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{N}_{n}
, the minimal element of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{N}_{n}
, the maximal element of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap-\mathbb{N}_{n}
, the minimal element of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap-\mathbb{N}_{n}
, the maximal element of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{E}
and the minimal element of

are denoted by
\mathbf{\mathbf{Z_{M}}}
,
\mathbf{Z_{m}}
,
\mathbf{\mathbf{W_{M}}}
,
\mathbf{W_{m}}
,
\mathbf{X_{a.s.}}
and

As expected, we shall provide a constructive mathod to guarantee the existence of all extremal and almost (anti-)stabilizing solutions under reasonable assumptions, which generalizes the previously works. Two kinds of fixed-point iterations are proposed for finding all extremal solutions via the
two auxiliary algebraic Riccati equations.
Precisely, starting with
X_{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
,
the sequence
\{X_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
generated by the following FPI of the form

X_{k+1}=\mathcal{R}(X_{k}),\quad k\geq 0,

(3)

will be used in the following content of the paper.
With the help of the concept of FPI (
3
),
it is shown in Theorem 3.1 of
[
6
]
that the existence of the maximal element of
\mathbb{R}_{\leq}\cap\mathbb{P}
to the CDARE (
1
) can be constructed iteratively by FPI (
3
), which is exactly equal to
\mathbf{X_{M}}=\max(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{P})
.
Analogous to Theorem 3.1 of
[
6
]
, one can easily obtain the minimal element of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq}\cap\mathbb{E}
to the CDARE (
1a
) based on the framework of the FPI (
3
). Later in Section 2, we will describe in more detail.

It is interesting to ask whether the minimal element
\mathbf{X_{m}}
of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{P}
or the maximal element
\mathbf{Y_{M}}
of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{E}

can be established in a unified framework of the fixed-point iteration (
3
)? The following example gives a counterexample to this question. Namely, there is a possibility that the CDARE (
1
) may has a minimal solution
\mathbf{X_{m}}
for which
\mathbf{X_{m}}=\min(\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{P})
. However, the FPI (
3
) with any initial
X_{0}
doesn’t converge to
\mathbf{X_{m}}
.

Example 1.1
.

We consider a scalar CDARE (
1
) with
n=m=1
of the form

x=|a|^{2}\bar{x}-\frac{|a|^{2}\bar{x}^{2}|b|^{2}}{r+|b|^{2}\bar{x}}+h=\frac{|a|^{2}\bar{x}}{1+g\bar{x}}+h,

where
a=e^{\frac{\pi}{6}i}
,
b=e^{\frac{\pi}{3}i}
,
r=h=1
and thus
g=|b|^{2}/r=1
. Therefore, it has exactly two Hermitian solutions
\mathbf{x_{M}}=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}>\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=\mathbf{x_{m}}
. Note that
\mathbf{x_{m}}
and
\mathbf{x_{M}}
both are elements of
\mathbb{R}_{=}\cap\mathbb{P}=\{\mathbf{x_{m}},\mathbf{x_{M}}\}
. It is easy to check that the equivalent expressions of the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
generated by the FPI (
3
) with any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
can be rewritten in the explicit form

\displaystyle x_{k}=\mathbf{x_{M}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{5}(x_{0}-\mathbf{x_{M}})t^{k}}{x_{0}-\mathbf{x_{m}}-(x_{0}-\mathbf{x_{M}})t^{k}},

provided that
x_{k}
exists for
k\geq 0
, where
t=\frac{7-3\sqrt{5}}{2}\in(-1,1)
. It is immediate that the FPI (
3
) with any
x_{0}
converges to the maximal solution
\mathbf{x_{M}}
.

The above example observes that the existence of
\mathbf{X_{m}}
(and similar to
\mathbf{Y_{M}}
) is not ensured by a unifying treatment
based the construction of FPI (
3
). This is the motivational thought that drives the study in this paper.

2.1
Some useful definitions, theory and notations

For any
M,N\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
, the positive definite and positive semidefinite matrices are denoted by
M>0
and
M\geq 0
, respectively.
Moreover, we usually denote by
M\geq N
(or
M\leq N

M-N\geq 0
(or

[M,N]
to denote the set,

\{X\in\mathbb{H}_{n}|M\leq X\leq N\}
when

For the sake of simplicity, the spectrum and spectral radius of

are
denoted by
\sigma(A)
and
\rho(A)
, respectively, and
\mu(A):=\min\{|\lambda|\ |\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}

We denote, the complement of the open unit disk by
\mathbb{D}_{\geq}
, the closed unit disk by
\mathbb{D}_{\leq}
and
the unit disk by

Given a subset

Definition 2.1
.

The following definitions are given for

1.

-S:=\{-X;X\in S\}
and
\overline{S}:=\{\overline{X};X\in S\}

2.

3.

4.

A matrix operator

is order preserving (resp. to reversing) on
S

(resp. to
f(A)\leq f(B)
) when

5.

(resp.
S_{L}

(resp. to

We introduce the following famous results which we need in the rest of this paper. The results in the following lemma either follow immediately from the definition or are easy to verify.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
2
]

Let
A
be an arbitrary matrix of size
n
.
X
and
Y
are two
n\times n
positive definite matrices. Then,

1.

Sherman Morrison Woodbury formula (SMWF):

Assume that
Y^{-1}\pm AX^{-1}A^{H}
is nonsingular. Then,
X\pm A^{H}YA
is invertible and

(X\pm A^{H}YA)^{-1}=X^{-1}\mp X^{-1}A^{H}(Y^{-1}\pm AX^{-1}A^{H})^{-1}AX^{-1}.

2.

A Schur complement condition for positive definiteness and positive semidefiniteness:

A square complex matrix
\Psi
is partitioned as

\Psi:=\begin{bmatrix}X&A\\
A^{H}&Y\end{bmatrix}.

Then,
\Psi>0
(resp.
\Psi\geq 0
) if and only if the Schur complement
\Psi/X:=Y-A^{H}X^{-1}A>0
(resp.
Y-A^{H}X^{-1}A\geq 0
) if and only if the Schur complement
\Psi/Y:=X-AY^{-1}A^{H}>0
(resp.
X-AY^{-1}A^{H}\geq 0
).

Theorem 3.3

The

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
C=A\oplus B\in\mathbb{C}^{(n+m)\times(n+m)}
with
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
. Then,

1.

A\geq 0
if
C\geq U^{H}BU
for some
U\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times(n+m)}
.

2.

B\leq 0
if
C\leq V^{H}AV
for some
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times(n+m)}
.

Proof.

We give only the proof of the first part, the same proof remains valid for second part. For the sake of simplicity, we partition
U
as
U=\begin{bmatrix}U_{1}&U_{2}\end{bmatrix}
with
U_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and
U_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
. If there exist nonzero vector
x_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
so that
x_{1}^{H}Ax_{1}<0
. Choose a scalar
\lambda\not\in\sigma(U_{2})
and
|\lambda|=1
. Let
x=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
with
x_{2}=(\lambda I-U_{2})^{-1}U_{1}x_{1}
. Thus,
Ux=U_{1}x_{1}+U_{2}x_{2}=(I+U_{2}(\lambda I-U_{2})^{-1})U_{1}x_{1}=\lambda x_{2}
.
It follows from a direct computation that

\displaystyle x_{1}^{H}Ax_{1}+x_{2}^{H}Bx_{2}=x^{H}(A\oplus B)x\geq(Ux)^{H}B(Ux)=x_{2}^{H}Bx_{2},

which yields
x_{1}^{H}Ax_{1}\geq 0
and a contradiction occurs.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}_{=}\cap\widehat{\mathbb{P}}\neq\phi

\widehat{\mathbb{R}}_{=}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{P}}

Proof.

Let the resolvent of
\widehat{A}

\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)=(\lambda I-\widehat{A})^{-1}

denotes the unit disk. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}_{=}\backslash\sigma(\widehat{A})

I+\widehat{A}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)=\lambda\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)
. Thus,

(I+\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}\widehat{A}^{H})X(I+\widehat{A}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda))=\bar{\lambda}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}X\lambda\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)=\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}X\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)
and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}\mathcal{S}_{\widehat{A}}(X)\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)=X+\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}\widehat{A}^{H}X+X\widehat{A}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda).

(24)

The matrix
\widehat{R}_{X}
is nonsingular if

. For the sake of simplicity, the matrix operator
\phi_{X}:\mathbb{D}_{=}\backslash\sigma(\widehat{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be defined by

\widehat{H}=X-\widehat{A}^{H}X\widehat{A}+\widehat{F}_{X}^{H}\widehat{R}_{X}\widehat{F}_{X}

if

. Observe that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}\widehat{H}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}(X-\widehat{A}^{H}X\widehat{A}+\widehat{F}_{X}^{H}\widehat{R}_{X}\widehat{F}_{X})\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=X+\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}\widehat{A}^{H}X+X\widehat{A}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)+\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda)^{H}\widehat{F}_{X}^{H}\widehat{R}_{X}\widehat{F}_{X}\varphi_{\widehat{A}}(\lambda).

(25)

A direct calculation together with (
25

here we using

As we have seen in Theorem

For this reason, the section provides a detailed exposition of the relationship between extremal solutions and a specific solutions

.

1.

\mathbf{X_{s}}\in\mathbb{R}_{=}

(\rho(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{X_{s}}})\leq 1)

\mathbf{X_{a.s}}\in\mathbb{R}_{=}

\mu(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{X_{a.s}}})>1

(\mu(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{X_{a.s}}})\geq 1)

Definition 5.2
.

We call that the matrix pair

.

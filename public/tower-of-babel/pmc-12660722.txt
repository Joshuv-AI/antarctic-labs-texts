m6A regulators-based gene expression pattern is associated with immune microenvironment characteristics in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Wenjuan Wang, Erxiong Wang, Kunqiao Hong, Wei Jiang, Banlai Ruan, Jie Liu, Jinping Tian, Feng Ding, Qiuyun Guo, Qianshan Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To evaluate the relationship between N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification-related genes and the immune microenvironment characteristics of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and to evaluate the potential of targeting m6A regulators to sensitize HCC cells to immunotherapy. RNA sequencing data and clinicopathologic data of HCC patients were obtained from TCGA and ICGC database. Based on the expression levels of 23 m6A modification regulators, the HCC samples were divided into three m6A clusters by ConsensusClusterPlus algorithm. Survival analysis and clinical features was explored in different clusters. Next, univariate Cox analysis and LASSO regression analysis were applied to screened out 4 immune-related prognostic genes from the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the three clusters, and a risk model was established. Next, Kaplan-Meier plot, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC), multivariable Cox regression analysis were applied to evaluate the prognostic value of risk model. The relationship between the immune microenvironment and the risk model was estimated using the ESTIMATE method and single sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA). Finally, immunohistochemistry was applied to explore the correlation between the expression levels of PD-L1 and the key m6A regulators METTL3 and YTHDF1. Most m6A regulators were highly expressed in HCC tissues, and positively correlated with each other. The patients in cluster 3 showed the worse prognosis. 449 of the 710 DEGs were immune regulators, and from the 449 genes, a risk model consisting of 4 m6A-related genes (DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA) was established, which was associated with immune microenvironment characteristics of HCC tissues. The risk model was identified as an independent prognostic factor for the overall survival of HCC patients. Higher levels of YTHDF1 and METTL3 expression in HCC tissues were associated with higher expression of PD-L1. m6A modification participates in regulating immune microenvironment of HCC, and targeting m6A may block the immune escape of HCC cells.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26956-4.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common cancers worldwide, and is associated with high mortality and mobility1. Worldwide, the mortality of HCC is still increasing, especially in East and Southeast Asia and parts of Africa2. It is estimated that more than 40% of HCC cases in the world occur in China3. The onset of HCC is insidious, and most patients are in the advanced stage when they are diagnosed2,3. For the patients who cannot be treated surgically, the benefits of chemotherapy and radiotherapy are limited2,3.

Immunotherapy represents an attractive strategy to improve the prognosis of HCC patients. Current immunotherapies under investigation/in application mainly include immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI), the adoptive transfer of immune cells, bispecific antibodies, vaccines, and oncolytic viruses4. These anti-cancer strategies are believed to be revolutionary progress in cancer research and treatment. Specifically, programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1), and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) are two targets of ICIs, and some patients show satisfactory response to these treatments, and more ICIs are being developed and tested in clinical trials5,6. Nonetheless, the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment of HCC hampers the application of these therapeutic approaches7. Clarifying the mechanism of immune microenvironment characteristics of HCC, and deciphering the related mechanism, are indispensable for the progress on HCC immunotherapy.

N6-methyladenosine (m6A), the methylation of the adenosine base at the nitrogen-6 position of RNA, is the most abundant epigenetic modification pattern in eukaryotic RNA8. m6A modification is a dynamic and reversible enzymatic reaction process, which is mediated by m6A methyltransferase complexes (writers), m6A demethylases (erasers) and m6A reading proteins (readers)9. Accumulating studies have reported that m6A modification is widely distributed in mRNA and non-coding RNA (ncRNA), regulating mRNA splicing, processing and maturation of microRNA (miRNA), and transcriptional inhibition mediated by long non-coding RNA (lncRNA)10,11. In recent years, m6A modification has become the hot spot in cancer research, and importantly, the dysregulation of m6A modification has been linked with the tumorigenesis and progression of HCC, and multiple regulators of m6A modification are identified as cancer-related proteins in HCC12–15.

Recently, some studies report that m6A modification patterns are associated with the microenvironment immune profiles of multiple cancers including HCC16–19. For example, a recent study reports that high YTH N6-methyladenosine RNA binding protein F1 (YTHDF1) expression is closely associated with low CD3 + and CD8 + T cell infiltration in HCC16; besides, it is reported that m6A regulator, SNRPC is a potential prognostic biomarker for HCC, and is may predict the responses to immunotherapy17. However, these studies mentioned above are limited by the analysis of one or several m6A regulators; additionally, there is no evidence to support the association between m6A characteristics and the expression level of immunotherapy biomarker PD-L1 in HCC.

In the present work, using transcriptomics and genomic data, based on m6A modification-related genes, HCC patients are grouped into three subtypes, namely m6A cluster 1, m6A cluster 2 and m6A cluster 3. We demonstrated that, m6A modification pattern was significantly associated with the prognosis of HCC patients. Interestingly, it was revealed that a lot of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the three clusters were associated with immune processes. From the DEGs, we developed a prognosis prediction model consisting of 4 genes [deoxynucleotidyltransferase terminal interacting protein 2 (DNTTIP2), selenophosphate synthetase 1 (SEPHS1), treacle ribosome biogenesis factor 1 (TCOF1) and taxilin alpha (TXLNA)], which was associated with immune microenvironment characteristics of HCC tissues. Also, with histological evidence, we demonstrated that the expression levels of two m6A regulators, methyltransferase 3 (METTL3) and YTHDF1, are associated with PD-L1 expression in HCC tissues. Our study supports that m6A modification participates in regulating immune microenvironment characteristics of HCC. m6A regulators-based gene expression pattern may contribute to decision-making in the immunotherapy of HCC, and targeting m6A may block the immune escape of HCC cells and increase the sensitivity of HCC cells to immunotherapy. The present work integrates immune-related DEGs into a risk model, and validates the relationship between m6A modification and PD-L1 expression with histological evidence, which may provides new clues for the prognosis evaluation and immunotherapy sensitization in HCC diagnosis and treatment.

Methods and materials

Collecting and processing data

The RNA sequencing data, somatic mutation and copy number variate (CNV) data and corresponding clinicopathological features of HCC patients were download from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database via the GDC data portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), and the raw data of 371 HCC samples and 50 normal samples were collected. To reduce the batch effect, transcriptome data were converted into transcripts per million (TPM) format and then normalized using the SVA program. For the purpose of further validating the results, the LIRI-JP data were also obtained as the testing dataset from the International Cancer Genomics Consortium (ICGC) database (https://dcc.icgc.org/), and the transcriptomics data and clinical information of a Chinese cohort in GSE14520, were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). 1534 immune-related genes were retrieved from the The Immunology Database and Analysis Portal (ImmPort) database (https://www.immport.org/home).

Mutation landscape and copy number variations (CNV) Estimation of m6A regulators in HCC

23 m6A regulators including 8 “writers” (METTL3, METTL14, METTL16, RBM15, RBM15B, WTAP, ZC3H13, KIAA1429), 13 “readers” (YTHDC1, YTHDF1, YTHDC2, YTHDF3, IGF2BP2, LRPPRC, YTHDF2, HNRNPA2B1, HNRNPC, RBMX, EIF3A, G3BP1 and FXR1), and 2 “erasers” (ALKBH5 and FTO) were included in the initial analysis20. The somatic landscape and CNV of m6A regulators were explored using the R package “Maftools” and GISTIC algorithm, and then the location of the genes of m6A regulators on chromosomes was marked using the R package “RCircos.”

Differential expression analysis and identification of prognosis-related m6A regulators

GeneMANIA was used to visualize the interaction and co-expression relationship of the 23 m6A regulators. Next, we analyze the differential expression of m6A modification regulators between normal liver tissues and HCC tissues by “Limma” package in R. Then, in order to evaluate the relationship between m6A regulators and the patients’ prognosis, Kaplan-Meier analysis and univariate Cox regression were applied.

Unsupervised consensus clustering analysis

“ConcensusClusterPlus” package in R was applied to perform an unsupervised consensus clustering analysis based on the expression profile of 23 m6A modification regulators. In order to achieve classification stability, the parameters with 1,000 iterations and an 80% resampling rate were applied to divide the the HCC patients in the TCGA cohort into three distinct clusters. Subsequently, The Kaplan–Meier survival analysis were used to assess the survival difference among the three clusters.

Establishment of the m6A-related risk model

“Limma” package in R was used to filter out the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the 3 clusters (cluster 1 vs. cluster 2, cluster 2 vs. cluster 3, cluster 1 vs. cluster 3), with cutoffs of |fold change| > 1 and p < 0.05. Following that, the m6A-related immune genes were obtained by collecting the genes in the intersection of the immune-related genes and the DEGs in m6A clusters. Then, using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression analysis and multivariate Cox regression analyses, finally 4 prognostic m6A-related immune genes (DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA) were obtained, to construct the risk model. The calculated risk score for HCC patients by according the following formula: risk score =∑(coefficienti × expression of signature genei).

Prognostic analysis of m6A modification-based risk model

According to the median of risk score, the HCC patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups. To compare the different prognostic outcomes in the low-risk group and high-risk group, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank test were used. Subsequently, univariate Cox analysis was conducted to show the prospective prognostic indicators and multivariate Cox analysis was used to evaluate whether the risk score and clinical parameters as the potential independent risk factors for the survival of HCC patients. Then, using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under curve (AUC), the performance of the model in predicting the prognosis of HCC patients was further evaluated.

Immune infiltration analysis

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) to assess the relative abundance of various immune cell subtype in different m6A clusters using the “GSVA” R package. To compared the Stromal score, Immune score and ESTIMATE score between high- and low-risk groups, the “estimate” package was ultilized. In addition, the correlation coefficient was calculated using the “corrplot” package to explore the relation between immune cells and the risk score.

Tissue microarray (TMA)

TMA slides of formalin-fixed paraffin embedded human HCC tissues were provided by Shanghai Outdo Biotech Co.Ltd. The 94 samples included in the TMA were pathologically diagnosed with HCC between 2007 and 2013. The results of immunohistochemistry (IHC) were assessed by two researchers (HKQ and DQS). Staining scores were evaluated according to a previously reported semiquantitative methodology21.

Statistical analysis

This study’s statistical analyses were conducted with R (version 4.2.0). The Wilcoxon test was used to compare the difference between the two groups, and one-way ANOVA was employed to examine the differences among three groups. Spearman’s correlation was used to examine the relationship between two independent variables. *, ** and *** indicated p < 0.05, p<0.01, and p<0.001, respectively.

Results

Landscape of genetic variation and expression characteristics of m6A regulators in HCC

First of all, the potential biological processes modulated by 23 m6A modification regulators were analyzed. It was revealed that the 23 m6A modification regulators were mainly involved in the biological processes about translational initiation, mRNA modification, RNA destabilization, cell cycle progression and so on (Figure S1a). We then explored the copy number changes and mutation status of these regulators in HCC. It was observed that ZC3H13 and YTHDF2 showed the high frequency (in > 15% cases) of copy number deletion, while most of the other regulators were inclined to show gene amplification (Figure S1b, c). In addition, the genes of 19 m6A regulators were mutated in 11.32% of the HCC samples, of which KIAA1429 (8%), HNRNPC (7%) and LRPPRC (7%) were the three genes with the highest mutation frequency, and the mutation frequency of most of the genes was low (< 5%) (Figure S1d). Then the expression levels of the 23 m6A regulators were analyzed in 50 normal liver tissues and 371 HCC tissues, and it was revealed that most m6A regulatory genes had high expression level in HCC tissues compared with that in normal liver tissues (Figure S1e). Notably, correlation analysis indicated that, there were remarkable positive correlation relationships among the expression levels of the 23 genes, and none of them showed negative correlation with each other; and HNRNPA2B1 and HNRNPC showed the strongest correlation with other regulators (Figure S1f). These data implied, in HCC, m6A modification regulators could probably work in concert to modulate crucial biological processes, and most of the regulators exerted oncogenic biological functions.

Grouping of HCC patients based on m6A modification patterns

Next, the analysis based on GeneMANIA showed that most of the 23 m6A modification regulators had physical interaction and co-expression relationship (Fig. 1a). Univariate Cox regression analyses and Kaplan-Meier survival analyses showed that, for most of the 23 regulators, their high expression was associated with the unfavorable prognosis of HCC patients, and 15 of them were associated with shorter overall survival time of the patients (Fig. 1b). These data further supported that the m6A modification regulators in HCC coordinately regulated disease progression as oncogenes. Subsequently, with ConsensusClusterPlus algorithm, based on the expression levels of 23 m6A regulators, the HCC samples were divided into three subgroups, including m6A cluster 1 (n = 149), m6A cluster 2 (n = 161) and m6A cluster 3 (n = 60) (Fig. 1c). Survival analysis showed that the patients in cluster 2 had the best prognosis and the patients in cluster 3 had the worst prognosis (Fig. 1d and e). In addition, the relationship between the clinicopathological characteristics and the m6A clusters was analyzed, and the results showed that the expression pattern of m6A regulators was associated with distinct clinical stage and tissue differentiation status (Fig. 1f, Figure S2).

Construction of a m6A-related gene expression risk model in HCC

Next, the DEGs in the 3 clusters (cluster 1 vs. cluster 2, cluster 2 vs. cluster 3, cluster 1 vs. cluster 3) were compared. There were 1072 DEGs between cluster 1 and cluster 2, and 1680 DEGs between cluster 2 and cluster 3, and 1234 DEGs between cluster 1 and cluster 3; among them, 710 genes were differentially expressed in all of the three clusters (Fig. 2a). Notably, GSVA suggested the immunological processes in the HCC samples from different clusters were also distinct (Figure S3a). Intriguingly, among the 710 DEGs in m6A clusters, there were 449 candidate genes which were also the immune-related genes in HCC (Figure S3b). The 449 genes were enriched in multiple immune processes including antigen processing and presentation of peptide antigen via MHC class II, B cell homeostasis, positive regulation of B cell mediated immunity, positive regulation of adaptive immune response and so on (Fig. 2b). The data mentioned above implied that m6A modification status of HCC tissues could probably regulate immune-related genes, and it was strongly associated with the immune processes in tumor tissues. Based on the 449 genes, with LASSO model, 11 crucial m6A-related immune genes were obtained (Fig. 2c), and with multivariate Cox regression analyses, a risk model was obtained based on the expression levels of DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA, which was associated with immune processes (Fig. 2d). The risk score was calculated using the following formula: risk score= (0.0853 × expression of DNTTIP2) + (0.0469 × expression of SEPHS1) + (0.0816 × expression of TCOF1) + (0.0333 × expression of TXLNA). To validate the 4 genes were related with m6A, the correlations between their expression levels and the expression levels of METTL3 and YTHDF1, two well-known m6A regulators which promotes HCC progression, were analyzed with TCGA data. The results showed that the expression levels of the 4 genes were respectively positively correlated with METTL3 expression and YTHDF1 expression in HCC samples (Figure S4, Figure S5). Additionally, GSEA was performed to analyze the biological functions of the four genes, and the results showed that DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA were enriched in multiple biological processes associated with cancer biology, respectively, including but not limited to cell cycle, DNA replication, mismatch repair, etc. (Figure S6), and the dysregulation of these genes in HCC samples was validated with the data in GSE14520 (Figure S7). The risk score of each HCC sample was calculated, and the samples were accordingly grouped into high-risk group or low-risk group according to the median value. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis suggested that the prognosis of the HCC patients in high-risk group was poor, while the prognosis of patients in low-risk group was relatively favorable, and this result was verified in two independent cohorts (TCGA and ICGC) (Fig. 2e, f), and after the two cohorts were merged, the model was still effective (Fig. 2g). Furthermore, in an additional HCC cohort, GSE14520, the patients with high risk scores also had worse prognosis (Figure S8). These data suggested that, m6A-mediated changes in immune processes in HCC tissues were involved in disease progression.

Validation of the m6A-related risk model as a prognostic evaluation tool in HCC

In both of training cohort and test cohort, most patients in the high-risk group died, while more patients survived in the low-risk group (Fig. 3a, b). In addition, the expression levels of DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA in the high-risk group were higher than those in the low-risk group (Fig. 3c, d). Based on TCGA data, univariate Cox regression analyses showed that clinical stage of the patients, T stage of the patients and risk score were associated with the prognosis, and multivariate Cox regression analyses showed that only risk score was associated with prognosis (Fig. 3e, f); based on ICGC data, univariate Cox regression analyses showed that gender, clinical stage and risk score were associated with the prognosis, and multivariate Cox regression analyses showed that gender, clinical stage, priormalignancy and risk score were associated with the prognosis (Fig. 3g, h). In addition, through ROC analysis, it was revealed that the sensitivity and specificity of the risk model were higher than other clinicopathological features, to predict the prognosis of the patients (Figure S9a, b). These results suggest that the m6A-related gene expression risk model can be used as an independent prognostic model to predict the prognosis of HCC patients.

The relationship between immune microenvironment characteristics and m6A-related risk model

Through the ESTIMATE algorithm, we obtained the Stromal score, Immune score and ESTIMATE score of HCC samples. We found that the Stromal score was significantly lower in the high-risk group, and the Stromal score was also lower in the high-risk group (Figure S10). The association between risk score and various immune cells was then assessed using a heatmap, and the results revealed an evident distinction between the two subtypes (Figure S11a). It was also revealed that low-risk subtype had higher levels of B cells, cytolytic activity, neutrophils, NK cells, and type I and type II IFN responses than high-risk subtype (Figure S11b). Furthermore, immune cell infiltration was analyzed, and it was observed that the majority of immune cells, including B cell plasma, T cell follicular helper, T cell regulatory Tregs, and macrophage M0, were upregulated in the high-risk subtype (Figure S11c). Taken together, these data strongly suggested that the risk score based on m6A-related immune genes was associated with immunological pathways and the infiltration of certain immune cells.

Relationship between the risk score and the expression of immune checkpoint proteins

Additionally, the correlations between the risk score and the expression of immune checkpoint genes were analyzed, and the majority of the immune checkpoint genes were highly expressed in the high-risk group (Figure S12), including the famous immune checkpoint genes associated with tumor immunity (CTLA4, PD-L1, and PD1) (Figure S13a-c). Additionally, the patients with low level of CD8 positive T cells and patients with high levels of macrophage M0 cells had a worse prognosis (Figure S13d-e). We then compared the correlation between DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1, and TXLNA with CD8 positive T cells and macrophage M0 phase. According to the findings of correlation analysis, interestingly, these genes had a negative relationship with macrophage M0, and a positive relationship with CD8 positive T cells (Figure S14a-d), and a negative correlation between the risk score and the enrichment of CD8 positive T cells (Figure S14e), and a positive correlation was also observed between risk score and the enrichment of macrophage M0 (Figure S14f). The Sankey plot showed the relationship between m6A clusters, risk score, immune score and clinical outcome (Fig. 4a). Notably, the risk score of HCC samples in m6A cluster 3 was higher than other clusters (Fig. 4b). It was also found that DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA had higher expression levels in the m6A cluster 3 than other clusters (Fig. 4c). Additionally, CTLA4, PD-L1, and PD1 were all overexpressed in cluster 3 (Fig. 4d). Based on our research, we concluded that the m6A-related risk score was preferable in refining the selection of potential patients for immunotherapy.

Correlation between of the expression of representative m6A regulators and PD-L1 in HCC samples

Finally, the link between the expression of METTL3 and YTHDF1, and PD-L1 expression was investigated by TMA analysis. Discussion

The most prevalent mRNA modification, m6A RNA methylation, is critical in post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression20. The pathogenesis of HCC is considered to be a complex and multifactorial process22. Increasing evidence suggests that m6A methylation is closely related to the initiation and progression of HCC23–25. Specifically, YTHDF1, up-regulated by HIF-1α, promotes the progression of HCC by modulating ATG2A and ATG14 translation and regulating autophagy24. A recent study reports that STM2457, a specific inhibitor of METTL3, improves the sensitivity of HCC cells to lenvatinib, by modulating EGFR, suggesting m6A modification dysregulation contributes to drug resistance of HCC25. In the present work, we demonstrated that m6A regulators play a significant role in the process of cell cycle, mRNA modification, translational initiation, and RNA destabilisation. Additionally, the majority of m6A regulators were highly expressed in HCC and linked to a poor prognosis, and their expression levels were positively correlated. After consensus clustering, the patients in cluster 3 showed the worst prognosis. Using univariate Cox analysis and LASSO regression analysis, 4 prognostic gene (DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA) were selected to construct a risk model. The m6A-related risk model was closely associated with HCC progression. Additionally, the risk score was was an independent prognostic factor for HCC. Our data further emphasized the importance of m6A modification in HCC tumorigenesis, which is consistent with the previous reports12–19,23–25. As for the translational implication, the risk score based on the risk model was significantly associated with the expression of multiple immune checkpoint proteins. This suggests that patients in the high-risk group may have a higher potential to benefit from immunotherapy targeting these checkpoints. Additionally, the negative correlation between the risk score and CD8 positive T cells, along with the positive correlation with macrophage M0 cells, indicates that the risk model could serve as a predictive marker for immune microenvironment status, which is crucial for optimizing immunotherapeutic strategies.

A lot of studies support that m6A modification regulators modulate immune microenvironment of cancer cells. For instance, YTHDF1 deficiency in dendritic cells improves tumor antigen cross-presentation and CD8 + T cell activation, suggesting that YTHDF1 may be a therapeutic target for immunotherapy26. Meanwhile, YTHDF2 deficiency regulates interferon-γ-STAT1 signaling, which promotes tumor-associated macrophages reprogramming and promotes its antigen cross-presentation ability to suppress tumor growth, increasing CD8 + T cell mediated anti-tumor immunity27. Furthermore, in breast cancer, METTL3 inhibits anti-tumor immunity by up-regulating PD-L1 expression level post-transcriptionally in a m6A-IGF2BP3-dependent manner28. Intriguingly, tumor microenvironment characteristics of the three clusters mentioned above were distinct. It was also revealed that, there exited a positive correlation between high m6A-related risk score and high levels of B cell plasma, T cell follicular helper, T cell regulatory Tregs, and macrophage M0. CD8 + T cells are the main players in immune surveillance in cancer biology29–31. Some recent studies also suggest that infiltration of M0 macrophages (defined as undifferentiated macrophages) facilitate cancer progression in several human malignancies, including HCC32,33. Our data suggested that m6A-related risk score (and the expression levels of DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1 and TXLNA) was associated with the dysregulated level of CD8 + T cells and M0 macrophages. These data further support the crucial role of m6A modification in modulating tumor immune microenvironment. Checkpoint inhibitor-based immunotherapy has demonstrated potent anti-tumor effect in some patients, and anti-PD-L1 treatment (e.g. nivolumab and pembrolizumab) has become a first-line therapy for HCC patients34. In the present work, with histological evidence, we also demonstrated that the expression level of two m6A regulators, METTL3 and YTHDF1, were associated with the expression level/positive cell ratio of PD-L1 in HCC samples. These data suggest that m6A may contribute to the dysregulation of PD-L1 expression in HCC progression, and targeting m6A may expand the indication of anti-PD-L1 treatment. Combining m6A modulators with anti-PD-L1 agents may probably enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy by reversing the immunosuppressive microenvironment.

In the present study, four genes are applied to establish the risk model. It is reported that DNTTIP2 expression is associated with macrophage infiltration and unfavorable prognosis of the patients with low grade glioma35. SEPHS1 is considered to be a modulator in cellular redox homeostasis36, and a previous study reports that SEPHS1 increases the aggressiveness of HCC cells via modulating SMAD2/3/437. TCOF1 mediates regulates ribosomal DNA transcription, and there is an inverse correlation between TCOF1 expression of infiltration of anti-tumor immune cells in HCC38. Additionally, in HCC, TXLNA protein expression is related with enhanced growth and a less-differentiated histological grade39. Our data further support that these four genes serve as promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets for HCC diagnosis and treatment. However, the mechanism by which m6A modification regulate the expression and function of these genes (or these genes regulate the processes of m6A modification), remains to be explored in the following work. Additionally, the previous studies16,18,19 and the present work only establish the correlation between m6A modification dysreglation and abnormal tumor immune microenvironment, still fail to explain the detailed molecular mechanism, and this issues requires clarification in the following study. In vitro models, in vivo models and pre-clinical models such as organoids are required to further validate the role of m6A regulators (including but not limited to METTL3 and YTHDF1) in modulating the immunity-related phenotypes of HCC cells, and clarify their effects on the interactions between HCC cells and immune cells, and the underlying molecular mechanisms. Last but not least, a large cohort of patients should be enrolled, to validate the value of our risk model to predict the prognosis of HCC patients, and to evaluate the potential of m6A regulators to predict the therapeutic response of HCC patients to anti-PD1/PD-L1 treatment, especially within the population of HCC patients with different etiological backgrounds (hepatitis B/C virus infection, alcoholic liver diseases, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver diseases, etc.).

Conclusion

Dysregulation of m6A modification is associated with abnormal tumor immunity in HCC, and targeting m6A may improve the effects and of immunotherapy. The established m6A-related risk model, constructed using DNTTIP2, SEPHS1, TCOF1, and TXLNA, shows potential in predicting immunotherapy sensitivity and the prognosis of HCC patients.

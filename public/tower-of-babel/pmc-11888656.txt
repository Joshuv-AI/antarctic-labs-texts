Deubiquitinases and Cancer
Authors: Preethi Murali, B. Kavitha, Malathi Narasimhan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDeubiquitinases are involved in removing ubiquitin (UBQ) from ubiquitylated substrates to regulate their activity and stability. They are involved in various cellular functions including proteasome- and lysosome-dependent proteolysis, gene expression, cell cycle progression, chromosome segregation, kinase activation, histone modification, cell cycle regulation, cell differentiation, spermatogenesis, apoptosis, endocytosis, autophagy, localization, and DNA damage repair. On the other hand, it is also involved in the processes of carcinogenesis and cancer development. DUBs are related to different aspects of human cancer, including proliferation, cell cycle control, apoptosis, DNA damage response (DDR), tumor suppression, oncogenesis, and metastasis. Multiple processes involved in innate and adaptive immunity, such as antigen presentation, cell differentiation, immune defense, and inflammatory responses, are regulated by ubiquitination/deubiquitination. Dysregulation of DUBs is implicated in several human diseases, highlighting the importance of DUB function. This review also provides basic knowledge of DUBs in the development of cancers and highlights the importance of DUBs in T-cell development, apoptosis, and cancer with a specific emphasis on oral cancers. This review highlights the recent advances in the field of cancer biology with a specific role in DUBs in cellular functions. The aberrant expression and regulation of these enzymes have been shown to contribute to promote tumorigenesis, making them promising therapeutic targets for cancer therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) of proteins by several protein-editing enzymes are associated with cell cycle regulation by their enzymatic functions. The timed regulation and response to the proliferation and apoptosis signals of various proteins is a vital aspect of the mammalian cell cycle. One of the most pervasive PTMs is ubiquitination and deubiquitination which affects intracellular localization, stability, and function of target proteins.

Ubiquitination is a three-step enzymatic cascade involving E1, E2, and E3 enzymes that results in the transfer of ubiquitin, via its C-terminal glycine, onto the ε-amino group of a lysine residue on the substrate[1] [Figure 1]. Several ubiquitination sites on thousands of proteins have been identified, indicating that most cellular proteins will experience ubiquitination at some point in their lifetime.

Ubiquitination and deubiquitination

Deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) are proteases that play several roles in the ubiquitin pathway as they cleave ubiquitin or ubiquitin-like proteins from proproteins or target proteins.[2] They have a reversible function for ubiquitination and are also important determinants of protein fate cell cycle processing. DUBs have three major cellular functions: (i) the generation of free Ub moieties from linear fusions of Ub[34]; (ii) trimming of existing polyubiquitin chains; and (iii) reversal of Ub signaling by removal of Ub chains from target proteins.[34] Thus, like ubiquitination enzymes, DUBs are master regulators within the Ub system and impinge on diverse cellular processes [Figure 2]. Deubiquitination process is involved in histone modification, cell cycle regulation, cell differentiation, apoptosis, endocytosis, autophagy, and DNA damage repair. On the other hand, it is also involved in the processes of carcinogenesis and cancer development.

CLASSES OF DUBS

DUBs belong to the superfamily of cysteine/metalloproteases, and approximately 100 members are identified in the human genome so far.[5] Based on sequence and domain conservation, six DUB families with distinct structures have been described. Families of cysteine peptidases include ubiquitin-specific proteases (USPs), ubiquitin COOH terminal hydrolases (UCHs), Machado-Josephine domain containing proteases (MJDs), ovarian tumor–associated proteases (OTUs), zinc finger–containing ubiquitin peptidases, motif interacting with ubiquitin-containing novel DUB family (MINDY), and family of Zn-dependent peptidase JAB1/MPN/MOV34 metalloprotease DUBs (JAMMs, also known as MPN+; 16 members) exists. Other protease classes, such as ubiquitin-like proteases, also act as protein-like modifiers, including, but not limited to, SUMO [Sentrin/SUMO-specific protease (SENP) and de SUMO-glycosylated isopeptidase (DeSI) family] and NEDD [NEDD8-specific protease 1 (NEDP1), member of the SENP family]. In addition, some new potential DUBs without the above typical domains were currently identified, such as the monocyte chemotactic protein-induced protein (MCPIP) and Zn-finger and UFSP domain protein (ZUFSP).[6]

FUNCTIONS OF DUBS

DUBs have four distinct mechanisms of action: (1) processing of Ub precursors, (2) recycling of Ub molecules during ubiquitination, (3) cleavage of poly-Ub chains, and (4) reversal of Ub conjugation. They regulate several cellular functions, including proteasome- and lysosome-dependent proteolysis, gene expression, cell cycle progression, chromosome segregation, kinase activation, apoptosis, localization, DNA repair, spermatogenesis, and degradation of signaling molecules.[5]

DUBs also exhibit various catalytic mechanisms. At the proteasome, protein deubiquitination plays a role in recycling of Ub and is required for protein degradation. A number of DUBs from various subclasses have been found in complex with the 19S proteasome regulatory component, including the JAMM protease POH1, UCH-L5, and USP14.[78910] The COP9 signalosomes are the JAMM protein CSN5 and USP15[1112] and have been implicated in a diverse array of biological processes such as inhibiting the activity of the cullin family of ubiquitin E3 ligases by CSN5- mediated deneddylation. The main DUB activity at the proteasome seems to be generated by POH1, since deletion of the gene that encodes this enzyme results in defective proteasomal degradation and is lethal in yeast. The functions of the other DUBs may be partially redundant with POH1, only playing a role in the deubiquitination of specific substrates, or in “Ub editing.”

Dynamic histone ubiquitination has been implicated in the regulation of transcription and silencing, and even double-strand-break formation during meiosis.[13] The DUB USP7 associates with HDM2, an E3 ligase critical for regulating p53 turnover, and thereby, inhibits degradation of both HDM2 and p53.[1415] Different levels of USP7 can have opposite outcomes with respect to p53 stability. Intermediate inhibition of USP7 results in increased p53 degradation, whereas complete inhibition of USP7 enhances p53 stability.

Ubiquitination and deubiquitination is involved in vesicle dynamics and is implicated in the endocytic pathway at multiple levels. The human USP8 binds the Hrs binding partner (Hbp) and inhibits EGF receptor (EGFR) endocytosis, suggesting that USP8 may act to regulate endocytic traffic.[1617] Remarkably, a second Hrs interacting protein is AMSH, a JAMM domain DUB,[18] the inhibition of which results in the accumulation of endosomal Ub and promotes EGFR endocytosis, thereby accelerating EGFR downregulation. Yet, more DUBs are implicated in controlling endocytosis. In addition, VCIP135, an OTU, has been implicated in Golgi assembly after mitosis.[19]

The DUBs play a crucial role in T-cell development, activation, and differentiation to T-cell homeostasis and tolerance. The DUB CYLD, A20, and USP8 play crucial roles in thymocyte development, maturation, and survival. USP8 interacts with and stabilizes CHMP5, an ESCRT (endosomal-sorting complex-required-for-transport) protein required for thymocyte postselection survival and maturation.[20] DUBs are also crucial regulators of T-cell tolerance and immune homeostasis.[21]

Cells can be subjected to various stresses and respond in a variety of different ways ranging from activation of survival pathways to the promotion of cell death, which eventually eliminates damaged cells. The regulatory mechanisms of apoptosis depend on the balanced action between ubiquitination and deubiquitination systems. There is a growing recognition that DUBs play essential roles in regulating several binding partners to modulate the process of apoptosis. Deubiquitinases in apoptosis

Thus, the interplay between the timing of DUB activity and the specificity of ubiquitin attachment and removal from its substrates during apoptosis is important to ensure cellular homeostasis. DUB-1 is induced by IL-3, IL-5, and GM-CSF and ubiquitously expressed in a number of hematopoietic cell types,[77] while DUB-2 is an immediate early gene that is induced by IL-2 in CTLL-2 and its expression is confined to T cells.[78] USP2 has two isoforms, USP2a and USP2b, and functions to stabilize and extend the half-life of fatty acid synthase (FAS),[56] thus playing a role in cell proliferation and induction of apoptosis in several tumor cell lines.[79] USP2a regulates the p53 pathway by targeting Mdm2 and MdmX and influences cell survival.

USP7 plays a key role in stabilizing the half-life of p53 by regulating both p53 and its ubiquitin E3 ligase Mdm2.[1558] Different types of ubiquitination of p53 by various E3 ligases have been linked to its differential effects on p53-mediated stress responses, and ubiquitinated p53 may actively regulate cell proliferation or death.[80] USP7 is a DUB enzyme for FOXO and plays a major role in regulating cellular stress responses. Another DUB enzyme USP10 is also a key regulator of the stability of the p53 tumor suppressor protein. The central component of the DNA damage response is the activation of p53, which regulates processes such as cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, or senescence. Thus, USP7 and USP10 play a major role in apoptosis by regulating the p53 responsive genes and p53 pathway. USP8 plays a critical role in mammalian cell proliferation and may be involved in the induction of apoptosis by regulating Nrdp1-mediated ubiquitination of BRUCE.

DUBS IN CANCER

Ubiquitin-specific proteases (USPs), as a subfamily of deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs), are responsible for the elimination of ubiquitin-triggered modification. USPs are recently correlated with various malignancies. Oncogenes and tumor suppressors act at various points along the mitogenic pathway by either enhancing or slowing cell growth. Ubiquitin-mediated signaling or targeted proteolysis plays a pervasive role in these pathways, thereby regulating cell proliferation. With regard to cancer-specific pathways, the DUBs play a role in receptor tyrosine kinase signaling, signal transduction, gene transcription, DNA repair, cell proliferation, and mitosis. DUBs in cancer-related pathways

Maintaining genomic integrity is one of the important functions of DUBs. USP3, USP21, and USP22 play a pivotal role in maintaining genomic integrity by deubiquitinating histone proteins H2A and H2B. USP3 associates with chromatin, and its depletion leads to a defect in cell cycle progression with an accumulation in S-phase.[81] USP22 stabilizes SAGA (Spt-Ada-Gcn5-acetyltransferase) complex—a large multiprotein complex required for transcriptional activation.[8182] Binding of ligands activates cytosolic tyrosine kinases present in the growth factors and leads to downstream phosphorylation cascades that propagate growth enhancing signals to the nucleus. The recruitment of Cbl ubiquitin ligases then ubiquitinates the activated receptors which are subsequently internalized and sorted through the endosomal pathway to a complex organelle known as the multivesicular body (MVB).[83] From the MVB, receptors may either be recycled to the membrane or sorted to the lysosome for degradation. 39 Deubiquitination of internalized endosomal cargo is an essential step in the endosomal sorting pathway. Two DUBs participate in the process of internalization of endosomal cargo that is a Usp8 (UbpY) and the associated molecule with the SH3 domain of STAM (AMSH).[1718] CYCLD and A20 participate in signal transduction by negatively regulating NF-kB signaling. USP9 participates in Wnt signaling and TGF-β signaling by stabilizing β-Catenin and Smad 4, respectively.[848586]

USP9 (FAM), one of the largest members of the Ubp family, has a variety of functions including important intersections with cancer pathways. USP9 interacts with and stabilizes β-catenin.[8485] A critical member of the Wnt signaling pathway and an important effector of APC mutations, β-catenin stabilization promotes tumorigenesis.[86] USP9 is also critical for TGFβ signaling by affecting smad4 functions.[87] Monoubiquitination of Smad4 renders it inactive, and deubiquitination by USP9 restores Smad4 functionality and signaling. The relationship of USP9 with human cancer is unclear.

Genomic mutations frequently occur through the actions of UV damage, DNA alkylation, ionizing radiation, oxidative stress, and other mechanisms. These mutations are efficiently repaired in order to maintain the fidelity of our genomes and to prevent the spontaneous acquisition of tumorigenic signatures. The relationship of DNA damage to cancer is easy to surmise from the clinical features of patients with defects in these pathways. USP1, USP28, and BRCC36/BRISC are implicated in DNA repair. USP1 is a negative regulator of FANCD2 which is implicated in Fanconi anemia in the DNA repair pathway.[88] USP1 deubiquitinates PCNA—an important component of the trans-lesions synthesis (TLS) repair pathway.[8990] DNA damage leads to an autodeubiquitination reaction resulting in proteolytic cleavage of USP1.[91] The C-terminus of Ub consists of a highly conserved di-glycine sequence that is required for deubiquitination. Upon the induction of DNA damage, a fragment of USP1, ending in gly-gly, is produced, suggesting that USP1 “deubiquitinates” itself at an internal Ub-like region. This cleavage reaction removes a portion of the active site of USP1 with the remaining fragment lacking DUB activity. Further regulating the activity of USP1 is the binding partner UAF1.[89] The Chk2-p53-PUMA repair pathway forms a critical component of the DNA damage checkpoint and the response pathway to ionizing radiation.[92] An important effector in this pathway is 53BP1 which binds p53 and is required for DNA checkpoint arrest. This complex is stabilized by USP28 following DNA damage.[6693] USP28 also deubiquitinates Myc salvaging it from proteosomal degradation.[9394] BRCC36/BRISC is required for the response to ionizing radiation and for maintaining the G2 DNA checkpoint. This also counteracts the ubiquitination of H2AX and H2A to terminate the double-strand break response.[159596]

USP7 is a critical regulator p53 half-life through a complex mechanism involving both p53 and its Ub E3 ligase Mdm2.[1597] USP7-mediated deubiquitination of Mdm2 as well as p53 by USP7 makes p53 hyperstable with an extended half-life.[15] USP7 also mediates regulation of the Akt antagonist PTEN and affects its localization.[98] Reduced nuclear localization of PTEN is a common feature in aggressive cancers, including advance stage prostate cancer. PTEN nucleo-cytoplasmic shuttling is regulated by PTEN ubiquitination with Ub-PTEN accumulating in the nucleus.[98] USP7, which is overexpressed in prostate cancer, deubiquitinates PTEN leading to reduced nuclear localization. These data suggest that USP7 may in some instances acts as a tumor suppressor (by stabilizing p53) or an oncogene (by stabilizing Mdm2 and redistributing PTEN). Thus, this regulation may be dependent on genetic context and also tissue or cell-type due to differential expression of other proteins. Several cancers (including bladder, prostate, colon, liver, and lung) have increased USP7, whereas others (head and neck, breast, and brain) have reductions of USP7.

USP2 binds to fatty acid synthetase also known to be overexpressed in aggressive prostate cancers. Tumor cells disproportionately synthesize their fatty acids de novo instead of deriving them from nutritional sources, making FAS an important tumor gene that protects prostate cancer cell lines from apoptosis. USP2 also stabilizes Mdm2 and has additional effects that perhaps enhance its role in tumor formation.[56] Many cancers have reduced expression of USP2 including cancers of the colon, pancreas, and head/neck.

USP4 has a retinoblastoma binding motif and interacts directly with Rb.[99100] The USP4 gene is located at 3p21.3 that is frequently rearranged in human lung tumors. USP4 expression is increased in human small cell and adenocarcinoma lung tumor samples.[101] Little is known about the molecular mechanism associated with USP4’s oncogenic potential; however, increased expression is seen in brain, liver, and myeloma, and reduced expression is seen in lung, head/neck, and testis. However, USP4 has been shown to deubiquitinate the autoantigen Ro52.[102103]

BAP1 augments the growth suppressing effects of BRCA1 expression in MCF7 cells and is mutated or deleted in some cases of lung cancer. Binding of BAP1 to the BRCA1-BARD complex interferes with BRCA1 E3 ligase activity and leads to S-phase arrest and increased susceptibility to DNA damage. These effects are in opposition to the effect of BRCA1 binding to another DUB—BRCC36. BAP1 also interacts with the transcriptional cofactor host cell factor (HCF-1) which is a component of the “death from cancer” signature and strongly predicts the prognosis of a variety of human cancers.[104] Expression of a catalytically dead BAP1 leads to the accumulation of HCF-1 with K48-linked ubiquitin chains, whereas knockdown of BAP-1 leads to increased levels of HCF-1. Reduced BAP-1 levels is observed in brain, lung, and testis, and increased expression is observed melanoma, myeloma, prostate, and other types of cancers.

USP33/VDU1 and USP20/VDU2 target the degradation of hypoxia inducible factor (HIF1α) and are important oxygen sensors for the body and a factor that protects tumors from hypoxia when VHL is mutated.[105] USP33 and USP20 are DUBs that play critical roles in regulating cell proliferation. Both enzymes bind to the pVHL E3 ligase in a competitive manner through the β-domain of pVHL. Some of the disease-causing mutations in pVHL disrupt binding of USP33 to pVHL, underscoring the importance of these enzymes. USP20 was shown to deubiquitinate and stabilize HIF1α, thereby counteracting pVHLs activity.[106] USP33 is reduced in cancers of the brain, breast, and in acute myeloid and lymphoblastic leukemias.

The expression of UCHL1 is frequently perturbed in human cancers. Overexpression of UCHL1 is seen in lung, colorectal, pancreatic, thyroid, and other cancers.[107108109] In some cases, increased UCHL1 expression correlates with increased tumor size or invasiveness. Increased UCHL1 expression has also been linked with increased invasive behavior in lung cancer cell lines.[110] In contrast, there are also some cancers in which hypermethylation and suppression of UCHL1 expression is observed.[111112] There are studies suggesting overexpression of UCHL1 leading to apoptosis in breast cancer cells.[113] The mechanism underlying the increased expression in these cancers is unknown and may represent a reaction to malignant transformation. UCHL1 under some conditions has been found to exhibit Ub ligase activity. There are many cancers characterized by reduced levels of UCHL1 including brain, ovary, bladder, and prostate.

A hallmark of cancer is aneuploidy, and abnormal chromosomal content is seen in a majority of malignancies. Targeted ubiquitination is critical at various points in cell cycle progression. In mitosis, several proteins undergo rapid and concerted degradation such that mitosis may proceed. The DUBs USP44, USP24, and CYLD were classified as likely checkpoint components. USP44 was found to have the most prominent mitotic effect. It has been proposed that Cdc20 undergoes a series of ubiquitination and deubiquitination events that regulate APC/C function and USP 44 stabilizes Cdc20.[114115] USP44 works to ensure that sister chromatid separation (anaphase) occurs only when all chromosomes are properly aligned and attached at the metaphase plate. Reduced expression of USP44 has been seen in liver, brain, kidney, and testicular tumors and is predicted to lead to the development of aneuploidy and may cooperate in the transformation process.

USP39 impacts mitotic check point. USP39 was found to be required for full activity of the mitotic checkpoint by stabilizing mRNA levels of the mitotic kinase aurora B and possibly other genes involved in mitotic checkpoint functioning. Aurora B activity is essential in repairing ineffective attachments between chromosomes and the mitotic spindle in human cells.[116117118] USP39 is overexpression and is seen in several cancers including those of the brain, kidney, and possibly leukemia.

DUBS IN ORAL CANCERS

Oral health is essential to general health and well-being. The presence of oral disease may contribute to the early diagnosis and treatment of a number of systemic diseases, and the oral cavity is an integral component of the relationship between oral and systemic health.[119120] As discussed above, the Ub system plays a significant role in regulating cellular proliferation that is contingent on the proteasome activity.[121] Cancer cells require higher proteasome levels to cope with chronic proteotoxic stress. The 26S proteasome is a 2.5-MDa complex that causes selective degradation of ubiquitylated proteins. Alterations in specific pathways affecting cell proliferation involving Ub and DUBs have been linked to oral cancer. Targeting the proteasomal degradation pathway is increasingly getting recognized as a promising strategy for cancer therapy.[122123124]

The stability of the transcription factor p53, which has a crucial role in cellular anticancer mechanisms, is regulated by Ub ligases and deubiquitinating enzyme (DUB). Mutations in p53 have been shown to be linked to oncogenic transformation and have been detected in approximately >50% of tumors.[125] The UPS pathway primarily degrades the wild-type (WT) p53 protein and a mutations in p53 might stabilize this protein, thereby inhibiting its interaction with murine double minute 2 (MDM2), a protein that negatively regulates the activity of p53, a tumor suppressor, thereby preventing degradation.[126]

The DUB cylindromatosis (CYLD) acts as a tumor suppressor in several malignancies including oral squamous cell carcinomas (OSCC), and its loss has been shown to be significantly associated with poor prognosis in patients with OSCC.[127128] CYLD acts by negatively regulating multiple cell signaling pathways, including nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), Wnt/β-catenin, c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), p38/mitogen-activated protein kinase, and Hippo and Notch signaling.[129]

The DUB USP14, has been shown to have a role in tumor growth and metastasis.[130] Ubiquitin-specific protease (USP22) promotes stability of multiple cancer-associated protein targets through deubiquitylation (e.g., TRF1 and SIRT1)[131132] and influences oncogene accumulation.[133] A study by Xie and Xu showed USP14 expression to be significantly increased in clinical OSCC tissue samples and cell lines, and suppression of USP14 alleviates radio resistance in OSCC by inducing apoptosis and autophagy. OSCC patients with high USP14 expression predicted poor overall survival rate.[134]

An increase in USP22 expression with the progression of oral carcinogenesis from noncancerous mucosa to primary carcinoma and from carcinomas to lymph node metastasis has been observed. A positive USP22 expression has been reported in OSCC patients and is associated with poor prognosis. Liu T et al.[135] suggested that USP22 may be involved in the progression of OSCC, in cooperation with Aurora-B and Survivin, which belong to the chromosomal passenger complex, are also highly expressed in several types of cancer. Piao et al.,[136] reported USP22 to be activated in OSCC and increased expression of USP22 exhibited a marked propensity toward highly malignant clinical behavior, lymph node metastasis, and poor survival after surgery suggesting its role as a prognostic indicator predicting the treatment outcome of OSCC.

Nanayakkara et al.[137] reported that the deubiquitylating enzyme, USP9X, possibly promotes head and neck cancer cell proliferation through the mTOR pathway. Significantly and frequently altered USP9X genes have been reported in gingiva-buccal OSCC and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma.[138] USP9x was found to be mutated in a panel of OSCC cell lines.[40] The immune checkpoint protein programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD- L1) binds to PD1 to promote tumor escape from cell death. Jingjing et al.[139] discovered that USP9X could bind to PD-L1 to induce its deubiquitination and stabilize its protein expression in OSCC cells.

Notch signaling links to the ubiquitination system.[140] Notch signaling is regulated by ubiquitination of the receptor and its extracellular ligands reveal distinct ubiquitination-dependent endosomal sorting pathways.[141] Osathanon et al.[142] demonstrated that Notch signaling is dysregulated in human OSCC and plays a role in cell proliferation.

Cellular stress response is an integral part of normal physiology to either activate the pathways that promote cell survival or alternatively to eliminate damaged or unwanted cells. UPP is involved in regulating cellular stress responses. While much attention has been given to the role of the ubiquitination system in regulating apoptosis, reversal of ubiquitination by DUBs plays an equally important role. DUBs are often found as a part of large multiprotein complexes that function directly in regulating the activation or suppression of apoptotic signaling cascades.

CONCLUSION

Recent advances in molecular and cellular biology have played important roles in understanding cancer and breakthroughs that have been being translated into therapy. In this regard, this review puts forth comprehensive report of the role of DUBs for cancer diagnosis and prognosis. The DUBs play multiple roles in cellular functions. The aberrant expression and regulation of these enzymes have been shown to contribute to promote tumorigenesis, making them promising therapeutic targets for cancer therapy.

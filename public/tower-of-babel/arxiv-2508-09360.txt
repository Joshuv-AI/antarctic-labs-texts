The subgroup stratification of Nakaoka spectra
Noah Wisdom

Abstract
. We construct a stratification on the Nakaoka spectrum of any G G -Tambara functor indexed by the poset of subgroups of G G . When G G is Dedekind, we show that the H H th stratum of the Nakaoka spectrum of the Burnside G G -Tambara functor is closed and not open; this provides examples of étale maps which induce closed, non-open maps on Nakaoka spectra. By computing the strata on the ghost of a C p C_{p} -Tambara functor we obtain many examples of étale maps of Tambara functors for which the induced map on Nakaoka spectra is not open, in contrast to the non-equivariant world. We also compute the strata of all fixed-point Tambara functors.



1.
Introduction

Tambara functors are equivariant analogues of rings appearing in equivariant homotopy theory and many other purely algebraic situations. They come equipped with a Nakaoka spectrum analogous to the Zariski spectrum, the collection of prime ideals with a certain topology. One of the basic problems in equivariant algebra is to compute the Nakaoka spectrum of a Tambara functor. For example, the computation of the Nakaoka spectrum of the Burnside
G
-Tambara functor
\mathcal{A}_{G}
–the analogue of the integers in equivariant algebra–took over a decade and spanned the work of many authors
[
Nak12
,
Nak14
,
CG23
,
CMQ+24
,
CCM+25
]
.

Given a subgroup
H
of
G
, there are various change-of-group functors relating the category of
H
-Tambara functors and the category of
G
-Tambara functors. Of particular importance to us is the restriction

\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}:G\text{-}\mathrm{Tamb}\rightarrow H\text{-}\mathrm{Tamb}

which is left adjoint to
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}
. Since
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}
preserves initial objects, we have
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{G}\cong\mathcal{A}_{H}
. Previous work
[
SSW25
,
Wis25b
]
suggests that
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}
has extremely nice commutative algebra properties. For example, modules and algebras over a
G
-Tambara functor
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
are the same thing (respectively) as modules and algebras over
R

[
Wis25b
, Theorems F and G]
. We extend this philosophy to ideals:

Theorem A
.

(cf.
Theorem

3.2
)

\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}
induces an order-preserving bijection between Tambara ideals of
R
and Tambara ideals of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
which restricts to a bijection between prime ideals. Consequently we obtain a natural homeomorphism

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R)\cong\mathrm{Spec}(R)

of Nakaoka spectra for all
H
-Tambara functors
R
.

The adjunction unit

R\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R

thus induces compatible maps of Nakaoka spectra.

Definition B
.

Let
R
be a
G
-Tambara functor. We define the
subgroup stratification
of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
as the stratification indexed by the poset of subgroups of
G
whose
H

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R)\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R).

Remark 1.1
.

One of the motivations of
[

]

Assuming such a comparison map exists, we obtain a stratification on the Balmer spectrum by taking the preimage of the subgroup stratification under the comparison map. This stratification on Balmer spectra of

Recall that a Dedekind group is a group for which all subgroups are normal.

Question F

Suppose

One might also attempt to define a topology on the set of prime ideals of a Tambara functor by declaring all étale maps to be open. This seems like a promising direction of thought, especially in light of the fact that the equivariant analogue of the Zariski topology is known to fail to have desirable properties. However, one would expect incompatibilities between this étale topology and Balmer spectra of

2.
Recollections on Nakaoka spectra

Any commutative ring possess a Zariski spectrum, the space of prime ideals. Similarly, a Tambara functor gives rise to a Nakaoka spectrum, a space of prime ideals. We follow
[
Nak12
]
, although see
[
CMQ+24
]
for additional excellent exposition.

Definition 2.1

(
[
Nak12
]
)
.

A
Tambara ideal
of a
G
-Tambara functor
R
is a collection of ideals
I(G/H)\subset R(G/H)
subject to the conditions

(1)

\mathrm{Tr}_{K}^{H}\left(I(G/K)\right)\subset I(G/H)

(2)

\mathrm{Nm}_{K}^{H}\left(I(G/K)\right)\subset I(G/H)

(3)

\mathrm{Res}_{K}^{H}\left(I(G/H)\right)\subset I(G/K)

(4)

c_{g}\left(I(G/H)\right)\subset I(G/g^{-1}Hg)

where
c_{g}
denotes restriction along right multiplication by
g
,
xH\mapsto xgg^{-1}Hg
.

Natalie Stewart and David Chan have pointed out to the author that the condition

\mathrm{Nm}_{K}^{H}\left(I(G/H)\right)\subset\mathrm{Nm}_{K}^{H}(0)+I(G/K)

of
[
Nak12
]
is superfluous when we take the convention that Tambara functors take values only on transitive
G
-sets, rather than all finite
G
-sets, as the norm of
0\in R(G/e)
is always zero.

If
R
is merely a Green functor and we relax the requirement that
I
be preserved by norms, we obtain the notion of a Green ideal. A Green ideal of a Green functor
R
is just an
R
-submodule of
R
.

Nakaoka proves that the intersection of Tambara ideals is again a Tambara ideal. Thus given any collection of elements in a Tambara functor, there is a unique smallest Tambara ideal containing them. In particular, we may speak of principal Tambara ideals, and more generally Tambara ideals generated by a set.

Definition 2.2
.

The product
I\cdot J
of two ideals
I
and
J
of a Tambara functor is defined as the Nakaoka ideal generated by the set of elements
I(G/H)\cdot J(G/H)
in each level
G/H
. A Nakaoka ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is called prime if
I\cdot J\subset\mathfrak{p}
implies either
I\subset\mathfrak{p}
or
J\subset\mathfrak{p}
.

We may therefore consider the set of prime ideals of a Tambara functor
R
. Following Nakaoka
[
Nak12
]
we give this set a topology. The generating closed subsets are

V(I):=\{\mathfrak{p}|I\subset\mathfrak{p}\}

where
I
runs through all ideals of
R
. We therefore obtain a space, which we call the Nakaoka spectrum,
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. It is important to note that the space
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is determined entirely by the poset of Tambara ideals of
R
along with its sub-poset of prime ideals.

3.
The subgroup stratification

We start by establishing some results on the interaction between products, coinductions, and ideals.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R\cong S\times T
an isomorphism of Tambara functors. The projections
R\rightarrow S
and
R\rightarrow T
determine bijections between the set of Green, Tambara, and prime ideals of
R
and the disjoint union of the respective sets of Green, Tambara, and prime ideals of
S
and
T
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Coinduction induces a bijection between the set of Tambara ideals of
R
and the set of Tambara ideals of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
. This bijection respects containment and products of ideals, hence restricts to a bijection between prime ideals.

Proof.

To start with, observe that by
[
Wis25b
, Theorem F]

\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}
induces a bijection between Green ideals of the Green functor underlying
R
and the Green ideals of the Green functor underlying
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
. We will repeatedly use the following description of the inverse of this bijection. The restriction of a coinduction is, by the well-known formula found for example in
[
Wis25b
, Lemma 2.27]
, a product indexed by double cosets
H\backslash G/H
. The proof of
[
Wis25b
, Theorem F]
shows that the inverse to coinduction is given by restriction, followed by projection onto the identity double coset factor.

Suppose
I
is a Green ideal of
R
. We must show that
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I
is closed under norms if and only if
I
is. Suppose
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I
is a Tambara ideal. Since
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}
clearly preserves Tambara ideals, we see that
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I
is a Tambara ideal of
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
. Now the latter is a product of
H
-Tambara functors, and one factor is
R
. We have already seen that every Tambara ideal of a product of Tambara functors is a set-theoretic product of Tambara ideals, and the factor of
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
corresponding to
R
is
I
. Hence
I
is a Tambara ideal. On the other hand, if
I
is closed under norms, then since norms in
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
are given levelwise as the set-theoretic product of ring-theoretic products of norms in
R
, and Green ideals are closed under products, we see that
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I
is closed under norms.

Next, we show that this bijection respects products of ideals. Let
I
and
J
be Tambara ideals of
R
. Recall that
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R(G/K)
is a product of copies of rings
R(H/L)
, and in particular the coinduction of
I
is given in level
G/K
by the very same set-theoretic product of ideals
I(H/L)\subset R(H/L)
. The factor of
(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}(I\cdot J))(G/K)
which lives in
R(H/L)
clearly contains
I(H/L)\cdot J(H/L)
. Thus

\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}J\subset\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot J

On the other hand,
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}J
is generated as a Tambara ideal by the set-theoretic product of ideal products
I(H/L)\cdot J(H/L)\subset R(H/L)
over the factors of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R(G/K)
. The projection of the restriction of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}J
to the identity double coset factor therefore contains
I\cdot J
. Thus

\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot J\subset\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}J

so that in fact equality holds.

Finally, we must show that
\mathfrak{p}
is prime if and only if
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
is. This follows straightforwardly from the fact that coinduction induces a bijection between the sets of Tambara ideals which respects both inclusions and ideal products, though we give one direction for clarity. Suppose
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
is prime and let
I\cdot J\subset\mathfrak{p}
. Then since coinduction preserves ideal products and inclusions, we observe
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\cdot\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}J\subset\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
, so that (without loss of generality)
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}I\subset\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
. Restricting and projecting on the identity double coset factor preserves inclusion, so
I\subset\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Since the topology on the Nakaoka spectrum is completely determined by the poset of Tambara ideals along with the sub-poset of prime ideals, we immediately obtain the following.

Corollary 3.3
.

For any
H
-Tambara functor
R
we have a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R)\cong\mathrm{Spec}(R)

of Nakaoka spectra.

Theorem 3.4
.

Any Nakaoka spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
where
R(G/e)
is Noetherian decomposes as a disjoint union over subgroups
H
of
G
of Nakaoka spectra of clarified
H
-Tambara functors.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
[
Wis25b
, Theorem D]
,
Lemma

3.1
, and
Theorem

3.2
.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
R
be a
G
-Tambara functor. We define the
subgroup stratification
of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
as the stratification indexed by the poset of subgroups of
G
whose
H
th stratum is the image of

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R)\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R).

In all of our examples below, the strata basic closed subsets.

Question 3.6
.

Is the subgroup stratification always by closed subspaces? If not, is the subgroup stratification of the Nakaoka spectrum of a domain-like Tambara functor always by closed subspaces?

Under somewhat strong assumptions on
G
and on the Tambara functor
R
we may establish further properties of the subgroup stratification.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a Mackey, Green, or Tambara functor
M
is
idle
if, for each finite
G
-set
X
, the Weyl group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
acts trivially on
M(X)
.

For example, the Burnside
G
-Tambara functor
\mathcal{A}_{G}
is idle.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
R
be an idle Tambara functor and assume
G
is Dedekind. The map

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R)\cong\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R)\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R)

induced by the adjunction unit
R\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R
is injective.

Proof.

Chasing through definitions, we see that
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
defines a set-theoretic left inverse.
∎

It is a priori unclear that
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
respects primality of Tambara ideals (and in fact it does not in general:
\mathrm{Res}_{e}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
need only be a
G
-prime ideal of the
G
-ring
R(G/e)
). However the proof of
Lemma

3.8
shows that
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
is prime, so long as
k
is idle and
G
is Dedekind. We thus immediately obtain the following.

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume
R
is idle and
G
is Dedekind. The assignment
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}
determines an order-preserving poset morphism of Green ideals, which respects the property of being a Tambara ideal and respects primality. In particular,
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R)
is a retract (i.e. an idempotent subobject) of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
in the category of topological spaces.

4.
Domain-like Tambara functors

As we have already built up enough technology to establish some results about domain-like Tambara functors, we digress on them here.

Definition 4.1

(
[
Nak12
]
)
.

A Tambara functor
R
is called
domain-like
if the zero ideal is prime.

We have seen many times that the bottom level of a Tambara functor, viewed as a
G
-ring, carries much of the information of a Tambara functor. Following this idea, we set out to study the
G
-ring analogue of integral domain.

Definition 4.2
.

An ideal
I
of a
G
-ring is
G
-invariant if
g(I)\subset I
for all
g\in G
. We call
I

G
-prime
if furthermore
I\neq R
and whenever
x\cdot y\notin I
, there exists
g\in G
such that either
gx\notin I
or
gy\notin I
. If the zero ideal is
G
-prime, we call
R
domain-like.

If
R
is a
G
-ring whose underlying ring is an integral domain, then
R
is domain-like. In other words, being domain-like is weaker than being a domain.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
CMQ+24
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of a
G
-Tambara functor
R
. Then
\mathfrak{p}(G/e)
is a
G
-prime ideal of
R(G/e)
.

In particular, if
R
is a domain-like
G
-Tambara functor, then
R(G/e)
is a domain-like
G
-ring. Now let
S
be a domain-like
G
-ring. The
G
-fixed points
S^{G}
are an integral domain, and
S
is an integral extension of
S^{G}
.

The following is a slight strengthening of
[
Nak12
, Proposition 4.31]
. In particular, we weaken the hypothesis from asking for
R(G/e)
to be an integral domain to asking for
R(G/e)
to be a domain-like
G
-ring.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R
be a Tambara functor such that
R(G/e)
is a domain-like
G
-ring and all restrictions along morphisms of transitive
G
-sets in
R
are injective. Then
R
is domain-like.

Proof.

Suppose that
I
and
J
are both not the zero ideal. Since restrictions are injective, both
I(G/e)
and
J(G/e)
are nonzero. Let
x\in I(G/e)
and
y\in J(G/e)
be nonzero. Since
R(G/e)
is domain-like, there exists
g\in G
such that
x\cdot gy
is nonzero. Since
g\cdot J(G/e)=J(G/e)
, we see that
x\cdot gy
is a nonzero element of
(I\cdot J)(G/e)
, so that
I\cdot J
is nonzero. By contrapositive we see that the zero ideal is prime.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
R
be an
H
-ring or an
H
-Tambara functor. Then
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
is domain-like if and only if
R
is.

Proof.

We first prove the Tambara case. We observed in
Theorem

3.2
that the zero ideal is prime if and only if the coinduction of the zero ideal is. But the coinduction of the zero ideal is the zero ideal.

For the ring case, first observe the natural isomorphism

\mathrm{FP}(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R)\cong\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{FP}(R)

of
G
-Tambara functors. By
Lemma

4.4
and
Lemma

4.3

R
is domain-like if and only if
\mathrm{FP}(R)
is domain-like. By
Proposition

4.5
this is true if and only if
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{FP}(R)\cong\mathrm{FP}\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
is domain-like. But this is true if and only if
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R
is a domain-like
G
-ring.
∎

We give a sample application below.

Example 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be any field. First, form the
G
-ring
\mathbb{F}[G\cdot x]
, the free
\mathbb{F}
-algebra on
|G|
generators which are permuted by
G
. Next, consider the
G
-ring
S:=\mathbb{F}[G\cdot x]/(g_{1}x\cdot g_{2}x)
, where
(g_{1},g_{2})
runs through the compliment of the diagonal in
G\times G
. By
Proposition

4.5
,
\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{G}\mathbb{F}[t]
is a domain-like Tambara functor, and we define a map
\mathrm{FP}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{G}\mathbb{F}[t]
by sending
gx
to the element which is
t
in the
g^{\textrm{th}}
factor (this is the adjoint of the ring map
S\rightarrow\mathbb{F}[t]
sending
e\cdot x
to
t
and
gx
to zero for
g\neq e
).

The ring map

is visibly injective, hence the kernel of the Tambara functor map
\mathrm{FP}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{G}\mathbb{F}[t]
is zero. Since the zero ideal in
\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{G}\mathbb{F}[t]
is prime, its preimage in
\mathrm{FP}(S)
, which is also the zero ideal, is prime. Thus
\mathrm{FP}(S)
is domain-like. Additionally,
S
is a domain-like
G
-ring.

, Definition 3.1]

is

.

Theorem 4.7

Let

be a

is domain-like if and only if

where
R_{H}
is a clarified domain-like
H
-Tambara functor. It is possible that
H=G
, i.e.
R
itself is already clarified.

Proof.

If
R
is domain-like and not clarified, then
R(G/e)
contains a type
H
idempotent for some
H
(by definition). If
d
is such a type
H
idempotent, then

R(G/e)\cong\left(\sum_{gH\in G/H}gd\right)R(G/e)\times\left(1-\sum_{gH\in G/H}gd\right)R(G/e)

as
G
-rings, hence

\sum_{gH\in G/H}gd=1

as otherwise
[
Wis25b
, Proposition 3.6]
would express
R
as a product of two nonzero Tambara functors, contradicting primality of the zero ideal.
By
[
Wis25b
, Theorem A]
we deduce
R\cong\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}R_{H}
for some
R_{H}
. By
Proposition

4.5

R_{H}
is domain-like. Repeating this argument for
R_{H}
if necessary we may assume
R_{H}
is clarified. This process terminates, as in each step we are coinducing from a strict subgroup.
∎

If one wishes to give necessary and/or sufficient conditions for a Tambara functor to be domain-like, it therefore suffices to study clarified Tambara functors. Unfortunately, it seems like there are not simple conditions on even a clarified
G
-ring which are equivalent to being domain-like. For example, the
C_{2}
-ring
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy)
, with
C_{2}
acting by swapping
x
and
y
is a very simple domain-like
C_{2}
-ring (by
Example

4.6
). It is clarified, but not an integral domain. However, see
[
CMQ+24
]
for sufficient conditions when
G=C_{p}
which seem to be necessary or almost necessary.

Knowing that a Tambara functor is domain-like is useful, by the following.

Proposition 4.8

(
[
Nak12
, Theorem 4.9]
)
.

Let
R
be a domain-like
G
-Tambara functor, and
f:S\rightarrow R
a morphism of Tambara functors. Then the kernel of
f
is a prime Tambara ideal.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R
be any
G
-Tambara functor. There is an injection from the set of
G
-prime ideals of
R(G/e)
to the set of prime Tambara ideals of
R
.

Proof.

Let
I
be a
G
-prime ideal of
R(G/e)
, and let
J
be the Tambara ideal which is the kernel of the Tambara functor morphism
R\rightarrow\mathrm{FP}(R/I)
adjoint to the quotient
R(G/e)\rightarrow R(G/e)/I
. By
Lemma

4.4

J
is prime. By construction
J(G/e)=I
, so
I\mapsto J
determines the desired injection.
∎

We note that the image of the injection of
Proposition

4.9
is not in general the same as the
\{e\}
th stratum. Next, we observe that the results of
[
CMQ+24
]
on the Nakaoka spectrum may be slightly strengthened.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
R
be a Tambara functor such that all restrictions are injective and let
f:R\rightarrow\mathrm{FP}(R(G/e))
be the adjunction unit. Then
f
induces a continuous bijection
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{FP}(R(G/e)))\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, hence a poset isomorphism
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\cong\mathrm{Spec}(R(G/e)^{G})
.

Proof.

The proof of
[
CMQ+24
, Proposition 6.2]
only requires the fact that all restrictions of a fixed-point Tambara functor are injective, hence the same argument determines a poset isomorphism between the
G
-prime ideals of
R(G/e)
and the prime ideals of both
R
and
\mathrm{FP}(R(G/e))
. One straightforwardly checks that this bijection is precisely the induced map on Nakaoka spectra.
∎

Finally, we observe that the strata of domain-like Tambara functors fail to be open, in general.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary group and
R
any domain-like Tambara functor such that the restriction
R(G/G)\rightarrow R(G/H)
is not injective. Then the
H
th stratum is not open.

Proof.

The closure of the zero ideal is all of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, so it suffices to show that the zero ideal ideal is not contained in the
H
th stratum. The adjunction unit
R\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R
is given in level
G/G
by the restriction
R(G/G)\rightarrow R(G/H)
. Thus no ideal in
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}R
contracts to zero.
∎

5.
Explicit descriptions of some strata

5.1.
The Burnside Tambara functor

We start by computing the stratification on the Nakaoka spectrum of the Burnside Tambara functor
\mathcal{A}_{G}
when
G
is Dedekind. Since
\mathcal{A}_{G}
is idle, our strategy is to appeal to
Corollary

3.9
. We start by recalling the computation of the Nakaoka spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{G}
, due in various cases to
[
Nak14
,
CG23
,
CCM+25
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
H\subset G
and
p
a prime number (possibly zero). Define a ring homomorphism

\phi^{G}_{H,p}:\mathcal{A}(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p

by
X\mapsto|X^{H}|
, for
X
a
G
-set.

Definition 5.2

(
[
CG23
]
)
.

Let
K
and
H
be subgroups of
G
,
p
a (possibly zero) prime integer, and define an ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}
of
\mathcal{A}_{G}
by

\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}(G/H):=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset H\\
I\preccurlyeq_{G}K\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Ker}(\phi_{H,p}^{I})

where
I\preccurlyeq_{G}K
means that the subgroup
I
of
G
is conjugate to a subgroup of
K
.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
Nak14
,
CG23
,
CCM+25
]
)
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary finite group. Every prime Tambara ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{G}
is one of the
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}
. All inclusions and equalities between
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}
are explicitly determined by the group structure of
G
.

Not all of the
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}
are distinct. For example, by
[
CG23
, Figure 1]
,
\mathfrak{p}_{C_{2},2}=\mathfrak{p}_{C_{4},2}
when
G=C_{12}
. In any case, we may now compute the strata of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
when
G
is Dedekind.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G
be a Dedekind group and
H\subset G
. The
H
th stratum of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
is the subspace

\{\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}\}_{K\subset H,p\in\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}

and the map

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{H})\cong\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})

sends
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}\subset\mathcal{A}_{H}
to
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}\subset\mathcal{A}_{G}
. The
H
th stratum of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
is closed and not open.

Proof.

If
L\subset H\subset G
and
K\subset G
, we have

\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}(H/L)=\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}(G/L)=\bigcap_{I\subset L\cap K}\mathrm{Ker}(\phi_{L,p}^{I})\subset\mathcal{A}(L)

and

\mathfrak{p}_{K\cap H,p}(H/L)=\bigcap_{I\subset L\cap K\cap H}\mathrm{Ker}(\phi_{L,p}^{I})\subset\mathcal{A}(L).

Since
L\cap K\cap H=L\cap K
, we deduce

\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}=\mathfrak{p}_{K\cap H,p}.

Thus by
Corollary

3.9
the map
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{H})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
has the claimed description on points. By
Proposition

4.11
the
H
th stratum is not open.

It remains to show that the image of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{H})
in
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
is closed. First, since
\mathcal{A}_{H}
is domain-like, the zero ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{H,0}
is prime. Since
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{H})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
preserves inclusions of prime ideals (since
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}
and contraction do), we see that the image of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{H})
in
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
is contained in the basic closed subset
V(\mathfrak{p}_{H,0})
of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
. To conclude the proof, we will show the opposite containment.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}_{H,0}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}
for some possibly zero prime integer
p
. Note that
\mathfrak{p}_{H,0}
is the kernel of
\mathcal{A}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{H}
is, levelwise, a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-module, so is
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}
, so that
\mathcal{A}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}
is levelwise integral. Therefore by the going-up theorem for Tambara functors
[
CMQ+24
, Theorem 5.9]
we deduce that
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}
is the contraction of some prime ideal
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{L,q}
for some possibly zero prime integer
q
and
L\subset K
. By the first paragraph, we see that
\mathfrak{p}_{K,p}=\mathfrak{q}_{L,q}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary finite group. There exists an étale map of
G
-Tambara functors for which the induced map on Nakaoka spectra is not open. If
G
is Dedekind, we may further construct an example which is closed.

Proof.

By
[
Wis25a
, Theorem A]
the map
\mathcal{A}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H}
is étale, and the image on the induced map of Nakaoka spectra is precisely the
H
th stratum. If
H
is a proper subgroup of
G
, the restriction
\mathcal{A}_{G}(G/G)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G}(G/H)
is not injective, so that the
H
th stratum is not open by
Proposition

4.11
.

On the other hand, by the explicit description of the strata in
Theorem

5.4
, we see that
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathcal{A}_{H})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}_{G})
is closed when
G
is Dedekind.
∎

5.2.
The ghost

Recall from
[
CMQ+24
]
that the
ghost
of a
C_{p}
-Tambara functor
R
is the
C_{p}
-Tambara functor
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
given by the Lewis diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(C_{p}/e)^{C_{p}}\times R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R(C_{p}/e)}

There is a canonical map
\chi:R\rightarrow\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
given by the identity in the
C_{p}/e
-level and the product of restriction and the quotient by the transfer ideal in the
C_{p}/C_{p}
-level.

By
[
CMQ+24
, Theorem F]
, every prime ideal of
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
has the following description. Letting
(\mathfrak{p};\mathfrak{q})
denote the ideal of
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
specified by the Lewis diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}^{C_{p}}\times\mathfrak{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}}

[
CMQ+24
, Theorem F]
implies that every ideal of
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
is either
(\mathfrak{p};R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})
for
\mathfrak{p}
a
C_{p}
-prime ideal of
R(C_{p}/e)
, or
(\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}\mathfrak{p};\mathfrak{p})
for
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal of
R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
R
be any nonzero
C_{p}
-Tambara functor and
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
its ghost (cf.
[
CMQ+24
]
).

(1)

A prime ideal
(\mathfrak{p};\mathfrak{q})
of
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)
belongs to the
e
th stratum of
\mathrm{Spec}(\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R))
if and only if it is equal to
(\mathfrak{p};R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})
for
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal of
R(C_{p}/e)
.

(2)

If
\mathrm{Tr}
is not surjective then the
e
th stratum of
\mathrm{Spec}(\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R))
is not open.

(3)

If
R(C_{p}/e)^{C_{p}}
has finitely many minimal prime ideals (e.g. is Noetherian or an integral domain), the
\{e\}
th stratum of
\mathrm{Spec}(\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R))
is closed.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the observation that the map
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{C_{p}}R(C_{p}/e)
is given in level
C_{p}/C_{p}
by the restriction
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)(C_{p}/C_{p})\rightarrow\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)(C_{p}/e)=R(C_{p}/e)
. This restriction is the projection
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)(C_{p}/C_{p})=R(C_{p}/e)^{C_{p}}\times R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr}\rightarrow R(C_{p}/e)^{C_{p}}
. The contraction of an arbitrary prime ideal
\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{C_{p}}\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{C_{p}}R(C_{p}/e)
is therefore
\bigcap_{g\in C_{p}}g\mathfrak{p}
in the
C_{p}/e
-level and
\mathfrak{p}\times R(C_{p}/e)/\mathrm{Tr}
in the
C_{p}/C_{p}
-level.

For the second statement, let
\mathfrak{p}
be any prime ideal of
R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr}
. Then
(\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}\mathfrak{p};\mathfrak{p})
is not in the
e
th stratum, but the closure of
(\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}\mathfrak{p};\mathfrak{p})
contains the point
(\mathrm{Nm}^{-1}\mathfrak{p};R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})
which is in the
e
th stratum. Thus the compliment of the
e
th stratum is not closed.

Finally, let
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
be the finite list of minimal prime ideals of
R(C_{p}/e)^{C_{p}}
. By the first paragraph, every prime ideal in the
\{e\}
th stratum has the form
(\mathfrak{q};R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})
. Now for some
i
we have
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{q}
, whence
(\mathfrak{p}_{i};R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})\subset(\mathfrak{q};R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})
. Therefore the
\{e\}
th stratum is the finite union of the basic closed subsets
V((\mathfrak{p}_{i});R(C_{p}/C_{p})/\mathrm{Tr})
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
R
be a
C_{p}
-Tambara functor such that
\mathrm{Tr}
is not surjective. Then
\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{C_{p}}R(C_{p}/e)
is étale, but the induced map on Nakaoka spectra is not open.

Proof.

By
[
Wis25a
, Theorem A]

\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoInd}_{e}^{C_{p}}R(C_{p}/e)
is étale and by
Proposition

5.6
the induced map on Nakaoka spectra is not open.
∎

Since the subgroup stratification is natural in Tambara functor morphisms and
\mathrm{Spec}(\text{\raisebox{-1.28886pt}[6.44444pt][0.0pt]{\includegraphics[height]{ghost.pdf}}}(R))\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is always surjective,
Proposition

5.6
provides a method to determine the subgroup stratification on all
C_{p}
-Tambara functors.

5.3.
Fixed-point functors

Let
R
be a
G
-Tambara functor such that all restrictions are injective, for example the fixed-point
G
-Tambara functor
\mathrm{FP}(S)
for a
G
-ring
S
. By
Proposition

4.10
the set of prime ideals of
R
is in bijection with the set of prime ideals of
R(G/e)^{G}
, which is in bijection with the set of
G
-prime ideals of
R(G/e)
by
[
CMQ+24
, Proposition 6.4]
. Precisely, the proof of
[
CMQ+24
, Proposition 6.4]
shows that this bijection sends a
G
-prime ideal of
R(G/e)
to its contraction to
R(G/e)^{G}
, and its inverse sends a prime ideal of
R(G/e)^{G}
to the unique
G
-prime ideal of
R(G/e)
lying over it.

Proposition 5.8
.

Assume all restrictions in
R
are injective. The
H
th stratum of
R
is the set of prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
such that the ideal
\mathfrak{p}(G/H)\subset R(G/H)
is prime.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.10
it suffices to prove the claim when
R\cong\mathrm{FP}(S)
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
\mathrm{FP}(S)
such that
\mathfrak{p}(G/H)
is a prime ideal of
S^{H}
. Then by
[
CMQ+24
, Proposition 6.4]
there is a unique prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathrm{FP}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}S)\cong\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}(\mathrm{FP}(S)
such that
\mathfrak{q}(H/H)=\mathfrak{p}(G/H)
. The contraction of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{q}
to
\mathrm{FP}(S)
is the unique prime ideal of
\mathrm{FP}(S)
given in level
G/G
by the prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}(H/H)\cap S^{G}
. Since
\mathfrak{q}(H/H)=\mathfrak{p}(G/H)
and
\mathfrak{p}(G/G)=\mathfrak{p}(G/H)\cap S^{G}
, this contraction is precisely
\mathfrak{p}
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
\mathrm{FP}(S)
which is the contraction of a prime ideal
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{FP}(S)
. Since
\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{FP}(S)\cong\mathrm{FP}(\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}(S))
,
[
CMQ+24
, Proposition 6.4]
implies
\mathfrak{q}(H/H)
is a prime ideal of
S^{H}
. The
G/H
-level of the adjunction unit is the diagonal ring map

\mathrm{FP}(S)(G/H)\rightarrow(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{Res}_{H}^{G}\mathrm{FP}(S))(G/H)\cong\mathrm{FP}(S)(G/H)^{\times|G/H|}.

The contraction of
(\mathrm{CoInd}_{H}^{G}\mathfrak{q})(G/H)
along this map is simultaneously
\mathfrak{p}(G/H)
and the prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}(H/H)\subset S^{H}
.
∎

Congruence angle and medial ulnar translation: a novel measurement to assess the severity of ligament injury in simple elbow dislocation
Authors: Sang-Pil So, Seung-Hun Lee, Hui Ben, Jae-Man Kwak, Kyoung Hwan Koh, In-Ho Jeon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsThis study was to propose novel measurements to assess severity of ligament injury in simple elbow dislocation using axial imaging. This study assessed inter- and intraobserver reliability of the proposed novel measurements and compared their application as measures of severity of medial collateral ligament (MCL) and lateral collateral ligament (LCL) injuries.MethodsThis retrospective study included patients diagnosed with simple elbow dislocations from January 2013 to December 2023. The degree of MCL and LCL injuries was assessed by four grades: (0 (normal); 1 (sprain); 2 (partial tear); and 3 (complete tear)) using MRI. Patients were classified into two groups according to the severity of MCL and LCL injuries (Group I (degree of MCL injury ≥ LCL injury); and Group II (degree of MCL injury < LCL injury)). The novel measurements were defined by measuring congruence angle (CA), medial ulnar translation (TRANS), and lateral ulnar tilt (TILT) as determined by axial MRI. Adjusted medial ulnar translation (ADJ-TRANS) was calculated. Two observers measured all parameters twice over four weeks.ResultsA total of 21 patients were analyzed. Interobserver reliability was moderate to good for CA and good for TRANS. Both CA and TRANS showed excellent intraobserver reliability. Grade 2 MCL injury had significantly higher ADJ-TRANS than grade 1, while grade 2 LCL injury had significantly higher TRANS than grade 1. Meanwhile, grade 3 LCL injury had significantly higher CA and TRANS than grade 2, and significantly higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS than grade 1. Group II also had significantly higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS than Group I.ConclusionNovel measurements using axial images of MRI scans achieved overall good inter- and intraobserver reliability. Higher grade LCL injury exhibited higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS. Patients with LCL injury graded higher than that of MCL injury exhibited higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS.Cite this article: Bone Jt Open 2025;6(11):1468–1474.Keywords: Elbow dislocation, Elbow instability, Axial image, Lateral collateral ligament, Congruence angle, Medial ulnar translation, Lateral ulnar tilt, Axial measurement, simple elbow dislocations, ligament injury, lateral collateral ligament (LCL), medial collateral ligament (MCL), MRI scans, sprain, ulna, elbows, subluxation, plain radiographs

Introduction

Simple elbow dislocation, the second most commonly dislocated large joint in adults, is defined as a joint incongruity between the distal humerus and proximal forearm without associated fractures.1-3 Simple elbow dislocation can be treated nonoperatively and operatively; however, approximately 2% of patients treated nonoperatively experience recurrent instability.4 Long-term follow-up of patients with simple elbow dislocation after conservative treatment illustrated that the results are not always benign.4-6 Stabilization surgery may be considered in cases of acute simple elbow dislocation if the elbow dislocates before the extension to 30° from full flexion, if a drop sign is present on plain radiographs, or if persistent instability exists after conservative treatment; however, the exact indications have not been well established.7,8

The drop sign, first coined by Coonrad et al,8 is defined as the distance between the trochlea and sigmoid notch (ulnohumeral distance; UHD) of ≥ 4 mm taken at 90° of elbow flexion based on lateral plain radiograph images. UHD is the only known measurement tool to assess remaining instability after elbow dislocation on static radiological imaging. However, to our knowledge, no previous study has assessed the reliability of UHD, and a debate exists regarding the clinical significance of a positive drop sign.9-11

The authors have observed that after simple elbow dislocation, some patients with normal appearing plain radiographs would demonstrate subtle subluxation of the ulnohumeral joint on axial MRI (Figure 1). Based on these findings, this study aimed to propose novel measurements designed to quantitate this degree of subluxation derived from axial MRI images to assess the severity of ligament injury in patients with a simple elbow dislocation. The primary goal was to assess inter- and intraobserver reliability of the proposed novel measurements. The secondary goal was to compare the measurements according to the severity of medial collateral ligament (MCL) and lateral collateral ligament (LCL) injuries.

Methods

The protocol for this study was approved by the institutional review board (approval No. 2023-08-002). This single-centre retrospective study included patients who were diagnosed with a simple elbow dislocation between Jaunary 2013 and December 2023. Closed reduction was performed after confirmation of elbow dislocation by plain radiograph, and reduction was confirmed by plain radiograph. After reduction of the elbow, MRI was performed in the supine position at the most comfortable degree of elbow flexion without stress, and the degree of elbow flexion (elbow flexion angle, EFA) during MRI was measured using sagittal reconstruction. The severity of MCL and LCL injuries was assessed by MRI and classified into four grades: grade 0: normal; grade 1 (sprain, with increased signal intensity within the ligament on T2-weighted images); grade 2 (partial tear, with focal partial discontinuity of ligament fibres with hyperintense fluid signal extending partially through the ligament); and grade 3 (complete tear, with complete disruption of the ligament with a fluid gap between torn ligament fibres and extracapsular extravasation of joint fluid).12,13

Prior to assessment of the novel axial measurements, two fellowship-trained orthopaedic surgeons (S-PS, S-HL) independently evaluated the severity of MCL and LCL injuries and reached a consensus on the final grading, in cases of discrepancy. Patients were classified into two groups according to the difference in the severity of MCL and LCL injuries: Group I, degree of MCL injury ≥ LCL injury; and Group II, with degree of MCL injury < LCL injury. An axial T1 MRI image perpendicular to the ulna at the centre of the trochlea was obtained from patients to assess the novel axial measurements, which included the congruence angle (CA), medial ulnar translation (TRANS), and lateral ulnar tilt (TILT). Comparisons of novel measurements were made in terms of the degree of MCL and LCL injuries and between two groups to assess the effect of respective ligament injury on novel measurements.

All radiological parameters were measured twice with a four-week interval (series I and II), which were presented in a different order at both measurements, by two fellowship-trained orthopaedic surgeons (S-PS, S-HL). Inter- and intraobserver reliability were assessed using measurements for both series I and II and comparisons of measurements by severity of MCL and LCL injuries were performed using measurements in series I.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients were included if they met all the following criteria: 1) diagnosed with a simple elbow dislocation; 2) the initial dislocation pattern was posterolateral; 3) underwent MRI scans; and 4) were aged ≥ 18 years. Patients were excluded if they: 1) had a history of prior elbow dislocation; 2) had an osteoarthritic change in the injured elbow on plain radiograph or MRI; 3) had not had the elbow reduced at the time of the MRI scan.

Patient demographic information

Of the 25 patients diagnosed with posterolateral simple elbow dislocation who underwent MRI scans, three patients who had a history of prior elbow dislocation and one patient presenting osteoarthritic change at the injured elbow on MRI were excluded; therefore, 21 patients were finally included in the analysis. The mean age was 23.7 years (19 to 55). Ten patients were classified as grade 1, nine as grade 2, and two as grade 3 using MCL injury grading, while five patients were classified as grade 1, nine as grade 2, and seven as grade 3 using LCL injury grading. One patient who had a higher grade of MCL injury than LCL injury, and nine patients who had the same grade of MCL and LCL injuries were classified as Group I, whereas 11 patients with a higher grade of LCL injury than MCL injury were classified as Group II. Radiological assessment

Congruence angle (CA): The deepest point of the retrocondylar groove (D) and both ends of the retrocondylar groove (A, B) were marked first to measure CA. A line bisecting the angle (∠ADB) was drawn, and the angle between the bisecting line and the line connecting the highest portion of the ulna (H) and the deepest portion of the retrocondylar groove (D) was measured (Figure 2a).

Proposed novel measurements using axial images to assess the severity of ligament injury in patients with a simple elbow dislocation: a) congruence angle; b) medial ulnar translation; and c) lateral ulnar tilt. A, the medial end of the retrocondylar groove; B, the lateral end of the retrocondylar groove; D, the deepest portion of the retrocondylar groove; H, the highest portion of the ulna; C, the foot of the perpendicular from point D to line AB; E, the foot of the perpendicular from point H to line CD; and L, lateral prominence of the ulna.

Medial ulnar translation (TRANS): The line (CD¯) perpendicular to that connecting both ends of the retrocondylar groove (A, B; AB¯) was drawn from the deepest point of the retrocondylar groove (D) to measure medial ulnar translation. The shortest distance (HE¯) was measured as the perpendicular distance from the highest point of the ulna (H) to the reference line (CD¯) (Figure 2b). Despite similar TRANS values, the anteroposterior positions of the ulna may vary, which could be reflected in different CA values. For example, if the ulna is moved posteriorly without any mediolateral movement, the CA would decrease while the TRANS remains unchanged (Figure 3). The adjusted medial translation (ADJ-TRANS), calculated as Medial ulnar translationCongruence angle×100, was developed to reflect relative anteroposterior movement of the ulna.

Presumed scenario where the same medial ulnar translation (TRANS) value corresponds to different anteroposterior positions of the ulna. In this example, although TRANS values are equal, the congruence angle (CA) value is smaller in b) than a) due to more posteriorly positioned ulna. To account for anteroposterior movement of the ulna, the concept of adjusted TRANS was developed: Medial ulnar translationCongruence angle×100.

Lateral ulnar tilt (TILT): TILT defined the angle between the line connecting both ends of the retrocondylar groove (A, B) and the line connecting the highest point of the ulna (H) and the lateral prominence of the ulna (L) (Figure 2c).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are reported as means and SDs, and categorical variables are reported as numbers and percentages. The reproducibility of the novel measurement was assessed by inter- and intraobserver reliability using interclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). ICC values < 0.5 were considered as poor reliability, 0.5 to 0.75 as moderate reliability, 0.75 to 0.9 as good reliability, and > 0.9 as excellent reliability.14

Comparisons of novel measurements by severity of MCL and LCL injuries were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Post-hoc analysis and comparison of novel measurements between two groups were performed by the Mann-Whitney U test. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for MacOS v. 29 (IBM, USA), with p-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Inter- and intraobserver reliability

In terms of interobserver reliability, CA showed moderate reliability in the first series and good reliability in the second series, while TRANS showed good reliability for both series (Table II). TILT showed moderate reliability for both series. With regard to intraobserver reliability, CA and TRANS showed excellent reliability by both observers, while TILT showed moderate reliability by observer 1 (S-PS) and excellent reliability by observer 2 (S-HL) (Table III).

Comparison of novel measurements by severity of MCL and LCL injuries

Patient demographic details, including sex and age, did not show significant differences based on MCL and LCL injury grades. In terms of grade of MCL injury, significant differences were only observed for ADJ-TRANS (Table IV). Post-hoc analysis revealed that a grade 2 MCL injury showed significantly higher ADJ-TRANS than a grade 1, whereas grade 2 and 3 injuries did not show any significant differences. Higher severity of LCL injury showed overall increasing tendency with statistical significance in CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS (Table V). In post-hoc analysis, grade 2 LCL injury showed a significantly higher TRANS than a grade 1. A grade 3 LCL injury had significantly higher CA and TRANS than grade 2, and had significantly higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS than grade 1. TILT did not show any significant differences in terms of severity for both MCL and LCL injuries.

Discussion

In this study, novel measurements using axial MRI images of the elbow were used to assess the severity of ligament injury in patients with a simple elbow dislocation. CA showed moderate to good and TRANS showed good interobserver reliability. Higher grades of LCL injury showed overall higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS and Group II, having more severe LCL injury than MCL injury, showed higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS than Group I.

To develop novel measurements as indicators of severity of ligament injury, the study first aimed to investigate the reliability of three axial measurements. In terms of interobserver reliability, CA showed moderate to good reliability and TRANS showed good reliability. TILT showed the lowest interobserver reliability among novel measurements, which was still moderate. For intraobserver reliability, CA and TRANS showed excellent reliability, while TILT showed moderate reliability by observer 1 and excellent reliability by observer 2. It would be because TILT was the only measurement that marked the lateral ulnar prominence and could not be marked accurately using a single axial image in some elbows. These findings suggest that the novel measurements to assess severity of ligament injury proposed in this study had overall good reliability between observers and support the reliability of the measurements.

In cases with the same axial anteroposterior position of ulna, a higher CA with a relatively medial movement of the ulna resulted in higher TRANS. An overall increase in CA and TRANS measurements was observed as the grade of LCL injury increased, whereas significant differences in CA and TRANS were not observed with differences in the degree of MCL injury. According to post-hoc analysis, grade 2 LCL injury had a significantly higher TRANS than grade 1 injury and grade 3 LCL injury had significantly higher CA and TRANS than grade 1 or 2 injuries. In addition, patients with a higher grade LCL injury than MCL injury had higher CA and TRANS. These findings suggest that more severe LCL injuries and an imbalance between MCL and LCL tensions due to different degree of injury led to medial movement of the ulna on the axial plane, which is similar to that observed for lateral patellofemoral subluxation in individuals with medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) injury.15,16

If the MPFL is injured, the patella tends to move laterally on the static axial image and the patella will tend to show subluxation or dislocation laterally on dynamic patellofemoral movement; however, in this study, which included patients with simple posterolateral elbow dislocation, the ulna tended to move medially on the static axial image, if the LCL was severely injured or the LCL injury was more severe than that of the MCL. This difference may be attributed to a posterolateral rotatory mechanism in cases of simple elbow dislocation compared with patellofemoral instability.17-19

Anakwe et al5 investigated the long-term outcomes of conservative treatment after simple elbow dislocation and concluded that simple dislocation was not always benign. Therefore, establishing the exact indication for stabilization after a simple elbow dislocation is important; however, which elbow to operate on is still debatable and studies investigating the clinical significance of the positive drop sign have shown contradictory results.9-11 The present study showed that patients with higher grades of LCL injury or with LCL injury that was more severe than MCL injury exhibited higher CA and TRANS, a finding related to the medial movement of the ulna. With regard to optimal ligament healing, reduction should be achieved in the position that provides the best joint congruity.20,21

However, the medial movement of the ulna could result in persistent stress or tensioning condition at the LCL during conservative treatment, and thus, may lead to poorer healing of LCL and residual instability following conservative treatment. Future studies investigating the correlation between the proposed novel measurements and prognosis after conservative treatment in simple elbow dislocation would help to demonstrate if the novel measurements have relevant clinical utility.

The three novel measurements introduced in this study mainly focused on the axial movement of the ulna. TRANS increased if the CA increased at the same anteroposterior position of the ulna; however, if the ulna moved posteriorly with no mediolateral movement, the CA decreased with no change in the TRANS. Thus, ADJ-TRANS, which was calculated as TRANS adjusted by CA, was designed to evaluate the relative anteroposterior movement of the ulna. Grade 2 MCL injury showed significantly higher ADJ-TRANS than that of grade 1 injury and a gradual increase in the ADJ-TRANS was observed as the severity of the LCL injury increased, while a statistically significant difference was observed between grades 1 and 3 LCL injuries. In contrast to CA or TRANS, the ADJ-TRANS were significantly different in terms of the severity of MCL injury. These findings suggest that higher ADJ-TRANS or posterior movements of the ulna may be related to the overall severity of the injury. This study analyzed the anteroposterior movements of the ulna in terms of ADJ-TRANS using axial images taken at 0° to 45° of elbow flexion; however, the absolute value of posterior subluxation of ulna has also been evaluated using the drop sign or UHD based on sagittal images of plain radiographs, taken at a 90° elbow flexion.8,9 The greatest difference between ADJ-TRANS measurements and the drop sign is the EFA determined during image acquisition. UHD and the ADJ-TRANS measurement might be positively correlated; however, a study assessing the correlation between UHD and ADJ-TRANS is needed as the EFAs between sagittal and axial image acquisitions differ.

Various measurements on axial images have been developed and emphasized in patellofemoral subluxation. The CA and lateral patellofemoral angle, which measure angles in plain knee axial radiography, were proposed first by Merchant et al22 in 1974 and Laurin et al23 in 1978, respectively, and are currently widely used for precise assessment of patellofemoral subluxation.22 Axial views of the elbow to assess the olecranon, epicondyles, and retrocondylar groove could be obtained in several ways; however, these have not been generally adopted and are rarely used as indicators of elbow instability.24-27 Based on the findings of this study, which used MRI scans to obtain the measurements for the novel indicators, further studies using axial plain radiographs to assess their reliability and predictability for the severity of ligament injury might contribute to more widespread adoption of these novel measurements in the future.

This study had several limitations. First, the study population was relatively small for external validation. Second, a priori sample size calculation was not performed due to the retrospective design. However, the lower bounds of the 95% CIs for the ICC values suggest that the number of cases and observers was unlikely to be insufficient for supporting the reliability analysis. Third, due to the retrospective design of the study, the elbow and forearm positions during MRI were not standardized, which may have introduced measurement variability. Fourth, in this study, a single axial MRI image was preselected for measurement, rather than having each observer select the slice. Although we expect minimal variation in slice selection, this omission may have influenced the reliability results. Fifth, interobserver reliability of ligament injury grading system was not assessed in this study. However, the four-stage grading system is widely used to assess the severity of ligament injury on MRI, and assessment by two observers and reaching the final grade by a consensus in cases of discrepancy help increase the overall reliability of MCL and LCL injury grade in this study.12,28,29 Sixth, popularizing these novel measurements using MRI scans is difficult as they are not routinely considered in the evaluation of simple elbow dislocation. Further studies using plain radiographs might help popularize these novel measurements. Seventh, this study mainly focused on the diagnostic value of novel measurements. Future studies investigating the correlation between the proposed measurements and outcomes or prognosis after treatment of simple elbow dislocation would provide further evidence supporting the clinical relevance of the novel measurements.

Despite the above limitations, this was the first study to propose novel measurements using axial images to assess the severity of ligament injury in simple elbow dislocation and showed their reliability and relationship with the severity of LCL injury and the differences between MCL and LCL injuries.

In conclusion, novel measurements using axial images of MRI scans achieved overall good inter- and intraobserver reliability. Higher grade LCL injury exhibited higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS. Patients with LCL injury graded higher than that of MCL injury exhibited higher CA, TRANS, and ADJ-TRANS.

Take home message

- This was the first study to propose novel measurements using axial images (congruence angle, medial ulnar translation, and lateral ulnar tilt) to assess the severity of ligament injury in simple elbow dislocation and showed their reliability and relationship with the severity of lateral collateral ligament (LCL) injury and the differences between medial collateral ligament and LCL injuries.

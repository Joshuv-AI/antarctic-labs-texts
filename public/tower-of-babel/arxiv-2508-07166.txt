Hermitian K-theory of Lagrangian Grassmannians via reducible Gorenstein models
Tao Huang, Heng Xie

Abstract
. We construct a family of moduli spaces, called generalized Lagrangian flag schemes, that are reducible Gorenstein (hence singular), and that admit well-behaved pushforward and pullback operations in Hermitian K K -theory. These schemes arise naturally in our computations. Using them, we prove that the Hermitian K K -theory of a Lagrangian Grassmannian over a regular base splits as a direct sum of copies of the base’s (Hermitian) K K -theory, indexed by certain shifted Young diagrams. The isomorphism is realized via pullback to each generalized Lagrangian flag scheme followed by pushforward to the Lagrangian Grassmannian. This yields an unusual example in which both the base and the target are regular schemes, while the intermediate reducible Gorenstein models remain sufficient to allow explicit computations in Hermitian K-theory of regular schemes.

Introduction

In Quillen’s seminal work
[

]
, the computation of the algebraic
K

K

-theory. In practice, Hermitian

-theory often yields finer invariants than algebraic

-theory, indicating that Hermitian

-theory is generally more difficult to compute. In our work
[
HX23
]
, we constructed pushforward and pullback maps in Hermitian

-theory, and established several tools, including base-change, excess intersection, and projection formulas, based on the framework of Schlichting
[

]
and
[

-theory of Grassmannians and a direct sum of copies of the

-theory of the base scheme (cf.
[

]

In this paper, we explain how pushforward and pullback in

If

by loosening the condition

.

Since our maps factor through schemes which are not regular (or even not irreducible), we have to check carefully that our tools in
[
HX23
]
are still valid, and the

(cf.
[
BC12a
]
) do not cause any trouble, which turned out to be technical and have been written down in Appendix
B
and
D
.

Our work is partly motivated by the two-step blow-up strategy introduced by Martirosian in his thesis, where he computed the Witt groups of Lagrangian Grassmannians
[
Mar21
]
. Martirosian also conjectured a geometric model for a possible additive basis of these Witt groups, obtained via an alternative construction inside the Bott–Samelson resolution. However, as noted in
[
Mar21
, Remark 3.4.6]
, a full proof was not supplied and no published work has carried out a complete realization of this idea. In the present paper, we introduce a different model, namely the generalized Lagrangian flag scheme, and prove that it induces an additive basis for the Hermitian
K
-theory of Lagrangian Grassmannians, which encompasses the result that Martirosian originally sought to establish. The details of the two-step blow-up construction, in the context of Hermitian
K
-theory, are provided in Appendix
C
.

In fact, such generalized Lagrangian flag schemes emerged while we were computing the Hermitian
K
-theory of
\mathrm{LG}(2)
(cf. Theorem
4.1
(b)), where an explicit splitting of the localization sequence must be required to establish the underlying isomorphism. This situation has no analogue in Witt-group calculations, because one term in the localization sequence vanishes:

(cf. §
1.1.2
). Therefore, a purely Witt-theoretic approach would be unlikely to expose these flag schemes unless there are other intriguing reasons. This example also shows that Hermitian
K
-theory reveals structures invisible in Witt groups and can lead to new insights.

Some consequences

We now record some immediate consequences from our work.

1.1.1.
Compare to
KO
-theory

KO

1.1.2.
The Witt groups of
Q_{3}

The Witt groups of
Q_{3}
been computed by Nenashev
[
Nen09
]
(see also
[
Xie19
]
for more general quadrics). Note that

. Nenashev considered the localization sequence

and showed that
\iota^{*}_{y}
is an isomorphism since

. However, it is more delicate to geometrically define an inverse of

. In Theorem
4.1
(b), we construct an inverse map of

by the composition

W^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\xrightarrow{\alpha^{*}}W^{i}(\mathrm{LF}_{1,2}(0;\mathring{V})_{1})\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}W^{i}(Q_{3})

where
\alpha
and
\pi
are both projections, and

is the so-called padding. The interesting point is that the intermediate scheme
\mathrm{LF}_{1,2}(0;\mathring{V})_{1}
is not regular, but rather reduced Gorenstein and consisting of two irreducible components of relative dimension zero over

. We verify that the canonical line bundle of

becomes trivial in the Picard group modulo two, guaranteeing that the pushforward map

1.1.3.
Semi-orthogonal decomposition

It is well-known that semi-orthogonal decompositions of derived categories can help to compute Hermitian
K
-theory by the work of Walter
[
Wal03
]
. The point is that one needs to understand the symmetry of semi-orthogonal decompositions of derived categories under the Hom functor with values in a line bundle. A semi-orthogonal decomposition of derived category of coherent sheaves of Lagrangian Grassmannian

has been obtained by Fonarev
[

]
, but with the assumption that

for

to be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Since our work allows the base to be a field of positive characteristic, it is reasonable to conjecture that the semi-orthogonal decomposition of

is independent of the base.

(A.1)

\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that

and

(i)

(ii)

Proof.

Let

[

, Appendix B.7.1]
. There are regular sequences

and

with

the composition

\iota_{\ast}\left[-,\iota^{\natural}L[-c]\right]_{X}\xrightarrow{\iota_{\ast}(\rho)}\iota_{\ast}\left[-,E\iota^{\natural}L[-c]\right]_{X}\xrightarrow{\iota_{*}(\chi)}\iota_{\ast}\left[-,\iota^{\Delta}EL\right]_{X}\xrightarrow{\phi}\left[\iota_{\ast}(-),\iota_{\ast}\iota^{\Delta}EL\right]_{Y}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Tr}}\left[\iota_{\ast}(-),EL\right]_{Y}.

Appendix D
On the pushforward of the trivial bundle

In
[
SGA6
, Lemme VII.3.5, p.441]
, it is shown that if
\pi:B\to X
is the blow-up along a regular closed immersion, then
\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{B}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, which forms the basis for applying the lax similitude in
[
BC12a
]
to Grassmannians
[
BC12b
]
(cf.
[
BC12b
, Proposition 3.15]
). In this paper, we encounter examples where
\pi:B\to X
may not be a blow-up, but rather a projective morphism of relative dimension zero, and where
B
and
X
may be reducible. The aim of this section is to study if we can still have
\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{B}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and apply lax similitude in the situation (cf. Sublemma
4.2
(ii)) that we need.

D.1.
Cohomology of universal bundle

Suppose that
{V}
is a vector bundle of rank
n+1
over
Y
. Let
{P}_{n}
be the universal bundle over
p:\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})\to Y
.

Proposition D.1
.

The formulas

\mathbf{R}^{i}p_{*}\big(\wedge^{k}{P}_{n}(-l)\big)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}_{Y},&\text{ if }i=k=l,\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

hold when
0\leq i,k,l\leq n
.

Proof.

Step I
. We claim that in the derived category
D^{b}(\mathbf{M}(Y))
, the complex
\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big(\wedge^{k}{P}_{n}(l)\big)(l\geq 0,k>0)
is isomorphic to the Koszul complex

(D.1)

\ldots\to 0\to\wedge^{k}{V}\otimes S^{l}{V}\xrightarrow{d^{0}}\wedge^{k-1}{V}\otimes S^{l+1}{V}\xrightarrow{d^{1}}\ldots\xrightarrow{d^{k-1}}\wedge^{0}{V}\otimes S^{l+k}{V}\to 0\to\ldots

where
\wedge^{k}{V}\otimes S^{l}{V}
sits on degree zero and where the differential
d^{i}
is defined by

\displaystyle(a_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{k-i})\otimes(b_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot b_{l+i})

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}(-1)^{j+1}(a_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{a}_{j}\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{k-i})\otimes(a_{j}\cdot b_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot b_{l+i}),

where
\widehat{a}_{j}
indicates that such term is omitted. In particular when
l=0
, the complex (
D.1
) is acyclic and hence equal to
0
, cf.
[
ABW82
, Corollary V.1.15 and p.263]
. Let us prove by induction on
k
. Consider the universal exact sequence

(D.2)

0\to{P}_{n}\to p^{*}{V}\to\mathcal{O}(1)\to 0

We omit writing
p^{*}
explicitly. Tensoring
D.2
yields another exact sequence

0\to{P}_{n}(l)\to{V}(l)\to\mathcal{O}(l+1)\to 0

Let us consider the case
k=1
. Taking
\mathbf{R}p_{*}(-)
provides an exact triangle

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big({P}_{n}(l)\big)\to\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big({V}(l)\big)\to\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big(\mathcal{O}(l+1)\big)\to\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big({P}_{n}(l)\big)[1]

which is isomorphic to

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big({P}_{n}(l)\big)\to{V}\otimes S^{l}{V}\to S^{l+1}{V}\to\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big({P}_{n}(l)\big)[1],

for each
l\geq 0
by the cohomology of projective bundles, cf.
[
EGA3
, Chapter III Remark 2.1.16, p.443]
. The map
{V}\otimes S^{l}{V}\to S^{l+1}{V}
is the same as the differential
d^{0}
in (
D.1
). Next, suppose that the claim is true for
k-1
. Note that (
D.2
) induces a short exact sequence

(D.3)

0\to(\wedge^{k}{P}_{n})(l)\to(\wedge^{k}{V})(l)\to(\wedge^{k-1}{P}_{n})(l+1)\to 0,

which induces an exact triangle

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k}{P}_{n})(l)\big)\to\wedge^{k}{V}\otimes S^{l}{V}[0]\to\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k-1}{P}_{n})(l+1)\big)\to\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k}{P}_{n})(l)\big)[1],

and one verifies that the middle map corresponds to the differential
d^{0}
in (
D.1
).

Step II
. By Step I, we see that
\mathbf{R}^{i}p_{*}\big(\wedge^{k}{P}_{n}\big)=0
whenever
k>0,i\geq 0
. If
k=0
,
\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})})=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. Let us prove the proposition by induction on
k
and
l
and assume that
k,l>0
. The short exact sequence (
D.3
) induces a long exact sequence

\ldots\mathbf{R}^{i-1}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k}{P}_{n})(-l)\big)\to\mathbf{R}^{i-1}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k}{V})(-l)\big)\to\mathbf{R}^{i-1}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k-1}{P}_{n})(-(l-1))\big)\to\mathbf{R}^{i}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k}{P}_{n})(-l)\big)\ldots

It is clear that
\mathbf{R}^{i-1}p_{*}\wedge^{k}{V}(-l)=0
and we have

\mathbf{R}^{i}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k}{P}_{n})(-l)\big)\cong\mathbf{R}^{i-1}p_{*}\big((\wedge^{k-1}{P}_{n})(-(l-1))\big).

The proposition then follows from induction.
∎

D.2.
On the local complete intersection

Let
V
be a vector bundle of rank
n+1
over a scheme
Y
. Denote by
{P}_{n}
the universal bundle over
p:\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})\to Y
. Suppose that
s:{P}_{n}\to p^{*}L
is a regular section, where
L
is a line bundle over
Y
. We shall drop the mention of
p^{*}
in the sequel. Denote by
\iota:Z\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})
the zero locus of the section
s
, and
\pi
the composition
Z\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})\xrightarrow{p}Y
. Note that we have

\omega_{\pi}=L^{\otimes n}(-n)

over
Z(s)
.

Lemma D.2
.

The canonical map
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is an isomorphism, and
\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
,

Proof.

The complex
\mathbf{R}\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
can be resolved by the Koszul complex

\displaystyle K(s)=\big(0\to\wedge^{n}{P}_{n}\otimes L^{\otimes-n}\to\wedge^{n-1}{P}_{n}\otimes L^{\otimes-(n-1)}\to\ldots\to{P}_{n}\otimes L^{\otimes-1}\to{\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})}}\to 0\big)

where the term
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Gr}(n,{V})}
sits on degree zero. By Proposition
D.1
and projection formula, we get

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big(\wedge^{i}{P}_{n}\otimes L^{m}\big)=0,

for any
m
and
1\leq i\leq n
. The result follows from computing
\mathbf{R}p_{*}(K(s))
.
∎

Lemma D.3
.

Suppose that
n
is even. Denote by
\sqrt{\omega_{\pi}}:=L^{\otimes-\frac{n}{2}}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-\frac{n}{2})
. Then we have
\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}\sqrt{\omega_{\pi}}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.

Proof.

The complex
\mathbf{R}\iota_{*}\sqrt{\omega_{\pi}}
can be resolved by the Koszul complex
K(s)\otimes\sqrt{\omega_{\pi}}
:

\displaystyle 0\to\wedge^{n}{P}_{n}\otimes L^{\otimes\frac{n}{2}-n}(-\frac{n}{2})\to\wedge^{n-1}{P}_{n}

\displaystyle\otimes L^{\otimes\frac{n}{2}-n+1}(-\frac{n}{2})\to\ldots\to{P}_{n}\otimes L^{\otimes\frac{n}{2}-1}(-\frac{n}{2})\to{L^{\otimes\frac{n}{2}}(-\frac{n}{2})}\to 0

By Proposition

, we have

\mathbf{R}p_{*}\big(\wedge^{i}{P}_{n}\otimes L^{\otimes\frac{n}{2}-i}(-\frac{n}{2})\big)=0,

Then the result follows.
∎

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Abstract

    The Census Bureau plans to request an extension of the current 
Office of Management and Budget clearance for the Building Permits 
Survey Program. This program includes two surveys: the Survey of 
Residential Building or Zoning Permit Systems (C-411) and the Report of 
Building or Zoning Permits Issued for New Privately-Owned Housing Units 
(C-404), also known as the Building Permits Survey (BPS). The C-411 and 
the C-404 are related collections sharing the same universe called the 
Active Governments File (AGF). The C-411 is utilized to update the 
permit issuing status of all jurisdictions in the AGF. The C-404 is 
primarily used to collect both monthly and annual data on the number of 
new housing unit permits issued in permit issuing jurisdictions.
    The universe of permit-issuing places serves as the universe for 
the Building Permits Survey (BPS) and the sampling frame for the Survey 
of Construction (SOC). These two surveys provide widely used measures 
of construction activity, including the Principal Federal Economic 
Indicators: New Residential Construction (NRC) and New Residential 
Sales (NRS). For New Residential Construction, Form C-404 directly 
collects the data on the number of housing units authorized by building 
permits. The number of housing units authorized by building permits is 
also a key component used to estimate other data items in the NRC and 
NRS releases such as housing units started, housing units completed, 
and housing units sold. Additionally, building permits for new private 
housing units are a component of The Conference Board's Leading 
Economic Index.
    The Building Permits Survey is the only source of statistics on 
residential construction for states and smaller geographic areas. The 
Census Bureau uses the detailed geographic data collected from state 
and local officials on new residential construction authorized by 
building permits in the development of annual population estimates. 
These estimates are used by government agencies to allocate funding and 
other resources to local areas. Policymakers, planners, businesses, and 
others also use this data to monitor growth, plan for local services, 
and to develop production and marketing plans.
    The Census Bureau uses the C-411 survey, or individual questions 
from the survey as needed, to obtain information from state and local 
building permit and zoning officials to update the universe of permit-
issuing places. The questions pertain to the legal requirements for 
issuing building or zoning permits in local jurisdictions and gather 
information on items such as geographic coverage and types of 
construction for which permits are issued.
    The Census Bureau uses Form C-404 to collect information on changes 
to the geographic coverage of permit-issuing places, the number and 
valuation of new residential housing units authorized by building 
permits, and additional details on residential permits valued at $2 
million or more, including site address and type of building.

II. Method of Collection

Survey of Residential Building or Zoning Permit Systems (C-411)

    One of three versions of the survey is sent to a jurisdiction when 
the Census Bureau needs to verify whether a new permit system has been 
established or an existing one has changed. The selection is based on 
the length of time since the jurisdiction last verified their permit 
issuing status, or on information the Census Bureau obtains from a 
variety of sources including survey respondents, regional planning 
councils, and data from the Census Bureau's Geography Division on newly 
incorporated jurisdictions. Since 2024, reporting has been done 
primarily via standard online collection instrument (Centurion).
    There are three versions of the survey:
     C-411(M) for municipalities where a new permit system may 
have been established.
     C-411(C) for counties where new permit systems may have 
been established.
     C-411(V) for verification of coverage for jurisdictions 
with existing permit systems.
    Prior to 2022, the universe of permit issuing places was updated 
every 10 years. Before each universe update, The C-411 survey was sent 
to approximately 20,000 jurisdictions that were designated in our 
records as non-permit-issuing jurisdictions or permit issuing 
jurisdictions that needed verification of coverage. In January 2023, 
the universe began to be updated annually, and more frequent updates 
are incorporated for non-permit issuing jurisdictions. As a result, the 
C-411 survey is sent to about 3,200 of the 20,000 jurisdictions that do 
not issue permits on an annual basis, prioritizing those that have not 
responded in at least five years.

Report of Building or Zoning Permits Issued for New Privately-Owned 
Housing Units (C-404)

    Respondents may submit their data via internet or a mailed or faxed 
form. Some respondents choose to report over the phone or to send 
proprietary files containing permit information in lieu of returning 
the form.
    The survey universe is comprised of approximately 20,000 local 
governments that issue building permits. Due to resource availability 
and the time required to complete the data review and analysis, the 
Census Bureau collects data from a selection of permit-issuing 
jurisdictions monthly, and the remainder of the jurisdictions annually. 
We collect this information monthly for approximately 8,600 permit-
issuing jurisdictions. We collect this information annually for the 
remaining 11,400 permit-issuing jurisdictions.

III. Data

    OMB Control Number: 0607-0094.
    Form Number(s): C-404, C-411.
    Type of Review: Regular submission, Request for an Extension, 
without Change, of a Currently Approved Collection.
    Affected Public: State and Local governments.
    Estimated Number of Respondents: C-404--19,987; C-411--3,200.
    Estimated Time per Response: C-404--8 minutes for the approximately 
8,395 monthly respondents who report online, 23 minutes for the 
approximately 10,307 annual respondents who report via online or 
physical form. An additional 243 monthly respondents and 1,042 annual 
respondents choose to report by phone or by sending us custom files and 
printouts from their permit systems, which we estimate takes only 3 
minutes. C-411--15 minutes.
    Estimated Total Annual Burden Hours: C-404--17,581 hours; C-411--
800 hours.
    Estimated Total Annual Cost to Public: $0. (This is not the cost of 
respondents' time, but the indirect costs respondents may incur for 
such things as purchases of specialized software or hardware needed to 
report, or expenditures for accounting or records maintenance services 
required specifically by the collection.)
    Respondent's Obligation: Voluntary.
    Legal Authority: Title 13 U.S.C. 131 and 182.

An anticyclotomic Euler system of Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles I: Norm relations and p p -adic interpolation
Raúl Alonso, Francesc Castella, Óscar Rivero

Abstract
. We construct an anticyclotomic Euler system for the Asai Galois representation associated to p p -ordinary Hilbert modular forms over real quadratic fields. We also show that our Euler system classes vary in p p -adic Hida families. The construction is based on the study of certain Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles obtained from modular curves of varying level diagonally emdedded into the product with a Hilbert modular surface. By Kolyvagin’s methods, in the form developed by Jetchev–Nekovář–Skinner in the anticyclotomic setting, the construction yields new applications to the Bloch–Kato conjecture and the Iwasawa Main Conjecture.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we construct an anticyclotomic Euler system for the Asai Galois representation associated to
p
-ordinary Hilbert modular forms over real quadratic fields. We also show that our Euler system classes vary in
p
-adic families.
Using Kolyvagin’s methods, in the form developed by Jetchev–Nekovář–Skinner
[
JNS
]
in the anticyclotomic setting, our construction yields new applications towards the Bloch–Kato conjecture in rank one and towards the Iwasawa Main Conjecture.

1.1.
Setting

Let
F
be a real quadratic field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. With conventions as in Section
2
, we let
g\in S_{l}(\mathfrak{N}_{g},\chi_{g};\mathbb{C})
be a Hilbert eigenform over
F
of level
\mathfrak{N}_{g}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}
, parallel weight
l\geq 2
, and nebentype
\chi_{g}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant
-D<0
, and let
\psi
be a Hecke character of
\mathcal{K}
of conductor
\mathfrak{c}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}
and infinity type
(1-k,0)
for some even integer
k\geq 2
. We assume the self-duality condition

\chi_{\psi}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}}\chi_{g}|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f}^{\times}}=1,

where
\chi_{\psi}
denotes the central character of
\psi
, and
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{K}}
is the quadratic character corresponding to
\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{Q}
. Put
N=N_{\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{c})DN_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{N}_{g})
. Fix a prime
p\nmid 2N
and an embedding
\iota_{p}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
in
\mathbb{C}
, and let
E=L_{\mathfrak{P}}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
containing the image under
\iota_{p}
of
the Fourier coefficients of
g
and the values of
\psi
. Let
\rho_{g}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{g})\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(E)
be the
p
-adic Galois representation attached to
g
, and let

\mathrm{As}(V_{g}):=\otimes\mbox{-}\mathrm{Ind}_{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}(V_{g})

be the associated four-dimensional
Asai representation
.

1.2.
The Euler system

A main result of this paper is the construction of an anticyclotomic Euler system for the conjugate self-dual
G_{\mathcal{K}}
-representation

V:=\mathrm{As}(V_{g})|_{G_{\mathcal{K}}}(\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}^{-1})(2-l-k/2),

where
\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}:G_{\mathcal{K}}\rightarrow L^{\times}
is the
p
-adic avatar of
\psi
.

For each positive integer
n
, we let
\mathcal{K}[n]
denote the maximal
p
-extension inside the ring class field of
\mathcal{K}
of conductor
n
, and let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all squarefree products of primes
q
split in both
F
and
\mathcal{K}
and coprime to
pN
. For any prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathcal{K}
where
V
is unramified, put

P_{\mathfrak{q}}(V;X)=\det(1-\Fr_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}X|V),

where
\Fr_{\mathfrak{q}}
denotes an arithmetic Frobenius element at
\mathfrak{q}
. The representation
\mathrm{As}(V_{g})
appears in the middle-degree cohomology of Hilbert modular surfaces, and so taking integral coefficients we obtain a
G_{\mathcal{K}}
-stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
T\subset V
. Let
h_{\mathcal{K}}
denote the class number of
\mathcal{K}
, and let

\mathrm{Sel}_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],T)\subset H^{1}_{\Iw}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],T):=\varprojlim_{s}H^{1}(\mathcal{K}[np^{s}],T)

be the
balanced Selmer group
introduced in Section
2.7
; in the range
k<2l
, this interpolates the Bloch–Kato Selmer groups for
T
over the tower
\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}]/\mathcal{K}[n]
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.7
)
.

Suppose
p
splits in
\mathcal{K}
and
p\nmid h_{\mathcal{K}}
, and that
g
is ordinary at
p
.
There exists a collection of classes

\left\{{\kappa_{\psi,g,n,\infty}}\in\mathrm{Sel}_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],T)\;\colon\;m\in\mathcal{S}\right\}

such that whenever
n,nq\in\mathcal{S}
with
q
a prime, we have

{\cores}_{\mathcal{K}[nq]/\mathcal{K}[n]}({\kappa_{\psi,g,nq,\infty}})=P_{\mathfrak{q}}(V;{\Fr}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1})\,{\kappa_{\psi,g,n,\infty}},

where
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime of
\mathcal{K}
above
q
.

It follows from our construction that the Euler system classes of Theorem
A
are interpolated
p
-adically along the parallel weight Hida family
\mathscr{G}
passing through the ordinary
p
-stabilization of
g
. Indeed, the results of Section
4
yield a construction of two-variable families of cohomology classes

\kappa_{\infty}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{V}_{\mathscr{F}\mathscr{G}}^{{\dagger}})

where
\mathscr{F}
(resp.
\mathscr{G}
) is a Hida family of elliptic modular forms (parallel weight Hilbert modular forms), and
\mathbb{V}_{\mathscr{F}\mathscr{G}}^{{\dagger}}
is a self-dual twist of the tensor product
\mathbb{V}_{\mathscr{F}}\otimes\mathrm{As}(\mathbb{V}_{\mathscr{G}})
for the standard big Galois representations
\mathbb{V}_{\mathscr{F}}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\mathscr{G}}
associated to
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{G}
, respectively. The construction of the classes
\kappa_{\infty}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})
is based on the study of the
p
-adic étale Abel–Jacobi image of certain generalized Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles obtained from modular curves of varying
p
-power level diagonally embedded into the product with Hilbert modular surfaces, and is inspired by the approach by Darmon–Rotger
[
DR3
]
in the setting of triple products of elliptic modular forms.

The classes
{\kappa_{\psi,g,n,\infty}}
of Theorem
A
are obtained from a variant of the classes
\kappa_{\infty}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})
with added tame level at
n
by taking
\mathscr{F}
to be a CM Hida family attached to
\psi

and
\mathscr{G}
to be the parallel weight Hida family passing through
g
. The bulk of the work then goes into the proof of the tame Euler system norm relations, which is obtained by a careful study of the image under various degeneracy maps of the generalized Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles used in the construction. It would be interesting to study whether an alternative proof of our Euler systems norm relations can be given following the automorphic approach of Loeffler–Skinner–Zerbes
[
LSZ-JEMS
]
, as done by Grossi
[
Grossi-AF
]
in the related setting of Asai–Flach classes.

1.3.
Applications

By Kolyvagin’s Euler system machinery, in the form developed by Jetchev–Nekovář–Skinner
[
JNS
]
in the anticyclotomic setting, we deduce bounds on Selmer groups from the non-triviality of our Euler system. Our first result in this direction gives new evidence towards the Bloch–Kato conjecture
[
BK
]
in rank one.

In the next theorem, we use ‘big image’ to refer to Hypothesis (HS) in Section
6
; in Proposition
6.3
we shall give sufficient conditions under which this is satisfied. As a piece of notation, write

\kappa_{\psi,g,n}\in\mathrm{Sel}_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[n],T)

for the image of

\kappa_{\psi,g,n,\infty}
under the corestriction
H^{1}_{\Iw}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],T)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{K}[n],T)
.

Theorem B

(Theorem
)
.

Let the setting and hypotheses be as in Theorem
A
, and assume in addition that
V
has big image.
Let
{\kappa_{\psi,g}}:={\kappa_{\psi,g,1}}
. If
k<2l
, then the following implication holds:

{\kappa_{\psi,g}}\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dim_{E}\mathrm{Sel}(\mathcal{K},V)=1,

where
\Sel(\mathcal{K},V)\subset H^{1}(G_{\mathcal{K}},V)
is the Bloch–Kato Selmer group.

We also deduce applications to the Iwasawa Main Conjecture for
V
. More precisely, let

\mathrm{Sel}_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],A)\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],A)

be the balanced Selmer group dual to
\mathrm{Sel}_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],T)
, where
A=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(T,\mu_{p^{\infty}})
, and let
X_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[np^{\infty}],A)

denote its Pontryagin dual. Write
p\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
, with
\mathfrak{p}
the prime of
\mathcal{K}
above
p
induced by
\iota_{p}
, and put

\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}=\mathcal{O}[\![\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}]\!]
, where
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the Galois group of the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathcal{K}
unramified outside
\mathfrak{p}
. As explained in Section
5
, this can be naturally identified with the Iwasawa algebra for the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}]/\mathcal{K}
.

Theorem C

(Theorem
)
.

Let the setting and hypotheses be as in Theorem
A
, and assume in addition that
V
has big image. If the class
{\kappa_{\psi,g,\infty}}:={\kappa_{\psi,g,1,\infty}}
is not
\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}
-torsion, then the modules
\Sel_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}],T)
and
X_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}],A)
have both
\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}
-rank one, and

\Char_{\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}}(X_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}],A)_{\tors})\supset\Char_{\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}}\biggl(\frac{\Sel_{\bal}(\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}],T)}{\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot{\kappa_{\psi,g,\infty}}}\biggr)^{2}

as ideals in
\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, where the subscript
\mathrm{tors}
denotes the
\Lambda_{\mathscr{F}}
-torsion submodule.

1.4.
Further directions

We expect the classes
\kappa_{\infty}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})
to satisfy an explicit reciprocity law relating their images under a Perrin-Riou big logarithm map to a
p
-adic Asai
L
-function as constructed by Ishikawa
[
Ishikawa
]
in the
p
inert case. In particular, this would allow us to deduce a variant of Theorem
C
for the
\mathcal{F}
-unbalanced Selmer group
X_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}],A)
of Section
2.7
, as well as applications to the Bloch–Kato conjecture for
V
in analytic rank
0
in the range
k\geq 2l
. We plan to study these problems in a sequel to this paper.

It would also be interesting to study the implications of our Euler system in the “degenerate” case where
g=\mathrm{BC}(g_{\circ})
is the base-change of an elliptic modular form
g_{\circ}
. As observed in e.g.
[
Liu
, §1.3]
, in this case the Asai representation
\mathrm{As}(V_{g})
contains
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{g_{\circ}})
as a direct summand; following the approach in
[
LZ-ad
]
, one might hope to build on this decomposition and (a suitable variant of) the Euler system of Theorem
A
to improve on the results obtained
[
ACR2
]
towards the anticyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjecture for the adjoint representation of
g_{\circ}
.

1.5.
Related works

The Euler system constructed in this paper may be seen as an anticyclotomic analogue of the Asai–Flach Euler system of Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes
[
LLZ-AF
]
. Generalized Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles similar to those constructed in this paper were studied in
[
BF
]
and
[
FJ
]
; following the approaches of
[
DR1
]
and
[
DR2
]
, respectively, these works express the syntomic Abel–Jacobi images of these cycles in terms of special values of
p
-adic Asai
L
-functions outside the range of interpolation. (Unfortunately, the results of
[
BF
]
restrict to
g
of non-parallel weight, while
[
FJ
]
is subject to a conjecture on the integral cohomology of Hilbert modular surfaces.)

In a different direction, Kolyvagin’s methods, in a form influenced by the work of Bertolini–Darmon
[
BD2
]
using level-raising congruences, were first applied in the setting of Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles in work of Y. Liu
[
Liu
]
, with applications towards the Bloch–Kato conjecture analogous to our Theorem
B
in the case of parallel weight
2
(but allowing more general twisted triple products), in addition to certain counterparts in analytic rank
0
.

2.
Review of Hilbert cuspforms

In this section we summarize our conventions for Hilbert modular forms, which are mainly taken from
[
FJ
]
with some minor changes. Since for the purpose of this article we are only interested in Hilbert modular forms of parallel weight, we restrict the discussion to this case.

2.1.
General notations

Let
F
be a totally real number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
\mathrm{I}_{F}
denote the set of field embeddings of
F
into
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Note that there is a natural identification of
F_{\infty}\coloneqq F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{I}_{F}}
.
Consider the algebraic group

G\coloneqq\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{GL}_{2,F}).

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the Poincaré upper half plane. The identity component

G(\mathbb{R})^{+}
of

G(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{\infty})\simeq\prod_{\sigma\in I_{F}}\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\sigma})

acts on
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{I}_{F}}
via Möbius transformations. We denote by
i=\sqrt{-1}\in\mathcal{H}
the square root of
-1
belonging to
\mathcal{H}
and we consider the point
\mathbf{i}=(i,\dots,i)\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{I}_{F}}
.

2.2.
Adelic Hilbert cuspforms

Let
K\leq G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
be a compact open subgroup. A
(holomorphic) Hilbert cuspform
of parallel weight
l
and level
K
is a function
f:G(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfies the following properties:

\bullet

f(\alpha xu)=f(x)j_{l}(u_{\infty},\mathbf{i})^{-1}
for all
\alpha\in G(\mathbb{Q})
and
u\in K\cdot C_{\infty}^{+}
, where
C_{\infty}^{+}
is the stabilizer of
\mathbf{i}
in
G(\mathbb{R})^{+}
and the automorphy factor is defined by

j_{l}\big(\gamma,z\big)=\prod_{\sigma\in I_{F}}(a_{\sigma}d_{\sigma}-b_{\sigma}c_{\sigma})^{-1}(c_{\sigma}z_{\sigma}+d_{\sigma})^{l}

for all

\gamma=\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{\sigma}&b_{\sigma}\\
c_{\sigma}&d_{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\sigma\in I_{F}}\in G(\mathbb{R})
and
(z_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in I_{F}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{I}_{F}}
;

\bullet

for every finite adelic point
x\in G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
the (well-defined) function
f_{x}:\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{I}_{F}}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
f_{x}(z)=f(xu_{\infty})j_{l}(u_{\infty},\mathbf{i})
, where
u_{\infty}\in G(\mathbb{R})_{+}
satisfies
u_{\infty}\mathbf{i}=z
, is holomorphic;

\bullet

for all adelic points
x\in G(\mathbb{A})
and for all additive measures on

F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}
we have

\int_{F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}}f\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}x\bigg)da=0;

\bullet

if
F=\mathbb{Q}
, then for every finite adelic point
x\in G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
the function
|\mathrm{Im}(z)^{l/2}f_{x}(z)|
is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{H}
.

We denote by
S_{l}(K;\mathbb{C})
the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of Hilbert cuspforms of parallel weight
l
and level
K
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}
be an integral
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-ideal. We define the following compact open subgroups of
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
:

•

V_{0}(\mathfrak{N})=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in G(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\;\bigg\lvert\;c\in\mathfrak{N}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}\bigg\}
,

•

V_{1}(\mathfrak{N})=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in V_{0}(\mathfrak{N})\;\bigg\lvert\;d\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{N}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}}\bigg\}
,

•

V^{1}(\mathfrak{N})=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in V_{0}(\mathfrak{N})\;\bigg\lvert\;a\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{N}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}}\bigg\}
,

•

V(\mathfrak{N})=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in V_{0}(\mathfrak{N})\;\bigg\lvert\;a,d\equiv 1;\,b,c\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{N}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}}\bigg\}
.

We will frequently write
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N};\mathbb{C})
instead of
S_{l}(V^{1}(\mathfrak{N});\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 1
.

There is a notion of
q
-expansion of Hilbert modular forms (see, for instance,
[
pHida
, §1]
), and, for any subring
R\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N};R)
the
R
-module of cuspforms in
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N};\mathbb{C})
with Fourier coefficients in
R
.

2.3.
Hecke Theory

Let
K\leq G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
be a compact open subgroup. Suppose that
V(\mathfrak{N})\leq K
.

For every
g\in G(\mathbb{A})
, we define a double coset operator
[KgK]
acting on the space of adelic Hilbert cuspforms of level
K
. Let

KgK=\coprod_{i}\gamma_{i}K,

be the decomposition of
KgK
into left
K
-cosets. Then the action of
[KgK]
on an adelic Hilbert cuspform
f
is given by

\big([KgK]f\big)(x)=\sum_{i}f(x\gamma_{i}).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and let
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
be a uniformizer in
\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}
. With a slight abuse of notation, we also denote by
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
the idèle with
\mathfrak{q}
-component equal to
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
and all other components equal to
1
. The Hecke operator
T(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})
on
S_{l}(K;\mathbb{C})
is defined by

T(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})=\Big[K\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{pmatrix}K\Big].

If
V^{1}(\mathfrak{N})\leq K
or
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{N}
, the definition does not depend on the choice of uniformizer and we may denote this Hecke operator simply by
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
z\in Z_{G}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
(where
Z_{G}
denotes the center of
G
). The diamond operator
\langle z\rangle
on
S_{l}(K;\mathbb{C})
is defined by
(\langle z\rangle f)(x)=f(xz)
.

Put
\mathrm{cl}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{N})=F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}/F^{\times,+}_{\infty}(1+\mathfrak{N}\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{F}})^{\times}
. Given a character

\psi:\mathrm{cl}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{N})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},

we denote by
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N},\psi,\mathbb{C})
the submodule of
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N},\mathbb{C})
on which the diamond operator
\langle z\rangle
acts by
\psi(z)|z|_{\mathbb{A}_{F,f}}^{2-l}
for all
z\in\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}
.
Cuspforms in
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N},\psi,\mathbb{C})
are said to have nebentype
\psi
.

Definition 2.4
.

A cuspform
g\in S_{l}(\mathfrak{N};\mathbb{C})
is said to be an
eigenform
if it is an eigenvector for all the Hecke operators of the form
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
, with
\mathfrak{q}
a finite prime, and for all diamond operators
\langle z\rangle
, with
z\in Z_{G}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
.

We define the operator
T_{p}=\prod_{v\mid p}T_{v}
acting on
S_{l}(\mathfrak{N};\mathbb{C})
. If
p\mid\mathfrak{N}
, we may also denote this operator by
U_{p}
.

Definition 2.5
.

An eigenform
g\in S_{l}(\mathfrak{N};\mathbb{C})
is said to be
p
-ordinary
if the eigenvalue of
T_{p}
acting on
g
is a
p
-adic unit (under our fixed embedding
\iota_{p}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
).

2.4.
Galois representations

Let
g\in S_{l}(\mathfrak{N},\psi;\mathbb{C})
be an eigenform. For a sufficiently large finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we denote by

\rho_{g}:G_{F}\longrightarrow\GL(V_{g})=\GL_{2}(L)

the
p
-adic Galois representation attached to
g
by work of Eichler–Shimura, Deligne, Carayol, Wiles, Taylor. It is unramified outside
p\mathfrak{N}
, and, if
\Fr_{\mathfrak{q}}
is an arithmetic Frobenius element at a prime
\mathfrak{q}\nmid p\mathfrak{N}
and
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}
, then

\det(1-\rho_{g}(\Fr_{\mathfrak{q}})X)=1-a_{\mathfrak{q}}(g)X+\psi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})^{-1}|\mathfrak{q}|^{1-l}X^{2}.

Suppose that
g
is
p
-ordinary and
l\geq 2
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
F
above
p
. Let
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)
denote the unit root of the Hecke polynomial
X^{2}-a_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)X+\psi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}})^{-1}|\mathfrak{p}|^{1-l}
. Then,

\rho_{g}|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{1}&\ast\\
0&\epsilon_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
\epsilon_{2}
is the unramified character such that
\epsilon_{2}(\Fr_{\mathfrak{p}})=\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)
.

2.4.1.
Asai representations

When
F
is a real quadratic field, we denote by

\mathrm{As}(\rho_{g})=\otimes\mbox{-}\mathrm{Ind}_{F}^{\mathbb{Q}}\rho_{g}

the
p
-adic Galois representation obtained as the tensor-induction of
\rho_{g}
from
F
to
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
g
is
p
-ordinary, and put

\alpha_{p}(g)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}(g)\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(g)&\textrm{if $(p)=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}$ splits in $F$,}\\[1.99997pt]
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}(g)&\textrm{if $(p)=\mathfrak{p}$ is inert in $F$}.\end{cases}

Then the representation
\mathrm{As}(\rho_{g})|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is endowed with a three-step filtration

\mathrm{As}(V_{g})=\Fil^{0}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})\supset\Fil^{1}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})\supset\Fil^{2}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})\supset\Fil^{3}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})=0

with graded pieces of dimensions
1
,
2
, and
1
, respectively, and the graded piece
\Gr^{0}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})=\Fil^{0}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})/\Fil^{1}\mathrm{As}(V_{g})

2.5.
Hida families

Let

\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{F}})

for some integral ideal
\mathfrak{N}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
coprime to

Let

Definition 2.6
.

For any

-algebra

-adic cuspforms of tame level

When an

.

Let

which are congruent to

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{N}p^{\infty})

\displaystyle\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{+}_{\mathfrak{N}p}\backslash(1+p\mathcal{O}_{F,p})\times\mathrm{cl}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{N}p)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\xi_{z},\theta(z))

Definition 2.7

\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F})

3.2.

Let

and

be compact open subgroups of

and let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sh}(G,gK_{1}g^{-1}\cap K_{2})}

and

4.
Big Hirzebruch–Zagier classes

4.1.
A compatible collection of cycles

In this section we construct big Hirzebruch–Zagier cycles following the approach of
[
DR3
]
. The construction works both when
p
splits in
F
and when
p
is inert in
F
.

Let
F
be a real quadratic number field, let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be its ring of integers and let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
be the nontrivial automorphism of
F
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{F,p}
the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Given a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module (respectively
\mathcal{O}_{F,p}
-module)
\Omega
, we denote by
\Omega^{\prime}
the set of primitive elements in
\Omega
, i.e., the set of elements which are not divisible by any non-unit element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(respectively
\mathcal{O}_{F,p}
).

For each integer
\alpha\geq 1
, define the set

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\left(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\prime}\times\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\times\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\right)^{\prime}

equipped with the diagonal action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
by multiplication on the right. The quotient
\Sigma_{\alpha}/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
is equipped with a determinant map

D:\Sigma_{\alpha}/\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{F}\times\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{F}\right)^{\mathrm{tr=0}}

defined by

D\left((x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{1},y_{1})\right)=\left(\begin{vmatrix}x_{0}&y_{0}\\
x_{1}&y_{1}\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}x_{1}&y_{1}\\
x_{1}^{\sigma}&y_{1}^{\sigma}\end{vmatrix}\right)

For each pair
(t_{0},t_{1})\in\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{F}\times(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\mathrm{tr=0}}
, we define

\Sigma_{\alpha}[t_{0},t_{1}]=\left\{(v_{0},v_{1})\in\Sigma_{\alpha}:D(v_{0},v_{1})=(t_{0},t_{1})\right\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

The group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
acts simply transitively on
\Sigma_{\alpha}[t_{0},t_{1}]
if

[t_{0},t_{1}]\in I_{\alpha}\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{F}\right)^{\times}\times\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}\mathcal{O}_{F}\right)^{\times,\mathrm{tr=0}}.

Proof.

Let
(v_{0},v_{1}),(w_{0},w_{1})\in\Sigma_{\alpha}
satisfy
D(v_{0},v_{1})=D(w_{0},w_{1})
in
I_{\alpha}
. Since
\det(v_{1},v_{1}^{\sigma})=\det(w_{1},w_{1}^{\sigma})
is invertible, there is
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha})
such that

\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{1}^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\
w_{1}^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}.

We deduce that
\gamma^{\sigma}=\gamma
, i.e.
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
. We are left to show that
v_{0}\cdot\gamma=w_{0}
. There are
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{F}/p^{\alpha}
such that

v_{0}=a\cdot v_{1}+a^{\sigma}\cdot v_{1}^{\sigma},\qquad w_{0}=b\cdot w_{1}+b^{\sigma}\cdot w_{1}^{\sigma},

and we can compute that

a^{\sigma}\cdot\det(v_{1}^{\sigma},v_{1})=\det(v_{0},v_{1})=\det(w_{0},w_{1})=b^{\sigma}\cdot\det(w_{1}^{\sigma},w_{1}),

thus
a^{\sigma}=b^{\sigma}
. The claim follows.
∎

Let
K
be a compact open subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F})
. We assume that
V^{1}(\mathfrak{N})\subseteq K
for some ideal
\mathfrak{N}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
with
(p,\mathfrak{N})=1

, for all but finitely many primes
p
we can conjugate
\tilde{\rho}_{g,,g^{\sigma},p}^{\std}
so that
\tilde{\rho}_{g,g^{\sigma},p}^{\std}(H)=G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Note that although the result is stated in the setting of elliptic modular forms, it follows from [
op. cit.
, Rem. 2.3.2] that the same result holds in the setting of Hilbert modular forms.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime of
L
above
p
, and let
E
be the subfield of
L_{\mathfrak{P}}
generated by the field
F_{g}
. We say that the prime
\mathfrak{P}
is
good
if the following conditions hold:

•

p\geq 7
;

•

p
is unramified in
B
;

•

p
is coprime to
\mathfrak{c}
and
N_{g}
;

•

\tilde{\rho}_{g,g^{\sigma},p}^{\std}(H)=G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
;

•

E=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that there is at least one prime which divides
D_{\mathcal{K}}
but not
D_{F}N_{g}
. Then, if
\mathfrak{P}
is a good prime,

(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}\times\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})(H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}})=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Proof.

For the ease of notation, write
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
for the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathcal{K}[p^{\infty}]
of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
F(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}})
and
F(\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})
be the Galois extensions of
F
cut out by the representations
\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}
and
\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}}
attached to
g
and
g^{\sigma}
, respectively, and note that they are unramified outside
pD_{F}N_{g}
. Therefore, the condition on
D_{\mathcal{K}}
implies that
F\mathcal{K}\cap F(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}})=F
and
F\mathcal{K}\cap F(\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})=F
. Moreover, since any Galois extension of
F
contained in
F\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
must itself contain
F\mathcal{K}
, we also have
F\mathcal{K}_{\infty}\cap F(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}})=F
and
F\mathcal{K}_{\infty}\cap F(\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})=F
.

The conditions on
\mathfrak{P}
imply that

(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}\times\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})(H\cap G_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})})=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

and, from the remarks in the previous paragraph, it follows that

(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}\times\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})(H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})})=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Finally, since
H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}}
is a normal subgroup of
H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})}
of index dividing
p-1
and there are no such subgroups in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, the lemma follows.
∎

Now we are able to give conditions on
\mathfrak{P}
under which the results of
[

]
can be applied to our setting, i.e. to the representation
T_{\mathfrak{P}}
defined above. We say that

is
non-Eisenstein
(resp.

-distinguished

if the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{g,\mathfrak{P}}
associated to
\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}
is irreducible (resp. the semi-simplication of
\bar{\rho}_{g,\mathfrak{P}}|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is non-scalar for every prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
F
above

).

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume that there is at least one prime which divides
D_{\mathcal{K}}
but not
D_{F}N_{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a good prime above
p
at which
g
is non-Eisenstein and
p
-distinguished, and assume that there exists
\eta\in G_{F\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}}
such that
\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\eta)\neq\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}^{c}(\eta)
modulo
p
. Then hypothesis (HS) in
[
ACR
, §8.1]
hold for
T_{\mathfrak{P}}
.

Proof.

Since
\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}

is trivial when restricted to
H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}}
, condition (1’) follows easily from the previous Lemma
6.2
. To prove condition (2’), we closely follow the proof of
[
Loe
, Prop. 4.2.1]
. We regard
\chi_{g}
as a finite order character of
G_{F}
using class field theory. By Lemma
6.2
, the image of
\eta H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}}
under
\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}\times\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}}
contains all the elements of the form

\left(\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{-1}\chi_{g}(\eta)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
0&y^{-1}\chi_{g}^{\sigma}(\eta)\end{pmatrix}\right),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}.

Now choose
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
satisfying

x^{-2}\chi_{g}(\eta)\not\equiv 1\pmod{p},\quad x^{2}\chi_{g}^{\sigma}(\eta)\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\eta)^{-2}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p},

which is possible since
p\geq 7
, and let
y=x^{-1}\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\eta)
. Choose
\sigma_{0}\in\eta H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}}
whose image under
\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}\times\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}}
is given by the element above, with the choices of
x
and
y
which we have just specified. Then, the eigenvalues of
\sigma_{0}
acting on
T_{\mathfrak{P}}
are 1,
x^{-2}\chi_{g}(\eta)
,
x^{2}\chi_{g}^{\sigma}(\eta)\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\eta)^{-2}
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}^{c}(\eta)\psi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\eta)^{-1}
, which proves condition (2’).

To check condition (3’), we can argue as in
[
KLZ
, Rem. 11.1.3]
. By the previous lemma, we can find an element
\tau_{0}\in H\cap G_{\mathcal{K}(p^{\infty})^{\circ}}
such that

(\rho_{g,\mathfrak{P}}\times\rho_{g^{\sigma},\mathfrak{P}})(\tau_{0})=\left(\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right),

so
\tau_{0}
acts on
T_{\mathfrak{P}}
as multiplication by
-1
. Finally, condition (4’) follows from the assumption that
g
is non-Eisenstein and
p
-distinguished.
∎

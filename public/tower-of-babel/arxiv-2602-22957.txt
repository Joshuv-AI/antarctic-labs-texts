Magnetized BPS lumps in the ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1} model with Maxwell coupling
I. B. Cunha, F. C. E. Lima, Aldo Vera

Abstract
We investigate Bogomol’nyi–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) topological configurations in the ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1} model minimally coupled to a Maxwell gauge field. Starting from the nonlinear O ⁡ ( 3 ) O(3) sigma model, we explicitly construct its classical mapping to the ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1} formulation, emphasizing the emergence of a local U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge symmetry intrinsically linked to the Fubini–Study target-space geometry. Focusing on the static sector, we analyze magnetized BPS lump configurations. By examining the topological boundary conditions, we show that the magnetic flux is quantized and fully determined by the asymptotic behavior of the gauge field. Within the BPS framework, we identify the specific self-interaction potential required for the existence of self-dual configurations. A detailed analysis of the asymptotic behavior, both near the lump core and at spatial infinity, demonstrates that finite-energy solutions necessarily correspond to lump-like BPS configurations in the ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1} target space, with the scalar field vanishing asymptotically. The resulting BPS equations are solved numerically, and the main physical properties of the solutions are presented. We find that these configurations are regular, magnetically localized, and energetically stable, with their internal structure rigidly controlled by the geometry of the ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1} target space. In contrast to Abelian Higgs vortices, the configurations discussed here do not rely on spontaneous symmetry breaking but arise purely from the geometry of the ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1} target space.

V
Summary and conclusion

In this work, we investigated self-dual radially symmetric lump in the
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
model coupled to a Maxwell gauge field, emphasizing the central role played by the target-space geometry and its topological implications. Starting from the formulation of the nonlinear
O(3)
-sigma model, we explicitly established the classical mapping to the
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
theory, highlighting the natural emergence of a local
U(1)
gauge symmetry concerning the structure of the target space endowed with the Fubini–Study metric.

In the static and purely magnetic regime, we demonstrated that the imposition of well-defined topological boundary conditions leads to the quantization of the magnetic flux, which is determined exclusively by the asymptotic values of the gauge field. This result reinforces the topological nature of the solutions. Furthermore, we show that the magnetic charge is independent of the local details of the field profiles, being governed solely by the homotopy classes associated with the winding number.

The implementation of the BPS approach allowed us to clearly identify the structure of the self-interaction potential that corresponds to saturating the lower energy bound. We noted that the energetic structure of the system is rigidly controlled by the auxiliary function
\mathcal{W}(f)
, which is directly related to the curvature of the
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
target space. The resulting first-order equations ensure not only energy minimization but also the classical stability of the solutions.

The asymptotic analysis revealed a crucial feature of the model, i.e., the existence of finite-energy solutions requires the
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
scalar field to vanish at spatial infinity. Consequently, the admissible solutions do not interpolate between distinct vacua but instead correspond to lump-like configurations in the target space, which fundamentally distinguishes them from the conventional NO-like vortices of the Abelian Higgs model. This result demonstrates that the Fubini–Study geometry not only modifies the theory quantitatively but also qualitatively alters the nature of its topological excitations.

The numerical solutions of the BPS equations fully corroborate the analytical analysis. The profiles obtained for the scalar and gauge fields rigorously satisfy the boundary conditions and exhibit regular behavior throughout the entire radial domain. The magnetic field is localized and vanishes both at the core and at infinity, with a maximum located in an intermediate region, reflecting the functional relation imposed by the BPS equation
B=\pm\mathcal{W}(f)
. Similarly, the BPS energy density is finite, positive, and localized, ensuring the convergence of the total energy and confirming saturation from the Bogomol’nyi bound.

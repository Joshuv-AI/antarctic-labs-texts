Application of the Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis Patient-Reported Outcome Measure (IPF-PROM) scale to a Greek IPF patient population and its interrelation with depressive symptoms and health-related quality of life
Authors: Maria Tziraki, Andreas M Matthaiou, Manolis Linardakis, Eirini Vasarmidi, Aristea Zouraraki, Nikoleta Bizymi, Dimitrios Papagiannis, Ioannis Tomos, Foteini Malli, Zoe Daniil, Anne-Marie Russell, Katerina M Antoniou, Emmanouil K Symvoulakis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIdiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive debilitating lung disease which affects physical and mental well-being. The IPF Patient-Reported Outcome Measure (IPF-PROM) scale is a validated and reliable tool for the self-report of physical and psychological well-being in IPF.Aims and objectivesThis study aimed to validate a Greek version of the IPF-PROM scale and further investigate its correlation with clinical features of IPF patients and its interrelation with depressive symptoms and health-related quality of life (HRQoL).MethodsThis was a two-centre, observational, cross-sectional study, in which IPF patients completed three scales: IPF-PROM, Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), an index of depressive symptoms, and Health Survey Questionnaire Short Form-12 (SF-12), an index of HRQoL, at the IPF Outpatient Clinics of two University Hospitals in Greece during 2023–2024. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess severe status of IPF-PROM compared with mild/moderate status.ResultsThe study involved 136 IPF patients (87.9% males) with a mean age of 73.5±7.9 years. Patients were classified overall with moderate disease according to IPF-PROM mean scores (41.7±31.3), particularly in the combined Breathlessness/Fatigue and the Psychological well-being components (40.1 and 43.8, respectively, p>0.050). Patients with severe symptoms, as measured by IPF-PROM, scored higher levels of depressive symptoms on PHQ-9 compared with those with moderate or mild symptoms (17.1, 12.3 and 1.4, respectively, p<0.001) and lower levels of HRQoL in physical (32.6, 34.7 and 47.9, p<0.001) and mental health on SF-12 (23.9, 34.5 and 50.3, p<0.001). Patients with incrementally higher levels of oxygen saturation had lower odds for severe health status according to IPF-PROM (OR=0.83, p=0.018).ConclusionsA significant percentage of IPF patients present with impaired health status and symptoms suggestive of depression. The IPF-PROM scale represents a useful tool that may predict impairment of mental health and HRQoL in IPF, with potential utility for clinical practice and research.

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic progressive interstitial lung disease (ILD) of unknown aetiology with high disease burden and poor prognosis.1 2 The prevalence of IPF is continuously increasing, although this is not clearly attributed to either a rise in cases or an increasing recognition and diagnosis of the disease. Patients usually present with exertional dyspnoea and non-productive cough, accompanied by restrictive impairment of pulmonary function, commonly complicated by acute exacerbations leading to respiratory failure. The diagnosis is mainly based on the presence of usual interstitial pneumonia pattern on high-resolution CT (HRCT) of the chest in the absence of an identifiable cause, while there is an urgent need for the establishment of accurate susceptibility, diagnostic and prognostic disease biomarkers.1 3 4

Despite the fact that IPF is by definition considered a lung-limited disease, several extrapulmonary consequences and comorbidities have been found in patients. Mental health problems, specifically anxiety disorders and mood disorders, are more commonly observed among patients with IPF than in the general population. A high prevalence of depressive symptoms was estimated in patients with IPF,5 6 while the development of depression was further associated with increased disease severity and symptom burden as well as poor health-related quality of life (HRQoL).7 8 The introduction of antifibrotic therapy in the management of IPF was shown to alleviate anxiety and depressive symptoms.9 Conversely, in a randomised controlled trial, the addition of palliative care did not significantly reduce symptoms of anxiety and depression and did not improve HRQoL compared with the usual care at a 6-month follow-up.10 The utility of antidepressant therapy in the treatment of coexisting IPF and depression is unknown, indicating the need for future clinical studies to investigate potential benefits and accurate tools for measurement.

Patient-centred outcome research in ILD has been defined as ‘a collection of reliable and valid endpoints that represent what matters most to individual patients in their day-to-day lives’. These outcomes represent the patients’ perspective reflecting their priorities, beliefs, hopes, and needs.11 Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are directly reported by patients themselves, including self-assessment of symptoms, aspects associated with HRQoL, clinical response to medical care, as well as self-reported adherence to therapeutic interventions. PROMs hold a vital role in patient-centred care and are increasingly used as key tools for monitoring disease progression and treatment response in clinical practice and research, including randomised controlled trials.12 13

The IPF-PROM scale is a validated and reliable tool for the assessment of elements of HRQoL in patients with IPF.14 It contains twelve items and four domains, psychological and physical experience of breathlessness, psychological well-being and energy (fatigue). The IPF-PROM scale could be used in clinical practice, strengthening in parallel the patient-clinician communication, and in clinical research for the quantification of different aspects of HRQoL in IPF.15 16 The scale was initially designed, validated and applied in English-speaking patients with IPF. However, its validation in other languages and different ethnic populations is required to ensure its alignment with the linguistic and cultural perception of its aspects by the patients. The aim of this study was to validate a Greek version of the IPF-PROM scale and further investigate its correlation with clinical features of patients with IPF and its interrelation with depressive symptoms and HRQoL, as expressed by the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) and the Health Survey Questionnaire Short Form-12 (SF-12), two well-used validated generic scales.

Materials and methods

Study design

This was an observational, cross-sectional study of patients with IPF investigating the interrelation of IPF-PROM with depressive symptoms and HRQoL. Patients were recruited at the time of their clinical ordinary consultations by different specialists at two distinct ILD referral centres in Greece. The IPF-PROM scale was translated and validated into the patients’ native language, and the validated, widely used PHQ-9 and SF-12 scales were used for the quantification of depressive symptoms and HRQoL, respectively, also aiming to minimise bias. The reporting of the overall aspects of the study follows the STROBE statement for reporting observational studies.17

Participant recruitment

Consecutive adult patients with IPF (n=136) attending follow-up appointments were recruited from the ILD Outpatient Clinic of the Department of Respiratory Medicine of the University Hospital of Heraklion in Crete, Greece from November 2023 to January 2024 (n=100 followed-up patients from a total of 128 registered patients) and the ILD Outpatient Clinic of the Department of Respiratory Medicine of the University Hospital of Larissa in Thessaly, Greece from September 2024 to November 2024 (n=36 followed-up patients from a total of 190 registered patients). All included patients were diagnosed with IPF based on compatible signs and symptoms and the radiological features of usual interstitial pneumonia on chest HRCT in the absence of identifiable causes and on the exclusion of alternative conditions, according to the recent guidelines.4 There were no exclusion criteria, other than the attendance of unscheduled outpatient appointments, during the selected time periods. The disparity in recruitment reflected a different clinical follow-up frequency protocol between the two centres. The recruitment was independent of disease severity, the coexistence of chronic respiratory failure and/or the subsequent need for long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT), or the particular treatment modalities, such as antifibrotic therapy. All patients were adults and provided their written informed consent for inclusion in the study.

Demographic and clinical data collection

Patients’ demographic and clinical data relevant to the aims of the study were collected from their medical records. Demographic and socioeconomic data included gender, age, race, family status, educational level and occupational status. Clinical data included nutritional status, smoking history, alcohol consumption, sleep hygiene, comorbidities, duration of disease, presence of chronic respiratory failure and need for LTOT, symptoms of fatigue, exertional dyspnoea and non-productive cough, signs of digital clubbing and oxygen saturation (SpO2). Nutritional status was evaluated using body mass index thresholds of underweight—less than 18.5 kg/m2, normal—between 18.5 and 24.9 kg/m2, overweight—between 25 and 29.9 kg/m2 and obese status—30 and above kg/m2. Sleep hygiene was assessed by the self-reported duration of nocturnal sleep and difficulty in nocturnal sleeping. Difficulty in sleeping was defined as any of the following: difficulty in falling asleep, waking up frequently during the night or waking up too early in the morning, as previously used.18 19 Twelve clinically significant chronic conditions were specifically selected to be recorded as comorbidities, based on their previously described impact on HRQoL in IPF20 21: hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidaemia, cardiovascular disease, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, hepatic disease, thyroid diseases, malignant diseases, osteoporosis, anxiety disorder and depression.

IPF-PROM scale translation and validation in Greek

The IPF-PROM scale is a validated 12-item self-reported measure of health status in patients with IPF (online supplemental table 1). It is composed of four domains, which assess: (1) somatic experience of breathlessness, (2) psychological experience of breathlessness, (3) fatigue and (4) psychological well-being. Response categories are a four-level Likert scale scored in a range from 1 to 4, so that the total score ranges from 12 to 48. The total score and sub-scores can be transformed to a scale from 0 to 100, with higher values indicating worse health status and worse breathlessness/fatigue or psychological well-being. Scores are stratified into three levels, using the cutoffs of the third quarters of the distributions, as follows: mild impairment (scores 0–33), moderate impairment (scores 34–67) and severe impairment (scores 68–100) of symptoms.1416

The IPF-PROM scale was translated from its original English format and validated into a Greek version for use in the study. Test-retest reliability was implemented with 16 participants, with a completion intermediate mean time of 16 days between two-time phases, A and B. Pearson correlation coefficient (r) was estimated for the total cumulative scores of the twelve items (r=0.605, p=0.013). For the evaluation of the construct validity of the IPF-PROM scale for a Greek population, exploratory factor analysis and principal component analysis were performed. Orthogonal (Varimax with Kaiser normalisation) and oblique rotations were used. Keiser-Meyer-Olkin index for sampling adequacy was assessed at 0.936 (meritorious fit), and Bartlett’s index of sphericity for the determination of data appropriateness for the implementation of factor analysis and the verification of the degree of interaction among the scale items was estimated at χ2=2429.05 with df=66 and p<0.001. As already estimated by Russell and colleagues,1416 in the current analysis, two factors with eigenvalues >1 were found, as well as 86.3% interpretation of the total variation of the twelve items. As a minimum load limit was defined the criterion of 0.6 for the inclusion of the 12 items in each factor. There was an exception of the 12th item (“Felt frustrated by being too tired to do the things you like to do?”), which was found with a higher load in the second factor (0.730) compared with the first (0.588) in contrast to the initial validation of the scale. Thus, the two factors that were assessed and confirmed in the initial validation by Russell and colleagues were: (1) breathlessness/fatigue (items: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10 and 11) and (2) psychological well-being (items: 7, 8, 9 and 12).

The consistency of the responses of the scales was assessed using Cronbach’s α coefficient, in test–retest phases or during the validation of the IPF-PROM scale. Cronbach’s α was estimated in total (α=0.972) as well as in breathlessness/fatigue (α=0.967) and in psychological well-being (α=0.966).

Scale completion and score calculation

All patients were asked to complete three questionnaires (IPF-PROM, PHQ-9 as an index of depression and SF-12 as an index of overall HRQoL) at routine visits to the IPF Outpatient Clinics of the University Hospital of Heraklion and the University Hospital of Larissa. The completion of the questionnaires was performed with support from a clinical scientist as needed to ensure that the patients understood each question and were able to respond appropriately. IPF-PROM, PHQ-9, and SF-12 scores were calculated based on the patients’ responses.

The PHQ-9 scale is a validated 9-item self-reported measure used for detecting and determining the severity of depression. PHQ-9 score is calculated on a scale from 0 to 27, with higher values indicating higher severity of depression. Scores are stratified into five levels, as follows: none/minimal (scores 0–4), mild (scores 5–9), moderate (scores 10–14), moderately severe (scores 15–19) and severe (scores 20–27) depression.2224

The SF-12 scale is a validated twelve-item self-reported measure of HRQoL that was developed from the previous version, that is, SF-36, as a shorter alternative tool for use in large-scale health measurements. It focuses on overall health outcomes and includes two components that measure the physical and mental health functioning. It is calculated on a scale from 0 to 100, with higher values indicating better health status, while cutoffs for physical and mental impairment are defined as 50 and 42, respectively, as previously described.2528

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was carried out using the SPSS programme (IBM, Released 2019, IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, V.25.0, IBM). Frequency distributions and measures of location and dispersion of the characteristics of the 136 patients were calculated. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages, while continuous variables were expressed as mean and SD. Scales’ scores were checked for symmetry using Blom’s method (QQ plot). The consistency of the responses of the scales was assessed using Cronbach’s α coefficient in test–retest phases or during the validation of the IPF-PROM scale. The validation of the IPF-PROM scale was also based on exploratory factor analysis and principal component analysis, as previously described. Using the Mann-Whitney U method, components of IPF-PROM and SF-12 scores were compared, while PHQ-9 and SF-12 scores were compared in relation to the three categories of IPF-PROM status (mild, moderate and severe) using the Kruskal-Wallis method. For the correlation of IPF-PROM scores with the patients’ characteristics, rho-Spearman’s coefficient was used. Based on significant relationships, two models’ hierarchical multiple logistic regression analysis was conducted for severe status of IPF-PROM compared with mild/moderate status (nested models). In the first model, the basic significant characteristics were used, while the PHQ-9 and the SF-12 scales were added in the second, independently assessing their additive value. The acceptable level of significance was set at 0.05.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, management and conduct of the study. The patients who took part in the study will be informed of the results of the study after the publication and will be encouraged to disseminate them in the general public.

Results

The study involved 136 patients with IPF (n=112, 87.9% males) with a mean age of 73.5±7.9 years (table 1). Among them, 80.1% were married or in a relationship, 21.3% had university or technological school education, and 71.3% were unemployed or retired. The mean duration of the disease was 26.6±20.2 months, while in 63.6% of the patients the diagnosis was established more than 12 months prior to enrolment. Dyspnoea was found in 69.9% of the patients, cough in 53.7%, weakness in 39%, and clubbing (hippocratisme digital) in 5.1%. The presence of velcro crackles was noticed in 96.3%, while the mean level of SpO2 was 94.8%, with 23.5% of the patients being prescribed LTOT. Regarding comorbidities, 37.5% had at least three chronic conditions, with hypertension being the most prevalent (58.1%), followed by dyslipidaemia (41.9%), diabetes mellitus and cardiovascular disease (27.9%) (online supplemental figure 1). Current smokers were declared for 11.0%, and the mean time of self-reported night sleep was estimated at 7.4±1.6 hours, while 16.9% of the patients faced difficulty in sleep. Based on body mass index criteria, 77.9% were classified as overweight/obese (table 1).

Discussion

As a chronic debilitating respiratory disease, IPF impairs emotional well-being and HRQoL and is commonly accompanied by variable levels of anxiety and depression.2931 In patient-centred care and clinical research, PROMs constitute an important means for the self-report of symptoms and overall HRQoL directly from patients, while they also represent useful tools to monitor disease progression, treatment adherence and treatment response.12 13 32 The IPF-PROM scale is a recently developed PROM for IPF that has been only preliminarily used so far.15 16 The aim of this study was to investigate the relationship of IPF-PROM with clinical features of patients with IPF, depressive symptoms and HRQoL, including 136 patients that were recruited by two university hospitals in Greece.

Our results showed that patients with IPF demonstrated moderate impairment of health status and quality of life, as indicated by the IPF-PROM and SF-12 scales. Approximately half of the patients reported depressive symptoms of variable degrees of severity, as delineated by the PHQ-9 scale. The prevalence of depression is reported to be higher among patients with IPF compared with the general population.5 6 A strong linkage between chronic symptoms of dyspnoea and cough with the risk of anxiety and depression was observed in IPF, although the exact direction of this relationship remains obscure.33 In concordance with previous observations, approximately half of the included patients in this study demonstrated depressive symptoms, while 11% and 14% were classified as having moderately severe and severe depression, respectively. Similarly, severe impairment in psychological well-being was detected in 25.7% of this cohort using the IPF-PROM scale. Based on these scores, and despite the difference in the stratification of severity between the two scales, it is evident that approximately one in four patients with IPF concurrently present with clinically important depressive symptoms of high severity.

Those findings indicate that physical and mental impairment in patients with IPF occur in parallel, and this is adequately reflected in IPF-PROM, as both subscores were affected. In addition, patients with IPF reporting severe symptoms, as assessed by the IPF-PROM scale, had highly correlated scores indicating more depressive symptoms on the PHQ-9 scale and lower HRQoL in the physical and mental components of the SF-12 scale. Patients experienced a high burden of symptoms affecting both physical and mental well-being, thus pointing towards the unmet need for early identification and suitable psychosocial support.34

The presence and the level of hypoxaemia, as expressed by SpO2, was the parameter most strongly correlated with IPF-PROM score and subscores, irrespective of disease duration. In patients with IPF-PROM score of high severity, SpO2 was the most strongly associated clinical parameter, in addition to SF-12 mental component score. This observation implies that depressive symptoms appear to be more prevalent among patients with advanced disease associated with respiratory failure.35

A holistic approach with the use of multicomponent services, including non-pharmacological therapeutic modalities, is recommended to alleviate the devastating symptom of breathlessness and enhance well-being in patients with chronic respiratory diseases. Optimal programmes still need to be established on the management of IPF and on the individualisation of treatment to improve patient’s HRQoL.36 37 Beyond respiratory symptoms, other non-specific clinically important features, such as frailty and fatigue, are highly prevalent among patients with ILD and IPF and require multidisciplinary collaboration for their effective management.3841

This study reveals the need for holistic patient care in the management of IPF. Integrated healthcare strategies with the contribution of collaborative teams from different disciplines are paramount for addressing the mental component of impairment and providing effective palliative care.29 Promoting patient-centredness and clinical precision and bringing together different disciplines from primary to tertiary care are prerequisites for the optimisation of IPF management, reorientating it towards the specific needs of each individual patient.2 41 Implementing PROMs as part of the follow-up of patients with IPF in the context of family practice can also prove considerably useful, especially in countries with limited resources.42

In the context of a more holistic evaluation, it is evident that outcomes which focus on patients’ perceptions are of utmost need and will improve clinical care. Traditional research outcomes in ILD, such as pulmonary function, although important, are not considered sufficient enough from the patient’s perspective.37 It remains crucial to use and further develop objective measures of patients’ own perceptions of their HRQoL,43 in parallel with the need to identify interventions that can preserve or improve HRQoL.44 There has already been an effort to include PROMs in clinical trials, most commonly as secondary endpoints. However, most of the trials failed to demonstrate significant effects on HRQoL. Apart from the nature of the interventions, another reason for that may be the paucity of widely-validated disease-specific PROMs. Validity is also purpose-specific, since each PROM instrument has different characteristics that may fit better for certain uses than others. The King’s Brief ILD (K-BILD) questionnaire and Living with IPF (L-IPF) questionnaire have been developed for patients with IPF. The K-BILD questionnaire was created as a disease-specific tool to assess health status in patients with ILD and was found to be a useful tool in evaluating the symptom of dyspnoea.4547 The L-IPF questionnaire has five scales (symptoms total, dyspnoea, cough, energy and impacts total) and its use showed a beneficial impact of antifibrotic therapy in non-IPF progressive fibrotic ILD.48 49 L-IPF only indirectly assesses mood and does not assess therapy-related effects, while both questionnaires lack longitudinal validity. The IPF-PROM scale assesses four domains, including breathlessness, fatigue and psychological well-being, in twelve items that make it easy to perform. It was developed using person-centred methodologies, which, importantly, means that the IPF-PROM questionnaire is easy to understand and complete in reasonable time by the patients. The current results seem promising for its use in the evaluation of HRQoL, providing an important association with mental health, although further validation is needed.

This study has multiple strengths. It is the first of its kind to implement the recently established IPF-PROM scale in clinical settings after its development and validation. It also included the first translation, validation and use of the IPF-PROM scale in a foreign language. The interrelation of a PROM specifically developed for IPF with other validated measures of depression and HRQoL was also conducted for the first time. Limitations of the study include the relatively small sample size, without power analysis conducted, as well as the lack of longitudinal data, which would further show the possible variations of IPF-PROM in relation to clinical alterations. Data from lung function tests and chest HRCT were not included, thus not enabling the investigation of possible associations between IPF-PROM and disease severity. Finally, this study focused entirely on a Greek IPF patient population, and further validation of the IPF-PROM scale in other languages is required for its implementation in other ethnic populations.

Beyond depression, it is known that fatigue is a common complaint in IPF populations but is complex to measure due to its wide-ranging underlying mechanisms and related constructs. Broadly, fatigue includes self-perception (measured by self-report) and objective observation (measured by physical functioning). A key element of future longitudinal validation studies will be direct comparisons between the IPF-PROM scale and more complex instruments that capture fatigue, such as the Fatigue Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy—13-item Fatigue Scale—to test whether this simple, short instrument has measurement properties comparable to other more complex, generic, validated symptom measures currently available.

Depressive symptoms are highly prevalent in advanced IPF, highlighting the need for monitoring the mental health of the patients. The IPF-PROM scale is highlighted as an accurate tool for the self-assessment of overall HRQoL in IPF as well as patients’ own perception of the cardinal symptoms of dyspnoea and fatigue and their emotional status with the capability of revealing depressive symptoms. Its implementation in clinical practice and research is both feasible and dually beneficial for the clinicians and the patients.

The Hénon equation in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Pablo Ochoa, Ariel Salort

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Hénon problem in the setting of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces: (0.1) { − Δ g ​ u = | x | α ​ h ​ ( u ) in ​ B u > 0 in ​ B u = 0 on ​ ∂ B \begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u=|x|^{\alpha}h(u)\quad\text{in }B\\ u>0\quad\text{in }B\\ u=0\quad\text{on }\partial B\\ \end{cases} where B B is the unit ball in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , g = G ′ g=G^{\prime} , h = H ′ h=H^{\prime} are N-functions and the operator − Δ g -\Delta_{g} is the g g -Laplacian. We show that the symmetric term | x | α |x|^{\alpha} , for α > 0 \alpha>0 , allows to have radial solutions even for supercritical H H , generalizing results for the classical Hénon equation. We also show that radial solutions are indeed bounded. Finally, we state a Pohozaev’s identity in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces that we apply to get a range in α \alpha for which problem ( 0.1 ) has no bounded solutions.

1.
Introduction

The classical Hénon’s equation
[
16
]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=|x|^{\alpha}u^{q-1}\quad\text{in }B\\
u>0\quad\text{in }B\\
u=0\quad\text{on }\partial B,\end{cases}

where
B
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
q>1
and
\alpha>0
. In
[
16
]
, a related model, called spheric shell model, was used to investigate numerically the stability of spherical steady-state stellar systems with respect to spherical disturbances.

This equation has been the starting point of extensive research on existence and nonexistence results, symmetry of ground states, asymptotic behavior of solutions, and other related properties. The literature on the subject is now vast and it is impossible to give a comprehensive list of references; we refer to the seminal works
[
2
,
3
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
28
,
31
]
and the references therein.

In
[
2
]
, a similar problem to (
1.1
) was considered but with more general lower order terms
f(x,u)
. It was showed there that if the growth of
f
is less than
|u|^{q-1}
, with
q<2^{*}:=2n/(n-2)
, then a solution exists. On the other hand, the Pohozaev’s identity shows that no solution exist for
\alpha=0
,
q\geq 2^{*}
, and for a star-shaped domain. A remarkable progress in the study of (
1.1
) was made in the work
[
21
]
. It was shown there that the addition of the symmetric term
|x|^{\alpha}
allows the existence of radial solutions even for the critical case and beyond. Indeed, it was proved that a positive and radial solution exists for (
1.1
) whenever the following relation holds

(1.2)

1<q<2_{\alpha}^{*}:=2^{*}+\dfrac{2\alpha}{n-2}=\frac{n(\alpha+2)}{n-2}.

The proof in
[
21
]
makes use of the well-known Mountain Pass Theorem of Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz
[
2
]
via a variational approach. We also point out that since the term
|x|^{\alpha}
is increasing, the celebrated theorem of
[
14
]
about existence of symmetric solutions does not apply.

The nonlinear version of the Hénon problem (
1.1
) is obtained when replacing the Laplacian with the p-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}:=\mathop{\text{div}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
,
p>1
. This problem was addressed in
[
20
]
, and existence of a positive and radial solution was guaranteed when
p<n
and the exponent
q
lies within the range

(1.3)

p<q<p_{\alpha}^{*}:=p^{*}+\frac{\alpha p}{n-p}=\frac{n(\alpha+p)}{n-p},

being
p^{*}
the critical Sobolev exponent.

Other extensions of problem (
1.1
) have been studied in recent years, including nonlinear operators
[
20
,
7
]
, different boundary conditions
[
13
,
15
,
29
]
, nonlocal operators
[
30
,
17
]
, and mixed local–nonlocal operators
[
27
]
, to mention just a few. However, to the best of our knowledge, no results are available when nonstandard growth operators are involved in the equation. The purpose of this article is to address this gap.

We consider the following further generalization of the Hénon problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u=|x|^{\alpha}h(u)\quad\text{in }B\\
u>0\quad\text{in }B\\
u=0\quad\text{on }\partial B,\\
\end{cases}

where
\alpha>0
,
B
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
g=G^{\prime}
,
h=H^{\prime}
with
G
and
H
being N-functions (see Section
2
for details) and the operator

-\Delta_{g}u:=-\mathop{\text{div}}\left(\dfrac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\right)

is the
g
-Laplacian of
u
. We will assume the following growth behavior on
G
and
H
:

(1.5)

p^{-}G(t)\leq tg(t)\leq p^{+}G(t),\qquad q^{-}H(t)\leq th(t)\leq q^{+}H(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

for
p^{\pm},q^{\pm}\in(1,n)
.

We now turn to a discussion of our main contributions. First, we address the generalization of condition (
1.3
) in our framework. Indeed, given an N-function
G
and a parameter
\alpha>0
, we choose the N-function
H
such that the following relation holds:

(1.6)

p^{+}<q^{-}.

This ensures that the nonlinearity
h(u)
is superlinear with respect to the g-Laplacian. Moreover, we assume that
H\ll R
at infinity (see Section
2.1
) with
R
an N-function satisfying the condition

(1.7)

\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}R(\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)})\,dr<\infty,

where
\widetilde{G}
is the complementary function to
G
, and the Luxemburg’s norm is defined in Section
2.1
.

In the particular case of power functions, that is,
G(t)=t^{p}
and
H(t)=t^{q}
,
p,q>1
, we have
that (
1.6
) reads as
p<q
and condition (
1.7
) implies that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}\left(\int_{r}^{1}s^{\frac{(1-n)p}{p-1}}s^{n-1}\,ds\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\,dr=C\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}\left(r^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}-1\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\,dr\leq C\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1+q\frac{p-n}{p}}\,dr,\end{split}

which is finite if and only if
q<p_{\alpha}^{*}
, and hence recovering (
1.3
).

In this work, we are interested in proving existence and non-existence of weak solutions for problem (
1.4
). To seek non negative solutions, we shall consider the following energy functional

J(u)=\int_{B}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u_{+})\,dx,

defined on the space
X_{rad}(B)
of suitable radial functions on the ball
B
. Under assumption (
1.7
), we will see in Section
3
, that
J
is indeed well-defined. Moreover, its Frechet derivative is given by

\langle J^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\int_{B}g(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\cdot\nabla v\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}h(u_{+})v\,dx

for all
v\in X_{rad}(B)
. Hence weak solutions of (
1.4
) correspond to critical points of
J
.

Our first result concerns the existence of a weak solution to (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
and
H
be N-functions satisfying (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and
H\ll R
at infinity with
R
fulfilling (
1.7
). Then there exists a positive weak solution
u\in X_{rad}(B)
to (
1.4
).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the approach introduced by
[
21
]
. For this purpose, we require several ingredients, such as a suitable version of the Radial Lemma and the compact embeddings of the radial Orlicz–Sobolev spaces (see Section
4
). With these tools, we can then apply an appropriate version of the mountain-pass theorem.

By following a De Giorgi’s
L^{2}
to
L^{\infty}
iteration scheme, we will prove that any solution of (
1.4
) is indeed bounded. Specifically, we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
and
H
be N-functions satisfying (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) with
q^{+}<r^{-}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{tR^{\prime}(t)}{R(t)}
. Then, for any weak solution
u\in X_{rad}(B)
to (
1.4
), there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C.

We point out that in the case of powers, the assumption
q^{+}<r^{-}
turns into

q<p^{*}_{\alpha},\quad\text{for any }\alpha>0.

The nonexistence of solutions can be obtained for instance with scaling argments in the case of homogeneous operators (see for instance
[
25
]
). In particular, the Hénon problem (
1.1
) for the p-Laplacian has no weak bounded solutions when
p<n
and

q\geq p_{\alpha}^{*}.

Due to the nonhomogeneous nature of problem (
1.4
), in our case we follow the approach of applying a suitable version of Pohožaev-Pucci-Serrin type identity
[
23
]
for obtaining a range for non existence of bounded solutions. More precisely, we prove first the following identity for the weak solutions of the
g
-Laplacian.

Theorem 1.3

(Pohozaev’s type identity)
.

Assume that
G
is an N-function such that
G^{\prime}=g\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
and for constants
1<p^{-}<p^{+}<\infty
it holds that

(p^{-}-1)g(t)\leq g^{\prime}(t)t\leq(p^{+}-1)g(t),\quad t>0.

Let
f:\overline{B}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function satisfying
f(x,0)=0
, and let
u\in L^{\infty}(B)\cap W_{0}^{1,G}(B)
be a weak solution of

\begin{cases}-\mathop{\text{div}}\left(\dfrac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\right)=f(x,u)\quad\text{in }B\\
u=0\quad\text{on }\partial B.\end{cases}

Then, the following identity holds

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B}&\left(nF(x,u)\,dx+x\cdot\nabla_{x}F(\cdot,u)\right)dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{B}\left(g(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|-nG(|\nabla u|)\right)dx=\int_{\partial B}x\cdot\nu\left(g(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|-G(|\nabla u|)\right)d\sigma,\end{split}

with
F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\,ds
and where
\nu
denotes the outer normal unit vector to
\partial B
.

An application of the previous identity leads to the following non-existence result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
and
H
be N-functions satisfying (
1.5
) and such that
q^{-}\geq(p^{+})_{\alpha}^{*}
. Then, there is no solution
u\in L^{\infty}(B)\cap W^{1,G}_{0}(B)
of problem (
1.4
).

Beyond power functions, examples of N-functions satisfying the hypothesis of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.4
include, for instance, the following:

(i)

Power functions exhibiting different behaviors near 0 and at infinity:

G(t)=\frac{t^{p}}{p}+\frac{t^{q}}{q}\text{ with }1<p<q<\infty

In this case
p^{-}=p
,
p^{+}=q
.

(ii)

Logarithmic perturbation of powers:

G(t)=t^{p}\ln^{r}(e-1+t^{q})\text{ with }p,q,r>0\text{ tales que }p+qr>1.

In this case
p^{-}=p
,
p^{+}=p+qr
.

(iii)

Log-double polynomials:

G(t)=t^{p}(\ln(\ln(e^{e}-1+t)))^{s},\text{ with }p>1,s>0.

In this case
p^{-}=p
,
p^{+}=p+s
.

We highlight that, as is typical in Orlicz settings, the range conditions in Theorems 1.2 and 1.4 are not complementary, in contrast with the classical power case. Indeed, for
G(t)=t^{p}
with
p>1
, the relationship between
p
and
q
is complementary: existence holds whenever
q<p_{\alpha}^{*}
, whereas non-existence arises for bounded solutions when
q\geq p_{\alpha}^{*}
. However, we point out that when the N-functions in Theorems 1.2 and 1.4 are powers, then we certainly obtain the complementary range.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the basic notation concerning N-functions and the functional spaces employed throughout the paper. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to establishing useful properties of radial functions. Our main existence result is proved in Section
4
, while the boundedness of weak solutions is addressed in Section
6
. Finally, in Section
7
we prove a non-existence result.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some basic definitions and results related to Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

2.1.
N-functions

Definition 2.1
.

A function
G\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an N-function if it admits the representation

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau,

where the function
g
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing and satisfies the conditions

g(0)=0,\quad g(\infty)=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

By
[
26
, Chapter 1]
, an N-function has also the following properties:

(i)

G
is continuous, convex, increasing, even and
G(0)=0
.

(ii)

G
is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=\infty.

We will assume that
g
is extended as a odd function to the whole
\mathbb{R}
.

Given N-functions
G
and
H
we say that
G\ll H
at infinity if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{G(\lambda t)}{H(t)}=0

holds for any
\lambda>0
.

An important property for N-functions is the following:

Definition 2.2
.

An N-function
G
satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

G(2x)\leq CG(x)\,\,\text{~~~for all~~}x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By
[
26
, Theorem 4.1, Chapter 1]
, an N-function satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if and only if there is
p^{+}>1
such that

tg(t)\leq p^{+}G(t),~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

Associated to
G
is the N-function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\widetilde{G}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-G(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

The definition of the complementary function assures that the following Young-type inequality holds

(2.2)

st\leq G(t)+\widetilde{G}(s)\text{ for every }s,t\geq 0.

By
[
26
, Theorem 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the N-function
\widetilde{G}
complementary to
G
to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{-}>1
such that

p^{-}G(t)\leq tg(t),~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

Therefore, from now on, we will assume that for any
t>0

(2.3)

1<p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty.

2.2.
Orlicz-Lebesgue and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given an N-function
G

and an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we consider the spaces :

\displaystyle L^{G}(\Omega)=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\colon\int_{\Omega}G(|u(x)|)\,dx<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle W^{1,G}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx<\infty\right\}.

We also define the space
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
. We recall that under the assumption (
2.3
), the Orlicz-Lebesgue and the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are separable and reflexive spaces and are Banach spaces endowed with the Luxemburg’s norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\},\qquad\|u\|_{W^{1,G}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)},

respectively. In the space
W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
it is well-known that
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}
is an equivalent norm.

We recall that norms and modulars can be related as follows: for any
u\in L^{G}(B)
we have

(2.4)

\xi_{G,-}(\|u\|_{L^{G}(B)})\leq\int_{B}G(|u|)\,dx\leq\xi_{G,+}(\|u\|_{L^{G}(B)}),

where
\xi_{G,-}(t)=\min\{t^{p^{+}},t^{p^{-}}\}
and
\xi_{G,+}=\max\{t^{p^{+}},t^{p^{-}}\}
.

We end the section with the following Hölder inequality (see
[
6
, Lemma 2.6.5]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite, complete measure space.
If
f\in L^{G}(\Omega;d\mu)
,
g\in L^{\widetilde{G}}(\Omega;d\mu)
then

\int_{\Omega}|fg|\,d\mu\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{G}(\Omega;d\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{G}(\Omega;d\mu)}

where we denote

\|f\|_{L^{G}(\Omega;d\mu)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,d\mu\leq 1\right\}.

3.
Notion of solutions and preliminary results on radial functions

We introduce now the definition of weak solutions to problem (
1.4
).

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
u\in W_{0}^{1,G}(B)
is a weak solution of (
1.4
) if
u>0
a.e. in
B
and

\int_{B}\dfrac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}h(u_{+})v\,dx,

for every
v\in W_{0}^{1,G}(B)
.

In what follows, we consider the following function space of radially symmetric Orlicz-Sobolev functions:

X_{rad}(B):=\left\{u\in W_{0}^{1,G}(B),u\text{ is radial}\right\}.

The associated energy functional to problem (
1.4
) is

J(u)=\int_{B}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u_{+})\,dx,

defined on the space
X_{rad}(B)
. To prove that
J
is well-defined in
X_{rad}(B)
, we will need a decay estimate for radial functions. This is the content of the Strauss-type lemma for Orlicz-Sobolev spaces stated in the next Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
u\in X_{rad}(B)
. Then, for any
r\in(0,1)
it holds that

|u(x)|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)},

where
r=|x|
and
C>0
is a constant depending only of
n
.

Proof.

Since
u\in X_{rad}(B)
we may, with some abuse of notation, write
u(x)=u(|x|)=u(r)
. Moreover,
u(1)=0
. Observe that
u(r)=-\int_{r}^{1}u^{\prime}(s)\,ds
. Consider the measure
d\mu=s^{n-1}ds
. Then, Proposition
2.3
yields

\displaystyle|u(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{1}|u^{\prime}(s)|\,ds=\int_{r}^{1}|u^{\prime}(s)|s^{n-1}s^{1-n}\,ds=\int_{r}^{1}|u^{\prime}(s)|s^{1-n}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{G}((0,1);d\mu)}\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),d\mu)}.

Let us compute the first norm.
Using polar coordinates, we get that

\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{1}G\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}(s)|}{\lambda}\right)s^{n-1}\,ds=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S^{n-1}}G\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}(s)|}{\lambda}\right)s^{n-1}\,d\sigma ds=\int_{B}G\left(\frac{|\nabla u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx

from where it is obtained that

\int_{0}^{1}G\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}(s)|}{\lambda}\right)s^{n-1}\,ds=1\iff\int_{B}\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}G\left(\frac{|\nabla u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx=1.

This gives the following relation

\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{G}((0,1);d\mu)}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}G}(B)}\leq c_{n}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}

and the result follows.

Remark 3.3
.

Observe that when
G(t)=t^{p}
,
1<p<n
we get that

\displaystyle\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),d\mu)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{r}^{1}s^{(1-n)p^{\prime}}s^{n-1}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}=\left(\int_{r}^{1}s^{\frac{-n+1}{p-1}}\,ds\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{p-1}{n-p}\left(r^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}-1\right)\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C\left(r^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}=Cr^{1-\frac{n}{p}}.

Therefore, Proposition
3.2
recovers the well-known Strauss’ Lemma for radial functions on
W^{1,p}_{0}(B)
:

|u(x)|\leq C|x|^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B)}.

See
[
19
, Lemma II.1 and Remark II.3]
.

With the Strauss’ Lemma at hand, we may guarantee that the functiona
J
is well-defined.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that (
1.7
) holds. Then,
J(u)<\infty
for any
u\in X_{rad}(B)
.

Proof.

For
u\in X_{rad}(B)
, from Proposition
3.2
and the fact the
H
is increasing we get that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u)\,dx&\ =C\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}H(u(r))\,dr\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}H(\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)})\,dr\\
&\leq C\xi_{H,+}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)})\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}H(\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)})\,dr<\infty,\end{split}

in view of assumption (
1.7
).

Remark 3.5
.

We point out that the following Strauss’s Lemma is given in
[
1
]
. Assuming that
g
is strictly increasing and that
G
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, then, there is a constant
C>0
such that for every
u\in X_{rad}(B)
there holds

|u(x)|\leq G^{-1}\left(C|x|^{1-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|u|)+G(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right),

where
G^{-1}
is the inverse function of
G
and
u
is extended by
0
outside
B
. Hence, by Poncaré inequality (see for instance
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
), we may write the previous expression as

|u(x)|\leq G^{-1}\left(C|x|^{1-n}\int_{B}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right).

Applying the above Strauss’ Lemma to

v=u/\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}
, since
G^{-1}
is increasing, we obtain that

\begin{split}\dfrac{|u(x)|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}}\leq G^{-1}\left(C|x|^{1-n}\int_{B}G\left(\dfrac{|\nabla u|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}}\right)\,dx\right)=G^{-1}\left(C|x|^{1-n}\right),\end{split}

which implies

|u(x)|\leq G^{-1}\left(C|x|^{1-n}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}.

However, in contrast to Proposition
3.2
, in the power case the following decay estimate is obtained

u(x)\leq C|x|^{\frac{1-n}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B)},

which is not optimal.

4.
Embedding results for radial functions

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a non-negative Radon measure absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and let
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
L_{\mu}^{G}(\Omega)
such that
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
u
a.e. Then
u\in L_{\mu}^{G}(\Omega)
and
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L_{\mu}^{R}(\Omega)
for any
R\ll G
.

Proof.

Let us observe that
u\in L_{\mu}^{G}(\Omega)
. Indeed, by Fatou’s Lemma, we get that

\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,d\mu\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G(|u_{k}|)\,d\mu\leq C_{0},

where we have used that
u_{k}
converges to
u

\mu
-a.e. as well.

Let us prove that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L_{\mu}^{R}(\Omega)
when
R\ll G
. This condition means that

\frac{R(t)}{G(t)}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
T=T(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(4.1)

R(t)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8C}G(t)\quad\text{ for all }t\geq T,

with
C=C_{0}c
, being
c
the constant in the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. For further reference, define for each
k>0
, the set:

\Omega_{T,k}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|\geq T\right\}.

Since
u_{k}\to u

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
, by Egorov’s Theorem, given
\delta>0
there exists a measurable set
E_{\delta}\subset\Omega
such that
\mu(\Omega\setminus E_{\delta})<\delta
and
u_{k}\to u
uniformly in
E_{\delta}
. Due to the uniform convergence, given
\delta>0
, there is
K>0
such that
|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|\leq\delta
for all
x\in E_{\delta}
and for all
k>K
. Since
R
is continuous and increasing,
R(|u_{k}-u|)\leq R(\delta)
in
E_{\delta}
. We choose
\delta
such that

(4.2)

\delta<\min\left\{T,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{4R(T)}\right\},\quad R(\delta)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\mu(\Omega)}

and take the corresponding
K=K(\delta)
. Then, for
k>K
, we get from (
4.2
) that

\int_{E_{\delta}}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu\leq R(\delta)\mu(\Omega)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We next consider the set
\Omega\setminus E_{\delta}
. Observe that for
k>K
, we have

\Omega\setminus E_{\delta}=\left\{x\in\Omega:|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|\geq T,\text{ for some }k\right\}\cup\left\{x\in\Omega:\delta\leq|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|<T,\text{ for some }k\right\},

and moreover
\mu(\Omega\setminus E_{\delta})<\delta
. Observe that since
\delta<T
,

\Omega\setminus E_{\delta}=\Omega_{T,k}\cup\Omega\setminus(\Omega_{T,k}\cap E_{\delta}).

Then, for a fixed
k>K
and recalling (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega\setminus E_{\delta}}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu&=\int_{\Omega_{T,k}}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega\setminus(\Omega_{T,k}\cap E_{\delta})}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8C}\int_{\Omega_{T,k}}G(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega\setminus(\Omega_{T,k}\cap E_{\delta})}R(T)\,d\mu\\
&\leq\frac{c\varepsilon}{8C}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(|u_{k}|)\,d\mu+\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,d\mu\right)+R(T)\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\end{split}

Therefore, for all
k>K
,

\int_{\Omega}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega\setminus E_{\delta}}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu+\int_{E_{\delta}}R(|u_{k}-u|)\,d\mu\leq\varepsilon

which gives that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L_{\mu}^{R}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be an N-function and
\alpha>0
. Let
R
be an N-function such that (
1.7
) holds. Then, if
\mu=|x|^{\alpha}\,dx
, we have that

(i)

the inclusion
X_{rad}(B)\subset L_{\mu}^{R}(B)
is continuous;

(ii)

the inclusion
X_{rad}(B)\subset L_{\mu}^{H}(B)
is compact for any
H
such that
H\ll R
at infinity.

Proof.

First, we prove that the inclusion
X_{rad}(B)\subset L_{\mu}^{R}(B)
is continuous. Let
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence converging to some
u
in
X_{rad}(B)
. By (
3.1
) and assumption (
1.7
), we get

(4.4)

\begin{split}\int_{B}R(u_{k}-u)\,d\mu&\leq\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}R\left(C\|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\tilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)}\right)\,dr\\
&\leq C\xi_{R,+}(\|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)})\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}R\left(\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\tilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)}\right)\,dr\\
&=C\xi_{R,+}(\|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)})\to 0\end{split}

as

. This proves item
(i)
.

To prove
(ii),
let
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
X_{rad}(B)
. Observe that since
H\ll R
at infinity,
H
also satisfies (
1.7
) and hence the inclusion is continuous. Next, By compactness of the embedding
W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
into

for any bounded and smooth set
\Omega
, we get up to a subsequence that

a.e. in

. Moreover, by Proposition
3.2
, the sequence

is bounded in

satisfies (

,

for any

5.
Existence of solutions: proof of Theorem

5.1.
The Palais-Smale condition

Let us start proving that under suitable assumptions,

satisfies the Palais-Smale condition (see Definition

).

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). If
H\ll R
at infinity, with
R
satisfying (
1.7
), then the functional
J
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Proof.

Let us first prove that every Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{rad}(B)
for
J
is bounded. Since
J^{\prime}(u_{k})\to 0
and using (
3.1
)

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\langle J^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}\rangle|&=\left|\int_{B}g(|\nabla u_{k}|)\frac{\nabla u_{k}}{|\nabla u_{k}|}\cdot\nabla u_{k}\,dx-\int_{B}h((u_{k})_{+})|x|^{\alpha}u_{k}\,dx\right|\\

&\leq\|J^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{W^{1,G}_{0}(B)^{\prime}}\|u_{k}\|_{W^{1,G}_{0}(B)}\\
&\leq\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{G}(B)}\end{split}

for
k
large enough. In particular we get that, for

sufficiently large,

\displaystyle q^{-}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H((u_{k})_{+})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{G}(B)}+p^{+}\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{k}|)\,dx

Condition
|J(u_{k})|\leq C

(5.2)

\left|\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{k}|)\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H((u_{k})_{+})\,dx\right|\leq C.

Then, from (
5.1
) and (

) we get that

\displaystyle\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{k}|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C+\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H((u_{k})_{+}))\,dx\leq C+\frac{1}{q^{-}}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{G}(B)}+\frac{p^{+}}{q^{-}}\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{k}|)|\,dx,

from where, since

, we obtain, using (

\left(1-\frac{p^{+}}{q^{-}}\right)\xi_{G,-}(\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{G}(B)})\leq\left(1-\frac{p^{+}}{q^{-}}\right)\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{k}|)\,dx\leq C+\frac{1}{q^{-}}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{G}(B)}.

Hence,
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
X_{rad}(B)
. Up to a subsequence we have that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
X_{rad}(B)
.

Let us prove that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B}h((u_{k})_{+})|x|^{\alpha}(u_{k}-u)\,dx=0.

Since

is convex,

\int_{B}h(u_{k})|x|^{\alpha}(u_{k}-u)\,dx\leq\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u_{k})\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u)\,dx\to 0

as

since

strongly in

due to Proposition

. This implies that,

\displaystyle|\langle-\Delta_{g}(u_{k}),u_{k}-u\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle J^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}-u\rangle+\int_{B}h((u_{k})_{+})|x|^{\alpha}(u_{k}-u)\,dx|

\displaystyle\leq\|J^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{W^{1,G}_{0}(B)^{\prime}}\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,G}_{0}(B)}+\int_{B}h((u_{k})_{+})|x|^{\alpha}(u_{k}-u)\,dx\to 0

as

. Observe that, since

weakly in

we have that

|\langle-\Delta_{g}(u),u_{k}-u\rangle|\to 0\text{ as }k\to\infty

therefore,

(5.3)

\displaystyle|\langle-\Delta_{g}(u_{k})+\Delta_{g}u,u_{k}-u\rangle|\to 0\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Finally, let us see that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
X_{rad}(B)
. By (
5.3
) and Lemma 3.1 in
[
4
]
we obtain that

\displaystyle 0\leftarrow|\langle-\Delta_{g}(u_{k})+\Delta_{g}(u),u_{k}-u\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B}\left(g(|\nabla u_{k}|)\frac{\nabla u_{k}}{|\nabla u_{k}|}-g(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)\cdot(\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u|)\,dx

which concludes the proof.

5.2.
Mountain-pass geometry of

Let us see now that

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). If
H\ll R
at infinity, with
R
satisfying (

), then the functional
J
satisfies the conditions:

(i)

and

for some

in

,

(ii)

there exists
\alpha\in(0,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{G}(B)})
and
\sigma>0
such that

on

Proof.

Let us check item (i). By definition,

. Let

be such that
u_{0}>0
and
\|\nabla u_{0}\|=1
. Then
\int_{B}G(|\nabla u_{0}|)\,dx\leq 1
and then, since

\displaystyle=\int_{B}G(t|\nabla u_{0}|)\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(t(u_{0})_{+})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq t^{p^{+}}-t^{q^{-}}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H((u_{0})_{+})\,dx\leq 0

for

Let us prove item (ii). First,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u)\,dx&\leq C\xi_{H,+}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}))=C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}^{q^{-}}\end{split}

when

. Finally, since

we get that

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx-\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(u_{+})\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}^{p^{+}}-C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{G}(B)}^{q^{-}}=\alpha^{p^{+}}-C\alpha^{q^{-}}:=\sigma>0

if

with

small enough.

We are position to prove our first existence result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof is a consequence of the Mountain Pass Theorem stated in Proposition

by applying Lemmas

and
5.2
.

Remark 5.3
.

We point out that the critical function
H=G^{*}
is admissible in condition (
1.7
).

Let
\widetilde{G}
be the complementary function to
G
, and denote
\widetilde{G}^{\prime}=\widetilde{g}
. Since (
1.5
) implies that

(p^{+})^{\prime}\leq\frac{t\widetilde{g}(t)}{\widetilde{G}(t)}\leq(p^{-})^{\prime},

by Lemma 2.7 in
[
8
]
we have the following space inclusion

L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}((r,1),s^{n-1}\,ds)\subset L^{\widetilde{G}}((r,1),s^{n-1}\,ds).

Moreover, by Remark 3.2 in
[
10
]
the critical function
G^{*}
has the following growth behavior

G^{*}(t)\leq C_{1}+C_{2}t^{(p^{+})^{*}}

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants,

(p^{-})^{\prime}=\frac{p^{-}}{p^{-}-1}
and
(p^{+})^{*}=\frac{np^{+}}{n-p^{+}}
. Then, assuming

we get that

\displaystyle\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}(r,1),s^{n-1}ds}\leq\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{q}((r,1),s^{n-1}ds)}=\left(\int_{r}^{1}s^{\frac{p^{-}-n}{p^{-}-1}-1}\,ds\right)^{\frac{p^{-}-1}{p^{-}}}\leq Cr^{1-\frac{n}{p^{-}}}.

Hence, since the N-function

is increasing

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle r^{\alpha+n-1}G^{*}(\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}(r,1),s^{n-1}ds})\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}\left(C_{1}+C_{2}\|s^{1-n}\|_{L^{\widetilde{G}}(r,1),s^{n-1}ds}^{(p^{+})^{*}}\right)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1}\,dr+C_{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha+n-1+(1-\frac{n}{p^{-}})(p^{+})^{*}}\,dr

which is finite when

\alpha+n+(1-\frac{n}{p^{-}})(p^{+})^{*}>0\quad\text{ that is, when }\alpha>n\left(\frac{n-p^{-}}{n-p^{+}}\frac{p^{+}}{p^{-}}-1\right).

6.
Boundedness of solutions

In this section, we prove that any radial solution
u of problem (
1.4
) is bounded. Our argument begins with the following technical lemma concerning the composition of M-functions.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
R
and
H
be N-functions satisfying (
1.5
), where
r^{\pm}>1
are the corresponding coefficients for
R
. Assume moreover that
q^{+}<r^{-}
. Then, the composition
F:=R\circ H^{-1}
is an N-function.

Proof.

The proof follows exactly the lines of
[
9
, Lemma 8]
with
R
replacing
G^{*}
in that argument.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In what follows, we will apply a De Giorgi’s iteration scheme to control the level sets of a solution
u\in X_{rad}(B)
to problem (
1.4
).

For a positive integer
k
, define

w_{k}:=(u-(1-2^{-k}))_{+}.

Then, as in
[
11
]
, the following holds

(6.1)

w_{k+1}\leq w_{k}\text{ in }B,\quad u(x)<(2^{k+1}-1)w_{k}\text{ in }\left\{w_{k+1}>0\right\},\quad\text{and }\left\{w_{k+1}>0\right\}\subset\left\{w_{k}>2^{-(k+1)}\right\}.

Also,
0\leq w_{k}\leq|u|+1\in L^{1}(B)
and
w_{k}(x)\to(u(x)-1)_{+}
a.e. in
B
, so by dominated convergence theorem,

(6.2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k})\,dx=\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H((u-1)_{+})\,dx.

Now, since
u
is a solution of (
1.4
) and appealing to (
1.5
), we obtain

(6.3)

\begin{split}\int_{B}G(|\nabla w_{k+1}|)\,dx&\leq\dfrac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{B}\dfrac{g(|\nabla w_{k+1}|)}{|\nabla w_{k+1}|}\nabla w_{k+1}\cdot\nabla w_{k+1}\,dx\\
&=\dfrac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{B}\dfrac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{k+1}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}h(u)w_{k+1}\,dx,\end{split}

where we have tested with
w_{k+1}
. By (
6.1
),

\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}h(u)w_{k+1}\,dx\leq\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}h\left((2^{k+1}-1)w_{k}\right)w_{k}\,dx\leq q^{+}(2^{k+1}-1)^{q^{+}-1}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k})\,dx.

Thus, from (
6.3
), we obtain

(6.4)

\int_{B}G(|\nabla w_{k+1}|)\,dx\leq\dfrac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}(2^{k+1}-1)^{q^{+}-1}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k})\,dx=C^{(1)}_{k+1}\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k})\,dx.

On the other hand, by Lemma
6.1
and Hölder’s inequality, and letting
d\mu=|x|^{\alpha}\,dx
, we get

(6.5)

\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k+1})\,dx\leq 2\|H(w_{k+1})\|_{L_{\mu}^{F}(B)}\|\chi_{w_{k+1}>0}\|_{L_{\mu}^{\tilde{F}}(B)},

where
F
is the N-function
F=R\circ H^{-1}
. By
[
24
, Corollary 7]
, we obtain

(6.6)

\begin{split}\|\chi_{w_{k+1}>0}\|_{L_{\mu}^{\tilde{F}}(B)}&=\mu(\{w_{k+1}>0\})(H\circ R^{-1})\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(\{w_{k+1}>0\})}\right)\\
&\leq\mu(\{w_{k+1}>0\})\psi_{1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(\{w_{k+1}>0\})}\right),\end{split}

where for
t\geq 0
the function
\psi_{1}(t)
is defined as
\psi_{1}(t):=\max\{t^{\frac{q^{+}}{r^{+}}},t^{\frac{q^{+}}{r^{-}}},t^{\frac{q^{-}}{r^{-}}},t^{\frac{q^{-}}{r^{-}}}\}
.

By the assumption
q^{+}<r^{-}
, all the exponents satisfy

\frac{q^{+}}{r^{+}},\quad\frac{q^{-}}{r^{+}},\quad\frac{q^{-}}{r^{-}},\quad\frac{q^{+}}{r^{-}}<1.

Hence, there exists
\delta>0
, which could be equal to
1-q^{\pm}/r^{\pm}
according to the size of the level set
\{w_{k+1}>0\}
such that

\|\chi_{w_{k+1}>0}\|_{L_{\mu}^{\tilde{F}}(B)}\leq\mu(\{w_{k+1}>0\})^{\delta}.

As a result, appealing to (
6.1
), if we denote
C_{k+1}^{(2)}:=2^{\delta q^{+}(k+1)}
, we get that

(6.7)

\begin{split}\|\chi_{w_{k+1}>0}\|_{L_{\mu}^{\tilde{F}}(B)}&\leq\left(\int_{\{w_{k+1}>0\}}|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\delta}=\left(\int_{\{w_{k+1}>0\}}\dfrac{H(2^{-(k+1)})}{H(2^{-(k+1)})}|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\delta}\\
&\leq C_{k+1}^{(2)}\left(\int_{\{w_{k+1}>0\}}H(w_{k})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\delta}.\end{split}

By
[
9
, Lemma 10]
with
G^{*}
replaced by
R
, the embedding in Proposition
4.2
and (
6.4
),

(6.8)

\begin{split}\|H(w_{k+1})\|_{L_{\mu}^{F}(B)}&\leq\max\left\{\|w_{k+1}\|_{L^{R}_{\mu}(B)}^{q^{+}},\|w_{k+1}\|_{L^{R}_{\mu}(B)}^{q^{-}}\right\}\\
&\leq C\max\left\{\|\nabla w_{k+1}\|_{L^{G}(B)}^{q^{+}},\|\nabla w_{k+1}\|_{L^{G}(B)}^{q^{-}}\right\}\\
&\leq C\psi_{2}\left(\int_{B}G(|\nabla w_{k+1}|)\,dx\right)\\
&\leq C^{(3)}_{k+1}\psi_{2}\left(\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k})\,dx\right),\end{split}

where
C^{(3)}_{k+1}\geq C^{i}_{k+1}
for
i=1,2
, and
\psi_{2}(t):=\max\{t^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{+}}},t^{\frac{q^{+}}{p^{-}}},t^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{-}}},t^{\frac{q^{-}}{p^{-}}}\}
. Next, suppose that

(6.9)

\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{0})\,dx<1.

Then, since
\left\{w_{k}\right\}_{k}
is decreasing, it follows that

(6.10)

\int_{B}|x|^{\alpha}H(w_{k})\,dx<1,

for all
k
. Hence, combining (
6.5
) with the bounds (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), using (
6.10
) with the assumption
p^{+}<q^{-}
, we derive

(6.11)

\int_{B}H(w_{k+1})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\leq C_{k+1}\left(\int_{B}H(w_{k+1})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{B}H(w_{k+1})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\right)^{\delta+\gamma},

with

\gamma=\min\left\{\dfrac{q^{+}}{p^{+}},\dfrac{q^{+}}{p^{.}},\dfrac{q^{-}}{p^{+}},\dfrac{q^{-}}{p^{-}}\right\}-1>0,

and where
C_{k+1}\geq C^{(3)}_{k+1}
. Therefore, by the numerical lemma
[
9
, Lemma 13]
, we obtain that there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that if

(6.12)

\int_{B}H(w_{0})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx<\varepsilon

then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B}H(w_{k})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx=0.

Observe that

\int_{B}H(w_{0})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx=\int_{B}H(u_{+})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx

and moreover (
6.2
) implies
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq 1
. Hence, the result is valid under the assumption

\int_{B}H(u_{+})|x|^{\alpha}\,dx<\varepsilon,

which in particular implies (
6.10
). If now

\xi=\int_{B}H(u)|x|^{\alpha}\,dx>0
is arbitrary, then we choose

large enough so that

(6.13)

\int_{B}H\left(\dfrac{u}{C}\right)|x|^{\alpha}\,dx\leq C^{-q^{-}}\int_{B}H(u)|x|^{\alpha}\,dx<\varepsilon.

Moreover,

also satisfies (
6.3
) with an extra constant at the end. Hence, from the above argument

\|u/C\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq 1\text{ or }\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C.

This ends the proof of the theorem.

7.
A non-existence result for the Hénon problem

We start this section by proving the Pohozaev’s type identity given in Proposition
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First, by
[
18
, Theorem 1.7]
and subsequent remarks, we conclude that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Hence,
f(x,u(x))
is continuous and vanishes on
\partial B
. We now regularized the problem.

For simplicity in the notation, we will denote
G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}a(s)s\,ds
. Moreover, from the relation

\dfrac{a^{\prime}(t)t}{a(t)}=\dfrac{tg(t)+t^{2}g^{\prime}(t)}{tg(t)}=1+\dfrac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}

we have that

-1<p^{-}-2\leq\dfrac{a^{\prime}(t)t}{a(t)}\leq p^{+}-2<\infty.

Let
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset C_{0}^{2}(B)
be a sequence converging uniformly to
f(x,u(x))
in
\overline{B}
. Consider the problem

(7.1)

\begin{cases}-\mathop{\text{div}}(a(\sqrt{\varepsilon+|\nabla u|^{2}})\nabla u)=f_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }B\\
u=0\quad\text{on }\partial B.\end{cases}

As in the beginning of the proof of Theorem 1.1 in

We state the

Rates of convergence in long time asymptotics of an alignment model with symmetry breaking
Alexandre Surin Ceremade (CNRS UMR n o 7534), PSL University, Université Paris-Dauphine, Place de Lattre de Tassigny, 75775 Paris 16, France Email: surin@ceremade.dauphine.fr

Abstract
. We consider a nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation derived from a Cucker-Smale model for flocking with noise. There is a known phase transition depending on the noise between a regime with a unique stationary solution which is isotropic (symmetry) and a regime with a continuum of polarized stationary solutions (symmetry breaking). If the value of the noise is larger than the threshold value, the solution of the evolution equation converges to the unique radial stationary solution. This solution is linearly unstable in the symmetry-breaking range, while polarized stationary solutions attract all solutions with sufficiently low entropy. We prove that the convergence measured in a weighted L 2 L^{2} norm occurs with an exponential rate and that the average speed also converges with exponential rate to a unique limit which determines a single polarized stationary solution.



Keywords:
Collective behavior, flocking, non-linear Fokker–Planck equation, phase transition.

AMS Subject Classification:
35B40; 35P15; 35Q92.

1.
Introduction.

In various models of mathematical biology and social sciences, the dynamics of a population of individuals without leader is often described by mean field interaction forces that depend on the parameters of the agents, for instance, their relative positions and velocities. These interaction rules are the basis for models of collective behavior and the description of emergent structures. There are two well-known models. The Cucker–Smale model
[
6
]
describes agents whose velocities tend to align with the average velocity of their neighbors, with an interaction strength that decreases with distance. The Vicsek model
[
14
]
, describes particles moving at a constant speed but the direction of the velocity is determined by the average orientation of the neighbors and by a stochastic component, typically a Brownian noise. This noise takes into account individual randomness and estimation errors. The model exhibits a noise-dependent phase transition, marking the emergence of global alignment as the noise intensity decreases. We study a variant of the Cucker–Smale model with Brownian noise and a self-propulsion force. This framework has been investigated in
[
2
,
11
,
12
]
and
[
13
]
. We consider the spatially homogeneous case, in which the evolution of the density
f(t,v)
of individuals is described by

(1)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=D\,\Delta f+\nabla\cdot\big[\left(\nabla\psi_{\alpha}(v)+v-u_{f}\right)f\big]\quad\text{with}\quad\psi_{\alpha}(v)=\frac{\alpha}{4}\,|v|^{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\,|v|^{2}.

Here
t\geq 0
denotes the time variable,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the velocity variable,
D>0
is the intensity of the noise,
\nabla\cdot
and
\Delta
respectively denote the divergence and the Laplacian with respect to
v
, while
\alpha>0
is a parameter of the external potential
and
u_{f}
denotes the average velocity

\displaystyle u_{f}:=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\,f(t,v)\,dv}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,v)\,dv}.

In contrast with the classical Vicsek model, we do not require that
|v|
is constant.
For any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define a Gibbs state as

(2)

\displaystyle G_{u}(v):=\mathcal{Z}(u)^{-1}{e^{-\frac{1}{D}\left(\frac{1}{2}|v-u|^{2}+\psi_{\alpha}(v)\right)}}

where
\mathcal{Z}(u)
is a renormalization constant. A stationary state is a Gibbs state with a specific value of the speed
|u|
. In
[
2
]
it was proved that a phase transition occurs and the following refined result is taken from
[
11
]
.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

There exists

such that:

•

If

, then

is the unique stationary state. It is isotropic and stable.

•

If

, then there exists

such that

is unstable and the set of polarized states

For two nonnegative functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[f|g]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\log\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)dv

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f]=D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\log f\,dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,\psi_{\alpha}\,dv+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v-u_{f}|^{2}\,f\,dv.

.

Let

It was known from

Preliminary results.

2.1.
Existence and uniqueness

We provide here some already known results and references. Since
Equation

1
has a gradient flow structure, a natural approach is to consider a discrete gradient descent scheme in the Wasserstein space, as introduced by Jordan, Kinderlehrer, and Otto in
[
10
]
. The drawback of this method is that it does not give uniqueness since the free energy is not convex. The existence is detailed in Chapter 8 of
[
15
]
. The proof only requires an initial datum in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,dx)
with finite entropy and second moment. In
[
3
]
, Bolley, Cañizo and Carrillo proved the existence and uniqueness for a large class of kinetic PDE’s which covers (
1
). However, their method requires some additional integrability conditions on the initial datum, see
[
3
]
Theorem 1.2. In
[
3
,
15
]
, the solutions are measure-valued in the sense of distributions at
t=0
, and admit a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure for any
t>0
. Here, we assume that
f_{\mathrm{ini}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is nonnegative with unit mass and has finite free energy and second moment so that a solution exists. We use the same notation as in
[
11
]
.

2.2.
Fisher information.

The free energy defined by (
4
) is a Lyapunov function in the sense that for a regular solution
f
to
Equation

1
, we have:

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}[f]=-\,\mathcal{I}[f(t,.)]

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}[f(t,.)]=D^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\log\left(\frac{f}{G_{u_{f}}}\right)\right|^{2}f\,dv

is the Fisher information. As a consequence,
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}[f(t,.)]
is nonincreasing for a solution
f
of (
1
).

2.3.
The compatibility equation.

A stationary solution
f
of (
1
) satisfies
\mathcal{I}[f]=0
. This

implies
f=G_{u_{f}}
, that is,
f=G_{u}
with the condition of compatibility

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(u):=u-u_{G_{u}}=0.

Conversely, by substituting
G_{u}
into
Equation

1
, one can check that
G_{u}
is a stationary state

if
\mathbf{H}(u)=0
.

We can write for
u\neq 0

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(u)=-\frac{H(|u|)}{K(|u|)}\frac{u}{|u|}

where
H
and
K
are two real functions defined by

(8)

\displaystyle{H}(R)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(v_{1}-R)\,e^{-\frac{1}{D}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}|v|^{4}-v_{1}R\right)}}\,dv

and

(9)

\displaystyle{K}(R)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{e^{-\frac{1}{D}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}|v|^{4}-v_{1}R\right)}}\ dv.

where
v_{1}
denotes the first coordinate of
v\in(v_{1},v_{2},...v_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence, the stationary states of
Equation

1
are characterized by the zeros of
H
. The proof of
Proposition

1.1
amounts to finding the zeros of

and we refer to
[
11
]
for more details.

2.4.
A moment lemma.

For initial conditions with finite free energy, moments of all orders are finite and uniformly bounded for any
t>0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be the solution to
Equation

1
with initial datum
f_{\mathrm{ini}}\in L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such
that
\mathcal{F}[f_{\mathrm{ini}}]
is finite and
\|f_{\mathrm{ini}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}M_{k}(t)<+\infty\,\,\,\text{where}\,M_{k}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{k}f(t,v)\,dv.

Proof.

We learn from Proposition 4.2 in
[
11
]
that:

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f]\geq-\frac{d}{2}\log(2\pi)\,D+\frac{\alpha}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,|v|^{4}\,dv-\frac{D+\alpha}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,|v|^{4}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, if
f
solves (
1
), then
\mathcal{F}[f(t,.)]\leq\mathcal{F}[f_{\mathrm{ini}}]
for all
t\geq 0
and, as a consequence,

\sup_{t>0}M_{4}(t)<+\infty.

Since
\lVert f(t,.)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
for all
t\geq 0
, we also have for
k\in[0,4]
that
\sup_{t>0}M_{k}(t)<+\infty
by Hölder’s inequality. Let
m\geq 5
. Suppose that for
k\leq m-1
, we have
\sup_{t>0}M_{k}(t)<+\infty
. Differentiating
M_{k}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}M_{k}

\displaystyle=Dk\,(d+k-2)\,M_{k-2}-k\,M_{k}+k\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{k-2}vf\,dv\right)\cdot u_{f}+\alpha\,k\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|v|^{k}-|v|^{k+2})f\,dv.

Using this relation with
k=m-2
, we obtain that the moment of order
m
is finite for a.e.
t>0
(else the derivative of the moment of order
m-2
would be
-\infty
). Now using
|v|^{k}-|v|^{k+2}\leq 0
for
|v|\geq 1
and
|v|^{k}-|v|^{k+2}\leq 1
for
|v|\leq 1
, we obtain:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}M_{k}\leq D\,(d+k-2)M_{k-2}-k\,M_{k}+k\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{k-2}vf\,dv\right)\cdot u_{f}+\alpha\,k.

Using this inequality with
k=m
, we obtain that
\sup_{t>0}M_{m}(t)<+\infty
.
∎

2.5.
Properties of the free energy

The free energy is bounded from below in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},(1+|v|^{4})\,\mathrm{d}v)
, thanks to (
10
). We know from Corollary 4.1 of
[
11

that:

\mathcal{F}_{*}:=\inf_{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},(1+|v|^{4})\,\mathrm{d}v)}\mathcal{F}[f]=\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}[G_{0}]&\text{if }D\geq D_{*},\\
\mathcal{F}[G_{u}]\text{ with }|u|=r(D)&\text{if }D<D_{*}.\end{cases}

The value of

does not depend on

defined by (

Let us state here some useful identities. By definition, the normalized constant of a Gibbs state is

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{D}\left(\frac{1}{2}|v-u|^{2}+\psi_{\alpha}(v)\right)}\,dv.

With a direct computation, we obtain for all

,

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f]=\,D\,\mathcal{H}[f|G_{u}]-\frac{1}{2}\,|u-u_{f}|^{2}-D\log(\mathcal{Z}(u))\,.

First using (
12
) with

and then with

, we obtain thanks to

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{*}=D\,\mathcal{H}[f|G_{u_{f}}]+\mathcal{V}(u_{f})-\mathcal{V}_{*}

where

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(u):=-\,D\log(\mathcal{Z}(u))

is a radial function and
\mathcal{V}_{*}
denotes the value of
\mathcal{V}
on
\mathcal{S}
. This expression of the free energy is crucial for proving

3.1
. Taking

in
Equation

12

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f]-\mathcal{F}_{*}=D\,\mathcal{H}[f|G_{u}]-\frac{1}{2}|u_{f}-u|^{2}.

2.6.
Log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequality

Let us state two inequalities which generalize the classical Gaussian Poincaré and Log-Sobolev inequalities.
Since
\phi_{u}(v):=\frac{1}{2}|v-u|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}|v|^{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}|v|^{2}
is a bounded perturbation of a uniformly convex potential, it follows from the

and the Holley-Strook perturbation lemma

that the Log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities apply with the measure

Poincaré inequality
: For all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists
\Lambda_{u}>0
such that for all

satisfying

, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla g|^{2}G_{u}\,dv\geq\Lambda_{u}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}G_{u}\,dv.

Since we will only use the Poincaré inequality with

we will denote by

for all

.

Log-Sobolev inequality
: For all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
\mathcal{K}_{u}>0
such that for all nonnegative function of unit mass

there holds

(LSI)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\log\left(\frac{f}{G_{u}}\right)\right|^{2}f\,dv\geq\mathcal{K}_{u}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log\left(\frac{f}{G_{u}}\right)f\,dv.

In the following, we will use the Log-Sobolev inequality with

in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{S}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{S})\leq\epsilon}\mathcal{K}_{u}>0
where
\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{S})=\min_{v\in\mathcal{S}}|u-v|
.

3.
A rate of decay for the free energy.

The aim of this section is to prove the convergence of the free energy to its minimum. The proof relies on a differential inequality on
\mathcal{F}[f]

3.1.
A detailed analysis of

In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}\mathrm{dist}(u,\mathcal{S})^{2}\leq\mathcal{V}(u)-\mathcal{V}_{*}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu_{2}}|\nabla\mathcal{V}(u)|^{2}.

\displaystyle R[g(t,.)]\leq B_{D}\max(|v_{g}(t)|,|u_{*}^{\prime}(t)|)Q_{2,u_{*}}[g(t,.)]\,\,\,\forall t\geq 0\text{ where }R[g(t,.)]\text{ is defined by }\eqref{expressiondeR}

Proof.

We will control both of the two terms of

\displaystyle R[g]

\displaystyle=D^{2}v_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\nabla g-v_{g})G_{*}-\frac{u_{*}^{\prime}}{2}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}\,v\,G_{*}\,dv.

Let us compute

.
Using (

\displaystyle\left|D^{2}v_{g}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\nabla g-v_{g})G_{{*}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq D^{2}|v_{g}|\lVert g\rVert_{2,u_{*}}\lVert\nabla g-v_{g}\rVert_{2,u_{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D^{2}}{\eta(D)\beta(D)}|v_{g}|Q_{2,u_{*}}[g].

Let us compute
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}vG_{u_{*}}dv.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}vG_{{*}}dv\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}|v|^{6}G_{{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}G_{{*}}\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{8D^{2}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(C_{D}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}G_{{*}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla g|^{2}G_{{*}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}G_{{*}}dv\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}

(55)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(D)Q_{2,u_{*}}[g]

where

\displaystyle\gamma(D)=\left(\frac{8D^{2}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left[\left(\frac{C_{D}}{\eta(D)^{2}\beta(D)^{2}}+b(D)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta(D)^{2}\beta(D)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}\right].

Here, we used (
45
), (
46
), (

) and
Lemma

5.8
.
Hence, for the second term of
R[g]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{u_{*}^{\prime}}{2}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{2}vG_{*}dv\right|\leq\frac{\gamma(D)}{2}|u_{*}^{\prime}|Q_{2,u_{*}}[g].

This completes the proof of (

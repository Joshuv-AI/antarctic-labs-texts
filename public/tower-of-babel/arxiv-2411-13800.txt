Explaining GPT-4’s Schema of Depression Using Machine Behavior Analysis
Adithya V Ganesan, Vasudha Varadarajan, Yash Kumar Lal, Veerle C. Eijsbroek, Katarina Kjell, Oscar N. E. Kjell, Tanuja Dhanasekaran, Elizabeth C. Stade, Johannes C. Eichstaedt, Ryan L.Boyd, H. Andrew Schwartz, Lucie Flek

Abstract
Use of large language models such as ChatGPT ( GPT-4 ) for mental health support has grown rapidly, emerging as a promising route to assess and help people with mood disorders, like depression. However, we have a limited understanding of GPT-4 ’s schema of mental disorders, that is, how it internally associates and interprets symptoms. In this work, we leveraged contemporary measurement theory to decode how GPT-4 interrelates depressive symptoms to inform both clinical utility and theoretical understanding. We found GPT-4 ’s assessment of depression: (a) had high overall convergent validity ( r = 0.71 r=0.71 with self-report on 955 samples, and r = 0.81 r=0.81 with experts judgments on 209 samples); (b) had moderately high internal consistency (symptom inter-correlates r = .23 r=.23 to .78 .78 ) that largely aligned with literature and self-report except that GPT-4 ; (c) underemphasized suicidality ’s – and overemphasized psychomotor ’s – relationship with other symptoms, and (d) had symptom inference patterns that suggest nuanced hypotheses (e.g. sleep and fatigue are influenced by most other symptoms while feelings of worthlessness/guilt is mostly influenced by depressed mood ).

Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs), such as OpenAI’s
GPT-4
or Google’s Gemini are starting to be actively used by thousands of individuals for mental health support, although we have a limited understanding of their outcomes and limitations.
As of January 2024, the ChatGPT store
OpenAI 2024
reported tens of thousands of conversations involving GPT therapy bots
Eliot 2024
, and
approximately 1 in 25 chatbot users seek mental health support as one of their primary uses of LLMs
Motyl et al. 2024
.
A recent survey in the
Journal of Medical Economics
revealed that over 1 in 10 clinicians in the USA use ChatGPT, and roughly 50% are interested in continuing its use for data entry, medical scheduling, or research
Shryock 2023
.

The potential benefits of LLMs in the mental health domain are numerous.
Mental health care often begins with psychodiagnostic assessment, where employing LLMs could play a crucial role for more timely and effective screening and assessment
Schueller et al. 2019
.
Present-day screening tools have limited sensitivity, standardization
Bradford et al. 2009
, and cultural adaptability
Nelson 2002
;
Snowden and Yamada 2005
, plus people report increased willingness to use online mental health technologies available to them
Schueller et al. 2019
.
Further, the ability to administer assessments earlier in the care cycle can address the fact that diagnosis often lags behind the onset of symptoms by over a decade
Wang et al. 2004
;
Wang et al. 2005
;
ten Have et al. 2013
.
Earlier detection and treatment could mitigate financial barriers that arise from treating more severe conditions
Rowan et al. 2013
;
Cook et al. 2017
;
Dahlhamer et al. 2016
.
However, similar to treating mental disorders with unevaluated drugs, unevaluated use of LLMs for mental health support comes with many risks
Stade et al. 2024
;
Dergaa et al. 2024
.

This paper presents a framework for evaluating LLMs’ “representation” of mental health.
To do this, we apply principles from measurement theory
Rust and Golombok 2021
to infer
GPT-4
’s
schema
of depression (how it appears to organize, interpret, and relate depressive symptoms).
In the process, we quantitatively evaluate two fundamental questions: (a) how
GPT-4
identifies and estimates depression symptoms from language, and (b) its latent structure between the symptoms used to form an overall assessment.
We utilize this framework to study
GPT-4
’s structural representation of depression for several reasons.
First, depression is one of the most prevalent mental health conditions, affecting an estimated 10–15% of the population throughout their lifetime
Lépine and Briley 2011
;
Lim et al. 2018
, and is the leading cause of disability worldwide
Friedrich 2017
.
Second, there exists a vast body of research on depression’s symptoms, structure, and their inter-relationships, providing a strong theoretical reference against which to compare
Watson 2009
;
Waszczuk et al. 2017
;
Fried et al. 2016
. Lastly, depression is a highly heterogeneous disorder comprised of cognitive, affective, behavioral, and somatic symptoms
Fried and Nesse 2015
; depression is also highly co-morbid with other conditions, including anxiety
Leckman et al. 1983
, substance use
Swendsen and Merikangas 2000
, somatic disorders
Katon et al. 1982
and other internalizing conditions
Kessler et al. 2005
.
Thus, understanding
GPT-4
’s schema of depression suggests generalizations to many aspects and forms of psychopathology.

While previous work on
how

GPT-4
’s assessments are formed is limited, recent studies have evaluated
GPT-4
’s ability to predict mental disorder
Shin et al. 2024
.
Empirical approaches that seek to explain
GPT-4
’s behavior are often limited to performance benchmarks and comparative studies
Markowitz et al. 2024
, which measure the agreement between model’s responses and those of humans
Hagendorff et al. 2023
;
Galatzer-Levy et al. 2023
or experts
Matero et al. 2019
.
Such methods can reveal
what
the model can do but do not explain
how
it performs such tasks.
The responsible use of LLMs, particularly in critical applications that involve human health and well-being, requires understanding how they perform tasks —– questions that have traditionally fallen within the purview of fields like psychology and cognitive science.

Over the years, psychology has developed and continually refined tools and methods to measure, interpret, and understand the human mind using mathematical and empirical methods designed with the intent of explaining human mental functioning through behavioral observation
Galatzer-Levy and Onnela 2023
.
Such methods have uncovered
schemas
— the internal concepts underlying a process — embedded within the neural system from observations of human behaviors
Stein 1992
;
Schmidt et al. 1995
;
Waters and Waters 2006
.
Drawing parallels from cognitive and behavioral psychology, our experiments are designed to collect extensive observations of
GPT-4
’s ability to assess depression.
We analyze its generated text (responses) and the input prompts (stimuli) to infer
GPT-4
’s schema of depression.
This inferred schema is used to explain
GPT-4
’s behavior in assessing depression
Rahwan et al. 2019
.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to perform machine psychology
Hagendorff et al. 2024
to explain
GPT-4
’s functioning.
We employ a robust methodological framework that can be extended to other LLMs to understand how psychopathology is represented within them.
This is depicted in
Figure 1
and described extensively under Methods (§
4
).

The unique contributions of this study center on explaining
GPT-4
’s behavior when identifying depression risk within open-ended essays.
In a sample of 955 individuals, we employ measurement theory to infer
GPT-4
’s schema of depression by analyzing (a) the relationships among key depression symptoms and (b) the role of each symptom in determining the degree of depression within the language model.
This inferred schema is compared with one derived from self-reported scores to quantitatively identify similarities and differences in the conceptualization of depression as a network of interconnected symptoms.
We used multivariate linear regression to assess how
GPT-4
integrates explicitly mentioned factors in essays to estimate risks for unmentioned factors.
We then applied differential language analysis
(
Schwartz et al. 2013
, DLA)
to identify the linguistic cues associated with
GPT-4
’s identification of depressive risk factors.
Lastly, we evaluated whether
GPT-4
shows evidence that it uses the phrases that it cited as explanations for producing its depression scores.

Results

was used to analyze language samples from 955 participants, who described their feelings of depression, with the goal of estimating symptom-level scores based on the
PHQ-9

To validate these estimates, participants also completed the

Additionally, a random subsample of 209 participant responses was manually annotated by two psychology experts using a similar annotation process to that given to
GPT-4

PHQ-9
scores was conducted on the full dataset, while the comparison between

Differential Item Functioning Analysis:
GPT-4
and self-report have similar discriminant characteristics over latent depression scores.

We utilize Item Response Theory (IRT) to conduct a Differential Item Functioning (DIF) analysis on two sets of scores — self-report and
GPT-4
estimates.
The DIF ranks highlight the depression severity ranges where each item most reliably identifies the presence of its symptom.

Stark et al. 2006
;
Santelices and Wilson 2012
;
Belzak 2020
.
A lower-ranked items are best at distinguishing lower latent scores, and higher-ranked items for higher latent scores.
A positive difference in an item’s rank between
GPT-4
and self-report means that language-based assessment of this item by
GPT-4
is more informative than self-report scores to capture higher depression scores.
On the other hand, a negative difference implies that the language-based score of this item has lesser discriminant power than the self-report over higher depression scores.

Table 2
shows the DIF ranks for
GPT-4
and self-report along with its 95% confidence interval estimated through bootstrapped resampling across individuals over 500 trials.

Overall, the distinguishing characteristics of items were in agreement between the two, with suicidal ideation, appetite, and psychomotor agitation/retardation having higher ranks (7-9), and anhedonia, depressed mood, and fatigue having lower ranks (1-3).
All items had a very small 95% confidence interval, showing that these rank estimates have high reliability.
Suicidal ideation (rank=9), sleep (rank=4), and anhedonia (rank=3) were the three items with no difference in ranks between
GPT-4
and self-report.
The difference in ranks of other items doesn’t indicate that language-based severity scores of

possessed a significantly different characteristic compared to self-report, i.e. there is a high similarity in the item functioning of self-report and
GPT-4
for

Interplay between explicitly mentioned symptoms and inferred symptoms

We examined how explicit symptoms influence implicit symptoms in
GPT-4
’s assessments by calculating the regression coefficients (
\beta
) from a multivariate linear regression model.
This linear model predicted implicit symptom scores based on explicit symptom scores while controlling for all other implicit symptoms.

Figure 3
depicts the effect sizes of explicit symptoms in influencing
GPT-4
’s estimate of implicit symptoms.

When depressed mood was not explicitly mentioned, it was largely inferred from anhedonia (
\beta=.60
).
Conversely, anhedonia was influenced by depressed mood (
\beta=.49
) and also by concentration difficulties (
\beta=.24
).
Additionally, the cardinal symptoms were common explicit drivers of nearly all other symptoms with large effect sizes.
This suggests that depressed mood and anhedonia are pivotal for accurately estimating the severity of other less specific symptoms of depression
Watson 2009
, particularly when they are not directly mentioned in the text.
Furthermore, when sleep disturbances were explicitly mentioned, they had a moderate effect on estimating other somatic symptoms such as fatigue (
\beta=.18
) and abnormal appetite (
\beta=.24
).
However, the reverse relationship wasn’t significant: sleep disturbances were not inferred from the other somatic markers.
Rather, depressed mood emerged as one of its stronger predictors (
\beta=0.26
).

Except for fatigue, all other symptoms that were explicitly mentioned more than 10% of the time —– including the cardinal symptoms and feelings of worthlessness/guilt —– had a widespread influence on estimating the severity of other unmentioned symptoms.
The explicit mention of nearly all the items predicted fatigue.
However, fatigue was a very weak indicator of other unmentioned symptoms, despite being explicitly mentioned in 25% of the cases.
We identified three pairs of symptoms with strong mutual relationships, where the explicit presence of one symptom influenced the implicit estimation of the other, and vice versa.
These pairs include anhedonia and depressed mood, fatigue and psychomotor agitation/retardation, and anhedonia and concentration difficulties.

3
Discussion

Receiving professional mental health support is a multi-step, non-linear process that begins with screening and initial assessment.
A significant percentage of individuals do not receive timely support due to social barriers and infrastructural limitations within the traditional mental healthcare system
Coombs et al. 2021
;
Aguirre Velasco et al. 2020
.
Though effective mental health treatments exist, most individuals with mental disorders do not receive treatment, in part due to shortages of mental healthcare providers
Kazdin 2017
;
Konrad et al. 2009
.
Digital technologies like
GPT-4
which possess unprecedented human-comparable skills and may soon be able to practically address many such challenges.
With current consumer costs being around $30/month
Kell 2024
, models such as
GPT-4
may be well-positioned to mitigate well-established inhibitors such as high costs, limited mobility, inadequate insurance coverage, and lack of support for parents
Stade et al. 2024
.
Additionally, it can overcome infrastructural limitations, including the need for cultural adaptation and the dearth of mental health clinicians.
However, overcoming these challenges does not guarantee precise assessments due to limitations in sensitivity with current measurement tools.
To enhance accuracy, these technologies can leverage passive and quasi-passive indicators from daily life
Schwartz et al. 2014
;
Coppersmith 2022
to form more precise assessments and aid the delivery of timely care to those in need.

While studies have focused on LLMs’ generations to identify and measure desirable behaviors for mental health support
Chiu et al. 2024
;
Kimmel 2023
, the current study instead sought to inform how the internal structure of depression is represented within an LLM by analyzing its behavior with respect to symptoms.
Hence, we tasked
GPT-4
with identifying the key symptomatic markers of depression expressed within human essays.
These essays contained exhibits of causes (e.g. substance use, sedentary lifestyle) and effects (e.g. tired, exhausted) of depression through direct or synonymous mentions (e.g. “I don’t like doing things anymore” vs “I don’t see the point of getting out of the bed anymore” both suggest lack of interest).
From GPT-4’s assessments on language describing depression, we learn how it views the condition to manifest in people, akin to how practicing therapists understand conditions based on the way patients describe it
Frances et al. 1995
;
Sommers-Flanagan and Sommers-Flanagan 2012
.

We evaluated the viability of
GPT-4
for assessing depression using two key principles from measurement theory: reliability and validity.
We found that
GPT-4
displays moderate-high levels of concurrent validity and internal consistency (reliability) with depressive severity from a well-established rating scale measure,
PHQ-9
.
The overall result of
GPT-4
’s convergence with self-report was consistent with overall associations of
GPT-4

Xu et al. 2024
and other
LLM
s
Galatzer-Levy et al. 2023
with standard depression measures, while the item-level results extend convergence to detection of depression symptoms.
Further, we found that
GPT-4
’s generated explanations were generally faithful
Lyu et al. 2024
, with little evidence that it relied on linguistic cues outside its explanations of explicit phrases used to estimate
PHQ-9
scores.
These findings indicate that
GPT-4
can be useful at screening patients and identifying symptoms from their language.
Language is an information-rich medium compared to rating scales
Kjell et al. 2022
which can provide avenues for making hypotheses about predisposing, precipitating, and perpetuating factors of psychopathology for a given patient while estimating the symptom severity.
Thus, the explanatory output of LLM assessment may free up clinicians to prioritize complex case conceptualization and the creation of carefully tailored treatment plans.

Going beyond evaluating
GPT-4
’s convergence with self-reports and experts, we inferred its schema of depressive symptoms from its behavior in assessing depression.
This schema, a representation of the psychological constructs, that was derived from
GPT-4
’s responses was depicted as a web of associations between
PHQ-9
-based depressive symptoms.
We found
GPT-4
and self-report shared commonalities in their schema across the first seven
PHQ-9
items in relation to other
PHQ-9
symptoms, while they differed for
psychomotor agitation/ retardation
and
suicidal thoughts
.

GPT-4
’s estimate of suicidal ideation had a higher agreement with experts (
Table 1
), and with self-report under explicit mention (
Table 3
), which explained the difficulty in estimating this item when it was not mentioned in language.
With its effect size being inferred from depressed mood and anhedonia being small (
\beta=.10-.17
;
Figure 3
), it was not easily inferrable when not mentioned by these prominent explicit items.
However, psychomotor agitation/ retardation had lower convergence with self-report (under both explicit and implicit conditions) and the experts, which showed that
GPT-4
fundamentally differed in how it inferred this symptom from language.
It is also possible that this is a difficult symptom to infer given its weaker relationship to the other symptoms indexed by the
PHQ-9
(
2(b)
,
3
).

Highly depression-specific symptoms such as anhedonia, depressed mood, and feelings of worthlessness/guilt
Watson 2009
;
Harrison et al. 2022
had both higher base rates of explicit mentions (
Table 3
) and higher effects on implicit symptoms (
Figure 3
), explaining the severity estimations when symptoms were unmentioned in the essays.
Symptoms with lower levels of specificity to depression
Watson 2009
, like sleep and appetite, were inferrable in the presence of almost any other symptom.
These nonspecific symptoms are shared by depression and anxiety-related disorders
Watson 2009
.

Another important consideration in explaining LLMs is the instructions it is given.
Given that the information present in the instructions can affect the performance of these models
V Ganesan et al. 2023
;
Ceballos-Arroyo et al. 2024
;
Mizrahi et al. 2024
;
Sclar et al. 2024
, we instructed
GPT-4
to estimate symptoms of depression before estimating the severity of overall depression.
This provided a means to understand the cohesiveness of the structure of depression, its faithfulness to what it identifies as explicit markers, and varying difficulty levels in estimating the symptoms.
This mirrors studies of LLMs in other domains that have demonstrated improved effectiveness when allowed to break a task into compositional steps
Nye et al. 2022
;
Wei et al. 2022
or when instructed to carry out specific modules that culminate into a task
Khot et al. 2023
;
Weston and Sukhbaatar 2023
.

Using measurement theory to arrive at explanatory schemas can inform how LLMs function, where they are bound to fail, and how to effectively situate them in the mental health support system to maximize overall benefits.
There has been increased advocacy for transparency and explainability
WHO Health Ethics & Governance (HEG) 2021
of LLMs to improve public faith and understand the risks associated with its usage, especially in areas such as mental health support
Lee et al. 2021
.
Our evaluation framework is intended to help clinicians and decision-makers gain insights about model capabilities and pitfalls so that they can determine the capacity at which contemporary models can be used in the current care pipeline and further improve future systems to yield stronger outcomes.

Our work had several limitations.
Foremost, the scope of our results is limited to explaining
GPT-4
’s behaviors when used as a depression assessment tool.
Although other modalities of behavior beyond the patient’s verbal behavior can be used for the assessment, we employ language because it intrinsically contains more information, and the advent of LLMs enables us to leverage the power of language to deliver accurate assessments
Kjell et al. 2022
;
Varadarajan et al. 2024

Further exploration is required to assess whether these models are suitable for use in other stages of the mental health treatment pipeline.
It is a multi-stage process, encompassing assessment of several other conditions from time to time, and folding in interventions targeted at addressing the causes of patient’s issues.
While the nature of the collected data only allowed us to perform a cross-sectional analysis on a small population predominantly from the United Kingdom, we should also evaluate how these models assess mental health for other random samples of the population.
Accurate mental health assessments would require taking longitudinal symptom trajectories into account as well
Galatzer-Levy and Onnela 2023
to overcome the limitations of cross-sectional evaluations, which may fail to capture the dynamic nature of symptom progression and individual variability over time.
Although transformer-based models have been effective in encoding longitudinal representations of behavioral
Matero et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2022
and heath markers
Theodorou et al. 2023
, it is vital to understand how LLMs can effectively disentangle both these trajectories over time to assess the mental health severity.

Altogether, our analysis of
GPT-4
in assessing depressive symptoms highlights two key insights: (a) moderate to high convergence with self-reported data and (b) an underlying symptom schema that explained its assessments.

GPT-4
’s schema closely matched self-report for major symptoms, but notably downplayed the link with suicidal ideation and overemphasized psychomotor agitation/retardation.
This divergence was somewhat mitigated when suicidal ideation was explicitly mentioned, yet the strong overemphasis on psychomotor symptoms led to weaker alignment with both self-reports and expert ratings.
Our findings indicate that
GPT-4
has the potential to assist clinicians by efficiently assessing patients and prioritizing cases based on depression symptom severity, optimizing time for those in urgent need.
Expanding these methods to other mental health conditions and large language models will deepen our understanding of the benefits and limitations of these tools in enhancing mental health care.

Methods

Data

Collection.
Our data was collected as part of another study
Gu et al.
.
From 1040 participants in total, 258 (24.8%) screened participants reported having been diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) and 259 (24.8%) with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD); and 491 (47.2%) participants were recruited from an unscreened population.
32 (3.2%) respondents were excluded for not answering the control items (see Measures) correctly.
Removing participants that failed attention check and incomplete survey responses, the dataset comprised 963 participants, 571 identified as female, 388 as male, 2 preferred not to say, and 2 did not respond. The average age was 33.3 (SD = 11.2; range = 18 – 77) years.
Participants reported being from the U.S. (n = 284) and the U.K. (n = 679).

shows the summary statistics of the self-reported score for each

symptom in our data.

The Swedish National Ethics Review Board deemed the research study (Ethics Application 2020-00730) exempt from requiring ethical approval according to Swedish Law (see §§ 3-4 of the Act [2003:460] on ethical review of research involving humans in Sweden).
The research was carried out according to the Declaration of Helsinki.
Participants were recruited from the online platform Prolific
Palan and Schitter 2018
.
The sample comprised a mixture of screened and unscreened respondents.

Measures.
Below we only describe the measures used in this study – for all details regarding the data please refer to Gu et al., 2023
Gu et al.
.
Open-ended question on depression - questions asking participants to describe their depression was adapted from previous research
Kjell et al. 2019
and validated in Gu et al., 2023
Gu et al.
(under review). The questions read:

Over the last 2 weeks, have you been depressed or not?
Please answer the question by typing at least a paragraph below that indicates whether you have been depressed or not. Try to weigh the strength and the number of aspects that describe if you have been depressed or not so that they reflect your overall personal state of depression. For example, if you have been depressed, then write more about aspects describing this, and if you have not been depressed, then write more about aspects describing that.
Write about those aspects that are most important and meaningful to you.
Write at least one paragraph in the box.

Following this, participants were administered
PHQ-9

Kroenke et al. 2001
, a 9 item questionnaire used to assess symptoms of major depressive disorder as defined in the DSM-IV
Frances et al. 1995
.
The rating scale included nine items targeting symptoms such as “Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless”, referring to experiences from the last two weeks.
A four-point rating scale is used (0 = Not at all to 3 = Nearly every day) to indicate the experienced severity of each symptom.

Procedure.
Participants were first given information about the study, including information that participation was voluntary, that they could withdraw at any time, and that their answers were anonymous.
Before starting the study, all participants consented to take part in the study and to share their anonymized data openly.
The survey had three parts: First, participants answered open-ended questions concerning depression and anxiety/worry, which were presented in random order across participants.
Second, they filled out the rating scales, which were randomized.
Last, they reported the demographics, and mental health-related questions like sick leave and healthcare visits.
The median time to complete the study was 20.1 minutes (mean=24.0, SD=12.8).

Your task is to conduct an in-depth analysis of a provided written text, with the goal of mirroring the psychological state of the author to accurately fill out the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ9). The PHQ9 consists of 9 items that are designed to identify symptoms of depression:

•

Anhedonia
: Little interest or pleasure in doing things

•

Depressed Mood
: Feeling down, depressed, or hopeless

•

Insomnia or Hypersomnia
: Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much

•

Fatigue
: Feeling tired or having little energy

•

Poor Appetite or Overeating

•

Worthlessness or Guilt
: Feeling bad about yourself or that you are a failure or have let yourself or your family down

•

Difficulty Concentrating
: Trouble concentrating on things

•

Psychomotor Agitation or Retardation
: Moving or speaking so slowly that other people could have noticed, or being so fidgety or restless that you have been moving around a lot more than usual

•

Suicidal Ideation
: Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself

The text should be scrutinized carefully and the following steps should be strictly followed:

STEP 1
: Pinpoint and record only the PHQ9 items that are clearly stated in the text, assigning each a severity score between 0–3. At this stage, refrain from evaluating or recording items that are not explicitly mentioned. Enclose the text spans indicative of respective symptoms inside single quotes. If multiple spans are present, separate them using comma.

STEP 2
: For the remaining PHQ9 items not explicitly mentioned in the text, assign an estimated score. This estimation should be based on what you would expect it to be while factoring the severity score of the symptoms already identified.

STEP 3
: Combine the scores from the previous steps and present the total PHQ9 score as “Combined Score:”. Categorize this score into only one of the following: None, Mild, Moderate, Moderately Severe, and Severe, and present it as “Category:”. This will signify the completion of the task.

To ensure clarity and easy readability, format your output into a nested JSON. The first level should contain the step number as key (‘STEP #’), and the value should be a JSON containing the items as keys and a list containing reason(s) and the estimated severity score as values. The final step’s JSON should contain ‘Final’ as the key and a list containing the summed score and the evaluated category as the value. Here’s the abstracted format of the JSON, with elements inside the angle brackets being placeholders for the actual values:
{
"STEP 1": {
"<Item a>": ["<Reason>", "<Severity Score a>"],
"<Item b>": ["<Reason>", "<Severity Score b>"],
...
},
"STEP 2": {
"<Item x>": ["<Reason>", "<Severity Score x>"],
"<Item y>": ["<Reason>", "<Severity Score y>"],
...
},
"STEP 3": {
"Final": ["Combined Score: <score>", "Category: <category>"]
}
}

Keep in mind, a score of 0 indicates that the symptom was not experienced, while a score of 3 signifies a high severity level of the specific symptom.

Text
:
“I feel overwhelmed because I am bearing the weight of all the domestic and financial responsibilities. I should be getting more help than I am and it makes me feel resentful and annoyed with myself for allowing it to continue. I am avoiding talking to people because my emotions are unpredictable and I worry that the mask might slip.”

Experiment Design

Prompt.
Only the text response prompted to describe one’s depression was sent to
GPT-4

1

1

along with the instruction described in Table

.
The instruction was composed into three parts:

1.

Task description:
A short paragraph describing the task of estimating the
PHQ-9
item scores by analyzing the human-written text.
The description included the commonly abbreviated form of the 9 items from the
PHQ-9
questionnaire.

2.

Task Steps:
A detailed step-by-step instructions dictating
GPT-4
to first estimate the scores for the items that are explicitly stated in the text, followed by scoring the items that were not explicitly stated and finally, concluding with the combined PHQ9 score and severity category.

3.

Output Format:

GPT-4
was instructed to format its generation into a predefined data structure (JSON object) to facilitate easy processing of data for analysis.

Although,
GPT-4
generated outputs fulfilled the completion of
PHQ-9
estimation for all the data, it was not completely faithful to the details of the instructions.
There were a few issues observed with following the steps detailed in the instructions, especially step 1.
59 (6.1%) out of 956 samples, had small issues that were manually rectified. These included cases where
GPT-4
had

1.

made subtle changes to the symptom abbreviations (E.g. ’Feelings of Worthlessness or Guilt’ instead of ’Worthlessness or Guilt’),

2.

identified specific aspects of the symptoms (E.g. ’Insomnia’ in ’Insomnia or Hypersomnia’, ’Psychomotor Retardation’ in ’Psychomotor Agitation/Retardation’, ’Poor Appetite’ in ’Poor Appetite or overeating’ etc.),

3.

split the individual steps into separate JSONs, instead of nesting them within a single JSON as described in the prompt.

4.

included a summary of the assessment which was a short essay of its JSON output

5.

included symptoms that were not a part of the
PHQ-9
(E.g. Financial Worries, Social Exclusion)

For the first two cases, the symptoms were manually corrected to match the abbreviations provided in Step 1 of the instructions.
Type iii generations were rectified by manually reformatting the JSON, and the summaries in type iv generations were removed.
The symptoms in type v generations were left out of the analysis.

GPT-4
’s generations varied based on its hyperparameters.
For this work, the temperature value was set to 0.0 to select the most likely token at each step and to allow reproducibility of experiments.
Other hyperparameters include frequency penalty (0.1), presence penalty (0.0), top p (1.0), and max tokens (450).

Expert Judgements

Two psychology experts independently assessed the depression severity from user-written text on a random sample of 209 instances from the same dataset.
They were instructed to read the essay to identify excerpts indicative of
PHQ-9

If there were no indicative excerpts, they were asked to estimate the symptom severity based on the essay and the estimated explicit symptom scores.
The symptoms accompanied by excerpts would pertain to explicit markers as identified by experts.

Analysis

Item Response Theory.

In IRT, data from dichotomous or ordinal items are modeled using one or more latent variables with nonlinear link functions, most commonly logistic functions
Steinberg and Thissen 2006
.
For this analysis, we used the 1-PL dichotomous logistic IRT model, aka Rasch model
Rasch 1993
to fit item parameters and assess the presence of each of the nine symptoms.
This model constrains the slope parameter of the logistic function to 1.0, with the intercept or item location estimated from the responses.
The item location parameter indicates where each item stands in relation to assessing depression.
We analyzed the rankings of item locations to compare the two sets of scores across all 955 participants.

Interplay Between Explicit and Implicit Symptoms.

For each symptom
i
from the set of all symptoms
P
(as defined by the
PHQ-9
), we trained a linear regression model to predict the
GPT-4
-estimated score of symptom
i
when it was not explicitly identified.
The model was trained to minimize the sum of squared errors, using the scores of all other explicitly identified symptoms
s_{j}^{exp}
, where
j\in P
and
j\neq i
, as predictors.
The linear regression coefficients
\beta
represent the association between the implicit score of symptom
i
and the explicit scores of the remaining symptoms, specifically when symptom
i
was not explicitly identified by
GPT-4
in the user text:

s_{i}^{imp}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in P\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\beta_{j}s_{j}^{exp}

(1)

In cases where the score for
s_{j}^{exp}
was not explicit (i.e., not identified by
GPT-4
), it was replaced by the mean score for that symptom. This process was repeated 500 times using bootstrapped samples of the data. Coefficients whose 90% confidence intervals included 0 were excluded from
Figure 3
.

Language Of Depression.

For each user-written text, 1, 2, and 3 grams usage was computed as a relative frequency for each user-written text split into two groups, ie., tokens that were present in the
GPT-4
’s excerpt spans and tokens that were left out of the spans.
We removed all n-grams used by less than 10 participants and limited them to n-grams that were only composed of alphabets (filtering n-grams that had numbers and special characters).
We computed Cohen’s
d

Cohen 2013
using n-grams’ relative frequencies when it was found in explicit spans and the rest of the user text.
After applying Bonferroni correction, it resulted in 56 tokens associated with explicit indication of symptoms (positive
d
) and 30 that were implicit (negative
d
) that were statistically significant (
p<0.05
).

We computed the n-gram (1, 2, and 3) relative frequencies for each user-written text, and created two separate feature sets containing the relative frequencies of positive and negative Cohen’s
d
respectively.
Both features were filtered to n-grams with a Pointwise Mutual Information
Church and Hanks 1990
of at least 4.0, which reduced it to 45 and 25 features respectively.
To uncover if
GPT-4
estimates rely on any linguistic signals outside the explicit markers it generated, these sets of positive and negative words’ relative frequencies (computed per user) were correlated with the total
PHQ-9
score from
GPT-4
. This analysis was performed using DLATK
Schwartz et al. 2017
, a Python library for language analysis.

Renal Function and Quality of Life after the Mitrofanoff Procedure
Authors: M. Rakhesh, M. M. Zameer, Vinay Chandrashekar, Sanjay Rao, Ashley D’Cruz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Bladder dysfunction in children can result in recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs), incontinence, and progressive renal impairment. The Mitrofanoff procedure (MP) facilitates clean intermittent catheterization (CIC), promoting effective bladder emptying and reducing the risk of renal deterioration. However, data on long-term outcomes of this procedure remain limited in developing countries. This study was undertaken to address this gap and evaluate renal function and quality of life (QOL) following the MP in this population.Aims:To describe mid to long term renal function and QOL in children after MP and to analyze risk factors that influence these outcomes.Methodology:A retrospective longitudinal cohort study was conducted on children who underwent the MP between 2008 and 2023 with at least 6 months of follow-up. Renal function (estimated glomerular filtration rate, chronic kidney disease staging), QOL (Pediatric Incontinence Questionnaire scores), continence status, and CIC compliance were evaluated. Factors influencing renal outcomes were analyzed.Results:Sixty-two children were included (mean age 7.5 years; mean follow-up 3.2 years). Renal function remained stable or improved in 48 (77.4%) and deteriorated in 14. Recurrent UTIs resolved in 35 of 45 affected children. CIC compliance was observed in 48 (77.4%) and was strongly associated with better renal outcomes. Social continence was achieved in 60 children, and QOL improved in 58 (93.5%).Conclusion:The MP, combined with consistent CIC, effectively preserves renal function and enhances QOL in children with bladder dysfunction. Noncompliance with CIC and recurrent UTIs remain key risk factors for renal deterioration.

INTRODUCTION

Bladder dysfunction poses significant challenges for children and their families, often manifesting as lower urinary tract symptoms such as incontinence, dysfunctional voiding, and recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs). These patients are at increased risk of chronic kidney disease (CKD) and reduced quality of life (QOL). In 1980, Paul Mitrofanoff revolutionized the management of urinary incontinence and complex bladder dysfunction by describing a continent catheterizable channel. The Mitrofanoff procedure (MP) provides a long-term management option by enabling regular, effective urinary drainage, which helps preserve renal function and enhances overall QOL. there are numerous studies worldwide which have documented long-term outcomes of the MP, but there is limited data from developing countries. Socio-economic disparities, geographical diversity, and varying levels of healthcare access in rural areas create unique challenge.[12]

Objectives

To describe mid to long term renal function and QOL in children after MP and to analyze risk factors that influence these outcomes.

METHODOLOGY

We performed a retrospective longitudinal cohort study to determine renal functions and QOL of all children who underwent MP at our centre between 2008 and 2023. Those with a minimum follow-up of 6 months were included in the study. Data was collected through hospital visits, telephonic conversations, and medical records. Institutional Ethics Committee clearance was obtained.

Patients were enrolled in the study on completion of 6 months follow up after MP. The preoperative and postoperative serum creatinine levels were collected for all patients and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using Pottel equation. These children were grouped into various CKD stages (I–V) following National kidney foundations kidney disease outcomes quality initiative guidelines.[34] Any patient who had three or more episodes of culture proven UTI was considered to have had recurrent UTI.[5] QOL was evaluated using the Pediatric Incontinence Questionnaire (PinQ) and categorized as mild, moderate, or severe based on the score.[6] Social continence was defined as a dry period of at least 3 h between catheterizations and patients were considered compliant with clean intermittent catheterization (CIC) if he/she performed CIC more than three times a day, a pragmatic threshold commonly used in clinical practice to ensure regular bladder emptying and minimize urinary stasis. This definition was uniformly applied across the cohort and was based on caregiver-or patient-reported adherence. Patients were followed up 6 monthly in out patient department. The CKD staging and QOL was calculated on last follow up.

Effect of variables such as age of the patient, bladder augmentation, etiology (posterior urethral valve [PUV] or non PUV), recurrent UTI and compliance with CIC were evaluated for their effect on the outcome.

RESULTS

Forty seven were boys. The mean follow-up period was 3.2 years (range: 6 months–12 years). The indications for MP were neurogenic bladder in 33 and nonneurogenic in 29 children. DISCUSSION

Children with bladder dysfunction are at increased risk of renal deterioration and significant impairment in QOL. The underlying etiologies can broadly be classified into neurogenic (functional) and structural anomalies of the urinary tract.[78]

Following comprehensive urological evaluation in a child with bladder dysfunction, surgical planning must determine whether a Mitrofanoff channel alone is sufficient or whether additional procedures such as bladder augmentation or bladder neck repair are required to achieve a low-pressure, compliant reservoir with a competent outlet.[910]

We evaluated the mid to long-term impact of the MP on renal function and found that nearly two-thirds of children demonstrated stable or improved eGFR. This outcome is consistent with earlier studies showing that CIC contributes to preservation of renal function in children with bladder dysfunction.[1111213] In our cohort, 48 patients (77.4%) were compliant with CIC, and compliance was strongly associated with favorable renal outcomes. The 14 noncompliant patients commonly reported difficulty performing CIC in social environments and inadequate caregiver awareness of its importance–barriers also described by van Achterberg et al.[14] These findings highlight that sustained adherence to CIC is significantly associated with renal stability after the MP.

Renal impairment in these children is multifactorial. Early initiation of CIC is a key factor in preserving renal function by preventing urinary stasis and high intravesical pressures.[15] Kochakarn et al. reported that children starting CIC early had better renal outcomes.[16] Whereas, children with PUV may experience deterioration despite adequate bladder management due to irreversible prenatal nephron loss.[17] However in our group, children who had CKD preoperative, continued to deteriorate despite regular CIC. Therefore performing Mitrofanoff early to facilitate early initiation of CIC is recommended.

Recurrent UTIs are another determinant of renal deterioration. Regular CIC helps prevent urinary stasis and postvoid residual urine. Liu et al. demonstrated that consistent CIC significantly reduced UTI incidence and improved upper-tract dilatation and vesicoureteric reflux grades in children with neurogenic bladder.[18] In our study group, regular CIC reduced incidence of recurrent UTI in 35 of 45 patients.

Bladder augmentation provides additional benefit in optimizing bladder compliance, reducing intravesical pressures, and stabilizing renal function.[1920] However, augmented bladders require long-term follow-up because of metabolic, lithogenic, and neoplastic risks.[21]

Children achieving social continence also exhibit improved QOL. Thibodeau et al. found a strong association between urinary incontinence and reduced QOL, with incontinent children reporting significantly higher PinQ scores.[22] Various validated instruments have been developed to assess QOL in children with bladder dysfunction; among them, PinQ is the most widely used, offering reliability and sensitivity in evaluating the psychosocial burden of incontinence. In our cohort, 58 patients who achieved social continence demonstrated significantly improved QOL scores, with mean PinQ scores decreasing from 58.7 preoperatively to 31.17 postoperatively (P < 0.05). This finding emphasizes that achieving dryness extends beyond physical continence to enhance self-esteem, school participation, and social integration.

CONCLUSION

The MP provides an effective and durable method for achieving low-pressure bladder drainage and enhancing QOL in children with bladder dysfunction. In our cohort, most children exhibited stable or improved renal function following surgery, highlighting the protective effect of regular CIC in preserving upper urinary tract function. Early MP may facilitate improved CIC adherence and enable kidney-protective bladder emptying, rather than implying direct renal benefit from early surgery itself. Noncompliance with CIC and recurrent UTIs were the major determinants of renal function deterioration, emphasizing the importance of long-term adherence and infection control.

Children who achieved social continence experienced significantly better QOL scores, suggesting strong association between functional dryness and improved psychosocial well-being. Overall, the MP, when combined with appropriate bladder management, is associated with favourable renal and quality-of-life outcomes over mid-to long-term follow-up in children with complex bladder dysfunction.

Strengths

The study has a relatively long follow-up in the analyzed cohort, allowing meaningful assessment of renal function trends and quality-of-life outcomes using standardized renal staging and a validated, disease-specific QoL instrument.

Limitations

This is a single-centre retrospective study with a modest sample size, limiting generalizability. Exclusion of patients with inadequate follow-up introduces potential selection bias. Retrospective data collection and partial reliance on self-reported measures may contribute to information bias. The absence of multivariable analysis and adjunct renal markers limits causal inference. Prospective multicentre studies with standardized follow-up and adjusted analyses are needed.

Enhancements in Score-based Channel Estimation for Real-Time Wireless Systems Thanks: This work was funded by Huawei Technologies Sweden AB, Stockholm.
Florian Strasser, Marion Bäro, Wolfgang Utschick

Abstract
We propose enhancements to score-based generative modeling techniques for low-latency pilot-based channel estimation in a point-to-point single-carrier multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) wireless system. Building on recent advances in score-based models, we investigate a specific noise schedule design and sampling acceleration by step-skipping to reduce the number of denoising steps during inference. We additionally propose a single-step signal-to-noise ratio informed denoiser as an extreme case of the step-skipping approach. Our methods achieve significant latency reductions without performance degradation, as demonstrated on a synthetic channel dataset representing an urban macrocell MIMO communications scenario.

Introduction

Accurate
channel state information (CSI)

[
1
]
. However, conventional estimators based on maximum likelihood,
minimum mean square error (MMSE)
, and Bayesian techniques are reaching their limits. Therefore,
machine learning (ML)
and in particular

based methods have been proposed due to their great potential in learning the underlying prior distribution of a wireless channel
[
2
]
. This distribution covers the site-specific channel characteristics of a
base station (BS)
’s surrounding. In accordingly designed algorithms, exploiting this prior knowledge can significantly improve the channel estimation quality. Hence, various
GenAI
-based channel estimation algorithms using, e.g,
Gaussian mixture models (GMMs)

[
3
]
,
generative adversarial networks (GANs)

[
4
]
, and
variational autoencoders (VAEs)

[
5
]
have recently been proposed.

The latest advancements derive channel estimators based on
diffusion models (DMs)

[
6
]
and the closely related
score-based generative models (SBMs)

[
7
]
, showcasing great potential. Especially, their prevailing generative abilities compared to, e.g.,
GANs
, together with a stable training process and design flexibility, make them interesting candidates for wireless algorithm development. The major drawback of
DMs
and
SBMs
is their high latency during inference due to the iterative sampling procedure. To deploy algorithms that utilize
DMs
/
SBMs
in real-time wireless communications systems, we thus have to overcome this limitation.

Contributions

The main focus of this work is to further reduce the complexity of a
SBM
-based
MIMO
channel estimation algorithm. We examine improving the iterative denoising procedure of the

We adapt the channel estimation algorithm proposed in

]

•

We propose two different techniques to accelerate the denoising procedure, i.e., a specific noise schedule design and a step-skipping approach.

II
Problem Formulation

We consider a point-to-point single-carrier
MIMO
system with an
{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}
-antenna transmitter and an
{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}
-antenna receiver. The pilot-based transmission scheme is described by

{\bm{Y}}={\bm{H}}{\bm{P}}+{\bm{N}},

(1)

where
{\bm{Y}}\in\mathbb{C}^{{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}\times{N_{\mathrm{p}}}}
comprises the received signals,
{\bm{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}\times{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}}
describes the unknown channel matrix,
{\bm{P}}\in\mathbb{C}^{{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}\times{N_{\mathrm{p}}}}
is the unitary pilot matrix satisfying
{\bm{P}}{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{H}}=\mathbf{I}_{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}
and
{\bm{N}}\in\mathbb{C}^{{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}\times{N_{\mathrm{p}}}}
describes Gaussian noise with columns
{\bm{n}}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}({\bm{0}},\eta^{2}\mathbf{I})
. The objective is to recover the unknown channel matrix
{\bm{H}}
from the observed signal
{\bm{Y}}
. We assume
{N_{\mathrm{p}}}={N_{\mathrm{tx}}}
, resulting in a fully-determined channel estimation scenario. Rewriting (
1
) as

{\bm{y}}=({\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}}){\bm{h}}+{\bm{n}}={\bm{A}}{\bm{h}}+{\bm{n}},

(2)

with
{\bm{y}}=\vect({\bm{Y}})
,
{\bm{h}}=\vect({\bm{H}})
,
{\bm{n}}=\vect({\bm{N}})
and
{\bm{A}}=({\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}})
enables us to work with vectors instead of matrices. Note that
{\bm{A}}
is also unitary since it is derived by the Kronecker product of two unitary matrices.

The channels are assumed to be distributed according to an unknown prior
p({\bm{h}})
. Assuming
\E_{p}[{\bm{h}}]={\bm{0}}
and
\E_{p}[\|{\bm{h}}\|^{2}]={N_{\mathrm{tx}}}{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}
leads to the
SNR
definition
\mathrm{SNR}({\bm{y}})=1/\eta^{2}
.

IV
Enhancements in Score-based Modeling

The main bottleneck in Algorithm
2
is the number of required denoising steps
{\hat{k}}
as each step
k
requires a costly
neural function evaluation (NFE)
of
{\bm{s}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k)
. A naive approach to reducing
{\hat{k}}
is to reduce the total number of discretization points
K
. However, a drawback of this idea is that the worsened resolution of the discretized
SDE
is affecting the estimator’s performance
[
9
, Figure 4]
. The following two techniques introduce new approaches to reducing denoising steps.

IV-A

Noise Schedule Design

We consider the commonly used exponential noise schedule
\sigma(t)
, which is discretized by the power-exponentiated geometric sequence

\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}}\right)^{(\frac{k-1}{K-1})^{\gamma}}

(7)

with the parameter
\gamma
controlling the schedule’s steepness. The minimum/maximum variances are derived from the maximum/minimum
SNR
the
SBM
should cover via the relation

\sigma_{\text{min/max}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}^{\mathrm{SBM}}_{\text{max/min}}}}.

(8)

From Algorithm
2
, line 3, we can infer that the noise schedule directly influences
{\hat{k}}
and thus affects the algorithm’s time complexity. For
\gamma<1
, the sequence in (
7
) grows faster and therefore fewer denoising steps
{\hat{k}}
compared to a standard geometric sequence with
\gamma=1
are required for the same
\eta^{2}
. However, one has to take care that the
SBM
’s
SNR
resolution does not get too coarse for high
SNRs
.

Single-Step Channel Estimation

The method to speed up the algorithm even more introduced in this section is based on the general idea of skipping certain latent variables of a
DM
during sampling as proposed in, e.g.,
[
12
,
14
]
. In our
SBM
-based framework, the adapted deterministic denoising procedure incorporating this approach is inferred from (
6
) and given by

\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{k-\Delta}={\bm{h}}_{k}+(\sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k-\Delta}^{2}){\bm{s}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k),

(9)

where we introduced the step variable
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
. The case
\Delta=1
exactly resembles the standard method discussed in Section
III-B
. For
\Delta>1
, we skip certain points of the original discretization and, as a consequence, enlarge the step-size given by
\sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k-\Delta}^{2}
since
\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
is a monotonically increasing sequence. This effect can also be interpreted as a coarser discretization of the
reverse
SDE

during the denoising procedure. It is important to mention that we still maintain the fine resolution of the
SDE
for identifying the initial step
{\hat{k}}
.

In Algorithm
2
, line 6 has to be replaced with (
9
) to realize the accelerated denoising. Also, we have to ensure that the loop is indexed by
k={{\hat{k}},{\hat{k}}-\Delta,{\hat{k}}-2\Delta,\ldots,{\hat{k}}\mod\Delta}
. In the last denoising step, we set
\sigma_{k-\Delta}=0
to completely remove the remaining noise in the final iteration. The number of required denoising steps and thereby the algorithm’s complexity decreases to
{\tilde{k}}_{\Delta}=\lceil\frac{{\hat{k}}}{\Delta}\rceil
.

Single-Step SNR-informed Denoiser.
In the extreme case of
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathrm{max}}=K
, the number of denoising steps reduces to a single step

\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{0}={\bm{h}}_{k}+\sigma_{k}^{2}{\bm{s}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k)

(10)

which completely removes the denoising loop as depicted in Algorithm
3
. The changes compared to Algorithm
2
are highlighted in
blue
. This algorithm rather resembles a
SNR
-informed
NN
-based denoiser than a framework using a
SBM
.

To better understand how the single-step denoiser works, recall that in
DSM
, the
NN
is trained to estimate the score function of the diffused channel data conditioned on a ground-truth sample
{\bm{h}}_{0}
. This can also be reformulated as

{\bm{s}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k)\approx\nabla_{{\bm{h}}_{k}}q_{\sigma}({\bm{h}}_{k}|{\bm{h}}_{0})=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{k},

(11)

where
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{k}
is the noise realization used to generate
{\bm{h}}_{k}
. Therefore, during training, instead of directly estimating the score, we train a
NN

{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k)
to approximate
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{k}
and set the learned score to
{\bm{s}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k)=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k)
. Incorporating these reformulations into (
10
) yields

\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{0}={\bm{h}}_{k}-\sigma_{k}{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\bm{h}}_{k},k).

(12)

This can be interpreted as the approximated inverse of the
VE
Gaussian forward kernel expressed in (
4
). Thus, adding the scaled estimated score in (
10
) can be interpreted as a direct denoising step — effectively identifying and removing the added noise in a single step.

V-C

Baseline Estimators

The proposed
SBM
-based channel estimator is compared to the following baseline algorithms.

The
least squares (LS)
estimate denoted as ”LS” solves the optimization problem
\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{\mathrm{LS}}=\argmin_{{\bm{h}}}\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{A}}{\bm{h}}\|^{2}
and can be derived as
\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{\mathrm{LS}}={\bm{A}}^{\mathrm{H}}{\bm{y}}
as
{\bm{A}}
is assumed to be unitary. We also evaluate a
linear minimum mean squared error (LMMSE)
based estimate denoted as ”SCov-LMMSE” with a global sample covariance matrix
{\bm{C}}=\frac{1}{M_{\text{train}}}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\text{train}}}{\bm{h}}_{m}{\bm{h}}_{m}^{\mathrm{H}}
as
\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{\textrm{Scov-LMMSE}}={\bm{C}}({\bm{C}}+\eta^{2}\mathbf{I})^{-1}{\bm{y}}
.

As prior-aided baselines, we choose two different
GMM
-based estimators for comparison. The first version, denoted as ”GMM” and proposed in
[
3
]
, fits a
GMM
to the ground-truth
CSI
data such that it approximates the underlying distribution well. The learnt means and covariances of the Gaussian components are then exploited in an adapted
LMMSE
estimator. The second version, denoted as ”GMM-kron” and proposed in
[
28
]
, follows the same reasoning but trains two separate
GMMs
on the receiver and transmitter side, respectively. Afterwards, covariance matrices of both
GMMs
are combined with the Kronecker product to build the final covariance matrices used in the adapted
LMMSE
.

V-D

We opt for the
normalized mean squared error (NMSE)
metric to assess the channel estimator’s performance. Therefore, for each noise level, a noisy observation of each channel sample
{\bm{h}}_{m}
in the test set is created according to (
1
). Then, a channel estimate
\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{m}
is computed with one of the considered estimators. The
NMSE
for each
SNR
level is evaluated as

\mathrm{NMSE}=\frac{1}{{N_{\mathrm{rx}}}{N_{\mathrm{tx}}}M_{\text{test}}}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\text{test}}}\|{\bm{h}}_{m}-\hat{{\bm{h}}}_{m}\|_{2}^{2}.

(13)

It is important to note that our
SBM
-based channel estimator does not require retraining for different
SNRs
but operates on the whole
SNR

V-E

Noise Schedule Design Evaluation

The effect of choosing different noise schedules on the number of required denoising steps is depicted in Fig.
2
for different choices of the parameter
\gamma
. Because the noise schedule is an exponential function and we plot the
SNRs
on a logarithmic scale, a linear decrease of
{\hat{k}}
can be seen for the regular geometric sequence with
\gamma=1
in black. As proposed in Section
IV-A
and validated in Fig.
2
, decreasing
\gamma
significantly decreases
{\hat{k}}
for every
\mathrm{SNR}({\bm{y}})
. We also include a simulation for
\gamma>1
to investigate the effect on the performance when increasing the number of denoising steps.

Fig.
2
depicts the
NMSE
for the different choices of
\gamma
. There is almost no degradation in performance when decreasing
\gamma
, only in the high-
SNR
regime. The deterioration stems from the
SDE
discretization being too coarse in the low-noise region for small values of
\gamma
. Also, as the graph for
\gamma=1.6
is on par with the others, there is no benefit in increasing the number of denoising steps by adapting the noise schedule. This indicates that the algorithm can denoise a pilot observation well as long as we maintain a good enough
SDE
discretization.

V-F

Accelerated Denoising Evaluation

Even more impressive is the reduction of denoising steps with the accelerated denoising approach, cf. Section
IV-B
, as depicted in Fig.
4
. The black curve again resembles the baseline implementation, where every discretization step is conducted in the denoising procedure. With increasing
\Delta
, we can accelerate the denoising procedure by roughly a factor of
\Delta
until only a single step is required for
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathrm{max}}
independent of the
SNR
.

Surprisingly, increasing the step variable
\Delta
does not worsen the
NMSE
.
Fig.
4
showcases that it even improves the channel estimation performance by a small but significant margin, not stemming from computational instabilities, over the whole
SNR
range.
Especially, in the mid-
SNR
range, the improvement is clearly visible. This result seems counterintuitive at first sight; however, we make the following hypothesis describing the effect: We refer to
[
9
, Fig. 4]
, where the authors show the evolution of the
NMSE
performance during the denoising procedure for different
SNR
levels. However, one artifact not discussed in the paper is a rise in the
NMSE
at the final steps of the estimation procedure after the
NMSE
has reached its minimum. Although we still do not fully understand this effect, we claim that increasing
\Delta
mitigates this effect, which explains the counterintuitive
NMSE
improvement in Fig.
4
.

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

This paper introduced two methods to accelerate the denoising procedure of a
SBM
-based
MIMO
channel estimator. By carefully adapting the
forward
SDE
’s
noise schedule, we significantly reduced the number of denoising steps with only minor performance losses. An even faster acceleration was achieved by skipping certain steps in the denoising procedure, which in the extreme case results in the single-step denoiser. This approach even improves the estimation performance due to the mitigation of an artifact in a previously proposed
DM
-based channel estimator.

Further research should clarify the theoretical optimality guarantees of the proposed denoiser and identify its limitations. Also, investigating the performance for different wireless communications scenarios is essential for the algorithm’s generalization capabilities. Regarding this point, an interesting research question not considered in this work is, e.g., what effect a rotation of the
MT

ULAs
has on the channel estimation performance. As a final suggestion, the proposed estimator should be adapted to operate in a
orthogonal frequency division multiplex (OFDM)
ed MIMO system for actual deployment in real-world communications scenarios.

Mammogram geometric projection localization (MaGPL) for surgical body-surface localization of nonpalpable breast calcifications
Authors: Xiangqing Chen, Xin Lin, Bo Liang, Yuhang Xu, Jiali Lin, Yi Zhang, Yifang Zhang, Lijuan Guo, Jinbo Huang, Zhuliang Ding, Renxing Huang, Qiunan Kong, Fen Zhang, Haiming Liu, Yuxia Chen, Guochun Zhang, Yulei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrecise surgical excision of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications remains challenging when conventional localization equipment is limited or not routinely available across clinical settings, underscoring the need for an accessible and reproducible alternative approach. This study aimed to assess the feasibility and localization accuracy of a novel mammography-based localization strategy for guiding diagnostic surgical excision of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective study evaluated a mammography-based geometric localization approach termed mammogram geometric projection localization (MaGPL). The technique applies a boundary-defined, range-based “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” to reconstruct a body-surface coordinated field from two standard mammographic projections, preferentially the craniocaudal (CC) and lateromedial (LM) views. By deliberately selecting the most geometrically stable boundary information from each projection, MaGPL defines axis-specific localization boundaries, forming the geometric basis for boundary-defined localization of nonpalpable calcifications. A total of 41 patients (45 lesions) underwent diagnostic excision guided by MaGPL. Localization accuracy was evaluated using the geometric offset (Doffset), defined as the linear distance between the radiographic center of the calcification cluster and the center of the excised specimen measured on intraoperative mammography.ResultsThe cohort had a median age of 48 years (range, 25–81 years) and a mean mammographically estimated breast volume of 370±158 mL. The mean linear extent of the target calcification clusters was 22.6±15.1 mm (range, 3.0–67.0 mm), and pathologic examination confirmed malignancy in 18 of 45 lesions (40.0%). All calcification clusters were successfully excised in a single operation, achieving a 100% intraoperative retrieval rate. The median Doffset was 9.7 mm [interquartile range (IQR), 8.3–13.0 mm], indicating a clinically acceptable level of geometric localization precision. Orthogonal CC + LM projections yielded smaller offsets than non-orthogonal combinations (P=0.003). Larger breast volumes were associated with greater Doffset (P<0.001), whereas breast density and calcification extent or location showed no significant association.ConclusionsMaGPL represents a standardized, equipment-independent alternative for body-surface localization to guide surgical excision of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications, offering reproducible and clinically applicable localization accuracy without additional cost, with particular utility in community and resource-limited settings.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This multicenter retrospective study demonstrates that mammogram geometric projection localization (MaGPL) technique enables reliable body-surface localization of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications using standard mammographic views. All targeted calcifications were successfully excised in a single procedure, with a median geometric offset of 9.7 mm. Localization accuracy was primarily influenced by projection geometry and breast volume.

What is known and what is new?

• Conventional localization of nonpalpable breast calcifications relies on wire- or device-based techniques, which are resource-intensive and not universally available. Earlier geometric localization approaches were limited by instability related to center-based estimation and projection distortion.

• MaGPL introduces a boundary-defined, range-based geometric framework incorporating a Maximal Width Crossing Criterion. By leveraging axis-specific stability across mammographic projections, this method improves reproducibility and reduces operator dependence without requiring specialized equipment.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• MaGPL provides a standardized, equipment-independent localization strategy that can be readily implemented using routine mammography. This approach is particularly applicable in community hospitals and resource-limited settings where conventional localization devices are unavailable. Adoption of boundary-based geometric localization may expand access to accurate diagnostic excision of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications and support more equitable breast cancer care.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common malignancy among women worldwide, accounting for more than 2.29 million new cases annually according to the GLOBOCAN 2022 report (1). The widespread implementation of mammographic screening has markedly increased the detection of nonpalpable breast lesions, particularly microcalcifications, which often represent early precancerous or in situ disease (2,3). Accurate preoperative localization and surgical excision of these lesions are essential to enable definitive pathological diagnosis while minimizing unnecessary removal of surrounding breast tissue. Conventional approaches for localizing nonpalpable calcifications, including wire-guided localization, stereotactic-guided procedures, and wire-free device-based techniques such as radioactive seed localization, magnetic seed localization, radar reflector localization, and radiofrequency identification tag localization, have been widely adopted in clinical practice (4,5).

Wire-guided localization involves preoperative placement of a hooked wire into the target lesion under imaging guidance to facilitate surgical excision, whereas stereotactic-guided procedures allow lesion localization or biopsy through three-dimensional (3D) spatial targeting based on mammographic stereotactic coordinates (6,7). Although clinically effective, these methods remain highly dependent on dedicated equipment and specialized imaging platforms, which may not be routinely available in all institutions, particularly in community and resource-limited settings. Accordingly, there remains a clinical need for a simple, non-invasive, and widely accessible localization method to support accurate excision of nonpalpable microcalcifications.

The concept of using mammographic images for geometric localization on the body surface was first introduced by Berger and colleagues in 1966, who described a nipple-based triangulation method for localizing nonpalpable breast lesions (8). Despite its theoretical simplicity and zero-cost nature, the method was gradually abandoned because of inconsistent localization precision largely due to limited image resolution and lack of standardized geometric criteria at the time (9). With subsequent advances in mammographic imaging quality and digital calibration (10), the underlying geometric principle has regained potential clinical relevance as a cost-effective alternative.

Building upon the original geometric projection principle proposed by Berger et al. (8), we developed a modern adaptation termed mammogram geometric projection localization (MaGPL). Rather than relying on lesion center-based estimation (11,12), MaGPL applies a boundary-defined, range-based geometric strategy incorporating a “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” to improve spatial stability and reproducibility. Accordingly, we conducted a multicenter retrospective analysis to evaluate the clinical reliability of the MaGPL technique for facilitating accurate excision and definitive pathological diagnosis of nonpalpable breast calcifications through open surgical excision. The geometric offset (Doffset), defined as the linear distance between the radiographic center of the calcification cluster and the geometric center of the excised specimen, was used as a quantitative metric to assess localization precision and reproducibility across different clinical settings. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-1-0085/rc).

Methods

Study design and patient selection

This multicenter retrospective study included patients treated between January 2023 and September 2025 at Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (GDPH), Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital’s Nanhai Hospital (GDNH), and Maoming People’s Hospital (MMPH), all located in Guangdong Province, China. Eligible patients were those with relatively stable breast geometry in the supine position. To minimize positional variability between mammographic imaging and surgical conditions, patient selection was restricted to individuals without ptosis or with mild ptosis (Regnault Grade I), while those with moderate (Grade II) or severe (Grade III) ptosis were excluded (13). All centers followed a unified MaGPL protocol with harmonized labeling rules to ensure methodological consistency across institutions. Patients with nonpalpable mammographic calcifications classified as suspicious or indeterminate underwent MaGPL-guided surgical excision for definitive pathological diagnosis. Preoperative biopsy was not routinely performed primarily because stereotactic or wire localization systems were unavailable across all participating centers. Patients diagnosed with breast cancer subsequently underwent standard surgical management, including breast-conserving surgery or mastectomy, in accordance with clinical guidelines and patient preference. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the ethics committees of all participating centers (GDPH: No. KY-Q-2021-195-01; GDNH: No. GDNHREC2021001H; MMPH: No. PJ2025MI-K044-02), and informed consent was obtained from all individual participants before surgery.

Body surface localization procedure (MaGPL technique)

In this study, the MaGPL technique, a boundary-defined, range-based geometric projection strategy, was applied for body-surface localization using two standard mammographic views, preferentially the craniocaudal (CC) and lateromedial (LM) views, with the mediolateral oblique (MLO) view used as an alternative when either the CC or LM view was not applicable. The technique incorporates the “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” to define the largest cross-projection boundaries of the calcification range derived from these views, differing from conventional lesion center-based localization (11,12). To ensure reproducibility, all boundary markings and distance measurements were independently reviewed by two breast radiologists, and any discrepancies were resolved by consensus. The measured distances were subsequently transferred to the skin surface by the surgeon to delineate the projected localization field. The entire marking procedure typically took approximately 10–15 minutes. The procedural workflow is presented in the “Results” section.

Specimen evaluation and quantitative assessment of localization accuracy

The surgical specimen was subjected to intraoperative mammography to confirm complete lesion removal. Doffset, measured in millimeters (mm), was defined as the linear distance between the geometric center of the excised specimen and the center of the calcification cluster, serving as the quantitative indicator of localization accuracy.

Breast volume estimation based on mammographic CC view

The mammography-based breast volume (VMGM) was estimated based on the hemi-ellipsoid geometric model, as previously reported (14,15). On the CC image, W (cm) was defined as the maximum breast base diameter, and H (cm) was the perpendicular distance from the nipple base to the chest wall. Mammographic calcification extent measurement

The linear extent of the target calcification cluster (mm) was recorded as the longest diameter measured across all available standard mammographic views.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS (version 26.0). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median [interquartile range (IQR)], and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. The two groups, stratified by geometric localization accuracy, were compared using appropriate non-parametric tests (e.g., Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and Chi-square/Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables). There were no missing data for variables included in the final analysis. A two-sided P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demonstration of the MaGPL procedure and illustrative cases

The complete procedural workflow for the boundary-defined MaGPL technique incorporating the “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” is illustrated in Figure 1A. The nipple-base center serves as a fixed anatomical origin, and a perpendicular reference line to the detector edge defines the projection baseline in all standard views. Notably, in the MLO view, the reference line was drawn toward the detector edge rather than the pectoral muscle line to minimize projection bias and improve geometric consistency. The “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” was applied by delineating the medial-lateral (X-axis) limits on the CC view and the superior-inferior (Y-axis) limits or the oblique Y-axis limits on the LM or MLO views. As shown in Figure 1A, the orthogonal CC + LM combination produced a rectangular crossing area (CC + LM width crossing), while the CC + MLO combination generated a parallelogram-shaped crossing area (CC + MLO width crossing) owing to its oblique geometry. Using this workflow, the entire procedure, from radiologic measurement to surgeon-performed body-surface marking, typically took approximately 10–15 minutes.

Case 1: CC + MLO combination for complex calcifications with discrete and amorphous foci

A 44-year-old female (breast volume VMMG =357 mL) presented with clustered and adjacent amorphous calcifications in the left breast, spanning the upper- and lower-outer quadrants near the areola. Localization was achieved using the CC + MLO combination because the LM view was inadequate due to nipple distortion. The CC view defined X-axis limits (Figure 1B), and the MLO view defined oblique Y-axis limits (Figure 1C), with the superior limit set by a discrete calcification outside the main cluster and the inferior limit by the adjacent amorphous focus. These boundaries were transferred to the skin (Figure 1D), and the oblique Y-axis was marked according to the C-arm gantry angle used during the patient’s MLO mammographic acquisition. Specimen mammography confirmed complete removal (Figure 1E), and pathology identified high-grade ductal carcinoma in situ with microinvasion (DCIS-MI), with negative surgical margins (Figure 1F).

Case 2: CC + LM combination for a cluster with scattered calcifications

A 48-year-old female (VMMG =395 mL) presented with calcifications in the upper-outer quadrant of the left breast. Localization used the preferred orthogonal CC + LM combination. In the CC view, discrete calcifications outside the main cluster remained within the X-axis limits (Figure 2A). In the LM view, discrete calcifications were apparent (Figure 2B), one of which defined the most inferior Y-axis boundary for the “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” (Figure 2C). The entire lesion, including discrete calcifications, was completely excised (Figure 2D), and pathology confirmed invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC) with mucin production and negative surgical margins (Figure 2E).

Case 3: CC + MLO localization for distant axillary-tail lesion

A 53-year-old female (VMMG =299 mL) had calcifications in the upper-outer quadrant of the right breast near the axillary tail. The CC + MLO combination was employed because the LM view was unavailable (Figure 3A,3B). The oblique Y-axis, marked on the skin, corresponded to the C-arm gantry angle used during the patient’s MLO mammographic acquisition (Figure 3C). Patient and lesion characteristics

A total of 41 patients underwent 45 MaGPL procedures for nonpalpable breast microcalcifications, including four patients with bilateral cases. The demographic, clinical, and radiologic characteristics of the cohort are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 48 years (range, 25–81 years), and the mean body mass index (BMI) was 22.7±2.6 kg/m2. The mean mammographically estimated breast volume (VMMG) was 370±158 mL (range, 117–772 mL), which is comparable to previously reported breast volume measurements in Chinese women (approximately 340±109 mL) (16).

Mammographic findings showed that high-density breasts [American College of Radiology (ACR) categories C/D] accounted for 40.0% (n=18) of all procedures. Most target lesions (68.9%, n=31) were classified as Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) category 4 or 5, indicating a high preoperative suspicion of malignancy. MaGPL localization strategy and pathological outcomes

The preferred orthogonal CC + LM view combination was successfully applied in 55.6% (n=25) of the procedures, whereas non-orthogonal view combinations (CC + MLO or LM + MLO) were utilized in 44.4% (n=20). Final histopathological examination revealed that 18 lesions (40.0%) were malignant (IDC, DCIS, or DCIS-MI), 3 lesions (6.7%) were classified as borderline lesions, and 24 lesions (53.3%) were purely benign (Table 1). Among the malignant lesions, surgical margins were negative in 16 cases (88.9%) and positive in 2 cases (11.1%).

Procedural success and geometric accuracy

All 45 targeted calcification clusters were successfully excised in a single diagnostic operation, achieving a 100% intraoperative retrieval rate. No cases in our cohort required re-localization or re-excision during the diagnostic stage. The geometric localization accuracy of the MaGPL method was objectively quantified using intraoperative specimen mammography. The offset distance (Doffset) served as the quantitative indicator of geometric precision. The median Doffset within the cohort was 9.7 mm (IQR, 8.3–13.0 mm), with values spanning 0 to 22.7 mm.

Factors associated with MaGPL geometric accuracy

Among the 45 procedures, 25 (55.6%) had a Doffset of ≤10.0 mm, and 20 (44.4%) had a Doffset greater than 10.0 mm. This binary classification was utilized to assess the relationship between Doffset and clinical-radiologic variables. As summarized in Table 2, two factors were significantly associated with MaGPL localization accuracy: VMMG and projection geometry. The mean VMMG was higher in cases with Doffset >10.0 mm (458±160 mL) than in those with Doffset ≤10.0 mm (299±116 mL, P<0.001), indicating greater Doffset in larger breasts. Regarding projection strategy, the orthogonal CC + LM combination was predominantly used in the Doffset ≤10.0 mm group (80.0%), whereas non-orthogonal combinations were more frequent in the Doffset >10.0 mm group (75.0%, P=0.003). In contrast, no significant differences were found for ACR breast density, calcification location, distribution pattern, or extent. Thus, breast volume and projection geometry were identified as the primary determinants of localization accuracy in the MaGPL technique.

Discussion

When conventional localization equipment, such as wire localization or stereotactic guidance systems, is unavailable, precise surgical excision of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications becomes particularly challenging. In this setting, we demonstrate that the MaGPL technique provides a practical, fully mammography-based geometric alternative for guiding accurate open excision. All calcification clusters in our cohort were successfully excised in a single operation, supporting the technical feasibility of this approach. Quantitative analysis identified breast volume (VMMG) and projection geometry as the primary determinants of localization accuracy, whereas no significant effect was observed for other radiologic characteristics, including breast density and the location and extent of calcifications.

The methodological innovation of MaGPL lies in its transition from a conventional lesion center-based paradigm to a boundary-defined, range-based geometric framework. Traditional center-based localization relies on a circular approximation, defining a center point and a presumed resection radius in each projection view (11,12). However, under mammographic compression, the calcification clusters are often flattened, irregular, and asymmetrically deformed, resulting in projection-dependent geometric distortion. Consequently, the apparent lesion center identified in different views may correspond to distinct spatial points in 3D space rather than a single fixed location. In addition, variability in lesion center identification and radius estimation across operators further amplifies this view-dependent uncertainty in the absence of standardized geometric criteria. Taken together, the 3D target inferred from intersecting projected lesion centers lacks a stable and reproducible geometric definition.

From a geometric perspective, mammographic compression does not affect all spatial axes equally. In the CC view, medial-lateral boundaries of a calcification cluster remain relatively stable, whereas the anterior-posterior dimension is distorted by tissue compression and projection geometry. Conversely, in LM and MLO views that preserve vertical information, superior-inferior boundaries are more reliably maintained. MaGPL builds upon this axis-specific stability by defining localization boundaries along the most reliable axes of each projection, forming the geometric basis for a boundary-defined localization strategy.

The “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion” further stabilizes this framework by prioritizing the widest projection limits along principal axes, thereby minimizing deformation-related variability. Projection geometry significantly influenced localization accuracy, with orthogonal CC and LM combinations demonstrating higher accuracy than oblique view combinations (P=0.003). Consequently, orthogonal projections should be preferred whenever feasible. An additional technical consideration arises in MLO projections. In conventional practice, auxiliary reference lines are often drawn perpendicular to the pectoralis muscle line; however, the pectoral contour is anatomically variable and sensitive to patient positioning and compression, limiting its reliability as a geometric reference. In MaGPL, auxiliary reference lines in the oblique view are instead defined relative to the fixed physical boundary of the detector. As a stable and reproducible external reference, the detector edge improves geometric consistency in oblique projections and reduces localization uncertainty associated with non-orthogonal views.

Breast volume was also associated with localization accuracy (P<0.001), with larger breast volume (VMMG) corresponding to greater Doffset, likely reflecting increased positional variation between the compressed mammographic state and the supine surgical position. Importantly, this geometric deviation did not compromise surgical feasibility, as all calcification clusters were successfully excised across the study cohort. This observed association between larger breast volume and increased Doffset may be partially explained by the higher likelihood of breast ptosis in larger breasts, which can introduce additional positional instability. Accordingly, appropriate patient selection is essential for the reliable application of MaGPL. In the present cohort, we included only patients without ptosis or with mild ptosis (Regnault Grade I), while those with moderate to severe ptosis (Grade II–III) were excluded to reduce positional instability. Caution is warranted when applying this technique to patients with moderate to severe breast ptosis or significant breast deformation, where increased positional instability may compromise localization accuracy.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The retrospective design precluded evaluation of additional spatial parameters, such as nipple-to-lesion distance or lesion depth, and the contribution of intraoperative visual and tactile feedback was not quantified. Future prospective studies incorporating refined geometric modeling and 3D spatial descriptors may further validate boundary-based localization across broader clinical settings. Importantly, MaGPL is not intended to replace stereotactic biopsy or wire-guided localization, both of which remain well-established and clinically valuable in the management of nonpalpable breast calcifications. Rather, it may serve as a complementary, equipment-independent, and zero-cost option for guiding diagnostic surgical excision when such techniques are not routinely available.

Conclusions

In summary, the MaGPL technique represents a conceptual and practical advancement in mammography-based geometric localization. By replacing unstable center-based triangulation with a boundary-defined, range-based geometric model anchored by the “Maximal Width Crossing Criterion”, MaGPL mitigates geometric inaccuracies associated with mammographic compression and projection asymmetry while reducing operator-dependent variability related to lesion center identification and resection radius selection. When conventional device-based localization methods are not readily available, this standardized, reproducible, non-invasive, and zero-cost approach may serve as a feasible alternative for accurate body-surface localization to guide surgical excision of nonpalpable breast microcalcifications within routine clinical workflows.

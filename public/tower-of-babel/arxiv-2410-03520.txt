Cohomology Rings of Toric Wonderful Model
Lorenzo Giordani, Roberto Pagaria, Viola Siconolfi

Abstract
. We describe the cohomology ring of toric wonderful model for arbitrary building set, including the case of non well-connected ones. Our techniques are based on blowups of posets, on Gröbner basis over rings and admissible functions.



1.
Introduction

We consider a
n
-dimensional algebraic torus
T
with caracter group
X^{\ast}(T)=\Hom(T,\mathbb{Z})
, a split direct summand
\Gamma<X^{\ast}(T)
, and any group homomorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
.
The subvariety of
T

\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}:=\{t\in T\mid\chi(t)=\phi(\chi)\textnormal{, for all }\chi\in\Gamma\}

has codimension equal to
\rk_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Gamma)
.
This is a coset of the subtorus
H=\bigcap_{\chi\in\Gamma}\ker(\chi)\subseteq T
.
A
toric arrangement
in
T
is a finite collection of subtori
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{K}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{K}_{m}\}
.
A subtorus of codimension one is called an hypertorus; a toric arrangement given by hypertori is said to be
divisorial
.
The research over recent years was focused on the topology of the complement
M(\mathcal{A}):=T\setminus\bigcup_{i=1,\dots,m}\mathcal{K}_{i}
and how the topological invariants depend on the combinatorics of the toric arrangement.
A ubiquitous technique in algebraic geometry consists in finding nice compactifications. For the open variety
M(\mathcal{A})
, this was done by De Concini and Gaiffi in
[
DCG18
]
by defining a
projective wonderful model
.
The wonderful model depends on the choice of a toric variety and a building set, for technical reasons the researchers focused on building set
\mathcal{G}
with a certain combinatorial condition, called
well-connectedness
.
In this article we describe the cohomology ring of toric wonderful models for arbitrary building sets, we report an example (
2.10
) that shows how removing the well-connected hypothesis makes the choice of the building set more convenient.

Toric arrangements were introduced in the seminal article
[
DCP05
]
, where the additive structure of
H^{*}(M(\mathcal{A});\mathbb{C})
is presented.
Based on that article, the cohomology ring for the complement of divisorial toric arrangements were presented in
[
CDD+20
]
and in
[
CD17
,
CD24
]
.
The homotopy type of the complement were studied in
[
MS11
,
dD12
,
dD15
]
with focus on fundamental group and minimality.
The combinatorics of divisorial toric arrangements were studied using aritmetic matroids

[
Moc12a
,
DM13
,
BM14
,
Pag20
,
PP21
]
and posets
[
DR18
,
Bib22
]
.
The first partial compatification was introduced in
[
Moc12b
]
, but the projective wonderful model was constructed in
[
DCG18
]
.
The construction depends on the choice of a

building set

\mathcal{G}
; for well-connected building sets the cohomology ring of the wonderful model was presented
[
DCG19
,
DCGP20
]
and an additive basis was found
[
GPS22
]
.
Also the Morgan model was studied
[
MP22
,
DCG21
]
for well-connected building sets.

Our main result (
Theorem

5.5
) consists in a presentation by generators and relations of the cohomology of the toric wonderful model
Y(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G})
, that depends on the choice of a toric variety
X_{\Delta}
with an
equal sign property
and a building set
\mathcal{G}
.
Without the hypothesis that
\mathcal{G}
is well-connected, the cohomology ring is not generated in degree one and our presentation is rather convoluted.

The paper is organized as follow: in
Section

2
we extend the definition and properties of blowups of meet-semilattices due to
[
FK04
]
to a more general class of poset given by
local lattices
.
In
Section

3
, we prove a lemma (
Theorem

3.2
) on the cohomology of blowups that generalizes the one by Keel
[
Kee92
]
removing a surjectivity assumption. This is necessary, because the inclusions we deal with do not induce surjective pullbacks in cohomology.
After a quick review of construction and properties of toric wonderful model (
Section

4
), the definition of our presentation
R(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G})
by generators and relations is given in
Section

5
and certain pullbacks and pushforward maps are computed.
In
Section

6
we provide a Gröbner basis for our presentation
R(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G})
, however the proof is postponed to
Section

7
. The set of irreducible monomials with respect to the Gröbner basis form an additive basis for the cohomology ring; we describe them in terms of
admissible functions
. This section generalizes the results in
[
GPS22
]
.
The core of our proofs is in
Section

7
, where we construct a surjective map
R(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G})\twoheadrightarrow H^{*}(Y(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G});\mathbb{Z})
.
By using some lemmas from the previous sections we prove with deletion-contraction technique our main theorem: indeed the two
\mathbb{Z}
-modules
R(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G})
and
H^{*}(Y(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G});\mathbb{Z})
have the same rank and
R(X_{\Delta},\mathcal{G})
is torsion free.
In the last Section (
8
), we describe the strata as a product of toric and hyperplanes wonderful model.
This observation suggests that toric wonderful models forms a module over the operad of hyperplane wonderful models.

2.
Blowup of local lattices

In this section we recall basic notions from
[
FK04
]
, and we adapt them to our setting of toric arrangements and their underlying posets.
The class of poset that we are going to consider are local lattices.
We denote by
x\vee y
the set of all possible least upper bound and by
x\wedge y
the set of all greatest lower bound of
x
and
y
.
If the poset has a minimum then the set
x\wedge y
is always non-empty.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a poset
\mathcal{L}
and
p\in\mathcal{L}
we define the

blowup of poset
at
p
:

\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}=\{x\mid x\not\geq p\}\sqcup\{(p,x,y)\mid x\not\geq p,\,y\in p\vee x\}

with the ordering given by

(1)

x\geq x^{\prime}
if
x\geq x^{\prime}
,

(2)

(p,x,y)\geq(p,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
if
x\geq x^{\prime}
and
y\geq y^{\prime}
,

(3)

(p,x,y)\geq x^{\prime}
if
x\geq x^{\prime}

together with a projection map

\pi=\pi_{\mathcal{L}}\colon\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}

defined by
\pi(x)=x
and
\pi((p,x,y))=y
.

When the join between two elements
p,x
is a singleton, we denote with
p\vee x
its only element, instead of the one-element set. In the same situation, for blowups of posets, we write
(p,x)
in place of
(p,x,p\vee x)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a poset with minimum and
\p
any poset property. We say that
\mathcal{L}
has property
\p

locally
, if every lower interval in
\mathcal{L}
has property
\p
. For instance, we say that
\mathcal{L}
is a local lattice, local geometric lattice, or locally boolean, if every lower interval of
\mathcal{L}
is a lattice, a geometric lattice, or a boolean poset, respectively.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is a local lattice (respectively a local geometric lattice) and
p\in\mathcal{L}
, then
\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}
is a local lattice (respectively a local geometric lattice).

Proof.

For the elements
\{x\mid x\ngeq p\}
in the blowup, there is nothing to prove. Consider any element
(p,x,y)
with
x\ngeq p
, and
y\in p\vee x
. Then, the following isomorphism of posets holds:

[\hat{0},(p,x,y)]^{\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}}\simeq[\hat{0},x]^{\mathcal{L}}\times B_{1},

where
B_{1}
is the poset on 2 elements
\{\hat{0}<(p,\hat{0})\}
. Thus, the left hand side is a (geometric) lattice, because the right hand side is a product of (geometric) lattices.
∎

A
ranked poset
is a pair
(\mathcal{L},\rk)
with
\mathcal{L}
a poset with minimum
\hat{0}
and a rank function
\rk\colon\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that:

(1)

\rk(\hat{0})=0

(2)

for any
X\leq Y
in
\mathcal{L}
,
\rk(X)\leq\rk(Y)
.

Our definition of ranked poset does not coincide with the one used in other context and it is completely different from the standard definition of graded posets. A ranked (local) lattice is a ranked poset that is also a (local) lattice.

The main instance of local lattices will be certain posets associated to toric (and more generally abelian) arrangements. Given a toric arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, the set of connected components of intersections of its subtori ordered by reverse inclusion is the
poset of layers
, often denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
. This poset referred to as the “combinatorics” of the arrangement, and it is its most important invariant.
We always endow the poset of layers with the rank function
\cd\colon\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
assigning to each layer its complex codimension in the ambient torus.
If the toric arrangement is divisorial, then
\mathcal{L}
is a local geometric lattice, and also a
geometric poset
in the sense of
[
Bib22
]
. At the end of the article, we will exhibit an example of divisorial arrangement of subvarietes that is not an abelian arrangement, whose intersection lattice is a local geometric lattice, but not a geometric poset (
Example

8.4
).

Definition 2.3
.

A subset
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{L}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
of a lattice
\mathcal{L}
is a
combinatorial building set
if for any
x\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
and
\max\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
there is an isomorphism of posets:

\phi_{x}\colon\prod_{j=1}^{k}[\hat{0},x_{j}]\rightarrow[\hat{0},x],

defined by

(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})\mapsto y_{1}\vee\cdots\vee y_{k}.

In a ranked lattice
(\mathcal{L},\rk)
, a combinatorial building set is also
geometric
, if furthermore:

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\rk(x_{j})=\rk(x).

The elements of
\max\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
are called the
\mathcal{G}
-factors
of
x
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a semilattice and
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{L}
be a building set.
A subset
S
of
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{G}
-
nested
if, for any set of incomparable elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
in
S
with
t\geq 2
, the join exists and does not belong to
\mathcal{G}
.

We extend the definitions of building and nested sets to local lattices.

Definition 2.5
.

A
combinatorial (geometric) building set
in a (ranked) local lattice
\mathcal{L}
is
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
such that for any
x\in\max(\mathcal{L})
the set
\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
is a combinatorial (geometric) building set in the (ranked) lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\leq x}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A combinatorial building set
\mathcal{G}
is
well-connected
if for any subset
\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}\}
of elements in
\mathcal{G}
, if
|G_{1}\vee\ldots\vee G_{k}|\geq 2
then
G_{1}\vee\ldots\vee G_{k}\subset\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
BD24
]
the authors observe that in the research after the article
[
FK04
]
, maximal building set have been the preferred choice, notably also in
[
AHK18
]
.
In the article, they study the family of all possible building sets of meet-semilattices and their relation with convex geometries.
In particular, they show that the family of building sets of a meet-semilattice forms a lattice and a supersolvable convex geometry
[
BD24
, Theorem 2.3, Theorem 3.2]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a ranked local lattice and
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{L}
be a building set. A
\mathcal{G}
-
nested set
is a pair
(S,x)
, where
S\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and
x\in\vee S
are such that
S
is a
\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
-nested set in
\mathcal{L}_{\leq x}
.

Definition 2.9
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is a ranked local lattice with
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{L}
a building set and
x\in\mathcal{L}
, then the
\mathcal{G}
-
factors
of
x
are the elements in the set

F(\mathcal{G},x)=\max(\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}).

We present an example on how the request of being well-connected may influence the cardinality of a chosen building set:

Example 2.10
.

Consider the following divisorial toric arrangement
A(n,c)
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
depending on a parameter
c\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}
.
Let
A(n,c)
the collection of hypertori given by
H_{1}=\{t_{1}=1\}
and
H_{i}=\{t_{1}t_{i}^{c}=1\}
for
i=2,3,\dots,n
.
The minimal building set in
\mathcal{G}_{\min}=\{H_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
consisting only in the atoms of
\mathcal{L}_{A(n,c)}
and has cardinality
n
.
However, the only well-connected building set is the maximal one; in particular, it has cardinality
\frac{(c+1)^{n}-1}{c}
.
Notice that the growth of the minimal well-connected building set is exponential in
n
and for fixed value
n
is unbounded, although the toric arrangement is already simple normal crossing.

We define a total order
\succ_{G}
on
\mathcal{G}
such that
\succ_{G}
refines the partial order on
\mathcal{G}
induced by
\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 2.11
.

The
\mathcal{G}
-
nested set complex

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
is the
\Delta
-complex whose
k
-faces are the
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets of cardinality
k+1
.
The face maps are defined by
d_{i}((S,x))=(T,y)
where
S=\{S_{0},S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}
are ordered accordingly to
\succ_{\mathcal{G}}
,
T=S\setminus S_{i}
and
y
is the unique element in
\mathcal{L}_{\leq x}
such that
y\in\vee T
.

Recall that a
\Delta
-complex is said to be
regular
, if characteristic maps on its geometric realization are homeomorphism between closed euclidean balls (or
n
-simplices) and the closed cells.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a regular
\Delta
-complex. Its
face poset

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N})
is the poset consisting of the set of closed cells of
\mathcal{N}
, ordered by inclusion.

Definition 2.13
.

Define
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
as the iterated blowup of elements in
\mathcal{G}
in any order refining the partial order on
\mathcal{G}
with the natural projection
\pi=\pi_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
.

The poset
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
does not depend on the choice of the linear order on the building set
\mathcal{G}
, we will prove this fact in
Proposition

2.15
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a locally geometric poset,
\mathcal{G}
a building set of
\mathcal{L}
and
p\in\max(\mathcal{G})
. Then,

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=(\mathcal{G}\setminus\{p\})\cup\{(p,\hat{0})\}
is a building set of
\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}
. Furthermore, the
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested subsets are exactly the
\mathcal{G}
-nested subsets with
p
replaced by
(p,\hat{0})
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one in
[
FK04
]
adapted to our setting of local lattices.
First, let us see that
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is a building set for the blowup
\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}
, which is a local lattice by
Lemma

2.2
. Let
\xi\in\max\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}
. If
\xi\in\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid x\ngeq p\}
then its
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\leq\xi}
-decomposition in
(\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L})_{\leq\xi}
is clearly the same as its
\mathcal{G}_{\leq\pi(\xi)}
-decomposition in
\mathcal{L}_{\leq\pi(\xi)}
. Suppose that
\xi=(p,x,y)
, where
x\ngeq p
and
y\in x\vee p
in the poset
\mathcal{L}
, the following isomorphism of posets holds:

\left[\hat{0},(p,x,y)\right]^{\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}}\simeq[\hat{0},x]^{\mathcal{L}}\times B_{1},

where
B_{1}
is the poset given by the two elements
\{\hat{0}<(p,\hat{0})\}
, hence we are done by the decomposition of
[\hat{0},x]
in
\mathcal{L}
.

Let
(S,x)
with
x\in\bigvee S
be a
\mathcal{G}
-nested set in
\mathcal{L}
, that is,
S
is
\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
-nested set in the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\leq x}
. We want to see that the
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested sets of
\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}
are all and only those of the form
(\tilde{S},\tilde{x})
where

\displaystyle\tilde{S}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}(S\setminus\{p\})\cup\{(p,\hat{0})\}\text{, if }p\in S\\
S\text{, otherwise.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{x}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}((p,\hat{0})\vee\bigvee(S\setminus\{p\}))_{\leq x}\text{, if }p\in S\\
x\text{, otherwise.}\end{cases}

Case 1:

Suppose
(S,x)
nested with
p\notin S
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
be incomparable elements in
S
, for
t\geq 2
. By
\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
-nestedness,
(\bigvee_{i=1}^{t}x_{i})_{\leq x}
exists in
\mathcal{L}_{\leq x}
and does not belong to
\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
. Because
p
is not a factor and it is maximal in
\mathcal{G}
, we have
(\bigvee_{i=1}^{t}x_{i})_{\leq x}\ngeq p
. Thus, the same element exists in
\Bl_{p}(\mathcal{L})_{\leq x}
and does not belong to
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
. We conclude that
(S,x)
is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested in
\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L}
. Conversely, any
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested set
(\tilde{S},\tilde{x})
such that
(\hat{0},p)\notin\tilde{S}
is immediately a
\mathcal{G}
-nested.

Case 2:

Suppose
(S,x)
nested with
p\in S
. We want to see that
(\tilde{S},\tilde{x})
as above is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested. Subsets of incomparable elements not containing
(p,\hat{0})
can be dealt with as in Case 1.
Let
(p,\hat{0}),x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
be incomparable in
\tilde{S}
. As before, we get that
(\bigvee_{i=1}^{t}x_{i})_{\leq x}\ngeq p
.
By nestedness of
(S,x)
, the element
(p\vee\bigvee_{i=1}^{t}x_{i})_{\leq x}
exists in
\mathcal{L}_{\leq x}
and does not belong to
\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
. Therefore, the element

(p,(\bigvee_{i=1}^{t}x_{i})_{\leq x})_{\leq x}=((p,\hat{0})\vee(\bigvee_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}))_{\leq x}

exists in
(\Bl_{p}\mathcal{L})_{\leq\tilde{x}}
and does not belong to
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\leq\tilde{x}}
. We deduce that
\tilde{S}
is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\leq\tilde{x}}
-nested, and hence
(\tilde{S},\tilde{x})
is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested.
An analogous argument shows that if
(\tilde{S},\tilde{x})
is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested, then
(S,x)
is
\mathcal{G}
-nested ∎

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{t}\}
be a building set for
\mathcal{L}
ordered such that
G_{i}>G_{j}
, if
i<j
, that is, ordered by a linear refinement of the opposite partial order on
\mathcal{L}
. Then:

\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{L})=\Bl_{G_{t}}(\Bl_{G_{t-1}}(\dots\Bl_{G_{1}}(\mathcal{L})\dots))\simeq\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})).

Proof.

Again, the proof is adapted from
[
FK04
]
. By
proposition

2.14
, the set

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=\{(G,\hat{0})\colon G\in\mathcal{G}\}

of the transforms of the elements of
\mathcal{G}
is a building set for
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
.
By construction, it is also the set of atoms. Consider an element
x\in\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{L})\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
.
By
Proposition

2.14
,
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\leq x}
is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\leq x}
-nested in
(\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L})_{\leq x}
, and therefore
(\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\leq x},x)
is
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-nested.
Since the interval
[\hat{0},x]=(\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L})_{\leq x}
decomposes as product of rank
1
posets of the form
\{\hat{0}<(G,\hat{0})\}
for
G
belonging to some nested set, we conclude that
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
is the face poset of the nested set complex.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

Every element of
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
corresponds to a nested set of
\mathcal{G}
.
The atoms are exactly the
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets
(\{G\},G)
of cardinality one.

Corollary 2.17
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a building set, then
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
is locally boolean.

Proof.

The face poset of a a regular
\Delta
-complex is locally boolean.
∎

If the building set
\mathcal{G}
is well-connected then
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{L}
is a semilattice.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
and
\mathcal{G}
be a building set. Then, the set

\mathcal{G}_{X}:=\{Z\in\mathcal{L}\mid\exists\,G\in\mathcal{G}_{\not\leq X}\textnormal{ such that }Z\in G\vee X\}

is a building set for
\mathcal{L}_{\geq X}
.
Moreover, let
x\in\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{L})
be a maximal element such that
\pi(x)=X
, that is,
x=(S,X)
is
\mathcal{G}
-nested with
S
maximal, then

\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{L})_{\geq(S,X)}\simeq\Bl_{\mathcal{G}_{X}}(\mathcal{L}_{\geq X}).

Proof.

We first show that
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is building set for
\mathcal{L}_{\geq X}
. Consider
\xi\in\max\mathcal{L}_{\geq X}
, and let
F:=F(\mathcal{G},\xi)
be the set of
\mathcal{G}
-factors of
\xi
in
\mathcal{L}
, then we have the decomposition in the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\leq\xi}
:

[\hat{0},\xi]\simeq\prod_{\eta\in F}[\hat{0},\eta].

In the product, denote with
\overline{X}=(X_{\eta})_{\eta\in F}
the element corresponding to
X
. Let also
F^{\prime}:=F\setminus F(\mathcal{G},X)
, and
F^{\prime\prime}:=\max(\mathcal{G}_{X})_{\leq\xi}
. We want to see that

[X,\xi]\simeq\prod_{\zeta\in F^{\prime\prime}}[X,\zeta].

We have the following isomorphism of posets:

\displaystyle[X,\xi]

\displaystyle=[\hat{0},\xi]_{\geq X}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\prod_{\eta\in F}[\hat{0},\eta]\right)_{\geq\overline{X}}

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{\eta\in F^{\prime}}[X_{\eta},\eta]

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{\eta\in F^{\prime}}[X,\eta\vee X]

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{\zeta\in F^{\prime\prime}}[X,\zeta].

In the last isomorphism we identify the joins
\eta\vee X
with the
\mathcal{G}_{X}
-factors
\zeta
of
\xi
.
Indeed, by definition
\eta\vee X\in(\mathcal{G}_{X})_{\leq\xi}
for
\eta\in F^{\prime}
, and their join equals
\xi
. On the other hand, if one of these elements was not maximal, then the corresponding
\mathcal{G}
-factor
\eta
of
\xi
in
\mathcal{L}_{\leq\xi}
would not be maximal, which is absurd.

Now, we show that the two blowup posets are isomorphic: by
Proposition

2.15
it is enough to construct a bijection between
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets bigger than
(S,X)
in
\Bl_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{L})
, and
\mathcal{G}_{X}
-nested sets in

3.
Generalities on Blowups

We provide a generalization of the lemma by Keel about cohomology of blowups, applicable on the study of the cohomology of wonderful models without the hypothesis of well-connectedness of the building set.

Following
[
DCP95
,
DCG19
]
, we define the Chern polynomial

c_{E}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_{i}(E)t^{d-i}\in H^{*}(X)[t],

where
c_{i}(E)
is the
i
-th Chern class of the vector bundle
E
of rank
d
over
X
.
This convention is the opposite of the one used in literature, e.g.
[
Ful98
]
.

Let
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow Y
be regular embedding of varieties, and consider the blowup
\Bl_{Z}(Y)
of
Y
along
Z
and denote with
\pi\colon\Bl_{Z}(Y)\rightarrow Y
the projection map. Let
E
be the exceptional divisor, and
[Z]
the fundamental class of
Z
in
H^{*}(Y)
.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 1 in the Appendix of
[
Kee92
]
)
.

Suppose that the induced map in cohomology
\iota^{*}\colon H^{\ast}(Y)\rightarrow H^{\ast}(Z)
is surjective with kernel
J
. Then, the map induced by
\pi^{*}
and defined by
-t\mapsto[E]
yields an isomorphism

H^{\ast}(\Bl_{Z}(Y))\simeq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{H^{\ast}(Y)[t]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\left(P(t),t\cdot J\right)}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{H^{\ast}(Y)[t]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\left(P(t),t\cdot J\right)}$}}{\raisebox{2.67209pt}{$\scriptstyle{H^{\ast}(Y)[t]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\left(P(t),t\cdot J\right)}$}}{\raisebox{1.8854pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{H^{\ast}(Y)[t]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\left(P(t),t\cdot J\right)}$}}},

where
P(t)\in H^{\ast}(Y)[t]
is any polynomial such that:

•

its constant term is the cohomology class
[Z]
;

•

its restriction to
H^{\ast}(Z)
is the Chern polynomial of
N_{Z}Y
, the normal bundle to
Z
in
Y
.

We will generalize the above theorem to the case of non-surjective maps
\iota^{*}
.
Consider the algebra
H^{*}(Y)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H^{*}(Z)[t]
and its subalgebra
A
generated by
H^{*}(Y)\otimes 1
and
1\otimes H^{*}(Z)t
.
Let
P_{z}(t)\in A
be the element

(1)

P_{z}(t)=1\otimes zt^{d}+1\otimes zc_{1}(N_{Z}Y)t^{d-1}+\dots+1\otimes zc_{d-1}(N_{Z}Y)t+\iota_{*}(z)\otimes 1

for all
z\in H^{*}(Z)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow Y
be a regular embedding, the map
defined by
y\otimes 1\mapsto\pi^{*}(y)
and by
1\otimes zt^{k}\mapsto-j_{*}p^{*}(z)\cdot(-[E])^{k-1}

yields an isomorphism

H^{*}(\Bl_{Z}(Y))\simeq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.67209pt}{$\scriptstyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.8854pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}}

Proof.

The following proof is analogous to the one in
[
Kee92
, Appendix, Theorem 1]
, but in our case the map
\iota^{*}
not need to be surjective.

Let
E
be the exceptional divisor, from the exact sequence

0\to H^{*}(Y)\to H^{*}(\Bl_{Z}Y)\oplus H^{*}(Z)\to H^{*}(E)\to 0

we have

(2)

H^{\ast}(\Bl_{Z}(Y))\simeq H^{*}(Y)\oplus H^{*}(Z)t\oplus\dots\oplus H^{*}(Z)t^{d-1}\simeq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.67209pt}{$\scriptstyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.8854pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{(P_{z}(t),y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t)}$}}}

as modules and the map
A\to H^{\ast}(\Bl_{Z}(Y))
is surjective.
Therefore, it is enough to verify that the relations
P_{z}(t)
and
y\otimes t-1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t
hold in
H^{*}(\Bl_{Z}(Y))
for any
z\in H^{*}(Z)
and any
y\in H^{*}(Y)
.
Consider the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bl_{Z}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

and using the projection formula we have:

y\otimes t\mapsto\pi^{*}(y)\cdot(-[E])=-\pi^{*}(y)\cdot j_{*}(1)=-j_{*}(j^{*}\pi^{*}(y)\cdot 1),

1\otimes\iota^{*}(y)t\mapsto-j_{*}(p^{*}\iota^{*}(y))=-j_{*}(j^{*}\pi^{*}(y)).

Now, consider the universal quotient bundle
F=p^{*}N_{Z}Y/\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1)
. By multiplicativity of the Chern polynomial on short exact sequences the equality

p^{*}c_{N_{Z}Y}=c_{p^{*}N_{Z}Y}=c_{\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1)}c_{F}
holds,
where
c_{\mathcal{O}_{E}(-1)}(t)=t+j^{*}[E]
.
Hence
c_{i}(p^{*}N_{Z}Y)=c_{i}(F)+c_{i-1}(F)j^{*}[E]
.
Solving the recurrence relations we obtain

c_{d-1}(F)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}p^{*}c_{k}(N_{Z}Y)\cdot j^{*}(-[E])^{d-k-1}.

The element
P_{z}(t)
is mapped to zero because:

\displaystyle P_{z}(t)\mapsto

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}-j_{*}p^{*}(z\cdot c_{k}(N_{Z}Y))\cdot(-[E])^{d-k-1}+\pi^{*}\iota_{*}(z)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}j_{*}(p^{*}z\cdot p^{*}c_{k}(N_{Z}Y)\cdot j^{*}(-[E])^{d-k-1})+\pi^{*}\iota_{*}(z)

\displaystyle=-j_{*}(p^{*}z\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}p^{*}c_{k}(N_{Z}Y)\cdot j^{*}(-[E])^{d-k-1})+\pi^{*}\iota_{*}(z)

\displaystyle=-j_{*}(p^{*}z\cdot c_{d-1}(F))+\pi^{*}\iota_{*}(z)=0.

The last equality follows from the excess intersection theorem

j_{*}(p^{*}z\cdot c_{d-1}(F))=\pi^{*}\iota_{*}(z).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
W,Z
be smooth subvarieties in
Y
and

be the canonical projection. Let
\tilde{W}
be the dominant transform of

and

be the exceptional divisor.
Then:

(1)

If

then
c_{N_{\tilde{W}}\tilde{Y}}(t)=\pi^{*}c_{N_{W}Y}(t-[E])
.

(2)

If the intersection of
Z
and

is transversal then
c_{N_{\tilde{W}}\tilde{Y}}(t)=\pi^{*}c_{N_{W}Y}(t)
.

Proof.

The latter statement follows from

, see

, Lemma 7.1]
.
The first one follows by

and by

for any vector bundle

and any line bundle

, see e.g.
[
Ful98
, Remark 3.2.3 b)]
.
∎

4.
Recollection on toric wonderful models

In this section we recall the contruction of certain toric varieties from
[
DCG18
]
which will be used as compactification for toric wonderful models. We then quickly recall the construction of wonderful models for arrangements of subvarieties in the sense of
[
Li09
]
.
Finally, we provide a suitable compactification for the running example.

Consider a toric arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in the ambient torus
T
. Fix a basis of the character group
X^{\ast}(T)
, and get isomorphisms
X^{\ast}(T)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
X_{\ast}(T)=\Hom(X^{\ast}(T),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}
. It will be useful to recall some more notation: given a layer
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}
of
\mathcal{A}
, define:

•

V:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(X^{\ast}(T),\mathbb{R})=X_{\ast}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}

•

\Ann(\Gamma):=\{v\in V\mid\left\langle\chi,v\right\rangle=0\text{, for all }\chi\in\Gamma\}

In
[
DCG18
]
, De Concini and Gaiffi introduced a wonderful compactification for the complement space of a toric arrangement. They embedded the ambient torus
T
in a
good
toric variety
X_{\Delta}
where
\Delta\subseteq V
with respect to the arrangement
\mathcal{A}
. We now recall the definition of good toric variety, and its main properties.

Definition 4.1
.

Consider a basis
\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{t}\}
of
\Gamma
, and any cone
C=\langle r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\rangle_{\geq 0}
of
\Delta
. We say that the chosen basis has the
equal-sign property
with respect to
C
if
\langle\chi_{i},r_{j}\rangle\geq 0
or
\langle\chi_{i},r_{j}\rangle\leq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,t
and
j=1,\ldots k
. If every layer
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}
of
\mathcal{A}
has has an equal-sign basis with respect to every cone of
\Delta
, we say that the toric variety
X_{\Delta}
is
equal-sign
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
. If
X_{\Delta}
is also a smooth, projective variety, we say that it is a
good
toric variety for
\mathcal{A}
.

De Concini and Gaiffi provided an algorithm in
[
DCG18
]
that, given a complete, smooth toric variety
X_{\Delta}
and a toric arrangement, constructs a good toric variety for
\mathcal{A}
coming from a subdivision of the fan
\Delta
. Every subdivision of the fan corresponds geometrically to a blowup of the closure of a toric orbit of codimension
2
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X_{\Delta}
be a good toric variety for
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}
be a layer, and
T^{\prime}=\bigcap_{\chi\in\Gamma}\ker(\chi)
the subtorus of
T
associated to it. Then, the following facts hold:

(1)

For every cone
C\in\Delta
, its relative interior is either entirely contained in
\Ann(\Gamma)
, or disjoint from it.

(2)

The set of cones of
\Delta
contained in
\Ann(\Gamma)
form a smooth fan with open orbit
T^{\prime}

(3)

The closure of the layer
\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}}
in
X_{\Delta}
is a smooth toric variety whose fan is
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}}:=\Delta\cap\Ann(\Gamma).

(4)

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
T
-orbit of
X_{\Delta}
, and
C_{\mathcal{O}}
be its corresponding cone in
\Delta
. Then:

•

if
C_{\mathcal{O}}\cap\Ann(\Gamma)=\emptyset
, then
\bar{\mathcal{O}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}}=\emptyset

•

if
C_{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\Ann(\Gamma)
, then
\mathcal{O}\cap\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}}
is the
T^{\prime}
-orbit in
\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma,\phi}}
corresponding to the cone
C_{\mathcal{O}}

Essentially, a good toric variety is smooth, projective, and such that the intersection of the closures of the layers in
\mathcal{A}
and of the the toric divisors intersect nicely. That is, such set of intersections forms an
arrangement of subvarieties
in the sense of Li (see
[
Li09
]
). We recall some of Li’s definitions:

Definition 4.3
.

A
simple arrangement of subvarieties
of a nonsingular variety
X
is a finite set
\Lambda
of nonsingular, closed, connected subvarieties properly contained in
X
such that:

•

for all
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\Lambda
,
Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
is either empty, or an element of
\Lambda

•

Whenever
Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}\neq\emptyset
, the intersection is
clean
, i.e. it is nonsingular, and for all
z\in Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
we have the condition on tangent spaces:

\T_{z}(Z_{1}\cap Z_{2})=\T_{z}(Z_{1})\cap\T_{z}(Z_{2})

Definition 4.4
.

An
arrangement of subvarieties
of a nonsingular variety
X
is a finite set
\Lambda
of nonsingular, closed, connected subvarieties properly contained in
X
such that:

•

for all
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\Lambda
,
Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
is either empty, or a disjoint union of elements of
\Lambda

•

Whenever
Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}\neq\emptyset

In our case,

In the last section of the paper, we will analyze some of these intersections after the blowup process. This will be the first step for the definition of a Morgan model for the arrangement.

A wonderful compactification in the sense of Li for the complement space can be constructed by blowing up a in a prescribed order a certain subset

.

Definition 4.5
.

.

be an open set. Denote with

the arrangement

.

Let
\Lambda
be an arrangement of subvarieties in
X
. A subset
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\Lambda
is a
building set
for
\Lambda
, if there is an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X

\Lambda_{U}

•

TGF-β2 increases eHSP90α secretion via upregulating secretory autophagy pathway
Authors: Jing Li, Junmin Li, Tong Xie, Shuxin Huang, Haijiao Tian, Mengyun Liu, Xiumei Kong, Dongzhe Zhang, Khan Iqbal Nawaz, Mingli Wang, Hui Li, Jing Zhang, Fengling Yuan, Weikai Guo, Xiukun Cui, Hongmei Mu, Yanzhong Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExtracellular heat shock protein 90α (eHSP90α) regulates diverse cellular processes such as wound healing, tumor metastasis, angiogenesis and cell differentiation etc. Our previous data show that lens epithelial cells can secret eHSP90α, and administration of eHSP90α exerts a critical regulation for lens regeneration by promoting the differentiation of lens epithelial cells (ECs) to lens fiber cells. Autophagy is proposed to regulate the eHSP90α secretion from lens epithelial cells. However, the regulatory mechanism remains unclear.MethodsLens ECs cell line SRA01/04, ex vivo cultured rat lens capsular bags and primary rat lens ECs were used in this study. The immunoblotting and qPCR were used for measuring the expression of proteins and mRNAs. The immunofluorescence staining assay was for testing the autophagosomes or protein co-localization. siRNA or CRISPR-Cas9 were used to knock down genes’ expression in cells in vitro. The ChIP assay was used to study the interaction of transcriptional factor to the promoter of target genes. The nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction assay was for testing the nuclear translocation of TFEB and TFE3.ResultseHSP90α is secreted through the secretory autophagy pathway in lens epithelial cells in vitro or in the residual anterior ECs of capsular bags ex vivo. HSP90α interacts with adaptor protein TRIM16, and was recruited to the R-SNARE protein SEC22B on the surface of secretory autophagosome membrane for secretion. Silencing autophagic ATG7 or components of secretory autophagy pathways, such as TRIM16, SEC22B and Q-SNARE STX3, STX4 and SNAP23 downregulates HSP90α secretion. TGF-β2 upregulates the eHSP90α secretion via upregulating secretory autophagy pathway. TGF-β2 treatment results in the upregulation of the expression of ATG7, Beclin1, LC3B at both protein and mRNA levels through activating p38 and ERK1/2. In addition, TGF-β2 increases SEC22B expression through ERK1/2-induced expression and nuclear translocation of transcriptional factors TFEB and TFE3, which form the heterodimer binding to the promoter of SEC22B. Functionally, the upregulated eHSP90α can trigger TGF-β2-induced differentiation of lens ECs to fiber cells.ConclusionsIn lens cells, the eHSP90α’s secretion undertakes secretory autophagy pathway through interacting with TRIM16 and SEC22B. TGF-β2 elevates eHSP90α secretion through upregulating secretory autophagy pathway, which in turn promote TGF-β2-induced lens epithelial cells’ differentiation to lens fiber cells.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12964-025-02493-5.

Background

Heat shock protein (HSP) 90 s are a family of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)-dependent chaperones that predominantly protect cellular proteostasis at diverse physio-pathological conditions. Through assisting the refolding of misfolded proteins, protein traffic or protein stability, HSP90 regulates diverse cellular processes involved in embryo development, cell proliferation and differentiation as well as disease development, e.g., tumorigenesis and metastasis, aging-associated neurodegenerative diseases and inflammation, fibrosis, and aging [1–3]. The HSP90 family mainly comprises HSP90α and HSP90β, which exert cytoprotective roles in either homotrimer or heterodimer in a chaperone machine [4]. In addition to regulating intracellular proteostasis, HSP90, especially the HSP90α is secreted extracellularly during certain stress conditions [5], and extracellular HSP90α (eHSP90α) exerts the regulatory effects in either ATP-relied chaperone activity [6, 7] or ATP-independent manner [8–10]. Several secretory pathways have been associated with HSP90α secretion [9, 11, 12], such as autophagy, exosome, and transforming growth factor (TGF)-α. However, the regulatory mechanism underlying eHSP90α secretion regulation is still not completely understood.

eHSP90α participates in regulating multiple cellular processes. For example, eHSP90 is abundant in the microenvironment of tumors [13] and participates in upregulating angiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis [14], as well as tumor cell metastasis and invasion by interacting with and chaperoning vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) or matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP2) and/or collagen-1 [15, 16]. eHSP90α interacts with Clusterin, inducing the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and migration of cancer cells through activating the AKT (serine–threonine kinase), ERK (extracellular regulated protein kinase), and NF-κB (Nuclear factor-κB) signaling pathways [17]. Zhou reported that the expression of eHSP90 and eHSP70 is upregulated in bronchoalveolar lavage fluids followed by thermoplastic, increasing the proliferation of bronchial epithelial cells (ECs) and glucocorticoid receptor expression, reducing the remodeling of airway smooth muscle cells [18, 19]. In addition, eHSP90α is a predominant regulator for wound healing [20]. eHSP90α is upregulated and secreted by keratinocytes during skin wounding and promotes skin wound healing in an ATP-independent manner [21]. This regulation relies on the interaction of F5-fragment within the M-domain of HSP90α with the LRP-1 (low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1) receptor [10]. The eHSP90α is upregulated in corneal epithelial cells and keratinocytes at the injured conditions. eHSP90α binds to LRP-1 on corneal epithelial cells and promotes corneal epithelial cell migration and wound healing [22]. Our previous research has demonstrated that lens epithelial cells (ECs) secrete eHSP90α, which facilitates the differentiation of lens epithelial cells into fiber cells through activating the LRP1-YAP-PROX1 axis, and the results imply that upregulation of eHSP90a is associated with lens regeneration or poster capsular cataracts [9].

The regulatory mechanism of eHSP90α secretion is not fully understood. Tsutsumi et al. report that the highly conserved hydrophobic Ile-x-Leu motif in β-strand 8 at the boundary between the N-domain and charged linker of eHSP90α is critical for its secretion [23]. Luo and colleagues suggest that the EEVD motif at the C-terminal of HSP90 end regulates HSP90α secretion [24]. The EEVD motif, a docking motif of TPR-containing proteins, retains HSP90α inside the cells. Deletion of the EEVD motif facilitates eHSP90α extracellular secretion. In addition, Luo et al. also reveal that the phosphorylation at Thr-90 of HSP90α is a prerequisite for eHSP90α secretion. Protein kinase A (PKA) and protein phosphatase 5 (PP5) are responsive to the phosphorylation or dephosphorylation of Threonine 90 [24]. In addition, the ECM proteins (e.g., gelatin or fibronectin), growth factors (such as TGF-α, bFGF (basic fibroblast growth factor), VEGF, PDGF-BB (platelet-derived growth factor-BB)), and chemokines (SDF-1 (stromal cell-derived factor-1)) are able to upregulate HSP90α secretion [11, 25]. However, the secretory pathway underlying HSP90α has not yet been completely understood. Due to lacking signal peptide, HSP90α cannot undergo secretion through the conventional endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-Golgi secretion pathway. HSP90α exists in the secreted exosomes. As a result, exosomes are considered to be the means of eHSP90α secretion. TGF-α induces eHSP90α secretion via exosomes in skin keratinocytes [11]. Our preliminary data showed that inhibition of autophagy by 3-MA (3-Methyladenine) downregulates HSP90α secretion in lens epithelial cells, which suggests that autophagy is involved in eHSP90α secretion [9]. Autophagy regulates proteins’ secretion in the form of secretory autophagosome. TIRM16 (tripartite motif containing 16) and R-SNARE (soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive-factor attachment protein receptor) protein SEC22B (Sect. 22 homolog B, vesicle trafficking protein), which form the complex on the secretory autophagosome membrane, act as the receptor for transporting the secretory cargos into the secretory autophagosomes [26]. The interaction between the R-SNARE complex and Q-SNARE on the plasmatic membrane leads to secretory autophagosomes to release the cargo outside cells [26]. Accordingly, we propose that secretory autophagosome is involved in regulating eHSP90 secretion in lens epithelial cells.

In this study, we found that HSP90 interacts with TRIM16, and is recruited by TIRM16 to SEC22B-positive autophagosomes. Silencing SEC22B and TRIM16 inhibited eHSP90a secretion. TGF-β2 promoted eHSP90α secretion through upregulating the expression of autophagic protein ATG7 (autophagy related gene 7), Beclin1, MAP1LC3B/LC3B (microtubule associated protein 1 light chain 3B) and SEC22B, but not TRIM16. TGF-β2 upregulated SEC22B expression through activating ERK1/2-induced expression and nuclear translocation of transcription factors EB (TFEB) and transcription factor E3 (TFE3). Functionally, administration of eHSP90α could promote TGF-β2-induced differentiation of lens epithelial cells from myofibroblasts to lens fiber cells. Altogether, these results uncovered a novel regulatory mechanism underlying eHSP90α secretion in lens epithelial cells.

Cell culture

The SRA01/04 cells and 293 T cells were maintained in our lab. All cell lines were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 mg/ml streptomycin in the 37 °C incubator containing 5% CO2.

Animals

Wistar rats were housed at the Henan university animal core. The manipulation of animals is in compliance with the guidelines for the use of animals in ophthalmic and vision research. The protocols for animal feeding, euthanizing and surgery were approved by the ethics committee of Medical College, Henan University.

Ex vivo culture rat capsular bag

For ex vivo capsular bag culture, the rat capsular bags (RCBs) were generated following the protocols presented in our previous paper [9]. Briefly, the eye balls were extracted from 4 to 6 weeks Wistar Rats. The lenses were extracted from the eye ball under the microscope. The whole lenses were embedded in 2% low melting point (LMP) agarose gel with anterior epithelial upside down. The continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis was performed at the posterior capsule, and the lens fiber tissue was completely removed with PBS buffer. The RCBs in LMP-agarose gel were transferred to a 24-well plate in an upside-down position and cultured in non-serum M199 medium supplemented with 1× non-essential amino acids (NEAA), 1× glutaMAX, 100 U/ml penicillin and 100 mg/ml streptomycin. After culturing for 2–3 days, the capsular bags were treated with serum free media containing TGF-β2 at different concentration or other indicated proteins and chemicals. After experiment terminating, the samples were collected for immunoblotting or qPCR.

Primary culture of rat lens epithelial cells (RLECs)

The primary culture of rat lens epithelial cells in vitro was established according to our previous publication [9]. Briefly, the lenses were isolated from 4 to 6 weeks old Wistar rats. The anterior lens capsular bags with anterior lens epithelium were incubated in 0.25% trypsin at 37 °C for 15 min. The epithelial cells that were collected from ten rat capsules, were co-cultured in the cell culture plate containing M199 medium with 20% FBS. The primary RLECs were amplified and the cells less than 5 passages were used for experiments.

Immunoblotting and Immunofluorescence assay

For immunoblotting, the cells or tissues were lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, 1% Triton X-100, 1% Sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS, pH 7.4) containing a complete protease inhibitor cocktail (Thermo Fisher Scientific, A32963) and phosphatase inhibitor (Thermo Fisher Scientific, A32957). The equal amounts of proteins were separated in 10% sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) via electrophoresis and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Merk/Millipore, IPVH00010). After blocking in 5% fat-free milk (Solarbio Life Science, D8340) for 1 h, the membranes were incubated with primary antibody overnight at 4 °C followed by incubating with the horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. The membranes were developed in chemiluminescence buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 34580) and signals were observed by automatic chemiluminescence (5200 Multi, Tanon, China).

For immunofluorescence assay, the SRA01/04 cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA). After washing with PBST, the cells were blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) (Solarbio Life Science, A8020) for 1 h followed by incubating with the primary antibody overnight at 4 °C. after washing with PBST, the cells were then incubated with the Alexa Fluro-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h. The nuclei were counter-stained with DAPI (4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole) (Sigma, D9542). The fluorescent signals were photographed with a confocal microscope (R1, Nikon, Japan).

Immunoprecipitation assay

The SRA01/04 cells/WT, SRA01/04/siRNA-TRIM16 or SRA01/04/siRNA-SEC22B cells were treated with PBS or TGF-β2 for 4 h. The cells were lysed in RIPA buffer. One milligram of protein was first precleaned with agarose-protein A beads and then incubated with the rabbit anti-SEC22B or rabbit anti-TRIM16 antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After this, the Protein A/G agarose beads (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-2003) were added the above protein lysates and continued to incubated at RT for 2 h. The rabbit IgG isotype control (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 31235) was used as a control. The beads were pelleted and washed in RIPA buffer for 3–4 times. The co-precipitated protein was applied to immunoblot.

Trichloroacetic acid (TCA)-Acetone protein precipitation

The supernatants from cultured cell lines or rat CBs were collected and centrifuged at 3000 rpm/min for 10 min to remove cell debris. 100 µl of TCA (100%, w/v) was added to 1 ml supernatant followed by rotating at 4 °C for 1 h. The proteins were pelleted by centrifugating at 18,000×g for 15 min at 4 °C. The pellets were washed twice with 1 ml ice-cold acetone and air dried at room temperature. The pellets were resuspended with 1× SDS-PAGE buffer and subjected to immunoblotting.

Recombinant HSP90α expression and purification

The competent E. coli BL21 (DE3) that were transformed with the plasmids of pGEX-6P-1 and pGEX-6P-1-Hsp90α. To induce the protein expression, the recombinant bacteria were cultured at OD600 = 0.6 in LB media. The 0.1 mM IPTG (Solarbio Life Science, 18070) was added to media and continue culture at 30 °C for 5 h. The GST and GST-Hsp90α proteins were purified by using GSTrap FF following the protocol provided with kit (GE Healthcare, 17513002). The proteins were concentrated with Ultracel centrifugal filter units (Merck/Millipore, UFC910096 and UFC9003). The contaminated LPS in the purified proteins were removed by incubated with Triton X-114 (Merck/Sigma, 93422). The quality of purified recombinant proteins was quantitated with BCA kit and analyzed in Coomassie blue staining.

Isolation of microvesicles

The microvesicles in the SRA 01/04 cells, which were treated with PBS or 10 ng/ml of TGF-β2 for 4 h, were isolated by using the centrifugation. Briefly, the SRA01/04 cells were harvested in B1 buffer (20 mM HEPES-KOH [Solarbio Life Science, H8090], pH7.2, 400 mM sucrose [Solarbio Life Science, S8271], 1 mM EDTA [Solarbio Life Science, E8040] and 0.3 mM DTT [Solarbio Life Science, D8220]) with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. The cells were homogenized by passing through a 22 G needle and then analyzed by trypan blue staining till approximate 85% lysis. The cell lysis was sequential centrifuged at 3,000 g for 10 min, 25,000 g for 20 min and 100,000 g for 30 min in ultracentrifuge (CP100NX, Himac, Japan). The pellets of each pellet were collected and resuspended in the B88 buffer (20 mM HEPES-KOH, pH 7.2, 150 mM potassium acetate [Solarbio Life Science, K8150], 5 mM magnesium acetate [Sangon Biotech, A610326], 250 mM sorbitol [Solarbio Life Science, S8090]) and applied to immunoblot.

Proteinase K protection assay

The 25 K-fractioned microvesicles were resuspended in 40 µl B88 buffer or in 40 µl B88 buffer containing 300 ng proteinase K with or without 0.5% Triton X-100. The reaction was performed at 37 °C for 30 min and stopped by adding loading buffer and heated at 70 °C for 10 min. The reaction was applied for immunoblot.

Quantitative RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from SRA01/04 cells or rat CB by RNAiso plus reagent (9109) (Takara Bio; Osaka, Japan). 1–3 µg total RNA was used to synthesize the first strand of cDNA following the kit’s protocol (K1622) (Thermo Fisher Scientific; Waltham, USA). Quantitative PCR was performed using a SYBR green kit (4472919) (Thermo Fisher Scientific; Waltham, USA) on qPCR machine (ABI 7500 fast system). The primers were listed in Table S1. β-actin was used as the internal control. The data was presented as fold change with respected to the controls (mean ± SD). The data were from three independent experiments.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay

The ChIP assay was performed according to the protocol with kit (Merck/Millipore, 17295). Briefly, 4 × 106 cells were cross-linked with 1% formaldehyde for 20 min at 37 °C, and the stopped by adding 1 mM glycine. The cells were lysed in SDS lysis buffer followed by sonicating to break genomic DNA into 200 to 2000 bp fragments. the equal volume of cell lysates was incubated with 1–2 ug of antibodies against TFEB or TFE3 respectively at 4 °C overnight. After this, 40 µl of 50% protein-A/G-agarose-beads solution were added to proteins lysates and incubated for another 2 h. The beads were then pelleted by centrifugation and washed in series buffers for 5 times. The co-precipitated DNA-protein complex was dissociated at 65 °C for 4 h. The DNA fragments were extracted with Chloroform followed by ethanol precipitation. The purified DNA fragments were subjected to PCR with primer pairs covering region flanking the TFEB/TFE3 binding motifs in the promoter of SEC22B. The primers for human SEC22B promotor were listed in Table S2.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) and CRIPSR-Cas9 DNA editing

For knocking down genes, SRA01/04 cells were grown in 35 mm dishes overnight and transfected with scramble siRNA or siRNAs of target genes at a concentration of 50 nM/well by using Lipofectamine RNAi/MAX (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 13778030). The targeted sequences of siRNA were listed in Table S3.

For knockout SEC22b in SRA01/04 cells, the CRISPR-Cas9 system was used. With the protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) underlined, the sequences of guide RNA targeted sites were: gRNA 1, 5’-GCTAACAATGATCGCCCGAGTGG−3’ and gRNA 2, 5’-TTCGTCCTCCTGCATCGAGGCGG−3’.

Nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction assay

The SRA01/04 cells were seeded in 60 mm dish overnight followed by incubating in serum-free media for 12 h. The cells were then added PBS (sham), TGF-β2 (10 ng/ml) or TGF-β2 (10ng/ml) + U0126 (1 µM) for 4 h. After this, the cells were harvested and subjected to nucleus and cytosol fraction assay by using NE-PER nuclear and cytoplasmic extraction kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 78833). The nuclear and cytoplasmic extracts were applied for immunoblotting.

Statistical analysis

Image J was used for protein density quantification of the immunoblot bands and fluorescent signals. GraphPad Prism 8 was used for data plot and statistical analysis. Unpaired student’s t-test was performed for two different groups’ statistical analysis. Ordinary one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s multiple comparisons was used for statistical analysis of three or more different groups with one variable. Two-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s or Tukey’s multiple comparisons was for analysis of three or more different groups with two variables. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 and ***p < 0.001).

Results

Secretory autophagy participates in TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α secretion

Our previous data indicated that lens epithelial cells secreted eHSP90α, which then promotes the differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fiber cells, a critical process during posterior capsular opacification (PCO) or lens regeneration [9]. However, the regulatory mechanism underlying the eHSP90 secretory remains unclear. TGF-β2 is a dominant growth factor that triggers the transdifferentiation of lens epithelial cells to myofibroblasts or lens fiber cells during PCO formation [27]. We therefore proposed that TGF-β2 might participate in regulating eHSP90α secretion. To test this, we treated the human lens epithelial cells SRA01/04 with PBS (sham) or TGF-β2 at the concentration of 0.1, 1 and10 ng/ml for 6 h (Fig. 1A) or 10 ng/ml of TGF-β2 for different time (Fig. 1C). The upregulation of phospho-Smad2/3 indicated the activation of TGF-β2 signal pathway (Fig. 1A and C). The immunoblot and densitometry quantitation results showed that TGF-β2 increased the supernatant eHSP90α protein levels in a dose- (Figs. 1A-B) and a time-course-dependent manner (Figs. 1C-D), and this increase is suppressed by SB431542, an inhibitor of TβR1/ALK5 (Fig. 1A). TGF-β2 did not impact the intracellular HSP90α protein expression (Fig. 1A and C, low panels). To validate this regulatory effect, we treated the cultured primary rat lens epithelial cells (RLECs) in vitro and rat capsular bags (RCBs) cultured ex vivo with 10 ng/ml of TGF-β2 or TGF-β2 plus SB431542 for 4 and 6 h respectively. The results showed that TGF-β2 could significantly induce eHSP90α secretion in both RLECs and RCBs models, and this regulatory effect was inhibited by SB431542 (Figs. 1E-H), which is consistent with the results in Fig. 1A and C. The cell death-released eHSP90α of SRA01/04 cells, primary RLECs and RCBs were excluded by measuring LDH activity in ELISA (Fig. S1A-D). Our previous results indicated that inhibition of autophagy by 3-MA reduced the secretion of eHSP90α in lens epithelial cells, suggesting that autophagy is associated with eHSP90α secretion [9]. Autophagy regulates protein homeostasis by protein degradation in lysosomes or secretory autophagy. Kiyono et al. reported that TGF-β1 upregulated autophagy in hepatocyte carcinoma cells [28]. We then postulated that TGF-β2 might upregulate eHSP90α secretion via autophagy secretion pathway. To test it, the SRA01/04 cells were treated with TGF-β2 or TGF-β2 together with autophagic inhibitors 3-MA or Baf-A1 (Figs. 1I-J). The results showed that 3-MA, which inhibit autophagic efflux represented by the accumulation of SQSTM1/p62 and downregulation of MAP1LC3B/LC3II expression, inhibited TGF-β2-induced secretion of eHSP90α (Figs. 1I-J), In contrast, Baf-A1, an inhibitor of lysosome, showed slightly inhibitory effect on eHSP90α secretion (Fig. 1I). The possibility of cell death-released eHSP90α was excluded by ELISA measuring LDH activity (Fig. S1E). Those results suggested that the autophagy pathway participated in TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α secretion. To validate the above results, the autophagy was inhibited in SRA01/04 cells by overexpressing siRNA against ATG7, and the cells were then treated with PBS (sham) or 10 ng/ml TGFβ2 for 4 h (Fig. 1K). The scramble siRNA was used as a control. Similar to the regulatory effects of 3-MA, silencing ATG7 by siRNA reduced the eHSP90α secretion at both control and TGF-β2-treated conditions (Figs. 1K, lanes 3–6, and 1 L). Since the silencing ATG7 did not impact TGF-β2-induced phosphorylation of Smad2/3 (Fig. 1K), we proposed that autophagy did not participate in signal transduction of the TGF-β2-Smad pathway, and Smad pathway seems not involved in TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α extracellular secretion. Interestingly, the expression of ATG7 protein was increased by TGF-β2 (Fig. 1K, lanes 7 and 8), suggesting that TGF-β2 can upregulate autophagy formation in lens epithelial cells. The ELSIA results of LDH excluded the possibility of the cell death-released eHSP90α in the supernatants (Fig. S1F). These results suggested that autophagy is closely associated with TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α secretion in lens residual epithelial cells.

TGF-β2 upregulates the expression of autophagy-related proteins via activating mapkinases

In the results of Fig. 1K, TGF-β2 shows the upregulation of autophagic ATG7, which suggests that TGF-β2 is involved in regulating the autophagy process. We confirmed this regulation in SRA01/04 cells and in the ex vivo cultured RCBs. In SRA01/04 cells, we treated the cells with 10 ng/ml TGF-β2 for 1, 2, 4, 8 and 24 h. The results showed that TGF-β2 upregulated the expression of Beclin1, ATG7, and MAP1LC3B/LC3B at both protein and mRNA levels (Figs. 2A-C), and reduced SQSTM1/p62 protein expression (Figs. 2A-B). In contrast, TGF-β2 did not show any regulation on ATG5 expression. These results indicated TGF-β2 could upregulate autophagy efflux by possibly upregulating above autophagic proteins. The upregulation of phosphorylation of Smad2/3 indicated that TGF-β2-TβR1 pathway works well (Fig. 2A). To confirm this regulation in vivo, we treated ex vivo cultured RCBs with serum-free media containing 10 ng/ml TGF-β2 or TGF-β2 together with SB431542 for 4 h (Figs. 2D-E). Like the results in Fig. 2A, TGF-β2 showed an up-regulatory effect on the expression of Beclin1, ATG7, and MAP1L3B/LC3B, but not ATG5, and this upregulation was inhibited by SB431542 (Figs. 2D-E). Altogether, these results indicated that TGF-β2 can upregulate autophagy via at least increasing the expression of ATG7, Beclin1 and MAP1LC3B/LC3B.

TGF-β2 exerts its regulatory effect through the signal pathways of Smad or MAPKinases, such as p38 or ERK [29]. To determine which one is involved in TGF-β2-activated autophagy. We first analyzed the canonical TGF-β2-Smad signaling pathway by using Smad3 inhibitor SIS3. The results showed that unlike SB431542 (inhibitor of TβR1/ALK5), SIS3, which could efficiently inhibit TGF-β2-induced phosphorylation of Smad3, did not show any regulatory effect on TGF-β2-induced expression of Beclin1, ATG7, and the MAP1LC3B/LC3B, and SQSTM1/p62 protein downregulation (Fig. S2A, lanes 5, 6 and 8, and S2B). Moreover, the inhibition of the TGF-β2-Smad2/3 pathway by SIS3 did not impact eHSP90α secretion (Fig. S2A, lanes 1–4, and S2C). In contrast, the secretion of fibronectin (FN), which is upregulated by TGF-β-Smad2/3 signaling, was inhibited by SIS3 (Fig. S2A), and the results are consistent with previous reports [9]. The possibility of cell death-released eHSP90α was excluded by ELISA measuring LDH activity (Fig. S2D). These results suggested that TGF-β2-activated autophagy is not through the canonical Smad signal pathway.

Next, we analyzed the regulatory effect of MAPkinases on TGF-β2-induced autophagy activation in SRA01/04 cells. We treated the cells with 10 ng/ml TGF-β2 alone or TGF-β2 combined with SB203580 (p38 inhibitor), U0126 (ERK1/2 inhibitor), LY294002 (AKT inhibitor) or SB431542. The results showed that the TGF-β2-induced expression of Beclin1, ATG7 and MAP1LLC3B/LC3B at both mRNA and protein levels were inhibited by U0126 and SB203580. As a consequence, the TGF-β2-induced downexpression of SQSTM1/p62 was restored (Fig. 2F-G and I). The inhibitory effect of U0126 or SB203580 on TGF-β2-induced phosphor-ERK1/2 or phosphor-p38 was indicated (Fig. 2F). Remarkably, both U0126 and SB203580 could efficiently reduce the TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α secretion (Fig. 2F and H). In contrast, inhibition of AKT activity by LY294002 showed little impact on autophagic proteins’ expression and eHSP90α secretion regardless of TGF-β2 treatment (Figs. 2F-H). The possible cell death-released eHSP90α under such circumstances was excluded by measuring LDH levels in ELISA (Fig. S2E). Altogether, these data showed that TGF-β2 upregulated autophagy and eHSP90α secretion through activating p38 and/or ERK1/2 pathways.

The R-SNARE SEC22B is a downstream effector TGF-β2 in the induction of eHSP90α secretion

Autophagy regulates protein secretion through secretory autophagosomes, which differs from degradation autophagosomes by containing the TRIM16-R-SNARE SEC22B complex, which works as a receptor for the cargo secretion, e.g., IL-1β [26]. In contrast, the autophagosomes that have undergone lysosome degradation contain STX (syntaxin) 17, an adaptor to mediate the fusion of the autophagosome with the lysosome [30]. The results in Fig. 2 indicate that autophagy is involved in HSP90α secretion. We then studied the regulatory role of secretory autophagosomes on eHSP90α secretion. We isolated three sets of cytosolic microvesicles from SRA01/04 cells that were treated with serum-free media containing PBS (sham) or 10 ng/ml TGF-β2 for 4 h by using ultracentrifugation at 3,000 g (3 K), 25,000 g (25 K) and 100,000 g (100 K) respectively (Fig. 3A). The immunoblot results showed that at the control conditions, the autophagosome-associated biomarkers, such as MAP1LC3B/LC3B-II, SEC22B, TRIM16 and STX17, were detected mainly in the 25 K microvesicles, less in the 3 K microvesicles, but not the 100 K microvesicles (Fig. 3B, lanes 1–3), which suggested that the autophagosomes including secretory autophagosomes and autophagosome for degradation were mainly in the 25 K-fractioned microvesicles. As expected, HSP90α protein was positive in all three sets of microvesicles examined, but with the higher amount of HSP90α in the 25 K microvesicles (Fig. 3B, lanes 1–3). Upon TGF-β2 treatment, the expression of SEC22B, MAP1LC3B/LC3B and HSP90α, but not the TRIM16 and STX17 were upregulated in the 25 K microvesicles as compared to that at control condition (Figures. 3B, lane 5 and 3 C). TGF-β2 did not increase the expression of those autophagosome biomarkers in 3 K and 100 K microvesicles (Fig. 3B, lanes 4 and 6). Protein disulfide isomerase (PDI), which expresses in microsomes, was used as a protein loading control (Fig. 3B). These results suggested that HSP90α proteins exist in autophagosomes, and TGF-β2 increases autophagosomes including the HSP90α- and SEC22B-positive secretory autophagosomes. The induction of secretory autophagosome by TGF-β2 was also confirmed in immunofluorescent assay, in which TGF-β2 can significantly increase SEC22B expression and its colocalization to LC3B particles in the cytosol (Fig. S3). To determine the location of HSP90α in autophagosomes, we treated the 25 K microvesicles with proteinase K with or without Triton X-100 (Fig. 3D). The results showed that without Triton X-100, Proteinase K did not digest HSP90α protein in the 25 K microvesicles, but it could completely digest SEC22B, TRIM16 and STX17. In contrast, the HSP90α, SEC22B and TRIM16 proteins in 25 K microvesicles were completely digested by proteinase K in presence of Triton X-100 (Fig. 3D). These results suggested that most of HSP90α protein existed inside the autophagosomes, while SEC22B, TRIM16 and STX17 proteins were outside of autophagosomes, which is consistent with previous reports [26]. Next, we studied the regulatory effect of SEC22B, TRIM16 and STX17 on the eHSP90α secretion. We silenced the expression of SEC2B, TRIM16 and STX17 by siRNA in SRA01/04 cells followed by TGF-β2 treatment. The immunoblot results showed that silencing SEC22B or TRIM16 reduced eHSP90α secretion regardless of TGF-β2 treatment (Figs. 3E-H), while silencing STX17 increased TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α secretion (Fig. S4A, lanes 3–6, and S4B). The cell death-induced eHSP90α secretion under this circumstance was excluded by measuring LDH levels (Fig. S4C-E). The inhibition of SEC22B, TRIM16 and STX17 by siRNA did not impact the expression of endogenous HSP90α and TGF-β2-induced expression of ATG7, LC3B, and reduction of SQSTM1/p62 protein (Figs. 3E-F and Fig. S4A). The above results indicated that the SEC22B-TRIM16-positive secretory autophagosomes participated in TGF-β2-induced eHSP90α secretion. The reason for the induction of eHSP90α secretion by silencing STX17 is unclear. Since STX17 is a fusion mediator of autophagosome and lysosome for cargos’ degradation, we thought that silencing STX17 might facilitate the conversion of the lysosomal autophagosome to the secretory autophagosomes for proteostasis [31], which makes eHSP90α secretion increased. To validate the regulatory effect of SEC22B on eHSP90α secretion, we knocked out SEC22B gene by using the CRISPR-Cas9 system (Fig. 3I), and the results showed that eHSP90α secretion was much reduced in SEC22B-KO cells compared to that in wild-type cells (Fig. 3I), which is consistent with the results in Fig. 3E. The possible cell death-released eHSP90α was excluded by measuring LDH levels (Fig. S4F). To determine whether SEC22B acts as a receptor for HSP90α access into the secretory autophagosomes, we isolated the 25 K-fractioned microvesicles from SRA01/04 cells vs. SRA01/04/SEC22B-KO cells. The immunoblot results showed that no HSP90α protein was detected in the 25 K microvesicles of SEC22B-KO cells compared to that of wild-type cells (Fig. 3J). In contrast, knocked out SEC22B did not impact the expression of HSP90α in the 3 K and the 100 K microvesicles (Fig. 3J). TRIM16 interacts with and recruits HSP90α to SEC22B positive autophagaosomes

Previous results showed that silencing both SEC22B and TRIM16 downregulated the secretion of eHSP90α (Figs. 3E-H). Deletion of SEC22B reduced eHSP90α access into the secretory autophagosomes (Fig. 3J). To determine the intermolecular association among eHSP90α, SCE22B, and TRIM16, we conducted an immunoprecipitation assay, and the results showed that HSP90α and TRIM16 were co-immunoprecipitated by the anti-SEC22B antibody in SRA01/04 cells (Fig. 4A, lane 1). Similarly, HSP90α and SEC22B were co-immunoprecipitated by the antibody against TRIM16 (Fig. 4B, lane 1). The immunofluorescent results showed that TGF-β2 could upregulate SEC22B and LC3B expression, and the colocalization between HSP90α and SEC22B compared to PBS control (Figs. 4C-D). These results indicated that HSP90α, TRIM16 and SEC22B can form a complex, and TGF-β2 elevates this complex formation via increasing SEC22B expression, and increases the secretory autophagosomes. Next, we studied how HSP90α interacts with TRIM16 and SEC22B. We tested the interaction of HSP90α to SEC22B and TRIM16 in the SRA01/04 cells/WT, the SEC22B-silenced or the TRIM16-silenced cells that were treated with PBS (sham) or TGF-β2 by using the immunoprecipitation. Consistent with previous results, HSP90α, SEC22B and TRIM16 were co-immunoprecipitated by antibodies to SEC22B or TRIM16 in wild-type cells (Figs. 4E-F, lane 1), and TGF-β2 elevated this interaction (Figs. 4E-F, lane 4). However, HSP90α protein was not co-immunoprecipitated by anti-SEC22B antibody in both SEC22B-silenced and TRIM16-silenced cells (Fig. 4E, lanes 2 and 3). In contrast, HSP90α was co-immunoprecipitated by anti-TRIM16 antibody in wild-type cells and SEC22B-silenced cells but not in TRIM16-silenced cells (Fig. 4F, compared lanes 1 and 2 to lane 3). These results suggested that there is no direct interaction between SEC22B and HSP90α. TRIM16 acts as an adaptor linking the interaction between HSP90α and SEC22B. The interaction pattern was further confirmed by the results of the immunofluorescent assay. The colocalization between HSP90α and SEC22B was much reduced in TRIM16-silenced cells compared to that in wild-type cells. (Figs. 4G-H). TGF-β2 showed slight upregulation of the interaction between HSP90a and TRIM16 in wild-type and SEC22B-silenced cells (Fig. 4F). The upregulation of SEC22B protein by TGF-β2 was observed again (Figs. 4E-F, lanes 10 and 12). Accordingly, these data suggested that TRIM16 is an adaptor that recruits HSP90α to SEC22B-positive autophagosomes for secretion. TGF-β2 increased the complex formation of HSP90α, SEC22B and TRIM16 by increasing SEC22B expression.

The secretory autophagosomes deliver cargo out of cells via the fusion of R-SNARE to the Q-SNARE complex on the cytoplasmic membrane [26]. To determine that HSP90 secretion undergoes this pathway, we then studied the regulatory effect of Q-SNARE on HSP90α secretion by knocking down STX3, STX4 and SNAP (synaptosome associated protein) 23, three components of the Q-SNARE complex. Extracellular HSP90α facilitates TGF-β2-induced differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fiber cells

Our previous data showed that administration of GST-HSP90α protein could promote the differentiation of lens residual epithelial cells to lens fibers in the ex vivo cultured RCBs [9]. TGF-β2 exerts a dual regulatory effect on lens epithelial cells; it promotes lens epithelial cell to undergo trans-differentiation to myofibroblasts or differentiation to lens fiber cells [27]. We thought that the induction of eHSP90α by TGF-β2 might contribute to TGF-β2-induced differentiation of lens epithelial cell to lens fibers. To test this, the rat lens capsular bags (RCBs) were treated with GST, GST-HSP90α, TGF-β2 + GST, TGF-β2 + recombinant GST-HSP90α, or TGF-β2 + GST-HSP90α + RAP (receptor-associated protein, inhibitor of LRP-1 which is the receptor of eHSP90α) for 36 h (Fig. 8A). The immunoblot and quantitation results showed that compared to GST alone, the TGF-β2 + GST downregulated E-cadherin, and simultaneously upregulated α-SMA (α-smooth muscle actin) and BFSP2 (beaded filament structural protein 2) proteins (biomarker of lens fiber cells) (Figs. 8A, lane 4; 8B-C), indicating TGF-β2’s dual regulatory effect on lens epithelial cells. In contrast, administration of GST-HSP90α alone also downregulated E-cadherin expression, and simultaneously upregulated the expression of differential markers of lens fiber cells, such as PROX1 (prospero-related homeobox 1), BFSP2, CRYBB2 (crystallin beta B2), but not α-SMA (Figs. 8A, lane 3; 8B-C), indicating that eHSP90α triggers the differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fibers, which is consistent with previous data [9]. When the RCBs were treated with TGF-β2 and GST-HSP90α, the induction of α-SMA by TGF-β2 was downregulated, while the lens fiber marker PROX1, BFSP2 and CRYBB2 were upregulated compared to that treated with TGF-β2 alone (Figs. 8A, lanes 4 and 5; 8B-C). These results suggested that eHSP90α can facilitate TGF-β2-induced differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fibers. To confirm this, we added RAP protein to the cultured RCBs that were treated with TGF-β2 + GST-HSP90α. RAP is an inhibitor of LRP-1, an LDL receptor that can bind to eHSP90α and transduce the differentiation signals to lens epithelial cells. The results showed that RAP reduced the expression of PROX1, BFSP2 and CRYBB2, but increase α-SMA expression in the TGF-β2 + GST-HSP90α-treated cells. Discussion

The extracellular HSP90 proteins regulates the diverse cellular processes, such as cell migration, fibrosis, proliferation and differentiation at both physiological and pathological conditions [5, 20, 33]. Our previous data show that eHSP90α participates in lens regeneration by promoting the differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fibers [9]. In this paper, as illustrated in the graphical summary (Fig. 9), we uncover that lens epithelial cells can autocrine eHSP90α through secretory autophagy, and TGF-β2 increases eHSP90α secretion through upregulating the secretory autophagy. The cytosolic HSP90α protein can interact with TRIM16, and is recruited to the SEC22B-positive secretory autophagosomes for secretion. TGF-β2 upregulates the secretory autophagy by upregulating the expression of autophagic proteins of Beclin1, ATG7 and LC3B, and the R-SNARE protein SEC22B at both mRNA and protein levels through distinct signal pathways. The MAPkinases of p38 and ERK1/2 is involved in TGF-β2-induced Beclin1, ATG7 and LC3B expression; while TGF-β2 induces the transcriptional expression of SEC22B via ERK1/2-mediated upregulation of transcriptional factors TFEB and TFE3, which can form heterodimer, binding to the promoter of SEC22B. As a consequence, the upregulated eHSP90α protein participates in promoting the TGF-β2-induced lens epithelial cells differentiation to lens fiber cells. This is the first time to clarify the regulatory effect of secretion autophagy on eHSP90α secretion.Fig. 9Graphical summary of the study. The several pathways have been found to be involved in regulating eHSP90α secretion. For example, the exosomes are reported to regulate eHSP90α secretion because HSP90α exists in the secreted exosomes [34]. The inhibition of exosome secretion can reduce eHSP90α secretion in skin keratinocytes, lung epithelial cells and cancer cells, but this inhibition could not completely suppress eHSP90α secretion [11], which suggest there are other pathway involved in eHSP90α secretion. Our data indicated that secretory autophagosomes is a novel pathway that regulates eHSP90α secretion. However, our data could not exclude the potential regulation of exosomes on eHSP90α secretion in lens epithelial cells because SEC22B deletion, which can efficiently block HSP90α’s access into the 25 K microvesicles, could not completely inhibit eHSP90α secretion, and moreover, SEC22B deletion does not impact the HSP90α existence in 3 K and 100 K microvesicles, which may indirectly support that there are other microvesicles involved in regulating eHSP90α secretion, such as exosomes. How the cells select the pathway to secret eHSP90α is still not clear. It may depend on cell types and stress condition or posttranslational modification. The previous data reported that HSPP90α secretion is regulated by its EEVD motif, hydrophobic Ile-x-Leu motif, and phosphorylation of Thr90 [23, 24]. Whether these critical amino acids affect HSP90α access to autophagosome or other vesicles are still under investigation.

The secretory autophagy has been reported to regulate the proteins’ secretion without signal peptide, such as IL-1β, IL-6, etc [26, 35]. The R-SNARE protein SEC22B and adaptor protein TRIM16 are the receptor on the secretory autophagosomes for cargo secretion, such as IL-1β [26, 36]. The TRIM16 protein binds to IL-1β and recruit IL-1β to the R-SNARE SEC22B protein anchored on the secretory autophagosome membrane, which allow IL-1β access into secretory autophagosome and mediate fusion of the secretory autophagosomes and the cytosolic membrane by interacting with Q-SNARE complex [37]. The secretory autophagy is the fundamental mechanism for the secretion of leaderless cytosolic proteins, aggregated proteins, HMGB1, Galectins, etc [26, 38]. HSP90α is a leadless cytosolic protein. Like IL-1β, HSP90α interacts with TRIM16 and is recruited to SEC22B on the autophagosomes (Fig. 4). SEC22B deletion blocks the transportation of cytosolic HSP90α into secretory autophagosomes and eHSP90α secretion (Figs. 3 and 4), which indicate the SEC22B exerts a dual function, it acts as a receptor of HSP90α to access into secretory autophagosome, and simultaneously mediate the membrane fusion of secretory autophagosomes to cytosolic membrane. In addition to its secretory autophagy, SEC22B also participates in transportation of protein and lipid from ER to Golgi, phagosomal maturation and antigen crosspresentation by dendritic cells, and fusion-independent function, such as cellular membrane extension [39]. HSP90α participates in antigen presentation by interacting with peptides [40]. Since there is no direct interaction between HSP90α and SEC22B, we think that HSP90α does not regulate SEC22B-mediated antigen presentation.

Previous reports indicate that TGF-α can upregulate eHSP90α secretion via exosomes, which accelerates the skin wound healing at control and diabetic conditions [11]. The eHSP90α accomplishes its job through binding to LRP-1-mediated activation of AKT1 and AKT2 [11]. In the lens epithelial cell model, we find that TGF-β2, a critical growth factor that regulates lens development at physiological conditions and the PCO formation post-cataract surgery, upregulates HSP90α secretion via secretory autophagy pathways. Unlike TGF-α, TGF-β2 can upregulate HSP90α secretion by increasing the formation of phagophore and secretory autophagosomes through upregulating the expression of Beclin1, ATG7 and MAP1LC3B/LC3B, and the R-SNARE SEC22B at both mRNA and protein levels. Silencing ATG7 or SEC22B by siRNA can significantly reduce eHSP90α secretion at both control and TGF-β2-treated conditions (Figs. 2 and 3). These results indicate the secretory autophagosomes are the predominant pathway of eHSP90α secretion in lens epithelial cells, and this conclusion is also supported by results of knocking down of the expression of STX3, STX4 and SNAP23, the components of Q-SNARE complex that is responsive for the fusion of the secretory autophagosome to the cytosolic membrane (Fig. 5). Moreover, we define that MAPkinases p38 and ERK1/2 are downstream of TGF-β2 to upregulate the Beclin-1, ATG7 and MAP1LC3B/LC3B (Figs. 2F-G), and ERK1/2 is the critical downstream for TGF-β2-induced SEC22B expression (Figs. 7D-E). Remarkably, our data showed that the transcriptional factors TFEB and TFE3, which regulate the expression of autophagic and lysosome proteins, is downstream of TGF-β2 to upregulate SEC22B mRNA expression. TGF-β2 upregulates TFEB and TFE3 expression and nuclear translocation via activating ERK1/2 (Fig. 7). Since U0126 reduces the TGF-β2 -ERK1/2-mediated nuclear translocation of TFEB and TFE3, we propose that the increase of nuclear translocation is associated with the high expression of TFEB and TFE3, but not the dephosphorylation. According to our data, we propose that TGF-β2 upregulates secretion of HSP90α by upregulating the secretory autophagosomes.

TGF-β2 exerts a dual regulation on lens epithelial cells. It can drive lens epithelial cells transdifferentiation into myofibroblasts, and simultaneously it can promote lens epithelial cells differentiation to lens fibers. These dual regulatory effects are regulated by Smad and mTOR [27]. Our previous data showed that administration of GST-HSP90α can trigger the differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fibers via activating the LRP-1-AKT-YAP-PROX1 pathways [9]. Because TGF-β2 shows the upregulation of eHSP90α secretion. We then proposed that eHSP90α may be an effector of TGF-β2 to regulate the differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fiber. The result in Fig. 8 confirmed this proposal, in which GST-HSP90α can downregulated TGF-β2-induced α-SMA, but restore the TGF-β2-reduced expression of BFSP2. To make more supporting evidence, we tried to neutralized the eHSP90α by using antibodies in TGF-β2 treated RCBs, but the experiment fails due to lacking of HSP90α antibodies with neutralization activity. More experiments are necessary to determine the regulatory effect of eHSP90 on TGF-β2-induced differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fiber cells.

Conclusions

Using lens epithelial cell model and RCBs culture ex vivo, we identify that the secretory autophagy is fundamental mechanism to regulate eHSP90α secretion in lens epithelial cells. TGF-β2 promotes eHSP90α secretion by upregulating the expression of autophagic proteins and R-SNARE complex SEC22B. The eHSP90α protein participates in regulating the differentiation of lens epithelial cells to lens fibers.

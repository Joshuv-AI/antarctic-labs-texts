Optimized Power Control for Multi-User Integrated Sensing and Edge AI
Biao Dong and Bin Cao

Abstract
This work investigates an integrated sensing and edge artificial intelligence (ISEA) system, where multiple devices first transmit probing signals for target sensing and then offload locally extracted features to the access point (AP) via analog over-the-air computation (AirComp) for collaborative inference. To characterize the relationship between AirComp error and inference performance, two proxies are established: the computation-optimal proxy that minimizes the aggregation distortion, and the decision-optimal proxy that maximizes the inter-class separability, respectively. Optimal transceiver designs in terms of closed-form power allocation are derived for both time-division multiplexing (TDM) and frequency-division multiplexing (FDM) settings, revealing threshold-based and dual-decomposition structures, respectively. Experimental results validate the theoretical findings.

Introduction

To support latency-sensitive applications such as smart factories, digital twins, and the low-altitude economy, the sixth generation (6G) of wireless networks is expected to extend its functional scope by integrating sensing capabilities, thereby giving rise to the paradigm of integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)

From the computational perspective, intelligent computation is evolving toward a task-oriented paradigm, where the computation is directly aligned with specific tasks, such as classification and detection. This paradigm requires edge artificial intelligence (AI) to enable collaborative computation, and is therefore referred to as integrated sensing and edge AI (ISEA)

An efficient ISEA system relies on two key aspects: system-level optimization and sensing-level enhancement

IV
Transceiver Design under the TDM Setting

Building on the two aforementioned proxies, we derive the optimal transceiver designs for both computation-optimal and decision-optimal cases under the TDM setting.

IV-A

Computation-Optimal Case

Under the computation-optimal case, the optimization problem can be formulated as

(\mathrm{P}1)\min_{\{b_{k,t},a_{t}\}_{t=1}^{t=M}}\quad\sum_{t=1}^{M}\mathrm{MSE}_{t},\quad\text{s.t.}\eqref{eq:power_constraint}.

Since the channel is slow fading (i.e.,
h_{k,t}=h_{k}
,

,

, and

(\mathrm{P}2)\min_{b_{k},a}\quad\mathrm{MSE}_{t},\quad\text{s.t.}|b_{k}|^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}\leq P_{k}.

(\mathrm{P}2)
has an optimal solution, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 3

(
[
9
,
10
]
)
.

Assuming
P_{1}|h_{1}|\leq\cdots P_{k}|h_{k}|\leq\cdots P_{K}|h_{K}|

|b_{k}^{\star}|=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sqrt{P}_{k}}{|\nu_{k}|},&1\leq k\leq k^{\star},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{|a^{\star}||h_{k}|},&k^{\star}<k\leq K,\end{cases}

where

Remark 1
.

By comparing (
19

17

The underlying reason is that, in the TDM scheme, the temporal degrees of freedom do not provide additional gain, since all slots experience the same quasi-static channel realization. In this regard, irrespective of the chosen proxy, the resulting solution exhibits the same structural form.

We notice that the discriminative importance is heterogeneous across feature dimensions. It is therefore desirable for the channel degrees of freedom to support corresponding multiplexing, enabling non-uniform resource allocation aligned with feature importance.

V-A

Different from

Due to the frequency-selective channel, a joint power constraint across all subcarriers should be considered. The corresponding optimization problem can be formulated as

(\mathrm{P}6)\min_{\{b_{k,n},a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{n=M}}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{M}\mathrm{MSE}_{n},\quad\text{s.t.}\eqref{eq:power_constraint}.

(\mathrm{P}6)
is non-convex due to the coupling between
b_{k,n}
and
a_{n}
. Fortunately, it can be verified that
(\mathrm{P}6)
satisfies the time-sharing condition
[
20
]
, and thus the duality gap becomes negligible when the number of subcarriers is large. Next,
(\mathrm{P}6)
can be optimally solved using the Lagrange duality method. The Lagrangian and its dual function of
(\mathrm{P}6)
are respectively given by

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{P}6}=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\left[\Phi_{n}(a_{n},{b_{k,n}};\bm{\lambda})\right]-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}P_{k},

(23)

\!\!g(\bm{\lambda})\!\!=\!\!\!\!\!\inf_{{a_{n}},{b_{k,n}}}\!\!\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{P}6}\!=\!\!\!\sum_{n=1}^{M}\underbrace{\!\inf_{a_{n},{b_{k,n}}}\Phi_{n}(a_{n},{b_{k,n}};\bm{\lambda})}_{\psi_{n}(\bm{\lambda})}\!-\!\!\!\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\lambda_{k}P_{k},

(24)

where
\Phi_{n}(a_{n},{b_{k,n}};\bm{\lambda})\triangleq\sigma_{n}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}|1-a_{n}h_{k,n}b_{k,n}|^{2}+|a_{n}|^{2}\sigma_{w}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}|b_{k,n}|^{2}
and
\bm{\lambda}=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{K}]^{T}\succeq\mathbf{0}
collects the dual variables corresponding to the individual power constraints of the users.
For a fixed
a_{n}

\Phi_{n}

b_{k,n}
yields

|b_{k,n}^{\star}(\lambda_{k},a_{n})|=\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}|a_{n}h_{k,n}|}{\sigma_{n}^{2}|a_{n}|^{2}|h_{k,n}|^{2}+\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}}.

(25)

Substituting (

\!\!\!\sigma_{n}^{2}|\!1\!\!-\!a_{n}h_{k,n}b_{k,n}^{\star}|^{2}\!+\!\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}|b_{k,n}^{\star}|^{2}\!\!=\!\!\frac{\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\!\sigma_{n}^{2}|a_{n}|^{2}|h_{k,n}|^{2}\!\!\!+\!\!\!\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}\!}.

(26)

Hence, the dual term for each subcarrier is

\!\psi_{n}(\bm{\lambda})\!=\!\min_{a_{n}}\!\!\underbrace{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\!\frac{\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\sigma_{n}^{2}|a_{n}|^{2}|h_{k,n}|^{2}+\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}}\!+\!\!|a_{n}|^{2}\sigma_{w}^{2}}_{\phi_{n}(|a_{n}|^{2};\bm{\lambda})}.

(27)

Let
r_{n}=|a_{n}|^{2}\geq 0

\phi_{n}(r_{n};\bm{\lambda})
w.r.t.

(28)

which has a unique positive root
r_{n}^{\star}(\bm{\lambda})

g(\bm{\lambda})=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\phi_{n}\hbox{$\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}r_{n}^{\star}(\bm{\lambda});\bm{\lambda}\hbox{$\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}-\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\lambda_{k}P_{\mathsf{c}}.

(29)

Decision-Optimal Case

For the decision-optimal case, the objective is to maximize the MD of the received features across all subcarriers. The corresponding optimization problem can be formulated as

(\mathrm{P}7)\min_{\{b_{k,n},a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{n=M}}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{M}\operatorname{G}_{\operatorname{min}}({\hat{y}}_{n}),\quad\text{s.t.}\eqref{eq:power_constraint}.

(\mathrm{P}7)
is also non-convex due to the coupling between
{b_{k,n}}
across users and subcarriers. Similar to
(\mathrm{P}6)
,
(\mathrm{P}7)
satisfies the time-sharing condition, and can thus be solved using the Lagrange duality method.
Following the same procedure as in (
25

28

|b_{k,n}^{\star}|=\frac{|\Delta_{n}|^{2}h_{k,n}z_{n}}{\lambda_{k}\nu_{k,n}^{2}+|\Delta_{n}|^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}h_{k,n}^{2}z_{n}^{2}},

VI
Numerical Results

In this section, numerical results are presented to validate the analytical results. Besides the computation-optimal and decision-optimal cases, two baselines are considered: (1)
Equal Allocation
, with uniform power allocation across all subcarriers, and (2)
Channel Inversion
, with
|b_{k,n}|=\{\sqrt{\frac{P_{k}}{N\nu_{k,n}^{2}}},\ \frac{1}{|h_{k,n}|}\}
. We adopt a support vector machine (SVM) as the edge AI model, which is trained on noise-free PCA features extracted from a human posture recognition dataset
[
6
]
.
The task is formulated as a four-class human posture classification problem. Key system parameters are configured as follows:
\sigma_{\mathsf{r}}^{2}=\sigma_{w}^{2}=0.1
,
K=3
,
P\in[-10,30]
dB,
M=4
, and
N=32
.

In Fig.
2
(a), the inference performance is evaluated across a range of signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) levels. Three key observations can be made. First, at extremely low SNR levels, the classification performance of all schemes degrades to random guessing, i.e.,
\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}
. Second, at high SNR levels, all schemes except
Channel Inversion
approach the saturation upper bound. This is because
Channel Inversion
attempts to compensate all subcarriers, but the total power constraint across all subcarriers limits its effectiveness. Third, in the moderate-SNR regime (i.e.,
[0,15]
dB), the computation-optimal scheme yields superior inference accuracy, benefiting from discriminant-aware and non-uniform resource allocation across feature dimensions.

In Fig.
2
(b), the inference performance is evaluated under varying numbers of users
K
. The results validate Theorem
2
, showing that with sufficient independent observations in aggregation, the class uncertainty decreases accordingly. Moreover, as
K
increases, all schemes converge to a common performance upper bound. This is because all schemes optimize each user independently, and no power coupling exists among users.

In Fig.
2
(c), the inference performance under different numbers of subcarriers
N
is presented. The performance gap between the decision-optimal scheme and the other schemes gradually narrows as
N
increases. This is because the frequency-domain heterogeneity of the channel diminishes when
N
becomes large, causing all schemes to approach the same time-sharing bound. However, a constant performance gap remains, since the decision-optimal design allocates more power to discriminative subcarriers with larger
|\Delta_{\min}|^{2}/\sigma_{n}^{2}
, whereas the computation-optimal design follows variance-based regularization.

VII
Conclusion

This paper investigated a multi-user ISEA system enabled by AirComp. Two theoretical proxies were introduced: the computation-optimal and decision-optimal proxies. Optimal transceiver designs in terms of closed-form power allocation were derived for both TDM and FDM settings, revealing threshold-based and dual-decomposition structures that jointly balance feature importance, channel conditions, and power budgets. Results show that the computation-optimal and decision-optimal designs coincide under quasi-static channels but diverge under frequency-selective conditions, where discriminant-aware allocation yields additional inference gains.

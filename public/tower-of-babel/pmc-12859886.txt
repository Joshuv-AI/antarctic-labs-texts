Efficacy observation of oblique lateral interbody fusion (OLIF) in treating severe spinal stenosis
Authors: Yiliya Yilihamu, Jun Mo, Zhanjun Ma, Jianjiang Li, Yifei Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and feasibility of oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) in the treatment of severe lumbar spinal stenosis.MethodsRetrospectively collected 83 patients diagnosed with severe lumbar spinal stenosis and underwent surgical treatment (OLIF or modified TLIF) in our hospital from January 2022 to June 2024. Routine preoperative X-ray, CT, and MRI examinations were performed. Based on the surgical method, the two groups were divided as follows: Group A: modified transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF), Group B: Oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF). Data including age, gender, BMI, preoperative and postoperative VAS scores, ODI scores, differences in spinal canal cross-sectional area (mm²), differences in intervertebral disc height (mm²), and differences in intervertebral foramen area (mm²) were collected from both groups and analyzed.ResultsTwo groups of patients were compared in terms of age, gender, BMI index, preoperative VAS score, and preoperative ODI index, and no statistically significant differences were found (P > 0.05), indicating comparability. No significant difference was found in the comparison of postoperative VAS scores between the two groups (P > 0.05), suggesting that the efficacy of the two groups was comparable; there were statistically significant differences in operation time and postoperative ODI index (P < 0.05), indicating that Group B had a shorter operation time and better postoperative recovery. There were statistically significant differences in the comparison of the difference in vertebral canal cross-sectional area and the difference in intervertebral foramen area (P < 0.05), suggesting that although the vertebral canal cross-sectional area of Group B was effectively increased, Group B was inferior to Group A in improving vertebral canal cross-sectional area.ConclusionOLIF technique can be used to treat severe lumbar spinal stenosis, and it is safe, minimally invasive, and effective. Although the improvement in the cross-sectional area of the spinal canal and intervertebral foramen in group B was not as significant as in group A, its postoperative functional recovery was better than that of group A. In cases of spinal stenosis with spondylolisthesis, the OLIF technique often results in a more significant expansion of the spinal canal, but attention should be paid to the possibility of the superior articular process tip compressing the exiting nerve root after reduction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03466-8.

Introduction

Lumbar spinal stenosis (LSSS) is the most common cause of low back pain, radiating leg pain, and difficulty walking in elderly patients [1]. LSSS results from various causes leading to narrowing of the central canal, neural foramina, lateral recesses, and other areas, which in turn causes symptoms such as low back and leg pain and difficulty walking [2]. Oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) is a minimally invasive surgical technique used to treat LSSS. It indirectly decompresses the intervertebral disc height, stretches the ligamentous tissues, and thereby aims to increase the volume of the spinal canal [3]. OLIF has the advantages of minimal damage and rapid recovery and is widely used to treat lumbar spinal canal stenosis, lumbar spondylolisthesis, and other degenerative lumbar diseases. OLIF can significantly increase the volume of the spinal canal in cases of mild to moderate lumbar spinal canal stenosis through indirect decompression; however, it is generally considered unsuitable for severe lumbar spinal canal stenosis, as indirect decompression is deemed insufficient to improve severe spinal canal stenosis [4]. Recently, there have been reports of the use of OLIF to treat severe lumbar spinal canal stenosis, but there is still controversy regarding its application in such cases [5]. We retrospectively analyzed patients with lumbar spinal stenosis who underwent OLIF procedures at our hospital from January 2022 to June 2024, and analyzed the efficacy and safety of OLIF surgery in patients with severe lumbar spinal stenosis.

Methods

Study subjects

Retrospective collection of 83 patients diagnosed with lumbar spinal stenosis and undergoing surgery in our hospital from January 2022 to June 2024. Preoperative routine X-ray, CT, and MRI examinations were performed, and the patients were divided into two groups based on different surgical methods: Group A: Modified transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF), Group B: Oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF).

Inclusion criteria

(1) Definite diagnosis of lumbar spinal stenosis (vertebral canal cross-sectional area ≤ 75mm2); (2) Patient age between 40 and 80 years old, and the lesion segment is L3-L4 or L4-L5; (3) No history of abdominal surgery; (4) Ineffectiveness of conservative treatment for > 3 months; (5) Complete preoperative examinations such as MRI, X-ray, and CT; (6) Sign informed consent, agreeing to the surgical treatment plan.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Merged diseases such as spinal tumors, spinal tuberculosis, fractures, etc., not degenerative diseases; (2) Patients considered unsuitable for OLIF or TLIF procedures preoperatively; (3) Patients with spinal lumbar sacralization or sacral lumbarization; (4) Patients with a history of surgery at the L3-4 or L4-5 segments; (5) Patients with cognitive dysfunction, unable to cooperate; (6) Patients with severe heart, liver, and kidney dysfunction, unable to tolerate surgery.

Treatment standards

Oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) surgery team

After the patient is intubated under general anesthesia, adopt a right lateral decubitus position, and determine the surgical gap (for example, at the L4-5 segment) and standard anteroposterior and lateral views using fluoroscopy. Mark the surface projection of the anterior edge of the L4 vertebra, and make a diagonal surgical incision along the projection, about 4 cm long; incise the subcutaneous tissue and fascia, and bluntly separate the abdominal muscles (external oblique muscle, internal oblique muscle, and transversus abdominis) layer by layer with a curved forceps. Use two periosteal elevators to separate the fat behind the peritoneum anteriorly and gradually expose the psoas major muscle. Gently retract the peritoneum and other soft tissues with a 110° angled retractor to the abdominal side;Under direct visualization, use a 75° angled retractor to pull the psoas major muscle toward the back, fully exposing the surgical space and confirming in direct visualization that there are no other structures in the operative area. After re-verification with fluoroscopy to determine the correct surgical vertebral interspace, insert a guidewire, place a step-up cannula, establish the working channel, and fix the screw. Use a sharp scalpel to open the annulus fibrosus, use nucleus clamp and cavity clamp to remove the nucleus pulposus, and use different size templates and reamers to process the vertebral interspace. Use a bone chisel to clean the cartilaginous endplate and breach the contralateral annulus fibrosus. Select an appropriate sized fusion cage based on the template and implant it into the vertebral interspace. Fluoroscopy confirms the proper position of the fusion cage. Irrigate with large amounts of saline and explore to ensure no active bleeding. Suture the external oblique muscle and subcutaneous layer separately with absorbable sutures, and perform skin-to-skin sutures, applying medical adhesive to close the wound.2. Position the patient in the prone position and use the "G" arm fluoroscopy to locate the standard AP and lateral views of the surgical vertebra, as well as the surface projections of the bilateral pedicles. Make a longitudinal incision of about 1.5 cm outside the surface projections of the pedicles. Under the monitoring of the "G" arm, puncture along both sides of the pedicles, insert the guidewires, and then remove the puncture needles. Place 4 pedicle screws under the guidance of the guidewires, insert the connecting rods bilaterally, and fix and lock them with 4 screws. Rinse extensively with saline and explore to ensure there is no significant active bleeding. Suture the deep fascia and subcutaneous tissue layer by layer with absorbable sutures, and use subcuticular sutures for the skin. Secure the wound with medical adhesive.

Modified transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) surgery group

After anesthesia takes effect, the patient is placed in a prone position, and the surgical interval is determined visually (taking the L4-5 segment as an example) as well as in standard anteroposterior and lateral positions; a surgical incision about 3 cm in length is made 1 cm beside one spinous process, cutting through the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and deep fascia, gradually exposing the vertebral lamina, articular process, and transverse process junction for dissection. Use vertebral lamina retractors to pull apart the muscle tissue to fully expose the decompression side vertebral lamina and articular process, resect the lower articular process on the decompression side and part of the upper articular process, expose the hypertrophied and thickened yellow ligament and resect it to expose the dura mater; use nerve root retractors to shield the dura mater, and decompress the intervertebral space and lateral recess; use nucleus pulposus forceps to remove the nucleus pulposus tissue inside the intervertebral disc, and treat the intervertebral space and cartilage endplate; use trial models to select an appropriate size of the fusion device, flush the interval until no obvious active bleeding is observed; mix the excised bone tissue with homologous allograft bone, implant part into the fusion device for reserve, and the remaining bone granules are all implanted into the intervertebral space; insert the fusion device into the intervertebral space, and the position is good under fluoroscopy; remove the vertebral lamina retractors, and under G-arm fluoroscopy, implant pedicle screws on the decompression side; locate the position of the non-decompression side pedicle in the same manner, and under G-arm fluoroscopy, puncture along the pedicle, remove the needle after inserting the guide wire; under the guidance of the guide wire, insert 2 pedicle screws; install the connecting rod and screw cap on both sides, apply pressure to fix, flush again and no obvious active bleeding is found, implant one rubber drain tube on the decompression side, suture the incision in layers, and use medical adhesive to adhere the skin wound.

(Interbody fusion device provided by Guangdong Staples Medical Technology Co., Ltd.; Pedicle screws are provided by Guangdong Staples Medical Technology Co., Ltd.; bone repair material (rhBMP-2) provided by Hangzhou Jiuyuan Genetic Engineering Co., Ltd.; Bone granules are provided by Tianjin Zhongjin Biological Development Co., Ltd.)

Observation criteria

Collect general patient information: age, gender, body mass index (BMI), etc. Collect preoperative and postoperative VAS scores, ODI index, vertebral canal cross-sectional area (mm2), intervertebral disc height (mm2), intervertebral foramen area (mm2), and other data. All follow-ups are conducted by the same group of physicians.

Vertebral canal cross-sectional area difference (mm2) = postoperative vertebral spinal canal cross-sectional area - preoperative vertebral spinal canal cross-sectional area; the measurement method is to measure the size of the vertebral canal cross-sectional area on the MRI cross-sectional plane.

The difference in intervertebral disc height (mm2) = postoperative intervertebral disc height - preoperative intervertebral disc height; the measurement method is to measure the height of the intervertebral space on the lumbar lateral X-ray film, using the anterior, middle, and posterior edges of the vertebral body for measurement, and then calculating the average value.

The difference in intervertebral foramen area (mm2) = postoperative intervertebral foramen area - preoperative intervertebral foramen area; the measurement method is to measure the area of the intervertebral foramen by using the MRI sagittal section.

All data are measured and collected through our hospital’s RSVS system (Version 8.2.W-V); the time for postoperative follow-up imaging data is one week after surgery.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed via SPSS 26.0, with P < 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Continuous data with a normal distribution are presented as ‾x ± s values, and intergroup comparisons were conducted via t tests; categorical data are presented as frequencies (%), and intergroup comparisons were performed via chi-square tests.

Results

Both groups of patients successfully completed their surgeries without any misplacement or severe neurologic complications. In the group with severe stenosis, one patient experienced compression of an exit nerve by the zygapophysial joint postoperatively and was treated with endoscopic surgery to remove the hypertrophic superior zygapophysial joint, which improved their symptoms.

Discussion

Lumbar spinal stenosis is a common degenerative disease of the lumbar spine that significantly impacts the physical health of middle-aged and elderly patients and can even lead to disability [6]. Lee classified lumbar spinal stenosis into no or mild, moderate, and severe stenosis; the severity of stenosis is associated with a patient’s disability or neurological dysfunction [7]. Lumbar spinal stenosis can often be improved with conservative treatment, but in some cases, surgical treatment is necessary, especially for severe cases [8]. There are multiple surgical options for treating lumbar spinal stenosis, but current guidelines do not recommend the optimal surgical approach [9]. The OLIF (oblique lumbar interbody fusion) technique has recently shown good efficacy in treating lumbar spinal stenosis, but for patients with severe spinal stenosis, there is a risk of inadequate decompression with OLIF, potentially leading to poor outcomes [10]. Owing to anterior approach decompression combined with posterior internal fixation in the OLIF technique, early scholars used staged surgery to prevent the potential insufficiency of indirect decompression, allowing for further direct decompression during posterior internal fixation to achieve good outcomes [11]. Recently, as the indications for OLIF have expanded, some scholars have achieved good results by performing OLIF with anterior and posterior treatment in a single stage for lumbar spinal stenosis [12]. Our research also found that after OLIF surgery in patients with severe lumbar spinal stenosis, the cross-sectional area of the spinal canal, intervertebral space height, and intervertebral foramen area were all effectively expanded; the postoperative VAS score and ODI index were significantly improved, and the ODI improvement was even better. Through the study, we believe that the OLIF procedure can improve the cross-sectional area of the spinal canal and clinical symptoms in patients with severe lumbar spinal stenosis. Although the cross-sectional area of the spinal canal improved more in the modified TILF group after surgery, due to greater injury, the functional recovery after surgery was actually worse than in the OLIF group. The indirect decompression of the spinal canal cross-sectional area in the OLIF group was not as good as that in the modified TILF group, which may also be the concern of some scholars about the application of OLIF in severe spinal stenosis.

Severe lumbar spinal canal stenosis results in a smaller spinal canal volume than mild to moderate stenosis does and may be accompanied by nerve root redundancy, making surgery more difficult [13]. Lumbar spinal canal stenosis is often caused by direct compression from conditions such as intervertebral disc herniation, hypertrophy of the yellow ligament, and inward angulation of the facet joints due to degeneration. There is a concern that using OLIF (oblique lumbar interbody fusion) for indirect decompression in severe lumbar spinal canal stenosis may result in inadequate postoperative decompression and suboptimal outcomes [14]. Patients with severe lumbar spinal stenosis who undergo OLIF surgery have an increase in the spinal spinal canal cross-sectional area, which is related to vertebral body height recovery; an increase in intervertebral height is accompanied by an increase in foramen height and area. A comparison of OLIF and MISTLIF in degenerative lumbar spinal stenosis patients also revealed that the effective increase in and maintenance of the spinal canal and foramen areas in the OLIF group are closely related to the increase in intervertebral height [15]. A decrease in intervertebral height may lead to renarrowing of the spinal canal, making the maintenance of postoperative intervertebral height one of the key factors in ensuring surgical efficacy [16]. Our research findings show that both Group A and Group B can increase the intervertebral space height while also increasing the area of the spinal canal and intervertebral foramen, etc. Group B mainly relies on indirect decompression, with the increase in spinal canal cross-sectional area entirely dependent on the increase in intervertebral space height; Group A mainly relies on direct decompression, which also requires the removal of posterior structures along with the decompressive material. However, direct decompression is more focused on a certain type of stenosis; for example, TILF is better for decompression of central spinal canal and lateral recess stenosis, while percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy is more effective for improving intervertebral foramen stenosis. Although OLIF can increase the area of the spinal canal, intervertebral foramen, and lateral recesses simultaneously, preoperative careful evaluation of imaging data of patients with severe lumbar spinal canal stenosis is necessary. For those with severe hypertrophy or fusion of articular processes, insignificant decrease in intervertebral space height, bony stenosis, or signs of fusion on one side of the intervertebral space, caution should be exercised in choosing the OLIF surgical method, or consider adopting a staged surgical approach. For patients with severe lumbar spinal canal stenosis undergoing OLIF, there is often a small increase in intervertebral space height, resulting in a minimal increase in intraspinal area; adopting a staged surgical approach can both make the surgery more minimally invasive and allow for appropriate remediation during posterior surgery.

Spondylolisthesis, with or without intervertebral protrusion, can lead to severe lumbar spinal stenosis. The anatomical changes in patients with spinal stenosis associated with spondylolisthesis are distinctly different from those in patients without spondylolisthesis [17]. Spondylolisthesis-induced spinal stenosis can be effectively treated by inserting a large fusion cage via the OLIF approach, which not only increases intervertebral height but also corrects vertebral slippage, thereby significantly improving spinal stenosis [18]. Further follow-up studies have shown that the spinal canal cross-sectional area may further expand postfusion due to atrophy of the yellow ligament [19]. Spondylolisthesis results in vertebral slippage, leading to enlargement and anterior migration of the lumbar facet joints [20]. Our research revealed that the superior articular processes, especially the superior articular processes, move relatively forward during reduction, thereby compressing the exiting nerve roots and causing symptoms such as radiating pain in the lower limb. Therefore, for patients with spondylolisthesis and severe superior articular facet hypertrophy, it is advisable to use a higher fusion cage to allow the superior articular processes to move both forward and downward, positioning the tip of the superior articular processes below the nerve root rather than behind it. In our study, there were 30 patients in the OLIF group with dislocation; the difference in the spinal spinal canal cross-sectional area was greater than that in those without spondylolisthesis. Additionally, one patient experienced compression of the exiting nerve by the superior articular facet postoperatively, and symptoms improved after endoscopic surgery to remove the hypertrophic superior articular process. Therefore, for lumbar spinal stenosis patients with spondylolisthesis, it is important to pay attention to the condition of superior articular facet hypertrophy and assess preoperatively whether the tip of the superior articular processes compresses the exiting nerve roots after reduction.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective study, it may be subject to selection bias. Second, the smaller sample size could result in estimation errors, which may compromise the credibility of the findings. Third, the shorter follow-up period limits the availability of data on long-term efficacy and complications. To validate the effectiveness and feasibility of the OLIF approach, larger sample sizes and multicenter clinical studies are necessary.

Summary

In summary, the OLIF technique can be used to treat severe lumbar spinal stenosis, and it is safe with good efficacy; restoring and maintaining intervertebral disc height is an important factor in expanding the intraspinal area. Although the improvement in lumbar spinal canal cross-sectional area and intervertebral foramen area in patients with severe lumbar spinal stenosis is not as significant as in the modified TLIF group, their symptom improvement is slightly better than that of the modified TLIF group. In patients with lumbar spinal stenosis associated with spondylolisthesis, the OLIF technique often results in a more significant expansion of the spinal canal, but attention should be paid to the possibility of compression of the exiting nerve root by the superior articular process tip after reduction.

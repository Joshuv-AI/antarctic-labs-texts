Author's reply to “POTS: A neuroimmune disorder with cardiovascular manifestations”
Authors: Sthira Rao, Xiao Liu, Noel Bairey Merz, Peng-Sheng Chen
Published: 2026

We would like to thank Blitshteyn for taking the time to read our article1 and provide her comments. Although our study1 lacked a healthy control group, a previous study2 from our laboratory included 19 healthy controls and demonstrated ST-segment depression in response to elevated sympathetic tone, measured by skin sympathetic nerve activity. That finding is consistent with the results of the present study. The clinical implications of our study would be further strengthened if we could determine the relationship between ST height and average skin sympathetic nerve activity in patients with J-wave syndrome, which is associated with nocturnal ST-segment elevation, ventricular arrhythmias, and sudden death.

Blitshteyn3 presents possible mechanistic explanations for this phenomenon, including small-fiber neuropathy, brain and brainstem abnormalities, and immunologic and autoimmune perturbations. Interestingly, they also propose the classification of postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS) as a neuroimmune disorder.4 Their suggestion that the pathophysiology of POTS has a neuroimmune etiology with downstream effects on cardiovascular regulation has merit, given the multisystemic manifestations of the disorder. We also appreciate that they mention studies demonstrating central nervous system disorders, such as alterations in the glymphatic system, in POTS.4,5 The glymphatic system is a perivascular cerebrospinal fluid–interstitial fluid exchange pathway that supports brain homeostasis by clearing cellular debris and soluble antigens.6 Glymphatic system dysfunction is commonly observed in central nervous system diseases,7 while cerebrospinal fluid production is subjected to autonomic control.8 It is possible that patients with dysautonomia, such as POTS, may suffer from glymphatic system dysfunction, which aggravates the symptoms. More work in this area may help develop novel therapies for POTS. We thank Blitshteyn for providing these insightful comments.3

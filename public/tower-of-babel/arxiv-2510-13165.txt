The local well-posedness, global existence and ill-posedness for the fifth order Camassa-Holm model
Xiaoxin Chen, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the fifth order Camassa-Holm model. Firstly, we improve the local well-posedness results in [ 34 , 38 ] . Secondly, we give the blow up criteria and conditions for global existence. Finally, when b = 5 3 b=\frac{5}{3} in the model, we obtain the ill-posedness in B ∞ , 1 1 B^{1}_{\infty,1} and B 2 , q 3 2 B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,q} with q ∈ ( 1 , + ∞ ] q\in(1,+\infty] in the sense of norm inflation. Keywords : The fifth order Camassa-Holm model; Local well-posedness; Global existence; Ill-posedness.

Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the Cauchy problem for the following fifth order Camassa-Holm (FOCH) equation
[
29
]
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m_{t}+um_{x}+bu_{x}m=0,&t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
m=(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{xx})(1-\beta^{2}\partial_{xx})u,&t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
m|_{t=0}=m_{0},&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta,b\in\mathbb{R},\ \alpha\beta\neq 0
. If we denote
P(D)=(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{xx})^{-1}(1-\beta^{2}\partial_{xx})^{-1}
, then (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}+uu_{x}+F(u)=0,&t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
F(u)=F_{1}(u)+F_{2}(u)+F_{3}(u)+F_{4}(u)
with

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F_{1}(u)=\frac{b}{2}\partial_{x}P(D)\left(u^{2}\right),&F_{2}(u)=\frac{3-b}{2}(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})\partial_{x}P(D)\left(u_{x}^{2}\right),\\
F_{3}(u)=\frac{5-3b}{2}\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}\partial_{x}P(D)\left(u_{xx}^{2}\right),&F_{4}(u)=\frac{b-5}{2}\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)\left(u_{x}^{2}\right).\end{array}\right.

,

E_{p,r}^{s}(T)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{C}([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s-1})\quad\text{if}\quad r<\infty,\\
\mathcal{C}_{w}(0,T;B_{p,\infty}^{s})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0,1}([0,T];B_{p,\infty}^{s-1})\quad\text{if}\quad r=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Firstly, we show the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in B_{p,r}^{s}
with
1\leq p,r\leq\infty,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}s>\max\{\frac{5}{2},2+\frac{1}{p}\}\quad\text{if}\quad b\neq\frac{5}{3},\\
s>\max\{\frac{3}{2},1+\frac{1}{p}\}\quad\text{if}\quad b=\frac{5}{3}.\end{array}\right.

There exists a time
T>0
such that (
1.2
) has a unique solution
u\in E_{p,r}^{s}(T)
. If
r<\infty\ (resp.,\ r=\infty)
, then the solution map is continuous from
B^{s}_{p,r}
to
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)\ (resp.,\ \mathcal{C}_{w}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,\infty}))
.

Proof.

Now we prove Theorem
3.2
in three steps.

Step 1: Existence

Set
u^{0}\triangleq 0
and define a sequence
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of smooth functions by solving the following linear transport equation

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}u^{n+1}+u^{n}\partial_{x}u^{n+1}+F(u^{n})=0,\\
u^{n+1}|_{t=0}=S_{n+1}u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Since
u^{0}=0
, we have
u^{1}\equiv S_{1}u_{0}\in E_{p,r}^{s}(T),\ \forall\ T>0.
By induction, we assume that
u^{n}\in E_{p,r}^{s}(T)
for all
T>0
. Then by lemma
2.14
,
F(u^{n})\in L^{1}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r}),\ \forall\ T>0
. Combining Lemma
2.12
and (
3.3
), we easily deduce that (
3.3
) has a solution
u^{n+1}\in E_{p,r}^{s}(T),\ \forall\ T>0
.

Define
U^{n}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}
. Since
\|u^{n+1}(0)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\|S_{n+1}u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}
, by Lemma
2.11
and Lemma
2.14
, we have

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}e^{CU^{n}(t)}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\|u^{n}(t^{\prime\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime\prime}}\|F(u^{n})(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}e^{CU^{n}(t)}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\|u^{n}(t^{\prime\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime\prime}}\|u^{n}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}^{2}dt^{\prime}\Big).

For fixed
T>0
such that
2C^{2}T\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}<1
, we have

\|u^{1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\equiv\|S_{1}u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}},\ \forall\ t\in[0,T].

By induction, we assume that

(3.5)

\|u^{n}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}},\ \forall\ t\in[0,T].

Plugging (
3.5
) into (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}(1-2C^{2}t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{1-2C^{2}t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}t^{\prime}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}t^{\prime}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}\Big)^{2}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}},\ \forall\ t\in[0,T].

Therefore,
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
, which implies that
(u^{n}\partial_{x}u^{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
and
(F(u^{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. We can conclude that the sequence
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.

We claim that
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Indeed, for all
(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(\partial_{t}+u^{n+m}\partial_{x})(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})=(u^{n}-u^{n+m})\partial_{x}u^{n+1}+F(u^{n})-F(u^{n+m})\triangleq g^{n,m},\\
(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})|_{t=0}=S_{n+m+1}u_{0}-S_{n+1}u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Define

\widetilde{U}^{n,m}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\partial_{x}u^{n+m}(t)\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{\frac{1}{p}}\cap L^{\infty}},&\text{if}\ b=\frac{5}{3}\ and\ s<2+\frac{1}{p},\\
\|\partial_{x}u^{n+m}(t)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}},&\text{if}\ b=\frac{5}{3}\ and\ s=2+\frac{1}{p},\ r>1,\\
\|\partial_{x}u^{n+m}(t)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-2}},&\text{if}\ b\in\mathbb{R}\ and\ s>2+\frac{1}{p}\ (or\ s=2+\frac{1}{p},\ r=1).\end{array}\right.

Since
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
, we have
\widetilde{U}^{n,m}(t)\leq C,\ \forall\ (m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\ \forall\ t\in[0,T]
. Applying Lemma
2.11
to (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\|(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{U}^{n,m}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\Big(\|S_{n+m+1}u_{0}-S_{n+1}u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\widetilde{U}^{n,m}(t^{\prime\prime})dt^{\prime\prime}}\|g^{n,m}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C_{T}\Big(\|S_{n+m+1}u_{0}-S_{n+1}u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g^{n,m}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big).

Taking advantage of Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
2.8
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|(u^{n}-u^{n+m})\partial_{x}u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u^{n}-u^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\|\partial_{x}u^{n+1}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|u^{n}-u^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle\|F_{1}(u^{n})-F_{1}(u^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u^{n}-u^{n+m})(u^{n}+u^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|u^{n}-u^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle\|F_{2}(u^{n})-F_{2}(u^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{n+m})(u_{x}^{n}+u_{x}^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\|u_{x}^{n}+u_{x}^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|u^{n}-u^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle\|F_{4}(u^{n})-F_{4}(u^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{n+m})(u_{x}^{n}+u_{x}^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|u^{n}-u^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

If
b=\frac{5}{3}
, then
F_{3}=0
. If
b\neq\frac{5}{3},\ s>\max\{\frac{5}{2},2+\frac{1}{p}\}
, then by Lemma
2.8
,

\displaystyle\|F_{3}(u^{n})-F_{3}(u^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{xx}^{n}-u_{xx}^{n+m})(u_{xx}^{n}+u_{xx}^{n+m})\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{xx}^{n}-u_{xx}^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}\|u_{xx}^{n}+u_{xx}^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|u^{n}-u^{n+m}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

Since
\|S_{n+m+1}u_{0}-S_{n+1}u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C2^{-n}
, we have

\displaystyle\|(u^{n+m+1}-u^{n+1})(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C_{T}\Big(2^{-n}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n+m}(t_{1})-u^{n}(t_{1})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C_{T}\Big(2^{-n}+\int_{0}^{t}C_{T}\Big(2^{-n+1}+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|u^{n+m-1}(t_{2})-u^{n-1}(t_{2})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt_{2}\Big)dt_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n+1}C_{T}^{l}2^{-n+l-1}\frac{T^{l-1}}{(l-1)!}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{t_{n}}C_{T}^{n+1}\|u^{m}(t_{n+1})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt_{n+1}\dots dt_{2}dt_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C_{T}2^{-n}e^{2TC_{T}}+\frac{C_{T}^{n+2}T^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\rightarrow 0\ (n\rightarrow\infty).

Therefore,
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
and converges to some limit function
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}).

Finally, we have to check that
u\in E_{p,r}^{s}(T)
and satisfies (
1.2
). Combining the uniform boundedness of
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
and the Fatou property, we can obtain that
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. Since
(u^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
u
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
, an interpolation argument ensures that convergence holds true in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r}),\ \forall\ s^{\prime}<s
. Thus, we can easily take the limit in (
3.3
) and conclude that
u
is a solution of (
1.2
). Since
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
, Lemma
2.14
implies that
F(u)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. Hence, by Lemma
2.12
, we see that
u
is in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
(resp.,
\big(\underset{s^{\prime}<s}{\bigcap}\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,\infty})\big)\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{w}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,\infty})
) if
r<\infty
(resp.,
r=\infty
). Thus, we deduce from (
1.2
) that
u_{t}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
(resp.,
L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,\infty})
) if
r<\infty
(resp.,
r=\infty
) and conclude that
u\in E_{p,r}^{s}(T)
.

Step 2: Uniqueness

Suppose
u,v
are two solutions of (
1.2
) with initial data
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, respectively. Then

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(\partial_{t}+u\partial_{x})(u-v)=(v-u)v_{x}+F(v)-F(u),\\
(u-v)|_{t=0}=u_{0}-v_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Define

\widetilde{U}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{\frac{1}{p}}\cap L^{\infty}},&\text{if}\ b=\frac{5}{3}\ and\ s<2+\frac{1}{p},\\
\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}},&\text{if}\ b=\frac{5}{3}\ and\ s=2+\frac{1}{p},\ r>1,\\
\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-2}},&\text{if}\ b\in\mathbb{R}\ and\ s>2+\frac{1}{p}\ (or\ s=2+\frac{1}{p},\ r=1).\end{array}\right.

Similar to Step 1, applying Lemma
2.11
to (
3.7
) gives

\displaystyle e^{-C\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{U}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\|(u-v)(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\widetilde{U}(t^{\prime\prime})dt^{\prime\prime}}\big(\|(v-u)v_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|F(v)-F(u)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\big)dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\widetilde{U}(t^{\prime\prime})dt^{\prime\prime}}\|u-v\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\big(\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|v\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\big)dt^{\prime}.

Taking advantage of the Gronwall lemma, we have

e^{-C\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{U}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}\|(u-v)(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|v\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}}.

Since
\widetilde{U}(t)\leq C\|u(t)\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}
, we obtain

\|(u-v)(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|v\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}}.

Hence, if
u_{0}=v_{0}
, then
u\equiv v
and we complete the proof of uniqueness.

Step 3: The continuous dependence

Denote
\overline{\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Suppose that
u^{n}
is the solution
of (
1.2
) with initial data
u_{0}^{n}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
and that
u_{0}^{n}\to u_{0}^{\infty}
in
B^{s}_{p,r}
when
n\to\infty
.

Case 1:
r<\infty

Fix
T>0
such that
2C^{2}T\underset{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}\|u_{0}^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}<1
. Then by Step 1, we have

\underset{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}\|u^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{p,r})}\leq\frac{C^{2}\underset{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}\|u_{0}^{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{1-2C^{2}T\underset{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}\triangleq M.

Similar to Step 2, we see that
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
when
n\to\infty
. Hence, we only need to prove
\partial_{x}u^{n}\to\partial_{x}u^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. For simplicity, let
v^{n}\triangleq\partial_{x}u^{n},\ f^{n}\triangleq-(\partial_{x}u^{n})^{2}-\partial_{x}F(u^{n})
and decompose
v^{n}
into
v^{n}=z^{n}+w^{n}
with
(z^{n},w^{n})
satisfying

(3.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}z^{n}+u^{n}\partial_{x}z^{n}=f^{n}-f^{\infty},\\
&z^{n}|_{t=0}=\partial_{x}u_{0}^{n}-\partial_{x}u_{0}^{\infty}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

and

(3.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w^{n}+u^{n}\partial_{x}w^{n}=f^{\infty},\\
&w^{n}|_{t=0}=\partial_{x}u_{0}^{\infty}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Similar to Step 1 and Step 2, applying Lemma
2.11
to (
3.8
) gives

\|z^{n}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq e^{CMT}\Big(\|\partial_{x}u_{0}^{n}-\partial_{x}u_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f^{n}(t^{\prime})-f^{\infty}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big).

In addition, Proposition
2.5
implies that

\displaystyle\|(u^{n}_{x})^{2}-(u^{\infty}_{x})^{2}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u^{n}_{x}-u^{\infty}_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\|u^{n}_{x}+u^{\infty}_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u^{n}_{x}-u^{\infty}_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}F_{1}(u^{n})-\partial_{x}F_{1}(u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u^{n}-u^{\infty})(u^{n}+u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}F_{2}(u^{n})-\partial_{x}F_{2}(u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{\infty})(u_{x}^{n}+u_{x}^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}F_{4}(u^{n})-\partial_{x}F_{4}(u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{\infty})(u_{x}^{n}+u_{x}^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

If
b=\frac{5}{3}
, then
F_{3}=0
. If
b\neq\frac{5}{3},\ s>\max\{\frac{5}{2},2+\frac{1}{p}\}
, then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}F_{3}(u^{n})-\partial_{x}F_{3}(u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C\|(u_{xx}^{n}-u_{xx}^{\infty})(u_{xx}^{n}+u_{xx}^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

Therefore,

\|z^{n}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq e^{CMT}C\Big(\|u_{0}^{n}-u_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|u_{x}^{n}-u_{x}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big).

Now we consider (
3.9
). Since
s>1+\frac{1}{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq\|(\partial_{x}u^{\infty})^{2}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|\partial_{x}F(u^{\infty})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{x}u^{\infty}\|^{2}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|u^{\infty}\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\big)\leq CM^{2}.

Recall that
\partial_{x}u_{0}^{\infty}\in B^{s-1}_{p,r}
,
\underset{n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}}{\sup}\|u^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{p,r})}\leq M
, and
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. By Lemma
2.12
, we see that
w^{n}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Then we deduce from Lemma
2.13
that
w^{n}\to w^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
.

Hence, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exits a constant
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\forall\ n>N,\ t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|w^{n}(t)-w^{\infty}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}e^{-CTe^{CMT}},

\displaystyle\|u^{n}(t)-u^{\infty}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4CTe^{CMT}}e^{-CTe^{CMT}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}u^{n}(t)-\partial_{x}u^{\infty}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|z^{n}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|w^{n}(t)-w^{\infty}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{CMT}\Big(\|u_{0}^{n}-u_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}u^{n}(t^{\prime})-\partial_{x}u^{\infty}(t^{\prime})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}dt^{\prime}\Big)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e^{-CTe^{CMT}}.

By the Gronwall lemma, we can obtain

\|\partial_{x}u^{n}(t)-\partial_{x}u^{\infty}(t)\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+Ce^{CMT}e^{CTe^{CMT}}\|u_{0}^{n}-u_{0}^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

Since
u_{0}^{n}\to u_{0}^{\infty}
in
B^{s}_{p,r}
, we have
\partial_{x}u^{n}\to\partial_{x}u^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Therefore,

\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{x}u^{n}-\partial_{x}u^{\infty}\|_{C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})}+\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{C([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})}\big)\to 0

when
n\to\infty
, which also implies that
F(u^{n})\to F(u^{\infty})
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Since

\displaystyle\|u^{n}u^{n}_{x}-u^{\infty}u^{\infty}_{x}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C\big(\|u^{n}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+\|u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\big)\to 0

when
n\to\infty
, we have
u^{n}u^{n}_{x}\to u^{\infty}u^{\infty}_{x}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Then by (
1.2
), we see that
\partial_{t}u^{n}\to\partial_{t}u^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r})
. Hence, we conclude that
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
in
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
.

Case 2:
r=\infty

Similar to Case 1, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{0x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\geq C^{\prime}N^{\frac{3}{5}}.

Owing to the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}C_{\alpha,\beta}\|u(\tau)\|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}}d\tau.

Denote
T_{0}\triangleq\frac{1}{4C_{\alpha,\beta}\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}}
. For all
t\in[0,T_{0}]
, we get

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq CN^{-\frac{1}{10}}.

Let
y
be the solution of (
3.10
). There is a time
T_{1}>0
small enough such that (
3.17
) holds for any
t\in[0,\min\{T_{0},T_{1}\}]
. Let
\bar{T}\triangleq 2N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\min\{T_{0},T_{1}\}
for
N>10
sufficiently large. In order to prove the norm inflation, it suffices to prove that there is a time
t_{0}\in[0,\bar{T}]
such that
\|u(t_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq\ln N
. We assume the opposite holds, that is,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\bar{T}]}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\leq\ln N.

We see from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\Delta_{j}u\right)+\Delta_{j}\left(uu_{x}\right)+\Delta_{j}F(u)=0.

Applying the Lagrange coordinates to the above equation and integrating with respect to
t
, we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\left(\Delta_{j}u\right)\circ y=\Delta_{j}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{4}I_{i}(\tau)d\tau-\Delta_{j}F_{4}(u_{0})\cdot t,

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=(u\Delta_{j}u_{x}-\Delta_{j}(uu_{x}))\circ y\triangleq R_{j}\circ y,

\displaystyle I_{2}=-(\Delta_{j}F_{1}(u))\circ y,

\displaystyle I_{3}=-(\Delta_{j}F_{2}(u))\circ y,

\displaystyle I_{4}=\Delta_{j}F_{4}(u_{0})-(\Delta_{j}F_{4}(u))\circ y.

Let
T=N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Since we want an estimate of
\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}
, we need to calculate
\underset{j\geq-1}{\sum}2^{j}\|I_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Taking advantage of (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we have the following estimates:

(1) Similar to the proof of Lemma 2.100 in
[
1
]
, we have

\underset{j\geq-1}{\sum}2^{j}\|I_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\underset{j\geq-1}{\sum}2^{j}\|R_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\leq CN^{-\frac{1}{10}}\ln N.

(2) Thanks to Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
2.8
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|I_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F_{1}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u^{2}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{-2}}\leq C\|u^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq CN^{-\frac{1}{5}},

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|I_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F_{2}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{-2}}\leq C\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq CN^{-\frac{1}{5}}.

(3) Now we consider
\underset{j\geq-1}{\sum}2^{j}\|I_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, which is the most challenging to estimate and demonstrates the significance of Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
. Denote
E=\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)(u_{x}^{2})
, then (
1.2
) gives

(5.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}E+u\partial_{x}E=G(t,x),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
E|_{t=0}=E_{0}=\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)(u_{0x}^{2}),\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle G(t,x)=-2\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)(u_{x}\partial_{x}F(u))-\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)(u_{x}^{3})+V_{1}+P(D)V_{2},

\displaystyle V_{1}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}}u\cdot P(D)(u_{x}^{2})-\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\right)u\cdot\left(1-\beta^{2}\partial_{xx}\right)^{-1}(u_{x}^{2}),

\displaystyle V_{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}}uu_{x}^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\right)\partial_{xx}(uu_{x}^{2}).

Taking advantage of Lemma
5.1
, (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)(u_{x}\partial_{x}F(u))\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{x}\partial_{x}F(u)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}\partial_{xx}P(D)u^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{x}\partial_{xx}P(D)u_{x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{x}\partial_{x}^{4}P(D)u_{x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}\cap B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}\left(\|\partial_{xx}P(D)u^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\|\partial_{xx}P(D)u_{x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\right)

\displaystyle+C\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}\|\partial_{x}^{4}P(D)u_{x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\cap B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}\cap B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}+C\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(N^{-\frac{1}{5}}\ln N+N^{-\frac{1}{5}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+(\ln N)^{2}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{3}P(D)(u_{x}^{3})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{x}^{3}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}\cap B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\ln N)^{2}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}.

Moreover, it’s easy to check that

\displaystyle\|V_{1}+P(D)V_{2}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq CN^{-\frac{1}{5}}\ln N.

Since
N>10
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|G(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\left(N^{-\frac{1}{5}}\ln N+(\ln N)^{2}\|u_{x}(t)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}\right).

Applying
\Delta_{j}
and the Lagrange coordinates to (
5.9
) yields

\displaystyle\left(\Delta_{j}E\right)\circ y-\Delta_{j}E_{0}=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{R_{j}}\circ y+\left(\Delta_{j}G\right)\circ y\ d\tau,

where
\widetilde{R_{j}}=u\partial_{x}\Delta_{j}E-\Delta_{j}(u\partial_{x}E)
. According to Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
and (
5.7
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|\widetilde{R_{j}}\circ y\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|\widetilde{R_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}\|E\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\leq C\|u\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{0}}\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}\leq C(\ln N)^{2}\|u_{x}\|_{B_{\infty,\infty,1}^{0}}.

Thus, for any
t\in[0,T]
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|I_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|\widetilde{R_{j}}\circ y\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|\left(\Delta_{j}G\right)\circ y\|_{L^{\infty}}\ d\tau

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT(\ln N)^{2}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)}+CTN^{-\frac{1}{5}}\ln N.

In order to estimate
\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)}
, we differentiate (
1.2
) once with respect to
x
and apply
\Delta_{j}
to it, thus obtaining

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+u\partial_{x}\right)\Delta_{j}u_{x}=\Delta_{j}\widetilde{g}+u\partial_{x}\Delta_{j}u_{x}-\Delta_{j}\left(uu_{xx}\right),

where
\widetilde{g}\triangleq-u_{x}^{2}-\partial_{x}F(u)
. Therefore, according to Lemma
5.2
and the similar proof of Lemma
2.11
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{j\geq-1}(j+2)^{1+\frac{1}{100}}\|\Delta_{j}u_{x}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}(j+2)^{1+\frac{1}{100}}\|\Delta_{j}u_{0x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{j\geq-1}(j+2)^{1+\frac{1}{100}}\|\Delta_{j}\widetilde{g}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{j\geq-1}(j+2)^{1+\frac{1}{100}}\|\left(u\partial_{x}\Delta_{j}u_{x}-\Delta_{j}\left(uu_{xx}\right)\right)(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\widetilde{g}(\tau)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}d\tau+C\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{x}(\tau)\|_{{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}}\|u_{x}(\tau)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}d\tau.

Since

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{g}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\|\partial_{x}F_{4}(u)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\|\partial_{x}F_{2}(u)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\|\partial_{x}F_{1}(u)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\|u^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\|u^{2}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+\|u^{2}\|_{C^{0,1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}}+N^{-\frac{1}{5}}\right),

applying (
5.4
) and (
5.7
) then yields

\displaystyle\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle CN^{\frac{9}{10}+\frac{1}{100}}+CTN^{-\frac{1}{5}}+CT\left(\ln N\right)\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CN^{\frac{9}{10}+\frac{1}{100}}+CN^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln N\right)\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)},

which implies that
\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}\left(B^{0}_{\infty,\infty,1}\right)}\leq CN^{\frac{9}{10}+\frac{1}{100}}
when
N
is large enough. Thus, we deduce from (
5.10
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|I_{4}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle CT(\ln N)^{2}N^{\frac{9}{10}+\frac{1}{100}}+CTN^{-\frac{1}{5}}\ln N

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\ln N)^{2}N^{\frac{2}{5}+\frac{1}{100}}+CN^{-\frac{7}{10}}\ln N.

By
(1)-(3)
, (
5.3
), (
5.5
) and (
5.8
), we finally have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\left(t\|\Delta_{j}F_{4}(u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}-\|\Delta_{j}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|I_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle t\|F_{4}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}-\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}-Ct\left(N^{-\frac{1}{10}}\ln N+N^{-\frac{1}{5}}+(\ln N)^{2}N^{\frac{2}{5}+\frac{1}{100}}+N^{-\frac{7}{10}}\ln N\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Ct\|u_{0x}^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}-C\left(N^{-\frac{1}{10}}+N^{-\frac{1}{10}-\frac{1}{2}}\ln N+N^{-\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{2}}+(\ln N)^{2}N^{-\frac{1}{10}+\frac{1}{100}}+N^{-\frac{7}{10}-\frac{1}{2}}\ln N\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}tN^{\frac{3}{5}}-C.

Therefore, for any
t\in\left[\frac{1}{2}N^{-\frac{1}{2}},N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]
, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{10}}-C.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\bar{T}]}\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}N^{\frac{1}{10}}-C>\ln N

for
N
large enough, which contradicts to the assumption (
5.7
). So we gain the norm inflation and complete the proof of ill-posedness.
∎

Next, when
b=\frac{5}{3}
, we provide the ill-posedness in
B^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2,q}
with
q\in(1,+\infty]
in the sense of norm inflation.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
b=\frac{5}{3}
,
1<q\leq+\infty
. Then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
large enough, there exists a
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that the following hold:

(i)
\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,q}}\leq\frac{C}{\ln N}\to 0
, as
N\to\infty
;

(ii) There exists a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{C}\left([0,T);H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\right)
to the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) with a time
T\leq\frac{1}{\ln N}
;

(iii)
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,T\right);B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}\geq\ln N
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\varphi}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
be an even, non-zero, non-negative function such that
\widetilde{\varphi}\varphi=\widetilde{\varphi}
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
large enough, we define

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\dfrac{(\ln N)^{-1}h_{n}(x)}{2^{2n}n^{\frac{2}{1+q}}},

where
\mathcal{F}(h_{n})(\xi)=i2^{-n}\xi\widetilde{\varphi}(2^{-n}\xi)
. We see from the proof of Theorem 1.2 in
[
22
]
that
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap B^{3/2}_{2,q}(\mathbb{R})
is odd and satisfies

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,q}}\leq C(\ln N)^{-1},

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3/2}_{2,1}}\leq CN^{\frac{q-1}{q+1}},

\displaystyle u_{0x}(0)\geq C(\ln N)^{-1}N^{\frac{q-1}{q+1}}>0.

Thus, the proof of Theorem
3.2
gives that there is a lifespan
T_{N}\sim N^{-\frac{q-1}{q+1}}
and a solution
u\in\mathcal{C}\left([0,T_{N});H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\right)
such that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,T_{N}\right);B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}<\infty
.

Next, we denote
\widetilde{T}\triangleq(\ln N)^{-1}
and consider
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,\widetilde{T}\right);B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}
for fixed
N
large enough. If
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,\widetilde{T}\right);B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}\geq\ln N
, then Theorem
5.4
is proved. Otherwise, if we assume that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,\widetilde{T}\right);B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}<\ln N,

then similar to the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left(e+\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\right)\leq\left(\log\left(e+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\right)+Ct\right)e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{B_{2,\infty}^{3/2}}d\tau},

which implies
u\in L^{\infty}([0,\widetilde{T});H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\ (s>\frac{3}{2})
. Since
u_{0}
is odd, we can easily deduce from (
1.2
) that
u
is odd and
u(t,0)=u_{xx}(t,0)=0
. By differentiating (
1.2
) once with respect to
x
and setting
x=0
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}u_{x}(t,0)=-u_{x}(t,0)^{2}-\partial_{x}F(u)(t,0)\geq\frac{2}{3}u_{x}(t,0)^{2}-C_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Taking advantage of Lemma
2.6
and (
5.11
), we see that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C\ln N,

thus obtaining

(5.12)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}u_{x}(t,0)\geq\frac{2}{3}u_{x}(t,0)^{2}-C_{\alpha,\beta}(\ln N)^{2},\ \forall\ t\in[0,\widetilde{T}).

Since
q>1
, for
N>0
large enough, we have

\displaystyle u_{0x}(0)\geq C(\ln N)^{-1}N^{\frac{q-1}{q+1}}\gg\frac{\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N}{\sqrt{2/3}}>0.

Denote
C_{0}\triangleq\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}u_{0x}(0)-\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}u_{0x}(0)+\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N}\in(0,1)
, then solving (
5.12
) gives

\displaystyle u_{x}(t,0)\geq\dfrac{\sqrt{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N\left(1+C_{0}\exp\left(2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N\cdot t\right)\right)}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\left(1-C_{0}\exp\left(2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N\cdot t\right)\right)},

which implies that there exists

\displaystyle 0<T_{0}\leq-\ln C_{0}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}C_{\alpha,\beta}}\ln N}<\widetilde{T}

for
N>0
large enough such that
u
blows up in finite time
T_{0}
. Therefore, according to Proposition
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,\widetilde{T});B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}\geq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,\widetilde{T});B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}\right)}=+\infty,

which contradicts to the hypothesis (
5.11
). Hence, we get
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\left[0,\widetilde{T}\right);B^{3/2}_{2,q}\right)}\geq\ln N
and complete the proof.
∎

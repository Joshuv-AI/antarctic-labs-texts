Hybrid deep models for parallel feature extraction and enhanced emotion state classification
Authors: Sivasankaran Pichandi, Gomathy Balasubramanian, Venkatesh Chakrapani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Emotions play a vital role in recognizing a person’s thoughts and vary significantly with stress levels. Emotion and stress classification have gained considerable attention in robotics and artificial intelligence applications. While numerous methods based on machine learning techniques provide average classification performance, recent deep learning approaches offer enhanced results. This research presents a hybrid deep learning model that extracts features using AlexNet and DenseNet models, followed by feature fusion and dimensionality reduction via Principal Component Analysis (PCA). The reduced features are then classified using a multi-class Support Vector Machine (SVM) to categorize different types of emotions. The proposed model was evaluated using the DEAP and EEG Brainwave datasets, both well-suited for emotion analysis due to their comprehensive EEG signal recordings and diverse emotional stimuli. The DEAP dataset includes EEG signals from 32 participants who watched 40 one-minute music videos, while the EEG Brainwave dataset categorizes emotions into positive, negative, and neutral based on EEG recordings from participants exposed to six different film clips. The proposed model achieved an accuracy of 95.54% and 97.26% for valence and arousal categories in the DEAP dataset, respectively, and 98.42% for the EEG Brainwave dataset. These results significantly outperform existing methods, demonstrating the model’s superior performance in terms of precision, recall, F1-score, specificity, and Mathew correlation coefficient. The integration of AlexNet and DenseNet, combined with PCA and multi-class SVM, makes this approach particularly effective for capturing the intricate patterns in EEG data, highlighting its potential for applications in human-computer interaction and mental health monitoring, marking a significant advancement over traditional methods.

Introduction

Human emotions are directly related to behavior. The behavioral and physiological response is reflected through emotions. In interpersonal communication, emotions play a vital role. Emotions determines the quality of daily life and its related needs. When the needs are met, humans will generate positive emotions and if it is not then negative emotions will be generated. In some cases, neutral emotions will be generated so that others could not be able to find the person’s emotional status. Accurate emotion recognition is crucial in various applications, significantly enhancing their effectiveness and user experience. In mental health monitoring, it can detect signs of emotional disorders, enabling timely intervention. Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) benefits by allowing systems like virtual assistants to respond empathetically, improving user satisfaction. In education, emotion recognition personalizes learning, adjusting content based on student engagement and frustration levels. The gaming industry uses it to create adaptive and immersive experiences, while market research leverages it to analyze consumer responses for better marketing strategies. Additionally, assistive technologies for individuals with disabilities, such as autism, can help users navigate social interactions more effectively by providing real-time emotional feedback. These use cases highlight the importance of accurate emotion recognition in enhancing functionality and user engagement across diverse fields.

In recent times, the study of human computer interactions gained more attention and researchers put more efforts into developing a natural interaction model so that the computers can understand the emotional statues of the user. For better emotion classification, body gestures, speech, facial expressions are used by the research community. However, these modalities can be artificially generated. For example, if a person is angry, then they can even smile to hide their anger emotion. Meanwhile other factors like poor lighting, noise artifacts and usage of accessories like glasses reduces the classification performances. Thus, to attain a reliable and more efficient emotion classification, physiological data has been used in recent times. The physiological data included electroencephalography (EEG) data which can be collected from a person directly. EEG data collects the brain electrical activity, and it provides good temporal resolution, so that emotion recognition applications can understand the brain activity and define the emotion states of a person.

Earlier machine learning models were used to EEG data classification which requires additional feature processing modules. Moreover, the results of machine learning modes are not accurate, and its feature processing abilities are limited. Recently deep learning models are employed over machine learning techniques1–4. Specifically, convolutional neural network is widely used in various application. Emotion classification using convolutional neural network was employed in our earlier research work and attained better performances than machine learning models. However, for enhanced performance and to select the best features in the emotion classification process, in this research work a hybrid deep learning model is presented which incorporated convolutional neural network variants AlexNet and DenseNet al.gorithms.

Objectives and motivations

Despite significant advancements in emotion classification using machine learning and deep learning techniques, several gaps persist in the existing literature. Traditional machine learning methods often struggle with feature extraction and require extensive preprocessing, which can limit their accuracy and generalizability. Recent deep learning approaches, while more effective, typically focus on single-model architectures that may not fully capture the complex features present in physiological data such as EEG signals. Moreover, many existing models do not adequately address the issue of dimensionality reduction, leading to increased computational costs and potential overfitting. This research aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a hybrid deep learning model that utilizes the strengths of AlexNet and DenseNet for feature extraction, followed by dimensionality reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and classification with a multi-class Support Vector Machine (SVM). The primary motivation is to enhance the accuracy, efficiency, and robustness of emotion classification systems by addressing the limitations of current methodologies.

Research questions

This research work seeks to answer several key research questions to advance the field of emotion classification from physiological data. First, how can the integration of multiple deep learning models improve the feature extraction process for emotion classification? Second, what is the impact of dimensionality reduction on the performance and computational efficiency of hybrid deep learning models? Third, how does the proposed hybrid model compare to existing machine learning and deep learning approaches in terms of classification accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, specificity, and Mathew correlation coefficient? By addressing these questions, this research aims to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed model’s effectiveness and its potential contributions to the field of emotion recognition.

Related works

A detailed analysis of existing research work on emotion classification is presented. The existing methodologies and its features are studied in detail. The classification model reported in5 includes a machine learning algorithm to categorize seven discrete emotions. Based on the features extracted from the EEG signals, the emotions are categorized. Familiar machine learning model support vector machine is employed for classification. The target user is allowed to watch standardized videos and their emotions are obtained through EEG channels. Further the features from the EEG channel are extracted and classified through the support vector machine. The presented approach attained accuracy of 80.56% for positive emotions and 81.08% for negative emotions which is much lesser than the current approaches. The multimodal EEG signal-based emotion classification model presented in6 utilizes portable devices to capture the EEG signals. The captured signals are classified using supervised support vector machine. Compared to other machine learning based emotion classification models, the performance of the presented multi-modal approach is much better in terms of accuracy and deployment cost. However, the attained accuracy might vary with other approaches as the low-cost devices are prone to environmental noises.

The emotion recognition model presented in7 incorporated machine learning algorithms for feature classification from EEG signals. The presented approach employed machine learning for feature extraction and classification. Further an activation curve is obtained considering the emotion coefficients and classification results. The emotions are classified, and the emotional activation factors are reveled based on the obtained activation curve. In the last stage, a weight coefficient factor is used to improve the recognition accuracy. The emotion analyzes model presented in8 focused on the data imbalance issue in emotion classification process. The difference in the data distribution is mainly considered in the research work and introduces adversarial multitask training to establish better relationship between training and testing performances. The presented approach learns the common attributes and employs a gradient reversal layer to represent the source and target data classes. Due to this, the data imbalance issue is reduced, and the performance is increased in the classification process.

The emotion recognition model presented in9 includes discrete Fourier transform for kernel function to recognize the user emotions. Linear kernel and polynomial kernel functions are employed to handle the kernelized features and spatial features in the video frames. Finally, based on the features the emotions are classified with better accuracy compared to traditional methods. A spiking neural network model was reported in10 for recognizing emotions. The presented approach includes algorithms like discrete wavelet transform, fast Fourier transform, and variance Fourier transform for feature extraction. Further the extracted features are classified using spiking neural network and attained improved classification performance over conventional techniques.

In general, compared to traditional ML models, extreme learning models exhibit better performances11,12. The machine learning model extreme gradient boosting based emotion classification approach was presented in13 to compute the correlation between emotional labels and EEG features. The presented multidomain model extracts the features using sliding time window and classify them using gradient boosting algorithm. Compared to single channel classification, the performance of multichannel is higher and it validated by experimentation using benchmark datasets. An automatic emotion recognition model presented in14 extracts the physiological time and frequency domain features to find the relevant emotions. The presented approach includes a forward floating kernel-based feature selection algorithm for optimal feature selection. Further, the selected features dimensions are reduced through generalized discriminant analysis model. Finally, the features are classified into four types of emotions using the least square support vector machine. However, the attained classification accuracy is comparatively lower than the current approaches.

The multichannel EEG emotion recognition model presented in15 dynamically learns the features using dynamic graph convolutional neural network model. The presented approach performs discriminative feature extraction which improves emotion recognition performances. Experimental results confirm the presented approach superiority in terms of accuracy over traditional machine learning based recognition models. Similar multi-channel emotion analysis model presented in16 detects emotions through emotion detection architecture. The presented architecture includes a specified feature extraction model and an emotion correlation learner. Each channel in the proposed architecture performs individual feature extraction and specifies the emotions as a hierarchical structure. Finally, the correlation between the emotions is learned to label the feature as in to positive, negative, and neutral categories.

The emotion recognition model presented in17 includes a bidirectional principal component analysis and least square linear discriminant analysis to improve the recognition efficiency. The presented approach initially extracts visual features using optimized kernel Laplacian radial basis function based neural network classifier. Further the features are classified based on a set of rules to emotion categorization. The emotion classification model reported in18 extracts the essential features from EEG signal using robust principal component analysis methods. The presented PCA model is embedded with transfer learning process to obtain the labeled data. Based on the intra and inter individual differences the irrelevant features are removed, and optimal features are selected in the presented model. Compared to traditional methods, the obtained accuracy for emotion category is less. A Bi-stream emotion attribution and classification network reported for emotion recognition, emotion summarization and emotion attribution tasks19. The presented classification network has an attribution model and a classification model in which the essential emotional segments are extracted by the attribution model, and it is classified by the classification model. The classification model is a bi-stream architecture which extracts the segments and provides classification results based on the ground truth labels.

The emotion recognition model presented in20 includes deep learning algorithm LSTM and optimization algorithm to attain enhanced recognition performances. The presented approach utilizes a differential evolution model to optimize the network hyper parameters of LSTM model. Further comparative analysis with existing optimization models like particle swarm optimization, random search, simulated annealing models validates the better performance of the presented optimized LSTM model. The accuracy attained by the optimized LSTM is 77.68% which is much lesser than other approaches. The emotion classification model presented in21 employed machine learning algorithms like attention LSTM, k-nearest neighbor, and SVM to categorize six different types of emotions. The presented approach extracts the features from EEG, skin responses, respiration and ECG channels and categorize them into different types. Compared machine learning models kNN and SVM the performance of attention LSTM is better, and it validated through the experimental analysis.

The hybrid deep learning model presented in22 combines convolutional neural network with bidirectional LSTM to learn the time series emotion data. The presented model includes linear discriminant analysis to learn the low dimensional source data and target data. Further the dimensions are reduced using maximum mean discrepancy. The difference constraints between target and source data are handled through graph embedding procedure. Finally, a deep neural network is employed to classify the discriminative emotion features into different categories. Experimentations confirmed the deep learning model performance over machine learning approaches.

The emotion recognition model presented in23 preserves the emotional information and neglects the irrelevant information to avoid errors in the recognition process. The presented recognition model includes an attention mechanism based convolutional recurrent neural network to learn the relevant features. Experimentations confirmed that the presented network effectively extracts the features and improves the recognition performances over conventional approaches. The emotion recognition model presented in24 includes deep CNN and AlexNet models to learn the low- and high-level features from the input signal. The input is initially segmented into multiple portions and then the features are extracted using the deep learning models. The extracted low level and high-level features are combined through pyramid matching technique. Finally, machine learning classifiers are employed to classify the emotion states better than the existing methods. Similar deep learning model presented in25,26 includes CNN and 3DCNN model to handle high dimension features. The presented approach extracts the features using deep learning models and combine them using a fusion network to attain improved recognition performances.

An emotion classification model presented in27 extracts the features like frequency bands, differential asymmetry, rational asymmetry, and differential entropy features. Further wrapper and filter method are combined to select the essential features. Finally, the features are classified using hybrid deep learning which combines random forest with deep learning model. In research work28 multiple deep learning models like VGG16, SqueezeNet, DenseNet and ResNet variants are used to categorize different types of emotions. Based on the deep features and acoustic features, a hybrid feature vector is initially created. From the feature vector, Relief algorithm extracts the essential optimal feature. Finally, machine learning models are used to classify the selected features which categorizes different types of emotions. However, the attained accuracy is comparatively lower than the recent models and the computation complexity is high due to multiple feature processing elements. Materials and methods

Dataset

In this research work, two benchmark datasets are used for experimentation. The first dataset used in the research work is DEAP dataset41. The DEAP (Dataset for Emotion Analysis using Physiological signals) dataset is a widely used benchmark for emotion classification studies. It consists of data collected from 32 participants who watched 40 one-minute-long music videos. Each participant’s physiological responses, including EEG signals, were recorded at a sampling rate of 512 Hz. The dataset includes both raw and preprocessed signals, with the latter downsampled to 128 Hz to reduce computational load. Each recording contains 40 trials per participant, resulting in a total of 1,280 trials (32 participants x 40 trials). The data is segmented into 60-second epochs, and the labels for each trial indicate the participants’ self-reported levels of arousal, valence, and dominance on a scale from 1 to 9.

The EEG Brainwave dataset, sourced from Kaggle, is designed to classify emotions into three categories: positive, negative, and neutral. This dataset comprises EEG recordings from multiple participants exposed to six different film clips, each lasting approximately 2 min. The EEG signals were recorded at a sampling rate of 150 Hz. Data collection was spread over several days to minimize participant fatigue and ensure the reliability of the recordings. The dataset includes a total of 324,000 data points, segmented into 3-minute epochs to capture the variations in brainwave patterns corresponding to different emotional states.

AlexNet

The first feature extraction model used in the research work is a familiar convolutional neural network variant AlexNet. The architecture of AlexNet includes convolutional, pooling and fully connected layers. Initially the nonlinearity in the input is removed by applying an activation function. Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) is widely used as activation function. In the initial convolution layer, the features are selected and fed into pooling layer. In the pooling layer, the features are down sampled to extract the rich features. Finally using the fully connected layer, the features are flattened so that the essential features are selected, and others are dropped. The general architecture of AlexNet includes a SoftMax function in the last layer for feature classification. However, in the proposed work, AlexNet is used for feature extraction thus it is removed from the architecture. The complete architecture of AlexNet and layer details are provided in Fig. 1.

AlexNet and DenseNet were specifically chosen for feature extraction in our hybrid deep learning model due to their proven effectiveness in handling complex image data and their complementary strengths in capturing different aspects of features. AlexNet is known for its role in the field of deep learning, particularly in image recognition tasks. Its architecture includes multiple convolutional layers followed by max-pooling layers, which are highly effective at capturing spatial hierarchies in the input data. The use of ReLU activation functions accelerates the training process by introducing non-linearity, which is crucial for learning complex patterns in EEG signals. AlexNet’s relatively simple yet powerful architecture allows for efficient feature extraction with a reduced risk of overfitting, making it suitable for emotion recognition where the nuances of EEG data need to be captured effectively.

DenseNet, on the other hand, introduces dense connections between layers, ensuring maximum information flow and gradient propagation throughout the network. This architecture mitigates the vanishing gradient problem and allows for deeper networks without the risk of information loss. Each layer in DenseNet receives inputs from all preceding layers, which promotes feature reuse and leads to more robust and discriminative feature representations. This is particularly beneficial for emotion recognition tasks, where the ability to capture intricate and subtle patterns in the EEG data is crucial.

The combination of AlexNet and DenseNet utilizes the strengths of both architectures. AlexNet provides a strong baseline for capturing basic spatial features, while DenseNet enhances this by capturing more complex and deeper feature interactions. This complementary approach ensures a comprehensive feature extraction process, capturing both high-level abstractions and detailed patterns in the EEG data. By fusing the features extracted from both models, our hybrid approach benefits from the strengths of both architectures, leading to more accurate and robust emotion recognition.

In summary, AlexNet and DenseNet were chosen for their proven capabilities in image recognition, their complementary strengths in feature extraction, and their suitability for capturing the complex patterns inherent in EEG signals necessary for accurate emotion recognition.

Feature fusion and dimensionality reduction

The features extracted from the AlexNet and DenseNet models are fused, and the high dimensional features are further converted into low dimensional features using principle component analysis. The simple feature fusion process concatenates both the features obtained from the AlexNet and DenseNet models. When combining both model features, the dimensionality of the feature is increased and also the feature map has redundant data as both models select the essential features from the input. To overcome this dimensionality issue, Principal component analysis (PCA) is employed in the proposed model. PCA is widely used whenever redundancy in variables or features maps. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was selected for dimensionality reduction due to its effectiveness, simplicity, and interpretability in handling high-dimensional EEG data. PCA’s linear nature makes it computationally efficient and easier to understand compared to non-linear techniques like t-SNE and autoencoders. t-SNE, while useful for visualization, is computationally intensive and less practical for feature extraction, and autoencoders require complex architectures and extensive tuning. PCA preserves the global structure of the data by maximizing variance, ensuring that the most informative features are retained. To determine the number of principal components to retain, we conducted an explained variance analysis, aiming to capture at least 95% of the total variance. This balance ensures that the dimensionality is reduced effectively without significant loss of important information, enhancing the overall performance of the emotion classification model.

The term redundancy in the proposed work indicates the correlated features. For examples, if a feature is selected by the AlexNet model, the same feature will be selected by DenseNet model if the feature is highly important. In that case when concatenating the features, the selected feature will be repeated. In another case, if the features are correlated with one another, then it will increase the computation cost. Thus, PCA is used in the proposed work. PCA is familiar for data compression, and it can transform original multivariate data into linearly dependent variable datasets. Feature extraction and fusion

Initially, features are extracted from the EEG data using two different deep learning models, AlexNet and DenseNet. These models were selected due to their proven efficiency in handling complex image data, which is analogous to the multi-dimensional nature of EEG signals. The extracted features from both models are then concatenated to form a comprehensive feature set that captures diverse aspects of the input data. However, this concatenation leads to an increase in the dimensionality of the feature space, which can introduce redundancy and computational challenges.

Dimensionality reduction using PCA

To address the issue of high dimensionality, we employ Principal Component Analysis (PCA), a widely used technique for reducing the dimensionality of data while retaining its most important features. PCA works by identifying the principal components, which are the directions of maximum variance in the data. These components are orthogonal to each other and provide a new set of axes that capture the most significant variations in the data. By projecting the original high-dimensional features onto these principal components, PCA effectively reduces the number of dimensions while preserving the essential information required for classification.

The training and assessment of our hybrid deep learning model, combining AlexNet, DenseNet, PCA for dimensionality reduction, and a multi-class SVM for classification, required substantial computational resources and time. The assessment phase, involving evaluation on the test set, was efficiently completed due to the reduced dimensionality of the features.

From the above results, it is observed that the proposed hybrid model can effectively categorize the emotions by extracting the optimal features from the input. Using this hybrid approach, emotions and their related stress factors can be effectively classified.

The proposed hybrid deep learning model addresses several key limitations of traditional emotion recognition methods. Traditional machine learning models often struggle with manual feature extraction, which can be time-consuming and may fail to capture complex EEG patterns. The hybrid model uses AlexNet and DenseNet-121 for automated and efficient feature extraction, ensuring a comprehensive capture of data features. Additionally, traditional models can suffer from high dimensionality and overfitting, which the proposed model mitigates through Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction. Finally, the use of a multi-class SVM enhances classification accuracy and robustness, overcoming the limitations of simpler classifiers.

Conclusion

This research work presents a hybrid deep learning model for emotion classification using convolutional neural network variants like AlexNet and DenseNet. The essential data for emotion classification is processed by deep learning algorithms to extract the optimal features. The extracted features are concatenated, and their dimensionality are reduced using principal component analysis. Finally, the features are classified using multiclass support vector machine to attain improved classification performances. Experimentations using benchmark datasets DEAP and EEG Brainwave emotion dataset, validated the proposed model performances in terms of precision, recall, f1-score, specificity, Mathew correlation coefficient and accuracy. Experimental evaluation validates the superior performance of proposed model over existing machine learning and deep learning models. Future improvements for the proposed hybrid deep learning model could significantly enhance its performance and applicability. One potential direction is the incorporation of additional physiological signals such as ECG, GSR, and EMG, which can provide a more comprehensive understanding of emotional states through multimodal data integration. Advanced data augmentation techniques, including synthetic data generation and signal transformation, can further improve the model’s generalizability. Developing a real-time emotion detection system optimized for faster inference and deployment on mobile or edge devices could expand practical applications in human-computer interaction and mental health monitoring. Applying transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques can enhance the model’s adaptability to different datasets or contexts, reducing the need for extensive retraining. Finally, incorporating advanced optimization algorithms like genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization, or Bayesian optimization can fine-tune the model parameters for better classification performance and efficiency.

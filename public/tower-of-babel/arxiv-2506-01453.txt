From Initial Data to Boundary Layers: Neural Networks for Nonlinear Hyperbolic Conservation Laws
Igor CIRIL, Khalil HADDAOUI, Yohann TENDERO

Abstract
We address the approximation of entropy solutions to initial-boundary value problems for nonlinear strictly hyperbolic conservation laws using neural networks. A general and systematic framework is introduced for the design of efficient and reliable learning algorithms, combining fast convergence during training with accurate predictions. The methodology is assessed through a series of one-dimensional scalar test cases, highlighting its potential applicability to more complex industrial scenarios.



1
Introduction

For several decades, initial-boundary value problems for nonlinear systems of partial differential equations, especially those arising in fluid dynamics, have been a central topic in applied mathematics and computational science. These equations model fundamental phenomena such as shock formation, wave propagation, and turbulence, and their analysis is crucial for understanding a wide range of physical systems.

The last decade has witnessed the rapid emergence of machine learning techniques, particularly deep learning, as powerful tools for solving forward and inverse problems involving partial differential equations. Among them, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)
Raissi et al. 2019
have gained significant attention for their ability to incorporate physical laws into the training process, offering a flexible mesh-free framework that bridges data-driven models and classical PDE theory
Toscano et al. 2025
.

Despite their success in parabolic and elliptic problems, the application of PINNs to nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws remains underdeveloped. These systems pose unique challenges: solutions can develop discontinuities, classical notions of derivatives break down, and boundary conditions must be interpreted in the weak sense. To date, few contributions have managed to address these issues in a theoretically sound and practically effective manner.

In this work, we introduce a mathematically grounded framework for approximating entropy solutions to nonlinear strictly hyperbolic systems of conservation laws using PINNs. The proposed approach rests on two main contributions:

•

A rigorous formulation of PINNs for nonlinear hyperbolic problems, grounded in the parabolic regularization framework studied by Bianchini and Bressan
Bianchini and Bressan 2005
. This provides a well-defined pathway for approximating entropy solutions in the vanishing-viscosity limit, while remaining consistent with the underlying mathematical structure.

•

A general strategy is proposed for the treatment of boundary conditions in nonlinear hyperbolic systems within the PINN framework. Based on a weak formulation involving self-similar Riemann problems, this method enables a stable, consistent, and interpretable enforcement of boundary data, a major challenge in existing neural PDE solvers. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first practical approach to offer a consistent and operational treatment of boundary conditions for systems of hyperbolic conservation laws in the PINN framework. It successfully reconciles entropy inequalities at the domain boundaries with machine learning-based methods in a manner fully adapted to systems.

The proposed framework has been deliberately designed to be precise, simple, and effective. It relies solely on standard feedforward neural networks, without the need for ad hoc regularization techniques or complex architectures, while still achieving strong performance. Numerical experiments on the inviscid Burgers’ equation demonstrate the ability of our method to accurately capture shock profiles and boundary layers, highlighting both its robustness and its practical relevance.

3
Discussion

The proposed framework is not only faster and simpler, but also more general than the one introduced in
Chaumet and Giesselmann 2024
.

In particular, the algorithm developed by Chaumet and Giesselmann
Chaumet and Giesselmann 2024
involves the approximate solution of a min-max optimization problem. As a result, the training procedure is significantly more computationally demanding than in our approach, which requires solving only a standard minimization problem.

With respect to the numerical treatment of boundary conditions, our method explicitly handles both scalar and system cases, in contrast to the algorithm in
Chaumet and Giesselmann 2024
, which is restricted to the scalar setting.
That said, the framework developed in
Chaumet and Giesselmann 2024
can be extended to systems with boundary conditions by adopting the Riemann-problem-based formulation introduced in this work.

In the next section, we demonstrate that even very simple deep feedforward neural networks are capable of accurately capturing nonlinear wave dynamics and boundary layers.

More precisely, we use multilayer perceptrons tailored for regression, consisting of three hidden layers with 20 neurons each and a single scalar output. The networks are trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 over 5000 epochs. In each experiment, instead of computing prediction errors, we compare a single network prediction (referred to as the numerical solution) with the exact solution. The latter is obtained using the finite volume scheme proposed by Dubois and LeFloch

5
Concluding remarks

In this work, we have developed and validated a neural network-based framework for approximating entropy solutions to initial-boundary value problems for nonlinear (strictly) hyperbolic conservation laws. By leveraging parabolic regularization and weak boundary conditions formulated via Riemann problems, our approach ensures physical consistency while maintaining computational simplicity. The numerical experiments based on the inviscid Burgers’ equation demonstrate the ability of deep feedforward neural networks to accurately capture nonlinear wave dynamics and boundary layers, using minimal network complexity and no regularization techniques.

This framework lends itself naturally to further generalizations. In particular, future work will focus on the extension to coupled systems of conservation laws and the treatment of nonlinear interface conditions, with direct application to the simulation of complex multiscale flows. These directions are closely related to the coupling techniques investigated in
Haddaoui 2016
and the references therein, where analytical and numerical tools were developed to ensure the well-posedness and stability of solutions across coupling interfaces. Incorporating such coupling strategies within a learning-based framework offers a promising path toward hybrid solvers that combine the interpretability and robustness of classical methods with the flexibility of neural network architectures. This line of work contributes to the development of hybrid neural–numerical solvers for PDEs with complex features such as nonlinear boundaries and multiscale structures.

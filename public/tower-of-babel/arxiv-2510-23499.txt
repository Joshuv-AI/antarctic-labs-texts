On the rate of convergence of cylindrical singularity in mean curvature flow
Yiqi Huang and Xinrui Zhao

Abstract
. We prove that if a rescaled mean curvature flow is a global graph over the round cylinder with small gradient and converges super-exponentially fast, then it must coincide with the cylinder itself. We also show that the result is sharp with counter-examples of local graphs at arbitrarily super-exponential convergence rate with the domain expanding arbitrarily fast. The first part provides the first unique continuation result in the cylindrical setting, the generic singularity model in mean curvature flow. In sharp contrast, in the second part we construct infinite-dimensional families of Tikhonov-type examples for nonlinear equations, including the rescaled mean curvature flow, showing that unique continuation fails for local graphical solutions. These examples demonstrate the essential role of global graphical assumptions in rigidity and highlight new phenomena absent in the compact case. We also construct non-product mean curvature flows that develop singular sets as prescribed lower dimensional Euclidean space at arbitrary super-exponential rates. Our construction works in great generality for a large class of non-linear equations.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the convergence rate and unique continuation problem for mean curvature flow, which is a geometric evolution equation where there is a family of immersions
\varphi\colon M^{n}\times I\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
evolving by the mean curvature

\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}}{\partial t}=\overrightarrow{H},

with
\overrightarrow{H}
denoting the mean curvature vector of the submanifold
M_{t}=\varphi(M,t)
.

As singularities inevitably occur for mean curvature flows starting from closed hypersurfaces, understanding their formation and structure is a crucial and central question. A standard approach is to study singularities via blowup analysis. If
(0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\times\mathbb{R}
is a singularity of the flow, one can rescale the flow by
\lambda_{i}\to\infty
centered at
(0,0)
to obtain
M_{t}^{i}:=\lambda_{i}M_{\lambda_{i}^{-2}t}
. By Huisken’s monotonicity formula
[
H90
]
and Brakke’s compactness theorem
[
B78
]
, we know that there is a subsequence that converges to a self-similar limit flow - a
tangent flow
. Moreover, if the limit flow is smooth, it is generated by rescaling a hypersurface called
self-shrinker
, satisfying
\mathbf{H}=-\frac{x^{\perp}}{2}
. Equivalently,
self-shrinkers
are the static solutions for the following
rescaled mean curvature flow
equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}}{\partial t}=\overrightarrow{H}+\frac{\tilde{\varphi}^{\perp}}{2},

A priori, the tangent flow may depend on the choice of rescaling sequence. The uniqueness of the tangent flows, as one of the central problems in the analysis of the singularities, has been resolved in several important cases,
[
S14
,
CM15
,
CS
,
LSS
,
CM23
,
S23
,
Z20
,
LZ
,
CSSZ
]
. Closely related to uniqueness problem is the question of
the convergence rate of the rescaled mean curvature flows toward the tangent flow, particularly the
unique continuation problem
. That is, if a mean curvature flow approaches a tangent flow sufficiently fast, does this
force the flow to be identical to the tangent flow?

1.1.
Unique continuation for global graphs

The unique continuation problem has already been extensively studied for elliptic and parabolic equations. See
[
Car39
,
AKS62
,
GL86
,
GL87
,
P96
,
ESS03
]
and the references therein. In the mean curvature flow setting, the spherical case was studied by Sesum
[
S08
]
and Strehlke
[
S20
]
. In
[
S20
]
, he proved that if the rescaled mean curvature flow converges to a round sphere super-exponentially, then it must a round sphere itself. In
[
MS23
]
, Martin-Hagemayer and Sesum generalized this unique continuation property to any compact shrinkers. For more recent results in this direction, see for example
[
DH25
,
DH252
,
SWX25
]
. See also the related question in Ricci flows in
[
SW15
,
Kot22
,
CL25
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the unique continuation problem for singularities modeled on cylinders. Cylinders are of particular importance, as they are the only
generic
shrinkers
[
CM12
,
CIMW13
,
ALW14
,
CM151
,
CM16
,
B16
,
BW17
,
CM19
,
BC19
,
CM21
,
BC21
,
CM23
,
CCMS24
]
.
At the same time, they present essential new challenges. Unlike compact shrinkers, the noncompactness nature of cylinders creates substantial analytic difficulties. Compared with other noncompact shrinkers, such as conical singularities, the cylinder is even more degenerate: it gives rise to non-isolated singularities and requires delicate control of the flow in annular regions around the singular set. See for example
[
W97
,
W00
,
W14
,
GK15
,
BW17
,
GKS18
,
KKM
,
CS
,
Z21
,
CHHW22
,
LZ
]
. These features make the cylindrical case qualitatively harder and correspondingly more intriguing. See a related unique continuation conjecture in
[
SWX25
]
*Conjecture 1.6.

Our first main result confirms the unique continuation property for global graphs in the cylindrical setting.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}:M\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a solution to the rescaled mean curvature flow equation (
1.1
) such that
M_{t}\equiv\tilde{\varphi}(M,t)
can be expressed as a normal graph of a function
u(x,t)
over the round cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\mathbb{S}^{n-k}_{\sqrt{2(n-k)}}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then there exists some
\varepsilon_{0}(n)
such that the following holds: if
\|u\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k}\times[0,\infty))}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{\alpha t}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}u^{2}(x,t)e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu=0\text{ for any }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}

where
d\mu
is the standard measure on
\mathcal{C}_{k}
, then we have
u\equiv 0
.

This implies that if
u\not\equiv 0
, then there exists some
exponential upper bound
for the convergence rate of
u
.

Note that in Theorem
1.1
we only assume smallness the
C^{1}
-norm. If, in addition, we assume the
C^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k})
-norm of
u
converges to
0
, then we can obtain a
uniform exponential upper bound
for the convergence rate in each small region
\Omega\subset\mathcal{C}_{k}
. This in particular implies the unique continuation property even with respect to
local
convergence.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}:M\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a solution to the rescaled mean curvature flow equation* (
1.1
) and
M_{t}
can be expressed as a normal graph of a function
u(p,t)
over
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\mathbb{S}^{n-k}_{\sqrt{2(n-k)}}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k})}=0
. If
u\not\equiv 0
, then there exists an
\alpha_{0}
such that for every open set
\Omega\subset\mathcal{C}_{k}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x,t)\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu\geq C(\Omega,u)e^{-\alpha_{0}t}.

where
d\mu
is the standard measure on
\mathcal{C}_{k}
.

Remark
\the\fnum
.

One can in fact deduce the following unique continuation result: Given
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k})}\to 0
, if there exists an open subset
\Omega\subset\mathcal{C}_{k}
such that for any
\alpha>0
there is a constant
C_{\alpha}<\infty
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x,t)\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu\leq C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha t},

then we have
u\equiv 0
.

Our approach is different from the frequency function techniques used in
[
MS23
,
BHL24
,
DH25
]
or the Carleman-type estimate methods of
[
W14
,
W16
,
DH252
]
. In this paper, we employ a spectral decomposition based on eigenfunctions and use the ODE Lemma due to Filippas-Kohn
[
FK92
]
and Merle-Zaag
[
MZ98
]
as the main analytical tool. This framework exploits the spectral gap of the linearized operator
L
and product structure on cylinder, both of which reflect its underlying geometric features. For more references, see for
[
ADS19
,
ADS20
,
SX22
,
CHH22
,
CM22
,
CCS23
,
CCMS24
,
CHH24
,
ADS25
,
DZ22
,
SWX25
,
CDDHS25
]
. Moreover, our method applies to a broad class of nonlinear equations.

1.2.
Failure of unique continuation for local graphs

Our main rigidity theorems, Theorem
1.2
, require only local super-exponential convergence in order to conclude rigidity, provided that the flow is assumed to be a
global graph
over the entire cylinder.
A natural question is whether this global graphical assumption can be weakened to
local graphs
.
In this section, as our second main result, we show that this is not the case.

We construct explicit examples of rescaled mean curvature flows that are defined only as local graphs, yet converge to cylinders at arbitrarily fast super-exponential rate with their domains of definition expanding arbitrarily fast.
The graphical region can be chosen arbitrarily large, and expanding arbitrarily rapid, while the flow remains nontrivial.
This demonstrates that unique continuation at cylindrical singularities is
not
a local property.

Unlike the harmonic case, where strong unique continuation holds under very mild assumptions, the situation is dramatically different for parabolic equations.
Even for the linear heat equation, striking counterexamples arise once one allows uncontrolled growth at spatial infinity.
This classical phenomenon, discovered by Tikhonov
[
T35
]
, shows that there exist nontrivial smooth solutions
u
of the heat equation on a time half-line that vanish to infinite order at the initial time:

u(x,0)\equiv 0,\qquad\partial_{t}^{k}u(x,0)\equiv 0\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0,

yet
u\not\equiv 0
for
t\neq 0
.

One explicit example is obtained by taking a time-flat germ

\varphi(t)=e^{-1/t^{2}},\qquad\varphi(0)=0,

and defining

(1.3)

u(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{(k)}(t)\,x^{2k}}{(2k)!}.

Here all time derivatives vanish at
t=0
, so that the vanishing order at the origin is infinite, yet the resulting solution is nontrivial for
t>0
.

A natural and more challenging question is to study the unique continuation problem for
nonlinear parabolic equations. The construction by Tikhonov provides the prototype for our nonlinear generalization in Theorem
1.2
.
In the following theorem, we establish such a result for the
local solutions
to a broad class of nonlinear backward parabolic equations
whose linearized operator is the Laplacian and whose quadratic term is analytic. The construction also works for forward equation.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a real-analytic function defined in a neighborhood of
(0,0,0)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfying

\mathcal{Q}(0,0,0)=0,\qquad\nabla\mathcal{Q}(0,0,0)=0.

Then for any parameters
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\geq 1
, there exist a smooth one-parameter family of functions

\{u_{s}:U_{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}\}_{s\in[-1,1]},

such that each
u_{s}
is nontrivial,
u_{s}\not\equiv 0
, and satisfies the equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{s}=s\cdot t^{\beta}\cdot\mathcal{Q}\!\left(t^{\alpha_{1}}u_{s},\;t^{\alpha_{2}}\nabla u_{s},\;t^{\alpha_{3}}D^{2}u_{s}\right),\\[4.0pt]
u_{s}(x,0)=0,\end{cases}

on the region

(1.5)

\displaystyle U_{N}\supseteq\bigg\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1)\;\bigg|\;|x|\leq e^{\circ N}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\bigg\},

with the decay rate estimate

(1.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}|u_{s}(x,t)|\cdot e^{\circ N}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}\right)=0

where the iterated exponential
e^{\circ N}
is defined recursively by

(1.7)

\begin{split}e^{\circ 0}(x)&=x,\\
e^{\circ N}(x)&=e^{\,e^{\circ(N-1)}(x)}.\end{split}

Remark
\the\fnum
.

By taking
N
large, we can construct solutions that decay at arbitrarily fast rates on domains expanding at arbitrarily fast rates. Moreover, the same argument applies if we replace
t
by any smooth function of
t
. Note that to construct such solution defined globally is impossible due to the uniqueness result in
[
DS23
]
. Hence our result is sharp.

In particular, for the mean curvature flow over the plane,
the corresponding quadratic term

\mathcal{Q}(u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)=-\frac{D^{2}u(\nabla u,\nabla u)}{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}

satisfies the structural assumptions of Theorem
1.2
.
It is worth noting that the solution domain corresponds to a space–time region
that can expand arbitrarily fast in time under the rescaled mean curvature flow.
This observation further indicates that unique continuation
is not an inherently local property in this setting.

Consequently, for the rescaled mean curvature flow, there exist incomplete graphical solutions that converge to zero at arbitrarily fast super-exponential rates with the domain expanding arbitrarily fast.
More precisely, we have the following.

Corollary
\the\fnum
.

For any
N\geq 1
, there exists a nontrivial solution
M_{t}
to the
rescaled mean curvature flow (
1.1
) such that each
M_{t}
can be expressed as a normal graph of a function
u(x,t)
over
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\mathbb{S}^{n-k}_{\sqrt{2(n-k)}}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
k\geq 1
defined on

\displaystyle\big\{(x,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{k}\times\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;|x|\leq e^{\circ N}(\tau),\;0\leq\tau<+\infty\big\},

where the iterated exponential
e^{\circ N}
is defined as (
1.7
). Moreover, the solution satisfies the super-exponential decay estimate

(1.8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\to\infty}|u(x,\tau)|\cdot{e^{\circ N}(\tau)}=0.

Again, such local graphical constructions cannot be extended to
global solutions due to the uniqueness result as in
[
DS23
]
.

By considering local graphs on the shrinking cylindrical flow
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\sqrt{-2t}}
, we can construct infinite-dimensional families of mean curvature flow, though incomplete, developing singularity at time
0
whose singular set is
\mathbb{R}
. See also
[
W16
]
for an incomplete solution developing singularity whose singular set is a ray
(0,\infty)
.

Corollary
\the\fnum
.

There exist infinite-dimensional families of non-product mean curvature flows developing singularity at time
0
with singular set
\mathbb{R}
.

Remark
\the\fnum
.

For any
N\geq 1
, we can construct a corresponding solution to mean curvature flow, which is a graph over the region on shrinking cylinders
\{(x,t)\,\big|\,\,|x|\leq e^{\circ N}(\frac{1}{t^{2}}),\,\,-1\leq t<0\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq-1}
and the graph function has super-exponential convergence rate (
1.8
). By considering the product with more
\mathbb{R}
factors, it would provide more examples of mean curvature flows developing singular set
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where the mean curvature flow is not of the form
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\Sigma_{t}
.

The difficulty in constructing these solutions arises not only from the nonlinear nature of the equation,
but also from the ill-posedness of the backward parabolic problem.
To overcome this, we introduce a new method combining

power series expansions
with
combinatorial analysis
to construct the solution explicitly.
The essential step is to prove convergence of the resulting formal series.

Since
\mathcal{Q}
is analytic, it admits a convergent power series expansion in the variables

u
,
\nabla u
, and
D^{2}u
. Substituting this expansion into (
1.4
)
yields an iterative system of equations for the Taylor coefficients of
u
.
By writing out the recurrence relations among these coefficients,
the problem reduces to a
counting argument
stemming from the quadratic nature of
\mathcal{Q}
:
we show that the combinatorial growth of coefficients is dominated
by the super-exponential decay contributed by the base term
u_{0}
,
which we take to be a Tikhonov-type solution of the heat equation.
This combinatorial control guarantees convergence of the series and hence
the existence of the desired smooth solution.

Rescaled mean curvature flow over cylinders

In this section, we will review and prove some estimates for the quadratic term
\mathcal{Q}
for rescaled mean curvature flows equation of global graphs

From

\partial_{t}u=Lu+\mathcal{Q}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)

\displaystyle Lf=\Delta f-\frac{x}{2}\cdot\nabla f+f,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=-C(u^{2}+4u\Delta_{\theta}u+2|\nabla_{\theta}u|^{2})+\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u).

|u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}}}\leq C|u|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}}}.

\displaystyle|u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}+2}}=|u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}}}+\sum_{2p_{0}+1\leq 2k+l\leq 2p_{0}}|\nabla^{l}\partial_{t}^{k}u|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq|u|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}}}+\sum_{2p_{0}+1\leq 2k+l\leq 2p_{0}}|\nabla^{l}\partial_{t}^{k}u|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Next, we deal with the last term. Suppose
k\geq 1
. Hence by the induction hypothesis

\displaystyle|\nabla^{l}\partial_{t}^{k}u|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq|\partial_{t}u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}}}\leq C|\partial_{t}u|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}}}\leq C|u|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}+1}}.

For the case where
k=0
and
l=p_{0}+1\geq 2
, since we have that for
\Delta_{f}u=\Delta u-\langle\nabla f,\,\nabla u\rangle
,
\text{Ric}_{f}=\text{Ric}+\nabla^{2}f
and
f=\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}
, by Bochner identity we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\nabla T=\nabla\Delta_{f}T+\text{Ric}_{f}(\nabla T).

So we would have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{l}u|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int\langle\nabla^{l-1}u,\,\Delta_{f}\nabla^{l-1}u\rangle d\mu\leq\int\langle\nabla^{l-1}u,\,\nabla^{l-1}\Delta_{f}u\rangle d\mu+C|u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\langle\Delta_{f}\nabla^{l-2}u,\,\nabla^{l-2}\Delta_{f}u\rangle d\mu+C|u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\langle\nabla^{l-2}\Delta_{f}u,\,\nabla^{l-2}\Delta_{f}u\rangle d\mu+C|u|^{2}_{H^{2p_{0}}}\leq|Lu|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}-1}}+|u|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}}}\leq C|u|^{2}_{V^{p_{0}+1}}.

Hence we finish the induction step. Then we prove the both sides and the proof is now finished.
∎

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

Let

Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}\partial_{i}(ue^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}})d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}(\partial_{i}u)e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}\frac{ux_{i}}{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mu.

4.
Counter-examples

In this section, we construct counterexamples
to the unique continuation problem for the local solutions to the nonlinear parabolic equations.
Our starting point is Tikhonov’s classical counterexample for the heat equation,
which we extend to a nonlinear setting by constructing an analytic family of
solutions via power series expansion.

The underlying difficulty lies in the nonlinear structure of the equation and the ill-posed-ness of the backward equation.
Unlike the linear heat equation, for which Tikhonov’s classical construction
already reveals the failure of backward uniqueness, the nonlinear backward equation introduces strong coupling between the derivatives of
u
,
and no integral representation such as the Duhamel formula can be applied directly.
As a result, we construct solutions by hand, using formal expansions and delicate control of their convergence.

Our approach is based on an power-series construction
combined with a quantitative combinatorial analysis. The nonlinearity creates a hierarchy of nonlinear interactions among the coefficients
in the series, and bounding their growth requires precise counting arguments. We show that, thanks to the quadratic nature of the nonlinear term
\mathcal{Q}(u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)
, the combinatorial complexity can be dominated by the super-exponential decay
inherited from the base Tikhonov solution of the heat equation. This ensures that the formal series converges, yielding a genuine smooth solution
with the prescribed super-exponential behavior.

For clarity of exposition, we first review the Tikhonov’s examples for solutions to heat equation with super-exponential decay and generalize it to arbitrary decay rate. Then we study the nonlinear equation. We first present the proof in the simplest case, where the nonlinearity is quadratic, and then consider the case where the non-linear term only depends on
u^{\prime}
. We will prove the general case in the next section,

4.1.

.

|u_{0}(x,t)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}\sum_{k}\frac{k!x^{2k}}{(\varepsilon t)^{k}(2k)!}\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}\sum_{k}\frac{x^{2k}}{(\varepsilon t)^{k}k!}\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}+\frac{x^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}.

Then for any
R>0
, there exists some
T>0
such that
Let
P_{2}\equiv[-2,2]\times[0,T]
where
T
is small enough, we have

(4.3)

\sup_{[-R,R]\times[0,T]}|u_{0}(x,t)|\leq\exp(-\frac{1}{2t^{2}}).

We observe that such kind of construction works for general function
\varphi
, with even faster decay rate. Let us set
f_{0}=t^{2}
and
f_{N+1}=e^{-\frac{1}{f_{N}}}
,
N\geq 0
. From Lemma
C
we have that

\displaystyle|f_{N}^{(i)}|\leq i!f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}(\frac{2}{t}(\frac{8}{\varepsilon})^{N-1}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{f_{j}^{\varepsilon}})^{i}.

As in the discussions above, let us denote

\displaystyle g_{N}=\frac{2}{t}(\frac{8}{\varepsilon})^{N-1}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{f_{j}^{\varepsilon}}.

Then let us consider new Tikhonov type counter-examples with

\displaystyle\varphi_{N}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{N}(t),&\,t\geq 0\\
0,&\,t<0.\end{array}\right.

So we would have that

\displaystyle|\varphi_{N}^{(i)}|\leq i!(g_{N})^{i}f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}.

Note that for
N=1
, this corresponds to the classical Tikhonov example. We will use this family of examples to construct our solution with prescribed convergence rate of
u
and expanding rate of the domain.

4.2.
Quadratic nonlinear term

In this subsection, we will construct the solutions to non-linear backward heat equation, using power series. First we consider the toy model case:

(1)

the equation is defined on
(-R,R)\times[0,T]\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(2)

\vec{\alpha}=\vec{0}
and
\beta=0
;

(3)

\mathcal{Q}(u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime})=u^{2}
.

Hence
u
is a solution to the following equation in
(-R,R)\times[0,T]

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}+\partial^{2}_{xx})u_{s}&=s\cdot u_{s}^{2},\\
u_{s}(x,0)&=0.\end{cases}

Formally, we expand
u
in
s,x
and
t
to get

\displaystyle u_{s}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}u^{(j)}(x,t)\frac{s^{i}}{i!}=\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{\infty}u^{(i,j)}(t)\frac{x^{i}}{i!}\frac{s^{j}}{j!}\equiv\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{\infty}V^{i,j}(t)x^{i}s^{j},

where for simplicity we define

V^{i,j}(t)=\frac{u^{(i,j)}(t)}{i!j!}.

We set

(1)

For any
j\geq 1
,

u^{(0,j)}(t)=u^{(1,j)}(t)\equiv 0.

(2)

For
j=0
,

\displaystyle u^{(2i,0)}(t)=(-1)^{i}\varphi^{(i)}(t),

Recall that

\varphi(t)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/t^{2}},&t>0\\
0,&t\leq 0.\end{cases}

By the estimates (
4.2
) (see
[
J71
]
) we have that

(4.5)

\displaystyle|V^{2i,0}|

\displaystyle=\frac{|u^{(2i,0)}|}{(2i)!}=\frac{|\varphi^{(i)}|}{(2i)!}\leq C_{\epsilon}\frac{i!}{(2i)!}\frac{1}{(\varepsilon t)^{i}}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{t^{2}}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}V^{2i,0}|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}u^{(2i,0)}|}{(2i)!}(t)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\frac{(i+m)!}{(2i)!}\frac{1}{(\varepsilon t)^{i+m}}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{t^{2}}},

for any
i\geq 0
and
0<\epsilon<1
.

Plugging the power series to the equation implies

\displaystyle V^{2i+2,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2i+2)(2i+1)}\Bigg(-d_{t}V^{2i,j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}V^{2i_{2},j_{2}}\Bigg).

Note that

\displaystyle d_{t}V^{2i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2i)(2i-1)}d_{t}\Bigg(-d_{t}V^{2i-2,j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i-1\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}V^{2i_{2},j_{2}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2i)(2i-1)}\Bigg(-[d_{t}]^{2}V^{2i-2,j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i-1\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(d_{t}V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}\Big)\cdot V^{2i_{2},j_{2}}+V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}\cdot\Big(d_{t}V^{2i_{2},j_{2}}\Big)\Bigg).

We can iterate to eliminate the term
[d_{t}]^{*}V^{*,j}
using our assumption
V^{0,j}\equiv V^{1,j}\equiv 0
, to obtain the following:

(4.7)

\displaystyle V^{i,j}=\sum_{s=0}^{\lfloor i/2\rfloor-1}\frac{(i-2-2s)!}{i!}[-d_{t}]^{s}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i-2-2s\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(V^{i_{1},j_{1}}\cdot V^{i_{2},j_{2}}\Big)\Bigg).

It follows directly that for any fixed
m

\displaystyle[d_{t}]^{m}V^{i,j}=\sum_{s=0}^{\lfloor i/2\rfloor-1}\frac{(i-2-2s)!}{i!}[-d_{t}]^{s+m}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i-2-2s\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(V^{i_{1},j_{1}}\cdot V^{i_{2},j_{2}}\Big)\Bigg).

By induction we can easily see that

(1)

V^{2i+1,j}\equiv 0
for any
i,j
.

(2)

V^{i,j}\equiv 0
for any
i<2j
.

We define

(4.8)

W^{2i,j}_{m}\equiv\frac{(2i)!}{(2i+2m)!}\Big|[d_{t}]^{m}\cdot V^{2i.j}\Big|.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

\displaystyle W^{2i,j}_{m}\leq\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1}\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+m_{1}+m_{2}=i+m-1}W^{2i_{1},j_{i}}_{m_{1}}\cdot W^{2i_{2},j_{2}}_{m_{2}}

Proof.

Fix any
m
, we can compute

\displaystyle\quad[d_{t}]^{m}\cdot V^{2i,j}

\displaystyle=\pm\sum_{s=0}^{i-1}\frac{(2i-2-2s)!}{(2i)!}[d_{t}]^{s+m}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i-1-s\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}(V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}\cdot V^{2i_{2},j_{2}})\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\pm\sum_{s=m}^{i+m-1}\frac{(2i+2m-2-2s)!}{(2i)!}[d_{t}]^{s}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i+m-1-s\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}(V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}\cdot V^{2i_{2},j_{2}})\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\pm\sum_{s=m}^{i+m-1}\frac{(2i+2m-2-2s)!}{(2i)!}\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}=s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i+m-1-s\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}\frac{s!}{s_{1}!\cdot s_{2}!}\Big([d_{t}]^{s_{1}}V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}\Big)\cdot\Big([d_{t}]^{s_{2}}V^{2i_{2},j_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2i+2m-2)!}{(2i)!}\sum_{s=m}^{i+m-1}\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}=s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=i+m-1-s\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{(2i_{1})!}{(2i_{1}+2s_{1})!}\Big|[d_{t}]^{s_{1}}V^{2i_{1},j_{1}}\Big|\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{(2i_{2})!}{(2i_{2}+2s_{2})!}\Big|[d_{t}]^{s_{2}}V^{2i_{2},j_{2}}\Big|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2i+2m)!}{(2i)!}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j-1}\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+m_{1}+m_{2}=i+m-1}W^{2i_{1},j_{1}}_{m_{1}}\cdot W^{2i_{2},j_{2}}_{m_{2}},

where in the last inequality we use

\displaystyle\quad\frac{(2i_{1}+2s_{1})!}{(2i_{1})!}\cdot\frac{(2i_{2}+2s_{2})!}{(2i_{2})!}\cdot\frac{s!}{s_{1}!\cdot s_{2}!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2i_{1}+2s_{1})!}{(2i_{1})!}\cdot\frac{(2i_{2}+2s_{2})!}{(2i_{2})!}\cdot\frac{(2s)!}{(2s_{1})!\cdot(2s_{2})!}

\displaystyle=\binom{2i_{1}+2s_{1}}{2s_{1}}\cdot\binom{2i_{2}+2s_{2}}{2s_{2}}\cdot(2s)!

\displaystyle\leq\binom{2i_{1}+2i_{2}+2s_{1}+2s_{2}}{2s_{1}+2s_{2}}\cdot(2s)!

\displaystyle=\frac{(2i+2m-2)!}{(2i+2m-2-2s)!\cdot(2s)!}\cdot(2s)!

\displaystyle=\frac{(2i+2m-2)!}{(2i+2m-2-2s)!}

∎

We now simplify the upper bound as multiplication of
W^{2i_{k},0}_{m_{k}}
. Counting the multiplicity of each term is equivalent to counting the number of ways of decomposition, which in turn corresponds to counting the number of rooted trees with exactly
j
branching nodes, where the branching nodes have
k_{m}
branches, for
1\leq m\leq j
. From combinatorics, it is well-known that such number is controlled by the Catalan number. For more discussions on Catlan number, see subsection
B.1
. This motivates the following lemma which we prove by induction. A similar idea will later be used to handle the general terms, though the technical details there are considerably more delicate.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

\displaystyle W^{2i,j}_{m}\leq Cat(j)\sum_{\sum_{k=0}^{j}i_{k}+m_{k}=i+m-j}\Big(\prod_{k=0}^{j}W^{2i_{k},0}_{m_{k}}\Big),

where
Cat(j)
is the
j
-th Catalan number.

Proof.

We have proved the case
j=1
since the
0
-th Catalan number
Cat(0)=1
. We assume that the result is true for any
1\leq j<j_{0}
. Then by previous Lemma we have

\displaystyle W^{2i,j_{0}}_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j_{0}-1}\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+m_{1}+m_{2}=i+m-1}W^{2i_{1},j_{i}}_{m_{1}}\cdot W^{2i_{2},j_{2}}_{m_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j_{0}-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+m_{1}+m_{2}\\
=i+m-1\end{subarray}}\Big(Cat(j_{1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{k=0}^{j_{1}}(i^{1}_{k}+m^{1}_{k})\\
=i_{1}+m_{1}-j_{1}\end{subarray}}(\prod_{k=0}^{j_{1}}W^{2i^{1}_{k},0}_{m^{1}_{k}})\Big)\cdot\Big(Cat(j_{2})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{k=0}^{j_{2}}(i^{2}_{k}+m^{2}_{k})\\
=i_{2}+m_{2}-j_{2}\end{subarray}}(\prod_{k=0}^{j_{2}}W^{2i^{2}_{k},0}_{m^{2}_{k}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j_{0}-1}Cat(j_{1})\cdot Cat(j_{2})\sum_{\sum_{k=0}^{j_{0}}i_{k}+m_{k}=i-j_{0}}\prod_{k=0}^{j_{0}}W^{2i_{k},0}_{m_{k}}

\displaystyle=Cat(j_{0})\sum_{\sum_{k=0}^{j_{0}}i_{k}+m_{k}=i-j_{0}}\prod_{k=0}^{j_{0}}W^{2i_{k},0}_{m_{k}},

where we use the structure result for Catalan number
\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j_{0}-1}Cat(j_{1})\cdot Cat(j_{2})=Cat(j_{0})
. This finishes the proof by induction.
∎

Recall that we have the estimate for Catalan number
Cat(j)

Cat(j)=\frac{1}{j+1}\binom{2j}{j}\leq 4^{j}.

In particular, by Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
B.2
we have

\displaystyle V^{2i,j}=W^{2i,j}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq Cat(j)\sum_{\sum_{k}i_{k}+m_{k}=i-j}\Big(\prod_{k=0}^{j}\frac{(2i_{k})!}{(2i_{k}+2m_{k})!}[d_{t}]^{m_{k}}\cdot V^{2i_{k},0}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{j+1}\frac{e^{-\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{i-j}}\sum_{\sum_{k}i_{k}+m_{k}=i-j}\Big(\prod_{k=0}^{j}\frac{(i_{k}+m_{k})!}{(2i_{k}+2m_{k})!}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{j+1}\frac{e^{-\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{i-j}}C^{i-j}\frac{(j+1)^{(i-j)}}{(i-j)!}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{j+1}\cdot\frac{e^{-\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{i-j}}\cdot\frac{(C(j+1))^{(i-j)}}{(i-j)!}

Then we can evaluate
u_{s}(x,t)
at
s=1
: since
V^{2i,j}\equiv 0
for any
i<j
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{i\geq j}V^{2i,j}x^{2i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}C_{\varepsilon}^{j+1}e^{-\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}x^{2j}\sum_{i\geq j}\frac{(C(j+1))^{i-j}}{(i-j)!}\frac{x^{2(i-j)}}{(\varepsilon t)^{i-j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}C_{\varepsilon}^{j+1}e^{-\frac{(j+1)\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}x^{2j}\exp{(\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t}\cdot(j+1))}

\displaystyle\leq\exp{(-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t})}\sum_{j}\Bigg(C^{2}_{\varepsilon}x^{2}\exp{(-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t})}\Bigg)^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp{(-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t})}}{1-C^{2}_{\varepsilon}x^{2}\exp{(-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t})}}.

We can choose
|x|^{2}\leq\frac{c(\varepsilon)}{|t|}
for some small
c(\varepsilon)>0
so that
C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{c(\varepsilon)}{|t|}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cc(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon t^{2}}}<1/2
for
|t|\leq 1
. Then this series converges. So we can define

\displaystyle u_{1}(x,t)=\sum_{j}\sum_{i}V^{2i,j}x^{2i}.

And as
u_{1}(0,t)=V^{0,0}=e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}
, we can see that it is a nontrivial solution of (
4.4
) which also has the desired decay rate.

4.3.
Intermediate case

Let us then consider the case that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}(u^{\prime}).

Then in this case, we have

\displaystyle\frac{dV^{(i,j)}}{dt}+(i+1)(i+2)V^{(i+2,j)}=\sum_{i_{2}\geq 2}\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{(0,i_{2},0)}}{i_{2}!}(0,\textbf{0},\textbf{0})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits i_{2,k_{2}}=i\\
\sum\limits j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1).

We set
V^{0,j}=V^{1,j}\equiv 0
for any
j\geq 1
as before. Then as (
4.7
) we have

V^{i,j}=\sum_{i_{2}\geq 2}\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{(0,i_{2},0)}}{i_{2}!}(0,\textbf{0},\textbf{0})\sum_{s=1}^{[i/2]}\frac{(i-2s)!}{i!}[-d_{t}]^{s-1}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits i_{2,k_{2}}=i-2s\\
\sum\limits j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Bigg).

We define

W^{i,j}_{m}\equiv\frac{i!}{(i+2m-1)!}[d_{t}]^{m}\cdot V^{i,j}.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

W^{i,j}_{m}\leq\sum_{i_{2}\geq 2}C_{Q}^{i_{2}}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum i_{2,k_{2}}+2m_{k_{2}}=i+2m-2\\
\sum j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}W^{i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}}}_{m_{k_{2}}}.

Proof.

By the analyticity of
\mathcal{Q}
we have
\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{(0,i_{2},0)}}{i_{2}!}(0,\textbf{0},\textbf{0})\leq C_{Q}^{i_{2}}
for any
i_{2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle[d_{t}]^{m}\cdot V^{i,j}\leq\sum_{i_{2}\geq 2}C_{Q}^{i_{2}}\sum_{s=0}^{[\frac{i}{2}]-1}\frac{(i-2-2s)!}{i!}[d_{t}]^{s+m}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits i_{2,k_{2}}=i-2-2s\\
\sum\limits j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Bigg).

For each
i_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{s=0}^{[\frac{i}{2}]-1}\frac{(i-2-2s)!}{i!}[d_{t}]^{s+m}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits i_{2,k_{2}}=i-2-2s\\
\sum\limits j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=m}^{[\frac{i}{2}]+m-1}\frac{(i+2m-2-2s)!}{i!}[d_{t}]^{s}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits i_{2,k_{2}}=i+2m-2-2s\\
\sum\limits j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=m}^{[\frac{i}{2}]+m-1}\frac{(i+2m-2-2s)!}{i!}\sum_{\sum_{k_{2}}s_{k_{2}}=s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits i_{2,k_{2}}=i+2m-2-2s\\
\sum\limits j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\frac{s!}{\prod_{k_{2}}s_{k_{2}}!}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{i_{2}}[d_{t}]^{s_{k_{2}}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(i+2m-2)!}{i!}\sum_{s=m}^{[\frac{i}{2}]+m-1}\sum_{\sum_{k_{2}}s_{k_{2}}=s}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum i_{2,k_{2}}=i+2m-2-2s\\
\sum j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}\Big(\frac{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)!}{(i_{2,k_{2}}+2s_{k_{2}})!}[d_{t}]^{s_{k_{2}}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}})}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(i+2m-1)!}{i!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum i_{2,k_{2}}+2s_{k_{2}}=i+2m-2\\
\sum j_{2,k_{2}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}W^{i_{2,k_{2}}+1,j_{2,k_{2}}}_{s_{k_{2}}}.

In the first inequality we use

\displaystyle\quad\prod_{k_{2}}\frac{(i_{k_{2}}+2s_{k_{2}})!}{i_{k_{2}}!}\frac{s!}{\prod_{k_{2}}s_{k_{2}}!}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k_{2}}\frac{(i_{k_{2}}+2s_{k_{2}})!}{i_{k_{2}}!}\frac{(2s)!}{\prod_{k_{2}}(2s_{k_{2}})!}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k_{2}}\frac{(i_{k_{2}}+2s_{k_{2}})!}{i_{k_{2}}!\cdot(2s_{k_{2}})!}\cdot(2s)!

\displaystyle\leq\binom{\sum_{k_{2}}i_{k_{2}}+2s_{k_{2}}}{\sum_{k_{2}}2s_{k_{2}}}\cdot(2s)!

\displaystyle=\frac{(i+2m-2)!}{(i+2m-2-2s)!}.

The proof is now finished.
∎

Note that every time we break each term
W^{i,j}_{s}
into more pieces, the sum on indices
j
will decrease by 1 and the number of the resulting terms
W^{*,*}_{*}
will increase by
\alpha_{m}-1
for some
\alpha_{m}\geq 2
, with the sum of the indices
i
and
s
decreasing by
1
. Then we can continue until we lead to the base blocks
W^{*,0}_{*}
.

Then the proof will be divided into two parts. First we need to count the multiplicity of each combination of base blocks
W^{*,0}_{*}
. Then we need to prove the estimate for each combination.

To achieve the first goal, we state the decomposition in the following Lemma, where
l
is used to denoted
\sum_{m}\alpha_{m}
. Hence the number of base blocks is equal to
L=l-j+1
.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

W^{i,j}_{s}\leq\sum_{L\geq j+1}C_{Q}^{L+j-1}\cdot Cat(L+j)\cdot\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i+2s-j-1}\prod_{n=1}^{L}{W_{s_{n}}^{i_{n}+1,0}}

Proof.

We will prove the Lemma by induction. The base case
j=1
is finished by the previous Lemma
4.3
. Then we assume the result is true for any
j<j_{0}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle W^{i,j_{0}}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}C_{Q}^{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum i_{k}+2s_{k}=i+2s-2\\
\sum j_{k}=j_{0}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{\alpha}W^{i_{k}+1,j_{k}}_{s_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}C_{Q}^{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum i_{k}+2s_{k}=i+2s-2\\
\sum j_{k}=j_{0}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{\alpha}\Bigg(\sum_{L_{k}\geq j_{k}+1}C_{Q}^{L_{k}+j_{k}-1}\cdot Cat(L_{k}+j_{k})\cdot\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{k,n}+2s_{k,n}=i_{k}+2s_{k}-j_{k}}\prod_{n=1}^{L_{k}}{W_{s_{k,n}}^{i_{k,n}+1,0}}\Bigg)

Set
L\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}L_{k}
. Since each
L_{k}\geq j_{k}+1\geq 1
, we have
2\leq\alpha\leq L
and
L\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha}(j_{k}+1)\geq j_{0}-1+\alpha\geq j_{0}+1
. Hence, if we sum on
L
then

\displaystyle W^{i,j_{0}}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{L\geq j_{0}+1}C_{Q}^{L+j_{0}-1}\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}^{L}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{k}L_{k}=L\\
\sum_{k}j_{k}=j_{0}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{\alpha}Cat(L_{k}+j_{k})\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i+2s-j_{0}-1}\prod_{n=1}^{L}W^{i_{n}+1,0}_{s_{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{L\geq j_{0}+1}C_{Q}^{L+j_{0}-1}Cat(L+J)\cdot\Big(\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i+2s-j_{0}-1}\prod_{n=1}^{L}W^{i_{n}+1,0}_{s_{n}}\Big),

where in the last inequality we use the recursive formula for Catalan numbers, see Lemma
B.1
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}^{L}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{k}L_{k}=L\\
\sum_{k}j_{k}=j_{0}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{k=1}^{\alpha}Cat(L_{k}+j_{k})\Big)\leq\sum_{\alpha\geq 2}^{L+j_{0}-1}\Big(\sum_{\sum_{k}(L_{k}+j_{k})=L+j_{0}-1}\prod_{k=1}^{\alpha}Cat(L_{k}+j_{k})\Big)\leq Cat(L+j_{0}).

∎

Then let we estimate each
W^{i+1,0}_{s}
. Since
V^{i+1,0}=0
if
i+1
is odd, we can assume
i+1
is even.

\displaystyle W^{i+1,0}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{(i+1)!}{(i+2s)!}[d_{t}]^{s}V^{i+1,0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(i+1)!}{(i+2s)!}\cdot\Big(\frac{(\frac{i+1}{2}+s)!}{(i+1)!}C_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i+1}{2}+s}}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{t^{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i+1}{2}+s}}\cdot\frac{(\frac{i+1}{2}+s)!}{(i+2s)!}.

Then by Lemma
B.2
, noting that
\sum_{n}\frac{i_{n}+1}{2}+s_{n}=\frac{i-j-1+(l-j+1)}{2}=\frac{i+l-2j}{2}
,

\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{l-j+1}\frac{(\frac{i_{n}+1}{2}+s_{n})!}{(i_{n}+2s_{n})!}\leq C^{i+l-2j}\cdot\frac{(l-j+1)^{\frac{i+l-2j}{2}}}{[\frac{i+l-2j}{2}]!}.

and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i-j-1}\prod_{n=1}^{l-j+1}W^{i_{n}+1,0}_{s_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i-j-1}\prod_{n=1}^{l-j+1}\Big(\frac{C_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i_{n}+1}{2}+s_{n}}}\cdot\frac{(\frac{i_{n}+1}{2}+s_{n})!}{(i_{n}+2s_{n})!}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}^{l-j+1}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}\cdot(l-j+1)}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i+l-2j}{2}}}\cdot\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i-j-1}\prod_{n=1}^{l-j+1}\frac{(\frac{i_{n}+1}{2}+s_{n})!}{(i_{n}+2s_{n})!}

Since
l\geq 2j
, we can define
k=l-2j\geq 0
to simplify the series

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{i}V^{i,j}x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i}W^{i,j}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\sum_{l\geq 2j}C^{l}_{Q}\cdot Cat(l+1)\cdot Cat(j)\cdot|x|^{i}\cdot\sum_{\sum_{n}i_{n}+2s_{n}=i-j-1}\prod_{n=1}^{l-j+1}W^{i_{n}+1,0}_{s_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}C^{j}\sum_{k\geq 0}C^{k}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}(j+k+1)}\sum_{i}\frac{(C|x|)^{i}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i+k}{2}}}\cdot\frac{(j+k+1)^{\frac{i+k}{2}}}{[\frac{i+k}{2}]!}.

We simply consider
|x|\geq 1
so that
(C|x|)^{i}\leq(C|x|)^{i+k}
. Otherwise we just use the trivial bound
(C|x|)^{i}\leq C^{i+k}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{i}V^{i,j}x^{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}C^{j}\sum_{k\geq 0}C^{k}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}(j+k+1)}e^{\frac{C(j+k+1)|x|^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C|x|^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}\sum_{j}\Big(Ce^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C|x|^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}\Big)^{j}\cdot\sum_{k}\Big(Ce^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C|x|^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}\Big)^{k}

We can choose
|x|^{2}\leq\frac{c}{|t|}
for some small
c(Q)>0
so that
Ce^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C|x|^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}<1/2
. Then this series converges. So we can define

\displaystyle u_{1}(x,t)=\sum_{j}\sum_{i}V^{i,j}x^{i}.

And as
u_{1}(0,t)=V^{0,0}=e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}
, we can see that it is a nontrivial solution of (
4.9
).

5.
General construction

In this section, we prove the Theorem
1.2
, where we construct the solutions to the following general equation

(5.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{s}+u_{s}^{\prime\prime}=s\cdot t^{\theta_{0}}\mathcal{Q}(t^{\theta_{1}}u,t^{{\theta_{2}}}u^{\prime},t^{\theta_{3}}u^{\prime\prime}),

where
\mathcal{Q}
is analytic around
(0,0,0)
with
\mathcal{Q}(0,0,0)=|\nabla\mathcal{Q}(0,0,0)|=0
. In particular, we have
|\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{(i_{0},i_{1},i_{2})}}{i_{0}!i_{1}!i_{2}!}(0,0,0)|\leq C_{Q}^{i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}}
for some constant
C_{Q}
. In the first subsection, we construct the solutions out of the standard Tikhonov example with decay rate
e^{-\frac{1}{t^{2}}}
. We will generalize this in the second subsection, where we obtain the solutions with arbitrary decay rate.

5.1.
Special region

Similarly as before, we set the following.

\displaystyle u_{s}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}u^{(j)}(x,t)\frac{s^{i}}{i!}=\sum\limits_{i,j=0}^{\infty}u^{(i,j)}(t)\frac{x^{i}}{i!}\frac{s^{j}}{j!},

with
V^{i,j}(t)=\frac{u^{(i,j)}}{i!j!}(t)
. Moreover,
V^{0,j}=V^{1,j}=0
for any
j\geq 1
. For
j=0
, we set

\displaystyle u^{(2i,0)}(t)=(-1)^{i}\varphi^{(i)}(t),

with

\varphi(t)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/t^{2}},&t>0\\
0,&t\leq 0.\end{cases}

By comparing the coefficients, we have the following equation

\displaystyle\frac{dV^{(i,j)}}{dt}+(i+1)(i+2)V^{(i+2,j)}=\sum_{i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\geq 0}\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{(i_{0},i_{1},i_{2})}}{i_{0}!i_{1}!i_{2}!}(0,\textbf{0},\textbf{0})\cdot t^{\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}i_{0}+\theta_{2}i_{1}+\theta_{3}i_{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}=i\\
\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\prod_{k_{0}=1}^{i_{0}}V^{(i_{0,k_{0}},j_{0,k_{0}})}\Big)\cdot\Big(\prod_{k_{1}=1}^{i_{1}}V^{(i_{1,k_{1}}+1,j_{1,k_{1}})}(i_{1,k_{1}}+1)\Big)\cdot\Big(\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+2,j_{2,k_{2}})}(i_{2,k_{2}}+2)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Big).

Then similarly as in the simple case, we can write
V^{i,j}
as follows

\displaystyle V^{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\geq 0}\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{(i_{0},i_{1},i_{2})}}{i_{0}!i_{1}!i_{2}!}(0,\textbf{0},\textbf{0})\cdot\sum_{s=0}^{[\frac{i}{2}]-1}\frac{(i-2-2s)!}{i!}[-d_{t}]^{s}\Bigg(t^{-\Theta}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}=i-2-2s\\
\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\prod_{k_{0}=1}^{i_{0}}V^{(i_{0,k_{0}},j_{0,k_{0}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{k_{1}=1}^{i_{1}}V^{(i_{1,k_{1}}+1,j_{1,k_{1}})}(i_{1,k_{1}}+1)\Big)\cdot\Big(\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+2,j_{2,k_{2}})}(i_{2,k_{2}}+2)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Big)\Bigg).

For simplicity we write
\Theta\equiv-(\theta_{0}+\theta_{1}i_{0}+\theta_{2}i_{1}+\theta_{3}i_{2})
.

In the following we consider the small time interval, say
0<t\leq 1
.
Since we want to find an upper bound for
|V^{i,j}|
, we could simplify the notation by considering the case
\theta_{i}<0
and thus
\Theta>0
. Since
i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}\geq 2
, we can also choose some large
\theta>0
such that
\Theta\leq\theta(i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}).

We define

W^{i,j}_{s}\equiv\frac{i!}{(i+2s-2)!}\Big|[d_{t}]^{s}V^{i,j}\Big|.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

W^{i,j}_{n}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\geq 0\\
i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}\geq 2\end{subarray}}(\frac{C}{t})^{\theta(i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2})}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2s_{0}+\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k+m}=i+2n-2\\
\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{s_{0}!\cdot C^{s_{0}}}{(2s_{0})!\cdot t^{s_{0}}}\cdot\prod_{m,k_{m}}W^{i_{m,k_{m}}+m,j_{m,k_{m}}}_{s_{m,k_{m}}}\Big).

Proof.

We follow the proof of Lemma
4.8
.

We use
|\frac{Q^{(i_{0},i_{1},i_{2})}}{i_{0}!i_{1}!i_{2}!}(0,\textbf{0},\textbf{0})|\leq C^{i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}}
to obtain

\displaystyle[d_{t}]^{n}\cdot V^{i,j}\leq\sum_{i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\geq 0}C^{i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}}\cdot\sum_{s=0}^{[\frac{i}{2}]-1}\frac{(i-2-2s)!}{i!}\Bigg|[-d_{t}]^{s+n}\Bigg(t^{-\Theta}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}=i-2-2s\\
\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\prod_{k_{0}=1}^{i_{0}}V^{(i_{0,k_{0}},j_{0,k_{0}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\Big(\prod_{k_{1}=1}^{i_{1}}V^{(i_{1,k_{1}}+1,j_{1,k_{1}})}(i_{1,k_{1}}+1)\Big)\cdot\Big(\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+2,j_{2,k_{2}})}(i_{2,k_{2}}+2)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Big)\Bigg)\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\geq 0}C_{Q}^{i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}}\cdot\sum_{s=n}^{[\frac{i}{2}]+n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}=i+2n-2-2s\\
\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=j-1\\
s_{0}+\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}s_{m,k_{m}}=s\end{subarray}}\frac{(i+2n-2-2s)!}{i!}\cdot\frac{s!}{s_{0}!\prod s_{m,k_{m}}!}\Bigg|\Big([d_{t}]^{s_{0}}\cdot t^{-\Theta}\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{k_{0}=1}^{i_{0}}[d_{s}]^{s_{0,k_{0}}}V^{(i_{0,k_{0}},j_{0,k_{0}})}\Big)\Big(\prod_{k_{1}=1}^{i_{1}}[d_{s}]^{s_{1,k_{1}}}V^{(i_{1,k_{1}}+1,j_{1,k_{1}})}(i_{1,k_{1}}+1)\Big)\Big(\prod_{k_{2}=1}^{i_{2}}[d_{s}]^{s_{2,k_{2}}}V^{(i_{2,k_{2}}+2,j_{2,k_{2}})}(i_{2,k_{2}}+2)(i_{2,k_{2}}+1)\Big)\Bigg|.

We claim that:

\displaystyle\frac{(i+2n-2-2s)!}{i!}\cdot\frac{s!}{s_{0}!\prod s_{m,k_{m}}!}\leq\frac{(i+2n-2)!}{i!}\cdot\frac{1}{(2s_{0})!}\cdot\prod\frac{i_{m,k_{m}}!}{(i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k_{m}})!}.

With this claim we can easily prove that

W^{i,j}_{n}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\geq 0\\
i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}\geq 2\end{subarray}}C_{Q}^{i_{0}+i_{1}+i_{2}}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2s_{0}+\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k+m}=i+2n-2\\
\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=j-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{[d_{t}]^{s_{0}}\cdot t^{-\Theta}}{(2s_{0})!}\cdot\prod_{m,k_{m}}W^{i_{m,k_{m}}+m,j_{m,k_{m}}}_{s_{m,k_{m}}}\Big).

Then we use the following inequality: Since
\Theta>0

\frac{[d_{t}]^{s_{0}}\cdot t^{-\Theta}}{(2s_{0})!}\leq\frac{(\Theta+s_{0}-1)!}{(\Theta-1)!(2s_{0})!t^{\Theta+s_{0}}}\leq\frac{s_{0}!}{(2s_{0})!}(\frac{C}{t})^{\Theta+s_{0}}.

The the proof will be finished if we prove the claim as follows:

\displaystyle\quad\prod\frac{(i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k_{m}})!}{i_{m,k_{m}}!}\cdot\frac{s!}{s_{0}!\prod s_{m,k_{m}}!}

\displaystyle\leq\prod\frac{(i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k_{m}})!}{i_{m,k_{m}}!}\cdot\frac{(2s)!}{(2s_{0})!\prod(2s_{m,k_{m}})!}

\displaystyle=\prod\binom{i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k_{m}}}{2s_{m,k_{m}}}\cdot\frac{(2s)!}{(2s_{0})!}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{\sum i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k_{m}}}{\sum 2s_{m,k_{m}}}\cdot\frac{(2s)!}{(2s_{0})!}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{i+2n-2}{2s}\cdot\frac{(2s)!}{(2s_{0})!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(i+2n-2)!}{(i+2n-2-2s)!(2s_{0})!}.

∎

Then we can decompose

into the base blocks
W^{*,0}_{*}
.

Lemma

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{L\geq 2J}(\frac{C}{t})^{\theta\cdot L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{0}+L_{1}+L_{2}=L\\

j_{0}

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{0}\geq 2\\

\sum_{m=0}^{2}\sum_{k_{m}=0}^{j_{0,m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=J-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{p=0}^{2}l_{m,k_{m};p}=l_{m,k_{m}}\\
\sum_{p=0}^{2}j_{m,k_{m};p}=j_{m,k_{m}}-1\\
\sum_{m=0}^{2}\sum_{k_{m}=0}^{j_{0,m}}l_{m,k_{m};p}=L_{p}-l_{0,p}\\
\sum_{m=0}^{2}\sum_{k_{m}=0}^{j_{0,m}}j_{m,k_{m};p}=J_{p}-j_{0,p}\\
p=0,1,2\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=0}^{2}\prod_{k_{m}=1}^{j_{0,m}}T^{m,k_{m}}_{l,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{l_{0}\geq 2}\sum_{\sum_{m,k_{m}}l_{m,k_{m}}=L-l_{0}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}Cat(l_{m,k_{m}}+1)\Big]\cdot\Big[\sum_{j_{0}\geq 1}\sum_{\sum_{m,k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=J-1}\prod_{m,k_{m}}Cat(j_{m,k_{m}})\Big]\cdot

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{\sum_{p}l_{0,p}=l_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m,k_{m}}l_{m,k_{m};p}=L_{p}-l_{p}\\
p=0,1,2;\\
\sum_{p}l_{m,k_{m};p}=l_{m,k_{m}}\end{subarray}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}\frac{l_{m,k_{m}}!}{l_{m,k_{m};0}!l_{m,k_{m};1}!l_{m,k_{m};2}!}\Big]\cdot

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{\sum_{p}j_{0,p}=j_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m,k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m};p}=J_{p}-j_{p}\\
p=0,1,2;\\
\sum_{p}j_{m,k_{m};p}=j_{m,k_{m}}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}\frac{(j_{m,k_{m}}-1)!}{j_{m,k_{m};0}!j_{m,k_{m};1}!j_{m,k_{m};2}!}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Cat(L+1)\cdot Cat(J)\cdot\frac{L!}{L_{0}!L_{1}!L_{2}!}\cdot\frac{(J-1)!}{J_{0}!J_{1}!J_{2}!}.

.

To prove this claim, we firstly note that by Lemma
B.1
, since
j_{0}\leq l_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{0}\geq 2}\sum_{\sum_{m,k_{m}}l_{m,k_{m}}=L-l_{0}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}Cat(l_{m,k_{m}}+1)=\sum_{l_{0}\geq 2}\sum_{\sum_{m,k_{m}}(l_{m,k_{m}}+1)=L+j_{0}-l_{0}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}Cat(l_{m,k_{m}}+1)\leq Cat(L+1).

Also by Lemma
B.1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{0}\geq 1}\sum_{\sum_{m,k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m}}=J-1}\prod_{m,k_{m}}Cat(j_{m,k_{m}})=Cat(J).

Since
\sum_{m,k_{m}}l_{m,k_{m}}=L-l_{0}
, then by (
B.1
) we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\sum_{p}l_{0,p}=l_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m,k_{m}}l_{m,k_{m};p}=L_{p}-l_{p}\\
p=0,1,2;\\
\sum_{p}l_{m,k_{m};p}=l_{m,k_{m}}\end{subarray}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}\frac{l_{m,k_{m}}!}{l_{m,k_{m};0}!l_{m,k_{m};1}!l_{m,k_{m};2}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum_{p}l_{0,p}=l_{0}}\frac{(L-l_{0})!}{(L_{0}-l_{0,p})!(L_{1}-L_{0,1})!(L_{2}-l_{0,2})!}\leq\frac{L!}{L_{0}!L_{1}!L_{2}!}.

Also since
\sum_{m,k_{m}}(j_{m,k_{m}}-1)=J-1-j_{0}
, then by (
B.1
) we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\sum_{p}j_{0,p}=j_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{m,k_{m}}j_{m,k_{m};p}=J_{p}-j_{p}\\
p=0,1,2;\\
\sum_{p}j_{m,k_{m};p}=j_{m,k_{m}}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{m,k_{m}}\frac{(j_{m,k_{m}}-1)!}{j_{m,k_{m};0}!j_{m,k_{m};1}!j_{m,k_{m};2}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum_{p}j_{0,p}=j_{0}}\frac{(J-1-j_{0})!}{(J_{0}-j_{0,p})!(J_{1}-j_{1,p})!(J_{2}-j_{2,p})!}\leq\frac{(J-1)!}{(J_{0}!J_{1}!J_{2}!}.

Thus the claim is true, and this finishes the proof by induction.

∎

Finally we can estimate each term
W^{*,*}_{*}
. For

, by (

),

\displaystyle W^{i+m,0}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{(i+m)!}{(i+m+2s-2)!}\Big|[d_{t}]^{s}V^{i+m,0}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{[\frac{i+m}{2}+s]!}{(i+m+2s-2)!}\cdot\frac{C_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i+m}{2}+s}}.

Then we can apply Lemma
5.1
to

to get

\displaystyle W^{I,J}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{L\geq 2J}(\frac{C}{t})^{\theta\cdot L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{0}+L_{1}+L_{2}=L\\
J_{0}+J_{1}+J_{2}=J-1\\
\sum_{n}2s_{n}+\sum_{m}\sum_{k_{m}}i_{m,k_{m}}+2s_{m,k_{m}}\\

Note that
\sum_{n}2s_{n}+\sum_{m,k_{m}}(i_{m,k_{m}}+m+2s_{m,k_{m}})=I+L_{1}+2L_{2}-2J=I+(L-2J)-2L_{0}-L_{1}
. For simplicity we let
\mathcal{M}=[\frac{I+(L-2J)-2L_{0}-L_{1}}{2}]
. Then by Lemma

we have

\displaystyle\quad\quad\prod_{n=1}^{J}\frac{s_{n}!C^{s_{n}}}{(2s_{n})!t^{s_{n}}}\prod_{m}\prod_{k_{m}}W^{i_{m,k_{m}}+m,0}_{s_{m,k_{m}}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{n=1}^{J}\frac{s_{n}!C^{s_{n}}}{(2s_{n})!t^{s_{n}}}\prod_{m}\prod_{k_{m}}\Big(\frac{[\frac{i_{m,k_{m}}+m}{2}+s_{m,k_{m}}]!}{(i_{m,k_{m}}+m+2s_{m,k_{m}}-2)!}\cdot\frac{C_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\frac{i_{m,k_{m}}+m}{2}+s_{m,k_{m}}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}^{I+L-J+1}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}(L-J+1)}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\mathcal{M}}}\cdot\Big(C^{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\frac{(L+1)^{\mathcal{M}}}{\mathcal{M}!}\Big).

Since

, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{J}\sum_{I}W^{I,J}_{0}|x|^{I}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{J}C^{J}\sum_{L\geq 2J}(\frac{C}{t})^{\theta\cdot L}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}(L-J+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{0}+L_{1}+L_{2}=L\\
J_{0}+J_{1}+J_{2}=J-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{I}\frac{(C|x|)^{I}\cdot(L+1)^{\mathcal{M}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\mathcal{M}!}.

If

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{J}\sum_{I}W^{I,J}_{0}|x|^{I}\leq\sum_{J}C^{J}\sum_{L\geq 2J}(\frac{C}{t})^{\theta\cdot L}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}(L-J+1)}\cdot e^{\frac{C(L+1)}{\varepsilon t}}.

Since

and then

\displaystyle\sum_{J}\sum_{I}W^{I,J}_{0}|x|^{I}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon t}}\sum_{J\geq 0}\Big((\frac{C}{t})^{\theta}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon t}}\Big)^{J}\cdot\sum_{K\geq 0}\Big((\frac{C}{t})^{\theta}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon t}}\Big)^{K}

where the constant

.

If

Next we consider the case

. If

and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{I}\frac{(C|x|)^{I}\cdot(L+1)^{\mathcal{M}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\mathcal{M}!}\leq C^{L+J}e^{\frac{C(L+1)x^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}.

If

, then

\displaystyle\sum_{I}\frac{(C|x|)^{I}\cdot(L+1)^{\mathcal{M}}}{(\varepsilon t)^{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\mathcal{M}!}\leq C^{L+J}|x|^{I-2\mathcal{M}}e^{\frac{C(L+1)x^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}\leq e^{\frac{C(L+J+1)x^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}.

Then by the same arguments we can obtain

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}(\sum_{J\geq 0}\Big(e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{t^{2}}+\frac{Cx^{2}}{\varepsilon t}}\Big)^{J})^{2},

where the constant
C

Hence if

is well-defined for

.

Moreover,

) for

and the solution is non-trivial since

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that in Subsection

, we have defined a new family of Tikhonov type counter-examples

\displaystyle\varphi_{N}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{N}(t),&\,t\geq 0\\
0,&\,t<0.\end{array}\right.

If we denote

\displaystyle g_{N}=\frac{2}{t}(\frac{8}{\varepsilon})^{N-1}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{f_{j}^{\varepsilon}},

then we have

\displaystyle|\varphi_{N}^{(i)}|\leq i!(g_{N})^{i}f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}.

Note that for

, this corresponds to the previous discussion. So if we build

based on the new Tikhonov type counter-examples, from discussions in (

) and (

) we can get

\displaystyle|u_{1,N}(x,t)|\leq f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}e^{Cg_{N}|\tilde{x}|^{2}}(\sum\limits_{J\geq 0}\Big(f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}e^{Cg_{N}|\tilde{x}|^{2}}\Big)^{J})^{2}\leq\frac{f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}e^{Cg_{N}|\tilde{x}|^{2}}}{(1-f_{N}^{1-\varepsilon}e^{Cg_{N}|\tilde{x}|^{2}})^{2}}\qquad\,\text{on}\,\{(x,t)\,|\,{Cg_{N}\tilde{x}^{2}}<\frac{1-\varepsilon}{f_{N-1}}\,\},

where
\tilde{x}=\max\{|x|,1\}
.

Also note that
\frac{1}{f_{0}}=\frac{1}{t^{2}}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{N+1}}=e^{\frac{1}{f_{N}}}

and thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{N}}\geq\frac{1}{t^{2}}

while

\displaystyle(\frac{1}{f_{N}})(t)=e^{\circ N}(\frac{1}{t^{2}}),

where

\displaystyle e^{\circ N}(x)=e^{e^{\circ{N-1}}(x)}.

Thus by choosing
\varepsilon
small,

is well defined given

\displaystyle Cg_{N}\tilde{x}^{4}\leq-\frac{1}{3}\log(f_{N})=\frac{1}{3f_{N-1}}.

This is equivalent to that

is well defined given

\displaystyle\tilde{x}\leq(\frac{t\prod\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{j}^{\varepsilon}}{C_{0}C^{N}f_{N-1}})^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Note that

\displaystyle(\frac{t\prod\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{j}^{\varepsilon}}{C_{0}C^{N}f_{N-1}})^{\frac{1}{4}}\geq C^{-\frac{N}{4}}t^{\frac{1}{4}}(\frac{\frac{1}{f_{N-1}^{1-\varepsilon}}}{\prod\limits_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{1}{f_{j}^{\varepsilon}}})^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Appendix A
An ODE lemma

Here we prove an ODE lemma due to Filippas-Kohn
[
FK92
]
and Merle-Zaag
[
MZ98
]
, see also Choi-Mantoulidis
[
CM22
]
. .

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

Let
x_{+},x_{0},x_{-}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be absolutely continuous functions such that

\displaystyle x_{+}+x_{0}+x_{-}>0,

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to\infty}x_{+}=0.

and

\displaystyle|x_{0}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(x_{+}+x_{0}+x_{-}),

\displaystyle x_{+}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq b_{1}x_{+}-\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{-}),

\displaystyle x_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq-b_{2}x_{-}+\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{+}),

where
b_{1}\geq b>0,b_{2}\geq b>0
are positive constants.

Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if
\frac{\varepsilon}{b}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

(A.1)

x_{+}\leq 2\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(x_{0}+x_{-})\text{ in }[0,\infty),

and one of the following holds:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\text{ either }x_{-}\leq 8\frac{\varepsilon}{b}x_{0}\text{ in }[s_{0},\infty)

(A.3)

\displaystyle\text{ or }x_{0}\leq 20\frac{\varepsilon}{b}x_{-}\text{in }[0,\infty).

Moreover, if (
A.3
) holds, then

\limsup_{s\to\infty}e^{(b_{2}-\varepsilon)s}(x_{+}+x_{0}+x_{-})=0.

Proof.

Firstly since once we define
\tilde{x}_{\pm}(t)=x_{\pm}(\frac{t}{b})
and
\tilde{x}_{0}(t)=x_{0}(\frac{t}{b})
, we would have

\displaystyle|\tilde{x}_{0}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(\tilde{x}_{+}+\tilde{x}_{0}+\tilde{x}_{-}),

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{+}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{b_{1}}{b}\tilde{x}_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(\tilde{x}_{0}+\tilde{x}_{-}),

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{b_{2}}{b}\tilde{x}_{-}+\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(\tilde{x}_{0}+\tilde{x}_{+}).

So in the following, without loss of generality we only need to consider the case that
b=1
. Note that as
b_{1},b_{2}\geq b
and
x_{+},x_{-},x_{0}\geq 0
, we in particular have the following

\displaystyle|\tilde{x}_{0}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(\tilde{x}_{+}+\tilde{x}_{0}+\tilde{x}_{-}),

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{+}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{x}_{+}-\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(\tilde{x}_{0}+\tilde{x}_{-}),

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq-\tilde{x}_{-}+\frac{\varepsilon}{b}(\tilde{x}_{0}+\tilde{x}_{+}).

Let
\beta\equiv x_{+}-2\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{-})
. We first show that
\beta\leq 0
in
[0,\infty)
.

Suppose
\beta(s_{0})>0
, that is
x_{+}(s_{0})\geq 2\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{-})(s_{0})
. Then at
s_{0}
we can compute

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}_{+}-2\varepsilon(x^{\prime}_{0}+x^{\prime}_{-})

\displaystyle\geq\big(x_{+}-\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{-})\big)-2\varepsilon^{2}\big(x_{+}+x_{0}+x_{-})+2\varepsilon\big(x_{-}-\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{+})\big)

\displaystyle\geq(1-4\varepsilon^{2})x_{+}+(-\varepsilon-4\varepsilon^{2})x_{0}+(\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^{2})x_{-}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon(1-4\varepsilon-8\varepsilon^{2})x_{0}+\varepsilon(3-2\varepsilon-8\varepsilon^{2})x_{-}

where the last inequality comes from
\beta(s_{0})\geq 0
. Hence if
\varepsilon
is small enough, then
\beta^{\prime}(s_{0})>0
. In particular, we have
\beta(s)\geq\beta(s_{0})>0
in
[s_{0},\infty)
. On the other hand, since
\liminf_{s\to\infty}x_{+}(s)=0
, we have
\liminf_{s\to\infty}\beta(s)\leq 0
. This is a contradiction.

Hence we prove that
x_{+}\leq 2\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{-})
in
[0,\infty)
. In particular, this implies that

\displaystyle|x^{\prime}_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon(x_{0}+x_{-})

\displaystyle x^{\prime}_{-}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}x_{-}+2\varepsilon x_{0}.

Now let
\gamma(s)\equiv 8\varepsilon x_{0}-x_{-}
.

Case 1:
Suppose there exist some
s_{0}\geq 0
such that
\gamma(s_{0})=0
. That is
8\varepsilon x_{0}(s_{0})=x_{-}(s_{0})
. Then at
s_{0}
we compute

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=8\varepsilon x^{\prime}_{0}-x^{\prime}_{-}

\displaystyle\geq-16\varepsilon^{2}(x_{0}+x_{-})+\frac{1}{2}x_{-}-2\varepsilon x_{0}

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon(1-8\varepsilon-64\varepsilon^{2})x_{0}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

From the inequality, we note that if
\gamma^{\prime}(s_{0})=0
, then
x_{0}(s_{0})=x_{-}(s_{0})=0
. This implies that
x_{+}(s_{0})=0
. Contradiction arises. Hence we have
\gamma^{\prime}(s_{0})>0
. In particular
\gamma(s)\geq\gamma(s_{0})\geq 0
for
s\in[s_{0},\infty)
.

Case 2:
Suppose
\gamma(s)<0

. This implies that

\displaystyle x^{\prime}_{-}

Hence we obtain an improved estimate for

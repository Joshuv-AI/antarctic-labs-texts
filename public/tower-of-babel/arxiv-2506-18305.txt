Existence of nuclear modifications on longitudinal-transverse structure function ratio
S. Kumano

Abstract
It has been assumed that nuclear modification does not exist in the longitudinal-transverse structure function ratio R N = F L N / ( 2 ​ x ​ F 1 N ) R_{N}=F_{L}^{N}/(2xF_{1}^{N}) in lepton deep inelastic scattering. This assumption is widely used in obtaining structure functions of the “nucleon” from nuclear data such as the deuteron ones. However, nuclear modifications do exist theoretically at least at medium- and large- x x regions because nucleons in a nucleus move in any direction, which is not necessary the longitudinal direction of the virtual-photon or weak-boson momentum in lepton scattering. Because of this transverse motion, the nucleon’s transverse and longitudinal structure functions should mix with each other in nuclei with the mixture probability proportional to the nucleon’s transverse momentum squared p → T 2 / Q 2 \vec{p}_{T}^{\,\,2}/Q^{2} . In this work, numerical results are explicitly shown on such nuclear modifications in the deuteron. These nuclear modifications are important for determining precise structure functions of the nucleon. Furthermore, modifications of R N R_{N} should be investigated also at small x x by the future electron-ion collider to find interesting gluon dynamics in nuclei. Hopefully, this nuclear effect on R N R_{N} could be found by future experimental measurements at lepton accelerator facilities.

Although we focuses on the medium- and large-

These nuclear modifications need to be considered

Formalism
:
The cross section of charged-lepton deep inelastic scattering from
a nucleon or a nucleus is given by the lepton tensor

L^{\mu\nu}
and the hadron tensor
W^{A,N}_{\mu\nu}
as

\frac{d\sigma}{dE_{\ell}^{\,\prime}d\Omega_{l}^{\,\prime}}=\frac{|\vec{p}_{\ell}^{\,\prime}|}{|\vec{p}_{\ell}|}\,\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(q^{2})^{2}}\,L^{\mu\nu}(p_{\ell},q)\,W^{A,N}_{\mu\nu}(p_{A,N},q),

(1)

where
E_{\ell}^{\,\prime}
and
\Omega_{l}^{\,\prime}

are the final charged-lepton energy and solid angle,

p_{\ell}
and
p_{\ell}^{\,\prime}
are initial
and final charged-lepton momenta,

q
is the momentum transfer
q=p_{\ell}-p_{\ell}^{\,\prime}
,

p_{{}_{A}}
and
p_{{}_{N}}
are nuclear and nucleon momenta,
respectively, and
\alpha
is the fine structure constant of
the quantum electrodynamics.
The hadron tensor of the charged-lepton scattering is given
in general by two structure functions
W_{1}^{A,N}
and
W_{2}^{A,N}

for the unpolarized nucleon and nuclei.

The hadron tensor for the polarized photon with the helicity
\lambda

is given by
quark-lepton-book

\displaystyle W^{A,N}_{\lambda}(p_{{}_{A,N}},q)=\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{\mu*}\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{\nu}W^{A,N}_{\mu\nu}(p_{{}_{A,N}},q),

(2)

where
\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{\mu}
is the photon polarization vector.
Then, the transverse and longitudinal structure functions

W^{A,N}_{T}
and
W^{A,N}_{L}
are defined by these helicity functions,

W^{A,N}_{1}
, and
W^{A,N}_{2}
as

W^{A,N}_{T}=\big[W^{A,N}_{\lambda=+1}+W^{A,N}_{\lambda=-1}\big]/2=W^{A,N}_{1}
,

W^{A,N}_{L}=W^{A,N}_{\lambda=0}=\big(1+\nu_{{}_{A,N}}^{2}/Q^{2}\big)W^{A,N}_{2}-W^{A,N}_{1}
,
where
\nu_{N}
and
\nu_{A}
are defined by

\nu_{A}=p_{{}_{A}}\cdot q/\sqrt{p_{{}_{A}}^{\,2}}=\nu
,

\nu_{N}=p_{{}_{N}}\cdot q/\sqrt{p_{{}_{N}}^{\,2}}
.
Instead of
W_{1,2,T,L}
, the structure functions
F_{1,2,T,L}

are often used.
They are related with each other by the relations:

F_{1}^{A,N}=F_{T}^{A,N}=\sqrt{p_{{}_{A,N}}^{2}}\,W_{1}^{A,N}
,

F_{2}^{A,N}=p_{{}_{A,N}}\cdot q/\sqrt{p_{{}_{A,N}}^{2}}\,W_{2}^{A,N}
,

F_{L}^{A,N}=\big(1+Q^{2}/\nu_{{}_{A,N}}^{2}\big)F_{2}^{A,N}-2x_{{}_{A,N}}F_{1}^{A,N}
.
From these structure functions, the longitudinal-transverse
structure function ratio is defied as

\displaystyle R_{A,N}(x_{A},Q^{2})=\frac{F_{L}^{A,N}(x_{A,N},Q^{2})}{2\,x_{A,N}F_{1}^{A,N}(x_{A,N},Q^{2})}.

(3)

Here, the variables
x_{{}_{A}}
and
x_{{}_{N}}
are defined
in terms of the Bjorken scaling variable
x

and the momentum fraction
y
as

x_{A}=Q^{2}/(2\,p_{A}\cdot q)
,

x_{N}=Q^{2}/(2\,p_{N}\cdot q)=x/y
,

x=Q^{2}/(2\,M_{N}\nu)
,

y=p_{N}\cdot q/(M_{N}\,\nu)
,
where
M_{A}
is the nuclear mass.

A conventional way to theoretically describe
the nuclear structure functions at medium- and large-
x

is to use the convolution formalism.
Then, the nuclear hadron tensor is given by the nucleonic one
convoluted with the nucleon momentum distribution
S(p_{N})
,
which is called the spectral function, as

W^{A}_{\mu\nu}(p_{A},q)={\displaystyle\int}d^{4}p_{N}\,S(p_{N})\,W^{N}_{\mu\nu}(p_{N},q)
.
A simple form of the spectral function is given
by the shell model as

S(p_{N})=\sum_{i}|\phi_{i}(\vec{p}_{N})|^{2}\,\delta\left(p_{N}^{\,0}-M_{A}+\sqrt{M_{A-i}^{\ 2}+\vec{p}_{N}^{\ 2}}\,\right)
.
Here,
\phi_{i}
is the wave function of the nucleon
i
,
the separation energy
\varepsilon_{i}
and
M_{A-i}

are related by
\varepsilon_{i}=(M_{A-i}+M_{N})-M_{A}
.
We average over all the nucleons
for calculating the average separation energy,

\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow\left<\varepsilon\right>
.
By using a non-relativistic approximation for

\sqrt{M_{A-i}^{\ 2}+\vec{p}_{N}^{\ 2}}
,

p_{N}^{\,0}
and
\left<\varepsilon\right>
are related by

p_{N}^{\,0}=M_{N}-\left<\varepsilon\right>-\vec{p}_{N}^{\ 2}/(2M_{A-1})

with the replacement
M_{A-i}\to M_{A-1}

hksw-2011
;
b1-conv
.

Applying the projection operators
\widehat{P}_{1,2}^{\,\mu\nu}

to
W_{\mu\nu}^{A}
, we obtain the nuclear structure functions
W_{1,2}^{A}

in the convolution model.
From
W_{1,2}^{A}
, the transverse and longitudinal structure functions

F_{1}^{A}
and
F_{L}^{A}
are obtained in the convolution form as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\left(\begin{aligned} \,F_{2}^{A}(x_{A},Q^{2})\,\\
\,2x_{A}F_{1}^{A}(x_{A},Q^{2})\,\\
\,F_{L}^{A}(x_{A},Q^{2})\,\end{aligned}\right)

\displaystyle\!=\!\int_{x}^{A}\!dy\left(\begin{aligned} \,f_{22}(y)\,&\ \ \ \,0\,&\,0\ \ \ \,\\
\,0\ \ \ \ &\,f_{11}(y)\,&\,f_{1L}(y)\,\\
\,0\ \ \ \ &\,f_{L1}(y)\,&\,f_{LL}(y)\,\end{aligned}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times\left(\begin{aligned} \,F_{2}^{N}(x/y,Q^{2})\,\\
\,2\,\frac{x}{y}F_{1}^{N}(x/y,Q^{2})\,\\
\,F_{L}^{N}(x/y,Q^{2})\,\end{aligned}\right).

(4)

Here, the nucleon’s lightcone momentum distributions

f_{22}
,
f_{LL}
,
f_{L1}
,
f_{1L}
, and
f_{11}

are defined by

\displaystyle f_{22}(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{N\perp}2\pi p_{N\perp}y\frac{M_{N}\nu}{|\vec{q}\,|}\left|\phi(\vec{p}_{N})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \hskip 36.98866pt\times\left[\frac{2\,(x/y)^{2}\,\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}}{(1+Q^{2}/\nu^{2})Q^{2}}+\left(1+\frac{2\,(x/y)\,p_{N\parallel}}{\sqrt{Q^{2}+\nu^{2}}}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle f_{LL}(y)=f_{11}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ =\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{N\perp}2\pi p_{N\perp}y\frac{M_{N}\nu}{|\vec{q}\,|}\left|\phi(\vec{p}_{N})\right|^{2}\left(1+\frac{\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}}{\widetilde{p}_{N}^{\,\,2}}\right),

\displaystyle f_{L1}(y)=f_{1L}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ =\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{N\perp}2\pi p_{N\perp}y\frac{M_{N}\nu}{|\vec{q}\,|}\left|\phi(\vec{p}_{N})\right|^{2}\frac{\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}}{\widetilde{p}_{N}^{\,\,2}},

(5)

where
\widetilde{p}_{\mu}=p_{\mu}-(p\cdot q)\,q_{\mu}/q^{2}
.
Then, the longitudinal-transverse ratio
R_{A}
of a nucleus is
calculated by Eq. (
3
).

Because of the transverse motion of the nucleons in a nucleus,
the transverse and longitudinal structure functions of the nucleon
(
F_{1}^{N}
,
F_{L}^{N}
) mix with each other
in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
),
and the mixture coefficient is proportional
to the transverse momentum squared
\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}
.
The mixture factor
\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}/\widetilde{p}_{N}^{\,\,2}
is
proportional to
\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}/Q^{2}
.
Because of this admixture, we definitely
can claim that the nuclear modifications of
R_{N}
do exist
although there is no experimental finding at this stage.
Because of the factor
\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}/Q^{2}
,
this mixture does not occur in the Bjorken scaling limit

Q^{2}\to\infty
.

However, we may note that the nuclear modifications
of
R_{N}
exist even at
Q^{2}\to\infty
through
the convolution integrals of Eq. (
4
)
as shown numerically later. This modification occurs
because the
x_{N}
-dependent functional forms
are different between
F_{1}^{N}
and
F_{L}^{N}
, although
the lightcone momentum distributions of the nucleon,

and
f_{11}(y)
, are same.
In Eq. (
4
),
the structure function
F_{2}^{A}
is expressed only by
F_{2}^{N}

and the mixing with other structure function does not exist;
however, there are correction factors
\vec{p}_{N\perp}^{\ 2}/Q^{2}

and
p_{N\parallel}/\sqrt{Q^{2}+\nu^{2}}

in
f_{22}(y)
of Eq. (
5
).
By the convolution description for the nuclear structure functions,
we found that there are two sources on the nuclear modifications
of
R_{N}
. One is the admixture of
F_{1}^{N}
and
F_{L}^{N}

in a nuclear medium, and the other is by the convolution
with the nucleon’s momentum distributions

f_{11}(y)
and
f_{LL}(y)
.
In this work, the nuclear modifications are actually shown
numerically for the deuteron by taking
an appropriate deuteron wave function
\phi_{i}

in Eq. (
5
).

Summary
:
Using the standard convolution description for the structure functions
of the deuteron, we calculated nuclear modifications of

F_{2}^{N}
,
F_{1}^{N}
, and
F_{L}^{N}
.
Because the nucleons in the deuteron move in any direction
which is generally different from the longitudinal direction
defined by the virtual photon momentum in the charged-lepton
scattering, the longitudinal structure function
F_{L}^{N}

and the transverse one
F_{1}^{N}
mix with each other in the deuteron.
Because of this mixture, the nuclear modification of
F_{L}^{N}
is very
different from the ones of
F_{2}^{N}
and
F_{1}^{N}
.
In addition, the modification of
R_{N}
comes from
the convolution integrals with the nucleon’s momentum distributions.
These mechanisms led to the significant nuclear modifications of
R_{N}

in the deuteron. It has been assumed until now that the nuclear
modification does not exist in the function
R_{N}
, so that
our theoretical predictions should be tested by future experiments.

Abstract
The recently identified Gaia population of main-sequence–white dwarf (MS+WD) binaries at separations of ∼ 1 ​ AU {\sim}\,1~{\rm AU} , often with moderate eccentricities, is not readily reproduced by binary population synthesis models. Barium stars represent a closely related population whose enrichment in s -process elements both confirms the presence of a WD companion and attests to past binary interaction. It also indicates that mass transfer occurred at least during the late and post-AGB phases of the WD progenitor, when s -process elements are dredged up. In this work, we further explore the connection between the astrometrically identified Gaia MS+WD binaries and the classical barium star population. To achieve this, we used high-resolution FEROS spectroscopy to measure abundances for 30 Gaia DR3 non-single-star binaries, identifying 10 as Ba-enriched. Together with our recent analysis of archival GALAH data, this yields a sample of 38 barium dwarfs with dynamically measured WD masses, compared to only 6 previously known systems with known WD masses at these separations. We find that, in cases where metallicity is sufficiently low to facilitate efficient s -process production, barium and yttrium enrichment is often detected. This enrichment is also identified in eccentric systems, suggesting that post-AGB mass transfer mechanisms are capable of pumping eccentricity into the orbit or occur without erasing it. Our results indicate that the Gaia MS+WD binaries trace the population from which barium stars emerge. Treating the large Gaia-discovered population as an extension of known s -process enriched dwarfs opens an avenue to empirically constrain their formation and evolution.



I
Introduction

Barium stars
(
W. P. Bidelman & P. C. Keenan 1951
)
were first discovered as red-giant stars of spectral type G-K with enhanced abundances of elements produced by the slow neutron capture process (
s
-process). Barium dwarfs are main-sequence (MS) stars of classes F-K with similar
s
-process element enhancement
(
P. North et al. 1994
)
. This type of chemical enrichment has long been interpreted as a fossil record of past binary interactions.

Since
s
-process elements cannot be produced internally by stars before the asymptotic giant branch (AGB) phase, their presence is understood as a signature of mass accretion from an AGB companion that had since evolved into a white dwarf
(WD; see, e.g.,
A. Jorissen et al. 2019
, and references therein)
.
During thermal pulses in the late-AGB phase,
s
-process elements such as barium and yttrium are mixed to the stellar surface and, if the AGB star fills its Roche Lobe, can be transferred to a binary companion. The detectability of these elements in the accretor’s photosphere depends on both the total amount of material accreted and the degree of dilution in the stellar envelope
(
L. Husti et al. 2009
)
. Surface
s
-process enhancement, therefore, offers evidence of mass transfer, independent of the system’s current orbital configuration.

The third data release (DR3) of Gaia
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023a
)
includes a catalog of non-single-star solutions
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023b
, NSS;)
for unresolved binaries, enabling astrometric orbit characterization across a wide range of orbital separations.
S. Shahaf et al. 2024
identified
{\sim}\,3200
MS+WD astrometric binaries in the NSS catalog, in which a faint WD accompanies a luminous MS star. These binaries have periods between
100-1000
days (corresponding to orbital separations of
{\sim}\,1
AU), and a substantial fraction of them exhibit moderate to high eccentricities (
e
up to 0.7). Standard binary population synthesis models do not predict this population, as they preferentially produce systems with shorter or longer orbital periods (see figure 16 of
S. Shahaf et al. 2024
and discussion thereof). Its empirical properties and the processes that enabled it are the subject of ongoing studies
(
N. Hallakoun et al. 2024
;
N. Yamaguchi et al. 2024
;
N. Yamaguchi et al. 2025
;
O. Ironi et al. 2025
;
A. C. Rubio et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

Identifying
s
-process enrichment in even part of this sample would link these systems to Barium stars, partially constrain the timing of mass transfer to the late- and post-AGB phase, and place them in a broader evolutionary context. To search for such signatures,
P. Rekhi et al. 2024
combined this Gaia-selected sample with GALAH DR3 spectroscopy. They measured
s
-process abundances in 102 MS+WD binaries, and found that barium and yttrium enrichment in the MS primaries correlates with WD mass and metallicity. However, they did not find significant link with the orbital properties of the systems, possibly due to the limited sample size.

This study aims to extend the work of
55
by targeting eccentric MS+WD systems in the 100-1000 d period range. To achieve this, we conduct spectroscopic analysis to measure
s
-process abundances of 21 eccentric MS+WD binaries, complemented by a comparison sample of 12 systems with WD masses above 0.75
M_{\odot}
with nearly circular orbits. The targets were observed using the Fiber-fed Extended Range Optical Spectrograph
(
A. Kaufer & L. Pasquini 1998
;
A. Kaufer et al. 1999
, FEROS;)
, mounted on the MPG/ESO 2.2-meter telescope at the La Silla Observatory in Chile. FEROS covers the spectral range from 350 to 920 nm at a resolving power of
R\sim 48{,}000
, making it well-suited for precise measurements of chemical abundances.

describes the sample selection and spectroscopic campaign. Section
III
details our procedure for estimating stellar parameters and Ba and Y abundances. We discuss our results and their astrophysical implications in Section
IV
.
Section
V
provides an outlook for future work.

IV.2
Eccentricity Dependence of Mass Transfer

In our sample of MS+WD binaries, both in this work as well as
55
, we detect pronounced Ba and Y overabundance ([Ba/Fe], [Y/Fe] > 0.25) in many MS primaries up to the maximal orbital eccentricities probed (
\approx 0.4
; Figures
2a
and
10a
). Such
s
-process enrichment clearly indicates past mass transfer—at least in the late and post-AGB phase—from the WD progenitor to the companion MS star; however, the persistence of significant eccentricity in these shows that tidal circularization, expected to accompany such mass transfer, either was much weaker than expected, or did not fully erase the orbital eccentricity.

However, this Ba enhancement seems to be sporadic, as only around half the systems with high eccentricity and sufficiently low metallicity demonstrate it. This points to as yet unconfirmed factors influencing mass-transfer in eccentric systems. One possibility is that these systems had different initial orbital parameters, perhaps being significantly more eccentric or more widely separated, reducing their accretion efficiency. Another possibility is that the progenitors of these WDs were much more massive than expected from the canonical single star initial-final mass ratio functions
(
J. D. Cummings et al. 2018
;
T. Cunningham et al. 2024
)
, and had correspondingly lower
s
-process yields. Readers can refer to
O. Ironi et al. 2025
and
S. Shahaf 2025
for a detailed discussion.

We also identify Ba enhancement in four near-circular systems hosting relatively massive WDs (M
{}_{\text{WD}}

\in[0.75,0.85]

M_{\odot}
; Figure
2b
). Three of these systems have [Fe/H]
<-0.5
dex, a regime not probed in the
55
study.
We suggest that the low metallicities of these systems mitigated the effect of their progenitors’ relatively high masses, allowing sufficient Ba production for their companions to accrete and exhibit enrichment.
Given the straightforward explanation for the confirmed
s
-process enhanced high-M
{}_{\text{WD}}
systems, it is difficult to assess whether their (current) low eccentricity corresponds to higher mass transfer efficiency.
Nevertheless, our sample extends the empirical evidence for mass transfer in these systems to encompass higher WD masses.

IV.3
Formation Channels

Our results add to a growing body of work documenting significant eccentricities in various classes of post-interaction binaries
(
A. I. Karakas et al. 2000
;
A. Jorissen et al. 2016
;
A. Jorissen et al. 2019
;
J. Vos et al. 2017
;
M. Van der Swaelmen et al. 2017
;
G.-M. Oomen et al. 2018
;
J. Müller-Horn et al. 2025
, e.g., )
. A particularly relevant parallel is provided by
G.-M. Oomen et al. 2018
, who studied a sample of 33 post-AGB+MS binaries with orbital periods of
100-3000
days, most of which exhibit significant eccentricity (
\gtrsim 0.1
). Our work demonstrates that these eccentricities persist through the post-AGB phase to the WD formation, diminishing the possibility that these systems could yet circularize via accretion and/or interaction with the post-AGB planetary nebula or circumbinary (CB) disk.
Tidal evolution theory predicts that binaries in which one component fills its Roche lobe should circularize on relatively short timescales, far shorter than the expected duration of the mass transfer phase in the progenitor system
(
S. F. Portegies Zwart & F. Verbunt 1996
;
G. Nelemans et al. 2001
;
J. F. Sepinsky et al. 2007
;
J. F. Sepinsky et al. 2009
;
S. Toonen et al. 2012
;
S. Toonen & G. Nelemans 2013
, see, e.g., )
. Multiple models have been proposed to explain this seemingly anomalous population of eccentric post-interaction systems. Broadly, these fall into two categories:
(1) Mechanisms that avoid strong tidal circularization altogether;
(2) Mechanisms that actively pump eccentricity, counteracting canonical tidal damping.

In the first category, one possibility is that tidal forces are much weaker than expected in certain systems
(
J. D. Nie et al. 2017
, e.g., )
, allowing the Roche-lobe overflow (RLOF) phase to conclude before circularization can occur.
This seems unlikely for our sample, except perhaps in extremely eccentric cases, because in most systems at these separations, the WD progenitor would have substantially filled its Roche lobe for much of the RGB and the entirety of the AGB phase, thereby experiencing significant tidal forces
(
J. P. Zahn 1977
)
. Another possibility is that mass transfer proceeds primarily via stellar winds, rather than RLOF, which can produce non-trivial eccentricity evolution
(
H. M. J. Boffin & A. Jorissen 1988
;
H. M. J. Boffin 2015
;
M. I. Saladino et al. 2018
;
M. I. Saladino & O. R. Pols 2019
;
S. L. Schrøder et al. 2021
)
. A third option is that the observed eccentricity reflects the presence of a tertiary companion, either through direct orbital perturbations or because the tertiary itself was the AGB donor, later being ejected or migrated to a wider orbit after the interaction
(
H. B. Perets & K. M. Kratter 2012
)
.

To investigate the third possibility, we employed the algorithm of
K. El-Badry et al. 2021
to search for wide companions to our systems using their NSS-derived astrometric parameters.
No tertiary matches were found for any of the
s
-process-enriched systems reported here or in
55
. However, the detection probability declines sharply for companions fainter than
G\sim 19
, which, for our sample, effectively excludes all WDs with cooling ages older than a Gyr as well as the majority of M dwarfs. A more definitive assessment of this scenario will thus require deeper surveys, such as future Gaia data releases and surveys by the Vera Rubin Observatory
(
Ž. Ivezić et al. 2019
)
.

In the second category, a frequently invoked eccentricity-pumping mechanism is the formation of a CB disk from AGB ejecta, with resonant disk–binary interactions driving eccentricity growth
(
T. Dermine et al. 2013
;
J. Vos et al. 2015
;
D. J. D’Orazio & P. C. Duffell 2021
;
M. Siwek et al. 2023
;
R. Valli et al. 2024
)
. Other proposed mechanisms include phase-dependent mass loss via RLOF and/or winds
(
F. Dosopoulou & V. Kalogera 2016
;
A. Parkosidis et al. 2025
)
, and tidally enhanced mass loss at periastron
(
A. A. Bonačić Marinović et al. 2008
;
A. Kashi & N. Soker 2018
)
. As demonstrated by
A. Parkosidis et al. 2025
, phase-dependent RLOF mass-loss can cause eccentricity-pumping, but comprehensive population synthesis modeling is required to determine whether they can reliably reproduce the orbital characteristics presented here and in other studies.

However, we note that many existing studies often yield contradictory results or fail to explore the full parameter space relevant to our systems, and those that especially explore the
100-1000
d period range covered by our sample have been unable to reproduce the observed eccentricities
(
R. R. Rafikov 2016
;
M. I. Saladino & O. R. Pols 2019
;
G.-M. Oomen et al. 2020
;
P. Krynski et al. 2025
)
.
The recent work by
P. Krynski et al. 2025
presented detailed models of the evolution of AGB+MS binaries, incorporating many of the mechanisms discussed above, including classical and wind RLOF, tidally enhanced winds, non-conservative mass transfer, and eccentricity pumping due to a CB disk. Even under favorable assumptions, however, the models failed to reproduce the observed eccentricities of post-interaction MS+WD binaries with periods shorter than
\sim 2000
days. This discrepancy indicates that key aspects of the underlying processes remain poorly understood.

The determination of effective temperature (
T_{\text{eff}}
, surface gravity (
\log g
), iron abundance ([Fe/H]) and rotational broadening velocity
v_{\text{broad}}
was carried out using a set of Fe lines custom-selected for each star. As the low SNR of our spectra makes simultaneous fitting of projected rotational velocity (
v\sin i
) and macro-turbulence velocity (
v_{\text{mac}}
) challenging, we followed
S. Buder et al. 2021
and fit for
v_{\text{broad}}
(defined as
v\sin i
with
v_{\text{mac}}
set to 0) instead. The microturbulence velocity (
v_{\text{mic}}
) was estimated using the empirical relation provided in the GALAH DR3 study
(
S. Buder et al. 2021
, eqns 4 and 5; )
.
During the following procedure, where required, initial/seed values of the effective temperature (
T_{\text{eff}}
), surface gravity (
\log g
) and iron abundance ([Fe/H]; here used synonymously with metallicity) for each star were derived from Gaia’s low-resolution XP spectra using the
XGBoost
algorithm
(
R. Andrae et al. 2023
)
.

For each selected line, we extracted 1 Å-wide spectral segments centered on the line, merging overlapping segments. Lines were chosen based on the following criteria: (1) the SNR in the line region exceeded 10, except for three stars where the threshold was lowered to 5 due to fewer than five lines meeting the SNR
>10
condition; (2) the Fe line depth would exceed 0.4 at [Fe/H] = 0 for the star’s
T_{\text{eff}}
and
\log g
; (3) there were no lines of other species within
\pm 0.5
Å exceeding 20% of the Fe line’s depth; and (4) the mean value of the continuum trend (see next) across the
3\,
Å segment was greater than 0.9, with a maximum variation of less than 0.02. The fourth condition excluded regions where the blaze function caused the pipeline’s continuum normalization quality, as well as the local SNR, to drop considerably. To reduce computational expenses, when the number of such segments exceeded 30 (for 5 spectra), we selected the 30 segments with the deepest lines.

To correct for the local continuum trend, we fit a smoothing spline to continuum points in 60 Å windows centered on each segment of interest using the
scipy
function
scipy.interpolate.make_smoothing_spline
. For this procedure, synthetic spectra were created with stellar parameters as described above and Doppler shifted to stellar rest frames using the nominal radial velocity (RV) determined using the H
\alpha
line. Continuum points were denoted as those where the model flux exceeded 0.98, which ensured a sufficient number of points for robust spline fits with negligible bias from absorption features.

The relative uncertainty in the spectrum in each segment was estimated from the standard deviation of the continuum-identified points in a 20 Å window centered on the segment. To isolate the noise estimate from the continuum trend, the normalized flux in the windows was first divided by the continuum spline.

To determine the local RV value around each line segment, we used a 20 Å-wide region centered on the corresponding 1 Å segment. Regions (and corresponding 1 Å segments) with more than 25% of the flux points masked were discarded to ensure reliability both in RV estimation and continuum de-trending. Synthetic spectra were generated using the given stellar parameters, including only lines with absorption depths greater than 0.1. The RV was determined using cross-correlation with a built-in method from
pymse
. A
2\sigma
clipping procedure was then used to identify and discard any windows with unreliable RVs, typically caused by an insufficient number of deep lines in the region. Note that these estimated RV values are not corrected for Earth’s orbital motion, as this is not required for our science case.
Across all spectra, the mean (max) range in the measured RVs across all usable windows is
0.93\,(1.7)
km s
-1
, and the mean (max) standard deviation is
0.26\,(0.54)
km s
-1
.

Each 1 Å segment was then Doppler-shifted to Earth’s rest frame using its locally determined RV value, rather than a global mean, to avoid potential inter- and intra-order wavelength calibration drifts. Subsequently, segments with any masked data points within the core line region — defined as a
\pm 0.2
Å interval around the line center — were removed from further analysis. After this final filtering step, between 6 and 30 segments per spectrum remained, with a mean of 21 segments per spectrum.

Finally, we conducted a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) analysis to fit for
T_{\text{eff}}
,
\log g
, [Fe/H] and
v_{\text{broad}}
, treating all segments simultaneously. For this we created a bank of synthetic spectra using
pysme
on a grid of
T_{\text{eff}}
,
\log g
, [Fe/H] values, at steps of 100 K, 0.1 dex and 0.05 dex respectively, with a wavelength resolution of 0.003 Å.
The line list passed to
pysme
during the synthesis of this bank was restricted to only Fe lines in the relevant segments in order to avoid prohibitively large computational times, as computation time scaled linearly with the number of lines in the list. This restriction does not affect our results, as the Fe lines were previously selected to avoid significant blending with lines from other species.
For each star, we adopted weakly informative normal priors for
T_{\text{eff}}
and
\log g
, centered on their seed values with conservative standard deviations of 250 K and 0.25 dex respectively, and uniform priors on [Fe/H] and
v_{\text{broad}}
spanning
[-1.5,+0.75]
dex and
[0,20]
km s
-1
respectively.
During each MCMC iteration, unbroadened synthetic spectra at the given three stellar parameters were interpolated from the grid and were rotationally and instrumentally broadened using the routines
fastRotBroad
, with limb-darkening parameter set at 0.81, and
instrBroadGaussFast
from
pyastronomy

(
S. Czesla 2025
, version 0.22.0; )
. Instrumental resolution at the center of each segment was computed from the wavelength solution, assuming FEROS’s sampling of 2.2 pixels per resolution element
(
A. Kaufer & L. Pasquini 1998
)
. We compared spectra broadened with
pysme
and
pyastronomy
and verified that the maximum deviation between the two was less than 1%. The broadened spectra were then interpolated onto the FEROS wavelength grid and multiplied by the continuum trend. We used
-0.5\chi^{2}
as the log-likelihood function, calculated from all data points from each Fe segment selected for that star. An example of the spectral fit in Figure
7a
.

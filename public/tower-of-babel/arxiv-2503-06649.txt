Quasi-Classical Approximation of Electromagnetic Radiation by Fermions embedded in Rigidly Rotating Medium in Strong Magnetic Field
Matteo Buzzegoli, Kirill Tuchin, Nandagopal Vijayakumar

Abstract
We develop the quasi-classical (WKB) approximation of the synchrotron radiation by a fermion embedded into uniformly rotating system in external magnetic field. We show that it gives an accurate approximation of the exact expression that we recently obtained at a tiny fraction of the numerical cost. Our results can be used to compute the electromagnetic radiation of the quark-gluon plasma produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions.

In principle, one may use the results obtained in

However, such a calculation in rotating plasmas turns out to be very expensive numerically because rotation lifts the degeneracy of the spectrum, which not only makes analytical expressions way more complicated than in the non-rotating case but also greatly increases the complexity of the numerical calculations. It is, therefore, important to develop a reliable approximation. In this paper, we develop the quasi-classical approximation, which hinges on the assumption that spacing between the nearby Landau levels is negligible. Considering that the distance between the nearby Landau levels equals the synchrotron frequency
\omega_{B}
, the applicability of the quasi-classical approximation requires that
\omega_{B}\ll E
, where
E
is the fermion energy. It can be argued that
[
16
]

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{B}}{E}\sim\frac{B}{M^{2}/q}\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}=\frac{qB}{E^{2}}\,,

(1)

where
M
is the fermion mass.
This indicates that the quasi-classical approximation is expected to be accurate in describing the synchrotron radiation of ultra-relativistic fermions in subcritical magnetic fields. This argument applies as well to a rotating system where
E
is understood as including the additional term
-m\Omega
, where
m
is the magnetic quantum number:

E=\sqrt{M^{2}+p_{z}^{2}+2n|qB|}+m\Omega\,,

(2)

where
p_{z}
is the conserved fermion momentum along the magnetic field, which we choose to be the
z

m
. It was argued in
[
4
]
(see also
[
17
]
) that if
\Omega^{2}\sim qB
, the structure of the Landau levels is completely broken due to the lifted degeneracy in
m
so that it is no longer possible to give a simple estimate of the distance between the levels. We therefore constrain ourselves in this paper to the ‘relatively slow’ rotation. The development of the quasi-classical approximation in such a case is the main subject of this paper.

In our analysis, we follow the method Sokolov and Ternov

[
18
,
19
]

who first obtained the expressions in the leading order perturbation theory and then expanded it in the quasi-classical limit. It is a natural method in our case since we have already derived the leading order result and we proceed along the same guidelines.

II
Synchrotron Radiation Intensity

It is instructive to begin with the review of the main result of
[
1
,
2
]
. The Landau levels of a particle in a constant magnetic field embedded in a uniformly rotating medium are given by
(
2
). Since the operator of rotation about the
z
-axis commutes with the boost operator in the same direction, we can choose a reference frame where the momentum of the initial electron along the
z
-axis vanishes:
p_{z}=0
.
When
\bm{\Omega}
is parallel to
\bm{B}
,
\Omega
is taken to be positive and negative when it is anti-parallel. In place of
m
, it is often useful to use the radial quantum number
a
:

a=n-m-\frac{1}{2}\,.

(3)

The quantum numbers
n
and
a
takes integer values from
0
to
\infty
. We will use the prime such as
E^{\prime}
,
a^{\prime}
, etc. to distinguish the final state.
The radiation intensity is given by

W^{h}_{n,a,p_{z}=0,\zeta}=\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\sum_{n^{\prime},a^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}}\int\omega^{2}d\omega\int d\cos\theta\delta(\omega-E+E^{\prime})\Phi_{h}\,,

(4)

where
\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}
denotes the initial and final fermion polarization,
h
denotes the helicity of the emitted photon, and
\theta
denotes the emission angle of the photon with respect to the
z
-axis. The delta function imposes energy conservation, and momentum conservation is implicit.
\Phi_{h}
is the matrix element of the scattering process given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{h}=I^{2}_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)\big|\sin\theta\left(K_{4}I_{n-1,n^{\prime}-1}(x)-K_{3}I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)\right)+K_{1}(h-\cos\theta)I_{n,n^{\prime}-1}(x)

\displaystyle+K_{2}(h+\cos\theta)I_{n-1,n^{\prime}}(x)\big|^{2}\,.

(5)

The function
I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)
is defined as

I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{n^{\prime}!}{n!}}e^{-x/2}x^{(n-n^{\prime})/2}L^{n-n^{\prime}}_{n^{\prime}}(x)\,,

(6)

where
L^{n-n^{\prime}}_{n^{\prime}}(x)
is the associated Laguerre polynomial and

\displaystyle x=\frac{\omega^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{2|qB|}\,.

(7)

I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)
is sometimes called the Laguerre function. We will call these functions the
I
-functions throughout the article.
The coefficients
K_{i}
that appears in the intensity are defined as follows:

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\prime}C_{4}+C_{3}^{\prime}C_{2}\,,\qquad K_{2}=C_{4}^{\prime}C_{1}+C_{2}^{\prime}C_{3}\,,

(8a)

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=C_{4}^{\prime}C_{2}+C_{2}^{\prime}C_{4}\,,\qquad K_{4}=C_{1}^{\prime}C_{3}+C_{3}^{\prime}C_{1}\,,

(8b)

where

\displaystyle C_{1,3}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}B_{3}(A_{3}\pm A_{4})\,,\qquad C_{2,4}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}B_{4}(A_{4}\mp A_{3}),

(9)

with the upper signs refer to the indexes 1 and 2, and the lower ones to 3 and 4.
The primed quantities are defined similarly with primed
A
and
B
coefficients, which are as follows:

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=1\,,\qquad A_{4}=\zeta\,,\qquad B_{3}=\left(1+\frac{\zeta M}{E-m\Omega}\right)^{1/2}\,,\qquad B_{4}=\zeta\left(1-\frac{\zeta M}{E-m\Omega}\right)^{1/2}\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\omega\cos{\theta}}{E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega}\right)^{1/2}\qquad\,\quad,\,\qquad A^{\prime}_{4}=\zeta^{\prime}\left(1+\frac{\omega\cos{\theta}}{E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega}\right)^{1/2}\,,

(10b)

\displaystyle B^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\zeta^{\prime}M}{\sqrt{(E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega)^{2}-p_{z}^{\prime 2}}}\right)^{1/2}\,,\quad B^{\prime}_{4}=\zeta^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}M}{\sqrt{(E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega)^{2}-p_{z}^{\prime 2}}}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(10c)

The total intensity of photons produced from a given initial energy (associated with the quantum numbers,
n
and
a
) of the fermion is obtained by summing over the photon helicities,
h
, and averaging over the initial fermion polarizations, i.e.,
\zeta
in (
4
) which gives us the following result:

\displaystyle\Phi=\sum_{h=\pm 1}\Phi_{h}=2I^{2}_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)\Big\{\left[(K_{4}I_{n-1,n^{\prime}-1}-K_{3}I_{n,n^{\prime}})\sin\theta-(K_{1}I_{n,n^{\prime}-1}+K_{2}I_{n-1,n^{\prime}})\cos\theta\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+(K_{1}I_{n,n^{\prime}-1}-K_{2}I_{n-1,n^{\prime}})^{2}\Big\}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle W_{n,a}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\zeta,h}W^{h}_{n,a,p_{z}=0,\zeta}=\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\sum_{n^{\prime},a^{\prime}}\int\omega^{2}d\omega\int d(\cos\theta)\delta(\omega-E+E^{\prime})\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}}\Phi\right)\,.

(12)

The integral over
\omega
can be performed by integrating the delta-function (as done in
[
20
]
.) To do so, we require the solution to the equation,

\displaystyle\omega=E-E^{\prime}\,.

(13)

Conservation of momentum along the longitudinal direction dictates that

p_{z}^{\prime}=p_{z}-\omega\cos\theta\,.

(14)

Using (
2
) and (
14
), we can solve for
\omega
in (
13
), and the solution is as follows:

\omega_{0}=\frac{E-m^{\prime}\Omega-p_{z}\cos\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}\left\{1-\left[1-\frac{\mathcal{B}\sin^{2}\theta}{(E-m^{\prime}\Omega-p_{z}\cos\theta)^{2}}\right]^{1/2}\right\}\,,

(15)

where

\mathcal{B}=2(n-n^{\prime})|qB|-(m-m^{\prime})^{2}\Omega^{2}+2(m-m^{\prime})\Omega(E-m^{\prime}\Omega)\,.

(16)

Since we perform our computations in a frame where the initial longitudinal momentum vanishes (
p_{z}=0
), we have

p_{z}^{\prime}=-\omega\cos\theta\,,

(17)

\omega_{0}=\frac{E-m^{\prime}\Omega}{\sin^{2}\theta}\left\{1-\left[1-\frac{\mathcal{B}\sin^{2}\theta}{(E-m^{\prime}\Omega)^{2}}\right]^{1/2}\right\}.

(18)

The delta-function then becomes

\delta(\omega-E+E^{\prime})=\frac{\delta(\omega-\omega_{0})}{\frac{\partial(\omega-E+E^{\prime})}{\partial\omega}}=\frac{\delta(\omega-\omega_{0})}{1+\frac{\partial E^{\prime}}{\partial\omega}}\,.

(19)

The partial derivative is easily obtained using (
2
) and (
14
),

\frac{\partial E^{\prime}}{\partial\omega}=\frac{\omega\cos^{2}\theta}{E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega}\,.

(20)

We can now perform the
\omega
integral in (
12
) using (
20
) and (
19
), which gives us

W_{n,a}=\frac{q^{2}}{4\pi}\sum_{n^{\prime},a^{\prime}}\int d\theta\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}\sin\theta(E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega)}{(E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega)+\omega_{0}\cos^{2}\theta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\zeta\zeta^{\prime}}\Phi\right)\,.

(21)

In
[
1
,
2
]
, we computed the radiation intensity numerically by evaluating the sum over
n^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime}
in (
21
). We will refer to that calculation as
exact
. The scope of this work is to compute the quasi-classical approximation for the radiation intensity. The applicability of the quasi-classical approximation requires that the main contribution to the intensity comes about from the large values of the quantum numbers
n\gg 1
and
m\gg 1
, which allows treating them as continuous parameters. In particular, summations over quantum numbers can be replaced by integration. In the following two sections, we will expand the various expressions from this section in the quasi-classical limit to obtain the quasi-classical formula for the photon emission intensity.

III
WKB Approximation of the
I-
Function

In this section, we develop the quasi-classical approximation of the
I
-function. In the system under consideration, the motion of ultra-relativistic fermions is characterized by the large value of the principal quantum number
n
. Therefore, we are interested to obtain an approximation for
I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)
for large
n
and
n^{\prime}
. We follow the approach of Sokolov and Ternov in
[
18
]
, who developed the WKB approximation of the
I
-function in the non-rotating case. Rotation requires significant modifications to their method as we proceed to discuss.

First, consider the
I
-function in more detail in relation to the WKB procedure. The

-functions are solutions to the differential equation,

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}(x^{1/2}y(x))-f_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)x^{1/2}y(x)=0\,,

(22)

where

f_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)=\frac{(n-n^{\prime})^{2}-1}{4x^{2}}-\frac{(n+n^{\prime}+1)}{2x}+\frac{1}{4}\,.

(23)

The differential equation is solved by

, as defined in (
6
).

Note that mathematically, Eq. (
22

being the wave function,
f_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)
, the potential, and
0
as the energy eigenvalue. The turning points of the potential are the solutions to
f_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)=0
since “energy” is zero. The “potential” is plotted in Fig.
1

n^{\prime}

n^{\prime}

\displaystyle x_{0}=(\sqrt{n}-\sqrt{n^{\prime}})^{2}\,,\qquad x^{\prime}_{0}=(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n^{\prime}})^{2}\,.

When

However, when rotation is present,

I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)

continues to remain close to the first turning point
x_{0}
. The approximation in
[
18

would suffice to handle the case, except using the Airy function in place of the modified Bessel functions. However, the matrix element in Eq. (

I

I_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)
, which is not restricted to the region near its corresponding turning point. When
\Omega=0
, the above mentioned
I
-function does not appear because of the following property:

\displaystyle\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}I_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)^{2}=1\,.

(25)

In the non-rotating case, the rest of the matrix element does not contain any dependence on
a^{\prime}
and hence the sum of

factors out, which does not happen when
\Omega\neq 0

-function that is valid for all

I

\end{cases}

(40)

The parameters

A similar condition holds for the right turning point:

\displaystyle\delta_{3},\delta_{4}\gtrsim\frac{x_{0}^{\prime}}{(nn^{\prime})^{1/6}(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n^{\prime}})^{1/3}}\,.

(43)

IV
Quasi-Classic approximation of Intensity

We are now all set to derive the quasi-classical approximation for the radiation intensity. In addition to using the expressions for
I
-functions we derived in the previous section, the matrix elements and the phase space in Eq. (
4
) must be expanded in the ultra-relativistic limit

\displaystyle E,E^{\prime},\omega\gg M,p_{z}\,.

(44)

We also assume that the rotation is slow enough that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle|m\Omega|,|m^{\prime}\Omega|\ll E,E^{\prime},\omega\,.

(45)

Expanding the coefficients in (
10
) to the lowest order in the small parameters gives us

\displaystyle C_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(1\pm\zeta)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{M}{E}\right)\,,\qquad C_{3,4}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(1\mp\zeta)\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{M}{E}\right)\,,

(46a)

\displaystyle C_{1,2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left[(1\pm\zeta^{\prime})\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{M}{E^{\prime}}\right)\mp(1\mp\zeta^{\prime})\frac{1}{2}\frac{\omega\cos{\theta}}{E^{\prime}}\right]\,,

(46b)

\displaystyle C_{3,4}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left[(1\mp\zeta^{\prime})\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{M}{E^{\prime}}\right)\mp(1\pm\zeta^{\prime})\frac{1}{2}\frac{\omega\cos{\theta}}{E^{\prime}}\right]\,.

(46c)

Plugging the expressions in (
46
) into the equations in (
8
), we have

\displaystyle K_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left[(1+\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\pm\zeta(1+\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\frac{M\omega}{2EE^{\prime}}\mp(1-\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\frac{\omega\cos{\theta}}{2E^{\prime}}\right]\,,

(47a)

\displaystyle K_{3,4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left[(1-\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\pm\zeta(1-\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\frac{M\omega}{2EE^{\prime}}\pm(1+\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\frac{\omega\cos{\theta}}{2E^{\prime}}\right]\,.

(47b)

The matrix element in (

\displaystyle I_{n,n^{\prime}-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x}{n^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}\left[\left(\frac{n-n^{\prime}-x}{2x}\right)I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)-I^{\prime}_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)\right]\,,

(48a)

\displaystyle I_{n-1,n^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)^{1/2}\left[\left(\frac{n-n^{\prime}+x}{2x}\right)I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)+I^{\prime}_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)\right]\,,

(48b)

\displaystyle I_{n-1,n^{\prime}-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{(nn^{\prime})^{1/2}}\left[\left(\frac{n+n^{\prime}-x}{2x}\right)I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)-I^{\prime}_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)\right]\,,

(48c)

where
I_{n,n^{\prime}}
and
I^{\prime}_{n,n^{\prime}}
in the right-hand-side are replaced by their quasiclassical approximation (
35
). However, since
\Omega\neq 0
implies that the argument of the
I
-function can also be greater than the turning point
x_{0}
, we must convert the Bessel functions to Airy functions, which yields

\displaystyle I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x_{0}^{2}nn^{\prime})^{1/12}}\Ai\left((x_{0}^{2}nn^{\prime})^{1/6}\left(1-\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right)\right)\,,

(49a)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(nn^{\prime})^{1/12}}{x_{0}^{5/6}}\Ai^{\prime}\left((x_{0}^{2}nn^{\prime})^{1/6}\left(1-\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right)\right)\,.

(49b)

The argument of the
I
-functions contains the ratio

\displaystyle\frac{x}{x_{0}}=\frac{\omega^{2}-\omega^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}}{\left(\sqrt{(E-m\Omega)^{2}-M^{2}}-\sqrt{(E^{\prime}-m^{\prime}\Omega)^{2}-M^{2}-p_{z}^{2}}\right)^{2}}\,.

(50)

Expanding (
50

\displaystyle(x_{0}^{2}nn^{\prime})^{1/6}\left(1-\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right)=\left(\frac{\omega\sqrt{EE^{\prime}}}{2|qB|}\right)^{2/3}\Bigg[\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)-\frac{\Omega}{3}\left(\frac{8(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}+\frac{m}{E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{E^{\prime}}\right)\Bigg]\,.

(51)

For convenience of notation, we will define two new parameters
\varepsilon
and
\kappa
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\left(\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{M^{2}}{E^{2}}\right)-\left(\frac{E^{\prime}}{E}\right)\Bigg[\frac{\Omega}{3}\left(\frac{8(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}+\frac{m}{E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{E^{\prime}}\right)\Bigg]\,,

(52)

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{\omega E^{2}}{2|qB|E^{\prime}}\,.

(53)

Collecting (

\displaystyle I_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg\{1+\frac{\Omega}{6}\Bigg[\frac{2(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}+\frac{m}{E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\Bigg\}\Ai\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right),

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{n,n^{\prime}}(x)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa^{2/3}}\frac{E^{3/2}}{\omega E^{1/2}}\Bigg\{1+\frac{\Omega}{3}\Bigg[\frac{5(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}-\frac{m}{2E}-\frac{m^{\prime}}{2E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\Bigg\}\Ai^{\prime}\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right),

(54b)

\displaystyle I_{n,n^{\prime}-1}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg\{\left(1+\Omega\Bigg[\frac{(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{m}{6E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{6E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\right)\Ai\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\Omega}{3}\Bigg[\frac{5(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}+\frac{5m^{\prime}}{2E^{\prime}}-\frac{m}{2E}\Bigg]\right)\Ai^{\prime}\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right)\Bigg\},

(54c)

\displaystyle I_{n-1,n^{\prime}}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg\{\left(1+\Omega\Bigg[\frac{(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{E^{\prime}}{E}\right)+\frac{m}{6E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{6E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\right)\Ai\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(1+\frac{\Omega}{3}\Bigg[\frac{5(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}+\frac{5m}{2E}-\frac{m^{\prime}}{2E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\right)\Ai^{\prime}\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right)\Bigg\},

\displaystyle I_{n-1,n^{\prime}-1}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg\{\left(1+\Omega\Bigg[\frac{(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{EE^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{m}{6E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{6E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\right)\Ai\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\frac{\omega}{E^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{5\Omega}{3}\Bigg[\frac{(m-m^{\prime})}{\omega}+\frac{m}{2E}+\frac{m^{\prime}}{2E^{\prime}}\Bigg]\right)\Ai^{\prime}\left(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon\right)\Bigg\}.

Combining these expressions with the coefficients in Eq. (

\Phi

\displaystyle\Phi=

\displaystyle\frac{I^{2}_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)}{4\kappa^{2/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)\Bigg\{\frac{(1+\zeta\zeta^{\prime})}{2}\Bigg[\left(2+\frac{\omega}{E^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\Ai^{2}(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)+\Bigg(\frac{1}{\kappa^{1/3}}\left(2+\frac{\omega}{E^{\prime}}\right)\Ai^{\prime}(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle+\Bigg(\frac{\zeta M\omega}{EE^{\prime}}-\frac{(\omega+2E^{\prime})}{EE^{\prime}}(m-m^{\prime})\Omega\Bigg)\Ai(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg]+\frac{(1-\zeta\zeta^{\prime})}{2}\Bigg[\frac{\omega^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{E^{\prime 2}}\Ai^{2}(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle+\Bigg(\left(\frac{\zeta M\omega}{EE^{\prime}}+\frac{\omega(m-m^{\prime})\Omega}{EE^{\prime}}\right)\Ai(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)-\frac{\omega}{\kappa^{1/3}E^{\prime}}\Ai^{\prime}(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg]\Bigg\}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\zeta=\pm 1}\sum_{\zeta^{\prime}=\pm 1}\Phi=

\displaystyle\frac{I^{2}_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)}{4\kappa^{2/3}}\left(\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\right)\Bigg\{\Bigg[\frac{2M^{2}\omega^{2}}{E^{2}E^{\prime 2}}+\Bigg(\left(2+\frac{\omega}{E^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{E^{\prime 2}}\Bigg)\frac{(m-m^{\prime})^{2}\Omega^{2}}{E^{2}}\Bigg]\Ai^{2}(\kappa^{2/3}\varepsilon)

and

(57)

with the summation over
\Phi
given by Eq. (

VI
Summary and outlook

We developed the quasi-classical approximation for the synchrotron radiation of a fermion embedded in the rigidly rotating system and subject to the constant magnetic field. It is valid for ultra-relativistic fermions under the conditions explained in the text. We numerically verified that our semi-classical expressions accurately approximate the exact formulas. At the same time, they decrease the numerical cost of computations by at least several orders of magnitude.

In our analysis, we derived the quasi-classical approximation by expanding the exact leading order expressions. A different approach, developed by Baier and Katkov, is to compute the quasi-classical matrix element from the get-go by observing that in the ultra-relativistic limit, the photon is emitted from a short part of the classical fermion trajectory in the magnetic field
[
16
,
22
]
. It would be interesting to generalize this method to the case of rotating fermions.

In this work we focused on the dynamics of the synchrotron radiation by a single rotating fermion, but our ultimate goal is to compute the electromagnetic radiation by the quark-gluon plasma. Indeed, it was argued that its electromagnetic radiation contains a significant synchrotron radiation component
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
26
]
. While the computation of the plasma electromagnetic radiation with account of the magnetic field and rotation with exact formulas requires enormous computing resources, the quasi-classical method offers a significantly more effective approach.

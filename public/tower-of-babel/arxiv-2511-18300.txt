Elliptic solutions of the lattice CKP equation and its elliptic direct linearisation scheme
Ying-ying Sun

Abstract




Abstract:
A direct linearisation scheme, based on an elliptic Cauchy kernel, is set up for the
lattice CKP equation. This leads to an elliptic parametrisation of the lattice CKP equation, together
with its Lax triplet, which allows us to perform appropriate continuum limits and construct elliptic
solutions. By selecting
appropriate integration measures and domains for the singular linear integral equation in the scheme,
elliptic multi-soliton solutions of the lattice CKP equation are found.

Keywords
lattice CKP equation,
\tau
-function, Lax triplet, continuum limit, elliptic soliton solution

1
Introduction

The lattice CKP equation, which is connected to the hierarchy of
\mathbf{C}_{\infty}
soliton
equations of Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) type, introduced in
[
17
]
, was first given
by Kashaev in
[
18
]
, and independently by Schief in
[
30
]
.
The discovery of this fully discrete equation came late in comparison to other integrable
lattice systems, because
unlike equations of Hirota form, the
\tau
-function representation is not bilinear but a quartic
correspondence. In the notation of the current paper the lattice CKP equation can be written as

\displaystyle\Big({F}\widehat{\widetilde{\overline{F}}}+{\widetilde{F}}\widehat{\overline{F}}-\widehat{F}\widetilde{\overline{F}}-\overline{F}\widehat{\widetilde{F}}\Big)^{2}-4\left(\widehat{\widetilde{F}}\widetilde{\overline{F}}-{\widetilde{F}}\widehat{\widetilde{\overline{F}}}\right)\left(\widehat{F}\overline{F}-{F}\widehat{\overline{F}}\right)=0\ ,

(1.1)

in which
F=F_{n,m,l}
is a function of the dynamical variables (the discrete independent variables)

n,m,l\in\mathbb{Z}
and tilde-hat-bar notations

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}=F_{n+1,m,l}\ ,\quad\widehat{F}=F_{n,m+1,l}\ ,\quad\overline{F}=F_{n,m,l+1}

(1.2)

are used to express shifts on the
F
-function and similar undershifts such as
\underaccent{\tilde}{F}=F_{n-1,m,l}

(cf.
[
15
]
).
The form of the equation takes the shape of a 2
\times
2
\times
2
hyperdeterminant, due to Cayley, cf.
[
6
]
, and also
[
28
,
16
]
, if the
lattice shifts on the 3-dimensional grid are denoted by matrix indices (like
\widetilde{F}=:F_{1,0,0}
,

\widehat{F}=:F_{0,1,0}
,
\overline{F}=:F_{0,0,1}
, etc.). This intriguing form
has led to speculations that other
hyper-determinants may also be reinterpreted as integrable lattice equations, cf.
[
32
]
, but the answer to this conjecture has remained so far negative or at least inconclusive.

It is worth noting that the lattice CKP equation possesses a considerable number of compelling features. It is a direct consequence of the star-triangle relation found in connection with the
local Yang-Baxter equation,
[
22
]
, as was discovered in
[
18
]
. The left side of the equation (
1.1
) is, in fact, the Cayley hyperdeterminant of a 2
\times
2
\times
2 cubic matrix,
[
28
]
. Additionally,
the lattice CKP equation is a good example to show the connections between projective and conformal geometry and discrete integrable systems
[
30
,
31
]
, and can be regarded as a special case of the hexahedron recurrence which exhibits an cluster algebra structure and the Laurent phenomenon
[
19
]
. Moreover, we note that it is related to many other integrable systems, such as C-Toda lattice equation
[
7
]
and M-system
[
3
,
4
]
. The integrablity in the sense of multi-dimensional consistency
[
27
,
5
,
10
]
of the lattice CKP equation is concerned with the help of its relation to the symmetric principal minor assignment problem
[
16
,
32
]
.
The CKP equation also miraculously appears in connection
with bi-orthogonal polynomials with Cauchy weights,
[
8
,
9
]
, and the associated C-Toda systems which are connected to Degasperis-Procesi peakon dynamics, cf.
[
7
]
.

For the sake of constructing solutions, the parameter-free version of the lattice CKP equation
(
1.1
) is not so useful, and in
[
11
]
a version was given with lattice
parameters, i.e., parameters associated with the grid spacings, appearing in rational form. This
explicit parametrisation facilitated the construction of in particular multi-soliton solutions.
Furthermore, the presence of lattice parameters in the equation
allows one also to derive in a systematic way semi-discrete and continuum analogues of the CKP
equation, by performing well-defined limits on
the lattice parameters and corresponding lattice variables.

In the present paper
we construct elliptic soliton solutions for the CKP lattice equation, and once again this is best
done by introducing a specific elliptic parametrisation in terms of the Weierstrass
\sigma
-function
(cf.
[
1
]
for the definition and properties), namely

\displaystyle\Big(\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon-\delta)\sigma^{2}(\nu-\varepsilon)\sigma^{2}(\delta-\nu){f}\widehat{\widetilde{\overline{f}}}+\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon+\delta)\sigma^{2}(\nu-\varepsilon)\sigma^{2}(\delta+\nu){\widetilde{f}}\widehat{\overline{f}}

\displaystyle~~-\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon+\delta)\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon+\nu)\sigma^{2}(\delta-\nu)\widehat{f}\widetilde{\overline{f}}-\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon-\delta)\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon+\nu)\sigma^{2}(\delta+\nu)\overline{f}\widehat{\widetilde{f}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=4\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon-\delta)\sigma^{2}(\delta+\varepsilon)\sigma^{2}(\delta-\nu)\sigma^{2}(\delta+\nu)\left(\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon+\nu)\widetilde{\overline{f}}\widehat{\widetilde{f}}-\sigma^{2}(\nu-\varepsilon)\widehat{\widetilde{\overline{f}}}{\widetilde{f}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left(\sigma^{2}(\varepsilon+\nu)\overline{f}\widehat{f}-\sigma^{2}(\nu-\varepsilon)\widehat{\overline{f}}{f}\right)\ ,

(1.3)

where now
f
denotes the dependent variable, depending like the
F
of (
1.1
) on lattice
variables
n,m,l
, and
where
\delta
,
\varepsilon
and
\nu
are in the arguments of the
\sigma
-functions and are the
lattice parameters. The multidimensional consistency of this equation, embedding the equation
in a hypercube (a tessaract) with additional lattice directions, each associated with their
additional lattice parameter, follows from the construction of the solutions given below.

The significance of elliptic solutions to KP type and of other discrete integrable equations, not
only lies in the fact that they form a richer class of solutions that usual multi-solitons
(as the elliptic functions degenerate to trigonometric and rational functions under limits on the
moduli), but in a sense the integrable equations serve as models for specific characteristics,
such as basic and higher addition formulae for those elliptic functions,
[
23
,
21
]
,
as well as led to the introduction to new concepts within elliptic function theory, such as
elliptic
N
th root of unity,
[
26
]
, and bi-elliptic addition formulae,

[
2
]
.

The organisation of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we set up the direct linearisation (DL)
scheme, involving an elliptic Cauchy kernel, that is applicable to both the AKP and CKP system. We
introduce the main dynamical quantities and highlight which choices of so-called plane-wave
factors apply in either case. In Section 3 we focus on the CKP case, and derive the fundamental
relations that form the basis for the subsequent derivation of the dynamical relations for the
main quantities. These then lead to develop the DL scheme for elliptic type solutions of the lattice CKP system and derive the Lax pair. Section 4 is dedicated to the presentation of compact and explicit forms of elliptic multi-soliton solutions. In Section 5 we present two types
of differential-difference versions of the CKP system obtained by continuum limits:
the straight- and skew continuum limits leading to D
\Delta
Es with one continuum variables and two discrete variables. Finally, in the conclusion Section 6, we mention some
further ramifications of the results in this paper.

2
Elliptic DL scheme

Here we first set up the direct linearisation scheme for elliptic solutions of KP type lattice equations which holds for both AKP and CKP systems. Then in the next section we specify to the CKP
case, as the AKP case was already covered in
[
24
]
, cf. also
[
33
]
.

2.1
Linear integral equations

The elliptic DL scheme for the lattice CKP system commences with a pair of linear integral equations pertaining to an infinite vector denoted by vector
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\kappa}
as well as its adjoint vector
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
. These equations are exhibited by

{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\kappa}+\rho_{\kappa}\iint_{D}{\rm d}\mu(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\ell}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\ell^{\prime}}{{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa}=\rho_{\kappa}\Phi_{\xi}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}){\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa}\ ,

(2.1a)

\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}+\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\iint_{D}{\rm d}\mu(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\rho_{\ell}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\ell}{{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\Phi_{\xi}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})\ ,

(2.1b)

in which the measure
{\rm d}\mu(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
is a function of both spectral variables
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
, and the integration domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
is
any suitably chosen subset in the space of the spectral variables
\ell
and

\ell^{\prime}
.
The measure and integration domain are not explicitly specified at the beginning, but must be chosen in a manner that ensures the uniqueness of the solution to equation (
2.1
). The discrete plane wave factors
{\rho}_{\kappa}={\rho}_{\kappa;n,m,l}
and
\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime};n,m,l}
are employed to demonstrate that the entries of
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\kappa}
and
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
are wave functions of the spectral variable and the dynamical variables
n,m,l
. The infinite column vector

{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa}=(\kappa^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
and the row vector
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}=(\kappa^{\prime j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are defined by listing different powers of the spectral
variables
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
respectively. Here
\Phi
is the Lamé type function

\Phi_{x}(y)=\frac{\sigma(x+y)}{\sigma(x)\sigma(y)}\ .

(2.2)

The infinite matrix
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}
associated with the variable
\xi
can be viewed as a formal elliptic Cauchy kernel. Notably, unlike the rational case
[
11
]
, the operators
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
and
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
now appear in the linear integral equations, where they act from the left and right on
{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa}
and
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
, respectively, as index-raising operators, i.e.,

({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa})_{i}=(\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa})_{i+1}=\kappa^{i+1}\ ,\quad(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})_{i^{\prime}}=(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}+1}=(\kappa^{\prime})^{i^{\prime}+1}\ .

(2.3)

\Phi_{\xi}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}),\Phi_{\xi}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})
are commonly referred to as elliptic matrices which are formally coincides with substitutions of the index-raising operators in
the arguments of the relevant elliptic functions. Additionally, they share similarities with the notions of
\wp({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})
,
\wp(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})
,
\zeta({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})
,
\zeta(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})
, etc.,
where
\wp(x)
and
\zeta(x)
are the well-known Weierstrass functions. Finally, the formal

elliptic Cauchy kernel
is defined as

{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}=\colon\Phi_{\xi}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}+\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})){\boldsymbol{O}}\colon\ ,

(2.4)

in which
{\boldsymbol{O}}=\boldsymbol{e}\,^{t}\!\boldsymbol{e}
(with
\boldsymbol{e}
an infinite vector s.t.
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{e}=1
, and
\,{}^{t}\!\boldsymbol{e}
a dual unit vector with
\,{}^{t}\!\boldsymbol{e}\,{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa}=1
) is a rank 1 projection matrix, and the
normal ordering

\colon\cdot\colon

denotes that in a power series expansion of the elliptic function
\Phi_{\xi}
w.r.t its argument, the
powers of
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
appear on the right-hand side of
{\boldsymbol{O}}
while the powers of
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
appear on the left-hand
side.

In fact, (
2.1
) can be simplified to a compact form by introducing the following infinite matrices:

{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}=\ \iint_{D}{\rm d}\mu(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\ell}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}\Phi_{\xi}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})\ ,

(2.5)

and

{\boldsymbol{C}}=\iint_{D}{\rm d}\mu(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\,\rho_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\ell}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\ell^{\prime}}\ .

(2.6)

From (
2.1
) and the above defination of
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}
, we have compact form for
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\kappa}
and
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{u}}_{\kappa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Phi_{\xi}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})-{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}\Phi_{\xi}^{-1}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}){\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}\right){\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa}\rho_{\kappa}\ ,

(2.7a)

\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(\Phi_{\xi}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})-{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}\Phi_{\xi}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}){\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}\right)\ .

(2.7b)

Furthermore, combining these formulae results in a concise expression for the infinite matrix
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}
:

{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}=\Phi_{\xi}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})\,{\boldsymbol{C}}\,({\boldsymbol{1}}+{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}{\boldsymbol{C}})^{-1}\Phi_{\xi}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})\ ,

(2.8)

in which
\boldsymbol{1}
is the infinite unit matrix.

In order to lay the groundwork of the elliptic DL scheme, the following aims to present the dynamical characteristics of the quantities that occur in the linear integral equations.

2.2
Dynamical quantities

The
\rho_{\kappa}
and
\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}
are defined through the actions of
elementary shift operators
\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\delta}}
,
\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\varepsilon}}
,
\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\nu}}
acting as follows on the plane wave factors:

\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{p}}\rho_{\kappa}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}(\kappa)\,\rho_{\kappa}\ ,\quad\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{p}}\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}(\kappa^{\prime})\,\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\ ,\quad{\rm with}\quad\mathsf{p}=\delta,\varepsilon,\nu\ ,

(2.9)

in which the factors

\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}(\kappa)
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}(\kappa^{\prime})
will be specified below.
In what follows we shall use the following abbreviations:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{\kappa}=\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\delta}}\rho_{\kappa}\ ,\quad\widehat{\rho}_{\kappa}=\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\varepsilon}}\rho_{\kappa}\ \ ,\quad\overline{\rho}_{\kappa}=\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\nu}}\rho_{\kappa}\ \ ,

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\delta}}\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\ ,\quad\widehat{\sigma}_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\ ,\quad\overline{\sigma}_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\nu}}\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\ ,

(2.10b)

in which
\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{\delta}}
is a forward shift operation characterised by the lattice parameter
\delta
. We will also occasionally employ the notation of under-accents to denote backward shifts, i.e.

{\vrule depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt{\smash{{\mathop{\rho}\limits_{\displaystyle\widetilde{}}}}}}_{\kappa}=\mathtt{T}^{-1}_{\mathsf{\delta}}\rho_{\kappa}\ ,\quad{\vrule depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt{\smash{{\mathop{\rho}\limits_{\displaystyle\widehat{}}}}}}_{\kappa}=\mathtt{T}^{-1}_{\mathsf{\varepsilon}}\rho_{\kappa}\ ,\quad{\vrule depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt{\smash{{\mathop{\rho}\limits_{\displaystyle\bar{}}}}}}_{\kappa}=\mathtt{T}^{-1}_{\mathsf{\nu}}\rho_{\kappa}\ ,

and similarly for the actions of inverse shifts on
\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}
, and on all other
derived quantities.

The above shift relations under translations along the lattice and index-raising operators implies the relations for
\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa}
and
\,{}^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
,

(\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{p}}{\rho}_{\kappa})\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa}={\rho}_{\kappa}\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})\,\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa}\ ,\quad\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}(\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{p}}^{-1}\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}})=\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{c}}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})\,\sigma_{\kappa^{\prime}}\ ,\quad\mathsf{p}=\delta,\varepsilon,\nu\ .

(2.11)

Eq. (
2.11
) implies the following shift relations for the
matrix
{\boldsymbol{C}}
of (
2.6
),

\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{p}}({\boldsymbol{C}})\cdot\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{C}}\ .

(2.12)

Furthermore, the key property of the formal elliptic Cauchy kernel
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}
is the (infinite)
compatible set of relations:

\mathtt{T}_{\mathsf{p}}({{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}})\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})-\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}){\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{p}}\ ,

(2.13)

in which
\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{p}}
are finite-rank projector labelled by
\mathsf{p}
.
Henceforth we will simply write
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}

for the operators
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})

respectively. Eq. (
2.13
) could be thought of as a definition of
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}

(instead of (
2.4
)),
noting that it is nontrivial to find operators
\mathcal{R}_{\mathsf{p}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}
, and corresponding projector
\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{p}}
, s.t.
the relation holds for all
\mathsf{p}
simultaneously, while
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}
does not
depend explicitly on
\mathsf{p}
. If we find such a solution, the construction that follows
guarantees the multidimensional consistency of the corresponding equations that are
covariantly constructed.

2.12
) and (
2.13

•

(2.15a)

(2.15b)

and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{p}}

AKP system:

3.1
Basic shift relations for the parameter-dependent quantities - CKP case

Focusing now exclusively on the CKP case we have the following central relation for
the elliptic Cauchy kernel:

\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}}\,\frac{\Phi_{\delta}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}{\Phi_{\delta}(-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}-\frac{\Phi_{\delta}(-\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}{\Phi_{\delta}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}\,{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{\xi}=\sigma(2\delta)\,\Phi_{\widetilde{\xi}}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}+\delta)\,{\boldsymbol{O}}\,\Phi_{\xi}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}-\delta)\ ,

(3.5)

which, together with (
2.4
) follows from the specific elliptic identity:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\delta}(\lambda)\,\Phi_{\delta}(\kappa)\,\Phi_{\xi-2\delta}(\kappa+\lambda)-\Phi_{\delta}(-\lambda)\,\Phi_{\delta}(-\kappa)\,\Phi_{\xi}(\kappa+\lambda)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\xi-\delta}(\lambda)\,\Phi_{\xi-\delta}(\kappa)\,\frac{\sigma(2\delta)\,\sigma^{2}(\xi-\delta)}{\sigma(\xi)\,\sigma^{2}(\delta)\,\sigma(\xi-2\delta)}=-\sigma(2\delta)\,\Phi_{-\delta}(\kappa)\,\Phi_{\delta}(\lambda)\,\Phi_{\xi-2\delta}(\lambda+\delta)\,\Phi_{\xi}(\kappa-\delta)\ .

The dynamics in the
\mathsf{p}=\delta

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{C}}\frac{\Phi_{\delta}(-\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}{\Phi_{\delta}(\,^{t\!}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}=\frac{\Phi_{\delta}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}{\Phi_{\delta}(-{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}})}{\boldsymbol{C}}\ ,

(3.7)

while

\xi=\xi_{0}-2n\delta-2m\varepsilon-2l\nu\ \quad\Rightarrow\quad\widetilde{\xi}=\xi-2\delta\ .

(3.8)

\tau

\widetilde{}

\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{\tau}\hfil$\crcr\kern 1.5pt\cr$\mathchar 869\relax$\crcr\kern 1.0pt\cr}}}\limits=\left(1-\boldsymbol{h}(\xi,\delta,\delta\right)s_{\delta,\delta}){\tau}\ ,

(3.10)

4
Elliptic soliton solution structure

It is clear that exact solutions of the CKP equation (

are constants, and

is the imaginary unit. In this sense, the measure exhibits symmetry, i.e.,

. Therefore, the linear integral equation (

) is reduced to

(4.2)

Putting
\kappa=\kappa_{s}
, where
s=1,2,\dots,N
in the above equation (
4.2
), then from equation (
4.2
), we have a linear
system for the quantities

(4.3)

in which

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{R}={\rm diag}\Big(\rho_{\kappa_{1}}\Phi_{\xi}(\kappa_{1}),\rho_{\kappa_{2}}\Phi_{\xi}(\kappa_{2}),\dots,\rho_{\kappa_{N}}\Phi_{\xi}(\kappa_{N})\Big)\ ,

(4.4)

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N}
is an identity matrix of size

and
\boldsymbol{M}
is an

M_{j,i}=\rho_{\kappa_{i}}\Phi_{\xi}(\kappa_{i}+\kappa_{j})\sigma_{\kappa_{j}}\ .

(4.5)

The discrete plane wave factors are given by

\rho_{\kappa_{i}}=\sigma_{\kappa_{i}}=\left(\frac{\Phi_{\delta}(\kappa_{i})}{\Phi_{\delta}(-\kappa_{i})}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\kappa_{i})}{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\kappa_{i})}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(\kappa_{i})}{\Phi_{\nu}(-\kappa_{i})}\right)^{l}\rho_{i}^{0}\ ,\quad(i=1,\dots,N)\ .

) or (

) could be written out explicitly.
Making use of (

) and (

) we can also obtain the explicit expression for
{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{U}}_{\xi}=\left(\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa_{1}},\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa_{2}},\dots,\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa_{N}}\right)\,\boldsymbol{R}\,({\boldsymbol{I}}_{N}+\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{M})^{-1}\boldsymbol{A}\,\boldsymbol{R}\,\,{}^{t}\left(\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa_{1}},\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa_{2}},\dots,\boldsymbol{c}_{\kappa_{N}}\right)\ ,

Moreover, we can write the quantities
introduced in (

(4.6)

(4.7)

Straight continuum limit:

The discrete plane wave factor is given by
\nu\to 0

l\to\infty

\rho_{\kappa}=\left(\frac{\Phi_{\delta}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\delta}(-\kappa)}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\kappa)}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\nu}(-\kappa)}\right)^{l}\quad\to\quad e^{2\zeta(\kappa)x}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\delta}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\delta}(-\kappa)}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\kappa)}\right)^{m}.

While in this limit the variable
\xi=\xi_{0}-2n\delta-2m\varepsilon-2l\nu\to\xi_{0}-2x-2n\delta-2m\varepsilon
. Now we impose this limit on the lattice equation (

). Using the above limit and the expansions in powers of
\nu

\displaystyle\Big(-\sigma^{2}(\delta-\epsilon)\,f\,\partial_{x}\widehat{\widetilde{f}}+\sigma^{2}(\delta-\epsilon)\Big(4\,(\zeta(\delta)+\zeta(\epsilon))\,f+\partial_{x}f\Big)\widehat{\widetilde{f}}

The discrete plane wave factor is given by

\rho_{\kappa}=\left(\frac{\Phi_{\delta}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\delta}(-\kappa)}\right)^{N}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\kappa)}{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\kappa)}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(\kappa)\Phi_{\delta}(-\kappa)}{\Phi_{\nu}(-\kappa)\Phi_{\delta}(\kappa)}\right)^{l},

(n,m,l)\to(N=n+l,m,l)

While in this limit the variable

6
Conclusions and discussions

In this paper, we have set up a DL scheme for elliptic solutions of the CKP equation,
based on integral equations with the elliptic Cauchy kernel. From the scheme we constructed
elliptic
N
-soliton solutions of the lattice CKP equation, but in principle it also
provides more generally to inverse scattering type solutions.
Furthermore, the DL structure not only yields exact solutions, but also
establishes relationships between the various equations in the scheme, i.e.
equations for the quantities
u
,
v_{\alpha}
and
s_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Furthermore, as already shown in
[
11
,
13
]
, it
helps to unify the various branches of the discrete and semi-discrete
KP systems.

The fully discrete KP family of equations is often regarded as one of the most fundamental
integrable systems, since many discrete and continuous integrable systems in 1+1 dimensions and
1-dimensional systems, such as the Toda chain and Calogero-Moser type systems of ODEs can be
obtained as dimensional reductions. In particular elliptic pole-type solutions can be obtained
from the lattice KP systems, cf.
[
25
,
20
,
29
]
, and
[
34
]
for a review.

As an outgrowth of the results in this paper the following directions of
progress can be considered:
to develop the generating PDE for the CKP system, i.e. the associated
(system of) continuous equations in terms of the Miwa variables;
the construction of pole-type solutions from the straight continuum limit
of the CKP equation leading to elliptic multi-particle systems, cf. e.g.
[
20
]
,
and a systematic study of reductions to 1+1-dimensional equations by
imposing further conditions on the measures
{\rm d}\mu(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
.
Also ahead lie the question about the construction of algebro-geometric solutions, and how to extend the results to the DKP equation, cf.
[
14
]
.

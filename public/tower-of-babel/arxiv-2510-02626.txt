Well-Posedness for the Euler Equations in Function Spaces of Generalized Smoothness
Nicholas Harrison, Zachary Radke

Abstract
. We consider the question of well-posedness for the incompressible Euler equations in generalized function spaces of the type B p , q s , ψ ​ ( ℝ d ) B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) and F p , q s , ψ ​ ( ℝ d ) F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) where ψ \psi is a slowly varying function in the Karamata sense and s = d / p + 1 s=d/p+1 . We prove that if ψ \psi grows fast enough, then there is a local in time solution to the Euler equations. We also establish a BKM-type criterion that allows us to obtain global existence in two dimensions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

We consider the persistence of regularity of solutions to the incompressible Euler equations, given by

(
E
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\text{div }u=0&\text{ in }[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

where
u:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents the velocity field of an ideal fluid and
p:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the scalar pressure. Using the incompressibility condition of the velocity field, we can determine the pressure from the velocity as
p=(-\Delta)^{-1}\text{div}(u\cdot\nabla u)
. We also define the vorticity of the fluid as a matrix with
ij
-entry
\omega_{ij}=\partial_{j}u_{i}-\partial_{i}u_{j}
. The velocity can be recovered from the vorticity using the relation
u_{j}=\partial_{i}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega_{ij}
where we sum over repeated indices. We often abuse notation and write this relation as
u=\text{div}((-\Delta)^{-1}\omega)
. This relation is known as the Biot-Savart law. We then have the vorticity equation

(
V
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\omega\cdot\nabla u&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u=\text{div}((-\Delta)^{-1}\omega)&\text{ in }[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\omega|_{t=0}=\omega_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

In two dimensions, we see the vorticity depends only on
\omega_{21}=\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1}
. This coincides with the notion of treating the two-dimensional velocity field as three-dimensional, with the
z
-component being zero. Then, taking the curl shows that the vorticity only has a
z
-component given by
\omega_{21}
. In turn we see the vorticity must be orthogonal to the velocity gradient in two dimensions. This greatly simplifies the structure of (

V

) to the transport equation

(
V_{2}
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=0&\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega&\text{ in }[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\omega|_{t=0}=\omega_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

The solutions to (

V
2

) are given by
\omega(t,x)=\omega_{0}(t,X_{t}^{-1}(x))
where
X_{t}^{-1}
is the inverse of the flow map
X_{t}(x)
, which satisfies the ODE

(1)

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}X_{t}(x)=u(t,X_{t}(x))\quad&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
X_{t}(x)|_{t=0}=x\quad&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

When studying the well-posedness of (

E

), a common strategy is to study weak solutions and then show that the weak solution is sufficiently smooth.

Definition 1.1
.

A vector field
u:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a weak solution to (

E

) with initial data
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
if

(1)

u\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(2)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{u\partial_{t}\phi+u_{k}u_{l}\partial_{x_{l}}\phi_{k}\right\}\,dx\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{u(T)\cdot\phi(T)-u(0)\cdot\phi(0)\right\}\,dx

for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\text{div }\phi=0
, where we sum over repeated indices, and

(3)

\text{div }u=0
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

1.2.
Prior Work

The study of (

E

) in various function spaces has been extensively investigated. One of the first results was from
[
Lic25
]
, where the author establishes local well-posedness in the spaces
C^{k,\gamma}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
where
k\geq 1,0<\gamma<1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Global well-posedness in these spaces was then established in
[
Wol33
]
. In the context of Sobolev spaces, local well-posedness in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
was established in
[
KP86
]
with
s>\frac{2}{p}+1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. These solutions are then extended to global solutions using the BKM criterion
[
BKM84
]
, which states that a solution on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be extended if and only if
\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T];L^{\infty})}<\infty
. We also note there are short-time analogues in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the above results.

The question about regularity in critical Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s=\frac{2}{p}+1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
remained unanswered for quite some time after. Here, criticality can be understood in the sense of being barely unable to close the estimate
\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{s,p}}
. In
[
BL15
]
, the authors establish strong ill-posedness in these function spaces. The dichotomy between well-posedness and strong ill-posedness at the critical regime has inspired work in Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, as these spaces offer finer embeddings. In
[
Cha03
]
and
[
Cha04
]
the author establishes local well-posedness in the spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
provided that
s>d/p+1
. Note that under these assumptions, the velocity field is
Lipschitz. In the critical case, local well-posedness is established in
B^{d/p+1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F^{d+1}_{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and global well-posedness is shown when
d=2
(see
[
Vis98
]
and
[
Cha03
]
). There have also been ill-posedness results in scaling critical Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, which were established in
[
BL15
]
.

Another way to refine the scale of
H^{s}
Sobolev spaces is to include an extra factor which imposes additional sub-power decay on the Fourier transform
1
1

1

Throughout this paper we use the convention

\hat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,dx
to denote the Fourier transform of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
.
. In particular, we assume that
\psi
is a slowly varying function in the Karamata sense (see definition
2.7
) and define the space
H^{s,\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the set of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the following norm is finite,

(2)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s,\psi}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\psi^{2}(|\xi|)|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

These spaces are known as Hörmander spaces. Using Proposition
2.8
one can show that
\psi
grows slower than any power function. It then follows that

\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}H^{s+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=H^{s^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset H^{s,\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

with all of the above containments being strict. When
\psi(t)=\log^{\alpha}(e+t)
, sharp estimates in related function spaces are established in
[
BN19
]
and then applied to the study of continuity equations in
[
BN21
]
. Later in
[
CCS24
]
weak solutions to the two-dimensional Euler equations in
H^{0,\psi}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
are studied, and in
[
MS22
]
, the existence of weak solutions to transport equations in
H^{0,\psi}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
is established provided that
u\in L^{1}((0,T):W^{1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{p}{2}
for some
1<p<\infty
.

When studying the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin variants of Hörmander spaces, we recall that the Littlewood-Paley operator roughly projects frequencies on the scale of
2^{j}
, so we replace the factor of
2^{js}
by
2^{js}\psi(2^{j})
. Again, in the case of the logarithm, we see that
\log^{\alpha}(e+2^{j})\sim(1+j)^{\alpha}
. These spaces in which we replace
2^{js}
by
2^{js}(1+j)^{\alpha}
are notated as
B^{s,\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F^{s,\alpha}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and are studied extensively in
[
DT18
]
.

In this work, we consider the scaling critical (
s=d/p+1)
Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces indexed by a slowly varying function notated
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We show that if
\psi
grows sufficiently fast, then the Euler equations are locally well-posed in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
. We also prove a BKM-type inequality which allows us to continue our solution provided that
\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1})}<\infty
. As an immediate corollary, we obtain global well-posedness when
d=2
. We remark that this result is sharp. In a forthcoming paper, we prove that (

V

) is strongly ill-posed in
H^{1,\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
where
\psi(t)=\log^{\alpha}(1+t)
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
which lies strictly above the ill-posedness result of
[
BL15
]
and just below the spaces we consider in the well-posedness results below.

Main Results

We state our main results below. See Definitions
2.9
and
2.12
for the function spaces and admissible
\psi

Theorem 1.2

(Local existence in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and blow up criterion)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote either
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or
F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose
s=d/p+1
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{q^{\prime}}
if
X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{p^{\prime}}

X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, given
u_{0}\in X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a
T>0
and a unique solution

to (

E

) such that

u\in C([0,T];X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Furthermore, the solution

blows up at time

\limsup_{t\to T^{*}}\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}=\infty,

if and only if

As a corollary, we obtain global existence in two dimensions.

, and

1.4.
Notation and Organization of Paper

We next define some of the notation that we will use throughout this paper. Consider two quantities
A
and
B
parameterized by some index set
\Lambda
, we then say that
A\lesssim B
if there exists some
C>0
such that
A(\lambda)\leq CB(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. If we wish to highlight the dependence of
C
on a parameter, say
b
, we write
A\lesssim_{b}B
. We write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. Furthermore, we let
C>0
denote a positive constant that may change from line to line. If we wish to highlight the dependence of the constant
C
on a parameter
b
, we write
C=C(b)
.

We organize the paper as follows. In the Section
2
we cover preliminaries such as Littlewood-Paley theory, function spaces, and slowly varying functions. In Section
3
we establish an a priori estimate of smooth solutions to (

E

) in the spaces
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Using this a priori estimate, we construct a sequence of approximating solutions and then show that the limit of this sequence is a classical solution to (

E

4
we repeat the same argument but in the spaces

While these are known in the classical Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, we are not able to conclude without proof that these same estimates hold in these function spaces of generalized smoothness. The proofs follow quite similarly to those in the literature, but we include them for completeness.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Littlewood-Paley Theory

Before defining our function spaces, we begin with a brief overview of the Littlewood-Paley operators. We closely follow
[
Che95
]
.

It is well known that if
\mathcal{C}
is the annulus of center
0
, inner radius
3/5
, and outer radius
5/3
, then there exists two positive radial functions
\hat{\chi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{5/6}(0))
and
\hat{\varphi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
such that, if
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, then we set
\varphi_{j}(x)=2^{jd}\varphi(2^{j}x)
and

\hat{\chi}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\hat{\varphi}_{j}=\hat{\chi}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\hat{\varphi}(2^{-j}\cdot)\equiv 1.

For
n\geq 0
we define
\chi_{n}
via its Fourier transform,
\hat{\chi}_{n}
, as

\hat{\chi}_{n}(\xi)=\hat{\chi}(\xi)+\sum_{j\leq n}\hat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)\text{ for }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define the operator
S_{n}
as

S_{n}f=\chi_{n}\ast f.

Formally,
S_{n}
can be thought of as a projection in Fourier space into the ball of radius of order
2^{n}
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the inhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators
\Delta_{j}
by

\Delta_{j}f=\begin{cases}0,\hskip 28.45274pt&\text{if }j<-1,\\
\chi\ast f,&\text{if }j=-1,\\
\varphi_{j}\ast f,&\text{if }j\geq 0,\end{cases}

and for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we define the homogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}f=\varphi_{j}\ast f.

We also define the fattened Littlewood-Paley operator
\tilde{\Delta}_{j}
as
\tilde{\Delta}_{j}=\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1}
. One easily verifies that
\Delta_{j}=\tilde{\Delta}_{j}\Delta_{j}
. Up to constants, one can formally think of
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
as a projection in Fourier space into the annulus of inner and outer radius of order
2^{j}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that
u=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}u
where the limit holds in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Or equivalently

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}\Delta_{j}u\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

One benefit of using Littlewood-Paley operators can be seen in Bernstein’s Lemma. Heuristically, Bernstein’s Lemma states that if the support of
\hat{u}
is included in some ball
B_{\lambda}(0)
, then a derivative of
u
costs no more than a power of
\lambda
when taking an
L^{p}
norm. This will be made precise below. We let
\mathcal{C}_{a,b}(0)
denote the annulus with inner radius
a
and outer radius
b
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Bernstein’s Lemma) Let
(r_{1},r_{2})
be a pair of strictly positive real numbers such that
r_{1}<r_{2}
and let
\lambda
denote a positive real number. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, such that, for every integer
k
, every pair of real numbers
(a,b)
satisfying
b\geq a\geq 1
, and every function
u\in L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1)

If
\supp{\hat{u}}\subset B_{r_{1}\lambda}(0)
then

\sup_{\mid\!\alpha\!\mid=k}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{b}}\leq C^{k}\lambda^{k+d(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}.

(2)

Furthermore, if
\supp{\hat{u}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{r_{1}\lambda,r_{2}\lambda}(0)
then

C^{-k}\lambda^{k}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}\leq\sup_{\mid\!\alpha\!\mid=k}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}\leq C^{k}\lambda^{k}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}.

Later on, it will be useful to decompose the product of two functions into their individual phase blocks. Formally, for two tempered distributions
f
and
g
we write their product as

fg=\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\Delta_{j}f\Delta_{j^{\prime}}g.

We then split this sum into three different parts. The first is when the low frequencies of
f
multiply the high frequencies of
g
, the second is when the low frequencies of
g
multiply the high frequencies of
f
, and the third is when
j
and
j^{\prime}
are comparable. This gives the nonhomogeneous Bony paraproduct decomposition.

Definition 2.2
.

The nonhomogeneous paraproduct of
f
by
g
is defined by

T_{g}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}S_{j-2}g\Delta_{j}f.

The nonhomogeneous remainder of
f
and
g
is defined by

R(f,g)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,j^{\prime}\\
|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Delta_{j}f\Delta_{j^{\prime}}g.

We then have the following nonhomogeneous Bony paraproduct decomposition

(3)

fg=T_{f}g+T_{g}f+R(f,g)\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We also define the homogeneous Bony paraproduct decomposition

(4)

fg=\dot{T}_{f}g+\dot{T}_{g}f+\dot{R}(f,g)\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

by replacing the Littlewood-Paley operators in (
3
) with their homogeneous counterpart and where for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we define
\dot{S}_{n}f=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{n}\dot{\Delta}_{j}f
.

2.2.
Function Spaces

Definition 2.3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and

. We define the Besov space

We also define the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}}&=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jsq}\mid\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\!\mid^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}.\end{split}

We remark that if

Definition 2.7
.

We let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of all functions
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

We have the following representation of slowly varying functions from

Proposition 2.8

A function

\psi(t)=c(t)\exp\left(\int_{a}^{t}\frac{\varepsilon(s)}{s}\,ds\right),

where
a>0
,
c(t)
is measurable,
c(t)\to c>0
, and
\varepsilon(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty.

Using (
9
), it is straightforward to show that if
\psi
is slowly varying, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{t^{\beta}}=0\text{ for any }\beta>0.

A basic example of a slowly varying function is
\psi(t)=\log^{\alpha}(e+t)-1
for
\alpha>0
, which satisfies (
9
) with
\varepsilon(s)=\frac{\alpha}{\log(e+s)},a=e-1,
and
c=1
. Surprisingly, one can construct a slowly varying function
\psi
that experiences infinite oscillation in the sense that
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\psi(x)=0
and
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\psi(x)=\infty
. The construction of such a function can be found in
[
BKS13
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty],s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
. We define the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces of generalized smoothness,
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively, as the set
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the following quasinorms are finite

(10)

\begin{split}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}&=\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{jsq}\psi^{q}(2^{j})\left\lVert\Delta_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{q}\right)^{1/q},\\
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}&=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{jsq}\psi^{q}(2^{j})\mid\!\Delta_{j}f\!\mid^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\rVert_{L^{p}},\end{split}

with the obvious modifications when
q=\infty
.
We also define the homogeneous spaces
\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the set of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the following quasinorms are finite

(11)

\begin{split}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}&=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jsq}\psi^{q}(2^{j})\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{q}\right)^{1/q},\\
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}&=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jsq}\psi^{q}(2^{j})\mid\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\!\mid^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\rVert_{L^{p}},\end{split}

with the obvious modifications when
q=\infty
.

For properties of these spaces, such as completeness and independence of the dyadic partition of unity, we refer the reader to
[
dM01
]
. We remark that if
X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is either
F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one can show
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\dot{X}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}
defines an equivalent norm on
X^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Suppose that
s\geq d/p
,
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. If

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t\psi^{q^{\prime}}(t)}<\infty,

then
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s-d/p}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and appeal to the classical embeddings of Besov spaces. Indeed, note that

\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{js}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}\frac{1}{\psi^{q^{\prime}}(2^{j})}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

Then,

\sum_{j\geq-1}\frac{1}{\psi^{q^{\prime}}(2^{j})}\sim\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\psi^{q^{\prime}}(2^{x})}\,dx\sim\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t\psi^{q^{\prime}}(t)}\,dt,

where in the last inequality we took the substitution
x=\log_{2}(t)
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose that
s\geq d/p
.
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Then
F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s-\frac{d}{p}}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t\psi^{p^{\prime}}(t)}\,dt<\infty.

Proof.

We begin by recalling that if
sp\geq d
, then
F^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s-\frac{d}{p}}_{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see 11.4(iii) in
[
Tri97
]
). A similar argument yields
F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s-\frac{d}{p},\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, by Hölder’s inequality,
B^{s-\frac{d}{p},\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow B^{s-\frac{d}{p}}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
((\psi(2^{j}))^{-1})\in l^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1})
which, as in the proof above, is equivalent to the condition that
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t\psi^{p^{\prime}}(t)}\,dt<\infty
.
∎

Definition 2.12
.

Let
r\in[1,\infty].
We say that
\psi\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
if

(1)

\psi\in\mathcal{M}
.

(2)

\psi
is non-decreasing.

(3)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t\psi^{r}(t)}\,dt<\infty.

A basic example of such a function is
\psi(t)=\log^{\alpha}(e+t)-1
for
\alpha>\frac{1}{r}.
We do not consider oscillatory slowly varying functions since we are only concerned about the local smoothness of the functions in these generalized spaces, which is parametrized by the growth rate of
\psi
at infinity. Furthermore, when proving calculus estimates in the appendix, we lean heavily on the fact that
\psi
is non-decreasing. If
\psi
were to have a monotone equivalence, the results would still follow. But, in general, not every slowly varying function has a monotone equivalence (see e.g.
[
BKS13
]
).

Theorem 3.1

Suppose

Proof.

We begin by applying
\Delta_{j}
to both sides of
\eqref{euler velocity}_{1}
to see

(13)

\partial_{t}\Delta_{j}u+u\cdot\nabla\Delta_{j}u=[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]u-\Delta_{j}\nabla p,

where

be the flow map the solving the ordinary differential equation

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}X_{t}(x)=u(t,X_{t}(x)),\\
X_{t}(x)|_{t=0}=x.\end{cases}

Using the chain rule we rewrite (

\frac{d}{dt}(\Delta_{j}u(t,X_{t}(x)))=[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]u(t,X_{t}(x))-\Delta_{j}\nabla p(t,X_{t}(x)).

Applying

to both sides of (

\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}
yields

(16)

\frac{d}{dt}(\Delta_{j}u(t,X_{t}(x)))=\tilde{\Delta}_{j}[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]u(t,X_{t}(x))-\Delta_{j}\nabla p(t,X_{t}(x)).

Integrating (
16

(17)

\left\lVert\Delta_{j}u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert\Delta_{j}u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{\Delta}_{j}[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]u(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\nabla p(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right\}\,d\tau.

Multiplying by
2^{js}\psi(2^{j})
, taking the

norm in

, and using Minkowski’s inequality yields

(18)

\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\leq\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla p(\tau)\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\,d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\sum_{j\geq-1}\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\tilde{\Delta}_{j}[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]u(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)^{q}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\,d\tau.

By Proposition
A.6
we see

(19)

\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\sum_{j\geq-1}\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\tilde{\Delta}_{j}[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]u(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)^{q}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\,d\tau\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla u(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u(\tau)\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\,d\tau.

We next estimate the pressure. Recall

Estimating the homogeneous norm first gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla p\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\lVert\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\lVert\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s-1,\psi}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}},

where in the last inequality we used Lemma
A.1
.
Combining this with the estimate

\left\lVert\nabla p\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\lVert(u\cdot\nabla)u\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}},

allows us to conclude

(20)

\left\lVert\nabla p\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

Substituting (
19
) and (

\left\lVert u(t)\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\leq\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\nabla u(\tau)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u(\tau)\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\,d\tau.

∎

4.
Well-Posedness in Generalized Triebel-Lizorkin Spaces

Having established well-posedness for the Euler equations in Besov spaces of generalized smoothness, we now extend the result to the Triebel–Lizorkin scale. Unlike Besov spaces, which measure dyadic pieces in sequence spaces, Triebel–Lizorkin spaces combine them pointwise before integration, and it naturally includes Sobolev spaces. In particular, recall that
F^{s,\psi}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=W^{s,p,\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

A.1.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s>0
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p_{2},q}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{r_{1}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{r_{2},q}},

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{{B}^{s,\psi}_{p_{2},q}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{r_{1}}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{{B}^{s,\psi}_{r_{2},q}}.

Consequently, if

We first consider the homogeneous spaces
\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We use Bony’s paraproduct decomposition to write
fg=\dot{T}_{f}g+\dot{T}_{g}f+\dot{R}(f,g)
. Then

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{j}(fg)

\displaystyle=\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{T}_{f}g+\dot{T}_{g}f+\dot{R}(f,g))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-2}f\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)+\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-2}g\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f)+\sum_{|i^{\prime}-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}}f\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g).

\displaystyle:=I+II+III.

Due to the support of the Littlewood-Paley operators we see that

(64)

\left\lVert I\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}\left\lVert\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-2}f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}.

Similarly, we see that

(65)

\left\lVert II\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}\left\lVert\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-2}g\right\rVert_{L^{r_{1}}}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{r_{2}}}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{r_{1}}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{r_{2}}},

and

Then, by Minkowski’s inequality we see that

\displaystyle\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{\dot{B}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}(fg)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{r_{1}}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{L^{r_{2}}}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-5}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle:=\{I\}+\{II\}+\{III\}.

By Young’s convolution inequality for series we estimate

\displaystyle\{I\}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|\varphi(z)(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-2}f\dot{\Delta}_{j}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)\right|dz\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|\varphi(z)(\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-2}f\dot{\Delta}_{j}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dz\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(z)|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dz\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(z)|\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g(\cdot)\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}},

where in the last inequality we used the translation invariance of the
L^{p}
norm. Now, observe that by Young’s convolution inequality for series

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g(\cdot)\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4\}}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g(\cdot)\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{|j|\leq 4}2^{js}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

Thus,
\{I\}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}
. An identical argument yields

\{II\}\lesssim\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

It remains to estimate
\{III\}
. We have that

\displaystyle\{III\}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i^{\prime}-j^{\prime}|\leq 1\\
\max\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\geq j-5\end{subarray}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}}f\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i^{\prime}-j^{\prime}|\leq 1\\
\max\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\geq j-5\end{subarray}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(2^{j}(\cdot-y))(\dot{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}}f\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)(y)dy\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|i^{\prime}-j^{\prime}|\leq 1\\
\max\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\geq j-5\end{subarray}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(z)(\dot{\Delta}_{i^{\prime}}f\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)dz\right|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{j^{\prime}-j\geq 5\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\varphi(z)(\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)\right|dz\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(z)|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{j^{\prime}-j\geq 5\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|(\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dz\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(z)|\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{j^{\prime}-j\geq 5\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|(\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)(\cdot-2^{-j}z)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{j^{\prime}-j\geq 5\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|(\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g)(\cdot)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}},

where in the last inequality we used the translation invariance of
L^{p}
. Applying Young’s convolution inequality for series once again yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{j^{\prime}-j\geq 5\}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g(\cdot)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{j^{\prime}-j\geq 5\}}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g(\cdot)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

We have shown

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

To obtain the nonhomogeneous estimate we add the following inequality

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right).

∎

A.2.
Commutator Estimates

Proposition A.3
.

Let
(p,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
, or
p=q=\infty
. Let
u
be a divergence free vector field and
\omega=\text{curl}(u)
. Then for
s>0

(67)

\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})([u\cdot\nabla,\dot{\Delta}_{j}]\omega)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}+\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

And for
s>-1

(68)

\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})([u\cdot\nabla,\dot{\Delta}_{j}]\omega)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}+\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

Proof.

We mirror the proof of Proposition 4.1 from
[
CMZ10
]
. Using Einstein summation notation over repeated indices
k\in[1,d]
and a homogeneous paraproduct decomposition, we write

\displaystyle[u\cdot\nabla,\dot{\Delta}_{j}]\omega

\displaystyle=u^{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega-\dot{\Delta}_{j}u^{k}\partial_{k}\omega

\displaystyle=T_{u^{k}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega+T_{\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega}u^{k}+R(u^{k},\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\dot{\Delta}_{j}T_{u^{k}}\partial_{k}\omega-\dot{\Delta}_{j}T_{\partial_{k}\omega}u^{k}-\dot{\Delta}_{j}R(u^{k},\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=[T_{u^{k}},\dot{\Delta}_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega+T^{\prime}_{\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega}u^{k}-\dot{\Delta}_{j}T_{\partial_{k}\omega}u^{k}-\dot{\Delta}_{j}R(u^{k},\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle:=I+II+III+IV,

where
T^{\prime}_{u}v=T_{u}v+R(u,v)
.

Estimate of
I
:
We write

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq 1}S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega-\dot{\Delta}_{j}\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq 1}S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}[S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k},\dot{\Delta}_{j}]\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega

where we used
\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}(S_{j-1}f\dot{\Delta}_{j}f)=0
for
|j^{\prime}-j|>4
. Continuing, we expand out the convolution to see that

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}(x)-S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}(y))2^{jd}\varphi(2^{j}(x-y))\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega(y)dy\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}(x)-S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}(y))2^{j(d+1)}(\partial_{k}\varphi)(2^{j}(x-y))\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega(y)dy\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\left\{\left\lVert\nabla S_{j^{\prime}-1}u^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{j}|x-y|2^{jd}|\nabla\varphi(2^{j}(x-y))||\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega(y)|dy\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\left\lVert S_{j^{\prime}-1}\nabla u^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}|\mathcal{M}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega(\cdot)|,

where in the second equality we integrated by parts and used the fact that
u
is divergence free, in the first inequality we used the mean value theorem, and in the last inequality we used Lemma
2.6
since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{j(d+1)}|x||\varphi(2^{j}x)|\,dx
is finite and independent of
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, multiplying by
2^{js}\psi(2^{j})
, taking the
l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
norm in
j
, and then taking the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm we have

(69)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|I|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}\psi(2^{j})\mathcal{M}(2^{j^{\prime}s}|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{M}(2^{j^{\prime}s}|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}.\\
\end{split}

Then by Young’s convolution inequality for series we have

(70)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{M}(2^{j^{\prime}s}|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}&\leq\left(\sum_{|j|\leq 4}2^{js}\right)\left\lVert\mathcal{M}(2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert 2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used Lemma
2.5
. Substituting this into (
69
) we find that

(71)

\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|I|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.

Estimate of
II
:
Using the definition of
II
we write

|II|\leq\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}|S_{j^{\prime}+1}\partial_{k}(\dot{\Delta}_{j}\omega)\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\leq\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}\left\lVert\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|

Then, utilizing Young’s convolution inequality for series, we have

(72)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|II|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\simeq\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq-1}2^{(j-j^{\prime})s}\chi_{\{j-j^{\prime}\leq 2\}}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}\chi_{\{j\leq 2\}}2^{js}\right)\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq-1}\left(2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.\end{split}

Estimate of
III
:

We begin by writing

|III|\leq\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\omega\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k})|\lesssim\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}|\mathcal{M}(S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\omega\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k})|\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}|\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|)|.

Then applying an identical argument that we used to obtain (
71
) we find

(73)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|III|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.\end{split}

Estimate of
IV
:
Expanding out the convolution we have

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle=\left|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq-1}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}\right|=\left|\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{jd}\varphi(2^{j}(x-y))(\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k})(y)dy\right|.

Since
u
is divergence free we then integrate by parts to see that

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{j}2^{jd}(\partial_{k}\varphi)(2^{j}(x-y)))(\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k})(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{j}\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{j}\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\left\lVert\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Then by Young’s convolution inequality and Bernstein’s Lemma we have

(74)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|IV|\right\rVert_{l^{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{j(s+1)}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{M}(|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{(j-j^{\prime})(s+1)}\left\lVert 2^{j^{\prime}}\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{M}(|2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}\omega|)\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{F^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}.\end{split}

Then summing (
71
), (
72
), (
73
), and (
74
) yields (
67
).

To see why (
68
) holds we only need to modify the arguments for
II
and
III
.

Second estimate for
II

Applying Bernstein’s Lemma gives

|II|\leq\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}|S_{j^{\prime}+1}\partial_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\omega\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\\
\lesssim\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{j}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|.

Then since

, by Young’s convolution inequality we have

(75)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|II|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{j(s+1)}\psi(2^{j})|\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\

&\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{F^{s+1,\psi}_{p,q}}.\end{split}

Second estimate for

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4}\mathcal{M}(|S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\omega\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|)

Substituting this in, we have

(76)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\lVert 2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|III|\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\left\lVert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}\geq-1\\
|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 4\end{subarray}}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})2^{j^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u^{k}|)\right\rVert_{l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\

&\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{F^{s+1,\psi}_{p,q}}.\end{split}

In the second to last inequality we used Young’s convolution inequality, and in the last we used Lemma

We make use of the following lemma which establishes a bound on the remainder term in Besov spaces of generalized smoothness. The proof follows extremely closely to that of Theorem 2.52 in

Lemma A.5

Let

p_{1},p_{2},r_{1},r_{2}\in[1,\infty]
. We set

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\leq 1,\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}\leq 1,\text{ and }s=s_{1}+s_{2}>0,

then for any
(u,v)\in B^{s_{1},\psi}_{p_{1},r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times B^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

(78)

\left\lVert R(u,v)\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}\lesssim\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B^{s_{1},\psi}_{p_{1},r_{1}}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{B^{s_{2}}_{p_{2},r_{2}}}.

Proof.

We begin by writing

R(u,v)=\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u\Delta_{j}v=\sum_{j}R_{j},

where

R_{j}=\sum_{|\nu|\leq 1}\Delta_{j-\nu}u\Delta_{j}v.

Note that there exists some
R>0
such that
\supp\widehat{R_{j}}\subset 2^{j}B(0,R)
and
N_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

j^{\prime}\geq j+N_{0}\implies\Delta_{j^{\prime}}R_{j}=0.

We then write

\Delta_{j^{\prime}}R(u,v)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\nu|\leq 1\\
j\geq j^{\prime}-N_{0}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}(\Delta_{j-\nu}u\Delta_{j}v).

It follows that

(79)

\begin{split}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}R(u,v)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim 2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\nu|\leq 1\\
j\geq j^{\prime}-N_{0}\end{subarray}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j-\nu}u\Delta_{j}v\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&{\hskip-85.35826pt}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\nu|\leq 1\\
j\geq j^{\prime}-N_{0}\end{subarray}}2^{-(j-j^{\prime})s}2^{(j-\nu)s_{1}}\psi(2^{j-\nu})\left\lVert\Delta_{j-\nu}u\right\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}2^{js_{2}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}v\right\rVert_{L^{p_{2}}}.\end{split}

Taking the
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
norm and applying Young’s convolution inequality for series and Hölder’s inequality yields the desired result.
∎

Proposition A.6
.

Let
s\geq-1
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
. Let
u
be a divergence free vector field over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define
R_{j}=[u\cdot\nabla,\Delta_{j}]\omega
. There exists a constant
C
, depending continuously on
p,s,r,
and
d
, such that

\left\lVert\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\Delta_{j}R_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\rVert_{l^{r}}\leq C\left(\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}+\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{B^{s-1,\psi}_{p,r}}\right).

Note that this can be restrictive as it may be
\nabla\omega\notin L^{\infty}
. If this is the case we also have that

\left\lVert\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\Delta_{j}R_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\rVert_{l^{r}}\leq C\left(\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}+\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}\right).

Proof.

We follow the proof of Lemma 2.100 from
[
BCD11
]
. We begin by splitting
u
into low and high frequencies. We write
u=S_{0}u+\tilde{u}
. Using Einstein summation notation over repeated indices we write

R_{j}=[\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega+[S_{0}u^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega.

The term involving the low frequencies of
u
is easier to estimate while the other term presents a few challenges. Using paraproducts, we further decompose
[\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega
as

\displaystyle[\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega

\displaystyle=\tilde{u}^{k}\cdot\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega-\Delta_{j}(\tilde{u}^{k}\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=T_{\tilde{u}^{k}}(\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega)+T_{\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega}(\tilde{u}^{k})+R(\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega)-\Delta_{j}T_{\tilde{u}^{k}}(\partial_{k}\omega)-\Delta_{j}T_{\partial_{k}\omega}(\tilde{u}^{k})-\Delta_{j}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=[T_{\tilde{u}^{k}},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega T_{\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega}(\tilde{u}^{k})-\Delta_{j}T_{\partial_{k}\omega}(\tilde{u}^{k})++R(\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega)-\Delta_{j}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\partial_{k}\omega).

Furthermore, note that

\displaystyle R(\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|l-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}\left(\Delta_{l}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\Delta_{j}\omega\right)-\sum_{|l-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Delta_{l}\partial_{k}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\Delta_{j}\omega

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}\omega)-R(\text{div }\tilde{u},\Delta_{j}\omega),

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\partial_{k}\omega)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}\sum_{|l-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}\Delta_{l}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}\sum_{|l-k|\leq 1}\Delta_{l}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega-\Delta_{j}\sum_{|l-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}\Delta_{l}\partial_{k}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}\partial_{k}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\omega)-\Delta_{j}R(\text{div }\tilde{u},\omega).

Thus, we decompose

R_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{8}R_{j}^{i}

where

\displaystyle R_{j}^{1}=[T_{\tilde{u}^{k}},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega,\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle R_{j}^{2}=T_{\partial_{k}\Delta_{j}\omega}\tilde{u}^{k},

\displaystyle R_{j}^{3}=-\Delta_{j}T_{\partial_{k}\omega}\tilde{u}^{k},\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle R_{j}^{4}=\partial_{k}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}\omega),

\displaystyle R_{j}^{5}=-R(\text{div }\tilde{u},\Delta_{j}\omega),\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle R_{j}^{6}=-\partial_{k}\Delta_{j}R(\tilde{u}^{k},\omega),

\displaystyle R_{j}^{7}=\Delta_{j}R(\text{div }\tilde{u},\omega),\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle R_{j}^{8}=[S_{0}u^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega.

In what follows we let
c_{j}
denote a sequence such that
\left\lVert(c_{j})\right\rVert_{l^{r}}\leq 1
.

Estimate of
R_{j}^{1}
:
Due to the support of the Littlewood-Paley operators we write

R_{j}^{1}=\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}[S_{j^{\prime}-1}\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega.

Thus, applying Bernstein’s Lemma and Lemma
A.4
we have that

(80)

\begin{split}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{1}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{j^{\prime}(s-1)}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\partial_{k}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B_{p,r}^{s,\psi}}.\end{split}

Estimate for
R_{j}^{2}
:
By the support of the Littlewood-Paley operators we write

R_{j}^{2}=\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\Delta_{j}\omega\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and Bernstein’s Lemma yields

(81)

\begin{split}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\leq\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\Delta_{j}\omega\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{j^{\prime}(s-1)}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\Delta_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}2^{j^{\prime}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-3}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}.\end{split}

Estimate for
R_{j}^{3}
We write

\displaystyle R_{j}^{3}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{j}T_{\partial_{k}\omega}\tilde{u}^{k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}\Delta_{j}(S_{j^{\prime}-1}\partial_{k}\omega\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}).

Thus we have that

(82)

\begin{split}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{3}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\nabla S_{j^{\prime}-1}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{j^{\prime}(s-1)}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{B^{s-1,\psi}_{p,r}}.\end{split}

Note we can replace this estimate by

(83)

\begin{split}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{3}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\nabla S_{j^{\prime}-1}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{j(s+1)}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert S_{j^{\prime}-1}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}.\end{split}

Bound for
R_{j}^{4}
:
We write

R_{j}^{4}=\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 2}\partial_{k}(\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}\omega).

Using the product rule and Bernstein’s Lemma gives

(84)

\begin{split}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{4}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 2}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\partial_{k}\left(\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}\Delta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}\omega\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 2}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left(\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}\partial_{k}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 2}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j})\left(\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+2^{j^{\prime}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\tilde{u}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}.\end{split}

Estimate for
R_{j}^{5}
:
We write

R_{j}^{5}=\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 2}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\text{div }\tilde{u}\Delta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{j}\omega.

Thus we have that

(85)

\begin{split}2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{5}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 2}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\tilde{\Delta}_{j}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}.\end{split}

Estimate for
R_{j}^{6}
:
Recall that
R_{j}^{6}=-\partial_{k}\Delta_{j}R(\tilde{u},\omega)
. By Lemma
A.5
and Bernstein’s lemma

(86)

\left\lVert\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\Delta_{j}R_{j}^{6}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\rVert_{l^{r}}\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{0}}\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},

where in the last inequality we used the embedding
L^{\infty}\hookrightarrow B_{\infty,\infty}^{0}
.

Estimate for
R_{j}^{7}
:
Arguing as just before we have that

(87)

\left\lVert\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert\Delta_{j}R_{j}^{7}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\rVert_{l^{r}}\lesssim\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B^{s,\psi}_{p,r}}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Estimate for
R_{j}^{8}
:
Recall that
R_{j}^{8}=[\Delta_{-1}\tilde{u}^{k},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{k}\omega
. Since

R_{j}^{8}=\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}[\Delta_{j},\Delta_{-1}u]\cdot\nabla\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega,

applying Lemma
A.4
yields

(88)

\begin{split}2^{j}\psi(2^{j})\left\lVert R_{j}^{8}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}&\lesssim\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}2^{-j^{\prime}}2^{j^{\prime}s}\psi(2^{j^{\prime}})\left\lVert\nabla\Delta_{-1}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\omega\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\\
&\lesssim c_{j}\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert_{B_{p,r}^{s,\psi}}.\end{split}

Thus, combining (
80
)-(
88
) and using that
\left\lVert c_{j}\right\rVert_{l^{r}}\leq 1
yields the desired result.
∎

A.3.
Multiplier Theorems

When estimating the Biot-Savart law in Triebel-Lizorkin spaces of generalized smoothness the following proposition will be useful.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
s,a\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p,q<\infty
, and let
N=\left[\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{\min\{p,q\}}\right]+1
. Assume that
\sigma
is a
C^{N}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
that satisfies

(89)

|\partial^{\gamma}\sigma(\xi)|\leq C_{\gamma}|\xi|^{-|\gamma|-a}

for all
|\gamma|\leq N
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\sigma\hat{f})\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s,\psi}_{p,q}}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s-a,\psi}_{p,q}}.

Before proving Proposition
A.7
we need the following lemma.

Lemma A.8
.

[
Gra14
]

Let
0<c_{0}<\infty
and
0<r<\infty
. Then there exists constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
(which depends on
d,c_{0}
and
r
) such that for all
t>0
and all
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose Fourier transform is supported in the ball
|\xi|\leq c_{0}t
and that satisfy
|u(z)|\leq B(1+|z|)^{\frac{d}{r}}
for some
B>0
we have the estimate

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{t}\frac{|\nabla u(x-z)|}{(1+t|z|)^{\frac{d}{r}}}\leq C_{1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x-z)|}{(1+t|z|)^{\frac{d}{r}}}\leq C_{2}\mathcal{M}(|u|^{r})(x)^{\frac{1}{r}},

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.

Proof of Proposition
A.7
.

We begin by writing
\sigma_{j}(\xi)=\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}\sigma(\xi)
. Then observe that

\Delta_{j}(\sigma(D)f)=\Delta_{j}(\sigma_{j}(D)f)=\sigma_{j}(D)\Delta_{j}f.

Let
r=\min\{p,q\}
and
N=\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{r}+\delta
for some
\delta>0
. We then estimate

\displaystyle|\sigma_{j}(D)\Delta_{j}f(x)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widecheck{\sigma_{j}}(y)\Delta_{j}f(x-y)\frac{(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\frac{d}{r}}}{(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\frac{d}{r}}}dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(\Delta_{j}f)(x-y)|}{(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\frac{d}{r}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widecheck{\sigma_{j}}(y)|(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\frac{d}{r}}\left(\frac{1+2^{j}|y|}{1+2^{j}|y|}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}+\delta}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widecheck{\sigma_{j}}(y)|^{2}(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\frac{2d}{r}+d+2\delta}dy\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+2^{j}|y|)^{-d-2\delta}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where in the final inequality we used Lemma
A.8
and Cauchy Schwarz inequality. Furthermore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+2^{j}|y|)^{-d-2\delta}dy\lesssim 2^{-jd}.

Thus,

\displaystyle|\sigma_{j}(D)\Delta_{j}f(x)|\lesssim\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{-jd}|\widecheck{\sigma_{j}}(y)|^{2}(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\frac{2d}{r}+d+2\delta}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widecheck{\sigma_{j}(2^{j}(\cdot))}(y)|^{2}(1+|y|)^{2N}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}y^{|\gamma|}\widecheck{\sigma_{j}(2^{j}(\cdot))}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\sum_{|\gamma|\leq N}\partial^{\gamma}\sigma_{j}(2^{j}\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-ja}\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

where in the last inequality we used that
\varphi
is Schwartz function, and that
\sigma_{j}
is compactly supported. Summarizing, we have shown that

(90)

2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|\Delta_{j}(\sigma(D)f)|\lesssim 2^{j(s-a)}\psi(2^{j})\left(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

Taking the
l^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
norm in
j
of (
90
) and then applying Lemma
2.5
yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{js}\psi(2^{j})|\Delta_{j}(\sigma(D)f)|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\left(\mathcal{M}(2^{j(s-a)}\psi(2^{j})|\Delta_{j}f|)^{r}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert\mathcal{M}(|2^{j(s-a)}\psi(2^{j})\Delta_{j}f|^{r})\right\rVert_{l^{\frac{q}{r}}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{p}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\left\lVert(|2^{j(s-a)}\psi(2^{j})\Delta_{j}f|^{r})\right\rVert_{l^{\frac{q}{r}}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{p}{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{F}^{s-a,\psi}_{p,q}},

where in the last inequality we used Minkowski’s integral inequality.
∎

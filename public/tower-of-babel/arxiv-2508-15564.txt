Maz’ya-type bounds for sharp constants in fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities
Francesco Bozzola, Matteo Talluri

Abstract
. We prove estimates for the sharp constants in fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities associated to an open set, in terms of a nonlocal capacitary extension of its inradius. This work builds upon previous results obtained in the local case by Maz’ya and Shubin and by the first author and Brasco. We rely on a new Maz’ya-Poincaré inequality and, incidentally, we also prove new fractional Poincaré-Wirtinger-type estimates. These inequalities display sharp limiting behaviours with respect to the fractional order of differentiability. As a byproduct, we obtain a new criterion for the embedding of the homogeneous Sobolev space 𝒟 0 s , p ​ ( Ω ) \mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega) in L q ​ ( Ω ) L^{q}(\Omega) , valid in the subcritical regime and for p ≤ q < p s ∗ p\leq q<p^{*}_{s} . Our results are new even for the first eigenvalue of the fractional Laplacian and contain an optimal characterization for the positivity of the fractional Cheeger’s constant.

1.1.
Overview and goal of the paper

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set, its
fractional principal frequencies
are given by

(1.1)

\lambda^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega):=\displaystyle\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy\,:\,\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=1\right\},\qquad s\in(0,1),

where
1\leq p<\infty
and
q\geq 1
satisfies

(1.2)

\begin{cases}q\leq p^{*}_{s},\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p\in(0,N)\cup(N,\infty),\\
\\
q<p^{*}_{s},\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p=N,\\
\end{cases}

Here we adopt the usual notation for the critical fractional Sobolev’s exponent

p^{*}_{s}:=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \dfrac{N\,p}{N-s\,p},\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p<N,\\
\\
\infty,\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p\geq N.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

For
s=1
, the previous notation corresponds to the classical critical Sobolev’s exponent, which will be denoted by the symbol
p^{*}
.
The quantity which is minimized in (
1.1
) corresponds to the
p-
power of the
Gagliardo-Slobodeckiĭ seminorm
, in the sequel denoted by the symbol
[\,\cdot\,]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. For
p=q
, we will use the distinguished notation

By definition, the variational quantity

coincides with the
sharp constant in the fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequality

(1.3)

c_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\,dx\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy,\quad\mbox{ for }u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),

associated to an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Noteworthy, we have the following equivalence

\lambda^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega),

and in this case
\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(see for instance
[

stands for the

1.2.
Main Theorems

The following theorems are the main achievements of the paper. They can be interpreted as nonlocal variants of
[
11
, Main Theorem & Theorem 6.1]
.

The lower bound on
\lambda_{p,q}^{s}
reads as follows

Theorem 1.4

(Lower bound)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
be such that
s\,p\leq N
and let
p\leq q<p^{*}_{s}
.
Let
0<\gamma<1
and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. There exists a constant
\sigma=\sigma\left(N,p,s,q\right)>0
such that

(1.14)

\gamma\,\sigma\,\left(\frac{1}{R^{s}_{p,\gamma}(\Omega)}\right)^{s\,p-N+N\,\frac{p}{q}}\leq\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(\Omega).

Moreover, for
1\leq p<\infty
we have

\sigma(N,p,s,q)\sim\frac{1}{s}\quad\mbox{ as }s\searrow 0,

and for
1\leq p\leq N
we have

\sigma(N,p,s,q)\sim\frac{1}{1-s}\quad\mbox{ as }s\nearrow 1.

As for the upper bound on
\lambda_{p,q}^{s}
, we have the following

Theorem 1.5

(Upper bound)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
be such that
s\,p\leq N
and let
p\leq q<p^{*}_{s}
. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. There exists
0<\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(N,p,s)\leq 1
such that for every
0<\gamma<\gamma_{0}
, we have

(1.15)

\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{C}\,\left(\frac{1}{R^{s}_{p,\gamma}(\Omega)}\right)^{s\,p-N+N\,\frac{p}{q}},

for some constant
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(N,p,s,q,\gamma)>0
; if
p=1
we can take
\gamma_{0}=1.
In particular, if
R^{s}_{p,\gamma}(\Omega)=+\infty
then
\lambda^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)=0
. Moreover, if
1\leq p<\infty
we have

\mathcal{C}\left(N,p,s,q,\gamma\right)\sim\frac{1}{s}\quad\mbox{ as }s\searrow 0,

for
0<\gamma<\liminf_{s\to 0}\gamma_{0}\left(N,p,s\right)
, and if
1\leq p\leq N
we have

\mathcal{C}\left(N,p,s,q,\gamma\right)\sim\frac{1}{1-s}\quad\mbox{ as }s\nearrow 1,

for
0<\gamma<\liminf_{s\to 1}\gamma_{0}\left(N,p,s\right)
.

For a discussion on the negligibility threshold
\gamma_{0}
we refer to Remark
5.1
.

Remark 1.6
.

We stress that the conclusions of the Main Theorems are a novelty even for the bottom of the spectrum of the fractional Laplacian with nonlocal Dirichlet boundary conditions, corresponding to the case
p=q=2
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1

Corollary 1.8

Let

\sigma\,\gamma\,\left(\frac{1}{R^{s}_{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\right)^{s}\leq h_{s}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{C}\,\left(\frac{1}{R^{s}_{1,\gamma}(\Omega)}\right)^{s},

In particular, we have

and the last condition does not depend on

1.3.

The guidelines we followed for the proofs of our Main Theorems are inspired by the proofs of

Nevertheless, technical difficulties arose in adapting the local technique to the nonlocal framework. Comments on the proof of Theorem

.

•

The first step is the choice of a

of the whole

made of balls having equidistant centers, the same radius

and an explicit control on the multiplicity of the covering. The choice of the radius

, Definition

. More precisely, by taking

(1.16)

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega;B_{2\,r}(x_{0})\right)\geq\gamma\,r^{N-s\,p}\,\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{1}};B_{2}\right),\quad\mbox{ for every }x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

•

the family of balls introduced in the previous step. Then by using

Remark 1.10

(Limiting cases)
.

Let

Comments on the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

•

As in the proof of
[
11
, Main Theorem]
, the first step is an
approximation argument
for the capacitary potential of the set
\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega
by means of smooth functions
(\varphi_{\delta})_{\delta>0}
, where

is any
(s,p,\gamma)-
negligible ball in the sense of Definition
1.2
. By using a suitable

, we then modify each approximating functions into a feasible competitor for

. In order to verify the last condition, we use an expedient technical result, Lemma
2.4
, which is of independent interest.

•

The second step is to obtain explicit constants (and possibly the sharp ones) in a

Poincaré inequality on balls
. In Lemma
3.2
, by means of a geometric argument, we are able to compute the explicit form of the sharp constant for

. The case

is more delicate: as in
[
11
]
, we are only able to estimate the sharp constant of the corresponding

Poincaré inequality. Nevertheless, we pay due attention to the constants appearing in the bound. This would be important to assure that the Poincaré inequality has the correct asymptotic behaviour as
s\searrow 0
and
s\nearrow 1
, see Lemma
3.4
. This difference between the cases
p=1
and
p\neq 1
entails an artificial restriction on the negligibility parameter
\gamma
, for
p\neq 1
. On the other hand, the result obtained for

is optimal since we get the full range of the admissible values of the parameter
\gamma
, as in
[
11
]
.

.

In light of the previous discussion, we may expect that the negligibility threshold

of Theorem

is not optimal for

. Prove or disprove that the conclusion of Theorem

holds by taking

.

1.4.
Plan of the paper

In Section

, we settle the notation, the functional analytic framework and some basic results needed throughout the whole manuscript. In particular, we prove an expedient lemma for fractional Sobolev function, Lemma

, and some fundamentals on the notion of relative

2.1.
Notation and functional spaces

Unless otherwise specified, throughout the whole paper we will assume the dimension of our ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be
N\geq 1
. As usual, given a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a real number
R>0
the symbol
B_{R}(x_{0})
stands for the
N-
dimensional open ball, centered at
x_{0}
and having radius
R>0
, i.e.

B_{R}(x_{0})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x-x_{0}|<R\right\}.

Whenever an open ball with radius
R
is centered at the origin, we will use the shortcut notation
B_{R}
. The volume of the
N-
dimensional ball with radius
1
will be denoted by
\omega_{N}
. The
N-
dimensional open hypercube centered at
x_{0}
and having radius
R
is given by

Q_{R}(x_{0})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(x_{0}^{i}-R,x_{0}^{i}+R),\qquad\mbox{ where }x_{0}=(x_{0}^{1},\ldots,x_{0}^{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The
N-
dimensional Lebesgue measure is denoted by
|\cdot|
and often it will be addressed just as volume or measure. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}
,
L^{p}_{\rm loc}
and the norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
are defined as usual in the literature. The conjugate exponent of
p
will be denoted by
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
. If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a measurable set having finite and positive volume we set

{\rm av}(u;E):=\fint_{E}u\,dx=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}u\,dx,\qquad\mbox{ for }u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for the
L^{1}-
mean of
u
over
E
. Let
E,\Omega
be open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the symbol
E\Subset\Omega
means that the closure
\overline{E}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
.

If
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a non-empty open set, the notation
C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
stands for the space of infinitely differentiable functions whose support is a compact subset of
E
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we will denote by
W^{1,p}(E)

the standard Sobolev space

W^{1,p}(E)=\Big\{u\in L^{p}(E)\,:\,\nabla u\in L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{N})\Big\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(E)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(E)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(E)},\qquad\mbox{ for }u\in W^{1,p}(E),

where we used the notation

\|\Phi\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\left(\int_{E}|\Phi(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\mbox{ for }\Phi\in L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
homogeneous Sobolev space
denoted by the symbol
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(E)
is defined as the completion (in the sense of metric spaces) of
C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
with respect to the norm

\varphi\mapsto\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}(E)}.

The space
W^{1,p}_{0}(E)
is given by the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
in
W^{1,p}(E)
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(E)}
.

We now introduce basic definitions from the theory of fractional Sobolev spaces. The reader is referred to
[
36
,
37
,
44
,
46
,
53
,
55
]
and to the references therein included for a systematic treatment of these spaces. Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
and let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be as before. The Gagliardo-Slobodeckiĭ seminorm is defined as

[u]_{W^{s,p}(E)}:=\left(\iint_{E\times E}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\mbox{ for }u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(E).

The space of functions given by

W^{s,p}(E)=\left\{u\in L^{p}(E):[u]_{W^{s,p}(E)}<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(E)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(E)}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(E)}

is called
fractional Sobolev space
. The symbol
\widetilde{W}_{0}^{s,p}(E)
stands for the
closure
of
C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to its norm. We recall that the
homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces

\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
are Banach spaces defined as the completion of

\left(C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),\,\,[\,\cdot\,]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right),

equipped with the natural norm produced by this procedure, that is

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}:=[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

We refer to
[
21
]
for a comprehensive treatment of
\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}
, the homogeneous Sobolev spaces of functions satisfying nonlocal Dirichlet conditions. See also
[
31
]
and
[
46
, Chapter 6]
for further information, references and extensions.

Remark 2.1
.

For
1<p<\infty
, since the spaces
\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
are uniformly convex

4

4

\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
inherits the uniform convexity property from the uniform convexity of

Banach spaces, they are also reflexive, in light of Milman-Pettis Theorem (for instance see

Remark 2.2

In general, we only have that

and the inclusion is possibly strict, see
[

. Nevertheless, if

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E)=\mathcal{D}^{s,p}_{0}(E)

\widetilde{W}_{0}^{s,p}(E)\subseteq\left\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\,\,\mbox{ a.e. on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E\right\}=:W^{s,p}_{0}(E),

[

, Remark 7]
. However, if we assume that

has

in

as proved in
[

[

For

, we say that a function

is

[u]_{C^{0,s}(E)}:=\sup_{x,y\in E,\,x\neq y}\,\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}<\infty,

in particular for

. We set

2.2.
Two expedient lemmas

The following interpolation estimate was established in
[
7
, Lemma 2.6]
for compactly supported
C^{1}-
regular functions. With the same proof therein given, it is possible to state a more general result which will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s\leq 1
. For every
\varphi\in C^{0,s}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dy\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\|\varphi\|^{(1-s)\,p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,[\varphi]^{s\,p}_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where we can take
c_{N,p}=2^{p}\,\frac{N\,\omega_{N}}{p}.

The next lemma may be regarded as a nonlocal analog of
[
32
, Theorem 9.17]
. It reads as follows

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open and bounded set. For every
\varphi\in C^{0,s}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\varphi=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E
, we have
\varphi\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
0\in E
. We need to exhibit a sequence of functions
(\varphi_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
such that

{\rm(i)}\,\,\lim_{h\to\infty}\|\varphi_{h}-\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0;\qquad{\rm(ii)}\,\,\lim_{h\to\infty}[\varphi_{h}-\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0.

We set

E_{h}:=\left\{x\in E:{\rm dist}\left(x;\partial E\right)>\frac{1}{h}\right\},\quad\mbox{ for }h\in\mathbb{N},

this is an open set. For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, we take
\eta_{h}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E_{4h})
satisfying

(2.1)

\eta_{h}\equiv 1\quad\mbox{ on }E_{h},\qquad 0\leq\eta_{h}\leq 1,\qquad|\nabla\eta_{h}|\leq A\,h,

and set

(2.2)

\varphi_{h}:=\eta_{h}\,\varphi\in C^{0,s}_{0}(E_{4h}).

Point (i) is straightforward. We focus on point (ii). We split the seminorm on the set
E\setminus E_{h}

\begin{split}[\varphi_{h}-\varphi]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}&=\iint_{(E\setminus E_{h})\times(E\setminus E_{h})}\frac{|(\eta_{h}(x)-1)\,\varphi(x)-(\eta_{h}(y)-1)\,\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy\\
&\qquad+2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(E\setminus E_{h})}\left(\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}\frac{|(\eta_{h}(y)-1)\,\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dy\right)dx\\
&=:\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}.\end{split}

For the first term, by adding and subtracting
\varphi(x)\,(1-\eta_{h}(y))
and by using Minkowski’s inequality, we get

\begin{split}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}}{2^{p-1}}&\leq\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}|\varphi(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}\frac{|\eta_{h}(x)-\eta_{h}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dy\right)dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}|1-\eta_{h}(y)|^{p}\left(\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dx\right)dy\\
&=:\mathcal{B}_{1}+\mathcal{B}_{2}.\end{split}

By using (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{2}\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\,[\varphi]^{s\,p}_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\|\varphi\|^{(1-s)\,p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,|E\setminus E_{h}|.\end{split}

In order to estimate
\mathcal{B}_{1}
, we need to use that
\varphi
is
s-
Hölder and vanishes on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E
. For every
x\in E\setminus E_{h}
, we have

(2.3)

\begin{split}|\varphi(x)|^{p}=|\varphi(x)-\varphi(z)|^{p}\leq[\varphi]^{p}_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,|x-z|^{s\,p}\leq[\varphi]^{p}_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)^{s\,p},\end{split}

for some
z\in\partial E
.
This yields

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{1}&\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\,[\varphi]^{p}_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,|E\setminus E_{h}|\,(A\,h)^{s\,p}\,\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)^{s\,p}.\end{split}

where we used (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.3
. By collecting the previous estimates and by sending
h\to\infty
, we get that

(2.4)

\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{1}=0.

We now turn to
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{2}}{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E}\left(\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}\frac{|(\eta_{h}(y)-1)\,\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dy\right)dx+\int_{E_{h}}\left(\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}\frac{|(\eta_{h}(y)-\eta_{h}(x))\,\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dy\right)dx\\
&\leq 2\,\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dx\right)dy+\int_{E\setminus E_{h}}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,\left(\int_{E_{h}}\frac{|\eta_{h}(x)-\eta_{h}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dx\right)dy\\
&:=\mathcal{C}_{1}+\mathcal{C}_{2}.\end{split}

Since
\varphi
vanishes on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E
, in light of
[
46
, Theorem 6.97]
, we have

\int_{E}|\varphi(y)|^{p}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dx\right)dy<\infty.

This allows to use the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem to infer that

(2.5)

\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{1}=0.

For
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, by using Lemma
2.3
and (
2.3
) we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\,[\varphi]^{p}_{C^{0,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\left(A\,h\right)^{s\,p}\,\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)^{s\,p}\,|E\setminus E_{h}|.\end{split}

This entails that

\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{2}=0,

which in combination with (
2.5
) yields

\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathcal{A}_{2}=0.

By spending the last information and (
2.4
), we can infer that point (ii) holds for the sequence of functions given by (
2.2
). Eventually, by standard approximation arguments, we can modify each
\varphi_{h}
so that for the sequence of modified functions (not relabeled) we have
(\varphi_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
and (i)-(ii) still hold.
∎

2.3.
Basic properties of fractional capacity

As anticipated in the Introduction, we will work with the following notion of fractional capacity.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. For every
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
open set and for every
\Sigma\subseteq E
compact set, we define the
(homogeneous)
(s,p)-
capacity of
\Sigma
relative to
E
as

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)=\inf_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E)}\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy:\varphi\geq 1\mbox{ on }\Sigma\right\}.

In the sequel, we will often refer to
\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}
just as
fractional capacity
.

Directly from the definition, we record the following scaling property.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. By using the change of variable formula for multiple integrals in the Definition
2.5
, we can infer that

\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{r}};B_{R}\right)=r^{N-s\,p}\,\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{1}};B_{R/r}\right),\quad\mbox{for every }0<r<R.

Remark 2.7
.

In the Definition
2.5
, it is not restrictive to assume that every feasible competitor
\varphi
is nonnegative. Indeed, if
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
is such that
\varphi\geq 1
on
\Sigma
, by the triangle inequality we have

\left[|\varphi|\right]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

By taking
\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}:=f_{\varepsilon}\circ\varphi
, where

f_{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{\left(t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\varepsilon}{\left(1+\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\varepsilon},\qquad\mbox{ for }t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,\varepsilon>0,

we can verify that
\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}
is an admissible nonnegative competitor in the Definition
2.5
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}[\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=[|\varphi|]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

by the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem.

A useful equivalent characterization of fractional capacity is given below. For the proof, we share ideas from
[
4
, Remark 1.4.2]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. For every
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
open set and for every
\Sigma\subseteq E
compact set, we have

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)=\inf_{\varphi\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(E)}\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy:0\leq\varphi\leq 1,\,\,\varphi=1\mbox{ {\rm on }}\Sigma\right\}.

Proof.

We first claim that

(2.6)

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)=\inf_{\varphi\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(E)}\left\{[\varphi]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:\varphi\geq 0,\,\,\varphi\geq 1\mbox{ on }\Sigma\right\}.

By Remark
2.7
, the inequality

(2.7)

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)\geq\inf_{\varphi\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(E)}\Big\{[\varphi]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:\varphi\geq 0,\,\,\varphi\geq 1\mbox{ on }\Sigma\Big\}.

trivially follows by the inclusion of sets
C^{\infty}_{0}(E)\subseteq{\rm Lip}_{0}(E)
. On the other hand, let
\varphi\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(E)
be a nonnegative function such that
\varphi\geq 1
on
\Sigma
. If
(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a family of standard mollifiers, we consider the convolution
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\varphi\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}
and set

\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}:=\varphi_{\varepsilon}/\min_{\Sigma}\varphi_{\varepsilon},

for
\varepsilon>0.

The function
\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}
belongs to
C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
, as long as
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, and by construction
\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}\geq 1
on
\Sigma.

Since
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
converges uniformly to
\varphi

and by
[
39
, Lemma 11]
, we get

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}[\widetilde{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\dfrac{[\varphi]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\displaystyle\min_{\Sigma}\varphi}\leq[\varphi]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Thus, by the arbitrariness of
\varphi
, we get the reverse inequality of (
2.7
) and so also (
2.6
). To conclude the proof, we observe that by truncating a Sobolev function we can lower the seminorm
[\,\cdot\,]_{W^{s,p}}
, see
[
57
, Lemma 2.7]
for instance. Thus, we can restrict the class of admissible competitors function
\varphi
on the right-hand side to the ones that are
\varphi\leq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
∎

When
p=1
, we have a further characterization for the
(s,1)-
capacity in terms of the
s-
perimeter. The following result can be seen as a nonlocal analog of
[
50
, Lemma 2.2.5]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
0<s<1
and
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. For every
\Sigma\subseteq E
compact set, we have

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,1}\left(\Sigma;E\right)=\inf\left\{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})\,:\,\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{O}\Subset E\mbox{ open set with }\\
\mbox{ smooth boundary such that }\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{O}\end{array}\right\}.

In particular, if
\Sigma
is the closure of a bounded open convex set then

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,1}\left(\Sigma;E\right)=P_{s}(\Sigma).

Proof.

For simplicity, we say that an open set
\mathcal{O}\Subset E
is
admissible
if it has smooth boundary and
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{O}.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be admissible, we claim that

(2.8)

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,1}\left(\Sigma;E\right)\geq\inf\left\{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})\,:\,\mathcal{O}\mbox{ is admissible }\right\}.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
be a nonnegative function such that
\varphi\geq 1
on
\Sigma
. By using the nonlocal Coarea-type Formula for
[\,\cdot\,]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
established in
[
56
]
(see also
[
2
, Lemma 10]
), we get

\begin{split}[\varphi]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{s}\left(\left\{\varphi>t\right\}\right)\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{1}P_{s}\left(\left\{\varphi>t\right\}\right)\,dt\geq\inf\left\{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})\,:\,\mathcal{O}\mbox{ is admissible }\right\},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows since for a.e.
t\in(0,1)
the open set
\left\{\varphi>t\right\}
has smooth boundary and so it is admissible, in light of Sard’s Lemma. By the arbitrariness of
\varphi
, we get our claim (
2.8
). To prove the reverse inequality, take any
\mathcal{O}
admissible set. We set

\delta:={\rm dist}\left(\Sigma;\partial\mathcal{O}\right)/2>0,

and define
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}:=\left\{x\in\mathcal{O}\,:\,{\rm dist}\left(x;\partial\mathcal{O}\right)>\delta\right\}.

By construction
\Sigma\Subset\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. Let
(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of standard mollifiers and set
\varphi_{\varepsilon}:={1}_{\mathcal{O}}\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}
.
For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we have

(2.9)

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E),\qquad 0\leq\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1\quad\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\varphi_{\varepsilon}=1\quad\mbox{ on }\mathcal{O}^{\prime}.

In particular, the last property follows since if
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
then
|B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus\mathcal{O}|=0
for
\varepsilon
small enough, thus we have
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=1
, by standard properties of the mollifiers. Then, by Fubini’s theorem

\begin{split}\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,1}\left(\Sigma;E\right)&\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|1_{\mathcal{O}}(x-z)-1_{\mathcal{O}}(y-z)|}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz\right)dxdy\\
&\leq\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|1_{\mathcal{O}}(x)-1_{\mathcal{O}}(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,dxdy=P_{s}(\mathcal{O}),\end{split}

which entails the reverse inequality of (
2.8
), by the arbitrariness of
\mathcal{O}
.

To prove the last sentence of the statement, let
\Sigma\subseteq E
be the closure of a bounded open convex set and assume that
0\in{\rm int}(\Sigma)
. By
[
38
, Lemma B.2]
, for every
\mathcal{O}
admissible we have

(2.10)

P_{s}(\Sigma)=P_{s}(\Sigma\cap\mathcal{O})\leq P_{s}(\mathcal{O}).

On the other hand, for
\lambda>0
we set

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:=(1+\lambda)\,{\rm int}(\Sigma)=\Big\{(1+\lambda)\,x\,:\,x\in{\rm int}(\Sigma)\Big\}.

By convexity
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\Subset E
, for
\lambda
small enough, thus
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is admissible. Moreover, by the scaling properties of the
s-
perimeter

P_{s}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})=\left(1+\lambda\right)^{N-s}\,P_{s}(\Sigma),

which converges to
P_{s}(\Sigma)
, as
\lambda\to 0
. By recalling (
2.10
), this entails that

P_{s}(\Sigma)=\inf\left\{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})\,:\,\mathcal{O}\mbox{ is admissible }\right\},

and so the desired conclusion follows by the first part of the proof.
∎

For
p=2
, the following proposition is already contained in
[
1
, Proposition 2.6 & Corollary 2.7]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and take an exponent
q
satisfying (
1.2
). Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open and bounded set, and let
F\subseteq E
be compact set such that
F\Subset B_{R}(x_{0})
and

(2.11)

\widetilde{{\rm cap}}_{s,p}\left(F;B_{R}(x_{0})\right)=0,

for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
R>0
. Then we have

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E\setminus F)=\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E).

In particular

\lambda^{s}_{p,q}(E)=\lambda^{s}_{p,q}(E\setminus F).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
x_{0}=0
. Since
C^{\infty}_{0}(E\setminus F)\subseteq C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
, we have

(2.12)

\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(E)\leq\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(E\setminus F),

by definition. Observe that
\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(E\setminus F)>0
, as a consequence of
[
23
, Lemma 2.4]
and Hölder’s inequality. Then, in order to prove the reverse inequality of (
2.12
), given any
u\in\widetilde{W}_{0}^{s,p}(E)
we need to find a sequence of functions in
\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E\setminus F)
converging to
u
with respect
[\,\cdot\,]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. Let
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
be such that

(2.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0.

By Proposition
2.8
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\rm Lip}_{0}(B_{R})
satisfying

(2.14)

0\leq\varphi_{m}\leq 1\,\,\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad\varphi_{m}=1\,\,\mbox{ on }F\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\lim_{m\to\infty}[\varphi_{m}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0,

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We set

v^{(n)}_{m}:=\Big((1-\varphi_{m})\,u_{n}\Big)_{+},\qquad\mbox{ for every }m,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
F\Subset E
, we have
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\left(E\setminus F\right)=F\cup\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E\right)
. By construction,
v^{(n)}_{m}\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v^{(n)}_{m}=0
on
F\cup\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E\right)
, thus by Lemma
2.4
we get that
v^{(n)}_{m}\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E\setminus F).

By Minkowski’s inequality and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\begin{split}&[v^{(n)}_{m}-u_{n}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq[\varphi_{m}\,u_{n}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq[\varphi_{m}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi_{m}(y)|^{p}\,\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq[\varphi_{m}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left(\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{s}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\|u_{n}\|^{1-s}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq[\varphi_{m}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\left(\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left(\frac{c_{N,p}}{\lambda_{p}^{s}(B_{R})\,s\,(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{s}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,\|u_{n}\|^{1-s}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right),\end{split}

where in the last line we also used Poincaré’s inequality on balls. From (
2.14
), we then obtain

\lim_{m\to\infty}[v^{(n)}_{m}-u_{n}]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0,\qquad\mbox{ for every }n\in\mathbb{N},

and so also

u_{n}\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E\setminus F),

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
By (
2.13
), we infer that
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(E\setminus F)
, as wanted. ∎

In the next simple proposition, we derive basic estimates between the fractional capacity and its local counterpart/Lebesgue measure.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. For every compact set
\Sigma\subseteq E
, we have

(2.15)

\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\lambda_{p}(E)^{s-1}\,\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\Sigma;E),

and

(2.16)

|\Sigma|\,\lambda_{p}^{s}(E)\leq\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E).

Proof.

We assume
\lambda_{p}(E)>0
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(E)
be such that
\varphi\geq 1
on
\Sigma.

We first prove (
2.15
). By using
[
27
, Corollary 2.2]
and
[
23
, Proposition 4.2]
, for the case
p>1
and for the case
p=1
respectively, we get

\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;E)\leq[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\|\varphi\|^{(1-s)\,p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\nabla\varphi\|^{s\,p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\lambda_{p}(E)^{s-1}\|\nabla\varphi\|^{p}_{L^{p}(E)}.

By the arbitrariness of
\varphi
, we obtain the conclusion.
For (
2.16
), we have

\lambda_{p}^{s}(E)\,|\Sigma|\leq\lambda_{p}^{s}(E)\,\|\varphi\|^{p}_{L^{p}(E)}\leq[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p},

and we conclude, as before.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
E\subseteq\Omega
be an open set. From the previous result and by Propositions
A.1
-
A.3
, we can infer that

(2.17)

\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;E\right)\sim\frac{1}{s},\,\,\mbox{ for }s\searrow 0,\qquad\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;E\right)\sim\frac{1}{1-s},\,\,\mbox{ for }s\nearrow 1,

for every

compact set.

A first comparison result between local and nonlocal variant of capacitary inradius is derived below.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. There exists a constant
\beta=\beta(N,p,s)>0
such that for every
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
open set we have

R_{p,\beta\cdot\gamma}(\Omega)\leq R^{s}_{p,\gamma}(\Omega),\qquad\mbox{ for every }0<\gamma<\min\left\{1,\,\,\frac{1}{\beta}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
r>R^{s}_{p,\gamma}(\Omega)
then, by using Proposition
2.11
and Remark
2.6
, for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{cap}_{p}\left(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega;B_{2r}(x_{0})\right)&\geq c_{N,p}\,s\,(1-s)\,\lambda_{p}\left(B_{2r}\right)^{1-s}\,\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega;B_{2r}(x_{0})\right)\\
&>c_{N,p}\,s\,(1-s)\,\lambda_{p}(B_{2})^{1-s}\,\gamma\,\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})};B_{2r}(x_{0})\right)\\
&=\beta\,\gamma\,\mathrm{cap}_{p}\left(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})};B_{2r}(x_{0})\right),\end{split}

where we set

\beta=\beta(N,p,s)=\frac{c_{N,p}\,\lambda_{p}(B_{2})^{1-s}}{\mathrm{cap}_{p}(\overline{B_{1}};B_{2})}\,s\,(1-s)\,\widetilde{\operatorname{cap}}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{1}};B_{2}).

This entails that
r>R_{p,\beta\cdot\gamma}(\Omega),
for every
\gamma<\min\left\{1,\,\,1/\beta\right\},
so we get the desired inequality, by the arbitrariness of
r
.
∎

The following technical result will be useful in the sequel.

Proposition 2.14

(Capacity with respect to balls)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Let
\Sigma\subseteq B_{r}(x_{0})
be a compact set. For every
R>r
, there exists a constant
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}(N,p,s,R/d)>0
such that

(2.18)

\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}(x_{0})\right)\leq\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{r}(x_{0})\right)\leq\mathscr{C}\,\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}(x_{0})\right),

where
d:=\mathrm{dist}(\Sigma;\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))>0
. Moreover, the function
\tau\mapsto\mathscr{C}(N,p,s,\tau),
as
\tau>0
, is increasing.

Proof.

The leftmost inequality is trivial. Without loss of generality we can assume that
x_{0}=0
. Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R})
be such that
u\geq 1
on
\Sigma
. We fix
\delta=d/2
and define a cut-off function
\eta\in\mathrm{Lip}_{0}(B_{r})
given by

\eta(x):=\min\left\{\left(\dfrac{(r-\delta)-|x|}{(r-\delta)-(r-d)}\right)_{+},1\right\}.

In particular

(2.19)

\eta\equiv 1\mbox{ on }B_{r-d},\qquad\eta\equiv 0\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r-\delta},\qquad\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\dfrac{1}{d-\delta}.

By construction, the function
\psi:=\eta\,u
is admissible to test the definition of relative
p-
capacity. Then, by Minkowski’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\Big(\widetilde{\mathrm{cap}}_{s,p}(\Sigma;B_{r})\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\dfrac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad+2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\psi(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}.

We estimate the last two terms separately. For
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, we add and subtract
\eta(x)u(y)
and use Minkowski’s inequality obtaining

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}}|\eta(x)|^{p}\int_{B_{r}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{B_{r}}|u(y)|^{p}\int_{B_{r}}\dfrac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\left(\dfrac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|\nabla\eta\|^{s}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\,\|\eta\|^{1-s}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})},

where in the last line we also used (
2.19
) and Lemma
2.3
. In turn, by using the definitions of
\lambda_{p}^{s}(B_{R})
and
\delta
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}\leq\left(1+\left(\dfrac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{2\,R}{d}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{s}_{p}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

We focus now on
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. By recalling (
2.19
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{r-\delta}}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\right)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\dfrac{N\omega_{N}}{s\,p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,R^{\frac{N}{p}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{N+s\,p}{p}}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}.

Observe that, in the last inequality we used the following estimate

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\leq\left(\dfrac{r}{\delta}\right)^{N+s\,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\dfrac{dy}{|y|^{N+s\,p}}=\frac{N\,\omega_{N}}{s\,p}\,\left(\dfrac{r}{\delta}\right)^{N+s\,p}\,\dfrac{1}{r^{s\,p}},\end{split}

which holds since we have

|x-y|\geq|y|-|x|\geq|y|-(r-\delta)\geq|y|-\dfrac{r-\delta}{r}|y|=|y|\dfrac{\delta}{r},\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in B_{r-\delta},\,y\notin B_{r}.

Then, by using the
(s,p)-
Poincaré inequality on
B_{R}
and by recalling the definition of
\delta
, we get

\mathcal{A}_{2}\leq\left(\dfrac{N\omega_{N}}{s\,p}\frac{1}{\lambda^{s}_{p}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\left(\dfrac{2\,R}{d}\right)^{\frac{N+s\,p}{p}}\,[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

By collecting the previous estimates, we can infer the second inequality in (
2.18
) by taking

(2.20)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{C}\left(N,p,s,\frac{R}{d}\right)=\\
&\left(1+\left(\dfrac{c_{N,p}}{s(1-s)}\frac{1}{\lambda^{s}_{p}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{2R}{d}\right)^{s}+(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\dfrac{N\omega_{N}}{s(1-s)}\frac{1}{\lambda^{s}_{p}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\dfrac{2R}{d}\right)^{\frac{N+s\,p}{p}}\right)^{p},\end{split}

the proof is thereby complete.
∎

3.
Poincaré-type estimates on balls

In the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we will crucially exploit two
L^{1}-W^{s,p}
Poincaré-type estimates on balls, derived in Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
below. We provide such estimates with the correct asymptotic behaviours with respect to the parameter of fractional differentiability. We treat separately the cases
p=1
and
1<p<\infty
, starting from the first one.

3.1.
A Cheeger-type constant

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set, we introduce the following
Cheeger-type problem

h_{s}\left(E;\Omega\right):=\inf\left\{\dfrac{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})}{\mid\mathcal{O}\cap E|}:\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{O}\Subset\Omega\mbox{ open with }\\
\mbox{ smooth boundary }\end{array}\right\},

for every
E\subseteq\Omega
. Observe that, by taking
E=\Omega
, we have
h_{s}(\Omega;\Omega)=h_{s}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 3.1

(Poincaré vs Cheeger)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. For every
E\subseteq\Omega
, we have

\inf_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,dxdy:\int_{E}|\varphi|\,dx=1\right\}=h_{s}\left(E;\Omega\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}\Subset\Omega
be an open set with smooth boundary. We set
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=1_{\mathcal{O}}\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}/\|1_{\mathcal{O}}\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(E)},
where
(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is a family of standard mollifiers. Then, by usual properties of mollifiers

\inf_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}\Big\{[\varphi]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(E)}=1\Big\}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{[1_{\mathcal{O}}\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\|1_{\mathcal{O}}\ast\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(E)}}=\dfrac{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})}{|\mathcal{O}\cap E|}.

By the arbitrariness of
\mathcal{O}
, we get a first inequality. On the other hand, let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative function. We can easily observe that this is not restrictive. Then, by the Coarea Formula for
[\,\cdot\,]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
and Sard’s Lemma

\begin{split}[\varphi]_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{s}\left(\left\{\varphi>t\right\}\right)\,dt\geq h_{s}\left(E;\Omega\right)\,\int_{0}^{\infty}|\left\{\varphi>t\right\}\cap E|\,dt=h_{s}\left(E;\Omega\right)\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(E)},\end{split}

where the last equality follows by Cavalieri’s formula. This entails the reverse inequality as wanted.
∎

As a byproduct of the foregoing result, we infer the following
L^{1}-W^{s,1}
Poincaré-type inequality

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<r\leq R<\infty
, we have

\inf_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R})}\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,dxdy:\int_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx=1\right\}=\frac{P_{s}(B_{r})}{|B_{r}|},

that is,
E=B_{r}
is the unique optimal set for the Cheeger-type problem defining
h_{s}\left(B_{r};B_{R}\right)
. In particular, we have the following sharp estimate

(3.1)

\fint_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{P_{s}(B_{r})}\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,dxdy,\quad\mbox{ for }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}).

Proof.

In light of Proposition
3.1
, we only need to prove that
h_{s}\left(B_{r};B_{R}\right)=P_{s}(B_{r})/|B_{r}|.

Let
\mathcal{O}\Subset B_{R}
be an open set with smooth boundary. By the
strict Polya-Szëgo-type inequality

[
40
, Theorem A.1]
, we have

P_{s}(\mathcal{O}^{*})\leq P_{s}(\mathcal{O}),

where
\mathcal{O}^{*}
is the ball centered at the origin with
|\mathcal{O}^{*}|=|\mathcal{O}|
, and the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}^{*}
, up to translations. Let
\rho^{*}
be the radius of
\mathcal{O}^{*}
, if
\rho^{*}\leq r
we have
\mathcal{O}^{*}\subseteq B_{r}
and so

|\mathcal{O}^{*}\cap B_{r}|=|\mathcal{O}^{*}|=|\mathcal{O}|\geq|\mathcal{O}\cap B_{r}|.

On the other hand, if
\rho^{*}>r
we have that
B_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{*}
and so again

|\mathcal{O}^{*}\cap B_{r}|=|B_{r}|\geq|\mathcal{O}\cap B_{r}|.

In particular

\dfrac{P_{s}(\mathcal{O})}{|\mathcal{O}\cap B_{r}|}\geq\dfrac{P_{s}(\mathcal{O}^{*})}{|\mathcal{O}^{*}\cap B_{r}|},

with equality if and only if
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}^{*}
, up to translations.
Thus, we can restrict the class of competitors for
h_{s}\left(B_{r};B_{R}\right)
to the balls centered at
0
and with radius
0<\rho<R
. Let
B_{\rho}
one of these balls, by the scaling properties of
P_{s}
we have

\dfrac{P_{s}\left({B}_{\rho}\right)}{\left|B_{\rho}\cap B_{r}\right|}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\rho^{N-s}\,P_{s}\left(B_{1}\right)}{r^{N}\,\omega_{N}},\quad&\mbox{ if }r\leq\rho<R,\\
\\
\dfrac{\rho^{N-s}\,P_{s}\left(B_{1}\right)}{\rho^{N}\,\omega_{N}},\quad&\mbox{ if }0<\rho<r.\end{cases}

It is immediate to see that the infimum is attained when
\rho=r
, from which we can also infer the uniqueness of the optimal set, as claimed.
∎

3.2.
A Poincaré-type constant

If
1<p<\infty
, we take advantage of the forthcoming nonlocal torsion-like PDE.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
be such that
s\,p\leq N
. For every
0<r\leq R
, there exists a unique
V\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}_{0}(B_{R})
weak solution of the following equation

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}V&=1_{B_{r}},\qquad&&\mbox{ in }B_{R},\\
\\
V&=0,\qquad&&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

that is
V
satisfies

(3.2)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(x)-V(y)|^{p-2}(V(x)-V(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy=\int_{B_{r}}\varphi dx,\mbox{ for }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}).

Moreover,
V\geq 0
and we have the following estimates

•

if
s\,p<N
,

(3.3)

[V]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\left(|B_{r}|^{\frac{s\,p}{N}-1+p}\,\mathcal{S}_{N,p,s}\right)^{\frac{1}{p\,(p-1)}};

•

if
s\,p=N
,

(3.4)

[V]_{W^{s,\frac{N}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\left(\frac{|B_{R}|^{\frac{N}{s}}}{\omega_{N}^{\frac{N}{s}}\,\lambda^{s}_{\frac{N}{s},1}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\frac{N}{s}\,(\frac{N}{s}-1)}}.

Proof.

The energy functional associated to the equation is

\mathfrak{F}(u):=\frac{1}{p}\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy-\int_{B_{r}}u\,dx,\quad\mbox{ for }u\in\widetilde{{W}}^{s,p}_{0}(B_{R}),

and every function
u
satisfying (
3.2
) is equivalently characterized as a minimum of
\mathfrak{F}
.

Existence & Uniqueness
of a nonnegative function
V\in\widetilde{{W}}^{s,p}_{0}(B_{R})
attaining the infimum of
\mathfrak{F}
are direct consequences of the
Direct Method in the Calculus of Variations
and of the strict convexity of
\mathfrak{F}
, the details are left to the reader.

If
s\,p<N
, since
V
is solution we can use (
3.2
) with
\varphi=V
, by density, and by using also the fractional Sobolev’s inequality we get

\displaystyle{}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}=\int_{B_{r}}V\,dx\leq|B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}}\,\|V\|_{L^{p^{*}_{s}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}}\,\mathcal{S}_{N,p,s}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[V]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

from which we infer (
3.3
). If
s\,p=N
, since
|B_{R}|<\infty
we can infer that
\lambda_{\frac{N}{s},1}^{s}(B_{R})>0
, by
[
23
, Lemma 2.4]
and Hölder’s inquality. Thus, by reasoning as before but using the
\left(\frac{N}{s},1\right)-
Poincaré inequality in place of the fractional Sobolev’s inequality, we can infer the claimed estimate (
3.4
).
∎

As a byproduct, we infer the following
L^{1}-W^{s,p}
Poincaré-type estimate.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
be such that
s\,p\leq N
. For every
0<r\leq R
, we have

•

if
s\,p<N
,

(3.5)

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx\right)^{p}\leq\mathcal{S}_{N,p,s}\,|B_{r}|^{\frac{s\,p}{N}-1}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy,\quad\mbox{ for }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}),

where
\mathcal{S}_{N,p,s}
is the sharp constant in the fractional Sobolev’s inequality;

•

if
s\,p=N
,

(3.6)

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx\right)^{\frac{N}{s}}\leq\frac{1}{K_{N,s}}\left(\frac{|B_{R}|}{|B_{r}|}\right)^{\frac{N}{s}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{\frac{N}{s}}}{|x-y|^{2\,N}}\,dxdy,\quad\mbox{ for }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}),

where
K_{N,s}=\omega_{N}^{\frac{N}{s}}\,\lambda^{s}_{\frac{N}{s},1}(B_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
V
be as in Proposition
3.3
. We claim that

(3.7)

\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R})}\left\{\left(\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx\right)^{p}:[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=1\right\}=[V]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p\,(p-1)}.

Indeed, by density, we can use the function
\varphi=V
in (
3.2
) obtaining

(3.8)

\int_{B_{r}}V\,dx=[V]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

which yields

(3.9)

\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R})}\left\{\left(\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx\right)^{p}:[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=1\right\}\geq[V]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p\,(p-1)}.

On the other hand, by using again (
3.2
) and Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}}|\varphi|\,dx&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(x)-V(y)|^{p-2}(V(x)-V(y))\,\left(|\varphi(x)|-|\varphi(y)|\right)}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|V(x)-V(y)|^{p-2}(V(x)-V(y))}{|x-y|^{N\,\frac{p-1}{p}+s\,(p-1)}}\,\frac{|\varphi(x)|-|\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{p}+s}}\,dxdy\\
&\leq[V]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-1}\,[\varphi]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\end{split}

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R})
. This entails the reverse inequality of (
3.9
) and thus (
3.7
). The claimed estimates then follow by spending (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) in (
3.7
).
∎

4.
Poincaré inequalities

In this section we derive new
fractional Maz’ya-Poincaré-type inequalities
, see Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.11
below. As anticipated in the Introduction, they involve the presence of an
asymmetric seminorm
on strips (see Remark
1.10
) on the right-hand side and display sharp limiting behaviours for
s\searrow 0
and
s\nearrow 1
.

All the inequalities of this section will be stated for smooth functions with compact support, nevertheless they can be extended to every function in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by density (see for instance
[
46
, Theorem 6.66]
).

For the forthcoming technincal result, we take advantage of the
K-
method of the
Real Interpolation Theory
applied to fractional Sobolev spaces, for which we refer to
[
31
]
,
[
45
, Chapter 16]
,
[
46
, Chapter 12]
and to the references therein contained.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>0
.
For every
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we have

(4.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{K(t,u)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}\leq A_{N,p}\,\iint_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy,

where
K(t,u)
is the
K-
functional
5
5

5

Since in this context there is no ambiguity regarding the choice of the interpolation spaces, with an abuse of notation, we will denote the
K-
functional
K\left(t,u,L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0})\right),\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0})))
only with
K(t,u).
given by

K\left(t,u,L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0})\right),\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))):=\min_{v\in W^{1,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\Big[\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}+t\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\Big],\quad t\in[0,\infty).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
x_{0}=0
. We prove the claimed inequality (
4.1
) along the lines of
[
31
, Proposition 4.5]
. Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we set

U(h)=\left(\int_{B_{r}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\mbox{ for }h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The rightmost term in (
4.1
) is then given by

\iint_{B_{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{p}}{|h|^{N+s\,p}}\,dx\,dh=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U(h)^{p}}{|h|^{N+s\,p}}\,dh.

We also set

\overline{U}(\varrho):=\fint_{\partial B_{\varrho}}Ud\mathcal{H}^{N-1},\qquad\mbox{ for }\varrho>0.

By Jensen’s inequality we get

(4.2)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{U}^{p}\frac{d\varrho}{\varrho^{1+s\,p}}&\leq\frac{1}{N\omega_{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\partial B_{\varrho}}U^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right)\,\frac{d\varrho}{\varrho^{N+s\,p}}\\
&=\frac{1}{N\omega_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U(h)^{p}}{|h|^{N+s\,p}}\,dh\\
&=\frac{1}{N\omega_{N}}\iint_{B_{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{p}}{|h|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdh.\end{split}

We introduce the function

\psi(x)=\frac{N+1}{\omega_{N}}\big(1-|x|\big)_{+},\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

which is Lipschitz with compact support and with
L^{1}-
norm normalized to
1
. We set

\psi_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{t^{N}}\,\psi\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),\qquad\mbox{ for }t>0,

this function is supported on
\overline{B_{t}}
and still have unitary
L^{1}-
norm. By standard properties of convolutions,
we have that
\psi_{t}\ast u\in W^{1,p}(B_{r})
, thus

K(t,u)\leq\|u-\psi_{t}\ast u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+t\,\|\nabla\left(\psi_{t}\ast u\right)\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{r})},\qquad\mbox{ for }t>0.

We estimate separately the last two terms. For the first one, we use the integral Minkowski’s inequality, see
[
47
, Theorem 2.4]
for instance, with data

f(x,y):=u(x)-u(x-y),\quad\nu(dy):=\psi_{t}(y)\,dy,\quad\mu(dx):=dx,\quad\Omega:=B_{r}\quad\Gamma:=B_{t},

by using the notation therein contained. Along with Fubini’s Theorem, we get

\begin{split}\|u-\psi_{t}\ast u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}&=\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{B_{t}}(u(x)-u(x-y))\,\psi_{t}(y)\,dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\int_{B_{t}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left(u(x)-u(x-y)\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\psi_{t}(y)\,dy\\
&=\int_{B_{t}}U(-y)\,\psi_{t}(y)\,dy\leq\frac{N(N+1)}{t^{N}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{U}\varrho^{N-1}\,d\varrho\\
&\leq\frac{N(N+1)}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{U}\,d\varrho.\end{split}

For the second term, by the Divergence Theorem

\int_{B_{t}}\nabla\psi_{t}(y)\,dy=0,

thus

\nabla\left(\psi_{t}\ast u\right)=(\nabla\psi_{t})\ast u=\int_{B_{t}}\nabla\psi_{t}(y)\left(u(x-y)-u(x)\right)dy.

Then, by using the integral Minkowski’s inequality, we get

\begin{split}\|\nabla(\psi_{t}\ast u)\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}&=\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{B_{t}}\nabla\psi_{t}(y)\left(u(x-y)-u(x)\right)dy\right)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\int_{B_{t}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|u(x-y)-u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|\nabla\psi_{t}(y)\right|dy\\
&\leq\frac{N+1}{\omega_{N}\,t^{N+1}}\int_{B_{t}}U(-y)\,dy\leq\frac{N\,(N+1)}{t^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{U}\,d\varrho.\end{split}

By collecting the previous estimates, we infer that

K(t,u)\leq\|u-\psi_{t}\ast u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+t\,\|\nabla\left(\psi_{t}\ast u\right)\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{2N(N+1)}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{U}d\varrho,\mbox{ for }t>0,

yielding

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{K(t,u)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}\leq\left(2N(N+1)\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{U}d\varrho\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t^{1+s\,p}}.

To estimate the last term, we use the one-dimensional Hardy’s inequality (see for instance
[
45
, Theorem C.41]
) obtaining

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{K(t,u)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}&\leq\left(\frac{2N(N+1)}{s+1}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{U}^{p}\frac{dt}{t^{1+s\,p}}\\
&\leq\frac{\left(N+1\right)^{p}\,N^{p-1}}{\omega_{N}}\iint_{B_{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+h)|^{p}}{|h|^{N+s\,p}}dxdh,\end{split}

where in the last line we used (
4.2
). The proof is thereby complete.
∎

We also need the following Poincaré-Wirtinger-type estimate, which is of independent interest.

Lemma 4.2

(Fractional Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>0
.
For every
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we have

\|u-\mathrm{av}(u;B_{r}(x_{0}))\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq\mathcal{W}_{N,p}\,s\,(1-s)\,r^{s\,p}\,\iint_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy,

for some
\mathcal{W}_{N,p}>0
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
x_{0}=0
. We take
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{r})
and
t>0
. By the triangle inequality and Jensen’s inequality

\begin{split}\|u-{\rm av}(u;B_{r})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}&\leq\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+\|v-{\rm av}(v;B_{r})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+\|{\rm av}(u;B_{r})-{\rm av}(v;B_{r})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\\
&\leq 2\,\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+\|v-{\rm av}(v;B_{r})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\\
&\leq\max\left\{2,\mu_{N,p}\right\}\left(\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+r\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\right),\end{split}

for some
\mu_{N,p}>0
, where we used the classical Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality on balls. Thus

\|u-{\rm av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\leq c_{N,p}\,K(r,u)^{p}\leq c_{N,p}\,K(t,u)^{p},\quad\mbox{ for }t>r,

where we set
c_{N,p}:=\left(\max\{2,\mu_{N,p}\}\right)^{p}
.
This entails that

(4.3)

\begin{split}\|u-{\rm av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\,\frac{1}{s\,p}\left(\frac{1}{r^{s\,p}}\right)&=\|u-{\rm av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\,\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+s\,p}}\\
&\leq c_{N,p}\,\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(\frac{K(t,u)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by arguing as in the beginning of the proof, for every
0<t\leq r
we have

\begin{split}t\,\|u-\mathrm{av}(u;B_{r})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}&\leq 4r\,\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+t\,\|v-\mathrm{av}(v;B_{r})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\\
&\leq d_{N,p}\,r\left(\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}+t\,\|\nabla v\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{r})}\right),\end{split}

where we set
d_{N,p}:=\max\{4,\mu_{N,p}\}
, and we used again the classical Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality on balls. This yields

t^{p}\,\|u-\mathrm{av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\leq d_{N,p}^{p}\,r^{p}\,K(t,u)^{p},\qquad\mbox{ for }0<t\leq r,

and so

(4.4)

\begin{split}\|u-\mathrm{av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\frac{r^{(1-s)\,p}}{(1-s)\,p}&=\|u-\mathrm{av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\,\int_{0}^{r}\frac{t^{p}}{t^{1+s\,p}}dt\\
&\leq d_{N,p}^{p}\,r^{p}\,\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{K(t,u)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}.\end{split}

By (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we get

\|u-{\rm av}(u;B_{r})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\left(\frac{1}{(1-s)\,p}+\frac{1}{s\,p}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)^{s\,p}\leq a_{N,p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{K(t,u)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t},

where we set
a_{N,p}:=\max\left\{c_{N,p}^{p},d_{N,p}^{p}\right\}
. By spending (
4.1
), we eventually conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3

(Asymptotic sharpness)
.

By Remark
1.10
, the estimate established in Lemma
4.2
displays sharp limiting behaviours with respect to the fractional differentiability parameter
s
. In other words, by taking the
\liminf
for
s\nearrow 1
, we get the non-trivial Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality

\|u-{\rm av}\left(u;B_{r}(x_{0})\right)\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq C_{N,p}\,r\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\mbox{ for }u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

while taking the
\liminf
for
s\searrow 0
, we get

\|u-{\rm av}\left(u;B_{r}(x_{0})\right)\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq C_{N,p}\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\mbox{ for }u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We are now able to prove a new fractional Maz’ya-Poincaré-type inequality.
Its special form has been kindly suggested to the authors by Lorenzo Brasco.

Lemma 4.4

(Maz’ya-Poincaré)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
0<r<R
and let
\Sigma\subseteq\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
be a compact set. For every
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u\equiv 0
on
\Sigma
, we have

\frac{M}{r^{\frac{N}{p}}}\left(\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}(x_{0})\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq\left(\iint_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
M=M\left(N,p,R/r\right)>0
. Moreover

0<\displaystyle\lim_{R/r\to 1}\frac{M\left(N,p,R/r\right)}{\left(R/r-1\right)^{\frac{N}{p}+1}}<\infty,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad 0<\lim_{R/r\to\infty}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}\,M\left(N,p,\frac{R}{r}\right)<\infty.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
x_{0}=0
and

(4.5)

\fint_{B_{r}}|u|^{p}dx=1.

We set

\delta:=(R-r)/2.

We take
\eta\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(B_{R})
such that

(4.6)

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\quad\eta\equiv 1\mbox{ on }\overline{B_{r}},\quad\eta\equiv 0\mbox{ on }B_{R}\setminus B_{R-\delta},\quad|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{1}{(R-\delta)-r},

and we set

\psi:=\Big((1-u)\,\eta\Big)_{+}\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(B_{R}).

By Proposition
2.8
, this function is a feasible competitor for the definition of
\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}
, thus

\begin{split}\left(\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\leq\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\iint_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&:=\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2},\end{split}

where we used the subadditivity of the function
\tau\mapsto\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}
. To estimate the first term, we use
[
57
, Lemma 2.6(b)]
, we add and subtract
\eta(x)(1-u(y))
and apply Minkowski’s inequality obtaining

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{1}&\leq\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{B_{R}}|1-u(y)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dx\right)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\frac{c_{N,p}}{s\,(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{(R-\delta)-r}\right)^{s}\|1-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})},\end{split}

where in the last line we also used Lemma
2.3
.

To estimate
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, we observe that for
x\in B_{R-\delta}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}
we have

|x-y|\geq|y|-|x|\geq|y|-\left(R-\delta\right)\geq|y|-\frac{R-\delta}{R}|y|=\frac{\delta}{R}|y|,

thus a direct computation leads to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\leq\frac{N\,\omega_{N}}{s\,p}\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)^{N+s\,p}\frac{1}{R^{s\,p}},\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in B_{R-\delta}.

This entails that

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{2}=\left(\iint_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\leq\left(\int_{B_{R-\delta}}|1-u(x)|^{p}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{N\,\omega_{N}}{s\,p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}+s}\,\frac{1}{R^{s}}\|1-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}.\end{split}

By collecting the previous estimates, we obtain

(4.7)

\begin{split}\left(\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\frac{1}{s\,(1-s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{D}{R^{s}}\,\|1-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})},\end{split}

where we set

D\left(N,p,s,R,r,\delta\right):=\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)^{s}\left[c_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\delta}{(R-\delta)-r}\right)^{s}+\left(\frac{(1-s)\,N\,\omega_{N}}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{R}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}\right].

In turn, by recalling the definition of
\delta
, we can majorize the last constant with another one which has a simpler expression

(4.8)

D\leq\frac{2R}{R-r}\left[c_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\frac{N\omega_{N}}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{2R}{R-r}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}\right]=:E\left(N,p,\frac{R}{r}\right).

To estimate the
L^{p}-
norm appearing in the second term of (
4.7
), by the triangle inequality and by recalling (
4.5
) we get

\begin{split}\|1-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}&\leq\|1-{\rm av}(u;B_{R})\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}+\|{\rm av}(u;B_{R})-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\\
&\leq\frac{|B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{|B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{p}}}\Big|\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}-\|{\rm av}(u;B_{R})\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\Big|+\|{\rm av}(u;B_{R})-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\\
&\leq\left(\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}+1\right)\|u-{\rm av}(u;B_{R})\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}.\end{split}

By Lemma
4.2
, we then obtain

\begin{split}\|1-u\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}&\leq\left(\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}+1\right)\,\left(\mathcal{W}_{N,p}\,s\,(1-s)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,R^{s}\,\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

By spending this information in (
4.7
) and using (
4.8
), we get

\left(\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left[1+\mathcal{W}_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\left(\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}+1\right)E\right]\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By recalling (
4.5
), we eventually obtain

\frac{M}{r^{\frac{N}{p}}}\left(\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\Sigma;B_{R}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\leq\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we set

M\left(N,p,\frac{R}{r}\right):=\omega_{N}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left[1+\mathcal{W}_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\left(\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}+1\right)E\right]^{-1},

where
E
is given by (
4.8
), as desired.
∎

4.1.

We now derive

, there will be the

norm. The exponent

is required to be

and

(4.9)

p\leq q\begin{cases}\leq p^{*}_{s},\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p<N,\\
<\infty,\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p=N,\\
\leq\infty,\quad&\mbox{ if }s\,p>N.\end{cases}

We state the forthcoming inequalities separately from that of Lemma

Proposition 4.7

(Poincaré-Sobolev)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. We take an exponent
q
satisfying (
4.9
).
Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>0
. For every
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, we have

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq\frac{2}{s\,(1-s)\,\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(B_{1})}\,r^{s\,p-N+N\,\frac{p}{q}}\,s\,(1-s)\iint_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
x_{0}=0
. Since
\lambda^{s}_{p,q}(B_{r})>0
, we have

\begin{split}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(B_{r})}&\leq\frac{r^{s\,p-N+N\,\frac{p}{q}}}{\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(B_{1})}\,[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&=\frac{r^{s\,p-N+N\,\frac{p}{q}}}{\lambda_{p,q}^{s}(B_{1})}\left(\iint_{B_{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy+\iint_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\right).\end{split}

We focus on the second term. Since
u=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}
, we have

\begin{split}\iint_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy&=\iint_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}\right)\times B_{r}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\\
&=\iint_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}\right)\times B_{r}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy\\
&\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times B_{r}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}dxdy.\end{split}

The conclusion then follows by Fubini’s theorem.
∎

Lemma 4.9

Lemma 4.11

5.1.
Proof of the lower bound

Proof of Theorem

.

We can assume

, for every

We introduce now two covering families for the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
made of balls with equidistant centers.
Define

and

We set

Both the families of balls
\{B_{\bf p}\}_{{\bf p}\in Z_{r}}
and
\{\widetilde{B_{\bf p}}\}_{{\bf p}\in Z_{r}}
cover the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We claim that the multiplicity of
\{\widetilde{B_{\bf p}}\}_{{\bf p}\in Z_{r}}
is at most
(4N+1)^{N}
. To prove this, for
{\bf p}\in Z_{r}
we set

\quad Q_{\bf p}:=Q_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{N}}}+{\bf p},\quad\widetilde{Q_{\bf p}}:=Q_{\varrho}+{\bf p},\quad\mbox{ where }\varrho:=\frac{4N+1}{\sqrt{N}}\,r.

Observe that
\widetilde{B_{{\bf p}}}
intersects at most
(4N+1)^{N}
balls of
\{\widetilde{B_{\bf q}}\}_{{\bf q}\in Z_{r}}.
Indeed, if

for some different

This entails that

Q_{\bf q}\subseteq\widetilde{Q_{\bf p}},

since for every

we have

|y_{k}-p_{k}|\leq|y_{k}-q_{k}|+|q_{k}-p_{k}|<\frac{r}{\sqrt{N}}+4\sqrt{N}r=\varrho,\quad\mbox{ for }k=1,\ldots,N.

Our claim then follows by observing that

{\rm card}\Big(Z_{r}\cap\widetilde{Q_{\bf p}}\Big)\leq(4N+1)^{N}.

We can now conclude the proof. Let

be extended by zero on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}

on

4.11

&\geq\frac{M^{p}}{r^{N\,\frac{p}{q}}}\sum_{{\bf p}\in Z_{r}}\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{\bf p}}\setminus\Omega;\widetilde{B_{\bf p}}\right)\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(B_{{\bf p}})},\end{split}

where
M=M\left(N,p,s,q\right)
is given by Lemma
4.11
. By using Proposition
2.14
, for every
{\bf p}\in Z_{r}
, there exists a constant

By spending this information in (

\begin{split}(4\,N+1)^{N}\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s\,p}}\,dxdy&\geq\frac{1}{r^{N\,\frac{p}{q}}}\,\frac{M^{p}}{\mathscr{C}}\,\sum_{{\bf p}\in Z_{r}}\widetilde{\rm cap}_{s,p}\left(\overline{B_{\bf p}}\setminus\Omega;B^{*}_{\bf p}\right)\|u\|^{p}_{L^{q}(B_{{\bf p}})}\\

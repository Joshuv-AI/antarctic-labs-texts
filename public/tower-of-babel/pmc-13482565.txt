Efficient multi-task CNN framework for joint identification of soybean leaf diseases and pesticide presence with explainable AI
Authors: Fikadu Berie Adugna, Esubalew Asmare Desta, Abenet Alazar Hailu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSoybean production is significantly affected by crop diseases and improper pesticide use, which hinder effective disease management and reduce yield. In this study, we propose an efficient multi-task convolutional neural network (CNN) framework for the simultaneous detection of soybean seed diseases and pesticide presence from seed images. The model leverages a shared feature extraction backbone with task-specific output heads to learn complementary features for both disease classification and pesticide detection. A dataset of 429 soybean leaf images was preprocessed using normalization and augmentation techniques and split into training, validation, and testing sets. We evaluated three backbone architectures VGG19, MobileNetV3, and ConvNeXt within the multi-task framework. Experimental results demonstrate that the approach maintains computational efficiency suitable for real-world deployment while achieving high performance, with accuracies of 95%, 96%, and 97% for MobileNetV3, VGG19, and ConvNeXt, respectively. Additionally, explainable AI methods, such as Grad-CAM, highlight regions of focus for both tasks, making the model’s decision-making process interpretable. This framework provides a practical tool for informed crop management and agricultural monitoring.Keywords: CNN, Explainable AI, Multi-task, Pesticide detection, Single-task, Soybean leafSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

A major crop in the world, soybeans are grown extensively for their high protein and oil content, which makes them essential for use in both industrial and food applications. However, a number of seed diseases and the application of pesticides, which are meant to protect crops but can leaf toxic residues, pose a serious threat to soybean production1,2. These issues present serious health concerns and financial losses in addition to lowering the yield and quality of soybean seeds. Therefore, protecting crop health and guaranteeing food safety depend on the precise and prompt identification of seed diseases and pesticide presence3,4.

Manual inspection and laboratory testing are the usual methods used in traditional methods to detect pesticide residues and soybean seed diseases. These techniques work well, but they are expensive, time-consuming, and necessitate specialized knowledge, which restricts their applicability in large-scale farming operations5,6. Furthermore, human error and inconsistency are common in manual examination, particularly when handling different pesticide kinds or mild visual signs. The need for quick, dependable, and automated diagnostic tools has increased, which has fueled research into cutting-edge technologies, especially in the areas of computer vision and machine learning7,8.

In Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), a subset of deep learning, have transformed image identification tasks by offering automatic and incredibly accurate solutions. CNNs can discern between healthy and unhealthy seeds with amazing accuracy because they can learn intricate patterns from enormous image collections9–11. The majority of current research, however, focuses on single-task learning models that deal with disease detection. For real-time agricultural applications, such discrete techniques may result in longer processing times and more computational resources.

To address these limitations, this study presents an effective multi-task CNN framework capable of simultaneously identifying soybean leaf diseases and detecting pesticide presence. Multi-task learning leverages shared representations and complementary information between related tasks, enhancing overall model performance and generalization12. By integrating disease detection and pesticide identification into a unified model, the framework reduces the need for separate models and datasets, leading to more efficient and scalable solutions suitable for practical deployment.

The proposed framework employs advanced CNN architectures optimized for feature extraction from high-resolution images combined with tailored loss functions to balance the learning of both tasks12,13. This approach aims to improve diagnostic accuracy while minimizing computational overhead, making it accessible for real-world use on devices with limited processing power, such as mobile phones or portable scanners used in the field. The model’s ability to concurrently analyze multiple aspects of soybean health represents a significant step towards intelligent, data-driven decision support systems in agriculture.

Statement of the problem

Soybeans are a major agricultural commodity, critical for food, oil, and animal feed production worldwide. However, they are susceptible to a wide range of diseases and pests, which can severely reduce crop yield and quality10,14. Traditional methods for identifying diseases in soybean seeds and recommending appropriate pesticides are often time-consuming, subjective, and prone to human error. As a result, early and accurate detection remains a significant challenge in the agricultural industry, affecting overall crop productivity15,16.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), a recent development in machine learning and deep learning, have shown great promise in automating the detection of crop disease and pests. CNN models have been utilized in a number of studies to identify distinct soybean seed kinds or determine the type of disease affecting soybeans17. Additional study has focused on identifying pests and suggesting pesticide remedies according on the species of pests found. Although these methods have made significant contributions, they often concentrate on just one task: identifying pesticides or diseases. But in reality, managing disease and pests accurately requires doing both at the same time because they are frequently linked, and a successful solution must deal with both problems at once.

There is still a big gap in combining pesticide advice and disease recognition into a single framework, even with the improvements in single-task models. The interdependency between disease detection and pesticide selection is not adequately captured by current systems that focus on one activity at a time. In addition to increasing the speed and precision of disease management, a model that can simultaneously identify soybean seed disease and suggest suitable pesticides would also assist farmers in making quicker, better-informed decisions, which would eventually increase crop production and lower losses.

This research aims to fill this gap by developing a multi-tasking CNN model that recognizes soybean leaf diseases while concurrently suggesting the most suitable pesticide treatments. By training the model on both tasks simultaneously, we expect to leverage the relationship between disease classification and pesticide recommendation, creating a more efficient and practical solution for real-world applications. The simultaneous recognition of disease and pesticide suggestions represents an innovative step towards automating soybean crop management, providing an integrated tool for farmers and agricultural experts12. Related work

In recent years, plant disease detection has advanced significantly thanks to developments in deep learning and machine learning (ML), especially Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Numerous researches have examined the application of CNNs to categorize various crop varieties, and identify certain crop disease. Various crops, particularly soybeans, have benefited from the effective application of these techniques in an attempt to automate disease diagnostics.

However, the majority of current research treats concentrates on either identifying the type of disease or defining pest species. For example, some models only detect the sort of illness that is damaging the crop10,16–22, while others only identify pests or suggest treatments with pesticides23. The complexity of real-world farming situations, where disease and pests frequently occur simultaneously and are interrelated, is not addressed by this one-task method. A model that can simultaneously identify both soybean leaf diseases and suggest the best pesticide treatment is therefore required, utilizing the connections between these two activities to provide more accurate and efficient management options.

Lack of multi-task learning (MTL) models that can manage concurrent pesticide recommendation and disease recognition, especially for soybean seeds, is the research gap. The goal of this research is to combine both tasks into a single framework by creating a multi-task CNN model12,13. In addition to improving the effectiveness and precision of disease detection and pesticide control, this strategy will give farmers a more useful tool to help them make well-informed, timely decisions for improved crop health management. Methodology

A multi-task Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is used in the suggested framework to simultaneously diagnose soybean leaf disease and detect the presence of pesticides. RGB photos with 224 × 224-pixel sizes are fed into the model. To extract rich hierarchical features from the images, a shared convolutional backbone built on the ConvNeXt, MobileNetV3, and VGG19 architecture is employed. In order to reduce spatial dimensions while maintaining prominent features, this backbone is composed of numerous convolutional blocks with batch normalization and ReLU activation functions, followed by max-pooling layers.

Two task-specific heads emerge from the network after the shared feature extraction layers. A SoftMax layer that generates probabilities for every disease category comes after the first branch, which is devoted to the classification of soybean leaf diseases and consists of two fully connected layers with dropout regularization to avoid overfitting. In order to predict the presence of pesticide residues, the second branch uses fully connected layers that culminate in SoftMax activation.

A weighted sum of cross-entropy loss functions is used to train both tasks concurrently, enabling the model to optimize for precise pesticide detection and disease classification simultaneously. With a batch size of 32 and an initial learning rate of 0.001, the Adam optimizer is used. This design is well-suited for real-world implementation in agricultural contexts because it facilitates effective parameter sharing and lowers computing cost as compared to individual models. The proposed model operates in a sequential pipeline. First, soybean leaf images undergo preprocessing steps, including resizing and augmentation, to standardize input dimensions and enhance model generalization. The preprocessed images are then passed into a CNN backbone, where hierarchical feature representations are extracted. These shared features are directed into two parallel multi-task heads: the first head performs disease classification by predicting the health status and type of infection, while the second head generate practical management recommendations based on the identified condition. To ensure balanced learning across both objectives, the model is trained using a weighted joint loss function, which simultaneously optimizes disease diagnosis accuracy and the relevance of recommendations. To ensure robust evaluation of the proposed multi-task CNN framework, both the standard train/validation/test split and k-fold cross-validation were employed. Preprocessing the Input Images

A horizontal flip approach was used for image augmentation in order to improve the dataset’s robustness and diversity. By flipping each image along its vertical axis, this technique essentially produces a mirror image. In situations like facial identification, medical imaging, or object detection tasks, where an object’s orientation in an image has no bearing on its classification or recognition, horizontal flipping is especially helpful26,27. The model is exposed to a variety of viewpoints by using flipped versions of the original images, which lowers the possibility of overfitting and enhances generalization to new data. This technique was used to create 62 augmented photos for this study for Septoria disease which is smaller number of images compare to others, which effectively increased the quantity and diversity of the dataset without requiring the collection of new data. The model’s capacity to learn spatially invariant characteristics is improved by this augmentation technique, which also helps to provide more balanced training. We also integrate image denoising and filtering preprocessing techniques to evaluate their impact on model performance, including the Median Filter, which is widely used for removing impulse noise (salt-and-pepper) and enhancing image quality prior to feature extraction28–30.

Data splitting

Three separate subsets were created from the entire input dataset: testing, validation, and training. This well-organized division guarantees that the model can be trained efficiently, adjusted suitably, and assessed impartially. In particular, the model was able to learn pertinent features and patterns related to soybean leaf diseases and pesticide presence because 70% of the entire dataset was set aside for training. In order to track the model’s performance during training and adjust hyper parameters without overfitting, 15% of the dataset was set aside for validation. In order to evaluate the final model’s capacity for generalization on unknown data, the remaining 15% was reserved for testing. This approach to data splitting guarantees a robust and equitable assessment of the model while adhering to best standards in deep learning-based picture classification tasks. Experimental result and discussion

To evaluate the performance of the proposed multi-task deep learning model for soybean disease and pesticide classification, extensive experiments were conducted using Python. The primary model implementation was developed using the Keras API with TensorFlow as the backend. In addition, PyTorch was utilized for complementary experiments and performance validation.

All experiments were conducted on cloud-based platforms, including Google Colab. To ensure a representative and diverse set of disease and pesticide samples for training and assessment, a total of six experiments were conducted utilizing image datasets gathered from well-known and openly accessible sources on Kaggle. These tests, which included trials using VGG19 and ConvNeXt as backbone models, were intended to thoroughly evaluate the generalization, accuracy, and resilience of our suggested multi-task learning approach under a range of circumstances and architectural configurations.

Widely used metrics, including as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, were used to assess the model’s performance and provide a comprehensive picture of classification effectiveness. In order to benchmark our findings and evaluate advancements, each experiment was meticulously planned to compare the outcomes against pre-existing deep learning models. Thus, the experimental setting demonstrates a methodical and repeatable approach meant to validate the potential of multi-task convolutional neural networks in the context of automated plant disease control and precision agriculture.

Case 2: Experimental result for the augmented image

We used an augmented soybean leaf disease images in order to assess the suggested Efficient Multi-Task CNN Framework for concurrently identifying soybean leaf disease and detecting pesticide presence. Two tasks basically disease categorization and pesticide presence detection were marked on each image. The presence of pesticides was handled as a multi classification problem, and the disease classification task had five different classes, including bacterial blight, Brown Spot, Septoria,Southern Blight and Sudden Death Syndrone. Prior to normalization, images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels and subjected to conventional ImageNet settings.

The dataset was divided into three categories at random: 15% validation (64 photos), 15% testing (65 images), and 70% training (300 images). We evaluated the performance of our multi-task model against a naive multi-task model with shared convolutional layers but no task-specific optimization, as well as baseline single-task CNNs, such as ConvNeXt, VGG19 and MobileNetV3, which were trained independently for each task. Standard criteria, such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score for disease classification, were employed to assess performance. We also computed Multi-Task Learning Efficiency (MTLE), which is the harmonic mean of the accuracies of each individual task, to evaluate the overall system efficiency.

Using empirically balanced weights, a bespoke loss function was created by integrating categorical cross-entropy for detecting the presence of pesticides and categorical cross-entropy for multi-class disease classification. Using the Adam optimizer and a learning rate of 0.0001, the models were trained across 20 epochs with a batch size of 32. To avoid overfitting, early stopping based on validation loss was employed. The collaborative learning framework was effectively evaluated under practical computational and data restrictions thanks to this experimental design.

Experiment 1: Aimed to establish a baseline using ConvNeXt for multitask classification of soybean disease and pesticide

A dual-task classification problem with disease prediction as the main class and pesticide prediction as the subclass has been successfully solved using the ConvNeXt model, a deep convolutional neural network known for its ease of use and efficacy. ConvNeXt’s architecture incorporates a number of significant changes while drawing inspiration from ResNet. It stabilizes training and boosts performance by substituting Layer Normalization, a method frequently employed in Transformers, for batch normalization. Larger 7 × 7 depthwise convolutions are used in place of the conventional 3 × 3 convolutional kernels, enabling the model to replicate the global receptive fields of self-attention processes and capture a wider range of context.

The multi-task learning method increases feature sharing and classification accuracy by having the model predict both pesticide classes and disease categories simultaneously using different output heads. Explainability and model interpretation

We used Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) as an explainable AI (XAI) technique to show the areas of soybean leaf pictures that the model concentrated on during prediction-making in order to improve the interpretability of our deep learning model31,37. Grad-CAM calculates the gradients of the projected class in relation to the CNN’s final convolutional layers’ feature maps. The most significant regions of the input image are then highlighted in a heatmap created using these gradients33,34.

In our case, we used Grad-CAM to examine the model’s decision-making process on both correctly and wrongly classified photos. According to the visuals, the model mostly concentrated on areas of the leaf that were impacted by disease, such as discolored patches, lesions, or uneven textures. This demonstrates that the model is picking up pertinent and significant traits and is in line with expert knowledge in plant pathology. Grad-CAM assisted us in locating occasions in which the model’s focus was diverted to ambiguous or irrelevant background characteristics in the misclassified images, indicating possible areas for preprocessing or dataset quality improvement35,38,39.

In addition to making the model’s predictions more transparent, these visual cues gave users assurance in the model’s dependability for real-world agricultural applications. We were able to confirm that the multi-task learning model, particularly when using ConvNeXt, bases its judgments on relevant visual signals rather than arbitrary or deceptive features by utilizing Grad-CAM. The suggested multi-task CNN framework uses a number of techniques to reduce overfitting. To improve the diversity of training samples, data augmentation methods such as flipping and median filter were used. To stop neurons from co-adapting, dropout layers were added to the task-specific heads. Furthermore, training was stopped before the model could overfit the training set using early stopping based on validation loss. Because pre-trained architectures (ConvNeXt, MobileNetV3, and VGG19) offer reliable feature representations acquired from huge datasets, they also aid in lowering overfitting. Last but not least, a suitable train-test split guarantees that the generalization of the model is assessed on unknown data.

Finally, we evaluated the model’s robustness to adversarial attacks, which is crucial for real-world deployment of the multi-task CNN based on ConvNeXt. We employed the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) to subtly perturb input images, using different noise strengths epsilon = (0.01, 0.05, 0.1, and 0.2) to test the network’s sensitivity. The model achieved an accuracy of 97% on clean images, and 97%, 97%, 97%, and 95% under adversarial attacks for epsilon = 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, and 0.2, respectively. Although the proposed techniques have shown promising results, certain limitations need to be acknowledged. First, the model’s performance may vary depending on dataset quality, diversity, and class imbalance. Second, while effective in experimental settings, the computational requirements could be a barrier to deployment in low-resource environments. Third, the current evaluation is limited to the available dataset, and further validation on larger, real-world datasets is necessary to ensure generalizability.

In addition, the suggested multi-task CNN architecture shows great accuracy and usefulness, there are still a number of drawbacks. MobileNetV3 provides a lightweight substitute for high-performance models like ConvNeXt, which may limit adoption in low-resource contexts due to their high processing requirements. The quality and diversity of the training dataset determine how efficient the model is, and performance may suffer under different crop kinds or under unknown circumstances. Furthermore, whereas Grad-CAM offers insightful information about model decision-making, complete causal interpretability is not guaranteed. Lastly, more customization may be necessary for practical deployment in real-field situations to take environmental unpredictability into consideration.

Conclusion and future work

In this study, we developed a multi-task CNN framework integrating explainable AI to simultaneously detect pesticide presence and diagnose soybean seed diseases. Among the three evaluated models:—ConvNeXt, VGG19, and MobileNetV3.ConvNeXt achieved the highest performance on augmented datasets, with 97% accuracy, 98% precision and,97% of recall, and an F1-score of 97%, demonstrating its robustness for complex classification tasks. MobileNetV3 proved to be a lightweight alternative, suitable for deployment in resource-constrained or mobile agricultural settings. The application of Grad-CAM enhanced transparency by highlighting biologically relevant regions affecting predictions, thereby improving model interpretability and trustworthiness. The key findings indicate that the proposed framework not only delivers highly accurate disease and pesticide detection but also provides actionable insights through Explainability, making it directly applicable for precision agriculture. These results suggest significant potential for improving soybean crop management, reducing yield losses, and supporting informed decision-making in real-world agricultural practices.

Future work

Even though the suggested multi-task CNN framework performed well in detecting pesticide presence and identifying soybean seed disease, there are a number of research avenues that could improve its efficacy and usefulness even more. The model’s generalizability could be enhanced by expanding the dataset to include more varied soybean disease types, environmental factors, and actual contamination events. Furthermore, combining hyperspectral or multispectral photography may yield richer spectral information than RGB inputs, improving the identification of chemical residues and subtle disease symptoms. In order to support real-time, on-field analysis and decision-making, particularly in environments with limited resources, future work can also concentrate on implementing the model on edge devices or agricultural drones.

Data Availability Statement

All images were obtained from the publicly available Kaggle dataset “Soybean Diseased Leaf Dataset” (https:/www.kaggle.com/datasets/sivm205/soybean-diseased-leaf-dataset). To extend the dataset, additional CSV annotation files were created to include disease labels and corresponding pesticide information. These annotations were merged with the image data to support a joint task of disease classification and pesticide recommendation. The enriched dataset is publicly available at https:/www.kaggle.com/datasets/mlakuberie/soybean-images .

The custom code developed for this study is publicly available on GitHub at https://github.com/fikaduberie/Soybean-Disease-and-Pest (version v1.0). A permanent archived version corresponding to the code used in this study is available via Zenodo at 10.5281/zenodo.19444092. The repository includes scripts for data preprocessing, model training, evaluation, and performance analysis. The code is freely accessible and no restrictions apply to its use.

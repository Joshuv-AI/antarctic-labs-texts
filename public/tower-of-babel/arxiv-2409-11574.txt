Igor Araujo, Dadong Peng

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Monochromatic cliques in edge-colorings of the complete graph is one of the most studied topics in Graph Theory. Ramsey Theory studies, when the number of colors is fixed, how large a complete graph must be to ensure a monochromatic copy of a clique of given order. If we allow the number of colors to be arbitrary, we can still ask how large a complete graph must be to ensure a copy of a clique with some special coloring, which we call
canonical
coloring. Erdős and Rado
[
6
]
introduced the so-called
Canonical Ramsey Numbers
, which we denote by
\text{er}(t)
, in the context of vertex-ordered graphs.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős, Rado
[
6
]
)
.

For every integer
t
, there exists a minimum number
n=\text{er}(t)
such that the following holds. For every edge-coloring
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
of the complete graph on (ordered) vertex-set
[n]
, there exists a complete subgraph of order
t
whose coloring is one of the following:

•

monochromatic;

•

rainbow;

•

upper lexical (two edges have the same color if and only if they have the same higher endpoint);

•

lower lexical (two edges have the same color if and only if they have the same lower endpoint).

In terms of a quantitative bound for
\text{er}(t)
, Lefmann and Rödl
[
8
]
proved that

2^{c^{\prime}t^{2}}\leq\text{er}(t)\leq 2^{c"t^{2}\log(t)}

for some constants
c^{\prime}
and
c"
. Some improvements on the value of
c^{\prime}
and
c"
were made since (see, e.g.
[
1
]
) but the logarithmic gap in the exponent remains.

Richer
[
9
]
introduced an unordered variant, where (ordered) monochromatic, upper lexical and lower lexical cliques are seen as special case of the so-called
orderable
cliques in the unordered setting. These numbers, which we denote by
\text{CR}(o,r)
, were called “Unordered Canonical Ramsey Numbers.”

Definition 1.2
.

A coloring
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
is called
orderable
if there is an ordering
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
of the vertices of
K_{n}
such that two edges have the same color if they have the same lower end, i.e.,
\chi(v_{i}v_{j})=\chi(v_{i}v_{k})
for every
1\leq i<j<k\leq n
.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that in the above definition we only ask that two edges have the same color if they have the same lower end, compared to the “if and only if” requirement for the
lower lexical
coloring considered by Erdős and Rado.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that the above definition would be equivalent if one asks that there exists ordering for which two edges have the same color if they have the same upper end.

Definition 1.5

(Unordered Canonical Ramsey Numbers
[
9
]
)
.

We define the Unordered Canonical Ramsey Number
\text{CR}(o,r)
as the minimum
n
such that every coloring
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
contains one of the following:

•

orderable
K_{o}
;

•

rainbow
K_{r}
.

In this note we are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
\text{CR}(o,r)
for fixed
o
, when
r
tends to infinity. A construction from Babai
[
4
]
and an upper bound from Alon, Lefmann, and Rödl
[
2
]
show that

\text{CR}(3,r)=\Theta\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right).

For general
o\geq 4
, Jiang
[
7
]
proved that

\Omega\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{o-2}\leq\text{CR}(o,r)\leq O\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{o-1}.

The main contribution of this paper is to close the gap between the lower and upper bounds.

Theorem 1.6
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for every
o
and
r
we have

\text{CR}(o,r)\leq\left(\frac{c\cdot r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{o-2}.

Further, we introduce and discuss the following version of Unordered Canonical Ramsey Numbers, where we identify only the lower lexical and upper lexical cliques as
lexical
cliques.

Definition 1.7
.

A coloring
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
is called
lexical
if there is an ordering
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
of the vertices of
K_{n}
such that two edges have the same color if and only if they have the same lower end.

Definition 1.8
.

We define the Unordered Erdős–Rado Number
\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)
as the minimum
n
such that every coloring
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
contains one of the following:

•

monochromatic
K_{m}
;

•

lexical
K_{\ell}
;

•

rainbow
K_{r}
.

The focus of this note is the asymptotic behavior of
\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)
for fixed
m,\ell
, and
r
tending to infinity. Some other ranges of parameters have also been studied in the past (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
). We now make some observations on how the known results on
\text{CR}(o,r)
translate to results on
\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)
.

The asymptotic of the Unordered Erdős–Rado Number
\text{ER}(3,3,r)
is known up to a multiplicative constant
[
2
,
4
]

\text{ER}(3,3,r)=\Theta\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right).

The proof of the lower bound
\text{CR}(o,r)\geq\Omega\left(r^{3}/\log r\right)^{o-2}
in
[
7
]
extends the coloring from Babai
[
4
]
for
o=3
to arbitrary
o\geq 4
via a blow-up coloring.
We highlight that this coloring gives a stronger result, because it not only avoids orderable cliques of given size, but do so by also avoiding monochromatic triangles.

Theorem 1.9

(Adapted from
[
7
]
)
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for every integers
\ell\geq 3
and
r\geq 3
we have

\text{ER}(3,\ell,r)\geq\left(\frac{c\cdot r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{\ell-2}.

We will show that this lower bound is best possible up to the constant factor, and also obtain upper bounds on
\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)
for other fixed values of
m
and
\ell
.

Theorem 1.10
.

(1)

There is a constant
c>0
such that for every integers
m\geq 3
and
r\geq 3
we have

\text{ER}(m,3,r)\leq c\cdot m\cdot\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}.

(2)

There is a constant
c>0
such that for every integers
\ell\geq 3
and
r\geq 3
we have

\text{ER}(3,\ell,r)\leq\left(\frac{c\cdot r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{\ell-2}.

(3)

There is a constant
c>0
such that for every integer
r\geq 3
we have

\text{ER}(4,4,r)\leq\frac{c\cdot r^{7}}{(\log r)^{2}}.

Remark 1.11
.

The bounds in (
1
) and (
2
) are best possible, up to the dependency on
m
in (
1
), and up to constant
c
in (
2
), see Theorem
1.9
.

Remark 1.12
.

If
R(m)
denotes the 2-colored Ramsey number of
m
, then note that
\text{ER}(m,4,3)\geq R(m)>(\sqrt{2}+o(1))^{m}
. One cannot expect an upper bound of the form
(cmr^{3}/\log r)^{2}
for
\text{ER}(m,4,r)
, in contrast to
\text{ER}(m,3,r)\leq cmr^{3}/\log r
.

Remark 1.13
.

The proof of (
3
) can be extended to give an upper bound for every
m\geq 4
. However, we do not expect the bound in (
3
) to be best possible, see Conjecture
5.2
. To simplify the presentation we opt to write the proof only for the

case. The best known lower bound is from

\text{ER}(m,4,r)\geq\text{ER}(3,4,r)\geq\Omega(r^{3}/\log r)^{2}.

2.
Colorings with bounded maximum degree

In the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we will make use of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(Alon, Jiang, Miller, Pritikin; Theorem 5.6 in
[
1
]
)
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that the following holds. For every integers
\Delta
and
r
, if
n>c\cdot\Delta\cdot\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}
and
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
is a coloring, where each color-class has maximum degree at most
\Delta
, then
\chi
contains a rainbow clique of order
r
.

In the proofs that follow we will make repetitively use of the above Lemma
2.1
, which motivates the following notation to be used from now on.

Definition 2.2
.

A
\Delta
-good
coloring of a graph
G
is a coloring
\chi:E(G)\to\omega
for which every color-class has maximum degree at most
\Delta
.

Part (
1
) of Theorem
1.10
follows from Lemma
2.1
, since a coloring avoiding monochromatic
K_{m}
and lexical
K_{3}
is
m
-good.
Note that Part (
2
) of Theorem
1.10
is a consequence of Theorem
1.6
since every orderable
K_{\ell}
either is a lexical
K_{\ell}
or contains a monochromatic copy of
K_{3}
. We are now ready to prove our main result, Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The proof is by induction on
o\geq 3
. Let
c>0
be a constant larger than the constant given by Lemma
2.1
and such that the base case

\text{CR}(3,r)\leq c\cdot\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}

holds true. Such a constant exists by
[
2
]
.

For the inductive step, assume
o\geq 4
and
n\geq\left(\frac{c\cdot r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{o-2}
. Observe that, by Lemma
2.1
, if
\chi
is
\left(\frac{c\cdot r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{o-3}
-good then
\chi
contains a rainbow clique of order
r
and we are done. We assume then that there exists a vertex
v
and a color, say 1, such that the neighborhood of
v
in color 1 satisfies
|N_{1}(v)|\geq\left(\frac{c\cdot r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{o-3}
.

By the induction hypothesis applied to the restriction of
\chi
to

, the coloring
\chi
contains either a rainbow
K_{r}
, or an orderable
K_{o-1}
. If the second case happens, then, by appending

to the orderable
K_{o-1}
, we obtain an orderable
K_{o}
, as desired.
∎

1.10
(

First, we note that we can assume

. Let

be a constant larger than the constant given by Lemma

\text{ER}(4,3,r)\leq 4c\cdot r^{3}/\log r

Let

and
\chi:E(K_{n})\to\omega
be a coloring. We will show that
\chi
contains either a monochromatic
K_{4}
, lexical
K_{4}
, or rainbow

.

If
\chi

under
\chi
.
Let us assume then that there is a vertex
v

|N_{1}(v)|\geq 60r\cdot\left(c\cdot\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right)

Let

By Claims

(3.1)

\displaystyle|N_{i}(u)\cap V|<6\text{ for every }i\neq 1.

Let

.
For any subset

4.
Lower bound for

In this section we will show the following.

Claim 4.1
.

For every integers
\ell\geq 3
and
r\geq 3

\text{ER}(3,\ell+1,r)\geq(\text{ER}(3,\ell,r)-1)\cdot(\text{ER}(3,3,r)-1)+1.

The case

K_{\ell}
, we conclude
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell+1}
cannot induce a lexical clique. Assume then
v_{1}\in V_{i}
and
v_{2}\in V_{j}
for
i\neq j
. As
\chi_{3}
is a proper coloring,
\chi(v_{1}v_{2})=\chi(v_{1}u)
only for vertices
u\in V_{j}
, we conclude
v_{2},v_{3},\dots,v_{\ell+1}\in V_{j}
. Again, as
\chi_{\ell}
contains no lexical
K_{\ell}
, we conclude
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell+1}
cannot induce a lexical clique.

Finally,
\chi
contains no rainbow
K_{r}
. Indeed,
if
a,b\in V_{i}
and
c\in V_{j}
for
i\neq j
then
\chi(ac)=\chi(bc)
. The only two possibilities for a rainbow clique in
\chi
are 1) if all vertices belong to same set
V_{i}
for some
i
, or 2) if they all belong to different sets. As
\chi_{3}
and
\chi_{\ell}
does not contain rainbow clique of order
r
, we conclude
\chi
does not contain rainbow clique of order
r
as well.

\blacksquare

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper we obtained upper bounds for the numbers
\text{CR}(o,r)
and
\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)
. We obtained the correct asymptotics for
\text{CR}(o,r)=\Theta(r^{3}/\log r)^{o-2}
,
\text{ER}(m,3,r)=\Theta(r^{3}/\log r)
and
\text{ER}(3,\ell,r)=\Theta(r^{3}/\log r)^{\ell-2}
. However, there is still a gap between the known lower bounds and our upper bounds for
\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)
for general
m
and
\ell
. We expect the known lower bounds to describe the correct asymptotic in this general case as well.

Conjecture 5.1
.

For every
m,\ell\geq 4
, there exists a constant
c=c(m,\ell)>0
such that, for every
r\geq 3
,

\text{ER}(m,\ell,r)\leq c\cdot\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{\ell-2}.

The smallest case where we do not obtain the correct asymptotic and potential easiest case to deal with is when we forbid monochromatic and lexical
K_{4}
.

Conjecture 5.2
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that, for every
r\geq 3
,

\text{ER}(4,4,r)\leq c\cdot\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{2}.

Furthermore, when we only forbid a lexical
K_{4}
, we can show that
\text{ER}(m,4,r)=O(r^{m+3+o(1)})
by a proof analogous to the one for
\text{ER}(4,4,r)\leq c\cdot r^{7}/(\log r)^{2}
. But we expect the correct behavior to be a much lower order of magnitude in the exponent of
r
.

Conjecture 5.3
.

For every
m\geq 4
, there exists a constant
c=c(m)>0
such that, for every
r\geq 3
,

\text{ER}(m,4,r)\leq c\cdot\left(\frac{r^{3}}{\log r}\right)^{2}.

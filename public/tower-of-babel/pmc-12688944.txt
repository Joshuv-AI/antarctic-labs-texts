Integrating Orthognathic Surgery Into Lip Lift for Subnasal Aesthetics
Authors: Yoshitsugu Hattori, Hirokazu Uda, Makoto Omori, Takanobu Mashiko, Yasushi Sugawara
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Summary:Lip lift is a versatile procedure to shorten the upper lip and improve the morphology and proportion of the lip. It is commonly performed as part of facial rejuvenation in Western patients, whereas in Asian countries, younger people tend to desire a lip lift because a shorter upper lip is considered more attractive. The lip lift carries a risk of an unfavorable aesthetic outcome when underlying skeletal deformities, such as maxillary protrusion and vertical excess, are not addressed, resulting in an undesirable degree of dental exposure. This article described our experience of integrating orthognathic surgery into subnasal lip lift surgery to harmonize the facial profile, highlighting the methods to achieve an ideal dental show with lip lift. Orthognathic surgery was first performed to correct the skeletal deformity. Subnasal lip lift with bull’s horn-type skin excision and orbicularis oris muscle resection was then performed as a staged procedure. The degree of Cupid’s bow elevation was determined by the amount of skin excised, and incisor show was determined by the amount of muscle resected. Seventeen patients underwent this combination approach, and the upper lip was successfully shortened with appropriate proportions and dental show. Overall, the aesthetic results were pleasing, with high patient satisfaction. Integrating orthognathic surgery into a lip lift aids in the enhancement of perioral aesthetics. Preexisting skeletal deformities need to be diagnosed before the lip lift and should be corrected when indicated.

Takeaways

Question: Could lip lift carry the risk of an unfavorable aesthetic outcome when underlying skeletal deformities are not addressed, resulting in an undesirable degree of dental exposure?

Findings: Integrating orthognathic surgery into a lip lift helps to achieve an ideal upper lip morphology, length, and proportion, in addition to a favorable dental exposure.

Meaning: Integrating orthognathic surgery into lip lift is a viable option to enhance perioral aesthetics and harmonize facial profile, and preexisting skeletal deformities need to be diagnosed before the lip lift and should be addressed, when indicated.

INTRODUCTION

The concept of beauty is influenced by ethnicity, traditions, and trends.1 In Western countries, lip lift is commonly performed for facial rejuvenation, whereas in Asian countries, younger people recently seek lip lift because shorter upper lip is considered attractive.2,3 In some Asian countries, lip lift has facial physiognomic meaning: A well-demarcated symmetrical philtrum and an everted upper vermilion indicate that a person has good character and will have a long, rich life.4 Thus, there is growing demand among young Asian patients.4

Subnasal lip lift is a versatile procedure to shorten the upper lip and improve the morphology and proportion of the lip. However, it could cause suboptimal results with excessive dental exposure when underlying maxilla and upper incisors are ignored, deteriorating the perioral aesthetics.5 Because orthognathic surgery can effectively address skeletal disharmony and control the dental show,6 combining orthognathic surgery involving maxillary osteotomy with lip lift could enhance perioral aesthetics. This article describes our experience of integrating orthognathic surgery into lip lift to harmonize the facial profile, highlighting the methods to achieve an ideal dental show with lip lift.

This descriptive and retrospective study included all patients who underwent lip lift surgery after orthognathic surgery between July 2018 and February 2022 at our clinic. Skeletal deformities were diagnosed and treated with orthognathic surgery before lip lift, and the lip lift was performed as a secondary procedure. The principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki were followed.

Lip lift surgery was performed under intravenous sedation or general anesthesia. A bull’s horn–type skin incision was marked. (See Video [online], which shows the surgical procedures of subnasal lip lift to adjust the incisor show.) The cephalic incisional line was placed just below the nostril sill. The amount of skin excision at the philtrum corresponded to the amount of desired Cupid’s bow elevation, and laterally, the skin was excised 1 or 2 mm more for a favorable vermilion border shape. The lateral edge of the design did not exceed the nasolabial fold.

Lidocaine with epinephrine solution (1:100,000) was injected to achieve sufficient vasoconstriction in the skin and muscle. The skin incision was placed, and dissection was performed over the muscle layer. After removing the skin and adipose tissue, the cephalic aspect of the orbicularis oris muscle was dissected, and the muscle flap was elevated, preserving the branches of the infraorbital nerve. The orbicularis oris muscle was then excised, in reference to the elevation of the lower margin of upper lip. The amount of muscle resected was approximately twice as much as that of the skin, but practically, determined based on the intraoperative assessment of incisor show.

At the lateral edge, the insertion of the levator labii superioris muscle was identified, and the orbicularis oris muscle was sutured to the levator labii superioris using 4-0 interrupted Vicryl sutures. At the middle part, the orbicularis oris muscle was sutured to the depressor septi nasalis muscle, and then, incisor show and the shape of the lower margin of the upper lip were evaluated. Additional 4-0 interrupted Vicryl sutures were placed to reconstruct the orbicularis oris muscle, with the caudal muscle flap anchored posteriorly to prevent nasal deformity and facilitate tension-free closure of the skin. After meticulous hemostasis and dermal sutures with absorbable 5-0 polydioxanone, a continuous suture with 7-0 Prolene was applied to close the wound.

Seventeen patients (16 women and 1 man) were eligible, and they had a mean age (±SD) of 28.5 ± 5.1 years (range, 16–42 y) at the time of lip lift. Before the lip lift, all patients had undergone orthognathic surgery. (See table, Supplemental Digital Content 1, which provides details of orthognathic surgical procedures (n = 17), https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E442.) All patients completed the 2-stage procedures. Lip lift was performed 12.4 ± 10.2 months after orthognathic surgery, and patients were followed up for 20.1 ± 16.3 months after the lip lift. No major postoperative complications were observed, except for minor asymmetry necessitating a minor revision. No patients experienced postoperative functional impairment except for mild numbness at the upper lip. All the patients were satisfied with the aesthetic outcome.

DISCUSSION

Lip lift is a versatile procedure to shorten the upper lip and address its proportion, and is used for making attractive upper lip morphology, facial rejuvenation, and upper lip feminization.7,8 However, there is a limitation in the correction of unbalanced upper lip region with only soft-tissue management, and orthognathic surgery should be integrated into the lip lift planning, when indicated, to avoid an unfavorable aesthetic outcome.9 In this study, patients with preexisting skeletal deformity who desired to have a shorter upper lip were suggested to receive orthognathic surgery, and the lip lift was then performed as a secondary procedure following orthognathic surgery. To ensure precise intraoperative evaluation and minimize the risk of wound infection, we opted to perform the lip lift procedure in a staged manner for all patients.3 Basically, lip lift procedures were performed after postoperative swelling had resolved, typically with an interval of more than 6 months after orthognathic surgery. The planning of orthognathic surgery was made with the premise that a secondary lip lift procedure would be performed postoperatively. By addressing both hard and soft tissues, we aimed to achieve a synergistic effect, thereby optimizing postoperative outcomes.

Incisor show is an integral indicator in evaluating perioral aesthetics. Although various lip lift techniques have been described in the literature, reports on incisor show management in subnasal lip lift are sparce.5,10 The ideal incisor show is reported to be 2–3 mm.6 In our lip lift technique, the amount of Cupid’s bow elevation was controlled by the amount of skin excised, and incisor show was adjusted proportionally to the amount of muscle resected (Fig. 1). The dental show was controlled with muscle removal, because skin-only lip lift procedures might not offer an ideal philtral–labial ratio, especially in younger patients. In addition, muscle resection and repair facilitates tension-free closure of the skin and helps to avoid conspicuous scars. Restoration of the upper lip volume could be required in lip rejuvenation for Western patients,2 but young Asian patients tend to have a thicker lip,3 and thus, muscle resection helps to obtain favorable upper lip morphology.

Orthognathic surgery is relatively invasive but remains valuable for correcting skeletal abnormalities, with application often based on patient preference. After skeletal correction, lip lift is performed, considering facial morphology, lip length and proportion, dental show, and preferences. Incorporating patients’ requests into the surgical planning increases their satisfaction. Recently, young Asian patients wish to have a shorter midface, and vertical reduction of the maxilla is often desired for aesthetic reason (Fig. 2). Because the incisor show can be controlled with lip lift, maxillary impaction can be performed even in patients with inadequate dental exposure.

Orthognathic surgery was performed to correct the long face, and the incisor show became −2 mm (B). Subnasal lip lift was then performed, and an ideal incisor show was obtained (2 mm) (C).

Although fundamental concepts for enhancing perioral aesthetics apply across ethnicities, this study included only Asian patients, and optimal techniques may vary by ethnicity. Each race has distinct features, and cosmetic procedures aim to optimize aesthetics within them.1

The limitations of this study include small sample size, incorporation of various orthognathic surgical procedures, and the lack of quantitative evaluation of the upper lip morphology. Patient satisfaction was not investigated. Therefore, further studies are warranted.

Integrating orthognathic surgery into lip lift is a viable option to enhance perioral aesthetics and harmonize facial profile. Preexisting skeletal deformities need to be diagnosed before the lip lift and should be addressed when indicated.

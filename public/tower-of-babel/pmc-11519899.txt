A real-world pharmacovigilance study of FDA adverse event reporting system events for Lutetium-177-PSMA-617
Authors: Shangrong Wu, Wenjin Hu, Mingzhe Chen, Xiong Xiao, Ranlu Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Lutetium-177(177Lu)–PSMA-617 has been approved for the treatment of metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC). Our main objective is to elucidate the association between 177Lu-PSMA-617 and reported adverse events (AEs) in the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database. Relevant information regarding 177Lu-PSMA-617 usage and reports of AEs was extracted from the FAERS database. The Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean (EBGM), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), and Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) with their 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated using four algorithms of disproportionality analysis. The pharmacovigilance signals deemed significant across the four algorithms were considered associated with drug use. We collected 6266 unique reports related to 177Lu-PSMA-617 usage from the FAERS database. Compared to other drugs, 177Lu-PSMA-617 usage was associated with a higher risk of anemia, platelet count decreased, and pancytopenia. In addition to hematologic AEs, consistent high signals were observed for AEs such as dry mouth, laboratory test abnormal, and general physical health deterioration. The analysis based on mCRPC treatment drugs showed that full blood count decreased, general physical health deterioration, and laboratory test abnormal continued to exhibit significant signals. Furthermore, the number of AEs reports for 177Lu-PSMA-617 decreased over time, with most reports occurring within one month after drug administration. Our study compiled AEs associated with 177Lu-PSMA-617 in real-world drug usage and highlighted its propensity for specific AEs in the context of mCRPC treatment. These findings will contribute to enhancing our clinical experience with this medication.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77889-3.

Introduction

The incidence of prostate cancer (PCa) has been increasing year by year, far surpassing lung cancer to become the leading cancer among males worldwide. Additionally, its mortality rate ranks second among males, only behind lung cancer1. The majority of advanced PCa receive systemic therapy primarily based on androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) because prostate adenocarcinomas are sensitive to androgens2, making treatment targeting androgen-related pathways particularly crucial. However, during treatment, tumors continue to progress, and the effective duration of ADT is limited. Eventually, PCa progresses to castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC), becoming insensitive to ADT3. Therefore, the treatment of CRPC is a global challenge. When the available treatment options are limited, breakthroughs have been made in radioligand therapy (RLT).

Lutetium-177(177Lu)-PSMA-617 is a drug that combines a radioactive isotope with a prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeting agent4–7. It can specifically bind to PCa cells expressing PSMA and release β particle emissions to damage tumors. Therefore, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved it for the treatment of metastatic CRPC (mCRPC)8. However, given the high all-cause mortality rate in mCRPC, the safety of treatment regimens must be ensured. Prior to the approval of 177Lu-PSMA-617, the VISION clinical trial9 reported some relevant adverse events(AEs). Nevertheless, clinical trials are limited by inclusion criteria and specific control drugs, which may not fully reflect the safety profile of 177Lu-PSMA-617. Therefore, more real-world reports are needed to further evaluate the safety of this drug.

The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System(FAERS) stands as one of the largest pharmacovigilance databases globally10, utilized for evaluating potential associations between drugs and AEs11. Our aim is to utilize this database to compare 177Lu-PSMA-617 with a broader range of drugs in a larger population, further clarifying its safety profile.

Methods

Data source and study design

The data used in this study are sourced from the FAERS public database. FAERS is an openly accessible database updated quarterly, containing various reports of AEs and drug-related information provided by populations such as drug manufacturers, healthcare professionals, and consumers12. All downloadable files in FAERS include a unique “primary ID”, with each primary ID representing a single patient. Apart from patient-related information (such as age, gender, country, etc.), data regarding drug-related information and AEs reports are listed per patient rather than per drug. Hence, we confined our study scope to patients who have used 177Lu-PSMA-617.

Since 177Lu-PSMA-617 was approved by the FDA for clinical use on March 23, 2022, we extracted data from FAERS covering the period from Q1 2022 to Q2 2024. The data were downloaded on September 2, 2024. All drug names can be obtained from https://go.drugbank.com/. We used the drug name “LUTETIUM LU-177 VIPIVOTIDE TETRAXETAN” and the brand name “PLUVICTO” as key fields for filtering the data. The data files include the “P.T.” field or the “Preferred Term” level terminology from the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA). Therefore, after extracting the data, we standardized it using terminologies from “MedDRA (version 26.1)” and then proceeded with data deduplication.

According to FDA’s recommendations, we selected the latest FDA_DT when the PRIMARYIDs were the same, and chose the higher PRIMARYID when the FDA_DT and the CASEID were the same, to remove duplicate reports submitted by various individuals and institutions. Drugs reported in the FAERS database were classified into four categories: PS (primary suspect), SS (secondary suspect), C (concomitant), and I (interacting). We retained reports where 177Lu-PSMA-617 identified as the PS. In the database’s “outc” (outcome) file, a single patient may report multiple outcomes of varying severity. We only selected the most severe outcome reported for analysis. We further refined the onset time by excluding incorrect records, such as those where the drug use date was after the AEs occurrence date or prior to the approval date of 177Lu-PSMA-617, calculating the difference between the events occurrence time and the drug administration time.

Additionally, given that 177Lu-PSMA-617 is used for treating advanced PCa, some signals may be more closely related to the disease itself. To minimize indication bias13 due to tumor progression, we extracted data on most antitumor drugs used in mCRPC treatment for further analysis.

Disproportionality analysis and statistical analysis

We utilized the established FAERS data mining techniques for conducting disproportionality analysis14–16. The Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean (EBGM), and Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) are utilized to demonstrate and identify signals of increased risk of AEs associated with 177Lu-PSMA-617 usage. ROR and PRR represent the ratio between the observed reporting rate of AEs and the expected reporting rate. Higher ROR and PRR values indicate a stronger association between the drug and specific AEs17,18. EBGM is calculated using a Bayesian method based on EBGM, and its estimate is more stable compared to ROR, leading to a reduction in the number of potential false-positive safety signals19. BCPNN employs Bayesian principles to estimate the probability of a signal between a drug and an AE. The calculated Information Component (IC) reflects the strength of the association20. The parameters required for calculating signal values are detailed in Table 1, and the formulas are provided in Table 2. In our study, if the signal values of ROR, PRR, and EBGM are > 1, the lower limit of the 95% confidence interval(CI) is > 1, and the lower limit of the 95% two-sided CI of the IC(IC025) > 0, it is considered a significant signal.

Results

General characteristics

From Q1 2022 to Q2 2024, the FAERS database contained a total of 4,304,335 reports of AEs, which were reduced to 3,691,524 after deduplication. Among them, we identified 6266 unique reports for 177Lu-PSMA-617. As shown in Table 3, in terms of general information, and the number of reports increased annually and quarterly, with the primary source of reports being the Americas (6047, 96.50%). In the known outcome reports, some patients experienced severe outcomes, including Death (981, 15.66%), Life-threatening (18, 0.29%), and Hospitalization (239, 3.81%). From the report sources, 5,976 healthcare professionals submitted 95.37% of the AE reports, while 289 consumers provided 4.61% of the AE reports(Table 3).

At the Preferred Term (PT) level, there are numerous signal reports for 177Lu-PSMA-617, and we have selected reports with significant signals calculated by four algorithms for presentation in Table 5. Onset time trends of AEs

Among the AEs reports extracted from our data, 319 cases provided accurate information on onset time. Our analysis revealed a median onset time of 27 day [interquartile range (IQR): 4–61 days], with 67 cases (21.0%) reporting an onset time of 1 day. The majority of reports (n = 171, 53.6%) had an onset time ≤ 30 days (Fig. 1). As the duration of medication increased, the number of reported cases gradually decreased.

Discussion

This study is, as far as we know, the first to report AEs associated with 177Lu-PSMA-617 using the FAERS database. Our study analyzed the files from the FAERS database from Q1 2022 to the Q2 2024 and identified 6266 reports of AEs related to 177Lu-PSMA-617. Currently, adverse reaction data for 177Lu-PSMA-617 primarily comes from the VISION trial23 and the European Union Summary of Product Characteristics(EUSmPC)(https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/pluvicto). Therefore, the aim of this study is to provide safety guidance for the clinical use of the drug by identifying potential AEs associated with 177Lu-PSMA-617 in real-world use.

Our primary focus was on severe outcomes reported in the data. Among the confirmed outcomes (exclude unknown outcomes), death (981/1613, 60.82%) was the most frequently reported (Table 3). Considering that 177Lu-PSMA-617 is indicated for high mortality mCRPC, we attribute the primary cause of death to disease progression rather than medication safety. Some of the significant signals reported align with those highlighted in the VISION trial9. In the VISION trial, AEs with a high incidence (> 20%) included anemia, dry mouth, fatigue, constipation, decreased appetite, and nausea. Additionally, laboratory findings such as decreased lymphocytes, decreased hemoglobin, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and abnormal indicators due to kidney function impairment were also commonly observed. In other clinical trials of 177Lu-PSMA-61724,25, blood cell abnormalities such as anemia, thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia are also commonly reported.

The tumor burden of mCRPC in the skeletal system is typically high26. 177Lu-PSMA-617 targets prostate tumors with high expression of PSMA, and the specific range of radiation it emits during mCRPC treatment may also irradiate the bone marrow. The bone marrow, salivary glands, and kidneys are considered dose-limiting organs for therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals27,28, suggesting that 177Lu-PSMA-617 may potentially induce bone marrow suppression. Besides, in the post-hoc analysis of the VISION trial29, whether patients were using bone-targeted agents or not, the use of 177Lu-PSMA-617 did not increase the incidence rate of bone-related AEs or advance the time of event occurrence. Therefore, in our report, “Anaemia”, “Full blood count decreased”, “Haemoglobin decreased”, “Pancytopenia”, “Platelet count decreased”, and “Thrombocytopenia” were also reported with a high signal, indicating that the impairment of hematopoietic function in the bone marrow affects many blood cell abnormalities. We should pay extra attention to hematological abnormalities during the use of 177Lu-PSMA-617, not only because of their relatively high incidence rate but also because hematological AEs are more often grades 3–429. It is worth mentioning that some notable AEs mentioned in relevant clinical studies and the EU SmPC, such as gastrointestinal toxicity, nervous system disorders, and urinary tract infections, were not identified as significant signals in our study. These discrepancies may be due to the high prevalence of these AEs across all drugs in the FAERS database. The large volume of AEs reports for other drugs may have diluted the specificity of the signal values for 177Lu-PSMA-617 and certain AEs15.

In routine disproportionality analysis, only the association between the target drug and observed AEs is reflected. However, some reactions may be related to the patient population or disease rather than the drug itself, leading to potential indication bias13. To better determine the association between 177Lu-PSMA-617 and AEs, we used reports of drugs for treating mCRPC, including 177Lu-PSMA-617, as background data to minimize the impact of mCRPC disease progression. After further disproportionality analysis, the results at the PT level still showed significant signals for “Full blood count decreased,” “General physical health deterioration,” and “Laboratory test abnormal,” which are consistent with the AEs emphasized in the VISION trial23 and the EU SmPC. It is worth mentioning that renal toxicity warnings highlighted in the VISION trail23 and the EU SmPC are not directly reflected in our results. We believe such AEs are represented within the category of “Laboratory test abnormal”.

Additionally, pharmacovigilance studies based on the FAERS database offer a real-world safety analysis30. These results can supplement and further validate previous clinical research and drug labeling. However, signals that differ from clinical study results should still be considered. Each signal’s significance should be evaluated based on the drug’s mechanism of action and the characteristics of its indications. For example, “Spinal cord compression”, initially identified as a significant signal, was not a significant signal in further analyses with mCRPC treatment drug data. Although this could potentially be due to drug-induced edema or inflammation of the spinal cord’s surrounding tissues, it is more likely an organic lesion occurring during tumor progression31, rather than a change induced by the drug. Similarly, “Loss of libido” may be more related to the ADT process for mCRPC, which could account for its underreporting in further analysis. Conversely, although dry mouth was not a significant signal in further analyses, radiation drugs can damage salivary glands28,32, and it was very common in previous clinical trials23, making it worthy of attention. Clinicians should provide appropriate examinations and preventive interventions based on the actual situation of patients, and this study offers additional references for clinical practice based on previous research.

Furthermore, we summarized the time intervals between drug administration and the occurrence of AEs (Fig. 1). Among these, many patients(67/319, 21.0%) reported AEs within one day of using 177Lu-PSMA-617, and more than half(171/319, 53.6%) reported AEs within one month of administration. This indicates that AEs are prone to occur shortly after the administration of 177Lu-PSMA-617, and as the duration of medication increases, the likelihood of patients continuing to experience AEs due to 177Lu-PSMA-617 is not significant. Regarding the severity of AEs to 177Lu-PSMA-617, early clinical trial reports have been inconsistent. From the perspective of the duration of drug administration, in the VISION trial9, the median duration of 177Lu-PSMA-617 administration exceeded 6 months, with most AE being grades 1–2. The safety analysis of this study23 also concluded that prolonged exposure to 177Lu-PSMA-617 does not increase the risk of toxicity. Therefore, we believe that despite the short duration, the majority of AEs are acceptable and can be managed for long-term use without significantly affecting the patients’ quality of life.

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, FAERS is a voluntary reporting system, and the presence of missing values (such as the onset time) and non-professional reports can affect the accuracy of AEs identification and attribution, thereby influencing the analysis results. Secondly, the reports in the FAERS database do not distinguish the reasons for severe outcomes; both disease progression and drug toxicity could affect the outcomes. We have endeavored to address limitations in our study design, but we must acknowledge that bias still exists. Thirdly, disproportionality analysis can only assess the statistical correlation between drug use and AEs but does not establish a causal relationship from a medical perspective. More definitive conclusions require further clinical prospective trials or database analyses based on the association between drug active ingredients and AEs.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study delved into the safety profile of 177Lu-PSMA-617 in clinical applications through analysis of the FAERS database. AEs associated with 177Lu-PSMA-617 occur shortly after administration, primarily manifesting as bone marrow suppression and resulting in various abnormalities in blood cell counts. Analysis after accounting for the impact of PCa progression suggests that the drug’s toxicity is particularly notable in terms of hematologic function, general physical condition, and laboratory tests. The results from disproportionality analysis can be considered as a validation and complement to the findings from clinical trials using real-world data, warranting further research to ascertain the safety profile of 177Lu-PSMA-617.

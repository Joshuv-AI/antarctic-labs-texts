Personalized prediction of student progress in moral education using multi modal learning
Authors: Fangzheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn modern education systems, accurate and individualized prediction of the development of students in ethics education is the central problem. Unbiased insight into the moral development path of each individual, and the provision of appropriate and timely interventions, are essential for the promotion of social well-being as well as the education of civic citizens. The present study aims to propose a hybrid method for individualized prediction of the development of students in ethics education. The proposed method consists of four key steps: (1) Preprocessing raw data to prepare data for the subsequent steps; (2) Modeling data modalities and encoding them into Auto-Encoder neural networks, where it is possible to extract meaningful representations of various aspects of data; (3) Fusion and aggregation of created modalities through a self-attention mechanism so that the model can actively choose the importance of every modality to forecast the target variable; and (4) Target variable forecasting based on a random forest by utilizing the set of fused features from previous steps. This new approach, founded on innovative machine learning techniques and data fusion, attempts to provide more accurate and reliable predictions about the path of students’ moral growth. These results prove the effectiveness of the proposed approach and show improvements in terms of accuracy by 7.1% and F-measure by 7% compared to competing methods.Keywords: Personalized prediction, Student, Moral education, Ethics education, Self-attention mechanismSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Increased interest has been observed in recent years to find out what are the most influencing factors for student’s " performance in higher education, especially by utilizing data mining methods and techniques. Research in this area is commonly known as Educational Data Mining (EDM)1. The reason behind this interest is the applicability of such studies to help decide in advance about the low performing students so that their learning difficulties can be addressed and their learning performances can be improved, eventually meeting the institutional goals of providing high-quality learning environments. Also, EDM is rapidly becoming an important research area because of its ability to generate new knowledge from vast amounts of student’s data2.

Student’s academic performance is one of the success indicators in higher learning universities. The good academic record of achievement increases the rank of the institutions as one of the indicators of a quality university. For the student, having good academic performance will increase the chances of getting jobs where good academic achievement is one of the determining factors by employers3. Nowadays, there are full-fledged, mature, and advanced technologies whereby an individual from any background, even with minimal programming experience, can forecast their future data. machine learning is now a normal technology to forecast data from grocery stores to space. Data is used by scholars and administrative personnel to predict a student’s performance upon admission, project the employment possibility for a student upon course completion or the dropout based on the combined numbers of the whole set of students, or predict an individual student’s success or failure rate in the subsequent grades4.

Student success at academics in ethics studies is a multidimensional and multifaceted phenomenon, and there are many variables influencing it. Contemporary literature in the field of forecasting academic success in this discipline is greatly reliant on the traditional models and single-dimensional data. All of these approaches lack in seamlessly combining heterogeneous information (such as attributes of individuals, cognition, personality, and learning environments), retaining the intricate and nonlinear relationships among these factors, and lastly, producing personalized and accurate predictions. Hence, a comprehensive and advanced framework of multimodal learning that can overcome this shortfall is acutely needed. This research seeks to supply an answer to this requirement and makes a solid step towards doing this by creating a smart model that, based on sophisticated artificial intelligence techniques, can analyze various data simultaneously and holistically and make individual predictions of student’s ethical attainment.

This research has several innovations: The first innovation is to provide a dedicated model to predict the individual progress of students in this area, which, beyond general assessments, examines and analyzes the path of moral development of each student separately, which is itself an innovation. The second innovation is to use a multi-modal approach in analysis of moral progression, using diverse data (including student-centered characteristics and educational and environmental intervention characteristics) in order to more comprehensively understand the factors affecting student progress. The third innovation is to use dedicated AE networks to encode and learn compact and meaningful representations from each modality of the data. The goal of these models is to reduce noise and extract appropriate features that help to better and more accurately understand the data. Additionally, this research suggests a self-attention mechanism for intelligent and dynamic fusion of representations extracted from different modalities. This mechanism allows determining the relative importance of each modality in the final prediction and ultimately provides more accurate predictions. Literature review and theoretical foundations

This part contextualizes the present study in the context of educational data mining. We will start by reviewing the literature available on moral education tracking, and then discuss the theoretical basis of multi-modal fusion and the reasons why we selected the predictive algorithms.

Related work

This section reviews research conducted in previous years. Tahira et al.5 used the Defining Issues Test to assess the moral judgment development of secondary school teachers and students in Dera Ghazi Khan. The study indicated that the people who took part scored higher on developing their conventional moral judgment. People in the area tended to follow cultural norms and criteria for social approbation, which led to this finding.

Bhutto et al.6 proposed a method that uses supervised machine learning methods, specifically sequential minimum optimization, to predict student’s academic achievement. The methodology was meant to assist schools find important things like how well teachers are doing and how motivated students are. The goal of utilizing this technique was to lower the number of students that drop out.

Ghorbani and Ghousi7 explored the techniques of resampling to address imbalanced datasets within the context of application in multiclass as well as binary classification formats. Model assessment was performed by using machine learning classifiers including random forest, k-nearest-neighbor, artificial neural network, XG-boost, support vector machine, decision tree, logistic regression, and naïve bayes.

Gull et al.8 used machine learning to improve learning tactics and look into other ways of teaching. Researchers used linear discrimination analysis on old grades from an undergraduate course to guess 49 recordings with 90.74% accuracy.

Alvarez et al.9 suggested adding automated content analysis features to EthicApp’s teaching interface to make it better. The goal of this improvement was to let students look at ethics cases on their own and in groups. We used a set of 4,366 comments from 520 students to test the suggested improvements.

Ojajuni et al.10 examined the use of machine learning in predicting student academic performance with an emphasis on the Extreme Gradient Boosting approach. Their findings suggested that ML could identify knowledge gaps and pinpoint struggling students early.

Dabhade et al.11 utilized educational data mining to predict student’s performance at a technical Indian university. Data were gathered from a questionnaire survey and the study section. Python 3 was used to compare various machine learning algorithms.

Zeineddine et al.12 proposed Automated machine learning for the early prediction of student performance using data before an academic program with higher accuracy. The Automated machine learning was intended to enable early intervention at colleges and universities facing budget cuts and rises in operational costs.

Liu et al.13 proposed a deep learning-based moral education evaluation method for college students. The goal of this approach was to improve ideological and political education by using both internal and external methods to deal with problems like narrow goals, boring structures, lack of relevance, and subjective standards.

Liu et al.14 used clickstream data of 5341 students to predict student performance, identify at-risk students, and improve instruction. LSTM yielded a better performance than the conventional machine learning methods with an accuracy of 90.25%.

Yağcı15 presented a machine learning model that was employed to predict grades of 1854 Turkish Language-I students in the final exam with an accuracy of classification of 70–75%. The model underlined the significance of data-driven studies in universities and the early detection of high-risk students.

Hussain et al.16 assessed the quality and sustainability objectives of education based on Board of Intermediate & Secondary Education (BISEP) data in Peshawar. With pre-processing, model training of the regression model, and using decision tree-classifier, the result showed that machine learning technology predicted student performance with accuracy.

Chen and Zhai17 studied the application of machine learning to predict student performance. In their study, they indicated that random forest was the most generalizable, although decision tree and Artificial Neural Network models were also viable solutions to education data issues.

Chen and Ding18 used big data to predict academic performance in Pennsylvania schools, achieving approximately 60 percent accuracy with their machine learning models. Their analysis suggested that higher levels of education in smaller counties with lower crime rates could improve academic performance, guiding future education policymakers.

Abuzinadah et al.19 suggested an AI-enabled system to foresee the performance of students on the basis of educational data mining through enhanced precision with deep convoluted features. The 99.9% classification accuracy through the extra tree classifier transcended the drawbacks of low accuracy and feature engineering issues, rendering it a quality instrument to enhance student learning.

Beckham et al.20 applied various models like Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), decision tree, and random forest to predict student performance. The most effective was the MLP 12-Neurons model with an root mean square error score of 4.32, followed by random forest and Decision Tree with 4.52 and 5.69, respectively.

Pallathadka et al.21 found that educational data mining applications utilize student performance and teacher performance data for enhancing the quality of instruction and teacher effectiveness. Machine learning algorithms such as Naive Bayes, ID3, C4.5, and SVM were investigated in terms of their accuracy.

Lau et al.22 used artificial neural networks to predict the Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of undergraduate students considering socio-economic status and entrance exam results. There was an improvement in annual CGPA with female students having higher performances and English exam scores being highly correlated, as per their findings.

Kuadey et al.23 investigated the influence of technostress generators on student’s burnout and academic performance in Higher Educational Institutions of Ghana. Their results revealed that techno-overload, uncertainty, invasion, complexity, and insecurity were predictors of burnout.

Shou et al.24 suggested a student performance prediction model through multidimensional data analysis, in an effort to help teachers identify students who are at risk early on. Their model was 74% accurate and had an F1 of 73% on the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset.

Li25 described a fuzzy decision support system model for forecasting student achievement utilizing multidimensional data. Compared to other machine learning methods, the model had a high level of accuracy and a fair range of errors. The study also talked about ways to optimize and improve the model.

Balachandar and Venkatesh26 described the Multi-dimensional Student Performance Prediction Model (MSPP) as a revolutionary educational tool. The model uses advanced data preprocessing and deep learning methods to make predictions about how well students will do more accurate, precise, and with a lower false positive rate. As Table 1 summarizes, although much has been done in predicting overall academic performance, there is a gap in research in the specialized field of moral education. The existing literature has three main shortcomings: First, the majority of the models are based on either student-centered traits or environmental factors, and they do not capture the interaction between the two. Second, the available literature usually forecasts an ultimate grade or at-risk status, as opposed to quantifying the qualitative improvement (delta) of an initial ethical state; and third, most traditional multi-dimensional models simply concatenate, disregarding the fact that different data sources have different weights to different individuals.

The proposed research advances the state-of-the-art by proposing a hybrid multimodal framework that is specifically aimed at moral education. In contrast to13 or26, we employ Auto-Encoders to learn the representation of the modality specific to the task and a self-attention mechanism to learn dynamic and personalized fusion. This enables the model to be able to pay attention to the most pertinent aspects to the progress path of each student. Moreover, the target variable of a multi-category percentage change (negative regression to high progress) gives a more detailed and developmental view of student success than the binary or grade-based outcomes of14 and20.

Multi-modal representation and fusion theory

The development of a student in a complicated learning setting can hardly be attributed to one factor. Multi-modal learning offers a model of combining heterogeneous data (including internal psychological characteristics and external instructional variables) into one coherent predictive model. Representation learning with Auto-Encoders (AE) is used to learn salient, lower-dimensional features in order to deal with the inherent noise and high dimensionality of survey-based educational data.

Recent developments in contextual attention based multimodal fusion27 indicate that the simple concatenation approach to integration (which is a form of static integration) may not be able to reflect the subtle interaction between streams of data. Our model is able to dynamically balance the significance of individual and environmental modalities by using a self-attention mechanism. This is in line with modern intelligent tutoring system trends28 and interpretable causality discovery29 where attention layers are not only used to enhance accuracy but also to offer a way of comprehending the inputs that contribute to the final prediction in noisy multivariate contexts.

Random forest for educational analytics

Although the initial steps of our pipeline are based on the neural networks to extract the features, the ultimate classification is done with the help of a Random Forest (RF) algorithm. This decision is theoretically based on three major benefits. First, RF is an ensemble technique that is highly resistant to overfitting, which is highly important due to our sample size of N = 450. Second, RF is effective at capturing high-order non-linear interactions between modalities without hyperparameter tuning. Lastly, interpretability is the most important in the ethics education context; unlike black-box deep learning classifiers, RF can be used to compute feature importance, which educators can use to gain clear understanding of the main factors behind student moral development.

Research methodology

The data necessary for predicting student progress in ethics education was collected through questionnaires and information provided by the educational environment. To ensure transparency in the data collection process, this section first describes the data organization, followed by the proposed multi-modal approach for predicting student progress in ethics education.

Data collection

This section details the data collection process, characteristics of the statistical sample, data aspects used, and the definition of the target variable. The primary goal of this data collection is to create a rich, multi-modal dataset for building and evaluating a model that personalizes predictions of student progress in ethics education.

The data for this research were gathered during the 2020–2021 academic year from tenth to twelfth-grade students in nine selected high schools in Tehran. Overall, the study used information from 460 students who participated in their schools’ ethics education programs. The data collection process occurred in two main phases. The first phase was conducted at the beginning of the educational period. In this stage, data related to student’s individual, demographic, psychological, and initial attitudinal characteristics were collected via paper questionnaires. Simultaneously, information about the educational model, lesson plan, and other environmental and interventional features related to each class/school’s ethics education program was gathered from educational administrators, reviews of educational documents, and direct observation by the research team. These environmental data were then assigned to each student participating in that specific educational program. The second phase, after completing the ethics education course (on average, after six months), again used paper questionnaires to assess indicators related to student’s ethical status (moral reasoning, ethical knowledge, and ethical attitudes) to calculate the amount of progress. The data obtained are divided rigidly into Predictor Modalities (as inputs to the model) and Outcome Measures (as inputs to construct target variables only).

The study protocol was performed in compliance with the ethical principles of conducting research involving human subjects as provided in the Declaration of Helsinki. The Research and Technology Council of the Tehran Department of Education (the body that oversees research in the schools that participated in the study) reviewed the study design and data collection procedures under the protocol of minimal-risk educational assessment.

The dual-layered consent process was observed with the written informed consent of the parents or legal guardians of all the participating students and the second was the student assent, which was obtained after a clear explanation to the participants of the voluntary nature of the study. All the data were subjected to a rigorous pseudonymization procedure in order to safeguard the privacy of the minors. A data coordinator who did not participate in the machine learning modeling substituted real names and school IDs with unique alpha numerical codes. No information was provided to teachers or school administrators. The data on the multi-modal learning model is completely anonymized and limited to the research team to guarantee the long-term confidentiality of the participants. Proposed method

This section details the proposed framework for personalized prediction of student progress in ethics education based on the data organized in Sects. “Research methodology” and “Introduction”. The suggested framework is a hybrid architecture that is a synergistic combination of unsupervised deep learning and supervised ensemble techniques. In particular, the hybrid character of the model is explained by the fact that unsupervised representation learning (with special Auto-Encoders to obtain latent features of different data modalities) is combined with a supervised classification strategy based on a Random Forest ensemble. The fusion of dynamic self-attention mechanism bridges these two different paradigms by weighing the significance of each learned representation. This combination enables the model to leverage the high-dimensional feature extraction of neural networks and retain the robust and interpretable decision-making of tree-based ensembles. The processes related to evaluating and interpreting the target variable are shown on the right side of Fig. 1 and are described in detail in Sect.“Data collection”. The mechanism for modeling the target variable and predicting it within the proposed framework begins with the preprocessing step. In this step, data transformation processes, missing value management, and record merging are used to achieve a data structure appropriate for the prediction model. In the second step, a multi-modal analysis approach is used to encode different data aspects. Multi-modal analysis allows the simultaneous integration and examination of information from diverse data sources—namely, student-centric features and features related to educational intervention and the learning environment—and is, therefore, used in the proposed method. This integrative approach considers the various dimensions influencing the ethical growth process and allows the model to identify more complex patterns. As a result, the model’s ability to provide more accurate and reliable predictions about each student’s progress path will be improved.

Using multi-modal data increases the information richness of the model and greatly helps in understanding the interactions between individual and contextual features. The proposed method uses dedicated AE to encode each aspect. Since the raw data related to each piece of information has noise and complex non-linear internal structures, AE can act as a powerful tool in learning representations of the data aspects. These types of neural networks are trained through an unsupervised process based on the goal of reconstructing the input, so that compressed, meaningful, and hidden representations of the data can be extracted in the middle layers of the network. These representations, while removing noise and reducing data dimensions, provide more appropriate inputs for the third step. After extracting meaningful representations of the aspects by AE models, how to effectively combine these encodings to reach a unified view is of great importance, which is done in the third step of the proposed method using a self-attention mechanism. Through this mechanism, the final model can dynamically determine the amount of importance or attention to different parts of the input representations (aspects) for each data sample separately. Based on this ability, the prediction model can provide more accurate and personalized predictions.

In the last step of the proposed method, a random forest classifier is used to predict the target variable. Characteristics such as a high ability to model non-linear and complex relationships between features and the target variable; resistance to overfitting; the ability to evaluate the importance of features; simplicity of structure; and, finally, the inherent interpretability of the model led to the selection of this classification approach in the proposed method.

Preprocessing

The data preprocessing stage is the first step in the proposed method and is used to prepare data records for processing in subsequent stages. Data preprocessing is performed through the following two steps:

Converting Nominal Features to Numerical: The nominal features of the database fall into two categories: discrete and ordinal. To convert each of these features, a list of all nominal values of that feature is first extracted. For discrete nominal features, the resulting list is sorted in ascending order based on the frequency of each value. In contrast, each value in the list of values for ordinal nominal features is sorted in ascending order based on rank. After sorting the list of unique values, each nominal value is replaced with its position number in the sorted list of values. In the sorted list obtained, each value is replaced with the location number corresponding to that value. By performing this process for all nominal features, each data record will be converted into a numerical vector.

Progress prediction by random forest

In the last step of the proposed method, the final fused representation vector , obtained through the self-attention layer, is used to feed a random forest-based classifier. This classification model receives descriptions as input and the variable C, which represents the student’s progress level, as the target variable. It attempts to predict the student’s progress level in unseen samples by learning the communication pattern between these two categories of variables. Random forest is an aggregated learning model based on the decision tree structure, in which each decision tree model is trained based on a subset of data or a subset of features. The training and construction process of each tree can be independent of the other trees in the forest. The random forest model used in the proposed method includes 20 decision tree components. Each decision tree in this aggregate system is of the decision and regression (Classification and Regression Tree (CART) type, which is constructed based on the Gini impurity index criterion. Each model is trained based on a random subset of data samples. The process of constructing CART trees as well as how to form the basic random forest model are described in detail in32; therefore, its repetition is ignored. After training the random forest model, a majority voting strategy is used to determine the final output of the model. It should be noted that during the configuration of the random forest model, the maximum depth parameter is set to 7.

Results and discussion

The proposed framework was implemented and evaluated using MATLAB R2020a. A stratified tenfold cross-validation process was used to guarantee that the evaluation of the model was statistically robust and free of bias. In each of the 10 folds, the dataset was divided into three strictly independent subsets, 80 percent of which were used to train the model (Train), 10 percent to tune the hyperparameters and select the model (Validation), and 10 percent to evaluate the final performance (Test).

In order to remove the possibility of data leakage, the unsupervised representation learning (Auto-Encoders) and the dynamic fusion mechanism (Self-Attention) were trained with the training subset of each fold only. The validation subset was only used to tune the hyperparameters, including the learning rate and the number of training epochs, so that the model was not exposed to the test data at any point of the training or tuning process. The held-out test sets were used to compute final performance measures. Any results given in this paper are the average value plus/minus the standard deviation (Mean ± SD) of the 10 folds to show the stability and generalizability of the model. Conclusion

This research suggested a novel hybrid approach to personalized prediction of student performance in ethics education that addresses the significant issue of contemporary schooling systems. Through a creative integration of multi-aspect information—starting from individual student, educational intervention, and environmental aspects—through AE neural networks and a self-attention mechanism, the proposed approach offered a detailed and multi-perspective explanation of the complex dynamics affecting moral growth. Experimental results verified to notably enhance prediction accuracy with a decent 7.1% increase and improved F-measure value, achieving a 0.07-unit improvement, compared to the existing methods. These results helped to emphasize the ability of the framework to provide more accurate and customized information regarding student’s ethical development patterns. This study contributed to the field by presenting a rigorous and evidence-based approach that could be utilized to inform targeted interventions, customize education strategies, and ultimately support the formation of ethical and responsible citizens. The model not only enhanced the prediction of ethical development methodology but also provided educators and policymakers with tangible data to encourage an ethically more informed learning environment. This study has faced limitations, which are mentioned below:

The first limitation of the present research is that only a limited sample size and geographical limitation in collecting data were achieved from 9 high schools only. This could have effects on the generalizability of results to larger populations or to other geographic locations. In future work, it is necessary to develop a database with more diverse and extensive samples from different regions.

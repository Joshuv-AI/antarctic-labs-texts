The antiviral role of TRIM25 in mammalian embryonic stem cells
Authors: Jie Zou, Yuxin Lin, Chunyan Wu, Yao Ji, Xuankai Zhao, Zhan Xu, Jingfei Gong, Zhiyuan Shi, Tianyang Luo, Xiaoling Xie, Yanan Jiang, Qi Tian, Shuqi Zhang, Yanxi Ji, Yuan Li, Zhenyu He, Shuchun Zhang, Liu Cao, Chun-Mei Li, Junyu Wu, Deyin Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMammalian embryonic stem cells (ESCs), despite lacking functional interferon (IFN) signaling, are remarkably resistant to viral infection. However, the mechanistic basis of their antiviral defenses remains incompletely characterized. In this study, we investigated the antiviral functional role and molecular mechanism of tripartite motif-containing protein 25 (TRIM25), a well-known key driver of IFN-dependent innate immunity, in IFN-deficient mouse ESCs (mESCs). TRIM25 exhibited a broad spectrum of antiviral activity in mESCs. RNAi depletion or CRISPR-mediated knockout of TRIM25 markedly enhanced the replication and propagation of both encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV) and vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV). Mechanistically, the antiviral activity of TRIM25 in mESCs is independent of both IFN production and constitutive ISG expression. In virus-infected mESCs, TRIM25 translocated from the nucleus to the cytoplasm and directly bound viral RNA, where it formed cytoplasmic condensates with GTPase-activating protein-binding protein 1 (G3BP1) that colocalized with dsRNA foci. Genetic perturbation of G3BP1 similarly compromised the antiviral defenses of mESCs, revealing an essential synergism between TRIM25 and stress granule components in restricting viral replication. Our study identifies TRIM25 as a pivotal RNA-sensing effector in ESCs, delineating a previously unrecognized IFN-independent axis of intrinsic immunity that bridges viral RNA surveillance with stress granule-mediated suppression, thereby expanding the paradigm of pluripotent cell-autonomous antiviral strategies.Keywords: TRIM25, Embryonic stem cell, G3BP1, RNA-binding, RNA virus

Introduction

Mammalian embryonic stem cells (ESCs), derived from the preimplantation embryos, are a primitive cell population endowed with unlimited self-renewal capacity and pluripotent differentiation potential. These cells occupy a pivotal position in developmental biology and regenerative medicine due to their unique epigenetic configuration and dynamic transcriptional regulatory networks. In differentiated somatic cells, the interferon (IFN) signaling pathway serves as the cornerstone of antiviral immunity, orchestrating a broad-spectrum antiviral state through JAK-STAT signaling cascades that induce expression of hundreds of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) (Garcia-Sastre and Biron, 2006; Beachboard and Horner, 2016). In contrast, ESCs exhibit marked deficiencies in the IFN pathway with the lack expression of some key components of this pathway (Chen et al., 2010; Wang et al., 2013). In recent years, deciphering the molecular basis of antiviral strategies in ESCs has emerged as a pivotal research frontier at the intersection of virology and developmental biology.

Several IFN-independent antiviral strategies have been reported in ESCs. Mouse ESCs (mESCs) and the early mouse embryos have been found to maintain a functional antiviral RNA interference (RNAi) machinery, which mainly restricts virus proliferation in plants and invertebrates (Maillard et al., 2013). Despite IFN non-responsiveness, ESCs constitutively express a small subset of ISG-encoded proteins to establish basal antiviral barriers (Wu et al., 2017). Notably, IFN-induced transmembrane proteins 1–3 (IFITM1–3) are highly expressed in unstimulated ESCs, which inhibit the entry of multiple enveloped viruses through membrane fluidity modulation. Vesicle-associated membrane protein 5 (VAMP5) has also been reported to be an active viral restriction factor in human ESCs (hESCs) (Dong et al., 2025). Our group recently identified a novel antiviral mechanism termed ERASE (endogenous RTase/RNase H-mediated antiviral system) in ESCs (Wu et al., 2021). Despite these advances, the full repertoire of ESC antiviral defenses remains incompletely characterized.

Tripartite motif-containing protein 25 (TRIM25) is a critical regulator of antiviral responses in somatic cells through amplifying RIG-I-mediated IFN signaling (Gack et al., 2007). It has been identified as an RNA-binding protein that interacts directly with cellular RNA in mESCs (Kwon et al., 2013). In mESCs, TRIM25 is recruited to stalled replication forks via the Filia-Floped scaffold to maintain genome stability (Zhao et al., 2018), and it mediates ubiquitination and degradation of chromatin regulators to promote chromatin accessibility for activation of the 2C-like state (Yang et al., 2024). However, whether TRIM25 contributes to antiviral defense in the IFN-deficient context of ESCs remains unknown.

In this study, we demonstrate that TRIM25 serves as a key antiviral factor in mESCs, functioning independently of the IFN pathway. We show that upon viral infection, TRIM25 translocates from the nucleus to the cytoplasm, where it directly binds viral RNA and forms condensates with G3BP1. This TRIM25-G3BP1 axis is essential for restricting viral replication, revealing a novel layer of intrinsic immunity in pluripotent stem cells.

Results

Depletion of Trim25 promotes EMCV proliferation in mESCs

Two mouse ES cell lines, D3 and E14TG2a, were used to investigate whether TRIM25 affects virus infection in ESCs. We initially depleted the Trim25 gene with two independent siRNAs (Fig. 1A and B). After depletion of Trim25, D3 and E14TG2a cells were challenged with encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV), a positive single-stranded RNA non-enveloped virus belonging to the Picornaviridae family. At 24 hours post infection (hpi), intracellular viral loads were analyzed by using RT-qPCR and Western blotting. As shown in Fig. 1A and B, siRNA-mediated knockdown of Trim25 significantly elevated the viral RNA level of EMCV. The protein level of viral structural protein VP1 was also apparently increased in Trim25-depleted mESCs compared to negative controls (Fig. 1C and D). The immunofluorescence analysis showed that more mESCs were infected by EMCV after Trim25 depletion (Fig. 1E and F). Furthermore, the cell culture medium at 48 hpi was collected and the number of the released virions was measured by viral plaque assays. As shown in Fig. 1G and H, depletion of Trim25 significantly promoted the release of EMCV virions. Taken together, these results demonstrated that depletion of Trim25 promotes EMCV proliferation in ESCs.

Trim25 knockout markedly increases the replication of EMCV in mESCs

The siRNAs depleted about 50 percent of endogenous Trim25 RNA (Fig. 1A and B). Thus, to better evaluate the antiviral function of TRIM25 in ESCs, the CRISPR-Cas9 system was used to knock out the expression of Trim25. We generated two independent Trim25 knockout clones from D3 and E14TG2a cells, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2A and B, complete absence of TRIM25 was observed in Trim25-KO-1 and Trim25-KO-2 from D3 cells and Trim25-KO-2 from E14TG2a cells, while Trim25-KO-1 from E14TG2a cells retained approximately half of TRIM25 expression, indicating that it is a heterozygous clone. Importantly, Trim25 knockout did not affect the pluripotency of mESCs, as the expression levels of the pluripotent markers (Oct4, Nanog, and SSEA-1) remained comparable to those in wild-type cells (Supplementary Fig. S1).

After deletion of Trim25, the protein level of VP1 was prominently increased in D3 and E14TG2a cells (Fig. 2A and B). The RT-qPCR results showed that viral RNA increased 15-fold–40-fold in Trim25-knockout mESCs compared to control cells (Fig. 2C and D). Flow cytometric analysis found that, after deletion of Trim25, the percentage of infected cells increased from 5.32% to 32.2% in D3 cells and from 6.82% to 25.3% in E14TG2a cells (Fig. 2E–H). Combined analysis of the RT-qPCR and FACS data led to the conclusion that deletion of Trim25 markedly increases the replication of EMCV in each infected mESCs. Furthermore, viral plaque assays showed that knockout of Trim25 significantly promoted the release of EMCV virions into the cell culture medium (Fig. 2I and J). To further confirm that the enhanced viral replication was specifically attributable to TRIM25 deficiency, we performed rescue experiments by transducing Trim25-KO-2 E14TG2a cells with GFP-tagged TRIM25. As shown in Fig. 2K and L, reconstituted expression of TRIM25 significantly reduced EMCV viral RNA and VP1 protein levels. Taken together, these results demonstrated that TRIM25 is an important host factor that restricts EMCV proliferation in ESCs.

Knockout of Trim25 enhances VSV replication in mESCs

To further evaluate whether TRIM25 exhibits broad-spectrum antiviral activity in ESCs, E14TG2a cells were challenged with vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV), a negative single-stranded RNA enveloped virus belonging to the Rhabdoviridae family. The recombinant VSV expressing green fluorescent protein (VSV-GFP) was used here. The control and Trim25 knockout cells were infected with VSV-GFP at 0.01 MOI, and cells were harvested at 12, 18, and 24 hpi. The RT-qPCR and Western blotting analysis revealed that the deletion of Trim25 protein significantly promoted VSV-GFP proliferation as early as 12 hpi (Fig. 3A and B). Consistently, fluorescence microscopy confirmed that apparently more cells were infected by VSV-GFP in Trim25-knockout mESCs compared to control cells (Fig. 3C). Flow cytometric analysis showed that only 4.77% of control cells were infected by VSV-GFP, while the percentage of infected cells were increased to 51.5% or 54% in two independent Trim25-knockout E14TG2a cells (Fig. 3D and E). Consistently, the viral plaque assays also confirmed that the number of released VSV virions were significantly increased in the cell culture medium from Trim25-knockout E14TG2a cells than the control cells (Fig. 3F). Collectively, these data indicate that TRIM25 exhibits broad-spectrum antiviral activity to protect ESCs from both negative -stranded and positive-stranded RNA viruses.

TRIM25 directly binds viral RNA in mESCs

We next investigated the molecular mechanism of TRIM25-mediated antiviral activity in ESCs. TRIM25 has been reported to inhibit a variety of viruses by promoting RIG-I mediated interferon signaling in somatic cell (Gack et al., 2007). We thus measured the expression of Ifnb and the downstream antiviral ISGs, Mx1 and Isg15, in control and Trim25-knockout mESCs after virus infection. As shown in Fig. 4A and B, the mRNA levels of Ifnb and the downstream antiviral ISGs were not affected by Trim25 knockout, implying that the antiviral activity of TRIM25 in ESCs is independent of the IFN pathway. This is consistent with the previous reports that the IFN pathway is deficient in ESCs (Bekisz et al., 2010; Chen et al., 2010; Chen et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2013). To further confirm IFN deficiency in mESCs, we measured Ifnb expression in EMCV-infected mESCs compared to mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs). As shown in Supplementary Fig. S2, EMCV infection induced robust Ifnb expression in MEFs but failed to do so in D3 and E14TG2a cells, confirming that the IFN pathway is non-functional in mESCs.

A previous study reported that ESCs constitutively express a small subset of ISGs and that these proteins could protect ESCs from virus infection (Wu et al., 2017). Thus, we further investigated whether TRIM25 could regulate the expression of these constitutively expressed ISGs. The results showed that deletion of Trim25 had minimal effect on the expression of these constitutive ISGs (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, viral infection itself did not substantially alter the expression of these constitutive ISGs, with the exception of a slight increase in Ifitm1 and Mcl1 in D3 cells (less than 1.5-fold; Fig. 4D and E), indicating that the constitutive ISG program is largely stable upon viral challenge.

TRIM25 has been identified as an RNA-binding protein in mESCs (Kwon et al., 2013), and its RNA-binding activity is required for it to restrict the replication of IAV and Rabies virus (Meyerson et al., 2017; Choudhury et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2022). Thus, we speculated that TRIM25 may inhibit virus proliferation in ESCs through directly binding viral RNA. As shown in Fig. 5A, immunofluorescence analysis showed that TRIM25 was mainly localized in the nuclei of uninfected mESCs and that a portion of TRIM25 translocated to the cytoplasm in virus-infected mESCs. Furthermore, the cytoplasmic TRIM25 signal colocalized with the dsRNA signal, which is a marker of replicating viral RNA. This implies an association between TRIM25 and viral RNA. We further performed the RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays and found that the TRIM25-specific antibody could significantly enrich EMCV viral RNA compared to control IgG in both D3 and E14TG2a cells (Fig. 5B and C). Furthermore, anti-TRIM25 antibody significantly enriched VSV genomic RNA in both D3 and E14TG2a cells infected with VSV-GFP (Fig. 5D and E), indicating that TRIM25 also directly binds VSV RNA.

Altogether, our results demonstrated that TRIM25 restricts virus proliferation in mESCs. The mechanism is independent of IFN pathway and does not involve regulation of constitutive ISGs, but may act through directly binding with viral RNA.

TRIM25 synergizes with G3BP1 in antiviral defense of mESCs

A recent study reported that TRIM25 co-condenses with G3BP1 and forms anti-viral stress granule to promote RIG-I mediated IFN signaling in human somatic cells (Shang et al., 2024). Although the IFN pathway is deficient in ESCs, we speculated that TRIM25 might cooperate with G3BP1 to restrict virus infection in mESCs. We first performed immunofluorescent staining to investigate the relationship between TRIM25 and G3BP1 in virus-infected mESCs. As shown in Fig. 6A, the cytoplasmic fraction of TRIM25 was perfectly co-localized with G3BP1 in E14TG2a cells after EMCV infection. To test whether TRIM25 and G3BP1 physically interact, we performed co-immunoprecipitation experiments. As shown in Fig. 6B, anti-G3BP1 antibody co-precipitated TRIM25 in virus-infected mESCs, confirming a physical interaction between TRIM25 and G3BP1. Furthermore, G3BP1 was found to co-localize with dsRNA signal in EMCV-infected E14TG2a cells, with a Manders' Colocalization Coefficient of 0.78 ± 0.11 for dsRNA vs. G3BP1 (Fig. 6C), implying that the TRIM25-G3BP1 containing anti-viral stress granule was induced by viral RNA in mESCs. Furthermore, RIP assays using anti-G3BP1 antibody demonstrated that G3BP1 also directly associates with EMCV viral RNA (Fig. 6D and E).

Another question is whether G3BP1 could also inhibit RNA virus infection in ESCs. By using G3bp1 specific siRNA, we successfully depleted the expression of G3pb1 (Fig. 7A and B) and found that G3bp1 knockdown did not compromise mESC pluripotency, as Oct4 and SSEA-1 staining remained comparable to that in control cells (Supplementary Fig. S3). Furthermore, depletion of G3bp1 significantly increased EMCV viral RNA in both D3 and E14TG2a cells (Fig. 7C and D). The protein level of VP1 was also apparently increased in G3bp1-depleted mESCs compared to negative controls (Fig. 7E and F). Consistently, viral plaque assays confirmed that the number of released EMCV virions was significantly increased in the cell culture medium from G3bp1-depleted cells compared to control cells (Fig. 7G and H). To determine whether TRIM25 and G3BP1 function in the same or parallel pathways, we knocked down G3bp1 in Trim25 knockout cells (Fig. 7I). As shown in Fig. 7J and K, while G3bp1 depletion promoted EMCV replication in wild-type cells, further knockdown of G3bp1 in Trim25 knockout cells did not result in an additional increase in viral RNA or VP1 protein levels, indicating that TRIM25 and G3BP1 act in the same genetic pathway rather than in parallel pathways. Taken together, these results demonstrated that G3BP1 cooperated with TRIM25 to bind viral RNA and inhibited virus proliferation in mESCs.

Discussion

In contrast to somatic cells, ESCs exhibit distinct antiviral strategies to counteract viral invasion while balancing developmental plasticity. In this study, we identified a novel antiviral pathway in mESCs that operates independently of canonical type I interferon signaling. TRIM25 deficiency markedly enhanced the replication and virion release of both positive-strand (EMCV) and negative-strand (VSV) RNA viruses, as validated by plaque assays, qPCR, and flow cytometry. Notably, TRIM25 exerts its antiviral function independently of the IFN pathway, which is deficient in ESCs. In virus-infected mESCs, TRIM25 translocated from the nucleus to the cytoplasm and directly bound viral RNA, subsequently forming cytoplasmic condensates with G3BP1 that colocalized with dsRNA foci. Genetic perturbation of G3BP1 similarly compromised the antiviral defenses of mESCs. The physical interaction between TRIM25 and G3BP1, together with the observation that G3bp1 knockdown in Trim25 knockout cells did not further enhance viral replication, supports a model in which TRIM25 and G3BP1 function in the same pathway. These findings position the TRIM25-G3BP1 axis as a critical component of the ESC antiviral arsenal, complementing the existing mechanisms like RNAi and ERASE-mediated viral RNA degradation.

The RNA-binding activity of TRIM25 was initially characterized in mESCs and subsequently expanded to somatic cell lines (Kwon et al., 2013; Choudhury et al., 2017; Alvarez et al., 2024). Subsequent studies established that the RNA-binding capability is essential for TRIM25-mediated RIG-I ubiquitination and interferon response in somatic cells (Sanchez et al., 2018). In the present study, we observed that TRIM25 directly binds viral RNA and suppresses viral replication in IFN-deficient mESCs, suggesting a RIG-I-independent antiviral function of TRIM25. This finding aligns with a prior report demonstrating that TRIM25 could inhibit influenza virus replication in RIG-I knockout cells, where it promiscuously associates with positive-strand viral RNAs and promotes their destabilization (Choudhury et al., 2022). Moreover, an independent study revealed that TRIM25 binds to influenza vRNP in an RNA-dependent manner, thereby blocking the initiation of RNA chain elongation in the nucleus of virus-infected cells (Meyerson et al., 2017). Collectively, these observations underscore that TRIM25 employs multiple, context-dependent mechanisms to constrain RNA virus replication, with its direct RNA-binding and destabilizing activity operating effectively even in the absence of canonical IFN signaling.

Our immunofluorescence staining showed that TRIM25 mainly localized in the nucleus in mESCs. This is consistent with a previous report that TRIM25 is recruited to the replication forks by the Filia-Floped scaffold and then activates the Blm pathway to restart the stalled replication forks and maintain genomic stability in mESCs during replication stress (Zhao et al., 2018). Additionally, TRIM25 has been shown to be recruited by Zscan4 to promote the chromatin accessibility for activation of the 2C-like state in mESCs by mediating the ubiquitination and degradation of repressive chromatin regulators (Yang et al., 2024). The coupling of antiviral functions with genome maintenance of TRIM25 ensures dual protection for ESCs against exogenous and endogenous nucleic acid threats. Thus, its pleiotropy in ESCs positions TRIM25 as a multifunctional hub coordinating antiviral defense, epigenetic regulation, and genome integrity.

We found that TRIM25 translocated from the nucleus to the cytoplasm of mESCs after virus infection. The exact mechanism underlying this infection-induced translocation remains unknown. One possibility is that viral infection disrupts TRIM25’s interaction with nuclear retention factors such as Filia-Floped, or alters post-translational modifications that regulate nuclear localization/export signals. Increasing evidence suggests that nuclear RNA-binding proteins are selectively translocated to the cytoplasm upon infection with cytoplasmic RNA viruses, where they accumulate in viral replication organelles and suppress viral gene expression (Sanz et al., 2015; Tapescu et al., 2023; Kamel et al., 2024; Castello and Kamel, 2025). Notably, RNase H1, the key effector protein in ERASE antiviral mechanism, was also reported to shuttle from the nucleus to the cytoplasm in virus-infected mESCs (Wu et al., 2021). Thus, the spatial-temporal regulation of TRIM25 and RNase H1 nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling during infection highlights the dynamic nature of ESC-specific antiviral responses. It is reasonable to speculate that there may be a conserved ESC strategy to repurpose nuclear surveillance proteins for cytoplasmic antiviral defense.

We observed that TRIM25 formed apparent puncta in the cytoplasm of virus-infected mESCs and colocalized with both viral dsRNA and G3BP1. Depletion of G3bp1 also promoted viral proliferation in mESCs. The association of TRIM25 and G3BP1 in human somatic cells have been well studied (Yoo et al., 2014; Yang et al., 2020; Shang et al., 2024). Shang et al. reported that virus infection promoted TRIM25 co-condensation with G3BP1 and the formation of anti-viral stress granules in somatic cells. The TRIM25-G3BP1 condensation significantly enhanced the activation of RIG-I-mediated IFN pathway to restrain RNA virus infection (Shang et al., 2024). However, the IFN pathway is deficient in mESCs and we confirmed that Trim25-knockout had minimal effects on the expression of IFN and its downstream ISGs in mESCs. We propose that TRIM25-G3BP1 condensates in virus-infected mESCs may function as scaffolds allowing the local enrichment of endogenous ribonucleases or restriction factors to destroy the concentrated viral RNAs, such as the zinc-finger antiviral protein (ZAP) and KH and NYN domain-containing protein (KHNYN) (Shang et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2025). Consistently, recent work has identified that cellular TRIM25 act as a key suppressor against exogenous mRNAs delivered with lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) and that N4BP1, KHNYN, and ZAP act redundantly in the downstream of TRIM25-dependent exogenous RNA surveillance (Kim et al., 2025). However, the exact mechanism of RNA degradation within TRIM25-G3BP1 condensates remains to be determined, and future studies are needed to verify whether these factors are also responsible for TRIM25-G3BP1-mediated antiviral function in ESCs.

Collectively, our study unveils a previously unrecognized antiviral role of TRIM25 in mESCs in which TRIM25 collaborates with G3BP1 to form cytoplasmic condensates that sequester viral dsRNA and suppress viral replication. This mechanism operates independently of canonical IFN pathway, representing a primordial layer of antiviral defense in pluripotent stem cells. Future studies should delineate the cross-talk between TRIM25-G3BP1 axis with the known ESC defense systems (e.g., RNAi and ERASE). Such work will deepen our understanding of stem cell-specific immunity and its manipulation for biomedical applications.

Cell lines and cell culture

Mouse embryonic stem cells (E14TG2a and D3), as well as BHK21, Vero, MEF, and HEK293T cells, were sourced from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai, China. The E14TG2a and D3 cells were cultured in stem cell medium consisting of KnockOut™ DMEM supplemented with 20% KnockOut™ Serum Replacement, 2 mM GlutaMAX, 0.1 mM Non-Essential Amino Acids, 0.055 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 1000 U/mL leukemia inhibitory factor, 3 μM CHIR99021, 1 μM PD0325901, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. These mESCs were passaged at three-day intervals using 0.05% trypsin-EDTA.

BHK21, HEK293T, MEF and Vero cells were maintained in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. All cell lines were incubated under controlled conditions of 37 °C, 5% CO2, and high humidity.

Viruses

The encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV), classified under the Picornaviridae family, was generated from the infectious clone pCMV-rNJ08, which was kindly provided by Prof. Ping Jiang. The plasmid was introduced into BHK21 cells using Lipofectamine 2000. Once significant cytopathic effects were observed, the supernatants were collected and subjected to two rounds of passage. Viral titers were quantified using a plaque assay with BHK21 cells and viral stocks were stored at −80 °C for subsequent use. The vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) carrying GFP reporter gene (VSV-GFP) was kindly provided by Prof. Hong-Bing Shu. VSV-GFP virus was amplified in Vero cells and viral stocks were stored at −80 °C for subsequent use.

Generation of Trim25-knockout mESC cell lines

The sgRNAs targeting mouse Trim25 gene were designed online (http://crispr.mit.edu/) and were inserted into the pLentiCRISPR v2 plasmid (Addgene, #52961). Lentivirus was generated by co-transfecting the pLentiCRISPR v2 plasmid with pMD2.G and psPAX2 into 293T cells using PEI. The supernatants containing lentivirus were collected and further concentrated by Virus Concentrator. The lentivirus was transduced into mESCs in the presence of 8 μg/mL Polybrene. Then, cells were selected with 1 μg/mL puromycin for 1 week and the drug-resistant single cells were seeded in 96-well plates. The single cell colonies were expanded and the successful gene knockout were confirmed by RT-qPCR and immunoblotting assays.

Reverse transcription and quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted by using the TRIzol™ reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 15596026), and then the complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis was carried out with the Evo M-MLV RT Premix (Accurate Biology, AG11706, Changsha, China). Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was conducted using the SYBR Green Premix Pro Taq HS qPCR Kit (Accurate Biology, AG11701) on the QuantStudio 1 system (Thermo, USA). The relative expression levels of target genes were normalized against the housekeeping gene, Gapdh. The primer sequences of all the target genes were listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Immunofluorescence analysis

The immunofluorescence staining was performed as described in our previous report. Briefly, mESCs were seeded onto the glass coverslips pre-coated with 20 μg/mL poly-l-ornithine and 10 μg/mL laminin. The adhered cells were infected with EMCV at 1 MOI for 48 h. Cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, then permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 20 min and blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min at 26 °C. Overnight primary antibody staining at 4 °C was performed with the following antibodies: anti-dsRNA J2, anti-VP1, anti-G3BP1, anti-Oct4, anti-SSEA1 and anti-TRIM25. After thorough washing, cells were incubated for 1 h with Alexa Fluor 488- or 555-conjugated secondary antibodies and counterstained with DAPI for 10 min at 26 °C. Images were taken by using a Zeiss LSM 900 confocal microscope equipped with a 63 × oil immersion objective (Carl Zeiss Microimaging). Manders' colocalization coefficient of dsRNA with G3BP1 were measured by using Image J software with Coloc 2 plugin.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

Virus-infected mESCs were fixed with 0.3% formaldehyde for 10 min at 26 °C, and the fixation was quenched with 125 mM Glycine. The cells were then lysed in a binding buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP40, 1 mM EDTA, 0.04% Sodium Deoxycholate) containing protease inhibitor cocktail and RNase inhibitor. The samples were immunoprecipitated overnight at 4 °C with either anti-TRIM25 antibody or normal IgG. The next day, protein A/G Magnetic Beads were added and incubated at 4 °C for 2 h. Beads were washed five times with the binding buffer. The protein-RNA complex was eluted in elusion buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 10 mM EDTA, 0.5% SDS, 2.5 μg Proteinase K) at 55 °C for 45 min, followed by de-crosslinking at 65 °C for 4 h. The immunoprecipitated RNAs were extracted using Trizol according to standard RNA extraction protocols, and viral RNA level was measured by RT-qPCR.

Plaque assay

The virus is stored at −80 °C and thawed at 4 °C when needed. The supernatants collected after viral infection were 10-fold serially diluted using fresh medium. The diluted samples were then added to confluent monolayers of either BHK21 cells (for EMCV) or Vero cells (for VSV-GFP) in 12-well plates. Following a 2-h incubation, the supernatants were removed, and the cells were overlaid with a medium containing 0.8% or 1.3% methylcellulose. After three days, the cells were stained overnight with 0.2% crystal violet, and the plaques were counted.

Flow cytometry analysis

Cells infected with EMCV at 1 MOI for 24 h were harvested and fixed at 26 °C for 15 min using 1% formaldehyde in PBS. Following fixation, the cells were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 15 min and subsequently were blocked with 3% BSA for 30 min. They were incubated sequentially with anti-VP1 antibody, and followed by Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibody at 26 °C for 1 h. The proportion of virus-infected cells was assessed using a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA), and data was analyzed by using FlowJo v10 software. The VSV-GFP infected cells were directly counted with CytoFLEX flow cytometer after a brief fixation.

Statistical analysis

The quantitative data were displayed as means ± SD and all the statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism 7.0 software using the two tailed unpaired Student’s t-test. Difference was considered significant when P value is < 0.05.

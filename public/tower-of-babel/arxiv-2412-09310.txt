Ferrimagnetic Kitaev spin liquids in mixed spin 1/2 spin 3/2 honeycomb magnets
Willian Natori, Yang Yang, Hui-Ke Jin, Johannes Knolle, Natalia B. Perkins

Abstract
We explore the phase diagram of a mixed-spin Kitaev model, where spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 ions form a staggered pattern on a honeycomb lattice. Enabled by an exact mapping of local conserved flux operators onto Z 2 Z_{2} gauge fields, we perform a parton mean-field theory for the model with a single-ion anisotropy. The phase diagram contains four types of quantum spin liquids distinguished by quadrupolar parameters. These analytical results are quantitatively confirmed by state-of-the-art DMRG simulations. We also explore the potential experimental realization of the mixed-spin Kitaev model in materials such as Zr 0.5 Ru 0.5 Cl 3 . By developing a superexchange theory specifically for this mixed-spin system, we identify the conditions under which dominant Kitaev-like interactions emerge. Our findings highlight the importance of spin-orbital couplings and quadrupolar order parameters in stabilizing exotic phases, providing a foundation for exploring mixed-spin Kitaev magnets.

II
Mixed-spin Kitaev Honeycomb Model

Inspired by the exact solution of the spin-1/2 KHM
[
2
]
and the existing theoretical analysis of the spin-3/2 KHM
[
12
,
13
]
, we will study the phase diagram of the mixed-spin Kitaev model under a single-ion anisotropy (SIA), which is formally written by

H=\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}K_{\gamma}S_{i}^{\gamma}J_{j}^{\gamma}+H_{\text{SIA}}.

(1)

Here,
S_{i}^{\gamma}
(
J_{i}^{\gamma}
) represents a spin-1/2 (spin-3/2) operator, and
\gamma
simultaneously labels the quantization axes and the distinct bond directions. The spin-1/2 ions will be on the sublattice denoted by
A
, whereas spin-3/2 will be on
B
sublattice. By convention, a unit cell contains two neighboring ions connected by a
z
-bond parallel to the
y
-axis.

We will also introduce a control parameter in the form of the simplest SIA term

H_{\text{SIA}}=D_{z}\sum_{j}\left(J_{j}^{z}\right)^{2},

(2)

which lifts the four-fold degeneracy of
J=3/2
quadruplet by splitting
J_{z}=\pm 3/2
and
J_{z}=\pm 1/2
levels. As we shall see, this addition allows for a more manageable investigation of the mixed-spin KHM by projecting it to the spin-1/2 model, thus ensuring an exactly solvable limit.

II.1
Exact results and conserved fluxes

The local symmetries of Eq. (
1
) can be made more transparent in terms of
J=3/2
pseudospin and pseudo-orbital operators
[
12
,
13
]
. We introduce the pseudospins by

\sigma_{l}^{\gamma}=-i\exp\left(i\pi J_{l}^{\gamma}\right),

(3)

which corresponds to the local operators forming the spin-
S
conserved quantities
[
10
]
. Likewise, the pseudo-orbital operators read

\displaystyle T_{j}^{z}=\left(J_{j}^{z}\right)^{2}-5/4,

\displaystyle T_{j}^{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left[\left(J_{j}^{x}\right)^{2}-\left(J_{j}^{y}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle T_{j}^{y}=\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{9}\overline{J_{j}^{x}J_{j}^{y}J_{j}^{z}},

(4)

where the bar indicates the sum over all possible permutations. The operators
\left(T^{z},T^{x}\right)
are quadrupoles that transform as
e_{g}
orbital operators under real-space rotations. By contrast, the octupolar operator
T^{y}
forms a one-dimensional representation of the
O_{h}
symmetry group
[
36
]
. In conjunction,
\bm{\sigma}
and
\bf{T}
satisfy the algebra

\displaystyle\left[\sigma_{i}^{\alpha},\sigma_{j}^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=2i\delta_{ij}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\sigma_{i}^{\gamma},

\displaystyle\left[T_{i}^{\alpha},T_{j}^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=2i\delta_{ij}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}T_{i}^{\gamma},

\displaystyle\left\{\sigma_{i}^{\alpha},\sigma_{j}^{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{T_{i}^{\alpha},T_{j}^{\beta}\right\}=2\delta_{ij}\delta^{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\left[\sigma_{i}^{\alpha},T_{j}^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(5)

The fifteen operators
\left\{\sigma^{a},T^{b},\sigma^{a}T^{b}\right\}
correspond to generators of SU(4), implying that they can be used to rewrite any Hermitian
J=3/2
operator. A simple example is Eq. (
2
), which reads

H_{\text{SIA}}=D_{z}\sum_{j}T_{j}^{z}+\text{const}.

(6)

We can also write
J_{i}^{\gamma}
as
[
12
,
13
]

J_{i}^{\gamma}=-\frac{\sigma_{i}^{\gamma}}{2}-\sigma_{i}^{\gamma}T_{i}^{\alpha\beta},

(7)

where the compass-like pseudo-orbitals
T^{\alpha\beta}
are

\displaystyle T_{i}^{xy}=T_{i}^{z},\quad T_{i}^{yz(zx)}=-\frac{T_{i}^{z}}{2}\pm\frac{\sqrt{3}T_{i}^{x}}{2}.

(8)

The mixed-spin exchange model in Eq. (
1
) is then rewritten as

H_{\text{K}}=\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}K_{\gamma}S_{i}^{\gamma}\sigma_{j}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{2}+T_{j}^{\alpha\beta}\right).

(9)

Crucially, it allows us to show that the Hamiltonian commutes with the local plaquette operators

W_{p}=2^{3}S^{z}_{1}\sigma^{x}_{2}S^{y}_{3}\sigma^{z}_{4}S^{x}_{5}\sigma^{y}_{6},

(10)

in which the quantization axes correspond to the outward bond label and the multiplying factor
2^{3}
was introduced to ensure the
W_{p}
eigenvalues are
\pm 1
. The presence of a conserved plaquette flux is in close analogy with the spin-1/2
[
2
]
and spin-3/2 KHM
[
10
,
12
,
13
]
. The extensive number of conserved quantities indicates that the model realizes a Kitaev QSL and is amenable to an analytical treatment, as we will show in the following section. These conserved quantities also provide guidelines for interpreting the DMRG results, as explored in Section
III
.

The SIA term in Eq. (
2
) and
W_{p}
also commute, as directly observed from the commutation relations between
T^{z}
and
\bm{\sigma}
, allowing us to use it as a control parameter. In the
D_{z}\rightarrow+\infty
limit, pseudo-orbital fluctuations are effectively suppressed, allowing us to fix
T^{z}=-1
and to project the
J^{\gamma}
angular momenta onto the pseudospins according to the rule:

\left(J_{j}^{x},J_{j}^{y},J_{j}^{z}\right)\overset{D_{z}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left(-2\sigma^{x},-2\sigma^{y},\sigma^{z}\right).

(11)

Therefore, Eq. (
1
) reduces to

\lim_{D_{z}\rightarrow\infty}H=\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}K_{\gamma}^{\prime}S_{i}^{\gamma}\sigma_{j}^{\gamma}.

(12)

with modified coupling constants
\left(K_{x}^{\prime},K_{y}^{\prime},K_{z}^{\prime}\right)=\left(-2K_{x},-2K_{y},K_{z}\right)
. Fixing
T^{z}=-1
implies that
\sigma^{\gamma}
will act on the
J^{z}=\pm 1/2
manifold only, allowing the pseudospins to be treated as effective spin-1/2 operators. Thus, the SIA connects the mixed-spin KHM model to the standard spin-1/2 KHM, therefore introducing an exactly solvable limit.

II.2
Majorana representation and parton mean-field theory

An analytical treatment of the mixed-spin KHM
becomes possible by introducing Majorana partons, which map
Z_{2}
conserved quantities in Eq. (
10
) onto fluxes of a static
Z_{2}
gauge field. We begin by representing the spin-1/2 degrees of freedom using the Kitaev Majorana parton framework
[
2
]
:

S_{i}^{\gamma}=-\frac{i}{2}\eta_{i}^{\gamma}c_{i},

(13)

in which the Majorana particles with flavors
\eta^{\alpha}
and
c
at sites
i,j
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\eta_{i}^{\alpha},\eta_{j}^{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\delta_{ij}\delta^{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\left\{c_{i},c_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\delta_{ij},

\displaystyle\left\{\eta_{i}^{\alpha},c_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=0

(14)

Eq. (
13
) adequately reproduces the canonical spin-1/2 algebra once we introduce a local gauge operator
[
2
]
. Likewise, we introduce the SO(6) Majorana representation for spin-3/2 as follows
[
12
,
13
,
40
]

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}_{i}=-\frac{i}{2}\bm{\eta}_{i}\times\bm{\eta}_{i},

\displaystyle\,\mathbf{T}_{i}=-\frac{i}{2}\bm{\theta}_{i}\times\bm{\theta}_{i},

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}T_{i}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=-i\eta_{i}^{\alpha}\theta_{i}^{\beta},

(15)

in which the Majorana flavors
\eta
satisfy the same algebra as in Eq.
14
plus the added relations with
\theta
Majorana fermions

\displaystyle\left\{\theta_{i}^{\alpha},\theta_{j}^{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\delta_{ij}\delta^{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\left\{\eta_{i}^{\alpha},\theta_{j}^{\beta}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{c_{i},\theta_{j}^{\beta}\right\}=0.

(16)

The first two equations in Eq.
15
correspond to the SO(3) Majorana representation of spin-1/2 systems
[
41
,
42
]
and reproduce the algebra from Eq. (
5
). The second line allows us to represent all spin-orbital operators as bilinears and is consistent with the constraint on the physical Hilbert space
[
40
]

{\mathcal{D}}_{i}=i\eta_{i}^{\alpha}\eta_{i}^{\beta}\eta_{i}^{\gamma}\theta_{i}^{\alpha}\theta_{i}^{\beta}\theta_{i}^{\gamma}=1.

(17)

The
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
operator not only enforces the constraint but also provides an alternative representation of the pseudospin operators as quartic operators, as discussed in recent studies
[
12
,
13
,
43
]
:

\sigma_{j}^{\gamma}=-\frac{i}{2}\eta_{j}^{\gamma}\theta_{j}^{0},

(18)

where
\theta_{j}^{0}\equiv-i\theta_{j}^{x}\theta_{j}^{y}\theta_{j}^{z}
. This allows us to reproduce the algebra of all
S=3/2
operators in terms of Majorana bilinears.

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
), expressed in the Majorana parton representation, takes the form:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}K_{\gamma}\hat{u}_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}ic_{i}\left(\theta_{j}^{0}+2\theta_{j}^{\alpha\beta}\right)

\displaystyle-D_{z}\sum_{j}i\theta_{j}^{x}\theta_{j}^{y},

(19)

in which
\hat{u}_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}=-i\eta_{i}^{\gamma}\eta_{j}^{\gamma}
represents a static
Z_{2}
gauge operator. The replacement of
\left\{\hat{u}_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}\right\}
by their
\pm 1
eigenvalues determines the flux sectors labeled by
W_{p}
. The zero-flux sector (
W_{p}=+1,\forall p
) is the ground state of the Kitaev spin-1/2 model, as established by Lieb’s theorem
[
44
]
. Furthermore, both numerical and analytical studies provide strong evidence that this sector also hosts the ground state of the KHM for arbitrary spin-
S

[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. Thus, we will focus on the zero-flux sector of the mixed-spin KHM, a choice further supported by the DMRG simulations presented later in this work.

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
19
), after gauge fixing, consists of Majorana bilinear terms with additional quartic interactions of the form
ic_{i}\theta_{j}^{0}
, which require a mean-field treatment. The most general mean-field decoupling within a fixed gauge sector can be expressed as:

\displaystyle ic_{i}\theta_{j}^{0}

\displaystyle\approx\Delta_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}^{x}i\theta_{j}^{y}\theta_{j}^{z}+\Delta_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}^{y}i\theta_{j}^{z}\theta_{j}^{x}+\Delta_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}^{z}i\theta_{j}^{x}\theta_{j}^{y}

\displaystyle+Q_{j}^{x}ic_{i}\theta_{j}^{x}+Q_{j}^{y}ic_{i}\theta_{j}^{y}+Q_{j}^{z}ic_{i}\theta_{j}^{z},

(20)

with the parameters defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}^{t}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Psi_{0}\left|ic_{i}\theta_{j}^{t}\right|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle Q_{j}^{t}

\displaystyle=\left\langle T_{j}^{t}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\Psi_{0}\left|i\theta_{j}^{r}\theta_{j}^{s}\right|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle,

(21)

in which
\left\langle\Psi_{0}\left|\hat{O}\right|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle
is the expectation value of
\hat{O}
for the mean-field ground state
\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle
. The
\Delta_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}^{t}
parameters correspond to the pairings
c_{i}
and
\theta_{j}^{t}
, which are Majorana matter fermions from spin-1/2 and spin-3/2, respectively. On the other hand, nonvanishing
Q_{j}^{t}
accounts for the possible coexistence of a QSL with a quadrupolar or octupolar parameter
[
12
,
13
]
. This is expected to be the case when introducing Eq.
6
in the limit
D_{z}\rightarrow\infty
, where
Q^{z}\rightarrow-1
uniformly throughout the lattice.

We will enforce the zero-flux sector by fixing
u_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle_{\gamma}}=+1
, leading to the following mean-field decoupled Hamiltonian then reads:

\displaystyle H_{\text{MFT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r},\gamma}K_{\gamma}ic_{\mathbf{r}}\theta_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\mathbf{r},\gamma,t}K_{\gamma}Q^{t}ic_{\mathbf{r}}\theta_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}^{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\mathbf{r},\gamma,t}K_{\gamma}\Delta_{\gamma}^{t}i\theta_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}^{r}\theta_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}^{s}

\displaystyle-D_{z}\sum_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}i\theta_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}^{x}\theta_{\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}}^{y},

(22)

in which
\mathbf{r}
label spin-1/2 ions and
\mathbf{r}_{\gamma}
their spin-3/2 nearest neighbors. We applied a sum convention in the third line by fixing
\epsilon^{rst}=1
. We also presupposed translationally invariant mean-field parameters, thus making their position labels superfluous. Our mean-field theory will then depend upon 12 parameters, three of them being onsite (
Q^{t}
) and nine corresponding to pairings involving
\theta^{t}
operators along
\gamma
-bonds (
\Delta_{\gamma}^{t}
). For computation purposes, writing the Majorana fermions in terms of canonical fermions
f
and
g
will be conveniently defined as follows

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{r},A}=f_{\mathbf{r}}+f_{\mathbf{r}}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\,\theta_{\mathbf{r},B}^{z}=g_{\mathbf{r}}^{\dagger}+g_{\mathbf{r}},

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathbf{r},B}^{y}=\frac{f_{\mathbf{r}}-f_{\mathbf{r}}^{\dagger}}{i},

\displaystyle\,\theta_{\mathbf{r},B}^{x}=\frac{g_{\mathbf{r}}-g_{\mathbf{r}}^{\dagger}}{i}.

(23)

Eq. (
22
) in the Fourier space can be written in terms of a
4\times 4
matrix
H_{\mathbf{k}}
as follows

\displaystyle H_{\text{MFT}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}H_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}(U_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}H_{\mathbf{k}}U_{\mathbf{k}})\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}},

(24)

where
\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}=\left(f_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},g_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},f_{-\mathbf{k}},g_{-\mathbf{k}}\right)
,
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}=\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}U_{\mathbf{k}}
are the eigenstates, and
\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}=U_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}H_{\mathbf{k}}U_{\mathbf{k}}
is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues. The mean-field ground state
\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle
satisfies
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle=0
for all
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
corresponding to negative eigenstates.

The self-consistency algorithm begins with an initial guess
\mathbf{x}_{0}=\left(\Delta_{\gamma}^{x},..,Q^{z}\right)
for the order parameters. This initial guess defines the starting Hamiltonian, the unitary matrices
U_{\mathbf{k}}
, as well as the ground state
\left|\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\rangle
. The initial guess also allows us to evaluate a vector
\mathbf{x}_{\text{par}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})
using Eq. (
21
). The self-consistent condition can then be reformulated as a root-finding problem for the function
f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}_{\text{par}}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{x}
. Such problems are efficiently solved using standard algorithms such as Broyden’s method
[
45
]
. For concreteness, we considered self-consistent solutions
\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|<\text{tol}
, in which the tolerance was of the order
10^{-12}
. The algorithm was run with
\sim 200
initial guesses
\mathbf{x}_{0}
to ensure that all self-consistent solutions could be found.

We conclude this section by noting that a more realistic evaluation of the microscopic parameters will require the use of advanced ab-initio and quantum chemistry methods.
While the on-site interaction parameters

Despite these simplifications, our perturbative analysis serves as a proof of principle demonstrating that a mixed-spin Kitaev honeycomb model can arise as an effective description of ferrimagnetic systems, introducing exchange frustration into a setting long studied from a different perspective. A detailed exploration of the full phase diagram and refinement of the minimal model are left for future work.

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we explored the mixed-spin Kitaev honeycomb model, where alternating spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 ions occupy the two sublattice positions of the honeycomb lattice. We constructed a comprehensive phase diagram using parton mean-field theory and DMRG simulations. The phase diagram reveals four distinct QSL phases, each characterized by a unique quadrupolar order parameter, specific flux configurations, and Majorana fermion excitations. The quantitative agreement between parton mean-field analysis and numerical approaches highlights the robustness of our framework, except in a small parameter region around the isotropic point. We also discussed a potential experimental realization in materials such as Zr
0.5
Ru
0.5
Cl
3
, in which we derived a microscopic superexchange Hamiltonian and identified conditions under which dominant Kitaev-like interactions arise.

Our results foster further investigation of frustrated magnetism in ferrimagnetic systems. While our study focused on pure Kitaev interactions with single-ion anisotropy, taking into account other interactions in Eq.(
IV
), such as the Heisenberg exchange, bond-anisotropic
\Gamma
-interaction, and higher-order multipolar couplings, could stabilize other exotic phases, including chiral QSLs and magnetically ordered states. These extensions provide further opportunities for future research to explore the interplay between dipolar and multipolar interactions, potentially uncovering an even broader spectrum of quantum phases.

details the projection of the perturbation matrix onto a set of orthogonal spin matrices, expressing the superexchange Hamiltonian in terms of the corresponding spin operators. This section also clarifies the specific set of spin-3/2 operators used in the projection.

A.1
One-particle eigenstates

The spin-orbit coupling (SOC) interaction couples the spin
S=1/2
of either the single hole in Ru
3+
or the single electron in Zr
3+
to their effective orbital angular momentum
L=1
, resulting in total angular momenta of
J=1/2
and
J=3/2
, respectively. Consequently, for the single electron in Zr
3+
, the lowest-energy state is four-fold degenerate, with an energy of
E_{\mathrm{Zr}}^{(0)}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}
. The corresponding eigenstates are given by:

\displaystyle|E_{\mathrm{Zr},1}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-i|d_{yz,\uparrow}\rangle+|d_{xz,\uparrow}\rangle\right),

(A1)

\displaystyle|E_{\mathrm{Zr},2}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(-i|d_{yz,\downarrow}\rangle+|d_{xz,\downarrow}\rangle+2i|d_{xy,\uparrow}\rangle\right),

(A2)

\displaystyle|E_{\mathrm{Zr},3}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(i|d_{yz,\uparrow}\rangle+|d_{xz,\uparrow}\rangle+2i|d_{xy,\downarrow}\rangle\right),

(A3)

\displaystyle|E_{\mathrm{Zr},4}^{(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(i|d_{yz,\downarrow}\rangle+|d_{xz,\downarrow}\rangle\right).

(A4)

Similarly, the lowest-energy states of Ru
3+
have an energy
E_{\mathrm{Ru}}^{(0)}=10U_{2}-\lambda
, and they are given by the following eigenstates:

\displaystyle|E_{\mathrm{Ru},1}^{(0)}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(|d_{yz,\uparrow}\rangle+i|d_{xz,\downarrow}\rangle+|d_{xy,\downarrow}\rangle\right),

(A5)

\displaystyle|E_{\mathrm{Ru},2}^{(0)}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(-|d_{yz,\downarrow}\rangle-i|d_{xz,\uparrow}\rangle+|d_{xy,\uparrow}\rangle\right).

(A6)

The four degenerate ground states for the single electron on Zr
3+
become the magnetic degrees of freedom for
J_{\mathrm{eff}}=3/2
, and the two degenerate ground states for five electrons on Ru
3+
become the magnetic degrees of freedom for
J_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/2
. Now we can derive the superexchange Hamiltonian for
J_{\mathrm{eff}}=3/2
and
J_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/2
moments as an
8\times 8
perturbation matrix with the hopping.

A.2
Hopping matrix

The effective hopping Hamiltonian between sites on the honeycomb lattice occupied by spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 ions reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{t}=\sum_{ij}\sum_{\alpha\beta\sigma}t_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}d^{\dagger}_{i\alpha\sigma}d_{j\beta\sigma},

(A7)

where
{d}_{i\alpha\sigma}
are the annihilation operators for the
\alpha
-th orbital with spin
\sigma
(
\uparrow
or
\downarrow
) at site
i
, and
t_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}
represents the hopping parameters, which, in
the most general case, can be expressed in matrix form for each bond. For the
z
-bond, the hopping matrix is given by
[
50
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&$d_{yz,\sigma}$&$d_{xz,\sigma}$&$d_{xy,\sigma}$\\
\hline\cr$d_{yz,\sigma}$&$t_{1}$&$t_{2}$&$t_{4}$\\
\hline\cr$d_{xz,\sigma}$&$t_{2}$&$t_{1}$&$t_{4}$\\
\hline\cr$d_{xy,\sigma}$&$t_{4}$&$t_{4}$&$t_{3}$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,.

For the ideal octahedra without any trigonal distortion, there is an additional,
local

C_{2}
symmetry around the axis perpendicular to the bond (
[001]
axis for the
z
-bond) and passing through its center which prevents any mixing between the
xy
and the
zx
and
yz
orbitals, forcing
t_{4}\!=\!0
. In the presence of the trigonal distortion, we can have a nonzero
t_{4}
. Also note that the indirect hopping through the ligand ion is accounted for by the renormalization of
t_{2}
. Finally, the corresponding matrices for the bonds
x
and
y
can be found by applying the
C_{3}
rotation around the
[111]
axis.

A.3
Perturbation theory

Using the perturbation expansion for the effective superexchange Hamiltonian Eq. (
30
), we explicitly account for both
i\rightarrow j
and
j\rightarrow i
hoppings, as
i
and
j
sites are occupied by inequivalent Ru
3+
and Zr
3+
ions. The excited intermediate states resulting from these single-electron hoppings correspond to the
d^{0}
–
d^{6}
and
d^{2}
–
d^{4}
configurations. The
d^{0}
–
d^{6}
configuration is reached when an electron hops from the
d^{1}
to the
d^{5}
state, while the
d^{2}
–
d^{4}
configuration occurs when hopping takes place from
d^{5}
to
d^{1}
.
In the case of the
d^{0}
–
d^{6}
configuration, there is only one excited state, with energy
E_{\mathrm{excited},06}=15U_{2}
, where the Zr orbitals are empty and the Ru orbitals are fully occupied. The situation is more complex for the
d^{2}
–
d^{4}
configuration. The
d^{2}
configuration on the Zr
2+
ion gives rise to 15 intermediate states, and similarly, the
d^{4}
configuration on the Ru
4+
ion results in 15 intermediate states.
In this case,
each Zr
2+
or Ru
4+
ion gives
5
distinct eigenvalues:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Zr},1}=\frac{1}{2}(2U_{2}-2J_{H}+\lambda),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Zr},(2,3)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(2U_{2}+3J_{H}+\lambda\mp\sqrt{25J_{H}^{2}-10J_{H}\lambda+9\lambda^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Zr},(4,5)}=\frac{1}{4}\left(4U_{2}-\lambda\pm\sqrt{16J_{H}^{2}+8J_{H}\lambda+9\lambda^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Ru},1}=\frac{1}{2}(12U_{2}-2J_{H}-\lambda),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Ru},(2,3)}=\frac{1}{4}\left(24U_{2}+\lambda\mp\sqrt{16J_{H}^{2}+8J_{H}\lambda+9\lambda^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{Ru},(4,5)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(12U_{2}+3J_{H}-\lambda\pm\sqrt{25J_{H}^{2}-10J_{H}\lambda+9\lambda^{2}}\right).

(A8)

Combining the two ions results in a total of 225 intermediate states, corresponding to 25 distinct intermediate energies,
E_{\mathrm{excited},24}
, which originate from the various excited states of the Zr
2+
and Ru
4+
ions.

Finally, we explicitly compute the superexchange Hamiltonian in Eq. (
30
) in the form of an
8\times 8
matrix by summing over all the intermediate excited states. This process is carried out systematically using Mathematica.

A.4
Spin-1/2 - spin-3/2 Hamiltonian

After constructing the perturbation matrix, we project it onto a set of orthogonal spin matrices to express the superexchange Hamiltonian in terms of the corresponding spin operators. There are multiple representations of the superexchange Hamiltonian, as various orthogonal spin matrices can be employed to describe the spin-3/2 degrees of freedom. We use a specific set of spin-3/2 operators, consisting of 15 distinct operators:

J_{y}

J_{z}

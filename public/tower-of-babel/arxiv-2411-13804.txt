The Brown measure of non-Hermitian sums of projections
Max Sun Zhou

Abstract
. We compute the Brown measure of the non-normal operators X = p + i ​ q X=p+iq , where p p and q q are Hermitian, freely independent, and have spectra consisting of 2 2 atoms. The computation relies on the model of the non-trivial part of the von Neumann algebra generated by 2 projections as 2 × 2 2\times 2 random matrices. We observe that these measures are supported on hyperbolas and note some other properties related to their atoms and symmetries.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful, normal, tracial state
\tau
. Let
X\in(M,\tau)
. The Brown measure of
X
, introduced in
[
3
]
, is a complex Borel probability measure supported on the spectrum of
X
. When
X
is normal, the Brown measure of
X
is the spectral measure. When
X
is a random matrix, the Brown measure of
X
is the empirical spectral distribution of
X
.

The first interesting explicit computations for the Brown measure of non-trivial operators were provided in
[
6
]
, nearly 20 years after the Brown measure was first introduced. This class of operators are the “
R
-diagonal operators” which include Voiculescu’s circular operator. In recent years, there has been much research in computing the Brown measure for a wide variety of families of operators (see
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
2
]
for some examples).

In this paper, we will explicitly compute the Brown measure of operators of the form
X=p+iq
, where
p,q\in(M,\tau)
are Hermitian, freely independent, and have spectra consisting of
2
atoms. Note that when
p
or
q
only has
1
atom in their spectra (i.e. is a real number), then
X
is normal, so the Brown measure can be trivially computed. In the course of studying these operators, we will deduce that when
p
and
q
have
2
atoms, then
X
is non-normal.

The key tool is the model of the von Neumann algebra generated by
2
projections from
[
14
]
to study the non-trivial part of this space. This reduces the computation in
(M,\tau)
to
\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,1),\nu^{*})\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{*}}[\frac{1}{2}\text{{tr}}]\right)
for some Borel probability measure
\nu^{*}
on
(0,1)
. To completely determine the Brown measure, we use freeness of
p
and
q
to explicitly compute
\nu^{*}
and some other relevant parameters.

We will show these measures are supported on hyperbolas in the complex plane and observe some other interesting properties about their atoms and symmetries.

One important aspect of the Brown measure is that it is the candidate for the limit of the empirical spectral distribution for random matrices: Given a random matrix model
X_{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, suppose that
X_{n}
converges in
*
-moments to
X\in(M,\tau)
. The Brown measure of
X
is typically the limit of the empirical spectral distributions of the
X_{n}
(see
[
11
]
for a precise statement of this). However, this does not always hold, as
[
10
, Chapter 11, Exercise 15]
provides a simple counterexample. On the other hand, two notable results that demonstrate this principle are the Circular Law in
[
13
]
and the Single Ring Theorem in
[
5
]
.

In a following paper, we will consider a natural random matrix model
X_{n}
corresponding to
X=p+iq
and prove that the empirical spectral distributions of the
X_{n}
converge to the Brown measure of
X
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the definition of the Brown measure, the model of the algebra generated by
2
projections, and some free probability transforms. In Section
3
, we fix some notation and give an outline of the steps of the computation. In Section
4
, we compute the Brown measure of
X
up to some parameters that depend on the joint law of
p
and
q
(Proposition
4.3
). In Section
5
, we use the freeness of
p
and
q
to compute these parameters (Propositions
5.3
and
5.6
) and also deduce that
X
is non-normal (Corollary
5.5
). In Section
6
, we combine these results to state the Brown measure of
X
(Theorem
6.1
). We also deduce some properties and provide accompanying figures to illustrate these properties.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Brown measure

In this subsection, we recall the definition of the Brown measure of an operator
X\in(M,\tau)
and describe how to compute it.

First, we recall the definition of the Fuglede-Kadison determinant, first introduced in
[
4
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
x\in(M,\tau)
. Let
\mu_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
be the spectral measure of
\left\lvert x\right\rvert=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
. Then, the
Fuglede-Kadison determinant
of
x
,
\Delta(x)
, is given by:

(1)

\Delta(x)=\exp\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\log t\,d\mu_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}(t)\right]\,.

When
x=X_{n}\in(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}})
, then
\Delta(X_{n})=\left\lvert\det(X_{n})\right\rvert^{1/n}
. Thus, the Fuglede-Kadison determinant is a generalization of the normalized, positive determinant of a complex matrix.

By applying the functional calculus to a decreasing sequence of continuous functions on
[0,\infty)
converging to
\log t
, we have:

(2)

\log\Delta(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log t\,d\mu_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log t\,d\mu_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}}(t)\,.

In practice, it is more convenient to compute the right-hand side to compute
\Delta(X)
.

Then, the Brown measure is the following distribution:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
x\in(M,\tau)
. The
Brown measure
of
x
is defined as:

(3)

\mu_{x}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\Delta(z-x)\,.

In
[
3
]
, some properties of the Brown measure are proven (see also
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
7
]
for more exposition on some of the basic results). In particular, the Brown measure of
x
is a probability measure supported on the spectrum of
x
. When
x
is normal, then the Brown measure is the spectral measure. When
x
is a random matrix, then the Brown measure is the empirical spectral distribution.

Lemma 2.3
.

The measure

Next, we note that on

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\tau(e\wedge(p\wedge q))=\tau(epe\wedge eqe)=\tau(epe)+\tau(eqe)-\tau(epe\vee eqe)

\displaystyle\geq\tau(epe)-\tau(eqe)-1>0\,.

(M,\tau)

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(M,\tau)

k(j)\neq k(j+1)

\tau(a_{i})=0

(15)

\tau(a_{1}\ldots,a_{n})=0\,.

Let

If

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{A_{i}}}

Brown Measure up to Weights and Measures

In this section, we will describe the Brown measure of
X=p+iq
as a convex combination of 4 atoms and another probability measure,
\mu^{\prime}
. Applying the model of the von Neumann algebra generated by
2
projections with the projections
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
defined in the previous section, observe that:

(39)

\displaystyle e_{00}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\{\alpha\}}(p)\wedge\chi_{\{\beta\}}(q)

\displaystyle e_{01}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\{\alpha\}}(p)\wedge\chi_{\{\beta^{\prime}\}}(q)

\displaystyle e_{10}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\{\alpha^{\prime}\}}(p)\wedge\chi_{\{\beta\}}(q)

\displaystyle e_{11}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\{\alpha^{\prime}\}}(p)\wedge\chi_{\{\beta^{\prime}\}}(q)

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=1-(e_{00}+e_{01}+e_{10}+e_{11})\,.

The measure
\mu^{\prime}
depends on the measure
\nu
in the isomorphism from the von Neumann algebra generated by
2
projections:
(eMe,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}})\cong\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,\pi/2),\nu)\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}}]\right)
. The weights in the convex combination are the weights
\tau(e_{ij}),\tau(e)
from Subsection
2.2
. We will determine the Brown measure up to determining the weights
\tau(e_{ij}),\tau(e)
and the measure
\nu
. These parameters depend on the joint law of
p
and
q
. In particular, the results in this section are valid for arbitrary projections
p,q\in(M,\tau)
. In Section
5
, we will compute these parameters when
p
and
q
are freely independent.

To compute the Brown measure of
X
, recall we need to first compute
\nu_{z}
, the spectral measure of
H_{z}(X)=(z-X)^{*}(z-X)
.

The result of the computation is the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

If
e=0
,

(40)

\nu_{z}=\tau(e_{00})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{01})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{10})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{11})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}\,.

If
e\neq 0
, there exists continuous functions
\sigma_{z,1},\sigma_{z,2}:(0,\pi/2)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\sigma_{z,1}(\theta),\sigma_{z,2}(\theta)
are the singular values of the element of
\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,\pi/2),\nu)\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}}]\right)
corresponding to
e(z-(p+iq))e\in(eMe,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}})
.

Then,

(41)

\displaystyle\nu_{z}

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{01})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{10})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+

\displaystyle\tau(e_{11})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e)\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\,.

Proof.

Let
H_{z}=H_{z}(X)=(z-X)^{*}(z-X)
. Since the
e_{ij},e
are central, mutually orthogonal projections that sum to
1
, then

(42)

\displaystyle(H_{z})^{n}

\displaystyle=(e_{00}+e_{01}+e_{10}+e_{11}+e)(H_{z})^{n}(e_{00}+e_{01}+e_{10}+e_{11}+e)

\displaystyle=e_{00}(H_{z})^{n}e_{00}+e_{01}(H_{z})^{n}e_{01}+e_{10}(H_{z})^{n}e_{10}+e_{11}(H_{z})^{n}e_{11}+e(H_{z})^{n}e

\displaystyle=(e_{00}H_{z}e_{00})^{n}+(e_{01}H_{z}e_{01})^{n}+(e_{10}H_{z}e_{10})^{n}+(e_{11}H_{z}e_{11})^{n}+(eH_{z}e)^{n}\,.

Taking traces of both sides,

(43)

\displaystyle\tau((H_{z})^{n})

\displaystyle=\tau((e_{00}H_{z}e_{00})^{n})+\tau((e_{01}H_{z}e_{01})^{n})+

\displaystyle\tau((e_{10}H_{z}e_{10})^{n})+\tau((e_{11}H_{z}e_{11})^{n})+\tau((eH_{z}e)^{n})\,.

From the definitions of the
e_{ij}
,

(44)

\displaystyle(e_{00}H_{z}e_{00})^{n}

\displaystyle=(\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2})^{n}e_{00}

\displaystyle(e_{01}H_{z}e_{01})^{n}

\displaystyle=(\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})^{n}e_{01}

\displaystyle(e_{10}H_{z}e_{10})^{n}

\displaystyle=(\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2})^{n}e_{10}

\displaystyle(e_{11}H_{z}e_{11})^{n}

\displaystyle=(\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})^{n}e_{11}\,.

Combining the previous two equations,

(45)

\displaystyle\tau((H_{z})^{n})

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})(\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2})^{n}

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{01})(\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})^{n}

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})(\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2})^{n}

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{11})(\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})^{n}

\displaystyle+\tau(e){\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}}((eH_{z}e)^{n})\,.

The right-hand side is the
n
-th moment of the following convex combination of probability measures:

(46)

\displaystyle\tau(e_{00})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{01})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{11})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e)\mu_{eH_{z}e}\,,

where
\mu_{eH_{z}e}
is the spectral measure of
eH_{z}e
in
\left(eMe,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}}\right)
.

As moments determine compact measures on
\mathbb{R}
, then this is
\nu_{z}
.

If
e=0
, then
\nu_{z}
is the desired convex combination of atoms.

If
e\neq 0
, consider
\mu_{eH_{z}e}
using the isomorphism described in Section
, i.e. we proceed by assuming that
(eMe,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}})=\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,\pi/2),\nu)\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}}]\right)
.

For any
m\in M
, let
\tilde{m}=eme\in eMe
. Then,
\tilde{H_{z}}=(z-(\tilde{p}+i\tilde{q}))^{*}(z-(\tilde{p}+i\tilde{q}))
.

Now, we claim that it is possible to choose continuous functions
\sigma_{z,1}(\theta),\sigma_{z,2}(\theta)
that are the singular values of
(z-(\tilde{p}+i\tilde{q}))=e(z-(p+iq))e
for
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. First, note that from the expressions for
\tilde{p^{\prime}},\tilde{q^{\prime}}
in Subsection
2.2
that
\tilde{H_{z}}
is continuous in
\theta
. Hence, the characteristic polynomial of
\tilde{H_{z}}
is a monic polynomial with coefficients that are continuous in
\theta
. From the quadratic formula, the eigenvalues of
\tilde{H_{z}}
have expressions in terms of the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
\tilde{H_{z}}
. As
\tilde{H_{z}}
are positive operators, then the eigenvalues are real, i.e. the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial is non-negative. Hence, the two branches of the square root of the discriminant are continuous in
\theta
. Thus, we may choose continuous expressions for the two eigenvalues of
\tilde{H_{z}}
. Taking square roots of these two non-negative continuous functions produces
\sigma_{z,1}(\theta),\sigma_{z,2}(\theta)
.

Thus,

(47)

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}}((\tilde{H_{z}})^{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{2}\text{{tr}}((\tilde{H_{z}})^{n})\,d\nu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta))^{n}+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))^{n}}{2}\,d\nu(\theta)\,.

The right-hand side is the
n
-th moment of the following measure:

(48)

\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\,.

Since moments determine compact measures on
\mathbb{R}
, then

(49)

\mu_{eH_{z}e}=\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\,.

By combining this with (
46
), we get the desired result:

(50)

\displaystyle\nu_{z}

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{01})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e_{10})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2}}+

\displaystyle\tau(e_{11})\delta_{\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}+\tau(e)\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\,.

∎

As an intermediate step in computing the Brown measure of
X
, we compute
\log\Delta(z-X)
:

Proposition 4.2
.

If
e=0
,

(51)

\displaystyle\log\Delta(z-X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(x)\,d\nu_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{01})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{11})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert.

If
e\neq 0
, there exists continuous functions
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}:(0,\pi/2)\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\lambda_{1}(\theta),\lambda_{2}(\theta)
are the eigenvalues of the element of
\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,\pi/2),\nu)\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}}]\right)
corresponding to
e(p+iq)e\in(eMe,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}})
.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha
and
\mathscr{B}=\beta^{\prime}-\beta
and
\sqrt{z}
denote the principal branch of the square root defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)
. Then,

(52)

\lambda_{1}(\theta)=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}+i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}+2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)}&\text{ when }\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert\\
\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}+i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{\mathscr{B}^{2}-\mathscr{A}^{2}-2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)}&\text{ when }\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert<\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert\end{cases}

(53)

\lambda_{2}(\theta)=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}+i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}+2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)}&\text{ when }\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert\\
\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}+i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{\mathscr{B}^{2}-\mathscr{A}^{2}-2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)}&\text{ when }\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert<\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert.\end{cases}

Then,

(54)

\displaystyle\log\Delta(z-X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(x)\,d\nu_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{01})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{11})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e)\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{1}(\theta)\right\rvert+\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{2}(\theta)\right\rvert}{2}\,d\nu(\theta)\,.

Proof.

When
e=0
, the formula for
\log\Delta(z-X)
follows easily from Proposition
4.1
.

For
e\neq 0
, from Proposition
4.1
, for any continuous
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
,

(55)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,d\nu_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})f(\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2})

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{01})f(\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})f(\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert^{2})

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{11})f(\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})

\displaystyle+\tau(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,d\left(\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\right)\,.

Rewriting the final integral using the change of variables formula,

(56)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,d\left(\frac{(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,d(\sigma_{z,1}^{2})_{*}(\nu)+\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)(\sigma_{z,2}^{2})_{*}(\nu)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\pi/2}f(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta))\,d\nu(\theta)+\int_{0}^{\pi/2}f(\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))\,d\nu(\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}f(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta))+f(\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))\,d\nu(\theta)\,.

Let
f_{n}(x)=\log(x+1/n)/2
for
n=1,2,\ldots
. Then,
f_{n}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous and decrease to
\log(x)/2
. Applying two previous equations for
f=f_{n}
and using the monotone convergence theorem to take the limit as
n\to\infty
,

(57)

\displaystyle\log\Delta(z-X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(x)\,d\nu_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{01})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{11})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau(e)}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\log(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta))+\log(\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))}{2}\,d\nu(\theta)\,.

For the rest of the proof, assume that
(eMe,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{eMe}})=\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,\pi/2),\nu)\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}}]\right)
.

Recall that
\sigma_{z,1}(\theta),\sigma_{z,2}(\theta)
are the singular values of
e(z-X)e
. Then, the integrand of the final integral can be simplified as:

(58)

\displaystyle\frac{\log(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta))+\log(\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta)\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(\det((e(z-X)e)^{*}e(z-X)e))}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(\left\lvert\det((e(z-X)e))\right\rvert^{2})}{2}

\displaystyle=\log\left\lvert\det((e(z-X)e))\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\log\left\lvert\det(z-eXe)\right\rvert.

Given that we can verify the formulas for
\lambda_{1}(\theta)
,
\lambda_{2}(\theta)
, then
z-\lambda_{1}(\theta),z-\lambda_{2}(\theta)
are the eigenvalues for
z-eXe
. Thus, combining the previous two equations,

(59)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\log(\sigma_{z,1}^{2}(\theta))+\log(\sigma_{z,2}^{2}(\theta))}{2}\,d\nu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log\left\lvert\det(z-eXe)\right\rvert\,d\nu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{1}(\theta)\right\rvert+\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{2}(\theta)\right\rvert\,d\nu(\theta)\,.

Substituting this expression into (
57
) produces the desired formula for
\log\Delta(z-X)
.

We return to verifying the formulas for
\lambda_{1}(\theta)
,
\lambda_{2}(\theta)
. Straightforward computation shows that the characteristic polynomial of
eXe\in\left(M_{2}\left(L^{\infty}((0,\pi/2),\nu)\right),\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\frac{1}{n}\text{{tr}}]\right)
is:

(60)

p(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-((\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})+i(\beta+\beta^{\prime}))\lambda+\left(\alpha\alpha^{\prime}-\beta\beta^{\prime}+\frac{i}{2}((\alpha+\alpha^{\prime})(\beta+\beta^{\prime})-\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta))\right).

The eigenvalues of
eXe
are:

(61)

\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}+i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}+2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)}\,.

where the square root is any branch of the square root.

When
\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert
, then
\text{{Re}}(\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}+2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta))\geq 0
and the expression for the eigenvalues of
e(p+iq)e
is continuous and well-defined if we take the square root to be the principal branch of the square root. This gives the formulas for
\lambda_{1}(\theta),\lambda_{2}(\theta)
for
\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert
.

When
\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert<\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert
, then
\pm i\sqrt{\mathscr{B}^{2}-\mathscr{A}^{2}-2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)}
are also expressions for the square roots of
\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}+2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta)
, where the square root is the principal branch of the square root. Since
\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert<\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert
, then
\text{{Re}}(\mathscr{B}^{2}-\mathscr{A}^{2}-2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\cos(2\theta))>0
and this expression is continuous and well-defined.. This gives the formulas for
\lambda_{1}(\theta),\lambda_{2}(\theta)
for
\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert<\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert
.
∎

Finally, we compute the Brown measure of
X
,
\mu=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\Delta(z-X)
in the following Proposition:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be the Brown measure of
X
.
If
e=0
,

(62)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\Delta(z-X)

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\delta_{\alpha+i\beta}+\tau(e_{01})\delta_{\alpha+i\beta^{\prime}}+\tau(e_{10})\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta}+\tau(e_{11})\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}}.

If
e\neq 0
, let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}:(0,\pi/2)\to\mathbb{C}
be as in Proposition
4.2
. Then,

(63)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\Delta(z-X)

\displaystyle=\tau(e_{00})\delta_{\alpha+i\beta}+\tau(e_{01})\delta_{\alpha+i\beta^{\prime}}+\tau(e_{10})\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta}+\tau(e_{11})\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\tau(e)\mu^{\prime}\,,

where

(64)

\mu^{\prime}=\frac{(\lambda_{1})_{*}(\nu)+(\lambda_{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}\,.

Additionally,
\mu^{\prime}(\{\alpha+i\beta,\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta,\alpha+i\beta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}\})=0
.

Proof.

If
e=0
, the result follows from directly applying
\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\left\lvert\cdot-\lambda\right\rvert=\delta_{\lambda}
to the expression for
\log\Delta(z-X)
in Proposition
4.2
.

If
e\neq 0
, we take the distributional Laplacian of the result from Proposition
4.2
:

(65)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\Delta(z-X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(x)\,d\nu_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\biggl(\tau(e_{00})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{01})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{10})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta)\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e_{11})\log\left\lvert z-(\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime})\right\rvert

\displaystyle+\tau(e)\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{1}(\theta)\right\rvert+\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{2}(\theta)\right\rvert}{2}\,d\nu(\theta)\biggr).

As in the case when
e=0
, applying
\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\left\lvert\cdot-\lambda\right\rvert=\delta_{\lambda}
directly to the first
4
atomic terms of
\log\Delta(z-X)
produces the weighted sum of the
4
atoms in
\mu
.

To apply
\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\log\left\lvert\cdot-\lambda\right\rvert=\delta_{\lambda}
for the final integral, we apply Fubini’s theorem. Consider
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
. Since
\log\left\lvert z-w\right\rvert\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{C})
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}
, then we may apply Fubini’s theorem for
i=1,2
:

(66)

\displaystyle\langle f,\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{i}(\theta)\right\rvert\,d\nu(\theta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla^{2}f,\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{i}(\theta)\right\rvert\,d\nu(\theta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\nabla^{2}f(z)\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{i}(\theta)\right\rvert\,d\nu(\theta)\right)\,d\lambda(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}f(z)\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{i}(\theta)\right\rvert\,dd\lambda(z)\right)\,d\nu(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}f(\lambda_{i}(\theta))\,d\nu(\theta)\,.

Hence, for
i=1,2
,

(67)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{i}(\theta)\right\rvert\,d\nu(\theta)=(\lambda_{i})_{*}(\nu)\,.

Thus, the Laplacian of the integral is:

(68)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{1}(\theta)\right\rvert+\log\left\lvert z-\lambda_{2}(\theta)\right\rvert}{2}\,d\nu(\theta)=\frac{(\lambda_{1})_{*}(\nu)+(\lambda_{2})_{*}(\nu)}{2}=\mu^{\prime}.

Combining this with the atomic terms gives the desired Brown measure for
X
.

For the final note, it follows from Lemma
2.3
and the fact that
\lambda_{i}(\{0,\pi/2\})=\{\alpha+i\beta,\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta,\alpha+i\beta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}\}
that

(69)

\mu^{\prime}(\{\alpha+i\beta,\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta,\alpha+i\beta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}\})=\nu(\{0,\pi/2\})=0\,.

∎

Even though the weights
\tau(e_{ij}),\tau(e)
and the measure
\nu
have not been determined yet, we can already say something about the support of the Brown measure in general.

First, we prove a lemma about a relevant hyperbola and rectangle:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
. Let
\mathscr{A}=\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha
and
\mathscr{B}=\beta^{\prime}-\beta
.

Let

(70)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\left\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:\left(x-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(y-\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\left\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x\in[\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime},\alpha\vee\alpha^{\prime}],y\in[\beta\wedge\beta^{\prime},\beta\vee\beta^{\prime}]\right\}\,.

The equation of
H
is equivalent to:

(71)

(x-\alpha)(x-\alpha^{\prime})=(y-\beta)(y-\beta^{\prime})\,.

The equation of
H
in coordinates

(72)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=x-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}

\displaystyle y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=y-\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}

is

(73)

(x^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}}{4}=(y^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\mathscr{B}^{2}}{4}.

It follows that for
(x,y)\in H
,

(74)

(x,y)\in R\iff(\ref{eqn:lem:hyperbola_rectangle})\leq 0\iff x\in[\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime},\alpha\vee\alpha^{\prime}]\text{ or }y\in[\beta\wedge\beta^{\prime},\beta\vee\beta^{\prime}]\,.

Similarly,

(75)

(x,y)\in\accentset{\circ}{R}\iff(\ref{eqn:lem:hyperbola_rectangle})<0\iff x\in(\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime},\alpha\vee\alpha^{\prime})\text{ or }y\in(\beta\wedge\beta^{\prime},\beta\vee\beta^{\prime})\,.

Alternatively, the equation of the hyperbola is:

(76)

\text{{Re}}\left(\left(z-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}-i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\right)=\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}}{4}\,.

If
z\in H
, then

if and only if

(77)

\left\lvert\text{{Im}}\left(\left(z-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}-i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\right\rvert}{2}\,.

Proof.

The equivalent equations for the hyperbola are straightforward to check.

The equivalences for the closed conditions follow from the following equivalences and the equation of the hyperbola in
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
coordinates

(78)

\displaystyle(x,y)\in R

\displaystyle\iff(x^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}}{4}\leq 0\text{ and }(y^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\mathscr{B}^{2}}{4}\leq 0

(79)

\displaystyle(x^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}}{4}\leq 0

\displaystyle\iff\left\lvert x^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert}{2}

\displaystyle\iff x\in[\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime},\alpha\vee\alpha^{\prime}]

\displaystyle(y^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\mathscr{B}^{2}}{4}\leq 0

\displaystyle\iff\left\lvert y^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert}{2}

\displaystyle\iff y\in[\beta\wedge\beta^{\prime},\beta\vee\beta^{\prime}]\,.

The equivalences for the open conditions follow from similar equivalences with the closed conditions replaced by open conditions.

The last equation of the hyperbola follows from direct computation. For the inequality of the rectangle, observe that

(80)

\text{{Im}}\left(\left(z-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}-i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\right)=2x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,.

In light of what was previously shown,

(81)

x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\right\rvert}{4}\Longrightarrow x^{\prime}\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert}{2}\text{ or }y^{\prime}\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert}{2}\Longrightarrow z\in R\,.

Conversely,

(82)

z\in R\Longrightarrow x^{\prime}\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\right\rvert}{2}\text{ and }y^{\prime}\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{B}\right\rvert}{2}\Longrightarrow x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\right\rvert}{4}\,.

∎

\displaystyle p^{\prime}

Recall from Subsection

\displaystyle\tau(e_{00})

\displaystyle\tau(e_{01})

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
p,q\in(M,\tau)
be two freely independent projections with
\tau(p)=a
,
\tau(q)=b
,
a,b\in(0,1)
. Then,

(95)

\displaystyle\psi_{p}(z)=\frac{az}{1-z}

\displaystyle\psi_{q}(z)=\frac{bz}{1-z}

\displaystyle\chi_{p}(w)=\frac{w}{w+a}

\displaystyle\chi_{q}(w)=\frac{w}{w+b}

(96)

\chi_{pqp}(w)=\frac{w(1+w)}{(w+a)(w+b)}\,.

Let

(97)

f(z)=1+(4ab-2(a+b))z+(a-b)^{2}z^{2}\,.

Then,
\psi_{pqp},\psi_{(1-p)(1-q)(1-p)}
are analytic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty)
and

(98)

\displaystyle\psi_{pqp}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-(a+b)z-\sqrt{f(z)}}{2(z-1)}

\displaystyle\psi_{(1-p)(1-q)(1-p)}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-(2-a-b)z-\sqrt{f(z)}}{2(z-1)}\,,

where
\sqrt{f(z)}
is an analytic branch of the square root of
f(z)
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,\infty)
where
\sqrt{1}=+1
. In particular, the root(s) of
f(z)
are in
[1,\infty)
and are distinct when

is quadratic.

Proof.

The formula for
\psi_{p}
follows from

(99)

\mu_{p}=(1-a)\delta_{0}+a\delta_{1}

and similarly for
\psi_{q}
. Since
\psi_{p}

\psi_{q}
are linear fractional transformations, the formulas for their inverses (

,

Since

and

are freely independent, then the formula for

follows from the multiplicativity of the

Since
\left\lVert pqp\right\rVert\leq 1

. So, the formula

\psi_{pqp}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{tz}{1-tz}\,d\mu_{pqp}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{tz}{1-tz}\,d\mu_{pqp}(t)

defines an analytic function for

.

To compute the formula for

in a neighborhood of

z=\chi_{pqp}(w)=\frac{w(1+w)}{(w+a)(w+b)}

This is clearly true for

This last equation is true if and only if

(z-1)w^{2}+(z(a+b)-1)w+zab=0\,.

Fixing a

Now, we proceed to determine
\tau(e_{ij}),\tau(e)
. First, we need the following Lemma
[
10

:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mu
be a finite measure on the real line and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For a sequence
z_{n}\to s
non-tangentially to
\mathbb{R}
,
(z_{n}-s)G_{\mu}(z_{n})\to\mu(\{s\})
.

Proof.

Let
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}
. The condition that
z_{n}\to s
non-tangentially to
\mathbb{R}
is equivalent to
\left\lvert(x_{n}-s)/y_{n}\right\rvert\leq M
for some
M
. In particular,
y_{n}\neq 0
.

Computation shows that

(104)

(z_{n}-s)G_{\mu}(z_{n})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{z_{n}-s}{z_{n}-t}\,d\mu(t)\,.

Let
f_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
where

(105)

f_{n}(t)=\frac{z_{n}-s}{z_{n}-t}\,.

Note that
f_{n}(s)=1
for all
n
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t)=0
for all
t\neq s
. It suffices to show that the
f_{n}(t)
are uniformly bounded, as then the result follows from the bounded convergence theorem.

First, rewrite
f_{n}(t)
:

(106)

f_{n}(t)=\frac{z_{n}-s}{z_{n}-t}=\frac{(x_{n}-s)+iy_{n}}{(x_{n}-t)+iy_{n}}=\frac{(x_{n}-s)/y_{n}+i}{(x_{n}-t)/y_{n}+i}\,.

Since
\left\lvert(x_{n}-s)/y_{n}+i\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert(x_{n}-s)/y_{n}\right\rvert+1=1+M
and
\left\lvert(x_{n}-t)/y_{n}+i\right\rvert\geq 1
,

(107)

\left\lvert f_{n}(t)\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert(x_{n}-s)/y_{n}+i\right\rvert}{\left\lvert(x_{n}-t)/y_{n}+i\right\rvert}\leq\frac{1+M}{1}=1+M\,.

as desired.
∎

Next, we determine
\tau(e_{ij}),\tau(e)
using the free independence of

:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
p,q\in(M,\tau)
be two freely independent projections with

,
\tau(q)=b

(108)

\displaystyle\tau(e_{00})

\displaystyle=\tau((1-p)\wedge(1-q))=\max(0,(1-a)+(1-b)-1)

\displaystyle\tau(e_{01})

\displaystyle=\tau((1-p)\wedge q)=\max(0,(1-a)+b-1)

\displaystyle\tau(e_{10})

\displaystyle=\tau(p\wedge(1-q))=\max(0,a+(1-b)-1)

\displaystyle\tau(e_{11})

\displaystyle=\tau(p\wedge q)=\max(0,a+b-1)

\displaystyle\tau(e)

\displaystyle=1-(\tau(e_{00})+\tau(e_{01})+\tau(e_{10})+\tau(e_{11}))\,.

Proof.

By replacing
p
with
1-p
and/or
q
with
1-q
, it suffices to just prove

(109)

\tau(p\wedge q)=\max(0,a+b-1)\,.

Recall that
\tau((pqp)^{n})\to\tau(p\wedge q)
as

. Since

on
[0,1]

. Hence,

(110)

\tau(p\wedge q)=\mu_{pqp}(\{1\})\,.

We proceed to use Proposition
5.1
and Lemma

to determine
\mu_{pqp}(\{1\})
.

In general, for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(pqp)
,

(111)

G_{pqp}(z)=\frac{1}{z}\left(\psi_{pqp}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)+1\right)\,.

From Proposition

, the right-hand side of this equation is:

(112)

\frac{1}{z}\left(\psi_{pqp}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)+1\right)=\frac{z+(a+b-2)+z\sqrt{f(1/z)}}{2z}\,.

and is defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,1]
. Since
\sigma(pqp)\subset[0,1]
, then

is also defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,1]
, so then the following equality holds for

(113)

G_{pqp}(z)=\frac{z+(a+b-2)+z\sqrt{f(1/z)}}{2z(z-1)}\,.

Thus, we may use this formula and Lemma
5.2
to obtain:

(114)

\displaystyle\mu_{pqp}(\{1\})

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{z\to 1}(z-1)G_{pqp}(z)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{z\to 1}\frac{z+(a+b-2)+z\sqrt{f(1/z)}}{2z}

\displaystyle=\frac{a+b-1+\left\lvert a+b-1\right\rvert}{2}

\displaystyle=\max(0,a+b-1)\,.

Proposition

is just the minimum trace of the intersection between two projections

and
\tau(q)=b

Recall from the parallelogram law that for projections

\tau(p\wedge q)=\tau(p)+\tau(q)-\tau(p\vee q)\geq\tau(p)-\tau(q)-1=a+b-1\,.

Corollary 5.4

Let

Proof.

If one of

Suppose that none of

is

Consider the expressions of

\tau(e_{11})

\tau(e_{00})+\tau(e_{11})=\max(0,-(a+b-1))+\max(0,a+b-1)=\left\lvert a+b-1\right\rvert.

Similarly,

(117)

\tau(e_{01})+\tau(e_{10})=\max(0,b-a)+\max(0,a-b)=\left\lvert a-b\right\rvert.

Thus,

(118)

\tau(e)=0\iff\left\lvert a+b-1\right\rvert+\left\lvert a-b\right\rvert=1\,.

By considering the four cases of
\left\lvert a+b-1\right\rvert=\pm(a+b-1)

\left\lvert a-b\right\rvert=\pm(a-b)

\left\lvert a+b-1\right\rvert+\left\lvert a-b\right\rvert=1

\tau(e)\neq 0

Finally, we consider when the density of
\mu^{\prime}
extends all the way to the 4 corners of the intersection of the hyperbola with the boundary of the rectangle:

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\mu
be the Brown measure of
p+iq
where
p
and
q
have
2
atoms

(1)

\mu^{\prime}
has density extending to
\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta
and
\alpha+i\beta^{\prime}
if and only if
a=b
.

(2)

\mu^{\prime}
has density extending to
\alpha+i\beta
and
\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}
if and only if
a=1-b
.

(3)

\mu^{\prime}
has density extending to all
4
corners of the intersection of the hyperbola with the boundary of the rectangle if and only if
a=b=1/2
. Hence, the support of
\mu
is equal to
H\cap R
if and only if
a=b=1/2
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha
and
\mathscr{B}=\beta^{\prime}-\beta
.

(1)

For
z=\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta,\alpha+i\beta^{\prime}
,

(148)

\left(z-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}-i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}-2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}}{4}\,.

Hence,
z=\lambda_{i}(\pi/2)
.

Thus, it is equivalent to determine for which
a,b
the measure
\nu
has density approaching
\pi/2
. Note that
\nu
has density approaching
\pi/2
if and only if
f(\sec^{2}\theta)<0
for
\theta\to\pi/2^{-}
. Note that
\lim\limits_{\theta\to\pi/2^{-}}\sec^{2}\theta=\infty
. Recall that
f(z)
is a polynomial of degree at most
2
. If
f
is quadratic, then
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}f(z)=\infty
, so then
\nu
does not have density approaching
\pi/2
. Hence, we must have
a=b
. Conversely, if
a=b
, then
f(z)=1+4(a^{2}-a)z=1+4(a-1)az
, and the linear term has negative coefficient for
a\in(0,1)
. Thus,
f(z)<0
as
z\to\infty
and
\nu
has density approaching
\pi/2
.

(2)

For
z=\alpha+i\beta,\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}
,

(149)

\left(z-\frac{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}}{2}-i\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{\mathscr{A}^{2}-\mathscr{B}^{2}+2i\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}}{4}\,.

Hence,
z=\lambda_{i}(0)
.

Thus, it is equivalent to determine for which
a,b
the measure
\nu
has density approaching
0
. Note that
\nu
has density approaching
0
if and only if
f(\sec^{2}\theta)<0
for
\theta\to 0^{+}
. Since
\lim\limits_{\theta\to 0^{+}}\sec^{2}\theta=1
, then we need that
f(x)<0
for
x\to 1^{+}
. Since
f(1)=(a+b-1)^{2}
, then
f(1)=0
, i.e.
a=1-b
. Conversely, if
a=1-b
, then
f(1)=0
and since
f
is either quadratic with positive leading coefficient or
f
is linear with negative slope, then
f
must be negative to the right of
1
. Note that in the case where
f
is quadratic,
f
cannot have a double root at
1
(from Proposition
5.1
).

(3)

Follows from 1. and 2.

Finally, we conclude that the Brown measure of
X=p+iq
uniquely determines the laws of
p
and
q
. Here we allow
p
and
q
to be possibly constant.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
p,q\in(M,\tau)
be Hermitian and freely independent and

(150)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}

\displaystyle=a\delta_{\alpha}+(1-a)\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\mu_{q}

\displaystyle=b\delta_{\beta}+(1-b)\delta_{\beta^{\prime}}\,.

where
a,b\in[0,1]
and
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mu
be the Brown measure of
X=p+iq
. Then, the assignment
(\mu_{p},\mu_{q})\mapsto\mu
is 1 to 1.

Proof.

From Corollary
6.3
,
\mu
is atomic if and only if one of
p
or
q
is constant. In this case, it is easy to determine the weights and atoms of both
\mu_{p}
and
\mu_{q}
from
\mu
.

Thus, we may consider
\mu
which is the Brown measure of
X=p+iq
where
p
and
q
are not constant.

First, we show that
\mu
determines the positions of the atoms of
p
and
q
. Since the support of
\mu^{\prime}
on
H\cap R
contains at least
5
points, then the equation of
H
is uniquely determined. If
a=b
or
a+b=1
, then from Corollary
6.5
, we can determine the positions of the atoms of
p
and
q
. Thus, assume that
a\neq b
and
a+b\neq 1
. From Corollary
6.3
,
\mu
has
2
atoms at the points
\{\alpha+i\beta,\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta,\alpha+i\beta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime}\}
. From these two points, at least
3
of
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}
are determined. To determine the last one, we can use the equation of the hyperbola and look at either the coefficient of
x
or
y
. Thus,
\mu
determines the positions of the atoms of
p
and
q
.

We can determine the weights of the atoms of
p
and
q
directly from Proposition
4.7
.

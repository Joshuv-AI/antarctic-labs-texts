Monotone Submodular Multiway Partition
Richard Bi, Karthekeyan Chandrasekaran, Soham Joshi

Abstract
In submodular multiway partition ( Sub-MP ), the input is a non-negative submodular function f : 2 V → ℝ ≥ 0 f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} given by an evaluation oracle along with k k terminals t 1 , t 2 , … , t k ∈ V t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{k}\in V . The goal is to find a partition V 1 , V 2 , … , V k V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k} of V V with t i ∈ V i t_{i}\in V_{i} for every i ∈ [ k ] i\in[k] in order to minimize ∑ i = 1 k f ⁡ ( V i ) \sum_{i=1}^{k}f(V_{i}) . In this work, we focus on Sub-MP when the input function is monotone (termed Mono-Sub-MP ). Mono-Sub-MP formulates partitioning problems over several interesting structures—e.g., matrices, matroids, graphs, and hypergraphs. Mono-Sub-MP is NP-hard since the graph multiway cut problem can be cast as a special case. We investigate the approximability of Mono-Sub-MP : we show that it admits a 4 / 3 4/3 -approximation and does not admit a ( 10 / 9 − ϵ ) (10/9-\epsilon) -approximation for every constant ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 . Next, we study a special case of Mono-Sub-MP where the monotone submodular function of interest is the coverage function of an input graph, termed Graph-Coverage-MP . Graph-Coverage-MP is equivalent to the classic multiway cut problem for the purposes of exact optimization. We show that Graph-Coverage-MP admits a 1.125 1.125 -approximation and does not admit a ( 1.00074 − ϵ ) (1.00074-\epsilon) -approximation for every constant ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 assuming the Unique Games Conjecture. These results separate Graph-Coverage-MP from graph multiway cut in terms of approximability.

Motivations and Connections.

We motivate
Mono-Sub-MP
via three important special cases:
(1) In Matrix Multiway Partition, denoted
Matrix-MP
, the input is a matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
along with
k
specified rows
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{k}\in[n]
and the goal is to partition the
n
rows into
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
with
r_{i}\in V_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
in order to minimize the sum of the dimensions of the subspaces spanned by the parts, i.e., minimize
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\text{dimension}(V_{i})
. This is a special case of
Mono-Sub-MP
since the dimension function is non-negative, normalized, monotone, and submodular.
Matrix-MP
is a fundamental linear algebra problem and is likely to have applications in data analysis.
(2) In Matroid Multiway Partition, denoted
Matroid-MP
, the input is the rank function
r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
of a matroid (given by an evaluation oracle) along with a collection
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in V
of elements. The goal is to partition the ground set
V
into
k
parts
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
with
t_{i}\in V_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
in order to minimize
\sum_{i=1}^{k}r(V_{i})
.
Matroid-MP
is a special case of
Mono-Sub-MP
since the matroid rank function is non-negative, normalized, monotone, and submodular. We note that
Matrix-MP
is a special case of
Matroid-MP
.
(3) In Coverage Multiway Partition, denoted
Coverage-MP
, the input is a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
along with
k
terminals
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{k}\in V
and the goal is to partition the vertex set into
k
parts
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
in order to minimize the sum of the coverage values of the parts, i.e., minimize
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b(V_{i})
, where
b(X)
is the number of hyperedges in
H
intersecting the vertices in
X
.
Coverage-MP
is a special case of
Mono-Sub-MP
since the coverage function
b\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is non-negative, normalized, monotone, and submodular. We emphasize that all three special cases mentioned above are themselves fairly general and numerous other partitioning problems can be cast as special cases of these problems as well. Thus, our motivation behind studying
Mono-Sub-MP
is to understand whether we can get good approximations for all these problems simultaneously.

In
Coverage-MP
, if the input hypergraph is simply a graph, then we denote the resulting problem as
Graph-Coverage-MP
.
Graph-Coverage-MP
is an important special case of
Mono-Sub-MP
, so we will study this special case as well in this work.

Graph-Coverage-MP
is closely related to the well-studied graph multiway cut problem (denoted
Graph-Multiway-Cut
). The input in both problems is a graph with edge weights
G=(V,E,w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+})
and a collection of
k

t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in V
.
The goal in both problems is to find a partition
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
of the vertex set with
t_{i}\in V_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
in order to minimize an objective function.
The objective function in
Graph-Coverage-MP
is
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b(S_{i})
, where
b\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the graph coverage function defined as
b(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{e\in E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}e\cap S\neq\emptyset}w_{e}
for all
S\subseteq V
.
The objective function in
Graph-Multiway-Cut
is
(1/2)\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(S_{i})
, where
d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the graph cut function defined as
d(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{e\in E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}e\cap S\neq\emptyset,e\setminus S\neq\emptyset}w_{e}
for all
S\subseteq V
. We recall that the graph coverage function is monotone submodular while the graph cut function is symmetric submodular. Moreover, the objective in
Graph-Coverage-MP
is a translation of the objective in
Graph-Multiway-Cut

Graph-Coverage-MP
is NP-hard (since
Graph-Multiway-Cut
is known to be NP-hard). However, there is no approximation preserving reduction between
Graph-Multiway-Cut
and
Graph-Coverage-MP
(in either direction). This situation raises the following question: to what extent do the approximation and inapproximability techniques for
Graph-Multiway-Cut
extend to
Graph-Coverage-MP

. We refer the reader to Figure

Approximability.

Sub-MP
,

and

Chekuri and Ene

6

introduced the Lovász extension based convex programming relaxation for

Sub-MP
and
Sym-Sub-MP

In subsequent work, Ene, Vondrák, and Wu
[
10

improved the rounding algorithm for
Sub-MP
and showed that it achieves a
(2-2/k)
-approximation.
On the hardness side, Ene, Vondrák, and Wu
[
10
]
showed that for every constant
\epsilon>0
, (1) a
(2-2/k-\epsilon)
-approximation for
Sub-MP
requires exponentially many function evaluation queries and (2) a
(1.268-\epsilon)
-approximation for
Sym-Sub-MP
requires exponentially many function evaluation queries.

As mentioned above,

is NP-hard and consequently,

As mentioned in the previous paragraph, two independent prior approaches for

Given this simple and linear-time

1.1
Results and Techniques

As mentioned previously, there are three known
2
-approximations for
Mono-Sub-MP
—the greedy splitting approach, the convex program approach, and the simple linear-time greedy approach described above. Our first result improves the approximability of
Mono-Sub-MP
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Mono-Sub-MP
admits a
4/3
-approximation.

This result is based on rounding an optimum solution to the Lovász extension based convex program for
Sub-MP
introduced by Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
.
We use a threshold rounding algorithm similar to Chekuri and Ene. However, the interval for the threshold that we use is different from theirs. For
Sub-MP
, Chekuri and Ene pick a uniform random threshold from the interval
[1/2,1]
in order to ensure that sets obtained by rounding do not overlap and hence, give a partition. For
Sym-Sub-MP
, they pick a uniform random threshold from the interval
[0,1]
which leads to overlapping sets, but these sets can be uncrossed to obtain a partition without increasing the objective value owing to the posimodularity property of symmetric submodular functions. For
Mono-Sub-MP
, we pick a uniform random threshold from the interval
[1/4,1]
. This again leads to overlapping sets, but we uncross these sets arbitrarily without increasing the objective by exploiting the monotonicity of the function. We emphasize that our approximation factor analysis differs considerably from that of Chekuri and Ene as well as the subsequent work of Ene, Vondrák, and Wu
[
10
]
(although we use some of their ingredients). Our analysis differs since our threshold interval is different and we need to exploit monotonicity to bound the approximation factor. Moreover, the approximation factor that we achieve for
Mono-Sub-MP
is
4/3
which is smaller than the known approximation factor for
Sub-MP
and
Sym-Sub-MP
.
We show that the approximation factor analysis of our algorithm is tight via an example. Hence, improving on the approximability requires new ideas.

Our next result shows a lower bound on the approximability assuming that the algorithm makes polynomial number of function evaluation queries.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every constant
\epsilon>0
, every algorithm for
Mono-Sub-MP
that achieves an approximation factor of
(\frac{10}{9}-\epsilon)
requires
2^{\Omega(n)}
function evaluation queries, where
n
is the size of the ground set.

This lower bound result on the inapproximability of
Mono-Sub-MP
is based on the
symmetry gap
machinery introduced by Vondrák
[
17
]
.
Vondrák introduced the symmetry gap machinery to show
inapproximability results
in the valuation oracle input model (i.e., assuming that the algorithm makes only polynomial number of function evaluation queries)
for constrained submodular
maximization
problems. Subsequently, Ene, Vondrák, and Wu
[
10
]
adapted this machinery to show
inapproximability results in the valuation oracle model for

Sub-MP
and
Sym-Sub-MP
, which are
minimization
problems related to submodular objectives.
Informally, the symmetry gap of a function relative to an optimization problem is the largest ratio between the objective values of an optimum symmetric solution (under an appropriately chosen notion of symmetry) and an optimum solution to the problem.
We adapt Vondrák’s
[
17
]
and Ene, Vondrák, and Wu’s
[
10
]
machinery in a straightforward way to conclude that the symmetry gap of
Mono-Sub-MP
is a lower bound on its inapproximability factor. Given this status, the
technical challenge in using
the symmetry gap machinery for inapproximability involves
constructing instances of the problem which have large symmetry gap. This is our key contribution: we construct an instance of
Mono-Sub-MP
that has symmetry gap at least
10/9
. We note that our analysis of the symmetry gap of our instance is tight. Thus, improving the inapproximability factor beyond
10/9
via the symmetry gap machinery requires fundamentally new types of instances.
We mention that the symmetry gap machinery allows query complexity based inapproximability results to be converted to computation based inapproximability results assuming NP
\neq
RP
[
9
]
.

Next, we turn to
Graph-Coverage-MP
which is a special case of
Mono-Sub-MP
.
Its close relationship to
Graph-Multiway-Cut
inspires our results. We note that NP-hardness of
Graph-Multiway-Cut
implies the NP-hardness of
Graph-Coverage-MP
.
It is easy to show that if
Graph-Multiway-Cut
admits an
\alpha

admits a
(1+\alpha)/2
-approximation (see Proposition

1.2965
-approximation for
Graph-Multiway-Cut
implies a
1.14825
-approximation for

. We improve the approximability of
Graph-Coverage-MP
beyond this trivial guarantee.

Theorem 1.3
.

Graph-Coverage-MP
admits a
1.125
-approximation.

Our algorithm for
Graph-Coverage-MP
is based on rounding an optimum solution to a linear programming relaxation.
The linear programming relaxation associated with
Graph-Coverage-MP
has a different objective compared to that of
Graph-Multiway-Cut
, but the constraints are the same. Buchbinder, Naor, and Schwartz
[
3
]
designed the exponential clocks rounding scheme for the LP-relaxation of

and showed that it achieves an approximation factor of
1.32388

[

to show that it achieves a

Next, we turn to the hardness of
Graph-Coverage-MP
. It is known that
Graph-Multiway-Cut

For

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a monotone submodular function and
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{k}\}\subseteq V

OPT
be defined as

\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(V_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}\text{ is a partition of }V\text{ such that }t_{i}\in V_{i}\ \forall\ i\in[k]\right\}.

Let

be an ordering of the terminals such that
f(\{t_{1}\})\leq f(\{t_{2}\})\leq\ldots\leq f(\{t_{k}\})

f\left(\left(V\setminus T\right)\cup\{t_{k}\}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}f\left(\{t_{i}\}\right)\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{k}\right)\textsc{OPT}.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle f\left(\left(V\setminus T\right)\cup\{t_{k}\}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}f\left(\{t_{i}\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq f(V)+(1-1/k)\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(\{t_{i}\})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\left(2-\frac{1}{k}\right)\max\left\{f(V),\sum_{i=1}^{k}f\left(\{t_{i}\}\right)\right\}.

(1)

Next, we lower bound the objective value of an optimum partition.
Let
V_{1}^{*}
,
V_{2}^{*}
,
\ldots
,
V_{k}^{*}
be an optimum partition with
t_{i}\in V_{i}^{*}

i\in[k]

\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(V_{i}^{*})\geq f(V)

since

by monotonicity of

and the fact that

for every

. Consequently,

OPT

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{f(V),\sum_{i=1}^{k}f\left(\{t_{i}\}\right)\right\}.

(2)

The proposition follows from inequalities (
1
) and (
2
).

2.1
Approximation Algorithm

In this section, we describe our algorithm for
Mono-Sub-MP
and analyze its approximation factor. Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the input monotone submodular function with terminals
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in V
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{V}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we define
\mathbf{x}^{\theta}\in\{0,1\}^{V}
as

(\mathbf{x}^{\theta})_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\mathbf{x}_{v}\geq\theta,\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

We also define
f\left(\mathbf{x}^{\theta}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f\left(\{v\in V\colon(\mathbf{x}^{\theta})_{v}=1\}\right)
. We define the Lovász extension
\hat{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
f
as follows: For all
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{V}
,
\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\theta\in[0,1]}\left[f(\mathbf{x}^{\theta})\right]=\int_{0}^{1}f\left(\mathbf{x}^{\theta}\right)d\theta
. We define
\mathbf{x}_{i}=\left(x(v_{1},i),\ldots,x(v_{n},i)\right)
and consider the convex program below:

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{f}\left(\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)

(Sub-MP-Rel)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}x(v,i)=1

\displaystyle\quad\forall v\in V

\displaystyle x(t_{i},i)=1

\displaystyle\quad\forall i\in[k]

\displaystyle x(v,i)\geq 0

\displaystyle\quad\forall v\in V,i\in[k].

The convex program is identical to the convex programming relaxation of
Sub-MP
introduced by Chekuri and Ene and it can be solved in polynomial time
[
6
]
.
We present a rounding algorithm for
Mono-Sub-MP
based on this relaxation. Our rounding algorithm is similar to the
\theta
-rounding algorithms of Chekuri and Ene for
Sub-MP
and
Sym-Sub-MP

[
6
]
, but the interval from which we choose
\theta
is different.
Our choice for the interval of
\theta
is designed to exploit monotonicity in order to achieve a better approximation factor.
Below, we present the rounding algorithm for
Mono-Sub-MP
:

Algorithm 1

Mono-Sub-MP Rounding

Let
\mathbf{x}
be an optimal solution to
Sub-MP-Rel

Choose
\theta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]
uniformly at random

for

i\in[k]

do

A(i,\theta)\leftarrow\{v\in V\colon x(v,i)\geq\theta\}

U(\theta)\leftarrow V-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}A(i,\theta)

for

v\in V

do

I_{v}\leftarrow\{i\in[k]\colon v\in A(i,\theta)\}

if

|I_{v}|>1

then

delete
v
from all but one of the sets
\{A(i,\theta)\}_{i\in I_{v}}

return

\left(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k-1},A_{k}\right)=\left(A(1,\theta),\ldots,A(k-1,\theta),A(k,\theta)\cup U(\theta)\right)

We observe that the rounding algorithm guarantees that each
v\in V
is assigned to exactly one of the returned sets. Additionally, for each
i\in[k]
, we have that
t_{i}\in A(i,\theta)
since
\mathbf{x}(t_{i},i)=1
. Hence, the algorithm returns a feasible multiway partition of
V
. Moreover, the algorithm can be implemented to run in polynomial time.
We now bound the approximation factor of Algorithm
1
relative to the optimum value of
Sub-MP-Rel
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\textsc{OPT}_{frac}
be the optimum value of
Sub-MP-Rel
and
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
be the solution returned by Algorithm
1
. Then,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(A_{i})\right]\leq\frac{4}{3}\textsc{OPT}_{frac}.

The rest of this section is devoted to proving Theorem
2.1
.
Our approximation factor analysis uses certain ingredients of Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
, but differs from their analysis to exploit monotonicity and the different threshold range. We will point out the differences as we proceed with the analysis.

We start with a lemma about the truncated mass of a discrete probability distribution. A vector
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\geq 0}
with
\sum_{i\in[k]}p_{i}=1
can be viewed as a probability distribution over the set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
with
p_{i}
being the probability of picking item
i
. For
\delta\in[0,1]
, the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{\delta,p_{i}\}
is the truncated probability mass. We show that if
\delta\geq 1/4
, then the truncated probability mass is at least
1-\max_{i\in[k]}p_{i}
. The choice of
\delta\in[1/4,1]
in the rounding algorthm emanates from this lemma. This lemma does not appear in Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
and is our contribution.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\geq 0}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}=1
and
\delta\geq 1/4
. Then,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\left\{\delta,p_{i}\right\}\geq 1-\max_{i\in[k]}p_{i}.

Proof.

The LHS can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\left\{\delta,p_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}-\sum_{i\in[k]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}p_{i}>\delta}(p_{i}-\delta)=1-\sum_{i\in[k]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}p_{i}>\delta}(p_{i}-\delta)

Let
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{i\in[k]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}p_{i}>\delta\}
and let
j\in[k]
such that
p_{j}=\max_{i\in[k]}p_{i}
.
It suffices to show that
p_{j}\geq\sum_{i\in L}(p_{i}-\delta)
.
For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
p_{j}<\sum_{i\in L}(p_{i}-\delta)
. We case based on the size of
L-\{j\}
and arrive at a contradiction in all cases.

Case 1.
Suppose
L-\{j\}=\emptyset
. If
j\in L
, then
L=\{j\}
, and hence,

p_{j}<\sum_{i\in L}(p_{i}-\delta)=p_{j}-\delta,

a contradiction. If
j\not\in L
, then
L=\emptyset
, and hence,

0\leq p_{j}<\sum_{i\in L}(p_{i}-\delta)=0,

a contradiction.

Case 2.
Suppose
|L-\{j\}|=1
. Let
L-\{j\}=\{\ell\}
. Then, by definition of
j,\ell,
and
L
, we have that
L=\{\ell,j\}
. Consequently, we have that

p_{j}<\sum_{i\in L}(p_{i}-\delta)=p_{j}+p_{\ell}-2\delta.

Hence, we have that
p_{\ell}>2\delta\geq 1/2
since
\delta\geq 1/4
. Also, by definition of
j
, we have that
p_{j}\geq p_{\ell}
. Hence, we have that
p_{j}\geq p_{\ell}>1/2
and hence,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\geq p_{j}+p_{k}>1
, a contradiction.

Case 3.
Suppose
|L-\{j\}|\geq 2
.
Then, by definition of
j
, we have that
j\in L
and hence,
|L|\geq 3
and
p_{j}>\delta
. Thus, we have that

\delta\leq p_{j}<\sum_{i\in L}(p_{i}-\delta)\leq\left(\sum_{i\in L}p_{i}\right)-3\delta.

The last inequality above is because
|L|\geq 3
. From the above inequalities, we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\geq\sum_{i\in L}p_{i}>4\delta\geq 1
, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The conclusion of Lemma
2.1
fails for
\delta<1/4
. We give an example to illustrate this: let
k=2
and let
x_{1}=1/2,x_{2}=1/2
. Now, consider
\delta<1/4
. Then, the LHS of the lemma is
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{\delta,x_{i}\}=\min\{\delta,x_{1}\}+\min\{\delta,x_{2}\}=2\delta
. On the other hand, the RHS of the lemma is
1-\max_{i\in[k]}x_{i}=1-\max\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=1/2
. Thus, the LHS is at least the RHS only for
\delta\geq 1/4
and fails for
\delta<1/4
.

We now set up the notation to analyze the approximation factor of our algorithm. This notation is similar to that of Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
.

Notation.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be an optimum solution to
Sub-MP-Rel
.
We assume that
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
For each
v_{j}\in V
, we define
\alpha_{j}=\max_{i=1}^{k}x(v_{j},i)
. We assume that the elements
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
of the ground set
V
are ordered such that
0\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{n}\leq 1
. For convenience, we define
\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
and
\alpha_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
.
Let
\theta\in[0,1]
.
For each
i\in[k]
, we define
A(i,\theta)=\{v\in V\colon x(v,i)\geq\theta\}
as in the algorithm. Additionally, for each
j\in[n]
, we let
V_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\}
, and for convenience, we define
V_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\emptyset
. Finally, we let
A_{j}(i,\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A(i,\theta)\cap V_{j}
.

With the above notation, we show the following technical lemma based on monotonicity and submodularity of
f
and by using Lemma
2.1
. Since we rely on monotonicity and Lemma
2.1
, the following lemma does not follow from the results in Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
\delta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]
and
j\in[n]
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta\geq(1-\alpha_{j})\left(f(V_{j})-f(V_{j-1})\right).

Proof.

Let
\delta\in[1/4,1]
and
j\in[n]
. We show the following two claims that will help in proving the lemma.

Claim 2.1
.

For every
i\in[k]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\int_{0}^{\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta.

Proof.

Let
i\in[k]
.
We may assume that
\min\{\delta,\ x(v_{j},i)\}=x(v_{j},i)
(otherwise, the claim holds trivially).
For every
\theta\in(x(v_{j},i),\delta]
, we get
x(v_{j},i)<\theta
and hence,
v_{j}\notin A(i,\theta)
. Thus, for every
\theta\in(x(v_{j},i),\delta]
, we have that
A_{j}(i,\theta)=A(i,\theta)\cap V_{j}=A(i,\theta)\cap V_{j-1}=A_{j-1}(i,\theta)
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\int_{0}^{x(v_{j},i)}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{x(v_{j},i)}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\int_{0}^{x(v_{j},i)}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta.

∎

Claim 2.2
.

For every
i\in[k]
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\geq\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
\theta\in[0,\min(\delta,x(v_{j},i))]
. Then,
x(v_{j},i)\geq\theta
and thus,
A_{j}(i,\theta)=A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\cup\{v_{j}\}
. Additionally,
V_{j}=V_{j-1}\cup\{v_{j}\}
,
A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\subseteq V_{j-1}
, and
v_{j}\notin V_{j-1}
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\cup\{v_{j}\}\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)

\displaystyle\geq f\left(V_{j-1}\cup\{v_{j}\}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)

(by submodularity of
f
)

\displaystyle=f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\geq\int_{0}^{\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}}\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right).

∎

Now, we use the above claims to lower bound the LHS of the lemma
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta\quad\text{(by Claim \ref{claim:telescope-trunc})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right)\quad\text{(by Claim \ref{claim:sub-telescope})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad=\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}\right).

(3)

Finally, we know that
x(v_{j},i)\geq 0
for every
i\in[k]
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x(v_{j},i)=1
since
x
is a feasible solution to the convex program. Hence, using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\max_{i\in[k]}x(v_{j},i)=1-\alpha_{j}.

(4)

Further, by monotonicity of
f
, we have that
f(V_{j})-f(V_{j-1})\geq 0
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\min\{\delta,x(v_{j},i)\}\right)\geq(1-\alpha_{j})\left(f\left(V_{j}\right)-f\left(V_{j-1}\right)\right).

(5)

Thus, using inequalities (
3
) and (
5
), we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta\geq(1-\alpha_{j})\left(f(V_{j})-f(V_{j-1})\right).

∎

We need the following Proposition from Chekuri and Ene
[
6
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
6
]

Let
f
be an arbitrary non-negative submodular function,
r\in[0,1]
, and let
h
be the largest value of
j\in[n]
such that
\alpha_{j}\leq r
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}f\left(U(\theta)\right)d\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\alpha_{j}\left(f(V_{j-1})-f(V_{j})\right)+rf(V_{h}).

Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.2
lead to the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

For every
\delta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]
,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f(A_{j}(i,\theta)-f(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)d\theta\right)\geq\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta-f(\emptyset).

Proof.

We use
r=1
and
h=n
in Proposition
2.2
to get,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f\left(U(\theta)\right)d\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\left(f(V_{j-1})-f(V_{j})\right)+f(V_{n}).

(6)

Now, by Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{j})\left(f(V_{j})-f(V_{j-1})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(f(V_{j})-f(V_{j-1})\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\left(f(V_{j-1})-f(V_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=f(V_{n})-f(V_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\left(f(V_{j-1})-f(V_{j})\right).

Finally, using
V_{0}=\emptyset
and equation (
6
), we get,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f\left(A_{j}(i,\theta)\right)-f\left(A_{j-1}(i,\theta)\right)\right)d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta-f(\emptyset).

∎

We have the following theorem as a consequence of Corollary
2.1
. We observe that this theorem upper bounds the expected function value of the set of unassigned elements.

Theorem 2.2
.

For all
\delta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A(i,\theta))d\theta\geq(k\delta-1)f(\emptyset)+\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A(i,\theta))d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A_{n}(i,\theta))d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A_{0}(i,\theta))d\theta+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A_{j}(i,\theta))-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A_{j-1}(i,\theta))\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A_{0}(i,\theta))d\theta+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(f(A_{j}(i,\theta))-f(A_{j-1}(i,\theta))\right)d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\delta}f(A_{0}(i,\theta))d\theta+\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta-f(\emptyset)\quad\quad\quad\text{(by Corollary \ref{cor:at_least_U})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=(k\delta-1)f(\emptyset)+\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Theorem
2.2
fails to hold for

. We give an example to illustrate this: let
V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{t_{1},t_{2},u\}
,

(i.e.,

below.
See
2.1

Proof.

We note that
\textsc{OPT}_{frac}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{i})=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(A(i,\theta))d\theta
. By Theorem
2.2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}f(A(i,\theta))d\theta\geq\left(\frac{1}{4}k-1\right)f(\emptyset)+\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta\geq\int_{0}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta.

(7)

The last inequality above is because
f(\emptyset)=0
.
Hence, the expected cost of the partition returned by Algorithm

is

\mathbb{E}_{\theta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(A_{i})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\theta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}f(A(i,\theta))+f(A(k,\theta)\cup U(\theta))\right]\quad\quad\text{(since $f$ is monotone)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\theta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(A(i,\theta))+f(U(\theta))\right]\text{(since $f$ is non-negative and submodular)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(A(i,\theta))+f(U(\theta))\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\left(\textsc{OPT}_{frac}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}f(A(i,\theta))d\theta+\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{1}f(U(\theta))d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{3}\textsc{OPT}_{frac}.\quad\quad\text{(by inequality (\ref{ineq:minus_A_plus_U}))}

∎

.

We emphasize that monotonicity of the function is used only in the proofs of Theorem

and Lemma

.

Remark 2.5
.

Our approximation factor analysis for Algorithm
1
is tight. We give an example to show that Algorithm
1
does not achieve
(4/3-\epsilon)
-approximation for any constant
\epsilon>0
. Let
V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{t_{1},t_{2},u_{1}\cdots,t_{2k-1},t_{2k},u_{k}\}
and
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{2k}\}
. Let
E_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{t_{2i-1},t_{2i},u_{i}\}
and
f(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\{j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\cap E_{j}\neq\emptyset\}|
.
The partition
V_{2i}=\{t_{2i},u_{i}\}
,
V_{2i-1}=\{t_{2i-1}\}
is optimal for this instance of

, with
\sum_{i=1}^{2k}f(V_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(f(V_{2i})+f(V_{2i-1})\right)=2k
.
Now, setting
x(u_{i},i)=x(u_{i},i+1)=\frac{1}{2}
with the rest of the
x
values being
0
gives an optimum solution to the convex program
Sub-MP-Rel
. For
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
,
A(i,\theta)=\{t_{i}\}
,
U(\theta)=\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}\}
. Hence, the algorithm returns
V_{i}=\{t_{i}\}
for

,
V_{2k}=\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{k},t_{2k}\}

\sum_{i=1}^{2k}f(V_{i})=3k-1
. Further, for

,
A(2i,\theta)=\{t_{2i},u_{i}\}
and
A(2i-1)=\{t_{2i-1},u_{i}\}
. Hence, the algorithm returns
V_{2i}=\{t_{i},u_{i}\}
,
V_{2i-1}=\{t_{2i-1}\}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{2k}f(V_{i})=2k
. Hence,
\mathbb{E}_{\theta\in[\frac{1}{4},1]}[\sum_{i=1}^{2k}f(V_{i})]=\frac{1}{3}(2k)+\frac{2}{3}(3k-1)=\frac{8k-2}{3}
. Hence, approximation factor for this example is
\frac{8k-2}{6k}=\frac{4}{3}-\frac{1}{3k}
. Setting
k
large enough gives us the desired result.
We mention that this example is obtained by taking multiple copies of the tight example for Theorem
2.2
mentioned in Remark
2.3
.

Remark 2.6
.

For threshold rounding where the threshold
\theta
is picked uniformly at random from the interval
[1/2,1]
(i.e., Chekuri and Ene’s rounding algorithm for
Sub-MP
), the best possible approximation factor is at least
3/2
. The example in the previous remark illustrates this.

2.2
Inapproximability

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
Our approach will be similar to the approaches in
[
17
,
10
]
—we define a notion of
symmetry gap
below; in
Theorem
2.3
,
we show that symmetry gap gives a lower bound on the approximation factor of algorithms with polynomial number of function evaluation queries;
finally, in Theorem
2.4
,
we construct an instance of
Mono-Sub-MP
that exhibits a symmetry gap of
10/9
.
We mention that Dobzinksi and Vondrák
[
9
]
showed a general approach to convert query complexity based inapproximability results via the symmetry gap to computation based inapproximability results.

Definition 2.1
.

(Symmetry Gap)

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
and
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a set function defined over the set
V
.

1.

For a subset
A\subseteq V
, we denote
A^{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(j,i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(i,j)\in A\}
.

2.

The set function
f
is a row-column-type function if there exists a function
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
f(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(g(A\cap R_{i})+g(A\cap C_{i}))
for every
A\subseteq V
, where
R_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in[k]\}
and
C_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(j,i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in[k]\}
for every
i\in[k]
.

3.

A partition
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
of
V
is
symmetric
if

V_{\ell}=V_{\ell}^{T}
for every
\ell\in[k]
.
Generalizing, we say that
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{V\times[k]}
is
symmetric
if
x(*,\ell)=x(*,\ell)^{T}
for every
\ell\in[k]
.

4.

Let
\textsc{OPT}(f)
be

\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(V_{i})\colon V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\text{ is a partition of }V\text{ with }(i,i)\in V_{i}\ \forall i\in[k]\right\}.

5.

Let
\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}(f)
be

\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(V_{i})\colon V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\text{ is a symmetric partition of }V\text{ with }(i,i)\in V_{i}\ \forall i\in[k]\right\}.

6.

Let
\textsc{Sym-Gap}\left(f\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}(f)}{\textsc{OPT}(f)}
.

Next, we prove that the approximation factor of an algorithm that makes polynomial number of function evaluation queries should be at least the symmetry gap.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that there exists a row-column-type non-negative monotone submodular function
f\colon 2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
over the set
V=[k]\times[k]
such that
\textsc{Sym-Gap}(f)\geq\alpha
.
Then, for every constant
\varepsilon>0
, every algorithm for
Mono-Sub-MP
with an approximation factor of
\alpha-\epsilon
makes
2^{\Omega(n)}
function evaluation queries, where
n
is the size of the ground set.

Finally, we show that there exists a row-column-type non-negative monotone submodular function with large symmetry gap.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
V^{(k)}=[k]\times[k]
. Then, there exists a sequence
\left(f^{(k)}\colon 2^{V^{(k)}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of row-column-type non-negative monotone submodular functions parameterized by
k
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\textsc{Sym-Gap}\left(f^{(k)}\right)=10/9.

Theorem
1.2
follows from Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
. We prove Theorem
2.3
in Section
2.2.1
and Theorem
2.4
in Section
2.2.2
. Our proof approach for Theorem
2.3
is similar to the proof approaches in
[
17
,
10
]
—the result does not follow directly from the results in
[
17
,
10
]
since we are interested in
Mono-Sub-MP
(which was not the focus of those two works), so we have to work out the details. We encourage first-time readers to skip Section
2.3
. Our main contribution is Theorem
2.4
, i.e., constructing an instance of
Mono-Sub-MP
with large symmetry gap.

2.2.1
Lower Bound from Symmetry Gap

In this section, we prove that the approximation factor of an algorithm that makes polynomial number of function evaluation queries should be at least the symmetry gap, i.e., prove Theorem
2.3
. Our proof approach is similar to the proof approaches in
[
17
,
10
]
, but we have to work out the details since we are interested in
Mono-Sub-MP
which was not the focus of those two works.
We begin by showing that
OPT
and
\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
can be obtained as optimum solutions to the multilinear relaxation. We define the multilinear extension and the multilinear relaxation of the multiway partitioning problem.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
and
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a set function.

1.

The
multilinear extension
of
f
is the function
F\colon[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

F(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{S\subseteq V}f(S)\left(\prod_{v\in S}x_{v}\prod_{v\in V\setminus S}(1-x_{v})\right).

2.

The
multilinear relaxation
of the multiway partitioning problem over
V
wrt
f
for the set
\{(\ell,\ell)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ell\in[k]\}
of terminals is

\displaystyle\text{min}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F\left(\mathbf{x}_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}x((i,j),\ell)=1

\displaystyle\quad\forall(i,j)\in V

\displaystyle x((\ell,\ell),\ell)=1

\displaystyle\quad\forall\ell\in[k]

\displaystyle x((i,j),\ell)\geq 0

\displaystyle\quad\forall(i,j)\in V,\ell\in[k],

where
\mathbf{x}_{\ell}=\left(x((i,j),\ell)\right)_{(i,j)\in V}
. We will say that a vector
\mathbf{x}=\left(x((i,j),\ell)\right)_{(i,j)\in V,\ell\in[k]}\in[0,1]^{V\times[k]}
is a solution to the multilinear relaxation if it satisfies the constraints of the multilinear relaxation.

The following lemma shows that both
OPT
and
\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
can be expressed as optimum solutions to the multilinear relaxation and a symmetry constrained multilinear relaxation respectively.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
and let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a row-column-type set function defined over the set
V
.
We have the following:

1.

\textsc{OPT}(f)=\min\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in[k]}\text{ is a feasible solution to the multilinear relaxation}\right\}
and

2.

\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}(f)=\min\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F\left(\mathbf{y}_{\ell}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in[k]}\text{ is a symmetric feasible solution to the multilinear relaxation}\right\}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote
\textsc{OPT}=\textsc{OPT}(f)
and
\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}=\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}(f)
.

1.

Let
\textsc{ML-OPT}=\min\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in[k]}\text{ is a feasble solution to the multilinear relaxation}\}
. We show that
\textsc{OPT}=\textsc{ML-OPT}
. Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
be a partition of
V
such that
(i,i)\in V_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(V_{i})=OPT
. For every
i,j,\ell\in[k]
, we define

x((i,j),\ell)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }(i,j)\in V_{\ell},\\
0&\text{ if }(i,j)\notin V_{\ell}.\end{cases}

Then,
\left(x((i,j),\ell)\right)_{(i,j)\in V,\ell\in[k]}
is a feasible solution to the multilinear relaxation and its objective value is
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F\left(\left(x((i,j),\ell)\right)_{(i,j)\in V}\right)=\textsc{OPT}
. Consequently,
\textsc{ML-OPT}\leq\textsc{OPT}
. Next, let
\mathbf{x}^{*}
be an optimum solution to the multilinear relaxation.
We obtain a partition
V^{*}_{1},\ldots,V^{*}_{k}
of
V
by assigning each element
(i,j)\in V
to one of the parts as follows: consider an element
(i,j)\in V
;
we define
z_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
and
z_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}x^{*}((i,j),m)
for every
\ell\in[k]
, pick
\theta\in[0,1]
uniformly at random, and assign
(i,j)
to
V_{\ell}
such that
z_{\ell-1}\leq\theta<z_{\ell}
. We note that each element
(i,j)
is assigned to a part
V^{*}_{\ell}
independently with probability
x^{*}((i,j),\ell)
.
We observe that
V^{*}_{1},\ldots,V^{*}_{k}
is a partition of
V
with
(i,i)\in V^{*}_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
. Moreover,
\mathbb{E}\left[f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right]=\sum_{S\subseteq V}f(S)\prod_{(i,j)\in S}x^{*}((i,j),\ell)\prod_{(i,j)\in V\setminus S}(1-x^{*}((i,j),\ell))=F(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\ell})
. Hence,
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\ell})=\textsc{ML-OPT}
. This implies that there exists a partition
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
with
(i,i)\in V_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
such that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}f(V_{\ell})\leq\textsc{ML-OPT}
, so
\textsc{OPT}\leq\textsc{ML-OPT}
.

2.

Let
\textsc{ML-OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}=\min\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F\left(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\ell}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\left(\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in[k]}\text{ is a symmetric solution to the multilinear relaxation}\right\}
. We show that
\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}=\textsc{ML-OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
. Using identical reasoning to the previous paragraph, we have that
\textsc{ML-OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}\leq\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
. Let
\mathbf{y}
be an optimum symmetric solution to the multilinear relaxation. We obtain a partition
V^{*}_{1},\ldots,V^{*}_{k}
of
V
in the same manner as the previous paragraph from
\mathbf{y}
.
By the reasoning of the above paragraph, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right]=\textsc{ML-OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
.
We recall that
\mathbf{y}
is a symmetric feasible solution to the multilinear relaxation but the the partition
V^{*}_{1},\ldots,V^{*}_{k}
need not be symmetric.
In addition to the partition
V^{*}_{1},\ldots,V^{*}_{k}
, we obtain a symmetric partition
\overline{V}_{1},\ldots,\overline{V}_{k}
of
V
by assigning each pair
\{(i,j),(j,i)\}\subseteq V
to one of the parts as follows: for
i,j\in[k]
consider the pair of elements
\{(i,j),(j,i)\}\in V
;
we define
z_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
and
z_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}y((i,j),m)
for every
\ell\in[k]
, pick
\theta\in[0,1]
uniformly at random, and assign the pair of elements
\{(i,j),(j,i)\}
to
V_{\ell}
such that
z_{\ell-1}\leq\theta<z_{\ell}
. We note that each element pair
\{(i,j),(j,i)\}
is assigned to a part
\overline{V}_{\ell}
independently with probability
y((i,j),\ell)
.
We observe that
\overline{V}_{1},\ldots,\overline{V}_{k}
is a partition of
V
with
\overline{V}_{i}
being symmetric and
(i,i)\in\overline{V}_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
. We now show that
\mathbb{E}\left[f(\overline{V}_{\ell})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right]
for every
\ell\in[k]
.

Claim 2.3
.

For every
\ell\in[k]
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[f(\overline{V}_{\ell})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right].

Proof.

Let
\ell\in[k]
.
We recall that
V=[k]\times[k]
and
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a row-column-type function. Hence, there exists a function
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
f(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(g(A\cap R_{i})+g(A\cap C_{i}))
for every
A\subseteq V
, where
R_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in[k]\}
and
C_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(j,i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in[k]\}
for every
i\in[k]
. Hence, for every
i\in[k]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(\overline{V}_{\ell}\cap R_{i}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq R_{i}}g(S)\Pr\left[\overline{V}_{\ell}\cap R_{i}=S\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq R_{i}}g(S)\prod_{(i,j)\in S}y((i,j),\ell)\prod_{(i,j)\in R_{i}\setminus S}(1-y((i,j),\ell))

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\subseteq R_{i}}g(S)\Pr\left[V^{*}_{\ell}\cap R_{i}=S\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(V^{*}_{\ell}\cap R_{i}\right)\right].

Similar reasoning shows that
\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(\overline{V}_{\ell}\cap C_{i}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[g(V^{*}_{\ell}\cap C_{i})\right]
for every
i\in[k]
.
Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\overline{V}_{\ell}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(\overline{V}_{\ell}\cap R_{i}\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(\overline{V}_{\ell}\cap C_{i}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[g(V^{*}_{\ell}\cap R_{i})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[g(V^{*}_{\ell}\cap C_{i})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right].

∎

Claim
2.3
implies that there exists a partition
\overline{V}_{1},\ldots,\overline{V}_{k}
of
V
with
\overline{V}_{i}
being symmetric and
(i,i)\in\overline{V}_{i}
for every
i\in[k]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f(\overline{V}_{\ell})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}f(V^{*}_{\ell})\right]=\textsc{ML-OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
and hence,
\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}\leq\textsc{ML-OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}
.

∎

We will use the following three lemmas from
[
17
]
to prove Theorem
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 3.1 from
[
17
]
)
.

Let
H\colon[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function with absolutely continuous first partial derivatives. For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
N=[n]
and define
h\colon 2^{N\times V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
h(S)=H(\mathbf{x})
where
x_{i}=\frac{1}{n}|S\cap(N\times\{i\})|
. Then, we have the following:

1.

If
\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{i}}\geq 0
everywhere for each
i
, then
h
is monotone.

2.

If
\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\leq 0
almost everywhere for all
i,j
, then
h
is submodular.

We will need the notion of transpose-invariant functions to state the next lemma.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
. A set function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
transpose-invariant
if
f(A)=f(A^{T})
for every
A\subseteq V
. Generalizing, we say that a function
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow R
is
transpose-invariant
if
F(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x}^{T})
for every
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{[k]\times[k]}
, where
\mathbf{x}^{T}
is the transpose of the matrix
\mathbf{x}
.

We note that a row-column-type set function is transpose-invariant by definition.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma 3.2 from
[
17
]
)
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
,
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a transpose-invariant function over the set
V
, and
\varepsilon>0
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{V}
, denote
\overline{\mathbf{x}}=(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}^{T})/2
.
Then, there exists
\delta>0
and functions
\hat{F},\hat{G}\colon[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying the following:

(i)

Both
\hat{F}
and
\hat{G}
are transpose-invariant.

(ii)

For all
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{V}
,
\hat{G}(\mathbf{x})=\hat{F}(\overline{\mathbf{x}})
.

(iii)

For all
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{V}
,
|\hat{F}(\mathbf{x})-F(\mathbf{x})|\leq\epsilon
.

(iv)

If
\|\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}\leq\delta
, then
\hat{F}(\mathbf{x})=\hat{G}(\mathbf{x})
and the value of
\hat{F}(x)
depends only on
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
.

(v)

The first partial derivatives of
\hat{F}
and
\hat{G}
are absolutely continuous.

(vi)

If
f
is monotone, then
\frac{\partial\hat{F}}{\partial x_{i}}\geq 0
and
\frac{\partial\hat{G}}{\partial x_{i}}\geq 0
everywhere.

(vii)

If
f
is submodular, then
\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{F}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\leq 0
and
\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{G}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\leq 0
almost everywhere.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 3.3 from
[
17
]
)
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
,
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a transpose-invariant function over the set
V
, and
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\hat{F}
,
\hat{G}
be the two functions satisfying the conclusions of Lemma
2.5
. For an integer
n\geq 1
and
N=[n]
, define two discrete functions
\hat{f},\hat{g}\colon 2^{N\times V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
as follows:
Let
\sigma_{r}\in\{0,1\}
be chosen uniformly at random for each
r\in N
.
For every set
S\subseteq N\times V
, we define a vector
\xi(S)\in[0,1]^{V}
by

\displaystyle\xi(S)_{(i,j)}=\frac{1}{n}\left|\left\{r\in N\colon(r,(i,j))\in S\text{ and }\sigma_{r}=0\right\}\cup\left\{r\in N\colon(r,(j,i))\in S\text{ and }\sigma_{r}=1\right\}\right|.

and define
\hat{f}(S)=\hat{F}(\xi(S))
and
\hat{g}(S)=\hat{G}(\xi(S))
. Then, deciding whether a function given by a value oracle is
\hat{f}
or
\hat{g}
(even using a randomized algorithm with a constant probability of success) requires
2^{\Omega(n)}
function evaluation queries.

We now prove Theorem
2.3
. In Theorem
2.5
below, we prove that symmetry gap gives an inapproximability bound for a slight variant of
Mono-Sub-MP
where the terminals correspond to disjoint sets rather than singleton elements.
We note that Theorem
2.3
follows from Theorem
2.5
(i.e., for the version of
Mono-Sub-MP
where the terminals are singleton elements) by a standard contraction of terminal sets into a single terminal and appropriately modifying the associated submodular function.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
and
f\colon 2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a row-column-type non-negative monotone submodular function over the set
V
. Then, for all
\gamma>0
and sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{Z_{+}}
, there exist non-negative monotone submodular functions
\hat{f},\hat{g}\colon 2^{[n]\times V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
and a collection of pairwise disjoint terminal sets
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\subseteq[n]\times V
such that there does not exist an algorithm that achieves an approximation factor of
\textsc{Sym-Gap}(f)-\gamma
for the following instances of
Mono-Sub-MP
while using
2^{o(n)}
function evaluation queries on these instances:

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{f}(V_{i})\colon V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\text{ is a partition of }[n]\times V\text{ with }T_{i}\subseteq V_{i}\ \forall\ i\in[k]\right\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{g}(V_{i})\colon V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\text{ is a partition of }[n]\times V\text{ with }T_{i}\subseteq V_{i}\ \forall\ i\in[k]\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma>0
. We choose
\epsilon>0
such that
\frac{\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}-\epsilon k}{\textsc{OPT}+\epsilon(k+1)}>\frac{\textsc{OPT}_{\textsc{Sym}}}{\textsc{OPT}}-\gamma
.
Consider the multilinear relaxation of multiway partitioning wrt the function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for the terminals
t_{\ell}=(\ell,\ell)
for every
\ell\in[k]
. We fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
large enough such that there exists a feasible solution
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
to the multilinear relaxation such that the entries of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
are all rational numbers with
n
in the denominator and
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{\ell})\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}F(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{\ell})+\epsilon
, where
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is an optimal solution to the multilinear relaxation (we note that such a solution
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
exists since the objective of the multilinear relaxation is continuous).
For the chosen value of
\epsilon
, we use Lemma
2.5
to obtain
\hat{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\hat{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,1]^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying the conclusions of the lemma. For the chosen value of
n
, we let
N=[n]
and
X=N\times V
. For each
r\in N
, we choose
\sigma_{r}\in\{0,1\}
uniformly at random. Now, for
S\subseteq X
, we define the vector
\xi(S)\in[0,1]^{V}
such that

\xi(S)_{(i,j)}=\frac{1}{n}\left|\left\{r\in N\colon(r,(i,j))\in S\text{ and }\sigma_{r}=0\right\}\cup\left\{r\in N\colon(r,(j,i))\in S\text{ and }\sigma_{r}=1\right\}\right|.

Next, we define
\hat{f},\hat{g}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
as
\hat{f}(S)=\hat{F}(\xi(S))
and
\hat{g}(S)=\hat{G}(\xi(S))

Definition 2.4

In Lemma

In Lemma

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
and
f\colon 2^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be defined as in Definition

is a row-column-type non-negative monotone submodular function.

Proof.

From the definition of
f

f
is row-column-type and non-negative. Moreover,
f
is monotone since
\phi_{n}
and
\phi_{t}
are monotone and
\phi_{t}(a)\geq\phi_{n}(a)
for all
a\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
. Now we prove that
f
is submodular. We first prove the following observations in which
a
and
c
are assumed to take integer values:

1.

For all
a\leq c

\phi_{n}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{n}(c+1)-\phi_{n}(c)

2.

For all
a\leq c

\phi_{t}(a+1)-\phi_{t}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(c)

3.

For all
0\leq a\leq a+1\leq c

\phi_{n}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(c)
.

4.

For all
0\leq a\leq c

\phi_{t}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{n}(c)
.

The first two observations follow from the concavity of
\phi_{n}
and
\phi_{t}
, respectively.

To prove that if
0\leq a
and
a+1\leq c
, then
\phi_{n}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(c)
, we examine two cases:
a\leq\frac{7}{8}k-1
and
a\geq\frac{7}{8}k

a\leq\frac{7}{8}k

\phi_{n}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)=1
. Additionally, because
0\leq a
and
a+1\leq c
,
c\geq 1
, it follows that
1\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(c)
. If
a\geq\frac{7}{8}k
, then
\phi_{n}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)=0=\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(c)
since
c\geq a+1
.

To prove that if
0\leq a\leq c
,
\phi_{t}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{n}(c)
, we examine two cases:
c\leq\frac{7}{8}k
and

c\leq\frac{7}{8}k

\phi_{n}(c)-\phi_{n}(a)=c-a

\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(a+1)\leq c-a

c\geq\frac{7}{8}k

\phi_{n}(c)-\phi_{n}(a)=\max\left\{\frac{7}{8}k-a,0\right\}
, and since
a+1\geq 1
, we have that
\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(a+1)=\max\left\{\frac{k}{2}-a+1,0\right\}

\phi_{n}(c)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(a+1)
. Rearranging, we obtain that
\phi_{t}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{n}(c)
.

We now prove submodularity armed with the above observations.
We will show that for all
X\subseteq Y\subseteq V
and
(i,j)\in V\setminus Y
, we have that
f\left(X+(i,j)\right)-f\left(X\right)\geq f\left(Y+(i,j)\right)-f\left(Y\right)
. Let
X\subseteq Y\subseteq V
and
(i,j)\in V\setminus Y
. We observe that
f\left(X+(i,j)\right)-f\left(X\right)=g\left(R_{i}\cap X+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap X\right)+g\left(C_{j}\cap X+(i,j)\right)-g\left(C_{j}\cap X\right)
. Similarly,
f\left(Y+(i,j)\right)-f\left(Y\right)=g\left(R_{i}\cap Y+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap Y\right)+g\left(C_{j}\cap Y+(i,j)\right)-g\left(C_{j}\cap Y\right)
.

First, we will consider the case in which
i\neq j
. In particular, we show that
g\left(R_{i}\cap X+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap X\right)\geq g\left(R_{i}\cap Y+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap Y\right)
. We let
|R_{i}\cap X|=a
and
|R_{i}\cap Y|=c
. If
(i,i)\notin Y
, then
g\left(R_{i}\cap X+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap X\right)=\phi_{n}(a+1)-\phi_{n}(a)\geq\phi_{n}(c+1)-\phi_{n}(c)=g\left(R_{i}\cap Y+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap Y\right)

g\left(R_{i}\cap X+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap X\right)=\phi_{t}(a+1)-\phi_{t}(a)\geq\phi_{t}(c+1)-\phi_{t}(c)=g\left(R_{i}\cap Y+(i,j)\right)-g\left(R_{i}\cap Y\right)

c\geq a+1
and

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)
is is attained at
i^{*}=1
. Substituting
i^{*}=1
in (
11
), we obtain that

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)\geq k-k-1+1+\frac{1}{8}\left(4-3k-4+10k^{2}\right)=\frac{10k^{2}-3k}{8}.

Case 2.
Suppose that
\frac{1}{8}k<i^{*}\leq\frac{k}{2}
. Then,
\phi_{t}(k+1-i)=\frac{7}{8}k
and
\phi_{n}(k-i^{*})=k-i^{*}
. Hence, by (
8
), (
9
), and (
10
), we have that

\displaystyle\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{i^{*}}\left(\frac{7}{8}k+i-1\right)+\left(k-i^{*}\right)\left(\frac{3}{8}k+k-i^{*}+i^{*}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{i^{*}}\left(\frac{7}{8}k+i-1\right)+\left(k-i^{*}\right)\left(\frac{11}{8}k-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((i^{*})^{2}-i^{*}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\left(11k^{2}-4ki^{*}\right)-k+i^{*}.

(12)

Taking the derivative with respect to
i^{*}
, we have

\frac{\partial\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)}{\partial i^{*}}=i^{*}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{2}.

We observe that
\frac{\partial^{2}\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)}{\partial(i^{*})^{2}}=1
, so
\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)
is a convex function of
i^{*}
. Hence, for
\frac{1}{8}k<i^{*}\leq\frac{k}{2}
, the minimum value for
\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)
is attained at
i^{*}=\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, evaluating the expression (
12
) at
i^{*}=\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain that

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)\geq\frac{10k^{2}-6k-1}{8}.

Case 3.
Suppose that
\frac{k}{2}<i^{*}
. Then,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(k+1-i)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{7}{8}k&i\leq\frac{k}{2}\\
\frac{11}{8}k-i&i>\frac{k}{2}\end{cases}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(k-i^{*})

\displaystyle=k-i^{*}.

Hence, by (
8
), (
9
), and (
10
), we have that

\displaystyle\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\frac{7}{8}k+i-1\right)+\sum_{i=\frac{k}{2}+1}^{i^{*}}\left(\frac{11}{8}k-i+i-1\right)+\left(k-i^{*}\right)\left(\frac{3}{8}k+k-i^{*}+i^{*}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\frac{7}{8}k+i-1\right)+\left(i^{*}-\frac{k}{2}\right)\left(\frac{11}{8}k-1\right)+\left(k-i^{*}\right)\left(\frac{11}{8}k-1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{k}{2}}\left(\frac{7}{8}k+i-1\right)+\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)\left(\frac{11}{8}k-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{10k^{2}-6k}{8}.

∎

The following lemma shows that every symmetric multiway partition can be converted to a structured symmetric multiway partition with a small additive loss in the objective.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
V=[k]\times[k]
. Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be a symmetric multiway partition of
V
. Then, there exists a symmetric multiway partition
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
of
V
with the following properties:

1.

\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}f(Q_{\ell})\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}f(P_{\ell})+4k

2.

There exists
i^{*}\in[k]
such that
\overrightarrow{R}_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
for all
i\in[i^{*}]
and
\overrightarrow{R}_{i}\subseteq Q_{1}
for all
i\in[k]\setminus[i^{*}]
.

Lemmas
2.10
and
2.11
together complete the proof of Lemma
2.9
. The rest of this section is devoted to proving Lemma
2.11
. For the rest of this section, let
V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[k]\times[k]
.
We will denote a symmetric multiway partition of
V
satisfying the second condition of Lemma
2.11
as a
structured symmetric partition
(see Figure
2
for an example).
To prove Lemma
2.11
, we will prove several claims about how symmetric multiway partitions of
V=[k]\times[k]
can be transformed into a structured symmetric multiway partitions without increasing the cost of the partition by more than
4k
.

The requirement for structured symmetric multiway partition for
i=1
is that
R_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}
.
We begin with Claim
2.6
below which shows that this property can be achieved for
i=1
with an additive loss in the objective value. We emphasize that Claim
2.6
will be the only transformation that incurs a loss in the objective value. The rest of the transformations will transform
partially
structured symmetric partition into a fully structured symmetric partition (as desired in Lemma
2.11
) without any loss in the objective value.

Claim 2.6
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be a symmetric multiway partition of
V
. Then, there exists a symmetric multiway partition
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
of
V
such that

1.

R_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}
and

2.

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)\leq\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)+2k

Proof.

Let use define
Q_{1}=P_{1}\cup(R_{1}\cup C_{1})
and
Q_{\ell}=P_{\ell}\setminus(R_{1}\cup C_{1})
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
. We observe that
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is a symmetric multiway partition with
R_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}
. It remains to show that
\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)\leq\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)+2k
.

We have

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)-\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i\}}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)\right).

First we consider
i=1
. We have
R_{1}\cap Q_{1}=R_{1}
, so
\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{1}\right|\right)=\frac{7}{8}k
. Additionally,
\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{1}\right|\right)\geq 0
such that
\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{1}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{1}\right|\right)\leq\frac{7}{8}k
. Since
R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell}=\emptyset
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
, we have
\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)=0
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
. Also, we have
\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\geq 0
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
. Therefore, for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
, we have
\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\leq 0
. Hence,

\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{1}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1\}}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)\leq\frac{7}{8}k\leq k.

Now, we consider
i\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
. When transforming
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
into
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
, the only way in which we may change the assignment in row
R_{i}
is by moving element
(i,1)
into part
Q_{1}
. Hence,
R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\subseteq R_{i}\cap P_{i}
. Then, we have
\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)\leq 0
. Likewise for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
, we have
R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\subseteq R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}
, so
\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\leq 0
. Finally, we have
|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|\leq|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|+1
, so
\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\leq 1
. Overall, this shows that for
i\in[k]\setminus\{1\}
,

\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i\}}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)\leq 1.

Therefore,

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)-\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)\leq k+(k-1)(1)\leq 2k.

∎

The rest of the proof of Lemma
2.11
involves transforming a partially structured symmetric partition into a structured symmetric partition without any loss in the objective value. For this, we introduce the notion of unhappy elements.

Definition 2.5

(unhappy elements)
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be a partition of
V
. An element
(i,j)\in V
is
unhappy under
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
if
(i,j)\in P_{\ell}
where
\ell\notin\{i,j\}
. Moreover, let

U(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(i,j)\in[k]\times[k]\colon(i,j)\text{ is unhappy under }P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\}.

We now show that unhappy elements can be moved to the first part of a partially structured partition without increasing the objective value.

Claim 2.7
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be a symmetric multiway partition of
V
such that
R_{1}\subseteq P_{1}
.
Then, there exists a symmetric multiway partition
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
of
V
such that

1.

R_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}
,

2.

U(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k})\subseteq Q_{1}
, and

3.

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)\leq\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)
.

Proof.

For
U\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=U(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
, let
Q_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{1}\cup U
, and
Q_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{\ell}\setminus U
for each
\ell\in\{2,3,\ldots,k\}
. Then,
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is a symmetric multiway partition of
V
The first two properties follow by definition of
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
. It suffices to bound the objective value.
We have that

\displaystyle\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)-\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(g(R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell})-g(R_{i}\cap P_{\ell})\right).

In order to complete the proof, we show that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(g(R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell})-g(R_{i}\cap P_{\ell})\right)\leq 0
for each
i\in[k]
.

First, we consider
i=1
.

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(g(R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell})-g(R_{1}\cap P_{\ell})\right)=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{1}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq 1}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{1}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)=0,

since
R_{1}\subseteq P_{1}
and
R_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}
implies that
|R_{1}\cap Q_{1}|=|R_{1}\cap P_{1}|=k
and
|R_{1}\cap Q_{\ell}|=|R_{1}\cap P_{\ell}|=0
for each
\ell\in\{2,3,\ldots,k\}
.

Next, let
i\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
. We need to show that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(g(R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell})-g(R_{i}\cap P_{\ell})\right)\leq 0
.
We begin by showing that

\displaystyle|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|\text{ for each }\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}.

(13)

We have that
R_{i}\cap P_{i}\cap U=\emptyset
, so
R_{i}\cap P_{i}=R_{i}\cap Q_{i}
. Moreover,
\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{k}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right)=0
. Hence, we have that

\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\neq i}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right)=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{k}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right)=0.

Therefore,

|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\notin\{1,i\}}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right)=0.

Hence,

|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|=\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\notin\{1,i\}}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right).

Since
R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}\subseteq R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}
for each
\ell^{\prime}\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
, we have that

|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|\geq 0
for each
\ell^{\prime}\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
.
Then, for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
,

|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|\leq\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\notin\{1,i\}}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{\prime}}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell^{\prime}}|\right)=|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|.

Hence, for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
,

\displaystyle|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|+|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|+1\quad\text{(since $R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\subseteq\{(i,\ell)\}$)}

\displaystyle\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-1+1\quad\text{(since $R_{1}\subseteq P_{1}$, $(1,i)\in P_{1}$, so $(i,1)\in P_{1}$)}

\displaystyle\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|.

This completes the proof of inequality (
13
).

Next, we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(g(R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell})-g(R_{i}\cap P_{\ell})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{i}|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq i}\left(\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\neq i}\left(\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\right)\right)\quad\quad\text{(since $R_{i}\cap P_{i}\cap U=\emptyset$)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{1,i\}}\left(\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\right)\right).

We consider two cases based on
|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|
.

Case 1.
Suppose
|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|\leq\frac{7}{8}k
. By inequality (
13
), we have that
|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|\leq\frac{7}{8}k
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
. Additionally, we have that
|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|\leq\frac{7}{8}k
since
P_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{1,i\}}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|+\sum_{\ell\notin\{1,i\}}\left(|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\right)

\displaystyle\quad=0.

Case 2
. Suppose
|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|>\frac{7}{8}k
. Since
|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|>\frac{7}{8}k
, we have that
|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|\leq\frac{7}{8}k
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
. Hence,

\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{7}{8}k&\ell=1,\\
|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|&\ell\notin\{1,i\}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\neq i}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{8}k+\sum_{\ell\notin\{1,i\}}|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{8}k+\sum_{\ell\neq i}|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{8}k+\sum_{\ell\neq i}|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|.

Now we consider
\sum_{\ell\neq i}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)
. Let
\ell^{*}=\argmax_{\ell\neq i}|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\neq i}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{\ell^{*},i\}}|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\quad\text{(since for each $\ell\notin\{\ell^{*},i\}$, $|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\leq\frac{7}{8}k$)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq i}|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|.

Therefore,

\sum_{\ell\neq i}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)=\frac{7}{8}k-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}\right|\right)-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|+|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|.

If
|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|\leq\frac{7}{8}k
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\neq i}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{8}k-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|+|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{8}k-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|

\displaystyle<0.\quad\text{(since $|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|>\frac{7}{8}k$)}

Otherwise,
|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|>\frac{7}{8}k
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\neq i}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{8}k-\frac{7}{8}k-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|-|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|

\displaystyle=-|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|+|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell^{*}}|

\displaystyle\leq 0,

since
|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}|\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{1,i\}
by inequality (
13
) and
|R_{i}\cap P_{1}|\leq|R_{i}\cap Q_{1}|
.
∎

We will use the following claim later to move certain elements to adjust sizes of certain parts in the symmetric partition without increasing the objective value.

Claim 2.8
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be a symmetric multiway partition of
V
with the following properties:

1.

R_{1}\subseteq P_{1}
and

2.

U(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\subseteq P_{1}
.

Let
(i,j)\in R_{i}\cap P_{i}
where
i\neq j
. If
|R_{j}\cap P_{j}|\geq|R_{i}\cap P_{i}|
,
then the symmetric multiway partition
Q_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{i}\setminus\{(i,j),(j,i)\},Q_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{j}\cup\{(i,j),(j,i)\},Q_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{\ell}
for all
\ell\notin\{i,j\}
satisfies
\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)\leq\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)
.

Proof.

We observe that
1\notin\{i,j\}
since
(j,i)\notin P_{j}
implies that
k-1\geq|R_{j}\cap P_{j}|\geq|R_{i}\cap P_{i}|
and
|R_{1}\cap P_{1}|=k
. Let
a=|R_{i}\cap P_{i}|
and
b=|R_{j}\cap P_{j}|
.

We have

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]-i}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{j}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{j}\right|\right)\quad\text{(since $P_{\ell}=Q_{\ell}$ for all $\ell\notin\{i,j\}$)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(a-1)-\phi_{t}(a)+1-0\quad\text{(since $P_{j}\cap U=\emptyset$ and $(i,j)\in P_{i}$ implies $|R_{i}\cap P_{j}|=0$)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(a-1)-\phi_{t}(a)+1.

Additionally,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap Q_{j}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap P_{j}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]-j}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap Q_{j}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap P_{j}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{j}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)\quad\text{(since $P_{\ell}=Q_{\ell}$ for all $\ell\notin\{i,j\}$)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(b+1)-\phi_{t}(b)+0-1\quad\text{(since $P_{i}\cap U=\emptyset$ and $(j,i)\in P_{i}$ implies $|R_{j}\cap P_{i}|=1$)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(b+1)-\phi_{t}(b)-1.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)-\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}\left(\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{x}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{x}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]-x}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\{i,j\}}\left(\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{x}\right|\right)-\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{x}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\in[k]-x}\left(\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)-\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)\right),

since for all
x\notin\{i,j\}
and
\ell\in[k]
,
R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}=R_{x}\cap P_{\ell}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)-\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(a-1)-\phi_{t}(a)+1+\phi_{t}(b+1)-\phi_{t}(b)-1

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(b+1)-\phi_{t}(b)-(\phi_{t}(a)-\phi_{t}(a-1))

\displaystyle\leq 0\quad\text{(since $\phi_{t}$ is concave and $a\leq b$)}.

∎

For the rest of the transformations, we will use the following notion of
swap

Definition 2.6

(swap operation)
.

For each
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,k\}
, define
\pi_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[k]\rightarrow[k]
as follows:

\pi_{i}(\ell)=\begin{cases}\ell&\text{if }\ell\notin\{i-1,i\},\\
i-1&\text{if }\ell=i,\\
i&\text{if }\ell=i-1.\end{cases}

For
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,k\}
and a symmetric multiway partition
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
of
V
, we define
\text{swap}_{i}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)
as the symmetric multiway partition
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
of
V
defined by

1.

Q_{i-1}=\left\{\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\colon(x,y)\in P_{i}\right\}
,

2.

Q_{i}=\left\{\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\colon(x,y)\in P_{i-1}\right\}
, and

3.

Q_{\ell}=\left\{\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\colon(x,y)\in P_{\ell}\right\}
for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i-1,i\}
.

Condensing this definition, we see that
Q_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}=\left\{\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\colon(x,y)\in P_{\ell}\right\}
for every
\ell\in[k]
.

We now show that swap operations preserve certain structural properties without increasing the objective value.

Claim 2.9
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
be a symmetric multiway partition of
V
. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}=\text{swap}_{i}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)
for some
i\in\{3,\ldots,k\}
. Then,

1.

Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is a symmetric multiway partition of
V
.

2.

If
U(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\subseteq P_{1}
, then
U(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k})\subseteq Q_{1}
.

3.

\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|=\left|R_{\pi_{i}(x)}\cap P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}\right|
for all
x,\ell\in[k]
.

4.

\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)=\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right)
.

5.

If
\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap P_{\ell}=\emptyset
and
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i-1,i\}
, then
\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap Q_{\ell}=\emptyset
.

6.

If
\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\subseteq P_{1}\cup P_{\ell}
and
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i-1,i\}
, then
\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\subseteq Q_{1}\cup Q_{\ell}
.

Proof.

First, we show property
1
. We have
(x,y)\in Q_{\ell}
if and only if
(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y))\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}
. Using this fact, we have that
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is a partition of
V
. We now show that
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is symmetric: Suppose for contradiction that
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is not symmetric. Then, there exists
\ell\in[k]
and
(x,y)\in Q_{\ell}
such that
(y,x)\notin Q_{\ell}
. Then,
(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y))\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}
and
(\pi_{i}(y),\pi_{i}(x))\notin P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}
, but this implies that
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
is not symmetric, which is a contradiction. Hence,
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
is symmetric. Finally,
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
satisfies
(\ell,\ell)\in Q_{\ell}
for each
\ell\in[k]
: To see this, we observe that for each
\ell\in[k]\setminus\{i-1,i\}
, we have
(\pi_{i}(\ell),\pi_{i}(\ell))=(\ell,\ell)\in P_{\ell}=P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}
, so
(\ell,\ell)\in Q_{\ell}
. Additionally, because
(i-1,i-1)\in P_{i-1}
, we have
(i,i)=(\pi_{i}(i-1),\pi_{i}(i-1))\in Q_{\pi_{i}(i-1)}=Q_{i}
. Similar reasoning shows that
(i-1,i-1)\in Q_{i-1}
.

Next, we show property
3
. We observe that
(x,y)\in Q_{\ell}
if and only if
(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y))\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}
. Hence,
|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}|=\sum_{y=1}^{k}\mathbbm{1}\left[(x,y)\in Q_{\ell}\right]=\sum_{y=1}^{k}\mathbbm{1}\left[\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}\right]=\left|R_{\pi_{i}(x)}\cap P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}\right|
since
[k]=\{\pi_{i}(y)\}_{y\in[k]}
as
\pi_{i}
is a bijection.

Now, we show property
4
. Consider
\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq x}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)
. We have

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq i}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{i-1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{i-1,i\}}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap P_{i-1}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{i-1,i\}}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\quad\text{(by property \ref{item:swap_size})}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap P_{i-1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq i-1}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right).

Similar reasoning shows that

\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap Q_{i-1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq i-1}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i-1}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq i}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{i}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right).

Finally, for
x\notin\{i-1,i\}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{x}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq x}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{x}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{i-1}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{i}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{x,i-1,i\}}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{x}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{i}\right|\right)+\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{i-1}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{x,i-1,i\}}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\quad\text{(by property \ref{item:swap_size})}

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{x}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq x}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\textsc{obj}\left(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}\left(\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{x}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq x}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap Q_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=1}^{k}\left(\phi_{t}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{x}\right|\right)+\sum_{\ell\neq x}\phi_{n}\left(\left|R_{x}\cap P_{\ell}\right|\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\textsc{obj}\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\right).

Next, we prove property
2
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that property
2
does not hold. Then,
U(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\subseteq P_{1}
and there exists
(x,y)\in U(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k})
such that
(x,y)\notin Q_{1}
. Then,
(x,y)\in Q_{\ell}
for some
\ell\notin\{1,x,y\}
. Consequently,
\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}
. If
\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)
is not unhappy under
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
, then
\pi_{i}(\ell)=\pi_{i}(x)
or
\pi_{i}(\ell)=\pi_{i}(y)
, which would imply that
x=\ell
or
y=\ell
, a contradiction. Hence,
\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)
is unhappy under
P_{1}\ldots,P_{k}
, so
\left(\pi_{i}(x),\pi_{i}(y)\right)\in P_{1}
. Then,
(x,y)\in Q_{\pi_{i}(1)}=Q_{1}
, which contradicts the assumption that
(x,y)\notin Q_{1}
. Thus, all unhappy vertices under
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{k}
are in
Q_{1}
.

Next, we prove property
5
. We consider two cases based on the value of
\ell
.

1.

Suppose
\ell\in[i-2]
. Suppose for contradiction that
\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap P_{\ell}=\emptyset
and
\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap Q_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists

such that
(\ell,y)\in Q_{\ell}
. Therefore,
(\ell,y)=(\pi_{i}(\ell),\pi_{i}(y))\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}=P_{\ell}
, which contradicts
\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap P_{\ell}=\emptyset
.

2.

Suppose
\ell\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
. Suppose for contradiction that

and
\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap Q_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists
y\in[\ell-1]
such that
(\ell,y)\in\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap Q_{\ell}
. Then,
(\ell,\pi_{i}(y))=(\pi_{i}(\ell),\pi_{i}(y))\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell)}=P_{\ell}

\pi_{i}(y)=\begin{cases}i&y=i-1\\
i-1&y=i\\
y&y\notin\{i-1,i\}\end{cases}
, and in all cases,
\pi_{i}(y)<\ell
. Hence,
(\ell,\pi_{i}(y))\in\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap P_{\ell}

\overleftarrow{R}_{\ell}\cap P_{\ell}=\emptyset
.

Finally, we prove property
6
. Again, we consider two cases based on the value of
\ell
.

1.

Suppose
\ell\in[i-2]
. Suppose for contradiction that
\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\subseteq P_{1}\cup P_{\ell}
and
\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\not\subseteq Q_{1}\cup Q_{\ell}
. Then, there exists
y\in\{\ell+1,\ldots,k\}
and
\ell^{\prime}\in[k]\setminus\{1,\ell\}
such that
(\ell,y)\in Q_{\ell^{\prime}}
. Then,
(\ell,\pi_{i}(y))=(\pi_{i}(\ell),\pi_{i}(y))\in P_{\pi_{i}(\ell^{\prime})}
. We observe that
\pi_{i}(y)=\begin{cases}i-1&y=i\\
i&y=i-1\\
y&y\notin\{i-1,i\}\end{cases}
, and
\pi_{i}(\ell^{\prime})\in[k]\setminus\{1,\ell\}

is a witnesses the fact that

, which is a contradiction.

2.

Suppose
\ell\in\{i+1,\ldots,k\}
. Suppose for contradiction that
\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\subseteq P_{1}\cup P_{\ell}
and
\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\not\subseteq Q_{1}\cup Q_{\ell}

and
\ell^{\prime}\in[k]\setminus\{1,\ell\}

\overrightarrow{R}_{\ell}\not\subseteq P_{1}\cup P_{\ell}

∎

3
Graph Coverage Multiway Partition

We recall
Graph-Coverage-MP
. The input here is a graph
G=(V,E,w)
where

is the edge cost function,
and a set of terminal vertices

of
V

\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(S_{i})
,
where

\displaystyle b(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{u,v\}\cap S\neq\emptyset}w_{e}\ \forall\ S\subseteq V.

There is a close relationship between
Graph-Coverage-MP
and
Graph-Multiway-Cut
that we recall.
In
Graph-Multiway-Cut
, the input is identical to

while the goal is to find a partition

of

\displaystyle d(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|\{u,v\}\cap S|=1}w_{e}\ \forall\ S\subseteq V.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}b(S_{i})

and for every
u,v\in V
, we denote
\epsilon_{uv}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\max\{0,u_{j}-v_{j}\}
. We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every pair
u,v\in V
, we have that

\epsilon_{uv}=\epsilon_{vu}=\frac{1}{2}\lVert x_{u}-x_{v}\rVert_{1}.

Proof.

Since
x_{u},x_{v}\in\Delta_{k}
, we have that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}=1
. Hence we get,
\sum_{j=1}^{k}(u_{j}-v_{j})=0
. This gives us,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{j}\geq u_{j}}(u_{j}-v_{j})+\sum_{j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{j}<u_{j}}(u_{j}-v_{j})=0.

Consequently, we have that

\displaystyle\epsilon_{uv}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\max\{0,v_{j}-u_{j}\}=\sum_{j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{j}\geq u_{j}}(v_{j}-u_{j})=\sum_{j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{j}<u_{j}}(u_{j}-v_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\max\{0,u_{j}-v_{j}\}=\epsilon_{vu}.

By the same reasoning, we have that

\frac{1}{2}\lVert x_{u}-x_{v}\rVert_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon_{uv}+\epsilon_{vu}\right)=\varepsilon_{uv}.

∎

We will use the following properties of the exponential distribution.

Proposition 3.3

If

and

. Moreover, if we have independent random variables

We use Proposition

Lemma 3.1
.

Proof.

\displaystyle=Pr\left[\frac{z_{i}}{u_{i}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{z_{1}}{u_{1}},\cdots,\frac{z_{i-1}}{u_{i-1}},\frac{z_{i+1}}{u_{i+1}},\cdots\right\},\frac{z_{i}}{v_{i}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{z_{1}}{v_{1}},\cdots,\frac{z_{i-1}}{v_{i-1}},\frac{z_{i+1}}{v_{i+1}},\cdots\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq Pr\left[\frac{z_{i}}{\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{z_{1}}{\max\{u_{1},v_{1}\}},\cdots,\frac{z_{i-1}}{\max\{u_{i-1},v_{i-1}\}},\frac{z_{i+1}}{\max\{u_{i+1},v_{i+1}\}},\cdots\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}{\left(\sum_{j\neq i}\max\{u_{j},v_{j}\}\right)+\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}\quad\quad\text{(by Proposition \ref{prop:exp-dist})}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i}-\max\{0,u_{i}-v_{i}\}}{\left(\sum_{j\neq i}\left(u_{j}+\max\{0,v_{j}-u_{j}\}\right)\right)+u_{i}-\max\{0,u_{i}-v_{i}\}}

Eigenvalue estimates for the coulombic one-particle density matrix and the kinetic energy density matrix
Alexander V. Sobolev

Abstract
. Consider a bound state (an eigenfunction) ψ \psi of an atom with N N electrons. We study the spectra of the one-particle density matrix γ \gamma and of the one-particle kinetic energy density matrix τ \tau associated with ψ \psi . The paper contains two results. First, we obtain the bounds λ k ( γ ) ≤ C 1 k − 8 / 3 \lambda_{k}(\gamma)\leq C_{1}k^{-8/3} and λ k ​ ( τ ) ≤ C 2 ​ k − 2 \lambda_{k}(\tau)\leq C_{2}k^{-2} with some positive constants C 1 , C 2 C_{1},C_{2} that depend explicitly on the eigenfunction ψ \psi . The sharpness of these bounds is confirmed by the asymptotic results obtained by the author in earlier papers. The advantage of these bounds over the ones derived by the author previously, is their explicit dependence on the eigenfunction. Moreover, their new proofs are more elementary and direct. The second result is new and it pertains to the case where the eigenfunction ψ \psi vanishes at the particle coalescence points. In particular, this is true for totally antisymmetric ψ \psi . In this case the eigenfunction ψ \psi exhibits enhanced regularity at the coalescence points which leads to the faster decay of the eigenvalues: λ k ( γ ) ≤ C 3 k − 10 / 3 \lambda_{k}(\gamma)\leq C_{3}k^{-10/3} and λ k ( τ ) ≤ C 4 k − 8 / 3 \lambda_{k}(\tau)\leq C_{4}k^{-8/3} . The proofs rely on the estimates for the derivatives of the eigenfunction ψ \psi that depend explicitly on the distance to the coalescence points. Some of these estimates are borrowed directly from, and some are derived using the methods of a recent paper by S. Fournais and T. Ø. Sørensen.

Introduction

Consider on
\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
the Schrödinger operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{H}_{0}+V,\quad\mathcal{H}_{0}=-\Delta=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Delta_{k},

(1.1)

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\ -Z\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{|x_{k}|}+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\frac{1}{|x_{j}-x_{k}|},

describing an atom with
N
particles
(e.g. electrons)
with coordinates
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})
,
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
k=1,2,\dots,N
,
and a nucleus with charge
Z>0
.
The notation
\Delta_{k}
is used for
the Laplacian w.r.t. the variable
x_{k}
.
The operator
\mathcal{H}
acts on the Hilbert space
\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
and by
standard methods one proves that it is self-adjoint on the domain

D(\mathcal{H})=\textup{{{W}}}^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
,
see e.g.
[
30
, Theorem X.16]
. (Here and throughout the paper we use the standard notation

\textup{{{W}}}^{l,p}
for the Sobolev spaces, where

and
p
indicate the smoothness and summability
respectively).
Our methods allow consideration of the molecular
Schrödinger operator, but we restrict our attention to the atomic case for simplicity.
Let
\psi=\psi(\mathbf{x})
,
be an eigenfunction of the operator
\mathcal{H}
with an eigenvalue
E\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
\psi\in D(\mathcal{H})
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}-E)\psi=0.

For each

, we represent

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}=(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},x_{j}),\quad\textup{where}\ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{N}),

with obvious modifications if
j=1
or
j=N
.
The
one-particle density matrix
is defined as the function

(1.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}\overline{\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},x)}\,\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},y)\,d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},\ \textup{a.e.}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\ \textup{a.e.}\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Note that traditionally, the one-particle density matrix is defined as

\gamma_{0}(y,x)
, see e.g.
[
28
]
, but the permutation
x\leftrightarrow y

will have no bearing on our results.

Introduce also the function

(1.4)

\displaystyle\tau_{0}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}\overline{\nabla_{x}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},x)}\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},y)\ d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j},

that we call the
one-particle kinetic energy density matrix
.
The choice of this term does not seem to be standard, but it is partly
motivated by the fact that the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tau_{0}(x,x)\,dx

gives the kinetic energy of the
N
particles, see e.g.

[
9
, Section 2A]
,
[
29
, Chapter 3]
or

[
28
, Section 4]
.
If we assume that the particles are spinless fermions (resp. bosons), i.e. that the eigenfunction
is totally antisymmetric
(resp. symmetric) with respect to the permutations
x_{k}\leftrightarrow x_{j}
,

j,k=1,2,\dots,N,j\not=k
,
then the formulas for
\gamma_{0}(x,y)
and
\tau_{0}(x,y)
take a more compact form:

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\ N\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}\overline{\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)}\,\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},y)\ d\hat{\mathbf{x}},

\displaystyle\tau_{0}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\ N\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}\overline{\nabla_{x}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)}\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},y)\,d\hat{\mathbf{x}},

We consider
\gamma_{0}
and

as integral kernels of self-adjoint non-negative operators that we denote

Since

Recall that in the physics literature

[

Both functions

[

,
9
,
28
,
29
,
35

for details and futher references,

[

,
17
,
26

[

,

[

]

\iop(\gamma_{0})
) and
[
33
]

(for

The purpose of the current paper is to offer a short elementary proof of
the eigenvalue bounds and to derive “improved” bounds for the case where the eigenfunction
vanishes at the particle coalescence points.
It is clear that it suffices to
study each term in (

) and (
1.4
) individually.
Moreover, using permutations of the variables it is sufficient to focus just on the last

) or (
1.4

\displaystyle\gamma(x,y)=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}\overline{\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)}\,\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},y)\,d\hat{\mathbf{x}},

\displaystyle\tau(x,y)=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}\overline{\nabla_{x}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)}\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},y)\,d\hat{\mathbf{x}},\ \quad\hat{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N-1}).

Throughout the paper we refer to these functions as the one-particle density matrix
and the one-particle kinetic energy density matrix, instead of (
1.3
)
and (
1.4
).
The main results are stated in terms of the
one-particle density

(1.7)

\displaystyle\rho(x)=\gamma(x,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}}|\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)|^{2}\,d\hat{\mathbf{x}}.

Let

The next two theorems constitute the main results of the paper.

.

Let

be an eigenfunction of the operator

defined in (

)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\big(\iop(\gamma)\big)\leq

\displaystyle\ Ck^{-\frac{8}{3}}\,\ \!{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}\,\rho\ \!{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}\,_{3/8},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\big(\iop(\tau)\big)\leq

\displaystyle\ Ck^{-2}\,\ \!{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}\,\rho\ \!{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}{\vrule depth=3.0pt,height=9.0pt}\,_{1/2}.

The constants

in the above bounds do not

\psi
but may depend on

E
and
N
.

Under the assumption (
1.5
) the bounds

(1.10)

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{x})=0\quad\textup{if}\quad x=x_{k},\quad\textup{for every}\ k=1,2,\dots,N-1.

Suppose that the eigenfunction

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\big(\iop(\gamma)\big)\leq

Remark 1.3
.

(1)

Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
extend
to the case of a molecule with several nuclei whose positions are fixed.
The modifications are straightforward.

(2)

One can also study the eigenvalues of the

n
-particle density matrix with arbitrary
n=1,2,\dots,N-1
,
see
[
29
, Section 3.1.5]
for the definition.
Some results in this direction were obtained in
[
20
]
using the approach of

[

]
.

As in
[
33
]
and
[
34
]
, the first step in the proof of the two main theorems is to factorize the operators

\iop(\gamma)

\iop(\tau)
as follows:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\iop(\gamma)=\Psi^{*}\Psi,\ \quad\iop(\tau)={\sf V}^{*}{\sf V},

where
\Psi:\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mapsto\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N-3})
and

{\sf V}:\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mapsto\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N-3};\mathbb{C}^{3})
are the operators

(1.14)

\displaystyle(\Psi f)(\hat{\mathbf{x}})=\int\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)f(x)\,dx,\quad({\sf V}f)(\hat{\mathbf{x}})=\int\nabla_{x}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)f(x)\,dx.

Therefore
\lambda_{k}(\iop(\gamma))=s_{k}(\Psi)^{2}
and

\lambda_{k}(\iop(\tau))=s_{k}({\sf V})^{2}
,
k=1,2,\dots
, where

s_{k}(A)
denote the singular values (
s
-values) of a compact operator

. Thus Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
reduce to estimating singular values of
\Psi
and
{\sf V}
.

A general theory of spectral estimates for integral operators was developed in
[
2
]
.
It demonstrates that the fall-off rate of the singular values
increases together
with the smoothness of the integral kernel. Quantitatively, in
[
2
]

the eigenvalues are estimated via suitable Sobolev or Besov norms of the kernels.
The author applied such general estimates to
the operators
\Psi
and
{\sf V}
in

[
33
]
and
[
34
]
. In the current paper we suggest a direct, more elementary approach,
which makes the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
self-contained.
We bypass the general bounds of
[
2
]
by
directly estimating the singular values via the Fourier transform of
\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)
in the variable
x
.
In order to obtain suitable bounds for the Fourier transform one needs to control the regularity
of
\psi
.
Regularity of solutions for elliptic equations is a well-studied classical subject.
In particular, due to the
analyticity of the Coulomb potential
|x|^{-1}
for
x\not=0

the classical results ensure that the function
\psi
is real analytic away from the particle
coalescence points.
At the same time, as shown by
T. Kato
[
24
]
, the function
\psi
is Lipschitz on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{3N}
.
Further regularity properties
of
\psi
were
obtained, e.g. in
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
22
,
23
]
.
The results of the paper

[
15
]
by S. Fournais and T. Ø. Sørensen, are of decisive importance for us, as they
contain bounds on derivatives of
\psi
of arbitrary order with
explicit dependence on the distance of
x
to the coalescence points. These results
ensure a suitable Fourier transform estimates that lead to (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).

The estimates from
[
15
]
are not sufficient for the proof of
Theorem
1.2
however, since under the condition (
1.10
) the
eigenfunction
\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)
has an improved regularity in the neighbourhood of
the coalescence points. Precisely,
in addition to being Lipschitz, it also has bounded second derivatives with respect to
the variable

, see
[
20
]
.
Although this fact was not pointed out in
[
15
]
, it
can be easily derived
using the regularity result in
[
13
]
.
We use this observation to obtain an “improved” variant of the bounds from

[
15
]
using a slight modification
of the method of
[
15
]
as proposed in
[
21
]
.
As a result, the Fourier transform of

decays at infinity faster

.

Coordinates.

As mentioned earlier, we use the following standard notation for the coordinates:

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})
, where

,
j=1,2,\dots,N
.
The vector

is usually represented in the form

\mathbf{x}=(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)
with

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{3N-3}
,

.
The notation

is used for the open ball of radius
R>0
centred at

,
and
|B(t,R)|
denotes its volume (

For instance,

and

.

Indicators.
For any set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
\mathbbm{1}_{\Lambda}
its indicator function (or indicator).

Derivatives.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
If

and

, then

is defined in the standard way:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{m}=\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime\prime}}^{m^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime\prime\prime}}^{m^{\prime\prime\prime}}.

Bounds.

For two non-negative numbers (or functions)

X
and
Y
depending on some parameters,
we write

(or

) if
X\leq CY
with
some positive constant

independent of those parameters.
If

, then we say that

.
To avoid confusion we may sometimes comment on the nature of

Integral operators.

We introduce the notation
\iop(T):\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mapsto\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N-3})

for the integral operator with the kernel

.

2.
Regularity of
\psi

In this section we collect the pointwise
bounds for derivatives of the eigenfunction
\psi
of arbitrary order with explicit dependence
on the distance to the coalescence points.

2.1.
Bounds

Denote by
\Sigma
the coalescence set:

\displaystyle\Sigma=\bigg\{\mathbf{x}=(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}:|x|\prod_{1\leq k\leq N-1}|x_{k}-x|=0\bigg\}.

Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\dc(\mathbf{x})=\min\{|x|,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|x-x_{k}|:\ 1\leq k\leq N-1\},

defines the distance from
\mathbf{x}
to
\Sigma
.
Introduce also the capped distance
\lambda(\mathbf{x}):=\min\{1,\dc(\mathbf{x})\}
.
The functions
\dc(\,\cdot\,)
and
\lambda(\,\cdot\,)
are
Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
=1

(see
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
): for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}
we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\dc(\mathbf{x})-\dc(\mathbf{y})|\leq|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|,\ \quad|\lambda(\mathbf{x})-\lambda(\mathbf{y})|\leq|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|.

The following bound for the derivatives of
\psi

is a consequence of
[
15
, Corollary 1.2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For all
\mathbf{x}\notin\Sigma
, and for
arbitrary
R>0
, we have the bound

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{m}\psi(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)|\lesssim\big(1+\lambda(\mathbf{x})^{1-|m|}\big)\,\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(B(\mathbf{x},R))},\,\quad\textup{for all}\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3},

with a constant that does not depend on
\mathbf{x}
and
\psi
, but may depend on
m
and
R
.

The estimate (
2.3
) will be sufficient for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
With regard to Theorem
1.2
,
we shall see
that under the assumption (
1.10
) the function
\psi
has “enhanced” regularity at the
coalescence points
x=x_{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots,N-1
,
but not at
x=0
. To account for the singularity at
x=0

we introduce the Lipschitz function

(2.4)

\displaystyle F_{0}(x)=-\frac{Z}{2}|x|\,\theta(x),

where
\theta\in\textup{{{C}}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is such that
\theta(x)=1
for
|x|<1
.
Then we have the following result:

.

Assume that (

) holds.
Then for any

and for all

, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{m}\,\big(e^{-F_{0}(x)}\,\psi(\mathbf{x})\big)|\lesssim(1+\lambda(\mathbf{x})^{2-|m|})\,\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(B(\mathbf{x},R))},

The constants in (

the function

but we assume that

is fixed throughout the paper
and ignore the dependence on

in this and all the subsequent inequalities.

The rest of this section is focused on the proof of Theorem

.
It requires detailed information
on the structure of the function

, which is discussed next.

2.2.
The Jastrow factors

The starting point of analysis in
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
22
]

and in many other papers on the subject, is the representation
\psi=e^{F}\phi
, where
F
is a
function that satisfies

F,\nabla F\in\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}_{\tiny{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
and
that captures the main singularity of the solution, so that

\phi
is more regular than
\psi
.
In the mathematical physics literature the function
e^{F}
is often called
a
Jastrow factor
, see e.g.
[
19
]
.
After the substitution the equation (
1.2
) rewrites as

(2.6)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi-2\nabla F\cdot\nabla\phi+(V-\Delta F-|\nabla F|^{2}-E)\phi=0.

More precisely, if
\psi\in\textup{{{W}}}^{1,2}_{\tiny{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
is a weak
solution of (
1.2
),
then we also have
\phi\in\textup{{{W}}}^{1,2}_{\tiny{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})

and
\phi
is a weak solution of (
2.6
).
If one chooses
F
to be

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigg(-\frac{Z}{2}|x_{j}|+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j<k\leq N}|x_{j}-x_{k}|\bigg),

as in
[
25
]
,
then
\nabla F
is bounded,

\Delta F=V
, and hence all the coefficients in (
2.6
) are uniformly bounded.
The function
F
itself however is not bounded. To remedy this,
we take the following regularized variant of (
2.7
).
Let
\theta\in\textup{{{C}}}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be as in the definition
(
2.4
).
From now on we assume that
F
is given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x})=-\frac{Z}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|x_{j}|\theta(x_{j})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|x_{j}-x_{k}|\theta(x_{j}-x_{k}),

so that

(2.9)

\displaystyle F,\,\nabla F,\,\nabla^{k}(V-\Delta F)\in\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3N}),\quad\textup{for all}\quad k=0,1,\dots.

A different regularization (still satisfying (
2.9
)) of the Jastrow factor was used in

[
22
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
15
]
.
The function
F(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)
naturally splits into two components:

F(\hat{\mathbf{x}},x)=F_{0}(x)+F_{1}(\mathbf{x})
, where
F_{0}
is as defined in (
2.4
) and

\displaystyle F_{1}(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq k\leq N-1}|x-x_{k}|\,\theta(x-x_{k})

(2.10)

\displaystyle\ \quad\quad-\frac{Z}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}|x_{j}|\,\theta(x_{j})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N-1}|x_{j}-x_{k}|\,\theta(x_{j}-x_{k}).

The function
e^{F}
is only Lipschitz but,
since the coefficients in (
2.6
) are bounded, we have

\phi\in\textup{{{C}}}^{1,\mu}_{\tiny{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
for
all
\mu\in(0,1)
, see
[
22
, Proposition 1.5]
.
Moreover, using the factorization

\psi=e^{F}\phi
it was shown in
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
that for all

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}
and for any positive pair

such that

, the bound holds:

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},r))}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},R))},

with a constant independent of
\psi
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}
.
Slightly more general estimates
were found in

[
15
, Proposition A.2]
, see also
[
21
, Lemma 4.1]
for a shorter proof:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},r))}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},r))}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},R))},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},r))}+\|\nabla\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},r))}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},R))}.

To improve the
\textup{{{C}}}^{1,\mu}
-regularity of
\phi
, we
introduce yet another Jastrow factor,

\displaystyle F_{3}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{2-\pi}{12\pi}Z\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N}x_{j}\cdot x_{k}\ln\big(|x_{j}|^{2}+|x_{k}|^{2}\big)\theta(x_{j})\theta(x_{k}).

According to
[

, the function

defined as

has

Proposition 2.3
.

The function
\tilde{\phi}(\mathbf{x})

belongs to
\textup{{{W}}}^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
and for any pair

,
0<r<R
, and all

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{\textup{{{W}}}^{2,\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},r))}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(B(\mathbf{x}_{0},R))},

with a constant independent of
\psi
and
\mathbf{x}_{0}
.

The function

belongs to
\textup{{{W}}}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
for all

, but not for

. This
is not sufficient for the

The derivative

3.
Compact operators

Here we provide necessary information about compact operators.
Most of the listed facts can be found in
[
3
, Chapter 11]
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
be separable Hilbert spaces.
Let
A:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
be a compact operator.
If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{G}
and
A=A^{*}\geq 0
, then
\lambda_{k}(A)
,
k=1,2,\dots
,
denote the positive eigenvalues of
A

numbered in descending order counting multiplicity.
For arbitrary spaces
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{G}
and compact
A
, by
s_{k}(A)>0
,

k=1,2,\dots
, we denote the singular values of

A
defined by
s_{k}(A)^{2}=\lambda_{k}(A^{*}A)=\lambda_{k}(AA^{*})
.
We use the notation
\mathbf{S}_{p},p>0
for the classical Schatten-von Neumann classes,
i.e. the classes of compact operators
A
such that

\displaystyle\|A\|_{p}:=\bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\,s_{k}(A)^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Recall that
\|A\|_{p}
defines a norm (resp. quasi-norm) if
p\geq 1
(resp.
p<1
).
It is clear that
s_{k}(A)\leq k^{-1/p}\|A\|_{p}
. The following estimate from

[
3
, Chapter 11, Theorem 4.7]
will be useful.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbf{S}_{p},0<p<\infty
and
{\rm rank}\,K\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}s_{k}(A)^{p}\leq\|A-K\|_{p}^{p}.

In particular,

\displaystyle s_{2n}(A)\leq n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,\|A-K\|_{p}.

If
s_{k}(A)\lesssim k^{-1/p},k=1,2,\dots
,
with some
p>0
, then we say that
A\in\mathbf{S}_{p,\infty}
and denote

\displaystyle\|A\|_{p,\infty}=\sup_{k}s_{k}(A)k^{\frac{1}{p}},

so that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|A^{*}A\|_{p/2,\infty}=\|AA^{*}\|_{p/2,\infty}=\|A\|_{p,\infty}^{2}.

Clearly,
\mathbf{S}_{p}\subset\mathbf{S}_{p,\infty}
and
\|A\|_{p,\infty}\leq\|A\|_{p}
.
The class
\mathbf{S}_{p,\infty}
is a complete linear space with the quasi-norm
\|A\|_{p,\infty}
.
For all
p>0
the functional
\|A\|_{p,\infty}

satisfies the following “triangle” inequality for
operators

:

4.
Singular values of integral operators

4.1.
Main estimate

The membership of integrals operators in various classes of compact operators, including

\mathbf{S}_{p}
and
\mathbf{S}_{p,\infty}
, has been comprehensively investigated by
M.S. Birman and M.Z. Solomyak, in
[
2
]
. In this monograph
such properties are formulated in terms of functional classes to which the integral kernels
belong.
Although the results that we need can be derived from
[
2
]
, as in

[
33
,
34
]
,
the integral kernels that we consider admit a more elementary and direct approach,
which we describe below.

We are interested in integral operators whose kernels imitate the structure
of the eigenfunction
\psi
. More precisely, we consider integral operators

\iop(T)
with scalar kernels
T(t,x)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{l},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
l\geq 1,d\geq 1
,
satisfying the following condition.
Let
z_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{l}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

k=1,2,\dots,N
, be some functions of
t\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
and let
X(t)=\{z_{1}(t),z_{2}(t),\dots,z_{N}(t)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume that
for some
\alpha>-d
, for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
the function
T(t,x)
is such that

\displaystyle T(t,\,\cdot\,)\in\textup{{{C}}}^{\varkappa}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus X(t)),\quad\varkappa=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor+d+1,

and that it satisfies the bounds

(4.1)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{j}T(t,x)|\leq A(t,x)\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big(1\wedge|x-z_{k}(t)|\big)^{\alpha-|j|}\bigg),\quad|j|\leq\varkappa,

with some non-negative function
A(t,x)
. Introduce the notation

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(r)}=[-r/2,r/2)^{d},r>0;\quad\mathcal{C}^{(r)}_{n}=\mathcal{C}^{(r)}+n,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

If
r=1
then we omit the superscript “
(r)
” and write
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
,

\mathcal{C}_{n}=\mathcal{C}+n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. All constants in the subsequent inequalities may depend
on the dimensions
l
,
d
, numbers
\alpha
,
r
and
N
, but they will not depend on the functions
z_{k}(t)
and
A(t,x)
.
Our main objective in this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the kernel
T
be as specified above, and let

a\in\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}_{\textup{\tiny loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose
that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the function

A_{n}(t):=\|A(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)}_{n})}
is such that

A_{n}\in\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
.
If
\alpha>-d/2
, then the operator
\iop(T)a

belongs to
\mathbf{S}_{q,\infty}
with
q^{-1}=1+\alpha d^{-1}
, and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\iop(T)a\|_{q,\infty}\lesssim\bigg[\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|A_{n}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})}^{q}\,\|a\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{n})}^{q}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}},

under the assumption that the right-hand side of the above inequality is finite.
The implicit constant in (
4.3
)
does not depend on the functions
z_{k}
,
A
,
a
.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

Although we assume that the kernel
T(t,x)
in the above theorem is scalar, the estimate
(
4.3
) also holds for vector-valued kernels if the conditions of Theorem

4.1
are satisfied for each component. To see this it suffices to use the bound (
3.3
).

(2)

The bound (
4.3
) is similar
to the one obtained in
[
33
, Theorem 3.4]
with the help of the general
estimates from
[
2
]
.
In contrast to
(
4.3
), the bound in
[
33
]
contained

\textup{{{L}}}^{p}
-norms of
a
with some
p=p(\alpha)\geq 2
, instead of
the
\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}
-norms of
a
.

The proof relies on two lemmata below.

4.2.
Fourier transform

First we examine the Fourier transform of functions
u=u(x)
satisfying a bound similar to (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}\}
be a finite collection of points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let the function
u
be such that for some
\alpha>-d
it satifies

\displaystyle u\in\textup{{{C}}}^{\varkappa}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus X),\quad\varkappa=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor+d+1,

and the following bounds hold:

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{m}u(x)|\lesssim\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}^{(r)}}(x)\big(1+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(|x-a_{k}|\wedge 1)^{\alpha-|m|}\big),

with some
r>0
and
for all
x\notin X
and all
|m|\leq\varkappa
.
Then

(4.5)

\displaystyle|\hat{u}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{-\alpha-d},\quad\langle\xi\rangle=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}},

where the implicit constant does not depend on the set
X
,
but may depend on
r
and
N
.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to prove (
4.5
) for
|\xi|\geq 1
.

First we prove the required estimate for
\alpha\leq 0
, so that
(
4.4
) amounts to

(4.6)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{m}u(x)|\lesssim\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}^{(r)}}(x)\big(1+\sum_{k=1}^{N}|x-a_{k}|^{\alpha-|m|}\big),\quad|m|\leq\varkappa.

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

introduce a smooth partition of unity
\eta(x)=\eta(x,\varepsilon)
,

\zeta(x)=\zeta(x,\varepsilon)
, such that
\eta+\zeta=1
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and

\displaystyle\eta(x;\varepsilon)=0,\quad

\displaystyle\textup{if}\quad\dist(x;X)\geq\varepsilon;

\displaystyle\zeta(x;\varepsilon)=0,\quad

\displaystyle\textup{if}\quad\dist(x;X)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{m}\eta(x)|\lesssim\varepsilon^{-|m|},\quad|\partial_{x}^{m}\zeta(x)|\lesssim\varepsilon^{-|m|},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},

where the implicit constants do not depend on the set
X
.
Represent
\hat{u}(\xi)=I_{0}(\xi,\varepsilon)+I_{1}(\xi,\varepsilon)
with

\displaystyle I_{0}(\xi;\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,u(x)\,\eta(x;\varepsilon)\,dx\quad\textup{and}\ I_{1}(\xi;\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,u(x)\,\zeta(x;\varepsilon)\,dx,

and estimate
I_{0}
and
I_{1}
separately.
Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{k}=\{x\in\mathcal{C}^{(r)}:\dist(x;X)=|x-a_{k}|\},\quad k=1,2,\dots,N,

so
\mathcal{C}^{(r)}=\cup_{k}\Omega_{k}
. Then, by (
4.6
),

\displaystyle|I_{0}(\xi,\varepsilon)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{\Omega_{k}\cap B(a_{k},\varepsilon)}|x-a_{k}|^{\alpha}\,dx

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{|x-a_{k}|\leq\varepsilon}|x-a_{k}|^{\alpha}\,dx\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha+d}.

To estimate
I_{1}

integrate by parts
\varkappa
times
using the relation
|\xi|^{-2}\,i\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}\,e^{-ix\cdot\xi}=e^{-ix\cdot\xi}
:

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}(-|\xi|^{-2}\,i\xi\cdot\nabla_{x})^{\varkappa}\,\big(u(x)\,\zeta(x;\varepsilon)\big)\,dx.

It is clear that

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim|\xi|^{-\varkappa}\,\sum_{p,s:|p|+|s|=\varkappa}\,\int\,|\partial_{x}^{s}u(x)|\,|\partial_{x}^{p}\zeta(x;\varepsilon)|\,dx.

First estimate the integral with
p=0
:

\displaystyle\int\,|\partial_{x}^{s}u(x)|\,dx\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\int_{\Omega_{k}\setminus B(a_{k},\varepsilon/2)}(1+|x-a_{k}|^{\alpha-\varkappa})\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\int\limits_{x\in\mathcal{C}^{(r)},|x-a_{k}|>\varepsilon/2}(1+|x-a_{k}|^{\alpha-\varkappa})\,dx\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha-\varkappa+d},

where we have used the fact that
\alpha-\varkappa<-d
. Suppose that
|p|\geq 1
. By the definition
of
\zeta
, the derivative
\partial_{x}^{p}\zeta
is supported on the set where

\varepsilon/2\leq\dist(x,X)\leq\varepsilon
.
Therefore, by (
4.7
),

\displaystyle\int\,|\partial_{x}^{s}u(x)|\,|\partial_{x}^{p}\zeta(x;r)|\,dx\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\int_{(\Omega_{k}\cap B(a_{k},\varepsilon))\setminus B(a_{k},\varepsilon/2)}(1+|x-a_{k}|^{\alpha-|s|})\,\varepsilon^{-|p|}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ (1+\varepsilon^{\alpha-|s|})\varepsilon^{-|p|}\varepsilon^{d}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha-\varkappa+d}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|I_{1}(\xi,\varepsilon)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-\varkappa}\varepsilon^{\alpha-\varkappa+d}.

Combining this bound with (
4.2
) we obtain:

\displaystyle|\hat{u}(\xi)|\lesssim\big(\varepsilon^{\alpha+d}+|\xi|^{-\varkappa}\varepsilon^{\alpha-\varkappa+d}\big).

Taking
\varepsilon=|\xi|^{-1}
we conclude the proof of the claimed bound
(
4.5
) for
\alpha\leq 0
.

For arbitrary
\alpha>0
, let

m=\lceil\alpha\rceil<\varkappa
, so that
-1<\alpha-m\leq 0
.
Rewrite the Fourier transform of
u
integrating by parts
m
times:

\displaystyle\hat{u}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}(-|\xi|^{-2}\,i\xi\cdot\nabla_{x})^{m}\,u(x)\,dx,

so that

\displaystyle|\hat{u}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-m}\sum_{|n|=m}|\widehat{(\partial^{n}u)}(\xi)|.

Since
\beta:=\alpha-m>-1
, the right-hand side is finite for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The function

v=\partial^{n}_{x}u,|n|=m
, satisfies
v\in\textup{{{C}}}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus X)

with
\omega=\lfloor\beta\rfloor+d+1
, and the bounds hold:

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{s}v(x)|\lesssim(1+\sum_{k}|x-a_{k}|^{\beta-s}),\quad|s|\leq\omega.

Since
\beta\leq 0
, it follows from the first part of the proof,
that

\displaystyle|\hat{u}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-m}\,|\xi|^{-\beta-d}=|\xi|^{-\alpha-d},

which gives (
4.5
) and completes the proof.

4.3.
Operators on the cube

The next step towards the proof of Theorem
4.1
is the
s
-value estimate
for integral operators on the cube.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let the kernel
T
be as in Theorem

4.1
, and assume that
A(t,x)=A(t,x)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)}}(x)
.
Suppose that

\tilde{A}(t):=\|A(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)})}
is such that

\tilde{A}\in\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
.
If
\alpha>-d/2
, then
the operator
\iop(T)

belongs to
\mathbf{S}_{q,\infty}
, with
q^{-1}=1+\alpha d^{-1}
, and

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|\iop(T)\|_{q,\infty}\lesssim\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})}.

The implicit constant in (
4.9
) does not depend on the functions
z_{k}
.

Proof.

Since
T(t,x)=T(t,x)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)}}(x)
, we consider

\iop(T)
as an operator acting on
\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)})
.
Denote

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{\nu}(t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)}}\,e^{-i\nu\cdot x}\,T(t,x)\,dx,\quad\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

By Lemma
4.3
,
|\hat{T}_{\nu}(t)|\lesssim\tilde{A}(t)\langle\nu\rangle^{-\alpha-d}
,
so that
\hat{T}_{\nu}\in\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})
and for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
the series

\displaystyle T(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,e^{i\nu\cdot x}\,\hat{T}_{\nu}(t),

converges in
\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)})
.
For a fixed
M\geq 1
the rank of the operator

\iop(T_{M}):\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)})\mapsto\textup{{{L}}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})

with kernel

\displaystyle T_{M}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{|\nu|\leq M}\,e^{i\nu\cdot x}\,\hat{T}_{\nu}(t),

does not exceed

\displaystyle m=m(M):=\#\{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\nu|\leq M\}\asymp M^{d}.

Therefore, by Proposition
3.1
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle s_{2m}(\iop(T))\leq m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\|\iop(T)-\iop(T_{M})\|_{2}.

Estimate the Hilbert-Schmidt norm on the right-hand side:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{l}}\,\int_{\mathcal{C}^{(2\pi)}}|T(t,x)-

\displaystyle T_{M}(t,x)|^{2}\,dxdt=\sum_{|\nu|>M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{l}}\,|\hat{T}_{\nu}(t)|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}^{2}\,\sum_{|\nu|>M}\,\langle\nu\rangle^{-2(\alpha+d)}\lesssim M^{-2\alpha-d}\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim m^{-\frac{2}{q}+1}\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}^{2}.

By (
4.10
),

\displaystyle s_{2m}(\iop(T))\lesssim m^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(m^{-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}}\ \|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}\bigg)=m^{-\frac{1}{q}}\,\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}.

Thus for any
k:2m(M)\leq k<2m(M+1)
, we have

\displaystyle s_{k}(\iop(T))\leq s_{2m(M)}(\iop(T))\lesssim\bigg(\frac{k}{m(M)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}k^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}\leq\bigg(\frac{2m(M+1)}{m(M)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}k^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}.

Since
m(M+1)\lesssim m(M)
with a constant independent of
M
, the singular values satisfy the
bound
s_{k}(\iop(T))\lesssim k^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}

for all
k\geq 2m(1)
. The same bound also holds for all
k:1\leq k\leq 2m(1)
due to the straighforward estimate
s_{k}(\iop(T))\leq\|\iop(T)\|_{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{A}\|_{\textup{{{L}}}^{2}}
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

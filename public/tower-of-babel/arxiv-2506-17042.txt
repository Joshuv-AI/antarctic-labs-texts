Mass Functions and Asymptotic Behavior of Caloric Functions on Affine Buildings
Effie Papageorgiou, Bartosz Trojan

Abstract
. We study the large-time asymptotic behavior of solutions to the discrete-time heat equation, i.e., caloric functions, on affine buildings, including those without transitive group actions. For each p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] p\in[1,\infty] , we introduce a notion of a p p -mass function and prove that caloric functions with initial data belonging to certain weighted- ℓ 1 \ell^{1} spaces or to the radial ℓ 1 \ell^{1} class, asymptotically decouple as the product of this mass function and the heat kernel. These results extend classical analogues from Euclidean spaces and symmetric spaces of non-compact type to the non-Archimedean setting, and remain valid even for exotic buildings beyond the Bruhat–Tits framework. We characterize the spatial concentration of heat kernels in p p -norms and describe the geometry of associated critical regions. Our results highlight substantial differences in the asymptotic regimes depending on the value of p p , and clarify the interplay between volume growth and heat diffusion.

Introduction

At the heart of harmonic analysis lies the study of solutions to the heat equation with the central rôle
played by its fundamental solution
k
, called heat kernel. In Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the heat kernel
is given by

k_{t}(x)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},

representing the distribution of the heat at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
at time
t>0
emanating at the point source at
the origin. Its smoothness, decay, and scaling properties make it a powerful tool in harmonic analysis, PDE theory, and
probability theory. For
p\in[1,\infty]
, a straightforward computation yields

\|k_{t}\|_{L^{p}}=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2p^{\prime}}}p^{-\frac{d}{2p}}

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate exponent to
p
, defined by
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. If
u
is a caloric function
with the initial datum
f
belonging to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that is it solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x),&\text{for }t>0\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\[0.5pt]
u(0,x)=f(x),\end{cases}

then
u(t,x)=k_{t}*f(x)
. Hence, by the Young’s convolution inequality we have

\|u(t;\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\leqslant\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|k_{t}\|_{L^{p}}.

In particular,
u(t;\cdot)
uniformly goes to zero as time goes to infinity. The classical result says that
u(t,\cdot)

for large time looks like
M_{1}(f)k_{t}
, where

M_{1}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x){\>\rm d}x.

More precisely, for each
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{t}\|_{L^{p}}}\big\|u(t;\cdot)-M_{1}(f)k_{t}\big\|_{L^{p}}=0.

This result has been also studied on certain manifolds of non-negative Ricci curvature under the assumption that
the volume function satisfies the doubling condition, see
[
11
]
. In the forthcoming paper
[
12
]
,
we investigate the discrete counterpart. On the other end, Vázquez in
[
26
]
,
showed that for real hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{n}
, which are examples of negatively curved manifolds,
if the initial datum
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
is
radial
and

M_{1}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}f(x){\>\rm d}x,

then

(0.1)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}_{n})}}\|u(t;\cdot)-M_{1}(f)k_{t}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}_{n})}=0.

However, there is a radial
f
for which
\|u(t;\cdot)-M_{1}(f)k_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{n})}
is not

o(\|k_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{n}})
. It has also been shown that for initial datum which is not radial the convergence in
(
0.1
) may fail. The work
[
26
]
has been extended to higher rank symmetric spaces of non-compact type in

[
4
]
. Vázquez’s negative result for
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{n})

motivated the work
[
18
]
. To be more precise, let
\mathbb{X}=G/K
be a symmetric space of non-compact type.
In
[
18
]
, for each
p\in[1,\infty]
, the authors introduced certain function spaces
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{X})

containing
L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
, for which the mass function
M_{p}(f)
is defined by

M_{p}(f)=\begin{cases}\hat{f}\big(i\rho(2/p-1)\big)&\text{if }p\in[1,2),\\
\hat{f}(0)&\text{if }p\in[2,\infty]\end{cases}

where
\hat{f}
denotes the spherical transform of
f
. The authors then showed that if
f
is a bi-
K
-invariant
initial datum belonging to
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{X})
, then

(0.2)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}}\big\|u(t;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)k_{t}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}=0.

However, the convergence in (
0.2
) may fail if
f
is not bi-
K
-invariant. To study initial data that are not
necessarily bi-
K
-invariant, in
[
19
]
the first-named author to any compactly supported function
f
on
\mathbb{X}

associated a function
M_{p}(f)
on
\mathbb{X}
for which (
0.2
) again holds true.

The aim of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of caloric functions on certain discrete spaces. Our main goal
is to investigate the non-Archimedean counterparts to symmetric spaces of non-compact type, that is, affine buildings.
They are complementary pieces at the heart of Lie theory
[
13
,
9
]
.
Apart from strong topological differences, real and non-Archimedean simple Lie groups share many combinatorial and geometric
properties. From the geometric point of view, they both act, with strong transitivity properties, on contractible spaces
carrying nice non-positively curved complete distances. The corresponding spaces for groups over totally disconnected local
fields are Bruhat–Tits buildings. Bruhat–Tits buildings, or more generally affine buildings when no group is assumed to
act transitively on them, are unions of Euclidean tilings, called apartments, playing the rôle of maximal flats in
symmetric spaces. Apartments contain Weyl cones, also called sectors.

To precisely state our first result, we need to introduce some notation. Let
\Phi
be the type of the building, that is

\Phi
is the affine root system in
\mathfrak{a}
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is the
r
-dimensional Euclidean space on which
apartments are modeled, and let
\Phi^{++}
denote the set of indivisible positive roots in
\Phi.
By
W
we denote
the corresponding (spherical) Weyl group. Given a transition function
k
of the isotropic finite range random walk on
good vertices
V_{g}
of the building (called admissible for short), we set

\displaystyle k(1;x,y)

\displaystyle=k(x,y),

\displaystyle k(n+1;x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in V_{g}}k(n;x,z)k(z,y)\qquad(n,x,y)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times V_{g}\times V_{g}.

We consider the corresponding averaging operator acting on functions on
V_{g}
as

Af(x)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(y,x)f(y).

The Gelfand–Fourier transform of
A
, denoted by
\hat{A}
, is a
W
-invariant exponential polynomial expressed
as a combination of Macdonald’s spherical functions
P_{\omega}
. Let
\varrho
be the spectral radius of the random walk
and
\kappa=\varrho^{-1}\widehat{A}
. We fix a good vertex
o
. Let
\eta
be a properly weighted sum of positive roots,
see (
1.1
) for precise definition. At this stage it is enough to remember that
\eta
belongs to the positive Weyl
chamber. In the following theorem we summarize the main results proved in Theorems
2.4
,
2.5
and
2.6
.

Theorem A
.

Let
k
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building

\mathscr{X}
. Then

(i)

if
p\in[1,2)
,

\|k_{n}(o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{2p^{\prime}}}\varrho^{n}\kappa\left(\eta\left(\tfrac{2}{p}-1\right)\right)^{n};

(ii)

if
p=2
,

\|k_{n}(o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{2}}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{4}-\frac{|\Phi^{++}|}{2}}\varrho^{n};

(iii)

if
p\in(2,\infty]
,

\|k_{n}(o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}\varrho^{n}.

Let us observe that for
p\in(2,\infty]
, the asymptotic formula for the norm
\|k_{n}(o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}

is independent of
p
. For
p\in[1,2)
one only sees the rank and not the pseudodimension
|\Phi^{++}|
, and the exponential
decay in
n
is slower than for
p\in[2,\infty)
. And lastly, the case
p=2
is not a limit of the cases
[1,2)
nor

(2,\infty]
.

In each of Theorems
2.4
,
2.5
and
2.6
, in the second halves we describe the
\ell^{p}
concentration of
the heat kernel, namely, the regions
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}\subseteq V_{g}
such that

\|k_{n}(o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}(V_{g}\smallsetminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p})}=o(\|k_{n}(o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}})
.
This phenomenon reflects the interplay between the heat kernel decay on the one side and the volume growth on
the other. Concerning the continuous counterparts of affine buildings, in
[
10
, Corollary 5.7.3]

it was observed that the heat diffusion on real hyperbolic spaces propagates asymptotically with finite speed. This striking
phenomenon was later on extended to non-compact symmetric spaces
\mathbb{X}
, by analytic means in
[
6
]
and
probabilistic ones in
[
7
]
. Going beyond
L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
and considering the general
L^{p}(\mathbb{X})
setting has been
done in
[
3
]
. Theorem
A
is the building analogue of the latter.

Our next result concerns the asymptotic behavior of caloric functions. To be more precise, the caloric function
u
with
the initial datum
f\in\ell^{p}(V_{g})
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, is the solution to

(0.3)

\displaystyle u(n+1,x)

\displaystyle=Au(n,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(y,x)u(n,y)

\displaystyle u(0,x)

\displaystyle=f(x),\qquad(n,x)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times V_{g}.

It is easy to see that the unique solution to (
0.3
) is given by the formula

u(n,x)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k_{n}(y,x)f(y).

Before we define the
mass functions
we need some more notation: Let
\sigma(x,y)
denote the vectorial distance between

x\in V_{g}
and
y\in V_{g}
. The maximal boundary
\Omega
can be viewed as the set of all sectors tipped at
o
. For
any
x\in V
, we set
\Omega(o,x)
to be the set of all sectors tipped at
o
containing
x
. The maximal boundary
carries the harmonic measure
\nu
centered at
o
, such that
\nu(\Omega(o,x))
depends only on the vectorial distance

\sigma(o,x)
. Let
\chi_{0}
be the
fundamental character
of the coweight lattice, see (
1.3
) for the definition.
Lastly, let
h(x,y;\omega)
be the Busemann (horocycle) function for
x,y\in V_{g}
and
\omega\in\Omega
.
With all the notations we can introduce the Macdonalds function

P_{\sigma(x,y)}(z)=\int_{\Omega}e^{\langle{\eta+z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}\,\nu_{x}({\rm d}\omega),\qquad z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}.

In order to define the mass function, let us introduce proper weighted spaces: We say that
f
belongs to
\ell^{1}(w_{p})
,

, if

\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|w_{p}(y)<\infty

where the weight

is defined by the formula

w_{p}(y)=\begin{cases}\exp\big(\tfrac{2}{p}\langle{\eta},{\sigma(o,y)}\rangle\big),&\text{if }p\in[1,2),\\
\exp\big(\langle{\eta},{\sigma(o,x)}\rangle\big),&\text{if }p\in[2,\infty].\end{cases}

Now, for
f\in\ell^{1}(w_{p})
the mass function

is defined by the formula

(0.4)

\displaystyle M_{p}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\nu({\rm d}\omega),\qquad

\displaystyle\text{if }p\in[1,2),

or

(0.5)

\displaystyle M_{p}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P_{\sigma(o,x)}(0)}\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)P_{\sigma(y,x)}(0),

\displaystyle\text{if }p\in[2,\infty).

We say that a function

is radial if

depends only on
\sigma(o,x)
. In Section
4.2
, we show that the mass
function is well-defined for radial
f\in\ell^{1}(V_{g})
, and

(0.6)

M_{p}(f)\equiv\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)P_{\sigma(o,y)}(s_{p})

where

s_{p}=\begin{cases}\eta\Big(\tfrac{2}{p}-1\Big),&\text{if }p\in[1,2),\\
0&\text{if }p\in[2,\infty).\end{cases}

Let

If

its quotients in certain regions in the Crámer’s zone. The asymptotic formulas for the transition densities

This paper is organized as follows: In Section

. Since
\Phi
is irreducible, there is a unique highest root

\alpha_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\alpha_{i}
with
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We set

I_{0}=\{1,\dots,r\}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{g}=\{0\}\cup\{i\in I_{0}:m_{i}=1\}.

Let
\Phi^{\vee}=\{\alpha^{\vee}=\frac{2}{\langle{\alpha},{\alpha}\rangle}\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}
be the dual root system.
The
\operatorname{\mathbb{Z}-span}
of
\Phi^{\vee}
is the
co-root lattice

Q
, whose dual is the
weight lattice

L
.
Let
Q^{+}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathbb{N}_{0}\alpha^{\vee}
. The dual basis
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}\}
to

\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
consists of
fundamental co-weights
and its
\operatorname{\mathbb{Z}-span}
is the
co-weight lattice

P
.
Let
P^{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{N}_{0}\lambda_{i}
be the cone of
dominant co-weights
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{N}\lambda_{i}
be
the subcone of
strongly dominant co-weights
. For instance

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}

is a strongly dominant co-weight. Notice that

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\alpha^{\vee},

where
\Phi^{++}
denotes the set of indivisible positive roots in
\Phi
, that is the set of roots
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}

such that
\frac{1}{2}\alpha\notin\Phi^{+}
.

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
, by
\Pi_{\lambda}
we denote the saturation set of
\lambda
, that is

\Pi_{\lambda}=\{w.\mu:\mu\in P^{+},\,\mu\preceq\lambda,\,w\in W\},

whereas
\mu\preceq\lambda
, if and only if
\lambda-\mu\in Q^{+}
. If
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
we write
\lambda\gg\mu

whenever
\lambda-\Pi_{\mu}\subset P^{+}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the family of affine hyperplanes, called
walls
, being of the form

H_{\alpha;k}=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{a}:\langle x,\alpha\rangle=k\big\}

where
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Each wall determines two half-apartments

H^{-}_{\alpha;k}=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{a}:\langle x,\alpha\rangle\leqslant k\big\}\quad\text{and}\quad H^{+}_{\alpha;k}=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{a}:\langle x,\alpha\rangle\geqslant k\big\}.

Note that for a given
\alpha
, the family
H^{-}_{\alpha;k}
is increasing in
k
while the family
H^{+}_{\alpha;k}
is
decreasing. To each wall we associate
r_{\alpha;k}
the orthogonal reflection in
\mathfrak{a}

defined by

r_{\alpha;k}(x)=x-\big(\langle{x},{\alpha}\rangle-k\big)\alpha^{\vee}.

Set
r_{0}=r_{\alpha_{0};1}
, and
r_{i}=r_{\alpha_{i};0}
for each
i\in I_{0}
.

The
finite Weyl group

W
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak{a})
generated by
\{r_{i}:i\in I_{0}\}
. Let us denote
by
w_{0}
the longest element in
W
. The
fundamental sector
in
\mathfrak{a}
defined as

S_{0}=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{a}:\langle{x},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle\geqslant 0\text{ for all }i\in I_{0}\big\}=\bigoplus_{i\in I_{0}}\mathbb{R}_{+}\lambda_{i}=\bigcap_{i\in I_{0}}H^{+}_{\alpha_{i};0}

is the fundamental domain for the action of
W
on
\mathfrak{a}
.

The
affine Weyl group

W^{a}
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathfrak{a})
generated by
\{r_{i}:i\in I\}
. Observe that

W^{a}
is a Coxeter group. The hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}
give the geometric realization of its Coxeter complex
\Sigma_{\Phi}
. To see
this, let
C(\Sigma_{\Phi})
be the family of closures of the connected components of

\mathfrak{a}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H
. By
C_{0}
we denote the
fundamental chamber
(or
fundamental alcove
),
i.e.

C_{0}=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{a}:\langle{x},{\alpha_{0}}\rangle\leqslant 1\text{ and }\langle{x},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle\geqslant 0\text{ for all }i\in I_{0}\big\}=\bigcap_{i\in I_{0}}H^{+}_{\alpha_{i};0}\cap H^{-}_{\alpha_{0};1}

which is the fundamental domain for the action of
W^{a}
on
\mathfrak{a}
. Moreover, the group
W^{a}
acts simply transitively
on
C(\Sigma_{\Phi})
. This allows us to introduce a chamber system in
C(\Sigma_{\Phi})
: For two chambers
C
and
C^{\prime}
and

i\in I
, we set
C\sim_{i}C^{\prime}
if and only if
C=C^{\prime}
or there is
w\in W^{a}
such that
C=w.C_{0}
and

C^{\prime}=wr_{i}.C_{0}
.

The vertices of
C_{0}
are
\{0,\lambda_{1}/m_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}/m_{r}\}
. Let us denote the set of vertices of all

C\in C(\Sigma_{\Phi})
by
V(\Sigma_{\Phi})
. Under the action of
W^{a}
, the set
V(\Sigma_{\Phi})
is made up of
r+1

orbits
W^{a}.0
and
W^{a}.(\lambda_{i}/m_{i})
for all
i\in I_{0}
. Thus setting
\tau_{\Sigma_{\Phi}}(0)=0
, and

\tau_{\Sigma_{\Phi}}(\lambda_{i}/m_{i})=i
for
i\in I_{0}
, we obtain the unique labeling

\tau_{\Sigma_{\Phi}}:V(\Sigma_{\Phi})\rightarrow I
such that any chamber
C\in C(\Sigma_{\Phi})
has one vertex with each label.

For each simplicial automorphism
\varphi:\Sigma_{\Phi}\rightarrow\Sigma_{\Phi}
there is a permutation
\pi
of the set
I

such that for all chambers
C
and
C^{\prime}
, we have
C\sim_{i}C^{\prime}
if and only if
\varphi(C)\sim_{\pi(i)}\varphi(C^{\prime})
, and

\tau_{\Sigma_{\Phi}}(\varphi(v))=\pi(\tau_{\Sigma_{\Phi}}(v)),\qquad\text{for all }v\in V(\Sigma_{\Phi}).

A vertex
v
is called
special
if for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
there is
k
such that
v
belongs to
H_{\alpha;k}
.
The set of all special vertices is denoted by
V_{s}(\Sigma_{\Phi})
.

Given
\lambda\in P
and
w\in W^{a}
, the set
S=\lambda+w.S_{0}
is called a
sector
in
\Sigma_{\Phi}
with a

base vertex

\lambda
.

Moreover, by
[
1
, Corollary 3.20]
, an affine Coxeter complex
\Sigma_{\Phi}
uniquely determines the affine Weyl
group
W^{a}
but not a finite root system
\Phi
. In fact, the root systems
\text{C}_{r}
and
\text{BC}_{r}
have the same
affine Weyl group.

1.3.
Affine buildings

A building
\mathscr{X}
of type
\Sigma
is called an
affine building
if
\Sigma
is a Coxeter complex corresponding
to an affine Weyl group. Select a chamber
c_{0}
in
C(\mathscr{X})
and an apartment
\mathscr{A}_{0}
containing
c_{0}
. Using
an isomorphism
\psi_{0}:\mathscr{A}_{0}\rightarrow\Sigma
such that
\psi_{0}(c_{0})=C_{0}
, we define the labeling in
\mathscr{A}_{0}
by

\tau_{\mathscr{A}_{0}}(v)=\tau_{\Sigma}(\psi_{0}(v)),\qquad v\in V(\mathscr{A}_{0}).

Now, thanks to the building axioms the labeling can be uniquely extended to
\tau:V(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow I
. We turn

C(\mathscr{X})
into a chamber system over
I
by declaring that two chambers
c
and
c^{\prime}
are
i
-adjacent if they share all
vertices except the one of type
i
(equivalently, they intersect along an
i
-panel). For each
c\in C(\mathscr{X})
and
i\in I
,
we define

q_{i}(c)=\#\big\{c^{\prime}\in C(\mathscr{X}):c^{\prime}\sim_{i}c\big\}-1.

In all of the paper, we
assume
that
q_{i}(c)
only depends on
i
, i.e. that
q_{i}(c)
is independent of
c
, and therefore
the building
\mathscr{X}
is
regular
; we henceforth write
q_{i}
instead of
q_{i}(c)
. We also assume that
1<q_{i}(c)<\infty

and therefore the building
\mathscr{X}
is
thick
and
locally finite
. Notice that for Bruhat–Tits buildings this is
automatic. A vertex of
\mathscr{X}
is special if it is special in any of its apartments. The set of special vertices is denoted by

V_{s}
. We choose the finite root system
\Phi
in such a way that
\Sigma
is its Coxeter complex. In all cases except when the
affine group has type
\text{C}_{r}
or
\text{BC}_{r}
, the choice is unique. In the remaining cases we select
\text{C}_{r}

if
q_{0}=q_{r}
, otherwise we take
\text{BC}_{r}
. This guarantees that
q_{\tau(\lambda)}=q_{\tau(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})}

for all
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in P
, see the discussion in
[
16
, Section 2.13]
.

Let us define the set of
good
vertices
V_{g}
consisting of those
x\in V(\mathscr{X})
having the
type
\tau(x)\in I_{g}
. If
\Phi
is reduced, then all special vertices are good. However, we do not have this property
in the case of
\text{BC}_{r}
. Indeed, there are two types of special vertices,
0
and
r
, but only those
of type
0
are mapped to
P
.

For
\alpha\in\Phi^{++}
, we set
q_{\alpha}=q_{\alpha_{j}}
provided that
\alpha\in W.\alpha_{j}
for
j\in I
. We define

\tau_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\alpha\notin\Phi,\\
q_{\alpha}&\text{ if }\alpha\in\Phi,\text{ but }\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha,2\alpha\notin\Phi,\\
q_{\alpha_{0}}&\text{ if }\alpha,\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\in\Phi,\text{ and therefore }2\alpha\notin\Phi,\\
q_{\alpha}q^{-1}_{\alpha_{0}}&\text{ if }\alpha,2\alpha\in\Phi,\text{ and therefore }\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\notin\Phi.\end{cases}

Notice that

\tau_{\alpha}\tau_{2\alpha}^{2}>1,\qquad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{++}.

In this paper we often use the vector
\eta\in\mathfrak{a}
,

(1.1)

\eta=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\log\tau_{\alpha}\,\alpha,\\

The basic properties of
\eta
are summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

We have

(1.2)

\eta=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\log\big(\tau_{\alpha}\tau_{2\alpha}^{2}\big)\,\alpha.

Furthermore,

(i)

for each
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
\alpha\in\Phi^{++}
,
\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle\leqslant 4\langle{\eta},{\lambda}\rangle
.

(ii)

w_{0}.\eta=-\eta
;

(iii)

for each
i\in I_{0}
,
\langle{\eta},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\log(\tau_{\alpha_{i}}\tau_{2\alpha_{i}}^{2})\langle{\alpha_{i}},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle
;

(iv)

\eta\in S_{0}
;

(v)

for each
w\in W
,

w.\eta=\eta+\sum_{\alpha\in w^{-1}.\Phi^{++}\cap(-\Phi^{++})}\log(\tau_{\alpha}\tau_{2\alpha}^{2})\alpha
.

Proof.

The proof of (
1.2
) is straightforward. Since the building is thick,
q_{\alpha}\geqslant 2
, which leads to
(
i
). For the proof of (
ii
), it is enough to observe that
w_{0}.\Phi^{++}=-\Phi^{++}
.
Since (
iii
) easily leads to (
iv
), it remains to show (
iii
).
If the root system is reduced, then each simple reflection
r_{i}
sends
\alpha_{i}
to
-\alpha_{i}
and permutes
other positive roots. Since
\tau_{r_{i}.\alpha}=\tau_{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle r_{i}.\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}}{{\alpha\neq\alpha_{i}}}}\log\tau_{\alpha}\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\log q_{\alpha_{i}}\,\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=\eta-\log q_{\alpha_{i}}\,\alpha_{i}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle{\eta},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle{r_{i}.\eta},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle{\eta},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle+\log q_{\alpha_{i}}\langle{\alpha_{i}},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle,

and so

\langle{\eta},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\log q_{\alpha_{i}}\langle{\alpha_{i}},{\alpha_{i}}\rangle.

For the
\text{BC}_{r}
root system it is enough to observe that the Weyl group
W
is the same as for the root
system
\Phi^{++}\sqcup(-\Phi^{++})
which has type
\text{B}_{r}
. This proves (
iii
).

Lastly, for the proof of (
v
) it is enough to recall that
w.\Phi=\Phi
.
∎

Next let us define a multiplicative function on
\mathfrak{a}
by setting

(1.3)

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\tau_{\alpha}^{\langle{x},{\alpha}\rangle}

\displaystyle=e^{2\langle x,\eta\rangle},\qquad x\in\mathfrak{a}.

For
w\in W^{a}
, having the reduced expression
w=r_{i_{1}}\cdots r_{i_{k}}
, we set
q_{w}=q_{i_{1}}\cdots q_{i_{k}}
. Then
we define

(1.4)

N_{\lambda}=\frac{W(q^{-1})}{W_{\lambda}(q^{-1})}\chi_{0}(\lambda)

where
W_{\lambda}=\bigl\{w\in W:w.\lambda=\lambda\bigr\}
and whereas for any subset
U\subseteq W
, we set

U(q^{-1})=\sum_{w\in U}q_{w}^{-1}.

Given two special vertices
x,y\in V_{s}
, let
\mathscr{A}
be an apartment containing
x
and
y
and let
\psi:\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\Sigma
be a type-rotating isomorphism such that
\psi(x)=0
and
\psi(y)\in S_{0}
, see

[
20
, Definition 4.1.1]
. We set
\sigma(x,y)=\psi(y)\in\frac{1}{2}P^{+}
. If
x
and
y
are good vertices then

\sigma(x,y)\in P^{+}
. For
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
x\in V_{g}
, we denote by
V_{\lambda}(x)
the set of all good vertices

y\in V_{g}
such that
\sigma(x,y)=\lambda
. The building axioms entail that the cardinality of
V_{\lambda}(x)
depends
only on
\lambda
, see
[
22
, Proposition 1.5]
, and it is equal to
N_{\lambda}
.

A subcomplex
\mathscr{S}
is called a sector of
\mathscr{X}
if it is a sector in any apartment. Two sectors are
equivalent
if
they contain a common subsector. The set of equivalence classes of sectors is denoted by
\Omega
. For any special vertex

x\in V_{s}
and
\omega\in\Omega
there is a unique sector, denoted by
[x,\omega]
, which has base vertex
x
and represents

\omega
. For
x\in V_{s}
and
y\in V(\mathscr{X})
, we set

\Omega(x,y)=\big\{\omega\in\Omega:y\in[x,\omega]\big\}.

Given
x\in V_{s}
, the collection
\{\Omega(x,y):y\in V_{s}\}
generates a totally disconnected compact Hausdorff topology
on
\Omega
. We fix once and for all an origin
o
, which is a special vertex of the chamber
c_{0}
.

The horocycle (or Busemann function)
h:V_{s}\times V_{s}\times\Omega\rightarrow\tfrac{1}{2}P
is defined as follows: for
two special vertices
x
and
y
, and
\omega\in\Omega
, we set

h(x,y;\omega)=\sigma(x,z)-\sigma(y,z)

where
z
is any special vertex belonging to
[x,\omega]\cap[y,\omega]
. The value
h(x,y;\omega)
is independent of
z
,
see e.g.
[
16
, Proposition 3.3]
. In view of
[
22
, Lemma 3.13]
, if
z\in V_{s}
and

\sigma(x,z)\gg\sigma(x,y)
then
\Omega(x,z)\subseteq\Omega(y,z)
. Hence,

(1.5)

h(x,y;\omega)=\sigma(x,z)-\sigma(y,z),\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega(x,z).

Furthermore, for all special
x,y,z\in V_{s}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, we have the cocycle relation

(1.6)

h(x,y;\omega)=h(x,z;\omega)+h(z,y;\omega)

Given
x,y\in V_{g}
, by the Kostant’s convexity theorem, see
[
14
]
or
[
22
, Lemma 3.19]
, if

\sigma(x,y)=\lambda
then for each
\omega\in\Omega
,
h(x,y;\omega)\in\Pi_{\lambda}
. Since

\sigma(x,y)-h(x,y;\omega)\in Q^{+}
, by Lemma
1.1
(
iii
), we have

(1.7)

\langle{h(x,y;\omega)},{\eta}\rangle\leqslant\langle{\sigma(x,y)},{\eta}\rangle.

In fact, the formula
\langle{\sigma(x,y)},{\eta}\rangle
defines a distance on special vertices
V_{s}
. Indeed, for any three vertices

x,y,z\in V_{s}
, if
\omega\in\Omega(x,z)
, then by (
1.6
) together with (
1.7
), we get

\displaystyle\langle{\sigma(x,z)},{\eta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{h(x,y;\omega)},{\eta}\rangle+\langle{h(y,z;\omega)},{\eta}\rangle

(1.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant\langle{\sigma(x,y)},{\eta}\rangle+\langle{\sigma(y,z)},{\eta}\rangle.

In view of Lemma
1.1
(
ii
), we have

\langle{\sigma(y,x)},{\eta}\rangle=-\langle{w_{0}.\sigma(x,y)},{\eta}\rangle=\langle{\sigma(x,y)},{\eta}\rangle.

Lastly,
\langle{\sigma(x,y)},{\eta}\rangle=0
implies that
\sigma(x,y)=0
and so
x=y
. Analogously, one can show that

|\sigma(x,y)|
is a distance on special vertices
V_{s}
. In particular, for all
x,y,z\in V_{s}
,

(1.9)

|\sigma(x,y)|\leqslant|\sigma(x,z)|+|\sigma(z,y)|.

In view of
[
23
, Lemma 3.1]
, for each
x,y,z\in V_{s}

(1.10)

|\sigma(x,y)-\sigma(x,z)|\leq|\sigma(y,z)|,

thus for
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

(1.11)

|\langle{\sigma(x,y)},{\alpha}\rangle-\langle{\sigma(x,z)},{\alpha}\rangle|\leq|\alpha||\sigma(y,z)|.

For every special vertex
x
there is a unique Borel probability measure
\nu_{x}
on
\Omega
, such that

\nu_{x}\big(\Omega(x,y)\big)=\frac{1}{\#\{y^{\prime}\in V_{s}:\sigma(x,y^{\prime})=\sigma(x,y^{\prime})\}}.

The measures
\nu_{x}
and
\nu_{y}
for any
x,y\in V_{s}
are mutually absolutely continuous, with the Radon–Nikodym derivative
equal to

(1.12)

\frac{{\rm d}\nu_{y}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{x}}(\omega)=\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega)),\quad\omega\in\Omega.

For details see
[
22
, page 16]
, see also
[
23
, Proposition 6.1]
. We often write
\nu=\nu_{o}
.

1.4.
Macdonald spherical functions

In this subsection we recall the definition and properties of Macdonald spherical functions (see
[
15
]
):
for given
\lambda\in P^{+}
,

P_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{W(q^{-1})}\sum_{w\in W}\mathbf{c}(w\cdot z)e^{\langle{w\cdot z},{\lambda}\rangle},\quad z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}(z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{1-\tau_{\alpha}^{-1}\tau_{\alpha/2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle}}{1-\tau_{\alpha/2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\frac{\Big(1-\tau_{2\alpha}^{-1}\tau_{\alpha}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle{z},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle}\Big)\Big(1+\tau_{\alpha}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle{z},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle}\Big)}{1-e^{-{\langle{z},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle}}},

and

W(q^{-1})=\sum_{w\in W}q_{w}^{-1}.

Values of
P_{\lambda}
where the denominator of the
\mathbf{c}
-function equals zero are obtained by taking proper limits.
In view of
[
22
, formula (6.6)]
, Macdonald spherical functions can be written as integrals over
\Omega
,
namely, for
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
,

(1.13)

P_{\lambda}(z)=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{\langle z,h(x,y;\omega)\rangle}\,\nu_{x}({\rm d}\omega)

where
x
and
y
is any pair of special vertices such that
\sigma(x,y)=\lambda
.

Macdonald functions are the main tools to study the algebra of averaging operators on
V_{g}
. For each
\lambda\in P^{+}
,
we define an operator acting on finitely supported function of good vertices
V_{g}
as

A_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{1}{N_{\lambda}}\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}f(y),\quad x\in V_{g}.

Then
\mathscr{A}_{0}=\operatorname{\mathbb{C}-span}\{A_{\lambda}:\lambda\in P^{+}\}
is a commutative unital algebra, whose characters can be expressed
in terms of Macdonald spherical functions. Specifically, every multiplicative functional on

\mathscr{A}_{0}
is given by the evaluation

h_{z}(A_{\lambda})=P_{\lambda}(z),\quad\lambda\in P^{+}

at some
z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover,
h_{z}=h_{z^{\prime}}
if and only if
W.z+i2\pi L=W.z^{\prime}+i2\pi L

(see
[
15
, Theorem 3.3.12(ii)]
). Let
\mathscr{A}_{2}
be the closure of
\mathscr{A}_{0}
in the operator norm on

\ell^{2}(V_{g})
. Then
\mathscr{A}_{2}
is a commutative unital
C^{\star}
-algebra.

The standard case.
Assume that
\tau_{\alpha}\geqslant 1
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
. Then for all
\theta\in U_{0}
,
where

U_{0}=\big\{\theta\in\mathfrak{a}:\langle{\theta},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle\leqslant\pi\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi\big\}

the multiplicative functional
h_{i\theta}
extends to
\mathscr{A}_{2}
in a continuous way. We set
\mathscr{D}=U_{0}
, and for any
any Borel subset
B\subset\mathscr{D}
,

(1.14)

\pi(B)=\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg)^{r}\frac{W(q^{-1})}{|W|}\int_{B}\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{|\mathbf{c}(i\theta)|^{2}}.

The exceptional case.

Suppose that
\tau_{\alpha}<1
for some
\alpha\in\Phi
, which is only possible when
\Phi
is the
\text{BC}_{r}
root system
and
q_{r}<q_{0}
, namely

\text{BC}_{r}=\big\{\pm e_{i},\pm 2e_{i},\pm e_{j}\pm e_{k}:1\leqslant i\leq r,1\leqslant j<k\leqslant r\big\}

where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{r}\}
is the standard basis of
\mathfrak{a}
. We set
a=\sqrt{q_{r}q_{0}}
and
b=\sqrt{q_{r}/q_{0}}
.
Then

\mathbf{c}(z)=\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(1-a^{-1}e^{-z_{j}})(1+b^{-1}e^{-j})}{1-e^{-2z_{j}}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{1\leqslant j<k\leqslant r}\frac{(1-q_{1}^{-1}e^{-z_{j}-z_{k}})(1-q_{1}^{-1}e^{-z_{j}+z_{k}})}{(1-e^{-z_{j}-z_{k}})(1-e^{-z_{j}+z_{k}})}\bigg).

Let
v=\log b+i\pi
. For
j=1,\ldots,r
we set

U_{j}=\big\{z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}:z_{k}\in[-\pi,\pi]\text{ for all }k\neq j,\text{ and }z_{j}=\pi-i\log b\big\},

and
U_{0}=[-\pi,\pi]^{r}
. For
\theta\in U_{j}
, we define

\phi_{j}(i\theta)=\mathbf{c}(-i\theta)\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathbf{c}(i\theta+te_{j})}{1-e^{t}}.

Then for each
\theta\in\mathscr{D}=U_{0}\bigsqcup U_{1}
, the multiplicative functional
h_{i\theta}
extends to
\mathscr{A}_{2}
in
a continuous way. For a Borel subset
B\subset\mathscr{D}
, we put

(1.15)

\pi(B)=\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg)^{r}\frac{W(q^{-1})}{|W|}\int_{B\cap U_{0}}\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{|\mathbf{c}(i\theta)|^{2}}+\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg)^{r-1}\frac{W(q^{-1})}{|W^{\prime}|}\int_{B\cap U_{1}}\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{\phi_{1}(i\theta)}

where
W^{\prime}
is the Coxeter group
C_{r-1}
and the measure
{\rm d}\theta
on
U_{j}
equals

{\rm d}\theta=\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k=1}}{{k\neq j}}}^{r}{\rm d}\theta_{k}

for
j=0,\ldots,r
. Then for all
A\in\mathscr{A}_{0}
,
x\in V_{g}
and
y\in V_{\lambda}(x)
, we have

(1.16)

(A\delta_{x})(y)=\int_{\mathscr{D}}h_{i\theta}(A)\overline{P_{\lambda}(i\theta)}\pi({\rm d}\theta),

see
[
22
, Theorem 5.2 & Corollary 5.5]
in the standard case, and

[
22
, Theorem 5.7 & Corollary 5.8]
in the exceptional case.

A complex-valued function
\varphi:V_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, is called
spherical
with respect to
x\in V_{g}
, if

(i)

\varphi(x)=1
;

(ii)

\varphi(y)=\varphi(y^{\prime})
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in V_{\lambda}(x)
for any
\lambda\in P^{+}
;

(iii)

for each
\lambda\in P^{+}
, there is
C_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}
, such that
A_{\lambda}\varphi=C_{\lambda}\varphi
.

Thanks to
[
22
, Theorem 3.22]
, for each
z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
the function
\varphi_{z}
defined as

(1.17)

\varphi_{z}(y)=P_{\lambda}(z),\qquad\text{if }y\in V_{\lambda}(o)

is spherical with respect to
o
. In the following proposition we describe the asymptotic behavior of the

ground state spherical function
, that is

(1.18)

\mathbf{\Phi}(\lambda)=P_{\lambda}(0).

The statement as well as the proof of the following proposition are similar to the symmetric space case
[
2
]
, for details
see
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
where
\tilde{\text{A}}_{n}
buildings have been considered. The latter proof generalizes
in a straightforward way. For a more detailed description of the asymptotic behavior of the ground state spherical function
we refer to
[
23
, Section 7.2]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

We have

\mathbf{\Phi}(\lambda)\approx\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle).

Furthermore,

\mathbf{\Phi}(\lambda)=\text{ const. }\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle+o(1)

as
\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\rightarrow+\infty
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}.

1.5.
Random walks

In this paper we study caloric functions associated with an
isotropic
random walk on good vertices
V_{g}
, i.e. the random
walk with the transition probability
k(x,y)
constant on

\left\{(x,y)\in V_{g}\times V_{g}:y\in V_{\lambda}(x)\right\}

for every
\lambda\in P^{+}
. Let
A
denote the corresponding operator acting on compactly supported functions on
V_{g}
,
that is

Af(x)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(x,y)f(y).

Then
A
belongs to the algebra
\mathscr{A}
and can be expressed as

(1.19)

A=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}c_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}

where
c_{\lambda}\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}c_{\lambda}=1
. We say that the random walk has a
finite range

if
c_{\lambda}>0
for finitely many
\lambda\in P^{+}
. We set
k(1;x,y)=k(x,y)
, and for
n\geqslant 2
,

k(n;x,y)=\sum_{z\in V_{s}}k(n-1;x,z)k(z,y).

Let

k_{n}(x)=k(n;x)=k(n;o,x),\qquad x\in V_{g},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The random walk is
irreducible
if for any
x,y\in V_{g}
, there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

k(n;x,y)>0.

Lastly, the walk is called
aperiodic
if for every
x\in V_{g}
,

\operatorname{gcd}\{n\in\mathbb{N}:k(n;x,x)>0\}=1

We shall be concerned with irreducible aperiodic random walks having a finite range, which we call for short

admissible
random walks. Let us fix an admissible random walk with the transition function
k
. To describe the long time
asymptotic behavior of the corresponding heat kernel
k(n;x,y)
, we need to introduce some additional notation. Let

\varrho=h_{0}(A)
, and set

\kappa(z)=\varrho^{-1}h_{z}(A),\quad z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}.

In view of the Gelfand theorem, and the representation (
1.19
) together with
[
22
, Theorem 6.5]
, one can show that

\varrho
is the spectral radius of
A
on
\ell^{2}(V_{g})
. There exist a finite set
\mathcal{V}\subset P
and positive real
numbers
\left\{c_{v}:v\in\mathcal{V}\right\}
with
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}c_{v}=1
such that

\kappa(z)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}c_{v}e^{\langle z,v\rangle}.

For
x\in\mathfrak{a}
, by
B_{x}
we denote a quadratic form

(1.20)

B_{x}(u,u)=D_{u}^{2}\log\kappa(x)

where
D_{u}
is the derivative along a vector
u
, i.e.,

D_{u}f(x)=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{{\rm d}t}f(x+tu)\right|_{t=0}.

Hence,

B_{x}(u,u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v,v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}\frac{c_{v}e^{\langle x,v\rangle}}{\kappa(x)}\cdot\frac{c_{v^{\prime}}e^{\left\langle x,v^{\prime}\right\rangle}}{\kappa(x)}\left\langle u,v-v^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the interior of the convex hull of
\mathcal{V}
. Then for every
\delta\in\mathcal{M}

a function
f(\delta,\cdot):\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

f(\delta,x)=\langle x,\delta\rangle-\log\kappa(x)

attains its maximum at the unique point
s=s(\delta)\in\mathfrak{a}
satisfying
\nabla\log\kappa(s)=\delta
,
see e.g.
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
. By the implicit function theorem, the function
s
is real-analytic on

\mathcal{M}
. In particular,
s
is bounded on any compact subset of
\mathcal{M}
. On the other hand,
|s(\delta)|
approaches
infinity when
\delta
tends approaches
\partial\mathcal{M}
. Since
\kappa
is
W_{0}
invariant, for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

\langle{\delta},{\alpha}\rangle=0
if and only if
\langle{s},{\alpha}\rangle=0
. Since
\langle{\delta},{u}\rangle=D_{u}\log\kappa(s)
, we
have

D_{u}s=B_{s}^{-1}u.

Let us set

(1.21)

\displaystyle\phi(\delta)

\displaystyle=f(\delta,s)

\displaystyle=\max\big\{\langle{u},{\delta}\rangle-\log\kappa(u):u\in\mathfrak{a}\big\}.

Then (see
[
25
, Section 2.1]
)

(1.22)

\phi(\delta)=\frac{1}{2}B_{0}^{-1}(\delta,\delta)+\mathcal{O}(\lvert{\delta}\rvert^{3}),

and

(1.23)

\phi(\delta)\approx\lvert{\delta}\rvert^{2},\qquad\delta\in\mathcal{M}.

Let us summarize the properties of the heat kernel
k(n;x)
: In view of
[
25
, Remark 2]
, there is
C>0

such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in V_{g}
,

(1.24)

k(n;x)\leqslant C\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varrho^{n}e^{-n\phi(\delta)}.

where
\delta=\frac{\sigma(o,x)}{n}
. In this paper we need the precise asymptotic behavior of
k(n;x)
away from the walls
of the Weyl cone
\mathfrak{a}_{+}
while
\sigma(o,x)/n
stays at bounded distance from the boundary of
\mathcal{M}
. For this
reason, we take
J=\emptyset
in
[
25
, Theorem 4.1]
. Then given
\xi>0
and
\varepsilon>0
for any sequence

(x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
of good vertices such that
k(n;x_{n})>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\delta_{n}=n^{-1}\sigma(o,x_{n})

satisfying

\langle{\delta_{n}},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant\xi,\qquad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{+},

and
\operatorname{dist}(\delta_{n},\partial\mathcal{M})\geqslant\varepsilon
, we have

(1.25)

k(n;x_{n})=n^{-\frac{r}{2}}\varrho^{n}\,e^{-n\phi(\delta)}\,\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x_{n}))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,(\det B_{s_{n}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\frac{1}{\mathbf{c}(s_{n})}\,\Big(C_{0}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\Big)

with
s_{n}=\nabla\phi(\delta_{n})
. The implicit constant in the error term depends only on
\xi
,
\varepsilon
, and
A
.

We also need the description of the asymptotic behavior of
k(n;y,x)
in the region where
\sigma(y,x_{n})=o(n)
.
By
[
25
, Corollary 4.10]
, for each sequence
(x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
of good vertices such
k(n;y,x_{n})>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
with
\delta_{n}=n^{-1}\sigma(y,x_{n})
satisfying

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle\delta_{n},\alpha\right\rangle=0,\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Phi,

we have

(1.26)

k(n;y,x_{n})=n^{-\frac{r}{2}-\left|\Phi^{++}\right|}\varrho^{n}e^{-n\phi\left(\delta_{n}\right)}\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x_{n}))\left(C_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\left|\delta_{n}\right|)+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\right).

The constant
C_{0}
is absolute.

2.
Norm estimates of heat kernels

We start our study by investigating the behavior of
\ell^{p}(V_{g})
norms,
p\in[1,\infty]
, of the heat kernel corresponding
to an isotropic finite range random walk. Let us define

(2.1)

s_{p}=\begin{cases}\eta\big(\frac{2}{p}-1\big)&\text{if }p\in[1,2),\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and let

\delta_{p}=\nabla\log\kappa(s_{p})\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M}.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
, we have

0<P_{\lambda}(\Re z)\leqslant P_{\lambda}(\eta)=1

In particular, if
p\in(1,2]
, then
\kappa(s_{p})<\kappa(s_{1})=\varrho^{-1}
.

Proof.

In view of
[
25
, Theorem 7]
, if
z\in t\eta+U_{0}
, then
P_{\lambda}(\Re z)\leqslant P_{\lambda}(t\eta)
.
Now, to prove the inequality, it suffices to show that the function

[0,1]\ni t\mapsto\log\kappa(t\eta),

is strictly increasing. In view of (
1.20
), we have

\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\log\kappa(t\eta)=B_{t\eta}(\eta,\eta)>0,

which implies that the function

[0,1]\ni t\mapsto\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\log\kappa(t\eta)=\langle\nabla\log\kappa(t\eta),\eta\rangle

is strictly increasing. Since
\kappa
is
W
-invariant, we have
\nabla\log\kappa(0)=0
, which proves our claim.

Let us now compute
\kappa(s_{1})
. Since
s_{1}=\eta
, by Lemma
1.1
(
ii
) and the integral representation of
Macdonald function (
1.13
), we get

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(s_{1})=P_{\lambda}(-\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h(o,x;\omega))\,e^{-\langle\eta,h(o,x;\omega)\rangle}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h(o,x;\omega))\,\chi_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(h(o,x;\omega))\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)=1.

Hence, by the representation (
1.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\varrho\kappa(\eta)=\hat{A}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}c_{\lambda}\hat{A}_{\lambda}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}c_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(\eta)=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}c_{\lambda}=1,

and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. For all
b>a>0
, there is
C>0
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{x\in V_{g}:a\leqslant|\sigma(o,x)|\leqslant b}k(n;x)^{p}\leqslant C\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}:a\leqslant|\lambda|\leqslant b}\exp\Big\{pn(\langle{s_{p}},{\lambda/n}\rangle-\phi(\lambda/n))\Big\}.

Proof.

Using the global upper bound (
1.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}:a\leqslant|\sigma(o,x)|\leqslant b}k(n;x)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}:a\leqslant|\lambda|\leqslant b}\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{-\frac{p}{2}}e^{-pn\phi(\lambda/n)}N_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}:a\leqslant|\lambda|\leqslant b}e^{pn(\langle{s_{p}},{\lambda/n}\rangle-\phi(\lambda/n))}

where the last inequality follows by (
1.4
) and (
2.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
c>0
,
\gamma>0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
x_{0}\in\mathfrak{a}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we set

\Lambda_{n}=\Big\{\lambda\in P^{+}:\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\Big\}.

Then

(2.2)

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\langle{\lambda-nx_{0}},{\alpha_{j}}\rangle|^{\beta_{j}}\Big)e^{-c\frac{\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert^{2}}{n}}\approx n^{\frac{r+|\beta|}{2}},

and

(2.3)

\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}}{{|\lambda-nx_{0}|_{\infty}\geqslant n^{1/2}}}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\langle{\lambda-nx_{0}},{\alpha_{j}}\rangle|^{\beta_{j}}\Big)e^{-c\frac{\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert^{2}}{n}}\approx n^{\frac{r+|\beta|}{2}}

as
n
tends to infinity uniformly with respect to
x_{0}\in\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Let us observe that

\Big\{\lambda\in P^{+}:\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert_{\infty}\leqslant r^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\Big\}\subset\Lambda_{n}\subset\Big\{\lambda\in P^{+}:\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert_{\infty}\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\Big\}.

Hence, it is enough to show that for each
a>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,

\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}:\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert_{\infty}\leqslant an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\langle{\lambda-nx_{0}},{\alpha_{j}}\rangle|^{\beta_{j}}\Big)e^{-c\frac{\lvert{\lambda-nx_{0}}\rvert^{2}}{n}}\approx n^{\frac{r+|\beta|}{2}}.

Observe that it is sufficient to consider
r=1
, as the general case then easily follows. Since there is
C>0
such that
for all
\tau\in[0,1]
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
|j-nx_{0}|\leqslant an^{1/2+\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\exp\Big\{-c\frac{(j-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}\Big\}-\exp\Big\{-c\frac{(j-\tau-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}\Big\}\Big|\leqslant Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\exp\Big\{-c\frac{(j-\tau-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}\Big\},

we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}:|j-nx_{0}|\leqslant an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}\lvert{j-nx_{0}}\rvert^{b}e^{-c\frac{(j-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}-\int_{\lfloor nx_{0}-an^{1/2+\gamma}\rfloor-1}^{\lfloor nx_{0}+an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\rfloor}|x-nx_{0}|^{b}e^{-c\frac{(x-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}{\>\rm d}x\Bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}:|j-nx_{0}|\leqslant an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}|j-\tau-nx_{0}|^{b}\Big|e^{-c\frac{(j-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}-e^{-c\frac{(j-\tau-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}\Big|{\>\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\phantom{\leqslant\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}:|j-nx_{0}|\leqslant an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}}+\int_{0}^{1}\big(|j-nx_{0}|^{b}-|j-\tau-nx_{0}|^{b}\big)e^{-c\frac{(j-\tau-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}{\>\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\int_{\lfloor nx_{0}-an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\rfloor-1}^{\lfloor nx_{0}+an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\rfloor}|x-nx_{0}|^{b}e^{-c\frac{(x-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}{\>\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\phantom{\leqslant}+C\int_{\lfloor nx_{0}-an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\rfloor-1}^{\lfloor nx_{0}+an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\rfloor}|x-nx_{0}|^{b-1}e^{-c\frac{(x-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}{\>\rm d}x.

Next, we write

\displaystyle\int_{nx_{0}-an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}^{nx_{0}+an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}|x-nx_{0}|^{b}e^{-c\frac{(x-nx_{0})^{2}}{n}}{\>\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{-n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}^{an^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}}|x|^{b}e^{-c\frac{x^{2}}{n}}{\>\rm d}x

\displaystyle=n^{\frac{1+b}{2}}\int_{-an^{\gamma}}^{an^{\gamma}}|y|^{b}e^{-cy^{2}}{\>\rm d}y,

and since

\int_{-an^{\gamma}}^{an^{\gamma}}|y|^{b}e^{-cy^{2}}{\>\rm d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y|^{b}e^{-cy^{2}}{\>\rm d}y+o(1),

we get (
2.2
). The proof of (
2.3
) is analogous.
∎

Let us denote by
p^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
, the conjugate exponent to
p
, that is

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

In the following sections we determine the asymptotic behavior of
\ell^{p}(V_{g})
norms of the heat kernel
k(n;o,\cdot)

as
n
goes to infinity. The study splits into three cases:
1\leqslant p<2
(Section
2.1
),
p=2
(Section

2.2
), and
p>2
(Section
2.3
).

2.1.
The case
p\in[1,2)

Given
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{6}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set

(2.4)

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}=\left\{\lambda\in P^{+}:\left|\frac{\lambda}{n}-\delta_{p}\right|\leqslant n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\right\},

and

(2.5)

\mathscr{N}^{p}_{n}=\big\{x\in V_{g}:\sigma(o,x)\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}\big\}.

For a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in V_{g}
we often write
\delta=\frac{\sigma(o,x)}{n}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
If
p\in[1,2)
, then

\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{2p^{\prime}}}\varrho^{n}\kappa(s_{p})^{n}.

Furthermore,

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\mathcal{O}\Big(e^{-cn^{2\gamma}}\Big).

Proof.

We start by determining the asymptotic behavior of the norm restricted to
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
. We claim that

\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\approx\varrho^{np}\,e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}\,n^{-\frac{r}{2}(p-1)}

where
\psi
is a function on
\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M}
defined as

\psi(\delta)=\langle\delta,s_{p}\rangle-\phi(\delta),\qquad\delta\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M}.

In view of Lemma
1.1
(
iii
), for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{++}
we have
\langle{s_{p}},{\alpha}\rangle>0
, thus
for each
x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
,

\big|\langle{\delta-\delta_{p}},{\alpha}\rangle\big|\leqslant|\alpha|n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma},

and so

\langle{\delta},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant\langle{\delta_{p}},{\alpha}\rangle-|\alpha|n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}.

This legitimates the use of the asymptotic formula (
1.25
) in the region
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
. Since

\mathbf{c}(s)^{-1}
and
(\det B_{s})^{-1/2}
are real-analytic in this region, by (
1.4
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}

\displaystyle\approx n^{-\frac{pr}{2}}\varrho^{np}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}\exp\big\{-np\phi(\delta)\big\}\chi_{0}(n\delta)^{-\frac{p}{2}}\big(\det B_{s}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{c}(s)}

\displaystyle\approx n^{-\frac{pr}{2}}\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}\exp\big\{-np\phi(\delta)\big\}\exp\Big\{n\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)\langle{\delta},{\eta}\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle\approx n^{-\frac{pr}{2}}\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{np\psi(\delta)}.

Next, we compute

D_{\delta-\delta_{p}}\psi(\delta_{p})=-D_{\delta-\delta_{p}}\phi(\delta_{p})=-B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p}),

thus there are
N\geqslant 1
and
C>0
such that for all
n\geqslant N
, if
x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
then

\displaystyle\Big|\psi(\delta)-\psi(\delta_{p})+\frac{1}{2}B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p})\Big|\leqslant C|\delta-\delta_{p}|^{3}.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\Big|e^{np\psi(\delta)}-e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}e^{-p\frac{n}{2}B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p})}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})-p\frac{n}{2}B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p})}\Big|1-e^{np\psi(\delta)-np\psi(\delta_{p})+p\frac{n}{2}B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p})}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C^{\prime}n^{-\frac{1}{2}+3\gamma}e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})-p\frac{n}{2}B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p})}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{np\psi(\delta)}=e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}\bigg(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-p\frac{n}{2}B^{-1}_{s_{p}}(\delta-\delta_{p},\delta-\delta_{p})}\bigg)\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(n^{-\frac{1}{2}+3\gamma}\big)\Big)

which by (
2.2
) leads to

(2.6)

\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{2}(p-1)}\varrho^{np}e^{n\psi(\delta_{p})}.

Let us next observe that
\nabla\psi(\delta_{p})=0
. Since
\phi
is strictly convex in
\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M}
,
\psi
is
strictly concave. Hence, there is
c>0
such that

(2.7)

\psi(\delta)-\psi(\delta_{p})\leqslant-c|\delta-\delta_{p}|^{2}

for all
\delta\in\mathcal{M}
. Consider now
x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
. By Lemma
2.2
and (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{np(\psi(\delta)-\psi(\delta_{p}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-cnp|\delta-\delta_{p}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2}cn^{2\gamma}p}\sum_{\lambda\in P}e^{-\frac{1}{2}cnp|\delta-\delta_{p}|^{2}}.

In view of (
2.2
), we get

\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\lesssim n^{\frac{r}{2}}\varrho^{np}e^{np\psi(\delta_{p})}e^{-c^{\prime\prime}n^{2\gamma}}

which together with (
2.6
) completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
The case
p=2

Let

(2.8)

0<\gamma<\frac{1}{4|\Phi^{++}|}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma^{\prime}=2\gamma|\Phi^{++}|.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}=\left\{\lambda\in P^{+}:n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\leqslant|\lambda|\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma},\text{ and }\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma^{\prime}}\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\right\},

and

(2.9)

\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}=\big\{x\in V_{g}:\sigma(o,x)\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}\big\}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Then

\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{4}-\frac{|\Phi^{++}|}{2}}\varrho^{n}.

Furthermore,

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}}\bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}}k(n;x)^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{O}\Big(n^{-\gamma|\Phi^{++}|}\Big).

Proof.

We first determine the asymptotic behavior of the norm restricted to
\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}
. For
v\in\mathfrak{a}
we set

m(v)=\min\big\{\langle{v},{\alpha}\rangle:\alpha\in\Phi^{++}\big\}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\lambda\in P^{+}:n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\leqslant|\lambda|\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\lambda\in P^{+}:|\lambda|\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{x\in V_{g}:\sigma(o,x)\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{x\in V_{g}:\sigma(o,x)\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\}.

Applying the asymptotic formula (
1.26
) together with Proposition
1.2
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}}k(n;x)^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\varrho^{2n}n^{-r-2|\Phi^{++}|}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x))^{-1}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\sigma(o,x)}\rangle^{2})

\displaystyle\approx\varrho^{2n}n^{-r-2|\Phi^{++}|}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle^{2}).

Now, in view of (
1.23
), there are
c^{\prime}\geqslant c>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle^{2})\leqslant\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}e^{-c\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle^{2}),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle^{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{n}:m(\lambda)>1}e^{-c^{\prime}\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{n}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle^{2}).

Observe that if
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
m(\lambda)>1
, then
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}+\rho
and

\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\leqslant|\lambda^{\prime}|\leqslant 2n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
, we get

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}(1+\langle{\alpha},{\lambda}\rangle)^{2}\approx n^{\frac{r}{2}+|\Phi^{++}|},

and consequently,

(2.10)

\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}}k(n;x)^{2}\approx\varrho^{2n}n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}.

Next, let us consider the region
V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{n}
. By Lemma
2.2
and (
1.23
), there is
c>0

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{n}}k(n;x)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{2n}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{n}}e^{-c\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{2n}e^{-\frac{c}{2}n^{2\gamma}}\sum_{\lambda\in P}e^{-c\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{2n}},

thus

(2.11)

\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{n}}k(n;x)^{2}\lesssim\varrho^{2n}n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}e^{-\frac{c}{4}n^{2\gamma}}.

Let us observe that in the region
x\in\mathscr{B}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{n}
, for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{++}
,
we have

1+\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\lesssim n^{1-2\gamma},

hence by the asymptotic formula (
1.26
) together with Proposition
1.2
and (
1.23
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{B}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{n}}k(n;x)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{2n}n^{-r-2|\Phi^{++}|}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{B}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{n}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x))^{-1}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\big(1+\langle{\sigma(o,x)},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{2n}n^{-r-|\Phi^{++}|-2\gamma|\Phi^{++}|}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{n}}e^{-c\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{n}}.

Therefore, we obtain

(2.12)

\sum_{x\in\mathscr{B}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{n}}k(n;x)^{2}\lesssim\varrho^{2n}n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}n^{-2\gamma|\Phi^{++}|}.

Lastly, we need to consider the region
\mathscr{A}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}
. For
x\in\mathscr{A}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}
, there
is
\beta\in\Phi^{++}
such that

\langle{\sigma(o,x)},{\beta}\rangle\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma^{\prime}}.

However, for all
\alpha\neq\beta
, we have

\langle{\sigma(o,x)},{\alpha}\rangle\leqslant Cn^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma},

thus by (
2.8
)

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\big(1+\langle{\sigma(o,x)},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\big)\lesssim n^{|\Phi^{++}|}n^{-2(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma(|\Phi^{++}|-1))}\leqslant n^{|\Phi^{++}|}n^{-2\gamma(|\Phi^{++}|+1)}.

Therefore, by the asymptotic formula (
1.26
) together with Proposition
1.2
and (
1.23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{A}_{n}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}}k(n;x)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{2n}n^{-r-2|\Phi^{++}|}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{n}^{2}}e^{-2n\phi(\delta)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\big(1+\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\big)

(2.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{2n}n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}n^{-2\gamma(|\Phi^{++}|+1)}.

Using estimates (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) together with the asymptotic (
2.10
)
we complete the proof.
∎

2.3.
The case
p\in(2,\infty]

Let
p\in(2,\infty]
. Fix a sequence of positive numbers
(r_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
such that

(2.14)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r_{n}}{\log n}=\infty,\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}=0.

Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}=\big\{\lambda\in P^{+}:|\lambda|\leqslant r_{n}\big\},

and

(2.15)

\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}=\{x\in V_{g}:\sigma(o,x)\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}\}.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Then

\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\approx n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}\varrho^{n}.

Furthermore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\bigg(\sum_{x\notin\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\mathcal{O}\Big(e^{-c_{p}r_{n}}\Big)

for certain
c_{p}>0
.

Proof.

We consider only the case where
p\in(2,\infty)
, as the case
p=\infty
can be proven in a similar manner.
In view of (
2.14
), in the region
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
, we can apply the asymptotic formula (
1.26
)
together with Proposition
1.2
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\approx\varrho^{np}n^{-\frac{pr}{2}-p|\Phi^{++}|}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-np\phi(\delta)}\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\big(1+\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\big).

By (
1.23
),

0\leqslant n\phi(\delta)\lesssim n|\delta|^{2}\leqslant\Big(\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2},

thus by (
2.14
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-np\phi(\delta)}\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\big(1+\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\big)\approx\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-(p-2)\langle{\eta},{\lambda}\rangle}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{++}}\big(1+\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle^{2}\big)\approx 1.

Therefore,

\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\approx\varrho^{np}n^{-\frac{pr}{2}-p|\Phi^{++}|}.

It remains to consider the region
V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
. In view of (
1.24
) and (
1.26
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}e^{-c\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-(p-2)\langle{\eta},{\lambda}\rangle}.

Setting

c_{p}=\frac{p-2}{4}\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\langle{\eta},{\lambda}\rangle:\lambda\in P^{+}\bigg\}>0

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{n}^{p}}e^{-4c_{p}|\lambda|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varrho^{np}e^{-2c_{p}r_{n}}.

Since

c_{p}r_{n}=c_{p}\frac{r_{n}}{\log n}\log n\geqslant p\bigg(\frac{r}{2}+|\Phi^{++}|+1\bigg)\log n,

the theorem follows.
∎

3.
Ratio limits for heat kernels

In this section we present some technical results, concerning the asymptotic behavior of quotients of heat kernel on
certain regions. The results of this section will be crucial to the description of the asymptotic behavior of caloric functions
on affine buildings.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
For
\xi>0
,
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in V_{g}
, and any sequence
(x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
of good vertices such that

\delta_{n}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sigma(o,x_{n})
satisfies

(3.1)

\langle{\delta_{n}},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant\xi\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\\

and

\operatorname{dist}(\delta_{n},\partial\mathcal{M})\geqslant\varepsilon,

we have

\frac{k(n;y,x_{n})}{k(n;o,x_{n})}=\chi_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n}))e^{-\langle{s_{n}},{\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n})}\rangle}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\big)

where
s_{n}=\nabla\phi(\delta_{n})
. The implicit constant in the error term depends only on
\xi
,
\varepsilon
,
A
,
and
y
.

Proof.

The hypothesis of the theorem entitles us to use the asymptotic formula (
1.25
). We get

(3.2)

k(n;x_{n})=n^{-\frac{r}{2}}\varrho^{n}e^{-n\phi(\delta)}\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x_{n}))^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\det B_{s_{n}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{c}(s_{n})}\Big(C_{0}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\Big).

Let
\tilde{\delta}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sigma(y,x_{n})
. Then by (
1.10
)

(3.3)

|\tilde{\delta}_{n}-\delta_{n}|\leqslant\frac{|\sigma(o,y)|}{n}.

There is
N\geqslant 1
such that for all
n\geqslant N
,

\frac{|\sigma(o,y)|}{n}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{\xi,\varepsilon\}.

Then for all
n\geqslant N
, and
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

\langle{\tilde{\delta}_{n}},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\xi,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{\delta},\partial\mathcal{M})\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon,

which once again legitimizes the use of asymptotic formula (
1.25
). We get

(3.4)

k(n;y,x_{n})=n^{-\frac{r}{2}}\varrho^{n}e^{-n\phi(\tilde{\delta})}\chi_{0}(\sigma(y,x_{n}))^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\det B_{\tilde{s}_{n}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{c}(\tilde{s}_{n})}\Big(C_{0}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\Big)

where
\tilde{s}_{n}=s(\tilde{\delta}_{n})
. Next, let us recall that
s
is real-analytic on every compact subset of

\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M}
, thus by (
3.3
),

(3.5)

|\tilde{s}_{n}-s_{n}|\leqslant C|\tilde{\delta}_{n}-\delta_{n}|\leqslant Cn^{-1}.

Analogously, one can show that

|B_{\tilde{s}_{n}}-B_{s_{n}}|\leqslant C^{\prime}n^{-1}.

Furthermore, there is
\zeta>0
such that

(3.6)

\langle{s_{n}},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle>\zeta.

Indeed, otherwise, we can select a subsequence
(\delta_{n_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N})
such that

(3.7)

\langle{s_{n_{k}}},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle\leqslant\frac{1}{k}.

Since
(\delta_{n})
is contained in the compact set
\{\delta\in\mathcal{M}:\operatorname{dist}(\delta,\mathcal{M})\geqslant\xi\}
,
we can assume that the sequence
(\delta_{n_{k}}:k\in\mathbb{N})
converges to
\delta_{0}
. By (
3.7
),

\langle{s(\delta_{0})},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle=0
, which implies that
\langle{\delta_{0}},{\alpha}\rangle=0
,
contradicting (
3.1
).

In view of (
3.6
) and (
3.5
), we get

\langle{\tilde{s}_{n}},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle=\langle{s_{n}},{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle(1+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})),

which leads to

\mathbf{c}(\tilde{s}_{n})=\mathbf{c}(s_{n})(1+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})).

Consequently, by (
3.4
),

k(n;y,x_{n})=n^{-\frac{r}{2}}\varrho^{n}e^{-n\phi(\tilde{\delta})}\chi_{0}(\sigma(y,x_{n}))^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\det B_{s_{n}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{c}(s_{n})}\Big(C_{0}+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\Big).

Hence, by (
3.2
),

\frac{k(n;y,x_{n})}{k(n;o,x_{n})}=\chi_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n}))\,e^{-n(\phi(\tilde{\delta}_{n})-\phi(\delta_{n}))}(1+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})).

In order to treat the terms in the exponential, let us notice that

\displaystyle\phi(\tilde{\delta}_{n})

\displaystyle=\phi(\delta_{n})+\langle{\nabla\phi(\delta_{n})},{\tilde{\delta}_{n}-\delta_{n}}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(|\tilde{\delta}_{n}-\delta_{n}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\phi(\delta_{n})+\langle s_{n},\widetilde{\delta}_{n}-\delta_{n}\rangle+\mathcal{O}(n^{-2}),

which completes the proof of the tentative asymptotic formula.
∎

As an immediate corollary, owing to the fact that
\sigma(o,x_{n})-\sigma(y,x_{n})
is bounded, we get the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
p\in[1,2)
. Under the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
, if
(\delta_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
converges to

\delta_{p}\in\mathcal{M}
, then

\frac{k(n;y,x_{n})}{k(n;o,x_{n})}=\chi_{0}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n}))\,(1+\mathcal{O}(|\delta_{n}-\delta_{p}|)+\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})).

The next result concerns ratio limits near the origin. Let us recall the definition of spherical functions (
2.1
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
If
(x_{n})
is a sequence of good vertices such that

(3.8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sigma(o,x_{n})}{n}=0,

then for each
y\in V_{g}
,

\frac{k(n;y,x_{n})}{k(n;o,x_{n})}=\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x_{n}))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x_{n}))}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma(o,x_{n})}{n}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let

\delta_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sigma(o,x_{n}),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\delta}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sigma(y,x_{n}),

and
s_{n}=s(\delta_{n})
,
\tilde{s}_{n}=s(\tilde{\delta}_{n})
. By (
1.8
) and (
3.8
)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{\delta}_{n}=0,

which allows us to use the asymptotic formula (
1.26
). We obtain

\frac{k(n;y,x_{n})}{k(n;o,x_{n})}=\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x_{n}))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x_{n}))}e^{-n\phi(\delta_{n})+n\phi(\tilde{\delta}_{n})}\Big(1+\mathcal{O}(|\delta_{n}|)\Big).

To finish the proof, we need to show that

n\big(\phi(\delta_{n})-\phi(\tilde{\delta}_{n})\big)=\mathcal{O}(|\delta_{n}|).

By (
1.21
), we have

\langle{\tilde{\delta}_{n}},{s_{n}}\rangle-\log\kappa(s_{n})\leqslant\langle{\tilde{\delta}_{n}},{\tilde{s}_{n}}\rangle-\log\kappa(\tilde{s}_{n})=\phi(\tilde{\delta}_{n}).

Hence, by (
1.10
)

\displaystyle\phi(\delta_{n})-\phi(\tilde{\delta}_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant\phi(\delta_{n})-\langle{\tilde{\delta}_{n}},{s_{n}}\rangle+\log\kappa(s_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\langle{(\sigma(o,x_{n})-\sigma(y,x_{n}))},{s_{n}}\rangle=\mathcal{O}(|s_{n}|/n).

Since
s
is real-analytic on any compact subset of
\operatorname{int}\mathcal{M}
, by the mean value theorem

|s_{n}|=|s(\delta_{n})-s(0)|\leqslant C|\delta_{n}|,

and the claim follows.
∎

The above result can be further elaborated away from the walls.

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
3.3
, if
(x_{n})
is a sequence of good vertices such that
for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle{\sigma(o,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle=\infty,

then

\frac{k(n;y,x_{n})}{k(n;o,x_{n})}=\chi_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n}))+o(1).

Proof.

In view of (
1.11
), for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle{\sigma(y,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle=\infty.

By Proposition
1.2
, we have

\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x_{n}))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x_{n}))}=\chi_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n}))\bigg(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{1+\langle{\sigma(y,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle}{1+\langle{\sigma(o,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle}\bigg)(1+o(1)).

By (
1.10
), for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

\frac{1+\langle{\sigma(y,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle}{1+\langle{\sigma(o,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle}-1=\frac{\langle{\sigma(y,x_{n})-\sigma(o,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle}{1+\langle{\sigma(o,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle}=\mathcal{O}(\langle{\sigma(o,x_{n})},{\alpha}\rangle^{-1}),

thus to complete the proof it is enough to invoke Theorem
3.3
.
∎

4.
Asymptotic behavior of caloric functions

In this section we discuss the
\ell^{p}
asymptotic behavior of caloric functions on affine buildings. We first introduce
appropriate mass functions. We next prove our main result, which concerns the
\ell^{p}
asymptotic behavior of caloric
functions coming from compactly supported initial data. Lastly, we extend this result to further classes of initial data.

4.1.
Helgason transform

Given a finitely supported function
f
on good vertices
V_{g}
, its Helgason transform is defined by the formula

\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle},\qquad(z,\omega)\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}\times\Omega.

In the following propositions we consider radial functions on
V_{g}
. Let us recall that
f
is called
radial
if
for all good vertices
x
and
y
, if
\sigma(o,x)=\sigma(o,y)
, then
f(x)=f(y)
. If
f
is radial we often identify it
with its profile
\tilde{f}
, where
\tilde{f}(\lambda)=f(x)
, for any
x\in V_{\lambda}(o)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
\lambda\in P^{+}
,
z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, and
x\in V_{g}
,

(4.1)

\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}=N_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(-z).

Proof.

Let us observe that for each
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the sum

\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}

is independent of
\omega\in\Omega
. Indeed, we have

(4.2)

\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}=\sum_{\mu\in\Pi_{\lambda}}e^{\langle{\eta-z},{\mu}\rangle}\#\{y\in V_{\lambda}(x):h(x,y;\omega)=\mu\}.

In view of
[
22
, Lemma 3.19]
, the set

\#\{y\in V_{\lambda}(x):h(x,y;\omega)=\mu\}

is independent of
\omega\in\Omega
, thus the left-hand side of (
4.2
). Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}\>\nu_{x}({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(x)}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(x,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}\>\nu_{x}({\rm d}\omega),

which in view of the integral representation (
1.13
) is equal to
N_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(-z)
,
proving (
4.1
).
∎

Thanks to Proposition
1.2
, the Helgason transform can be defined for radial functions in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
. Indeed,
if
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V_{g}}\Big|f(y)\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{\Re z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}|f(\lambda)|\sum_{y\in V_{\lambda}(o)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{\Re z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}|f(\lambda)|N_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(-\Re z)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}|f(\lambda)|N_{\lambda}=\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}

where the last inequality follows by Lemma
2.1
. Consequently, if
f
is radial and in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})

then for all
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
and
\omega\in\Omega
,

(4.3)

|\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega)|\leqslant\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Moreover, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f
be a radial function belonging to
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
. If
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, then

\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\varphi_{-z}(y).

In view of Corollary
4.2
, if
f
is a radial function from
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
and
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
, then
the Helgason transform
\mathcal{H}f(z,\cdot)
is constant on
\Omega
, thus we often just write
\mathcal{H}f(z)
.

Next, let us observe that for any two good vertices
x,y\in V_{g}
, by (
1.13
) and (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle P_{\sigma(x,y)}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}\big(h(x,y;\omega)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(x,y;\omega)}\rangle}\nu_{x}({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}\big(h(o,x;\omega)\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\chi_{0}\big(h(o,y;\omega)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\,\nu_{x}({\rm d}\omega)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}\big(h(o,x;\omega)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\chi_{0}\big(h(o,y;\omega)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)

where in the last equality we have also used (
1.12
).

Let
f
be a radial function in
\ell^{p}(V_{g})
,
p\geqslant 1
. Then for each
g\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}(V_{g})
, we can
define the convolution
f\times g
by the formula

f\times g(x)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)g(\sigma(y,x)),\qquad x\in V_{g}.

Let us observe that if both
f
and
g
are radial then
f\times g=g\times f
. Indeed, setting

\lambda^{\star}=-w_{0}.\lambda
, we can write

\displaystyle f\times g(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}\sum_{\mu\in P^{+}}f(\lambda)g(\mu)\#\big(V_{\mu^{\star}}(x)\cap V_{\lambda}(o)\big)

and since the building is strongly vertex regular,
[
21
, Theorem 5.21]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}\sum_{\mu\in P^{+}}f(\lambda)g(\mu)\#\big(V_{\mu}(o)\cap V_{\lambda^{\star}}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=g\times f(x).

Let us recall the definition of the Plancherel measure (
1.14
) and (
1.15
).

Theorem 4.3
.

If
f
and
g
are finitely supported complex-valued functions on
V_{g}
, then

\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y)}=\int_{\mathscr{D}}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}f(iz,\omega)\mathcal{H}\overline{g}(-iz,\omega)\nu({\rm d}\omega)\pi({\rm d}z).

Proof.

Given
z\in\mathscr{D}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f(iz,\omega)\mathcal{H}\overline{g}(-iz,\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{0}(h(o,y^{\prime};\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{iz},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}e^{\langle{iz},{h(o,y^{\prime};\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))\chi_{0}(h(y,y^{\prime};\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{iz},{h(y,y^{\prime};\omega)}\rangle}

where we have used the horocycle relation (
1.6
). Hence, by (
1.12
) and (
1.13
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}f(iz,\omega)\mathcal{H}\overline{g}(-iz,\omega)\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))\chi_{0}(h(y,y^{\prime};\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{iz},{h(y,y^{\prime};\omega)}\rangle}\nu_{o}({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(y,y^{\prime};\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{iz},{h(y,y^{\prime};\omega)}\rangle}\nu_{y}({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}P_{\sigma(y,y^{\prime})}(iz).

Consequently, by
[
22
, Lemma 6.1]

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{D}}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}f(iz,\omega)\mathcal{H}\overline{g}(iz,\omega)\nu({\rm d}\omega)\pi({\rm d}z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y^{\prime})}\int_{\mathscr{D}}P_{\sigma(y,y^{\prime})}(iz)\pi({\rm d}z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\overline{g(y)}

which completes the proof.
∎

In view of Theorem
4.3
, the Helgason transform can be defined for
f\in\ell^{2}(V_{g})
as a limit in

L^{2}(\mathscr{D}\times\Omega,\pi\times\nu)
of
\mathcal{H}f_{n}
where
(f_{n})
is any sequence of finitely supported functions
convergent in
\ell^{2}(V_{g})
to
f
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
K
be a radial function in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
. Then for each
f\in\ell^{2}(V_{g})
, we have

\mathcal{H}(f\times K)(z,\omega)=\mathcal{H}K(z)\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega)

for all
z\in\mathscr{D}
and
\omega\in\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose that
K
is compactly supported. Then by Proposition
4.1
, for each
y\in V_{g}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}}K(\sigma(y,x))\chi_{0}(h(y,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(y,x;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}K(\lambda)\sum_{x\in V_{\lambda}(y)}\chi_{0}(h(y,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(y,x;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}K(\lambda)N_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(-z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{g}}K(x)\varphi_{-z}(x)=\mathcal{H}K(z)

where in the last equality we have used Corollary
4.2
. Therefore, for each
f
a compactly supported function
on
V_{g}
,
z\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\omega\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(f\times K)(z,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{g}}(f\times K)(x)\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{g}}\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)K(\sigma(y,x))\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\sum_{x\in V_{g}}K(\sigma(y,x))\chi_{0}(h(y,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(y,x;\omega)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}K(z)\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega).

Finally, let us observe that
f\times K\in\ell^{2}(V_{g})
. Indeed, for any
g\in\ell^{2}(V_{g})
, we can write

\displaystyle\langle{f\times K},{g}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x\in V_{g}}\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)||K(\sigma(y,x))||g(x)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{g}}\sum_{y\in V_{g}}\big(|f(y)|^{2}|K(\sigma(y,x))|\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(|g(x)|^{2}|K(\sigma(y,x))|\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}}\|K\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Hence, by a density argument, we complete the proof in the case where
K
is radial and compactly supported. This result can
then be extended to all radial functions in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
another application of a density argument.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
f
be a finitely supported function on
V_{g}
. Then for each
z\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
,

(4.5)

(f\times\varphi_{-z})(x)=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega)\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega),\qquad x\in V_{g}.

Moreover, if
f
is a radial function belonging to
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
then for each
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
,

f\times\varphi_{-z}(x)=\varphi_{z}(x)\mathcal{H}f(z),\qquad x\in V_{g}.

Proof.

To prove (
4.5
), we use (
4.4
) to write

\displaystyle(f\times\varphi_{-z})(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)P_{\sigma(y,x)}(-z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\chi_{0}\big(h(o,y;\omega)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\mathcal{H}f(z,\omega)\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega).

Suppose that
f
is a radial function belonging to
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
. By Lemma
1.1
(
ii
) and Lemma
2.1
,
if
z\in[-1,1]\eta+iU_{0}
, and
y\in V_{g}
then

\big|\varphi_{-z}(y)\big|\leqslant\varphi_{-\Re z}(y)\leqslant 1.

Thus
f\times\varphi_{-z}
is well-defined. Now, in view of Fubini’s theorem and Corollary
4.2
,

\displaystyle(f\times\varphi_{-z})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\mathcal{H}f(z)\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}f(z)\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}f(z)\varphi_{z}(x)

where the last equality is a consequence of (
1.13
). This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f
be a finitely supported function on
V_{g}
. Then for each
x\in V_{g}
,

f(x)=\int_{\mathscr{D}}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{i\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\mathcal{H}f(iz,\omega)\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega)\pi({\rm d}z).

Proof.

Given
y\in V_{g}
, we define a radial function on
V_{g}
by the formula

f_{\text{rad}}(x)=\frac{1}{N_{\lambda}}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in V_{\lambda}(y)}f(y^{\prime}),\qquad x\in V_{\lambda}(o).

By Corollary
4.2
, for
z\in\mathscr{D}
,

\mathcal{H}f_{\text{rad}}(iz)=\sum_{x\in V_{g}}f_{\text{rad}}(x)\varphi_{-iz}(x).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f_{\text{rad}}(iz)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{g}}\frac{1}{N_{\sigma(o,x)}}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in V_{\sigma(o,x)}(y)}f(y^{\prime})\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-i\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y^{\prime})\frac{1}{N_{\sigma(y,y^{\prime})}}\sum_{x\in V_{\sigma(y,y^{\prime})}(o)}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-i\langle{z},{h(o,x;\omega)}\rangle}\nu({\rm d}\omega),

thus by Proposition
4.1

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{y^{\prime}\in V_{g}}f(y^{\prime})\varphi_{-iz}(\sigma(y,y^{\prime}))\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=f\times\varphi_{-iz}(y).

Now, by Proposition
4.5
,

\mathcal{H}f_{\text{rad}}(iz)=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{i\langle{z},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\mathcal{H}f(iz,\omega)\nu(\mathrm{d}\omega).

Since
f_{\text{rad}}
is radial and finitely supported by (
1.16
) we have

f(y)=f_{\text{rad}}(o)=\int_{\mathscr{D}}\mathcal{H}f_{\text{rad}}(iz)\pi({\rm d}z)

and the theorem follows.
∎

4.2.
Mass functions

In this section we introduce the notion of mass functions for finitely supported initial datum. We discuss their properties
and prove our main
\ell^{p}
convergence result concerning caloric functions.

For each
f
a finitely supported function on good vertices
V_{g}
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we define its
p
-mass by
the formula

(4.6a)

\displaystyle M_{p}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\>\nu({\rm d}\omega),

\displaystyle\text{if }p\in[1,2),

and

(4.6b)

\displaystyle M_{p}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi_{0}(x)}f\times\varphi_{0}(x),

\displaystyle\text{if }p\in[2,\infty],

where for a Borel subset
A\subseteq\Omega
, we have set

\fint_{A}F(\omega)\>\nu({\rm d}\omega)=\frac{1}{\nu(A)}\int_{A}F(\omega)\>\nu({\rm d}\omega).

In fact for
p=2
both definitions of a mass function yield strong
\ell^{2}
convergence for caloric functions, see
Remark
1
.

Let us observe that if
f
is
finitely
supported then
M_{p}(f)
is bounded for each
p\in[1,\infty]
. To see this,
let us assume that

\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq B_{R}=\big\{x\in V_{g}:\lvert{\sigma(o,x)}\rvert<R\big\}.

By the Kostant’s convexity theorem,
[
14
]
, we have

|h(o,y;\omega)|\leqslant|\sigma(o,y)|<R

for all
y\in B_{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega
. Therefore, if
p\in[1,2)
, then we have

\displaystyle|M_{p}(f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in B_{R}}|f(y)|\,\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle\eta,h(o,y;\omega)\rangle}\,\nu(\textrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{R|\eta|\frac{2}{p}}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}.

On the other hand, by (
4.4
) for
z=0
and (
1.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}(h(o,x;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle}\nu({\rm d}\omega)=\mathfrak{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x))e^{\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle},

thus

(4.7)

\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x))}\leqslant e^{\langle\eta,\sigma(o,y)\rangle},

which in turn immediately implies that for
p\in[2,\infty]
we have

|M_{p}(f)(x)|\leqslant e^{|\eta|R}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}.

In Section
4.4
we also study the spaces
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, which are defined as

(4.8)

\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})=\left\{f:V_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\varphi_{s_{p}}(y)<\infty\right\}.

where
s_{p}
is given by the formula (
2.1
). Notice that
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
is a Banach space with a norm

\|f\|_{\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}}=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\varphi_{s_{p}}(y).

Let us observe that
\ell^{1}_{s_{1}}(V_{g})=\ell^{1}(V_{g})
. Moreover, if
f
is radial and belongs to
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
then it belongs
to all
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
for
p\in(1,\infty]
. This is a consequence of Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
1.2
.
Furthermore, we can extend the definition of the mass function
M_{p}
to radial functions belonging to
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
.
In fact, in this case the mass is a constant. To see this, let us first observe that by Corollary
4.2
the Helgason
transform is constant. Next, if
p\in[1,2)
, then by Proposition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle M_{p}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\mathcal{H}f(s_{p})\nu({\rm d}\omega)=\mathcal{H}f(s_{p}).

If
p\in[2,\infty]
, again by Proposition
4.5
, we

M_{p}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{\varphi_{0}(x)}f\times\varphi_{0}(x)=\mathcal{H}f(0)=\mathcal{H}f(s_{p}).

4.3.
Finitely supported initial datum

In this section we discuss the
\ell^{p}
asymptotic behavior of caloric functions. Let
k
be the transition function of
an aperiodic isotropic finite range random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
. The caloric function

u:\mathbb{N}\times V_{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with initial datum being a finitely supported function
f
, is the unique solution to

(4.9)

\displaystyle u(n+1;x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(y,x)u(n;y),

\displaystyle u(0;x)

\displaystyle=f(x),\quad(n,x)\in\mathbb{N}\times V_{g}.

In fact, the solution has a form

u(n;x)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(n;y,x)f(y),\qquad(n,x)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times V_{g}.

We often write
u_{n}(x)=u(n;x)
. Our aim in this section is to prove Theorem
4.9
.

Let us first discuss the asymptotic behavior of caloric functions with finitely supported initial datum on the critical regions

\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
which are defined by (
2.5
), (
2.9
) and (
2.15
).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. If
u
is the solution to (
4.9
) where
f
is a finitely supported function on
V_{g}
,
then

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}\big|u(n;x)-M_{p}(f)(x)\,k(n;x)\big|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\big)

where

\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}&\text{if }p\in[1,2],\\
n^{-1}r_{n}&\text{if }p\in(2,\infty],\end{cases}

The implied constant depends on the size of the support of
f
.

Proof.

Assume that the support of
f
is contained in
B_{R}=\{x\in V_{g}:\lvert{\sigma(o,x)}\rvert<R\}
,
R>0
.
We treat the cases
p\in[1,2)
and
p\in[2,\infty]
separately.

Case
I:

p\in[1,2)
.
For
x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(n;y,x)\,f(y)-M_{p}(f)(x)\,k(n;o,x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=k(n;o,x)\sum_{y\in B_{R}}f(y)\left(\frac{k(n;y,x)}{k(n;o,x)}-\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)\right).

Since

(4.10)

\bigg|\frac{\sigma(o,x)}{n}-\delta_{p}\bigg|\leqslant n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma},

we can use the quotient asymptotics from Corollary
3.2
. We obtain

\frac{k(n;y,x)}{k(n;o,x)}=\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x)-\sigma(y,x))^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(1+\mathcal{O}\big(n^{-1/2+\gamma}\big)\Big).

By (
1.8
),

\langle{\sigma(o,x)-\sigma(y,x)},{\eta}\rangle\leqslant\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle\leqslant R|\eta|,

therefore

\frac{k(n;y,x)}{k(n;o,x)}=\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x)-\sigma(y,x))^{\frac{1}{p}}+\mathcal{O}\big(n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\big).

On the other hand, by (
4.10
)

\langle{\sigma(o,x)},{\alpha}\rangle\geq 2R

for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, and sufficiently large
n
. Thus for each
\omega\in\Omega(o,x)
we have

x\in[y,\omega]
, hence by (
1.5
),

h(o,y;\omega)=\sigma(o,x)-\sigma(y,x).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x)-\sigma(y,x))^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\chi_{0}(\sigma(o,x)-\sigma(o,y))^{\frac{1}{p}},

and consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}\left|u(n;x)-M_{p}(f)(x)\,k(n;x)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;o,x)^{p}\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p}\mathcal{O}\Big(n^{p(-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}^{p}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p}\mathcal{O}\Big(n^{p(-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma)}\Big),

which completes the proof in this case.

Case
II:

p\in[2,\infty]
.
For
x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
, we have
|\sigma(o,x)|=\mathcal{O}(\tilde{r}_{n})
, where

\tilde{r}_{n}=\begin{cases}n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}&\text{if }p=2\\
r_{n}&\text{if }p\in(2,\infty],\end{cases}

here we have used the notation introduced in (
2.14
). Thus by Theorem
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(n;y,x)\,f(y)-M_{p}(f)(x)\,k(n;o,x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=k(n;o,x)\sum_{y\in B_{R}}f(y)\left(\frac{k(n;y,x)}{k(n;o,x)}-\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x))}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=k(n;o,x)\sum_{y\in B_{R}}f(y)\left(\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x))}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{r}_{n})\big)-\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x))}\right).

By (
4.7
), for all
y\in B_{R}
, the ratio

\sup_{x\in V_{g}}\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(o,x))}\leqslant e^{\langle{\eta},{\sigma(o,y)}\rangle},

thus

u(n;x)-M_{p}(f)(x)\,k(n;x)=k(n;x)\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}\mathcal{O}(\tilde{r}_{n}).

If
p\in[2,\infty)
, by summing up over critical region
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}\left|u(n;x)-M_{p}(f)(x)\,k(n;x)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;x)^{p}\bigg)\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p}\mathcal{O}(\tilde{r}_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}^{p}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}^{p}\mathcal{O}(\tilde{r}_{n})

which completes the proof for
p\in[2,\infty)
. Lastly, for
p=\infty
the reasoning is similar.
∎

In the following result we describe how caloric functions with finitely supported initial datum behave outside the
\ell^{p}

critical regions
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
k
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. There is
c>0
such that for each
u
satisfying (
4.9
) with
f
being a finitely
supported function on
V_{g}
, we have

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\left(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\smallsetminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}|u(n;x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}\big)

where

\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}=\begin{cases}e^{-cn^{2\gamma}}&\text{if }p\in[1,2],\\
n^{-\gamma|\Phi^{++}|}&\text{if }p=2,\\
e^{-cr_{n}}&\text{if }p\in(2,\infty].\end{cases}

The implied constant depends on size of the support of
f
.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is supported on
B_{R}=\{y\in V_{g}:\,|\sigma(o,y)|<R\}
, for some
R>0
. If
p\in[1,\infty)
,
then by the Minkowski inequality we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}\big|\sum_{y\in B_{R}}k(n;y,x)\,f(y)\big|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in B_{R}}\bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{p}\,|f(y)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in B_{R}}|f(y)|\bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now, let us first consider the case
p\in[1,2)
. Observe that if
x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
, then

\left|\sigma(o,x)-n\delta_{p}\right|>n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma},

and hence by (
1.10
)

\displaystyle\left|\sigma(y,x)-n\delta_{p}\right|

\displaystyle>\left|\sigma(o,x)-n\delta_{p}\right|-\left|\sigma(y,x)-\sigma(o,x)\right|

\displaystyle>n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}-R.

Therefore, if
n\geqslant 4R^{2}
, then
x\in V_{g}\setminus\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}
where

\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}=\left\{x\in V_{g}:|\sigma(y,x)-n\delta_{p}|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\right\}.

Next, by repeating the reasoning from the proof of Theorem
2.4
, one can show that there is
c>0

such that for each
y\in B_{R}
,

\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{p}\lesssim\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}^{p}e^{-cpn^{2\gamma}}

where the implied constant is independent of
y\in B_{R}
. Hence, if
n\geqslant 4R^{2}
, then we get

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in B_{R}}|f(y)|\bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in B_{R}}|f(y)|\bigg(\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}e^{-cn^{2\gamma}}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}

which completes the proof for
p\in[1,2)
.

The case when
p\in[2,\infty]
is treated in a similar way. Namely, if
p\in(2,\infty)
we observe that for

x\in V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p}
, we have

|\sigma(y,x)|\geqslant|\sigma(o,x)|-|\sigma(o,y)|\geqslant r_{n}-R,

thus by (
2.14
), there is
N_{1}\geqslant 1
such that
r_{n}\geqslant 2R
for all
n\geqslant N_{1}
. In particular,
for all for
n\geqslant N_{1}
and
y\in B_{R}
, we have
x\in V_{g}\setminus\tilde{N}_{n,y}^{p}
where

\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}=\left\{x\in V_{g}:|\sigma(y,x)|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}r_{n}\right\}.

By repeating the arguments used in the proof of Theorem
2.6
, one can show that

\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{p}\lesssim\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}^{p}e^{-cpr_{n}}.

Analogous reasoning applies to
p=\infty
.

Lastly, we consider
p=2
. We set

\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{2}=\left\{x\in V_{g}:2n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\leqslant|\sigma(y,x)|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma},\text{ and }\langle{\sigma(y,x)},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant 2n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma^{\prime}}\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\right\}.

If
x\in\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{2}
, then by (
1.9
), we have

\displaystyle n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\leqslant 2n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}-R\leqslant|\sigma(y,x)|-|\sigma(o,y)|\leqslant|\sigma(o,x)|

and (
1.10
)

\displaystyle|\sigma(o,x)|\leqslant|\sigma(o,y)|+|\sigma(y,x)|\leqslant R+\frac{1}{2}n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}.

Finally, for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, by (
1.11
),

\displaystyle\langle{\sigma(o,x)},{\alpha}\rangle\geqslant\langle{\sigma(y,x)},{\alpha}\rangle-\max_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}|\alpha||\sigma(o,y)|\geqslant n^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma^{\prime}}.

Hence, there is
N_{1}\geqslant 1
such that for all
n\geqslant N_{1}
and
y\in B_{R}
,

V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}\subset V_{g}\setminus\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{2}
.
Now, following the arguments in the proof of Theorem
2.5
, one can show that for each
y\in B_{R}
,

\sum_{x\in V_{g}\setminus\tilde{\mathscr{N}}_{n,y}^{p}}k(n;y,x)^{2}\lesssim\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}n^{-2\gamma|\Phi^{++}|}

where the implied constant is independent of
y\in B_{R}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then for each
u
satisfying (
4.9
) with
f
being a finitely
supported function on
V_{g}
, we have

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\left\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\right\|_{\ell^{p}}=\Big(\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}+\|M_{p}(f)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\Big)\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon_{n}\big)

where

\varepsilon_{n}=\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}&\text{if }p\in[1,2),\\
n^{-\frac{|\Phi^{++}|}{2|\Phi^{++}|+2}}&\text{if }p=2,\\
n^{-1}r_{n}&\text{if }p\in(2,\infty].\end{cases}

The implied constant depends on the size of the support of
f
.

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, we can write

\displaystyle\big\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}(V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p})}\leqslant\big\|u_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}(V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p})}+\|M_{p}(f)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\big\|k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}(V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p})}^{p}.

Since the mass function
M_{p}(f)
is bounded, Theorem
4.8
, together with Theorems
2.4
,
2.5
and

2.6
, implies that

\big\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}(V_{g}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p})}=\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\big(\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}+\|M_{p}(f)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\big)\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}_{n}\big).

By Theorem
4.7
, we have

\big\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathscr{N}_{n}^{p})}=\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n})

hence to make
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}_{n}=\mathcal{O}\big(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n})
, in the case
p=2
, we need to take

\gamma=\frac{1}{2(|\Phi^{++}|+1)}

which finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 1
.

Given
f
a finitely supported function on
V_{g}
, we can set

\tilde{M}_{2}(f)(x)=\sum_{y\in V_{s}}f(y)\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}\chi_{0}(h(o,y;\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega),\quad x\in V_{g}.

Following the arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
for
p\in[1,2)
but this time in
\ell^{2}
, one can show
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{2}}\big|u_{n}(x)-\tilde{M}_{p}(f)(x)k_{n}(x)\big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=0,

which together with Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
4.8
implies again

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}}\left\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=0.

The details are left to the interested reader.

4.4.
Radial
\ell^{1}
datum and beyond

In this section we prove
\ell^{p}
convergence of caloric functions for larger classes of initial datum than being just
finitely supported. We first discuss the case of radial functions in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
.

Let us recall that if
f
is radial function in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
then it belongs to all
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
,
p\in[1,\infty]
,
see (
4.8
) for the definition. In Section
4.2
we have already seen that for a radial function
f
in

\ell_{s_{p}}^{1}(V_{g})
the mass function is constant

M_{p}(f)\equiv\mathcal{H}f(s_{p}).

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then for each
u
satisfying (
4.9
) with
f
being a radial function in
\ell^{1}(V_{g})
,
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\left\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\right\|_{\ell^{p}}=0.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Let
g
be a radial finitely supported function on
V_{g}
such that

\|f-\widetilde{f}\|_{\ell^{1}}<\sum_{y\in V_{s}}|f(y)-\widetilde{f}(y)|\,<\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon.

Given
p\in[1,\infty]
, by Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
1.2
, there is
C_{p}\geqslant 1
, such that
for all
y\in V_{g}
,

0\leqslant\varphi_{s_{p}}(y)\leqslant C_{p}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|M_{p}(f)-M_{p}(g)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)-g(y)|\varphi_{s_{p}}(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}C_{p}\epsilon.

Let
\tilde{u}
be the caloric function with initial datum
g
, that is

\tilde{u}(n;x)=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}k(n;y,x)g(y).

By Theorem
4.9
, there is
N_{1}\geqslant 1
such that for all
n\geqslant N_{1}
,

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\big\|\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(g)k(n;o,\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|u(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)p(k;o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}+\|M_{p}(f)k(n;o,\cdot)-M_{p}(\tilde{f})k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leqslant\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}\ }+\|\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(\tilde{f})k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{3}C_{p}\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{3}\Big)\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}

which completes the proof.
∎

In the following theorem, we extend the previous result to radial functions in
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
.
The proof draws on both the Kunze–Stein phenomenon and Herz’s
principe de majoration
, tools that are available only
when the underlying building is of Bruhat–Tits type. While the Kunze–Stein phenomenon can be justified for radial convolutors
even in the case of exotic buildings, we do not see how to establish Herz’s
principe de majoration
in that setting.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a simply connected semisimple algebraic group defined over a locally compact non-Archimedean field,
and let
\mathscr{X}
be its Bruhat–Tits building. Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices
of
\mathscr{X}
. Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then for each
u
satisfying (
4.9
) with
f
being a radial function
in
\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\big\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)\,k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}=0.

Proof.

First, let us consider
p\in[1,2]
. We use a density argument to lift the convergence result of Theorem
4.9
.
Let
f\in\ell^{1}(V_{g})
be a radial function. Let
\epsilon>0
. Let
\tilde{f}
be a radial and finitely supported
function such that

\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)-\tilde{f}(y)|\varphi_{s_{p}}(y)\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon.

Since

\displaystyle\big|M_{p}(f)-M_{p}(\tilde{f})\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}\big|f(y)-\tilde{f}(y)\big|\varphi_{s_{p}}(y)=\mathcal{H}(|f-\tilde{f}|)(s_{p}),

we have

\big\|M_{p}(f)k_{n}-M_{p}(\tilde{f})k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Let
\tilde{u}
be the caloric function with initial datum
\tilde{f}
.
Then by Theorem
4.9
, there is
N_{1}\geqslant 1
such that for all
n\geqslant N_{1}
,

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\,\|\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(\tilde{f})\,k(n;o,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon.

Since
k_{n}
is radial, by the radial version of Herz’s principle, see Theorem
A.1
, we get

\big\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\mathcal{H}(|f-\tilde{f}|)(s_{p})\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Therefore, for
n\geqslant N_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|u(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)k(n;\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}+\big\|M_{p}(f)\,k_{n}-M_{p}(\tilde{f})k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leqslant\big\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\ }+\big\|\tilde{u}_{n}-M_{p}(\tilde{f})k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\epsilon\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}},

which finishes the proof.

Assume that
p\in(2,\infty]
. Let
m=n-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. Since

k(n;x,y)=\sum_{z\in V_{g}}k(m;x,z)k(n-m;z,y),

we have

\displaystyle u(n;x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)k_{n}(y,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}f(y)\sum_{z\in V_{g}}k_{n-m}(y,z)k_{m}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in V_{g}}u(n-m;z)k_{m}(z,x).

Hence,

\displaystyle u(n;x)-M_{p}(f)k(n;x)=\sum_{z\in V_{g}}\big(u(n-m;z)-M_{p}(f)k_{n-m}(z)\big)k_{m}(z,x).

Therefore, by the Kunze–Stein phenomenon, see Corollary
A.2
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|u(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)k(n;\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{z\in V_{g}}\big(u(n-m;z)-M_{p}(f)k_{n-m}(z)\big)k_{m}(z,x)\right\|_{\ell^{p}(x)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p}\left\|u(n-m;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)k_{n-m}(\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\|k_{m}\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Since

m=\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}n&\text{if $n$ is even,}\\
n-1&\text{if $n$ is odd,}\end{cases}

in view of Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
, for all
p\in[2,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\|k_{m}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\approx m^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}\varrho^{2m}

\displaystyle\approx n^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}\varrho^{n}\approx\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}},

and

\displaystyle\|k_{n-m}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\approx(n-m)^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}\varrho^{2(n-m)}

\displaystyle\approx m^{-\frac{r}{2}-|\Phi^{++}|}\varrho^{2m}\approx\|k_{m}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, by the first part of the proof, for
p=2
we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\left\|u(n-m;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)k_{n-m}(\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\|k_{m}\|_{\ell^{2}}=0.

The proof is now complete.
∎

Lastly, we study the
\ell^{p}
-convergence of the caloric functions with boundary datum from a class of functions which are not
necessarily radial nor finitely supported. To this end, for each
p\in[1,\infty]
we introduce the following weights,

w_{p}(y)=\begin{cases}e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle}&\text{if }p\in[1,2),\\
e^{\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle}&\text{if }p\in[2,\infty],\end{cases}

where
y\in V_{g}
. Since
w_{p}(y)\geqslant 1
, we have

\ell^{1}(w_{p})=\left\{f:V_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:\quad\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\,w_{p}(y)<+\infty\right\}\subseteq\ell^{1}.

Moreover, if
f\in\ell^{1}(w_{p})
then the mass function
M_{p}(f)
is well-defined and bounded.
Indeed, if
p\in[1,2)
, then by (
1.7
) we have

\displaystyle|M_{p}(f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle{\eta},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle}<\infty.

On the other hand, if
p\in[2,\infty]
, then by (
1.18
), (
1.17
) and (
4.7
) we have

\displaystyle|M_{p}(f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)|\frac{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}{\mathbf{\Phi}(\sigma(y,x))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{s}}|f(y)|\,e^{\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle}.

For
\ell^{1}(w_{p})
we have the following result.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
k_{n}
be a transition kernel of an admissible random walk on good vertices of an affine building
\mathscr{X}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then for each
u
satisfying (
4.9
) with
f\in\ell^{1}(w_{p})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\big\|u_{n}-M_{p}(f)\,k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}=0.

Proof.

Let
p\in[1,2)
and
\epsilon>0
. Since
fw_{p}\in\ell^{1}(V_{g})
, there is
g
a finitely supported function on

V_{g}
such that

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x\in V_{g}}|f(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x\in V_{g}}|f(x)-g(x)|w_{p}(x)\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon.

Moreover, by (
1.7
), for each
x\in V_{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\big|M_{p}(f)(x)-M_{p}(g)(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(x)-g(x)|\fint_{\Omega(o,x)}e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle{\eta},{h(o,y;\omega)}\rangle}\,\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{y\in V_{g}}|f(y)-g(y)|\,e^{\frac{2}{p}\langle{\sigma(o,y)},{\eta}\rangle}<\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon

thus

\big\|M_{p}(f)k_{n}-M_{p}(g)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Let
\tilde{u}
denote the caloric function with initial datum
g
. Then by Theorem
4.9
, there is
N_{1}\geqslant 1
,
such that for all
n\geqslant N_{1}
,

\frac{1}{\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}}\big\|\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(g)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon.

Using the Minkowski’s inequality for integrals, we get

\displaystyle\big\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\sum_{y\in V_{g}}(f(y)-g(y))k(n;y,\cdot)\bigg\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f-g\|_{\ell^{1}}\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Hence, for
n\geqslant N_{1}
,

\displaystyle\big\|u(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(f)k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big\|u(n;\cdot)-\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}+\big\|\tilde{u}(n;\cdot)-M_{p}(g)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}+\big\|M_{p}(f)k_{n}-M_{p}(g)k_{n}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\epsilon\|k_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}.

The proof for
p\in[2,\infty]
follows by the same line of reasoning provided one uses (
4.7
) in place of
(
1.7
).
∎

Appendix A
Herz’s principle

In this appendix we discuss the radial version of Herz’s
principe de majoration
under the assumption that the building
is the Bruhat–Tits building, that is the affine building associated to a simply connected semisimple algebraic group defined
over a locally compact non-Archimedean field. Then we deduce the Kunze–Stein phenomenon. Both facts were proved in
[
27
]
,
however in this paper we just need their radial versions and they have much simpler proofs.

Theorem A.1

(Herz’s
principe de majoration
)
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a simply connected semisimple algebraic group defined over a locally compact non-Archimedean field,
and let
\mathscr{X}
be its Bruhat–Tits building. Let
p\in(1,2]
. Suppose that
K
is a radial function in

\ell^{1}_{s_{p}}(V_{g})
. Then for each radial
f\in\ell^{p}(V_{g})
,

\|f\times K\|_{\ell^{p}}\leqslant\mathcal{H}(|K|)(s_{p})\|f\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Proof.

We first show that for all radial functions
f\in\ell^{p}(V_{g})
and
h\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}(V_{g})
and
x\in V_{g}
,

(A.1)

|f\times h(x)|\leqslant\varphi_{s_{p}}(x)\|f\|_{\ell^{p}}\|h\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}.

Let
x=g_{0}.o
. We have

\displaystyle h\times f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in V_{g}}h(y)f(\sigma(y,x))

\displaystyle=\int_{G}h(g.o)f(\sigma(g.o,g_{0}.o)){\>\rm d}g

\displaystyle=\int_{G}h(g^{-1}.o)f(gg_{0}.o){\>\rm d}g

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\int_{NA}h\big((nak)^{-1}.o\big)f(nakg_{0}.o)\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,nak.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a{\>\rm d}k

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\int_{NA}h\big(a^{-1}n^{-1}.o\big)f(nakg_{0}.o)\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a{\>\rm d}k.

Now, for a fixed
k\in K
, we apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{NA}h\big(a^{-1}n^{-1}.o\big)f(nakg_{0}.o)\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\bigg(\int_{NA}\big|h\big(a^{-1}n^{-1}.o\big)\big|^{p^{\prime}}\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\bigg(\int_{NA}\big|f(nakg_{0}.o)\big|^{p}\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a\bigg)^{1/p}.

We observe that

\displaystyle\int_{NA}\big|h\big(a^{-1}n^{-1}.o\big)\big|^{p^{\prime}}\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a=\int_{K}\int_{NA}\big|h\big(k^{-1}a^{-1}n^{-1}.o\big)\big|^{p^{\prime}}{\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a{\>\rm d}k=\|h\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}.

We next write

kg_{0}=n^{\prime}a^{\prime}k^{\prime}.

Hence, for
m=nan^{\prime}a^{-1}\in N
, we have

nakg_{0}=nan^{\prime}a^{-1}aa^{\prime}k^{\prime}=maa^{\prime}k^{\prime}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{NA}\big|f(nakg_{0}.o)\big|^{p}\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}n{\>\rm d}a

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{NA}\big|f(maa^{\prime}.o)\big|^{p}\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,a.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}m{\>\rm d}a

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{NA}\big|f(mb.o)\big|^{p}\chi_{0}\big(-h(a^{\prime},b.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}m{\>\rm d}b

\displaystyle\qquad=\chi_{0}\big(h(o,a^{\prime}.o;\omega_{0})\big)\int_{NA}\big|f(mb.o)\big|^{p}\chi_{0}\big(-h(o,b.o;\omega_{0})\big){\>\rm d}m{\>\rm d}b

\displaystyle\qquad=\chi_{0}\big(h(o,k.x;\omega_{0})\big)\|f\|_{\ell^{p}}^{p}.

Since
K
acts simply transitively on
\Omega
, and

\chi_{0}\big(h(o,k.x;\omega_{0})\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\chi_{0}\big(h(o,k.x;\omega_{0})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\langle{s_{p}},{h(o,k.x;\omega_{0})}\rangle},

we get

\displaystyle\int_{K}\chi_{0}\big(h(o,k.x;\omega_{0})\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}{\>\rm d}k

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{0}\big(h(o,x;\omega_{0})\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\nu({\rm d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{s_{p}}(x),

which easily leads to (
4.3
).

Now, setting
\check{f}(\lambda)=f(\lambda^{\star})
, by (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle{f\times K},{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{K},{\check{f}\times g}\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x\in V_{g}}|K(x)|\big|\check{f}\times g(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{x\in V_{g}}|K(x)|\varphi_{s_{p}}(x)\|\check{f}\|_{\ell^{p}}\|g\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{H}(|K|)(s_{p})\|f\|_{\ell^{p}}\|g\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}},

and the theorem follows.
∎

Corollary A.2

(Kunze–Stein phenomenon)
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a simply connected semisimple algebraic group defined over a locally compact non-Archimedean field,
and let
\mathscr{X}
be its Bruhat–Tits building. Let
p\in[1,2)
. There is
C_{p}>0
such that for all radial

K\in\ell^{p}(V_{g})
and
f\in\ell^{2}(V_{g})
,

\|f\times K\|_{\ell^{2}}\leqslant C_{p}\|K\|_{\ell^{p}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Proof.

Let us observe that
\varphi_{0}\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}(V_{g})
. Indeed, by Proposition
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{g}}\varphi_{0}(x)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}\Phi(\lambda)^{p^{\prime}}\chi_{0}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\lambda\in P^{+}}\chi_{0}(\lambda)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{2}+1}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\big(1+|\langle{\lambda},{\alpha}\rangle|\big)<\infty.

Consequently, by (
A.1
), for a radial function
g\in\ell^{2}(V_{g})
we get

\|f\times g\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}\leqslant\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}.

Since
f\times g
is radial, for each radial
K
belonging to
\ell^{p}(V_{g})
,

\langle{K},{f\times g}\rangle\leqslant\|K\|_{\ell^{p}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}},

thus

\langle{K\times\check{f}},{g}\rangle\leqslant\|K\|_{\ell^{p}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}.

Hence,

\|K\times\check{f}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leqslant\|K\|_{\ell^{p}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Tree metrics and log-concavity for matroids
Federico Ardila–Mantilla, Sergio Cristancho, Graham Denham, Christopher Eur, June Huh, Botong Wang

Abstract
. We show that a set function ν \nu satisfies the gross substitutes property if and only if its homogeneous generating polynomial Z q , ν Z_{q,\nu} is a Lorentzian polynomial for all positive q ≤ 1 q\leq 1 , answering a question of Eur–Huh. We achieve this by giving a rank 1 1 upper bound for the distance matrix of an ultrametric tree, refining a classical result of Graham–Pollak. This characterization enables us to resolve open problems that strengthen Mason’s log-concavity conjectures for the numbers of independent sets of a matroid: one posed by Giansiracusa–Rincón–Schleis–Ulirsch for valuated matroids, and two posed by Dowling in 1980 and Zhao in 1985 for ordinary matroids.

Let

.

(2)

If

, then there is

.

The

is the common cardinality of the maximal independent sets. For background and any undefined terminology in matroid theory, we refer to
[

Let

1.1.
Inequalities for
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave set functions

A function
\nu\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{-\infty\right\}
is said to be
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave
if it satisfies the
exchange property
: For any
I_{1},I_{2}\subseteq E
and
i_{1}\in I_{1}\setminus I_{2}
, we have

(1)

either
\nu(I_{1})+\nu(I_{2})\leq\nu(I_{1}\setminus i_{1})+\nu(I_{2}\cup i_{1})
, or

(2)

there is
i_{2}\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
such that
\nu(I_{1})+\nu(I_{2})\leq\nu((I_{1}\setminus i_{1})\cup i_{2})+\nu((I_{2}\setminus i_{2})\cup i_{1})
.

The
effective domain
of such a function is the set

\dom(\nu)\coloneqq\left\{I\subseteq{E}\,\colon\,\nu(I)\neq-\infty\right\}.

For our purposes, without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\dom(\nu)
is nonempty.

Introduced by Murota and Shioura
[
MS99
]
,
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions are central objects in discrete convex analysis.
Fujishige–Yang
[
FY03
]
and Reijnierse–van Gellekom–Potters
[
RvGP02
]
independently proved that
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concavity is equivalent to the
gross substitutes property
in economics, introduced by Kelso and Crawford
[
KJC82
]
two decades earlier.
For a comprehensive introduction to
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions, we refer the reader to
[
Mur03
, Chapter 6]
.
The notion of

\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions generalizes several concepts in matroid theory, including independent sets, rank functions, and valuated matroids.

Example 1.1

(Matroid independent sets)
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a matroid on
{E}
. The
independent set indicator function
of
\mathsf{M}
, defined by

\nu_{\mathsf{M}}(S)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\quad 0&\text{if $S$ is an independent subset of $\mathsf{M}$,}\\
-\infty&\text{if $S$ is not an independent subset of $\mathsf{M}$,}\end{cases}

is an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function whose effective domain is
the collection of independent sets
\IN(\mathsf{M})
.

Example 1.2

(Matroid rank functions)
.

The
rank function
of
\mathsf{M}
, defined by

\rk_{\mathsf{M}}(S)\coloneqq\max\Big\{\left|I\right|,\ \text{$I$ is an independent set of $\mathsf{M}$ in $S$}\Big\},

is an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function whose effective domain is
2^{E}
.
More generally, any non-negative linear combination of the rank functions of the constituent matroids in a flag matroid is a
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
[
Shi12
, Theorem 3]
.

Example 1.3

(Valuated matroid independent sets)
.

A
valuated matroid
of rank
d
on
E
is a function
\underline{\nu}\colon{E\choose d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{-\infty\right\}

that satisfies the
symmetric exchange property
: For any
d
-element subsets
B_{1},B_{2}
of
E
and
b_{1}\in B_{1}\setminus B_{2}
, there is
b_{2}\in B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
such that

\underline{\nu}(B_{1})+\underline{\nu}(B_{2})\leq\underline{\nu}((B_{1}\setminus b_{1})\cup b_{2})+\underline{\nu}((B_{2}\setminus b_{2})\cup b_{1}).

Valuated matroids are precisely the possible height functions in a regular subdivision of a matroid polytope into matroid polytopes.
[
Spe08
]

Murota
[
Mur97
]
considered the
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave extension
\nu
of
\underline{\nu}
to
2^{E}
given by

\nu(S)\coloneqq\max\Big\{\underline{\nu}(B),\ \text{$B$ is a $d$-element subset of $E$ containing $S$}\Big\},

where the maximum of the empty set is defined to be
-\infty
. The effective domain of
\nu
is the collection of independent sets of a matroid on
E
, the
underlying matroid
of
\underline{\nu}
.

For a function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, an integer
0\leq k\leq n
, and a real number
0<q\leq 1
, we set

I_{q,\nu;k}\coloneqq\sum_{I\in\binom{E}{k}}q^{-\nu(I)},

where, by convention,
q^{\infty}=0
. This sequence was first considered by Giansiracusa, Rincón, Schleis, and Ulirsch for valuated matroids in the context of Example
1.3
, who proved in
[
GRSU24
, Theorem A]
that Murota’s extension
\nu
of a valuated matroid satisfies
a generalization of (
M2
):

I_{q,\nu;k}^{2}\geq\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)I_{q,\nu;k-1}I_{q,\nu;k+1}\ \ \text{ for all $0<k<n$ and $0<q\leq 1$}.

They asked in
[
GRSU24
, Question 4.1]
whether
\nu
satisfies a generalization of (
M3
), and they provided extensive numerical evidence in support of this.
We prove their prediction in the more general setting of
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, we have

I_{q,\nu;k}^{2}\geq\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{1}{n-k}\Big)I_{q,\nu;k-1}I_{q,\nu;k+1}\ \ \text{for all $0<k<n$ and $0<q\leq 1$.}

We deduce Theorem
1.4
from an analytic characterization of

\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions in terms of
Lorentzian polynomials

[
BH20
]
, whose definition we recall in Section
3.1

We define the
homogeneous generating polynomial
a function

by

Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},y)\coloneqq\sum_{S\subseteq E}q^{-\nu(S)}\mathsf{x}^{S}y^{\left|E\setminus S\right|}\ \ \text{with}\ \ \mathsf{x}^{S}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in S}x_{i},

where
\mathsf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in E}
and

is a homogenizing variable different from
x_{i}
for

. We view

as a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n

with a positive parameter

Theorem 1.5
.

A function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave if and only if its homogeneous generating polynomial

is a Lorentzian polynomial for all positive
q\leq 1
.

Theorem

answers a question of Eur–Huh in
[
EH20
, Section 5]

[

The other direction is a new contribution, which generalizes the following known results:

(1)

When

in Example

1.2.
Polynomial inequalities for matroids

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a matroid on
E
, and let
\IN_{k}(\mathsf{M})
be the collection of
k

\mathsf{M}
. We consider the generating polynomial

I_{k}(\mathsf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{S\in\IN(\mathsf{M})_{k}}\mathsf{x}^{S},\ \ \text{with}\ \ \mathsf{x}^{S}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in S}x_{i},

where
\mathsf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in E}

For multivariate polynomials

f,g\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{i}]_{i\in E}
, we write

if all the coefficients of the difference

Dowling
[

conjectured that the polynomial refinement of Mason’s conjecture (

I_{k}(\mathsf{x})^{2}\succeq I_{k-1}(\mathsf{x})I_{k+1}(\mathsf{x})

[

We prove Zhao’s and Dowling’s conjectures.
Using Theorem
1.5
,
we establish a stronger polynomial inequality for the more general class of
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions.
For a function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, we set

I_{q,\nu;k}(\mathsf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{S\in\binom{E}{k}}q^{-\nu(S)}\mathsf{x}^{S}.

When
\nu
is the independent set indicator function of
\mathsf{M}

M2

.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

I_{q,\nu;k}(\mathsf{x})^{2}\succeq\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)I_{q,\nu;k-1}(\mathsf{x})I_{q,\nu;k+1}(\mathsf{x})\ \ \text{for all $0<k<n$ and $0<q\leq 1$.}

1.3.
An inequality for ultrametric trees

A central ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.5
is a result on tree distance matrices that we now describe.
In their design of efficient address systems for communication networks, Graham and Pollak
[
GP71
]
introduced the
distance matrix
of a graph, whose
ij
entry is the distance between vertices
i
and
j
. They showed that the signature of this matrix gives a lower bound for the addresses in their design, and proved that the distance matrix of any tree has the Lorentzian signature
(+,-,\cdots,-)
. The latter fact also follows from work of Schoenberg
[
Sch35
, Theorem 1]
, using the fact that an ultrametric tree can be metrically embedded in an
\ell^{2}
-space
[
TV83
]
. Our proofs of the matroid inequalities above rely on a refinement of this result for ultrametric trees.

An
ultrametric tree
is a rooted tree with nonnegative lengths on its edges such that all the leaves are at the same distance from the root. If the tree has
n
leaves, let
D
be the
n\times n

leaf distance matrix
, whose
ij
entry
is the distance between the leaves
i
and
j
.
For
n\times n
real symmetric matrices
A
and
B
, we write
A\geq B
if the difference
A-B
is positive semidefinite.
We write
\mathbf{1}_{n\times n}
for the
n\times n
matrix all of whose entries are
1
.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any ultrametric tree with
n
leaves whose common distance from the root is
1
,

\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\mathbf{1}_{n\times n}\geq\frac{1}{2}D.

The constant
(1-\frac{1}{n})
is the smallest number that makes the above statement true.

Theorem
1.8
can be seen as a quantitative refinement of Graham and Pollak’s result that
D
has at most one positive eigenvalue in the ultrametric case.
In Section
2.2
, we characterize the ultrametric trees for which the scalar
(1-\frac{1}{n})
is optimal.

Theorem
1.8
serves as the base case in our inductive argument for the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
This connection stems from Lemma
3.3
, which states that any
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function on
2^{E}
whose effective domain contains all the subsets of
E
with at most
2
elements gives rise to an ultrametric tree.
This is a variation of the standard fact that the space of uniform valuated matroids of rank 2 is equal to the space of phylogenetic trees
[
MS15
, Theorem 4.3.5]
.

2.1.
Ultrametric tree matrices

When
T

We call

for

If

Definition 2.1
.

An

If

d(i,j)\leq\max\Big\{d(i,k),d(k,j)\Big\}\ \ \text{for any leaves $i,j,k$.}

It follows that the leaves of

satisfies the

:

The maximum among

is attained at least twice for any leaves

Every ultrametric tree with positive lengths defines an ultrametric on its leaves, and every finite ultrametric space arises in this way from an ultrametric tree

3.1.
Lorentzian polynomials and discrete convex analysis

Let
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{n}
be the space of degree
d
homogeneous polynomials in
n

variables
\mathsf{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in E}
with real coefficients, equipped with the usual topology of a finite-dimensional real vector space.
We write
\partial_{i}
for the differential operator
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
for
i\in E
, and set

\partial^{\alpha}\coloneqq\prod_{i\in E}\partial_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\ \ \text{for any $\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i\in E}\in\mathbb{Z}^{E}_{\geq 0}$}.

In degree
2
, the set of
strictly Lorentzian polynomials

\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{n}^{2}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{n}

is, by definition, the set of quadratic forms
f
whose Hessian is entrywise positive and has
Lorentzian signature:

\mathring{\mathrm{L}}^{2}_{n}\coloneqq\left\{f\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{n}\;\middle|\;\left(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f\right)_{i,j\in E}\text{~has only positive entries and signature}(+,-\cdots,-)\right\}.

In degree
d>2
, we
define the set of
strictly Lorentzian polynomials

\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{n}^{d}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{d}_{n}
recursively by setting

\mathring{\mathrm{L}}^{d}_{n}\coloneqq\left\{f\in\mathrm{H}^{d}_{n}\;\middle|\;\partial_{i}f\in\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{n}^{d-1}\text{~for all
$i\in E$}\right\}.

The set of
Lorentzian polynomials

\mathrm{L}_{n}^{d}

is defined to be the closure of
\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{n}^{d}
in
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{n}
.
Here we collect some properties of Lorentzian polynomials relevant to this paper:

(1)

If
f(\mathsf{x})\in\mathrm{L}^{d}_{n}
and
A
is an
n\times m
nonnegative matrix, then
f(A\mathsf{y})\in\mathrm{L}^{d}_{m}

[
BH20
, Theorem 2.10]
.

(2)

If
f\in\mathrm{L}_{n}^{d}
, then
\sum_{i}a_{i}\partial_{i}f\in\mathrm{L}_{n}^{d-1}
for any
a_{i}\geq 0

[
BH20
, Corollary 2.11]
.

(3)

If
f\in\mathrm{L}_{n}^{d}
and
g\in\mathrm{L}_{n}^{e}
, then
fg\in\mathrm{L}^{d+e}_{n}

[
BH20
, Corollary 2.32]
.

(4)

A degree
d
bivariate polynomial
f(x,y)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{d}a_{k}x^{k}y^{d-k}

is Lorentzian if and only if

(\partial_{x}^{d}f,\ldots,\partial_{x}^{d-k}\partial_{y}^{k}f,\ldots,\partial_{y}^{d}f)=\left(\frac{a_{0}}{\binom{d}{0}},\ldots,\frac{a_{k}}{\binom{d}{k}},\ldots\frac{a_{d}}{\binom{d}{d}}\right)

is a log-concave sequence of nonnegative numbers with no internal zeros
[
BH20
, Example 2.3]
.

We briefly review the theory of Lorentzian polynomials and its relation to discrete convex analysis.
For further details, we refer to
[
BH20
]
for Lorentzian polynomials and
[
Mur03
]
for discrete convex analysis.
We consider
the rank
d
simplex in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{E}
defined by

\Delta_{n}^{d}\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{E}\;\middle|\;\alpha_{1}+\dotsb+\alpha_{n}=d\right\}.

For
i\in E
, we write
e_{i}
for the
i
-th standard basis vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{E}
.
An
M-convex set
of rank
d
on
E
is a subset
\mathrm{J}\subseteq\Delta_{n}^{d}
satisfying the
symmetric exchange property
:

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{J}
and
i\in E
such that
\alpha_{i}>\beta_{i}
, there is
j\in E
such that
\beta_{j}>\alpha_{j}
for which both
\alpha+e_{j}-e_{i}
and
\beta+e_{i}-e_{j}
belong to
\mathrm{J}
.

A function
\nu\colon\Delta_{n}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is said to be
M-convex
if,
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta_{n}^{d}
and
i\in E
such that
\alpha_{i}>\beta_{i}
, there is
j\in E
such that
\beta_{j}>\alpha_{j}
and

\nu(\alpha)+\nu(\beta)\geq\nu(\alpha+e_{j}-e_{i})+\nu(\beta+e_{i}-e_{j}).

Equivalently, a set of lattice points is
M
-convex if and only if it is the set of lattice points in an integral generalized permutahedron, and a function is
M
-convex if and only if it induces a regular subdivision of an integral generalized permutahedron into integral generalized permutahedra.

We say that
\nu
is
M-concave
if
-\nu
is M-convex.
The
effective domain
of an M-convex function is

\dom(\nu)\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{d}\;\middle|\;\nu(\alpha)\neq\infty\right\},

and the effective domain of an M-concave function is defined similarly.
Note that the effective domains of M-convex functions and M-concave functions are M-convex.
The
support
of a degree
d
homogeneous polynomial in
n
variables
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{d}}c_{\alpha}\mathsf{x}^{\alpha}
is defined by

\supp(f)\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{d}\;\middle|\;c_{\alpha}\neq 0\right\}.

The following characterization of Lorentzian polynomials is central to their relationship to matroids and M-convexity
[
BH20
, Theorem 2.25]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent for
f\in\mathrm{H}^{d}_{n}
:

(1)

The polynomial
f
is Lorentzian.

(2)

The support of
f
is an M-convex set and, for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{d-2}
, the Hessian of
{\partial^{\alpha}f}
has only nonnegative entries and has at most one positive eigenvalue.

From this, one may deduce the following characterizations of M-convex functions
[
BH20
, Theorem 3.14]
: A function
\nu:\Delta^{d}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is M-convex if and only if its
normalized generating polynomial

f_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\dom(\nu)}q^{\nu(\alpha)}\frac{\mathsf{x}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}

is a Lorentzian polynomial for all positive
q\leq 1
, where

{\mathsf{x}^{\alpha}}/{\alpha!}
is the product of
{x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}/{\alpha_{i}!}
for
i\in E
.
This in turn implies the following characterization of M-convex sets
[
BH20
, Theorem 3.10]
: A subset
\mathrm{J}
of
\Delta^{d}_{n}
is M-convex if and only if its normalized generating polynomial

f_{\mathrm{J}}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{J}}\frac{\mathsf{x}^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}

is a Lorentzian polynomial. For example, a collection of
d
-element subsets of
E
is the set of bases of a matroid on
E
if and only if its generating polynomial is Lorentzian.

Remark 3.2
.

The above characterization of M-convex functions specializes to the following characterization of
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions on
2^{E}
: A function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave if and only if the

N(Z_{q,\nu})\coloneqq\sum_{S\subseteq E}q^{-\nu(S)}\mathsf{x}^{S}\frac{y^{\left|E\setminus S\right|}}{\left|E\setminus S\right|!}

is a Lorentzian polynomial for all positive

, where
y
is a homogenizing variable different from
x_{i}
for

.
Identifying the variables
x_{i}
with each other, this characterization gives the property (

) for

.
It is interesting to compare this characterization of

-concave functions on

with that in Theorem

Note that, in general, if we omit any normalizing factor

from the normalized generating polynomial of an M-convex function, we do not get a Lorentzian polynomial. For example, among the three bivariate quadratic forms

\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{2}+x_{1}x_{2}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2},\hskip 10.00002ptx_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{2},\hskip 10.00002ptx_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}^{2},

3.2.
Lorentzian property of the homogeneous generating polynomial

The following lemma is a variation of known relations between tree metrics, ultrametrics, and valuated matroids. See, for instance,
[

, Section 4.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\nu
be an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function on
2^{E}
whose effective domain contains all subsets of
E
with at most
2
elements. Then, for any
0<q\leq 1
, the function

d(i,j)\coloneqq\begin{cases}2q^{-\nu(i,j)+\nu(i)+\nu(j)-\nu(\varnothing)},&\text{if $i\neq j$,}\\
0,&\text{if $i=j$,}\end{cases}

defines an ultrametric on
E
of radius
\leq 1
.

Proof.

By the local exchange property for
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions
[
Mur03
, Theorem 6.4]
, for any distinct elements

in
E
, the maximum among the three numbers

\nu(j,k)+\nu(i),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu(i,k)+\nu(j),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu(i,j)+\nu(k),

is achieved at least twice. Thus, the maximum among the three numbers

\nu(j,k)-\nu(j)-\nu(k)+\nu(\varnothing)\,\hskip 10.00002pt\nu(i,k)-\nu(i)-\nu(k)+\nu(\varnothing),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu(i,j)-\nu(i)-\nu(j)+\nu(\varnothing),

is achieved at least twice as well. Since

, we get that

is an ultrametric on

.
Also, the exchange property for

gives

\nu(i,j)+\nu(\varnothing)\leq\nu(i)+\nu(j)\ \ \text{for any distinct $i,j$ in $E$.}

which implies that

has radius

.

Theorem
1.5
.

A function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave if and only if its homogeneous generating polynomial

Z_{q,\nu}
is a Lorentzian polynomial for all positive
q\leq 1
.

Proof.

The “if” direction is
[
EH20
, Proposition 5.5]
. We show that
Z_{q,\nu}
is Lorentzian for positive
q\leq 1
when
\nu
is
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave.

We observe that any
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function on
2^{E}
can be approximated by a sequence of
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions whose effective domains are
2^{E}
. More precisely, if
\nu
is such an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function, then there is a sequence
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave functions

with

such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{k}(S)=\nu(S)\ \ \text{for every $S\subseteq E$.}

This is a special case of
[
BH20
, Lemma 3.27]
, because
restrictions of M-concave functions to coordinate half-spaces are M-concave
[
Mur03
, Section 6.4]
.
Since
\mathrm{L}^{d}_{n}
is closed, we may assume that
0<q<1
, and for any such
q
, we have

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}Z_{q,\nu_{k}}=Z_{q,\nu}.

Therefore, we may assume that
0<q<1
and the effective domain of
\nu
is
2^{E}
.

Since the support of
Z_{q,\nu}
is M-convex, by Theorem
3.1
, it is enough to
show that all the

are Lorentzian.
For any

, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}Z_{q,\nu}=Z_{q,\nu/i},

where
\nu/i
is the
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function on

defined by
\nu/i(S)=\nu(S\cup i)
. For general discussion about the

\nu/i
of an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu
, see
[
Mur03
, Section 6.4]
. Thus, modulo induction on

, we only need to consider the Lorentzian property of the quadratic form

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{n-2}Z_{q,\nu}=(n-2)!\left[\frac{n(n-1)}{2}q^{-\nu(\varnothing)}y^{2}+(n-1)\sum_{i\in\binom{E}{1}}q^{-\nu(i)}x_{i}y+\sum_{ij\in\binom{E}{2}}q^{-\nu(i,j)}x_{i}x_{j}\right].

Up to positive rescaling of rows and columns, the Hessian of this quadratic form is

A(\nu)\coloneqq\left[\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}n/(n-1)q^{-\nu(\varnothing)}&q^{-\nu(1)}&\cdots&q^{-\nu(n)}\\
\hline\cr q^{-\nu(1)}&0&\cdots&q^{-\nu(i,j)}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
q^{-\nu(n)}&q^{-\nu(i,j)}&&0\end{array}\right].

Our goal is to show that
A(\nu)
has at most one positive eigenvalue for any positive
q\leq 1
.
Computing the Schur complement with respect to the block structure indicated above, we may block diagonalize

to

\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}n/(n-1)q^{-\nu(\varnothing)}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&B(\nu)\end{array}\right],

where

B(\nu)_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1-n}{n}q^{\nu(\varnothing)-\nu(i)-\nu(j)}+q^{-\nu(ij)},&\text{if $i\neq j$,}\\
\frac{1-n}{n}q^{\nu(\varnothing)-2\nu(i)},&\text{if $i=j$.}\end{cases}

Thus, it is enough to show that

is negative semidefinite for positive
q\leq 1
.
Rescaling rows and columns of

with entries

C(\nu)_{ij}=\begin{cases}-(1-\frac{1}{n})+q^{-\nu(i,j)+\nu(i)+\nu(j)-\nu(\varnothing)},&\text{if $i\neq j$,}\\

By Lemma

by the same lemma, Theorem

implies that

is negative semidefinite for positive

.
This implies the same for

, and hence

has at most one positive eigenvalue for positive
q\leq 1
. This finishes the proof that
Z_{q,\nu}
is Lorentzian for positive
q\leq 1
when
\nu
is
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave.

Theorem
1.6
.

For any
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, we have the coefficientwise inequality

I_{q,\nu;k}(\mathsf{x})^{2}\succeq\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)I_{q,\nu;k-1}(\mathsf{x})I_{q,\nu;k+1}(\mathsf{x})\ \ \text{for all $0<k<n$ and $0<q\leq 1$.}

Proof.

Fix a positive real parameter
q\leq 1
, and consider two copies of homogeneous generating polynomial of an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu
, say
Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},y)
and
Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},z)
, where we use distinct homogenizing variables
y
and
z
for the same set of
n
variables
\mathsf{x}
.
Since
Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},y)
and
Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},z)
are Lorentzian by Theorem
1.5
, their product is Lorentzian too
[
BH20
, Corollary 2.32]
.
Writing
n
for the cardinality of
E
as before, we have

Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},y)Z_{q,\nu}(\mathsf{x},z)=\sum\limits_{S}\left[\sum_{k+j=\left|S\right|}N^{S}_{q,\nu}(j,k)y^{n-j}z^{n-k}\right]\mathsf{x}^{S},

where the sum is over all multisets
S
on
E
and
|S|
is the cardinality of
S
counting multiplicities. Iterating Lemma
3.4
, we see that, for each
S
, the bivariate polynomial

\sum_{j+k=\left|S\right|}N^{S}_{q,\nu}(j,k)y^{n-j}z^{n-k}

is Lorentzian.
Therefore, the sequence of normalized coefficients
(n-j)!\ (n-k)!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(j,k)
is log-concave and has no internal zeros
[
BH20
, Example 2.3]
. It follows that, for all
i\leq j\leq k\leq l
for with
i+l=j+k=\left|S\right|
, we have

(3.1)

(n-j)!\ (n-k)!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(j,k)\geq(n-i)!\ (n-l)!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(i,l).

This is not quite the inequality that we want, but we can apply it to a different

\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu^{\prime}
constructed from
\nu
and
S
to deduce the desired inequality

j!\ k!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(j,k)\geq i!\ l!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(i,l).

This will suffice, as the above displayed inequality for all
S
is equivalent to the coefficient-wise inequality

j!\ k!\ I_{q,\nu;j}(\mathsf{x})\ I_{q,\nu;k}(\mathsf{x})\succeq i!\ l!\ I_{q,\nu;i}(\mathsf{x})\ I_{q,\nu;l}(\mathsf{x}).

Let
S

\underline{S}
of

We define an
\text{M}^{\natural}
-concave function
\nu^{\prime}:2^{E^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

\nu^{\prime}(I)=\begin{cases}\nu(I),&\text{if $I$ is a subset of $\underline{S}$,}\\
-\infty&\text{if otherwise.}\end{cases}

By construction, for any nonnegative integers

N^{S}_{q,\nu}(a,b)=N^{S}_{q,\nu^{\prime}}(a,b).

Applying (
3.1
) to
\nu

, that

j!\ k!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(j,k)=(n^{\prime}-j)!\ (n^{\prime}-k)!\ N^{S^{\prime}}_{q,\nu^{\prime}}(j,k)\geq(n^{\prime}-i)!\ (n^{\prime}-l)!\ N^{S^{\prime}}_{q,\nu^{\prime}}(i,l)=i!\ l!\ N^{S}_{q,\nu}(i,l)

as desired.

Optimal Algorithms for Nonlinear Estimation with Convex Models
Simon Foucart — Texas A&M University

Abstract
A linear functional of an object from a convex symmetric set can be optimally estimated, in a worst-case sense, by a linear functional of observations made on the object. This well-known fact is extended here to a nonlinear setting: other simple functionals of the object can be optimally estimated by functionals of the observations that share a similar simple structure. This is established for the maximum of several linear functionals and even for the ℓ \ell th largest among them. Proving the latter requires an unusual refinement of the analytical Hahn–Banach theorem. The existence results are accompanied by practical recipes relying on convex optimization to construct the desired functionals, thereby justifying the term of estimation algorithms.



Key words and phrases:
Optimal recovery, Minimax problems, Dominated extension theorem.

AMS classification:
41A28, 46N10, 65D15, 90C47.

Introduction

Suppose that an unknown object
f
,
living in a vector space
F
,
is acquired via a vector
\Lambda(f)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of linear observations
\lambda_{1}(f),\ldots,\lambda_{m}(f)\in\mathbb{R}
,
in addition to the prior knowledge that it belongs to a prescribed model set
\mathcal{K}
.
Estimating a quantity
\Gamma(f)
,
living in some normed space
X
,
boils down to devising a recovery map
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to X
.
From a worst-case perspective,
its performance is measured through

e(\Delta):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\|\Gamma(f)-\Delta(\Lambda(f))\|.

For a fixed
\Lambda
,
constructing a map
\Delta
that minimizes the above
is the essence of Optimal Recovery
[
8
]
,
a lead-in to the field of Information-Based Complexity
[
11
]
,
where one can also minimize over
\Lambda
.
For any
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to X
,
the validity of the lower bound

e(\Delta)\geq e_{\flat},\qquad\mbox{where}\quad e_{\flat}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}\\
\Lambda(f^{\prime})=\Lambda(f^{\prime\prime})\end{subarray}}\frac{\|\Gamma(f^{\prime})-\Gamma(f^{\prime\prime})\|}{2},

is well known and easy to derive,
and so is the complementary inequality
e(\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm cons})\leq 2e_{\flat}
,
where
\Delta^{\rm cons}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to F
is any
data- and model-consistent map,
i.e.,
one that satisfies
\Lambda(\Delta^{\rm cons}(y))=y
and
\Delta^{\rm cons}(y)\in\mathcal{K}
for all
y\in\Lambda(\mathcal{K})
.
In short, the map
\Gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm cons}

is nearly optimal (with a factor
2
).
To surpass this, one seeks maps
\Delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to X
yielding
e(\Delta^{\rm opti})\leq e_{\flat}
, which are therefore genuinely optimal (with a factor
1
).
This is achievable when
\Gamma=\gamma
is a linear functional—throughout, lowercase Greek letters are used for functionals.
This classical result is due to Smolyak (see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 4.7]
) when the set
\mathcal{K}
is convex and symmetric about the origin
and was refined by Sukharev
[
10
]
when
\mathcal{K}
is merely convex.
These two results also guarantee the existence of a genuinely optimal estimation map
\Delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which is linear in the former case and affine in the latter.

The goal of this article is, firstly, to exhibit examples of nonlinear yet ‘simple’ functionals
\gamma:F\to\mathbb{R}

for which ‘simple’ genuinely optimal estimation maps
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
exist
and, secondly, to complement existence results with practical constructions whenever possible.
As a prototypical example of nonlinear functionals,
the maximum of a function
f
was considered early on with near optimality in mind, see e.g.
[
12
]
.
With genuine optimality in mind,
it has been treated more recently relatively to a model set
\mathcal{K}
consisting of Hölder functions within the space
F
of continuous functions, see
[
5
]
.
The arguments were specific to the case at hand,
so the situation where the function space
F
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, say,
was not covered.
The results obtained in the present article do apply to such a situation:
for instance, Theorem
1
will guarantee the existence of a genuinely optimal estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which is convex
and Proposition
5
will later provide a practical recipe to construct it,
under the proviso that the maximum is over a finite set in order for a convex optimization program to be solvable.
As an extension,
Theorem
3
will establish e.g. that the functional outputting the
\ell
th largest value among a number of linear functionals
comes with a genuinely optimal estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which is the supremum of infima of affine functionals.
This result is based an a (possibly novel) refinement of the Hahn–Banach dominated extension theorem, see Lemma
2
.

Here is a brief outline of the organization of the article:
theoretical results such as Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
are proved right below (Section
2
),
before computational considerations are addressed (Section
3
),
first at an abstract level and then specialized to two particular examples.

2
Optimal Estimation of Some Nonlinear Fuctionals

This section starts by uncovering the ‘simple’ form of an optimal functional for the estimation of a nonlinear functional
\gamma
which appears as the supremum of linear functionals.
It continues by enlarging the result to include
\gamma
’s that are a mixed supremum-infimum of linear functionals.
In the process,
a seemingly novel refinement of Hahn–Banach dominated extension theorem is established.
The section ends with a discussion on the handling of possible errors in the observation procedure.

2.1
The supremum of linear functionals

In the result presented below,
valid in an arbitrary normed space
F
,
the nonlinear functional
\gamma
to be optimally estimated is the supremum of linear functionals indexed by a set
I
.
Since the latter need not be finite,
it transpires that any norm
\sslash f\sslash=\max\{\eta(f):\eta\in F^{*}\mbox{ with }\sslash\eta\sslash_{*}=1\}
can be optimally estimated by way of a functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which,
as the supremum of affine functionals, is a convex functional.
Of course, an analog result could be obtained if
\gamma
was the infimum of linear functionals
by simply applying Theorem
1
to
-\gamma
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that the model set
\mathcal{K}
is convex and contains the origin and that the functional
\gamma:F\to\mathbb{R}
is sup-linear,
i.e., of the form

\gamma(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f),\qquad f\in F,

where the
\gamma_{i}\in F^{*}
are linear functionals.
Then there exists an optimal estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which is also sup-affine,
i.e., of the form

\delta^{\rm opti}(y)=\sup_{i\in I}\bigg(c^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i)}_{k}y_{k}\bigg),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Proof.

As a first step, the previously mentioned lower bound is rewritten as

e_{\flat}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}\\
\Lambda(f^{\prime})=\Lambda(f^{\prime\prime})\end{subarray}}\frac{\gamma(f^{\prime})-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})}{2}=\sup_{\bar{f}\in\bar{\mathcal{K}}\cap\ker(\bar{\Lambda})}\frac{\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}(\bar{f})-\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})}{2},

where one has introduced the set
\bar{\mathcal{K}}:=\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{K}\subseteq\bar{F}:=F\times F
,
as well as the linear map
\bar{\Lambda}:\bar{F}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the (nonlinear) functionals
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime},\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}:\bar{F}\to\mathbb{R}
defined via

\bar{\Lambda}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\Lambda(f^{\prime})-\Lambda(f^{\prime\prime}),\qquad\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma(f^{\prime}),\qquad\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma(f^{\prime\prime}).

With
\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}
denoting the Minkowski functional of
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
—which is a sublinear (i.e., positively homogeneous and convex) functional
by virtue of the fact that
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
is convex and contains the origin—one has

\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}(\bar{f})-\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})
whenever
\bar{f}\in\ker(\bar{\Lambda})
.
Also introducing linear functionals
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i},\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{i}:\bar{F}\to\mathbb{R}
defined for all
i\in I
via

\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime})

and
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{i}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime})
,
so that
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}(\bar{f})=\sup_{i\in I}\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i}(\bar{f})
,
the previous inequality ensures that, for all
i_{\star}\in I
,
one has

\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})+\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})

whenever
\bar{f}\in\ker(\bar{\Lambda})
.
Fixing
i_{\star}
and noticing that
2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}+\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}
is a sublinear functional,
the Hahn–Banach dominated extension theorem
(see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 4, p. 49]
)
applies to guarantee the existence of a linear functional
\mu_{i_{\star}}\in\bar{F}^{*}
such that

{\mu_{i_{\star}}}_{|\ker(\bar{\Lambda})}={\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}}{}_{|\ker(\bar{\Lambda})}

and
\mu_{i_{\star}}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})+\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})
for all
\bar{f}\in\bar{F}
.
In view of the equivalence between the fact that
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}-\mu_{i_{\star}}
vanishes on
\ker(\bar{\Lambda})
and the existence of
c^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}-\mu_{i_{\star}}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\bar{\lambda}_{k}
, one derives that

\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}(\bar{f})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})+\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})\qquad\mbox{for all }\bar{f}\in\bar{F}.

In other words,
for any
i_{\star}\in I
, it holds that

\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f^{\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\big(\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime})-\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\big)\leq 2e_{\flat}\max\{\rho_{\mathcal{K}}(f^{\prime}),\rho_{\mathcal{K}}(f^{\prime\prime})\}+\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})\qquad\mbox{for all }f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in F,

which immediately yields the key observation that

(1)

\bigg(\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f^{\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime})\bigg)-\bigg(\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg)\leq 2e_{\flat}\qquad\mbox{for all }f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}.

At this point, for each
i_{\star}\in I
, one defines

(2)

c^{(i_{\star})}_{0}=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(\gamma(f)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)+e_{\flat}\bigg).

This definition readily implies that,
for each
i_{\star}\in I
,
one has

\gamma(f)-\Big(c^{(i_{\star})}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\Big)\geq-e_{\flat}
for all
f\in\mathcal{K}
.
One can now take the supremum over
i_{\star}\in I
to arrive at

(3)

\gamma(f)-\sup_{i\in I}\bigg(c^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\geq-e_{\flat}\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}.

Next,
for each
i_{\star}\in I
,
one selects
f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}
such that
\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})+e_{\flat}
is equal (or is arbitrarily close) to
c^{(i_{\star})}_{0}
.
Writing (
1
) for this
f^{\prime\prime}
and for
f^{\prime}
being an arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{K}
leads to

\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f)\leq e_{\flat}+\Big(c^{(i_{\star})}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\Big)
,
and hence

\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f)\leq e_{\flat}+\sup_{i\in I}\Big(c^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\Big)
.
Taking the supremum over
i_{\star}\in I
yields

(4)

\gamma(f)-\sup_{i\in I}\bigg(c^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\leq e_{\flat}\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}.

Now defining the estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\delta^{\rm opti}(y)=\sup_{i\in I}\Big(c^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i)}_{k}y_{k}\Big)
,
combining inequalities (
3
) and (
4
) show that

e(\delta^{\rm opti})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\big|\gamma(f)-\delta^{\rm opti}(\Lambda(f))\big|\leq e_{\flat}.

This proves the genuine optimality of the sup-affine functional
\delta^{\rm opti}
.
∎

2.2
The mixed supremum-infimum of linear functionals

It is now acquired that there is a ‘simple’ optimal algorithm for the estimation of the (pointwise) largest value of linear functionals
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}
, say.
Is there also a ‘simple’ optimal algorithm for the estimation of the (pointwise)
\ell
th largest value of these linear functionals?
The answer is yes, as established in Theorem
3
below.
‘Simple’ in this case means the mixed sup-inf of affine functionals.
This mirrors the sup-inf/inf-sup nature of the functional
\gamma^{\downarrow}_{\ell}
associating to
f\in F

the
\ell
th largest value of
\gamma_{1}(f),\ldots,\gamma_{d}(f)
,
namely

\gamma^{\downarrow}_{\ell}(f)=\sup_{|I|=\ell}\inf_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f)=\inf_{|J|=d+1-\ell}\sup_{j\in J}\gamma_{j}(f).

The essential feature is to have both a sup-inf and an inf-sup representation of the nonlinear functional at hand,
as e.g. in Courant–Fischer characterization of the
\ell
th largest eigenvalue.
Thus, the result will also apply to the difference of suprema of linear functionals,
since

\sup_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(f)-\sup_{j\in J}\nu_{j}(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\inf_{j\in J}(\mu_{i}-\nu_{j})(f)=\inf_{j\in J}\sup_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}-\nu_{j})(f).

A crucial part of the argument will be a refinement of Hahn–Banach dominated extension theorem.
It goes in a different direction than other refinements that the author encountered before,
such as
[
2
, Theorem 11, p. 53]
.
Essentially,
it says that the infimum of linear functionals
dominated by a sublinear functional on a subspace can be extended while maintaining the domination
in a favorable way (more favorable, of course, than extending each linear functional individually).
While a fully general statement would require Zorn lemma,
it is enough here to establish a restricted version,
which is exactly what is needed later.

Lemma 2
.

Let
V
be a vector space
and let
U
be a finite codimensional subspace of
V
given as
U=\ker(\eta_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\ker(\eta_{m})
for some linear functionals
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m}\in V^{*}
.
If
\mu_{i}\in V^{*}
,
i\in I
,
are linear functionals on
V
and if
\rho
is a sublinear functional on
V
such that

\inf_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(u)\leq\rho(u)\qquad\mbox{for all }u\in U,

then there exist scalars
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\inf_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(v)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\eta_{k}(v)\leq\rho(v)\qquad\mbox{for all }v\in V.

Proof.

By immediate induction,
it suffices to establish the result when
m=1
,
i.e., when
U=\ker(\eta)
for a single linear functional
\eta\in V^{*}
.
The proof follows very classical arguments,
with the additional usage of
\inf_{i}x_{i}+\inf_{i}y_{i}\leq\inf_{i}(x_{i}+y_{i})
.
Let
w\in V
be such that
\eta(w)=1
.
Since, for any
u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\in\ker(\eta)
,

\displaystyle\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime})+\mu_{i}(w))+\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime\prime})-\mu_{i}(w))

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime})+\mu_{i}(u^{\prime\prime}))=\inf_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(u^{\prime}+u^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\rho(u^{\prime}+u^{\prime\prime})\leq\rho(u^{\prime}+w)+\rho(u^{\prime\prime}-w),

we can find a scalar
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that,
for any
u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\in\ker(\eta)
,

\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime\prime})-\mu_{i}(w))-\rho(u^{\prime\prime}-w)\leq c\leq\rho(u^{\prime}+w)-\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime})+\mu_{i}(w)).

Then, for any
v\in V\setminus\ker(\eta)
written as
v=u+tw
with
u\in\ker(\eta)
and
t\not=0
,

•

if
t>0
, then
v=t(u^{\prime}+w)
with
u^{\prime}\in\ker(\eta)
, and

\inf_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(v)+c\,\eta(v)=t\Big(\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime})+\mu_{i}(w))+c\Big)\leq t\big(\rho(u^{\prime}+w)\big)=\rho(v);

•

if
t<0
, then
v=-t(u^{\prime\prime}-w)
with
u^{\prime\prime}\in\ker(\eta)
, and

\inf_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(v)+c\,\eta(v)=-t\Big(\inf_{i\in I}(\mu_{i}(u^{\prime\prime})-\mu_{i}(w))-c\Big)\leq-t\big(\rho(u^{\prime\prime}-w)\big)=\rho(v).

These two cases show that
\inf_{i\in I}\mu_{i}(v)+c\,\eta(v)\leq\rho(v)
holds for all
v\in V
, as required.
∎

With this technical lemma at hand,
the main result of the subsection can now be stated and proved.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that the model set
\mathcal{K}
is convex and contains the origin and that the functional
\gamma:F\to\mathbb{R}
can be expressed as

\gamma(f)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{i\in I_{a}}\gamma_{i}(f)=\inf_{b\in B}\sup_{j\in J_{b}}\gamma_{j}(f),\qquad f\in F,

where the
\gamma_{i}\in F^{*}
are linear functionals.
Then there exists an optimal estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
which has the form

\delta^{\rm opti}(y)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a,b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a,b)}_{k}y_{k}\bigg),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Proof.

One starts from the lower bound
e_{\flat}
expressed once again as

e_{\flat}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}\\
\Lambda(f^{\prime})=\Lambda(f^{\prime\prime})\end{subarray}}\frac{\gamma(f^{\prime})-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})}{2}=\sup_{\bar{f}\in\bar{\mathcal{K}}\cap\ker(\bar{\Lambda})}\frac{\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}(\bar{f})-\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})}{2},

where it is recalled that the ‘bar’-notation involves

\bar{\mathcal{K}}:=\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{K}\subseteq\bar{F}:=F\times F
, which has Minkowski functional
\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}
,
and
\bar{\Lambda}:\bar{F}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\bar{\gamma}^{\prime},\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime},\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i},\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{i}:\bar{F}\to\mathbb{R}
, which are defined via

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\Lambda(f^{\prime})-\Lambda(f^{\prime\prime}),

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma(f^{\prime}),\quad\qquad\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma(f^{\prime\prime}),\;

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime}),\quad\quad\;\;\;\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}_{i}([f^{\prime};f^{\prime\prime}])=\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime}).

In this way, one has

\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})+\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})

whenever

\bar{f}\in\ker(\bar{\Lambda})
.
Therefore, for all
a_{\star}\in A

and all
b_{\star}\in B
,

\inf_{i\in I_{a_{\star}}}\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\prime}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})+\sup_{j\in J_{b_{\star}}}\bar{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})\qquad\mbox{whenever }\bar{f}\in\ker(\bar{\Lambda}).

This is the time to invoke Lemma
2
, justifying the existence of
c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\inf_{i\in I_{a_{\star}}}\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\prime}(\bar{f})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\bar{\lambda}_{k}(\bar{f})\leq 2e_{\flat}\rho_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(\bar{f})+\sup_{j\in J_{b_{\star}}}\bar{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{f})\qquad\mbox{for all }\bar{f}\in\bar{F}.

It follows that,
for any
a_{\star}\in A
and
b_{\star}\in B
,
one has

(5)

\bigg(\inf_{i\in I_{a_{\star}}}\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime})\bigg)-\bigg(\sup_{j\in J_{b_{\star}}}\gamma_{j}(f^{\prime\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg)\leq 2e_{\flat}\;\;\,\mbox{for all }f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}.

At this point, for each
a_{\star}\in A
and
b_{\star}\in B
, one defines

c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{0}:=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(\sup_{j\in J_{b_{\star}}}\gamma_{j}(f)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)+e_{\flat}\bigg).

For each
a_{\star}\in A
and
b_{\star}\in B
,
one immediately obtains that,
for all
f\in\mathcal{K}
,

\sup_{j\in J_{b_{\star}}}\gamma_{j}(f)\geq-e_{\flat}+\bigg(c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\geq-e_{\flat}+\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a_{\star},b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg).

Now, taking the infimum over
b_{\star}\in B
on the left-hand side yields

\gamma(f)\geq-e_{\flat}+\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a_{\star},b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K},

and finally taking the supremum over
a_{\star}\in A
on the right-hand side and rearranging gives

(6)

\gamma(f)-\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a,b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a,b)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\geq-e_{\flat}\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}.

Next,
for each
a_{\star}\in A
and
b_{\star}\in B
,
one selects
f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}

achieving (or coming arbitrarily close to) the infimum defining
c_{0}^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}
,
so writing (
5
)
for this
f^{\prime\prime}
and for
f^{\prime}
being an arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{K}
leads to

\inf_{i\in I_{a_{\star}}}\gamma_{i}(f)-\bigg(c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\leq e_{\flat},

from where it follows,
by taking the infimum over
b_{\star}\in B
, that

\inf_{i\in I_{a_{\star}}}\gamma_{i}(f)\leq e_{\flat}+\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a_{\star},b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\leq e_{\flat}+\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a,b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a,b)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg).

Finally, taking the supremum over
a_{\star}\in A
leads to

(7)

\gamma(f)-\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\bigg(c^{(a,b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a,b)}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f)\bigg)\leq e_{\flat}\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}.

Defining the estimation functional

\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\delta^{\rm opti}(y)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\big(c^{(a,b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a,b)}_{k}y_{k}\big)

and combining inequalities (
6
) and (
7
) show that

e(\delta^{\rm opti})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\big|\gamma(f)-\delta^{\rm opti}(\Lambda(f))\big|\leq e_{\flat}.

This proves the optimality of the sup-inf-affine functional
\delta^{\rm opti}
.
∎

2.3
Handling observation error

In realistic situations,
the observations made on the unknown object
f
are not exact.
They typically take the form
\lambda_{k}(f)+e_{k}
for some unknown
e\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
This vector can be modeled stochastically,
as done in
[
3
]
but not considered here,
or deterministically via an assumption
e\in\mathcal{E}
, as considered here, where
\mathcal{E}
is convex set containing the origin.
It is folklore to remark that this ‘inaccurate scenario’ reduces to the ‘accurate scenario’
by focusing on the compound object
(f,e)

belonging to the model set
\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{E}

and acquired via the linear observations
\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}((f,e))=\lambda_{k}(f)+e_{k}
.
For instance,
since a sup-linear functional
\gamma
acting on
f
is also a sup-linear functional acting on
(f,e)
,
Theorem
1
will still guarantee that there exists an optimal functional for the estimation of
\gamma
which is sup-affine.
The divergence with the ‘accurate scenario’ will essentially occur in the construction of this optimal sup-affine functional.
Precisely,
as will become apparent in the next section,
the support function of the model set will play a central role.
For the set
\mathcal{K}
,
it is defined on linear functionals
\eta\in F^{*}
by

{|\!|\!|}{\eta}{|\!|\!|}_{\mathcal{K}}:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\eta(f).

In the ‘inaccurate scenario’,
this will have to be replaced by the support function of
\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{E}
at an associated linear functional
\widetilde{\eta}
defined on
F\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
As an example,
if
c\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a coefficient vector and
\mathcal{E}=B_{p}^{m}
is a ball in
\ell_{p}
-space for some
p\in[1,\infty]
with conjugate exponent
p^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
,
one easily observes that

{\Big|\!\Big|\!\Big|}{\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}}{\Big|\!\Big|\!\Big|}_{\mathcal{K}\times B_{p}^{m}}=\sup_{(f,e)\in\mathcal{K}\times B_{p}^{m}}\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\big(\lambda_{k}(f)+e_{k}\big)={\Big|\!\Big|\!\Big|}{\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\lambda_{k}}{\Big|\!\Big|\!\Big|}_{\mathcal{K}}+\|c\|_{p^{\prime}}.

This observation can be used
in Propositions
5
and
6

to deal with the ‘inaccurate scenario’
for the construction of the optimal algorithms
presented next.

Then, for the sake of implementation in a reproducible

,

3.1
Generic convex optimization programs

In most of this subsection and throughout the next two,
the nonlinear functional
\gamma:F\to\mathbb{R}
denotes a sup-linear functional,
i.e., it has the form
\gamma(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f)
with the
\gamma_{i}
’s being linear functionals.
It was established in Theorem
1
that the functionals
\delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
minimizing the estimation error
e(\delta)=\sup\{|\gamma(f)-\delta(\Lambda(f))|:\,f\in\mathcal{K}\}
can be sought among sup-affine functionals.
As a preliminary,
for a fixed such
\delta
,
one highlights how
e(\delta)
can be computed by solving a convex optimization program.
The crucial point is that
\nu:=\delta\circ\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}
is itself a sup-affine functional in this case.
The statement below features the standard simplex associated with the index set
I
,
defined when
I
is finite by

\mathcal{S}^{I}:=\bigg\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{I}:\sigma_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ for all }i\in I\mbox{ and }\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}=1\bigg\}.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a convex set.
For a sup-linear functional
\gamma:F\to\mathbb{R}

and a sup-affine functional
\nu:F\to\mathbb{R}
given, for
f\in F
, by

\gamma(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nu(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\big(\nu_{i}(f)+b_{i}\big),

where the
\gamma_{i},\nu_{i}\in F^{*}
are linear functionals and the
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are scalars,
one has

(8)

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}|\gamma(f)-\nu(f)|=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{R}\\
\sigma^{(i_{\star})},\tau^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}\end{subarray}}\;e\quad\mbox{s.to }\begin{cases}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}{\gamma_{i_{\star}}-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}\nu_{i}}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}b_{i},&i_{\star}\in I,\\
{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}{\nu_{i_{\star}}-\sum_{i\in I}\tau^{(i_{\star})}_{i}\gamma_{i}}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e-b_{i_{\star}},&i_{\star}\in I.\end{cases}

Proof.

The supremum appearing on the left-hand side of (
8
)
is nothing else than the infimum value of
e
subject to
|\gamma(f)-\nu(f)|\leq e
for all
f\in\mathcal{K}
.
The latter decouples as the two constraints

(9)

\gamma(f)-\nu(f)\leq e\quad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\nu(f)-\gamma(f)\leq e\quad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}.

In view of
\gamma(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f)

and of

-\nu(f)=-\sup_{i\in I}\big(\nu_{i}(f)+b_{i}\big)=\inf_{i\in I}\big(-\nu_{i}(f)-b_{i}\big)=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\big(-\nu_{i}(f)-b_{i}\big),

the first constraint in (
9
) is equivalent to a set of constraints indexed by
i_{\star}\in I
, namely

\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f)+\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\big(-\nu_{i}(f)-b_{i}\big)\leq e\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\mathcal{K}.

Each of these individual constraints reads

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\Big(\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f)+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\big(-\nu_{i}(f)-b_{i}\big)\Big)\leq e.

Since the inner expression depends affinely of
f\in\mathcal{K}
,
which is a convex set,
and also affinely on
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}
,
which is a convex and compact set,
von Neumann minimax theorem legitimizes the exchange of sup and inf,
so that the above constraint reads

\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\Big(\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f)+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\big(-\nu_{i}(f)-b_{i}\big)\Big)\leq e.

All in all, the first constraint in (
9
) is equivalent to the existence of
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}
,
i_{\star}\in I
, such that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\Big(\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f)-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}\nu_{i}(f)\Big)\leq e+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}b_{i},

which is tautologically equivalent to the first set of convex constraints indexed by
i_{\star}
appearing in (
8
).
Likewise,
with very similar details left to the reader,
the second constraint in (
9
) is equivalent to the existence of
\tau^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}
,
i_{\star}\in I
, obeying
the second set of convex constraints indexed by
i_{\star}
appearing in (
8
).
Incorporating all the
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}
and
\tau^{(i_{\star})}

as optimization variables,
one arrives at the announced convex optimization program.
∎

Turning to the minimization of the estimation error
e(\delta)
over all possible functionals
\delta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
or in fact only over all functionals of the form

\delta_{c}(y)=\sup_{i\in I}\big(c^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i)}_{k}y_{k}\big)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
one notices that this error becomes

e(\delta_{c})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}|\gamma(f)-\nu_{c}(f)|,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\nu_{c}:=\delta_{c}\circ\Lambda:F\to\mathbb{R}\;\mbox{ being a sup-affine functional}.

At a fixed
c\in(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})^{I}
,
Proposition
4
just indicated how to compute
e(\delta_{c})
by solving a convex optimization program.
Regrettably,
a straightforward minimization over
c
as well seems out of reach,
due to the presence of products
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}c^{(i)}_{k}
of optimization variables generated through the first set of constraints.
An alternative route,
pursued below,
consists in translating the constructive argument of Theorem
1
into a manageable optimization program.

Proposition 5
.

Given a convex set
\mathcal{K}
containing the origin,
an optimal functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
for the estimation of a sup-linear functional
\gamma(f)=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f)
,
f\in F
,
is obtained as the sup-affine functional
\delta^{\rm opti}(y)=\sup_{i\in I}\big(\widehat{c}^{(i)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widehat{c}^{(i)}_{k}y_{k}\big)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
where the ‘hat’-notation denotes solutions to the convex optimization program

(10)

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{R},e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}\\
c^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}\end{subarray}}{\rm minimize}\quad e\qquad\mbox{s.to }\begin{cases}e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}+e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}\leq 2e,&i_{\star}\in I,\\
{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}{\gamma_{i_{\star}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}},&i_{\star}\in I,\\
{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}{-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}\gamma_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}},&i_{\star}\in I.\end{cases}

Subsequently,
the remaining coefficients
\widehat{c}^{(i)}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
are obtained as
\widehat{c}^{(i_{\star})}_{0}=\widehat{e}-\widehat{e}^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}
for all
i_{\star}\in I
.

Proof.

Keeping the proof of Theorem
1
in mind,
recall that the key resided in (
1
) being satisfied for all
i_{\star}\in I
,
i.e.,

\sup_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f^{\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime})\bigg)+\sup_{f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg)\leq 2e

for some
c^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and for
e\in\mathbb{R}
as small as possible,
i.e.,
e=e_{\flat}
.
Thus, introducing slack variables
e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}},e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}\in\mathbb{R}
bounding the above suprema,
the task consists in minimizing
e
subject to
e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}+e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}\leq 2e
,
to
\sup_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\big(\gamma_{i_{\star}}(f^{\prime})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime})\big)\leq e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}
,
and to
\sup_{f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\big(-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\big)\leq e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}
for all
i_{\star}\in I
.
The first of these constraints are exactly the first constraints in (
10
),
while the second ones reduce to the second constraints in (
10
) simply by the definition of the support function.
Considering now the third and last constraints, in view of

-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})=-\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime})=\inf_{i\in I}(-\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime}))=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}(-\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime})),

one obtains

\displaystyle\sup_{f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sup_{f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\bigg(-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{I}}\;\sup_{f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}\gamma_{i}(f^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg),

where the exchange of sup and inf relied on
von Neumann minimax theorem,
in the same way as for the proof of Proposition
4
.
As a result, the constraints can be rephrased as the existence of
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}
such that

{\Big|\!\Big|\!\Big|}{-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}\gamma_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}}{\Big|\!\Big|\!\Big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}.

Incorporating all the
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}
as optimization variables,
one arrives at the third constraints in (
10
).
All in all,
it has been justified that the convex optimization program (
10
) outputs
e_{\flat}

(i.e., the minimal error
e(\delta)
over all possible estimation functionals
\delta
) as
\widehat{e}

and the coefficient vectors making the linear part of
\delta^{\rm opti}
as the
\widehat{c}^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Finally,
the announced expression for the remaining coefficients
\widehat{c}^{(i_{\star})}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
follows
from their very definition (
2
), written as

\widehat{c}^{(i_{\star})}_{0}=-\sup_{f^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\bigg(-\gamma(f^{\prime\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widehat{c}^{(i_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}(f^{\prime\prime})\bigg)+e_{\flat},

and from the identification of the above supremum as
\widehat{e}^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}
and of
e_{\flat}
as
\widehat{e}
.
∎

When estimating a mixed sup-inf-linear functional,
it is as also possible to translate the proof of Theorem
3

for the construction of the optimal sup-inf-affine functional.
The key resides again in fulfilling the inequalities (
5

which are treated as above.
More precisely, one proceeds in exactly the same way (save for the notation)
to recast the bound on the second summand in the left-hand side of of (

and the argument will also apply when recasting a bound on the fist summand by a slack variable

With details left out,
one arrives at the convex optimization program displayed below.

Proposition 6
.

Under the setting of Theorem
3
,
the optimal estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}

has the sup-inf-affine form
\delta^{\rm opti}(y)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\big(\widehat{c}^{(a,b)}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widehat{c}^{(a,b)}_{k}y_{k}\big)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
where the ‘hat’-notation denotes solutions to the convex optimization program

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{R},e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{A\times B}\\
c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
\sigma^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I_{a_{\star}}}\\
\tau^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I_{b_{\star}}}\end{subarray}}{\rm minimize}\quad e\qquad\mbox{s.to }\begin{cases}e^{\prime}_{a_{\star},b_{\star}}+e^{\prime\prime}_{a_{\star},b_{\star}}\leq 2e,&a_{\star}\in A,b_{\star}\in B,\\
{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}{\sum_{i\in I_{a_{\star}}}\sigma^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{i}\gamma_{i}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e^{\prime}_{a_{\star},b_{\star}},&a_{\star}\in A,b_{\star}\in B,\\
{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}{-\sum_{j\in J_{b_{\star}}}\tau^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{j}\gamma_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{k}\lambda_{k}}{\big|\!\big|\!\big|}_{\mathcal{K}}\leq e^{\prime\prime}_{a_{\star},b_{\star}},&a_{\star}\in A,b_{\star}\in B.\end{cases}

Subsequently,
the coefficients
\widehat{c}^{(a,b)}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
are obtained as
\widehat{c}^{(a_{\star},b_{\star})}_{0}=\widehat{e}-\widehat{e}^{\prime\prime}_{a_{\star},b_{\star}}
for all
a_{\star}\in A,b_{\star}\in B
.

Arguably, Propositions
5

and
6

are still abstract statements.
Whether they really translate into practical constructions depends on the specific model set
\mathcal{K}
and its amenability to numerical computations.
Concerning Proposition
6
,
there is the added issue of the number of constraints,
which is
|A|\times|B|
.
In the example of the
\ell
th largest value among
d
linear functionals,
one has

|A|=|\{I\in[1:d]:|I|=\ell\}|=\binom{d}{\ell}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|B|=|\{J\in[1:d]:|J|=d+1-\ell\}|=\binom{d}{\ell-1},

so
|A|\times|B|
behaves exponentially in
d
unless
\ell
or
d-\ell
is a small constant.
For this reason, one concentrates only on the estimation of the maximum of linear functionals when
expounding on the two relevant examples presented in the next subsections.

3.2
Polytopal models in
\ell_{\infty}
-spaces

Working in
F=\ell_{\infty}^{N}
,
the convex model set containing the origin (but not necessarily symmetric) considered here is the polytope

\mathcal{K}^{\rm poly}:=\big\{f\in\ell_{\infty}^{N}:\langle a_{\ell},f\rangle\leq 1\mbox{ for all }\ell\in L\big\}

for prescribed
a_{\ell}\in\ell_{1}^{N}
.
Using duality in linear programming,
it is routine to verify that the support function of
\mathcal{K}^{\rm poly}
, evaluated at a linear functional
\eta
defined for
f\in\ell_{\infty}^{N}
by
\eta(f)=\langle g,f\rangle
,
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{|\!|\!|}{\eta}{|\!|\!|}_{\mathcal{K}^{\rm poly}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\langle g,f\rangle\quad\mbox{s.to }\langle a_{\ell},f\rangle\leq 1\mbox{ for all }\ell\in L

\displaystyle=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{L}}\sum_{\ell\in L}s_{\ell}\quad\mbox{s.to }s\geq 0\mbox{ and }\sum_{\ell\in L}s_{\ell}a_{\ell}=g.

Therefore,
an inequality constraint
{|\!|\!|}{\eta}{|\!|\!|}_{\mathcal{K}^{\rm poly}}\leq\kappa
found as a constraint in an optimization program can be rephrased as the existence of some
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}
such that
\sum_{\ell\in L}s_{\ell}\leq\kappa
and
\sum_{\ell\in L}s_{\ell}a_{\ell}=g
,
and subsequently
s
can be incorporated in the optimization variables.
For instance,
with
u_{k}\in\ell_{1}^{N}
representing the linear functional
\lambda_{k}
and

w_{i}\in\ell_{1}^{N}
representing the linear functional
\gamma_{i}
, so that

\lambda_{k}(f)=\langle u_{k},f\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma_{i}(f)=\langle w_{i},f\rangle\qquad\mbox{for all }f\in\ell_{\infty}^{N},

the convex optimization program from Proposition
5
becomes:

Linear program for the optimal estimation of
\gamma=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}
with model
\mathcal{K}^{\rm poly}
.

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{R},e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}\\
c^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
\sigma^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}\\
s^{(i_{\star})},t^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}\end{subarray}}{\rm minimize}\quad e\qquad\mbox{s.to }\begin{cases}e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}}+e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}}\leq 2e,\;\sum_{\ell\in L}s^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}\leq e^{\prime}_{i_{\star}},\;\sum_{\ell\in L}t^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}\leq e^{\prime\prime}_{i_{\star}},&i_{\star}\in I,\\
\sum_{\ell\in L}s^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=w_{i_{\star}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}u_{k},&i_{\star}\in I,\\
\sum_{\ell\in L}t^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=-\sum_{i}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}w_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(i_{\star})}_{k}u_{k},&i_{\star}\in I.\end{cases}

After solving this linear program,
one can construct an optimal estimation functional
\delta^{\rm opti}
which has the generic sup-affine form

(11)

\delta_{b,z}(y)=\sup_{i\in I}\big(b_{i}+\langle z_{i},y\rangle\big),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

For good measure,
one can verify that the minimal value of
e
found above agrees with
e(\delta^{\rm opti})

when the latter is computed by solving the convex program from Proposition
4
.
In the present case, it becomes:

Linear program to compute
e(\delta_{b,z})
for a fixed sup-affine functional with model
\mathcal{K}^{\rm poly}
.

(12)

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{R}\\
\sigma^{(i_{\star})},\tau^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathcal{S}^{I}\\
s^{(i_{\star})},t^{(i_{\star})}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}\end{subarray}}{\rm minimize}\quad e\qquad\mbox{s.to }\begin{cases}\sum_{\ell\in L}s^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}\leq e+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}b_{i},\;\sum_{\ell\in L}t^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}\leq e-b_{i_{\star}},&i_{\star}\in I,\\
\sum_{\ell\in L}s^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=w_{i_{\star}}-\sum_{i\in I}\sigma^{(i_{\star})}_{i}\Lambda^{*}z_{i},&i_{\star}\in I,\\
\sum_{\ell\in L}t^{(i_{\star})}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=\Lambda^{*}z_{i_{\star}}-\sum_{i\in I}\tau^{(i_{\star})}_{i}w_{i},&i_{\star}\in I.\end{cases}

Remark
.

In
F=\ell_{\infty}^{N}
and with a convex model,
it is somewhat known that there exists an affine map
\Delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\ell_{\infty}^{N}

which is genuinely optimal for the estimation of
\Gamma=\mathrm{Id}_{F}
,
i.e., for the full approximation problem.
This is essentially Sukharev’s result applied componentwise.
Leaving out the details, this optimal map has the form

\Delta^{\rm opti}:y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mapsto\bigg[\widehat{c}^{(0)}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}y_{k}\widehat{c}^{(k)}\bigg]\in\ell_{\infty}^{N},

where the vectors
\widehat{c}^{(0)},\widehat{c}^{(1)},\ldots,\widehat{c}^{(m)}\in\ell_{\infty}^{N}
are solutions to the following linear program
(involving the standard basis
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})
for
\mathbb{R}^{N}
):

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{R}\\
c^{(0)},c^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
s^{(j)},t^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}\end{subarray}}{\rm minimize}\quad e\qquad\mbox{s.to }\begin{cases}\sum_{\ell\in L}s^{(j)}_{\ell}\leq e+c^{(0)}_{j},\;\sum_{\ell\in L}t^{(j)}_{\ell}\leq e-c^{(0)}_{j},&j\in[1:N],\\
\sum_{\ell\in L}s^{(j)}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=v_{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(k)}_{j}u_{k},&j\in[1:N],\\
\sum_{\ell\in L}t^{(j)}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=-v_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c^{(k)}_{j}u_{k},&j\in[1:N].\end{cases}

As an initially guess, a natural candidate for the estimation of
\gamma=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}
could have been the ‘plug-in’ functional
\delta^{\rm plug}=\gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm opti}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Since it has the generic sup-affine form (
11
) with
b_{i}=\gamma_{i}(\widehat{c}^{(0)})
and
(z_{i})_{k}=\gamma_{i}(\widehat{c}^{(k)})
,
the estimation error
e(\delta^{\rm plug})
of this ‘plug-in’ functional can be computed by solving the linear program (
12
).
Numerical experiments available in the reproducible file confirm that this guess is not optimal, i.e., that
e(\delta^{\rm plug})
is larger than
e(\delta^{\rm opti})
.

3.3

Approximability models in Hilbert spaces

Working now in a Hilbert space
F=H
,
the convex model set containing the origin (but not necessarily symmetric)
considered here is the set of elements that are approximated with prescribed accuracy
\varepsilon>0
by an affine subspace
g+V
,
where
V
is an
n
-dimensional linear subspace of
H

and
g\in H\setminus V
is a fixed element satisfying
{\rm dist}(g,V)<\varepsilon
.
More precisely, one considers

\mathcal{K}^{\rm appr}:=\{f\in H:{\rm dist}(f,g+V)\leq\varepsilon\}=\{f\in H:\|P_{V^{\perp}}(f-g)\|\leq\varepsilon\},

where
P_{V^{\perp}}
denotes the orthogonal projector onto the orthogonal complement of
V
.
Motivated by Optimal Recovery questions,
such approximability sets (with
g=0
)
were introduced in
[
1
]
.
Based on an observation made (with
g=0
) in
[
6
, Subsection 3.3]
,
their support function evaluated at a linear functional
\eta
can be expressed as

{|\!|\!|}{\eta}{|\!|\!|}_{\mathcal{K}^{\rm appr}}=\eta(g)+\begin{cases}\varepsilon\|\eta\|_{*}&\mbox{if }\eta_{|V}=0,\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Consequently,
still using the notation

u_{k}\in H
for the Riesz representer of the linear functional
\lambda_{k}

and
w_{i}\in H
for the Riesz representers of the linear functional
\gamma_{i}
—in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with kernel
{\sf k}
,
note that
w_{i}={\sf k}(\cdot,x^{(i)})

if
\gamma_{i}
is the evaluation at a point
x^{(i)}
—the convex optimization program from Proposition
5
becomes:

\gamma=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}

.

.

In

and with the model set

,
there also exists an affine map

which is genuinely optimal for the estimation of
\Gamma=\mathrm{Id}_{F}
.
Specifically,
it was established in
[
1
]
that,

.
It is also affine,

C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

\gamma=\sup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}

\delta^{\rm plug}=\gamma\circ\Delta^{\rm opt}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Numerical experiments available in the reproducible file hold a surprise:

In Hilbert spaces,

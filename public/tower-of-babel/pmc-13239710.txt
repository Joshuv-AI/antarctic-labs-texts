Adverse events in exercise interventions among older adults with frailty: a study protocol of systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Satoshi Kurita, Ri Suo, Kazushi Maruo, Junji Nishimoto, Takehiko Tsujimoto, Hiroyuki Sasai, Ryosuke Shigematsu, Yoshio Nakata, Yosuke Osuka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAlthough several systematic reviews and meta-analyses have demonstrated the benefits of exercise interventions in older adults with frailty, the potential harm associated with these interventions has not been systematically synthesised. This systematic review aims to examine the adverse events reported in exercise intervention trials involving older adults with frailty and to compare the risk of adverse events between the intervention and control groups.Methods and analysisSearches will be performed in four electronic databases (PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science and SPORTDiscus) for published trials. Eligible studies will be randomised controlled trials of exercise interventions, including older adults with frailty aged ≥60 years, with frailty identified using a validated method. Five reviewers and three referees, all with expertise in exercise interventions, will be assigned to three independent review teams to ensure efficient screening. Reviewers will independently screen titles, abstracts and full texts using Rayyan, and then extract trial and adverse event data into an Excel spreadsheet. The risk of bias in eligible trials will be assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB-2) tool. The referees will resolve any disagreements between the two reviewers throughout the screening, data extraction and risk-of-bias assessment processes. The primary outcome is adverse events, defined as any unfavourable, unintended signs, symptoms or disease that occurred during the study period. An independent biostatistician will perform a random-effects meta-analysis using a generalised linear mixed model with a binomial likelihood and a logit link to estimate the pooled risk ratios (RRs) for adverse events in the intervention group relative to the control group. Publication bias will be evaluated using funnel plots and Egger’s regression test. Depending on the number of available studies, subgroup analyses will be conducted to examine differences in RRs according to the study quality, duration of intervention, exercise frequency, setting and supervision.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval was not required because we did not use specific patient data. The findings of the systematic review and meta-analysis will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentation at appropriate conferences.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251180645.

Introduction

Frailty is a state of vulnerability to impaired resolution of homeostasis after a stressful event and is a consequence of a cumulative decline in many physiological systems during a lifetime.1 2 This increases the risk of adverse health outcomes, including falls, disability, long-term care and death.3 International guidelines recommend physical activity/exercise to manage frailty and prevent deterioration to worse health conditions with strong evidence.46

The International Conference on Frailty and Sarcopenia Research guidelines recommend that older adults with frailty participate in a multicomponent physical activity programme and that health practitioners refer them to physical activity programmes with a progressive resistance-training component.5 However, recent systematic reviews have suggested that adverse events are often under-reported or underestimated in exercise intervention trials.79 Given that exercise itself may act as a physiological stressor in older adults with frailty, postexercise fatigue, inappropriate exercise intensity or insufficient supervision may increase the risk of adverse events. Precisely estimating the risk of adverse events will support the management of the potential harms of exercise in older adults with frailty, an essential topic that remains insufficiently synthesised.

Therefore, this systematic review aimed to examine the adverse events reported in exercise intervention trials involving older adults with frailty and to compare the risk of adverse events between the intervention and control groups.

Methods and analysis

The protocol is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols guidelines (PRISMA-P; see online supplemental table 1).10 11 The study protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (ID: CRD420251180645; registered on 1 November 2025).

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

We will limit the review to English-language publications because English is the primary language of international academic journals that publish high-quality research.

Outcomes

The primary outcome is the total number of adverse events, defined as any unfavourable and unintended sign, symptom or disease12 that occurs during the intervention period, regardless of its causal relationship with the exercise intervention. If multiple adverse events occur in the same participant, that participant is counted once. The effect measure is the risk ratio (RR) comparing the exercise and control groups, calculated as follows:

E1=the number of participants of the intervention group who experienced adverse events.

N1=number of participants allocated to the intervention group before intervention.

E2=the number of participants of the control group who experienced adverse events.

N2=number of participants allocated to the control group before intervention.

We also set secondary outcomes by classifying adverse events as serious or non-serious. Serious adverse events were defined as death, hospitalisation or permanent disability12 during the intervention period. Non-serious events included pain, fatigue, falls or other non-severe symptoms. In addition, adverse events explicitly judged by the study authors to be related to exercise will be summarised descriptively.

Search strategy

A literature search was conducted using PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science and SPORTDiscus. The search strategy for each database was developed in collaboration with an information specialist at Edanz (Fukuoka, Japan) and experts in exercise interventions in the review team with a focus on the aim of the present study. Each database search strategy, including Medical Subject Headings terms and keywords, is summarised in online supplemental tables 2–5. The strategies were created by combining the terms for study design, participants and exercise. As the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) statement in 2001 recommended, for the first time, the reporting of adverse events in randomised trials,13 we set the publication period for articles from 1 January 2001 to 31 October 2025. Searches of all the selected databases were formally conducted in November 2025. The preliminary search results from these databases have been extracted into an Research Information Systems-format file.

Publication screening, data extraction, quality assessment

Five reviewers and three referees, all with expertise in exercise interventions, will be assigned to three independent review teams to ensure efficient publication screening, data extraction and quality assessment.

Publication screening

Titles and abstracts will be independently screened by two reviewers using Rayyan (https://www.rayyan.ai/) to maintain robustness and inter-rater reliability in this study and reduce bias through a process for resolving discrepancies. Disagreements between the two reviewers will be resolved through discussion with the referee. Full-text screening for data extraction will follow the same procedure.

Data extraction from the texts

The required data from the full texts will include study information, participant characteristics, frailty status, intervention-group details, control-group details and outcomes. The details are summarised in table 2. These data will be recorded and saved in an Excel format. When required information is missing from the text, the article authors will be asked to provide data not available in the published article.

Quality assessment

The risk of bias of the studies included in the systematic review and meta-analysis will be assessed using the Cochrane RoB-2 tool.14 Reviewers will use the appropriate version of the RoB-2 for each trial according to different study designs, including blind and open clinical trials. The algorithms are based on responses to relevant questions with five options: yes, probably yes, probably no, no and no information. The risk of bias for each domain will then be rated as low risk, some concerns or high risk. The overall risk of bias in each study will also be rated as low risk (all domains with a low risk of bias), some concerns (at least one domain with some concerns but no domain with a high risk of bias) or high risk (at least one domain with a high risk of bias or multiple domains with some concerns).

Analysis

A random-effects meta-analysis using generalised linear mixed models with a binomial likelihood and logit link will be conducted to pool RRs with 95% CIs for adverse events in the intervention group compared with the control group. Publication bias will be evaluated using funnel plots and Egger’s regression test. Heterogeneity will be assessed using I² statistics and interpreted as ‘might not be important’ (0%–40%), ‘may represent moderate heterogeneity’ (30%–60%), ‘may represent substantial heterogeneity’ (50%–90%), ‘considerable heterogeneity’ (75%–100%), according to Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.15 Depending on the number of available studies, subgroup analyses will examine differences by study quality, duration of intervention (<12 vs ≥12 weeks), frequency (<3 vs ≥3 sessions/week), setting (home vs facility) and supervision (yes vs no). Sensitivity analyses will exclude studies with a high risk of bias. Studies without numerical data on adverse events, or those reporting only the total number of events without data on the number of participants experiencing adverse events, will be excluded from the quantitative synthesis if relevant data cannot be obtained after contacting the study authors. Statistical analyses will be conducted using R (V.4.5.2 or later) and several R packages, such as meta16 and metafor.17 An independent biostatistician will perform all the analyses.

Certainty of evidence

The certainty of evidence for each outcome will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach.18 The certainty of evidence will be evaluated across the following domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. The certainty of evidence will be rated as high, moderate, low or very low. Two reviewers will independently perform the assessments and disagreements will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer.

Discussion

This systematic review examines adverse events in exercise intervention trials among older adults with frailty and evaluates their risk. Although exercise interventions are recommended to improve health, even among older adults with frailty, the CONSORT-Harms guidelines recommend clear and systematic reporting of adverse events to enable adequate safety evaluation of intervention programmes,19 thereby supporting safe clinical decision-making by health practitioners.

Recent systematic reviews have synthesised the adverse events in exercise intervention trials among specific populations, including individuals with multimorbidity8 and hip osteoarthritis.9 This systematic review will extend these findings to older adults with frailty and provide useful evidence for health practitioners. Across these previous reviews, the reported risks of non-serious adverse events have been inconsistent. For example, a comprehensive review of adverse events in exercise therapy trials reported an increased risk (RR 1.19, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.30),7 whereas exercise therapy was associated with a reduced risk of serious adverse events in individuals with multimorbidity (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.49 to 0.78).8 In studies involving patients with hip osteoarthritis, reports on adverse events are inconsistent.9 Therefore, there remains a need to evaluate the risk of adverse events among older adults with frailty.

This study has some limitations. First, this review will include only publications in English, which may introduce language bias and limit its generalisability. There are publications regarding interventions for frailty in other languages, such as Chinese,20 but this review does not include them. Second, we cannot collect detailed information on adverse events, such as injury severity, which prevents a more thorough analysis.

Ethics and dissemination

The findings of the systematic review will be shared through publishing in a peer-reviewed journal and presentation at appropriate conferences. Since we will not use specific patient data, ethical approval is unnecessary.

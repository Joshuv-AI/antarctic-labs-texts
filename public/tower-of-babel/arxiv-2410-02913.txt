Conditional Non-Soficity of p-adic Deligne Extensions: on a Theorem of Gohla and Thom
Michael Chapman, Yotam Dikstein, Alexander Lubotzky

Abstract
A long standing problem asks whether every group is sofic, i.e., can be separated by almost-homomorphisms to the symmetric group Sym ​ ( n ) \textup{Sym}(n) . Similar problems have been asked with respect to almost-homomorphisms to the unitary group U ⁡ ( n ) U(n) , equipped with various norms. One of these problems has been solved, for the first time in [ De +20 ] : some central extensions Γ ~ \widetilde{\Gamma} of arithmetic lattices Γ \Gamma of S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ g , ℚ p ) Sp(2g,\mathbb{Q}_{p}) were shown to be non-Frobenius approximated by almost homomorphisms to U ⁡ ( n ) U(n) . Right after, it was shown that similar results hold with respect to the p p -Schatten norms [ LO20 ] . It is natural, and has already been suggested in [ CL23a ] and [ GT24 ] , to check whether the Γ ~ \widetilde{\Gamma} are also non-sofic. In order to show that they are (also) non-sofic, it suffices: (a) To prove that the permutation Cheeger constant of the simplicial complex underlying Γ \Gamma is positive, generalizing [ EK24 ] . This would imply that Γ \Gamma is stable. (b) To prove that the (flexible) stability of Γ \Gamma implies the non-soficity of Γ ~ \widetilde{\Gamma} . Clause (b) was proved by Gohla and Thom [ GT24 ] . Here we offer a more algebraic/combinatorial treatment to their theorem.

Introduction

A group is said to be
sofic
if its elements can be separated by almost actions on finite sets.
This notion is due to Gromov
[
Gro99
]
and Weiss
[

]

Separation properties by almost homomorphisms to other “nice” metric groups are studied, and are known as
approximation properties
.
Specifically, the case of almost unitary representations, where the unitaries are equipped with some matrix norm, is well studied. For example, Connes
[
Con76
]
studied hyperlinearity, Brown and Ozawa
[
BO08
]
studied property MF, and De Chiffre–Glebsky–Lubotzky–Thom
[

]
studied Frobenius approximable groups. The latter were the first to provide examples of

Definition 1.1

(Hamming distance with errors)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\Sigma
be finite sets. The (normalized)
Hamming distance
(with errors) between
\sigma\in\textup{Sym}(\Omega)
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\textup{Sym}(\Sigma)
is

d_{H}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}):=1-\frac{|\{\star\in\Omega\mid\sigma.\star=\sigma^{\prime}.\star\}|}{|\Sigma|}=\Probability_{\star\in\Sigma}[\sigma.\star\neq\sigma^{\prime}.\star],

where
\sigma.\star=\mathfrak{error}\notin\Sigma
for every
\star\notin\Omega
, and
\Probability_{\star\in\Sigma}
is the uniform distribution over
\Sigma
.

2

Though it is not clear that

satisfies the triangle inequality, it does (see

This distance extends to (an

-type distance on) the functions
\varphi\colon S\to\textup{Sym}(\Omega)
and
\psi\colon S\to\textup{Sym}(\Sigma)
by letting

d_{H}(\varphi,\psi):=\max_{s\in S}[d_{H}(\varphi(s),\psi(s))].

Definition 1.2

(Defect and Almost Actions)

Let

The (

\textup{def}(\varphi):=\max_{r\in R}[d_{H}(\varphi(r),\textup{Id}_{\Omega})]=\max_{r\in R}\Probability_{\star\in\Omega}[\varphi(r).\star\neq\star].

We say that

-almost action
of

.

Definition 1.3

(Soficity)
.

A finitely presented group

Definition 1.6

(Simplicial complexes and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-cohomology)
.

Let

be a finite,

; a

\sigma\in X

\tau\in X
; every cell in
X
is contained in some

-coefficients is a function
\alpha\colon X(k)\to\mathbb{F}_{2}

\forall\sigma\in X(k+1)\ \colon\ \ \delta\alpha(\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\subseteq\sigma\\
\tau\in X(k)\end{subarray}}\alpha(\tau).

The

-coboundaries

. It is straightforward to check that

is a subspace of

. The

\mathbb{F}_{2}

\nicefrac{{Z^{k}}}{{B^{k}}}

There is a fully supported probability density function

, defined by

\forall\sigma\in X(k)\ \colon\ \ w(\sigma)=\frac{1}{\binom{d+1}{k+1}}\Probability_{\tau\in X(d)}[\sigma\subseteq\tau],

(1.2)

where
\Probability_{\tau\in X(d)}
is the uniform distribution over
d
-cells in
X
.
This weight function induces a norm on cochains as follows

\forall\alpha\in C^{k}\ \colon\ \ \|\alpha\|=\sum_{\sigma\colon\alpha(\sigma)=1}w(\sigma),

(1.3)

which in turn induces a metric

on the
k
-cochains by letting
d_{w}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=\|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\|

, that is, for every set

\ProbOp_{{\sigma\overset{w}{\sim}X(i)}}\left[E\right]=\sum_{\sigma\in E}w(\sigma).

Definition 1.7

(Cocycle and Coboundary expansion)
.

A (finite,
d
-dimensional, pure) simplicial complex

It is said to be an

The combinatorial statement and proof

The notion of coboundary expansion was developed independently by Linial–Meshulam
[
LM06
]
and by Gromov
[
Gro10
]
. Gromov proved that coboundary expansion implies topological overlapping. Later, it was shown in
[
DKW18
]
that it is enough to be a cocycle expander with large cosystols (a.k.a cosystolic expander) to satisfy topological overlapping. In
[
KKL16
,
EK24
]
it was shown that the
d
-dimensional skeleton of certain quotients of
(d+1)
-dimensional Bruhat–Tits buildings are cocycle expanders with large cosystols, providing the first examples of bounded degree complexes with the topological overlapping property.
The group
\Gamma
in the
p
-adic Deligne extension (
1.1
) is the fundamental group of a complex
X
that satisfies the conditions of
[

,
EK24
]
, and in particular has large cosystols.
Actually, because the conditions of
[
KKL16
,
EK24
]
are
local
in nature, the same is true for every (connected) finite covering space of
X
, namely, all these coverings have large cosystols.
The main goal of this paper is to provide a combinatorial treatment to the following theorem of Gohla and Thom:

Let us sketch our proof of

, as it is quite elementary. It is standard (see

) that a central

on

(up to a

that (almost) separates

from

that maps

As

Basic observations on stability and cohomology

This Section contains standard definitions and results. We include proofs for a self contained and clear presentation.

2.1
Elementary stability results

Claim 2.1

(Close to almost action is an almost action)
.

Let
\Gamma=\langle S|R\rangle
be a finitely presented group, and let
\ell
be the maximal length of a word in
R
. Let
\varphi\colon S\to\textup{Sym}(\Omega)
and
\psi\colon S\to\textup{Sym}(\Sigma)
, where
\Omega\subseteq\Sigma
, be such that
\varphi
is an
\varepsilon_{1}
-almost action of
\Gamma
, and
d_{H}(\varphi,\psi)\leqslant\varepsilon_{2}
. Then
\psi
is an
(\varepsilon_{1}+(\ell+1)\cdot\varepsilon_{2})
-almost action of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Recall that if
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\textup{Sym}(\Omega)
and
\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}\in\textup{Sym}(\Sigma)
, then the following triangle inequality holds:

d_{H}(\sigma\sigma^{\prime},\zeta\zeta^{\prime})\leqslant d_{H}(\sigma,\zeta)+d_{H}(\sigma^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime}).

(2.1)

This is because, if
\sigma\sigma^{\prime}.\star\neq\zeta\zeta^{\prime}.\star
, then one of the following must occur — either
\sigma^{\prime}.\star\neq\zeta^{\prime}.\star
or
\sigma^{\prime}.\star=\zeta^{\prime}.\star=\diamond
but
\sigma.\diamond\neq\zeta.\diamond
. The probability of the first occurring is
d_{H}(\sigma^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
, and the probability of the second occurring is at most
d_{H}(\sigma,\zeta)
, which proves the inequality.

Recall that

\max_{s\in S}(d_{H}(\varphi(s),\psi(s))=d_{H}(\varphi,\psi)=\varepsilon_{2}
.
By using (
2.1
), we can deduce that for every word in the free group over
S
,
w\in\mathcal{F}_{S}
of length at most
\ell
,
d_{H}(\varphi(w),\psi(w))\leqslant\ell\cdot\varepsilon_{2}
. In particular, for every
r\in R
we have
d_{H}(\varphi(r),\psi(r))\leqslant\ell\cdot\varepsilon_{2}
. From the triangle inequality of the Hamming distance with errors, for every
r\in R
,

d_{H}(\psi(r),\textup{Id}_{\Sigma})\leqslant d_{H}(\psi(r),\varphi(r))+d_{H}(\varphi(r),\textup{Id}_{\Omega})+d_{H}(\textup{Id}_{\Omega},\textup{Id}_{\Sigma})\leqslant\ell\cdot\varepsilon_{2}+d_{H}(\varphi(r),\textup{Id}_{\Omega})+\varepsilon_{2}.

Note that in the right-most inequality we use the inequality
d_{H}(\textup{Id}_{\Omega},\textup{Id}_{\Sigma})\leqslant\varepsilon_{2}
that follows the fact that
d_{H}(\varphi,\psi)\leqslant\varepsilon_{2}
. Thus

\textup{def}(\psi)=\max_{r\in R}[d_{H}(\psi(r),\textup{Id}_{\Sigma})]\leqslant(\ell+1)\varepsilon_{2}+\underbrace{\max_{r\in R}[d_{H}(\varphi(r),\textup{Id}_{\Omega})]}_{\textup{def}(\varphi)}\leqslant(\ell+1)\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1},

as claimed.
∎

Claim 2.2

(Almost involutions are close to involutions)
.

Let
\zeta\in\textup{Sym}\left(\Omega\right)
. Then,
there is
\tau\in\textup{Sym}\left(\Omega\right)
such that
\tau^{2}=\textup{Id}
and

d_{H}\left(\zeta,\tau\right)=d_{H}\left(\zeta^{2},\textup{Id}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
W=\left\{\star\in\Omega\mid\zeta^{2}.\star=\star\right\}
.
Note that the restriction
\zeta|_{W}
is an involution
W\rightarrow W
.
Define

\tau\left(\star\right)=\begin{cases}\zeta\left(\star\right)&\star\in W,\\
\star&\star\notin W.\end{cases}

Then
\tau^{2}=\textup{Id}
and
d_{H}\left(\zeta,\tau\right)=1-\frac{|W|}{|X|}=d_{H}\left(\zeta^{2},\textup{Id}\right)
.
∎

Claim 2.3

(Almost fixed point free involutions are close to fixed point free involutions)
.

Let
\zeta\in\textup{Sym}(\Omega)
be an involution. Assume
d_{H}(\zeta,\textup{Id})\geqslant 1-\varepsilon
. Then, there is an involution with no fixed points

Let

Claim 2.4

(Almost commuting with a fixed point free involution is close to commuting with the involution)
.

Let
\Omega
be a finite set, and let
\Omega_{\pm}
be as above. For every permutation
\zeta\in\textup{Sym}(\Omega_{\pm})
, there is a permutation
\sigma\in\textup{Sym}(\Omega_{\pm})
that commutes with sign flips, namely

Proof.

Let

d_{H}([-\textup{Id},\zeta],\textup{Id})=1-\nicefrac{{|W|}}{{|\Omega|}}
. Let

\overline{\sigma}

\zeta.+\star=\zeta.+\diamond\quad\textrm{or}\quad\zeta.+\star=-\zeta.+\diamond=_{\diamond\in W}\zeta.-\diamond.

As

+\star=+\diamond\quad\textrm{or}\quad+\star=-\diamond,

but the second case is impossible as their left coordinates (i.e., their signs) disagree. This means
+\star=+\diamond
,
and in particular their right coordinates agree, namely

is injective.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let

be a finitely presented sofic group with a generator
\tau\in S
that is a central non-trivial involution in
\Gamma
. Then, there is a sofic approximation
\psi_{n}\colon S\to\textrm{Sym}((\Omega_{n})_{\pm})
, such that

is always sent to the sign flip
\psi_{n}(\tau)=-\textup{Id}
, and every permutation in the image

commutes with the sign flip.

Proof.

First, we can assume without loss of generality that
\tau^{2}\in R
, and that
[\tau,s]\in R
for every
s\in S
. Furthermore, let
\ell
be the maximal length of a relation
r\in R
. As
\Gamma
is sofic, it has some sofic approximation consisting of
\varepsilon_{n}
-almost actions
\varphi_{n}\colon S\to\textup{Sym}(\Omega_{n})
, where
\varepsilon_{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
. In particular,

d_{H}(\varphi_{n}(\tau^{2}),\textup{Id}_{\Omega_{n}})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}:=1-d_{H}(\varphi_{n}(\tau),\textup{Id}_{\Omega_{n}})
, and as
\textup{Id}_{\Gamma}\neq\tau\in\Gamma
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
as well.

By
Claim
2.2
, there are functions
f_{n}\colon S\to\textup{Sym}(\Omega_{n})
such that
f_{n}(\tau^{2})=\textup{Id}
,
f_{n}(s)=\varphi_{n}(s)
for every
\tau\neq s\in S
, and
d_{H}(f_{n}(\tau),\varphi_{n}(\tau))\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}
. Thus,
d_{H}(f_{n},\varphi_{n})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}
, and by
Claim
2.1
,

\textup{def}(f_{n})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}+(\ell+1)\cdot\varepsilon_{n}=(\ell+2)\varepsilon_{n}.

In addition, using the triangle inequality of the Hamming metric,

d_{H}(f_{n}(\tau),\textup{Id}_{\Omega_{n}})\geqslant\underbrace{d_{H}(\varphi_{n}(\tau),\textup{Id}_{\Omega_{n}})}_{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}}-\underbrace{d_{H}(f_{n}(\tau),\varphi_{n}(\tau))}_{\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}}\geqslant 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}-\varepsilon_{n}.

Hence, by
Claim
2.3
, there are functions

, where
\Omega_{n}\subseteq(\Sigma_{n})_{\pm}
, such that
g_{n}(\tau)=-\textup{Id}
, and
d_{H}(g_{n},f_{n})\leqslant 2(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}+\varepsilon_{n})
.
Thus, by
Claim

,

\textup{def}(g_{n})\leqslant\textup{def}(f_{n})+(\ell+1)d_{H}(g_{n},f_{n})\leqslant(\ell+2)\varepsilon_{n}+(\ell+1)\cdot 2(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}+\varepsilon_{n})=(2\ell+2)\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}+(3\ell+4)\varepsilon_{n},

for every

independently, there are functions

such that
\psi_{n}(\tau)=-\textup{Id}
(namely
\tau
is sent to the sign flip),

(namely all the images commute with the sign flip), and

\psi(\widetilde{zx}).(+\star)=(-1)^{\zeta((z,\circ)(x,\star))}\circ
. As the images of
\psi
commute sign flips, this implies that

\psi(\widetilde{xy})\psi(\widetilde{yz})\psi(\widetilde{zx}).(+\star)=\psi(\widetilde{xy})\psi(\widetilde{yz}).(-1)^{\zeta((z,\circ)(x,\star))}\circ=(-1)^{\zeta((z,\circ)(x,\star))}\psi(\widetilde{xy})\psi(\widetilde{yz}).+\circ\ .

Repeating this calculation twice more, for the edges of the first type
(y,\diamond)(z,\circ)
and
(x,\star)(y,\diamond)
, we deduce that

\psi(\widetilde{xy})\psi(\widetilde{yz})\psi(\widetilde{zx}).(+\star)=(-1)^{\zeta((z,\circ)(x,\star))+\zeta((y,\diamond)(z,\circ))+\zeta((x,\star)(y,\diamond))}\star=(-1)^{\delta\zeta(\triangle)}\star\ .

Combining the above equality with (
3.4
), we deduce that
\phi(xyz)=\delta\zeta(\triangle)
. On the other hand,
\phi^{\prime}(\triangle)
was defined to be equal to
\phi(xyz)
, which proves the required equality.

Let us bound the weighted distance (induced by (
1.3
)) between the
2
-cocycle
\phi^{\prime}
and the
2
-coboundary
\delta\zeta
.
By the above analysis,
d_{w}(\phi^{\prime},\delta\zeta)
is bounded by the sum of probabilities, when
\triangle=(x,\star)(y,\diamond)(z,\circ)
is sampled according to its weight (
1.2
), of the following two events:

(Event 1)

The triangle
\triangle
contains an edge of the second type.

(Event 2)

Equation (
3.4
) is not satisfied by
\triangle
.

As
Y
is a covering space of
X
, (oriented) edges in
Y
are parametrized by pairs
xy\in\vec{X}(1),\star\in\Sigma
, and (oriented) triangles in
Y
are parametrized by
xyz\in\vec{X}(2),\star\in\Sigma
.
Furthermore, the weight of the covering cell is
\nicefrac{{1}}{{|\Sigma|}}
times the weight of the cell it covers.
An edge
(x,\star)(y,f(yx).\star)\in\vec{Y}(1)
is of the first type if and only if
f(yx).\star=\varphi(yx).\star
.
So,

\begin{split}\Probability_{(x,\star)(y,\diamond)\overset{w}{\sim}\vec{Y}(1)}[(x,\star)(y,\diamond)\text{ is of the first type}]&=\Expectation_{xy\overset{w}{\sim}\vec{X}(1)}\left[\Probability_{\star\in\Sigma}\ [(x,\star)(y,f^{-1}(xy).\star)\in\vec{Y}(1)\text{ is of the first type}]\right]\\
&\leqslant\max_{xy\in\vec{X}(1)}\Probability_{\star\in\Sigma}\ [(x,\star)(y,f^{-1}(xy).\star)\in\vec{Y}(1)\ {\text{ is of the first type}}]\\
&=\max_{xy\in\vec{X}(1)}d_{H}(f(xy),\varphi(xy))\\
&=d_{H}(f,\varphi)\leqslant\rho(\varepsilon).\end{split}

Using the above and a union bound, we can deduce that the probability that (Event
1
) occurs is at most
3\rho(\varepsilon)
.

For (
3.4
) to be satisfied by
\triangle=(x,\star)(y,\diamond)(z,\circ)
, first of all
\star
needs to be in
\Omega
. But, as sampling a triangle in
\vec{Y}(2)
requires sampling
xyz\overset{w}{\sim}\vec{X}(2)
and
\star\in\Sigma
uniformly,
\star\notin\Omega
happens exactly
1-\frac{|\Omega|}{|\Sigma|}
of the times. Recall that
1-\frac{|\Omega|}{|\Sigma|}\leqslant d_{H}(f,\phi)\leqslant\rho(\varepsilon)
. Given that
\star
was sampled such that
\star\in\Omega
, as
\textup{def}(\psi)\leqslant\varepsilon
, we have that (
3.4
) is not satisfied with probability at most
\varepsilon
. Therefore, the probability (Event 2) occurs is upper bounded by
\rho(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon
.

All in all,
d_{w}(\phi^{\prime},\delta\zeta)\leqslant\varepsilon+4\rho(\varepsilon)
, which tends to zero with
\varepsilon
. Note that this means that there is a connected component
Y_{0}
of
Y
, such that the restrictions of
\phi^{\prime}
and
\delta\zeta
to
Y_{0}
are still at most
(\varepsilon+4\rho(\varepsilon))
-apart, namely

d_{w}(\phi^{\prime}|_{Y_{0}},\delta\zeta|_{Y_{0}})\leqslant\varepsilon+4\rho(\varepsilon).

On the other hand, as
Y_{0}
is a connected covering of
X
, its fundamental group
H
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
, the fundamental group of
X
. By the subgroups correspondence theorem (fourth isomorphism theorem), as
\pi\colon\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
is an epimorphism, there is a subgroup
\widetilde{H}
of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
that contains
\tau
and satisfies
\pi(\widetilde{H})=H
. Furthermore, according to
Fact
2.10
,
\phi^{\prime}
is in the same cohomology class as the
2
-cocycle defined by the central
2
-extension

0\to\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{2\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{\iota}\widetilde{H}\xrightarrow{\pi|_{\widetilde{H}}}H\to 1.

As
\tau\in\widetilde{H}
, and
\tau
is trivial in every finite quotient of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, this extension cannot split. Thus,
\phi^{\prime}|_{Y_{0}}
is a non-coboundary cocycle, and by the
\kappa
-large cocycles condition on
Y
,
we deduce that
d_{w}(\phi^{\prime}|_{Y_{0}},B^{2}(Y_{0};\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{2\mathbb{Z}}}))\geqslant\kappa
. But,
{\delta}\zeta\in B^{2}
, and we can deduce that

\kappa\leqslant\varepsilon+4\rho(\varepsilon).

as
\rho
is a rate function, this gives a positive lower bound on
\varepsilon
which is a contradiction. Hence,
\widetilde{\Gamma}
cannot be sofic.
∎

4
A non-stable central extension of a stable group

The following family of groups were used in
[
FG23
]
. Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be a subset of positive integers. Let

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}\cong\langle a,t\mid a^{2}=1\ ,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\ \colon\ [a,t^{n}at^{-n}]=1\rangle

be the (standard) lamplighter group. One can define a
2
-central extension of
\Gamma
using
I
in the following way (compare this special case to the general machinery discussed in
Section
2.2
):

\Gamma_{I}=\left\langle a,t,z\ \middle|\ a^{2}=z^{2}=[a,z]=[t,z]=1\ ,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\ \colon\ [a,t^{n}at^{-n}]=\begin{cases}1&n\notin I\\
z&n\in I\end{cases}\right\rangle\ ,

(4.1)

with the short exact sequence

1\to\{1,z\}\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{I}\xrightarrow{\pi}\Gamma\to 1\ ,

(4.2)

where
\pi
is defined by mapping
a\mapsto a,t\mapsto t
and
z\mapsto 1
. The following claim and corollary were communicated to us by Corentin Bodart.

Claim 4.1
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
\Gamma_{I}
as in (
4.1
). If there is a homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Gamma_{I}\to F
, where
F
is a finite group, and
\varphi(z)\neq 1
, then
I_{\pm}=I\sqcup-I=\{\pm n\mid n\in I\}
is
|F|
-periodic; namely, if
n\in I_{\pm}
, then
n\pm|F|\in I_{\pm}
.

Proof.

From the elementary identities
g[h,k]g^{-1}=[ghg^{-1},gkg^{-1}]
and
[h,k]=[k,h]^{-1}
, together with the fact
z=z^{-1}
in
\Gamma_{I}
, we can deduce that

[a,t^{n}at^{-n}]=t^{n}[t^{-n}at^{n},a]t^{-n}=t^{n}(\underbrace{[a,t^{-n}at^{n}]}_{z\ \textrm{or}\ 1})^{-1}t^{-n}=[a,t^{-n}at^{n}].

Hence, the identity on
[a,t^{n}at^{-n}]
in
\Gamma_{I}
can be extended as follows:

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\ \colon\ \ [a,t^{n}at^{-n}]=\begin{cases}1&n\notin I_{\pm}\\
z&n\in I_{\pm}\end{cases}\ .

Let
f=|F|
. By Lagrange’s theorem,
\varphi(t^{f})=\varphi(t)^{f}=1.
Hence, if
n\in I_{\pm}
, then

\varphi([a,t^{n+f}at^{-n-f}])=\varphi([a,t^{n}at^{-n}])=\varphi(z)\neq 1,

which implies in particular that
[a,t^{n+f}at^{-n-f}]
is not the identity in
\Gamma_{I}
. As
[a,t^{m}at^{-m}]
is either
1
or
z
for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we deduce that
[a,t^{n+f}at^{-n-f}]=z
, and thus
n+f\in I
. The argument for
n-f
is similar.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
I=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then,
\Gamma_{I}
(
4.1
) is not residually finite.

Proof.

As
I\pm=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is not periodic for any positive integer
f
, by
Claim
4.1
, there is no homomorphism from
\Gamma_{I}
to a finite group that separates
z
from the identity. In particular,
\Gamma_{I}
is not residually finite.
∎

Fact 4.3
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then, the group
\Gamma_{I}
(
4.1
) is solvable.

Proof.

Recall that central extensions of solvable groups are solvable, and that the lamplighter group
\Gamma
is solvable.
∎

Definition
1.3
and
Definition
1.4
define soficity and stability for a finitely presented group. For the generalized notion of soficity (for any countable group) see, for example,
[
Pes08
, Definition 3.1]
. For the generalized notion of stability see, for example,
[
LL22
]
.

Claim 4.4
.

Let
I=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then,
\Gamma_{I}
(
4.1
) is not stable.

Proof.

In
[
GR09
]
it was shown that a sofic and stable group must be residually finite. As
\Gamma_{I}
is solvable (Fact
4.3
) and thus sofic, yet not residually finite (Corollary
4.2
), the claim is deduced.
∎

So, fixing
I=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the central extension
\Gamma_{I}
in the short exact sequence (
4.2
) is not stable. But, the lamplighter group
\Gamma
is stable, by
[
LL22
, Theorem 1.3]
. Hence, we found the required example. We do not know if such examples exist where
\Gamma
(and hence
\widetilde{\Gamma}
) are finitely presented.

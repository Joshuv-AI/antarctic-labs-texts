In silico and In vitro studies of EGFR kinase and Anilide derivatives as inhibitors against Breast Cancer
Authors: Mala M. Parab, Pramodkumar P. Gupta, Shraddha Shetty, Santosh S. Chhajed, Geetanjali Shukla, Kshitij Bapat, Debjani Dasgupta, Prerona Boruah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The benzanilide scaffold is well known for its biological activities, such as antifungal, antibacterial, herbicide, and anti-tumor activity. Benzanilides with halogen substitution were computationally designed and screened against EGFR kinase, with erlotinib as a reference compound, using molecular docking. Two compounds were synthesized by the Schotten-Baumann reaction and confirmed based on FT-IR spectrophotometry, TLC, and sharp, narrow-range melting point analyses. The synthesized compounds were tested for antiproliferative activity and acute toxicity using the MTT assay with the (MCF-7) human breast cancer cell line. The effect of the compounds on non-cancerous cells in the human body was tested using blood lymphocytes. The mechanism of action evaluated by an in vitro cytotoxicity study revealed IC50 values of compound A = 75.54 ± 4.7 μM and compound B = 178.5 ± 9.0 μM (mean ± SEM, n = 3), showing promising growth-inhibitory cytotoxic actions with increasing concentrations against EGFR in breast cancer. Docking studies revealed that synthesized compounds interacted with a similar enzyme and ATP binding site of EGFR-TK in the reference drug erlotinib, along with additional strong interactions indicating efficacy and potency. To predict better tolerability and late failure, toxicity predictions were made for the synthesized compounds. Therefore, this research aimed to demonstrate the activity of several promising benzanilide molecules.

Introduction

Cancer is the biggest epidemic of the 21st century and the foremost cause of fatality in the world. Globally, 12 percent of the total cancer burden is accounted for by breast cancer.1 Tamoxifen is widely employed by patients among several other drugs available commercially, such as gefitinib and erlotinib for the treatment of cancer, and is projected to have partial effectiveness in reducing the risk of breast carcinoma.2,3

Treatment with tamoxifen leads to an increase in the occurrence of emerging blood clots, which may elevate the risk of stroke, uterine sarcoma, and inadequate identification of non-invasive breast malignancies in patients.2 Targeted techniques are used to lessen the negative effects of standard chemotherapy. Therefore, there is a great need for targeted therapy to help plan the new focused and successful treatments. EGFR kinase was one of the selected targets that was first suggested for targeted cancer therapy since it plays several roles in cell growth, proliferation, survival, and differentiation.

In several cancer types, including breast cancer, protein EGFR kinase is regarded as a prime and most extensively researched therapeutic target.4 Since the development in the 1980s of the first protein kinase inhibitor known as imatinib, the FDA has approved 37 kinase inhibitors for breast and lung cancer. The kinase inhibitors target kinome members such as EGFR, providing clinical outcomes for targeted therapy with small molecules. The inhibitors target the ATP-binding sites, or non-ATP competitive inhibitors target the allosteric sites. These inhibitors block the kinase activity, resulting in anti-proliferative activity. Despite 35 approved drugs, including orally effective kinase inhibitors that target a limited number of enzymes, and encouraging outcomes, there exist downsides such as drug resistance, short- and long-term toxicity, and compromised efficacy, which are major challenges in clinical trials.4,5 In case of targeted agents inhibitors to kinases, the resistance may arise due to the reduced affinity of the receptor kinase for its inhibitors, compensatory mechanisms or point mutations leading to resistance.6, 7, 8

Relapses in patients are due to gatekeeper residues involved in the binding site region. The other factors, such as pathway crosstalk, signal redundancies, negative feedback, cross-inhibition, cross-activation, and pathway convergence, which respond to intra- and extracellular signals, are emerging conditions to evolve drug resistance. The Ras-ERK and PI3K-mTOR signaling pathways depict major mechanisms in differentiation, proliferation, metabolism, survival and other important roles in cellular mechanisms, and are activated in almost 80% of all tumors.9 To overcome resistance to the drugs erlotinib and gefitinib, inhibitors that covalently bind to the ATP-binding site of EGFR have been designed.10

Due to the diverse activity of kinases in cancer, approved and numerous kinase inhibitors are under clinical development despite potential side effects, most patients often relapse after long-term effects of kinase inhibitor drugs and several lines of treatments. It is extremely important to minimize the side effects with the development of selective EGFR kinase inhibitors. Patients on TKI treatments often develop resistance and relapse of disease. The T790 M exon 20 point mutation at the “gatekeeper” threonine residue causes a secondary EGFR mutation with 50% of TKI resistance.11, 12, 13

The objective of this research is to identify prospective benzanilides and related derivatives to treat breast cancer by targeting inhibition of EGFR kinase activity with upgraded efficacy, minimized side effects, and low cost of treatment. This has been done by employing structure-based designing of drugs using homology modelling, refinement of proteins using GROMACS, molecular docking, toxicity prediction, and validation using in vitro cell line screening. What makes this study novel is the integration of drug designing through in silico means, molecular docking, ADMET prediction, chemical synthesis, and in vitro cytotoxicity testing for identification and validation of benzanilides as anticancer agents.

For receptor preparation, the protein (EGFR) Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor Tyrosine Kinase domain, which has missing residues in the protein crystal structures in the Protein Data Bank,18 was considered. Here, we considered the homology-modeled 3D structure of the EGFR Tyrosine Kinase domain using the Swiss-Model server.19 The modeled protein structure was cleaned by removing any unwanted elements and non-amino acid residues using the BioVia Discovery Visualizer tool.20 It was further refined and energy-minimized using GROMACS 5.0 (Groningen Machine for Chemical Simulation)21 with the OPLS-AA force field. The results were analyzed in graph form using the XMGRACE plotting tool and viewed using the VMD visualization tool to obtain the coordinates after energy minimization.22,23 The quality check of the model was based on the RMSD value analyzed by the PyMOL tool.24 Further validation of the model was done using the Z-score and Ramachandran plot via ProSA-web25 and Procheck, as well as the ERRAT SAVES server.26,27 Active site prediction in the protein structure was performed using CASTp 3.0,28 multiple sequence alignment using Clustal Omega, and analysis of structural information using PDBsum for 1M17.pdb. The binding site region was selected using the AutoDock tool based on the default binding site region of the drug erlotinib in the EGFR kinase domain 1M17.pdb, including the active site predictions from the CASTp server and MSA results, and then proceeded to molecular docking study. The binding site region grid box selected for the cavity in the protein file was center-x = 38.317; center-y = 30.455; center-z = 39.467; size-x = 28; size-y = 38; size-z = 44 and saved in.pdbqt file format. The docking process was carried out using the command window of the AutoDock Vina tool,29 and docking results were viewed using UCSF Chimera.30 Further interactions were studied using BIOVIA Discovery Visualizer.20 The nine in silico designed compounds were tested for ADMET properties using the SwissADME server.31

Experimental

Synthesis of compounds: Two compounds, A and B, from the list were synthesized at the Department of Pharmaceutical Chemistry, MET Institute of Pharmacy, Bhujbal Knowledge City, Nashik, India. The method described by Chhajed et al.32 was used to synthesize the compounds. Solvents and reagents were obtained from commercial sources.33 Characterization of the synthesized compounds and melting points of the products are reported using a Thiele tube melting point apparatus. The percentage yield is based upon products obtained after purification.34

Compound A: Accurately weighed 0.128 g (0.01 mol) of p-chloroaniline was transferred into an iodine flask. To this, 20 mL of 10% sodium hydroxide solution was added. The contents of the flask were swirled constantly, and 1.01 g (0.01 mol) of benzoyl chloride was added dropwise to the solution in the flask. After complete addition of benzoyl chloride, the contents of the flask were shaken for 15 min. The reaction mixture was neutralized using dilute hydrochloric acid to form crude compound A. It was then filtered at the pump and air-dried. The crude compound was recrystallized using boiling ethanol (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Synthesis scheme of compound A.

Synthesis scheme of compound A.

Compound B: Accurately weighed 0.172 g (0.01 mol) of p-bromoaniline was transferred into an iodine flask. It was then dissolved in 20 mL of 10% aqueous sodium hydroxide solution. The contents of the flask were swirled constantly, and 1.01 g (0.01 mol) of benzoyl chloride was added dropwise to the solution in the flask. After complete addition of benzoyl chloride, the contents of the flask were swirled for 15 min. The reaction mixture was neutralized using dilute hydrochloric acid to form crude compound B. It was then filtered at the pump and air-dried. The crude compound was recrystallized using boiling ethanol (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Synthesis scheme of compound B.

Synthesis scheme of compound B.

An MTT colorimetric test was performed to study the in vitro cytotoxicity of MCF-7 cells and normal human peripheral blood lymphocytes using 96-well microplate culture system. The MCF-7 cell line was obtained from NCCS, Pune, and grown between passage numbers 15-25. Healthy donor human peripheral blood lymphocytes (n = 5; age group 35–40 years) were taken as subjects. MCF-7 cells (2.5 × 103 cells/well) were grown in 100 μL of culture media for 24 h in an incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 atmosphere. The treated cells were exposed to various concentrations (25–400 μM) of the test samples for 24 h. Untreated cells, DMSO (vehicle) control and blanks (only culture media) served as controls. After incubation, 20 μL of MTT (5 mg/mL) was added into the well and incubated for 4 h. The formazan crystals formed were solubilized using 100 μL of DMSO, and the absorbance was read at 570 nm (reference 630 nm). All experiments were done in triplicate well system and repeated thrice independently (n = 3). Inhibition (%) of cells was calculated as [(Ac − At)/(Ac − Ab)] × 100, where At = test absorbance, Ac = vehicle control absorbance and Ab = blank absorbance. The results are expressed as mean ± SD of three independent experiments.35

Human peripheral blood lymphocytes were isolated from healthy, non-smoking adult volunteers (n = 5; three males, two female; age range 35–40 years, Voluntary given by research team) by density gradient centrifugation. Briefly, heparinized venous blood was diluted 1:1 with phosphate-buffered saline, layered over Ficoll-Histopaque (density 1.077 g/mL, Sigma-Aldrich), and centrifuged at 400×g for 30 min at room temperature. The mononuclear cell layer at the interface was collected, washed three times with PBS, and resuspended in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 2 mM l-glutamine, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Cells were seeded at 1 × 105 cells per well in 96-well plates and stimulated with 5 μg/mL phytohemagglutinin (PHA) for 48 h prior to compound treatment.

Statistical analysis: IC50 values were calculated by non-linear regression analysis using GraphPad Prism 8. Raw absorbance data from MTT assays were imported into XY format, with log-transformed concentrations plotted against normalized response (% inhibition). Data were fitted to a sigmoidal dose-response curve (variable slope) using the equation: Y = Bottom + (Top - Bottom)/(1 + 10^((LogIC50 - X) × HillSlope)). The goodness of fit was evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R2). R2 values ≥ 0.8 were considered indicative of acceptable model fit. All experiments were performed in triplicate and repeated in three independent biological replicates.

Results

In silico Study: Sequence of the EGFR domain of Homo sapiens (human) was obtained from the UniProtKB protein database (accession ID: P00533) for homology modeling purpose. It contained sequence of residues from 712 to 979 with an amino acid length of 268 from the total length of 1210 residues in the complete EGFR kinase protein sequence. Homology modeling was performed on the retrieved EGFR domain protein sequence using SWISS-MODEL based on the concept that higher similarity in sequence leads to higher structural similarity. Quality of the model depends upon templates chosen according to their sequence similarity (coverage) and X-ray resolution; X-ray resolution varies between 1.4 Å and 2.7 Å. Models are generated according to target-template alignment using ProMod3. Coverage of the selected template was 100% showing the similarity of the template to the target sequence with a resolution of 2.6 Å. 3D structure of the selected EGFR domain protein sequence was a monomer having the ligand AQ4. The model created by selecting a template with high identity had a GMQE score of 0.98 and a QMEAN score of −0.89 which indicated the quality of the modeled structure. The sequence identity was 100%, and the sequence similarity score was 0.61, with a full coverage score of 1.00 (Fig. 3).Fig. 3The selected model result of the high identity template selected from Swiss-model server.

The selected model result of the high identity template selected from Swiss-model server.

The preliminary 3D model of the EGFR domain protein was refined by energy minimization to eliminate structural errors and improve its overall structural accuracy using GROMACS 5.0 software. To obtain an electroneutral system with no steric clashes or inappropriate geometry, energy minimization was carried out for the predicted model using the OPLS-AA force field, which was set to run for one lakh (100,000) steps or until convergence to machine precision. The output was potential energy, which determines whether energy minimization was successful; the value should be negative, and no water molecules should be present. Second, the maximum force (Fmax) was set in the file (em tol = 1000.0), indicating a target Fmax of no greater than 1000 kJ mol−1 nm−1. The steepest descents converged to Fmax <1000 in 1035 steps (Fig. 4, Fig. 5).Fig. 4Potential energy of protein.Fig. 5Superimposition of energy minimized protein model with original model 1M17.

Energy minimization of EGFR kinase domain was studied with help of GROMACS and OPLS-AA force fields. Potential energy graph shows the change of total potential energy, measured in kJ/mol depending on simulation time in picoseconds. Energy varied from −5 × 105 to −2 × 106 kJ/mol, indicating that energy decreased with time. Graph shows potential energy, which consists of both bonded and non-bonded interaction energies. In a solvent system and in presence of ions, it was observed that there was an abrupt fall in potential energy, signifying relaxation of the model due to lack of steric hindrances. This is common when there are equilibration and rearrangement of solvent molecules around the biomolecule with stabilized intermolecular interactions. After reaching the lowest energy point, there was attainment of a plateau, suggesting thermodynamic equilibrium.

The protein attained minimum potential energy, and the RMSD between the reference and modeled structures was 0.210 Å for 260 out of 260 atoms, indicating close similarity. The value of Z-score through ProSA-web analysis for the X-ray structure obtained was −6.82. Insilico ADMET predictions

Drug success depends not only on target-specific molecule development but also on safety, efficacy, and minimal side effects over prolonged periods. SwissADME outputs are grouped into physicochemical properties, lipophilicity, pharmacokinetics, drug-likeness, and medicinal chemistry. The hydrogen bond donor/acceptor counts for compounds A and B comply with Lipinski's rule of five (Lipinski et al., 2001). Lipophilicity (XLOGP3) ranged from 1.78 to 4.24, within the acceptable range (Log P ≤ 5). Compounds A and B showed XLOGP3 values of 3.25 and 3.31, respectively, indicating permeability and solubility. Water solubility (ESOL log S) predictions ranged from moderate to soluble for all compounds.

All nine compounds showed favorable pharmacokinetic properties, including high GI absorption, BBB permeation, low P-gp substrate activity, and CYP450-mediated metabolism. No compound acted as a P-gp substrate. The bioavailability score was 0.55 for all compounds (F > 10% in rats). Compounds A–G showed partial metabolism, while H and I were non-inhibitors of all five CYP450 isoforms (Supplementary File 2). Synthetic accessibility scores ranged from 1.11 to 1.47; compounds A and B scored 1.23 and 1.32, respectively, indicating ease of synthesis.

Overall, SwissADME analysis indicates that synthesized compounds A and B, along with other lead molecules, possess good biological activity with fewer toxic effects. Therefore, they can be considered druggable candidates for human EGFR in treating breast cancer and inhibiting its biologically significant pathways.

All compounds were located in the yolk region, indicating high brain penetration probability and non-P-gp substrate status (PGP−), as shown by red points. Given that brain metastases occur in 20–40% of breast cancer patients,36,37 compounds with BBB penetration are significant for long-term therapy. Moreover, certain tyrosine kinase inhibitors have shown positive responses against breast cancer brain metastases.36,38

The half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values were calculated by non-linear regression analysis (sigmoidal dose-response, variable slope) using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). For MCF-7 cells, compound A exhibited an IC50 of 75.54 ± 4.7 μM (mean ± SEM, n = 3; 95% CI: 62.31–89.67 μM), and compound B exhibited an IC50 of 178.5 ± 9.0 μM (mean ± SEM, n = 3; 95% CI: 152.3–209.1 μM). For non-cancerous human lymphocytes, compound A exhibited an IC50 of 341.2 ± 17.2 μM (mean ± SEM, n = 3; 95% CI: 312.8–372.4 μM), and compound B exhibited an IC50 of 351.6 ± 15.5 μM (mean ± SEM, n = 3; 95% CI: 322.9–383.7 μM).

The goodness of fit for the non-linear regression model was evaluated using R2 values. For MCF-7 cells, compound A demonstrated excellent model fit (R2 = 0.942), and compound B demonstrated good model fit (R2 = 0.921). For lymphocyte cultures, compound A (R2 = 0.896) and compound B (R2 = 0.839) demonstrated acceptable model fit. These values indicate that the sigmoidal dose-response model appropriately describes the relationship between log-transformed concentration and response, with 84–94% of the variability in cell viability explained by the model. The slightly lower R2 values for lymphocyte data reflect the inherent biological variability of primary human cells compared to immortalized cell lines.

One-way ANOVA was performed to compare the effect of different concentrations of test compounds on cell viability. For compound A on MCF-7 cells, a significant concentration-dependent effect was observed [F (5,12) = 24.67, p = 0.0191]. For compound B on MCF-7 cells, a significant effect was also observed [F (5,12) = 19.83, p = 0.0184]. Similarly, for lymphocyte cultures, compound A [F (5,12) = 21.45, p = 0.0199] and compound B [F (5,12) = 18.92, p = 0.0194] demonstrated significant concentration-dependent effects. Dunnett's post-hoc test was applied to compare each treatment concentration with the vehicle control. Statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) were observed at concentrations ≥50 μM for compound A and ≥100 μM for compound B on MCF-7 cells (Supplementary file 3).

The cytotoxicity analysis on MCF-7 cell line by the compounds were found to be indirectly linked to varying concentrations as the tetrazolium salts are reduced by metabolically active cells and not by non-viable cells. MCF-7 cells were selected as a widely used and well-characterized human breast cancer model for preliminary cytotoxicity screening. Although EGFR expression in MCF-7 cells is relatively lower compared with EGFR-overexpressing breast cancer cell lines, several studies report detectable EGFR expression and functional activation of downstream signaling pathways such as Ras–ERK and PI3K–AKT/mTOR, which regulate cell proliferation, survival, and metabolism in breast cancer cells.9,40 Therefore, MCF-7 cells can serve as a suitable model for initial evaluation of compounds predicted to interact with EGFR signaling, while we acknowledge that further validation using EGFR-overexpressing breast cancer cell lines and direct kinase assays will be important in future studies to confirm target-specific activity.

The compounds A and B decrease the mitosis and increase apoptosis indicating stronger toxicity with increasing dose concentrations suggesting that targeting EGFR has broader downstream effects. The graphical representations indicated that addition of compounds A and B to MCF-7 cell line culture at 25 μM revealed a very slight increase in % inhibition of the cells in comparison to cell control with no visible changes and constant cell density throughout the MTT assay. The number of dead cells increased with each increasing concentration of compounds along with change in adherence patterns observed on MCF-7 cell line. At the highest concentration of 400 μM there was a high decrease in cell density and more dead cells observed.39 Compound A has shown to be more potentially active compound triggering apoptosis and change in cell density as compared to compound B with moderate activity being observed with increasing concentrations.

Discussion

An interesting class of benzanilide derivatives with halogen substituents was designed, synthesized, and evaluated for in vitro anti-tumor activity against the MCF-7 breast cancer cell line. Molecular docking studies suggested that compounds A and B have binding energies of −6.9 and −6.8 kcal/mol, respectively, similar to the reference drug erlotinib (−6.6 kcal/mol). Compound A complexed with EGFR-TK similarly to erlotinib, with an additional strong pi-sulfur bond between MET 742 and one of its benzene rings, along with alkyl/pi-alkyl interactions with LEU 694, VAL 702, ALA 719, LYS 721, and LEU 764. Compound B showed no extra interactions, only alkyl/pi-alkyl interactions with LEU 694, VAL 702, ALA 719, LYS 721, and LEU 820.

SwissADME toxicity prediction indicated that compounds A and B comply with Lipinski's rule of five. Lipophilicity (A = 3.25, B = 3.31) was within LogP ≤5, indicating good permeability and solubility. Bioavailability was 0.55 (F > 10% in rats), and synthetic accessibility scores (A = 1.23, B = 1.32) indicated ease of synthesis. Both compounds showed high GI absorption and Caco-2 permeability, were not P-gp substrates, but inhibited CYP1A2, CYP2C19, and CYP2C9, suggesting potential toxicity and reduced hepatic clearance. Thus, further preclinical trials are needed.

Overall, compounds A and B are promising lead inhibitors of human EGFR for breast cancer therapy. Both were synthesized as pure solids with narrow-range melting points (A = 220–222 °C, B = 268–270 °C),3,41,42 partially soluble in water, and completely soluble in DMSO. TLC Rf values were 0.6 (A) and 0.4 (B). FTIR confirmed functional groups: 3430 (N–H), 3009 (C–H aromatic), 2933–2874 (C–H aliphatic), 1767 (CO), and 750 (C–Br) cm−1.

The MTT assay demonstrated concentration-dependent cytotoxicity of benzanilide derivatives against MCF-7 cells, with compound A (IC50 = 75.54 ± 4.7 μM) exhibiting 2.4-fold greater potency than compound B (IC50 = 178.5 ± 9.0 μM). This superior activity correlates with molecular docking predictions showing an additional pi-sulfur interaction with MET 742 in the EGFR kinase domain—an interaction absent in compound B and erlotinib. The enhanced activity of para-chloro substituted compound A aligns with structure-activity relationship studies demonstrating that smaller halogens often optimize fit within kinase ATP-binding pockets.3,41,42

Selective toxicity toward malignant cells is critical for therapeutic development. Against non-cancerous lymphocytes, compound A (IC50 = 341.2 ± 17.2 μM) and compound B (IC50 = 351.6 ± 15.5 μM) yielded selectivity indices of 4.52 and 1.97, respectively. Compound A exceeds the established threshold (SI > 3) for high selectivity toward cancer cells,43,44 indicating favorable therapeutic potential with minimal normal cell toxicity at efficacious concentrations. The lower R2 values for lymphocyte data (0.839–0.896) versus MCF-7 cells (0.921–0.942) reflect inherent biological variability of primary human cells, consistent with published cytotoxicity studies.45,46 Despite this variability, one-way ANOVA confirmed significant concentration-dependent effects at higher concentrations (p < 0.05). The differential sensitivity likely reflects EGFR hyperactivation in breast cancer cells creating “oncogene addiction”,47 while quiescent lymphocytes exhibit low baseline EGFR expression.9

Study limitations include the need for direct EGFR kinase assay confirmation, absence of erlotinib as positive control, and evaluation limited to a single cell line. Future directions include biochemical kinase assays, downstream signalling analysis, apoptosis assessment, cell cycle studies, in vivo pharmacokinetic evaluation, and scaffold optimization to enhance MET 742 interactions while maintaining selectivity.

Compound A was found to be most active against EGFR (MET A: 742) by strong pi-sulfur bond as analyzed by docking interactions analysis. It is believed to trigger downstream signaling through the PI3K/AKT and Ras/Raf pathway. Compound A & B complexed with EGFR-TK in a similar fashion to reference Erlotinib drug. Further, the inhibition of the ATP-binding domain by compound A puts light on their efficacy as potent kinase blocking inhibitor and downstream regulation of cell signaling. The results of this study demonstrated compound A as active and compound B as moderately active towards MCF-7 breast tumor cell line possessing anti-cancer activity against breast cancer.

Conclusion

Benzanilide analogs have shown to be an appropriate model moiety for further design of potent inhibitors of EGFR against breast cancer. They have an enhanced effect as anti-cancer molecules with less toxicity, higher specificity compared to erlotinib in silico docking results, potency, and affinity to the target EGFR signaling pathway. The recrystallization, melting point analyses, TLC, and FTIR results were used to obtain a pure form of the drug & thereby confirming the purity of compounds synthesized. Cytotoxicity and IC50 values of compounds were calculated by MTT assay, indicating stronger toxicity with increasing dose concentrations triggering apoptosis and change in cell density while, human lymphocytes confirmed the compounds to be non-toxic on non-cancerous cells. Additionally, further pharmacological investigations are needed to evaluate compounds' potential as therapeutic agents against breast cancer.

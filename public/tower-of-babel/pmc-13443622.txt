Relation-aware context aggregation framework for sparse knowledge graph completion
Authors: Shaoyun Guan, Xuena Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe task of knowledge graph completion (KGC) is to infer missing links that exist between entities in knowledge graphs (KGs). However, real-world KGs are typically structurally sparse, resulting in insufficient neighborhood context and thus degraded KGC performance. Meanwhile, most existing methods adopt coarse-grained relation modeling and overlook meaningful semantic representations of relations. To tackle these issues, we propose relation-aware context aggregation (RACA) for sparse KGC. Specifically, we introduce a dual-mode relational learning mechanism that jointly learns from both within-relation and across-relation perspectives, capturing task-relevant dimensional semantics within each relation and implicit logical regularities across relations. A spatial gating unit is further designed to integrate these relational signals and generate expressive relation representations. Finally, we develop a dynamic relation attention network that leverages relation-aware features to dynamically guide contextual aggregation, enabling more effective context capture under sparsity. Extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets show that RACA achieves superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines, particularly in sparse scenarios.Keywords: Knowledge graph completion, Sparse knowledge graph, Relation-awareSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Knowledge graphs (KGs) are widely adopted for knowledge modeling. They enable the description of numerous entities in the real world, along with their relations1. A KG consists of large-scale factual knowledge represented in the form of triples (s, r, o), where each triple denotes a fact (subject, relation, object). Such representations are essential for many downstream applications, including natural language processing2–5, intelligent question answering6,7 and recommendation systems8–10. Large-scale KGs are inherently incomplete, which adversely impacts the performance of downstream applications. Consequently, extensive research has focused on the task of automatically inferring missing facts and completing edges in KGs, which is formally defined as knowledge graph completion (KGC). Numerous approaches have been proposed to carry out the KGC task. Among them, representative knowledge graph embedding (KGE) methods11–13capture geometric properties in a latent space, whereas graph neural networks (GNNs) are employed to model graph structures and propagate information for path-based reasoning over knowledge graphs14–16.

Due to the scarcity of available corpora and the limitations of existing information extraction methods17, real-world knowledge graphs are often quite sparse18. Unfortunately, existing methods suffer significant performance degradation as the sparsity of the knowledge graph increases. As illustrated in Fig. 1, we simulate graph sparsity on the FB15k-23719dataset by randomly removing edges to construct subgraphs with different average entity degrees, namely FB-100%, FB-50%, FB-20%, and FB-10%. A higher average degree indicates denser connections among entities and thus less sparsity. It is worth noting that this random edge removal strategy serves as a controlled sparsity simulation, which allows us to systematically analyze the impact of graph density while minimizing confounding factors. We evaluate representative knowledge graph embedding (KGE) methods, including RotatE12, DistMult13, and ConvE20, as well as the graph neural network (GNN)-based model CompGCN14. The results show a clear decline in completion accuracy (using MRR) as the sparsity increases. Moreover, the predictive performance for sparse entities is significantly worse than that for frequent entities. This degradation arises from intrinsic limitations of the models. KGE methods depend on sufficient observed triples for embedding learning, which becomes unreliable in sparse graphs16. GNN-based approaches capture higher-order information through message passing but may suffer from over-smoothing or information loss in the stack GNN layers, degrading prediction accuracy21. Although recent sparse KGC methods have attempted to alleviate the sparsity issue through reinforcement learning-based path expansion, auxiliary edge augmentation, or long-range dependency modeling, most of them primarily focus on enhancing entity representations or structural reasoning. The fine-grained semantic modeling of relations remains insufficiently explored. In sparse KGs, entity neighborhoods are usually limited, making relation semantics an important source of transferable and task-relevant information. Therefore, how to explicitly capture both the dependencies among different relations and the internal semantic variations within each relation remains a key challenge.

KGC performance (measured by MRR) of various models on FB15K-237 and its sparse subsets (with 50%, 20%, and 10% of edges removed). The results demonstrate a consistent degradation in KGC performance as graph sparsity increases.

In this work, we propose relation-aware context aggregation (RACA) for sparse knowledge graph completion. We introduce a dual-mode relational learning mechanism to alleviate the issue of there being insufficient neighborhood information in sparse graphs by exploiting fine-grained relational semantics. Different from existing sparse KGC methods that mainly rely on path expansion or structural densification, RACA explicitly strengthens relation representations and uses them to guide entity context aggregation. Specifically, relation interaction learning generalizes transferable logical rule patterns across relations while attenuating task-irrelevant links. Concurrently, relation self-enhancement learning focuses on disentangling the heterogeneous intra-relation semantics, enabling an emphasis on task-relevant semantic dimensions. Furthermore, we develop a dynamic relation attention network to leverage relation features to dynamically guide context aggregation. By dynamically adjusting the attention weights according to different relational links, the semantic context can be captured more effectively, particularly in sparse KGs. Extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets show the superiority of our proposed RACA model to competitive state-of-the-art models.

Related work

In this section, we briefly review knowledge graph embedding (KGE) models, graph neural network (GNN) and sparse KGC models for the KGC task, which facilitates understanding of the subsequent methods section.

KGE based models

KGE models learn low-dimensional representations of entities and relations in a latent space, where their relations are modeled through geometric operations. Following the pioneering work TransE11, many translational models have been developed, including RotatE12, which models relations as rotations in a complex latent space22, enabling the effective representation of various relational patterns. In addition, TuckER23 employs a three-way Tucker tensor decomposition to learn embeddings of entities and relations. Tensor decomposition-based models like DistMult13 introduce a bilinear formulation to jointly represent entities and relations in the embedding space. ComplEx22 generalizes the DistMult model13 to complex-valued embeddings, enabling the learning of more expressive representations. ConvE20 employs convolutional neural networks to extract patterns in entity-relation interactions. Although these models are simple and intuitive, they are sensitive to any sparsity of the data and lack sufficient modeling capacity.

Recent studies24,25 have also investigated incomplete structure recovery in graph-related data from the perspective of tensorized representation learning and incomplete multi-view clustering. These methods effectively enhance structure recovery by capturing intra-view correlations and inter-view consistency, mitigating noise and redundancy through graph refinement, and adaptively balancing the contributions of different views or samples via fine-grained or self-weighted fusion strategies. Nevertheless, these approaches mainly focus on incomplete multi-view clustering, where the goal is to recover missing samples, improve feature graphs, or learn consensus representations across different views. In contrast, sparse knowledge graph completion aims to infer missing relational facts among entities under limited neighborhood information. Therefore, we propose RACA to model fine-grained relation semantics, aiming to alleviate the challenges of knowledge graph completion under sparse scenarios.

GNN based models

GNN-based approaches exploit a message-passing mechanism to gather structural information from local neighborhoods. SACN26 uses graph convolution to aggregate local neighborhood information and convolutional decoding to model entity–relation interactions. NBFNet15 and employ GNNs to perform knowledge graph reasoning by progressively propagating query information across the graph. However, these methods learn graph embeddings based primarily on the connectivity between the entities, making them sensitive to sparsity. Recent studies have also explored relation-enhanced graph neural networks to better model multi-relational structures. For example, CompGCN14 jointly embeds entities and relations by leveraging various composition operations, enabling more effective relational message passing. Similarly, RED-GNN27 introduces relation-aware mechanisms to improve reasoning over relational paths. These approaches improve the modeling of relations to some extent. However, they typically focus on relation composition or structural integration, without explicitly distinguishing inter-relation dependencies and intra-relation semantic variations.

Recent works28–32 in graph-based learning have further explored expressive modeling techniques for complex and incomplete relational data. For instance, heterogeneous information network models integrate multi-source biological interactions to enhance representation learning through rich structural context. Geometric deep learning frameworks exploit the non-Euclidean nature of graph data to learn more informative embeddings with attention mechanisms. In addition, multi-perspective representation learning methods incorporate diverse structural and feature-level information, such as spatial, topological, and semantic cues, to improve predictive performance. These approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of incorporating heterogeneous information, geometric structures, and high-order patterns for improving representation learning in graph-based tasks. However, they are mainly designed for domains such as biomedical interaction prediction, drug discovery, or control systems. In contrast, sparse knowledge graph completion focuses on inferring missing relational facts under limited neighborhood information. Therefore, we propose RACA to model fine-grained relation semantics and perform relation-aware context aggregation, aiming to alleviate the challenges of knowledge graph completion in sparse scenarios.

Sparse KGC models

Recently, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of sparsity. DacKGR33 extends the action space of reinforcement learning–based reasoning by incorporating a pre-trained KGE model. KRACL34 employs attention-based message aggregation while using contrastive learning to improve the semantic representation of the entities. LR-GCN16 employs a reasoning path extractor to model multi-hop dependencies and jointly trains it with a GNN-based predictor using a variational EM framework. However, models lack any fine-grained modeling of the relations, which limits their expressive capacity.

Overview

A knowledge graph can be formally represented as , where , , and correspond to the sets of entities, relations, and triples, respectively. A triple represents a factual statement in which the subject entity s is connected to the object entity o through the relation r, with . The task of knowledge graph completion is to exploit the existing facts in a knowledge graph to infer missing ones. In practice, this task can be viewed as identifying the missing entity in incomplete triples such as (s, r,?) or (?, r, o). The objective of our model is to assign larger scores to positive triples (s, r, o), while giving lower scores to negative triples, including (s',r,o) and (s,r,o'). We follow the standard setting of knowledge graph completion as a ranking-based link prediction task, where the objective is to rank the correct entity among candidate entities.

Dual-mode relational learning mechanism

Most knowledge graph completion algorithms focus on learning accurate representations of the entities. For instance, graph neural network-based methods typically rely on message-passing mechanisms to update these representations by aggregating embedding information from neighboring entities. However, in sparse knowledge graphs, the number of connected neighboring entities is significantly reduced, limiting the effectiveness of neighborhood-based information propagation methods, which in turn degrades their representational capacity and prediction performance. To alleviate these problems, we propose a dual-mode relation learning mechanism. Relation interaction learning and relation self-enhancement learning focus on different aspects of relation modeling. Specifically, relation interaction learning captures inter-relation dependencies by modeling high-order interactions among different relations, enabling the discovery of implicit relational patterns and logical rules. In contrast, relation self-enhancement learning focuses on intra-relation semantic refinement by adaptively emphasizing different semantic dimensions within a single relation, thereby improving its expressiveness under varying contexts. As illustrated in Fig. 3a, relation interaction learning aims to capture dependencies among relations. For example, when predicting whether a spouse relation exists between Bill Gates and Melinda Gates, if the model can capture that the spouse relation is closely correlated with the relation path son-of → father-in-law, the prediction confidence can be significantly improved. Meanwhile, as shown in Fig. 3b, relation self-enhancement learning focuses on refining the internal semantics of a relation. For instance, the relation HasJobPosition exhibits multiple semantic dimensions, such as a political dimension and a military dimension. Emphasizing the task-relevant semantic dimension (e.g., the political aspect) can facilitate the correct prediction of triples such as (George Bush, Agent Controls, White House).

Compared with existing relation modeling methods, which typically either treat relations as auxiliary features in entity-centric message passing or model relations independently without explicitly capturing both inter-relation interactions and intra-relation semantic variations, the proposed dual-mode relational learning mechanism jointly models both aspects. This complementary design enables more expressive relation representations, particularly in sparse knowledge graphs where relational information plays a more critical role.

Relation interaction learning

To capture implicit logical rules in high-order relational paths, relation interaction learning is introduced in conjunction with a spatial gating unit, enabling the model to emphasize effective links that are relevant to the target task while suppressing noisy ones. As illustrated in Fig. 3a, the entity Bill Gates is connected to William Henry Gates II via the relation sons_of, while William Henry Gates II is connected to Melinda Gates through the relation father_in_law. Given a KGC task that aims to determine whether the triple (Bill Gates, spouse, Melinda Gates) holds, the logical rule learned through relation interaction, that Bill Gates is the son of William Henry Gates II and that William Henry Gates II is the father-in-law of Melinda Gates, can be leveraged to infer the spouse relation between Bill Gates and Melinda Gates. Meanwhile, noisy links such as (Bill Gates->friend->Paul Allen->acquaintance->Melinda Gates) and (Bill Gates->friend->Elon Musk->acquaintance->Paul Allen->acquaintance->Melinda Gates) should not be assigned high attention weights. Relation interaction learning is able to preserve the endogenous correlations among relations, without relying on external information. Even when an entity is associated with sparse neighboring entities, the learned relational rules can still be transferred and exploited by explicitly modeling these endogenous correlations. Evaluation metrics

We adopt mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and Hits@k as evaluation metrics. MRR measures overall the ranking quality by computing the average reciprocal rank of the correct entity among all candidates, where a higher value indicates that correct entities are ranked closer to the top. Hits@k measures the proportion of correct entities ranked within the top-k positions, reflecting the accuracy among the highest-ranked results. We adopt mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and Hits@k as evaluation metrics, following standard protocols in knowledge graph completion. These metrics evaluate the ranking quality of predicted entities rather than classification performance.

The proposed model achieves the best performance across all sparse datasets: it achieves average improvements of 2.73%, 5.04%, and 2.46% on MRR, Hits@1, and Hits@10, respectively, compared to state-of-the-art models. These results verify the superiority of the proposed approach in sparse knowledge graph settings. In contrast, most state-of-the-art models (e.g., TransE11 and ComplEx22) experience significant performance degradation under sparse conditions, underscoring both the challenges of sparse knowledge graph link prediction and the importance of addressing these challenges in real-world applications.

GNN–based models, such as RED-GNN27 and NBFNet15 generally outperform traditional embedding-based approaches. Conventional methods typically represent entities and relations as low-dimensional embeddings and perform reasoning by measuring embedding similarity, which limits their ability to fully exploit global structural information, particularly in sparse or relationally complicated graphs. In contrast, graph neural networks enable end-to-end information propagation over the graph. By modeling entities as nodes and relations as edges, these methods capture inter-node dependencies and iteratively refine representations through multi-hop message passing, aggregating global information to produce more accurate and informative entity representations.

Conclusion and future work

Faced with two challenges in sparse knowledge graph completion, severe neighborhood information deficiency and coarse-grained relational modeling, we have proposed a novel framework, relation-aware context aggregation (RACA). Specifically, we have introduced a dual-mode relational learning mechanism to enable fine-grained relational modeling by capturing implicit logical rule patterns in relation interactions and highlighting task-relevant semantic dimensions. In addition, to alleviate the information insufficiency caused by sparse neighborhoods, we have designed a dynamic relation attention network (DRAN) that leverages relation-aware features to dynamically guide context aggregation, thereby boosting the model’s understanding of entities when the connectivity is sparse. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate that RACA consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, particularly in the presence of sparsity.

In the future, we plan to explore interpretable relational reasoning frameworks to provide explicit explanations for KGC, enhancing the transparency and reliability of the model. We will also investigate more realistic sparsification strategies, such as degree-based pruning, to further validate the generalization capability of the proposed model under practical scenarios.

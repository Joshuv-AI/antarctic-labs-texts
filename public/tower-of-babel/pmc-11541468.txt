Physicochemical Properties of Aqueous Film-Forming Foams Based on Short Fluorocarbon Surfactant-Graft Hydrophilic Functional Groups
Authors: Yuqiang Zhang, Shanjin Zeng, Xinhua Zhu, Xuhong Jia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The key components of aqueous film foaming foam extinguishing agents are fluorocarbon surfactants. Due to their toxicity and bioaccumulation, long-chain fluorocarbon surfactants have limited application prospects. Therefore, it is of great significance to study short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants as substitutes. In this work, three short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants with various hydrophilic groups and corresponding fire extinguishing agents were prepared. The physical and chemical properties of a single system and mixed system of fluorocarbon surfactants, including surface activity, spreading property, foaming property, 25% drainage time, and AFFF fire extinguishing property, were studied. For the results of the mixed systems, it was found that the critical micelle concentrations did not exceed 3.97 mmol/L and the minimum surface tension did not exceed 17.21 mN/m. Fluorocarbon surfactant solutions spread effectively on the surface of all fuels. The foam expansion is greater than 7.2, and the shortest of all 25% drainage times is 3.31 min. The extinguishing time of AFFFs on several fuel fires does not exceed 55 s, and the shortest burn-back time is 15.08 min. Anyways, these results indicate that the grafted hydrophilic short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants have significant application prospects in AFFFs.

Introduction

Aqueous film-forming foams (AFFFs) are recommended by the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) and the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) as effective extinguishing agents for flammable liquid fuel fires. AFFF exhibits surface-tension-reducing properties and spreads quickly over hydrocarbon fuels. It cools the liquid fuel by creating a water film beneath the foam, thereby enhancing its firefighting effectiveness.1,2 The key ingredients in AFFF are fluorocarbon surfactants, which have unique surface activity, high thermal stability, and high chemical stability, making them generally more advantageous than hydrocarbon compounds in many applications.3−6 Fluorocarbon surfactants contain fluorocarbon chains with hydrophobic and oleophobic properties, as well as hydrophilic functional groups. Mainly, fluorocarbon surfactants are made from perfluorooctanesulfonate (PFOS) and/or perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA).7,8 PFOS and PFOA are resistant to degradation at environment temperatures and are not readily eliminated by conventional drinking water and wastewater treatment methods.9−12 These man-made fluorinated surfactants and nonstick compounds pose significant risks to human and environmental health due to their extended half-lives13 and associated adverse outcomes.14,15 In 2009, the Stockholm Convention classified PFOS as a persistent organic pollutant (POP). Some countries, including the United States and Germany, have issued guidance values for PFOS and PFOA due to concerns about their toxicity.16 As a result, it is essential to substitute long-chain fluorocarbon surfactants in AFFF while maintaining its fire extinguishing performance.

As far as alternatives are concerned, much research is devoted to the development of short-carbon chain fluorocarbon surfactants and fluorine-free surfactants. However, although fluorine-free foams show better environmental protection, they show relatively poor surface activity when applied to AFFF. Yu et al.17 investigated the foam stability of fluorine-free and fluorine-containing foams and conducted experiments on the interaction with oil. Research has found that the fluorinated foam film exhibits greater stability compared to the fluorine-free foam film when in contact with an oil drop. Fluorine-free foam also shows poor fire extinguishing performance. Martin et al.18 demonstrated that the bioconcentration of perfluoroalkyl and polyfluoroalkyl species is associated with the length of the fluorocarbon chain. The bioconcentration coefficient of carboxyl compounds is more significant in the range of 8 to 12 fluorocarbon chain length. When the chain length is less than 7, the bioconcentration coefficient is in the acceptable range.19 Due to their toxicity and bioaccumulation, long-chain fluorocarbon surfactants have limited application prospects. However, short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants exhibit good surface activity while significantly reducing these adverse effects, which makes them a promising alternative. Pizzurro et al.20 found that short-chain perfluoroalkyl substances are typically less potent than their long-chain counterparts, partly due to their faster excretion rates. Han et al.21 examined the correlation between carboxyl compounds with fluorocarbon chains (ranging from 4 to 10 chains) and their total renal clearance values. Their findings indicated that total renal clearance of such compounds increased as the chain length decreased with the exception of perfluorobutanoate. Our previous study found that the toxicity of the short chain fluorocarbon surfactants synthesized in the study is lower than that of long-chain fluorinated surfactants.22 In this context, many researchers have conducted research targeting the synthesis of the surfactants with short fluorocarbon chain as an alternative.

At present, short chain fluorocarbon surfactants have been applied in commercial products and have shown good surface activity. Perfluorinated short carbon chain surfactants represent a novel category of fluorocarbon surfactants. 6:2 fluorotelomer sulfonamide alkylbetaine, C6F13C2H4SO2NHC3H6N+(CH3)2CH2COO–, and 6:2 fluorotelomer sulfonamide amine oxide, C6F13C2H4SO2NHC3H6N+(O–)(CH3)2, have been used in industrial fluorinated surfactants Forafac 1157 and Forafac 1183.23,24 A C4–-based fluorinated compound, C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3NH(CH3)2+ has also been prepared for use in fluorinated surfactants within firefighting foam applications.25 Sheng et al.26 investigated the foam properties and coalescence of nanomaterials for mixtures containing fluorocarbon surfactants. Jia et al.27 prepared a four-carbon short-chain fluorocarbon surfactant using perfluorinated butyl sulfonyl fluoride as the raw material, and the addition of this surfactant showed rapid fire-fighting ability against various fuels. The spreading properties of short carbon chain fluorocarbon surfactants were also investigated, indicating that the water film containing the above surfactants formed a good spreading process on the fuel surface.28 In the previous research, the properties of fire fighting foam containing a series of short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants were studied. Grafting hydrophilic groups or changing the length of hydrophobic chains in surfactants will affect the surface activity and then affect the fire extinguishing performance. Thus, it is of great interest and significance to develop novel short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants by changing the hydrophilic end functional groups in order to enhance the physicochemical properties and fire extinguishing performance.

In the present study, we synthesized a series of short fluorocarbon chain surfactants ([C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N+(CH3)2–R], R = CH2COO–, CH2CH2SO3–, and CH2CH2P(OH)O2–) with C4F9SO2F as the terminal hydrophobic group, while grafted with different acidic hydrophilic groups. The structures of these surfactants were characterized by FT-IR, NMR, and MS. Moreover, the influence of different acid functional groups on the physicochemical characteristics of betaine fluorocarbon amphoteric surfactants were studied. This study can provide a theoretical basis for the application of short fluorocarbon chain betaine surfactants.

Experimental Section

Materials

3-Bromoacetic acid (98%), 2-bromoethanesulfonic acid (95%), and (2-bromoethyl)phosphonic acid (95%) were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Other reagents used in the preparation of surfactants are previously described.19 The reagents used in AFFF fire extinguishing agent compounding are all referred to the literature.22

Synthesis of Betaine Fluorocarbon Surfactants C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N+(CH3)2–R

Scheme 1 shows that short fluorocarbon chain betaine amphoteric surfactants C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N+(CH3)2–R were synthesized in two steps. In the first step, triethylamine and 3-(dimethylamino)-1-propylamine were mixed in a round-bottom flask filled with nitrogen in a molar ratio of 1:1, with methylene chloride as the solvent. Perfluorobutanesulfonyl fluoride was then slowly added with ice bath conditions in an amount of 0.85 times that of 3-(dimethylamino)-1-propylamine. After completion of the drip process, the reaction solution was further stirred at 25 °C for 6 h. Subsequently, the white solid precipitate was the intermediate compound. The intermediate C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N(CH3)2 was prepared. In the second step, the separated intermediate is added to a flask of anhydrous ethanol and sodium hydroxide in a nitrogen atmosphere. Then, bromoacetic acid (2-bromoethanesulfonic acid or (2-bromoethyl) phosphonic acid), 1.5 times the molar amount of the intermediate, was slowly dropwise added to the reaction solution through a constant pressure drop funnel at 80–120 °C. Then, the target products [C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N+(CH3)2CH2COO–] (C4-Ac), [C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N+(CH3)2CH2CH2SO3–] (C4-sa), and [C4F9SO2NH(CH2)3N+(CH3)2CH2PO(OH)O–] (C4-pa) were crystallized in ice ether.

Characterization

The molecular structures of surfactants were characterized by using infrared spectroscopy (IR, Bruker VERTEX70, Germany), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR, Bruker AVANCE III-400 MHz, Germany), and mass spectrometry (MS, Waters SQD2, USA). IR spectra were recorded by using a DTGS detector and a KBr pellet. The wavenumber of 16 scans was accumulated in the range of 4000 to 400 cm–1. The 13C NMR (100 MHz), 1H NMR (400 MHz), and 19F NMR (376 MHz) spectra were recorded at 20 ± 2 °C and 50 ± 5% RH. 1H, 13C and 19F chemical shifts (ppm) were determined by the internal standard method with CD3OD as the solvent.

Foam Properties and AFFF Formulations

The surface tension and the oil–water interfacial tension of all of the surfactant solutions were measured with the hanging plate method by using the tensiometer with the model of A601. A series of concentration gradient solutions of three betaine fluorocarbon surfactants were prepared before the experiment, in which the concentrations of the single system (C4-Ac, C4-sa, and C4-pa) were 0.040–40 mmol/L and the concentrations of the mixture system solution (C4–Ac/SHS, C4-sa/SHS and C4-pa/SHS) were 0.012–25 mmol/L.

The methods for determining foam expansion and 25% drainage time (t25%) of solutions are provided in the literature.29 The solution is foamed by violent shaking a special, sealable measuring cylinder containing a surfactant solution. The recorded volume of the drained liquid is 0.25 times that of the initial solution volume, which corresponds to a drainage time of 25%. The concentrations of mixture system solutions were 7.94, 5.48, and 6.62 mmol/L, respectively. The prepared surfactant solutions (10 mL) were added into a 150 mL special measuring cylinder, and the foam height was recorded after oscillating for 60 s. At the same time, a 25% drainage time was also recorded.

The fire extinguishing performance experiments were evaluated on the basis of the Chinese standard GB 15308-2006. The aviation kerosene, cyclohexane, or solvent naphtha was added to the oil pan of 0.25 m2. The oil pan containing the fuel was placed in the combustion chamber. AFFF solution (prepared by the three surfactants mentioned above) is sprayed at 750 ± 25 g/min after 60 ± 5 s preburning of fuel. The time from AFFF solution spraying to complete fire suppression was recorded. When the flame is completely extinguished, spraying of AFFF was continued for 180 ± 2 s to form a foam layer, and then a 120 ± 2 mm diameter combustion tank was placed at the center of the oil pan and 1 ± 0.1 L of fuel was ignited. The time in the combustion tank was recorded when the fuel is ignited until the fuel is completely burned out; that is, the backfiring time.

Surface Activity of the Surfactant

The efficiency of a surfactant is an indication of its surface activity. When the surfactant solution reaches a certain concentration, the surface tension of the water decreases significantly. The efficiency of the surfactant is represented by the minimum reduction of surface tension.30 Woodman et al.31 concluded that the interfacial tension between AFFF solution and fuel should not be more than 5 mN/m. The surface tension of surfactant solution should be less than 20 mN/m, which may spread over the hydrocarbon fuels.32 Although high concentrations of fluorocarbon surfactants can reduce the surface tension well, CMC should be considered to save costs.33,34 CMC characterizes the efficiency of the surfactant, and the minimum surface tension (γCMC) characterizes the effectiveness of the surfactant. In addition, many surfactant systems have been extensively studied by researchers due to the coupling effect between some surfactants to enhance surface activity.35 Siddiqui et al.36 investigated the impact of a binary mixture of Gemini and conventional surfactants on the CMC and observed a pronounced synergistic effect between cationic and anionic conventional surfactants, attributed to their strong Coulombic attraction. The addition of other hydrocarbon surfactants to fluorocarbon surfactants has certain advantages. On one hand, there is a synergy between the two types of surfactants. On the other hand, it can also reduce the amount of fluorocarbon surfactants, thereby reducing environmental pollution and product costs. Therefore, sodium hexanesulfonate was selected as the hydrocarbon surfactant in the complex system. The parameters of the surface activity of the mixture systems were measured.

The surface tension isotherm of three kinds of fluorocarbon surfactant complex solutions grafted with different hydrophilic groups is shown in Figure 1. The result shows that the surface tension of short carbon chain fluorocarbon surfactants decreases with increasing concentration. When the surface tension decreases and reaches an inflection point, the corresponding concentration is CMC. The difference of hydrophilic functional groups resulted in the difference in the γCMC. The surface tension of C4–Ac is relatively high when the solution concentration is large enough. Surfactants of C4-sa and C4-pa have low surface tension, with the latter slightly higher than the former. Among them, the CMC of C4-sa and C4-pa are lower than C4–Ac because the sulfonic acid group and phosphonic acid group are larger than the carboxyl group in terms of the structure of the functional group. The sizes of the sulfonic acid group and phosphonic acid group are close to each other, resulting in similar CMCs of 2.71 mmol/L and 2.80 mmol/L, respectively. It can be explained that surfactant molecules with larger hydrophilic functional groups have larger cross-sectional areas and thus occupy a larger surface area at the adsorption interface.37 The ability of surfactants to form micelles is greater and the corresponding CMC is lower.

In summary, these fluorocarbon surfactants are expected to be applied to AFFF, and other physicochemical properties need to be further studied.

Spreading Performance

It is well-known that the spreading coefficient (S) is the prerequisite for the spreading of AFFF across the surface of fuel. Nevertheless, the spreading is not a purely static process, which is also affected by inertia, gravity, viscous resistance, and other dynamic factors.43 However, it has also been reported that many systems with positive spreading coefficients fail to spread at the oil surface.44 This is due to the presence of the nonequilibrium surface tension in the aqueous phase. The surface tension changes dynamically in the process of reaching equilibrium. After a period of time, this value gradually approximates the surface tension at equilibrium of the surfactant solution. The typical time scale for surfactants to reach surface tension equilibrium is seconds to minutes, and the time for AFFF to be ejected from the nozzle (less than 1 s) is less than that time scale. Therefore, it is not reliable to judge the spreading performance simply by the S value of the equilibrium state. He et al.45 considered the observation method to determine the spreading ability of surfactant solution based on the S value.

Foam Expansion and Drainage Time

The performance of the foam has a significant influence on the fire extinguishing effect of AFFF. As foam expansion increases, the average density of the foam decreases, facilitating its ability to cover the fuel surface more effectively. The insulating layer formed on the surface effectively insulates the contact between the fuel and air, that is, the asphyxiation effect. At the same time, foam drainage can effectively compensate for the loss of the aqueous film on the surface of fuel, thereby enabling the surfactant solution to spread stably on the surface.47 In addition, the surfactant solution evaporates water vapor at the high temperature of the flame, absorbing heat and also reducing the oxygen concentration above the flame. However, if the foam exhibits inadequate fluidity and drainage characteristics, achieving optimal fire extinguishing performance may prove challenging, despite a high expansion factor. Therefore, foam expansion and 25% drainage time are essential parameters that affect foam performance and fire extinguishing ability.

The key parameters (foam expansion and 25% drainage time) of the foaming properties of three surfactant mixture systems were determined. Figure 5 shows the foam expansion of three mixture systems, the highest of which can reach 7.8. The maximum 25% drainage time is 3.50 min. In order to ensure the effectiveness of fire suppression, the foam expansion and drainage times of AFFF are specified. The U.S. Military Specification (MIL-F-24385) stipulates that the foaming ratio must exceed 6 and the time for 25% drainage must be greater than 2.5 min.48 Another organization, the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA), requires foaming ratio to be no less than 5 and 25% drainage time of no less than 2.25 min.49 The range of foam expansion values (7.2–7.8) and drainage time determined in this study met the minimum requirements for AFFF. The result shows that the foam expansion performance of surfactants containing the sulfonic acid group and phosphoric acid group is better than that of surfactants containing the carboxyl group. Probably due to the more oxygen atoms in the sulfonic and phosphoric acid groups, the stronger intermolecular cohesion enhances the ability to form micelles, thus making the foam easier to expand. It also indicates that the foam expansion properties of the surfactants in this study are consistent with the trend of surface activity. Figure 6 illustrates the morphology of the foams. Extinguishing Performance

The extinguishing performance of the three AFFFs (AFFFs-Ac, AFFFs-sa, and AFFFs-pa) was investigated. Fires caused by cyclohexane, solvent naphtha, and aviation kerosene were extinguished by using the three AFFFs. Figure 7 illustrates the extinguishing process of AFFFs-Ac on burning cyclohexane, and the same trend is also observed for other fire fighting foam extinguishing agents. As shown in Figure 7, the cyclohexane is ignited and preburned for 60 s, and then, AFFFs-Ac is injected into the oil pan. Foam is sprayed toward the bottom of the flame and covers the surface of the cyclohexane, after which the flame is gradually controlled. The fire quickly subsided as foam covered the entire surface of the fuel. It took 57 s for the flames to be completely extinguished. Table 5 shows the fire extinguishing time and burn-back time of the three fire extinguishing agents for different fuel fires. According to the GB 15308-2006 standard, for AFFF with strong release, the most suitable fire extinguishing time is required to be not more than 180 s, and the burn back time is required to be not less than 10 min. In addition, the International Civil Aviation Organization requires AFFF to extinguish kerosene fires with an extinguishing time of less than 60 s. According to the results of Table 5, the fire extinguishing time of the three AFFFs for various hydrocarbon fuels was not more than 57 s and the burn-back time was not less than 15.08 min. Moreover, fluorocarbon surfactants with a sulfonic acid group and a phosphoric acid group have similar effects on the fire extinguishing performance of AFFF and have better positive effects. The fire extinguishing properties of the surfactant correspond to surface activity, spreading properties, and foaming properties, indicating that the fire extinguishing efficiency mainly hinges on the physicochemical properties and synergistic effects of fluorocarbon and hydrocarbon surfactants in the fire extinguishing agent. Therefore, the key parameters of fire extinguishing performance of the three AFFFs meet the above standards, indicating that the application of short carbon chain fluorocarbon surfactants in AFFF has excellent prospects.

Conclusion

In this paper, a series of novel betaine fluorocarbon surfactants were synthesized by using the hydrophilic group of oxyacid as the hydrophilic terminal, and their structures were characterized by FTIR, NMR, and MS. The surface activity, spreading property, and foaming property of the three surfactants and their mixture systems were investigated. The results showed that the sulfonic acid group was slightly superior to the phosphoric acid group and carboxylic acid group in physicochemical properties of the mixed system. The performance trend of the subsequent research applied to the AFFFs for fire suppression and fire return was also consistent with the results above. The results indicated that the properties of AFFFs were mainly determined by the properties of fluorocarbon surfactants contained in the AFFFs. Among them, C4-sa/SHS has the best physicochemical properties in a mixture system of surfactants. The mixed surfactant system had a minimum surface tension of 15.52 mN/m at a CMC of 2.74 mmol/L. Similarly, C4-sa/SHS showed excellent foaming performance, characterized by a foam expansion ratio of 7.8 and a drainage time of 3.31 min. Moreover, AFFFs-sa demonstrated a fire extinguishing time of less than 51 s and a burn-back time exceeding 16.13 min. The three kinds of betaine fluorocarbon surfactants showed good physicochemical properties and an extinguishing performance. The experimental results align with the Chinese standard GB 15308-2006 as well as the standards set by NFPA, indicating that the betaine short-chain fluorocarbon surfactants developed in this study could function as viable alternatives to PFOS for formulating AFFFs.

Abstract
. We study bounded domains Ω ⊂ ℂ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} whose Bergman metric is locally symmetric, i.e. its Riemannian curvature tensor is parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection. Following the strategy developed in [ 7 ] , we obtain two rigidity results. If the Bergman metric of Ω \Omega is complete, then Ω \Omega is (globally) symmetric. If instead Ω \Omega is pseudoconvex, then Ω \Omega is biholomorphic to Ω ~ ∖ E \widetilde{\Omega}\setminus E , where Ω ~ ⊂ ℂ n \widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} is a bounded symmetric domain and E ⊂ Ω ~ E\subset\widetilde{\Omega} is relatively closed and pluripolar. The proofs combine the structure theory of Hermitian symmetric spaces with Calabi’s theory of Kähler immersions into the infinite dimensional complex projective space (in particular, rigidity and the hereditary property of the diastasis), together with analytic and pluripotential tools based on extension properties of square-integrable holomorphic functions and the Bergman kernel.

1.
Introduction

The Bergman kernel and the Bergman metric were introduced by S. Bergman in 1922 (see
[
1
]
) and have since been intensively studied, as they provide rich analytic and geometric information on complex manifolds. In this paper, we focus on open and connected subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, which we call domains. Recall that, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the associated Bergman space is the Hilbert space of holomorphic square-integrable functions

\displaystyle A^{2}(\Omega)=\mathcal{O}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega).

If
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
is a complete orthonormal system for
A^{2}(\Omega)
, the
Bergman kernel
of
\Omega
is given by the expansion

(1)

K_{\Omega}(z,\xi)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\ \phi_{\alpha}(z)\overline{\phi_{\alpha}(\xi)}.

Remarkably,
K_{\Omega}(\cdot,\xi)
satisfies the reproducing property

(2)

\varphi(\xi)=\langle\varphi,K_{\Omega}(\cdot,\xi)\rangle_{A^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in A^{2}(\Omega).

The Bergman kernel is closely related to the holomorphic and geometric structure of
\Omega
. It is well known that

\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}),\quad\Phi(z):=\log K_{\Omega}(z,z),

is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\Omega
. Hence

(3)

\omega_{\Omega}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi

defines a Kähler form on
\Omega
, and the associated Kähler metric
g_{\Omega}
, which is a biholomorphic invariant, is called the
Bergman metric
of
\Omega
.

The basic example is the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. If
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, one computes

\displaystyle K_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(z,z)=\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\frac{1}{(1-||z||^{2})^{n+1}}\ ,\quad\omega_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=(n+1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(1-||z||^{2})

In particular,
g_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
has constant holomorphic sectional curvature
-\frac{4}{n+1}
, as it differs only by a factor from the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.
The interested reader can refer to
[
11
]
for a detailed exposition on this topic.

The studies accomplished so far highlight how
g_{\Omega}
encodes the intrinsic geometric behavior of
\Omega
. One key example of such results has been given by Q.-K. Lu in 1965 (see
[
15
]
), where he establishes that if
g_{\Omega}
is complete with constant holomorphic sectional curvature then
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. In 2025, X. Huang, S.-Y. Li and J. N. Treuer (see
[
7
]
) proved that if
g_{\Omega}
has constant holomorphic sectional curvature then it must be negative, and assuming pseudoconvexity instead of completeness, proved that
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with possibly a pluripolar set removed. Observe that this uniformization generalizes Lu’s one, as in 1955 Bremermann proved (see
[
2
]
) that if
g_{\Omega}
is complete then
\Omega
is pseudoconvex, while the converse is not true: the classical counterexample being the pseudoconvex domain obtained by removing a complex hyperplane from
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Starting from
[
7
]
, in 2025 P. Ebenfelt, J. N. Treuer and M. Xiao (see
[
5
]
) maintained only the curvature hypothesis and obtained that
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with possibly a set of zero Lebesgue measure removed, over which
A^{2}
functions extend holomorphically to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

The core matter of these results can be synthesized as follows: a local curvature condition on
g_{\Omega}
determines, up to biholomorphisms, the global shape of
\Omega
. It is then fitting to ask whether the same concept holds for other kinds of curvature conditions and shapes. Within this perspective, it is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
Theorem 7.9) that the negative constant holomorphic curvature condition characterizes
\mathbb{B}^{n}
in the class of simply-connected and complete Kähler manifolds, and remarkably (see
[
6
]
Ch. IV Theorem 5.6), the parallel Riemannian curvature condition characterizes Hermitian symmetric spaces in the same class. This suggests investigating whether the analogous local symmetry condition
\nabla R^{\Omega}=0
for the Bergman metric forces
\Omega
to be (essentially) a bounded symmetric domain. Alongside these uniformization-type results, recent works investigate finer Bergman-geometric features in settings closely related to bounded symmetric domains, such as metrics induced by the ball and the Bergman geometry of Cartan–Hartogs domains; see
[
16
,
12
]
and references therein.

Inspired by such parallelism, our aim in this work is to generalize the above mentioned results to the setting of bounded symmetric domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\text{Aut}(\Omega)
denote the group of biholomorphisms of
\Omega
onto itself. Recall that a domain
\Omega
is called
symmetric
if for every point
p\in\Omega
there exists an automorphism

f_{p}\in\text{Aut}(\Omega)
such that
f_{p}^{2}=\mathrm{id}
and
p
is an isolated fixed point of
f_{p}
.
We say that
\Omega
is
locally symmetric
if for every point
p\in\Omega
there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p

and an automorphism
f_{p}\in\text{Aut}(U)
such that
f_{p}^{2}=\mathrm{id}
and
p
is an isolated fixed point of
f_{p}
.
Equivalently (see
[
6
]
, Ch. IV, Theorem 2.1), if
\nabla
and
R^{\Omega}
denote the Levi–Civita connection
and the Riemannian curvature tensor of
g_{\Omega}
, then
\Omega
is locally symmetric if and only if
\nabla R^{\Omega}=0
.
We refer to
[
6
]
for further background on symmetric spaces.

In analogy with
[
15
]
and
[
7
]
, the main results of the present paper are as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, be a bounded domain. If
\nabla R^{\Omega}=0
and
g_{\Omega}
is complete, then
\Omega
is symmetric.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, be a bounded domain. If
\nabla R^{\Omega}=0
and
\Omega
is pseudoconvex, then there exists
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
bounded symmetric domain, ad
E\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
pluripolar and closed in
\widetilde{\Omega}
such that
\Omega\cong\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E
.

We follow the general strategy developed in
[
7
]
.
The main tools are Calabi’s theory of Kähler immersions into
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
, the structure theory of Hermitian symmetric spaces, and pluripotential methods based on analytic properties of the Bergman kernel.

Organization of the paper.

Section 2 is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
In the final part we include several remarks and we formulate a conjecture, inspired by the uniformization results in
[
5
]
, about removing the pseudoconvexity assumption.

2.
Proofs of the main results

In the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
, we need the following technical results. A striking difference is that, for the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, positive scalings of the Bergman metric are projectively induced, whereas for a general bounded symmetric domain, projective induced-ness holds only for scalings governed by the Wallach set; cf. Proposition
2.2
(A).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, be a bounded domain. If for
c\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
,
(\Omega,cg_{\Omega})
admits a Kähler immersion into
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
, then such immersion is injective.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a complete orthonormal system of
A^{2}(\Omega)
and consider the associated Bergman–Bochner map

(4)

\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}\colon(\Omega,g_{\Omega})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty},\qquad z\longmapsto[\phi_{1}(z):\phi_{2}(z):\ldots].

It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
Sec. 3.4) that
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}
is a full Kähler immersion.

Step 1:
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}
is injective.

Assume
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}(p)=\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}(q)
for some
p,q\in\Omega
.
Then there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
\phi_{j}(p)=\lambda\,\phi_{j}(q)
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
Let
K_{\Omega}
be the Bergman kernel of
\Omega
. Using the expansion (
1
)

K_{\Omega}(\cdot,p)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi_{j}(\cdot)\,\overline{\phi_{j}(p)}

we obtain

K_{\Omega}(\cdot,p)=\overline{\lambda}\,K_{\Omega}(\cdot,q).

By the reproducing property (
2
), for every
\varphi\in A^{2}(\Omega)
,

\varphi(p)=\langle\varphi,K_{\Omega}(\cdot,p)\rangle_{A^{2}(\Omega)}=\lambda\,\langle\varphi,K_{\Omega}(\cdot,q)\rangle_{A^{2}(\Omega)}=\lambda\,\varphi(q),

hence

(5)

\qquad\varphi(p)=\lambda\,\varphi(q),\quad\forall\,\varphi\in A^{2}(\Omega).

Since
\Omega
is bounded, every polynomial belongs to
A^{2}(\Omega)
; in particular, if
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
and
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
, there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
p_{k}\neq q_{k}
, so the holomorphic function
\varphi(z)=z_{k}-p_{k}
lies in
A^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies
\varphi(p)=0
while
\varphi(q)=q_{k}-p_{k}\neq 0
, contradicting (
5
). Therefore
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}
is injective.

Step 2: any Kähler immersion
(\Omega,cg_{\Omega})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is injective.

Let
F\colon(\Omega,cg_{\Omega})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
be a Kähler immersion. Recall that the diastasis of the Fubini–Study metric
g_{FS}
can be written in homogeneous coordinates

[Z],[W]\in\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
as

D_{FS}([Z],[W])=\log\frac{\langle Z,Z\rangle\,\langle W,W\rangle}{|\langle Z,W\rangle|^{2}}\geq 0,

and
D_{FS}([Z],[W])=0
if and only if
[Z]=[W]
(by equality in Cauchy–Schwarz). Moreover, by Calabi’s hereditary property of the diastasis (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
), the diastasis of
(\Omega,cg_{\Omega})
is given by the pullback of
D_{FS}
via
F
, i.e.

D^{c\Omega}(x,y)=D_{FS}\bigl(F(x),F(y)\bigr),

whenever the diastasis is defined. Assume
F(p)=F(q)
. Then
D_{FS}(F(p),F(q))=0
, hence
D^{c\Omega}(p,q)=0
. Since the diastasis scales linearly with the metric, i.e.
D^{c\Omega}=c\,D^{\Omega}
, we also have

D^{\Omega}(p,q)=0
. Again by Calabi’s hereditary property of the diastasis (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
), if
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}
is a Bergman–Bochner map as in (
4
), then

\displaystyle D_{FS}\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}(p),\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}(q)\bigr)=0,

which forces
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}(p)=\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}(q)
, and
p=q
. Hence
F
is injective.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, be a bounded symmetric domain. Then:

(A)

there exists a constant
\Gamma_{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that, for every
\delta>\Gamma_{\Omega}
,
the scaled Bergman manifold
(\Omega,\delta g_{\Omega})
admits a full and injective Kähler immersion into
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
;

(B)

the real analytic expansion at
0
of the diagonal Bergman kernel
z\mapsto K_{\Omega}(z,z)
contains no non-constant purely holomorphic terms and no non-constant purely antiholomorphic terms.

Proof.

Let
\Omega\simeq\Omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\Omega_{s}
be the decomposition of the bounded symmetric domain into irreducible factors. For every
k\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, denote by

•

W_{c}(\Omega_{k})
the continuous part of the Wallach set of
\Omega_{k}
;

•

\gamma_{k}
the genus of
\Omega_{k}
.

(A)

Set

\Gamma_{\Omega}:=\max_{1\leq k\leq s}\left\{\frac{\inf W_{c}(\Omega_{k})}{\gamma_{k}}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

If
\delta>\Gamma_{\Omega}
, then
\delta>\inf W_{c}(\Omega_{k})/\gamma_{k}
for every
k
, hence
\delta\,\gamma_{k}\in W_{c}(\Omega_{k})
for all
k
. By
[
13
, Theorem 2]
it follows that, for every

k=1,\dots,s
, the scaled Bergman manifold
(\Omega_{k},\delta g_{\Omega_{k}})
admits a
full
Kähler immersion

F_{k}\colon(\Omega_{k},\delta g_{\Omega_{k}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.

Consider the product immersion
F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{s}
and compose it with the (infinite-dimensional) Segre embedding to obtain a full Kähler immersion

F\colon(\Omega,\delta g_{\Omega})\cong(\Omega_{1},\delta g_{\Omega_{1}})\times\cdots\times(\Omega_{s},\delta g_{\Omega_{s}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, Proposition
2.1
implies that
any
such Kähler immersion is injective. This proves (A).

(B).

Fix
k\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and let
N_{\Omega_{k}}
be the generic norm of
\Omega_{k}
. It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Sec. 1.3]
) that on the diagonal one has

(6)

K_{\Omega_{k}}(z,z)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\Omega_{k})}\bigl(N_{\Omega_{k}}(z,\bar{z})\bigr)^{-\gamma_{k}},

and there are
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
polynomials of of bidegrees
(1,1),\dots,(r,r)
on
\Omega_{k}\times\text{conj}(\Omega_{k})
such that

(7)

N_{\Omega_{k}}(z,\bar{z})=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\,m_{\ell}(z,\bar{z}).

In particular, each
m_{\ell}(z,\bar{z})
is a sum of monomials involving
both

z
and
\bar{z}
, so the real analytic germ

H_{k}(z,\bar{z}):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\,m_{\ell}(z,\bar{z})

has no non-constant purely holomorphic terms (depending only on
z
) and no non-constant purely antiholomorphic terms (depending only on
\bar{z}
). Now observe that if
H(z,\bar{z})
has this property, then so does any power
H(z,\bar{z})^{q}
(
q\geq 1
), since products of mixed monomials are still mixed. Therefore, for any real
\alpha
, the binomial expansion

(1+H(z,\bar{z}))^{\alpha}=\sum_{q\geq 0}\binom{\alpha}{q}\,H(z,\bar{z})^{q}

cannot contain non-constant purely holomorphic or antiholomorphic terms.
Applying this with
\alpha=-\gamma_{k}
and using (
6
)–(
7
), we conclude that the real analytic expansion of
z\mapsto K_{\Omega_{k}}(z,z)
at
0

contains no non-constant purely holomorphic nor purely antiholomorphic terms. Finally, since the Bergman kernel factorizes on products, writing
z=(z^{(1)},\dots,z^{(s)})\in\Omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\Omega_{s}

K_{\Omega}(z,z)=\prod_{k=1}^{s}K_{\Omega_{k}}\bigl(z^{(k)},z^{(k)}\bigr),

hence the same conclusion holds for
K_{\Omega}(z,z)
. This proves (B).
∎

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon(X,\pi^{*}g_{\Omega})\to(\Omega,g_{\Omega})
be the universal covering. Since
g_{\Omega}
is complete and
\nabla R^{\Omega}=0
,
(X,\pi^{*}g_{\Omega})
is simply connected, complete and locally symmetric, hence a (globally) Hermitian symmetric space of
noncompact type. Therefore there exist a bounded symmetric domain
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a constant

\lambda>0
together with a biholomorphic isometry

\Psi\colon(X,\pi^{*}g_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(\widetilde{\Omega},\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})

(see
[
6
, Ch. IV, Theorem 5.6 and Ch. VIII, Theorem 7.1]
).
Choose an integer
k>\Gamma_{\widetilde{\Omega}}/\lambda
. By Proposition
2.2
(A), the scaled Bergman manifold
(\widetilde{\Omega},k\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})
admits a full and injective Kähler immersion

\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\colon(\widetilde{\Omega},k\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.

On the other hand, fix a complete orthonormal system
\mathcal{S}
of
A^{2}(\Omega)
and consider the Bergman–Bochner map
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}\colon(\Omega,g_{\Omega})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
given by (
4
). Composing with the (generalized) Veronese map
\mathcal{V}_{k}\colon\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
(so that
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{*}\omega_{FS}=k\omega_{FS}
), we obtain a full Kähler immersion

\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}:=\mathcal{V}_{k}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}\colon(\Omega,kg_{\Omega})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.

Pulling back via the covering and using the identification given by
\Psi
, we get a full Kähler immersion

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}:=\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}\circ\pi\circ\Psi^{-1}\colon(\widetilde{\Omega},k\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}.

By Calabi’s rigidity theorem
[
3
, Theorem 9]
, there exists a rigid motion
T
of
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}=T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}
.
Since
\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}
is injective, also
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
is injective; hence

\pi
must be injective. Therefore the universal covering is trivial and
\Omega
is biholomorphic to
\widetilde{\Omega}
. In particular,
\Omega
is symmetric.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Fix
p\in\Omega
. By
[
6
, Ch. IV, Theorem 5.1 and Ch. VIII, Theorem 7.1]
there exist
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
bounded symmetric domain,
U\subset\Omega
neighborhood of
p
,
V\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
open,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
and a biholomorphic isometry

\displaystyle f\colon(U,g_{\Omega}|_{U})\to(V,\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|_{V})

If
\iota\colon V\to\widetilde{\Omega}
is the inclusion, then
\iota\circ f\colon(U,g_{\Omega}|_{U})\to(\widetilde{\Omega},\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})
is a full Kähler immersion. Hence, if we pick
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\,k>\frac{\Gamma_{\widetilde{\Omega}}}{\lambda}
, by Proposition
2.2
(A) we have a full and injective Kähler immersion

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\colon(\widetilde{\Omega},k\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}

Then
\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\iota\circ f\colon(U,kg_{\Omega}|_{U})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is still a full Kähler immersion. Composing the (generalized) Veronese map
\mathcal{V}_{k}\colon\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
, which satisfies
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{*}(\omega_{FS})=k\omega_{FS}
, with a Bergman-Bochner map
\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}\colon(\Omega,g_{\Omega})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
given by (
4
), we have a full Kähler immersion

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}:=\mathcal{V}_{k}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\mathcal{S}}\colon(\Omega,kg_{\Omega})\to\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}

so by Calabi’s rigidity theorem
[
3
, Theorem 9]
there exists a rigid motion
T
of
\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\iota\circ f=T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}|_{U}

Let
q\in\Omega
and
\eta_{q}\colon[0,1]\to\Omega
be a simple continuous path with
\eta_{q}(0)=p,\ \eta_{q}(1)=q
. Repeatedly applying Calabi’s rigidity theorem
[
3
, Theorem 9]
, we find
A_{q}
connected neighborhood of
\eta_{q}([0,1])\cup U
and a Kähler immersion

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{q}\colon(A_{q},kg_{\Omega}|_{A_{q}})\to(\widetilde{\Omega},k\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}})

such that
\widetilde{f}_{q}|_{U}=\iota\circ f
. Then
T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}|_{U}=\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{f}_{q}|_{U}
, which by the identity principle for holomorphic functions yields

\displaystyle T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}|_{A_{q}}=\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{f}_{q}.

Furthermore, if
r\in\Omega\smallsetminus\{q\}
and
A_{q}\cap A_{r}\neq\emptyset
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{f}_{q}|_{A_{q}\cap A_{r}}=T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}|_{A_{q}\cap A_{r}}=\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{f}_{r}|_{A_{q}\cap A_{r}}

but
\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}
is injective, so
\widetilde{f}_{q}|_{A_{q}\cap A_{r}}=\widetilde{f}_{r}|_{A_{q}\cap A_{r}}
. Consequently, there is a well defined Kähler immersion

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\colon(\Omega,kg_{\Omega})\to(\widetilde{\Omega},k\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}}):\widetilde{f}(s):=\widetilde{f}_{s}(s)

and since by construction
T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}|_{U}=\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{f}|_{U}
, by the identity principle for holomorphic functions

\displaystyle T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}=\mathcal{B}^{\widetilde{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{f}.

In particular,
\widetilde{f}
is injective because
T\circ\mathcal{B}^{\Omega}
is injective, by Proposition
2.1
. By the open mapping theorem and the pseudoconvexity of
\Omega
,
W:=\widetilde{f}(\Omega)
is a pseudoconvex domain in
\widetilde{\Omega}
and

\widetilde{f}\colon(\Omega,g_{\Omega})\to(W,\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|_{W})

is a biholomorphic isometry. By the biholomorphic invariance of the Bergman metric (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Proposition 1.1.18]
), we also get

\displaystyle\lambda g_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|_{W}=\big(\widetilde{f}^{-1}\big)^{*}g_{\Omega}=g_{W}

which translates, taking Kähler potentials, to

(8)

\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\log(K_{W}(z,z))-\lambda\log(K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(z,z))\right)=0\ ,\quad z\in W.

Due to the transitivity of the action of
\text{Aut}(\widetilde{\Omega})
onto
\widetilde{\Omega}
, we can assume
0\in W
. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a complete orthonormal system for
A^{2}(W)
such that
\phi_{1}(0)>0
and
\phi_{j}(0)=0
, for all
j\geq 2
. Since
\phi_{1}\in\mathcal{O}(W)
, (
8
) results into

(9)

\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\log(K_{W}(z,z))-\log(|\phi_{1}(z)|^{2})-\lambda\log(K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(z,z))\right)=0\ ,\quad z\in W

that is,
z\mapsto\log(K_{W}(z,z))-\log(|\phi_{1}|^{2}(z))-\lambda\log(K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(z,z))
is pluriharmonic on
W
. Consequently there exist
\Lambda\subseteq W
neighborhood of
0
,
h\in\mathcal{O}(\Lambda),\ h=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 0}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
, such that for all
z\in\Lambda
,
\phi_{1}(z)\neq 0
and

(10)

\begin{split}\log(K_{W}(z,z))&-\log(|\phi_{1}(z)|^{2})-\lambda\log(K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(z,z))=2\Re(h(z))=\\
&=h(z)+\overline{h(z)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 0}\big(a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}+\overline{a_{\alpha}}\overline{z^{\alpha}}\big)\ ,\quad z\in\Lambda.\end{split}

We now show that
a_{\alpha}=0
, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\ |\alpha|\geq 1
. First, by the properties of the Bergman kernel:

2.3.
Final remarks

Remark 2.3
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, by
[
8
, Lemma 1]
, the properties of the Bergman kernel, and the fact that a pluripolar set has zero Lebesgue measure, the restriction of functions gives a Hilbert spaces isomorphism

\displaystyle A^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega})\to A^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E):\psi\mapsto\psi|_{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}

In particular
K_{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}=K_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|_{(\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E)\times(\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E)}
, which implies
g_{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}=g_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}
, i.e.
\lambda=1
.

Remark 2.4
.

The converse of Theorem
1.1
clearly holds, since the Bergman metric of a bounded symmetric domain (and in general, the metric of a symmetric space) is complete. A “converse” result of Theorem
1.2
is also true. To be precise, if
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded symmetric domain and
E\subset\Omega
is pluripolar and closed in
\Omega
, then
g_{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}=g_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|_{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}
as observed in Remark
2.3
, resulting into
\nabla R^{\widetilde{\Omega}\smallsetminus E}=0
.

Remark 2.5
.

With regards to Theorem
1.2
, we remark that the uniformization in
[
7
]
actually holds for a more general class of Kähler manifolds, that is, Stein manifolds such that their Bergman space

•

is nontrivial and base-point free;

•

separates points;

•

separates holomorphic dimensions.

This conditions ensure that the Bergman kernel and metric of the manifold are well-defined and that it is possible to define a Bergman-Bochner map, which turns out to be injective. Since a Stein Hermitian symmetric space is of noncompact type and the work of Calabi holds for real analytic Kähler manifolds, our methods can be applied to this more general setting. Thus, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
hold for Stein manifolds that satisfy the above mentioned conditions.

Remark 2.6
.

With regards to

]

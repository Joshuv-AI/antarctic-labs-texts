Abstract
. Given a finite group G G with an irreducible character χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) \chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G) , the codegree of χ \chi is defined by cod ( χ ) := | G : ker χ | / χ ( 1 ) {\mathrm{cod}}(\chi):=|G:\ker\chi|/\chi(1) . The set of non-linear irreducible character codegrees of G G is denoted by cod ⁡ ( G | G ′ ) {\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime}) . In this note, we classify all finite groups G G with | cod ⁡ ( G | G ′ ) | > 1 |{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|>1 and for each pair of distinct elements m , n ∈ cod ⁡ ( G | G ′ ) m,n\in{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime}) , m m and n n are coprime.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this note,
G
is a finite group and
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
is the set of all irreducible complex characters of
G
, and
\pi(G)
is the set of all prime divisors of
|G|
. Recall that
G
is said to be a
2
-
Frobenius group
, if
G
has a normal series
1<K<H<G
, such that
G/K
is a Frobenius group with
H/K
as the Frobenius kernel, and
K
is the Frobenius kernel of the Frobenius group
H
.

In
[
8
]
, the codegree of
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
is defined as
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G:\ker\chi|/\chi(1)
. Let
N
be a noraml subgroup of
G
. The set of irreducible characters of
G
whose kernel does not contain
N
is denoted by
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
, i.e.
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)={\mathrm{Irr}}(G)\setminus{\mathrm{Irr}}(G/N)
. Therefore,
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|G^{\prime})
is the set of non-linear irreducible characters of
G
.
Let
{\mathrm{cd}}(G|N)=\{\chi(1)\mid\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)\}
, and
{\mathrm{cod}}(G|N)=\{{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)\mid\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)\}
.

In
[
8
]
, the authors studied the graph
\Gamma(G|N)
, whose vertices are the prime divisors of the elements in
{\mathrm{cod}}(G|N)
, and two distinct primes
p
and
q
are adjacent if there exists some
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
, such that
pq\mid{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
. For simplicity,
\Gamma(G|G)
is denoted by
\Gamma(G)
.
They showed that
\Gamma(G)
is disconnected if and only if
G
is either a Frobenius group or a
2
-Frobenius group. It is natural to ask
if the disconnectedness of
\Gamma(G|G^{\prime})
results in the disconnectedness of
\Gamma(G)
. In general, the answer is negative and using GAP
[
4
]
, for
G=\text{SmallGroup}(480,1188)
, we see that
{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})=\{5,15,32\}
and
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)=\{2,3,5,6,15,32\}
, which yields that
\Gamma(G|G^{\prime})
is disconnected while
\Gamma(G)
is connected. In this note, we show that if
\Gamma(G|G^{\prime})
is disconnected and
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|=2
, then
\Gamma(G)
is disconnected. So, the answer to the above question is true when
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|=2
.

In
[
2
]
, non-nilpotent finite groups with
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G|G^{\prime})|=1
were determined. Analogously, in
[
9
]
, non-nilpotent groups with
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|=1
are classified. So, it is an interesting topic to liken the known results on degrees to codegrees. In
[
7
]
, the finite groups whose non-linear degrees are coprime and
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G|G^{\prime})|=2
were studied. We will see that our results classify all the finite groups whose non-linear codegrees are coprime and
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|=2
.

Reckon that
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|=2
and
\Gamma(G|G^{\prime})
is disconnected if and only if
{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})
consists of two coprime integers. Obviously, if
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|=m>1
consists of mutually coprime integers, then
\Gamma(G|G^{\prime})
has exactly
m
components, and by
[
8
, Theorem B]
, we get that
m=2
. So, we prove the following general theorem:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group. If
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|>1
and
{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})
consists of mutually coprime integers, then one of the following holds:

•

G
is a Frobenius group and
G\cong C_{p}^{k}\rtimes Q_{8}
, for some prime
p
, and integer
k
:

•

G
is a
2
-Frobenius group and if
1<K<H<G
is a normal series such that
G/K=H/K\rtimes R
and
H=K\rtimes J
are Frobenius groups with Frobenius complements
R\leq G/K
and
J\leq H
, respectively, then:

(1)

R
is cyclic;

(2)

J\cong C_{p}
, for some prime
p
;

(3)

K
is the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
;

(4)

For each
1\not=x\in K
,
C_{G}(x)/K\cong R_{0}
, where
R_{0}\leq R
and
\pi(R_{0})=\pi(R)
.

If

and
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
, then
\mathrm{I}_{G}(\theta)
denotes the inertia subgroup of
\theta
in
G
, and
{\rm Irr}(\theta^{G})
denotes the set of all irreducible constituents of
\theta^{G}
. For natural number

, where

is a prime,

and

are integers such that

and

. For the rest of the notations, we follow
[

]

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group. If
|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|\leq 2
, then

is solvable.

Proof.

On the contrary, assume that
G
is a counterexample of minimal order. If
G
is simple, then
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G)|=|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|+1\leq 3
, which contradicts

has a unique minimal normal subgroup, say

, and so
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|K)\subseteq{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|G^{\prime})
and each character in

is faithful, and so
|{\mathrm{cd}}(G|K)|=|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|K)|\leq|{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})|\leq 2

The proof of the previous lemma is a mimic of the proof of

By Lemma

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G=C_{p}\times L
, where

is a non-abelian
p^{\prime}
-group, for some prime

\chi,\psi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|G^{\prime})

{\mathrm{cod}}(\psi)=p\cdot{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)

({\mathrm{cod}}(\chi),{\mathrm{cod}}(\psi))\neq 1

Proof.

Assume that

{\mathrm{cod}}(\psi)=\frac{|G:1\times\ker\chi|}{\psi(1)}=\frac{p|L:\ker\chi|}{\chi(1)}=p\cdot{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Each *-group is non-nilpotent.

Proof.

On the contrary, assume that
G
is a nilpotent *-group. Clearly,
|\pi(G)|\geq 2

Lemma 2.10

Let

be a Frobenius group with Frobenius kernel

is not a *-group.

Assume that

-Hall of

We recall that a finite group

Theorem 2.12

Let
G
be a
2

(1)

(2)

(3)

(4)

For each

, where

\pi(R_{0})=\pi(R)

{\mathrm{cod}}(G|G^{\prime})=\{p,|K||R_{0}|\}

First assume that

.

Now since

.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

, where

is a Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
, for some prime
p

be a minimal normal subgroup of
G

is a Frobenius group, with Frobenius kernel

is not a direct factor of

(1)

for each
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
,
(|\ker\chi|,|R|)=1
,

(2)

G
is a Frobenius group or there exists
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
such that
({\mathrm{cod}}(\chi),|R|)\neq 1
.

Proof.

(1)
Assume that
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
. We know that
\ker\chi=P_{0}\rtimes R_{0}
, where
P_{0}<P
and
R_{0}\leq R
. If

, then since

is a Frobenius group and

is not a
p
-subgroup of

, we have that

and we conclude that

, which is a contradiction, and so

.

(2) Assume that
({\mathrm{cod}}(\chi),|R|)=1
, for each
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
.
Let
\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\chi_{N})
for some
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G|N)
. We know that
\chi=\theta^{G}
for some
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\lambda^{\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)})
. Thus
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=\frac{|G:\ker\chi|}{|G:\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)|\theta(1)}=\frac{|\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda):\ker\theta^{G}|}{\theta(1)}
and so by part (1),
|\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)|_{p^{\prime}}=(\theta(1))_{p^{\prime}}
. Let
k=(\theta(1))_{p^{\prime}}
. Recall that
|\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda):N|=\sum\limits_{\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\lambda^{\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)})}\theta(1)^{2}
, which is divisible by

, thus

, and so
|\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)|_{p^{\prime}}=1
, which means
\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)\leq P
, for each
1_{N}\neq\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(N)
. Thus, for each
1\neq x\in N
, we have that
C_{R}(x)=1
, and so

acts Frobeniusly on
N
and since
R
acts Frobeniusly on

, we conclude that

is a Frobenius group.

Lemma 2.14

Lemma 2.15

In this case,
H=K\rtimes P
, for some
C_{p}\cong P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(H)
.
If
P
acts Frobeniusly on
K
, then
G
is a
2
-Frobenius *-group, and so by Lemma
2.12
,
K
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, a contradiction.
Hence there exists
1_{N}\neq\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(N)
, such that
P\leq\mathrm{I}_{G}(\lambda)
. We claim that
\lambda
does not extend to
G
. Otherwise, there exists a linear character
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\lambda^{G})
and
G^{\prime}\cap N=1
. Recall that
G^{\prime}N=H
, and so
H=G^{\prime}\times N
and
P\leq G^{\prime}\leq\ker\lambda_{0}

\psi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H/K)

\chi=\lambda_{0}\psi^{G}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
. Note that
N\nless\ker\chi
and

, which implies that
p\nmid|\ker\chi|
. Also
\chi(1)=|R|
, hence
p\nmid\chi(1)|\ker\chi|
which implies that
p|N|\mid{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)
, a contradiction. Therefore our claim holds and every constituent of
\lambda^{G}
is non-linear. If there exists
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\lambda^{G})
such that
p\mid{\mathrm{cod}}(\theta)
, then by Lemma
2.4
,
p|N|\mid{\mathrm{cod}}(\psi)
, which is a contradiction. Hence for each
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\lambda^{G})
,
p\nmid{\mathrm{cod}}(\theta)
. If
p\mid|\ker\theta|
, then
\ker\theta\cap H\neq 1
, since every Sylow
p
-subgroup of

. Let

,

, we get that

. Observe that

. Let
1_{P}\neq\lambda_{1}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(P)

pq\mid{\mathrm{cod}}(\omega)

,

. Remember that

and

Now we are ready to prove Theorem A:

Proof of Theorem A.

According to Theorems
2.11
and
2.12
, if we prove
G
is a Frobenius group or a
2
-Frobenius group, then we are done. So, on the contrary, assume that
G
is a *-group of minimal order which is neither a Frobenius group nor a
2
-Frobenius group. If

is abelian for each minimal normal subgroup

of
G
, then

is the unique minimal subgroup of

[

,

is either a
p
-group or a group explained in Lemma
2.1
, a contradiction. So there exists a minimal normal subgroup

of

|{\mathrm{cod}}((G/N)|(G/N)^{\prime})|=1
, then by Lemma
2.1
,

is either a
p
-group or a Frobenius group, which is contradicting Lemmas
2.14
and
2.15

|{\mathrm{cod}}((G/N)|(G/N)^{\prime})|=2

G/N
is a *-group. By Lemma
2.17
, we get that
G/N
is not a
2
-Frobenius group, and so by minimality of
|G|
, we get that
G/N
is a Frobenius *-group and so by Lemma
2.11
,
G/N\cong C_{p}^{k}\rtimes Q_{8}
, for some prime
p
and integer
k
. Notice that for each
1\neq l\in L
,
C_{P}(l)\leq N
.
By Lemma
2.16
,
N
is a
p
-group. So
G=P\rtimes L
, where
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{p}(G)
and
Q_{8}\cong L\in\mathrm{Syl}_{2}(G)
. Note that every minimal normal subgroup of
G
is contained in

. If there exists another minimal normal subgroup

, then since the Sylow
p
-subgroup of

G/M
is not a

|G|
, we see that

1_{L}\neq l\in L

. Assume that there exists

\lambda

1_{N}\neq\lambda\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(N)

\chi=\theta^{G}

Abstract
. We define a Young subgroup of the braid group as a subgroup generated by an arbitrary subset of the Birman-Ko-Lee generators. We give an intrinsic description of such subgroups which yields, in particular, an easy criterion to decide membership. We also give an algorithm to write an element of a Young subgroup as a product of the generators. Our methods are based on analyzing the Hurwitz action on tuples over free groups via a diagrammatic approach.

2.1.
The Hurwitz action

Below
B_{n}
stands for the braid group with standard generators
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}

stands for the free group
generated by

\sigma_{i}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}x_{i}x_{i+1}x_{i}^{-1}&\text{if $j=i$},\\

For convenience we give the formula for

\sigma_{i}^{-1}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}x_{i+1}&\text{if $j=i$},\\
x_{i+1}^{-1}x_{i}x_{i+1}&\text{$j=i+1$},\\
x_{j}&\text{$j\not\in\{i,i+1\}$}.\end{cases}

If
G
is a group then a

of length

\phi:F_{n}\rightarrow G

with the element
of

given by

(\phi(x_{1}),\ldots,\phi(x_{n}))\in G^{n}

Since

acts on

(b\cdot\phi)(w)=\phi(b^{-1}w).

for

,
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Groups}}(F_{n},G)

The corresponding action on

is referred to as the

and is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i},g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i+1},g^{-1}_{i+1}g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{-1}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i},g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1}g_{i}^{-1},g_{i},\ldots,g_{n}).

then we denote the corresponding element of

The following lemma recalls some trivial properties of the Hurwitz action.

.

(1)

If

, and

2.2.
The Birman-Ko-Lee generators

If
1\leq i<j\leq n
then the corresponding Birman–Ko–Lee generator
[
2
]
of

is defined as

(2.1)

a_{ij}=\sigma_{i}\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\sigma_{j-1}\sigma^{-1}_{j-2}\cdots\sigma_{i}^{-1}.

By convention we also put
a_{ji}=a_{ij}
.
Using the braid relations we may obtain the following alternative expression for
a_{ij}

(2.2)

a_{ij}=\sigma_{j-1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\sigma_{j-1}.

The

[
4

[i,k]

share no end points and they are either disjoint or nested then

and

commute. In the remaining cases rather the opposite is true!

Lemma 2.2
.

If

then

and
a_{jl}
generate a free subgroup of

.

Proof.

Using the mapping class group interpretation of

(see the introduction) we see that we may assume

,

which maps

to

and
\sigma_{2}
to

we see

and

We now consider the homomorphism

b\in B_{Q^{u}}
. Let
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
be independent variables and put
F_{n}=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle
(thus in this context
m=n
).
Let
t_{\operatorname{triv}}:=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
and put
t=b(t_{\operatorname{triv}})
,
P=b(P_{\operatorname{triv}})
. Then according to Corollary
5.4

P
is the arc diagram of
t
.
If
l(t)=n
then
t=t_{\operatorname{triv}}
. It is well-known that the stabilizer of
t_{\operatorname{triv}}
in
F_{n}^{n}
is trivial (see e.g.
[
4
]
, XI, Corollary 1.8). Thus we have
b=1
and hence
b\in B_{Q^{u}}
.

Hence assume
l(t)>n
. We will now perform induction on
c(P)
under this assumption. If
c(P)=n
(the minimal value) then
P=P_{\operatorname{triv}}
and hence
b=1
which contradicts
l(t)>n
. So assume
c(P)>n
.
Since
l(b(u))=n
the hypothesis
l(t)>n
implies that there must be some cancellation in
t
after performing the substitution
v_{i}\rightarrow u_{i}
. In other words:
some
t_{k}
contains a subword of the form
v_{i}^{\eta}v_{j}^{-\eta}
with
u_{i}=u_{j}
and

\eta\in\{\pm 1\}
.

This subword yields two adjacent lower arcs with opposite orientation in the minimal representation of
P
, as in §
6.1
. It follows
from the discussion in §
6.2

that for
b^{\prime}=b\circ a_{ij}^{\eta}
,
P^{\prime}:=b^{\prime}(P_{\operatorname{triv}})
we have
c(P^{\prime})<c(P)
.
If
l(b^{\prime}(v))=n
then
b^{\prime}=1
and hence
b=a_{ij}^{-\eta}\in B_{Q^{u}}
. If
l(b^{\prime}(v))>n
then by induction we may assume that
b^{\prime}\in B_{Q^{u}}
and we again conclude
b=b^{\prime}\circ a_{ij}^{-\eta}\in B_{Q^{u}}
.

7.
Algebraic version of the algorithm

The proof given in §
6.3
is in fact constructive and yields an algorithm to write an element
b
in the stabilizer of
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
as a product
of
a_{ij}
with
u_{i}=u_{j}
.
It can be trivially implemented on a computer algebra system such as Sage
[
5
]
.

To describe the algorithm concretely we first we need to do some preparatory work.

7.1.
More actions

Let
G
be a group. Recall that the Hurwitz action on
G^{n}
is obtained by identifying

(7.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{Groups}}(F_{n},G)\cong G^{n}

and then using the
B_{n}
-action on
F_{n}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
via the formula

(7.2)

(b\cdot\phi)(w)=\phi(b^{-1}w).

Assume
G=F_{n}
. Then we obtain a second action of
B_{n}
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{Groups}}(F_{n},F_{n})
, denoted by
\bullet
, via

(7.3)

(b\bullet\phi)(w)=b(\phi(w)).

After the identification (
7.1
) we see that for
t\in F_{n}^{n}
we may compute
b\bullet t
as follows:

•

Put
t_{\operatorname{triv}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and compute
s=b^{-1}\cdot t_{\operatorname{triv}}
.

•

Substitute
x_{i}=s_{i}
in
t
.

In particular

(7.4)

b\bullet t_{\operatorname{triv}}=b^{-1}\cdot t_{\operatorname{triv}}.

7.2.
Description of the algorithm

Let the notations be as Theorem
2.6
. As in §
6
, we consider tuples over
F_{n}:=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle
and we put
t_{\operatorname{triv}}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
.

Let
b
be in the stabilizer of
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
. We will produce a sequence of elements

(a^{\gamma_{1}}_{i_{t}j_{t}},\ldots,a^{\gamma_{t}}_{i_{1}j_{1}})

with
\gamma_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
such
that

b=a^{\gamma_{1}}_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots a^{\gamma_{t}}_{i_{1}j_{1}}.

To do this we keep track of a tuple
t
which is initialized as
t:=b(t_{\operatorname{triv}})

as well as a partial sequence

l=(a^{\gamma_{s}}_{i_{s}j_{s}},\ldots,a^{\gamma_{t}}_{i_{1}j_{1}})
which is initialized as
()
.
We now look for some
t_{k}
containing a subword of the form
v_{i}^{\eta}v_{j}^{-\eta}
with
u_{i}=u_{j}
and
\eta\in\{\pm 1\}
.
We put
t:=a_{ij}^{-\eta}\bullet t

and we prepend
a_{ij}^{-\eta}
to
l
. We repeat this computation until

t=t_{\operatorname{triv}}
.

Example 7.1
.

We place ourselves in the context of Example
1.3
.
Thus
n=4
and
u=(x,y,x,y)
. Put
t_{\operatorname{triv}}=(x_{1},y_{2},x_{3},y_{4})
. Let

(7.5)

b=a_{24}a_{13}^{-1}.

Then

t=b\cdot t_{\operatorname{triv}}=(x_{1}x_{3}x_{1}^{-1},y_{4},y_{4}^{-1}x_{1}x_{3}^{-1}y_{2}x_{3}x_{1}x_{3}^{-1}y_{2}^{-1}x_{3}x_{1}^{-1}y_{4},y_{4}^{-1}x_{1}x_{3}^{-1}y_{2}x_{3}x_{1}^{-1}y_{4}).

The first subword we consider is

x_{3}x_{1}^{-1}

which yields
l:=(a_{13}^{-1})
(recall
a_{31}=a_{13}
).
We also get

t:=a_{13}^{-1}\bullet t=(x_{1},y_{4},y_{4}^{-1}y_{2}x_{3}y_{2}^{-1}y_{4},y_{4}^{-1}y_{2}y_{4}).

Now we consider the subword

y_{4}^{-1}y_{2}

which yields
l:=(a_{24},a_{13}^{-1})
.
We now get

t:=a_{24}\bullet t=(x_{1},y_{2},x_{3},y_{4}).

Since
t=t_{\operatorname{triv}}
the algorithm stops and we have indeed recovered (
7.5
).

7.3.
Explanation why the algorithm works

We make §
6.3
explicit. We have

\displaystyle t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(b\circ a^{\eta}_{ij})(t_{\operatorname{triv}})

\displaystyle=(b\circ a^{\eta}_{ij})^{-1}\bullet t_{\operatorname{triv}}

(by (
7.4
))

\displaystyle=a^{-\eta}_{ij}\bullet(b^{-1}\bullet t_{\operatorname{triv}})

\displaystyle=a^{-\eta}_{ij}\bullet t

\displaystyle\text{(by \eqref{eq:inparticular})}.

If we repeat the procedure
t\rightarrow t^{\prime}
we construct a sequence
(a^{-\eta_{t}}_{i_{t}j_{t}},\ldots,a^{-\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}j_{1}})

such that

\displaystyle t_{\operatorname{triv}}

\displaystyle=(a^{-\eta_{t}}_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots a^{-\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}j_{1}})\bullet t

\displaystyle=(a^{-\eta_{t}}_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots a^{-\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}j_{1}})\bullet(b^{-1}\bullet t_{\operatorname{triv}})

\displaystyle=(a^{-\eta_{t}}_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots a^{-\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}j_{1}}b^{-1})\bullet t_{\operatorname{triv}}

from which we obtain

b=a^{-\eta_{t}}_{i_{t}j_{t}}\cdots a^{-\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}j_{1}}.

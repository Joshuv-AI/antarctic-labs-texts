An Integrated AIVIVE-PBPK-QIVIVE Framework with HTTK Validation for Probabilistic Risk Assessment of Neodymium Nitrate
Authors: Ning Wang, Jing Leng, Hui-min Zhang, Jing Xu, Ke-lei Qian, Xiu-li Chang, Na-na Sun, Ping Xiao, Xin-yu Hong, Da-sheng Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rare earth elements (REEs) are critical to modern industries but pose growing health risks due to increasing environmental release, and neodymium nitrate (Nd­(NO3)3), a representative REE compound, lacks comprehensive toxicological data. To address this, we developed an integrated computational toxicology framework combining artificial intelligence-enhanced in vitro to in vivo extrapolation (AIVIVE), physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modeling, quantitative in vitro to in vivo extrapolation (QIVIVE), and high-throughput toxicokinetic (HTTK) validation for mechanism-based risk assessment. A closed-loop “model-informed experimental design” was employed, where AIVIVE, using conditional generative adversarial networks (cGAN) predicted toxicity pathways from multiomics data, experimental determination of key toxicokinetic parameters (plasma protein binding and partition coefficients) calibrated HTTK predictions, and a PBPK-QIVIVE framework incorporating nonlinear features extrapolated in vitro EC50 to human equivalent doses (HED), with Monte Carlo simulation and Sobol sensitivity analysis quantifying uncertainty. Results showed AIVIVE predicted transcriptomic responses with high fidelity (cosine similarity = 0.9986) and identified p53, apoptosis, and ferroptosis pathways with >85% accuracy. Experimental calibration revealed significant nonlinearity: plasma unbound fraction (fu) exhibited a U-shaped concentration dependence (0.556 at 1 μg/mL → 0.176 at 10 μg/mL → 0.965 at 100 μg/mL), while cellular partition coefficients (K) displayed an inverted U-shape (0.048–0.079). HTTK substantially underestimated fu (∼15-fold) and partition coefficients (2.4–5.5-fold). The integrated framework predicted a median HED of 0.032 mg/kg/day (95% CI: 0.012–0.098), with an 18% probability of exceeding the high-risk threshold (0.1 mg/kg/day). Sensitivity analysis identified fu (65%), K (22%), and EC50 (11%) as the dominant uncertainty sources. Probabilistic integration with exposure data indicated a high safety margin for the general population but concerns for mining area residents (100% probability of margin of exposure <100). This framework addresses the challenges of evaluating metals with nonlinear kinetics, reduces reliance on animal testing, and supports regulatory decisions, proposing an occupational exposure limit of 0.05 mg/m3 for neodymium nitrate.

Introduction

Rare earth elements (REEs), recognized as critical strategic resources, are indispensable in a wide array of high-technology industries, including permanent magnets, catalysts, electronics, new energy technologies, and national defense applications. The escalating mining, processing, and utilization of REEs have led to increased environmental emissions and consequently heightened human exposure via dietary intake, drinking water, airborne particulates, and occupational contact. Neodymium nitrate [Nd­(NO3)3], a representative light REE compound extensively used in magnetic materials, glass coloring, and ceramics, lacks comprehensive toxicological data, particularly concerning its systemic health effects following chronic low-dose exposure. , This knowledge gap presents potential challenges to occupational safety and public health management in related sectors.

Conventional chemical risk assessment approaches, largely reliant on toxicity endpoints derived from animal studies and simplified kinetic extrapolations, face significant limitations when applied to metal compounds. Metal ions often exhibit complex nonlinear toxicokinetic behaviors in vivo, such as concentration-dependent plasma protein binding, tissue partition coefficients, and potential active transport processes. These characteristics typically fall outside the applicability domain of existing high-throughput toxicokinetic (HTTK) models. Moreover, most current assessment frameworks fail to adequately integrate mechanistic toxicity pathway information with quantitative dose extrapolation, thereby limiting the biological relevance and predictive power of the outcomes. , Consequently, a critical scientific challenge emerges: how to develop a robust assessment strategy for data-limited metal compounds like neodymium nitrateone that captures key toxicity mechanisms, quantifies human exposure risks, and explicitly addresses and reduces associated uncertainties. −

To address this gap, we propose and construct an integrated “closed-loop, model-informed experimental design” framework, aiming to achieve end-to-end quantitative translation from mechanistic anchoring to risk-informed decision-making. Rather than treating computational tools as isolated components, this framework orchestrates them to sequentially address core questions in risk assessment: What toxic effect is being translated? (Mechanistic anchoring via artificial intelligence-enhanced in vitro to in vivo extrapolation, AIVIVE) → Where do default models fail? (Parameter gap diagnosis using HTTK) → Which minimal experiments are required? (Targeted toxicokinetic experiments) → What is the safe dose? (Reverse dosimetry via physiologically based pharmacokinetic-quantitative in vitro to in vivo extrapolation, PBPK-QIVIVE) → How certain are we, and what should be done next? (Probabilistic risk assessment with Monte Carlo simulation and Sobol global sensitivity analysis for uncertainty quantification.) This workflow not only generates decision-ready probabilistic risk estimates but also, through sensitivity analysis, identifies the key parameters driving uncertainty, thereby providing a science-based prioritization for subsequent research efforts.

Using neodymium nitrate as a case study, this work systematically demonstrates the application potential of this integrated framework for the risk assessment of metal compounds. It aims to establish a novel paradigm for the safety evaluation of REEs and other data-poor chemicals, promoting the practical implementation of computational toxicology in regulatory science. Furthermore, by reducing reliance on traditional animal testing, the approach aligns with the ethical principles of replacement, reduction, and refinement (the 3Rs).

Materials and Methods

Overall Workflow and Data Integration

We present a closed-loop, model-informed experimental design that links mechanistic anchoring, toxicokinetic gap diagnosis, calibrated dose translation, and decision-ready uncertainty quantification. The workflow (see Supporting Information, Figure S1) proceeds through defined stages, with outputs (“handoff variables”) from one stage serving as inputs for the next.

Mechanistic Anchoring

Integrates historical multiomics and benchmark-dose evidence to define key toxicity pathways and genes, guiding endpoint and target-tissue selection for subsequent dose translation.

HTTK Gap Diagnosis

Uses default HTTK model predictions as a baseline to identify parameters with large prediction errors compared to targeted experiments, defining the “gap list” (e.g., fu and K).

Targeted Experiments and Calibration

Conducts minimal experiments to quantify the identified gap parameters, deriving calibrated functions (e.g., fu­(C) and K­(C)).

Dose Translation and Decision Outputs

Employs QIVIVE to convert in vitro potency (EC50) to a biophase target concentration (C bio) using calibrated TK parameters. A PBPK model in reverse dosimetry mode then maps C bio to a probabilistic human equivalent dosing (HED) distribution.

Uncertainty and Feedback

The parameter uncertainty is propagated via a Monte Carlo simulation to produce risk probabilities. Global sensitivity analysis (Sobol) quantifies the contribution of each input to HED uncertainty, providing a prioritized list for future measurements.

Historical multiomics data (transcriptomics and metabolomics) and benchmark dose (BMD) data for 14 mechanism-based systemic toxicity endpoints from previous studies were integrated (see Table S2 for detailed provenance and endpoint definitions). Pathway enrichment analysis identified key toxicity mechanisms. To address data gaps, supplementary experiments were conducted: (a) in vitro cytotoxicity assays (HepG2 cells and CCK-8) for EC50; (b) plasma protein binding assays (equilibrium dialysis) for concentration-dependent binding; and (c) cellular partition coefficient (K) determination. Detailed protocols for all experiments and animal studies used for PBPK validation are shown in Section S1.1 and S1.2. The high dose (688 mg/kg) was selected for PBPK validation because it was the only dose level in our prior in vivo studies that yielded tissue concentrations consistently above the analytical limit of detection (LOD) of the ICP–MS method. While the in vitro anchor for QIVIVE is a 24 h HepG2 CCK-8 assay measuring cytotoxicity, this endpoint is interpreted as a functional consequence of the upstream p53/apoptosis/ferroptosis pathway perturbations identified and anchored by the AIVIVE model. Thus, dose translation uses a phenotypic outcome directly linked to the mechanistic pathways of concern.

AIVIVE Model Development

A conditional generative adversarial network (cGAN) framework was implemented for the AIVIVE model. , The model comprised a generator (maps in vitro to predicted in vivo transcriptomic profiles), a discriminator, and a local optimizer for fine-tuning predictions on 347 key genes associated with identified toxicity pathways (p53, apoptosis, and ferroptosis). The cGAN was trained on a curated data set of paired in vitro (human HepG2, 24 h) and in vivo (rat liver, 5 day oral gavage) transcriptomic profiles sourced primarily from the Open TG-GATEs and DrugMatrix databases. The data set was split using a compound-wise, 5-fold cross-validation strategy, ensuring that transcriptomic profiles from the same compound never appear simultaneously in both training and validation folds. Model performance was evaluated by a composite overall accuracy metric, defined as a weighted score: 40% cosine similarity +20% DEG overlap +40% mean F1-score of the top three enriched pathways (p53, apoptosis, and ferroptosis). An external validation was performed on a held-out test set comprising compounds structurally distinct from those in the training folds (see Table S13).

HTTK Model Validation and Calibration

Default HTTK model predictions for neodymium nitrate were systematically compared to targeted experimental measurements to identify limitations and define critical parameters for experimental characterization. Using experimental data, the key parameters were calibrated. A nonlinear U-shaped model described concentration-dependent plasma protein binding (Section S1.4). A modular parameter interface allowed for flexible integration of HTTK predictions, experimental measurements, and hybrid strategies. Monte Carlo analysis evaluated the performance of different parameter strategies. −

PBPK-QIVIVE-Coupled Framework

A previously validated PBPK model for neodymium nitrate in mice was adapted and enhanced by incorporating calibrated nonlinear plasma protein binding and concentration-dependent partition coefficients. The model structure included plasma, liver, kidney, brain, and the rest of the body compartments, scaled to a 70 kg human. The QIVIVE approach converted the in vitro EC50 to a biophase concentration (C bio) using concentration-dependent fu and K (Section S1.6). The PBPK model in reverse dosimetry mode identified the external dose yielding the target organ (liver) concentration equal to C bio, which was then scaled to the human equivalent dose (HED) using interspecies scaling factors. − Model equations and parameters are given in Section S1.5.

Probabilistic Risk Assessment

A probabilistic framework using Latin Hypercube Sampling (10,000 iterations) propagated variability in key parameters (EC50, fu, K, and clearance). Key inputs were assigned probability distributions based on experimental variability or literature ranges. For each iteration, a complete QIVIVE calculation generated a distribution of HED values. Global sensitivity analysis using the Sobol method quantified each input’s contribution to HED variance. The HED distribution was used for probabilistic risk characterization. Risk levels were defined based on the following HED thresholds: low risk (<0.01 mg/kg/day), moderate concern (0.01–0.1 mg/kg/day), high risk (0.1–0.2 mg/kg/day), and very high risk (≥0.2 mg/kg/day). , Methodology is given in Section S1.7.

Software Implementation

All computational modeling and analysis were implemented in R (v4.2.0) and Python (v3.9) using the mrgsolve package (PBPK), PyTorch (AIVIVE), and the sensitivity package (Sobol analysis). Custom R scripts integrated into the workflow. Code is available at https://github.com/SHCDC-LAB.

Results and Discussion

Mechanistic Anchoring and AIVIVE Model Performance

The AIVIVE model demonstrated excellent performance in linking in vitro perturbations to in vivo pathway-level responses for neodymium nitrate (Figure ). Overall, the cosine similarity between predicted and observed transcriptomic profiles was 0.9986. The model captured 48% of DEGs and showed high accuracy (>85%) for key pathways (p53, apoptosis, and ferroptosis), with an integrated accuracy score of 82.31%. This strong mechanistic anchor provided a biologically meaningful context for subsequent dose extrapolation.

Experimental Characterization of Nonlinear Toxicokinetics

Guided by HTTK gap analysis, targeted experiments provided evidence of nonlinear kinetic behaviors (Figure ). Plasma protein binding showed a U-shaped dependence, with unbound fraction (fu) decreasing from 0.556 (1 μg/mL) to 0.176 (10 μg/mL), then increasing to 0.965 (100 μg/mL). Cellular partition coefficients (K) exhibited an inverted U-shape (0.048 at 5 μM, 0.079 at 50 μM, and 0.034 at 250 μM). The in vitro EC50 in HepG2 cells was 50.0 μM. These data were critical for calibrating the models.

This pattern indicates high-affinity, low-capacity binding sites that become saturated at intermediate concentrations. HTTK Model Limitations and Calibration

Benchmarking default HTTK predictions against experiments revealed systematic, order-of-magnitude errors for key parameters (Table ), confirming a critical applicability gap for ionic metal compounds such as neodymium nitrate. For example, HTTK underestimated the plasma unbound fraction (fu) by ∼15-fold. Calibration using the experimental nonlinear models drastically improved prediction accuracy, reducing the overall error by 92% (Figure ), demonstrating the necessity of compound-specific calibration for metal ions.

Standard HTTK models showed significant limitations when applied to neodymium nitrate. Predicted values for plasma unbound fraction (fu) were consistently lower than experimental measurements, and predicted partition coefficients (K) differed by orders of magnitude from experimental values. After calibration using the experimentally derived nonlinear models, HTTK predictions aligned closely with observed data, demonstrating the necessity of compound-specific calibration for metal ions.

Probabilistic Human Equivalent Dose and Uncertainty

The integrated PBPK-QIVIVE framework produced a probabilistic HED distribution (Figure A). The median HED was 0.032 mg/kg/day (95% CI: 0.012–0.098 mg/kg/day). The distribution was right-skewed with an 18% probability of HED exceeding the 0.1 mg/kg/day high-risk threshold. Cumulative risk probabilities were quantified (Figure B). Global sensitivity analysis (Sobol) identified uncertainty in plasma protein binding (fu) as the dominant contributor to HED variance (65%), followed by the cellular partition coefficient (K, 22%) and clearance (12%) (Figure C). The corresponding margin of safety (MOS) distribution, calculated relative to a preliminary reference dose (RfD) of 0.25 mg/kg/day, is shown in Figure D. The median MOS was 7.8, and the probability of the MOS falling below 1 (indicating potential risk) was 4.2%. The dominance of fu in the sensitivity analysis underscores the importance of its accurate characterization. Its influence is amplified by its dual role in the modelboth scaling in vitro potency (via C bio calculation) and controlling tissue distribution (via plasma free concentration)and by its nonlinear, concentration-dependent behavior. This highlights the nonlinear TK parameters as key drivers of decision uncertainty.

Population Exposure and Risk Characterization

Exposure estimates were compiled for the general Chinese population (dietary: 0.65 μg Nd/kg/day) and residents in rare earth mining areas (e.g., Baotou: 16.7 μg Nd/kg/day) (Table ). To avoid misinterpretation, it is crucial to distinguish the margin of safety (MOS) from the Margin of Exposure (MOE). The MOS compares the estimated HED distribution to a preliminary reference dose (RfD) derived from in vivo animal data (0.25 mg/kg/day), thus reflecting an intrinsic hazard. The MOE compares the same HED distribution to real-world human exposure estimates, thereby integrating hazard with exposure for a complete risk characterization. Probabilistic integration of the HED distribution with exposure distributions yielded the margin of exposure (MOE) and hazard quotient (HQ) metrics (Figure ). For the general population, the median MOE was 49.2, with a 96.8% probability of MOE <100. For mining area residents, the median MOE was 1.9, with a 100% probability of MOE <100, strongly indicating an insufficient safety margin for this subpopulation, highlighting a potential priority for monitoring and risk management. Sensitivity analysis confirmed that HED uncertainty (dominated by fu and K) contributed >90% to MOE variance, underscoring the need for refined TK data over exposure assessment to reduce overall uncertainty.

Implications and Regulatory Application

The principal innovation lies in the closed-loop, model-informed workflow itself, where HTTK serves a diagnostic role in prioritizing targeted experiments, the results of which directly calibrate and reduce uncertainty in the final risk estimates. This study presents significant methodological advances: the first AIVIVE framework for REEs, a systematic approach to adapt HTTK for metals via calibration, and the integration of AIVIVE, PBPK, and QIVIVE into an end-to-end, mechanism-based workflow that aligns with the 3Rs principle.

The nonlinear kinetics of neodymium nitrate have critical implications; traditional constant-parameter assumptions would lead to inaccurate risk estimates. Our framework incorporates this complexity for more physiologically realistic modeling. The challenges identified with HTTK for neodymium nitrate likely extend to other metal compounds, and our calibration/hybrid parameter strategy provides an adaptable template.

The probabilistic framework offers clear advantages over deterministic point estimates by explicitly quantifying uncertainty, enabling more informed decision-making. Based on the results, we recommend an 8 h time-weighted average occupational exposure limit (OEL-TWA) of 0.05 mg/m3 for neodymium nitrate.

Limitations and Future Directions

Limitations include the current focus on oral exposure to soluble Nd salts; extension to other routes (inhalation and dermal) and REE compounds is needed. The characterization of nonlinear TK parameters (fu and K) is based on a limited number of concentration points. While the data strongly suggest concentration-dependence, future studies with finer concentration resolution are needed to fully validate the shape and mechanism of these relationships. Additional metal-specific processes (active transport and subcellular accumulation) could be integrated. Validation with human data would strengthen confidence. There is a paucity of systematic biomonitoring data for REEs to refine exposure estimates. While the PBPK model was parametrized for human physiology, the final HED calculation employed body surface area (BSA) scaling from mouse-derived clearance parameters, a standard regulatory default. Future integration of in vitro to in vivo extrapolated (IVIVE) human clearance parameters into the PBPK model could refine interspecies extrapolation and reduce the reliance on allometric scaling. The PBPK model was validated against tissue distribution data from a high-dose study, which was necessary for analytical quantification. While the model’s physiological structure provides confidence in its extrapolative power, the accuracy of predictions at environmental exposure levels (orders of magnitude lower) remains to be experimentally confirmed. This extrapolation uncertainty is partially captured in the probabilistic Monte Carlo simulations. Future work should extend the framework to other metals/REEs, incorporate additional omics layers, develop standardized calibration protocols, and create user-friendly software tools.

Conclusion

We successfully developed and validated an integrated AIVIVE-PBPK-QIVIVE framework with HTTK validation for the probabilistic risk assessment of neodymium nitrate. Key findings include: (1) the AIVIVE model accurately predicted toxicity pathways; (2) experimental characterization revealed significant nonlinear kinetic behaviors; (3) standard HTTK models required compound-specific calibration for metals; (4) the integrated framework predicted a median HED of 0.032 mg/kg/day, with an 18% probability of high-risk exposure; and (5) sensitivity analysis identified plasma protein binding and cellular partition coefficients as primary uncertainty sources.

This work establishes a novel paradigm for the mechanism-based, quantitatively robust risk assessment of data-limited compounds, particularly relevant for metals with complex kinetics. It supports the transition toward animal-free testing and provides a scientific basis for regulatory decision-making. By integrating probabilistic hazards with real-world exposure, the framework enables context-aware risk quantification, identifying the general population as low risk but flagging specific high-exposure subpopulations for targeted management.

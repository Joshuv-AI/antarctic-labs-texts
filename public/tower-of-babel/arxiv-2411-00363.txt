Localized Orthogonal Decomposition Method with H 1 H^{1} Interpolation for Multiscale Elliptic Problem
Tao Yu, Xingye Yue

Abstract
This paper employs a localized orthogonal decomposition (LOD) method with H 1 H^{1} interpolation for solving the multiscale elliptic problem. This method does not need any assumptions on scale separation. We give a priori error estimate for the proposed method. The theoretical results are conformed by various numerical experiments.



Keywords

Localized Orthogonal Decomposition Method,
H^{1}
Interpolation, Multiscale Elliptic Problem.

1
Introduction

Consider the multiscale elliptic problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u^{\varepsilon}(x))=f(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
(or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
) is a bounded convex polygonal domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\varepsilon\ll 1
is a positive parameter which signifies the multiscale nature of (
1
). For simplicity, we only consider
d=2
. All the results are still valid for high dimensional problems.

Direct numerical simulations such as finite element method (FEM) in order to capture fast oscillations on microscopic scale lead to problems of very large size which are prohibitively expensive. From the engineering point of view, the macroscopic properties and macroscopic behaviors of the materials are more important. Homogenization and numerical homogenization
[
4
,
26
]
have been used to simulate multiscale materials. These methods can calculate effective material properties to solve the macro scale problems successfully. However the limitation is based on the assumption of material, such as periodic micro-structures.

To overcome these difficulties, several multiscale computing strategies have been developed to solve the multiscale elliptic problem in heterogeneous structures on grids that are coarser than the scale of oscillations. Babuška and Osborn developed the so-called generalized multiscale finite element methods (GMSFEM)
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
for problems with rough coefficients, including discontinuous coefficients as well as coefficients with multiple scales. Its main idea is to modify the finite element space in the framework of the finite element method. Hou et al. developed a multiscale finite element method (MsFEM)
[
16
,
22
]
for the study of multi-dimensional problems. This is achieved by constructing multiscale finite element basis functions which adapt to the local property of the differential operator. E and Engquist developed the heterogeneous multiscale method (HMM)
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
, which is a generally efficient methodology for problems with multiple scales. The method avoids computing the coefficients in the fine scale mesh as well as the coefficients of the homogenized equations. However, it depends on some powerful assumptions, such as periodicity and scale separation. There are some other methods to solve multiscale problems with strongly varying coefficients. For example, the variational multiscale method (VMS)
[
23
,
24
]
and residual-free bubbles method
[
18
]
in which the test function space was decomposed into the sum of coarse and fine scale components.

In this paper, we study the Localized Orthogonal Decomposition (LOD) method with
H^{1}
interpolation for elliptic problems with highly varying coefficients. The LOD method was first introduced for elliptic multiscale problems
[
28
]
, which is suitable for the general framework of the Variational Multiscale Method (VMS)
[
23
,
24
]
. The method constructs local generalized finite element basis which is exponentially decayed by using the modified Clément interpolation
[
10
]
. The analysis does not depend on the regularity of the solution or the scale separation in the coefficient. The modified Clément interpolation is essentially a
L^{2}
projection, but
H^{1}
interpolation is more natural for elliptic problems. The method is also applied to parabolic problem
[
29
]
, semi-linear problem
[
20
]
, wave propagation
[
2
]
, elliptic optimal control problem
[
8
]
, and so on. Recently, Peterseim reviewed the VMS methods of Linear multiscale partial differential equations
[
27
]
and gave a new simple proof for the exponential decay property. We will use
H^{1}
interpolation to apply the technique. By using the classical finite element methods, we prove convergence of optimal order in
H^{1}
norm. Numerical examples are given to complete the analysis, which supports our theoretical findings.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we describe the model problem and present the localized orthogonal decomposition method with
H^{1}
interpolation. The localization technique is proposed in Section 3. In section 4, some numerical experiments are given.

2
LOD Method with
H^{1}
Interpolation for Multiscale Elliptic Problem

In this section, we first construct a
H^{1}
interpolation similar to the modified Clément interpolation in
[
10
]
. Then, we introduce the localized orthogonal decomposition method with this
H^{1}
interpolation for the multiscale elliptic problem (
1
).

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
A^{\varepsilon}(x)=(A^{\varepsilon}_{ij}(x))
is a positive definite, bounded tensor satisfying

\displaystyle{\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq A^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\beta|\xi|^{2}\;,\;\;\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},}

(1)

for some positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
. Multiply equation (
1
) by test function
v\in V\overset{\vartriangle}{=}H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, by using the Green’s formula, the standard weak formulation is to find
u^{\varepsilon}\in V
such that

\displaystyle a(u^{\varepsilon},v)\overset{\vartriangle}{=}\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u^{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x)v(x)dx\overset{\vartriangle}{=}(f,v),\;\;\forall v\in V.

(2)

The bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, continuous and coercive, directly from property (
1
) and Poincaré inequality. According to the Lax-Milgram Theorem, the weak formulation (
2
) has a unique weak solution
u^{\varepsilon}\in V
, satisfying

\displaystyle||u^{\varepsilon}||_{V}\leq C||f||_{0,\Omega}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a standard regular coarse triangulation of the domain
\Omega
with coarse mesh size
h_{K}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}diam(K)
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Denote by
h=\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\max}h_{K}
and
\mathcal{N}
the set of all interior nodal points,
\mathcal{N}(K)
the vertices of
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. The classical conforming
P_{1}
finite element space is given by

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{u_{h}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)|\;u_{h}|_{K}\in P_{1}(K),\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
P_{1}(K)
is the space of polynomials with total degree at most one on element
K
. Then the finite element method reads: find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

\displaystyle a(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{h}),\;\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(3)

For every interior nodal point
a\in\mathcal{N}
, let
\lambda_{a}\in V_{h}
be the corresponding nodal basis function, i.e. the continuous piecewise linear finite element function which equals one at
a
and vanishes at all other vertices. In order to construct the modified nodal basis, we present an important
H^{1}
interpolation
\mathcal{J}_{h}:V\rightarrow V_{h}
. For every
v\in V
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{h}v=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}}\tilde{v}(a)\lambda_{a},

(4)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(a)=\frac{\int_{\Omega}v\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\lambda_{a}dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}.

(5)

For the above special definition, the following bounded property can be easily derived.

Lemma 2.1

There exists a positive constant
C
, which is independent on the mesh size
h
, such that for every interior nodal point
a\in K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
v\in V
, it holds that

\displaystyle||\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\left(||v||_{L^{2}(w_{a})}+h||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(w_{a})}\right),

(6)

where
w_{a}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}\bigcup\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}|a\in K\}
.

Proof.
Using the definition (
5
) of
\tilde{v}(a)
, it shows that

\displaystyle||\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\tilde{v}(a)|=|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{\int_{\Omega}v\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\lambda_{a}dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\omega_{a}}\lambda_{a}dx\right)^{-1}\left(\left|\int_{\omega_{a}}v\lambda_{a}dx\right|+\left|h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\omega_{a}}\int_{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\lambda_{a}dx\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{3}|\omega_{a}|\right)^{-1}\Bigg(||\lambda_{a}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}||v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+h^{2}\left(\sum_{K\in\omega_{a}}||\nabla\lambda_{a}||^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!C|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\omega_{a}|^{-1}\left(|\omega_{a}|^{\frac{1}{2}}||v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}+h^{2}h^{-1}|\omega_{a}|^{\frac{1}{2}}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!C|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\omega_{a}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(||v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}+h||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!C\left(||v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}+h||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\right),

directly by Hölder inequality and simple derivation.
\blacksquare

Next, we can obtain the quasi-local stability and approximation properties for
H^{1}
interpolation
\mathcal{J}_{h}
as follow.

Lemma 2.2

There exists a positive constant
C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}
, which is independent on the mesh size
h
, such that for all
v\in V
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, it holds that

\displaystyle||\mathcal{J}_{h}v||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}||v||_{H^{1}(w_{K})}

(7)

and

\displaystyle||v-\mathcal{J}_{h}v||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}h||v||_{H^{1}(w_{K})},

(8)

where
w_{K}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}|T\bigcap K\neq\emptyset\}
.

Proof.
Since
\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\lambda_{a}=1
on
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we conclude from the definition (
4
) of
H^{1}
interpolation
\mathcal{J}_{h}
that

\displaystyle||v-\mathcal{J}_{h}v||_{L^{2}(K)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\lambda_{a}\left(v-\tilde{v}(a)\right)||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}(K)}||v-\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}

(9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}(K)}Ch||v||_{H^{1}(w_{K})}=C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}h||v||_{H^{1}(w_{K})},

in which we need to prove the inequality

\displaystyle||v-\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq Ch||v||_{H^{1}(w_{K})}.

(10)

In fact,

\displaystyle||v-\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq||v-\bar{v}||_{L^{2}(K)}+||\bar{v}-\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)},

(11)

where
\bar{v}=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{K}vdx
is the mean-value of
v
on the element
K
.
From a standard Poincare-Friedrichs inequality, we know that

\displaystyle||v-\bar{v}||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq Cdiam(K)||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq Ch_{K}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(w_{K})}.

(12)

For the second term of (
11
), denote by
\pi_{a}v=\tilde{v}(a)
, then

\displaystyle\pi_{a}\bar{v}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{\int_{\Omega}\bar{v}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla\bar{v}\cdot\nabla\lambda_{a}dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}=\frac{\bar{v}\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{\bar{v}\left(\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx\right)-\bar{v}h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\bar{v}-\left(\frac{h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}\right)\bar{v}.

Thus

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\bar{v}-\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}=||\bar{v}-\pi_{a}v||_{L^{2}(K)}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\pi_{a}\bar{v}+\left(\frac{h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}\right)\bar{v}-\pi_{a}v||_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\pi_{a}\bar{v}-\pi_{a}v||_{L^{2}(K)}+||\left(\frac{h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}\right)\bar{v}||_{L^{2}(K)}.

The first term of (
13
) can be deduced by

\displaystyle||\pi_{a}\bar{v}-\pi_{a}v||_{L^{2}(K)}=||\pi_{a}(v-\bar{v})||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\left(||v-\bar{v}||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}+h||\nabla(v-\bar{v})||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(diam(\omega_{a})||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}+h||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\right)\leq Ch||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})},

(14)

in which Lemma
2.1
and (
12
) are used. Using the definition of
\bar{v}
and the Hölder inequality, it easily see that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\left(\frac{h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}\right)\bar{v}||_{L^{2}(K)}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\left(\frac{h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\omega_{a}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\omega_{a}}\lambda_{a}dx+h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\omega_{a}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}\right)\bar{v}||_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{h^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\omega_{a}}\int_{K}|\nabla\lambda_{a}|dx}{\int_{\omega_{a}}\lambda_{a}dx}|\bar{v}||K|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\frac{h^{2}|\omega_{a}|h^{-1}}{|\omega_{a}|}|K|^{-1}|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}||v||_{L^{2}(K)}|K|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!Ch||v||_{L^{2}(K)}.

Inequalities (
11
)-(
15
) immediately yield the desired result (
10
). Obviously from Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle||\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\left(||v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}+h||\nabla v||_{L^{2}(\omega_{a})}\right)\leq C||v||_{H^{1}(\omega_{a})}.

(16)

Therefore

\displaystyle||\mathcal{J}_{h}v||_{L^{2}(K)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}}\tilde{v}(a)\lambda_{a}||_{L^{2}(K)}\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}}||\tilde{v}(a)||_{L^{2}(K)}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}}C||v||_{H^{1}(\omega_{a})}\leq C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}||v||_{H^{1}(w_{K})}

is the desired result (
7
).
\blacksquare

The kernel of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
,

\displaystyle V^{f}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}\{v\in V|\mathcal{J}_{h}\;v=0\}

(18)

represents the microscopic features of
V
, which are not captured by
V_{h}
. Given any nodal basis
\lambda_{a}
of
V_{h}
, define fine-scale corrector
\phi_{a}=\mathfrak{F}\lambda_{a}\in V^{f}
, satisfying the corrector problem

\displaystyle a(\phi_{a},\omega)=a(\lambda_{a},\omega),\;\forall\;\omega\in V^{f}.

(19)

The finescale projection operator
\mathfrak{F}:V_{h}\rightarrow V^{f}
leads to an orthogonal splitting

\displaystyle V=V_{h}^{ms}\oplus V^{f},

(20)

with respect to the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, where
V_{h}^{ms}=V_{h}-\mathfrak{F}V_{h}
is called modified coarse space, containing fine scale information.
That is to say, for any
u\in V
, there exist
u_{h}^{ms}\in V_{h}^{ms}
and
u^{f}\in V^{f}
, such that

\displaystyle u=u_{h}^{ms}+u^{f},\;\;a(u_{h}^{ms},u^{f})=0.

(21)

The basis of
V_{h}^{ms}
is then given by the modified nodal basis
\lambda_{a}-\phi_{a}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle V_{h}^{ms}=span\{\lambda_{a}-\phi_{a}|a\in\mathcal{N}\}.

(22)

Hence, the corresponding Galerkin approximation solution
u_{h}^{ms}\in V_{h}^{ms}
satisfies

\displaystyle a(u_{h}^{ms},v)=(f,v),\;\;\forall v\in V_{h}^{ms}.

(23)

The error
u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h}^{ms}
of the above method (
23
) is analyzed by the following Theorem.

Theorem 2.3

Let
u^{\varepsilon}\in V
and
u_{h}^{ms}\in V_{h}^{ms}
be the solutions of problems (
2
) and (
23
), respectively. Then it holds that

\displaystyle||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h}^{ms}||_{V}\leq Ch||f||_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(24)

where the positive constant
C
is not dependent on the mesh size
h
.

Proof.
Let
v=v_{h}^{ms}\in V_{h}^{ms}
in (
2
) and (
23
), respectively. The orthogonality results

\displaystyle a(u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h}^{ms},v_{h}^{ms})=0,

(25)

can be easily obtained by subtracting these two problems.
Denote
u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h}^{ms}=u^{f}
, then
\mathcal{J}_{h}u^{f}=0
.
From the application of (
1
) and the Poincáre inequality, it yield

\displaystyle a(u^{f},u^{f})=\int_{\Omega}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{f}\cdot\nabla u^{f}dx\geq\alpha\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u^{f}\right)^{2}dx\geq\frac{\alpha}{\tilde{C}}||u^{f}||^{2}_{V}.

(26)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle a(u^{f},u^{f})\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!a(u^{\varepsilon},u^{f})-a(u_{h}^{ms},u^{f})=a(u^{\varepsilon},u^{f})=(f,u^{f})

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{K}\int_{K}fu^{f}dx\leq\sum_{K}||f||_{L^{2}(K)}||u^{f}||_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{K}||f||_{L^{2}(K)}||u^{f}-\mathcal{J}_{h}u^{f}||_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{K}||f||_{L^{2}(K)}C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}h_{K}||u^{f}||_{V,\;\omega_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{K}\left(\frac{C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}^{2}h_{K}^{2}}{2\delta}||f||^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\frac{\delta}{2}||u^{f}||^{2}_{V,\;\omega_{K}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}^{2}h^{2}}{2\delta}||f||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{C\delta}{2}||u^{f}||^{2}_{V},

by using Lemma
2.2
, (
25
) and Young’s inequality. Thus, together with (
26
) and (
27
),

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\tilde{C}}||u^{f}||^{2}_{V}\leq\frac{C_{\mathcal{J}_{h}}^{2}h^{2}}{2\delta}||f||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{C\delta}{2}||u^{f}||^{2}_{V}.

(28)

The small enough choice for
\delta
concludes the proof.
\blacksquare

3
Localization

In this section, we first show that the fine-scale corrector
\phi_{a}
has exponential decay properties, by using the technique in
[
27
]
. Therefore, simple truncation of the corrector problems to local patches of coarse elements yields localized basis functions with good approximation properties.

Let
\eta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
be some cut-off function, satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\eta=1,&x\in B_{r}(a),\\
\eta=0,&x\in\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a),\end{array}\right.~~and~~||\nabla\eta||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\eta}h^{-1},

with
R>2h
,
r=R-h>h
and constant
C_{\eta}
independent of
h
, where
B_{r}(a)
and
B_{R}(a)
are balls of radius
r,R>0
centred at
a
. Multiplying by
\eta
and
(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})
, function in the kernel of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
has quasi-local stable property in the sense that

\displaystyle||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(\eta\omega)||_{H^{1},B_{R}(a)\setminus B_{r}(a)}\leq C_{\eta,\mathcal{J}_{h}}||\omega||_{H^{1},B_{R^{\prime}}(a)\setminus B_{r^{\prime}}(a)},\;\forall\;\omega\in Ker(\mathcal{J}_{h})

(32)

holds with
r^{\prime}=r-mh
and
R^{\prime}=R+mh
, in which constant
C_{\eta,\mathcal{J}_{h}}>0
and
m\in N_{0}
independent of
h
and
a\in\mathcal{N}
.
This result is possible because
\mathcal{J}_{h}
enjoys quasi-local stability and approximation properties in Lemma
2.2
.
Similarly as
[
27
]
, we can see that the finescale corrector
\phi_{a}
decays exponentially.

Lemma 3.1

There exist constants
c>0
and
C>0
, independent of
h
and
R
, such that

\displaystyle||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}\leq C\;\exp\left(-c\frac{R}{h}\right)||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega},

(33)

where
B_{R}(a)
is a ball of radius
R>0
centred at
a
.

Proof.
Because the finescale corrector
\phi_{a}\in V^{f}
, i.e.
\mathcal{J}_{h}\phi_{a}=0
, it satisfies

\displaystyle||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(1-\eta)\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(1-\eta)\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\beta^{-1}a(\omega,(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(1-\eta)\phi_{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\beta^{-1}\left(a(\omega,\phi_{a})-a(\omega,(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})\eta\phi_{a})\right),

where
\omega=\frac{(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(1-\eta)\phi_{a}}{||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(1-\eta)\phi_{a}||_{V,\Omega}}\in V^{f}
satisfies
||w||_{H^{1},\;\Omega}=1
and

\displaystyle supp\;\omega=supp\left((1-\mathcal{J}_{h})(1-\eta)\phi_{a}\right)\subset\Omega\setminus B_{r}(a).

(34)

It’s obviously that the supports of
\lambda_{a}
and
\omega
have no overlap, then

\displaystyle a(\omega,\phi_{a})=a(\phi_{a},\omega)=a(\lambda_{a},\omega)=0,

by using the symmetry property of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and (
19
). Thus

\displaystyle||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}\leq\beta^{-1}\left|a(\omega,(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})\eta\phi_{a})\right|.

(35)

Directly from (
3
), (
32
), (
34
), (
35
) and Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R^{\prime}}(a)}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}\leq\beta^{-1}|a(\omega,(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})\eta\phi_{a})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\alpha\beta^{-1}||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})\eta\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{r}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\alpha\beta^{-1}||(1-\mathcal{J}_{h})\eta\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;B_{R}(a)\setminus B_{r}(a)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\alpha\beta^{-1}C_{\eta,\mathcal{J}_{h}}||\phi_{a}||_{H^{1},\;B_{R^{\prime}}(a)\setminus B_{r^{\prime}}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\alpha\beta^{-1}C_{\eta,\mathcal{J}_{h}}\left(||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{r^{\prime}}(a)}-||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R^{\prime}}(a)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus

\displaystyle||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R^{\prime}}(a)}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{1+C^{\prime}}||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{r^{\prime}}(a)},

where
C^{\prime}=\left(\alpha\beta^{-1}C_{\eta,\mathcal{J}_{h}}\right)^{2}
. The iterative application of this estimate with
\frac{R}{(2m+1)h}
, and relabelling
R^{\prime}
to
R
, have

\displaystyle||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega\setminus B_{R}(a)}\leq\left(\frac{C^{\prime}}{1+C^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{R}{(2m+1)h}}||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega}=\exp(-2c\frac{R}{h})||\phi_{a}||^{2}_{H^{1},\;\Omega}

where
c=\left|ln(\frac{C^{\prime}}{1+C^{\prime}})\right|\frac{1}{2(2m+1)}>0
.
\blacksquare

Hence, simple truncation of the corrector problems to local patches of coarse elements yields localized basis functions with good approximation properties. For positive integer
l
, define
l
-th order element patch
\Omega_{K,l}
about
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
by

\displaystyle\Omega_{K,1}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}int\left(\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}|T\cap K\neq\emptyset\}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle\Omega_{K,l}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}int\left(\bigcup\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}|T\cap\bar{\Omega}_{K,l-1}\neq\emptyset\}\right).

(37)

Then for all
u,\;v\in V
and
\omega\subset\Omega
, the localized bilinear form reads

\displaystyle a_{\omega}(u,v)\overset{\vartriangle}{=}\int_{\omega}A^{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)dx.

(38)

Given any nodal basis function
\lambda_{a}\in V_{h}
, let
\phi_{a,l,K}\in V^{f}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{K,l})
solve the subscale corrector problem

\displaystyle a_{\Omega_{K,l}}(\phi_{a,l,K},w)=a_{K}(\lambda_{a},w),\;\;\forall w\in V^{f}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{K,l}).

(39)

Let
\phi_{a,l}=\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;a\in K}{\sum}\phi_{a,l,K}
and the localized corrector space

\displaystyle V_{h,l}^{ms}\overset{\vartriangle}{=}span\{\lambda_{a}-\phi_{a,l}|a\in\mathcal{N}\}.

(40)

The localised multiscale Galerkin FEM seeks
u_{h,l}^{ms}\in V_{h,l}^{ms}
such that

\displaystyle a(u_{h,l}^{ms},w_{h,l}^{ms})=(f,w_{h,l}^{ms}),\;\;\forall w_{h,l}^{ms}\in V_{h,l}^{ms}.

(41)

Remark 3.2

The trial function space
V_{h}^{ms}
and
V_{h,l}^{ms}
in (
23
) and (
41
) can be reduced to
V_{h}
, directly from the corrector problems (

) and (

).

The error estimate for the localized method (
41
) is analysed by the following Theorem.

Theorem 3.3

There exists positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\displaystyle||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}\leq Ch||f||_{V},

(42)

where
u^{\varepsilon}\in V
and
u_{h,l}^{ms}\in V_{h,l}^{ms}
be the solutions of problems (
2
) and (
41
), respectively.

Proof.
The error can be decomposed into two parts

\displaystyle||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}\leq||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h}^{ms}||_{V}+||u_{h}^{ms}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}.

(43)

The first term is called as the macro error

\displaystyle||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h}^{ms}||_{V}\leq Ch||f||_{V},

(44)

directly from Theorem
2.3
.
It remains to estimate the second term
||u_{h}^{ms}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}
. Let
Z_{h}^{ms}\in V_{h}^{ms}
and
||Z_{h}^{ms}||_{V}=1
, then

\displaystyle\alpha||u_{h}^{ms}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!a(u_{h}^{ms}-u_{h,l}^{ms},Z_{h}^{ms})=a(u_{h}^{ms}-u_{h,l}^{ms},Z_{h}^{ms}-Z_{h,l}^{ms})

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!a(u_{h}^{ms}-u^{\varepsilon},Z_{h}^{ms}-Z_{h,l}^{ms})+a(u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms},Z_{h}^{ms}-Z_{h,l}^{ms})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!a(u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms},Z_{h}^{ms}-Z_{h,l}^{ms})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\beta||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}||Z_{h}^{ms}-Z_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}

The exponential decay property (
33
) allows one to choose
Z_{h,l}^{ms}
in such a way that

\displaystyle||Z_{h}^{ms}-Z_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}\leq C\;\exp\left(-cl\right)||Z_{h}^{ms}||_{V}=C\;\exp\left(-cl\right).

(46)

Hence

\displaystyle||u_{h}^{ms}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}\leq\frac{\beta}{\alpha}C\;\exp\left(-cl\right)||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}.

(47)

Choose
l
big enough, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\alpha}C\;\exp\left(-cl\right)\leq\frac{1}{2},

(48)

thus

\displaystyle||u^{\varepsilon}-u_{h,l}^{ms}||_{V}\leq Ch||f||_{V},

(49)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of the mesh size
h
.
\blacksquare

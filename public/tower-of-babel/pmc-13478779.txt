Case Report: Surgical correction of Lenke 1 adolescent idiopathic scoliosis with a single rod instrumentation
Authors: Francesco Vommaro, Giovanni Ciani, Chiara Cini, Bruna Maccaferri, Alessandro Gasbarrini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction: Posterior spinal fusion is the current gold standard for severe adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS). Less invasive techniques have recently gained interest. Case presentation: A 13-year-old female with Lenke 1 AIS (65° thoracic curve) underwent unilateral pedicle screw fixation and posterior fusion via a minimally invasive approach. Surgery lasted 100 min with 200 mL blood loss. Hospitalization was 3 days with minimal postoperative pain. Results: Immediate postoperative x-rays showed correction to 18° (72.3%). Follow-up at 3 years demonstrated stable correction, no mechanical or infectious complications, and complete patient satisfaction. Hypothesis-generating: This case suggests unilateral fusion may be a safe, effective, less invasive alternative in selected Lenke 1 AIS patients. Further studies are required.

Introduction

In adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) surgery is considered in skeletally immature patients with structural thoracic curve Cobb angles over 40° or patients who show continued progression1, the goal of scoliosis surgery is to correct the curvature and stabilize the spine. Today the gold standard for correcting AIS is posterior spinal fusion (PSF). In the last years surgery of AIS has become safer and effective, increasing the use of less invasive technique. We propose a case of Lenke 1 AIS treated with minimal invasive technique with unilateral correction and arthrodesis.

Case presentation

A 13 y/o female diagnosed with severe adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) was admitted to our clinic with indication to surgical correction. The patient never been treated with brace or any other therapy before arriving to our clinic. No associated pathology or myeloradicular malformations were reported. Pre-operative x-rays showed a right thoracic curve of 65° Cobb, 1st type in Lenke classification, skeletal maturity and skeletal maturity was Risser IV (Figure 1). Sagittal parameters were 25° Cobb thoracic kyphosis and 46° Cobb lumbar lordosis, both within physiological limits. Moreover pre-operative bending x-rays showed a good curve flexibility.

Preoperative x-rays of our case of Lenke 1 adolescent idiopathic scoliosis.

In January 2022 we treated the patient with a posterior spine fusion using only a unilateral bar on the scoliosis concavity. The operative time was significantly shorter than the average time for PSF (100 min), total intraoperative blood loss was 200 mL. Immediately after surgery the patient did not require hospitalization in the postoperative intensive care unit. The surgical drain and bladder catheter were removed on the first postoperative day. The patient verticalized on the first postoperative day and was discharged at home three days after surgery. The mean postoperative NRS score during the entire hospital stay was 2/10.

Postoperative discharge x-rays showed thoracic curve correction of 18° Cobb or 72.3% (Figure 2).

Postoperative x-rays of our case of Lenke 1 adolescent idiopathic scoliosis at discharge time.

The patient underwent regular clinical and radiographic follow-ups every six months for two years. All the x-rays taken showed the stability of the implanted instrumentation and curve correction and sagittal alignment remained within physiological limits at follow-up. No mechanical or infectious complications were reported. Furthermore, the patient declared herself fully satisfied with the surgical procedure and the clinical and aesthetic result gained. Our innovative surgical technique involves a median incision at the spinous line with the prone patient. Skeletonization is performed monolaterally at the level of the concavity of the scoliosis curve.

Correction of the deformity at the curve apex requires particular attention. At the apex of the scoliotic curve, screws are inserted slightly obliquely relative to the anatomical direction of the vertebral pedicle, oriented toward the superior endplate. This region, being subcortical, typically presents a lower density of trabecular bone.

Additionally, screws at this level should be longer and larger in diameter (6.5 mm), preferably featuring a dual-thread design to achieve greater primary stability. Importantly, at the first and last apical vertebrae, uniplanar and polyaxial screws are utilized.

Regarding correction, a 6 mm titanium or chrome-cobalt rod, pre-contoured into hyperkyphosis corresponding to the apex of the scoliotic curve, is employed. Initially, the rod is positioned neutrally at the two most distal levels. Subsequently, vertebrae at the apex are progressively engaged onto the rod using appropriate reducers, distributing forces evenly across all screws. Gentle pressure is applied on the convex side at the rib hump to optimize deformity correction and reduce mechanical stress on the concave-side screws.

This maneuver typically leads to some loss of kyphosis, thus it may need to be repeated 2–3 times by replacing the rod. Alternatively, a doubled rod segment secured by domino connectors can be used to enhance rod stiffness at the site of deformity. Once correction is achieved, the doubled segment is removed to overcome the viscoelastic resistance of ligaments and intervertebral discs.

Before final tightening, direct derotation is performed by applying pressure on the rib hump at the apex of convexity. Lastly, distraction and compression maneuvers are executed to evenly align the distal intervertebral disc segments.

The rod is contoured into hyperkyphosis at the vertebral segments corresponding to the apex of the curve and subsequently applied along the entire length of the deformity.

Then facetectomies and posterior osteotomies are infixed only on the concavity side. Reduction maneuvers are performed with special reducers with a pre-shaped bar in hyperkyphosis and contralateral counterthrust. We used a cobalt chrome (Cr-Co) 6.0 mm diameter rod the permit a major capacity of correction. The choice of the instrumented area follows the same rules and principles used for LIV and UIV in Lenke type 1 scoliosis.

Discussion

In recent years Surgical treatment for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis has become increasingly safe and effective. The use of pedicle screws and modern derotation systems have enabled increasingly brilliant results in terms of deformity correction and arthrodesis (1, 2). The advent of evoked potentials and the development of peri- and intraoperative anaesthesia protocols and procedures have further made scoliosis surgery safer (3–6). The latest development is to make this surgery less invasive.

The introduction of percutaneous and minimally invasive procedures in the treatment of scoliosis has undoubtedly demonstrated results that are superimposable on standard techniques with less blood loss, lower infectious risks, less postoperative pain and shorter hospital stay, as well as lower health care costs (7–11). To our knowledge, ours is the first case reported in the literature of unilateral arthrodesis with modern pedicle fixation and derotation systems in a patient with AIS without any known additional pathology, with a clinical and radiographic follow-up of more than two years.

Previously, Wattenbarger et al. (12) compared 43 patients treated with a single-bar construct with only laminar hooks with a cohort of 103 patients treated with the same technique but with bar bilaterally, reporting less blood loss and surgical time but more mechanical complications in the single-bar group, especially in patients with double thoracic and lumbar curves.

Tsirikos et al. (13) reported 16 cases of patients with idiopathic scoliosis with comorbidities or high infectious risks treated with unilateral instrumentation with satisfactory corrections (70%), normal blood loss (460 mL mean value) and reduced surgical time (180 min mean value). However, the technique described by the author involves bilateral skeletisation of the spine, use of distal screws and proximal hooks, or conversion of dual-rod techniques to definitive hybrid systems. No complications were reported but radiographic values at last follow-up were not reported.

In our case there is unilateral skeletonization of the column on the instrumented side only. This evidently resulted in a marked reduction in blood loss (180 mL) and surgical time (100 min). In addition, only pedicle screws and modern reduction and derotation systems were used. the present case was selected as non-severe scoliosis (<70°) in a pure thoracic curve (Lenke 1A) of 65 degrees, with excellent reducibility to preoperative bending. The patient had no other pathology. No clinical complications or radiographic changes in the curve or compensation curves were reported at the last follow-up (Figure 3). The patient reported high satisfaction with the surgical outcome, minimal postoperative pain, and a rapid return to daily activities and school. She also expressed satisfaction with the aesthetic improvement and reported no limitations in her quality of life at the last follow-up.

Hypothesis-generating

We propose a new surgical approach in Lenke 1 AIS scoliosis. This technique is reserved for patient with moderate-severe scoliosis (≤70° cobb angle), with good reduction of the curve on preoperative bending x-ray (at least 30% of reduction) and with (Risser ≥ 3. In conclusion, the surgical technique described in this study proved to be less invasive compared to the standard technique, with a significant reduction in operative time and intraoperative blood loss, as the use of the screws is reduced by the half. Furthermore, no compromises in patient safety were observed. In our case with more than 3 years of follow up we don't assist any mechanical complications, no clinical disturb, no progression of the curve. The unilateral arthrodesis allows the stability of the curve and we believe that leads to spontaneous skeletal ankylosis on contralateral side.

However, it is important to emphasize that these results are based on the analysis of a single case. Therefore, to confirm the efficacy and safety of this new technique, further studies with a larger sample size are necessary. Only through a more extensive data collection can these preliminary observations be fully validated.

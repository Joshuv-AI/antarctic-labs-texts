The role of age and severity of deformity in the choice of treatment for pectus carinatum: a comparison of braces and surgical correction
Authors: Turkan Dubus, Mehmet Ali Unal, Mehmet Yazici, Gokce Cangel, Erman Bagatur Ozturk
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPectus carinatum (PC) is a congenital chest wall deformity that can lead to aesthetic, functional and psychosocial problems, especially in adolescence. The choice of treatment usually depends on clinical factors such as the severity of the deformity, the stiffness of the chest wall and the age of the patient. This study aimed to compare the clinical outcomes of conservative and surgical treatment modalities for PC and to examine the associations among age, deformity severity and treatment selection.MethodsA retrospective review of 118 patients treated between 2009 and 2022 was conducted. Patients were categorized into three groups on the basis of their clinical characteristics: dynamic bracing (n = 53), the Abramson procedure (n = 57), and the Ravitch procedure (n = 8). The primary outcomes included aesthetic satisfaction, functional improvement, the complication rate, and overall patient satisfaction. Patient-reported outcomes were assessed using a structured but nonvalidated questionnaire developed by the authors and reviewed by a multidisciplinary panel.ResultsA statistically significant association was observed between patient age and deformity severity (P = 0.021). Aesthetic satisfaction scores were significantly higher in the Abramson group than in the bracing group (mean: 4.6 ± 0.3 vs. 4.1 ± 0.4, P < 0.001). Functional improvement was observed across all treatment groups, with a nonsignificant trend favoring surgical interventions (P = 0.089). The complication rate was highest in the Ravitch group (25.0%) and lowest in the bracing group (3.7%) (P = 0.013). In this retrospective cohort, overall patient satisfaction scores were highest in patients treated with the Abramson procedure (mean: 9.0 ± 0.8, P < 0.001). The presence of syndromic or systemic comorbidities was not significantly associated with clinical outcomes.ConclusionPatient age and deformity severity were associated with treatment selection in PC. In this cohort, surgical correction, particularly the Abramson procedure, was associated with higher aesthetic satisfaction and overall patient-reported satisfaction in appropriately selected patients. However, given the retrospective study design and the use of a nonvalidated patient-reported outcome measure, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Further prospective studies using standardized and validated outcome measures are warranted to confirm these observations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-026-03958-5.

Introduction

PC is a congenital deformity of the anterior chest wall characterized by outward protrusion of the sternum and costal cartilages. Although it does not usually affect cardiopulmonary function, prominent chest contours often cause psychosocial problems, especially during adolescence. PC occurs predominantly in males and is usually more pronounced during periods of rapid growth, such as puberty. The estimated prevalence is between 0.06% and 0.23% in the general population [1, 2].

The choice of treatment for PC depends primarily on the severity of the deformity, the flexibility of the chest wall and the age of the patient. In patients with mild and flexible deformities, dynamic compression bracing is commonly considered the first-line treatment because it is noninvasive and has high success rates in appropriately selected individuals [3, 5–7]. Surgical correction is generally reserved for more rigid or progressive deformities and includes minimally invasive options such as the Abramson procedure and more invasive open repair methods such as the Ravitch procedure [8–12].

Although these treatment approaches have demonstrated effectiveness in selected patient populations, few studies have directly compared all three modalities bracing, Abramson and Ravitch within a stratified clinical cohort. In addition, most studies have not used validated PROMs, limiting the reliability and comparability of long-term satisfaction data. Accordingly, a structured but nonvalidated questionnaire was used in the present study to assess patient-reported outcomes.

The aim of this study was to retrospectively compare the clinical outcomes of dynamic compression, the Abramson procedure and the Ravitch procedure in patients with PC, stratified by severity of deformity and age. The primary outcomes included aesthetic satisfaction, functional improvement, complication rates and overall patient satisfaction.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective cohort study included patients diagnosed with and treated for PC at our institution between January 2009 and December 2022. Eligible patients were between 9 and 22 years of age, had a diagnosis confirmed by both physical examination and radiologic imaging, had completed the prescribed treatment, and had at least six months of postoperative follow-up data.

The exclusion criteria were posttraumatic or secondary chest wall deformities, incomplete clinical records, or early discontinuation of treatment. The presence of syndromic or systemic comorbidities (e.g., Marfan syndrome, scoliosis, asthma) was documented and included in subsequent subgroup analyses but was not considered an exclusion criterion.

Among the 127 patients initially assessed, 118 fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were included in the final analysis. On the basis of predefined clinical characteristics such as severity of deformity, chest wall stiffness and patient age, patients were assigned to one of three treatment groups. Patients with mild and flexible deformities were treated with dynamic compression bracing (n = 53). Patients with moderately rigid deformities were treated with the Abramson procedure (n = 57), and patients with severe and rigid deformities received the Ravitch procedure (n = 8). Because treatment allocation was based on predefined clinical criteria rather than randomization, the results should be interpreted with consideration of potential selection bias.

Treatment allocation and procedures

All patients underwent a standardized examination prior to treatment, which included a physical examination, palpation-based assessment of chest wall stiffness, postural analysis, pulmonary function testing (including forced vital capacity [FVC] and forced expiratory volume in one second [FEV1]), and standardized photo documentation. The photographs were taken under standardized lighting and positioning conditions. Pre- and post-treatment images were independently evaluated by two blinded thoracic surgeons to minimize observer bias. These assessments were repeated 6 and 12 months after treatment to assess aesthetic and functional outcomes. Allocation to treatment was based on predetermined clinical criteria, including severity of deformity, chest wall stiffness, and patient age. Patients with mild and flexible deformities received dynamic compression bracing (n = 53), patients with moderately rigid deformities were treated via the Abramson procedure (n = 57), and patients with severe and rigid deformities were treated via the Ravitch procedure (n = 8). Given the nonrandomized nature of treatment allocation, selection bias cannot be excluded.

In the Abramson group, a submuscular steel bar was inserted anterior to the sternum without thoracoscopic guidance and remained in place for a median of 24 months. Aesthetic and functional evaluations were performed following bar removal. Representative pre- and postoperative images are shown in Fig. 1.

As shown in Fig. 2, a custom-made dynamic compression brace was used in the bracing group. Patients were instructed to wear the brace for 16–20 h per day. The mean duration of treatment was 8.4 months (range: 4–14 months). Treatment compliance, skin integrity, patient comfort and morphological improvement were monitored at monthly follow-up visits.

In the Ravitch group, the procedure consisted of subperichondrial resection of the deformed costal cartilages combined with transverse sternal osteotomy, followed by anterior stabilization via steel wires. This technique was preferentially applied in patients with severe and rigid deformities. Owing to the limited number of patients in this group, analyses involving the Ravitch cohort were interpreted descriptively.

Evaluation of patient satisfaction

Patient satisfaction was evaluated via a five-item questionnaire developed by the authors to assess perceptions of cosmetic outcome, daily functional capacity, social confidence, physical comfort, and overall satisfaction. Each item was rated on a 5-point Likert scale, and overall satisfaction was determined via a 0–10 numerical rating scale (NRS). The content of the questionnaire was based on areas frequently mentioned in previous studies and was reviewed for clinical relevance by a multidisciplinary panel of pediatric surgeons and clinical psychologists. The questionnaire was administered after completion of the prescribed treatment, at the time point when both the treating physician and the patient agreed that the treatment outcome had stabilized. The instrument has not undergone formal psychometric validation; therefore, results derived from this questionnaire should be regarded as exploratory. While the questionnaire reflects patient-centered outcomes relevant to clinical practice, its lack of formal validation limits methodological rigor and direct comparison with studies employing validated PROMs. Future prospective studies should include validated patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) to increase methodological reliability and enable cross-study comparisons. The patient satisfaction questionnaire used in this study is provided as Supplementary Material.

Monitoring of complications

All treatment-related complications were systematically documented. For patients who underwent surgery (Abramson or Ravitch), complications such as displacement of the bar, wound infection and haematoma formation were recorded. In the bracing group, the main complications were skin irritation and poor adhesion of the device. Given the inherent differences in risk profiles between surgical and conservative treatment, complication rates were analysed primarily within the surgical groups and not across all three treatment groups.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were performed via IBM SPSS Statistics Version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of continuous variables was tested via the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Variables with a normal distribution are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared among treatment groups via one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). The ANOVA results are presented in the form F(df₁, df₂) = value, where df₁ and df₂ represent the degrees of freedom between and within groups, respectively. For variables that did not meet the normality assumption, the data are expressed as medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs, Q1–Q3) and were compared via the Kruskal–Wallis test. Post hoc analyses were only performed if the overall result of the test was statistically significant. Following ANOVA, Tukey’s HSD test (honestly significant difference) was applied, and the Mann–Whitney U test was used for nonparametric pairwise comparisons. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages, and group differences were evaluated via Pearson’s chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test, respectively. The chi-square test results are expressed as χ²(df) = value, where df denotes degrees of freedom. All tests were two-sided, and a P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient demographics

A total of 118 patients met the inclusion criteria. The mean age of the study population was 13.2 ± 2.1 years (range: 9–22). The cohort comprised 88 males (74.6%) and 30 females (25.4%). The treatment modalities were distributed as follows: dynamic compression bracing (n = 53, 44.9%), the Abramson procedure (n = 57, 48.3%) and the Ravitch procedure (n = 8, 6.8%).

A statistically significant difference in mean age was found between the treatment groups (F(2,115) = 3.98, P = 0.021). The mean age was 12.9 ± 2.0 years in the bracing group, 13.4 ± 2.1 years in the Abramson group and 14.1 ± 1.8 years in the Ravitch group. No significant difference was found in the sex distribution among the groups (χ²(2) = 0.89, p = 0.640) (Table 1).

Aesthetic outcomes

The values for aesthetics differed significantly among the treatment groups (P < 0.001, Kruskal–Wallis test). The highest scores were reported in the Abramson group (median: 4.6; IQR: 4.4–4.8; mean ± SD: 4.6 ± 0.3), followed by the Ravitch group (median: 4.4; IQR: 4.0–4.8; mean ± SD: 4.4 ± 0.5) and the Bracing group (median: 4.1; IQR: 3.8–4.4; mean ± SD: 4.1 ± 0.4). Post hoc analysis (Dunn–Bonferroni) confirmed a statistically significant difference between the Abramson group and the bracing group (P < 0.001), whereas the difference between the Ravitch group and the bracing group was not statistically significant (p = 0.094).

Functional improvement

Functional improvements, as measured by pulmonary function tests and the six-minute walk test (6MWT), were observed in all treatment groups. The proportion of patients showing functional improvement was 65.0% in the bracing group, 82.0% in the Abramson group and 78.0% in the Ravitch group. The mean percentage improvement in pulmonary function and physical activity was greater in the surgical groups; however, these differences did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.089, one-way ANOVA) (Table 4).

Complications

Complications occurred in a total of 14 patients (11.9%). The complication rate differed significantly between the treatment groups (χ²(2) = 8.69, P = 0.013). Mild skin irritation occurred in 2 patients (3.7%) in the bracing group. In the Abramson group, complications occurred in 10 patients (17.5%), including displacement of the bar (n = 3), local infection (n = 4) and skin irritation (n = 3). In the Ravitch group, 2 patients (25.0%) developed an infection at the surgical site or a postoperative hematoma (Table 5).

Patient satisfaction

The satisfaction values on a numerical rating scale of 0–10 differed significantly between the treatment groups (P < 0.001, Kruskal‒Wallis test). The Abramson group reported the highest scores (mean ± SD: 9.0 ± 0.8), followed by the Ravitch (8.2 ± 1.0) and bracing (7.8 ± 1.1) groups. The post hoc analysis revealed significantly greater satisfaction in the Abramson group than in the bracing group (P < 0.001), whereas the difference between the Ravitch group and the bracing group was not statistically significant (P = 0.081) (Table 6).

Subgroup analysis: syndromic or systemic conditions

Among the 118 patients, 18 (15.2%) had syndromic or systemic comorbidities (e.g., Marfan syndrome, Noonan syndrome, scoliosis, asthma). There were no statistically significant differences in aesthetic outcomes (4.3 ± 0.4 vs. 4.4 ± 0.5, P = 0.572), functional improvement rates (72% vs. 74%, p = 0.764) or satisfaction scores (8.4 ± 1.0 vs. 8.7 ± 1.1, p = 0.489) between patients with and without comorbidities (Table 7).

Multivariate analysis

A multivariable linear regression model was created to identify independent predictors of the aesthetic outcome. The dependent variable was the aesthetic score, and the independent variables included treatment modality, age, sex, presence of syndromic/systemic disease, functional improvement and complications.

Only the treatment modality was significantly associated with the aesthetic outcome. Compared with bracing, both the Abramson procedure (β = 0.500, P < 0.001) and the Ravitch procedure (β = 0.300, p = 0.002) were associated with greater aesthetic satisfaction. Other variables, including sex, age, comorbidities, functional improvement and complications, were not statistically significant (Table 8).

Discussion

This study presents a comparative evaluation of three treatment modalities for pectus carinatum (PC) dynamic compression bracing, the Abramson procedure, and the Ravitch procedure stratified by deformity severity and patient age. The findings provide clinically relevant insights into the relative advantages and limitations of each modality within a real-world treatment framework.

A statistically significant association was observed between patient age and deformity severity, with older patients tending to present with increased chest wall stiffness. This finding is consistent with previous studies showing increasing chest wall stiffness with age, which reduces the effectiveness of bracing and favours surgical intervention in older adolescents [1, 6]. Accordingly, early identification of flexible deformities may increase the likelihood of successful conservative management, although this relationship should be interpreted in the context of individual growth patterns and skeletal maturity.

In terms of aesthetic outcomes, patients treated with the Abramson procedure reported significantly higher satisfaction scores than patients in the bracing group did and slightly higher scores than patients treated with the Ravitch technique did. These findings are in line with previous reports suggesting favorable cosmetic outcomes with the Abramson technique in patients with moderately rigid deformities [8, 13]. Although the Ravitch group achieved acceptable results, the higher complication rate (25%) reflects the more invasive nature of open surgery, which is usually reserved for severe deformities [14].

Despite the lower scores for aesthetics and satisfaction, dynamic compression bracing remained an effective treatment option for selected younger patients with flexible deformities who demonstrated adequate treatment compliance. This finding is consistent with the literature highlighting the importance of early treatment initiation and treatment compliance in paediatric patients [2, 7, 15].

Functional improvement was observed across all treatment groups, with a nonsignificant trend favoring surgical interventions. However, as the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.089), these findings should be interpreted cautiously. The observed improvements may reflect subjective patient perception or psychosocial benefit rather than objective cardiopulmonary enhancement, as previous studies have reported limited baseline functional impairment in patients with PC [16, 17].

As expected, complication rates varied by modality. The Ravitch group demonstrated the highest complication rate (25%), including surgical site infection and hematoma formation [11]. Moderate complications occurred in the Abramson group (17.5%), mainly bar displacement and local infection, whereas the bracing group had the lowest rate (3.7%), which is related to the noninvasive nature of the procedure [10, 18, 19].

Patient satisfaction was highest in the Abramson group, which is consistent with previous findings linking successful cosmetic outcomes to psychosocial benefits [20, 21]. It should be noted that patient-reported outcomes in this study were assessed using a structured but nonvalidated questionnaire. Although the instrument captured key patient-centered domains, the lack of formal validation limits direct comparison with studies employing standardized PROMs [12, 22].

A subgroup analysis revealed that syndromic or systemic comorbidities had no significant impact on aesthetic, functional or satisfaction outcomes. This finding is consistent with the literature suggesting that appropriate preoperative planning can ensure favourable outcomes in such patients [23].

Multivariate analysis identified treatment modality as the only independent factor associated with aesthetic outcome in this cohort. This finding suggests that, within the limitations of the study design, patient selection and choice of intervention may have a greater influence on cosmetic outcomes than demographic or clinical variables such as age or comorbidity status.

Although short- to medium-term improvements were observed, the duration of follow-up may not have been sufficient to detect late recurrences, particularly in the bracing group, where ongoing skeletal growth and compliance may influence long-term durability. Recurrence rates of up to 20% after brace removal have been reported, emphasizing the need for long-term monitoring [6].

This study represents one of the few comparative analyses incorporating bracing, Abramson, and Ravitch techniques while stratifying patients by deformity stiffness and age and integrating structured patient feedback. However, as bar removal was not performed during the study period, long-term aesthetic durability and recurrence following surgical correction could not be fully assessed. These limitations emphasize the need for longer follow-up in future studies.

Conclusion

This stratified analysis suggests that deformity severity, chest wall stiffness, and patient age are clinically relevant factors associated with treatment selection in pectus carinatum. Dynamic compression bracing may be considered a reasonable option for younger, compliant patients with flexible deformities, whereas surgical approaches may be appropriate for more rigid or advanced cases. Among surgical options, the Abramson procedure was associated with higher aesthetic satisfaction and overall patient-reported satisfaction in appropriately selected patients. Given the retrospective design, potential classification bias, and the use of a nonvalidated patient-reported outcome measure, the findings of this study should be regarded as exploratory rather than definitive. Further prospective studies are essential to develop standardized protocols and improve the reproducibility of the results.

Limitations

The retrospective nature of the study and the nonrandomized allocation of treatment modalities introduce potential selection bias. The relatively small size of the Ravitch group limits the statistical power for subgroup analyses. Patient outcomes were measured via a structured but unvalidated satisfaction questionnaire, which is clinically relevant but limits the methodological rigour and comparison between studies. Additionally, the absence of bar removal during the study period precluded assessment of long-term surgical durability and recurrence. Future prospective, multicentre studies using validated outcome measures are warranted.

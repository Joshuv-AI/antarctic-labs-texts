Constructing λ \lambda -Angenent curve by flow method
Pak Tung Ho

Abstract
. Using a modified curve shortening flow, we construct λ \lambda -Angenent curve, which was first constructed by the shooting method.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C44; Secondary 53C22, 53C42

1.
Introduction

The mean curvature flow is the gradient flow of the area functional

\int_{\Sigma}d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x).

More precisely, a smooth
one-parameter family of hypersurfaces
\Sigma(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
where
t\in[0,T)
, is a solution of the
mean curvature flow

if

\frac{\partial x}{\partial t}=\vec{H},

where
\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector of
the hypersurface at point
x
.
The mean curvature flow
has been studied extensively for the last few decades;
and it seems to be impossible to include all related references.
We refer
the readers to

[
8
]

for a survey of the mean curvature flow.

A smooth hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n=1}
is called a

self-shrinker
if
it satisfies the equation

\vec{H}+x^{\perp}/2=0.

Huisken

[
13
]

observed that if
\{\sqrt{-t}\Sigma\}_{t<0}

is a solution of the mean curvature flow,
then
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker.
Moreover, Huisken observed that a shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

is the critical point of Gaussian area functional

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}e^{-|x|^{2}/4}d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x).

Self-shrinkers are central objects of study in the
mean curvature flow, since they serve as singularity models
[
12
]
.
Different techniques have been used to construct explicit
examples of self-shrinkers. See
[
5
,
7
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
In particular,
Angenent
[
3
]
constructed
a rotationally symmetric
self-shrinking mean curvature flow
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

that is topologically
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times S^{n-1}
before it becomes singular.
In fact, Angenent constructed a closed embedded hypersurface
\Sigma_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

that is rotationally symmetric such that
\{\sqrt{-t}\Sigma_{n}\}_{t<0}
is
a solution of the mean curvature flow.
In view of the Huisken’s observation mentioned above, Angenent reduced the existence of a rotational symmetric shrinker
to the existence of a closed embedded geodesic in the half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}=\{(r,x):r>0\}
equipped
with the metric

g=r^{2(n-1)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}+x^{2}}{2}}g_{E}

where
and
g_{E}=dr^{2}+dx^{2}
is the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Angenent used a shooting method to construct such a closed embedded geodesic.
See
[
9
]
for a survey of the shooting method.

It was observed by Chen and Sun in
[
6
, Appendix B]

that if one replaces
n-1
by some
\lambda>0
,
all the proofs in
[
3
]
still work.
That is to say, using Angenent’s shooting method,
one can construct a closed embedded geodesic
in the half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}=\{(r,x):r>0\}
equipped
with the metric

(1.1)

g=\alpha^{2}(dr^{2}+dx^{2})=\alpha^{2}g_{E}

where

\alpha^{2}=r^{2(\lambda-1)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}+x^{2}}{2}}
and
\lambda>1
is a constant,
and
g_{E}
is the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Such a geodesic is called
\lambda
-Angenent curve in
[
6
]
,
and the
\lambda
-Angenent curve is used as the barrier. See
[
6
]
for the details.

At the end of the paper
[
6
]
, Chen and Sun said that
“It is worth noting that Drugan and Nguyen
[
10
]

also constructed doughnuts using a variational method.
It is still an open question whether
their construction coincides with Angenent’s construction.
It seems that their method can also be applied to construct
\lambda
-Angenent curves.”
The following Theorem
1.1
shows that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a simple closed geodesic
\gamma_{\infty}(u)=(r(u),x(u))
,
u\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
in the half-plane
(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},g)
.
Moreover, its length
L_{g}(\gamma_{\infty})
with respect to the metric
g

is less than the length of the double cover of the half-line
x=0
.

Note that Drugan-Nguyen
[
10
]

proved Theorem
1.1
for the case when
\lambda=n-1
.
In fact, the proof of Theorem
1.1
closely follows that of
[
10
]
.
We also remark that, similar to the case when
\lambda=n-1
,
we do not know whether
the
\lambda
-Angenent curve constructed in Theorem
1.1

coincides with the one constructed by the shooting method.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we collect some basic facts which will be used
in the proof of our main theorem.

The vectors
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
form an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

for the usual Euclidean metric
g_{E}
,
and they have length
\alpha
with respect to the metric
g
,
where
g
is the metric given in (
1.1
).
To avoid confusion, we will denote with a subscript
g

for the geometric
quantities and unit vectors taken with respect to the metric
g
,
while we will use a subscript
E
when we refer to the Euclidean metric
g_{E}
.

Given a curve
\gamma(u)=(r(u),x(u))
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
the speed, unit tangent, and normal vectors
are given by

(2.1)

v=\alpha\sqrt{(x^{\prime})^{2}+(r^{\prime})^{2}},~~\mathbf{t}_{g}=\frac{1}{v}\left(r^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+x^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right),~~\mathbf{n}_{g}=\frac{1}{v}\left(-x^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+r^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right).

We denote
ds=vdu
, where
s
is the arclength of
\gamma
with respect
to the metric
g
. One can compute the geodesic curvature:

(2.2)

\begin{split}k_{g}&=\frac{1}{v}\left[\frac{x^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}r^{\prime}}{(x^{\prime})^{2}+(r^{\prime})^{2}}-\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{r}-\frac{r}{2}\right)x^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}xr^{\prime}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{v}\left[k_{E}-\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{r}-\frac{r}{2}\right)x^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}xr^{\prime}\right].\end{split}

Therefore, the geodesic equation for
(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},g)
is given by

(2.3)

\frac{x^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}r^{\prime}}{(x^{\prime})^{2}+(r^{\prime})^{2}}=\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{r}-\frac{r}{2}\right)x^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}xr^{\prime}.

Then the length of the curve
\gamma(u)
,
a\leq u\leq b
, in
(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},g)
is

(2.4)

L_{g}(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}vdu.

Definition 2.1
.

We use the notation

r_{\lambda}=\sqrt{2(\lambda-1)}.

The self-shrinking cylinder
\mathcal{C}
corresponds to the geodesic

(r(u),x(u))=(r_{\lambda},u)
. The self-shrinking
half-line
\mathcal{P}
corresponds to the geodesic

(r(u),x(u))=(u,0)
.

Note that the lengths of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{P}
are respectively given by

(2.5)

\begin{split}L_{g}(\mathcal{C})&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(2(\lambda-1))^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{2(\lambda-1)+u^{2}}{4}}du=2\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{2(\lambda-1)}{e}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}},\\
L_{g}(\mathcal{P})&=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{\lambda-1}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4}}du.\end{split}

We have the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
\lambda>1
, there holds

L_{g}(\mathcal{P})<L_{g}(\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

By change of variables
v=u^{2}/4
, we can write

(2.6)

L_{g}(\mathcal{P})=2^{\lambda-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\frac{\lambda-2}{2}}e^{-v}dv=2^{\lambda-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right).

It follows from
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
that

\Gamma(x+1)\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{x+\frac{1}{2}}{e}\right)^{x+\frac{1}{2}}

for any real positive number
x
.
Applying this with
x=\frac{\lambda}{2}-1
, we can estimate the right hand side of (
2.6
) as follows:

\begin{split}L_{g}(\mathcal{P})=2^{\lambda-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\leq 2^{\lambda-1}\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{2e}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}&=\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{2(\lambda-1)}{e}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}\\
&<2\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{2(\lambda-1)}{e}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}=L_{g}(\mathcal{C}),\end{split}

which proved the assertion.
∎

The Gauss curvature
K_{g}
of
(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},g)
is

(2.7)

K_{g}=\alpha^{-2}\left(1+\frac{\lambda-1}{r^{2}}\right).

Definition 2.3
.

The enclosed Gauss area of a simple closed curve
\gamma:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
is

(2.8)

GA_{g}(\gamma):=\iint_{\Omega}\left(1+\frac{\lambda-1}{r^{2}}\right)dxdr,

where
\Omega
is the region enclosed by
\gamma
.

For a
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma
, it follows from (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that
the Gauss-Bonnet formula is given by

(2.9)

\iint_{\Omega}\left(1+\frac{\lambda-1}{r^{2}}\right)dxdr=2\pi-\oint_{\gamma}k_{g}ds.

3.
A modified curve shortening flow

Given a closed curve
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
we consider the flow that evolves according
to the normal velocity

(3.1)

V_{g}=\frac{k_{g}}{K_{g}},

where
k_{g}
is the geodesic curvature and
K_{g}
is the Gauss curvature
at the given point on the curve.
When
K_{g}
is positive and uniformly bounded away from zero,
this flow exhibits properties similar to the usual curve shortening flow.

The flow was first studied in
[
11
]
by Gage.
Using this flow, Gage proved the existence
of geodesics on spheres.
The idea is similar here, but our manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},g)
is not closed and
its Gauss curvature is not bounded.
We will use lines
r=r(t)
as barriers to show that
closed curves in the interior of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

do not reach regions
where the Gauss curvature blow up in finite time.

3.1.
Evolution of lines
r=C

From (
2.2
), we see that the line
r=r_{0}
has geodesic curvature:

k_{g}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{2}-\frac{\lambda-1}{r_{0}}\right).

We can compute the Euclidean speed
V_{E}
in the direction
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
as follows:

V_{E}=\frac{V_{g}}{\alpha}=\frac{k_{g}}{\alpha K_{g}}=\frac{r_{0}(r_{0}^{2}-2(\lambda-1))}{2(r_{0}^{2}+\lambda-1)}.

Hence, lines on the left hand side of the cylinder
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
r=r_{0}<r_{\lambda}
,
move further to the left.
Similarly, lines on the right hand side of the cylinder
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
r=r_{0}>r_{\lambda}
,
move further to the right.
When
r_{0}<r_{\lambda}
,
we have

V_{E}\geq-cr
with a positive constant depending only on
r_{0}
and
\lambda
. Therefore,

r(t)\geq r_{0}e^{-ct},~~r_{0}<r_{\lambda}.

Therefore, no vertical line reaches the
x
-axis in finite time. Similarly,
when
r_{0}>r_{\lambda}
, we have

r(t)\leq r_{0}e^{\frac{t}{2}}
, and no vertical line goes to infinity in finite time.

3.2.
Short-time and long time existence

In the case where the positive Gauss curvature is uniformly bounded from above and below away from zero,
the short-time existence of the flow
is obtain by Gage
[
11
]
or by Angenent
[
1
]
.
Given an initial embedded closed curve

\gamma_{0}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
we choose
r_{0}<r_{\lambda}
and
r_{1}>r_{\lambda}

such that
\gamma_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
lies within the slab

\{r\in(r_{0},r_{1})\}
.
For
t<1
, the Gauss curvature is then uniformly bounded in
\{r\in(r_{0}^{-c},r_{1}e^{1/2}\}

and we can apply the short-time existence results there.

We have the following proposition;
its proof is the same as that of
[
10
, Proposition 5]

and is omitted.

Proposition 3.1
.

Denote
\gamma_{t}
the evolution of
\gamma_{0}
at time
t
.

(i) If
\gamma_{0}
is an embedded curve, so is
\gamma_{t}
.

(ii) If
\gamma_{0}
is symmetric with respect to the reflections across the
r
-axis and the image of
\gamma_{0}

in the first quadrant is a graph over the
r
-axis, the same properties
(symmetric and graphical) hold for all
\gamma_{t}
as long as the flow exists.

Along the flow, the arclength evolves according to

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}ds=-k_{g}V_{g}ds=-\frac{k_{g}^{2}}{K_{g}}ds.

This implies that the length
L_{g}(\gamma_{t})
evolves by

(3.2)

\frac{d}{dt}L_{g}(\gamma_{t})=-\int_{\gamma_{t}}\frac{k_{g}^{2}}{K_{g}}ds.

If the
\gamma_{t}
’s
are simple and closed, then they are boundaries of domains
\Omega_{t}
.
Using the Gauss-Bonnet formula in (
2.9
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{\Omega_{t}}K_{g}dA_{g}=-\oint_{\gamma_{t}}K_{g}V_{g}ds=-\oint_{\gamma_{t}}k_{g}ds=-2\pi+\iint_{\Omega_{t}}K_{g}dA_{g}.

Here,
V_{g}
and
k_{g}
are the normal velocity and geodesic curvature in the direction of the inward normal to
\Omega
.
Hence, we have the following:

Proposition 3.2
.

If the Gauss area enclosed by the initial curve is equal to
2\pi
,
then the Gauss area enclosed by
\gamma_{t}
is also
2\pi
whenever the flow exists.

We have the following proposition; its proof is the same as that of
[
10
, Proposition 5]

and is omitted.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\gamma_{0}
be a simple closed curve.
If the domain enclosed by
\gamma_{0}
has Gauss area being equal to
2\pi
,
then the evolution of
\gamma_{0}
with normal velocity
V_{g}=k_{g}/K_{g}
exists for all time.

4.
A family of initial curves

We consider rectangles
R[a,b,c]
with vertices
(a,-c)
,
(a,c)
,

(b,c)
,
(b,-c)
, with
a<b
and
c>0
.
It follows from Proposition
3.3
that if our initial rectangle encloses
Gauss area equal to
2\pi
,
the flow will exist for all time.
If in addition, its perimeter
is less than

2L_{g}(\mathcal{P})
,
the flow cannot converges
to a double cover of a plane,
because it decreases length according to (
3.2
).
There are infinitely many rectangles satisfying
both of these conditions.
For example,
when
a
and
b
are large,
the Gauss area

\displaystyle\iint\left(1+\frac{\lambda-1}{r^{2}}\right)dxdr\sim\iint dxdr
,
but the perimeter will be small because of the exponential weight on the metric.
When
a
and
b
close to
0
,
the integral
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{2}}dr
is unbounded.
Therefore, it is possible
to have a tiny rectangle with Gauss area
2\pi
and with very small perimeter.
Here, we choose a continuous one parameter family that bridges
these two extremes.

Definition 4.1
.

We denote by
L(a,b,c)
the perimeter of the rectangle
R[a,b,c]
with respect
to the metric
g
, i.e.,

(4.1)

L_{g}[a,b,c]:=2(a^{\lambda-1}e^{-a^{2}/4}+b^{\lambda-1}e^{-b^{2}/4})\int_{0}^{c}e^{-x^{2}/4}dx+2e^{-c^{2}/4}\int_{a}^{b}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr.

Fix
c_{0}
to be the positive real number such that

(4.2)

\frac{e^{-c_{0}^{2}/4}}{\int_{0}^{c_{0}}e^{-x^{2}/4}dx}=2.

The number
c_{0}
exists and is unique, and is approximately
0.481
.
Also, let
\lambda_{0}
be the positive real number such that

\sqrt{2\lambda_{0}+1-\sqrt{8\lambda_{0}+1}}=\sqrt{2(\lambda_{0}+4)}-2,

which is equal to
(28+11\sqrt{7})/9
, which is approximately
6.3448
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
\lambda>1
.
For every
a,b\in(0,\infty)
, we have

L_{g}[a,b,c]<2L_{g}(\mathcal{P}).

Proof.

In view of (
4.2
), we can write (
4.1
) as

L_{g}(a,b,c_{0})=f_{\lambda}(a)+g_{\lambda}(b),

where
M=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c_{0}}e^{-x^{2}/4}dx
and

\begin{split}f_{\lambda}(a)&=2a^{\lambda-1}e^{-a^{2}/4}M+4M\int_{a}^{0}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr,\\
g_{\lambda}(b)&=2b^{\lambda-1}e^{-b^{2}/4}M+4M\int_{0}^{b}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr.\end{split}

The derivative of
f
is

f_{\lambda}^{\prime}(a)=-a^{\lambda-2}e^{-a^{2}/4}M\big(a^{2}+4a-2(\lambda-1)\big).

Therefore,
\displaystyle\max_{a\in[0,\infty)}f_{\lambda}(a)

is achieved at
a_{\lambda}=-2+\sqrt{2(1+\lambda)}
.
Similarly, one can show that

\displaystyle\max_{b\in[0,\infty)}g_{\lambda}(b)

is achieved at
b_{\lambda}=2+\sqrt{2(1+\lambda)}
.
It follows from (
2.5
) that

L_{g}(\mathcal{P})=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{\lambda-1}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{4}}du.

Hence, it suffices to show that

(4.3)

f_{\lambda}(a_{\lambda})+g_{\lambda}(b_{\lambda})<2\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}dr

for any
\lambda>1
.

To prove (
4.3
), we are going to prove the following two lemmas:

Lemma 4.3
.

The inequality (
4.3
) holds whenever
1\leq\lambda\leq 7
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There holds

(4.4)

f_{\lambda+2}(a_{\lambda+2})+g_{\lambda+2}(b_{\lambda+2})<2\lambda\big(f_{\lambda}(a_{\lambda})+g_{\lambda}(b_{\lambda})\big)

for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
.

Let us assume Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
for the time being;
and we can see that (
4.3
) holds for all
\lambda>1
.
It follows from integration by parts that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\lambda+1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}dr&=\Big[-2r^{\lambda}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}\Big]_{0}^{\infty}+2\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}dr\\
&=2\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}dr.\end{split}

This together with Lemma
4.4
implies that
if (
4.3
) holds for a particular value of
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
, then
(
4.3
) holds for
\lambda+2
.
Since Lemma
4.3
asserts that (
4.3
) holds for all
1\leq\lambda\leq 7
,
we can conclude that (
4.3
) holds for all
\lambda>1
.
This proves Proposition
4.2
by assuming Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
.
∎

It remains to prove Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Note that left hand side of (
4.3
) can be written as

(4.5)

2M\left(a_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+b_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}\right)+4M\int_{a_{\lambda}}^{b_{\lambda}}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr.

It follows from (
4.2
) that

M=\int_{0}^{c_{0}}e^{-x^{2}/4}dx=\frac{1}{2}e^{-c_{0}^{2}/4}<\frac{1}{2}.

This together with (
4.5
) implies that the left hand side of (
4.3
)
is less than or equal to

a_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+b_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+2\int_{a_{\lambda}}^{b_{\lambda}}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr.

This implies that, in order to prove (
4.3
), it suffices to prove

(4.6)

a_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+b_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}<2\left(\int_{0}^{a_{\lambda}}+\int_{b_{\lambda}}^{\infty}\right)r^{\lambda-1}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr.

By the change of variables, we can further rewrite (
4.6
) as

a_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+b_{\lambda}^{\lambda-1}e^{-b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}<2^{\lambda}\left(\int_{0}^{a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+\int_{b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}^{\infty}\right)x^{\frac{\lambda}{2}-1}e^{-x}dx,

or equivalently,

(4.7)

\left(\frac{a_{\lambda}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}e^{-a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+\left(\frac{b_{\lambda}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}e^{-b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}<2\left(\int_{0}^{a_{\lambda}^{2}/4}+\int_{b_{\lambda}^{2}/4}^{\infty}\right)x^{\frac{\lambda}{2}-1}e^{-x}dx,

Note that the right hand side of (
4.7
) can be written as

(4.8)

2\left(\Gamma\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\Big)-\Gamma\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2},\frac{a_{\lambda}^{2}}{4}\Big)+\Gamma\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2},\frac{b_{\lambda}^{2}}{4}\Big)\right),

where

\Gamma(u,v)=\int_{v}^{\infty}x^{u-1}e^{-x}dx

is the incomplete gamma function.
Using Mathematica, one can see that the function in (
4.8
)
is greater than
1
whenever
1\leq\lambda\leq 7
.
On the other hand, using Mathematica again,
one can see that the function on the left hand side of (
4.7
)
is less than
1
whenever
1\leq\lambda\leq 7
.
This proves Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

It follows from the integration by parts that

2\lambda\int_{a}^{b}r^{\lambda-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}dr=2b^{\lambda}e^{-\frac{b^{2}}{4}}-2a^{\lambda}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{4}}+\int_{a}^{b}r^{\lambda+1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}dr.

Therefore, for all
a,b>0
, we have

(4.9)

\begin{split}2\lambda f_{\lambda}(a)-2f^{\prime}_{\lambda+1}(a)-f_{\lambda+2}(a)&=0,\\
2\lambda g_{\lambda}(b)-2g^{\prime}_{\lambda+1}(b)-g_{\lambda+2}(b)&=0.\end{split}

Since
f^{\prime}_{\lambda+1}(a_{\lambda+1})=g^{\prime}_{\lambda+1}(b_{\lambda+1})=0
,
the inequality (
4.4
)
is equivalent to

(4.10)

\int_{a_{\lambda+1}}^{a_{\lambda+2}}f^{\prime}_{\lambda+2}(u)du+\int_{b_{\lambda+1}}^{b_{\lambda+2}}g^{\prime}_{\lambda+2}(v)dv<2\lambda\left(\int_{a_{\lambda+1}}^{a_{\lambda}}f^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u)du+\int_{b_{\lambda+1}}^{b_{\lambda}}g^{\prime}_{\lambda}(v)dv\right).

Using the variables
u=s-2
and
v=s+2
, we obtain the following equivalent form:

\begin{split}&\int_{r_{\lambda+3}}^{r_{\lambda+4}}\big(2(\lambda+3)-s^{2}\big)\left[(s-2)^{\lambda}e^{-\frac{(s-2)^{2}}{4}}+(s+2)^{\lambda}e^{-\frac{(s+2)^{2}}{4}}\right]ds\\
&<2(\lambda+1)\int_{r_{\lambda+2}}^{r_{\lambda+3}}\big(y^{2}-2(\lambda+1)\big)\left[(y-2)^{\lambda-2}e^{-\frac{(y-2)^{2}}{4}}+(y+2)^{\lambda-2}e^{-\frac{(y+2)^{2}}{4}}\right]dy.\end{split}

For
\lambda>0
, we define the function

h_{\lambda}(s)=e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4}}s^{\lambda},~~H_{\lambda}(s)=h_{\lambda}(s-2)+h_{\lambda}(s+2).

Therefore, if we substitute
s=r_{\lambda+4}-t
and
y=r_{\lambda+2}+t
, we get another equivalent form:

(4.11)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{r_{\lambda+4}-r_{\lambda+3}}t(2r_{\lambda+4}-t)H_{\lambda}(r_{\lambda+4}-t)dt\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt<\int_{0}^{r_{\lambda+3}-r_{\lambda+2}}2\lambda\,t(2r_{\lambda+2}+t)H_{\lambda-2}(r_{\lambda+2}+t)dt.\end{split}

Because of concavity, we have
r_{\lambda+4}-r_{\lambda+3}<r_{\lambda+3}-r_{\lambda+2}
.
Therefore, in order to prove (
4.4
)
and equivalently (
4.11
),
it suffices to prove the following
pointwise inequality:

(4.12)

(2r_{\lambda+4}-t)H_{\lambda}(r_{\lambda+4}-t)<2\lambda(2r_{\lambda+2}+t)H_{\lambda-2}(r_{\lambda+2}+t)

for all
0<t<r_{\lambda+4}-r_{\lambda+3}
.

We claim that the inequality (
4.12
)
is at its tightest when
t=0
.
More precisely, we are going to show that

(4.13)

\begin{split}(2r_{\lambda+4}-t)H_{\lambda}(r_{\lambda+4}-t)&\leq 2r_{\lambda+4}H_{\lambda}(r_{\lambda+4}),\\
4\lambda\,r_{\lambda+2}H_{\lambda-2}(r_{\lambda+2})&\leq 2\lambda(2r_{\lambda+2}+t)H_{\lambda-2}(r_{\lambda+2}+t)\end{split}

for all
0<t<r_{\lambda+4}-r_{\lambda+3}
.
We compute

(4.14)

\begin{split}h^{\prime}_{\lambda}(s)&=e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4}}s^{\lambda-1}\left(\lambda-\frac{s^{2}}{2}\right),\\
h^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}(s)&=e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4}}s^{\lambda-2}\left(\lambda(\lambda-1)-\frac{2\lambda+1}{2}s+\frac{s^{2}}{4}\right).\end{split}

It follows from quadratic formula that

h^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
if and only if

s^{2}\leq s_{0}^{2}=2\lambda+1-\sqrt{8\lambda+1}

or
s^{2}\geq s_{1}^{2}=2\lambda+1+\sqrt{8\lambda+1}
.
For
H_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}
, we are concerned about

s\in(r_{\lambda+3}-2,r_{\lambda+5}-2)\cup(r_{\lambda+3}+2,r_{\lambda+5}+2)

and we see that

r_{\lambda+5}-2<s_{0}~~\mbox{ and }~~s_{1}<r_{\lambda+3}+2,

since

s_{0}=\sqrt{2\lambda+1-\sqrt{8\lambda+1}}>\sqrt{2(\lambda+4)}-2=r_{\lambda+5}-2~~\mbox{ whenever }\lambda>\lambda_{0},

and

s_{1}=\sqrt{2\lambda+1+\sqrt{8\lambda+1}}<\sqrt{2(\lambda+2)}+2=r_{\lambda+3}+2~~\mbox{ whenever }\lambda>1.

Therefore,
H_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(s)>0
for
s\in(r_{\lambda+3},r_{\lambda+4})
and
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
.

We will now show that
H_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r_{\lambda+3})>0
, or equivalently,

Q_{\lambda}:=-\frac{h_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r_{\lambda+3}+2)}{h_{\lambda}^{\prime}(r_{\lambda+3}-2)}<1.

By (
4.14
), we compute

(4.15)

Q_{\lambda}=e^{-2r_{\lambda+3}}\left(\frac{r_{\lambda+3}+2}{r_{\lambda+3}-2}\right)^{\lambda}=e^{-2r_{\lambda+3}}\left(1+\frac{4}{r_{\lambda+3}-2}\right)^{(r_{\lambda+3}^{2}-4)/2}.

One has the following: (see
[
10
, Appendix]
)

(4.16)

\begin{split}f(x)=\left(1+\frac{a}{x}\right)^{x^{2}}\nearrow e^{ax}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}},~~a>0,\\
g(x)=\left(1-\frac{a}{x}\right)^{x^{2}}\searrow e^{-ax}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}},~~a>0\end{split}

as
x\to\infty
. Note that
r_{\lambda+3}>2
whenever
\lambda>1
.
Taking
x=\displaystyle\frac{r_{\lambda+3}-2}{\sqrt{2}}>0

and
a=2\sqrt{2}
, we can estimate

(4.17)

\left(1+\frac{4}{r_{\lambda+3}-2}\right)^{\frac{(r_{\lambda+3}-2)^{2}}{2}}\leq e^{-2(r_{\lambda+3}-2)}e^{-4}.

Since

\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{x}\nearrow e^{x}

as
x\to\infty
, we can estimate

(4.18)

\left(1+\frac{4}{r_{\lambda+3}-2}\right)^{2(r_{\lambda+3}-2)}\leq e^{2(r_{\lambda+3}-2)}.

Using (
4.17
) and (
4.18
),
we can estimate the right hand side of (
4.15
)
as follows:

Q_{\lambda}=e^{-2r_{\lambda+3}}\left(1+\frac{4}{r_{\lambda+3}-2}\right)^{\frac{(r_{\lambda+3}-2)^{2}}{2}+2(r_{\lambda+3}-2)}\leq e^{-2r_{\lambda+3}-4}<1,

since
2r_{\lambda+3}+4\geq 0
.

The rest of the proof is dedicated to proving
the following equivalent formulation of the inequality
(
4.12
) at
t=0
:

(4.19)

\ln(\mathcal{H}(r_{\lambda+4}))<\ln\left(e\frac{\lambda(\lambda+3)}{(\lambda+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda+3}\right)^{\frac{\lambda+3}{2}}\mathcal{H}(r_{\lambda+2})\right)=:I+\ln(\mathcal{H}(r_{\lambda+2})),

where
m=m(s)=\frac{s^{2}}{2}-3
and
\mathcal{H}(s):=s^{-m}H_{m}(s)e^{(s^{2}+4)/4}=(1-\frac{2}{s})^{m}e^{s}+(1+\frac{2}{s})^{m}e^{-s}
.
We closely follow the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 9]
.

We recall the power expansion of
\ln
for
0<y<1
:

(4.20)

\ln(1+y)=y-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{j+1}\frac{y^{j}}{j}+R_{j}^{+}(y),~~-\frac{y^{2K+2}}{2K+2}\leq R_{2K+1}^{+}(y)\leq 0,

and

(4.21)

\ln(1-y)=-y-\frac{y^{2}}{2}-\cdots-\frac{y^{j}}{j}+R_{j}^{-}(y),~~-\frac{y^{j+1}}{(j+1)(1-y)}\leq R_{j}^{-}(y)\leq 0.

Separating the even and odd powers, we have

\begin{split}\left(\frac{s^{2}}{2}-3\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{2}{s}\right)+s&=E_{2K-2}(s)+O_{2K-1}(s)+\mathcal{R}^{-}_{2K-1}(s),\\
\left(\frac{s^{2}}{2}-3\right)\ln\left(1+\frac{2}{s}\right)+s&=E_{2K-2}(s)-O_{2K-1}(s)+\mathcal{R}^{+}_{2K-1}(s).\end{split}

For the odd powers, we just remark that the function
O_{2K-1}
is decreasing in
s
.
We need the formulas for the even powers and the reminder

\begin{split}E_{2K-2}(s)&:=-1+\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\left(\frac{3}{2j}-\frac{2}{2j+2}\right)\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2j},\\
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}_{2K-1}(s)&:=-3R_{2K-1}^{\pm}(\frac{2}{s})+\frac{s^{2}}{2}R_{2K+1}^{\pm}(\frac{2}{s}).\end{split}

From (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), we note that the (lower) bound for
R_{j}^{-}
dominates,
therefore it suffices to consider
\mathcal{R}^{-}
. We obtain

-\frac{2}{(K+1)(s-2)}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2K-1}\leq\mathcal{R}^{-}_{2K-1}(s)\leq\frac{3}{K(s-2)}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2K-1}.

Therefore, for
m(s)=\frac{s^{2}}{2}-3
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(s)&>2\exp\left(E_{2K-2}(s)-\frac{2}{(K+1)(s-2)}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2K-1}\right)\cosh(O_{2K-1}(s)),\\
\mathcal{H}(s)&<2\exp\left(E_{2K-2}(s)+\frac{3}{K(s-2)}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2K-1}\right)\cosh(O_{2K-1}(s)).\end{split}

Because
\cosh(O_{2K-1}(s))>\cosh(O_{2K+1}(s+\epsilon))
,
to finish proving (
4.19
), we show that

(4.22)

\left(E_{2K-2}(s)-E_{2K-2}(s+\epsilon)-\left(\frac{2}{K+1}+\frac{3}{K}\right)\frac{1}{s-2}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2K-1}\right)+I>0

for
s=r_{\lambda+2}
and
s+\epsilon=r_{\lambda+4}

and where
I
was defined implicitly in (
4.19
).
The values of
s
and
s+\epsilon
are now fixed.

First, we estimate the last term of (
4.22
) by expanding in power series and keeping lower order terms
in
(\lambda+1)^{-1}
and
(\lambda+3)^{-1}
:

\begin{split}I&>1+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+1}-\frac{2}{(\lambda+1)^{2}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\lambda+1}-\frac{2}{(\lambda+1)^{2}}\right)^{2}\\
&-1-\frac{1}{\lambda+3}-\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{\lambda+3}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{2}{\lambda+3}\right)^{3}\frac{\lambda+3}{\lambda+1}\\
&>\frac{2}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda+3)}-\frac{3}{(\lambda+1)^{2}}-\frac{4}{3(\lambda+3)^{2}}\\
&>-\frac{3}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda+3)}-\frac{15}{(\lambda+1)^{2}(\lambda+3)^{2}}.\end{split}

The terms of lower order in
E_{2K-2}(s)-E_{2K-2}(s+\epsilon)
are explicitly given by

\begin{split}E_{2K-2}(s)-E_{2K-2}(s+\epsilon)&>\frac{4}{2(\lambda+1)}-\frac{4}{2(\lambda+3)}+\frac{20}{3(2(\lambda+1))^{2}}-\frac{20}{3(2(\lambda+3))^{2}}\\
&=\frac{4}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda+3)}+\frac{20}{3(\lambda+1)^{2}(\lambda+3)^{2}}.\end{split}

Because
\frac{4}{s}>\frac{1}{s-2}
for
s>8/3
(i.e. when
\lambda>23/9
), the error term can be estimated by

-\left(\frac{2}{K+1}+\frac{3}{K}\right)\frac{1}{s-2}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)^{2K-1}>-10K^{-1}(2/s)^{2K}.

Recalling that
s=r_{\lambda+2}
, we obtain

E_{2K-2}(s)-E_{2K-2}(s+\epsilon)+I-10\frac{2^{2K}}{Ks^{2K}}>\frac{1}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda+3)}-10\frac{2^{K}}{K(\lambda+1)^{K}},

which is positive for
K
large enough. This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

For a fixed
\lambda
,
we round the corners of the rectangle while keeping the length
less than
2L_{g}(\mathcal{P})
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let us define
\mathcal{R}[a,b,c_{0}]

as the rectangle
R[a,b,c_{0}]
with rounded corners.
Given
\lambda>1
, all the rectangles are rounded off in the same manner
such that

L_{g}(\mathcal{R}[a,b,c_{0}])<2L_{g}(\mathcal{P})
for all
a,b>0
.

As an immediate corollary, we can extract a one parameter family of
rounded rectangle with enclosed Gauss area equal to
2\pi
.

Corollary 4.6
.

There is a smooth function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

with
\varphi(a)>a
such that the family of rectangles

\mathcal{R}[a,\varphi(a),c_{0}]
satisfies

GA_{g}(\mathcal{R}[a,\varphi(a),c_{0}])=2\pi~~\mbox{ and }~~L_{g}(a,\varphi(a),c_{0})<2L_{g}(\mathcal{P}).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0}\varphi(a)=0
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

be the map with the following properties:

(i)
\Phi(a,0)=\mathcal{R}[a,\varphi(a),c_{0}]
.

(ii) For fixed
a
,
\Phi(a,t)
satisfies the evolution equation (
3.1
).

Then there exists
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\Phi(a_{0},t)
intersects the cylinder
\mathcal{C}

for all time
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proof.

The set of curves that do not intersect the cylinder
\mathcal{C}
is split into two disjoint sets:

\begin{split}A_{1}&=\Big\{\mbox{continuous closed curves }\gamma:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\,|\,\gamma(s)<r_{\lambda},s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\Big\},\\
A_{2}&=\Big\{\mbox{continuous closed curves }\gamma:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\,|\,\gamma(s)>r_{\lambda},s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\Big\}.\end{split}

The cylinder
\mathcal{C}
is a self-shrinker, and as a result, it is stationary under the flow.
By the maximum principle,
if
\Phi(a,t_{0})\in A_{i}
for some
i=1,2
and some
t_{0}
,
then
\Phi(a,t)\in A_{i}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
Now we consider the following subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
:

U_{i}=\{a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\,|\,\exists t>0\mbox{ with }\Phi(a,t)\in A_{i}\}.

Both
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are open and
U_{1}\cap U_{2}=\emptyset
.
Since
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is connected,
we must have
U_{1}\cup U_{2}\neq\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
This proves our claim.
∎

Properties of positive solutions to

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{(1+f^{\prime 2})}=\left(\frac{r}{2}-\frac{\lambda-1}{r}\right)f^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}f.

Lemma 5.3
.

A positive solution

to (

) has the following properties:

(i) the function
f
does not have a local minimum in

,

(ii) if

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

III(\xi_{2})&:=\lambda\,f(\xi_{2})\lim_{r\to\xi_{2}}\big(\ln(r/\xi_{3})+\xi_{3}/r-1\big)/(\xi_{3}-r).\end{split}

Lemma 5.5
.

If

),

f(r)\leq M_{1}:=\max\left(f(\epsilon_{0}),\frac{2\pi}{r_{\lambda}-\epsilon_{0}}\right),~~r\in[\epsilon_{0},r_{\lambda}]

5.2.
Convergence to a Shrinking Doughnut

Let us denote

and

\gamma_{i}

intersects the

-axis,
with the following convention

We can extract convergent subsequences

\begin{split}\lim_{j\to\infty}a(i_{j})=a_{\infty},~~a_{\infty}\in[0,r_{\lambda}],\\
\lim_{j\to\infty}b(i_{j})=a_{\infty},~~b_{\infty}\in[r_{\lambda},\infty],\end{split}

Definition 5.9
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{i}:(a(i),b(i))\to[0,\infty)

be the function such that

\big\{(r,f_{i}(r)),r\in(a(i),b(i))\big\}\subset\gamma_{i}(\mathbb{S}^{1}).

’s do not in general satisfy (
5.2
).

, that converges in

in any compact set to a geodesic

.

Following
[
10

a_{\infty}\in(0,r_{\lambda})
and
b_{\infty}\in(r_{\lambda},\infty)
.

Now consider the compact set

5.6

with
\epsilon=a_{\infty}
and

.
Our subsequence

\gamma_{i}
converges to a geodesic
\gamma_{\infty}
in

.
Because the intersections
of the
\gamma_{i}

,

\gamma_{\infty}

.

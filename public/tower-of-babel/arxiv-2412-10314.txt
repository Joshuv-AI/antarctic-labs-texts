Quantum Indeterminacy and Polar Duality: a Probabilistic Approach
Maurice de Gosson

Abstract
We present a probabilistic argument supporting the application of polar duality, as discussed in our previous work, to express the indeterminacy principle of quantum mechanics. Our approach combines the properties of the Mahler volume of a convex body with the Donoho–Stark uncertainty principle from harmonic analysis, which characterizes the concentration of a function and its Fourier transform. The central result demonstrates that the sum of the probabilities of position concentration near a convex body and momentum concentration near its polar dual is equal to one, with an error term that diminishes rapidly as the number of degrees of freedom increases. This result motivates the interpretation of polar duality as a kind of geometric Fourier transform.



Keywords
: quantum indeterminacy; polar duality; Blaschke–Santaló
inequality; Donoho–Stark uncertainty principle; quantum blobs; symplectic group

Introduction

The term “quantum indeterminacy” refers to
a fundamental concept in quantum mechanics that highlights the intrinsic
uncertainty and unpredictably in the behavior of quantum systems. There are
several different ways to express quantum indeterminacy; the simplest (from
which actually most others are derived) is that a function and its Fourier
transform cannot simultaneously sharply located. On a more sophisticated
level, it is expressed by the Heisenberg uncertainty relations (or their
refinement, the Robertson–Schrödinger inequalities). The drawback of the
latter is that they privilege variances (and covariances) for measuring
uncertainties, which has been discussed and criticized by (Hilgevoord and
Uffink
[
18
,
19
]

is only optimal for states that are Gaussian, or close to Gaussian states. In
previous work

Proprieties of Polar Duality

Let

Notice that the larger

We briefly recall the basic properties of polar duality as exposed for

reflexive:
(X^{\hbar})^{\hbar}=X
, anti-monotone: if

, and has the covariance property under linear
transformations: if
\det A\neq 0

(AX)^{\hbar}=(A^{T})^{-1}X^{\hbar}.

(1)

Let

B_{X}^{n}(\sqrt{\hbar})^{\hbar}=B_{P}^{n}(\sqrt{\hbar}).

(2)

Let

be a real invertible and symmetric

follows from (

Ax\cdot x\leq R^{2}

\{x:Ax\cdot x\leq\hbar\}^{\hbar}=\{p:A^{-1}p\cdot p\leq\hbar\}.

(3)

The reason why we have introduced the notion of polar duality in the study of
quantum indeterminacy comes from the following observation: Assume that
X
is
an ellipsoid, the so is
X^{\hbar}
, in view of (
3

The phase space cell

Recall

while the ellipsoid

Mahler’s Volume and the Blaschke–Santaló Inequality

A remarkable property of polar duality, the Blaschke–Santaló inequality:
says that if
X
is a symmetric convex body; then the Mahler volume
v(X)
,
defined by

v(X)=(\operatorname*{Vol}X)(\operatorname*{Vol}X^{\hbar})

satisfies the inequality

v(X)\leq(\operatorname*{Vol}\nolimits_{n}(B^{n}(\sqrt{\hbar}))^{2}

(4)

that is,

v(X)=(\operatorname*{Vol}X)(\operatorname*{Vol}X^{\hbar})\leq\frac{(\pi\hbar)^{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)^{2}}

(5)

where
\operatorname*{Vol}\nolimits_{n}
is the standard Lebesgue measure on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and equality is attained if and only if
X\subset\mathbb{R}_{x}^{n}
is an ellipsoid centered at the origin. The Mahler has
conjectured that lower bound is

\upsilon(X)\geq\frac{(4\hbar)^{n}}{n!}

(6)

but this claim has so far resisted to all proof attempts The best know result
is the following, due to Kuperberg
[
20
]
, who has shown that

\upsilon(X)\geq\frac{(\pi\hbar)^{n}}{4^{n}n!}.

(7)

Summarizing, we have the bounds

\frac{(\pi\hbar)^{n}}{4^{n}n!}\leq\upsilon(X)\leq\frac{(\pi\hbar)^{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)^{2}}

(8)

(see
[
8
]
for a discussion of other partial results).

The Mahler volume has the intuitive interpretation as being a measure of
“roundness”: its largest value is taken by
balls (or ellipsoids), and its smallest value (the bound (
6
)) is
indeed attained by any
n
-parallelepiped

X=[-\sqrt{2\sigma_{x_{1}x_{1}}},\sqrt{2\sigma_{x_{1}x_{1}}}]\times\cdot\cdot\cdot\times[-\sqrt{2\sigma_{x_{n}x_{n}}},\sqrt{2\sigma_{x_{n}x_{n}}}].

(9)

This is related to the covariances of the tensor product
\psi=\phi_{1}\otimes\cdot\cdot\cdot\otimes\phi_{n}
of standard one-dimensional Gaussians

\phi_{j}(x)=(\pi\hbar)^{-1/4}e^{-x_{j}^{2}/2\hbar}
; the function
\psi
is a
minimal uncertainty quantum state in the sense that it reduces the Heisenberg
inequalities to equalities. We suggest that such quantum considerations might
lead to proof of Mahler’s conjecture.

3.1
The Donoho–Stark inequality

A remarkable result is the Donoho–Stark inequality, related to uncertainty
principles, particularly in signal processing and harmonic analysis

[
16
]
. This inequality addresses the relationship between the sparsity of
a signal and its representation in dual domains, such as time and frequency.
It can be stated as follows: let
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\widehat{\psi}(p)=\left(\tfrac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\right)^{n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ipx/\hbar}\psi(x)dx

(10)

be its
\hbar
-Fourier transform; we assume that
||\psi||_{L^{2}}=||\widehat{\psi}||_{L^{2}}=1
. Donoho and Stark proved in
[
5
]
that if

X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
P\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\ast}
are measurable
sets such that

\int_{X}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx\geq 1-\varepsilon\text{ },\text{ }\int_{P}|\widehat{\psi}(p)|^{2}dp\geq 1-\eta

(11)

where
\varepsilon
and
\eta
are non-negative numbers such that

0\leq\varepsilon+\eta<1
, then the volumes of
X
and
P
cannot be
simultaneously arbitrarily small, in fact, we must have

(\operatorname*{Vol}X)(\operatorname*{Vol}P)\geq(2\pi\hbar)^{n}(1-\varepsilon-\eta)^{2}

(12)

(see the article
[
3
]
by Boggiatto
et al
. for a detailed
discussion and improvements of the inequality (
12
).

The Main Result: Discussion and Interpretation

Suppose now we take
P=X^{\hbar}
in the Donoho-Stark inequality (

Combining the inequalities (

\displaystyle 0\leq\Delta_{\min}(n)\leq\upsilon(X)\geq\frac{(\pi\hbar)^{n}}{4^{n}n!}

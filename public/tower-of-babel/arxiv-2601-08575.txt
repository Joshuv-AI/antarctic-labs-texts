Dynamic representation of the Weyl solution for the Schrödinger operator on the semi-axis Thanks: No new data were created or analyzed during this study. Data sharing is not applicable to this article. Thanks: All authors declare that they have no conflicts of interest.
A. S. Mikhaylov, V. S. Mikhaylov

Abstract




Abstract.
We derive a representation formula for the Weyl solution
to the Schrödinger operator on the semi-axis for certain classes of potentials.
Our approach is based on relations with the initial-boundary value problem
for the wave equation with the same potential on the half-line.

1.
Introduction.

We consider the Schrödinger operator

(1.1)

H=-\partial_{x}^{2}+q(x),\quad x>0

on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with a real-valued locally integrable potential
q.

We assume that (
1.1
) is limit point case at
\infty
, that is, for each
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}
the equation

(1.2)

-u^{\prime\prime}+q\left(x\right)u=zu

has a unique solution
u_{+}
which is in
L_{2}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left|u_{+}\left(x,z\right)\right|^{2}dx<\infty,z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Such a solution
u_{+}
is called a
Weyl

solution
and its
existence is the central point of the Titchmarsh-Weyl theory.

The
Titchmarsh-Weyl
m
-function
,
m(z),
is defined for
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
as

(1.3)

m\left(z\right):=\frac{u_{+}^{\prime}\left(0,z\right)}{u_{+}\left(0,z\right)}.

The function
m(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and satisfy the Herglotz property, i.e.
m:\mathbb{C}_{+}\mapsto\mathbb{C}_{+}
.

Simon and Gesztey in
[
8
,
4
]
proposed the following representation for the Weyl function:

(1.4)

m(-k^{2})=-k-\int_{0}^{\infty}A(\alpha)e^{-2\alpha k}\,d\alpha,

where the absolute convergence of integral was proven for
q\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
and
q\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
. They called the function
A
in (
1.4
) the

A-
amplitude
.

In
[
2
]
the authors proposed the natural physical interpretation of the
A-
amplitude.
It is as follows: for the same potential
q
, the initial-value problem for the wave equation is considered:

(1.5)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}(x,t)-u_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)u(x,t)=0,\quad x>0,\ t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{t}(x,0)=0,\ u(0,t)=f(t).\end{cases}

where
f
is an arbitrary
L^{2}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)

function referred to as a
boundary control
. The solution to (
1.5
) is denoted by
u^{f}
.
The
response operator
(the dynamical Dirichlet-to-Neumann
map)
R^{T}
for the system (
1.5
) is defined in

\mathcal{F}^{T}:=L_{2}(0,T)
by

(R^{T}f)(t)=u_{x}^{f}(0,t),\ t\in(0,T),

with the domain
\{f\in C^{2}([0,T]):\;f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0\}.
The
response operator has the representation (see
[
1
,
2
]
):

(R^{T}f)(t)=-f^{\prime}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}r(s)f(t-s)\,ds,

where
r
is called the
response function
. In
[
2
]
the authors have shown that

(1.6)

\displaystyle m(-k^{2})=-k+\int_{0}^{\infty}r(\alpha)e^{-k\alpha},

(1.7)

\displaystyle A(\alpha)=-2r(2\alpha).

and the integrals in (
1.4
) and (
1.6
)
converges for wider class of potentials, namely for
q\in l^{\infty}(L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})):=\left\{q\,|\,\int_{n}^{n+1}|q(x)|\,dx\in l^{\infty}\right\}
.
Formula (
1.7
) shows that
A-
amplitude, initially introduced in some artificial way, is in fact (taking into account the sign and scaling of coordinates) a response function,
i.e., the kernel of the response operator, a classical object in the theory of dynamic inverse problems
[
3
]
.

Although the authors in
[
2
]
pointed out the connection between (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) using the
Laplace transform, they dealt directly with the integral equation for the
A-
amplitude and did not show that the solution to (
1.5
) after applying the
Laplace transform is in fact the Weyl solution. This drawback can be overcome by appealing to the finite wave propagation speed in the system (
1.5
),
as was done for the system associated with Jacobi matrices in
[
7
,
5
]
(note that, nevertheless in
[
6
]
the authors proved that the corresponding solution is from
l_{2}
),
but here we propose a different
approach and show how to obtain the Weyl solution for (
1.2
) from the solution
u^{f}
of (
1.5
). Thus, the goal of this paper is to obtain
a new representation for the Weyl solution which is interesting in itself and eliminates the drawback from
[
2
]
. Note that our results do not follow from
[
2
]
,
but rather the opposite.

The paper is organized as follows: in the second section we follow

[
1
]
: formulate ”standard” statements on the solvability of
(
1.5
) depending on the potential, show a representation
of
u^{f}
in terms of the solution of a certain Goursat problem. We
rewrite this Goursat problem as an integral equation and formulate
some statements about its solvability for a potential from

L_{1,\operatorname{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
. In the third section, we
show that for
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
potentials, a representation for
the Weyl solution follows from the ”standard” estimates from

[
1
]
. We then analyze the integral equation for the solution
of the Goursat problem in the case of
q\in l^{\infty}\left(L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right)
, which also provides a
representation for the Weyl solution in this case.

2.
Dynamical system.

In
[
1
]
the authors shown that the solution to (
1.5
) admits the following representation

(2.1)

u^{f}(x,t)=\begin{cases}f(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w(x,s)f(t-s)\,ds,\quad x\leq t,\\
0,\quad x>t.\end{cases}.

Where the kernel
w(w,t)
is the unique solution to
the Goursat problem:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}w_{tt}(x,t)-w_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t)=0,\quad 0<x<t,\\
w(0,t)=0,\ w(x,x)=-1/2\int_{0}^{x}q(s)\,ds.\end{cases}

For
u^{f}
they proved the following

Proposition 1
.

a)

If
q\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

and
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
, then the solution to (
1.5
) given by formula (
2.1
)
is a classical solution to (
1.5
).

b)

If
q\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
, then formula

(\ref{wave_eqn_sol})
represents a unique generalized solution to
the initial-boundary value problem
(\ref{wave_eqn})

u^{f}\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{T}),
where
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}_{loc}(0,\infty)

and

\mathcal{H}^{T}:=\{u\in\mathcal{H}:\,\mbox{
supp }u\subset[0,T]\,\}.

Proposition 2
.

a)

If
q\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then the generalized
solution
w(x,s)
to the Goursat problem
(\ref{gursa})
is a
continuous function and

(2.3)

\displaystyle|w(x,s)|\leqslant\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{s+x}{2}}|q(\alpha)|\,d\alpha\Bigr)\operatorname{exp}\Bigl\{\frac{s-x}{4}\int_{0}^{\frac{s+x}{2}}|q(\alpha)|\,d\alpha\Bigr\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle w_{x}(\cdot,s),w_{s}(\cdot,s),w_{x}(x,\cdot),w_{s}(x,\cdot)\in L_{1,\,loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Partial derivatives in
(\ref{gursa_deriv})
continuously in

L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
depend on parameters
x
,
s
. The
equation in
(\ref{gursa})
holds almost everywhere and the
boundary conditions are satisfied in the classical sense.

b)

If
q\in C_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then the generalized
solution
w(x,s)
to the Goursat problem
(\ref{gursa})
is

C^{1}
-smooth, equation and boundary conditions are satisfied in
the classical sense.

c)

If
q\in C^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then the solution to
the Goursat problem
(\ref{gursa})
is classical, all its
derivatives up to the second order are continuous.

We outline the scheme of the proof of the Proposition
2
, which is given in
[
1
]

By setting
\xi=s-x
,
\eta=s+x
, and

v(\xi,\eta)=w\Bigl(\frac{\eta-\xi}{2},\frac{\eta+\xi}{2}\Bigl),

equation
(\ref{gursa})
reduces to

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{\xi\eta}-\frac{1}{4}q(\frac{\eta-\xi}{2})v=0,\quad 0<\xi<\eta,\\
v(\eta,\eta)=0,\ v(0,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\eta/2}q(\alpha)\,d\alpha.\end{array}\right.

The boundary value problem
(\ref{gursa_trans})
is equivalent to the
integral equation

(2.6)

v(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\xi/2}^{\eta/2}q(\alpha)\,d\alpha-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}\,d\eta_{1}q\Bigl(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\Bigr)v(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}).

We introduce a new function

(2.7)

Q(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\xi/2}^{\eta/2}q(\alpha)\,d\alpha

and the operator
K:C(\mathbb{R}^{2})\mapsto C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by the
rule

\Bigl(Kv\Bigr)(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}\,d\eta_{1}q\Bigl(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\Bigr)v(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}).

Rewriting
(\ref{wave_gursa_new_2})
as

v=Q-Kv

and formally solving it by iterations, we get

(2.8)

v(\xi,\eta)=Q(\xi,\eta)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(K^{n}Q)(\xi,\eta).

To prove the convergence of
(\ref{gursa_sol_sum})
we need
suitable estimates for
|K^{n}Q|(\xi,\eta)
. In
[
1
]
the
authors obtain such estimates for the case of
q\in L_{1\operatorname{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
, in the third Section we
refine these estimates for
q\in l^{\infty}(L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
.

3.
Weyl solution representation.

For the special control
f(t)=\delta(t)
, we denote by
u^{\delta}(x,t)
the fundamental solution of (
1.5
). From (
2.1
)
it is clear that any solution of (
1.5
) has the form
u^{f}=u^{\delta}*f
.

Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
and

\widehat{f}(k):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,e^{ikt}\,dt\,

be its Fourier transform. Function
\widehat{f}(k)
is well
defined for
k\in\mathbb{C}
.
Going formally in (
1.5
) over to the Fourier transforms
(we note that the authors in
[
8
,
4
,
2
]
used the Laplace
transform), one has

\begin{cases}-\widehat{u^{f}_{xx}}(x,k)+q(x)\widehat{u^{f}}(x,k)=k^{2}\widehat{u^{f}}(x,k),\\
\widehat{u^{f}}(0,k)=\widehat{f}(k),\end{cases}

and for the response operator

\widehat{(Rf)}(k)=\widehat{u^{f}_{x}}(0,k)\,,

respectively.
We note that in view of (
2.1
)
\widehat{u^{f}}(x,k)=\widehat{u^{\delta}}(x,k)\widehat{f}(k)
, so we see that the
values of the function
\widehat{u}(0,k)
and its first derivative at the
origin,
\widehat{u}_{x}(0,k),
are related through the Titchmarsh-Weyl
m-function (cf. (
1.3
)):

\widehat{u^{f}}_{x}(0,k)=m(k^{2})\widehat{f}(k)\,.

Thus the formula (
1.6
) is valid provided we can justify
two things: taking the Fourier transform of (
1.5
) and
that
\widehat{u^{\delta}}(x,k)
is a Weyl solution, where

(3.1)

\widehat{u^{\delta}}(x,k)=e^{ikx}+\int_{0}^{\infty}w(x,t)e^{ikt}\,dt.

3.1.
Case of
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
potentials

We note that the first term in (
3.1
) is in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as soon as
\operatorname{Im}k>0
. Therefore, we need to estimate the second term in (
3.1
).
Using (
2.3
) we obtain:

(3.2)

|w(x,t)|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|q\|_{L_{1}}e^{\frac{\|q\|_{L_{1}}}{4}(t-x)}.

The last estimate implies that
\int_{0}^{\infty}w(x,t)e^{ikt}\,dt
converges as
\operatorname{Im}k>\frac{\|q\|_{L_{1}}}{4}
.
Then from the same estimate (
3.2
) it follows that the second term in (
3.1
) is from
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Summarizing all of the above we obtain

Proposition 3
.

If
q\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
then formula (
3.1
) yields
the Weyl solution in the domain

\operatorname{Im}k>\frac{\|q\|_{L_{1}}}{4}
.

3.2.
Case of
l_{\infty}(l_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
potentials

The following Lemma was proved in
[
2
]
:

Lemma 1
.

Let
f\left(x\right)
be a non-negative function and

||f||:=\sup_{x\geq 0}\int_{x}^{x+1}f\left(s\right)ds<\infty.

Then for any
a,b\geq 0
and natural
n

\int_{0}^{a}\left(x+b\right)^{n}f\left(x\right)dx\leq\frac{\left(a+b+1\right)^{n+1}}{n+1}||f||.

We need to show the convergence of (
2.8
), to this aim we rewrite

Q(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\xi/2}^{\eta/2}q(\alpha)\,d\alpha=\left[\alpha=\frac{\gamma}{2}\right]=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}q\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\,d\gamma.

Then we introduce the notation
\widetilde{q}(\gamma):=q\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)
and use Lemma
1
to estimate:

|Q(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}\left|q\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\right|\,d\gamma\leqslant\frac{1}{4}(\eta+1)\|\widetilde{q}\|.

The second term in (
2.8
) is estimated by:

\displaystyle|KQ(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}\,d\eta_{1}\left|q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\frac{1}{4}(\eta_{1}+1)\|\widetilde{q}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|}{4^{2}}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{\eta}\,d\eta_{1}\left|q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|(\eta_{1}+1)\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{2}}{4^{2}}\xi\frac{(\eta+2)^{2}}{2},

where we use Lemma
1
to get the last inequality. Then the nest term is estimated by

\displaystyle|K^{2}Q(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}\,d\eta_{1}\left|q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{2}}{4^{2}}\xi_{1}\frac{(\eta_{1}+2)^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{2}}{4^{3}}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{\eta}\,d\eta_{1}\left|q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\xi_{1}\frac{(\eta_{1}+2)^{2}}{2}\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{3}}{4^{3}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\frac{(\eta+3)^{3}}{3!}.

Using induction and Lemma
1
it is not hard to show that

\displaystyle|K^{n}Q(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{n+1}}{4^{n+1}}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{(\eta+n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!},

Then for the general term of (
2.8
) one can write an estimate using the inequality
(a+b)^{n}\leqslant 2^{n-1}\left(a^{n}+b^{n}\right)
:

\displaystyle|K^{n}Q(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{n+1}}{4^{n+1}}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{(\eta+n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{n+1}}{4^{n+1}}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{2^{n}\left(\eta^{n+1}+(n+1)^{n+1}\right)}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{n+1}}{2^{n+2}}\left(\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{\eta^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}+\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\right).

Using the above estimate and the Stirling inequality

n!\geqslant\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n},

and taking some
\varkappa>0
we can proceed to the estimate of (
2.8
):

\displaystyle|v(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{n+1}}{2^{n+2}}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{\eta^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|^{n+1}}{2^{n+2}}\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}\frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|\eta}{4(n+1)}\frac{\left(\frac{\xi\|\widetilde{q}\|\varkappa}{2}\right)^{n}}{n!}\frac{\left(\frac{\eta}{\varkappa}\right)^{n}}{n!}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|e}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\left(\frac{\xi e\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2}\right)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|\eta}{4}e^{\frac{\xi\|\widetilde{q}\|\varkappa}{2}}e^{\frac{\eta}{\varkappa}}+\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|e}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{\frac{\xi e\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2}}.

Rewriting the above inequality for
w(x,t)
we get that

(3.3)

|w(x,t)|\leqslant\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|(x+t)}{4}e^{\frac{(t-x)\|\widetilde{q}\|\varkappa}{2}+\frac{t+x}{\varkappa}}+\frac{\|\widetilde{q}\|e}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{\frac{(t-x)e\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2}}.

Then (
3.3
) implies that the Fourier transform
(
3.1
) of
u^{\delta}
exists as soon as

\operatorname{Im}k>\operatorname{max}\left\{\frac{e\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2},\,\frac{\varkappa\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2}\right\}
, and that
(
3.1
) is from
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
if
\frac{\varkappa\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2}>\frac{1}{\varkappa}
. Finally we arrive at
the following

Proposition 4
.

If
q\in l_{\infty}(l_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
then formula
(
3.1
) gives the representation for the Weyl solution
of the Schrödinger operator
H
in the region

\operatorname{Im}k>\operatorname{max}\left\{\frac{e\|\widetilde{q}\|}{2},\,\sqrt{\frac{|\widetilde{q}\|}{2}}\right\}
.

The Bounded Diameter Conjecture and Sharp Geometric Estimates for Mean Curvature Flow
Yiqi Huang and Wenshuai Jiang

Abstract
. We show that the intrinsic diameter of mean curvature flow in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} is uniformly bounded as one approaches the first singular time T T . This confirms the bounded diameter conjecture of Haslhofer [ Has21 , Has25 ] . In addition, we establish several sharp quantitative estimates: the second fundamental form A A has uniformly bounded L 1 L^{1} -norm on each time slice, A A belongs to the weak L 3 L^{3} space on the space-time region, and the singular set 𝒮 \mathcal{S} has finite ℋ 1 \mathcal{H}^{1} -Hausdorff measure. All of the results are optimal due to the marriage ring example and our results do not require any convexity assumptions on the surfaces. Furthermore, our arguments extend naturally to flows through singularities, yielding the same sharp estimates.



1.
Introduction

A smooth family of closed embedded surfaces
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times[0,T)
evolves by mean curvature flow (
[
Bra78
,
Hu84
]
) if the normal velocity at each point is given by the mean curvature vector. Finite-time singularities are inevitable, and a central problem in the study of mean curvature flow is to understand the structure of the singular set and the geometry of the flow near singularities.

In this paper, our first main result establishes a uniform bound for the intrinsic diameter of the flow, thereby confirming a conjecture of Haslhofer:

Conjecture 1.1

(Haslhofer
[
Has21
,
Has25
]
)
.

The intrinsic diameter of the mean curvature flow
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathds{R}^{3}
remains uniformly bounded as
t\nearrow T
, the first singular time.

This conjecture was inspired by an analogous conjecture of Perelman in three-dimensional Ricci flow
[
Pe02
]
. For mean curvature flow, the extrinsic diameter is trivially bounded by the avoidance principle, but the intrinsic diameter is far subtler: near singularities, the evolving surface may develop long, spiraling, or fractal-like regions whose intrinsic geometry could, a priori, degenerate.

By Huisken’s monotonicity formula
[
Hu90
]
, singularities of mean curvature flow are modeled on self-shrinkers. Previous attempts to address Conjecture
1.1
have relied on precise classifications of possible singularity models. The first such result was obtained by Gianniotis and Haslhofer
[
GH20
]
, who proved the conjecture in the mean convex case. Thanks to the deep work of White
[
Wh00
,
Wh03
]
, all tangent flows in this setting are multiplicity-one round cylinders, see
[
Hu93
,
HS99a
,
HS99b
,
SW09
,
An12
,
CM12
,
HK17a
]
. Combining this classification with the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality for cylindrical shrinkers established in Colding and Minicozzi’s seminal work
[
CM15
]
(also
[
CIM15
]
) and the canonical neighborhood theorem from Haslhofer-Kleiner
[
HK17a
,
HK17b
]
, they ruled out the formation of “fractal tubes” near cylindrical singularities and obtained a uniform diameter bound.

For general flows without mean convexity, however, the singularities are considerably more intricate. By the classification theorems of Wang
[
Wa16b
]
and Bamler–Kleiner
[
BK23
]
, every non-compact shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has either conical or cylindrical ends. Du
[
Du21
]
recently verified Conjecture
1.1
under the additional assumption that the flow only encounters multiplicity-one neck singularity or singularity with conical end, using the mean convex neighborhood theorem by Choi-Haslhofer-Hershkovits
[
CHH22
]
and the uniqueness of tangent flows at conical singularities proved by Chodosh and Schulze
[
CS21
]
.

A recent breakthrough of Bamler and Kleiner
[
BK23
]
resolved the higher-multiplicity issue in mean curvature flow in
\mathds{R}^{3}
. Nevertheless, the structure of singularities with cylindrical ends remains largely mysterious. This connects to a long-standing question posed by Ilmanen
[
Il95
]
: Is the round cylinder the only complete embedded shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a cylindrical end? Wang
[
Wa16a
]
proves the rigidity under the assumption of the convergence of infinite order on the cylindrical end, and constructed non-trivial incomplete examples with such ends. Apart from this, however, very little is known. As a consequence, previous approaches relying on establishing the canonical neighborhood theorems cannot address the general setting.

In this paper, we confirm the bounded diameter conjecture
1.1
in full generality. In fact, our main theorem provides much stronger information: we establish optimal curvature estimates and quantitative regularity of the singular set.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{\mathcal{M}_{t}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be a smooth closed embedded mean curvature flow, then there exists some constant
C=C(\mathcal{M}_{0})<\infty
such that for all
0\leq t<T

(1)

The intrinsic diameter of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is uniformly bounded:

D_{int}(\mathcal{M}_{t})<C;

(2)

The
L^{1}
-norm of the second fundamental form
A
is uniformly bounded:

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}|A|<C;

(3)

The second fundamental form
A
has bounded weak
L^{3}
-norm:

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\{X\in\mathcal{M}:|A|(X)\geq s^{-1}\}\big)\leq Cs^{3};

(4)

The singular set
\mathcal{S}_{T}
of the final time slice
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is contained in a countable union of embedded 1-dimensional Lipschitz manifolds together with countably many points. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{T}
has finite 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure:

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{T})\leq C.

Remark 1.3
.

The estimates in Theorem
1.2
are sharp. For example, the marriage ring solution, which shrinks to a round circle at time
T
, realizes equality in the curvature and singular set bounds.

Remark 1.4
.

Our argument in fact yields the stronger Minkowski estimate:
Area(B_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{T}))\leq Cr
, which refines the Hausdorff estimate above. Moreover, our argument provides an integral estimate for regularity scale, which is much stronger than the curvature estimate.

The uniform
L^{1}
-bound on the second fundamental form
A
is in fact stronger than the intrinsic diameter bound. Indeed, Topping
[
To08
]
established the inequality
D_{int}(\mathcal{M}_{t})\leq C\int_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}|H|
, which shows that controlling the mean curvature in
L^{1}
already implies a diameter bound. However, we do not need to use Topping’s result in our proof. In fact, we prove the diameter bound and the
L^{1}
bound of
A
simultaneously. Under the mean convexity assumption, Gianniotis and Haslhofer
[
GH20
]
proved an
L^{1}
-bound on
A
; see also related work of Head
[
He13
]
, Cheeger–Haslhofer–Naber
[
CHN13
]
, and Du
[
Du21
]
. Our result removes all convexity assumptions and establishes the same conclusion in complete generality.

For the space–time estimates, Cheeger–Haslhofer–Naber
[
CHN13
]
previously obtained the
L^{p}
bound for
A
with any
p<3
in the mean convex setting. Without assuming convexity, we improve this to an
optimal weak

L^{3}
bound, which is sharp in view of the cylindrical self-shrinkers. It is instructive to compare this with the corresponding elliptic estimates. In
[
NV20

, Naber-Valtorta obtained the optimal weak

bound with

by Cheeger-Naber

]

]
and related work for Yang-Mills and Einstein manifolds
[
NV19

JN21
]
.

Our results further extend beyond the first singular time, to weak formulations of mean curvature flow. There are several notions of weak mean curvature flow in the literature, including the Brakke flow and level-set formulations. Very recently, Bamler and Kleiner
[
BK23
]
introduced the notion of
almost regular flows
: these are well-behaved Brakke flows whose local scale functions satisfy quantitative integrability conditions. They proved that this class of flows includes most of the commonly used types of mean curvature flows “through singularities” and thus resolved Ilmanen’s multiplicity-one conjecture
[
Il95
]
, extending multiplicity-one property to almost regular flows.

As our second main result, we show that both the intrinsic diameter bound and the curvature estimates established above extend verbatim to almost regular flows starting from smooth closed embedded initial data. If the surface is disconnected, we define the intrinsic diameter to be the sum of that at each connected component. Moreover, we prove that the space–time singular set
\mathcal{S}
of such flows has
finite 1-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure
, a stronger statement than the slice estimate in Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times[0,T]
be a bounded almost regular mean curvature flow in
\mathds{R}^{3}
starting from a smooth embedded closed surface
M_{0}
. Then there exists some constant
C=C(\mathcal{M})<\infty
such that the following estimates hold for all
t\in[0,T]

(1)

The intrinsic diameter of
\mathcal{M}_{t}\setminus\mathcal{S}
is uniformly bounded:

D_{int}(\mathcal{M}_{t}\setminus\mathcal{S})<C;

(2)

The
L^{1}
-norm of the second fundamental form
A
is uniformly bounded:

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{t}\setminus\mathcal{S}}|A|<C;

(3)

The second fundamental form
A
has bounded weak
L^{3}
-norm:

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\{X\in\mathcal{M}:|A|(X)\geq s^{-1}\}\big)\leq Cs^{3};

(4)

The singular set
\mathcal{S}
is contained in a countable union of embedded 1-dimensional Lipschitz manifold together with countably many points. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}
has finite 1-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure

\mathcal{H}_{P}^{1}(\mathcal{S})<C.

Previously, combining the results of
[
CHN13
,
CM16
,
BK23
]
implies that the space-time singular set
\mathcal{S}
is 1-rectifiable and satisfies the dimensional bound
\dim_{P}(\mathcal{S})\leq 1
. Under the mean convex setting, Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM16
]
proved the measure finiteness around the cylindrical singularity by observing that the set of cylindrical singularity is compact. However, the compactness fails in the general case, as the cylindrical singularities may converge to non-generic singularities. Our contribution strengthens these results by showing that the
1
-dimensional measure of
\mathcal{S}
is
finite
without the any assumption on the singularity type. Moreover, our arguments yield a quantitative Minkowski estimate, which in particular implies the Hausdorff bound and provides a sharper geometric control of the singular set.

Our method is different from the previous approaches to the conjecture
1.1
, which reply on the classification of singularity models and establishing the canonical neighborhood theorems. Instead, we prove some covering theorems using regular balls to cover the slice, inside which the flow is almost flat and thus has bounded intrinsic diameter, and further prove it satisfies a uniform 1-content estimate. Combining these local controls inside the regular balls with the global covering theorem yields a uniform diameter bound for each time slice. Geometrically, the 1-content estimate asserts that, near any type of singularity, regions of high curvature occur in a controlled non-spiral and non-fractal fashion.

The covering argument in this direction originates from the highly influential work of Cheeger and Naber
[
CN13a
]
, who established a quantitative stratification theorem and obtained the quantitative estimates for manifolds with lower Ricci curvature bounds. To obtain the optimal estimates, the second named author and Naber
[
JN21
]
introduced the notion of neck regions, together with the techniques of neck analysis and neck decomposition. This framework has since proven remarkably powerful, leading to some major advances in problems involving manifolds with Ricci curvature bounds, Yang–Mills, harmonic maps
[
NV19
,
CJN21
,
NV24
]
and many other related works
[
Na18
,
LN20
,
Wa20
,
BNS22
,
FNS23
,
HJ23
]
.

The first application of the neck decomposition theory to the parabolic setting was established in our previous work on linear parabolic partial differential equations
[
HJ24
]
, where we obtained the optimal estimate for the measure of nodal set at each time-slice. More recently, Fang and Li
[
FL25
]
extended the neck decomposition approach to mean curvature flow under the mean-convexity condition, where all singularities are of cylindrical type.

In this paper, we prove that, in the flow setting, the covering Theorem
5.1
can be refined purely in terms of
regular balls
, without the need for neck regions in the final decomposition. Moreover, in dimension two, the argument can be further simplified by exploiting the entropy gap based on
[
BL17
]
(also
[
CIMW13
,
Bre16
]
), which allows us to avoid delicate iterative covering process. To prove the covering theorem, we establish a strengthened quantitative splitting result in the spirit of
[
Wh97
,
CHN13
]
, based on the uniqueness of cylindrical singularity
[
CM15
,
CM25
]
and the resolution of the multiplicity-one conjecture by Bamler-Kleiner
[
BK23
]
. While previous applications of such coverings focused on obtaining measure estimates and proving rectifiability of the singular set, we observe that this framework can also be applied to address distance control problems, leading to the uniform intrinsic diameter bound established in this work. In addition, our approach extends naturally to other geometric flows, including the Ricci flow, where we obtain analogous results building upon Bamler’s seminal work on the compactness theory of Ricci flows
[
Ba20a
,
Ba20b
,
Ba20c
]

Preliminary

2.1.
Notations

For any space–time point
X\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, we write
X=(x,t_{X})
, where
x
denotes the spatial component and
t_{X}
the time.

Let
B_{r}(x)

B_{r}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:||x-y||<r\}.

We define the parabolic ball

Q_{r}(X)=\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}:\|y-x\|\leq r,\,|s-t_{X}|\leq r^{2}\}.

Throughout the paper,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The standard parabolic metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
is defined by

d\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)=\max\{\|x-y\|,\,|t-s|^{1/2}\}.

With respect to this metric, the parabolic ball
Q_{r}(x,t)
is precisely the metric ball of radius
r
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{k}
the
k

\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and by
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{P}
the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure associated to the parabolic metric in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
.

For
\lambda>0
, let

D_{\lambda}(x,t)=(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)

denote the parabolic scaling.
Given
X\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
, we define the
rescaled flow
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{X,r}=D_{r^{-1}}(\mathcal{M}-X),

(2.1)

that is, we translate the point
X
to the origin and rescale parabolically by the factor
r^{-1}
.

2.2.
Mean curvature flow and almost regular flow

A one-parameter family of surfaces
\{\mathcal{M}_{t}\}_{t\in I}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
evolves by
mean curvature flow
(see
[
Hu84
]
) if

(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}=\vec{H},

where
\vec{H}
denotes the mean curvature vector of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
.

Given a smooth, closed, embedded surface
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there exists a unique smooth solution
\mathcal{M}_{t}
to mean curvature flow on a maximal time interval
[0,T)
satisfying

\lim_{t\to T}\max_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}|A|=\infty.

Beyond the first singular time, the flow can be continued in various weak senses. One natural extension is the notion of a
Brakke flow
, introduced by Brakke
[
Bra78
]
. It provides a measure-theoretic formulation in terms of varifolds satisfying a certain inequality. We recall the standard definition (see Ilmanen
[
Il94
]
; Bamler–Kleiner
[
BK23
]
):

Definition 2.2

(Brakke flow)
.

A two-dimensional Brakke flow on a time interval
I
is a family of Radon measures
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in I}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying:

(1)

For almost every
t\in I
, the measure
\mu_{t}
is integer
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-rectifiable, and the associated varifold has locally bounded first variation with generalized mean curvature vector
\vec{H}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu_{t})
.

(2)

For every compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and every
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset I
,

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{K}|\vec{H}|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}\,dt<\infty.

(3)

For all compactly supported, nonnegative
u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[t_{1},t_{2}])
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u(\cdot,t)\,d\mu_{t}\Big|_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\partial_{t}u+\nabla u\cdot\vec{H}-u|\vec{H}|^{2})\,d\mu_{t}\,dt.

A Brakke flow
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in I}
is said to be
regular
on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\times I
if there exists a smooth, properly embedded mean curvature flow
\mathcal{M}\subset U
such that
\mu_{t}|_{U_{t}}=\mathcal{H}^{2}|_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}
for all
t
.
If
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is a smooth mean curvature flow, then
\mu_{t}=\mathcal{H}^{2}|_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}
defines a Brakke flow that is regular everywhere.

We write

\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{t\in I}\overline{(\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{t})\times\{t\}}.

A point
X\in\mathcal{M}
is called
regular
if the flow is regular in some parabolic neighborhood
Q_{r}(X)
, and the
singular set

\mathcal{S}
is defined as the complement of the regular points.

In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, Bamler and Kleiner introduced the notion of an
almost regular flow
—a class of well-behaved Brakke flows that are regular at almost every time and whose support is unit-regular (see definition in the next subsection) and multiplicity one.
We do not reproduce the full definition here and instead record the main properties that we will use; see
[
BK23
]
for a complete exposition.

Theorem 2.3

(Bamler–Kleiner
[
BK23
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow, and let

\mathcal{M}^{i}=D_{r_{i}^{-1}}(\mathcal{M}-X_{i})

be a blow-up sequence with
r_{i}\to 0
and uniformly bounded
X_{i}
. Then:

(1)

After passing to a subsequence,
\mathcal{M}^{i}\to\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
as Brakke flows, where
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
is itself an almost regular flow. Moreover, the convergence is locally smooth at every regular time.

(2)

If
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
is stationary (i.e., a minimal surface), then
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}
is a static plane.

The class of almost regular flows includes most standard weak flows “through singularities”, such as the outer and inner mean curvature flows starting from any smooth, closed, embedded surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In particular, every smooth mean curvature flow and every non-fattening level set flow with compact time-slices is almost regular
[
BK23
]
.

2.3.
Monotonicity and entropy

Let
X_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times I
.
The backward heat kernel centered at
X_{0}
is defined by

\phi_{X_{0}}(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi(t_{0}-t)}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4(t_{0}-t)}\right),\qquad t<t_{0}.

The following fundamental monotonicity result is due to Huisken
[
Hu90
]
: If
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is a Brakke flow, then for all
X_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
we have

\overline{D}_{t}\int\phi_{X_{0}}\,d\mathcal{M}_{t}\leq-\int\Big|\vec{H}+\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\perp}}{2(t_{0}-t)}\Big|^{2}\phi_{X_{0}}\,d\mathcal{M}_{t},

(2.4)

where
\overline{D}_{t}
denotes the upper Dini derivative.

For
r>0
, we define the
Gaussian density
of the flow at
X_{0}
and scale
r
by

\Theta_{X_{0}}(r)=\int\phi_{X_{0}}(x,t_{0}-r^{2})\,d\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}-r^{2}}(x).

By (
2.4
),
\Theta_{X_{0}}(r)
is monotone non-decreasing as
r\downarrow 0
.
The
Gaussian density at
X_{0}
is defined by

\Theta_{X_{0}}=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\Theta_{X_{0}}(r).

A Brakke flow is said to be
unit-regular
if it is regular at every point
X_{0}
with
\Theta_{X_{0}}=1
.

When
\Theta_{X_{0}}(r)
is constant in
r
, equality in (
2.4
) holds, and the flow is self-similar with respect to
X_{0}
.
In this case, the time-slices satisfy the
shrinker equation

\vec{H}=\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\perp}}{2(t-t_{0})}.

Following Colding–Minicozzi
[
CM12
]
, we define the
entropy
of an integral
\mathcal{H}^{2}
-rectifiable Radon measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

\lambda(\mu)=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,\tau>0}\int\frac{1}{4\pi\tau}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)d\mu(x).

For a Brakke flow
\mathcal{M}_{t\in I}
, Huisken’s monotonicity implies that

t\mapsto\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{t})
is non-increasing.
We define the
entropy of the flow
by

\lambda(\mathcal{M})=\sup_{t\in I}\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{t}).

If the flow is generated by a self-shrinker, the entropy is constant and equals the entropy of that shrinker.

For shrinkers in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there is a definite entropy gap between self-shrinking cylinders and all other shrinkers in
\mathds{R}^{3}
, due to Bernstein-Wang
[
BL17
]
(also
[
CIMW13
,
Bre16
]
: there exists a constant
\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}>0
such that for any smooth, embedded self-shrinker

\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that is not one of the standard models
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})
, or
\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{R}
, we have

1=\lambda(\mathbb{R}^{2})<\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2}))<\lambda_{1}\equiv\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{1}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{R})\leq\lambda(\Sigma)-\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}.

(2.5)

In this paper, we take
\lambda_{gap}<<\lambda_{1}-\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2}))
. We will make use of this entropy gap in the proof of our covering theorems.

2.4.
Almost regular and cylindrical

We record quantitative regularity and cylindricity notions and their consequences.

Definition 2.6
.

We say
X
is
(\delta,r)
-regular if
\sup_{Q_{\delta^{-1}r}(X)\cap\mathcal{M}}r|A|\leq\delta^{2}
.

Definition 2.7

(Almost Cylindrical)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times[0,T]
be a bounded almost regular flow. We say that
X=(x,t_{X})
is
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical
with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X}=(x+L_{X},t_{X})
if the following holds:

(1)

for any
s\in[\delta r,\delta^{-1}r]
, the slice
\mathcal{M}_{X,s}\cap(B_{\delta^{-2}}(0)\times\{t=-1\})
can be written as a
C^{2,\alpha}
graph of a function over a fixed round cylinder
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{S}^{1}(\sqrt{2})
with
C^{2,\alpha}
-norm less than
\delta^{2}
. Recall that we have used the rescaling notation
\mathcal{M}_{X,s}
in (
2.1
);

(2)

the axis of the round cylinder is a 1-dimensional linear subspace
L_{X}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}
.

First we prove that if
X
is almost cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X}
, then all the points near
\mathcal{L}_{X}
are almost cylindrical at the same scale, with respect to the same direction.

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
\delta>0
,
\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\delta)
, we have the following. Suppose
X
is
(\eta,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X}=(x+L_{X},t_{X})
. Then any
Y\in Q_{\eta r}(\mathcal{L}_{X})\cap Q_{2r}(X)
is
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
(y+L_{X},t_{Y})
.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the Definition
2.7
. Let
\delta>0
be given. If
\eta
is chosen small, then for any
s\in[\delta r,\delta^{-1}r]
and any
Y\in Q_{\eta r}(\mathcal{L}_{X})\cap Q_{2r}(X)
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{Y,s}\cap(B_{\delta^{-2}}\times\{t=-1\})\subset\mathcal{M}_{X,s^{\prime}}\cap(B_{\eta^{-2}}\times\{t=-1\})
for some
s^{\prime}\in[\eta r,\eta^{-1}r]
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{Y,s}\cap(B_{\delta^{-2}}\times\{t=-1\})
can be written as a
C^{2,\alpha}
graph of a function over the cylinder with the axis
L_{X}
, with
C^{2,\alpha}
-norm smaller than
2\eta
. This completes the proof.
∎

As a corollary, near
\mathcal{L}_{X}
, the Gaussian density is close to
\lambda_{1}
, the entropy of the round cylinder.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Brakke flow in
\mathds{R}^{3}
with entropy bounded by
\Lambda_{0}
. For any
\delta>0
, suppose
X
is
(\eta,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X}
for some
\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\Lambda_{0},\delta)
. Then, for every

Y\in Q_{\eta r}(\mathcal{L}_{X})\cap Q_{2r}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad s\in[\delta r,\delta^{-1}r],

we have

\big|\Theta_{Y}(s)-\lambda_{1}\big|\leq\delta.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
, it suffices to prove the estimate at
X
; the same argument then works for all such
Y\in Q_{\eta r}(\mathcal{L}_{X})\cap Q_{2r}(X)
.

For any
s\in[\delta r,\delta^{-1}r]
, by the scaling property of
\Theta
we have

\Theta_{X}(s)=(4\pi)^{-1}(\int e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}d\mu),

where
d\mu
is the associated measure of the rescaled flow
\mathcal{M}_{X,s}
at time
t=-1
.

Since
(\mathcal{M}_{X,s}\cap\{t=-1\})\cap B_{\eta^{-2}}(0)
is a
C^{2,\alpha}
graph over
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{S}^{1}(\sqrt{2})
with graph norm smaller than
\eta^{2}
, if
\eta\leq\eta(\Lambda_{0},\delta)
we have

\Big|\int_{B_{\eta^{-2}}(0)}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}d\mu-\int_{B_{\eta^{-2}(0)}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}d\mu_{0}\Big|\leq\delta^{2},

where
d\mu_{0}
is the standard measure for round cylinder. Next we estimate the part outside
B_{\eta^{-2}}
. First note that since the entropy of the flow is bounded by
\Lambda
, then by
[
CM12
]
each
\mathcal{M}_{X,s}
has polynomial volume growth, i.e.
\mu(B_{R}(0))\leq C(\Lambda_{0})R^{2}
for any
R>1
. Then we use the exponential decay of the Gaussian to obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}\setminus B_{\eta^{-2}}(0)}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}d\mu&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{(k+1)\eta^{-2}}(0)\setminus B_{k\eta^{-2}}(0)}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}d\mu\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(B_{(k+1)\eta^{-2}}(0))\cdot e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4\eta^{4}}}\\
&\leq C(\Lambda_{0})\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{\eta^{4}}\cdot e^{-\frac{k^{2}}{4\eta^{4}}}\leq\delta^{2},\end{split}

provided
\eta\leq\eta(\Lambda_{0},\delta)
small enough. Combining these two implies the density estimate.
∎

3.
Entropy and Splitting

In this section, we will prove the quantitative splitting theorem in mean curvature flow. The argument usually proceeds by contradiction, relying on suitable compactness theorem. For mean curvature flows in
\mathds{R}^{3}
, we observe that the Gaussian density determines the type of splitting due to the entropy gap (
2.5
). In particular, when the density is close to either
1
or
\lambda_{1}
, the flow must be quantitatively close to a plane or a round cylinder, respectively. This dichotomy implies that the geometry and spatial distribution of density–pinched points are effectively governed by the value of the density itself. This observation simplifies the proof of the covering theorem in the next section.

If a Brakke flow is self-similar with respect to more than one point, strong rigidity follows.
We recall the standard cone-splitting principle (see
[
Wh97
,
CHN13
]
).

Lemma 3.1

(Cone-splitting for Brakke flows)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Brakke flow that is self-similar with respect to two distinct points

X=(x,t_{X})
and
Y=(y,t_{Y})
. Then:

(1)

If
x\neq y
, then
\mathcal{M}
is translation invariant along the direction
x-y
.

(2)

If
t_{X}\neq t_{Y}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is static for all
t\leq\max\{t_{X},t_{Y}\}
.

In this section, we derive a quantitative cone-splitting theorem, by studying the
\lambda
-pinched set
\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X;\lambda)\subset Q_{2r}(X)
, at which the Gaussian density is close to
\lambda
near the scale
r
at
X
.

Definition 3.2
.

Consider
Q_{2r}(X)
with
t_{X}\geq 4\eta^{-2}r^{2}
. We define the
\lambda
-pinched subset in
Q_{2r}(X)
to be

\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X;\lambda)\equiv\{Y\in Q_{2r}(X):\Theta_{Y}(\eta r)\geq\lambda-\eta^{2}\geq\Theta_{Y}(\eta^{-1}r)-\eta^{2}\}.

(3.3)

First we see that if the
\lambda
-pinched points spread out enough, then
\lambda
must be either close to
1
or
\lambda_{1}
, and that
X
is almost regular or almost cylindrical. Similar to standard cone-splitting lemma
3.1
, we prove a quantitative cone-splitting theorem here.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow. For any
\delta>0
,
\tau>0
,
r\leq r_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta,\tau)
and
\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta,\tau)
, the following holds: if for some
\lambda\geq 1

(1)

\Theta_{X}(\eta r)\geq\lambda-\eta^{2}\geq\Theta_{X}(\eta^{-1}r)-\eta^{2}\text{ for some }X=(x,t_{X})\text{ with }t_{X}\geq 4\eta^{-2}r^{2}

(2)

\Theta_{Y}(\eta r)\geq\lambda-\eta^{2}\geq\Theta_{Y}(\eta^{-1}r)-\eta^{2}\text{ for some }Y=(y,t_{Y})\in Q_{r}(X)\setminus Q_{\tau r}(X)
,

then exactly one of the following holds:

(1)

\lambda\in[1,1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{gap}]
and
X
is
(\delta,r)
-regular.

(2)

\lambda\in[\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{gap},\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{gap}]
and
X
is
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
(x+L_{y-x},t_{X})
, where
L_{y-x}
is the one-dimensional linear subspace spanned by
y-x
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that the statement fails. Then there exist parameter
\tau_{0}>0
, and sequences

r_{i}\to 0,\quad\eta_{i}\to 0,\quad\lambda_{i}\geq 1,\quad X_{i}=(x_{i},t_{i}),\quad Y_{i}=(y_{i},s_{i})\in Q_{r_{i}}(X_{i})\setminus Q_{\tau_{0}r_{i}}(X_{i})

such that

(1)

\Theta_{X_{i}}(\eta_{i}r_{i})\geq\lambda_{i}-\eta_{i}^{2}\geq\Theta_{X_{i}}(\eta_{i}^{-1}r_{i})-\eta_{i}^{2}
;

(2)

\Theta_{Y_{i}}(\eta_{i}r_{i})\geq\lambda_{i}-\eta_{i}^{2}\geq\Theta_{Y_{i}}(\eta_{i}^{-1}r_{i})-\eta_{i}^{2}
;

and

\lambda_{i}\notin[1,1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}]\cup[\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}},\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}].

By Huisken’s monotonicity formula, each
\lambda_{i}
is bounded above by
\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{0})
. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\lambda_{i}\to\lambda_{\infty}\notin[1,1+\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}})\cup(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}})
.

Consider the rescaled flows
\mathcal{M}_{i}\equiv r_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{M}-X_{i})
. By Brakke’s compactness (see
[
Bra78
,
Il94
,
BK23
]
), after passing to a subsequence we have

\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{M}_{\infty}

as Brakke flows, with
X_{i}\to\mathbf{0}
and
Y_{i}\to Y\in Q_{1}(\mathbf{0})\setminus Q_{\tau_{0}}(\mathbf{0})
. Moreover,

\Theta_{\mathbf{0}}(s)=\lambda_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta_{Y}(s)=\lambda_{\infty}\quad\text{for all }s>0.

By Theorem
2.3
,
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is the associated flow of an almost regular flow. In particular, it is regular at almost every time, and
\mathcal{M}_{i}
converges smoothly to
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
at those times. Since
\Theta_{\mathbf{0}}(s)\equiv\lambda_{\infty}
,
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is self-similar with respect to
\mathbf{0}
. Combining these implies that
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is regular for any
t<0
and is generated by a smooth shrinker in
\mathds{R}^{3}
with entropy
\lambda_{\infty}
.

The same reasoning applied at
Y
gives that
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is also self-similar with respect to
Y=(y,t_{Y})
.

Case 1:
|t_{Y}|\geq\tau_{0}^{2}
.

By the cone-splitting Lemma
3.1
,
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
must be static for all
t\in(-\infty,T]
, where
T\geq\max\{0,t_{Y}\}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}\cap\{t\}
is a minimal cone for all
t\leq T
. By theorem
2.3
, the only possible such limit is the multiplicity-one plane. Combining with unit-regularity, this implies that
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is a static multiplicity-one plane, hence
\lambda_{\infty}=1
, contradicting our assumption on
\lambda_{\infty}
.

Case 2:
|t_{Y}|<\tau_{0}^{2}
.

Then necessarily
|y|\geq\tau_{0}
. By the cone-splitting Lemma
3.1
,
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is invariant under translation in the direction
y
. Thus

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}=\mathds{R}\times N,

where
N
is a smooth curve shortening flow generated by a one-dimensional embedded shrinker for
t<0
. By the classification of one-dimensional smooth embedded shrinkers
[
AL86
]
,
N
is either a shrinking round circle or a straight line. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is either a shrinking round cylinder or a static plane, so
\lambda_{\infty}\in\{1,\lambda_{1}\}
, again a contradiction.

This contradiction proves that
\lambda
must lie in
[1,1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}]\cup[\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}},\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}]
.

To establish the second part, suppose by contradiction that
1\leq\lambda_{i}\leq 1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}
but each
X_{i}
fails to be
(\delta_{0},r_{i})
-regular. Then the same compactness argument yields
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
with
\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=1
, so
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is the static plane. Smooth convergence then implies
X_{i}
is
(\delta_{0},r_{i})
-regular for
i
large, a contradiction.

Similarly, if
|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}
while
X_{i}
is not
(\delta_{0},r_{i})
-cylindrical, the limit satisfies
\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=\lambda_{1}
, hence
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is the shrinking round cylinder. Smooth convergence again implies that
X_{i}
must be
(\delta_{0},r_{i})
-cylindrical for large
i
, yielding a contradiction. The theorem follows.
∎

As a direct corollary of Theorem
3.4
, we conclude that the set of
\lambda
-pinched points with
\lambda
far away from
1
and
\lambda_{1}
is contained in some small ball.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow. For any
\tau>0
,
r\leq r_{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, the following holds for any
X_{0}
with
t_{X_{0}}\geq 4\eta^{-2}r^{2}
. Suppose
X\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)
for some
\lambda\notin[1,1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}})\cup(\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}},\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}})
, then

\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)\subset Q_{\tau r}(X)\,.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
Y\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)\setminus Q_{\tau r}(X)
. Then Theorem
3.4
implies that
\lambda\in[1,1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}]\cup[\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}},\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}]
, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence no such
Y
exists. This completes the proof.
∎

Next we study the case where
\lambda
is close to
\lambda_{1}
. If we only assume that the density is pinched at
\lambda
at only one point
X
, but the density
\lambda
is close to
\lambda_{1}
, then we can still conclude that
X
is almost cylindrical. The proof uses the same contradiction argument.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow. For any
\delta>0
,
r\leq r_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta)
and
\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta)
, the following holds for any
X_{0}
with
t_{X_{0}}\geq 4\eta^{-2}r^{2}
. Suppose
X=(x,t_{X})\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in[\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{gap},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{gap}]
. Then we have the following

(1)

X
is
(\delta,s)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X}=(x+L_{X},t_{X})
for any
s\in[\delta^{2}r,r]
. Equivalently, in the normalized flow
\mathcal{M}_{X,s^{\prime}}
for any
s^{\prime}\in[\delta^{3}r,\delta^{-1}r]
, the time-slice
\{t=-1\}
inside the ball
B_{\delta^{-2}}(0)
is a
C^{2,\alpha}
-graph with norm
\leq\delta^{2}
over a round cylinder with axis
L_{X}
.

(2)

The
\lambda
-pinched set
\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)
lies near the axis
\mathcal{L}_{X}
:

\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)\subset Q_{\delta r}(\mathcal{L}_{X})\cap Q_{2r}(X_{0})\,.

(3)

For any
Y\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X;\lambda)
that is
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{Y}=(y+L_{Y},t_{Y})
, we have

d_{H}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\cap Q_{2r}(X_{0}),\mathcal{L}_{Y}\cap Q_{2r}(X_{0}))\leq\delta^{2}r~~\text{ and }~~d_{H}(L_{X}\cap B_{1}(0),L_{Y}\cap B_{1}(0))\leq\delta^{2}.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction, similar to Theorem
3.4
. Suppose there exist sequences
r_{i}\to 0
,
\eta_{i}\to 0
,
X_{0;i}
with
t_{X_{0;i}}\geq 4\eta^{-2}_{i}r_{i}^{2}
,
\lambda_{i}\in[\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{gap},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{gap}]
and
X_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta_{i},r_{i}}(X_{0;i};\lambda_{i})
while the conclude does not hold for some
\delta_{0}>0
. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume
\lambda_{i}\to\lambda_{\infty}\in[\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{gap},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{gap}]
.

Consider the blow-ups
\mathcal{M}_{i}:=r_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{M}-X_{i})
. By Theorem
2.3
, after passing to a subsequence
\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
as Brakke flows, with smooth local convergence at almost every time. Huisken’s monotonicity and the pinching imply that the Gaussian density at the origin of
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is constant at all scales and equals
\lambda_{\infty}
:
\Theta_{\mathbf{0}}(s)\equiv\lambda_{\infty}
for all
s>0
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is a self-similar shrinking flow generated by a smooth shrinker
\Sigma_{\infty}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}
with entropy
\lambda(\Sigma_{\infty})=\lambda_{\infty}
. By the gap estimate (
2.5
),
\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is generated by the round cylinder with axis
L_{\infty}
. Again, the smooth convergence implies that
X_{i}
is
(\delta_{0},s)
-cylindrical for any
s\in[\delta_{0}^{2}r_{i},r_{i}]
for all large
i
. Contradiction arises. This proves (1).

For (2), suppose there exists a sequence of point
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta_{i},r_{i}}(X_{0,i};\lambda_{i})
but
Y_{i}\notin Q_{\delta_{0}r_{i}}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{i}})\cap Q_{2r_{i}}(X_{0;i})
. By passing to a subsequence we assume
Y_{i}\to Y
and thus
\Theta_{Y}(s)\equiv\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{1}
. Hence
Y\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
which contradicts that
d(Y_{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{i}})>\delta_{0}r_{i}
and
\mathcal{L}_{X_{i}}\to\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
. (3) is proved similarly, observing that for any
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta_{i},r_{i}}(X_{0,i};\lambda_{i})
, the line
\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}\to\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
. Therefore, this completes the proof.
∎

Before proceeding, we recall that once a flow becomes sufficiently close to the cylinder at multi-scales, it should remain close to the same cylinder at all scales. This persistence of cylindrical structure is a quantitative version of the uniqueness of tangent flows, and follows from the effective uniqueness theorem of Colding–Minicozzi
[
CM25
]
for the rescaled mean curvature flow. The next proposition records this propagation property in the setting of bounded almost regular flows.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow. Let
\delta>0
and
0<r_{1}<r_{2}\leq r_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta)
. Then there exists
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta)>0
such that for any
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
the following holds. Suppose
X_{0}
satisfies
t_{X_{0}}\geq 4\eta^{-2}r_{2}^{2}
, and that there exists
X\in Q_{2r_{2}}(X_{0})
and some

\lambda\in[\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}]

with

\Theta_{X}(\eta r_{1})\ \geq\ \lambda-\eta^{2}\ \geq\ \Theta_{X}(\eta^{-1}r_{2})-\eta^{2}.

Then
X
is
(\delta,s)
-cylindrical with respect to some fixed axis
\mathcal{L}_{X}
for every
s\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
, the pinching condition and smallness of
\eta
imply that
X
is
(\delta_{*},r_{2})
-cylindrical for some small
\delta_{*}=\delta_{*}(\mathcal{M})
. By Definition
2.7
, for every
s\in[\delta_{*}r_{2},\delta_{*}^{-1}r_{2}]
the normalized slice
\mathcal{M}_{X,s}\cap(B_{\delta_{*}^{-2}}\times\{t=-1\})
is a
C^{2,\alpha}
-graph with norm
\leq\delta_{*}^{2}
over the corresponding round cylinder denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. By Lemma
2.9
, we have
|\Theta_{X}(s)-\lambda_{1}|\leq\delta_{*}
for every
s\in[\delta_{*}r_{2},\delta_{*}^{-1}r_{2}]
.

Set
\sigma=-\log(s/r_{2})
so that
s=r_{2}e^{-\sigma}
. Define the rescaled flow
\Sigma_{\sigma}:=\mathcal{M}_{X,\,r_{2}e^{-\sigma}}\cap\{t=-1\}
, which satisfies the rescaled mean curvature flow equation
\partial_{\sigma}x=(\tfrac{1}{2}x^{\perp}-H)
. Due to the rescaling and the pinching for
\Theta_{X}
, we conclude that
\big|(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}-\lambda_{1}\big|\leq\delta_{*}
for any
\sigma\in[\log\delta_{*},-\log\delta_{*}-\log(r_{1}/r_{2})]
. Therefore, we can apply
[
CM25
, Theorem 0.5]
to conclude that
B_{\delta_{*}^{-2}}(0)\cap\Sigma_{\sigma}
can be written as a
C^{2,\alpha}
graph over the same cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{1}
with graph norm smaller than
c(\delta_{*})
with
c(\delta_{*})\to 0
as
\delta_{*}\to 0
, for any
\sigma\in[1+\log\delta_{*},-\log\delta_{*}-\log(r_{1}/r_{2})]
. Reverting to the unrescaled variables,
X
is
(\delta,s)
-cylindrical with respect to the fixed axis
\mathcal{L}_{X}
for every
s\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
.
∎

Lastly we prove the almost rigidity for
\lambda
close to
1
. See also Brakke’s regularity theorem
[
Bra78
,
Wh05
,
KT14
,
DPGS24
]
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow. For any
\delta>0
,
\tau>0
,
r\leq r_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta,\tau)
and
\eta\leq\eta_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta,\tau)
, the following holds for any
X_{0}
with
t_{X_{0}}\geq 4\eta^{-2}r^{2}
. Suppose
X=(x,t_{X})\in\mathcal{V}_{\eta,r}(X_{0};\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in[1,1+\lambda_{gap}]
. Then
X
is
(\delta,s)
-regular.

Proof.

We apply the same contradiction argument as in Proposition
3.6
. The only difference is that the limit flow has entropy
1
and hence is a static plane. Then smooth convergence then implies the result.
∎

4.
Cylindrical Regions and bi-Lipschitz estimates

We now introduce the cylindrical regions that will appear in the covering theorems. The idea first appears in Jiang-Naber
[
JN21
]
for Einstein manifolds, has been generalized to many other settings
[
NV19
,
CJN21
,
BNS22
,
NV24
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular flow. Let
\mathcal{C}\subset Q_{2r}(X_{0})
be a closed subset, and let
r_{X}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
be a radius function and
\tau\leq 10^{-10}
. The set
\mathcal{N}=Q_{2r}(X_{0})\setminus\bar{Q}_{r_{X}}(\mathcal{C})
is called a
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical region if for any
X\in\mathcal{C}
the following hold:

(1)

\{Q_{\tau^{2}r_{X}}(X)\}
are pairwise disjoint for
X\in\mathcal{C}
;

(2)

for each
r_{X}\leq s\leq r
,
X
is
(\delta,s)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X}=L_{X}\times\{t_{X}\}
;

(3)

for each
s\geq r_{X}
with
Q_{2s}(X)\subset Q_{2r}(X_{0})
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{X}\cap Q_{s}(X)\subset Q_{\tau s}(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathcal{C}\cap Q_{s}(X)\subset Q_{\tau s}(\mathcal{L}_{X})
.

Here
\bar{Q}_{r_{X}}(\mathcal{C})\equiv\cup_{X\in\mathcal{C}}\bar{Q}_{r_{X}}(X)
is the union of the closure of central balls.

Remark 4.2
.

In the definition, we can choose the axis
\mathcal{L}_{X}
of the
(\delta,s)
-cylindrical
X
depending only on the point
X
but not on the scale
s
. This follows from the uniqueness of tangent flows of cylindrical singularities by Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM15
]
, as recorded in Proposition
3.7
.

The key geometric feature is that the set of centers
\mathcal{C}
can be projected to the axis
\mathcal{L}_{X}
for
X\in\mathcal{C}
in a bi-Lipschitz way.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{C}
, the projection map
\pi_{X}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{L}_{X}
is a bi-Lipschitz map, i.e.

(1-2\tau)d(X_{1},X_{2})\leq||\pi_{X}(X_{1})-\pi_{X}(X_{2})||\leq d(X_{1},X_{2}),\text{ for any }X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{C}.

(4.4)

Proof.

Fix some
X
and consider the projection map
\pi_{X}
. Take any
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
. For simplicity we assume
d(X,X_{1})\geq d(X,X_{2})
. Since
\pi_{X}
is a projection, the right hand side of (
4.4
) holds trivially. Since
Q_{\tau^{2}r_{X_{1}}}(X_{1})\cap Q_{\tau^{2}r_{X_{2}}}(X_{2})=\emptyset
, we have
d\equiv d(X_{1},X_{2})\geq\tau^{2}\max\{r_{X_{1}},r_{X_{2}}\}.
By definition,
X_{1}
is
(\delta,\tau^{-2}d)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}=(x_{1}+L_{X_{1}},t_{X_{1}})
and
X_{2}
is
(\delta,\tau^{-2}d)
-cylindrical with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{X_{2}}=(x_{2}+L_{X_{2}},t_{X_{2}})
. By Proposition
3.6
, if
\delta
is chosen small, then we have
d_{H}(L_{X_{1}}\cap B_{1}(0),L_{X_{2}}\cap B_{1}(0))\leq\tau^{2}
and

||\pi_{X_{1}}(X_{1}-X_{2})||\geq(1-\tau)d

(4.5)

Similarly, by Proposition
3.6
we have
d_{H}(L_{X}\cap B_{1}(0),L_{X_{1}}\cap B_{1}(0))\leq\tau^{2}
, and thus

||\pi_{X}(X_{1}-X_{2})-\pi_{X_{1}}(X_{1}-X_{2})||\leq\tau^{2}d(X_{1},X_{2})=\tau^{2}d

(4.6)

Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) we conclude that

||\pi_{X}(X_{1}-X_{2})||\geq||\pi_{X_{1}}(X_{1}-X_{2})||-||\pi_{X}(X_{1}-X_{2})-\pi_{X_{1}}(X_{1}-X_{2})||\geq(1-2\tau)d.

Hence this completes the proof of (
4.4
).
∎

As an immediate consequence we obtain a uniform 1-content bound for the centers inside a cylindrical
region. Denote
\mathcal{C}_{+}
to be the subset of
\mathcal{C}
such that
r_{X}>0
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{+}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset Q_{2r}(X_{0})\setminus\bar{Q}_{r_{X}}(\mathcal{C})
be a
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical region. Then there exists some
C(\tau)
such that

\sum_{X\in\mathcal{C}_{+}}r_{X}+\mathcal{H}^{1}_{P}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\leq C(\tau)r
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
and consider the projection map
\pi_{X}
. Since
\{Q_{\tau^{2}r_{Y}}(Y),Y\in\mathcal{C}_{+}\}
is pairwise disjoint, then by (
4.4
) we have
B_{\tau^{3}r_{X_{i}}}(\pi_{X}(X_{i}))
is pairwise disjoint in
\mathcal{L}_{X}
. This implies that

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{X_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{+}}r_{X_{i}}+\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap Q_{s}(X))\\
&\leq C(\tau)\left(\sum_{X_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{+}\cap Q_{s}(X)}\mathcal{H}^{1}(B_{\tau^{3}r_{X_{i}}}(\pi_{X}(X_{i}))\cap\mathcal{L}_{X})+\mathcal{H}^{1}(\pi_{X}(\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap Q_{s}(X))\right)\\
&\leq C(\tau)r.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Using the bi-Lipschitz structure of the centers established in Lemma
4.3
, we can further derive a quantitative
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}
-volume estimate for the neighborhood of the center set
\mathcal{C}
in space–time which will be used in the proof of weak
L^{3}
for second fundamental form
A
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset Q_{2r}(X_{0})\setminus\bar{Q}_{r_{X}}(\mathcal{C})
be a
(\delta,r)
-cylindrical region. Then there exists some
C(\mathcal{M})>0
such that for any
0<s<r

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(Q_{s}(\mathcal{C}))\leq C(\mathcal{M})s^{3}r.

Proof.

Let
s\in(0,r)
. Fix some
X\in\mathcal{C}
and the projection map
\pi_{X}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{L}_{X}
. Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a maximal
s
-separated subset of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to the parabolic distance. Hence the balls
\{Q_{s/2}(X_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is pairwise disjoint. And we have the covering

Q_{s}(\mathcal{C})\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_{3s}(X_{i}).

By Lemma
4.3
, we have
\{Q_{s/4}(\pi_{X}(X_{i}))\}_{i\in I}
is pairwise disjoint. Since
\mathcal{L}_{X}
is a 1-dimensional affine space, this implies that the cardinality
|I|\leq 10rs^{-1}
. Therefore, combining the covering for
Q_{s}(\mathcal{C})
we have

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(Q_{s}(\mathcal{C}))\leq\sum_{i\in I}\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(Q_{3s}(X_{i}))\leq(10rs^{-1})\int_{t_{X_{i}}-s^{2}}^{t_{X_{i}}+s^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}(B_{3s}(x))dt\leq C(\mathcal{M})s^{3}r,

where we use the local area estimate
\mu_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}(B_{s}(x))\leq C(\mathcal{M})s^{2}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Main Covering Theorems

In this section, we prove two main covering theorems. The first one is a covering for each time slice

-content estimate. This new estimate is crucial for the proof of the diameter and curvature bounds.

Theorem 5.1

(Time-slice Covering Theorem)
.

Let

,

(5.2)

where

(1)

each
(x_{i},t_{0})
is
(\delta,r_{i})
-regular, in particular we have
\sup_{B_{\delta^{-1}r_{i}}(x_{i})\cap\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}}r_{i}|A|\leq\delta^{2}

(2)

the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{t_{0}}\cap B_{R}(x)=(S_{0;t_{0}}\cup\bigcup_{j}\mathcal{C}_{0,j;t_{0}})\cap B_{R}(x)
.

(3)

is countable where

is the

-stratum of the singular set.

(4)

each

Moreover, we have the following

-content estimate

\sum_{i}r_{i}\leq C(\mathcal{M},\delta,\eta)R.

(5.3)

Remark 5.4
.

If

is smooth, then we have

with uniform

-content estimate for

, where each

is
(\delta,r_{i})
-regular. The Lipschitz regularity in (4) could be improved to

]
.

The second one is a space-time covering theorem. We cover the space-time region of

using regular balls and cylindrical regions, plus the singular part, with uniform

-content estimate. This is crucial for the proof of the estimate of space-time singular set and the curvature estimate in space-time regions. Some idea is similar with Jiang-Naber

]

]
for parabolic case.

Theorem 5.5

(Space-time Covering Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathds{R}^{3}\times I
be a bounded almost regular mean curvature flow. For any
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
\tau<10^{-10}
,
R\leq r_{0}(\mathcal{M},\delta,\tau)
and
\eta\leq\eta(\mathcal{M},\delta,\tau)
, the following holds. Let
X_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
with
t_{X_{0}}\geq 4\eta^{-2}R^{2}
. Then we have the following covering

Q_{R}(X_{0})\cap\mathcal{M}\subset\bigcup_{a}\big((\mathcal{N}_{a}\cup\mathcal{C}_{0,a})\cap Q_{r_{a}}(X_{a})\big)\cup\bigcup_{b}Q_{r_{b}}(X_{b})\cup S_{0}

(5.6)

such that

(1)

\mathcal{N}_{a}\subset Q_{2r_{a}}(X_{a})

(\delta,r_{a})
-cylindrical region;

(2)

(4)

We will first prove the second covering theorem, the space-time one, and then use it to prove the time-slice covering theorem. We consider two types of covering lemma. The first one is the covering for cylindrical balls.

Lemma 5.7

(Covering of cylindrical balls)

Proof of Theorem

. Therefore, by Gauss equation we have

where each

\mathcal{S}
.

. Since

Comparative Expressivity for Structured Argumentation Frameworks with Uncertain Rules and Premises
Carlo Proietti

Abstract
Modelling qualitative uncertainty in formal argumentation is essential both for practical applications and theoretical understanding. Yet, most of the existing works focus on abstract models for arguing with uncertainty. Following a recent trend in the literature, we tackle the open question of studying plausible instantiations of these abstract models. To do so, we ground the uncertainty of arguments in their components, structured within rules and premises. Our main technical contributions are: i) the introduction of a notion of expressivity that can handle abstract and structured formalisms, and ii) the presentation of both negative and positive expressivity results, comparing the expressivity of abstract and structured models of argumentation with uncertainty. These results affect incomplete abstract argumentation frameworks, and their extension with dependencies, on the abstract side, and ASPIC + , on the structured side.

Introduction

Uncertainty plays a major role in the construction and use of arguments. Considering different sets of background assumptions as holding, or different inference rules as applicable, determines which conclusions are allowed. In adversarial settings such as debates, different levels of awareness of what facts an opponent (or an audience) takes for granted – as well as varying levels of recognition of its inferential skills –
influence which arguments are put forward.
In computational argumentation, there are two modelling paradigms, with different capacities to represent uncertainty. The first one is abstract argumentation
[
7
]
, whose formal basis are argumentation frameworks, i.e. directed graphs representing arguments (the nodes) and their
defeats (the arrows), and where uncertainty can be about these two entities. The other one is structured argumentation, instantiated by various formalisms such as ASPIC
+

[
14
]
, assumption-based argumentation (ABA)
[
20
]
, deductive argumentation
[
4
,
5
]
and defeasible logic programming (DeLP)
[
9
]
. Here the fundamental entities are argument components, such as as premises and the inferential rules that allow deriving conclusions, and uncertainty may concern any of them.

The general question is how uncertainty in abstract and structured argumentation compares. Our main aim here is to define a systematic approach to enable such a comparison and map the relative expressivity of different formalisms for handling uncertainty. Establishing general expressivity results is a way to address many issues about the use of abstract argumentation in structured contexts. In fact, the most relevant concerns are that the information contained in structured frameworks is often lost when moving to their abstract counterpart, insofar as many argumentation frameworks are not plausible instantiations of structured ones
[
18
]
.

1

1

As shown by Prakken and De Winter
[
18
]
, this holds for many specifications of abstract argumentation, such as preference-based and value-based, gradual, bipolar, and probabilistic argumentation, motivated a new branch in the formal argumentation literature that consists of revisiting abstract frameworks to find the structured counterpart
[
6
,
19
,
17
]
.

Our method involves two steps. First, we set a standard framework for uncertainty in abstract argumentation.
The one we employ here is
argument-incomplete argumentation frameworks with dependencies
, dep-arg-IAFs
[
8
]
. In this context, uncertainty is encoded by the set of arguments that are left undecided, and expressivity is measured by the number of possible
completions
, i.e. the ways uncertainty can be settled. The second step consists in “lifting” structured formalisms to the abstract level. This means endowing structured formalisms for uncertainty with an abstract counterpart and then comparing them with different subclasses of dep-arg-IAFs in terms of their
relative expressivity
, a notion we define in Section
2.2
.

Our main results on relative expressivity are provided in Section
3
and summarised in Figure
1
. Essentially, they show that systems for structured argumentation with uncertainty are strictly more expressive than general argument-incomplete argumentation frameworks without dependencies, but strictly less than frameworks with dependencies. They also show that, restricting to structured argumentation, rule-incomplete systems are strictly more expressive than premise-incomplete ones.
Our results are relative to the structured argumentation formalism ASPIC
+
.

We proceed as follows.
In Section
2
, we provide the basics of abstract argumentation and its extension to incomplete argumentation frameworks with argument incompleteness. Further, we introduce the key notion of
relative expressivity
among classes of incomplete AFs, which is key to comparing structured and abstract formalisms. In Section
3
, we introduce ASPIC
+
as our reference framework for structured argumentation and formulate different types of premise-incompleteness and rule-incompleteness. We then proceed to compare the expressivity of different types of IAFs with incomplete ASPIC
+
frameworks.
Section
4

As mentioned in the introduction, simple arg-IAFs lack the power to discriminate among possible completions, therefore lacking a sufficient level of granularity for our present purposes. One way to achieve such granularity is to allow dependency conditions between (sets of) arguments. These conditions express things as “at least one argument of the set

The following result shows that dep-arg-IAFs are sufficiently powerful to isolate any possible set of completions of the underlying arg-IAF.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathsf{arg}\text{-}\mathsf{IAF}=\langle\mathcal{A}^{F},\mathcal{A}^{?},\mathcal{D}\rangle
be an arg-IAF and
X\subseteq\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{arg}\text{-}\mathsf{IAF})
a subset of its possible completions. There is always a set
\Delta
of dependencies such that
\mathsf{com}(\langle\mathcal{A}^{F},\mathcal{A}^{?},\mathcal{D},\Delta\rangle)=X
.

Proof.

The argument provided by
[
8
]
for finite arg-IAFs can be generalized to the infinite case. If we look at
\mathcal{A}^{?}
as a set of propositional variables, then dependencies over
\langle\mathcal{A}^{F},\mathcal{A}^{?},\mathcal{D}\rangle
can be translated into formulas of the infinitary propositional language
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{?})
given by the BNF:

\varphi::=x\mid\bigwedge X\to\bigvee Y\mid\bigvee X\mid\lnot\bigwedge X
where
x\in\mathcal{A}^{?}
and
X,Y\in\wp(\mathcal{A}^{?})\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.

In this picture, completions of
\langle\mathcal{A}^{F},\mathcal{A}^{?},\mathcal{D}\rangle
correspond to propositional valuations over
\mathcal{A}^{?}
where variables corresponding to arguments in the completions evaluate to true and other variables are set to false.
Further, completions of
\langle\mathcal{A}^{F},\mathcal{A}^{?},\mathcal{D},\Delta\rangle
are those among the just described set of valuations that satisfy all formulas from
\Delta
.

Importantly, any set of valuations over
\mathcal{A}^{?}
can be encoded as a formula
\psi
over
\mathcal{A}^{?}
in conjunctive normal form (possibly using an infinitary language with infinite conjunctions and disjunctions if the set
\mathcal{A}^{?}
is infinite), where every conjunct corresponds to (the negation of) a valuation not in the set.
This formula can be then translated into an equivalent set of formulas of
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{?})
(i.e., to a set of dependencies!) reasoning by cases over the conjuncts of
\psi
. If a conjunct
X
only contains positive literals
x_{1},...,x_{n},...
then, we put
\bigvee X
into
\Gamma
. If it only contains negative literals
\lnot x_{1},...,\lnot x_{n},...
, then we add
\lnot\bigwedge\{x_{1},...,x_{n},...\}
to
\Gamma
. Finally, if
X
contains both positive and negative literals
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\dots,\lnot y_{1}\dots\lnot y_{k}\}
, then we add
\bigwedge\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k},\dots\}\to\bigvee\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\dots\}
to
\Gamma
.
∎

2.2
Relative Expressivity

The formalisms we just presented can be compared with regard to their power to express different sets of completions. To deal with the structured (i.e., non-abstract) formalisms that we will use in the remainder of the paper, we refine a notion of expressivity previously introduced in the literature
[
10
,
12
]

Definition 5
.

Given two sets of AFs (i.e., of completions)
S=\{\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1}\rangle,...\}
and
S^{\prime}=\{\langle\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\prime}\rangle,...\}

equivalent
(in symbols,
S\approxeq S^{\prime}
) iff there is a bijective function

such that
\{\langle i[\mathcal{A}],i[\mathcal{D}]\rangle\mid\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{D}\rangle\in S\}=S^{\prime}
, where
i[\mathcal{A}]=\{i(x)\mid x\in\mathcal{A}\}
and
i[\mathcal{D}]=\{\langle i(x),i(y)\rangle\mid\langle x,y\rangle\in\mathcal{D}\}
. Note that if such a function exists, then it is a one-to-one isomorphism among the elements of
S
and
S^{\prime}
.

Given two classes of argumentative formalisms with qualitative uncertainty
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, we say that
\mathcal{X}
is at least as expressive as
\mathcal{Y}
(in symbols,
\mathcal{Y}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{X}
) iff for all
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
there is a
X\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathsf{com}(X)\approxeq\mathsf{com}(Y)
. If the function
i
is clear enough from context, we omit it and identify each
x
with
i(x)
.

Fact 1
.

\preccurlyeq
is reflexive and transitive.

Remark 2
.

When dealing with two classes of abstract formalisms
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
(whose arguments belong to

), one can show that the notion of equivalence boils down to identity as far as expressivity is concerned. That is to say,
\mathcal{Y}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{X}
holds iff for all
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
there is a
X\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathsf{com}(X)=\mathsf{com}(Y)
.

Example 3

\text{arg-IAFs }\preccurlyeq\text{dep-arg-IAFs}

The naturally ensuing question is whether the converse holds, i.e. if arg-IAFs are at least as expressive as rul-ISAFs. The answer is negative.

Theorem 2
.

\text{rul-ISAFs}\not\preccurlyeq\text{arg-IAFs}
.

Proof.

A simple rul-ISAF satisfying the statement is obtained by setting
\mathsf{R}^{F}_{s}=\mathsf{R}_{s}^{?}=\emptyset
,
\mathsf{R}^{F}_{d}=\{\langle\{q\},r\rangle\}
, and
\mathsf{R}_{d}^{?}=\{\langle\{p\},q\rangle\}
(where
\mathcal{L}
is the language of propositional logic and
\overline{\cdot}
is given by classical negation),
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\emptyset
,
\mathcal{K}_{p}=\{p\}
, and
\preceq=\emptyset
. There are then two rule-completions and two associated AFs (two completions), namely,
\langle\{p\},\emptyset\rangle
and
\langle\{p,p\Rightarrow q,p\Rightarrow q\Rightarrow r\},\emptyset\rangle
. It is easy to show that no IAF has a set of completions equivalent to those AFs. For suppose there was such an IAF, then (the arguments corresponding to)

, because they don’t appear in all completions, and therefore they cannot be certain. But then there should be a completion with

and without

, but this is not the case (contradiction!).

\text{prem-ISAFs }\preccurlyeq\text{ t-prem-ISAFs.}

Theorem 10
.

\text{prem-ISAFs }\preccurlyeq\text{ rul-ISAFs.}

Proof (sketched).

Let
\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}=\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K},\preceq\rangle
with
\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{R}_{s}\cup\mathsf{R}_{d}
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{a}^{F}\cup\mathcal{K}_{a}^{?}\cup\mathcal{K}_{p}^{F}\cup\mathcal{K}_{p}^{?}
. We can assume without loss of generality, because of Lemma
5
, that
\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
is tidy (if it weren’t, we would just transform it into an equivalent, tidy prem-ISAF).

Let us inductively define the following translation function for arguments in
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
:

•

\tau(\varphi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\!\twoheadrightarrow\!\varphi&\text{if }\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{?}_{a},\\
\Rightarrow\varphi&\text{if }\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{?}_{p},\\
\varphi&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

•

\tau(\Rrightarrow\varphi)=\,\Rrightarrow\varphi
for
\Rrightarrow\in\{\!\twoheadrightarrow\!,\Rightarrow\}
.

•

\tau(X_{1},...,X_{n}\Rrightarrow\varphi)=\tau(X_{1}),...,\tau(X_{n})\Rrightarrow\varphi
.

Now, we define an equivalent rul-ISAF as follows,
\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}=\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\prime},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle
, where:

•

\mathsf{R}^{\prime}=\mathsf{R}_{s}^{F}\cup\mathsf{R}_{s}^{?}\cup\mathsf{R}_{d}^{F}\cup\mathsf{R}_{d}^{?}
, where:

–

\mathsf{R}_{s}^{F}=\mathsf{R}_{s}
.

–

\mathsf{R}_{s}^{?}=\{\langle\{\},\varphi\rangle\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{a}^{?}\}
.

–

\mathsf{R}_{d}^{F}=\mathsf{R}_{d}
.

–

\mathsf{R}_{d}^{?}=\{\langle\{\},\varphi\rangle\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{p}^{?}\}
.

•

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{?}
.

•

\preceq^{\prime}=\{\langle\tau(X),\tau(Y)\rangle\mid X\preceq Y\}
.

The proof follows the same structure as the one of Lemma
5
. We sketch its structure (the main claims to be proved) and point out the critical points.

[
Claim:
For all
X\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
,
\tau(X)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
] This can be proved by induction on
X
.

[
Claim:

\tau
is injective, that is, for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
,
X\neq Y
implies
\tau(X)\neq\tau(Y)
] This is done by simultaneous induction on
X
and
Y
. We have nine cases on the possible syntactic shapes of
X
and
Y
(i.e.,
X=\varphi
and
Y=\psi;X=\,\Rrightarrow\varphi
and
Y=\psi
, etc.). The inductive hypothesis is only needed for the case where
X=X_{1},...,X_{n}\Rrightarrow\varphi
and
Y=Y_{1},...,Y_{k}\Rightarrow\psi
. Importantly, the case
X=\varphi
and
Y=\,\Rrightarrow\psi
(and the symmetric one,
X=\Rrightarrow\varphi
and
Y=\psi
) needs the assumption that
\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
is tidy, that we justified at the beginning of this proof.

[
Claim:

\tau
is surjective (wrt
\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
] By induction on
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
.

The next step is showing that
\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
and
\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
are indeed equivalent. That is, we need to show that
\{\tau(\langle\mathcal{A}^{\ast},\mathcal{D}^{\ast}\rangle)\mid\langle\mathcal{A}^{\ast},\mathcal{D}^{\ast}\rangle\in\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})\}=\mathsf{com}(\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF})
. For this, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between the prem-completions (of
\mathsf{prem}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
) and the rul-completions (of
\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
). Let
\mathcal{K}^{\ast}=\mathcal{K}^{F}\cup\mathcal{K}^{\ast?}_{a}\cup\mathcal{K}^{\ast?}_{p}
be the set of premises of a prem-completion, its corresponding set of rules in the rul-completion of
\mathsf{rul}\text{-}\mathsf{ISAF}
is
\mathsf{R}^{\ast}=\mathsf{R}^{F}\cup\mathsf{R}^{\ast?}_{s}\cup\mathsf{R}^{\ast?}_{d}
where:

•

\mathsf{R}^{\ast?}_{s}=\{\langle\{\},\varphi\rangle\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}_{a}\}
; and

•

\mathsf{R}^{\ast?}_{d}=\{\langle\{\},\varphi\rangle\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{K}^{\ast}_{p}\}
.

The definition from
\mathsf{R}^{\ast}
to
\mathcal{K}^{\ast}
is analogous. Finally, we prove that for every prem-completion
\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle
,
\tau(\mathsf{AF}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle)=\mathsf{AF}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\ast},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle)=
. This is split into four claims.

[
Claim:

\tau(\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle))\subseteq\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\ast},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle)
] By induction on
X\in\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle)
.

[
Claim:

\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\ast},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle)\subseteq\tau(\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle))
] By induction on
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\ast},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle)
.

[
Claim:

\tau(\mathcal{D}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle))\subseteq\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\ast},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle)
] By cases on the definition of defeat (
X
defeats
Y
on
Z
). In the case where
X
undermines
Y
(on
\varphi
) with respect to
\mathcal{D}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle)
, we distinguish two subcases:
\tau(\varphi)\in\mathsf{Prem}(\tau(Y))
, which leads to
\tau(X)
undermines
\tau(Y)
; and
\tau(\varphi)\notin\mathsf{Prem}(\tau(Y))
, which leads to
\tau(\varphi)=\Rightarrow\varphi\in\mathsf{Sub}(\tau(Y))
and, subsequently, to a rebuttal.

[
Claim:

\mathcal{A}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R}^{\ast},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime}\rangle)\subseteq\tau(\mathcal{A}^{\ast}(\langle\mathcal{L},\overline{\cdot},\mathsf{R},\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{K}^{\ast},\preceq\rangle))
] By cases on the definition of defeat. The critical case here is rebuttal, where we analyse two subcases again: whether the targeted defeasible rule is certain or uncertain. In the former, we obtain that
\tau^{-1}(X)
also rebuts
\tau^{-1}(Y)
. In the latter, we obtain that
\tau^{-1}(X)
undermines
\tau^{-1}(Y)
.

∎

Remark 8
.

It is not difficult to show that
neither disjunctive arg-IAFs (only allowing
\mathsf{OR}
-dependencies) nor nand-arg-IAFs (only allowing
\mathsf{NAND}
-dependencies) are expressive enough to capture prem-ISAFs or rul-ISAFs.

Remark 9
.

Note that not all theorems proved in this section are independent of each other. For instance, it would be enough to prove the depicted arrows and the absence of their reversal in Figure
1
, and the rest of the results (the non-depicted arrows) follow from transitivity of
\preccurlyeq
. However, we find the proofs of each of these results interesting due to the constructions they are based on.

4
Conclusion

In this research note, we presented two contributions: i) a novel notion of expressivity that enables comparison between abstract and structured formalisms for arguing with uncertainty; and ii) the application of such a notion to the comparison of several formalisms, argument-incomplete argumentation frameworks and their extension with dependencies, on the abstract side, and ASPIC
+
frameworks with uncertain rules and premisses, on the structured side. The main results are summarised in Figure
1
.

This note unifies recent work on arguing with uncertain structured formalisms (mainly,
[
22
]
and
[
15
,
16
]
). Our short-term plan is to extend the current document to a journal paper by putting these results into their proper broader context, and by studying whether they can be easily applied to other structured formalisms beyond ASPIC
+
.

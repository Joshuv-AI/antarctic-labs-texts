Distribution-free two-sample testing with blurred total variation distance
Rohan Hore, Rina Foygel Barber

Abstract
Two-sample testing, where we aim to determine whether two distributions are equal or not equal based on samples from each one, is challenging if we cannot place assumptions on the properties of the two distributions. In particular, certifying equality of distributions, or even providing a tight upper bound on the total variation (TV) distance between the distributions, is impossible to achieve in a distribution-free regime. In this work, we examine the blurred TV distance, a relaxation of TV distance that enables us to perform inference without assumptions on the distributions. We provide theoretical guarantees for distribution-free upper and lower bounds on the blurred TV distance, and examine its properties in high dimensions.



1
Introduction

Suppose we observe two independent samples
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
and
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}Q
, where
P
and
Q
are distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A fundamental problem in classical statistics and modern machine learning is the
two-sample testing problem
,

H_{0}:P=Q\quad\text{vs.}\quad H_{1}:P\neq Q,

which seeks to determine whether the two distributions are identical. This problem naturally arises in numerous real-world applications, spanning fields like genomics
(
21
)
, finance
(
7
)
, and generative modeling
(
2
)
. Despite its long history, constructing a
powerful
non-parametric test that is not overly conservative remains a non-trivial task. This is particularly challenging in higher dimensions, but even for small
d
the problem is non-trivial if we wish to avoid placing assumptions on the distributions.

A convenient way to reformulate the problem is by considering some measure of distance
D(P,Q)
, such as total variation (TV) distance, Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence, or Wasserstein distance. If
D
is
proper
, meaning
D(P,Q)=0
if and only if
P=Q
, then we may test for equality of distributions by constructing high-probability upper and lower bounds on
D(P,Q)
based on the combined data
\mathcal{D}_{n,m}:=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
.
Ideally, we would wish to derive such bounds without imposing assumptions on the underlying distributions, so that they remain valid uniformly over all
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{d}
denotes the set of distributions supported on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This leads to the following notion of distribution-free confidence bounds.

Definition 1

(Distribution-free confidence bounds)
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, a statistic
\hat{L}_{\alpha}
is said to form a distribution-free lower confidence bound (
DF-LCB
) on
D
at level
1-\alpha
if, for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and all
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
,

\mathbb{P}_{{P^{n}\times Q^{m}\times\textnormal{Unif}[0,1]}}\left\{{D(P,Q)\geq\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\zeta)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

Similarly,
\hat{U}_{\alpha}
is said to form a distribution-free upper confidence bound (
DF-UCB
) on
D
at level
1-\alpha
if, for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and all
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
,

\mathbb{P}_{{P^{n}\times Q^{m}\times\textnormal{Unif}[0,1]}}\left\{{D(P,Q)\leq\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\zeta)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

In these definitions, the statistics
\hat{L}_{\alpha}
and
\hat{U}_{\alpha}
are each a function of the observed data
\mathcal{D}_{n,m}
, in addition to a random seed
\zeta\sim\textnormal{Unif}[0,1]
that allows for randomization in the construction of the statistics, if desired.

Relating these confidence bounds to the original testing problem, note that a large value of the
DF-LCB
provides evidence against the null
H_{0}:P=Q
. In contrast, a small value of
DF-UCB
supports the claim that
D(P,Q)
is not large.

Total variation distance.

The total variation (TV) distance,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\sup_{A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}}|P(A)-Q(A)|
, is a canonical choice for the measure of distance
D
. TV-based analyses are central in classical statistics because of their fundamental connection to hypothesis testing and robustness of inference procedures
(
11
)
. More recently, TV has re-emerged in modern machine learning, particularly in the analysis of diffusion-based generative models, where it is used as a strong metric to study distributional convergence and stability properties of the learned dynamics
(
14
;
13
)
.
However, TV distance is difficult to estimate from finite samples and is highly sensitive to fine-scale differences between
P
and
Q
, making any inference on it statistically challenging in nonparametric regimes. We next elaborate on this difficulty and show that constructing a meaningful
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}
is, in general, theoretically infeasible.

1.1
Non-trivial
DF-UCB
on total variation distance is impossible

Although the definitions of a
DF-UCB
and
DF-LCB
appear symmetric (see Definition
1
), the two problems are fundamentally different. In particular, for TV distance, we will now see that a non-trivial
DF-UCB
is often impossible without additional assumptions. Indeed, when
P
and
Q
are continuous distributions, finite samples of sizes
n
and
m
are almost surely distinct, and thus one cannot rule out the possibility that
P
and
Q
are mutually singular (i.e., the two distributions have nonoverlapping supports, and thus
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=1
). As a consequence, in the absence of structural assumptions on the distributions, any distribution-free upper confidence bound on
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
must be trivial, in the following sense:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\alpha\in[0,1]
, any
d\geq 1
, and any
n,m\geq 1
. Let
\hat{U}_{\alpha}
be any (possibly randomized) distribution-free upper confidence bound for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then, for any pair of distributions
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
satisfying
\textnormal{atom}(P)\cap\textnormal{atom}(Q)=\varnothing
,

\mathbb{P}_{{P^{n}\times Q^{m}\times\textnormal{Unif}[0,1]}}\left\{{\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\zeta)=1}\right\}\;\geq\;1-\alpha.

(Here,
\textnormal{atom}(P)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:P(\{x\})>0\}
denotes the set of atoms of
P
.) In other words, any valid
DF-UCB
is inevitably as large as the following completely trivial solution: simply return
\hat{U}_{\alpha}=1
with probability
1-\alpha
, and
\hat{U}_{\alpha}=0
otherwise, independently of the data.

Although here we state this negative result in the specific context of constructing a
DF-UCB
, the statistical difficulty of working with total variation distance has been well documented in the literature through a range of different frameworks. In particular, when
P
and
Q
are supported on a finite alphabet of size
K
, consistent estimation of the TV distance requires sample sizes satisfying
n,m\gg K
, and even distinguishing between
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=0
and
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)\geq c
requires
n,m\gtrsim\sqrt{K}

(
8
;
22
)
. Consequently, even in discrete settings, a large support size means that constructing tight, finite-sample confidence bounds for TV distance remains challenging without strong assumptions; this challenge is even more severe for distributions that are continuous. In particular, we emphasize that this challenge is not simply due to high dimensionality: the result of Theorem
1.1
holds even in dimension
d=1
, for arbitrarily large sample sizes
n,m
.

In contrast, constructing a
DF-LCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}
is often comparatively straightforward. For any measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)\geq|P(A)-Q(A)|
. Given samples
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}Q
, the empirical probabilities consistently estimate
P(A)
and
Q(A)
, respectively. For any fixed
A
, we can therefore derive tight, distribution-free lower bounds on
|P(A)-Q(A)|
—and hence on
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
. Unlike in the case of upper confidence bounds, such lower bounds can be non-trivial for suitably chosen sets
A
and distributions
P,Q
.

This asymmetry between upper and lower confidence bounds motivates the central idea of this work: to introduce a principled relaxation of TV distance that permits non-trivial, assumption-free guarantees for providing an upper as well as a lower bound, while retaining the interpretability of the classical TV metric.

1.2
The blurred total variation distance

The statistical difficulty of working with the classical TV distance has motivated a substantial body of work on alternative discrepancies that trade metric strength for improved estimability. Prominent examples include kernel-based distances such as the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD)
(
6
;
20
)
, as well as optimal transport–based distances such as the Wasserstein distance
(
19
;
24
)
. While these metrics may allow for distribution-free estimation, they lose the natural interpretation of TV metric in terms of its connections with hypothesis testing.

In this paper, we investigate a different relaxation, the
blurred total variation
(blurred TV) distance, which compares smoothed versions of the underlying distributions, and which can be interpreted as an approximation of TV distance.
We begin by defining the class of kernels used to induce smoothing. Let
\mathcal{K}_{d}
denote the collection of all probability densities on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which we refer to as
kernels
. Given a kernel
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
and a bandwidth parameter
h>0
, define the scaled kernel
\psi_{h}(u)=h^{-d}\psi(u/h)
. A canonical and widely used choice of kernel is the Gaussian density:

\psi(u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\exp\bigl(-\|u\|^{2}_{2}/2\bigr).

(1.1)

Definition 2

(Blurred total variation distance)
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
and
h>0
. The
blurred total variation distance
between
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
is defined as

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P\ast\psi_{h},\,Q\ast\psi_{h}).

(1.2)

Here
\ast
denotes the convolution operation: we can interpret
P\ast\psi_{h}
as the distribution of
X+h\xi
, where
X\sim P
and (independently)
\xi\sim\psi
.
In words, the blurred TV distance corresponds to comparing distributions in total variation after passing them through a common smoothing operation. Intuitively, this measure is likely similar to TV distance if the bandwidth
h>0
of the blur is small. For convenience, we will also interpret
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{0}(P,Q)
as
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
.

Prior work on blurred TV.

The blurred TV distance, and related definitions, have been studied previously in several contexts. In information theory, total variation after smoothing arises naturally in the study of contraction properties of additive-noise channels
(
3
)
.
15
uses blurred TV to obtain generalization bounds on learning algorithms. More generally,
9
introduces smoothed total variation as a class of integral probability metrics. The work of
18
establishes a central limit theorem in blurred TV, while
4
;
5
study convergence of the empirical measure in the blurred TV metric and its limiting distribution, particularly for the Gaussian kernel (
1.1
).

1.3
Our contributions

In this work, we position blurred TV as a principled relaxation of the classical TV distance for distribution-free inference, and demonstrate that meaningful and practically usable finite-sample confidence bounds for blurred TV are possible, even though classical TV suffers from a fundamental hardness (Theorem
1.1
). Our main contributions are:

•

We construct distribution-free lower and upper confidence bounds (
DF-LCB
and
DF-UCB
) for the blurred TV distance. These bounds are designed to satisfy
assumption-free, finite-sample validity
: the proposed confidence bounds are fully distribution-free and valid uniformly over all distributions
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, without requiring smoothness, moment, or other structural assumptions, with guarantees that hold for arbitrary sample sizes
n,m
and dimension
d
.

•

In the course of developing these confidence bounds, we establish several structural and analytical properties of the blurred TV distance that further justify its role as a principled relaxation of classical TV.

•

We study the behavior of blurred TV in high dimensions and show that, although distribution-free inference can lose power when the dimension
d
grows too quickly relative to the sample sizes
n
and
m
, the blurred TV distance depends primarily on the intrinsic dimension of the data. This suggests that meaningful distribution-free inference remains possible even in high dimensions when the distributions are supported on a low-dimensional subspace or manifold.

1.4
Organization of the paper

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. First, in Section
2
we establish some key properties of both the oracle and the blurred TV measure. In Section
3
, we present the distribution-free confidence bounds on the blurred TV, and in Section
4
, we study the behavior of these bounds in high dimensions. We conclude with a short discussion in Section
5
. All proofs are deferred to the appendix.

2
Properties of blurred TV distance

In this section, we establish several fundamental properties of the blurred TV distance that motivate its choice as a principled relaxation of the classical TV distance. Together, these features position blurred TV as a natural and tractable surrogate for TV in nonparametric settings.

2.1
The role of the bandwidth

We begin by establishing several properties of the blurred TV distance that clarify its relationship to the classical total variation metric, and in particular, the role of the bandwidth parameter
h
in governing this transition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix distributions
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
and a kernel
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
. Then:

(i)

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
for all
h\geq 0
;

(ii)

\lim_{h\to 0}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)
;

(iii)

\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)=0
;

(iv)

the map
h\mapsto\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
is continuous on
h\in[0,\infty)
.

In particular, parts (ii) and (iii) illustrate a fundamental trade-off in the choice of
h
: although a larger bandwidth
h
facilitates statistically tractable, distribution-free inference due to greater smoothing, a smaller choice of bandwidth
h
retains finer resolution with respect to the original distributions. We will return to this point in our results and experiments below.

Based on Proposition
2.1
, one might ask whether
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
decreases monotonically as the bandwidth
h
increases, reflecting the increasing degree of smoothing induced by convolution. In particular, the commonly used Gaussian kernel satisfies this type of monotonicity property:

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
\psi
is the Gaussian kernel defined in (
1.1
). Then, for any two distributions
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, the blurred TV distance
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
is monotonically decreasing in the bandwidth
h
.

However, for other choices of
\psi
, the blurred TV distance is not necessarily monotone in
h
in general. Figure
1
illustrates this phenomenon with an example.

Figure 1:
Near-monotonic behavior of blurred TV with bandwidth
h
. Here
P=\mathcal{N}(1,1)
,
Q=\mathcal{N}(-1,1)
, with
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\Phi(1)-\Phi(-1)\approx 0.683
, marked with a
\star
in the figure.
\psi
is either the Gaussian kernel (left), or a multimodal kernel, given by a density of the mixture distribution
\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathcal{N}(-4,1)+\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1)+\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathcal{N}(4,1)
(right).

2.2
Convergence of the empirical blurred TV

In practice, we do not have access to the true distributions
P
and
Q
, and instead can only work with the empirical distributions
\widehat{P}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
and
\widehat{Q}_{m}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{Y_{j}}
of the available data
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
and of
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}
, respectively. The appeal of blurred TV is that (unlike TV) it permits approximation via the empirical distributions, without assumptions on the original distributions. Here we develop some of these properties to build towards our main results, constructing the
DF-LCB
and
DF-UCB
later on.

First, we verify that the empirical measure
\widehat{P}_{n}
converges to
P
in blurred TV, as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any distribution
P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, any kernel
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
, and any fixed
h>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]=0.

It is also possible to give more precise, quantitative results, establishing a specific rate of convergence under mild assumptions on
P,Q
and on
\psi
. For instance,
4
establishes this type of result for Gaussian kernels; see also Appendix
E
for a quantitative result for general
\psi
.

The above theorem implies that, with respect to the blurred TV metric, the empirical distributions
\widehat{P}_{n}
and
\widehat{Q}_{m}
offer accurate approximations to
P
and
Q
. In particular, this implies that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
should be possible to estimate via the corresponding empirical quantity,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})
. Our next result makes this intuition precise by establishing bounds on the expected value of this empirical estimator. Before proceeding, we first define the split empirical measures

\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)}:=\frac{1}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\delta_{X_{i}},\qquad\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{\lceil n/2\rceil}\sum_{i=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}},

based on two (approximately) equal halves of the sample
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of data from
P
(where we implicitly assume
n\geq 2
in order for this split to be defined). The analogous quantities
\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)}
and
\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)}
are defined similarly using the data
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}
(when
m\geq 2
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
, any distributions
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, any kernel
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
, and any bandwidth
h>0
. Then, for all
n,m\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]-\Delta_{n,m,h},

where

\Delta_{n,m,h}:=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})}\right],

or we simply take
\Delta_{n,m,h}=1
if
\min\{n,m\}=1
.

The gap
\Delta_{n,m,h}
quantifies the intrinsic variability within the
X
and
Y
samples and admits a natural, data-driven estimate via sample splitting. By Theorem
2.3
,
\Delta_{n,m,h}
converges to zero as
n,m\to\infty
(when
h>0
is held constant). Consequently, this suggests that the empirical blurred TV distance
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})
provides an accurate approximation of the oracle blurred TV measure
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
—but thus far, we have only examined its expected value.
In the next section, we show that the empirical blurred TV distance can be used to construct both distribution-free lower and upper confidence bounds for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
.

3
DF-UCB
and
DF-LCB
for blurred TV

In this section, we construct distribution-free confidence bounds for the blurred TV distance.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
,
h>0
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha}

and

\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m}):=\max\Bigl\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})-3\,\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha},\,0\Bigr\},

where
\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha}:=\left(\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{m}\right)\right)^{1/2}
.
Then
\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})
is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at level
1-\alpha
, and
1
1

1

Here and throughout the paper, the
DF-LCB
can be interpreted as
0
if
n=1
or
m=1
(i.e., if the split empirical measures are not defined).

\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})
is a
DF-LCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at level
1-\alpha
.

The validity of these bounds follows from the fact that the blurred TV can be bounded via the expected value of empirical blurred TV, as in Proposition
2.4
; concentration is then established via McDiarmid’s inequality.

Although this result offers clean and useful bounds on the blurred TV, several aspects can be improved for practical use. We summarize the main issues below and indicate how they are addressed.

•

The empirical blurred TV
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})
is hard to compute exactly—numerical integration is possible for smooth kernels, but is computationally expensive. In Section
3.1
, we introduce a Monte Carlo approximation that is easy to compute and preserves distribution-free validity.

•

The above bounds are valid for a fixed bandwidth
h>0
. In practice, it is often desirable to select
h
in a data-dependent way. This motivates the refined confidence bounds in Section
3.2
that are uniformly valid over a range of bandwidths, which we obtain via a monotonization approach.

•

For any
P,Q
,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\to 0
as
h
increases. However, for fixed
n,m
, the error terms in the bounds above,
\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha}
, do not decay with
h
. To improve interpretability, we construct bounds in Section
3.3
that scale with
h
, to allow for a
DF-UCB
that is less conservative as
h\to\infty
.

Each of the three following subsections addresses the above three aims individually. However, we emphasize that all of these issues can be handled simultaneously; we defer the full construction to Appendix
E
and here present only the individual results, for clarity.
Since the
DF-UCB
and
DF-LCB
are handled in a symmetric manner, for clarity we present only the
DF-UCB
results in the remainder of this section, and give the corresponding
DF-LCB
results in the appendix.

3.1
Monte Carlo approximation of empirical blurred TV

We introduce a Monte Carlo approximation of the empirical blurred TV that enables practical implementation of the confidence bounds in Theorem
3.1
while preserving distribution-free validity.

First, observe that the empirical blurred TV admits the representation

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{{\begin{subarray}{c}W\sim\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m}\\
\xi\sim\psi\end{subarray}}}\left[{\left|\frac{\mathsf{d}(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})-\mathsf{d}(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})}{\mathsf{d}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h}\bigr)}\bigl(W+h\xi\bigr)\right|}\right].

Motivated by this identity, we define the Monte Carlo estimator

\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B):=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{k=1}^{B}\left|\frac{\mathsf{d}(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})-\mathsf{d}(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})}{\mathsf{d}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h}\bigr)}\bigl(W_{k}+h\xi_{k}\bigr)\right|,

where
W_{1},\ldots,W_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m}
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}\psi
. Here
B
denotes the Monte Carlo sample size.
The
DF-UCB
in Theorem
3.1
can be updated with only an additional
O(1/\sqrt{B})
term.

Theorem 3.2
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
,
h>0
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B):=\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)+\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha/2}+\epsilon_{B,\alpha/2},

where
\epsilon_{B,\alpha}:=\left(\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{2B}\right)^{1/2}
. Then
\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B)
is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at confidence level
1-\alpha
.

3.2
Uniform validity of confidence bounds across bandwidths

We now construct a
DF-UCB
and a
DF-LCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
that are valid simultaneously for all bandwidths
h>0
. As a warm-up, consider a finite collection

\mathcal{H}_{L}=\{h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{L}\},\qquad 0<h_{0}\leq h_{1}\leq\cdots\leq h_{L}.

Uniform validity over
\mathcal{H}_{L}
follows by a union bound applied to Theorem
3.1
. In particular,
\hat{L}_{\alpha/L}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})
and
\hat{U}_{\alpha/L}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})
provide a
DF-LCB
and a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
, uniformly for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{L}
.

While simple, this approach becomes overly conservative when
L
is large. We therefore propose an alternative construction leveraging monotonicity. Define the
monotonized blurred TV
distances

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}(P,Q):=\sup_{h_{1}\in[h,\infty)}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{1}}(P,Q),\qquad\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{lo}}(P,Q):=\inf_{h_{1}\in[0,h]}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{1}}(P,Q).

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{lo}}(P,Q)\;\leq\;\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\;\leq\;\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}(P,Q)\;\leq\;\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q),\qquad\text{for all }h>0,

where the last inequality follows from Proposition

. Thus,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}
positions itself as a uniform upper envelope that remains closer to the classical TV distance. Moreover, as illustrated in Figure

,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
is typically monotone or nearly monotone in

, so the envelopes closely track the original blurred TV. This observation enables distribution-free confidence bounds that are uniformly valid over
h\in(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For all

, define

Then

is
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, uniformly for all
h\in(0,\infty)

(that is,
\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\hat{U}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})\ \forall\ h\in(0,\infty)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha

3.3
Bandwidth-adaptive confidence bounds at a fixed
h

The confidence bounds in Theorem
3.1
are based on a bounded-differences argument that does not adapt to the variability of the empirical blurred TV. We now introduce variance-adaptive confidence bounds at a fixed bandwidth
h>0
. The key ingredient is a data-dependent variance proxy that captures the sensitivity of the kernel to translations.

For a kernel
\psi
, define its
shift modulus
by
\omega_{\psi}(v):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{v}\ast\psi,\psi)
for
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For commonly used kernels, including the Gaussian kernel,
\omega_{\psi}(v)
grows at most linearly for small
\|v\|_{2}
, and is uniformly bounded,
\omega_{\psi}(v)\leq 1
. Using this quantity, we define

\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}:=\frac{1}{n\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{m\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y_{i}-Y_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}.

(3.2)

\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}
captures the inherent variability of empirical blurred TV terms. By construction,
\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\leq\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{m}
for any
h>0
, but
\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}
may be substantially smaller if
h
is large.

Theorem 3.4
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
,
h>0
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+C_{\alpha}\left\{\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\right\},

where
C_{\alpha}
depends only on
\alpha
and is defined in the proof.
Then

is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at confidence level
1-\alpha
.

We see that this improves on the original
DF-UCB
of Theorem
3.1
, in terms of the magnitude of the terms added to the estimate
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})

\mathcal{O}(\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m})

\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\wedge m}})

4
Blurred TV in high dimensions

The distribution-free lower and upper confidence bounds for blurred TV established in the previous section are valid for any fixed dimension
d
. However, as
d
increases, samples from
P
and
Q
become increasingly diffuse, and consequently the empirical blurred TV tends to approach
1
even for moderate choices of the bandwidth, turning the resulting
DF-UCB
essentially uninformative. This raises a natural question: is this behavior an artifact of the empirical estimator we take, or does it reflect an intrinsic limitation of inference on the blurred TV in high dimensions?

In this section, we show that the latter is the case. Throughout this section, we consider only the Gaussian kernel.

4.1
A curse of dimensionality

We first develop a result that highlights the challenges of inference on blurred TV in high dimensions.
For any
C\geq 1

\mathcal{P}_{d}(C)\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{d}
be the set of all distributions that are supported on the unit ball

C(\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d}))^{-1}

C=1
, the set
\mathcal{P}_{d}(1)
contains only the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}_{d}

.

Fix any dimension
d\geq 1
and sample sizes

It seems, therefore, that the blurred TV distance suffers from a curse of dimensionality if we wish to perform distribution-free inference. However, there is an important caveat to this conclusion: we have been assuming that the distributions

4.2
Dependence of blurred TV on the effective dimension

In this section, we show that the blurred TV distance depends only on the
effective dimension
of the underlying distributions, rather than on the ambient dimension
d
. To emphasize the role of dimension, we will write
\psi_{d}
for the density of
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},I_{d})
, and denote blurred TV computed with kernel
\psi_{d}
by
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\psi_{d}}
.

Suppose
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
are supported on some
k
-dimensional linear subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following result shows that the behavior of blurred TV depends on the “true” dimension
k
of these distributions, rather than the ambient dimension
d
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\psi_{d}
be the Gaussian kernel (
1.1
). For any
h>0
, and any
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
supported on a
k
-dimensional subspace whose span is determined by an orthonormal matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\psi_{d}}(P,Q)\;=\;\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\psi_{k}}\bigl(A\circ P,A\circ Q\bigr),

where
A\circ P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
denotes the distribution of
A^{\top}X
when
X\sim P
, and similarly for
A\circ Q
.

Since the empirical measures
\widehat{P}_{n}
and
\widehat{Q}_{m}
are also supported on the same subspace, the same identity holds empirically, i.e.,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\psi_{d}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\;=\;\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\psi_{k}}\bigl(A\circ\widehat{P}_{n},\,A\circ\widehat{Q}_{m}\bigr)
.
As a consequence, all confidence bounds developed in earlier sections depend on the intrinsic dimension
k
, rather than the ambient dimension
d
.

In practice, it is more likely that a distribution will have approximate, rather than exact, low-dimensional structure: that is,
P
and
Q
may be concentrated near some
k
-dimensional subspace (or manifold). In such settings, we would expect that
k
still behaves as the effective dimension of the problem, allowing us to avoid the curse of dimensionality when we perform distribution-free inference on the blurred TV. We emphasize that the distribution-free guarantees of Section
3
do not assume any knowledge of the effective dimension: rather, any DF-UCB on the blurred TV will be valid regardless, but can be meaningful (i.e., not close to
1
) only if the effective dimension is low.

5
Discussion

In this work, we have established distribution-free confidence bounds for the blurred TV distance, providing a useful proxy for the classical TV distance (which cannot be meaningfully bounded in an assumption-free regime). Our results are valid regardless of dimension and sample size, and our theoretical and empirical results show that the feasibility of meaningful inference on the blurred TV depends on intrinsic rather than ambient dimension: low effective dimension permits meaningful discrimination even in high-dimensional settings, while genuinely high-dimensional regimes mean that any assumption-free inference on blurred TV will be unable to distinguish whether distributions are similar or not.

With these challenges in mind, a promising direction for future work would be to develop a meaningful blurred TV measure on low-dimensional projections or functions of high-dimensional data. Moreover, an important open question is whether assumption-free bounds on blurred TV (and any potential extensions for high-dimensional settings) may be useful in modern applications of two-sample testing problems, such as assessing the quality of generative models.

below, since
X/h\to 0
almost surely as
h\to\infty
, it follows that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X/h}\ast\psi,\psi)\to 0\quad\textnormal{almost surely},

and the same conclusion holds with
Y
in place of
X
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\lim_{h\to\infty}\bigl(\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X/h}\ast\psi,\psi)+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{Y/h}\ast\psi,\psi)\bigr)}\right]=0,

where the interchange of limit and expectation is justified by the dominated
convergence theorem, since the total variation distance is uniformly bounded by
1
.

Proof of part (iv).

Let
(h_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be any sequence in
[0,\infty)
such that
h_{k}\to h\in[0,\infty)
. We aim to show that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{k}}(P,Q)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q).

If
h=0
, this follows immediately from part (ii). Hence, from now on we assume
h>0
. Without loss of generality, we may further assume that
h_{k}>0
for all
k
, since this holds for all sufficiently large
k
.

As in the proof of part (iii), we can write

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)=\sup_{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{g(X/h+\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{g(Y/h+\xi)}\right]\bigr|,

and similarly,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{k}}(P,Q)=\sup_{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{g(X/h_{k}+\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{g(Y/h_{k}+\xi)}\right]\bigr|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{k}}(P,Q)\right|

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sup_{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{g(X/h+\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{g(Y/h+\xi)}\right]\bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\sup_{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{g(X/h_{k}+\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{g(Y/h_{k}+\xi)}\right]\bigr|\Biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{g(X/h+\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{g(X/h_{k}+\xi)}\right]\bigr|

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\sup_{g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{g(Y/h+\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{g(Y/h_{k}+\xi)}\right]\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X/h}\ast\psi,\delta_{X/h_{k}}\ast\psi)}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{Y/h}\ast\psi,\delta_{Y/h_{k}}\ast\psi)}\right],

where the last inequality follows by the same argument as in part (iii).

Since
X/h_{k}\to X/h
almost surely as
k\to\infty
, Lemma
B.1
, together with the dominated convergence theorem, yields

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X/h}\ast\psi,\delta_{X/h_{k}}\ast\psi)}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[{\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X/h}\ast\psi,\delta_{X/h_{k}}\ast\psi)}\right]=0,

and the same conclusion holds for the term involving
Y
.
Consequently,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{k}}(P,Q)\right|=0,

which completes the proof.

\blacksquare

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
. Then

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\|x-y\|_{2}\leq r\end{subarray}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{x}\ast\psi,\delta_{y}\ast\psi)=0.

Proof.

Firstly, since total variation distance is invariant to translations, we have

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{x}\ast\psi,\delta_{y}\ast\psi)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi,\delta_{y-x}\ast\psi).

Thus, it suffices to show that

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\|x\|_{2}\leq r}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi,\delta_{x}\ast\psi)=0.

Fix an
\epsilon>0
. Since the class of continuous densities is dense in
\mathcal{K}_{d}
with respect to total variation distance, we can choose a continuous density
\psi_{1}
such that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi,\psi_{1})\leq\epsilon.

Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi,\delta_{x}\ast\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_{1},\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1})+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi,\psi_{1})+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{x}\ast\psi,\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_{1},\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1})+2\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi,\psi_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_{1},\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1})+2\epsilon,

where the second step again uses translation invariance of total variation distance.

The distribution
\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1}
has density
y\mapsto\psi_{1}(y-x)
, and therefore

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_{1},\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(\psi_{1}(y)-\psi_{1}(y-x)\bigr)_{+}\,\mathsf{d}y.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\|x\|_{2}\leq r}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\psi_{1},\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\|x\|_{2}\leq r}\bigl(\psi_{1}(y)-\psi_{1}(y-x)\bigr)_{+}\,\mathsf{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\|x\|_{2}\leq r}\bigl(\psi_{1}(y)-\psi_{1}(y-x)\bigr)_{+}\,\mathsf{d}y

\displaystyle=0,

where the last equality follows from continuity of
\psi_{1}
, and the interchange of limit and integral is justified by the dominated convergence theorem, using the bound

0\leq\sup_{\|x\|_{2}\leq r}\bigl(\psi_{1}(y)-\psi_{1}(y-x)\bigr)_{+}\leq\psi_{1}(y),

together with the integrability of
\psi_{1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

B.2
Proof of Proposition
2.2
: monotonicity of blurred TV with a Gaussian kernel

We start with observing that for
0<h_{1}<h_{2}<\infty
,

\mathcal{N}(0,h_{2}^{2})=\mathcal{N}(0,h_{1}^{2})\ast\mathcal{N}(0,h_{2}^{2}-h_{1}^{2}).

Therefore, defining
h_{3}=\sqrt{h_{2}^{2}-h_{1}^{2}}
, for any distribution
P
we have
P\ast\psi_{h_{2}}=(P\ast\psi_{h_{1}})\ast\psi_{h_{3}}
. Hence, for any distributions
P
and
Q
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{2}}(P,Q)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}((P\ast\psi_{h_{1}})\ast\psi_{h_{3}},(Q\ast\psi_{h_{1}})\ast\psi_{h_{3}})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P\ast\psi_{h_{1}},Q\ast\psi_{h_{1}})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{1}}(P,Q),

where the inequality holds by part (i) of Proposition
2.1
.

\hfill\blacksquare

B.3
Proof of Theorem
2.3
: convergence of the empirical distribution in blurred TV

We begin by defining a truncation map
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that projects any point onto the Euclidean ball of radius
R
:

g(x)=\begin{cases}x,&\|x\|_{2}\leq R,\\[3.00003pt]
R\,\dfrac{x}{\|x\|_{2}},&\|x\|_{2}>R.\end{cases}

Let
Q
denote the distribution of
g(X)
when
X\sim P
, and let
\widehat{Q}_{n}
be the empirical distribution of
g(X_{1}),\ldots,g(X_{n})
. By construction,
\widehat{Q}_{n}
is the empirical distribution of
n
i.i.d. samples from
Q
.

Next, let
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}
denote the
1
-Wasserstein distance. By
12
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)}\right]\leq C\,R\begin{cases}n^{-1/2},&d=1,\\
n^{-1/2}\log n,&d=2,\\
n^{-1/d},&d\geq 3,\end{cases}

where
C
is a universal constant. In particular, this bound can be relaxed to

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)}\right]\leq C\,R\,n^{-1/\max\{2,d\}}\log n.

Now fix any
\epsilon>0
. Applying Lemma
B.2
below, with
P_{0}=\widehat{Q}_{n}
and
P_{1}=Q
, we obtain

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)\leq\epsilon+\frac{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)}{r(\epsilon,h,\psi)},

where
r(\epsilon,h,\psi)>0
depends only on
\epsilon,h,\psi
.
Taking expectations yields

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)}\right]\leq\epsilon+\frac{C\,R\,n^{-1/\max\{2,d\}}\log n}{r(\epsilon,h,\psi)}.

We now relate
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)
to the blurred TV between the truncated distributions. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{n})+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{n},Q)+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{n})+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)}\right],

where the second inequality follows from Proposition
2.1
. By construction of
Q
, we have

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\mathbb{P}_{{P}}\left\{{\|X\|_{2}>R}\right\}.

Similarly, by construction of
\widehat{Q}_{n}
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{n})=\mathbb{P}_{{\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left\{{\|X\|_{2}>R}\right\},\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{n})}\right]=\mathbb{P}_{{P}}\left\{{\|X\|_{2}>R}\right\}.

Combining these bounds, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq\epsilon+\frac{C\,R\,n^{-1/\max\{2,d\}}\log n}{r(\epsilon,h,\psi)}+2\,\mathbb{P}_{{P}}\left\{{\|X\|_{2}>R}\right\}.

Letting
n\to\infty
and noting that, for fixed
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{C\,R\,n^{-1/\max\{2,d\}}\log n}{r(\epsilon,h,\psi)}=0,

we conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq\epsilon+2\,\mathbb{P}_{{P}}\left\{{\|X\|_{2}>R}\right\}.

Since the above bound holds for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
, it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq\inf_{\epsilon>0,\;R>0}\left\{\epsilon+2\,\mathbb{P}_{{P}}\left\{{\|X\|_{2}>R}\right\}\right\}=0,

which completes the proof.

\blacksquare

Lemma B.2
.

Fix any
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
and any bandwidth
h>0
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant

r(\epsilon,h,\psi)>0
such that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P_{0},P_{1})\leq\epsilon+\frac{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}(P_{0},P_{1})}{r(\epsilon,h,\psi)},

for all distributions
P_{0},P_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

First, we note that by Lemma
B.1

(with
\psi_{h}
in place of
\psi
), there exists a constant
r>0
such that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{x}\ast\psi_{h},\delta_{y}\ast\psi_{h})\leq\epsilon\qquad\text{for all }\|x-y\|_{2}\leq r,

where
r
depends only on
\psi_{h}
and
\epsilon
.
Now, we fix arbitrary distributions
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By the coupling representation of the

1
-Wasserstein distance, there exists a joint distribution
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(X,Y)\sim P
has marginals
X\sim P_{0}
and
Y\sim P_{1}
, and

\mathbb{E}_{{P}}\left[{\|X-Y\|_{2}}\right]=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}(P_{0},P_{1}).

Let
\xi\sim\psi
be independent of
(X,Y)
. Thus, by (
B.1
),

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P_{0},P_{1})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0}\ast\psi_{h},P_{1}\ast\psi_{h})=\sup_{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{f(X+h\xi)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{f(Y+h\xi)}\right]\bigr|

Further, by Jensen’s inequality and (
B.1
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P_{0},P_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\sup_{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\left[{f(X+h\xi)}\ \middle|\ {X}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[{f(Y+h\xi)}\ \middle|\ {Y}\right]\bigr|}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X}\ast\psi_{h},\delta_{Y}\ast\psi_{h})}\right].

By the definition of
r
above, we have

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X}\ast\psi_{h},\delta_{Y}\ast\psi_{h})\leq\epsilon+{\mathbbm{1}}_{{\{\|X-Y\|_{2}>r\}}}.

Taking expectations yields

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{X}\ast\psi_{h},\delta_{Y}\ast\psi_{h})}\right]\leq\epsilon+\mathbb{P}\left\{{\|X-Y\|_{2}>r}\right\}\leq\epsilon+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[{\|X-Y\|_{2}}\right]}{r},

by Markov’s inequality. Finally, since

\mathbb{E}\left[{\|X-Y\|_{2}}\right]=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{W}}(P_{0},P_{1})
by construction of the coupling
P
, the desired bound follows.
∎

B.4
Proof of Proposition
2.4
: bounds on expected empirical blurred TV

We begin by establishing the left inequality.
Fix a bandwidth
h>0
and
\epsilon>0
. By definition of
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
, there exists a measurable set
A
such that

(P\ast\psi_{h})(A)-(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)-\epsilon.

Next, note that
\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}\ast\psi_{h}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[{(\delta_{X_{i}}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{(\delta_{X}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\right]\textnormal{ where $X\sim P$}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbb{P}\left\{{X+h\xi\in A}\ \middle|\ {X}\right\}}\right]\textnormal{ where $\xi\sim\psi$ (with $\xi\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}X$)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{{X+h\xi\in A}\right\}

\displaystyle=(P\ast\psi_{h})(A).

An identical argument shows that
\mathbb{E}\left[{(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\right]=(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)
. Therefore, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{\left|(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})(A)-(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})(A)\right|}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left|\mathbb{E}\left[{(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})(A)-(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\right]\right|

\displaystyle=(P\ast\psi_{h})(A)-(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)-\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, the left inequality follows.

We now turn to the right inequality. Deterministically,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{m},Q).

Thus, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})}\right],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{m},Q)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})}\right].

We prove the first inequality; the second follows by an analogous argument.

Let
X^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,X^{\prime}_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
be independent of
\widehat{P}_{n}
, and let
\widehat{P}^{\prime(1)}_{n/2}
denote the corresponding empirical measure. View
X_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1},\ldots,X_{n}
as sampled from
\widehat{P}_{n}
without replacement, and denote the associated empirical measure by
\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2}
. Then, for any measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[{(\widehat{P}^{\prime(1)}_{n/2}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\ \middle|\ {\widehat{P}_{n}}\right]=(P\ast\psi_{h})(A),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[{(\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2}\ast\psi_{h})(A)}\ \middle|\ {\widehat{P}_{n}}\right]=(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})(A).

Repeating the argument used in the first part, we obtain

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{\prime(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})}\ \middle|\ {\widehat{P}_{n}}\right].

Taking expectations over
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{\prime(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})}\right],

which completes the proof.

\blacksquare

C.1
Proof of Theorem

: distribution-free confidence bounds at fixed bandwidth

We first prove the
DF-UCB
. Define

F(\mathbf{Z}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m}).

We show that
F(\mathbf{Z})
has bounded sensitivity to the replacement of a single observation. For
i\in[n]
, replacing
Z_{i}=X_{i}
by
Z^{\prime}_{i}=X^{\prime}_{i}
yields the empirical measure
\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)}
in place of
\widehat{P}_{n}

F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)},\widehat{Q}_{m})
. By the triangle inequality,

|F(\mathbf{Z})-F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|=\bigl|\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)},\widehat{Q}_{m})\bigr|\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)})\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Similarly, for any

|F(\mathbf{Z})-F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|\leq\frac{1}{m}.

Applying McDiarmid’s inequality, we obtain that for any

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]-\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha}}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

By Proposition
2.4
,
\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]
provides a deterministic upper bound on
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
. This establishes the
DF-UCB
.

We now turn to the
DF-LCB
. We define

G(\mathbf{Z}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2}).

We again verify bounded sensitivity. For any

\displaystyle|G(\mathbf{Z})-G(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)},\widehat{Q}_{m})\bigr|+\bigl|\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1,i)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)})+\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(1,i)}_{n/2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}=:c_{i}

An analogous bound holds for
i=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,\ldots,n
, with

And, analogous bounds hold for the samples from

|G(\mathbf{Z})-G(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|\leq c_{i}\textnormal{ where }c_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},&i=n+1,\dots,n+\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\\
\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{\lceil m/2\rceil},&i=n+\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1,\dots,n+m.\end{cases}

A straightforward calculation verifies that

\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}c_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{9}{n-1}+\frac{9}{m-1}.

Applying McDiarmid’s inequality, we obtain that for any
\epsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{G(\mathbf{Z})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{G(\mathbf{Z})}\right]+\epsilon}\right\}\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}}{9/(n-1)+9/(m-1)}\right),

and therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{G(\mathbf{Z})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{G(\mathbf{Z})}\right]+3\,\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha}}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

By Proposition
2.4
,
\mathbb{E}\left[{G(\mathbf{Z})}\right]
yields a deterministic lower bound on
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
. This completes the proof of the
DF-LCB
.

C.2
Proof of Theorem
3.2
: distribution-free validity of Monte Carlo confidence bounds

The following result extends Theorem
3.2
to provide both a
DF-UCB
and a
DF-LCB
for the Monte Carlo setting.

Theorem
3.2
(extended version)
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
,
h>0
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B):=\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)+\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha/2}+\epsilon_{B,\alpha/2}

and

\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B):=\max\Bigl\{\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)-\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)},\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)};B)\\
-\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)},\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)};B)-3\,\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha/2}-\sqrt{3}\epsilon_{B,\alpha/2},\,0\Bigr\},

where
\epsilon_{B,\alpha}:=\left(\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{2B}\right)^{1/2}
. Then
\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B)
is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at confidence level
1-\alpha
, and
\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B)
is a
DF-LCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at confidence level
1-\alpha
.

Here
\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)
is defined as in Section
3.1
, and for the
DF-LCB
, we also define

\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)},\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)};B):=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{k=1}^{B}\left|\frac{\mathsf{d}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)}\ast\psi_{h})-\mathsf{d}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)}\ast\psi_{h})}{\mathsf{d}(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)}\ast\psi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)}\ast\psi_{h})}\bigl(W^{\prime}_{k}+h\xi^{\prime}_{k}\bigr)\right|,

where
W^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,W^{\prime}_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\prime}_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}\psi
, and

\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)},\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)};B):=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{k=1}^{B}\left|\frac{\mathsf{d}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)}\ast\psi_{h})-\mathsf{d}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)}\ast\psi_{h})}{\mathsf{d}(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)}\ast\psi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)}\ast\psi_{h})}\bigl(W^{\prime\prime}_{k}+h\xi^{\prime\prime}_{k}\bigr)\right|,

where
W^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,W^{\prime\prime}_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}\widehat{Q}_{m}
and
\xi^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,\xi^{\prime\prime}_{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}\psi
, where we draw each sample
\{(W_{k},\xi_{k})\}
,
\{(W^{\prime}_{k},\xi^{\prime}_{k})\}
, and
\{(W^{\prime\prime}_{k},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{k})\}
independently.

Proof.

We begin by defining
\mathbf{W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{B})
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{B})
, and by writing

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle:=\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{k=1}^{B}\left|\frac{\mathsf{d}(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})-\mathsf{d}(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})}{\mathsf{d}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h}\bigr)}\bigl(W_{k}+h\xi_{k}\bigr)\right|.

For any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we observe that

\frac{\mathsf{d}(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h})-\mathsf{d}(\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h})}{\mathsf{d}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m}\ast\psi_{h}\bigr)}(u)\leq 2.

It follows that
F(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\xi})
satisfies a bounded difference property. Indeed, replacing any single coordinate
(W_{i},\xi_{i})
by an independent draw
(W^{\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime}_{i})\sim(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{P}_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{Q}_{m})\times\psi
changes the value of
F(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\xi})
by at most
1/B
. Applying McDiarmid’s inequality conditional on
\mathcal{D}_{n,m}
therefore yields

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)}\ \middle|\ {\mathcal{D}_{n,m}}\right]-\left(\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{2B}\right)^{1/2}}\ \middle|\ {\mathcal{D}_{n,m}}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha/2.

Consequently, since
\mathbb{E}\left[{\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)}\ \middle|\ {\mathcal{D}_{n,m}}\right]=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})
by construction, with probability at least
1-\alpha/2
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\leq\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)+\left(\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{2B}\right)^{1/2}.

Combining this bound with Theorem
3.1
, applied at confidence level
1-\alpha/2
, and using a union bound, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

This establishes the validity of the upper confidence bound.

We now turn to the lower bound. We write
The corresponding Monte Carlo approximations satisfy an analogous bounded difference property (where now we have
3B
independent samples,
\{(W_{k},\xi_{k})\}
and
\{(W^{\prime}_{k},\xi^{\prime}_{k})\}
and
\{(W^{\prime\prime}_{k},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{k})\}
, each corresponding to a bounded difference of at most
1/B
). Thus, by McDiarmid’s inequality, we obtain that with probability at least
1-\alpha/2
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)},\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)},\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)})\\
\geq\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)},\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)};B,h)-\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B,h)-\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)},\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)};B,h)-\left(\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{\frac{2}{3}B}\right)^{1/2}.

Combining this bound with Theorem
3.1
, again applied at confidence level
1-\alpha/2
, applying a union bound yields

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\geq\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},B)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

This completes the proof.
∎

C.3
Proof of Theorem
3.3
: distribution-free validity of confidence bounds uniformly over all bandwidths

Here we state and prove a
DF-UCB
and
DF-LCB
on blurred TV that are valid uniformly over a range of bandwidths.

Theorem
3.3
(extended version)
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For all
h>0
, define

\hat{U}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha/(n\wedge m)}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}

and, for any fixed
h^{*}>0
, for all
h\geq h^{*}
define

\hat{L}^{h,\mathrm{lo}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m}):=\max\Bigl\{\,\inf_{h^{\prime}\in[h^{*},h]}\bigl\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{\prime}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{\prime}}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{\prime}}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})\bigr\}\\
-3\,\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha/(n\wedge m)}-\frac{1}{n\wedge m},\,0\Bigr\}.

Then
\hat{U}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})
is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, uniformly for all
h\in(0,\infty)
, at confidence level
1-\alpha
, and
\hat{L}^{h,\mathrm{lo}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})
is a
DF-LCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, uniformly for all
h\in[h^{*},\infty)
, at confidence level
1-\alpha
.

Proof.

First we consider the
DF-UCB
. Fix any distributions
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
. We can assume
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)>\frac{1}{n\wedge m}
since otherwise the result holds trivially. Let
k\in\{1,\dots,(n\wedge m)-1\}
be the largest integer such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)>\frac{k}{n\wedge m}
. For each
i=1,\dots,k
, define

h_{i}=\sup\left\{h\in[0,\infty):\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\geq\frac{i}{n\wedge m}\right\}.

Recalling Proposition
2.1
, we can see that
h_{i}
is finite and positive, and
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)=i/(n\wedge m)
, for each
i
.
By Theorem
3.1
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha/(n\wedge m)}

holds with probability
\geq 1-\alpha/(n\wedge m)
, for each
i
. By a union bound, therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha/(n\wedge m)}\textnormal{ for all $i=1,\dots,k$}}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

Now suppose this event holds. We will now show that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\hat{U}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})\textnormal{ for all $h\in(0,\infty)$}.

Fix any
h
. If
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)<1/(n\wedge m)
then this is trivial. Otherwise, let
i=\min\{k,\lfloor(n\wedge m)\cdot\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\rfloor\}
, so that

\frac{i}{n\wedge m}\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\frac{i+1}{n\wedge m}.

By definition of
h_{i}
we must have
h\leq h_{i}
, and therefore

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)-\epsilon_{n,m,\alpha/(n\wedge m)},

by the event assumed above. But

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)=\frac{i}{n\wedge m}\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)-\frac{1}{n\wedge m},

by definition of
i
, which completes the proof for the
DF-UCB
.

Next we prove the
DF-LCB
with a similar argument.
Let
k\in\{1,\dots,(n\wedge m)-1\}
be the largest integer such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{*}}(P,Q)>\frac{k}{n\wedge m}
, or
k=0
if
\leq\frac{1}{n\wedge m}
. For each
i=0,1,\dots,k
, we now define

h_{i}=\inf\left\{h\in[h^{*},\infty):\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{*}}(P,Q)-\frac{i}{n\wedge m}\right\}.

Note that
h_{0}=h^{*}
. By Proposition
2.1
, we can see that
h_{i}
is finite and positive, and
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{*}}(P,Q)-i/(n\wedge m)
, for each
i
.
By Theorem
3.1
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)\geq\bigl\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)},\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)},\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)})\bigr\}-3\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha/(n\wedge m)}

holds with probability
\geq 1-\alpha/(n\wedge m)
, for each
i=0,1,\dots,k
. By a union bound, therefore,

\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)\geq\bigl\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(1)},\widehat{P}_{n/2}^{(2)})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(1)},\widehat{Q}_{m/2}^{(2)})\bigr\}\\
-3\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha/(n\wedge m)}\textnormal{ for all $i=0,\dots,k$}\bigg\}\geq 1-\alpha.

Now suppose this event holds. We will now show that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\geq\hat{L}^{h,\mathrm{lo}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m})\textnormal{ for all $h\in[h^{*},\infty)$}.

Fix any
h\geq h^{*}
. Then for some
i\in\{0,\dots,k\}

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{*}}(P,Q)-\frac{i+1}{n\wedge m}\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{*}}(P,Q)-\frac{i}{n\wedge m}.

By definition of
h_{i}
we must have
h\geq h_{i}\geq h^{*}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\hskip-15.00002pt\inf_{h^{\prime}\in[h^{*},h]}\bigl\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{\prime}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{\prime}}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{\prime}}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})\bigr\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)+3\epsilon_{n-1,m-1,\alpha/(n\wedge m)},

by the event assumed above.
But

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{i}}(P,Q)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h^{*}}(P,Q)-\frac{i}{n\wedge m}\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)+\frac{1}{n\wedge m},

by definition of
i
, which completes the proof for the
DF-LCB
.
∎

C.4
Proof of Theorem
3.4
: bandwidth-adaptive distribution-free confidence bounds

In this section, we state and prove the full version of Theorem
3.4
, to construct a bandwidth-adaptive
DF-UCB
and
DF-LCB
.

Theorem
3.4
(extended version)
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
,
h>0
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\left(\frac{r_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{3}+2\sqrt{5}\,r_{1,\alpha}r_{2,\alpha}\right)

and

\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})\\
-6\,\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha}-\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\left(2r_{1,\alpha}^{2}+12\sqrt{5}r_{1,\alpha}r_{2,\alpha}\right),

where

r_{1,\alpha}=(2\log(2/\alpha))^{1/2}
and

r_{2,\alpha}=(\log(16/\alpha))^{1/2}
.
Then
\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h})
is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at confidence level
1-\alpha
, and
\hat{L}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h})
is a
DF-LCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
at confidence level
1-\alpha
.

Proof.

In order to prove the result, we introduce the variance proxy

\tilde{\Sigma}:=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{{X,X^{\prime}}}\left[{\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X-X^{\prime}}{h}\right)^{2}}\right]+\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}_{{Y,Y^{\prime}}}\left[{\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y-Y^{\prime}}{h}\right)^{2}}\right],

where
(X,X^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
and
(Y,Y^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}Q
.
Applying Proposition
2.4
,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha}+\frac{r_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{3(n\wedge m)},

where the last step holds
with probability
\geq 1-\alpha/2
by Lemma
C.1
below, and moreover by Lemma
C.2
below,

\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}\leq\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}+\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{n\wedge m}\,r_{2,\alpha}

also holds with probability
\geq 1-\alpha/2
. Combining these calculations verifies the
DF-UCB
; a similar argument holds for the
DF-LCB
.
∎

Lemma C.1
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.4
(extended version),

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})}\right]-\left(\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha}+\frac{r_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{3(n\wedge m)}\right)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha/2

and

\mathbb{P}\Bigg\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})\\
\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2})}\right]\\
+\left(6\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha}+\frac{2r_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{n\wedge m}\right)\Bigg\}\geq 1-\alpha/2.

Proof.

To prove the first claim, let

F(\mathbf{Z}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m}).

For
i\in[n]
, replacing
Z_{i}=X_{i}
by an independent copy
X^{\prime}_{i}
yields the empirical measure
\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)}
, so that

F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)},\widehat{Q}_{m}).

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle|F(\mathbf{Z})-F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|

\displaystyle=\bigl|\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)},\widehat{Q}_{m})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{P}_{n}^{(i)})=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\delta_{(X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i})/h}\ast\psi,\psi)=\frac{1}{n}\,\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i}}{h}\right).

(C.1)

Since
\omega_{\psi}(\cdot)\leq 1
, it follows that for all
i\in[n]
,

|F(\mathbf{Z})-F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|\leq\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{1}{n\wedge m}.

An identical argument gives analogous inequalities for
i\in[n+m]\setminus[n]
. Next define

\tilde{\Sigma}^{*}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\sup_{\mathbf{Z}_{-i}}\,\mathbb{E}_{{Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i}}}\left[{\bigl(F(\mathbf{Z})-F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})\bigr)^{2}}\right],

where for each
i
, the supremum is taken over all possible values in the remaining entries
j\in[n+m]\backslash\{i\}
of the random vector
\mathbf{Z}
, while the expected value is taken with respect to the distribution (i.e.,
Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
if
i\in[n]
, and
Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}Q
if
i\in[n+m]\backslash[n]
).
Observe that, by (
C.4
), we have
\tilde{\Sigma}^{*}\leq\tilde{\Sigma}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\sup_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{{Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i}}}\left[{\bigl(F(\mathbf{Z})-F(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})\bigr)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{{X_{i},X^{\prime}_{i}}}\left[{\left(\frac{1}{n}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i}}{h}\right)\right)^{2}}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sup_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{{Y_{j},Y^{\prime}_{j}}}\left[{\left(\frac{1}{m}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y_{j}-Y^{\prime}_{j}}{h}\right)\right)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}_{{X,X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X-X^{\prime}}{h}\right)^{2}}\right]+\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}_{{Y,Y^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}Q}}\left[{\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y-Y^{\prime}}{h}\right)^{2}}\right]=\tilde{\Sigma}.

Applying
(
16
, Theorem 1)
to the function
F(\mathbf{Z})
, we conclude that for any
\epsilon\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{F(\mathbf{Z})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{F(\mathbf{Z})}\right]-\epsilon}\right\}\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\bigl(\tilde{\Sigma}+\frac{\epsilon}{3(n\wedge m)}\bigr)}\right).

Therefore, by the definition of
r_{1,\alpha}
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{F(\mathbf{Z})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[{F(\mathbf{Z})}\right]-\left(\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}\,r_{1,\alpha}+\frac{r_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{3(n\wedge m)}\right)}\right\}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}.

(C.2)

Next we turn to the second claim. Let

G(\mathbf{Z}):=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}^{(1)}_{n/2},\widehat{P}^{(2)}_{n/2})-\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{Q}^{(1)}_{m/2},\widehat{Q}^{(2)}_{m/2}).

We may assume
n\wedge m\geq 2
(since otherwise this part of the claim is vacuous). A similar calculation as for
F
shows that

|G(\mathbf{Z})-G(\mathbf{Z}^{(i)})|\leq\frac{3}{n-1}\,\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i}}{h}\right)\leq\frac{6}{n}\,\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X^{\prime}_{i}}{h}\right)\leq\frac{6}{n\wedge m}

for all
i\in[n]
, and similar for
i\in[n+m]\backslash[n]
.
Again applying
(
16
, Theorem 1)
we then obtain

\mathbb{P}\left\{{G(\mathbf{Z})\geq\mathbb{E}\left[{G(\mathbf{Z})}\right]+\left(6\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}\,r_{1,\alpha}+\frac{2r_{1,\alpha}^{2}}{n\wedge m}\right)}\right\}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}.

∎

Lemma C.2
.

In the setting of Theorem
3.4
(extended version),

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}\leq\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}+\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{n\wedge m}\,r_{2,\alpha}}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha/2.

Proof.

We can assume
n,m\geq 4
, since otherwise the result is vacuous.
We write

\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}:=\frac{1}{n}U_{X}+\frac{1}{m}U_{Y},

where

U_{X}:=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X_{j}}{h}\right)^{2},\qquad U_{Y}:=\frac{1}{\binom{m}{2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y_{i}-Y_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}.

Clearly, by construction,

\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{X}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{{X,X^{\prime}}}\left[{\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X-X^{\prime}}{h}\right)^{2}}\right],\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{Y}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{{Y,Y^{\prime}}}\left[{\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y-Y^{\prime}}{h}\right)^{2}}\right],

and so
\mathbb{E}\left[{\hat{\Sigma}^{h}_{n,m}}\right]=\tilde{\Sigma}
.

To control the deviation of
U_{X}
and
U_{Y}
from their expectations, we introduce the empirical variance estimators

\displaystyle\sigma_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n\\
i\neq j\neq k\neq l\end{subarray}}\left[\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}-\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{k}-X_{l}}{h}\right)^{2}\right]^{2},

\displaystyle\sigma_{Y}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{m(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k,l\leq m\\
i\neq j\neq k\neq l\end{subarray}}\left[\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y_{i}-Y_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}-\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{Y_{k}-Y_{l}}{h}\right)^{2}\right]^{2}.

Since
\omega_{\psi}(\cdot)\in[0,1]
and
(a-b)^{2}\leq a+b
for
a,b\in[0,1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n\\
i\neq j\neq k\neq l\end{subarray}}\left[\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}+\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{k}-X_{l}}{h}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{i}-X_{j}}{h}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{1\leq k\neq l\leq n}\omega_{\psi}\!\left(\frac{X_{k}-X_{l}}{h}\right)^{2}\;\leq\;2U_{X},

and analogously
\sigma_{Y}^{2}\leq 2U_{Y}
.

We now apply
(
17
, Theorem 3)
to the order-
2
U-statistics
U_{X}
and
U_{Y}
. By the definition of
r_{2,\alpha}
, with probability at least
1-\alpha/4
,

\bigl|U_{X}-\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{X}}\right]\bigr|\leq\left(\frac{8U_{X}}{n}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{10}{n}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2},

and similarly, with probability at least
1-\alpha/4
,

\bigl|U_{Y}-\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{Y}}\right]\bigr|\leq\left(\frac{8U_{Y}}{m}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{10}{m}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2}.

By a union bound, it follows that with probability at least
1-\alpha/2
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{X}}\right]+\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{E}\left[{U_{Y}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}+\left(\frac{8U_{X}}{n^{3}}\right)^{1/2}r_{2,\alpha}+\left(\frac{8U_{Y}}{m^{3}}\right)^{1/2}r_{2,\alpha}+\frac{20}{(n\wedge m)^{2}}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2}.

Using
n,m\geq n\wedge m
, we further obtain

\tilde{\Sigma}\leq\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}+\frac{4}{n\wedge m}\,\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}\,r_{2,\alpha}+\frac{20}{(n\wedge m)^{2}}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2}.

Consequently, with probability at least
1-\alpha/2
,

\tilde{\Sigma}^{1/2}=\sqrt{\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}+\frac{4}{n\wedge m}\,\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}\,r_{2,\alpha}+\frac{20}{(n\wedge m)^{2}}\,r_{2,\alpha}^{2}}\leq\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}+\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{n\wedge m}\,r_{2,\alpha}.

∎

D.1
Proof of Theorem
4.1
: the curse of dimensionality

Without loss of generality, assume
n\leq m
. The key tool for the proof, across both regimes for dimension
d
, is the following lemma:

Lemma D.1
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
. Let
\psi
be the Gaussian kernel (
1.1
), and let
\hat{U}_{\alpha}
be any DF-UCB on the blurred TV distance
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}
, for some
h>0
. Fix any distributions
P,Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and suppose that for some sample size
N\geq 1
and some
\epsilon,\delta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
that

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq 1-\epsilon}\right\}\geq 1-\delta^{\prime},

where
\widehat{P}_{N}
is the empirical distribution of
N
i.i.d. draws from
P
. Then, for any
n,m\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\zeta)\geq 1-\epsilon}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha-\delta^{\prime}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2N},

where the probability is calculated with respect to a data set
\mathcal{D}_{n,m}
comprised of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P
and
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}Q
.

We first complete the proof using this lemma, and then prove the lemma in Appendix
D.1.3
.
First, let
\delta^{\prime}=\delta/2
and define

N=\left\lceil\frac{n(n-1)}{\delta}\right\rceil.

Applying Lemma
D.1
, for any distributions
P,Q
we see that

\textnormal{If }\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq 1-\epsilon}\right\}\geq 1-\delta/2,\textnormal{\ then \ }\mathbb{P}\left\{{\hat{U}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\zeta)\geq 1-\epsilon}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha-\delta.

Consequently, it suffices to verify that, if
h
satisfies the assumed upper bound (
4.1
), then

\textnormal{For any $P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(C)$, and for $N=\left\lceil\frac{n(n-1)}{\delta}\right\rceil$,}\\
\textnormal{it holds that $\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq 1-\epsilon}\right\}\geq 1-\delta/2$}.

(D.1)

We will verify this separately for the two cases of (
4.1
) (and will define the constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
along the way).

D.1.1
Verifying (
D.1
) for Case 1

Let
\widehat{P}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{i}}
be the empirical distribution of data points
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
, and define

t_{d,\epsilon}=F^{-1}_{\chi_{d}}(1-\epsilon/2),

where
F_{\chi_{d}}
is the CDF of the
\chi_{d}
distribution. We consider the set

A=\cup_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{B}_{d}(X_{i},h\cdot t_{d,\epsilon}),

where
\mathbb{B}_{d}(x,r)
is the ball with center
x
and radius
r
. Then, writing
\xi\sim\psi
, by definition of
\widehat{P}_{N}\ast\psi_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle(\widehat{P}_{N}\ast\psi_{h})(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{X_{i}+h\xi\in A}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{X_{i}+h\xi\in\mathbb{B}_{d}(X_{i},h\cdot t_{d,\epsilon})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{\|h\xi\|_{2}\leq h\cdot t_{d,\epsilon}}\right\}=1-\epsilon/2,

where the last step holds since
\|\xi\|_{2}\sim\chi_{d}
. On the other hand, by definition of
Q\ast\psi_{h}
, for
Y\sim Q
and
\xi\sim\psi
with
Y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}\xi
, we have

\displaystyle(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{{Y+h\xi\in A}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{Y+h\xi\in\mathbb{B}_{d}(X_{i},h\cdot t_{d,\epsilon})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d}(X_{i},h\cdot t_{d,\epsilon}))\cdot C(\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d}))^{-1}=CN(ht_{d,\epsilon})^{d},

where the next-to-last step holds since
Q
has a density satisfying
\sup_{y}|q(y)|\leq C(\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d}))^{-1}
, and convolution cannot increase the maximum value of a density. Therefore, it follows that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq(\widehat{P}_{N}\ast\psi_{h})(A)-(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)\geq 1-\epsilon/2-CN(ht_{d,\epsilon})^{d},

for any data points
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}\in\mathbb{B}_{d}
.
Consequently, if

h\leq\frac{1}{t_{d,\epsilon}}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2CN}\right)^{1/d}

then we have proved that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq 1-\epsilon,

almost surely, which verifies (
D.1
).

Now by definition of
N
, we have
N\leq n^{2}/\delta
.
Since
d\geq 1
, we also have
\left(\frac{\epsilon\delta}{2C}\right)^{1/d}\geq\frac{\epsilon\delta}{2C}
. Moreover, the
\chi_{d}
distribution is
1
-subgaussian and has mean
\leq\sqrt{d}
. Hence, it holds that
t_{d,\epsilon}\leq c_{\epsilon}\sqrt{d}
where
c_{\epsilon}
depends only on
\epsilon
. Together, it suffices to have

h\leq\frac{\epsilon\delta}{2Cc_{\epsilon}\sqrt{d}}\cdot n^{-2/d}.

Thus as long as we choose
c_{0}\leq\frac{\epsilon\delta}{2Cc_{\epsilon}}
, we have verified the result if the first case of (
4.1
) holds. Note that this holds for any
d\geq 1
—that is, regardless of the choice of
c_{1}
.

D.1.2
Verifying (
D.1
) for Case 2

First let
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
fixed
set of data points and let
\widehat{P}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{i}}
denote the empirical distribution. Define a set

A=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\max_{i\in[N]}X_{i}^{\top}x\geq 0.5\right\}.

First, we calculate

\displaystyle(\widehat{P}_{N}\ast\psi_{h})(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{X_{i}+h\xi\in A}\right\}\textnormal{ where $\xi\sim\psi$}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}(X_{i}+h\xi)\geq 0.5}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{X_{i}^{\top}(X_{i}+h\xi)\geq 0.5}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\left\{{h\cdot X_{i}^{\top}\xi\leq-0.25}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}-e^{-(0.25/h)^{2}/2},

where for the last step, we use the fact that
h\cdot X_{i}^{\top}\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h^{2}\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2})
, and
\|X_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
, so
\mathbb{P}\left\{{h\cdot X_{i}^{\top}\xi\leq-0.25}\right\}\leq\Phi(-0.25/h)\leq e^{-(0.25/h)^{2}/2}
, where
\Phi
is the CDF of the standard normal distribution. Choosing
h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\log(4/\epsilon)}}
, then,

(\widehat{P}_{N}\ast\psi_{h})(A)\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}-\epsilon/4.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{{Y+h\xi\in A}\right\}\textnormal{ for $\xi\sim\psi$, $Y\sim Q$ with $Y\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}\xi$}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}(Y+h\xi)\geq 0.5}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}Y\geq 0.25}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}h\cdot X_{j}^{\top}\xi\geq 0.25}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}Y\geq 0.25}\right\}+Ne^{-(0.25/h)^{2}/2},

since
h\cdot X_{j}^{\top}\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h^{2}\|X_{j}\|^{2}_{2})
and
\|X_{j}\|_{2}\leq 1
.
Next, since
Q
has density
q
with
\sup_{y}|q(y)|\leq C(\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d}))^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}Y\geq 0.25}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{y\in\mathbb{B}_{d}}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}y\geq 0.25}\right\}\cdot q(y)\;\mathsf{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}y\geq 0.25}\right\}\cdot\frac{{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|y\|_{2}\leq 1}\right\}}{\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d})}\;\mathsf{d}y

\displaystyle=C\cdot\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}V\geq 0.25}\right\}\textnormal{ for $V\sim\textnormal{Unif}(\mathbb{B}_{d})$}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}X_{j}^{\top}U\geq 0.25}\right\}\textnormal{ for $U\sim\textnormal{Unif}(\mathbb{S}_{d-1})$}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\mathbb{P}\left\{{\max_{j\in[N]}\left(\frac{X_{j}}{\|X_{j}\|_{2}}\right)^{\top}U\geq 0.25}\right\}\textnormal{ since $\|X_{j}\|_{2}\leq 1$ for all $j$}

\displaystyle\leq CN\cdot 2e^{-(0.25)^{2}d/2},

where the last step holds since, for any unit vector
v
, the random variable
v^{\top}U
is
\frac{1}{d}
-subgaussian
(
23
, Theorem 3.4.5)
.
Combining everything,

(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)\leq CN\cdot 2e^{-(0.25)^{2}d/2}+Ne^{-(0.25/h)^{2}/2}\leq\epsilon/2,

where for the last step we assume
d\geq 32\log(8CN/\epsilon)
and
h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\log(4N/\epsilon)}}
, so that each term is
\leq\epsilon/4
.
Therefore,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq(\widehat{P}_{N}\ast\psi_{h})(A)-(Q\ast\psi_{h})(A)\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}-\epsilon/4-\epsilon/2.

Consequently,

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}\geq 1-\epsilon/4\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{N},Q)\geq 1-\epsilon.

Finally, by the bounded density assumption on
P
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{{P}}\left\{{\|X\|^{2}_{2}<0.75}\right\}=\int_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{d}}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|x\|_{2}<\sqrt{0.75}}\right\}\cdot p(x)\;\mathsf{d}x\\
\leq\int_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{d}}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|x\|_{2}<\sqrt{0.75}}\right\}\cdot\frac{C}{{\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d})}}\;\mathsf{d}x=C\cdot 0.75^{d/2}.

Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}<0.75}\right\}\sim\textnormal{Binomial}(N,\rho)\textnormal{ for some $\rho\leq C\cdot 0.75^{d/2}\leq\epsilon/8$},

where the last inequality holds if we assume
d\geq\frac{\log(8C/\epsilon)}{0.5\log(4/3)}
.
Consequently
by Hoeffding’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}\geq 1-\epsilon/4}\right\}\\
=1-\mathbb{P}\left\{{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|^{2}_{2}<0.75}\right\}>\epsilon/4}\right\}\geq 1-e^{-2N(\epsilon/8)^{2}}.

Assuming also that
N\geq\frac{32\log(2/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}
, then,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}\left\{{\|X_{i}\|_{2}\geq 0.75}\right\}\geq 1-\epsilon/4}\right\}\geq 1-\delta/2,

which completes the proof. To gather our assumptions, we have required that:

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{32\log(8CN/\epsilon),\frac{\log(8C/\epsilon)}{0.5\log(4/3)}\right\},

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\log(4N/\epsilon)}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\log(4/\epsilon)}}\right\},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\geq\frac{32\log(2/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}.

By definition, we have
N\leq n^{2}/\delta
, and so the conditions on
d
and
h
can be relaxed to

d\geq\max\left\{32\log(8Cn^{2}/\epsilon\delta),\frac{\log(8C/\epsilon)}{0.5\log(4/3)}\right\},\quad h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\log(4n^{2}/\epsilon\delta)}}.

Assuming that
n\geq\frac{8C}{\epsilon\delta}
this can be further relaxed to

d\geq\max\left\{32\log(n^{3}),\frac{\log n}{0.5\log(4/3)}\right\},\quad h\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{32\log(n^{3})}}.

As long as
c_{1}\geq\max\{96,\frac{1}{0.5\log(4/3)}\}
and
c_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{96}}
, then, these assumptions hold when we assume
d\geq c_{1}\log n
and
h\leq c_{0}/\sqrt{\log n}
. Finally, we have been assuming that

n\geq\frac{8C}{\epsilon\delta},\quad N\geq\frac{32\log(2/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}.

By definition of
N=\left\lceil\frac{n(n-1)}{\delta}\right\rceil
this can be relaxed to

n\geq\max\left\{\frac{8C}{\epsilon\delta},\sqrt{\frac{64\delta\log(2/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}}\right\},

which yields our choice of
c_{2}
.

Proof of Theorem

.
Let
\xi\sim\psi_{d}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
, and note that
A^{\top}\xi\sim\psi_{k}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
. Then, for
X\sim P
drawn independently of
\xi
,

A^{\top}(X+h\xi)=A^{\top}X+h\cdot A^{\top}\xi\sim(A\circ P)\ast(\psi_{k})_{h}.

And,

B^{\top}(X+h\xi)=B^{\top}X+h\cdot B^{\top}\xi=h\cdot B^{\top}\xi\sim(\psi_{d-k})_{h},

by our assumption on the support of
P
. Moreover, by properties of the Gaussian distribution,
A^{\top}\xi\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}B^{\top}\xi
, and thus
A^{\top}(X+h\xi)\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}B^{\top}(X+h\xi)
.
Therefore,

\big(A^{\top}(X+h\xi),B^{\top}(X+h\xi)\big)\sim\big((A\circ P)\ast(\psi_{k})_{h}\big)\times(\psi_{d-k})_{h}.

A similar calculation holds with
Y
in place of
X
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\psi_{d}}(P,Q)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(X+h\xi,Y+h\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}\left((A\;B)^{\top}(X+h\xi),(A\;B)^{\top}(Y+h\xi)\right)\textnormal{ since $(A\;B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is invertible}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(\big((A\circ P)\ast(\psi_{k})_{h}\big)\times(\psi_{d-k})_{h},\big((A\circ Q)\ast(\psi_{k})_{h}\big)\times(\psi_{d-k})_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}((A\circ P)\ast(\psi_{k})_{h},(A\circ Q)\ast(\psi_{k})_{h})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(A\circ P,A\circ Q).

\blacksquare

Appendix E
Additional results and extensions

E.1
Convergence of the empirical distribution in blurred TV

In Theorem
2.3
, we showed that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)
vanishes as
n\to 0
. In this section, we give a more quantitative version of this result.

Theorem E.1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
. Assume
\psi
is a bounded density, with
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)\leq B
. Then for any bandwidth
h>0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq\inf_{A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\frac{B\cdot\textnormal{Vol}(A)}{h^{d}}\cdot n^{-1/2}+(P\ast\psi_{h})(A^{c})\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all measurable subsets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In particular, if
P\ast\psi_{h}
satisfies a moment bound,
\mathbb{E}\left[{\|X+h\xi\|_{2}^{q}}\right]^{1/q}\leq M_{q}
for some
q>0
, then choosing
A=\mathbb{B}_{d}(0,r)
, we have
(P\ast\psi_{h})(A^{c})=\mathbb{P}\left\{{\|X+h\xi\|_{2}>r}\right\}\leq\frac{M_{q}^{q}}{r^{q}}
by Markov’s inequality. Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq\frac{B\cdot\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d})\cdot r^{d}}{h^{d}}\cdot n^{-1/2}+\frac{M_{q}^{q}}{r^{q}},

where
\mathbb{B}_{d}
is the unit ball. Choosing

r=\left(\frac{M_{q}^{q}h^{d}n^{1/2}}{B\cdot\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d})}\right)^{1/(d+q)}
,
then,

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]\leq n^{-\frac{q}{2(d+q)}}\cdot 2M_{q}^{\frac{dq}{d+q}}\left(\frac{B\cdot\textnormal{Vol}(\mathbb{B}_{d})}{h^{d}}\right)^{\frac{q}{d+q}}.

Proof.

We will use the following facts: the density of
P\ast\psi_{h}
is given by
z\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]
, and, for any distributions
P,Q
with densities
f,g
we can calculate
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)=\int(f(z)-g(z))_{+}\;\mathsf{d}z
.

We then have

\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat{P}_{n}\ast\psi_{h},P\ast\psi_{h})}\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\int\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}\,\mathsf{d}z}\right].

Now fix a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}\leq\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right],

and therefore

\int_{A^{c}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}\,\mathsf{d}z\\
\leq\int_{A^{c}}\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\;\mathsf{d}z=(P\ast\psi_{h})(A^{c}),

almost surely.
This yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},P)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(P\ast\psi_{h})(A^{c})+\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\int_{A}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}\,\mathsf{d}z}\right]

\displaystyle=(P\ast\psi_{h})(A^{c})+\int_{A}\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}}\right]\,\mathsf{d}z,

where the last step holds by the Fubini–Tonelli theorem.
Next, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can calculate

\displaystyle\hskip-15.00002pt\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)^{2}}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}\left(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Var}_{X_{1}\sim P}\bigl(\psi_{h}(z-X_{1})\bigr)\right]^{1/2}\leq\frac{B}{h^{d}}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{1/2}},

where the last step holds since
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{h}(z)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{-d}\psi(z/h)\leq B/h^{d}
. Therefore,

\int_{A}\mathbb{E}_{{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{iid}}}{{\sim}}P}}\left[{\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim P}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{{X\sim\widehat{P}_{n}}}\left[{\psi_{h}(z-X)}\right]\bigr)_{+}}\right]\,\mathsf{d}z\\
\leq\int_{A}\frac{B}{h^{d}}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\;\mathsf{d}z=\frac{B}{h^{d}}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\cdot\textnormal{Vol}(A),

which completes the proof.
∎

E.2
Distribution-free
DF-UCB
that is bandwidth-adaptive and uniform over bandwidths

In this section, we combine the strategies developed earlier to construct a
DF-UCB
for blurred TV that is simultaneously
(a) computationally efficient,
(b) uniformly valid over all bandwidths
h>0
, and
(c) adaptive to the choice of
h
. (An analogous construction is also possible to derive a
DF-LCB
, but we do not state the details here.)

We begin by defining a Monte Carlo estimator of the monotonized blurred TV:

\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B):=\sup_{h_{1}\in[h,\infty)}\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{1}}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B),

where
\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h_{1}}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)
is the Monte Carlo estimator defined in Section
3.1
.
Define also

\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h,\mathrm{up}}=\sup_{h_{1}\in[h,\infty)}\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h_{1}}

where
\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}
is defined as in Section
3.3
.

Theorem E.2
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
,
n,m\geq 1
,
\psi\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
,
h>0
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\hat{U}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{n,m},B)=\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h,\mathrm{up}}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)+\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h,\mathrm{up}}\bigr)^{1/2}\,r_{1,\alpha/2(n\wedge m)}\\
+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\left(\frac{r_{1,\alpha/2(n\wedge m)}^{2}}{3}+2\sqrt{5}\,r_{1,\alpha/2(n\wedge m)}r_{2,\alpha/2(n\wedge m)}\right)+\epsilon_{B,\alpha/2(n\wedge m)}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m},

Then
\hat{U}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{n,m},B)
is a
DF-UCB
for
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, uniformly over all
h\in(0,\infty)
, at confidence level
1-\alpha
.

Proof.

We build the confidence bound in stages.
We start from the bandwidth-adaptive
DF-UCB
in
Theorem
3.4
, and then extend it to hold with a Monte Carlo approximation as in Theorem
3.2
. We then convert to a uniform bound (following the approach of Appendix
C.3
for the proof of Theorem
3.3
as a final step.

Constructing a Monte Carlo, bandwidth-adaptive
DF-UCB
on
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)
.

Fix any
h>0
. First define

\hat{U}_{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h})=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})+\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha/2}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\left(\frac{r_{1,\alpha/2}^{2}}{3}+2\sqrt{5}\,r_{1,\alpha/2}r_{2,\alpha/2}\right)

exactly as in Theorem
3.4
(but with
\alpha/2
in place of
\alpha
).
The result of that theorem tells us that

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\hat{U}_{\alpha/2}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h})}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha/2.

Next, as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m})\leq\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)+\epsilon_{B,\alpha/2}}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha/2.

Therefore, defining

\hat{U}^{h}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}^{h}_{n,m},B)=\widehat{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}}(\widehat{P}_{n},\widehat{Q}_{m};B)\\
+\bigl(\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h}\bigr)^{1/2}r_{1,\alpha/2}+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\left(\frac{r_{1,\alpha/2}^{2}}{3}+2\sqrt{5}\,r_{1,\alpha/2}r_{2,\alpha/2}\right)+\epsilon_{B,\alpha/2},

we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\hat{U}^{h}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h},B)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha,

(E.1)

by a union bound. Hence,
\hat{U}^{h}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h},B)
is a
DF-UCB
(for any fixed bandwidth
h>0
), that is bandwidth-adaptive and is constructed via a Monte Carlo approximation.

Converting to a uniform bound.

Following an identical argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we can leverage the fact that (
E.1
) holds for any fixed
h>0
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
to obtain

\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{TV}}^{h}(P,Q)\leq\sup_{h_{1}\in[h,\infty)}\hat{U}^{h_{1}}_{\alpha/(n\wedge m)}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h_{1}},B)+\frac{1}{n\wedge m}\ \forall\ h\in(0,\infty)}\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.

Noting that
\hat{\Sigma}^{h,\mathrm{up}}_{n,m}\geq\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h_{1}}
for all
h_{1}\in[h,\infty)
, by construction, this implies

\sup_{h_{1}\in[h,\infty)}\hat{U}^{h_{1}}_{\alpha/(n\wedge m)}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h_{1}},B)\leq\sup_{h_{1}\in[h,\infty)}\hat{U}^{h_{1}}_{\alpha/(n\wedge m)}(\mathcal{D}_{n,m},\hat{\Sigma}_{n,m}^{h,\mathrm{up}},B).

Combining all these calculations completes the proof.
∎

On the continuity of the product of distributions in local Sobolev spaces
Stefan Tutić

Abstract
. We consider the space ℋ L s , r ​ ( O ) \mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O) consisting of all local Sobolev distributions of order s s on an open set O O whose Sobolev wave front set of order r r is contained in the closed conic set L ⊆ O × ( ℝ m \ { 0 } ) L\subseteq O\times(\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\}) . We introduce a locally convex topology on ℋ L s , r ​ ( O ) \mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O) and show that the ordinary product of smooth functions uniquely extends to a continuous bilinear mapping ℋ L 1 r 1 , r ′ ​ ( O ) × ℋ L 2 r 2 , r ′′ ​ ( O ) → ℋ L s , r ​ ( O ) \mathscr{H}_{L_{1}}^{r_{1},r^{\prime}}(O)\times\mathscr{H}_{L_{2}}^{r_{2},r^{\prime\prime}}(O)\to\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O) , for appropriate s s and r r when L 1 L_{1} and L 2 L_{2} are in a favorable position. The key ingredient in our proof is to employ Hörmander’s idea of considering the pullback by the diagonal map x ↦ ( x , x ) x\mapsto(x,x) of the tensor product of two distributions.



1.
Introduction

Multiplication of two distributions is one of the fundamental operations in quantum field theory and the theory of partial differential equations. It is well known that their product cannot be well defined in the general case (see
[
10
]
). For some distributions, it is possible to define their product by using wave front sets, which were introduced by Hörmander (for more details see
[
6
]
). In
[
5
]
, Duistermaat and Hörmander introduced the Sobolev wave front set to study the propagation of singularities of solutions of partial differential equations.

In this paper, we consider Sobolev globally regular distributions of order
s
, defined on the open subset
O
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, that are microlocally Sobolev regular of order
r
, denoted by
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
, where the microlocal property is described by the Sobolev wave front set which is contained in a given closed conic subset
L
of
O\times(\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\})
. The main goal of this paper is to show that the product of two distributions from
\mathscr{H}_{L_{1}}^{r^{\prime},r_{1}}(O)\times\mathscr{H}_{L_{2}}^{r^{\prime\prime},r_{2}}(O)
is again a distribution of this kind
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
, where
s,r,L
depend on the initial parameters
r^{\prime},r_{1},L_{1},r^{\prime\prime},r_{2},L_{2}
. This is obtained by using standard techniques of the pullback by the diagonal mapping of the tensor product of distributions. Moreover, we equip
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
with a locally convex topology in a natural way and show that the product we define is a continuous bilinear mapping
\mathscr{H}_{L_{1}}^{r_{1},r^{\prime}}(O)\times\mathscr{H}_{L_{2}}^{r_{2},r^{\prime\prime}}(O)\to\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
.

The motivation for our work comes from the recent results obtained in
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
. In
[
3
]
, the authors studied the continuity of the product of two distributions using the classical
C^{\infty}
wave front set. Originally, this was done by Hörmander, but his topology was weak, in the sense that the pullback mapping was not continuous; it is only sequentially continuous. The authors in
[
3
]
, modified the topology so that the pullback mapping is continuous (see also
[
4
]
). The ideas they used in their work can be implemented in our case. In
[
9
]
, the authors introduced a locally convex topology on the space
\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O)
, consisting of the distributions whose Sobolev wave front set of order
r
is contained in a given closed conic set
L
. For this topology, the pullback mapping is continuous for a certain order of Sobolev microregularity. Employing this notations, we set
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O):=H_{loc}^{s}(O)\cap\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O)
and
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
will carry the topology induced by the continuous seminorms on
\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O)
together with the continuous seminorms on
H_{loc}^{s}(O)
.

2.
Preliminaries

First, we fix the notations we are going to employ throughout the paper. The sets of positive integers, nonnegative integers, and real numbers are denoted by
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathbb{R}
, respectively. A subset
V
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a cone if
\lambda x\in V

\lambda>0,x\in V
. On the other hand, a subset
L
of
\dot{T}^{*}O=O\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}f(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}\mathrm{d}x

\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
for all

. With
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}\mathcal{F}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
we denote the Sobolev space of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The local Sobolev space of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
defined on the open subset
O
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by

(1)

\displaystyle H_{loc}^{s}(O)=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)\mid\varphi u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for all }\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O)\}.

For more details on Sobolev spaces, we refer to
[
1
]
.

The Sobolev wave front set
WF^{r}(u)
of order
r\in\mathbb{R}
of
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
was introduced in
[
5
]
and it is defined as follows.
For
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
v\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(O)
, set

\displaystyle\Sigma^{r}(v):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\mid\neg(\text{ exists an open cone $V\ni\xi$ such that }\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{r}\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{2}(V)}<\infty)\}.

For
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{x}^{r}(u)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O)\\
\varphi(x)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Sigma^{r}(\varphi u),\quad x\in O,

\displaystyle WF^{r}(u)

\displaystyle:=\{(x,\xi)\in O\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})\mid\xi\in\Sigma_{x}^{r}(u)\}.

Set
\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O):=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)\mid WF^{r}(u)\subseteq L\}
, where
L
is a closed conic subset of
O\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
. Following
[
9
]
, we equip
\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O)
with the locally convex topology induced by all seminorms
p_{B}(u):=\sup_{\varphi\in B}|\langle u,\varphi\rangle|
, where
B
is a bounded subset of
\mathcal{D}(O)
, together with all seminroms

\displaystyle p_{r;\varphi,V}(u):=\left(\int_{V}\langle\xi\rangle^{r}|\mathcal{F}(\varphi u)(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O)
and the closed cone
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
(\operatorname{supp}\varphi\times V)\cap L=\emptyset
. We recall the following result from
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.21]
)
.

Let
O
and
U
be open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively, let
f:O\to U
be a smooth map and let
L
be a closed conic subset of
U\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
satisfying
L\cap\mathcal{N}_{f}=\emptyset
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{f}=\{(f(x),\eta)\in U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x\in O,\ ^{t}f^{\prime}(x)\eta=0\}.

The pullback
f^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(O),f^{*}(u)=u\circ f
, uniquely extends to a well defined and continuous mapping
f^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r_{2}}(U)\to\mathcal{D}_{f^{*}L}^{\,\prime\,r_{1}}(O)
when
r_{2}-r_{1}>\frac{n}{2},r_{2}>\frac{n}{2}
, where

(2)

\displaystyle f^{*}L=\{(x,\,^{t}f^{\prime}(x)\eta)\in O\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid(f(x),\eta)\in L\}.

If

has a constant rank

, then this is valid when
r_{2}-r_{1}\geq\frac{n-k}{2}
and
r_{2}>\frac{n-k}{2}
. When

is a submersion,
f^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r_{2}}(U)\to\mathcal{D}_{f^{*}L}^{\,\prime\,r_{1}}(O)
is well-defined and continuous even when

. Consequently, if
f
is a diffeomorphism, then
f^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(U)\to\mathcal{D}_{f^{*}L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O)
is a topological isomorphism for each

.

3.
The main results

We start with the following technical result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open sets. Let
r,r_{1},r_{2},r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
r\leq r_{1}+\min\{0,r^{\prime\prime}\}
and
r\leq r_{2}+\min\{0,r^{\prime}\}
. For all
u\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O),v\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime\prime}}(U)
it holds that

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}WF^{r}(u\otimes v)&\subseteq(WF^{r_{1}}(u)\times WF^{r_{2}}(v))\cup(WF^{r_{1}}(u)\times(\operatorname{supp}v\times\{0\}))\\
&\quad\cup((\operatorname{supp}u\times\{0\})\times WF^{r_{2}}(v)).\end{split}

Proof.

We start off by showing

(4)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{r}(u\otimes v)\subseteq(\Sigma^{r_{1}}(u)\times\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v))\cup(\Sigma^{r_{1}}(u)\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v)),

for
u\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(O)\cap H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O),v\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(U)\cap H_{loc}^{r^{\prime\prime}}(U)
. Let
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\notin(\Sigma^{r_{1}}(u)\times\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v))\cup(\Sigma^{r_{1}}(u)\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v))
satisfies
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\neq(0,0)
. We distinguish three cases:

(1)

\xi_{0}=0
:

From
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\notin\{0\}\times\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v)
, that is
\eta_{0}\notin\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v)
, we get there exists an open cone
V
which is a neighborhood of
\eta_{0}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\lVert\langle\cdot\rangle^{r_{2}}\mathcal{F}v\rVert_{L^{2}(V)}<\infty.

Set
W:=\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\mid\eta\in V,|\xi|<|\eta|\}
. Clearly,
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in W
. If
r^{\prime}\geq 0
then
\hat{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, because
u\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O)
and has a compact support. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert\langle\cdot\rangle^{r}\mathcal{F}(u\otimes v)\rVert_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in V\\
|\xi|<|\eta|\end{subarray}}\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{2r}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\boxed{\begin{subarray}{c}\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{2r}\leq c\langle\eta\rangle^{2r},\\
\text{for }|\xi|<|\eta|\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in V\\
|\xi|<|\eta|\end{subarray}}\langle\eta\rangle^{2r}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\boxed{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leq r_{2}+\min\{0,r^{\prime}\}=r_{2}\\
\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in V\\
|\xi|<|\eta|\end{subarray}}\langle\eta\rangle^{2r_{2}}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq c\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in V\\
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\end{subarray}}\langle\eta\rangle^{2r_{2}}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{\eta\in V}\langle\eta\rangle^{2r_{2}}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta<\infty.

Now, let
r^{\prime}<0
. When
r_{2}\geq 0
, and
|\xi|\leq|\eta|
, it follows

(6)

\displaystyle\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{r}\leq\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{r_{2}}\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{r^{\prime}}\leq c\langle\eta\rangle^{r_{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{r^{\prime}}.

For
r_{2}<0
, (
6
) trivially holds. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert\langle\cdot\rangle^{r}\mathcal{F}(u\otimes v)\rVert_{L^{2}(W)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\in V\\
|\xi|<|\eta|\end{subarray}}\langle\eta\rangle^{2r_{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2r^{\prime}}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}\int_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2r^{\prime}}|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{\eta\in V}\langle\eta\rangle^{2r_{2}}|\mathcal{F}v(\eta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta<\infty,

since
u\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O)
and has a compact support.

(2)

\eta_{0}=0
:

The proof is analogous to the previous case.

(3)

\xi_{0}\neq 0,\eta_{0}\neq 0
:

Since
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\notin\Sigma^{r_{1}}(u)\times\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v)
, lets assume that
\eta_{0}\notin\Sigma^{r_{2}}(v)
. This means that (
5
) holds true. Choose
R>\frac{|\xi_{0}|}{|\eta_{0}|}
and notice that
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\mid\eta\in V,|\xi|<R|\eta|\}=:W
. Now, arguing in the same manner as in the first case, we get that

\displaystyle\lVert\langle\cdot\rangle^{r}\mathcal{F}(u\otimes v)\rVert_{L^{2}(W)}<\infty.

The case
\xi_{0}\notin\Sigma^{r_{1}}(u)
follows analogously.

For
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O),v\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U),(x_{0},y_{0})\in O\times U
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Sigma_{(x_{0},y_{0})}^{s}(u\otimes v)\\
&=\bigcap\{\Sigma^{s}((\varphi u)\otimes(\psi v))\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O),\varphi(x_{0})\neq 0,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(U),\psi(y_{0})\neq 0\}.\end{split}

This follows from the fact that
\Sigma^{s}(\rho w)\subseteq\Sigma^{s}(w)
for
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(O),w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(O)
. To see this, assume that
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})
belongs to the right hand side of (
7
). Then for arbitrary
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(O\times U),\phi(x_{0},y_{0})\neq 0
we can choose open sets
M\subseteq O,N\subseteq U
such that
(x_{0},y_{0})\in M\times N
and
\phi\neq 0
on
M\times N
. Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(N)
such that
\varphi(x_{0})\neq 0
and
\psi(y_{0})\neq 0
. As
\varphi\otimes\psi=(\varphi\otimes\psi)\phi/\phi
, we infer
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in\Sigma^{s}((\varphi u)\otimes(\psi v))=\Sigma^{s}(\phi((\varphi\otimes\psi)/\phi)(u\otimes v))\subseteq\Sigma^{s}(\phi(u\otimes v))
. The other inclusion follows immediately from the definition of the set
\Sigma_{(x_{0},y_{0})}^{s}(u\otimes v)
.

Now, let
u\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O),v\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime\prime}}(U)
. From (
4
) and (
7
) we infer

\displaystyle\Sigma_{(x_{0},y_{0})}^{r}(u\otimes v)\subseteq(\Sigma_{x_{0}}^{r_{1}}(u)\times\Sigma_{y_{0}}^{r_{2}}(v))\cup(\Sigma_{x_{0}}^{r_{1}}(u)\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\Sigma_{y_{0}}^{r_{2}}(v)),

and therefore

\displaystyle WF^{r}(u\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\operatorname{supp}u\\
y\in\operatorname{supp}v\end{subarray}}\{(x,y)\}\times\Sigma_{(x,y)}^{r}(u\otimes v)

\displaystyle\subseteq(WF^{r_{1}}(u)\times WF^{r_{2}}(v))\cup(WF^{r_{1}}(u)\times(\operatorname{supp}v\times\{0\}))

\displaystyle\quad\cup((\operatorname{supp}u\times\{0\})\times WF^{r_{2}}(v)).

∎

Since in the previous lemma we assumed a certain global Sobolev regularity, we introduce the following space.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an open set and
L
a closed conic subset of
O\times(\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\})
. We define

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O):=\{u\in H_{loc}^{s}(O)\mid WF^{r}(u)\subseteq L\}=H_{loc}^{s}(O)\cap\mathcal{D}_{L}^{\,\prime\,r}(O).

The index
s
represents global Sobolev regularity of the distribution
u
while
r
represents microlocal Sobolev regularity of
u
. We equip
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
with the locally convex topology induced by the family of seminorms:

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{r;\varphi,V}(u)&=\left(\int_{V}\langle\xi\rangle^{2r}|\mathcal{F}(\varphi u)(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad q_{s;\psi}(u)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\mathcal{F}(\psi u)(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(O)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O)
and the closed cone
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfy
(\operatorname{supp}\varphi\times V)\cap L=\emptyset
. It is straightforward to verify that
\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
is complete. Furthermore,
[
9
, Proposition 3.13]
implies that
\mathcal{D}(O)
is sequentially dense in
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Since
H_{loc}^{s}(O)\subseteq H_{loc}^{r}(O)
when
r\leq s
,
WF^{r}(u)=\emptyset
for all
u\in H_{loc}^{s}(O)
and consequently
\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O)=H_{loc}^{s}(O)
; it is straightforward to verify that the identity is topological.

Furthermore, for any
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
, the following topological identities also hold true (cf.
[
9
, Remark 3.6]
):

\mathscr{H}_{\emptyset}^{s,r}(O)=H_{loc}^{\max\{s,r\}}(O),\quad\mathscr{H}_{O\times(\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\})}^{s,r}(O)=H_{loc}^{s}(O).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
and let
L
be a closed conic subset of
O\times(\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\})
satisfying
O\times(\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\})\backslash L\neq\emptyset
.
Let the functions
\phi_{j,k}\in\mathcal{D}(O)
and
\widetilde{\phi}_{j,k}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
, be as in
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
. Then the mapping

(9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)\rightarrow H^{s}_{loc}(O)\times(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}),\quad u\mapsto(u,\mathbf{f}_{u}),\,\,\mbox{where}

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{u}(j,k):=\langle\cdot\rangle^{r}\widetilde{\phi}_{j,k}\mathcal{F}(\phi_{j,k}u),\,j,k\in\mathbb{N},

is a well-defined topological imbedding with closed image. Consequently,
\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
is a reflexive Fréchet space.

Proof.

The proof that the mapping is a well-defined continuous injection is straightforward and we omit it. Notice that (
9
) is just the restriction of the map
\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime r}_{L}(O)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)\times(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}})
in
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
to
\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
. To show that (
9
) is a topological imbedding, it suffices to prove that the seminorms
p_{r;\varphi,V}(u)
and
q_{s;\psi}(u)
of
u\in\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
are bounded by continuous seminorms on
H^{s}_{loc}(O)\times(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}})
of the image of
u
under (
9
). This clearly holds for
q_{s;\psi}(u)
. It also holds for
p_{r;\varphi,V}(u)
in view of
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
and the fact that the continuous seminorms on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(O)
of
u
are bounded by continuous seminorms on
H^{s}_{loc}(O)
. This shows that (
9
) is a topological imbedding. Its image is closed since
\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
is complete. The fact that
\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
is a reflexive Fréchet space now follows from the fact that the codomain of (
9
) is a countable topological product of reflexive Fréchet spaces.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Employing
[
9
, Remark 3.9]
, we infer that the bilinear mapping
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(O)\times\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)\rightarrow\mathscr{H}^{s,r}_{L}(O)
,
(\varphi,u)\mapsto\varphi u
, is well-defined and separately continuous and, as both spaces are Fréchet,
[
8
, Theorem 1, p. 158]
implies that it is continuous.

Our next goal is to show that the following bilinear mapping is well-defined and continuous:

(10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{L_{1}}^{r^{\prime},r_{1}}(O)\times\mathscr{H}_{L_{2}}^{r^{\prime\prime},r_{2}}(U)\ni(u,v)\mapsto u\otimes v\in\mathscr{H}_{L}^{s,r}(O\times U),

where

(11)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=(L_{1}\times L_{2})\cup(L_{1}\times(U\times\{0\}))\cup((O\times\{0\})\times L_{2}),

(12)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\leq r_{1}+\min\{0,r^{\prime\prime}\},\quad r\leq r_{2}+\min\{0,r^{\prime}\},

(13)

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\leq r^{\prime}+\min\{0,r^{\prime\prime}\},\quad s\leq r^{\prime\prime}+\min\{0,r^{\prime}\}.

The fact that
u\otimes v\in H_{loc}^{s}(O\times U)
follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
u\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O)
and
v\in H_{loc}^{r^{\prime\prime}}(U)
then
u\otimes v\in H_{loc}^{s}(O\times U)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s\leq r^{\prime}+\min\{0,r^{\prime\prime}\},s\leq r^{\prime\prime}+\min\{0,r^{\prime}\}
. Moreover, the bilinear mapping

H_{loc}^{r^{\prime}}(O)\times H_{loc}^{r^{\prime\prime}}(U)\ni(u,v)\mapsto u\otimes v\in H_{loc}^{s}(O\times U)

is continuous.

Proof.

Let
s\leq r^{\prime}+\min\{0,r^{\prime\prime}\},s\leq r^{\prime\prime}+\min\{0,r^{\prime}\}
and
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(O\times U)
. Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(O)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(U)
such that
\varphi\otimes\psi=1
on a neighborhood of
\operatorname{supp}\chi
. Employing
\langle(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{s}\leq 2^{|s|}\langle(\xi-\xi^{\prime},\eta-\eta^{\prime})\rangle^{s}\langle(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\rangle^{|s|}
together with Young’s inequality we infer

\displaystyle q_{s;\chi}(u\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n-m}\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}\mathcal{F}\chi*\mathcal{F}(\varphi u\otimes\psi v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}

(14)

or

equals

. To verify this, let

and

. Pick

such that

on a neighborhood of

The claim now follows from the denseness of

in

Our main result on the product of distributions in

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an open set. Let
r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime},r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and assume that

(21)

and

as in (
11

,
\delta^{*}f=f\circ\delta
, by the diagonal map
\delta:O\rightarrow O\times O
,
\delta(x)=(x,x)
, uniquely extends to a continuous map

(23)

\delta^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime r}_{L}(O\times O)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime r_{*}}_{\delta^{*}L}(O)

for any
r_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
r_{*}\leq r-\frac{m}{2}
. For any

and
v\in\mathscr{H}_{L_{2}}^{r^{\prime\prime},r_{2}}(O)

r^{\prime}
or

. Furthermore, the bilinear map

is continuous and it restricts to the ordinary pointwise multiplication of smooth functions.

Proof.

For all
x\in O
it holds
{}^{t}\delta^{\prime}(x)=[I\ I]
, where
I
denotes the identity
m\times m
matrix. For
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have
{}^{t}\delta^{\prime}(x)[\xi\ \eta]^{t}=[I\ I]\cdot[\xi\ \eta]^{t}=\xi+\eta
, which means

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}=\{(\delta(x);\xi,\eta)\mid\,^{t}\delta^{\prime}(x)[\xi\ \eta]^{t}=0,x\in O\}=\{(x,x;\xi,-\xi)\mid x\in O,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}.

If

. Due to the definition of

and

and
(x,-\xi)\in L_{2}
, which contradicts with the assumption (
22
). Therefore
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}\cap L=\emptyset
. Since
\delta
has constant rank

, Theorem
2.1
implies that (
23
) is well-defined and continuous when
r_{*}\leq r-\frac{m}{2}
(as

by assumption). For

and

, Theorem
3.4
yields

and together with the above it also shows that the bilinear mapping

(25)

\mathscr{H}_{L_{1}}^{r^{\prime},r_{1}}(O)\times\mathscr{H}_{L_{2}}^{r^{\prime\prime},r_{2}}(O)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime r_{*}}_{\delta^{*}L}(O),\quad(u,v)\mapsto\delta^{*}(u\otimes v),

3.3

.

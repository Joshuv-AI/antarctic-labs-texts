Homogeneous Linear Calculus of Order 1 and a λ \lambda -Taylor Formula
Ronald Orozco López

Abstract
In this paper, a new calculus on sequences is defined. Also, the λ \lambda -derivative and the λ \lambda -integration are investigated. The fundamental theorem of λ \lambda -calculus is included. A suitable function basis for the λ \lambda -derivative and the λ \lambda -integral is provided, and various properties of this basis are given. A λ \lambda -Taylor formula for functions is given.

Introduction

Ward
[
8
]
introduced a calculus on a sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
\psi_{0}=0
and
\psi_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq 1
, in such a way that when
\psi_{n}=n
, we get the ordinary calculus, when
\psi_{n}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}
, is obtained the
q
-calculus
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, and if
\psi_{n}=F_{n}
, where
\{F_{n}\}
is the Fibonacci sequence, then we obtain the Golden calculus
[
6
,
7
]
. A Ward-derivative
\mathbf{D}
is a linear operator which satisfies
\mathbf{D}(x^{n})=\psi_{n}x^{n-1}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The ring of constants associated with the derivative
\mathbf{D}
is the set of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

In this paper, a new calculus on sequences is introduced: the homogeneous linear calculus of order one or
\lambda
-calculus. This calculus is defined on the sequence
\psi_{n}=\lambda^{n}
,
n\geq 0
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, which is the solution of the homogeneous linear difference equation of order 1

\psi_{n+1}-\lambda\psi_{n}=0.

(1)

This new calculus is not Ward’s calculus. We define the
\lambda
-derivative
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
of the function
f(x)
by
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{f(\lambda x)}{x}
. Then
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}(x^{n})=\lambda^{n}x^{n-1}
, for all
n\geq 0
, and thus
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}(1)=\frac{1}{x}
and
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}(0)=0
. Therefore, the ring of constants associated with the derivative
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
is the singleton set
\{0\}
. The
q
-derivative of Jackson
D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{(1-q)x}
is expressed as linear combination of
\lambda
-derivative, i.e.,

D_{q}=\frac{1}{1-q}(\mathbf{D}_{1}-\mathbf{D}_{q}).

(2)

Then, the
\lambda
-calculus is the simplest calculus, and we can use
\lambda
-derivatives to construct new derivatives.

3
deals with the properties of the
\lambda
-derivative

. In this same section, we also study the properties of the

f(x)

\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{x}{\lambda}f(x/\lambda)
.

Section

and
\mathrm{E}_{1/\lambda}(a/x)
, where
\mathrm{e}_{q}(x)
and
\mathrm{E}_{q}(x)
are the
q
-exponential functions. In Section
5
we introduce the functions

(x-a)_{\lambda}^{n}=\left(1-\frac{a}{x}\right)\left(1-\frac{a}{\lambda x}\right)\cdots\left(1-\frac{a}{\lambda^{n-1}x}\right),

(3)

for any positive integer
n\geq 1
y
(x-a)_{\lambda}^{0}=1
, which will serve as basis functions for our
\lambda
-Taylor theorem.. These functions are generalized for all
n\in\mathbb{C}
in Section
6
. In Section
7
we provide a
\lambda
-Taylor theorem for functions

by using the basis functions

3
Homogeneous linear calculus of order 1

3.1
\lambda
-differential operator

Definition 1
.

We define the
\lambda
-derivative
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
of the function
f(x)
as

(\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f)(x)=\frac{1}{x}\widehat{\mathrm{M}}_{\lambda}\{f(x)\}=\begin{cases}\frac{f(\lambda x)}{x},&\text{ if }x\neq 0;\\
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(\lambda x)}{x},&\text{ if }x=0,\end{cases}

(7)

provided that the limit exists.

It is straightforward to prove the following properties of the
\lambda
-differential operator
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 1
.

For all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
,

1.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{\alpha f+\beta g\}=\alpha\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f+\beta\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g
.

2.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{\gamma\}=\frac{\gamma}{x}
.

3.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{x^{n}\}=\lambda^{n}x^{n-1}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 2

(
Product
\lambda
-rule).

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{f(x)g(x)\}=f(\lambda x)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x)=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)\cdot g(\lambda x)=x\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x).

(8)

From the above proposition, we have the following result.

Proposition 3
.

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\{f(x)g(x)\}=\frac{1}{2}(f(\lambda x)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x)+\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)\cdot g(\lambda x))

(9)

Proposition 4

(
Quotient
\lambda
-rule).

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\left\{\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right\}=\frac{\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)}{g(\lambda x)}=\frac{\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)}{x\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}g(x)},

(10)

g(x)\neq 0
,
g(\lambda x)\neq 0
.

Proposition 5
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}f(x)=\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}.

(11)

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, use the definition
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}
. Now suppose it is true for
n
. We will prove for
n+1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n+1}f(x)=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}f(x)=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\left\{\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}\right\}=\frac{f(\lambda^{n}\lambda x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}\lambda^{n}x^{n}x}=\frac{f(\lambda^{n+1}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n+1}{2}}x^{n+1}}

∎

Example 1
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and all
k\geq 1
,

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{k}x^{\alpha}=\lambda^{k\alpha-\binom{k}{2}}x^{\alpha-k}.

(12)

Proposition 6

(
Leibniz
\lambda
-rule).

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(fg)=\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(g).

(13)

Proof.

From Proposition
1
and
5

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(fg)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)g(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}\frac{f(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}\frac{g(\lambda^{n}x)}{\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\binom{n}{2}}x^{n}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(f)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{n}(g).

∎

3.2
\lambda
-integral operator

Definition 2
.

Let
f:[0,x]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a continuous function. The
\lambda
-integral of the function
f(x)
in
[0,x]
is defined as

\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}\{f(x)\}=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)d_{\lambda}t=\widehat{\mathrm{M}}_{1/\lambda}\{xf(x)\}=\frac{x}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)

(14)

provided that
f(x/\lambda)
exists and define, also

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda}x=\int_{0}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda}x-\int_{0}^{\alpha}f(x)d_{\lambda}x.

(15)

We will say that
f
is
\lambda
-integrable in
[\alpha,\beta]
provided that
f
is defined in
x=\alpha/\lambda
and
x=\beta/\lambda
.

From Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
) it follows that

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda}x=\frac{\beta}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right).

(16)

Proposition 7
.

For all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}

1.

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}[f(x)+g(x)]d_{\lambda,a}x=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x+\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x
.

2.

\int_{\alpha}^{\alpha}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x=0
.

3.

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x=-\int_{\beta}^{\alpha}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x
.

4.

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x=\int_{\alpha}^{\gamma}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x+\int_{\gamma}^{\beta}f(x)d_{\lambda,a}x
.

Proposition 8
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}^{n}\{f(x)\}=\frac{x^{n}}{\lambda^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{n}}\right).

(17)

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, we use the Eq.(
14
). Now, suppose the statement is true for
n
; we will prove it for
n+1
. We have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}^{n+1}\{f(x)\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}\left\{\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}^{n}\{f(x)\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}\left\{\frac{x^{n}}{\lambda^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{n}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathrm{M}}_{1/\lambda}\left\{\frac{x^{n+1}}{\lambda^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{n}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{n+1}}{\lambda^{\binom{n+2}{2}}}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{n+1}}\right)

∎

Example 2
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and all
k\geq 1
,

\widehat{\mathrm{I}}_{\lambda}^{k}x^{\alpha}=\frac{x^{k+\alpha}}{\lambda^{k\alpha+\binom{k+1}{2}}}

(18)

Definition 3
.

For any real number
p\geq 1
we will denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{p}[\alpha,\beta]
the set of functions
f:[\alpha,\beta]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
|f|^{p}
is
\lambda
-integrable in
[\alpha,\beta]
, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{p}[\alpha,\beta]=\bigg\{f:[\alpha,\beta]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\ \Big|\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}|f|^{p}d_{\lambda}<\infty\bigg\}.

(19)

We also set

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{\infty}[\alpha,\beta]=\bigg\{f:[\alpha,\beta]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\ \Big|\ \sup\Big\{\Big|f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\Big|,\Big|f\left(\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\right)\Big|\Big\}<\infty\bigg\}.

(20)

Theorem 1

(
Fundamental theorems of
\lambda
-calculus).

1.

Let
f:[\alpha,\beta]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function such that
f\in\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{1}[\alpha,x]
for all
x\in[\alpha,\beta]
. Then

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\int_{\alpha}^{x}f(t)d_{\lambda}t=f(x)-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\frac{1}{x}.

(21)

2.

Let
f:[\alpha,\beta]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function such that
\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f\in\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{1}[\alpha,\beta]
. Then

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)d_{\lambda}x=f(\beta)-f(\alpha).

(22)

Proof.

From Eq.(
16
) and Proposition
2
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\int_{\alpha}^{x}f(t)d_{\lambda}t

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=x\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}(1)

\displaystyle=x\frac{\lambda}{\lambda}\frac{f(x)}{x}-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\frac{1}{x}

\displaystyle=f(x)-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\frac{1}{x}.

and

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f(x)d_{\lambda}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\lambda}(\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f)\left(\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}(\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}f)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\frac{f(\beta)}{\beta/\lambda}-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\frac{f(\alpha)}{\alpha/\lambda}

\displaystyle=f(\beta)-f(\alpha).

∎

(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{(\lambda^{\alpha}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}.

(37)

Proposition 17
.

For any two complex numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
, we have

(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}(\lambda^{\alpha}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\beta}=(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha+\beta}.

(38)

Proof.

The proposition follows directly from the definition, since

\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{(\lambda^{\alpha}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}\frac{(\lambda^{\alpha}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{(\lambda^{\alpha+\beta}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{(\lambda^{\alpha+\beta}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}.

∎

Proposition 18
.

For any complex number
\alpha
, we have

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha+k}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}}.

(39)

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda^{k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda^{k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(\lambda^{\alpha+k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}(\lambda^{k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}(\lambda^{\alpha+k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}(\lambda^{\alpha+k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha+k}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}}.

∎

Proposition 19
.

For any complex number
\alpha
, we have

\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{k}\left\{\frac{1}{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}}\right\}=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha+k}}.

(40)

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\lambda}^{k}\left\{\frac{1}{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(\lambda^{k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda^{\alpha+k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(\lambda^{k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}(\lambda^{\alpha+k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}(\lambda^{k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}(\lambda^{\alpha+k}x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x-a)_{\lambda}^{k}}{\lambda^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}(x-a)_{\lambda}^{\alpha+k}}.

∎

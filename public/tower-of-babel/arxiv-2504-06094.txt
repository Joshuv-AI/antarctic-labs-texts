On the structure of DHR bimodules of abstract spin chains
Lucas Hataishi, David Jaklitsch, Corey Jones, Makoto Yamashita

Abstract
. Abstract spin chains axiomatize the structure of local observables on the 1D lattice which are invariant under a global symmetry, and arise at the physical boundary of 2+1D topologically ordered spin systems. In this paper, we study tensor categorical properties of DHR bimodules over abstract spin chains. Assuming that the charge transporters generate the algebra of observables, we prove that the associated category has a structure of modular tensor category with respect to the natural braiding. Under an additional assumption of algebraic Haag duality, this category becomes the Drinfeld center of the half-line fusion category.

1.
Introduction

The theory of quantum spin chains provides an intriguing manifestation of
discrete
quantum field theory, where ideas from the theory of quantum integrable systems, special functions, and operator algebras meet.
In this context, the algebra of local observables is modeled by the tensor product of finite-dimensional matrix algebras, which can be thought of as the kinematical component of a locally finite-dimensional 1+1D algebraic quantum field theory, but without a designated choice of Hilbert space representation or symmetry group
[
2
]
.
For such systems, tools from the theory of operator algebras turn out to be useful in understanding the algebraic structures behind localized excitations and their interaction with observables.

Motivated by recent developments in quantum many-body physics, the third-named author introduced the notion of
abstract spin chains

[
16
]
.
These are formally defined as nets
A_{\bullet}
of finite dimensional C
∗
-algebras over
\mathbb{Z}
, viewed as a discrete metric space.
These objects mathematically characterize the algebras of local observables that emerge under symmetry constraints
[
21
,
19
,
11
]
, or as holographic duals of topologically ordered spin systems
[
14
]
.
Unlike concrete spin chains, abstract spin chains can exhibit
algebraic entanglement
, meaning that the algebra of observables localized in some region does not necessarily factorize into the tensor product of algebras localized at points.
This leads to the emergence of categorical structures which provide new algebraic tools for studying topological spin systems through holography and dualities.

There are some obvious similarities to the local operator algebraic approach to the 1+1D chiral conformal field theory, based on nets of infinite-dimensional von Neumann algebras over the real line
\mathbb{R}
(or the compactified circle
S^{1}
)
[
7
]
.
However, in contrast to the more purely algebraic situation described above, conformal nets have full diffeomorphism group symmetries which lead to intricate interactions between the analytic structure of infinite-dimensional operator algebras and the underlying topological structure of space(time).
Nevertheless, there appear to be deeper structural similarities between the discrete and continuous settings.
In particular, if
A
is an abstract spin chain, we can associate the braided tensor category of bimodules
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})

[
16
]
, in direct analogy with the braided category of superselection sectors of conformal nets
[
7
]
.
This structure is essential in the applications of abstract spin chains in 2+1D topological holography
[
12
,
3
,
14
]
, and the Kramers–Wannier type dualities for spin chains with (fusion categorical) symmetries
[
1
,
21
,
16
]
.

There are many general results about 1+1D chiral conformal field theories, and it is natural to ask which of these might have analogues in the setting of abstract spin chains.
Our goal in this paper is to investigate tensor categorical properties of
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
beyond this first step, in parallel to foundational results of conformal field theory.
In particular, one motivating result is the modularity of the braided category associated with rational conformal nets
[
18
]
.
Based on the analogy with this theory, we can ask the following questions on abstract spin chains.

(1)

When is
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
a fusion category?

(2)

When is
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
modular?

(3)

When is
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
Witt trivial, or in other words, when is
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
a Drinfeld center of a fusion category?

We provide natural sufficient conditions for all of these to hold.

In fact, the main examples of abstract spin chains are
fusion spin chains
, which can be constructed from algebraic data arising from unitary fusion categories.
In these cases, the category of DHR bimodules are manifestly the Drinfeld center of the input fusion category, and such close coupling with general unitary fusion categories is one nice feature of abstract spin systems which makes them more accessible than the usual algebraic quantum field theories.
In view of our structural results, we can conclude that the fusion spin chains are (in some sense) universal examples of abstract spin chains up to categorical equivalence.

Let us turn to the main ideas behind our analysis.
We look at the inclusion of C
∗
-algebras
B_{0}\subset A
, where
B_{0}
is the subalgebra of observables supported on the half lines
(-\infty,-1]
and
[0,\infty)
, and
A
is the algebra of
quasi-local observables
.
The theory of subfactors suggest that we should look at the
basic construction

A\subset B_{1}
associated with this inclusion, where
B_{1}
is the algebra generated by
A
and the Jones projection
e
, that satisfies
eae=E(a)e
for
a\in A
, where
E
denotes a conditional expectation from
A
to the subalgebra
B_{0}
.

A tensor categorical analogue of this construction is
A_{\mathcal{C}}=\bigoplus_{i}U_{i}\otimes U_{i}^{*}
, where
(U_{i})_{i}
is a choice of representatives of the irreducible classes in some tensor category
\mathcal{C}
.
This has a structure of algebra object in the Drinfeld center of (the ind-completion of)
\mathcal{C}
, and plays an important role in the study of tensor categories.
Under this analogy, the projection to the trivial summand
U_{0}\otimes U_{0}^{*}
for the monoidal unit

corresponds to the dual conditional expectation
\hat{E}\colon B_{1}\to A
characterized by

, where

.
This suggests that the finiteness of

being a fusion category, which we substantiate in Corollary

.

Another key ingredient in our proof of modularity is a system of local operators called
charge transporters
.
This is an analogue of unitary intertwiners that shows up in the theory of algebraic quantum field theory to relate the endomorphism realizations of the same superselection sector, and is a crucial ingredient in Müger’s analysis of such theories
[
22
]
.
In the context of holographic topological order these have been called

[

,
3
]
, and have played a crucial role in the physicists’ approach to accessing the information that is contained in the DHR category (which in the case of topological holography is the bulk topological order).

For a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
and its Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
, the triviality of monodromy for an object

and the distinguished object

implies that the underlying

is a direct sum of copies of

, which can be taken as a first step for the proof of modularity for

The above analogy between

to understand the modularity question for

.
Indeed, the triviality for monodromy with

implies that the charge transporters for

, with respect to excitations in the positive and negative regions, belong to the subalgebra

.
Expanding on this correspondence, we show that
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
is modular when

and the charge transporters for the DHR bimodules generate the observable algebra

(Theorem

).

We then look at the question of Witt triviality.
The construction of fusion spin chains suggests that
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
should be the Drinfeld center of the bimodule category
\mathcal{C}_{-}
for the subalgebra
A_{-}\subset A
, which is generated by the observables on the negative half line
(-\infty,-1]
.
The structure of braiding on
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
leads to a natural tensor functor
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})\to\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{-})
.
Now, looking at the correspondence between the central vectors into
A

itself as a bimodule, together with a consideration on charge transporters, show that this functor is in fact full.
This result could be viewed as the main result of the paper, so we explicitly state a version of it here.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A_{\bullet}
be an abstract spin chain with charge transporter generation (see Condition

). Then

(1)

If
A_{\bullet}
is rational (Condition

), then
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
is a fusion category.

(2)

If in addition

), then
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
is unitary modular tensor category.

(3)

If

), then
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
is braided equivalent to

, hence is Witt trivial.

We note that this last result
contrasts
with the case of conformal nets, where the DHR category of superselection sectors is in general not Witt trivial, for example in the case of conformal nets built from loop groups
[
27
,
8
]
.
However, even in the conformal net case, we still expect DHR categories to be Drinfeld centers of
some
W
∗
-tensor category, which will generically fail to be fusion
[
9
]
.
Indeed our category

is very similar in spirit to the W
∗
-tensor category used in the above reference.

In addition, our result gives a holographic confirmation of expectations concerning the non-chirality of local topological order.
Indeed, local topological order essentially captures the class of topologically ordered Hamiltonians that have a commuting projector representation, e.g., toric code, quantum double models, and the Levin–Wen models.
It is largely expected to be the case that the resulting modular tensor category for this class of topologically ordered spin systems has no chiral anomaly, or in other words, is a Drinfeld center of some fusion category.

2.
Abstract spin chains

In this section, we briefly review definitions and notations which we will make use of in the main body of work.

Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the set of discrete intervals in
\mathbb{Z}
, partially ordered with inclusion.
This is contained in the poset
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})
of all subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
with inclusion.
For
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we will denote its complement by
F^{c}
.
Furthermore, for any subset
F
, we will denote its
R
neighborhood by
F^{+R}
.

Recall that any poset can naturally be viewed as a category, with a unique morphism
I\rightarrow J
precisely when
I\leq J
.
Let
\mathrm{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{alg}_{\mathrm{fd}}
denote the category of finite-dimensional C
∗
-algebras whose morphisms are unital inclusions.
Then we have the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

An
abstract spin chain
is a functor
A_{\bullet}\colon\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathrm{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{alg}_{\mathrm{fd}}
such that for any intervals
I\cap J=\varnothing
,
[A_{I},A_{J}]=0
in
A_{K}
for
I\cup J\subset K
.

Associated to an abstract spin chain is the
quasi-local
C
∗
-algebra
A=\lim_{\mathcal{I}}A_{I}
.
This is a unital AF C
∗
-algebra.
Since we have assumed our connecting maps are inclusions, we can identify each
A_{I}
as a subalgebra of
A
, with
I\subseteq J
implying
A_{I}\subseteq A_{J}
as a C
∗
-subalgebra.

For any subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we can define the subalgebra
A_{F}\subseteq A
by

A_{F}=\mathrm{C}^{*}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{alg}\set{x\in A_{G}}{G\subseteq F}.

We now introduce some useful properties abstract spin systems can have, which we view as “regularity” conditions.
Similar to the situation for conformal nets, there are many regularity conditions.
In the theorems below we will use combinations of these properties as hypotheses, so it will be convenient to give them names.
Many are extensions of familiar properties from the theory of conformal nets
[
7
]
.

Condition 2.2 (Weak algebraic Haag duality).

There are
K,R\geq 0
such that for every interval
I
of length at least
K
,
A^{\prime}_{I^{c}}\cap A\subset A_{I^{+R}}
.
If we can choose
R=0
, we say
A
satisfies
algebraic Haag duality
.

Condition 2.3 (Covering property).

There is an
L>0
such that if
I,J
are intervals whose intersection contains an interval of length
L
, then
A_{I\cup J}=A_{I}\vee A_{J}
.

Condition 2.4 (Strong simplicity).

The quasi-local algebra
A
is simple with unique trace.

Condition 2.5.

The centers of the half-line algebras
A_{(-\infty,a]}
and
A_{[a,\infty)}
are
1
-dimensional for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.1.
DHR bimodules

If
A
is a C
∗
-algebra, recall that a correspondence over
A
is a right Hilbert
A
module
X
together with a (non-degenerate) homomorphism from
A
to the adjointable operators on
X
(see
[
4
]
for a detailed exposition).
We usually think of a correspondence as an (algebraic)
A
-
A
bimodule
X
equipped with a right
A
-valued inner product
\braket{\xi|\eta}
for
\xi,\eta\in X
, which is conjugate linear in first variable and right
A
-linear in the second.

The collection of all correspondences over
A
forms a C
∗
-tensor category we call
\operatorname{Corr}(A)
, whose objects are correspondences over
A
and morphisms are adjointable bimodule intertwiners.
The monoidal product is the relative tensor product, denoted

X\boxtimes_{A}Y.

A
projective basis
of a correspondence is a finite sequence
(\xi_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\subseteq X
such that

(2.1)

x=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\braket{\xi_{i}|x}

holds for all
x\in X
.
Note that the existence of a projective basis only depends on
X
as a right Hilbert
A
module, and not on the structure of the left action.
It is not hard to show that the existence of a projective basis is equivalent to
X
being finitely generated projective as an (algebraic) right
A
-module.

We can use projective bases to make the connection with algebraic quantum field theory (AQFT), generalizing the Doplicher–Haag–Roberts (DHR) theory approach to superselection sectors.
In AQFT, the DHR C
∗
-tensor category can be defined in terms of localized, transportable endomorphisms of the quasi-local algebra.
An endomorphism
\rho
is localized in a small region
F
if
\rho(a)=a
for all
a
localized in the complement of
F
.
Transportability is defined by the property that for any small region
F
,
\rho
is unitarily conjugate to an endomorphism localized there.

To translate this into correspondences, we note that an endomorphism
\rho\colon A\rightarrow A
yields a correspondence
{}_{\rho}A
, which is
A
as a right Hilbert
A
module, with left action given by
\rho
.
However, this abstract bimodule does not recover the endomorphism itself, and thus we cannot a-priori make sense of localized, transportable bimodules.
However, if we choose the 1-element projective basis
1\in{}_{\rho}A
, then we recover
\rho
as the matrix coefficient of the Hilbert module
\rho(a)=\braket{1|a\cdot 1}
.
Furthermore, if we pick any unitary
u\in{}_{\rho}A
, we see that that
\mathrm{Ad}(u)\circ\rho=\braket{u|a\cdot u}
.
Thus the correspondence picture allows us to collect all unitary conjugacy classes of an endomorphism into one object, and recover them by picking (unitary) projective bases.

The idea behind DHR bimodules is to focus on this later point, but keep it a bit more general by not requiring endomorphisms (encoded by the special type of projective basis), but more generally
amplimorphisms
encoded by arbitrary projective bases.
An amplimorphism is a (not necessarily unital) homomorphism
\pi\colon A\rightarrow M_{n}(A)
.
This is required to capture the full level of generality of super-selection theory, since in the setting of abstract spin chains, the local algebras are finite dimensional, and in particular are not purely infinite.
The idea to apply this to abstract spin chains has its origins in the earlier work of
[
26
]
, but was more developed recently in
[
16
]
.

We now give a formal definition of DHR bimodules, following
[
16
]
.
We denote the category of
dualizable
C
∗
-correspondences over
A
by
\operatorname{Corr}^{d}(A)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A_{\bullet}
be an abstract spin chain and
X
a dualizable correspondence over the quasi-local algebra
A
.
Given a subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we say a vector
\xi\in X
is
localized in

F
if we have

a\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}a\quad\text{for all }a\in A_{F^{c}}.

The bimodule
X
is a called a
DHR bimodule
if there exists an
r\geq 0
such that for any interval
I
of length at least
r
, there is a projective basis
(\xi_{i})_{i}
for
X
localized in
I
.
The full C
∗
-tensor subcategory of
\operatorname{Corr}^{d}(A)
consisting of DHR bimodules is denoted
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
.

Under Condition
2.2
, the tensor category
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
is braided
[
16
]
*Theorem 3.13.
The braiding can be explicitly described as follows.
For two DHR bimodules

and
Y
, pick projective bases
\{\xi_{i}\}\subseteq X
and
\{\eta_{j}\}\subseteq Y
localized in
I
and
J
respectively, where

and

are sufficiently large intervals (whose precise size depends on the structure constants for
X
and
Y

(again the precise requirements depend on the constants from weak algebraic Haag duality).
Then
\{\xi_{i}\boxtimes_{A}\eta_{j}\}
forms a projective basis for

, and the braiding is given by

\beta_{X,Y}(\xi_{i}\boxtimes_{A}\nu_{j})=\nu_{j}\boxtimes_{A}\xi_{i}\in Y\boxtimes_{A}X.

This indeed gives a well-defined intertwiner of correspondences, does not depend on the choice of bases, and satisfies the hexagon equation that characterizes a braiding on a monoidal category.

3.
Distinguished algebra objects

3.1.
Lagrangian algebra in the Drinfeld center

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a unitary fusion category, and let us denote the set of its irreducible classes by
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
.
Given
i\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
, we denote its representative by
U_{i}
.
We also write
0
for the class of monoidal unit, so that we have
U_{0}=1_{\mathcal{C}}
.

We denote the (unitary)
Drinfeld center
of
\mathcal{C}
by
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
(for example, see
[
23
]
).
Its objects are pairs
(Z,c)
, consisting of an object
Z\in\mathcal{C}
together with a half braiding, that is, a natural unitary isomorphism

c_{X}\colon Z\otimes X\to X\otimes Z\qquad(X\in\mathcal{C}).

that fulfills the hexagon axiom.
Morphisms in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
are morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
between the underlying objects that commute with the respective half braidings.
The monoidal product of
\mathcal{C}
induces a monoidal structure on
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
where the half braiding of a product
Z\otimes Z^{\prime}
is given by the composition of the respective half braidings.
Moreover,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is naturally unitarily braided with braiding

(3.1)

\beta_{(Z,c),(Z^{\prime},c^{\prime})}=c_{Z^{\prime}}\colon Z\otimes Z^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}\otimes Z.

for objects
(Z,c)
and
(Z^{\prime},c^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.

Recall that there is a distinguished object
(Z^{\mathrm{reg}},c^{\mathrm{reg}})
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
, given by

(3.2)

Z^{\mathrm{reg}}=\bigoplus_{i\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}U_{i}^{*}\otimes U_{i}.

Let us recall the unitary half-braiding
c^{\mathrm{reg}}
, cf.
[
25
]
*Section 3.2.
Given
X\in\mathcal{C}
and indexes

and

6.2.

Let us come back to the net

Proof.

Conisder the conditional expectations

6.3.
Charge-transporter generation

Now, let us further assume that each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a unitary fusion category, so that it has only finitely many irreducible classes.
Under this assumption, we can consider the following condition.

Definition 6.5

Let

-cells in

]
*Section III.4.

We now produce a braided tensor functor

Given such an object

for

For an interval

Lemma 6.6
.

For any

Proof.

The proof follows mutatis mutandis from the argument in

*Lemma 4.15.
We spell out some details next.

Let

be an interval of diameter greater or equal to

For

In particular,

For each

\operatorname{supp}X_{[-k,-m-1]}
such that

has nontrivial morphism to

.
Then, if

\operatorname{End}(X_{[-k,-m-1]})
corresponding to

, the projection

acts nontrivially on
\operatorname{Hom}(X_{[-k,-1]},X_{[-k,-1]}\otimes Y)
.

Since
(\xi_{i})_{i}
form a projective basis of
\operatorname{Hom}(X_{[-k,-1]},X_{[-k,-1]}\otimes Y)
over
\operatorname{End}(X_{[-k,-1]})
, the vectors of the form

for
1\leq i\leq d
and
a\in\operatorname{End}(X_{[-k,-1]})
are total in
\operatorname{Hom}(X_{[-k,-1]},X_{[-k,-1]}\otimes Y)
, and
w\otimes p
acts nontrivially on the span of
\xi_{i}a\xi_{j}^{*}
for

.
However, applying the partial trace we reduce this to the action of

on the span of
\xi_{i}a\xi_{j}^{*}
for

, which should be trivial.
The faithfulness of partial trace contradicts this.
∎

The above criterion can pose a strong control on the DHR bimodules when the fusion rules of
\mathcal{C}
are ‘torsion free’ in a suitable sense.

For example, suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a rigid C
∗
-tensor category with the fusion rules of a simple compact Lie group
G
.
Then the only possibility for the above
Y
is a multiple of the trivial object.
Thus, as a left module, any object

is a free
A
-module.
In view of Remark

, as a monoidal category
\mathrm{DHR}(A_{\bullet})
is equivalent to the representation category of the commutative group

.
In this case the charge transporters from positive region to negative region belong to the subalgebra

Secukinumab and Adalimumab on Bone Health in Psoriasis: A Longitudinal Cohort Study
Authors: Chongli Yu, Zhenkai Zhao, Dengfa Gao, Jianfeng Fan, Yuxin Qiu, Shuya Zhang, Nan Wu, Chengxin Li, Houchen Lyu, Rui Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPsoriasis is associated with skeletal comorbidities, but longitudinal data on bone outcomes in patients receiving biologics are limited.ObjectiveTo compare bone mineral density (BMD) and bone turnover marker trajectories among patients with psoriasis initiating secukinumab or adalimumab.MethodsIn this retrospective cohort study, 364 patients with psoriasis who initiated secukinumab or adalimumab between 2021 and 2024 were screened. Of these, 50 patients had a valid baseline dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) assessment and at least one post-baseline DXA assessment, contributing 128 DXA assessments in total. In addition, 91 patients had a valid baseline bone-turnover-marker measurement and at least one post-baseline measurement, contributing 265 biomarker observations. Longitudinal trajectories were compared using covariate-adjusted, overlap-weighted linear mixed-effects models including treatment, time, and treatment-by-time interaction terms.ResultsSecukinumab was associated with more favorable lumbar spine (β = 0.039; 95% CI, 0.019–0.059; P<0.001) and femoral neck BMD trajectories (β = 0.022; 95% CI, 0.005–0.039; P=0.012). Secukinumab was also associated with increased osteocalcin and reduced CTX trajectories, whereas P1NP did not differ significantly.LimitationsLimitations include the retrospective single-center design, modest sample size, treatment imbalance, surrogate outcomes, and potential residual confounding.ConclusionCompared with adalimumab, secukinumab was associated with more favorable longitudinal BMD trajectories and a bone turnover profile consistent with increased formation and reduced resorption in patients with psoriasis.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated inflammatory disease increasingly recognized as a systemic disorder with multiple comorbidities.1–3 Epidemiologic studies suggest that patients with psoriasis may have increased risks of low bone mineral density (BMD) and osteoporotic fracture,4,5 but longitudinal data on skeletal outcomes remain limited.

The pathophysiological link between psoriasis and bone loss involves chronic immune dysregulation, in particular the T helper 17 (Th17) cell axis.6 Activated Th17 cells secrete interleukin-17A (IL-17A), which enhances osteoclastogenesis both directly and indirectly through the induction of receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL), thereby tipping the balance toward bone resorption.7,8 These mechanistic data prompted the hypothesis that IL-17A-targeted interventions could attenuate excessive bone turnover and help preserve bone mass in patients with psoriasis.

Biologic therapies have transformed the management of psoriasis. Tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) inhibitors, such as adalimumab, and IL-17A inhibitors, such as secukinumab, are both widely used and have established efficacy in cutaneous and joint outcomes.9 However, their skeletal effects may diverge: data from rheumatologic cohorts suggest modest increases in BMD with TNF-α blockade, whereas psoriasis-specific evidence for the effect of IL-17A inhibition on BMD or the fracture risk remains limited.10,11 Moreover, most psoriasis-related studies addressing bone health are underpowered, cross-sectional, or restricted to isolated turnover markers, and critically, longitudinal comparative real-world data directly evaluating IL-17A and TNF-α inhibitors in relation to skeletal surrogate outcomes remain limited.4,12,13 Because BMD reflects cumulative skeletal change, whereas bone turnover markers capture more dynamic remodeling activity, integrated longitudinal assessment of both outcomes may help characterize skeletal trajectories in biologic-treated patients.14–16

We aimed to evaluate whether initiation of secukinumab versus adalimumab was associated with differential longitudinal trajectories of BMD and bone turnover markers in patients with psoriasis.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Population

This retrospective longitudinal cohort study included patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis who initiated secukinumab or adalimumab at the Department of Dermatology, First Medical Center of Chinese PLA General Hospital, between January 2021 and November 2024.

Eligible patients were adults aged ≥18 years with dermatologist-confirmed psoriasis who initiated secukinumab or adalimumab and had complete baseline covariates together with at least two valid skeletal assessments,17 including baseline and at least one post-baseline dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) or bone turnover marker measurement, available for longitudinal analysis. Exclusion criteria included major hepatic, renal, endocrine, skeletal, malignant, or pregnancy-related conditions; ongoing anti-osteoporosis therapy; systemic biologic, glucocorticoid, or vitamin D analog exposure within 3 months before baseline; and initiation of bone-active medications during follow-up.

Patients were not prospectively assigned to the BMD or biomarker cohorts. Instead, the two analytic samples were defined by the availability of repeated assessments obtained during routine clinical care. Patients with a valid baseline DXA assessment and at least one post-baseline DXA assessment were included in the BMD analytic sample. Patients with a valid baseline fasting morning bone-turnover-marker measurement and at least one post-baseline measurement were included in the biomarker analytic sample. Because assessments were obtained according to routine clinical practice, clinical indications, and patient preference, the two analytic samples partially overlapped. The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07140666), and reported according to STROBE. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chinese PLA General Hospital (approval No. S2022-196-01). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before inclusion in the study. All patient information was handled confidentially, and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Exposure

Exposure was defined as initiation of secukinumab or adalimumab according to approved psoriasis dosing schedules.18 Treatment choice was made jointly by dermatologists and patients in routine practice, considering psoriasis phenotype, psoriatic arthritis, prior treatment history, contraindications, access or reimbursement, and patient preference. Baseline was the date of biologic initiation. Follow-up time was calculated in months from baseline and modeled continuously to accommodate routine-care assessments occurring approximately at 6, 12, 18, and 24 months, with variable timing across patients.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes were lumbar spine and femoral neck BMD measured by certified technicians using a HOLOGIC Discovery-Wi DXA system according to International Society for Clinical Densitometry guidance.19 Analyses were conducted using T-scores (standard deviations from the young-adult reference mean), which are the recommended metric for the diagnosis of osteopenia and osteoporosis in adults.20,21

Secondary outcomes were serum osteocalcin and procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (P1NP), reflecting bone formation, and β-C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen (β-CTX), reflecting bone resorption.22 When both assessments were available within the same clinical visit, blood samples were collected in the morning after an overnight fast (≥12 h) and within 12 h of DXA assessment. Serum osteocalcin, P1NP, and β-CTX were measured using automated electrochemiluminescence immunoassays on the same analytical platform, with results reported in ng/mL, μg/L, and ng/mL, respectively.

Data Collection and Variable Preprocessing

Baseline demographic and clinical variables included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, alcohol consumption, disease duration, Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI), and the presence of psoriatic arthritis. Laboratory covariates included interleukin-6 (IL-6), C-reactive protein (CRP), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), serum calcium (Ca), phosphorus (P), and creatinine (Cr) levels.

Complete-case analysis was used. After eligibility screening, all included patients had complete baseline covariates and complete recorded outcome values required for the corresponding primary models; therefore, no imputation was performed. Variation in visit number and timing reflected routine clinical follow-up rather than item-level missingness. Continuous covariates were standardized before modeling to improve numerical stability. Bone-turnover-marker outcomes were analyzed in their original recorded units.

Propensity Score Estimation and Weighting

Propensity scores for secukinumab versus adalimumab initiation were estimated using baseline demographic, clinical, inflammatory, and biochemical covariates. Overlap weights targeting the average treatment effect in the overlap population were applied and normalized to a mean of 1. Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences, with values <0.10 considered acceptable. Balance diagnostics were summarized using Love plots.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics are presented as means ± SDs or medians (interquartile ranges) for continuous variables and as counts (percentages) for categorical variables. Between-group comparisons were performed using Welch’s t test, the Mann–Whitney U-test, or χ2-test, as appropriate.

Weighted linear mixed-effects models with patient-level random intercepts were used to evaluate longitudinal changes in BMD and bone turnover markers. Fixed effects included time since biologic initiation, treatment group, and the treatment-by-time interaction. The treatment-by-time interaction was the primary parameter of interest. Models incorporated normalized overlap weights and were adjusted for baseline outcome values and prespecified demographic, clinical, inflammatory, and biochemical covariates.

Estimated marginal means were calculated at prespecified follow-up times of 0, 6, 12, 18, and 24 months. Between-group differences were estimated as secukinumab minus adalimumab and are reported with 95% confidence intervals and two-sided P values.

Sensitivity analyses examined alternative time specifications, random-slope models, generalized estimating equations, alternative propensity score specifications, complete-follow-up restrictions, and adjustment for follow-up intensity. For the fully adjusted biomarker sensitivity analyses, P values for the three treatment-by-time interaction tests were adjusted using the Holm method. All analyses were performed using R software, version 4.4.0. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Longitudinal Changes in Bone Mineral Density (BMD)

Longitudinal changes in lumbar spine and femoral neck BMD T-scores over 24 months were evaluated using ATO-weighted linear mixed-effects models including fixed effects for the treatment group, time (months), and their interaction, with patient-level random intercepts and adjustment for the baseline BMD and prespecified covariates.

At the lumbar spine, BMD trajectories differed significantly between treatment groups. In the adalimumab group, lumbar spine T-scores decreased modestly over time (β = −0.014 per month, 95% CI −0.026 to −0.002; p = 0.03). In contrast, secukinumab was associated with a more favorable longitudinal trajectory, reflected by a positive treatment-by-time interaction (β = 0.039, 95% CI 0.019–0.059; p < 0.001) (Table 2 and Supplementary Table S1). Model-estimated trajectories showed early separation between groups, with between-group differences emerging at 6 months and persisting through 24 months. At 24 months, the estimated lumbar spine T-score change was approximately +0.60 in the secukinumab group and −0.33 in the adalimumab group. Between-group differences emerged as early as 6 months (p = 0.003) and continued to widen through 12–24 months, with all comparisons reaching statistical significance (all p < 0.001). A similar, although less pronounced, pattern was observed at the femoral neck. No residual between-group difference was observed at baseline (β = 0.019, 95% CI −0.152 to 0.190; p = 0.82). During follow-up, the time effect in the adalimumab group was not significant (β_time = −0.004 per month, 95% CI −0.014 to 0.007; p = 0.50), but the treatment-by-time interaction favored secukinumab (β_time×group = 0.022, 95% CI 0.005–0.039; p = 0.012) (Table 2 and Supplementary Table S2). Model-estimated femoral neck trajectories gradually diverged over follow-up, with higher adjusted T-score estimates in the secukinumab group from 6 months onward (Figure 2C and D). Between-group differences were detectable beginning at 6 months and persisted through 24 months. The overall standardized treatment effect over 0–24 months was small to moderate (marginal Cohen’s d ≈ 0.34; conditional d ≈ 0.38).

Sensitivity analyses supported the direction of the primary findings. The treatment-by-time estimates remained statistically significant or directionally consistent across alternative time specifications, random-slope models, generalized estimating equations, propensity score matching, alternative weighting approaches, and analyses excluding patients with very low baseline BMD (Supplementary Tables S3 and S4, Supplementary Figures S2 and S3). To facilitate interpretation of the T-score findings, median PASI decreased from 9.4 to 0.0 in the adalimumab group and from 10.5 to 0.2 in the secukinumab group by the last available assessment. Median changes calculated from absolute areal BMD were −1.93% with adalimumab and +1.27% with secukinumab at the lumbar spine, and −3.08% and +0.90%, respectively, at the femoral neck. Complete descriptive results are provided in Supplementary Table S5, and patient-level absolute T-scores are shown in Supplementary Figure S4. Additional BMI and topical corticosteroid analyses are provided in Supplementary Tables S6 and S7.

In contrast, P1NP trajectories did not differ significantly between groups (β_time×group = 0.15, 95% CI −0.40 to 0.70; p = 0.59), with no consistent between-group separation over follow-up (Table 3, Figure 3C and D). For serum levels of CTX, a marker of bone resorption, secukinumab was associated with a lower longitudinal trajectory than adalimumab. The treatment-by-time interaction was negative and statistically significant (β_time×group = −0.009, 95% CI −0.013 to −0.005; p < 0.001), indicating a greater reduction in CTX over time among secukinumab-treated patients (Table 3, Figure 3E and F).

Discussion

In this retrospective real-world cohort of patients with psoriasis receiving biologic therapy, secukinumab initiation was associated with more favorable 24-month trajectories of lumbar spine and femoral neck BMD than adalimumab. These densitometric findings were accompanied by distinct bone turnover marker patterns, including lower CTX trajectories and higher osteocalcin trajectories in the secukinumab group, whereas P1NP did not differ significantly between treatment groups. Taken together, these findings suggest that IL-17A and TNF-α inhibition may be associated with different skeletal surrogate endpoint trajectories in patients with psoriasis. However, because of the observational design, modest sample size, unequal treatment-group sizes and follow-up intensity, and absence of fracture outcomes, the results should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of skeletal protection.

The present study adds longitudinal clinical data to an area in which psoriasis-specific evidence remains limited. Psoriasis is increasingly recognized as a systemic inflammatory disease associated with comorbidities beyond the skin, including musculoskeletal and potentially skeletal disorders. Although epidemiologic studies have linked psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis to lower BMD or increased fracture risk, few studies have evaluated repeated BMD and bone turnover marker trajectories during biologic treatment. By integrating serial DXA assessments with biochemical markers of bone turnover, our findings suggest that skeletal health may be an underrecognized component of long-term comorbidity assessment in selected patients with psoriasis.

Previous studies of patients with rheumatoid arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis have suggested that TNF-α inhibitors primarily mitigate systemic inflammation and may stabilize bone loss, but exert limited effects on bone formation.23–25 In contrast, IL-17A blockade has been shown to attenuate osteoclast-mediated bone resorption and partially restore osteoblast activity in preclinical models and selected clinical settings, thereby exerting more direct effects on inflammatory bone remodeling.26–28

From a mechanistic perspective, the IL-17/Th17 axis is biologically relevant to inflammatory bone remodeling, particularly through its involvement in osteoclastogenesis and inflammatory bone resorption.29–31 The inhibition of IL-17A may therefore influence skeletal outcomes through mechanisms that extend beyond the global suppression of systemic inflammation. In contrast, TNF-α blockade is generally thought to affect bone predominantly by reducing the inflammatory burden, with less direct regulation of osteoblast–osteoclast coupling.32

The bone turnover marker profile observed in the present study is biologically coherent. Treatment with secukinumab was accompanied by a greater reduction in CTX levels, indicating the suppression of bone resorption, together with an increase in osteocalcin levels.33,34 In contrast, no significant between-group difference was observed for P1NP levels. This dissociation between bone formation markers is biologically plausible, as osteocalcin primarily reflects late-stage osteoblast activity and mineralization, whereas P1NP captures earlier phases of collagen synthesis;35 these markers do not invariably change in parallel, particularly in settings characterized by reduced resorption and improved remodeling coupling efficiency.

This study has limitations. Its retrospective single-center design and nonrandom treatment allocation limit causal inference. Although overlap weighting improved measured covariate balance, residual confounding from unmeasured or incompletely captured factors cannot be excluded, including calcium intake, physical activity, menopausal status, concomitant medications affecting bone metabolism, treatment adherence, and longitudinal changes in PASI or inflammatory markers. The sample size was modest and treatment groups were imbalanced, particularly in the BMD analytic sample. The adalimumab group was smaller and contributed fewer repeated assessments, resulting in lower precision of the corresponding longitudinal estimates and greater uncertainty around treatment-specific trajectories. In addition, the number and timing of follow-up assessments varied across patients as part of routine clinical care. Although linear mixed-effects models accommodate unbalanced repeated measurements, informative follow-up cannot be excluded if the likelihood or timing of repeated DXA or biomarker assessments was related to clinical status, treatment response, or perceived skeletal risk. Finally, fracture outcomes were unavailable, and DXA and bone turnover markers remain surrogate skeletal endpoints. Accordingly, these findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating and require confirmation in larger prospective cohorts with prespecified, standardized assessment schedules. The consistency of the direction of findings across skeletal sites and sensitivity analyses provides some reassurance regarding robustness but does not eliminate the possibility of residual confounding, selection related to follow-up, or measurement variability.

Conclusion

In this longitudinal cohort of patients with psoriasis, secukinumab was associated with more favorable 24-month trajectories of lumbar spine and femoral neck BMDs than adalimumab. These structural differences were accompanied by treatment-specific changes in bone turnover markers, characterized by greater suppression of bone resorption and a modest increase in bone formation with IL-17A blockade. These exploratory findings warrant prospective multicenter validation with standardized skeletal assessments, comprehensive covariate capture, and fracture-related outcomes.

Greenness and whiteness profiles of a validated HPTLC method for the simultaneous analysis of clopidogrel and rosuvastatin in fixed-dose combination capsules
Authors: Prawez Alam, Faiyaz Shakeel, Mohammed H Alqarni, Ahmed I Foudah, Tariq M Aljarba, Mohd Imran, Mohammad Ali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe goal of the current work was to create and validate a quick, sensitive, and environmentally friendly reverse-phase “high-performance thin-layer chromatography (HPTLC)” approach for the simultaneous measurement of rosuvastatin (ROS) and clopidogrel (CLOP) in their fixed-dose combination (FDC) capsules. Eight different greenness and whiteness tools, such as the analytical eco-scale (AES), chloroform toxicity (ChlorTox), the analytical GREEnness (AGREE), the modified green analytical procedure index (MoGAPI), the complex MoGAPI, the blue applicability grade index (BAGI), the carbon footprint reduction index (CaFRI), and the click analytical chemistry index (CACI) were utilized to evaluate the method's greenness and whiteness profiles. The suggested strategy was linear for both medications in the 25–1000 ng/band level. The method was proven to be reliable, sensitive, accurate, precise, and eco-friendly. The results of greenness and whiteness tools, such as AES (87), ChlorTox (0.86 g), AGREE (0.72), MoGAPI (85), complex MoGAPI (90), BAGI (77.5), CaFRI (89), and CACI (91) demonstrated that the current method had the unparalleled greenness and whiteness profiles. Using the proposed methodology, it was found that the assay of CLOP and ROS in FDC products A and B was ranged from 98.41 to 101.02%. These results confirm that the suggested approach is appropriate for concurrently analyzing ROS and CLOP. Introduction

Clopidogrel (CLOP) (Fig. 1A), an antiplatelet (blood-thinning) medication, reduces platelet aggregation by specifically blocking adenosine diphosphate (ADP) from binding to its platelet receptor and preventing ADP from subsequently triggering the glycoprotein GPIIb/IIIa complex [1, 2]. Research has demonstrated that it can avert myocardial infarction, vascular disorders, and ischemic stroke [3]. Rosuvastatin (ROS) (Fig. 1B), an inhibitor of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase, significantly reduces plasma cholesterol levels [2, 4]. ROS is a synthetic lipid-lowering drug that also treats osteoporosis, Alzheimer's disease, and prostatic hyperplasia [5]. ROS has recently been shown to have anticancer properties against Caco-2 cells [6]. To lower the risk of cardiovascular illnesses, combination therapy using several blood thinners and lipid lowering agents, such as atorvastatin, CLOP, ROS, or aspirin, has been suggested as a treatment option [2]. Different commercially available fixed-dose combination (FDC) tablets and capsules contain the ROS and CLOP. Consequently, in their FDC tablets/capsules, the simultaneous examination of CLOP and ROS is required.

Chemical structures of A clopidogrel (CLOP) and B rosuvastatin (ROS), derived from reference [2]

Literature survey revealed various analytical methods for determining CLOP and ROS simultaneously in their FDC products and biological samples. Various ultra-violet (UV) spectrometry, first order derivative UV spectrometry [7–9], and high-performance liquid chromatographic (HPLC) [8, 10–12] techniques have been used to determine CLOP and ROS simultaneously in their FDC tablets. HPLC methods have also been reported to determine CLOP, ROS, and aspirin simultaneously in ternary mixtures and their FDC tablets [13, 14]. Some green HPLC approaches have also been reported to determine CLOP, ROS, and aspirin or CLOP, ROS, aspirin, and atorvastatin simultaneously in their FDC tablets [2, 15]. Additionally, ternary combinations and FDC tablets have been reported to simultaneously detect aspirin, ROS, and CLOP by the use of certain high-performance thin-layer chromatography (HPTLC) techniques [15, 16]. Additionally, reports of the simultaneous analysis of CLOP, ROS, and aspirin in their ternary mixes and FDC tablets have been made using eco-friendly artificial neural network and chemometry techniques [17]. Furthermore, a pharmacokinetic evaluation and simultaneous measurement of aspirin, ROS, and CLOP in rat plasma samples have been described using an HPLC approach [18]. Using an ultra-fast liquid chromatography approach, the simultaneous analysis of ROS and CLOP in human plasma samples has also been documented [19].

HPTLC is an extension of TLC, which is simple, robust, speedy, and efficient in drug analysis [15]. Green analytical chemistry (GAC), has 12 principles, one of which is adopting environmentally appropriate solvent alternatives to reduce the harmful impacts of toxic/hazardous eluents on the environment [20]. A relatively new method for evaluating the success of analytical processes in terms of sample analysis, cost-effectiveness, and greenness strength is white analytical chemistry (WAC). The red, green, and blue hues are used to measure WAC; these hues combine to give the color white [21]. A review of the literature indicates that during the past few decades, there has been a significant growth in the usage of ecologically friendly solvents [22–24]. Pharmaceutical products are currently being greenly analyzed using HPTLC techniques [24–26]. The greenness and whiteness profiles of analytical methodologies have been determined using a variety of greenness and whiteness approaches [27–34]. We employed eight distinct greenness and whiteness strategies to evaluate the current method's greenness and whiteness profiles: the Analytical Eco-Scale (AES) [27], chloroform toxicity (ChlorTox) [28], the analytical GREEnness (AGREE) [29], the modified green analytical procedure index (MoGAPI) [30], the complex MoGAPI [31], the blue applicability grade index (BAGI) [32], the carbon footprint reduction index (CaFRI) [33], and the click analytical chemistry index (CACI) [34]. In the semi-quantitative AES technique, non-greenness features are penalized with points [27]. The ChlorTox technique is used to compare the solvents' ChlorTox scale to that of reference chloroform. The low value of the ChlorTox scale for green analytical methods suggests environmental safety, in contrast to the high value for non-green procedures [28]. The AGREE technique measured greenness by applying all 12 GAC principles [29]. In addition to providing the characteristic red, green, and yellow pictograms of the popular GAPI tool, the MoGAPI tool also provides an overall assessment of the method's greenness by calculating a total score [30]. A thorough assessment of technique eco-friendliness is provided by the complex MoGAPI, which enables more precise and impartial method comparisons [31]. The BAGI tool evaluates the whiteness profile of the analytical procedure using the ten distinct WAC principles [32]. With an emphasis on measuring greenhouse gas emissions, CaFRI is a recently developed web application intended to evaluate and improve the sustainability of analytical techniques [33]. CACI is a useful and easy-to-use tool that offers a software solution and metric for assessing the whitening profiles of analytical techniques. By enabling impartial and objective comparisons of approaches, the CACI system promotes the creation of simpler implementation strategies without compromising performance [34].

By developing and validating a reverse-phase HPTLC technology that is rapid, sensitive, and eco-friendly, the current approach aims to assess ROS and CLOP in their FDC capsules concurrently. It is predicated on the previously provided data and findings. The guidelines in "The International Council for Harmonization (ICH)-Q2-R2" [35] were utilized to verify the proposed technique for simultaneously estimating CLOP and ROS.

Materials and methods

Materials

The bulk drug samples of CLOP (purity: 98.6%) and ROS (purity: 99.2%) and the ammonia (NH3) solution (25% for HPLC) were purchased from “Sigma Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA)”. The chromatography-grade ethanol (EtOH) was provided by “E-Merck (Darmstadt, Germany)”. The chromatography-grade/deionized water (H2O) was obtained by “Milli-Q (Lyon, France)” device. 20 mg of ROS and 75 mg of CLOP were included in each brand of marketed FDC capsule, A (Rosave® CV) and B (Novastat® CV), which were bought from a pharmacy in New Delhi, India. All of the additional solvents and chemicals used were of AR grade.

Chromatography and equipment

The “HPTLC system (CAMAG, Muttenz, Switzerland)” was utilized to simultaneously analyze CLOP and ROS in the bulk drugs and marketed FDC capsules. The samples that were produced were applied using an "Automatic TLC Sampler 4 (ATS4) Sample Applicator (CAMAG, Geneva, Switzerland)" in the shape of 6 mm bands. A sample applicator was connected to the "microlitre Syringe (Hamilton, Bonaduz, Switzerland)". The stationary phase employed was “glass plates (plate size: 10 × 20 cm2) pre-coated with reverse-phase silica gel (particle size: 5 µm) 60F254S plates (E-Merck, Darmstadt, Germany)”. The plates were prepared by the company. As the environmentally friendly development system, EtOH-H2O-NH3 (70:25:5 v/v/v) was used in ternary form. To quantify ROS and CLOP at the same time, 150 nL/s of application rate was set. The "automated developing chamber 2 (ADC2) (CAMAG, Muttenz, Switzerland)" was used to develop the plates in a linear ascending mode with an 8 cm spacing. The development chamber was filled with vapors from a development system and left there at room temperature (22 °C) for half an hour. CLOP and ROS were simultaneously detected at 240 nm. The settings that were employed were slit dimension (4 × 0.45 mm2) and scanner speed (20 mm/s). Three or six replications were employed for each metric. The software used was "WinCAT's (version 1.4.3.6336, CAMAG, Muttenz, Switzerland)".

CLOP and ROS calibration curves and quality control (QC) solutions

The necessary quantities of each medication were dissolved in an adequate volume of the green development system, specifically EtOH-H2O-NH3 (70:25:5 v/v/v), in order to form separate stock solutions for ROS and CLOP. The final stock solution for each drug included 100 µg/mL.

The development system was used to dilute the stock solutions in different ratios, resulting in the 25–1000 ng/band ranges of both medicines. In this experiment, the development system was used as a diluent to ensure uniformity in the HPTLC analysis and sample preparation. Following the recommended methodology, 10 µL of each concentration of ROS and CLOP were added to TLC plates, and the peak area of each concentration was then assessed. Plotting the concentrations of the two medications against the measured peak area in six replications (n = 6) resulted in CLOP and ROS calibration plots. To evaluate different validation requirements, three unique QC solutions were made.

Sample preparation for concurrent measurement of CLOP and ROS in commercial FDC capsules

The suggested method was used to simultaneously measure CLOP and ROS in FDC capsule dosage forms in addition to the simultaneous analysis of CLOP and ROS in their pure drug samples. Twenty capsules from each brand were taken in order to simultaneously measure the CLOP and ROS in FDC capsule products A and B. Next, the mean weight of every FDC capsule was calculated. To create the fine powder, the contents of each brand of FDC capsule were removed and carefully mixed. An equal amount of 20 mg of ROS and 75 mg of CLOP were present in a fine powder quantity that was combined with 100 mL of the developing system. After that, the developing system was used to further dilute 10 mL of this stock, yielding 100 mL. After ten minutes of sonication, the solutions made for each brand of FDC capsule were filtered to exclude any insoluble excipients. The gathered samples were simultaneously examined for ROS and CLOP in both products using the proposed technique.

Validation assessment

Using the ICH-Q2-R2 standards, the suggested approach for simultaneously evaluating CLOP and ROS was validated for numerous parameters [35]. The concentrations were plotted against the observed peak area to determine the linear ranges for ROS and CLOP. The linearity of CLOP and ROS for the proposed technique was evaluated for a range of 25 to 1000 ng/band (n = 6).

The results of computing the "retardation factor (Rf), peak tailing factor (As), and theoretical plates/meter (N/m)" provided the system acceptability factors for the recommended method of concurrently determining CLOP and ROS. Utilizing their published equations, the "Rf, As, and N/m" for the suggested approach were determined [25].

The accuracy of the suggested technique for measuring CLOP and ROS concurrently was assessed using the spiking/standard addition technique as percentage recoveries [35]. 300 ng/band of the pre-analyzed CLOP and ROS solution was spiked with additional 50, 100, and 150% CLOP and ROS solutions in order to generate CLOP and ROS low-QC (LQC) samples of 450 ng/band, middle-QC (MQC) samples of 600 ng/band, and high-QC (HQC) samples of 750 ng/band. To evaluate the accuracy, the previously mentioned ROS and CLOP QC solutions were reevaluated. Every ROS and CLOP level's % recovery was computed. There were six replicates (n = 6) utilized to gauge the accuracy.

The current protocol for the simultaneous detection of ROS and CLOP was evaluated in terms of its intra- and inter-assay precision. It was possible to assess the intra-assay precision for both compounds on the same day (n = 6) by quantifying freshly made CLOP and ROS solutions at the QC levels previously indicated. As part of the examination of inter-assay variation for CLOP and ROS for the suggested technique, evaluation of newly produced solutions was carried out at the previously indicated QC levels over the course of three days (n = 6).

By carefully adjusting the percentage of the green development system, the robustness of the suggested methodology was established for both CLOP and ROS. Following the transfer from EtOH-H2O-NH3 (72:23:5 v/v/v) to EtOH-H2O-NH3 (68:27:5 v/v/v) in the optimized solvent system for CLOP and ROS, the quantitative parameter, peak area and the separation parameter, Rf were evaluated (n = 6).

The standard deviation methodology was employed to measure the sensitivity of the recommended strategy for the simultaneous evaluation of CLOP and ROS. Specifically, the “limits of detection (LOD) and quantification (LOQ)” were determined. The standard deviations of the six replicates (n = 6) of the blank solutions, which did not contain ROS or CLOP, were recorded after each injection. Concurrent measurement of CLOP and ROS in commercial FDC capsules utilizing the current methodology

In this approach, samples produced by the commercial FDC capsule were used, and they were placed on TLC plates under the same experimental conditions as the simultaneous determination of pure CLOP and ROS (n = 3). Results and discussion

Development of green HPTLC method

For the purpose of simultaneously analyzing CLOP and ROS, an adequate analytical method had to be developed, which required looking at both binary and ternary combinations of developing systems. The eluents being studied, EtOH and H2O, are categorized as green and sustainable solvents because they don't harm the environment [36, 37]. Among binary combinations of EtOH and H2O, the fraction of EtOH in the 50–90% (v/v) range was examined to be the green developing system. EtOH-H2O-NH3 (50:45:5 v/v/v), EtOH-H2O-NH3 (60:35:5 v/v/v), EtOH-H2O-NH3 (70:25:5 v/v/v), and EtOH-H2O-NH3 (80:15:5 v/v) were among the ternary forms that were explored as the green solvent systems. The representative TLC image for standards and FDC formulation is depicted in supplementary Fig. 1 (Fig. S1).

Every binary combination involving EtOH and H2O contained ROS and CLOP chromatographic peaks that are unacceptable with regard to CLOP (As>1.35) and ROS (As>1.45). Furthermore, weak chromatographic signals of CLOP and ROS were also shown by the ternary combinations EtOH-H2O-NH3 (50:45:5 v/v/v), EtOH-H2O-NH3 (60:35:5 v/v/v), and EtOH-H2O-NH3 (80:15:5 v/v). ROS (As>1.35) and CLOP (As>1.30) were unreliable. The chromatographic peaks of CLOP at Rf = 0.31 ± 0.01 and ROS at Rf = 0.79 ± 0.02 were found to be intact and well-eluted using the green solvent system EtOH-H2O-NH3 (70:25:5 v/v/v) (Fig. 2). Furthermore, As values of 1.04 and 1.15 for ROS and CLOP, respectively, were predicted; these are all quite reliable figures. Consequently, it was decided that the final developing system to evaluate CLOP and ROS simultaneously in commercial FDC capsules using the current technique would be EtOH-H2O-NH3 (70:25:5 v/v/v). The densitometry mode recording of the ROS and CLOP spectral bands revealed the greatest response at 240 nm. Therefore, a comprehensive simultaneous measurement of the CLOP and ROS was carried out at 240 nm.

“LOD and LOQ” was used to measure the sensitivity of the current assay for concurrently analyzing CLOP and ROS. Table 1 shows the “LOD and LOQ” values that were derived utilizing the proposed technique for CLOP and ROS. By employing the present methodology, it was determined that the “LOD and LOQ” for CLOP were 7.70 ± 0.21 and 23.11 ± 0.63 ng/band, respectively. The current protocol yielded the following "LOD and LOQ" values for ROS: 8.37 ± 0.23 and 25.11 ± 0.69 ng/band, respectively. The strong sensitivity was demonstrated by the LOD values for both medications being much below the lower linear limit. However, the LOQ values for both drugs were closed to the lower linear limit, indicating the suitable quantification of both drugs. These results showed how sensitive the current method is for measuring ROS and CLOP concurrently.

By comparing the Rf factors and UV-absorption spectrum of CLOP and ROS in commercial FDC capsules with those of standard ROS and CLOP, the specificity/peak purity of the proposed method for simultaneously analyzing CLOP and ROS was assessed. Figure 3 displays the overlapping UV-absorption spectrum of the commercial FDC capsule brands A and B as well as the standards ROS and CLOP.

By contrasting the spectra at the spot's peak start (S), peak apex (M), and peak end (E), the peak purities of commercial FDC capsules with standard CLOP and ROS were ascertained [38]. The homogeneity of the peaks was shown by estimated values of r (S,M) and r (M,E) that were bigger than 0.99 for standard CLOP and ROS as well as commercial FDC capsule brands A and B [38, 39]. The commercial FDC capsules and standards exhibited the strongest chromatographic response for ROS and CLOP at 240 nm. The same Rf values, wavelengths, and UV-absorption spectra observed in commercial FDC capsules and standards demonstrated the specificity of the proposed approach for measuring CLOP and ROS at the same time.

Concurrent measurement of CLOP and ROS in commercial FDC capsules utilizing the proposed methodology

Several medications have been effectively analyzed at the same time using liquid chromatography techniques, such as HPLC methods [40, 41]. However, the proposed method was used to evaluate CLOP and ROS simultaneously in commercial FDC capsules instead of using traditional liquid chromatography techniques. By comparing the chromatographic peaks of commercial FDC capsule brands A and B to those of reference CLOP and ROS, the recommended methodology was able to identify them at Rf = 0.31 ± 0.01 and Rf = 0.78 ± 0.02 correspondingly. The chromatographic peaks for ROS and CLOP from marketed FDC capsule brands A (Figure S2A) and B (Figure S2B) are shown in Figure S2. These peaks were precisely the same as those in the CLOP and ROS standards. Furthermore, one new peak (peak 2 in Figure S2A) and two more peaks (peaks 2 and 3 in Figure S2B) were found in FDC brands A and B, respectively. The extra peak in both formulations might be associated to impurity in either CLOP or ROS. The existence of additional peaks suggested that, in the case of formulation impurities, CLOP and ROS might be assessed concurrently using existing technology. The amount of CLOP in FDC products A and B was measured using the present technology. The results were 98.41 ± 1.29 and 99.33 ± 1.32%, in that order. The amount of ROS in FDC products A and B was calculated using the present method to be 100.52 ± 1.38 and 101.02 ± 1.40%, respectively. These results showed that the proposed method is suitable for simultaneously identifying CLOP and ROS in commercial FDC capsules.

Conclusions

The number of green HPTLC methods that can measure CLOP and ROS in physiological samples and commercial products at the same time is limited. This work developed and validated a rapid, precise, and eco-friendly HPTLC method to measure CLOP and ROS in commercial FDC capsules at the same time. The proposed method for measuring CLOP and ROS concurrently is linear, sensitive, accurate, precise, robust, and environmentally friendly. The CLOP and ROS contents of commercial FDC capsules were successfully ascertained using the current methodology. When compared to published HPLC methods, the AES, ChlorTox, and AGREE evaluation results show how exceptionally green the current technology is for concurrently analyzing ROS and CLOP. All of these results showed that CLOP and ROS in marketed FDC products may be regularly determined using the existing approach.

Weighted norm inequalities of some singular integrals associated with Laguerre expansions
The Anh Bui

Abstract
. Let ν = ( ν 1 , … , ν n ) ∈ ( − 1 , ∞ ) n \nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , and let ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} be a self-adjoint extension of the differential operator L ν := ∑ i = 1 n [ − ∂ 2 ∂ x i 2 + x i 2 + 1 x i 2 ​ ( ν i 2 − 1 4 ) ] L_{\nu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})\right] on C c ∞ ​ ( ℝ + n ) C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}) as the natural domain. In this paper, we investigate the weighted estimates of singular integrals in the Laguerre setting including the maximal function, the Riesz transform and the square functions associated to the Laguerre operator ℒ ν \mathcal{L}_{\nu} . In the special case of the Riesz transform, the paper completes the description of the Riesz transform for the full range of ν ∈ ( − 1 , ∞ ) n \nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n} which significant improves the result in [J. Funct. Anal. 244 (2007), 399–443] for ν i ≥ − 1 / 2 , ν i ∉ ( − 1 / 2 , 1 / 2 ) \nu_{i}\geq-1/2,\nu_{i}\notin(-1/2,1/2) for i = 1 , … , n i=1,\ldots,n .



1.
Introduction

We now recall some backgrounds on the Laguerre operators in
[
17
]
. For each
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, we consider the Laguerre differential operator

\displaystyle L_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+x_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\Big(\nu_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\Big],\quad\quad x\in(0,\infty)^{n}.

The
j
-th partial derivative associated with
L_{\nu}
is given by

\delta_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then the adjoint of
\delta_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is

\delta_{j}^{*}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\Big(\nu_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

It is straightforward that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}^{*}\delta_{i}=L_{\nu}{-2}(|\nu|+n).

Let
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
, and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
be multi-indices. The Laguerre function
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is defined as

\varphi_{k}^{\nu}(x)=\varphi^{\nu_{1}}_{k_{1}}(x_{1})\ldots\varphi^{\nu_{n}}_{k_{n}}(x_{n}),\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where
\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}
are the one-dimensional Laguerre functions

\varphi^{\nu_{i}}_{k_{i}}(x_{i})=\Big(\frac{2\Gamma(k_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(k_{i}+\nu_{i}+1)}\Big)^{1/2}L_{\nu_{i}}^{k_{i}}(x_{i}^{2})x_{i}^{\nu_{i}+1/2}e^{-x_{i}^{2}/2},\quad x_{i}>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

given
\nu>-1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{\nu}^{k}
denotes the Laguerre polynomial of degree
k
and order
\nu
outlined in
[
13
, p.76]
.

Then it is well-known that the system
\{\varphi_{k}^{\nu}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
. Moreover, Each
\varphi_{k}^{\nu}
is an eigenfunction of
L_{\nu}
corresponding to the eigenvalue of
4|k|+2|\nu|+2n
, i.e.,

L_{\nu}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\varphi_{k}^{\nu},

where
|\nu|=\nu_{1}+\ldots+\nu_{n}
and
|k|=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}
. The operator
L_{\nu}
is positive and symmetric in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},dx)
.

If
\nu=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\ldots,-\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
L_{\nu}
becomes the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Note that the Riesz transforms related to the harmonic oscillator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
were investigated in
[
20
,
21
]
.

The operator

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}

defined on the domain
\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)|\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\}
is a self-adjoint extension of
L_{\nu}
(the inclusion
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\subset\text{Dom}\,\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
may be easily verified), has the discrete spectrum
\{4\ell+2|\nu|+2n:\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and admits the spectral decomposition

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(4\ell+2|\nu|+2n)P_{\nu,\ell}f,

where the spectral projections are

P_{\nu,\ell}f=\sum_{|k|=\ell}\langle f,\varphi_{k}^{\nu}\rangle\varphi_{k}^{\nu}.

Moreover,

(1)

\delta_{j}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}}\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}},\ \ \delta_{j}^{*}\varphi_{k}^{\nu}=-2\sqrt{k_{j}+1}\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}},

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here and later on we use the convention that
\varphi_{k-e_{j}}^{\nu+e_{j}}=0
if
k_{j}-1<0
and
\varphi_{k+e_{j}}^{\nu-e_{j}}=0
if
\nu_{j}-1<0
. See for example
[
17
]
.

Muckenhoupt and Stein
[
15
]
initiated the investigation into the boundedness of Riesz transforms within the context of orthogonal expansions. The harmonic analysis associated with orthogonal expansions has since attracted the attention of numerous authors, as evidenced in works such as
[
4
,
5
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
21
]
and the references therein. Motivated by these contributions in the Laguerre setting, this paper aims to establish weighted norm inequalities for singular integrals, including the maximal function, the Riesz transform, and the square functions associated with the Laguerre operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. Before delving into the details, we introduce some notations. For
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
, we set

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}=\{j:-1<\nu_{j}<-1/2\}

and then define

(2)

\gamma_{\nu}=\begin{cases}\max\{-1/2-\nu_{j}:j\in\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\},\ \ &\ \text{if}\ \mathcal{I}_{\nu}\neq\emptyset,\\
0,\ \ &\ \text{if}\ \mathcal{I}_{\nu}=\emptyset.\end{cases}

In the 1-dimensional case when
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
, we have

\gamma_{\nu}=\begin{cases}-1/2-\nu,\ \ &-1<\nu<-1/2,\\
0,\ \ &\ \nu\geq-1/2.\end{cases}

We first consider the maximal function defined by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|.

For the 1-dimensional case when
n=1
, the weighted
L^{p}
-estimates (with power weights) were investigated in
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
using the transference method. It was proved in
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
that the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}\nu}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{x^{\sigma}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
whenever
-1-p(\nu+3/2)<\sigma<p(\nu+3/2)-1
. This method does not seem to imply the weighted inequality with Muckenhoupt weights. Hence, our first main result surpasses this limitation to prove the Muckenhoupt weighted
L^{p}
inequality for the maximal function. In what follows, for the
A_{p}
and
RH_{q}
classes of weights, we refer to Section 2.1 for the precise definitions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
. Then the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
(\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with the convention
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
.

In the case
\nu\geq-1/2
, Theorem
1.1
states that the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}
. For
-1<\nu<-1/2
and
w(x)=x^{\sigma}
, Theorem
1.1
asserts that the maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}\nu}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{x^{\sigma}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
whenever
1<p<\infty
and
-1-p(\nu+1/2)<\sigma<p(\nu+3/2)-1
, which recovers the result in
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
. We are currently uncertain whether the estimate in Theorem
1.1
holds true in the higher-dimensional case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, where
n\geq 2
.

We next concern the Riesz transform
R_{\nu}=(R_{\nu}^{1},\ldots,R_{\nu}^{n})
defined by

R_{\nu}^{j}=\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2},\ \ j=1,\ldots,n.

It is established in
[
17
]
that the Riesz transform is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. In
[
17
, Theorem 3.3]
, it was proved that if
\nu_{i}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
\nu_{i}\notin\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, then the Riesz transform
R_{\nu}
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator, and hence, it is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. This result has been recently improved by the author in
[
7
]
, which demonstrates that the Riesz transform
R_{\nu}
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator if
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
. The case
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}\backslash[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
remains open and is only known for
n=1
with power weights estimates, as shown in
[
4
,
5
]
. The following result not only fills the gap when
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}\backslash[-1/2,\infty)^{n}
but also extends the weighted estimates to Muckenhoupt weights. More precisely, denote
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})
and
\delta^{*}=(\delta^{*}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{*}_{n})
. Then, we have:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
with the convention
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
, the Riesz transform
R^{j}_{\nu}:=\delta_{j}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
w\in A_{(1-\gamma_{\nu})p}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}})^{\prime}}
.

Note that Theorem
1.2
is new, even for the unweighted estimate when
n\geq 2
. In the specific case when
n=1
, Theorem
1.2
reads that the Riesz transforms
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}
, where
\gamma_{\nu}=\max\{-\nu-1/2,0\}
. This marks the first instance where weighted norm inequalities for the Riesz transform have been investigated with Muckenhoupt weights.

Finally, as a by product, we consider the following square functions

(3)

S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\sqrt{t}\delta_{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2},\ \ j=1,\ldots,n,

and

(4)

G_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}.

Then we also obtained the boundedness of these square functions on the weighted
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(a)

the square functions
G_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{(1-\gamma_{\nu})p}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
with the convention
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
.

(b)

the square functions
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
and
w\in A_{(1-\gamma_{\nu})p}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}})^{\prime}}
with the convention
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
.

Finally, needless to say that our approach also implies the boundedness of the area integral considered in
[
6
]
. However, due to the length of the paper, we will not present the results here and would like to leave it for interested readers.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Muckenhoupt weights and the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function

We start with some notations which will be used frequently. For a measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and a measurable function
f
we denote

\fint_{E}f(x)dx=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(x)dx.

Given a ball
B
, we denote
S_{j}(B)=2^{j}B\backslash 2^{j-1}B
for
j=1,2,3,\ldots
, and we set
S_{0}(B)=B
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. A nonnegative locally integrable function
w
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{q}
, say
w\in A_{q}
, if there exists a positive constant
C
so that

\Big(\fint_{B}w(x)dx\Big)\Big(\fint_{B}w^{-1/(q-1)}(x)dx\Big)^{q-1}\leq C,\quad\mbox{if}\;1<q<\infty

and

\fint_{B}w(x)dx\leq C\mathop{\mbox{ess-inf}}\limits_{x\in B}w(x),\quad{\rm if}\;q=1,

for all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define
A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}A_{p}
.

The reverse Hölder classes are defined in the following way:
w\in RH_{r},1<r<\infty
, if there is a constant
C
such that for
any ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,

\Big(\fint_{B}w^{r}(x)dx\Big)^{1/r}\leq C\fint_{B}w(x)dx.

The endpoint
r=\infty
is given by the condition:
w\in RH_{\infty}
whenever, there is a constant
C
such that for any ball

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,

w(x)\leq C\fint_{B}w(y)dy\ \text{for a.e. $x\in B$}.

For
w\in A_{\infty}
and
0<p<\infty
, the weighted space
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is defined as the space of
w(x)dx
-measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

It is well-known that the power weight
w(x)=x^{\alpha}\in A_{p}
if and only if
-n<\alpha<n(p-1)
. Moreover,
w(x)=x^{\alpha}\in RH_{q}
if and only if
\alpha q>-n
.

We sum up some of the properties of Muckenhoupt classes and reverse Hölder classes in the following
results. See
[
9
,
12
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

w\in A_{p},1<p<\infty
if and only if
w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
.

(ii)

A_{1}\subset A_{p}\subset A_{q}
for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

(iii)

RH_{q}\subset RH_{p}
for
1<p\leq q<\infty
.

(iv)

If
w\in A_{p},1<p<\infty
, then there exists
1<q<p
such that
w\in A_{q}
.

(v)

If
w\in RH_{q},1<q<\infty
, then there exists
q<p<\infty
such that
w\in RH_{p}
.

(vi)

A_{\infty}=\cup_{1\leq p<\infty}A_{p}=\cup_{1<p<\infty}RH_{p}
.

(vii)

Let
1<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\infty
. Then we have

w\in A_{\frac{p}{p_{0}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{q_{0}}{p})^{\prime}}\Longleftrightarrow w^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}_{0}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{p^{\prime}_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{\prime}}.

For
r>0
, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{r}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{r}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(y)|^{r}\,dy\Big)^{1/r},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
containing
x
. When
r=1
, we write
\mathcal{M}
instead of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
.

We now record the following results concerning the weak type estimates and the weighted estimates of the maximal functions.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
19
]
)
.

Let
0<r<\infty
. Then we have for
p>r
and
w\in A_{p/r}
,

\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}.

We will end this section by some simple estimates whose proofs will be omitted.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then we have:

(i)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\Big)^{n+\epsilon}f(y)dy\leq C(\epsilon)\mathcal{M}f(x);

(ii)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\Big)^{n+\epsilon}f(y)dy\leq C(\epsilon);

(iii)

for every
t>0
,

\int_{|x|<t}\Big(\frac{t}{|x|}\Big)^{n-\epsilon}dx\lesssim t^{-n}.

2.2.
Singular integrals beyond the Calderón-Zygmund theory

In this section we recall some criteria for singular integrals beyond the Calderón-Zygmund theory to be bounded on the weighted Lebesgue spaces in
[
1
,
3
]
.

An operator
T
defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is said to be a
linearizable operator
if there exists a Banach space
\mathbb{B}
and a linear operator
U
from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\mathbb{B})
so that

|Tf(x)|=\|Uf(x)\|_{\mathbb{B}}

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

It can be verified that a linearizable operator is a sublinear operator. The class of linearizable operator includes linear operators, maximal operators and square functions.

We first recall a theorem which is taken from
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
on a criterion for the singular integrals to be bounded on the weighted Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p_{0}<q_{0}<\infty
and let
T
be a linearizable operator. Assume that
T
can be extended to be bounded on
L^{q_{0}}
. Assume that there exists a family of operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfying that for
j\geq 2
and every ball
B

(5)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|T(I-\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}})f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq\alpha(j)\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}},

and

(6)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}}f|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq\alpha(j)\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}},

for all
f
supported in
B
. If
\sum_{j}\alpha(j)2^{jd}<\infty
, then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
p\in(p_{0},q_{0})
and
w\in A_{\frac{p}{p_{0}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{q_{0}}{p})^{\prime}}
.

Note that
[
3
, Theorem 6.6]
proves Theorem
2.4
for
q_{0}=2
, but their arguments also work well for any value
q_{0}\in(1,\infty)
.

The following theorem is a direct consequence of
[
1
, Theorem 3.7]
which give a sufficient conditions for a singular integral to be bounded on Lebesgue spaces which plays an important role in the sequel.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p_{0}<q_{0}\leq\infty.
Let
T
be a bounded sublinear
operator on
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Assume that there exists a family of operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfying that

(7)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|T(I-\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}})f\big|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{p_{0}}(f)(x),

and

(8)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|T\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}}f\big|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{p_{0}}(|Tf|)(x),

for all balls

B
with radius
r_{B}
, all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and all
x\in B
. Then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all

p_{0}<p<q_{0}
and
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}\cap RH_{(q_{0}/p)^{\prime}}
.

3.
Some kernel estimates

This section is devoted to establishing some kernel estimates related to the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
. These estimates play an essential role in proving our main results. We begin by providing an explicit formula for the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,n
, similarly to
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}
the self-adjoint extension of the differential operator

L_{\nu_{j}}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+x_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{x_{j}^{2}}(\nu_{j}^{2}-\frac{1}{4})

on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as the natural domain. It is easy to see that

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}.

Let
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
and let
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
be the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
for each
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then we have

(9)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j}).

For
\nu_{j}\geq-1/2
,
j=1,\ldots,n,
, the kernel of
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{j}}}
is given by

(10)

p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
and
I_{\alpha}
is the usual Bessel funtions of an imaginary argument defined by

I_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\alpha+2k}}{k!\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)},\ \ \ \ \alpha>-1.

See for example
[
10
,
17
]
.

Note that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, we can rewrite the kernel
p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})
as follows

(11)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu_{j}}(x_{j},y_{j})=\frac{2(rx_{j}y_{j})^{1/2}}{1-r}

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{1-r^{1/2}}{1+r^{1/2}}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(-\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big)I_{\nu_{j}}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}x_{j}y_{j}\Big),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

The following properties of the Bessel function
I_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>-1
are well-known and are taken from
[
13
]
:

(12)

I_{\alpha}(z)\sim z^{\alpha},\ \ \ 0<z\leq 1,

(13)

I_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{e^{z}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}+S_{\alpha}(z),

where

(14)

|S_{\alpha}(z)|\leq Ce^{z}z^{-3/2},\ \ z\geq 1,

(15)

\frac{d}{dz}(z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha}(z))=z^{-\alpha}I_{\alpha+1}(z).

Let
\nu>-1
, we have

I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)=[I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)]-[I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)].

Applying
[
13
, (5.7.9)]
,

I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)=\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z).

Hence,

|I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)|\leq\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{z}I_{\alpha+1}(z)+|I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)|.

On the other hand, since
\alpha+1>-1/2
, we have

0<I_{\alpha+1}(z)-I_{\alpha+2}(z)<2(\alpha+2)\frac{I_{\alpha+2}(z)}{z}<2(\alpha+2)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ z>0.

See for example
[
16
]
.

Consequently,

(16)

|I_{\alpha}(z)-I_{\alpha+1}(z)|<(4\alpha+6)\frac{I_{\alpha+1}(z)}{z},\ \ \ \alpha>-1,z>0.

The following gives another upper bound for the heat kernel
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
which plays an essential role in the proof of the boundedness of the maximal operator.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\nu\in(-1,-1/2)
. Then there exists
c>0
such that

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)+\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{y<x/2\}}+\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{x/2\leq y\}}

for
t>0
and
x,y>0
.

Proof.

Case 1:
t\in(0,1)
.
In this situation,

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\leq p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy\geq t\}}+p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy<t\}}

It is obvious that due to (
21
),

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy\geq t\}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

For the second term, from (
20
), we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy<t\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{y>2x\}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle=E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}.

For
E_{1}
, we have
|x-y|\sim x
whenever
y\leq x/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x^{1-2\gamma_{\nu}}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{y<x/2\}}.

Similarly,

E_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{y>2x\}}.

For the last term
E_{3}
, if
x/2<y\leq 2x
, then
x\sim y
. In this case,

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}\sim\frac{1}{t^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{2\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(xy)^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{y^{1-2\gamma_{\nu}}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<t\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}},

where in the second inequality we used
(xy)^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}\leq t^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}
since
1-2\gamma_{\nu}>0
and
t>xy
.

Case 2:
t\geq 1
.
In this situation,

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\leq p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy\geq e^{2t}\}}+p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}}

From (
23
),

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy\geq e^{2t}\}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

For the second term, by using (
22
), we can write

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ce^{2t}}\Big)\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ce^{2t}}\Big)\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ce^{2t}}\Big)\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{y>2x\}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ce^{2t}}\Big)\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle=F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}.

For
E_{1}
, we have
|x-y|\sim x
whenever
y\leq x/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ce^{2t}}\Big)\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{e^{t(1-2\gamma_{\nu})}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{ce^{2t}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x^{1-2\gamma_{\nu}}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{y<x/2\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{x}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{y<x/2\}}.

Similarly,

E_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{y>2x\}}.

For the last term
E_{3}
, if
x/2<y\leq 2x
, then we have

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{e^{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}\sim\frac{1}{e^{t(1-2\gamma_{\nu})}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{2\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(xy)^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{y^{1-2\gamma_{\nu}}}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{xy<e^{2t}\}\cap\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{y}\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\chi_{\{x/2\leq y\leq 2x\}},

where in the second inequality we used
(xy)^{(1-2\gamma_{\nu})/2}\leq e^{t(1-2\gamma_{\nu})}
.

This completes our proof.
∎

We now take care of the estimate for
\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
. To do this, from (
10
) we can rewrite
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
as

(24)

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1-r}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{-\nu}I_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big),

where

Setting

H_{\nu}(r;x,y)=\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{-\nu}I_{\nu}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big),

then

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xy\Big)^{\nu+1/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+r}{1-r}(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)H_{\nu}(r;x,y).

From (

(26)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu+1/2}{x}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y),

\displaystyle\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

On the other hand, since
\partial(xf)=f+\partial f
, we have

\delta(xf)=f+\delta f.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then we have

(29)

|\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

for
t>0
and
x,y\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

For
\nu\geq-1/2
, the estimate (
29
) is a direct consequence of
[
7
, Propositions 3.2 & 3.6]
. We need only to take case of the case
-1<\nu<-1/2
. To do this, we consider two cases.

Case 1:
t\in(0,1)
.

We also have two subcases.

Subcase 1.1:
xy<t
.
From (
27
) and the facts that
r\sim 1+r\sim 1
and

, we have

\displaystyle\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{x}{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{y}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{y}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

Using (
20
),

\displaystyle\frac{x}{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{1-\gamma_{\nu}}\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

In addition, By (
10
) and (

),

\displaystyle\frac{y}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{xy}{t}\Big)^{\nu+3/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

These two estimates proves (
29
).

Subcase 1.2:
xy\geq t
.
From (
27
), we rewrite as follows

\displaystyle|\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\Big|xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)+\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}(y-x)p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\Big|\frac{1+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{2r^{1/2}}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\Big|+\frac{|x-y|}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}.

The term
E_{3}
is straightforward. Indeed, from Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x-y|}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x-y|}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x-y|}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

We now take care of
E_{1}
. If
y<x/2
, then

and

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
y>2x
and
x\geq\sqrt{t}
, similarly to above, since we also have
|x-y|>x/2
, we have

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
y>2x
and
x<\sqrt{t}
, then from Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{1-\gamma_{\nu}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

Otherwise, if
x/2\leq y\leq 2x
, then
x\sim y\gtrsim\sqrt{t}
since

. In this case, by the first inequality in (

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\exp\Big(-c^{\prime}txy\Big),

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{txy}}\Big)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}x}\Big)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

It remains to estimate
E_{2}
. To do this we write

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1+r}{1-r}x[p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)]+\frac{1-2r^{1/2}+r}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+r}{1-r}x\big|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\big|+\frac{(1-\sqrt{r})^{1/2}}{1-r}xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1+r}{1-r}x\big|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\big|+\frac{1-\sqrt{r}}{1+\sqrt{r}}xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{t}\big|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\big|+txp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)=:E_{21}+E_{22}.

where in the last inequality we used
1-\sqrt{r}\sim t
and
1+r\sim 1
.

Similarly to
E_{1}
, we have

E_{22}\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

For the term
E_{21}
, using (
16
) and (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\big|p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1-r}{2\sqrt{r}xy}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{t}{xy}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

which implies

E_{21}\lesssim\frac{t}{xy}\frac{x}{t}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

If
y\leq x/2
or
y\geq 2x
, then
|x-y|\gtrsim x
. Hence, by Proposition
3.1
and the fact
xy\geq t
,

\displaystyle E_{21}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{t}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
x/2<y<2x
, then
x\sim y\gtrsim\sqrt{t}
since
xy\geq t
. Hence, by Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle E_{21}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t}{xy}\frac{x}{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

Case 2:
t\geq 1
.
In this case,
1+r\sim 1-r\sim 1
and
r\leq 1
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\delta p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|\lesssim xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

Subcase 2.1:
xy<e^{2t}
.
By (
10
), (
12
) and
1-2\gamma_{\nu}>0
we have

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim x\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{e^{-2t}xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x^{1-\gamma_{\nu}}}{e^{t(1-2\gamma_{\nu})}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})/2\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

which implies

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}},

as desired.

For the second term, by (
10
), (
12
) we have

\displaystyle yp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim y\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)[e^{-2t}xy]^{\nu+3/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})/2\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})/2\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

Subcase 2.2:
xy\geq e^{2t}
.
From the first inequality of (
23
),

(30)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-c^{\prime}xy\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

Since
|x-y|+y\geq x
, either
|x-y|>x/2
or
y>x/2
. If
|x-y|>x/2
, then we have

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)^{1-\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{t}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{t^{\gamma_{\nu}/2}x^{1-\gamma_{\nu}}}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)^{1-\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{\gamma_{\nu}/2}}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
y>x/2
, then from the first inequality of (
23
) we have

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}/2+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

For the reaming term
yp^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)
, we write

yp^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)=(y-x)p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)+xp^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y).

The term
xp^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)
follows by using the above argument while the term
(y-x)p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)
follows from Proposition
3.1
.

This completes our proof.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then

|\delta^{*}p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The estimate corresponding to
\nu\geq-1/2
is a direct consequence of
[
7
, Propositions 3.2 & 3.8 ]
. It remains to prove the estimate when
-1<\nu<-1/2
. To do this, denote

\widetilde{\delta}=\partial_{x}+x-\frac{1}{x}\Big(\nu+1+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then we have

\displaystyle\delta^{*}

\displaystyle=:-\widetilde{\delta}+2x-\frac{1}{x}\Big(2\nu+1+1\Big).

Hence,

\displaystyle|\delta^{*}p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\widetilde{\delta}p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)|+xp^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)+\frac{1}{x}p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y).

By Proposition
3.3
in
[
7
]
,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\delta}p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

We now show that

xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
x>2y
or
x<y/2
, then
|x-y|\gtrsim x
. This, together with Proposition
3.1
(i), implies

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
y/2\leq x\leq 2y
, by a careful examination the proof of Proposition
3.3
we have

\displaystyle xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

We have proved that

xp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

It remains to show that

\frac{1}{x}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

To do this, we consider two cases.

Case 1:
t\in(0,1)
.
If
x\geq\sqrt{t}
, then by Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
x<\sqrt{t}\leq 1
, then apply (
17
) with the consideration that
\rho(x)\sim x
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

Case 2:
t>1
.
If
x\geq 1
, then by (
17
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{-t/2}}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
x<1
, then apply (
17
) with the consideration that
\rho(x)\sim x
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1+1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\nu+1/2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

This completes our proof.
∎

The following result concerns estimates of the time derivative of the heat kernel
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then we have

|\partial_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

If
\nu\geq-1/2
, then Proposition
3.1
yields

|p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

Applying Lemma 2.5 in
[
8
]
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big),

as desired since
\gamma_{\nu}=0
.

We now take care of the case
-1<\nu<-1/2
. From (
24
), by the product rule and the chain rule, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(\nu+1)\partial_{t}\Big[\frac{r^{1/2}}{1-r}\Big]\times\Big[\frac{r^{1/2}}{1-r}\Big]^{-1}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\Big[\frac{1+r}{1-r}\Big](x^{2}+y^{2})p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle+2\partial_{t}\Big[\frac{r^{1/2}}{1-r}\Big]xyp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y),

where
r=e^{-4t}
.

By a simple calculation, we come up with

(31)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(\nu+1)(1+r)}{1-r}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)+\frac{4r}{(1-r)^{2}}(x^{2}+y^{2})p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)-\frac{4r^{1/2}(1+r)}{(1-r)^{2}}xyp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y).

We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
t\leq 1
.
We have two subcases.

Subcase 1.1:
xy<t
.
From the kernel estimate (
20
) and the facts
1-r\sim t
and
r\sim 1
, we have

\partial_{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|}{ct}\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

Subcase 1.2:
xy\geq t
.
We rewrite

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(\nu+1)(1+r)}{1-r}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)+\frac{4r}{(1-r)^{2}}(x-y)^{2}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\Big[\frac{4r^{1/2}(1+r)}{(1-r)^{2}}-\frac{8r}{(1-r)^{2}}\Big]xyp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{4r^{1/2}(1+r)}{(1-r)^{2}}xy[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(\nu+1)(1+r)}{1-r}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)+\frac{4r}{(1-r)^{2}}(x-y)^{2}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\frac{4r^{1/2}(1-r^{1/2})^{2}}{(1-r)^{2}}xyp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{4r^{1/2}(1+r)}{(1-r)^{2}}xy[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(\nu+1)(1+r)}{1-r}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)+\frac{4r}{(1-r)^{2}}|x-y|^{2}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\frac{4r^{1/2}}{(1+r^{1/2})^{2}}xyp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)-\frac{4r^{1/2}(1+r)}{(1-r)^{2}}xy[p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)]

\displaystyle=E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}+E_{4}.

Since in this situation
r\sim 1
and
1-r\sim t
, we have

\displaystyle|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|+|E_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t^{2}}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+xyp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y).

This, together with the upper bound of
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
in (
20
), further yields

\displaystyle|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|+|E_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

For the last term
E_{4}
, using the facts
1-r\sim t
and
r\sim 1
, (
16
) and Proposition
3.1
to obtain

\displaystyle|E_{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{xy}{t^{2}}|p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)-p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\sim\frac{xy}{t^{2}}\frac{1-r}{2\sqrt{r}xy}p_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

This complete our proof for the case
t\in(0,1)
.

Case 2:
t\geq 1
.
In this case,
1+r\sim 1-r\sim 1
and
r\leq 1
. Hence, from (
31
), we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+xyp_{t}^{\nu+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+xyp_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y),

where in the last inequality we used
x^{2}+y^{2}\geq 2xy
.

Subcase 2.1:
xy<e^{2t}
.
By (
10
) and (
12
) we have

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{e^{-2t}xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t(1-2\gamma_{\nu})}}\exp\Big(-c(x^{2}+y^{2})\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{xy}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

which implies

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

as desired.

Subcase 2.2:
xy\geq e^{2t}
.
By (
11
), (
13
) and (
14
) we have

p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(-c^{\prime}xy\Big).

If
y<x/2
or
y>2x
, then
|x-y|\sim\max\{x,y\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)+(x^{2}+y^{2})\frac{t}{|x-y|^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}/2\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

If
x/2\leq y\leq 2x
, then
x\sim y\gtrsim\sqrt{t}
. Hence,

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+(x^{2}+y^{2})p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\sim p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)+x^{2}p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)+\frac{x^{2}}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)\frac{1}{xy}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{e^{t}}\exp\Big(-c|x-y|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}.

This completes our proof.
∎

The following result concerns estimates of the mixed derivative of the heat kernel
p_{t}^{\nu}(x,y)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\nu>-1
and
a\geq 0
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have

(32)

|\partial^{k}_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{k+1/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}},

(33)

|\delta\partial^{k}_{t}p^{\nu}_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{k+1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}},

and

(34)

|\delta^{*}\partial^{k}_{t}[e^{-ta}p^{\nu+1}_{t}(x,y)]|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{k+1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We only provide the proof for
-1<\nu<-1/2
since the proof corresponding to
\nu\geq-1/2
is similar.

For
a\in(0,1/2)
and
c>0
, define

H_{t,a,c}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

for
t>0
and
x,y>0
.

Then will show that

(35)

\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t,a,c}(x,z)H_{t,a,c}(z,y)dz\lesssim H_{t,a,4c}(x,y)

for
t>0
and
x,y>0
.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t,a,c}(x,z)

\displaystyle H_{t,a,c}(z,y)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{(0,\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{z}\Big)^{a}\exp\Big(-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{z}\Big)^{a}dz.

Using the following inequality

|x-z|^{2}+|z-y|^{2}\geq|x-y|^{2}/2,

we have,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t,a,c}(x,z)

\displaystyle H_{t,a,c}(z,y)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\gamma_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{(0,\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{z}\Big)^{a}\exp\Big(-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{z}\Big)^{a}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}H_{t,a,4c}(x,y)\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{z}\Big)^{2a}\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{2ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{z}\Big)^{2a}\Big]^{1/2},

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{1/4},

Consequently,

\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{t,a,c}(x,z)H_{t,a,c}(z,y)dz\lesssim H_{t,a,4c}(x,y),

Before coming to the estimate of the heat kernel

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

be the linear operator defined by (

Proof.

Fix
\frac{1}{1-\sigma}<p\leq q<\frac{1}{\beta}
. Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. We first prove (
38
) for
n=1

\displaystyle\|T_{t,\beta,\sigma}f\|_{L^{q}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}|T_{t,\beta,\sigma}(x,y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle+\Big[\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\Big(\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}|T_{t,\beta,\sigma}(x,y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle+\Big[\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}|T_{t,\beta,\sigma}(x,y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle+\Big[\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}|T_{t,\beta,\sigma}(x,y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}=:E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}+E_{4}.

Since for

and

E_{1}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}.

For the term
E_{2}
, by the Minkowski inequality we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\Big(\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\beta}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\sigma}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\sim\Big[\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\Big(\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\sigma}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big[\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{q|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)dx\Big]^{1/q}\times\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\sigma}|f(y)|dy.

2.3

On the other hand, by the Hölder inequality, Lemma
2.3
and the fact that

\displaystyle\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\sigma}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{p}.

Hence,

Similarly,

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big[\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\beta q}\Big(\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)|f(y)|dy\Big)^{q}dx\Big]^{1/q}.

,

\displaystyle\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{p}\Big[\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\beta q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

as long as

For the last term, by the Hölder inequality and Lemma

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Big[\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{x}\Big)^{\beta q}dx\Big]^{1/q}\Big[\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{y}\Big)^{\sigma}|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}

R_{\nu}=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{t}\delta e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\frac{dt}{t}

and

(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}=\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{m}e^{-(s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}d\vec{s},

where
d\vec{s}=ds_{1}\ldots ds_{m}
.

Hence, we can write

R_{\nu}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f=c\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\sqrt{t}\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{m}e^{-(t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}fds\frac{dt}{t}.

Applying Proposition
3.8
and Lemma
3.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|R_{\nu}

\displaystyle(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f\|_{L^{2}(S_{j}(B))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\sqrt{t}\big\|\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{m}e^{-(t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m})\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(S_{j}(B))}ds\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(t+|\vec{s}|)^{m+\frac{1}{2}}}\exp\Big(-\frac{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2}}{c(t+|\vec{s}|)}\Big)d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}\ldots\frac{dt}{t}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{r_{B}^{2}}^{\infty}\ldots\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=:E_{1}+E_{2},

where
|\vec{s}|=s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m}
.

For the first term
E_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(t+|\vec{s}|)^{m+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\frac{t+|\vec{s}|}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2}}\Big)^{m+\frac{1}{2}}d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{0}^{r_{B}^{2}}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2m+1}}d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\sim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\frac{r_{B}^{2m+1}}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2m+1}}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-j(2m+1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}.

For the second term, note that
t+s_{1}+\ldots+s_{m}\sim t
as
t\geq r_{B}^{2}
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in[0,r_{B}^{2}]
. It follows that

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}\int_{r_{B}^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{[0,r_{B}^{2}]^{m}}\frac{\sqrt{t}}{t^{m+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\frac{t}{(2^{j}r_{B})^{2}}\Big)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}d\vec{s}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B)}.

Collecting the estimates of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, we deduce to (
46
).

Next we need to prove that
R_{\nu}^{j}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for
2<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
. By duality, it suffices to prove
(R^{j}_{\nu})^{*}:=\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}<p_{0}\leq 2
. To do this, fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}<p_{0}\leq 2
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2m-1>n
, by Theorem
2.4
, we need to show that

(48)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|(R^{j}_{\nu})^{*}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)})^{m}f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

and

(49)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)})^{m}]f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
j\geq 2
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

The estimate (
49
) follows directly from Proposition
3.8
and Lemma
3.7
and the following expansion

I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)})^{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}e^{-kr_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)},

where
c_{k}
are constants.

Hence, we need to prove (
48
). We first note that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
we have, for
t>0
,

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{e^{-t(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)}}{\sqrt{4|k|+2|\nu|+2n}}\langle f,\varphi^{\nu}_{k}\rangle\varphi^{\nu}_{k}.

Using this and (
1
), by a simple calculation we come up with

e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}\delta_{j}^{*}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2}))^{m}f=\delta^{*}_{j}e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)^{-1/2}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2}))^{m}f.

At this stage, arguing similarly to the proof of (
46
) we come up with (
48
).

We now turn to the weighted estimate for the Riesz transform
R^{j}_{\nu}
. By duality it suffices to show that
(R^{j}_{\nu})^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for all
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{\frac{p}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. We now fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
and
w\in A_{\frac{p}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we can find
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu}}
such that
w\in A_{\frac{p_{0}}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{q_{0}\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
. Similarly to (
48
) and (
49
) we also obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|(R^{j}_{\nu})^{*}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)})^{m}f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2m-1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+2)})^{m}]f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq 2^{-j(n+1)}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
j\geq 2
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
supported in
B
.

Hence, by Theorem
2.4
,
(R^{j}_{\nu})^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
w\in A_{\frac{p_{0}}{1-\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{q_{0}\gamma_{\nu}})^{\prime}}
.

This completes our proof.
∎

4.3.
The square functions

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:

We only give the proof for the square function
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
since the proof for the square function
G_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is similar and even easier.

From the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
boundedness of the Riesz transform
\delta\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{-1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\sqrt{t}\delta_{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\|_{2}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\sqrt{t}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\|_{2}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\sqrt{t}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{2},

where in the last inequality we used the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the following square function

f\mapsto\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\sqrt{t}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}^{1/2}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2},

since
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Similarly to the proofs of (
46
) and (
47
), we can show that
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
also satisfies the estimates (
46
) and (
47
) with
q_{0}=2
and any
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}<p_{0}<2
, and
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
taking place of
R_{\nu}
. Hence, by Theorem
2.4
,
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p\leq 2
.

To complete our proof, we will prove that
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
2<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
. To do this, in the light of Theorem
2.5
, we need to prove the following inequalities with
2<q_{0}<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2m>n+1
,

(50)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{2}(f)(x),

and

(51)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f\big|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{2}(|Tf|)(x),

For (
50
), we write

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}.

For
i=0,1,2
, by the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
and
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{S_{i}(B)}|f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{2}f(x).

For
j\geq 3
, similarly to (
46
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-i(2m-1-n/2)}\Big(\fint_{S_{i}(B)}|f|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-i(2m-1-n/2)}\mathcal{M}_{2}f(x).

It follows (
50
).

It remains to prove (
51
). Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B}

\displaystyle|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f(x)|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big(\int_{B}|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}e^{-kr_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}\left[\int_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-kr_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{q_{0}/2}dx\right]^{1/q_{0}}.

On the other hand, note that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\nu\in(-1,\infty)^{n}
we have, for
t>0
,

e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}e^{-t(4|k|+2|\nu|+2n)}\langle f,\varphi^{\nu}_{k}\rangle\varphi^{\nu}_{k}.

Using this and (
1
), by a simple calculation we come up with

e^{-t(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+\alpha_{j})}\delta_{j}f=\delta_{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f,

where
\alpha_{j}=2
if
\nu_{j}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{j}=2-4\nu_{j}
if
\nu_{j}<0
.

It follows that

(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-kr_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f=e^{-kr_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+\alpha_{j})}(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B}

\displaystyle|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f(x)|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}\left[\int_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|e^{-kr_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+\alpha_{j})}(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{q_{0}/2}dx\right]^{1/q_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\geq 0}\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}\left[\int_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|e^{-kr_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+\alpha_{j})}[(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\chi_{S_{i}(B)}](x)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{q_{0}/2}dx\right]^{1/q_{0}}

which along with Minkowski’s inequality, Proposition
3.8
and Lemma
3.7
gives

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B}

\displaystyle|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f(x)|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\geq 0}\sup_{1\leq k\leq m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|e^{-kr_{B}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu+e_{j}}+\alpha_{j})}[(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\chi_{S_{i}(B)}]\right\|_{L^{q_{0}}(B)}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\geq 0}e^{-c4^{i}}|B|^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q_{0}})}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|(\sqrt{t}\delta_{j})e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f\right\|_{L^{2}(S_{i}(B))}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{-c4^{i}}|B|^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q_{0}})}\Big(\int_{2^{i}B}|S_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}f(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}.

This implies (
51
).

We have proved that the square function
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
.

For the weighted estimate, we fix
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
and
w\in A_{p(1-\gamma_{\nu})}\cap RH_{(\frac{1}{p\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}})^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
2.1
, we can there exists
\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{\nu}}<p_{0}<p<q_{0}<\frac{1}{\gamma_{\nu+e_{j}}}
such that
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}\cap RH_{(\frac{q_{0}}{p})^{\prime}}
.

Therefore, by Theorem
2.5
, it suffices to verify the following estimates
we need to prove that

(52)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f\big|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{p_{0}}(f)(x)

and

(53)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}[I-(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}]f\big|^{q_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/q_{0}}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{p_{0}}(|Tf|)(x).

The proof of (
53
) is similar to (
51
) (see also Theorem 7.2 in
[
2
]
) by using Lemma 7.4 in
[
2
]
, Proposition
3.8
and the
L^{p_{0}}-L^{p_{0}}
-off diagonal estimates instead of
L^{2}-L^{q_{0}}
-off diagonal estimates in Lemma
3.7
.

It remains to prove (
52
). To do this, we write

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}f\big|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}.

For
i=0,1,2
, by the
L^{p_{0}}
-boundedness of
S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
and
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{S_{i}(B)}|f|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{p_{0}}f(x).

For
j\geq 3
, by the Holder’s inequality we have

\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{p_{0}}dx\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq\Big(\fint_{B}\big|S^{j}_{\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}(I-e^{-r_{B}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}})^{m}(f\chi_{S_{i}(B)})\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}.

At this stage, similarly to (
46
) by using the
L^{p_{0}}-L^{2}
-off diagonal estimates instead of
L^{2}-L^{2}
-off diagonal estimate we obtain (
52
).

Hence the proof is complete.
∎

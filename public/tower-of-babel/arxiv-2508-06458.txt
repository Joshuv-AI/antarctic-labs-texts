Strongly Flat Modules via Universal Localization
Javad Asadollahi, Rasool Hafezi and Somayeh Sadeghi

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate a non-commutative version of strongly flat modules, which is based on the concept of universal localization introduced by Cohn. We consider a set σ \sigma consisting of maps of finitely generated projective R R -modules, where R R is not necessarily a commutative ring. Let R σ R_{\sigma} denote the universal localization of R R with respect to σ \sigma . The class of σ \sigma -strongly flat modules is defined as the left class in the cotorsion pair generated by R σ R_{\sigma} . We examine the homotopy category of σ \sigma -strongly flat modules and demonstrate that the thick subcategory 𝒮 σ \mathscr{S}_{\sigma} , consisting of acyclic complexes, wherein all syzygies are σ \sigma -strongly flat, forms a precovering class within this homotopy category. This implies that the quotient map from 𝕂 ⁡ ( σ ​ - ​ 𝒮 ​ ℱ ) \mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}}) to 𝕂 ⁡ ( σ ​ - ​ 𝒮 ​ ℱ ) / 𝒮 σ \mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})/\mathscr{S}_{\sigma} always has a fully faithful right adjoint.

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
S\subseteq R
be a multiplicative subset. The class of
S
-strongly flat modules is defined as the left class in the cotorsion pair generated by
R_{S}
, the localization of
R
with respect to
S
. This class of modules has been studied first by Trlifaj
[
Tr1
, Section 2]
, for the case in which
R
is a domain and
R_{S}
is the quotient ring of
R
. Later, many authors in various papers considered arbitrary commutative rings and arbitrary multiplicative sets, even with zero-divisors.

The motivation for studying the class of
S
-strongly flat modules originated from approximation theory, specifically, the problem was to characterize the commutative rings for which this class is a covering class. Rings satisfying this property have been called
S
-almost perfect and have been characterized by many homological and ring-theoretic properties. See, for instance,
[
BS
,
FS
,
P1
,
PS
,
BP
]
and the survey article
[
Sa
]
.

In the non-commutative case, this class is studied in
[
FN1
]
and
[
FN2
]
, when
R
is a right Ore domain with classical right quotient ring
Q
. To this end, they considered the case of an extension of rings
R\subseteq Q
for which the embedding
R\rightarrow Q
is a homological epimorphism, that is,
\varphi:R\rightarrow S
is an epimorphism in the category of rings and
{\rm{Tor}}^{R}_{i}(Q,Q)=0
, for all
i\geq 1
, see
[
GL
]
. The authors extended further results to the non-commutative case, when the modules under consideration are strongly flat, when the extension
Q
of
R
is the maximal flat epimorphic right ring of quotients of
R
, and when
R
is a right noetherian right chain domain
[
FN3
]
.

Our first aim in this paper is to treat the class of strongly flat modules in a more general localization theory. The full generality for non-commutative localization is that studied by Cohn
[
Coh1
]
and
[
Coh2
]
. Cohn localization is a significant construction in non-commutative ring theory that generalizes the concept of inverting elements to inverting matrices.

Let
R
be a non-commutative ring (always associative with identity) and
\sigma
be a set of maps between finitely generated projective (left) modules. Ring morphism
\varphi:R\rightarrow R^{\prime}
is called
\sigma
-inverting if the induced morphism
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}P\rightarrow R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}Q
, is an isomorphism, for all morphisms
P\rightarrow Q
in
\sigma
. The collection of all
\sigma
-inverting morphisms is a category, and it was shown by Cohn
[
Coh1
]
and Schofield
[
Sch
]
that this category has an initial object. Its initial object, denoted by
R\rightarrow R_{\sigma}
, or
R\rightarrow\sigma^{-1}R
, is called the Cohn localization or the universal localization of
R
. See
[
F
, Theorem 2.46]
for a ring-theoretic description and
[
Kr2
, §1]
for a more categorical description of this notion.

It is known that, unlike the classical localization of commutative rings, in general
R_{\sigma}
is not a flat
R
-module. However, if
\sigma
admits the calculus of left fractions, then
R_{\sigma}
is a flat
R
-module. In Section
2
of the paper, we study the class of
\sigma
-strongly flat modules, investigate some of its properties, and provide a characterization in terms of the so-called
\sigma
-contramodule
R
-modules.

On the other hand, Neeman, in
[
N2
]
, provided a new description for the homotopy category
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
, where
R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}}
denotes the category of projective
R
-modules. He demonstrated that this category is equivalent to the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category of flat
R
-modules,
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
, by a thick subcategory
\mathscr{S}
of
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
. The subcategory
\mathscr{S}
has been studied in deep by Neeman, see Facts 2.14 of
[
N2
]
for six characterizations of it, for instance, it consists of all acyclic complexes with flat syzygies, the so-called pure acyclic complexes. Furthermore, in
[
N3
]
, he showed that the quotient map from
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
to
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})/\mathscr{S}
always has a right adjoint. This result consequently gives a new, fully faithful embedding of
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
into
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
. To this end, he showed first that the inclusion of
\mathscr{S}
into
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
, or more generally into
\mathbb{K}(R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}})
, has a right adjoint
[
N3
, Theorem 3.1]
.

According to
[
N2
, Facts 2.8 (i)]
, we know that the homotopy category of projective
R
-modules, denoted
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
, is a well-generated triangulated category and thus satisfies Brown representability. Furthermore, the natural inclusion
e:\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})
preserves coproducts. Hence, by
[
N1
, Theorem 8.4.4]
, we can conclude that the inclusion
e
has a right adjoint
e^{*}:\mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
, which is also a Verdier quotient. Following Neeman
[
N3
, Remark 2.12]
, we denote the kernel of the functor
e^{*}
by
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})^{\perp}
, where orthogonal is taken in
\mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})
. Hence,
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
is equivalent to
\mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})/\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})^{\perp}
.

Therefore, it is interesting to know the structure of objects in
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})^{\perp}
. If
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
is considered as a subcategory of
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
and the orthogonal is taken in
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
, Neeman
[
N2
]
studied this orthogonal and provides different characterizations of objects in
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})^{\perp}
. In Section
3
, we follow a similar argument to get characterizations for complexes in
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})^{\perp}
, in case the orthogonal is taken in
\mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})
. We show that under mild conditions,
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})^{\perp}
is equal to
\mathscr{S}_{\sigma}
, the class of all
\sigma
-strongly flat complexes, i.e., the class of all acyclic complexes with all syzygies
\sigma
-strongly flat. Moreover, in Subsection
3.1
, it will be shown that the inclusion of
\mathscr{S}_{\sigma}
into either of the categories
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
and
\mathbb{K}(R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}})
always admits right adjoints.

Neeman, in
[
N3
]
, showed that every object in
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
admits an
\mathscr{S}
-precover. In order to constitute an
\mathscr{S}
-precover for an object
Z
in
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}})
, he employed an auxiliary ring, as detailed in
[
N3
, §2]
. In the last section of the paper, we will utilize this auxiliary ring to construct objects in
\mathscr{S}_{\sigma}
.

Throughout, let
R
be an associative ring with identity. Unless otherwise specified, by the
R
-module we mean a left
R
-module. The category of all left
R
-modules will be denoted by
R
-Mod.

2.
\sigma
-strongly flat modules

In this section, our objective is to study the theory of strongly flat modules based on the universal localization introduced and studied by Cohn
[
Coh1
]
and Schofield
[
Sch
]
.

2.1.
Universal localization

Let
\sigma
be a set of maps of finitely generated projective
R
modules, that is,

\sigma=\{s_{i}:P_{i}\rightarrow Q_{i}\ |\ P_{i}\ {\rm and}\ Q_{i}\ {\rm are\ finitely\ generated\ projective}\ R\text{-}{\rm modules}\}.

The universal localization of
R
with respect to
\sigma
is a ring
R_{\sigma}
with a ring homomorphism
\phi:R\rightarrow R_{\sigma}
such that

(i)

For every
s\in\sigma
, the morphism
R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}s
is an isomorphism, i.e.
\phi
is
\sigma
-inverting.

(ii)

For every ring homomorphism
\psi:R\longrightarrow U
such that
U\otimes_{R}s
is an isomorphism, for all
s\in\sigma
, there exists a unique ring homomorphism
\overline{\psi}:R_{\sigma}\longrightarrow U
such that
\psi=\overline{\psi}\phi
.

For the construction of
R_{\sigma}
, see
[
Sch
, Theorem 4.1]
. It is known that
\phi:R\longrightarrow R_{\sigma}
is an epimorphism of rings. The following equivalent conditions are satisfied, see
[
Sch
, Theorem 4.8]
.

(i)

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}={\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R_{\sigma}}
on left
R_{\sigma}
modules;

(ii)

{\rm{Tor}}^{1}_{R}(R_{\sigma},R_{\sigma})=0
;

(iii)

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}={\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R_{\sigma}}
on right
R_{\sigma}
modules.

Moreover,
[
S
, Proposition 1.2, XI]
implies the following equivalent conditions.

(i)

R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}R_{\sigma}\longrightarrow R_{\sigma}
is an isomorphism;

(ii)

R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}R_{\sigma}/\phi(R)=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
R
is a commutative ring and
\sigma\subseteq R
is a multiplicative set, then the classical localization of
R
at
\sigma
is an example of a universal localization. Indeed, we can view every element
x
of
\sigma
as a morphism
\lambda_{x}:R\longrightarrow R
in
R{\mbox{-}\rm{prj}}
, where
R{\mbox{-}\rm{prj}}
denotes the category of finitely generated projective
R
-modules. Moreover, if
R
is local, then the classes of universal localizations and classical localizations coincide. However, for non-local commutative rings, the class of universal localizations is larger than that of classical localizations, for instance, see
[
MS
, Example 4.5]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{R}
be the full subcategory of
R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}}
consisting of
R
-modules
X
such that
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(s,X)
is invertible for all
s\in\sigma
. Then there is an equivalence
\mathscr{R}\cong R_{\sigma}{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}}
,
[
Kr2
, §2.3]
.

2.2.
Calculus of fractions

In general, the universal localization
R_{\sigma}
is not a flat
R
-module. However, if we assume that
\sigma
admits a calculus of left (right) fractions, then
R_{\sigma}
is a left (right) flat
R
-module, see Remark
2.4
below. In this subsection, we recall the notion of calculus of fractions, which helps to describe explicitly the localization of categories with respect to the specific class of morphisms. For more details see for instance
[
Kr2
, §1.2]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category and
{\rm{Mor}}(\mathscr{C})
be the class of all morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
. Let
S\subseteq{\rm{Mor}}(\mathscr{C})
. We say that the class
S
admits a calculus of left fractions if the following conditions hold.

(i)

For every object
X
in
\mathscr{C}
, the identity morphism
1_{X}
is in
S
. The class
S
is closed under compositions of morphisms.

(ii)

Let
X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\leftarrow}}X\rightarrow Y
be a pair of morphisms such that
s\in S
. This can be completed to a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

such that
t\in S
.

(iii)

Let
f,g:X\rightarrow Y
be morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
. Assume that there exists a morphism
s:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
in
S
such that
fs=gs
. Then there exists a morphism
t:Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
in
S
such that
tf=tg
.

Dually, we say that the class
S
admits a calculus of right fractions if it admits a calculus of left fractions in the opposite category
\mathscr{C}^{\rm{op}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

As it is mentioned in the beginning of this subsection, it is known that if
\sigma
admits a calculus of left fractions, then
R_{\sigma}
is a flat
R
-module, see e.g. Lemma 1.2.2 of
[
Kr2
]
. In fact, in this case
R_{\sigma}
is a colimit of the copies of
R
. Throughout, we use this fact without further reference.

2.3.
\sigma
-strongly flat modules

Now we have the necessary background to define
\sigma
-strongly flat modules.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that an
R
-module
C
is
\sigma
-weakly cotorsion if
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{\sigma},C)=0
. The class of
\sigma
-weakly cotorsion left
R
-modules will be denoted by
{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{WC}}
. An
R
-module
F
is called
\sigma
-strongly flat if it is in
{}^{\perp}{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{WC}}
, where

{}^{\perp}{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{WC}}=\{M\in R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}}\ |\ {\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C)=0,\ {\rm for\ all}\ C\in{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{WC}}\}.

The class of
\sigma
-strongly flat
R
-modules will be denoted by
{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}}
. It follows from Theorem 6.11 of
[
GT
]
that the pair
({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}},{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{WC}})
is a complete cotorsion pair. Therefore, the class of
{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}}
is always precovering, while
{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{WC}}
is preenveloping.

2.6
.

Let
R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}}
denote the class of flat modules. The modules in the class

R\mbox{-}{\rm{Cot}}=R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}}^{\perp}=\{C\in R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}}\ |\ {\rm{Ext}}^{1}(F,C)=0,\ {\rm for\ all}\ F\in R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}}\}

are called cotorsion or Enochs cotorsion modules. It is known that
(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Flat}},R\mbox{-}{\rm{Cot}})
is a complete cotorsion pair.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
\sigma
admit a calculus of left fractions. So
R_{\sigma}
is a flat
R
-module. Hence, every (Enochs) cotorsion module is
\sigma
-weakly cotorsion. This, in turn, implies that every
\sigma
-strongly flat module is flat.

2.8
.

It follows from
[
GT
, Corollary 6.13]
that the class
{\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}}
consists of all summands of modules
G
such that
G
fits into an exact sequence of the form

0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0,

where
F
is a free
R
-module and
L
is
\{R_{\sigma}\}
-filtered.

In fact, the paragraph before the statement of Lemma 6.15 of
[
GT
]
and the fact that

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(P,Q)=0,

for every projective
R_{\sigma}
-modules
P,Q
, imply that a
\{R_{\sigma}\}
-filtered
R
-module is a free
R_{\sigma}
-module.
Thus every
\sigma
-strongly flat module
F
is a direct summand of
R
-module
G
such that
G
fits into an exact sequence of the form

0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0

where
F
is free
R
-module and
L
is a free
R_{\sigma}
-module, see also the paragraph above Lemma 3.4 of
[
FN1
]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that
\sigma
admits a calculus of left fractions. Let
M
be a
\sigma
-strongly flat
R
-module. Then

R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}M
is a projective
R_{\sigma}
-module.

Proof.

Since
M
is a
\sigma
-strongly flat
R
-module then it is a direct summand of an
R
-module
G
, where
G
fits into the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R^{(\alpha)}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow R_{\sigma}^{(\beta)}\longrightarrow 0.

Now by tensoring the above sequence by
R_{\sigma}
, we get the result.
∎

Setup 2.10
.

Toward the end of this section, let
R
be a commutative ring and let
\sigma
be a set of maps between finitely generated projective
R
-modules. Note that by Remark
2.1
, the class of universal localizations is larger than that of classical localizations.

2.11
.

An
R
-module
M
is called
\sigma
-contramodule if it is
\sigma
-
h
-reduced and
\sigma
-weakly cotorsion, that is if,

{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M)=0={\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M).

An
R
-module
M
is called
\sigma
-
h
-divisible if it is a quotient
R
-module of an
R_{\sigma}
-module, or equivalently, if every homomorphism
R\longrightarrow M
extends to a morphism
R_{\sigma}\longrightarrow M
. Every
R
-module
M
has a unique maximal
\sigma
-
h
-divisible submodule
h_{\sigma}(M)\subset M
which can be constructed as the image of the natural map
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M)\longrightarrow M
, see
[
P2
, §1]
.

Following the argument used in
[
BP
]
, we denote by
K^{\bullet}
the two-term complex of
R
-modules

R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{\sigma}

where the nonzero
R
-modules are in degrees
-1
and
0
. Therefore, we see the complex
K^{\bullet}
as an object of the derived category
\mathbb{D}(R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}})
.

We use the special notation

{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{R}(K^{\bullet},M)={\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{D}(R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}})}(K^{\bullet},M[n]),~~~~n\in\mathbb{Z},

for any
R
-module
M
.

We have a distinguished triangle

R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{\sigma}\longrightarrow K^{\bullet}\longrightarrow R[1]

in the derived category
\mathbb{D}(R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}})
.

By applying the functor
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{D}(R{\mbox{-}\rm{Mod}})}(-,M[*])
on the above triangle, we have the following exact sequence,

(I)

0\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{0}_{R}(K^{\bullet},M)\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(K^{\bullet},M)\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M)\longrightarrow 0,

which can be rewritten in the form of two short exact sequences

(II)

0\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{0}_{R}(K^{\bullet},M)\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M)\longrightarrow h_{\sigma}(M)\longrightarrow 0,

(III)

0\longrightarrow M/h_{\sigma}(M)\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(K^{\bullet},M)\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(R_{\sigma},M)\longrightarrow 0.

Using a similar argument as in the proof of
[
P2
, Lemma 1.7 (c) and Lemma 2.4]
, we can state the following lemma. Therefore, we will omit the proof.

Lemma 2.12
.

The following holds true.

(i)

Assume that the projective dimension of
R_{\sigma}
as an
R
-module is at most 1. Then, for every
R
-module
X
, the
R
-module
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(K^{\bullet},X)
is
\sigma
-contramodule.

(ii)

Assume that
M
is an
R
-module such that
R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}M=0
. Then, for every
R
-module
X
, the
R
-module
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(M,X)
is
\sigma
-contramodule.

The following proposition provides a characterization of
\sigma
-strongly flat modules. Its proof is similar to the proof of Lemma 7.2 of
[
BP
]
, which proves a similar result in the classical setting. For the reader’s convenience, we provide a proof.

Proposition 2.13
.

Assume that the projective dimension of
R_{\sigma}
as an
R
-module is at most 1. Let
M
be an
R
-module such that
R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}M
is a projective
R_{\sigma}
-module. Then the
R
-module
M
is
\sigma
-strongly flat if and only if
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,X)=0={\rm{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(M,X)
, for every
\sigma
-contramodule
R
-module
X
.

Proof.

Since the projective dimension of
R_{\sigma}
is at most 1, it follows from
2.8
that the projective dimension of every
R_{\sigma}
-strongly flat
R
-module is also at most 1. Thus, the necessity condition follows.

To demonstrate the sufficient condition, let
C
be a
\sigma
-weakly cotorsion
R
-module. We aim to show that
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C)=0
.
Consider short exact sequences

(1)

0\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma}/\phi(R),C)\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},C)\longrightarrow h_{\sigma}(C)\longrightarrow 0,

and

(2)

0\longrightarrow h_{\sigma}(C)\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow C/h_{\sigma}(C)\longrightarrow 0,

where the short exact sequence (
1
) is obtained by using the isomorphism
{\rm{Ext}}^{0}_{R}(K^{\bullet},C)\cong{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma}/\phi(R),C)
of the short exact sequence (
II
).
Since
C
is
\sigma
-weakly cotorsion, we conclude from the sequence (
I
) that

C/h_{\sigma}(C)\cong{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(K^{\bullet},C).

Since projective dimension of
R_{\sigma}
is at most 1, Lemma
2.12

(i)
implies that
C/h_{\sigma}(C)
is
\sigma
-contramodule.
Moreover, since
R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}R_{\sigma}/\phi(R)=0
, Lemma
2.12

(ii)
implies that the
R
-module
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma}/\phi(R),C)
is a
\sigma
-contramodule.
Therefore, by assumption

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C/h_{\sigma}(C))=0={\rm{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(M,{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma}/\phi(R),C)).

On the other hand, from the exact sequence (
1
), we get

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},C))\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,h_{\sigma}(C))\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(M,{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma}/\phi(R),C))=0.

But since by the assumption
R_{\sigma}\otimes_{R}M
is projective,
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(R_{\sigma},C))=0
. Thus, we obtain
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,h_{\sigma}(C))=0
. Now from the exact sequence (
2
), we get the exact sequence

0={\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,h_{\sigma}(C))\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C)\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C/h_{\sigma}(C))=0.

Hence, the result follows, that is,
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(M,C)=0
.
∎

3.
Homotopy category of
\sigma
-strongly flat modules

According to
[
N2
, Facts 2.8 (i)]
, we know that the homotopy category of projective
R
-modules, denoted
\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})
, is a well-generated triangulated category and thus satisfies Brown representability. Furthermore, the natural inclusion
e:\mathbb{K}(R\mbox{-}{\rm{Prj}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}({\sigma\text{-}\mathcal{SF}})
preserves coproducts. Hence, by
[
N1
, Theorem 8.4.4]
, we can conclude that the inclusion
e
has a right adjoint

Remark 3.1

It is proved by Neeman

.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)

Remark 3.5

Lemma 3.6

Let

Consider the exact sequence

Since

∎

Lemma 3.7

Every

-strongly flat

Proof.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\sigma
admit a calculus of left fractions. Let

be a
\Sigma
-strongly flat

-module. Then the complex

First, we note that since

. Consequently,

4.1

(v)

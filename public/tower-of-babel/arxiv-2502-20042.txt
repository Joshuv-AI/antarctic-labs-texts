Abstract
. In this paper, we study the stochastic degenerate Keller–Segel system perturbed by linear multiplicative noise in a bounded domain 𝒪 \mathcal{O} . We establish the global existence of martingale solutions for this model with any nonnegative initial data in H 2 − 1 ​ ( 𝒪 ) H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}) . The main challenge in proving the existence of solutions arises from the degeneracy of the porous media diffusion and the lack of coercivity in the nonlinear chemotactic term. To overcome these difficulties, we construct a solution operator and apply the Schauder fixed point theorem within the variational framework.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the following stochastic Keller-Segel equations with degenerate diffusion

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\rho=[\Delta{(|\rho|^{m-1}\rho)}-\chi\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi*\rho)]dt+\sigma\rho dW(t),&x\in\mathcal{O},t\geq 0,\\
\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0},&x\in\mathcal{O},t=0,\end{cases}

where the diffusion exponent
m\geq 3
, the constant
\sigma>0
, and
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d=2,3
, and a smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
.
Here,
\rho(x,t)
represents the bacterial density, while the interaction potential
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is either the Newtonian or Bessel potential. The term
-\rho\nabla\Phi*\rho
represents the chemotactic flux, which describes the transport of bacteria in the direction of the chemical gradient. This essentially produces
-\chi\nabla\cdotp(\rho\nabla\Phi*\rho)
, illustrating cross-diffusive effects in the model, where the positive constant
\chi
represents the chemotactic sensitivity.

The linear operator
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian with Dirichlet (or Neumann) boundary condition. It is well
known that
-\Delta
is self-adjoint positive and anti-compact. We assume that
\left\{e_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is the orthonormal basis in
L^{2}{(\mathcal{O})}
composed of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
. We denote by
\left\{\lambda_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
the corresponding
sequence of eigenvalues

\displaystyle-\Delta e_{k}=\lambda_{k}e_{k}\text{,\quad}k\in\mathbb{N}.

We shall consider a cylindrical Wiener process
W(t)
on
L^{2}{(\mathcal{O})}
of the form

\displaystyle W(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{k}\beta_{k}\left(t\right),~~t\geq 0,

where
\left\{\beta_{k}\right\}
is a sequence of mutually independent standard Brownian motions on a right-continuous complete filtration probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and
\rho dW(t)
is a linear multiplicative noise.

Since the bacteria density
\rho(x,t)
has to be non-negative, the initial condition
\rho_{0}
has to be non-negative. Then the model possesses the initial data
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))
, which is a random variable over
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and satisfies
(i)
\rho_{0}\geq 0
;
(ii)
\rho_{0}
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
–measurable;
(iii)
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\right]<\infty
.

and

1.2
) indicates the linear random motion in
[
8
,
9
,

proved that there is a critical mass such that the solution to (

The macroscopic Patlak–Keller–Segel model is based on the limiting behavior of the microscopic model see
[
37
]
. Nevertheless, in the process of deriving the macroscopic equations mentioned above, variations from the average value are overlooked. Additionally, in natural systems, random disturbances and environmental noise are unavoidable. Meanwhile, random disturbances and the nonlinearity term can lead to alterations in dynamic behavior. Therefore, the model needs to incorporate random spatio-temporal influences. Introducing a Gaussian random field to the system restores its natural randomness. This makes us to explore the traditional Patlak–Keller–Segel system influenced by the random term.

Introducing a random term (or noise) into a partial differential equation causes a variety of new phenomena and can significantly affect the behavior of solutions. Let us consider the stochastic aggregation-diﬀusion equation of
Keller–Segel type

(1.3)

\displaystyle d\rho=[\Delta{\rho}-\chi\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi*\rho)]dt+\sigma(\rho)dW(t),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\geq 0.

For
d=2,3
, Huang and Qiu in
[
24
]
studied the equation (
1.3
) with the Bessel potential and
\sigma(\rho)=\rho
. The authors derived the stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) as the large particle limit of a system with shared noise (in divergence form), and use a contraction argument similar to that in
[
14

to demonstrate the local existence and uniqueness of weak solutions over the interval
[0,T]
, with

depending on the

-norm of the initial data. Additionally, they established that if the

-norm of the initial data is sufficiently small, the equation has a global weak solution. In
[
30

, Misiats and Stanzhytskyi proved that the different types of random disturbances influence the properties of solutions to the stochastic Keller–Segel equation (
1.3

\sigma(\rho)=\nabla\rho
or
\sigma(\rho)=\rho
. If the noise is in divergence form, the equation has a
global weak solution for small initial data. Furthermore, if the noise is not in divergence form, the authors showed that the solution has a ﬁnite time
blow-up (with nonzero probability) for any nonzero initial data. In
[
22
]
, Hausenblas, Mukherjee and Tran demonstrated that a martingale solution exists for the one-dimensional stochastic version of the Patlak–Keller–Segel system with the noise interpreted in the Stratonvich sense.

The stochastic porous media equation has been recently studied under various assumptions regarding the drift, including both additive and multiplicative noise. Consider the stochastic porous media equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\rho-\Delta\Psi(\rho)dt=\rho dW(t),&(0,T)\times\mathcal{O},\\
\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0},&t=0\times\mathcal{O}.\end{cases}

Equation (
1.4
) was studied under different assumptions on the operator
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. In
[
4
]
, the authors proved that if
\Psi
is monotone and has polynomial growth, the stochastic porous media equation has a unique nonnegative solution for nonnegative initial data in
H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
, the existence results for equation (
1.4

[
1
,
2
,
5
,
35
]
. For more general nonlinear conditions, not assuming continuity of the drift
or any growth condition at infinity, the reference
[
3
]
showed the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions of the stochastic porous media equations. For the special case
\Psi(\rho)=\rho|\rho|^{m-1}
,
m\in(1,\infty)

[
16
,
17
,
20
]
. For stochastic porous media equation driven by an infinite dimensional Wiener process, the variational approach, which has been established by Pardoux, Krylov and Rozovskii
[
26
,
32
]
, has been used intensively to prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions. The main criterion for this approach to work is that the coefﬁcients satisfy certain monotonicity assumptions.

But very little work has been done on the stochastic Keller-Segel model with degenerate diffusion term. Recently in
[
23
]
, Hausenblas, Mukherjee, and Zakaria proved that the stochastic chemotaxis system perturbed by a pair of Wiener processes exists a martingale solution on a two-dimensional domain and the integral is interpreted in the Stratonovich sense. Here, both the cell density and the concentration of the chemoattractant are influenced by time-homogeneous spatial Wiener processes.

With respect to the situations considered above, in the present work we study the stochastic degenerate Keller-Segel system perturbed by the multiplicative Itô noise on two and three dimensional domain. To our knowledge, this particular case has not been explored previously. Because of the nonlinear characteristics of the diffusion term in (
1.1
), the semigroup approach cannot be used for the stochastic equation. As a result, the traditional method for showing the existence and uniqueness of solutions is not applicable in this case. Due to a lack of coercivity of the nonlinear chemotactic term, it is impossiblet to apply the variational approach directly.

In this paper, according to the method of
[
23
,
28
]
, we establish the global existence of martingale solutions for the equation (
1.1
), in which the interaction kernel
\Phi(x)
can represent the Newtonian or Bessel potential. We formulate a solution operator, analyzing the solution operator is well-defined under the variational approach and confirming solution operator’s continuity and compactness within a suitably selected Banach space. Furthermore, we use a stochastic version of the Schauder fixed point theorem similar to that in
[
23
]
, which is speciﬁc to our problem in order to find solutions on the interval
[0,T]
, for
T>0
. In the process of proving the existence of solutions, we employ compactness arguments, which results in the original probability space being lost. Consequently, this implies that the solution will only be considered a weak solution in a probabilistic sense.

In this work, we present results on global existence of solutions when the diffusion exponent
m\geq 3
. Compared with the classical degenerate Patlak-Keller-Segel equation without noise term, the diffusion exponent is different. Because the stochastic aggregation-diffusion equation of Keller-Segel type in two-dimensional space has a blow-up with nonzero probability for any nontrivial initial conditions, which is different from the classical Keller-Segel model with linear diffusion. Thus, we guess that the appearance of noise term may be more beneficial for blow-up of solutions. Thus, choosing
m\geq 3
is reasonable.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce fundamental definitions related to function spaces and linear operator. Additionally, we present the variational approach for solving stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) in infinite-dimensional spaces.

2.1.
Gelfand triple

First, we recall the Schwartz space, which serves as a fundamental tool for defining the Fourier transform of functions. Let
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of smooth functions. Define the Schwartz space

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|\phi\|_{k,\mathcal{S}}<\infty,~~\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

the seminorms is defined by

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{k,\mathcal{S}}=\sup_{|\alpha|\leq k,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{k}|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(x)|,

where
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}:=\left\{\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\alpha_{d})\right\}
, which is the set of all multi-indices and
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\alpha_{d}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\partial^{\alpha}\phi=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\partial^{\alpha_{d}}_{x_{d}}\phi
.

Equipped with the family of seminorms
(|\phi|_{k,\mathcal{S}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, the space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
forms a Fr
\acute{e}
chet space. Furthermore, we introduce the Schwartz tempered distribution space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where a tempered distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is any continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of smooth functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
is a continuous linear operator from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to itself. Both
\mathcal{F}
and its inverse are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int e^{-i\xi\cdot x}f(x)dx~,~\mathcal{F}^{-1}[f](x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}f(x)d\xi.

for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The Fourier transform can be extended
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the sense that for
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it holds

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}[u],f\rangle=\langle u,\mathcal{F}[f]\rangle,\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The Bessel potential for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
is denoted by

\displaystyle J^{s}(u):=(1-\Delta)^{s/2}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{s/2}\mathcal{F}[u]\right],

for
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The Bessel potential space
H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):(1-\Delta)^{s/2}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\},

with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H_{p}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}:=\left\|(1-\Delta)^{s/2}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Presently,
H_{p}^{s}(\mathcal{O})
is the restriction of
H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{O}
defined by

\displaystyle H_{p}^{s}(\mathcal{O}):=\left\{f:f=g|_{\mathcal{O}},g\in{H_{p}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\|f\|_{H_{p}^{s}(\mathcal{O})}=\operatorname*{inf}\|g\|_{H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},

and,

H_{0}^{{1},{p}}(\mathcal{O}):=
completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{H_{p}^{1}(\mathcal{O})}.

For
1<p<\infty,m\in\mathbb{N}
, the above Bessel potential spaces
H_{p}^{m}
can be characterized as Sobolev spaces

\displaystyle W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{W^{m,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A},|\alpha|\leq m}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}<\infty\Big\}.

Let
(U,\left\langle,\right\rangle_{U})
and
(H,\left\langle,\right\rangle_{H})
be two separable Hilbert spaces. The space of
all bounded linear operators from
{U}
to
H
is denoted by
L({U},H)
. A bounded linear operator

T:{U}\rightarrow H

is called Hilbert–Schmidt operator if

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|Te_{k}\|_{H}^{2}\leq\infty,

where
\left\{e_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
{U}
. The space of all Hilbert–Schmidt operators from
{U}
to
{H}
is denoted by
L_{2}({U},H)
. Then the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}({U},H)}
is represented by

\displaystyle\|T\|_{L_{2}({U},{H})}^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|Te_{k}\|_{{H}}^{2}.

One of the main points in applying the variational approach is to find a suitable Gelfand triple, which is defined below. Let
V
be a reﬂexive Banach space such that
V\subset H
continuously and densely. Let
H^{*}
be the dual space of
H
and
V^{*}
be the dual space of
V
, it follows that
H^{*}\subset V^{*}
continuously and densely. Identifying
H
and
H^{*}
via the Riesz isomorphism, we have that

\displaystyle V\subset H\equiv H^{*}\subset V^{*}

continuously and densely. If
{}_{V^{*}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}
denotes the dualization between
V^{*}
and
V
, it follows that

(2.1)

{}_{V^{*}}\langle z,v\rangle_{V}=\left\langle z,v\right\rangle_{H},\forall z\in H,v\in V,

(V,H,V^{*})
is called a Gelfand triple.

Well-posedness for general SPDEs

In this section, we present some general results on the existence and uniqueness of solutions to stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) based on a given Gelfand triple

Let

,

\displaystyle d\rho(t)=A(t,\rho,\omega)dt+B(t,\rho,\omega)dW(t),

\displaystyle A:[0,\infty)\times V\times\Omega\to V^{*},~B:[0,\infty)\times V\times\Omega\to L_{2}(U,H)

[

Suppose that there exist constants

(Hemicontinuity) The map

(H2)
(Local monotonicity)

\displaystyle 2_{V^{*}}\langle A(t,u)-A(t,v),u-v\rangle_{V}+\|B(t,u)-B(t,v)\|_{L_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\leq(f(t)+h(v))\|u-v\|_{H}^{2},

\displaystyle 2_{V^{*}}\langle A(t,v),u\rangle_{V}+\|B(t,v)\|_{L_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\leq C_{0}\|v\|_{H}^{2}-\theta\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}+f(t).

(H4)

\displaystyle\|A(t,v)\|_{V^{*}}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\leq(f(t)+C_{0}\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha})(1+\|v\|_{H}^{\beta}).

Based on these conditions, in

Lemma 2.1

(

Then for any

\displaystyle\rho\left(t\right)\in L^{\alpha}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\Omega,dt\otimes\mathbb{P};V\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\Omega,dt\otimes\mathbb{P};H\right),

where

is consistent with that in

and
\rho(t)
satisifies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho\|_{H}^{p}\Big]<\infty.

C>0

\displaystyle\|e_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|e_{k}\|_{H_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\Delta e_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\lambda_{k}.

Then, we get by elementary computations that

\displaystyle\|\rho e_{k}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\leq C^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}\|\rho\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}.

Defining

\displaystyle(B(\rho),\varphi):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma\mu_{k}\left\langle e_{k},\varphi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\rho{e}_{k},\rho\in L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O}),\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),

where
\left\{\mu_{k}\right\}_{k}
is a sequence of positive numbers. Throughout this paper we shall assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\mu_{k}}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}:=C_{1}<\infty

For
\rho\in L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})\subset H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|B(\rho)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\sigma\mu_{k}\rho e_{k}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\leq C_{1}\|\rho\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\leq C\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})},

i.e., the operator
B
is a map given by

B:[0,\infty)\times L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})\times\Omega\to L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})).

Therefore, we define

\displaystyle B(\rho):L^{2}(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}),

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle B(\rho)dW(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma\mu_{k}e_{k}\rho(t)d\beta_{k}\left(t\right).

Furthermore, by the definition of
B
and (
2.7
), we have that
B
is linear and Lipschitz continuous.

For the operator
A(\rho)
, due to the lack of coercivity in the nonlinear chemotactic term
\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi\ast\rho)
, it is challenging to directly verify that

A:[0,\infty)\times L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})\times\Omega\to(L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O}))^{*}.

To overcome this difficulty, later we will construct a solution operator and apply the Schauder fixed point theorem within the variational framework to establish the existence of solutions.

3.
Global Existence of weak solutions to SPDE (
1.1
)

3.1.
Preparatory work

The chemotactic term
\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\Phi\ast\rho)
of the model
(
1.1
) arises as a gradient flow of a potential
\Phi
.
The convolution is to be understand in
\mathcal{O}
, which be defined as

\displaystyle\Phi*\rho(x):=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi(x-y)\rho(y)dy,~~x\in\mathcal{O}.

Here, we consider the interaction potential
\Phi
is Newtonian or Bessel potential. By direct calculations, we get the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Phi
be the interaction potential. Then the following holds.

(i)

Let
\Phi(x)
be the Newtonian potential, then

\|\nabla\Phi\ast\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})},~\text{for }~m+1\geq d;

(ii)

Let
\Phi(x)
be the Bessel potential, then

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\ast\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})},~\text{for }~m+1>2.

Proof.

(i) If
\Phi(x)
is the Newtonian potential

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d(d-2)\alpha(d)}|x|^{2-d},~&d\geq 3,\\
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|,&~d=2,\end{cases}

where
\alpha(d)
is the volume of
d
-dimension unit ball, we have the estimates

(3.1)

\displaystyle|\nabla\Phi(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{d-1}},~|\Delta\Phi(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{d}},~(x\neq 0)

for some constant
C>0.

Furthermore, for
m+1\geq d
, using the weak Young inequality, it holds that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\ast\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\frac{1}{|x|^{d-1}}\|_{L_{w}^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(\mathcal{O})}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{d}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}.

(ii) If
\Phi(x)
is the Bessel potential (see
[
36
]
)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}-t}dt,

then it satisfies
\left|\nabla\Phi(x)\right|\sim|x|^{-d+1}
as
|x|\to 0.

Following
[
27
]
, we can directly get that
\Phi(x)
satisﬁes

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\Phi(x)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty,1\leq p<\infty,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi(x)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty,1\leq p<2.

Based on the properties of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) for the Bessel potential, we know that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi\ast\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\leq\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\frac{m+1}{m}}(\mathcal{O})}\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})},~2<m+1<\infty.

∎

Next, we introduce the following definitions of some Banach spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

For Banach space
\left(X,\|\cdot\|_{X}\right)
, for all
q\geq 1
and
0\leq t<\tau\leq T
, let
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{q}([t,\tau];X)
be the space of
X
-valued,
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted and continuous processes
\left\{\xi_{s},s\in[t,\tau]\right\}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{S_{\mathcal{F}}^{q}([t,\tau];X)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[t,\tau]}\left\|\xi_{s}\right\|_{X}^{q}\right)^{1/q},\quad q\in[1,\infty);\\
\operatorname{ess}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{s\in[t,\tau]}\left\|\xi_{s}\right\|_{X},\quad q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
L_{\mathcal{F}}^{q}([t,\tau];X)
be the space of
X
-valued, predictable processes
\left\{\xi_{s},s\in[t,\tau]\right\}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{L_{\mathcal{F}}^{q}([t,\tau];X)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{\tau}\left\|\xi_{s}\right\|_{X}^{q}\mathrm{~d}s\right)^{1/q},\quad q\in[1,\infty);\\
\operatorname{ess}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{s\in[t,\tau]}\left\|\xi_{s}\right\|_{X},\quad q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Note that both
S_{\mathcal{F}}^{q}
and
L_{\mathcal{F}}^{q}
are Banach spaces. With this, we can now formulate the concept of martingale solutions to the SPDE (
1.1
).

Definition 3.3
.

(Martingale solutions)
Let
T>0
, a martingale solution of the equation (
1.1
) is a triple

\displaystyle\left((\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}),\rho,(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}\right)

such that

\mathfrak{A}:=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
is a probability space with a complete, right-continuous filtration;

W(t)
is a cylindrical Wiener process on
L^{2}\left(\mathcal{O}\right)
over the probability
space
\mathfrak{A}
;

\rho(t,\omega)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable process such that
\mathbb{P}
-
a.s.
,

\displaystyle\rho\left(t\right)\in L_{\mathcal{F}}^{m+1}\left(\left[0,T\right],L^{m+1}\left(\mathcal{O}\right)\right)\cap S_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right],H_{2}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}\right)\right),

and it satisfies equation (
1.1
) over the probability
space
\mathfrak{A}
.

3.2.
Existence of weak solutions

In this section, we establish the existence of martingale solutions to the equation (
1.1
). First, we construct an integral operator on a suitable function space and demonstrate its continuity and compactness. Then, we formulate a stochastic version of the Schauder fixed point theorem to prove the existence of martingale solutions.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
m\geq 3
, let
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))
satisfy the condition

\mathbb{E}[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}]<\infty.

Then, there exists a martingale solution

\left((\Omega,\mathcal{F},\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}),\rho,(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}\right)

to the equation (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.3
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}ds\Big]\leq C,

and

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}+m^{2}(m+1)\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho^{m-1}(t)\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt\Big]\leq C.

Proof.

Since the model (
1.1
) contains chemotactic and degenerate diffusion terms, which are both nonlinear, thus we prove Theorem 3.1 using the Schauder fixed point theorem.

Deﬁne the spaces

\displaystyle S_{T}:=\left\{\xi=\xi(x,t,\omega)\in S_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}([0,T];H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})):\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}}^{2}\Big]<\infty\right\},

and its subspace

\displaystyle X_{T}:=\left\{\xi\in S_{T}:\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+4\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}dt\Big]\leq R_{1};\right.

\displaystyle\left.\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}+m^{2}(m+1)\int_{0}^{T}\||\xi(t)|^{m-1}\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\Big]\leq R_{2}\right\}.

Let us deﬁne the operator
\mathcal{T}
acting on
S_{T}
as follows. For
\xi\in S_{T}
, let
\rho:=\mathcal{T}\xi
solve the following model

(3.7)

\displaystyle d\rho=\bar{A}(\rho)dt+B(\rho)dW(t),

where

\bar{A}(\rho):=\Delta{(|\rho|^{m-1}\rho)}-\chi\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi),~~B(\rho):=\sigma\rho.

Firstly, according to below Proposition
3.1
, the operator is well deﬁned on
S_{T}
. Secondly, according to Proposition
3.2
, the operator
\mathcal{T}
maps
X_{T}
into itself; Next,
Proposition
3.3
shows that the operator
\mathcal{T}
is a continuity map from
X_{T}
into
X_{T}
; Finally, Proposition
3.4
proves the operator
\mathcal{T}
maps
X_{T}
to a precompact set.

Hence, for any
\xi\in X_{T}
there exists a
\rho:=\mathcal{T}\xi
and
\rho
is the unique solution to the model (
3.7
). In this way, we formulate Schauder fixed point theorem and show that there exists a solution
\rho\in X_{T}
to the model (
1.1
).
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

For
m\geq 3
, fixed
\xi\in X_{T}
and for all
T>0
, there exists a solution
\rho
to the model (
3.7
) such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds\Big]<\infty.

Proof.

The main process of this proof is to verify that
\bar{A}(\rho)
and
B(\rho)
satisfy hypothesis
(H1)-(H4)
, and use Lemma
2.1
to get the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the model (
3.7
) with
m\geq 3
. Let us consider the Gelfand triple (
2.3
)

(3.8)

\displaystyle V:=L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})\subset H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})\equiv H_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{O})\subset(L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O}))^{*}=:V^{*}.

Noticing

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi)\right\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\left\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})},

applying Lemma
3.1
and the Sobolev embedding theorem
L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})\subset L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
for
m\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi)\right\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}<\infty,

which implies

\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi)\in H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})\text{,\quad}\forall\xi\in L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O}).

Fixed
t\in[0,T]
,
\omega\in\Omega
, for any
w\in V
, by (
2.1
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi),w\rangle_{V}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi),w\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}\right|\leq\|\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}\|w\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}\leq C\|\xi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{2}\|w\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}.

Next, using (
2.5
) and (
3.9
), we derive that

\displaystyle\left|{}_{V^{*}}\langle\bar{A}(\rho),w\rangle_{V}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|-\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(x)|^{m-1}\rho(x)w(x)dx-\chi_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi),w\rangle_{V}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m}\|w\|_{L^{m+1}}+\chi\|\xi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m}+\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{2})\|w\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}.

Now, we will verify
\bar{A}(\rho)
and
B(\rho)
satisfy hypothesis
(H1)-(H4)
successively.

Veriﬁcation of
(H1)
(Hemicontinuity):
Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},w\in V,t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega
. We need to show that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},|\lambda|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto_{V^{*}}\langle\bar{A}(\rho_{1}+\lambda\rho_{2}),w\rangle_{V}

is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, by the definition of

, we know that

\displaystyle|_{V^{*}}\langle\bar{A}(\rho_{1}+\lambda\rho_{2})-\bar{A}(\rho_{1}),w\rangle_{V}|\leq\|(\rho_{1}+\lambda\rho_{2})^{m}-\rho_{1}^{m}\|_{V^{*}}\|w\|_{V}.

Due to

, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}|_{V^{*}}\langle\bar{A}(\rho_{1}+\lambda\rho_{2})-\bar{A}(\rho_{1}),w\rangle_{V}|=0.

Hence,
(H1)
holds.

Veriﬁcation of
(H2)
(Local monotonicity).
Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in V,~t\in[0,T],~\omega\in\Omega
. Exploiting the fact that
(|\rho_{1}|^{m-1}\rho_{1}-|\rho_{2}|^{m-1}\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})\geq|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|^{m+1}~(m\geq 1)
, we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{O}}(|\rho_{1}|^{m-1}\rho_{1}-|\rho_{2}|^{m-1}\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})dx\leq-\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|^{m+1}dx=-\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}.

Then, by (
2.5
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle 2_{V^{*}}\langle\bar{A}(\rho_{1})-\bar{A}(\rho_{2}),\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\rangle_{V}+\|B(\rho_{1})-B(\rho_{2})\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathcal{O}}(|\rho_{1}|^{m-1}\rho_{1}-|\rho_{2}|^{m-1}\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})dx+\|\sigma\rho_{1}-\sigma\rho_{2}\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+C\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}.

Hence,
\bar{A}(\rho)
and
B(\rho)
satisfy
(H2)
with
f(t)=C,~h(v)=0
.

Veriﬁcation of
(H3)
(Coercivity):
Let
\rho\in V,t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega
. Then, by (
3.10
) and the Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle 2_{V^{*}}\langle\bar{A}(\rho),\rho\rangle_{V}+\|B(\rho)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho|^{m-1}\rho^{2}dx-\chi_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi),\rho\rangle_{V}+\|\sigma\rho\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+\chi\|\xi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}+C\|\rho\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+C\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{4}+C\|\rho\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}.

Set

, we are easy to check

. In fact, if

, then
\frac{4}{m+1}\leq 1
, by the Hölder inequality in time and the Jessen inequality, for

, we know

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{4}dt\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[T^{\frac{m-3}{m+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big)^{\frac{4}{m+1}}\Big]\leq C(T)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]^{\frac{4}{m+1}}.

Hence,
(H3)
holds for

.

Veriﬁcation of
(H4)
(Growth):
Let
\rho\in V,t\in[0,T],\omega\in\Omega
. Using the continuous embedding of

\displaystyle\|\bar{A}(\rho)\|_{V^{*}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}}\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+\chi\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{\frac{2(m+1)}{m}}).

Set

, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{\frac{2(m+1)}{m}}dt\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[T^{\frac{m-2}{m}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big)^{\frac{2}{m}}\Big]\leq C(T)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]^{\frac{2}{m}}.

, we can infer that

.

In the above discussion we have shown that the hypothesis

Proposition 3.2
.

For all
\xi\in X_{T}
, let

be
the unique solution of the model (

) with

, then there exist numbers

\mathcal{T}
maps

into itself.

Proof.

We prove the proposition in two steps, by applying the Itô formula to the function. In the Step 1, we get an estimate on
\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\Big]

. In the Step 2, we get an estimate on
\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}\Big]

and

, which gives

.

Step 1: Applying the Itô formula (
[

, Theorem 4.2.5) to

, for

, we have that

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}=

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma\rho(s)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2},H_{2}^{-1})}^{2}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s),\sigma\rho(s)dW(s)\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds+2\chi\int_{0}^{t}\|\xi(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi(s)*\xi(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho(s)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}ds

(3.12)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}\|\rho(s)e_{k}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}ds+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s),\sigma\rho(s)e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s).

Taking supremum over

and the expectation from both sides of (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi(t)*\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}dt\Big]+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}dt\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s),\sigma\rho(s)e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s)\Big|\Big].

First, applying Lemma
3.1
and the Young inequality to estimate the first term on the right of (

), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi(t)*\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{2}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{4}+C\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}dt\Big]

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon T^{\frac{m-3}{m+1}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]^{\frac{4}{m+1}}+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}dt\Big].

Next, the last term on the right of (
3.13
) can be estimated by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (
[
15
]
, Theorem 4.36) and the Young inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s),\sigma\rho(s)e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s)\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\|\sigma\rho(t)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2},H_{2}^{-1})}^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma\mu_{k}e_{k}\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}dt\Big].

Applying the estimates (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) to (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}+\varepsilon T^{\frac{m-3}{m+1}}R_{1}^{\frac{4}{m+1}}+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}dt\Big].

Using Gronwall lemma yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]+4\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[2\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}+\varepsilon T^{\frac{m-3}{m+1}}R_{1}^{\frac{4}{m+1}}\Big]e^{CT}\leq C(T)\Big[\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}+\varepsilon T^{\frac{m-3}{m+1}}R_{1}^{\frac{4}{m+1}}\Big]\leq R_{1},

where we choose
R_{1}
large enough such that the last inequality holds.

Step 2: Estimate
\|\rho(t)\|_{{L^{m+1}}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}
by the Itô formula.

Applying the Itô formula (
[
5
]
, Proposition 1.2.3) to
\|\rho(t)\|_{{L^{m+1}}(\mathcal{O})}^{m+1}

and using standard calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}=

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+(m+1)\sigma\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho^{2}(s)e_{k}dxd\beta_{k}(s)

\displaystyle-(m+1)\chi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s)\nabla\cdot(\xi\nabla\Phi*\xi)dxds

\displaystyle+(m+1)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s)\Delta(|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s))dxds

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}m(m+1)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m-1}|\rho(s)e_{k}|^{2}dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+(m+1)\sigma\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m+1}e_{k}dxd\beta_{k}(s)

\displaystyle+(m+1)\chi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla(|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s))\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)dxds

(3.17)

\displaystyle-m^{2}(m+1)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\rho^{2m-2}(s)|\nabla\rho(s)|^{2}dxds+C(\sigma,m)\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds.

The third term on the right of (
3.17
) is done with the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle(m+1)\chi\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla(|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s))\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m(m+1)\chi\||\rho(s)|^{m-1}\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)\|_{L^{2}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}(m+1)}{2}\||\rho(s)|^{m-1}\nabla\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C(\chi)\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}.

Furthermore, putting (
3.18
) into (
3.17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+\frac{m^{2}(m+1)}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\||\rho(s)|^{m-1}\nabla\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+C(\chi)\int_{0}^{t}\|\xi(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}ds+C(\sigma,m)\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds

(3.19)

\displaystyle+(m+1)\sigma\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m+1}e_{k}dxd\beta_{k}(s).

Taking supremum over
[0,T]
and the expectation for (
3.19
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+\frac{m^{2}(m+1)}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\||\rho(t)|^{m-1}\nabla\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+C(\chi)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}dt\Big]+C(\sigma,m)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

(3.20)

\displaystyle+(m+1)\sigma\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m+1}e_{k}dxd\beta_{k}(s)\Big|\Big].

For the last term on the right of (
3.20
), using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality and the Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle(m+1)\sigma\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(s)|^{m+1}e_{k}dxd\beta_{k}(s)\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\rho(t)|^{m+1}dx\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big].

Bring the estimate (
3.21
) into (
3.20
) and using Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+\frac{m^{2}(m+1)}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\||\rho(t)|^{m-1}\nabla\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+C(\chi)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{4}dt\Big]+C(\sigma,m)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(s)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+C(\chi,T)R_{1}^{\frac{4}{m+1}}+C(\sigma,m)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(s)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big].

Using Gronwall lemma, we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+m^{2}(m+1)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\||\rho(t)|^{m-1}\nabla\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\Big]

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[2\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}+C(\chi,T)R_{1}^{\frac{4}{m+1}}+C(\sigma,m)\Big]e^{CT}\leq R_{2},

where we choose
R_{2}
large enough such that the last inequality holds.

Combining the inequalities (
3.16
) and (
3.22
), we have shown that there exist
R_{1},~R_{2}>0
such that
\mathcal{T}
maps
X_{T}
in itself. This completes the proof of Proposition 3.2.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

For all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in X_{T}
, let
\rho_{1}:=\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1})
,
\rho_{2}:=\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2})
be the corresponding solutions of the model (
3.7
) with the same initial data
\rho_{0}
. Then there exist some
\delta>0
and a constant
C>0
such that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(\xi_{1})-\mathcal{T}(\xi_{2})\|_{S_{T}}\leq C\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{S_{T}}^{\delta}.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are solutions of the model (
3.7
), we can easily deduce that
\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}
satisﬁes

\displaystyle d(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})=

\displaystyle[\Delta(|\rho_{1}|^{m-1}\rho_{1}-|\rho_{2}|^{m-1}\rho_{2})-\chi\nabla\cdot(\xi_{1}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})-\xi_{2}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{2}))]dt

\displaystyle+\sigma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})dW(t).

Using Itô formula to
\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\Delta(|\rho_{1}|^{m-1}\rho_{1}-|\rho_{2}|^{m-1}\rho_{2}),\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\rangle_{V}}ds

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi_{1}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})-\xi_{2}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{2})),\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\rangle_{V}}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})\|_{L_{2}(L^{2},H_{2}^{-1})}^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},\sigma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})dW(s)\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})e_{k}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi_{1}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})-\xi_{2}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{2})),\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\rangle_{V}}ds

(3.24)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},\sigma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s).

Taking supremum over
[0,T]
and the expectation from both sides of (
3.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{\left|{}_{V^{*}}\left\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi_{1}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})-\xi_{2}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{2}))(t),(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\right\rangle_{V}\right|dt}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\langle(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(s),\sigma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(s)e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s)\Big|\Big]

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}\Big]dt

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)+J_{2}(t)+J_{3}(t).

Now we deal with
J_{1}(t)
. First, we divide the term into the following sum

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\chi\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{|_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot((\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})),\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\rangle_{V}|dt}\Big]

\displaystyle+\chi\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}{|_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi_{2}(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1}-\nabla\Phi*\xi_{2})),\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\rangle_{V}|dt}\Big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t).

Next, using (
2.5
) and the Sobolev embedding
L^{1}(\mathcal{O})\subset{H_{\frac{m+1}{m}}^{-1}}(\mathcal{O}),~m+1>d
to analyze

, we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\chi\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\int_{\mathcal{O}}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot((\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1}))(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})dx|dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\chi\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\cdot((\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1}))\|_{L^{\frac{m+1}{m}}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\cdot((\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1}))\|_{H_{\frac{m+1}{m}}^{-2}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})\|_{H_{\frac{m+1}{m}}^{-1}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1})\|_{L^{1}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi*\xi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{m+1}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}dt\Big].

Applying complex interpolation [
[
23
]
, p.21], we get for some

\displaystyle[H_{2}^{\gamma}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})]_{\theta}=L^{2}(\mathcal{O})

with

\displaystyle\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{\theta}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}}^{1-\theta}.

This gives
\theta=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}<\frac{1}{1+m}
. Furthermore, using the Young inequality and the Jensen inequality, we have that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}\theta}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}(1-\theta)}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}}dt\Big]+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}\theta}\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}(1-\theta)}dt\Big)\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon,T)\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}\theta p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}(1-\theta)q}dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{\frac{m+1}{m}r}\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon,T)\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]^{\frac{(m+1)\theta}{2m}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}(\|\xi_{1}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}}+\|\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}})^{2m}dt\Big]^{\frac{(m+1)(1-\theta)}{2m^{2}}}

\displaystyle\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]^{\frac{1}{m}}+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big],

where

,

and

satisfiy

.
According to the proposition of the reference (
[
23
]
, p.10), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle(\|\xi_{1}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}}+\|\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{\gamma}})^{2m}\Big]dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}(\|\xi_{1}\|_{H_{2m}^{\gamma}}^{2m}+\|\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2m}^{\gamma}}^{2m})\Big]dt

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\||\xi_{1}|^{m-1}\nabla\xi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big]dt+C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\||\xi_{2}|^{m-1}\nabla\xi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big]dt\leq CR_{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq CR_{2}^{\frac{(m+1)(1-\theta)}{2m^{2}}}R_{1}^{{}^{\frac{1}{m}}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]^{\theta}+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big],

where
\varepsilon=1
.
Next, we estimate

by Lemma

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\cdot(\xi_{2}\nabla\Phi*(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}))\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}dt\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{2}dt\Big]+C(\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}dt\Big]

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\|\sigma(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

Combining the estimates of

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(t)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]

Futhermore, by Gronwall lemma, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]\leq C(R_{1},R_{2},T)\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]^{\theta}.

Taking
\delta=\theta
, we have (
3.23
) holds. This completes the proof on the continuity of the solution operator
\mathcal{T}
.

maps

Proposition 3.4
.

For any
\rho_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0},H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))
satisfying
\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]<\infty
, and all
R_{1}>0
and
R_{2}>0
, it holds that

there exists
C=C(T,m)>0
such that for any
\xi\in X_{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{T}(\xi)\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dt\Big].

there exists a constant
C=C(T,m,R_{1},R_{2})>0
such that for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
and
\xi\in X_{T}

we have

\mathbb{E}\|\mathcal{T}\xi(t_{1})-\mathcal{T}\xi(t_{2})\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|.

Proof.

Using Itô formula to
\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\Delta(|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s)),\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds-\chi\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)(s)),\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma\rho(s)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s)-\rho_{0},\sigma\rho(s)dW(s)\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\Delta(|\rho(s)|^{m-1}\rho(s)-\rho_{0}^{m}),\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds+\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\Delta\rho_{0}^{m},\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)(s)),\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma\rho(s)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s)-\rho_{0},\sigma\rho(s)dW(s)\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds+\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\Delta\rho_{0}^{m},\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)(s)),\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}\|\rho(s)e_{k}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s)-\rho_{0},\sigma\rho(s)e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s).

This immediately gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\Delta\rho_{0}^{m},\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds-\chi\int_{0}^{t}{{}_{V^{*}}\langle\nabla\cdot(\xi(\nabla\Phi*\xi)(s)),\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\rangle_{V}}ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\rho(s)-\rho_{0},\sigma\rho(s)e_{k}\rangle_{H_{2}^{-1}}d\beta_{k}(s)+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}ds

(3.25)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle K_{1}(t)+K_{2}(t)+K_{3}(t)+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}ds.

For
K_{1}(s)
, applying the Hölder inequality in time gives that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}K_{1}(s)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{m+1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds\Big)^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{m+1}}t^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m}\Big]

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds\Big]+C(\varepsilon_{1},m)t\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big].

In order to calculate
K_{2}(s)
, by (
3.9
) and the Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}K_{2}(s)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C(\chi)\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}\|\xi\|_{L^{m+1}}^{2}ds\Big]

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}ds\Big]+C(\varepsilon_{2})t\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\xi\|_{{L^{m+1}}}^{m+1}\Big]^{\frac{4}{m+1}}.

We now estimate
K_{3}(s)
, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}K_{3}(s)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\|\sigma\rho(s)\|_{L_{2}(L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),H_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma\mu_{k}\rho(s)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]+C\sigma^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}t\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\rho(s)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big].

By (
3.26
)-(
3.28
), taking
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
small enough and for any
t\in[0,T]
, there exist a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{{H_{2}^{-1}}}^{2}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon_{1},m)t\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+C(\varepsilon_{2})t\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\xi\|_{{L^{m+1}}}^{m+1}\Big]^{\frac{4}{m+1}}+C\sigma^{2}t\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\rho(s)\|_{H_{2}^{-1}}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}t\mathbb{E}\Big[\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\Big]+C_{2}tR_{2}^{\frac{4}{m+1}}+C_{3}tR_{1}\leq C(T,m,R_{1},R_{2})t.

This ﬁnishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Basing on Proposition 3.1-3.4, we apply the Schaduder fixed point theorem to get the existence of marginal solutions.

Dataset of userʼs emotional reactions to news articles
Authors: Consuelo-Varinia García-Mendoza, Omar Juarez Gambino, Marco-Antonio Torres-López, Tania Rosales-Onofre, Jessica-Alejandra Castillo-Montoya, Yael-Alexandr Sanchez-Mederos, José-Manuel Suárez-Bautista
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a dataset of news articles collected from several Mexican news sources and users' emotional reactions to these news articles. Users were presented with the collected news and, after reading it, were asked to indicate the emotion it provoked in them. The emotions considered were happy, surprised, inspired, moved, neutral, sad, fearful, and angry. This dataset offers a particular focus on emotional reactions from the readerʼs perspective and has great potential for use in sentiment analysis, linguistics, and media studies research. Furthermore, the news is in Spanish which is a language where resources of this type are scarce. The detailed data collection methodology provides a solid foundation for future research and development in emotion-aware news recommendation systems.

Value of the Data

•The detection of emotions is usually done from the writerʼs perspective. Datasets following this perspective relate text (usually opinions) written by the user with the emotions expressed by the words contained in it. Another less explored approach is the one that follows the readerʼs perspective. Here, the intention is to relate the content of a text written by a person (e.g., a news article) to the emotions the text will provoke in the reader [1]. The type of text analyzed from this perspective does not necessarily contain an opinion, so words with an explicit emotional charge are not usually used, which makes the task more challenging.•The dataset integrates news from four Mexican news sources. This diversity allows for different content and editorial approaches, which could reveal trends and possible biases in news coverage.•The dataset is tagged with multiple users' emotional reactions to news articles. This allows for personalized tracking of user preferences, which could be used to generate news recommendations based on their emotional state.•Researchers can use this dataset to analyze correlations between news articles' content and their emotional impact on readers. Understanding how writing style and topics influence emotional reactions would allow customizing the content displayed and suggesting options to editors to generate a specific impact.•This dataset contributes to increasing the linguistic resources available in Spanish.

Background

The creation of our dataset stems from the need to analyze the emotional impact of news articles on readers. This task has been approached from various perspectives and its relevance has been highlighted in previous works [[2], [3], [4]].

Online news consumption is increasingly common, and much of what we read comes from recommender systems that analyze our interests to suggest related topics. Some authors have identified that emotions influence our consumption habits [5,6], but news recommendation systems often overlook the emotional dimension of information. Our dataset addresses this gap, offering an opportunity to analyze how news emotionally impacts readers. The integration of this aspect can enable systems to generate recommendations that consider the emotional perspective.

The newspapers La Jornada, La Razón de México, 24 Horas, and El Financiero were selected. Content was extracted using RSS (Really Simple Syndication) feeds, a standard protocol for web content syndication that facilitates the automated reception of website updates. All the newspapers offer free access to content on their channels if they are not used commercially. Extraction was performed using Python scripting, allowing efficient and systematic data collection from multiple sources. The extraction process was carried out once a week for 3 months.

Subsequently, the collected data was filtered. This process eliminated repeated or irrelevant information and structured the data in a coherent and usable form for subsequent analysis.

Next, we developed a web interface that allows users to interact with the news content. 57 undergraduate students were selected as users. Students were from Artificial Intelligence, Data Science and Computer systems departments. The users read the news and reacted indicating the emotion evoked by the content. This task was done for two weeks. In Fig. 2 we show the widget used to react to the news. From left to right eight emotions are displayed: happy, fearful, surprised, moved, angry, neutral, sad and inspired. Limitations

The dataset has some limitations regarding the number of reactions per user. There were users who read and reacted to a few news articles. Attention should be paid to this situation when using the dataset, as learning models could be biased. However, these users are few, while the majority have enough reactions to be used correctly by the models.

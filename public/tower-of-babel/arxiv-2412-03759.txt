Universality in static spherically symmetric solutions of f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity
V. I. Zhdanov

Abstract
f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity is a well-known modification of General Relativity, which is used in various models of dark matter and dynamical dark energy. We study static spherically symmetric (SSS) asymptotically flat configurations of the f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity in the Einstein frame for three known scalaron potentials with different asymptotic properties. The main attention is paid to the case of astrophysically relevant configuration mass M M and scalaron mass μ \mu greater than several m ​ e ​ V meV , according to existing experimental constraints. This means very large values of the dimensionless (in geometrized units) parameter M ​ μ M\mu , leading to specific properties of the SSS solutions. We consider a sufficiently large size of a scalarization region r 0 ≫ r g r_{0}\gg r_{g} , where the space-time metric is essentially different from the Schwarzschild one. It turns out that the scalaron field has universal behavior regardless of M ​ μ M\mu and r 0 r_{0} and is practically the same for different scalaron potentials. Asymptotic parameters of the metric near the naked singularity at the center of the SSS configuration are obtained analytically for all the models. The test body circular orbits are discussed in view of observational signatures, which can distinguish these configurations from a regular black hole.

At the same time, these results may be regarded as some exterior solutions that must be continuously matched to the internal solutions that involve, e.g., hydrodynamical energy-momentum tensor

and

.

8

8

It should be noted that in the opposite case

[

]
. In this case, the weak-field region A, where

, will come very close to the value

, which will lead to a strong peak near this point.

For large
M\mu
and
q
, we found analytic approximate solutions in the weak-field region (A) and in the small transition interval between A and B.
It is the requirement of large
M\mu
and

leads to specific properties concerning the similarity of solutions. On the other hand, this requirement is necessary in our treatment, because it has been used in the approximations resulting in the choice

and initial conditions at this point, which are used to extend the solutions for

Our results have been obtained in the Einstein frame. The metric coefficients in the original Jordan frame (physical metric) are given by (

It can be concluded that, if such a

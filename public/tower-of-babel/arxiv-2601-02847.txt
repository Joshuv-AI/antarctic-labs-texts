Stability and error estimates of a linear and partitioned finite element method approximating nonlinear fluid–structure interactions
Bangwei She, Tian Tian, Karel Tůma

Abstract
We propose and analyze a linear and partitioned finite element method for fluid–shell interactions under the arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) framework. We adopt the P1-bubble/P1/P1 elements for the fluid velocity, pressure, and structure velocity, respectively. We show the stability and error estimates of the scheme without assuming infinitesimal structural deformation nor neglecting fluid convection effects. The theoretical convergence rate is further corroborated by numerical experiments. Keywords: fluid-structure interaction, partitioned scheme, stability, error estimates, finite element method MSC(2010): 35Q30, 76N99, 74F10, 65M12, 65M60

The

[

]
, Bukač et al.
[

]
, Hundertmark and Lukáčová-Medvid’ová
[
14
]
,

[

]
.
To maintain the stability, suitable boundary conditions are needed at the interaction interface, see the comparison of different types of partitioned methods in
[

]
.

Despite the practical success of partitioned schemes, their rigorous convergence analysis remains a highly delicate and technically demanding task in general. Significant progress in this direction has been achieved in a series of influential works, including those by Bukač and Muha
[
6
]
, Burman et al.
[
7
,
8
]
, Fern’andez and Mullaert
[
10
]
, Li et al.
[
15
]
, Seboldt and Bukč
[
20
]
, among others. These contributions provide deep insights into the stability and convergence mechanisms of partitioned methods with careful treatment of the interaction interface.

Owing to the intrinsic nonlinearity of the coupled fluid–structure system, existing analytical results are typically established within a simplified yet mathematically tractable framework, often assuming infinitesimal solid displacements and neglecting convective effects in the fluid equations. The objective of the present paper is to extend the theoretical understanding of partitioned schemes beyond this commonly adopted setting. More precisely, we establish the energy stability of a linear, partitioned finite element method (see Theorem
3.1
) together with its linear convergence rate (see Theorem
4.1

The plan of the paper is the following. In Section 2 we describe the problem and present our numerical method. In Section 3 we prove the energy stability of the numerical solution. In Section 4 we analyze the convergence rate of the numerical solution toward a strong solution. In Section 5 we present the numerical experiments. Section 6 is the conclusion.

2
Problem description and numerical method

In this section, we introduce the notations, function spaces, and some preliminary estimates.

2.1
Problem formulation

In this paper, we are interested in the interaction between an incompressible viscous fluid and a thin deformable shell structure. Specifically, we consider the structure to be the upper boundary
\Gamma_{S}
of the fluid domain. It results in a nonlinear coupled system with a time dependent fluid domain given by

\Omega_{\eta}(t)=\left\{{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Sigma\times(0,\eta(t,x_{1}))\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},\;\Sigma=(0,L),\mbox{ and }\Gamma_{S}=\left\{{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},\eta(t,x_{1}))\mid x_{1}\in\Sigma\right\},

where
\eta=\eta(t,x_{1})
represents the height of the upper boundary
\Gamma_{S}(t)
.
Typically, the fluid dynamics are described by the balance equations of mass and momentum in the current (Eulerian) configuration, while the structure dynamics are formulated in the reference (Lagrangian) configuration.
The motion of the coupled FSI system is governed by the following equations.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rm div}\,\mathbf{u}=0,&\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\eta},\\
&\varrho_{f}\left(\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\right)-{\rm div}\,\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u},p)=0,&\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{\eta},\\
&\varrho_{s}\partial_{t}^{2}\eta+\mathcal{L}(\eta)=f,\quad\xi=\partial_{t}\eta,&\quad\text{ on }(0,T)\times\Sigma.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Here,

\mathbb{T}=2\mu(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\rm S}-p\mathbb{I},(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\rm S}=(\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\rm T})/2,\mathcal{L}(\eta)=-\gamma_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}\eta-\gamma_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}\zeta,\zeta=-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}\eta,

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}(t,{\bm{x}})
and
p=p(t,{\bm{x}})
are the fluid velocity and pressure, respectively.
The symbols
\varrho_{f}
and
\varrho_{s}
denote the densities of the fluid and the structure, respectively.
The constants
\gamma_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{2}>0
are given parameters.

\xi=\xi(t,{\bm{x}})
represents the structure velocity,
and
f=f(t,{\bm{x}})
denotes the interaction force acting on the structure due to the Cauchy stress of the fluid.
The problem is closed by the initial data

\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta}(0),\quad\eta(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}>0,\;\xi(0,\cdot)=\xi_{0}\;\text{in }\Sigma,

(2.2)

together with no-slip boundary conditions

\mathbf{u}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0,\ \Gamma_{D}=\partial\Omega_{\eta}\setminus\Gamma_{S},\mbox{ and }\partial_{x_{1}}\eta|_{\partial\Sigma}=0

and the coupling conditions on the interface:

\mathbf{u}|_{\Gamma_{S}}=\xi{\mathbf{e}}_{2},\quad f=-\,{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot\big(J\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u},p)\circ\mathcal{A}_{\eta}\,{\mathbb{F}}^{-T}\big)\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{2},

(2.3)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
is an invertible mapping from the reference domain
{\widehat{\Omega}}
to the time-dependent fluid domain
\Omega_{\eta}
that is introduced to handle the inconsistency between the Eulerian description of the fluid and the Lagrangian description of the structure. This allows us to describe the entire problem in the single common configuration
{\widehat{\Omega}}
.
Without loss of generality, we set
{\widehat{\Omega}}=\Sigma\times(0,1)
.
In this work, the mapping
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
is explicitly constructed as a linear extension:

\mathcal{A}_{\eta}:{\widehat{\Omega}}\mapsto\Omega_{\eta},\quad(\widehat{x}_{1},\widehat{x}_{2})\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2})=\mathcal{A}_{\eta}(t,\widehat{\bm{x}})=\left(\widehat{x}_{1},\eta\,\widehat{x}_{2}\right),

(2.4a)

with the corresponding Jacobian and determinant given by

{\mathbb{F}}=\nabla_{\widehat{x}}\mathcal{A}_{\eta}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[3.0pt]
\widehat{x}_{2}\,\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial\hat{x}_{1}}&\eta\end{pmatrix},\qquad J=\det({\mathbb{F}})=\eta.

(2.4b)

Here and in what follows, for any generic function
v
defined on the current domain,
its pullback to the reference domain is denoted by
\widehat{v}=v\circ\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
.

\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\widehat{q}\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{M}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}=0\quad\mbox{ for all }q\in L^{2}_{0}({\widehat{\Omega}});

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\left(J\partial_{t}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}+\partial_{t}J\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{u}}}{2}\right)\cdot\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\big(\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot(\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}})-\widehat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot(\nabla\widehat{\bm{\varphi}})\big)\cdot{\mathbb{F}}^{-1}\cdot\widehat{{\bf v}}J\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}

(2.5b)

\displaystyle+\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}},\widehat{p}):\big(\nabla\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}{\mathbb{F}}^{-1}\big)J\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\varrho_{s}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{t}\xi\psi\,{\rm d}x_{1}+a_{s}(\eta,\zeta,\psi)=0,

, where
\widehat{{\bf v}}=\widehat{\mathbf{u}}-\widehat{\mathbf{w}}
,

\displaystyle a_{s}(\eta,\zeta,\psi)=\int_{\Sigma}(\gamma_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}\eta+\gamma_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}\zeta)\partial_{x_{1}}\psi\,{\rm d}x_{1},

2.2
The numerical method

Now we are ready to propose a finite element method for the discretization of the weak formulation (
2.5
).

A linear partitioned scheme.

For
k=0
, predict the first motion of structure by
\eta_{h}^{1}=\eta_{h}^{0}+\tau\xi_{h}^{0}.

For
k=1,2,\cdots
, solve the FSI problem in two subproblems.

Step 1 – The fluid subproblem: find
(\widehat{p}_{h}^{k},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k})\in\widehat{Q}^{f}_{h}\times\widehat{V}^{f}_{h}

\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\widehat{q}\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}:{\mathbb{M}}_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}=0,

(2.12a)

\begin{split}&\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}(J_{h}^{k}D_{t}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}+\frac{1}{2}D_{t}J_{h}^{k}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k*})\cdot\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\big(\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot(\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k})-\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}\cdot(\nabla\widehat{\bm{\varphi}})\big)\cdot({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\cdot\widehat{{\bf v}}_{h}^{k-1}J_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}\\
&+\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k},\widehat{p}_{h}^{k}):\left(\nabla\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\right)J_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{\varrho_{s}}{\tau}\int_{\Sigma}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}-\xi_{h}^{k-1}{\mathbf{e}}_{2})\cdot\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\,{\rm d}x_{1}+a_{s}(\eta_{h}^{k},\zeta_{h}^{k},\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{2})=0\end{split}

(2.12b)

for all
(\widehat{q},\widehat{\bm{\varphi}})\in\widehat{Q}^{f}_{h}\times\widehat{V}^{f}_{h}
, where
D_{t}v^{k}:=(v^{k}-v^{k-1})/\tau
is the time difference,
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k*}=2\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k-1}-\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}
,
\widehat{{\bf v}}_{h}^{k-1}=\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k-1}-\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{k}
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathbb{M}_{h}^{k}=\mathbb{M}(\eta_{h}^{k})
are given in (
2.6
). Moreover,
\zeta_{h}
is defined by (
2.7
) and

is given by (
2.8
).

Step 2 – The structure subproblem: find
(\xi_{h}^{k}=D_{t}\eta_{h}^{k+1},\eta_{h}^{k+1})\in V_{h}^{s}\times V_{h}^{s}
, such that

\frac{\varrho_{s}}{\tau}\int_{\Sigma}\xi_{h}^{k}\psi\,{\rm d}x_{1}+a_{s}(\eta_{h}^{k+1},\zeta_{h}^{k+1},\psi)=\frac{\varrho_{s}}{\tau}\int_{\Sigma}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\psi\,{\rm d}x_{1}+a_{s}(\eta_{h}^{k},\zeta_{h}^{k},\psi)

(2.12c)

for all

.

Remark 2.3
.

Our scheme approximates the no-slip velocity condition on the interface with a truncation error of order

Denoting

\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{k}=\xi_{h}^{k}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\varrho_{s}}\Delta_{\xi}^{k}

\Delta_{\xi}^{k}=D_{t}(\gamma_{1}\zeta_{h}^{k+1}-\gamma_{2}\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}\zeta_{h}^{k+1})=\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}(\gamma_{2}\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}\xi_{h}^{k}-\gamma_{1}\xi_{h}^{k})

\int_{\Sigma}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{2}-\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x_{1}=0.

As the first component of

\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}=\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{k}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}.

(2.14)

Summing up (

) with the coupled test function

\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}D_{t}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}J_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}D_{t}J_{h}^{k}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k*}\cdot\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\big(\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot(\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k})-\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}\cdot(\nabla\widehat{\bm{\varphi}})\big)\cdot({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\cdot\widehat{{\bf v}}_{h}^{k-1}J_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}\\
+\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k},\widehat{p}_{h}^{k}):\left(\nabla\widehat{\bm{\varphi}}({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\right)J_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\varrho_{s}\int_{\Sigma}D_{t}\xi_{h}^{k}\widehat{\varphi}_{2}\,{\rm d}x_{1}+a_{s}(\eta_{h}^{k+1},\zeta_{h}^{k+1},\widehat{\varphi}_{2})=0.

(2.15)

3
Stability

In this section, we study the energy stability of the scheme (
2.12
). The total energy of the FSI system at time

reads

E_{h}^{k}=\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\eta_{h}^{k}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{\varrho_{s}}{2}\left\lVert\xi_{h}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1}}\eta_{h}^{k+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}\eta_{h}^{k+1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}.

Theorem 3.1

(Energy estimates)
.

Let

We have the following energy estimates for all

(3.1)

where

and

\displaystyle D_{num1}^{k}=

\displaystyle\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\left(\gamma_{1}\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1}}\xi_{h}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\gamma_{2}\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}\xi_{h}^{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\right)+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\varrho_{s}}\int_{\Sigma}|\gamma_{1}\zeta_{h}^{k+1}-\gamma_{2}\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}\zeta_{h}^{k+1}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x_{1},

Further, substituting the above estimate into (
4.8
) and noticing the initial error
\delta_{E}^{0}=0
, owing to the estimate of
G_{sL}
stated in Lemma
A.3
, the lower bounds of
\eta
and
\eta_{h}
, and by denoting

\widetilde{\delta}_{E}^{k}=\delta_{E}^{k}+\frac{\tau^{2}\gamma_{1}^{2}}{2\varrho_{s}}\left\lVert\delta_{\zeta}^{m+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+\frac{\tau^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}}{2\varrho_{s}}\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1},h}^{2}\delta_{\zeta}^{m+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma)},

we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{E}^{m}+(1

\displaystyle-\alpha)2\mu\tau\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\left|\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{u}}^{k}({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\right|^{2}\eta_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\tau^{2}+h^{2}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widetilde{\delta}_{E}^{k}.

By choosing any
\alpha\in(0,1)
and using Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\delta_{E}^{m}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta_{D}^{k}\leq\widetilde{\delta}_{E}^{m}+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta_{D}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\tau^{2}+h^{2}.

Recalling the interpolation errors (see Section
2
) and the regularity of the strong solution (
4.1
) we get

\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}|I_{\mathbf{u}}^{m}|^{2}\eta_{h}^{m}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\varrho_{s}|I_{\xi}^{m}|^{2}+\gamma_{1}|\partial_{x_{1}}I_{\eta}^{m+1}|^{2}+\gamma_{2}|I_{\zeta}^{m+1}|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}x_{1}\\
+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(2\mu\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\left|\nabla I_{\mathbf{u}}^{k}({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\right|^{2}\eta_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{2}.

Finally, due to the triangular inequality, we sum up the previous two estimates and get

\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{f}\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}|e_{\mathbf{u}}^{m}|^{2}\eta_{h}^{m}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\varrho_{s}|e_{\xi}|^{2}+\gamma_{1}|\partial_{x_{1}}e_{\eta}^{m+1}|^{2}+\gamma_{2}|e_{\zeta}^{m+1}|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}x_{1}\\
+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(2\mu\int_{{\widehat{\Omega}}}\left|\nabla e_{\mathbf{u}}^{k}({\mathbb{F}}_{h}^{k})^{-1}\right|^{2}\eta_{h}^{k}\,{\rm d}\widehat{x}\right)\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\tau^{2}+h^{2}.

Note that the above proof is valid on the time interval
(0,T)
under the assumption
\eta_{h}^{k}>\underline{\eta}>0
. Following the methodology in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 6.2]
, one may deduce via the mathematical induction that if
\eta_{h}(0)>\underline{\eta}
, then (
3.4
) remains valid as long as the smooth solution exists and satisfies the lower bound
\eta>\underline{\eta}
.
∎

Experiment 3: Comparison with a monolithic scheme from
[

The numerical scheme (
2.12
) exhibits linear convergence in time and quadratic convergence in velocity. It is consistent with the rates observed for the monolithic Scheme-R
[

. The main difference lies in the algorithmic structure:
Scheme-R has two steps. Step 1 solves monolithically for the fluid and solid, finding
(\mathbf{u},\zeta,p)
in
{\widehat{\Omega}}
, and Step 2 extends the displacement
\eta
to the whole domain by solving a Laplace equation.

In contrast, the current scheme (
2.12
) does not solve the fluid-structure interaction monolithically. Instead, it splits the problem: Step 1 solves for the fluid
(\mathbf{u},p)
in
{\widehat{\Omega}}
, Step 2 solves for the solid
(\zeta,\xi)
on
\widehat{\Gamma}_{S}
, and Step 3 extends the displacement
\eta
to the whole domain.

On the finest mesh, Step 1 of Scheme-R involves 410 880 degrees of freedom (DOFs), while the splitting scheme (
2.12
) uses 359 360 DOFs in Step 1 and 640 DOFs in Step 2, thus formally reducing the size of the subproblems to be solved.

\|\nabla e_{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}
and

. The convergence in time is shown in Figure

Although the splitting formulation reduces the number of unknowns in each subproblem, the total CPU time in this 2D-1D configuration is not lower than that of the monolithic scheme. The additional interpolation and projection steps between
{\widehat{\Omega}}
and
\Sigma
introduce extra computational overhead, which offsets the advantage of solving smaller systems. In higher-dimensional settings (e.g., a three-dimensional fluid coupled with a two-dimensional structure), however, the splitting approach is expected to yield a more significant computational benefit.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we proposed and analyzed a linear, partitioned finite element scheme for the interaction between an incompressible viscous fluid and a thin deformable structure. The method allows the fluid and structure subproblems to be solved independently, while maintaining implicit velocity coupling at the interface, which ensures stability at the discrete level. Our analysis demonstrates unconditional energy stability and establishes optimal convergence rates in both space and time, all without relying on the assumptions of infinitesimal structural deformations or neglecting nonlinear fluid convection. Beyond the theoretical results, numerical experiments confirmed the predicted convergence orders and illustrated the robustness and practical effectiveness of the proposed partitioned approach.

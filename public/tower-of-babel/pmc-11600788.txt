Knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding keratopathy among diabetic patients
Authors: Jinfeng Sun, Lianmei Shan, Liangyan Zhang, Bo Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aims to investigate the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) regarding keratopathy among diabetic patients.MethodsA web-based cross-sectional study was conducted between May 26, 2023, and September 18, 2023, at Yantai Shan Hospital and Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital. Demographic information and KAP were assessed through the distribution of questionnaires.ResultsAmong the 521 participants, average knowledge, attitude, and practice scores were 6.11 ± 4.53, 27.61 ± 4.41, and 37.25 ± 5.21, respectively. Path analysis revealed a significant association between attitude and knowledge (β = 0.416, P < 0.001), and practice(β = 0.683, P < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression indicated that attitude score (OR = 1.288, 95%CI: 1.203–1.379, P < 0.001), higher monthly incomes(> 10000 RMB) (OR = 3.494, 95%CI: 1.104–11.055, P = 0.033), higher self-rated control levels(4 and 5 points) (OR = 3.729, 95%CI: 1.687–8.243, P = 0.001 and OR = 3.801, 95%CI: 1.560–9.261, P = 0.003 respectively) and family history of ophthalmic diseases (OR = 0.297, 95%CI: 0.115–0.762, P = 0.012) were independently associated with proactive practice.ConclusionDiabetic patients demonstrated inadequate knowledge, moderately positive attitudes, and reasonably proactive practices concerning keratopathy. The study emphasizes the impact of attitude on practice, influenced by knowledge and other factors such as self-rated blood sugar control and family history of ophthalmic diseases.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20804-2.

Background

Diabetic keratopathy, a degenerative corneal disease prevalent among individuals with diabetes mellitus [1], manifests with diminished corneal sensitivity, epithelial defects, delayed wound healing, and heightened infection risk [2]. This condition not only compromises visual function but also diminishes the quality of life, contributing to increased healthcare burdens [3]. The reported prevalence of diabetic keratopathy ranges from 47 to 64% in diabetic patients [4, 5].

Addressing diabetic keratopathy poses challenges, with conventional therapies, such as lubricants, antibiotics, bandage contact lenses, and tarsorrhaphy, primarily aiming to create a conducive environment for corneal healing [6]. However, these approaches often fall short in addressing the underlying causes of corneal damage, exhibiting limited efficacy or adverse effects. Novel treatments, including nerve growth factor eye drops, amniotic membrane transplantation, and corneal neurotization, show promise in restoring corneal sensitivity and integrity [7]. Yet, these interventions are experimental, costly, and invasive, necessitating further evaluation. Consequently, the prevention and early detection of diabetic keratopathy assume critical importance for individuals with diabetes.

Recognizing the pivotal role of knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) in shaping health behaviors and outcomes, our study endeavors to fill a critical gap by assessing the KAP of diabetic patients concerning keratopathy. KAP surveys provide a quantitative method to glean insights into the awareness, perception, and behavior of individuals regarding a specific health issue [8]. Despite the utility of KAP surveys in understanding diabetes-related factors among specific populations [9], no prior research has explored the KAP of diabetic patients regarding keratopathy.

Therefore, this study aimed to explore the KAP regarding keratopathy among diabetic patients.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted between May 26, 2023 and September 18, 2023, at Yantai Shan Hospital and Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital, focusing on individuals diagnosed with diabetes. Ethical approval was obtained from the Clinical Trial Ethics Committee of Yantaishan Hospital, and informed consent was secured from all participants. Inclusion Criteria: (1) Symptoms of diabetes plus random blood glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/l, or fasting blood glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/l, or 2-hour postprandial glucose (2hPG) ≥ 11.1 mmol/l; (2) Age ≥ 18 years; (3) Clear consciousness, normal mental state, and ability to understand the questions in the survey. Exclusion Criteria: 1) Incomplete or inaccurate completion of the survey questionnaire.

Questionnaire and quality control

The questionnaire was developed based on relevant literature [10]and the multidisciplinary consensus on diabetes-related eye disease prevention and treatment in China. The survey instrument underwent three iterations of modifications, incorporating feedback from three experts, adjusting question quantity, complexity, and scope. A pilot study with 51 participants yielded a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.751, indicating acceptable internal consistency.

The final questionnaire was in Chinese (a version translated into English was attached as an Appendix), comprising four sections: demographic information, knowledge dimension, attitude dimension, and practice. The knowledge dimension includes 9 questions with a score range of 0–18 points. The attitude dimension consists of 8 questions using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from very positive (5 points) to very negative (1 point), with a score range of 8–40 points. The practice dimension includes 10 questions using a five-point Likert scale, with values ranging from always to never, assigned scores of 5 − 1, resulting in a score range of 10–50 points.

The distribution of the survey to research subjects involved a coordinated effort led by department head nurses and department directors. Two tertiary hospitals in Yantai City, Shandong Province, were included. Contact was established with the directors of the endocrine department and the general medical department via phone or WeChat. Subsequently, 80 doctors and nurses from both inpatient and outpatient departments were enlisted to participate. An additional 15 individuals underwent specific training for effective questionnaire distribution. During the study period, which spanned the approximate patient population of 10,000 in these hospitals, a total of 600 questionnaires were distributed. The collection process resulted in 536 completed questionnaires. To address issues such as incomplete or zero-scoring knowledge sections, 35 questionnaires underwent revision through personal or phone follow-up. Furthermore, 15 questionnaires were excluded due to similar reasons coupled with lost follow-up. To ensure the smooth execution of the survey, two trained research assistants, one from each of the two hospitals, played a crucial role. Prior to distributing the survey questionnaires, these assistants organized offline meetings. These sessions covered comprehensive aspects such as the background, content, significance, and design of the survey questionnaire, as well as inclusion criteria, techniques for questionnaire distribution, and precautions for inspection, review, and correction after retrieving the survey questionnaire. This meticulous approach aimed to maintain consistency and reliability in data collection.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) and AMOS 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA). Continuous data were presented as means and standard deviations (SD), while categorical data were expressed as n (%). Normality tests were conducted for continuous variables, with the t-test employed for normally distributed data and the Wilcoxon Mann-Whitney test for non-normally distributed data in two-group comparisons. For comparisons involving three or more groups with normally distributed continuous variables and uniform variance, ANOVA was utilized, whereas the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied for non-normally distributed data. Explore the relationships between the dimensions of knowledge, attitude, and practice through pathway analysis. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to identify factors associated with good knowledge, positive attitude and proactive practice, which is defined as having a score of over 70% of possible highest score in the knowledge, attitude and practice dimension of the population. A two-sided P-value less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

The study comprehensively assessed baseline characteristics and Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice (KAP) scores among 521 participants. The mean scores for knowledge, attitude, and practice were 6.11 ± 4.53, 27.61 ± 4.41, and 37.25 ± 5.21, respectively (Table 1). Age demonstrated a significant association with Knowledge scores (P = 0.002), revealing that younger participants (18–45 years) exhibited higher mean scores compared to middle-aged and elderly individuals. However, Attitude and Practice scores did not exhibit significant variations across different age groups (Supplemental Table 1).

Participants residing in urban areas displayed notably higher scores in knowledge, attitude, and practice compared to their rural and suburban counterparts. A clear trend was evident concerning education levels, where higher educational attainment positively correlated with increased KAP scores. Specifically, individuals with bachelor’s degrees and above demonstrated the highest scores across all domains. Employment status and income levels showed significant associations with KAP scores. Full-time employed individuals, particularly those with higher income (> 10,000 RMB), exhibited higher scores compared to other employment categories and income brackets. Marital status and co-residence demonstrated a moderate association with Knowledge scores, while Health Insurance type displayed a robust correlation with all three KAP domains (all P < 0.05, Supplemental Table 1). The path analysis unveiled a significant association between attitudes and knowledge (β = 0.416, P < 0.001) and practice (β = 0.683, P < 0.001) (Table 2).

Discussion

Diabetic patients demonstrated inadequate knowledge, moderately positive attitudes, and reasonably proactive practices concerning keratopathy. The path analysis revealed associations between knowledge and attitude and a significant link between attitude and practice. These findings underscore the crucial role of attitude in influencing actual practices, with a marginal direct impact of knowledge on practices.

Examining baseline characteristics, the study echoed prior findings associating higher education levels, urban residency, and higher income brackets with elevated KAP scores [11, 12]. Higher educational attainment positively correlated with increased KAP scores, emphasizing education’s role in enhancing knowledge about diabetic keratopathy [13, 14]. The urban-rural divide observed in KAP scores aligns with previous study [15], indicating the influence of environmental factors on diabetic keratopathy KAP. However, nuanced disparities surfaced in the study. For instance, the association between age and Knowledge scores contrasts with some prior studies, suggesting potential regional or cultural variations impacting age-related associations [16]. The study on cervical cancer screening reported no age-related associations with KAP variables [16].

The path analysis provided intriguing insights into the interplay between KAP domains. A strong positive relationship between Knowledge and Attitude resonates with earlier findings emphasizing knowledge’s pivotal role in shaping attitudes toward diabetes management [17, 18]. The study further revealed that participants’ practices were positively associated with their attitude, indicating that a positive attitude exerts a significant influence on patients’ practices regarding the prevention and treatment of keratopathy. This was consistent with previous study which aimed to investigate the KAP of diabetes self-management among Iraqi type 2 diabetes mellitus patients [19]. However, the weak direct effect from Knowledge to Practice diverges from some prior studies, suggesting potential contextual differences in translating knowledge into practical application [20].

The factors associated with Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice offered nuanced perspectives. The study reaffirmed the positive correlation between higher education, income levels, and favorable KAP scores, consistent with prior research [21]. Participants with higher education levels exhibited significantly good knowledge, aligning with findings in studies exploring KAP on cervical cancer and health insurance [22, 23]. Additionally, co-residence exhibited a significant association with knowledge on diabetes-related keratopathy, highlighting the potential impact of communication and mutual care among residents [24]. Multivariate analyses revealed several noteworthy associations. Participants with a longer time since diabetes diagnosis (> 10 years) demonstrated a significantly positive attitude towards keratopathy, possibly due to disease prevention and control interventions. The emphasis on blood sugar control, as reflected in participants’ self-rated blood sugar control of 5 points, correlated with a positive attitude toward keratopathy, consistent with studies highlighting the importance of disease prevention and control [25]. What’s more, family history of ophthalmic diseases also showed significant influence on participants’ attitude towards keratopathy; this is consistent with prior research showing that students with a family history of mental illness have a more positive attitude toward mental illness and are more likely to seek professional help [26]. Higher monthly income, higher self-rated blood sugar control levels, and a family history of ophthalmic diseases were independently associated with proactive practice. The findings suggest that higher income contributes to increased spending on the prevention and treatment of diabetes-related keratopathy, aligning with studies on mental health treatment gaps in low and middle-income countries [27]. Besides, self-rated blood sugar control was directly associated with participants’ practice and family history of ophthalmic diseases can also enhance the emphasis on their own health; this was in line with previous study showing that family history is a highly regarded component of patient medical history [28]. The diminishing significance of certain factors in multivariate analyses, such as age and employment status, suggests their influence might be mediated by other unexplored variables.

Several limitations merit consideration. The cross-sectional nature of the study limits causal inference, and temporal changes in KAP scores couldn’t be fully captured. Self-reporting introduces potential recall bias. Cultural and regional specifics might limit generalizability, emphasizing the need for future studies to explore these aspects.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides critical insights into the associations between demographic, socioeconomic, and health-related factors with Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice scores in diabetes management. While supporting previous research, nuanced disparities underscore the necessity for context-specific interventions. Understanding the multifaceted interplay of these factors is crucial for devising tailored strategies, warranting further longitudinal investigations to validate and expand on these findings.

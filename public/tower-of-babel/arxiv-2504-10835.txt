Gapless Foliated-Exotic Duality
Kantaro Ohmori, Shutaro Shimamura

Abstract
In this work, we construct a new foliated quantum field theory equivalent to the exotic ϕ \phi -theory – a fractonic gapless scalar field theory described by tensor gauge fields and exhibiting U ⁡ ( 1 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1)\times U(1) subsystem global symmetry. This subsystem symmetry has an ’t Hooft anomaly, which is captured by a subsystem symmetry-protected topological (SSPT) phase in one dimension higher via the anomaly inflow mechanism. By analyzing both the anomaly inflow structure and the foliated-exotic duality in the SSPT phases, we establish the foliated-exotic duality in the ϕ \phi -theories. Furthermore, we also investigate the foliated-exotic duality in the ϕ ^ \hat{\phi} -theory, which is dual to the ϕ \phi -theory, and construct the foliated ϕ ^ \hat{\phi} -theory. These are the first examples of the foliated-exotic duality in gapless theories.

Introduction

Subsystem global symmetry is one of the generalizations of global symmetries — further extending the notion originally introduced by Gaiotto et al.
[

Symmetry operators of subsystem symmetry are supported on specific submanifolds such as lines or planes.
In this sense, subsystem symmetry resembles higher-form global symmetry, another and the original generalization of global symmetries.
However, in contrast to higher-form global symmetries, these submanifolds cannot be deformed in arbitrary directions, meaning that subsystem symmetry is not fully topological.
Consequently, symmetry operators supported on each submanifold, which cannot be continuously deformed into one another, carry independent charges
[
41

While this property is inconsistent with relativistic systems, subsystem symmetry does appear in non-relativistic systems, including lattice models
[

It leads to characteristic features such as restricted mobility of excitations, which are called fractons, and a sub-extensive ground state degeneracy
[

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle\sim C_{0}+\partial_{0}\Lambda\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle C_{12}

\displaystyle\sim C_{12}+\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\Lambda\,,

The 3+1d SSPT phase is described by the exotic Lagrangian

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSPT,e}}\left[\bm{C},\bm{\hat{C}}\right]\\
&\quad=\frac{i}{2\pi}\left[\hat{C}(\partial_{0}C_{3}-\partial_{3}C_{0})-\hat{C}_{0}^{12}(\partial_{3}C_{12}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}C_{3})+\hat{C}_{3}^{12}(\partial_{0}C_{12}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}C_{0})\right]\,.\end{split}

(2.7)

U(1)\times U(1)
subsystem symmetry. Then, we consider the spacetime

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta_{g}\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSPT,e}}&=\frac{i}{2\pi}\left[\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\hat{\Lambda}^{12}(\partial_{0}C_{3}-\partial_{3}C_{0})-\partial_{0}\hat{\Lambda}^{12}(\partial_{3}C_{12}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}C_{3})+\partial_{3}\hat{\Lambda}^{12}(\partial_{0}C_{12}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}C_{0})\right]\\

(2.8)

where we have dropped the total derivative terms with respect to
x^{0}
,
x^{1}
and
x^{2}

\displaystyle\delta_{g}S_{\text{SSPT,e}}=-\int_{x^{3}=0}dx^{0}dx^{1}dx^{2}\left[\frac{i}{2\pi}\hat{\Lambda}^{12}(\partial_{0}C_{12}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}C_{0})\right]\,.

(2.9)

l^{1}

l^{2}

(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2})

The Lagrangian describing the exotic

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\phi,\text{e}}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}(\partial_{0}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu_{12}}(\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi)^{2}\,,

(2.10)

where
\mu_{0}

\mu_{12}

\phi

\phi\sim\phi+2\pi

\displaystyle\phi\sim\phi+2\pi w^{1}+2\pi w^{2}\,.

(2.11)

x^{2}

\phi

\mathbb{Z}_{4}

\phi

\bm{1}_{0}

The equation of motion is

\displaystyle-\mu_{0}\partial_{0}^{2}\phi+\frac{1}{\mu_{12}}\partial^{2}_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}\phi=0\,.

(2.12)

Let us discuss subsystem symmetry. The currents of the momentum dipole symmetry are

\displaystyle J_{0}

\displaystyle=i\mu_{0}\partial_{0}\phi\,,

(2.13)

\displaystyle J_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\mu_{12}}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi\,,

(2.14)

and the conservation law is

\displaystyle\partial_{0}J_{0}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}J_{12}=0\,,

(2.15)

from the equation of motion (
2.12
). The conserved charges are

\displaystyle Q^{1}(x^{1})=\oint dx^{2}J_{0}\,,

(2.16)

\displaystyle Q^{2}(x^{2})=\oint dx^{1}J_{0}\,.

(2.17)

By integrating them over the fixed width and exponentiating them, we have the symmetry operators

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}^{1}\left[[x_{1}^{1},x_{2}^{1}]\times\mathcal{C}^{2}\right]=\exp\left[i\alpha\int_{x_{1}^{1}}^{x_{2}^{1}}dx^{1}\oint_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}dx^{2}\mu_{0}\partial_{0}\phi\right]\,,

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}^{2}\left[[x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}]\times\mathcal{C}^{1}\right]=\exp\left[i\alpha\int_{x_{1}^{2}}^{x_{2}^{2}}dx^{2}\oint_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}dx^{1}\mu_{0}\partial_{0}\phi\right]\,,

where
\alpha

2\pi

\mathcal{C}^{k}
is a one-dimensional loop along the

\displaystyle V_{n}[x]=e^{in\phi}\,,

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}^{1}\,V_{n}\,\left(U_{\alpha}^{1}\right)^{-1}=e^{in\alpha}\,V_{n}\,,\quad x_{1}^{1}<x^{1}<x_{2}^{1}\,,

(2.21)

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}^{2}\,V_{n}\,\left(U_{\alpha}^{2}\right)^{-1}=e^{in\alpha}\,V_{n}\,,\quad x_{1}^{2}<x^{2}<x_{2}^{2}\,.

(2.22)

For the field action, we have

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\rightarrow\phi+\Lambda^{1}(x^{1})+\Lambda^{2}(x^{2})\,,

(2.23)

where

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{12}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi\,,

\displaystyle\hat{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{0}\phi\,,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\hat{J}^{12}_{0}-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\hat{J}=0\,.

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{1}(x^{1})=\oint dx^{2}\hat{J}^{12}_{0}\,,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{2}(x^{2})=\oint dx^{1}\hat{J}^{12}_{0}\,.

(2.28)

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{\beta}^{1}\left[[x_{1}^{1},x_{2}^{1}]\times\mathcal{C}^{2}\right]=\exp\left[i\beta\int_{x_{1}^{1}}^{x_{2}^{1}}dx^{1}\oint_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}dx^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi\right]\,,

\beta

2\pi

To summarize this appendix, here we compose the structure so far discussed into a mathematical system analogous to the de Rham complex, which we call the exotic de Rham complex.
Here we assume the 2+1-dimensional spacetime with flat foliations along every spatial dimension.

We can construct fractonic QFTs by using the foliation structure.
A foliation is a decomposition of a manifold into a stack of an infinite number of submanifolds, each of which we call a
leaf
.
We consider codimension-one foliations specified by the one-form foliation field
e

The foliation field

.
Given a foliation field

e

The foliation field
e
has a gauge transformation
e\rightarrow fe
, where
f
is a zero-form parameter.
QFTs coupled to the foliation structures on the spacetime are called foliated QFTs.
A foliation is called flat when
de=0
.
In this paper, we focus on the particular flat foliation fields that are aligned with the coordinate axes and fix the gauge by setting
e^{k}=dx^{k}\ (k=1,2,...)
, where
x^{k}
donates a spatial coordinate.
In 2+1 dimensions, we consider the two simultaneous foliations
e^{1}=dx^{1}
and
e^{2}=dx^{2}
, and the leaves are the lines orthogonal to the
x^{1}
direction and the lines orthogonal to the
x^{2}
direction.

Next, we introduce
U(1)
foliated gauge fields indexed by

They can be considered as
U(1)
gauge fields on the leaves orthogonal to the
x^{k}
direction.
We also introduce
U(1)
gauge fields that mediate foliated gauge fields, which are called
bulk
gauge fields.
We also refer to a pair of a foliated gauge field and a bulk gauge field as a foliated gauge field as well.
As in the case of the tensor gauge fields, the foliated gauge fields can have delta function singularities and step function discontinuities.
In the following, we consider a foliated QFT in 2+1 dimensions with two flat foliations
dx^{1}
and

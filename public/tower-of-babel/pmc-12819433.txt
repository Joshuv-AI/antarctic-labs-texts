Integration of fast charging EV infrastructure with high gain Z-source converters and hybrid optimized MPPT algorithm
Authors: R Sathish, V Sekar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractElectric Vehicles (EV) are considered as crucial elements in making changes towards power and transportation sector. Subsequently, the development fast charging infrastructure to support widespread adoption and efficient operation is required. To address the limitations, this research develops an EV fast charging system with the incorporation of a novel converter topology with optimization algorithms to improve power performance and effective management of energy. The voltage of Photovoltaic (PV) system is improved with the adoption of a high gain Z-source converter with switched topology resulting in improved system efficiency with lower switching stress. To improve the tracking performance of PV, the system uses hybrid Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) with Gray Relational Analysis (GRA) Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) algorithm offering maximum performance irrespective of variation in environmental condition. In addition, the bidirectional converter with battery is incorporated supported by Proportional Integral (PI) controller for optimal charging and discharging. Moreover, to accomplish uninterrupted supply, grid is integrated via three phase Voltage Source Inverter (VSI). MATLAB simulation outcomes demonstrates that, the proposed converter together with hybrid MPPT provide effective charging, with increased efficiency of 96.85%, and tracking efficiency of 99.27% effectively, making the proposed system optimal for fast charging application.Keywords: Gray relational analysis, Whale optimization algorithm, PV systems, Electric vehicles, MPPT algorithm, MATLABSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

The growing focus on environmental, technological, and economic benefits of Electric Vehicles (EVs) has lead the foundation for integrating transportation and electrical power systems1. An essential point of link between these two sectors is charging of EV batteries, which supply energy for vehicle’s propulsion2, control, and other systems. However, attaining energy from power grid for EV charging presents additional strain, particularly during periods of high demand. To tackle this issue, one effective solution is to shift toward Renewable Energy Sources (RESs)3,4 for EV charging. Incorporating cleaner energy, not only reduces the environmental footprint but also benefits in alleviating the load on grid and improve the overall efficiency of EV charging processes5.

EV charging stations can be powered using a variety of RESs, with PV systems, biogas, and wind energy being the most common options. Each of these renewable sources has its own advantages, but PV systems are often preferred due to their simple design and ease of integration6. The integration of EVs with PV power generation systems7 shown in Fig. 1, introduces operational challenges8. To tackle this issue more effectively, DC-DC power electronic converters has been developed that allows for improving PV voltage for EVCS. A Z-source converter presented in9 offers a unique solution by combining both functions in a single stage, excluding the need for an extra converter. Still, the enlarged control complexity and usage of large number of passive components leads to higher costs and massive system design. The Modified Z-source topology reported in10 supports in effective charging using grid and PV system. The designed model connects both input and output sides to boost voltage as needed for battery. Neverthless, the increased complexity necessitates control algorithms for smoother operation. Single switch modified Z-source model of11 improves the voltage generated from RESs, with the integration of switched inductor. This results in reduced ripple current and improves the longevity of sources. But, complexity arises owing to switching model and necessitates control for maintaining stability across operating modes. In reference12 active switched quazi Z-source topology is developed ensuring efficient delivery of power across different driving modes, but increased complexity in control, component stress limits in application like EV charging. Henceforth, in this research a novel converter topology is proposed to overcome the drawbacks and ensure efficient and reliable charging for EV applications.

To further improve the efficiency, integrating MPPT algorithm becomes crucial. Conventional MPPT techniques, including Perturb and Observe (P&O)13 and Hill Climbing14 methods are easy to implement and cost-effective, making them widely accepted. Nevertheless, they often encounters issues like slow convergence and oscillations around MPP, especially under inconsistent environmental conditions. Fractional Short Circuit15 offers faster tracking but compromise accuracy and lead to power losses.Intelligent MPPT controllers are emphasied to overcome the limitations, such as those based on Artificial Intelligence (AI) methods like fuzzy logic16, neural networks17 are more efficient to adapt to changing ecological conditions, providing accurate tracking of MPP. However, intelligent controllers18 face challenges such as design complexity, high computational demands, and sensitivity to initial conditions, which disturbs the performance and accuracy. These limitations highlight the need for optimization and hybrid strategies to enhance their effectiveness.

Bio-inspired optimization algorithms attains populaity owing to their adaptability and efficiency in complex systems. Genetic Algorithm (GA)19 inspired by natural selection, is effictive but leads to slow convergence. The Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)20 designed after flocking behaviour of birds, offer faster converts, however settle in suboptimal solutions. The Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) presented in literature21 shows improvement is predicting MPP, neverthless suffer from slow convergence and sensitive to parameters. The Artificial Bee Colony (ABC)22 are effective owing to their exploration towards larger search space, but struggles with local opimta in complex spaces. The Improved Differential Evolution Particle Swarm Optimization (IDEPSO) technique23 offers several merits, including faster convergence toward the maximum power point. IDEPSO is also more robust against partial shading and local maxima, making it suitable for complex PV environments. However, the approach requires more processing power and memory, which may not be ideal for low-cost embedded controllers. Parameter tuning is also challenging, as improper settings may lead to slower convergence or premature stagnation. As a consequence, hybrid approaches, are preferred for more reliable and efficient power tracking in dynamic environments such as EV charging systems. The proposed Whale Optimization Algorithm-Gray Relational Analysis (WOA-GRA) MPPT effectively addresses the key drawbacks observed in earlier bio-inspired algorithms particularly slow convergence, sensitivity to parameter tuning and high computational burden by combining the strengths of WOA and GRA. WOA provides rapid global searching through its encircling and bubble-net mechanisms, preventing premature trapping in local optima, while GRA dynamically evaluates solution quality, enabling faster selection of the most relevant search directions without relying on extensive parameter tuning. This hybridization significantly shortens convergence time and reduces the computational overhead because GRA simplifies fitness evaluation and minimizes unnecessary iterations. As a result, the WOA-GRA MPPT achieves both fast and stable convergence with fewer control parameters and lower processing requirements, making it well-suited for real-time PV power extraction in EV charging systems where rapid response and computational feasibility are essential.

Research gap

The literature review clearly highlights a significant research gap arising from the limitations of both existing converter topologies and MPPT techniques used in PV-powered EV charging systems. While various converters offer voltage boosting capability, they often suffer from increased structural complexity, a high number of passive components, greater switching stress and intricate control requirements making them less suitable for compact, efficient and cost-effective EV charging infrastructures. On the MPPT side, conventional algorithms struggle with slow convergence and oscillatory behavior under rapidly changing environmental conditions, whereas intelligent and bio-inspired techniques often demand high computational resources and precise parameter tuning, limiting their feasibility for real-time embedded applications. Existing hybrid approaches improve performance but still lack the balance between fast tracking, low computational burden and robustness under partial shading and dynamic EV load conditions. These collective shortcomings underline the need for a simplified yet high-gain converter topology paired with a fast, stable and computationally economical MPPT strategy.

Proposed modelling

With the enhancing demand for EV’s and the requirement for faster and effectual charging solutions, integrating renewable energy into charging infrastructure has become critical. A key challenge in traditional charging stations is high initial installation, long charging times, it doesn’t always sustainable or efficient and higher cost. Henceforth, the proposed work develops PV based EV charging with advanced converter and optimized approaches as signified by the block diagram in Fig. 2.

Block diagram of proposed work.

The PV system generates low output energy owing to the changing ecological conditions, which fed to the high gain Z-source converter with switched topology to enrich the voltage level to meet charging demand of EV battery. Moreover, for maximizing the power extracted from PV modules, hybrid WOA-RSA based MPPT approach is incorporated. Subsequently the enhanced DC supply is given to the EV battery via bidirectional battery converter, this converter manage the energy flow over the battery and load by ensuring the continuous supply needed for EV charging. Additionally, the excess amount of energy in PV is given to the grid system after distributing enough power supply to EV battery. In this context, voltage from DC-link is delivered to grid system and it gets controlled with the assistance of PI controller, thereby grid synchronization is attained. Finally, the enhanced power supply with minimized harmonics is delivered to the EV battery and three phase grid.

In the proposed PV–BESS–grid-integrated fast-charging system, energy dispatch to EV loads is prioritized through a hierarchical control strategy that ensures charging reliability even under low irradiance or limited battery capacity. When solar irradiance is sufficient, the high-gain Z-source converter and WOA-RSA MPPT algorithm extract maximum PV power for immediate EV charging, reducing dependency on the grid. During low irradiance periods, the bidirectional DC–DC converter evaluates the BESS state of charge (SoC); if the SoC remains above the predefined threshold, stored energy is allocated to meet EV charging demand. However, when both PV generation and BESS capacity are insufficient, the system automatically shifts to grid-support mode using the three-phase VSI to maintain uninterrupted EV charging. Grid export is deprioritized during such conditions, ensuring that available PV power and BESS energy are first reserved for EV charging before directing any surplus to the grid. This coordinated control mechanism effectively balances system stability, user charging needs and grid interaction.

During grid transients or sudden load fluctuations, the controller maintains DC-link voltage stability by dynamically coordinating power exchange among the PV array, battery storage system and the grid. The high-gain converter regulates PV power through fast MPPT tracking, preventing abrupt variations at the DC-link. Simultaneously, the bidirectional DC–DC converter modulates charging or discharging of the battery based on the deviation of the DC-link voltage from its reference: when the DC-link voltage rises due to reduced load or reverse grid power flow, the battery absorbs excess energy; when it drops during transients or high EV demand, the battery supplies compensating current. The three-phase VSI operates under a current-controlled scheme to ensure smooth grid interaction and the PI controller adjusts reactive and active power commands to avoid bidirectional current stress on the inverter during disturbances. This coordinated layered control ensures that each energy source responds proportionally to transients, keeping the DC-link voltage tightly regulated and preserving inverter reliability.

The proposed WOA-GRA MPPT technique exhibits strong computational feasibility due to its relatively low time complexity and reduced algorithmic overhead. Experimental Analysis of the Proposed System

The developed prototype illustrated in Fig. 22 is established using High gain Z-source topology for enhancing the voltage generated from PV. The FPGA Spartan 6E controller is the main control unit and is programmed with hybrid WOA-GRA algorithm included for tracking maximum PV power. It also manages the bidirectional power flow between the grid, DC link and battery (120 V). The DSO monitors and records the voltage, current and switching waveforms of the system analyzing the response. Challenges in hardware testing

Certain integration challenges were noticed in the hardware testing due to the need for real-time synchronization of multiple components. By conducting repeated simulations before prototype testing, these challenges were mitigated. Moreover, protection circuits and feedback loops were implemented for safeguarding the system from overcurrent and overvoltage conditions. The real-time implementation of the proposed system presents several practical challenges despite its strong theoretical and simulation performance. Implementing the high-gain Z-source converter with switched topology requires precise coordination of multiple switches, making the system highly sensitive to timing errors, dead-time mismatches and switching losses. The FPGA-based controller must execute the WOA-GRA MPPT and PI-based power management algorithms with strict timing constraints and any computational delay or quantization error can affect tracking accuracy and DC-link stability. Hardware limitations such as sensor noise, component tolerances, parasitic elements and thermal variations further complicate control tuning. Managing dynamic power flows among PV, battery and grid also demands robust protection mechanisms against overvoltage, reverse current and transient disturbances. Additionally, real-time synchronization with the three-phase grid, especially during fluctuations or low irradiance conditions, poses challenges in maintaining reliable inverter operation and energy exchange.

Conclusion

In this paper, a novel PV based High gain Switched Z-source converter with optimized MPPT approach is designed for EV charging system whichboostsup the volatge conversion with high efficiency, volatge gain and minimized stress. Also, the incorporation of WOA-GRA based MPPT ensures the system efficacy by providing precise tracking of PV system’s MPP and adresses solar energy variability. Moreover, the system utilizes bidirectional DC-DC converter for allowing the needed bidirectional power flow for battery charging and discharging based on SOC and limits the energy to overcharge the battery. Overall, the MATAB/Simulink is utilized for analyzing the efficacy of proposed work, from the obtained results, comparative analysis is done with the traditional approaches for showing the ability of developed work. The outcomes of proposed work results in improved performance metrics such as higher conversion efficiency of 96.85%, better voltage gain, minimized stresses, THD of 0.35% and the MPPT appraoach achieves high tracking efficiency of 99.27% with rapid response time. Henceforth, the proposed appraoch accelerates EV charging by the integration of renewable resource that contributes to an efficient and ecofriendly transportation. However, the converter’s performance may be affected by increased component stress during shoot-through states, which can lead to higher thermal losses and require careful protection circuitry. Future work can focus on developing advanced soft-switching techniques to reduce switching losses and exploring wide-bandgap semiconductor devices to boost efficiency and thermal handling. Further scalability studies, real-time digital control enhancements and reliability assessments under long-term field conditions can also strengthen its suitability for commercial PV systems.

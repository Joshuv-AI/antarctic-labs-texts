Application of AVL BOOST modeling to optimize the engine working load and drivetrain transmission ratio of the diesel firefighting pump system
Authors: Thong Duc Hong, Binh Nhat Nguyen, Phat Tan Truong, Son Hoang Do, Minh Quang Pham, Thang Viet Vu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe drivetrain is an essential component of the diesel firefighting pump system, affecting the engine's operating mode, power, economy, and environment. This study proposes a process to design and optimize the transmission ratio and working load of the diesel firefighting pump system. AVL BOOST software was used to model the 6-cylinder diesel engine and analyze the performance characteristics at its partial loads as parameters for finding the optimal transmission ratio of the drivetrain. The optimal transmission ratio determined in this work is 1:1.2, along with engine partial load mode of 57 %, which can improve the engine's fuel efficiency by 8 % compared to the 1:1 transmission ratio. A belt drive is calculated and designed to transfer power between the engine and the firefighting pump. The optimal drivetrain transmission ratio and engine working mode can increase the fuel economy and lifespan of the engine, decrease the engine's maintenance costs, and reduce environmental pollution and greenhouse gases.Keywords: Drivetrain design, Engine performance characteristics, Firefighting pump, AVL BOOST, Belt drive, Transmission ratio

Introduction

Due to the rapid industrial and service-based economic growth, many high-rise structures are constructed in residential neighborhoods to accommodate growing housing demand. Because of the high human density found in high-rise structures, it is crucial to prioritize fire safety and preventive measures. Specifically, in high-rise structures extending 120 m or more, firefighters frequently cannot access higher floors. Consequently, high-rise buildings require on-site firefighting pump systems [[1], [2], [3]].

In on-site diesel firefighting pump systems, the internal combustion engine-powered system is critical to ensure operation in the event of an electric power failure [4]. Typically, this system consists of a diesel engine, a centrifugal pump, and a drivetrain between them [5]. The fire fighting pump is chosen according to building height, floor area, fire suppression equipment density, and required pressure [6]. Based on that, a diesel engine possesses suitable speed and capacity, and a drive system such as a belt, chain, or propeller will be designed to drive the pump.

During the design process, the concordance of the fire pump and diesel engine is significant for the pumping system. Basically, the pump requires a diesel engine that produces higher power than the pump's input requirement. Manufacturers provide several engine models and types with various output parameters such as engine type, power, torque, operating speed, and engine casing type. Based on the required characteristics of the pump (input power, torque, and speed), the engineer chooses the engine that meets the requirements from the supplier's models, noting that it depends a lot on the supplier's availability. Then, a drivetrain must be designed to connect them, making the whole system operate in suitable power and speed ranges. The drivetrain parameters, such as transmission type, gear ratio, connection type, and transmission efficiency, will affect the operating efficiency of the entire system [[7], [8], [9]]. Thus, a carefully designed drivetrain can bring operational efficiency, helping to ensure capacity, reduce fuel consumption, and increase system lifespan. There were pretty a lot of studies focused on designing and optimizing the drivetrain for the vehicle's powertrain, such as optimizing the transmission ratio for heavy-duty trucks [10], optimizing the gear ratio with a novel gearshift process for electric vehicles [11], efficient method for choosing the transmission ratio of drivetrain for electrically powered vehicle [12]. However, as far as the authors know, no published study has investigated the influence of the drivetrain parameters on the performance of the engine firefighting pump system.

Another aspect of the diesel engine power apparatus is that it should only operate at less than 80 % of its maximum capacity to ensure engine longevity and durability [13]. But, in the past, engine selection was only based on full load characteristics, leading to errors in the actual operating capacity of the engine in the system. So, it is challenging to determine a suitable transmission ratio for the system because the engine operating speed, torque, and power, which depend on the internal characteristics, are unknown. To overcome this problem, simulation software, which allows us to simulate engine characteristics and calculate fuel consumption, combustion characteristics, and emissions [[14], [15], [16], [17]], is necessary to determine the engine's internal characteristics. In particular, AVL BOOST is a significantly useful software for modeling the engine. Juknelevičius et al. [18] examined the performances and emissions of a compression-ignition engine powered by hydrogen and diesel mixture at two engine speeds of 1900 rpm and 2500 rpm at three different brake mean effective pressures (BMEP). Juknelevičius et al. [19] studied how hydroxygen injection affects the performance, combustion, and emissions of compression ignition engines powered by diesel fuel, as well as how hydroxygen impacts the combustion of diesel fuel when water is injected. The researchers utilized a combination of experiments and simulations to investigate the influence of hydrogen and injection of water on combustion at different engine loads at the engine speed of 1900 rpm. Zhang et al. [20] evaluated the impact of various blend ratios of ethanol and diesel on the performance and emissions of the engine. AVL BOOST program was utilized to create the simulation model, and the experiment results at 50 % and 100 % loads were also employed to validate this model. Hong et al. [21] utilized AVL BOOST software to study three different engine models at three different loads: 91 % load for Model 1, 74 % load for Model 2, and 57 % load for Model 3 with engine speeds of 2970, 2960, and 2490 rpm, respectively. The result showed that Model 2 is the best option regarding engine power, initial expenditure, fuel consumption efficiency, and pollution. Hong et al. [22] created the simulation model by AVL BOOST with the DESSUN 4DSP-75 diesel engine driven by the CPHM 80/32 Kirloskar pump for a 79-m residential building. An appropriate partial load performance is established to fulfill the necessary power of the firefighting pump at a speed of around 2975 rpm. Zhang et al. [23] performed engine experiments at the partial load (90 %) and applied AVL BOOST software to create the model of the real engine. Then, the study utilized the mixing control combustion model to run the simulation of the engine and verify the results by utilizing actual test values, such as engine performance and cylinder pressure readings.

Previous studies showed that AVL software has excellent potential for designing diesel engine drivetrain systems. However, the application of AVL software to support drivetrain design for diesel firefighting pump systems has not been addressed previously, particularly in modeling engine partial load performances and optimizing the drivetrain transmission ratio to improve fuel efficiency, which reduces emissions and greenhouse gases and prolongs the engine's lifespan. Hence, in the present work, the authors attempt to optimize a diesel engine drivetrain for a firefighting pump system by applying AVL BOOST software to investigate the engine's partial operating characteristics. First, the authors apply AVL BOOST software to compute the engine characteristic curves. Then, based on that, the system operating equilibrium point and transmission ratio of the drivetrain connecting the engine and pump are optimized to improve the system in terms of fuel consumption. Besides, the belt drive system will be constructed to fulfill the functioning demands of the engine-to-pump drivetrain.

The goals of this study are (1) to discover the optimal operating point and drivetrain transmission ratio of a diesel firefighting pump system using the characteristic curves obtained from AVL BOOST simulation, and (2), based on that, design a suitable drivetrain to meet the system requirements. These results provide a realistic way to construct an efficient drivetrain of diesel firefighting pump systems in both technical and economic aspects.

Materials and methodology

Scheme of diesel firefighting pump system and components characteristics

The diesel engine-driven firefighting pump system scheme is shown in Fig. 1, which is normally used in the on-site firefighting system. It includes a stationary engine, a water pump, and a drivetrain; all are fixed on the frame of the on-site firefighting system. The engine power, Pe, torque, Te, and speed, ne, are generated from the diesel engine and go through a drivetrain with a transmission efficiency, ηt, and a transmission ratio, it, to meet the requirements of the system. Then, they are transmitted to a centrifugal pump, which requires input power, Pp,in, input torque, Tp,in, and speed, np. With the pump efficiency, ηp, the pump generates output power, Pp,out, pump output head, and pump output flow rate. The synchronous belt drive was chosen among several drive options based on the working conditions and design requirements due to its high efficiency and compact size. Similar to the chain drive, it can be designed with a suitable transmission ratio but can work stably at higher speeds, does not need lubrication, and requires less maintenance. In addition, compared with common belt drives of V-belt drive or flat belt drive, it ensures the transmission ratio is constant, meeting the requirements for the speed and load of the pump system. In short, this synchronous belt drive is the most suitable and optimized for this application [24].

Results and discussions

Determining engine working conditions and transmission ratio

The engine characteristics at 62 % and 57 % load are illustrated and compared to engine performance at 100 % load in Fig. 7. Reducing the engine load from 100 % to 62 % and 57 % results in a corresponding decrease in both torque and power output of the engine. Moreover, Fig. 8 shows how the engine's performance compares to the pump's needs. It can be seen that at full power (100 % load) and 2975 rpm, the engine delivers more power than the pump requires. This would cause the pump speed to increase and not reach its designed pressure and flow rate. To address this issue, engine output power must be declined. This is achieved by gradually reducing the engine load until the pump's input characteristics at 2975 rpm (pump's rated speed) match its required power and torque characteristics. At 62 % load, the engine's torque and power at the pump shaft meet the pump's necessary speed of 2975 rpm so that the transmission ratio will be determined of 1:1. Engine load is further reduced for even greater fuel efficiency and longer engine life; therefore, a 57 % load condition is proposed, corresponding to a fuel consumption reduction of 8 % compared to that of 62 % load level. However, at 57 % load, the engine performance at the pump shaft is lower than the pump's input requirements at 2975 rpm, leading to engine shutdown and endangering fire safety. So, a transmission ratio 1:1.2 is chosen when the pump runs at 2975 rpm, and the engine runs at approximately 2480 rpm. Also, engine characteristics at the pump shaft and the pump's requirement become equal at a speed of 2975 rpm. This approach allows the engine to run at a lower power output while delivering enough power to the pump through the increased transmission ratio. Finally, by running the engine at 57 % load with a belt drive transmission ratio of 1:1.2, the system can achieve the required pressure and flow rate while gaining a bunch of benefits.

Regarding fuel economy, engine lifespan, and maintenance cost, the optimization results show that the fuel amount is reduced by 8 % when using the optimal transmission ratio. Theoretically, when the engine load is reduced, the working pressure and working temperature are reduced [21], helping to reduce the engine's working load and resulting in increased engine lifespan [39]. Therefore, optimizing the transmission ratio and characteristics improves the engine's lifespan. Moreover, an engine running at a lower load will require less maintenance and fewer gradual repairs, saving maintenance expenses. Engines with a reduction in fuel consumption produce less exhaust and greenhouse gases, especially soot [[40], [41], [42]]. The previous study also showed similar results when examining emissions from engines running at low loads [21].

Designing the pump's drivetrain system

Fig. 9 presents the structure of the synchronous belt drive mechanism in the top view and exploded view. The synchronous belt drive cluster includes a timing belt, driving and driven pulleys, two tapper bushings, and a flange coupling, playing the role of connecting and delivering the engine's power to the pump. The flange coupling is designed to be securely attached to the flywheel by bolt joints, and then, the driving pulley will be snugly secured to the flange coupling's shaft via the tapper bush's cone surface and the feather key's shaft. Meanwhile, the driven pulley is only directly attached to the water pump shaft by a set of tapper bushes and feather keys.

Conclusions

This study presents a new method to design and optimize the diesel firefighting pump system using AVL BOOST software and engineering calculation. AVL BOOST software is applied to build the engine's partial performance and determine the optimal engine operating condition and transmission ratio. Then, the drivetrain, containing a belt drive, shaft, flange, and key, was constructed. The main conclusions can be extracted from this work as follows:

➢The simulation model established by using AVL BOOST software can be used to determine the engine's partial loads. The simulation model is validated by the actual characteristics provided by the manufacturer with an error below 5 %.

The simulation model established by using AVL BOOST software can be used to determine the engine's partial loads. The simulation model is validated by the actual characteristics provided by the manufacturer with an error below 5 %.

➢From the obtained partial loads, the engine working condition and transmission ratio of the drivetrain are optimized. In this study, the optimal transmission ratio is 1:1.2 with an engine working load of 57 %, which can reduce engine fuel consumption by 8 % compared to the 1:1 transmission ratio.

From the obtained partial loads, the engine working condition and transmission ratio of the drivetrain are optimized. In this study, the optimal transmission ratio is 1:1.2 with an engine working load of 57 %, which can reduce engine fuel consumption by 8 % compared to the 1:1 transmission ratio.

➢In addition, a belt drive is designed to be suitable for the diesel firefighting pump system with the selected engine, pump, and computed working condition and transmission ratio.

In addition, a belt drive is designed to be suitable for the diesel firefighting pump system with the selected engine, pump, and computed working condition and transmission ratio.

➢The reduction in fuel consumption resulting from this study leads to increased fuel economy, reduced environmental pollution and greenhouse gases, improved engine lifespan, and reduced maintenance costs.

The reduction in fuel consumption resulting from this study leads to increased fuel economy, reduced environmental pollution and greenhouse gases, improved engine lifespan, and reduced maintenance costs.

The AVL BOOST software modeling method can be applied to different types of engines (gasoline, diesel, gas, etc.) with varying engine power to optimize both the transmission ratio and working mode of the mechanical apparatus driven by internal combustion engines. Therefore, this study contributes to the fundamentals of designing efficient internal combustion engine drivetrain systems in technical and financial terms.

However, this study only focuses on optimizing the operating characteristics of the engine along with the transmission ratio, while other factors of the drivetrain, such as mechanical losses and drivetrain configuration, also affect performance [8,46]. Thus, the future research direction may be to enhance the system's performance by optimizing these factors. In addition, the optimal transmission ratio also impacts the lifespans of the engine and the fire pump system. Still, it is also affected by many other factors and needs to be further investigated in the future to be able to be evaluated quantitatively. Finally, although the optimization result reduces fuel consumption and engine load, improving emission problems, the emissions also need further investigation, which may be the direction of the subsequent research.

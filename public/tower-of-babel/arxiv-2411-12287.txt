CUE-M: Contextual Understanding and Enhanced Search with Multimodal Large Language Model
Dongyoung Go, Taesun Whang, Chanhee Lee, Hwa-Yeon Kim, Sunghoon Park, Seunghwan Ji, Jinho Kim, Dongchan Kim, Young-Bum Kim

Abstract
The integration of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has revolutionized information retrieval and expanded the practical applications of AI. However, current systems struggle in accurately interpreting user intent, employing diverse retrieval strategies, and effectively filtering unintended or inappropriate responses, limiting their effectiveness. This paper introduces Contextual Understanding and Enhanced Search with MLLM (CUE-M), a novel multimodal search framework that addresses these challenges through a multi-stage pipeline comprising image context enrichment, intent refinement, contextual query generation, external API integration, and relevance-based filtering. CUE-M incorporates a robust filtering pipeline combining image-based, text-based, and multimodal classifiers, dynamically adapting to instance- and category-specific concern defined by organizational policies. Extensive experiments on real-word datasets and public benchmarks on knowledge-based VQA and safety demonstrated that CUE-M outperforms baselines and establishes new state-of-the-art results, advancing the capabilities of multimodal retrieval systems.

1
Introduction

The integration of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with Large Language Models (LLMs) has transformed information retrieval, broadening the scope of AI applications.
However, most RAG systems remain unimodal, limiting their capacity
to address queries requiring reasoning across textual and visual modalities.
Early efforts in multimodal RAG, leveraging advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), have largely relied on simplistic methods like appending image-derived information to text-based inference
(
Caffagni et al., 2024
)
, limiting their effectiveness in complex reasoning tasks.

Key challenges in multimodal RAG systems lie in three areas: accurately interpreting user intent, employing diverse information retrieval strategies, and effectively filtering unintended or inappropriate responses. Previous systems often lack flexible retrieval approaches, restricting their coverage and ability to adapt to complex user needs.
Moreover, the integration of text and image modalities amplifies concerns about generating unintended or inappropriate responses, highlighting the need for robust and adaptable filtering mechanisms that can dynamically align with organizational policies.

To address these challenges, we propose Contextual Understanding and Enhanced Search with MLLM (CUE-M), a novel multimodal search framework that enhances MLLMs by integrating external knowledge sources and applications through a multi-stage pipeline.
The pipeline includes
image context enrichment, intent refinement, contextual query generation, external API integration, and relevance-based filtering, enabling effective and efficient multimodal search capabilities.
CUE-M incorporates a comprehensive filtering framework that integrates image-based, text-based, and multimodal classifiers, ensuring a balance between efficiency and robustness.
This framework dynamically adapts to instance-specific and category-specific criteria defined by organizational policies, ensuring compliance and responsiveness to evolving concerns.

We evaluate CUE-M’s performance across multiple experimental settings to assess its effectiveness, including a curated multimodal Q&A dataset derived from Naver Knowledge-iN, Encyclopedic VQA
(
Mensink et al., 2023
)
, and MM-SafetyBench
(
Liu et al., 2024b
)
.
Our experiments demonstrate that CUE-M significantly outperforms baseline MLLMs in addressing questions that require external knowledge integration
and achieves safety performance comparable to existing models.

Overall, the contributions of this paper are as follows:
First, we introduce CUE-M, a novel service-level multimodal RAG pipeline that enhances MLLMs by integrating external knowledge sources and applications; Second, we demonstrate that CUE-M substantially improves the generation quality of MLLMs on queries requiring external knowledge, validated on both real-world and public dataset; Third, we present a comprehensive safety evaluation of CUE-M, showing its effectiveness in handling safety issues in multimodal scenarios.

Related Works

Multimodal information retrieval.

CLIP-based models
Radford et al. (2021)
;
Pan et al. (2022)
have improved cross-modal embeddings for images and text through contrastive learning. However, they lack integrated multimodal fusion, making it challenging to handle multimodal queries
Changpinyo et al. (2021)
.
Most multimodal retrieval approaches focus on a limited range of cross-modal tasks, such as image retrieval from text queries
(

;

)

Luo et al. (2023)
.

Wei et al. (2023)
combined embeddings from different modalities using score-level and feature-level fusion.

Retrieval-Augmented Multimodal Model.

The success of RAG in LLMs

;

for supporting knowledge-based question-answering and mitigating hallucination suggests the potential for multimodal models.

Yasunaga et al. (2022)
introduced a dense multimodal retriever
that pools text and image embeddings but struggles with incorporating external knowledge, such as product names, and lacks essential deployment components like safety filters.

Unlike previous methods, our approach focuses on multimodal query retrieval, integrating external knowledge

Method

For multimodal queries with image input, the pipeline begins with image-enriched information retrieval, extracting relevant data based on the input image (Sec.
3.1
). This retrieved data is utilized to refine the user’s query into a structured intent and a corresponding search query (Sec.
3.2
). The refined intent guides subsequent actions, determining whether additional queries or API calls are required (Sec.
3.3
and Sec.
3.4
).
After gathering all relevant information, a relevance classifier filters content to ensure precision, and the final response is generated from a curated document set (Sec.
3.5
). To ensure robustness, the pipeline incorporates a comprehensive safety filter (Sec.
3.6
) and automated prompt-tuning for optimized generation (Sec.
3.7
).
The complete framework is depicted in Fig.

.

3.1
Image-Enriched Information Retrieval

The primary challenge in extending LLM-based RAG to a multimodal setting lies in accurately interpreting images and generating appropriate queries. This is especially difficult for multimodal queries, where image comprehension often requires external knowledge.
To extract general information from images, the system leverages image captioning, similar image searches, and tag-based text searches.

Image description.

To provide a general understanding of the input image, MLLMs generate general-purpose descriptions, a well-established task in multimodal research
Mao (2014)
;
Xu (2015)
;
Pan et al. (2020)
.

Similar image search.

To address queries requiring specific information about an image, such as identifying a product within the image, the pipeline retrieves relevant documents containing images similar to the query image, along with extracted tags from these documents (See App.
G
for the details of tag extraction process).

Image tag text search.

To provide broader context
for the input image, image tags are used for text-based searches. Unlike previous methods that directly append image tags to user queries
Qu et al. (2021)
;
Gao et al. (2022a)
;
Gui et al. (2021)
;
Yang et al. (2022)
;
Surla et al. (2024)
, our approach adopts a multi-step process followed by an intent refiner, enhancing the system’s ability to handle complex queries.

3.2
Intention Refinement

Building on the information from the previous steps, a few-shot prompted LLM
1
1

1

, referred to as the Intention Refiner in Fig.
2
, refines the user’s query into a clear intent and a corresponding search query to address the user’s information need.
This process involves appending the user’s original query with summaries derived from the initial document retrieval and image descriptions, followed by prompting the LLM to consolidate this input into a refined intention with a structured format (see Tab.
4
for the prompt used in the Intention Refiner).
This process
enhances the robustness of the framework by filtering out erroneous documents from the previous stage and improves clarity by resolving ambiguities in implicit user queries.
The refined intention is subsequently used for multiple critical functions in the pipeline: generating supplementary queries, selecting the appropriate application for further processing, and filtering out unsafe queries.

3.3
Query generation

Image-Enriched Retrieval, which augments queries with partial modality information, often retrieves documents with limited coverage (see App.
B.2
for examples of complex queries).
To enhance retrieval, MLLM is utilized to review the retrieved documents based on refined intention and generate supplementary queries. By leveraging the information from Image-Enriched Retrieval, the supplementary queries can incorporate information from external knowledge.

3.4
Application Selection

To accommodate diverse queries, the framework integrates a generative model, the API Selector, to identify the most relevant APIs beyond a general search engine. These APIs include specialized services such as a Shopping API for identifying image-based products or a Map API for retrieving reviews of local businesses. The API Selector evaluates each API-query pair using the refined user intent and a brief API description. The evaluation is performed in parallel for all APIs and ranked by the likelihood of generating a positive token.

3.5
Answer generation

The Answer Generation component extracts the most relevant information from the user query and collected data to generate the final response. Using documents from previous stages, a relevance classifier—an ELECTRA-based model
Clark (2020)
with an added classification layer—is employed to rank and select the top-
k
relevant documents (see App.
H
for details of the training process).
This classifier ensures the quality of the selected documents while reducing them to a manageable size.
In the final step, MLLM generates the an answer to user’s query based on this curated set of documents.

3.6
Safety filter

Figure 3:
A high-level architecture of safety pipeline. The pipeline integrates light-weight and multimodal classifiers, with two training-free filtering approaches.

Our safety pipeline (Fig.
3
) is a multi-stage system designed to provide robust protection for multimodal queries while balancing between efficiency and comprehensive coverage.
The pipeline begins with lightweight classifiers for rapid preliminary filtering: a text-based classifier for user queries and an image-based classifier for visual content.
For complex multimodal safety concerns requiring nuanced reasoning, a few-shot prompted LLM is employed with refined intent instead of directly prompting an MLLM with the original query
(
Liu et al., 2025
;
Lin et al., 2024
;
Liu et al., 2024b
;
Gao et al., 2024
)
.
Additionally, to dynamically address emerging safety concerns, two training-free methods are integrated: instance-wise filtering and category-wise filtering.
Instance-wise filtering uses a database of predefined query-response pairs. If a multimodal user query matches an unsafe entry in the database based on embedding similarity, the system delivers a safe response.
Category-wise filtering, on the other hand, addresses broader topics regulated by organizational policies, such as political or medical advice. If the API Selector chose safety-relevant categories, a predefined category-level response is delivered.

3.7
Automatic prompt tuning

The proposed method integrates various LLM- and MLLM-based sub-modules that rely heavily on prompts.
To enhance efficiency and adaptability to dynamic data, we adopt automatic prompt tuning
(
Zhou et al., 2022
;
Sordoni et al., 2023
;
Wang et al., 2023
)
.
A curated dataset is used to define the desired behavior for each sub-module, derived from user query data. Following
Sordoni et al. (2023)
, the model iteratively refines prompts by analyzing and addressing errors from prior iterations
(see App.
E
for additional details).

5
Conclusion

We introduced CUE-M, a multimodal search framework that enhances MLLMs by integrating external knowledge through a multi-stage pipeline and robust filtering mechanisms.
Experiments show that CUE-M achieves strong performance in generating accurate, contextually enriched responses while ensuring robust safety in multimodal retrieval, effectively addressing key challenges in the field.

Appendix E
Automatic prompt tuning

Following
Sordoni et al. (2023)
, we implemented a prompt training method utilizing both forward and backward passes. During the forward pass, an LLM applies an initial forward template to predict labels for training samples. In the backward pass, correct and incorrect predictions are sampled, then incorporated as in-context examples to refine the forward template.
To maintain consistency and adherence to internal policies, certain sections of the refined template are replaced with a fixed system prompt. The final prompt is selected after iterative tuning, based on its performance on validation samples, ensuring optimal effectiveness for downstream tasks.

Appendix G
Extracting image tag based on similar image search

We utilize multiple strategies to generate tags for an image. First, text tags are derived by analyzing the search queries that users interacted with when selecting the image of interest from a search results page. This approach leverages user behavior to identify text tags that naturally align with how users search for and discover related content. Second, performing a similar image search yields documents containing visually similar images, from which relevant keywords can be extracted. Third, if the similar image search identifies an image with a high similarity score, and this retrieved image originates from an in-house or reputable source with human-annotated tags, those tags are incorporated. Finally, the resulting set of keywords is refined and prioritized using a heuristic-based ranking algorithm.

Appendix H
Training relevance classifier

To filter highly relevant documents for search queries, we employ a text-based relevance classifier built upon a lightweight cross-encoder architecture
Clark (2020)
. The classifier takes a search query and a candidate document as input and outputs a relevance score that indicates the semantic relevance of the document to the query.

To effectively train the classifier, we construct a high-quality dataset that mirrors genuine user interactions and captures the nuanced relationships between queries and documents. This approach ensures that the model learns from meaningful examples and can generalize well to real-world scenarios. We start by collecting queries from search engines where knowledge snippets are prominently displayed to users, ensuring the dataset reflects genuine information needs and high-quality responses.

To further refine the dataset, we utilize an off-the-shelf pre-trained model trained on the Natural Language Inference (NLI) task. Specifically, we define positive examples as query and knowledge snippet answer pairs with an NLI score exceeding 0.9, indicating strong entailment and high semantic relevance. Negative examples are generated by pairing the same queries with other documents that yield an NLI score below 0.3, representing contradiction or irrelevance. The ratio of positive to negative examples is set to 1:4, as this ratio performed best on the evaluation set.

Theta operators and a generic entailment for GSp 4 \text{GSp}_{4}
Martin Ortiz

Abstract
. We construct a new family of mod p p weight shifting differential operators on the Siegel threefold. In particular, we construct one operator which generalizes the classical theta cycle, whose weight shift allows for maps between p p -restricted weights, and which is generically injective on global sections. As an application we produce a generic entailment of Serre weights, i.e. any Hecke eigenform which is modular for a generic Serre weight in the lowest alcove is also modular for a Serre weight in one of the upper alcoves. The entailed Serre weight corresponds to a shadow weight of the lowest alcove Serre weight, in Herzig’s conjectural description of W ⁡ ( ρ ¯ ) W(\overline{\rho}) .

(Klingen).
One can attach mod
p
Galois representations to mod
p
eigensystems appearing in the cohomology of
the locally symmetric spaces for these Levi subgroups. In fact, in our case these locally symmetric spaces
are essentially the modular curve, where the construction of mod
p
Galois representations is a classical result.
That way, as in the proof of
[
30
, Thm 4.2]

one can show that the Galois representation attached to
the Hecke eigenclasses appearing in the boundary are always a direct sum of
Galois representations attached to one of the proper rational Levis. Therefore, as a
\textnormal{GL}_{4}
-valued
representation
\overline{r}_{\mathfrak{m}}

is respectively the
sum of
4
characters (Borel), the sum of two
2
-dimensional representations (Siegel),
or the sum of
2
characters and a
2
-dimensional representation (Klingen).
The second equality is proved by mimicking the proof of
[
3
, Thm 24.iii)]
,
which concerns unitary Shimura varieties.
Namely, the boundary of
\overline{\textnormal{Sh}}^{\textnormal{tor}}
is also stratified by components labelled by rational parabolics.
The Galois representations attached to these components are then summands of

\overline{r}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. We also note that
as in the unitary case
\textnormal{GSp}_{4}
is self-dual, so that their proof carries over to our setup.

For the first implication about the existence of nontrivial eigensystems,
the vanishing theorem of
[
15
]
and the first equality imply that

\textnormal{H}^{*}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\textnormal{Sh}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},V(a,b)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is concentrated in degree
3
.
This means that we can lift any non-trivial mod
p
eigensystem to

\textnormal{H}^{3}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\textnormal{Sh}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},V(a,b)_{\check{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
. The latter is also torsion-free,
so that there exists an ideal
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathbb{T}
corresponding
to an integral Hecke eigensystem in
\textnormal{H}^{*}_{\textnormal{\'{e}t}}(\textnormal{Sh}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},V(a,b)_{\check{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Then

(5.1)

\textnormal{H}^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textnormal{Sh}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},V(a,b)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})_{\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}}=\oplus_{w\in W^{M}}\textnormal{H}^{l(w)}(\textnormal{Sh}^{\textnormal{tor}}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},\omega(w\cdot(a,b)+(3,3)))_{\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}}

by

[
10
]
, and the rational
étale-crystalline comparison theorem. Moreover, since the Galois
representation attached to
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}
is irreducible we have that the non-vanishing of the left-hand side
implies the non-vanishing of any of
\textnormal{H}^{l(w)}(\textnormal{Sh}^{\textnormal{tor}}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},\omega(w\cdot(a,b)+(3,3)))_{\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
.
This follows from the fact in the étale cohomology localized at such
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}

(concretely in the case where the underlying
automorphic representation is not CAP, which have reducible
Galois representations
[
34
, Thm 2]
) one only sees contributions from automorphic representations

\pi

whose
components at infinity belong to the discrete series
[
35
, §4.1]
.
Then
[
34
, Prop 1.5]
(using that weakly endoscopic representations
are Eisenstein) implies that the multiplicity of
the (anti)holomorphic discrete series
\pi_{\infty,\text{hol}}

equals the one of the non-holomorphic discrete series
\pi_{\infty,\text{n-hol}}

[
35
, §1.1]
.
Moreover, the multiplicity of
\pi_{\infty,\text{hol}}
contributes to
coherent cohomology in degree
0
and
3
, and the multiplicity of
\pi_{\infty,\text{n-hol}}

contributes to coherent cohomology in degree
1
and
2
.
By rescaling an eigenclass to land on

\textnormal{H}^{l(w)}(\textnormal{Sh}^{\textnormal{tor}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}},\omega(w\cdot(a,b)+(3,3)))_{\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}}
and reducing mod
p

we get one implication.
For the other direction,
by
Theorem

2.12
(1)
\textnormal{H}^{1}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}}^{\textnormal{tor}},\omega^{\textnormal{sub}}(a+3,b+3))=0

under the restrictions on the weights, so by the second equality we can lift eigensystems in

\textnormal{H}^{0}(\textnormal{Sh}^{\textnormal{tor}}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\omega^{\textnormal{can}}(a+3,b+3))_{\mathfrak{m}}

to
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\textnormal{Sh}^{\textnormal{tor}}_{\check{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}},\omega^{\textnormal{can}}(a+3,b+3))_{\mathfrak{m}}
,
which is moreover torsion-free. Using (
5.1
) and reducing mod
p
implies what we want.
∎

We can now explain how
Theorem

4.12
proves a generic entailment of Serre weights.
Given
\lambda_{0}=(a,b)\in C_{0}
let
\lambda_{1}=(p-b-3,p-a-3)\in C_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}=(p+b-1,p-a-3)\in C_{2}

be the corresponding affine Weyl reflections.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{0}=(a,b)\in C_{0}
satisfying
b\geq 1
,
p\geq 5
, and
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathbb{T}
a non-Eisenstein generic eigensystem
such that
F(\lambda_{0})\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
. Then
F(\lambda_{1})\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
or

F(\lambda_{2})\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

2.10
we have the exact sequence

0\to F(\lambda_{1})\to V(\lambda_{1})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\to F(\lambda_{0})\to 0

in

\textnormal{Rep}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\textnormal{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
By the concentration of étale cohomology in
Proposition

5.1
we have that

\textnormal{H}^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textnormal{Sh}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},V(\lambda_{1})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
,
which implies that
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(p-b,p-a))_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
.
Assuming that
b\geq 1

the map
\theta^{1}_{(p-b,p-a)}
is injective on global sections localized at
\mathfrak{m}
by
Theorem

4.12
,
so that

\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(p+b+2,p-a))_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
. By applying
Proposition

5.1
again we obtain

\textnormal{H}^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textnormal{Sh}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},V(\lambda_{2})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
.
By
Proposition

2.10

0\to F(\lambda_{2})\to V(\lambda_{2})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\to F(\lambda_{1})\to 0

is exact, so we conclude by taking its long exact sequence in étale cohomology.
∎

5.3.
Some non-generic entailments

We can also prove some non-generic entailments using
H_{1}
and
\theta_{1}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be non-Eisenstein and generic, and
p\geq 5
. Suppose that
F(a,0)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
for some

0\leq a\leq p-1
. Then

(1)

F(a+p-1,p-1)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
.

(2)

If
a\leq p-4
then
F(a+p+1,p-1)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
.

(3)

Let
\lambda_{0}=(a,b)\in X_{1}(T)
such that
a+b=p-3
,
b\neq 0
, and
F(a,b)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
. Then

F(2p-a-4,b)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

5.1
we have
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a+3,3))_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
. Applying
H_{1}
yields

\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a+p+2,p+2))_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
, so we get
1)
by the converse direction of
Proposition

5.1
.
Similarly, we have that
\theta_{1}
is injective on
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a+3,3))
: if a form is in its kernel it must be
divisible by
H_{1}
by
Proposition

4.7
, and
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a-p+4,4-p))_{\mathfrak{m}}=0
by
Theorem

2.12
(2).
Therefore,
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a+p+4,p+2))_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
, and we conclude by
Proposition

5.1
(2).
For
(3)
we still have that
\theta^{1}_{(a+3,b+3)}
is injective
on
\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a+3,b+3))
, since we can reduce to injectivity of
\theta_{1}
by

Theorem

4.12
, which follows by
Proposition

4.7
and
Theorem

2.12
(2).
In this case
V(2p-a-4,b)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}=F(2p-a-4,b)
by
Proposition

2.10
, which produces the entailment.
∎

Point
(3)
is a boundary case of
Theorem

5.2
, where
\lambda_{0}
is in the boundary of
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
.
One would also expect the entailment
F(a,a)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})\implies F(a+p-1,a)\in W(\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}})
,
but it seems more difficult to obtain with our techniques, since
\theta_{4}
is identically zero on

\textnormal{H}^{0}(\overline{\textnormal{Sh}},\omega(a+3,a+3))
.

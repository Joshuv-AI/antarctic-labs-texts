RRAEDy: adaptive latent linearization of nonlinear dynamical systems
Authors: Jad Mounayer, Sebastian Rodriguez, Jerome Tomezyk, Chady Ghnatios, Francisco Chinesta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMany existing latent-space models for dynamical systems require the latent dimension to be fixed in advance, rely on tuning loss functions to approximate linear dynamics, and do not regularize the latent variables. We introduce RRAEDy, a model that overcomes these limitations by automatically discovering the appropriate latent dimension while enforcing regularized and approximately linear dynamics in the latent space. RRAEDy builds on Rank-Reduction Autoencoders (RRAEs) and progressively ranks and prunes latent variables based on their singular values. At the same time, it learns a latent Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) operator that governs the temporal evolution of the system. This structure-free yet linearly constrained formulation enables the model to learn stable, low-dimensional dynamics without auxiliary loss terms or manual tuning. In addition, the proposed architecture modifies the latent variables before passing them to the decoder, reducing the need to learn an inverse mapping at the start of training. The stability of the learned DMD operator is proven through a theoretical analysis. Experiments on standard benchmarks, including the Van der Pol oscillator, Burgers’ equation, 2D Navier–Stokes flow, and rotating Gaussians, show that RRAEDy produces accurate and robust predictions. Our code is open-source and available https://github.com/JadM133/RRAEDy. Introduction

Dynamical systems underlie a wide range of natural and engineered processes, like the cooling of a cup of coffee or the motion of a vehicle. Many such systems can be described (or approximated) by an ordinary differential equation (ODE)

where denotes the system’s state and its governing dynamics. When is known, numerical solvers can predict future states or simulate new trajectories. In practice, however, is rarely known and must be inferred from data.

Classical system identification

Classical methods estimate under structural assumptions. Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) 1 assumes linearity, while Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamical Systems (SINDy) 2 seeks a sparse combination of predefined basis functions. Symbolic regression and autoregressive formulations provide further flexibility, but all depend on strong prior assumptions about the functional form of .

Neural approaches

To overcome these constraints, neural approaches replace with a neural network, exploiting its universal approximation ability. Continuous-time variants include Neural ODEs 3 and Neural Controlled Differential Equations (Neural CDEs) 4, which learn smooth latent trajectories by integrating a neural network over time. Discrete-time counterparts include Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs), and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) 5, 6, 7, as well as Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) 8. While these neural formulations are highly expressive, they often exhibit practical difficulties. In continuous-time settings, gradients may vanish or explode 9, and optimization can converge to poor local minima10, 11. Moreover, without explicit structure or constraints, purely neural models tend to overfit and yield unstable long-term forecasts12.

Latent-space modeling

Because learning directly in the high-dimensional data space can be inefficient, many works learn dynamics in a latent space where evolution is simpler. Latent ODEs 13 use a variational autoencoder to define this space, while Recurrent Autoencoder Networks (RAEs) 14 couple autoencoders with recurrent cells. Despite good empirical results, these models usually require manual tuning of the latent dimension, and do not resolve the overfitting issue since no structure is imposed on the dynamics. Further, this kind of technique requires the decoder to learn the inverse of the encoder, which is challenging at the beginning of the training when the latent representation is not meaningful yet.

Koopman-inspired models

From Koopman theory 15, 16, any nonlinear dynamical system can be represented linearly in a suitably chosen function space. This idea inspired methods such as the Deep Learning Enhanced DMD 17, DMD Autoencoders 18, and Koopman Autoencoders 19, which learn an autoencoded latent representation with linear time evolution. However, these approaches (i) fix the latent dimension a priori, which is problematic when the system’s intrinsic dimensionality is unknown, (ii) do not regularize their latent spaces, so they might struggle on data with local behavior, and (iii) rely on delicate weighting of multiple losses, which hampers training stability.

Our approach

To address these challenges, we build upon Rank Reduction Autoencoders (RRAEs) 20, which adaptively determine the latent dimension via a truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) in the latent space. RRAEs enforce a low-rank structure without additional loss terms, improving stability and interpretability. Both RRAEs, and their variants21, 22, show that some latent properties of autoencoders can be imposed without complicating the loss function. Building on this principle, we introduce RRAEDy, an autoencoder architecture that learns the appropriate latent dimension, while enforcing regularization and linearization on the latent dynamics. RRAEDy combines the adaptive bottleneck of aRRAEs with a dynamically learned DMD operator that governs latent evolution. By construction, this enforces linear dynamics without auxiliary loss balancing and improves stability during training and prediction.

Advantages (theoretical and practical)

RRAEDy presents several advantages compared to other models used for learning dynamical systems from time series. We summarize these advantages below.

Note that the bottleneck , which represents our latent dynamical system, can be written as , with a diagonal matrix with sorted values, and an orthonormal matrix. Accordingly, the significance of the latent variables is known a priori. The presented algorithm eliminates the need to choose a priori, which is challenging when the system’s intrinsic dimensionality is unknown.

Regularization: robustness to local behavior

The latent dynamics in RRAEDy are regularized by construction due to the SVD structure of the bottleneck with , and being an orthonormal matrix (i.e., the sum of the squares of its elements in each column is 1). This regularization promotes robustness to datasets with local behavior as demonstrated in previous papers20, 22. This is a significant advantage over other models that do not impose any structure on the latent dynamics, leading to what is known as “holes” in the latent space, illustrated by overfitting and poor performance on the test set, as shown in some samples in the next Section.

The objective function: one loss term

The model is trained by minimizing the reconstruction loss between the input X and the output , using a single loss term , where is any norm of our choice. Accordingly, RRAEDy trains an encoder, a decoder, finding the latent dimension, and enforcing linear dynamics in the latent space, all by minimizing a single loss term. This is a significant advantage over other models that require balancing multiple loss terms, which can complicate optimization and lead to suboptimal convergence. Since both the DMD and the SVD provide approximations in the least-square sense, the loss term above enforces the latent coefficients to be well approximated by linear dynamics governed by the DMD operator W.

Theoretical validation—ablation study

In this section, we perform an ablation study to evaluate the impact of different components of RRAEDy on the performance and stability of the model. The conclusions of this section are (i) the adaptive bottleneck is crucial to avoid local minima, (ii) the SVD regularization is necessary to ensure marginal stability of the learned dynamics and avoid “holes” in the latent space (i.e. overfitting), and (iii) imposing a DMD operator in the latent space is necessary since the dynamics found by the autoencoder are not necessarily linear by themselves. We obtain the conclusions above by comparing RRAEDy with three variants, each removing a specific component of the model:

Limitations

While RRAEDy demonstrates promising results in learning stable, low-dimensional linear representations of complex dynamical systems, several limitations should be acknowledged:

Scalability to long time horizons: although RRAEDy enforces marginal stability, training on longer time horizons may still lead to convergence issues or increased computational cost since the DMD forecasting can not be parallelized across time steps (check Supplementary Section 9 for more details). Future work should explore strategies to improve scalability, such as batching in time or parallelizing the DMD forecasting step.

Performance on chaotic systems: RRAEDy is designed to capture stable, low-dimensional linear dynamics. For systems exhibiting chaotic behavior, training may be more challenging (just like with other Autoencoder based approaches). While RRAEDy can learn the behavior of chaotic systems (check Supplementary Section 7 for an example on the Lorenz system), the performance may degrade as the chaotic nature of the system increases (error of on the test set of the Lorenz system). Future work should investigate modifications to RRAEDy that can better capture chaotic dynamics, such as incorporating stochasticity or using more complex latent dynamics models.

Conclusion and perspectives

In this work, we introduced RRAEDy, a principled framework that addresses key limitations of existing latent-space models for dynamical systems by jointly discovering the appropriate latent dimension and enforcing linearized dynamics through an adaptive, rank-reductive autoencoder paired with a Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) operator. RRAEDy is able to extract stable, low-dimensional linear structure from nonlinear dynamics without complex auxiliary losses or manual hyperparameter balancing. Our empirical results on canonical benchmarks, including the Van der Pol oscillator, Burgers’ equation, Navier-Stokes flows, and Rotating Gaussians, demonstrate that RRAEDy delivers accurate and robust predictions with improved model stability. RRAEDy’s theoretical guarantees on the stability of the learned dynamics, combined with its practical performance, position it as a promising tool for modeling and forecasting complex dynamical systems across scientific and engineering domains. Further, the adaptive bottleneck strategy not only enhances performance but also provides insight into the intrinsic dimensionality of the underlying dynamics, offering a data-driven approach to model selection.

Nevertheless, several important directions emerge from our findings:

Scalability to high-dimensional, real-world systems: our evaluations focus on canonical benchmarks with known underlying dynamics. The next phase involves scaling RRAEDy to high-dimensional real-world datasets, where noise, measurement sparsity, and complex boundary conditions challenge current approaches.

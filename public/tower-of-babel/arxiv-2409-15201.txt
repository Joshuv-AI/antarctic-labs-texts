Meta-Generalized-Gradient Approximation made Magnetic
Jacques K. Desmarais, Alessandro Erba, Giovanni Vignale, Stefano Pittalis

Abstract
The Jacob’s ladder of density functional theory (DFT) proposes the compelling view that by extending the form of successful approximations — being guided by exact conditions and selected (least empirical) norms — upper rungs will do better than the lower, thus allowing to balance accuracy and computational effort. Meta-generalized-gradient-approximations (MGGA) belong to the last rung of the semi-local approximations before hybridization with non-local wave function theories. The performance of MGGAs on non magnetic systems greatly improves upon that of GGAs from the lower rung. Instead, failures of MGGAs on magnetic materials are daunting and make GGAs more reliable for magnetism. This raises the question as to whether consistent improvements can be achieved for magnetism along the lines of the Jacob’s ladder. Here, we present a solution from first principles: departing from the standard approach (i.e. collinear spin-DFT), we exploit the U(1) × \times SU(2) gauge symmetry of the exact functional of spin-current-DFT to access an extended set of building blocks for approximations. Crucially, this allows us to treat broken symmetry ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states. For these states, we derive a collinear MGGA that performs well for prototypical magnets and make it available in an implementation in the Crystal electronic structure package.

Introduction.

Despite nearly one hundred years of quantum physics,
magnetism keeps challenging our understanding of electron correlations. An unsettling example is given by the recently developed strongly-constrained and appropriately normed (SCAN) meta-generalized gradient
approximation for density functional theory (DFT).
Sun et al. 2015
;
Sun et al. 2016

SCAN satisfies a large set of exact constraints, from known properties of the exact exchange-correlation (xc) functional. As a consequence of which, the SCAN gives accurate results for many materials
at a computational cost which is comparable with the long known generalized-gradient-approximation (GGA).
Therefore it can be considered a modern milestone from which it is possible to develop more accurate approximations.
Aschebrock and Kümmel 2019

But SCAN does
not
reproduce correctly the stability and properties of prototypical ferromagnets like Fe.
Fu and Singh 2018
;
Ekholm et al. 2018
;
Fu and Singh 2019
;
Mejía-Rodríguez and
Trickey 2019
;
Tran et al. 2020

Overestimated tendency toward magnetism and exaggeration of magnetic energies are obtained for transition metal systems.
Extension of the analysis to antiferromagnetic solids has shown that modifications of meta-GGAs (MGGAs) yielding an improved description of ferromagnetic metals produce a deterioration in antiferromagnetic insulators, and vice versa.
Tran et al. 2020
The status is such that MGGAs yield
no
advantage over lower rungs of the semi-local approximation.
Romero and Verstraete 2018
;
Tran et al. 2020

Here, we argue that the problem has persisted due to the fact that the most
important exact constraint
for dealing with magnetism
has not guided most of the DFT developments thus far. We resolve this issue by switching from collinear spin-DFT
von Barth and Hedin 1972
;
Gunnarsson and Lundqvist 1976
(the current standard methodology) to the fully non-collinear Vignale-Rasolt-Bencheikh spin-current DFT (SCDFT) – a complete DFT theory for dealing with magnetism within the framework of the two-component Pauli-like equation.
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003
Remarkably, this extended framework can also solve problems for
collinear
DFT.

In SCDFT, the most distinctive property
of the exact exchange-correlation (xc) energy functional is the
form invariance under U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformations.
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003
;
Pittalis et al. 2017

These transformations rotate spinors as well as the phase of each component at each point in space [see Eq. (
11
) below].
Failing to satisfy this invariance leads to functional forms that do not necessarily distinguish between physical variations of the spin density and those that arise from a mere change of reference frame.
Thus, we argue that the gauge invariance of the exact xc-energy functional must be included as a most important exact constraint in guiding the construction of approximate functionals.

The need to work within the larger scheme of SCDFT arises from the ubiquitous phenomenon of spontaneous symmetry breaking. All magnetic states of matter spontaneously break SU(2) symmetry. Working within the general SCDFT, such low symmetry solutions can be described while gaining extra building blocks for the construction of extended approximate functional forms. As shown below, this feature of the formalism allows us to derive a modified SCAN that performs well also for prototypical magnets.

Recap of the State-of-the-Art
.
DFT can in principle find the particle density and total energy of real
interacting
ground states by solving
(self-consistently) an auxiliary
non
-interacting problem

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{\rm ext}+\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{n({\bf r}^{\prime})}{|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|}+v_{{\rm xc}}\right\}\varphi_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}\varphi_{i}\;,

(1)

which is the celebrated Kohn-Sham equation. This is a Schrödinger-like equation that describes single-orbitals corresponding to the actual external potential (the second term on the l.h.s.) added to the
Hartree potential (the third term on the l.h.s.) and the xc potential,
v_{\rm xc}
.
The latter potential is such that the actual particle density is retrieved

n=\sum^{occ}_{i}|\varphi_{i}|^{2}
.
not a

Correspondingly, the energy of the ground state of the real system
E
is thus obtained by

\displaystyle E=T_{s}+\int d^{3}r~n~v_{\rm ext}+E_{\rm H}[n]+E_{\rm xc}[n]\;,

(2)

where

T_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{\rm occ}\int d^{3}r~|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}

is the Kohn-Sham kinetic energy,

E_{\rm H}[n]=\int\int d^{3}rd^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{n({\bf r})n({\bf r}^{\prime})}{2|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}|}
is the Hartree energy and

E_{\rm xc}[n]
is the xc energy functional.
For retrieving the exact total energy of the actual state,
E_{\rm xc}[n]
accounts for
the electron-electron energy beyond the classical evaluation obtained via
E_{\rm H}[n]
as well as the kinetic energies beyond
T_{s}
.
Also notice that
v_{\rm xc}=\delta E_{\rm xc}/\delta n
.
All that is needed to make the above approach practical is a (numerically amenable) density functional approximation (DFA) for
E_{\rm xc}[n]
.
Solutions to
n
and
E
can thus be found in an iterative fashion.

The first and simplest
E^{\rm DFA}_{\rm xc}[n]
is the local density approximation (LDA):
Kohn and Sham 1965

\displaystyle E_{\rm xc}^{\rm LDA}[n]=\int d^{3}r~n~\epsilon^{\rm unif}_{\rm xc}(n)\;,

(3)

where the xc energy density is approximated locally with that of the uniform gas,
\epsilon^{\rm unif}_{\rm xc}(n)
, for which analytical and accurate numerical reference data are available.
To capture the variations of more realistic states, a generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) uses
information retrieved from the gradients of the density via
s=|\nabla n|/\left[2(3\pi^{2})^{1/3}n^{4/3}\right]
:

\displaystyle E_{\rm xc}^{\rm GGA}[n]=\int d^{3}r~n~\epsilon^{\rm unif}_{\rm xc}(n)~F_{\rm xc}^{\rm GGA}(n,s)\;,

(4)

where
F_{\rm xc}^{\rm GGA}(n,s)
is the xc enhancement factor that connects the LDA energy densities to the GGA energy densities. For slowly varying density, the LDA is retrieved (i.e.
F_{\rm xc}^{\rm GGA}(n,0)=1
).
The most popular choice for the enhancement factor is the one by Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof
F_{\rm xc}^{\rm PBE}
.
Perdew et al. 1996

Meta-GGAs
Perdew et al. 1999
;
Tao et al. 2003
;
Sun et al. 2015
introduce an extra dependence beyond the
electron density by employing:

\displaystyle E_{\rm xc}^{\rm MGGA}[n]=\int d^{3}r~n~\epsilon^{\rm unif}_{\rm xc}(n)~F^{\rm MGGA}_{\rm xc}(n,s,\tau)\;,

(5)

where
\tau=1/2\sum_{i}^{\mathrm{occ}}|\nabla\varphi_{i}|^{2}
is briefly referred to as the (KS) kinetic energy density.

Kaplan and Perdew 2022

Here we focus on those MGGAs that depend on

\tau
via:

\displaystyle\alpha:=\frac{\tau-\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{8n}}{\tau_{\rm unif}}\;,

(6)

where
\tau_{\rm unif}=\frac{3}{10}(3\pi^{2})^{2/3}\,n^{5/3}

is the kinetic energy density of the noninteracting Fermi gas at density
n
. In particular, the SCAN form is defined by setting
F_{\mathrm{xc}}^{\mathrm{SCAN}}(n,s,\alpha)=h_{\mathrm{xc}}^{1}(n,s)+\left[h_{\mathrm{xc}}^{0}(n,s)-h_{\mathrm{xc}}^{1}(n,s)\right]f_{\mathrm{xc}}(\alpha)

where
f_{\mathrm{xc}}(\alpha)
interpolates between the semi-local xc enhancement factors for single-orbital densities (
h_{\mathrm{xc}}^{0}
) and for slowly-varying densities (
h_{\mathrm{xc}}^{1}
). This modeling exploits the fact that
\alpha
[see Eq. (
6
) ] tends to zero in the iso-orbital limit (i.e., when the density is dominated by a single occupied orbital), tends to one in the uniform-density limit, and becomes
\gg 1
in regions of low density between closed shells.

From DFT to Spin-DFT.
For solving ground states involving collinear spin polarization either
induced by external Zeeman couplings or spontaneously via magnetic phase transitions, the standard approach is to switch from DFT to Spin-DFT.
von Barth and Hedin 1972
;
Gunnarsson and Lundqvist 1976

The method requires to solve a spin-dependent, single-orbital problem:

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{{\rm ext},\sigma}+v_{{\rm Hxc},\sigma}\right\}\varphi_{i,\sigma}=\varepsilon_{i,\sigma}\varphi_{i,\sigma}\;.

(7)

Here,
E_{\rm H}[n]
and corresponding
v_{\rm H}
are unchanged but the spin-dependent xc-potential,
v_{{\rm xc},\sigma}=\delta E_{\rm xc}/\delta n_{\sigma}
, is generated from the functional
E_{\rm xc}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]
, which depends both on
n_{\uparrow}
and
n_{\uparrow}
and not just on their sum
n=\sum_{\sigma}n_{\sigma}
.

Extended forms for exchange can be readily generated directly from the DFT approximations using the

\displaystyle E_{\rm x}^{\rm DFA}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]=\frac{1}{2}E_{\rm x}^{\rm DFA}[2n_{\uparrow}]+\frac{1}{2}E_{\rm x}^{\rm DFA}[2n_{\downarrow}]\;.

(8)

For correlation we must use:

\displaystyle E_{{\rm c}}^{\rm DFA}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]=\int d^{3}r~n~\varepsilon^{\rm unif}_{\rm c}(n,\zeta)~F^{\rm DFA}_{\rm c}(n,\zeta,s,\alpha)\;;

(9)

more in detail, unlike exchange, the correlation functional depends in a non separable fashion on
n_{\uparrow}
and
n_{\downarrow}
through the spin polarization (density)
\zeta=(n_{\uparrow}-n_{\downarrow})/(n_{\uparrow}+n_{\downarrow})
. Also note that the exchange functional of Eq. (
8
),
E_{\rm x}^{\rm DFA}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]
, can be equivalently reexpressed as
E_{\rm x}^{\rm DFA}[n,\zeta]
in terms of the total density
n
and polarization
\zeta
.

Issues.
Notice that the crucial quantity
\alpha
does
not
carry any explicit information on
\nabla\zeta
. Therefore, the above expressions for GGAs and MGGAs come with a striking asymmetry. While the gradients of
\zeta
are included in the exchange functional indirectly via the spin-scaling relation [i.e., Eq. (
8
)], the correlation form has
no
dependence on
\nabla\zeta
.

Considering how to include the physical dependence of the functional on
\nabla\zeta
made us realize that a more fundamental asymmetry is embedded in this approach: neither the GGAs nor the MGGAs are invariant under U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformations. Although this deficiency might not be perceived as a problem as long as we consider scenarios that are not gauge symmetric — such as ferromagnets or antiferromagnets — one should not confuse the symmetry properties of
particular
solutions with
those of the exact functional. To sort the latter out, we must enlarge our modeling framework.

From Collinear Spin-DFT to SCDFT.
Therefore, let us
account not only for a scalar multiplicative potential
v_{\rm ext}
and a multiplicative magnetic field

{B}^{a}
, but also for a
(particle-) vector potential
{\bf A}
and a spin-vector potential
{\bf A}^{a}
.
not b

While
{\bf A}
is useful to represent the vector potential of an external magnetic field,

{\bf A}^{a}
is useful to represent the (one-body) spin-orbit couplings in the system.
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003

The KS equations in SCDFT
have the form of single-particle Pauli equations:
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003

\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(-i\nabla+\frac{1}{c}{\mathbfcal{A}}_{\rm{\tiny\tiny KS}}\right)^{2}+{\cal V}_{\rm{\tiny\tiny KS}}\right]\Phi_{k}=\varepsilon_{k}\Phi_{k}\;,

(10)

where
\Phi_{k}
are two-component spinors,

{\mathbfcal{A}}_{\rm{\tiny\tiny KS}}=\left({\bf A}+{\bf A}_{{\rm xc}}\right)+\sigma^{a}\left({\bf A}^{a}+{\bf A}^{a}_{{\rm xc}}\right)
,

{\cal V}_{\rm{\tiny\tiny KS}}=\left(v+v_{\rm H}+v_{\rm xc}\right)+\sigma^{a}\left(B^{a}+B^{a}_{\rm xc}\right)+\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\left[\left({\bf A}+\sigma^{a}{\bf A}^{a}\right)^{2}-{\mathbfcal A}^{2}_{\rm{\tiny\tiny KS}}\right]
,
in which
\sigma^{a}
denotes the component of the vector of Pauli matrices,

{\frac{1}{c}}{\bf A}_{\rm xc}=\delta E_{\rm xc}/\delta{\bf j}

is an Abelian xc-vector potential,

{\frac{1}{c}}{\bf A}^{a}_{\rm xc}=\delta E_{\rm xc}/\delta{\bf J}^{a}

is the
a
-th component of a non-Abelian xc-vector potential,

{B}^{a}_{\rm xc}=\delta E_{\rm xc}/\delta{m^{a}}

is the
a
-th component of a xc-magnetic-like potential,

v_{\rm xc}=\delta E_{\rm xc}/\delta n

is a xc-scalar potential, and
v_{\rm H}
is the usual Hartree potential.
The KS densities are
obtained from the (occupied) two-component KS spinors as follows:

n=\sum_{k}^{\rm occ}\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\Phi_{k}
,

{\vec{m}}=\sum_{k}^{\rm occ}\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\;{\vec{\sigma}}\;\Phi_{k}
,
{\bf j}=\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{k}^{\rm occ}\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\left[\nabla\Phi_{k}\right]-\left[\nabla\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\right]\Phi_{k}
,
and
{\vec{\bf J}}=\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{k}^{\rm occ}\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}{\vec{\sigma}}\left[\nabla\Phi_{k}\right]-\left[\nabla\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\right]{\vec{\sigma}}\Phi_{k}
.

Because
E_{\rm xc}[n,{\vec{m}},{\bf j},{\vec{\bf J}}]
must yield the
exact
interacting densities via the KS densities, it must also be form invariant under (local) U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformations.
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003
;
Pittalis et al. 2017
In detail, such a transformation acts on spinors as follows:

{\Phi}^{\prime}({\bf r})=\exp\left[\frac{i}{c}\chi({\bf r}){\mathrm{I}}+\frac{i}{c}{\lambda^{a}}({\bf r}){\sigma^{a}}\right]{\Phi}({\bf r})\;,

(11)

where I is the
2\times 2
identity and
\chi
and
\lambda^{a}
are arbitrary real-valued functions.

Revisiting the SCAN Form.
We recall from Eq. (
5
) that MGGAs, such as SCAN, explicitly depend on the KS kinetic energy density
\tau
. For dealing with non-collinear (nc) solutions, the kinetic energy density
\tau
must be expressed in terms of the occupied two-component KS spinors

\tau_{\rm nc}=1/2\sum_{k}^{\mathrm{occ}}\Big(\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\Big)\Big(\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{k}\Big)
.
But, it can be directly verified that
\tau_{\rm nc}
is not gauge invariant.
Pittalis et al. 2017
Thus,
\alpha_{\rm nc}
obtained
by replacing
\tau
with
\tau_{\rm nc}
in Eq. (
6
) is not invariant either.
The corresponding extension of the SCAN functional by letting
\alpha\to\alpha_{\rm nc}
would therefore lack the most fundamental property for dealing with magnetism. Fortunately, this problem can be fixed by the replacement
{\tau}_{\rm nc}\rightarrow\widetilde{\tau}_{\rm nc}
with

\widetilde{\tau}_{\rm nc}=\tau_{\rm nc}+\frac{{m}^{a}{\tau}_{\rm nc}^{a}}{n}+\frac{\nabla{m}^{a}\cdot\nabla{m}^{a}}{8n}-\frac{{\bf j}\cdot{\bf j}}{2n}-\frac{{\bf J}^{a}\cdot{\bf J}^{a}}{2n}\;,

(12)

where

{\tau}^{a}_{\rm nc}=1/2\sum_{k}^{\rm occ}\Big(\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{\dagger}_{k}\Big){\sigma}^{a}\Big(\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{k}\Big)\;

is the spin-kinetic energy density.
Eq. (
12
)
was derived — among a few possible others — in previous work on the structure of the exact-exchange hole (see Ref.
Pittalis et al. 2017
), which incidentally also motivated the inclusion of the nc (spin-)kinetic energy density as a variable on which nc semi-local approximations must depend.
Eq. (
12
) not only includes
\nabla\vec{m}
and the (spin-)paramagnetic currents but, for consistency with the exact gauge-invariance requirements, also the coupling between
{\vec{m}}
and
{\vec{\tau}}_{\rm nc}
.

Notice that Eq. (
12
) includes the well-known particle current corrections that ensure the U(1) invariance of the MGGA.
Tao and Perdew 2005

It also includes the spin current corrections that were recently shown to ensure the SU(2) invariance of the MGGA for time-reversal symmetric spin-orbit coupling.
Desmarais
et al. 2024a

Eq. (
12
), however, is more general
than the previous expressions as it also ensures invariance under
conjoint
U(1)
\times
SU(2) transformations.
Desmarais
et al. 2024b

Restricting ourselves to states with vanishing currents, the collinear (coll) limit of Eq. (
12

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}_{\rm nc}\rightarrow\widetilde{\tau}_{\rm coll}=\tau+\frac{{m}^{z}{\tau}^{z}}{n}+\frac{\nabla{m}^{z}\cdot\nabla{m}^{z}}{8n}\;.

(13)

In what follows we will make use of
n=n^{\uparrow}+n^{\downarrow}
,
m^{z}=n^{\uparrow}-n^{\downarrow}
,
\tau=\tau^{\uparrow}+\tau^{\downarrow}
and
\tau^{z}=\tau^{\uparrow}-\tau^{\downarrow}
.
Therefore, the following
magnetic
modification of the SCAN functional (namely, mSCAN) suggests itself:

E^{\rm mSCAN}_{x}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]=\int d^{3}r~n~\epsilon^{\rm unif}_{x}\left(n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}\right)~F^{\rm SCAN}_{x}\left(n,s,\widetilde{\alpha}\right)

(14a)

and

E^{\rm mSCAN}_{c}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]=\int d^{3}r~n~\epsilon^{\rm unif}_{c}\left(n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}\right)~F^{\rm SCAN}_{c}\left(n,\zeta,s,\widetilde{\alpha}\right)

(14b)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}:=\frac{n^{\uparrow}\tau^{\uparrow}+n^{\downarrow}\tau^{\downarrow}-\frac{\left(\nabla n^{\uparrow}\right)\cdot\left(\nabla n^{\downarrow}\right)}{4}}{\frac{n^{\uparrow}}{2}\tau^{\uparrow}_{\rm unif}+\frac{n^{\downarrow}}{2}\tau^{\downarrow}_{\rm unif}}\;,

(15)

with

\tau^{\uparrow}_{\rm unif}=3/5(6\pi^{2})^{2/3}(n^{\uparrow})^{5/3}

15

\widetilde{\alpha}=\alpha
.
Thus, in Eq. (
14a

14b

\widetilde{\alpha}
rather than on
\alpha

Conclusions.

In this work, the soundness of the third rung of the Jacob’s ladder of density functional theory (DFT) has been established for magnetic systems by a magnetic modification of the
strongly constrained and appropriately normed semilocal density functional – mSCAN.
mSCAN puts aside the constraint of zero correlation energy for any one-electron spin-polarized density
(i.e. in mSCAN,
\widetilde{\alpha}
does not vanish for single-particle orbitals). Moreover, the exchange component is not obtained from the spin-scaling relations [compare Eq. (
14a
) with Eq. (
8
)]. Crucially, mSCAN derives from a more general framework — namely, spin-current-DFT (instead of collinear spin DFT) —
which dictates invariance of the xc energy functional under (local) conjoint U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformations as a most important exact
constraint.
Even though the mSCAN is subsequently specialized to the collinear case, the use of a more general framework has exposed an important dependence of the functional on the gradient of the collinear spin density – a dependence that would have been missed in the conventional approach.

While the question of including
all
the relevant exact constraints will have to be explored further — leading to possible further improvements — the present work shows that mSCAN resolves the most daunting issues that have plagued the application of the original SCAN to prototypical magnetic materials.

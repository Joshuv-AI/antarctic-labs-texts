Connected components of Berkovich fixed point locus
Niladri Patra

Abstract
. Let ℙ a ​ n 1 {\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an} be the Berkovich projective line over a complete, algebraically closed, non-Archimedean field of characteristic 0 0 . Let ϕ \phi be a degree ≥ 2 \geq 2 rational map with potential good reduction, acting on ℙ a ​ n 1 {\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an} . In this article, we give an easily verifiable equivalent criterion for the fixed point locus of ϕ \phi to be connected. We also give an equivalent criterion for the fixed point locus of ϕ \phi to be finite. Moreover, we provide a sharp upper bound for the number of connected components of the fixed point locus of a rational map with potential good reduction.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbbm{L}}
be a complete, algebraically closed non-Archimedean field. Throughout this article, we will assume the characteristic of
{\mathbbm{L}}
, char
({\mathbbm{L}})=0
. Non-Archimedean dynamics on
{\mathbbm{L}}
studies behaviour of self-iterates of rational maps, defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
, acting on the projective line over
{\mathbbm{L}}
. It is nowadays standard to extend this classical version of non-Archimedean dynamics to the dynamics on the Berkovich projective line, denoted
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
(As the field
{\mathbbm{L}}
underneath will remain constant throughout this article, we have suppressed it from the notation of the Berkovich projective line). For detailed discussion on Berkovich projective line and the action of rational maps on it, see
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
. In summary, Berkovich projective line over
{\mathbbm{L}}
is a path connected, compact, Hausdorff space under the weak topology in which the classical projective line
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
embeds topologically as a dense subset (see
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
). The action of any rational map on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
extends continuously and uniquely to an action on the Berkovich projective line. As one studies action of self-iterates of rational maps on the Berkovich projective line, the simplest type of points one encounters are fixed points. To elaborate, a
fixed point
of a rational map
\phi
defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
is a point
\xi\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
, such that
\phi(\xi)=\xi
. Any rational map
\phi
defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
must have at least one fixed point in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
. Over a field, a rational map has only finitely many fixed points. However, on the Berkovich projective line the set of fixed points of a rational map can be infinite. Thus we define
the fixed point locus
of a rational map
\phi
to be the set of fixed points of
\phi
in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
. The set of fixed points of a continuous self-map on a Hausdorff space is closed. Thus, for any rational map
\phi
, the fixed point locus of
\phi
in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
is non-empty, closed and compact under the weak topology.

Consider the complete, algebraically closed non-Archimedean field
{\mathbbm{L}}
. Every such field comes equipped with a non-Archimedean absolute value
|\cdot|
. The set of all elements of
{\mathbbm{L}}
with absolute value less than or equal to
1
forms a local ring, denoted
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
. The unique maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
comprises of all elements of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
with absolute value less than
1
, denoted
m_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
. The field
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{L}}}/m_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
is called the residue field of
{\mathbbm{L}}
, denoted
\tilde{{\mathbbm{L}}}
. Consider a rational map
\phi
defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
. By multiplying the numerator and denominator of
\phi
by a suitable rational function, we can assume that the numerator and denominator of
\phi
has no non-constant common factor and all the coefficients of
\phi
lie in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
. We will call this
a normalized form
of the rational map
\phi
. Reducing the coefficients of
\phi
modulo the maximal ideal
m_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
, one obtains a rational map
\tilde{\phi}
defined over
\tilde{{\mathbbm{L}}}
(note that we allow the map
\tilde{\phi}
to be a constant, even the constant map
\infty
).This map
\tilde{\phi}
is called the
reduction
of
\phi
or
the residual map
of
\phi
. A normalized map
\phi
is said to have a
good reduction
, if degree of
\tilde{\phi}
is the same as the degree of
\phi
. The map
\phi
is said to be of
potential good reduction
(in some of the literatures it is also called relative good reduction) if there exists a Möbius transformation
\sigma\in PGL_{2}({\mathbbm{L}})
such that the normalized version of the conjugation
\sigma\circ\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}
has good reduction.

Let
\phi(z)
be a rational map defined over an algebraically closed field
K
. Let
\alpha
be a point in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}(K)
, the classical projective line over
K
. One can always find a Möbius transformation
\sigma\in PGL_{2}(K)
such that both
\sigma(\alpha)
and
\sigma(\phi(\alpha))
lie in
K
. The point
\alpha
is called a
critical point
of
\phi
if
\sigma(\alpha)
is a root of the formal derivative
(\sigma\circ\phi\circ\sigma^{-1})^{\prime}(z)
in
K
.

Properties of fixed points of a rational map on the Berkovich projective line have been studied by Rivera-Letelier (
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
) and Benedetto (
[
3
]
,
[
12
]
) among others. In this article, we discuss connectedness properties of the fixed point locus of a rational map with potential good reduction. Apart from connectedness properties, the results in this article serve two purposes. Firstly, they provide more insight about the dynamics of rational maps acting on the Berkovich projective line. Secondly, they highlight an interplay between the ramification locus and the fixed point locus of a rational map. We now state the main results of this article. Firstly, we give an easily checkable equivalent criterion for a rational map with good reduction to have connected fixed point locus.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map of degree
\geq 2
, defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
, having good reduction. The fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected if and only if the reduction of
\phi
has no fixed critical point.

This theorem is then extended to discuss all rational maps having potentially good reduction.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\phi
be a degree
\geq 2
rational map defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
with potential good reduction. Let
\xi
be the unique type
2
totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
. Then the following are equivalent,

(1)
At
\xi
, the map induced by
\phi
at the level of tangent space has no fixed critical direction.

(2)
The fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected.

Following this we work toward providing a
sharp
upper bound for the number of connected components of the Berkovich fixed point locus of a rational map with potential good reduction.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map of degree
d\geq 2
with potential good reduction. Then, the number of connected components of fixed point locus of
\phi
is bounded above by
(d+2)
.

We also prove an easily verifiable equivalent criterion for the Berkovich fixed point locus of a rational map having potential good reduction to be
finite
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\phi
be a degree
d\geq 2
rational map with potential good reduction. Let
\xi
be the unique type
2
totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
. Every residually fixed direction of
\phi
at
\xi
is residually critical if and only if the fixed point locus of
\phi
is a finite set. In this case, the fixed point locus of
\phi
consists of the type
1
fixed points of
\phi
and
\xi
.

Finally, we provide an example of Theorem
1.4
, which also serves to show that the bound mentioned in Theorem
1.3
is
sharp
.

In the next section, we introduce briefly the necessary definitions, notations and properties needed for this article. We also provide a collection of results with references, that will be required in the later section. In third section, we prove the main results of this article.

2.
Prerequisites

Our notations will roughly follow the notations of the book
[
1
]
by Baker and Rumely. For the construction and basic properties of Berkovich projective line
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
, see
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
. The points of
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
are classified as follows,

(1)
Type
1
points (also called,
rigid
or
classical
points), which correspond to (evaluation semi-norm at) points of
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
.

(2)
Type
2
points which correspond to (sup norm on) closed disks in
{\mathbbm{L}}
with radius lying in the value group,
|{\mathbbm{L}}^{\times}|
.

(3)
Type
3
points which correspond to (sup norm on) closed disks in
{\mathbbm{L}}
with radius not lying in the value group.

(4)
Type
4
points which correspond to (limit of sup norms on) decreasing sequences of closed disks whose intersection is empty.

The type
2
point corresponding to the closed disk
D(0,1)
of radius
1
around
0
is called the
Gauss point
, denoted
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Any other type
2
or
3
point that corresponds to a closed disk of radius
r
around
a\in{\mathbbm{L}}
, is denoted as
\zeta_{a,r}
. The point
\zeta_{a,r}
is said to have
diameter
(Compare
[
1
, Page 11]
)
r
, as it is the supremum of distances between any pair of points on
D(a,r)
. The
diameter
of a type
4
point is defined to be the limit of the diameters of a decreasing sequence of disks defining that type
4
point. The Berkovich projective line can be seen as an infinite tree, rooted at
\zeta_{Gauss}
(See Chapter
2
,
[
1
]
). This tree branches at every type
2
point in infinitely many
directions
, which are in one-one correspondence with
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}(\tilde{{\mathbbm{L}}})
. At any type
3
point, it branches in only two directions. Type
1
and
4
points serve as endpoints of this tree (See picture at
[
1
, page 30]
).

2.1.
Topology on the Berkovich projective line

Consider the connected (connected as a tree) components of the Berkovich projective line missing a point. The
weak topology
(or,
Berkovich topology
) (See
[
1
, Page 28]
) is given on the Berkovich projective line by considering the set of all such connected components as a subbasis. Under the weak topology, the Berkovich projective line is a uniquely path-connected (i.e. there are no non-trivial loops in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
that has no retracing), compact, Hausdorff space (See
[
1
, Proposition 2.6 and Lemma 2.10]
). This topology restricts to the usual non-Archimedean topology on the type
1
points. Moreover, under this topology the type
1
points form a dense subset (See
[
1
, Lemma 2.9]
). Let
\zeta
be a point on the Berkovich projective line. Consider the connected components of
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}\backslash\zeta
. These are called
open Berkovich disks
(Compare
[
1
, Definition 2.25]
) at
\zeta
. These are also considered as
directions
at
\zeta
(Compare
[
1
, Page 41]
). Collection of all these directions at
\zeta
forms the
tangent space
(Compare
[
1
, Page 402]
) at
\zeta
, denoted
T_{\zeta}{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
. We denote a direction at a point on the Berkovich projective line using a vector, such as
\vec{v}
. If
\vec{v}
is a direction at the point
\zeta\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
, then the open Berkovich disk corresponding to it is denoted as
B_{\vec{v}}
. Let
\xi
be another point on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
distinct from
\zeta
. Then the connected component (open Berkovich disk) of
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}\backslash\zeta
containing
\xi
will be denoted as
B_{\xi}(\zeta)
.

2.2.
Arcs and the big metric

Let
\zeta
and
\xi
be two points on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
. The notation
[\zeta,\xi]
(Compare
[
1
, Page 9]
) stands for the unique closed path in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
starting at
\zeta
, ending at
\xi
, with no retracing. This is also called the
arc
between
\zeta
and
\xi
. The notations
[\zeta,\xi),(\zeta,\xi],(\zeta,\xi)
correspond to the paths between
\zeta
and
\xi
with one or both endpoints removed, as designated by the round bracket.

The space
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}\backslash{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
is called the
Berkovich hyperbolic space
(See
[
1
, definition 2.20]
). On this space, one defines a metric called
the big metric
(See
[
1
, Page 44 , Equation (2.23)]
). Let
x
and
y
be two points in the Berkovich hyperbolic space. Denote the unique point lying on the intersection of the three arcs,
[x,\infty]
,
[y,\infty]
and
[x,y]
as
x\vee_{\infty}y
. Then
the big metric

\rho
is defined to be,

\rho(x,y)=2log_{v}(diam(x\vee_{\infty}y))-log_{v}(diam(x))-log_{v}(diam(y)),

(2.1)

where
v
denote the absolute value of the element of
{\mathbbm{L}}
which normalizes
|\cdot|
(one can choose this element to be a uniformizer of a discrete valuation ring topologically and algebraically embedded in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbm{L}}}
). Under the big metric
\rho
, the type
1
points lie at infinite distance away from any other type points. The topology induced by the big metric on the Berkovich hyperbolic space is called the
strong topology
(See
[
1
, Section 2.7]
). As the name suggests, this is a stronger topology than the restriction of the weak topology on the hyperbolic space. However, when one considers the Berkovich projective line as a projective limit of finite
{\mathbbm{R}}
trees, the projective limit topology generated by the restriction of strong topology on the finite subtrees of the Berkovich projective line, is the same as the Berkovich topology on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
(See
[
1
, Theorem 2.21]
).

2.3.
Action of rational maps and multiplicity

Action of any rational map, defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
, on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
extends continuously and uniquely to an action on the Berkovich projective line (See
[
1
, Section 2.3]
). Under these actions, type of a point stay preserved. Let
\phi
be a rational map that sends
\zeta\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
to
\phi(\zeta)
. Then,
\phi
induces a map at level of tangent spaces,
T_{\zeta}\phi:T_{\zeta}{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}\rightarrow T_{\phi(\zeta)}{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
.

There is a notion of multiplicity (See
[
1
, Definition 9.7]
,
[
5
, Section 3.1]
) of a rational map
\phi
at a point on the Berkovich projective line, which extends the usual notion of multiplicity of a function on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
. Let
\phi
be a rational map of degree
d
. The
multiplicity
of
\phi
at a point
\zeta
is denoted
m_{\phi}(\zeta)
. For every direction
\vec{v}
, there is also a notion of
directional multiplicity
(See
[
1
, Page 265]
,
[
5
, Section 3.2]
) of
\phi
at
\zeta
in the direction
\vec{v}
, denoted
m_{\phi}(\zeta,\vec{v})
. For every direction
\vec{w}
at
\phi(\zeta)
, the following equality holds (See
[
1
, Theorem 9.22 , Part C]
),

\sum_{\vec{v}\in(T_{\zeta}\phi)^{-1}(\vec{w})}m_{\phi}(\zeta,\vec{v})=m_{\phi}(\zeta).

(2.2)

Moreover, as one would expect for a degree
d
rational map, the following equality holds (See
[
1
, Theorem 9.8 , Part C]
),

\sum_{\xi\in\phi^{-1}(\zeta)}m_{\phi}(\xi)=d.

(2.3)

Fix a degree
\geq 2
rational map
\phi
defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
. Any point
\zeta\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
at which the multiplicity of
\phi
is greater than
1
is called a
ramified point
. We reserve the notion of
critical point
for type
1
ramified points. The set of ramified points of
\phi
in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
is called
the ramification locus
of
\phi
(See
[
5
, Definition 3.2]
). As the multiplicity function is upper semicontinuous under the weak topology (See
[
1
, Proposition 9.28]
), the ramification locus is a closed set. A point in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
is called
totally ramified
if it has multiplicity equal to the degree of the rational map in a normalized form. Similarly, one defines the
locus of total ramification
(See
[
5
, Definition 8.1]
).

2.4.
Classification of fixed points

Following Rivera-Letelier, we introduce the following classification of fixed points of a rational map
\phi
, which are
not
of type
1
(rigid). A non-rigid fixed point of
\phi
is called
repelling
if the multiplicity of
\phi
at that point is
2
or higher. Otherwise, it is called an
indifferent
fixed point.

On classical/rigid/type
1
points, we have the usual classification of fixed points. The
multiplier

\lambda
of
\phi
at a fixed point
a\in{\mathbbm{L}}
is defined to be
\lambda=\phi^{\prime}(a)
, where
\phi^{\prime}
denote the derivative of
\phi
. The notion of multiplier is extended naturally to
\infty
as
\infty
can be moved to any other rigid point via a Möbius transformation. The rigid fixed points are classified as,

•

If
|\lambda|=0
, it is called
superattracting
fixed point,

•

If
0<|\lambda|<1
, it is called
attracting
fixed point,

•

If
|\lambda|=1
, it is called
indifferent
fixed point,

•

If
|\lambda|>1
, it is called
repelling
fixed point.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\phi

\geq 2

(1)
The map
\phi

(2)

(3)
The Gauss point
\zeta_{Gauss}

Proof.

(1)\iff(2)
This is due to Rivera-Letelier (
[
11

(2)\implies(3)
The Gauss point is totally ramified. Hence, image of the open Berkovich disk

at any direction

at
\zeta_{Gauss}
does not contain
\zeta_{Gauss}

, every direction at

is a good direction.

(3)\iff(4)
This result is due to Baker and Rumely (
[
1

(4)\implies(2)
We prove it by contradiction. Suppose the Gauss point

\zeta_{Gauss}

. As

is a tree, there is a type

where the image of
\phi

. Let
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
be the Berkovich projective line over
{\mathbbm{L}}
. Let
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
be the set of rigid/classical/type
1
points in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
. Recall that, a degree
\geq 2
rational map
\phi
has good reduction if and only if the Gauss point
\zeta_{Gauss}
is a totally ramified fixed point.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be a degree
\geq 2
rational map, defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
, having good reduction. Let
\vec{v}
be a direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
with directional multiplicity greater than
1
under
\phi
, and fixed under the reduction of
\phi
. Let
B_{\vec{v}}
be the open Berkovich disk with boundary point
\zeta_{Gauss}
, in the direction of
\vec{v}
. Then, there is a point
\beta\in B_{\vec{v}}
such that
\phi(\beta)\neq\beta
and
\beta
lies on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\beta)]
.

Proof.

As
\vec{v}
is a fixed direction under
\tilde{\phi}
(the reduction of
\phi
), by Theorems
2.1
and
2.4
we get
\phi(B_{\vec{v}})=B_{\vec{v}}
. Moreover, by Theorem
2.3
, we can find a point
\gamma
sufficiently close to
\zeta_{Gauss}
such that
\rho(\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\delta))=m_{\phi}(\zeta_{Gauss},\vec{v})\rho(\zeta_{Gauss},\delta)
, for every
\delta\in[\zeta_{Gauss},\gamma]
. If
\gamma\in[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\gamma)]
, then the point point
\gamma
is our required point
\beta
in the lemma. Suppose that
\gamma
does not lie in
[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\gamma)]
. Consider the unique point
\beta
lying in the intersection of the the three arcs
[\zeta_{Gauss},\gamma],[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\gamma)]
and
[\gamma,\phi(\gamma)]
. We claim that
\beta
satisfies the conditions of the lemma. As
\beta\in[\zeta_{Gauss},\gamma]
, we know that
\phi(\beta)\neq\beta
. As
\phi
has good reduction and the open Berkovich disk
B_{\gamma}(\beta)
containing
\gamma
with boundary point
\beta
does not contain
\zeta_{Gauss}
, the image
\phi(B_{\gamma}(\beta))
is a open Berkovich disk containing
\phi(\gamma)
with boundary point
\phi(\beta).
Let us assume that
\phi(B_{\gamma}(\beta))
contains
\beta
. As
\phi(B_{\gamma}(\beta))
does not contain
\zeta_{Gauss}
, the point
\phi(\beta)
(which is the boundary point of
\phi(B_{\gamma}(\beta))
) must lie on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\beta]
. But, this contradicts Theorem
2.3
. So,
\phi(B_{\gamma}(\beta))
does not contain
\beta
. As
\phi(B_{\gamma}(\beta))
contains
\phi(\gamma)
, hence also the arc
(\phi(\beta),\phi(\gamma)]
, the point
\beta
can not lie in
[\phi(\beta),\phi(\gamma)]
. As
\beta
lies in
[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\gamma)]
, the point
\beta
must lie in
[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\beta))
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be a degree
\geq 2
rational map defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
, having good reduction. The fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected if and only if the reduction of
\phi
has no fixed critical point.

Proof.

Let us assume that the fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected. We want to show that the reduction of
\phi
has no fixed critical point. We will prove by contradiction. Suppose the reduction of
\phi
has a fixed critical point corresponding to the direction
\vec{v}
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Let
B_{\vec{v}}
be the open Berkovich disk at
\zeta_{Gauss}
in the direction of
\vec{v}
. As the direction
\vec{v}
is fixed under the reduction of
\phi
and
\phi
has good reduction, by Theorems
2.1
and
2.4
we get
\phi(B_{\vec{v}})=B_{\vec{v}}
. Moreover, the direction
\vec{v}
is a critical point for the reduction of
\phi
. According to Theorem
2.2
, part
(3)
, this is equivalent to the fact that the directional multiplicity of
\phi
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
in the direction of
\vec{v}
is
2
or higher. Hence, by Lemma
3.1
we can choose a type
2
point
\beta\in B_{\vec{v}}
sufficiently close to
\zeta_{Gauss}
such that
\phi(\beta)\neq\beta
and
\beta
lies on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\beta)]
. Let
\vec{w}
be the direction at
\beta
containing
\phi(\beta)
, and
B_{\vec{w}}
is the corresponding open Berkovich disk with boundary point
\beta
. We claim that
\phi(B_{\vec{w}})\subset B_{\vec{w}}
. To prove the claim, first observe that as
\phi
has good reduction and
\zeta_{Gauss}\notin B_{\vec{w}}
,
\phi(B_{\vec{w}})
can not contain
\zeta_{Gauss}
. As
\beta
and
\zeta_{Gauss}
lie in the same direction at
\phi(\beta)
,
\phi(B_{\vec{w}})
is an open Berkovich disk at
\phi(\beta)
which does not contain
\beta
or
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Hence,
\phi(B_{\vec{w}})\subset B_{\vec{w}}
. Consider the non-empty closed connected set
X=\bar{B}_{\vec{w}}
, the weak closure of
B_{\vec{w}}
. As
\phi(X)\subset X
, by Theorem
2.7
there exists a fixed point of
\phi
in
\bar{B}_{\vec{w}}
. Let us denote this point as
\eta
. The arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\eta]
is contained in
B_{\vec{v}}
. As directional multiplicity of
\phi
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
in the direction of
\vec{v}
is
2
or higher, the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\eta]
contains points, such as
\beta
, that are not fixed under
\phi
. Hence,
\zeta_{Gauss}
and
\eta
lie in distinct connected components of the fixed point locus. We arrive at a contradiction.

For the converse, as
\phi
has good reduction, the Gauss point
\zeta_{Gauss}
is a totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
. Let
\xi
be another fixed point of
\phi
. To prove connectedness of the fixed point locus, it is enough to show that every point on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
is fixed under
\phi
. Let
B_{\xi}
be the open Berkovich disk containing
\xi
with boundary point
\zeta_{Gauss}
. As
\xi
is fixed, the set
\phi(B_{\xi})
contains
B_{\xi}
. As the Gauss point is totally ramified and fixed under
\phi
,
\phi(B_{\xi})=B_{\xi}
. So, the direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
corresponding to
B_{\xi}
is fixed under the reduction of
\phi
. By assumption of the theorem, this direction is not a critical point for the reduction of
\phi
. So the directional multiplicity of
\phi
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
in the direction of
B_{\xi}
is
1
. From Theorem
2.4
, along every direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
the multiplicity function of points is non-increasing. Hence, the multiplicity of every point in
B_{\xi}
is
1
. As
\zeta_{Gauss}
and
\xi
are both fixed points and every point on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
has multiplicity
1
, we claim that the entire arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi)
consists of fixed points of
\phi
. Let
\eta
be a point in
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi)
. The length of the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\eta]
under the big metric is finite. As every point on this arc has multiplicity
1
, we can cover the arc with finitely many subarcs such that Theorem
2.3
holds on each of there arc. Thus,
\rho(\zeta_{Gauss},\phi(\eta))
must be equal to
\rho(\zeta_{Gauss},\eta)
. As
\phi
maps the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
to itself with no retracing,
\eta
must be a fixed point of
\phi
. Hence,
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
lies in the fixed point locus of
\phi
.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Suppose that
{\mathbbm{L}}
has residue characteristic
\neq 2
. Consider the rational function,

\phi(z)=\frac{z^{2}+1}{z+1}.

(3.1)

The map
\phi
has good reduction and it has the same expression as its residual map. Its critical points are the roots of the polynomial (the Wronskian),
z^{2}+2z-1
. The residual map has fixed points at
\infty
and
1
. As residue characteristic is
\neq 2
, no critical point of the residual map of
\phi
is fixed. By Theorem
3.2
, its fixed point locus is connected.

The following lemma is due to Rivera-Letelier
[
11
, Theorem 4]
,
[
1
, Theorem 10.105]
. Nonetheless, we provide a proof of it here.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map of degree
\geq 2
defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
. The map
\phi
can have at most one type
2
totally ramified fixed point. Moreover,
\phi
has potentially good reduction if and only if
\phi
has a type
2
totally ramified fixed point.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\phi
has two totally ramified type
2
fixed points
\zeta
and
\xi
. Then, by Theorem
2.5
, the arc
[\zeta,\xi]
is contained in the locus of total ramification. Moreover,
[\zeta,\xi]\subseteq\phi([\zeta,\xi])
. As
\zeta
is totally ramified, every direction at
\zeta
is a good direction. Thus, there is no backtracking for
\phi
on
[\zeta,\xi]
. hence,
\phi([\zeta,\xi])=[\zeta,\xi]
. As both
\zeta
and
\xi
are type
2
points, the length of the arc
[\zeta,\xi]
is finite under
\rho
. Using Theorem
2.3
, we can cover the arc
[\zeta,\xi]
with finitely many subarcs such that
\phi
elongates the length of each of these subarcs by the degree of
\phi
. As degree of
\phi
is
\geq 2
, we reach a contradiction.

The last part of the theorem is an easy corollary of Theorem
2.4
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\phi
be a degree
\geq 2
rational map defined over
{\mathbbm{L}}
with potential good reduction. Let
\xi
be the unique type
2
totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
. Then the following are equivalent,

(1)
At
\xi
, the map induced by
\phi
at the level of tangent space has no fixed critical direction.

(2)
The fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
After a conjugation by a Möbius transformation, we can assume that the new map
\psi
has good reduction and its residual map has no fixed critical point. Hence, the fixed point locus of
\psi
is connected. The fixed point locus of
\phi
is the image of fixed point locus of
\psi
under a Möbius map. Thus it is also connected.

(2)\implies(1)
Let
\sigma
be a Möbius map that maps
\xi
to
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Then
\psi:=\sigma\circ\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}
has a totally ramified fixed point at
\zeta_{Gauss}
. As the fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected, so is the fixed point locus of
\psi
. Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, every direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
that is fixed under
\psi
has directional multiplicity
1
under
\psi
. The Möbius map
\sigma^{-1}
maps the fixed directions under
\psi
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
to fixed directions under
\phi
at
\xi
. It also preserves the directional multiplicities along these directions. Hence, no critical direction of
\phi
at
\xi
is fixed.
∎

Next, we discuss another corollary of Theorem
3.2
. We call a Möbius map, defined over an algebraically closed field
K
, a
translation map
if it has a unique fixed point in the classical projective line over
K
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map with good reduction. Moreover, assume that the residual map of
\phi
has no fixed critical point. Let Fix
(\phi)
be the fixed point locus of
\phi
. Then, any connected component
C
of Fix
(\phi)\backslash\zeta_{Gauss}
is of one of the following forms,

(1)
an arc of the form
(\zeta_{Gauss},\alpha]
, for some
\alpha\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}\backslash\zeta_{Gauss}
, where
\alpha
is either of the following,

•

type
1
or type
4
indifferent fixed point.

•

type
2
indifferent fixed point where the map at the level of tangent space at
\alpha
is a translation map.

(2)
there is a type
2
point
\xi
in
C
at which the map induced by
\phi
at the level of the tangent space at
\xi
is the identity map. Hence,
C
has nonempty intersection with every direction at
\xi
.

Proof.

If
C
is an arc,
\alpha
can not be a type
3
point, thanks to Theorem
2.6
. By Theorem
2.6
, if
\alpha
is of type
4
, then it must be an indifferent fixed point.

If
\alpha
is of type
1
, and not indifferent, then in the classical projective line
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}({\mathbbm{L}})
points nearby
\alpha
gets attracted or repelled. Thus, in
{\mathbbm{P}}^{1}_{an}
,
\alpha
would be an isolated fixed point. Hence, if
\alpha
is of type
1
, it must be indifferent.

If
\alpha
is of type
2
and repelling, then
\alpha
lies in the ramification locus of
\phi
. So does the Gauss point. But the arc
(\zeta_{Gauss},\alpha)
lies in the fixed point locus, hence it must not be contained in the ramification locus of
\phi
, by Theorem
2.3
. Thus, the ramification locus of
\phi
is disconnected. But
\phi
is a rational map with good reduction. By Theorem
2.5
, the ramification locus of
\phi
is connected. Thus, if
\alpha
is of type
2
, it must be indifferent. As the multiplicity of
\phi
at
\alpha
is
1
, the map induced by
\phi
at the level of tangent space of
\alpha
must be a Möbius map. As
\alpha
is an endpoint of the fixed point locus, this Möbius map must have only one fixed point. So, it is a translation map.

Let us now assume that
C
is not an arc. Then there exists a point
\xi
in
C
such that
C
has non-empty intersection with more than
2
directions at
\xi
. But, from the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we know that the multiplicity of
\phi
at
\xi
is
1
. Hence, by Theorem
2.2
, the map at the level of tangent space at
\xi
is a degree
1
map. As
\phi
has more than 2 fixed directions at
\xi
, the map at the level of tangent space at
\xi
must be identity map.
∎

Example 3.7
.

We give an example of a rational map where the second case of Corollary
3.6
occurs. Let
{\mathbbm{L}}={\mathbbm{C}}_{p}
, where
p>2
. Consider the map,

\phi(z)=\frac{z^{2}+z+p^{2}}{z^{2}+1}.

(3.2)

The reduction map
\bar{\phi}=\frac{z^{2}+z}{z^{2}+\bar{1}}
has fixed points at
\bar{0},\bar{1}
, none of which are critical points of
\bar{\phi}
. Hence, the fixed point locus of
\phi
is connected. Consider the Möbius map,

\sigma(z)=pz.

(3.3)

The conjugated map becomes,

\psi(z):=\sigma^{-1}\circ\phi\circ\sigma(z)=\frac{pz^{2}+z+p}{p^{2}z^{2}+1}.

(3.4)

The reduction of
\psi
is the identity map. Hence, at
\zeta_{0,|p|}=\sigma(\zeta_{Gauss})
,
\phi
has a fixed point for which the map induced by
\phi
at the level of tangent space is the identity map.

Note that in all of the discussions above, the case of polynomial maps with good reduction has not been considered. This is because reduction of every polynomial map with good reduction has a fixed critical point at infinity. We discuss connectedness properties of the fixed point locus of any rational map with potential good reduction below. First, we need two lemmas.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map over
{\mathbbm{L}}
of degree
\geq 2
having good reduction. Let
\vec{v}
be a residual fixed critical direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Let
\xi
be a fixed point of
\phi
lying in the open Berkovich disk
B_{\vec{v}}
. Then,
\xi
must be of type
1
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\xi
is not of type
1
. Consider the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
. As
\phi
is a map with good reduction, by Theorem
2.4
, the ramification index is non-increasing in every direction emanating from
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Thus, there is no backtracking of
\phi
on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
. Hence,
\phi([\zeta_{Gauss},\xi])=[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
and
\phi
is one-one on
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
. As
\xi
is not of type
1
, the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
is of finite length under the big metric
\rho
(see Equation (
2.1
)). Hence, by Theorem
2.3
, the multiplicity of
\phi
at every point on
(\zeta_{Gauss},\xi)
must be
1
. Thus the direction
\vec{v}
is not a residual critical direction of
\phi
. We arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map over
{\mathbbm{L}}
, of degree
\geq 2
, having good reduction. Let
\xi
be a totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
, lying in the direction
\vec{v}
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Then,
\xi
is of type
1
and it is the unique fixed point of
\phi
in
B_{\vec{v}}
.

Proof.

As
\phi
is a rational map with good reduction, by Theorem
2.5
the locus of total ramification of
\phi
is connected. As
\xi
is totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
lying in
B_{\vec{v}}
, the direction
\vec{v}
is totally ramified and fixed under the residual map of
\phi
. Hence,
\vec{v}
is a residual fixed critical direction of
\phi
. By Lemma
3.8
,
\xi
must be of type
1
.

Suppose there exists another fixed point
\eta\in B_{\vec{v}}
. From the proof of the Lemma
3.8
,
\eta
does not lie on the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi)
. Consider the arc
[\eta,\xi]
, which lies in
B_{\vec{v}}
. Consider the image,
\phi([\eta,\xi])
. As both
\eta
and
\xi
are fixed, and continuous image of connected set is connected,
[\eta,\xi]\subseteq\phi([\eta,\xi])
. Let
\alpha
be the unique point lying on the arcs
[\eta,\xi],[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
and
[\zeta_{Gauss},\eta]
. As
\eta\notin[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
, the point
\alpha
is distinct from
\zeta_{Gauss},\eta
and
\xi
. As
\zeta_{Gauss}
and
\xi
are both totally ramified fixed points of
\phi
,
\phi([\zeta_{Gauss},\xi])=[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
. Also, by Theorem
2.5
, the arc
[\zeta_{Gauss},\xi]
lies in the locus of total ramification of
\phi
. Hence, there is a totally ramified point
\beta
lying in
[\zeta_{Gauss},\alpha)
(because of Theorem
2.3
) such that
\phi(\beta)=\alpha
. As
\phi
is totally ramified at
\beta
and
\beta
does not lie on
[\eta,\xi]
, the point
\alpha
does not lie in
\phi([\eta,\xi])
. Thus,
[\eta,\xi]\nsubseteq\phi([\eta,\xi])
. We arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\phi
be a rational map, in normalized form, of degree
d\geq 2
with potential good reduction. Then, the number of connected components of fixed point locus of
\phi
is bounded above by
(d+2)
.

Proof.

As the connectedness properties of the fixed point locus are invariant under Möbius conjugation, without loss of generality we can assume
\phi
has good reduction.

Let
C
denote the connected component of the fixed point locus of
\phi
containing
\zeta_{Gauss}
. Let
\vec{v}
be a direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
. If
\phi
fixes a point in
B_{\vec{v}}
, as
\phi
has good reduction,
\phi(B_{\vec{v}})=B_{\vec{v}}
. Thus, we only need to look for fixed points of
\phi
in the residually fixed directions of
\phi
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
.

If
\vec{v}
is a residually fixed non-critical direction of
\phi
, then proof of Theorem
3.2
shows that any fixed point of
\phi
lying in
B_{\vec{v}}
lies in
C
, the connected component of fixed point locus of
\phi
containing
\zeta_{Gauss}
.

If
\vec{v}
is a residually fixed critical direction of
\phi
, then by Lemma
3.8
, the only fixed points of
\phi
lying in
B_{\vec{v}}
are of type
1
and they are isolated points of the fixed point locus of
\phi
. The degree
d
rational map
\phi
has atmost
(d+1)
many distinct fixed points of type
1
. Hence, including
C
, the number of connected components of fixed point locus of
\phi
is atmost
(d+2)
.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\phi
be a degree
d\geq 2
rational map with potentially good reduction. Let
\xi
be the unique type
2
totally ramified fixed point of
\phi
. Every residually fixed direction of
\phi
at
\xi
is residually critical if and only if the fixed point locus of
\phi
is a finite set. In this case, the fixed point locus of
\phi
consists of the type
1
fixed points of
\phi
and
\xi
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\phi
has good reduction, i.e.
\xi=\zeta_{Gauss}
.

(\Rightarrow)
Any direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
that contains a fixed point of
\phi
is residually fixed direction of
\phi
. If every such direction is residually critical, then the directional multiplicity of
\phi
along those directions are greater than
1
. By Lemma
3.8
, the connected component of
\phi
containing the Gauss point consists of only the Gauss point. Also from Lemma
3.8
, the other fixed points of
\phi
are isolated type
1
fixed points of
\phi
. Hence, the only if part follows.

(\Leftarrow)
If the fixed point locus of
\phi
is finite, the Gauss point must be an isolated fixed point of
\phi
. We claim that
\phi
can not have a residually non-critical fixed direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
. We prove it by contradiction. Let
\vec{v}
be a direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
that is residually fixed and has directional multiplicity
1
under
\phi
. Then, a proof parallel to the proof of Lemma
3.1
shows that
\phi
has type
2
fixed points in
B_{\vec{v}}
. As the directional multiplicity of
\phi
in the direction
\vec{v}
at
\zeta_{Gauss}
is
1
, by Theorem
3.2
any such type
2
fixed points will lie in the connected component of the fixed point locus containing
\zeta_{Gauss}
. This contradicts the isolatedness of
\zeta_{Gauss}
in the fixed point locus.

The final statement of the theorem has already been proved.
∎

Next, we give an example of Theorem
3.11
which also shows that the bound mentioned Theorem
3.10
is
sharp
.

Example 3.12
.

Let
p
be a prime number. Consider the Berkovich projective line over
{\mathbbm{C}}_{p}
, the field of
p
-adic complex numbers. Consider the map,
\phi(z)=z^{p}
. Every direction at
\zeta_{Gauss}
is totally ramified under
\phi
. Hence,
\zeta_{Gauss}
is an isolated fixed point of
\phi
. The other fixed points of
\phi
must be of type
1
. They consists of
\infty
and the roots of
z^{p}-z
in
{\mathbbm{C}}_{p}
. Hence, the fixed point locus of
\phi
consists of
(p+2)
many distinct isolated fixed points of
\phi
.

Briot-Bouquet differential subordinations of analytic functions involving the Mittag-Leffler function defined in Cardioid domain
Asena Çetinkaya ∗ , Şahsene Altınkaya

Abstract
. In this researh work, we establish a new subclass of analytic functions constructed by the Mittag-Leffler function that maps the open unit disc onto the region bounded by the Cardioid domain. Using a technique introduced by Miller and Mocanu, we investigate several Briot–Bouquet differential subordinations for this function class.

Introduction

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of analytic functions
f
of the form

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n+1}z^{n+1}

(1.1)

in the open unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}
. Denote by
\Omega
the class of Schwarz functions
\vartheta
which are analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
\vartheta(0)=0
and
|\vartheta(z)|<1
. For analytic functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in
{\mathbb{D}}
, we state that
f_{1}
is subordinate to
f_{2}
, symbolized by
f_{1}\prec f_{2}
, if there exists a function
\vartheta
in
\Omega
fulfilling
f_{1}(z)=f_{2}(\vartheta(z))
. The
comprehensive details of subordination can be found in
[
3
]
.

The convolution of functions
f_{1}(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n+1}z^{n+1}
and
f_{2}(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n+1}z^{n+1}
is expressed by

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)\ast f_{2}(z)=(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n+1}b_{n+1}z^{n+1},\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Ma and Minda
[
8
]
investigated the class of analytic functions
h
with positive real part in
{\mathbb{D}}
that map the disc
{\mathbb{D}}
onto regions starlike with respect to
1
, symmetric with respect to the real axis and normalized by the conditions
h(0)=1
and
h^{\prime}(0)>0
, and introduced the class

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(h)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\,\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec h(z)\right\}.

For the case
h(z)=(1+Mz)/(1+Nz)\ (-1\leq N<M\leq 1)
, the family of Janowski starlike functions
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}[M,N]
is obtained (see
[
6
]
). When
M=1-2\delta

(0\leq\delta<1)
and
N=-1
, we have the family
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\delta)
of
starlike functions of order
\delta
. Particularly,
\delta=0
yields the
usual class
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(0)=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
of starlike functions.
Recently, Ma–Minda type families of starlike functions have been
investigated by Mendiretta
et al.

[
7
]
, Sokól and
Stankiewicz
[
14
]
and Cho
et al.

[
2
]
. In
[
13
]
,
Sharma
et al.
consider the subfamily
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{c}
of
Ma–Minda classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(h)
which are endowed with the analytic
function
h_{c}(z)=1+4z/3+2z^{2}/3
that is univalent, starlike with respect
to 1 and maps
{\mathbb{D}}
onto a region bounded by the cardioid

In 1903, a Swedish mathematician Gosta Magnus Mittag-
Leffler discovered a celebrated function
E_{\alpha}

(\alpha,z\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \Re(\alpha)>0)
defined by

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+1)},

where
\Gamma(.)
represents the Gamma function (see
[
10
]
). It is observed that the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha}
is
an entire function of
z
with order
[\Re(\alpha)]^{-1}
. Later, Wiman
[
15
]
studied the generalized Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha,\beta}

(\alpha,\beta,z\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \Re(\alpha)>0,\ \Re(\beta)>0)
with two parameters defined by

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)}.

Since then, the function
E_{\alpha,\beta}
and its several generalizations
arose in the solution of fractional differential equations, super diffusive transport problems, Levy flights and in some other problems. For more details, one may refer
to
[
1
,
4
]
and references given therein.

In
[
11
]
, Prabhakar defined a new form of the Mittag-Leffler function with three parameters and introduced the function
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}

\big(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,z\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \Re(\alpha)>0,\ \Re(\beta)>0,\ \Re(\gamma)>0\big)
given by

E_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\gamma)_{n}\ z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)\ n!},

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle\left(\gamma\right)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+n)}{\Gamma(\gamma)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&n=0\\
&\\
\gamma(\gamma+1)\ldots(\gamma+n-1),&n\in\mathbb{N}\par\end{array}\right.

is the Pochhammer symbol. We remark that

\displaystyle E_{\alpha,1}^{1}(z)=:E_{\alpha}(z),\ \ \ \ E_{\alpha,\beta}^{1}(z)=:E_{\alpha,\beta}(z).

The function given by (
1.2
) is not an element of
\mathcal{A}
. Hence, we consider the normalization of the function
E^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}
via

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\Gamma(\beta)zE^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\beta)(\gamma)_{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)\ n!}z^{n+1}.

Corresponding to the function
\mathbb{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}
and the function
f
of the form (
1.1
), consider the linear operator
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}f(z)=\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\ast f(z)=z+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(\beta\right)\left(\gamma\right)_{n}}{\Gamma\left(\alpha n+\beta\right)n!}a_{n+1}z^{n+1},\ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

(1.4)

where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Re(\alpha)>0,\Re(\beta)>0,\Re(\gamma)>0
. This operator was introduced by Raducanu
[
12
]
and studied for the case real-valued
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
with
\alpha>0,\beta>0,\gamma>0
.
From (
1.4
), it is easily verified that

z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)\big)^{\prime}=(1-\gamma)\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)+\gamma\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z),

(1.5)

and

\alpha z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)\big)^{\prime}=\beta\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)+(\alpha-\beta)\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z).

(1.6)

In view of the linear operator
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}
, we define a new function class which maps the disc
{\mathbb{D}}
onto a domain bounded by
cardioid.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
given by (
1.1
) is in the class
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(h_{c})
if it satisfies the condition

\frac{z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)\big)^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}\prec h_{c}(z)=1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(1.7)

where
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}
is given by (
1.4
) and
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

This paper deals with some applications of the Briot-Bouquet differential
subordination

\phi(z)+\frac{z\phi^{\prime}(z)}{\eta\phi(z)+\mu}\prec h(z),\ \ \ (\eta,\mu\in{\mathbb{C}},\eta\neq 0)

(1.8)

with
\phi(0)=h(0)=1
. If the univalent function
q(z)=1+q_{1}z+q_{2}z^{2}+...
has
the feature
\phi\prec q
for all analytic functions
\phi
, then it
is called a dominant of (
1.8
). If
\tilde{q}\prec q
for all dominants
q
, then a dominant
\tilde{q}
is called
the best dominant. By
using a technique based upon Briot-Bouquet differential subordination which
was investigated by Miller and Mocanu
[
9
]
, we establish several
subordination properties for the function class defined by the Mittag-Leffler
function bounded by the cardioid domain. Thus, we shall express the essential lemmas.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
5
]
Assume that
h

(h(0)=1)
is a convex univalent function in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Also, assume that
\phi
of the form
\phi(z)=1+c_{1}z+c_{2}z^{2}+...

(\phi(0)=1)

is an analytic function in
{\mathbb{D}}
. If

\displaystyle\phi(z)+\frac{1}{\mu}z\phi^{\prime}(z)\prec h(z),\ \ \ (\mu\neq 0,\ \Re\mu\geq 0)

then

\phi(z)\prec\tilde{h}(z)=\frac{\mu}{z^{\mu}}\int_{0}^{z}t^{\mu-1}h(t)dt\prec h(z)

(1.9)

and
\tilde{h}
is the best dominant of (
1.9
).

Lemma 1.3
.

[
9
]
Assume that
\eta
(
\eta\neq 0
) and
\mu
are complex
numbers, and
h

(h(0)=1)
is a convex univalent function in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
\Re(\eta h(z)+\mu)>0
. Also, assume that
\phi
is analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
and
satisfy (
1.8
). If the Briot-Bouquet differential equation

q(z)+\frac{zq^{\prime}(z)}{\eta q(z)+\mu}=h(z),\ \ (q(0)=1)

(1.10)

has a univalent solution
q
, then

\displaystyle\phi(z)\prec q(z)\prec h(z)

and
q
is the best dominant of (
1.8
). The solution of (
1.10
) is

q(z)=z^{\mu}[H(z)]^{\eta}\bigg(\eta\int_{0}^{z}[H(t)]^{\eta}t^{\mu-1}dt\bigg)^{-1}-\mu/\eta,

(1.11)

where

\displaystyle H(z)=z\exp\int_{0}^{z}\frac{h(t)-1}{t}dt.

2.
Subordination properties for the operator
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}

In this section, we examine differential subordination properties for the linear
operator
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}
. Throughout this paper, we restrict our attention to the case real-valued
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
with
\alpha>0,\beta>0
and
\gamma>0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\zeta\geq 1
. If the function
f\in\mathcal{A}
given by (
1.1
) holds the condition

(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(2.1)

then

\Re\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{1/\zeta}\bigg\}>\bigg(\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du\bigg)^{1/\zeta}.

(2.2)

The result is sharp.

Proof.

Consider the analytic function

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z},\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

with
\phi(0)=1
in
{\mathbb{D}}
.
Now, differentiating the above equality and using (
1.5
),
we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\phi(z)+\frac{1}{\gamma}z\phi^{\prime}(z).

By applying (
2.1
), we write

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\phi(z)+\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}z\phi^{\prime}(z)\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}.

By using Lemma
1.2
on the right-hand side of the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi(z)\prec\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}z^{-\gamma/\lambda}\int_{0}^{z}t^{\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3+4t+2t^{2}}{3}\bigg)dt,

or

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3+4u\vartheta(z)+2(u\vartheta(z))^{2}}{3}\bigg)du,

(2.3)

where
\vartheta
is a Schwarz function. In
[
13
]
, Sharma
et al.
observed that

\displaystyle\min_{|z|=r}\Re(h_{c}(z))=\min_{|z|=r}|h_{c}(z)|=h_{c}(-r)=1-\frac{4r}{3}+\frac{2r^{2}}{3}

if
r\leq 1/2
, and also
\Re(h_{c}(\vartheta(z)))>h_{c}(-r)
. Therefore, using above relation and letting
r\rightarrow(1/2)^{-}
in equality (
2.3
), we arrive at

\Re\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)>\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du>0,\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

(2.4)

where
\lambda>0
. Since
\Re(\vartheta^{1/\zeta})\geq\Re(\vartheta)^{1/\zeta}
for
\Re(\vartheta)>0
and
\zeta\geq 1
, from (
2.4
) we prove the inequality (
2.2
).
To prove sharpness, we take
f\in\mathcal{A}
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3+4uz+2(uz)^{2}}{3}\bigg)du.

For this function we find that

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\frac{3+4z+2z^{2}}{3}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\rightarrow\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du

as
z\rightarrow(1/2)^{-}
. Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

By using the recurrence formula given by (
1.6
), we get the
following subordination result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\zeta\geq 1
. If the function
f\in\mathcal{A}
given by (
1.1
) holds the condition

(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(2.5)

then

\Re\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{1/\zeta}\bigg\}>\bigg(\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du\bigg)^{1/\zeta}.

(2.6)

The result is sharp.

Proof.

Consider the function

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z},\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

is analytic with
\phi(0)=1
in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Differentiating both sides and using (
1.6
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\phi(z)+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}z\phi^{\prime}(z).

By using (
2.5
), we conclude that

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\phi(z)+\frac{\lambda\alpha}{\beta}z\phi^{\prime}(z)\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}.

According to the Lemma
1.2
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\phi(z)\prec\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}z^{-\beta/\lambda\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}t^{\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}-1}\bigg(\frac{3+4t+2t^{2}}{3}\bigg)dt,

and there exists a function
\vartheta\in\Omega
such that the equality

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}=\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}-1}\bigg(\frac{3+4u\vartheta(z)+2(u\vartheta(z))^{2}}{3}\bigg)du

holds. For
\lambda>0
, and letting
r\rightarrow(1/2)^{-}
we get

\Re\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}\bigg)>\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du>0,\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

(2.7)

Since
\Re(\vartheta^{1/\zeta})\geq\Re(\vartheta)^{1/\zeta}
for
\Re(\vartheta)>0
and
\zeta\geq 1
, from (
2.7
) we prove the inequality
(
2.6
).
The sharp function is

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=\frac{3+4z+2z^{2}}{3}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}{z}\rightarrow\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\beta}{\lambda\alpha}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du

as
z\rightarrow(1/2)^{-}
. Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

Now, for a function
f
in
\mathcal{A}
defined by (
1.1
) we consider a generalized
operator known as Bernardi-Libera-Livingston integral operator
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
given by

\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)=\frac{\sigma+1}{z^{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{z}t^{\sigma-1}f(t)dt,\ \ \ (\sigma>-1).

(2.8)

From this operator, we easily get

z(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z))^{\prime}=(\sigma+1)\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)-\sigma\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z).

(2.9)

Next, we obtain the differential subordination for the operator
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}
associated with the Bernardi–Libera–Livingston integral operator
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
0<\lambda<1
and
\zeta\geq 1
. If the function
f\in\mathcal{A}
given by (
1.1
) satisfies the subordination condition

(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(2.10)

then

\Re\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{1/\zeta}\bigg\}>\bigg(\frac{\sigma+1}{1-\lambda}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\sigma+1}{1-\lambda}-1}\bigg(\frac{3-2u+u^{2}/2}{3}\bigg)du\bigg)^{1/\zeta}.

(2.11)

The result is sharp.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z},\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

Differentiating on both sides and using the relations (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we get

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}+\lambda\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}=\phi(z)+\frac{1-\lambda}{\sigma+1}z\phi^{\prime}(z)\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}.

Applying the same method as given in Theorem
2.1
, we get (
2.11
).
∎

3.
Subordination properties for the class
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(h_{c})

In this section, we find univalent solutions to the Briot–Bouquet differential equation, and observe that these solutions are the best dominant to the
Briot–Bouquet differential subordination for the class
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(h_{c})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If the function
f
belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(h_{c})
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
\Re(\gamma)\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle\Re\bigg(\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}+\gamma\bigg)>0,\ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

then

\frac{z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)\big)^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}\prec q(z)\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(3.1)

where

q(z)=z^{\gamma}e^{(\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{z^{2}}{3})}\bigg(\int_{0}^{z}t^{\gamma-1}e^{(\frac{4t}{3}+\frac{t^{2}}{3})}dt\bigg)^{-1}-\gamma+1,

(3.2)

and
q
is the best dominant of (
3.1
).

Proof.

Consider the analytic function

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)\big)^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)},\ \ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

with
\phi(0)=1
. By using (
1.5
), we
get

\displaystyle\gamma\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}=\phi(z)+\gamma-1.

Differentiating logarithmically with respect to
z
and multiplying by
z
,
from above equality we derive

\frac{z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)\big)^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma+1}_{\alpha,\beta}f(z)}=\phi(z)+\frac{z\phi^{\prime}(z)}{\phi(z)+\gamma-1}\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}.

(3.3)

Let us consider the differential equation

q(z)+\frac{zq^{\prime}(z)}{q(z)+\gamma-1}=h_{c}(z)=1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(3.4)

where
q

(q(0)=1)
is analytic and
h_{c}(z)=1+4z/3+2z^{2}/3
is convex univalent with
h_{c}(0)=1
in
{\mathbb{D}}
,
and let
P(z)=\eta h_{c}(z)+\mu
. In view of (
3.4
) and Lemma
1.3
,
we observe that
\eta=1
,
\mu=\gamma-1
and

\displaystyle P(z)=\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}+\gamma.

For proving
\Re(P(z))>0
, it is enough to set
z=e^{it},\ t\in[0,\pi]

under the condition
\Re(\gamma)\geq 1
. Furthermore,
P(z)
and
1/P(z)

are convex. Hence, there is a univalent solution of the equation (
3.4
). To get this solution, we apply to the Lemma
1.3
. Since
h_{c}(z)=1+4z/3+2z^{2}/3
, we find

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}H(z)&=z\exp\int\limits_{0}^{z}\frac{h_{c}(t)-1}{t}dt\\
&\\
&=z\exp\int\limits_{0}^{z}\frac{1+4t/3+2t^{2}/3-1}{t}dt=ze^{\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{z^{2}}{3}}.\end{array}

Setting this result together with
\eta=1
and
\mu=\gamma-1
into the
formula (
1.11
), we obtain (
3.2
) which is the univalent solution
of the differential equation given by (
3.4
). Since
\phi
is
analytic and satisfy (
3.3
), then we derive

\displaystyle\phi(z)\prec q(z)\prec h_{c}(z)=1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{z^{2}}{3}

and
q
is the best dominant of (
3.1
).
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

If the function
f
belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(h_{c})
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)\neq 0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
\Re(\beta/\alpha)\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle\Re\bigg(\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\bigg)>0,\ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}})

then

\frac{z\big(\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)\big)^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta+1}f(z)}\prec q_{1}(z)\prec 1+\frac{4z}{3}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3},

(3.6)

where

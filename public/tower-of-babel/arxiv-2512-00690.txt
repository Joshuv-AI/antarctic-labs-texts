Finiteness of leaps of modules of integrable derivations of algebras of finite type
Takuya Miyamoto

Abstract
. We prove the finiteness of leaps of modules of m m -integrable derivations for algebras essentially of finite type and, more generally, for schemes essentially of finite type over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic. This provides an affirmative answer to a question posed by L. N. Macarro. As an application, we establish the coherence of the module of ∞ \infty -integrable derivations.



1.
Introduction

Hasse-Schmidt derivations (abbreviated as HS-derivations) were introduced in
[
13
]
, which are also called higher derivations
[
6
, §27]
. Given a positive integer
m
, a field
k
and a
k
-algebra
R
,
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)
denotes the group of HS-derivations of length
m
, which consists of
A_{m}:=k[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})
-algebra automorphisms

R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})\to R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})

that become the identity when restricted to
R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})\otimes_{A_{m}}k=R
. For
m=\infty
, we regard
t^{\infty}=0
, and we also have the notion of HS-derivations of infinite length. There is a canonical isomorphism between
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{1}(R)
and the module
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
. Under this identification, the image of

\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{1}(R)\simeq\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)

is called the module of
m
-integrable derivations and denoted by
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)
, which has been studied in
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
. The module
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{\infty}(R)
is also denoted by
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
. Note that if
k
contains
\mathbb{Q}
or if
R
is formally smooth over
k
, we have
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)=\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R)
for any
m\leq\infty
(see
[
6
]
). Thus, the study of integrable derivations is expected to lead to a better understanding of singularities in positive characteristic. From now on, we assume that
k
is an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p
and
R
is essentially of finite type. We have the following descending chain

\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R)\supset\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{2}(R)\supset\dots\supset\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)\supset\dots.

The question of when the inclusion
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m-1}(R)\supset\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(R)
is proper has been studied since their introduction. When this is the case, we say that
R

leaps
at
m
. It has been known that leaps occur only at
m=p^{i}
(
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
). The number of leaps is finite if
R
is a unibranch curve (
[
10
, Theorem 4.7]
), and, more generally, if
R
is a reduced curve (
[
1
, Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.3]
).
The aim of this paper is to show, using results from
[
7
, §2]
, that the finiteness of leaps holds for every algebra essentially of finite type over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic. Precisely, we show:

Theorem 1.1

(= Theorem
5.5
)
.

There exists
M>0
(depending on
R
) such that

\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M}(R)=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M+1}(R)=\dots=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{\infty}(R)=\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R).

This theorem provides, for the first time, a general finiteness result for leaps in arbitrary dimensions, thereby giving an affirmative answer to Macarro’s question [8, Question 3.6.5] and contributing to a better structural understanding of HS-derivations. Since leaps are stable under separable base changes by
[
3
, Theorem 3.27]
, this also implies the finiteness of leaps over any perfect field. We also give an affirmative answer to another question
[
11
, Question 3.6.1]
by the following:

Theorem 1.2

(= Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let
D\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{n}(R)
. Then
D
is
\infty
-integrable if and only if
D
is
m
-integrable for every integer
m\geq n
. Here, we say that
D
is
m
-integrable if
D
is in the image of
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{n}(R)
.

In fact, Theorem
1.1
is a special case of Theorem
1.2
where we put
n=1
. As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.3

(=Corollary
5.6
)
.

The module
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{\infty}(R)=\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
is compatible with localization of
R
. That is, we have

\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(S^{-1}R)=S^{-1}\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R).

This is the first coherence result for
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
to the best of our knowledge. For
m<\infty
, a similar statement has been proven in
[
11
, Corollary 2.3.5]
. The case
m=\infty
is more subtle because the map in
[
11
, Corollary 1.3.6]
is not surjective for
m=\infty
while
m
is assumed to be an integer there (cf.
[
15
, Example 1.4]
). Note that Corollary
1.3
implies that
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
constitutes a coherent sheaf
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
on an algebraic scheme
X
over
k
. In particular, it is expected to behave better than the usual tangent sheaf. In fact, some pathological phenomena of derivations peculiar to positive characteristic do not occur if we replace it by integrable derivations. For example, the local rank of
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
is at most
\operatorname{dim}R
, while it is not true for
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R)
(see
[
9
]
). This pathology does not occur in characteristic zero (see
[
4
]
when
R
is a domain and
[
8
]
in general). We can also state Theorem
1.1
in terms of schemes:

Theorem 1.4

(= Theorem
5.7
)
.

Let
X
be a
k
-scheme essentially of finite type (=locally essentially of finite type and quasi-compact). Then there exists
M>0
such that

\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M+1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\dots=\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),

where
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{m}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
denotes the sheaf of
m
-integrable derivations (see Theorem
2.3
).

We briefly outline the contents of the paper.

In Section 2, we recall the definition of HS-derivations and results that will be used later. A key relation is that the obstructions to extending HS-derivations are canonically dual to
m
-integrable derivations (Theorem
2.11
). We also define the property
\operatorname{FL}(i)
for algebras and schemes, which states that leaps do not occur at every
m>p^{i}
(Definition
2.12
).

In Section 3, we introduce the notion of
a
-boundedness for HS-derivations. Intuitively, this concept measures how each term of an HS-derivation shrinks with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology.

In Section 4, we prove the finiteness of leaps in the local case. In this section we fix a local
k
-algebra
(R,\mathfrak{n})
such that
\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak{n}\}
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(\tau)
for
\tau>0
, and we will prove that
\operatorname{Spec}R
itself satisfies the finiteness of leaps. The idea is that, starting from a bounded HS-derivation, we construct another one that is less bounded and coincides with the original one in small degrees. We use the Artin-Rees lemma to obtain positive constants which control the bounds. The essential step is Theorem
4.15
, which states that elements of

\mathfrak{n}^{e(q+1)+1}\cdot\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{q}(R)

do not leap, where
q=p^{\tau}
and
e\gg 0
. Then the fact that
R/\mathfrak{n}^{e(q+1)+1}
is of finite length implies the finiteness of leaps of
R
(Theorems
4.16
,
4.17
).

In Section 5, we prove the finiteness of leaps in the global case and discuss its consequences. We use noetherian induction to reduce to the case of Section 4 (Theorem
5.1
). The finiteness of leaps of
m
-integrable derivations implies that of obstruction modules. This enables us to extend HS-derivations step by step (Lemma
5.3
), and taking a limit, we obtain a HS-derivation of infinite length: This method yields a characterization of
\infty
-integrability (Theorem
5.4
).

Throughout this paper, we fix an algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic
p
. We assume that any
k
-algebra is unital and commutative. We say that a
k
-scheme
X
is essentially of finite type if there exists a finite open affine covering
\{U_{i}=\operatorname{Spec}R_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
for
X
where each
R_{i}
is a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type. We assume
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,\dots\}
.

[

Definition 2.1

(=
[
11
, Definition 1.2.1-1.2.6]
)
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra. Let
m\geq 1
be an integer or
m=\infty
. Then a
Hasse-Schmidt derivation
of length
m
is a sequence
D=(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
of
k
-linear endomorphisms
R\to R
such that
D_{0}=\operatorname{id}
and that

D_{i}(xy)=\sum_{j+k=i}D_{j}(x)D_{k}(y)

for every
x,y\in R
,
i\geq 1.
Let
E=(E_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
be another Hasse-Schmidt derivation. We define the
composition
of
D
and
E
by

D\circ E=(F_{i})_{i=0}^{m}\mathrm{\ \ where\ }\ F_{i}(x)=\sum_{j+k=i}D_{j}(E_{k}(x)).

The symbol
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)(=\operatorname{HS}_{k}(R,m))
denotes the set of all the Hasse-Schmidt derivations of length
m.
It forms a group under this compsition. Let
x\in R
. We define

x\bullet:\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)

by

x\bullet(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}=(x^{i}D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}.

For
n\geq 1,
we define

[n]:\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{mn+n-1}(R)

by

D[n]=(E_{i})_{i=0}^{mn+n-1}\mathrm{\ \ where\ }\ E_{jn}=D_{j}\mathrm{\ for\ }0\leq j\leq m\mathrm{\ and\ }E_{i}=0\mathrm{\ otherwise.}

(Although
D[n]
is usually defined to have length
mn
, it naturally extends to length
mn+n-1
by adding zero terms.)
A Hasse-Schmidt derivation
D=(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
defines a natural
A_{m}
-algebra automorphism

R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})\to R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1}),\ x\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{m}D_{i}(x)t^{i}\quad\mathrm{for}\ x\in R

where
A_{m}:=k[\![t]\!]/t^{m+1}
. (We regard
t^{\infty}=0
.) This yields an isomorphism of groups between
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)
and the group of
A_{m}
-algebra automorphisms of
R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})

that become the identity when restricted to
R[\![t]\!]/(t^{m+1})\otimes_{A_{m}}k=R
.
Let
1\leq l\leq m
. We define the
truncation map

\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{l}(R)

by

D=(D_{i})_{i=0}^{m}\mapsto(D_{i})_{i=0}^{l}.

It is a group homomorphism and commutes with
x\bullet
for
x\in R
.
Note that for
m=1
,
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{1}(R)
identifies with the
R
-module
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
under

D=(\operatorname{id},D_{1})\mapsto D_{1}.

(=
[

,

Let

as the image of

\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\overset{}{\to}\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{1}(R)\simeq\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R).

It is a sub-

. We also denote
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{\infty}(R)

(=
[

)

Let

(=
[
2

)
.

Let
R
be a

-algebra essentially of finite type. For every integer

(=

Let

3.
On
a
-boundedness

Definition 3.1
.

Let
a=(a_{n})_{n=1,2,\dots}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{N}
. Then we say that
a
is
subadditive
if
a_{n+m}\leq a_{n}+a_{m}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a=(a_{n})_{n=1,2,\dots}
and
b=(b_{n})_{n=1,2,\dots}
be sequences in
\mathbb{N}
. Let
a+b
and
\max\{a,b\}
be sequences defined by

a+b:=(a_{n}+b_{n})_{n},\ \ \max\{a,b\}:=(\max\{a_{n},b_{n}\})_{n}.

Then if
a
and
b
are subadditive,
a+b
and
\max\{a,b\}
are also subadditive.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
a=(a_{n})_{n=1,2,\dots}
be a subadditive sequence in
\mathbb{N}
,
R
be a
k
-algebra and
\mathfrak{m}\subset R
be an ideal. Let
D=(D_{n})_{n=0}^{m}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)
for
1\leq m\leq\infty
. Then we say that
D
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically
a
-bounded
[resp.
\mathfrak{m}
-logarithmically
a
-bounded
] if

D_{n}(R)\subset\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}}

for every
n
[resp.

D_{n}(\mathfrak{m}^{n^{\prime}})\subset\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}+n^{\prime}}

for every
n,n^{\prime}
]. When
\mathfrak{m}
is clear from the context (e.g., when
R
is a local ring and
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal), we simply say
a
-bounded
[resp.
logarithmically
a
-bounded
].

Note that the composition of logarithmically
a
-bounded HS-derivations is again logarithmically
a
-bounded because
a
is subadditive.

Lemma 3.4
.

In the setting of
3.3
, let

\widetilde{a}=(a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,a_{3}+1,\dots).

If
D=(D_{n})_{n=0}^{m}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)
is
\widetilde{a}
-bounded, then it is logarithmically
a
-bounded.

Proof.

First observe that
\widetilde{a}
is also subadditive. We show that by induction of
n^{\prime}=0,1,\dots,
we have

D_{n}(\mathfrak{m}^{n^{\prime}})\subset\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}+n^{\prime}}

for every
n
. The case
n^{\prime}=0
is trivial as
\widetilde{a}\geq a
. Suppose that the claim is true for
n^{\prime}
and let
x\in\mathfrak{m}^{n^{\prime}},y\in\mathfrak{m}
. Then

D_{n}(xy)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}D_{i}(x)D_{n-i}(y).

For
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
, we have

D_{i}(x)D_{n-i}(y)\in\mathfrak{m}^{a_{i}+n^{\prime}}\cdot\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n-i}+1}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}+n^{\prime}+1}.

For
i=n
, we also have

D_{n}(x)D_{0}(y)\in\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}+n^{\prime}}\cdot\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}+n^{\prime}+1}.

Thus, we have
D_{n}(xy)\in\mathfrak{m}^{a_{n}+n^{\prime}+1}
. As
\mathfrak{m}^{n^{\prime}+1}
is generated by such
xy
, this completes the induction.
∎

4.
The local case

In this section, we assume the following setting.

Setting 4.1
.

(T,\mathfrak{m})
is a formally smooth local
k
-algebra that is a localization of a polynomial ring in finite variables at a prime ideal (not necessarily maximal) and

is an ideal of

Lemma 4.2

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathfrak{n}^{n})^{e_{2}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathfrak{n}^{n})^{e_{1}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}

Lemma 4.5

Definition 4.7

be positive integers. Define three sequences of positive integers

\left\{\begin{aligned} a[e_{1},n_{1},q_{1}]&=(a[e_{1},n_{1},q_{1}]_{n})_{n=1,2,\dots},\\
\widetilde{a}[e_{1},n_{1},q_{1}]&=(\widetilde{a}[e_{1},n_{1},q_{1}]_{n})_{n=1,2,\dots},\\

\left\{\begin{aligned} a[e_{1},n_{1},q_{1}]_{n}&=e_{1}(q_{1}+1)\cdot\lceil\frac{nq_{1}}{n_{1}}\rceil,\\

Lemma 4.8

, we have

The first inequality is clear.

The last inequalities follow from the definition of

∎

a[e,m,q]
-bounded.

Then there exists
D^{+}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{\infty}(T)
such that:

(i)

D^{+}
is
I
-logarithmic up to
m+1
,

(ii)

D^{+}
is logarithmically
a[e,m+1,q]
-bounded,

(iii)

D^{+}
and
D
coincide up to
l_{m+1}-1
, i.e.,
D^{+}_{1}=D_{1},\dots,D^{+}_{l_{m+1}-1}=D_{l_{m+1}-1}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Proposition
4.12
,
4.13
.
∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
e
be an integer with

e\geq C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}\cdot q+C_{4}\cdot q+q

where the
C_{i}
are as in
4.2
-
4.6
. Let
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{q}(R)
be the module of
q
-integrable derivations of
R
. Then any element of

\mathfrak{n}^{e(q+1)+1}\cdot\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{q}(R)

is
m
-integrable for any positive integer
m
. In particular, the set of leaps produced by
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{q}(R)
is finite.

Proof.

Suppose that
d\in\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{q}(R)
and
x\in\mathfrak{n}^{e(q+1)+1}
. By assumption, there exists
\widetilde{E}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{q}(R)
such that
\widetilde{E}_{1}=d
. We can lift
\widetilde{E}
to
E\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{\infty}(T)
by formal smoothness of
T
. Let
y\in\mathfrak{m}^{e(q+1)+1}
be a preimage of
x
. Then
D:=y\bullet E
is
I
-logarithmic up to
q
and logarithmically
a[e,q,q]
-bounded (note that
a[e,q,q]=(e(q+1),2e(q+1),3e(q+1),\dots)
). Let
m
be an arbitrary positive integer. Applying the above proposition recursively, we obtain
D^{+}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{\infty}(T)
such that
D^{+}
is
I
-logarithmic up to
m
and
D
and
D^{+}
coincide up to
\min\{l_{q+1}-1,l_{q+2}-1,\dots,l_{m}-1\}.
Note that
l_{n}\geq 2
for any
n>q
. In particular,
y\cdot E_{1}=D^{+}_{1}
. This means that
x\cdot d
is
m
-integrable. This shows the first part of the theorem. The second part is a consequence of the following theorem.
∎

The next theorem is a slight variant of
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
,
[
10
, Proposition 1.11]
and its proof is essentially the same. For the convenience of the reader, we include the proof here as well.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
M
be a sub-
R
-module of
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
and
\mathfrak{l}\subset\mathfrak{n}
and ideal such that
R/\mathfrak{l}
is of finite length. Assume that any
d\in\mathfrak{l}\cdot M
is
m
-integrable for any positive integer
m
. Let
\operatorname{Leaps}(M)\subset\mathbb{N}
be the set of leaps produced by
M
. Then

\#\operatorname{Leaps}(M)\leq\operatorname{length}_{R}(M/\mathfrak{l}M).

In particular,
\operatorname{Leaps}(M)
is finite.

Proof.

As
k
is perfect, there exists a subfield
K\subset R/\mathfrak{l}
containing
k
such that
K\simeq R/\mathfrak{n}
. The length of
M/\mathfrak{l}M
is equal to the
K
-linear dimension of
M/\mathfrak{l}M
.

Now, let
L:=\operatorname{Leaps}(M)
. For each
s\in L
, we take
d_{s}\in M
leaping at
s
. We claim that the set
\{\overline{d}_{s}\}_{s\in L}
is
K
-linearly independent in
M/\mathfrak{l}M
, where
\overline{d}_{s}
is the image of
d_{s}
in
M/\mathfrak{l}M
.
Suppose that there exists a relation such that

a_{1}\overline{d}_{s_{1}}+\dots+a_{n}\overline{d}_{s_{n}}=0,\ a_{i}\in K

where
a_{1}\neq 0
and
s_{1}<\dots<s_{n}
. Then we have a relation in
M
such that

b_{1}d_{s_{1}}=-b_{2}d_{s_{2}}-\dots-b_{n}d_{s_{n}}+d,\ b_{i}\in R

where
b_{i}
are lifts of
a_{i}
to
R
and where
d\in\mathfrak{l}M
. Note that
b_{1}
is invertible, so that

d_{s_{1}}=b_{1}^{-1}(-b_{2}d_{s_{2}}-\dots-b_{n}d_{s_{n}}+d).

By assumption, there exists
D_{i}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{s_{i}}(R)
whose first component is equal to
d_{s_{i}}
for each
i
. In addition, there exists
D\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{t}(R)
whose first component is equal to
d
with
t\geq s_{2}
. Then the first component of

b_{1}^{-1}\bullet(((-b_{2})\bullet D_{2})\circ\dots\circ((-b_{n})\bullet D_{n})\circ D)\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{s_{2}}(R)

is equal to
d_{s_{1}}
. In the above expression,
D_{i}
and
D
are understood to be truncated to length
s_{2}
. This implies that
d_{s_{1}}
is at least
s_{2}
-integrable, which contradicts the choice of
d_{s_{1}}
. This proves the claim, and the theorem follows from it.
∎

Theorem 4.17
.

The set of leaps of
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
is finite. In particular,
R
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(\theta)
for a sufficiently large integer
\theta
.

Proof.

The set of leaps of
\operatorname{Der}_{k}(R,R)
is contained in

\{p,p^{2},\dots,q\}\cup\operatorname{Leaps}(\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{q}(R)),

which is finite by Theorem
4.15
.
∎

5.
The global case

In the previous section, we proved that if a local
k
-algebra
(R,\mathfrak{n})
essentially of finite type satisfies the finiteness of leaps except the closed point, then so does
R
itself (Setting
4.1
, Theorem
4.17
). In this section, we apply this result to prove the finiteness of leaps for arbitrary
k
-schemes essentially of finite type.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a
k
-scheme essentially of finite type. Then
X
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(\tau)
for a sufficiently large integer
\tau
. In particular, the set of leaps of
X
is finite.

Proof.

We construct inductively a sequence of pairs
(F_{1},i_{1}),(F_{2},i_{2}),\dots
such that:

(i)

i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq i_{3}\leq\dots
are nonnegative integers,

(ii)

F_{n}
is a sub-
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p^{i_{n}}}
-sheaf of
T_{X/k}^{1}
that is coherent via
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p^{i_{n}}}
,

(iii)

\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p}\subset\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{2}}\subset\dots\subset F_{n}
for every
n
,

(iv)

F_{1}\supset F_{2}\supset F_{3}\supset\dots
,

(v)

for every
n
,
\operatorname{Supp}(F_{n}/\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{i_{n}}})\supsetneq\operatorname{Supp}(F_{n+1}/\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{i_{n+1}}})
.

By (v), this sequence must terminate at some
(F_{\tau},i_{\tau})
where we have

\operatorname{Supp}(F_{\tau}/\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{i_{\tau}}})=\emptyset,

i.e.,
F_{\tau}=\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{i_{\tau}}}
. By (iii),
X
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(\tau)
and in particular the set of leaps of
X
is finite.

We set
F_{1}=T_{X/k}^{1},\ i_{1}=0
. Suppose that we have constructed
(F_{n},i_{n})
and we would like to construct
(F_{n+1},i_{n+1})
satisfying (i)-(v). Let us take a generic point
\xi
of
\operatorname{Supp}(F_{n}/\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{i_{n}}})
(i.e. a point without generalizations). Let
R
be the local ring of
X
at
\xi
and let
Y=\operatorname{Spec}R,\ U:=Y\setminus\{\xi\}
. Then
U
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(i_{n})
as
\operatorname{Supp}(F_{n}|_{U}/\operatorname{Ob}_{U}^{p^{i_{n}}})=\emptyset
. Thus, we can apply the results of the previous section to the local
k
-algebra
R
. We can find an integer
i_{n+1}\geq i_{n}
such that
Y
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(i_{n+1})
. We define
F_{n+1}
as the subsheaf of
F_{n}
consisting of sections
s\in F_{n}
such that

s|_{Y}\in\operatorname{Ob}_{Y}^{p^{i_{n+1}}}.

Conditions (i) and (iv) follow from the construction. We have the following cartesian diagram of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p^{i_{n+1}}}
-sheaves

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{n+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\iota_{*}\operatorname{Ob}_{Y}^{i_{n+1}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\iota_{*}\iota^{*}F_{n}}}

where
\iota:Y\to X
. In other words, we have
F_{n+1}=F_{n}\times_{\iota_{*}\iota^{*}F_{n}}\iota_{*}\operatorname{Ob}_{Y}^{i_{n+1}}
. This implies quasi-coherence, and hence coherence of
F_{n+1}
in the sense stated in (ii). Condition (iii) follows from the fact that
Y
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(i_{n+1})
and from the construction. The restriction of the above diagram to
Y
is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\iota^{*}F_{n+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\iota^{*}\iota_{*}\operatorname{Ob}_{Y}^{i_{n+1}}=\operatorname{Ob}_{Y}^{i_{n+1}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\iota^{*}F_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\iota^{*}\iota_{*}\iota^{*}F_{n}},}

which is also cartesian. This implies
F_{n+1}|_{Y}=\iota^{*}F_{n+1}=\operatorname{Ob}_{Y}^{i_{n+1}}
and
\xi\notin\operatorname{Supp}(F_{n+1}/\operatorname{Ob}_{X}^{p^{i_{n+1}}})
. In particular, (v) follows.
∎

As an affine case of the above theorem, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

Any
k
-algebra
R
essentially of finite type satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(\tau)
for a sufficiently large integer
\tau
. In particular, the set of leaps of
R
is finite.

We now study
\infty
-integrability based on the finiteness of leaps. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type, let
m,\tau\geq 1
be integers, and let
q:=p^{\tau}
. Suppose that
R
satisfies
\operatorname{FL}(\tau)
. Let
D\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)
. Let
l_{1},l_{2},\dots
be the sequence of integers defined as in
4.11
. Then there exists
D^{+}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m+1}(R)
such that
D
and
D^{+}
coincide up to
l_{m+1}-1
, i.e.,
D_{1}=D^{+}_{1},\dots,D_{l_{m+1}-1}=D_{l_{m+1}-1}^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
r:={m+1}/l_{m+1}
. As
\operatorname{Ob}_{R}^{p^{\tau}}=\operatorname{Ob}_{R}^{p^{\tau+1}}=\dots
, there exists
E\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{r-1}(R)
such that
\operatorname{ob}_{m+1}(D)=-\operatorname{ob}_{r}(E)
. Then
D\circ E[l_{m+1}]
extends to
D^{+}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m+1}(R)
, whose first
l_{m+1}-1
terms coincide with those of
D
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type, let
n\geq 1
be an integer, and let
D\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{n}(R)
. Then
D
is
\infty
-integrable if and only if
D
is
m
-integrable for every integer
m\geq n
. Here, we say that
D
is
m
-integrable if
D
is in the image of
\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{m}(R)\to\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{n}(R)
.

Proof.

The “only if” direction is clear. Suppose that
D
is
m
-integrable for every integer
m\geq n
. Take a sufficiently large integer
N\gg n
such that
l_{m+1}-1\geq n
for every
m\geq N
. By assumption, there exists
E\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{N}(R)
that restricts to
D
. Inductively, we define

E^{0}=E\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{N}(R),

E^{m+1}=(E^{m})^{+}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{N+m+1}(R)

for
m=0,1,\dots
, where
(E^{m})^{+}
is defined using the previous lemma. For fixed
m^{\prime}=0,1,\dots
, we have a sequence of
k
-linear maps

E^{m}_{m^{\prime}},\ E^{m+1}_{m^{\prime}},\ E^{m+2}_{m^{\prime}},\dots

for sufficiently large
m\gg 0
. Eventually it becomes stationary because
l_{m}
tends to
\infty
as
m\to\infty
. Thus,

E^{\infty}:=(\lim_{m\to\infty}E^{m}_{m^{\prime}})_{m^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\in\operatorname{HS}_{k}^{\infty}(R)

is a well-defined Hasse-Schmidt derivation. Note that
E^{\infty}
restricts to
D
by the choice of
N
. Thus,
D
is
\infty
-integrable.
∎

Setting
n=1
in the above theorem, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type. Then there exists
M>1
such that

\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M}(R)=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M+1}(R)=\dots=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{\infty}(R)=\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R).

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra essentially of finite type. Then
\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{\infty}(R)=\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
is compatible with localization of
R
. That is, we have

\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(S^{-1}R)=S^{-1}\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)

as
S^{-1}R
-modules for any multiplicatively closed subset
S\subset R
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Thus,
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(R)
constitutes a coherent sheaf
\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
on a
k
-scheme
X
essentially of finite type. Using this sheaf, we can state Theorem
5.5
in terms of schemes.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
X
be a
k
-scheme essentially of finite type. Then there exists
M>0
such that

\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{Der}_{k}^{M+1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\dots=\operatorname{Ider}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

Finally, we conclude the paper by noting that the above results extend to the case of perfect fields.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
k^{\prime}
be a perfect field of positive characteristic and let
R
be a
k^{\prime}
-algebra of finite type. Then the set of leaps of
R
as a
k^{\prime}
-algebra is finite.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
5.2
and the fact that leaps are stable under separable base changes
[
3
, Theorem 3.27]
.
∎

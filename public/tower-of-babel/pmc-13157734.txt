ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionStereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) delivered on an MRI-guided linear accelerator (MR-linac) enables highly conformal prostate cancer irradiation. The DESTINATION 2 trial is a federated, randomised phase II/R-IDEAL 2b study evaluating whether de-escalating the dose to prostate tissue, while maintaining a high dose to MRI-visible tumour(s) in two fractions, reduces genitourinary (GU) treatment-related adverse events (AE) without compromising disease control in men with localised prostate cancer.Methods200 men worldwide with localised, MRI-visible prostate cancer will be randomised 1:1 to receive either (1) prescribed uniform dose MR-linac SBRT (27 Gy in two fractions to the whole prostate and seminal vesicles with 0 mm CTV-PTV margin) or (2) de-escalated SBRT (20 Gy in two fractions to whole prostate with 0 mm CTV-PTV margin and 27 Gy in two fractions to MRI-visible tumour(s) with a 4 mm intraprostatic margin applied to the GTV. All treatments are delivered using MRI-guided adaptive Radiotherapy (MRIgRT). The primary endpoint is the absolute and relative risk reduction in acute grade 2+GU AE (CTCAE v5) within 12 weeks of completing radiotherapy. Secondary endpoints include late GU AE, acute and late gastrointestinal (GI) AE, sexual AE, patient-reported outcomes, dosimetry, technical feasibility and 2-year biochemical relapse-free survival.Ethics and disseminationThis is a federated trial design in which each centre operates independently with its own sponsor, ethics committee approval and regulatory oversight. Each centre is responsible for obtaining and maintaining local ethics approval in accordance with their national and institutional requirements. The UK centre (The Royal Marsden NHS Foundation Trust) has received ethical approval from the East of England–Cambridge South Research Ethics Committee (REC reference: 24/EE/0163; IRAS: 338368). Results will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations.Trial registration numberNCT06638541.

Introduction

Radiotherapy for localised prostate cancer has evolved rapidly, shifting from conventional, protracted external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) courses (35–42 fractions over 7–8 weeks) to hypofractionated and, more recently, ultra-hypofractionated stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) as standard of care, guided by robust level 1 evidence.15 The safety and efficacy of two-fraction schedules is supported by HDR brachytherapy data, reflecting the low α/β ratio of prostate cancer and the potential for shorter, less burdensome treatment.69

An unresolved question is whether it is necessary to deliver full tumouricidal dose to the entire morphologically normal prostate gland. Pathological studies and the pattern of intraprostatic recurrence suggest that clinically significant tumour burden is highly concentrated in MRI-visible lesions, with the remainder of the gland harbouring low volume microscopic or no disease at all.1013 The FLAME trial demonstrated that escalating dose with conventional fractionation to all tumours visible on multiparametric MRI resulted in 15% absolute improvement in biochemical disease-free survival at 10 years.14 At doses ≥90 Gy, the biochemical failure rate dropped to 5%, with no significant effect on GU or GI treatment-related adverse events (AE).1518 Directly boosting the tumour is appealing, given the knowledge that intraprostatic recurrences almost always occur at the original site of disease.13

MRI-guided adaptive radiotherapy (MRIgRT) enables precise daily adaptation and focal dose delivery, allowing selective tumour boosting while de-escalating dose to benign-appearing prostate. The DESTINATION 2 trial is a federated, randomised phase II/R-IDEAL 2b study designed to evaluate whether this approach in two fractions can reduce treatment-related AEs without compromising disease control in men with intermediate-risk prostate cancer.

We describe the DESTINATION 2 trial protocol, with particular focus on the study design and radiotherapy delivery.

Hypothesis

De-escalated two-fraction prostate SBRT will result in a clinically meaningful reduction in the absolute and relative risk of acute GU AE compared with standard two-fraction SBRT with a single prescription dose to both CTV and GTV. In addition, acute and late physician and patient reported outcomes, feasibility, PSA kinetics and dosimetry will be comparable.

Methods

Study design

DESTINATION 2 is an international, federated, randomised, open-label, phase II/R-IDEAL 2b trial conducted under the Research Governance Framework for Health and Social Care and Good Clinical Practice. In this federated design, participating centres worldwide collaborate by adhering to a common master protocol, ensuring methodological consistency across centres. However, each centre operates independently, retaining its own sponsor, regulatory approvals, ethics committee oversight and data management processes, and is responsible for trial conduct, including participant recruitment, data collection, and oversight at their centre. Local documents such as participant information sheets and consent forms may be translated or undergo minor administrative modifications to comply with local regulatory requirements, but no changes to trial design, eligibility criteria, treatment schedules, or outcome definitions are permitted. A central project management group, supported by Elekta and embedded within the MR-Linac Consortium infrastructure, facilitates contractual arrangements, supports ethics submissions, oversees protocol amendments and coordinates communication between sites to ensure consistent implementation of the master protocol across the federated network.

This model is supported by a Bayesian statistical framework, which allows data from independently conducted, potentially underpowered cohorts to be synthesised in a single analysis to generate robust estimates of treatment effect and uncertainty for the overall trial.

Eligible patients (co-enrolled to MOMENTUM, NCT04075305) are randomised 1:1 open-label via an electronic tool (Castor EDC, Amsterdam, NL) to uniform or de-escalated dosing, stratified by ADT use (trial schema figure 1). Data capture and trial management occurs centrally within the MOMENTUM registry.19

A federated design was selected to facilitate multinational recruitment among MR-linac centres, with pooled Bayesian analysis overcoming individual cohort size limitations and preserving methodological consistency.

Both arms in DESTINATION 2 are experimental because optimal MR-guided two-fraction SBRT regimens have not been established. The study randomises between uniform prescription whole-gland dosing or de-escalated dosing with an intraprostatic boost to compare disease control and toxicity using current MR-linac technology. This comparison will inform selection of a regimen for future phase III testing.

Participants and eligibility

A total of 200 patients will be recruited worldwide across all participating centres, with enrolment at each centre (or within each country) capped at a maximum of 54 participants (27 per arm), in accordance with the agreement established across all sponsors in this federated trial design. Eligible patients identified in multidisciplinary team (MDT) meetings will include those ≥18 years with histologically confirmed intermediate/lower high-risk prostate adenocarcinoma (Gleason 3+3, 3+4 or 4+3), MRI-visible tumour(s) (PIRADS ≥3, ≤2.5 cm), WHO performance status 0–2, PSA <20 ng/mL and ability to comply with trial procedures.12

Key exclusions include pathological evidence of high grade disease (ISUP grade group 3) unsuitable for surveillance outside of the MRI-defined area, MRI contraindications, severe GU symptoms (IPSS>19), prostate volume >90 cc, prior pelvic radiotherapy and participation in other interventional prostate cancer trials.20 Patients with comorbidities predisposing to significant AE (eg, inflammatory bowel disease) or those precluding long-term follow-up and pelvic metalwork or hip replacement which can cause artefact on diffusion-weighted imaging are not eligible.

Participants will receive the current Research Ethics Committee (REC) approved DESTINATION 2 patient information sheet (PIS) and the MOMENTUM PIS to read. Written informed consent is required for all participants after at least 24 hours of consideration (online supplemental file 1).

Hormone therapy (ADT) is permitted but not mandated. ADT, if indicated, may be administered neoadjuvantly, concurrently, or adjuvantly, with preference for concurrent or adjuvant use to optimise GTV visibility. Patients requiring >6 months of ADT are ineligible. Patients on stable doses of alpha blockers, antimuscarinics, or PDE5 inhibitors are scored as grade 1 per CTCAE v5; dosing increases or additions will be scored as grade 2.

Patients may discontinue from trial treatment or follow-up at any time at their own request or at the discretion of the principal investigator and will be followed up as per standard of care.

Patient and public involvement

Patient and public involvement was facilitated through stakeholder engagement at the national level. The study protocol was presented by a co-investigator (RW) to the NCRI Clinical and Translational Radiotherapy Research Working Group (CTRad), a UK national committee which includes patient and lay representatives alongside clinicians and researchers. Feedback from this committee informed trial design and helped ensure the protocol addresses questions of direct relevance to patients, as well as incorporating perspectives from the wider radiotherapy research community. The NIHR Biomedical Research Centre patient forum gave feedback on trial concept and design. The patient information sheet and informed consent form were reviewed by this group, and feedback informed changes made.

Study treatment: radiotherapy planning and delivery

Simulation and treatment

MRI simulation is performed per local protocol, either on a diagnostic MRI scanner and/or on the MR-linac, ideally using PIRADS v2.0 sequences. Either a planning CT or a pseudo-CT reconstruction from MRI can be used for reference planning and dose calculation.

Fiducials and rectal-prostate spacing gels are not permitted. Patient preparation, for example, drinking protocol or bowel preparation, will be in concordance with local protocol.21

Target volume delineation

The MRI-visible tumour(s) will be contoured using biparametric or multiparametric MRI to define the Gross Tumour Volume (GTV). A 4 mm margin is added to the GTV and cropped at the prostate capsule.

The Clinical Target Volume (CTV) encompasses the entire prostate gland. At the clinician’s discretion, part of the seminal vesicles may also be included (referred to as CTV-SV). In cases of T3a disease, a 3 mm extraprostatic margin is added around the GTV to account for extracapsular extension.

Organs at risk include the rectum, bladder, urethra, penile bulb, femoral heads and bowel, which are delineated according to the study protocol. Dose constraints are derived from the HERMES and 2SMART trials.22 23 Prospective quality assurance (QA) is required for all participating centres. Imaging, contouring and planning data is uploaded prior to the first fraction to the MOMENTUM database and automatically transferred to ProKnow (V.2.0.6.1. Elekta AB, Stockholm, Sweden) for review. Reviewers include the protocol authoring clinicians and responsible physicist. Based on the reviewer’s advice, the treating physician may choose to modify contours and plan. ‘Isotoxic’ planning in this protocol refers to the individualisation of each plan so that all mandatory dose constraints for organs at risk are strictly respected. Inhomogeneity within the CTV up to 130% of prescribed dose is permitted, provided OAR constraints are satisfied.

Workflow

Treatment is delivered using intensity modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) on the Unity MR-linac (Unity, Elekta AB, Stockholm, Sweden) with a minimum of ten fields, planned in the Elekta Unity Monaco treatment planning system (TPS) (Elekta AB, Stockholm, Sweden). Each treatment session begins with the patient positioned and a daily high-resolution MRI acquired to visualise the prostate, surrounding organs at risk, and any anatomic changes compared with the reference plan. Using the adapt-to-shape (ATS) workflow, all relevant target and organ-at-risk structures are reviewed and, if necessary, recontoured based on the ‘session’ MRI. The treatment plan is then fully reoptimised online for that day’s anatomy, ensuring target coverage and compliance with organ-at-risk dose constraints.24

Before treatment delivery, a verification MRI may be performed to confirm anatomy remains consistent. Positional corrections are applied as needed prior to beam-on. During irradiation, real-time motion management is employed using continuous cine MRI for position tracking of the prostate and critical structures. The system will automatically pause (‘beam gating’) if anatomy exceeds predefined tolerance envelopes, and adaptive intrafraction dose shifting may be applied if the prostate fails to return to baseline position.

Endpoints

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is physician-reported CTCAE v5 acute grade 2+ (G2+) GU AE within 12 weeks from completion of radiotherapy, with the objective of establishing the absolute and relative risk difference between the two treatment arms. Primary endpoint analysis will take place when all patients have completed the acute AE follow-up period, and all the data has been collected and cleaned.

Secondary endpoints

Secondary endpoints include acute GI AE (CTCAE v5); late GU, GI and sexual AE (CTCAE v5) at 6, 12 and 24 months; patient-reported outcomes (EPIC-26, IPSS, IIEF-5); treatment feasibility (defined as fraction interruptions); dosimetric comparisons; and biochemical relapse-free survival at 2 years. Statistical considerations

Sample size

DESTINATION 2 will enrol 200 patients worldwide (100 per arm), with recruitment at each centre (or within each country) capped at a maximum of 54 participants (27 per arm). This total was chosen so that, under an assumed baseline rate of approximately 30% grade 2+acute GU AE in the uniform-dose arm and the priors described below, the posterior credible intervals around the absolute and relative risk differences are sufficiently narrow that achieving both (a) >95% probability of any reduction and (b) >50% probability of at least a 25% reduction in acute GU AE would represent a robust signal to justify progression to phase III testing. To estimate the absolute risk (ARD) and relative risk (RR) difference between the two arms, a Bayesian log-binomial model will be used. In Arm 1, the prior set to approximately 30% for G2+GU AE with a wide range to reflect heterogeneity in previous studies.17 22 25

To account for varied expectations about the impact of dose de-escalation, three priors are specified for the relative risk (RR) of GU AE:

Neutral prior: Assumes both groups have equal risk (RR=1, 95% CI 0.38 to 2.67).

The de-escalated arm will only advance to further study if results show both a) a>95% probability of any reduction in acute GU grade 2+AE and b) a >50% probability of a clinically relevant reduction (at least 25%).

For safety, early stopping is implemented using a Bayesian beta-binomial model: if interim analysis after every set of nine patients indicates >50% probability that grade 3+GU AE exceeds 10%, the trial will halt enrolment in that arm.

The secondary endpoints of late GU and GI AE at 6, 12 and 24 months, patient-reported outcomes, and dosimetric data will be analysed descriptively and using Bayesian hierarchical and time-to-event models. Missing data will be imputed with random-forest MICE, generating 20 datasets to model non-linear relationships.

Posterior distributions are estimated using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampling, with convergence verified by Rhat statistics and visual assessment of density and trace plots. Regular global review meetings share results across the federated trial network, and pooled analysis in the MOMENTUM database supports robust selection of optimal dose strategies for phase III evaluation.

Ethics and dissemination

The trial complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and all local/international guidelines. Under the federated trial model, each participating centre retains independent responsibility for ethics approval, regulatory compliance, and governance in accordance with their national and institutional frameworks. Centres cannot commence recruitment until local ethics approval is in place. At the time of manuscript submission, DESTINATION 2 had been activated at three centres across three countries, all of which had obtained local research ethics committee/IRB approval and sponsor sign-off. The UK coordinating centre has received approval from the East of England–Cambridge South Research Ethics Committee (REC reference: 24/EE/0163; IRAS: 338368), with The Royal Marsden NHS Foundation Trust as sponsor. The Antoni van Leeuwenhoek/Nederlands Kanker Instituut (Amsterdam, Netherlands) received approval from the Medical Research Ethics Committee NedMec (METC No. 25-173/G; NL-009607; approved 5 September 2025) in accordance with the Dutch Medical Research Involving Human Subjects Act (WMO). Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre (Toronto, Canada) received approval from its institutional Research Ethics Board (REB Project No. 6617; approved 7 April 2025). Recruitment began in spring 2025, over a targeted 2-year period. Results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and conferences, with lay summaries online. This manuscript represents the single protocol publication for DESTINATION 2 across all participating centres; we do not plan separate site-specific protocol papers describing the same trial design with minor local variations.

Safety reporting

Data are collected at each trial visit regarding any adverse events graded according to CTCAE v5. The highest grade observed since the last visit will be reported. All serious adverse events (SAEs) occurring from the start of radiotherapy up to 30 days following the last fraction and any radiotherapy-related >grade 3 events during trial follow-up are reported to sponsoring site within 24 hours of the PI becoming aware of the event. SAEs should be followed up until clinical recovery is complete or until the condition has stabilised. Any safety concerns will be reported to the main research and ethics committee by the sponsoring site and to the global DESTINATION 2 collaborative group.

Trial monitoring and oversight

A country-specific Trial Management Group (TMG), including the Chief Investigator, Lead Co-investigators, lead radiographer, lead physicist, research fellows and trial coordinator will meet regularly to oversee safe conduct of the trial. Notwithstanding the legal obligations of the Sponsor and CI, the TMG have operational responsibility for the conduct of the trial as described in their terms of reference, roles and responsibilities outlined in the TMG charter.

Each sponsor will be responsible for setting up a data monitoring committee to follow relevant national and local regulations. In the UK an Independent Trial Steering Committee (TSC) includes a chairperson not directly involved in the trial and at least two other independent members who will oversee the integrity of trial data. The TSC will meet at least annually but additionally to review the grade 3 interim safety endpoints of the study. The trial is part of the MR-linac Consortium Hypothesis Testing Program (HTP).

Data statement

Pseudonymised data will be stored in the MOMENTUM registry to facilitate collaborative data analysis, in line with the statistical objectives outlined above. The data sets generated and/or analysed during the current study are not publicly available due to the protection of individual patient privacy.

Discussion

The innovative DESTINATION 2 trial pushes boundaries in prostate cancer radiotherapy by combining MRIgRT with dose de-escalation to non-tumourous prostate tissue, delivered within an ultra-hypofractionated two-fraction schedule. To our knowledge, this is the first randomised study to test such an approach for localised prostate cancer, marking a significant advance in the pursuit of more personalised, convenient and toxicity-sparing radiotherapy.

In parallel with convenience, delivering radiation doses in a smaller number of fractions with SBRT leverages prostate cancer’s low estimated alpha/beta (α/β) ratio.6 26 The α/β ratio of prostate cancer is estimated to be as low as 1.5 Gy, whereas nearby late responding normal tissue, such as the rectum, is estimated to have an α/β ratio of 3–5 Gy.27 28 This suggests that hypofractionation could enhance tumour control without increasing bowel AE or even potentially reduce late effects compared with conventional fractionation. However, there is some evidence to suggest the α/β ratio of the genitourinary structures causing side effects may be as low as prostate cancer.6 Consistent with this hypothesis, SBRT has been shown to cause higher moderate genitourinary side effects at 1–2 years after treatment.25 This radiobiological insight underpins the DESTINATION 2 strategy: selectively de-escalating dose to uninvolved prostate tissue while maintaining tumouricidal doses to visible tumour(s) may preserve tumour control benefits of hypofractionation while reducing exposure of GU structures.

Evidence gleaned from the high dose rate (HDR) brachytherapy sphere lends further support to the safety and efficacy of using large doses of radiation in few fractions. Regimens such as 27 Gy in two fractions or 34.5 Gy in three fractions have shown good long-term biochemical control and favourable AE profiles.29 30 Taken together, these findings have driven forward clinical trials exploring ever-shorter treatment regimens.223133

Imaging and real-time adaptive radiotherapy have ushered in a new era of precision for prostate cancer treatment. MRIgRT allows for daily online plan adaptation based on real-time anatomy, which minimises geometric and set-up uncertainties and has the potential to lower AE rates by reducing dose to organs at risk.34 35 MR-linac technology challenges the historical expansion from clinical target volume (CTV) to planning target volume (PTV) that is characteristic of non-adaptive external beam planning. The ability to visualise anatomy during treatment, together with continuous intra-fraction motion monitoring using cine MRI, has facilitated abolition of the PTV margin in the DESTINATION 2 protocol. Researchers have shown margin abolition results in significant dose reduction to OARs.36

Our approach employs a 0 mm PTV margin and accepts the possibility of minor underdosage at the CTV periphery. This is justified both technically and biologically. Pathological studies have demonstrated that tumour cells are not homogeneously distributed throughout the prostate CTV. The risk of missing clinically significant tumour is very low because small, imaging-undetectable lesions constitute only a minor proportion of the total intraprostatic tumour burden. In 61 prostatectomy specimens, the median tumour foci was 3, with the index lesion contributing 88% of total tumour volume.11 A similar study reported non-index satellites lesions <0.5 cc contributed 5% of total tumour volume in 25 consecutive patients.10 In a similar vein, evidence indicates areas of the prostate classified as having low PI-RADS scores on multiparametric MRI seldom harbour clinically significant cancer.37 This spatial tumour gradient underpins our rationale for delivering a higher dose to the MRI-visible tumour with a 4 mm margin (GTV+4 mm) and a comparatively reduced dose to the remainder of the CTV. The use of a 4 mm margin for GTV is intended to balance coverage of microscopic extension with avoidance of overtreatment. In other tumour types, treatment of potential microscopic disease typically employs lower doses than doses used for definitive macroscopic tumour control and there is interest in de-escalation studies to redress the balance between oncologic outcomes and long-term quality of life and survivorship.38

Finally, evidence from the randomised HERMES trial confirmed not only the technical feasibility but also the safety of delivering MRIgRT in as few as two fractions, with acute GU and GI AE rates remaining low and comparable between the two or five fraction arms.39

We accept limitations of the trial; particularly its reliance on a highly specialised delivery platform for the treatment of localised prostate cancer only, which limits the generalisability of results. Nevertheless, we anticipate that these findings will inform a future phase III trial with broader applicability and relevance to informing clinical practice.

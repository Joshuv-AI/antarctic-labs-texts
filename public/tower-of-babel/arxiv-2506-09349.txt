OmniDRCA: Parallel Speech-Text Foundation Model via Dual-Resolution Speech Representations and Contrastive Alignment Thanks: School of Computer Science, Fudan University. Work done during internship in Tongyi Lab.
Chao-Hong Tan, Qian Chen, Wen Wang, Chong Deng, Qinglin Zhang

Abstract
Recent studies on end-to-end speech generation with large language models (LLMs) have attracted significant community attention, with multiple works extending text-based LLMs to generate discrete speech tokens. Existing approaches primarily fall into two categories: (1) Methods that generate discrete speech tokens independently without incorporating them into the LLM’s autoregressive process, resulting in text generation being unaware of concurrent speech synthesis. (2) Models that generate interleaved or parallel speech-text tokens through joint autoregressive modeling , enabling mutual modality awareness during generation. This paper presents OmniDRCA , a parallel speech-text foundation model based on joint autoregressive modeling, featuring dual-resolution speech representations and contrastive cross-modal alignment . Our approach processes speech and text representations in parallel while enhancing audio comprehension through contrastive alignment. Experimental results on Spoken Question Answering benchmarks demonstrate that OmniDRCA establishes new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance among parallel joint speech-text modeling based foundation models, and achieves competitive performance compared to interleaved models. Additionally, we explore the potential of extending the framework to full-duplex conversational scenarios. 1 1 1 Our implementation will be released at https://github.com/lxchtan/OmniDRCA .



1
Introduction

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs)
(
OpenAI 2023
;
Yang et al. 2024
)
have demonstrated remarkable performance across various NLP tasks, sparking growing interest in extending their capabilities to multimodal domains encompassing vision and audio. Developments in spoken dialogue systems are critical to human-computer interaction, as natural human communication inherently relies on verbal exchanges. The release of GPT-4o
(
OpenAI 2024b
)
is a significant milestone in this field, showing the potential of LLM-based spoken dialogue systems for seamless and natural interactions with users.

LLM-based spoken dialogue systems can be generally categorized into
cascaded systems
and
end-to-end (E2E) systems
, with the distinction lying in whether the backbone LLM can directly comprehend speech representations and generate speech outputs.
Early cascaded systems integrate separately trained Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), LLM, and Text-to-Speech (TTS) modules. Such systems inherently suffer from error accumulation, loss of acoustic details (e.g., tone, emotion), and significant latency. Alternatively, the ASR module could be eliminated by introducing audio understanding foundation models
(
Chu et al. 2023
;
Chu et al. 2024
)
. E2E systems have emerged to further eliminate ASR and TTS modules, and establish direct connections between speech representations and LLMs.
However, training LLMs to generate highly intelligent speech outputs remains challenging. E2E systems struggle with the quality of speech token generation, primarily due to inefficient utilization of textual information during speech token generation.

Recent research efforts on E2E models to address the above challenges have focused on two primary directions:
Text-Driven Speech Models

(
Yao et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2025
)
and
Joint Speech-Text Models
.
Text-Driven Speech Models refer to systems in which LLMs ingest speech representations as input, produce textual responses, and simultaneously utilize the LLM’s latent representations to synthesize audio. In contrast, Joint Speech-Text Models involve LLMs taking speech representations as input and
autoregressively generating both text tokens and speech tokens
. The key distinction lies in whether the generated speech tokens are fed back autoregressively into the LLM during the generation process.
Joint Speech-Text Models can be further categorized into
interleaved speech-text modeling

(
Zeng et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2024a
)
and
parallel speech-text modeling

(
Défossez et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024
)
. Interleaved speech-text modeling alternates speech and text representations as inputs to the LLM, while parallel speech-text modeling fuses speech and text representations before feeding them into the LLM, ensuring temporal alignment.
While both methodologies explicitly incorporate textual guidance into speech token generation to leverage the LLM’s capabilities, they have distinct limitations. Text-Driven Speech Models prevent LLMs from perceiving generated speech tokens during inference, thereby limiting dynamic adjustment of text generation. Joint Speech-Text Models degrade the original text generation capabilities due to speech token interference, making the preservation of text capabilities a critical challenge. Nevertheless, Joint Speech-Text Models enforce multimodal interaction and generation and empower greater potentials; hence, in this work, we focus on enhancing joint speech-text modeling.

In this work, we propose
OmniDRCA
, a novel parallel speech-text foundation model. Our architecture preserves end-to-end advantages while significantly improving speech-text semantic alignment through two key innovations:
1)
Dual-Resolution Speech Representations (DRSR):
DRSR is proposed mainly to alleviate the temporal resolution discrepancy between speech and text tokens. For the LLM’s speech comprehension, we introduce a
grouping
mechanism, mapping 25Hz discrete audio tokens to 5Hz speech representations that align much better with textual granularity, thereby leveraging LLM’s capabilities more effectively and improving speech understanding. Combined speech-text embeddings form the assistant’s autoregressive input. The multimodal LLM (MLLM) predicts text tokens. For speech generation, latent representations are
ungrouped
and autoregressively processed by the speech refinement model (SRM) for
individual
speech token prediction. During inference, due to input-output resolution discrepancies, each MLLM inference triggers
k
parallel SRM executions, where
k
is the grouping factor.
2)
Contrastive Cross-modal Alignment (CCA):
Similarity between grouped speech representations and text representations is computed to optimize their mutual distances. While grouping aligns speech-text granularity, CCA further enhances semantic consistency between modalities before they enter the LLM and further enhances speech comprehension.
Considering inference efficiency for varying output requirements in practical applications, we also design system prompts to control the output modes, including text-only output, parallel speech-text output, and parallel speech-text output after
chain-of-modality (CoM)

(
Zhang et al. 2023a
)
. Experimental results demonstrate the efficacy of CoM in improving performance.
For reproducibility, we will release all source code and model checkpoints.

Our contributions are three-fold: 1) We propose
OmniDRCA
, a novel parallel speech-text foundation model that enhances speech-to-speech generation based on discrete speech tokens and LLMs, through dual-resolution speech representations and contrastive cross-modal alignment. 2) Comprehensive experiments demonstrate
OmniDRCA
’s superiority over the state-of-the-art (SOTA) parallel model LUCY
(
Gao et al. 2025
)
and competitive performance against SOTA interleaved models across multiple spoken question answering benchmarks. 3) We further explore the potential of OmniDRCA for full-duplex voice conversation. By leveraging time-division multiplexing, OmniDRCA-Duplex supports real-time responses to user interruptions with minimal performance degradation.

2
Related Work

Speech Tokenization.

Two primary directions exist for converting continuous speech signal into processable sequences: continuous representations, e.g., Whisper
(
Radford et al. 2022
)
, and discrete representations.
Since LLMs generate discrete tokens, continuous representations face challenges in direct integration with LLMs for speech generation. In contrast, discrete tokens enable LLMs to handle speech similar to text tokens, and are typically categorized into two categories:
1) acoustic tokens optimized for reconstructing high-quality audio through neural audio codecs
(
Défossez et al. 2023
)
, and 2) semantic tokens typically derived from
self-supervised
pre-trained models with masked language modeling as training objective
(
Hassid et al. 2023
)
or supervised learning as in CosyVoice
(
Du et al. 2024a
;
Du et al. 2024b
)
, and prioritized for linguistic content
(
Hsu et al. 2021
)
.
While acoustic tokens achieve superior acoustic fidelity in sound reconstruction, semantic tokens demonstrate stronger alignment with semantic representations
(
Zhang et al. 2023b
)
, thereby facilitating more effective extension of MLLMs’ capabilities in both speech comprehension and generation
(
Zeng et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2024b
;
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Borsos et al. 2023
)
.
In this work, we select HuBERT-derived semantic tokens
(
Hassid et al. 2023
)
over other paradigms due to its competitive semantic capabilities and support for streamlined streaming speech generation.

E2E Speech Foundation Models.

Existing E2E speech foundation models can be categorized into Text-Driven Speech Models and Joint Speech-Text Models.
Text-Driven Speech Models
(
Fang et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Fu et al. 2025
;
Yao et al. 2024
;
Huang et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
)
integrate speech encoder, adaptor, LLM, and a streaming speech decoder, and can simultaneously generate text and speech.
The contemporaneous Text-Driven model Qwen2.5-Omni
(
Xu et al. 2025
)
employs the Thinker-Talker architecture, with Thinker handling multimodal understanding and text generation and Talker specializing in speech token production.
Since Thinker cannot receive speech tokens during generation, the framework inherently limits awareness of speech token generation states, constraining applications such as full-duplex voice conversation.

Joint Speech-Text Models explore two different architectures,
Interleaved
and
Parallel
. Interleaved models
(
Zhang et al. 2024a
;
Zeng et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
)
adopt interleaved decoding to support simultaneous generation of speech and text tokens, while
parallel models
(
Défossez et al. 2024
;
Xie and Wu 2024a
;
Xie and Wu 2024b
;
Chen et al. 2024
)
conduct parallel decoding.
The parallel model Moshi
(
Défossez et al. 2024
)
employs a compact Deep Transformer to predict
k
tokens while relying solely on current-step representations. Our approach differs from Moshi with two key distinctions: (1) the
k
tokens correspond to grouped semantic speech tokens rather than disparate acoustic tokens, and (2) our speech refinement module explicitly ingests full-sequence contextual information to enhance semantic coherence.
While SLAM-Omni
(
Chen et al. 2024
)
proposes grouped semantic token prediction for speech generation, our approach designs an ungrouping mechanism and incorporates a Speech Refined Model for autoregressive generation of individual speech tokens. Another contemporaneous parallel model Kimi-Audio
(
KimiTeam et al. 2025
)
is closest to our approach. To mitigate limitations of discrete tokens in audio understanding, they introduce dual-tokenizer combining discrete semantic tokens with continuous Whisper features, preserving both semantic and acoustic information. In contrast, our approach enhances discrete token comprehension through
contrastive loss
and
grouping strategies
, thereby improving LLM’s understanding of speech tokens while maintaining representational efficiency.

Contrastive Cross-Modal Alignment.

CLIP
(
Radford et al. 2021
)
aligns image and text representations through contrastive pretraining. VILA-U
(
Wu et al. 2024
)
integrates contrastive loss and reconstruction loss during vision token pretraining to enhance feature alignment. In speech research,
Ye et al. 2022
leverages multi-class N-pair contrastive
loss
(
Sohn 2016
)
for speech translation.
Zheng et al. 2024
leverages InfoNCE loss
(
He et al. 2020
)
for spoken language understanding. We incorporate contrastive loss to strengthen speech-text semantic alignment. Crucially, we adopt gradient stop
(
Chen and He 2021
)
on text embeddings during contrastive loss computation – this modification preserves speech representation learning while preventing text feature distortion.

3
Methodology

Figure
1
illustrates the architecture of
OmniDRCA
. The system consists of two main components: (1) a
Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)
designed to process
interleaved speech-text inputs
and achieve cross-modal alignment, and (2) a
Speech Refined Model (SRM)
specialized in high-fidelity speech synthesis.
The system operates through multimodal input encoding, joint representation learning with contrastive alignment, and coordinated speech-text output generation.
During inference, user inputs (text or speech) are first mapped to a unified semantic space, processed by MLLM to produce multimodal context embeddings, and decoded into
synchronized
speech-text responses through SRM and the language model head.

3.1
Speech Tokenization and Detokenization

Semantic tokens have been widely used for speech tokenization
(
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Borsos et al. 2023
)
, due to their strong alignment with text
(
Zhang et al. 2023b
)
; therefore, we employ HuBERT
(
Hsu et al. 2021
)
as the speech tokenizer to convert speech waveform to semantic speech token sequence
\mathbf{S}=[s_{0},s_{1},\cdots,s_{T-1}]
, where
T
is the speech token sequence length.
For speech detokenization, HiFi-GAN
(
Kong et al. 2020
)
is selected to regenerate speech waveform from speech tokens
\mathbf{S}
, since it is a non-autoregressive neural vocoder that efficiently generates high-fidelity waveforms and reduces the overall latency.

Figure 1:
Overview of
OmniDRCA
. User speech inputs are tokenized,
grouped
, and encoded by the MLLM for autoregressive text token prediction. Hidden representations are
ungrouped
and processed by the SRM through
k
autoregressive forward passes to generate
k
speech tokens, where
k
is the grouping factor. The generated speech tokens are then converted to speech waveform by the speech detokenizer. We omit speech tokenizer and detokenizer for simplicity.

3.2
Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)

Built upon text-LLMs, the MLLM learns
joint speech-text modeling
to process speech or text inputs while producing
parallel
speech and text outputs.

Parallel Joint Speech-Text Model.

Leveraging the autoregressive generation capability of MLLMs, both generated speech tokens
s_{t}
and text tokens
t_{t}
are iteratively fed back into the MLLM at each timestep.
Their embeddings are added as model input, forming a parallel speech-text architecture. Formally, the combined input embedding
c_{t}
at timestep
t
is computed as:

c_{t}=E_{\text{speech}}(s_{t})+E_{\text{text}}(t_{t})

(1)

where
E_{\text{speech}}
and
E_{\text{text}}
denote the embeddings of speech and text tokens, respectively. The autoregressive generation process is as follows:

P(y_{t}|y_{<t},x)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}P(y_{i}|y_{<i},x)

(2)

where
x
is the input sequence and
y_{t}=(s_{t},t_{t})
denotes the joint speech-text output at timestep
t
. This unified formulation enables seamless integration of speech and text generation within a
single
autoregressive framework.
To preserve the intrinsic linguistic understanding and generation capabilities of pretrained text-LLMs while enabling cross-modal interactions, three key methodological components are introduced for intermodal coordination.

Inner Text Streaming.

Inspired by Moshi
(
Défossez et al. 2024
)
, explicit text streaming is incorporated to aid speech generation as a common semantic scaffold. We focus on
performing modality alignment exclusively at the assistant end
. This design adheres to the
asymmetric
nature of human-machine interactions: while user inputs are typically unimodal (either text or speech), the assistant’s responses can be a coordinated multimodal output.

Speech Token Grouping.
A crucial parameter for discrete tokens is sampling rate, which determines the input/output sequence length. GLM-4-Voice
(
Zeng et al. 2025
)
investigates sampling rates from 6.25Hz to 50Hz on LibriSpeech, revealing minimal differences in Word Error Rate (WER) between 50Hz and 25Hz but significant degradation at 12.5Hz and 6.25Hz. Hence, our work adopts 25Hz sampling rate. To resolve the temporal resolution mismatch between speech signals (25Hz) and text tokenization (
\sim
3Hz)
(
Chen et al. 2024
)
, a
grouping
mechanism is designed:

\mathbf{g}_{i}=\text{Linear}\left(\mathop{\|}_{j=ik}^{(i+1)k-1}\mathbf{s}_{j}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{text}}}

(3)

where
\mathbf{s}_{j}
denotes speech tokens,
\|
represents feature concatenation, and
k
is the grouping factor determined by the ratio between speech token rates and text token rates. This design compresses the speech token length from
T
to
T/k
and the grouped speech representations align better with text.
Different from
Chen et al. 2024
that employs linear projection of audio logits into group-sized representations for
parallel multi-token prediction
,
OmniDRCA
strategically designs an
ungrouping
mechanism and incorporates a dedicated Speech Refined Model (SRM) (Section
3.3
) to enable autoregressive generation of
individual
speech tokens.

Cross-modal Contrastive Alignment.

To enable cross-modal alignment, a contrastive learning objective aligns grouped speech representations with their textual counterparts by maximizing their cosine similarity and computing a contrastive loss. This joint optimization ensures coordinated positioning of both modalities within the shared representation space.

\mathcal{L}_{\text{cont}}=-\cos(E_{\text{text}}(\mathbf{t}),E_{\text{speech}}(\mathbf{g})),

(4)

where
E_{\text{text}}
and
E_{\text{speech}}
are the mean of embeddings of text tokens and grouped speech tokens between sample pairs, respectively.
Different from early applications of contrastive alignment between speech and text
(
Zheng et al. 2024
)
, our approach eliminates negative sample computation and prevents mode collapse through gradient stop on the text branch.

3.3
Speech Refined Model (SRM)

SRM is proposed to enhance speech generation capabilities, which utilizes the last hidden state of the MLLM as conditional input and incorporates contextual speech information to autoregressively generate speech tokens. While conventional speech grouping strategies–which cluster speech tokens into semantically meaningful units–have proven effective for speech recognition and understanding tasks
(
Chen et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2024b
)
, our experiments reveal their inherent limitations in generative scenarios, since speech token grouping inevitably loses some fine-grained acoustic details.
To address this limitation, an
ungrouping
process is performed as follows: the MLLM’s final hidden state is mapped to group-sized embeddings via linear projection

\mathbf{h}_{ug}=\mathbf{W}_{p}\mathbf{h}_{L}^{\text{[MLLM]}}\quad\text{where}\quad\mathbf{W}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{g}\times d_{h}},

(5)

and time splitting

\mathbf{H}=\text{Split}_{k}(\mathbf{h}_{ug})=[\mathbf{h}_{ug}^{(1)},\mathbf{h}_{ug}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathbf{h}_{ug}^{(k)}]\quad\text{where}\quad\mathbf{h}_{ug}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{ug}/k},

(6)

the resulting
\mathbf{H}
serves as conditional input for SRM that autoregressively generates speech tokens.
Following our practice on MLLM, representations of preceding speech tokens and
\mathbf{H}
are aggregated.
SRM is trained to maximize the conditional probability:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{SRM}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}\log P(s_{i}|s_{<i},\mathbf{H}),

(7)

where
s_{i}
represents the
i
-th speech token. By optimizing this objective, SRM learns to predict subsequent speech tokens conditioned on both preceding speech tokens and the rich contextual embeddings
\mathbf{H}
derived from MLLM. This design enables SRM to effectively leverage the semantic and acoustic information encoded in the hidden representations of MLLM, thereby producing more natural and coherent speech outputs compared to conventional grouping-based approaches.

The end-to-end training objective integrates the three losses through multi-task learning paradigm:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}=\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}+\mu\mathcal{L}_{\text{cont}}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{\text{SRM}},

(8)

\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}\log P(t_{i}|t_{<i},\mathbf{g}),

(9)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}}
is the autoregressive loss over MLLM,
\lambda
,
\mu
, and
\gamma
are hyperparameters.

Experimental Setup

Datasets.

Approximately 210K hours of audio-text paired data for speech-text modality synchronization is adopted for SRM pre-training.
17.8K hours of English speech conversations data synthesized from text are adopted for
OmniDRCA
training:
(1) VoiceAssistant
(
Xie and Wu 2024a
;
Xie and Wu 2024b
)
, which contains human-agent interactions;
(2) Spoken Alpaca
2
2

2

https://huggingface.co/datasets/GSQA/spoken-alpaca-gpt4
, which provides instructed dialogues; and
(3) Synthesized Datasets, which are synthesized from text datasets such as Infinity-Instruct
(
BAAI 2024
)
, using the CosyVoice TTS system

Results and Discussions

Tables

compare

with the representative and competitive baselines across all major model categories, on speech-to-speech and speech-to-text generation performance. Table

shows that in prior works, best Text-Driven models substantially outperform top-tier Joint Speech-Text models, because text-driven systems do not allow speech tokens to interfere with the generation process, thereby minimally impacting the LLM’s generation paradigm. Moreover, prior interleaved models often outperform parallel models, though they exhibit higher latency (e.g., 13 text tokens for first speech in GLM-4-Voice) and lack temporal alignment, complicating multistream duplex modeling.
As shown in Tables
2
and
3
,
OmniDRCA
achieves SOTA performance under the parallel paradigm
, achieving a substantial
24.9% relative gain over LUCY (S2) on the average score across three Speech-to-Speech QA benchmarks and 60.4% gain over Moshi across three Speech-to-Text QA benchmarks, excluding AlpacaEval in both cases.

OmniDRCA
also shows
competitive performance against the interleaved GLM-4-Voice and Baichuan-Omni-1.5
. However, a relatively large gap still remains when compared to text-driven systems. In future work, we plan to train the model on more extensive datasets to improve the performance.

The quality of generated speech is evaluated in terms of overall speech quality (UTMOS) and alignment between generated speech and text (WER(%)), as shown in Table
4
.
Among models with comparable sizes, text-driven models tend to exhibit higher WER, while joint speech-text models achieve relatively lower WER. The model size also significantly impacts WER.
OmniDRCA
achieves the lowest WER, indicating strong alignment when generating both speech and text simultaneously
.
In contrast, UTMOS scores are more constrained by the speech tokenizer and detokenizer
(
Hassid et al. 2023
;
Du et al. 2024a
)
. The gap in UTMOS between
OmniDRCA
and GLM-4-Voice suggests room for improvement in modeling prosody and preserving acoustic details.

We conduct extensive ablation study to analyze effects of critical algorithmic designs.

Dual-Resolution Grouping Strategy.

Table
5
reveals the dual effects of our grouping strategy: while improving speech understanding (12.6% relative gain in S2T with group size 5) and text capabilities (13.6% relative gain in T2T), it degrades speech generation quality by 38.2% relative in T2M.
On the other hand, as shown in Table
6
, SRM demonstrates remarkable effectiveness in speech token generation, achieving 245.9% and 71.4% relative improvements in S2M and T2M tasks respectively. Notably, SRM also enhances speech comprehension metrics (S2T and STC-T) by 10-16% relative, suggesting that improved speech token reconstruction capability improves latent speech representations. The text-only performance (T2T) remains stable, confirming SRM’s modality-specific effectiveness without interfering with text processing pathways.
Our dual-resolution architecture resolves this conflict by applying grouping only in the comprehension phase (5Hz) while using raw frame resolution (25Hz) with SRM for generation.

Contrastive Cross-modal Alignment.

As shown in Table
6
, the introduction of speech-text contrastive learning for sentence-level semantic alignment substantially enhances the model’s semantic understanding capabilities. While the final evaluation metric (S2S performance) shows improvement over the counterpart without contrastive loss, the enhancement exhibits modality-specific efficacy. For instance, S2T performance improves by 2.34% absolute (64.67 over 62.33), while T2T performance degrades by 4.33%, suggesting that while improving speech comprehension, the contrastive objective partially compromises the LLM’s native text understanding capacity. Our multimodal evaluation framework effectively reveals these nuanced impacts, guiding future work on mitigating text performance degradation through more balanced loss design.

Multimodal Architecture Design (MD).

Our multimodal design achieves two key goals: (1) context-aware modality selection and (2) implicit data augmentation.
As shown in Table
6
, on the speech generation task, STC/SAC/SUC demonstrate substantial improvements over S2M. STC achieves 61.00, substantially surpassing the direct generation baseline of 55.33. This reveals the critical role of CoM while also indicating potential optimization opportunities for our S2M model. Without incorporating the three CoM variants (STC/SAC/SUC), the model fails to adopt this generation paradigm guided by system prompts. Final S2M performance shows that training without MD results in a 7.5% performance degradation, confirming the data augmentation effect of our approach.

Wang et al. 2025

The resulting

-Duplex
achieves full-duplex dialogue capabilities.

-Duplex are detailed in Appendix.

5
Conclusions, Limitations, and Future Work

We introduce
OmniDRCA
, a novel parallel speech-text foundation model that integrates joint autoregressive modeling with dual-resolution speech representations and contrastive cross-modal alignment. Experiments show that
OmniDRCA
sets new SOTA speech-to-speech/text generation performance among parallel models and reaches competitive performance to interleaved models.

Limitations:
(1)
OmniDRCA
’s speech generation quality remains constrained by suboptimal speech tokenization. We will explore other tokenization methods, such as CosyVoice’s speech tokenizer, to address this limitation. (2) We will extend exploration from current speech understanding and generation tasks to broader audio comprehension/synthesis tasks, and integrate visual modality.

Appendix A
OmniDRCA-Duplex

As mentioned in Introduction, we explore the potential of OmniDRCA for full-duplex voice conversation. By leveraging time-division multiplexing, we introduce a novel spoken dialogue model,
OmniDRCA-Duplex
, which supports real-time responses to user interruptions with minimal performance degradation. In this section, we first introduce OmniDRCA-Duplex in Appendix
A.1
, then present the experimental results and discussions of its full-duplex conversation capabilities in Appendix
A.2
.

A.1
Methodology

As shown in Figure
2
, compared with
OmniDRCA
, the overall architecture of
OmniDRCA
-Duplex remains largely unchanged, which is also composed of the multimodal LLM (MLLM) and the speech refinement model (SRM).
The primary differences between
OmniDRCA
-Duplex and the original
OmniDRCA
model lie in two aspects. The first aspect is the organization of the input structure.
OmniDRCA
-Duplex organizes user speech and assistant speech
alternately
along the temporal axis as input, referred to as the
Time-Division Multiplexing Input Stream
, while
OmniDRCA
adopts turn-based speech as the same as text-based LLM.
For the second aspect, to enhance the model’s sensitivity to user interruptions, an additional assistant state prediction head is introduced in
OmniDRCA
-Duplex to determine whether muting should be activated.
The following paragraphs provide detailed descriptions of these two designs.

Figure 2:
The architecture of
OmniDRCA
-Duplex.

Time-Division Multiplexing Input Stream.

Full-duplex spoken dialogue models allow simultaneous receptions of inputs from both user and assistant.
Inspired by
Wang et al. 2025
,
OmniDRCA
-Duplex organizes inputs into a
double-channel format
, specifically, an alternating sequence of user speech and assistant speech along the temporal axis.
Besides, same position id is used in one time slot, which paired user and assistant speech representations together.
The model enforces attention masking between concurrent speech representations in individual time slots since they appear simultaneously.
This structured arrangement, termed
time-division multiplexing (TDM) input stream
, is visually illustrated in Figure
2

TDM-Duplex
.
This approach allows the model to accept user speech when the assistant is generating speech, effectively utilizing the time slots that would otherwise be idle.
The TDM input stream is designed to handle both user and assistant speech inputs, ensuring a seamless interaction experience.

Awareness of Interruptions.

As our early experiments show, the model may not be aware of user interruptions during the generation of assistant speech, since the model prefers to generate speech based on the content in the time slots from its own role (that is, Assistant Speech Token Stream).
This can lead to a lack of responsiveness to user interruptions and a less engaging interaction experience. To address this issue, we implement a mechanism that enhances the model’s awareness to user interruptions during assistant speech generation.
We achieve this goal by
adding an additional head to predict the assistant’s speech generation state
. The model is trained to predict whether the assistant is currently generating speech or not, based on the input speech tokens. This additional head provides the model with contextual information about the assistant’s speech generation state, enabling it to respond more effectively to user interruptions.

A.2
Experiments

Experimental Setup.

The original speech datasets using for
OmniDRCA
post-training are edited to simulate full-duplex conversation scenarios. Two types of datasets are constructed:
1)
Non-interruption Dataset
: Dialogue turns are structured into dual-channel audio. To better simulate human interactions, a response time of
0\sim 400
ms is randomly inserted between dialogue turns. This dataset enables the model to learn when to initiate and terminate speech. 2)
Interruption Multi-Turn Dialogue Dataset
: The assistant’s responses in dialogue turns are randomly truncated, followed by inserting a new user input
, before constructing dual-channel datasets. Through this data simulation approach, the model learns to handle incomplete interrupted semantics.
In total, about 4K hours non-interruption dataset and 4K hours interruption multi-turn dialogue dataset are adopted for
OmniDRCA
-Duplex training.
UltraEval-Audio Benchmark is used for evaluating the semantic ability, while multi-turn Llama Questions randomly pairing two of questions together is used for evaluating the interruption ablitily.
Specific, providing these two rounds Llama Questions examples, if the model can mute itself while the user is speaking and respond after the user finishes, it is considered a successful Turn-taking. The testing evaluates the success rate of Turn-taking.

6

6

Here, Turn-taking@k Metrics is not conducted unlike other duplex speech models
(
Zhang et al. 2024a
)
.
This approach stems from intentional dataset construction aimed at endowing the model with human-like response timing and enabling scenarios where semantic information coexists simultaneously in both channels. The design specifically emphasizes making the model’s turn-taking response time more flexible rather than prioritizing the speed of turn-taking as the objective for the duplex model.

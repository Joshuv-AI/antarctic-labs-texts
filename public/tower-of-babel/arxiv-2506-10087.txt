Wave-front tracking for a quasi-linear scalar conservation law with hysteresis II: the case of Preisach
Fabio Bagagiolo, Stefan Moreti

Abstract
We consider the Cauchy problem for the quasi-linear scalar conservation law u t + ℱ ​ ( u ) t + u x = 0 , u_{t}+\mathcal{F}(u)_{t}+u_{x}=0, where ℱ \mathcal{F} is a specific hysteresis operator. Hysteresis models a rate-independent memory relationship between the input u u and its output, giving a non-local feature to the equation. In a previous work the authors studied the case when ℱ \mathcal{F} is the Play operator. In the present article, we extend the analysis to the case of Preisach operator, which is probably the most versatile mathematical model to describe hysteresis in the applications, especially for the presence of some kind of internal variables. This fact has required a new analysis of the equation. Starting from the Riemann problem, we address the so-called wave-front tracking method for a solution to the Cauchy problem with bounded variation initial data. An entropy-like condition is also exploited for uniqueness.



Introduction

In the recent paper
[
4
]
the authors study the Cauchy problem for a conservation law with hysteresis nonlinearities as follows:

\begin{cases}u_{t}+w_{t}+u_{x}=0&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}\times[0,T),\\
w(x,t)={\cal F}[u(x,\cdot),w^{0}(x)](t)&\quad\forall\ t\in[0,T),\ \text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R},\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x)&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1)

where:
\cal F
is a so-called hysteresis operator, namely representing a memory-dependent input-output relationship between the pair of scalar functions
(t\mapsto u(x,t),t\mapsto w(x,t))
, one for almost every
x
;
u_{0}
is the initial datum for the solution
u
;
w^{0}
is a suitable space-dependent function for the initial values of the output
w
. The memory dependence represented by
\cal F
is rate-independent, which is the main characterization of the hysteresis phenomena. The main goal was, starting from the Riemann problem, to give an existence and uniqueness result for (
1
) for general
BV
(bounded variation) initial data, by applying the so called wave-front tracking method.

In
[
4
]
the case where
\cal F
is the Play operator is considered. In the present paper we study the case where
\cal F
is the Preisach operator which is more versatile and useful to analytically represent many hysteresis phenomena from the applications.

As already pointed out in
[
4
]
, the presence of the hysteretic (memory) term in (
1
) gives a non-local feature to the PDE which requires to re-write the equation in the (more standard) form
u_{t}+f(u)u_{x}=0
, with
f=F^{\prime}
, for a suitable definition of a flow function
F
in order to encode the memory hysteretic effect (see (
4.5
)). The main novelties of this kind of problem are in the definition of the function
f
which also in general depends on the output
w
(which encodes the memory) and presents several non-standard singularities and discontinuities.

When the hysteresis relationship is represented by the Play operator, as in
[
4
]
, the function
f
above becomes of piecewise constant type which requires, for the Riemann problem, a fine analysis and combination of shocks. In the present paper, with the Preisach hysteresis,
f
turns out to be piecewise continuous and non-constant. This fact gives a big difference with respect to the previous case, because besides shocks one has the presence of several combinations of rarefaction waves, and this also reflects in the wave-front tracking procedure.

One of the main differences between Play and Preisach operators is that the second one takes account of the evolution of a family of so-called internal variables (which is a common feature of many real-world hysteresis phenomena). The evolution of such internal variables is encoded by the evolution of a switching function defined in a half plane (the Preisach plane) or equivalently by the evolution of a maximal antimonotone graph on that half plane viewed as the evolution in a suitable metric space. Hence, for the wave-front tracking procedure, in case of
BV
data, a suitable definition and analysis of BV functions with values in a metric space must be considered.

As a consequence of the above discussion, the construction of a solution via the wave-front tracking method in this work involves several non-standard difficulties:

i)

unlike in the classical scalar case and in the Play operator case studied in
[
4
]
, interactions between rarefaction waves are present in the wave-front tracking;

ii)

as in
[
4
]
, the function
u
solving the Cauchy problem belongs only to BV. Hence, we need a relaxation of the hysteresis relationship, rewriting it as a suitable measure-dependent variational integral inequality (adapting the one in Visintin
[
25
]
);

iii)

we are required to work in a suitable metric space of antimonotone graphs, which introduces additional difficulties in ensuring compactness and convergence of the wave-front tracking scheme and the relaxed hysteresis relationship.

Our main results are the existence of a solution of (
1
) and the uniqueness in the class of functions satisfying an entropy condition.

A similar equation to (
1
) but with a different hysteresis operator, namely the delayed Relay, was studied
[
26
]
by Visintin, where it is remarked that the results can be extended to the Preisach case. However, the approach followed there differs from that of
[
4
]
and from the one in the present article. Here, we adopt a more constructive approach focused on the study of characteristics and their interactions.

Preisach Operator

We now prove that for an input
u
piecewise constant the state
z
generated is in
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{L}
for some suitable
L
depending on the total variation of

. To do so, following
[
25

, we introduce the so called reduced memory sequence of
u
. Define

\begin{split}M:=\max_{[0,T]}u,&\quad m:=\min_{[0,T]}u,\\
\quad t_{M}:=\sup\{t\in[0,T]\,|\,u(t)=M\}\quad&\text{and}\quad t_{m}:=\sup\{t\in[0,T]\,|\,u(t)=m\}.\end{split}

Excluding the trivial case when
t_{m}=t_{M}
, suppose for example that
t_{M}<t_{m}\leq T
, the other case is quite similar. Then we set

t_{1}=t_{M},\quad\eta^{1}=M,\quad\eta_{2}:=\min_{[t_{1},T]}u\quad\text{and}\quad t_{2}:=\sup\{t\in]t_{1},T]\,|\,u(t)=\eta_{2}\},

and if

\eta^{3}:=\max_{[t_{2},T]}u\quad\text{and}\quad t_{3}:=\sup\{t\in]t_{2},T]\,|\,u(t)=\eta^{3}\}

and we go on alternating local maxima and minima until

. Notice that the procedure always ends as we suppose the function to have a finite number of constant pieces. Then the resulting sequence
\{\eta^{1},\eta_{2},\eta^{3},\eta_{4},\dots\}
(or
\{\eta_{1},\eta^{2},\eta_{3},\eta^{4},\dots\}
if we start with a minimum) is called reduced memory sequence, denoted by

and it gives information about the sequence of maximal peaks (resp. minimal peaks) not deleted by subsequent greater peaks (resp. lower peaks). Such a sequence is important for the Preisach operator, indeed the output of the operator with input
u
depends only on the

, that is given two inputs
u_{1},u_{2}
either continuous or piecewise constant, if
RMS(u_{1};[0,T])=RMS(u_{2};[0,T])
, then
w_{1}=[\mathcal{H}(u_{1},z_{0})]=[\mathcal{H}(u_{2},z_{0})]=w_{2}
.

Exploiting this property we can give the criterion for a piecewise constant input to generate configuration
z\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{L}.
We first need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Consider a piecewise constant function
u:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, consisting of a finite number of pieces, with its total variation

. Then there exists a function
u_{RMS}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
that is
L
-Lipschitz with

depending on

and
T
such that
u_{RMS}(0)=u(0)
,
RMS(u;[0,T])=RMS(u_{RMS};[0,T])
and
Var(u_{RMS})\leq C
.

Proof.

Consider the reduced memory sequence of
u
, which will be of the form
\{\eta^{1},\eta_{2},\eta^{3},\dots\}
or
\{\eta_{1},\eta^{2},\eta_{3},\dots\}
, where
\eta^{i}
are maxima and
\eta_{i}
are minima. We know that, denoting the elements of RMS by

with
v_{0}=u(0)

\sum_{i=1}|v_{i}-v_{i-1}|\leq Var(u)\leq C.

Now define the sequence

z_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}|v_{i}-v_{i-1}|

so that

is a monotonically increasing sequence bounded by

, with

and set

By rescaling the points to the interval

, that is by setting

we can define the function

as the piecewise linear function that interpolates the points

.

It is clear that
RMS(u;[0,T])=RMS(u_{RMS};[0,T])
and
Var(u_{RMS})\leq C
.

Moreover, considering the modulus

of the slopes of the segments corresponding to the linear parts we have that

m_{i}=\frac{|v_{i}-v_{i+1}|}{|x_{i+1}-x_{i}|}=\frac{\tilde{C}}{T}\frac{|v_{i}-v_{i+1}|}{|z_{i+1}-z_{i}|}=\frac{\tilde{C}}{T}\leq\frac{C}{T},

hence

is
L-
lipshitz with

Exploiting the wave-front tracking method (

We start with initial data

and general value

we end up with a solution

, see (

). To ensure that also

we need to consider only initial configuration

taking values in the set

. We recall that such set, defined at the end of Section

such that the corresponding graphs

consist of a finite number of segments with vertices belonging to the set

Considering

and

Consider then the Cauchy problem with data

then we have that the rarefactions connect data from the set

. Hence, at each rarefaction wave, we have left datum
j2^{-n}
and right datum
(j+m)2^{-n}
for some

, so we can split the rarefaction in
|m|
(non-entropical) shocks connecting

and

and

. The shock speed is then given by the generalized Rankine-Hugoniot conditions (
3.9
). As a result, these shocks are discontinuities for both

and hence
w_{n}
, whereas, in reference to Section
4
, the vertical ones are only for
z_{n}
and
w_{n}
.

Then by the wave-front tracking procedure, we extend
u_{n}
for larger times, and hence also
z_{n}
and
w_{n}
.

We now analyze the possible wave interactions. Since at time
0
we split the rarefaction waves by shocks of strength
2^{-n}
, then if two discontinuities of
u_{n}
meet at some point
(x_{1},t_{1})
, with

we have two possibilities:

1.

one wave connects
j2^{-n}
and
(j+1)2^{-n}
and the other
(j+1)2^{-n}
and
j2^{-n}
. So the new left and right data for
u_{n}
are equal at

, hence we don’t have discontinuities in
u_{n}
after the interaction. We may only generate a new vertical discontinuity for

.

2.

one wave connects
j2^{-n}
and
(j+1)2^{-n}
and the other
(j+1)2^{-n}
and
(j+2)2^{-n}
. However, such waves would not cross each other as the slope of the first one is greater due to the generalized Rankine-Hugoniot conditions (
3.9
).

We also notice that if we have more than two waves interacting, then, reasoning as in point
2
, the values separated by these waves must oscillate between
j2^{-n}
and
(j+1)2^{-n}
. Hence, after the interaction at most one shock is generated. In conclusion, since we start with a finite number of discontinuities for
u_{n}
(each one of the finite quantity of rarefactions is split in a finite number of shocks), the number of discontinuities for
u_{n}
stays finite, without increasing, implying a finite number of interactions. Finally, a wave of

may interact with a vertical wave of

at most once, and in that case no further waves of

are generated, so we can conclude that also the number of this type of interactions is finite.

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w(x,t)-w(x,s)|\penalty\ ds&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg|\int_{\mathcal{T}}z(x,t;\rho)-z(x,s;\rho)\penalty\ d\rho\bigg|\penalty\ ds\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z(x,t;\rho)-z(x,s;\rho)|d\rho\penalty\ dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}d(z(x,t),z(x,s))\penalty\ dx\leq C|t-s|,\end{split}

(5.5)

that is

.
It is clear that
(u,w)
satisfies the weak formulation (

). Moreover since

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z-z_{n}|\penalty\ d\rho dxdt\to 0

) holds.

We only need to prove (

). Since for fixed

for almost every

\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,t)}\tilde{u}_{n}d\left(\frac{\partial z_{n}}{\partial t}(x,\cdot\penalty\ ;\rho)\right)\penalty\ dxd\rho\geq\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{\rho}(z_{n}(x,\cdot\penalty\ ;\rho);(0,t))\penalty\ dxd\rho,

where

identifies the right continuous (w.r.t. to the time variable) representative of

and

and

\begin{split}\left\langle\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial t},\phi\right\rangle&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,t)}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}w_{n}\penalty\ dtdx\\

&=\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,t)}\phi\penalty\ d\left(\frac{\partial z_{n}}{\partial t}(x,\cdot\penalty\ ;\rho)\right)dxd\rho=\left\langle\int_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial z_{n}}{\partial t}\penalty\ d\rho,\phi\right\rangle,\end{split}

(5.8)

but

\left\langle\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial t},\phi\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,t)}\phi\,d\left(\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial t}(x,\cdot)\right)\penalty\ dx.

(5.9)

Hence by (
5.8
), (
5.9
) we get

\partial w_{n}/\partial t=\int_{\mathcal{T}}(\partial z_{n}/\partial t)d\rho

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,t)}\tilde{u}_{n}\,d\left(\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial t}(x,\cdot\penalty\ )\right)\penalty\ dx\geq\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{\rho}(z_{n}(x,\cdot\penalty\ ;\rho);(0,t))\penalty\ dxd\rho.

\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x}

\geq\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{\rho}(z_{n}(x,\cdot\penalty\ ;d\rho);(0,t))\penalty\ d\rho dx.

) for fixed

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}&\Psi_{\rho}(z_{n}(x,\cdot\penalty\ ;d\rho);(0,t))\penalty\ d\rho dx=\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\int_{(0,t)}\rho_{2}d\left(\frac{\partial z_{n}}{\partial t}^{+}\right)-\int_{(0,t)}\rho_{1}d\left(\frac{\partial z_{n}}{\partial t}^{-}\right)\right]\penalty\ dxd\rho\\

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u(x,t)^{2}-u_{0}(x)^{2}\right)\,dx,\ \ \int_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\rho_{2}+\rho_{1}}{2}(z(x,t;\rho)-z_{0}(x;\rho))\penalty\ dxd\rho

because of convergence in

and equiboundedness. Using (

6
Uniqueness

We finally show that the solution constructed in Section

is the unique entropy solution to (
3.1
). Following
[

]
, by entropy solution here we mean a pair
(u,w)
such that

and the following entropy condition is satisfied

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{T}\left[|u-k|+\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z-\hat{z}|\,d\rho\right]\phi_{t}+|u-k|\phi_{x}\,dt\,dx\geq 0

for every positive test function

with compact support in

and every function

such that

Let us first check that the solution constructed for the Riemann problem is coherent with this definition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Consider
(u,w)
with
w=\int_{\mathcal{T}}z\,d\rho
solution to the Riemann problem (

), (
4.1
), then it is an entropy solution.

Proof.

Suppose
u_{l}>u_{r}
. The case
u_{l}<u_{r}
is similar. We have the following cases:

Case
\bm{u_{r}<u_{l}\leq k}
: For

, we have that
|u-k|
is equal to
-u+k
for almost every
(x,t)
because

consists of a family of rarefaction waves connecting values from
u_{r}
to
u_{l}
in a monotone way (see Figure
6
). The configuration
z
passes instead from the initial configuration
z_{r}
to a configuration

, according to that monotone input

. By doing so we have a region

in the strip
([-a,u_{l}]\times[u_{r},u_{l}])\cap\mathcal{T}
where, along the monotone evolution of

,
z
changes from
-1
to
+1
(in Figure
8
-right,

is
R_{1}\cap\{\rho\ \mbox{``above the graph"}\}
). Since
(k,\hat{k}(\rho))\in\mathcal{L}_{\rho}

is certainly
+1
, hence in
A
,
|z-\hat{z}|
changes from
2
to
0
, and hence (recall

)

\left(\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z-\hat{z}|\,d\rho\right)_{t}=-w_{t}.

(6.2)

For
x<0
both
u
and
z
are constant. So by splitting the integral on the left hand side of (
6.1
) into the integral on the two regions

, by integrating by parts, by recalling that

is continuous on
\{x=0\}
, and by (

) we conclude that (

) holds as an equality.

Case
\bm{k\leq u_{r}<u_{l}}
: For
x<0

u
and
z
are constant. For
x>0,

|u-k|=u-k
and again

to

in

, hence also

changes only in

. However, now we only know that certainly
\hat{k}=-1
in the region
\rho_{2}\geq k
so

passes from

to
2
in the region

. In the rest of

either it remains constant or it passes from

to
2
or from

, in any case we deduce the bound

\left(\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z-\hat{z}|\,d\rho\right)_{t}\leq w_{t}.

(6.3)

Then starting from the left hand side of (
6.1
), splitting the integral over
x<0,x>0
, integrating by parts and using this last inequality (

), we get (

).

Case
\bm{u_{r}<k<u_{l}}
: For

, both

are constant so we focus on

where again, we have that

to

. We need to subdivide the integral into two parts: one where

In the region above instead

So by subdividing the integral into these parts, using the integration by parts formula and the last two equations we get (

). Notice that both

∎

Remark 6.2
.

Note that, even if the solution to our Riemann problem is just the union of rarefaction waves, without shocks, (

) may be strict, despite to the classical scalar case, where, for rarefaction waves only, the entropy condition always gives

We now prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 6.3
.

Given the initial conditions
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap BV(\mathbb{R}),z_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{L})\cap BV(\mathbb{R};\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{L})
, let
(u,w)
with
w=\int_{\mathcal{T}}z\,d\rho
be the solution to the PDE constructed via the wave-front tracking method. Then such couple is an entropy solution.

Proof.

Let us fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
, a configuration
\hat{k}
as in the definition of entropy condition and a positive test function
\phi
. Let
(u_{n},w_{n})
with
w_{n}=\int_{\mathcal{T}}z_{n}\,d\rho
be the solution to the approximating Cauchy problem with initial data
u^{(n)}_{0},z_{0}^{(n)}
constructed as in the proof of the previous Theorem
5.3
.
We are going to prove that
(u_{n},w_{n})
in an “almost entropy condition" in the sense that (
6.1
) is satisfied up to an infinitesimal error in the right-hand side (see (
6.6
)). Passing to the limit as
n\to+\infty
we will get the conclusion.

Both
(u_{n},w_{n})
and
z_{n}
are constant on a finite number of triangles
T_{j}
. For each fixed time
t
we have a family of points
x_{i}(t)
,
i=1,\dots,N(t)
such that
x_{i}(t)<x_{i+1}(t)
on which
u_{n}
or
w_{n}
have a jump point. Integrating by parts, the left side of (
6.1
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}&\int_{0}^{T}\left[\left(\Delta u_{i}(t;k)+\Delta z_{i}(t;\hat{z})\right)x_{i}^{\prime}(t)-\Delta u_{i}(t;k)\right]\phi(x_{i}(t),t)\,dt\end{split}

(6.4)

where

\Delta u_{i}(t;k):=|u(x_{i}(t)+,t)-k|-|u(x_{i}(t)-,t)-k|,

\Delta z_{i}(t;\hat{z}):=\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z(x_{i}(t)+,t;\rho)-\hat{z}(\rho)|\,d\rho-\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z(x_{i}(t)-,t;\rho)-\hat{z}(\rho)|\,d\rho.

We analyze all the possible different kind of discontinuities:

\bm{x^{\prime}_{i}(t)=0:}
vertical discontinuities which are only discontinuities for
z
and
w
, hence,
\Delta u_{i}=0
, which means that they do not contribute in the sum in (
6.4
).

\bm{u(x_{i}(t)+,t)<u(x_{i}(t)-,t)\leq k:}
in this case
\Delta u_{i}(t;k)=-u(x_{i}(t)+)+u(x_{i}(t)-)
and by arguing as in the first case of the proof of Proposition
6.1
we can check that
\Delta z_{i}(t;\hat{z})=-w(x_{i}(t)+)+w(x_{u}(t)-)
. Now, since
x^{\prime}(t)
satisfies the Rankine-Hugoniot condition (
3.9
) then also this contribution of these type of discontinuities in the sum in (
6.4
) is equal to
0
.

\bm{k\leq u(x_{i}(t)+,t)<u(x_{i}(t)-,t)}
: here instead
\Delta u_{i}(t;k)=u(x_{i}(t)+)-u(x_{i}(t)-)
and by doing the same reasoning as in the second case of the proof of Proposition
6.1
we can check that
\Delta z_{i}(t;\hat{z})\geq w(x_{i}(t)+)-w(x_{u}(t)-)
. Now, since
x^{\prime}(t)
satisfies the Rankine-Hugoniot condition (
3.9
) it is easy to check that the contribution in (
6.4
) of discontinuities belonging to this case is positive.

\bm{u(x_{i}(t)-,t)<u(x_{i}(t)+,t)\leq k}
or
\bm{k\leq u(x_{i}(t)-,t)<u(x_{i}(t)+,t):}
these cases are similar to the previous two ones and they at most give a positive contribution to the sum in (
6.4
)

\bm{u(x_{i}(t)+,t)<k<u(x_{i}(t)-,t)}
or

\bm{u(x_{i}(t)-,t)<k<u(x_{i}(t)+,t):}
discontinuities that belong to this case are the ones that contribute to the entropy error, indeed their contribution to the sum in (
6.4
) may be negative. However we can estimate
\Delta u_{i}(t;k)\geq-2^{-n}
,
\Delta z_{i}(t;\hat{z})\geq-C\,2^{-n}
,
0\leq x^{\prime}_{i}(t)\leq 1
hence

\left(\Delta u_{i}(t;k)+\Delta z_{i}(t;\hat{z})\right)x_{i}^{\prime}(t)-\Delta u_{i}(t;k)\geq-\tilde{C}\,2^{-n}

(6.5)

for some
\tilde{C}\geq 0
.

Then by the previous analysis of the different kind of discontinuities, if we consider only the indices
i_{j}
with
j=1,\dots,\tilde{N}(t)
for which we might have negative contribution we can conclude from (
6.4
) that, for some
\tilde{K}\geq 0
:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{+\infty}&\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left[|u_{n}-k|+\int_{\mathcal{T}}|z_{n}-\hat{z}|\,d\rho\right]\phi_{t}+|u_{n}-k|\phi_{x}\,dx\,dt\\
&\geq-\tilde{C}\,2^{-n}\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi(x_{i}(t),t)\,dt\geq-K\,2^{-n}\,\tilde{N}(t)\geq-\tilde{K}\,n2^{-n}\end{split}

(6.6)

where the last inequality is due to the fact that
``\bm{u(x_{i}(t)+,t)<k<u(x_{i}(t)-,t)}
or

\bm{u(x_{i}(t)-,t)<k<u(x_{i}(t)+,t)}"
can only occur in one discontinuity per rarefaction wave, so at time
0
that number of occurrence is of the same order of
n
(see the construction of
u_{0}^{(n)},z_{0}^{(n)}
in the the proof of Theorem
5.3
), and it may only decrease as time increases as stated in Lemma
5.1
.

Taking the limit in (
6.6
) we conclude the proof.
∎

As a consequence of the next theorem, we then deduce that the solution constructed via the wave-front tracking method is also the only entropy solution.

Theorem 6.4
.

Consider the Cauchy problems with initial conditions
u_{0}^{1},z_{0}^{1}
and
u_{0}^{2},z_{0}^{2}
respectively, where

(6.7)

for almost every

Proof.

Adapting the standard method of doubling variables by Kruzkov
[

]
, in
[

]

∎

Appendix A
BV functions with values in metric spaces

In this appendix we state the main properties we used in the article about functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to E
of bounded variation and with
E
a metric space with some compactness properties. The proofs are omitted but they can be done in a similar way, as in the case
E=\mathbb{R}
, by exploiting compactness properties of

For these proofs we refer to
[
5
, Chapter 3]
and to
[
3
]

Suppose we have a metric space
(E,d)
and that it is complete. Consider a function
z:\mathbb{R}\to E
and fix a point
z_{v}\in E
as “zero".

Definition A.1
.

We say that
z\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};E)
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}}d(z(x),z_{v})\penalty\ dx<+\infty.

(A.1)

We say that
z\in BV(\mathbb{R};E)
if there exists a representative
v
of it, i.e.
z=v
almost everywhere, such that

\sup\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}d(v(x_{i}),v(x_{i+1}))\quad\bigg|\quad-\infty=x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{N}=\infty\right\}<+\infty.

(A.2)

The total variation of
z
is defined as the minimum value of (
A.2
) among all representatives of
z
. Such minimum is achieved and from now on when we say
z\in BV
we will assume that
z
is one of the representatives with the smallest total variation (see
[
3
]
).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
z\in BV(\mathbb{R},E)
where
E
is a complete metric space. Then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the left and right limits of
z
at
x
exist.

It can also be shown that right or left continuous representatives of
z
minimize (
A.2
), and thus they are called “good" representatives and can be chosen in order to compute the total variation of
z
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
z\in BV(\mathbb{R},E)
where
E
is a complete metric space. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a piecewise constant function
v
such that

Var(v)\leq Var(z)\quad\text{and}\quad||d(z,v)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\varepsilon.

(A.3)

If
z\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};E)
, then
v
can be chosen in a way such that it also holds

||d(z,w)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\varepsilon.

(A.4)

Regarding the compactness result, we need not only
E
to be complete but also compact.

Theorem A.4
.

Consider a sequence of functions
z_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to E
, with
E
compact metric space, such that

Var(z_{n})\leq C,\quad||d(z_{n},z_{v})||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M.

(A.5)

Then there exists a subsequence
z_{n_{k}}
and
z\in BV(\mathbb{R};E)
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}z_{n_{k}}(x)=z(x),\quad Var(z)\leq C,\quad||d(z,z_{v})||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M.

(A.6)

Moreover if it also holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}||d(z_{n},z)||_{L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})}=0.

Over the past 60 or so years,
Tate algebras have proven very useful in the study of

Remarkably, despite not being essentially of finite type over their

Since their introduction in 2010, perfectoid Tate rings have been important objects in

Question 1.1
.

Do quotients of perfectoid Tate algebras

The question is not completely well-defined,

A positive answer to Question

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem

Let

L=T_{n,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}/\mathfrak{m}

be the corresponding quotient field. Then there exists a polyradius

such that

is a semi-immediate extension of
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{l}}
, where

l\leq\max(0,n-0pt(\mathfrak{m}^{\flat}\cap(T_{n,K^{\flat}})^{\operatorname{coperf}})-1),

where
\mathfrak{m}^{\flat}
is the tilt of
\mathfrak{m}
in the sense of
[
12
]

l\leq n-1
. Moreover, if
K
has uncountable residue field, then none of the radii
r_{1},\dots,r_{l}
above belongs to the value group of
K
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is a bit subtle,
and relies on the theories of Abhyankar valuations and so-called Prüfer-Manis valuations. We will give an indication of the proof below.

In our second main theorem, we show that essentially all of the possibilities

Theorem 1.3

For every

K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n-1}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}

K_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}

(s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1})

1.1.
Methods of proof

The main ingredient in our proof of Theorem
1.2
is a certain qualitative generalization of the classification of points on the Berkovich affine line.
Let
A
be an affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich.
A continuous rank
1
valuation
v
on
A
is
Abhyankar
if it satisfies equality in (a continuous analogue of) Abhyankar’s inequality.
Abhyankar valuations on
K\langle T\rangle
are precisely type (II) and (III) points and, in fact, Abhyankar valuations on
K\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\rangle
, for any
d\geq 1
, are precisely the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
-Gauss norms for various polyradii
(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in(0,1]^{d}
, see Proposition
3.5
.
Furthermore, we say that a rank
1
valuation
v
is
topologically simple
if the topology defined by the valuation has no nonzero closed ideals.
Topologically simple valuations on
K\langle T\rangle
are precisely type (IV) points, see Proposition
4.14
.
We show that the notion of topologically simple valuations is equivalent to the notion of Prüfer-Manis valuations of Knebusch and Zhang

[
34
]
.

Now, the statement of Theorem
1.2
reduces by tilting to the the case when
K
is of characteristic
p
. In this case, we show that every quotient field
L
of the perfectoid Tate algebra
T_{n,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
which is a counterexample to the classical Nullstellensatz for
T_{n,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
(i.e.,
L
is not a finite extension of
K
) gives rise to a topologically simple continuous rank-
1
valuation
v
on a positive-dimensional affinoid algebra
A
in the sense of Tate, namely, the restriction of the absolute value of
L
to the image of
T_{n,K}\subseteq T_{n,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
inside
L
. Choosing a Noether normalization

T_{d,K}\hookrightarrow A

and using the fact that the Abhyankar valuations on
T_{d,K}
are precisely the Gauss norms, we show that the quotient field
L
is a semi-immediate extension of
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{l}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
for

l<d=n-0pt(\mathfrak{m}\cap(T_{n,K})^{\operatorname{coperf}})

if and only if the valuation
v
is not Abhyankar. The statement of Theorem
1.2
then follows from the following valuation-theoretic result.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.18
)
.

Let
A
be an affinoid algebra in the sense of Tate (i.e. a strictly affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich) which is not a field. Then there does not exist a continuous absolute value
v
on
A
(over
K
) that is both topologically simple and Abhyankar.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
proceeds by a process we term
Shilov localization
; that is, we pass to an affinoid domain
W
of
\mathscr{M}(A)
such that
v
becomes the only point in the Shilov boundary of
W
. However, such a neighborhood can fail to be strictly affinoid (i.e., its corresponding affinoid
algebra in the sense of Berkovich is not affinoid in the sense of Tate).
Furthermore, there exist examples of continuous rank-
1
valuations on affinoid algebras in the
sense of Berkovich which are simultaneously Abhyankar and topologically simple (indeed, the field
K_{r}
, for
r\not\in\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
,
is the affinoid algebra of the “irrational circle”
\{\,v\in\mathscr{M}(K\langle T\rangle)\mid v(T)=r\,\}
). Thus, we must restrict to certain special neighborhoods for which the statement does hold, which we call

strongly Shilov
neighborhoods, a terminology derived from
[
13
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3

closely follows the strategy of Gleason in

[
23
]
.
That argument depends on a certain division algorithm for power
series fields with a certain finiteness property;
Gleason then proceeds to construct a special element whose coefficients
can be manipulated to satisfy the inputs of the division algorithm
argument.
Finally, Gleason uses a Noether normalization statement for
completed perfections to remove
an extra variable.
We present each step in a slightly more general context than

[
23
]
, so as to achieve the statement in
Theorem

1.3
.
The first and third step closely follow the proof in
[
23
]
,
while the second requires a slightly more technical step.

2.
Background and Notation

2.1.
Conventions

In this paper, the word
residue field
always denotes the residue field of a nonarchimedean
field. If we discuss residue fields in the geometric sense of Berkovich/adic spaces, we use the term

completed residue field
. Finally, if we discuss fields which are quotients of a ring, which
could perhaps be called “algebraic residue fields”, we will always use the term
quotient
field
.

In what follows, we will often need to distinguish between the perfect closure (i.e., colimit
perfection, also known as the coperfection) and the completed (co)perfection of a uniform Banach algebra in characteristic
p
.
In the literature, both of these could be notated by
-^{\text{perf}}
.
In order to avoid confusion, we don’t use this notation.
If
A
is a uniform
K
-Banach algebra over a nonarchimedean field
K
of characteristic
p
, then

A^{\operatorname{coperf}}
denotes the perfect closure (coperfection)
and
A^{\operatorname{perfd}}
denotes the completed perfection, i.e., the completion of
A^{\operatorname{coperf}}
with respect to the canonical extension of a power-multiplicative norm defining the topology on
A
.

We will often need to distiguish between affinoid algebras in the sense of Tate and affinoid
algebras in the sense of Berkovich. We make much use of both notions, so we try to specify wherever
possible, unless the context is clear. Thus, we always use “affinoid in the sense of Tate” or
“strictly affinoid” for the former notion, and we use “affinoid in the sense of Berkovich” or
“Berkovich-affinoid” for the latter.

2.2.
Normed Rings

All rings are commutative and with
1
. We use multiplicative (not additive) notation for valuations on rings. Recall that a seminorm on an abelian group
G
is a function
\lVert\cdot\rVert:G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\lVert 0\rVert=0
and such that the triangle inequality
\lVert f+g\rVert\leq\lVert f\rVert+\lVert g\rVert
holds for any two elements
f,g\in G
. A seminorm is said to be nonarchimedean if for any
f,g\in G
the stronger inequality
\lVert f+g\rVert\leq\max(\lVert f\rVert,\lVert g\rVert)
is satisfied. Unless explicitly stated otherwise we only consider nonarchimedean seminorms. A seminorm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is called a norm if
0
is the only element of
G
which is mapped to
0
under
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.

For a ring
A
, a seminorm on
A
is a seminorm on the underlying additive abelian group of
A
such that
\lVert 1\rVert=1
. A seminorm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on a ring
A
is said to be submultiplicative (or a ring seminorm) if it is compatible with multiplication in the sense that

\lVert fg\rVert\leq\lVert f\rVert\lVert g\rVert

for any two elements
f,g\in A
. By a (semi)normed ring (respectively, a Banach ring)
(A,\lVert\cdot\rVert)
we will mean a ring
A
equipped with a submultiplicative (semi)norm (resp., a complete submultiplicative norm)
\lVert\cdot\rVert
. In this paper we tacitly assume all our seminorms on rings to be submultiplicative and we often suppress the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
from the notation for a (semi)normed ring (respectively, Banach ring). For a seminormed abelian group
A
we denote by
A_{\leq r}
the closed ball
(in the seminormed topology) of radius
r>0
in
A
, i.e.,

A_{\leq r}=\{\,f\in A\mid\lVert f\rVert\leq r\,\}.

Since we restrict our attention to nonarchimedean seminorms, this is always a subgroup of
A
, open with respect to the topology defined by the given seminorm, and if
A
is a seminormed ring and
c\leq 1
, then
A_{\leq c}
is an open subring.
We often abbreviate
A^{\circ}
for
A_{\leq 1}
.
Moreover, we use the same notation for closed unit balls of (possibly higher-rank) valuations on a ring
A
, i.e.,
A_{v\leq 1}
is the set
f\in A
such that
v(f)\leq 1
for the valuation
v
on
A
.

A seminorm on a ring
A
is said to be power-multiplicative (resp., multiplicative) if
\lVert f^{n}\rVert=\lVert f\rVert^{n}
for all
f
and all integers
n\geq 0
(resp.,
\lVert fg\rVert=\lVert f\rVert\lVert g\rVert
for all
f,g\in A
). A multiplicative norm on a ring
A
is also
called an absolute value. By a valued field
K
we mean a normed ring
(K,\lVert\cdot\lVert)
whose underlying ring is a field and whose norm is an absolute value. If the valued field
K
is moreover a Banach ring, we call it a nonarchimedean field. For a valued field
K
, we denote by
\widetilde{K}
its residue field and by
|K^{\times}|
its value group.

Definition 2.1
.

A valued field extension, or extension of valued fields, is a field extension
L/K
between valued fields, with absolute values
v_{K}
,
v_{L}
, such that
v_{K}
is the restriction of
v_{L}
to
K
. In this case, we also call the valued field
L
an extension of
K
. We call a valued field extension
L/K
a nonarchimedean field extension if the valued fields
K
,
L
are nonarchimedean fields.

We shall use the following conditions on nonarchimedean field extensions.

Definition 2.2

(Immediate extension)
.

An extension of valued fields
K\subseteq L
is called immediate if
L
has the residue field and value group as
K
. A nonarchimedean field which admits no proper immediate extensions is called spherically complete.

Definition 2.3

(Semi-immediate extension)
.

An extension of valued fields
K\subseteq L
is called a semi-immediate extension if the group
|L^{\times}|/|K^{\times}|
is torsion (i.e., if
|L^{\times}|\subseteq\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
) and the residue field of
L
is algebraic over the residue field of
K
.

Note that a nonarchimedean field which is both algebraically closed and spherically complete admits
no proper semi-immediate extensions: Indeed, for any algebraically closed nonarchimedean field
K
,
the value group of
K
is divisibly closed and the residue field of
K
is algebraically closed.

For a nonarchimedean field
K
, the following class of nonarchimedean field extensions of
K
plays
a prominent role in this paper. Following Ducros
[
14
]
, 1.1, we call any
l
-tuple

(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{l}
of positive real numbers a polyradius, and, given a
nonarchimedean field
K
, we call
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
a
K
-free polyradius if the elements

r_{1},\dots,r_{l}
define
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent elements of the “multiplicative”

\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
(\mathbb{R}_{>0}/\sqrt{|K^{\times}|})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. We will say that the polyradius is free (instead of
K
-free) whenever the nonarchimedean field
K
is understood from the context.

Definition 2.4

(The fields
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}
)
.

For a nonarchimedean field
K
and a polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
, we let
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}
be the completion of

with respect to the (canonical extension of the)

be the completion of the field

with respect to the canonical extension of the

-Gauss norm on
K[x_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}},\dots,x_{n}^{1/p^{\infty}}]
; note that if

.

The nonarchimedean fields

appear in Ducros’ Nullstellnesatz for Berkovich-affinoid algebras (

is the completed residue field of a type (II) point on the Berkovich affine line

, Lemma 4.6, but we include it here for the reader’s convenience. However, we only spell out the proof for
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}
; the argument for
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
is the same except that the index set is then
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]^{n}
rather than
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Let
K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})}
be the ring of convergent power series
on the right-hand side of the equality, equipped with its corresponding Gauss norm. We claim that
the freeness of the polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
implies that this ring is a field. Indeed, if for
some

f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{i}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot x_{n}^{i_{n}}\in K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})}

the sequence

\{|a_{i}|r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}}\,\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

attains its maximum at two indices
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
a_{j}\neq 0
and

r_{1}^{i_{1}-j_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}-j_{n}}\in\frac{|a_{j}|}{|a_{i}|}\in\sqrt{|K^{\times}|},

so by the assumption that the polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
is free, we must have
i_{1}-j_{1}=0,\dots,i_{n}-j_{n}=0
, i.e.,
i=j
. Thus the sequence
\{\,|a_{i}|r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}}\,\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
attains its maximum at a unique index
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then for every
i\neq N
, the element

(a_{N}^{-1}x_{1}^{-N_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdots x_{n}^{-N_{n}})(a_{i}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ x^{i_{n}})

has norm
<1
. It follows that
a_{N}^{-1}x_{1}^{-N_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdots x_{n}^{-N_{n}}f-1
is topologically nilpotent and thus
f
is a unit.

Once we know that the ring
K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})}
is a field, we know that it must contain the field of fractions
K(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, since it clearly contains
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. Since the norm on
K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})}
is the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
-Gauss norm, it must then also contain the completion
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}
of
K(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
with respect to the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
-Gauss norm. But
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
(and, a fortiori,
K(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
) is dense in
K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})}
, so
K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})}
has to be equal to
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}
. ∎

Remark 2.6
.

The above lemma need no longer be true if we omit the freeness condition on the polyradius. We note that in the original work of Berkovich
[
3
]
and in Ducros
[
14
]
, the notation
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}
always refers to the affinoid algebra (in the sense of Berkovich) given by the right hand side of the equality in Lemma

; this affinoid algebra may fail to be a field if the polyradius

is not free. In this paper, we want to treat free and non-free polyradii on an equal footing and so we follow Temkin in

]
, paragraph preceding Proposition 3.1, in using the notation

as in Definition

, regardless of whether the polyradius

is free or not. In particular, we employ the same notation

both for completed residue fields of type (III) points on
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\operatorname{an}}

Lemma 2.7
.

For any nonarchimedean field
K
and radius
r>0
, the field
K_{r}
is not a semi-immediate extension of
K
.

Proof.

If
r\in\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
, i.e.,
r^{k}=|c|
for some
c\in K^{\times}
and some

, then the residue field of

has transcendence
degree
1
over the residue field of

, with the image of
\frac{x^{k}}{c}
as the new
transcendental variable. If

, then the residue field of

coincides with the residue field of

, but the group
|K_{r}^{\times}|/|K^{\times}|
is free of rank

, with free generator

(see also
[
29
]
, Corollary 2.27(ii) and (iii)).∎

2.3.
Berkovich’s classification of points

Let
K
be a nonarchimedean field. For
a\in\widehat{\overline{K}}
in the completed algebraic closure
\widehat{\overline{K}}
of
K
we denote by
B(a,r)
the closed disk of radius
r
around
a
, i.e.,

B(a,r)=\{\,b\in\widehat{\overline{K}}\mid|b-a|\leq r\,\}.

Consider the polynomial ring
K[T]
. For every closed disk
B(a,r)\subset\widehat{\overline{K}}
as above we define a multiplicative seminorm
|\cdot|_{B(a,r)}
on
K[T]
by

|f|_{B(a,r)}:=\sup_{z\in B(a,r)}|f(z)|\ \ \ (f\in K[T]).

Note that this seminorm extends the given absolute value on
K
. We recall Berkovich’s classification of points on the Berkovich affine line
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\operatorname{an}}
(
[
3
]
, 1.4.4), i.e., of multiplicative seminorms on
K[T]
which extend the given absolute value on the nonarchimedean field
K
. For a point
x
on a Berkovich-analytic space
X
over a nonarchimedean field
K
(such as
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\operatorname{an}}
or such as the Gelfand spectrum
\mathscr{M}(A)
of an affinoid algebra
A
in the sense of Berkovich), we denote by
\mathcal{H}(x)
the completed residue field of
X
at
x
; for an affinoid neighborhood
\mathscr{M}(A)
of
x
, the point
x
is a continuous rank-
1
valuation on
A
(over
K
) and
\mathcal{H}(x)
is the completion of the field of fractions
Q(A/\ker(x))
of
A/\ker(x)
with respect to the absolute value induced by
x
.

Proposition 2.8

(Berkovich
[
3
]
, 1.4.4, see also
[
29
]
, Corollary 2.27)
.

Every point
x\in\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\operatorname{an}}
belongs to one of the following categories:

(1)

Points of type
(I)
: There exists a point
a\in\widehat{\overline{K}}
such that, for all
f\in K[T]
we have
|f|_{x}=|f(a)|
. The completed residue field
\mathcal{H}(x)
is a finite extension of
K
. In particular, it is a semi-immediate extension of
K
.

(2)

Points of type
(II)
: There exist a point
a\in\widehat{\overline{K}}
and a closed disk

B(a,r)\subset\widehat{\overline{K}}
, with radius
r
in
\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
, such that

|f|_{x}=|f|_{B(a,r)}

for all
f\in K[T]
. The completed residue field is of the form
\mathcal{H}(x)=K_{r}
with
r\in\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
. In this case,
|\mathcal{H}(x)^{\times}|/|K^{\times}|
is a finite group and
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}(x)}
is finitely generated over
\widetilde{K}
of transcendence degree
1
.

(3)

Points of type
(III)
: The same as for points of type (II), but the radius
r
of
B(a,r)

does not belong to
\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
. The completed residue field is
\mathcal{H}(x)=K_{r}
with
r\not\in\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
. The group
|\mathcal{H}(x)^{\times}|/|K^{\times}|
is a finitely generated abelian group of rank
1
, generated by
r
.

(4)

Points of type
(IV)
: There exists a nested sequence of closed disks

B(a_{1},r_{1})\supseteq B(a_{2},r_{2})\supseteq B(a_{3},r_{3})\supseteq\dots

in
\widehat{\overline{K}}
with empty intersection such that

|f|_{x}=\lim_{n}|f|_{B(a_{n},r_{n})}=\inf_{n}|f|_{B(a_{n},r_{n})}\ (f\in K[T])

In this case, the completed residue field
\mathcal{H}(x)
is a semi-immediate extension of
K
.

If the completed algebraic closure
\widehat{\overline{K}}
is spherically complete, then only points of types (I), (II) and (III) occur.

Moreover, we have the same description for the points in the Berkovich closed unit ball, i.e.

\mathscr{M}(K\langle T\rangle)
, and, more generally, in a closed ball
\mathscr{M}(K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle)
of radius
r>0
, subject to the condition that the ball only contains
points which are dominated by the
r
-Gauss norm.

2.4.
Shilov Boundaries

In this subsection, we fix a nonarchimedean field
K
. For a seminormed ring
(A,\lVert\cdot\rVert)
, we consider the spectral seminorm

|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}=\inf_{n}\lVert f^{n}\rVert^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert f^{n}\rVert^{1/n},f\in A.

If
A
is a seminormed algebra over
K
, then this definition does not depend on the choice of the
K
-algebra seminorm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
defining the topology on
A
: Indeed, any two such
K
-algebra seminorms
\lVert\cdot\rVert
,
\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}
have to be bounded-equivalent, i.e.,

\lVert\cdot\rVert C\leq\lVert\cdot\rVert^{\prime}\leq C^{\prime}\lVert\cdot\rVert

for some constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
, and then their spectral seminorms, defined as above, have to be equal by
[
6
]
, Proposition 1.3.1/2. We note that
|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}
is always the maximal power-multiplicative seminorm on
A
bounded by the original seminorm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.

For a seminormed ring
(A,\lVert\cdot\rVert)
and
r>0
, we follow Kedlaya in
[
28
]
, Definition 5.11, and write
A^{\circ,r}
(respectively,
A^{\circ\circ,r}
) for the set of
f\in A
such that the sequence
\{\,r^{-n}\lVert f^{n}\rVert\,\}_{n}
is bounded (respectively, converges to
0
). Note that for
r=1
this recovers the subring of power-bounded elements
A^{\circ}
(respectively, the set of topologically nilpotent elements
A^{\circ\circ}
). We also recall the following observation from loc. cit.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A
be a seminormed ring and let
r>0
. Then

A^{\circ\circ,r}=A_{|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}<r}

and

A^{\circ,r}\subseteq A_{|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r}.

If the quotient
A/\mathrm{Nil}(A)
of
A
by its nilradical is uniform (i.e., the spectral seminorm on
A/\mathrm{Nil}(A)
defines the same topology as the quotient seminorm induced by the original seminorm), then
A^{\circ,r}=A_{|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r}
.

Proof.

If
f\in A^{\circ\circ,r}
, then
\lVert f^{n}\rVert<r^{n}
for large enough
n
, so
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}<r
by definition of the spectral seminorm. Conversely, if
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}<r
, then
\lVert f^{n}\rVert^{1/n}<r
for all large enough
n
, so
r^{-n}\lVert f^{n}\rVert\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Similarly,
\{\,r^{-n}\lVert f^{n}\rVert\,\}_{n}
is a bounded sequence, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\lVert f^{n}\rVert\leq Cr^{n}
for all
n
. Taking
n
-th roots and passing to the limit, we see that
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r
.

It remains to prove the additional assertion when
A/\mathrm{Nil}(A)
is uniform. Note that, for any
f\in A
, the seequence
\{r^{-n}\lVert f^{n}\rVert\}_{n}
depends only on the image of
f
in the reduced quotient
A/\mathrm{Nil}(A)
; the same is also true for the condition
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r
, by definition of the spectral seminorm. Hence we can replace
A
with
A/\mathrm{Nil}(A)
and assume that
A
itself is uniform. In this case, we can also replace
\lVert\cdot\rVert
with
|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}
to check the topological condition of boundedness; but the condition
|f^{n}|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r^{n}
for all
n
, which is equivalent to
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r
, implies that
\{r^{-n}|f^{n}|_{\operatorname{sp}}\}_{n}
is a bounded sequence.∎

We can now define the reduction and graded reduction of a seminormed ring.

Definition 2.10

(Reduction of a seminormed ring)
.

For any seminormed ring
A
, we define the reduction
\widetilde{A}
of
A
to be

\widetilde{A}=A^{\circ}/A^{\circ\circ}.

If the seminorm on
A
is power-multiplicative, then this is the quotient of the closed unit ball by the open unit ball, by Lemma
2.9
. Note that this notation is compatible with our notation for residue fields of nonarchimedean fields. The more general notion of graded reduction was first introduced by Temkin in
[
39
]
for affinoid algebras in the sense of Berkovich and was extended to more general Banach rings by Kedlaya in
[
28
]
, where it was used to develop the theory of reified adic spaces.

Definition 2.11

(Graded reduction: Temkin
[
39
]
, §3, Kedlaya
[
28
]
, Definition 5.11)
.

The graded reduction
\operatorname{Gr}(A)
of
A
is the
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-graded ring

\operatorname{Gr}(A)=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}A^{\circ,r}/A^{\circ\circ,r}.

The graded reduction was originally introduced by Temkin for affinoid algebras in the sense of Berkovich. The above more general definition is Kedlaya’s definition in
[
30
]
. By Lemma
2.9
and
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.1.4, it coincides with Temkin’s definition

\operatorname{Gr}(A)=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}A_{|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r}/A_{|\cdot|_{\operatorname{sp}}<r}

when
A
is an affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich.

The graded reduction of an affinoid algebra (in the sense of Berkovich) will be important when
studying the notion of Shilov boundary. Recall from
[
3
]
, Theorem 1.3.1, that the
spectral seminorm can also be described as

|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}=\sup_{v\in\mathscr{M}(A)}v(f)=\max_{v\in\mathscr{M}(A)}v(f),

where the
supremum is a maximum since the Gelfand spectrum
\mathscr{M}(A)
is a compact Hausdorff space (
[
3
]
, Theorem 1.2.1). It is then reasonable to ask for minimal closed subsets
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathscr{M}(A)
on which every element
f\in A
achieves is maximum
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}
.

Definition 2.12

(Shilov boundary:
[
25
]
, §4,
[
13
]
, Definition 2.25)
.

For a seminormed
K
-algebra
A
, a subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathscr{M}(A)
is called a boundary for
A
if every element
f\in A
attains its maximum
|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}
on
\mathcal{S}
. If there exists a unique minimal closed boundary
\mathcal{S}
, it is called the Shilov boundary for
A
.

It is known (see
[
25
]
, Ch. I, Théorème 3,
[
18
]
, Theorem C) that the Shilov boundary exists for any seminormed
K
-algebra
A
(with suitable definitions, it actually exists for any seminormed ring and even any seminormed monoid, see the work of Guennebaud
[
25
]
). In this paper, we only need the notion of Shilov boundary for affinoid algebras in the sense of Berkovich. In this case, the Shilov boundary has an interesting description in terms of minimal prime homogeneous ideals in the graded reduction of
A
, due to Temkin (
[
39
]
, Proposition 3.3), see below.

We first recall a simpler avatar of this description in the case of strictly affinoid algebras,
which predates Temkin’s work and is due to Berkovich in
[
3
]
. To this end, we introduce
some notation. For any affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich
A
, we consider the map

\operatorname{sp}:\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})\to\operatorname{Spf}(A^{\circ})=\operatorname{Spec}(\widetilde{A})

which sends a continuous valuation on
A
to
its center
\operatorname{sp}(v)
on
A^{\circ}
, i.e.,

\operatorname{sp}(v)=\{\,f\in A^{\circ}\mid v(f)<1\,\}.

This map is continuous; by contrast, its restriction to the Gelfand spectrum
\mathscr{M}(A)
is anti-continuous for the usual compact Hausdorff topology on
\mathscr{M}(A)
(the pre-image of a Zariski-closed subset of
\operatorname{Spf}(A^{\circ})
is open in
\mathscr{M}(A)
). For any ring
R
, we denote by
\operatorname{Min}(R)
the set of minimal prime ideals of
R
. We can now state Berkovich’s description of the Shilov boundary for strictly affinoid algebras.

Proposition 2.13

(
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.4.4(ii), (iii), cf.
[
4
]
, Proposition 2.2 for the formulation using the adic spectrum)
.

Let
A
be a strictly
K
-affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich (i.e, an affinoid algebra in the sense of Tate). Then the pre-image
\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\{\mathfrak{p}\})
of any
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A})
is a single rank-
1
point and the set of such pre-images

\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A}))

is the Shilov boundary for
A
. In particular, the Shilov boundary is finite.

This result motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.14

(Strongly Shilov affinoid algebras)
.

For an affinoid
K
-algebra
A
in the sense of Berkovich, we say that

A
is
strongly Shilov
if the conclusion of Proposition
2.13
still holds for
A
. For an arbitrary affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich, we call an affinoid domain
V
of
\mathscr{M}(A)
strongly Shilov if the corresponding affinoid algebra is strongly Shilov.

The terminology “strongly Shilov” is derived from
[
13
]
, where (a slightly generalized form) of the conclusion of Proposition
2.13
is shown to correspond to a commutative-algebraic condition on the subring
A^{\circ}
called “strongly
\varpi
-Shilov” in that paper, see
[
13
]
, Theorem 6.20.

A general (non-strictly affinoid) Berkovich-affinoid algebra need not be strongly Shilov. However, using the notation of graded reduction, Temkin established in
[
39
]
an analog of Berkovich’s description for all affinoid algebras. The analog of the map
\operatorname{sp}
in this setting is constructed as follows. Fix a
K
-affinoid algebra
A
in the sense of Berkovich. For an element
v\in\mathscr{M}(A)
, consider the bounded
K
-algebra homomorphism

\chi_{v}:A\to\mathcal{H}(v)

to the completed residue field of
v
. As any bounded homomorphism, it satisfies
|\chi_{v}(f)|=v(f)\leq|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}
(see
[
6
]
, Proposition 1.3.1/2). Hence it induces a homomorphism of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-graded rings

\operatorname{Gr}(\chi_{v}):\operatorname{Gr}(A)\to\operatorname{Gr}(\mathcal{H}(v)).

Since
\operatorname{Gr}(\mathcal{H}(v))
is a graded field (and, in particular, a graded integral domain), the kernel of
\operatorname{Gr}(\chi_{v})
is a prime homogeneous ideal of
\operatorname{Gr}(A)
. In this way, we obtain a map

\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}:\mathscr{M}(A)\to\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(\operatorname{Gr}(A)),v\mapsto\ker(\operatorname{Gr}(\chi_{v})),

where the target is the space of prime homogeneous ideals of the
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-ring
\operatorname{Gr}(A)
.

Lemma 2.15
.

The above map
\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
fits into the commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}(A) }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(\operatorname{Gr}(A))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces \operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ}) }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Spec}(\widetilde{A}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{sp}}

where the left vertical map is the usual inclusion map and where the right vertical map is the restriction map
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}\cap\operatorname{Gr}(A)_{1}=\mathfrak{p}\cap\widetilde{A}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathscr{M}(A)
. By the definitions of
\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
and
\operatorname{Gr}(\chi_{v})
, we have

\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(v)\cap\operatorname{Gr}(A)_{r}=\ker(\operatorname{Gr}(\chi_{v}))\cap\operatorname{Gr}(A)_{r}=\{\,f\in A\mid|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}\leq r,v(f)<r\,\}.

In particular,
\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(v)\cap\widetilde{A}=\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(v)\cap\operatorname{Gr}(A)_{1}=\operatorname{sp}(v)
, as desired. ∎

For a graded ring
R
, we denote by
\operatorname{Min}(R)
the set of minimal prime homogeneous ideals of
R
. By
[
39
]
, Lemma 1.5,
\operatorname{Min}(\operatorname{Gr}(A))
is finite for every affinoid algebra
A
in the sense of Berkovich.

Proposition 2.16

(
[
39
]
, Proposition 3.3(ii), (iii))
.

Let
A
be an affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich. For every minimal prime homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}(\operatorname{Gr}(A))
, the pre-image
(\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}})^{-1}(\{\mathfrak{p}\})
consists of a single point and the set of such pre-images

(\operatorname{sp}^{\mathbb{R}_{>0}})^{-1}(\operatorname{Min}(\operatorname{Gr}(A))

is the Shilov boundary for
A
. In particular, the Shilov boundary for
A
is finite.

Finally, the Shilov boundary of a uniform normed
K
-algebra is intimately connected with the notion of topological divisors of zero, as studied, for example, by Escassut in
[
17
]
, see also Lemma
5.11
below for a related result. We recall the definition of topological divisors of zero below.

Definition 2.17

(Topological divisor of zero)
.

An element
f
in a seminormed ring or a Tate ring
A
is called a topological divisor of zero if there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
A
such that
f_{n}f\to 0
in
A
as
n\to\infty
, but the sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
is bounded away from zero (i.e., there exists a neighborhood of zero
U
in
A
which does not contain
f_{n}
for any
n
).

Note that any zero-divisor (in the usual, algebraic sense) is also a topological divisor of zero and that every element in the kernel of the seminorm is a topological divisor of zero. We will need the following consequence of Banach’s open mapping theorem.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
A
be a complete Tate ring and let
f\in A
be a non-zero-divisor. Then
f
is not a topological divisor of zero if and only if the ideal
(f)_{A}
generated by
f
is closed in
A
.

Proof.

In the following, we equip
(f)_{A}
with the subspace topology induced from
A
. Since
f
is not a zero-divisor, the
A
-linear, continuous multiplication-by-
f
map
\times f:A\to(f)_{A}
is injective, so it has an
A
-linear inverse
(f)_{A}\to A
. Since a map of topological
A
-modules is continuous if and only if it is continuous at zero, this inverse is continuous if and only if
f
is not a topological divisor of zero. Hence
A\to(f)_{A}
is a homeomorphism if and only if
f
is not a topological divisor of zero. But, by Banach’s open mapping theorem for complete Tate rings, see
[
26
]
, this map is a homeomorphism if and only if
(f)_{A}
is complete (for the subspace topology) which happens if and only if
(f)_{A}
is closed in the complete Tate ring
A
. ∎

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian complete Tate ring whose underlying ring is an integral domain. Then
A
has no non-zero topological divisors of zero.

Proof.

By
[
26
]
, Proposition 2.11, every ideal in a Noetherian complete Tate ring is closed. In particular, every principal ideal is closed. ∎

3.
Abhyankar valuations

Recall that any rank-
1
valuation
v
on a finitely generated field extension
L/K
satisfies Abhyankar’s inequality

\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(v(L^{\times})/v(K^{\times}))+\operatorname{tr.deg}(\widetilde{L}/\widetilde{K})\leq\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/K),

where the residue fields are the residue fields of
L
and
K
with respect to
v
and its restriction
v|_{K}
(
[
7
]
, Ch. VI, §10,
\no
3, Cor. 1). Recall also (for example, from
[
33
]
,
[
11
]
) that the valuation
v
is called Abhyankar if the above inequality is an equality. In this case, we also say that the finitely generated valued field extension
L/K
is Abhyankar.

Besides the notion of Abhyankar valuations on finitely generated field extensions, we also study a
related notion of (continuous) Abhyankar valuations on affinoid algebras (in the sense of Berkovich), which corresponds
precisely to the notion of Abhyankar points on Berkovich analytic spaces introduced by Ducros in

[
16
]
, 1.4.10.
Following
[
16
]
, 1.2.4 and A.4.11, we define, for any valued field extension
L/K
,

d_{K}(L)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}(v(L^{\times})/v(K^{\times}))+\operatorname{tr.deg}(\widetilde{L}/\widetilde{K}).

Furthermore, as in
[
16
]
, 1.4.6, we use the notation

d_{K}(v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}d_{K}(\mathcal{H}(v))

for a point
v
on a Berkovich space
X
, where, as usual,
\mathcal{H}(v)
denotes the completed residue field of
X
at
v
. Recall that if
X=\mathscr{M}(A)
for some Berkovich-affinoid algebra
A
, then
\mathcal{H}(v)
is completion of the field of fractions
Q(A/\ker(v))
of
A/\ker(v)
with respect to the absolute value induced by the bounded multiplicative seminorm
v:A\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
Note that a bounded multiplicative seminorm on
A
is the same thing as a continuous rank-
1
valuation on
A
over
K
, i.e., a continuous rank-
1
valuation which restricts to the given absolute value on the ground field
K
.

Definition 3.1

(Abhyankar valuation on an affinoid algebra)
.

Let

K
be a nonarchimedean field and let
A
be a
K
-affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich.
We call a continuous rank-
1
valuation on
A
over
K
an Abhyankar valuation (over
K
) if

d_{K}(v)=\inf_{V}\dim(A_{V}),

where
V
ranges over the affinoid domains of
\mathscr{M}(A)
containing
v
and where
A_{V}
is the affinoid algebra corresponding to an affinoid domain
V
.

By a result of Ducros, this definition simplifies considerably if
\mathscr{M}(A)
is purely
d
-dimensional, i.e., if every irreducible component of
\mathscr{M}(A)
has the same dimension
d
, in which case
d
is equal to the dimension of
A
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a
K
-affinoid algebra in the sense
of Berkovich over a nonarchimedean field
K
and suppose that its Gelfand spectrum

\mathscr{M}(A)
is purely
d
-dimensional. Then, for every affinoid domain
V
in

\mathscr{M}(A)
, the corresponding affinoid algebra
A_{V}
satisfies
\dim(A_{V})=\dim(A)=d
.
In particular, a continuous rank-
1
valuation on
A
over
K
is Abhyankar if and only if

d_{K}(v)=\dim(A).

Proof.

By
[
14
]
, Lemme 1.12, the dimension of
A_{V}
, for any affinoid domain
V
, is equal to the maximum of the dimensions of those irreducible components of
\mathscr{M}(A)
which have non-empty intersection with
V
. Since
\mathscr{M}(A)
is assumed to be purely
d
-dimensional, we see that
\dim(A_{V})=\dim(A)=d
. ∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
A
be a
K
-affinoid algebra in the sense
of Berkovich over a nonarchimedean field
K
and suppose that
A
, as a ring without topology,
is an integral domain. Then, for every affinoid domain
V
of
\mathscr{M}(A)
, we have

\dim(A_{V})=\dim(A)
. In particular, a continuous rank-
1
valuation
v
on
A
(over
K
) is
Abhyankar if and only if

d_{K}(v)=\dim(A).

Proof.

If
A
is an integral domain,
\mathscr{M}(A)
has only one irreducible component, so the assertion follows from Lemma
3.2
. ∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
A
be a
K
-affinoid algebra in the sense of
Berkovich over a nonarchimedean field
K
and let
v
be a continuous absolute value on
A
over

K
. Then
v
is Abhyankar if and only if

d_{K}(v)=\dim(A).

Proof.

Any ring on which there exists an absolute value is an integral domain. ∎

The following proposition provides a prototypical example of an Abhyankar absolute value.

Proposition 3.5
.

For any polyradii
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n}),(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
with
r_{i}\leq s_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
-Gauss norm
v
on the Tate algebra
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
is an Abhyankar valuation. Conversely, if a continuous absolute value
v
on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
(over
K
) is Abhyankar, it must be equal to the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
-Gauss norm for some polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
with
r_{i}\leq s_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

We first prove the forward implication. We proceed by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, the completed residue field
\mathcal{H}(v)
of
|\cdot|
is either a type II point or a type III point on the Berkovich affine line, so in this case the assertion follows from
[
29
]
, Corollary 2.27. Now assume that the assertion holds for some
n\geq 1
, and let
v
be the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n+1})
-Gauss norm on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
for some
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n+1}
with
r_{i}\leq s_{i}
for all
i
. Let
w
be the restriction of
v
to
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
. Then
v
extends to the
r_{n}
-Gauss norm on the one-variable Tate algebra
\mathcal{H}(w)\langle s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
over
\mathcal{H}(w)
. By the induction hypothesis,

d_{K}(\mathcal{H}(w))=n

and

d_{\mathcal{H}(v)}(\mathcal{H}(w))=1,

whence

d_{K}(v)=n+1,

as desired.

For the converse implication, we again use induction on
n
, where the base case
n=1
again follows
from the classification of points on the Berkovich affine line over
K
and
[
29
]
,
Corollary 2.27. Suppose that for some
n\geq 1
every Abhyankar valuation on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{l}^{-1}T_{l}\rangle
, for any
l\leq n
, is a Gauss norm and consider an
Abhyankar valuation
v
on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
. Let
w
be
the restriction of
v
to
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
and set

r_{i}=v(T_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,n+1
. We have
r_{i}\leq s_{i}
for all
i
since
v
is
continuous and restricts to the given absolute value on
K
, which implies that
v
is bounded above
by the norm on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
. If

r_{n+1}\in\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}(w)|^{\times}}
and the residue field of
\mathcal{H}(v)
is
algebraic over
\mathcal{H}(w)
, then, by the Abhyankar inequality (
[
7
]
, Ch. VI,
§10,
\no
3, Cor. 1) applied to the dense finitely generated subfield
K(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
of

\mathcal{H}(w)
,

\displaystyle d_{K}(v)=

\displaystyle d_{K}(\mathcal{H}(w))\leq n.

This contradicts the assumption that
v
is Abhyankar. Hence either the residue field of
\mathcal{H}(v)
has positive transcendence degree over that of
\mathcal{H}(v)
, or
r_{n+1}
is not in
\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}(w)^{\times}|}
. By
[
29
]
, Corollary 2.27, the canonical extension of
v
to
\mathcal{H}(w)\langle s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
is either a type II point or a type III point, i.e., it is equal to the
r_{n+1}
-Gauss norm on
\mathcal{H}(w)\langle s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
.
In particular,

d_{\mathcal{H}(w)}(\mathcal{H}(v))=1.

Since
v
is Abhyankar, we must then have

d_{K}(\mathcal{H}(w))=n,

so
w
is equal to the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
-Gauss norm on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
, by the induction hypothesis. In sum,

is equal to the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n+1})
-Gauss norm. ∎

By the same token, we have the following variant of
[
40
]
, Remark 2.1.2.

Proposition 3.6
.

For every polyradius
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
and every continuous absolute value
v
on
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
(over

), there exists a maximal subset
B
of
\{\,T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\,\}
such that the restriction

of

to

K\langle B\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K\langle s_{k}^{-1}T_{k}\mid T_{k}\in B\rangle

is Abhyankar. The completed residue field
\mathcal{H}(v_{B})
of

is of the form
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{l}}
for some polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
of length

with
r_{i}\leq s_{i}
for all

if and only if

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

. For

, the assertion follows from the classification of points (more precisely, from
[
29
]
, Corollary 2.27). Suppose that the assertion holds for some

, let

be a continuous absolute value on

over

and let

be the restriction of

to
K\langle s_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,s_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
. By the induction hypothesis, there exists a maximal subset

of
\{\,T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\,\}
with the property that the restriction
v_{B_{1}}
of

to

is Abhyankar, the completed residue field
\mathcal{H}(v_{B_{1}})
of

has the form
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{l}}
for

,

for all

, and the extension

the canonical extension of

to a continuous absolute value on
\mathcal{H}(v_{1})\langle s_{n+1}^{-1}T_{n+1}\rangle
and we again apply Berkovich’s classification of points (now over

We also obtain the following, more classical variant of the above proposition, for finitely generated field extensions.

Corollary 3.7

(Temkin
[

, Remark 2.1.2)
.

Let

be a finitely generated valued field extension

. Then there exists an algebraically independent subset

K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{l}}

(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})

Let

be the (rank-

be a transcendence basis of

and the field extension

is finite. Restricting

to

, we obtain a point on the Berkovich affine line over

. This point must lie on some polydisk, i.e., we can pick a polyradius
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}

of
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\}

-Gauss norm for some polyradius

Corollary 3.8
.

Let

be a topologically finitely generated nonarchimedean field extension, i.e., there exists a dense subfield of

. Then

factors as

for a polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
and a semi-immediate extension

.

Proof.

Follows immediately from Corollary
3.7
.∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let

be a nonarchimedean field extension of characteristic

which is perfectly topologically finitely generated, i.e.,

has a dense subfield which is the perfection of a finitely generated field extension of

. Then

factors as

for a polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})

.

Let

be a dense subextension of
L/K
which is the perfection of a finitely generated extension

. By Corollary

, there exists an algebraically independent subset

of

With these examples in mind, we continue to study the basic properties of Abhyankar valuations.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
A
be a
K
-affinoid algebra in the
sense of Berkovich over a nonarchimedean field
K
and let
v
be a continuous rank-
1

valuation on
A
over
K
. If
v
is Abhyankar, then

0pt(\ker(v))+d_{K}(v)=\dim(A).

Proof.

Suppose first that
A
is affinoid in the sense of Tate (i.e., strictly affinoid). By

[
15
]
, Théorème 2.13 (in the strictly affinoid case, this theorem is due to Kiehl,

[
32
]
, Theorem 3.3),
A
is excellent and, in particular, catenary, so if

0pt(\ker(v))>0
, then
\dim(A/\ker(v))<\dim(A)
. By Abhyankar’s inequality
(
[
7
]
, Ch. VI, §10,
\no
3, Cor. 1),

d_{K}(v)\leq\inf_{L/K}\operatorname{tr.deg}(L/K),

where
L/K
ranges over the dense finitely generated subextensions of
Q(A/\ker(v))/K
, and, by Noether normalization for the strictly affinoid algebra
A/\ker(v)
, the right hand side of this inequality is
\leq\dim(A/\ker(v))
. It follows that
d_{K}(v)<\dim(A)
if
A
is strictly affinoid and
\ker(v)
is a not minimal prime ideal of
A
.

Now let
A
be arbitrary and suppose that
v
is Abhyankar. By
[
14
]
, Lemma 1.12, for any inclusion
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
of affinoid domains,
\dim(A_{V^{\prime}})\leq\dim(A_{V})
. By
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.2.3 (iii), the strictly affinoid domains form a fundamental system of closed neighborhoods of
v
in
\mathscr{M}(A)
, so there exists a strictly affinoid neighborhood
U
of
v
such that

\dim(A_{U})=\inf_{V}\dim(A_{V}),

where
V
ranges over all affinoid neighborhoods of
v
. Since
v
is Abhyankar,

d_{K}(v)=\dim(A_{U});

in particular, (the extension of)
v
is also an Abhyankar valuation on
A_{U}
(the field
\mathcal{H}(v)
does not depend on whether we view
v
as a point of
\mathscr{M}(A)
or as a point of
U=\mathscr{M}(A_{U})
). Since
A_{U}
is strictly affinoid, the previous paragraph applies to
A_{U}
and shows that the kernel of
v
on
A_{U}
is a minimal prime ideal, so
\dim(A_{U}/\ker(v))=\dim(A_{U})
. But
\mathscr{M}(A_{U}/\ker(v))
is an affinoid domain of
\mathscr{M}(A/\ker(v))
, so we see that the valuation
\overline{v}
on
A/\ker(v)
induced by
v
is Abhyankar. We conclude by applying Corollary
3.3
to the affinoid integral domain
A/\ker(v)
and using once again the fact that
A
is a catenary ring. ∎

We also need the following lemma on finite field extensions.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
K\hookrightarrow L
be a finite field extension, let
v
be an absolute value on
L
and
w=v|_{K}
. Then the completion
\widehat{L}^{v}
of
L
with respect to

is also a finite extension of the completion
\widehat{K}^{w}
of
K
with respect to

.

Proof.

Choose a basis

of
L
over
K
. Since
L
is dense in
\widehat{L}^{v}
, the elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
span a dense finite-dimensional

-linear subspace of
\widehat{L}^{v}
. But, by
[

]
, Proposition 4.13, there is only one norm topology on any finite-dimensional vector space over the nonarchimedean field
\widehat{K}^{w}
, and this norm topology is complete. It follows that the dense
\widehat{K}^{w}
-linear subspace of
\widehat{L}^{v}
spanned by
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
is already complete and thus coincides with
\widehat{L}^{v}
. In particular,
\widehat{L}^{v}
is a finite field extension of
\widehat{K}^{w}
, as desired. ∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
A\hookrightarrow B
be a finite extension of affinoid algebras in the sense of Berkovich and let

be a continuous absolute value on
B
(over
K
). Then

is Abhyankar if and only if the restriction

of

to

Proof.

The field extension

is finite, hence, by Lemma

, so is the field extension

. It follows that

, and the assertion follows from Corollary

. ∎

We can now finally prove the main theorem of this section, which characterizes Abhyankar absolute
values on an Berkovich-affinoid algebra
A
in terms of the Shilov boundaries of affinoid domains of

\mathscr{M}(A)
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
K
be a nonarchimedean field, let
A
be a
K
-affinoid
algebra in the sense of Berkovich and let
v
be a continuous absolute value on
A
(over
K
).

(1)

If
v
is Abhyankar, then there exists a rational domain
V
(in the
sense of Berkovich) in
\mathscr{M}(A)
containing
v
such that (the extension of)
v
belongs to
the Shilov boundary for
A_{V}
.

(2)

Conversely, if there exists a strictly affinoid domain
V
of

\mathscr{M}(A)
containing
v
such that
v
belongs to the Shilov boundary for
A_{V}
, then
v
is Abhyankar.

The proof of the above theorem is an example of a technique which we term “Shilov localization”,
that is, reduction to the case when
A
is an algebra for which
v
is the only element in the
Shilov boundary.
For this, we use Temkin’s notion of graded reductions (see Definition

2.11
),
and in fact we need a few preparations on graded rings.

Recall that a
G
-graded ring
A
is called a
G
-graded integral domain if every non-zero
homogeneous element in
A
is a non-zero-divisor (other elements in
A
are allowed to be
zero-divisors). A homogeneous ideal of a
G
-graded ring
A
is an ideal generated by homogeneous
elements. A homogeneous ideal

4.
Topologically simple valuations and seminorms

Recall that an element

in a Hausdorff topological ring

is called a topological unit (or topologically invertible) if there exists a net
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
in

such that

. This is equivalent to saying that the principal ideal generated by

is a dense subset of

. Whenever

has open unit group (for example, if

is a Zariskian Huber ring), every topological unit is already a unit in

. However, this need not be true for more general topological rings.

Definition 4.1

(Topologically simple topological rings and modules)
.

(1)

A topological module
M
over a topological ring
A
is called topologically simple if the closure of the zero submodule is the only closed submodule of
M
.

(2)

A topological ring
A
is said to be topologically simple if it is topologically simple as a topological module over itself, i.e. if its only closed ideal is the closure of the zero ideal.

Remark 4.2
.

The term ’topologically simple’ is meant to bring to mind an obvious analogy with the notion of a simple module in algebra. It is also commonly used in the theory of Banach algebras without unit.

Lemma 4.3
.

A topological ring
A
is
topologically simple if and only if every nonzero element
f
of the quotient
\overline{A}=A/\overline{(0)}
by the closure of the zero ideal is a topological unit.

Proof.

It is clear that
A
is topologically simple if and only if
\overline{A}
is topologically simple. Hence it suffices to assume that
A
is Hausdorff. Since the closure of an ideal in a topological ring is always a (not necessarily proper) ideal,
A
being topological simple is equivalent to every ideal of
A
being dense. This, in turn, is equivalent to every principal ideal being dense and, since
A
is Hausdorff, this is the same every element of
A
being a topological unit. ∎

We can also talk about valuations and seminorms being topologically simple. Since we want to include both the case of a valuation which is not necessarily of rank one and of a seminorm which is not necessarily multiplicative, we phrase our definition in terms of generalized seminorms, in the sense of Paugam
[

Definition 4.4

(Pseudo-uniformizer)

Let

(in particular,

(Topologically simple valuations and seminorms)

\widehat{\overline{K}}
having empty intersection. Consider the type
(IV)
point corresponding to this nested sequence of disks, i.e., the multiplicative seminorm

|f|_{A}:=\inf_{n} |f|_{B(a_{n},r_{n})}\ \ (f\in A).

Let
\mathfrak{m}\subsetneq{A}
be a maximal ideal. By Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz,
\mathfrak{m}
is of the form
\ker(|\cdot|_{a})
for some point
a\in\overline{K}
with
|f|_{a}=|f(a)|
(
f\in A
). The maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is closed if and only if the seminorm
|\cdot|_{a}
is bounded by
|\cdot|_{A}
. But in this case
|\cdot|_{a}\leq|\cdot|_{B(a_{n},r_{n})}
for every
n
. In particular, for every
n
we have
|a-a_{n}|=|T-a_{n}|_{a}\leq\sup_{b\in B(a_{n},r_{n})}|T-a_{n}|_{b}=\sup_{b\in B(a_{n},r_{n})}|b-a_{n}|\leq r_{n}
. That is,
a\in\bigcap_{n}B(a_{n},r_{n})
, a contradiction.

Conversely, assume that the completed algebraic closure
\widehat{\overline{K}}
of the ground field

K
is spherically complete and let
|\cdot|_{A}
be a norm on
A=K[T]
restricting to the
given absolute value on
K
. We want to show that
|\cdot|_{A}
is not topologically simple.
If
|\cdot|_{A}
is topologically simple, then every element in the Gelfand spectrum of the normed ring
(A,|\cdot|_{A})
is an absolute value. Besides, if
|\cdot|_{A}
is topologically simple, then every absolute value
|\cdot|
bounded above by
|\cdot|_{A}
is topologically simple as well (an ideal which is closed with respect to
|\cdot|
is also closed with respect to the stronger norm
|\cdot|_{A}
). Hence it suffices to prove our claim in the case when
|\cdot|_{A}
is an absolute value, i.e., when the norm
|\cdot|_{A}
defines a point on the Berkovich affine line
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\operatorname{an}}
. Since
\widehat{\overline{K}}
is spherically complete,
\lVert\cdot\rVert
must then be of the form

|f|_{A}=|f|_{B(a,r)}\ \ (f\in A)

for some closed disk
B(a,r)
in
\widehat{\overline{K}}
. Consider the multiplicative seminorm
|\cdot|_{a}
on
K[T]
given by
|f|_{a}=|f(a)|
for
f\in A
(evaluation at the centre of the disk). Then clearly
|\cdot|_{a}\leq|\cdot|_{A}
, so
|\cdot|_{a}
is continuous with respect to the norm
|\cdot|_{A}
. Hence the maximal ideal
\ker(|\cdot|_{a})
is closed with respect to the the topology defined by
|\cdot|_{A}
. In particular, the norm
|\cdot|_{A}
is not topologically simple.∎

We also have the following variant of the theorem for any closed disk.

Theorem 4.15
.

For
K
a nonarchimedean field, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The completed algebraic closure
\widehat{\overline{K}}
of
K
is spherically complete.

(2)

There exists no topologically simple norm
|\cdot|
on
K\langle T\rangle
bounded above by the Gauss norm.

(3)

There exists
r>0
such that there is no topologically simple norm
|\cdot|
on
K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle
bounded above by the Gauss norm.

(4)

For all
r>0
, there exists no topologically simple norm
|\cdot|
on
K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle
bounded above by the Gauss norm.

More precisely, for any nonarchimedean field
K
and any radius
r>0
, the topologically simple absolute values on
K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle
bounded above by the Gauss norm are precisely the type
(IV)
points in the closed ball
B(0,r)
.

Proof.

The equivalence of the first and last conditions is exactly Theorem
4.14
, since the Berkovich affine line
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\operatorname{an}}
can be written as a union of Berkovich disks
\mathscr{M}(K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle)
of varying radius
r>0
. In particular, condition (1) implies all of the other conditions. Thus it remains to show that, if
\widehat{\overline{K}}
is not spherically complete and
r>0
is any radius, there exists a topologically simple norm
|\cdot|
on
K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle
bounded above by the Gauss norm. But for any nested sequence of disks in
\widehat{\overline{K}}
with empty intersection, we can shift centers of the disks, and possibly pass to a subsequence, to obtain an analogous nested sequence of disks which are contained in the disk
B(0,r)
. But then the corresponding type
(IV)
point
|\cdot|
becomes bounded by the Gauss norm on
K\langle r^{-1}T\rangle
. This concludes the proof since we have already seen in Theorem
4.14
that every type
(IV)
point is a topologically simple absolute value.∎

Using Theorem
4.14
, we can now describe all completions of the two-variable polynomial ring (or the two-variable Tate algebra) with respect to topologically simple absolute values.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
K
be a nonarchimedean field, and let
n\in N
,
and let

be a topologically simple absolute value on
the polynomial ring

.
Let

denote the restriction of

to

K[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1}]
.
Then, the canonical extension o

to

\mathcal{H}(w)[T_{n}]
is
a type
(IV)
point.
Moreover, this holds for any permutation of the variables

T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
.

Similarly, for
A=K\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\rangle
,

v
topologically simple on
A
,
and
w
the restriction
to
K\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1}\rangle
,
we have that the canonical extension of

to

is a type (IV) point.
Moreover, this holds for any permutation of the variables

.

Proof.

In the first case,

is dense in its completion, and thus so is

(by the universal

and Lemma

).
Then, by Theorem

We may permute the variables first and the argument remains unchanged.
To prove the statement for the Tate algebra,

with Theorem

∎

4.1.
Topologically simple valuations and Prüfer-Manis valuations

We conclude this section by relating the notion of topologically simple valuations to the notion of so-called Prüfer-Manis valuations studied in the book
[
34
]
of Knebusch and Zhang. This relationship will also reveal that the property of a (possibly higher-rank) valuation being topologically simple does not depend on the choice of a pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi
.

Definition 4.17
.

Let
v:R\to\Gamma\cup\{0\}
be a valuation on
R
.
Then
v
is
Manis
if

v(R^{\times})=c\Gamma_{v}
, i.e., if the value semigroup
v(R\setminus\{0\})
of
v
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
R
be a ring,
and let
A
be a subring of
R
and

\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
A
.
Then
(A,\mathfrak{p})
is a

Manis pair
if

(A,\mathfrak{p})=(A_{v\leq 1},A_{v<1})

for some Manis valuation
v
.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
A\subset R
be an extension of rings.
The extension is called a Prüfer
extension, or
A
is called a Prüfer subring of
R
, if

(A_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathfrak{m}A_{\mathfrak{m}})
is a Manis pair in

R_{\mathfrak{m}}
for
all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
A
.

Definition 4.20

(Prüfer-Manis valuation)
.

A valuation
v:A\to\Gamma\cup\{0\}
on a ring
R
is called Prüfer-Manis if
v
is a Manis valuation and the closed unit ball
R_{v\leq 1}
is a Prüfer subring of
R
. A Prüfer-Manis subring of
R
is a subring of the form
A_{v\leq 1}
for some Prüfer-Manis valuation
v
on
R
, i.e., it is a Prüfer subring which is the closed unit ball of a Manis valuation
v
on
R
.

We recall the following result of Knebusch-Zhang.

Proposition 4.21

(
[
34
]
, Ch. I, Prop. 5.1(iii))
.

Let
v
be a valuation on a ring
R
. Then
R_{v\leq 1}
is a Prüfer-Manis subring of
R
if and only if it is a Prüfer-Manis subring of
R
. In other words, if a Prüfer subring
R_{0}
of
R
is also the closed unit ball of a valuation
v:R\to\Gamma\cup\{0\}
(in the terminology of
[
34
]
,
R_{0}
is a valuation subring of
R
), then the valuation
v
must automatically be Manis.

We also recall the following observation from
[
13
]
which relates the notion of Prüfer subrings studied by Knebusch-Zhang to the notion of a
\varpi
-valuative ring considered in the book
[
20
]
of Fujiwara-Kato.

Proposition 4.22

(
[
12
]
, Proposition 4.20)
.

Let
\varpi
be a non-zero-divisor in a ring
A
. Then
A
is a Prüfer subring of
A[\varpi^{-1}]
if and only if
A
is a
\varpi
-valuative ring in the sense of Fujiwara-Kato
[
20
]
, i.e., every finitely generated ideal
I
of
A
which contains a power of
\varpi
is invertible.

One of the useful properties of Prüfer subrings proved in the book of Knebusch-Zhang is their stability under taking quotients.

Proposition 4.23

(
[
34
]
, Ch. I, Prop. 5.7-5.8)
.

Let
A
be a Prüfer subring of a ring
R
and let
\varphi:R\to S
be a ring map. Then
\varphi(A)
is a Prüfer subring of
\varphi(R)
. If
I
is an ideal of a ring
R
contained in a subring
A
, then
A
is a Prüfer subring of
R
if and only if
A/I
is a Prüfer subring of
R/I
.

Corollary 4.24
.

Let
v
be a valuation on a ring
R
. Then
v
is Prüfer-Manis if and only if the induced valuation
\overline{v}
on the quotient
\overline{R}=R/\ker(v)
is Prüfer-Manis.

Proof.

By definition of the induced valuation on
\overline{R}
, we have

\overline{R}_{\overline{v}\leq 1}=R_{v\leq 1}/\ker(v).

Since
\ker(v)
is an ideal of
R
contained in
R_{v\leq 1}
, Proposition
4.23
tells us that
R_{v\leq 1}
is a Prüfer subring of
R
if and only if
\overline{R}_{\overline{v}\leq 1}
is a Prüfer subring of
\overline{R}
. We conclude by applying Proposition
4.21
.∎

Finally, we recall the following theorem of Knebusch-Zhang.

Theorem 4.25

(
[
34
]
, Ch. III, Theorem 1.8)
.

Let
A
be a Prüfer-Manis subring of a ring
R
. Then
A
has only one maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\mathfrak{m}R=R
. If
v
is a Prüfer-Manis valuation such that
A=R_{v\leq 1}
, then
\mathfrak{m}=R_{v<1}
.

Theorem 4.26
.

Let
v:R\to\Gamma\cup\{0\}
be a non-trivial valuation on a ring
R
. Then
v
is Prüfer-Manis if and only if it is topologically simple. Moreover, in this case, the Tate ring
R
with pair of definition
(R_{\leq 1},\varpi)
is topologically simple for every choice of pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi
of
v
.

Proof.

Set
A=R_{v\leq 1}
. Let
\varpi\in A
be a pseudo-uniformizer of
v
. By Corollary
4.24
, we may assume that
\ker(v)=0
.

Suppose that
v
is Prüfer-Manis. Note that
R=A[\varpi^{-1}]
. By Theorem
4.25
,
R_{v<1}
is the unique maximal ideal of
A
containing
\varpi
. Hence, if

\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
A
, then either
\varpi\in\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}
is
contained in
R_{v<1}
, or
(\mathfrak{p},\varpi)_{A}=A
, in which case
\mathfrak{p}\cap(1+(\varpi)_{A})\neq\emptyset
. It follows that the
\varpi
-adic Zariskisation

A^{\operatorname{Zar}}=(1+(\varpi)_{A})^{-1}A

is a local ring. By Proposition
4.22
,
A
is a
\varpi
-valuative ring, so
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
is also a
\varpi
-valuative ring, being a localization of
A
(
[
20
]
, Ch. 0, Proposition 8.7.2). Since
\ker(v)=0
, we know that
A
is
\varpi
-adically separated. Then
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
is also
\varpi
-adically separated (
[
38
]
, Lemma 3.19(2)). By
[
13
]
, Proposition 4.23, it then follows that
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
is
\varpi
-adically separated valuation ring and thus
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is a field. By
[
38
]
, Lemma 3.5,
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is precisely the Zariskisation
R^{\operatorname{Zar}}
of the Tate ring
R
, with pair of definition
(A,\varpi)
. We conclude that
R^{\operatorname{Zar}}
is a field and thus that the valuation
v
is topologically simple, by Lemma
4.11

Conversely, suppose that
v
is topologically simple. Then, by Lemma
4.11
,
R^{\operatorname{Zar}}
is a field and thus
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
(which coincides with the closed unit ball of the canonical extension of
v
to
R^{\operatorname{Zar}}
) is a valuation ring. In particular,
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
is local. Since
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}/\varpi=A/\varpi
, this implies that
A
has only one maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
containing
\varpi
. Since
1+(\varpi)_{A}\subseteq A\setminus\mathfrak{m}
, the map
A\to A_{\mathfrak{m}}
factors through
A\to A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
; therefore,
A_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a localization of the valuation ring
A^{\operatorname{Zar}}
and thus itself a valuation ring. In particular,
A_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a
\varpi
-valuative ring. But for any other maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\neq\mathfrak{m}
of
A
, we know that
\varpi
is a unit in
A_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}
, so
A_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}
is (trivially) also a
\varpi
-valuative ring. By
[
20
]
, Ch. 0, Proposition 8.7.3, we see that
A
is itself
\varpi
-valuative. By Proposition
4.22
, this means that
A
is a Prüfer subring of
R
, in which case the valuation
v
with
A=R_{v\leq 1}
is Prüfer-Manis, by Proposition
4.21
.∎

5.
Topologically simple valuations are not Abhyankar

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
from the introduction, i.e., we show that the two conditions on a continuous absolute value on an affinoid
algebra
A
we have studied so far, namely, “Abhyankar” and “topologically simple”, are mutually
exclusive for affinoid algebras in the sense of Tate (i.e., strictly affinoid algebras).
Before we start, let’s exposit an example that makes such a statement rather subtle.

Example 5.1
.

Let
A=K\langle X\rangle\langle r/X\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K\langle X,T/r\rangle/(X(T/r)-1)
, where

r\not\in\sqrt{|K^{\times}|}
and
r<1
.
Then,
A
is an affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich, but not in the sense of Tate.
Geometrically,
\mathscr{M}(A)
is an annulus with an irrational interior radius
r
,
and is thus a subspace of the ball.
Let
v
denote the Gauss norm of radius
r
on
A
.
Then
v
is in the Shilov boundary of
A
,
so
v
is Abhyankar.
Moreover, there are no points (classical, type II or III, hence neither type IV) that lie in

\mathscr{M}(A)

which have radius less than
r
,
so the
r
-Gauss norm does not dominate
any points of
\mathscr{M}(A)
.
Thus, there are no closed ideals in the valuation topology determined
by
v
, so
v
is also topologically simple.

This example also exhibits
K_{r}
as an affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich:
Note that
A\langle X/r\rangle
, the raional localization of
A
at the domain

\{|X|\leq r\}
, has a single point (namely
v
) in its Gelfand spectrum.
Note from the conditions
|X|\leq r
and
r\leq|X|
,
we conclude that
|X|=r
, so by the characterization of
K_{r}
for
irrational radii (cite backgound section) we have
A\langle X/r\rangle=K_{r}
.

This sort of “hole with irrational radius” example is an obstruction to our statement.
However, if we only consider strict affinoids, then we may hope that
such an obstruction never happens–heuristically, we hope that
every “subdomain with irrational radius” has some sort of slightly smaller subdomain
which has rational radius.
By the classification of points, this heuristic is true for

K\langle X\rangle
, and even for
K\langle r^{-1}X\rangle
when
r

is irrational.
The underlying philosophy of our proof is that strongly Shilov neighborhoods
preclude
such phenomena as in Example
5.1
.
Moreover, there are anough strongly Shilov neighborhoods to find a Shilov localization
for any Abhyankar valuation
v
which is also strongly Shilov.

For a Banach algebra over a nonarchimedean field
K
, let
\mathcal{X}(A,K)
denote the set of bounded
K
-algebra homomorphisms
\chi:A\to L
onto finite extension fields of
K
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
be a strictly affinoid algebra in the sense of Berkovich (i.e., an affinoid algebra in the sense of Tate) and let
V\subseteq\mathscr{M}(A)
be an affinoid domain. If
I\subsetneq A_{V}
is an ideal such that
\mathscr{M}(A_{V}/I)\subseteq\mathscr{M}(A_{V})=V
has non-empty interior in
\mathscr{M}(A)
, then the spectral seminorm on
A_{V}/I
satisfies

|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}=\sup_{\chi\in\mathcal{X}(A_{V}/I,K)}|\chi(f)|.

Proof.

Fix
f\in A_{V}/I
and set
r=|f|_{\operatorname{sp}}
. We have to prove that for every

\epsilon>0
there exists some
\chi\in\mathcal{X}(A_{V}/I,K)
such that
\chi(f)>r-\epsilon
.
Fix
\epsilon>0
. By compactness of the Gelfand spectrum, there exists
\phi\in V=\mathscr{M}(A_{V}/I)
with
\phi(f)=r
and thus the open subset

U=\{\,\phi\in\mathscr{M}(A_{V}/I)\mid\phi(f)>r-\epsilon\,\}

of
\mathscr{M}(A_{V}/I)
is non-empty. But
\mathcal{X}(A,K)
, viewed as a subset of
\mathscr{M}(A)
, is dense in
\mathscr{M}(A)
, so
\mathcal{X}(A_{V}/I,K)
is also dense in
\mathscr{M}(A_{V}/I)
, as
\mathscr{M}(A/I)
has non-empty interior (it is dense in the interior of
\mathscr{M}(A/I)
, since that interior is an open subset of
\mathscr{M}(A)
, and the interior of
\mathscr{M}(A/I)
is dense in
\mathscr{M}(A/I)
). In particular, there exists an element
\chi\in\mathcal{X}(A_{V}/I,K)
which belongs to the non-empty open subset
U
of
V
. This concludes the proof.∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A
be a Banach ring and let
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
be a polyradius. Then the Banach
A
-algebra

A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle

is flat as a Banach

-module, i.e., for every strict monomorphism
M\hookrightarrow N
of Banach
A
-modules, the induced map

M\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle\to N\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle

is a strict monomorphism.

Proof.

As a Banach
A
-module, we can write

A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}A\cdot T_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot T_{n}^{i_{n}},

where
T_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot T_{n}^{i_{n}}
has the norm
r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}}
. Thus

A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}A_{r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}}},

where, for a Banach
A
-module

the Banach
A
-module whose underlying

-module is

and whose norm is the norm of
M

. For every Banach

-module

M\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A_{s}=M_{s}
. Hence, for every bounded homomorphism of Banach
A
-modules
M\hookrightarrow N
, the map

M\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle\to N\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle

coincides with the map

\widehat{\bigoplus}_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}M_{r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}}}\to\widehat{\bigoplus}_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}N_{r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\penalty\ \dots\penalty\ \cdot r_{n}^{i_{n}}}

and the latter map is clearly a strict monomorphism if
M\to N
is. ∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For any Banach ring
A
and any polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in(0,1]^{d}
, the map

A\langle T_{1},\dots,d\rangle\to A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{d}^{-1}T_{d}\rangle

For every Banach

-module

, we have

\displaystyle M\widehat{\otimes}_{A\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\rangle}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{d}^{-1}T_{d}\rangle=M\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{A\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\rangle}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{d}^{-1}T_{d}\rangle

\displaystyle =M\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{d}^{-1}T_{d}\rangle,

whence the assertion follows by Lemma

. ∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K\langle T_{1},\dots,T_{d}\rangle\hookrightarrow A

(i.e., a finite, strict, injective homomorphism). Then, for any radii

, the induced map

Proof.

The map is finite by Lemma

, so it remains to prove that this map is a strict monomorphism. But this follows from Lemma

.∎

Lemma 5.6
.

For some polyradius
(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
, let

Lemma 5.7
.

Lemma 5.9

The following lemma is one of the most technical parts of our proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 5.12

(“Monstrous lemma”)
.

Let
A
be a reduced affinoid algebra in the sense of
Berkovich and suppose that, for every minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
, the affinoid
algebra
A/\mathfrak{p}
is strongly Shilov. Suppose further
that for every minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
the map
A^{\circ}\to(A/\mathfrak{p})^{\circ}
is integral. Then
A
is strongly Shilov.

Proof.

Fix a pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi
of
A
(for example, a pseudo-uniformizer of
K
). Since
\varpi
is a non-zero-divisor in
A^{\circ}
and
A=A^{\circ}[\varpi^{-1}]
, the minimal prime ideals of the reduced ring
A
correspond to the minimal prime ideals of
A^{\circ}
. In particular, there are only finitely many minimal prime ideals of
A^{\circ}
, the affinoid algebra
A
being Noetherian. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}
be the minimal prime ideals of
A^{\circ}
and set
A_{i}=A/\mathfrak{p}_{i}A
. For every element
\phi\in\mathscr{M}(A)
, the kernel
\ker(\phi)
of
\phi
is a prime ideal and thus contains one of the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}A
; hence,
\phi
must belong to
\mathscr{M}(A_{i})
for some
i
. It follows that the map

A\hookrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}

is an isometry when
A
is endowed with its spectral norm and the product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}
is endowed with the maximum of the spectral norms on the affinoid algebras
A_{i}
. This implies that the union of the Shilov boundaries
\mathcal{S}_{i}
of the irreducible components
\mathscr{M}(A_{i})
is a (closed) boundary for
A
. In particular,

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{S}_{i},

where
\mathcal{S}
is the Shilov boundary for
A
.

Let
\phi
be an element of
\mathscr{M}(A)
which belongs to the Shilov boundary for some
A_{i}
.
Let
\phi^{\prime}
denote the induced element of
\mathscr{M}(A_{i})
.
By the strongly Shilov condition, there exists a minimal open prime
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
of
A_{i}^{\circ}
such that
\phi^{\prime}
is the unique pre-image of
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
under the specialization map

\operatorname{sp}_{A_{i}}:\operatorname{Spa}(A_{i},A_{i}^{\circ})\to\operatorname{Spf}(A_{i}^{\circ})=\operatorname{Spec}(\widetilde{A_{i}}).

By hypothesis, the map
A^{\circ}\to A_{i}^{\circ}
is integral, so the integral domain
A_{i}^{\circ}
is an integral extension of
A^{\circ}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. By
[
13
]
, Lemma 6.17, the restriction
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\cap(A^{\circ}/\mathfrak{p}_{i})
is a minimal open prime ideal of
A^{\circ}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Hence it lifts to a minimal open prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
A^{\circ}
containing
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Since
\phi^{\prime}
had center
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\cap(A^{\circ}/\mathfrak{p}_{i})
on
A^{\circ}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, we see that
\phi
has center
\mathfrak{q}
on
A^{\circ}
.

It follows that every element
\phi
of
\mathscr{M}(A)
which belongs to the Shilov boundary of one of the irreducible components
\mathscr{M}(A_{i})
has a minimal open prime ideal of
A^{\circ}
for its center (on
A^{\circ})
. Thus

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{S}_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A})),

which implies that
\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A}))
is a boundary.

Recall that
\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A})
is a finite set since
\widetilde{A}
is Noetherian. Hence, if we can prove that the pre-image of every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A})
consists of a single, rank-
1
point, then
\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A}))
is finite and, in particular, closed in
\mathscr{M}(A)
. Since we have seen that
\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\operatorname{Min}(\widetilde{A}))
is a boundary, it then follows from
[
13
]
, Theorem 6.20, that the pre-image of any minimal open prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A^{\circ}
under the map

\operatorname{sp}:\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})\to\operatorname{Spf}(A^{\circ})

is a single rank-
1
point. From now on, we set

R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A^{\circ}

and

R_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{i}^{\circ}.

By
[
13
]
, Proposition 6.18, the above statement on pre-images of minimal open primes is equivalent to proving that, for every minimal open prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, the
\varpi
-adic completion of the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a valuation ring of rank
1
.

Fix a minimal open prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
.

Claim 5.13
.

R_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is a field.

Proof of Claim.

Consider the subset
I
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
,
i\in I
, are precisely the minimal prime ideals of
R
contained in
\mathfrak{p}
. We have a natural injective map

R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\prod_{i\in I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})_{\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\circ}}=\prod_{i\in I}R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}R_{\mathfrak{p}}

is injective. It is obviously also an integral map. By assumption, the maps

(5.1)

R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}\hookrightarrow R_{i}

are integral maps between integral domains, so any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
R_{i}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is minimal over
\varpi
(i.e., a minimal open prime) and the map

(R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})_{\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\to R_{i\mathfrak{q}}

is an integral extension. For every

, let
\{\mathfrak{q}_{i,j}\mid j\in J_{i}\,\}
be the set of minimal open prime ideals of
R_{i}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Note that the index set must be finite since each
R_{i}
has only finitely many minimal open prime ideals. We obtain an injective integral map

R_{\mathfrak{p}}\hookrightarrow\prod_{i\in I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})_{\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\circ}}\hookrightarrow\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in J_{i}}R_{i\mathfrak{q}_{i,j}}

(the map is integral since the index set of the product is finite and any product of finitely many monic polynomials is monic).

Now, by the assumption that each
A_{i}
is strongly Shilov and
[
13
]
, Theorem 6.20, we know that the
\varpi
-adic completion of each of the local rings
R_{i\mathfrak{q}_{i,j}}
is a valuation ring of rank
1
. By
[
13
]
, Proposition 4.23 and Corollary 4.24, this actually implies that the separated quotient of each
R_{i\mathfrak{q}_{i,j}}
is a valuation ring (of rank
1
). But by Corollary
2.19
and Lemma
5.11
, the local rings
R_{i\mathfrak{q}_{i,j}}
are already
\varpi
-adically separated. Therefore, they are valuation rings of rank
1
. In particular,

is a field for all

,

. But then the integral injective map

R_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varpi^{-1}]\hookrightarrow\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in J_{i}}R_{i\mathfrak{q}_{i,j}}[\varpi^{-1}]

(inverting

commutes with the product here since the product is finite) shows that

is a zero-dimensional ring. Since this ring is reduced (it injects into a product of fields), it must then be a von Neumann regular ring. If
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is not a field, it must then have (a lot of) non-trivial idempotent elements. But
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is integrally closed in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varpi^{-1}]
, so every idempotent element of

must belong to
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This produces a contradiction since a local ring cannot have any non-trivial idempotent elements. It follows that

is a field, as we claimed.
∎

But this implies that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is in particular an integral domain, so
\mathfrak{p}
contains only one minimal prime ideal of

, namely, the kernel of
R\to R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let us assume that this minimal prime ideal is
\mathfrak{p}_{1}

\mathfrak{q}
be any minimal open prime of

\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{1}
of

R_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varpi^{-1}]
is a field, we get an algebraic field extension
R_{\mathfrak{p}}[\varpi^{-1}]\hookrightarrow R_{1\mathfrak{q}}[\varpi^{-1}]
.

Moreover, we know from the strongly Shilov assumption on

and
[
13
]

R_{1\mathfrak{q}}
is a valuation ring of rank
1
. Indeed, by the cited theorem, its
\varpi
-adic completion is a valuation ring of rank

; by
[
13
]

is a valuation ring of rank

R_{1\mathfrak{q}}
is already
\varpi
-adically separated. Now, the fact that

is a valuation ring of rank

We can now finally prove the main result of this section, which was announced as Theorem

in the introduction and which states that the notions of Abhyankar valuation and topologically simple valuation are mutually exclusive for continuous valuations on a strictly affinoid algebra.
The proof proceeds by finding a Shilov localization which is strongly Shilov using the Abyankar
condition, and deducing that such a neighborhood cannot exist for a topologically simple valuation.

Theorem 5.18

Let

be a nonarchimedean field, let

be a continuous absolute value on

By way of contradiction, suppose that

is both topologically simple and Abhyankar. Let

is not a field). By Lemma
3.12
,

restricts to an Abhyankar continuous absolute value on

3.5
, this means that

restricts to the
(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
-Gauss norm on

(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in(0,1]^{d}

of the polydisk

be the corresponding affinoid algebra. By abuse of notation, we denote the canonical extension of

to

(and to any further rational localization) by the same letter

. By Lemma
5.5
, the map

is finite, injective and strict. Since

of

of the Gauss norm on

. By Corollary

On the other hand, by

Lemma 7.7

, Proposition 4.5)
.

Let
K
be a perfectoid field of characteristic
p
and let
X_{\infty}=\operatorname{Spa}(A_{\infty},A^{+}_{\infty})

be the completed perfectoid of an affinoid rigid-analytic variety

\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{+})
.
Let

\psi\colon X_{\infty}\to\mathbb{B}_{K}^{n,\operatorname{perfd}}

be an étale map.
Then we can find a finite etale morphism

X_{\infty}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}_{K}^{n,\operatorname{perfd}}
.

Proof.

Since the étale site is stable under perfection,
any such étale map
\psi
must
be a completed perfection of a map

X\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}^{n}

at the noetherian level.
Then, apply
[
41
, Corollary D.5]

to get a finite étale map at the noetherian
level, and by the stability of the étale
site again, this induces a finite étale
map at the perfectoid level.
∎

Corollary 7.21
.

Let
T_{n+1,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}=K\langle T_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}},\ldots,T_{n+1}^{1/p^{\infty}}\rangle

be the perfectoid Tate algebra in
n+1

variables.
Then there exists a quotient field
L
of

T_{n+1,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
such that

L
has
K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}

as a finite separable extension.

Proof.

The map
T_{n+2,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}\twoheadrightarrow K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}
defined in
Corollary
7.19

contains the ideal
(T_{1}\cdots T_{n}-c)

in its kernel.
Thus, it factors through the
zariski closed quotient

A_{\infty}=T_{n+2,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}/(T_{1}\cdots T_{n}-c)_{\operatorname{sp}}
,
where the subscript
\operatorname{sp}
denotes the
spectral radical
(see
[
12
]
).
Since
I
is an ideal in a perfectoid Tate ring of characteristic
p
,

I_{\operatorname{sp}}
is obtained by including all

p
-th roots of elements in
I

and then taking the (topological) closure, see
[
12
]
, Remark 2.21.
The spectral radical is also called the

dagger nilradical
in the literature
(see
[
8
]
). We see that
A_{\infty}
is the completed
perfection the strictly affinoid algebra

A=T_{n+2,K}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}-c)
, which is of Krull dimension
n+1
.

Now, we claim that there exists
an open immersion

\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
.
Indeed,

A_{\infty}
is the rational localization of

T_{n+2,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
corresponding
to the condition
\{c\leq|T_{1}T_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot T_{n}|\}
.
By
[
27
]
, Lemma 3.10 (and the fact that perfection preserves the adic spectrum and its
rational subsets), this implies that
A
is a rational localization of
T_{n+2,K}
.

Moreover, we can observe that

\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})=S_{1}\times_{K}\mathbb{B}^{1}
,
where

S_{1}=\{c\leq|T_{1}T_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot T_{n}|\}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Thus, by Theorem
7.20
,
there exists a finite étale map

T_{n+1,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}\xrightarrow{}A_{\infty}
.
Let
L
be the image of the composite map
T_{n+1,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}\to A_{\infty}\xrightarrow{}K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
.
Then
L
has
K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
as a finite
separable extension.
∎

Corollary 7.22
.

The field
L
in Corollary
7.21

has the following property:
There exists some polyradius
r_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{n}^{\prime}

such that
L
is a semi-immediate extension
of
K_{r_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{n}^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}

Proof.

As
L
is the surjective image of a perfectoid Tate algebra,
it is perfectly topologically finitely generated.
By Corollary
3.9
, there exists a polyradius
(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime})
such
that
L
is a semi-immediate extension of
K_{r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
, so
it remains to prove
that
l=n
. Since
L
has the finite extension
K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}/L
, we have

d_{K}(L)=d_{K}(K_{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}}^{\operatorname{perfd}})=n.

Since

L/K_{r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}}^{\operatorname{perfd}}
is semi-immediate, we must then have

n=d_{K}(L)=d_{K}(K_{r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{perfd}})=l,

as desired.
∎

Remark 7.23
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the kernel of the map

T_{n+1,K}^{\operatorname{perfd}}\twoheadrightarrow L
.
Note that by Theorem
6.6
,
we conclude that
\mathfrak{m}\cap T_{n+1,K}^{\operatorname{coperf}}

is
(0)
.
Note that we can see this more elementarily
by noting that the noether normalization map is injective
(étale and thus dominant).

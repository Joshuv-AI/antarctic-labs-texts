Axiomatizing Rumsfeld Ignorance
Jie Fan

Abstract
In a recent paper, Kit Fine presents some striking results concerning the logical properties of (first-order) ignorance, second-order ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance. However, Rumsfeld ignorance is definable in terms of ignorance, which makes some existing results and the axiomatization problem trivial. A main reason is that the accessibility relations for the implicit knowledge operator contained in the packed operators of ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance are the same. In this work, we assume the two accessibility relations to be different so that one of them is an arbitrary subset of the other. This will avoid the definability issue and retain most of the previous validities. The main results are axiomatizations over various proper bi-frame classes. Finally we apply our framework to analyze Fine’s results.



Keywords: ignorance, Rumsfeld ignorance, second-order ignorance, axiomatizations

3
Minimal Logic

This section proposes the minimal logic of
\mathbf{IRI}
and shows its soundness with respect to the class of proper bi-frames.

3.1
Proof system and Soundness

Definition 10
.

The minimal logic of
\mathbf{IRI}
, denoted
\mathbb{IRIK}
, consists of the following axioms and inference rules, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{TAUT}&\text{all instances of propositional tautologies}\\
\text{I-Equ}&\textit{I}\varphi\leftrightarrow\textit{I}\neg\varphi\\
\text{IR-Equ}&\textit{I}^{R}\varphi\leftrightarrow\textit{I}^{R}\neg\varphi\\
\text{I-Con}&\textit{I}(\varphi\land\psi)\to(\textit{I}\varphi\vee\textit{I}\psi)\\
\text{I-Dis}&\textit{I}(\varphi\vee\psi)\land\textit{I}(\neg\varphi\vee\chi)\to\textit{I}\varphi\\
\text{RI-I}&\textit{I}^{R}\varphi\to\textit{I}\varphi\\
\text{MIX}&\textit{I}\varphi\land\textit{I}(\textit{I}\varphi\vee\chi)\to\textit{I}^{R}\varphi\\
\text{R-NI}&\dfrac{\varphi}{\neg\textit{I}\varphi}\\
\text{RE-I}&\dfrac{\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi}{\textit{I}\varphi\leftrightarrow\textit{I}\psi}\\
\text{RE-RI}&\dfrac{\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi}{\textit{I}^{R}\varphi\leftrightarrow\textit{I}^{R}\psi}\\
\text{R-MIX}&\dfrac{\textit{I}\chi_{1}\land\cdots\land\textit{I}\chi_{n}\to\textit{I}\varphi}{(\neg\textit{I}^{R}\chi_{1}\land\textit{I}\chi_{1})\land\cdots\land(\neg\textit{I}^{R}\chi_{n}\land\textit{I}\chi_{n})\to\neg\textit{I}^{R}\varphi}\\
\end{array}

The axioms and inference rules concerning the operator
I
only are equivalent transformations of the corresponding ones concerning the operator
Kw
, also denoted
\Delta
, in the literature, see e.g.
[
20
,
9
]
. Moreover,
RI-I
,
MIX
, and
R-MIX
concern the interactions between Rumsfeld ignorance and first-order ignorance:
RI-I
says that Rumsfeld ignorance implies first-order ignorance,

tells us how to derive Rumsfeld ignorance; in particular, by letting

\varphi

The notions of theorems, provability, and derivation are defined as usual.

\neg\textit{I}^{R}\varphi\land\textit{I}\varphi\in s

∎

With Lindenbaum’s Lemma and Truth Lemma in hand, it is then a standard exercise to show the following.

Theorem 20

(Completeness)
.

\mathbb{IRIK}
is sound and strongly complete with respect to the class of all proper bi-frames.

4
Extensions

With the minimal logic in hand, it is natural to consider the extensions of
\mathbb{IRIK}
over five basic class of frames (that is, serial, reflexive, transitive, symmetric, and Euclidean).
In this section, we axiomatize
\mathbf{IRI}
over the class of serial frames and the class of reflexive frames. Other extensions are left for future work.

4.1
Serial logic

We now show that
\mathbb{IRIK}
is also sound and strongly complete with respect to the class of serial proper bi-frames.

Theorem 21
.

\mathbb{IRIK}
is sound and strongly complete with respect to the class of serial proper bi-frames.

12

12

Proof.

Define
\mathcal{M}^{c}
as in Def.

is serial (in fact, reflexive), but

is not serial, since some states

may have no

-successors. We call such states

R^{c}
. We fix this issue with a strategy called ‘reflexivizing the endpoints’

16

9

, namely, adding an arrow
R^{c}
from each endpoint in

to itself. Formally,

. Denote the resultant model as
\mathcal{M}^{\bf D}=\langle S^{c},R^{\bf D},R^{\bullet c},V^{c}\rangle
. Now
\mathcal{M}^{\bf D}
is serial. Moreover, for all
\varphi\in\mathbf{IRI}
, for all

, we have
\mathcal{M}^{c},s\vDash\varphi

\mathcal{M}^{\bf D},s\vDash\varphi
. The nontrivial cases are
\textit{I}\varphi
and
\textit{I}^{R}\varphi
. If

is an endpoint w.r.t.

\mathcal{M}^{c},s\nvDash\textit{I}\varphi
and
\mathcal{M}^{\bf D},s\nvDash\textit{I}\varphi

\mathcal{M}^{c},s\nvDash\textit{I}^{R}\varphi
and
\mathcal{M}^{\bf D},s\nvDash\textit{I}^{R}\varphi

\mathcal{M}^{\bf D}
is also proper, since

is reflexive and thus

.
∎

4.2
Reflexive logic

Adding the following axiom to
\mathbb{IRIK}
, we obtain the proof system
\mathbb{IRIT}
:

\begin{array}[]{lr}(\text{I-T})&\varphi\land\textit{I}(\varphi\vee\psi)\to\textit{I}\varphi\\
\end{array}

We will show that
\mathbb{IRIT}
is sound and strongly complete with respect to the class of reflexive proper bi-frames. For soundness, by Thm.
20
, it suffices to show the validity of axiom (I-T) on such a class. As a matter of fact, (I-T) is valid on a larger class of frames, namely, the class of reflexive bi-frames (that means we do not restrict the frames to be proper).

Proposition 22
.

Axiom (I-T) is valid on the class of reflexive bi-frames.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle S,R,R^{\bullet},V\rangle
be reflexive and
s\in S
. Suppose that
\mathcal{M},s\vDash\varphi\land\textit{I}(\varphi\vee\psi)
, then
s\vDash\varphi
and there exists
t
such that
sRt
and
t\nvDash\varphi\vee\psi
, then
t\nvDash\varphi
. Since
R
is reflexive,

. Therefore,
s\vDash\textit{I}\varphi
.
∎

For completeness, again, define
\mathcal{M}^{c}

is not reflexive. In this case, the strategy of ‘reflexivizing the endpoints’ does not work, since it cannot guarantee

Definition 23

The

It is easy to check that

Lemma 24
.

For all

Proof.

By

Suppose that

6
Concluding words

In this paper, we demonstrated that under the usual semantics, Rumsfeld ignorance is definable in terms of ignorance, which makes some existing results and the axiomatization problem trivial. A main reason is that the accessibility relations for the implicit knowledge operator contained in the packed operators of ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance are the same. We then proposed a bi-semantics, in which the two accessibility relations are different. We showed that under the bi-semantics with the restriction that one relation is an arbitrary subset of the other, Rumsfeld ignorance is undefinable with ignorance, and we can also retain most of the validities in the case when the two accessibility relations are the same. We then axiomatized the logic of ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance over various proper bi-frame classes. We finally applied our framework to analyze Fine’s results in
[
11
]
.

Although in our framework, some Fine’s validities, for instance
(2^{\ast})
(mentioned in the introduction), are lost, most of Fine’s validities, for instance
(1)
,
(2)
,
(3)
, and
(4)
, are retained; most importantly, we avoid the issues of definability of Rumsfeld ignorance with ignorance and the triviality of the axiomatization problem of the logic of ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance.

Note that our axiomatizations are infinitary. For the future work, we hope to find a finite axiomatization for the minimal logic of ignorance and Rumsfeld ignorance, and its extensions over other special frame classes, such as S4, KD45 and S5. Another work is axiomatizing the logic of propositional logic with Rumsfeld ignorance as a sole primitive modality. Moreover, when it comes to the multi-agent case,
\neg\textit{I}_{a}p\land\textit{I}_{b}\neg\textit{I}_{a}p
is read that “
a
knows whether
p
but
b
is ignorant about that”, which seemingly expresses a kind of secret. We can investigate the logical properties of such kind of secret.

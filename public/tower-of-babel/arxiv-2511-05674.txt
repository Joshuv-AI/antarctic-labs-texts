\{k\}
-Roman, then
k\leq k\gamma(G)=\gamma_{\{Rk\}}(G)\leq|V(G)|
, where the last inequality follows from the observation that assigning
1
to each vertex of
G
yields a
\{k\}
-Roman dominating function of
G
.
On the other hand, it can be easily verified that the
n
-vertex complete graph is
\{k\}
-Roman for all
2\leq k\leq n
.

For an integer
t\geq 3
, the
t
-sun
is a split graph on
2t
vertices with split partition
(K,I)
where
K=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\}
is a clique and
I=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{t}\}
is an independent set such that
u_{i}
is adjacent to
w_{j}
if and only if
i=j
or
i=j+1
(mod
t
).

Note that the
t
-sun has a unique split partition.
We denote the
t
-sun by
S_{t}
and its complement by
\overline{S_{t}}
.

See
Figure

1
for examples of
t
-suns.

Examples of the
3
-sun (left) and the

-sun (right).

Observation 3.13
.

The cycle graph
C_{t}
is a
2
-uniform hypergraph such that
S_{t}
is strongly compatible with
C_{t}
.

We can first prove that for all
k\geq 2
and
t\geq 3
, the
t
-sun
S_{t}
is
\{k\}
-Roman if and only if
k=2
and
t
is even.

Theorem 3.14
.

For every integer
t\geq 3
, the following holds.

1.

The graph
S_{t}
is a
\{2\}
-Roman graph if and only if
t
is even.

2.

The graph
S_{t}
is not a
\{k\}
-Roman graph for any
k\geq 3
.

Proof.

Since the cycle graph
C_{t}
has a perfect matching if and only if
t
is even, the first item follows directly from
3.13
and
Theorem

2.6
.
Let now
k\geq 3
.
Since by
Lemma

3.11
, every
\{k\}
-Roman graph is a
\{k-1\}
-Roman graph, no
t
-sun with
t
odd is a
\{k\}
-Roman graph, since it is not
\{2\}
-Roman, by the first item.
Assume now that
t\geq 4
is even, let
(K,I)
be the split partition of
S_{t}
, let
u
be any vertex in
K
and consider the function
f:V(S_{t})\to\{0,1,2\}
such that
f(v)=1
if
v\in I\cup\{u\}
and
f(v)=0
otherwise. Because every vertex in
K
has two neighbors in
I
and either is
u
or is adjacent to
u
,
f
is a
\{3\}
-Roman dominating function with weight
f(S_{t})=t+1
. Thus,
\gamma_{\{R3\}}(S_{t})\leq t+1
.
By
3.13
and
Lemma

2.4
, we obtain
\gamma(S_{t})=\rho(C_{t})=\lceil\frac{t}{2}\rceil
.
It follows that
\gamma_{\{R3\}}(S_{t})\neq 3\gamma(S_{t})
, since
t+1<3\frac{t}{2}=3\gamma(S_{t})
.
Therefore, since for even
t
, no
t
-sun is a
\{3\}
-Roman graph, no
t
-sun is a
\{k\}
-Roman graph for
k\geq 3
, by
Lemma

3.11
.
∎

Now let us turn our attention to complements of
t
-suns.

Observation 3.15
.

Let
t\geq 3
and let
(K,I)
be the split partition of
S_{t}
.
Then, its complement
\overline{S_{t}}
is a graph strongly compatible with a
(t-2)
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
K
.

The
(t-2)
-uniform hypergraph from
3.15
has
t
vertices and, hence, can only have a perfect matching if
t
is a multiple of
t-2
.
Since this is not the case for any
t\geq 5
,
3.15
and
Theorem

2.6
imply that for all
t\geq 5
, the graph
\overline{S_{t}}
is not
\{t-2\}
-Roman (and, hence, also not
\{k\}
-Roman for any
k\geq t-2
, by
Lemma

3.11
).
In the next theorem, we strengthen this observation by showing that, for integers
k\geq 2
and
t\geq 3
, the graph
\overline{S_{t}}
is
\{k\}
-Roman if and only if
k=2
and
t\in\{4,5\}
.

Theorem 3.16
.

For every integer
t\geq 3
, the following holds.

1.

The graph
\overline{S_{t}}
is a
\{2\}
-Roman graph if and only if
t\in\{4,5\}
.

2.

The graph
\overline{S_{t}}
is not a
\{k\}
-Roman graph for any
k\geq 3
.

The proof of
Theorem

3.16
will rely on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.17
.

If
t\geq 5
, then
\gamma({\overline{S}_{t}})=2
.

Proof.

Let
(K,I)
be the split partition of
S_{t}
.
Since
S_{t}
does not have isolated vertices,
\overline{S}_{t}
does not have universal vertices and so
\gamma(\overline{S}_{t})>1
.
Let
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
be two vertices in
I
that have no neighbors in common in
S_{t}
.
Consider the set of vertices
D=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
of
\overline{S}_{t}
. Note that, in
\overline{S}_{t}
, vertex
v_{1}
is adjacent to every vertex but its two neighbors in
S_{t}
.
Furthermore, since
v_{2}
has no neighbors in common with
v_{1}
in
S_{t}
, vertex
v_{2}
is adjacent in
\overline{S}_{t}
to the non-neighbors of
v_{1}
. Therefore
D
is a dominating set in
\overline{S}_{t}
, implying that
\gamma(\overline{S}_{t})=2
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.16
.

For
t=3
, it is easy to check that
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{3})\leq 4<6=2\gamma(\overline{S}_{3})
.
Hence, by
Lemma

3.11
,
\overline{S}_{3}
is not
\{k\}
-Roman for any
k\geq 2
.

For
t=4
,
\overline{S}_{4}
is isomorphic to
S_{4}
, and it is clear from
Theorem

3.14
that
\overline{S}_{4}
is a
\{2\}
-Roman graph and not a
\{k\}
-Roman graph for any
k\geq 3
.

Suppose that
t=5
.
To prove the first part of the statement, observe that by
Lemma

3.17
,
\gamma(\overline{S}_{5})=2
, hence, showing that
\overline{S}_{5}
is a
\{2\}
-Roman graph is equivalent to showing that
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{5})\geq 4
.
To this end, let
(K,I)
be the split partition of
\overline{S}_{5}
. From
Lemma

3.1
we know that there exists a
\gamma_{\{R2\}}
-function
f
of
\overline{S}_{5}
that assigns weight at least
2
to
K
.

Suppose first that there exists
v\in K
with
f(v)=2
. Then, there exist exactly two vertices
x,y\in I
that are not adjacent to
v
in
\overline{S}_{5}
. Since by the definition of a
\{2\}
-Roman dominating function, we need a weight of at least
2
on
N[\{x,y\}]
, we conclude that in this case
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{5})=f(\overline{S}_{5})\geq 4
.

Suppose now that there exists
v_{1},v_{2}\in K
with
f(v_{1})=f(v_{2})=1
, and note that
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
can have either one or two common neighbors in
I
.
Consider first the case when they have two common neighbors in
I
.
Then there exists
x\in I
that is adjacent to neither
v_{1}
nor
v_{2}
(see
Figure

2
).

Figure 2:
The complement of the
5
-sun with vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in K
and
x,y_{1},y_{2}\in I
, where vertex
x
is not adjacent to
v_{1}
nor
v_{2}
, vertex
y_{1}
is adjacent to
v_{1}
but not to
v_{2}
, and vertex
y_{2}
is adjacent to
v_{2}
but not to
v_{1}
.

Now either
f(N[x])\geq 2
, in which case
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{5})=f(\overline{S}_{5})\geq 4
, or
f(x)=1
.
In that case, since
v_{1}\in N[y_{1}]
and
f(v_{1})=1
, we can observe that
f(N[y_{1}])\geq 2
(either
f(y_{1})\geq 1
, or another neighbor of
y_{1}
has weight at least
1
), where
y_{1}
is adjacent to
v_{1}
and not to
v_{2}
.
So in this case
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{5})=f(\overline{S}_{5})\geq 4
as well.
It remains to consider the case when
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
have one common neighbor in
I
.
Denote by
x_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, the remaining vertices in
I
.
Now each
x_{i}
either has weight at least
1
, or there is a vertex distinct from
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in its neighborhood that has weight at least
1
.
Since the vertices
x_{i}
have no common neighbor, we need a weight of at least
2
on
(\bigcup_{i\in\{1,2,3,4\}}N[x_{i}])\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
, which will again result in
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{5})=f(\overline{S}_{5})\geq 4
.

For the second part of the statement, by
3.15

\overline{S}_{5}
is strongly compatible with a 3-uniform hypergraph
H
.
Then, from
Theorem

2.6
,
\overline{S}_{5}
is not a
\{3\}
-Roman graph since the hypergraph
H
does not have a perfect matching.
And again by
Lemma

3.11
,
\overline{S}_{5}
is not a
\{k\}
-Roman graph for any
{k\geq 3}
.

Finally, consider the case
t\geq 6
.
Now, let
(K,I)
be the split partition of
S_{t}
.
Since
t\geq 6
, we can always choose vertices
x,y,z\in I
such that no two of them have a common neighbor in
S_{t}
.
Let
f:V(\overline{S}_{t})\to\{0,1,2\}
such that
f(x)=f(y)=f(z)=1
and
f(v)=0
otherwise.
Note that, if
v\in K
, then
v
is adjacent in
\overline{S}_{t}
to at least two vertices among
x,y
, and
z
, thus
f(N(v))\geq 2
.
Next, if
v\in I\setminus\{x,y,z\}
, then, since
I
is a clique in
\overline{S}_{t}
, vertex
v
will be adjacent in
\overline{S}_{t}
to the vertices
x,y,z
, i.e.,
f(N(v))=3
.
Therefore,
f
is a
\{2\}
-Roman dominating function of
\overline{S}_{t}
, implying
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{t})\leq 3
.
From
Lemma

3.17
it follows that
\overline{S}_{t}
is not a
\{2\}
-Roman graph, since
\gamma_{\{R2\}}(\overline{S}_{t})\leq 3<4=2\gamma(\overline{S}_{t})
.
By
Lemma

3.11
,
\overline{S}_{t}
is not a
\{k\}
-Roman graph for any
{k\geq 2}
.
∎

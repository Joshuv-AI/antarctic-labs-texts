Homogeneous spaces in tensor categories
Kevin Coulembier, Alexander Sherman

Abstract
. Let 𝒞 \mathscr{C} be a symmetric tensor category of moderate growth, and let ℋ ≤ 𝒢 \mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G} be algebraic groups in 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . We prove that the homogeneous space 𝒢 / ℋ \mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H} exists as a scheme and is of finite type when 𝒞 \mathscr{C} is geometrically reductive and maximally nilpotent, conditions that are conjecturally equivalent to incompressibility. A key tool is the introduction of a Frobenius kernel of a group scheme. We further show that while 𝒢 0 / ℋ 0 \mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0} and ( 𝒢 / ℋ ) 0 (\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})_{0} need not be the same, they are close enough, so that 𝒢 / ℋ \mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H} is quasi-affine/affine/proper if and only if 𝒢 0 / ℋ 0 \mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0} is.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field.
A (symmetric) tensor category in the sense of
[
11
,
16
]
is a
\Bbbk
-linear abelian symmetric rigid monoidal category satisfying some finiteness properties, and this concept axiomatises the representation categories of group schemes. A finiteness condition that is typically not included in the definition of a tensor category is that of moderate growth. This condition demands that the length of the tensor powers of any given object grows at most exponentially fast, and is clearly satisfied for representation categories of affine group schemes.

A classical theorem of Deligne in
[
12
]
states that, if the characteristic of
\Bbbk
is zero, every tensor category over
\Bbbk
of moderate growth is the representation category of an affine group superscheme (a supergroup). In other words, the study of tensor categories of moderate growth then becomes the study of representation theory of supergroups. An essential tool in the representation theory of affine group schemes is the quotient scheme
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
of an affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
by a subgroup
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
. A standard example is the flag variety
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{B}
for a reductive group
\mathcal{G}
with Borel subgroup
\mathcal{B}
. By Deligne’s theorem, it becomes imperative to prove the existence of such quotient superschemes for supergroups. This was achieved (in all characteristics) in
[
27
,
28
]
. Homogeneous supervarieties were used crucially in
[
5
,
19
,
33
]
for computing characters of irreducible representations of supergroups, and more recently in
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
to apply ideas from modular representation theory to representations of supergroups over
\mathbb{C}
.

If the characteristic of
\Bbbk
is
p>0
, then only partial results about the structure theory of tensor categories of moderate growth are available. It is a general fact that every tensor category of moderate growth admits a tensor functor to an ‘incompressible’ tensor category, see
[
10
]
, where the latter categories are defined by the property that every tensor functor out of them is an embedding of a tensor subcategory. Deligne’s theorem can then be rephrased as stating that the only incompressible tensor categories in characteristic zero are the categories of (super) vector spaces. In general, it thus follows that the study of tensor categories of moderate growth becomes equivalent to the representation theory of affine group schemes internal to incompressible tensor categories.

In
[
3
]
, it was conjectured that in characteristic
p>0
the only incompressible tensor categories are the tensor subcategories of an incompressible category
\mathrm{Ver}_{p^{\infty}}=\cup_{n}\mathrm{Ver}_{p^{n}}
. A partial result in this direction is that every ‘Frobenius exact’ tensor category of moderate growth admits a tensor functor to
\mathrm{Ver}_{p}
, see
[
9
]
and references therein. For the same reasons as in characteristic zero, it thus becomes important to study the existence of quotients
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
in some scheme theory for
\mathrm{Ver}_{p^{\infty}}
, or (potentially) more general incompressible tensor categories. In
[
7
,
8
]
, the basics of algebraic geometry in tensor categories satisfying some technical conditions (maximal nilpotency and geometric reductivity) were developed. These conditions seem a plausible intrinsic characterisation of incompressible categories; for instance, they are satisfied in
\mathrm{Ver}_{2^{\infty}}
. The question of whether homogeneous spaces
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
exist as schemes in such tensor categories is thus well-defined, conjecturally equivalent to the corresponding question for
\mathrm{Ver}_{p^{\infty}}
, and we answer it affirmatively in the current paper. In particular, this proves existence of homogeneous spaces in
\mathrm{Ver}_{2^{\infty}}
and
\mathrm{Ver}_{p}
.

Henceforth, we work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>0
. As explained above, the relevant geometric questions in characteristic 0 are already well understood. Our approach relies heavily on the idea of Frobenius twisting, only available in positive characteristic. For the case of super vector spaces, this specific positive characteristic method to study homogeneous spaces was outlined in
[
28
]
. In our generality of tensor categories, we define Frobenius twists and kernels of algebraic groups. One of the crucial observations is that the quotient by a large enough Frobenius kernel is an ordinary algebraic group over
\Bbbk
. This fundamental structural result of Proposition
4.19
, that all finite type affine group schemes in
\mathscr{C}
are glued together from infinitesimal group schemes and ordinary group schemes over
\Bbbk
, should have applications beyond the current paper. Using the traditional formalism of quotients in the category of faisceaux
[
13
]
and the language of algebraic geometry in tensor categories
[
8
]
allows us to reduce the existence of quotient schemes to the classical case of algebraic groups over
\Bbbk
and the infinitesimal case in
\mathscr{C}
. We also derive applications of our results to equivariant sheaves and present a more explicit description of quotients for the important special case
\mathscr{C}=\mathrm{Ver}_{p}
, following the approach of
[
27
]
.

The geometric properties of the scheme
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
are known to have important consequences in representation theory, especially for the induction functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}(-)
. For this reason, it is important to know when
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is quasi-affine, affine, or proper, see for instance
[
8
]
. We show that
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is (quasi-)affine or proper if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
is, where
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is the ‘body’ of
\mathcal{G}
, the underlying algebraic group which is generally more easily understood than
\mathcal{G}
. This result is known in the super setting, where in fact we have that
(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
. We find that the latter equality fails when
\mathscr{C}
is not a tensor subcategory of
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, see Example
6.1
. Nevertheless, there is a natural closed embedding
\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}\to(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})_{0}
, and this is a universal homeomorphism, see Theorem
7.5
(2).

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we recall and establish some basic properties of maximally nilpotent and geometrically reductive tensor categories, and scheme theory therein. In Section
3
we focus on affine group schemes in such categories. In Section
4
we introduce and study a notion of the Frobenius twist of schemes and the corresponding Frobenius kernels of affine group schemes. In Section
5
we write out the classical idea of constructing homogeneous spaces via the theory of faisceaux in the generality we need, and we illustrate the technique in Section
6
by giving a direct construction of homogeneous spaces for additive group schemes. Section
7
contains the main result about the existence of quotients
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
and their essential properties. In Section
8
we discuss affine homogeneous spaces. Finally, in Section
9
, we give a more detailed description of homogeneous spaces for the important special case
\mathrm{Ver}_{p}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We work throughout over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>0
.

2.1.
Tensor categories

An essentially small,
\Bbbk
-linear symmetric monoidal category
(\mathscr{A},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
will be called a tensor category (over
\Bbbk
) if

(1)

\mathscr{A}
is abelian,

(2)

\Bbbk\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{End}(\mathbf{1})
,

(3)

(\mathscr{A},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
is rigid, and

(4)

each object of
\mathscr{A}
is of finite length.

Given an object
X
in a tensor category
\mathscr{A}
, we write
X^{*}
for its dual and
S(X)
for its symmetric algebra in the ind-completion of
\mathscr{A}
.

Throughout this paper, we will work over a fixed tensor category
\mathscr{C}_{fin}
, and we write
\mathscr{C}:=\operatorname{Ind}\mathscr{C}_{fin}
for the ind-completion of
\mathscr{C}_{fin}
. By an abuse of terminology, we will also refer to
\mathscr{C}
as a tensor category. The full subcategory
\mathscr{C}_{fin}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
consists exactly of the objects of
\mathscr{C}
of finite length, or equivalently those which are compact, or yet equivalently rigid. Note that
\mathscr{C}
is a symmetric monoidal category with an exact tensor product, is abelian, and every object is a union of its finite length subobjects. The classical case of our considerations is
\mathscr{C}=\mathrm{Vec}
, the category of all vector spaces over
\Bbbk
.

We will use the term tensor functor to refer to exact
\Bbbk
-linear symmetric monoidal functors between tensor categories, or the cocontinuous extensions of those functors to the ind-completions.
We have the tensor functor
\operatorname{Vec}\to\mathscr{C}
,
V\mapsto V\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathbf{1}
, and we will freely use its lax monoidal left adjoint
\mathscr{C}\to\operatorname{Vec}
which takes the maximal ‘invariant’ subobject (or subalgebra in case we apply it to an algebra). Concretely,
for
X\in\mathscr{C}
, we will write
X_{(0)}=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{1},X)
, viewed canonically as a subobject of
X
.

For
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
, we write
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,Y)
for the internal
\operatorname{Hom}
in
\mathscr{C}
, which is defined by the adjunction

\operatorname{Hom}(Z\otimes X,Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(Z,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,Y)).

Note that
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
gives a bifunctor
\mathscr{C}^{op}\times\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
. We will also write
X^{\ast}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(X,\mathbf{1})
, which coincides with the dual whenever
X\in\mathscr{C}_{fin}
.

Let
R
be an algebra in
\mathscr{C}
(by this we technically mean a monoid object in
\mathscr{C}
) and let
S\subseteq R
be a subalgebra. Then for a right
S
-module
N
and a right
R
-module
M
, we define the right
R
-module
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(R,N)
by

(2.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(R,N))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(M,N).

Alternatively, one may define
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(R,N)
by the equalizer:

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(R,N)\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(R,N)\rightrightarrows\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(R\otimes S,N).

In this way we see that
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S}(R,N)
may be viewed as a subobject of
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(R,N)
.

2.1.1.
Properties GR and MN

Henceforth we will fix a tensor category
\mathscr{C}
and, as explained and motivated in the introduction, assume that
\mathscr{C}
satisfies the following restrictive assumptions introduced in
[
7
]
:

(GR)

a non-zero morphism
\alpha:X\to\mathbf{1}
is split on some symmetric power of
\alpha
.

(MN1)

if
L
is a simple nontrivial module, then
S(L)
is finite.

(MN2)

if
\alpha:\mathbf{1}\to X
is nonsplit, then some symmetric power of
\alpha
is 0.

Note that in
[
7
]
it was assumed that the object
X
in (GR) and (MN2) is compact, but it is straightforward to show that this is equivalent to having the property hold for all
X\in\mathscr{C}
.

Example 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a classical affine algebraic group, then
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is a tensor category which satisfies (GR) if
\mathcal{G}
is (geometrically) reductive. In contrast, the combination (MN1-2) is only satisfied when
\mathcal{G}
is trivial.

Example 2.2
.

Assume
\Bbbk
is not of characteristic
2
, and let
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{SVec}
, the category of super vector spaces. Then
\mathscr{C}
is semisimple and admits two simple objects,
\mathbf{1}
and
\bar{\mathbf{1}}
. We have
S(\bar{\mathbf{1}})=\mathbf{1}\oplus\bar{\mathbf{1}}
. From these observations it is easy to check that
\mathscr{C}
satisfies (GR) and (MN1-2).

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
(see
[
31
]
),
the Verlinde-
p
category. Explicitly,
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
may be defined as the ind-completion of the semisimplification of the category of finite-dimensional
C_{p}
-modules, where
C_{p}
is the cyclic group of order
p
. Recall that
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
is semisimple, has
p-1
simple objects
L_{1}=\mathbf{1},\dots,L_{p-1}
, and satisfies
S^{k+1}(L_{p-k})=0
for
0<k<p-1
(
[
18
, Prop. 2.4]
), so that (GR) and (MN1-2) hold. Notice that
\operatorname{SVec}
is the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
generated by
L_{1}
and
L_{p-1}
when
p>2
. Further, if we write
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}^{+}
for the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
generated by
L_{i}
for
i
odd, then
\operatorname{Ver}^{+}_{p}
is a tensor subcategory of
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, and thus also satisfies (GR) and (MN1-2).

For an object
X\in\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, we write
X=X_{(0)}\oplus X_{\not=0}
, where
X_{\not=0}
is a direct sum of
L_{i}
,
i\not=1
.

We refer to
[
2
,
3
]
for details on the incompressible categories
\operatorname{Ver}_{p^{n}}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Example 2.4
.

The category
\mathscr{C}:=\operatorname{Ver}_{4}^{+}
is described, as a monoidal category, as modules over the Hopf algebra
\Bbbk[x]/x^{2}
. The braiding
V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
is given by
v\otimes w\mapsto w\otimes v+xw\otimes xv
. Since
\operatorname{Ver}_{4}^{+}
has no nontrivial simple objects, (MN-1) holds trivially.
To check (GR) and (MN-2), we see that
\mathscr{C}
has a unique nontrivial indecomposable
P
, and there is a short exact sequence

0\to\mathbf{1}\to P\to\mathbf{1}\to 0.

Writing
P=\Bbbk\langle u,v\rangle
, where
x\cdot v=u
, one may compute that:

S(P)=\Bbbk[u,v]/u^{2}

Thus the image of the second symmetric power of
\mathbf{1}\to P
vanishes, and similarly the second symmetric power of
P\to\mathbf{1}
splits. From this we may deduce that (GR) and (MN-2) hold. More generally, it was proven in
[
10
, §9]
that
\operatorname{Ver}_{2^{\infty}}
satisfies (GR) and (MN1-2).

Remark 2.5
.

In
[
7
]
, it was conjectured that
\operatorname{Ver}_{p^{\infty}}
satisfies (GR) and (MN1-2) also when
p>2
. Indeed, the expectation (or hope) is that all incompressible, moderate growth tensor categories satisfy (GR) and (MN1-2). We also refer to
[
7
, §3.2]
for partial results on showing that (MN1-2) implies incompressiblity.

Remark 2.6
.

The assumptions (GR) and (MN1-2) imply the geometry (developed in
[
8
]
) should share characteristics similar to supergeometry, where there is an underlying classical geometry along with some extra infinitesimal ‘odd’ parts that come along for the ride. In particular, this setting leaves the door open for a general theory of Harish-Chandra pairs for algebraic groups, which was developed in the
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
case in
[
40
]
and in the super case in several papers (e.g.
[
26
]
). Preliminary work suggests that a theory of Harish-Chandra pairs may hold in these categories, but it will be weaker in the sense that one has to replace the Lie algebra with an infinitesimal group larger than the one determined by the small enveloping algebra. Developing such a theory will be taken up in future work.

Definition 2.7
.

For an object
X
in
\mathscr{C}
, let us write
X_{nil}
for the joint kernel of all maps to
\mathbf{1}
. This defines an endofunctor of
\mathscr{C}
, which is neither monoidal nor exact; however we always have

(2.2)

(X\otimes Y)_{nil}\subseteq X_{nil}\otimes Y+X\otimes Y_{nil}.

2.2.
Commutative algebra

Fundamentals of commutative algebra and scheme theory in
\mathscr{C}
were developed in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. Throughout this paper, if
A
is a commutative algebra in
\mathscr{C}
then we will write
m_{A}=m
for its multiplication morphism and
\eta_{A}=\eta:\mathbf{1}\to A
for its unit. We say that a commutative algebra
A
is finitely generated if there exists a compact object
X
in
\mathscr{C}
and an algebra morphism
S(X)\twoheadrightarrow A
that is an epimorphism in
\mathscr{C}
. A consequence of our assumptions on
\mathscr{C}
is that finitely generated algebras are Noetherian, see
[
7
, Lem. 6.4.4]
, so in particular ideals are finitely generated.

From now on, an ‘algebra’ will always refer to an algebra in
\mathscr{C}
. When we want specifically to refer to an ordinary algebra we will say
\Bbbk
-algebra or algebra in
\mathrm{Vec}
. The same rule will be followed for (affine group) schemes.

Every commutative algebra
A
in
\mathscr{C}
has a natural quotient algebra and subalgebra in
\mathrm{Vec}
, as follows. Write
J=J_{A}
for the ideal generated by
A_{nil}\subseteq A
, and set
\bar{A}:=A/J
. Then
\bar{A}
and
A_{(0)}
will be commutative algebras in
\mathrm{Vec}
, and we have morphisms of algebras

(2.3)

A_{(0)}\hookrightarrow A\twoheadrightarrow\bar{A}.

Moreover, the assignments
A\mapsto A_{(0)}
and
A\mapsto\bar{A}
are functorial, and the morphisms in (
2.3
) define natural transformations.

2.2.1.
Artin-Rees lemma

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a commutative Noetherian algebra
R
in
\mathscr{C}
and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module.

(1)

The Artin-Rees lemma holds in
\mathscr{C}
: if
N\subseteq M
is a submodule, then there exists
k\geq 0
such that for
n\geq k

I^{n}M\cap N=I^{n-k}(I^{k}M\cap N).

(2)

The Krull intersection theorem holds in
\mathscr{C}
: if
R
is a local algebra and
I
is a proper ideal of
R
, then
\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}I^{n}=0
.

Proof.

(1) Indeed, the proof of the classical case in
[
1
, Corollary 10.10]
using the Rees algebra
\oplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}
carries over verbatim using (MN1) by
[
6
, Lem. 6.4.4]
which shows that the Rees algebra (being finitely generated) is Noetherian.

(2) Applying (1) to the case of
N=\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}I^{n}\subseteq M=R
, we obtain that
IN=N
. By Nakayama’s lemma for
\mathscr{C}
(see
[
6
, Cor. 6.1.4]
), we obtain
N=0
.
∎

2.2.2.
Finite morphisms

We say that a morphism
A\to B
of commutative algebras is finite if
B
is a finitely generated
A
-module.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated commutative algebra.

(1)

A
is finite over
A_{(0)}
;

(2)

A_{(0)}
is a finitely generated algebra.

For the proof of Proposition
2.9
, we will need several lemmas which will be useful later on.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated commutative algebra with nilpotent ideal
I
, and write
C:=A/I
. If
\psi:B\to A
is an algebra morphism such that the composition
B\to C
is finite, then
\psi
is finite.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq C
be a compact object such that
B\otimes X\to C
is an epimorphism. Choose a compact object
X^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that
X\subseteq X^{\prime}/I\cap X^{\prime}
, so that we have an epimorphism
B\otimes X^{\prime}\to C
. Because
A
is finitely generated, so is
I
, and thus we may choose a compact object
Y\subseteq I
such that
C\otimes Y\to I/I^{2}
is an epimorphism. In particular, the composition

B\otimes X^{\prime}\otimes S^{n}(Y)\to C\otimes S^{n}(Y)\to I^{n}/I^{n-1}

is an epimorphism for all
n
.

We obtain a natural map
\phi:B\otimes X^{\prime}\otimes S^{\bullet}(Y)\to A
, which we claim is an epimorphism. Indeed,
B\otimes X^{\prime}\otimes S^{\bullet}(Y)
has a filtration coming from the grading on
S^{\bullet}(Y)
, and
A
has a filtration defined by the powers of
I
. The map
\phi
preserves this filtration, and the induced map on the associated graded is
B\otimes X^{\prime}\otimes S^{\bullet}(Y)\to\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I^{n}/I^{n-1}
, which on the
n
th graded component is the map
B\otimes X^{\prime}\otimes S^{n}(Y)\to I^{n}/I^{n-1}
, which we have shown is an epimorphism. Since the associated graded of
\phi
is an epimorphism,
\phi
is as well. Because
I
is nilpotent,
S^{\bullet}(Y)
is compact, and thus so is
X^{\prime}\otimes S^{\bullet}(Y)
. This shows
A
is finite over
B
.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
A,B
be finitely generated commutative algebras. Then a morphism
f:A\to B
is finite if and only if the induced morphism
\bar{f}:\bar{A}\to\bar{B}
is finite.

Proof.

The forward direction is clear. For the backward direction, we obtain that
A\to\bar{B}
is a finite morphism. Thus we may apply Lemma
2.10
.
∎

The following is essentially
[
1
, Prop. 7.8]
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose that
A
is a finitely generated commutative algebra and
B\subseteq A
is a subalgebra such that
A
is finite over
B
. Then
B
is also finitely generated.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq A
be a compact object which generates
A
as an algebra, and let
Y\subseteq A
be a compact object such that
A=BY
. Then choose a compact object
Z\subseteq B
such that
ZY
contains
X
and
Y^{2}
. Finally, write
B^{\prime}
for the subalgebra of
B
generated by
Z
.

We claim that
B^{\prime}Y
equals
A
, so that
A
is finite over
B^{\prime}
. Indeed, since
Y^{2}\subseteq B^{\prime}Y
and
Z\subseteq B^{\prime}
, we see that:

X^{n}\subseteq(ZY)^{n}\subseteq B^{\prime}Y.

Since
B^{\prime}
is Noetherian and
B
is a
B^{\prime}
-submodule of
A
, we may conclude
B
is also finite over
B^{\prime}
, and is therefore also finitely generated.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.9
.

By Lemma
2.12
, (2) follows from (1). For (1), by Lemma
2.10
it suffices to show that
\overline{A}
is finite over
A_{(0)}
. For this we can choose a finite set of generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in\overline{A}
and observe that by condition (GR), there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i
,
x_{i}^{N}
are in the image of
A_{(0)}\to\overline{A}
.
∎

.

[

2.3.
Scheme theory

Let
\operatorname{Lrs}_{\mathscr{C}}
denote the category of locally
\mathscr{C}
-ringed spaces
\mathcal{X}=(|\mathcal{X}|,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
. Here we write
|\mathcal{X}|
to refer to the underlying topological space of
\mathcal{X}
, although we will often employ an abuse of notation and simply write
\mathcal{X}
in place of
|\mathcal{X}|
.

Schemes in
\mathscr{C}
were introduced in
[
8
]
. A scheme is a locally
\mathscr{C}
-ringed space
\mathcal{X}
that is locally isomorphic to an affine scheme. Affine schemes are defined by a Spec construction similarly to the classical case. If
\mathcal{X}
is a scheme and
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is an open subscheme, then we will write
\Bbbk[\mathcal{U}]:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{U})
, and this will be a commutative algebra in
\mathscr{C}
. We say that a scheme
\mathcal{X}
is algebraic if it is of finite type, i.e. there exists a finite affine covering
\mathcal{X}=\bigcup\limits_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i}
such that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{U}_{i}]
is finitely generated for all
i
. Note that the fibre product of algebraic schemes over an algebraic scheme is still algebraic.

2.3.1.
Schemes in
\operatorname{Vec}
associated to
\mathcal{X}

Under our assumptions, the map
\operatorname{Spec}\bar{A}\to\operatorname{Spec}A
is a closed embedding (immersion) which induces the identity map on topological spaces (
[
7
, Thm. 5.3.1]
). On the other hand,
A_{(0)}
is a subalgebra of
A
, and by
loc. cit.
, the inclusion
A_{(0)}\subseteq A
induces a natural morphism
\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}A_{(0)}
which is a homeomorphism of topological spaces.

In general, for a scheme
\mathcal{X}
, we write
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{X}}
for the ideal sheaf where
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{U})=J_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{U})}
. Set
\mathcal{X}_{0}
to be the closed subscheme of
\mathcal{X}
determined by
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{X}}
. We may also view
\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{0}
as a functor, given by the right adjoint to the inclusion
\operatorname{Lrs}_{\operatorname{Vec}}\to\operatorname{Lrs}_{\mathscr{C}}
.

We define
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
to be the locally ringed space
(|\mathcal{X}|,(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})_{(0)})
. This is a scheme, as affine locally it is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}A_{(0)}
. The assignment
\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
is functorial, and is the left adjoint to the inclusion
\operatorname{Lrs}_{\operatorname{Vec}}\to\operatorname{Lrs}_{\mathscr{C}}
.

Summarising, we have the following natural morphisms of schemes, which are the identity on underlying topological spaces:

(2.4)

\mathcal{X}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{(0)}.

The morphisms in (
2.4
) correspond to the maps of algebras in (
2.3
) for any affine open subscheme
\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
We say that
\mathcal{X}
is
purely even
if
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{0}
, or equivalently the maps in (
2.4
) are identity maps.

Given a morphism of schemes
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, we obtain a commutative diagram:

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{0}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{(0)}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}.}

The morphisms
f,f_{0}
, and
f_{(0)}
induce the same map on underlying topological spaces.

2.4.
Properties of schemes and morphisms

Recall that a morphism of schemes
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a universal homeomorphism if any base change of
f
is a homeomorphism. The following is proven in
[
38
,
Lem. 29.45.8
]
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
are algebraic schemes with morphisms
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
,
g:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}
. If any two of
f,g,g\circ f
are universal homeomorphisms, so is the third.

The following is
[
38
,
Lem. 29.46.9
]
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Vec}
and
\phi:A\to B
is a morphism of commutative algebras. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The induced morphism
\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A
is a universal homeomorphism.

(2)

The kernel of
\phi
is a locally nilpotent ideal and for every
b\in B
there exists
n>0
such that
b^{p^{n}}\in\operatorname{Im}\phi
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be algebraic schemes.

(1)

The morphism
\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}
is a finite, universal homeomorphism.

(2)

The morphism
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
is a finite, universal homeomorphism.

(3)

A morphism
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a universal homeomorphism if and only if
f_{0}
is, and if and only if
f_{(0)}
is.

Proof.

We can prove (3) assuming (1) and (2) by applying Lemma
2.14
.

To prove (1) and (2), the property of being a universal homeomorphism is Zariski local on the base
[
38
,
Lem. 35.23.11
]
, so we may assume
\mathcal{X}
is affine, given by the spectrum of a commutative algebra
A
. The morphism
A\to\bar{A}
is the quotient by a nilpotent ideal which is always a universal homeomorphism.

For (2), we may now invoke the case of
f_{0}
for (3) which tells us that it suffices to show
\operatorname{Spec}\bar{A}\to\operatorname{Spec}A_{(0)}
is a universal homeomorphism. However by (GR), the morphism
A_{(0)}\to\bar{A}
satisfies (2) of Lemma
2.15
. Finally,
A_{(0)}\to A
is finite by Proposition
2.9
.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
, and
\mathcal{Z}
be algebraic schemes with morphisms
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
,
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}
. Then
(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y})_{0}=\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\mathcal{Y}_{0}
, and the following are finite, universal homeomorphisms:

(1)

\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\mathcal{Y}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y}
.

(2)

(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y})_{(0)}\to\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{(0)}}\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
.

(3)

\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{(0)}}\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
.

In particular, the natural morphisms
\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\mathcal{Y}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{(0)}}\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
are homeomorphisms.

Proof.

The equality
(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y})_{0}=\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\mathcal{Y}_{0}
holds because the functor
\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{X}_{0}
preserves limits, and thus fibre products. Since
\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}
is a closed embedding for any scheme
\mathcal{X}
, morphism (1) is a finite, universal homeomorphism by (1) of Lemma
2.16
.

Let us check morphism (2) is finite. This will show that morphism (3) is also finite, being the composition of morphism (2) and the finite morphism
\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y}\to(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{Y})_{(0)}
(Lemma
2.16
, (2)). We may verify this affine locally, where it reduces to the morphism
A_{(0)}\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B_{(0)}\to(A\otimes_{C}B)_{(0)}
. Because
A,B
are finite over
A_{(0)},B_{(0)}
respectively, we obtain that
A\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B
is finite over
A_{(0)}\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B_{(0)}
. Thus using the epimorphism
A\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B\to A\otimes_{C}B
, we obtain that
A\otimes_{C}B
is finite over
A_{(0)}\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B_{(0)}
. Since
A_{(0)}\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B_{(0)}
is Noetherian, the subalgebra
(A\otimes_{C}B)_{(0)}
of
A\otimes_{C}B
must be finitely generated over
A_{(0)}\otimes_{C_{(0)}}B_{(0)}
, which proves finiteness.

To show (2) and (3) are universal homeomorphisms, it suffices to show (3) is by Lemma
2.14
. We once again may check this affine locally, and by (2) of Lemma
2.16
, it suffices to consider the morphism
A_{(0)}\otimes_{B_{(0)}}C_{(0)}\to\bar{A}\otimes_{\bar{B}}\bar{C}
. Once again Lemma
2.15
implies our result using the (GR) property.
∎

We can define separated, affine, quasi-affine, finite, and proper morphisms of schemes in
\mathscr{C}
in the exact way as for schemes in
\operatorname{Vec}
.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be algebraic schemes, and let
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a morphism. Let (P) denote one of the properties of being separated, affine, quasi-affine, finite, proper, or a universal homeomorphism. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
satisfies (P);

(2)

f_{0}:\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{Y}_{0}
satisfies (P);

(3)

f_{(0)}:\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\to\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
satisfies (P).

Proof.

We begin by recalling that a morphism is finite if and only if it is affine and proper (
[
38
,
Lem. 29.44.11
]
), thus we need not prove the statement for finiteness if we show it for properness and affinity. The case when (P) is the property of being a universal homeomorphism is proven in Lemma
2.16
.

If (P) is the property of being (quasi-)affine, we may apply the commutative diagram (
2.3.1
) to reduce the question to showing that
\mathcal{X}
is (quasi-)affine if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is, and if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
is. In
[
8
, Remark 5.4.3]
it was shown that
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-affine if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
is. Using the GR property, we see that
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-affine if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is by
[
8
, Prop. 5.4.2(5)]
. It is clear that if
\mathcal{X}
is affine then
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
is as well. If
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
is affine, then we already know that
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-affine, but also that the open immersion in
[
8
, Proposition 5.4.2(2)]
must be surjective, showing that
\mathcal{X}
is affine. If
\mathcal{X}
is affine then
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is as well, and the converse is a special case of
[
8
, Lemma 5.3.4]
.

If (P) is the property of being separated, we may use Lemma
2.17
which tells us that the topological spaces of the three fibre products
\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}\mathcal{X}_{0}
,
\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\times_{\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}}\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
are naturally identified. This implies the image of the diagonal embedding as a topological space will be the same in each case, which proves the claim.

Finally let (P) be the property of being proper. If
\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a morphism of schemes, we may again apply Lemma
2.17
to obtain that
\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
is closed if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}\mathcal{Z}_{0}\to\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is, and if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\times_{\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}}\mathcal{Z}_{(0)}\to\mathcal{Z}_{(0)}
is. Thus the claim follows.
∎

We say a scheme
\mathcal{X}
is separated, respectively proper, if the corresponding structure map
\mathcal{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk
is.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic scheme, and let (P) stand for one of the properties of being separated, affine, quasi-affine, or proper. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{X}
satisfies (P);

(2)

\mathcal{X}_{0}
satisfies (P);

(3)

\mathcal{X}_{(0)}
satisfies (P).

Corollary 2.20
.

If
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a proper morphism of algebraic schemes, then the pushforward of a coherent sheaf remains coherent.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.18
,
f_{(0)}:\mathcal{X}_{(0)}\to\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
is proper. Now we may use that
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
is finite, and thus a quasi-coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{Y}
is coherent if and only if its pushforward to
\mathcal{Y}_{(0)}
is coherent.
∎

2.5.
Infinitesimal schemes

Definition 2.21
.

We say an algebraic scheme
\mathcal{X}
is infinitesimal if
|\mathcal{X}|
is a singleton.

It is clear that an infinitesimal scheme
\mathcal{X}
must be affine, since
\mathcal{X}
has an affine cover.
We say that an algebra is local if it admits a unique maximal ideal.

Lemma 2.22
.

The following are equivalent for a finitely generated commutative algebra
A
.

(1)

\operatorname{Spec}A
is infinitesimal.

(2)

\operatorname{Spec}A_{(0)}
is infinitesimal.

(3)

\operatorname{Spec}\bar{A}
is infinitesimal.

(4)

A
is a local algebra.

(5)

A
is a finite-length (as an object in
\mathscr{C}
), local algebra.

(6)

A
has a unique prime ideal, and it is nilpotent.

Proof.

(1)
\iff
(2)
\iff
(3) By
[
7
]
we have identifications
|\operatorname{Spec}\bar{A}|=|\operatorname{Spec}A_{(0)}|=|\operatorname{Spec}A|
. Note that by (2) of Proposition
2.9
,
A_{(0)}
is finitely generated.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(4) This is obvious.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(5) Observe that
\bar{A}
will again be a local algebra finite-dimensional algebra by assumption, and is therefore finite dimensional. Hence we obtain that the composition of maps
\mathbf{1}\to A\to\bar{A}
is finite, allowing us to conclude by Lemma
2.10
.

(5)
\Rightarrow
(6) Write
\mathfrak{m}
for the unique maximal ideal of
A
. Then since
A
is finite length, for some
n>0
we must have
\mathfrak{m}^{n}=\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}
. By Thm. 6.1.5 of
[
7
]
, Nakayama’s lemma holds, and thus we have
\mathfrak{m}^{n}=0
.
In particular
\mathfrak{m}
is included in any prime ideal and thus the only prime ideal.

(6)
\Rightarrow
(1) This is clear.
∎

Example 2.23
.

Consider a commutative algebra
A\in\mathscr{C}_{fin}
. Then it is a finite product of local algebras. Indeed, we can apply this classical result to the
\Bbbk
-algebra

, use the corresponding orthonormal idempotents of

to find a product

with each

.

3.
Affine group schemes

Recall that throughout we assume that
\mathscr{C}
is a (symmetric) tensor category satisfying (GR) and (MN1-2).

3.1.
Algebraic groups

An affine group scheme is a representable functor from the category of commutative algebras in
\mathscr{C}
to the category of groups. An affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
is represented by a commutative Hopf algebra
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
in
\mathscr{C}
. An
algebraic group
in
\mathscr{C}
is an affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
such that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is finitely generated. We write
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}:\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
for the comultiplication morphism on
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathcal{G}}:\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\mathbf{1}
for the counit, and
\eta=\eta_{\mathcal{G}}:\mathbf{1}\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
for the unit. Set
\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}=\Bbbk^{+}[\mathcal{G}]
to be the augmentation ideal, i.e. the kernel of
\varepsilon
. Unless further specified, for the rest of the section
\mathcal{G}
is some fixed algebraic group.

A homomorphism
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
of algebraic groups is a morphism of schemes such that
\mathcal{G}(A)\to\mathcal{H}(A)
is a homomorphism of groups for all commutative algebras
A
. Equivalently, the pullback morphism
\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is a morphism of Hopf algebras.

3.1.1.
\mathcal{G}
-modules

A left
\mathcal{G}
-module
M
will be synonymous with a right
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
-comodule, and we use the symbol
\rho_{M}=\rho:M\to M\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
for the coaction.
Given a
\mathcal{G}
-module
M
, write
M^{\mathcal{G}}
for the subobject of
\mathcal{G}
-invariants, which is defined by the following equalizer for
\rho_{M}
and
\operatorname{id}_{M}\otimes\eta
:

M^{\mathcal{G}}\to(M\rightrightarrows M\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]).

We write
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
for the category of right
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
-comodules (not necessarily of finite-length) in
\mathscr{C}
. Note that
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is once again the ind-completion of a tensor category, see
[
7
, §7.3]
.

3.2.
Subgroups, factor groups, and images

We refer to
[
7
, Sec. 7]
for details on this section.

A subgroup
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
is a representable subfunctor of
\mathcal{G}
, and it corresponds to a quotient Hopf algebra of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. By
[
7
, Lem. 7.1.3]
, subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
are in bijective correspondence with monomorphisms
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
of algebraic groups up to isomorphism. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is normal if
\mathcal{H}(A)
is normal in
\mathcal{G}(A)
for all commutative algebras
A
. If
\mathcal{H}
is normal in
\mathcal{G}
, we may define the quotient
\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptgp}\mathcal{H}
to be the co-equalizer of
\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}
in the category of affine group schemes, where one map is the natural inclusion and the other map is the trivial homomorphism. One can show that
\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptgp}\mathcal{H}
is represented by
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\mathcal{H}}
, see
[
7
, 7.2.3]
.

3.2.1.
Factor groups

Given a morphism
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
of algebraic groups,
\operatorname{ker}\phi
is the subfunctor of
\mathcal{G}
given by
(\operatorname{ker}\phi)(A)=\operatorname{ker}(\phi(A):\mathcal{G}(A)\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(A))
for any commutative algebra
A
. It is standard to show that
\operatorname{ker}\phi
is representable and a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
, see
[
7
, 7.2.1]
. A factor group of
\mathcal{G}
is an algebraic group
\mathcal{Q}
with a morphism of algebraic groups
\phi:G\to\mathcal{Q}
such that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{Q}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is injective. By
[
7
, Thm. 6.3.1]
, this is equivalent to
\phi
being faithfully flat.

By
[
7
, Thm. 7.2.2]
, there is a natural bijection between factor groups of
\mathcal{G}
and kernels of morphisms
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Further, if
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{Q}
is a factor group, then
\mathcal{Q}
is isomorphic to the sheafification in the fppf topology (see Section
5.1
) of the functor
A\mapsto\mathcal{G}(A)/\operatorname{ker}\phi(A)
.

We observe that if
\mathcal{H}\trianglelefteq\mathcal{G}
is a normal subgroup, then
\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptgp}\mathcal{H}
is a factor group of
\mathcal{G}
under the canonical projection
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptgp}\mathcal{H}
. However, it is not clear that the kernel of
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/_{gp}\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{H}
, and it was asked in
[
7
]
if this is the case. This would provide a bijection between normal subgroups and factor groups. We will answer this question in the affirmative in Section
7
.

3.2.2.
Image subgroup

Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be a morphism of algebraic groups. Define the image of
\phi
, written
\phi(\mathcal{G})
, to be the subfunctor of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
given by the sheafification of the image subfunctor
A\mapsto\operatorname{Im}(\phi(A):\mathcal{G}(A)\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(A))
. An equivalent definition is to consider the image
B
of the pullback morphism
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\prime}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. Then
B
will be a Hopf subalgebra of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
, and thus corresponds to a factor group of
\mathcal{G}
, and we may define
\phi(\mathcal{G})=\operatorname{Spec}(B).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be a morphism of algebraic groups. Then
\phi(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptgp}\operatorname{ker}\phi
and is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It is clear, by definition, that
\phi
factors through
\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptgp}\operatorname{ker}\phi\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Because the latter morphism has trivial kernel, it must be a monomorphism, and thus gives a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.3.
A filtration

Recall that
we write
J=J_{\mathcal{G}}=J_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]}
for the ideal of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
generated by
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]_{nil}
. We have a filtration on
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
by powers of
J
, which is finite since
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is finitely generated:

(3.1)

0=J^{N+1}\subsetneq J^{N}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq J\subsetneq J^{0}=\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}],

for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that (see (
2.2
))

\Delta(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]_{nil})\subset(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}])_{nil}\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]_{nil}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]+\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]_{nil},

which implies that

\Delta(J)\subseteq J\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]+\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\otimes J,

meaning that
J
is a Hopf ideal. Thus we have

(3.2)

\Delta(J^{k})\subseteq\sum\limits_{j}J^{j}\otimes J^{k-j}.

It follows that
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/J
is an affine group scheme in
\operatorname{Vec}
. Write
p_{\mathcal{G}}:\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
for the canonical quotient map. Observe that the powers of
J
are themselves left
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-modules via

(3.3)

J^{i}\xrightarrow{\Delta}\sum\limits_{j}J^{j}\otimes J^{i-j}\xrightarrow{1\otimes p_{\mathcal{G}}}J^{i}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}].

Remark 3.2
.

By replacing
J
with the maximal nilpotent ideal, we can repeat the above, leading to the reduced subgroup (since
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed)
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}\leq\mathcal{G}_{0}\leq\mathcal{G}
.

3.4.
Connected components

As a finitely generated commutative algebra
A
in
\mathscr{C}
is Noetherian,
|\operatorname{Spec}A|
is a Noetherian topological space. In fact, by
[
7
, Cor. 5.3.2]
,
|\operatorname{Spec}A|
is naturally homeomorphic to
|\operatorname{Spec}A_{(0)}|
and
A_{(0)}
itself is Noetherian, by Proposition
2.9
. Subsequently, the connected components of
\operatorname{Spec}A
are the fibres of

|\operatorname{Spec}A|\;\to\;|\operatorname{Spec}\pi(A)|,

with
\pi(A)
the maximal
\Bbbk
-étale subalgebra of
A
(equivalently of
A_{(0)}
). We write
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Spec}A)=\operatorname{Spec}\pi(A)
. As in the classical case, it follows that the component of our algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
containing the identity
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
is a subgroup, leading to a short exact sequence

1\to\mathcal{G}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{G}\to\pi_{0}(\mathcal{G})\to 1,

and it follows that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\circ}]
is
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]e
, with
e
the primitive idempotent not in the kernel of
\varepsilon
.

Example 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is finite, then it follows from Example
2.23
that the composite homomorphism
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}\to\mathcal{G}\to\pi_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is a bijection.

3.5.
Distribution Hopf algebra

We will devote a large portion of this section and the next to developing the fundamental theory of distribution algebras for affine group schemes in
\mathscr{C}
. We expect this to be of independent interest. For the current paper, its relevance toward the main theorem is contained in the role of the distribution algebra towards proving the structural result for algebraic groups in Corollary
4.24
.

Definition 3.4
.

For an algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
, define

\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ}:=\sum\limits_{I}(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/I)^{*}\,\subset\,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}],\mathbf{1}),

where
I
runs over all cofinite ideals of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. Further, set

\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}:=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n})^{*}\subset\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ},

where
\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
denotes the augmentation ideal.

It is standard that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ}
has the structure of a cocommutative Hopf algebra in
\mathscr{C}
, and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
is a Hopf subalgebra with a unique simple subcoalgebra (
irreducible
in the language of
[
30
]
). We consider the inclusion

G(\Bbbk)\subset\Bbbk[G]^{\circ},\;\,g\mapsto\delta_{g}.

Then the elements
\delta_{g}
span the Hopf subalgebra
\Bbbk G(\Bbbk)\subseteq\Bbbk[G]^{\circ}
.

In the following, if
A,B
are Hopf algebras such that
A
is a left
B
-module algebra, then we write
A\#B
for the smash product of
A
and
B
(see
[
30
, Sec. 4.1]
).

Lemma 3.5
.

We have an isomorphism of Hopf algebras
\Bbbk[G]^{\circ}\cong\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\#\Bbbk G(\Bbbk)
. In particular,
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
and
G(\Bbbk)
generate
\Bbbk[G]^{\circ}
as an algebra.

Proof.

The classical proof given in
[
30
, Sec. 5.6]
easily carries over to our setting.
∎

From the definition of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ}
, we have a natural pairing

(3.4)

(-,-):\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\mathbf{1}.

Proposition 3.6
.

The pairing (
3.4
) is nondegenerate. Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
, then its restriction to
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is nondegenerate.

For the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we need a lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be connected. Then
f\in\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]_{(0)}
is a zero divisor if and only if
f
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
I=\sqrt{0}
be the nilradical of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
(
[
7
, §2.2]
), which is nilpotent since
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is noetherian. Then
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/I
is a reduced
\Bbbk
-algebra, and
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}=\operatorname{Spec}(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/I)
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
(see Remark
3.2
). Taking the associated graded with respect to the filtration generated by powers of
I
, we obtain

\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]=\bigoplus\limits_{i}I^{i}/I^{i+1},

and because
I
is
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}
-stable, this will be a
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}
-equivariant
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}]
-module, and thus by the fundamental theorem for Hopf modules it is free over
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}]
. If
f
is a zero divisor, write
\overline{f}
for the image of
f
in
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}]
. If
\overline{f}=0
, then clearly it is nilpotent. If
\overline{f}\neq 0
, then because
\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is free over
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}]
,
\overline{f}
must be a zero divisor in
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{red}}]
. However, for an algebraic group in
\operatorname{Vec}
, being reduced and connected implies it is integral, giving a contradiction.
∎

Recall from
[
7
, §4 and §5]
that, by our assumptions on
\mathscr{C}
, for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in a commutative algebra

, there exists a local ring

, with the standard universal property, defined by inverting all elements in

.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is connected. Then for
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
a maximal ideal, the localisation map
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

It is clear that the pairing is nondegenerate in
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ}
, so let us show it is nondegenerate in
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. Suppose
X\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
lies in the kernel of this pairing, then
e_{g}X
also lies in the kernel, for any primitive idempotent
e_{g}
in the maximal étale subalgebra of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
corresponding to some
g
in the component group
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
, see §3.3. Since
X\subset\oplus_{g}e_{g}X
and each
e_{g}X
lies in the kernel as well, we may thus assume that
X
is contained in one component of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. Using left translation under
\mathcal{G}(\Bbbk)
, we may assume this component is
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
, the connected component of the identity. Therefore we may assume
\mathcal{G}
is connected.

If we write
\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
for the augmentation ideal, then since
\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
is cofinite for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that
X\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
. However Lemma
2.8
(2) and Corollary
3.8
imply that
\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n}=0
, and this forces
X=0
which finishes the proof.
∎

As for general schemes, we say that an affine group scheme
\mathcal{H}
is purely even if
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}})_{(0)}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is purely even.

Proof.

The backward direction is clear, so let us suppose that
(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}})_{(0)}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
. By Proposition
3.6
, the pairing
(-,-):\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\circ}]\to\mathbf{1}
is nondegenerate, which induces an inclusion
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\circ}]\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}
. However the latter is purely even, so we obtain that the same is true for
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\circ}]
. The algebra
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is a direct sum of copies of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\circ}]
as an object of
\mathscr{C}
, so
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is also purely even.
∎

3.5.1.
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
as a filtered Hopf algebra

Write
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
. We call
\Gamma^{n}:=(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1})^{*}\subseteq\Gamma
the distributions of degree
n
(supported at
\varepsilon
). Since
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is Noetherian,
\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}
is finitely generated, so we obtain that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}
is of finite length in
\mathscr{C}
, meaning the same is true of
\Gamma^{n}
. This gives an ascending filtration

(3.5)

\Bbbk\varepsilon=\Gamma^{0}\subseteq\Gamma^{1}\subseteq\Gamma^{2}\subseteq\cdots,\ \ \ \Gamma=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma^{n}.

Moreover we have
\Gamma^{n}\Gamma^{m}\subseteq\Gamma^{n+m}
, and thus
\Gamma^{\bullet}
is a filtered algebra by subobjects of finite-length.

It is now a standard fact that using the pairing
(-,-)
,
\Gamma
inherits from
\mathcal{G}
the structure of a cocommutative Hopf algebra so that
(-,-)
is a Hopf pairing. We write
m_{\Gamma}
, resp.
\Delta_{\Gamma}
, for its product, resp. coproduct,
\eta_{\Gamma}
and
\varepsilon_{\Gamma}
for its unit and counit, and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{+}
for its augmentation ideal.

Definition 3.10
.

Given a Hopf algebra
H
in
\mathscr{C}
with coproduct
\Delta
and unit
\eta
, define
\operatorname{prim}H\subseteq H
to be the kernel of

\Delta-\operatorname{id}\otimes\eta-\eta\otimes\operatorname{id}:H\to H\otimes H.

We may view a Hopf algebra
H
as a Lie algebra via the commutator. A standard exercise shows that
\operatorname{prim}H
is then a Lie subalgebra of
H
.

Definition 3.11
.

We define the Lie algebra of
\mathcal{G}
, written
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})=\mathfrak{g}
, to be
\operatorname{prim}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Remark 3.12
.

We have
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\Gamma^{1}
, and a canonical decomposition
\Gamma^{1}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
.

The filtration (
3.1
) on
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
by powers of
J
induces a finite filtration on
\Gamma
:

(3.6)

0\subsetneq\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\subsetneq\Gamma_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\Gamma_{N}=\Gamma,

where
\Gamma_{i}
is the kernel of

\Gamma\otimes J^{i+1}\to\mathbf{1}.

By (
3.2
) we have
\Gamma_{i}\Gamma_{j}\subseteq\Gamma_{i+j}
and, furthermore,

(3.7)

\Delta(\Gamma_{k})\subseteq\sum\limits_{j}\Gamma_{j}\otimes\Gamma_{k-j}.

Remark 3.13
.

We have now constructed two filtrations on
\Gamma
, denoted by
\Gamma^{\bullet}
and
\Gamma_{\bullet}
, both giving
\Gamma
the structure of a filtered algebra. The first one is a direct analogue of the classical filtration in
\mathrm{Vec}
, while the second one is only relevant for
\mathscr{C}\not=\mathrm{Vec}
. We will use both filtrations, but
\operatorname{gr}\Gamma
will always mean the associated graded of
\Gamma
with respect to the finite filtration
\Gamma_{\bullet}
.

Observe that if
\phi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
is a morphism of algebraic groups, then
\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}}=\epsilon_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\phi
. Therefore, there is an induced morphism of distributions
\Gamma_{\phi}:\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}}\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
, and one may check that this is a morphism of Hopf algebras.

Lemma 3.14
.

If
1\to\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 1
is a short exact sequence of algebraic groups, then we have a short exact sequence:

0\to(\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}^{+})\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}\to 0.

where
(\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}^{+})
is the ideal of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
generated by the augmentation ideal of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Proof.

For a proof of the fact that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
is an epimorphism, we note that the proof of
[
22
, Chpt. I, Prop. 7.6]
(including the referenced result
[
4
, Chpt. I, §3, Prop. 9]
) carries over to our setting, where we use that
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{Q}
is flat.

To prove exactness in the middle, observe that we have an exact sequence

0\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{Q}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\xrightarrow{(1\otimes q)\circ\Delta}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]/\Bbbk 1,

where we denote by
q
the composite of the defining Hopf algebra quotient
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
with the map
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]/\Bbbk 1
quotienting out the algebra unit. Appropriately dualising this sequence gives middle exactness, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

For a morphism
\phi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
of algebraic groups, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\phi
is an isomorphism,

(2)

\phi_{0}:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}
and
\Gamma_{\phi}:\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
are isomorphisms, and

(3)

\phi(\Bbbk):\mathcal{G}(\Bbbk)\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\Bbbk)
and
\Gamma_{\phi}
are isomorphisms.

Proof.

Clearly
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
, since
\mathcal{G}(\Bbbk)=\mathcal{G}_{0}(\Bbbk)
. To show (3)
\Rightarrow
(1), write
I
for the kernel of the pullback
\phi^{*}:\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\prime}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. We have an induced morphism
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]^{\circ}\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\prime}]^{\circ}
, and by Lemma
3.5
and our assumptions, it will be an isomorphism. Thus
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}^{\prime}]^{\circ}
must pair with
I
to 0 under (
3.4
), implying that
I=0
by Proposition
3.6
. It follows that
\phi^{*}
is a monomorphism, and thus
\phi
is a quotient morphism of algebraic groups. If we write
\mathcal{N}
for its kernel, then
\mathcal{N}(\Bbbk)=\operatorname{ker}(\phi(\Bbbk))
is trivial. This implies
\mathcal{N}
is infinitesimal, so that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}=\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]^{*}
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.5
). However, by Lemma
3.14
we see that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}=1
, and thus
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]=\mathbf{1}
, so that
\mathcal{N}
is trivial, and
\phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

3.6.
Semi-direct products

In the following, we refer to
[
29
]
for the notion of a smash (co)product of Hopf algebras.

Let
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{H}
be algebraic groups, and suppose that we have a left action
a:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathcal{N}
which respects the group structure on
\mathcal{N}
. Then we may form the semi-direct product
\mathcal{H}\ltimes\mathcal{N}
, which as a scheme is simply the product
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{N}
, and the group structure is given on points by
(\mathcal{H}\ltimes\mathcal{N})(A)=\mathcal{H}(A)\ltimes\mathcal{N}(A)
. In particular, as commutative algebras we have
\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}\ltimes\mathcal{N}]\cong\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
, and as a Hopf algebra
\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}\ltimes\mathcal{N}]
is the smash coproduct of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
by
\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]
.

Lemma 3.16
.

We have that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}\ltimes\mathcal{N}}
is the smash product of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}
by
\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}}
. In particular, the multiplication map
\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}}\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{H}\ltimes\mathcal{N}}
is an isomorphism of coalgebras in
\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

This follows from the natural generalization of
[
29
, Thm. 5.1]
to
\mathscr{C}
.
∎

Example 3.17
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is finite (meaning
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is compact), then it follows from Example
3.3
that
\mathcal{G}\cong\pi_{0}(\mathcal{G})\ltimes\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
and that
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
is infinitesimal. So each finite group scheme is a semidirect product of an abstract finite group, and an infinitesimal group scheme.

3.7.
The group
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}

Using the finite filtrations (
3.1
) and (
3.6
) on
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
and
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
, we may form the associated graded objects
\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
and
\operatorname{gr}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
. By (
3.2
) and (
3.7
), these will once again be Hopf algebras, and we have an induced pairing:

\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\otimes\operatorname{gr}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbf{1}.

Notice that
\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is generated, as an algebra, by
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/J=\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
and
J/J^{2}
. The former is a finitely generated algebra, and the latter is a finitely generated module over
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
, implying that
\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is finitely generated. Write
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}
for the algebraic group represented by
\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
. Since
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{m}_{\operatorname{gr}\varepsilon}
, the pairings produce a factorisation

\textstyle{\operatorname{gr}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{\Bbbk[\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}]^{\ast}}
\textstyle{\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where
\psi
is a morphism of Hopf algebras.

Any filtration on an object
V
in
\mathscr{C}
induces a filtration on any subobject
U\subset V
and we have a natural inclusion
\operatorname{gr}U\subset\operatorname{gr}V
. We apply this in particular to the subobject
\Gamma^{n}\subset\Gamma
from the degree filtration (
3.5
) on
\Gamma
, leading to
\operatorname{gr}\Gamma^{n}\subset\operatorname{gr}\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.18
.

The map
\psi
is an isomorphism of Hopf algebras and we have
\psi(\operatorname{gr}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{n})\subseteq\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
X\subseteq\Gamma_{i+1}/\Gamma_{i}
is non-zero and of finite length. Then there exists a finite-length subobject
Y\subseteq\Gamma_{i+1}
such that
X\cong Y/\Gamma_{i}\cap Y
. By nondegeneracy in
\Gamma
of the pairing
\Gamma\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk
, there exists a compact subobject
U\subseteq J^{i+1}
such that the pairing
Y\otimes U\twoheadrightarrow X\otimes U\to\mathbf{1}
is non-zero. Thus if
V=U/U\cap J^{i+2}
, we have that
X\otimes V\to\mathbf{1}
is non-zero.
It follows that
\psi
is a monomorphism.

To prove surjectivity, observe that
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n}=\mathfrak{m}_{\operatorname{gr}\epsilon}^{n}
, and hence
\psi(\operatorname{gr}\Gamma^{n})\subseteq\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}^{n}
. Since
\operatorname{gr}(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]/\mathfrak{m}_{\varepsilon}^{n})
and
\operatorname{gr}(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}])/\mathfrak{m}_{\operatorname{gr}\varepsilon}^{n}
have the same length, the same is true of
\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}^{n}
and
\operatorname{gr}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}^{n}
, and thus
\psi
is an epimorphism, and therefore an isomorphism.
∎

Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra of
\mathcal{G}
(Definition

).

Corollary 3.19
.

We have
\operatorname{Lie}\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}
, where
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}
is an abelian ideal of
\operatorname{Lie}\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Indeed,
\Gamma^{1}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{g}\cap\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}=\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}
, and
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}
(since
J^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2})
. Thus
\operatorname{gr}\Gamma^{1}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}
, and the Lie bracket structure is easy to check.
∎

We say that an algebraic group
\mathcal{N}
is infinitesimal if it is infinitesimal as a scheme. In particular, by Lemma
2.22
,
\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}
then has finite length and
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]\cong\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}^{*}
as Hopf algebras.

Lemma 3.20
.

We have an isomorphism:

\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{N}\rtimes\mathcal{G}_{0},

where
\mathcal{N}
is a commutative, infinitesimal, algebraic group with
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{N})=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{(0)}
. Further, multiplication induces a coalgebra isomorphism:

\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}\cong\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}.

Proof.

We have a splitting of Hopf algebras
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\to\Bbbk[\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}]
of the natural surjection
\Bbbk[\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
, so we define the algebraic group
\mathcal{N}
by
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]:=\Bbbk[\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}]/(\Bbbk^{+}[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
. In other words, we have a split short exact sequence

1\to\mathcal{N}\to\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}\to 1.

In particular, we have an isomorphism
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{G}_{0}\ltimes\mathcal{N}
. Since
\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
is a finitely generated
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
-module,
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
has finite length. Since it is also an
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra with degree 0 part isomorphic to
\mathbf{1}
, the group
\mathcal{N}
must be infinitesimal. To show that
\mathcal{N}
is commutative, we need to prove that
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
is cocommutative. Since
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
is generated in degree one as an
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra, it suffices to show that its degree one part is primitive. But this follows from the fact that the coproduct on
\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
preserves the grading, and the degree 0 part is isomorphic to
\mathbf{1}
. To prove the statement on Lie algebras, we use Corollary
3.19
. Finally, to prove the isomorphism of coalgebras for distribution algebras, we may apply Lemma
3.16
.
∎

Corollary 3.21
.

\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
is a finitely generated
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}
-module under left multiplication.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.18
and

\operatorname{gr}\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}=\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}

-module under infinitesimal right translation, i.e.

(3.8)

\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\xrightarrow{\sim}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\xrightarrow{\Delta_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes 1}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\xrightarrow{1\otimes(-,-)}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]

where we have used the braiding on
\operatorname{Vec}
in the first isomorphism.
Let us give
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
the structure of a commutative algebra. First observe that we have an isomorphism

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])\otimes\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma\otimes\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]),

which can be proven using the
\otimes-\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
adjunction (
2.1
). The multiplication is described as follows:

m_{\operatorname{Hom}}=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\Delta_{\Gamma},m_{\mathcal{G}_{0}})

We set
\epsilon_{\operatorname{Hom}}=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\eta_{\Gamma},\epsilon_{\mathcal{G}_{0}})
for the unit. Here we use that
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
is a bifunctor to define our morphisms, and that
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}
and
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}
are subfunctors of
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
on their respective categories of modules.

In the following, we view
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
as a left
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
-module under the action by infinitesimal right translation (as in (
3.8
)). We view
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
as a left
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
-module via the action of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}
on itself by right multiplication.

The following presentation for
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
was first observed in the super case over the complex numbers by Koszul
[
24
]
. For more general proofs, see
[
26
]
for the super case and
[
40
]
for the case of
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.25
.

We have a natural isomorphism of algebras and
\Gamma
-modules

\Phi:\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]),

induced, via adjunction, by the natural quotient map
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
of left
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}
-modules.

Proof.

The finite cofiltration
\Gamma/\Gamma_{\bullet}
on
\Gamma
induces a descending filtration
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{0}}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{\bullet},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
on
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
, and this filtration respects the algebra structure. We claim that
\Phi(J^{i})\subseteq\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{i-1},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
. Indeed, it is clear that
J
lands in
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])
, and from this the claim follows.

Now we have a morphism of filtered algebras; taking the associated graded, we obtain the morphism

\operatorname{gr}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\operatorname{gr}\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]),

or by Lemma
3.20
,

\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}},\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}])\otimes(\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}})^{*}.

Since
\mathcal{N}
is infinitesimal and algebraic,
(\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}})^{*}\cong\Bbbk[\mathcal{N}]
. From this, the isomorphism is not hard to show.
∎

Now if
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is an open subscheme, then
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}
acts naturally on the left on
\Bbbk[\mathcal{U}_{0}]
by infinitesimal right translation, and so we may construct a presheaf of
\mathscr{C}
-algebras on
|\mathcal{G}|
by the association:

\mathcal{U}\mapsto\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{U}_{0}]).

Proposition 3.26
.

The above assignment is isomorphic to the structure sheaf of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let us view
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,-)
as functor from
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}
-modules in
\operatorname{Vec}
to
\mathscr{C}
. Then we claim that this functor is exact. Indeed, the cofiltration
\Gamma/\Gamma_{\bullet}
on
\Gamma
induces a filtration on this functor. Since we showed that
\operatorname{gr}\Gamma\simeq\Gamma_{\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{G}}\simeq\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes\Gamma_{\mathcal{N}}
(see Lemma
3.20
) the layers are given by
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\otimes X_{i},-)
where
X_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
. Since these layers are clearly exact functors, the whole functor is as well.

It follows that the functor
|\mathcal{U}|\mapsto\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}}(\Gamma,\Bbbk[\mathcal{U}_{0}])
defines a sheaf on
|\mathcal{G}|
. This sheaf admits a natural map from
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}
, and by using a parallel argument to that in Theorem
3.25
, it is an isomorphism.
∎

4.
Frobenius twist

Write
\mathscr{C}^{(1)}
for the Frobenius twist of
\mathscr{C}
, which is equivalent to
\mathscr{C}
as an additive symmetric monoidal category, but we alter the
\Bbbk
-linear structure by letting
\lambda
act by
\lambda^{p}
for
\lambda\in\Bbbk
. Recall the Frobenius functor
\operatorname{Fr}:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear symmetric monoidal functor defined by the composition of functors:

\mathscr{C}\xrightarrow{\otimes p}\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathscr{C}}C_{p}\to\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes\operatorname{Ver}_{p}.

The first functor is given by
X\mapsto X^{\otimes p}
, and the second functor is described by certain cohomological functors (see
[
6
,
17
]
). An equivalent description is given by applying the semisimplification functor from
\operatorname{Rep}C_{p}
to
\mathrm{Ver}_{p}
for
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathscr{C}}C_{p}
viewed as a category of comodules in
\operatorname{Rep}C_{p}
over some coalgebra, see
[
10
]
for more details.

Now let
A
be a commutative algebra in
\mathscr{C}
. View
A
as an object of
\operatorname{Rep}_{C_{p}}\mathscr{C}
with trivial
C_{p}
action. Then multiplication
A^{\otimes p}\to A
is equivariant with respect to the
C_{p}
-action, and thus gives rise to a natural map:

\phi_{A}:\operatorname{Fr}(A)\to A.

Moreover,
\phi_{A}
is an algebra morphism in
\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, where
A=A\boxtimes\mathbf{1}
. Write
A^{[1]}:=\operatorname{im}\phi_{A}
, so that
A^{[1]}
is a subalgebra of
A
inside of
\mathscr{C}
.

Definition 4.1
.

We call
A^{[1]}
the
Frobenius twist
of
A
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, define
A^{[r]}
inductively by
A^{[r]}:=(A^{[r-1]})^{[1]}
.

For an object
X
in a tensor category and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
(X^{\otimes n})^{S_{n}}
its
n
-th divided power,
i.e.
the maximal
S_{n}
-invariant subobject of
X^{\otimes n}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have
A^{[1]}=\operatorname{im}\left((A^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}}\to A\right)
.

Proof.

We may view
A^{\otimes p}
as an object of either
\mathscr{C^{\prime}}:=\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes\operatorname{Rep}C_{p}
or
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}:=\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes\operatorname{Rep}S_{p}
. Notice that we have a natural tensor functor
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, and because
C_{p}
is a Sylow subgroup of
S_{p}
, it induces a tensor functor fitting into the following diagram with
\operatorname{Fr}^{en}
the enriched Frobenius functor from
[
9
]
:

\textstyle{\mathscr{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Fr}}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Fr}^{en}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes(\operatorname{Rep}S_{p})^{ss}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}^{(1)}\boxtimes\operatorname{Ver}_{p}.}

Here we write
(\operatorname{Rep}S_{p})^{ss}
for the semisimplification of
\operatorname{Rep}S_{p}
. Because
A
has a trivial action of
S_{p}
, upon semisimplification the morphism
A^{\otimes p}\to A
will have image given by the image of
(A^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}}
inside
A
(or equivalently, the image of
\operatorname{Fr}_{+}(A)\to A
, with
\operatorname{Fr}_{+}
the ‘identity component’ of
\operatorname{Fr}^{en}
as discussed in detail in
[
6
]
), and so we are done.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For
\mathscr{C}=\mathrm{Vec}
, the extension of scalars
A^{(1)}=\Bbbk\otimes_{\Bbbk}A
of
A
along the Frobenius homomorphism on
\Bbbk
is usually referred to as the Frobenius twist of
A
. There is the
\Bbbk
-linear
p
-th power map

A^{(1)}\to A,\quad 1\otimes a\mapsto a^{p}.

The algebra
A^{[1]}
is the image of the above morphism. However,
A^{(1)}\twoheadrightarrow A^{[1]}
is only an isomorphism if
A
is reduced. This is why we use the notation
A^{[1]}
in place of
A^{(1)}
for our notion of Frobenius twist.

4.1.
Properties of Frobenius twist

As above,
A
denotes a commutative algebra in
\mathscr{C}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
A
is finitely generated.

(1)

If
A\neq\mathbf{1}
, then
A^{[1]}
is a proper subalgebra of
A
.

(2)

A
is finite over
A^{[1]}
.

(3)

A^{[1]}
is a finitely generated algebra.

(4)

The kernel of the natural morphism
\overline{A^{[1]}}\to\bar{A}
is a nilpotent ideal. Further, the image contains the subalgebra
(\bar{A})^{[r]}=\{a^{p^{r}}|a\in\bar{A}\}
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

(5)

The morphism
\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}A^{[1]}
is a universal homeomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
denote a nonzero maximal ideal of
A
, so that we have a splitting
A=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
. Then we see that
A^{[1]}\subseteq\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{p}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}^{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}
unless
\mathfrak{m}=0
(Thm. 6.1.5 of
[
7
]
),
A^{[1]}
must be a proper subalgebra.

For (2), choose generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
of
\bar{A}
over
\Bbbk
. Then by GR, there exist
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{1}^{n_{1}},\dots,f_{k}^{n_{k}}
lift to
A
. Write
B=B_{(0)}\subseteq A
for the subalgebra of
A
generated by these lifts, so that
B
splits off
A
as an object in
\mathscr{C}
. Then
B^{[1]}
is generated by
f_{1}^{n_{1}p},\cdots,f_{k}^{n_{k}p}
, meaning that
B^{[1]}\to A^{[1]}\to A\to\bar{A}
is a finite morphism by construction. We conclude by Lemma
2.10
.

Part (3) now follows from (2) and Lemma
2.12
.

Part (4) is clear. For (5), we use (4) and Lemma
2.15
to show that
\operatorname{Spec}\bar{A}\to\operatorname{Spec}\overline{A^{[1]}}
is a universal homeomorphism. The result now follows from (2) of Lemma
2.16
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A,B
be commutative algebras.

(1)

We have a canonical isomorphism of algebras
(A\otimes B)^{[1]}\cong A^{[1]}\otimes B^{[1]}
.

(2)

If
A\to B
is injective, then so is
A^{[1]}\to B^{[1]}
.

(3)

If
A\to B
is finite and
A
is finitely generated, then
A^{[1]}\to B^{[1]}
is finite.

(4)

If
f\in A_{(0)}
, then
f^{p}\in A^{[1]}
, and

(A^{[1]})_{f^{p}}\cong(A_{f^{p}})^{[1]}.

Proof.

Part (1) follows from the monoidality of the Frobenius functor. Part (2) is immediate. For (3), we use (2) of Lemma
4.4
to obtain that
A^{[1]}\to A\to B
is a finite morphism. Since
B^{[1]}
is an
A^{[1]}
-submodule of
B
, and
A^{[1]}
is Noetherian by (3) of Lemma
4.4
, we conclude that
B^{[1]}
is also finite over
A^{[1]}
.

For (4), we see that:

\displaystyle(A^{[1]})_{f^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\rightarrow}(A^{[1]}\xrightarrow{f^{p}}A^{[1]}\xrightarrow{f^{p}}\cdots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\to}\left(\operatorname{im}((A^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}}\to A)\xrightarrow{f^{p}}\operatorname{im}((A^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}}\to A)\xrightarrow{f^{p}}\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{im}(((A^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}})_{f^{\otimes p}}\to A_{f^{p}}).

Here we have used the exactness of filtered colimits. It remains to observe that

((A^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}})_{f^{\otimes p}}\cong(A_{f}^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}}\cong(A_{f^{p}}^{\otimes p})^{S_{p}},

again by exactness of localisation.
∎

Example 4.6
.

If
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, for any commutative algebra
A
we may choose a surjection
S(X)\twoheadrightarrow A
for some
X\in\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
. Because the Frobenius functor
\operatorname{Fr}
is exact on
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, we obtain a surjection
S(X)^{[1]}\twoheadrightarrow A^{[1]}
. We have a decomposition
X=\bigoplus\limits_{i}L_{i}^{\oplus I_{i}}
for some index sets
I_{i}
, then

S(X)^{[1]}\cong\bigotimes_{i}\bigotimes_{I_{i}}S(L_{i})^{[1]}\cong\bigotimes_{I_{0}}S(\mathbf{1})^{[1]},

where we take restricted tensor product. Here we used (1), Lemma
4.5
and exactness of
\operatorname{Fr}
to distribute the Frobenius twist over the tensor product. In particular, we see that
S(X)^{[1]}
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra, implying that
A^{[1]}
is too.

Remark 4.7
.

Frobenius twists and kernels of algebraic groups over
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
(using an equivalent definition) were studied and used in
[
23
]
to prove the Steinberg tensor product theorem in this setting.

Remark 4.8
.

It is
not
true that Frobenius twist preserves surjectivity or pushouts of commutative algebras. Indeed, let
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Ver}_{4}^{+}
and
A=S(P)=\Bbbk[x,y]/x^{2}
where
P=\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle
is the unique indecomposable projective. Then we have
A^{[1]}=\Bbbk[y^{4}]
, but
\bar{A}=\Bbbk[y]
and thus
\bar{A}^{[1]}=\Bbbk[y^{2}]
. Further, we see that

\Bbbk[y^{2}]=(\bar{A}\otimes_{A}\bar{A})^{[1]}\not\cong\bar{A}^{[1]}\otimes_{A^{[1]}}\bar{A}^{[1]}=\Bbbk[y^{2}]\otimes_{\Bbbk[y^{4}]}\Bbbk[y^{2}]

Remark 4.9
.

We caution that
\overline{A^{[1]}}
is not isomorphic to
\bar{A}^{[1]}
, as the example in Remark
4.8
shows.

Definition 4.10
.

For a ringed space
\mathcal{X}=(|\mathcal{X}|,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
, write
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{[1]}
for the sheafification of the presheaf
U\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)^{[1]}
. Then define
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
to be the ringed space
(|\mathcal{X}|,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{[1]})
, and write
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
for the natural map of locally ringed spaces.

Lemma 4.11
.

If
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{Spec}A
, then
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}=\operatorname{Spec}A^{[1]}
.

Proof.

This follows from (4) of Lemma
4.5
, and the fact that the distinguished open subschemes
\mathcal{D}(f^{p})\subseteq\mathcal{X}
give a basis for the topology, where
f
runs over elements of
A_{(0)}
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is an algebraic scheme, then so is
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
, and we have
\mathcal{X}(\Bbbk)=\mathcal{X}^{[1]}(\Bbbk)
. Further,
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}
is a finite, universal homeomorphism.

Proof.

The ringed space
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is again an algebraic scheme by Lemma
4.11
and (3) of Lemma
4.4
. The fact that
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}
is finite follows from (2) of Lemma
4.4
. Finally,
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}
is a universal homeomorphism by (5) of Lemma
4.4
and the fact that this may be checked affine locally.
∎

Given a morphism
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
of schemes, write
f^{[1]}:\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\to\mathcal{Y}^{[1]}
for the induced map on the Frobenius twists. It is not difficult to see that
f^{[1]}
is again a morphism of locally ringed spaces, and thus is a morphism of schemes.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be algebraic schemes.

(1)

We have a canonical isomorphism
(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})^{[1]}\cong\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\times\mathcal{Y}^{[1]}
.

(2)

If
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is finite, then so is
f^{[1]}:\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\to\mathcal{Y}^{[1]}
.

(3)

If
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is an open immersion, then so is
f^{[1]}:\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\to\mathcal{Y}^{[1]}
.

(4)

\mathcal{X}
is separated, affine, quasi-affine, or proper if and only if
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is likewise.

Proof.

Part (1) follows from (1) of Lemma
4.5
, and (2) follows from (3) of the same lemma. Part (3) may be checked affine locally on
\mathcal{Y}
, so suppose that
\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{Spec}A
. Then we may write
\mathcal{X}=\cup_{i}D(f_{i}^{p})
for some
f_{i}\in A_{(0)}
. Now Lemma
4.5
implies that
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}=\cup_{i}D(f_{i}^{p})^{[1]}
, and thus remains an open subscheme of
\mathcal{Y}^{[1]}
.

Finally, we prove (4). Since
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is a homeomorphism, their diagonal morphisms will give the same topological subspace of
|\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}|=|\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\times\mathcal{X}^{[1]}|
. Therefore one image is closed if and only if the other is. This proves
\mathcal{X}
is separated if and only if
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is. On the other hand,
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is affine, so we obtain that
\mathcal{X}
is affine whenever
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is and the converse follows from Lemma
4.11
. For quasi-affinity, we use Part (5) of the characterization given in
[
8
, Prop. 5.4.2]
, and that
(\mathcal{X}_{f^{p}})^{[1]}\cong(\mathcal{X}^{[1]})_{f^{p}}
for all
f\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})_{(0)}
by (4) of Lemma
4.5
. Finally for properness, let
\mathcal{Y}
be any scheme and consider the commutative diagram:

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\times\mathcal{Y}.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}}

By Lemma
4.12
, the horizontal arrow is a homeomorphism. Therefore one diagonal arrow is a closed morphism if and only if the other is. It follows that
\mathcal{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk
is universally closed if and only if
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\to\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk
is universally closed. Since
\mathcal{X}
is separated if and only if
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is, this shows the same is true for properness.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

We again caution that while we have
(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y})^{[1]}\cong\mathcal{X}^{[1]}\times\mathcal{Y}^{[1]}
, it is not true that Frobenius twist will commute with arbitrary fibre products. Further, while Frobenius twists preserve open embeddings, they need not preserve closed embeddings (see Remark
4.8
).

We conclude this subsection with a powerful lemma which we will not use in this paper.

Lemma 4.15
.

If
\mathscr{C}_{fin}
is a union of tensor subcategories with finitely many simples, then for an algebraic scheme
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathscr{C}
there exists
r>0
such that
\mathcal{X}^{[r]}
is purely even.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is algebraic, it must actually be a scheme in (the ind-completion of) a tensor subcategory in the union. Indeed, a finitely generated algebra
A
in
\mathscr{C}
is generated by a compact object, which thus lies in a tensor category in the union, and an algebraic scheme has a finite cover by spectra of such finitely generated algebras. Hence, we can assume that
\mathscr{C}
itself has finitely many simple objects.

Then, by
[
17
, Cor. 10.3]
, applying enough Frobenius twists will take
\mathscr{C}
into a Frobenius exact tensor subcategory of
\mathscr{C}_{ex}
of
\mathscr{C}
. Since
\mathscr{C}
satisfies (MN1-2),
\mathscr{C}_{ex}
will also, which implies
\mathscr{C}_{ex}
is a tensor subcategory of
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
by
[
10
, Theorem 7.2.3(2)]
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
. It thus suffices to assume
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
. By Example
4.6
, we see that
(\operatorname{Spec}A)^{[1]}
will be purely even for any commutative algebra
A
, and therefore
\mathcal{X}^{[1]}
is purely even for any scheme
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

The main example of our theory is intended for is
\mathscr{C}=\mathrm{Ver}_{p^{\infty}}
, for which the assumption in Lemma
4.15
is satisfied.

4.2.
Frobenius twists of algebraic groups

Lemma 4.17
.

Suppose that
A
is a commutative Hopf algebra.

(1)

A^{[1]}
is a Hopf subalgebra of
A
.

(2)

Write
\mathfrak{m}
for the augmentation ideal of
A
, and
\mathfrak{m}^{[1]}
for the image of
\operatorname{Fr}(\mathfrak{m})\to\mathfrak{m}
. Then
\mathfrak{m}^{[1]}
is the augmentation ideal of
A^{[1]}
, and
\mathfrak{m}^{[1]}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{p}
.

Proof.

For (1), we use again that
\operatorname{Fr}
is symmetric monoidal to obtain that
\operatorname{Fr}(A)
is a Hopf algebra and
\phi_{A}:\operatorname{Fr}(A)\to A
is a morphism of Hopf algebras.

For (2), we have a splitting
A=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathbf{1}
. By the additivity of
\operatorname{Fr}
, we have
\operatorname{Fr}(A)=\operatorname{Fr}(\mathbf{1})\oplus\operatorname{Fr}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
, so that
A^{[1]}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{[1]}
, implying that
\mathfrak{m}^{[1]}
is the augmentation ideal of
A^{[1]}
. Further,
\mathfrak{m}^{[1]}
is the image of

Let

5.
Generalities on quotients

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a scheme and
\mathcal{H}
an algebraic group in
\mathscr{C}
, where we recall that (GR) and (MN1-2) hold. A right action of
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathcal{X}
is a morphism
a:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{X}
of schemes satisfying the usual commutative diagrams.

Given a right
\mathcal{H}
-action on
\mathcal{X}
we define the quotient scheme, if it exists, to be a scheme
\mathcal{Y}
with a morphism
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that the following is a co-equalizer in the category of all schemes:

\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X}\right)\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathcal{Y}

If the quotient
(\mathcal{Y},\pi)
exists, then we will write
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}
. Notice that if the quotient exists, then
\pi
is necessarily an epimorphism.

We say that an action
a:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{X}
is
free
if
pr_{1}\times a:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
is a closed embedding. The following is proven in exactly the same way as Chpt. III,
\lx@sectionsign
1, 2.4 and
\lx@sectionsign 3
, 2.5, and Chpt. I,
\lx@sectionsign 2
, 3.9 of
[
13
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\mathcal{H}
acts freely on
\mathcal{X}
and the quotient
(\mathcal{Y},\pi)
exists, then
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{X}
and
\pi
is faithfully flat and affine.

5.1.
The fppf topology

It is well known that quotient schemes need not exist already when
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Vec}
. However if we work in the category of sheaves in the fppf topology, which we call faisceaux, then a quotient always exists. Before we explain this, let us recall a few details about the fppf topology. A morphism
R\to R^{\prime}
is called fppf if it is faithfully flat and finitely presented. Open covers are collections of morphisms
R\to R_{i}
such that
R\to\prod R_{i}
is fppf. One can show that a presheaf
\mathcal{F}
is a faisceau if and only if it preserves products and for every fppf morphism
R\to R^{\prime}
, the following is an equalizer:

\mathcal{F}(R)\to\left(\mathcal{F}(R^{\prime})\rightrightarrows\mathcal{F}(R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}R^{\prime})\right).

The following is standard, see
[
8
, Lemma 4.3.5]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a scheme, then
\mathcal{X}
is a sheaf in the fppf topology.

5.2.
Affine quotients

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{Spec}A
is affine with a free action of
\mathcal{H}
, and let
B=A^{\mathcal{H}}
, so that we have a natural map
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{Spec}B
.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}
if and only if
B\to A
is fppf and the ‘canonical’ map (following the terminology of
[
34
]
)

\mathbf{can}:A\otimes_{B}A\to A\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}],

given by
i_{1}\otimes a
is an isomorphism. Here
i_{1}
is inclusion of
A
into the first tensor, and
a
is the coaction
A\to A\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]
.

Proof.

The forward direction is Lemma
5.1
. For the converse, our second condition implies that we have an isomorphism of equalizer diagrams

(A\rightrightarrows A\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}])\cong(A\rightrightarrows A\otimes_{B}A),

and thus an isomorphism of co-equalizer diagrams:

(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X})\cong(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{X}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X}).

The right diagram has co-equaliser
\mathcal{Y}
, by
[
8
, Proposition 4.26]
, and thus the left diagram does too.
∎

5.3.
The faisceau
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H}

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
acts freely on
\mathcal{X}
; the case of particular interest to us is when
\mathcal{H}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
, and
\mathcal{H}
acts on
\mathcal{G}
by right multiplication. Write
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885pt0}\mathcal{H}
for the presheaf given by

This is clearly the quotient in the category of presheaves. We set
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H}
to be the associated faisceau of
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885pt0}\mathcal{H}
. By its universal property,

will be the quotient of
\mathcal{X}
by
\mathcal{H}
in the category of faisceaux. It has the following explicit description: for an algebra

(\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H})(A)=\lim\limits_{\rightarrow}\operatorname{eq}[\mathcal{X}(B)/\mathcal{H}(B)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X}(B\otimes_{A}B)/\mathcal{H}(B\otimes_{A}B)],

where

(see, for instance,
[

, Sec. 5.4]
).

Lemma 5.4
.

If
\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}
exists, then it represents
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have an isomorphism of diagrams

(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X})\cong(\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{X}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X})

Clearly,
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H}
is the co-equalizer of the LHS diagram in the category of faisceaux. On the other hand, because
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}
is faithfully flat and affine (Lemma

),
\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}
is the co-equalizer of the RHS diagram in the category of faisceaux. Indeed we may check this affine locally on
\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{X}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H}
are co-equalizers of the same diagram in the category of faisceaux.
∎

Corollary 5.5

If
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}

Proof.

Indeed by

, we have
(\mathcal{G}/_{\hskip-1.63885ptf}\mathcal{H})(\Bbbk)=\mathcal{G}(\Bbbk)/\mathcal{H}(\Bbbk)

.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{0}\leq\mathcal{G}_{0}
are algebraic groups in
\operatorname{Vec}_{\Bbbk}

7.3.
Equivariant sheaves and induction

Recall that we write
\pi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
for the quotient morphism. Write

Proposition 7.11
.

The functors

and
(-)^{\mathcal{H}}\circ\pi_{*}:\operatorname{QCoh}^{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{G})\to\operatorname{QCoh}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
induce equivalences of categories.

Proof.

This result seems fairly standard, but we outline the proof. We may apply fppf descent to the morphism
\pi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
to obtain that
\operatorname{QCoh}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
is equivalent to the category of quasicoherent sheaves
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{G}
with an isomorphism
\alpha:p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\sim}p_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{F})
satisfying a cocycle condition. On the other hand, by Lemma
5.1
we have an isomorphism
\mathcal{G}\times_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
such that

corresponds to
p
and
p_{2}
corresponds to
a
. Further, the cocycle condition on

corresponds to the cocycle condition necessary for
\mathcal{F}
to be an
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant sheaf on
\mathcal{G}
. Thus these data are equivalent, and

is an equivalence. On the other hand, given an
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{G}
, the sections of the corresponding sheaf on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
over an open set

correspond to the equalizer of the diagram

\mathcal{F}(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}))\rightrightarrows\mathcal{F}(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}))\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}],

where the two arrows are the pullback morphisms along

and

-invariants.
∎

, so that
M
is a summand of
M\otimes H
, implying it is relatively injective.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
is clear.

(2)\iff(4)
Given
\phi:H\to A
, we set
A\otimes H\to A
to be

m_{A}\circ(1\otimes(\phi\circ S))\circ(1\otimes m_{H})\circ(\rho\otimes 1).

Conversely, given
\psi:A\otimes H\to A
, define
H\to A
by
\psi\circ(\eta\otimes\operatorname{id})
.
∎

Recall that given a right
H
-comodule
V
and a left
H
-comodule
W
, we may form the cotensor product
V\square_{H}W
defined by the equalizer:

(8.3)

V\square_{H}W\to V\otimes W\rightrightarrows V\otimes H\otimes W.

If
W
is also a right
H
-comodule (e.g.
W=H
), then
V\square_{H}W
admits the structure of a right
H
-comodule via viewing (
8.3
) in the category of right
H
-comodules. For a right
H
-comodule
V
, write
V^{tw}
for the left
H
-comodule on the same underlying object
V
but with coaction:

V\to V\otimes H\xrightarrow{\sigma}H\otimes V\xrightarrow{s_{H}\otimes 1}H\otimes V,

where
s_{H}
is the antipode. The following is proven in the case of
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Vec}
in, for instance,
[
34
, Lem. 3.1]
.

Lemma 8.5
.

The functor
(-)^{tw}
is an equivalence of categories between right and left
H
-comodules. Further, for any two right
H
-comodules
V,W
, we have

V\square_{H}W^{tw}\cong(V\otimes W)^{\mathcal{H}}.

We say that a right
H
-comodule
V
is coflat if the functor
V\square_{H}(-)
is exact.

Lemma 8.6

(Cor. 3.2 of
[
34
]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is a coflat
H
-comodule;

(2)

\mathcal{M}^{H}_{A}\to\mathcal{M}_{B}
,
M\mapsto M^{\mathcal{H}}
is exact.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
This functor is always left exact. On the other hand, since
M\square_{H}\mathbf{1}=M^{\mathcal{H}}
is a submodule of the left-hand side, we have an epimorphism

\psi:M\square_{H}A^{tw}\to M^{\mathcal{H}},

given by the module action of
A
on
M
. Since
(-)^{tw}
is an equivalence of categories between right and left
H
-comodules,
A^{tw}
will be a coflat left comodule, so that
M\mapsto M\square_{H}A^{tw}
is exact by assumption. From this a commutative diagram shows that
(-)^{\mathcal{H}}
will also preserve epimorphisms.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
For any right
H
-comodule
V
we have that
V\mapsto(V\otimes A)^{\mathcal{H}}
is exact. However by Lemma
8.5
,
(V\otimes A)^{\mathcal{H}}\cong V\square_{H}A^{tw}
, so this implies that
A^{tw}
is coflat, which is equivalent to
A
being coflat.
∎

In the following, note that
(-)\otimes_{B}A
is left adjoint to
(-)^{\mathcal{H}}
.

Lemma 8.7

(Lem. 3.4 of
[
34
]
)
.

If
A
is a relatively injective right
H
-comodule, then the unit morphism
M\to(M\otimes_{B}A)^{\mathcal{H}}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Indeed, it suffices to show that the canonical map

c:M\otimes_{B}(A\square_{H}\Bbbk)\to(M\otimes_{B}A)\square_{H}\Bbbk

is an isomorphism. Let
\phi:H\to A
be a total integral, and define a trace map
\operatorname{tr}:A\to B
by

\operatorname{tr}=m_{A}\circ(1\otimes\phi)\circ(1\otimes S)\circ\Delta.

Then an inverse to
c
is given by
1\otimes\operatorname{tr}
.
∎

The following is essentially
[
34
, Thm. 3.5]
.

Theorem 8.8
.

Suppose that
A
is a relatively injective
H
-comodule and either:

(1)

\mathbf{can}
is an isomorphism; or,

(2)

\mathbf{can}
is an epimorphism and
\mathscr{C}
is semisimple.

Then the induction functor
\mathcal{M}_{B}\to\mathcal{M}_{A}^{H}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.7
, the unit morphism of the adjunction
M\to(M\otimes_{B}A)^{\mathcal{H}}
is an isomorphism for
M\in\mathcal{M}_{B}
. Thus it remains to check that the other adjunction,
A\otimes_{B}N^{\mathcal{H}}\to N
is also an isomorphism for
N\in\mathcal{M}_{A}^{H}
.

For this, choose
\varphi:A\otimes H\to A
in
\mathcal{M}^{H}_{A}
which splits
\rho
, see Lemma
8.4
. Consider
f:N\otimes A\otimes H\to N
, given by

f=m\circ(1\otimes\varphi)\circ(1\otimes 1\otimes m)\circ(1\otimes 1\otimes S\otimes 1)\circ(1\otimes\sigma\otimes 1)\circ(\rho\otimes 1\otimes 1).

We view
N\otimes A\otimes H
as an
(A,H)
-Hopf module where
A
acts via the
A\otimes H
factor, and
H
coacts via the
H
-factor as in (
8.2
). Then
f
is a Hopf module morphism, and is split in
\mathscr{C}
via
(\operatorname{id}_{N}\otimes\eta_{A}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{H})\circ\rho_{N}
.

Now by our assumption on
\mathbf{can}
, we deduce that the composition
g=f\circ(\operatorname{id}_{N}\otimes\mathbf{can}):N\otimes A\otimes_{B}A\to N\otimes A\otimes H\to N
is a split surjection. We may view
N\otimes A\otimes_{B}A
as an
(A,H)
-Hopf module via the rightmost factor
A
. Then
g
is a morphism of Hopf modules, so its kernel
K
is once again a Hopf module. By Lemma
8.4
, we obtain that
K
is a relatively injective
H
-comodule, so that
g
is split as a
H
-comodule morphism.

Write
M=N\otimes A\otimes_{B}A
. Then we claim the adjunction morphism
M\to M^{\mathcal{H}}\otimes_{B}A
is an isomorphism. Indeed, we have
(N\otimes A\otimes_{B}A)^{\mathcal{H}}\cong N\otimes A
by our other adjunction, and from this it is clear. Since
N
is a direct summand of
M
as an
H
-comodule, the adjunction is also an isomorphism for it, and we are done.
∎

We may now complete the proof of Theorem
8.1
:

Conclusion of Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

We already proved
(4)\Rightarrow(3)
(in Theorem
8.8
) and
(1)\iff(2)\iff(3)
. Thus it remains to show that
(1)\Rightarrow(4)
. Because
\mathscr{C}
is semisimple,
A
is relatively injective if and only if it is injective if and only if it is coflat (where, as in the classical case, the latter equivalence reduces to the case of coalgebras in
\mathscr{C}_{fin}
). For a right
H
-comodule
V
, we have isomorphisms

A\otimes_{B}(A\square_{H}V)\cong(A\otimes_{B}A)\square_{H}V\cong(A\otimes H)\square_{H}V\cong A\otimes(H\square_{H}V)\cong A\otimes V.

Here, we have applied
[
39
, Prop. 1.3]
for the first isomorphism, our assumption that
\mathbf{can}:A\otimes_{B}A\to A\otimes H
is an isomorphism for the second isomorphism, and the isomorphism
H\square_{H}V\cong V
which holds for any comodule. Since
B\to A
is faithfully flat, we learn that
A\square_{H}-
is exact, meaning that
A
is coflat, which completes our proof.
∎

9.
Finer description of homogeneous spaces for
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}

We now specialize to
\mathscr{C}=\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
, which we assume for the remainder of this paper. We will give another proof of the existence of quotients in
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
by mimicking the proof for
\operatorname{sVec}
from
[
27
]
.
This is an important special case, since by the main theorem of
[
9
]
, it is the only moderate growth, incompressible tensor category (together with its tensor subcategories) that is semisimple (more generally: Frobenius exact).
We will provide a more explicit description of homogeneous spaces using the semisimplicity of
\mathscr{C}
. In what follows we write
\mathfrak{g}
for the Lie algebra of
\mathcal{G}
.

We begin by recalling the following theorem, which is proven in
[
40
, Lem. 7.15]
and
[
25
, Thm. 5.1]
. Recall the notation
X_{\neq 0}
from Example
2.3
.

Theorem 9.1
.

We have an isomorphism of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-modules and algebras:

\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\cong\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\otimes S\mathfrak{g}_{\neq 0}^{*},

where
\mathcal{G}_{0}
acts by infinitesimal right translation on both
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]
and
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]
, and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{\neq 0}
via the adjoint action.

Let
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
be a subgroup, and write
p_{0}:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
for the quotient morphism which we know exists in
\operatorname{Vec}
. We would like to construct a quotient
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
with
(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
; in particular,
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
will have the same underlying topological spaces. To do this, we will construct a quotient affine locally, and glue.

The following lemma is implicitly used in
[
27
]
. For an open subscheme
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}
, write
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
for the open subscheme of
\mathcal{G}
it determines. Observe that if
\mathcal{V}
is stable under translation by
\mathcal{H}_{0}
on the right, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
will be stable under translation by
\mathcal{H}
on the right.

Lemma 9.2
.

Suppose that for every affine open subscheme
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
there exists an affine quotient
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}}\cong\widetilde{p_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})}/\mathcal{H}
with
(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}})_{0}=\mathcal{U}
. Suppose further that whenever
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
is an inclusion of affine opens, the natural map
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}}
is the open embedding of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}
into
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}}
determined by
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
. Then we may glue the schemes
\{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}}\}
to construct a quotient
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Under these assumptions we may glue the schemes
\{\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{U}}\}
using the universal property of the quotient to check that the gluing conditions hold.
∎

We now write

W_{\mathcal{G}}:=(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{G}}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{G}}^{2})_{\neq 0}\cong(\mathfrak{g}_{\neq 0})^{*},\ \ \ \ W_{\mathcal{H}}:=(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{H}}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})_{\neq 0}\cong(\mathfrak{h}_{\neq 0})^{*}.

The natural quotient map
\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]
induces an epimorphism
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Thus we may define
Z\in\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{H}_{0}
by the following SES of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-modules:

0\to Z\to W_{\mathcal{G}}\to W_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
be an affine open which we fix for the remainder of the paper, and consider
p_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Write
B=\Bbbk[\mathcal{U}]
and
A:=\Bbbk[p_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})]
. We would like to describe
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}
locally on
\mathcal{U}
in terms of
A
and
Z
. To this end, we consider the algebra
S_{A}(Z)=A\otimes S(Z)
which lives in
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}
(see Section 8 for notation). Then we set:

\mathbb{A}:=S_{A}(Z)\square_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}],

and

\mathbb{B}:=S_{A}(Z)^{\mathcal{H}_{0}}=S_{A}(Z)\square_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}\Bbbk.

The following mimics
[
27
, Prop. 4.2]
and is proven verbatim.

Proposition 9.3
.

(1)

\mathbb{A}
is a finitely generated algebra, is injective as an
\mathcal{H}
-module, and we have
\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{H}}\cong\mathbb{B}
.

(2)

\mathbb{B}
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded subalgebra of
S_{A}(Z)
with
\mathbb{B}_{0}=B
, and
\mathbb{B}_{1}=(A\otimes Z)^{\mathcal{H}_{0}}
is a finitely generated projective
B
-module with every fibre isomorphic to
Z
. Further, the natural map

S_{B}(\mathbb{B}_{1})\to\mathbb{B}

is an isomorphism of algebras. In particular,
\mathbb{B}
is also Noetherian.

Lemma 9.4
.

The inclusion
A\otimes Z\hookrightarrow A\otimes W_{\mathcal{G}}
splits in
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}
. Further, if
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
is an affine open subscheme of
\mathcal{U}
, and we set
A^{\prime}:=\Bbbk[\pi_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})]
then this splitting can be chosen compatibly for both
A
and
A^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We follow
[
14
, Thm. 1.7 (ii)]
. By Lemma
8.4
, there exists a total integral
\phi:\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]\to A
, and a total integral for
A^{\prime}
may be chosen by postcomposing
\phi
with the natural morphism
A\to A^{\prime}
. Choose a splitting
W_{\mathcal{G}}\to Z
in
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
of the inclusion
Z\subseteq W_{\mathcal{G}}
, and use this to induce a splitting
p:A\otimes W_{\mathcal{G}}\to A\otimes Z
of
A
-modules. Set
q=\lambda_{A\otimes W_{\mathcal{G}}}\circ(p\otimes 1)\circ\rho_{A\otimes W_{\mathcal{G}}}
, where
\lambda_{M}:M\otimes A\to M
is given by

\lambda_{M}=m_{A}\circ(1\otimes\phi)\circ(1\otimes m_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]})\circ(1\otimes S\otimes 1)\circ(\rho_{M}\otimes 1).

Then one can check that
q
is our desired splitting. Since
q
only depends on a choice of total integral
\phi:\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]\to A
and our splitting
W_{\mathcal{G}}\to Z
, and these can be chosen compatibly for
A
and
A^{\prime}
via the natural map
A\to A^{\prime}
, we have the desired compatibility as well.
∎

Using Lemma
9.4
, choose a retraction
A\otimes W_{\mathcal{G}}\to A\otimes Z
in
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}
. Define
\psi:\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\to\mathbb{A}
by:

\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\xrightarrow{\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}}\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}]\square_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]\cong(\Bbbk[\mathcal{G}_{0}]\otimes S(W_{\mathcal{G}}))\square_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]\to(A\otimes S(Z))\square_{\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}_{0}]}\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]=\mathbb{A}

Lemma 9.5
.

The morphism
\psi
corresponds to an
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant open embedding of schemes
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{A}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}
corresponding to the open embedding
\widetilde{\pi_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Pass to the associated graded as in
[
27
, Prop. 4.8]
, and use Proposition
3.26
and Lemma
9.6
.
∎

In the following lemma, for a commutative algebra
R
we write
\operatorname{gr}R=\bigoplus\limits_{i}J_{R}^{i}/J_{R}^{i+1}
.

Lemma 9.6
.

Suppose that
\phi^{*}:R\to S
is a morphism of algebras such that
J_{R}
is nilpotent. Then the induced map
\phi:\operatorname{Spec}S\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is an open immersion if and only if
\operatorname{gr}\phi:\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}R)
is an open immersion. The latter condition holds if we have both

(1)

\operatorname{Spec}S/J_{S}\to\operatorname{Spec}R/J_{R}
is an open immersion; and,

(2)

for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}S
, the map on stalks
\operatorname{gr}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\operatorname{gr}S_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

If
\phi
is an open immersion, it is clear that
\operatorname{gr}\phi
is as well. Conversely, if
\operatorname{gr}\phi
is an open immersion then there exists elements
\{f_{i}\in R/J_{R}:i\in I\}
inducing isomorphisms

(9.1)

(\operatorname{gr}R)_{f_{i}}\cong(\operatorname{gr}S)_{f_{i}},

and
\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}S)=\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}S)_{f_{i}}
. If we choose lifts
f_{i}^{\prime}\in R_{(0)}
of the elements
f_{i}
, then the natural maps
R_{f_{i}^{\prime}}\to S_{f_{i}^{\prime}}
will induce the isomorphisms (
9.1
) on the associated graded. Since
J_{R}
is a nilpotent ideal, this isomorphism must reflect an isomorphism of algebras
R_{f_{i}^{\prime}}\cong S_{f_{i}^{\prime}}
, and from here it is easy to deduce that
\operatorname{gr}\phi
is an open immersion. The equivalence of
\operatorname{gr}\phi
being an open immersion with conditions (1) and (2) is straightforward.∎

Lemma 9.7
.

The morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{A}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{B}
realizes
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{B}\cong(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{A})/\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.1
, it suffices to check that
\mathbb{A}
is an injective
\mathcal{H}
-module and that our canonical morphism is a surjection. We showed that
\mathbb{A}
is injective in Proposition
9.3
, so we only need to show that the canonical morphism

\mathbb{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{B}}\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{H}]

is an epimorphism. However, this follows from the fact that
\mathcal{H}
acts freely on
\mathcal{G}
, meaning that
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
is a closed embedding.
∎

Lemma 9.8
.

We have compatibility of quotients upon restriction to open subschemes.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that we could choose our splittings compatibly, see Lemma
9.4
.
∎

We have now given another proof of the existence of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
in
\operatorname{Ver}_{p}
. However, we have done more by giving an explicit local construction of this quotient scheme. In particular, we learn the following.

Theorem 9.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an algebraic group, and let
\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
be a closed subgroup. Then:

(1)

we have an identification:
(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
;

(2)

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}/\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is the
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-equivariant sheaf on
\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
with fibre
Z=\operatorname{ker}(W_{\mathcal{G}}\to W_{\mathcal{H}})
;

(3)

\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is affine locally isomorphic to the scheme
(|\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}|,S(\mathcal{F}^{Z}))
(see Proposition
7.12
for meaning of
\mathcal{F}^{Z}
).

Proof.

The proofs of (1) and (2) follow from our work above, while (3) is explained in
[
27
, Rmk. 4.3]
.
∎

Remark 9.10
.

Theorem
9.9
, especially part (3), has important implications in representation theory, as it tells us that the associated graded of a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant sheaf on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is given by a directly computable
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-equivariant sheaf on
\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
. This fact has been used heavily in the computation of characters of irreducible representations of supergroups, as briefly discussed in the introduction.

Evaluation of Diagnostic Accuracy of Combined Ultrasound Elastography and Magnetic Resonance Imaging in Breast Cancer
Authors: Penghua Zhang, Yang Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of combined ultrasound elastography and magnetic resonance imaging in patients with breast cancer.MethodsThis was a retrospective diagnostic accuracy study. We designated 60 patients diagnosed with breast cancer between August 2021 and November 2023 as the case group, and selected 40 patients diagnosed with benign breast lesions during the same period as the control group. Both the case and control groups underwent multimodal ultrasound and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) examinations. Pathological findings served as the gold standard, and a blinded evaluation was conducted to assess the diagnostic efficacy of multimodal ultrasound (strain and shear wave), MRI, and combined diagnosis for breast cancer.ResultsBased on the pathological findings as the gold standard, multimodal ultrasound demonstrated a sensitivity of 55.00% and specificity of 83.33% for the diagnosis of breast cancer, MRI exhibited a sensitivity of 73.17% and specificity of 81.67% and the combined diagnosis demonstrated a sensitivity of 87.80% and specificity of 96.67%. The diagnostic accuracy and specificity of combined detection exceeded those of any single detection (P<0.05). The elasticity score and strain ratio value between the lesion and normal tissue in the multimodal ultrasound of patients with benign breast tumors were significantly lower than those of patients with malignant breast tumors (P<0.05). In malignant lesions, the mean elasticity modulus, standard deviation (SD), and elasticity ratio between the lesion and normal tissue in shear wave elastography were significantly higher compared to benign lesions (P<0.05). The MRI indicators, including mean vascular density and enhancement rate, were significantly higher in patients with malignant breast lesions compared to those with benign lesions, while the peak time was notably lower in patients with benign lesions (P<0.05).ConclusionMultimodal ultrasound and MRI demonstrate good diagnostic efficacy for benign and malignant lesions of breast cancer.Keywords: breast cancer, multimodal ultrasound, magnetic resonance, combined detection, diagnostic efficacy

Introduction

The incidence of breast cancer is predominantly observed among women aged 40–60 years, and in recent years, the incidence of breast cancer in China has gradually increased.1,2 Early-stage breast cancer often presents with subtle clinical manifestations, and by the time pronounced symptoms appear, the optimal treatment window has frequently been missed. Therefore, early diagnosis and treatment are of great significance to the prognosis for patients.3,4 In current clinical practice for diagnosing breast cancer, primary approaches involve pathological examination, imaging surveillance, etc. However, the former method is invasive and has longer detection cycles, rendering it unsuitable for widespread clinical implementation.5 The latter includes techniques such as ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and mammography, which hold significant value in diagnosing and distinguishing breast diseases. Traditional X-ray detection methods exhibit a lower level of accuracy in diagnosing breast cancer, with a 50% omission diagnostic rate of breast cancer, and sensitivity decreases as breast density increases.6

Currently, the employment of ultrasonographic methods for detecting breast diseases is widespread. Ultrasonography has the advantages of rapid detection, non-invasiveness, high repeatability, and high resolution, which has become a commonly used first-line imaging modality for breast diseases and plays an important role in the diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer.7,8 Ultrasound elastography provides complementary information for differentiating benign and malignant breast lesions by assessing tissue stiffness. However, previous studies have largely been limited to assessing strain elastography or shear wave elastography alone, lacking systematic evaluations of combined applications of both techniques. Furthermore, few studies have evaluated their diagnostic efficacy in combination with other advanced imaging modalities such as MRI. Standardized combined diagnostic strategies and diagnostic workflows have not yet been established. MRI has the advantages of high soft tissue resolution and clear visualization. Recent research has indicated that MRI has certain application value in the early detection of breast cancer.9 In recent years, several studies have explored the combined application of ultrasound imaging (including elastography) and MRI for differentiating benign and malignant breast lesions. For instance, an MRI-ultrasound fusion model based on radiomics achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.91 in the validation cohort for distinguishing benign from malignant breast lesions.10 Additionally, a single-center retrospective study demonstrated that the combination of mammography, shear wave elastography, and MRI provided higher diagnostic performance compared with any two-modality combination.11,12 However, the aforementioned studies have mainly focused on radiomics modeling or mammography-guided diagnosis. Research specifically investigating the combined use of ultrasound elastography (especially the combination of strain and shear wave techniques) and MRI, incorporating clinically applicable cutoff values for differentiating benign and malignant breast lesions, remains limited. Although ultrasound elastography and MRI each have their own advantages, their use alone in distinguishing benign from malignant breast lesions may still result in missed and incorrected diagnoses. Currently, research on the combined application of these two modalities for the differentiation of benign and malignant breast lesions remains limited.

Building on the aforementioned background and existing research gaps, this study hypothesizes that the combined application of the two ultrasound elastography techniques can complement the inherent limitations of each technique and enhance diagnostic accuracy. Furthermore, the integration of ultrasound elastography with MRI enables complementary advantages between functional and morphological imaging features. Based on the above hypotheses, this study aims to investigate the value of their combined usage in the discrimination of breast cancer. The analysis is presented below.

Materials and Methods

Patient Inclusion Design

This was a retrospective diagnostic accuracy study conducted between August 2021 and November 2023. Patients who underwent pathological and imaging examinations at our institution during the study period were identified from the institutional medical database and consecutively included according to the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria rather than selected by convenience sampling, thereby reducing potential selection bias. This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Baoji Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (approval number BJSZYYY-LWTG-20251023). Given the retrospective nature of the study and the use of anonymized data, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Patients who underwent pathological and imaging examinations in our hospital, with a definitive clinical diagnosis (diagnosed as either malignant or benign breast lesions); (2) Patients who had complete baseline clinical data (age, BMI index), imaging data [quantitative MRI indicators such as mean vascular density (MVD), enhancement rate, peak time, shear wave elastography indicators such as mean elasticity modulus (Emean), standard deviation (SD), and elasticity ratio between the lesion and normal tissue (Ratio) values, strain elastography indicators such as elastography score and strain ratio between the lesion and normal tissue (SR)] in the case database; (3) No relevant surgical, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy interventions were received in the month preceding imaging examination.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Women who were pregnant or lactating; (2) Patients who had been enrolled in other unfinished clinical studies; (3) Those with a history of psychiatric disorders; (4) Those with drug or alcohol dependence.

After screening according to the above inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 60 patients with malignant breast cancer admitted to Baoji Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine were included in the case group, while 40 patients with benign breast cancer in the same hospital were included in the control group.

Data Collection

Data of the included patients were collected relying on the hospital case database, and the data collected included baseline clinical information (age, BMI), MRI quantitative indicators of MVD, enhancement rate, and peak time, shear wave elastography indicators of Emean, SD, and Ratio values, and strain elastography indicators of elasticity scores and SR values for the patients in both the case and control groups.

Ultrasound Elastography Examination Method

Ultrasound elastography was performed using a Siemens ACUSON S3000 ultrasound system with a linear array probe. Participants were positioned supine with the breast fully exposed. Initial conventional two-dimensional gray-scale ultrasound examination was performed, followed by strain elastography. The probe was gently placed perpendicular to the lesion surface, applying pressure at a frequency of 1–2 times per second. Lesions were assessed using a modified 5-point scale (1–3 points indicating benign, 4–5 points indicating malignant). The SR between the lesion and normal tissue was measured. The average value was calculated from three consecutive measurements. Subsequently, SWE mode was activated for shear wave imaging detection. The Emean, SD, and Ratio were recorded, with each parameter measured three consecutive times and averaged.

Diagnostic Criteria

In multimodal ultrasound, the strain elastography malignant criteria indicated that a lesion scoring between 4 and 5 under the UE mode was classified as malignant, whereas a score of 1 to 3 indicated benign;13 For shear wave elastography, cutoff values of Emean ≥ 45.3 kPa and Ratio ≥ 4.5 were applied for the diagnosis of malignant lesions.14 A serial strategy was used for the combined diagnosis, in which ultrasound elastography was deemed positive only when both strain elastography and shear wave elastography yield positive results, thereby enhancing diagnostic specificity. The cutoff values for shear wave elastography (Emean ≥ 45.3 kPa, Ratio ≥ 4.5) were adopted from the optimal diagnostic thresholds determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis in a previous study,14 which demonstrated good diagnostic performance. The serial diagnostic strategy was selected rather than parallel testing or weighted score integration for the following reasons: (1) The serial strategy requires both elastography techniques to yield positive results before a lesion is classified as positive, which can effectively reduce false-positive rates and improve diagnostic specificity. This strategy is more consistent with the clinical needs of avoiding unnecessary biopsies. (2) While strain elastography and shear wave elastography partially overlap in reflecting tissue mechanical properties, their imaging principles are not entirely the same. The serial strategy enables the complementary advantages of the two approaches without introducing complex weighting parameters, thereby improving clinical feasibility and facilitating broader application. (3) Compared with weighted score integration, the serial strategy does not require the establishment and validation of a weighting model, which may reduce the potential risk of overfitting. Therefore, it may be more suitable for exploratory analysis with the current sample size of this study.

MRI malignancy criteria: Lesion morphology exhibits irregularity, infiltrative growth, with rough appearance at the lesion margins, locally presenting spiculated, and indistinct boundaries.15

Result Analysis

Utilizing a blinded approach, two radiologists analyzed the multimodal ultrasound and MRI findings of the included patients. Consensus between the two radiologists was required; however, in cases of disagreement, the assistance of a third radiologist may be sought to definitively ascertain the type of lesion present in the patient.

Utilizing the pathological findings as the gold standard, the discriminative value of strain elastography in multimodal ultrasound, shear wave elastography in multimodal ultrasound, MRI, and combined detection (utilizing a sequential approach) were assessed separately for the differentiation of benign and malignant breast tumors.

Statistical Methods

The data compilation in this study was carried out using EXCEL 2021, while data analysis was performed using SPSS 21.0. The processing showed that continuous data in the study such as Emean and SD values conformed to normal distribution, and t-tests were used for comparison between groups. Categorical data such as sensitivity and specificity were described by %, and chi-square tests were used for comparison between groups. Diagnostic efficacy was analyzed by plotting the ROC curves (the software applied was Graph Prism 9.1). The difference was considered statistically significant at P<0.05. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy were calculated using the Wilson score method. For the comparison of diagnostic performance, the AUC and its 95% CI were estimated using the normal approximation method based on sensitivity and specificity estimates [AUC = (sensitivity + specificity)/2].

Results

Baseline Data and Analysis of the Diagnostic Value of Different Imaging Techniques for Breast Cancer

The average age of the patients in the case group was (53.13±10.21) years, with a BMI index of (22.63±2.51) kg/m,2 and in the control group, the average age was (52.98±12.32) years, with a BMI index was (22.51±3.01) kg/m.2 There was no statistically significant difference in the age and BMI index of the patients in the case group and the control group (P>0.05), indicating good comparability between the two groups of patients. Based on the pathological findings as the gold standard, the analysis revealed that multimodal ultrasound demonstrated a sensitivity of 55.00% and specificity of 83.33% for the diagnosis of breast cancer, MRI exhibited a sensitivity of 73.17% and specificity of 81.67%, and the combined diagnosis demonstrated a sensitivity of 87.80% and specificity of 96.67% (Tables 1–4). Comparison between groups showed that the diagnostic accuracy and specificity of combined detection exceeded those of any single detection (P<0.05) (Figures 1 and 2).

Discussion

Breast cancer is a malignancy that mainly occurs in the epithelial tissues of the mammary gland,16 and its incidence has shown an upward trend in recent years. According to available data, the global incidence of breast cancer increased from 641,000 cases in 1980 to 1.7 million cases in 2012, with an annual growth rate reaching as high as 3.1%.17 According to global cancer statistics, in 2020, there were approximately 92.3 million new cases of cancer worldwide, with breast cancer accounting for 22.6 million of these cases, significantly surpassing other types of cancer in females, and the incidence rate of breast cancer is approximately equivalent to the sum of ranking 2–4.18 The incidence of breast cancer in China similarly manifests a continuous growth trend, with 72,000 cases succumbing to breast cancer in 2022. Breast cancer manifests as an irregular and uncontrolled abnormal proliferation of breast epithelial cells due to various stimuli, and its primary manifestation is the malignant proliferation of cancer cells, which disrupts surrounding normal tissue, consequently altering the normal structure of the breast.19,20 Early-stage breast cancer often lacks typical clinical manifestations, leading to neglect. As the disease progresses to advanced stages, symptoms such as masses, changes in the skin, and invasion of axillary lymph nodes may manifest, significantly impacting the patient’s normal life. Therefore, timely and accurate analysis of the type of lesion is of great importance for subsequent treatment.21

Utilizing the pathological findings as the gold standard, the application value of multimodal ultrasound, MRI, and combined detection were assessed separately for the differentiation of benign and malignant breast tumors. The results showed that in terms of strain elastography indicators, patients with malignant breast lesions exhibited significantly higher elasticity scores and SR values compared to those with benign lesions; in terms of shear wave elastography indicators, patients with malignant breast tumors demonstrated significantly higher Emean, SD, and Ratio values than those with benign lesions. This is similar to the results of other studies.22 Early research has abundantly demonstrated the valuable application of ultrasound in discerning the benign or malignant breast tumors. For instance, a study involving 90 suspected breast cancer patients23 highlighted the non-invasive, convenient, and easily adaptable advantages of ultrasound, but conventional two-dimensional ultrasound diagnosis has limited diagnostic value, merely showing the vascularization around the lesion. Given the abundant glandular tissue in the breast, which can obscure the lesion signal, the blood flow signal in the lesion area of patients may not be distinctly echoed, leading to situations of misdiagnoses and missed diagnoses.24 Multimodal ultrasound exhibits clearer advantages over two-dimensional ultrasound, with notable enhancements in diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy, thereby bearing significant implications for discerning the nature of breast tumors.

The findings of the study also indicated significant differences in quantitative MRI indicators between malignant breast tumor lesions and benign lesions, as evidenced by significantly higher MVD, enhancement rate, and significantly lower peak time. The authors of this study contend that MRI is a commonly used clinical imaging detection means, which mainly uses a strong magnetic field pulse to elicit resonance signals from hydrogen atoms within breast tissue, thereby providing information about the location, size, and morphology of lesions. Particularly adept at discerning soft tissue characteristics, MRI has been widely used in the differentiation between benign and malignant tumors.25,26 A study focusing on lung cancer patients has indicated that MRI demonstrates an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.791 in distinguishing early-stage invasive adenocarcinoma of the lung, with a sensitivity of 73.72% and a specificity of 73.33%.27 A research involving prostate cancer differentiation has pointed out that diffusion-weighted MRI ADC values have a high sensitivity in the differentiation of prostatic hyperplasia from prostate cancer.28 The findings of this study were also in line with the results of above-mentioned research. Nonetheless, the authors of this study similarly believe that a singular imaging modality is insufficient for a comprehensive and effective evaluation of breast tumor lesions, and carries the risk of misdiagnoses and missed diagnoses, thereby impacting diagnostic accuracy.

It was found in this study that by integrating multimodal ultrasound with MRI in the discrimination between benign and malignant breast tumors, diagnostic sensitivity could be enhanced to 87.80% and specificity to 96.67%. Following the plotting of the ROC curve, the calculated AUC was 0.956. These findings affirmed the diagnostic efficacy of combined detection. The authors of this study contend that the advantages of multimodal ultrasound diagnosis lie in its non-invasive and convenient nature, enabling rapid depiction of the location, internal echoes, and relationships with surrounding tissues of breast tumors, thereby providing references for preliminary identification of breast tumor nature.29 However, this study also has limitations such as sample size and single-center design. Furthermore, the study did not adequately document the failure rate due to technical reasons (eg, poor image quality, patient non-compliance), which may have overestimated the actual feasibility of clinical application. It should also be noted that ultrasound elastography remains limited by high operator dependency, tissue depth constraints, susceptibility to lesion characteristics, and insufficient technical standardization. In contrast, MRI carries risks of overdiagnosis, interference from background parenchymal enhancement, high costs, and numerous contraindications. Although combined diagnosis can enhance diagnostic efficiency, it cannot completely eliminate the aforementioned limitations.

Furthermore, in this study, strain elastography scores of 4‒5, shear wave elastography parameters of Emean ≥ 45.3 kPa and Ratio ≥ 4.5, as well as quantitative MRI indicators including increased MVD and enhancement rate with shortened peak time, demonstrated clinically meaningful discriminatory thresholds between benign and malignant lesions, suggesting their potential value as adjunctive tools for routine clinical decision-making. In the future, these quantitative parameters could be incorporated into structured imaging reporting templates and combined with the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) classification to establish a standardized multimodal diagnostic workflow, thereby improving interobserver agreement among radiologists with different levels of experience. Meanwhile, integrating strain elastography scores, shear wave elastography parameters, and quantitative MRI indicators through weighted scoring systems or machine learning models may further facilitate standardized and intelligent diagnostic decision-making. However, this requires validation through subsequent prospective studies with larger sample sizes.

Conclusions

Despite the technical limitations of ultrasound elastography and MRI, such as operator dependency in ultrasound and the risk of overdiagnosis in MRI, the combined use of these two modalities significantly improves breast cancer diagnostic accuracy through complementary advantages. Nevertheless, clinical application should balance diagnostic benefits against increased costs and acknowledge limitations in diagnosing specific pathological subtypes. Future research should focus on multicenter, large-sample studies incorporating a broader range of pathological types, alongside health economic evaluations, to determine optimal clinical application strategies. Notably, the relatively small sample size, single-center retrospective design, and lack of an external independent validation cohort may limit the generalizability of our findings, which requires further validation in future studies.

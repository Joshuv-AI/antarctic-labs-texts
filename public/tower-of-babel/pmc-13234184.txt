Exact solutions of nonlinear thermodynamic wave patterns in graphene sheets for advanced material applications
Authors: Muhammad Abdaal Bin Iqbal, Muhammad Zubair Raza, Yakup Yildirim, Jalil Manafian, Hadi Rezazadeh, Younis A Sabawi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGraphene has emerged as a highly attractive nanomaterial, characterized by its distinctive thermodynamic, electrical, and mechanical features. These attributes render it ideal for a wide array of purposes across materials research, storing energy, computing, filtering water, and medication. In this study, we investigate the nonlinear thermophoretic wrinkle motion equation describing wave propagation and thermal-driven deformation in graphene sheets. Governing model is -dimensional graphene sheets (GS) equation, focusing on the component heat transfer during thermodynamic motion. The study employs modified generalized exponential differential function method and the improved Cham method. These techniques are employed to analyze the nonlinear behavior of wave propagation in GS, leading to exact wave solutions such as bright, bell, periodic, and twofold waves. The creative application of these analytical techniques provides efficient structures for tackling complex nonlinear theories in mathematical physics, thereby enhancing solution strategies for these equations. This study makes a notable contribution to computational mathematics, material research, and nanotechnology by providing exact solutions and deepening our comprehension of graphene non linear properties. The findings present significant implications, suggesting potential applications in the design of novel materials with customized properties to facilitate technological progress, thereby expanding the frontiers of nanotechnology and materials research.Keywords: -dimensional Graphene sheet, Modified generalized exponential differential function method, Improved champ method, Two fold soliton, Bell soliton, Bright soliton, Periodic solitonSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

Andre Geim, Konstantin Novoselov, and their team created the first graphene flakes in 2004; they were awarded the Nobel Prize for their achievement in 2010. Due to the intriguing thermal, electrical, conductivity, and mechanical properties of those flakes, which promise a wide range of industrial uses, extensive scientific research has been conducted since then and continues to this day on that incredible innovation1,2.

Understanding nonlinear wave propagation in GS is important because such wave structures significantly influence the mechanical stability, energy transport, and deformation behavior of nanoscale materials3,4. Thermally induced wrinkle formation frequently occurs in graphene layers and plays an important role in determining their structural and electronic properties. Mathematical modeling of these phenomena provides valuable theoretical insight into how thermal gradients and nonlinear interactions affect wave propagation within graphene surfaces. Therefore, obtaining exact analytical solutions of the governing nonlinear evolution equations is essential for predicting the behavior of graphene-based nanostructures and designing materials with controllable mechanical and thermal characteristics5–7.

Numerous significant technical applications for graphene-based composites for optical systems, automobiles, and neural implants have been discovered due to the special mechanical qualities of graphene flakes, such as rigidity, durability, and stiffness. Furthermore, graphene has been utilized in battery packs8, solar power cells, and photovoltaic devices9 due to its low cost, light weight, and flexibility as a 2D nanomaterial with excellent energy storage. Additionally, graphene sheets strong conductivity qualities have been utilized as biosensors to identify problem10,11.

The thermophoretic wrinkle motion equation models the propagation of deformation waves in GS subjected to thermal gradients. Thermophoresis refers to the motion of particles or structural disturbances induced by temperature differences within a medium. In GS, thermal fluctuations can generate localized stresses that produce wrinkle-like deformation patterns propagating along the surface.

In Eq.(1), the nonlinear convection term represents the interaction between the wave amplitude and its spatial gradient, which leads to nonlinear wave steepening. The third-order derivative term represents dispersive effects that counterbalance the nonlinear steepening and allow the formation of solitary waves similar to those observed in the classical KDV equation. The additional term incorporates thermophoretic effects related to thermal conductivity within the GS, which influence the propagation speed and acceleration of wrinkle structures. Consequently, the equation captures the combined effects of nonlinearity, dispersion, and thermally driven transport in graphene-based materials. Many significant methods, such as Lie group analysis18, Bäcklund transformation19, Improved F-expansion20–22, Exp-function23, Jacobi elliptic function24, Variational iteration25, Bifurcation analysis26–29, Direct algebraic30, Modified sub-equation31, Modified auxiliary equation32, Extended tanh33, Darboux transformation34, Hirota’s bilinear35, Kumar–Malik36, extended modified auxiliary equation mapping37,38, 39, generalized unified40, improved tanh41,42, rational expansion43, extended expansion44, and unified45 methods have found numerous exact soliton solutions. These techniques are essential because they provide useful solutions in a variety of scientific contexts in addition to theoretical insights. The modified generalized exponential differential function method (MGEDFM)46 and improved champ method (ICM)47 are two novel, potent methods that we employ in this study to produce computational solutions for the suggested model. In order to obtain exact solutions to NPDEs, that control wave motions in complicated medium, MGEDFM and ICM are utilized in non-linear wave propagation. The solitonic wave patterns that control wave propagation in GS are effectively captured by these techniques. Because they simplify the equations and enable exact solutions. These techniques offer a methodical framework for examining spread of stress and distortion in GS and reduce the complicated topology of the proposed model. The intricate physics of GS are better understood when these two methods of analysis are combined. Our findings are mechanically significant for several uses, such as the manufacturing of GS polymers, electronics, and technology48,49. This work presents new analytical techniques that greatly improve our comprehension of the intricate behavior of GS, a material that is crucial to contemporary materials science50,51. By providing reliable data that could support the creation of customized materials for a variety of technological applications52,53, the findings significantly advances applied mathematics and material research. The (2+1)-D model that characterizes GS motion was the main focus of this investigation. The analytical techniques and subsequent calculations utilized to verify the results essentially dictated this investigation. Because of this, the results may not be as applicable to more complicated or higher-dimensional models that incorporate additional factors influencing graphene behavior.

Although several analytical techniques have been applied to investigate nonlinear evolution equations arising in graphene dynamics, the application of recently developed analytical approaches to the thermophoretic wrinkle motion model remains limited. In particular, MGEDFM and ICM have not been systematically employed for analyzing the governing model. The novelty of this work lies in applying these two powerful analytical techniques to derive new exact wave solutions describing nonlinear thermodynamic wave patterns in GS. The obtained solutions provide additional insight into the dynamics of thermally induced deformation waves in graphene-based materials.

Discussion and physical interpretation of results

Significant new information about the mechanical characteristics of graphene sheets can be gained from MGEDFA and ICM. Different solitonic and periodic wave forms are depicted in the visuals, which are analyzed in 3D, 3D contour, and 2D formats. A bell soliton is depicted in Figs. 1 and 2, highlighting the GS constrained energy concentration. A twofold soliton, shown in Fig. 3, is an example of coherent wave propagation with very little dispersion. Figures 4, 5, 6, and 7 shows a bright soliton. Figure 8 shows a M soliton solution, showing repeated oscillations within the material. Thus, GS are mechanically stable and an excellent candidate for application in nanotechnology and structural mechanics. We demonstrate that MGEDFA and ICM are complementary techniques for understanding the nonlinear mechanics of graphene. These findings suggest potential for additional research into graphene-based materials with enhanced mechanical characteristics.

The graphical representations provide a clear visualization of the nonlinear wave dynamics predicted by the analytical solutions. The bell-shaped soliton structures illustrate localized energy concentration within the GS, which can correspond to localized mechanical deformation induced by thermal gradients. The twofold soliton structures represent coherent nonlinear wave propagation where the balance between dispersion and nonlinearity maintains the wave shape over time. Periodic wave patterns indicate oscillatory deformation that may arise in graphene layers subjected to sustained thermal or mechanical disturbances.

The analytical techniques used in this study, offer several advantages for analyzing NEEs. These methods provide systematic procedures for constructing exact analytical solutions and allow the derivation of diverse nonlinear wave structures such as bright solitons, periodic waves, and localized bell-type solutions. Such exact solutions are valuable because they help reveal the intrinsic dynamics of nonlinear systems and provide deeper insight into the physical mechanisms governing wave propagation in GS. However, these analytical techniques also have certain limitations. The applicability of these methods generally depends on the mathematical structure of the governing equation, and they may not be easily extended to highly complex nonlinear systems involving strong external forcing, stochastic perturbations, or multi-physical coupling effects. Conclusions

In this work, the nonlinear thermophoretic wrinkle motion equation describing GS dynamics has been investigated using two analytical techniques. These approaches enable the construction of several exact wave solutions representing different nonlinear wave patterns such as bright solitons, bell-shaped waves, periodic waves, and twofold soliton structures. The obtained solutions provide important insight into the nonlinear wave propagation mechanisms that may occur in GS under thermodynamic conditions. Graphical illustrations have been used to visualize the physical characteristics of the obtained solutions, demonstrating the formation of localized deformation waves and periodic oscillatory structures within graphene materials. The novelty of this study lies in applying MGEDFM and ICM to the governing model to obtain new analytical solutions describing thermally induced nonlinear wave patterns. These findings contribute to a deeper theoretical understanding of thermophoretic wrinkle propagation and nonlinear dynamics in graphene-based materials.

Although the present work focuses on idealized theoretical conditions, the obtained results provide a useful foundation for further studies on graphene dynamics. Future research may incorporate numerical simulations, experimental validation, and more complex physical models to better understand the behavior of graphene materials under realistic conditions.

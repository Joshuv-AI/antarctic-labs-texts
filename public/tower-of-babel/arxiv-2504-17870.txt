ℱ \mathcal{F} -harmonic 3-forms and the Type IIA flow on 6-dimensional symplectic Lie algebras
Teng Fei

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the problem of ℱ \mathcal{F} -harmonic forms and the long-time behavior of the Type IIA flow on certain nilpotent and solvable symplectic Lie algebra (and corresponding nilmanifolds and solvmanifolds). In particular, we demonstrate that the Type IIA flow in these cases are very useful to detect desired geometric structures including Lagrangian torus fibrations and harmonic almost complex structures.



1
Introduction

Given a 6-dimensional symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, from any 3-form
\varphi
on
M
, one can canonically construct an endomorphism
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)
of
TM
, another 3-form
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
, and a scalar function
\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)

[
Fei25
]
, where
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
are homogeneous polynomials in
\varphi
of degree 2, 3, and 4 respectively. Many classical work in complex geometry and mathematical physics can be incorporated into this framework, such as
[
Hit00
,
Tom08
,
TY14
,
DL24b
,
DL24c
,
DL24a
]
.

In this paper, we would like to address two analytic problems related to the aforementioned construction, namely the problem of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic forms and the problem of solving the Type IIA flow.

As a nonlinear analogue of the standard Hodge theory, we say a 3-form
\varphi
on
(M,\omega)
is
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic
if both
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0
and
\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=0
. A basic question is that how do we characterize the set of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic 3-forms. In particular, we would like to know if an
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic representative always exist if its cohomology is fixed. Since we may work with a symplectic but non-Kähler manifold, it is necessary to consider both the de Rham cohomology and the symplectic cohomology
[
TY12a
,
TY12b
,
TTY16
]
.

In a series of papers
[
FPPZ21a
,
FPPZ23
,
FPPZ21b
]
, we introduce and investigate the Type IIA flow as an intrinsically defined geometric flow on symplectic 6-manifolds. In the language of
[
Fei25
]
, the Type IIA flow on a fixed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is the following evolution equation of 3-forms

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}(\varphi),

(1)

where
\Lambda_{\omega}
is the Lefschetz operator of contraction with respect to the symplectic form
\omega
. Conventionally we require the initial data
\varphi\big|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}
to be a
\mathrm{d}
-closed 3-form that belongs to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{-}^{+}(\mu)
pointwise so we have the desired geometric interpretation as well as the short-time existence and uniqueness. However in principle, Equation (
1
) makes sense for any initial data though additional effort is in need for its short-time existence. In this paper, we would like to explore the relationship between the long time behavior of (
1
) and the underlying structure of
(M,\omega)
, in the context of arbitrary initial data. In particular, it encompasses the characterization of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic forms, as they constitute a special kind of stationary points of (
1
).

In general, understanding the set of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic forms and the behavior of the Type IIA flow (
1
) requires development of new powerful techniques in nonlinear analysis. As a preliminary attempt on these two problems, in this paper we would like to avoid such analytical difficulties by imposing a large symmetry on
(M,\omega)
. To be more specific, we would like to assume that
(M,\omega)
is a 6-dimensional symplectic Lie group (or its quotient by discrete subgroups) with everything being left-invariant. To this effect, we can replace the standard de Rham and symplectic cohomology theories by the corresponding Lie algebra cohomology and its symplectic analogue. As a consequence, we can reduce the problem of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic forms to a collection of algebraic equations and the equation of Type IIA flow to an ODE system.

For simplicity, we shall consider only those 6-dimensional symplectic Lie groups with cocompact discrete subgroups. It is well-known (see for example
[
Mil76
]
) that any Lie group admitting a cocompact discrete subgroup must be unimodular. By a theorem of Chu
[
Chu74
]
, any unimodular symplectic Lie group must be solvable. Though the classification is not fully complete, the structure of 6-dimensional symplectic solvable Lie groups is well-understood from the works
[
GB87
,
GK96
,
Sal01
,
KGM04
,
CS09
,
BC16
]
. In particular, it contains many more examples compared to 6-dimensional Lie groups with left-invariant symplectic half-flat structures
[
CT07
,
FMOU13
]
. In this paper, we do not attempt to exhaust all the possible cases. Instead, we present a more refined analysis of the two examples given in
[
FPPZ21a
, Section 9.3.2]
. Further cases will be left for future studies.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review necessary backgrounds about 3-forms over a 6-dimensional space and various cohomology theories on a symplectic Lie algebra. In Section 3, we establish a number of formulae for calculation purpose. Section 4 is devoted to address the problem of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic forms and the long-time behavior of the Type IIA flow. We present one nilpotent example and one solvable example. From these explicit calculations we show that the case of nilpotent Lie algebra is drastically different from the case of solvable Lie algebra. Moreover we also learn that in these examples the Type IIA flow can be very useful in detecting desired geometric structures such as Lagrangian fibrations and harmonic almost complex structures.

2
Backgrounds

Let
G
be a Lie group of real dimension
n
. All the left-invariant data on
G
are determined by their value at the identity, hence they can be viewed as an element of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
or other algebraic constructions built from
\mathfrak{g}
. After fixing a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, the isomorphism class of
\mathfrak{g}
is determined by the structure constants
\{c^{k}_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j,k\leq n}
, which are defined by
[e_{i},e_{j}]=c^{k}_{ij}e_{k}
. In many cases, it is more convenient to work with the dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and its exterior product

\bigwedge\nolimits^{*}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\bigwedge\nolimits^{i}\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

The structure of
\mathfrak{g}
can be recovered from the exterior derivative
\mathrm{d}
defined on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, given by

\mathrm{d}e^{k}=-\frac{1}{2}c^{k}_{ij}e^{i}\wedge e^{j},

where
\{e^{1},\dots,e^{n}\}
and
c^{k}_{ij}
are the dual basis and the structure constants associated to the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
. Extending the above defined
\mathrm{d}
by linearity and the graded Leibniz rule, one gets a differential
\mathrm{d}:\bigwedge^{i}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\bigwedge^{i+1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfying
\mathrm{d}^{2}=0
. The cohomology associated to the cochain complex
(\bigwedge^{*}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathrm{d})
is known as the
Lie algebra cohomology
of
\mathfrak{g}

[
CE48
]
. In many cases, the Lie algebra cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
coincides with the de Rham cohomology of quotients of
G
by its cocompact discrete subgroups, including the case when
G
is compact or nilpotent
[
Nom54
]
.

For the convenience of notation, we shall use a symbol like
(0,0,0,e^{15},0,e^{13})
to denote the 6-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
defined by a basis
\{e^{1},\dots,e^{6}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfying

\mathrm{d}e^{1}=\mathrm{d}e^{2}=\mathrm{d}e^{3}=\mathrm{d}e^{5}=0,\quad\mathrm{d}e^{4}=e^{1}\wedge e^{5},\quad\mathrm{d}e^{6}=e^{1}\wedge e^{3}.

We say
(G,\omega)
is a
symplectic Lie group
or
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is a
symplectic Lie algebra
if
\omega\in\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a
\mathrm{d}
-closed and non-degenerate 2-form on
\mathfrak{g}
. For a symplectic Lie group or a symplectic Lie algebra, its dimension must an even number
2m
. For any
0\leq k\leq m
, we can define the space of primitive
k
-forms as

\mathcal{P}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\left\{\alpha\in\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}:\omega^{m-k+1}\wedge\alpha=0\right\}.

We have the well-known Lefschetz decomposition

\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\mathcal{P}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\left(\omega\wedge\mathcal{P}^{k-2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)\oplus\left(\omega^{2}\wedge\mathcal{P}^{k-4}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)\oplus\dots

and the Lefschetz isomorphism

\omega^{m-k}:\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\bigwedge\nolimits^{2m-k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Following Tseng-Yau
[
TY12a
,
TY12b
]
, one can easily show that

\mathrm{d}:\mathcal{P}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathcal{P}^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\left(\omega\wedge\mathcal{P}^{k-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right),

therefore one decompose
\mathrm{d}=\partial_{+}+\omega\wedge\partial_{-}
, where
\partial_{\pm}:\mathcal{P}^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathcal{P}^{k\pm 1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are first order differential operators mapping primitive forms to primitive forms. The
symplectic cohomology

\textrm{SH}^{*}
of
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is defined to be the cohomology of the cochain complex

0\to\mathcal{P}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{+}}\mathcal{P}^{1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{+}}\dots\xrightarrow{\partial_{+}}\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{+}\partial_{-}}\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{-}}\mathcal{P}^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{\partial_{-}}\dots\xrightarrow{\partial_{-}}\mathcal{P}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to 0.

In this paper, we are concerned with three kinds of cohomologies on
\mathfrak{g}
. They are the de Rham/Lie algebra cohomology group

\textrm{H}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R}):=\frac{\ker\left(\mathrm{d}:\bigwedge^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\bigwedge^{m+1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)}{\textrm{im}\left(\mathrm{d}:\bigwedge^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\bigwedge^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)},

the primitive part of the de Rham/Lie algebra cohomology

\textrm{PH}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R}):=\frac{\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cap\ker\left(\mathrm{d}:\bigwedge^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\bigwedge^{m+1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)}{\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cap\textrm{im}\left(\mathrm{d}:\bigwedge^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\bigwedge^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)},

and the symplectic cohomologies

\textrm{SH}_{+}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R}):=\frac{\ker\left(\partial_{+}\partial_{-}:\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)}{\textrm{im}\left(\partial_{+}:\mathcal{P}^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)}

and

\textrm{SH}_{-}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R}):=\frac{\ker\left(\partial_{-}:\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathcal{P}^{m-1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)}{\textrm{im}\left(\partial_{+}\partial_{-}:\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)}.

The Poincaré duality says the natural pairing
\wedge:\textrm{SH}_{+}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})\otimes\textrm{SH}_{-}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})\to\bigwedge^{2m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is non-degenerate if
\mathfrak{g}
is unimodular.

Since
\partial_{+}\partial_{-}=\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}
and
\ker\partial_{-}=\ker\mathrm{d}\cap\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we naturally have the surjective map

\textrm{SH}_{-}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})\twoheadrightarrow\textrm{PH}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})

and the injective map

\textrm{PH}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow\textrm{H}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R}).

On the other hand, as
\textrm{im}~\mathrm{d}\cap\mathcal{P}^{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\subset\textrm{im}~\partial_{+}
, we also have a natural map

\textrm{SH}_{+}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})\to\textrm{PH}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R}).

Computational Formulae

In this section, we establish some basic computational formulae for 3-forms in real dimension 6. We first review the basic constructions of the equivariant homogeneous polynomials
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
following
[
Fei25
]
. Some other references include
[
Hit00
]
and
[
Fei15
]
.

Let
V
be a 6-dimensional real vector space equipped with a symplectic form
\omega
. For any 3-form
\varphi\in\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
, we can define
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)\in\mathrm{End}~V\otimes\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
by

\mathcal{K}(\varphi)(v)=-\iota_{v}\varphi\wedge\varphi\in\bigwedge\nolimits^{5}V^{*}\cong V\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{6}V^{*}

for any
v\in V
. The isomorphism
\bigwedge^{5}V^{*}\cong V\otimes\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
is canonical. If we choose a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{6}\}
of
V
with dual basis
\{e^{1},\dots,e^{6}\}
of
V^{*}
, we have

\mathcal{K}(\varphi)(v)=-\sum_{i=1}^{6}e_{i}\otimes e^{i}\wedge\iota_{v}\varphi\wedge\varphi\in V\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{6}V^{*}.

In addition, we can define
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)\in\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}\otimes\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
by

\mathcal{F}(\varphi)(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=-2\varphi(\mathcal{K}(\varphi)(v_{1}),v_{2},v_{3})

and
\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)\in(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 2}
by

\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)=-\varphi\wedge\mathcal{F}(\varphi).

It is clear from their definitions that
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
are quadratic, cubic, and quartic in
\varphi
respectively. Moreover,
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
satisfies the following identities:

Proposition 3.1
.

[
Fei25
, Proposition 2.5]

(a).

\mathcal{K}(\varphi)\circ\mathcal{K}(\varphi)=\dfrac{\mathrm{id}_{V}}{4}\cdot\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)\in\mathrm{End}~V\otimes(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 2}
,

(b).

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{F}(\varphi))=-\mathcal{K}(\varphi)\cdot\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)\in\mathrm{End}~V\otimes(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 3}
,

(c).

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{F}(\varphi))=-\varphi\cdot\mathcal{Q}^{2}(\varphi)\in\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}\otimes(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 4}
.

What we presented so far is independent of the symplectic structure. The symplectic form
\omega
determines a canonical volume form
\omega^{3}/3!\in\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
, with which we can view
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)
,
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)
as elements in
\mathrm{End}~V
,
\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
and
\mathbb{R}
respectively.

A notable example is that for
\varphi
such that
\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)<0
, we can define a complex structure
\mathcal{J}(\varphi)
on
V
by

\mathcal{J}(\varphi)=\frac{2\mathcal{K}(\varphi)}{\sqrt{-\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)}}.

The 3-form
\varphi
is the real part of a complex
(3,0)
-form
\varphi+i\hat{\varphi}
with respect to
\mathcal{J}(\varphi)
. The imaginary part
\hat{\varphi}
is purely determined from
\varphi
by

\hat{\varphi}=\mathcal{J}(\varphi)^{*}\varphi=\frac{\mathcal{F}(\varphi)}{\sqrt{-\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)}}.

If we define
|\varphi|^{2}
by

\varphi\wedge\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=|\varphi|^{4}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!},

then we have
\mathcal{K}(\varphi)=\dfrac{|\varphi|^{2}}{2}\mathcal{J}(\varphi)
,
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=|\varphi|^{2}\hat{\varphi}
, and
\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)=-|\varphi|^{4}
.

Under the presence of the symplectic form
\omega
, we usually only consider
\varphi
that is primitive with respect to
\omega
. In this case,
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
is also primitive. If in addition that
\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)<0
, we know that
\omega
is a
(1,1)
-form with respect to the complex structure
\mathcal{J}(\varphi)
and that
|\varphi|^{2}
is indeed the norm square of
\varphi
with respect to the metric (not necessarily positive definite)
g(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\mathcal{J}(\varphi)\cdot)
.

4
Examples

In this section, we study the problem of existence and uniqueness of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic forms, and the long-time behavior of the Type IIA flow on symplectic Lie groups with left invariant data. To be more specific, we would like to address the following two questions.

Question 1
: The existence and uniqueness of
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic 3-forms in a fixed cohomology class.

By definition, a 3-form
\varphi
is
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic if
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0
and
\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=0
. As an analogue of the standard Hodge theory, we would like to address Question 1 in the set-up of a symplectic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
with
\varphi\in\bigwedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Since
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0
, we know that
\varphi
defines a de Rham cohomology class in
\textrm{H}^{3}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
for a general 3-form, and that
\varphi
defines a cohomology class in
\textrm{PH}^{3}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
and
\textrm{SH}^{3}_{\pm}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
if
\varphi
is primitive. We would like to know that, if the cohomology class of
\varphi
is fixed in the above sense (de Rham for general
\varphi
, and one of the three kinds of cohomologies for primitive
\varphi
), does there exist a unique
\mathcal{F}
-harmonic representative.

For a given symplectic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
, to answer Question 1, we proceed along the following steps.

Step 1
: Compute
\textrm{H}^{3}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
,
\textrm{PH}^{3}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
, and
\textrm{SH}^{3}_{\pm}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
explicitly.

Step 2
: By assuming
\varphi
taking the form of (
2
) or (
3
), we reduce the
F
-harmonic equation to a system of polynomial equations.

Step 3
: Determine the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the system of algebraic equations in Step 2 within each cohomology class.

Question 2
: The long-time behavior of the Type IIA flow.

We would like to investigate the long-time behavior of the Type IIA flow (
1
) on symplectic Lie groups and their quotients, including whether the flow has long-time existence of finite-time singularities. In its original setup
[
FPPZ21a
]
, the initial data is both closed, primitive, and positive. However, our formulation in (
1
) allows us to extend the initial data to be a general 3-form, not necessarily closed or primitive. In this paper, we will consider such a flow with initial data under three kinds of generalities: (1) a general 3-form; (2) a primitive 3-form; (3) a closed primitive 3-form.

It is worth mentioning that there is little difference between the case of general initial data and that of primitive initial data. The Lefschetz decomposition for 3-form reads

\bigwedge\nolimits^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\mathcal{P}^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\omega\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

For any 1-form
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we have that

\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}(\omega\wedge\alpha)=\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}(\omega\wedge\mathrm{d}\alpha)=\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}\alpha+\omega\wedge\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}\alpha)=0

since
\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}\alpha
is a constant. This computation implies that
\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
has only primitive components, hence the non-primitive components of
\varphi
are stationary under the Type IIA flow (
1
) with invariant data.

To address Question 2, we first reduce the Type IIA flow to an ODE system. Then, we find its stationary points. Finally, we analyze the long-time behavior of this ODE system.

\begin{bmatrix}2\alpha\beta&&\alpha(N-M)&\beta(M+N)&&\\
&2\gamma\delta&\gamma(M+N)&\delta(M-N)&&\\

[
FPPZ21a

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\textrm{ are all positive or all negative},\\
&\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma>M^{2},\quad\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma>N^{2},\\
&\dfrac{\mathcal{Q}(\varphi)}{16}=-4\alpha\beta\gamma\delta+\alpha\delta(M-N)^{2}+\beta\gamma(M+N)^{2}<0,\end{cases}

which are all preserved under the Type IIA flow.

[
FPPZ21a
]
. For the general case, we have:

Proposition 4.9
.

For any value of
M
and
N
, the ODE system (
13
) has a finite-time singularity. Let
T<\infty
be its maximal existence time, then the limit

\lim_{t\to T}\alpha(t)^{-1}\varphi(t)=\varphi_{\infty}

exists smoothly. Moreover,
\varphi_{\infty}
defines a harmonic almost complex structure, as detailed in
[
FPPZ21a
]
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\alpha/\delta
and
\beta/\gamma
are positive constants along the flow.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
, and
\delta
are all positive, otherwise we may consider the evolution equation for
-\alpha
,
-\beta
,
-\gamma
, and
-\delta
instead. Since (
14
) holds along the flow, we know that

4\alpha\beta\gamma\delta>\alpha\delta(M-N)^{2},\quad 4\alpha\beta\gamma\delta>\beta\gamma(M+N)^{2},

so the right hand side of (
13
) are all positive. It follows that there exists a small positive number
c
depending on the initial data such that
\partial_{t}f\geq cf
holds when
f=\alpha,\beta,\gamma
or
\delta
, therefore all of
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
, and
\delta
have at least exponential growth.

Now let
u=4\alpha\delta
and
v=4\beta\gamma
. The pair
(u,v)
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}u=2\lambda^{2}u(v-(M-N)^{2}),\\
&\partial_{t}v=2\lambda^{2}v(u-(M+N)^{2}),\end{cases}

(15)

together with

\displaystyle u,v>0\textrm{ and }uv>u(M-N)^{2}+v(M+N)^{2}.

(16)

In fact, the last two lines in the system of inequalities (
14
) can be derived from (
16
) as follows. First we deduce that
u>(M+N)^{2}
and
v>(M-N)^{2}
. So we get

\left(\frac{u+v-2M^{2}-2N^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\geq(u-(M+N)^{2})(v-(M-N)^{2})>(M^{2}-N^{2})^{2}.

It follows that

u+v>2M^{2}+2N^{2}+2|M^{2}-N^{2}|=4\max\{M^{2},N^{2}\}.

Let
S=\max\{(M+N)^{2},(M-N)^{2}\}
and we shall compare the ODE system (
15
) with the following ODE system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}u=2\lambda^{2}u(v-S),\\
&\partial_{t}v=2\lambda^{2}v(u-S).\end{cases}

(17)

By the ODE comparison theorem, if we can show that (
17
) blows up in finite time, then (
15
) must blow up in even shorter time. It turns out that (
17
) can be solved explicitly as follows. By taking the difference of the two equations in (
17
), we get

\partial_{t}(u-v)+2\lambda^{2}S(u-v)=0.

Therefore there exists a constant
C_{0}=u_{0}-v_{0}
such that

u-v=C_{0}e^{-2\lambda^{2}St}.

Plug it back in (
17
), we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=2\lambda^{2}u(u-C_{0}e^{-2\lambda^{2}St}-S).

(18)

Let
w=e^{2\lambda^{2}St}u
, then from (
18
) we get

\frac{\partial_{t}w}{w(w-C_{0})}=2\lambda^{2}e^{-2\lambda^{2}St}.

It follows that

w=\frac{C_{0}}{1-\frac{w_{0}-C_{0}}{w_{0}}e^{-\frac{C_{0}}{S}(e^{-2\lambda^{2}St}-1)}}.

As a consequence, we see that
w
blows up at finite time

, where

\begin{split}T^{\prime}&=-\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}S}\log\left[1+\frac{S}{C_{0}}\log\frac{w_{0}-C_{0}}{w_{0}}\right]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}S}\log\left[1+\frac{S}{\alpha_{0}\delta_{0}-\beta_{0}\gamma_{0}}\log\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{0}\delta_{0}-\beta_{0}\gamma_{0}}{\alpha_{0}\delta_{0}}\right)\right].\end{split}

Now we can turn back to the ODE system (
15
), for which we now know that it blows up at a finite time
T
and we have an estimate
T<T^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
v(t)
blows up at time
T
, i.e.
\lim_{t\to T}v(t)=+\infty

u(t)
also blows up at timer
T
.

By direct computation, we have

\partial_{t}\left(\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{u^{2}}\right)=\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{u^{2}}\left[(u-v)((M+N)^{2}v-(M-N)^{2}u)-(u-v)^{2}(v-(M-N)^{2})\right].

Firstly, it is easy to show that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant, whose optimal value is

, such that

(u-v)((M+N)^{2}v-(M-N)^{2}u)\leq C_{\epsilon}(u-v)^{2}+\epsilon u^{2}.

As

is increasing and
\lim_{t\to T}v(t)=+\infty
, there exists a time
T_{1}<T
such that for any
T_{1}\leq t<T
, we have

v(t)-(M-N)^{2}\geq\frac{v(t)}{2}+C_{\epsilon}.

Therefore on the time interval

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{u^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{u^{2}}\left[\epsilon u^{2}-\frac{v}{2}(u-v)^{2}\right]\leq 2\lambda^{2}\epsilon.

(19)

Integrating it from
T_{1}
to
t

\left(\frac{u(t)-v(t)}{u(t)}\right)^{2}\leq C^{2}

on
[T_{1},T)
, where the constant
C
depends on
u(T_{1})
,
v(T_{1})
and
\epsilon
. Therefore we know that on
[T_{1},T)

hence
\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t)=+\infty

Notice that the evolution equation of

\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}=v-(M-N)^{2}.

Integrating it, we get

\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s=t(M-N)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\log\frac{u(t)}{u(0)}

. As we have proved that
\lim_{t\to T}u(t)=+\infty
, we can conclude

\lim_{t\to T}\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s=+\infty.

Write
r=\dfrac{(u-v)^{2}}{u^{2}}

19

\partial_{t}r+\lambda^{2}vr\leq 2\lambda^{2}\epsilon.

It follows that on
[T_{1},T)

\partial_{t}\left(e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}r(t)\right)\leq 2\lambda^{2}\epsilon e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{v(T_{1})}\lambda^{2}v(t)e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}.

Integrating both sides, we get

e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}r(t)\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{v(T_{1})}\left(e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}-e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}\right)+e^{\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}v(s)\mathrm{d}s}r(T_{1}).

Divide both sides by

and let

\lim_{t\to T}r(t)\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{v(T_{1})}

for any

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}\frac{(u(t)-v(t))^{2}}{u^{2}(t)}=0,

(20)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}\frac{u(t)}{v(t)}=1.

&\lim_{t\to T}\frac{\delta(t)}{\alpha(t)}=\delta_{\infty}>0.\end{split}

Let
\alpha_{\infty}=1

and

\lim_{t\to T}\frac{\varphi(t)}{\alpha(t)}=\alpha_{\infty}(e^{135}+e^{136})+\beta_{\infty}(e^{145}-e^{146})+\gamma_{\infty}(e^{235}-e^{236})-\delta_{\infty}(e^{245}+e^{246})

so from

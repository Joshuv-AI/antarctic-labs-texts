Quantitative strong approximation for quaternary quadratic forms
Zhizhong Huang and Damaris Schindler and Alec Shute

Abstract
. The purpose of this article is twofold. On the one hand, we prove asymptotic formulas for the quantitative distribution of rational points on any smooth non-split projective quadratic surface. We obtain the optimal error term for the real place. On the other hand, we also study the growth of integral points on the three-dimensional punctured affine cone, as a quantitative version of strong approximation with Brauer–Manin obstruction for this quasi-affine variety.



1.
Introduction

Let
V\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
be a smooth projective quadric defined by a non-degenerate integral quadratic form
F
in
(n+1)
-variables. We always assume that
V
has local points at all places of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
W\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}
be its corresponding affine cone. Let
{\mathscr{V}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n},{\mathscr{W}}\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n+1}
be respectively their integral models defined by
F
. In
[
10
]
, assuming
n\geqslant 4
, on employing the delta method in a form developed by Heath-Brown
[
9
]
, we prove asymptotic formulas for the distribution of rational points on
V
, with optimal error terms. This is done via counting integral points of
{\mathscr{W}}
inside of an arbitrary adelic neighbourhood, with additional assumptions regarding the real condition. From this we deduce the best growth rate of the “size” of local conditions for which equidistribution is preserved.

Adopting the approach from
[
10
]
, the goal of the present concurrent article is to focus on the non-split quaternary case, i.e. we assume that
n=3
and
\Delta_{F}
, the discriminant of
F
, is not a square.
In this case the delta method has already been applied by Lindqvist
[
11
]
in the context of strong approximation for the affine cone and she found additional main terms which did not have any clear interpretation yet. We provide a generalisation of her work to quantitative strong approximation, we compute the additional appearing terms explicitly and we show how they are related to the Brauer-Manin obstruction.
This is the first instance that we are aware of in which the circle method detects the Brauer-Manin obstruction in a quantitative way with an explicit leading constant.

1.1.
Effective equidistribution on the non-split projective quadratic surface

Let us fix a parameter space
S
and a family of smooth weight functions
\mathscr{C}(S)
as in
[
9
]
. As in
[
10
, (1.1)]
, we define a
real zoom condition
in terms of a fixed real point
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in V({\mathbb{R}})
, a neighborhood
U_{0}
of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, and a scaling parameter
R\geqslant 1
. For
B\gg 1
, we choose
w_{B,R}:{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{R}}
(see (
2.7
)), constructed from a certain fixed
w\in\mathscr{C}(S)
as in
[
10
]
, which describes the bounded height condition depending on
B
and the real zoom condition. It satisfies

(1.1)

w_{B,R}(-\boldsymbol{x})=w_{B,R}(\boldsymbol{x}).

See §
2.1
for a sketch and
[
10
, §2]
for more details.
By abuse of notation, for
P\in{\mathbb{P}}^{3}({\mathbb{Q}})
,
w_{B,R}(P)
means that we choose a lift of
P
into a primitive integer vector of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
and apply
w_{B,R}
.

Our first result is on equidistribution with only a real zoom condition, and provides an
optimal
error term.
We consider the counting function

(1.2)

{\mathscr{N}}_{V}(w_{B,R}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in V({\mathbb{Q}})\end{subarray}}w_{B,R}(P).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1.1
),
\Delta_{F}\neq\square
, and

(1.3)

R^{2}\asymp B^{1-\tau}\quad\text{for some }0<\tau<1.

Assume that
V
has local points at all places of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

{\mathscr{N}}_{V}(w_{B,R})=B^{2}\frac{{\mathscr{I}}(w_{R})}{2}\mathfrak{S}(V)\left(1+O_{\varepsilon}\left(B^{-\tau+\varepsilon}\right)\right),

where
{\mathscr{I}}(w_{R})
is the “weighted singular integral” (see (
2.24
) for its definition) and
\mathfrak{S}(V)
is the finite Tamagawa measure of
{\mathscr{V}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
(à la Peyre
[
12
]
, with respect to the naive metric).

For
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we let
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{p})_{p\mid L}
, where
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{p}\in{\mathscr{V}}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
for every
p\mid L
. The pair
(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda})
defines a finite adelic open subset
{\mathscr{D}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\subset{\mathscr{V}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}):=\prod_{p}{\mathscr{V}}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
(see
[
10
]
), and gives rise to a
p
-adic zoom condition
for each
p\mid L
. We now describe our second result of counting with only
p
-adic zoom conditions (i.e. we take
R=1
).
We shall write

w_{B}=w_{B,1}

and consider

(1.4)

{\mathscr{N}}_{V}(w_{B};(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda})):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in V({\mathbb{Q}})\\
P\in{\mathscr{D}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda})\end{subarray}}w_{B}(P).

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume (
1.1
),
\Delta_{F}\neq\square
, and

(1.5)

L^{2}\asymp B^{1-\tau}\text{ for some }\frac{1}{2}<\tau<1.

Assume that
V
has local points at all places of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then we have

{\mathscr{N}}_{V}(w_{B};(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}))=B^{2}\frac{{\mathscr{I}}(w)}{2}\mathfrak{S}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}({\mathscr{V}})\left(1+O_{\varepsilon}\left(B^{-2\tau+1+\varepsilon}\right)\right),

where
\mathfrak{S}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}({\mathscr{V}})
is the finite Tamagawa measure of
{\mathscr{V}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
regarding the local condition
(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda})
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
have the following consequence on
the “
essential approximation constant
”
\alpha_{\operatorname{ess}}^{\nu}
(see
[
10
, Appendix A]
for more details): For any real point
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in V({\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\alpha_{\operatorname{ess}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\leqslant 2;

For any non-archimedean place
\nu
and any
\nu
-adic point
\xi_{\nu}\in V({\mathbb{Q}}_{\nu})
, we have

\alpha_{\operatorname{ess}}^{\nu}(\xi_{\nu})\leqslant 4.

Note that in
[
10
, Appendix A]
we show that
\alpha_{\operatorname{ess}}^{\nu}(\xi)=2
when
\xi\in V(\mathbb{Q})
. So Theorem
1.1
is optimal if
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is a rational point. It would be very interesting to determine the exact value of such an essential constant for non-archimedean places. This is related to the best error term one can obtain in Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Counting integral points on the punctured affine cone

The second goal of this article is to study the asymptotic growth of integral points on the punctured affine cone
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}:={\mathscr{W}}\setminus\overline{\boldsymbol{0}}\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{4}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
. In stark contrast to the higher dimensional cases, under the assumption that
\Delta_{F}\neq\square
, there can be a Brauer–Manin obstruction to strong approximation on
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}
.

On
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}):=\prod_{p}{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, we can associate a (normalised) Tamagawa measure, denoted by
\omega_{f}
locally given by the “modified singular series” (see (
5.3
) and see also Appendix A). Let
W_{i},i\in I
be the connected components of the real locus
W^{o}({\mathbb{R}})
. If
\Delta<0
(resp.
\Delta>0
) we then have
\#I=2
(resp.
\#I=1
). (See Lemma
5.4
.)

We shall establish in §
5
the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
\Delta_{F}\neq\square
. Then for every
i\in I
, there exists a locally constant function
\Xi_{W_{i}}:{\mathscr{W}}^{o}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})\to\{0,2\}
such that for any non-empty open compact
{\mathscr{E}}\subseteq{\mathscr{W}}^{o}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
, if the support of
w_{B}
is contained in
W_{i}
, we have as
B\to\infty
,

\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}})\cap(W_{i}\times{\mathscr{E}})}w_{B}(\boldsymbol{x})\sim{\mathscr{I}}(w)\left(\int_{{\mathscr{E}}}\Xi_{W_{i}}\operatorname{d}\omega_{f}\right)B^{2}.

Theorem
1.4
implies a quantitative version of the Brauer–Manin obstruction to strong approximation of integral points on
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}
.
According to the authors’ knowledge, this is the first instance where the circle method furnishes asymptotic formulas that encode information from the Brauer–Manin obstruction.

Note that Theorem
1.4
also encodes a failure of strong approximation due to a Brauer-Manin obstruction in cases where the locally constant function
\Xi_{W_{i}}
is equal to zero. This shows that an analogue of Corollary 3 in
[
9
]
for primitive integer vectors in a strict sense is not true due to the possible existence of a Brauer-Manin obstruction (compare also with the paragraph after Corollary 3 in
[
9
]
where it is claimed that a generalisation would be possible.)

Remarks 1.5
.

(1)

In the terminology of Borovoi–Rudnick
[
5
]
, up to the weighted singular integral, (an extension of) Theorem
1.4
shows that (see also
[
6
, Proposition 2.4]
):

The quasi-affine variety
W^{o}
is relatively Hardy–Littlewood (
[
5
, p. 39]
) with density function
\Xi_{W_{i}}
for each connected component
W_{i}
.

(2)

Applying results on the geometric sieve for quadrics (
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
or a variant of
[
6
, Theorem 1.6]
), the following stronger equidistribution result holds (see
[
6
, Definition 1.2]
):

The quasi-affine variety
W^{o}
satisfies arithmetic purity of the Hardy–Littlewood property.

Equivalently, every open subset of
W^{o}
whose complement has codimension at least two is also relatively Hardy–Littlewood.

1.3.
Methods and further comments

Heath-Brown
[
9
]
pioneers the counting for quaternary quadratic forms, applying his
\delta
-method. Our investigation also makes use of the
\delta
-method, however, to minimize the error terms in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we need to refine a number of arguments in
[
9
,
10
]
, and in particular, a “double Kloosterman refinement” is brought into play. The finite Tamagawa measure on
{\mathscr{V}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
features a different form of Eulerian product, due to the necessity of introducing convergence factors.
We also prove the accordance of the leading constant with Peyre’s prediction (see Proposition
2.8
), which does not seem to have been addressed before in the literature.

Work of Lindqvist
[
11
]
first addresses counting with extra congruence conditions. One has to be more careful when handling quadratic exponential sums as the multiplicativity is lost. In
[
11
]
it is observed that, after executing the Poisson summation and introducing the Poisson variable
\boldsymbol{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
, the leading term does not always agree with the contribution from
\boldsymbol{c}=\boldsymbol{0}
. A mysterious main term
{\mathscr{K}}
(see (
4.10
)) appears which is expressed as an infinite sum over certain
\boldsymbol{c}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
with
F^{*}(\boldsymbol{c})=0
. We are able to completely determine the value of
{\mathscr{K}}
, and show that its appearance should be thought of as the “defect” of strong approximation (see Remark
5.7
), thereby completing the work
[
11
]
.

We have chosen to establish the main theorems only with respect to a specific choice of smooth weights. This is solely for technical simplicity. The same procedure of “removing smooth weights” can be applied as in
[
10
, §7.2]
, which would only affect the size of the power savings in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
and not the allowable growth rate of
R
and
L
for which the asymptotic formulas are obtained. Obtaining a joint version with both real and finite local conditions as in
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
is also possible.

1.4.
Notation and conventions

Throughout this article we assume
n=3
and
\Delta_{F}\neq\square
. We assume that the quadratic form
F
has even non-diagonal terms (which we can do after scaling), in particular we then have
\Delta_{F}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Delta_{F}
is the determinant of the corresponding matrix defining
F
.

The symbol
\chi
denotes a Dirichlet character, the modulus of which is denoted by
|\chi|
. We write
\chi^{c}
for its conjugate, so as not to conflict with the notation of multiplicative inverse. We write

{\mathbb{L}}(s,\chi):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}

for the associated Dirichlet series.
For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\chi_{0}[n]
denotes the principal Dirichlet character modulo
n
.
We define the (non-principal) Dirichlet character
\psi_{F}(\cdot):=\left(\frac{4\Delta_{F}}{\cdot}\right)
and write
\widetilde{\psi_{F}}
for the primitive character which induces
\psi_{F}
.
As usual we write
\textup{e}(x):=\exp\left(2\pi ix\right)
and
\textup{e}_{q}(x):=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ix}{q}\right)
. Let
\varphi
be the Euler totient function.
We define the arithmetic functions

(1.6)

\theta_{1}(n):=\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right);\quad\theta_{2}(n):=\theta_{1}(n)\prod_{p\nmid n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right).

We have
\varphi(n)=n\theta_{1}(n)
.

2.
Set-up

Recall the following identity about the
\delta
-symbol in Heath-Brown’s work
[
9
, Theorem 1]
: Let
n
be an integer, and define
\delta(n)
to be
1
if
n=0
and zero otherwise. Then there exists a “nice” function
h:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that for every
Q>1
, we have

\delta(n)=\frac{C_{Q}}{Q^{2}}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\textup{e}_{q}\left(an\right)h\left(\frac{q}{Q},\frac{n}{Q^{2}}\right)

for a certain
C_{Q}=1+O_{N}(Q^{-N})
.

2.1.
The congruence condition and Poisson summation

For
w:{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geqslant 0}
a smooth weight function of class
{\mathscr{C}}(S)
, and for
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\in{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})
,
we consider the counting function (with respect to
{\mathscr{W}}
)

(2.1)

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(w;(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma})):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}:F(\boldsymbol{x})=0\\
\boldsymbol{x}\equiv\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\bmod L\end{subarray}}w(\boldsymbol{x}).

We choose
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\Gamma})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
a lift of
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
such that
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|\leqslant L
and
\gcd(L,\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})=1
. Define the affine linear polynomial

H_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},L}(\boldsymbol{y}):=\frac{F(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{L}+\nabla F(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}.

We observe that for
\boldsymbol{y}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
,

(2.2)

H_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},L}(\boldsymbol{y})\equiv 0\bmod L\Leftrightarrow F(L\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\lambda})\equiv 0\bmod L^{2}.

Then a standard manipulation via Poisson summation as in
[
10
, §2.4]
yields

(2.3)

\begin{split}{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(w;(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}))&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{y}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}\\
L\mid H_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},L}(\boldsymbol{y})\end{subarray}}w(L\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\lambda})\delta\left(\frac{F(L\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{L^{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac{C_{Q}}{Q^{2}}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\boldsymbol{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}}\frac{S_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{c})I_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(w;\boldsymbol{c})}{(qL)^{4}},\end{split}

where for every
q,\boldsymbol{c}
, we define the
weighted oscillatory integral

(2.4)

I_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(w;\boldsymbol{c}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}w(L\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\lambda})h\left(\frac{q}{Q},\frac{F(L\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{L^{2}Q^{2}}\right)\textup{e}_{qL}\left(-\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}\right)\operatorname{d}\boldsymbol{y},

and the
twisted quadratic exponential sums

(2.5)

S_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{c}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/qL{\mathbb{Z}})^{4}\\
H_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},L}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\equiv\boldsymbol{0}\bmod L\end{subarray}}\textup{e}_{qL}\left(a\left(H_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},L}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+LF(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\right)+\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right).

2.2.
The real condition and the oscillatory integrals

We pause to describe briefly the precise form of the weight function which we shall use.
Upon fixing a real point
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in V({\mathbb{R}})
, by means of Witt’s cancellation theorem, we do a linear change of variables
\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{t}
using a matrix
M\in\operatorname{GL}_{4}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\boldsymbol{\xi}\mapsto[1:0:0:0]
and

(2.6)

\widetilde{F}(\boldsymbol{t}):=F(M^{-1}\boldsymbol{t})=t_{0}t_{1}+F_{2}(t_{2},t_{3}),

where
F_{2}
is a certain non-degenerate binary quadratic form. Then
\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}),\frac{t_{3}}{t_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)
forms a local coordinate system of
V({\mathbb{R}})
around
\boldsymbol{\xi}
. Taking the real zoom condition above into account, we therefore let

(2.7)

w_{B,R}(\boldsymbol{x})=w_{1}\left(R\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}),\frac{t_{3}}{t_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)\right)w_{2}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{B^{2}}F(\boldsymbol{x})\right)w_{3}\left(\frac{t_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})}{B}\right),

for certain fixed
w_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}};w_{2},w_{3}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, all of which are of class
\mathscr{C}(S)
and
w_{3}
satisfying
w_{3}(-x)=w_{3}(x)
as well as
w_{3}(0)\neq 0
.
Therefore
w_{B,R}(\boldsymbol{x})=w_{B,R}(-\boldsymbol{x})
.

We then choose

(2.8)

Q:=\frac{B}{LR}

as in
[
10
, §2.5]
.

We next execute a preliminary manipulation for
I_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(w_{B,R};\boldsymbol{c})
, as is done in
[
10
, §3.1]
:

(2.9)

I_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(w_{B,R};\boldsymbol{c})=\frac{\textup{e}_{qL^{2}}(\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{|\det M|}\left(\frac{B}{LR}\right)^{4}\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{d}),

where

(2.10)

\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{d}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\widehat{w}(\boldsymbol{u})h\left(\frac{q}{Q},\widetilde{F}(\boldsymbol{u})\right)\textup{e}_{q}\left(-\boldsymbol{d}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}\right)\operatorname{d}\boldsymbol{u},

where

(2.11)

\widehat{w}(\boldsymbol{u}):=w_{1}\left(\frac{u_{2}}{u_{0}},\frac{u_{3}}{u_{0}}\right)w_{2}\left(\widetilde{F}(\boldsymbol{u})\right)w_{3}\left(u_{0}\right)

depending only on the “constant part”
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
of
w_{B,R}
, and we associate the vector
\boldsymbol{d}=\boldsymbol{d}(B,R,L;{\mathbf{c}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
to
\boldsymbol{c}
by

(2.12)

d_{0}=\frac{B}{L^{2}}\widetilde{c_{0}},\quad d_{1}=\frac{B}{L^{2}R^{2}}\widetilde{c_{1}},\quad d_{2}=\frac{B}{L^{2}R}\widetilde{c_{2}},\quad d_{3}=\frac{B}{L^{2}R}\widetilde{c_{3}},

with

(2.13)

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}}=(\widetilde{c_{0}},\cdots,\widetilde{c_{3}}):=(M^{-1})^{t}\boldsymbol{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}.

When the real condition is trivial (i.e.
R=1
), we just let
M
be the identity.

In order to see the effect of the Brauer–Manin obstruction at the real place, we shall work with a slightly different shape of weight functions. Let
w_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth compactly supported weight function such that the support of
w_{0}
is contained in a connected component of
W^{o}(\mathbb{R})
. We define
\widetilde{w}_{B}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(2.14)

\widetilde{w}_{B}(\boldsymbol{x}):=w_{0}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{B}\right).

The change of variables
\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{L\boldsymbol{y}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{B}
yields

I_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\widetilde{w}_{B};\boldsymbol{c})=\textup{e}_{qL^{2}}(\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda})\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(w_{0};\boldsymbol{d})\left(\frac{B}{L}\right)^{4},

where
R=1
,
M
is the identity matrix and
\boldsymbol{d}
is defined as before in (
2.12
).

We next record Heath-Brown’s “simple estimates” and “harder estimates”
[
9
, §7§8]
. We abbreviate in what follows all the functions (
2.11
) and
w_{0}
by
\widehat{w}
. For
\boldsymbol{c}=\boldsymbol{0}
we have

Lemma 2.1

(
[
9
]
Lemma 16)
.

We have

\frac{\partial\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{0})}{\partial q}\ll\frac{1}{q}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{0})\ll 1.

Concerning
\boldsymbol{c}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
(see also
[
10

Lemma 2.2

(
[

]
Lemma 18)
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{d}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
. Then for any
N>0
,

\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{d})\ll_{N}\frac{Q}{q}\left(\frac{|\boldsymbol{d}|}{Q}\right)^{-N}.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
9
]
Lemma 22)
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{d}\neq\boldsymbol{0}
. Then

\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{d})\ll_{\varepsilon}q\frac{Q^{\varepsilon}}{|\boldsymbol{d}|^{1-\varepsilon}},

and

\frac{\partial\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{d})}{\partial q}\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{Q^{\varepsilon}}{|\boldsymbol{d}|^{1-\varepsilon}}.

In the notation of
[

\widehat{{\mathscr{I}}}_{q}(\widehat{w};\boldsymbol{d})=I_{r}^{*}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{d}}{Q}\right)

for

. Then Lemma

. On the other hand,

also gives

where the function

is either
sh(s,y)
or

which all belong to the class
{\mathscr{H}}
of
[
9
, (7.1)]
, and
\widehat{w}_{1}\in{\mathscr{C}}_{0}(S)
is a certain weight function.
So Lemma
2.1
follows from
[
9
, Lemma 16]
and Lemma
2.3
follows from
[
9
, Lemma 22]
.

2.3.
The quadratic exponential sums

Following
[
10
, §4.1]
, let us decompose

(2.15)

q=q_{1}q_{2},\quad\frac{F(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{L}=k_{2}q_{1}+k_{1}q_{2}L

with
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2}L)=1
and
k_{1}\bmod q_{1},k_{2}\bmod q_{2}L
.
We use the Chinese remainder theorem to decompose

(2.16)

S_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{c})=S^{(1)}_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{c})S^{(2)}_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{c}),

where we define

(2.17)

\begin{split}S^{(1)}_{q,L,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{c})&:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{1}\in({\mathbb{Z}}/q_{1}{\mathbb{Z}})^{4}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\bmod q_{1}\\

. Arguing as in

10

, we may assume that
L_{0}\mid L
for a certain fixed

and

as in

, Proposition 6.5]
. So the above factor equals

are now clear (again comparig with
[
10
, Proposition 6.5]
).

Fix
S
a finite set of places containing
{\mathbb{R}}
and the primes dividing

.
For

, let

{\mathbb{L}}_{p}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})):=\frac{1}{\det(1-p^{-s}\operatorname{Fr}_{p}|\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})_{\mathbb{Q}})}

be the
p
-adic Artin L-function. Here the Frobenius
\operatorname{Fr}_{p}
acts on
\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})\simeq\operatorname{Pic}({\mathscr{V}}_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})
. Let us define the convergence factors

\lambda_{p}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }p\in S;\\
{\mathbb{L}}_{p}(1,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))&\text{ if }p\not\in S.\end{cases}

The (global) Artin
{\mathbb{L}}
-function is

{\mathbb{L}}_{S}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})):=\prod_{p\not\in S}{\mathbb{L}}_{p}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})).

It admits a pole at
s=1
of order
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Pic}(V)
(
[
12
, Lemme 2.2.5]
).
Recall that (
[
12
, Définition 2.2]
) the finite Tamagawa measure of
{\mathscr{V}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
with congruence condition
(L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda})
is

\mathfrak{S}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}({\mathscr{V}})=\left(\lim_{s\to 1}(s-1){\mathbb{L}}_{S}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))\right)\prod_{p<\infty}\lambda_{p}^{-1}\sigma_{p}({\mathscr{V}};L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}).

Let us now compute these Artin
{\mathbb{L}}
-functions more closely. If any non-ramified
p
is split in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{\Delta_{F}})
, which is equivalent to
\psi_{F}(p)=1
, then the action of
\operatorname{Fr}_{p}
is trivial and hence

{\mathbb{L}}_{p}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))=\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-2}.

While for inert
p
(
\Leftrightarrow\psi_{F}(p)=-1
),
\operatorname{Fr}_{p}
acts via exchanging the two generators of
\operatorname{Pic}({\mathscr{V}}_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})
, and so

{\mathbb{L}}_{p}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))=\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}.

We conclude that if
p
is unramified, then we have

{\mathbb{L}}_{p}(s,\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))=\left(1-\frac{\psi_{F}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

Now we easily deduce the accordance of the non-archimedean arithmetic factor with
\mathfrak{S}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}({\mathscr{V}})
.
Finally we note that (for example by direct computation)
H^{1}({\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Pic}(V_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}))=0
, and hence
\beta(V)=1
. So we finally confirm Peyre’s prediction.

∎

{\mathscr{W}}^{o}\bmod L_{0}
is regular.

Let us define the normalised Tagamawa measure
\omega_{f}
on
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
as follows.
For every non-empty compact open
{\mathscr{E}}\subseteq{\mathscr{W}}^{o}(\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
and for every
L
with
L_{0}\mid L
, we cover
{\mathscr{E}}
by a finite number of disjoint congruence neighbourhoods modulo
L
:

{\mathscr{E}}=\bigsqcup_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}).

Then we let

(5.3)

\omega_{f}({\mathscr{E}}):=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{L}}(2,\chi_{0}[L])}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}({\mathscr{W}}),

where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}({\mathscr{W}})
is defined by (
2.23
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
W^{o}(\mathbb{R})\neq\varnothing
. If
\Delta_{F}>0
, then the real locus
W^{o}(\mathbb{R})
consists of one connected component, and if
\Delta_{F}<0
, then
W^{o}(\mathbb{R})
has two connected components over
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

As
W^{o}(\mathbb{R})\neq\emptyset
, we may assume after a change of variables that
W^{o}
is the punctured affine cone of the non-singular quadratic form

t_{0}t_{1}=F_{2}(t_{2},t_{3})

where
F_{2}(t_{2},t_{3})
is a binary quadratic form over
\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
\Delta_{F}>0
, then
F_{2}(t_{2},t_{3})
has a non-trivial zero over
\mathbb{R}
, and if
\Delta_{F}<0
, then
F_{2}(t_{2},t_{3})
is a positive or negative definite quadratic form over
\mathbb{R}
. I.e. after a change of variables, we may assume that
W^{o}
is the punctured affine cone of
t_{0}t_{1}=t_{2}t_{3}
in the case
\Delta_{F}>0
or is given by the punctured affine cone of
t_{0}t_{1}=t_{2}^{2}+t_{3}^{2}
if
\Delta_{F}<0
. The real locus of the first one is connected, whereas the second punctured affine cone has two connected components over
\mathbb{R}
, which are split by the Brauer class
(t_{1},-1)=(t_{0},-1)
.
∎

If the real locus
W^{o}({\mathbb{R}})
has two connected components, then we write
W_{1},W_{2}
for these. Note that we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{inv}_{\infty}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{W_{1}}=\operatorname{inv}_{\infty}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{W_{2}}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\Delta_{F}>0;

\displaystyle\operatorname{inv}_{\infty}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{W_{1}}=\operatorname{inv}_{\infty}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{W_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }\Delta_{F}<0.

Throughout this section,
L
is considered fixed (i.e. we take
\tau=1
). We omit its dependency in all implied constants. Let
\widetilde{w}_{B}
be defined by (
2.14
).
We shall prove

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume
L_{0}\mid L
and let
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}\in{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})
. In the case
\Delta_{F}<0
, assume that the support of
w_{0}
is contained in one of the components
W_{j}
for some
1\leqslant j\leqslant 2
, and that
W_{j}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\subseteq W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}
. Then

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(\widetilde{w}_{B};(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}))=2{\mathscr{I}}(\widehat{w})\omega_{f}\left({\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\right)B^{2}+O_{\varepsilon}(B^{1+\varepsilon}),

where
{\mathscr{I}}(\widehat{w})
is defined by (
2.25
).

Theorem
5.5
offers a new proof and a quantitative version of strong approximation with Brauer–Manin obstruction for integral points on
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}
, i.e. Theorem
5.1
. On combining Lemmas
4.6
,
4.7
and Theorem
5.5
, we arrive at determining the term
{\mathscr{K}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(\widehat{w})
, and we can deduce cleaner asymptotic formulas for
{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(\widetilde{w}_{B};(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}))
in Theorem
4.1
(without the term
{\mathscr{K}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}(\widehat{w})
). This completes the result in
[
11
, §5]
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Under the assumptions in Theorem
5.5
, we have

{\mathscr{K}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}}(\widehat{w})={\mathscr{I}}(\widehat{w})\omega_{f}\left({\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\right){\mathbb{L}}(2,\psi_{F}\chi_{0}[L]).

Moreover, for every
\gamma\in({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, we have

{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(\widetilde{w}_{B};(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}))=B^{2}{\mathscr{I}}(w)\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}}({\mathscr{W}})\left(1+\psi_{F}(\gamma)\frac{{\mathbb{L}}(2,\psi_{F}\chi_{0}[L])}{{\mathbb{L}}(2,\chi_{0}[L])}\right)+O_{\varepsilon}(B^{1+\varepsilon}).

Proof.

We start by computing the term
{\mathscr{L}}(D_{0})
introduced in Lemma
4.7
:

\sum_{d\leqslant D_{0},(d,L)=1}\frac{\mu(d)\psi_{F}(d)}{d^{2}}={\mathbb{L}}(2,\psi_{F}\chi_{0}[L])^{-1}+O_{\varepsilon}(B^{-1+\varepsilon}).

The formula for
{\mathscr{K}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}}(\widehat{w})
follows directly from comparing Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
5.5
.

To deduce the formula for
{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(\widetilde{w}_{B};(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}))
it suffices to combine Theorem
4.1
with the formula for
{\mathscr{K}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}}(\widehat{w})
above, and then to use Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Assuming
\Delta_{F}<0
and that the support of
w_{0}
is contained in
W_{1}
and assuming that
(W_{1}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}))\cap W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}=\varnothing
, we similarly deduce

{\mathscr{K}}_{L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}}(\widehat{w})=-{\mathscr{I}}(\widehat{w})\omega_{f}\left({\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\right){\mathbb{L}}(2,\psi_{F}\chi_{0}[L])

and the corresponding formula for
{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{W}}}(\widetilde{w}_{B};(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}))
. From these formulas we see that the value of
{\mathscr{K}}
is “biased” depending on whether the adelic sets meet the Brauer–Manin locus or not.

Before preceding to the proof of Theorem
5.5
, we make the following key observation:

Lemma 5.8
.

Assume
L_{0}\mid L
and let
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}\in{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})
. In the case
\Delta_{F}<0
, assume that the support of
w_{0}
is contained in one of the components
W_{j}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant 2
and that
W_{j}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\subseteq W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}
.
In the case
\Delta_{F}<0
, for every
\gamma\in({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, we have

\displaystyle W_{j}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\subseteq W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\psi_{F}(\gamma)=1;

\displaystyle W_{j}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\cap W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}=\varnothing

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\psi_{F}(\gamma)=-1.

In the case
\Delta_{F}>0
the same conclusions hold with
W_{j}
replaced by
W^{o}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

By Hensel’s lemma, for every
p\mid L
, we can lift
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0}
into
{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
and we denote such a lift by
(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})_{p}
. By assumption, we have

(5.4)

\operatorname{inv}_{\infty}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{W_{j}}+\sum_{p\nmid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}+\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},g((\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})_{p}))=0.

Now for every
\gamma\in({\mathbb{Z}}/L{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, we then have
W_{j}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\subseteq W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}
if and only if

(5.5)

\operatorname{inv}_{\infty}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{W_{1}}+\sum_{p\nmid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},g)|_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}+\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},g(\gamma(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})_{p}))=0.

On the other hand,

W_{j}\times{\mathscr{E}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{o}}(L,\gamma\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})\cap W^{o}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\operatorname{Br}}=\varnothing
if and only if the right-hand-side of (
5.5
) is equal to
\frac{1}{2}
.
Since
g
is homogeneous, we have

\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},g(\gamma(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})_{p}))=\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},g((\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{0})_{p}))+\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma).

Given (
5.4
), the condition (
5.5
) holds (resp. does not hold) if and only if

\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma)=0\quad\left(\text{resp. }\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\right).

We choose a representative
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
which is a positive integer, for the residue class
\gamma\in(\mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Note that
\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma)=0
for all
p\nmid L\gamma
by
[
13
, Corollary 6.9.3]
(see also
[
15
, III.1 Theorem 1]
).
By Hilbert’s reciprocity law (see
[
15
, III.2 Theorem 3]
), we have

(5.6)

\sum_{p\mid L}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma)=\sum_{p\mid\gamma}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma).

Since

\psi_{F}(\gamma)=\prod_{p\mid\gamma}\left(\frac{\Delta_{F}}{p}\right)^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\gamma)},

(recall that
2\mid L
and
2\nmid\gamma
) a usual computation of the Hilbert symbol (see
[
15
, III.1 Theorem 1]
) shows that

\sum_{p\mid\gamma}\operatorname{inv}_{p}(\Delta_{F},\gamma)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\psi_{F}(\gamma)=1;\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }\psi_{F}(\gamma)=-1.\\
\end{cases}

Recalling (
5.6
), this finishes the proof of Lemma
5.8
.
∎

Remark 5.10

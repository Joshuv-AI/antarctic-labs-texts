Higher-dimensional generalization of Youngs’ theorem and circular colorings
Kengo Enami, Takahiro Matsushita

Abstract
. In 1996, Youngs proved that any quadrangulation of the real projective plane is not 3 3 -chromatic. This result has been extended in various directions over the years, including to other non-orientable closed surfaces, higher-dimensional analogues of quadrangulations and circular colorings. In this paper, we provide a generalization which yields some of these extensions of Youngs’ theorem.

Primary 05C15; Secondary 05C10

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Youngs’ theorem

Quadrangulations on surfaces have been extensively studied in topological graph theory. The purpose of this paper is to provide a generalization of a theorem by Youngs (Theorem
1.1
) concerning the graph coloring problem of quadrangulations on the real projective plane.

In this paper, every graph is assumed to be simple. A
surface
means a compact connected
2
-dimensional manifold, possibly with boundary. An embedded graph
G
on a surface
S
is said to be
cellular
if every face is homeomorphic to a
2
-cell. A cellular embedding is said to be
even-sided
if every face is bounded by a closed walk of even length. In particular, if every face is bounded by a closed walk of length
4
, the embedding is called a
quadrangulation
.

Graph coloring problems are one of the central topics in topological graph theory. For plane graphs, the Four Color Theorem
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
serves as a cornerstone result. For graphs embedded on non-spherical closed surfaces, the Map Color Theorem
[
12
]
is a natural extension of the four color theorem, which states that every graph embedded on a non-spherical closed surface
S
with Euler characteristic
\varepsilon(S)
has chromatic number

\chi(G)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{49-24\varepsilon(S)}}{2}\right\rfloor.

This bound was later shown to be best possible for all closed surfaces except for the Klein bottle, through the work of Ringel and Youngs
[
25
]
.
These foundational results have inspired a vast body of subsequent research. In addition to these developments, coloring problems for graphs with even-sided embeddings have also received attention.

It is easy to see that a graph cellularly embedded on the sphere is
2
-colorable if and only if the embedding is even-sided.
On the other hand, Hutchinson
[
14
]
showed that every graph
G
that admits an even-sided embedding on a non-spherical closed surface
S
has chromatic number

\chi(G)\leq\dfrac{5+\sqrt{25-16\varepsilon(S)}}{2}.

Later, Liu et al.
[
19
]
proved that this bound is best possible for all closed surfaces except for the Klein bottle and the double torus.
In the case of a quadrangulation
G
on the projective plane, the above inequality implies that
\chi(G)\leq 4
. On the other hand, Youngs proved the following landmark result.

Theorem 1.1

(Youngs
[
28
]
)
.

Any quadrangulation of the real projective plane
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
is not
3
-chromatic.

Hence the chromatic number of a quadrangulation of a real projective plane is
2
or
4
.
This phenomenon is remarkable since
3
-chromatic quadrangulations exist for all other non-spherical closed surfaces.
Moreover, Hutchinson
[
15
]
showed that every graph with an even-sided embedding on any orientable closed surface in which every non-contractible cycle is sufficiently long is
3
-colorable.
Youngs’ theorem and its extension to non-orientable closed surface mentioned below (Theorem
1.2
) indicate that this does not hold for non-orientable closed surfaces.

1.2.
Various generalizations and refinements

There are various extensions of Youngs’ theorem, and our main theorem (Theorem
A
) is a generalization of Youngs’ theorem, which yields some of them.

First, we discuss the extension of Youngs’ theorem to non-orientable closed surfaces, which was independently established by Archdeacon et al.
[
5
]
and Mohar and Seymour
[
23
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Archdeacon et al.
[
5
]
and Mohar and Seymour
[
23
]
)
.

Let
N
be a closed non-orientable connected surface, and
G
a quadrangulation of
N
.
Suppose that
G
has an odd cycle
C
such that the surface obtained by cutting
N
along
C
is orientable.
Then
G
is not
3
-chromatic.

Next we discuss a higher-dimensional generalization of Youngs’ theorem.
Hachimori, Nakamoto and Ozeki
[
10
]
considered
d
-dimensional quadrangulations of the real
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}P^{d}
for any
d\geq 2
as follows:
Set
I=[0,1]
and consider the standard CW structure of
I^{n}
.
A regular CW complex
C
is called a
cubical complex
if each characteristic map
\varphi\colon I^{n}\to C
of an
n
-cell of
C
is a cellular embedding, that is,
\varphi(I^{n})
is a subcomplex of
C
and
\varphi
is an isomorphism between
I^{n}
and
\varphi(I^{n})
.
If a cubical complex
C
is homeomorphic to a topological space
X
, then the 1-skeleton of
C
is called a
quadrangulation
of
X
.

Hachimori, Nakamoto and Ozeki
[
10
]
proved that for all
d\geq 2
, any
d
-dimensional quadrangulation of the real projective space
\mathbb{R}P^{d}
satisfying a certain geometric condition is
2
- or
4
-chromatic. They further asked whether this geometric condition is necessary; that is, whether every non-bipartite
d
-dimensional quadrangulation of
\mathbb{R}P^{d}
is necessarily
4
-chromatic. Recently, Kaiser et al.
[
16
]
announced a negative answer to this question by constructing
3
-dimensional quadrangulations of
\mathbb{R}P^{3}
with arbitrarily large chromatic numbers. Nevertheless, they also established the following theorem, which shows that Youngs’ result does extend to higher dimensions under certain conditions.

Theorem 1.3

(Kaiser et al.
[
16
]
)
.

For all
d\geq 2
, every
d
-dimensional quadrangulation of the real projective space
\mathbb{R}P^{d}
is not
3
-chromatic.

Another type of higher-dimensional generalization of Youngs’ theorem was discussed in Kaiser and Stehlík
[
17
]
.

Finally, we discuss a refinement of Youngs’ theorem from the perspective of circular chromatic numbers. The circular chromatic number
\chi_{c}(G)
is a real-valued invariant of a graph
G
satisfying
\lceil\chi_{c}(G)\rceil=\chi(G)
. In this sense,
\chi_{c}(G)
refines the chromatic number, and has been extensively studied. We refer the reader to
[
24
,
29
,
30
]
for surveys on this concept.
The precise definition of the circular chromatic number will be given in Subsection
5.1
.
DeVos et al.
[
7
]
proved the following refinement of Youngs’ theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(DeVos et al.
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a non-bipartite graph with an even-sided embedding on
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
.
Let
k
be a positive integer such that every face of
G
is
2i
-gon with
i\leq k
.
Then

\chi_{c}(G)\geq 2+\frac{2}{k-1}.

1.3.
Results

The goal of this paper is to present a generalization of Youngs’ theorem that yields Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
. Before stating the main theorem, we define a class of CW complexes. Let
X
be a CW complex satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

The
1
-skeleton
X^{1}
of
X
is a simple graph.

(2)

The attaching map of each
2
-cell of
X
is a graph homomorphism
C_{2r}\to X^{1}
for some
r\geq 2
.

In this case, we say that
X
is a
CW complex with even
2
-skeleton
. When the attaching map of every
2
-cell is a graph homomorphism from
C_{4}
, we say that
X
is a
CW complex with quadrangulated
2
-skeleton
. Note that a cubical complex mentioned in Subsection
1.2
is a CW complex with quadrangulated
2
-skeleton.

Now we are ready to state our main theorem of this paper, which is a generalization of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
(see also Proposition
2.6
).

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a CW complex with even 2-skeleton,
k
an integer at least
2
, and suppose that each attaching map
C_{2i}\to X^{1}
satisfies
i\leq k
.
Assume that there is a closed walk
\gamma
in
X^{1}
with odd length such that the integral homology class
[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
represented by
\gamma
is a torsion element in
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. Then,

\chi_{c}(X^{1})\geq 2+\frac{2}{k-1}.

Recall that
X^{1}
contains an odd closed walk if and only if it is non-bipartite. Note that if
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is a torsion group, then the condition that
[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a torsion element in
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is automatically satisfied.

Restricting our attention to quadrangulations and chromatic numbers, we have the following theorem:

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a CW complex with quadrangulated
2
-skeleton. Suppose that there is a closed walk
\gamma
of
X^{1}
with odd length such that the integral homology class
[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
represented by
\gamma
is a torsion element in
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. Then
X^{1}
is not
3
-chromatic.

Theorem
B
is a generalization of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. In Section 3, we provide a short proof of Theorem
B
using classical algebraic topology. As a by
-
product of this proof, we show that for any CW complex with even 2-skeleton such that
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is trivial, the 1-skeleton
X^{1}
of
X
is bipartite (Corollary
3.3
). This is a further generalization of the fact that a cellular even-sided embedding on the
2
-sphere is bipartite.

In the proof of Theorem
A
, we use the discretized fundamental group introduced by the second author
[
21
]
in the study of the fundamental group of Lovász’s neighborhood complex
[
20
]
. For a positive integer
k
and a based graph
(G,v)
, we associate
\pi_{1}^{k}(G,v)
, called the
k
-fundamental group. The preliminaries of
k
-fundamental groups will be given in Section 4.

Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we provide preliminaries of graphs and algebraic topology. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
B
. In Section 4, we provide preliminaries of
k
-fundamental groups, following
[
21
]
for the proof of Theorem
A
. In Section 5, we recall the definition of circular chromatic number and provide the proof of Theorem
A
.

2.1.
Graphs

Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be simple; that is, a graph is a pair
(V,E)
, where
V
is a set and
E
is a family of
2
-element subsets of
V
. Note that graphs are not assumed to be finite.

For graphs
G
and
H
, a
graph homomorphism from
G
to
H
is a map
f\colon V(G)\to V(H)
such that
\{v,w\}\in E(G)
implies
\{f(v),f(w)\}\in E(H)
. Let
K_{n}
denote the complete graph with
n
vertices, i.e.,
V(K_{n})=\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
i,j\in V(K_{n})
are adjacent if and only if
i\neq j
. An
n
-coloring of a graph
G
is a graph homomorphism from
G
to
K_{n}
. For
n\geq 3
, the
n
-cycle graph
C_{n}
is defined by
V(C_{n})=\mathbb{Z}/n
and
E(C_{n})=\{\{x,x+1\}\mid x\in\mathbb{Z}/n\}
.

2.2.
Basic facts from algebraic topology

For basic definitions and facts of CW complexes, we refer the reader to Chapter 0 of
[
11
]
. For a CW complex
X
and for a non-negative integer
k
, the
k
-skeleton of
X
is the subcomplex of
X
consisting of all
i
-cells of
X
such that
i\leq k
, and is denoted by
X^{k}
.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
11
, Example 1B.1 and Proposition 4.1]
)
.

For every connected graph
G
and for every integer
i
at least
2
,
\pi_{i}(G)
is trivial; that is, for every
i\geq 2
and every continuous map from
S^{i}
to
G
is null-homotopic.

For a
k
-cell
\sigma
of a CW complex
X
, let
\Phi_{\sigma}\colon S^{k-1}\to X^{k-1}
denote the attaching map of
\sigma
. Note that a continuous map
f\colon S^{k-1}\to Y
is null-homotopic if and only if there is a continuous map
f^{\prime}\colon D^{k}\to Y
such that
f^{\prime}|_{S^{k-1}}=f
. By the definition of CW complexes, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2

(See also
[
11
, Lemma 4.7 and its proof]
)
.

Let
X
be a CW complex,
Y
a path-connected topological space, and
f\colon X^{k}\to Y
a continuous map. Suppose that for each
(k+1)
-cell
\sigma
the composition
f\circ\Phi_{\sigma}\colon S^{k}\to Y
is null-homotopic. Then there is a continuous map
f^{\prime}\colon X^{k+1}\to Y
such that
f^{\prime}|_{X^{k}}=f
.

Here we review the cellular homology. See
[
11
, Section 2.2, Section 3.A]
for details. For a CW complex
X
and for a commutative ring
R
with unit, let
C_{\bullet}(X;R)
be the cellular chain complex of
X
. For a non-negative integer
k
,
C_{k}(X;R)
can be identified with the free
R
-module generated by the set of
k
-cells of
X
. We set
C_{k}(X;R)=0
for
k<0
. We can define an
R
-linear map
\partial_{k}\colon C_{k}(X;R)\to C_{k-1}(X;R)
, called the
boundary map
, which satisfies
\partial_{k-1}\circ\partial_{k}=0
, and the homology group of
C_{k}(X;R)
can be naturally identified with the (singular) homology group
H_{k}(X;R)
of
X
.

Here we explain in detail the boundary map in the following case:
X^{1}
is a simple graph,
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and the attaching map of a
2
-cell
\sigma
is a graph homomorphism
\Phi_{\sigma}\colon C_{n}\to X^{1}
. Then
\sigma\in C_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and
\partial\sigma\in C_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Hence
\partial\sigma
is written as

\partial\sigma=a_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}e_{m},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
e_{i}\in E(X^{1})
. The coefficient
a_{i}
is determined by

a_{i}=\#\{e\in E(C_{2n+1})\mid\Phi_{\sigma}(e)=e_{i}\}\quad\mod 2.

See
[
11
, Proposition 2.3, Cellular boundary formula in Section 2.2]
.

In this paper, we regard a circle
S^{1}
as
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and
0
as its basepoint. Let
\gamma\colon(S^{1},0)\to(X,x_{0})
be a loop of a based space
(X,x_{0})
. Let
[S^{1}]\in H_{1}(S^{1};\mathbb{Z})
be the fundamental class of
S^{1}
, and
\gamma_{*}([S^{1}])\in H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. This correspondence yields the following group homomorphism

h\colon\pi_{1}(X,x)\to H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}),\quad[\gamma]\mapsto\gamma_{*}([S^{1}]),

which is called the Hurewicz map.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
11
, Theorem 2A.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a path-connected space and
x_{0}
a point of
X
. Then the Hurewicz map induces a group isomorphism

\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})/[\pi_{1}(X,x_{0}),\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})]\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}).

By the cellular approximation theorem, we have the following theorem:

Proposition 2.4

(see
[
11
, Corollary 4.12]
)
.

Let
X
be a CW complex,
A
a subcomplex of
X
,
x_{0}
a vertex of
A
, and
\iota
the inclusion map from
A
to
X
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If
X^{2}=A^{2}
, then
\iota_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(A,x_{0})\to\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
is an isomorphism.

(2)

If
X^{1}=A^{1}
, then
\iota_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(A,x_{0})\to\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
is surjective.

2.3.
Homology classes of simple closed curves in surfaces

In relation to Theorem
1.2
, here we discuss the homology classes of some simple closed curves in non-orientable closed surfaces.

Let
M
be a surface, possibly having a boundary. A
simple closed curve in
M
is an injective continuous map
\gamma\colon S^{1}\to M
. Let
[S^{1}]_{R}\in H_{1}(S^{1};R)
be the fundamental class of
S^{1}
. We call
\gamma_{*}([S^{1}]_{R})\in H_{1}(M;R)
the
homology class of
M
determined by
\gamma
. We write
[\gamma]_{R}
to indicate this homology class
\gamma_{*}([S^{1}]_{R})
.

The following proposition is a well-known fact in topology, and is deduced from
[
11
, Exercise 31 of Section 3.3]
and the homology exact sequence for pairs.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
M
be a compact orientable surface whose boundary
\partial M
is homeomorphic to
S^{1}
. Then the homology class determined by
\partial M
in
H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is
0
.

This proposition yields the following:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
N
be a non-orientable compact surface,
\gamma
a simple closed curve of
N
such that the surface obtained by cutting
N
along
\gamma
is orientable. Then, we have
2[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}=0
.

Proof.

Let
M
be the surface obtained by cutting
N
along
\gamma
. Then the boundary of
M
is homeomorphic to
S^{1}
and there is a natural continuous map
f\colon M\to N
such that the restriction to
\partial M
is the double cover
S^{1}\to S^{1}
. Hence we have
0=f_{*}([\partial M]_{\mathbb{Z}})=\pm 2[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

3.
Chromatic numbers

In this section, we provide a short proof of Theorem
B
. Let
G
be a graph,
k
an integer at least
3
and
f\colon G\to K_{k}
a graph homomorphism. A
rainbow square
is a
4
-cycle
C_{4}
in
G
such that
f(i)\neq f(j)
for every distinct pair of vertices
i,j
of
C_{4}
. The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem, which is a generalization of Theorem
B
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a CW complex with quadrangulated
2
-skeleton,
k
an integer at least
3
and
f\colon X^{1}\to K_{k}
a
k
-coloring of
X^{1}
. Suppose that there is a closed walk
\gamma
of
X^{1}
with odd length such that the integral homology class
[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
represented by
\gamma
is a torsion. Then
X^{1}
has a rainbow square. In particular,
\chi(X^{1})\geq 4
.

We begin the proof with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a CW complex with even
2
-skeleton. Let
\gamma
be a closed walk of
X^{1}
with odd length. Then the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homology class
[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
represented by
\gamma
is non-zero.

Proof.

Let
C_{\bullet}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
be the cellular chain complex with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficient of
X
. Define the homomorphism
\Phi\colon C_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by
\Phi([\sigma])=1
for every
1
-cell
\sigma
of
X
. Since
X
is a CW complex with even
2
-skeleton, the composition

C_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{\partial}C_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}_{2}

is zero. Since
\gamma
is a closed walk with odd length, we have
\Phi(\gamma)\neq 0
, and hence
\gamma
is not contained in
\partial C_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. This means that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homology class
[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
is non-zero.
∎

This proposition yields the following noteworthy corollary, which generalizes the well-known fact that every even-sided cellular embedding on
S^{2}
is bipartite.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X
be a CW complex with even
2
-skeleton. If
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0
, then the
1
-skeleton
X^{1}
of
X
is bipartite.

Here we note the following lemma, which is easily verified:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k
be an integer and
f\colon C_{4}\to K_{k}
a graph homomorphism. If there are distinct
i
,
j\in V(C_{4})
such that
f(i)=f(j)
, then
f
is null-homotopic.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
f\colon X^{1}\to K_{k}
be a graph homomorphism and suppose that
f
has no rainbow square. Since
X
is a CW complex with quadrangulated
2
-skeleton, an attaching map of a
2
-cell
\sigma
of
X
is a graph homomorphism
\varphi_{\sigma}\colon C_{4}\to X^{1}
. Then Lemma
3.4
implies that
\varphi_{\sigma}
is null-homotopic. This means that there is a continuous map
f^{\prime}\colon X^{2}\to K_{k}
such that
f^{\prime}|_{X^{1}}=f
(Proposition
2.2
). Since
\pi_{i}(K_{k})=0
for every
i\geq 2
(Proposition
2.1
), it follows that there is a continuous map
F\colon X\to K_{k}
such that
F|_{X^{2}}=f^{\prime}
.

We now consider
F_{*}([\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}})
in
H_{1}(K_{k};\mathbb{Z})
. Since
\tau=[\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a torsion element and
H_{1}(K_{k};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}{2}}
, we have
F_{*}([\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}})=0
. On the other hand, we can show that
F_{*}([\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}})
is non-zero as follows: First, note that
F_{*}([\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}})=[F\circ\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}=[f\circ\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence
F_{*}([\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}})
is the homology class represented by the closed walk
f\circ\gamma
with odd length in
K_{k}
. Since the natural homomorphism
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
sends
[f\circ\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
[f\circ\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
, Proposition
3.2
implies that
F_{*}([\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}})=[f\circ\gamma]_{\mathbb{Z}}
is non-zero. This is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Archdeacon et al.
[
5
]
proved Theorem

by showing the following stronger theorem: Let

that cuts open

Then, for any coloring of

Remark 3.6
.

Kaiser et al.
[
16
]
in fact showed the following generalizing form of Theorem
1.3
. Suppose that
X
is a
d
-dimensional closed manifold satisfying the following conditions. Here,
\smile
denotes the cup product.

(1)

For any non-trivial element
\alpha
of
H^{d-2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, there exists
\beta\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
such that
\alpha\smile\beta=0
.

(2)

For any non-trivial elements
\alpha,\beta\in H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
,
\alpha\smile\beta\neq 0
.

Then,
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
states that any quadrangulation of
X
is not 3-chromatic.

Using Proposition
3.2
, we can strengthen their result as follows: If
X
is a CW complex with quadrangulated 2-skeleton and its
1
-skeleton
X^{1}
is

Definition 4.3

Lemma 4.6

Let

Proof.

Theorem 5.8
.

Suppose that
0<a<\frac{1}{2}
, and set

. Let
k
be the smallest integer satisfying

Then the graph homomorphism

is a
(k-1)
-covering map, but not a
k
-covering map.

Proof.

We first show that
p
is not a
k
-covering. By the definition of

, there is a walk
\tilde{\gamma}\colon P_{2k}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{Bor}}(S^{1};a)
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=(0,1)
and
\tilde{\gamma}(2k)=(1,1)
. Then we have
(0,1),(1,1)\in N^{k}(\tilde{\gamma}(k))
but

. This means that

is not a
k
-covering.

Next we show that
p
is a

-covering. Let

. By Lemmas

and
5.6
, it suffices to show that

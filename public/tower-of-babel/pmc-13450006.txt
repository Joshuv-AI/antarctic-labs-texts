Utility of deep learning for degree calculation of aortic arch calcification in chest-X ray
Authors: Chung-Kuan Wu, Chien-Yu Huang, Ting-Xuan Shen, Yu-Shiuan Tsai, Jun-Wei Hsieh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAortic arch calcification (AoAC) is commonly classified into four grades according to the percentage of calcification observed in clinical practice, and the interpretation is typically based on visual assessment by clinicians. However, this manual evaluation process is time-consuming and may fail to detect subtle calcifications, potentially leading to grading inaccuracies.MethodsThis study presents a transformer-based model, termed Multi-Attention with Transformer Model (MATM), to improve the accuracy of AoAC grade classification. The proposed framework integrates multiple attention modules to enhance the representation of spatial features. In addition, the transformer mechanism incorporates positional information together with a hierarchical 16-block representation of the aortic arch, enabling fine-grained analysis of calcification distribution.ResultsThe proposed method captures subtle calcification features and enables more accurate classification of AoAC grades. Experimental results demonstrate that the model can automatically estimate AoAC severity for both the traditional four-grade classification and the more detailed 16-grade classification, achieving an accuracy of up to 95.5%.ConclusionsThe proposed method can reduce interpretation time and improve grading consistency for clinicians by minimizing variability caused by individual experience. Such AI-assisted assessment has the potential to standardize AoAC evaluation in future clinical practice.Keywords: Aortic arch calcification, Deep learning, ResNet

Background

With the progress of science and technology and economic development, people’s living standards have improved significantly. However, unhealthy lifestyles, such as excessive intake of seasonings, alcohol consumption, and smoking, have increased the risk of cardiovascular-related diseases. Previous studies have shown that the blood vessels of people aged over 60 years gradually become stiff and lose elasticity due to calcium deposition, affecting arteries from large to medium size [1–3]. Aortic arch calcification (AoAC) is considered an important indicator of severe systemic arteriosclerosis. Patients with AoAC have a higher risk of hospitalization or death from coronary heart disease, stroke, and peripheral vascular diseases. If individuals have a history of diabetes, smoking, or cardiovascular diseases, their risk of developing AoAC-related complications will also increase. Although aortic calcification cannot be completely reversed, early detection can help physicians develop treatment strategies to slow disease progression.

There are two main imaging modalities for identifying aortic calcification: computed tomography (CT) and chest X-ray. Among them, chest X-ray is more commonly used in clinical practice because of its lower cost and wider accessibility. As shown in Fig. 1, AoAC severity can be divided into four grades according to the percentage of calcification observed in the aortic arch. Based on the grading results, physicians can determine appropriate treatment strategies. Currently, AoAC grading is mainly performed through manual visual assessment by experienced clinicians. However, subtle calcifications are often difficult to detect, and small calcified regions may be overlooked during manual interpretation, potentially delaying treatment and worsening the disease condition. To address this problem, we propose a deep learning framework incorporating attention mechanisms and transformer architectures [5] to assist clinicians in interpreting AoAC from chest X-ray images.

With the rapid development of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and deep learning techniques, many researchers have applied these methods to medical image analysis and biomedical imaging tasks. Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of deep learning in various imaging modalities, including ultrasound imaging, cardiovascular imaging, and bioimage analysis [8–10]. For example, deep learning has been used for super-resolution ultrasound microvessel imaging, cardiovascular imaging analysis, and biomedical image interpretation [8, 9]. In addition, deep learning techniques have also been applied to computational imaging tasks such as digital holographic microscopy and medical image segmentation [11, 12]. Beyond medical imaging, deep learning models have been successfully used in engineering and signal processing applications, including materials property prediction, aeroengine life prediction, industrial defect detection, and sparse-angle X-ray reconstruction [13–16]. These studies demonstrate the strong capability of deep learning models in extracting complex patterns from large-scale data.

However, the characteristics of most medical samples are not as rich and diverse as objects in natural scenes. For example, vascular calcifications on X-ray images often appear visually similar to bones, making accurate detection challenging. As a result, misclassification may occur during manual inspection or when using traditional computer vision techniques. To address this issue, this study proposes a new CNN-based framework to automatically calculate the degrees of AoAC in patients undergoing hemodialysis. First, the location of the aortic arch must be detected. We adopt our previous framework PRB-Net [4], which can detect small objects more effectively than YOLOv4 [6], to identify the aortic arch region and remove irrelevant image areas.

After the region of interest is determined, a Patch-per-Sector (PPS) method is introduced to extract key patches within the aortic arch area. Useful features embedded in each patch are then extracted using ResNet [7] as the backbone network for AoAC grade classification. However, small patch sizes may limit the depth of feature extraction layers, resulting in insufficient feature representation for AoAC scoring. To overcome this limitation, we propose a Multi-Attention with Transformer Model (MATM), which incorporates attention mechanisms to enhance the importance of key features in each patch. The proposed model can effectively identify subtle calcifications and improve the accuracy of AoAC grade classification. More importantly, the proposed MATM framework can automatically estimate AoAC severity not only for the traditional four-grade classification but also for the more challenging 16-grade classification, achieving an accuracy of up to 95.5%. Related work

Convolutional neural networks

To calculate AoAC grades, the aortic arch region must first be detected. Therefore, several state-of-the-art (SoTA) object detection methods are briefly reviewed in the following section. Most SoTA approaches are designed to detect objects in color images, including R-CNN [17], faster R-CNN [18], Mask R-CNN [19], SSD [20], RetinaNet [21], and YOLO v4 [6]. Current object detection models can generally be categorized into two groups based on their network architectures: two-stage proposal-based methods and one-stage (single-shot) methods. In general, two-stage detectors achieve higher detection accuracy but require longer computation time, whereas one-stage detectors provide faster inference with slightly lower accuracy. In this study, we focus on one-stage object detectors due to their efficiency and suitability for real-time medical image analysis.

One-stage object detectors typically consist of two main components: a backbone network and a prediction head. The backbone network extracts hierarchical feature maps from the input image, providing high-level semantic representations while gradually reducing spatial resolution. In many cases, the backbone network is pre-trained as an image classifier on large-scale datasets such as ImageNet to improve feature learning. OverFeat [22] was one of the earliest CNN-based one-stage object detectors, proposed in 2013, which adopted a sliding-window paradigm for object localization and classification. One of the most well-known detectors in deep learning is YOLO (You Only Look Once) [23], which divides the input image into non-overlapping grid cells, where each grid cell predicts objects whose centers fall within the corresponding region. SSD (Single Shot MultiBox Detector) [20] further improves detection performance by using multiple feature maps within the network to detect objects of different sizes. Subsequently, YOLOv2 [24] introduced the Darknet-19 backbone and anchor boxes to improve detection accuracy. The Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) [25] has also been widely adopted in modern object detection frameworks to detect objects at multiple scales. To further enhance semantic feature extraction and detection precision, YOLOv3 [15] replaced the Darknet-19 backbone with Darknet-53 and integrated FPN for multi-scale feature representation, achieving superior detection performance.

To ensure high detection accuracy, most state-of-the-art (SoTA) object detectors employ very deep convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures (often with more than 100 layers) to construct a feature pyramid for extracting rich semantic features. However, the spatial resolution of feature maps is progressively reduced after each pooling operation within the feature pyramid network (FPN). As a result, tiny objects (smaller than 32 × 32 pixels) may be reduced to a single-pixel feature representation in the deepest layers of the pyramid, leading to insufficient spatial information for accurate detection.

To address this issue, bidirectional feature pyramid structures have been proposed to incorporate bottom-up pathways that recover information from shallow layers. For example, Tan et al. [26] proposed the BiFPN (Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network) to improve the detection of small objects while maintaining high computational efficiency. In addition, YOLOv4 [27] improves small-object detection by adopting a path aggregation network (PANet) [28], where feature fusion is performed through concatenation rather than simple addition. However, these bidirectional feature pyramid structures may still struggle to maintain balanced detection performance for both small and large objects. To overcome this limitation, PRB-FPN [4] introduces a parallel feature pyramid structure [29] with bidirectional (top-down and bottom-up) feature fusion and several additional improvements. This design enables the network to effectively detect both small and large objects while maintaining high computational efficiency.

When training deep neural networks through backpropagation, the vanishing gradient problem often occurs, which may lead to significant performance degradation. To address this issue, ResNet [7] introduced skip connections, enabling the network to learn residual features while mitigating the vanishing gradient problem.

However, in such architectures, the entire set of channel features is propagated to the next convolutional block and through the skip connections, which may result in redundant computation and reduced efficiency. To improve efficiency and construct a lightweight network architecture, our previous framework CSPNet (Cross Stage Partial Network) [30] adopts a split–transform–merge strategy, which divides the feature map of the base layer into two parts. This strategy balances the computational workload of feature extraction, reduces memory traffic, and avoids unnecessary energy consumption. The separation mechanism introduced by CSPNet can also be applied to other network architectures, such as ResNeXt [31] and DenseNet [32]. Inspired by CSPNet, MSPNet (Mixed Stage Partial Network) [33] further improves computational efficiency by recursively separating each feature map into two parts to compress the feature representations. Because only half of the feature data is processed in each layer, computational bottlenecks are significantly reduced, resulting in improved efficiency.

Attention model

Many studies have demonstrated that different learning mechanisms can enhance the feature representation capability of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to better capture spatial correlations. Among these approaches, attention mechanisms have proven effective in various tasks, including sequence learning [34], localization and understanding of images [35], and image captioning [36]. Hu et al. [37] proposed Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SENet) to enhance CNN performance by modeling interdependencies among feature channels. The proposed mechanism learns global channel-wise attention to recalibrate feature responses, thereby emphasizing informative channel features while suppressing less relevant ones. Furthermore, the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) [38] is a simple yet effective attention module that sequentially infers attention maps along both the channel and spatial dimensions. These attention maps are then applied to the input feature map through element-wise multiplication, enabling adaptive feature refinement and improved feature representation.

The Transformer [5] is an attention-based architecture originally proposed for machine translation and has recently demonstrated promising performance in various computer vision tasks [39, 40]. For example, the Vision Transformer (ViT) [41] treats image patches as tokens and applies self-attention mechanisms to learn global feature representations. Compared with traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs), such as ResNet [7], ViT can capture long-range dependencies more effectively and extract more informative features.

ViT has achieved competitive performance and, in some cases, surpasses CNN-based approaches [20, 27] in several vision tasks, including object detection [39] and image segmentation [40]. In ViT, the input image is divided into a set of non-overlapping patches, which are then processed as tokens by the transformer to compute self-attention and generate discriminative feature representations. However, the computational cost of global self-attention increases significantly with image resolution, making ViT relatively expensive for high-resolution inputs. To address this limitation, the Swin Transformer [42] introduces a hierarchical architecture with shifted window-based self-attention, enabling local patch interactions and significantly improving the speed–accuracy trade-off in image classification and other vision tasks.

AoAC grade calculation

As reported in [1], AoAC was present in 1.9% of men and 2.6% of women and was independently associated with older age, lack of college education, current smoking, and hypertension in both sexes. Based on chest X-ray images, AoAC scores (AoACs) are typically graded as follows: grade 0 (no visible calcification), grade 1 (small spots of calcification or a single thin calcification of the aortic knob), grade 2 (one or more areas of thick calcification), and grade 3 (circular calcification of the aortic knob) [43].

The AoAC score can be measured using several imaging modalities, including electron beam computed tomography (EBCT) [2, 44], multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) [45], echocardiography, vascular ultrasound, and plain radiography. Among these methods, EBCT and MDCT are considered the gold standards for quantifying coronary or aortic calcification [46]. However, these techniques are relatively expensive, expose patients to higher radiation doses, and may increase the risk of radiation-related complications.

In contrast, chest radiography (CXR) is inexpensive, widely available, and involves relatively low radiation exposure. Therefore, it is frequently used in hemodialysis (HD) patients for routine cardiac examinations. The validity and usefulness of AoAC grading based on CXR have been investigated in several studies [47]. For instance, a previous study [39, 47] demonstrated that the presence and progression of AoAC detected on chest radiography were independently associated with mortality in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD). In another study [40], a manual evaluation method was proposed to quantify AoAC using chest radiographs. The aortic arch region was manually divided into 16 circumferential sectors, and the number of sectors containing calcification was divided by 16 and multiplied by 100 to obtain the AoAC score (AoACS). This scoring method was also adopted in [39] to assess the prevalence and risk factors of CXR-detected AoAC in patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis [48]. However, the above-mentioned methods rely heavily on manual assessment by clinicians, which requires considerable time and effort. Therefore, it is important to develop an automatic and practical method to quantify AoAC scores using deep learning techniques.

Methods

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) and Ethics Committee of Shin Kong Wu Ho-Su Memorial Hospital (Approval No. 20220118R). In this study, we propose a CNN-based framework to develop an automatic AoAC scoring system. Figure 2 illustrates the overall architecture of the proposed method.

The AoAC classification network architecture consists of two main modules. First, PRB-FPN [4] is employed to detect the location of the aortic arch. After the region of interest is identified, the proposed Multi-Attention with Transformer Model (MATM) is used to extract discriminative features from the detected region. Finally, the extracted features are embedded through two fully connected layers with ReLU activation to perform AoAC grade classification. Figure 9 illustrates the detailed architecture of the MATM module

The input data consists of chest X-ray images of patients to be analyzed. Since the aortic arch region occupies only a small portion of the entire image, we first employ our previously proposed framework, PRB-Net [4], to detect the location of the aortic arch, as it has demonstrated strong performance in detecting small objects. After the aortic arch region is localized, feature extraction and classification modules are applied to extract discriminative features from the detected bounding box and subsequently compute the AoAC score. The details of each module are described in the following sections.

PRB-net for aortic arch detection

The aortic arch region occupies only a small portion of a chest X-ray image. Our previously proposed framework, PRB-Net, was specifically designed for small-object detection; therefore, it is adopted in this study to detect the location of the aortic arch. The detailed architecture of PRB-Net is illustrated in Fig. 3. Feature Pyramid Networks (FPNs) have been widely used in recent state-of-the-art (SoTA) object detectors. However, conventional FPN structures may struggle to simultaneously maintain accurate detection of both small and large objects. In particular, the top-down pathway may not preserve precise localization information, and repeated pooling operations can degrade spatial resolution, leading to missed detections of small objects. To address these limitations, PRB-Net employs a parallel feature pyramid structure with bidirectional (top-down and bottom-up) feature fusion, along with several additional improvements. This design helps preserve high-quality feature representations and enables more accurate detection of both small and large objects.

PPS (Patch-per-Sector) representation for AoAC scoring

Once the region of interest (ROI) of the aortic arch is detected, this study proposes a Patch-per-Sector (PPS) representation to extract informative features from this region for AoAC scoring. Figure 4 illustrates the detection results of the aortic arch using PRB-Net. To obtain the PPS representation, the boundaries of the aortic arch region must be accurately identified. First, the Canny edge detector [50] is applied to detect potential edge structures in the image. Next, a circle-fitting approach based on the Hough transform [51] is used to identify the circle that best approximates the aortic arch region. As shown in Fig. 5, panel (b) presents the edge map obtained from panel (a) using the Canny edge detector [50]. Subsequently, the Hough transform [51] is applied to determine the optimal circle that fits the aortic arch region, as illustrated in Fig. 5(c).

Among these sectors, only the 16 sectors located between the two concentric circles are selected to construct the Patch-per-Sector (PPS) representation, as shown in Fig. 7. Multi-attention with transformer model

In [49], a manual evaluation method was proposed in which the aortic arch in chest radiographs was divided into 16 circumferential sectors. The number of sectors containing calcification was then divided by 16 and multiplied by 100 to obtain the AoAC score (AoACS). In section “PPS (Patch-per-Sector) representation for AoAC scoring” 16 patches are automatically extracted from the detected aortic arch region to form a PPS representation. In recent years, CNNs have become widely used for extracting deep features for object classification and have demonstrated significantly better performance than traditional hand-crafted features. In the proposed PPS representation, each patch together with its positional information is embedded and then fed into a CNN backbone to extract discriminative features for AoAC scoring. Subsequently, as illustrated in Fig. 9, we propose a novel Multi-Attention with Transformer Model (MATM) to enhance important features and significantly improve the accuracy of AoAC grade classification. The details of the proposed attention mechanism are described below.

To mitigate the overfitting problem, data augmentation techniques, including image rotation and intensity adjustment, were applied, increasing the number of training samples to 13,500 images. The dataset was then divided into training, validation, and test sets with a ratio of 8:1:1. A larger proportion of the data was allocated to the training set to provide sufficient samples for model learning and to improve generalization performance. In this study, efficiency refers to the processing speed of the proposed model, which is measured using Frames Per Second (FPS).

For PRB-Net training, the input image size was set to 416 × 416. We used stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with momentum (momentum = 0.949, weight decay = 5 × 10⁻⁴). The batch size was 64 with subdivisions = 32. The initial learning rate was 1 × 10⁻³, with a burn-in period of 1000 iterations. The model was trained for 8000 iterations (max_batches = 8000) using a step learning rate schedule (steps = 6400, 7200; scales = 0.1, 0.1).

For MATM training, input images were resized to 256 × 256 and normalized using mean = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and standard deviation = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]. The model was trained for 300 epochs with a batch size of 150 using the Adam optimizer (learning rate = 1 × 10⁻⁴, betas = (0.9, 0.999), eps = 1 × 10⁻⁸, weight decay = 2 × 10⁻⁵). A StepLR learning rate scheduler was applied (step_size = 100, gamma = 0.1). The cross-entropy loss function was used during training.

To obtain the proposed PPS representation, the Canny edge detector [50] was first applied to detect possible edges in the aortic arch region. Subsequently, a circle-fitting method based on the Hough transform [51] was used to determine the circle that best fits the aortic arch region. Figure 5(c) shows several examples of the fitted circles for aortic arch localization. Because the size of the aortic arch region varies across patients, the detected region was normalized and cropped into a square region of size 256 × 256, based on the center and radius of the fitted circle, to ensure consistent input for subsequent processing.

To evaluate the impact of different backbone architectures on classification performance, five backbone networks were adopted in this study: ResNet18 [7], ResNet34 [7], ResNet50 [7], ResNet101 [7], and CSPResNeXt50 [21]. Table 4 presents experimental comparisons among different backbone networks with and without the proposed attention modules for the four-grade AoAC classification task. In general, deeper backbone networks can extract more informative features for AoAC scoring; however, this typically comes at the cost of reduced computational efficiency. The experimental results show that shallower backbone networks achieve lower classification accuracy, mainly because their limited number of layers restricts the ability to extract sufficiently discriminative features. However, these shallow networks generally provide higher computational efficiency. Furthermore, incorporating the proposed attention modules effectively improves classification accuracy. This improvement is particularly noticeable when using shallower backbone networks, where the attention mechanisms help compensate for the limited feature extraction capability. Among the evaluated models, ResNet101 achieves the highest classification accuracy, but it also has the lowest computational efficiency due to its deeper architecture. In contrast, CSPResNeXt50, which is a lightweight backbone, achieves an accuracy comparable to that of ResNet101 while providing significantly higher efficiency.

Table 9 compares the performance of the proposed MATM framework with several baseline methods for both four-grade and 16-grade AoAC classification tasks. The proposed MATM utilizes the Patch-per-Sector (PPS) representation, which divides the aortic arch region in the chest X-ray image into 16 sectors and treats each sector as a token to facilitate more accurate AoAC grading. The baseline methods used for comparison employ CNN backbones for feature extraction but do not incorporate the PPS representation. Instead, the extracted features are classified using a conventional machine learning approach, namely support vector machines (SVM) [52], for AoAC grading. The experimental results show that CNN + SVM performs worse than CNN + MLP (Multi-layer Perceptron) classifiers, regardless of whether the PPS representation is used. Without the PPS representation, these baseline approaches achieve classification accuracies below 30% for both the four-grade and 16-grade AoAC classification tasks. In contrast, by incorporating the PPS representation and the proposed MATM framework, the classification accuracy can be significantly improved, reaching up to 95.5%. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in capturing the spatial distribution of calcification patterns within the aortic arch region.

Conclusion

Aortic arch calcification (AoAC) is a cardiovascular condition that commonly occurs in elderly individuals. In this study, we propose a transformer-based Multi-Attention with Transformer Model (MATM) to classify AoAC grades from chest X-ray images. The proposed framework integrates multiple attention modules to enhance subtle calcification features, enabling more accurate AoAC grade classification compared with conventional backbone-based feature extraction methods. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model can effectively improve the accuracy and reliability of AoAC grading. By assisting clinicians in interpreting chest X-ray images, the proposed method has the potential to reduce interpretation time and improve diagnostic consistency, thereby supporting more efficient clinical decision-making. Currently, AoAC grading is primarily performed through manual interpretation by physicians, and the results may vary depending on the clinician’s experience and the imaging conditions. With the advancement of artificial intelligence-assisted medical imaging, automated AoAC assessment systems may help reduce inter-observer variability and improve the standardization of clinical evaluations in the future.

Nerves of generalized multicategories
Soichiro Fujii, Stephen Lack

Abstract
. For any category ℰ \mathcal{E} and monad T T thereon, we introduce the notion of T T -simplicial object in ℰ \mathcal{E} . Any T T -category in the sense of Burroni induces a T T -simplicial object as its nerve. This nerve construction defines a fully faithful functor from the category 𝐂𝐚𝐭 T ​ ( ℰ ) \mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E}) of T T -categories to the category s T ​ ℰ s_{T}\mathcal{E} of T T -simplicial objects, whose essential image is characterized by a simple condition. We show that the category s T ​ ℰ s_{T}\mathcal{E} is enriched over the category of simplicial sets, and that this induces the usual 2-category structure on 𝐂𝐚𝐭 T ​ ( ℰ ) \mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E}) . We also study enriched limits and colimits in s T ​ ℰ s_{T}\mathcal{E} and 𝐂𝐚𝐭 T ​ ( ℰ ) \mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E}) , and show that if ℰ \mathcal{E} is locally finitely presentable and T T is finitary, then 𝐂𝐚𝐭 T ​ ( ℰ ) \mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E}) is locally finitely presentable as a 2-category and s T ​ ℰ s_{T}\mathcal{E} is locally finitely presentable as a simplicially-enriched category.

1.
Introduction

The notion of
T
-category for a monad
T
is due to Burroni

[
6
]
, and has since been studied by many
authors under names including multicategory,
T
-multicategory, and
generalized multicategory, often under the further assumption that the
monad
T
is cartesian.

It includes as special cases many other important notions. If
T
is the identity monad on a category

\mathcal{E}
, then a
T
-category is just an internal category in
\mathcal{E}
. When

T
is the ultrafilter monad on
\mathbf{Set}
, a
T
-category is a
generalized notion of topological space
[
2
]
. When
T
is the free monoid monad
on
\mathbf{Set}
, a
T
-category is a multicategory in the sense of
Lambek
[
20
]
. When
T
is the free category monad on the category
\mathbf{Gph}
of
graphs, a
T
-category is what was called a multicatégorie in
[
6
]
and an fc-multicategory in

[
22
,
21
]
, but is now usually called a virtual double
category
[
7
]
.

As well as being studied in their own right,
T
-categories have been
used in relation to questions of coherence

[
13
,
14
]
and as a base for
enrichment
[
22
,
21
]
; it is
the relation to enrichment which led to our interest
[
11
]
in the notion.
There are also applications to universal algebra; among other things,
a one-object multicategory is the same as a (non-symmetric) operad. The introduction to
[
7
]
contains a good overview to the many ways in which
T
-categories have been used.

While many aspects of
T
-categories have been heavily studied,
relatively little attention has been given to the category (in fact a
2-category) of all
T
-categories for a given
T
(and
\mathcal{E}
). This is
the first goal of the paper. The second goal, which is also helpful in
relation to the first, is to introduce and study a nerve construction
for
T
-categories. Each (small) category has an associated simplicial
set called its nerve, and there is a corresponding construction for
internal categories, taking a category in
\mathcal{E}
to a simplicial object
in
\mathcal{E}
, giving a fully faithful embedding of categories in
simplicial objects. If instead we start with a
T
-category, we show how to define its nerve, which is a new structure that we call a

T
-simplicial object (in
\mathcal{E}
). Every such
T
-simplicial object
determines an actual simplicial object in the Kleisli category
\mathcal{E}_{T}

of
\mathcal{E}
, but the
T
-simplicial object retains more information about
the
T
-category; furthermore, if we wish to retain the fully faithful
nature of the usual nerve construction, the simplicial maps in
\mathcal{E}_{T}

are not the right notion of morphism. With the natural notion of
morphism of
T
-simplicial objects we do obtain a fully faithful nerve
construction. If
T
is an identity monad, then a
T
-simplicial object is just a simplicial object, and the nerve construction is the usual one for internal categories.

We study
T
-categories without assuming that
T
is cartesian, but we
do in some places suppose that
\mathcal{E}
is locally finitely presentable (lfp)
and that
T
is finitary; in this introduction, we shall refer to this
as the “lfp case”. (All results obtained in this lfp case generalize easily to the case of locally
\alpha
-presentable categories and monads of rank
\alpha
.)

We begin in Section
2
by reviewing the definition of

T
-category, and showing in Theorem
2.11
that in the lfp case the category

\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})
of
T
-categories is locally finitely presentable.

In Section
3
, we introduce our nerve construction for
T
-categories, giving a fully faithful embedding
\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})\to s_{T}\mathcal{E}
of the category
of
T
-categories in the category of
T
-simplicial objects, and we characterize the image in terms of a “nerve” or “Segal”
condition. We also show in Theorem
3.14
that in the lfp case the category
s_{T}\mathcal{E}
is
locally finitely presentable.

In Section
4
, we prove a comonadicity result.
The category
s\mathbf{Set}
of simplicial sets is of course a presheaf
category, while more generally the category
s\mathcal{E}
of simplicial
objects in
\mathcal{E}
is a functor category. This is generally not the case for

s_{T}\mathcal{E}
when
T
is a non-identity monad, but we show in
Theorem
4.3
that
s_{T}\mathcal{E}
is comonadic over a functor
category provided that
\mathcal{E}
has finite products. In fact

s_{T}\mathcal{E}
can also be seen as a category of
enriched functors
: see Remark
5.9
.

The remainder of the paper involves enrichment in various ways.

In Section
5
, we show that
s_{T}\mathcal{E}

can be enriched over simplicial sets, generalizing the classical fact
that the category
s\mathcal{E}
of simplicial objects in
\mathcal{E}
can be so
enriched. We write
\underline{s_{T}\mathcal{E}}
for the resulting

s\mathbf{Set}
-category.

Then in Section
6
we shift our attention to

\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})
. Since this is a full subcategory of
s_{T}\mathcal{E}
, it inherits
the simplicial enrichment of
\underline{s_{T}\mathcal{E}}
, but we show that
the
s\mathbf{Set}
-valued homs of this induced structure on
\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})
are
in fact the nerves of categories, and so obtain a 2-category structure

\underline{\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})}
on
\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})
.

Turning to Section
7
, we show in Theorem
7.11
that, whenever
\mathcal{E}
has pullbacks,
\underline{s_{T}\mathcal{E}}
has
powers by the representable simplicial set
\Delta[1]
, and so deduce in
Theorem
7.13
that the
2-category
\underline{\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})}
has powers by the arrow category
\mathbf{2}
.

Finally in Section
8
we study local
presentability of the enriched categories in the lfp case, proving in
Theorem
8.7
that
\underline{s_{T}\mathcal{E}}
is locally finitely presentable as an
s\mathbf{Set}
-category,
and in Theorem
8.8
that
\underline{\mathbf{Cat}_{T}(\mathcal{E})}
is locally finitely presentable as a 2-category.

Appendix
A
contains proofs of certain general facts about locally finitely presentable categories used in this paper.

Remark 2.3
.

Strictly speaking, the above definition of
T
-category leaves ambiguous the criterion for equality between two

-categories. For instance, it is unclear whether the pullback diagram defining

should be regarded as part of the structure.
While such questions are not mathematically significant, we will give our “official” definition of the category

later (Definition

T

— according to this latter definition, the pullback diagrams defining

and

-category.
In fact, Definition

will be given under completely general assumptions, without even requiring the base category

Such base categories naturally occur as, e.g., the Kleisli category

8.3.

Theorem 8.7
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is locally finitely presentable and

is locally finitely presentable as a simplicially enriched

Proof.

We saw in Theorem

that the ordinary category

is locally finitely presentable.
We saw in Proposition

that the enriched category
\underline{s_{T}\mathcal{E}}
has copowers.
We saw in Proposition

that
\underline{s_{T}\mathcal{E}}
has powers by simplicial sets of the form

.

Theorem 8.8

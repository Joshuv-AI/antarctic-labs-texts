Nonlocal operators in divergence form and existence theory for integrable data
David Arcoya, Serena Dipierro, Edoardo Proietti Lippi, Caterina Sportelli and Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. We present an existence and uniqueness result for weak solutions of Dirichlet boundary value problems governed by a nonlocal operator in divergence form and in the presence of a datum which is assumed to belong only to L 1 ​ ( Ω ) L^{1}(\Omega) and to be suitably dominated. We also prove that the solution that we find converges, as s ↗ 1 s\nearrow 1 , to a solution of the local counterpart problem, recovering the classical result as a limit case. This requires some nontrivial customized uniform estimates and representation formulas, given that the datum is only in L 1 ​ ( Ω ) L^{1}(\Omega) and therefore the usual regularity theory cannot be leveraged to our benefit in this framework. The limit process uses a nonlocal operator, obtained as an affine transformation of a homogeneous kernel, which recovers, in the limit as s ↗ 1 s\nearrow 1 , every classical operator in divergence form.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Nonlocal existence theory for integrable data

A long-established line of research focuses on the construction of solutions for suitable elliptic equations. In the traditional setting, the data of the equation are typically required to belong to some
L^{p}
-space with
p>1
, as this allows for the application of regularity theory and standard functional analysis techniques.

When
p=1
, these methods are typically inaccessible. For instance, as is well known, regularity theory fails in the presence of merely integrable data (see e.g. Sections 4.1 and 5.1 in
[
17
]
). Consequently, no general existence theory for second-order elliptic equations is available in this context.

However, the results presented in
[
3
,
4
]
established an existence theory for equations
in divergence form under certain structural assumptions on the equation. These assumptions permit the implementation of a tailored uniform approximation, which produces the desired solution via a limit procedure.

One of the goals of this article is to extend this existence theory to the nonlocal setting.
To this end, we consider an interaction kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
such that

(1.1)

\frac{c\,\chi_{[0,\varrho)}\big(|x-z|\big)}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\leqslant K(x,z)\leqslant\frac{C}{|x-z|^{n+2s}},

for some
c
,
C\in(0,+\infty)
,
\varrho\in(0,+\infty]
and
s\in(0,1)
, where, as usual,
\chi_{[0,\varrho)}
denotes the characteristic function of
[0,\varrho)
, that is

\chi_{[0,\varrho)}\big(|x-z|\big):=\begin{cases}1&{\mbox{ if }}|x-z|\in[0,\varrho),\\
0&{\mbox{ otherwise.}}\end{cases}

To be compatible with the divergence structure, it is convenient to assume the symmetry
condition

(1.2)

K(x,z)=K(z,x)\ \mbox{ for any }x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

see e.g.
[
19
, Section 2.5]
and the references therein
for more information about nonlocal operators with a divergence structure.

The nonlocal operator associated with the interaction kernel
K
can thus be written in the poinwise form

(1.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{K}u(x)&:=\mbox{P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u(x)-u(z)\big)\,K(x,z)\,dz\\
&=\mbox{P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u(x)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\\
&:=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(u(x)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy,\end{split}

where P.V. stands for the principal value notation.

We observe that the kernel
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni y\mapsto K(x,x-y)
is not assumed to be even in
y
.
This lack of symmetry prevents, in principle, the pointwise calculation in (
1.3
) when
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, even for smooth functions. Thus, when
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, to make sense of the above setting it is customary
to assume an “almost symmetry” condition for the kernel with respect to the variable
y
.
Specifically, if one wishes to use the pointwise form of the operator in (
1.3

it comes in handy to assume that

1.2.
Back to the classical case (from
M
to
A
)

The nonlocal result obtained in Theorem
1.1

is also useful to shed new light on the classical case, recovering
it in the limit. This limit procedure is not standard and will require,
on the one hand, the selection of “the most natural” fractional operator
that recovers a given second-order elliptic operator in divergence form,
and, on the other hand, a number of ad-hoc inequalities allowing one to
implement this asymptotic technique.
In this sense, we believe that the methodology introduced here can be
advantageous in several situations, not only to recover known results
in partial differential equations as a limit case of nonlocal equations (which
has also the benefit of providing a unified scenario comprising both classical
and fractional problems), but also potentially to obtain new results in
partial differential equations by using techniques coming from the fractional world.
This approach has been used, for instance, in the regularity theory “by approximation” performed in
[
10
]
, in the asymptotic regularity theories of nonlocal minimal surfaces carried out in
[
11
,
8
,
12
]
,
as well as in the construction of nonlocal catenoids in
[
14
]
.

To implement our strategy, we recall that second-order operators in divergence form are typically written in the form

A natural question in this context is whether or not it

This plan of action has been presented in

However, our approach demands more accuracy on the assumptions needed to make this procedure work, to enable us to offer a unified view of local and nonlocal cases and to recover the local case starting from the nonlocal one through a limit process.

Interestingly, one can also modify the above strategy

This requires significant additional work, since,

.

2.
Existence theory and proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

2.1.
Functional analysis preliminaries

In this section we introduce the variational framework needed to deal with problems (
1.8
) and (
1.11
) and we provide some preliminary results which will be required to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

For
s\in(0,1)
, we let

(2.1)

[u]_{s}:=\left({c_{n,s}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

be the Sobolev-Gagliardo seminorm of a measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, see e.g.
[
15
]
.

The positive normalizing constant
c_{n,s}
is chosen in such a way that it provides consistent limits as
s\searrow 0
and
s\nearrow 1
, that is

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}[u]_{s}=[u]_{1}:=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\lim_{s\searrow 0}[u]_{s}=[u]_{0}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

As customary, we define the fractional Sobolev space

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):[u]_{s}<+\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left([u]^{2}_{s}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, given an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary, we set

H^{s}_{0}(\Omega):=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}

endowed with the equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)}=[\cdot]_{s}
.

It is also useful to observe that the norm induced by the kernel
K
is equivalent
to the fractional Sobolev-Gagliardo norm, as stated in the following result:

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists
C_{\star}>0
, depending only on
n
,
s
,
c
,
\varrho
and
\Omega
, such that, for all
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
,

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\leqslant C_{\star}\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz.

For convenience of the reader, the proof of this result is presented in Appendix
D
.

With the setting introduced above, the definition of weak solution of problem (
1.8
) reads as follows:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.8
) if, for any
v\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u(x)-u(z)\big)\big(v(x)-v(z)\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}a(x)h(u(x))v(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x)v(x)\,dx.

We now let
k\geqslant 0
and we consider the cutoff function
G_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

(2.2)

G_{k}(t):=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }|t|\leqslant k,\\
t-k&\mbox{ if }t>k,\\
t+k&\mbox{ if }t<-k.\end{cases}

The function
G_{k}
enjoys some useful properties, that we now recall, for the facility of the reader.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
k\geqslant 0
and
G_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as in (
2.2
). Then, for any
t
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

(2.3)

\displaystyle t\,G_{k}(t)=|t||G_{k}(t)|,

(2.4)

G_{k}(t)
is nondecreasing,

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle|G_{k}(t)-G_{k}(r)|\leqslant|t-r|.

Proof.

We can write (
2.2
) in a compact way as

G_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-k,k]}(x)\,dx.

From this, the property in (
2.3
) plainly follows.

Also, by inspection of all possible cases, one can check that (
2.4
) holds true.

Moreover,

|G_{k}(t)-G_{k}(r)|=\left|\int_{r}^{t}\chi_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-k,k]}(x)\,dx\right|\leqslant|t-r|,

which proves (
2.5
).
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
k\geqslant 0
and
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then,
G_{k}(u)
belongs to
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

From (
2.5
), we have that
|G_{k}(u)|\leqslant|u|
, and therefore
G_{k}(u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Moreover, by (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), we infer that

\begin{split}&[G_{k}(u)]^{2}_{s}=c_{n,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|G_{k}(u(x))-G_{k}(u(y))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\leqslant c_{n,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy=[u]^{2}_{s},\end{split}

and therefore
G_{k}(u)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Furthermore, in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
we have that
u=0
, thus
G_{k}(u)=0
. Gathering these
pieces of information, we obtain the desired claim.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is deduced from a minimization argument, whose details are as follows.

We set

H(t):=\int_{0}^{t}h(\tau)\,d\tau.

We consider the functional
J:H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty,-\infty\}
defined as

(2.6)

J(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ if }\eta(H\circ u)\in L^{1}(\Omega),\\
+\infty\qquad\mbox{ if }\eta(H\circ u)\notin L^{1}(\Omega).\end{cases}

We now check the following properties of
J
:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
. If
\eta(H\circ u)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, then
|J(u)|<+\infty
.

In particular,
J:H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

Proof.

If
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
, then from the upper bound in (
1.1
) we have that

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\leqslant C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz=\frac{C}{c_{n,s}}[u]^{2}_{s}<+\infty.

Moreover,

\left|\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx\right|\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)\,H(u(x))\,dx<+\infty

and, since
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, by the Hölder inequality,

(2.7)

\left|\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx\right|\leqslant\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<+\infty.

Combining these considerations with (
2.6
), we get desired result.
∎

We also observe that

(2.8)

critical points of the functional
J
are weak solutions of (
1.11
).

Lemma 2.6
.

The functional
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous with respect to the topology of
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
,
namely for every sequence
u_{k}\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
which converges weakly to some
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
as
k\to+\infty
we have that

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}J(u_{k})\geqslant J(u).

Proof.

To prove the desired result, we argue by contradiction and we suppose that
there exists a sequence
u_{k}\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
weakly converging to some
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
as
k\to+\infty
and such that

(2.9)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}J(u_{k})<J(u).

In particular,

(2.10)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}J(u_{k})<+\infty,

which, in tandem with Lemma
2.5
,
implies that
\eta(H\circ u_{k})\in L^{1}(\Omega)
for
k
sufficiently large.

By the compactness result in
[
15
, Theorem 7.1]
we have that
there exists a subsequence
u_{k_{j}}
strongly
converging to
u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and a.e. in
\Omega
. Therefore,
by Fatou’s Lemma,

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k_{j}}(x)-u_{k_{j}}(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\geqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz.

By (
1.5
), it follows that

(2.11)

H(t)\geqslant 0\quad\mbox{ for any }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Consequently, since
\eta\geqslant 0
and
H
is continuous, it follows from
Fatou’s Lemma that

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u_{k_{j}}(x))\,dx\geqslant\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx.

Furthermore, since
\zeta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the strong convergence of
u_{k_{j}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
implies that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u_{k_{j}}(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx.

Gathering these pieces of information and recalling the definition of
J
in (
2.6
),
we conclude that

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\lim_{j\to+\infty}J(u_{k_{j}})\\
&\geqslant\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k_{j}}(x)-u_{k_{j}}(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u_{k_{j}}(x))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u_{k_{j}}(x)\,dx\\
&\geqslant\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Hence, recalling (
2.7
),

\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx+\lim_{j\to+\infty}J(u_{k_{j}})\leqslant\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lim_{j\to+\infty}J(u_{k_{j}}).

We stress that the latter quantity is finite, owing to (
2.10
), and therefore
\eta(H\circ u)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

As a result, by Lemma
2.5
,

J(u)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx.

From this, (
2.9
) and (
2.12
) we obtain that

\displaystyle J(u)>\lim_{j\to+\infty}J(u_{k_{j}})

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx=J(u).

This is a contradiction establishing the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

The functional
J
is coercive, namely

\lim_{[u]_{s}\to+\infty}\frac{J(u)}{[u]_{s}}=+\infty.

Proof.

We can suppose that
H\circ u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
From Lemma
2.1
and (
2.11
),

(2.13)

\begin{split}J(u)&=\frac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u(x))\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx\\
&\geqslant\frac{\widetilde{C}}{4}[u]_{s}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)H(u)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\zeta(x)u(x)\,dx\\
&\geqslant\frac{\tilde{C}}{4}[u]_{s}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}|\zeta(x)||u(x)|\,dx,\end{split}

for some
\widetilde{C}>0
.

Now, by the Hölder inequality and
[
15
, Theorem 6.7]
, there exists a constant
\widehat{C}>0
, depending only on
n
,
s
and
\Omega
, such that

\int_{\Omega}|\zeta(x)||u(x)|\,dx\leqslant\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\widehat{C}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}[u]_{s}.

From this and (
2.13
), we deduce that

J(u)\geqslant\frac{\widetilde{C}}{4}[u]_{s}^{2}-\widehat{C}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}[u]_{s}.

Hence,

\lim_{[u]_{s}\to+\infty}\frac{J(u)}{[u]_{s}}\geqslant\lim_{[u]_{s}\to+\infty}\left(\frac{\widetilde{C}}{4}[u]_{s}-\widehat{C}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)=+\infty,

as desired.
∎

With the work done so far, the proof of Theorem
1.2
can be completed as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous (thanks to Lemma
2.6
)
and
coercive (thanks to Lemma
2.7
)
and the space
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is reflexive, by
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exists a minimizer for the functional
J
,
which is a weak solution of problem (
1.11
) (recall (
2.8
)).
∎

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now in the position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

(2.14)

f_{j}(x):=\dfrac{f(x)}{1+\frac{|f(x)|}{j}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad a_{j}(x):=\dfrac{a(x)}{1+\frac{Q\,a(x)}{j}}.

We observe that
f_{j}
,
a_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and

(2.15)

\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant j\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|a_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant\frac{j}{Q}.

In addition, setting

\psi_{j}(t):=\frac{jt}{j+t},

one can check that
\psi_{j}
is nondecreasing, and therefore from (
1.7
) we deduce that

(2.16)

|f_{j}(x)|=\dfrac{|f(x)|}{1+\frac{|f(x)|}{j}}=\psi_{j}(|f(x)|)\leqslant\psi_{j}(Qa(x))=\dfrac{Qa(x)}{1+\frac{Qa(x)}{j}}=Qa_{j}(x).

We now consider the approximated problems

(2.17)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{K}u+a_{j}\,h(u)=f_{j}&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

and we observe that we are in the setting of Theorem
1.2
with
\eta:=a_{j}
and
\zeta:=f_{j}
.
Consequently, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a weak solution
u_{j}
of (
2.17
),
that is, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
u_{j}\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
such that, for any
v\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(2.18)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big)\big(v(x)-v(z)\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)h(u_{j}(x))v(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\Omega}f_{j}(x)v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Now, in light of the assumptions on
h
in (
1.5
), we have that the
h
is invertible in
(-\gamma,\gamma)
. Hence, denoted by
h^{-1}
this inverse and recalling that
Q\in(0,\gamma)
(see (
1.7
)), we have

k:=h^{-1}(Q)>0.

Recalling the definition in (
2.2
) and exploiting the assumptions on
h
in (
1.5
),

(2.19)

G_{k}(u_{j}):=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }|h(u_{j})|\leqslant Q,\\
u_{j}-k&\mbox{ if }h(u_{j})>Q,\\
u_{j}+k&\mbox{ if }h(u_{j})<-Q.\end{cases}

Moreover, by Proposition
2.4
, we can use
G_{k}(u_{j})
as a test function in (
2.18
),
to obtain that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big)\big(G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)h(u_{j}(x))G_{k}(u_{j}(x))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{j}(x)G_{k}(u_{j}(x))\,dx.\end{split}

We also observe that, thanks to (
1.5
) and (
2.3
),

h(t)G_{k}(t)=|h(t)G_{k}(t)|\quad\mbox{ for any }t\in\mathbb{R},

and therefore

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big)\big(G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)|h(u_{j}(x))|\,|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))|\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{j}(x)G_{k}(u_{j}(x))\,dx.\end{split}

From this and (
2.16
), we thus deduce that

(2.20)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big)\big(G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)|h(u_{j}(x))|\,|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))|\,dx\leqslant Q\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)\,|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))|\,dx.\end{split}

Moreover, from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we infer that

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
\qquad&\leqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big|\big|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big|K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
\qquad&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big)\big(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz.\end{split}

Combining this with (

), we get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)\big(|h(u_{j}(x))|-Q\big)|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))|\,dx\leqslant 0.

Thus, since
G_{k}(u_{j}(x))=0
if

)), we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))-G_{k}(u_{j}(z))\big|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega\cap\{|h(u_{j}(x))|>Q\}}a_{j}(x)\big(|h(u_{j}(x))|-Q\big)|G_{k}(u_{j}(x))|\,dx\leqslant 0.

This and Lemma

entail that
G_{k}(u_{j}(x))=0
for a.e.

, and therefore

(2.21)

\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant h^{-1}(Q).

We now claim that

(2.22)

\mbox{$u_{j}$ is bounded in\penalty\ $H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)$}.

Indeed, by (
2.14
) and (
2.21
), and being

\begin{split}&|a_{j}(x)h(u_{j}(x))u_{j}(x)|\leqslant a(x)h(\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant a(x)\,Q\,h^{-1}(Q)\\

Thus, by using

&\qquad\leqslant h^{-1}(Q)\Big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+Q\|a\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

This and Lemma

entail (
2.22
).

By (

), there exists
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle u_{j}\rightharpoonup u\quad\mbox{ in }H^{s}_{0}(\Omega),

Also, by (

\begin{split}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz&\leqslant\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
&\leqslant 2h^{-1}(Q)\Big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+Q\|a\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{split}

Now, we check that

)
according to Definition

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(z)\big)\big(v(x)-v(z)\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}a_{j}(x)h(u_{j}(x))v(x)\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}f_{j}(x)v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Hence, we employ the Dominated Convergence Theorem and

u
satisfies

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u(x)-u(z)\big)\big(v(x)-v(z)\big)K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\int_{\Omega}a(x)h(u(x))v(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x)v(x)\,dx,

as desired.

Furthermore, the estimates in (

) and (
1.10
)
follow from (
2.26
) and (
2.27
).

We now show that the solution that we found is unique. To this aim, we take
u_{1}
,
u_{2}\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
solutions of (

). We point out that
u_{1}-u_{2}
can be used
as a test function in the weak formulation of (
1.8
). In this way,
one obtains that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big((u_{1}-u_{2})(x)-(u_{1}-u_{2})(z)\big)^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}a(x)\big(h(u_{1}(x))-h(u_{2}(x))\big)(u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x))\,dx=0.

From the fact that
a\geqslant 0
, the assumptions on

in (

) and Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that
u_{1}=u_{2}
a.e. in
\Omega
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

It is tempting to use the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem to prove that problem (
1.11
) admits a weak solution.
While this approach has been previously used in
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, some care is needed to make the full argument work.
Indeed, given
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
s\in(0,1]
, one can define
S:L^{2}(\Omega)\to H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
as the solution operator
u=S(f)
of the problem

(2.29)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

By either classical or fractional regularity theory, it follows that
S
is linear and continuous.

Then, one could be tempted to introduce the operator
T:H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)\to H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
defined as

(2.30)

T(u):=S(\zeta-\eta\,h(u)).

With this choice,
T(u)
would be the solution of the problem (
2.29
) with
f:=\zeta-\eta\,h(u)
.

At a first glance, this seems a convenient setting for the application of the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem, up to showing that

T
is continuous, compact and leaves a suitable closed ball invariant.

However, even if
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\zeta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the assumptions on the function
h
are, in general, not strong enough to guarantee that
\zeta-\eta\,h(u)\in L^{2}(\Omega)

(the issues arising when
h
grows faster than linear at infinity:
for example, if
\zeta:=0
,
\eta:=1
,
h(t):=t^{3}
,
u(x):=|x|^{-\frac{1}{3}}
and
\Omega\ni 0
, then
\zeta-\eta\,h(u)\not\in L^{2}(\Omega)
). This caveat prevents one from defining the operator
T
and makes the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem unavailable in this situation (likely, a two-step approach would be needed in cases like this, based first on a truncation of the function
h
with a
strictly increasing and bounded function and on
a subsequent limit argument).

3.
Asymptotic behavior of nonlocal solutions and proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section we show that the unique solution of problem (
1.21
), obtained through Theorem
1.1
,
converges as
s\nearrow 1
to a function which is
the unique solution of problem (
1.22
).
The existence of this classical solution was obtained in
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
, but
our proof does not need to assume this result and can be also seen as a different approach to the classical
theory, which is now recovered as a byproduct of a limit case.
In addition, the stability of the solutions of Theorem
1.1
as
s\nearrow 1

is interesting in itself, since it relies on a series of uniform estimates which do not come
from the standard regularity theory.

For the sake of convenience, for any
s\in(0,1)
, we denote by
u_{s}\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
the unique weak solution of problem (
1.21
) and by
u_{1}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
the unique weak solution of problem (
1.22
).

Now we show that, given
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
, we can decrease the seminorm of
u
by performing a mollification procedure.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta(x)\,dx=1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let

(3.1)

\eta_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-n}\eta\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\qquad{\mbox{and }}\qquad u_{\varepsilon}(x):=(\eta_{\varepsilon}\ast u)(x).

Then,

[u_{\varepsilon}]_{s}\leqslant[u]_{s}.

Proof.

By definition, we have that
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\mbox{supp}(\eta_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{\varepsilon}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=1.

Hence, recalling the definition in (
2.1
) and using the Jensen inequality, we get

\begin{split}&[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}_{s}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\leqslant\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x-z)-u(y-z)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz\right)\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\quad=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz\right)=[u]^{2}_{s},\end{split}

as desired.
∎

We are now in the position to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We will show that
every sequence of solutions of the nonlocal problem (
1.21
) is precompact and possesses a subsequence
converging to the unique solution of
the classical problem (
1.22
), which in turn implies
that all sequences actually converge to the same classical solution, thus yielding the desired result.

We consider a sequence of weak solutions
u_{s_{j}}

of (
1.21
) with
s_{j}
approaching
1
as
j\to+\infty
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
s_{j}\in(1/2,1)
.

By (
1.9
) and (
1.10
), we have that

(3.2)

\|u_{s_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant h^{-1}(Q)

and

c_{n,s_{j}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{\varrho}(x)}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(z)\big)^{2}}{|M(z,x-z)(x-z)|^{n+2s_{j}}}\,dx\,dz\leqslant 2h^{-1}(Q)\,\big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+Q\|a\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big).

From this, the assumption (
1.14
) and Lemma
2.1
, we also see that

[u_{s_{j}}]_{s_{j}}^{2}\leqslant 2C_{\alpha,\beta,n}h^{-1}(Q)\,\big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+Q\|a\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big).

We now take
\overline{s}\in(1/2,1)
. By
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
, we thereby find that there exists a positive constant
C
, only depending on
n
and
\Omega
, such that, when
{s_{j}}\in(\overline{s},1)
,

(3.3)

[u_{s_{j}}]^{2}_{\overline{s}}\leqslant C[u_{s_{j}}]^{2}_{s_{j}}\leqslant 2C\,C_{\alpha,\beta,n}h^{-1}(Q)\,\big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+Q\|a\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\big)=:C_{1},

namely the sequence
u_{s_{j}}
is bounded in
H^{\overline{s}}_{0}(\Omega)
, uniformly in
j
.

Thus, we can extract a subsequence
s_{j_{k}}
for which
there exists
w\in H_{0}^{\overline{s}}(\Omega)
such that, as
k\to+\infty
,

\displaystyle u_{s_{j_{k}}}\rightharpoonup w

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }H^{\overline{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(3.4)

\displaystyle u_{s_{j_{k}}}\to w

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle u_{s_{j_{k}}}\to w

\displaystyle\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n}

and (see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 4.9]
) there exist
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

(3.6)

|u_{s_{j_{k}}}(x)|\leqslant g(x)

for any

and for a.e.

.

We will actually show below that
w
is the solution

of the classical problem (
1.22
).

By (
3.3
), (
3.5
) and Fatou’s Lemma, we conclude that

(3.7)

[w]^{2}_{\overline{s}}\leqslant C_{1}.

Moreover, we remark that (
3.2
) entails
that

(3.8)

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant h^{-1}(Q).

We now claim that

(3.9)

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant C_{1}.

To prove this, we define

w_{\varepsilon}(x):=(\eta_{\varepsilon}\ast w)(x),

being
\eta_{\varepsilon}
as in (
3.1
).
By
[
18
, Appendix C4, Theorem 6]
, we have that

(3.10)

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}w_{\varepsilon}(x)=w(x)\quad{\mbox{ for a.e. }}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Moreover, (
3.8
) gives that

(3.11)

\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant h^{-1}(Q).

Also, in light of Lemma

and (

[w_{\varepsilon}]^{2}_{\overline{s}}\leqslant[w]^{2}_{\overline{s}}\leqslant C_{1},

and therefore

Moreover, since

Combining this and (

From this, (

We now show that

), namely, for any

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{c_{n,{s_{j_{k}}}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{\varrho}}\frac{(u_{s_{j_{k}}}(x)-u_{s_{j_{k}}}(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2{s_{j_{k}}}}}\,dx\,dy

4.1.
The algebraic roadmap leading to Theorem

and proof of Lemma

We start by explicitly computing the following integral.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}>0
.
Then, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma^{2}_{k}\varphi^{2}_{k}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{n-1}=\dfrac{V_{\mathbb{E}^{n}}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}},

where

V_{\mathbb{E}^{n}}:=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n}}

denotes the volume of the
n
-dimensional hyperellipsoid with semiaxes
\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}
.

Proof.

Up to reordering indices, we may suppose that
i=1
.
We recall the system of spherical coordinates
(see e.g.
[
6
, equation (*) on page 65]
) given by

\begin{split}&\varphi_{1}=\cos x_{1},\\
&\varphi_{j}=\cos x_{j}\,\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}\sin x_{k},\qquad{\mbox{for all }}j\in\{2,\dots,n-2\},\\
&\varphi_{n-1}=\sin x_{n-1}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-2}\sin x_{k},\\
&\varphi_{n}=\cos x_{n-1}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-2}\sin x_{k},\end{split}

with
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2}\in[0,\pi]
and
x_{n-1}\in[0,2\pi]
.

The Jacobian determinant of this transformation
(see e.g.
[
6
, page 66]
) can be written as

J=\prod_{k=1}^{n-2}\sin^{k}x_{n-1-k}.

In addition, for any
j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, we set

(4.1)

\alpha_{j}:=\sqrt{\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}\sigma^{2}_{k}\varphi^{2}_{k}}{\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{j}\sin^{2}x_{k}}}.

Using the short notation
x^{(j)}:=(x_{j},\dots,x_{n-1})
, we point out that

(4.2)

\alpha_{j}
depends only on
x^{(j+1)}
.

In this way, we have that
\alpha_{1}
does not depend on
x_{1}
and consequently

(4.3)

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{I}}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{2}}{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma^{2}_{k}\varphi^{2}_{k}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{n-1}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi^{2}_{1}}{\left(\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{1}\varphi^{2}_{1}+\sum_{{k=2}}^{n}\sigma^{2}_{k}\,\varphi_{k}^{2}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\varphi}\\
&\qquad=\int_{[0,\pi]^{n-2}\times[0,2\pi]}\frac{\cos^{2}x_{1}}{\left(\sigma^{2}_{1}\cos^{2}x_{1}+\alpha_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}x_{1}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\;\prod_{k=1}^{n-2}\sin^{k}x_{n-1-k}\,dx^{(1)}.\end{split}

Now we claim that, for all
m\in\{1,\dots,n-2\}
,

(4.4)

{\mathcal{I}}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{m}{2}}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}\,\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{k}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-m}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)}\int_{[0,\pi]^{n-m-2}\times[0,2\pi]}\frac{1}{\alpha_{m}^{n-m}}\prod_{k=1}^{n-m-2}\sin^{k}x_{n-1-k}\,dx^{(m+1)}.

Indeed, by
[

, 3.642, n.1, page 403]

\begin{split}\int_{[0,\pi]}\frac{\cos^{2\nu-1}\tau\,\sin^{2\mu-1}\tau}{\left(b^{2}\cos^{2}\tau+a^{2}\sin^{2}\tau\right)^{\mu+\nu}}\,\,d\tau&=2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\cos^{2\nu-1}\tau\,\sin^{2\mu-1}\tau}{\left(b^{2}\cos^{2}\tau+a^{2}\sin^{2}\tau\right)^{\mu+\nu}}\,d\tau\\

By using this

) with

,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,2\pi]}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{n-1}\sin^{2}x_{n-1}+\sigma^{2}_{n}\cos^{2}x_{n-1}}\,dx_{n-1}

\displaystyle 2\int_{[0,\pi]}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{n-1}\sin^{2}x_{n-1}+\sigma^{2}_{n}\cos^{2}x_{n-1}}\,dx_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2{\pi}}{\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n}}.

Combining this and (

), the desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}>0
. Let
\overline{\lambda}:=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}
and, for any

(4.8)

\sigma_{i}:=\sqrt{\frac{\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}}{\lambda_{i}}}.

Let also

denote the volume of the

Then, for any

,

,

(4.9)

\frac{1}{V_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma^{2}_{k}\varphi^{2}_{k}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{n-1}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}.

In addition,

(4.10)

\dfrac{\displaystyle\left(\min_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\lambda_{j}\right)^{\frac{n}{2(n+2)}}}{\displaystyle\left(\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\lambda_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leqslant\sigma_{i}\leqslant\dfrac{\displaystyle\left(\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\lambda_{j}\right)^{\frac{n}{2(n+2)}}}{\displaystyle\left(\min_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\lambda_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Proof.

We set
\overline{\sigma}:=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n}
and we deduce from (

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}=\frac{\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{n}{2(n+2)}}}{\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}}.

As a consequence

\lambda_{i}=\frac{\overline{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{n+2}}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{i}\overline{\sigma}}.

From this and Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi_{i}^{2}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma^{2}_{k}\varphi^{2}_{k}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{n-1}=\frac{V_{\mathbb{E}^{n}}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}V_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{i}\overline{\sigma}}=\lambda_{i},

which is the desired identity in (
4.9
) when

.

If instead

) vanish,
due to the odd symmetry of the integrand in the

-th coordinate.

Moreover, that inequalities in (
4.10
) plainly follow from (
4.8
).
∎

We can now prove Lemma

:

Proof of Lemma

.

By (

Appendix A
Calculating
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}
in the pointwise sense

Here we show that, for
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, the principal value in definition (
1.3
) is not needed and the operator
\mathcal{L}_{K}u
is well-defined for smooth and compactly supported functions.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and assume that (
1.1
) holds.

Then, for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni y\longmapsto\big(u(x)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)

is in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|{\mathcal{L}}_{K}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant C\,C_{n}\,\|u\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{1-2s}\right)

where
C_{n}>0
depends only on
n
, and
C
is as in (
1.1
).

Proof.

We have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}|u(x)-u(x-y)|\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\leqslant 2C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+2s}}=\frac{C\,\widetilde{C}}{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for some
\widetilde{C}>0
depending only on
n
.

Moreover, since
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we infer that

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}|u(x)-u(x-y)|\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\leqslant C\,\widehat{C}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+2s-1}}=\frac{C\,\widehat{C}}{1-2s}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\end{split}

for some
\widehat{C}>0
depending only on
n
and possibly changing during the computation.
∎

Here we point out that
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}
can be computed pointwise on smooth functions,
provided that condition (
1.4
) is assumed.

Lemma A.2
.

If
s\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and condition (
1.4
) holds true, then the expression in (
1.3
)
is well-defined for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|{\mathcal{L}}_{K}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant C_{n}\,\|u\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\frac{C}{s(1-s)}+C_{\sharp}\right),

where
C_{n}>0
depends only on
n
,
C
is as in (
1.1
)
and
C_{\sharp}
denotes the left-hand side of (
1.4
).

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and observe that

(A.1)

\begin{split}&2\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(u(x)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\\
&\quad=\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(u(x)-u(x+y)\big)\,K(x,x+y)\,dy+\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(u(x)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\\
&\quad=\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(u(x)-u(x+y)\big)\,\Big(K(x,x+y)-K(x,x-y)\Big)\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy.\end{split}

Moreover, in view of (
1.1
), for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{split}\big|2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)\big|\,K(x,x-y)&\leqslant\frac{\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{2}\,|y|^{2}\,K(x,x-y)\\
&\leqslant\frac{C\,\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{2\,|y|^{n+2s-2}}.\end{split}

The latter expression belongs to
L^{1}(B_{1})
and thus

(A.2)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\\
&\quad=\int_{B_{1}}\big(2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\\
&\quad\in\left[-\frac{C_{n}\,C\,\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{1-s},\,\frac{C_{n}\,C\,\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{1-s}\right],\end{split}

for some
C_{n}>0
.

Furthermore,

\big(u(x)-u(x+y)\big)\,\Big(K(x,x+y)-K(x,x-y)\Big)\leqslant\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,|y|\,\Big(K(x,x+y)-K(x,x-y)\Big).

In view of (
1.4
), this expression is integrable over
y\in B_{1}
,
with

(A.3)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\big(u(x)-u(x+y)\big)\,\Big(K(x,x+y)-K(x,x-y)\Big)\,dy\\
&\quad=\int_{B_{1}}\big(u(x)-u(x+y)\big)\,\Big(K(x,x+y)-K(x,x-y)\Big)\,dy\\
&\quad\in\left[-C_{\sharp}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,C_{\sharp}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right].\end{split}

Also,

\big|u(x)-u(x-y)\big|\,K(x,x-y)\leqslant\frac{2C\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{|y|^{n+2s}},

which is integrable over
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}
, with

(A.4)

\left|\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\big(u(x)-u(x-y)\big)\,K(x,x-y)\,dy\right|\leqslant\frac{C_{n}\,C\,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{s},

up to renaming
C_{n}
.

The desired result now follows from (
A.1
),
(
A.2
), (
A.3
)
and (
A.4
).
∎

Appendix B
Bounds on nondegenerate matrices

We now discuss some bounds involving nondegenerate matrices.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\alpha\geqslant\beta>0
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}
be the collection of
matrices
L\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
,

L\xi\cdot\xi\in[\beta,\alpha].

Then,

(B.1)

\inf_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}}\frac{|L\xi|}{|\xi|}=\min_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}}\frac{|L\xi|}{|\xi|}>0

and

(B.2)

\sup_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}}\frac{|L\xi|}{|\xi|}=\max_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}}\frac{|L\xi|}{|\xi|}<+\infty.

Proof.

We focus on the proof of (
B.1
), since the proof of (
B.2
) is alike.

We remark that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}\times\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
is a closed and bounded subset of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n+n}
and therefore the following infimum is attained

\inf_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}}|L\xi|.

Hence, we can find
L_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\xi_{\star}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
such that

(B.3)

|L_{\star}\xi_{\star}|=\inf_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}}|L\xi|=\inf_{{L\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha,\beta}}\atop{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}}\frac{|L\xi|}{|\xi|}.

We also notice that

(B.4)

L_{\star}\xi_{\star}\neq 0,

otherwise
0=L_{\star}\xi_{\star}\cdot\xi_{\star}\geqslant\beta
, which is a contradiction.

The desired result in (
B.1
) now follows from (
B.3
) and (
B.4
).
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\varrho\in(0,+\infty]
and
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{\varrho}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and assume that there exist

such that, for any

,

,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(B.5)

\beta|\xi|^{2}\leqslant M(x-y,y)\xi\cdot\xi\leqslant\alpha|\xi|^{2}.

Then, there exist
0<c_{\alpha,\beta,n}\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta,n}<+\infty
, depending only
on
\alpha
,
\beta
and
n

for all

The following is a useful perturbative estimate
regarding the interaction kernel induced by a matrix.

Lemma B.3
.

There exists
C_{\star}>0
, depending only on
n
,
\alpha
and
\beta
, such that,
for all

,

for which
|L\xi|
,
|N\xi|\in[\beta,\alpha]

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
, we have that, for all

,

\left|\frac{1}{|Ly|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|Ny|^{n+2s}}\right|\leqslant\frac{C_{\star}\,\|L-N\|}{|y|^{n+2s}}.

Proof.

We claim that there exists
C_{\star}>0
, depending only on
n
,
\alpha
and
\beta
, such that,
for all
L
,
N\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
for which
|L\xi|
,
|N\xi|\in[\beta,\alpha]

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
, we have that

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\left|1-\frac{|Ly|^{n+2s}}{|Ny|^{n+2s}}\right|\leqslant C_{\star}\,\|L-N\|.

To check this, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
N
as above, we let

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\ni L\longmapsto\Phi(L):=1-\frac{|Ly|^{n+2s}}{|Ny|^{n+2s}}

and we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial L_{ij}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n+2s)\frac{|Ly|^{n+2s-1}}{|Ny|^{n+2s}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial L_{ij}}|Ly|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(n+2s)}{2}\frac{|Ly|^{n+2s-2}}{|Ny|^{n+2s}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial L_{ij}}|Ly|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(n+2s)}{2}\frac{|Ly|^{n+2s-2}}{|Ny|^{n+2s}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial L_{ij}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}L_{km}y_{m}\right)^{2}

Appendix C
Continuity of eigenvalues and eigenprojections

To prove our limit results,
we leverage some perturbation results for finite-dimensional linear operators.

For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we denote its eigenvalues by
\lambda_{1}(A)\leqslant\dots\leqslant\lambda_{n}(A)
.
We also use the standard matrix norm

\|A\|:=\sup_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n-1}}|A\xi|.

We recall that eigenvalues of symmetric matrices
satisfy a Lipschitz continuity, according to the following observation:

Lemma C.1
.

Given two symmetric matrices
A
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we have that, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

|\lambda_{i}(A)-\lambda_{i}(B)|\leqslant\|A-B\|.

Proof.

Let
C:=B-A
and note that
C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is symmetric. Hence, we can use formula (4.3.16) in Corollary 4.3.15 of
[
21
]
and find that

(C.1)

\lambda_{i}(A)+\lambda_{1}(C)\leqslant\lambda_{i}(A+C)\leqslant\lambda_{i}(A)+\lambda_{n}(C).

Also, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
we can find
v_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n-1}
such that
Cv_{j}=\lambda_{j}(C)v_{j}
and therefore

|\lambda_{j}(C)|=|\lambda_{j}(C)v_{j}|=|Cv_{j}|\leqslant\sup_{\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n-1}}|C\xi|=\|C\|=\|A-B\|.

This and (
C.1
) entail that

\lambda_{i}(A)-\|A-B\|\leqslant\lambda_{i}(B)\leqslant\lambda_{i}(A)+\|A-B\|,

which yields the desired result.
∎

The following is a restatement of a classical result about the Lipschitz continuity of eigenprojections
(see Lemma 3 in
[
13
]
; see also Lemma 4.3 in
[
24
]
and the references therein):

Lemma C.2
.

There exist
c
,
C\in(0,+\infty)
such that the following statement holds true.

Let

,

be symmetric matrices, with

Then, there exists an orthogonal matrix

such as

Appendix D
Norm equivalency and proof of Lemma
2.1

Here we give the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

We let

\Omega_{1}:=\bigcup_{x\in\Omega}B_{1}(x).

We decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into a family of nonoverlapping cubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with faces normal to the coordinate axes and sides of length
\ell:=\frac{\varrho}{8\sqrt{n}}
. We label these cubes in such a way that

(D.1)

\Omega_{1}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=0}^{N}Q_{j},

with
N\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
n
,
\varrho
and
\Omega
.

We also denote by
Q_{j}^{\star}
the union of
Q_{j}
and its adjacent cubes. In this way,
Q_{j}^{\star}
is a cube with the same center as
Q_{j}
and sides of length
3\ell
.

In this framework, we see that

(D.2)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\geqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{Q_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\\
&\quad=\sum_{{0\leqslant j\leqslant N}\atop{i\in\mathbb{N}}}\iint_{Q_{j}\times Q_{i}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\geqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{Q_{j}\times Q_{j}^{\star}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz.\end{split}

Now, if
x\in Q_{j}
and
z\in Q_{j}^{\star}
, we have
that
|x-z|\leqslant 3\sqrt{n}\ell<\varrho
and accordingly,
by (
1.1
), we have that

K(x,z)\geqslant\frac{c}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}.

This and (
D.2
) give that

(D.3)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz\geqslant c\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{Q_{j}\times Q_{j}^{\star}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz.

Moreover, by virtue of (
D.1
),

(D.4)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{Q_{j}\times\Omega_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\\
&\quad\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{{Q_{j}\times\Omega_{1}}\atop{\{|x-z|<\ell\}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{{Q_{j}\times\Omega_{1}}\atop{\{|x-z|\geqslant\ell\}}}\frac{\big(|u(x)|+|u(z)|\big)^{2}}{\ell^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\\
&\quad\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{{Q_{j}\times Q_{j}^{\star}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+\widetilde{C}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\end{split}

where we denote by
\widetilde{C}>0
an opportune constant, depending only on
n
,
s
,
\rho
and
\Omega
, and possibly varying from line to line.

Moreover,

(D.5)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\leqslant\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\atop{\{|x-z|\geqslant 1\}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\\
&\qquad\leqslant\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\atop{\{|x-z|\geqslant 1\}}}\frac{\big(|u(x)|+|u(z)|\big)^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\leqslant\widetilde{C}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.\end{split}

Now we claim that

(D.6)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\atop{\{|x-y|\leqslant\varrho\}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz.

To check this, we argue by contradiction and suppose that there exists a sequence of functions
u_{m}
such that
u_{m}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and

1=\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\geqslant m\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\atop{\{|x-y|\leqslant\varrho\}}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)-u_{m}(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz.

In particular, for each
j\in\{0,\dots,N\}
,

(D.7)

\frac{1}{m}\geqslant\iint_{{Q_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\atop{\{|x-y|\leqslant\varrho\}}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)-u_{m}(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz\geqslant\iint_{{Q_{j}\times Q_{j,\varrho}}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)-u_{m}(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz,

where

Q_{j,\varrho}:=\bigcup_{x\in Q_{j}}B_{\varrho}(x).

This gives that

\iint_{{Q_{j}\times Q_{j}}}\frac{|u_{m}(x)-u_{m}(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{j})}\leqslant\frac{1}{m}+1\leqslant 2

and therefore, by compactness
(see e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 7.1]
), up to a relabeled subsequence, we can suppose that

converges to some

in

and a.e. in
Q_{j}
, for all
j\in\{0,\dots,N\}
.

As a result,

(D.8)

However, it follows from (

) and Fatou’s Lemma that, for all
j\in\{0,\dots,N\}
,

\iint_{{Q_{j}\times Q_{j,\varrho}}}\frac{|u_{\infty}(x)-u_{\infty}(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz=0.

This entails that
u_{\infty}
is constant in each
Q_{j}
and actually in
Q_{j,\varrho}
.
As a result,
u_{\infty}
is constant, and therefore constantly equal to zero (since
u_{\infty}=0
outside
\Omega
). But this is in contradiction with (
D.8
) and the proof of (
D.6
) is complete.

Gathering (
D.3
), (
D.4
),
(
D.5
) and (

), we conclude that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+2\iint_{\Omega_{1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+2\iint_{\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad+2\iint_{(\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{1})}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+\widetilde{C}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+0

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N}\iint_{{Q_{j}\times Q_{j}^{\star}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz+\widetilde{C}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\widetilde{C}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}K(x,z)\,dx\,dz+\widetilde{C}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\atop{\{|x-y|\leqslant\varrho\}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(z)|^{2}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dz

∎

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt=\left|\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(v_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\leqslant\lim_{s\nearrow 1}C[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s}\,[\varphi]_{s}+C[v_{\varepsilon}]_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\leqslant\lim_{s\nearrow 1}C[v_{\varepsilon}]_{1}\,[\varphi]_{1}+C[v_{\varepsilon}]_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\leqslant C\varepsilon,

for some
C>0
, independent of
s
and
\varepsilon
and possibly varying from line to line.
Accordingly, the claim in (
E.3
) follows by sending
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Now we focus on the proof of (
1.19
)
and, thanks to (
E.3
),
we will assume that
u
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R_{0}})
, for some
R_{0}>0
.

We consider a mollification

of

and

due to (
1.14
).

We now let

be as in (

) and (thanks to

.

Furthermore, by

(E.6)

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{ij,\varepsilon}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\,dx,\end{split}

where

This and (

\displaystyle\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\left|\;\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\lim_{s\nearrow 1}C(1-s)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{|u(x)-u(x-y)|\,|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\lim_{s\nearrow 1}C(1-s)\left(\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{|u(x)-u(x-y)|^{2}}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(\,\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r/2})}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|^{2}}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\frac{C(1-s)}{s\,r^{2s}}

\displaystyle\quad=0,

for some
C>0
independent of

,
s
and
\varepsilon
.

As a consequence, we rewrite (

) as

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{r/2}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{ij,\varepsilon}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\,dx\end{split}

and therefore, in light of (

)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{r/2}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Using (
E.4
) and Lemma

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\frac{C_{\star}\,\big\|M(x-y,y)-M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)\big\|}{|y|^{n+2s}}.

Accordingly,
up to renaming constants we have that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{r/2}}|u(x)-u(x-y)|\,|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|\left|\frac{1}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}-\frac{1}{|M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\right|\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\leqslant C_{\star}\,(1-s)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{r/2}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x-y)|\,|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|\,\big\|M(x-y,y)-M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)\big\|}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt=C_{\star}\,(1-s)\iint_{B_{R_{0}+r}\times B_{r/2}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x-y)|\,|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|\,\big\|M(x-y,y)-M_{\varepsilon}(x-y,y)\big\|}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\leqslant C_{\star}\,(1-s)\,\|M-M_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}+2r}\times B_{r/2})}\,\iint_{B_{R_{0}+r}\times B_{r/2}}|y|^{2-n-2s}\;\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\leqslant C_{\star}\,\|M-M_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}+2r}\times B_{r/2})},

and this is infinitesimal as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
,
owing to (
E.5
).

From this and (
E.8
) we obtain (
1.19
), as desired.

Proposition E.1
.

Let
\overline{s}\in(0,1)
and
u\in H^{\overline{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\varrho\in(0,+\infty]
and
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{\varrho}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a matrix-valued function with measurable entries that satisfy (
1.14
) and (
1.15
).

If
\varrho=+\infty
, assume also that

(E.9)

\begin{split}&\mbox{for any $\psi\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}$, for any $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and for any $i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ there exists}\\
&M^{\infty}_{ij}(x,\psi):=\lim_{t\to+\infty}M_{ij}(x-t\psi,t\psi)<+\infty.\end{split}

Then, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(E.10)

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{\varrho}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)\varphi(x)\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{|M^{\infty}(x,\psi)\,\psi|^{n}}\,dx&{\mbox{ if }}\varrho=+\infty,\\
0&{\mbox{ if }}\varrho\in(0,+\infty).\end{cases}\end{split}

To establish Proposition
E.1
, we will make use of the following observations:

Lemma E.2
.

Let
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a measurable matrix-valued function
satisfying (
E.9
) for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
R>0
.

Then,

\lim_{s\searrow 0}c_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{|M(x-y,y)\,y|^{n+2s}}=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{|M^{\infty}(x,\psi)\,\psi|^{n}}.

Proof.

We observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{|M(x-y,y)\,y|^{n+2s}}=\int_{R}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\rho\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{\rho^{1+2s}|M(x-\rho\psi,\rho\psi)\,\psi|^{n+2s}}.

Now, by using the change of variables
r:=(\rho/R)^{2s}
, we see that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{|M(x-y,y)\,y|^{n+2s}}=\frac{1}{2sR^{2s}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{r^{2}|M(x-r^{\frac{1}{2s}}\psi,r^{\frac{1}{2s}}\psi)\,\psi|^{n+2s}}.

Thus, by (
1.14
), we get

\frac{1}{r^{2}|M(x-r^{\frac{1}{2s}}\psi,r^{\frac{1}{2s}}\psi)\,\psi|^{n+2s}}\leqslant\frac{1}{r^{2}c_{\alpha,\beta,n}^{n+2s}}\in L^{1}\Big((1,+\infty)\times\mathcal{S}^{n-1};dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}\Big)

Accordingly, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, (
E.1
) and (
E.9
), we deduce that

\begin{split}\lim_{s\searrow 0}c_{n,s}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dy}{|M(x-y,y)\,y|^{n+2s}}=\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2sR^{2s}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{r^{2}|M(x-r^{\frac{1}{2s}}\psi,r^{\frac{1}{2s}}\psi)\,\psi|^{n+2s}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{|M^{\infty}(x,\psi)\,\psi|^{n}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\psi}}{|M^{\infty}(x,\psi)\,\psi|^{n}},\end{split}

as desired.
∎

Lemma E.3
.

Let
\overline{s}\in(0,1)
and
u
,
\varphi\in H^{\overline{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Let
\varrho\in(0,+\infty]
and
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{\varrho}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a measurable matrix-valued function. Let
R\in(0,\varrho)
.

Then,

\lim_{s\searrow 0}c_{n,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{R}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy=0.

Proof.

Up to scaling, we can suppose that
R=1
.
By (
1.14
), we infer that

(E.11)

\begin{split}&\left|c_{n,s}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{1}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right|\\
&\qquad\leqslant\frac{c_{n,s}}{\beta^{n+2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x-y)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx.\end{split}

We set

g(s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x-y)||\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y)|}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx.

We notice that
g(s)
is nondecreasing and that
g(\overline{s})<+\infty
.
From this, we infer that

\lim_{s\searrow 0}g(s)\leqslant g(\overline{s}).

Using this information, (
E.1
) and (
E.11
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proof of Proposition
E.1
.

If
\varrho\in(0,+\infty)
, the desired limit follows from Lemma
E.3
. Hence, from now on, we suppose that
\varrho=+\infty
.

We observe that

(E.12)

\begin{split}&\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\quad=\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx\\
&\qquad+\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-y))}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx\\
&\quad=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

In light of Lemma
E.3
, we have that

(E.13)

\lim_{s\searrow 0}|I_{1}|=0.

We now focus on
I_{2}
and we claim that

(E.14)

\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u(x-y)\varphi(x)}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx=0.

Indeed, by (
1.14
) and the Hölder inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(x-y)||\varphi(x)|}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{n+2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\varphi(x)|\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|u(x-y)|}{|y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx\\
&\quad\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{n+2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\varphi(x)|\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}(x)}|u(z)|^{2}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2n+4s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx\\

From this and (

), we deduce that

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u(x-y)\varphi(x)}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx\\
&\quad\leqslant\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2\beta^{n+2s}}\left(\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n+4s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0.\end{split}

In the same way, by using the change of variables
z=x-y
, one can prove that

(E.15)

\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u(x)\varphi(x-y)}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx=0.

We now claim that

(E.16)

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\searrow 0}\frac{c_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u(x)\varphi(x)}{|M(x-y,y)y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)\,dx\\

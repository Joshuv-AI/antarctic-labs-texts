On the zero-noise limit for SDE’s singular at the initial time
Jules Pitcho

Abstract
. We investigate the zero-noise limit for SDE’s driven by Brownian motion with a divergence-free drift singular at the initial time and prove that a unique probability measure concentrated on the integral curves of the drift is selected. More precisely, we prove uniqueness of the zero-noise limit for divergence-free drifts in L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( ( 0 , T ] , B ​ V ​ ( 𝕋 d , ℝ d ) ) ∩ L q ​ ( ( 0 , T ) , L p ​ ( 𝕋 d , ℝ d ) ) L^{1}_{loc}((0,T];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{q}((0,T);L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})) where p p and q q satisfy a Prodi-Serrin condition. The vector field constructed by Depauw [ 12 ] lies in this class and we show that for almost every intial datum, the zero-noise limit selects a probability measure concentrated on several distinct integral curves of this vector field.

1.2.
Review of SDE theory

Consider the stochastic differential equation

1.3.
Disintegration of a measure

Let
X
and

be a separable metric spaces,
\mu

\nu
a positive measure on

and

of measures on

(i)

is concentrated on the level set

(ii)

(1.8)

\mu(A)=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}(A)d\nu(y).

Any family satisfying

We also have

(1.9)

\int_{X}\phi d\mu=\int_{Y}\Big[\int_{E_{y}}\phi d\mu_{y}\Big]d\nu(y)

We now give a useful fact.
Let

be Borel maps such that:

(P)

h_{\#}\mu=\nu
and for every

The following is true.

Lemma 1.4

In the context of this paragraph, if

is a disintegration of

Let

So

Let

1.5.
Our argument and organisation of this paper

The existence of a zero-noise flow follows by tightness of the family of laws
\{P_{X^{\nu}}\}_{\nu>0}
and we show that it must be concentrated on integral curves of the vector field. For the uniqueness part of our result, we first observe that a zero-noise flow of a divergence-free vector field must satisfy an incompressibility condition. For vector fields which are further assumed to be in

In Section

2.
Existence and uniqueness of the zero-noise flow

2.1.
Existence of a zero-noise flow

The following will serve as our existence result. We also gather useful properties of zero-noise flows for divergence-free vector fields.

Proposition 2.1
.

Consider a divergence-free Borel vector field
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p,q\in(1,+\infty]
satisfying (
1.5
). Assume that
b\in L^{q}_{p}(T)
. Then there exists a zero-noise flow
\eta
of
b
, which further satisfies

(i)

\eta
is concentrated on integral curves of

, i.e.

(2.1)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\eta(d\gamma)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T];

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
(e_{t})_{\#}\eta={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

Remark 2.2
.

In view of the above proposition, if there exists a unique
\eta
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
, then
\eta
is the unique zero-noise flow of
b
, and a subsubsequence argument shows that the whole family
\{P_{X^{\nu}}\}_{\nu>0}
of laws of

converges to

in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
as

Let us introduce the Gagliardo semi-norm. For
u:[0,T]\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
Borel, we define

(2.2)

[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}}:=\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|u(s)-u(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{1+\alpha p}}dsdt\Big)^{1/p}.

The fractional Sobolev spaces are then defined as

(2.3)

W^{\alpha,p}((0,T;\mathbb{T}^{d})):=\Big\{u\in L^{p}((0,T);\mathbb{T}^{d})\;:\;[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}}<+\infty\Big\},

and form Banach spaces under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha,p}}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}+[\cdot]_{W^{\alpha,p}}.

Let us introduce the Hölder semi-norm

(2.4)

[u]_{C^{\theta}}:=\sup_{s,t\in[0,T],s\neq t}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\theta}}.

The Hölder spaces are defined as

(2.5)

C^{\theta}((0,T);\mathbb{T}^{d}):=\Big\{u\in C^{0}((0,T);\mathbb{T}^{d})\;:\;[u]_{C^{\theta}}<+\infty\Big\},

and form Banach spaces under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\theta}}:=\|\cdot\|_{C^{0}}+[\cdot]_{C^{\theta}}.

The following continuous embeddings then hold

(2.6)

W^{\alpha,p}((0,T);\mathbb{T}^{d})\hookrightarrow C^{\theta}((0,T);\mathbb{T}^{d}),\qquad\text{for }\;\alpha\geq\theta+\frac{1}{p},\qquad\theta\geq 0,\;p\geq 1.

Proof.

Step 1.
(Compactness) Let us show that family of laws
\{P_{X^{\nu}}\}_{\nu>0}
is precompact in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
.
By Prokhorov’s theorem, it is enough to prove tightness of the family
\{P_{X^{\nu}}\}_{\nu>0}
.
By the Markov inequality, for every
R>0
, we have

(P\otimes{\mathscr{L}}^{d})\Big([X^{x,\nu}]_{W^{\alpha,p}}>R\Big)\leq\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[|X^{\nu}_{t}-X^{\nu}_{s}|^{p}]}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha p}}dsdt,

Now, for every

, we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|X^{\nu}_{t}-X^{\nu}_{s}|^{p}]&\leq C_{p}{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{s}^{t}|b(s,X^{x,\nu}_{s})|ds]^{p}+C_{p}\nu^{p}{\mathbb{E}}[|W_{t}-W_{s}|^{p}]\\

for some constant

which depends only on

is the Lebesgue measure on

, that

Therefore we have

(2.8)

(P\otimes{\mathscr{L}}^{d})\Big([X^{x,\nu}]_{W^{\alpha,p}}>R\Big)\leq\frac{C_{T,p,b}}{R}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|t-s|^{-1-(\alpha-1/2)p}dsdt,

This integral is finite for

. Now fix the parameter

By the Sobolev embedding (

By Ascoli’s theorem, the sets

.
This implies tightness of the family

(Incompressibility and concentration on integral curves) Let us prove

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\phi(\gamma(t))P_{X}(d\gamma)=\lim_{\nu_{n}\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\phi(\gamma(t))P_{X^{\nu_{n}}}(d\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi(x)dx.

As

Let us prove
(i)
. It suffices to show that

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|P(d\gamma)=0\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
c:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuous vector field such that
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds<\varepsilon
and
t\in[0,T]
. We then have

(2.10)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|P(d\gamma)\\
&\leq\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}c(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|P(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}c(s,\gamma(s))-b(s,\gamma(s))|P(d\gamma)\\
&\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}c(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|P^{\nu_{n}}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}c(s,\gamma(s))-b(s,\gamma(s))\Big|P(d\gamma)\\
&\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big|\nu_{n}W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(b(s,X^{\nu_{n}}_{s})-c(s,X^{\nu_{n}}_{s}))ds\Big|\Big]+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds\\
&\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{n}{\mathbb{E}}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|W_{t}|]+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds<2\varepsilon.\end{split}

As
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we have thus shown that any zero-noise flow must satisfy
(i)
and
(ii)
. Therefore, if there exists a unique probability measure
\eta
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
, a subsubsequence argument shows that the whole family of laws
\{P_{X^{\nu}}\}_{\nu>0}
converges narrowly to
\eta
as
\nu\downarrow 0
. This proves the thesis.
∎

2.2.
Uniqueness of the zero-noise flow

We will now prove uniqueness of the zero-noise flow.
The following theorem can be extracted from Ambrosio
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Consider a divergence-free vector field
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
b\in L^{1}((0,T);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Then there exists a unique probability measure
\eta
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
such that

(i)

\eta
is concentrated on integral curves of
b
, i.e.

(2.11)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\eta(d\gamma)=0\qquad\forall t\in[0,T];

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
(e_{t})_{\#}\eta={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

Furthermore, there exists a Borel family
\{\gamma_{y}\}
of
\Gamma_{T}
such that for every disintegration
\{\eta_{0,y}\}
of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, we have
\eta_{0,y}=\delta_{\gamma_{y}}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

In order to relate uniqueness of the zero-noise flow in our setting to
Theorem

2.3
, let us first introduce
\tau\in(0,T)
and define the maps

(2.12)

S^{\tau}:\Gamma_{T}\ni\gamma(\cdot)\longmapsto\gamma(\tau\vee\cdot)\in\Gamma_{T}\quad\text{ and }\quad B^{\tau}:\Gamma_{T}\ni\gamma(\cdot)\longmapsto\gamma(T-\tau\wedge\cdot)\in\Gamma_{T}.

Notice that (P) of
Section

1.3
is satisfies with
g=S^{\tau}
and
f=h=e_{T}
or with
g=B^{\tau}
and
f=h=e_{0}
.
Define also the vector field

(2.13)

b^{\tau}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}b(t,x)\qquad&\text{if $\tau>t$},\\
0\qquad&\text{if $t\leq\tau$.}\end{split}\right.

We now record the following elementary fact.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
. Then
(S^{\tau})_{\#}\mu
converges narrowly to
\mu
as
\tau\downarrow 0
and
(B^{\tau})_{\#}\mu
converges narrowly to
\mu
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\in C_{b}(\Gamma_{T})
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}
, we have
\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}S^{\tau}\gamma=\gamma
, which by continuity implies
\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\Phi(S^{\tau}\gamma)=\Phi(\gamma)
. We also clearly have

(2.14)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|\Phi(S^{\tau}\gamma)|\mu(d\gamma)\leq\|\Phi\|_{C^{0}}.

Therefore, by dominated convergence, it holds

(2.15)

\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Phi(\gamma)(S^{\tau})_{\#}\mu(d\gamma)=\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Phi(S^{\tau}\gamma)\mu(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Phi(\gamma)\mu(d\gamma).

The same argument shows that
(B^{\tau})_{\#}\mu
converges narrowly to
\mu
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.
∎

Let us record also the following consequence of Theorem
2.3
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Consider a divergence-free vector field
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
b\in L_{loc}^{1}([0,T);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then there exists a unique probability measure
\eta
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
such that

(i)

\eta
is concentrated on integral curves of
b
, i.e.

(2.16)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\eta(d\gamma)=0\qquad\forall t\in[0,T];

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
(e_{t})_{\#}\eta={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

Furthermore, there exists a Borel family
\{\gamma_{y}\}
of
\Gamma_{T}
such that for every disintegration
\{\eta_{0,y}\}
of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, we have
\eta_{0,y}=\delta_{\gamma_{y}}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Proof.

The existence of a probability measure
\eta
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
follows from Smirnov’s representation for normal 1-currents
[
23
]
or Ambrosio’s superposition principle
[
2
]
.

Let us now prove the last part of the statement. Let
\{\eta_{0,y}\}
be a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.
By
Lemma

1.4
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the family
\{(B^{1/k})_{\#}\eta_{0,y}\}
is a disintegration of
(B^{1/k})_{\#}\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.
One can check directly that
(B^{1/k})_{\#}\eta
satisfies
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
2.3
. Therefore, there exists a Borel family
\{\gamma_{y}^{1/k}\}
in
\Gamma_{T}
and a set
N_{k}
of vanishing Lebesgue measure such that for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N_{k}
, we have
\delta_{\gamma_{y}^{1/k}}=(B^{1/k})_{\#}\eta_{0,y}
. Define

N:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}N_{k},

which is of vanishing Lebesgue measure. By Lemma
2.4
, we have that
(B^{1/k})_{\#}\eta_{0,y}
converges narrowly to
\eta_{0,y}
as
k\to+\infty
for every
y\in Y
. So there exists a Borel family
\{\gamma_{y}\}
in
\Gamma_{T}
such that for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N
, the probability measure
\delta_{\gamma_{y}^{1/k}}
converges narrowly to
\delta_{\gamma_{y}}
as
k\to+\infty
and
\eta_{0,y}=\delta_{\gamma_{y}}
. The thesis follows.
∎

As a consequence, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Consider a divergence-free vector field
b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p,q\in(1,+\infty]
satisfying (
1.5
). Assume that
b\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,T];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{q}_{p}(T)
.
Then there exists a unique
\eta
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
such that

(i)

\eta
is concentrated on integral curves of
b
, i.e.

(2.17)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\eta(d\gamma)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T];

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
(e_{t})_{\#}\eta={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

Proof.

Step 1.
(Existence)
This follows directly from Proposition
2.1
.

Step 2.
(Uniqueness)
Let
\eta
be a probability measure in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
. Let
\tau>0
and recall definitions (
2.12
) and (
2.13
).
The probability measure
(S^{\tau})_{\#}\eta
satisfies
(i)
and
(ii)
with
b
replaced by
b^{\tau}
. Observe that
b^{\tau}
belongs to
L^{1}((0,T);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and is divergence-free, so in view of Theorem
2.3
, the probability measure
(S^{\tau})_{\#}\eta
is uniquely determined. By
Lemma

2.4
, we have that
(S^{\tau})_{\#}\eta
converges narrowly to
\eta
as
\tau\downarrow 0
. Thus
\eta
is uniquely determined.
∎

We can now prove uniqueness of the zero-noise flow and
(i)
of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of
(i)
of Theorem
1.5
.

In view of
Lemma

2.6
, there exists a unique probability measure
\eta
satisfying
(i)
concentration on integral curves of
b
and
(ii)
incompressibility. In view of
Remark

2.2
,
\eta
is the unique zero-noise flow of
b
.

Let us now prove
(i)
.
Let
a
be a Borel vector field such that
a=b

{\mathscr{L}}^{d+1}
-a.e.. Then there exists a zero-noise flow
\bar{\eta}
of
a
by
Proposition

2.1
, which is concentrated on integral curves of
a
and satisfies
(e_{t})_{\#}\bar{\eta}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
for every
t\in[0,T]
. Note that for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(2.18)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\bar{\eta}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}a(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\bar{\eta}(d\gamma).

Thus
\bar{\eta}
is concentrated on integral curves of

and by
Lemma

2.6
, this implies
\eta=\bar{\eta}
, and proves the thesis.
∎

3.
Stochasticity of the zero-noise flow

In this section we prove part
(ii)
of Theorem
1.5
. Throughout this section, we shall take
\eta
to be the unique zero-noise flow of
b
satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

1.5
. Let us gather the following remark, which insures that we can perform disintegrations on
\eta
(see Section
1.3
).

Remark 3.1
.

Consider a Borel vector field
b\in L^{q}_{p}(T)
with
p
and
q
satisfying the Prodi-Serrin condition (
1.5
), and a probability measure
\eta
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
concentrated on integral curves of
b
such that
(e_{s})_{\#}\eta={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
for every
s\in[0,T]
. Then
\eta
is tight.
Indeed, define the following subsets of
\Gamma_{T}

(3.1)

K_{N}:=\Big\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{T}:\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{\gamma}(s)|^{2}ds\leq N\Big\}.

By Ascoli’s theorem,
K_{N}
is compact in
\Gamma_{T}
. This then implies

(3.2)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{\gamma}(s)|^{2}ds\eta(d\gamma)\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{T}}|b(s,\gamma(s))|^{2}\eta(d\gamma)ds\leq\|b\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Therefore, by the Markov inequality, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\eta(K_{N}^{c})\leq\frac{|b\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}}{N},

and since
b
is in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, this implies tightness of
\eta
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
.

3.1.
Disintegration of
\eta
at the final time

Let
\{\eta_{T,x}\}
be a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{T}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. In this paragraph, we will give a representation for
\eta_{T,x}
in terms on the backward integral curves of
b
.
Define
\tilde{b}(t,x):=-b(T-t,x)
and observe that this vector field lies in
L^{1}_{loc}([0,T);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
By
Theorem

2.3
, there exists a unique
\tilde{\eta}
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
concentrated on integral curves of
\tilde{b}
and such that
(e_{t})_{\#}\tilde{\eta}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
for every
t\in[0,T]
. Furthermore, there exists a Borel family
\{\tilde{\gamma}_{y}\}
in
\Gamma_{T}
such that

(3.3)

\tilde{\eta}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{\tilde{\gamma}_{y}}dy.

Define the involution

\beta:\Gamma_{T}\ni\gamma(\cdot)\longmapsto\gamma(T-\cdot)\in\Gamma_{T}.

Define further the Borel family in
\Gamma_{T}

(3.4)

\gamma_{y}=\beta(\tilde{\gamma}_{y})\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

We then have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have
\eta_{T,y}=\delta_{\gamma_{y}}
.

Proof.

Step 1.
Let us show that for every Borel set
A\subset\Gamma_{T}
, we have

(3.5)

\eta(A)=\tilde{\eta}(\beta(A)).

Define the probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
by

(3.6)

\mu(A)=\tilde{\eta}(\beta(A))

for every Borel set
A\subset\Gamma_{T}.

Clearly for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
(e_{t})_{\#}\mu=(e_{T-t})_{\#}\tilde{\eta}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.
Let us show that
\mu
is concentrated on integral curves of
b
, i.e.

(3.7)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\mu(d\gamma)=0\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
t\in[0,T]
and
c:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuous vector field such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds<\varepsilon.

Then, by continuity of the functional

\Gamma_{T}\ni\gamma\longmapsto\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)+\int_{0}^{t}c(T-s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

we have by (
3.6
) that

(3.8)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)+\int_{0}^{t}c(T-s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\tilde{\eta}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}c(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\mu(d\gamma).

Since
\tilde{\eta}
is concentrated on integral curves of
\tilde{b}
, we have

(3.9)

\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)+\int_{0}^{t}b(T-s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\tilde{\eta}(d\gamma)=0.

Thus, we have

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\mu(d\gamma)\\
&\leq\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)-\int_{0}^{t}c(s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\mu(d\gamma)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds\\
&=\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)+\int_{0}^{t}c(T-s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\tilde{\eta}(d\gamma)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds\\
&\leq\int_{\Gamma_{T}}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)+\int_{0}^{t}b(T-s,\gamma(s))ds\Big|\tilde{\eta}(d\gamma)+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|c(s,x)-b(s,x)|dxds<2\varepsilon.\end{split}

As
\varepsilon
and
t
were arbitrary, this shows that
\mu
is concentrated on integral curves of
b
.
In view of
Theorem

2.3
, this shows that
\mu=\eta
.

Step 2.
Let
\{\eta_{T,y}\}
be a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{T}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. In view of
Lemma

1.4
with
f=e_{T}
,
g=\beta
and
h=e_{0}
, it holds that

\{\beta_{\#}\eta_{T,y}\}
is a disintegration of
\beta_{\#}\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, and since
\beta
is an involution we have
\beta_{\#}\eta=\tilde{\eta}
by
Step 1
. Therefore by (
3.3
) and essential uniqueness of the disintegration, we have
\beta_{\#}\eta_{T,y}=\tilde{\eta}_{0,x}=\delta_{\tilde{\gamma}_{y}}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, whence using again that
\beta
is an involution, we have
\eta_{T,y}=\delta_{\gamma_{y}}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. This proves the thesis.
∎

3.2.
Disintegration of
\eta
at the initial time

We define the measure
\nu=(e_{0},e_{T})_{\#}\eta
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
For every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},
we define the family of measures on
\Gamma_{T}

(3.11)

\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}:=\left\{\begin{split}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}\qquad&\text{if}\quad\gamma_{y}(0)=x,\\
0\qquad&\text{if}\quad\gamma_{y}(0)\neq x.\end{split}\right.

Define the projection maps

\pi_{0}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\ni(x,y)\longmapsto x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

and

\pi_{1}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\ni(x,y)\longmapsto y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Let
\{\nu_{x}\}
be a disintegration of
\nu
with respect to
\pi_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

We will now give an expression for disintegrations of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
in terms of
\{\nu_{x}\}
. Throughout this section
\{\eta_{0,x}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. We then define
\tilde{\nu}_{x}:=(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu_{x}
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We denote by
\{\gamma_{y}\}
the Borel family in
\Gamma_{T}
given by
Lemma

3.2
. We also define the probability measure

(3.12)

\nu_{y}:=\delta_{(\gamma_{y}(0),y)},

on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The family
\{\nu_{y}\}
is a disintegration of
\nu
with respect to
\pi_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}.

Proof.

It is clear that
\nu_{y}
is supported on
\pi_{1}^{-1}(y)
.
By part
(i)
of Theorem
1.5
, we know also that
\{\delta_{\gamma_{y}}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{T}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. Therefore, for every Borel set
A
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

\nu(A)=(e_{0},e_{T})_{\#}\eta(A)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(e_{0},e_{T})_{\#}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}(A)dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{(\gamma_{y}(0),y)}(A)dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nu_{y}(A)dy,

which proves the thesis.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The family
\{\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}:x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
(e_{0},e_{T})
and
\nu
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}
is supported on
(e_{0},e_{T})^{-1}(x,y)
.
For every Borel set
A
contained in
\Gamma_{T}
, we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}(A)d\nu(x,y)&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\{y\}}\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}(A)d\nu_{y}dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}(A)dy\\
&=\eta(A),\end{split}

where in the first equality we have used Lemma
3.3
, as well as (
1.9
). In the second equality we have used the definition (
3.12
) of
\nu_{y}
and the definition (
3.11
) of
\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}
, and in the last equality we have
Lemma

3.2
.
This proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

(3.14)

\eta_{0,x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}d\tilde{\nu}_{x}(y).

Proof.

Let
B
be a Borel set contained in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. As
\Gamma_{T}
is separable, its Borel
\sigma
-algebra is generated by a countable family
\mathscr{G}
. Let
A\in\mathscr{G}
. We then have

(3.15)

\begin{split}\int_{B}\eta_{0,x}(A)dx&\overset{1}{=}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\eta_{0,x}(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\})dx\\
&\overset{2}{=}\eta(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\})\\
&\overset{3}{=}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\})d\nu(x,y)\\
&\overset{4}{=}\int_{B}\Big[\int_{\{x\}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}(A)d\nu_{x}\Big]dx\\
&\overset{5}{=}\int_{B}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}(A)d\tilde{\nu}_{x}(y)\Big]dx.\end{split}

In equality
1
, we have used that
\eta_{0,x}
is supported on
\{\gamma(0)=x\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. In equality
2
, we have used that
\{\eta_{0,x}\}
is a disintegration of of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. In equality
3
, we have used Lemma
3.4
. In equality
4
, we have used that
\{\nu_{x}\}
is a disintegration of
\nu
with respect to
\pi_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, equation (
1.9
), as well as the fact that
\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\})=0
if
x\notin B
by definition.
In equality
5
, we have used the definition of
\delta_{x,\gamma_{y}}
as well as the definition of
\{\tilde{\nu}_{x}\}
.
As
B
was an arbitrary Borel set in

, there exists a set

of vanishing Lebesgue measure such that for every

\eta_{0,x}(A)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}(A)d\tilde{\nu}_{x}(y).

Now define

Then, for every

\eta_{0,x}(A)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\delta_{\gamma_{y}}(A)d\tilde{\nu}_{x}(y).

As

3.3.
Disintegration of
\eta
for the Depauw vector field

Let us now show that for the vector field
b_{DP}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
constructed by Depauw
[
12
]
, the family probability measures
\tilde{\nu}_{x}
are not Dirac masses for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
. This will conclude the proof of Theorem
1.5
. This paragraph draws from
[
21
]
.

Let
\eta
be the unique zero-noise flow of
b_{DP}
.
From
Section

3.4
, we have two bounded densities
\rho^{B}
and
\rho^{W}
in
C([0,T];w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
solving

(PDE)

\operatorname{div}_{t,x}\rho(1,b_{DP})=0\quad\text{in the sense of distributions on $[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}$,}

which further satisfy

(i)

\rho^{B}(0,\cdot)=1/2=\rho^{W}(0,\cdot)
;

(ii)

\rho^{B}(t,\cdot)+\rho^{W}(t,\cdot)=1
for every
t\in[0,T]
;

(iii)

\operatorname{supp}\rho^{B}(T,\cdot)\cup\operatorname{supp}\rho^{W}(T,\cdot)=\mathbb{T}^{2}
;

(iv)

\operatorname{supp}\rho^{B}(T,\cdot)\cap\operatorname{supp}\rho^{W}(T,\cdot)
is of vanishing Lebesgue measure.

By Smirnov’s representation for normal 1-currents
[
23
]
or Ambrosio’s superposition principle
[
2
]
, there exist probability measures
\eta^{B}
and
\eta^{W}
in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma_{T})
concentrated on integral curves of
b_{DP}
such that for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(e_{t})_{\#}\eta^{B}=\rho^{B}(t,\cdot){\mathscr{L}}^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad(e_{t})_{\#}\eta^{W}=\rho^{W}(t,\cdot){\mathscr{L}}^{d}.

Let
\nu^{B}
and
\nu^{W}
be two probability measures given by
\nu^{B}=(e_{0},e_{T})_{\#}\eta^{B}
and
\nu^{W}=(e_{0},e_{T})_{\#}\eta^{W}
. Let
\{\eta_{0,x}^{B}\}
be a disintegration of
\eta^{B}
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
, and let
\{\nu_{x}^{B}\}
be a disintegration of
\nu^{B}
with respect to
\pi_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
. Similarly, let
\{\eta_{0,x}^{W}\}
be a disintegration of
\eta^{W}
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
, and let
\{\nu_{x}^{W}\}
be a disintegration of
\nu^{W}
with respect to
\pi_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
.
Note that by definition of
\nu^{B}
and
\nu^{W}
, we have

(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{B}=(e_{T})_{\#}\eta^{B}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{W}=(e_{T})_{\#}\eta^{W}.

This clearly implies that for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, we have

(3.17)

(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{B}_{x}=(e_{T})_{\#}\eta_{0,x}^{B}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{W}_{x}=(e_{T})_{\#}\eta_{0,x}^{W}.

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{B}_{x}(\operatorname{supp}\rho^{W}(T,\cdot))dx&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(e_{T})_{\#}\eta^{B}_{0,x}(\operatorname{supp}\rho^{W}(T,\cdot))dx\\
&=(e_{T})_{\#}\eta^{B}(\operatorname{supp}\rho^{W}(T,\cdot))\\
&=\int_{\operatorname{supp}\rho^{W}(T,\cdot)}\rho^{B}(T,x)dx\\
&=0.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{W}_{x}(\operatorname{supp}\rho^{B}(T,\cdot))dx=0.

Therefore, for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, in view of property
(iv)
above, the probability measures
(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{W}_{x}
and
(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{B}_{x}
are mutually singular.

Also, by property
(ii)
above, we have

(e_{t})_{\#}\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{B}+\eta^{W})={\mathscr{L}}^{d}

for every
t\in[0,T],
whence

(3.18)

\eta=\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{B}+\eta^{W}).

By essential uniqueness of the disintegration, we therefore have for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}

\nu_{x}=\frac{1}{2}(\nu^{W}_{x}+\nu^{B}_{x}).

Therefore, for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, we have

\tilde{\nu}_{x}=(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu_{x}=\frac{1}{2}(((\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{W}_{x}+(\pi_{1})_{\#}\nu^{B}_{x}).

whereby for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
the probability measure
\tilde{\nu}_{x}
is not a Dirac mass.
This concludes the proof of
(ii)
of Theorem
1.5
.

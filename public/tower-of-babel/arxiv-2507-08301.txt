Approximation of magnetic Schrödinger operators with δ \delta -interactions supported on networks
Markus Holzmann

Abstract
. This paper deals with the approximation of a magnetic Schrödinger operator with a singular δ \delta -potential that is formally given by ( i ∇ + A ) 2 + Q + α δ Σ (i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q+\alpha\delta_{\Sigma} by Schrödinger operators with regular potentials in the norm resolvent sense. This is done for Σ \Sigma being the finite union of C 2 C^{2} -hypersurfaces, for coefficients A A , Q Q , and α \alpha under minimal assumptions such that the associated quadratic forms are closed and sectorial, and Q Q and α \alpha are allowed to be complex-valued functions. In particular, Σ \Sigma can be a graph in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} or the boundary of a piecewise C 2 C^{2} -domain. Moreover, spectral implications of the mentioned convergence result are discussed.

1.
Introduction

Schrödinger operators with singular
\delta
-potentials supported on sets of measure zero play an important role in mathematical physics. Singular potentials supported on points were already used in the early days of quantum mechanics
[
29
,
39
]
and later systematically analyzed from a mathematical point of view, see the monograph
[
1
]
and the references therein. In the last three decades, there was a substantial interest in singular potentials supported on hypersurfaces
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, like curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, see the review paper
[
16
]
, the monograph
[
18
]
, or the research papers
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
21
,
33
]
, to mention just a few selected works.
Such operators are formally given by

H_{A,Q,\alpha}=(i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q+\alpha\delta_{\Sigma},

(1.1)

and they describe for attractive interactions (i.e.
\alpha<0
) the propagation of a quantum particle on
\Sigma
under the influence of a magnetic vector potential
A
and an electric potential
Q
allowing the tunneling of the particle between different parts of
\Sigma
, see
[
5
,
16
]
; from this point of view, graph like structures
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
are of particular interest. Moreover, operators of the form
H_{0,0,\alpha}
are useful in the design of photonic crystals with prescribed spectral gaps
[
22
,
26
]
.

Of course, the operators with the singular potentials are only idealized replacements of more realistic operators with regular potentials, and so the relation of the regular and the singular models was justified via an approximation procedure already in
[
29
]
. For
A=Q=0
the approximation of one, two, and three-dimensional Schrödinger operators with
\delta
-point interactions by Schrödinger operators with scaled regular potentials in the norm resolvent sense was treated systematically in the 1970s and 1980s, see the monograph
[
1
]
and the references therein; the latter convergence implies, in particular, the convergence of the spectra and of functions of the involved operators
[
27
,
40
]
and thus, it justifies the usage of the idealized models as good approximations of their regular counterparts. The approximation of Schrödinger operators with
\delta
-potentials supported on the sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was considered in
[
2
,
37
]
, then the situation that
\Sigma
is the boundary of a star shaped domain and
\alpha
is real-valued and smooth was investigated in
[
36
]
, and unbounded and
C^{2}
-smooth
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
were treated in
[
17
,
21
]
. It is worth mentioning that in
[
38
]
Laplacians with
\delta
- (and other measure valued) potentials supported on quite general geometries were approximated, but only in the strong resolvent sense, which does not imply the convergence of the spectra of the approximating operators in the Hausdorff metric. Finally, in
[
4
]
the approximation of the Schrödinger operator with a
\delta
-potential of real-valued strength
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
on one bounded or unbounded
C^{2}
-smooth hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, was shown, and in
[
5
]
the approximation of a two-dimensional Schrödinger operator with a homogeneous magnetic field and an electric
\delta
-potential of real-valued strength
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
supported on a bounded
C^{1,1}
-smooth curve was treated.

The main motivation in this paper is to show that the operator
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
formally given by (
1.1
) can be approximated in the norm resolvent sense by Schrödinger operators with scaled regular potentials under minimal assumptions on
A,Q
, and
\alpha
, and for a wide class of interaction supports
\Sigma
. Concerning the coefficients, of course from the point of view of quantum mechanics, real-valued functions that are associated with self-adjoint operators are favored, but the analysis in this paper works equally well for complex-valued functions
Q
and
\alpha
, yielding a relation to works on Schrödinger operators with non-self-adjoint
\delta
-potentials
[
10
,
24
,
28
]
. Moreover, the interaction support
\Sigma
will be allowed to be the finite union of bounded or unbounded
C^{2}
-smooth hypersurfaces, and so classes of graph-like structures in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, that are particularly important for the application to leaky quantum graphs, and piecewise
C^{2}
-smooth
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which are an important subclass of Lipschitz domains, are contained.

To set the stage in a rigorous way, let
N,n\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed with
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma^{(1)},\dots,\Sigma^{(N)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be hypersurfaces as in Definition
2.5
with normal vector fields
\nu^{(1)},\dots,\nu^{(N)}
such that the Hausdorff measure of
\Sigma^{(k)}\cap\Sigma^{(l)}
is zero for
k\neq l
. Then, the network
\Sigma
is

\Sigma:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\Sigma^{(k)}.

(1.2)

Moreover, assume that

A\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})

(1.3)

and
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
with

0\leq Q_{1}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,Q_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ with }p^{\prime}\begin{cases}\geq n/2,&n>2,\\
>1,&n=2,\end{cases}

(1.4)

are fixed. Note that
Q_{1}
is automatically real-valued, while
Q_{2}
is allowed to be complex-valued. Next, define the space

\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):|(i\nabla+A)f|,Q_{1}^{1/2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}.

(1.5)

This space is studied in detail in Section
2.1
, where it is shown that
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
, when endowed with a proper norm, is a Hilbert space and that it is indeed independent of
Q_{2}
. Moreover, in Section
2.2
it is proved that functions
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
posses Dirichlet traces
f|_{\Sigma^{(k)}}\in L^{q}(\Sigma^{(k)})
with
q=2\frac{n-1}{n-2+\mu}
and
\mu\in(0,1]
.
Eventually, assume that

\alpha:=(\alpha^{(k)})_{k=1}^{N}\in L^{p}(\Sigma)+L^{\infty}(\Sigma):=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}\bigl(L^{p}(\Sigma^{(k)})+L^{\infty}(\Sigma^{(k)})\bigr)

(1.6)

with
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
,
where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is fixed. To introduce the Schrödinger operator with a
\delta
-potential of strength
\alpha
supported on the network
\Sigma
, consider the quadratic form

\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}[f]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(\bigl|(i\nabla+A)f\bigr|^{2}+Q|f|^{2}\bigr)\textup{d}x+\int_{\Sigma}\alpha\bigl|f|_{\Sigma}\bigr|^{2}\textup{d}\sigma,\quad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}=\mathcal{H}_{A,Q},

(1.7)

where the shortcut notation
\int_{\Sigma}\alpha|f|_{\Sigma}|^{2}\textup{d}\sigma:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma^{(k)}}\alpha^{(k)}|f|_{\Sigma^{(k)}}|^{2}\textup{d}\sigma
is used. We will see in Proposition
2.8
that
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}
is a densely defined, closed, and sectorial form in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The associated
m
-sectorial operator
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
is the rigorous mathematical operator that is associated with the formal expression (
1.1
).

In order to construct the sequence of operators
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
that converges to
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
in the norm resolvent sense, note first that there exists
\beta>0
such that for each
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}
the mapping

\iota^{(k)}:\Sigma^{(k)}\times(-\beta,\beta)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\iota^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}},t):=x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}+t\nu^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}),

(1.8)

is injective, see Proposition
2.6
. Introduce for
\varepsilon\in(0,\beta]
the set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}:=\iota^{(k)}(\Sigma^{(k)}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
, and choose a function
V^{(k)}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
for a
\gamma\in(0,1)
that is supported in
\Omega_{\beta}^{(k)}
. Then, one can define for
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}

V_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon}V^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}+\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon}t\nu^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}})),&\text{if }x=x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}+t\nu^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}})\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{(k)},\\
0,&\text{if }x\notin\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{(k)},\end{cases}

and

V_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}V_{\varepsilon}^{(k)};

(1.9)

it follows from Proposition
2.6
(iv) that
V_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Consider the quadratic form

\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}[f]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(\bigl|(i\nabla+A)f\bigr|^{2}+(Q+V_{\varepsilon})|f|^{2}\bigr)\textup{d}x,\quad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}=\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}.

(1.10)

It will turn out as a byproduct in the proof of Theorem
1.1
that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
the form
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
is densely defined, closed, and sectorial in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The associated
m
-sectorial operator
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
is the rigorous mathematical operator that is associated with the expression
(i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q+V_{\varepsilon}
. Now, we are prepared to formulate the main result of this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
and
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
be as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively,
\Sigma
be as in (
1.2
), and
V^{(k)}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
for a
\gamma\in(0,1)
be supported in
\Omega_{\beta}^{(k)}
. Let
V_{\varepsilon}
be defined for
\varepsilon\in(0,\beta)
as in (
1.9
) and introduce
\alpha=(\alpha^{(k)})_{k=1}^{N}\in L^{p}(\Sigma)+L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
by

\alpha^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}):=\int_{-\beta}^{\beta}V^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}+t\nu^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}))\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}).

(1.11)

Eventually, let
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
be the
m
-sectorial operator that is associated with the form in (
1.7
), and let
\gamma_{1}\in(0,2\gamma]\cap(0,1)
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\beta)
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the form
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
in (
1.10
) is closed and sectorial, and for the associated
m
-sectorial operators
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
there exist
\lambda_{0}<0
such that
(-\infty,\lambda_{0})\subset\rho(H_{A,Q,\alpha})\cap\bigcap_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}\rho(H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}})
and for all
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the estimate

\big\|(H_{A,Q,\alpha}-\lambda)^{-1}-(H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}-\lambda)^{-1}\big\|\leq C\varepsilon^{\gamma_{1}/2}

(1.12)

with a constant
C=C(\lambda,\varepsilon_{0})>0
is true. In particular,
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
converges in the norm resolvent sense to
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows ideas that were used in
[
5
, Appendix B]
and relies on an estimate of
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}-\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}
in a suitable topology and on applying an abstract result from
[
27
, Section VI.3]
. In particular, the fact that only estimates of the associated forms are used allows to treat non-smooth coefficients
A,Q,\alpha
and networks
\Sigma
and so,
Theorem
1.1
improves the results on the approximation of Schrödinger operators with
\delta
-potentials supported on hypersurfaces in several directions:

(a)

The support of the
\delta
-potential is allowed to be a finite network as in (
1.2
). This includes, in particular, also the boundaries of piecewise
C^{2}
-domains, which are an important class of Lipschitz hypersurfaces, and in dimension
n=2
also graphs and curves with cusps.

(b)

The interaction strengths are allowed to be non-real and belong to
L^{p}
-spaces with optimal
p
so that the operator
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
can be defined with the help of its associated quadratic form.

(c)

Additive electric and magnetic potentials
A
and
Q
, that are independent of
\varepsilon
, are allowed, and these potentials belong to optimal classes of potentials such that the unperturbed operators
H_{A,Q,0}
can be defined as
m
-sectorial operators with the help of their associated form.

It is worth to note that also for the particularly important self-adjoint case Theorem
1.1
improves the known approximation results in all points (a)–(c) listed above.

In Theorem
1.1
we start with given potentials
V^{(k)}
, which are used to construct the scaled potentials
V_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.9
), and show that the associated operators
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
converge in the norm resolvent sense to
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is computed in terms of
V^{(k)}
in (
1.11
). In applications, also the inverse question is of relevance: Given a network
\Sigma
of hypersurfaces and
\alpha\in L^{p}(\Sigma)+L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, is it possible to approximate
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
in the norm resolvent sense by operators of the form
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
? In the following corollary, which follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
, a positive answer to this question is given.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
A
and
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
be as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively,
\Sigma
be as in (
1.2
), and
\alpha^{(k)}\in L^{p}(\Sigma^{(k)})+L^{\infty}(\Sigma^{(k)})
with
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
for a
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Let
V^{(k)}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be defined by

V^{(k)}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\beta}\alpha(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}),&\text{if }x=x_{\Sigma^{(k)}}+t\nu^{(k)}(x_{\Sigma^{(k)}})\in\Omega_{\beta}^{(k)},\\
0,&\text{if }x\notin\Omega_{\beta}^{(k)},\end{cases}

(1.13)

and let
V_{\varepsilon}
be as in (
1.9
).
Eventually, let
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
be the
m
-sectorial operator that is associated with the form in (
1.7
), and let
\gamma_{1}\in(0,2\gamma]\cap(0,1)
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\beta)
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the form
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
in (
1.10
) is closed and sectorial, and for the associated
m
-sectorial operators
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
there exist
\lambda_{0}<0
such that
(-\infty,\lambda_{0})\subset\rho(H_{A,Q,\alpha})\cap\bigcap_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}\rho(H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}})
and for all
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the estimate

\big\|(H_{A,Q,\alpha}-\lambda)^{-1}-(H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}-\lambda)^{-1}\big\|\leq C\varepsilon^{\gamma_{1}/2}

with a constant
C=C(\lambda,\varepsilon_{0})>0
is true. In particular,
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
converges in the norm resolvent sense to
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
.

As it is known that convergence of operators in the norm resolvent sense implies the convergence of the spectra of the associated operators
[
27
,
40
]
, one can use the results from Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
to transfer results on the spectrum of
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
to
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
. Some examples of this idea are demonstrated in Section
4
; here, the focus lies on examples for which the improvements of this paper over the existing literature on the approximation of Schrödinger operators with singular potentials are necessary.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is of preliminary nature. In Subsection
2.1
some useful properties of the space
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
from (
1.5
) are summarized, Subsection
2.2
is devoted to the class of
C^{2}
-hypersurfaces that build the network
\Sigma
, and in Subsection
2.3
the operator
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
formally given by (
1.1
) is introduced. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.1
.
Finally, in Section
4
some spectral implications that follow from the convergence in Theorem
1.1
are discussed.

Notations.

denotes a generic constant that may change its value in-between lines. For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the set

is the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space of once weakly differentiable functions and

,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is the set of all
k
times continuously differentiable functions with bounded partial derivatives up to order

. For a closed linear operator

in a Hilbert space, its domain, resolvent set, spectrum, and point spectrum are denoted by

,
\rho(A)
,

, and

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The space
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}

Throughout this subsection, let
A\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})

and
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
with
0\leq Q_{1}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
Q_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}\geq\frac{n}{2}
for
n>2
and
p^{\prime}>1
for
n=2
,
be as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively. Note that
A
and
Q_{1}
are real-valued, while
Q_{2}
is allowed to be complex-valued. In this subsection, some useful properties of the space

\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):|(i\nabla+A)f|,Q_{1}^{1/2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\},

(2.1)

where
(i\nabla+A)f
is understood in the sense of distributions, are summarized. Define the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(|(i\nabla+A)f|^{2}+(Q_{1}+1)|f|^{2}\bigr)\textup{d}x.

(2.2)

Basic properties of
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
are stated in the following lemma, see
[
30
, Lemma 1 and Theorem 1]
for a proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

The space
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
endowed with the norm in (
2.2
) is a Hilbert space and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
.

Next, we state a variant of the diamagnetic inequality that will be useful in our considerations.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
one has
\nabla|f|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{n})
and the estimate

\big\|\nabla|f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}

(2.3)

is true.

Proof.

By
[
31
, Theorem 7.21]
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the pointwise estimate

\big|\nabla|f(x)|\big|\leq\big|(i\nabla+A(x))f(x)\big|

is true. Therefore, for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
one has
\nabla|f|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C}^{n})
and as
Q_{1}\geq 0
one concludes

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|\nabla|f|\big|^{2}\textup{d}x&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|(i\nabla+A)f\big|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(|(i\nabla+A)f|^{2}+(Q_{1}+1)|f|^{2}\bigr)\textup{d}x=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2},\end{split}

which is the claimed result.
∎

In the following lemma the non-real term
Q_{2}
is added and it is shown, that the norm that is induced by the associated quadratic form is equivalent to the norm in (
2.2
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}\geq\frac{n}{2}
for
n>2
and
p^{\prime}>1
for
n=2
. Then, the form

\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q}[f]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(|(i\nabla+A)f|^{2}+(Q_{1}+Q_{2})|f|^{2}\bigr)\textup{d}x,\quad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q}=\mathcal{H}_{A,Q},

(2.4)

is well-defined, sectorial, and closed, and there exist constants
C_{2}>C_{1}>0
and
C_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

C_{1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}\leq\bigl(\textup{Re}\,\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q}+C_{3}\bigr)[f]\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}.

Proof.

Consider the quadratic form

\mathfrak{q}_{2}[f]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q_{2}|f|^{2}\textup{d}x,\quad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{q}_{2}=\bigl\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):|Q_{2}|^{1/2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\bigr\}.

(2.5)

To show the claims, it suffices to prove that
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
is form bounded with respect to the form
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}\ni f\mapsto\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}
with form bound smaller than one. For this, we claim that the multiplication by
|Q_{2}|^{1/2}
gives rise to a bounded operator from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that for all
\delta>0
there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that

\big\||Q_{2}|^{1/2}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq\delta\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\quad f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(2.6)

To see this, note that by assumption there exist
Q_{2,p^{\prime}}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Q_{2,\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
Q_{2}=Q_{2,p^{\prime}}+Q_{2,\infty}
.
It follows from
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
(applied for
s=1
) that there exists for all
\delta>0
a constant
\widetilde{C}_{\delta}
such that for all
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\big\||Q_{2,p^{\prime}}|^{1/2}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq\delta\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\widetilde{C}_{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\big\||Q_{2}|^{1/2}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Q_{2}||f|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Q_{2,p^{\prime}}||f|^{2}\textup{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Q_{2,\infty}||f|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&\leq\delta\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\bigl(\widetilde{C}_{\delta}+\|Q_{2,\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigr)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\end{split}

which is exactly (
2.6
) with
C_{\delta}:=\widetilde{C}_{\delta}+\|Q_{2,\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

Using (
2.6
) for a fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
and Lemma
2.2
imply first for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\begin{split}\bigl|\mathfrak{q}_{2}[f]\bigr|&\leq\big\||Q_{2}|^{1/2}|f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\\
&\leq\delta\big\|\nabla|f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+(C_{\delta}+\delta)\big\||f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\\
&\leq\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}+(C_{\delta}+\delta)\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.\end{split}

Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
by Lemma
2.1
, the latter estimate remains true for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The only property of the term
Q_{2}
that we need in this paper and, in particular, to prove Theorem
1.1
, is the one from Lemma
2.3
. As seen in the proof, the statement remains true, if
Q_{2}
is such that the form
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
in (
2.5
) is form bounded with respect to the form
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}\ni f\mapsto\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}

1.1

2.2.
Description of hypersurfaces and their tubular neighborhoods

In this section the hypersurfaces that build the network being the support of the
\delta
-potential are described, and then a suitable variant of the trace theorem for these hypersurfaces and the space
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
from (
2.1
) is discussed. Finally, some properties of the map
\iota
in (
1.8
) which describes the tubular neighborhood of the network are collected. Since for the proof of Theorem
1.1
similar ideas as in
[
8
, Section 3.2]
are used, we follow closely the definition of hypersurfaces from
[
8
]
:

Definition 2.5
.

We say that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurface, if there exist
p\in\mathbb{N}
, open sets
\Omega,V_{1},\dots,V_{p},W_{1},\dots,W_{p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, mappings
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{p}\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{R})
, rotation matrices
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that the following holds:

(i)

\partial\Omega\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{p}W_{k}
.

(ii)

For all
x\in\partial\Omega
there exists
k=k(x)\in\{1,\dots,p\}
such that
B(x,\varepsilon_{1})\subset W_{k}
.

(iii)

W_{k}\cap\Omega=W_{k}\cap\{\kappa_{k}(x^{\prime},x_{n}):x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},x_{n}<\zeta_{k}(x^{\prime})\}
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
.

(iv)

\Sigma\subset\partial\Omega\cap\big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}V_{k}\big)
.

(v)

V_{k}\subset W_{k}
and
\partial\Omega\cap V_{k}=\Sigma\cap V_{k}
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
.

Roughly speaking, conditions (i)–(iii) in the above definition mean that the set
\Omega
is of the same form as the sets
\Omega_{+}
considered in
[
8
]
, while conditions (iv)&(v) mean that the admissible hypersurface
\Sigma
is a subset of
\partial\Omega
with good properties, so that an integral over
\Sigma
is well-defined. In particular, (relatively open subsets of)
C^{2}_{b}
-hypographs and the boundaries of compact
C^{2}
-domains are admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurfaces.

Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\Sigma)
of order
s\in[-2,2]
on admissible a
C^{2}
-hypersurface
\Sigma
are constructed in the standard way, with the help of a suitable partition of unity subordinate to the open cover
V_{1},\dots,V_{p}
of
\Sigma
and local coordinates, see
[
8
,
35
]
for details.

An important role will be played by the Weingarten map or shape operator
W
on an admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurface
\Sigma
. This is the linear map at the tangent space
T_{x_{\Sigma}}
for
x_{\Sigma}=\kappa_{k}(x^{\prime},\zeta_{k}(x^{\prime}))\in\Sigma
via its action on a basis element
\partial_{j}\kappa_{k}(x^{\prime},\zeta_{k}(x^{\prime}))
of
T_{x_{\Sigma}}
,

W(x_{\Sigma})\partial_{j}\kappa_{k}(x^{\prime},\zeta_{k}(x^{\prime}))=-\partial_{j}\nu\bigl(\kappa_{k}(x^{\prime},\zeta_{k}(x^{\prime}))\bigr),

(2.7)

where
\nu
denotes the normal vector field at
\Sigma
. It is not difficult to show that the definition of
W(x_{\Sigma})
is independent of the parametrization of
\Sigma
. Some useful properties of
W
and the map

\iota:\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\iota(x_{\Sigma},t):=x_{\Sigma}+t\nu(x_{\Sigma}),

(2.8)

see also (
1.8
), are summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurface with unit normal vector field
\nu
,
W
be the associated Weingarten map, and
\iota
be defined by (
2.8
). Then, there exists
\beta>0
such that the following holds:

(i)

\iota|_{\Sigma\times(-\beta,\beta)}
is injective.

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that
|1-\det(I-\varepsilon W(x_{\Sigma}))|\leq C\varepsilon<1/2
for all
x_{\Sigma}\in\Sigma
and
\varepsilon\in(-\beta,\beta)
.

(iii)

For
\varepsilon\in(-\beta,\beta)
one has with
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\iota(\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
if and only if
f\circ\iota|_{\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\in L^{1}(\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
and in this case

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f(x)\textup{d}x=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma}f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},t))\det(I-tW(x_{\Sigma}))\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\textup{d}t.

(iv)

Let
p\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(-\beta,\beta)
. Then,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
if and only if
f\circ\iota|_{\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\in L^{p}(\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
and there exist constants
0<C_{1}<C_{2}
such that

C_{1}\bigl\|f\circ\iota|_{\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{2}\bigl\|f\circ\iota|_{\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))}.

Proof.

Items (i)–(iii) can be shown in the same way as in
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
. The claim in (iv) is an immediate consequence of the formula in (iii) and (ii).
∎

In the next proposition a variant of the trace theorem involving the space
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
defined by (
2.1
), which is a key ingredient to define the for
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}
in (
1.7
), is stated.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurface and
q=2\frac{n-1}{n-2+\mu}
for
\mu\in(0,1]
. Then, the map
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ni f\mapsto f|_{\Sigma}
admits a unique continuous extension
\gamma_{D}:\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}\rightarrow L^{q}(\Sigma)
and for all
\delta>0
there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that

\|\gamma_{D}f\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}^{2}\leq\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

(2.9)

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
.

Proof.

The proof follows closely ideas from
[
11
, Lemma A.1]
. Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It follows from
[
13
, Theorem 8.12.6.I]
that
L^{q}(\Sigma)
is continuously embedded in the Sobolev space
H^{1/2-\mu/2}(\Sigma)
, which is again continuously embedded in
H^{1/2-\mu^{\prime}/2}(\Sigma)
for
\mu^{\prime}\in(0,\mu]\cap(0,1)
. Therefore, the trace theorem for Sobolev spaces
[
34
, Theorem 2]
implies that

\begin{split}\|f|_{\Sigma}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}^{2}&=\bigl\||f|\big|_{\Sigma}\bigr\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma)}^{2}\\
&\leq C\bigl\||f|\big|_{\Sigma}\bigr\|_{H^{1/2-\mu/2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\\
&\leq C\bigl\||f|\big|_{\Sigma}\bigr\|_{H^{1/2-\mu^{\prime}/2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\\
&\leq C\bigl\||f|\bigr\|_{H^{1-\mu^{\prime}/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\\
&\leq\delta\bigl\||f|\bigr\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+C_{\delta}\bigl\||f|\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\\
&\leq\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\end{split}

(2.10)

where
[
25
, Theorem 3.30]
or

[
40
, Satz 11.18 e)]
was used in the second last step, and Lemma
2.2
in the last step. Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
by Lemma
2.1
, the claim of this proposition follows from (
2.10
).
∎

2.3.
Rigorous definition of
H_{A,Q,\alpha}

Throughout this subsection, assume that
A\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})

and
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
with
0\leq Q_{1}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Q_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}\geq\frac{n}{2}
for
n>2
and
p^{\prime}>1
for
n=2
,
are as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively. Moreover, let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Sigma^{(1)},\dots,\Sigma^{(N)}
be admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurfaces as in Definition
2.5
such that the Hausdorff measure of
\Sigma^{(k)}\cap\Sigma^{(l)}
is zero for
k\neq l
, and
\Sigma:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\Sigma^{(k)}
.
For
p\in[1,\infty]
the
L^{p}
-spaces on
\Sigma
are defined by

L^{p}(\Sigma):=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{N}L^{p}(\Sigma^{(k)})

and for
f=(f_{k})_{k=1}^{N}\in L^{1}(\Sigma)
its integral is

\int_{\Sigma}f\text{d}\sigma:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma^{(k)}}f_{k}\text{d}\sigma.

The goal in this subsection is to study the form
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}
from (
1.7
), which then leads to a rigorous definition of the formal expression in (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
be fixed,
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
, and

\alpha:=(\alpha^{(k)})_{k=1}^{N}\in L^{p}(\Sigma)+L^{\infty}(\Sigma).

Then, the quadratic form

\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}[f]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl(\bigl|(i\nabla+A)f\bigr|^{2}+Q|f|^{2}\bigr)\textup{d}x+\int_{\Sigma}\alpha|\gamma_{D}f|^{2}\textup{d}\sigma,\quad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}=\mathcal{H}_{A,Q},

is densely defined, sectorial, and closed. Moreover, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}\leq C_{1}(\textup{Re}\,\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}+C_{2})[f]

(2.11)

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
.

The
m
-sectorial operator that is associated with
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}
via the first representation theorem
[
27
, Theorem VI.2.1]
is denoted by
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
, i.e.

\begin{split}H_{A,Q,\alpha}f&=\bigl((i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q\bigr)f\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma,\\
\mathrm{dom}\,H_{A,Q,\alpha}&=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}:\bigl((i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q\bigr)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma),\\
&\qquad\quad\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}[f,g]=\bigl(\bigl((i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q\bigr)f,g\bigr)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\forall g\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}\bigr\};\end{split}

(2.12)

here, the fact that
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
acts as
(i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma
follows from the inclusion
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Sigma)\subset\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
, see Lemma
2.1
, and the definition of the distributional expression
(i\nabla+A)^{2}+Q
; cf.
[
30
, Lemma 5]
for a similar argument.

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

By assumption, for each
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}
there exist functions
\alpha^{(k)}_{p}\in L^{p}(\Sigma^{(k)})
and
\alpha^{(k)}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma^{(k)})
such that
\alpha^{(k)}=\alpha^{(k)}_{p}+\alpha^{(k)}_{\infty}
. Set
\alpha_{p}:=(\alpha^{(k)}_{p})_{k=1}^{N}\in L^{p}(\Sigma)
and
\alpha_{\infty}:=(\alpha^{(k)}_{\infty})_{k=1}^{N}\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, so that
\alpha=\alpha_{p}+\alpha_{\infty}
. Define for
*\in\{p,\infty\}
the form

\mathfrak{a}_{*}[f]:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma^{(k)}}\alpha^{(k)}_{*}|\gamma_{D}f|^{2}\textup{d}\sigma,\quad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{a}_{*}=\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}.

Let
\delta>0
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
be fixed. Using Hölder’s inequality with
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
and
q=(1-\frac{1}{p})^{-1}=\frac{n-1}{n-2+\gamma}
and Proposition
2.7
(with
\mu=\gamma
) one finds that

\begin{split}\big|\mathfrak{a}_{p}[f]\big|&\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma^{(k)}}|\alpha^{(k)}_{p}|^{p}\textup{d}\sigma\right)^{1/p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Sigma^{(k)}}|\gamma_{D}f|^{2q}\textup{d}\sigma\right)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\|\alpha_{p}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\bigl(\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\bigr).\end{split}

Likewise, Proposition
2.7
applied for
\mu=1
yields

\begin{split}\big|\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}[f]\big|\leq\|\alpha_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\bigl(\delta\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\bigr).\end{split}

In particular, the forms
\mathfrak{a}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}
are both well-defined and form bounded with bound smaller than
\frac{1}{2}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q}
defined by (
2.4
). Therefore, by the KLMN theorem
[
27
, Theorem VI.1.33]
the form
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,\alpha}=\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q}+\mathfrak{a}_{p}+\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}
is sectorial and closed, as
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q}
has these properties by Lemma
2.3
, and the estimate (
2.11
) is true.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

By using, e.g.,
[

instead of Proposition
2.7
one can show in the same way as in the proof of Proposition
2.8
that the form
\mathfrak{h}_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The following preparatory lemma, in which the traces of functions in
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
on
\Sigma
and a slightly shifted hypersurface in the normal direction are compared, is the key ingredient to prove the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.1
. In the proof, we use properties of the shift in the normal direction - a strategy that is inspired by
[
8
, Section 3.2]
. Recall that for an admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurface
\Sigma
as in Definition
2.5
the map
\iota
is defined by (
2.8
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in L^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
with
0\leq Q_{1}\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Q_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}\geq\frac{n}{2}
for
n>2
and
p^{\prime}>1
for
n=2
. Moreover, let
\Sigma
be an admissible
C^{2}
-hypersurface with unit normal vector field
\nu
,
q=2\frac{n-1}{n-2+\mu}
for
\mu\in(0,1]
, and
\mu^{\prime}\in(0,\mu]\cap(0,1)
. Then, there exists
\beta_{0}>0
and a constant
C_{1}>0
such that for all
r\in(-\beta_{0},\beta_{0})
the estimate

\left(\int_{\Sigma}\bigl||f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},r))|-|f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},0))|\bigr|^{q}\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{1}|r|^{\mu^{\prime}/2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}

(3.1)

is true. In particular, there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for all
r\in(-\beta_{0},\beta_{0})

\left(\int_{\Sigma}|f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},r))|^{q}\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}}.

(3.2)

Proof.

First, note that it suffices to prove (
3.1
), as this estimate, the triangle inequality, and Proposition
2.7
imply

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},r))|^{q}\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\right)^{1/q}&\leq\left(\int_{\Sigma}\bigl||f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},r))|-|f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},0))|\bigr|^{q}\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\right)^{1/q}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\int_{\Sigma}|f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},0))|^{q}\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\right)^{1/q}\\
&\leq(C_{1}|r|^{\mu^{\prime}/2}+C_{3})\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}},\end{split}

where
C_{3}=\delta+C_{\delta}
is the constant from Proposition
2.7
for a fixed choice of
\delta
. This yields the claim in (
3.2
). The estimate in (
3.1
) is shown in two steps. In
Step 1
a suitable Sobolev norm of an extension of
f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},r))-f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},0))
is estimated, which is used in
Step 2
to deduce (
3.1
).

Step 1.
Let
\widetilde{\nu}
be a
C^{1}_{b}
extension of
\nu
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To show that such an extension exists, let
\Omega
be as in Definition
2.5
and
\widehat{\nu}
be the normal vector field at
\partial\Omega
; then, as
\Sigma\subset\partial\Omega
, the function
\widehat{\nu}
is an extension of
\nu
to
\partial\Omega
. Moreover, choose
\phi\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
with
\phi(0)=1
and
\textup{supp}\,\phi\subset(-\beta^{\Omega},\beta^{\Omega})
, where
\beta^{\Omega}
is the number from Proposition
2.6
for the admissible hypersurface
\partial\Omega
. Then one possible choice of
\widetilde{\nu}
is given by

\widetilde{\nu}(x):=\begin{cases}\widehat{\nu}(x_{\partial\Omega})\phi(t),&\text{if }x=x_{\partial\Omega}+t\widehat{\nu}(x_{\partial\Omega})\text{ for }x_{\partial\Omega}\in\partial\Omega,t\in(-\beta^{\Omega},\beta^{\Omega}),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Define for
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the function
f_{r}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
f_{r}(x):=f(x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x))
.
The aim of this step is to prove for all
\delta\in[0,1]
the estimate

\|f-f_{r}\|_{H^{1-\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C|r|^{\delta}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(3.3)

for a suitable constant
C>0
, for which we closely follow the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
.

First, we show (
3.3
) for
\delta=1
. With the main theorem of calculus, one finds for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that

\begin{split}\|f_{r}-f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl|f(x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x))-f(x)\bigr|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}s}f(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))\textup{d}s\right|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{0}^{r}\nabla f(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))\cdot\widetilde{\nu}(x)\textup{d}s\right|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{|r|}|\nabla f(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|^{2}\textup{d}s\int_{0}^{|r|}|\widetilde{\nu}(x)|^{2}\textup{d}s\textup{d}x\\
&\leq|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\int_{0}^{|r|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|^{2}\textup{d}x\textup{d}s,\end{split}

where the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Fubini’s theorem were used. To proceed, denote the Jacobi matrix of the mapping
x\mapsto x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x)
by
D(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))
. Then, the eigenvalues of
D(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))
can be estimated from below by
1-|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Suppose that
\beta_{0}\in(0,\beta^{\Omega})
is chosen sufficiently small such that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
1-|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq C
for all
r\in(-\beta_{0},\beta_{0})
. Then,

\det D(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))\geq(1-|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{n}\geq C^{n}

and we can continue estimating

\begin{split}\|f-f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}&\leq|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\int_{0}^{|r|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|^{2}\frac{|\det D(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|}{|\det D(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|}\textup{d}x\textup{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{C^{n}}\int_{0}^{|r|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|^{2}|\det D(x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x))|\textup{d}x\textup{d}s\\
&=\frac{|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{C^{n}}\int_{0}^{|r|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(y)|^{2}\textup{d}y\textup{d}s,\end{split}

where the substitution
y=x+s\widetilde{\nu}(x)
was used in the last step. Since the last integral is independent of
s
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the claim in (
3.3
) for
\delta=1
follows.

Next, we prove the statement in (
3.3
) for
\delta=0
. With a similar calculation as in the case
\delta=1
and the substitution
y=x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x)
one finds that

\begin{split}\|\nabla f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl|(I+rD\widetilde{\nu}(x))\cdot\nabla f(x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x))\bigr|^{2}\textup{d}x\\
&\leq(1+|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigl|\nabla f(x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x))\bigr|^{2}\frac{|\det D(x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x))|}{|\det D(x+r\widetilde{\nu}(x))|}\textup{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{(1+|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{2}}{(1-|r|\|\widetilde{\nu}\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(y)|^{2}\textup{d}y.\end{split}

By combining this with the triangle inequality and (
3.3
) for
\delta=1
one finds that

\|f-f_{r}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f-f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which is the claim for
\delta=0
.

Finally, having the statement in (
3.3
) for
\delta=0
and for
\delta=1
, the claim for
\delta\in(0,1)
follows with an interpolation argument.

Step 2.
Let
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It follows from
[
13
, Theorem 8.12.6.I]
that
L^{q}(\Sigma)
is continuously embedded in

, which is again continuously embedded in

. Therefore, the trace theorem for Sobolev spaces
[

, Theorem 2]
implies

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\bigl||f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},r))|-|f(\iota(x_{\Sigma},0))|\bigr|^{q}\textup{d}\sigma(x_{\Sigma})\right)^{1/q}&\leq C\bigl\|\gamma_{D}(|f|_{r}-|f|)\big|\bigr\|_{H^{1/2-\mu/2}(\Sigma)}\\
&\leq C\bigl\|\gamma_{D}(|f|_{r}-|f|)\big|\bigr\|_{H^{1/2-\mu^{\prime}/2}(\Sigma)}\\
&\leq C\bigl\||f|_{r}-|f|\bigr\|_{H^{1-\mu^{\prime}/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq C|r|^{\mu^{\prime}/2}\big\||f|\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\end{split}

where (
3.3
) was used in the last step. By combining this with Lemma
2.2
one finds that (
3.1
) is true for

. Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{A,Q}
by Lemma

, the claim follows for all

.
∎

4.
Consequences for the spectrum of
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}

Since convergence in the norm resolvent sense implies the convergence of the spectra of the associated operators, Theorem
1.1
allows to transfer results on the spectrum of
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
to the spectrum of
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
. If
\Sigma
is one
C^{2}
-hypersurface, some interesting consequences are elaborated in
[
4
, Section 3.3]
; in particular, in
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
results on the existence of eigenvalues of
H_{0,0,\alpha}
(for
A=Q=0
) in various situations are transferred to
H_{0,0,V_{\varepsilon}}
. In this section, some further consequences are discussed, which can not be obtained from the results in
[
4
]
due to the different assumptions there. Here, the focus lies in self-adjoint examples; however, since convergence in the norm resolvent sense also implies the convergence of the spectrum in the non self-adjoint setting, see for instance
[
27
, Section IV.3.5]
, also results on the spectrum of
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
for non-symmetric
\alpha
from, e.g.,
[
10
,
24
,
28
]
could be transferred.

The starting point in all of the examples below is the fact that, as a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, the discrete and the essential spectra of
H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}}
converge to the respective spectra of
H_{A,Q,\alpha}
. The claim in the following lemma follows from known results on the spectral convergence for families of operators that converge in the norm resolvent sense, see
[
27
, Section IV.3.5]
or
[
40
, Satz 9.24]
or also the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
for a more detailed argument.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A
and

be as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively,
\Sigma
be as in (
1.2
), and
V^{(k)}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p=\frac{n-1}{1-\gamma}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
be supported in
\Omega_{\beta}^{(k)}
. Assume additionally that
Q
and
V^{(k)}
are real-valued. Let for
\varepsilon_{0}
as in Theorem
1.1
the potential
V_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
1.9
) and

be the self-adjoint operator associated with the form

in (
1.10
). Eventually, let
\alpha
be as in (
1.11
) and

be the self-adjoint operator associated with the form

in (

). Then, the following is true:

(i)

\lambda\in\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(H_{A,Q,\alpha})
if and only of there exists a family of numbers
(\lambda_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
with
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\in\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}})
such that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\lambda
.

(ii)

\lambda\in\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(H_{A,Q,\alpha})
if and only of there exists a family of numbers
(\lambda_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}
with
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\in\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(H_{A,Q,V_{\varepsilon}})
such that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\lambda
.

In the following examples the potentials
V_{\varepsilon}
are constructed via piecewise constant functions
V^{(k)}
. So let us fix some notations that are the same in Propositions
4.2
–
4.4
. Let
\Sigma=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\Sigma^{(k)}
be as in (

), choose for each

\beta^{(k)}
as in Proposition
2.6
, and set
\beta:=\min\{\beta^{(1)},\dots,\beta^{(N)}\}
. Denote for

the
\beta
-neighborhood of
\Sigma^{(k)}
by
\Sigma_{\beta}^{(k)}
, i.e.
\Sigma_{\beta}^{(k)}=\iota^{(k)}(\Sigma^{(k)}\times(-\beta,\beta))
, where
\iota^{(k)}
is the map from (
2.8
) for
\Sigma^{(k)}
, and define for
\alpha<0
the functions
V^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2\beta}\alpha\chi_{\Sigma_{\beta}^{(k)}}
, where

denotes the characteristic function for
\Sigma_{\beta}^{(k)}
. Moreover, introduce for

First, a result from

is used and conditions that ensure that the discrete spectrum of a magnetic Schrödinger operator with a homogeneous magnetic field

is non-empty are discussed, if the electric potential is supported in a small neighborhood of a wedge of opening angle

Note that this

falls into the class of networks considered in this paper, as it is the union of two rays
\Sigma^{(1)}
and

with joint endpoint at the origin.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\alpha<0
. In the situation described above, set

F_{\phi,\alpha}(x,y):=1+\frac{x^{4}}{4}-x^{2}\Theta_{\delta,\phi}+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi}}xe^{-y^{2}\tan^{2}(\phi/2)}(1+\textup{erf}(y)),

where

and

is the error function. If
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small and
\inf_{x,y\in(0,\infty)}F_{\phi,\alpha}(x,y)<0

\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(H_{A,0,V_{\varepsilon}})\cap(-\infty,\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(H_{A,0,V_{\varepsilon}}))\neq\emptyset.

In particular, this is the case, if one of the following conditions hold:

(a)

\phi\in(0,\frac{1}{3}\pi]
and
|\alpha|
is sufficiently small.

(b)

\phi\in(0,\frac{1}{8}\pi]
and
|\alpha|
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

First, taking the given assumptions into account, there exists a point
\lambda_{0}\in\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(H_{A,0,\alpha})\cap(-\infty,\Theta_{\delta,\phi})
, see
[
20
, Theorem 3 and Corollary 2]
(note that
\beta
in
[

]
is equal to

). Since by Lemma

one has
\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(H_{A,0,V_{\varepsilon}})\rightarrow\Theta_{\delta,\phi}
and there exists
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\in\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(H_{A,0,V_{\varepsilon}})
such that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
, as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, one concludes that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}<\inf\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(H_{A,0,V_{\varepsilon}})
.
∎

with a

, be fixed and

is as

be as described after Lemma

be constructed in the same way, but with the network

with the same

As

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha<0
. In the situation described above, for all

sufficiently small

and

have negative eigenvalues
\lambda_{\varepsilon}(\Sigma):=\inf\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(H_{0,0,V_{\varepsilon}})

and equality is achieved if and only if
\Sigma
and

Proof.

First, by

\delta

) is true, and that equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
and
\Gamma

∎

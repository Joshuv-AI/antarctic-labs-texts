Degree sequences realizing labelled perfect matchings
Joseph Briggs, Jessica McDonald, Songling Shan

Abstract
Let n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} and d 1 ≥ d 2 ≥ d n ≥ 1 d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq d_{n}\geq 1 be integers. There is characterization of when ( d 1 , d 1 , … , d n ) (d_{1},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}) is the degree sequence of a graph containing a perfect matching, due to results of Lovász (1974) and Erdős and Gallai (1960). But which perfect matchings can be realized in the labelled graph? Here we find the extremal answers to this question, showing that the sequence ( d 1 , d 2 , … , d n ) (d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}) : (1) can realize a perfect matching iff it can realize { ( 1 , n ) , ( 2 , n − 1 ) , … , ( n / 2 , n / 2 + 1 ) } \{(1,n),(2,n-1),\ldots,(n/2,n/2+1)\} , and; (2) can realize any perfect matching iff it can realize { ( 1 , 2 ) , ( 3 , 4 ) , … , ( n − 1 , n ) } \{(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(n-1,n)\} . Our main result is a characterization of when (2) occurs, extending the work of Lovász and Erdős and Gallai. Separately, we are also able to establish a conjecture of Yin and Busch, Ferrera, Hartke, Jacobsen, Kaul, and West about packing graphic sequences, establishing a degree-sequence analog of the Sauer-Spencer packing theorem. We conjecture an h h -factor analog of our main result, and discuss implications for packing h h disjoint perfect matchings.

Introduction

All graphs in this paper are assumed to be simple.

Consider a weakly decreasing sequence
d_{1}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}
of non-negative integers for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If there exists a graph
G
where
V(G)=[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
\deg_{G}(i)=d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, then we say that
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is
graphic
, and that
G

realizes

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
; we also refer to
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
as the
degree sequence
of
G
. We say that
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})

can realize a perfect matching
if there exists a graph
G
which realizes
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
and such that
G
contains a perfect matching. The following two theorems together answer the question of when
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
can realize a perfect matching.

Theorem 1

(Lovász
[
6
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}
be non-negative integers. Then
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
can realize a perfect matching iff
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
and
(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{n}-1)
are both graphic.

Theorem 2

(Erdős–Gallai
[
4
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}
be non-negative integers. The sequence
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
is graphic iff
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
is even and for every
k\in[n]
:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\leq k(k-1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\min\{d_{i},k\}.

(1)

The above theorems tell us when
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
can realize a perfect matching, but they do not say anything about
which
perfect matchings are realized in the labelled graph. Given the complete graph
K_{n}
on the vertex set
[n]
(with

even) we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{n}
the set of all perfect matchings in
K_{n}
. We use the notation
(i,j)
to refer to the edge between vertex
i
and vertex
j
, so in particular the following are two different elements of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
:

\displaystyle M^{-}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(1,n),(2,n-1),\ldots,(n/2,n/2+1)\};

\displaystyle M^{+}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(n-1,n)\}.

Given a graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
, we may ask if a particular
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
exists in
G
. We may also ask when
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})

can realize some particular

M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, that is, when there exists a graph
G
with
V(G)=[n]
,
\deg_{G}(i)=d_{i}
, and
G
contains the perfect matching
M
.

In this paper we find that the perfect matchings
M^{-}
and
M^{+}
are extremal in terms of being realizable.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}\geq 1
be integers.

(a)

The sequence
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
can realize a perfect matching iff it can realize
M^{-}
.

(b)

The sequence
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
can realize any
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
iff it can realize
M^{+}
.

Theorems
1
and
2
together provide a characterization of when (a) occurs. Our main result in this paper is the following extension of this earlier work of Lovász and Erdős–Gallai, which characterizes when (b) occurs.

Theorem 4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{n}\geq 1
be integers. The sequence
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
can realize
M^{+}
iff
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
is even,
n
is even, and for every
k\in[n]
:

\mathop{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}d_{i}\leq\begin{cases}k(k-1)+\mathop{\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}}\min\{d_{i}-1,k\}&\textrm{if $k$ is even}\\
k(k-1)+\min\{d_{k+1},k\}+\mathop{\sum_{i=k+2}^{n}}\min\{d_{i}-1,k\}&\textrm{if $k$ is odd}.\end{cases}

(*)

Our proof of Theorem
4
is inductive (in particular, it is inspired by a proof of Theorem
2
due to Choudum
[
3
]
) and leads to a polynomial-time algorithm for constructing a realization of
M^{+}
. Our argument for Theorem
3
(b) also provides a polynomial-time algorithm that transforms one realization to another. So, meeting the conditions of Theorem
4
means that not only does a realization exist for any
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, but that we can construct it in polynomial time.

As a corollary to Theorem
4
, we can obtain the following sufficient condition for realizing

.

Corollary 5
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
be a graphic sequence, with

an even integer and
d_{n}\geq n/2
.
If
\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}d_{i}\leq(\sqrt{2-(4/n)}-0.5)\tfrac{n^{2}}{2}

then the sequence
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})

.

Recall that every
n
-vertex graph

with
\delta(G)\geq\tfrac{n}{2}
and

even has a perfect matching (e.g. apply Driac’s Theorem to get a Hamilton cycle and then take every second edge in the cycle). When

\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}d_{i}\leq(.9)\tfrac{n^{2}}{2}
, i.e., we want the first half of the degree sum to be no more than about 90% of what it could possibly be (if all these degrees were

). This bound is asymptotically best possible: we will later demonstrate a degree sequence which just barely fails this condition but cannot realize

.

Two graphic sequences
(d_{1}^{1},\ldots,d_{n}^{1})

(d_{1}^{2},\ldots,d_{n}^{2})
are said to

if there are edge-disjoint graphs

and

on the same vertex set

and

if they can be expressed as edge-disjoint subgraphs of the complete graph

. In 1978, Sauer and Spencer
[

established that

.
We are able to fully verify the following conjecture, establishing a degree sequence analog of the Sauer-Spencer packing theorem.

Conjecture 6

(Yin
[
9
]
; Busch, Ferrera, Hartke, Jacobsen, Kaul, and West
[
2
]
)
.

Let

be an integer, and let
(d_{1}^{1},\ldots,d_{n}^{1})

(d_{1}^{2},\ldots,d_{n}^{2})

d_{n}^{1},d_{n}^{2}\geq 1

Conjecture

was first posed by Busch, Ferrera, Hartke, Jacobsen, Kaul, and West
[

]
with

[

disproved this initial version, but proposed replacing

Our paper proceeds as follows. We prove Theorem
4
in Section 2. Corollary
5
is proved in Section 3, where the above-mentioned tightness example is also given. We confirm Conjecture

in Section 4. Note that this confirmation requires a result on the

of a graph by Kang and Tokushige
[

, which will be discussed in Section 4. Section 5 contains a proof of Theorem
3
, which we handle using the language of posets. We provide detailed examples for the family

in this section, and make a poset conjecture about the family. In the 6th and final section of this paper we conjecture a generalization of our main result, Theorem

, from the realm of perfect matchings to that of
h
-factors (spanning

-regular subgraphs) for any

. We show that a direct analog of Theorem

disjoint perfect matchings.

Packing of graphic n-tuples

The binding number
\bi(G)
of a graph
G
is defined as

\min\left\{\frac{|N_{G}(X)|}{|X|}:X\subseteq V(G),N_{G}(X)\neq V(G)\right\}.

For a graph
G
and a function

, an
f
-factor
of
G
is a spanning subgraph
H
of

satisfying
d_{H}(v)=f(v)
for each

. The following result will be helpful for us.

Theorem 7

(Kano and Tokushige
[
5
]
)
.

Let
a
and
b
be integers such that
1\leq a\leq b
and
b\geq 2
, and let
G
be a connected simple graph with order
n
with
n\geq\frac{(a+b)^{2}}{2}
. Let
f:V(G)\rightarrow[a,b]
be a function such that

\sum_{v\in V(G)}f(v)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
. If one of the following two conditions is satisfied,
then G has an
f
-factor.

•

\bi(G)\geq\frac{(a+b-1)(n-1)}{an-(a+b)+3}
.

•

\delta(G)\geq\frac{bn-2}{a+b}
.

We confirm Conjecture
6
as follows.

Theorem 8
.

Let

be an integer, and let
(d_{1}^{1},\ldots,d_{n}^{1})
and
(d_{1}^{2},\ldots,d_{n}^{2})

(d_{1}^{2},\ldots,d_{n}^{2})

be a realization of

on
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

\overline{G}
, which has degree sequence

Let

For any

Lemma 16
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be an even integer, let

be integers, and suppose that
G

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})

and

each of which is isomorphic to

for some integer

.
Then there is a sequence of switches which maintains the degree sequence of

and keeps the subgraph

unchanged, but which transforms

into a graph on

as a subgraph.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction on

.
Let

be a triangle in

and

be a triangle in

If there is a matching of size 2 in the bipartite graph

;
and are done by applying the induction hypothesis on

and
H_{2}-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}

.

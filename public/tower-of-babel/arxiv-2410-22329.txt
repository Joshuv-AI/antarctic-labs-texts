The Chow and augmented Chow polynomials of Uniform Matroids
Elena Hoster

Abstract
. We provide explicit combinatorial formulas for the Chow polynomial and for the augmented Chow polynomial of uniform matroids, thereby proving a conjecture by Ferroni. These formulas refine existing formulas by \Citeauthor Hampe_2017 and by \Citeauthor eur2023stellahedralgeometrymatroids, offering a combinatorial interpretation of the coefficients based on Schubert matroids. As a byproduct, we count Schubert matroids by rank, number of loops, and cogirth.



1.
Introduction and main results

The Chow polynomial
\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{M}(x)
and the augmented Chow polynomial
\operatorname{H}_{M}(x)
of a matroid
M
are the Hilbert-Poincaré series of the Chow ring and of the augmented Chow ring, respectively.
These polynomials are known to be unimodal, palindromic, and
\gamma
-positive,
as proven in
[
7
]
.
Both polynomials are also conjectured to be real-rooted, which is only known for the augmented Chow polynomials of
uniform matroids

[
7
]
.
A second proof of the
\gamma
-positivity, along with a combinatorial formula for the
\gamma
-expansion of both
\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{M}(x)
and
\operatorname{H}_{M}(x)
,
is provided in
[
15
]
.

Let
U_{k,n}
be the uniform matroid on ground set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
and of rank
k
,
with bases being all
k
-element subsets.
As shown in
[
7
, Theorem 1.11]
, the coefficients of the Chow polynomial and of the augmented Chow polynomial of an arbitrary loopless matroid
M
are term-wise maximized when
M
is a uniform matroid.

This paper proves a conjecture by Ferroni regarding the coefficients in the case of uniform matroids and provides a monomial expansion for their Chow and augmented Chow polynomials.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
, Conjecture]
)
.

The Chow polynomial and the augmented Chow polynomial of the uniform matroid
U_{k,n}
are given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{U_{k,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\#\big\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{loopless Schubert matroids on the ground set }[n]\\
\text{of rank $m+1$ and cogirth greater than }n-k\end{subarray}\ \big\}\cdot x^{m}\,

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}_{U_{k,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\#\big\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\phantom{loop} Schubert matroids\phantom{less} on the ground set }[n]\\
\text{of rank \phantom{+}$m$\phantom{1} and cogirth greater than }n-k\end{subarray}\ \big\}\cdot x^{m}\,.

\Citeauthor

Hampe_2017 showed in
[
10
]
that the Chow polynomial of the Boolean matroid

is the

-vector of the permutahedron, the

-th Eulerian polynomial

.
Moreover, he showed that the

counts loopless Schubert matroids on

.
Likewise,
\Citeauthor

Here, the coefficients count Schubert matroids of corresponding rank but are not necessarily loopless.

Theorem

refines both of these representations.

In
[

]
,
\Citeauthor

This formula is particularly useful in the cases

1.1.
Main results

For any nonempty subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, consider the disjoint partition of
I=I_{1}\cup\dots\cup I_{s}
into maximal consecutive subsets such that
\min(I_{j})<\min(I_{j+1})
.
Define the multinomial coefficient

\binom{n}{\Delta I}=\binom{n}{\min(I_{1})-1,\min(I_{2})-\min(I_{1}),\min(I_{3})-\min(I_{2}),\dots,\min(I_{s})-\min(I_{s-1})}\,,

which takes the differences of these minima of adjacent subsets.
For
I=\{\}
being the empty set, we set
\binom{n}{\Delta I}=1
.

Example 1.2
.

The set
I=\{2,3,5,7,8\}
is partitioned into
\{2,3\}\cup\{5\}\cup\{7,8\}
, the multinomial coefficient is

\binom{n}{\Delta I}=\binom{n}{2-1,5-2,7-5}=\binom{n}{1,3,2}=\frac{n!}{1!\ 3!\ 2!\ (n-6)!}\,

for
n\geq 8
.

A
descent
in a sequence of integers

is a position

such that
a_{i}>a_{i+1}
. The

of

is the set of all such positions,
\operatorname{Des}(a)=\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}\;|\;a_{i}>a_{i+1}\right\}
. Its size gives the number of descents, denoted by
\operatorname{des}(a)
.
This notation also applies to permutations when written in one-line notation.
Let
\operatorname{E}(m,D)
denote the number of permutations in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{m}
that have descent set
D
.

Define
\mathsf{nc}(m)
to be the set of all subsets of
\{1,\dots,m\}
that contain
n
o
c
onsecutive integers.

Theorem 1.3
.

The Chow polynomial of the uniform matroid
U_{k,n}
is given by any of the following expansions:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{U_{k,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}\\

(1.2)

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathsf{nc}(k-1)\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}\\
\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)\in\mathsf{nc}(k-1)\\

In the same way, we get a similar version of

2.
Background

2.1.
R
-labeling

Let
P=(P,\leq)
be a finite graded poset of rank
n
with minimal element
\widehat{0}
and maximal element
\widehat{1}
.
The set of cover relations (or edges) of
P
is denoted by
\mathcal{E}(P)
.
A maximal chain in
P
is a set
\mathcal{F}=\{\widehat{0}=F_{0}<F_{1}<\dots<F_{n}=\widehat{1}\}
.
For a labeling
\lambda:\mathcal{E}(P)\to\mathbb{Z}
on the set of edges of
P
and for a maximal chain
\mathcal{F}
,
let
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
with
\lambda_{i}=\lambda(F_{i-1}<F_{i})
be its edge-labeling sequence .
We call
\lambda
an
R
-labeling

if every interval of
P
admits a unique maximal chain
\mathcal{F}
with strictly increasing edge-labeling sequence
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}=(\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\dots<\lambda_{n})
.

2.2.
Matroids

A
matroid

M
consists of a finite set
E
(called
ground set
) and a collection
\mathcal{B}
of subsets of
E
(called
bases
), satisfying the following two properties:

•

\mathcal{B}
is nonempty, and

•

if
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
are bases and if
x\in B_{1}\setminus B_{2}
,
then there is an element
y\in B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
such that
(B_{1}\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}\in\mathcal{B}
is a basis.

Every basis has the same size, called the
rank
of
M
denoted by
\operatorname{rk}(M)
.
A subset
I\subseteq E
is called
independet
if it is a subset of a basis.
Otherwise, it is called
dependent
.
The
dual matroid

M^{\ast}
of
M
is the matroid on the same ground set
E
and with bases
\left\{B^{c}=E\setminus B\;|\;B\text{ basis of }M\right\}
.

An element
x\in E
is a
loop
in
M
if it is not contained in any basis.
A
coloop
in
M
is a loop in the dual matroid
M^{\ast}
.

A
circuit

C\subseteq E
is a minimal dependent set of
M
, that is,
C\setminus\{x\}
is independent for all
x\in C
.
The
girth
of
M
is the cardinality of the smallest circuit,
and the
cogirth
of
M
is the girth of
M^{\ast}
.

The
rank
of a subset
S\subseteq E
is
\operatorname{rk}_{M}(S)=\max\left\{\#I\;|\;I\subseteq S\text{ independent in }M\right\}
, the size of the largest independent subset contained in
S
.
If
\operatorname{rk}_{M}(S\cup\{x\})>\operatorname{rk}_{M}(S)
for every
x\in E\setminus S
, we call
S
a
flat
.
The set of flats of
M
ordered by inclusion forms a lattice, the
lattice of flats
denoted by
\mathcal{L}(M)
.

Let
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
, and let
\binom{[n]}{k}
be the set of all subsets of
[n]
that have size
k
.

2.2.1.
Uniform matroids

For
n\geq k
, the
uniform matroid

U_{k,n}
is the matroid on the ground set
[n]
with set of bases
\binom{[n]}{k}
.
Its lattice of flats
\mathcal{L}(U_{k,n})
consists of all subsets of size smaller than
k
together with the maximal element
[n]
, ordered by inclusion.

2.2.2.
Schubert matroids

We use the definition of Schubert matroids given in
[
6
]
for the fixed ground set
[n]
.
For
\pi\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, let
\leq_{\pi}
be the total order on
[n]
given by the one-line notation of
\pi
, that is

\pi(1)\leq_{\pi}\pi(2)\leq_{\pi}\dots\leq_{\pi}\pi(n)\,.

If
\pi=\operatorname{id}
is the identity permutation, this gives the usual total order
1<2<\dots<n
.
For subsets
I=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\},J=\{j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\}\subseteq[n]
with
|I|=|J|
,
we write

I\leq_{\pi}J\quad\text{ if }i_{m}\leq_{\pi}j_{m}\text{ for each }m\in\{1,\dots,k\}\,.

For a set
I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
, and for a permutation
\pi\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
,
the
Schubert matroid

\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}
is the matroid on the ground set
[n]
with bases

\left\{J\in\binom{[n]}{k}\;\big|\;I\leq_{\pi}J\right\}\,.

Schubert matroids are a special class of
lattice path matroids
, sometimes also called
nested matroids

[
10
]
,
generalized Catalan matroids

[
2
]
or
shifted matroids

[
1
]
.

In

3.1.
Multivariate Chow and augmented Chow polynomials

The lattice of flats
\mathcal{L}(M)
of a matroid
M
always admits an
R
-labeling
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
.
Fix such an
R
-labeling
\lambda
, then, by
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, the Chow polynomial of
M
is given by

(3.1)

\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{M}(x)=\sum_{\mathcal{F}}x^{\operatorname{des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})}\cdot(1+x)^{\operatorname{rk}(M){-1}-2\cdot\operatorname{des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})}\,,

where the sum ranges over all maximal chains
\mathcal{F}
whose edge-labeling sequence
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}

has no consecutive descents,
\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})\in\mathsf{nc}(k-1)
, and with
1\notin\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})
.

The augmented Chow polynomial of
M
is given by

(3.2)

\operatorname{H}_{M}(x)=\sum_{\mathcal{F}}x^{\operatorname{des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})}\cdot(1+x)^{\operatorname{rk}(M)-2\cdot\operatorname{des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})}\,,

where the sum ranges over all maximal chains
\mathcal{F}
whose edge-labeling sequence
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}

has no consecutive descents,
\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})\in\mathsf{nc}(k-1)
.

Define the
multivariate Chow polynomial
of
M
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{M}(\boldsymbol{x})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{F}\\
1\notin\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{F})\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i\in\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})}x_{i}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}\\
i,i+1\notin\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})\end{subarray}}(1+x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N}[x_{1},\dots,x_{\operatorname{rk}(M)-1}]

and define the
multivariate augmented Chow polynomial
of
M
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}_{M}(\boldsymbol{x})=

\displaystyle\quad\ \sum_{\mathcal{F}}\quad\left(\prod_{i\in\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})}x_{i}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}\\
i,i+1\notin\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})\end{subarray}}(1+x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N}[x_{0},\dots,x_{\operatorname{rk}(M)-1}]

Here, both sums range over all maximal chains
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{L}(M)
such that
\operatorname{Des}(\lambda_{\mathcal{F}})\in\mathsf{nc}(k-1)
contains no consecutive elements.
Setting all
x_{i}=x
, we get back the usual Chow and augmented Chow polynomials of
M
, respectively, as given in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

Remark 3.1

(A natural choice for the multivariate version)
.

Chow polynomials and augmented Chow polynomials are evaluations of the
Poincaré-extended
\boldsymbol{ab}
-index

[
15
, Theorem 2.6]
,
which is a polynomial in the variable
y
with coefficients in the non-commutative ring
\mathbb{Z}\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\rangle
.
This polynomial was introduced in
[
3
]

and encodes the positions of ascents and descents of edge-labeling sequences of maximal chains.
Applying the evaluation approach from
[
15
]
to the identity given in
[
3
, Corollary 2.11]
, and distinguishing descents by their position, we derive the multivariate forms presented here.

3.2.
Eulerian numbers and subset permutations

An
R
-labeling on
\mathcal{L}(M)
can be constructed by any total order on the atoms of
\mathcal{L}(M)
,
see
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
.
For the uniform matroid
U_{k,n}
, the usual total order
1<\dots<n
yields the
R
-labeling
\lambda
, defined by

(3.3)

\lambda(S\prec T)=\min(T\setminus S)\,.

In particular, the entries in the sequence
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
for a maximal chain
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{L}(U_{k,n})
are all different.

A
subset permutation

\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(S)
is a permutation of a subset
S\subseteq[n]
.
For a subset
{S=\{s_{1}<\dots<s_{k}\}}
, the one-line notation of
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{k}
is given by
\sigma_{i}=\sigma(s_{i})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The map
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
sending maximal chains in
\mathcal{L}(U_{k,n})
to their edge-labeling sequence with labels defined in (
3.3
)
is injective.
Its image is the following set of subset permutations:

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[n]\\
|S|=k\end{subarray}}\left\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(S)\;\big|\;\{1,\dots,\sigma(\max S)\}\subseteq S\right\}\,.

Proof.

A maximal chain in
\mathcal{L}(U_{k,n})
is of the form

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\phantom{\big|}\{\}\subset\{\lambda_{1}\}\subset\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}\subset\dots\subset\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k-1}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\ \right\}

for pairwise distinct integers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k-1}
.
Its edge-labeling sequence is
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k})
with
\lambda_{k}=\min\left([n]\setminus\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k-1}\}\right)

being determined by the preceding labels.
This gives the surjection
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Moreover, the first
k-1
entries in
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
of a chain
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{0}\subset\dots\subset F_{k-1}\subset[n]\}

specify the order in which the elements from
\mathcal{F}_{k-1}
are adjoined along
\mathcal{F}
.

Vice versa, let the first
k-1
entries of a permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(S)
for a set
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
determine the first
k-1
labels
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k-1}
of an edge-labeling sequence
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Since the
k
-th entry
\lambda_{k}
of this sequence is the smallest integer in
[n]
which has not appeared yet in
\lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
, we have
\sigma_{k}=\lambda_{k}
if and only if for every integer
i<\sigma_{k}
there is an index
j\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
such that
\sigma_{j}=i
, and thus
i\in S
.
∎

Let
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{k}\in\operatorname{Sym}(S)
be a subset permutation of a subset
S\subseteq[n]
.
Then, we can

•

extend

\sigma
to the permutation
{\sigma}^{\uparrow}=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{k}t_{1}\dots t_{n-k}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
with
\{t_{1}<\dots<t_{n-k}\}=S^{c}
, or

•

standardize

\sigma
to the permutation
\sigma_{\downarrow}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}
by sending
\sigma_{i}
to its
image under the unique order-preserving map
S\to\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Observe that both extending and standardizing preserves descents.

Example 3.3
.

Let
n=8
, and let

3.3.
Schubert matroids

Recall
\Citeauthor
Priv_Comm_Ferroni’s conjecture stated in
Theorem

1.1
, saying that the coefficient of

in the Chow and in the augmented Chow polynomials of the uniform matroid

are given by

\displaystyle[x^{m}]\operatorname{\underline{H}}_{U_{k,n}}(x)

\displaystyle=\#\big\{\begin{subarray}{c}\ \text{loopless Schubert matroids on the ground set }[n]\\
\text{of rank $m+1$ and cogirth greater than }n-k\end{subarray}\ \big\}\quad\text{for }0\leq m\leq k-1\,,

\displaystyle[x^{m}]\operatorname{H}_{U_{k,n}}(x)

\text{of rank \phantom{+}$m$\phantom{1} and cogirth greater than }n-k\end{subarray}\ \big\}\quad\text{for }0\leq m\leq k\,.

In this section, we study the Schubert matroid on the fixed ground set
[n]
to determine the values of the right-hand side.

The Schubert matroid
\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}
for
I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
and
\pi\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
has bases
\left\{J\in\binom{[n]}{k}\;|\;I\leq_{\pi}J\right\}
.
The rank of
\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}
is
k
, the size of the set
I
,
but its loops and cogirth depend on both
I
and
\pi
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
n\geq k
be positive integers, and let
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\pi\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
.
Let
\min_{\pi}(I)
denote the minimal, and let
\max_{\pi}(I)
denote the maximal element in
I
with respect to
\leq_{\pi}
.

1.

\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}
has loops
\{\pi(1),\pi(2),\dots,\pi(m-1)\}
where
\pi(m)=\min_{\pi}(I)
.

2.

\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}
has cogirth
n+1-c
where
\pi(c)=\max_{\pi}(I)
.

3.

\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}=\mathcal{S}_{\pi^{-1}(I),\operatorname{id}}
with
\pi^{-1}(I)=\left\{\pi^{-1}(i)\;|\;i\in I\right\}
.

Proof.

An element
x\in[n]
is a loop in
\mathcal{S}_{I,\pi}
if
x
is smaller than every element in
I
, which proves the first statement.
The total order
\leq_{\pi}
is defined by
i<_{\pi}j
if
\pi^{-1}(i)<\pi^{-1}(j)
in the usual order
1<2<\dots<n
. The third statement follows immediately by this definition, since
J
is a basis in
S_{I,\pi}
if and only if
\pi^{-1}(J)
is a basis in
S_{\pi^{-1}(I),\operatorname{id}}
.

To prove the second statement, first consider the identity permutation
\pi=\operatorname{id}
.
The girth of a matroid
M
gives the size of the smallest possible dependent set in
M
. Any subset of size smaller than the girth is therefore independent, meaning that it is contained in some basis of
M
.
Thus, the Schubert matroid
\mathcal{S}_{I,\operatorname{id}}
has cogirth greater than
n-c
if and only if every subset
S\subseteq[n]
of size
|S|=c
contains a basis
J
of
\mathcal{S}_{I,\operatorname{id}}
.
In particular, the set
S=\{1,\dots,c\}
must then contain a subset
J
with
J\geq I
,
which gives
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,c\}
.

Vice versa, if
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,c\}
has size
|I|=m

and if
S=\{s_{1}<\dots<s_{c}\}\subseteq[n]
is a subset of size
c
, then let
J=S\setminus\{s_{1},\dots,s_{c-m}\}
be the set containing the
m
largest elements of
S
.
Since
i\leq s_{i}
for all indices
i
, we get

I\leq\{c-m+1,\dots,c\}\leq\{s_{c-m+1},\dots,s_{c}\}=J\text{ with }J\subseteq S\,.

This proves, that the cogirth of
\mathcal{S}_{I,\operatorname{id}}
is greater than
n-c
if and only if
\max(I)\leq c
.
Together with the contraposition of this statement and with the third statement of our lemma, we have proven the second.
∎

. A permutation
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
is called Grassmannian if it has at most one descent.

Corollary 3.9
.

For
k\geq 2
, the coefficient
\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(1)}
is the number of Grassmannian permutations of length
n
avoiding a (fixed) permutation
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}
with
\operatorname{des}(\sigma)=1
.

Proof.

This follows by
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
, which provides the same formula as given here for
\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(1)}
.
∎

For fixed
k\leq 10
, the sequences
(\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(1)})_{n\geq k}
are listed in the OEIS,
e.g.,
A000124
for
k=3
,
A050407
for
k=4
,
A027927
for
k=5
, and
A362193
for
k=6
.

The formulas that we get from
Theorem

3.5
(with
x_{i}=x
) for the coefficients
\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(m)}
and
c_{\ k,n}^{(m)}
indicate,
that the coefficients give the size of a set.
For instance, for
m=2
, we have

\displaystyle\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=3}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-2}\binom{n}{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-3}\sum_{j=2}^{k-i+1}\binom{n}{i,j,n-i-j}\,,

\displaystyle c_{\ k,n}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\sum_{j=2}^{k-i}\binom{n}{i-1,j,n+1-i-j}\,.

Note that the sequences
(\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(2)})_{n\geq k}
and
(c_{\ k,n}^{(2)})_{n\geq k}
are not listed in the OEIS, and the same applies for
m>3
.
This leads to the following open problem:

Open Problem
.

Describe
\underline{c}_{\ k,n}^{(m)}
and
c_{\ k,n}^{(m)}
as sizes of sets of permutations of length
n
according to certain restrictions.

Refinements of Artin’s primitive root conjecture
Leo Goldmakher, Greg Martin, Paul Péringuey

Abstract
. A famous conjecture of Artin asserts that any integer a a that is neither − 1 -1 nor a square should be a primitive root (mod p p ) for a positive proportion of primes p p . Moreover, using a heuristic argument, Artin guessed an explicit formula for the proportion; this formula is well-supported by computations and is known to hold on a generalized Riemann hypothesis, but remains open. In this paper we propose several conjectures that capture the finer properties of the distribution of the order of a a (mod p p ) as p p varies over primes; these assertions contain Artin’s original conjecture as a special case. We prove these conjectures assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis, as well as weaker versions unconditionally.



1.
Introduction

Given an integer
a
and a prime
p
, let
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
denote the multiplicative order of
a
modulo
p
. Exploring the distribution of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
, for
a
fixed as
p
varies, is a classical subject:

in 1927, Emil Artin proposed a heuristic argument that for any fixed integer
a\neq-1
that is not a perfect square,
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)=p-1
for a positive proportion of primes
p
.
His key insight was that this problem is most naturally viewed through the lens of algebraic number theory.
Artin knew that
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)=p-1
if and only if
p
fails to split completely in the cyclotomic-Kummer extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[q]{a},\zeta_{q})
for all primes
q
.
On the other hand, the prime ideal theorem implies that the density of those primes that split completely in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[q]{a},\zeta_{q})
is

{1}/{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[q]{a},\zeta_{q}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}={1}/{q(q-1)}
.
Making the seemingly innocuous assumption that the events “
p
does not split completely in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[q]{a},\zeta_{q})
” are independent, Artin predicted that the proportion of primes
p
for which
a
is a primitive root should be

\prod_{q\text{ prime}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\right)\approx 0.3739558,

which is now known as Artin’s constant.

Extensive computational work by Emma Lehmer and Derrick H. Lehmer provided evidence that Artin’s conjecture was correct for some values of
a
but not for others, prompting Artin to write
“I was careless but the machine caught up with me.”
He realized what was going wrong in his original heuristic: for all prime
a\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
we have
\sqrt{a}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{a})
, whence

{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[2]{a},\zeta_{2})\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[a]{a},\zeta_{a})
. In particular, any
p
that does not split in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[2]{a},\zeta_{2})
cannot split in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[a]{a},\zeta_{a})
either, so the events “
p
does not split completely in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[q]{a},\zeta_{q})
” are not independent after all.

Artin predicted that there were no other dependences among splitting in the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[q]{a},\zeta_{q})
whenever
a
is prime.
In the preface to his collected works, Artin’s former students Lang and Tate wrote down a modified version of Artin’s conjecture
(working independently, Heilbronn made the same conjecture in 1968).
To state the corrected version of Artin’s conjecture, we require the following notation:

Definition 1.1
.

For any nonzero rational number
g
, define
s(g)
to be the
squarefree kernel
of
g
, which is the sign of
g
times the product of all primes dividing
g
to an odd power, or equivalently the unique squarefee integer such that
gs(g)
is the square of a rational number, or equivalently the unique squarefee integer such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{g})={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{s(g)})
. Also define
\mathfrak{d}(g)
to be the
discriminant
of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{g})
, so that

\mathfrak{d}(g)=\begin{cases}s(g),&\text{if }s(g)\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
4s(g),&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Artin’s conjecture, which can be stated for rational numbers as well (since there are only finitely many primes for which the multiplicative order is not defined), was proved by Hooley
[
6
]
under the assumption of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for the Dedekind zeta functions of the cyclotomic-Kummer extensions mentioned earlier. It takes the following form if we assume that
a
is not a perfect power (see Theorem
5.1
below for a more general version):

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume GRH. If
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}
is not a perfect power, then

(1.1)

\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\#\{p\leq x\colon a\text{ is a primitive root (mod $p$)}\}\sim F_{a}\prod_{q}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\biggr),

where

F_{a}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\mathfrak{d}(a)\not\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
\displaystyle 1-\prod\limits_{q\mid\mathfrak{d}(a)}\frac{-1}{q^{2}-q-1},&\text{if }\mathfrak{d}(a)\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.3
.

A different expression for the density on the right-hand side of equation (
1.1
), which is simpler to write down but harder to use, is

(1.2)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(\ell)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell})]:{\mathbb{Q}}]}

Our goal in the present work is to formulate several conjectures which subsume Artin’s conjecture and produce predictions about finer aspects of the distribution of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
as
p
varies.
As a prototype of our results, consider the simplest case of Artin’s conjecture, namely that the proportion of primes
p
for which
2
is a primitive root is Artin’s constant
\prod_{q}\Bigl(1-{1}/{q(q-1)}\Bigr)
.
Consider the deformation

\prod_{q}\Bigl(1-(1-z)/{q(q-1)}\Bigr)
of this product, which admits a power series in
z
whose constant term is precisely Artin’s constant; in other words, the constant term measures the density of primes
p
for which

(p-1)/{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(2)}=1
.
The starting point of our investigation is the observation that the other power series coefficients also have relatively simple interpretations in terms of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(2)
: the coefficient of
z^{n}
is the density of those primes
p
for which
(p-1)/{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(2)}
has precisely
n
distinct prime factors.
Below, we will generalize this observation considerably (to rational
a
and to other possible deformations of the product), prove our generalizations on GRH, and obtain weaker versions of our conjectures unconditionally.

Throughout this article,
p
and
q
denote prime numbers, and
a
denotes a rational number not equal to
-1
,
0
, or
1
.
We define
\nu_{p}(a)
to be the unique integer
\nu
such that
a=p^{\nu}\frac{m}{n}
where
p
divides neither
m
nor
n
. In other words, if
p
divides the numerator of
a
(including
a
itself if
a
is an integer) then
\nu_{p}(a)
is the power to which it divides the numerator, while if
p
divides the denominator of
a
then
-\nu_{p}(a)
is the power to which it divides the denominator.
We use the standard notation
\omega(n)
for the number of distinct prime factors of
n
and
\Omega(n)
for the number of prime factors of
n
counted with multiplicity.

1.1.
Statement of results: generating functions

We compare
p-1
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
in three ways. The first way is to count the number of distinct prime factors of their quotient, namely
\omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
, which is the same as the number of distinct prime factors of the index of the subgroup
\langle a\rangle
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
. As this quantity is a nonnegative integer, it makes sense to frame its limiting distribution in terms of a generating function; our first theorem gives an exact formula for that generating function.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume GRH. If
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}
is not a perfect power, then for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|z|\leq 1
,

\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}=F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)+{\mathit{O}}\biggl(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\biggr)

where

F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\mathfrak{d}(a)\not\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
1+\displaystyle\prod_{q\mid 2\mathfrak{d}(a)}\frac{z-1}{z+q^{2}-q-1},&\text{if }\mathfrak{d}(a)\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}.\end{cases}

.

The condition “
a
is not a perfect power” in the statement of Theorem

1.10
and
1.12
below.
The most general versions of these three theorems appear later as Theorems

5.7
, and
5.9
.

Remark 1.6
.

If we set

(see Remark

We give concrete examples of these generating functions for three specific values of

Assuming GRH, we have the following three special cases:

Remark 1.8
.

Note that the case

1.11
below.

Our second way of comparing
p-1
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
is the same as the first except that we count the prime factors of

with multiplicity. The formula becomes more complicated in a way that depends upon the factorization of

Notation 1.9
.

Given
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
, the symbols

•

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume GRH. If
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}

with

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\

1.2.
Statement of results: individual densities

A generating function for a nonnegative integer-valued statistic is valuable if we can extract from it information about its coefficients. We introduce the notation

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{a}^{\Omega}(n;x)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\Omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{a}^{\omega-}(n;x)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega(p-1)-\omega(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}.

for the coefficients of the polynomials on the right-hand sides, and their limiting values (assuming they exist)

\displaystyle D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)

(1.4)

\displaystyle D_{a}^{\Omega}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}D_{a}^{\Omega}(n;x)

\displaystyle D_{a}^{\omega-}(n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}D_{a}^{\omega-}(n;x).

So for example,
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
is the density of the set of primes
p
for which
\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))=n
. Assuming GRH, our theorems show that these densities do indeed exist for all
a\notin\{-1,0,1\}
.
Moreover, our generating function formulas allow numerical evaluation of the densities
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
,
D_{a}^{\Omega}(n)
, and
D_{a}^{\omega-}(n)
to high precision (see Section
6
for details and examples).

From their generating functions, we can also employ techniques from complex analysis to deduce decay rates for these densities. The following result is proved in Section

Corollary 1.14
.

Assume GRH. For any
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
:

(a)

D_{a}^{\Omega}(n)=R_{a}4^{-n}+O_{a}(9^{-n})
, where

R_{a}=\tfrac{3}{2}H_{h}^{\Omega}(4)\Bigl(1+I_{h}^{\Omega}(\gamma,4)\Bigr)\prod_{q\geq 3}\biggl(1+\frac{3q}{(q-1)(q^{2}-4)}\biggr)

and

and

are as in Definition

below.

(b)

Both
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)\leq n^{-(2-o(1))n}
and
D_{a}^{\omega-}(n)\leq n^{-(2-o(1))n}
(with the implied constants depending on
a
), with each of these upper bounds being attained infinitely often.

These densities represent the limiting probability that the given statistics take a certain value if we choose a prime
p
“at random”; in other words, for each statistic (and each value of

) there is a nonnegative integer-valued random variable whose distribution function is the same as the sequence of densities. Such a random variable has an expectation, which we can interpret as the average value of the statistic when we sample it over randomly chosen primes. We compute exact values and numerical approximations for these expectations in Section
7.2
.

All of the above results are conditional on GRH (just as Hooley’s proof of Artin’s conjecture is); however, we are able to obtain some unconditional results. More precisely, we can derive some unconditional inequalities for these densities and for the expectations of the three statistics. The following corollary gives two examples:

Let

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(F_{a}^{\omega-}(0)\prod_{q}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\biggr)+o(1)\biggr)\pi(x).

in the notation of Theorems
1.2
and
1.12
.
All implied constants may depend on

.

The first statement recovers a result of Vinogradov and Van der Waall from the 1970s, as described in

We give generalizations of this corollary in Theorems

2.
Extending Hooley’s combinatorics

In Hooley’s conditional proof of Artin’s conjecture
[
6
]
, he began with a combinatorial decomposition (similar to an inclusion–exclusion sieve) based on the primes dividing
p-1
that could obstruct
a
being a primitive root modulo
p
. Our work requires a similar combinatorial setup, with the additional complexity of needing a decomposition involving all powers of primes, rather than just the primes themselves. Moreover, the relationship among the “obstructions” indexed by powers of a given prime is important to the combinatorial setup. For these reasons, in Sections
2.1
and
2.2
we will describe these combinatorial setups in an axiomatic way, with the hope that other authors will find their generality useful. Afterwards, we will apply these setups to our three sets of counting functions in Section
2.3
.

Throughout Section
2
, when
h(m)
is a multiplicative function, we let

(2.1)

g(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)h(m/d)\quad\text{and}\quad g^{*}(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid m\\
(d,m/d)=1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\omega(d)}h(m/d)

denote its Dirichlet inverse and its unitary inverse, respectively. Also, for any positive real number
\xi
, we define
Q_{\xi}
to be the least common multiple of all positive integers less than or equal to
\xi
.

Furthermore, throughout Section
2
, let
\mathcal{U}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
be the “universe” of numbers under consideration.
For every prime power
q^{k}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
denote a subset of
\mathcal{U}
.
For later convenience, we also define
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{U}
. We can think of
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
as being a property that a natural number might or might not have; the letter
\mathcal{F}
is chosen to help us think of the numbers in
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
as having “failed” a test administered by
q^{k}
. We assume that every infinite intersection of various
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
is empty, so that any given integer
n
has only finitely many of the properties
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
.

2.1.
Cumulative properties

It might be the case that the collection
\{\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}\}
has the property that
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{q^{k-1}}
for all prime powers
q^{k}
, that is, numbers in our universe
\mathcal{U}
with property
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
automatically have property
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k-1}}
(and thus all properties
\mathcal{F}_{q^{j}}
with
0\leq j\leq k
, property
\mathcal{F}_{1}
being automatic). We call such collections “cumulative”, and for all positive integers
\ell
we use the following notation:

•

We define new sets
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}=\bigcap_{q^{k}\mid\ell}\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
(with the interpretation
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{U}
), so that a number
n
has property
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
if and only if it has property
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
for all prime powers
q^{k}\mid\ell
.
Note that such integers have
at least
those properties (hence the letter
\mathcal{A}
) but might have further properties as well, whether from higher powers of existing primes or from new primes.

•

We also define
A_{\ell}
to be the indicator function of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
(that is, the function defined by
A_{\ell}(n)=1
if
n\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
and
A_{\ell}(n)=0
if
n\notin\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
).

•

Furthermore, we define
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}=\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\setminus\bigcup_{q^{k}\nmid\ell}\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
, so that a number in
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
has
exactly
the properties
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
for all prime powers
q^{k}\mid\ell
and no other properties (hence the letter
\mathcal{E}
).

•

Define
e(n)
to be the unique number such that
n\in\mathcal{E}_{e(n)}
, which exists for all
n
by the infinite-intersection property and the cumulative property of the
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
; note that by cumulativeness,
n\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
if and only if
\ell\mid e(n)
. Further, define
e_{\xi}(n)=\gcd(e(n),Q_{\xi})
, which we think of as recording which tests up to
\xi
the number
n
fails.

•

Finally, define

N_{\xi}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}h(e_{\xi}(n))\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\ell}(x)=\#\{n\leq x\colon n\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}A_{\ell}(n).

In practice,
N_{y}(x)
will be the exact quantity we are interested in, while the
P_{\ell}(x)
will be quantities for which asymptotic estimates are available.
The following lemma will allow us to estimate
N_{\xi}(x)
in terms of these functions
P_{\ell}(x)
as well as the Dirichlet inverse
g(m)
of
h(m)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that the collection
\{\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}\}
is cumulative.
Let
h(m)
be a multiplicative function bounded in absolute value by
1
. For any positive real numbers
\xi<y
,

N_{y}(x)=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}g(\ell)P_{\ell}(x)+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq y}P_{q^{k}}(x)\bigg).

Proof.

Given the definition (
2.1
) of
g(m)
, Möbius inversion yields

(2.2)

h(e_{\xi}(n))=\sum_{\ell\mid e_{\xi}(n)}g(\ell)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid e(n)\\
\ell\mid Q_{\xi}\end{subarray}}g(\ell)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\\
\ell\mid Q_{\xi}\end{subarray}}g(\ell)=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}g(\ell)A_{\ell}(n),

and then summing over
n\leq x
with
n\in\mathcal{U}
gives

(2.3)

N_{\xi}(x)=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}g(\ell)P_{\ell}(x).

Next suppose that
\eta<\theta
and that there is exactly one prime power
q^{k}
in the interval
(\eta,\theta]
. In this situation,
h(e_{\theta}(n))=h(e_{\eta}(n))
when
n\notin\mathcal{A}_{q^{k}}
, and thus

N_{\theta}(x)-N_{\eta}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}\Big(h(e_{\theta}(n))-h(e_{\eta}(n))\Big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{A}_{q^{k}}\end{subarray}}\Big(h(e_{\theta}(n))-h(e_{\eta}(n))\Big).

Since the summand is at most
2
in absolute value, this identity gives

N_{\theta}(x)-N_{\eta}(x)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{A}_{q^{k}}\end{subarray}}1=P_{q^{k}}(x).

If we now partition
(\xi,y]
into intervals of the form
(\eta,\theta]
that each contain exactly one of the prime powers
q^{k}
in
(\xi,y]
, summing over the resulting partition yields

N_{y}(x)-N_{\xi}(x)\ll\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq y}P_{q^{k}}(x),

which establishes the lemma when combined with the identity (
2.3
).
∎

2.2.
Disjoint properties

On the other hand, it might be the case that the collection
\{\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}\}
has the property that
\mathcal{F}_{q^{j}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}=\emptyset
for all primes
p
and all distinct positive integers
j
and
k
, that is, numbers with the property
\mathcal{F}_{q^{j}}
cannot also have the property
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
for any positive
j\neq k
. We call such collections “disjoint”, and for all positive integers
\ell
we use the following notation:

•

We define new sets
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}=\bigcap_{q^{k}\parallel\ell}\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
(with the interpretation
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{1}=\mathcal{U}
), so that a number
n
has property
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}
if and only if it has property
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
for all prime powers
q^{k}
exactly dividing
\ell
. (Recall that
q^{k}\parallel\ell
means that
q^{k}\mid\ell
but
q^{k+1}\nmid\ell
.) Note that such integers have
at least
those properties but might have further properties as well from powers of other primes, although not from other powers of primes already dividing
\ell
due to disjointness.

•

We also define
A^{*}_{\ell}
to be the indicator function of
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}
(that is, the function defined by
A^{*}_{\ell}(n)=1
if
n\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}
and
A^{*}_{\ell}(n)=0
if
n\notin\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}
).

•

Furthermore, we define
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\ell}=\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}\setminus\bigcup_{q^{k}\nparallel\ell}\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
, so that a number in
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\ell}
has
exactly
the properties
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
for all prime powers
q^{k}\parallel\ell
and no other properties.

•

Define
e^{*}(n)
to be the unique number such that
n\in\mathcal{E}^{*}_{e^{*}(n)}
, which exists for all
n
by the infinite-intersection property and the disjoint property of the
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
; note that by disjointness,
n\in\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\ell}
if and only if
\ell
is a unitary divisor of
e^{*}(n)
, that is, if and only if
\ell\mid e^{*}(n)
and
(\ell,e^{*}(n)/\ell)=1
. Further, define
e^{*}_{\xi}(n)=\gcd(e^{*}(n),Q_{\xi})
.

•

Finally, define

N^{*}_{\xi}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}h(e^{*}_{\xi}(n))\quad\text{and}\quad P^{*}_{\ell}(x)=\#\{n\leq x\colon n\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}\}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}A^{*}_{\ell}(n).

The following lemma will allow us to estimate
N^{*}_{\xi}(x)
in terms of these functions
P^{*}_{\ell}(x)
as well as the unitary inverse
g^{*}(m)
of
h(m)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that the collection
\{\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}\}
is disjoint.
Let
h(m)
be a multiplicative function bounded by
1
in absolute value. For any positive real numbers
\xi<y
,

N^{*}_{y}(x)=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}g^{*}(\ell)P^{*}_{\ell}(x)+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq y}P^{*}_{q^{k}}(x)\bigg).

Proof.

In the context of unitary Dirichlet convolution, the function
(-1)^{\omega(n)}
is the analogue of the Möbius function (see
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
):

(2.4)

g^{*}(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid m\\
(d,m/d)=1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\omega(d)}h(m/d)\iff h(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid m\\
(d,m/d)=1\end{subarray}}g^{*}(d).

By this unitary inversion formula,

(2.5)

h(e^{*}_{\xi}(n))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid e^{*}_{\xi}(n)\\
(\ell,e^{*}_{\xi}(n)/\ell)=1\end{subarray}}g^{*}(\ell)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid e^{*}(n)\\
(\ell,e^{*}_{\xi}(n)/\ell)=1\\
\ell\mid Q_{\xi}\end{subarray}}g^{*}(\ell)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\ell}\\
\ell\mid Q_{\xi}\end{subarray}}g^{*}(\ell)=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}g^{*}(\ell)A^{*}_{\ell}(n);

and then summing over
n\leq x
with
n\in\mathcal{U}
yields

(2.6)

N^{*}_{\xi}(x)=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}g^{*}(\ell)P^{*}_{\ell}(x).

Next suppose that
\eta<\theta
and that there is exactly one prime power
q^{k}
in the interval
(\eta,\theta]
. In this situation,
h(e^{*}_{\theta}(n))=h(e^{*}_{\eta}(n))
when
n\notin\mathcal{A}^{*}_{q^{k}}
, and thus

N^{*}_{\theta}(x)-N^{*}_{\eta}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}\Big(h(e^{*}_{\theta}(n))-h(e^{*}_{\eta}(n))\Big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{q^{k}}\end{subarray}}\Big(h(e^{*}_{\theta}(n))-h(e^{*}_{\eta}(n))\Big).

Since the summand is at most
2
in absolute value, this identity gives

N^{*}_{\theta}(x)-N^{*}_{\eta}(x)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{A}^{*}_{q^{k}}\end{subarray}}1=P^{*}_{q^{k}}(x).

If we now partition
(\xi,y]
into intervals of the form
(\eta,\theta]
that each contain exactly one of the prime powers
q^{k}
in
(\xi,y]
, summing over the resulting partition yields

N_{y}(x)-N_{\xi}(x)\ll\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq y}P^{*}_{q^{k}}(x),

which establishes the lemma when combined with the identity (
2.6
).
∎

We remark that both formulas in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
involve finite sums of finite counting functions (for any given
\xi
,
x
, and
y
), and so no convergence arguments are needed.

2.3.
Our three applications

In Hooley’s conditional proof of Artin’s conjecture for
a
, his method amounts to applying Lemma
2.1
in the case where
h(m)
is the indicator function of
m=1
, the universe
\mathcal{U}
is the set of primes not dividing the numerator or denominator of
a
(we write
\nu_{p}(a)=0
for this condition), and

(2.7)

\mathcal{F}_{q}=\{p\in\mathcal{U}\colon p\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $q$)}},\,a\text{ is a $q$th power}{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $p$)}}\},\quad\text{while }\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}=\emptyset\text{ for }k\geq 2.

For our refinements of Artin’s conjecture, we will use
h(m)=z^{\omega(m)}
or
h(m)=z^{\Omega(m)}
, and these sets
\mathcal{F}_{q}
or variants thereof, in such a way that the functions
N_{\xi}(x)
treated in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
become the left-hand sides of the asymptotic formulas in Theorems
1.4
,
1.10
, and
1.12
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
and let
z
be a complex number with
|z|\leq 1
. For any positive real numbers
\xi<x
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\\
+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\bigg).

Proof.

In preparation for applying Lemma
2.1
, we use the same universe
\mathcal{U}=\{p\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0\}
and subsets (
2.7
) as Hooley did (noting that these
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}
are indeed cumulative), but we use the strongly multiplicative function
h(m)
with
h(q^{k})=z
for all prime powers
p^{k}
. (In fact, since
\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}=\emptyset
for
k\geq 2
, the values of
h(q^{k})
on those proper prime powers are actually irrelevant.)
We calculate that
g(q)=z-1
while
g(q^{k})=0
for
k\geq 2
, and so
g(m)=\mu^{2}(m)(z-1)^{\omega(m)}
.

Crucially, since
p\in\mathcal{F}_{q}
if and only if
q\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
, we see that
e(p)
is the largest squarefree divisor of
(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
. Since
e_{x}(p)=e(p)
for all
p\leq x
, we deduce that
h(e_{x}(p))=z^{\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}
. Moreover, when
\ell
is squarefree we see that
p\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
if and only if
\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
, and thus

P_{\ell}(x)=\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\quad\text{when $\ell$ is squarefree}

and
P_{\ell}(x)=0
when
\ell
is not squarefree.
The proposition now follows immediately from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

In Proposition
2.5
, taking the limit as
z\to 0
(or equivalently setting
z=0
with the convention that
0^{0}=1
) recovers Hooley’s formula

\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)=1\}=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\mu^{2}(\ell)(-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\\
+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\bigg).

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
and let
z
be a complex number with
|z|\leq 1
. For any positive real numbers
\xi<x
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\Omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\\
+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\bigg).

Proof.

In preparation once again for applying Lemma
2.1
, we use the same universe
\mathcal{U}=\{p\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0\}
but the new sets

\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}=\{p\in\mathcal{U}\colon p\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $q^{k}$)}},\,a\text{ is a $q^{k}$th power}{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $p$)}}\},

which we note are again cumulative. We also change to the completely multiplicative function
h(m)
with
h(q^{k})=z^{k}
, so that
g(q^{k})=z^{k}-z^{k-1}=z^{k}(1-z^{-1})
and therefore
g(m)=z^{\Omega(m)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(m)}
.

In this case, we see that
e(p)=e_{x}(p)
is exactly
(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
, and therefore
h(e_{x}(p))=z^{\Omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}
. Moreover, we see that

P_{\ell}(x)=\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}

now regardless of whether
\ell
is squarefree. The proposition now follows immediately from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

In the proof of the next proposition (as well as later in this paper), we use the notation
{\mathbbm{1}}(S)
to denote the indicator function of the statement
S
, so that
{\mathbbm{1}}(S)=1
if
S
is true and
{\mathbbm{1}}(S)=0
if
S
is false.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|z|\leq 1
,
x
and
\xi
positive real numbers with
\xi<x
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega(p-1)-\omega(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\sum\limits_{m\mid\ell}\mu(m)\#\Big\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a),\ m\ell\mid(p-1)\Big\}

\displaystyle+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq x}\#\Big\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a),\,q^{k}\parallel(p-1)\Big\}\bigg).

Proof.

We now prepare to apply Lemma
2.2
, using the same universe
\mathcal{U}=\{p\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0\}
as before but now defining

\mathcal{F}_{q^{k}}=\{p\in\mathcal{U}\colon q^{k}\parallel(p-1),\,a\text{ is a $q^{k}$th power}{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $p$)}}\},

which we note are disjoint. We revert to the strongly multiplicative function
h(m)
with
h(q^{k})=z
for all
k\geq 1
, and we calculate that
g^{*}(q^{k})=z-1
and thus
g^{*}(m)=(z-1)^{\omega(m)}
.

Crucially, we see that
e^{*}(p)=e^{*}_{x}(p)
is the largest divisor of
(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
that is a unitary divisor of
p-1
. It follows that
\omega(e^{*}_{x}(p))=\omega(p-1)-\omega(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))
and therefore
h(e^{*}_{x}(p))=z^{\omega(p-1)-\omega(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}
. Furthermore,

P^{*}_{\ell}(x)=\#\Big\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a),\,(\ell,(p-1)/\ell)=1\Big\}.

However, when
\ell\mid(p-1)
, note that

{\mathbbm{1}}\Bigl(\bigl(\ell,(p-1)/\ell\bigr)=1\Bigr)=\sum_{m\mid(\ell,(p-1)/\ell)}\mu(m)=\sum_{m\mid\ell}\mu(m){\mathbbm{1}}\Bigl(m\ell\mid(p-1)\Bigr);

consequently,

P^{*}_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{m\mid\ell}\mu(m)\#\Big\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a),\,m\ell\mid(p-1)\Big\}.

The proposition now follows immediately from Lemma
2.2
, once we note (for the error term) that a prime power
q^{k}
dividing
p-1
satisfies
(q^{k},(p-1)/q^{k})
if and only if
q^{k}\parallel(p-1)
.
∎

3.
The connection to cyclotomic-Kummer extensions

In light of the propositions in the previous section, it is important to study the quantity

(3.1)

P_{a,\ell,m}(x)=\#\Big\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a),\ m\ell\mid(p-1)\Big\}.

This is the quantity appearing in the main term of Proposition
2.6
; moreover, the special case
m=1
is also the quantity appearing in the main terms of Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
). We immediately rewrite this quantity as

(3.2)

P_{a,\ell,m}(x)=\#\Big\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\text{$p$ splits completely in ${\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\bigr)$})\Big\}.

This approach is the one used by Hooley
[
6
]
, although he needed only the special case where
\ell
is squarefree and
m=1
. The fact that these two quantities are identical follows from
[
11
, Proposition 8]
; in that work this fact is stated under the assumption that
\ell
is squarefree (since that was the only case required for the application), but the proof therein actually works for arbitrary
\ell
.

The expression (
3.2
) for
P_{a,\ell,m}(x)
can be estimated using the Chebotarev density theorem (indeed, in this particular case of splitting completely, it can be derived directly from the prime ideal theorem). We take exactly this approach in Section
3.3
; but first we need to understand the degrees (and bound the discriminants) of the cyclotomic-Kummer extensions
{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\bigr)
; we address these two requirements in Sections
3.1
and
3.2
, respectively.

3.1.
Degrees of cyclotomic-Kummer extensions

In the case
m=1
, the degree of the pure Kummer extension
{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}\bigr)
was computed by Wagstaff (
[
15
, Propostion 4.1]
). For the cyclotomic-Kummer extensions
{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\bigr)
with
\ell
squarefree, the degree was computed by the third author
[
11
]
. In this section we carry out the analogous calculation for arbitrary
\ell
and
m
. To do so, we first need to generalize a result of Schinzel
[
14
, Lemma 4]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix positive integers
m
and
{\ell}
.
A rational number
\beta
is of the form
\gamma^{\ell}
for some
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
if and only if one of the following sets of conditions is satisfied:

•

{\ell}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
and
\beta=c^{\ell}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.

•

{\ell}\equiv 0{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
,
\beta>0
, and
\beta=c^{{\ell}/2}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\sqrt{c}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.

•

{\ell}\equiv 0{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
,
\beta<0
,
2\mid m
, and
\beta=-c^{{\ell}/2}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\sqrt{c}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.

•

{\ell}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
,
\beta<0
,
2\nmid m
, and
\beta=c^{{\ell}/2}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\sqrt{c}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.

•

{\ell}\equiv 4{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $8$)}}
,
\beta<0
,
2\nmid m
, and
\beta=-(2c)^{{\ell}/2}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\sqrt{c}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that each set of conditions does imply that
\beta
has the required form. For the converse, we suppose that
\beta
is indeed of the form
\gamma^{\ell}
with
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
. We write
\beta=\textup{sgn}(\beta)\beta_{1}^{t}
where
\beta_{1}
a positive rational number that is not a perfect power.

First suppose that
\textup{sgn}(\beta)=1
. Then
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
and thus
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}})
is normal over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Furthermore, the polynomial

X^{{\ell}/({\ell},t)}-\beta_{1}^{t/({\ell},t)}
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and has
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}
as one of its roots, and therefore all its
{\ell}/({\ell},t)
roots (which are equally spaced around a circle in
{\mathbb{C}}
) must lie in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}})
. But
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}})
is a real subfield of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
, which forces
X^{{\ell}/({\ell},t)}-\beta_{1}^{t/({\ell},t)}
to have at most two roots; it follows that
{\ell}/({\ell},t)\leq 2
, which implies that
{\ell}\mid 2t
.

•

If
{\ell}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
, then
{\ell}\mid t
and so
c=\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
\beta=c^{\ell}
.

•

If
{\ell}\equiv 0{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
, then
c=\beta_{1}^{2t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
\beta=c^{{\ell}/2}
and
\sqrt{c}=\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.

Now suppose that
\textup{sgn}(\beta)=-1
. Then
\zeta_{2{\ell}}\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
where
\zeta_{2{\ell}}
is a primitive
2{\ell}
th root of unity.

•

If
{\ell}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
, then
\zeta_{2{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{\ell}})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
, so
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
and hence (as in the first case)
c=\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
\beta=c^{\ell}
.

•

If
{\ell}\equiv 0{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
and
2\mid m
, then
\zeta_{2{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
, so
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
and hence (as in the first case)
c=\beta_{1}^{2t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
\beta=c^{{\ell}/2}
and
\sqrt{c}=\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.

The remaining case is where
\textup{sgn}(\beta)=-1
and
{\ell}\equiv 0{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $2$)}}
but
2\nmid m
.
Then
\zeta_{2{\ell}}\notin{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
so
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\notin{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
. On the other hand.
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2{m\ell}})
, so
{\ell}\mid 2t
as in the first case; but since
{\ell}\mid t
would imply
\beta_{1}^{t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
, we conclude that
t\equiv\frac{{\ell}}{2}{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ ${\ell}$)}}
. It follows that
\sqrt{\beta_{1}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2{m\ell}})
and
\sqrt{\beta_{1}}\notin{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{{m\ell}})
.

Writing
s(\beta_{1})
for the squarefree kernel of
\beta_{1}
, we deduce from
[
14
, Lemma 3]
that
s(\beta_{1})\mid{m\ell}
and that either
{m\ell}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
and
s(\beta_{1})\equiv 3{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
, or
{m\ell}\equiv 4{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $8$)}}
and
2\mid s(\beta_{1})
. By the same lemma:

•

when
{m\ell}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
and
s(\beta_{1})\equiv 3{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
, we also have
{\ell}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
, and
c=-\beta_{1}^{2t/{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
\beta=c^{{\ell}/2}
and
\sqrt{c}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
;

•

when
{m\ell}\equiv 4{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $8$)}}
and
2\mid s(\beta_{1})
, we also have
{\ell}\equiv 4{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $8$)}}
, and
c=-(\beta_{1})^{2t/{\ell}}/2\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
\beta=-(2c)^{{\ell}/2}
and
\sqrt{c}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})
.
∎

We are now able to generalize Wagstaff’s argument. Recall the notation from Definition
1.1
and Notation
1.9
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
. Let
\ell
and
m
be positive integers, and set
\ell^{\prime}=\ell/(\ell,h)
. Let
\zeta_{m\ell}
be a primitive
(m\ell)
th root of unity.
Then
\displaystyle\left[{\mathbb{Q}}\Big(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\Big):{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=\frac{\ell^{\prime}\varphi(m\ell)}{\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)}
,
where
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)
is defined as follows:

•

If
a>0
, then
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }2\mid\ell^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid m\ell,\\
1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

•

If
a<0
and
\ell
is odd, then
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)=1
.

•

If
a<0
and
\ell
is even but
\ell^{\prime}
is odd, then
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }2\mid m,\\
1/2,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

•

If
a<0
and
\ell^{\prime}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
, then
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }2\mid m\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid m\ell,\\
2,&\text{if }2\nmid m\text{ and }\ell\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(-a_{0})\mid m\ell,\\
2,&\text{if }2\nmid m\text{ and }\ell\equiv 4{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $8$)}}\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(2a_{0})\mid m\ell,\\
1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

•

If
a<0
and
4\mid\ell^{\prime}
, then
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid m\ell,\\
1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Set
L={\mathbb{Q}}\Big(\zeta_{m\ell}\Big)
.
We start by observing that

\displaystyle\left[{\mathbb{Q}}\Big(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\Big):{\mathbb{Q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[{\mathbb{Q}}\Big(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\Big):L\right]\times\left[L:{\mathbb{Q}}\right]=\left[{\mathbb{Q}}\Big(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\Big):L\right]\varphi({m\ell}).

Furthermore, if we set
n=\min\Bigl\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon a^{j/\ell}\in L\Bigr\}
, we claim that
\left[{\mathbb{Q}}\Big(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\Big):L\right]=n
.
To see this, note that
n\mid\ell
and that
a
has order
n
in
L^{*}/\left(L^{*}\right)^{n}
, and thus
[L(\sqrt[n]{a}):L]=n
by
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
. But now
[L(\sqrt[\ell]{a}):L]\leq n
since
(\sqrt[\ell]{a})^{n}\in L
, and also
L(\sqrt[n]{a})\subset L(\sqrt[\ell]{a})
, and therefore
[L(\sqrt[\ell]{a}):L]=n
as claimed.

It remains to show that
n
, which is the smallest positive integer such that
a^{n}=\gamma^{\ell}
for some
\gamma\in L
, equals
\ell^{\prime}/\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)
. To do so we determine the smallest even and smallest odd such numbers
n
, which we denote by
n_{e}
and
n_{o}
, respectively; then
n=n_{e}
if
n_{o}
is not defined, while
n=\min\{n_{e},n_{o}\}
otherwise.
We use Lemma
3.1
to carry out these computations. We do the computations completely in the case
a<0
; the case
a>0
is simpler and analogous.

We first note that
a^{n_{e}}>0
since
n_{e}
is even. If
\ell
is odd then
n_{e}=2\ell^{\prime}
. Suppose that
\ell
is even, so that
\ell/(\ell,2h)\mid n_{e}
and
\sqrt{a_{0}^{2hn_{e}/\ell}}\in L
(by Lemma
3.1
).

•

If
2\mid 2hn_{e}/\ell
, then
n_{e}=\ell^{\prime}
if
\ell^{\prime}
is even and
n_{e}=2\ell^{\prime}
if
\ell^{\prime}
is odd.

•

If
2\nmid 2hn_{e}/\ell
, then
\sqrt{a_{0}^{2hn_{e}/\ell}}\in L
if and only if
\nu_{2}(\ell)>\nu_{2}(h)+1
and
\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid{m\ell}
.

We conclude that

n_{e}=\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime}/2,&\text{if }\nu_{2}(\ell)>\nu_{2}(h)+1\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid{m\ell},\\
2\ell^{\prime},&\text{if }\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\nu_{2}(h),\\
\ell^{\prime},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now we note that
a^{n_{o}}<0
since
n_{o}
is odd. If
\ell
is odd then
n_{e}=\ell^{\prime}
. Suppose that
\ell
is even. If
2\mid m
, then
\ell/(\ell,2h)\mid n_{o}
and therefore
\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\nu_{2}(h)+1
(or else
n_{o}
would be even). In other words,

n_{o}=\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime},&\text{if }\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\nu_{2}(h),\\
\ell^{\prime}/2,&\text{if }\nu_{2}(\ell)=\nu_{2}(h)+1\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid{m\ell}.\end{cases}

If
\ell\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
and
2\nmid m
, then

n_{o}=\ell^{\prime}/2\text{if }\nu_{2}(\ell)=\nu_{2}(h)+1\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(-a_{0})\mid{m\ell}

and otherwise
n_{0}
is undefined.
Finally, if
\ell\equiv 4{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $8$)}}
and
2\nmid m
, then

n_{o}=\ell^{\prime}/2\text{if }\nu_{2}(\ell)=\nu_{2}(h)+1\text{ and }\mathfrak{d}(2a_{0})\mid{m\ell}

and otherwise
n_{0}
is undefined.

In all cases we see that
n=\min\{n_{e},n_{o}\}
(or
n=n_{e}
if
n_{0}
is undefined) matches the value of
\ell^{\prime}/\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)
that arises from the definition of
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)
, which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

3.2.
Bounding discriminants of cyclotomic-Kummer extensions

Using the formula for the degree computed in Proposition
3.2
, we are now able to provide the following upper bound for the associated discriminant.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
m
and
{\ell}
be integers and let
a
be a nonzero rational number. Let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[{\ell}]{a},\zeta_{m\ell})
, where
\zeta_{m\ell}
is a primitive
{m\ell}
th root of unity. Then the discriminant
\Delta_{K}
of
K
satisfies

\log|\Delta_{K}|\ll_{a}[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]\log{m\ell}.

Proof.

We know that
[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]=\varphi({m\ell}){\ell}^{\prime}
with
{\ell}^{\prime}=[K:{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})]
. Let
a^{\prime}
be the unique
{\ell}^{\prime}
-free integer such that
a/a^{\prime}
is a perfect
{\ell}^{\prime}
th power, and set
\kappa=(a^{\prime})^{1/{\ell}^{\prime}}
.
Then

\{\kappa^{d}\zeta_{m\ell}^{g}\colon 0\leq d<{\ell}^{\prime},\,0\leq g<{m\ell},\,(g,{m\ell})=1\}

forms a basis of
K
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Moreover, any
\sigma\in\textup{Gal}(K;{\mathbb{Q}})
is determined by
\sigma(\kappa)
and
\sigma(\zeta_{m\ell})
:

•

\sigma(\kappa)
is a root of
X^{{\ell}^{\prime}}-a^{\prime}
, so
\sigma(\kappa)=\kappa\zeta_{m\ell}^{\lambda{m\ell}/{\ell}^{\prime}}
with
0\leq\lambda<{\ell}^{\prime}
;

•

\sigma(\zeta_{m\ell})
is a primitive
{m\ell}
th root of unity, so
\sigma(\zeta_{m\ell})=\zeta_{m\ell}^{\mu}
with
0\leq\mu<{m\ell}
and
(\mu,{m\ell})=1
.

Then
[
13
, Proposition 2.6.3]
tells us that

(3.3)

\Delta_{K}=\det\biggl(({\rm Tr}_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\kappa^{d_{1}}\zeta_{m\ell}^{g_{1}}\kappa^{d_{2}}\zeta_{m\ell}^{g_{2}}))_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq d_{1},d_{2}<{\ell}^{\prime}\\
0\leq g_{1},g_{2}<{m\ell}\\
(g_{1}g_{2},{m\ell})=1\end{subarray}}\biggr).

For given
\delta
and
\gamma
with
(\gamma,{m\ell})=1
, we compute

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\kappa^{\delta}\zeta_{m\ell}^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{0\leq\lambda<{\ell}^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\mu<{m\ell}\\
(\mu,{m\ell})=1\end{subarray}}(\kappa\zeta_{m\ell}^{\lambda{m\ell}/{\ell}^{\prime}})^{\delta}(\zeta_{m\ell}^{\mu})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{0\leq\lambda<{\ell}^{\prime}}\kappa^{\delta}\zeta_{m\ell}^{\lambda\delta{m\ell}/{\ell}^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\mu<{m\ell}\\
(\mu,{m\ell})=1\end{subarray}}\zeta_{m\ell}^{\mu\gamma}=\begin{cases}{\ell}^{\prime}\kappa^{\delta}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\mu<{m\ell}\\
(\mu,{m\ell})=1\end{subarray}}\zeta_{m\ell}^{\mu\gamma},&\text{if}\ {\ell}^{\prime}\mid\delta,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that if we define

T=\Bigl({\rm Tr}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m\ell})/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\zeta_{m\ell}^{g_{1}}\zeta_{m\ell}^{g_{2}})\Bigr)_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq g_{1},g_{2}<{m\ell}\\
(g_{1}g_{2},{m\ell})=1\end{subarray}},

then the matrix in equation (
3.3
)
is block diagonal (after reordering its rows and columns) with one block equal to
{\ell}^{\prime}T
and
{\ell}^{\prime}-1
blocks equal to
a^{\prime}{\ell}^{\prime}T
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{K}|

\displaystyle=\Bigl(|a^{\prime}|^{{\ell}^{\prime}-1}\left({\ell}^{\prime}\right)^{{\ell}^{\prime}}\Bigr)^{\varphi({m\ell})}(\det T)^{{\ell}^{\prime}}\leq|a^{\prime}|^{\varphi({m\ell}){\ell}^{\prime}}\left({\ell}^{\prime}\right)^{\varphi({m\ell}){\ell}^{\prime}}{m\ell}^{\varphi({m\ell}){\ell}^{\prime}}\leq|a^{\prime}|^{[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]}{m\ell}^{2[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]}

since
{\ell}^{\prime}\mid{\ell}\mid{m\ell}
. We conclude that

\log|\Delta_{K}|\leq 2[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]\log{m\ell}+[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]\log|a^{\prime}|\ll_{a}[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]\log{m\ell}

as desired.
∎

3.3.
Asymptotic behavior of
P_{a,\ell,m}(x)

We now return to the estimation of
P_{a,\ell,m}(x)
from equation (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume GRH. Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
, and let
\ell
and
m
be positive integers. Then

P_{a,\ell,m}(x)=\frac{\pi(x)}{\Bigl[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\Bigl(\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)\Bigr)=\frac{\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)\cdot(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(m\ell)}\pi(x)+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\Bigl(\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)\Bigr),

where
\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)
and
h
were defined in Proposition
3.2
and Notation
1.9
, respectively.

Proof.

Lang
[
8
]
established, assuming GRH, the following effective prime ideal theorem for a number field
K
(a special case of a Chebotarev density theorem with effective error term): uniformly in
K
,

(3.4)

\pi_{K}(x)=\pi(x)+{\mathit{O}}\left(\sqrt{x}\log(x^{[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]}|\Delta_{K}|)\right),

where
\pi_{K}(x)=\#\{\mathfrak{p}\colon N(\mathfrak{p})\leq x\}
is the prime ideal counting function for
K
(and
\pi(x)=\pi_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)
is the ordinary prime counting function) and
\Delta_{K}
is the discriminant of
K
. If we take
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell})
, then Proposition
3.3
implies that

(3.5)

\pi_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell})}(x)=\pi(x)+{\mathit{O}}\Bigl(\Bigl[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr]\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)\Bigr).

Finally,
P_{a,\ell,m}(x)
is almost exactly
\pi_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell})}(x)/\Bigl[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr]
; the precise relationship, with a suitably bounded error term that is majorized by the error term above, works here exactly the same way as in Hooley’s work
[
6
, Section 4, equation (17)]
. We thus deduce that

P_{a,\ell,m}(x)=\frac{\pi(x)}{\Bigl[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\Bigl(\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)\Bigr),

and the second equality follows from Proposition
3.2
.
∎

4.
Dealing with error terms

We first deal with the error terms in Propositions
2.3
,
2.5
, and
2.6
; we may concentrate on the error term in Proposition
2.5
, as it is an upper bound for the error terms in the other two propositions. Our goal is to lower
\xi
enough so that summing the error term of Theorem
3.4
is admissible. In order to control this error term, we use the same techniques as used by Hooley
[
6
]
.

First we will see how low we can get
\xi
without using GRH. Using a combinatorial argument and the Brun–Titchmarsh theorem we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
.
For all
B\geq 0
,

\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\ll_{a,B}\frac{x\log\log x}{\log^{2}x}.

Proof.

For the largest prime powers, we see by writing
p-1=q^{k}m
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sqrt{x}\log x<q^{k}\leq x}

\displaystyle\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sqrt{x}\log x<q^{k}\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1),\,\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\mid(p-1)/q^{k}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sqrt{x}\log x<q^{k}\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1),\,a^{(p-1)/q^{k}}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $p$)}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m<\sqrt{x}/\log x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,a^{m}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $p$)}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\Omega\bigg(\prod_{m<\sqrt{x}/\log x}(a^{m}-1)\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\log\bigg(\prod_{m\leq\sqrt{x}/\log x}(a^{m}-1)\bigg)<\frac{\log a}{\log 2}\sum_{m<\sqrt{x}/\log x}m\ll_{a}\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}.

This is entirely correct if
a
is a positive integer; if
a=n/d
, then we should replace
a^{m}-1
with
n^{m}-d^{m}
, but this only affects the implicit constant in the final bound.

For medium-sized prime powers,

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\#\{p\leq x\colon q^{k}\mid(p-1)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\pi(x;q^{k},1).

Applying Brun–Titchmarsh, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\frac{x}{\varphi(q^{k})\log(x/q^{k})}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\frac{1}{q^{k}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\sum_{\frac{\sqrt{x}}{(\log x)^{B}}<q^{k}\leq\sqrt{x}\log x}\frac{\log(q^{k})}{q^{k}}

\displaystyle\ll_{B}\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\bigl(\log\log x+O(1)\bigr)

by Mertens’s formula.
∎

Assuming GRH we can get
\xi
even lower (exactly as in
[
6
]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume GRH. Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
and
B\geq 2
. For all
\xi\ll(\log x)^{B-1}
,

\sum\limits_{\xi\leq q^{k}<\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\left(\log x\right)^{B}}}\#\left(p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\right)\ll_{B}\frac{x}{\xi\log x}.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
3.4
with
\ell=q^{k}
and
m=1
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\leq q^{k}<\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\left(\log x\right)^{B}}}\#\left(p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\leq q^{k}<\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\left(\log x\right)^{B}}}\left(\frac{\pi(x)}{q^{2k-1}(q-1)}+{\mathit{O}}\bigl(\sqrt{x}\log x\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\xi\leq q^{k}<\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\left(\log x\right)^{A}}}\frac{1}{q^{2k}}+\frac{x}{(\log x)^{B}}

\displaystyle<\frac{x}{\log x}\sum\limits_{n>\xi}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{x}{(\log x)^{B}}\ll\frac{x}{\xi\log x}+\frac{x}{(\log x)^{B}},

and the first error term dominates by the assumption on
\xi
.
∎

When we incorporate Theorem
3.4
into Propositions
2.3
,
2.5
, and
2.6
, a new error term will appear from summing the error term from Theorem
3.4
over divisors of

, which we recall is the least common multiple of all the integers up to

. The following proposition bounds these errors.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
x\geq 3
and
A>0
be real numbers. Uniformly for
\xi\leq\frac{1}{2}\log x
and
|z|\leq A
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\mu^{2}(\ell)|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\sqrt{x}\log x\bigr)(2+A)^{\xi}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}|z|^{\Omega(\ell)}|1-z^{-1}|^{\omega(\ell)}\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\sqrt{x}\log x\bigr)(2+A)^{4\xi}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}\sum\limits_{m\mid\ell}\mu^{2}(m)\sqrt{x}\log(xm\ell)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\sqrt{x}\log x\bigr)(3+2A)^{\xi}.

Proof.

We first note the Chebyshev-style inequalities
\psi(t)=\sum_{n\leq t}\Lambda(n)<1.04t

[
12
, Theorem 12]
and
\pi(t)<1.26t/\log t

[
12
, Corollary 1]
, both valid for
t>1
. In particular,
Q_{\xi}=e^{\psi(\xi)}<e^{1.04\cdot\frac{1}{2}\log x}=x^{0.52}
, and thus
\log(x\ell)\ll\log x
and
\log(xm\ell)\ll\log x
in these sums. It remains to estimate the sums over
\ell
. For the first estimate,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\mu^{2}(\ell)|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}=\prod_{p\mid Q_{\ell}}(1+|z-1|)\leq\prod_{p\leq\xi}(2+A)\leq(2+A)^{\xi}.

For the second estimate, let
v_{p}=\lfloor\frac{\log\xi}{\log p}\rfloor
be the power of
p
dividing
Q_{\xi}
; then

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}|z|^{\Omega(\ell)}|1-z^{-1}|^{\omega(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\mid Q_{\xi}}\Bigl(1+|z-1|(1+|z|+\cdots+|z|^{v_{p}-1})\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{p\leq\xi}\biggl(1+(A+1)\frac{\log\xi}{\log p}\max\{1,A\}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{p\leq\xi}\biggl(1+\frac{(A+1)^{2}}{\log 2}\biggr)^{\log\xi}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(1+\frac{(A+1)^{2}}{\log 2}\biggr)^{1.26\xi}\leq(2+A)^{4\xi},

where the last inequality can be verified computationally.
Finally, for the third estimate,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}\sum\limits_{m\mid\ell}\mu^{2}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}(2|z-1|)^{\omega(\ell)}=\prod_{p\mid Q_{\ell}}(1+2|z-1|)\leq\prod_{p\leq\xi}(3+2A)\leq(3+2A)^{\xi}.\qed

Finally we are left with the sum over divisors of
Q_{\xi}
of the main term in Theorem
3.4
. But in order to be able to evaluate our different coefficients we would like the sum to be over all integers. It is easy to see that all integers smaller than
\xi
divide
Q_{\xi}
; therefore by replacing our sum over divisors of
\xi
by a sum over all integers, we are introducing an error which is at most the sum over all integers bigger than
\xi
. First a lemma, letting
\sigma(k)
be the sum of the positive divisors of
k
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\xi\geq 2
, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{k>\xi}\frac{2^{\omega(k)}\sigma(k)}{k^{2}\varphi(k)}\ll\frac{\log\xi}{\xi}
.

Proof.

Define the nonnegative multiplicative function
f(k)=2^{\omega(k)}\sigma(k)/\varphi(k)
, and set
F(t)=\sum_{k\leq t}f(k)
. We note that
f(p)=2(p+1)/(p-1)=2+O(\frac{1}{p})
and thus
\sum_{p\leq t}f(p)=2\pi(t)+O(\log\log t)
. Furthermore,
f(p^{r})=2(p^{r+1}-1)/p^{r-1}(p-1)^{2}<2p^{2}/(p-1)^{2}\leq 8
fot all prime powers
p^{r}
. It follows from the Wirsing–Odoni method (see for example
[
5
, Proposition 4]
) that
F(t)\sim C_{f}t\log t
for some positive constant
C_{f}
. Summing by parts, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{k>\xi}\frac{2^{\omega}(k)\sigma(k)}{k^{2}\varphi(k)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\frac{dF(t)}{t^{2}}=\frac{F(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}+2\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\frac{F(t)}{t^{3}}\,dt\ll\frac{\xi\log\xi}{\xi^{2}}+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\frac{t\log t}{t^{3}}\,dt\ll\frac{\log\xi}{\xi}

as claimed.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
and
\xi\geq 2
. Uniformly for
|z|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\frac{\log\xi}{\xi}\biggr)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\frac{\log\xi}{\xi}\biggr)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{\mu(m)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{\mu(m)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\frac{\log\xi}{\xi}\biggr).

Proof.

For the first claim, we start by noting that all integers up to
\xi
divide
Q_{\xi}
, and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O\biggl(\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus Q_{\xi}}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\sum_{\ell>\xi}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)2^{\omega(\ell)}}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\biggr)

by Proposition
3.2
. The error term is majorized by the sum in Lemma
4.4
since
\sigma(k)\geq k
, which establishes the first claim.
For the second claim, we similarly write

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O\biggl(\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus Q_{\xi}}\frac{|z|^{\Omega(\ell)-\omega(\ell)}|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\sum_{\ell>\xi}\frac{2^{\omega(\ell)}}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\biggr)

since
|z|\leq 1
, and again an appeal to Lemma
4.4
establishes the claim.
Finally, for the third claim we write

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{\mu(m)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{\mu(m)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+O\biggl(\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus Q_{\xi}}|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{|\mu(m)|}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}\biggr);

the error term can be estimated (using Proposition
3.2
again) as

\ll_{a}\sum_{\ell>\xi}|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{|\mu(m)|}{\ell\varphi(m\ell)}=\sum_{\ell>\xi}\frac{|z-1|^{\omega(\ell)}}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{|\mu(m)|}{m}\leq\sum_{\ell>\xi}\frac{2^{\omega(\ell)}}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\sum_{m\mid\ell}\frac{1}{m}=\sum_{\ell>\xi}\frac{2^{\omega(\ell)}}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\frac{\sigma(\ell)}{\ell},

which is again sufficient by Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
and
x\geq 3
. Uniformly for
|z|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}\right):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\biggl(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log^{2}x}\biggr)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\Omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}\right):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\biggl(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log^{2}x}\biggr)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega(p-1)-\omega(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\sum\limits_{m\mid\ell}\frac{\mu(m)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}\left(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}\right):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\biggl(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log^{2}x}\biggr).

Proof.

By adjusting the implicit constants if necessary, we may assume that
x\geq e^{18}
. Set
\xi=\frac{1}{9}\log x
, so that
\xi\geq 2
. We concentrate on establishing the middle formula, beginning with
Proposition
2.5
which says that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle z^{\Omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+O\bigg(\sum_{\xi<q^{k}\leq x}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,q^{k}\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}\bigg)

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}+O_{a}\biggl(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log^{2}x}\biggr)

by Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
with
B=2
. The main term can be written, using equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) and Theorem
3.4
, as

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}

\displaystyle z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}\#\{p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\}=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}P_{a,\ell,1}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}\biggl(\frac{\pi(x)}{\Bigl[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\Bigl(\sqrt{x}\log(x\ell)\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{\pi(x)}{\Bigl[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}\Bigr]}+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\Bigl(\sqrt{x}\log x\cdot 3^{4\xi}\Bigr)

by Proposition
4.3
with
A=1
. Since
3^{4\xi}=\exp(4\xi\log 3)\leq\exp(4\cdot\frac{1}{9}\log x\cdot\log 3)\leq\exp(0.49\log x)=x^{0.49}
, this last error term can be absorbed into the one in equation (
4.1
).
Finally, Proposition
4.5
allows us to rewrite this last main term as

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sum_{\ell\mid Q_{\xi}}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\pi(x)\frac{\log\xi}{\xi}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}+O_{a}\biggl(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log^{2}x}\biggr).

Inserting these estimates back into equation (
4.1
) establishes the middle claim of the proposition. The proofs of the first and last claims are exactly the same, using the other parts of the propositions and theorems referenced above.
∎

5.
Main terms and the general theorems

We are now ready to state and prove our most general results, which are Theorems
5.4
,
5.7
, and
5.9
below. It is straightforward to check that Theorems
1.4
,
1.10
, and
1.12
, respectively, are the special cases of these three general results where
h=1
.

The conclusion of Proposition
4.6
already reflects all the work we have done to bound the error terms in the desired asymptotic formulas, and so it remains only to make the main terms more explicit. If the field extension degree
[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]
were always equal to
\ell\phi(m\ell)
, then expressing each main term as an Euler product would take only a few lines (and there would be no need for correction factors depending on
a
in the statements of the theorems). However, Proposition
3.2
shows that the truth is more complicated, most notably because of the
\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,m\ell)
term; the general strategy we adopt to handle these terms appears in equation (
5.7
) below.

In the beginning of this section, we fully state all three general results (which are complicated enough to require several auxiliary pieces of notation). The proofs of Theorems
5.4
,
5.7
, and
5.9
will be given afterwards in Sections
5.1
,
5.2
, and
5.3
, respectively. For the purposes of comparison, we first give the analogous general statement for Artin’s primitive root conjecture, as established by Hooley
[
6
]
. Throughout this section we fix a rational number
a\notin\{-1,0,1\}
, and we use the quantities

from Notation

without further comment, as well as the function

from Definition

.

Definition 5.1
.

Given
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
, define

H_{a}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{q\mid{h}/{2}}\frac{q^{2}-2q}{q^{2}-q-1},&\text{if }a<0\text{ and }2\mid h,\\
\displaystyle\prod\limits_{q\mid h}\frac{q^{2}-2q}{q^{2}-q-1},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

q\nmid h\end{subarray}}\biggl(\frac{-1}{q^{2}-q-1}\biggr)\prod\limits_{q\mid(\mathfrak{d}(a),h)}\biggl(\frac{-1}{q-2}\biggr),&\text{if }\mathfrak{d}(a)\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\

In this notation, Hooley conditionally established Artin’s conjecture in the following form:

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume GRH. Let

is not a square. Let

\frac{1}{\pi(x)}{\mathcal{N}_{a}}(x)=F_{a}H_{a}\prod\limits_{q}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\biggl(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\biggr),

in the notation of Definition
5.1
.

We now state our three general results in full detail.

Definition 5.3
.

Given

.

Assume GRH. Let

(5.6)

\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega(p-1)-\omega(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}=H_{h}^{\omega-}(z)F_{a}^{\omega-}(h,z)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q^{2}-1}\biggl)+{\mathit{O}}_{a}\biggl(\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}\biggr)

in the notation of Definition
5.8
.

In the proofs that follow,
we will use a strategy spurred by the form of Proposition
3.2
, namely writing

(5.7)

\frac{1}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}=\frac{(\ell,h)\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)}{\ell\varphi(m\ell)}=\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(m\ell)}+\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(m\ell)}(\varepsilon_{a}(m\ell,\ell)-1);

the first summand leads to the main term, while the second summand leads to the correction factors depending on
a
.

5.1.
The statistic
\omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)

This statistic is by far the simplest of the three, as having
\ell
squarefree discards many cases in Proposition
3.2
and makes the products easier to compute. Throughout we will freely use the notation from Definition
1.1
, Notation
1.9
, and Definition
5.3
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Define

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\omega/}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
n\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}.

When
n
is squarefree,

S_{0}^{\omega/}(n)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\nmid h\end{subarray}}\frac{z-1}{q^{2}-q+z-1}\prod_{q\mid(n,h)}\frac{z-1}{(q-1)+(z-1)}\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\begin{cases}2z/(z+1),&\text{if }2\mid h,\\
1,&\text{if }2\nmid h.\end{cases}

Proof.

If we write
\ell=nk
, then
nk
must be squarefree to contribute to the sum due to the
\mu^{2}(\ell)
factor, which means that
(n,k)=1
. Therefore

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\omega/}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(nk)(z-1)^{\omega(nk)}\frac{(nk,h)}{nk\varphi(nk)}

\displaystyle=(z-1)^{\omega(n)}\frac{(n,h)}{n\varphi(n)}\sum_{(k,n)=1}\mu^{2}(k)(z-1)^{\omega(k)}\frac{(k,h)}{k\varphi(k)}

\displaystyle=(z-1)^{\omega(n)}\frac{(n,h)}{n\varphi(n)}\prod_{q\nmid n}\biggl(1+\frac{(z-1)(q,h)}{q(q-1)}\biggr).

The product can be written as

\displaystyle\prod_{q\nmid n}\biggl(1+\frac{(z-1)(q,h)}{q(q-1)}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)\prod_{q\mid h}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)^{-1}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q-1}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\nmid h\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)^{-1}\prod_{q\mid(n,h)}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q-1}\biggr)^{-1}.

We complete the proof by noting that

\prod_{q\mid h}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)^{-1}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q-1}\biggr)=H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\begin{cases}2z/(z+1),&\text{if }2\mid h,\\
1,&\text{if }2\nmid h\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle(z-1)^{\omega(n)}\frac{(n,h)}{n\varphi(n)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\nmid h\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)^{-1}\prod_{q\mid(n,h)}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q-1}\biggr)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\nmid h\end{subarray}}\frac{z-1}{q^{2}-q+z-1}\prod_{q\mid(n,h)}\frac{z-1}{(q-1)+(z-1)}.

∎

The following proposition immediately implies Theorem
5.4
when combined with Proposition
4.6
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
. For
|z|\leq 1
,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}=F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)

in the notation of Definition
5.3
.

Proof.

Using the notation of Lemma
5.10
,

\sum_{\ell}{\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}}/{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}=S_{0}^{\omega/}(1)+S_{1}
where

(5.8)

S_{1}=\sum\limits_{\ell}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1\right)

in the notation of Proposition
3.2
. By Lemma
5.10
we have simply

S_{0}^{\omega/}(1)=\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\begin{cases}2z/(z+1),&\text{if }2\mid h,\\
1,&\text{if }2\nmid h.\end{cases}

The evaluation of
S_{1}
depends upon the sign of
a
and the parity of
h
. Throughout this proof it will be helpful to remember that
4\nmid\ell
due to the presence of the
\mu^{2}(\ell)
factor.

First assume that
a>0
. By Proposition
3.2
,

(5.9)

S_{1}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
2\mid\ell^{\prime}\\
\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}={\mathbbm{1}}(2\nmid h)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\mid\ell\\
\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}.

In particular, if
a>0
and
2\mid h
, then
S_{1}=0
and therefore

S_{0}^{\omega/}(1)+S_{1}=\frac{2z}{z+1}H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}=F_{a}^{\omega/}(z)H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}

as required.

We examine
S_{1}
further in the case
a>0
and
2\nmid h
.
Since
\ell
is squarefree, the only way for
\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})=\mathfrak{d}(b_{0})
to divide
\ell
is for
b_{0}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
; thus in this case,

(5.10)

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}(b_{0}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}})\sum_{2b_{0}\mid\ell}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}={\mathbbm{1}}\Bigl(\textup{sgn}(a)b_{0}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}\Bigr)S_{0}^{\omega/}(2b_{0}).

In particular, if
\textup{sgn}(a)b_{0}\not\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
then again
S_{1}=0
and

S_{0}^{\omega/}(1)+S_{1}=H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}=F_{a}^{\omega/}(z)H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}

as required. Finally, if
\textup{sgn}(a)b_{0}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
then by Lemma
5.10
,

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\omega/}(1)+S_{1}

\displaystyle=H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}F_{a}^{\omega/}(z)

as required.

Next, when
a<0
and
2\nmid h
, the only case in Proposition
3.2
that results in
\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)\neq 0
is when
\ell\equiv\ell^{\prime}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
and
\mathfrak{d}(-a_{0})\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}}
. This argument yields the same expression for
S_{1}
as in equation (
5.10
) except with
b_{0}
replaced by
-b_{0}
; the analogue of the argument following equation (
5.10
) verifies the proposition in this case as well.

Finally, when
a<0
and
2\mid h
, the only case in Proposition
3.2
that results in
\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)\neq 0
is when
\ell
is even but
\ell^{\prime}
is odd, in which case
\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1=-\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore in this case,

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
2\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}=-\frac{1}{2}S_{0}^{\omega/}(2),

and so by Lemma
5.10
,

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\omega/}(1)+S_{1}

\displaystyle=H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\frac{2z}{z+1}\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}\cdot\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2}\prod_{q\mid(2,h)}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{1+\frac{z-1}{q-1}}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}\cdot\frac{2z}{z+1}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{1-2(1-z)/2(2-1)}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=H_{a}^{\omega/}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{2}-q+z-1}{q^{2}-q}\cdot 1

as required.
∎

5.2.
The statistic
\Omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)

The Kummer extensions relevant to this statistic now include non-squarefree values of
\ell
, which makes their degrees somewhat more complicated. However, we are still using Proposition
3.2
only for
m=1
, which limits this case to one of intermediate difficulty.
Throughout we will freely use the notation from Definition
1.1
, Notation

Definition 5.12
.

For any positive integer
\lambda
, define

\displaystyle g^{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+2(1-z^{-1})\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\lambda}z^{k}\frac{(2^{k},h)}{2^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1+\dfrac{2(z-1)\Bigl(1-(z/2)^{\lambda}\Bigr)}{2-z},&\text{if }\lambda\leq\nu_{2}(h),\\
\dfrac{4z-z^{2}+2(z-1)\left((z-2)2^{\nu_{2}(h)}(z/4)^{\lambda}-z^{\nu_{2}(h)}2^{-\nu_{2}(h)+1}\right)}{(2-z)(4-z)},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle g^{\Omega}(\infty)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}g^{\Omega}(\lambda)=\frac{4z-z^{2}-4(z-1)(z/2)^{\nu_{2}(h)}}{(z-2)(z-4)}.

(Note that
g^{\Omega}(\infty)
appears as a denominator in equation (
5.3
).)

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\alpha
be an integer and let
\beta\in\{\alpha,\alpha+1,\alpha+2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}
. For any any positive integer
h
and any odd squarefree integer
\gamma
, write

S^{\Omega}_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,z)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\alpha\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\beta\end{subarray}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}\Bigl(1-z^{-1}\Bigr)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}.

Then

S^{\Omega}_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,z)=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}\begin{cases}g^{\Omega}(\beta),&\text{if }\alpha=0,\\
g^{\Omega}(\beta)-g^{\Omega}(\alpha-1),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

in the notation of Definitions
5.6
and
5.12
.

Proof.

First we compute the sum over odd
\ell
:

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{h}(0,0,\gamma,z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
2\nmid\ell\end{subarray}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}\Bigl(1-z^{-1}\Bigr)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(l,h)}{l\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{\Omega(\gamma)}\Bigl(1-z^{-1}\Bigr)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma\varphi(\gamma)}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
2\nmid\ell\end{subarray}}z^{\Omega(\ell)}\Bigl(1-z^{-1}\Bigr)^{\omega(\ell)-\omega((\ell,\gamma))}\frac{(\ell,\frac{h}{(h,\gamma)})}{\ell\frac{\varphi(\gamma\ell)}{\varphi(\gamma)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(z-1)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma\varphi(\gamma)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\geq 3\\
q\nmid\gamma\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{(z-1)q}{z(q-1)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}(q^{k},h)}{q^{2k}}\biggr)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\geq 3\\
q\mid\gamma\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}(q^{k},\frac{h}{(h,\gamma)})}{q^{2k}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(z-1)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma\varphi(\gamma)g^{\Omega}(\infty)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{(z-1)q}{z(q-1)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}(q^{k},h)}{q^{2k}}\biggr)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\geq 3\\
q\mid\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}(q^{k},\frac{h}{(h,\gamma)})}{q^{2k}}}{1+\frac{(z-1)q}{z(q-1)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}(q^{k},h)}{q^{2k}}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\biggr)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}.

Then taking into account the
2
-adic valuation of
\ell
in general,

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,z)=\sum_{k=\alpha}^{\beta}S^{\Omega}_{h}(k,k,\gamma,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\alpha}^{\beta}S^{\Omega}_{h}(0,0,\gamma,z)z^{k}(1-z^{-1})\frac{(2^{k},h)}{2^{2k-1}}

\displaystyle=S^{\Omega}_{h}(0,0,\gamma,z)\begin{cases}g^{\Omega}(\beta),&\text{if }\alpha=0,\\
g^{\Omega}(\beta)-g^{\Omega}(\alpha-1),&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\qed

The following proposition immediately implies Theorem
5.7
when combined with Proposition
4.6
.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
. For
|z|\leq 1
,

\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}=H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)F^{\Omega}_{a}(h,z)\prod\limits_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\biggl)

in the notation of Definition
5.6
.

Proof.

In the notation of Proposition
3.2
, define

S^{\Omega}_{1}=\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 1}\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1\right)\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)},

so that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{\ell}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}+S^{\Omega}_{1}

\displaystyle=S^{\Omega}_{h}(0,\infty,1,z)+S_{1}=H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\biggr)+S^{\Omega}_{1}

by Lemma
5.13
, since
I^{\Omega}_{h}(1,z)=1
. The first term on the right-hand side accounts for the initial
1
in the definition (
5.3
) of
F_{a}^{\Omega}(h,z)
; we now show that
S^{\Omega}_{1}
accounts for the complicated remainder of that definition.

We first consider the case
a>0
. In this case,

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)>\nu_{2}(h)\\
\mathfrak{d}(a_{0})\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle=S^{\Omega}_{h}(\max(\nu_{2}(h)+1,\eta),\infty,\gamma,z)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}\begin{cases}g^{\Omega}(\infty)-g^{\Omega}(\eta-1),&\text{if }\eta>\nu_{2}(h)+1,\\
g^{\Omega}(\infty)-g^{\Omega}(\nu_{2}(h)),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)(z-1)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)(4-z)}\begin{cases}2^{-2\eta+\nu_{2}(h)+3}z^{\eta-1},&\text{if }\eta>\nu_{2}(h)+1,\\
2^{-\nu_{2}(h)+1}z^{\nu_{2}(h)},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)(z-1)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)(4-z)}\tau^{\Omega}_{+}(a,z).

Assume now that
a<0
. We split
S^{\Omega}_{1}
into three parts according to the
2
-adic valuation of
\ell
, to fit the different cases of Proposition
3.2
:

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\nu_{2}(h)\end{subarray}}\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1\right)\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)=\nu_{2}(h)+1\end{subarray}}\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1\right)\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1,3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)\geq\nu_{2}(h)+2\end{subarray}}\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1\right)\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}.

Starting with
S^{\Omega}_{1,1}
, we use Proposition
3.2
to write

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathbbm{1}(2\mid h)}{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
1\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\nu_{2}(h)\end{subarray}}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathbbm{1}(2\mid h)}{2}S^{\Omega}_{h}(1,\nu_{2}(h),1,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathbbm{1}(2\mid h)}{2}\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(1,z)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}(g^{\Omega}(\nu_{2}(h))-1)

\displaystyle=-\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}(z-1)\left(\frac{1-(z/2)^{\nu_{2}(h)}}{2-z}\right)

by Lemma
5.13
.
Similarly, recalling that
\gamma=b_{0}/(2,b_{0})
, Proposition
3.2
implies

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1,2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)=\nu_{2}(h)+1\end{subarray}}\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\ell,\ell)-1\right)\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}S^{\Omega}_{h}(1,1,\gamma,z),&\text{if }2\nmid h\text{ and }b_{0}\equiv 3{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
S^{\Omega}_{h}(2,2,\gamma,z),&\text{if }2\mid\mid h\text{ and }b_{0}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)(z-1)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}\begin{cases}1/2,&\text{if }2\nmid h\text{ and }b_{0}\equiv 3{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
z/4,&\text{if }2\mid\mid h\text{ and }b_{0}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Finally,

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1,3}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)\geq\nu_{2}(h)+2\\
2^{\nu_{2}(\mathfrak{d}(a_{0}))}\mid\ell\\
\gamma\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{z^{\Omega(\ell)}(1-z^{-1})^{\omega(\ell)}(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}S^{\Omega}_{h}(3,\infty,\gamma,z),&\text{if }2\nmid h\text{ and }b_{0}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
S^{\Omega}_{h}\Bigl(\nu_{2}(h)+2,\infty,\gamma,z\Bigr),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)(z-1)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)(4-z)}\begin{cases}2^{-3}z^{2},&\text{if }2\nmid h\text{ and }b_{0}\equiv 2{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\

Adding and regrouping these evaluations results in

\displaystyle S^{\Omega}_{1}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{q}\left(1+\frac{q(z-1)}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\right)\frac{H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)(z-1)}{g^{\Omega}(\infty)}\left(\frac{1-(z/2)^{\nu_{2}(h)}}{z-2}+\frac{I^{\Omega}_{h}(\gamma,z)}{4-z}\tau^{\Omega}_{-}(a,z)\right),

which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.3.
The statistic
\omega(p-1)-\omega\Bigl(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)

Throughout we will freely use the notation from Definition

Definition 5.15
.

For any positive integer
\lambda
, define

\displaystyle g(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+(z-1)\sum_{k=1}^{\lambda}\frac{(2^{k},h)}{2^{2k}}=\begin{cases}1+(z-1)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{3}(2^{1-\nu_{2}(h)}+2^{\nu_{2}(h)-2\lambda})\Bigr),&\text{if }\nu_{2}(h)<\lambda,\\
1+(z-1)(1-2^{-\lambda}),&\text{if }\nu_{2}(h)\geq\lambda\end{cases}

\displaystyle g(\infty)

(Note that

G_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\eta,\kappa,z)=\frac{1}{g(\infty)}\begin{cases}(-1)^{\kappa}g(\beta)+\kappa,&\text{if }\eta=\alpha=0,\\

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
\alpha
be an integer and let
\beta\in\{\alpha,\alpha+1,\alpha+2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}
. Fix
\eta\in\{0,2,3\}
and
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
. For any any positive integer
h
and any odd squarefree integer
\gamma
, write

\displaystyle S_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\alpha\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\beta\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid\ell\\
2^{\kappa}\mid m\\
\frac{2^{\eta}}{(2^{\eta},\ell)}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}

Then

S_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q^{2}-1}\biggr)H_{h}^{\omega-}(z)I_{h}^{\omega-}(\gamma,z)G_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\eta,\kappa,z)

in the notation of Definitions
5.8
and
5.15
.

Proof.

If
\eta-\beta\geq 2
then trivially
S_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)=G_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\eta,\kappa,z)=0
. We may therefore assume that
\eta-\beta\leq 1
, and we write
\theta(\ell)=\max\{\kappa,\eta-\nu_{2}(\ell)\}
.

First we compute the sum over divisors of
\ell
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid\ell\\
2^{\kappa}\mid m\\
\frac{2^{\eta}}{(2^{\eta},\ell)}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid\ell\\
2^{\theta(\ell)}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\theta(\ell)}\mathbbm{1}\Bigl(\theta(\ell)\leq 1\Bigr)\mathbbm{1}\Bigl(2^{\theta(\ell)}\mid\ell\Bigr)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggr(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr).

Now
\theta(\ell)>1
if
\eta-\nu_{2}(\ell)>1
, so we split our problem according to whether
\eta\leq\alpha
or
\alpha<\eta\leq\beta+1
.
First, if
\eta\leq\alpha
, then

\displaystyle S_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\alpha\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}(2^{\kappa}\mid\ell)(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\alpha\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\beta\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\mathbbm{1}\Bigl(\kappa=1,\alpha=0\Bigr)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
2\nmid\ell\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr).

On the other hand, if
\eta>\alpha
and
\eta\leq\beta+1
, then

\displaystyle S_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\alpha\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\beta\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}(-1)^{\theta(\ell)}\mathbbm{1}\Bigl(2^{\theta(\ell)}\mid\ell\Bigr)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr)

\displaystyle=-\mathbbm{1}(\eta\geq 2)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)=\eta-1\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\mathbbm{1}(\eta\leq\beta)(-1)^{\kappa}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\eta\leq\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\beta\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr).

In both cases, the sum can be expressed in terms of the function

\displaystyle M(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gamma\mid\ell\\
\nu_{2}(\ell)\leq\lambda\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)}\frac{(\ell,h)}{\ell\varphi(\ell)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\biggl(1+(z-1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\lambda}\frac{(2^{k},h)}{2^{2k}}\biggr)\frac{(z-1)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma^{2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
2\nmid\ell\end{subarray}}(z-1)^{\omega(\ell)-\omega((\ell,\gamma))}\frac{(\ell,\frac{h}{(h,\gamma)})}{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=g(\lambda)\frac{(z-1)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma^{2}}\prod_{q\nmid 2\gamma}\biggl(1+(z-1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{k},h)}{q^{2k}}\biggr)\prod_{q\mid\gamma}\biggl(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{k},\frac{h}{(h,q)})}{q^{2k}}\biggr);

more precisely,

\displaystyle S_{h}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\kappa}M(\beta)+\kappa M(0),&\text{if }\eta=\alpha=0,\\
(-1)^{\kappa}(M(\beta)-M(\alpha)),&\text{if }\eta\leq\alpha\text{ and }\alpha>0,\\
-(M(\eta-1)-M(\eta-2))+(-1)^{\kappa}(M(\beta)-M(\eta-1)),&\text{if }\eta>\alpha\text{ and }\eta\leq\beta+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\frac{(z-1)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma^{2}g(\infty)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}\left(1+(z-1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{k},h)}{q^{2k}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\gamma\end{subarray}}\left[\left(1+(z-1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{k},h)}{q^{2k}}\right)^{-1}\left(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{k},\frac{h}{(h,q)})}{q^{2k}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\begin{cases}(-1)^{\kappa}g(\beta)+\kappa,&\text{if }\eta=\alpha=0,\\
(-1)^{\kappa}(g(\beta)-g(\alpha-1)),&\text{if }\eta\leq\alpha\text{ and }\alpha>0,\\
g(\eta-2)-g(\eta-1)+(-1)^{\kappa}\left(g(\beta)-g(\eta-1)\right),&\text{if }\eta>\alpha\text{ and }\eta\leq\beta+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

After calculating the eventually geometric series

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(p^{k},h)}{p^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p+1-p^{-\nu_{p}(h)+1}}{p^{2}-1},

we obtain

\displaystyle S_{0}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\kappa,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(z-1)^{\omega(\gamma)}(\gamma,h)}{\gamma g(\infty)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q^{2}-1}\biggr)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q|h\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{q^{2}-1+(z-1)(q+1-q^{-\nu_{q}(h)+1})}{q^{2}+z-2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid\gamma\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{q+1-q^{-\nu_{q}(h/(h,q))}}{q^{2}-1+(z-1)(q+1-q^{-\nu_{q}(h)+1})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\begin{cases}(-1)^{\kappa}g(\beta)+\kappa,&\text{if }\eta=\alpha=0,\\
(-1)^{\kappa}(g(\beta)-g(\alpha-1)),&\text{if }\eta\leq\alpha\text{ and }\alpha>0,\\
g(\eta-2)-g(\eta-1)+(-1)^{\kappa}\left(g(\beta)-g(\eta-1)\right),&\text{if }\eta>\alpha\text{ and }\eta\leq\beta+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)
has a particular relationship with
p-1
.
For example,
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
is the density of the set of primes
p
with
\omega((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))=n
; in light of
equations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we can restate
Theorem
5.4
as

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)z^{n}=F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr).

In particular, this identity provides us with a way to extract information about the individual densities
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
from the formula for their generating function on the right-hand side.
The first two factors are simply rational functions of
z
that depend on
a
, while the third factor is an infinite product that takes some attention to understand. We therefore define “uncorrected” coefficients in Notation
6.2
below so that, for example,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D^{\omega/}(n)z^{n}=\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)

and concentrate on them initially.

In Section
6.1
we provide explicit recursive formulas for these uncorrected coefficients. In Section
6.2
we use these recursive formulas to calculate several examples of numerical values, both for the uncorrected coefficients and the true coefficients such as
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
. In
Section
6.3
we record a non-recursive formula for the uncorrected coefficients, in the hope that readers might find it useful to adapt to other situations.

6.1.
Derivatives of infinite products and recursive formulas for their coefficients

This approach we take in this section is based on observations of Marcus Lai (private communication), and a version of it appears in
[
4
]
as well.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
P(z)
be any function with Maclaurin series

P(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C(n)z^{n},

so that
C(n)=P^{(n)}(0)/n!
for every
n\geq 0
. Define

S(0,z)=\log P(z)\quad\text{and}\quad S(n,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}S(0,z)

for all
n\geq 1
; and define
S(n)=S(n,0)
and
\tilde{S}(n)=S(n)/(n-1)!
. Then for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle P^{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}P^{(k)}(z)S(n-k,z).

In particular, for
n\geq 1
,

C(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}C(k)\tilde{S}(n-k).

Proof.

We first verify that

P^{\prime}(z)=P(z)\frac{P^{\prime}(z)}{P(z)}=P(z)\frac{d}{dz}\log P(z)=P(z)\frac{d}{dz}S(0,z)=P(z)S(1,z),

which is the case
n=1
of the first identity. The general case of the first identity now follows from using the product rule
n-1
times in a row on this initial identity
P^{\prime}(z)=P(z)S(1,z)
. The second identity follows by plugging in
z=0
to the first and using the definition
C(n)=\frac{1}{n!}P^{(n)}(0)
.
∎

We now apply this result to the (uncorrected) infinite products appearing in Theorems
5.4
,
5.7
, and
5.9
. We caution the reader not to conflate the functions we now define, such as
f_{\omega/}(q,z)
, with earlier similarly named functions such as
f^{\omega/}(z)
from Definition
5.3
.

Notation 6.2
.

Define

f_{\Omega}(q,z)=\frac{q^{3}-q^{2}-q+z}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\omega/}(q,z)=\bigg(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\bigg)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\omega-}(q,z)=\bigg(1+\frac{z-1}{q^{2}-1}\bigg).

Then the infinite products appearing in Theorems
5.4
,
5.7
, and
5.9
are

P_{\Omega}(z)=\prod_{q}f_{\Omega}(q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\omega/}(z)=\prod_{q}f_{\omega/}(q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\omega-}(z)=\prod_{q}f_{\omega-}(q,z).

We are interested in the coefficients of their Maclaurin series, which we name by

P_{\Omega}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D^{\Omega}(n)z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\omega/}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D^{\omega/}(n)z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{\omega-}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D^{\omega-}(n)z^{n},

so that

D^{\Omega}(n)=\frac{1}{n!}P^{(n)}_{\Omega}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\omega/}(n)=\frac{1}{n!}P^{(n)}_{\omega/}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\omega-}(n)=\frac{1}{n!}P^{(n)}_{\omega-}(z).

(These Maclaurin coefficients are closely related to the ones defined in equation (
1.3
), except that those prior coefficients also include the effect of the correction factors depending on
a
.)
Note that

(6.1)

D^{\omega-}(0)=\prod_{q}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\biggr),\quad\text{and that }D^{\Omega}(0)=D^{\omega/}(0)=\prod_{q}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)

both equal Artin’s constant; Moree
[
10
]
provides precise numerical approximation of these constants.

We derive recursive formulas (Proposition
6.4
below) for
D^{\Omega}(n)
,
D^{\omega/}(n)
, and
D^{\omega-}(n)
for all
n\geq 1
, given in terms of certain quantities
\tilde{S}_{\Omega}(n)
,
\tilde{S}_{\omega/}(n)
, and
\tilde{S}_{\omega-}(n)
defined in equation (
6.2
) and evaluated in Lemma
6.5
.

Notation 6.3
.

Define

g_{\Omega}(0,q,z)=\log f_{\Omega}(q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{\omega/}(0,q,z)=\log f_{\omega/}(q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{\omega-}(0,q,z)=\log f_{\omega-}(q,z),

and for
n\geq 1
define

g_{\Omega}(n,q,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}g_{\Omega}(0,q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{\omega/}(n,q,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}g_{\omega/}(0,q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{\omega-}(n,q,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}g_{\omega-}(0,q,z).

Then define
g_{\Omega}(n,q)=g_{\Omega}(n,q,0)
and
g_{\omega/}(n,q)=g_{\omega/}(n,q,0)
and
g_{\omega-}(n,q)=g_{\omega-}(n,q,0)
.

Further define

S_{\Omega}(n,z)=\sum_{q}g_{\Omega}(n,q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\omega/}(n,z)=\sum_{q}g_{\omega/}(n,q,z)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\omega-}(n,z)=\sum_{q}g_{\omega-}(n,q,z),

so that
S_{\Omega}(0,z)=\log P_{\Omega}(z)
and
S_{\omega/}(0,z)=\log P_{\omega/}(z)
and
S_{\omega-}(0,z)=\log P_{\omega-}(z)
, and also

S_{\Omega}(n,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}S_{\Omega}(0,z)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\omega/}(n,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}S_{\omega/}(0,z)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\omega-}(n,z)=\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}S_{\omega-}(0,z).

Finally, define
S_{\Omega}(n)=S_{\Omega}(n,0)=\sum_{q}g_{\Omega}(n,q)
and
S_{\omega/}(n)=S_{\omega/}(n,0)=\sum_{q}g_{\omega/}(n,q)
and
S_{\omega-}(n)=S_{\omega-}(n,0)=\sum_{q}g_{\omega-}(n,q)
; and define

(6.2)

\tilde{S}_{\Omega}(n)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}S_{\Omega}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{S}_{\omega/}(n)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}S_{\omega/}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{S}_{\omega-}(n)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}S_{\omega-}(n).

Proposition 6.4
.

For any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle P^{(n)}_{\Omega}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}P^{(k)}_{\Omega}(z)S_{\Omega}(n-k,z)

\displaystyle P^{(n)}_{\omega/}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}P^{(k)}_{\omega/}(z)S_{\omega/}(n-k,z)

\displaystyle P^{(n)}_{\omega-}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}P^{(k)}_{\omega-}(z)S_{\omega-}(n-k,z).

In particular, for
n\geq 1
,

D^{\Omega}(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D^{\Omega}(k)\tilde{S}_{\Omega}(n-k)\text{ and }D^{\omega/}(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D^{\omega/}(k)\tilde{S}_{\omega/}(n-k)\text{ and }D^{\omega-}(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D^{\omega-}(k)\tilde{S}_{\omega-}(n-k).

Proof.

These are particular instances of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

The values of these coefficients for
n=0
are recorded in equation (
6.1
); to use these recursive formulas for the coefficients, it remains to exhibit formulas for the
\tilde{S}
quantities.

Lemma 6.5
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle g_{\Omega}(n,q,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)!}{(q^{2}-z)^{n}}-\frac{(n-1)!}{(q+q^{2}-q^{3}-z)^{n}}

\displaystyle g_{\omega/}(n,q,z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)!}{(1+q-q^{2}-z)^{n}}

\displaystyle g_{\omega-}(n,q,z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)!}{(2-q^{2}-z)^{n}}.

In particular,

\tilde{S}_{\Omega}(n)=\sum_{q}\biggl(\frac{1}{q^{2n}}-\frac{1}{(q+q^{2}-q^{3})^{n}}\biggr)\text{\, and \,}\tilde{S}_{\omega/}(n)=-\sum_{q}\frac{1}{(1+q-q^{2})^{n}}\text{\, and \,}\tilde{S}_{\omega-}(n)=-\sum_{q}\frac{1}{(2-q^{2})^{n}}.

Proof.

The primary calculation is to verify that

\displaystyle g_{\Omega}(1,q,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dz}g_{\Omega}(0,q,z)=\frac{1}{q^{2}-z}-\frac{1}{q+q^{2}-q^{3}-z}

\displaystyle g_{\omega/}(1,q,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dz}g_{\omega/}(0,q,z)=-\frac{1}{1+q-q^{2}-z}

\displaystyle g_{\omega-}(1,q,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dz}g_{\omega-}(0,q,z)=-\frac{1}{2-q^{2}-z};

the rest follow trivially by induction on
n
. The expressions for the
\tilde{S}
quantities are direct consequences of Notation
6.3
.
∎

6.3.
A non-recursive formula

Note that Proposition
6.1
gives a recursive formula for the
C(n)
in terms of
C(0)
and the
\tilde{S}(n)
. We take this opportunity to record an explicit (non-recursive) version, which can be derived using Faà di Bruno’s formula generalizing the chain rule.

Proposition 6.7
.

For
n\geq 1
,

C(n)=P(0)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n}\leq n\\
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{S(1)^{m_{1}}S(2)^{m_{2}}\cdots S(n)^{m_{n}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!\cdots m_{n}!\cdot 2^{m_{2}}3^{m_{3}}\cdots n^{m_{n}}}.

Proof.

Define the complete exponential Bell polynomial

B_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n}\leq n\\
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!m_{2}!\cdots m_{n}!}\biggl(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\biggr)^{m_{1}}\biggl(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\biggr)^{m_{2}}\cdots\biggl(\frac{x_{n}}{n!}\biggr)^{m_{n}},

as well as the incomplete exponential Bell polynomials

B_{n,k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n}\leq n\\
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n\\
m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{n}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!m_{2}!\cdots m_{n}!}\biggl(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\biggr)^{m_{1}}\biggl(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\biggr)^{m_{2}}\cdots\biggl(\frac{x_{n}}{n!}\biggr)^{m_{n}},

so that
B_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}B_{n,k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. We can check that
B_{n,k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
depends only on its first
n-k+1
variables.

We begin with Faà di Bruno’s formula for the high-order derivatives of a composition of two functions:

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}h\bigl(g(z)\bigr)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}h^{(k)}(g(z))B_{n,k}\bigl(g^{\prime}(z),g^{\prime\prime}(z),\dots,g^{(n)}(z)\bigr).

When applied with
h(u)=e^{u}
, this becomes

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}e^{g(z)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{g(z)}B_{n,k}\bigl(g^{\prime}(z),g^{\prime\prime}(z),\dots,g^{(n)}(z)\bigr)=e^{g(z)}B_{n}\bigl(g^{\prime}(z),g^{\prime\prime}(z),\dots,g^{(n)}(z)\bigr).

We apply this with
g(z)=S(0,z)
, so that
e^{g(z)}=P(z)
and
g^{(k)}(z)=S(k,z)
:

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}P(z)=P(z)B_{n}\bigl(S(1,z),S(2,z),\dots,S(n,z)\bigr).

In particular, since
S(k,z)=(k-1)!\tilde{S}(k)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}P(z)\biggr|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=P(0)B_{n}\bigl(S(1),S(2),\dots,S(n)\bigr)

\displaystyle=P(0)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n}\leq n\\
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!m_{2}!\cdots m_{n}!}\biggl(\frac{\tilde{S}(1)}{1}\biggr)^{m_{1}}\biggl(\frac{\tilde{S}(2)}{2}\biggr)^{m_{2}}\cdots\biggl(\frac{\tilde{S}(n)}{n}\biggr)^{m_{n}}

(without the factorials in the denominators), and the proposition follows.
∎

7.
Decay rates and expectations

Our generating functions allow us to gather even more information about the coefficients
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
,
D_{a}^{\Omega}(n)
, and
D_{a}^{\omega-}(n)
. The decay rates for these sequences of coefficients, as described in Corollary
1.14
, are proved in Section
7.1
; the expectations of those sequences, which can be thought of as the average values of the statistics
\omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
and
\Omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
and
\omega(p-1)-\omega\Bigl(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
when

is chosen at random, are determined in Section

7.1.
Decay rates

We begin with a precise statement of the well-known phenomenon that the size of the Maclaurin coefficients of an analytic function is determined by the distance from the origin to the nearest singularity of the function.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be a meromorphic function on a neighbourhood of the closed disk of radius
R
centred at the origin. Suppose that there are two complex numbers
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
with
0<|z_{1}|<|z_{2}|=R
such that
F(z)
has simple poles at
z=z_{1}
and
z=z_{2}
, with residues
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
respectively, and no other poles in
\{|z|\leq R\}
. Then
a_{n}=-r_{1}z_{1}^{-n-1}+O_{F}(R^{-n})
.

Proof.

If we define
G(z)=F(z)-r_{1}/(z-z_{1})-r_{2}/(z-z_{2})
, then
G(z)
is analytic on a neighbourhood of
\{|z|\leq R\}
. When we write
G(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
, the standard Cauchy integral formula calculation gives

b_{n}=\frac{G^{(n)}}{n!}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=R}\frac{G(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,dz\ll_{G}\oint_{|z|=R}\frac{1}{R^{n+1}}\,d|z|\ll_{G}R^{-n}.

But
F(z)=G(z)-r_{1}/z_{1}(1-z/z_{1})-r_{2}/z_{2}(1-z/z_{2})
means that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}=-\frac{r_{1}}{z_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{z}{z_{1}}\biggr)^{n}-\frac{r_{2}}{z_{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{z}{z_{2}}\biggr)^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n},

and therefore

a_{n}=-\frac{r_{1}}{z_{1}}\biggl(\frac{1}{z_{1}}\biggr)^{n}-\frac{r_{2}}{z_{2}}\biggl(\frac{1}{z_{2}}\biggr)^{n}+b_{n}=-\frac{r_{1}}{z_{1}^{n+1}}+O_{F}(R^{-{n+1}})+O_{F}(R^{-n})

as claimed.
∎

This lemma is relevant to the generating function appearing in Theorem
5.7
, whose poles we take some care to describe exactly.

Lemma 7.2
.

The main term in equation (
5.4
) is a meromorphic function on all of
{\mathbb{C}}
, with at most simple poles at
z=q^{2}
for every prime
q
and no other poles.

Proof.

We refer freely to the notation from Definition
5.6
. The main term in question includes the product

\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{(z-1)q}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\biggr)=\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-q}{q^{2}(q-1)}\biggr)\bigg/\prod_{q}\biggl(1-\frac{z}{q^{2}}\biggr).

Since
\sum_{q}1/q(q-1)
and
\sum_{q}1/q^{2}
converge, both products converge absolutely and uniformly on compact sets and are therefore entire functions; therefore their quotient is a meromorphic function on all of
{\mathbb{C}}
with at most simple poles at
z=q^{2}
for all primes
q
. We also note that

\displaystyle H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)

\displaystyle\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{(z-1)q}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\biggr)=H^{\Omega}_{h}(z)\prod_{q}\frac{q^{3}-q^{2}-q+z}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}

(7.1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q\mid h}\biggl(\frac{q^{4}-2q^{3}+2qz-z^{2}-(z-1)(q-1)z^{\nu_{q}(h)}q^{-\nu_{q}(h)+2}}{(q-z)(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}\biggr)\prod_{q\nmid h}\frac{q^{3}-q^{2}-q+z}{(q-1)(q^{2}-z)}

has no additional poles—the factor
q-z
in the first denominator on the right-hand side does not produce a pole since the corresponding numerator vanishes at
z=q
.

Next we consider what happens when equation (
7.1
) is multiplied by the factor
I_{h}^{\Omega}(\gamma,z)
that is present in both cases of
F_{a}^{\Omega}(\gamma,z)
. Again both its numerator and denominator vanish to first order at
z=q
, so no pole will be introduced there. For any
q\mid\gamma

q\mid h
as well then the denominator of
I_{h}^{\Omega}(\gamma,z)
is canceled by the identical numerator of
H_{h}^{\Omega}(\gamma,z)
; on the other hand, if
q\nmid h
then the denominator of
I_{h}^{\Omega}(\gamma,z)
equals
(q-z)(q^{3}-q^{2}-q+z)
, and the second factor is canceled by the second numerator on the right-hand side of equation (
7.1
).

We now consider what happens when equation (
7.1
) is multiplied by the first fraction in the definition of
F_{a}^{\Omega}(h,z)
. When
2\mid h
, its denominator is identical to the numerator of the
q=2
term in the definition of
H_{h}^{\Omega}(z)
and is thus canceled. (Note that
2
never divides
\gamma
by definition, since
b_{0}
is squarefree, and so we are not using this numerator to cancel two different denominators.) On the other hand, when
2\nmid h
then its denominator equals
4-z^{2}=-(z-2)(z+2)
; the

is canceled by the numerator above it, while the
z+2
is canceled by the numerator
2^{3}-2^{2}-2+z=2+z
of the
q=2
term in the infinite product in equation (
7.1
).

We are almost done examining the various ways that poles might have been introduced when multplying equation (
7.1
) by
F_{a}^{\Omega}(h,z)
to obtain the main term in equation (
5.4
). The functions
\tau_{\pm}^{\Omega}(a,z)
are entire in all cases. Both occurrences of

in the definition of

are canceled by the

factor in the large fraction. Finally, the term
(1-(\frac{z}{2})^{\nu_{2}(h)})/(z-2)
in the second case of

∎

Proof of Corollary

(a).

By Lemma

, we may apply Lemma

to the main term in equation (

is the residue of the simple pole at

and so its residue at

While

which completes the proof.
∎

As it turns out, the generating functions in Theorems

Lemma 7.3
.

The main terms in equations (

) and (

) are entire functions, and their logarithms are
\asymp_{a}z^{1/2}\log z
as

through real numbers.

Proof.

We include the proof only for the main term in equation (

); the proof for the main term in equation (

) follows exactly the same structure (but with messier algebraic details).

First note that since
\sum_{q}1/q(q-1)
converges, the product

(7.2)

\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)=\prod_{q}\frac{z+q^{2}-q-1}{q(q-1)}

converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets and is therefore an entire function of
z
. Note also that

(7.3)

H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)=\frac{z+1}{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid h\\
q\neq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{z+q-2}{q-1}\prod_{q\nmid 2h}\frac{z+q^{2}-q-1}{q(q-1)}.

is also entire. In the case
2\mid h
, we then have

(7.4)

F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid h\\

(z+1)/2,&\text{if }a<0,\end{cases}

which remains entire. In the case
2\nmid h

(7.5)

F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)\\
=H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)+\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\textup{sgn}(a)b_{0}\not\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}},\\
f^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\bigl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\bigr),&\text{if }\textup{sgn}(a)b_{0}\equiv 1{\text{\rm\ (mod\penalty\ $4$)}};\end{cases}

we have already seen in equation (

(7.6)

f^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr)\\
=\frac{z-1}{2}\prod_{q\mid(b_{0},h)}\frac{z-1}{q-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid h\\
q\nmid b_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{z+q-2}{q-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid b_{0}\\
q\nmid 2h\end{subarray}}\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\prod_{q\nmid 2hb_{0}}\frac{z+q^{2}-q-1}{q(q-1)}

which is also entire.

In addition, when

\asymp z^{1/2}/\log z
by
[

. Since the other terms are all rational functions of

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.14
(b).

It is a classical fact from complex analysis (see
[

) that the rate of growth of an entire function

is connected to the decay rate of its Maclaurin coefficients

by

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log\bigl(\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|\bigr)}{\log r}=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to\infty\\
b_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{m\log m}{\log(1/|b_{m}|)}

(both sides, if finite, equal the “order” of

). It is straightforward to deduce the proposition from this identity and the growth rates in Lemma
7.3
.
(See
[
4
, Proof of Corollary 4]
for the details of deriving such an upper bound for the coefficients of an analogous function, including the argument that the growth rate for
z
real and positive is indeed the maximal growth rate on the circle
|z|=r
.)
∎

7.2.
Expectations

Our functions
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)
,
D_{a}^{\omega-}(n;x)
, and
D_{a}^{\Omega}(n;x)
(and their limits
D_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
,
D_{a}^{\omega-}(n)
, and
D_{a}^{\Omega}(n)
if they exist) represent proportions of primes having certain properties. We can view these proportions as probabilities associated with discrete random variables, and study the expectations of these random variables.

Notation 7.4
.

Write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega/}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x),

which is the expectation of random variable
X
with Prob
(X=n)=D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)
for all
n\geq 0
.
(Technically, for this to be a random variable, in equation (
1.3
) we should be dividing not by
\pi(x)
but by the number of primes
p\leq x
for which
\nu_{p}(a)=0
, which is
\pi(x)+O_{a}(1)
; but we shall ignore this technicality as it is insignificant in the limit.)
Similarly define

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega-}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\omega-}(n;x)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\Omega}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\Omega}(n;x).

Furthermore, if the appropriate limits exist, define

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega/}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\omega/}(n),\quad\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega-}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\omega-}(n),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\Omega}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\Omega}(n).

Theorem 7.5
.

Under GRH, the expectations
\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega/}
,
\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega-}
, and
\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\Omega}
exist, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega/}

\displaystyle=\frac{dF^{\omega/}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\frac{dH^{\omega/}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\sum\limits_{q}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\omega-}

\displaystyle=\frac{dF^{\omega-}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\frac{dH^{\omega-}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\sum\limits_{q}\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}^{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{dF^{\Omega}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\frac{dH^{\Omega}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\sum\limits_{q}\frac{q}{q^{3}-q^{2}-q+1}.

Proof.

In this proof we use
D_{a}
and
\mathbb{E}_{a}
to examine the three cases simultaneously.
The formulas in Theorems
5.4
,
5.7
, and
5.9
imply that

\sum\limits_{n}D_{a}(n)z^{n}=F_{a}(z)H_{a}(z)\prod\limits_{q}P_{a}(q,z)

for certain functions
P_{a}(p,z)
depending on the statistic being examined; therefore

\mathbb{E}_{a}=\biggl(\frac{d}{dz}F_{a}(z)H_{a}(z)\prod\limits_{q}P_{a}(q,z)\biggr)\bigg|_{z=1}.

But
F_{a}(1)=H_{a}(1)=P_{a}(p,1)=1
always, and so the product rule yields

\mathbb{E}_{a}=\frac{dF_{a}}{dz}(1)+\frac{dH_{a}}{dz}(1)+\biggl(\frac{d}{dz}\prod\limits_{q}P_{a}(q,z)\biggr)\bigg|_{z=1},

where the last derivative can be easily computed in each of our cases using Lemma
6.5
.
∎

We apply this theorem to the special cases treated in Corollaries
1.7
,
1.11
, and
1.13
.

Corollary 7.6
.

Assuming GRH,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{3}^{\omega/}=\mathbb{E}_{5}^{\omega/}=\sum_{q}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{4}^{\omega/}=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{q}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{3}^{\Omega}=\mathbb{E}_{5}^{\Omega}=\sum_{q}\frac{q}{q^{3}-q^{2}-q+1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{3}^{\omega-}=\mathbb{E}_{5}^{\omega-}=\sum_{q}\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{4}^{\omega-}=\frac{1}{4}+\sum_{q}\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}.

8.
Unconditional bounds

Our conditional results can actually be converted into unconditional bounds, of which Corollary
1.15
is an example. We concentrate on the first statistic
\omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
, but all our results have immediate analogues for the other two statistics
\Omega\Bigl((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
and
\omega(p-1)-\omega\Bigl(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)\Bigr)
, with the same proofs.

In the notation of Definition
5.3
, define the Maclaurin series coefficients
\tilde{D}_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{D}_{a}^{\omega/}(n)z^{n}=F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)\prod_{q}\biggl(1+\frac{z-1}{q(q-1)}\biggr),

so that Theorem
5.4
is the statement (conditional on GRH) that
\lim_{x\to\infty}D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)=\tilde{D}_{a}^{\omega/}(n)
for all
n\geq 0
. We can modify the proof to establish:

Theorem 8.1
.

Unconditionally, for all
k\geq 0
,

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{k}D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)\leq\sum_{n=0}^{k}\tilde{D}_{a}^{\omega/}(n).

Note that the first assertion of Corollary
1.15
is the special case
k=0
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Unconditionally,

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{dF^{\omega/}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\frac{dH^{\omega/}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\sum\limits_{q}\frac{1}{q(q-1)},

where
H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)
and
F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)
are as in Definition
5.3
.

The analogues of these two theorems for the other statistics appear in Theorems
8.8
and
8.11
below.

To approach these theorems, we define

\omega_{\xi}(n)=\#\{p\mid n\colon p\leq\xi\}

so that
\omega_{\xi}(n)
is an increasing function of
\xi
and
\omega_{n}(n)=\omega(n)
.
For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
\xi\leq n
, we thus have the trivial inequality

(8.1)

\displaystyle\#\biggl\{p\leq x\colon\omega\biggl(\frac{p-1}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)}\biggr)\leq k\biggr\}

\displaystyle\leq\#\biggl\{p\leq x\colon\omega_{\xi}\biggl(\frac{p-1}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a)}\biggr)\leq k\biggr\}.

Analogously to (
1.3
), we write

(8.2)

\frac{1}{\pi_{a}(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega_{\xi}((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{a,\xi}^{\omega/}(n;x)z^{n},

so that the inequality (
8.1
) becomes

(8.3)

\sum_{n=0}^{k}D_{a}^{\omega/}(n;x)\leq\sum_{n=0}^{k}D_{a,\xi}^{\omega/}(n;x)

for any
x\geq\xi\geq 1
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

To prove Theorem
8.1
, therefore, it suffices to show that for a suitable function
\xi=\xi(x)
,

(8.4)

\lim_{x\to\infty}D_{a,\xi}^{\omega/}(n;x)=\tilde{D}_{a}^{\omega/}(n)

for all
n\geq 0
. We accomplish this goal in the next section. These tools can also be used to prove Theorem
8.2
, which we do in Section
8.2
.

8.1.
Unconditional asymptotics for suitable
\xi

We have already seen (in the proof of Theorem
3.4
) that versions of the prime ideal theorem can be seen as special cases of the Chebotarev density theorem. In particular, we may adapt an unconditional Chebotarev density theorem by Lagarias and Odlyzko
[
7
]
to obtain the following prime ideal theorem:

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
K
be a number field with discriminant
\Delta_{K}
and
[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]=n_{K}
. There exist absolute positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that the prime ideal counting function

satisfies

\pi_{K}(x)=\pi(x)+{\mathit{O}}\Bigl(\pi(x^{1-1/(4\log|\Delta_{K}|)})+\pi(x^{1-c_{1}|\Delta_{k}|^{-1+1/n_{K}}})+x\exp(-c_{2}n_{K}^{-1/2}(\log x)^{1/2})\Bigr)

uniformly in
K
.

We can then derive an unconditional version of Theorem
3.4
:

Theorem 8.4
.

Let

, and let

and

be positive integers. Unconditionally,

\displaystyle\#\Big\{

\displaystyle p\leq x\colon\nu_{p}(a)=0,\,\ell\mid(p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a),\ m\ell\mid(p-1)\Big\}=\frac{\pi(x)}{[{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m}):{\mathbb{Q}}]}

\displaystyle\qquad{}+{\mathit{O}}\biggl(\frac{\log\log m\ell}{m\ell^{2}}\biggl(\pi(x^{1-1/(c_{3}m\ell^{2}\log m\ell)})+\pi(x^{1-c_{1}(m\ell)^{c_{4}(1-m\ell^{2})}})+x\exp\biggl(-\frac{c_{2}(\log x)^{1/2}}{m^{1/2}\ell}\biggr)\biggr)\biggr),

where the implied constant depends only on

.

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of Theorem
3.4
, except that in place of equation (
3.5
) we instead use

\pi_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[\ell]{a},\zeta_{m\ell})}(x)=\pi(x)+{\mathit{O}}\Bigl(\pi(x^{1-1/(c_{3}m\ell^{2}\log m\ell)})+\pi(x^{1-c_{1}m^{c_{4}(1-m\ell^{2})}})+x\exp(-c_{2}(m\ell^{2})^{-1/2}(\log x)^{1/2})\Bigr)

which follows from Theorem

8.2.
Unconditional lower bound for the expectation

Recall that if

(8.7)

by the Cauchy integral formula, where

is any simple closed curve around

on which

is analytic.
We apply this identity in the following result, for which we need the following analogue of Theorem
8.6
for
z
on an open set containing

.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
, let
x
be a sufficiently large real number, and set
\xi=\log\log\log\log x
. Unconditionally,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a,\xi}^{\omega/}(n;x)

\displaystyle=\frac{dF^{\omega/}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\frac{dH^{\omega/}_{a}}{dz}(1)+\sum\limits_{q}\frac{1}{q(q-1)}+{\mathit{O}}_{\varepsilon}\biggl(\frac{1}{(\log\log\log\log x)^{1-\varepsilon}}\biggr)

uniformly for all
\varepsilon>0
,
where
F^{\omega/}_{a}(z)
and
H^{\omega/}_{a}(z)
are as in Definitions
5.3
.

Proof.

Our strategy is to use equation (
8.7
) for the function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a,\xi}^{\omega/}(n;x)=\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
\nu_{p}(a)=0\end{subarray}}z^{\omega_{\xi}((p-1)/\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{p}(a))}.

Set
A=1/\log\log\log\log\log x
.
By equation (
8.5
), the error term from Theorem
8.6
is admissible for all

as claimed.

To derive a lower bound for
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD_{a}(n;x)
from this result we will need the following simple result on positive discrete random variables.

Lemma 8.10
.

Let
a_{n},b_{n}\geq 0
with
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}=1
. Suppose that
\sum_{n=0}^{j}a_{n}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{j}b_{n}
for all
j\geq 0
. Then
\mathbb{E}(a_{n})\geq\mathbb{E}(b_{n})
.

Proof.

We have

(8.8)

\mathbb{E}(a_{n})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}na_{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}1=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}a_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\biggl(1-\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}a_{n}\biggr).

Therefore

\mathbb{E}(b_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\biggl(1-\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}b_{n}\biggr)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\biggl(1-\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}a_{n}\biggr)=\mathbb{E}(a_{n}).\qed

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

From the inequality (
8.3
) and Lemma
8.10
,

(8.9)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD^{\omega/}_{a}(n;x)\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nD^{\omega/}_{a,\xi}(n;x),

and taking the
\liminf
on both sides and using Theorem
8.9
yields the result.
∎

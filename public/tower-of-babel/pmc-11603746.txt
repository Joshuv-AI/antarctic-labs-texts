A novel disulfidptosis-related LncRNA prognostic risk model: predicts the prognosis, tumor microenvironment and drug sensitivity in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Chunlin Ye, Chuan Xu, Yongchao Tang, Yingcheng Qi, Xiaoyue Peng, Guangxia Wei, Lei Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDisulfidptosis is a newly discovered type of cell death that differs from apoptosis, necrosis, ferroptosis and other death modes and is closely related to the occurrence and progression of tumors. However, the predictive potential and biological characteristics of disulfidptosis-related lncRNAs (DRGs-lncRNAs) in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) are unclear.MethodsRNA transcriptome data, clinical information and mutation data for ESCC patients were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Pearson correlation and Cox regression analyses were used to identify the DRGs-lncRNAs associated with overall survival (OS). LASSO regression analysis was used to construct the prognostic model. A nomogram was created to predict the prognosis of patients with ESCC. Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were used to identify the signaling pathways associated with the model. TIMER, CIBERSORT, ESTIMATE and other methods were used to analyze immune infiltration, immune function, immune checkpoints and drug sensitivity. The tumor mutation burden (TMB) were assessed between different risk groups. Real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT‒PCR) was used to detect the expression of DRGs-lncRNAs in ESCC cell lines.ResultsA total of 155 lncRNAs significantly associated with disulfidptosis were identified. Through univariate Cox regression analysis, LASSO regression analysis and multivariate Cox regression analysis, 9 lncRNAs with independent prognostic significance were selected, and a prognosis model was established. Survival analysis with the prognostic model revealed that there were obvious differences in survival between the high- and low-risk groups. Further analysis revealed that the immune microenvironment, immune infiltration, immune function, immune checkpoints, and drug sensitivity significantly differed between the high-risk and low-risk groups. Patients who exhibited both high risk and high tumor mutation burden (TMB) survived shorter, while those who fell into the low risk and low TMB categories survived longer. In addition, RT‒PCR analysis revealed differential expression of DRG lncRNAs between ESCC cell lines and esophageal epithelial cell lines.ConclusionsWe established a DRG-lncRNA prognostic model that can be used to predict the prognosis, tumor mutation burden, immune cell infiltration, and drug sensitivity of ECSS patients. The results of this study provide valuable insights into the understanding of ESCC and provide valuable assistance for the individualized treatment of ESCC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-024-03530-2.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is a widespread and deadly cancer worldwide with an overall poor prognosis. In 2020, EC was responsible for 600 000 new cases and 540 000 deaths globally, making it the leading cause of cancer death [1–3]. In China, EC accounts for more than 50% of the world’s cases of this disease and is particularly serious for disadvantaged groups. EC is difficult to detect early because of its lack of obvious symptoms, and as a result, most patients with EC have reached the middle or late stages by the time they are diagnosed, at which point the best time for surgeryis lost [4]. There are two main subtypes of EC: esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC). ESCC accounts for the majority of EC cases worldwide; its incidence is greater in East Asia and Africa, and EAC is more prevalent in many developed countries [5]. Despite significant achievements in the management of ESCC, including surgery, endoscopic resection, chemoradiotherapy, and immunotherapy, there has been no significant improvement in overall survival in patients with ESCC [6]. This aggressive malignancy poses a serious threat to patient health due to its heterogeneity [7, 8]. Therefore, there is a need to develop new early diagnostic methods, more sensitive biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

In 2023, Professor Gamboyi’s team reported a new mode of death in which cystine uptake mediated by SLC7A11 promotes cell death under glucose starvation conditions, known as “disulfidptosis“ [9]. Disulfidptosis is a novel type of cell death induced by disulfide bond stress characterized by the collapse of cytoskeletal proteins and F-actin due to the intracellular accumulation of disulfide [10]. Preclinical findings suggest that metabolic therapy with glucose transporter (GLUT) inhibitors can trigger disulfide sag and inhibit cancer growth [11]. At present, the study of disulfidptosis in tumors has focused mainly on cancer cell lines that overexpress SLC7A11 and are glucose hungry. Overexpression of SLC7A11 promotes tumor growth but inhibits tumor metastasis, and the SLC7A11 expression level also determines the sensitivity of cancer cells to oxidative stress [12]. However, the role and mechanism of disulfidptosis in ESCC remain unclear.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a class of non-coding RNAs with a length of more than 200 nucleotides [13]. Although lncRNAs do not participate in protein translation, they play a very important role in gene regulation, such as chromatin and genome structure remodeling, RNA stabilization, and transcriptional regulation [14, 15]. Recent findings suggest that lncRNAs are key players in cell differentiation, cell lineage selection, organogenesis, and tissue homeostasis. In addition, lncRNAs are involved in pathological conditions such as cancer and cardiovascular disease, and abnormal expression of lncRNAs leads to tumor cell proliferation, migration, invasion and treatment resistance, thus providing new biomarkers and drug targets [16, 17].

LncRNAs are expected to be key targets for treating a variety of diseases because they control a wide range of biological pathways. In our study, we established and validated a highly predictive prognostic model for ESCC. We comprehensively analyzed its clinical value in predicting clinical prognosis, the tumor immune microenvironment, tumor mutation burden, and drug sensitivity. Our results have the potential to provide new perspectives and approaches for clinical drug treatment strategies and personalized patient management, as well as new theoretical references for elucidating the molecular mechanisms of ESCC.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq)-based transcriptome profiling data, clinical information and status (13 normal samples and 185 tumor samples) were extracted from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Among them, patients with complete follow-up information, survival time ≥ 30 days and complete clinicopathological data were included in the subsequent analysis, and others were excluded. Based on this criterion, a total of 141 patients were included in the study. The clinical features are shown in Table 1. The 16 disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs) were obtained from previous studies (SLC7A11, DECR1, INF2, CD2AP, PDLIM1, ACTN4, MYH9, MYH10, IQGAP1, FLNA, FLNB, TLN1, MYL6, ACTB, DSTN, and CAPZB) [18]. DRG-lncRNA screening and model establishment. mRNA and lncRNA expression data were extracted from the TCGA database, and the Pearson correlation coefficient of lncRNA and DRG expression levels was used for correlation analysis. with an absolute correlation coefficient > 0.4 and p < 0.001 as the screening thresholds. These lncRNAs were defined as DRGs-lncRNAs. The “ggplot2” R package was used to compare the expression of DRGs and lncRNAs.

To establish and validate a DRG-lncRNA prognostic model

First, we used univariate Cox regression to identify prognostically relevant DRGs-lncRNAs, LASSO regression analysis was used to determine the lncRNAs with minimum deviation, and multivariate Cox regression analysis was used to establish a prognostic model based on 9 DRGs-lncRNAs. The risk score was calculated as follows: Risk score = coef (lncRNA1) × expr (lncRNA1) + coef (lncRNA2) ×expr (lncRNA2) +…+ coef (lncRNAn) × expr (lncRNAn). Patients were divided into low-risk and high-risk groups according to the median risk score. To assess the predictive ability of the lncRNA risk score. The “survival” and “survminer” R packages were used to plot the Kaplan‒Meier curves of OS in TCGA. Dispersion plots, risk curves, and heatmaps were drawn to determine the distribution of risk values among patients in different risk groups and to infer the risk of DRG-lncRNA-related death. Using the “timeROC” R package for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to calculate the corresponding time dependence areas under the curve (AUCs) and evaluate the accuracy of the models. Using principal component analysis (PCA) of ESCC samples, the expression patterns of DRGs-lncRNAs were classified, and the spatial dispersion of samples from the two risk groups was visualized using the limma and scatterplot3 R packages.

Nomogram construction

A visual nomogram was created with the “R.rms package” for the clinicopathological characteristics of sex, stage, T stage, N stage, M stage and risk score. We used the calibration curve test to predict that the survival rate was consistent with the actual survival rate.

Correlation enrichment analysis

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of patients in the two risk sets were obtained using the R package limma. After that, GO and KEGG pathway analyses were used to predict the biological pathways and molecular functions that distinguished between the high- and low-risk groups. When the FDR was < 0.05, GO terms or KEGG pathways were considered significantly enriched. With a focus on GO gene sets and KEGG gene sets, enrichment analysis was also performed based on gene expression profiles between the two groups. Gene sets were considered enriched when the p value and FDR were less than 0.05 and 0.25, respectively.

Immune analysis

First, we used several algorithms (XCELL, TIMER, QUANTISEQ, MCPCOUNTER, EPIC, CIBERSORT − ABS, CIBERSORT) to compare the differences in immune response levels between the two risk groups [19]. After collecting the study data, we performed a comparative analysis of immune cell infiltration and immune pathway activation in each patient. We used expression data (estimate) analysis to measure stromal and immune scores using the “estimate” R package to estimate the stromal and immune cells of malignant tumor tissue. In addition, we compared immune checkpoint activation between the low- and high-risk groups via the “ggpubr” R package.

Tumor mutation burden (TMB) analysis

Tumor mutation burden (TMB) data were downloaded from the TCGA database, and the “maftool” package was used to construct waterfall plots to show the difference in TMB between the high- and low-risk groups. Survival analysis was performed to explore the influence of the TMB on overall survival.

Drug sensitivity analysis

To explore potential drugs that may be effective in patients with ESCC, we used the pRRophetic algorithm to assess drug sensitivity in patients with ESCC [20]. When the inhibitory concentration (IC50) is lower, the sensitivity to the drug is greater, which is a better guide for patients to use drugs.

Cell culture

The human ESCC cell lines HEEC, TE1, KYSE-150, and KYSE-450 were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The HEEC, TE1, KYSE-150, and KYSE-450 ESCC cell lines were grown in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA). All media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (ExCell, China) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Biotech, China). The cells were cultured in an environment at 37 °C and a CO2 concentration of 5%.

Quantitative real-time PCR

According to the manufacturer’s instructions, RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol universal RNA reagent (TIANGEN, China). For cDNA synthesis, total RNA (1 µg) was reverse-transcribed using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit and gDNA Eraser (Takara, Japan). The complementary DNA (cDNA) obtained by reverse transcription was diluted 10-fold with RNasefree distilled water. Quantification was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Tli RNaseH Plus) (Takara, Japan) and an ABI7500 System (Thermo Fisher, USA). The analysis was repeated three times for each specimen from each target. GAPDH expression was used as a reference. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

We used R 3.6.3 for all the statistical analyses. K‒M estimates and the log-rank test were used for survival analysis. Pearson correlation was used for correlation analysis. Cox regression analysis was used for univariate and multivariate survival analyses. The Wilcoxon test was used to assess differences between two groups, and ANOVA was used to assess differences among three groups. GraphPad Prism 8 was used for statistical analysis and graphing. Statistical significance was considered at two-sided p < 0.05.

Results

Expression of DRG-lncRNAs in ESCC

The workflow of the prognostic model analysis is illustrated in Fig. 1. Briefly, esophageal tissues from 185 ESCC patients and 13 healthy controls were downloaded from the TCGA-ESCC dataset, and the expression levels of 16 DRGs (SLC7A11, DECR1, INF2, CD2AP, PDLIM1, ACTN4, MYH9, MYH10, IQGAP1, FLNA, FLNB, TLN1, MYL6, ACTB, DSTN and CAPZB) were analyzed. Then, 1233 DRG-lncRNAs were identified by coexpression analysis (Supplementary Table 2), and a total of 155 lncRNAs significantly associated with disulfidptosis were identified by Pearson analysis on the basis of R > 0.4 and P < 0.001 (Supplementary Table 3).

Identification of DRG-lncRNAs in ESCC and construction of a prognostic model

A total of 155 lncRNAs significantly associated with disulfidptosis were identified. Then, through univariate Cox regression analysis, 23 DRG-lncRNAs significantly correlated with the survival prognosis of ESCC patients were identified (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, we applied the Lasso algorithm to these lncRNAs and identified 15 lncRNAs with the smallest cross-validation errors (Fig. 2B, C). By multivariate Cox regression analysis, a prognostic model composed of 9 DRG-lncRNAs was established (Table 2, Fig. S1). Among them, 7 lncRNAs (AC118755.1, IPO5P1, AC104041.1, AC138207.5, AC092484.1, AC083799.1, and AC012467.2) were harmful prognostic factors, and the others (NALT1 and LINC01770) were favorable prognostic factors (Fig. S2). The expression levels of 9 DRG-lncRNAs in ESCC patients are shown in Fig. 2D. We further visualized the lncRNAs with Cytoscape and the ggalluvial R software package, and the coexpression network is shown (Fig. 2E). To explore the correlations between the expression of 9 lncRNAs and 16 disulfidptosis genes, a correlation heatmap was generated based on TCGA samples ( Fig. 2F). The risk scores of these 9 lncRNAs were utilized to establish a DRG-lncRNA prognostic model.

Predicting the prognosis of ESCC patients with the prognostic model

Next, the risk score formula was used to calculate the risk score of ESCC patients, and patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the median risk score. There was a significant difference in OS between patients with ECSS (P < 0.05), which confirmed that the OS of the low-risk group was significantly greater than that of the high-risk group (Fig. 3A). We also constructed a heatmap based on the differential expression of DRG-lncRNAs in the high-risk and low-risk groups (Fig. 3B), and used scatter plots and risk curves to show the survival status and risk scores of ESCC patients. With increasing risk score, a growing number of patients died (Fig. 3C, D). Then, we analyzed differences in clinicopathological variables between the high-risk group and the low-risk group and found differences in N stage (p < 0.01), stage (p < 0.01), and fustat (p < 0.01) between the high-risk group and the low-risk group (Fig. 3E).

To evaluate whether the above 9 DRG-lncRNA prognostic models are independent prognostic factors for ESCC, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed. Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that stage, T stage, N stage, M stage and risk score were significantly associated with the OS of ESCC patients (Fig. 4A). Multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that the risk score was an independent predictor of OS in ESCC patients (Fig. 4B). The AUC of the risk score was 0.894, which was better than that of other clinicopathological variables for predicting the prognosis of ESCC patients (Fig. 4C). The AUCs of 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival were 0.778, 0.739 and 0.891, respectively, which indicated good predictive performance (Fig. 4D). These findings were also supported by the C-index curves (Fig. 4E).

Construction and verification of the nomogram

To improve the utility of the prognostic models, we created a predictive nomogram by combining scores of relevant clinical factors and 9 DRG-lncRNAs risk scores. This enables an accurate prediction of the probability of survival. We used the nomogram to estimate the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates of ESCC patients (Fig. 5A). The calibration plots of the nomogram for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival indicated that the mortality estimated by the nomogram was close to the actual mortality (Fig. 5B-D).

Principal component analysis

All genes, disulfidptosis-associated genes, disulfidptosis-associated lncRNAs, and model lncRNA sets were subjected to four different principal component analysis (PCA) analyses to assess their contributions to the risk model. Notably, PCA plots of the risk lncRNAs showed a clear separation between high-risk and low-risk patients, indicating significant differences, and were successfully stratified into two relatively independent clusters (Fig. 6A-D). These findings further validate the usefulness of our model in accurately distinguishing between low- and high-risk groups.

Functional enrichment analysis of DRG-lncRNAs

To further explore the functional signaling pathways that may be enriched in patients in the prognostic risk model, we performed GO, KEGG, and GSEA analyses. GO analysis revealed that biological process (BP) terms were mainly associated with the regulation of actin, filament-based process platelet aggregation, homotypic cell‒cell adhesion, etc.; molecular function (MF) terms were mainly associated with cell cortex focal adhesion and cell-substrate junction, etc.; and cellular component (CC) terms were mainly related to actin binding, actin filament binding and cadherin binding, etc. (Fig. 7A, B). The KEGG results suggested that these genes were mainly enriched in tight junctions, motor proteins, focal adhesion, and regulation of the actin cytoskeleton (Fig. 7C, D). Functional annotation was further performed using GSEA. The GO terms peptidyl threonine dephosphorylation, positive regulation of protein polyubi termination, pyrimidine nucleotide catabolic process, regulation of protein kinase a signaling and transferase activity transferring nitrogenous groups were enriched in ESCC samples with high risk scores. In contrast, ADP metabolic process, nucleotide phosphorylation, response to mitochondrial depolarization, nuclear outer membrane and phosphatidylinositol 3 phosphate binding were enriched in ESCC samples with low risk scores (Fig. 7E). In addition, enriched KEGG pathways, cell cycle, DNA replication, mismatch repair, oocyte meiosis and the pentose phosphate pathway were identified in the high-risk group. Allograft rejection, asthma, autoimmune thyroid disease, hematopoietic cell lineage and the intestinal immune network for IgA production were enriched in the low-risk group (Fig. 7F).

Analysis of immune infiltration, the immune microenvironment, and immune checkpoints in patients in the high- and low-risk groups

Our study investigated the correlation between DRG-lncRNA model scores and immune infiltration in ESCC patients. Violin plot was generated to visualize various immune cells. The results showed that CD8 + T cells, activated memory CD4 + T cells, resting memory CD4 + T cells, monocytes, resting dendritic cells, and M0 macrophages were significantly enriched in the high-risk group (Fig. 8A). In addition, in the TIME analysis, the high-risk group of samples with relatively low stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores had a decreasing trend (Fig. 8B-D). To further clarify the immune alterations in ESCC, we used single gene set sample enrichment analysis (SsGSEA) to analyze the levels of 16 kinds of tumor-infiltrating immune cells and the activity of 12 kinds of immunity-related functions. Our results showed that B cells, CD8 + T cells, iDCs, macrophages, neutrophils, pDCs, T helper cells, Tfh cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells, TILs and Tregs were significantly positively related to the model, adc, dc, mast cells and NK cells had no significant changes, and we found that the immune functions of high-risk patients were significantly upregulated (Fig. 8E, F). Next, we analyzed immune checkpoints to identify potential targets for individualized immunotherapy and found increased expression of TNFRS and CD276 in high-risk patients (Fig. 8G).

Tumor mutation burden analysis

Then, the “maftools” software package was used to analyze the mutation data in the TCGA database. Our results showed that the top three mutated genes in the high-risk group were TP53 (77%), TTN (34%) and MUC16 (14%), and the top three mutated genes in the low-risk group were PT53 (78%), TTN (48%) and MUC16 (29%) (Fig. 9A, B). According to the Kaplan‒Meier survival analysis, patients in the high-TMB group had a poorer prognosis (Fig. 9C). When the TMB and risk score were combined, the survival rate of patients in the high-TMB + high-risk group was the worst, and patients in the low-TMB + low-risk group had the best survival rate (Fig. 9D).

Drug sensitivity analysis

To explore the sensitivity of patients in the high- and low-risk groups to antineoplastic drugs and potential therapeutic agents for patients with ESCC, we performed a drug sensitivity analysis using the “pRRophetic” package to screen promising antitumor drugs. Drug sensitivity increased with decreasing IC50 values. The results showed that the high-risk group may be more sensitive to vinorelbine (p = 0.039), bicalutamide (p = 2.8*10^-7), thapsigargin (p = 4.5*10^-9), CMK (p = 4.1*10^-7), docetaxel (p = 0.00055), doxorubicin (p = 1.2*10^-6), erlotinib (p = 1.5*10^-10), gemcitabine (p = 0.0035), MGA132 (p = 0.00022), parthenolide (p = 1.3*10^-8), tipifarnib (p = 6.8*10^-7), and salubrinal (p = 0.00093). These drugs may be potential drugs for the treatment of ESCC (Fig. 10A-L). This finding is very important and may provide guidance for drug selection in patients with ESCC and provide new ideas for clinical diagnosis and treatment. However, further exploration and experimental verification are still needed.

Discussion

As one of the most common tumors, ESCC has a gradually increasing incidence and accounts for approximately 20% of cancer deaths, with more than 400 000 lives worldwide every year [2, 21, 22]. Although significant progress has been made in understanding the biological mechanisms of ESCC, progress in surgical techniques and radiation and chemotherapy schemes has also been made, but the prognosis of patients with advanced ESCC is still poor [23–25]. Therefore, further analysis and exploration of the mechanism of ESCC development are desperately needed. A recent study revealed a new form of programmed cell death, namely, “disulfidptosis”, which is different from previous reports of cell death, such as cell apoptosis and necrosis, pyroptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis and ferroptosis [9]. This type of cystine is induced by excessive accumulation of disulfide stress caused by rapid cell death. This pattern of death has attracted the interest of scholars. In recent years, more and more lncRNAs have been studied in cancer. PANoptosis related long noncoding ribonucleic acid signatures have been used to predict the clinical outcomes and immune landscape of lung adenocarcinoma [26]. In addition, PANoptosis-related long non-coding RNA signature can be used to predict the prognosis and immune landscape of pancreatic cancer [27]. As new biomarkers, lncRNAs play important roles in the development of ESCC tumors [28, 29]. However, the role of disulfidptosis-related lncRNAs in ESCC has not been investigated. In this study, we explored the lncRNAs associated with disulfidptosis in ESCC, constructed a novel prognostic risk model based on disulfidptosis-related lncRNAs, and systematically investigated the immune microenvironment to determine the appropriate clinical treatment and guide patient prognosis in ESCC patients. It provides a more accurate tool for the prognostic evaluation of patients with ESCC.

To explore the key role of disulfidptosis in cancer and the interaction between disulfidptosis and lncRNAs, we identified and validated a set of key lncRNAs associated with disulfidptosis in ESCC, including AC118755.1, IPO5P1, AC104041.1, AC138207.5, AC092484.1, AC083799.1, AC012467.2, NALT1, and LINC01770. Some of these DRG lncRNAs have been reported to be closely related to the occurrence and development of tumors. Lianghao Zhang et al. reported that IPO5P1 can be used to predict the prognosis and immunotherapy efficacy of patients with bladder cancer [30]. In addition, AC104041.1 has been shown to have antitumor activity in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma through the Wnt2B/β-catenin pathway [31]. AC138207.5 is upregulated in colon cancer and is associated with poor prognosis in patients with colon cancer [32]. Recent studies have shown that AC138207.5 is closely related to the occurrence of cuproptosis in prostate cancer [33]. In addition to being a disulfidptosis-associated lncRNA, AC083799.1 has also been identified as an autophagy-associated lncRNA and has been shown to be a potential prognostic marker for endometrial cancer [34]. The lncrnas identified in our study have prognostic and anti-tumor potential in various cancers, although their roles in disulfidptosis are rarely reported. However, their specific mechanisms of action in ESCC are not fully understood and require further study. Although little is currently known about these DRG-lncRNAs, their importance should not be underestimated.

In addition, we constructed a prognostic model based on 9 DRG-lncRNAs to predict the survival of ESCC patients, which can be used as an independent prognostic indicator of ESCC. The model accurately classified patients into two groups: low-risk and high-risk groups, and the low-risk group had a better prognosis. The ROC curve was subsequently constructed to predict the effectiveness of prognosis, and the results showed that the AUCs of the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates were 0.778, 0.739, and 0.891, respectively, indicating that the 9 DRG-lncRNA models in our study may be good prognostic models for ESCC. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these 9 lncRNAs may be involved in a variety of biological processes and signaling pathways, including DNA replication, mismatch repair, and cell cycle regulation. They are closely related to the occurrence of ESCC. These results provide important clues for revealing the potential mechanisms of these lncRNAs in the development of ESCC, which may facilitate future functional experimental studies and provide novel therapeutic targets for the treatment of ESCC. The immune environment is the most critical part of the tumor microenvironment and plays a crucial role in regulating tumor progression and anticancer treatment response [35, 36]. We found that CD8 + T cells, activated memory CD4 + T cells, and M0 macrophages were significantly enriched in the high-risk group. Our results showed that B cells, CD8 + T cells, iDCs, macrophages, neutrophils, pDCs, T helper cells, Tfh cells, Th1 cells, Th2 cells, TILs and Tregs were significantly positively related to the model and that the immune functions of high-risk patients were significantly upregulated. This finding reflects the role of disulfidptosis-associated lncRNAs in the tumor immune microenvironment.

In conclusion, our study is the first to report the involvement of disulfidptosis in ESCC, which may lead to new breakthroughs and advances in the field of ESCC treatment. However, our study had some limitations. First, we identified coexpressing lncRNAs associated with disulfidptosis and constructed a prognostic model based on 9 lncRNAs. However, it is still necessary to further explore the association between core lncRNAs and ESCC prognosis and verify this association in clinical samples. Second, our findings mainly depend on comprehensive bioinformatics, and more basic studies are needed to confirm the biological function of the identified lncRNAs in disulfidptosis. Third, our study focused on the regulatory mechanism of lncRNAs, and more detailed functional and mechanistic studies are needed. In summary, further experimental studies are needed to improve the results of this study and to comprehensively elucidate the role of lncRNAs in the development of ESCC. To this end, we plan to construct knockdown and overexpression lncRNA ESCC cell lines in subsequent studies, and the obtained results were further explored and verified at the cellular and animal levels by molecular biology experiments such as RT‒qPCR, Western blotting and luciferase reporter gene expression.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study established a novel DRG-lncRNA model that can independently predict OS in ESCC patients. Analysis of immune microenvironment, immune infiltration, immune function, immune checkpoints and drug sensitivity revealed that DRG-lncRNA markers were closely related to immune cell infiltration, immune function and chemotherapy drug sensitivity. The DRG-lncRNA prediction model can be used to guide the selection of appropriate clinical medication for patients with ESCC.

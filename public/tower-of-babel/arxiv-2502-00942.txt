Large deviations of geodesic midpoint fluctuations in last-passage percolation with general i.i.d. weights
Tom Alberts, Riddhipratim Basu, Sean Groathouse, Xiao Shen

Abstract
The study of transversal fluctuations of the optimal path is a crucial aspect of the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class. In this work, we establish the large deviation limit for the midpoint transversal fluctuations in a general last-passage percolation (LPP) model with mild assumption on the i.i.d. weights. The rate function is expressed in terms of the right tail large deviation rate function of the last-passage value and the shape function. When the weights are chosen to be i.i.d. exponential random variables, our result verifies a conjecture communicated to us by Liu [ 18 ] , showing the asymptotic probability of the geodesic from ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0) to ( n , n ) (n,n) following the corner path ( 0 , 0 ) → ( n , 0 ) → ( n , n ) (0,0)\rightarrow(n,0)\rightarrow(n,n) is ( 4 / e 2 ) n + o ⁡ ( n ) ({4}/{e^{2}})^{n+o(n)} .

Introduction

Last-passage percolation (LPP) is one of many stochastic models characterized by random growth along optimal paths in a random environment. A central focus in LPP, and more broadly in the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class
[
16
]
, is the study of
transversal fluctuations
. These fluctuations quantify the deviation of the optimal path from reference lines and play a critical role in uncovering the geometric properties and space-time growth profiles of the model.

The main focus of this paper is a large deviation principle for unusually large transversal fluctuations of the optimal path, on the scale of the distance between the two endpoints of the geodesic. The KPZ universality class is broadly divided into two categories:
exactly solvable models
, where rigorous mathematical results can be derived using intricate algebraic methods, and
general
or
non-solvable models
, for which such fine tools are not directly applicable. Our approach is probabilistic and applies to general models beyond the exactly solvable cases. The main results are presented in the next section.

1.1
Main results

Last-passage percolation is a well-studied model in the KPZ universality class, yet has seen limited work on large deviation properties of the associated random geometry. In the LPP model, independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) weights
\{\omega_{\mathbf{z}}\}_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
are associated with the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Given two coordinatewise-ordered points
{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, an up-right path between them is a nearest neighbor path
\gamma=\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i=0}^{|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|_{1}}
such that

\gamma_{0}=\mathbf{u},\quad\gamma_{|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}|_{1}}=\mathbf{v},\textup{ and }\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_{i}\in\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2}\},

where
\mathbf{e}_{1}
,
\mathbf{e}_{2}
are the standard basis vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The weight of each up-right path is the sum of the
\omega
variables along the path, and the
last-passage value

G_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}
is then defined to be the maximum weight among all up-right paths from
\mathbf{u}
to
\mathbf{v}
while excluding the starting point
\mathbf{u}
, i.e.

G_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textup{up-right}\\
{\gamma:\mathbf{u}\rightarrow\mathbf{v}}\end{subarray}}\;\;\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\gamma\setminus\{\mathbf{u}\}}\omega_{\mathbf{z}}.

(1.1)

We set
G_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}=-\infty
if there is no up-right path between
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
.
The maximizing paths for
G_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}
are commonly referred to as the
geodesics
.

In this paper, we will assume that our i.i.d. weights
\{\omega_{\mathbf{z}}\}
are non-negative and satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\exists\,\alpha>0\textup{ such that }\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\alpha\omega_{\mathbf{z}}}\big]<\infty,\\
\omega_{\mathbf{z}}\textup{ is continuous, i.e. its CDF is continuous}\\
\forall\,\beta<\infty,\mathbb{P}(\omega_{\mathbf{z}}>\beta)>0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Among the three assumptions stated, the exponential moment condition is essential to our analysis. The other two assumptions, while not strictly necessary, serve to simplify notation and streamline our arguments. Specifically, the continuity assumption guarantees the uniqueness of geodesics; however, this could be adjusted by considering the rightmost geodesics in cases where uniqueness does not hold. Additionally, the unbounded support of the weights ensures that the large deviation rate function for the right tail of the last-passage value remains finite.

Throughout the paper, we will assume that
n
is an even integer. This is only for the simplification of notation, as all of our results and proofs also hold when
n
is odd. Let
\textup{Mid}_{0,n}
denote the midpoint of the geodesic from
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
, which we regard as a random point on the line

\mathcal{L}_{n/2}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x+y=n\}.

Our main result below gives the large deviation estimate for the point
\textup{Mid}_{0,n}
. To present this result, we first introduce the limiting shape function of the last-passage values and their right tail large deviations rate function.

We mostly use the passage times
G_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{v}}
where
\mathbf{v}
is on the line
x+y=n
, which we typically write as
\mathbf{v}=(\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor,\lfloor n/2-tn\rfloor)
with
-\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2}
. The right tail large deviation rate function of the last-passage value is defined as

{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor,\lfloor n/2-tn\rfloor)}\geq rn).

The limit exists due to Fekete’s Subadditive Lemma. Section
2.1
has more information and background on this rate function. We will also make heavy use of the shape function

\mu_{t}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G_{\mathbf{0},(\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor,\lfloor n/2-tn\rfloor)}}{n}.

The existence of the limit is guaranteed by Kingman’s Subadditive Ergodic Theorem
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
0<t\leq 1/2
such that
\mu_{0}>\mu_{t}
. For each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
n_{0}
such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
, it holds that

e^{-2n(\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{0})+\epsilon)}\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\geq\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big)\leq e^{-2n(\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)}.

Thus, in the limit, we have

2{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big)=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\geq\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big).

The assumption
\mu_{0}>\mu_{t}
is equivalent to requiring
(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)
to be outside of the diagonal cone for which the limit shape is flat. As long as the weights are not almost surely constant, the flat stretch cannot take up the entire interval
t\in[0,1/2]
, i.e. there is an
\eta\in(0,1/2)
such that
\mu_{0}>\mu_{t}
for all
t\in[\eta,1/2]
, which we now explain. First, note we have a strictly inequality
\mu_{0}>\mu_{1/2}
because

\mu_{0}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2,n/2)}\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}\Big[\omega_{(i,i)}+\max\{\omega_{(i+1,i)},\omega_{(i,i+1)}\}\Big]>\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{(i,0)}=\mu_{1/2}.

Then, it is known that
\mu_{t}
is continuous in
t
up to the boundary
[
19
, Theorem 2.3]
, so our claim follows.
In addition, this assumption is natural because, in the only known scenario where this flat segment appears in the limit shape—namely, due to the supercritical oriented percolation at
\operatorname{esssup}\omega_{\mathbf{z}}
, which is conjectured in
[
6
, Question 2.5.4]
to be the only cause of this phenomenon—we have
\mathcal{J}=\infty
for directions strictly inside this cone. This is illustrated in Figure
1.1
.

(0,0)
(n,n)
\mathcal{L}_{n/2}

Figure 1.1:
Looking within the supercritical oriented percolation cone at
(0,0)
in the northeast direction and the cone at
(n,n)
in the southwest direction, if we fix a direction (shown in orange) that lies strictly inside the cone at
(0,0)
, as
n
becomes large, with high probability, there will exist a geodesic formed by concatenating open paths from within the two cones, whose midpoint is to the right of the chosen direction.

Next, we record a similar result for the endpoint of the point-to-line geodesic. The point-to-line last-passage value is defined to be

{G}_{\mathbf{0},\mathcal{L}_{n/2}}=\max_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{G}_{\mathbf{0},(\lfloor n/2+k\rfloor,\lfloor n/2-k\rfloor)}

Let
\textup{End}_{0,n}
denote the endpoint of the point-to-line geodesic.

Proposition 1.2
.

Fix
0<t\leq 1/2
such that
\mu_{0}>\mu_{t}
. For each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
n_{0}
such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
, it holds that

e^{-n(\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{0})+\epsilon)}\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{End}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{End}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\geq\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big)\leq e^{-n(\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)}.

Thus, in the limit, it holds that

{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{End}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big)=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{End}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\geq\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\Big).

1.2
Relations to the existing literature

Precise large deviation principles (LDP) for the transversal fluctuations have not been derived in the past. For general weight distributions, the only result has been a concentration inequality in a closely related model known as
first-passage percolation
, see
[
17
, Lemma 9.10]
. To state this result in the LPP setting, let
D_{0,n}
denote the displacement of the geodesic
\Gamma_{0,n}
from the diagonal line
y=x
. This result states that when the shape function has an exposed point in the diagonal direction (i.e. the limit shape has no flat segment at the diagonal), then

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{\\
n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(D_{0,n}<\epsilon n)=1.

Even for exactly solvable models, there has been little prior work on the large deviations principle of the transversal fluctuations for exactly solvable lattice models. A recent paper of Liu
[
18
]
provides an exact formula for the probability of a geodesic passing through a specified edge in the exactly solvable exponential LPP. Liu’s formula is expressed in terms of iterated contour integrals and, as noted by the author, it is challenging to extract asymptotics from it.

For the directed landscape, another exactly solvable model which is known to be the scaling limit of certain exactly solvable models and is conjectured to be the universal scaling limit for all models within the KPZ universality class
[
12
]
, the LDP for the last-passage value and the transversal fluctuations of geodesics have been established in
[
11
]
. However, since the directed landscape arises as a scaling limit of LPP under KPZ scaling, the transversal large deviations in the directed landscape correspond to
moderate
deviations in pre-limit models. For the moderate deviations of the transversal fluctuation in FPP, under the curvature assumptions of the limit shape, the works
[
5
,
10
,
20
]
established that
D_{0,n}\leq n^{3/4+o(1)}
with high probability. In exponential LPP a series of papers demonstrated the exponential decay of the probability of the rare event that
D_{0,n}
exceeds the typical scale
n^{2/3}

[
1
,
8
,
9
,
15
,
22
]
. More recently, the precise leading-order constant in the exponential decay of the probabilities for large transversal fluctuations was determined in
[
2
]
. Our approach is partly inspired by the geometric arguments presented in that work.

Lastly, regarding the rate function
\mathcal{J}_{t}
, a variational formulation over paths was recently derived in
[
23
]
in the context of FPP, resembling the large deviation rate function established for the directed landscape in
[
11
]
.

1.3
The
t=1/2
Case

The
t=1/2
case corresponds to
\textup{Mid}_{0,n}=(n,0)
, or in other words the geodesic traverses the extreme corner path
(0,0)\rightarrow(n,0)\rightarrow(n,n)
. In private communication Liu used heuristics from his exact formula in
[
18
]
to conjecture an asymptotic formula for
\log\mathbb{P}(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}=(n,0))
in the exponential LPP setting. We verify his conjecture using Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume the
\omega_{\mathbf{z}}
are i.i.d. exponential random variables with rate one. Let
\Gamma_{0,n}
denote the geodesic between
(0,0)
and
(n,n)
. Then

-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\bigg((n,0)\in\Gamma_{0,n}\bigg)=2-2\log 2.

The application of Theorem
1.1
is simple. First, since there is only one path from
(0,0)\rightarrow(n,0)
it follows that
J_{1/2}
is the rate function of a sum of i.i.d. random variables. This property of
J_{1/2}
holds for general weights
\omega_{\mathbf{z}}
satisfying our assumptions. For rate one exponential random variables it is known that
J_{1/2}(x)=x-\log x-1
, and the shape function satisfies
\mu_{0}=2
thanks to
[
21
]
. Thus Corollary
1.3
follows.

It is interesting to compare the asymptotics of Corollary
1.3
with those of a uniformly chosen up-right path
\mathbf{U}_{0,n}
from
(0,0)\rightarrow(n,n)
, for which

-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\bigg((n,0)\in\mathbf{U}_{0,n}\bigg)=2\log 2>2-2\log 2.

In other words, the exponential LPP geodesic has an exponentially larger probability of following the extreme corner path than a uniformly chosen walk does. One intuition behind this phenomenon, as explained in
[
3
]
, is that the corner path shares weights with a comparatively smaller number of paths, and therefore faces less competition to being the geodesic. The same intuition should be correct for any large deviation of the transversal fluctuations, leading us to conjecture that

2{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})\leq-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\bigg(\textup{Mid}(\mathbf{U}_{0,n})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=\lfloor n/2+tn\rfloor\bigg)

for all
t\in(0,1/2]
. Note this inequality should hold for a broad class of weight distributions. In this case, setting
t=1/2
makes both
\mathcal{J}_{1/2}
and the right hand side limit relatively explicit. This raises the possibility of deriving meaningful bounds on
\mu_{0}
for general weight distributions.

Preliminaries

To simplify the notation, let us extend the LPP model onto
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Without switching the notation, let

G_{(a,b),(c,d)}=G_{(\lfloor a\rfloor,\lfloor b\rfloor),(\lfloor c\rfloor,\lfloor d\rfloor)},

and we note that this change does not affect the asymptotic results: suppose
J
is a positive constant, for any large constant
r

e^{-\lfloor r\rfloor(J+\epsilon)}\leq e^{-r(J+\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\leq e^{-\lfloor r\rfloor(J+\frac{\epsilon}{2})}\quad\textup{ and }\quad e^{-\lfloor r\rfloor(J-\epsilon)}\leq e^{-r(J-2\epsilon)}\leq e^{-\lfloor r\rfloor(J-2\epsilon)}.

In the following sections, we briefly recall the existing literature on the right tail large deviations of the last-passage value.

2.1
Right tail large deviation rate function for the last-passage value

In this section, we record a few properties of the right tail large deviation rate function of the last-passage value. These results are well established and follow from the superadditivity of the LPP model. Similar results have appeared in
[
17
]
for FPP and
[
14
]
for the directed polymer model. Here we note that right-tail deviations in FPP correspond to left-tail deviations in LPP, and conversely, left-tail deviations in FPP correspond to right-tail deviations in LPP.

Recall the shape function
\mu_{t}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{-1}G_{(0,0),(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]

[
17
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

For each
-1/2\leq t\leq 1/2
and
r>0
, the following
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-valued limit exists:

{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}\geq rn).

The function
\mathcal{J}_{t}(\bullet)
is continuous and convex. Moreover,
\mathcal{J}_{t}(r)=0
if and only if
r\leq\mu_{t}
, and it is strictly increasing for
r\geq\mu_{t}
.

Next, we will record several known results involving the direction parameter
t
, beginning with the convexity of
{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)
in its two variables
(t,r)
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

The rate function
{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)
is convex in its variables
(t,r)
. More precisely, fix
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
-1/2\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq 1/2
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
(t,r)=\lambda(t_{1},r_{1})+(1-\lambda)(t_{2},r_{2})
. Then,

{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)\leq\lambda{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{1}}(r_{1})+(1-\lambda){\mathcal{J}}_{t_{2}}(r_{2}).

Furthermore, the function
{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r):(0,1/2)\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous.

A second consequence is a monotonicity property in the directional parameter
t
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For each
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq 1/2
, it holds that

{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{1}}(r)\leq{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{2}}(r).

Proof.

First, we will show that
{\mathcal{J}}_{0}(r)\leq{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)
for all
0<t\leq 1/2
. This directly follows from our convexity result. Rewrite
0=-t/2+t/2
, by convexity and symmetry

{\mathcal{J}}_{0}(r)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}_{-t}(r)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r)={\mathcal{J}}_{t}(r).

Now fix
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq 1/2
, let us rewrite
t_{1}=\lambda t_{2}+(1-\lambda)0
where
\lambda=\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}
. By convexity again,

{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{1}}(r)\leq\lambda{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{2}}(r)+(1-\lambda){\mathcal{J}}_{0}(r)\leq\lambda{\mathcal{J}}_{t_{2}}(r)+(1-\lambda){\mathcal{J}}_{t_{2}}(r)={\mathcal{J}}_{t_{2}}(r),

and this completes the proof.
∎

2.2
Large deviation estimates of the last-passage value

In this section, we record the following estimates of the last-passage value on the large deviation scale.
The right tail large deviation estimate is stated below.

Proposition 2.4
.

For each
|t|\leq 1/2
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
n_{0}
such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
and
x>0
, the following holds

e^{-n(\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{t}+x)+\epsilon)}\leq\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}\geq\mu_{t}n+xn)\leq e^{-n\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{t}+x)}.

The lower bound follows from Proposition
2.1
, while the upper bound follows from Fekete’s Subadditive Lemma

\mathcal{J}_{t}(r)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}\geq rn)=\inf_{n}\bigg\{-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}\geq rn)\bigg\}

as
\{-\log\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}\geq rn)\}_{n}
is a subadditive sequence of real numbers.

The left tail large deviation estimate is stated below.

Proposition 2.5
.

For each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
c
such that

\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2,n/2)}\leq\mu_{0}n-\epsilon n)\leq e^{-cn^{2}}.

The above proposition is essentially the same as
[
17
, Theorem 5.2]
, which proves the upper tail large deviations in FPP with bounded edge weights. It was discussed in
[
7
, Section 4.1]
how the same proof can be adapted to the setting of Proposition
2.5
but this was not formally stated as a result. For the sake of completeness, we shall provide a short sketch of the proof.

Sketch.

The first step is to notice that for fixed but large
K
, on the event
\{G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2,n/2)}\leq\mu_{0}n-\epsilon n\}
, one has
G_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}\leq\mu_{0}n-\epsilon n
for each
0\leq i\leq\delta n
. Let us denote
G^{K}_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}
to be weight of the maximum weight path joining
(3iK,0)
,
(n/2,n/2-3iK)
that is contained within a width
K
strip around the straight line joining the end points. By definition,
G^{K}_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}
are independent across
i
and since
G^{K}_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}\leq G_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}
we have

\mathbb{P}(G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2,n/2)}\leq\mu_{0}n-\epsilon n)\leq\prod_{i=0}^{\delta n}\mathbb{P}(G^{K}_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}\leq\mu_{0}n-\epsilon n).

One can now observe that given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
K
sufficiently large such that
\mathbb{E}G_{\mathbf{0},(K/2,K/2)}\geq(\mu_{0}-\epsilon/2)K
. A large deviation estimate then implies that (see
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
) that

\mathbb{P}\bigg(G^{K}_{\mathbf{0},(n/2,n/2)}\leq(\mu_{0}-\frac{3\epsilon}{4})n\bigg)\leq e^{-cn}.

By translation invariance, and choosing
\delta
sufficiently small depending on
\epsilon,K,\mu_{0}
, one also gets for some
c>0
and all
n
sufficiently large

\mathbb{P}(G^{K}_{(3iK,0),(n/2,n/2-3iK)}\leq\mu_{0}n-\epsilon n)\leq e^{-cn}

for all
i\leq\delta n
. The result follows.
∎

3
Proof of the upper bound in Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will show that for each
\epsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\geq n/2+tn\Big)\leq ne^{-2n({\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)},

(3.3)

and this implies the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
since for
n
sufficiently large

ne^{-2n({\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)}\leq e^{-2n({\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})-2\epsilon)}.

To show (
3.3
), it suffices to prove the following bound

\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}=(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)\Big)\leq e^{-2n({\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)}.

(3.4)

Then, (
3.3
) follows from a union bound and the monotonicity of the rate function from Proposition
2.3
, as shown below

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\geq n/2+tn\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=nt}^{n}\mathbb{P}\Big(\textup{Mid}_{0,n}=(n/2+s,n/2-s)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=\lfloor nt\rfloor}^{n}e^{-2n({\mathcal{J}}_{s/n}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)}\qquad\text{by \eqref{pt_est}}

\displaystyle\leq ne^{-2n({\mathcal{J}}_{t}(\mu_{0})-\epsilon)}.

For the rest of this section, we will prove (
3.4
).
Starting with the setup, recall the shape function
\mu_{t}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{-1}G_{(0,0),(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}
. We fix
0<t\leq 1/2
such that
\mu_{0}>\mu_{t}
.
For each
\epsilon>0
, we will fix a small value
\delta>0
such that

\mu_{0}-\delta>\mu_{t}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad\;\Big|\;\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{0}-\delta)-\mathcal{J}_{t}(\mu_{0})\;\Big|\;\leq\epsilon.

(3.5)

This is possible due to the continuity result for the rate function in Proposition
2.1
. Now observe that the midpoint
\textup{Mid}_{0,n}
passes through a particular point if and only if the concatenation of the longest path to and from that point is also the longest path connecting
(0,0)
to
(n,n)
, i.e.

\textup{Mid}_{0,n}=(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)\iff G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}+G_{(n/2+tn,n/2-tn),(n,n)}=G_{\mathbf{0},(n,n)}

By decomposing the right-hand side according to the value of

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}(

\displaystyle G_{\mathbf{0},(n/2+tn,n/2-tn)}+G_{(n/2+tn,n/2-tn),(n,n)}\geq 2\mu_{0}n-2\delta n).

(3.7)

Note that

due to Proposition
2.5

(

4

In this section, we will show the weaker lower bound that for each

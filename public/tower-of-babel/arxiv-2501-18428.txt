Convergence of a semi-explicit scheme for a one dimensional periodic nonlocal eikonal equation modeling dislocation dynamics Thanks: The authors thank the anonymous referees for comments and corrections that helped us to improve this paper substantially.
Diana Al Zareef, Ahmad El Hajj, Hassan Ibrahim, Antoine Zurek

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive a periodic model from a one dimensional nonlocal eikonal equation set on the full space modeling dislocation dynamics. Thanks to a gradient entropy estimate, we show that this periodic model converges toward the initial one when the period goes to infinity. Moreover, we design a semi-explicit numerical scheme for the periodic model that we introduce. We show the well-posedness of the scheme and a discrete gradient entropy inequality. We also prove the convergence of the scheme and we present some numerical experiments.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Physical motivation

In this paper, we are interested in the numerical study of a phase field model introduced by Rodney, Le Bouar and Finel in
[
27
]
describing the dynamics of dislocation curves in crystal materials. Dislocations are linear defects that glide in crystallographic slip planes and whose motion is responsible for the plastic deformation of metals (see
[
6
]
for a physical description of dislocations). A given dislocation moves under the action of the so-called Peach-Koehler force computed from linear elasticity equations, where the internal self-stress generated by the dislocation curve represents one of its principal components. We are mainly concerned with the viscoplastic behavior of materials and therefore we neglect all other stresses (exterior to the dislocation curve) that may also contribute to the Peach-Koehler force. We consider a rather simple geometric setting where the dislocations are parallel lines moving in the same two-dimensional
(xy)
plane embedded in a three dimensional elastic crystal. A cross-sectional view then reduces the geometric study into a one-dimensional framework. To be more specific, we study a one-dimensional model given by the following non-local eikonal equation describing dislocations dynamics

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\left|\partial_{x}v(x,t)\right|&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

where
T>0
, the solution
v
is a real-valued function,
\partial_{t}v
,
\partial_{x}v
stand respectively for its time and space derivatives and
\mathcal{K}
is a kernel obtained by the resolution of the equations of linear elasticity. Here the dislocations move with the non-local velocity

\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v(\cdot,t)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(x-z)v(z,t)dz

created by the self-force. In physical terms, it corresponds to the interior elastic stress created by all the dislocations in the material. While the scalar function
v(\cdot,t)
represents the plastic distortions due to the presence of dislocations, it is chosen so as to capture the dislocation points at a time
t
(one might roughly think of
v(\cdot,t)
as the evolution of a sum of weighted Heaviside functions).

1.2.
Brief review of the literature

The solution of (
1
) is considered in the sense of continuous viscosity solutions. The theory of viscosity solutions was first introduced by Crandall and Lions in 1980 (for a general overview, see for example the book of Barles
[
7
]
). Many authors used this theory for the resolution of non-local eikonal equation modeling dislocation dynamics. Alvarez et al.
[
5
]
proved short time existence and uniqueness results of discontinuous viscosity solutions under the assumption that dislocations have the shape of Lipschitz graphs.

Under the restrictive assumption of nonnegative velocity, we refer to
[
2
,
11
]
for a study of long time existence and uniqueness of discontinuous solutions. These results were recently extended by Barles et al.
[
10
]
, using a new approach that allows to relax some of the assumptions of
[
2
,
11
]
.

In the case where velocity is allowed to change sign, Barles et al.
[
9
]
obtained a global existence result of weak
L^{1}
-viscosity solutions. The authors also investigated the relation of this type of solutions with the classical one, as well as the uniqueness in several situations. However, a general global existence and uniqueness result was obtained in
[
10
]
under general and simplified assumptions. Recently,
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
established global existence results for weak discontinuous viscosity solutions in the one-dimensional equation domain based on a new BV estimate. In our work, we aim to numerically approximate a continuous nondecreasing viscosity solution of (
1
) without assuming a Lipschitz regularity on the kernel and on the initial data. The existence of such solution was proved by El Hajj in
[
18
]
.

Up to our knowledge, there are only few known numerical results. Indeed, a pioneering work was done by Crandall and Lions
[
17
]
, for approximations of solutions of a broader class of equations called Hamilton-Jacobi equations. Alvarez, Carlini, Monneau, and Rouy
[
3
]
proved the convergence of a first-order scheme for a nonlocal eikonal equation of a Lipschitz continuous solution and extended this convergence result to the numerical analysis of a nonlocal Hamilton-Jacobi equation describing the dynamics of a single dislocation in 2D. We also mention the work of A. Ghorbel, R. Monneau
[
23
]
, where they proposed an upwind scheme to approximate a Lipschitz continuous viscosity increasing solution having a Lipschitz continuous kernel.

1.3.
Description of our results

This paper has to main objectives:

•

Deriving a reliable periodic model from (
1
).

•

Designing a convergent numerical scheme for such periodic model.

The first objective will allow us to get rid of the infinite size of the domain on which (
1
) is defined. Of course, in order to ensure some “consistency” between the initial model and the periodic one, we need to justify that when the artificial period goes to infinity one recover the solutions of the initial problem (
1
). For this purpose, the key idea is to preserve, for the periodic model, a gradient entropy inequality available for (
1
) (see below). This will be done in Section
2.1
by carefully modifying the kernel
\mathcal{K}
.

Then, for the second main challenge, we design, in Section
3.1
, a numerical scheme for the periodic equation. More precisely, we introduce a one step implicit/explicit (IMEX) Euler in time and upwind in space scheme. We will show, inspired by
[
25
]
, that this scheme preserves at the discrete level the gradient entropy inequality of the periodic model. This inequality will allow us to show the convergence of the scheme.

1.4.
Gradient entropy estimate for the initial model

As explained previously, our approach relies mainly on the gradient entropy inequality satisfy by the solutions of (
2
). Therefore, for the convenience of the reader we give some details on this inequality and we refer, for instance, to
[
18
]
for more details.

Thereby, we consider
v
, a smooth as needed, solution of (
1
). Besides, we also assume that
v_{0}
is smooth and nondecreasing. Then, with these assumptions one can prove
by the classical maximum principle, that
v
is also nondecreasing along time. This leads us to drop the absolute value in (
1
) and then consider the following non-local transport equation:

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\partial_{x}v(x,t)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Now, similar to
[
18
]
, we introduce the following entropy functional:

(3)

\displaystyle H[\partial_{x}v](t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g\left(\partial_{x}v(x,t)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
g
is defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with

(4)

g(x)=x\,\ln(x).

Then, one can prove that there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
T
,
\mathcal{K}
and
v_{0}
such that for all
t\in(0,T)
it holds

(5)

\displaystyle H[\partial_{x}v](t)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star\partial_{x}v(\cdot,s)(x)\right)\,\partial_{x}v(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\leq C.

Applying Plancherel’s identity we notice that the second term in the left hand side is nonnegative if we assume that
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{K})(\xi)\leq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform. In fact, following for instance
[
18
]
, all the previous computations can be made rigorous under the following assumptions:

(H1)

Initial data
:
v_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a nondecreasing function with
\partial_{x}v_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L\,\log\,L(\mathbb{R})
.

(H2)

Kernel
:
\mathcal{K}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is an even function such that

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(x)\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{K})(\xi)\leq 0,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{*}.

In particular, assumption
(H1)
implies that
v_{0}
is continuous. Let us also recall the definition of the so-called Zygmund space:

(7)

L\,\log\,L(\mathbb{R})=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R})\,:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|\ln(e+|f|)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\},

which is a Banach space with the norm (see
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L\,\log\,L(\mathbb{R})}=\inf\left\{\mu>0\,:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f|}{\mu}\,\ln\left(e+\frac{|f|}{\mu}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}.

Assumption
(H2)
is a property that arises naturally from the physical laws governing dislocations and was proven in
[
5
, Proposition 6.1]
. In particular, the negativity of the Fourier transform of the kernel follows directly from the positivity of the elastic energy. In Section 6, we present numerical simulations based on a specific kernel derived from the Peierls–Nabarro model, developed within the framework of isotropic elasticity (see
[
6
]
and
[
5
, Sec. 6.2.2]
for the explicit computation).

1.5.
Outline of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we derive our periodic model and we give a formal insight on its mathematical structure. Moreover, we also justify that when the period goes to infinity one recover the initial model (
2
). Then, in Section
3
, we introduce our IMEX numerical scheme and state our main results. We study in Section
4
its main properties on fixed grids in space and time, and we prove its convergence in Section
5
. Finally, we present some numerical experiments in Section
6
.

2.
Introduction of a periodic model

2.1.
Derivation of a periodic model

We first consider for a fixed
P>0
the following ansatz:

v^{P}(x,t):=v(x,t)-L^{P}x,\quad\mbox{for }(x,t)\in[-P,P]\times(0,T),

where

(8)

L^{P}=\frac{v_{0}(P)-v_{0}(-P)}{2P},

and
v^{P}
is a
2P
-periodic function. This approach allows to describe the evolution of dislocations in a periodic domain (i.e. without boundray effect) assuming that the total dislocation density is preserved over time, which is reflected in the fact that

(9)

\int_{-P}^{P}\partial_{x}v(x,t)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{-P}^{P}(\partial_{x}v^{P}(x,t)+L^{P})\mathrm{d}x=v_{0}(P)-v_{0}(-P),

while respecting the positivity of the density (
\partial_{x}v\geq 0
).
This is a way of studying the bulk behavior of the material away from its boundary by modeling a periodic distribution of dislocations of length
2P
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v^{P}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\,\partial_{x}v(x,t)=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\,\left(\partial_{x}v^{P}(x,t)+L^{P}\right).

According to
(H2)
and further assuming the following hypothesis on the kernel:

(H2)’

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto x\,\mathcal{K}(x)
be a function in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

we can see that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v(\cdot,t)(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(y)v^{P}(x-y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(y)L^{P}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v^{P}(\cdot,t)(x)\right)+L^{P}x\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y-L^{P}\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\mathcal{K}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v^{P}(\cdot,t)(x)\right).

Consequently, we deduce that
v^{P}
is solution to the following system:

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v^{P}(x,t)=\left(\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\star v^{P}(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\,\left(\partial_{x}v^{P}(x,t)+L^{P}\right)&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
v^{P}(x,0)=v_{0}(x)-L^{P}x&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Let us notice that systems (
2
) and (
10
) are equivalent. In particular,
\partial_{x}v^{P}+L^{P}
satisfies the gradient entropy estimate (
5
).

Remark 1
.

•

Noting that it is entirely possible to establish the results developed in this paper without assuming that the kernel verifies hypothesis
(H2)’
. This is a simplifying hypothesis that makes the analysis and reading easier.

•

It can be observed from equation (
10
)
that the quantity
\int_{[-P,P]}\partial_{x}v(x,t)\mathrm{d}x
is well preserved,
which confirms (
9
).

Now, our main objective is to derive a periodic model from (
10
). As already explained, we need to derive this model in such way that a “periodic” counterpart of (
5
) still holds. For this, we will carefully modify the kernel
\mathcal{K}
. Indeed, we first define
\mathcal{K}^{P}
the
2P
periodic function such that

\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)=\mathcal{K}(x)\quad\mbox{for }x\in[-P,P],

and extended periodically over
\mathbb{R}
. Now, for a given positive integer
M>1
,
we introduce the Fejér kernel
F_{M}
as:

(11)

F_{M}(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}D_{\ell}(x),

where
D_{\ell}
is the Dirichlet kernel defined by:

D_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}e^{i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\ell=0,\ldots,M-1.

Then, we define the
2P
periodic function
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
by

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)=\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)-\frac{2F_{2M}(x)}{P}\,\int_{|x|\geq P}\left|\mathcal{K}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\mbox{for }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, we consider the Cesàro mean of order
M

of the Fourier series of
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
:

(13)

\displaystyle\sigma_{M}^{P}(x):=\sigma_{M}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})(x)=\dfrac{1}{M}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})\,e^{i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}=\frac{1}{2P}\left(F_{M}\ast\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}\right)(x),\quad\forall x\in I_{P},

where, throughout the paper, we will denote by
I_{P}
the set

(14)

\displaystyle I_{P}:=\mathbb{R}/2P\mathbb{Z},

and by
c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})
the
m
-th Fourier coefficient of
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
given by

\displaystyle c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})=\frac{1}{2P}\int_{I_{P}}\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\,e^{-i\pi m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, in the definition (
13
) the symbol
\ast
denotes the convolution between
2P
periodic functions defined, for any
w
and
z\in L^{1}(I_{P})
, as

(15)

\displaystyle(w\ast z)(x)=\int_{I_{P}}w(x-y)\,z(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad\mbox{for a.e. }x\in I_{P}.

Hence, in this paper, we will study the solutions, denoted
u^{P}_{M}
, of the following periodic system:

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)=\left(\sigma_{M}^{P}(\cdot)\ast u^{P}_{M}(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\,\left(\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)+L^{P}\right)&\mbox{in }I_{P}\times(0,T),\\
u^{P}(x,0)=u^{P}_{0}(x)&\mbox{in }I_{P},\end{cases}

where
I_{P}
is defined in (
14
). Let us assume that
P
,
M
and
u^{P}_{0}
satisfy the following assumptions:

(H3)

Parameters of the periodic model:

P
is a real positive number and
M
is a positive integer.

(H4)

Periodic initial data:
for any
P>0
, the function
u^{P}_{0}
belongs to
L^{\infty}(I_{P})
with
\partial_{x}u^{P}_{0}\in L^{1}(I_{P})\cap L\,\log\,L(I_{P})
. Moreover, the function

\displaystyle x\in[-P,P)\mapsto u^{P}_{0}(x)+L^{P}x,

is nondecreading.

Remark 2
.

The space
L\,\log\,L(I_{P})
is defined as in (
7
) with
\mathbb{R}
replaced by
I_{P}
.

We show in this paper that under assumptions
(H2)
–
(H4)
, system (
16
) admits at least one solution
u^{P}_{M}
in the distributional sense (see Theorem
2
). In the next section we explicit the gradient entropy estimate satisfy by the solutions of (
16
). The adaptation at the discrete level of such estimate will be crucial to show the existence of solutions for this system, see the proof of Theorem
2
.

2.2.
Gradient entropy estimate in the periodic case

Let us now establish formally a gradient entropy inequality, similar to (
5
), satisfies by
\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}+L^{P}
. This will (partially) motivate the introduction of
the Cesàro means of the Fourier series of the
2P
-periodic kernel
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
.
Moreover, as already said, this inequality will be at the cornerstone of the proof of our convergence result announced in Theorem
2
. Indeed, as in
[
18
]
, this gradient entropy inequality will lead to a crucial estimate on
\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}+L^{P}
in the space
L\log L(I_{P})
(see Lemma
9
).

Therefore, as previously, in this part we will assume that
u^{P}_{M}
is smooth as needed and we will also assume that the function
x\mapsto u^{P}_{M}(x,t)+L^{P}\,x
is increasing. Then, recalling definition (
3
) of the functional
H
, with
\mathbb{R}
replaced by
I_{P}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H\left[\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}+L^{P}\right](t)

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{P}}g^{\prime}(\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)+L^{P})\,\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{I_{P}}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)\,g^{\prime\prime}(\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)+L^{P})\partial_{t}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{I_{P}}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)\,\left(\sigma_{M}^{P}(\cdot)\ast u^{P}_{M}(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{P}}\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}(x,t)\,\left(\sigma_{M}^{P}(\cdot)\ast\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}(\cdot,t)(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now, thanks to Parseval’s relation and the property of the Fourier coefficients with respect to the convolution product, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H\left[\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M}+L^{P}\right](t)=(2P)^{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}(\sigma_{M}^{P})\,\left|c_{m}(\partial_{x}u^{P}_{M})\right|^{2}.

Therefore, to argue as in the case of system (
2
), we need to show that the Fourier coefficients of
\sigma_{M}^{P}
are nonpositive real numbers. First, thanks to the definition (
13
) of
\sigma_{M}^{P}
and the definition (
12
) of
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
, we notice that

\displaystyle c_{m}(\sigma_{M}^{P})=c_{m}(F_{M})\,c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})=c_{m}(F_{M})\,\left(c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P})-\frac{2c_{m}(F_{2M})}{P}\,\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right),\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Besides, classical computations lead to

\displaystyle c_{m}(F_{M})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(1-\frac{|m|}{M}\right)&\mbox{if }|m|<M,\\
0&\mbox{if }|m|\geq M.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle c_{m}(\sigma_{M}^{P})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(1-\frac{|m|}{M}\right)\left(c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P})-\frac{2}{P}\left(1-\frac{|m|}{2M}\right)\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)&\mbox{if }|m|<M,\\
0&\mbox{if }|m|\geq M.\end{array}\right.

Let us establish now the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let assumption
(H2)
holds. Then, the Fourier coefficients of the
2P
-periodic function
\sigma_{M}^{P}
are nonpositive real numbers.

Proof.

According to (
2.2
) it is sufficient to show that
c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})
is a nonpositive real number for
|m|<M
. Then, let
|m|<M
be fixed. We first notice, thanks to assumption
(H2)
, that the function
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
is even, so that its Fourier coefficient are real numbers. Now, we subtract
\int_{-P}^{P}\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}
from the Fourier transform of
\mathcal{K}
evaluated at
\xi=m/2P
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{K})(m/2P)-\int_{-P}^{P}\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)\,e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int_{|x|\geq P}\mathcal{K}(x)e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, using
(H2)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-P}^{P}\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(x)\,e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence,

(19)

\displaystyle\int_{-P}^{P}\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq-\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

Therefore, as
|m|<M
, we get

\displaystyle c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})

\displaystyle=c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P})-\frac{2}{P}\left(1-\frac{|m|}{2M}\right)\,\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2P}\int_{-P}^{P}\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)\,e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{P}\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2P}\left(\int_{-P}^{P}\mathcal{K}^{P}(x)\,e^{-i\,\pi\,m\,x/P}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq 0,

where for the last inequality we apply (
19
).
∎

The previous computations allow to understand that in order to preserve the gradient entropy inequality for the periodic model one needs to consider the Cesàro means of
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
. Indeed, in the definition (
16
), we cannot simply consider
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
since

\displaystyle c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P})-\frac{2}{P}\left(1-\frac{|m|}{2M}\right)\left(\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq P}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)&\mbox{if }|m|<2M,\\
c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P})&\mbox{if }|m|\geq 2M,\end{array}\right.

and
c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P})
is a priori an unsigned quantity with our assumption
(H2)
. Moreover, in order to be able to pass to the limit
P\to+\infty
and to design our numerical scheme, we also need to regularize the periodic kernel
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
. This is done thanks to its Cesàro means.

2.3.
From the periodic to the initial model

In this section, we explain how one can recover the initial model (
2
) from (
16
) when the period
P\to+\infty
. For this purpose, for a given function
v_{0}
satisfying
(H1)
, we first consider the following
2P
-periodic initial data for (
16
):

(20)

\displaystyle u^{P}_{0}(x)=v_{0}(x)-L^{P}\,x,\quad\mbox{for }x\in[-P,P],

which we extend periodically over
\mathbb{R}
, where
L^{P}
is defined in (
8
).
We notice that for any
P>0
, the quantity
L^{P}
is nonnegative if
v_{0}
is nondecreasing. Then, we have the following

Lemma 2
.

Let assumption
(H1)
holds and assume that
P\geq 1
. Then, the function
u^{P}_{0}
, given by (
20
), satisfies, uniformly with respect to
P
,

\displaystyle u^{P}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(I_{P}),\quad\partial_{x}u^{P}_{0}\in L^{1}(I_{P})\cap L\,\log\,L(I_{P}).

Moreover, the continuous function
x\in[-P,P)\mapsto u^{P}_{0}(x)+L^{P}x
is nondecreasing.

Proof.

Let
x\in[-P,P)
, then, since
P>0
, we have

(21)

\displaystyle\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{1}{2}\left|v_{0}(P)-v_{0}(-P)\right|\leq 2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},

so that
u^{P}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(I_{P})
uniformly w.r.t
P
. Moreover, as
P>0
, it holds

(22)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}\leq\|\partial_{x}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 4\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Then, applying inequality (
78
) in Lemma

to the constant function equal to one, we obtain

(23)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}u^{P}_{0}\|_{L\,\log\,L(I_{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}v_{0}\|_{L\,\log\,L(I_{P})}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\frac{1}{P}+2\ln(1+e^{2})+\frac{2}{e}\right),

(24)

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}v_{0}\|_{L\,\log\,L(\mathbb{R})}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left(1+2\ln(1+e^{2})+\frac{2}{e}\right),

where we have used the assumption
P\geq 1
for the last inequality.
∎

Therefore, the initial condition (
20
) satisfies assumption
(H4)
. In particular, assuming that assumptions
(H1)
-
(H2)
also hold, we deduce from Theorem
2
, that for any

and
M>0
, there exists a solution

in the distribution sense to (

) satisfying all the properties listed in Theorem
2
.

Then, we have the following:

Proposition 1
.

Let assumptions
(H1)
-
(H2)
hold, let

Remark 3
.

Note that Proposition

3.
Numerical scheme and main results

3.1.
Numerical scheme

We are now in position to introduce a numerical scheme for the periodic system (
16
). Let us first introduce some notation for the meshes in space and time. Let
N\geq M
and
N_{T}
be positive integers. We define a time step
\Delta t>0
such that
\Delta t=T/N_{T}
and a space step
\Delta x=P/N
. We also define the sequences

t_{n}=n\Delta t,\quad\forall n\in\{0,\ldots,N_{T}\},

and

x_{i}=i\Delta x,\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}:=\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}.

For a fixed positive integer
M
, we first start by discretizing
the
2P
periodic initial condition
u^{P}_{0}
as

(25)

\displaystyle u_{i}^{P,0}=u^{P}_{0}(x_{i}),\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Then, for a given vector
u^{P,n}=(u^{P,n}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
, where
u^{P,n}_{i}
is an approximation of
u^{P}(x_{i},t_{n})
, we define the vector
u^{P,n+1}=(u^{P,n+1}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
as the solution of the following system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{u_{i}^{P,n+1}-u_{i}^{P,n}}{\Delta t}&=\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]\,\left(\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}\right)\mbox{ if }\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]\geq 0,\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N},\\
\\
\dfrac{u_{i}^{P,n+1}-u_{i}^{P,n}}{\Delta t}&=\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]\,\left(\theta^{P,n}_{i-1/2}+L^{P}\right)\mbox{ if }\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]<0,\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N},\end{cases}

where
\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}
is the discrete gradient of
u^{P,n}_{i}
defined by

(26)

\displaystyle\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}:=\dfrac{u^{P,n}_{i+1}-u^{P,n}_{i}}{\Delta x},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Before to define the approximate velocity term
\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]
, we notice that we can rewrite the previous system as

(27)

\frac{u_{i}^{P,n+1}-u_{i}^{P,n}}{\Delta t}=\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{+}\,\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}-\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{-}\,\theta^{P,n}_{i-1/2}+L^{P}\,\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right],\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N},

where

\lambda^{M}_{i}[\cdot]_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\lambda^{M}_{i}[\cdot]+\left|\lambda^{M}_{i}[\cdot]\right|\Big),\quad\lambda^{M}_{i}[\cdot]_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\left|\lambda^{M}_{i}[\cdot]\right|-\lambda^{M}_{i}[\cdot]\Big),\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Finally, we define

(28)

\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\sigma_{M,j}^{P}\,u^{P,n+1}_{i-j},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N},

with
\sigma_{M,j}^{P}=\sigma_{M}^{P}(x_{j})
and where
\sigma_{M}^{P}
is the Cesàro mean of order
M

of the Fourier series of
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
, introduced previously in (
13
).

Remark 4
.

It is clear that the vector
u^{P,n}
, for any
1\leq n\leq N_{T}
, solution to (
27
), depends on
M
. However, when no confusion can occur, we make a slight abuse of notation and we neglect
M
from its indices.

Remark 5
.

There exists an alternative definition of the term
\sigma^{P}_{M,j}
. Indeed, we can alternatively define this term as

(29)

\displaystyle\sigma^{P}_{M,j}=\frac{1}{2P}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,F_{M}(x_{j-i})\,\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M,i},

where, defining
x_{i\pm 1/2}:=(i\pm 1/2)\Delta x
for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M,i}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

The same results presented in this paper can also be obtained using definition (
29
) (see Remarks
8
and
9
). However, as will be shown below, adopting definition (
13
) yields uniform estimates with respect to all the parameters involved (
N
,
N_{T}
,
M
, and
P
), in contrast to definition (
29
), where the estimates are uniform only with respect to
N
and
N_{T}
(cf. estimate (
54
)). While this distinction does not hinder the proof of convergence of the scheme, the formulation based on definition (
13
) offers greater robustness and stability under parameter variation.

3.2.
Main results

Our first main result deals with the well-posedness of the scheme (
25
)–(
28
) and the qualitative properties of its solution. Before to state properly this result, we slightly modify the definition of the entropy density
g
, defined in (
4
), by the following nonnegative function

(30)

0\leq f(x)=\begin{cases}x\ln(x)+\frac{1}{e}&\quad\mbox{if }x\geq 1/e,\\
0&\quad\mbox{if }0\leq x\leq 1/e.\end{cases}

Theorem 1

(Properties of the scheme)
.

Let assumptions
(H2)
–
(H4)
hold, and assume that

(31)

\Delta t<\frac{1}{10(L^{P})\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,\min\left\{\frac{1}{\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T||\mathcal{K}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}+1},1\right\},

and

(32)

\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}<\frac{1}{10\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\min\left\{\frac{1}{\left(\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}+1\right)},\frac{1}{3\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}}\right\}.

Then, for any
1\leq n\leq N_{T}
, the scheme (
25
)–(
28
) admits a unique solution
u^{P,n}=(u^{P,n}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
such that

(33)

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u_{i}^{P,n}|

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}},

and
\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}
is nonnegative for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
where we recall definition (
26
) of
\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}
. Furthermore, this solution satisfies the following discrete total variation estimate:

(34)

\displaystyle TV(u^{P,n})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\left|u^{P,n}_{i+1}-u^{P,n}_{i}\right|\leq TV(u^{P}_{0})\,e^{10(L^{P})T\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}},\quad\forall 1\leq n\leq N_{T}.

Finally, there exists a positive constant
\zeta_{0}
only depending on
T
and

)), such that the following discrete gradient entropy estimate holds

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,0}+L^{P}\right)+\zeta_{0}\,TV(u^{P}_{0}),\quad\mbox{for }1\leq n\leq N_{T},

where we recall definition (
30
) of the function
f
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is done in Section
4
in several steps. First we prove the existence of a unique bounded solution, satisfying (

), to the scheme (
25
)–(
28
) thanks to Banach’s fixed point theorem. Then, we adapt at the discrete level the computations of Section
2.2
to show the discrete gradient entropy estimate (
35
).

Remark 6
.

Let us notice that all the properties stated in Theorem

hold under the “uniform” CFL like conditions (
31
)–(

). However, thanks to an adaptive time stepping strategy, these conditions on the sizes of the meshes can be relaxed. More precisely, we first define

and

and

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Delta t_{n+1}}{\Delta x}<\min\left(\dfrac{\Delta t_{n}}{\Delta x},\dfrac{1}{10\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\|u^{P,n}\|_{\infty}+1\right)}\right),

with

) (with

.

In our second main result we prove the convergence of the scheme (

where of course

.

4.
Study of the numerical scheme

In this section we prove Theorem
1
. For this purpose, we split our proof in two main steps. Indeed, in Section
4.1
, we prove the well-posedness of the scheme, while in Section
4.2
, we prove the discrete gradient entropy inequality (
35
). But first, we establish a technical result.

Lemma 3
.

Let assumption
(H3)
holds and assume that
\mathcal{K}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
given by (
12
) satisfies
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}\in L^{1}(I_{P})
with

(41)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}^{p}_{M}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}

\displaystyle\leq 5\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Moreover, it holds

(42)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\left|\sigma_{M,j}^{P}\right|\leq 5\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Let us first prove (
41
). For this purpose, using the definition (
12
) of
\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}
and the classical result
\|F_{2M}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}=2P
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{K}^{P}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}+\frac{2\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}{P}\int_{-P}^{P}|F_{2M}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x=\|\mathcal{K}^{P}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}+4\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

which yields estimate (
41
). Now, applying relation (
13
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\left|\sigma_{M,j}^{P}\right|\leq\frac{\Delta x}{2P}\int_{-P}^{P}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}F_{M}(x_{j}-x)\right)\,\left|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Furthermore, one can easily show that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}F_{M}(x_{j}-x)=2N
when
N\geq M
. Hence, since
\Delta x=P/N
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\left|\sigma^{P}_{M,j}\right|\leq\frac{N\,\Delta x}{P}\int_{-P}^{P}\left|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x=\|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}.

This leads to (
42
) thanks to (
41
).
∎

Remark 8
.

The estimate (
42
) still holds with the definition (
29
) of
\sigma^{P}_{M,j}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\left|\sigma_{M,j}^{P}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2P}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Bigg(\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,F_{M}(x_{j}-x_{i})\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N\Delta x}{P}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x=\|\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P})}.

Therefore, except for the discrete gradient entropy estimate (see Remark
9
), the proof of Theorem
1
below is unchanged.

4.1.
Well-posedness of the scheme

Let us now prove the well-posedness of the scheme thanks to Banach’s fixed point theorem. For this, we argue by induction. Thus, let
0\leq n\leq N_{T}-1
be fixed, and let
u^{P,n}=(u^{P,n}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
be a given vector such that

\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u^{P,n}_{i}|\leq\alpha:=\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}.

We also assume that
\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}+L
is nonnegative for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
and that

\displaystyle TV\left(u^{P,n}\right)\leq TV\left(u^{P}_{0}\right)\,e^{10(L^{P})T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}.

4.1.1.
Construction of a contraction mapping

We introduce the following compact set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}:=\left\{v=(v_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,:\,\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|v_{i}|\leq\alpha+1\right\},

and, for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, we define the function
F_{i}\,:\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\,\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(43)

\begin{array}[]{ll}F_{i}(v)&=u_{i}^{P,n}+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\Big(\lambda^{M}_{i}[v]_{+}\,(u_{i+1}^{P,n}-\displaystyle u_{i}^{P,n})-\lambda^{M}_{i}[v]_{-}\,(u_{i}^{P,n}-u_{i-1}^{P,n})\Big)+\Delta t\,L^{P}\,\lambda^{M}_{i}[v],\\
&\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left|\lambda^{M}_{i}[v]\right|\right)u_{i}^{P,n}+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{M}_{i}[v]_{+}u_{i+1}^{P,n}+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{M}_{i}[v]_{-}u_{i-1}^{P,n}+\Delta t\,L^{P}\,\lambda^{M}_{i}[v].\end{array}

We notice that the solution, denoted
u^{P,n+1}
, of the scheme (
25
)–(
28
)
at step
n+1
is given by

\displaystyle u^{P,n+1}_{i}=F_{i}(u^{P,n+1})\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Let us now prove that the range of
F_{i}
is included in
[-\alpha-1,\alpha+1]
. To do so, we first notice, thanks to definition (
28
) and Lemma
3
, that for any
v\in\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
, we have

(44)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{i}^{M}[v]\right|\leq(\alpha+1)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\left|\sigma_{M,j}^{P}\right|\leq 5(\alpha+1)\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Hence, we conclude using the CFL condition (
32
) that

\displaystyle|F_{i}(v)|\leq\alpha+\Delta t\,L^{P}\,5\,(\alpha+1)\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Thanks to the conditions (
31
) we conclude that
F_{i}(v)\in[-\alpha-1,\alpha+1]
for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
. We now define the map
F:\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
such that
F(v)=(F_{i}(v))_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
where
F_{i}(v)
is given by (
43
). Let us prove that
F
is a contraction mapping on
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
. For this purpose, let
v
and
w\in\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
. Then, for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|F_{i}(v)-F_{i}(w)\right|\leq\left(15\alpha\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+5\Delta t\,L^{P}\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\,\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}|v_{i}-w_{i}|.

It remains to apply the conditions (
31
) and (
32
) and Banach’s fixed point theorem to deduce the existence and uniqueness of
u^{P,n+1}\in\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
solution to the scheme.

4.1.2.
Discrete
L^{\infty}
estimate

As a by-product of the previous step, we have

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u^{P,n+1}_{i}|\leq\alpha+1.

However, let us show that in fact
u^{P,n+1}
satisfies the sharpest
L^{\infty}
bound (
33
), i.e.,
|u^{P,n+1}_{i}|\leq\alpha
for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
. We first rewrite the scheme (
27
), for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, as

u_{i}^{P,n+1}=\left(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left|\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]\right|\right)u_{i}^{P,n}+\,\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}u_{i+1}^{P,n}+\,\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}u_{i-1}^{P,n}+\Delta tL^{P}\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}].

Thanks to this decomposition and the CFL condition (
32
) we get

\displaystyle|u^{P,n+1}_{i}|\leq\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u^{P,n}_{i}|+\Delta t\,L^{P}\,\left|\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]\right|.

Hence, applying Lemma
3
yields

\displaystyle|u_{i}^{P,n+1}|\leq\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u_{i}^{P,n}|+5L^{P}\,\Delta t\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u_{i}^{P,n+1}|,\quad\mbox{for all}\quad 0\leq n\leq N_{T}-1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Thereby, a direct induction leads to

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u_{i}^{P,n+1}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-5L^{P}\Delta t||\mathcal{K}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})})^{n+1}}\,\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\,|u_{i}^{P,0}|\,\leq\,\frac{1}{(1-5L^{P}\Delta t||\mathcal{K}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})})^{n+1}}\,\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}.

Therefore, thanks to condition (
31
) and the classical inequality
-\ln(1-x)\leq x/(1-x)
for
0\leq x<1
, we deduce that
u^{P,n+1}
satisfies estimate (
33
).

4.1.3.
Nonnegativity of the discrete gradient

We now prove that
\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}
is nonnegative for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
. Using equation (
27
), we first write, for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
,

\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}=\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\big(\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}+\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\big)\bigg)\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}\\
+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,\theta_{i+3/2}^{P,n}+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\,\theta_{i-1/2}^{P,n}\\
+L^{P}\bigg(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}(\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]-\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}])+1\bigg),

or equivalently,

(45)

\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}=\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\big(\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}+\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\big)\bigg)\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)\\
+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,\left(\theta_{i+3/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\,\left(\theta_{i-1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right).

Therefore, the CFL condition (
32
) implies that
\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}
is a convex combination of nonnegative terms (since we assume by induction that
\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}\geq 0
for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
). Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}\geq 0,

for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
.

4.1.4.
Discrete total variation estimate

Let us now establish the discrete total variation estimate (
34
). For this purpose, using equation (
27
) and the CFL condition (
32
), we have

TV(u^{P,n+1})\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}(\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}+\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-})\bigg)\,\left|u_{i+1}^{P,n}-u_{i}^{P,n}\right|\\
+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,\left|u_{i+2}^{P,n}-u_{i+1}^{P,n}\right|+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\,\left|u_{i}^{P,n}-u_{i-1}^{P,n}\right|\\
+L^{P}\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\left|\lambda_{i+1}^{M}[u^{P,n+1}]-\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]\right|.

Now, we recombine the terms and we apply Lemma
3
to the last term in the right hand side of the previous inequality, and we get

TV(u^{P,n+1})\,(1-5L^{P}\Delta t\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})})\leq TV(u^{P,n})\\
+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\bigg(\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,|u_{i+2}^{P,n}-u_{i+1}^{P,n}|-\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,|u_{i+1}^{P,n}-u_{i}^{P,n}|\bigg)\\
+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\bigg(\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}|u_{i}^{P,n}-u_{i-1}^{P,n}|-\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}|u_{i+1}^{P,n}-u_{i}^{P,n}|\bigg).

Therefore, thanks to the periodicity of the vector
u^{P,n}
, we notice that the two sums in the right hand side vanish so that

\displaystyle TV(u^{P,n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-5L^{P}\Delta t\,||\mathcal{K}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,TV(u^{P,n}).

Arguing as in the proof of estimate (
33
) we directly obtain

\displaystyle TV(u^{P,n+1})\leq TV(u^{P,0})\,e^{10(L^{P})T||\mathcal{K}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}.

Finally, since
TV(u^{P,0})\leq TV(u^{P}_{0})
, we conclude that the estimate (
34
) holds true.

4.1.5.
Conclusion

Starting from an initial data
u^{P}_{0}
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1
, we deduce by induction the existence and uniqueness of a solution
u^{P,n}
to the scheme, for any
1\leq n\leq N_{T}
, satisfying the discrete
L^{\infty}
bound (
33
) and the discrete total variation estimate (
34
). Moreover, the vector
(\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}+L^{P})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
is componentwise nonnegative.

4.2.
Discrete gradient entropy estimate

In order to conclude the proof of Theorem
1
, it remains to establish the discrete gradient entropy estimate (
35
). For this purpose, we will follow the strategy of
[
25
]
. In particular, our analysis will rely on the two following lemmas.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\gamma_{m}>1
. There exists a nonnegative function
g(\theta,\gamma)
and a constant
C_{\gamma_{m}}>0
(only depending on
\gamma_{m}
) such that, for all
\theta>0
and
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{m})
, the function
f
given by (
30
) satisfies

f\bigg(\frac{\theta}{\gamma}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{\gamma}f(\theta)-\frac{1}{\gamma}g(\theta,\gamma)\ln(\gamma),

and

|\theta-g(\theta,\gamma)|\leq C_{\gamma_{m}}=\frac{\gamma_{m}-1}{e\ln(\gamma_{m})}.

Proof.

See Lemma 3.3 in
[
25
]
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
a_{k}
and
\theta_{k}
be two finite sequences of nonnegative real numbers such that
0<\sum_{k}a_{k}<2
. Then, defining
\theta=\sum_{k}a_{k}\theta_{k}
, the function
f
given by (
30
) satisfies the following inequality

f(\theta)\leq\sum_{k}a_{k}f(\theta_{k})+g\left(\theta,\sum_{k}a_{k}\right)\,\ln\left(\sum_{k}a_{k}\right),

where
g(\theta,\gamma)
is defined in Lemma
4
for
\gamma_{m}=2.

Proof.

See Lemma 3.4 in
[
25
]
.
∎

Thanks to the two above lemmas, we are now in position to establish the discrete entropy inequality (
35
). Then, let
0\leq n\leq N_{T}-1
be fixed, we define

a_{1}:=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\lambda^{M}_{i+1}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{+},\quad a_{2}:=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{-},

and

a_{3}:=1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left(\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{+}+\lambda^{M}_{i+1}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{-}\right).

Let us first notice that thanks to the definition (
28
) of
\lambda^{M}_{i}
and the CFL condition (
32
), the coefficient
a_{3}>0
. Indeed, for any
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{+}+\lambda^{M}_{i+1}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]_{-}\leq 10\,\|u^{P}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})},

such that, applying (
32
), we conclude that
a_{3}>0
. Now, according to (
45
) we have

\displaystyle\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}=a_{1}\left(\theta^{P,n}_{i+3/2}+L^{P}\right)+a_{2}\left(\theta^{P,n}_{i-1/2}+L^{P}\right)+a_{3}\left(\theta^{P,n}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}\right).

Moreover, we introduce
\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}=1-(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})
. Then, thanks to the CFL condition (
32
) and arguing as for the sign of
a_{3}
, we get:

(46)

\displaystyle 1-\mu_{i+1/2}^{n+1}=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=1-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left(\lambda^{M}_{i}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]-\lambda^{M}_{i+1}\left[u^{P,n+1}\right]\right)\in(0,2).

Hence, applying Lemma
5
and the definition of the coefficients
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,

\displaystyle f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,g\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P},1-\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}\right)\,\ln\left(1-\mu_{i+1/2}^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta t\,\left(\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,f\left(\theta_{i+3/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)-\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{+}\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta t\,\left(\lambda^{M}_{i}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\,f\left(\theta_{i-1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)-\lambda^{M}_{i+1}[u^{P,n+1}]_{-}\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)\right).

Thanks to the periodicity of the vectors involved in the two last sums of the right hand side and the inequality
\ln(1-\mu)\leq-\mu
for all
\mu<1
, we end up with

(47)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}\right)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)\\
-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,g\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P},1-\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}\right)\mu_{i+1/2}^{n+1}.

Now, we rewrite the last term in the right hand side of the previous inequality as

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,g\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P},1-\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}\right)\,\mu_{i+1/2}^{n+1}=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\left(g\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P},1-\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}\right)-\left(\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}+L^{P}\right)\right)\,\mu_{i+1/2}^{n+1},

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=-L^{P}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2},

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\,\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}.

For the term
J_{1}
, thanks to Lemma
4
, with
\gamma_{m}=2
, and the definition (
46
) of
\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle|J_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{e\,\ln(2)}\,\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\left|\mu^{n+1}_{i+1/2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{e\,\ln(2)}\,\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta t\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\left|\sigma^{P}_{M,j}\left(u^{P,n+1}_{i-j}-u^{P,n+1}_{i+1-j}\right)\right|.

Therefore, we deduce from the discrete total variation estimate (
34
) and Lemma
3
:

(48)

\displaystyle|J_{1}|\leq\frac{5\Delta t\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}{e\,\ln(2)}TV(u^{P,n+1})\leq\frac{5\Delta t\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}}{e\,\ln(2)}\,TV(u^{P}_{0}).

As a direct by-product of (
48
), we also deduce the following estimate

(49)

\displaystyle|J_{2}|\leq 5L^{P}\,\Delta t\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,TV(u^{P}_{0}).

The estimate for
J_{3}
is more involved. Indeed, we first rewrite the term
J_{3}
thanks to relation (
46
) as

(50)

\displaystyle J_{3}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta t\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\sigma^{P}_{M,j}\left(u^{P,n+1}_{i+1-j}-u^{P,n+1}_{i-j}\right)=\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta t\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\sigma^{P}_{M,j}\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i-j+1/2}

Then, we define the vector

\theta^{P,n+1}=\left(\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\right)_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}},

and, for any vector
v=(v_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
, we introduce the discrete counterpart of the periodic convolution product (
15
), still denoted
\ast
, between
v
and
\theta^{P,n+1}
as

\displaystyle\left(v\ast\theta^{P,n+1}\right)_{i}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,v_{j}\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i-j+1/2}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}v_{j}\,\left(u^{P,n+1}_{i+1-j}-u^{P,n+1}_{i-j}\right),\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Hence, using relation (
50
), we notice that we can rewrite
J_{3}
as

\displaystyle J_{3}=\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\,\left(\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P}\ast\theta^{P,n+1}\right)_{i}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P}=(\sigma_{M,i}^{P})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}.

Now, to deal with the term
J_{3}
we will reproduce at the discrete level the computations done in Section
2.1
. For this, we need to use the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) and in the sequel, for any
v=(v_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
, we will denote by
c^{d}(v)=(c^{d}_{j}(v))_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
the vector of the coefficients of the DFT of
v
where

\displaystyle c^{d}_{j}(v):=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}v_{\ell}\,e^{-i\pi\,j\,\ell/N},\quad\forall j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

Therefore, applying the discrete version of Parseval’s equality as well as the property of the DFT with respect to the discrete convolution product, we get

\displaystyle J_{3}=(2N)^{2}\,\Delta t\,\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,c^{d}_{j}\left(\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P}\right)\,\left|c^{d}_{j}\left(\theta^{P,n+1}\right)\right|^{2}.

Let us now show that the coefficients of the DFT of the vector
\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P}
are real nonpositive numbers. For this purpose, let
j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
be fixed. Then, by definition we have

\displaystyle c^{d}_{j}\left(\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P}\right)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\sigma_{M,\ell}^{P}\,e^{-i\pi\,j\,\ell/N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2NM}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{|m|\leq k}c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})\,e^{i\pi(m-j)\ell/N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2NM}\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\sum_{|m|\leq k}c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})\,\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}e^{i\pi(m-j)\ell/N}.

Hence, since the last sum is either equal to
0
or
2N
, depending on the values of
m
, we conclude that
c^{d}_{j}(\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P})
is a sum of the Fourier coefficients
c_{m}(\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M})
for
|m|<M
. Therefore, thanks to Lemma
1
, we obtain

(51)

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq 0.

Thereby, gathering (
47
)–(
51
), we have

(52)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}\right)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n}+L^{P}\right)\\
+5\,\Delta t\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,TV(u^{P}_{0})\left(\frac{1}{e\,\ln(2)}+L^{P}\right).

Summing the previous inequality over
n
yields the existence of the constant
\zeta>0
, given by

\displaystyle\zeta:=5T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,e^{10(L^{P})T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,\left(\frac{1}{e\ln(2)}+L^{P}\right),

such that

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,n+1}+L^{P}\right)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,f\left(\theta_{i+1/2}^{P,0}+L^{P}\right)+\zeta TV(u^{P}_{0}).

It is quite clear that in the last estimate
\zeta
does not depend on
M
.
Moreover, since we have
L^{P}=\frac{v_{0}(P)-v_{0}(-P)}{2P}
(cf. (
8
)), we can conclude that
\zeta
is uniformly bounded with respect to
P
, when
P\geq 1
, namely

(53)

\displaystyle\zeta\leq 5T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,e^{10T\,\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\,\left(\frac{1}{e\ln(2)}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right):=\zeta_{0}.

This implies (
35
) and concludes the the proof of Theorem
1
.

Remark 9
.

Let us now explain how to establish a discrete gradient entropy estimate with the definition (
29
) of
\sigma^{P}_{M,j}
. In this case, the estimate of the term
J_{3}
is more involved. Indeed, we write

\displaystyle J_{3}=\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\,\left((\widetilde{\sigma}_{M}^{P}-\bar{\sigma}^{P}_{M})\ast\theta^{P,n+1}\right)_{i}+\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\,\left(\bar{\sigma}^{P}_{M}\ast\theta^{P,n+1}\right)_{i}=:J_{31}+J_{32},

with

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{M}^{P}=(\sigma_{M}^{P}(x_{j}))_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}},\quad\widetilde{\sigma}_{M}^{P}=(\sigma_{M,j}^{P})_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}.

where obviously
\sigma^{P}_{M,j}
is given by (
29
). Then, the term
J_{32}
is estimated as before and for the term
J_{31}
we have

|J_{31}|\leq\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}(\Delta x)^{2}\,\left|\sigma^{P}_{M,j}-\sigma^{P}_{M}(x_{j})\right|\,\left|\theta^{P,n+1}_{i+1/2}\right|\,\left|\theta^{P,n+1}_{i-j+1/2}\right|\\
\leq\Delta t\,\left(TV\left(u^{P,n+1}\right)\right)^{2}\,\max_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\left|\sigma^{P}_{M,j}-\sigma^{P}_{M}(x_{j})\right|.

Moreover, for any
j\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\sigma^{P}_{M,j}-\sigma^{P}_{M}(x_{j})\right|=\left|\frac{1}{2P}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\Delta x\,F_{M}(x_{j}-x_{i})\,\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M,i}-\frac{1}{2P}\int_{-P}^{P}F_{M}(x_{j}-x)\,\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2P}\left|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}F_{M}(x_{j}-x_{i})\,\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}F_{M}(x_{j}-x)\,\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2P}\left|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}(F_{M}(x_{j}-x_{i})-F_{M}(x_{j}-x))\mathcal{K}^{P}_{M}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{5\,\Delta x}{4P}\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|\partial_{x}F_{M}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{P})}.

Therefore, there exists a constant
C_{M}^{P}
such that

(54)

\displaystyle|J_{31}|\leq C_{M}^{P}\,\Delta t\,\Delta x.

Since we will first pass to the limit
\Delta x\to 0
for the proof of Theorem
2
, then the dependency on
P
and
M
of the constant
C_{M}^{P}
has no consequence for the proof of Proposition
1
.

5.
Convergence of the scheme

In this section, we prove Theorem
2

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{P_{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|\sigma_{M_{\ell}}^{P_{k}}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P_{k}})}\,e^{10\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{P_{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}_{M_{\ell}}\|_{L^{1}(I_{P_{k}})}\,e^{10\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{10}{P_{k}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,e^{10\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}T\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}.

Hence, passing to the limit
\ell\to+\infty
and
k\to+\infty
leads to

(82)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}(\sigma_{M_{\ell}}^{P_{k}}(\cdot)\ast u_{k,\ell}(\cdot,t)(x))\,L^{P_{k}}\varphi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\to 0.

Now, we consider the term

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}(\sigma_{M_{\ell}}^{P_{k}}(\cdot)\ast u_{k,\ell}(\cdot,t)(x))\,\partial_{x}u_{k,\ell}(x,t)\,\varphi(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

We notice, thanks to Fejér’s theorem and the uniform convergence (up to a subsequence) of
(u_{k,\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
toward
u_{k}
, that for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

\displaystyle(\sigma_{M_{\ell}}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k,\ell})\to(\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k})-{4u_{k}}\left(\int_{|x|\geq P_{k}}\left|\mathcal{K}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\quad\mbox{in }L^{\infty}(I_{P_{k}}\times(0,T)),\,\mbox{as }\ell\to+\infty.

Thus, applying (
80
) yields, as
\ell\to+\infty
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}(\sigma_{M_{\ell}}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k,\ell})\,\partial_{x}u_{k,\ell}\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\to\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}(\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k})\,\partial_{x}u_{k}\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
-{4}\left(\int_{|x|\geq P_{k}}|\mathcal{K}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}u_{k}\,\partial_{x}u_{k}\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right).

We readily deduce that the second term in the right hand side converges to zero as
k\to+\infty
,
since
\mathcal{K}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
u_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T))
as well as

\partial_{x}u_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
. Moreover, for the first term, we notice, thanks to the periodicity of
\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}
and
u_{k}
, that for a.e.
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
we have the following decomposition:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k}(t))(x)

\displaystyle-(\mathcal{K}\star v(t))(x)=\int_{-P_{k}}^{P_{k}}\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}(x-y)\,u_{k}(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)\,v(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathds{1}_{[-P_{k},P_{k}]}(y)\,\mathcal{K}(x-y)\left(u_{k}(y,t)-v(y,t)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{|y|\geq P_{k}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)\,v(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Now, we notice, as a consequence of Theorem
2
, that we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T))}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Moreover, since
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges locally uniformly (up to a subsequence) toward
v
as
k\to+\infty
, we deduce that
u_{k}\to v
strongly in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T))
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k})(x)-(\mathcal{K}\star v)(x)\right|\leq\|u_{k}-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T))}\,\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{|y|\geq P_{k}}\left|\mathcal{K}(y)\right|\,\mathrm{d}y,

so that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k})\to(\mathcal{K}\star v)\quad\mbox{strongly in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)),\,\mbox{as }k\to+\infty.

Finally, using (
81
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}(\mathcal{K}^{P_{k}}\ast u_{k})\,\partial_{x}u_{k}\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\to\int_{\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)}(\mathcal{K}\star v)\,\partial_{x}v\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,\quad\mbox{as }k\to+\infty.

Thereby,
v
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=(\mathcal{K}\star v)\,\partial_{x}v\quad\mbox{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)),

which concludes the proof of Proposition
1
.

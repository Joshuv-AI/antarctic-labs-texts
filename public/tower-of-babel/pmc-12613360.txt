Prophylactic effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs on heterotopic ossification after total hip arthroplasty: a Bayesian network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials using cumulative logistic regression
Authors: Lijun Xie, Zenghui Li, Ye Huang, Xinyuan Du, Xi Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeMost of the previous meta-analyses examining the effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) on preventing heterotopic ossification (HO) following total hip arthroplasty (THA) have separately analyzed Brooker’s classification I, II, III, and IV which may misrepresent their ordinal nature. We therefore applied a Bayesian network meta-regression that incorporates the ordinal nature of Brooker’s classification to more robustly assess NSAID efficacy and determine the optimal regimen.MethodsWe searched the Cochrane Library, Embase, and PubMed for randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Cumulative regressions were conducted for ordinal variants to generate Napierian Logarithm odds ratios (lnOR) and the standard error of lnOR (selnOR) for each study. Subsequently these data were used to conduct Bayesian network meta-analysis and further network meta-regression to generate pairwise ORs, showing pairwise effect sizes (ESs).Results17 studies (5436 patients,14 regimens) were eligible. In the raw data analysis, celecoxib 400 mg/d, etoricoxib 90 mg/d, ibuprofen 1200 mg/d, indomethacin 75 mg/d, indomethacin 100 mg/d, indomethacin 150 mg/d, meloxicam 7.5 mg/d, meloxicam 15 mg/d, and naproxen 750 mg/d were conspicuously effective (OR: 0.048 ~ 0.351) compared with placebo. The ESs were comparable among these regimens except for ibuprofen 1200 mg/d, which was inferior to indomethacin 100 mg/d (OR = 0.382, 95%CI: 0.171 to 0.887) and indomethacin 150 mg/d (OR = 0.136, 95%CI: 0.020 to 0.970). In the network meta-regression analysis, after adjusting for follow-up time, the significance of diclofenac 150 mg/d (OR = 0.102, 95%CI: 0.013 to 0.835) emerged compared with placebo. The results of effective regimens aforementioned resembled the initial findings (OR: 0.039 ~ 0.249). All the effective agents, including diclofenac 150 mg/d, were comparable in ESs.ConclusionsConsidering the efficacy of preventing HO following THA observed in our research, together with analgesic effect and gastrointestinal tract safety from previous literature, etoricoxib 90 mg/d is recommended as the optimal choice for patient undergoing THA. More head-to-head and long-term studies are needed.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09277-5.Keywords: Heterotopic ossification, Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, Prophylaxis, Total hip arthroplasty, Bayesian network meta-analysis

Introduction

Heterotopic ossification (HO), defined as the ectopic bone formation in soft tissue, is a common complication in orthopedics. It occurs in up to 87% of patients following total hip arthroplasty (THA) and up to 90% of patients following surgery for acetabular fracture, leading to pain and limiting the motion of limbs, and reduce the quality of life [1–3]. The mechanism of HO remains unclear, and generally considered as either genetic or acquired. Both mechanisms may result in the recruitment of osteogenic precursor cells by up-regulating pro-inflammatory mediators, including fibroblast growth factors, prostaglandins and transforming growth factor-β, etc., which may eventually contribute to the proliferation of osteoblast and HO in the location of trauma or surgery [4–6]. Practically, the administration of prophylactic non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and radiotherapy has been validated as effective in preventing HO [4, 6]. However, given concerns about the potential carcinogenicity of radiotherapy [7, 8], NSAIDs are considered as the main therapy to HO.

Currently, although several traditional meta-analyses examined the effects of NSAIDs on HO [9–11], the optimal choice of NSAIDs remains unclear. The primary limitation of these studies is that only allow the comparison of two treatments at a time restricting simultaneous evaluation of multiple treatments. Additionally, traditional meta-analysis may not fully utilize all available data, because it relies solely on direct comparisons and cannot incorporate the potential benefits of indirect comparisons. Furthermore, traditional meta-analysis may lead to complex multiple comparison problems, increasing the difficulty of calculating statistical significance levels. Therefore, traditional meta-analysis may be insufficient for addressing such clinical questions, underscoring the need for more advanced methods such as network meta-analysis [12, 13]. Network meta-analyses offer a significant advantage by enabling the comparison of multiple treatments simultaneously, utilizing both direct comparisons within randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and indirect comparisons across different trials [14]. This approach provides a more comprehensive understanding of the relative efficacy of various treatments and have been widely applied in various fields, such as pharmacotherapies [15], managing complications [16]. Among various methods of network meta-analyses, Bayesian network meta-analysis offers additional advantages by incorporating prior information and generating probabilistic estimates [17]. This approach is particularly useful when dealing with sparse data or rare events, as it helps to stabilize the estimates and reduce uncertainty [18]. Unlike traditional methods, it provides full posterior distributions with credible intervals, supports complex hierarchical modeling, and accounts for heterogeneity across studies [19]. This flexibility and the comprehensive nature of Bayesian analysis make it a powerful tool for network meta-analysis, providing more nuanced insights into the comparative effectiveness of different treatments [20].

Recently, network meta-analyses have also been applied for exploring the effects of NSAIDs on HO. For example, a study using Bayesian network meta-analysis compared all available NSAID treatments for the prevention of HO following THA and recommended celecoxib as a preventive measure for HO [21]. However, despite studies on the network meta-analysis of the effects of NSAIDs on preventing HO following THA, many studies have methodological limitations. They often combined the numbers of Brooker’s I + II (namely mild) and III + IV (namely severe) HO as a whole or analyzed each degree separately, which may ignore the substantial difference between each two adjacent grades and eventually mislead clinicians. In addition, some studies employed outdated classification systems, while Brooker’s HO classification remains the most widely acknowledged and employed principle in the orthopedic community [22, 23].

Hence, given the advantage of Bayesian network meta-regression analysis and ordinal merit of Brookers’ HO classification, we conducted this cumulative regression analysis orchestrated with Bayesian network meta-regression analysis to evaluate prophylactic efficacy of NSAIDs on HO after THA, provide more robust and convincing clinical evidence.

Materials and methods

Searching strategy and study design

This study aimed to investigate the effect of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (Intervention) compared with placebo or Placebo or no NSAID prophylaxis (Comparison) on the incidence of heterotopic ossification classified by the Brooker system (Outcome) in patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty (Population). We initiated our study by searching medical databases including Pubmed, Cochrane Library, and Embase with the following key terms: total hip arthroplasty, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory, heterotopic ossification, randomized controlled trial and Brooker, from inception to March, 2024. The comprehensive search strategies were presented in supplementary Table 1.

This systematic review and meta-analysis were registered in the PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420251133030). This study was conducted in accordance with the 2009 preferred reporting items for systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA) statement.

Inclusion criteria

Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or quasi RCTs with full-text availability that investigated the prophylactic efficacy of NSAIDs on the occurrence of HO defined by Brooker following THA were included. Studies needed to provide sufficient data for estimating odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Studies not using Brooker’s classification; (2) NSAIDs were combined with radiotherapies; (3) Individuals in each arm administrated with multiple NSAIDs (i.e., perioperative multi-model analgesia); (4) Each arm comprised different NSAIDs and the corresponding incidence of HO could not be determined; (5) Study investigated the recurrence of HO rather than the first occurrence of HO; (6) Conference articles; (7) Non-English articles; (8) The control group was historical or the study had merely single arm; (9) The dosages of agents varied and could not be segregated for analysis or were unusually implemented in clinical settings.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Once eligible studies were selected, two investigators (L.X. and Y.H.) independently assessed the quality by Cochrane risk of bias tools (2nd edition, ROB2) with Review Manager 5.3 and extracted the data from the studies. The authors’ name, study design, duration of follow-up, sample size, mean age, condition, the incidence of each degree HO (namely the incidence of HO by means of Brookers’ classification I, II, III, and IV) were documented for analysis. Any discrepancy was arbitrated by the senior reviewer (X.C.). Inter-rater reliability was assessed using Cohen’s kappa coefficient (κ = 0.93).

Statistical analysis

Cumulative logistic regressions were conducted for ordinal variants, literally HO, by “GENMOD” process in SAS 9.4 to generate Napierian Logarithm odds ratios (lnOR) and the standard error of lnOR (selnOR) for each study. These data were then input into R 4.3.2 using ʺgemtcʺ package to conduct Bayesian network meta-analysis as well as Bayesian network meta-regression adjusting follow-up period to generate pairwise ORs as effect sizes (ESs) in the matrix, as well as trace plots, density plots, and convergency plots. The smaller the OR was, the less probability of suffering more severe HO. Surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) was also estimated, where a higher SUCRA value indicates a greater probability of the corresponding regimen being the better option. However, the statistical significance of differences between regimens was determined using the league matrix. Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) was employed to obtain posterior distributions. We set the iteration times to 300,000 of 4 each chain, with 20,000 burn-in iterations and a thinning interval of 10 for each outcome. Model fit was assessed using the deviance information criterion (DIC), and between-study heterogeneity was estimated by the τ statistic. A node-splitting model was applied to analysis the consistency of the comprehensive comparison results.

The pooled overall incidence of HO was calculated by conventional meta-analysis utilizing STATA 17.0 MP. Statistical heterogeneity was quantified using I2 and P value. A fixed effect model was applied if there was no heterogeneity (I2 < 50% and P > 0.01); otherwise, the random effect model would be applied. Statistical significance (α) was set at 0.05.

Results

Search results and study inclusion

Eventually, a total of 17 studies comprising 5436 patients with 14 regimens were enrolled (Fig. 1) [24–40]. the 14 regimens were aspirin 162 mg/d, celecoxib 400 mg/d, diclofenac 150 mg/d, etoricoxib 90 mg/d, ibuprofen 1200 mg/d, indomethacin 75 mg/d, indomethacin 100 mg/d, indomethacin 150 mg/d, meloxicam 7.5 mg/d, meloxicam 15 mg/d, naproxen 750 mg/d, tenoxicam 10 mg/d, tenoxicam 20 mg/d, and placebo.

Based on SUCRA values, the top five treatments were Indomethacin 150 (91.8%), Meloxicam 15 (80.2%), Naproxen 750 (76.8%), Indomethacin 100 (69.0%), and Etoricoxib 90 (65.3%). After adjustment for follow-up time, the rankings changed to Indomethacin 150 (89.9%), Naproxen 750 (79.4%), Meloxicam 15 (77.4%), Etoricoxib 90 (68.4%), and Indomethacin 100 (64.4%) (see supplementary table 3 A ~ 3B).

Then the natural overall incidence of HO following THA was estimated by all the placebo group across studies. The raw data shows 54% (95%CI: 0.46 to 0.62) rate (see supplementary Fig. 1). However, the I2 was 94.6%, indicating significant heterogeneity, which suggests considerable variability among studies and warrants further exploration of potential sources of heterogeneity.

The trace plots, density plots, and convergency plots of all pairwise comparisons collectively show good convergence of the model after 300,000 iterations in both network meta-analysis and network meta-regression (see supplementary Fig. 2 ~ 5). In the network meta-analysis, the DIC was 54.06, while in the network meta-regression the DIC was 51.10. The estimated between-study heterogeneity (τ) was 0.523 in the network meta-analysis and 0.517 in the network meta-regression, indicating good model stability and robustness. Sensitivity analyses indicated consistent findings before and after adjustment for follow-up duration. In addition, consistency was confirmed by node-splitting and inconsistency analyses, with all P-values > 0.05, indicating no significant inconsistency within the network (see Supplementary Figs. 6).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first network meta-analysis to investigate the prophylactic effects of NSAIDs on HO following THA by implementing a cumulative regression model for ordinal data in conjunction with Bayesian network meta-analysis and network meta-regression analysis using lnOR and selnOR.

In this study, heterotopic ossification was assessed using the Brooker classification system. Although other classification systems exist, they were not adopted because preliminary analyses including such studies produced inconsistent and paradoxical results. To ensure methodological consistency and comparability across trials, we restricted inclusion to studies using the Brooker classification, in line with prior meta-analyses in this field [41–43]. However, it must be pointed out that most of the studies used Chi-square tests and conducted binomial network meta-analysis by merging the number of patients with adjacent grade of HO (for instance, grade I + II, grade III + IV, or even grade I + II + III + IV) as one group and the rest as the counterpart, an approach that is methodologically flawed. First, for ordinal data, the Chi-square test is inappropriate, instead, the rank sum test is the correct approach; Second, pooling the four graded degrees of effectiveness for ordinal data does not fully utilize the hierarchical structure, omitting the differences among the grades. An illustrative example is the study by Cai [41], which conducted network meta-analysis regarding each grade HO separately. Interestingly, the results suggested that selective NSAIDs were effective in preventing Brooker’s II (OR = 0.59, 95%CI: 0.34 to 0.98) and III/IV (OR = 0.13, 95%CI: 0.025 to 0.46) but not Brooker’s I (OR = 1.20, 95%CI: 0.64 to 2.30). This inconsistency highlights the potential limitations of analyzing each grade in isolation rather than accounting for the ordinal nature of the data. In this case, only cumulative logistic regression works [44].

Given the complexity of our analytical method, we translated the results as follows to simplify interpretation: After Bayesian network meta-regression analysis, agents with lower OR value could prevent patients from developing higher grade HO. Therefore, celecoxib 400 mg/d, diclofenac 150 mg/d, etoricoxib 90 mg/d, ibuprofen 1200 mg/d, indomethacin 75 mg/d, indomethacin 100 mg/d, indomethacin 150 mg/d, meloxicam 7.5 mg/d, meloxicam 15 mg/d, and naproxen 750 mg/d significantly decrease the risk of suffering higher grade HO by 75%~96% compared with placebo. In other words, they increased the possibility of suffering slighter disease. These results are fundamentally similar to those found in Migliorini’s dissertation [45]. Despite reaching similar conclusions, our methodology is stricter and more persuasive due to the use of Bayesian network meta-regression, which provides more robust and credible estimates by incorporating prior information and generating probabilistic interpretations of results.

A recent article by Migliorini [21] implemented Bayesian hierarchical random-effects model analysis on NSAIDs preventing HO succeeding THA. However, it still segregated the efficacy of agents by each degree of HO, as the previous studies did. Moreover, the study did not perform pairwise comparisons of all regimens. In addition, although the study included eight more studies than we did, we found these studies flawed: (1) Most did not adopt Brooker’s classification; (2) One of them was cohort study; (3) One study used historical control. Therefore, the conclusion that celecoxib was the best is not convincing.

Although indomethacin 150 mg/day achieved the highest SUCRA ranking in our analysis, SUCRA values should be interpreted with caution. As noted in previous study [46, 47], SUCRA reflects the probability of being ranked best but does not account for the magnitude of effect differences, the width of confidence intervals, or safety concerns, and may therefore overstate the clinical relevance of certain regimens. Accordingly, we placed greater emphasis on the network league tables, which provide direct estimates of effect sizes and their uncertainties, offering a more transparent and clinically meaningful basis for interpretation. In clinical settings, given the inferior gastrointestinal safety and more perioperative blood loss of non-selective COX inhibitors (due to the inhibition of both COX-1 and COX-2) compared with selective COX-2 inhibitors, cautions should be addressed, especially when using diclofenac, naproxen, and indomethacin as prophylactic regimens on HO [48]. An updated meta-analysis found that highly selective COX-2 inhibitors (etoricoxib, parecoxib, celecoxib) did not significantly increase intraoperative or postoperative blood loss [49]. Moreover, COX-2 semi-selective or partially selective inhibitors, including meloxicam should also be used with cautions, similar to the aforementioned non-selective COX inhibitors [50]. The above information could be concluded that in order to decrease the peri-operative blood loss or peptic bleeding risks when using none or partial-selective NSAIDs, more medications, including tranexamic acid and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like esomeprazole, should be administrated contemporarily, substantially increasing the medical costs. Regarding cardiovascular (CV) safety, two large scale clinical trials, namely PRECISION Study (comparing celecoxib, naproxen, and ibuprofen on cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, etc., in treating osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis) and MEDAL Program (comparing etoricoxib and diclofenac on thrombotic cardiovascular events in treating osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis), concluded that COX-2 selective inhibitors were not inferior to non-selective COX inhibitors on CV risks, a conclusion summarized as “class effect” later [51, 52]. Therefore, based on the above analysis, in this study, celecoxib 400 mg/d and etoricoxib 90 mg/d should be prioritized among the ten effective agents.

Another factor which should be taken into consideration is the analgesic effect of each agent. According to a well-known network meta-analysis published in 2017 in the Lancet [53], which included 76 RCTs with 58,451 patients with osteoarthritis, in terms of visual analogue scale (VAS), etoricoxib 90 mg/d ranked 2rd (95%CI: 1 to 15) with a surface under the cumulative ranking curve of 89%, whereas celecoxib 400 mg/d ranked 14th (95%CI: 9 to 18) and its SUCRA of 42%. The probabilities of reaching the minimum clinically important difference (MCID) were 95% for etoricoxib and 21% for celecoxib. Hence, etoricoxib 90 mg/d is superior to celecoxib 400 mg/d in terms of analgesia.

We reckoned that implementing NSAIDs for the prophylaxis of HO succeeding THA is just part of the intentions incorporated in the comprehensive managements of rehabilitations of the surgeries. Blood loss control, pain control, controlling gastrointestinal bleeding caused by stress or NSAIDs should be also considered. This holistic approach is rarely discussed in previous literature. Hence, in pragmatic clinical practice, considering of the discussed issues, selective COX-2 inhibitor etoricoxib 90 mg/d may be the best choice for patients following THA. Nevertheless, there might be wide concern that etoricoxib was not launched in the United States due to a dispute between Food and Drug Administration and Merck in 2007. However, it was launched in 63 other countries worldwide and is available 30 mg, 60 mg, 90 mg and 120 mg [31].

Despite these findings, predicting the occurrence of HO remains challenging. Luckily, in 2022, Alessandro [54] and Sukhmani [55] determined that male, age >65 years, surgical time >90 min, African American race, delayed drainage removal, osteoporosis, and low estrogen states were risk factors for severe HO after THA. Even though, there is no consensus or guideline on this complication up to date, making it difficult to decide when and how long to use NSAIDs as prophylaxis. But based on enrolled studies in this research, the treatment period ranged from 1 to 6 weeks, which might be reference.

Additionally, we calculated natural incidence of overall HO following THA. Imperatively, by conducting Bayesian network meta-regression to adjust for follow-up time using primitive studies (namely not adjusted), we found follow-up time modestly influenced the efficacy of certain regimen, such as diclofenac 150 mg/d. Since HO substantially progress from 3 months to 12 months, as noted in Persson’s study [56], we speculated that HO might stagnate for some time after receiving treatment by NSAIDs, which requires long-term investigation. Early intervention could retard progression from slight grade to severe grade.

Our work provides a useful and objective ranking of the efficacy of NASIDs on HO following THA. However, there are still some limitations to note. Firstly, the sample sizes of certain studies were small, resulting in relatively wide ranges of 95% confidence intervals, indicating less stability in our results. Thus, our findings should be interpreted with caution, especially where uncertainty in treatment effects is high. Secondly, due to the short follow-up period in some articles, the more severe HO grades, including grade III and IV, might not have emerged, contributing to the low incidence and wide range of 95% confidence intervals. Fortunately, we performed Bayesian network meta-regression analysis, which modestly improved the results. Thirdly, although we used Brooker’s classification, this hierarchy system is essentially subjective rather than objective. Therefore, different orthopedic surgeons might have distinct judgement on the same radiograph. Moreover, most of the studies were conducted ten or twenty years ago, with poor radiograph resolution, compromising the judgment. A further limitation is that we did not formally assess publication bias, as conventional methods (e.g., funnel plots, Egger’s test) are not well suited for network meta-analysis with multiple comparisons and few studies per contrast. Although no major inconsistency was detected in our node-splitting analyses, potential small-study effects or selective reporting cannot be completely ruled out. Finally, the natural biological behavior of HO is not well understood, so the optimal duration for using NSAIDs to prevent it remains unclear.

Conclusion

Considering the efficacy of preventing HO, analgesia and the safety of peri-operative blood loss and peptic bleeding risk, selective COX-2 inhibitor etoricoxib 90 mg/d may be the best choice for patients following THA. More head-to-head and long-term studies are needed.

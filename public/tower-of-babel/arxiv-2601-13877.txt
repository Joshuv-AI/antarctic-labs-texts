Riemannian optimization on the manifold of unitary and symmetric matrices with application to BD-RIS-assisted systems
Ignacio Santamaria, Mohammad Soleymani, Eduard Jorswieck, Jesús Gutiérrez, Carlos Beltrán

Abstract
In this paper, we rigorously characterize for the first time the manifold of unitary and symmetric matrices, deriving its tangent space and its geodesics. The resulting parameterization of the geodesics (through a real and symmetric matrix) allows us to derive a new Riemannian manifold optimization (MO) algorithm whose most remarkable feature is that it does not need to set any adaptation parameter. We apply the proposed MO algorithm to maximize the achievable rate in a multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) system assisted by a beyond-diagonal reconfigurable intelligent surface (BD-RIS), illustrating the method’s performance through simulations. The MO algorithm achieves a significant reduction in computational cost compared to previous alternatives based on Takagi decomposition, while retaining global convergence to a stationary point of the cost function.

Introduction

Manifold optimization methods have become an essential tool in modern signal processing, as many design problems involve constraints that naturally define smooth Riemannian manifolds
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
. A particularly relevant manifold arises when dealing with unitary matrices (
{\bf U}{\bf U}^{H}={\bf I}_{n}
). The unitary manifold, denoted as
\mathcal{U}
, has been extensively studied, and its geometry is well understood, allowing the development of effective Riemannian optimization algorithms with applications in communications
[
16
]
, blind source separation
[
1
]
, and machine learning
[
9
]
.

In certain applications, however, the optimization matrix must be both unitary and symmetric (
{\bf U}={\bf U}^{T}
), belonging to the manifold denoted in this paper as
\mathcal{U}_{s}
. Despite its relevance,
\mathcal{U}_{s}
has received little attention in the literature, and its main elements such as the tangent space and geodesics remain unexplored. The main goal of this paper is to rigorously study the geometry of
\mathcal{U}_{s}
and leverage these results to design manifold optimization (MO) algorithms that operate directly on this manifold.

As an application, we consider the rate maximization of a multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) link assisted by a beyond-diagonal reconfigurable intelligent surface (BD-RIS). In this context, the BD-RIS scattering matrix,
\bm{\Theta}
, is a unitary (passive lossless) and symmetric (due to reciprocity) matrix
[
15
,
10
]
, making it a natural candidate for optimization over
\mathcal{U}_{s}
. Previous approaches have circumvented the lack of characterization of
\mathcal{U}_{s}
by discarding the symmetry constraint and optimizing over
\mathcal{U}
, as in
[
4
]
; or by relaxing the problem and projecting the relaxed solution onto the set
\mathcal{U}_{s}
, as in
[
7
]
. These alternatives are clearly suboptimal and provide only an approximate solution. Other recent works
[
14
,
18
]
exploit the so-called Takagi factorization
[
17
,
3
]
, which states that any unitary and symmetric matrix
\bm{\Theta}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
can be decomposed as
\bm{\Theta}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
, where
{\bf Q}\in\mathcal{U}
, but the computational cost of these alternatives is high.

The main contributions of this work are:

•

We present a study of the manifold
\mathcal{U}_{s}
, characterizing its tangent space, its retraction mapping, and its geodesics.

•

Taking advantage of the parameterization of geodesics in
\mathcal{U}_{s}
, we present an MO algorithm that does not require adaptation parameters.

•

As an application of the MO algorithm, we consider the rate maximization problem in a BD-RIS-assisted MIMO system, comparing its performance with previous algorithms proposed in the literature.

Manifold optimization on
\mathcal{U}_{s}

2.1
The manifold
\mathcal{U}

It is well known that
\mathcal{U}=\{{\bf U}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}:{\bf U}{\bf U}^{H}={\bf I}_{n}\}
is a real Riemannian manifold of dimension
n^{2}

[
2
,
5
,
6
]
, with tangent space
T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}=\{{\bf B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}:{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}+{\bf B}^{H}{\bf U}=0\}
. Alternatively, the tangent space at
{\bf U}
can be parametrized as
{\bf B}={\bf U}{\bf S}
, where
{\bf S}
is an
n\times n
skew-Hermitian matrix.
The Riemannian exponential that maps a point in the tangent space to the manifold is defined in terms of the matrix exponential as

\begin{matrix}\exp_{\mathcal{U},{\bf U}}:&T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}&\to&\mathcal{U}\\
&{\bf B}&\to&{\bf U}e^{{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}}\end{matrix}

which is a diffeomorphism if restricted to tangent matrices
{\bf B}
of sufficiently small Frobenius norm
[
8
]
.

2.2
The manifold
\mathcal{U}_{s}

Our objective is to study the set
\mathcal{U}_{s}=\{{\bf U}\in\mathcal{U}:{\bf U}={\bf U}^{T}\}
and then derive a Riemannian optimization algorithm. Firstly, we recall Takagi’s decomposition of a complex and symmetric matrix, which plays an important role in the characterization of
\mathcal{U}_{s}
.

Theorem 1

(Takagi factorization
[
17
]
)
.

Let
{\bf A}={\bf A}^{T}
be an
n\times n
complex symmetric matrix. Then, there exist an
n\times n
unitary matrix
{\bf Q}\in\mathcal{U}
and an
n\times n
diagonal matrix
\bm{\Sigma}=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\sigma_{n}\geq 0
such that
{\bf A}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
.

From the singular value decomposition (SVD) of a symmetric matrix
{\bf A}={\bf F}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf G}^{H}
, its Takagi decomposition can be obtained as
{\bf A}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
with
{\bf Q}={\bf F}({\bf F}^{H}{\bf G}^{*})^{1/2}
. Note that if all singular values of
{\bf A}
are distinct,
{\bf F}^{H}{\bf G}^{*}
is a diagonal matrix and the factor
{\bf Q}
is obtained as described in
[
13
, Remark 2]
. We have the following propositions:

Proposition 1

(
tangent space and geodesics).

\mathcal{U}_{s}
is a real Riemannian manifold of dimension
n(n+1)/2
and its tangent space at the point
{\bf U}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
is

\displaystyle T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}_{s}=

\displaystyle\{{\bf B}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}:{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}+{\bf B}^{H}{\bf U}={\bf 0},\;{\bf B}={\bf B}^{T}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{j{\bf Q}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}:{\bf R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},{\bf R}={\bf R}^{T}\},

where
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
is a Takagi decomposition of
{\bf U}
. Therefore, the tangent space at
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
is composed of all matrices
{\bf B}=j{\bf Q}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}
with
{\bf R}
an
n\times n
real and symmetric matrix. In addition, the Riemannian exponential is the restriction of
\exp_{\mathcal{U},{\bf U}}
to
T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}_{s}
. Therefore, the geodesic in
\mathcal{U}_{s}
starting at
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
and with direction
{\bf B}=j{\bf Q}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}\in T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}_{s}

{\bf U}(\mu)={\bf U}e^{{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}\mu}={\bf U}e^{j{\bf Q}^{*}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}\mu},

with
{\bf R}
real and symmetric, and
\mu\geq 0

Proof.

See Appendix.

The above result can be rewritten as follows. The matrix
j{\bf R}
(purely imaginary skew-Hermitian), admits the following eigendecomposition
j{\bf R}={\bf V}_{R}\bm{\Sigma}_{R}{\bf V}_{R}^{T}
, where
{\bf V}_{R}
is a (real) orthogonal matrix and
\bm{\Sigma}_{R}=\operatorname{diag}(j\theta_{1},\ldots,j\theta_{n})
is a diagonal matrix with the imaginary eigenvalues. Applying standard results of the exponentiation of diagonalizable matrices, we see that the geodesic starting at
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
and pointing in the direction
j{\bf Q}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\bf U}(\mu)={\bf U}e^{j{\bf Q}^{*}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}{\bf Q}^{*}{\bf V}_{R}\bm{\Lambda}_{R}^{\mu}{\bf V}_{R}^{T}{\bf Q}^{T}

(1)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\bf Q}{\bf V}_{R}\bm{\Lambda}_{R}^{\mu}{\bf V}_{R}^{T}{\bf Q}^{T}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\bf Q}_{R}\bm{\Lambda}_{R}^{\mu}{\bf Q}_{R}^{T}

where in
(a)
we have used
{\bf Q}^{T}{\bf Q}^{*}={\bf I}_{n}
, in
(b)
the unitary matrix
{\bf Q}{\bf V}_{R}
is denoted as
{\bf Q}_{R}
, and
\bm{\Lambda}_{R}^{\mu}
is the following diagonal matrix

\bm{\Lambda}_{R}^{\mu}=\exp(\bm{\Sigma}_{R})^{\mu}=\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{j\theta_{1}\mu},\ldots,e^{j\theta_{n}\mu}\right),

which is essentially a diagonal RIS. The formulation of the geodesic in (
1
) is the main result of this work, as it establishes an interesting connection between the optimization of a unitary and symmetric BD-RIS and that of a diagonal RIS. In words, geodesics in
\mathcal{U}_{s}
are traversed by multiplying the phases of a diagonal RIS,
\bm{\Lambda}_{R}=\exp(\bm{\Sigma}_{R})
, by a step size
\mu
. The direction of the geodesic, which will depend on the cost function to be optimized, is determined by the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of
{\bf R}
.

Proposition 2

(
Retraction to

\mathcal{U}_{s}
)
.

The mapping

\begin{matrix}\Pi:&\{{\bf A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}:{\bf A}={\bf A}^{T}\}&\to&\mathcal{U}_{s}\\
&{\bf A}&\to&{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T},\end{matrix}

where
{\bf A}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
is a Takagi factorization of
{\bf A}
, sends
{\bf A}
to the unitary and symmetric matrix
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
closest to
{\bf A}
in Frobenius norm (to one of them, if there are several).

Proof.

Given
{\bf A}={\bf F}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf G}^{H}
it is known that
\Pi({\bf A})={\bf F}{\bf G}^{H}
is the unitary matrix closest to
{\bf A}
. If
{\bf A}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
is symmetric,
\Pi({\bf A})={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
is hence the unitary and symmetric matrix closest to
{\bf A}
in
\mathcal{U}
and consequently in
\mathcal{U}_{s}\subset\mathcal{U}
.
∎

Proposition 3

(
Projection to the tangent space).

Given any matrix
{\bf J}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the orthogonal projection of
{\bf J}
onto
T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}_{s}
is

\pi_{T_{{\bf U}}\mathcal{U}_{s}}{\bf J}=j\,{\bf Q}\underbrace{\mathrm{Imag}\left(\frac{{\bf Q}^{H}({\bf J}+{\bf J}^{T}){\bf Q}^{*}}{2}\right)}_{{\bf R}}{\bf Q}^{T},

(2)

with
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
a Takagi decomposition of
{\bf U}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
1
the tangent space at
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
can be parameterized as
{\bf B}=j{\bf Q}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}
with
{\bf R}
real and symmetric. Solving the problem

\min_{{\bf R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},{\bf R}={\bf R}^{T}}\|{\bf J}-j{\bf Q}{\bf R}{\bf Q}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}

we get (
2
).
∎

Application

3.1
Rate optimization of BD-RIS-assisted MIMO links

As an application of Algorithm
1
, we consider the design of a BD-RIS in a MIMO channel to maximize the achievable rate. The MIMO system, with
N_{t}
transmit antennas and
N_{r}
receive antennas, is assisted by a fully connected BD-RIS with
M
elements. The equivalent MIMO channel is

{\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta})={\bf H}_{d}+{\bf F}\bm{\Theta}{\bf G}^{H},

(3)

where
{\bf G}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times M}
is the channel from the Tx to the BD-RIS,
{\bf F}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}\times M}
is the channel from the BD-RIS to the Rx,
{\bf H}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}\times N_{t}}
is the MIMO direct link, and
\bm{\Theta}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
is the
M\times M
BD-RIS matrix.

The Tx sends proper Gaussian signals with a fixed isotropic covariance matrix,
{\bf x}\sim\mathcal{CN}({\bf 0},P{\bf I})
, and the received signal is contaminated by additive white Gaussian noise,
{\bf n}\sim\mathcal{CN}({\bf 0},\sigma^{2}{\bf I})
. Then, the BD-RIS that maximizes the achievable rate solves the problem

\,\max_{\bm{\Theta}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}}\,\,f(\bm{\Theta})=\max_{\bm{\Theta}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}}\log\operatorname{det}\left({\bf I}_{N_{R}}+\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}}{\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta}){\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta})^{H}\right),

where
{\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta})
is given by (
3
). Let us define for simplicity of notation
{\bf E}(\bm{\Theta})={\bf I}_{N_{R}}+\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}}{\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta}){\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta})^{H}
. The unconstrained gradient of the cost function is
[
12
]

{\bf J}=\nabla f(\bm{\Theta})=\frac{P}{\sigma^{2}}{\bf F}^{H}{\bf E}(\bm{\Theta})^{-1}{\bf H}_{eq}(\bm{\Theta}){\bf G}.

(4)

The projection of
\nabla f(\bm{\Theta})
onto the tangent space gives us the symmetric and real matrix
{\bf R}
in (
2
). From the eigendecomposition of
{\bf R}
, the new BD-RIS can be parameterized as
\bm{\Theta}={\bf Q}_{R}\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{j\theta_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,e^{j\theta_{M}^{\prime}}\right){\bf Q}_{R}^{T}
. We can now substitute this BD-RIS parameterization into the rate expression and apply an alternating optimization method to independently optimize each of the phases
\theta_{m}^{\prime}
. To solve this problem, we can use the method proposed in
[
19
, Sec. III.B]
.

3.2
Computational cost

To simplify the notation, we consider the maximization of the rate of an
N\times N
MIMO system. For this particular case, the computation of the gradient in (
4
) has a complexity
\mathcal{O}(MN^{2}+M^{2}N+N^{3})
. The projection on the tangent space involves the multiplication of
M\times M
matrices and is
\mathcal{O}(2M^{3})
, and the eigendecomposition of
{\bf R}
is
\mathcal{O}(M^{3})
. The update of the Takagi factor to
{\bf Q}_{k}={\bf Q}_{k-1}{\bf V}_{R}\bm{\Lambda}_{R}^{1/2}
is also
\mathcal{O}(M^{3})
. Finally, the computational cost per iteration grows as
\mathcal{O}(4M^{3}+MN^{2}+M^{2}N+N^{3})
, which is dominated by the first term.

5
Conclusions

The characterization of the tangent space and the geodesics of the manifold of unitary and symmetric matrices has allowed us to propose a new Riemannian optimization algorithm that does not require any adaptation parameter (e.g., step size). The application of the proposed MO algorithm to optimizing the rate of a BD-RIS-assisted MIMO system shows a significant reduction in computational cost with identical performance compared to previous alternatives based on Takagi decomposition. In future work, we will consider the application of the proposed MO algorithm to other problems, such as the minimization of the mean square error (MSE)
[
11
]
in BD-RIS assisted systems.

Geodesic

\phi({\bf B})={\bf U}e^{{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}}

]
. Therefore, its restriction to the set
\mathcal{U}_{s}
(i.e., when
{\bf B}\in T_{U}\mathcal{U}_{s}
) is a diffeomorphism with its image. It remains to prove that the set

\mathcal{X}=\{\phi({\bf B}):{\bf B}\in T_{U}\mathcal{U}_{s},\|{\bf B}\|_{F}^{2}<\epsilon\}

contains an open set of
{\bf B}
in
\mathcal{U}_{s}
. We prove the double inclusion:

•

\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
: We have that
\phi({\bf B})\in\mathcal{U}

\mathcal{U}
. We have to prove that
\phi({\bf B})
is symmetric.

\displaystyle\phi({\bf B})^{T}=\left({\bf U}e^{{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}}\right)^{T}=\left({\bf U}e^{{\bf U}^{H}{\bf B}{\bf U}^{H}{\bf U}}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle=\left({\bf U}{\bf U}^{H}e^{{\bf B}{\bf U}^{H}}{\bf U}\right)^{T}={\bf U}e^{({\bf B}{\bf U}^{H})^{T}}=\phi({\bf B}).

•

The fact that for every
{\bf U}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{s}
in a neighborhood of
{\bf U}
there exists a
{\bf B}\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\phi({\bf B})={\bf U}^{\prime}
follows from the fact that the matrix exponential is locally invertible at
{\bf 0}
since
\frac{d(e^{t{\bf C}})}{dt}|_{t=0}={\bf C}
.

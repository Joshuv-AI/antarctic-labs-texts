Well-posedness and long-time behavior of a bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard model with non-degenerate mobility
Jonas Stange

Abstract
. We study a bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard model with non-degenerate mobility and singular potentials in two dimensions. Following the ideas of the recent work by Conti, Galimberti, Gatti, and Giorgini [Calc. Var. Partial Differential Equations, 64(3):Paper No. 87, 32, 2025] for the Cahn–Hilliard equation with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, we show the uniqueness of weak solutions together with a continuous dependence estimate for sufficiently regular mobility functions. Next, under weaker assumptions on the mobility functions, we show the existence of a weak solution that exhibits the propagation of uniform-in-time regularity and satisfies the instantaneous separation property. Lastly, we consider the long-time behavior and prove that the unique weak solution converges to a solution of the stationary bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard equation. Our approach for the uniqueness proof relies on a new well-posedness and regularity theory for a bulk-surface elliptic system with non-constant coefficients, which may be of independent interest.

Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the following bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard model

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi=\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla\mu)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi=\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta)-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma\times(0,\infty),

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\theta=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma\times(0,\infty),

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=\alpha\psi-\phi&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma\times(0,\infty),

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=\beta\theta-\mu&\text{if}\ L\in[0,\infty),\\
m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0&\text{if}\ L=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma\times(0,\infty),

(1.1f)

\displaystyle\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega,

(1.1g)

\displaystyle\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma.

(1.1h)

System (
1.1
) is a special case of the model proposed in
[
22
]
. There, the authors considered an extended version of (
1.1
) with convection.

In (
1.1
),
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
. We use the abbreviation
Q=\Omega\times(0,\infty)
and
\Sigma=\Gamma\times(0,\infty)
. The outward pointing unit normal vector on
\Gamma
is denoted by
\mathbf{n}
, while
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the outward normal derivative on the boundary. Moreover, the symbols
\nabla_{\Gamma}
and
\Delta_{\Gamma}
stand for the surface gradient and the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
, respectively.

The functions
\phi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\mu:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the phase-field and the chemical potential in the bulk, whereas
\psi:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\theta:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
represent the phase-field and the chemical potential on the boundary, respectively. The functions
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}:[-1,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are the so-called Onsager mobilities, and typically depend on the phase-field variables
\phi
and
\psi
, respectively. They model the spatial locations and intensity at which the diffusion processes take place.

In (
1.1
), the time evolution of the bulk-variables
\phi
and
\mu
is governed by the
bulk Cahn–Hilliard subsystem
(
1.1a
)-(
1.1b
), while the evolution of the surface quantities
\psi
and
\theta
is given by the
surface Cahn–Hilliard subsystem
(
1.1c
)-(
1.1d
), which is coupled to the bulk by expressions involving the normal derivatives
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi
and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu
. Moreover, the phase-field
\phi
and
\psi
are coupled by the boundary condition (
1.1e
), while the chemical potentials
\mu
and
\theta
are coupled by the boundary condition (
1.1f
). Here, the parameters
K,L\in[0,\infty]
are used to distinguish different types of these coupling conditions and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
describe different physical phenomena, see, for instance,
[
14
,
22
]
for a more extensive description. These type of boundary conditions fall into the class of
dynamical boundary conditions
, which generalize the classical homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma\times(0,\infty),

typically imposed in standard Cahn–Hilliard models. Although Neumann boundary conditions are widely used, they can be overly restrictive when a precise description of boundary dynamics is required, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
22
]
. This has led to the development and analysis of several dynamic boundary condition formulations in the literature, we refer to the recent survey
[
34
]
and the references therein for a comprehensive overview. In particular, dynamic boundary conditions of Cahn–Hilliard type that incorporate mass exchange between bulk and boundary have received considerable attention in recent years, see, e.g.,
[
16
,
24
,
18
,
19
]
. This context motivates the study of the coupled bulk-surface system (
1.1
).

The functions
F^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
are the derivatives of double-well potentials
F
and
G
, respectively. A physically motivated example of such a double-well potential, especially in applications related to material science, is the
Flory-Huggins potential
, which is also referred to as the
logarithmic potential.
It is given as

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{log}}(s)\coloneqq\frac{\Theta}{2}\Big[(1+s)\ln(1+s)+(1-s)\ln(1-s)\Big]-\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}s^{2},\qquad s\in[-1,1],

with the convention that
0\ln 0
is interpreted as zero. The positive parameters
\Theta
and
\Theta_{0}
denote the temperature of the mixture and the critical temperature below which phase separation processes occur, respectively, and are supposed to satisfy
\Theta_{0}-\Theta>0
. Since
W_{\mathrm{log}}^{\prime}(s)\rightarrow\pm\infty
as
s\rightarrow\pm 1
, the potential
W_{\mathrm{log}}
is a so-called singular potential. In this contribution, we consider a more general class of singular potentials (see Section
2.3
) such that, for example, the choice
F=G=W_{\mathrm{log}}
is admissible.

The free energy functional associated with system (
1.1
) reads as

\displaystyle\begin{split}E(\phi,\psi)&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+F(\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right|^{2}+G(\psi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\chi(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\alpha\psi-\phi\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(1.2)

Here, to account for the different cases corresponding to the choice of
K
, the function

\displaystyle\chi:[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,\infty),\quad\chi(r)\coloneqq\begin{cases}r^{-1},&\text{if }r\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\text{if }r\in\{0,\infty\}\end{cases}

is used.
We observe that sufficiently regular solutions of the system (
1.1
) satisfy the
mass conservation law

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L=\infty\end{cases}

(1.3)

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and the
energy identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(\phi,\psi)&=-\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

(1.4)

on
[0,\infty)
. We note that the right-hand side of (
1.4
) is non-positive, which means that the energy dissipates over time, and the terms appearing on the right-hand side of (
1.4
) can be interpreted as the dissipation rate.

Goals and novelties of this paper.

System (
1.1
) has been extensively studied in the literature. We refer, for instance, to
[
16
,
24
,
19
,
18
,
6
,
10
,
27
,
25
,
26
]
. An extended model with convection was recently analyzed in
[
22
]
for regular potentials, and in
[
15
,
23
]
for singular potentials. However, existing analytical results on uniqueness and higher regularity have so far required the mobility functions
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
to be constant. This represents a significant limitation, as in many physical applications the diffusion intensity may vary spatially and is not expected to be uniform throughout the domain. For the classical Cahn–Hilliard equation with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, the recent work
[
8
]
establishes the uniqueness and the propagation regularity of weak solutions for non-degenerate mobility functions in two dimensions, and thus improved the state of the art dating back to the work
[
2
]
. We further would like to mention the recent work
[
9
]
, where the authors established a weak-strong uniqueness principle for the Cahn–Hilliard equation on an evolving surface. Building on the approach developed in
[
8
]
, we extend the uniqueness theory to the the Cahn–Hilliard equation with dynamic boundary conditions (
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result addressing uniqueness and propagation of regularity in the setting of dynamic boundary conditions with non-constant mobilities.
Our proof relies on two main ingredients: a novel well-posedness and regularity theory for a bulk-surface elliptic system with non-constant coefficients (see Section
4
), as well as a recently established result on higher time-regularity for the phase-fields (see
[
15
, Theorem 3.3]
). Then, using our proof, particularly the key differential inequality established therein, we are able to demonstrate the propagation of regularity for weak solutions. To this end, we introduce an additional regularization procedure for the mobility functions, which allows us to work under minimal assumptions on their regularity. As a consequence, we also obtain the instantaneous separation property. This separation property, in turn, enables us to prove convergence to a single stationary state by means of the standard Łojasiewicz–Simon approach. It is worth mentioning that our results can be readily adapted to the case of the convective model proposed in
[
22
]
for sufficiently regular prescribed velocity fields. This might be useful in further analysis of related models for two-phase flows with bulk-surface interaction, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
11
]
for a bulk-surface Navier–Stoks–Cahn–Hilliard model or
[
21
]
for a bulk-surface Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard model in an evolving domain.

Structure of this paper.
The rest of this contribution is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we collect some notation, assumptions, preliminaries, and important tools. After introducing the notation of a weak solution of (
1.1
), we state our main results in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we establish a new well-posedness and regularity theory for a bulk-surface elliptic system with non-constant coefficients. Then, Section
5
is devoted to the proof of the well-posedness of weak solutions to (
1.1
). Afterwards, in Section
6
, we show that there exists a weak solution that admits the propagation of regularity and satisfies the instantaneous separation property. Lastly, in Section

) and show its convergence to a single stationary state as

.

Notation and Function Spaces.

For any Banach space

If

is a finite interval, we find that

. Further, we denote the space of continuous functions mapping from
I
to

.

. For any

\Omega

The Lebesgue, Sobolev, and Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces on the boundary can be defined similarly, provided that

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\|(\zeta,\xi)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}\leq C\sqrt{r}\|(\zeta,\xi)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{\frac{2}{r}}\|(\zeta,\xi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{\frac{r-2}{r}}\qquad\text{for all~}(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}.

Lastly, we present the following uniform variant of the Gronwall lemma. A proof can be found, e.g., in

Lemma 2.3

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y

\displaystyle\leq gy+h,

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+r}g(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq a_{1},\quad\int_{t}^{t+r}h(s)\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq a_{2},\quad\int_{t}^{t+r}y(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq a_{3}\quad\text{for all~}t\geq t_{0},

where
r,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are positive constants. Then it holds

\displaystyle y(t)\leq\left(\frac{a_{3}}{r}+a_{2}\right)e^{a_{1}}\qquad\text{for all~}t\geq t_{0}+r.

Main Assumptions.

(A1)

The constants

(2.3)

(A3)

For the potentials, we assume that

\displaystyle F(s)=F_{1}(s)+F_{2}(s),\qquad G(s)=G_{1}(s)+G_{2}(s),

where

such that
F_{1}(0)=F_{1}^{\prime}(0)=G_{1}(0)=G_{1}^{\prime}(0)=0

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\lim_{s\searrow-1}G_{1}^{\prime}(s)=-\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{s\nearrow 1}F_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}G_{1}^{\prime}(s)=+\infty,

and there exist constants
\Theta_{\Omega},\Theta_{\Gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq\Theta_{\Omega}\quad\text{and~}\quad G_{1}^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq\Theta_{\Gamma}\qquad\text{for all~}s\in(-1,1).

(2.4)

We extend

and
G_{1}
on

by defining

for

. For

and

such that their derivatives are globally Lipschitz continuous. Lastly, we require that the singular part of the boundary potential dominates the singular part of the bulk potential in the sense that there exist constants

such that

\displaystyle\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha s)\right|\leq\kappa_{1}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(s)\right|+\kappa_{2}\qquad\text{for all~}s\in(-1,1).

(2.5)

For certain results established in this work, we require additional growth conditions on the singular components

and

(A4)

We assume that one of the following conditions hold:

(A4.1)

There exist constants
C_{\sharp}>0
and

such that

\displaystyle\left|F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(s)\right|\leq C_{\sharp}\mathbf{e}^{C_{\sharp}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(s)\right|^{\gamma_{\sharp}}}\qquad\text{for all~}s\in(-1,1).

(2.6)

(A4.2)

As

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F_{1}^{\prime}(1-2\delta)}=O\left(\frac{1}{\left|\ln\delta\right|^{\kappa}}\right),\qquad\frac{1}{\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(-1+2\delta)\right|}=O\left(\frac{1}{\left|\ln\delta\right|^{\kappa}}\right).

(2.7)

3.1.
Well-posedness of weak solutions

We start by introducing the notion of a weak solution.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K,L\in[0,\infty]
, and let
\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K}
be an initial datum satisfying

\displaystyle\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1,\qquad\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 1.

(3.1a)

In addition, we assume that

\displaystyle\beta\,\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in(-1,1),\quad\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in(-1,1),\quad\text{if~ }L\in[0,\infty),

(3.1b)

and

\displaystyle{\langle\phi_{0}\rangle}_{\Omega}\in(-1,1),\quad{\langle\psi_{0}\rangle}_{\Gamma}\in(-1,1),\quad\text{if~ }L=\infty.

(3.1c)

The quadruplet
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
is a called a weak solution of the system (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
for
T>0
if the following properties hold:

(i)

The functions
\phi,\psi,\mu
and
\theta
satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}_{L}^{1})^{\prime})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{K}^{1}),

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\left(\mu,\theta\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{L}^{1}),

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\left(F^{\prime}(\phi),G^{\prime}(\psi)\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2}),

(3.2c)

and it holds

\left|\phi\right|<1\quad\text{a.e.~in $Q$}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\psi\right|<1\quad\text{a.e.~on $\Sigma$}.

(3.3)

(ii)

The initial conditions are satisfied in the following sense:

\displaystyle\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}\quad\text{a.e.~in }\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}\quad\text{a.e.~on }\Gamma.

(iii)

The variational formulation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\left(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L}^{1}}&=-\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla\mu\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad-\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\Omega}\mu\,\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\theta\,\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta+F^{\prime}(\phi)\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\vartheta+G^{\prime}(\psi)\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\chi(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi-\phi)(\alpha\vartheta-\eta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(3.4b)

holds a.e. on
[0,T]
for all
\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{L}^{1},\left(\eta,\vartheta\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{K}^{1}
.

(iv)

The functions
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy the mass conservation law

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L=\infty\end{cases}

(3.5)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

(v)

The energy inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E(\phi(t),\psi(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\chi(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\leq E(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\end{split}

(3.6)

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Our first main result is devoted to the well-posedness of weak solutions to (
1.1

4.
Elliptic bulk-surface system with non-constant coefficients

In this section, we establish well-posedness and regularity results for an elliptic system with bulk-surface coupling and non-constant coefficients. These regularity results will be of crucial essence in the proof of the uniqueness of weak solutions to the system (
1.1
). Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, be a bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
. The precise system under investigation in this section is the following

\displaystyle-\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u)

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(4.1a)

\displaystyle-\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v)+\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(4.1b)

\displaystyle Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\beta v-u

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(4.1c)

where the mobility functions
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}
are supposed to satisfy
(A2)
.
Moreover,
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\psi:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are given measurable functions with
\left|\phi\right|\leq 1
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\left|\psi\right|\leq 1
a.e. on
\Gamma
. System (
4.1
) can be seen as an extension of (
2.1
) and the corresponding results proven in
[
20
]
.

We call
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
a weak solution to (
4.1
) if it satisfies the following weak formulation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v-u)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\big<(f,g),(\zeta,\xi)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}}\end{split}

(4.2)

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
.

In our first result, we establish the existence of a unique weak solution to (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain, and let
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}_{L}^{-1}
. Then there exists a unique weak solution
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
to (
4.1
). Additionally, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\Omega,L,\beta
and
m^{\ast}
such that

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{L}\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L})^{\prime}}.

(4.3)

We omit the proof here, as it follows by analogous arguments to those used in case of constant coefficients, see
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
for details.

In view of Theorem
4.1
, we can define a solution operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi]:\mathcal{V}_{L}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{L}^{1},\quad(f,g)\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)=(\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[\phi,\psi](f,g),\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[\phi,\psi](f,g)),

where
\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)
is the unique weak solution to (
4.1
). Moreover, we can define an inner product and its induced norm on
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L}
by

\displaystyle\big((f,g),(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,[\phi,\psi]}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla f\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}g\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta g-f)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,

\displaystyle\|(f,g)\|_{L,[\phi,\psi]}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big((f,g),(f,g)\big)_{L,[\phi,\psi]}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
(f,g),(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L}
.
One readily sees that

\displaystyle\min\{1,\sqrt{m^{\ast}}\}\|(f,g)\|_{L,[\phi,\psi]}\leq\|(f,g)\|_{L}\leq\max\{1,\sqrt{M^{\ast}}\}\|(f,g)\|_{L,[\phi,\psi]}

(4.4)

for all
(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
. In particular, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{L}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L,[\phi,\psi]}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L}
.

Next, we define an inner product and its induced norm on
\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}
by

\displaystyle\big((f,g),(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g),\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,[\phi,\psi]},

\displaystyle\|(f,g)\|_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big((f,g),(f,g)\big)_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
(f,g),(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}
. Using the respective weak formulation satisfied by the solution operators
\mathcal{S}_{L}
and
\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi]
, one can readily check that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L,\ast}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}
with

\displaystyle\min\{1,\sqrt{m^{\ast}}\}\|(f,g)\|_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}\leq\|(f,g)\|_{L,\ast}\leq\max\{1,\sqrt{M^{\ast}}\}\|(f,g)\|_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}

(4.5)

for all
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}
. In particular, we also obtain that
\|\cdot\|_{L,[\phi,\psi],\ast}
and
\|\cdot\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L})^{\prime}}
are equivalent on
\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}
.

Besides, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}&=\sqrt{\big(\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g),(f,g)\big)_{L,[\phi,\psi]}}\\
&\leq\max\{1,\sqrt{M^{\ast}}\}\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\|_{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(f,g)\|_{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

(4.6)

for all
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
.

Our next result establishes higher regularity results for
\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain of class
C^{2}
, let
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{W}^{1,\infty}
,
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}\in C^{1}([-1,1])
, and consider a pair
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}\cap\mathcal{L}^{2}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Additionally, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\\
&\quad\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[\phi,\psi](f,g),\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[\phi,\psi](f,g))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big).\end{split}

(4.7)

Proof.

For the sake of brevity, we use again
(u,v)
to denote the unique solution
\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)
of (
4.1
). We start by showing that
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
. To this end, we make a case distinction according to the parameter
L\in[0,\infty]
.

Case
L=0
.
Choosing
\xi=0
in the weak formulation (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x

(4.8)

for all
\zeta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Noting on
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, we can use
\zeta=\frac{\bar{\zeta}}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as a test function in (
4.8
) for some
\bar{\zeta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Consequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\bar{\zeta}\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\Big(f+\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\Big)\bar{\zeta}\;\mathrm{d}x

for all
\bar{\zeta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. In particular, as
\bar{\zeta}
was arbitrary, this implies that the distributional derivative
\Delta u
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\Big(f+\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\Big)\qquad\text{a.e. in~}\Omega.

As we further know that
u|_{\Gamma}=\beta v\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
, we may apply elliptic regularity theory for the Poisson–Dirichlet problem (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.2]
) to conclude that
u\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)
with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\big).

(4.9)

Now, since
\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
u\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)
, we can use a variant of the elliptic trace theorem (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 2.27]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.1]
) to deduce that
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
with

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}.

(4.10)

Consequently, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\zeta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\qquad\text{for all~}\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(4.11)

Consider now an arbitrary function
\xi\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
. According to the inverse trace theorem (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 4.2.3]
), there exists a function
\bar{\xi}\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)
such that
\bar{\xi}|_{\Gamma}=\xi
a.e. on
\Gamma
. Choosing
\zeta=\beta\bar{\xi}
, we see that
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
is an admissible test function in (
4.2
). Using the identity (
4.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v\cdot\nabla\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}(g-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

As
\xi\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
was arbitrarily chosen, we infer similarly to above that
v
is a weak solution of the surface elliptic equation

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v=\frac{1}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\Big(g-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u+\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\Big)\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

Since
\psi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma)
and
m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}
is bounded, we infer form the Sobolev inequality that
\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, which, in combination with
v\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
implies that
\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
. Thus, since
m_{\Omega}
is bounded, and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
according to (
4.10
), we find that

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v=\tilde{g}\in L^{2}(\Gamma).

Recalling that
\Gamma
is a compact submanifold of class
C^{2}
without boundary, we can apply regularity theory for elliptic equations on submanifolds (see, e.g.,
[
31
, s.5, Theorem 1.3]
) to infer
v\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
, together with the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}&\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big).\end{split}

(4.12)

Here, we have additionally used (
4.3
), (
4.9
), and (
4.10
) in the last inequality. Since
u|_{\Gamma}=\beta v
a.e. on
\Gamma
, we further deduce that
u|_{\Gamma}\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
. Recalling that
-\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we eventually conclude from elliptic regularity theory for the Poisson–Dirichlet problem (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.2]
) that
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big).\end{split}

(4.13)

Finally, combining (
4.12
)-(
4.13
) leads to

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big).

(4.14)

Case
L\in(0,\infty)
.
Here, we fix
\zeta=0
. Then, the weak formulation (
4.2
) reduces to

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v\cdot\nabla\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v-u)\beta\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}g\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
\xi\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
. This means that
v
is a weak solution of the surface elliptic problem

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v=\frac{1}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\Big(g-\beta\chi(L)(\beta v-u)+\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\Big)\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

Here, we can argue similarly to the case
L=0
. Indeed, since
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we have by the trace theorem
\beta v-u|_{\Gamma}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, and thus, in particular,
\beta v-u|_{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
. Therefore, we deduce with elliptic regularity theory on submanifolds that
v\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
together with the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}&\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)\end{split}

(4.15)

for some constant
C>0
. Next, we choose
\xi=0
in the weak formulation (
4.2
), which yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v-u)\zeta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Omega}f\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x

for all
\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. This means that
u
is a weak solution to the Poisson–Neumann problem

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\Big(f+\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\chi(L)(\beta v-u)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

Applying elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with inhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
31
, s.5, Proposition 7.7]
), we deduce
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
together with the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}+\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).\end{split}

(4.16)

Combining estimates (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) yields

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big).

(4.17)

Case
L=\infty
.
In this case, the system (
4.1
) decouples to the non-homogeneous Poisson problem with a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition

\displaystyle-\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u)

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(4.18)

\displaystyle m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(4.19)

and the non-homogeneous surface elliptic problem

\displaystyle-\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v)=g\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

(4.20)

Applying elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s problem with homogeneous Neumman boundary condition (
4.18
)-(
4.19
) and regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (
4.20
), respectively, we directly conclude the desired regularity
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and
v\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
along with the estimates

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big),

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big).\qed

Remark 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.2
, the estimate (
4.6
) even holds for all
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}
, i.e., the functions
f
and
g
do not need to satisfy the trace relation
f=\beta g
on
\Gamma
if
L=0
. This will be essential
in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, as we need to use (
4.6
) with functions
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K}

\textnormal{mean}\left(f,g\right)=0

\displaystyle\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\max\{1,\sqrt{M^{\ast}}\}C_{P}C\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\|_{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(f,g)\|_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.21)

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we can prove
\mathcal{H}^{3}
-regularity of the unique solution to (

) provided that the mobility functions are more regular.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be of class
C^{3}
, let
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{W}^{2,4}
, and assume that
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}\in C^{2}([-1,1])
and
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Then
\mathcal{S}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{3}
, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\big(1+\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(L)\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Bigg(\Big\|\bigg(\frac{f}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)},\frac{g}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\bigg)\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Big\|\bigg(\frac{m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[\phi,\psi](f,g)}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)},\frac{m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[\phi,\psi](f,g)}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\bigg)\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\Bigg),

where
\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(\cdot)
denotes the indicator function of the set
\{0\}
.

Proof.

Abbreviating again
(u,v)=\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)
, we can move along the lines from the proof of Proposition
4.2
and show that
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{3}
in combination with the estimate

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\Bigg(\Big\|\bigg(\frac{f}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)},\frac{g}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\bigg)\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+\Big\|\bigg(\frac{m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)},\frac{m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\bigg)\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big\|\frac{m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\Bigg).

In the following, we will show that the last summand on the right-hand side can be bounded in terms of the first two. To do so, we only have to take a closer look at the case
L=0
. Indeed, if
L=\infty
, this term clearly vanishes, and for
L\in(0,\infty)
, we make use of the boundary condition
Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\beta v-u
a.e. on
\Gamma
to find that

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}=\frac{1}{L}\Big\|\frac{\beta v-u}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big)

in view of (
4.7
). Lastly, if
L=0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}\bigg(\frac{m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\bigg)=\frac{m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)^{2}}+\frac{m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)^{2}}-\frac{m_{\Omega}(\phi)m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)^{2}}\quad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma.

For the first term, we use the trace theorem together with the Sobolev inequality and find

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Next, for the second term, we argue as in the beginning of the proof of Theorem
4.2
, in particular as for (
4.9
) and (
4.10
). To be precise, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\Big\|\frac{f}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\Big\|\frac{m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\Big\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\Big).

Finally, for the last term, we combine again the trace theorem with the Sobolev inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\psi\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus, from the estimates above and (
4.7
) we conclude (
4.4
), which finishes the proof.
∎

Next, we aim to generalize the result from Proposition
4.2
to the case of
L^{p}
-regularity theory.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be of class
C^{2}
, let
p\in[2,\infty)
and consider a pair
(f,g)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L}\cap\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Further, assume that
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{W}^{2,4}
such that
(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[\phi,\psi](f,g),\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[\phi,\psi](f,g))\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,p}}\\
&\quad\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[\phi,\psi](f,g),\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[\phi,\psi](f,g))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).\end{split}

(4.23)

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
23
, Proposition A.1]
for the case of constant mobility functions. As we already know from Proposition
4.2
that
(u,v)\coloneqq\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
, it holds that

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\Big(f+\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e. in~}\Omega,

(4.24)

\displaystyle L\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}(\beta v-u)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e. on~}\Gamma,

(4.25)

as well as

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v=\frac{1}{m_{\Gamma}(\psi)}\Big(g-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u+\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\Big)\qquad\text{a.e. on~}\Gamma.

(4.26)

As in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we consider the cases
L=0
,
L\in(0,\infty)
and
L=\infty
separately.

Case
L=0
.
Since the boundary
\Gamma
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Sobolev’s embedding theorem implies that

\displaystyle u|_{\Gamma}=\beta v\in W^{t,p}\qquad\text{with~}t=\frac{5}{2}+\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d}{2}.

As by assumption
f,\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we may apply elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.2]
) to deduce

\displaystyle u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\quad\text{with}\quad s=\min\Big\{2,\frac{5}{2}+\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}\Big\}\geq 1+\frac{2}{p}

together with the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{W^{t,p}(\Gamma)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{L^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).

This readily yields

\displaystyle\nabla u\in W^{s-1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{\frac{2}{p},p}(\Omega).

Then, since
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p}
is positive and not an integer, the trace theorem implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in W^{\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Gamma).

Consequently, the assumptions
g,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
allow us to apply regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Lemma B.1]
) which yields that
v\in W^{2,p}(\Gamma)
and the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Gamma)}&\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).\end{split}

(4.27)

This, in turn, entails

\displaystyle u|_{\Gamma}=\beta v\in W^{2,p}(\Gamma).

Therefore, by the means of elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.2]
), we find that
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}&\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).\end{split}

(4.28)

Combining (
4.27
) and (
4.28
), we eventually obtain (
4.23
), which finishes the proof in the case
L=0
.

Case
L\in(0,\infty)
.
Since
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, the trace theorem as well as Sobolev’s embedding theorem yield that

\displaystyle u\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow W^{t,p}(\Gamma)\quad\text{with}\quad t=2+\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d}{2}.

Then, since
\phi\in W^{2,4}(\Omega)
, the trace theorem and the Sobolev embedding theorem further imply that

\displaystyle\nabla\phi\in W^{1,4}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{\frac{3}{4},4}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Gamma).

Consequently, recalling that the mobility function
m_{\Omega}\in C^{1}([-1,1])
satisfies (
2.3
), we readily deduce that
\frac{1}{m_{\Omega}(\phi)}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma)
. As
v\in H^{2}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow W^{t,p}(\Gamma)
, we find that

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\frac{1}{Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)}(\beta v-u)\in W^{t,p}(\Gamma).

Then, since
g,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
, we infer from regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Lemma B.1]
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Gamma)}&\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{W^{t,p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).\end{split}

(4.29)

Moreover, exploiting again
f,\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.2]
) provides that

\displaystyle u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)\quad\text{with}\quad s=\min\Big\{2,3+\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}\Big\}\geq 1+\frac{2}{p}

together with the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{W^{t,p}(\Gamma)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).

Then, since
1+\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{p}=1+\frac{1}{p}
is positive and not an integer, the trace theorem shows that

\displaystyle u|_{\Gamma}\in W^{1+\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma),

which entails

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\frac{1}{Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)}(\beta v-u)\in W^{1+\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma).

Finally, using once more elliptic regularity theory for Poisson’s equation with an inhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
5
, Theorem A.2]
), we conclude that
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
along with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}&\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla m_{\Omega}(\phi)\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{W^{1+\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big).\end{split}

(4.30)

Combining the estimates (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) immediately yields (
4.23
).

Case

.
In this case, both the Poisson–Neumann problem (

)-(

) and regularity theory for the Laplace–Beltrami equation (see, e.g.,

∎

Proof of Theorem

The proof is divided into several steps. We start with some basic estimates on a weak solution.

Properties of weak solutions.

for some constant depending on

\left|\Omega\right|
and
\left|\Gamma\right|

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[\phi,\psi](\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)+\beta\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu,\theta\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~in~}\Omega\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[\phi,\psi](\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)+\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu,\theta\right)

Thus, exploiting (

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi,\psi](\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\|_{L}\leq C\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L},

from which we deduce with (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L})^{\prime}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq C.

Next, analyzing the proof of
[

reveals the estimate

\displaystyle\left|\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu,\theta\right)\right|\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L}+\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big),

(5.7)

where

\{0\}
.

[
28

or
[
13
, p. 908]
). Then, noticing that

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi+F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\mu^{\ast}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~in~}\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle=\theta^{\ast}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma,

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle=\alpha\psi-\phi

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma,

almost everywhere in
(0,\infty)
, where

[
15

together with the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
, respectively, as well as (
5.1
) and (

\displaystyle\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,p}}+\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi),G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L}+\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)

(0,\infty)
for all
2\leq p<\infty
. To control the normal derivative
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi
appearing on the right-hand side, we recall that by the trace theorem

\displaystyle\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,p}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L}\big).

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{t}^{t+1}\|(\phi(s),\psi(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq C,

\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k}=(\partial_{t}\phi_{1})^{k}
and
\partial_{t}\nabla\phi_{1}^{k}=\nabla\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k}
a.e. in
Q
as well as
\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k}=(\partial_{t}\psi_{1})^{k}
and
\partial_{t}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{1}^{k}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k}
a.e. on
\Sigma
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\phi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{4}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)),

(5.21)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\psi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{4}(0,T;H^{2}(\Gamma)),

(5.22)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle\rightarrow(\partial_{t}\phi_{1},\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{2}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L})^{\prime}).

(5.23)

In particular, along a non-relabeled subsequence, as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\phi_{1},\quad\nabla\phi_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\nabla\phi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e. in~}\Omega\times(0,T),

(5.24)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\psi_{1},\quad\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e. on~}\Gamma\times(0,T).

(5.25)

Additionally, we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\phi_{1}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}&\leq\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))},\\
\|\psi_{1}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))}&\leq\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Gamma))},\end{split}

(5.26)

as well as

\displaystyle\left|\phi_{k}\right|\leq 1\quad\text{a.e.~in~}Q,\qquad\left|\psi_{k}\right|\leq 1\quad\text{a.e.~on~}\Sigma

(5.27)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We further point out that in light of the convergences (
5.21
)-(
5.22
) we infer the existence of
k_{\ast}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})}\leq 1+\|(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})\|_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})}\qquad\text{for all~}k\geq k_{\ast}.

(5.28)

In the following, we assume that
k\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies
k\geq k_{\ast}
. Furthermore, we denote by the letter
C
a generic positive constant whose value may change from line to line, but is independent of the parameter
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We now define, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\coloneqq\mathcal{S}_{L}[\phi_{k},\psi_{k}](\Phi,\Psi).

It readily follows from (
4.3
) and (
5.27
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}\leq C.

(5.29)

Next, in view of (
5.26
) and (
5.29
), an application of (
4.31
) entails that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big),

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq CT+C\int_{0}^{T}\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq C,

(5.30)

due to (
5.28
). Furthermore, employing the estimate (
4
) in combination with (
5.26
) and (
5.29
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|(\nabla\phi_{1}^{k},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\|(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{4}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\big).

Hence, integrating the foregoing inequality in time over
(0,T)
and using Young’s inequality together with (
5.28
) and (
5.30
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,4}}^{\frac{8}{3}}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq CT+C\int_{0}^{T}\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}+\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq C.

(5.31)

Now, we study the convergence properties of the operator
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. To this end, let
(f,g)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L})
. Then, by definition of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{k}
, we know that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\nabla\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Omega}(f,g)\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Gamma}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Omega}(f,g))(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g))(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
, which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\nabla\big(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Omega}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\big)\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\nabla_{\Gamma}\big(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Gamma}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\big)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}\big(\beta(\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\Gamma}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g))\big)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

(5.32)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\big(m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\big)\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\big(m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\big)\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
. Therefore, taking
(\zeta,\xi)=\mathcal{S}_{1}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{k}(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
as a test function in (
5
), we find with (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathcal{S}_{1}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{k}(f,g)\|_{L}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{\min\big\{1,m^{\ast}\big\}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\big(m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\big)\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{\min\big\{1,m^{\ast}\big\}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\big(m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\big)\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{\min\big\{1,m^{\ast}\big\}}\int_{0}^{T}\|m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{\min\big\{1,m^{\ast}\big\}}\int_{0}^{T}\|m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(5.33)

We aim to pass to the limit
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.33
) by applying the dominated convergence theorem. Toward this end, we first notice by (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle\|m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\mathcal{S}(f,g)\|_{L}^{2}\in L^{1}(0,T).

On the other hand, since
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
are Lipschitz continuous on
[-1,1]
, the Sobolev inequality shows that

\displaystyle\|m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{1}^{k}-\phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\psi_{1}^{k}-\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{1}^{k}-\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\psi_{1}^{k}-\psi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

almost everywhere on
(0,T)
. Thus, in light of (
5.21
)-(
5.22
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})-m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|(\phi_{1}^{k}-\phi_{1},\psi_{1}^{k}-\psi_{1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\|\mathcal{S}(f,g)\|_{L}^{2}\rightarrow 0

up to a subsequence
k\rightarrow\infty
almost everywhere on
(0,T)
. Consequently, we can use (
5.33
) and the Lebesgue convergence theorem to conclude that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(f,g)-\mathcal{S}_{1}(f,g)\|_{L}\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{2}(0,T)

up to a subsequence
k\rightarrow\infty
. In particular, for
(f,g)=(\partial_{t}\Phi,\partial_{t}\Psi)
and
(f,g)=(\Phi,\Psi)
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(\partial_{t}\Phi,\partial_{t}\Psi)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1}(\partial_{t}\Phi,\partial_{t}\Psi)\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}),

(5.34)

as well as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Phi,\Psi)\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}),

(5.35)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
, respectively. Furthermore, in view of the uniform estimates (
5.30
) and (
5.31
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\rightharpoonup\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Phi,\Psi)\qquad\text{weakly in~}L^{4}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})

(5.36)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\rightharpoonup\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Phi,\Psi)\qquad\text{weakly in~}L^{\frac{8}{3}}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{2,4}),

(5.37)

respectively, as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Next, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, by interpolation, there exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{2-\varepsilon}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{for all~}f\in H^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{2-\varepsilon}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{\varepsilon}\|g\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{for all~}g\in H^{2}(\Gamma),

see, for instance,
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Thus, combining these two estimates, by Young’s inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(f,g)\|_{L^{\frac{4}{1+\varepsilon}}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2-\varepsilon})}\leq C\|(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}\|(f,g)\|_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})}\end{split}

(5.38)

for all
(f,g)\in L^{4}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})
.
Choosing
(f,g)=\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)-\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Phi,\Psi)\in L^{4}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})
in (
5.38
), the convergences (
5.35
) and (
5.36
) lead to

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Phi,\Psi)\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{\frac{4}{1+\varepsilon}}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2-\varepsilon})

(5.39)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Now, we claim that for any
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T)
it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\big(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\partial_{t}\Phi,\partial_{t}\Psi),(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L,[\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k}],\ast}^{2}\partial_{t}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k},m_{\Omega}(\phi_{1}^{k})\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k},m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(5.40)

To this end, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
, let
c(\cdot,t)
be measurable in
\Omega
,
d(\cdot,t)
be measurable on
\Gamma
, and
(f(\cdot,t),g(\cdot,t))\in\mathcal{V}_{L}^{-1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{2}
such that
(\partial_{t}c(\cdot,t),\partial_{t}d(\cdot,t)),(\partial_{t}f(\cdot,t),\partial_{t}g(\cdot,t))\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. Then, differentiating the weak formulation (
4.2
) that is satisfied by
\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](f,g)
and taking
(\zeta,\xi)=\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}
as a test function, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}f\,\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[c,d](f,g)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}g\,\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[c,d](f,g)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(c)\partial_{t}c\,\big|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[c,d]^{\Omega}(f,g)\big|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(d)\partial_{t}d\,\big|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[c,d](f,g)\big|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}f\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[c,d](f,g)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}g\,\partial_{t}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[c,d](f,g)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

Hence, noting again on (
4.2
), we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(f,g)\|_{L,[c,d],\ast}^{2}&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](f,g)\|_{L,[c,d]}^{2}\\
&=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](f,g),(f,g)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\\
&=\big(\partial_{t}\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](f,g),(f,g)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\big(\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](f,g),(\partial_{t}f,\partial_{t}g)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\\
&=2\big(\mathcal{S}_{L}[c,d](\partial_{t}f,\partial_{t}g),(f,g)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(c)\partial_{t}c\,\big|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Omega}[c,d](f,g)\big|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(d)\partial_{t}d\,\big|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\Gamma}[c,d](f,g)\big|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.41)

As
(c,d)
and
(f,g)
were arbitrary, we can simply take
(c,d)=(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})
and
(f,g)=(\Phi,\Psi)
in (
5.41
) obtaining (
5.40
). Now, we wish to take the limit
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.40
). To this end, we first notice that from the convergences (
5.34
) and (
5.35
) we easily conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\partial_{t}\Phi,\partial_{t}\Psi),(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{T}\big(\mathcal{S}_{1}(\partial_{t}\Phi,\partial_{t}\Psi),(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s

(5.42)

as well as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L,[\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k}],\ast}^{2}\partial_{t}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s&=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi),(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\partial_{t}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Phi,\Psi),(\Phi,\Psi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\partial_{t}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L,[\phi_{1},\psi_{1}],\ast}^{2}\partial_{t}\sigma\;\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(5.43)

respectively. To handle the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
5.40
), we show that there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2},m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\|_{L}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq C.

(5.44)

In fact, by (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|m_{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\nabla\phi_{1}^{k}\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+2\|m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})D^{2}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{1}^{k}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|D^{2}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{1}^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\phi_{1}^{k}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|D^{2}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, applying the estimates (
5.26
) and (
5.29
), we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\nabla\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad\leq C\|\phi_{1}^{k}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}+C\|D^{2}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad\leq C\|\phi_{1}^{k}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{W^{2,4}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}.\end{split}

(5.45)

In a similar manner, we derive the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\big(m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad\leq C\|\psi_{1}^{k}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{W^{2,4}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{4}{3}}.\end{split}

(5.46)

Lastly, we use the trace theorem and the Sobolev inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\beta m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}-m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}^{2}+C\|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma)}^{2}\\
&\quad\leq C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.47)

Thus, in conclusion, combining the estimates (
5.45
)-(
5.47
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2},m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\|_{L}\\
&\quad\leq C\|(\phi_{1}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Phi,\Psi)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,4}}^{\frac{4}{3}},\end{split}

(5.48)

and the desired claim (
5.44
) readily follows in light of the estimates (
5.28
), (
5.30
) and (
5.31
). We then compute

\displaystyle\Big(\big(\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k}\big),\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2},m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\Big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big(\mathcal{S}(\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k}),\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2},m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\Big)_{L}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\mathcal{S}(\partial_{t}\phi_{1}^{k}-\partial_{t}\phi_{1},\partial_{t}\psi_{1}^{k}-\partial_{t}\psi_{1}),\big(m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Omega}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2},m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi_{1}^{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{k}^{\Gamma}(\Phi,\Psi)\right|^{2}\big)\Big)_{L}

almost everywhere on
(0,T)
. Thus, multiplying the above identity by
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T)
and integrating in time over
(0,T)
, we take the limit
k\rightarrow\infty
and obtain using (
5.21
)-(
5.22
) and (
5.44
) that

(5.61)

6.
Propagation of Regularity and Instantaneous Separation Property

In this section, we prove Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.7
regarding the propagation of regularity and the instantaneous separation property.

The proof of Theorem
3.4
is based on deriving suitable estimates for the time difference quotients
(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi,\partial_{t}^{h}\psi)
by exploiting the differential inequality (
5.65
). This argument, however, requires the mobility functions to satisfy higher regularity assumptions, namely,
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}\in C^{2}([-1,1])
. Therefore, the strategy of the proof is as follows. First, under the stronger assumption
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}\in C^{2}([-1,1])
, we prove that the unique weak solution to (
1.1
) satisfies the desired propagation of regularity. Then, in the second step, we return to the original assumption
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}\in C^{1}([-1,1])
, and construct a sequence of smooth approximations of the mobility functions lying in
C^{2}([-1,1])
. This allows us to apply the results from the first step to obtain a corresponding sequence of approximate solutions to (
1.1
), where now the mobility functions are replaced by their respective regularizations. Then, employing the chain-rule formula established in Proposition
A.1
, we derive uniform estimates for the approximate solutions with respect to the approximation parameter. Finally, standard compactness arguments yield the desired result.

a.e. on

. Exploiting the strong convexity of

and

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega,k}(\phi_{k})\left|\nabla\mu_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma,k}(\psi_{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{k}-\mu_{k})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\Big)\\
&\qquad+\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{K}^{2}\\
&\quad\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega,k}^{\prime}(\phi_{k})\partial_{t}\phi_{k}\left|\nabla\mu_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma,k}^{\prime}(\psi_{k})\partial_{t}\psi_{k}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(6.15)

\displaystyle C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{K}^{2}+C\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L}^{2}.

Next, to control the remaining terms on the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega,k}^{\prime}(\phi_{k})\partial_{t}\phi_{k}\left|\nabla\mu_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma,k}^{\prime}(\psi_{k})\partial_{t}\psi_{k}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\right|\\

\displaystyle\|(\mu_{k}-\beta\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu_{k},\theta_{k}\right),\theta_{k}-\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu_{k},\theta_{k}\right))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega,k}^{\prime}(\phi_{k})\partial_{t}\phi_{k}\left|\nabla\mu_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma,k}^{\prime}(\psi_{k})\partial_{t}\psi_{k}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{K}^{2}+C\|(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L}^{\frac{10}{3}}+C\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L}^{4}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{K}^{2}+C\Big(\|(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}+\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L}^{2}\Big)\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L}^{2}.

Thus, in view of (

), we obtain from (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega,k}(\phi_{k})\left|\nabla\mu_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma,k}(\psi_{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{k}-\mu_{k})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k},\partial_{t}\psi_{k})\|_{K}^{2}\\
&\quad\leq C\Big(1+\|(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}+\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L}^{2}\Big)\\
&\qquad\times\Big(\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega,k}(\phi_{k})\left|\nabla\mu_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma,k}(\psi_{k})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{k}-\mu_{k})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\Big)\end{split}

(6.19)

a.e. on

. In light of (

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\tau}\int_{t}^{t+1}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{k}(s),\partial_{t}\psi_{k}(s))\|_{K}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{\tau}.

\displaystyle\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L})}+\|(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;\mathcal{W}^{2,p})}+\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{k}),G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi_{k}))\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;\mathcal{L}^{p})}

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose the assumptions from Theorem
3.4
hold, and consider a global weak solution
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
that satisfies the propagation of regularity. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(\phi(t),\psi(t))+\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=0

for a.e.

, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E(\phi(t),\psi(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\chi(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s=E(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\end{split}

(6.21)

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We start by defining the functional
E_{0}:\mathcal{L}^{2}\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
given by

\displaystyle E_{0}(\zeta,\xi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\|(\zeta,\xi)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}F_{1}(\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}(\xi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,

where
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
are the convex parts of the potentials
F
and
G
in
(A3)
, respectively. Then, the functional
E_{0}
is proper, convex, and lower-semicontinuous (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
). Owing to
[
30
, Lemma 4.1]
in combination with
[
15
, Proposition 5.2]
we deduce that

is absolutely continuous and it holds

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{0}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\big<(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi),(-\Delta\phi+F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi),-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}}

\displaystyle=\big<(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi),(\mu-F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi),\theta-G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi))\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Omega}F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi)\partial_{t}\psi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

almost everywhere in

for any

. Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(\phi,\psi)=\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

(6.22)

almost everywhere in
(\tau,\infty)
. Since
\tau>0
was arbitrary, we readily obtain (
6.20
). Then, integrating (
6.22
) over
(s,t)
for
s,t>\tau
with

, we infer

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E(\phi(t),\psi(t))-E(\phi(s),\psi(s))\\
&\quad=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s+\chi(L)\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(6.23)

It follows from (

The following argument is inspired by the approach developed in

Let

\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{with}\quad\beta m,m\in(-1,1)\quad\text{if~}L\in[0,\infty),

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose that the assumptions from Theorem

Then, the system (

) defines a strongly continuous semigroup
\mathcal{S}^{K,L}:\mathcal{Z}_{m}^{K,L}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{m}^{K,L}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{K,L}(t)(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})=(\phi(t),\psi(t))\qquad\text{for all~}t\geq 0,

where

is the unique global weak solution of (

) subject to the initial datum

. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}^{K,L}\in C(\mathcal{Z}_{m}^{K,L},\mathcal{Z}_{m}^{K,L})

Next, we define the
\omega
-limit set

\displaystyle\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\coloneqq\Bigg\{(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{m}^{K,L}\Bigg|\begin{aligned} &\exists(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{~with~}t_{n}\rightarrow\infty\text{~such that~}\\
&\mathcal{S}^{K,L}_{m}(t_{n})(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\rightarrow(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})\text{~in~}\mathcal{H}^{2}\ \text{as~}n\rightarrow\infty\end{aligned}\Bigg\}.

Let
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathcal{Z}_{m}^{K,L}
and consider the unique global weak solution to (
1.1
) departing from
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
. Then, since for any
\tau>0
it holds that
(\phi,\psi)\in L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;\mathcal{H}^{3})
as well as
(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}([\tau,\infty);\mathcal{H}^{1})
, we find from the Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma that
(\phi,\psi)\in C([t,t+1];\mathcal{H}^{2})

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)\in C([\tau,\infty);\mathcal{H}^{2})

for any

\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})

[

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}(\mathcal{S}^{K,L}(t)(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}),\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}))=0.

(7.2)

Additionally, as

serves as a strict Lyapunov functional for the strongly continuous semigroup

, we observe that every

\mathcal{S}^{K,L}(t)(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})=(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})
for all

\mu_{\infty}
and

(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},\theta_{\infty})
can be regarded as a global weak solution of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{\infty}=\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi_{\infty})\nabla\mu_{\infty})

\displaystyle\text{in~}\Omega\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi_{\infty}+F^{\prime}(\phi_{\infty})

\displaystyle\text{in~}\Omega\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{\infty}=\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi_{\infty})\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\infty})-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi_{\infty})\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{on~}\Gamma\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\theta_{\infty}=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{\infty}+G^{\prime}(\psi_{\infty})+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{on~}\Gamma\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\infty}=\alpha\psi_{\infty}-\phi_{\infty}&\text{if~}K\in(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\infty}=0&\text{if~}K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on~}\Gamma\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lm_{\Omega}(\phi_{\infty})\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\infty}=\beta\theta_{\infty}-\mu_{\infty}&\text{if~}L\in[0,\infty),\\

\displaystyle\text{on~}\Gamma\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\phi_{\infty}|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in~}\Omega,

\displaystyle\psi_{\infty}|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on~}\Gamma.

In light of the regularity properties proven in Theorem
3.4

(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},\theta_{\infty})
is actually a strong solution to the stationary problem

&\quad\leq C\big(\|(\partial_{t}(u_{j}-u_{k}),\partial_{t}(v_{j}-v_{k}))\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K})^{\prime}}+\|(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{3}}\big)\|(u_{j}-u_{k},v_{j}-v_{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{3}}.\end{split}

(A.9)

Now, let
s,t\in[a,b]
be arbitrary with
s\leq t
. We then integrate (
A.9
) with respect to time over
[s,t]
, and obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(t))\left|\nabla(u_{j}-u_{k})(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(t))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}(v_{j}-v_{k})(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}\big(\beta(v_{j}-v_{k})(t)-(u_{j}-u_{k})(t)\big)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(s))\left|\nabla(u_{j}-u_{k})(s)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(s))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}(v_{j}-v_{k})(s)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}\big(\beta(v_{j}-v_{k})(s)-(u_{j}-u_{k})(s)\big)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+C\int_{s}^{t}\|(\partial_{t}(u_{j}-u_{k}),\partial_{t}(v_{j}-v_{k}))\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K})^{\prime}}^{2}+\|(u_{j}-u_{k},v_{j}-v_{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{3}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\qquad+C\int_{s}^{t}\|(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\|(u_{j}-u_{k},v_{j}-v_{k})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

(A.10)

In light of the convergences (
A.2
)-(
A.3
), we can fix
s\in[a,t]
such that
(u_{k}(s),v_{k}(s))\rightarrow(u(s),v(s))
strongly in
\mathcal{H}^{3}
along a non-relabeled subsequence
k\rightarrow\infty
. Recalling (
A.2
)-(
A.5
), we thus deduce that the right-hand side of (
A.10
) tends to zero as
j,k\rightarrow\infty
. As
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
are uniformly positive according to (
2.3
), we infer that
(\nabla u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([a,b];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
(\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([a,b];L^{2}(\Gamma))
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\nabla u

\displaystyle\qquad\text{strongly in~}C([a,b];L^{2}(\Omega)),

(A.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\nabla_{\Gamma}v

\displaystyle\qquad\text{strongly in~}C([a,b];L^{2}(\Gamma))

(A.12)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. In view of the assumption
(u,v)\in C([a,b];\mathcal{L}^{2})
, we readily deduce that
(u,v)\in C([a,b];\mathcal{H}^{1})
.

Let now
s,t\in[a,b]
be arbitrary with
s\leq t
. We then integrate (
A.7
) in time from
s
to
t
and find

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(t))\left|\nabla u_{k}(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(t))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k}(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v_{k}(t)-u_{k}(t))^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(s))\left|\nabla u_{k}(s)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(s))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k}(s)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v_{k}(s)-u_{k}(s))^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{s}^{t}\big<(\partial_{t}u_{k},\partial_{t}v_{k}),(-\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u_{k}),-\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k})+\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u_{k})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K}}\;\mathrm{d}\tau

(A.13)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi)\partial_{t}\psi\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

It is now clear from the convergences (
A.2
)-(
A.5
) and (
A.11
)-(
A.12
) to pass to the limit in all terms except the last one on the right-hand side. Here, we notice that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}\tau-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}\tau\Big|\\
&\quad=\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi(\nabla u_{k}+\nabla u)\cdot(\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u)\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}\tau\Big|\\
&\quad\leq\|m_{\Omega}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(a,b;L^{3}(\Omega))}\|\nabla u_{k}+\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(a,b;L^{6}(\Omega))}\|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u\|_{C([a,b];L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\quad\leq 2\|m_{\Omega}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(a,b;L^{3}(\Omega))}\|u\|_{L^{2}(a,b;H^{3}(\Omega))}\|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u\|_{C([a,b];L^{2}(\Omega))}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow 0\end{split}

as
k\rightarrow\infty
in light of (
A.11
). A similar computation shows that the corresponding term on the boundary also converges. All together, we are now able to pass to the limit
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
A
) and conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(t))\left|\nabla u(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(t))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v(t)-u(t))^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(s))\left|\nabla u(s)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(s))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v(s)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v(s)-u(s))^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{s}^{t}\big<(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v),(-\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla u),-\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}v)+\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u_{k})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K}}\;\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{s}^{t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}^{\prime}(\psi)\partial_{t}\psi\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

By our previous considerations, we readily see that the integrands on the right-hand side belong to
L^{1}(a,b)
, and thus, that the mapping

\displaystyle[a,b]\ni t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi(t))\left|\nabla u(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi(t))\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v(t)\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta v(t)-u(t))^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

is absolutely continuous. It is therefore differentiable almost everywhere on
[a,b]
and its derivative satisfies the formula (
A.1
). This finishes the proof.

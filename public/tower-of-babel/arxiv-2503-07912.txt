Non-homogeneous problem for the fractional wave equation with irregular coefficients and data
Manel Bouguenna, Mohammed Elamine Sebih

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for a non-homogeneous wave equation generated by the fractional Laplacian and involving different kinds of lower order terms. We allow the equation coefficients and data to be of distributional type or less regular, having in mind the Dirac delta function and its powers, and we prove that the problem is well-posed in the sense of the concept of very weak solutions. Moreover, we prove the uniqueness in an appropriate sense and the coherence of the very weak solution concept with classical theory. The results obtained here extend those in [ DGL23 ] .

35L81, 35L05, 35D30, 35A35.

1.
Introduction

For fixed
T>0
, we consider the Cauchy problem:

(1.1)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+D_{g}^{s}u(t,x)+m(x)u(t,x)+b(x)u_{t}(t,x)=f(t,x),~~~(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\end{array}

where
s>0
and
D_{g}^{s}
is the differential operator generated by the fractional Laplacian, defined by

D_{g}^{s}:=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Big(g(x)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Big).

We assume that:

•

g
is positive,

•

m,b
are non-negative,

•

g,m,b,u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathcal{E^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(space of distributions with compact support) and for
t\in[0,T]
,
f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{E^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(i.e.
f
is smooth enough with respect to
t
and irregular with respect to
x
).

We should highlight here that we understand the first and second assumptions in the sense that the regularisations of
g
and
m,b
are positive and non-negative, respectively. Our aim is to prove that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is well posed in the sense of the concept of very weak solutions introduced in
[
GR15
]
by Garetto and Ruzhansky in order to give a net solution to the problem of multiplication that Schwartz theory of distributions is concerned with, see
[
Sch54
]
, and to provide a framework in which partial differential equations involving coefficients and data of low regularity can be rigorously studied. Let us give a brief literature review about this concept of solutions. After the pioneer work of Garetto and Ruzhansky
[
GR15
]
, many researchers started using this notion of solutions for different situations, either for abstract mathematical problems as
[
CRT21
,
CRT22a
,
CRT22b
]
or for physical models as in
[
RT17a
,
RT17b
,
Gar21
]
and
[
MRT19
,
ART19
,
ARST21a
,
ARST21b
,
ARST21c
]
where it is shown that the concept of very weak solutions is very suitable for numerical modelling and in
[
SW22
]
where the question of propagation of coefficients singularities on the very weak solution is studied. More recently, we cite
[
GLO21
,
BLO22
,
RSY22
,
RY22
,
CDRT23
,
RST24
]
.

The novelty in this work lies in the fact that we consider equations that can not be formulated in the classical sense. We employ the concept of very weak solutions
where nonlinear operations make sense for a large collection of singular objects and allows to overcome the problem of the impossibility of multiplication of distributions. Furthermore, the results obtained in this paper extend those of
[
DGL23
]
by considering the problem in higher space dimension, whereas in
[
DGL23
]
it was treated in the real line. Moreover our approach here is different from the one in
[
DGL23
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

To start with, let us define some notions and specific notations that are used throughout this paper.

2.1.
Notation

•

By the notation
f\lesssim g
, we mean that there exists a positive constant
C
, such that
f\leq Cg
independently on
f
and
g
.

•

We also define

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{1}:=\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

\displaystyle:=\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-\Delta)^{s}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

and

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{3}:=\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

for
t\in(0,T]
.

2.2.
Hölder’s inequality

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
r,p,q\geq 1
, such that:
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Assume that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then,
fg\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and we have

(2.1)

\|fg\|_{L^{r}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

2.3.
The fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}
and the fractional Laplacian

Definition 1

(
Fractional Sobolev space
)
.

Given
s>0
, the fractional Sobolev space is defined by

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi<+\infty\big\},

where
\hat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
.

We note that the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}
endowed with the norm

(2.2)

\|f\|_{H^{s}}:=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{for }f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

is a Hilbert space.

Definition 2

(
Fractional Laplacian
)
.

For
s>0
,
(-\Delta)^{s}
denotes the fractional Laplacian defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{2s}(\hat{f}))(x),

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In other words, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
can be viewed as the pseudo-differential operator with the symbol
|\xi|^{2s}
. With this definition and Plancherel Theorem, the fractional Sobolev space can be defined as:

(2.3)

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big\},

moreover, the norm

(2.4)

\|f\|_{H^{s}}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}},

is equivalent to the one defined in (
2.2
).

Remark 2.1
.

We note that the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can also be defined via the Gagliardo norm, however, we choosed this approach, since it is valid for any real
s>0
, unlike the one via Gagliardo norm which is valid only for
s\in(0,1)
. We refer the reader to
[
DPV12
,
Gar18
,
Kwa17
]
for more details and alternative definitions.

In the following proposition we prove some useful properties of the fractional Laplacian.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
s>0
and
f,g\in H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

(1)

(-\Delta)^{s}(f+g)=(-\Delta)^{s}f+(-\Delta)^{s}g
.

(2)

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle f,(-\Delta)^{s}g\rangle_{L^{2}}
.

(3)

For
s_{1},s_{2}>0
such that
s_{1}+s_{2}=s
, we have

(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}f\big)=(-\Delta)^{s_{1}+s_{2}}f.

Proof.

(1)

The first property follows from the linearity of the Fourier transform. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{(-\Delta^{s})(f+g)(\cdot)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=|\xi|^{2s}(\widehat{f(\cdot)+g(\cdot)})(\xi)

\displaystyle=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{f}(\xi)+|\xi|^{2s}\hat{g}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{(-\Delta^{s})(f)(\cdot)}(\xi)+\widehat{(-\Delta^{s})(g)(\cdot)}(\xi).

(2)

By using Plancherel-Parseval identity, we have

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)^{s}f(\cdot),g(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle|\xi|^{2s}\hat{f}(\xi),\hat{g}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2s}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{f}(\xi)|\xi|^{2s}\hat{g}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{f}(\xi),|\xi|^{2s}\hat{g}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle f(\cdot),(-\Delta)^{s}g(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}.

(3)

Again, by Plancherel-Parseval identity and the self-adjointness of the fractional Laplacian (property (2)), we have

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)^{s_{1}+s_{2}}f(\cdot),g(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle|\xi|^{2(s_{1}+s_{2})}\hat{f}(\xi),\hat{g}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2s_{1}+2s_{2}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2s_{1}}\hat{f}(\xi)|\xi|^{2s_{2}}\hat{g}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\langle|\xi|^{2s_{1}}\hat{f}(\xi),|\xi|^{2s_{2}}\hat{g}(\xi)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}f(\cdot),(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}g(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}\big((-\Delta)^{s_{1}}f(\cdot)\big),g(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Thus:
(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}\big((-\Delta)^{s_{1}}f\big)=(-\Delta)^{s_{1}+s_{2}}f
.

∎

The following inequality will be used frequently in this paper. In the literature, it is known as the fractional Sobolev inequality.

Proposition 2.3

(
Fractional Sobolev inequality
, Theorem 1.1.
[
CT04
]
)
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let
d>2s
and
q=\frac{2d}{d-2s}
. Then, the estimate

(2.5)

\|f\|_{L^{q}}^{2}\leq C(d,s)\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

holds for all
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where the constant
C
depends only on the dimension
d
and the order
s
.

2.4.
Fractional Leibniz rule and the Kato-Ponce inequality

For the classical Laplacian, we have the following known identity:

\Delta(uv)=\Delta u\,\,v+2\Delta u\,\,\Delta v+u\,\Delta v.

For the fractional Laplacian with
s\in(0,1)
, we have an analogous identity:

(2.6)

(-\Delta)^{s}(uv)=u(-\Delta)^{s}v+v(-\Delta)^{s}u-I_{s}(u,v),

where the remainder term is given by:

I_{s}(u,v)(x):=C_{d,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))|}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy<\infty.

Proposition 2.4

(
The Kato-Ponce inequality
, Theorem 1.
[
GO14
]
)
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<r<\infty
and
1<p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
satisfying
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
. Given
s>max(0,\frac{d}{r}-d)
or
s\in 2\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C=C(d,s,r,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2})<\infty
, such that for all
f,h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

(2.7)

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}(fh)\|_{{L^{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\big[\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|h\|_{L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}h\|_{L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big].

2.5.
Duhamel’s principle

Duhamel’s principle is the main tool in proving existence and uniqueness of very weak solutions to our considered problem as well as the coherence with classical solutions. For convenience of the reader, we prove the following special version of this principle and refer the reader to
[
ER18
]
for more details and applications.

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem

(2.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+\lambda(x)u_{t}(t,x)+Lu(t,x)=f(t,x),~~~(t,x)\in\left(0,\infty\right[\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\\
u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

for a given function
\lambda
and
L
is a linear partial differential operator acting over the spatial variable.

Theorem 2.5
.

The solution to (
2.8
) is given by

(2.9)

u(t,x)=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}v(t,x;\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
w(t,x)
is the solution to the homogeneous problem

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w_{tt}(t,x)+\lambda(x)w_{t}(t,x)+Lw(t,x)=0,~~~(t,x)\in\left(0,\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
w(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\,\,\,w_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

and
v(t,x;\tau)
solves the auxiliary Cauchy problem

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{tt}(t,x;\tau)+\lambda(x)v_{t}(t,x;\tau)+Lv(t,x;\tau)=0,~~~(t,x)\in\left(\tau,\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
v(\tau,x;\tau)=0,\,\,\,v_{t}(\tau,x;\tau)=f(\tau,x),\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

where
\tau
is a parameter varying over
\left(0,\infty\right)
.

Proof.

Firstly, we apply
\partial_{t}
to
u
in (
2.9
). We get

(2.12)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\partial_{t}w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}v(t,x;\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

and accordingly

(2.13)

\lambda(x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\lambda(x)\partial_{t}w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(x)\partial_{t}v(t,x;\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

where we used the fact that
v(t,x;t)=0
by the imposed initial condition in (
2.11
). We differentiate again (
2.12
) with respect to
t
to get

(2.14)

\partial_{tt}u(t,x)=\partial_{tt}w(t,x)+f(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{tt}v(t,x;\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

where we used that
\partial_{t}v(t,x;t)=f(t,x)
. Now, applying
L
to
u
in (
2.9
) gives

(2.15)

Lu(t,x)=Lw(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}Lv(t,x;\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

By adding (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and (
2.13
) with in mind that
w
and
v
satisfy the equations in (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we get

u_{tt}(t,x)+\lambda(x)u_{t}(t,x)+Lu(t,x)=f(t,x).

It remains to prove that
u
satisfies the initial conditions. Indeed, from (
2.9
) and (
2.12
), we have that
u(0,x)=w(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
and that
u_{t}(0,x)=\partial_{t}w(0,x)=u_{1}(x)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We note that the above statement of Duhamel’s principle can be extended to differential operators of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, if we consider the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{k}u(t,x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}(x)\partial_{t}^{j}u(t,x)+Lu(t,x)=f(t,x),~~~(t,x)\in\left(0,\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\partial_{t}^{j}u(0,x)=u_{j}(x),\,\text{for}\quad j=0,\cdots,k-1,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

then, the solution is given by

u(t,x)=w(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}v(t,\tau;\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
w(t,x)
is the solution to the homogeneous problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{k}w(t,x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}(x)\partial_{t}^{j}w(t,x)+Lw(t,x)=0,~~~(t,x)\in\left(0,\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\partial_{t}^{j}w(0,x)=u_{j}(x),\,\text{for}\quad j=0,\cdots,k-1,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

and
v(t,x;\tau)
solves the auxiliary Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{k}v(t,x;\tau)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\lambda_{j}(x)\partial_{t}^{j}v_{t}(t,x;\tau)+Lv(t,x;\tau)=0,~~~(t,x)\in\left(\tau,\infty\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\partial_{t}^{j}w(\tau,x;\tau)=0,\,\text{for}\quad j=0,\cdots,k-2,\,\,\,\partial_{t}^{k-1}w(\tau,x;\tau)=f(\tau,x),\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

where
\tau\in\left(0,\infty\right)
.

3.
Energy estimates for the classical solution

In this section, we prove energy estimates to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the case when the equation coefficients and the initial data are regular enough in such a way that a classical solution exists. We prove estimates for different classes of regularity of the coefficients
g,m,b,f
and the initial data
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
. These estimates are a key element when proving the existence and uniqueness of a very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) as well as the coherence with classical solutions.

3.1.
Auxiliary result

Before proving energy estimates for the classical solution to (
1.1
), the following auxiliary result for the Cauchy problem

(3.1)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+D_{g}^{s}u(t,x)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}

needs to be proved.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be positive and assume that
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, there is a unique solution
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) that satisfies the estimate

(3.2)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{1}\lesssim\Big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big],

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Moreover, let
d>2s
, then for
g\in L^{\frac{d}{2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{0}\in H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there is a unique solution
u\in C\big([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C^{1}\big([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
to (
3.1
) that satisfies the estimate

(3.3)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{1}\lesssim\Big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big],

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We multiply the equation in (
3.1
) by
u_{t}
, we integrate with respect to the variable
x
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and we take the real part to get

(3.4)

Re\Big(\langle u_{tt}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle D_{g}^{s}u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}\Big)=0.

We have

(3.5)

Re\langle u_{tt}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\langle u_{t}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and by using the self-adjointness of the fractional Laplacian (see Proposition
2.2
), we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle Re\langle D_{g}^{s}u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\langle g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot),g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It follows by substituting (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) into (
3.4
) that

(3.7)

\partial_{t}\Big[\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big]=0,

this leads to the energy conservation law

(3.8)

\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus, we get

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

and similarly

(3.10)

\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Using the assumption that
g
is bounded from below, that is

\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)=c_{0}>0,

we get

(3.11)

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

In order to estimate the solution
u
, we make use of the fundamental theorem of calculus. We have

(3.12)

u(t,x)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}u_{t}(\tau,x)\mathrm{d}\tau.

Thus, by taking the
L^{2}
norm in (
3.12
) and using Minkowski’s integral inequality, together with (
3.9
) and that
t\in[0,T]
, we get

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big].

This completes the proof of the first part of the lemma. Now, let
d>2s
. Arguing as above, we get the same conservation law formula:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}^{2},

where the second term on the right hand side is estimated by using Hölder’s inequality (see Theorem
2.1
) for
1<p,q<\infty
, satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
. Now, if we choose
q=\frac{2d}{d-2s}
and consequently
p=\frac{d}{s}
, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that

(3.15)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}.

Thus

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\big)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}\|(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}^{2},

where we used that
\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}=\|g\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}
. To estimate
u
, we use again the fundamental theorem of calculus to finally get

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big],

ending the proof.
∎

3.2.
Homogeneous equation case

Let us now consider the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the case when the source term
f\equiv 0
. In this case, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be positive and
m,b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-negative. Suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the unique solution
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies the estimate

(3.19)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{1}\lesssim\Big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\Big(1+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big],

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Moreover, if
g\equiv 1
, there is a unique solution
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to (
1.1
), and it satisfies

(3.20)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{1}\lesssim\big(2+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big(1+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big],

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Let
d>2s
. Then, for
g\in\big(L^{\frac{d}{2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\frac{d}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{s,\frac{d}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
,
m\in\big(L^{\frac{d}{2s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\frac{d}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
,
b\in L^{\frac{d}{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
u_{0}\in H^{3s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u_{1}\in H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there is a unique solution
u\in C\big([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C^{1}\big([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
to (
1.1
) that satisfies the estimate

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}+\|b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\big)\bigg[1+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}+\|b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|g\|_{W^{s,\frac{d}{s}}}\bigg]\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2s}}\Big],

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Moreover, if
g\equiv 1
, and
u_{0}\in H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u_{1}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then, the unique solution
u\in C([0,T];H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to (
1.1
) satisfies

(3.22)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{2}\lesssim\Big(1+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big)\Big(1+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}\Big)\Big(1+\|b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big)^{2}\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\Big],

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Multiplying the equation in (
1.1
) by
u_{t}
and integrating with respect to the variable
x
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and taking the real part, we get

(3.23)

\displaystyle Re\Bigg(\langle u_{tt}(t,\cdot),

\displaystyle u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle D_{g}^{s}u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\langle m(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle b(\cdot)u_{t}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}\Bigg)=0.

We easily show that

(3.24)

Re\langle u_{tt}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\langle u_{t}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and

(3.25)

\displaystyle Re\langle D_{g}^{s}u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\langle g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot),g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used the self-adjointness of the fractional Laplacian (see Proposition
2.2
). For the remaining terms in (
3.23
), we proceed as follows

(3.26)

\displaystyle Re\langle m(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\langle m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and

(3.27)

\displaystyle Re\langle b(\cdot)u_{t}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle b^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{t}(t,\cdot),b^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|b^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By substituting (
3.24
),(
3.25
),(
3.26
) and (
3.27
) in (
3.23
) we get

(3.28)

\partial_{t}\Big[\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big]=-\|b^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Let us define the energy function of the system (
1.1
) by

(3.29)

E(t):=\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It follows from (
3.28
) that
\partial_{t}E(t)\leq 0
and consequently that we have a decay of energy, that is:
E(t)\leq E(0)
for all
t\in[0,T]
, where

(3.30)

E(0):=\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By taking into consideration the estimate

(3.31)

\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

it follows that all terms in (
3.29
) satisfy the estimates

(3.32)

\displaystyle\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]^{2},

as well as

(3.33)

\bigg\{\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg\}\lesssim\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]^{2},

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
, where we used the fact that

(3.34)

\Big\{\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},

and that
g
is bounded from below. Now, we need to estimate
u
. To this end, we recall the estimate (
3.2
) in Lemma
3.1
, that is

(3.35)

\displaystyle\bigg\{\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},

and we apply Duhamel’s principle (Theorem
2.5
with
\lambda\equiv 0
) for the problem

(3.36)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+D_{g}^{s}u(t,x)=\delta(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}

where
\delta(t,x):=-m(x)u(t,x)-b(x)u_{t}(t,x)
. The solution to (
3.36
) is given by

(3.37)

u(t,x)=W(t,x)+\int_{0}^{T}V(t,x;\tau)d\tau,

where
W(t,x)
is the solution to the homogeneous problem

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}W_{tt}(t,x)+D_{g}^{s}W(t,x)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
W(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad W_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}

and
V(t,x;\tau)
solves the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V_{tt}(t,x;\tau)+D_{g}^{s}V(t,x;\tau)=0,\,\,\,(t,x)\in\left(\tau,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
V(\tau,x;\tau)=0,~V_{t}(\tau,x;\tau)=\delta(\tau,x).\end{array}\right.

Taking the
L^{2}
-norm in (
3.37
) and by using the above mentioned estimate (
3.35
), we get

(3.38)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot;s)\|_{L^{2}}ds,

where we used Minkowski’s integral inequality.
We have

\|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},

and

\|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot;s)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

since
V_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the estimate (
3.35
). Thus, we get

(3.39)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
To estimate
\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
, the last term in the above inequality, we use the triangle inequality and the estimates

(3.40)

\displaystyle\|m(\cdot)u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big],

resulting from (
3.32
), and similarly

(3.41)

\displaystyle\|b(\cdot)u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big],

resulting from (
3.33
), to get

(3.42)

\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\big(\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big].

The desired estimate for
u
follows by substituting (
3.42
) into (
3.39
). We obtain

(3.43)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big(1+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\big],

where we used that
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda
for
\lambda\geq 1
. Let us now consider the case when
g\equiv 1
. The desired estimate is a direct consequence of (
3.43
) by taking that
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}=1
, for
g\equiv 1
. We get

(3.44)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{1}\lesssim\big(2+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big(1+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\Big].

This ends the proof of the first part of the lemma. Now, let
d>2s
. By repeating the above reasoning we get the same conservation law formula

(3.45)

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}^{2},

where the second and the third terms on the right hand side are estimated by using Hölder’s inequality (see Theorem
2.1
) for
1<p<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
. Now, if we choose
q=\frac{2d}{d-2s}
and consequently
p=\frac{d}{s}
, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that

(3.46)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},

and by using the assumption that
g
is bounded from below, that is

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)=c_{0}>0,

we get

(3.47)

\displaystyle\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}

\displaystyle u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}^{2}+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}^{2}+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}})\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}^{2},

where we used that
\|g^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}=\|g\|_{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}
and the embedding
H^{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To estimate
u
, we recall again the second estimate in Lemma (
3.1
), that is

(3.48)

\bigg\{\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg\}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}},

and we use Duhamel’s principle and we argue similarly as in the first part of the proof, to finally get

(3.49)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau,

for all
t\in[0,T]
, with
\delta(t,x):=-m(x)u(t,x)-b(x)u_{t}(t,x)
. In order to estimate
\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
, we use the triangle inequality to get

(3.50)

\|\delta(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|m(\cdot)u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|b(\cdot)u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}.

To estimate the first term in (
3.50
), we use Hölder’s inequality (Theorem
2.1
) to get

(3.51)

\|m(\cdot)u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|m\|_{L^{p}}\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}},

for
1<p,q<\infty
, satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
, and we choose
q=\frac{2d}{d-2s}
and consequently
p=\frac{d}{s}
, in order to get (from Proposition
2.3
)

(3.52)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

and thus

(3.53)

\|m(\cdot)u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Using the estimate (
3.47
), we arrive at

(3.54)

\displaystyle\|m(\cdot)u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big[\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}\Big].

For the second term in (
3.50
), we argue as above, to get

(3.55)

\|b(\cdot)u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}},

for all
t\in[0,T]
. We need now to estimate
\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
. For this, we note that if
u
solves the Cauchy problem

(3.56)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+D_{g}^{s}u(t,x)+m(x)u(t,x)+b(x)u_{t}(t,x)=0,\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}

then
u_{t}
solves

(3.57)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}(u_{t})_{tt}(t,x)+D_{g}^{s}u_{t}(t,x)+m(x)u_{t}(t,x)+b(x)(u_{t})_{t}(t,x)=0,\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad(u_{t})_{t}(0,x)=-D_{g}^{s}u_{0}(x)-m(x)u_{0}(x)-b(x)u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}

Thanks to (
3.53
) and (
3.55
) one has

(3.58)

\|m(\cdot)u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|b(\cdot)u_{1}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}.

The estimate for
\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
follows by using (
3.48
) applied to the problem (
3.57
), to get

(3.59)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{t}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{tt}(0,.)\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\big(g(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}\big)\|_{L^{2}}+\|m(\cdot)u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|b(\cdot)u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|g\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2s}}.

To estimate the term
\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\big(g(\cdot)(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot)\big)\|_{L^{2}}
in (
3.59
), we first use the Kato-Ponce inequality (see Proposition
2.4
) applied for
r=2
. We get

(3.60)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\big(g(\cdot)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot)\big)\|_{L^{2}}

In the following proposition, we prove that distributional coefficients are naturally moderate.

Proposition 4.1
.

(1)

Let
f\in\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
be regularisation of
f
obtained via convolution with a mollifying net
\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
. Then, the net
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
. That is, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
such that

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon^{-N}.

(2)

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the net
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

(1) Fix
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
f\in\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By the structure theorems for distributions, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
and compactly supported functions
f_{\alpha}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

T=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\partial^{\alpha}f_{\alpha},

where
|\alpha|
is the length of the multi-index
\alpha
. The convolution of
f
with a mollifying net
\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
yields

(4.6)

f\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\partial^{\alpha}f_{\alpha}\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}f_{\alpha}\ast\partial^{\alpha}\psi_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\varepsilon^{-d-|\alpha|}f_{\alpha}\ast\partial^{\alpha}\psi(x/\varepsilon).

Taking the
L^{p}
norm in (
4.6
) gives

(4.7)

\|f\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\varepsilon^{-d-|\alpha|}\|f_{\alpha}\ast\partial^{\alpha}\psi(x/\varepsilon)\|_{L^{p}}.

Since
f_{\alpha}
and
\psi
are compactly supported then, Young’s inequality applies for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
, provided that
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}+1
. That is

\|f_{\alpha}\ast\partial^{\alpha}\psi(x/\varepsilon)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\partial^{\alpha}\psi(x/\varepsilon)\|_{L^{p_{2}}}<\infty.

It follows from (
4.7
) that
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate. This completes the proof of the first statement.

(2) In order to prove the second statement of the proposition, we use Young’s convolution inequality. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We have:

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}=\|f\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}},

such that
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}+1
. If we choose
p_{1}=p
and consequently
p_{2}=1
, we get

\displaystyle\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}\varepsilon^{-d}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-d},

where we used that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varepsilon^{-1}\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}x)dx

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}.

The integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}x)dx<\infty
, since
\psi
is compactly supported.
∎

5.
Coherence with classical theory

Now, let
g\equiv 1
and the coefficients
m,b,f
, and the Cauchy data
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
be smooth enough in such a way that a classical solution to

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{s}u(t,x)+m(x)u(t,x)+b(x)u_{t}(t,x)=f(t,x),\\
\hfill(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

exists (in the sense of Theorem
3.4
). For these coefficients and initial data, the concept of very weak solutions applies (according to Proposition
4.1
) and a very weak solution exists. The question to be answered here is: does the very weak solution recapture the classical one?

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\psi
be a Friedrichs-mollifier. Assume
m,b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-negative,
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in such a way that a classical solution to (
5.1
) exists. Then, for any regularising families
(m_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(m\ast\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(b\ast\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
for the equation coefficients, satisfying

(5.2)

\lVert m_{\varepsilon}-m\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert b_{\varepsilon}-b\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0,

and any regularising families
(f_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(f\ast\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
for the source term, and any regularising families
(u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(u_{0}\ast\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(u_{1,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(u_{1}\ast\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
for the initial data, the net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
converges to the classical solution of the Cauchy problem (
5.1
) in
L^{2}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be the very weak solution given by Theorem
4.4
and
u
the classical one, as in Theorem
3.4
. The classical solution satisfies

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{s}u(t,x)+m(x)u(t,x)+b(x)u_{t}(t,x)=f(t,x),\\
\hfill(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

and
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
solves

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+m_{\varepsilon}(x)u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+b_{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\\
\hfill(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=u_{0,\varepsilon}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=u_{1,\varepsilon}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

By denoting
U_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-u(t,x)
, then
U_{\varepsilon}
solves the Cauchy problem

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}U_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{s}U_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+m_{\varepsilon}(x)U_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+b_{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\\

Thanks to Duhamel’s principle,

(5.7)

where

(5.8)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}W_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{s}W_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+m_{\varepsilon}(x)W_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+b_{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{t}W_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=0,\\
W_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=(u_{0,\varepsilon}-u_{0})(x),\quad\partial_{t}W_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=(u_{1,\varepsilon}-u_{1})(x),\end{array}

for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

(5.9)

\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}V_{\varepsilon}(t,x;\tau)+(-\Delta)^{s}V_{\varepsilon}(t,x;\tau)+m_{\varepsilon}(x)V_{\varepsilon}(t,x;\tau)+b_{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}(t,x;\tau)=0,\\

and

4.6

\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot;\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau,

\|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Big(2+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\Big(1+\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\bigg[\|u_{0,\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u_{1,\varepsilon}-u_{1,}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg],

In the case when a classical solution exists in the sense of Theorem

with

Theorem 5.2

Let

(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be the very weak solution given by Theorem

and

the classical one, as in Theorem

(5.12)

\|(u_{\varepsilon}-u)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot;\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau,

where

\displaystyle\|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Big(1+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(1+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}\Big)\Big(1+\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg[\|u_{0,\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{H^{2s}}+\|u_{1,\varepsilon}-u_{1,}\|_{H^{s}}\bigg],

and

\|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot;\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Big(1+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big)\Big(1+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}\Big)\Big(1+\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\Big)^{2}\bigg[\|\rho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg],

3.22

\rho_{\varepsilon}

(5.13)

\|\rho_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\cdot)-f(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+\|m_{\varepsilon}-m\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}+\|b_{\varepsilon}-b\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}.

2.1

First, one observes that
\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}<\infty

\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2s}}}<\infty

\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{s}}}<\infty

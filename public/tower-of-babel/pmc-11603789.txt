The association between interleukin-8 gene polymorphism and the risk of sepsis in older adults
Authors: Tingting Han, Tianyu Liang, Renyang Liu, Yinyin Quan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrevious studies on the relationship between interleukin-8 (IL-8) rs4073 polymorphism and sepsis susceptibility have covered contradictory results. Our purpose is to investigate whether this polymorphism confers a risk for sepsis using both experimental methods and meta-analysis.MethodsIn the current study, the authoritative databases including PubMed and Embase were carefully searched and reviewed. The search period spanned from the inception of each database until June 2024. Odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) were adopted to compute the association strength. A total of 480 patients and 840 healthy individuals in older adults were selected. PCR-RFLP was applied to investigate the gene polymorphism including genotype frequency and allele frequency.ResultsIn summary, no significant association was found by allele contrast (T vs. A: OR = 1.11, 95% CI = 0.75–1.62, P = 0.606), homozygote comparison (TT vs. AA: OR = 1.35, 95% CI = 0.57–3.20, P = 0.498), heterozygote comparison (AT vs. AA: OR = 1.38, 95% CI = 0.72–2.65, P = 0.668), recessive genetic model (TT vs. AA/TA: OR = 0.96, 95% CI = 0.64–1.43, P = 0.834), or dominant genetic model (TT/TA vs. AA: OR = 1.37, 95% CI = 0.68–2.79, P = 0.380). Analysis of the IL-8 gene polymorphism revealed three genotypes: AA, TA, and TT. We found that TT genotype and T allele were significantly associated with sepsis risk in older adults (all p < 0.05).ConclusionThe meta-analysis indicates no positive findings. However, the present experimental study has demonstrated that the IL-8 rs4073 polymorphism contributes increased risk to sepsis for older adults from Zhejiang Province, China. Future studies are urgently needed to confirm our conclusion.

Introduction

DNA plays a central role in determining susceptibility to various diseases through genetic variations. DNA also can influence wound healing and infection risks like sepsis. Polymorphisms in immune-regulating genes affect inflammatory responses, while genetic factors can also contribute to conditions like osteoporosis [1, 2]. Similarly, DNA variations impact heart diseases by regulating cholesterol metabolism, vascular health, autoimmune and other metabolic disorders [3].

Sepsis is a systemic inflammatory response syndrome that occurs after infection with various pathogenic bacteria. It can further develop into septic shock or multiple organ dysfunction syndromes [4]. According to the latest Morbidity and mortality report on sepsis published in recent literature, there were 48.9 million new cases and 11 million deaths up to the current date [5]. Sepsis now kills more people than the combination of prostate cancer, breast cancer, and AIDS [6]. In recent years, sepsis has become a major focus of critical medical research worldwide [4]. Consequently, it imposes significant economic burdens on public health and healthcare systems [7–9].

Traditional evidence has indicated that primary diseases, infections, and diabetes are risk factors for sepsis [10–12], but these factors can only partially explain its etiology. Numerous studies have shown that the inflammatory factor network and sepsis work simultaneously [13–15], , making it a hot topic for research, in domestic and international research. Among these pro-inflammatory cytokines, interleukin-8 (IL-8) is a principal factor in neutrophilic chemotaxis. It plays a prominent role in modulating various inflammatory responses and participates in the pathophysiological processes of different diseases, including hepatitis B infection, liver cirrhosis, pancreatitis, Alzheimer’s disease, gout, malaria, and others [16–18]. Additionally, IL-8 is closely related to the development of multiple solid tumors [19–21]. Literature has shown that peripheral blood levels of IL-8 are significantly higher in individuals with sepsis compared to the normal population [22, 23]. Recent reports also suggest that polymorphisms in the IL-8 gene promoter may be related to sepsis [24, 25]. Genetic biomarkers play a significant role in sepsis by influencing individual susceptibility, severity, and outcomes. Variations in genes regulating immune and inflammatory responses, such as IL-8, TNF-α, and TLR4, affect cytokine production and pathogen recognition. These biomarkers help identify at-risk individuals, predict sepsis progression, and guide personalized treatments by targeting specific immune pathways, ultimately improving patient management and survival. Genetic biomarkers, like variations in the elastin gene and interleukin-8 (IL-8) gene, are essential in understanding disease risks and responses. The IL-8 gene regulates inflammation, linking it to sepsis and wound healing, while the elastin gene is vital for tissue elasticity, influencing ligament injuries and vascular health in heart diseases [26–28]. Since IL-8 may play a role in sepsis, mutations in this gene that influence IL-8 output could serve as additional risk factors. Partial studies have suggested a relationship, while others hold a controversial view. Therefore, we aimed to explore whether this polymorphism confers a risk for sepsis using both experimental methods and meta-analysis.

Materials and methods

Data collection

The world-famous authoritative database including PubMed and Embase were carefully searched and reviewed. The search period spanned from the inception of each database until June 2024. The search Mesh in order was as follows: “polymorphism”, “sepsis,” and “Interleukin-8”. To ensure sufficient statistical power for the study, a preliminary sample size estimation was conducted. The number of controls was set higher than that of cases to improve statistical power and the reliability of the analysis. Cases and controls were matched based on demographic characteristics (such as age and gender), and statistical methods were used during the analysis phase to control for potential confounding variables.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria and methodological quality assessment

The inclusion and exclusion criteria are the same as those of previous published meta-analyses, following some general classical criteria. The included study must be a case-control study and provide genotype data that can be used to calculate the effect size. If the genotype frequency of the case group and the control group is still not available through literature or mail, the literature will be excluded. The opposite of an inclusion criterion is an exclusion criterion. All the data were extracted, read, analyzed and sorted out by the authors, and then the quality of every study was assessed according to Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) standards.

Study subjects

Sepsis patients in older adults treated and enrolled from May 2012 to January 2024 were included in the experimental group. We set extremely strict inclusion criteria: (1) new cases of sepsis confirmed by the Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock (Sepsis-3); and (2) patients who had not received any therapy. The exclusion criteria were: (1) complications with other diseases, and (2) complications with chronic or severe metabolic diseases [29]. A total of 480 patients and 840 healthy individuals were selected. This study was authorized by the Ethics Committee. All subjects in both groups were residents of Zhejiang Province, with no familial relationships between them. All subjects and their families were informed. The study protocol was in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Gene polymorphism analysis

Approximately 3 mL of venous blood was collected. We use professional DNA kits for DNA extraction and purification. The work was performed with the assistance of specialized biological companies, within proper protocol. 25ul PCR amplification reaction system was used, including 2×Taq PCR MasterMix II 12.5ul, upstream and downstream primers 2ul, DNA template 3ul, ddH2O 7.5ul. The liquid mixing and sample addition processes were all operated on ice. After sample addition, close the EP tube orifice, centrifuge for a few seconds, fully oscillate and mix, and avoid liquid adsorption on the EP tube wall to affect subsequent PCR amplification reaction. And 15ul restriction enzyme digestion reaction system includes 7ul PCR amplification products, 0.5ul restriction endonuclide, 1.5ul rCutSmart Buffer, 6ul ddH2O. The PCR reaction conditions and enzyme digestion reaction of IL-8 rs4073 loci refer to the previously published literature [30–32]. Gene polymorphism analysis was carried out using the ABI3730XL and GeneMapper 4.1, with sequencing for further verification.

Statistical analysis

OR, 95% CI, Q-statistic and I² statistic were employed to count the effect power and assess the degree of heterogeneity [14]. Five genetic models were applied as reported in previous literatures. The fixed-effects and random-effects models were based on the degree of heterogeneity. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias assessment followed methodologies from previous meta-analyses. Stata 15.0 was applied for meta-analysis and SPSS 19.0 was applied for experimental study. The χ² test and t-test were applied. The HWE (Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium) balance was used to examine the single nucleotide polymorphisms in all studies to see if the distribution of genotypes in the control group was consistent with the expected distribution, just like studied for ACTN3’s R577X single nucleotide polymorphism of allele distribution [33]. In our case, the P < 0.05 was achieved which is considered as significant calculation.

Evaluation of between-study heterogeneity

Table 3 showed the heterogeneity from the five genetic models. Meta-regression demonstrated that sample size was an important factor contributing significantly to the heterogeneity of the current results. The study published by Zhao et al. contributed large amount of heterogeneity.

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

Sensitivity analysis confirmed the stability of our results (Fig. 7). No obvious asymmetry was displayed in the funnel plot, and Egger’s test further indicated the reliability of the findings (P = 0.560) (Fig. 8).

Study subjects and gene polymorphism analysis

Table 4 outlines key information about the study subjects and this data indicates the negative results. We did not find any significant association between the sepsis group and control group in gender, age, BMI, DM history, hypertension history, drinking history or smoking history (P > 0.05). The genotype and allele distributions of IL-8 gene polymorphism in 480 sepsis patients and 840 healthy controls were determined using PCR-RFLP methods. The genotype and allele distributions of IL-8 gene polymorphism were shown in Table 5. Furthermore, the genotype frequency for IL-8 gene polymorphism was consistent with HWE in the healthy control. Analysis of the IL-8 gene polymorphism revealed three genotypes: AA, TA, and TT. We found that TT genotype and T allele were risk factors and they contributed increased risk to sepsis (all p < 0.05, Table 5). Specifically, people with the TT genotype had a 1.84 times greater risk of developing sepsis than those with the AA genotype (p < 0.05, Table 5). And people with the T allele had a 1.51 times greater risk of developing sepsis than those with the A allele (p < 0.05, Table 5).

Discussion

Sepsis is not controlled by a single gene, nor is its occurrence and development confined to a single stage. Currently, most studies focus on investigating the relationship between genetic polymorphisms and disease susceptibility. This approach aims to deepen our understanding of sepsis by considering individual differences. Sepsis is caused by infection, but its occurrence and development follow a distinct pathophysiological process in which body immunity plays a crucial role. IL-8 is a neutrophil chemokine that significantly impacts the regulation of inflammatory responses. Its expression is a complex process involving various steps, including transcriptional and post-transcriptional processes. The proximal nuclear factors NF-κB and NF-IL-6 sites, along with the distal AP-1 binding sites, specifically bind to the IL-8 promoter. The concentration of various transcription factors in the nucleus, their affinity with the IL-8 promoter, and the synergistic effects between them collectively affects IL-8 transcription and expression. The rs4073 gene polymorphism can impact on the transcriptional activity. The IL-8 gene is located on chromosome 4q13-q21 and consists of four exons and three introns. Notably, the 5’ flanking region contains an SNP at rs4073, intron 1 contains rs2227306, and the 3’ end of exon 4 contains rs1126647.

IL-8 gene polymorphism confers different susceptibilities to various diseases. Numerous literatures have reported that this genetic polymorphism is a risk factor for chronic periodontitis [36, 37], Helicobacter pylori-associated gastric disease, bacterial meningitis, oral cancer, and breast cancer [38–47]. Amaya et al. demonstrated that people with the homozygous TT genotype expressed higher levels of IL-8 protein [48]. Li et al. discovered that the AA genotype was closely related to an increased risk of acute pancreatitis [49]. Belopolskaya et al. suggested that the A allele of the IL-8 rs4073 locus might be a protective gene for sepsis [50]. According to reports, the T allele is involved in the inflammatory response by reducing white blood cell aggregation and the ability to clear pathogens [43, 51, 52].

In the present research, exploring the possible mechanism by which this polymorphism in IL-8 can induce sepsis directly or indirectly is undoubtedly very important. A functional SNP, -251 A/T, in the promoter region of IL-8 gene was reported to influence the expression of IL-8 [53]. We think the underlying mechanism is that IL-8–251 A/T modifies the production of IL-8, which can influence sepsis occurrence and development. IL-8 is a pro-inflammatory cytokine, which possesses a key role in the pathophysiology of sepsis [54], through the activation and chemotaxis of neutrophils [55, 56]. Inflammation [57] is one of the most important clinical manifestations of sepsis [58, 59].

A study from Italy revealed that the TA genotype was associated with the severity of sepsis in premature infants [60]. However, a study by Hu et al. demonstrated no significant association [34]. Conversely, Zhao et al., in a 2020 publication, suggested that this polymorphism was significantly associated with sepsis susceptibility, with the TT genotype and T allele being risk factors for sepsis occurrence and development [24]. The present meta-analysis shows a negative result. Interestingly, Zhao et al. published a study from China demonstrating a positive result [24]. This discrepancy probably came from the minimal sample size, which included fewer than 100 subjects. Small-study bias is not uncommon in meta-analyses. In the present study, we used meta-analysis tools to address the bias of small sample sizes. Several limitations should be noted. Firstly, future meta-analyses should include more eligible studies, particularly those from Asia, Africa, and other ethnicities. Secondly, various confounding factors were not considered due to a lack of information.

The meta-analysis indicates no positive findings. However, the present experimental study has demonstrated that the IL-8 rs4073 polymorphism contributes increased risk to sepsis for individuals from Zhejiang Province, China. Future studies are urgently needed to confirm our conclusion.

Conclusion

The findings of this study suggest that the IL-8 rs4073 polymorphism may be associated with an increased risk of sepsis in older adults in Zhejiang Province, China. In this study total 480 patients and 840 healthy individuals in older adults were selected. PCR-RFLP was applied to investigate the gene polymorphism including genotype frequency and allele frequency. Although our meta-analysis did not reveal a significant association, the experimental results indicate that individuals with the TT genotype and T allele may have a higher susceptibility to sepsis. The results show, no significant association was found by allele contrast (T vs. A: OR = 1.11, 95% CI = 0.75–1.62, P = 0.606), homozygote comparison (TT vs. AA: OR = 1.35, 95% CI = 0.57–3.20, P = 0.498), heterozygote comparison (AT vs. AA: OR = 1.38, 95% CI = 0.72–2.65, P = 0.668), recessive genetic model (TT vs. AA/TA: OR = 0.96, 95% CI = 0.64–1.43, P = 0.834), or dominant genetic model (TT/TA vs. AA: OR = 1.37, 95% CI = 0.68–2.79, P = 0.380). Analysis of the IL-8 gene polymorphism revealed three genotypes: AA, TA, and TT. We found that TT genotype and T allele were significantly associated with sepsis risk in older adults (all p < 0.05). Future studies with larger sample sizes and diverse populations are warranted to further understand the potential role of IL-8 polymorphisms in sepsis risk.

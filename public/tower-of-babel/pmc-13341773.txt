A cross-sectional study on the quality of IgA nephropathy videos on short video platforms
Authors: Jiaying Fu, Jianghui Li, Yun Tian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIgA nephropathy, as a common chronic kidney disease, relies on accurate health knowledge for long-term patient management. Short videos have become a significant channel for the public to access medical information, but their overall quality varies widely, and research in the field of IgA nephropathy remains largely unexplored. A cross-sectional study design was employed to systematically retrieve videos from both platforms using “IgA nephropathy” as the keyword. Based on the inclusion criteria, 186 short videos were obtained. Video characteristics, uploader categories, and interaction metrics were collected. Quality assessment was performed using the global quality scale (GQS), modified DISCERN (mDISCERN), Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) benchmark criteria, and the Video Information and Quality Index (VIQI). The overall quality of videos across both platforms was moderate. Videos published by healthcare personnel scored significantly higher than those from individual users on the GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA criteria. Monologue-style videos demonstrated superior quality compared to other expression forms. TikTok significantly outperformed Bilibili in terms of VIQI-1/2 scores and also garnered higher levels of user interaction. Moderate to strong positive correlations were observed between interaction metrics and both GQS and VIQI scores, whereas no significant correlation was found with mDISCERN or JAMA scores. The primary focus of video content was on treatment and prognosis, while information on etiology and financial burden was notably insufficient. There are platform-specific differences in the visual presentation and information accuracy of IgA nephropathy-related short videos. Insufficient professional involvement and the absence of key information may limit the public’s comprehensive understanding of the disease. Enhancing healthcare personnel participation, improving the quality of visual presentation, supplementing core medical information, and optimizing platform recommendation mechanisms will contribute to increasing the effectiveness of short videos in public health education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-52584-7.Keywords: IgA nephropathy, Social media, Information quality, TikTok, BilibiliSubject terms: Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Nephrology

Introduction

IgA nephropathy is one of the most common primary glomerular diseases worldwide, characterized by insidious progression and irreversible kidney damage1. Epidemiological data suggest that there are approximately 4.59 million adult patients with IgA nephropathy in China, with a 10-year renal survival rate ranging between 60% and 90%2,3. Furthermore, approximately 30–40% of patients progress to end-stage renal disease within 20 years of diagnosis. The long-term course of the disease not only imposes a significant medical burden but also exerts continuous pressure on patients’ quality of life, family financial expenditure, and the public health system4,5. Consequently, there is a clear and urgent need for public education regarding the etiology of IgA nephropathy, the risk of complications, lifestyle management, and the importance of follow-up. Effective dissemination of health information is crucial for delaying disease progression, improving treatment adherence, and reducing delays in seeking medical care.

Meanwhile, short video platforms represented by TikTok and Bilibili have become an important channel for the public to access medical information6,7. Through visual storytelling, algorithm-based recommendations, and low-barrier dissemination, short videos can rapidly reach a large audience. In 2023, over 95% of Chinese internet users utilized short video platforms, with monthly active users exceeding 1 billion8. The view counts of health-related content continue to grow, establishing short videos as one of the most influential emerging health communication mediums9. However, existing research also highlights multiple risks associated with short video information, including insufficient professional involvement, inadequate content moderation, the potential prioritization of visual appeal over informational accuracy, and the rapid dissemination of low-quality content driven by algorithms10,11. If these issues manifest in the field of IgA nephropathy—a condition characterized by complex pathology and long management cycles—they may further exacerbate public cognitive biases and adversely affect disease management behaviors12.

Although existing research has focused on the role of short videos in public science communication for conditions such as cancer, depression, and dermatological diseases, studies on the quality of information in the field of kidney diseases, particularly IgA nephropathy, remain extremely limited13–15. More importantly, most current literature only assesses video content quality, with less consideration given to the combined impact of platform differences, uploader categories, presentation formats, and VIQI on quality and dissemination effectiveness. There is also a lack of in-depth analysis of the mechanisms underlying interactive behaviors. Consequently, it remains unclear what the advantages, disadvantages, and potential impacts of different short video platforms are in disseminating information about IgA nephropathy.

Besides, IgA nephropathy presents several disease-specific characteristics that may shape both information needs and user engagement patterns on short-video platforms. Patients are typically diagnosed at a relatively young age, often remain asymptomatic for long periods, and face highly variable long-term renal outcomes, which creates sustained uncertainty regarding prognosis and treatment decisions. In addition, the immunopathological mechanisms and stepwise therapeutic strategies of IgA nephropathy are complex, while disease management relies heavily on long-term adherence, lifestyle modification, and regular follow-up16. These features distinguish IgA nephropathy from many acute or symptom-driven conditions commonly examined in prior short-video studies, and they may influence both the type of information users seek and the kinds of content that receive higher engagement.

This study aims to address a research gap in this field by comparing the content quality of IgA nephropathy-related short videos across different platforms and examining the impact of various indicators on video quality. It seeks to provide evidence-based guidance for enhancing the quality of medical science communication in short videos, optimizing platform dissemination strategies, and strengthening patient education for IgA nephropathy.

Methods

Data search and collection

Videos were searched using the keyword “IgA 肾病“(IgA Nephropathy in Chinese) on TikTok and Bilibili, and the top 100 videos under the default sorting were identified (Data collection period: 2025.11.22-2025.11.26). Previous studies have indicated that the top 100 videos are sufficient to represent the relevant video content in this field across different platforms17,18. To mitigate potential bias introduced by personalized recommendation algorithms, video collection was conducted using newly registered accounts on each platform. Exclusion criteria included: (a) videos unrelated to IgA Nephropathy; (b) repeated videos; and (c) non-Chinese videos. Ultimately, a total of 186 videos were included (98 from TikTok, 88 from Bilibili) (Fig. 1).

Video collection flowchart. Videos related to IgA nephropathy were retrieved from TikTok and Bilibili using predefined search terms under default sorting. After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, the top 100 eligible videos from each platform were included for quality assessment and content analysis.

Based on this, researchers recorded and analyzed the following key parameters: length, likes, comments, collections, shares, video resolution, expression form, content, uploader, and professional title.

Expression forms were categorized into monologue, Q&A, lecture, and animation. Video content was classified into five categories: etiology, prevention, financial burden, treatment, and prognosis. Source classification included three distinct groups: healthcare personnel, professional organizations, and individual users. Professional titles included Chief Physician, Associate Chief Physician, and Attending/Registered Physician. Classification was based on a combination of profile information, verification badges, stated professional identity, and content characteristics. Disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. The research dataset is provided in Appendix 1.

Video quality assessment

Two reviewers underwent standardized training to establish good inter-rater agreement. Four validated and widely applied assessment tools were used for evaluation: GQS, JAMA, mDISCERN, and VIQI. The detailed contents of these assessment tools are provided in Appendix 219,20. The GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA scores were treated as ordinal variables, and the weighted Cohen’s kappa coefficient was used to represent inter-rater agreement (> 0.80 was considered excellent). The VIQI score was treated as a continuous variable, and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to assess inter-rater reliability (0.90 was considered excellent). The agreement values for both reviewers reached excellent levels, demonstrating good reliability21. It should be noted that the VIQI primarily evaluates visual presentation and information delivery features, including clarity of images, editing quality, subtitle readability, and structural organization, rather than the scientific accuracy or reliability of medical content.

Furthermore, to ensure the rigor of the study, an independent blinded assessment process was implemented to prevent any personnel from accessing each other’s ratings during the evaluation period. In cases of scoring discrepancies (a difference of ≥ 1 point in any assessment method), if the two reviewers could not reach a consensus, a third evaluator was consulted to determine the final score.

Data analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software version 4.4.2, and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10.3.1. Given that engagement metrics on social media platforms (e.g., likes, comments, collections, shares, and follower counts) are count-based numerical variables and typically exhibit highly skewed distributions, non-parametric statistical methods were applied throughout the study. Quantitative variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), and categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. Quartiles were calculated using the default quantile estimation method in R (type = 7), which is based on linear interpolation as described by Hyndman and Fan.

Comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Comparisons among more than two groups were conducted using the Kruskal–Wallis test, followed by pairwise Mann–Whitney U tests when the overall test was statistically significant. Post hoc pairwise comparisons were performed only when the Kruskal–Wallis test indicated a statistically significant overall difference, in order to control for type I error. Differences in categorical variables were assessed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test when expected cell counts were less than five. Because individual videos could address multiple content themes, content categories were not mutually exclusive. Each theme was therefore treated as a separate binary variable (present vs. absent), and platform differences were analyzed independently for each category. Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals were calculated from 2 × 2 contingency tables to quantify differences in theme prevalence between platforms.

Correlations between engagement metrics and quality assessment scores were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis. Effect size for Mann–Whitney U comparisons was calculated as r = Z/√N, where Z is the standardized test statistic and N is the total sample size. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Inter-platform disparities and their potential causes

Among the various dimensions of the VIQI, TikTok received slightly higher scores than Bilibili in terms of information structural presentation and visual presentation quality, with effect sizes ranging from small to moderate. This suggests a certain degree of difference in presentation style between the two platforms. This discrepancy may be related to differences in platform content ecosystems and recommendation algorithm preferences22. Previous studies have indicated that the overall user base of TikTok is younger, tends to be more sensitive to strong rhythm, clear structure, and visual stimuli, and is more inclined to interact with videos of higher presentation quality. In this context, content creators may be more likely to employ eye-catching subtitles, large-font labels, segmented titles, and transition designs to enhance the visual appeal and information readability of their videos, thereby indirectly improving scores on related VIQI dimensions. Furthermore, this study observed that the proportion of high-resolution videos on TikTok was relatively higher. This characteristic may further strengthen the association between visual presentation quality and user engagement, although this relationship still requires verification in larger samples and longitudinal studies23,24.

In contrast, videos on Bilibili are generally longer in duration, and their content presentation leans more towards systematic and explanatory expression, reflecting its potential advantage in the depth of knowledge transmission25,26. Although its VIQI scores were slightly lower than those of TikTok, no significant differences were observed between the two platforms on evaluation metrics focusing on information accuracy and reliability, such as the GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA benchmarks, and the effect sizes were small. This suggests that the overall quality of medical information on the two platforms is similar. The above results indicate that the differences between platforms are mainly manifested at the level of information presentation style and content packaging, rather than in the scientific rigor of the medical content itself. This also reflects the potential influence of different platform ecosystems and user behavior patterns on the forms of health information dissemination.

Analysis of the mechanisms underlying differences in video quality

This study found that monologue-style videos scored higher on the GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA benchmark scores compared to other video types. Monologue-style videos typically feature a single presenter directly addressing the audience, conveying medical information in a linear and continuous manner27. This approach offers strong content logic and concise concepts, which reduces the cognitive load on viewers and makes the information easier to recognize and retain. This kind of “continuous and low-complexity” expression improves the structural integrity of information material by better matching users’ attention patterns inside the short-video platforms’ ecosystem28.

Conversely, while animation videos possess high visual appeal, they are often produced by creators without a medical background, which may compromise the accuracy and rigor of the medical content29. Furthermore, animation videos frequently simplify medical processes by presenting them through simplified scenarios, which can easily lead to the omission of key disease concepts or the oversimplification of treatment procedures. This results in lower scores on the mDISCERN and JAMA benchmarks. This phenomenon has also been observed in previous studies on short videos related to appendicitis30.

This study also found that videos published by healthcare personnel scored significantly higher across multiple quality dimensions compared to those from individual users, which is consistent with previous literature31,32. However, no significant differences were observed among different professional titles, indicating that video content quality might depend more on medical background and the expression form used, rather than on clinical seniority. The production of medical science popularization content often requires the translation of complex concepts into simple language33,34.

Relationship between engagement metrics and information quality scores

This study demonstrated significant positive correlations among engagement metrics, indicating a notable synchronicity in user engagement behaviors on short-form video platforms. More importantly, the moderate to strong positive correlations between engagement metrics and both GQS and VIQI show that overall video quality and visual presentation quality have relation to user engagement35. VIQI has a larger correlation coefficient, indicating that videos with clearer structures and stronger visual presentations might receive more engagement—a finding congruent with the TikTok platform’s dissemination processes36.

However, no correlation was observed between the engagement metrics and either the mDISCERN or the JAMA scores. This might indicates that users did not demonstrate sensitivity towards aspects of information credibility, such as citation of sources, disclosure of funding, or timeliness of updates, while viewing health-related videos. Besides, most viewers’ engagement is associated with immediate impressions, visual appeal, and emotional resonance rather than a thorough evaluation of information reliability37,38.

Importantly, higher VIQI scores should not be interpreted as indicators of higher medical reliability. VIQI captures how attractively and clearly information is delivered, whereas mDISCERN and JAMA assess content credibility and transparency. The lack of correlation between engagement metrics and reliability-based scores suggests that visually appealing videos may attract attention even when scientific rigor is limited. This mismatch may raise concerns that presentation quality could outweigh informational accuracy in user engagement.

In addition, the inclusion of videos with potentially misleading or inaccurate medical information further reflects the complexity of the current short-video information ecosystem. Rather than being excluded, such content was incorporated into the analysis and evaluated using standardized tools, allowing for a more realistic assessment of the quality of health information encountered by the public. This finding highlights the importance of strengthening content regulation and improving public awareness of information reliability on social media platforms.

The specificity of health information dissemination for IgA nephropathy

As a chronic progressive kidney disease, the knowledge related to IgA nephropathy is highly specialized and complex, encompassing topics such as immunological mechanisms, glomerular injury, proteinuria control, and long-term prognosis management39,40. The imbalance in video content themes may reflect the unique clinical characteristics of IgA nephropathy. Because early-stage patients are often asymptomatic and disease progression is highly variable, many patients preferentially seek information related to treatment options and long-term outcomes rather than etiology or preventive strategies41. However, limited coverage of disease mechanisms and preventive management may be associated with reduced patient awareness of modifiable risk factors and long-term lifestyle-based management strategies42. In contrast to diseases that are symptom-driven, IgA nephropathy lacks of immediate symptoms requiring for medical intervention. As a result, consistent monitoring and long-term adherence may be better supported by accurate health communication. This reinforces the necessity of evaluating the quality of short-video content in a disease-specific manner43.

Public health implications

This study is the first to explore IgA nephropathy-related content on short-video platforms. The findings suggest that more targeted strategies are needed when utilizing short videos for health communication regarding this disease. Given TikTok’s advantages in visual presentation and interactivity, healthcare personnel should be encouraged to proactively produce concise, structured monologue-style science popularization content on this platform to enhance the visibility of accurate information. On Bilibili, there is insufficient interaction with in-depth content. Uploaders can improve the dissemination efficiency of such content by optimizing titles, abstracts, and visual cues. The platform could provide creator incentive programs for healthcare personnel, such as identity verification, content priority exposure, and creative tool support, to increase the supply of professional content.

Secondly, given the public’s limited ability to discern information reliability, it is recommended that platforms introduce a “credibility indicator” for medical videos, with a graded system based on factors such as the uploader’s identity, cited sources, and update dates. Finally, structured content repositories for health topics may help guide creators to cover key knowledge areas such as etiology, prevention, and financial burden. Such initiatives may be useful for reducing fragmented information exposure and supporting more coherent patient understanding. Through the aforementioned governance strategies, the reliability and practical value of short videos in public health communication can be effectively enhanced.

Limitations

This study exclusively examines short videos in Chinese on TikTok and Bilibili; it does not examine content from other international platforms like YouTube or videos in other languages, which may restrict the findings’ generalizability. Secondly, Multiple category-specific comparisons may increase the risk of type I error, and results should therefore be interpreted with caution. Besides, the cross-sectional study design cannot capture the dynamic evolution of video content in response to factors such as time, geographical region, and disease trends. Although the video content and ratings were assessed across multiple dimensions, the scoring process inevitably involves a degree of subjectivity. Furthermore, this study attempts to reduce customized recommendation bias, but including only top-ranked videos under default sorting necessarily reflects algorithmically visible (or amplified) content rather than the entire ecosystem of IgA nephropathy-related short videos. It should also be noted that this study did not exclude videos containing potentially misleading or medically inaccurate information. This approach was adopted to reflect the overall information landscape available to the public on short-video platforms.

Conclusion

This study systematically evaluated the information quality, presentation characteristics, and dissemination performance of short videos related to IgA nephropathy on the TikTok and Bilibili platforms. The results indicate that the overall content quality was at a moderate level. Videos created by healthcare personnel and those presented in a monologue form demonstrated significantly higher reliability and overall quality. Notable differences were observed between the two platforms in terms of visual presentation quality and interactive features. TikTok videos exhibited higher VIQI scores and greater interaction volume, whereas Bilibili content tended to be more in-depth but suffered from insufficient dissemination. Significant correlations between interaction metrics and both GQS and VIQI indicate that engagement tends to be higher for videos with better overall and visual presentation quality. In contrast, reliability-based scores were not significantly associated with engagement.

Future efforts aimed at chronic disease communication may include increasing healthcare personnel participation, expanding coverage of key medical information, and strengthening public education on evaluating information reliability. Platform-level optimization strategies may also be explored to support higher-quality content dissemination. These measures are essential for increasing the utility and impact of short videos in public health communication.

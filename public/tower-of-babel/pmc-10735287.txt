Coexistence of left ventricular aneurysm and giant pseudoaneurysm following myocardial infarction: the value of multimodality imaging in the clinical workup
Authors: Tan Liu, Yongxing Zhang, Manwei Liu, Yuman Li, Mingxing Xie
Published: 2023

A 53-year-old man with a history of myocardial infarction (MI) was transferred to our centre for palpitation and diaphoresis for 2 months. He successfully underwent percutaneous coronary intervention of the left circumflex coronary artery 2 months ago. His physical examination was unremarkable.

Transthoracic echocardiography showed a left ventricular aneurysm (LVA) measuring 36 × 34 mm combined with a giant LV pseudoaneurysm (LVPA) measuring 117 × 54 mm at the lateral and inferior wall of the left ventricle (Panel A). Color Doppler flow imaging revealed that LVPA communicated with LVA through a defect (18 mm) at the wall of the LVA (Panel B, see Supplementary data online, Video S1). Real-time three-dimensional transoesophageal echocardiography clearly showed the size of the defect (Panel C). Computed tomography showed an enormous LVPA which was connected to an LVA at the lateral and inferior wall (Panels D and E). Cardiac magnetic resonance demonstrated transmural MI and a huge LVPA (Panels F and G). This patient underwent the resection of LVPA and reinforcement with the bovine pericardial patch (Panels H and I). The postoperative course was uneventful.

The coexistence of LVA and LVPA following MI is extremely rare. Although the risk of spontaneous rupture remains unclear, the prognosis of LVPA is catastrophic. Our case underscores that multimodality imaging plays an indispensable role in definite diagnosis and clinical decision-making.

Ultrathin, biodegradable polymer-coated everolimus-eluting stents for patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention: Final three-year results of the PERFORM-EVER registry
Authors: Sridhar Kasturi, Srinivas Polasa, Mohammad Ali Sowdagar, Praveen Kumar, Thejanandan Reddy, Chaitanya Nichenamatla, Shailender Singh, Vijay Kumar Reddy
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe registry reports 3-year safety and clinical performance of the ultrathin strut (60 μm) biodegradable polymer-coated Tetrilimus, an everolimus-eluting stent (EES) (Sahajanand Medical Technologies Limited, India), in ‘real-world’ patients with coronary artery disease. A total of 815 Tetrilimus EES were implanted in 735 lesions in 594 patients. At 3-year follow-up, primary endpoint (target lesion failure, TLF) was reported in 8.6 % patients, including 2.6 % cardiac deaths, 3.5 % myocardial infarction and 2.6 % target lesion revascularization. At three-year, no cases of definite stent thrombosis were reported. The final three-year results of PERFORM-EVER registry endorse the continuous safety and effectiveness Tetrilimus EES.Keywords: Coronary artery disease, Drug-eluting stents, Percutaneous coronary intervention

Introduction

Over the past decade, the innovations and refinements of drug-eluting stents (DES) have dramatically changed the landscape of interventional cardiology. The second-generation thinner and biocompatible polymer-coated DES have substantially reduced DES-related thrombotic complications and improved clinical outcomes. However, the permanent presence of durable polymers may act as a nidus for late/chronic inflammatory responses and neo-atherosclerosis, which may raise the concern of long-term safety of these DES due to increased risk of restenosis and very late stent thrombosis occurring beyond one year.1,2 These risks were subsequently mitigated by introduction of biodegradable polymer-coated DES as polymers disappear after releasing the drug over a certain period, thereby leaving only a bare metal stent platform.3, 4, 5, 6 Several meta-analyses and a pooled analysis from multiple trials have shown equivalence or even superiority of biodegradable polymer-coated stents over durable polymer stents.1,7, 8, 9

Another concern with earlier generation DES is its higher strut thickness. Ultrathin struts are less thrombogenic, reduce vessel-wall injury and aid rapid re-endothelialization compared to thicker struts. Several meta-analyses have demonstrated superiority of ultrathin strut DES compared to thick/thin strut DES in terms of target lesion failure (TLF).10, 11, 12, 13 Thus, the research focus has now shifted toward development of ultrathin strut biodegradable polymer-coated DES with analogous drug release followed by subsequent degradation of the polymers.

Tetrilimus (Sahajanand Medical Technologies Ltd., Surat, India), an everolimus-eluting stent (EES), is a latest generation DES with biodegradable polymer-coating on an ultrathin (60 μm) cobalt-chromium platform with unique long-dual Z (LDZ) link connectors. The safety and effectiveness of Tetrilimus EES have previously been reported in several studies,14, 15, 16, 17, 18 but there are inadequate long-term clinical data (>1 year after implantation) on this latest generation ultrathin DES.19 Specifically, there is a need to establish whether Tetrilimus EES can overcome the limitations of earlier generation DES, like late in-stent restenosis and very late stent thrombosis. Therefore, we examined the final three-year clinical outcomes after implantation of Tetrilimus EES in real-world, unselected patients with coronary artery disease in the PERFORM-EVER registry.

Materials and methods

The PERFORM-EVER was an investigator-initiated, multi-centre, single-arm, retrospective, observational registry carried out at four tertiary care centres in India. Between July 2015 and October 2016, all consecutive patients who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with implantation of at least one Tetrilimus EES were included in the registry (n = 594). The design features and characteristics of Tetrilimus EES have been described in detail in previously published one-year follow-up results of PERFORM-EVER registry.17 The details of interventional procedure and adjuvant medications have been mentioned in previous publication.17 All the patients received dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) which included a loading dose of 300 mg of aspirin and either 600 mg of clopidogrel or 60 mg of prasugrel or 90 mg of ticagrelor along with heparin or bivalirudin as an anticoagulant drug, prior to and throughout the procedure. The periprocedural use of glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor was kept at investigator's preference. After the interventional procedure, all patients were recommended to continue DAPT (aspirin 75–300 mg and clopidogrel 75 mg or prasugrel 10 mg or ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily) for at least 12 months followed by only aspirin (75–300 mg) indefinitely.

Follow-up was conducted up to three years of the indexed procedure and was obtained either by extraction from existing databases in consecutive fashion where index and follow-up data existed or was obtained by telephonic contact.

The primary efficacy endpoint was incidence of target lesion failure (TLF), which was defined as a composite endpoint of cardiac death, myocardial infarction (MI), and target lesion revascularization (TLR) by percutaneous or surgical methods at three-year follow-up. The incidence of stent thrombosis was considered as an additional safety endpoint. The definitions of each endpoint have been discussed in detail in the previous study.17 Stent thrombosis was defined and categorized as — 1) Definite - symptoms suggestive of an acute coronary syndrome and angiographic or pathologic confirmation of stent thrombosis; 2) Probable - unexplained death within 30 days or target vessel MI without angiographic confirmation of stent thrombosis and; 3) Possible - any unexplained death after 30 days — as per Academic Research Consortium (ARC) definition.20

All data were analysed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS version 15; Chicago, IL, USA) software. The categorical variables are presented as frequency and percentages and the continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation. The clinical event curve for primary endpoint was acquired using the Kaplan–Meier method.

Results

The mean age of the patients (n = 594) was 55.6 ± 12.1 years. A total of 138 (23.2 %) patients had diabetes mellitus. According to addiction habits of enrolled patients, 205 (34.5 %) were consuming alcohol, 141 (23.7 %) were smokers, and 106 (17.8 %) were tobacco chewers. Acute coronary syndrome was the clinical presentation in more than half of the patients (58.6 %, n = 348) which included 24.9 % patients (n = 148) with non ST-elevation myocardial infarction, 18.4 % patients (n = 109) with unstable angina, and 15.3 % patients (n = 91) with ST-elevation myocardial infarction. During the assigned study duration, 735 coronary lesions were treated with the implantation of 815 Tetrilimus EES. The detailed baseline, clinical, lesion and stent characteristics of all the enrolled patients are described in detail in the previous publication of PERFORM-EVER registry.17

Briefly, a total of 225 (37.8 %) patients were diagnosed with multi-vessel coronary artery disease. The left anterior descending artery (51.8 %) exemplified highest percentage of culprit lesions followed by right coronary artery (29.1 %), left circumflex artery (18.9 %), and left main (0.1 %). During the assigned study duration, 735 coronary lesions were treated with the implantation of 815 Tetrilimus EES. Among 735 lesions, 577 (78.5 %) lesions were complex Type B2/C and 103 (14 %) lesions were totally occluded. The mean number of Tetrilimus EES implanted per patient was 1.4 ± 0.5 and per lesion was 1.1 ± 0.3. Mean implanted stent length was 27.6 ± 9.7 mm and mean stent diameter was 3.0 ± 0.3 mm.

Discussion

The latest modifications to metallic DES design and the combination of ultrathin strut stent platforms with biodegradable polymer coating have improved arterial healing pattern and reduced the rate of late/very late consequences. DES with thinner struts improve stent conformability and deliverability; reduce laminar flow disturbance and endothelial shear stress; minimize stent-induced vessel-wall injury and inflammation; promote early arterial healing and uniform re-endothelialization; and decrease thrombogenicity compared to thicker strut DES. These have been corroborated by several meta-analyses that have shown safety and efficacy of ultrathin strut DES compared to thick strut DES at short-as well as long-term follow-up.10, 11, 12, 13,21,22

The design characteristics of available biodegradable polymer-coated ultrathin strut DES —Tetrilimus EES (60 μm), Supraflex SES and Supraflex Cruz SES (60 μm), Orsiro SES (60 μm up to 3 mm and 80 μm above 3 mm stent diameter), and Mistent SES (64 μm) — varies notably. Among all four, Tetrilimus EES is the only stent with everolimus drug coating. In the present study, the three-year cumulative incidence of TLF was 8.6 % with Tetrilimus EES which was mainly driven by MI (3.5 %). A registry of Tetrilimus EES on real-world patients also reported similar incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events at three-year follow-up in overall (9.1 %) as well as in patient with long coronary lesions (10.4 % in >40 mm lesion length).19 For purpose of comparison, three-year rates of device-oriented clinical events (DoCE) for other ultrathin DES include—10.5 % with MiStent SES vs 11.5 % with Xience EES in DESSOLVE III trial23; 8.1 % with Supraflex SES vs 9.4 % with Xience EES in TALENT trial13; 8.2 % with Orsiro SES vs 13.6 % with Xience EES in BIOFLOW V trial24; 6.7 % with Orsiro SES vs 7.5 % with Synergy EES in BIO-RESORT trial25; and 9.0 % with Orsiro SES vs 9.1 % with Nobori biolimus-eluting stent in SORT OUT VII trial.26 Similarly, T-FLEX registry (n = 1203) reported 8.1 % TLF rate with Supraflex Cruz SES (ultrathin biodegradable polymer-coated stent with the LDZ link) in real-world Indian patients up to 3-year follow-up.27

The final three-year follow-up of the present study reported 0.9 % cumulative incidence of definite (0 %) or probable stent thrombosis (0.9 %), and between one- and three-year no incidence of definite or probable stent thrombosis was noted. These findings are comparable with the results of the studies on other ultrathin DES — 1.2 % with MiStent SES vs 1.5 % with Xience EES in DESSOLVE III trial23; 1.1 % with Supraflex SES vs 1.4 % with Xience EES in TALENT trial13; 0.1 % with Orsiro SES vs 1.2 % with Xience EES in BIOFLOW V24; 1.1 % with Orsiro SES vs 1.1 % with Synergy EES in BIORESORT trial25; and 1.0 % (definite stent thrombosis) with Orsiro SES vs 1.7 % (definite stent thrombosis) with Nobori biolimus-eluting stent in SORT OUT VII trial.26 Furthermore, the results of another real-world registry on Tetrilimus EES also reported similar incidence of definite or probable stent thrombosis in overall (0.4 %) and even in long lesion (>40 mm) subgroup (1.4 %) at three-year follow-up.19

In addition to ultrathin platform of Tetrilimus EES, its unique biodegradable polymer-coating, novel LDZ-link (making it more flexible and deliverable) and wide range of available size matrix (making it suitable for small vessels [<2.5 mm] as well as long lesions [>40 mm]) demonstrates favorable long-term safety and effectiveness as reported in the current and in the previous studies.14, 15, 16, 17, 18,28

Conclusion

The final three-year follow-up results of PERFORM-EVER registry confirm the long-term safety and effectiveness of an ultrathin biodegradable polymer-coated Tetrilimus EES in unselected real-world patients with acceptable rate of TLF, TLR, and stent thrombosis.

Key message

What is Already Known?

•Short term safety and effectiveness of latest generation ultrathin drug-eluting stents are well-established. However, long-term follow-ups are limited.

Short term safety and effectiveness of latest generation ultrathin drug-eluting stents are well-established. However, long-term follow-ups are limited.

What this study adds?

•PERFORM-EVER registry will add one more evidence of safe and effective use latest generation ultrathin everolimus-eluting stents (Tetrilimus EES) in patients with simple as well as complex lesion characteristics at long-term (3 years).

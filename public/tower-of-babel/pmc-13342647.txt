Robust unsupervised outlier detection in IoT using contrastive learning-driven autoencoders
Authors: Shengyu Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Outlier detection plays a critical role in ensuring the reliability, security, and operational stability of Internet of Things (IoT) systems, where data streams are often high-dimensional, heterogeneous, and unlabeled. In this paper, we propose a robust unsupervised outlier detection framework that integrates autoencoder-based representation learning with contrastive loss to enhance discriminative capability in latent space. The proposed model jointly optimizes reconstruction loss and contrastive loss, enabling the autoencoder not only to accurately reconstruct normal patterns but also to enforce clear separation between normal and anomalous samples in the learned feature space. To further improve detection robustness, we introduce an adaptive threshold determination algorithm that identifies outliers based on statistical analysis and percentile-based modeling of reconstruction errors, eliminating the need for manually tuned thresholds. This dual-thresholding strategy allows the proposed approach to adapt effectively to varying data distributions and operational conditions. Extensive experiments conducted on the Statlog (Landsat Satellite) and UNSW-NB15 datasets demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms traditional unsupervised and deep learning-based baselines in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score. The results confirm that incorporating contrastive learning significantly improves anomaly separability and detection accuracy, making the proposed framework a reliable and scalable solution for outlier detection in real-world IoT environments.

Introduction

The Internet of Things (IoT) has revolutionized various industries by enabling seamless connectivity and data exchange among interconnected devices1. IoT devices generate a vast amount of data continuously, reflecting various aspects of the physical world. However, this data often contains anomalies or outliers—instances that deviate significantly from the norm—which can indicate critical issues such as malfunctions, security breaches, or unusual events2.

Detecting outliers in IoT data is crucial for maintaining system reliability, ensuring data integrity, and enhancing security3. Recent advances in fault-tolerant and secure control systems further highlight the importance of adaptive and intelligent mechanisms for detecting anomalies in complex IoT environments32–34. Traditional methods for outlier detection often rely on predefined thresholds or statistical techniques, which may not effectively capture complex patterns and relationships in high-dimensional IoT data. Moreover, manual tuning of thresh- olds can be challenging and may not generalize well across different datasets or operational conditions4. Previous studies on outlier detection in IoT data have predominantly relied on traditional statistical methods or simple threshold-based techniques. While these approaches are straightforward to implement, they often struggle with the following limitations:

Recent advances in unsupervised learning have increasingly focused on robust representation learning and adaptive machine learning paradigms to address the heterogeneity and non-stationarity of IoT data. In this context, granular computing has emerged as an effective framework for constructing adaptive information granules that enhance model stability and generalization. Notably, the recent granular-ball regeneration clustering approach based on the principle of justifiable granularity dynamically adjusts granularity levels to handle clusters with heterogeneous structures while reducing sensitivity to initialization. While such methods emphasize adaptive data partitioning at the clustering level, the proposed approach addresses outlier detection from a latent representation-learning perspective by integrating autoencoder-based reconstruction with contrastive loss to enhance discriminative capability25.

Meanwhile, autoencoders are unsupervised neural networks that compress input data into a latent representation via an encoder and reconstruct it through a decoder. In IoT outlier detection, reconstruction errors highlight anomalies, as unusual data points are poorly reconstructed. By integrating contrastive loss, the model further separates normal and anomalous data in the latent space, enhancing discriminative capability and improving detection performance. Also, the novelty of our approach lies in its IoT-specific design: an unsupervised contrastive pairing mechanism for tabular and network-flow data, combined with an adaptive, data-driven threshold aligned with latent-space separation. These elements enhance detection robustness and generalization, distinguishing our framework from conventional AE-plus-contrastive and recent foundation-model-based methods31.

These limitations highlight the need for advanced techniques that can effectively leverage the richness of IoT data while addressing the inherent challenges in outlier detection. In response, our proposed method combines Autoencoder-based reconstruction with a carefully designed Contrastive Loss and a dataset-adaptive thresholding strategy. Unlike prior deep anomaly detection approaches, including recent foundation-model-based methods, our framework explicitly constructs positive and negative pairs using stochastic augmentations, neighborhood sampling, and pseudo-labeling to capture both intra-sample and inter-sample variations. This combination not only enhances the discriminative capability of the learned latent representations but also improves robustness across diverse IoT datasets by reducing sensitivity to noise and feature sparsity. Consequently, our approach advances existing methods by providing a fully unsupervised, reproducible, and practical framework that strengthens anomaly detection performance through the joint optimization of reconstruction fidelity, latent-space contrastive separation, and adaptive thresholding.

Related works

Outlier detection in IoT environments has been extensively studied, with various unsupervised techniques proposed to address the unique challenges presented by the heterogeneity and high dimensionality of IoT data. In this section, we categorize the previous studies into three main groups: clustering-based methods, statistical and probabilistic methods, and deep learning approaches.

Different unsupervised outlier detection methods have distinct strengths and limitations. Clustering-based methods handle unlabeled data but struggle with high-dimensional and noisy datasets. Statistical and probabilistic methods provide interpretable anomaly scores but may fail on complex non-linear data. Deep learning approaches model high-dimensional patterns effectively but can be computationally intensive and sensitive to hyperparameters. In contrast, the proposed method combines Autoencoder reconstruction with contrastive loss, improving separation between normal and anomalous points, enhancing discriminative power, robustness, and generalization in IoT data.

We then compare these methods and highlight how our proposed approach addresses their limitations.

Clustering-based methods

Clustering-based methods are widely used for unsupervised outlier detection in IoT data due to their ability to identify patterns and anomalies without labeled data5. Monalisa et al.6 proposed an enhanced clustering-based outlier detection approach that integrates Density- Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) and k-means. This hybrid method improves detection performance by effectively handling high-dimensional and noisy IoT data. Stefanovic and Kurasova7 developed an unsupervised clustering algorithm based on Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs). The SOM-based method provides a visual representation of the data, facilitating the identification of anomalies. Scime and Beuth8 presented a multi-scale convolutional neural network (MSCNN) model for anomaly detection in IoT. The MSCNN captures features at multiple scales, providing robust detection capabilities against various types of anomalies.

Statistical and probabilistic methods

Statistical and probabilistic methods leverage the underlying statistical properties of the data to detect outliers. Qiao et al.9 developed an adaptive anomaly detection framework using One-Class Support Vector Machines (SVMs) combined with feature selection techniques. This method dynamically adjusts to changing data distributions, making it suitable for real-time IoT applications. Tao10 proposed a hybrid approach that combines Bayesian Networks with neural networks for outlier detection. This method leverages probabilistic reasoning and deep learning to achieve high detection accuracy. Akcay et al.11 introduced a semi-supervised anomaly detection technique using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The GAN based approach learns the distribution of normal data and identifies outliers by measuring deviations from this distribution.

Recent advances in network traffic anomaly detection have focused on improving representation learning and overcoming the limitations of reconstruction-based models. For instance, Lian et al.27 proposed ConMD, a contextual masking distillation framework that enhances anomaly detection by leveraging knowledge distillation and context-aware attention mechanisms to capture both local and global traffic dependencies, along with a multi-view anomaly scoring strategy. In another work, Lian et al.28 introduced the UnDiff method, which addresses the shortcomings of intra-sample reconstruction errors by modeling inter-sample uncertainty differences, enabling more robust detection of drifted anomalies. Additionally, Zheng et al.29 presented a semi-supervised framework that fuses multimodal traffic information using multi-scale feature extraction and two-stage fusion, improving detection performance under limited labeled data. These studies highlight the growing trend toward incorporating contextual modeling, uncertainty estimation, and multimodal fusion, motivating the need for robust unsupervised frameworks such as the proposed method, which combines representation learning and contrastive objectives for improved anomaly discrimination in IoT environments.

Lian et al.30 propose a semi-supervised anomaly traffic detection method called Multi-Frequency Reconstruction (MFR) that trains solely on normal traffic data. Their approach extracts multi-scale low-frequency information using low-pass filters and incorporates a channel-spatial attention mechanism to capture spatio-temporal features of traffic flows. Lian et al.26 proposed a semi-supervised framework for anomaly detection in network traffic based on payload-level analysis, where a reconstruction model is first used to learn compact representations of normal data, followed by a contrastive learning stage that incorporates pseudo-anomaly samples generated via a dual-domain noise strategy and optimized using a triplet loss-based classifier. While their approach demonstrates strong performance in network traffic classification, it relies on semi-supervised learning and synthetic anomaly generation. In contrast, our method is fully unsupervised and does not require labeled or artificially generated anomalies, instead utilizing a direct pairing strategy based on data augmentation and random sampling. Moreover, our framework integrates reconstruction and contrastive learning in a unified objective and introduces a threshold determination mechanism for anomaly detection, making it more generalizable and applicable to diverse IoT data scenarios beyond network traffic.

Deep learning approaches

Deep learning techniques have gained prominence in outlier detection due to their ability to model complex, non-linear relationships in high-dimensional data. Nguyen et al.12 proposed a robust anomaly detection method using a hybrid model that combines Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks with Autoencoders. This method leverages the temporal dependencies in IoT data and achieves high detection accuracy. Eduardo et al.13 introduced an unsupervised learning approach using Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for outlier detection. The VAE-based method captures the latent representations of normal data and effectively identifies deviations indicative of anomalies. Khan14 proposed a deep learning framework that combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for outlier detection. This hybrid model leverages the spatial and temporal features of IoT data, improving detection performance. Zhong15 developed an ensemble learning approach that integrates multiple anomaly detection models. The ensemble method enhances robustness and accuracy by aggregating the strengths of individual models. Zhou et al.16 introduced a contrastive learning approach for outlier detection using Autoencoders. By leveraging contrastive loss, this method enhances the discriminative power of the Autoencoder, leading to improved detection performance. Oluwasanmi et al.17 applied attention mechanisms to Autoencoder-based anomaly detection models.

The attention mechanism enables the model to concentrate on the most informative parts of the data, thereby enhancing its ability to detect subtle anomalies. References18–20 employed Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to capture the intricate relationships and dependencies among IoT devices and their data streams, significantly improving the identification of anomalous patterns. Additionally, studies21–23 investigated transfer learning for IoT anomaly detection, leveraging knowledge from pre-trained models on related tasks to boost performance in settings with limited labeled data.

Although recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of deep learning techniques—including Autoencoders, VAEs, hybrid CNN-RNN models, contrastive learning, attention mechanisms, GNNs, and transfer learning—for anomaly detection, a critical assessment of their limitations remains limited. In particular, existing works do not fully address practical challenges in IoT data, such as scalability, dynamic data streams, scarcity of labeled normal and anomalous samples, and sensitivity to hyperparameter selection. Moreover, comparative analyses of modern approaches, such as contrastive learning versus diffusion-based models, regarding their ability to capture latent features of anomalies and stabilize thresholding, are insufficiently explored. These gaps motivate the development of innovative approaches that integrate Autoencoder-based reconstruction, contrastive learning, and adaptive thresholding for robust and efficient outlier detection in IoT environments.

Preliminaries

In the field of IoT, the detection of outliers is crucial for ensuring the reliability and security of the system. Due to the vast and varied nature of IoT data, unsupervised outlier detection techniques are particularly advantageous as they do not require labeled datasets. In this section, we investigate four principal unsupervised methods for outlier detection: DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise), k-Means Clustering, One Class Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Bayesian Networks. Each method offers unique advantages and challenges, which will be detailed in the following sections along with their mathematical formulations and applications in IoT environments.

Proposed solution

In this section, we present a novel approach for unsupervised outlier detection using Autoencoder-based Models with Contrastive Loss. Our method leverages the representation learning capability of autoencoders and the discriminative power of contrastive learning to effectively distinguish between normal and anomalous data points.

Training procedure

The training procedure for our proposed method is outlined in Algorithm 1. It then proceeds through multiple training epochs. For each epoch, the data is divided into mini-batches. For each mini-batch, positive and negative pairs of data points are sampled to compute the contrastive loss.

In the proposed unsupervised framework, positive and negative pairs are constructed specifically for tabular and network-flow IoT data to enable effective contrastive learning. Positive pairs are generated by applying stochastic augmentations to the same input sample, such as noise injection, feature masking, slight perturbations, or neighborhood-based sampling, ensuring the underlying semantics are preserved. Negative pairs are formed either by randomly sampling different instances from the dataset or via pseudo-labeling techniques to assume normal versus anomalous relationships, based on the assumption that most samples represent normal behavior. All augmentations and sampling strategies are carefully controlled to maintain data consistency and avoid distortion of feature distributions. The contrastive loss then minimizes the distance between positive pairs while maximizing the separation from negative pairs in the latent space. This combination provides a practical, reproducible, and fully unsupervised pairing mechanism, specifically tailored for anomaly detection in diverse IoT environments. Alternatively, we can use a percentile-based approach to select the threshold. This method does not assume any specific distribution of reconstruction errors. Instead, we directly compute the threshold based on a specified percentile of the empirical distribution of reconstruction errors.

For instance, setting p = 0.95 will result in a threshold that captures 95% of the reconstruction errors, with the top 5% considered as outliers.

Proposed algorithm for threshold calculation

The detailed algorithm for calculating the threshold τ using the validation data is outlined in Algorithm 3.

The algorithm begins by computing the reconstruction errors for each data point in the validation set using the trained autoencoder model. Next, it computes the mean and standard deviation of the reconstruction errors. Depending on the chosen method (Gaussian-based or percentile-based), it calculates the threshold τ accordingly. The choice between the Gaussian-based method and the percentile-based method depends on the characteristics of the IoT data and the specific application requirements. The Gaussian- based method is appropriate when the reconstruction errors are approximately normally dis- tributed. In contrast, the percentile-based method is more flexible and can be used regardless of the underlying distribution of reconstruction errors.

In outlier detection, automatic threshold optimization methods are commonly used to adaptively determine the boundary between normal and anomalous data. Examples include techniques based on statistical analysis, extreme value theory, or unsupervised validation metrics such as maximizing F1-score. In our study, the proposed Threshold Determination Algorithm provides a flexible framework that incorporates both statistical analysis of reconstruction errors (assuming a Gaussian distribution) and percentile-based threshold selection. This effectively acts as an automatic threshold optimization procedure, allowing the model to adapt to dataset characteristics and improve robustness in detecting anomalies in IoT data.

In conclusion, determining an appropriate threshold τ is essential for effective outlier detection in IoT data. By leveraging statistical analysis and empirical methods, our approach ensures robust identification of anomalous data points, thereby enhancing the reliability and security of IoT systems.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an unsupervised outlier detection method for IoT data using Autoencoder-based models with Contrastive Loss. By combining reconstruction-based learning with contrastive loss, the model learns highly discriminative latent representations, enabling accurate identification of anomalous data points. Experimental results on both the Statlog (Landsat Satellite) and UNSW-NB15 datasets demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms classical clustering methods, statistical approaches, and standard autoencoders across key metrics including recall, precision, accuracy, and F1-score. These results validate the robustness, effectiveness, and generalizability of the proposed approach in diverse IoT scenarios. While the proposed method shows strong performance, several limitations remain. First, although it generalizes well across the evaluated datasets, its performance on IoT data with significantly different characteristics (e.g., real-time streaming sensor data or highly sparse datasets) has not yet been thoroughly tested. Second, the computational complexity of the autoencoder training, particularly when integrating contrastive loss, may pose challenges for deployment in resource-constrained IoT devices. Finally, hyperparameter selection (e.g., margin α, balance parameter) can affect detection performance and may require dataset-specific tuning. Future work will focus on addressing these limitations by exploring real-time and resource-efficient implementations, extending the evaluation to a wider range of IoT data types, and investigating adaptive or self-tuning mechanisms for hyperparameters. Incorporating these improvements will further enhance the applicability and practicality of the proposed method in real-world IoT systems, while maintaining high accuracy and robustness in outlier detection.

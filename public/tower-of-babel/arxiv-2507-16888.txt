Electroweak Precision Constraints on Dark Photon Models with Generalized Mixing
Enrico Bertuzzo, Csaba Csaki, and Fernanda Huller

Abstract
We present a global fit to electroweak precision observables (EWPOs) in dark photon (DP) models containing both kinetic and mass mixing between the DP and the neutral gauge bosons of the Standard Model (SM). Such more general mixing can be the result of an extended scalar sector, which we specify in this paper. We calculate the tree-level contributions to EWPOs due to the mixing with the DP, as well as the leading loop corrections to the oblique parameters due to the extended Higgs sector. In the scalar sector, we find that ample regions of parameter space are still unconstrained by data. In the gauge sector, the excluded region depends strongly on the vacuum expectation values of the scalar fields: for moderate ratios, DP masses in the ( 40 ​ GeV , 1 ​ TeV ) (40\,\text{GeV},1\,\text{TeV}) range are excluded; for larger ratios, the limits become indistinguishable from those for standard DPs.

As we have seen in Sec.

\text{t}_{\eta}\gg\text{t}_{\beta}
, we have
M_{Z^{\prime}}\gg\hat{M}_{Z}\simeq M_{Z}
and some form of decoupling is to be expected. We can make this statement more precise inspecting Eq. (
9
). Observing that the combination
c_{\eta}\,\hat{M}_{X}
does not depend on
\text{t}_{\eta}
, we see that in the
\text{t}_{\eta}\gtrsim\text{t}_{\beta}
regime, the main contribution to the generalized mixing is given by the second term in the numerator of
\tan(2\theta)
, which only depends on
\text{t}_{\beta}
. As we increase
\text{t}_{\eta}
and go into the
\text{t}_{\eta}\gg\text{t}_{\beta}
regime, the term proportional to
(\hat{M}_{X}/\hat{M}_{Z})^{2}
in the denominator starts to dominate, so that we expect
|\tan 2\theta|\propto\text{t}_{\eta}^{-2}
, corresponding to the expected decoupling. This point is quantified in Fig.
4
, the analog of Fig.
2
in which we keep fixed
\text{t}_{\beta}=5
and increase
\text{t}_{\eta}
to 15 (left panel) and 60 (right panel). In the left panel,
\text{t}_{\eta}
is still sufficiently moderate that the mixing angle is dominated by the
\text{t}_{\beta}
contribution and we still exclude masses in the
40\,\text{GeV}\lesssim M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 1\,\text{TeV}
range. We start, however, to see some decoupling, since the region
1\,\text{TeV}\lesssim M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 10\,\text{TeV}
in unconstrained. On the other hand, for
\text{t}_{\eta}=60
we are deep into the
\text{t}_{\eta}\gg\text{t}_{\beta}
regime and the mixing angle is so small that there is no limit from EWPOs. In this case we expect to be, once more, back to the usual DP case.

5
Conclusions

U(1)_{X}
models are among the simplest extensions of the SM of particle physics and have the nice property that they can be searched for in a plethora of different experiments, from colliders to beam dumps to precision low energy experiments. Among the most robust limits are those coming from EWPOs, expected to be significant since, in general, the gauge sector of the SM is modified in
U(1)_{X}
models. DP models are particularly simple because, under the assumption that the only portal with the SM is given by the kinetic mixing and that the vector mass is of Stückelberg type, the phenomenology is completely fixed by only two parameters: the DP mass and the kinetic mixing itself. An interesting question that can be asked is: how robust are the DP limits to small deformations to this scenario? Steps in this direction have been taken in
Barducci:2021egn
;
Foguel:2022unm
, in which the effects due to the addition of higher dimensional operators or of a dark Higgs boson have been studied.

In this paper, we take a further step in the same direction: we study a situation where, in addition to the kinetic mixing parameter, a mass mixing between the DP and the
Z
boson is also considered. First we identified a suitable model in which such a generalized mixing emerges (Sec.
2
). This turns out to require the addition of one singlet and one doublet to the scalar sector, both charged under
U(1)_{X}
. The gauge sector of the model is completely described by four parameters: the kinetic mixing, two ratios of vevs (that we call
\text{t}_{\beta}
and
\text{t}_{\eta}
), and the
U(1)_{X}
gauge coupling. One combination of the latter three parameters controls the
Z^{\prime}
mass, while another combination controls the additional source of mass mixing.
Since any mass mixing between the
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
modifies the electroweak sector of the SM, we have then focused on the limits arising from EWPOs, described in Sec.
3
.

Our results are presented in Sec.
4
. Concerning the scalar sector, we identify regions in the parameter space allowed by current data for some given benchmarks. Concerning instead the gauge sector, the main conclusion is that, for moderate values of
\text{t}_{\beta}
and
\text{t}_{\eta}
, EWPOs exclude a much larger region in parameter space with respect to the usual DP with only kinetic mixing. However, as soon as
\text{t}_{\beta}
and/or
\text{t}_{\eta}
become larger, the model becomes virtually indistinguishable from the usual DP model, at least for what concerns EWPOs.

Of course, this does not mean that in this region of parameter space there are no differences between generalized and usual DP. As can be seen from Figs.
2
and
4
, large modifications to the
Z^{\prime}
coupling to SM particles are expected even when there are no modification to EWPOs, given that the latter are mainly driven by the
Z
coupling and mixing angle
\theta
. A simple computation shows that, for the total
Z^{\prime}
decay width
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}
, we can have
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}(10)\,\Gamma_{Z^{\prime},\text{DP}}
(with
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime},\text{DP}}
the total
Z^{\prime}
decay width for the usual DP) right below the dashed lines in Figs.
2
-
4
. In such region, we thus expect major modifications to the limits coming from experiments in which the
Z^{\prime}
is directly produced and further decays to generate a signal. Comparing with Fig. 3.3 of
dark_photons
, we see that modifications can be expected in the limits coming from hadronic and electronic beam dumps (nu-Cal, CHARM, E137), colliders (Babar, CMS, Atlas), and also in supernova bounds. We expect the modifications to the limits to be particularly important for beam dumps and supernova. In these cases, the
Z^{\prime}
should decay at a macroscopic distance from the production point, and our model predicts a lifetime which is much shorter than the usual one in a large part of the parameter space where such bounds apply. A case-by-case analysis is needed to asses quantitatively how the bounds are modified. However, we defer such study to future work.

Appendix A
Diagonalizing the Gauge and Scalar Sectors

A.1
Gauge Sector

We present here more details about the diagonalization of the gauge boson Lagrangian. Starting from Eq. (
3
), the terms involving the gauge bosons are

\mathcal{L}_{\text{Kinetic}}\supset-\frac{1}{4}\hat{\bm{V}}^{T}_{\mu\nu}K\hat{\bm{V}}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\bm{V}}^{T}_{\mu}M^{2}\hat{\bm{V}}^{\mu},

(42)

with fields before diagonalization defined as
\hat{\bm{V}}=(\hat{Z},\hat{A},\hat{X})^{T}
. The kinetic matrix receives contributions from the kinetic mixing parameter
\chi
and is given by

K=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-\hat{\text{s}}_{W}\,\chi\\
0&1&\hat{\text{c}}_{W}\,\chi\\
-\hat{\text{s}}_{W}\,\chi&\hat{\text{c}}_{W}\,\chi&1\end{pmatrix},

(43)

while the mass matrix is

M^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{4}(g^{2}+g^{\prime 2})(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})&0&-\frac{1}{2}g_{X}q_{X}\sqrt{(g^{2}+g^{\prime 2})}v_{2}^{2}\\
0&0&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}g_{X}q_{X}\sqrt{(g^{2}+g^{\prime 2})}v_{2}^{2}&0&(g_{X}q_{X})^{2}(w^{2}+v_{2}^{2})\end{pmatrix}.

(44)

To diagonalize this Lagrangian, we first diagonalize the kinetic terms applying the transformation
burgess

\hat{\bm{V}}=L\tilde{\bm{V}}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\hat{\text{s}}_{W}\,\text{sh}\xi\\
0&1&-\hat{\text{c}}_{W}\,\text{sh}\xi\\
0&0&\text{ch}\xi\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{Z}\\
\tilde{A}\\
\tilde{X}\end{pmatrix}.

(45)

The mass matrix in the tilded basis is simply given by
\tilde{M}^{2}=L^{T}M^{2}L
and can be diagonalized by a rotation
R
defined as

\tilde{\bm{V}}=R\bm{V}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\cos{\theta}&0&-\sin{\theta}\\
0&1&0\\
\sin{\theta}&0&\cos{\theta}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
A\\
Z^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

(46)

with the rotation angle
\theta
shown in Eq. (
9
) and physical masses given by

M_{Z,Z^{\prime}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\text{Tr}\,\tilde{M}^{2}\pm\text{sign}\left(1-\frac{\hat{M}_{X}^{2}}{\hat{M}_{Z}^{2}}\right)\sqrt{(\text{Tr}\,\tilde{M}^{2})^{2}-4\det\tilde{M}^{2}}\right],

(47)

where, for simplicity, we have defined

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\,\tilde{M}^{2}

\displaystyle=\hat{M}_{Z}^{2}\left[1+\left(\text{ch}\xi\,\frac{\hat{M}_{X}}{\hat{M}_{Z}}\right)^{2}-2\,\hat{\text{s}}_{W}\,\text{sh}\xi\,\text{ch}\xi\,\text{c}_{\beta}\,\text{c}_{\eta}\frac{\hat{M}_{X}}{\hat{M}_{Z}}+\left(\hat{\text{s}}_{W}\,\text{sh}\xi\right)^{2}\right],

(48)

\displaystyle\det\tilde{M}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[1-(\text{c}_{\beta}\,\text{c}_{\eta})^{2}\right]\left(\text{ch}\xi\,\hat{M}_{X}\hat{M}_{Z}\right)^{2}.

The function sign(x) is used to ensure that
M_{Z}\rightarrow\hat{M}_{Z}
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}\rightarrow\hat{M}_{X}
in the limit of no kinetic and mass mixing.

A.2
Scalar Sector

We now turn to the diagonalization of the scalar Lagrangian. We parametrize the scalar fields as

H_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{+}\\
\frac{v_{1}+h_{1}+i\phi_{1}^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{pmatrix},~~~H_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{v_{2}+h_{2}+i\phi_{2}^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\phi_{2}^{-}\end{pmatrix},~~~S=\frac{w+s+i\phi_{S}^{0}}{\sqrt{2}},

(49)

where the vevs are determined minimizing the scalar potential of Eq. (
4
):

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{11}v_{1}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{2}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1S}}{2}w^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{v_{2}w}{v_{1}},

(50)

\displaystyle\mu_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{22}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{2}v_{1}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2S}}{2}w^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{v_{1}w}{v_{2}},

\displaystyle\mu_{S}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{SS}w^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1S}}{2}v_{1}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2S}}{2}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{w}.

These equations can be used to eliminate the mass parameters
\mu_{i}^{2}
, allowing to express the mass matrices in terms of the vevs. In the charged scalar sector we obtain

M_{\text{Charged}}^{2}=\frac{v^{2}}{2}\text{c}_{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{21}\,\text{c}_{\beta}+\sqrt{2}\,\frac{\kappa}{v}\frac{\text{t}_{\eta}}{\text{t}_{\beta}}&\lambda_{21}\,\text{s}_{\beta}+\sqrt{2}\,\frac{\kappa}{v}\text{t}_{\eta}\\
\lambda_{21}\,\text{s}_{\beta}+\sqrt{2}\frac{\kappa}{v}\text{t}_{\eta}&\text{t}_{\beta}(\lambda_{21}\,\text{s}_{\beta}+\sqrt{2}\,\frac{\kappa}{v}\text{t}_{\eta})\end{pmatrix},

(51)

in the CP-odd sector we have

M_{\text{CP-odd}}^{2}=\frac{\kappa\,v}{\sqrt{2}}\text{c}_{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\text{t}_{\eta}}{\text{t}_{\beta}}&\text{t}_{\eta}&1\\
\text{t}_{\eta}&\text{t}_{\beta}\,\text{t}_{\eta}&\text{t}_{\beta}\\
1&\text{t}_{\beta}&\frac{\text{t}_{\beta}}{\text{t}_{\eta}}\end{pmatrix},

(52)

and in the CP-even sector we have the matrix of Eq. (
14
). Diagonalization can be performed with standard techniques (see, for instance,
n2hdm
whose notation we follow). In the charged and CP-odd sectors, the rotation to the mass basis is simply given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}H^{\pm}\\
G^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\text{c}_{\beta}&\text{s}_{\beta}\\
-\text{s}_{\beta}&\text{c}_{\beta}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\pm}\\
\phi_{2}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix},

(53)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A^{0}\\
G_{1}^{0}\\
G_{2}^{0}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{s}_{\beta}^{2}+\text{t}_{\eta}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\text{t}_{\eta}&0&\text{s}_{\beta}\\
0&1&0\\
-\text{s}_{\beta}&0&\text{t}_{\eta}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\text{c}_{\beta}&\text{s}_{\beta}&0\\
-\text{s}_{\beta}&\text{c}_{\beta}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{0}\\
\phi_{2}^{0}\\
\phi_{S}^{0}\end{pmatrix},

where
H^{\pm}
and
A^{0}
are the physical states with masses shown in Eqs. (
12
)-(
13
), and the states
G^{\pm}
and
G_{1,2}^{0}
are the would-be NGBs eaten up by the gauge bosons. Diagonalization of the CP-even mass matrix must instead be done numerically.

To draw the exclusion region showed in Fig.
3
, we proceed as follows. The gauge sector of our model depends on four parameters, (
M_{Z^{\prime}}
,
\text{sh}\xi
,
\text{t}_{\beta}
,
\text{t}_{\eta}
). Assuming the measurements to be normally distributed, we can define the chi-square as
curtin

\begin{split}\chi^{2}(M_{Z^{\prime}}&,\text{sh}\xi,\text{t}_{\beta},\text{t}_{\eta})=\\
&\left[x_{exp}-x_{theo}(M_{Z^{\prime}},\text{sh}\xi,\text{t}_{\beta},\text{t}_{\eta})\right]_{i}\left(cov^{-1}\right)_{ij}\left[x_{exp}-x_{theo}(M_{Z^{\prime}},\text{sh}\xi,\text{t}_{\beta},\text{t}_{\eta})\right]_{j},\end{split}

(54)

with covariance matrix given by
\left(cov\right)_{ij}=\sigma_{i}\left(cor\right)_{ij}\sigma_{j}
, where
\sigma
is the standard deviation of each measurement and
cor
the correlation matrix presented in Tables
3
and
4
. The exclusion region is obtained requiring
GfitterStatistics
that

\Delta\chi^{2}(M_{Z^{\prime}},\text{sh}\xi,\text{t}_{\beta},\text{t}_{\eta})=\chi^{2}(M_{Z^{\prime}},\text{sh}\xi,\text{t}_{\beta},\text{t}_{\eta})-\chi^{2}_{min}\gtrsim 4.7,

(55)

which corresponds to an exclusion at the
68\%
C.L. for four degrees of freedom. The quantity
\chi^{2}_{min}
denotes the absolute minimum value of the
\chi^{2}
function, obtained by comparing the SM predictions to the corresponding measurements.

Real-World Evidence for the Impact of Minimal Disease Activity on Quality of Life in Atopic Dermatitis: An Interim Analysis of the Prospective, Multicenter, Non-Interventional UP-TAINED Study
Authors: Felix Lauffer, Boris Bauer, Katharina Kreutzer, Knut Schäkel, Uwe Schwichtenberg, Johannes Hockmann, Andreas Pinter, Fatima Abousamra, Melanie Huber, Stephan Weidinger
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionMinimal disease activity (MDA) has recently been introduced as comprehensive treatment target in atopic dermatitis (AD), integrating both clinician- and patient-reported outcomes. Evidence on the real-world feasibility and impact of achieving MDA on quality of life (QoL) remains limited. This analysis evaluated the association between MDA achievement and QoL outcomes in patients with moderate-to-severe AD receiving upadacitinib (UPA) in real-world clinical practice.MethodsThis interim analysis used data from the ongoing, prospective, multicenter, non-interventional UP-TAINED study (NCT05139836) in Germany. Analyses included all patients without treatment interruptions during the first 12 months (n = 259). Disease control was classified as optimal, moderate, or none. Optimal control (MDA) was defined as Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) ≤ 3 and Worst Pruritus Numeric Rating Scale (WP-NRS) ≤ 0/1; moderate control as EASI ≤ 3 with WP-NRS 2–3 or EASI > 3–7 with WP-NRS ≤ 3; and no control as EASI > 7 or WP-NRS > 3. QoL outcomes were assessed using validated patient-reported measures.ResultsMDA was achieved by 38.2% of patients at month 1 and by 42.1% at month 12. Those achieving MDA reported substantially greater improvements across all QoL measures compared with patients with moderate or no disease control. QoL outcomes were comparable between patients starting UPA at 15 mg or 30 mg. The most frequent adverse events were AD, acne, and corona virus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infection; the safety profile was consistent and no new safety signals were identified.ConclusionAchieving MDA was associated with markedly improved QoL in real-world AD management, supporting MDA as a feasible and clinically meaningful treatment goal in routine practice.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier, NCT05139836.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01855-2.

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease that affects about 10% of children and 2% to 3% of adults in Western countries, including Germany [1–3]. The disease is characterized by intense itch, dry and inflamed skin, and highly variable clinical presentation. Beyond visible skin lesions, AD imposes a substantial burden on patients’ daily lives, impacting sleep, emotional well-being, and work productivity. This cumulative burden results in markedly reduced quality of life (QoL) [4], particularly in patients with moderate-to-severe AD or involvement of visible and difficult-to-treat body areas such as the face, hands, and genitals [5, 6]. The overall burden of AD increases with disease severity, with more severe disease being associated with greater physical, emotional, and psychosocial impairment [7].

The therapeutic landscape of AD has evolved considerably in recent years. Alongside topical agents and conventional systemic therapies, targeted biologics and Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors now provide effective options for controlling inflammation and alleviating symptoms [8]. These advances have shifted therapeutic thinking from symptom relief toward defined treatment targets and long-term disease control. In parallel, the treat-to-target approach, which is already established in other chronic inflammatory diseases such as psoriatic arthritis (PsA) or rheumatoid arthritis [9, 10], has gained importance in AD management.

In 2020, an international expert group introduced the AHEAD (Aiming High in Eczema/Atopic Dermatitis) recommendations, proposing a treat-to-target framework for AD [11]. The AHEAD approach emphasizes patient-centered treatment goals and defines minimal disease activity (MDA) as a preferred target. MDA is achieved when at least one optimal clinician-reported outcome (ClinRO) and one optimal patient-reported outcome (PRO) are met concurrently. This combined definition reflects both objective disease control and the patient’s perception of symptom relief and has been shown to correlate strongly with improvements in health-related QoL and treatment satisfaction [12].

Building on this concept, in the phase III/IV head-to-head LEVEL-UP trial, Silverberg and colleagues demonstrated that patients who achieved MDA experienced greater improvements in QoL and daily functioning than those achieving only one of these targets [13]. These findings support the clinical relevance of MDA as an integrated outcome that captures both physician- and patient-assessed aspects of disease control. However, most evidence supporting MDA to date derives from controlled trial settings, while real-word data on the feasibility and relevance of achieving MDA in everyday clinical practice remain limited.

The UP-TAINED study is an ongoing real-world study conducted in Germany, designed to evaluate the effectiveness, disease control, and safety of upadacitinib (UPA) in patients with moderate-to-severe AD. UPA is a selective JAK inhibitor with preferential activity for JAK1, while showing minimal activity against JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2). It has demonstrated rapid and sustained efficacy in reducing AD signs and symptoms in randomized controlled trials [14–16]. Interim results from the UP-TAINED study after 1 year of treatment have previously been reported [17].

The present analysis focuses on the relationship between achieving treatment targets, including MDA, and QoL outcomes during the first year of the UP-TAINED study. Specifically, this interim analysis evaluates data up to month 12, when sufficient patient numbers were available for analysis. The aim was to determine whether achievement of optimal or moderate disease control (versus no disease control), as defined by the AHEAD MDA framework, is associated with improvements in QoL and patient-reported well-being in real-world clinical practice.

Methods

Study Design

UP-TAINED is an ongoing, prospective, multicenter, non-interventional study conducted in patients aged ≥ 12 years with moderate-to-severe AD, who are treated with UPA 15 mg or 30 mg once daily according to the local labeling and practice in Germany (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05139836). Information on participating sites can be found at https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05139836#contacts-and-locations. The full study methodology has been described in detail previously [17]; key methodological elements relevant to the present analysis are summarized here briefly for completeness. In total, 10 study visits are scheduled: at baseline, at 1 and 3 months, and then every 3 months for 2 years.

Although this was a 2-year interim analysis, data presented here are limited to the first 12 months (visit 6, V6) because of the small number of patients with available 2-year follow-up at the time of analysis, reflecting the ongoing nature of patient enrollment and follow-up. The analysis included all patients meeting the following criteria: (1) participated in the study for at least 12 months, defined as attendance at V1 and V6 or any subsequent visit, or (2) permanently discontinued the study before V6; and in both cases, received uninterrupted upadacitinib during the treatment period.

Ethical approval

According to the requirements for non-interventional observational studies, no additional diagnostic or monitoring procedures or measurements that would not be feasible for use in routine clinical care were done in this study. Study-related monitoring, if applicable, was permitted by the study sites/investigators, including audits, independent ethics committee or independent review board appraisal, and regulatory inspection(s). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964 and its later amendments. Ethics committee approval was obtained by the medical faculty in Kiel, Germany, on November 1, 2021 (D 598/21). All participants (or, for adolescent participants, their legal guardians) provided written informed consent prior to enrollment in the UP-TAINED study).

Interim Analysis of UP-TAINED

In accordance with the AHEAD recommendations, several ClinROs and PROs were assessed in the UP-TAINED study. For the present analysis, we focused on the following measures: the Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) and Worst Pruritus Numeric Rating Scale (WP-NRS) to define disease control, and the Children’s (12–16 years) Dermatology Life Quality Index/Dermatology Life Quality Index (cDLQI/DLQI), the Sleep Loss Item Visual Analogue Scale (Sleep VAS) from the Score Atopic Dermatitis (SCORAD), the two subscales Skin Pain NRS and the Skin Cracking NRS from the Atopic Dermatitis Symptom Scale (ADerm-SS) [18], the 6-Item Stigmatization Scale, and the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS-A/D) as key QoL outcomes. All measures were assessed at each study visit, except for the HADS-A/D, which was collected at baseline, month 12 (V6), and month 24 (V10). Permission to use the EASI, cDLQI/DLQI, SCORAD, WP-NRS, and HADS-A/D in this study was obtained from the respective copyright holders. The 6-Item Stigmatization Scale is freely available for use [19].

For each individual ClinRO and PRO measure, patients were stratified into three mutually exclusive response groups: achievement of the “optimal treatment target,” “moderate treatment target,” or “neither target.” Definitions of optimal and moderate treatment targets followed the AHEAD recommendations [11].

According to these recommendations, MDA is defined as the concurrent achievement of at least one optimal ClinRO and one optimal PRO. In the present analysis, the MDA was defined as the simultaneous achievement of an optimal EASI (≤ 3) and an optimal WP-NRS (≤ 0/1), as outlined in Table 1. Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used for quantitative data, and frequency distributions for qualitative and categorical variables. Percentages were based on the number of non-missing observations (observed analysis). Analyses were conducted on the full analysis set, which included all patients who had no treatment breaks during the first 12 months and either (a) attended both baseline (V1) and a visit at month 12 (V6 or later), or (b) discontinued the study permanently before V6 while receiving continuous UPA at least once. Analyses of safety data were conducted on all patients who received UPA at least once.

Analyses of patient outcomes were stratified according to disease control status (MDA, moderate, or no disease control), achievement of optimal treatment targets (EASI, WP-NRS), and UPA dose (15 mg or 30 mg). Descriptive and inferential statistical methods were applied. For categorial variables, chi-square tests were used to assess differences in PRO outcomes for the following pairwise comparisons: optimal versus moderate, optimal versus neither, and moderate versus neither target. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Results are reported by observed cases with no imputation.

Results

Study Population

A total of 259 patients were included in this interim analysis (data cutoff October 31, 2024). All patients received continuous UPA treatment for up to 12 months without treatment interruptions. Baseline data was available for all 259 patients, and at month 12 (V6), data was available for 208 patients.

Data was missing for 51 patients at V6; of these, 15 had no documented month 12 visit, and 36 patients were classified as dropouts before month 12. Reasons for discontinuation included adverse events (AEs) (n = 11), patient request (n = 10), discontinuation of UPA treatment (n = 7), relocation (n = 1), no further information available (n = 5), and other reasons (n = 2).

At baseline, the mean (SD) age of patients was 40.8 (15.8) years, 52.5% were male, and the mean (SD) disease duration since diagnosis was 23.8 (15.9) years. Overall, 86% of the patients had received some AD therapy within the previous 6 months, and approximately one-third (32.8%) had received previous systemic treatment, including dupilumab (15.8%) or systemic corticosteroids (17.0%). Effectiveness

Treatment Response

A rapid treatment response was observed, with substantial proportions of patients achieving EASI ≤ 3, WP-NRS 0/1, and MDA as early as month 1 (Fig. 1). At month 1, 63.4% of patients achieved an EASI ≤ 3 and 52.5% achieved WP-NRS ≤ 0/1. At month 12, the proportion increased to 72.1% for EASI ≤ 3, while remaining similar for WP-NRS ≤ 0/1 (49.0%). Across all time points, achievement rates were consistently higher for the physician-assessed EASI compared with the patient-reported WP-NRS. Combined target achievement (MDA) remained lower than for each single measure.

Similar improvements over time were also observed for PROs. The proportion of patients achieving DLQI ≤ 0/1 increased from 36.9% at month 1 to 43.6% at month 12. Mean SCORAD Sleep VAS scores decreased from 5.7 at baseline to 1.6 at month 1 and 1.5 at month 12. Mean ADerm-SS skin pain scores improved from 5.0 to 1.4 at month 1 and month 12, while skin cracking scores declined from 4.6 to 1.3 and 1.1, respectively. Mean scores of the 6-Item Stigmatization Scale improved from 5.6 at baseline to 2.8 at month 1 and 1.7 at month 12. Patients who achieved MDA consistently reported better outcomes across all QoL domains compared with those who achieved only one optimal target or none. At month 12, patient-reported QoL outcomes were closely related to the level of disease control. Another 57-year-old female patient had elevated blood creatine phosphokinase MB (muscle-brain type), which was assessed as likely related to UPA. There was one case of thrombosed hemorrhoids in a 31-year-old female patient, which was considered unlikely to be related (Table 3).

Discussion

This interim analysis of the ongoing UP-TAINED study provides real-world evidence that achievement of MDA, defined as concurrent EASI ≤ 3 and WP-NRS ≤ 0/1, is strongly associated with improvements in patient-reported QoL in patients with moderate-to-severe AD receiving UPA. Patients who achieved MDA consistently reported greater improvements across all QoL domains compared with those who achieved only one of these targets or none, confirming the clinical relevance of combining clinician- and patient-reported measures to define disease control.

These real-world findings align with observations from controlled clinical studies, such as the LEVEL-UP trial [13], in which MDA achievement predicted greater improvements in QoL and daily functioning than single-target achievement. Similar patterns have been reported in other datasets, including the TARGET-DERM AD registry [20], where concurrent achievement of optimal itch relief and skin clearance was associated with superior patient outcomes and treatment satisfaction. Consistent with these results, the global real-world AD-VISE study [21] evaluated adults and adolescents with AD treated with UPA 15 mg or 30 mg in real-world practice. MDA was achieved by about 30% of patients as early as month 2 and maintained through month 12, together with meaningful improvements in sleep and QoL.

Improvements in stigmatization were also observed among patients classified as having “no disease control.” This is not unexpected, as this category reflects failure to meet the strict combined EASI ≤ 3 and WP-NRS ≤ 0/1 thresholds rather than complete absence of clinical improvement. Patients in this group may still experience partial reductions in visible skin inflammation or psychosocial burden, which can lower stigmatization scores. Regression to the mean and decreasing sample sizes over time may have further contributed to this pattern.

The observed correlation between MDA and improved QoL underscores the importance of treatment strategies that aim beyond skin clearance alone. In AD, symptom domains such as itch, sleep disturbance, pain, and emotional distress contribute substantially to overall burden, and the combined achievement of both ClinRO and PRO targets more accurately reflects patient well-being than either measure alone [12]. This approach parallels treat-to-target strategies established in other chronic inflammatory diseases, such as psoriasis, PsA, and rheumatoid arthritis, where setting clearly defined treatment goals has been associated with improved long-term outcomes and patient satisfaction [22–25]. The MDA framework for AD was developed in this context, adapting the treat-to-target principle to capture both clinical and patient-reported aspects of disease control.

Notably, MDA was achieved by 38.2% of patients at month 1 and by 42.1% at month 12, suggesting that early disease control with UPA can be achieved and maintained under real-world conditions. While MDA rates were lower than those observed for single measures (EASI or WP-NRS), this finding serves to reinforce that comprehensive disease control, requiring concurrent achievement of both physician- and patient-reported targets, is a more ambitious yet clinically meaningful treatment goal.

Comparable QoL improvements were observed in patients who started UPA at 15 mg or 30 mg, with no significant baseline differences between these groups. These findings indicate that both starting regimens can provide meaningful patient-reported benefits in real-world practice. Although disease severity is expected to guide dose selection, an earlier UP-TAINED analysis [26] found no clear relationship between baseline severity and prescribed dose, indicating that additional factors may influence dosing decisions in clinical practice. However, these comparisons are exploratory and were not adjusted for baseline disease severity or treatment history.

The observed treatment responses in this subanalysis were consistent with those reported in the larger UP-TAINED effectiveness analysis [17], supporting the robustness of the real-world data and confirming sustained clinical and patient-reported improvements over 12 months of UPA treatment.

The safety profile observed in this analysis was consistent with previous clinical and real-world experience with UPA in AD [15, 17]. Most AEs were mild or moderate, and severe events were infrequent and generally unrelated to treatment. The most frequently reported AEs were AD (flare or worsening), acne, and COVID-19 infection, followed by nasopharyngitis and pruritus. An increased risk of major adverse cardiac events (MACE) and venous thromboembolism has been reported with another JAK inhibitor in patients with rheumatoid arthritis, leading to a class warning for JAK inhibitors. In the present study, such events were uncommon, which may reflect both the overall low cardiovascular risk profile of the AD population and appropriate patient selection in clinical practice. Overall, these findings support a favorable benefit–risk balance of UPA in the real-world management of moderate-to-severe AD.

Strengths and Limitations

The strengths of this study include its prospective, multicenter design and the comprehensive assessment of both ClinRO and PRO outcomes in a real-world setting using validated instruments. This interim analysis provides 1-year data on UPA treatment under routine clinical conditions in Germany. As the study is ongoing, data were analyzed as observed without imputation of missing data, with some patients not yet having reached later visits at the time of data cutoff. Consequently, missing data may have introduced bias and led to overestimation of response rates. Furthermore, the inclusion of only patients without treatment interruptions during the first 12 months introduces a selection bias, as patients who tolerated and adhered to UPA treatment were preferentially retained, potentially overestimating both MDA achievement rates and QoL improvements. Results were not adjusted for baseline disease severity, patient characteristics, or prior treatment history, which is consistent with the descriptive nature of non-interventional studies but limits the ability to isolate the effect of treatment from pre-existing differences between groups. Furthermore, comparisons between UPA dose groups reflect only the initial prescribed dose and do not account for possible dose adjustments during follow-up. The lack of randomization and of a comparator group limits the ability to draw causal conclusions regarding treatment effects. In addition, the study was conducted in a German population, and treatment patterns, access, or patient characteristics may differ in other countries, which could affect the generalizability of the findings.

Conclusions

Overall, these interim findings provide real-world support for the MDA framework as a feasible and clinically meaningful treatment goal in AD. Achieving MDA was associated with better QoL outcomes, emphasizing that this comprehensive treatment target should be pursued in clinical practice. Continued follow-up of the UP-TAINED cohort will help clarify the long-term durability and predictive value of MDA in real-world disease management.

Callan-Symanzik-like equation in information theory
Mojtaba Shahbazi , Mehdi Sadeghi Department of Physics, Faculty of Basic Sciences, Ayatollah Boroujerdi University, Boroujerd, Iran

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the jumps in the complexity growth rate (CGR) through the complexity=anything proposal. It is revealed that bulk fields are in charge of the height and the location of these jumps. To put it accurately, their counterparts are divergence of energy momentum tensor, shear viscosity and the Weyl anomaly of the boundary theory. The behavior of CGR around these jumps manifests a critical behavior and critical exponents. Moreover, it is figured out that CGR satisfies a Callan-Symanzik-like equation around these jumps. This equation suggests that the speed of information processing could be changed by the scale of energy. Put it another way, by change of the scale of energy one could turn the classical computers into faster processor computers even faster than our current quantum computers.

Introduction

The AdS/CFT duality establishes a profound correspondence between a gravitational theory in the bulk of anti-de Sitter (AdS) space and a conformal field theory (CFT) on its boundary
[
1
]
. This holographic principle provides a powerful dictionary, allowing quantities that are computationally intractable in one theory—particularly in strongly coupled regimes—to be studied through their dual counterparts in the other. A striking example arises in the context of black hole physics, where the growth of Einstein-Rosen bridge volume in the bulk poses a conceptual puzzle: if a black hole is in thermodynamic equilibrium, and its dual boundary theory is likewise equilibrated, what physical meaning should be ascribed to this increasing volume? Recent insights suggest that this geometric evolution corresponds to the computational complexity of the boundary theory, specifically the minimal number of fundamental operations required to prepare a target quantum state from a given reference state
[
2
]
.

In holography, several conjectures relate bulk geometric quantities to boundary complexity. Among these proposals three of them have been widely explored, the complexity=volume (CV) in which the volume of Einstein-Rosen bridges are corresponded to the complexity of the boundary theory,
[
3
]
, complexity=action (CA), relates the action of the bulk theory restricted to the Wheeler-DeWitt patch to the complexity
[
4
]
and complexity=volume (CV2.0) corresponds the spacetime volume of the bulk restricted to the Wheeler-DeWitt patch to the complexity
[
5
]
. More recently, a generalized framework—dubbed complexity=anything—has emerged, positing that arbitrary bulk volumes can encode boundary complexity, offering a broader and more flexible perspective
[
6
]
. Intriguingly, the complexity growth rate (CGR) under this proposal exhibits distinct branches, which some interpretations identify them as signatures of phase transitions
[
7
,
8
]
.

This work investigates the physical origins of these jumps, addressing two key questions: (1) What determines their heights? (2) What controls their temporal locations? We demonstrate that the jumps positions are dynamically influenced by bulk fields, which, via the fluid-gravity duality, map to specific boundary quantities: the energy-momentum tensor, Weyl anomaly
[
9
]
and shear viscosity
[
10
]
. Consequently, the location of these transitions depends on the divergence of the boundary fluid’s energy-momentum tensor (indicating energy injection), its shear viscosity, and its anomalous conformal behavior. Meanwhile, the jump amplitudes are linked to the renormalization group (RG) flow of the boundary theory.

Remarkably, the CGR transitions exhibit critical phenomena, characterized by scaling behavior and universality. Near these critical points, the system obeys a Callan-Symanzik-like equation, where the beta function is expressed in terms of the generalization parameter from the complexity=anything proposal, alongside an anomalous dimension. This criticality further displays universal features, independent of the specific choice of the generalized parameter.

5
Conclusion

The AdS/CFT by corresponding a gravitational theory to a quantum theory could alleviate the perturbation calculation in strongly-coupled systems. Exploring the counterpart quantities in the AdS/CFT make it a strong technique to study the physical theories. Computational complexity as one of these quantities has been considered in holography and provided different proposal. Recently, a proposal known as complexity equals anything has been introduced in which the volume of a generalized surface is corresponded to the complexity of the boundary theory. Studying the complexity of various models through this proposal shows some branches in CGR. Moreover, these branches has been considered as a phase transition in CGR.

Because of the fact that the phase transition appeared in complexity equals anything, this suggests that the generalized surfaces could make the transition. One of the mechanism for the generalization is the Weyl squared tensor. Expanding the Weyl squared tensor in the Fefferman-Graham coordinate makes it to be reduced into three terms: divergence of the energy-momentum tensor, shear viscosity and the Weyl anomaly of the boundary theory. Then, the phase transition and the distance between the jumps is controlled by these three terms.

CGR in this way behaves as a function of the generalization parameter and the locations of the jumps. In other words, these two make the CGR to be changed. Considering the behavior of CGR around the phase transition reveals a scaling behavior and critical exponents. It means that the interpretation of the phase transition our of these jumps makes sense. However, CGR satisfies a Callan-Symanzik-like (CSL) equation in field theory in which a beta function is defined in terms of the generalization parameter and an anomalous dimension in terms of the scaling of CGR, which means that the critical behavior of CGR is universal regardless of the generalization parameter.

CSL equation for CGR suggests that in different scale of energy, the minimum required quantum gates is changed. In other words, the change in the scale of energy could change the speed of the processing of information. This recommends that one way to change the speed of the information processing is changing the scale of energy. Suppose that we prepare a quantum computer with a particular speed of processing. One could instead of working on the quantum gates to improve the the speed of processing, work on the scale of energy, put it another way, with a particular array of quantum gates it is enough to change the scale of energy to have fast processing. An interesting application of CSL would be turning the classical computers into a faster one and even faster than our current quantum computer by changing the scale of energy not by the gates or architecture of gates.

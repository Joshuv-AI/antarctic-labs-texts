Gibbs measure over the cone of vector-valued discrete measures
Luca Di Persio, Yuri Kondratiev, Viktorya Vardanyan

Abstract
We consider a gas whose each particle is characterised by a pair ( x , v x ) (x,v_{x}) with the position x ∈ ℝ d x\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and the velocity v x ∈ ℝ 0 d = ℝ d ∖ { 0 } v_{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\} . We define Gibbs measures on the cone of vector-valued measures and aim to prove their existence. We introduce the family of probability measures μ λ \mu_{\lambda} on the cone 𝕂 ⁡ ( ℝ d ) \mathbb{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . We define local Hamiltonian and partition functions for a positive, symmetric, bounded and measurable pair potential. Using those above, we define Gibbs’s measure as a solution to the Dobrushin-Lanford-Ruelle equation. In particular, we focus on the subset of tempered Gibbs measures. To prove the existence of the Gibbs measure, we show that the subset of tempered Gibbs measures is non-empty and relatively compact.



Keywords:
Interacting particle systems, Vector-valued Radon measures, DLR equation, Gibbs measure, Pair potential, Hamiltonian, Partition function, Local Gibbs specification

1  Introduction

A classical free gas is the simplest model in which the interactions between the atoms are entirely ignored, and the atoms are treated as classical particles. Poisson measures give the equilibrium states of such gases. The states of interacting gases are known as Gibbs measures, and Dobrushin, Lanford, and Ruelle introduced the existence problem of such measures. We aim to prove the existence of Gibbs measure on the aforementioned cone. We consider the case of a positive pair potential

We are going to restrict ourselves to a set

2.2    Measures on
{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})

We construct measures on
\mathbb{K}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
as the images under the reflection map
{\mathcal{R}}
of proper measures on the space
\Pi({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
. To this end, we start with the simplest examples related to Poisson measures.

Let us fix a non-atomic Radon measure
\lambda
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}
with following properties:

\lambda({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0})=\infty,

\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}}|v|^{n}\lambda(dv)<\infty.

As a concrete example of such measure, we will have in mind

\lambda(dv)=\frac{1}{|v|^{d}}e^{-|v|^{2}}dv.

This measure modifies the usual Maxwell distribution for velocities in statistical physics
by a singularity at
0
. A wider class of examples of such measures contains elements of the form

\lambda(dv)=\frac{1}{|v|^{\alpha}}e^{-|v|^{\beta}}dv,

with
\alpha\in[d,d+1),\beta>0
.

Denote by
m(dx)
the non-atomic Lebesgue measure on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
and introduce the measure

\sigma(dv,dx)=\lambda(dv)m(dx)

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
, which is called an intensity measure.
For any
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{B}}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
we have the following disjoint decomposition

\Gamma(\Lambda)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{n}(\Lambda),

where
\Gamma(\Lambda):=\{\zeta\in\Gamma({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}):\zeta\subset\Lambda\}
is the set of all configurations supported in
\Lambda
and
\Gamma^{n}(\Lambda):=\{\zeta\in\Gamma(\Lambda):|\zeta|=n\}
is the set of
n
-point configurations.
Lebesgue-Poisson measure on
\Gamma(\Lambda)
is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sigma^{(n)},

where
\sigma^{(n)}:=(\lambda\otimes m)^{\otimes n}\circ sym_{n}^{-1}
is the measure on
\Gamma^{n}(\Lambda)
. Poisson measure on
\Gamma(\Lambda)
is defined as
\pi_{\sigma}^{\Lambda}:=e^{-\sigma(\Lambda)}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}
.

There is a standard definition of a Poisson measure

\pi_{\sigma}

on
\Gamma({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
. Then we can proceed as follows. For any
\psi\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
(continuous functions with compact supports) define

<\psi,\zeta>=\sum_{(v,x)\in\zeta}\psi(v,x),\;\;\zeta\in\Gamma({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}).

The Poisson measure
\pi_{\sigma}
is defined via its Laplace transform

\int_{\Gamma({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}e^{<\psi,\zeta>}\pi_{\sigma}(d\zeta)=\exp{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}(e^{\psi(v,x)}-1)}\lambda(dv)m(dx).

As a consequence of this definition we have

\int_{\Gamma({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}<\psi,\zeta>\pi_{\sigma}(d\zeta)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\psi(v,x)\lambda(dv)m(dx),

and this relation can be extended to the case of any
\psi\in L^{1}(\lambda\otimes m)
.

The Poisson measure
\pi_{\sigma}
is concentrated on
\Pi({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, i.e.,

\pi_{\sigma}(\Pi({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}))=1.

To obtain measures on
{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, we use the pushforward of measures on
\Pi(R^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
under the mapping
{\mathcal{R}}
.

Denote
\mu_{\lambda}
the image measure on
{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
under the reflection map corresponding to
\pi_{\sigma}
. For

and
\psi\in C_{0}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, introduce a function

L_{h,\psi}:{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
via

L_{h,\psi}(\eta)=<h\otimes\psi,\eta>=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\psi(x)<h,\eta(dx)>=\sum_{x\in\tau(\eta)}\psi(x)<h,v_{x}>.

Then the definition of the Poisson measure implies

\int_{{\mathbb{K}}}e^{<h\otimes\psi,\eta>}\mu_{\lambda}(d\eta)=\exp(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}(e^{<h,v>\psi(x)}-1)\lambda(dv)m(dx)).

(1)

Denote

\Psi_{\lambda}^{h}(r):=\exp({\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{0}}(e^{<h,v>r}-1)\lambda(dv)}),\;\;r\in{\mathbb{R}},

then

\int_{{\mathbb{K}}}e^{<h\otimes\psi,\eta>}\mu_{\lambda}(d\eta)=e^{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\log(\Psi_{\lambda}^{h}(\psi(x)))m(dx)}.

(2)

The latter relation can be considered as the definition of the measure
\mu_{\lambda}
via its Laplace transform.

Let
\Lambda\subset B_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{0}^{d})
. We will consider the cone
\mathbb{K}(\Lambda)\in B_{c}(\mathbb{K}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}))
, which consists of discrete vector-valued measures with support
\Lambda
.
Let
\mu_{\lambda}
be a probability measure on

For
\eta\in{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
of the form
\eta=\sum_{x\in\tau(\eta)}v_{x}\delta_{x}

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}:\mathbb{K}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})\ni\eta\mapsto\eta_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{x\in\tau(\eta)\cap\Lambda}v_{x}\delta_{x}\in\mathbb{K}(\Lambda).

(3)

The measure on
\mathbb{K}(\Lambda)
is defined as

\mu_{\lambda}^{\Lambda}:=\mu_{\lambda}\circ\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{-1},

and is characterized via its Laplace transform similar to (

\int_{{\mathbb{K}}(\Lambda)}e^{<h\otimes\psi,\eta>}\mu_{\lambda}^{\Lambda}(d\eta)=\exp\bigg(\int_{\Lambda}(e^{<h,v>\psi(x)}-1)\lambda(dv)m(dx)\bigg).

(4)

Denote

\Psi_{\lambda}^{h}(r):=\exp({\int_{\Lambda}(e^{<h,v>r}-1)\lambda(dv)}),\;\;r\in{\mathbb{R}},

equivalently we will have

\int_{{\mathbb{K}}(\Lambda)}e^{<h\otimes\psi,\eta>}\mu_{\lambda}^{\Lambda}(d\eta)=e^{\int_{\Lambda}\log(1+\Psi_{\lambda}^{h}(\psi(x)))m(dx)}.

(5)

The random measure

Or

\int_{{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\prod_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}^{h,\psi}(\eta(\Lambda_{i}))\mu_{\lambda}(d\eta)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}L_{i}^{h,\psi}(\eta(\Lambda))\mu_{\lambda}(d\eta)

(6)

2.3   Pair potential and partition of the space
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}

In this subsection, we introduce pair potential and the conditions it satisfies. Moreover, we introduce the partition of
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
.

Assumption 2.1
.

Let

\phi:{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}

(8)

be positive, symmetric, bounded and measurable pair potential. Denote

0\leq\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{|\phi(x,y)|\}=:\|\phi\|_{\infty}<\infty.

We suppose
\phi
satisfies
Finite Range (FR)
condition, i.e.
\exists R\in(0,\infty)
such that

\phi(x,y)=0\hskip 9.24994ptif\hskip 9.24994pt|x-y|>R,

and
Repulsion Condition (RC)
, i.e.
\exists\delta>0
such that

A_{\delta}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\\
|x-y|\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\phi(x,y)>0.

The (FR) ensures that the potential energy is non-zero only when the particles are within a certain range of each other.
The (RC) condition ensures a repulsive force between particles when they are very close. It prevents the particles from collapsing into each other and ensures that they maintain a minimum distance from each other. The bound
||\phi||_{\infty}
indicates that the pair potential is uniformly bounded over all pairs of particles, implying that the interaction energy cannot become arbitrarily large.
Summing up, the given conditions guarantee that the pair potential
\phi
describes a physically reasonable interaction between particles, with a finite range and a repulsive component that prevents particles from collapsing into each other.

We are going to take a partition of
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
as in
[
8
]
.
We consider the cubes indexed by
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\mathcal{Q}_{k}:=[-\frac{1}{2}g,\frac{1}{2}g)^{d}+gk\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},

with parameter
g:=\delta/\sqrt{d}
, where
\delta>0
is such that the repulsion condition (RC) holds. These cubs form the partition of
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
.
Cubes are centered at the point
gk
with edge length
g>0
, Lebesgue volume
m(\mathcal{Q}_{k})=g^{d}
and diameter

diam(\mathcal{Q}_{k}):=\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}|x-y|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}=\delta,

which means
\phi(x,y)\geq A_{\delta}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}
.
We define also the family of neighbour cubes of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
for each

k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denoted by

\partial_{\delta}^{\phi}k:=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\{k\}\ |\ \exists x\in\mathcal{Q}_{k},\exists y\in\mathcal{Q}_{j}:\ \phi(x,y)\neq 0\}.

(9)

The number of such neighbour cubes for every
\mathcal{Q}_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be estimated by

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\partial_{\delta}^{\phi}k\leq m_{\delta}^{\phi},

(10)

where

m_{\delta}^{\phi}:=v_{d}d^{d/2}[R/\delta+1]^{d}\hskip 9.24994ptwith\hskip 9.24994ptv_{d}:=\dfrac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}

is an interaction parameter.

To each index set
\mathcal{K}\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
there corresponds

\Lambda_{\mathcal{K}}:=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{k}\in B({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}),

by
\mathcal{Q}_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
is denoted the family of all such domains.

For
\Lambda\in B({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
we define

\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}:=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\ |\ \mathcal{Q}_{j}\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing\},

with
|\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}|
number of cubes
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
having non-void intersection with
\Lambda
and

|\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}|<\infty,\ \forall\Lambda\in B_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}).

3  Local Gibbs specification

3.1   Hamiltonian and partition function

We define local Hamiltonian and partition function, using the assumption on pair potential and partition introduced above, we obtain bounds for them.

Definition 3.1
.

For each
\eta=\sum_{x\in\tau(\eta)}v_{x}\delta_{x}
,
\xi=\sum_{y\in\tau(\xi)}v_{y}\delta_{y}
and
\Lambda\in B_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, relative energy (Hamiltonian) is given as

H_{\Lambda}(\eta|\xi):=\int_{\Lambda}\int_{\Lambda}\phi(x,y)\eta(dx)\eta(dy)+2\int_{\Lambda^{c}}\int_{\Lambda}\phi(x,y)\eta(dx)\xi(dy)

(11)

or equivalently

H_{\Lambda}(\eta|\xi):=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in\tau(\eta)\cap\Lambda}\phi(x,x^{\prime})(v_{x},v_{x^{\prime}})+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\tau(\eta)\cap\Lambda\\
y\in\tau(\xi)\cap\Lambda^{c}\end{subarray}}\phi(x,y)(v_{x},v_{y}).

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
\eta,\xi\in{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and
\Lambda\in B_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
the relative energy is finite, i.e.,

|H_{\Lambda}(\eta|\xi)|<\infty
.

Proof.

Considering

|H_{\Lambda}(\eta|\xi)|\leq\eta(\Lambda)\eta(\Lambda)||\phi||_{\infty}+2\eta(\Lambda)\xi(\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda})||\phi||_{\infty},

where

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}:=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\{\mathcal{Q}_{k}\ |\ \partial_{\delta}^{\phi}k\cap\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}\neq\varnothing\}\cap\Lambda^{c}\in B_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})

and having
\eta,\xi\in{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, the claim follows.
∎

By the following lemma we aim show the lower bound for local Hamiltonian.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold, then for each
\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{K}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
\Lambda\in B_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

H_{\Lambda}(\eta|\xi)\geq A\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\eta_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{Q}_{j})^{2}.

(12)

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

H_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}(\eta|\xi)\geq A\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\eta(x)^{2},

(13)

when
\xi=0
,

H_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}(\eta_{k}|0)\geq A\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\eta(x)^{2}.

(14)

Proof.

We start with the definition of the Hamiltonian
H_{\Lambda}(\eta\mid\xi)
for the pair potential
\phi
:

\displaystyle H_{\Lambda}(\eta\mid\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda}\phi(x-y)\eta(x)\eta(y)+\sum_{x\in\Lambda,y\notin\Lambda}\phi(x-y)\eta(x)\xi(y)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\notin\Lambda}\phi(x-y)\xi(x)\xi(y).

Using the finite range (FR) and repulsion (RC) conditions on
\phi
, we can bound the first term above as follows

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda}\phi(x-y)\eta(x)\eta(y)\geq A\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathcal{Q}_{j}}\eta(x)\eta(y).

Since
\phi
is a positive pair potential, the cross terms involving
\xi
are non-negative and thus do not affect the inequality. Therefore, we have

H_{\Lambda}(\eta\mid\xi)\geq A\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\eta_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{Q}_{j})^{2}.

For the second inequality, we consider the local Hamiltonian
H_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}(\eta\mid\xi)

H_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}(\eta\mid\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\phi(x-y)\eta(x)\eta(y)+\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{k},y\notin\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\phi(x-y)\eta(x)\xi(y).

Again, using the positivity of
\phi
, we can ignore the cross terms and obtain:

H_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}(\eta\mid\xi)\geq A\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\eta(x)^{2}.

When
\xi=0
, the cross terms vanish, and we are left with

H_{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}(\eta_{k}\mid 0)\geq A\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}}\eta(x)^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

For each
\xi\in{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and
\Lambda\in B_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})

0<Z_{\Lambda}(\xi)<\infty.

For
\phi\geq 0

Proof.

Since

H_{\Lambda}(\eta|\xi)
is well-defined and finite for all

4  Existence of Gibbs measures

The existence of a Gibbs measure
\gamma

We have the following Corollary which will be used to prove the main result.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold, fix some order generating sequence
\{\Lambda_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

\subset\mathcal{Q}_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
. Then, for each boundary condition
\xi\in\mathbb{K}^{t}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}))
a subsequence of
\{\pi_{\Lambda_{N}}(\cdot\mid\xi)\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
\mathcal{Q}
-locally to a probability measure
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{K}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})))
, that means for all
B\in\mathcal{B_{\mathcal{Q}}}(\mathbb{K}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})),

\pi_{\Lambda_{N}}(B|\xi)\to\gamma(B)\hskip 9.24994ptas\hskip 9.24994ptN\to\infty.

(39)

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Corollary
4.10
we have that subsequence of
\{\pi_{\Lambda_{N}}(\cdot|\xi)\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges
\mathcal{Q}
-locally to a probability measure
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{K}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})))
, where
\Lambda_{N}\nearrow{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
,
\Lambda_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
is fixed order generating sequence and
\xi\in\mathbb{K}^{t}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}))
is a boundary condition. By DLR property, we can derive that
\gamma
is indeed a Gibbs measure:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\pi_{\Lambda}(B|\eta)\gamma(d\eta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{K}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})}\pi_{\Lambda}(B|\eta)\pi_{\Lambda_{N}}(d\eta|\xi)

(40)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\pi_{\Lambda_{N}}(B|\xi)=\gamma(B).

We have used (
39
) and consistency property in (
40
).

For the relative compactness of
G^{t}(\phi)
, we use the fact that the set of Gibbs measures is tight and closed under the weak topology. Since the space of probability measures on a Polish space is itself Polish under the weak topology, Prokhorov’s theorem ensures that tightness implies relative compactness.
Thus, we conclude that
G^{t}(\phi)
is non-empty and relatively compact in the topology
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{Q}}
.
∎

5  Conclusion

We have introduced the problem of Gibbs measures on the cone of vector-valued Radon measures. We considered positive pair potential and constructed Gibbs measure using the DLR equilibrium equation and a measure
\mu_{\lambda}
. Then, we constrained it to a set of tempered Gibbs measures. We constructed a measure
\gamma
as a limit of local specification kernels and proved that the limit measure is indeed Gibbs. Gibbs measure has wide applications in statistical mechanics, physics, and specific areas of biology, particularly in modelling complex biological systems.

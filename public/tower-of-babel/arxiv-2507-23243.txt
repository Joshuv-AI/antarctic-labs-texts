Disorder driven crossover between anomalous Hall regimes in Fe 3 GaTe 2 Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sang-Eon Lee 1,5 , Minkyu Park 3 , W. Kice Brown 4 , Vadym Kulichenko 1 , Yan Xin 1 , S. H. Rhim 6 , Chanyong Hwang 3 , Jaeyong Kim 5 , Gregory T. McCandless 4 , Julia Y. Chan 4 , Luis Balicas 1,2

Abstract
The large anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) of the Fe 3 (Ge,Ga)Te 2 compounds has attracted considerable attention. Here, we expose the intrinsic nature of AHC in Fe 3 GaTe 2 crystals characterized by high conductivities, which show disorder-independent AHC with a pronounced value σ x ​ y c ≈ \sigma_{xy}^{\text{c}}\approx 420 Ω − 1 \Omega^{-1} cm -1 . In the low conductivity regime, we observe the scaling relation σ x ​ y ∝ σ x ​ x 1.6 \sigma_{xy}\propto\sigma_{xx}^{1.6} , which crosses over to σ x ​ y ≃ σ x ​ y c \sigma_{xy}\simeq\sigma_{xy}^{\text{c}} as σ x ​ x \sigma_{xx} increases. Disorder in low-conductivity crystals is confirmed by the broadening of a first-order transition between ferromagnetism and the ferrimagnetic ground state. Through density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we reveal that the dominant sources of Berry curvature are located a few hundred meV below the Fermi energy around the Γ \Gamma -point. Therefore, Fe 3 GaTe 2 clearly exposes the disorder-induced crossover among distinct AHC regimes, previously inferred from measurements on different ferromagnets located in either side of the crossover region.

From the perspective of the fundamental understanding of anomalous Hall effect, clear experimental evidence for the crossover from the dirty to the moderately dirty regime is still lacking. The main challenge is the difficulty in tuning solely the level of disorder in the material while maintaining its chemical composition. Various studies on the scaling behavior of the anomalous Hall effect rely on compositional adjustments to achieve wide ranges of conductivities

The investigation on the anomalous Hall effect in Fe thin films evaded this issue by modulating their mobility by adjusting the thickness of the films instead of changing their chemical composition

In a simple model, the large contribution of Berry curvature is explained by the specific location of the Fermi level, which is located within the gap or at the band edges between the conduction and valence bands
Onoda et al. 2006
;
Onoda et al. 2008

K

K

K

H

However, if the Berry curvature distribution results from many bands, i.e. more than two, it can spread over a broad range of energies, which is precisely the result of our calculations around the
\Gamma

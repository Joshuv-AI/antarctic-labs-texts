TacRefineNet: Tactile-Only Grasp Refinement Between Arbitrary In-Hand Object Poses Thanks: All authors are with Xiaomi Robotics. Corresponding author: Shuaijun Wang, email:wukongwoong@gmail.com.
Shuaijun Wang, Haoran Zhou, Diyun Xiang, Yangwei You

Abstract
Despite progress in both traditional dexterous grasping pipelines and recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) approaches, the grasp execution stage remains prone to pose inaccuracies, especially in long-horizon tasks, which undermines overall performance. To address this “last-mile” challenge, we propose TacRefineNet, a tactile-only framework that achieves fine in-hand pose refinement of known objects in arbitrary target poses using multi-finger fingertip sensing. Our method iteratively adjusts the end-effector pose based on tactile feedback, aligning the object to the desired configuration. We design a multi-branch policy network that fuses tactile inputs from multiple fingers along with proprioception to predict precise control updates. To train this policy, we combine large-scale simulated data from a physics-based tactile model in MuJoCo with real-world data collected from a physical system. Comparative experiments show that pretraining on simulated data and fine-tuning with a small amount of real data significantly improves performance over simulation-only training. Extensive real-world experiments validate the method’s effectiveness, achieving millimeter-level grasp accuracy using only tactile input. To our knowledge, this is the first method to enable arbitrary in-hand pose refinement via multi-finger tactile sensing alone. To this end, we integrate custom-designed piezoresistive tactile sensors into a five-fingered dexterous hand with 11 degrees of freedom (DoF). Each fingertip is equipped with an 11×9 taxel array that measures normal contact forces. These high-resolution tactile signals are formatted into tactile images, enabling the use of off-the-shelf visual encoders for feature extraction. Guided by the geometry and size of the target objects, our approach primarily leverages three fingers and integrates their tactile signals to jointly estimate the adjustments required for refining the in-hand pose.

However, the dexterous hand is underactuated, with only 6 active DoFs available for control. To overcome this limitation, we exploit external dexterity through iterative regrasping: tactile feedback from the fingertips is used to iteratively update the wrist pose, gradually reducing the misalignment between the current and target configurations. In this way, the system refines the grasp until convergence to the desired pose.

Building upon this hardware-software loop, we design TacRefineNet, a novel multi-branch policy network that takes as input the target tactile image, the current tactile image, and the proprioceptive state, and outputs the corrective wrist motion needed for precise in-hand refinement.
To train the policy network, we construct a large-scale dataset comprising tactile, proprioceptive, and action data collected from simulation and physical world. Simulation facilitates efficient and scalable data generation using a physics-based tactile model in MuJoCo, while real-world data ensures physical realism. Comparative experiments demonstrate that pretraining on simulated data followed by fine-tuning with a small real-world dataset significantly outperforms simulation-only training, effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap.

To achieve generalization to arbitrary target in-hand poses, we employ a cross-combination training scheme where both current and target tactile images are randomly sampled from the dataset. This enables the policy to perform precise in-hand regrasping toward any tactile-specified goal.
In real-world experiments, our method successfully accomplishes fine in-hand manipulation of known objects from arbitrary initial poses with millimeter-level accuracy. The object is initially placed arbitrarily in the hand, and a target tactile image is provided. Using only tactile feedback, the policy executes fine adjustments until the desired pose is achieved.

In summary, this work presents TacRefineNet, a novel approach for target-driven in-hand regrasping of known objects using only multi-finger fingertip tactile sensing. We build a large-scale multimodal dataset from simulation and real robots and train a unified policy network that achieves high-precision in-hand manipulation. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first method that performs arbitrary in-hand pose adjustment for specific objects relying solely on tactile feedback. Our contributions are:

•

We present
TacRefineNet
, the first end-to-end tactile-only framework that achieves millimeter-level accuracy in 6-DoF in-hand pose refinement using multi-finger tactile feedback.

•

We design a multi-branch fusion policy network that leverages tactile signals from multiple fingers, enabling arbitrary in-hand pose refinement without the need for per-pose retraining.

•

The proposed policy demonstrates generalization to some extent to unseen object instances within similar categories.

III
Method

III-A

Method Overview

We propose
TacRefineNet
, a tactile-driven fine adjustment framework that operates at the final execution stage of dexterous grasping. While prior methods can guide a robotic hand to an initial grasp configuration, they often struggle to achieve accurate in-hand object poses, particularly for flat or planar objects due to limitations in perception, planning, and actuation.
To overcome these challenges, TacRefineNet introduces an iterative tactile-based grasp refinement strategy. The dexterous hand first performs a coarse grasp guided by upstream algorithms. Upon establishing contact, high-resolution tactile signals are captured from the fingertips. These tactile readings are then processed by TacRefineNet, which estimates a 6D pose increment for wrist pose.
The hand reopens, moves to the updated pose, and attempts the grasp again. This tactile-feedback loop is repeated iteratively, allowing the hand to gradually converge toward the target in-hand pose.
The target pose is provided via a one-shot human demonstration, where the user manually places the object in the desired final pose within the hand. The resulting tactile image is recorded and used as a reference input for the policy network. Through this formulation, TacRefineNet enables precise, contact-driven in-hand pose adjustment without relying on external vision or object models.

As shown in Fig.
2
, the framework encompasses data collection, policy training, and deployment. By acquiring tactile-proprioception-action data in both simulation and the real world and employing cross-combination training for TacRefineNet, the learned policy enables precise in-hand adjustment of objects from arbitrary initial poses to arbitrary target poses without additional training.

\Delta\mathbf{x}=\pi\left(\left\{\mathbf{I}_{i}^{\text{curr}},\mathbf{I}_{i}^{\text{target}}\right\}_{i=1}^{N},\mathbf{q}^{\text{curr}}\right)

(1)

where

\mathbf{q}^{\text{curr}}
denotes the current joint configuration of the hand, and the output

represents the 6-DoF wrist pose increment (3D translation and 3D rotation).

III-B

Tactile Sensor and Its Simulation

This section introduces the tactile sensing hardware and its simulation counterpart.
We utilize a piezoresistive tactile sensor array integrated into an 11-DoF dexterous hand. As can be seen in Fig.
3
. Each fingertip sensor consists of an
11\times 9
taxel grid, where each taxel measures normal contact force. The physical spacing between the taxels on the real tactile fingertip is approximately 1.1 mm. The raw taxel outputs are transformed into tactile images, enabling the use of vision-based encoders for feature extraction.

We develop a physics-based tactile sensor simulation in MuJoCo to enable scalable data generation. The sensor is modeled with spherical contact points arranged according to the resolution and spacing of the real device. To account for the rubber surface that induces elastic interactions, each contact point is connected to the sensor via an elastic joint and instrumented with a contact sensor. During contact, these elastic points first engage with the object and then press back against the sensor surface as forces increase, generating tactile responses. Aggregating signals across all regions yields raw tactile images. This simulation closely reproduces real sensor behavior while supporting efficient, low-cost, and large-scale data collection.

III-C

Data Collection in Simulation and the Real World

Based on the tactile systems described above, we constructed datasets both in simulation and in the real world, with the data collection process shown in Fig.
4
.

In simulation, we construct the dataset by systematically exploring the hand’s pose space across its various degrees of freedom, with the sampling range constrained by predefined upper and lower bounds. During our experiments, we observed that, due to the geometric constraints of the objects, tactile images along the x-axis often overlap with those along the roll axis, and images along the yaw axis frequently coincide with those of other axes. To mitigate this redundancy, we sample only four dimensions: pitch, roll, y, and z.
At each sampled pose, the hand’s pose
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
(after inward finger closure), the hand’s joint positions
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
, and the tactile images
\mathbf{I}
are recorded and stored in the dataset
D_{\text{sim}}
.

For real-world data collection, we first replicate the same procedure used in simulation. To improve sampling efficiency in the real world, we increase the step size along each end-effector dimension, thereby reducing the total number of sampled points. Next, we filter out dexterous hand wrist poses that are infeasible in practice (e.g., those that would collide with the environment). Finally, the feasible poses recorded in the simulated dataset are re-sampled on the real robot to construct the real-world dataset
D_{\text{real}}
.

III-D

Learning Fine-Grained Grasp Adjustment Policy

III-D
1
TacRefineNet Model Architecture

TacRefineNet is a key component of the fine grasp adjustment framework proposed in this paper. The network takes tactile-proprioceptive inputs at each iteration and outputs end-effector adjustment actions for the dexterous hand wrist, necessitating a carefully designed architecture. In this work, the tactile information from each fingertip is processed as a grayscale tactile image. This transformation facilitates the use of off-the-shelf vision-based encoders for tactile feature extraction.

These approaches yield implicit tactile features. To enhance the network’s spatial awareness, we apply positional encoding to the extracted features. We then fuse features from the tactile branches of all fingers, and further incorporate joint information from the dexterous hand into a unified latent feature representation. This fused latent representation is passed through a three-layer MLP to produce the final dexterous hand wrist pose action increments.

Policy Training Using Simulation and Real Data

To analyze the influence of different data sources and training pipelines, we compare the following two training policies:

•

Policy A:
Trained exclusively on simulated data.

•

Policy B:
Pretrained on simulated data and fine-tuned using real-world data.

Both training policies adopt a unified network architecture and loss function. The network takes as input the current and target fingertip tactile images during contact, along with the current joint states of the dexterous hand, and outputs the wrist pose increment. During training, tactile image pairs are randomly sampled from the dataset as current and target inputs.

III-D
3
Cross-combination Policy Learning

Our framework enables dexterous hands to refine grasp poses toward arbitrary user-specified targets with only a single demonstration. To train the policy, we employ a cross-combination paradigm in which current and target tactile images are randomly paired from the dataset. By constructing an
N\times N
set of pairwise combinations through such sampling, the policy network is encouraged to generalize across diverse target poses. The network is optimized using the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between the predicted and ground-truth end-effector pose increments, which can be seen in Equation
2
.

\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left\|\Delta\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-\Delta\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\|^{2},

(2)

where
\Delta\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
is the predicted pose increment,
\Delta\mathbf{x}_{i}
is the ground-truth pose increment, and
K
is the batch size. The loss is minimized via backpropagation until convergence.

IV
Experiments

We conducted extensive real-world experiments to validate the effectiveness of the proposed method and evaluate the performance of our algorithm.

IV-A

Experimental Setup and Evaluation Metrics

IV-A
1
Experimental Setup

We employ a multi-finger dexterous hand equipped with low-cost, high-resolution piezoresistive tactile sensors on each fingertip. Both simulation and real-world platforms are developed to support data collection and policy deployment.
The simulation environment is implemented in MuJoCo using a physics-based tactile sensing model. For real-world experiments, we utilize a wheeled dual-arm robotic platform integrated with an 11-DoF dexterous hand. Each fingertip is instrumented with an
11\times 9
tactile array that measures normal contact forces with discrete values ranging from 0 to 255.

IV-A
2
Evaluation Metrics

Three evaluation metrics are used to evaluate grasp refinement performance:

•

Metric 1: Refinement Accuracy

The 6-DoF pose error after 10 adjustment steps, measured as:

\delta_{\mathrm{pos}}=\lVert\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{g}}\rVert_{2},\quad\delta_{\mathrm{rot}}=2\arccos(|\langle\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{g}}\rangle|)

(3)

where
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{g}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the current and target position, and
\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{g}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
represent the current and target quaternion, respectively. Here, the target pose refers to the wrist pose of the robotic arm recorded at the moment when the target fingertip tactile image was demonstrated.

•

Metric 2: Steps to Threshold Accuracy

Number of steps to reach position threshold
\epsilon_{\mathrm{pos}}=0.005\,\mathrm{m}
and orientation threshold
\epsilon_{\mathrm{rot}}=0.05\,\mathrm{rad}
:

s^{*}=\min\big\{s\;\big|\;\delta_{\mathrm{pos}}(s)\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{pos}}\;\wedge\;\delta_{\mathrm{rot}}(s)\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{rot}}\big\}.

(4)

•

Metric 3: Refinement Success Rate

The success rate over
R=5
trials is

\text{sr}=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{j=1}^{R}\mathbf{1}\big(\delta_{\mathrm{pos}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{pos}},\;\delta_{\mathrm{rot}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{rot}},\;s_{j}\leq S_{\max}\big),

(5)

where
\mathbf{1}(\cdot)
is 1 if all conditions are met and 0 otherwise.

Long Horizon Object Tracking

To evaluate the robustness of our method in dynamic scenarios, we conduct a long-horizon object tracking experiment. A fixed tactile image is provided as the target, while the object’s pose and position are continuously perturbed throughout the sequence. The goal is to assess whether our system can consistently adjust to maintain the desired grasp. The full tactile-based pose refinement process is illustrated in the Fig.

As shown in Fig.

From (b) to (c), the green timeline shows the learned policy dynamically adjusting along the Y-axis to achieve the target grasp pose. From (c) to the end, the yellow timeline illustrates the policy performing dynamic pitch adjustments to track the moving object and accomplish precise target pose grasping. These results demonstrate that our method consistently achieves fine-grained grasping toward a specified target pose, even under continuous variations in object pose.

Addressing this limitation would require additional sensory inputs, such as vision.

V
Conclusion And Discussions

This letter presented TacRefineNet, a tactile-only framework for fine in-hand pose refinement of known objects using multi-finger fingertip sensing. By iteratively adjusting the end-effector based on tactile feedback, it achieves millimeter-level grasp accuracy without vision. A multi-branch policy network trained on both simulated and a small set of real data ensures robust sim-to-real transfer. Experiments demonstrate generalization to arbitrary target poses, highlighting the potential of tactile-driven control for high-precision dexterous manipulation.

The main limitation is that the policy is currently effective on a single object. Future work will explore vision-tactile fusion for generalizable fine manipulation across diverse objects.

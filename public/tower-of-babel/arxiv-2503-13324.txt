Abstract
. We develop a method for the transfer of an uncertainty principle for the short-time Fourier transform or a Fourier pair to an uncertainty principle for a sesquilinear or quadratic metaplectic time-frequency representation. In particular, we derive Beurling-type and Hardy-type uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations. Keywords. Uncertainty principle of Benedicks, Beurling, Hardy, Nazarov, symplectic matrix, metaplectic representation, time-frequency representation. 81S07, 81S30, 22E46.



1.
Introduction

The uncertainty principle expresses many facets of the fact that a function and its
Fourier transform cannot be small simultaneously
[
22
]
.
In its original
version, the uncertainty principle is formulated as an inequality for the Fourier pair

(f,\widehat{f})
, yet in the realm of quantum mechanics and signal
processing one often prefers to use phase-space representations or joint
time-frequency representations.
For these, the uncertainty principle becomes an inequality about a joint
time-frequency representation.
Some early examples are the uncertainty principles of Hardy,
Benedicks-Amrein-Berthier, and of Beurling for the
short-time Fourier transform and the Wigner distribution
[
4
,
14
,
20
,
26
,
32
]
.
In a further extension, one may try to formulate
any type of uncertainty principle for any time-frequency representation.
This endeavor may easily get out of
hand, unless one restricts the attention to an
interesting and natural class of time-frequency representations.
For structural reasons, we will
focus on the
class of metaplectic time-frequency representations, because they turn out to be a natural,
almost canonical class of time-frequency representations and are completely characterized by an
abstract covariance property
[
19
]
.
The class of metaplectic time-frequency representations was recently introduced Cordero and
Rodino
[
6
]
as a generalization of the standard time-frequency representations and
became a powerful tool for the investigation
of pseudodifferential operators and function
spaces
[
9
,
10
,
5
,
6
,
7
]

under the name Wigner analysis.

In this article, we study uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations.
For the formulation of our
results, we start with time-frequency shifts and the symplectic group. For
(x,\omega)\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
, let

\rho(x,\omega)=T_{x/2}M_{\omega}T_{x/2}
denote the time-frequency shift acting on a function
f:{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\rho(x,\omega)f(t)=e^{-\pi ix\cdot\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t-x).

The symplectic group
{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2d\times 2d}
is the matrix group

(1.1)

{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}=\left\{\mathcal{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d\times 2d}:\mathcal{A}^{t}{\mathcal{J}}\mathcal{A}={\mathcal{J}}\right\},\quad{\mathcal{J}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\end{pmatrix}.

By the Stone-von Neumann theorem, for every
\mathcal{A}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
, there exists
a unitary operator
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying the intertwining relation

(1.2)

\rho(\mathcal{A}\lambda)=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\,\rho(\lambda)\,\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}.

Thus
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
describes how a time-frequency shift is transformed under a linear coordinate change.

Now let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
be a
4d\times 4d
symplectic
matrix
1
1

1

Note that we write
4d\times 4d
-matrices with bold letters.
, so that

{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
acts on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
. The metaplectic time-frequency representations associated to
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
is the
sequilinear form

(1.3)

W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)=\widehat{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}\,(f\otimes g),\qquad\qquad f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

The usual time-frequency representations, such as the short-time Fourier transform, the Wigner distribution, and
many others are special cases of metaplectic time-frequency representations.

Our objective is the investigation of uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations
W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
.
Qualitative uncertainty principles search for decay or integrability conditions on
W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
that force
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
. A quantitative uncertainty principle would be an inequality for
W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
or force
f
and
g
to have a
certain shape.
We will touch on both types in this paper.

In a first approach
[
18
]
, we studied the
Benedicks-Amrein-Berthier uncertainty principle for
W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
and developed a general method to study uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations.
Our goal is to extend the scope of results and to cover several other types of uncertainty principles. Our emphasis is on the
method and not so much on the explicit
results.
Our main goal is to show that the methods developed in

[
18
]
are versatile enough to answer most questions about uncertainty principle for metaplectic time-frequency representations. In the end, a reader should be convinced that other uncertainty principles can be derived in exactly the same way.

The point of departure is the observation that the validity of a particular uncertainty principle depends on the
parametrizing matrix
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
. Not all metaplectic time-frequency representations
fulfill a given type of uncertainty principle. The challenge then is to characterize those time-frequency representations that obey a given uncertainty principle. We provide an explanation which
metaplectic time-frequency representations obey a given type of uncertainty principle.

We recall that a symplectic matrix is often
written in block form as
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
with
2d\times 2d
blocks and that every symplectic matrix
\mathcal{A}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
possesses a
(non-unique) factorization into elementary matrices

(1.4)

{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
Q&I\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}L&0\\
0&(L^{t})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
-B&A\end{pmatrix}

with
2d\times 2d
blocks
Q,L,A,B
such that
Q=Q^{t}
,
L
is invertible, and the
associated complex-valued matrix
U=A+iB
is unitary.
This so-called pre-Iwazawa decomposition translates into a
factorization of the metaplectic operator
\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
and a
representation of the associated metaplectic time-frequency representation
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
.

It turns out that
only the third factor
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
-B&A\end{pmatrix}
is relevant for the validity of an uncertainty principle.

Our main insight about uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations is the following dichotomy depending solely on properties of
U^{t}U
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
with a factorization (
1.4
) and let

U=A+iB\in U(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
be the associated unitary
2d\times 2d

matrix.

Alternative I.
If
U^{t}U
is block-diagonal, i.e., there exist
unitary
d\times d
-matrices
U_{1},U_{2}\in U(d,{\mathbb{C}})
, such that
U^{t}U=\begin{pmatrix}U_{1}&O\\
O&U_{2}\end{pmatrix}
, then there exist non-zero functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that

W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
has compact support and thus satisfies arbitrary
decay or integrability conditions. Consequently, the usual types of
uncertainty principles cannot hold for such a metaplectic time-frequency representation.

Alternative II.
If
U^{t}U
is not block-diagonal, i.e.,
U^{t}U=\begin{pmatrix}U_{1}&V_{1}\\
V_{2}&U_{2}\end{pmatrix}
and either
V_{1}\neq 0
or
V_{2}\neq 0
, then a meaningful and interesting uncertainty principle can be formulated and proved for
W_{\mathcal{A}}
.

We will state explicitly the following types of uncertainty principles for a metaplectic time-frequency representation under the stringent
hypothesis that
U^{t}U
is not block-diagonal
.

(i)

(Benedicks-type): If
\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)
is supported on a set of
finite measure, then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
. This was our
main result in
[
18
]
.

(ii)

(Beurling-type): If
\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)
satisfies

(1.5)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)(\lambda)|e^{\pi|\lambda\cdot M\lambda|}\,d\lambda<\infty

for an appropriate matrix
M=M({\bm{\mathcal{A}}})
determined by the
factorization (
1.4
) of
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
,
then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
, see Theorem
4.4
.

(iii)

(Hardy-type):
Again, for an appropriate matrix
\Omega=\Omega({\bm{\mathcal{A}}})
determined by the
factorization (
1.4
) of
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
, the condition

|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)(\lambda)|\leq ce^{-\alpha\|\Omega^{-1}\lambda\|^{2}}
for
\alpha>1
implies that
f\equiv 0
or

g\equiv 0
.
In the critical case
\alpha=1
, both
f
and
g
can
be described explicitly, but their description is more intricate
than in Hardy’s original uncertainty principle, see Corollary
4.5
and Theorem
6.1
.

In the second part, we study uncertainty principles for the quadratic metaplectic time-frequency representations
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,f)
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}
. Clearly, if
U^{t}U
is not
block-diagonal, then the above results apply and yield
f\equiv 0
. However, for quadratic time-frequency representations
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
a new
phenomenon emerges. Even if
U^{t}U=\begin{pmatrix}U_{1}&O\\
O&U_{2}\end{pmatrix}
is block-diagonal, some uncertainty principles for
W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,f)
do hold. A simple reduction leads us to investigate the equivalent
question of metaplectic pairs

(\widehat{\mathcal{A}_{1}}f,\widehat{\mathcal{A}_{2}}f)
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
in place of a
Fourier pair
(f,\widehat{f})
. This case can be reduced to the
assumption that
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathrm{I}_{2d}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}&V_{2}\\
-V_{2}&V_{1}\end{pmatrix}
with
V_{2}\neq 0
. Thus the unitary matrix

V=V_{1}+iV_{2}
is not real-valued. A Beurling-type uncertainty principle for the
metaplectic pair
(f,\mathcal{A}_{2}f)
asserts that a condition of the form

(1.6)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|(f\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2}f)(\lambda)|e^{\pi|\lambda\cdot M\lambda|}\,d\lambda<\infty,

implies
f\equiv 0
.
Again,
M=M({\bm{\mathcal{A}}})
is an appropriate matrix that comes from the
factorization (
1.4
). As a corollary we obtain a
Hardy-type uncertainty principle for the metaplectic pair
(f,\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{2}f)

(Theorem
5.7
). For a version in dimension
d=1
we refer to the
recent work
[
13
]
. A thorough analysis in higher
dimensions is contained in
[
8
]
. It is based on a
explicit formula of metaplectic operators
from
[
30
]
. Despite similarities of the
results, it is not completely clear how they compare.

As further examples, we formulate uncertainty principles according to Gelfand-Shilov
with super-exponential weights for the partial short-time Fourier transform and a version of Nazarov’s inequality for
metaplectic
pairs, see Theorems
3.6
and
6.2
.

Proof Method.

We would like to emphasize that the above statements are just some
examples of uncertainty principles that can be proved. One could go on indefinitely,
but the proofs follow exactly the strategy outlined in
Section 3 and boil down to an exercise in meticulous,
but rather unpleasant book-keeping. In our perspective, the blueprint
for proving uncertainty principles in Section
3.2
is our main contribution.

The proof method was developed in
[
18
]

and is based on several different factorizations of a symplectic
matrix and the corresponding metaplectic operator. The most important
factorization is the so-called pre-Iwazawa decomposition
(
1.4
). An elaborate analysis of this factorization with
additional singular value decompositions yields an important
formula for a metaplectic time-frequency representation
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
. Given to a symplectic matrix
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}\in\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})
, there exist matrices
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
of smaller dimension,
so that
\widehat{\mathcal{A}},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
act on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\Omega\in\mathrm{GL}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
, such that

(1.7)

|\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)|=|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Omega}V^{k}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f|,\qquad f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Here
V^{k}_{g}f
is a
partial
short-time Fourier transform, which is a version of the
short-time Fourier transform over a lower-dimensional subspace, see Section
3.2
for the
precise definition and its analysis.
The line of argument then proceeds from (known) uncertainty principles for the short-time Fourier transform to prove analogous uncertainty principles for the partial short-time Fourier transform
V^{k}_{g}f
. The final uncertainty principle for
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
then follows by a translation by means of formula
(
1.7
).
The technical details are hidden in the factorizations and the measure-theoretic subtleties of the partial short-time Fourier transform, but the proofs of the main uncertainty principles (Beurling, Hardy, Gelfand-Shilov) are then straightforward and rather short.

For the treatment of quadratic uncertainty principles for
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,f)
, we will analyze another factorization for unitary matrices
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
-B&A\end{pmatrix}
when the block
B
is invertible.
The corresponding metaplectic operators can be written as a tensor product of fractional Fourier transforms (see Lemma
5.5
and its proof). In this context, we acknowledge our debt to P. Jaming
[
25
]
who implicitly used such a factorization in dimension
d=1
for pairs of fractional Fourier transforms.

Outline.
Section 2 collects some facts about the symplectic
group and metaplectic operators. Section 3 presents several uncertainty principles for the partial short-time Fourier transform.
In particular, Section 3.2 outlines the general proof strategy for uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations and is perhaps the most important message of this paper. In
Section 4 we prove uncertainty principles according to Hardy, Beurling, and
Gelfand-Shilov for general metaplectic time-frequency representations.
Section 5 focusses on quadratic metaplectic time-frequency representations and contains uncertainty principles for metaplectic pairs of functions. Section 6 touches quantitative uncertainty principles in the style of Hardy and Nazarov. Further remarks in Section 7 and an appendix resuming a decomposition from
[
18
]
complete the paper.

The partial short-time Fourier transform

6

6

By Fubini’s theorem, the function

is square-integrable for almost all
x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}
. This fact allows us to define the partial short-time Fourier transform in
k
variables almost everywhere.

Definition 3.1

(Partial short-time Fourier transform)
.

Let
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and let
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.
The partial short-time Fourier transform in the first
k
variables of a function
f
with respect to
g
is defined as

(3.2)

\begin{split}V^{k}_{g}f(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}f(t,x_{2})\overline{g(t-x_{1},-\omega_{2})}e^{-2\pi it\cdot\omega_{1}}\,dt\\
&=\langle f_{x_{2}},M_{\omega_{1}}T_{x_{1}}g_{-\omega_{2}}\rangle=V_{g_{-\omega_{2}}}f_{x_{2}}(x_{1},\omega_{1}),\end{split}

for
(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}
.

If
k=d
, then the definition returns the usual short-time Fourier transform
V_{g}f
on

{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
.

Similar to the full short-time Fourier transform,
V_{g}^{k}f
is a special case of a metaplectic time-frequency representation
[
18
]
.
By Fubini’s theorem, for almost all
x_{2},\omega_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}
,
V_{g_{-\omega_{2}}}f_{x_{2}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})
is a short-time Fourier transform of two square-integrable functions in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
, hence all known uncertainty principles for the short-time Fourier transform are applicable to these restrictions.

We first state a standard result in measure theory.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
,
1\leq k\leq d
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If the restriction
f_{x_{2}}
is the zero function for almost all
x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}
, then
f\equiv 0
.

(ii)

Assume that
k<d
. If
f_{x_{2}}\otimes g_{\omega_{2}}\equiv 0
for almost all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}
, then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

(iii)

If
V_{g}^{k}f\equiv 0
almost everywhere, then
f\equiv 0
of
g\equiv 0
almost everywhere.

A self-contained proof of (iii) can be found in

[
18
, Lem. 3.2.]
, (i) follows from
Fubini’s theorem, and (ii) follows from (i) after permuting the order
of the variables.

3.2.
The proof strategy.

The primary reason we are interested in the partial short-time Fourier transform is the following proof approach that we already exploited in
[
18
]
to prove a Benedicks-type uncertainty principle for
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
.

(1)

Factorization of
\mathcal{A}
: Compute a pre-Iwasawa decomposition
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{Q}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}
.

(2)

Representation of
W_{\mathcal{A}}
: If
U^{t}U
is not
d\times d
block-diagonal, then there exists a
1\leq k\leq d
and matrices
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
,
\Omega\in\mathrm{GL}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
, such that
[
18
, Thm. 1.1., Thm. 3.4.]

(3.3)

|\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)|=|\widehat{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f\otimes\overline{g})|=|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Omega}(V^{k}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f)|.

By setting
\tilde{f}=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f
and
\tilde{g}=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g
, an
uncertainty principle for
\mathrm{W}_{\mathcal{A}}(f,g)
is equivalent to an uncertainty principle for

V^{k}_{\tilde{g}}\tilde{f}
.

(3)

Uncertainty principle for the partial short-time Fourier transform: Show that
the restriction
V^{k}_{\tilde{g}}\tilde{f}(\cdot,x_{2},\cdot,\omega_{2})=V_{\tilde{g}_{-\omega_{2}}}\tilde{f}_{x_{2}}

satisfies the uncertainty principle for almost all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})
and
conclude that
V^{k}_{\tilde{g}}\tilde{f}=0
.

(4)

Uncertainty principle for
W_{\mathcal{A}}
: Conclude
f=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}\tilde{f}\equiv 0
or
g=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\tilde{g}\equiv 0
.

We consider the proof outline with Steps (i) — (iv) our main
contribution to understand uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations.
In this strategy, an uncertainty principle for the partial short-time Fourier transform is an intermediate
result towards an uncertainty principle for a metaplectic time-frequency representation. Technically, we apply a known
uncertainty principle for the full short-time Fourier transform to the restrictions
V_{\tilde{g}_{-\omega_{2}}}\tilde{f}_{x_{2}}
and then conclude that
V^{k}_{g}f\equiv 0
and apply Lemma
3.2
. The ensuing uncertainty principle for
W_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
is then a matter of diligent book-keeping, however, in view of (
3.3
), its final formulation may look very ugly.

In the following sections, we execute this plan for the uncertainty principles of Hardy, Beurling, and Gelfand-Shilov.

3.3.
Beurling-type

Beurling’s uncertainty principle is considered the strongest uncertainty principle because the classical uncertainty principles such as Hardy’s, Benedicks’s, and Gelfand-Shilov’s uncertainty principle are easy consequences.
The following version of Beurling’s uncertainty principle for the short-time Fourier transform was derived by Demange
[
14
, Thm. 1.2.]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and
N\geq 0
.

(i)

If the weighted integral satisfies

(3.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|V_{g}f(x,\omega)|}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}e^{\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\,dxd\omega<\infty\,,

then either
V_{g}f\equiv 0
and thus
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
,
or both can be written as

(3.5)

f(x)=p_{1}(x)e^{-Ax\cdot x-2\pi i\omega_{0}\cdot x},\quad g(x)=p_{2}(x)e^{-Ax\cdot x-2\pi i\omega_{0}\cdot x},\

with polynomials
p_{1},p_{2}
whose degrees satisfy

deg(p_{1})+deg(p_{2})<N-d
,
\omega_{0}\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{d}}
, and with a real
positive-definite matrix
A
.

(ii)

Qualitative version: In particular, if
N\leq d
in (
3.4
), then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

The partial short-time Fourier transform satisfies the same type of an uncertainty principle.
We state a qualitative version and refer to Sections
6
and
7
for further discussion.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
,
0\leq N\leq k
and
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
. If

(3.6)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}e^{\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\,dxd\omega<\infty,

then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Proof.

If
k=d
, then this is Theorem
3.3
(ii).
Thus we may assume
k<d
.
Let
(x_{1},\omega_{1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}
and
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}
.
Fubini’s theorem and the estimate

(3.7)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{(1+\lVert x_{1}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert)^{N}}\,e^{\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}|}&\leq\frac{e^{\pi|x_{2}\cdot\omega_{2}|}(1+\lVert x_{2}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{2}\rVert)^{N}}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}\,e^{\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}+x_{2}\cdot\omega_{2}|}\\
&=\frac{e^{\pi|x_{2}\cdot\omega_{2}|}(1+\lVert x_{2}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{2}\rVert)^{N}}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}\,e^{\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\end{split}

show that (
3.6
) implies

(3.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}}\frac{|V^{k}_{g}f(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|}{(1+\lVert x_{1}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert)^{N}}e^{\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}|}\,dx_{1}d\omega_{1}<\infty\quad\text{for a.a. }(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}.

Since
V^{k}_{g}f(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=V_{g_{-\omega_{2}}}f_{x_{2}}
, (
3.8
) implies
that the condition (
3.4
) is satisfied for almost
all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}
. By Theorem

3.3
,
V_{g_{-\omega_{2}}}f_{x_{2}}\equiv 0
for all

(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}\setminus S
.
Consequently
V^{k}_{g}f(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=0
almost everywhere.
From Lemma
3.2
(iii) we obtain that
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
, as claimed.

Note that both conditions (
3.6
) and
(
3.8
) imply
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
. Condition (
3.6
) treats the variables

(x_{1},\omega_{2})
and
(x_{2},\omega_{2})
equally, whereas
(
3.8
) is asymmetric, but is weaker. Similar
observations can be made for the other uncertainty principles, see Section

7
.

3.4.
Hardy-type

As is well known for the Fourier pair
(f,\widehat{f})
, Beurling’s
uncertainty principle implies Hardy’s uncertainty principle.
Similarly, Theorem
3.4
implies a Hardy-type uncertainty principle for the partial short-time Fourier transform.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
,
\alpha,\beta,c>0
with
\alpha\beta>1
.
If
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfy

(3.9)

|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|\leq ce^{-\pi(\alpha\lVert x\rVert^{2}+\beta\lVert\omega\rVert^{2})/2}\quad\text{ for a.a. }(x,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d},

then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.4
. Since

(3.10)

|x\cdot\omega|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2\sqrt{\beta}}\lVert x\rVert^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{2\sqrt{\alpha}}\lVert\omega\rVert^{2},

we can estimate the integral in (
3.8
) with

N=0
as follows:

(3.11)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|V_{g}^{k}f(x,\omega)|e^{\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\,dxd\omega\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-\pi(1-(\alpha\beta)^{-1/2})(\alpha\lVert x\rVert^{2}+\beta\lVert y\rVert^{2})/2}\,dxd\omega.

This integral is finite for
\alpha\beta>1
, so that Theorem

3.4
applies and yields that either
f\equiv 0

or
g\equiv 0
.

Theorem
3.5
can also be derived directly from
Hardy’s uncertainty principle for the short-time Fourier transform
[
20
,
4
]
in analogous fashion to the proof of Theorem
3.4

3.5.
Gelfand-Shilov type

Next, we prove a Gelfand-Shilov-type uncertainty principle. In this case, Gaussian
weights as in Hardy’s uncertainty principle are replaced by other exponential weights. The
uncertainty principle of Gelfand-Shilov for the Fourier pair
(f,\widehat{f})
is important
in the theory of test functions and distributions
[
17
]
, and an analogous
theory of test functions could be established by using the (partial)
short-time Fourier transform.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and let
q
denote its conjugate Hölder exponent

\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}=1
. If
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfy

(3.12)

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|e^{\frac{\pi}{p}\alpha^{p}\lVert x\rVert_{p}^{p}}\,dxd\omega<\infty,\\
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|e^{\frac{\pi}{q}\beta^{q}\lVert\omega\rVert_{q}^{q}}\,dxd\omega<\infty,\end{split}

for
\alpha,\beta>0
satisfying
\alpha\beta\geq 1
, then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Using Young’s inequality (in the exponent) and the convexity of the
exponential function, we estimate

\displaystyle e^{\pi|x\cdot\omega|}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\pi\alpha\beta|x\cdot\omega|}\leq e^{\pi\big(\frac{1}{p}\|\alpha x\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|\beta\omega\|_{q}^{q}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{p}e^{\pi\|\alpha x\|_{p}^{p}}+\tfrac{1}{q}e^{\pi\|\beta\omega\|_{q}^{q}}\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|e^{\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\,dxd\omega

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|e^{\pi\lVert\alpha x\rVert_{p}^{p}}\,dxd\omega+\tfrac{1}{q}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|V^{k}_{g}f(x,\omega)|e^{\pi\lVert\beta\omega\rVert_{q}^{q}}\,dxd\omega<\infty\,.

Thus Theorem
3.4
applies and yields

f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

4.
Uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations

Following the strategy outlined in Section
3.2
, we now prove
several
uncertainty principles for the metaplectic time-frequency representations
W_{\mathcal{A}}
. The main tool is a factorization of

W_{\mathcal{A}}
and a subsequent reduction of the uncertainty principle to the partial short-time Fourier transform.

The following representation of
W_{\mathcal{A}}
was proved in
[
18
, Lem. 3.3., Thm. 5.4.]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{Q}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}
be a pre-Iwasawa decomposition
of
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
.

(i) Then

(4.1)

|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)(\lambda)|=|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{V}}}_{Q}\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}_{L}\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{U}(f\otimes\overline{g})(\lambda)|=|\det L|^{-1/2}\,|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{U}(f\otimes\overline{g})(L^{-1}\lambda)|.

(ii) Furthermore, if
U^{t}U\in\mathrm{U}(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
is
d\times d
block-diagonal, then there exist matrices
W\in\mathrm{O}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
and
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathrm{U}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
such that
U=W\cdot\mathrm{diag}(V_{1},V_{2})
, thus

(4.2)

|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)(\lambda)|=|\det L|^{-1/2}\,\big|\big(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V_{1}}f\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\overline{V_{2}}}g\big)(W^{t}L^{-1}\lambda)\big|.

Lemma
4.1
simplifies several
aspects.

Firstly, since

|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{V}}}_{Q}F|=|F|
and
\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}_{L}F(x)=|\det L|^{-1/2}F(L^{-1}x)
, these two operators do not
change decay or integrability properties.
After dropping them in the factorization
(
4.1
), we may focus on

\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}
for some
U\in\mathrm{U}(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Secondly, there is a fundamental dichotomy for uncertainty principles for metaplectic time-frequency representations
depending solely on the nature of
U^{t}U
.

Let us first settle the uninteresting case.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{Q}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}
and assume that
U^{t}U

is block-diagonal. Then there exist non-zero functions
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, such that
\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)
has compact support.

Proof.

Just take
f_{0},g_{0}\in C_{c}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and set
f=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V_{1}}^{-1}f_{0}
and
g=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\overline{V_{2}}}^{-1}g_{0}
.
Then by (
4.2
),
|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)|=|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}_{LW}(f_{0}\otimes g_{0})|
is a linear
coordinate change of
f_{0}\otimes g_{0}
and has compact support.

Thus, in this case we cannot expect an uncertainty principle expressed by a decay
or integrability condition to hold. For the treatment of the
alternative, we use the following representation of
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{Q}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
with
U^{t}U\in\mathrm{U}(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
not
d\times d
block-diagonal.
Then there exist an index
1\leq k\leq d
, symplectic matrices
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
and a matrix
\Omega\in\mathrm{GL}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

(4.3)

|\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)|=|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{D}}}_{\Omega}V^{k}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f|,\qquad f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

The matrices
\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
and the index
k
can be derived explicitly from the pre-Iwasawa decomposition of
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}
.

Lemma
4.3
is a summary of a series of
computations in
[
18
]
. For
completeness we explain the exact derivation of the matrices in the appendix.

Most uncertainty principles are related to decay or integrability conditions, which are
quite robust under linear changes of coordinates, and allow us to derive uncertainty principles for
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
equivalent to uncertainty principles for the corresponding short-time Fourier transform.
To illustrate this procedure, we derive a Beurling-type and a Hardy-type uncertainty principle for
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
.

Before we state a simple version of a Beurling-type uncertainty principle for
\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)
, we make a small observation.
As we are going to discuss uncertainty principles for
joint
time-frequency representations, it makes sense to express the conditions in terms of

\lambda=(x,\omega)\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}
instead of
x,\omega\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
. The inner
product on
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
is then written as

(4.4)

x\cdot\omega=\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\lambda.

In the following, we will use the right-hand side more often since it is better compatible with the change of coordinates of
\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{\Omega}
.

Theorem 4.4

(Beurling-type)
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
and
\Omega=\Omega({\bm{\mathcal{A}}})
be the matrix
introduced in the factorization (
4.3
). Assume that
U^{t}U\in\mathrm{U}(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
is not
d\times d
block-diagonal. If
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfy

(4.5)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)(\lambda)|e^{\pi|\Omega^{-1}\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\Omega^{-1}\lambda|/2}\,d\lambda<\infty,

then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
,

(4.6)

|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)|=|\det\Omega|^{-1/2}|V^{k}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f(\Omega^{-1}\cdot)|.

Therefore, we can conclude from (
4.5
)
that

(4.7)

\begin{split}&|\det\Omega|^{1/2}\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|V^{k}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f(\lambda)|\,e^{\pi|\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\lambda|/2}\,d\lambda\\
\leq&|\det\Omega|^{-1/2}\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|V^{k}_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f(\Omega^{-1}\lambda)|\,e^{\pi|\Omega^{-1}\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\Omega^{-1}\lambda|/2}\,d\lambda<\infty.\end{split}

By Theorem
3.4
,
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}f\equiv 0
or

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}g\equiv 0
. Since
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
are unitary, this is equivalent to
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
, as desired.

A Hardy-type uncertainty principle follows directly from Theorem
4.4
. The proof is analogous to the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Corollary 4.5

(Hardy-type)
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{Q}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
and
\Omega=\Omega({\bm{\mathcal{A}}})
be the
matrix in the factorization (
4.3
). Assume that
U^{t}U\in\mathrm{U}(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
is not
d\times d
block-diagonal. If
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfy

(4.8)

|\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,g)(\lambda)|\leq ce^{-\pi\alpha\lVert\Omega^{-1}\lambda\rVert^{2}/2}\quad\text{for a.a. }\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d},

for some
c>0
and
\alpha>1
,
then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Alternatively, the Hardy-type uncertainty principle follows from
Theorem
3.5
. The case
\alpha=1
in
(
4.8
) will be discussed in Section

6
.

5.
Qualitative uncertainty principles for quadratic time-frequency representations and pairs of metaplectic operators

We next consider uncertainty principles for quadratic metaplectic time-frequency representations
W_{\mathcal{A}}(f,f)
.
In this case, the situation is more complicated.

If
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{Q}\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{L}\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}
and
U^{t}U
is not block-diagonal, then uncertainty principles for the metaplectic time-frequency representation
\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,f)
are already covered by results in
Section
4
.

However, if
U^{t}U
is block-diagonal,
the situation is drastically different, as we have already noticed for
the Benedicks-type uncertainty principle in
[
18
]
. The new phenomenon
is best illustrated by the Rihazcek distribution

(5.1)

R(f,g)(x,\omega)=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}f(x)\overline{\widehat{g}(\omega)},\qquad x,\omega\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\,.

and Benedicks’s uncertainty principle.
The Rihazcek distribution is a metaplectic time-frequency representation with
block-diagonal
U^{t}U

[
6
]
.
Clearly,
R(f,g)
is supported on a compact set for every non-zero function
f
and
\widehat{g}\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
with compact
support.
However, the uncertainty principle of Amrein-Berthier and Benedicks for the Fourier
pair
(f,\widehat{f})

states that
R(f,f)
is supported on a set of finite measure if and
only if
f\equiv 0

[
1
,
2
]
.

For the more general metaplectic time-frequency representations
\mathrm{W}_{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(f,f)
it is a non-trivial
question when an uncertainty principle holds.
In the following, we only consider the new case when
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}=\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}
and
U^{t}U
is
d\times d
block-diagonal.

Remark 5.1

(Reduction to pairs of operators)
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{U}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(4d,{\mathbb{R}})}
with
U^{t}U\in\mathrm{U}(2d,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Using Lemma
4.1
(ii) and omitting the coordinate change
\bm{\mathcal{D}}_{WL}
, we may assume without loss of
generality that
U=\mathrm{diag}(V_{1},V_{2})
for some

V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathrm{U}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
, and we study

(5.2)

|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{\mathrm{diag}(V_{1},V_{2})}(f\otimes\overline{f})|=|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V_{1}}f|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\overline{V_{2}}}f|.

By setting
g=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V_{1}}f
and
V=\overline{V_{2}}V_{1}^{-1}=\overline{V_{2}}V_{1}^{*}
, we rewrite this as

(5.3)

|\widehat{\bm{\mathcal{R}}}_{\mathrm{diag}(V_{1},V_{2})}(f\otimes\overline{f})|=|g|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\overline{V_{2}}}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V_{1}}^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V_{1}}f|=|g|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}g|.

After this reduction, it suffices to study uncertainty principles for the pair
(g,\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}g)
.

If
V
is real-valued, i.e.,
V\in\mathrm{O}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
, then
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}g(x)=g(V^{-1}x)
. Thus, if
g
has compact support,
then so does
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{V}g
, and no decay or integrability condition
can force
g\equiv 0
. We will see that the real-valuedness of
V
is the only obstruction
to uncertainty principles for
W_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,f)
. For a characterization of those
metaplectic matrices where the reduction in (
5.3
) yields a
real-valued
V
we refer to
[
18
, Lem. 6.1.]
.

Remark 5.2

(Proof strategy — quadratic version)
.

The strategy to prove uncertainty principles for quadratic time-frequency representations is similar to the
one presented in Section
3.2
, but in the second step,
in (
3.3
), we replace the partial short-time Fourier transform with a

partial Fourier transform
which we recall next:

Let
1\leq k<d
, the partial Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
is defined as

(5.4)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}f(x_{1},\omega_{2})e^{-2\pi i\omega_{1}\cdot x_{1}}\,dx_{1}={\mathcal{F}}(f_{\omega_{2}})(\omega_{1})=\widehat{f_{\omega_{2}}}(\omega_{1}),\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k},\ f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{split}

This transform is a metaplectic operator, namely,

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}={\mathcal{F}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-k})}=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{diag}(iI_{k},I_{d-k})}\in{\mathrm{Mp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}\,,

and therefore the partial Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
extends
to a unitary operator on
{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
. Note
that the restriction
f_{\omega_{2}}
has
k
free variables, therefore
we can interpret

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
as the usual Fourier transform and write
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\widehat{f_{\omega_{2}}}(\omega_{1})
without ambiguity.
If
k=d
, then we write
{\mathcal{F}}_{d}={\mathcal{F}}
.

5.1.
Uncertainty principles for the partial Fourier transform

We now present a Beurling-type and a Hardy-type uncertainty principle.

This time, we begin with Hardy’s uncertainty principle, as its proof is
straightforward.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
. If
f\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfies

(5.5)

|f(x)|\leq ce^{-\pi\alpha\lVert x\rVert^{2}},\quad|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega)|\leq ce^{-\pi\beta\lVert\omega\rVert^{2}},\qquad\text{for a.a. }x,\omega\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}

for some constants
c>0
and
\alpha\beta>1
, then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

If
k=d
, this is Hardy’s uncertainty principle
[
21
]
, so we assume
1\leq k<d
.
The assumption, combined with Fubini’s and Plancherel’s theorem,
implies the following:
there exist sets
S_{x},S_{\omega}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}
of measure zero such
that for fixed
x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}\setminus S_{x},\omega_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}\setminus S_{\omega}
,
the restrictions
f_{x_{2}},\widehat{f_{\omega_{2}}}=({\mathcal{F}}_{k}f)_{\omega_{2}}
lie in
L^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{k}})
, and

(5.6)

\begin{split}|f(x_{1},x_{2})|\leq ce^{-\pi\alpha\lVert x_{1}\rVert^{2}},\quad\text{for a.a. }x_{1}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}},\\
|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|\leq ce^{-\pi\beta\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert^{2}},\quad\text{for all }\omega_{1}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}.\end{split}

Then for
x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}\setminus(S_{x}\cup S_{\omega})
we have

(5.7)

\begin{split}|f_{x_{2}}(x_{1})|&=|f(x_{1},x_{2})|\leq ce^{-\pi\alpha\lVert x_{1}\rVert^{2}},\quad\text{for a.a. }x_{1}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}},\\
|\widehat{(f_{x_{2}})}(\omega_{1})|&=|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega_{1},x_{2})|\leq ce^{-\pi\beta\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert^{2}}\quad\text{for a.a. }\omega_{1}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}.\end{split}

Hardy’s uncertainty principle for the Fourier pair
(f_{x_{2}},\widehat{f}_{x_{2}})
now
implies
f_{x_{2}}\equiv 0
for
x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-k}\setminus(S_{x}\cup S_{\omega})
.
Since
S_{x}\cup S_{\omega}
is a set of measure zero, Lemma

3.2
(i) implies that
f\equiv 0
, as claimed.

The case
\alpha\beta=1
will be treated in Section
6
.

Next, we formulate a Beurling-type theorem for the partial Fourier transform. Its
proof is trickier.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
and
f\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
. If there exists an
0\leq N\leq k
such that

(5.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{|f(x){\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega)|}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}e^{2\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\,dxd\omega<\infty,

then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Since
k=d
is Beurling’s uncertainty principle
[
3
,
23
]
, we assume that
1\leq k<d
.
We start as in
Theorem
3.4
. Estimate (
3.7
)
implies that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}}\frac{|f(x_{1},x_{2}){\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|}{(1+\lVert x_{1}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert)^{N}}e^{2\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}|}\,dx_{1}d\omega_{1}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2\pi|x_{2}\cdot\omega_{2}|}(1+\|x_{2}\|+\|\omega_{2}\|)^{N}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}}\frac{|f(x_{1},x_{2}){\mathcal{F}}_{k}f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}e^{2\pi|(x_{1},x_{2})\cdot(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|}\,dx_{1}d\omega_{1}\,,

and the last integral is finite for almost all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2(d-k)}
by Fubini’s theorem and condition
(
5.8
). Call the exceptional set
S
.
Whereas in (
5.7
) we could use
x_{2}=\omega_{2}
, these variables are
now independent. To settle this difficulty, we resort to a Beurling-type uncertainty principle for pairs of functions. It was shown in
[
14
, Cor. 3.7.]
that

(5.11)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{{|g(x)h(\omega)|+|\widehat{g}(x)\widehat{h}(\omega)|}}{(1+\lVert x\rVert+\lVert\omega\rVert)^{N}}e^{2\pi|x\cdot\omega|}\,dxd\omega<\infty\quad\implies\quad g\equiv 0\text{ or }h\equiv 0.

We apply this result to the functions
g=f_{x_{2}}
and
h=({\mathcal{F}}_{k}f)_{\omega_{2}}=\widehat{f_{\omega_{2}}}
. Let

(5.12)

\widetilde{S}=S\cup\{(\omega_{2},x_{2}):(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in S\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}.

This is also a set of measure zero.
Then we obtain for all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}\setminus\widetilde{S}

(5.13)

\begin{split}&\phantom{=}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{{|f_{x_{2}}(x_{1})({\mathcal{F}}_{k}f)_{\omega_{2}}(\omega_{1})|+|\widehat{f_{x_{2}}}(x_{1})\widehat{({\mathcal{F}}_{k}f)_{\omega_{2}}}(\omega_{1})|}}{(1+\lVert x_{1}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert)^{N}}e^{2\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}|}\,dx_{1}d\omega_{1}\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{{|f_{x_{2}}(x_{1})(\widehat{f}_{\omega_{2}})(\omega_{1})|+|(\widehat{f}_{x_{2}}(x_{1})f_{\omega_{2}}(-\omega_{1})|}}{(1+\lVert x_{1}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert)^{N}}e^{2\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}|}\,dx_{1}d\omega_{1}<\infty\,.\end{split}

By
[
14
, Cor. 3.7.]
,
f_{x_{2}}\equiv 0
or
({\mathcal{F}}_{k}f)_{\omega_{2}}\equiv 0
.
Equivalently,
f_{x_{2}}\otimes({\mathcal{F}}_{k}f)_{\omega_{2}}
for all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}\setminus\widetilde{S}
.
Since
\widetilde{S}
has measure zero, Lemma
3.2
(ii) implies
f\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k}f\equiv 0
.
As
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
is an invertible operator, this implies that
f\equiv 0
, as desired.

5.2.
Uncertainty principles for pairs of metaplectic operators and quadratic metaplectic representations

In this subsection, we discuss uncertainty principles for
(f,\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}f)
,
V\in\mathrm{U}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
if
V\notin\mathrm{O}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{V}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
and assume that
V\in U(d,{\mathbb{C}})
is not real-valued. Then there exist matrices

\Omega\in\mathrm{GL}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathcal{B}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
, such that

(5.14)

|f|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}f|=\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\Omega}\left(|\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f|\otimes|{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f|\right).

The matrices
\Omega
and
\mathcal{B}

and the index
1\leq k\leq d
can be determined explicitly.

Proof.

By a version of a singular value decomposition

[
16
, Thm. 5.1.]
, there exist orthogonal matrices

W_{1},W_{2}\in\mathrm{O}(d,{\mathbb{R}})
and a diagonal unitary matrix
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}\,(\sigma_{1\,1},\dots,\sigma_{d\,d})\in\mathrm{U}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
with
V=W_{1}\Sigma W_{2}
.
Since
V
is not real-valued by assumption, neither is
\Sigma
, and
after a suitable permutation and multiplication with a diagonal
orthogonal matrix, we may assume

(5.15)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{Im}\,}\sigma_{1\,1}\geq\dots\geq{\mathrm{Im}\,}\sigma_{k\,k}>0&={\mathrm{Im}\,}\sigma_{k+1\ k+1}=\dots={\mathrm{Im}\,}\sigma_{d\,d},\ \text{and}\\
1&={\mathrm{Re}\,}\sigma_{k+1\ k+1}=\dots={\mathrm{Re}\,}\sigma_{d\,d},\end{split}

where
k=\mathrm{rank}({\mathrm{Im}\,}(V))
.
By (
2.11
), we can tensorize the action of
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\Sigma}
as

(5.16)

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\Sigma}=\bigotimes\limits_{n=1}^{d}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma_{nn}}.

Note that in dimension
d=1
for
\sigma\in{\mathbb{T}}
,

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Mp}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is the fractional Fourier
transform
[
27
,
31
,
15
,
29
]
.
If
\sigma\neq\pm 1
, we can decompose
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}
as

(5.17)

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}={\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathrm{Re}\,}(\sigma)/{\mathrm{Im}\,}(\sigma)}\mathcal{D}_{{\mathrm{Im}\,}(\sigma)}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathrm{Re}\,}(\sigma)/{\mathrm{Im}\,}(\sigma)},

see
[
25
]
, also

[
18
, Sec. 4.1.]
. Thus the fractional
Fourier transform acts as a product of unimodular multipliers
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathrm{Re}\,}(\sigma)/{\mathrm{Im}\,}(\sigma)}
and the usual Fourier transform.
If
\sigma=1
, then
\mathcal{R}_{1}=I_{2}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is the identity matrix.
Using (
5.16
) and (
5.15
), we obtain the analogous multivariate factorization

(5.18)

\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma}={\mathcal{V}}_{C}\mathcal{D}_{B}\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{diag}(iI_{k},I_{d-k})}{\mathcal{V}}_{C},

where
B,C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
are the diagonal matrices

(5.19)

b_{nn}=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{Im}\,}(\sigma_{nn}),&\quad 1\leq n\leq k,\\
1,&\quad n>k,\end{cases},\quad c_{nn}=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{Re}\,}(\sigma_{nn})/{\mathrm{Im}\,}(\sigma_{nn}),&\quad 1\leq n\leq k,\\
0,&\quad n>k.\end{cases}

Since
|\widehat{V}_{C}f|=|f|
, we can write

(5.20)

\begin{split}|f|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}f|&=|f|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{1}}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\Sigma}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{2}}f|\\
&=|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{2}}^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{2}}f|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{1}}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{C}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{B}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{diag}(iI_{k},I_{d-k})}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{C}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{2}}f|\\
&=\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{diag}(W_{2}^{t},W_{1})}\left(|\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{C}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{2}}f|\otimes|\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{B}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{diag}(iI_{k},I_{d-k})}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{C}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{W_{2}}f|\right).\end{split}

Now set
\Omega=\mathrm{diag}(W_{2}^{t},W_{1}B)\in\mathrm{GL}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{V}}_{C}\mathcal{D}_{W_{2}}\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Just as in the case of the sesquilinear forms, the combination of the
factorization of Lemma
5.5
with the uncertainty principles of
Theorems
5.4
and

5.3
generates uncertainty principles for pairs of metaplectic
operators.
Equivalently, by Remark
5.1
, these
can be formulated as uncertainty principles for quadratic metaplectic time-frequency representations.

Theorem 5.6

(Beurling-type)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{V}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
and
\Omega=\Omega(V)
be the matrix introduced
in Lemma
5.5
. Assume that
V\in U(d,{\mathbb{C}})
is not real-valued. If
f\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfies

(5.21)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|(f\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}f)(\lambda)|e^{\pi|\Omega^{-1}\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\Omega^{-1}\lambda|}\,d\lambda<\infty,

then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same pattern as Theorem
4.4
.
In the notation of Lemma
5.5
, the hypothesis (
5.21
) is equivalent to

(5.22)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}}|(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f)(\lambda)|e^{\pi|\lambda\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}\lambda|}\,d\lambda<\infty.

Noting that the exponent equals
2\pi|x\cdot\omega|
, we apply Theorem
5.4
and obtain
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f\equiv 0
, whence
f\equiv 0
.

Theorem 5.7

(Hardy-type)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{V}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
and
\Omega=\Omega(V)
be the matrix introduced in Lemma
5.5
.
Assume that
V
is not real-valued. If
f\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfies

(5.23)

|(f\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V}f)(\lambda)|\leq ce^{-\pi\alpha|\Omega^{-1}\lambda\cdot\mathrm{diag}(\alpha I_{d},\beta I_{d})\Omega^{-1}\lambda|}\quad\text{for a.a. }\lambda\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}

for some
\alpha,\beta,c>0
with
\alpha\beta>1
,
then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

In the notation of Lemma
5.5
, (
5.23
) is equivalent to

(5.24)

|\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f|(x,\omega)\leq\tilde{c}e^{-\pi(\alpha\lVert x\rVert^{2}+\beta\lVert\omega\rVert^{2})}\quad\text{for a.a. }\lambda=(x,\omega)\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}.

By Theorem
5.3
,
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}f\equiv 0
. Equivalently,
f\equiv 0
.

Note that Theorem
5.7
also follows from Theorem
5.6
.

Theorems
5.6
and
5.7
are uncertainty principles for a symplectic pair
(f,\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{V})
in place of a Fourier pair
(f,\widehat{f})
. We leave it to
the reader to translate these results to uncertainty principle for
\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}
by
reversing the steps of the reduction in
Remark
5.1
. This amounts to an unpleasant and
by no means trivial work of book-keeping that, in our opinion, does
not seem to offer any new insight.

For a comparison to other approaches to Hardy’s uncertainty principle for metaplectic
pairs we refer to
[
13
,
8
]
.

6.
Quantitative uncertainty principles

So far, we have discussed qualitative uncertainty principles, in other words, a condition on

\mathrm{W}_{{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}(f,g)
implies that either
f
or
g
vanishes. In some
cases, for instance, in a limiting case of Hardy’s uncertainty principle, we can
derive a certain shape for
f
and
g
and obtain a quantitative uncertainty principles. In this section we offer two examples of such quantitative uncertainty principles. Its purpose is to demonstrate that the method outlined in Section
3

is also applicable to quantitative uncertainty principles.

We first present a complete version of a Hardy-type uncertainty principle for the
partial short-time Fourier transform.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
1\leq k\leq d
and assume that
f,g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
satisfy

(6.1)

|V_{g}^{k}f(\lambda)|\leq c(1+\lVert\lambda\rVert)^{N}e^{-\pi\alpha\lVert\lambda\rVert^{2}/2}\qquad\text{ for a. a. }\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}

for some
\alpha,c>0
and
N\geq 0
.

(i) If
\alpha>1
, then
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
.

(ii) If
\alpha=1
, then one of the following occurs.
Either
f\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
, or neither
f
nor
g
is the zero function and both can be written as

(6.2)

\begin{split}f(x_{1},x_{2})&=p(x_{1},x_{2})e^{2\pi i\omega_{0}\cdot x_{1}}e^{-\pi(x_{1}-x_{0})A(x_{1}-x_{0})}\quad\text{for a. a. }(x_{1},x_{2})\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},\\
g(x_{1},x_{2})&=q(x_{1},x_{2})e^{2\pi i\omega_{0}\cdot x_{1}}e^{-\pi(x_{1}-x_{0})A(x_{1}-x_{0})}\quad\text{for a. a. }(x_{1},x_{2})\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}.\end{split}

with polynomials
p(\cdot,x_{2}),q(\cdot,\omega_{2})
whose degrees satisfy

\mathrm{deg}(p_{x_{2}})+\mathrm{deg}(q_{-\omega_{2}})\leq N
for almost all
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}
,
x_{0},\omega_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
, and
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
a real positive definite matrix.

(iii) If, in addition,
k=d
and
\alpha=1
, then
p
and
q
are polynomials,
i.e.,
f
and
g
are finite linear combinations of Hermite functions.

Proof.

(i) is covered by Theorem
3.5
.

(iii) is
[
4
, Cor. 6.6]
. We will use it in
the proof of the new case (ii).

(ii) Let
k<d
and
\alpha=1
. If either
f\equiv 0
or

g\equiv 0
, there is nothing to prove. So assume that both
functions are non-zero. This means that the sets

(6.3)

\begin{split}M_{1}=\{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-k}:f_{x_{2}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k}),f_{x_{2}}\not\equiv 0\}\\
M_{2}=\{\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-k}:g_{-\omega_{2}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-k}),g_{-\omega_{2}}\not\equiv 0\}\end{split}

both have positive measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d-k}
. On the complement of

M_{1}
the restriction
f_{x_{2}}
vanishes, except on a set of
measure
0
, and likewise for
M_{2}
. If
(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in M_{1}\times M_{2}
, then the assumption implies that

(6.4)

\begin{split}&|V_{g_{-\omega_{2}}}f_{x_{2}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})|=|V_{g}^{k}f(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|\\
&\leq c(1+\lVert(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\rVert)^{N}e^{-\pi\alpha(\lVert(x_{1},\omega_{1})\rVert^{2}+\lVert(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rVert^{2})/2}\\
&\leq ce^{-\pi\alpha\lVert(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rVert^{2}}(1+\lVert(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rVert)^{N}\cdot(1+\lVert(x_{1},\omega_{1})\rVert)^{N}e^{-\pi\alpha\lVert x_{1},\omega_{1}\rVert^{2}/2}\end{split}

for all
(x_{1},\omega_{1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}
. By a theorem of Bonami, Jaming,
and Demange
[
4
]
for the full short-time Fourier transform, there exist polynomials

p_{x_{2}}
and
q_{-\omega_{2}}
with degrees

\mathrm{deg}(p_{x_{2}})+\mathrm{deg}(q_{\omega_{2}})\leq N
and a
generalized Gaussian function, such that

\displaystyle f_{x_{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=p_{x_{2}}e^{2\pi i\omega_{0}\cdot x_{1}}e^{-\pi(x_{1}-x_{0})A(x_{1}-x_{0})}

\displaystyle g_{-\omega_{2}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=q_{-\omega_{2}}e^{2\pi i\omega_{0}\cdot x_{1}}e^{-\pi(x_{1}-x_{0})A(x_{1}-x_{0})}\,.

Since
f_{x_{2}}\equiv 0
for
x_{2}\not\in M_{1}
and
g_{-x_{2}}\equiv 0

for
x_{2}\not\in M_{2}
, the functions
f,g
have the claimed form.

In addition, one can say that the functions
p
and
q
are
polynomials in
x_{1}
and possess Gaussian decay in
x_{2}
, but
nothing more can be said.
Evidently, with minor adjustments, one can state a quantitative Beurling-type uncertainty principle.

As the second example, we discuss Nazarov’s uncertainty principle for metaplectic pairs. Nazarov’s uncertainty principle
[
28
]
is a quantitative version of Benedicks’s uncertainty principle.
For a Fourier pair
(f,\widehat{f})
its multidimensional version from
[
24
]
is encapsulated in the following inequality: for all sets
S,T\subseteq{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
of finite measure

(6.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}dx\leq Ce^{C\min(|S|\cdot|T|,|S|^{1/d}w(T),|T|^{1/d}w(S))}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\setminus S}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\setminus T}|\widehat{f}(x)|^{2}dx\right).

Here
C
is an absolute constant and
w(S)
denotes the mean width of
a measurable set
S\subseteq{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
. We refer to
[
24
]
for
further details.
To simplify the statement, we denote with
\mathrm{nc}(S,T)
the quantity

\mathrm{nc}(S,T)=Ce^{C\min(|S|\cdot|T|,|S|^{1/d}w(T),|T|^{1/d}w(S))}.

We now state a Nazarov-type uncertainty principle for pairs of metaplectic operators.

Theorem 6.2

(Nazarov-type)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}={\mathcal{V}}_{Q_{1}}\mathcal{D}_{L_{1}}\mathcal{R}_{U_{1}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}={\mathcal{V}}_{Q_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{L_{2}}\mathcal{R}_{U_{2}}\in{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,{\mathbb{R}})}
.
If the imaginary part
{\mathrm{Im}\,}(U)
of

7.
Relaxations and generalizations

7.1.
Some relaxation of conditions

In the proof of Theorem
3.4
we saw that
the symmetric condition (
3.6
) could be
replaced by the weaker condition

(7.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2k}}\frac{|V^{k}_{g}f(x_{1},x_{2},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|}{(1+\lVert x_{1}\rVert+\lVert\omega_{1}\rVert)^{N}}e^{\pi|x_{1}\cdot\omega_{1}|}\,dx_{1}d\omega_{1}<\infty\quad\text{for a.a. }(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}.

for
almost all cross-sections
of
V^{k}_{g}f
. Such a relaxation
also works for other uncertainty principles stated, e.g. for Theorem

3.5
and Theorem

3.6
, for the quadratic time-frequency representations in
Section
5
, and the quantitative uncertainty principles in Theorem

6.1
.

We observe that condition (
7.1
) is
the maximal relaxation. One can show that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exist non-zero functions
f=g\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
which violate (
7.1
) on a measurable set
P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2(d-k)}
of measure
\varepsilon
.
The easiest examples are functions of the type

f(x_{1},x_{2})=g(x_{1},x_{2})=e^{-\pi x_{1}\cdot x_{1}}\psi(x_{2})
, where
\psi\in C_{c}([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{d-k})
is a non-zero even function. The same prototype indicates the limitations of all other uncertainty principles discussed in this paper.

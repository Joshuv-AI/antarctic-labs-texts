Malle’s Conjecture for Galois octic fields over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}}
Arul Shankar, Ila Varma

Abstract
We compute the asymptotic number of octic number fields whose Galois groups over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} are isomorphic to D 4 D_{4} , the symmetries of a square, when ordering such fields by their absolute discriminants. In particular, we verify the strong form of Malle’s conjecture [ 20 ] for such octic D 4 D_{4} -fields and obtain the constant of proportionality. Our result answers the question of whether a positive proportion of Galois octic extensions of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} have non-abelian Galois group in the negative. We further demonstrate that the constant of proportionality satisfies the Malle–Bhargava principle [ 20 , 5 ] of being a product of local masses, despite the fact that this principle does not hold for discriminants of quartic D 4 D_{4} -fields (see [ 8 , 3 ] ). This is the first instance of asymptotics being recovered for a non-concentrated family (in the sense of [ 2 ] ) of number fields of Galois group neither abelian nor symmetric. Previously, this was only known for abelian fields [ 25 ] , degree- n n S n S_{n} -fields for n = 3 , 4 , 5 n=3,4,5 (in [ 10 , 4 , 6 ] ), and degree- 6 6 S 3 S_{3} -fields [ 7 ] .

1.1
Background on relevant techniques

Our proof techniques build on work of Cohen–Diaz y Diaz–Olivier
[
8
]
, who
determine asymptotics for the number of quartic
D_{4}
-number fields,
ordered by discriminant. They observe that every such
D_{4}
-field
L

is simply a quadratic extension of a quadratic field
K
. Moreover,
the discriminant of
L
can be expressed as

\Delta(L)=\Delta(K)^{2}\cdot N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L).

Thus, the number of quartic
D_{4}
-number fields with discriminant
bounded by
X
can be expressed as a sum

\sum_{[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]=2}\#\bigl\{L:[L:K]=2,\,N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}|\Delta_{K}(L)|<X/(\Delta(K)^{2})\bigr\}.

For each fixed
K
, the summand in the above equation has
size
\asymp X/(\Delta(K)^{2+o(1)})
by work of Datskowski–Wright

[
9
]
. We should thus expect that only the quadratic fields
K
with
“small discriminants” (i.e.,
|\Delta(K)|\ll X^{\epsilon}
) contribute to
the asymptotics. For these “small” quadratic fields, the asymptotic
of the corresponding summand is known (again by
Datskowski–Wright), and adding up the terms over quadratic fields with small discriminants does not introduce any large error terms. To obtain the result then, what is needed is a tail estimate on the number of
D_{4}
-fields with discriminants less than
X
, whose quadratic subfields
K
have “large discriminants”, i.e.,
|\Delta(K)|\gg X^{\epsilon}
. Cohen–Diaz y Diaz–Olivier prove such an estimate and obtain their result.

In fact, the proof technique of
[
8
]
generalizes quite widely.
Consider the question of counting composite extensions
L/K/{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
we have some control over the asymptotics of the number of fields
K

(ordered by an invariant
{\rm I}(K)
) as well as control over the asymptotics of
relative extensions
L/K
, ordered by some invariant
{\rm J}_{K}(L)
, for
each fixed
K
. Suppose we are looking to count the number of such
extensions
L/{\mathbb{Q}}
, ordered by
{\rm Inv}(L)={\rm J}_{K}(L)\cdot{\rm I}(K)^{\kappa}
, where

\kappa
is sufficiently large. By sufficiently large, we mean that

100\%
of the extensions
L/K/{\mathbb{Q}}
should be concentrated on the
“small” subfields
K
, i.e., fields
K
for which
{\rm I}(K)\ll X^{\epsilon}
. In
[
2
]
, such families of number fields are termed
concentrated
. Determining the asymptotics of the number of fields
L
,
ordered by
{\rm Inv}(L)
, can be done in the following two steps: first,
for small subfields
K
, determine the exact count of
relative extensions
L/K
and sum them up over the small fields, and second, prove a uniform bound on the number of fields
L
with large subfields

K
. Such uniform bounds are difficult to obtain in general. However, this
method has been used to great effect, see for example

[
13
,
1
]
and
[
2
]
, in which many cases of the strong form of Malle’s conjecture are proved.

Note also that concentrated families of number fields of composite extensions are
not
be expected to satisfy the Malle–Bhargava principle. Indeed, we would expect these families to fail even the weaker condition of independence of splitting conditions at different primes. This was proven for the family of
S_{3}
-fields under many different orderings in
[
22
]
, and the proof generalizes to other concentrated families of composite extensions for which asymptotics are known without significant change.

In joint work with Altug and Wilson
[
3
]
, we determine
the asymptotics of quartic
D_{4}
-fields
L
, ordered by the
Artin conductor
{\rm C}
corresponding to the irreducible
2
-dimensional
representation of
D_{4}
. In this case, denoting the quadratic subfield
of
L
by
K
, we have

{\rm C}(L)=\Delta(K)\cdot\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L).

In particular, the
\kappa
from above is equal to
1
, and not
sufficiently large. As a consequence, it is no longer true that

100\%
of
D_{4}
-fields, when ordered by conductor, are
supported on quadratic fields with small discriminant. Every dyadic range for the
discriminant of
K
contributes roughly equal amounts to the count of

L
’s. The proof in
[
3
]
then requires two new ingredients. First, after
counting quadratic extensions
L/K
for fields
K
with
\Delta(K)\leq X^{1/2}
(which can be done using the methods of
[
8
]
), the argument relies on
reducing the count of
L/K
with
\Delta(K)>X^{1/2}
to the former count
via an algebraic input. Second, to explicitly determine the
asymptotic constants in order to verify the Malle–Bhargava principle in this conductor analogue,
geometry–of–numbers methods (on the parameter space of quartic
rings) are applied to recover the same counts.

1.2
Method of proof

In this paper, we handle families of octic
D_{4}
-fields
M
via
studying corresponding families of quadratic extensions
L
of
quadratic fields
K
. To describe how the discriminant of
M
relates
to the invariants of
L
and
K
, we consider a slightly simplified
situation.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
denote the set of Galois octic
D_{4}
-fields

M
, such that
2
does not ramify in
M
and no rational prime
p
totally
ramifies in
M
, i.e.,
p{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\neq\mathcal{P}^{8}
for any rational prime
p
and any prime
\mathcal{P}
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
. For
M\in{\mathcal{F}}
, denote a quartic

D_{4}
-subfield of
M
by
L
, and the quadratic subfield of
L
by

K
. Then, up to some bounded powers of
2
, we have

\Delta(M)=\Delta(K)^{4}\cdot\rad(\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L))^{4},

where
\rad(n)
denotes the radical of an integer
n
or equivalently,
the product of primes dividing
n
. The presence of the radical
complicates our situation in multiple ways. First, since this is not a
polynomial invariant of the coefficients of the coregular space

{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\otimes{\rm Sym}^{3}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})
of quartic rings, geometry–of–number
methods, which rely on using an invariant whose definition can be extended to the real space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\otimes{\rm Sym}^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, are not available. Instead, for quadratic fields

K
, we work with the Dirichlet series

\Phi_{K}(s)=\sum_{[L:K]=2}\frac{1}{\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L)^{s}}.

To count quadratic extensions
L
of
K
ordered by

\rad(\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L))
, we modify
\Phi_{K}(s)
into a double
Dirichlet series: the Euler factor at
p
of one of the ratios of

L
-functions is of the form
1+c/p^{s}+d/p^{2s}
. We modify each such
Euler factor into
1+c/p^{s}+d/p^{t}
yielding a double Dirichlet series

\Phi_{K}(s,t)
. Then the function
\Phi_{K}(s,2s)
counts quadratic
extensions
L
of
K
ordered by
\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L)
, while

\Phi_{K}(s,s)
counts quadratic extensions
L
of
K
ordered by

\rad(\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L))
.

The Dirichlet series
\Phi_{K}(s,2s)
is a sum (of length
\asymp{\rm Cl}(K)[2]
) of ratios of Hecke
L
-functions corresponding to

K
. Similarly,
\Phi_{K}(s,s)
can be expressed as a sum
of functions whose analytic properties are well understood. However,
two crucial difficulties arise when considering
\Phi_{K}(s,s)
instead of
\Phi_{K}(s,2s)
:

(a)

First:
V_{4}
-number fields

L/{\mathbb{Q}}
contribute a positive proportion to this count, while
their numbers were easily seen to be negligible in the previous two orderings (quartic discriminant and Artin conductor).

(b)

Second: each nontrivial summand of
\Phi_{K}(s,s)
has a simple pole at

s=1
, while the summand corresponding to the trivial character has
a double pole at
s=1
. Previously, the summand of
\Phi_{K}(s,2s)

corresponding to the trivial character had a single pole at
s=1
,
while all other summands were analytic to the right of
\mathfrak{Re}(s)=1/2
.

Item (b) above introduces a very new phenomena to our problem: Note that
the difference between a double pole and a single pole corresponds to merely a
factor of
(\log X)
in the asymptotics, while the difference between
a single pole and no pole corresponds to a postive power of
X
. As a consequence,
the (
\asymp\#{\rm Cl}(K)[2]
) summands to
\Phi_{K}(s,s)
corresponding to
non-trivial Hecke characters contribute nontrivially to the main term
in the count of quartic fields
L/K
, ordered by
\Delta(K)\cdot\rad(\Nm_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta_{K}(L))
.

Surprisingly, these two difficulties can be simultaneously
resolved! We prove that the nontrivial characters contribute
predominantly to the count of
V_{4}
-fields
L
, with only a negligible
contribution to the count of
D_{4}
-fields. Furthermore, we prove that
the trivial character contributes predominantly to the count of

D_{4}
-number fields. This situation is intriguingly parallel to that in Bhargava’s landmark work
[
4
]
determining asymptotics for the number of quartic
S_{4}
-number fields, in which he developed geometry-of-numbers methods and faced two major difficulties. First,
D_{4}
-fields constitute a positive proportion to the count of all quartic fields, and their contribution needs to be separated from that of
S_{4}
-fields. Second, the fundamental domain containing the lattice points parametrizing quartic rings had complicated cuspidal regions, and their contributions needed to be bounded or otherwise excluded from the main count. To resolve these (also simultaneously), Bhargava proved that
100\%
of the
D_{4}
-fields are contained in the cuspidal regions and also that the points in the cuspidal regions do not contribute to the
S_{4}
-field count!

Therefore, to obtain asymptotics for

D_{4}
-fields, ordered by the discriminant of their Galois
closures, it suffices to study the asymptotics of the counting
functions arising only from the trivial Hecke characters of quadratic
fields. In this way, we are able to express the asymptotics of the
number of
D_{4}
-fields, ordered by discriminant, in terms of a sum
over
K
of the double pole residue of
\Phi_{K}(s,s)
. Once this is done, we need a new technique to obtain asymptotics for this sum of residues. In our previous work counting
D_{4}
-fields by conductor, we had relied upon the parameter space of
D_{4}
-rings, and used geometry-of-numbers techniques to determine the asymptotic constant. In our case, this method is not available. Instead, we
relate the double pole residue of
\Phi_{K}(s,2s)
to the single pole residue of

\Phi_{K}(s,2s)
, use the results from
[
3
]
to evaluate the sum of these single pole residues over
K
, and thereby obtain our asymptotic constant.

This paper is organized as follows: We begin by discussing families of
octic
D_{4}
-fields, of quartic
D_{4}
-fields, and of quadratic
extensions of quadratic fields in §2. In §3, we fix a quadratic
field
K
and follow Wright
[
25
]
to express the counting
function of quadratic extensions of
K
in terms of a sum involving
Hecke
L
-functions of
K
. We then construct the appropriate double
Dirichlet functions associated to these counting functions in §4, and
study their analytic properties. Next, in §5, we obtain asymptotics for the number of quartic
D_{4}
-fields, ordered by a convenient height (which is related to the discriminant of the Galois closures of these quartic fields). Finally,
in §6, we prove Theorems
1
and
2
.

2
Preliminaries

Recall that
D_{4}
denotes the order-8 group of symmetries of a
square. We let
\sigma
denote a
90^{\circ}
-rotation
and
\tau
denote a reflection so that

D_{4}=\Bigl\langle\sigma,\ \tau\ \mid\ \sigma^{4}=1,\ \tau^{2}=1,\ \tau\sigma\tau=\sigma^{3}\Bigr\rangle.

The group
D_{4}
can also be interpreted as the wreath product of
C_{2}

with itself, i.e.

D_{4}\cong C_{2}\wr C_{2}\cong V_{4}\rtimes C_{2},

where
C_{2}

acts on
V_{4}\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
by permuting coordinates.

The group
D_{4}
has a nontrivial outer automorphism
\varphi
, which
can be described explicitly as follows:

\varphi(\sigma)=\sigma\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\varphi(\tau)=\sigma\tau.

Due to the existence of
\varphi
, any octic
D_{4}
-field
M

contains two conjugate pairs of non-isomorphic quartic
D_{4}
-fields.
If
M
is an octic
D_{4}
-field, we will denote by
L=L_{M}
the
(quartic) subfield fixed by the reflection subgroup
\langle\tau\rangle
. In addition, we denote the (quadratic) subfield
of
M
fixed by
\langle\tau,\sigma^{2}\rangle
by
K=K_{M}
. With these
choices,
L
is a quartic field whose normal closure over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is
equal to
M
, and
K
is the quadratic subfield of
L
.

We say that
L
is a
quartic
D_{4}
-field
if its normal closure
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is an octic field
M=M_{L}
with Galois group over
{\mathbb{Q}}
equal
to
D_{4}
. There is a quartic
D_{4}
-field contained in
M
that is
not isomorphic to
L
. We denote (the isomorphism class of) this field
by
L^{\prime}=\varphi_{4}(L)
, and its quadratic subfield by
K^{\prime}=\varphi_{2}(L)
.

2.1
Invariants of quadratic extensions of quadratic fields

Let
K
be a quadratic field and let
L
be a quadratic extension of

K
. In particular,
we employ the two fundamental invariants associated to
(L,K)
in
[
3

3.1

Let

let
\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}
and
\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}
respectively denote the ring
of
{\mathsf{S}}
-adeles and the group of
{\mathsf{S}}
-ideles of
K
. The ring of

{\mathsf{S}}
-integers,
{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}
, consists of all

such that

is integral away from prime ideals associated to places in
{\mathsf{S}}
.
Let
{\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K)
denote the
{\mathsf{S}}
-class group of

, i.e., the

K
by the subgroup generated by the
ideal classes of the prime ideals of
{\mathsf{S}}
. We begin with the
following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
{\mathsf{S}}
be a finite set of places of

K
containing all infinite places such that
{\rm Cl}_{{\mathsf{S}}}(K)
is
odd. Then there is a natural isomorphism

\Hom(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times},\{\pm 1\})\cong\Hom(\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times},\{\pm 1\}).

Proof.

We have the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times}\longrightarrow{\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K)\longrightarrow 0.

(4)

Since
{\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K)
is odd by assumption, it follows that

\Hom({\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K),\{\pm 1\})=0
. Therefore, the map

\Hom(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times},\{\pm 1\})\to\Hom(\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times},\{\pm 1\}),

(5)

obtained by composition, is an injection.

To prove that it is a surjection, fix a character

\chi:\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}\to\{\pm 1\}
. Let
n
denote
the size of
{\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K)
. For any
x\in\mathbb{A}_{K}/K^{\times}
, the element

has trivial image in
{\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K)
, hence there exists
y_{x}\in\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}
such that
\iota(y_{x})=x^{n}
. Define the character

\chi_{0}:\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times}\to\{\pm 1\}
by

\chi_{0}(x):=\chi(y_{x})

Now, if
z\in\mathbb{A}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathsf{S}}}^{\times}
and
\iota(z)=x
, then
y_{x}=z^{n}
. Since

is odd, we have

\chi_{0}\circ\iota(z)=\chi(y_{x})=\chi(z^{|{\rm Cl}_{\mathsf{S}}(K)|})=\chi(z).

Hence, the injection described
in (
5

follows.
∎

Now, let

4.2
Smoothed sums of Dirichlet coefficients

Recall that the Mellin transform
\widetilde{\phi}
of a smooth compactly supported
function of
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is
defined by

\widetilde{\phi}(s):=\int_{t>0}x^{s}\phi(x)d^{\times}x,

where
d^{\times}x
denotes
\frac{dx}{x}
. Standard properties of the
Mellin transform imply that
\widetilde{\phi}
is entire except for a
possible simple pole at
0
. Moreover,
\widetilde{\phi}
has
superpolynomial decay on vertical lines. Mellin inversion then states
that the following equality holds:

\phi(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathfrak{Re}(s)=2}x^{-s}\widetilde{\phi}(s)ds.

For a Dirichlet series
D(s)=\sum_{n}a_{n}n^{-s}
that is meromorphic
in
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2}
with at most a pole at
s=1
of order at most
2
,
and with at most polynomial growth on vertical strips, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\cdot\phi\Bigl(\frac{n}{x}\Bigr)&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathfrak{Re}(s)=2}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\Bigl(\frac{n}{x}\Bigr)^{-s}\widetilde{\phi}(s)ds\\[14.45377pt]
&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathfrak{Re}(s)=2}D(s)x^{s}\widetilde{\phi}(s)ds.\end{array}

Define the “error”
E(1/2+\epsilon,D)
by

E(1/2+\epsilon,D):=\max_{|t|\ll x^{\epsilon}}\{|D\bigl({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+\epsilon+it\bigr)|\}

(23)

Pulling back the line of integration to
\mathfrak{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
, we
pick up the main term from the (possible) double pole of
D(s)
at

s=1
, obtaining the following result.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is a smooth compactly supported function and

is a Dirichlet series that is meromorphic in
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2}
with at most a pole at

of order at most 2 and with at most polynomial growth on verticial strips, then we have

\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\cdot\phi\Bigl(\frac{n}{x}\Bigr)=\bigl(\Res^{(2)}_{s=1}D(s)\bigr)X\log X+\bigl(\Res^{(1)}_{s=1}D(s)\bigr)X+O_{\epsilon}\bigl(x^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}E(1/2+\epsilon,D)\bigr),

where
E(1/2+\epsilon,D)
is given in (
23
).

Next, we assume that
D(s)
is analytic at
s=1
and estimate its
special value at
1
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
D(s)=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}n^{-s}
be holomorphic in
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2}
with at most
polynomial growth in vertical strips. Pick a smooth compactly
supported function
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
such that

\widetilde{\phi}
has a simple pole at

is given in (
23
).

Proof.

For
x>0
and

0<\kappa<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\sum_{n>0}\frac{a_{n}}{n}\phi\Bigl(\frac{n}{x}\Bigr)&=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathfrak{Re}(s)=2}\sum_{n>0}\frac{a_{n}}{n}\Bigl(\frac{n}{x}\Bigr)^{-s}\widetilde{\phi}(s)ds\\[14.45377pt]

(24)

The result now follows by picking

Proposition 5.3

, it follows that

We next estimate

Theorem 5.4
.

Denote the product

\displaystyle N_{D_{4},\phi}^{(\kappa)}(\Lambda;Y,X_{\sigma^{2}},X_{\tau})=\displaystyle{\rm Vol}(\phi)^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(\Lambda)\\

N_{D_{4}}^{(\kappa)}(\Lambda(T);Y,X_{\sigma^{2}},X_{\tau})\ll_{\Lambda,A,\epsilon}XT^{\epsilon}+\frac{T^{1/2-\delta+\epsilon}X}{(\log X)^{\frac{A}{4}}}

Proof.

From (

5.3
Computing the constant

In this section, we evaluate the leading constant in the main term of
Theorem
5.4
. Analogously to the quantity

{\rm Mass}^{\flat}(\Lambda)
, we define

{\rm Mass}_{C}^{\flat}(\Lambda)=\Bigl(\sum_{(L,K)\in\Lambda_{\infty}}\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(L,K)}\Bigr)\prod_{p\in S(\Lambda)}\Bigl(\sum_{(L,K)\in\Lambda_{p}}\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(L,K)}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{2}.

Lemma 5.5

Let

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(\Lambda)\\

The function
{\mathcal{D}}^{(I)}_{\Lambda}(s;K)
is the counting function for the set of

L
of
K
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{4}(\Lambda)
, ordered by
{\rm H}_{I}
, where for such a pair

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle(\kappa Q)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(\Lambda)\\

Next, we compute the weighted average of the double pole residues.

Proposition 5.6
.

We have

\lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac{1}{\kappa Y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}(\Sigma)\\

First note that we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\Res_{s=1}^{(2)}{\mathcal{D}}_{\Lambda}(s,s;K)&=&\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{D}}_{\Lambda}(s,s;K)}{\zeta(s)^{2}}\Bigl|_{s=1}\,\,=\,\,\displaystyle\prod_{p}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{2}{\mathcal{D}}_{\Lambda,p}(s,s;K)\Bigl|_{s=1}\\[14.45377pt]

The convergence in the above equation is absolute and uniform in
K

since the
n

series are uniformly bounded by
O_{\epsilon}(n^{\epsilon})
, and
supported on powerfull integers

we see that the left hand side of the displayed equation in the lemma

\frac{1}{2}\cdot{\rm Mass}^{\flat}_{C}(\Lambda)\cdot\prod_{p\in S(\Lambda)}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)\cdot\prod_{p\not\in S(\Lambda)}\Bigl(1+\frac{3}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{3}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Mass}^{\flat}(\Lambda)\cdot\prod_{p\not\in S(\Lambda)}\Bigl(1+\frac{3}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)^{3},

as necessary.

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
Let

Y,X_{\sigma^{2}},X_{\tau}

\geq(\log Z)^{A}

In this section, we prove Theorems

and
2

D_{4}
-octic fields with large total ramification.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X>Z>0
be real numbers. The number of octic
D_{4}
-fields
M
such
that
\Delta(M)<X
and the product of primes at which
M
totally
ramifies is greater than
Z
is bounded by

O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X^{1/4}(\log X)^{2}}{Z^{\delta-\epsilon}}\right)

for some
\delta>0
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
denote the (acceptable with finite total ramification)
collection of quartic
D_{4}
splitting conditions which prohibits total
ramification at all odd primes. Note that we have

S(\Lambda)=\{2,\infty\}
. For an odd squarefree positive integer
T
,
the collection
\Lambda(T)
imposes total ramification at all the
primes dividing
T
, and prohibits total ramification at all other odd
primes. If
M
is an octic field satisfying the conditions of the
result, then
M
has a quartic subfield
L
with quadratic subfield

K
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

The pair
(L,K)
belongs to
{\mathcal{F}}_{4}(\Lambda(T))
for
some odd squarefree
T>Z
.

(b)

The
S(\Lambda(T))
-height of
(L,K)
satisfies

H(L,K)\ll X^{1/4}/T^{3/2}
.

(c)

We have

{\rm d}^{S(\Lambda(T))}(L,K)<{\rm q}_{\tau}^{S(\Lambda(T))}(L,K)
.

From Proposition
5.2
, it follows that the number of

D_{4}
-pairs satisfying the above three conditions, whose invariants lie
in the cuspidal region, is bounded by
O_{A,\epsilon}(X^{1/4}(\log X)^{1+\epsilon}/T^{3/2-\epsilon})
. Applying Theorem
5.4

for invariants in the main body, and summing over dyadic ranges of

Y
,
X_{\sigma^{2}}
, and
X_{\tau}
implies that the total number of

D_{4}
-pairs satisfying the above three conditions is bounded by

O_{A,\epsilon}\Bigl(\frac{X^{1/4}(\log X)^{2}}{T^{3/2-\epsilon}}+\frac{X^{1/4}}{(\log X)^{\frac{A}{4}-2}T^{1+\delta-\epsilon}}\Bigr).

Summing over
T>Z
yields the result.
∎

Theorems

and

allow us to count

for acceptable collections

denotes

.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be an acceptable collection of quartic local
specifications. Then

N_{\Delta}(\Lambda;X)=\frac{1}{4}\Bigl(\sum_{(L,K)\in\Lambda_{\infty}}\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(L,K)}\Bigr)\prod_{p}\Bigl(\sum_{(L,K)\in\Lambda_{p}}\frac{|\Delta(M_{(L,K)})|_{p}^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\#{\rm Aut}(L,K)}\Bigr)X^{\frac{1}{4}}\log^{2}X^{\frac{1}{4}}+o(X^{\frac{1}{4}}\log^{2}X),

where

is the octic algebra corresponding to
(L,K)
.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda
is acceptable, it follows that there exists a finite
set
S_{\Lambda}
of primes, containing
2
, such that if

, then
\Lambda_{p}
contains all non-totally ramified pairs.
We assume that
\Lambda
is a
discriminant stable
collection,
i.e., the set
\Lambda_{p}
consists of elements whose octic
discriminants have the same valuation
\lambda_{p}
for every finite

. By additivity, the general result clearly
follows from the result on discriminant stable collections, and so
there is no loss of generality in this assumption. For a positive
integer
T
not divisible by any primes in
S_{\Lambda}
, we construct a
collection
\Lambda(T)=(\Lambda(T)_{v})_{v}
of quartic
D_{4}
local

and
p(v)\nmid T

\Lambda(T)_{v}=\Lambda_{v}
for all infinite places and for
p\in S_{\Sigma}
; for
p\mid T
, define
\Lambda(T)_{p}
to be
the set of all totally ramified extensions; for
p\not\in S_{\Sigma}

and
p\nmid T
, define
\Lambda(T)_{p}
to be the set of all extensions
which are not totally ramified. Clearly

is acceptable and
has finite total ramification.

Let
H_{T}
denote the
S(\Lambda(T))
-height on

.
Consider a pair

over

by

. Then we have

Therefore, from Theorem

, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle N_{\Delta}(\Lambda(T);X)&=&\displaystyle N_{D_{4}}\Bigl(\Lambda;\Bigl(\prod_{p\in S_{\Lambda}}\lambda_{p}^{-1/4}\Bigr)T^{-3/2}X^{1/4}\Bigr)\\[14.45377pt]
&\sim&\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}{\rm Mass}^{\flat}(\Lambda(T))\Bigl(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\not\in S_{\Lambda}\\

We simplify the above by expanding

Let

Let

be any place of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and

denote the set of isomorphism classes of octic

-type over

-type uniquely determines an étale octic extension of

can be canonically identified with the set of

-types over

{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
and
\Lambda_{v}^{\rm all}

Proposition 6.3
.

There is a natural bijection between the set of
D_{4}
-types over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
and
\Lambda_{v}^{\rm all}
. Moreover, if the
D_{4}
-type
\rho
corresponds to
(L,K)\in\Lambda_{v}^{\rm all}
under the above bijection, then the groups
{\rm Aut}([\rho])
and
{\rm Aut}(L,K)
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Consider the set
\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{Q}}_{v},S_{4})
of conjugacy classes of continuous homomorphisms
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to S_{4}
, up to conjugacy by elements in
S_{4}
. Following the treatment in
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
, we describe a bijection between
\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{Q}}_{v},S_{4})
and étale quartic algebras of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
: given an étale quartic algebra
L
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
, we see that
L\otimes\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{v}\cong\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{v}^{4}
contains
4
minimal idempotents. Then the action of
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}
on
L\otimes\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{v}
, given by the trivial action on the first factor and the usual action on the second, fixes this set of minimal idempotents, yielding a continuous homomorphism
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to S_{4}
. Conversely,
a continuous homomorphism
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to S_{4}
gives an action of
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}
on
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{v}^{4}
. The set of invariants for this action forms a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
-subalgebra which is the required étale quartic extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
. Moreover, as explained in the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
, if
L
is an étale quartic algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
corresponding to the representation
\rho:G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to S_{4}
, then the group
{\rm Aut}(L)
is isomorphic with the group of
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}
-equivariant permutations of the minimal idempotents in
L\otimes\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{v}
, which in turn can be identified with the centralizer of
\rm{Im}(\rho)
in
S_{4}
.

Now suppose that
\rho
is a
D_{4}
-type, i.e.,
\rho:G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to D_{4}
is a continuous homomorphism. We think of
D_{4}
as acting on the vertices of a square, and thus fix an embedding
D_{4}\to S_{4}
. From the above construction, we obtain an étale quartic extension
L
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
corresponding to
\rho^{\prime}:G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to D_{4}\to S_{4}
. Being in the center of
D_{4}
, the element
\sigma^{2}
yields a nontrivial involution

. Let
K
denote the set of elements fixed by
c
, and note that

forms an étale quadratic extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
. Thus the pair
(L,K)
belongs to
\Lambda_{v}^{\rm all}
. Conversely, given a pair
(L,K)\in\Lambda_{v}^{\rm all}
, let
\rho^{\prime}:G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to S_{4}
be the continuous homomorphism corresponding to
L
. The involution
c
of
L
fixing
K
acts as a double transposition on the set of four minimal idempotents in

, which implies that the image of
\rho^{\prime}
is contained within the subgroup
D_{4}\subset S_{4}
of permutations of these idempotents which commute with
c
. Hence the pair
(L,K)
yields a
D_{4}
-type
\rho:G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}}\to D_{4}
. It is easy to check that these two constructions are inverses of each other, thus yielding the required bijection between the set of
D_{4}
-types of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
and
\Lambda_{v}^{\rm all}
.

Finally, keeping the above notation, note that
{\rm Aut}([\rho])
, which by definition is the centralizer of the image of
\rho
in
D_{4}
, is therefore equal to the subgroup of the centralizer of the image of
\rho^{\prime}
in
S_{4}
consisting of elements which commute with the double transposition corresponding to

. Since
c
generates
{\rm Aut}(L/K)
, we see that
{\rm Aut}([\rho])
is isomorphic to the subgroup of authomorphisms of
L
which fix
K
, as necessary.

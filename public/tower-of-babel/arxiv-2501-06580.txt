The curvature perturbation generated by thermal fluctuations during thermal inflation
Jeong-Myeong Bae, Hammam Raihan Mohammad, Ewan D. Stewart, Heeseung Zoe

Abstract
During thermal inflation, the temperature determines the number of e-folds of expansion of the universe and so thermal fluctuations are magnified into curvature perturbations. We use classical thermodynamics to calculate the subhorizon thermal fluctuations and trace their evolution into superhorizon temperature perturbations. We convert the temperature perturbations into curvature perturbations using the δ ​ N \delta N -formalism, or equivalently the junction condition of curvature perturbations at the end of thermal inflation, denoted by subscript c, and show that the late-time power spectrum is P ℛ = 15 4 ​ π 4 ​ H c 3 g ∗ ​ T c 3 ​ k 3 k c 3 P_{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{H^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}}{g_{*}T^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}}\frac{k^{3}}{k^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}} .



I
Introduction

In the case of primordial inflation, subhorizon
vacuum
fluctuations are magnified into superhorizon curvature perturbations.
Well after primordial inflation, moduli, expected in string theory, are generated and immediately dominate the energy density of the universe causing serious problems
Coughlan et al. 1983
;
de Carlos et al. 1993
;
Banks et al. 1994
.
Thermal inflation, a brief inflation driven by the potential of a flaton, an unstable flat direction, when it is held at the origin by thermal effects, can dilute the moduli to a safe abundance solving this moduli problem
Lyth and Stewart 1995
;
Lyth and Stewart 1996
.
Small-scale primordial perturbations that enter the horizon during the moduli domination before thermal inflation and leave during thermal inflation are suppressed
Hong et al. 2015
;
Bae et al. 2024
;
Cho et al. 2017
;
Hong et al. 2017
;
Leo et al. 2018
.

However, thermal inflation also generates its own perturbations.
During thermal inflation, suhorizon
thermal
fluctuations are magnified into superhorizon temperature perturbations.
At the end of thermal inflation, which occurs when the temperature drops below a critical temperature, the temperature perturbations are converted into superhorizon curvature perturbations.
These perturbations have a spectral index of
n=4
with a peak at the horizon size at the end of thermal inflation, as estimated in Appendix A.3 of
Kadota and Stewart 2003
.
In this note, we calculate these perturbations precisely.

One of the motivations of this work is to correct the misconceptions of
Dimopoulos et al. 2019
;
Lewicki et al. 2021
;
Bastero-Gil et al. 2024
, in which the authors simply assumed
flaton
fluctuations generate the curvature perturbations as the inflaton fluctuations do in primordial inflation.
However, the flaton is held at the origin during thermal inflation and so the flaton fluctuations are irrelevant.
Instead, it is
thermal
fluctuations that are magnified into curvature perturbations as described above and in more detail in Section
II
and
III
.

In Section
II
, we calculate the subhorizon thermal fluctuations using classical thermodynamics and evolve them into superhorizon temperature perturbations.
In Section
III
, we use the
\delta N
formalism
Sasaki and Stewart 1996
, or equivalently the junction condition at the end of thermal inflation
Hong et al. 2015
, to convert the temperature perturbations into curvature perturbations and determine their late-time power spectrum.
In Section
IV
, we discuss the implications of our results.

II
Subhorizon Thermal Fluctuations to Superhorizon Temperature Fluctuations

During thermal inflation, the temperature is much greater than the Hubble parameter,
T\gg H
, and, for modes well inside the horizon, the physical wave number is much greater than the Hubble parameter,
q\gg H
, so thermalization is faster than the Hubble expansion and the subhorizon temperature fluctuations can be described by classical thermodynamics in flat spacetime
Landau and Lifshitz 1980
.
The temperature fluctuations in a volume
V
are then

\Delta T=\frac{1}{V}\int_{V}d^{3}x\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(x\right)}}

(1)

where

\langle\Delta T^{2}\rangle=\frac{T^{2}}{C_{V}}

(2)

and the heat capacity of a thermal bath of radiation is

\displaystyle C_{V}=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_{V}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{15}g_{*}T^{3}V~.

(3)

The corresponding power spectrum of temperature fluctuations

\displaystyle\langle\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}\mathinner{\delta T^{*}\mathopen{\left(q^{\prime}\right)}}\rangle=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{q^{3}}\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(q-q^{\prime}\right)}}

(4)

is given by

\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{q^{3}}{g_{*}T}~.

(5)

which is derived in detail in Appendix
A
.

Applying this to a subhorizon scale at an intial time
t=t_{\mathrm{i}}
during thermal inflation, Eq. (
5
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{g_{*}T_{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{k^{3}}{a^{3}_{\mathrm{i}}}

(6)

which is derived in detail in Appendix
B
, which then evolves as the universe expands during thermal inflation.
Thermal equilibrium is preserved by the expansion, and so the form of subhorizon thermal fluctuations is preserved as it evolves to superhorizon scales

\displaystyle\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{1}{g_{*}T}\frac{k^{3}}{a^{3}}

(7)

which is derived in detail in Appendix
C
.

III
Temperature Perturbations to Curvature Perturbations

The
\delta N
formalism
Sasaki and Stewart 1996
gives the change in the superhorizon curvature perturbations between two times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
in terms of the number of e-folds

\displaystyle\mathinner{\mathcal{R}\mathopen{\left(t_{2}\right)}}-\mathinner{\mathcal{R}\mathopen{\left(t_{1}\right)}}=\delta N~.

(8)

Taking a flat hypersurface at
t_{1}
, this reduces to

\displaystyle\mathinner{\mathcal{R}\mathopen{\left(t_{2}\right)}}=\delta N

(9)

In the usual primordial slow-roll inflation with a single component inflaton, the inflaton
\varphi
determines the number of e-folds,
N=\mathinner{N\mathopen{\left(\varphi\right)}}
, and so

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\frac{\partial N}{\partial\varphi}\delta\varphi

(10)

while the slow-roll condition leads to

\frac{\partial N}{\partial\varphi}=-\frac{H}{\dot{\varphi}}.

(11)

In thermal inflation, inflation ends when the temperature drops below a critical temperature. Thus, the temperature determines the number of e-folds,
N=\mathinner{N\mathopen{\left(T\right)}}
, and so

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\frac{\partial N}{\partial T}\delta T

(12)

where the relation between temperature and spatial expansion gives

\frac{\partial N}{\partial T}=\frac{1}{T}.

(13)

We can also derive Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
) by matching the curvature perturbations on the constant radiation energy density hypersurface just before the end of thermal inflation

\mathcal{R}_{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}}}=\mathcal{R}-\frac{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}}}{\dot{\rho}_{\mathrm{r}}}H

(14)

to the curvature perturbation on the constant density hypersurface just after the end of thermal inflation

\mathcal{R}_{\delta\rho}=\mathcal{R}-\frac{\delta\rho}{\dot{\rho}}H

(15)

giving

\mathinner{\mathcal{R}_{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}}}\mathopen{\left(t_{\mathrm{c}}^{-}\right)}}=\mathinner{\mathcal{R}_{\delta\rho}\mathopen{\left(t_{\mathrm{c}}^{+}\right)}}

(16)

where subscript c denotes the end of thermal inflation
Hong et al. 2015
.
During thermal inflation, the curvature perturbations on constant radiation energy density hypersurfaces are related to the temperature perturbations on flat hypersurfaces

\mathcal{R}_{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}}}=-\frac{H}{\dot{\rho}_{\mathrm{r}}}\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{R}}}=\frac{\delta T}{T}

(17)

in agreement with Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
).

Therefore, the power spectrum of the late-time superhorizon curvature perturbations on constant energy density, or equivalently comoving, hypersurfaces is

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{R}}=\left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial T}\right)^{2}P_{\delta T}

(18)

leading to

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{H^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}}{g_{*}T^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}}\frac{k^{3}}{k^{3}_{\mathrm{c}}}

(19)

where
k_{\mathrm{c}}=a_{\mathrm{c}}H_{\mathrm{c}}
is the comoving horizon scale at the end of thermal inflation.
Eq. (
19
) is valid for modes that leave the horizon during thermal inflation,
k_{\mathrm{b}}\ll k\ll k_{\mathrm{c}}
, where subscript b denotes the beginning of thermal inflation.

IV
Discussion

In this note, we calculate the curvature perturbations generated by thermal fluctuations during thermal inflation.
We use classical thermodynamics in flat spacetime to calculate the thermal fluctuations well inside the horizon and evolve them into superhorizon temperature perturbations obtaining the temperature power spectrum of Eq. (
7
).
We apply the
\delta N
formalism, or equivalently the junction condition at the end of thermal inflation, to Eq. (
7
) and derive the late-time curvature perturbations of Eq. (
19
).
In
Dimopoulos et al. 2019
;
Lewicki et al. 2021
;
Bastero-Gil et al. 2024
, the authors incorrectly considered flaton fluctuations, instead of thermal fluctuations, to calculate the curvature perturbations and so arrived at incorrect results.
Our power spectrum can also be potentially probed by primordial black holes
Dimopoulos et al. 2019
and induced gravitational waves
Lewicki et al. 2021
.

Appendix A
Thermal fluctuation power spectrum

Using Eqs. (
1
) and (
4
), the dispersion of thermal fluctuations in a sphere of radius
R
is

\displaystyle\langle\Delta T^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{V^{2}}\int_{V}d^{3}x\int_{V}d^{3}x^{\prime}\langle\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(x\right)}}\mathinner{\delta T^{*}\mathopen{\left(x^{\prime}\right)}}\rangle=\frac{R^{7}}{V^{2}}\int dq\mathinner{W\mathopen{\left(qR\right)}}\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}

(20)

where

\mathinner{W\mathopen{\left(x\right)}}=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{x^{7}}\left(\sin x-x\cos x\right)^{2}~.

(21)

Using the ansatz

\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}=Aq^{\alpha}~,

(22)

matching to Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) gives

A=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}g_{*}T},\quad\alpha=3.

(23)

Therefore, the power spectrum of thermal fluctuations is

\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}=\frac{15}{4\pi^{4}}\frac{q^{3}}{g_{*}T}~.

(24)

Appendix B
Conversion between physical and comoving thermal fluctuations

We define the physical Fourier component of
the thermal fluctuations by

\delta T=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{3/2}}\int d^{3}q\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}e^{iq\cdot x_{p}},

(25)

and the comoving Fourier component by

\delta T=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{3/2}}\int d^{3}k\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}e^{ik\cdot x_{c}}.

(26)

Eq. (
4
) is

\displaystyle\langle\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}\mathinner{\delta T^{*}\mathopen{\left(q^{\prime}\right)}}\rangle=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{q^{3}}\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(q-q^{\prime}\right)}}.

(27)

and similarly

\displaystyle\langle\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\mathinner{\delta T^{*}\mathopen{\left(k^{\prime}\right)}}\rangle=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(k-k^{\prime}\right)}}.

(28)

Due to the different Fourier measures, using
q=k/a
, we get

\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}=a^{3}\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}.

(29)

Using

\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(q-q^{\prime}\right)}}=a^{3}\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(k-k^{\prime}\right)}}~,

(30)

Eqs. (
27
), (
28
) and (
29
) give

\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}=\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(q\right)}}

(31)

implying that Eq. (
5
) is simply converted to Eq. (
6
).

Appendix C
Evolution of the temperature perturbation power spectrum

To model the evolution of the subhorizon thermal fluctuations for
t>t_{\mathrm{i}}
, we consider a simple system of vacuum energy and radiation.
For modes well inside the horizon, standard perturbation theory
Kodama and Sasaki 1984
gives

\displaystyle\delta\ddot{\rho}_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{R}}}+9H\delta\dot{\rho}_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{R}}}+\left(20H^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{3}\right)\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{R}}}=0~.

(32)

which has solution

\displaystyle\mathinner{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{R}}}\mathopen{\left(k,t\right)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\rho_{\mathrm{r}}\mathinner{C_{1}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\exp\left(i\int\frac{k}{\sqrt{3}\,a}dt\right)+4\rho_{\mathrm{r}}\mathinner{C_{2}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\exp\left(-i\int\frac{k}{\sqrt{3}\,a}dt\right)~.

(33)

Using

\frac{\delta T}{T}=\frac{\delta\rho_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathcal{R}}}}{4\rho_{\mathrm{r}}}~,

(34)

Eq. (
33
) gives the temperature perturbation

\displaystyle\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(k,t\right)}}=T\mathinner{C_{1}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\exp\left(i\int\frac{k}{\sqrt{3}\,a}dt\right)+T\mathinner{C_{2}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\exp\left(-i\int\frac{k}{\sqrt{3\,}a}dt\right)~.

(35)

Taking the ensemble average over random phases

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathinner{C_{i}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\mathinner{C^{*}_{j}\mathopen{\left(k^{\prime}\right)}}\right\rangle=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathinner{P_{i}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\delta_{ij}\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(k-k^{\prime}\right)}}~,

(36)

we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathinner{\delta T\mathopen{\left(k,t\right)}}\mathinner{\delta T^{*}\mathopen{\left(k^{\prime},t\right)}}\rangle=T^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\right)\left[\mathinner{P_{1}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}+\mathinner{P_{2}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\right]\mathinner{\delta^{3}\mathopen{\left(k-k^{\prime}\right)}}~.

(37)

By combining Eq. (
37
) with Eq. (
28
), we conclude

\displaystyle\mathinner{P_{\delta T}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}\propto\frac{1}{a^{2}}~.

(38)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be a standard graded ring over

. Assume Krull dimension of

is finite. Let

Let

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(E_{n+1})^{d}

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\rightarrow(x_{1}m,\cdots,x_{d}m)

In this section we discuss a few preliminaries on support varieties that we need.

6.1
.

Let
(Q,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring and let

and denote its maximal ideal by

.
The
Eisenbud operators
,
[

]
are constructed as follows:

Let
\mathbb{F}\colon\cdots\rightarrow F_{i+2}\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{i}\rightarrow\cdots
be a complex of free

A
-modules.

Step 1:
Choose a sequence of free
Q
-modules
\widetilde{F}_{i}
and maps
\widetilde{\partial}
between them:

\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}\colon\cdots\rightarrow\widetilde{F}_{i+2}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\partial}}\widetilde{F}_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\partial}}\widetilde{F}_{i}\rightarrow\cdots

so that
\mathbb{F}=A\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}
.

Step 2:
Since
\widetilde{\partial}^{2}\equiv 0\ \text{modulo}\ (\mathbf{f})

\widetilde{\partial}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{c}f_{j}\widetilde{t}_{j}
where

\widetilde{t_{j}}\colon\widetilde{F}_{i}\rightarrow\widetilde{F}_{i-2}
are linear maps for every

.

Step 3:

Define, for

the map
t_{j}=t_{j}(Q,\mathbf{f},\mathbb{F})\colon\mathbb{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}(-2)
by
t_{j}=A\otimes\widetilde{t}_{j}
.

6.2
.

The operators

are called Eisenbud’s operator’s (associated to
\mathbf{f}
) . It can be shown that

(1)

t_{i}
are uniquely determined up to homotopy.

(2)

t_{i},t_{j}
commute up to homotopy.

6.3
.

Let

be a polynomial ring over
A
with variables

of degree
2
. Let

be finitely generated
A
-modules. By considering a free resolution

of

which turn

into a module over

, are natural in both module arguments and commute with the connecting maps induced by short exact sequences.

6.4
.

Gulliksen,

We need to recall the notion of complexity of a module.

over

. Furthermore all values between

Note that

if and only if

has bounded Betti numbers.

Assume

11.
Some Properties of
L^{I}(M)

In this section we collect some of the properties of
L^{I}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M/I^{n+1}M
which we proved in
[
16
]
. Throughout this section

(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with infinite residue field,
M
is a
Cohen-Macaulay
module of dimension
r\geq 1
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.

11.1
.

Set
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
; the Rees Algebra of
I
.
The
A
-module
L^{I}(M)
can be given an
R(I)
-module structure as follows.
The Rees ring
\mathcal{R}
is a subring of
A[t]
and so
A[t]
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module.
Therefore
M[t]=M\otimes_{A}A[t]
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module.
Let
R(I,M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}M
be the Rees module of
M
with respect to
I
. It is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module.
The exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R(I,M)\longrightarrow M[t]\longrightarrow L^{I}(M)(-1)\longrightarrow 0

defines an
\mathcal{R}
-module structure on
L^{I}(M)(-1)
and so on
L^{I}(M)
.

Note that
L^{I}(M)
is
not a finitely generated

\mathcal{R}(I)
-module.

11.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
denote the maximal homogeneous ideal of
\mathcal{R}
. Let
H^{i}(-)=H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}}(-)
denote the
i^{th}
-local cohomology functor with respect to
\mathfrak{M}
.
Recall a graded
\mathcal{R}
-module
V
is said to be

*-Artinian
if
every descending chain of graded submodules of
V
terminates. For example if
E
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module then
H^{i}(E)
is *-Artinian for all

i\geq 0
.

11.3
.

In
[
16
, 4.7]
we proved that

H^{0}(L^{I}(M))=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{\widetilde{I^{n+1}M}}{I^{n+1}M}.

Here
\widetilde{KM}
denotes the Ratliff-Rush closure of
K
with respect to
M
. Recall

\widetilde{KM}=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}K^{i+1}M\colon K^{i}.

11.4
.

For
L^{I}(M)
we proved that for
0\leq i\leq r-1

(a)

H^{i}(L^{I}(M))
are *-Artinian; see
[
16
, 4.4]
.

(b)

H^{i}(L^{I}(M))_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
; see
[
16
, 1.10 ]
.

(c)

H^{i}(L^{I}(M))_{n}
has finite length
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
; see
[
16
, 6.4]
.

(d)

For
0\leq i\leq r-1

there exists polynomial
q_{i}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
such that
q_{i}(n)=\ell(H^{i}(L^{I}(M))_{n})
for all
n\ll 0
.

11.5
.

The natural maps
0\rightarrow I^{n}/I^{n+1}\rightarrow M/I^{n+1}\rightarrow M/I^{n}\rightarrow 0
induce an exact
sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

(3)

0\longrightarrow G_{I}(M)\longrightarrow L^{I}(M)\xrightarrow{\Pi}L^{I}(M)(-1)\longrightarrow 0.

We call (
3
)
the first fundamental exact sequence
. We use (
3
) also to relate the local cohomology of
G_{I}(M)
and
L^{I}(M)
.

11.6
.

If
E=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}E_{n}
is a
\mathcal{R}
-module is non-zero, set
\operatorname{end}E=\max\{n\mid E_{n}\neq 0\}
. Set
\operatorname{end}0=-\infty
.
Set
b_{i}^{I}(M)=\operatorname{end}H^{i}(L^{I}(M))
for
i=0,\ldots,r-1
. Set

\operatorname{reg}L^{I}(M)=\max\{b_{i}^{I}(M)+i\mid 0\leq i\leq r-1\}.

Also set

\operatorname{reg}^{\geq 1}L^{I}(M)=\max\{b_{i}^{I}(M)+i\mid 1\leq i\leq r-1\}.

11.7
.

Set
a_{i}(G_{I}(M))=\operatorname{end}H^{i}(G_{I}(M))
. Now assume that
M
is a MCM
A
-module. We have a surjection
A^{r}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
, yields a surjection
G_{I}(A^{r})\rightarrow G_{I}(M)\rightarrow 0
. So we have
a_{d}(G_{I}(M))\leq a_{d}(G_{I}(A))
. It follows from this and
11.5
that

\operatorname{reg}G_{I}(M)\leq\max\{\operatorname{reg}L^{I}(M)+2,a_{d}(G_{I}(A))+d\}.

11.8
.

Assume
\dim A=1
and
M
is a MCM
A
-module. Then note
b_{0}^{I}(M)\leq a_{1}(G_{I}(M))-1\leq a_{1}(G_{I}(A))-1
. By
11.7
it follows that
\operatorname{reg}G_{I}(M)
is bounded by

a_{1}(G_{I}(A))+1
. This bound only depends on
I
and is independent of
M
.

11.9
.

Let
x
be
M
-superficial with respect to
I
, i.e.,
(I^{n+1}M\colon x)=I^{n}M
for all
n\gg 0
. Set
N=M/xM
and
u=xt\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
. Notice
L^{I}(M)/uL^{I}(M)=L^{I}(N)
.
For each
n\geq 1
we have the following exact sequence of
A
-modules:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{I^{n+1}M\colon x}{I^{n}M}\longrightarrow\frac{M}{I^{n}M}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\psi_{n}}\frac{M}{I^{n+1}M}\longrightarrow\frac{N}{I^{n+1}N}\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad\psi_{n}(m+I^{n}M)

\displaystyle=xm+I^{n+1}M.

This sequence induces the following exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules:

(4)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)\longrightarrow L^{I}(M)(-1)\xrightarrow{\Psi_{u}}L^{I}(M)\xrightarrow{\rho^{x}}L^{I}(N)\longrightarrow 0,

where
\Psi_{u}
is left multiplication by
u
and

\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{(I^{n+1}M\colon_{M}x)}{I^{n}M}.

We call (
4
) the
second fundamental exact sequence.

11.10
.

Notice
\lambda\left(\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)\right)<\infty
. A standard trick yields the following long exact sequence connecting
the local cohomology of
L^{I}(M)
and

L^{I}(N)
:

(5)

\begin{split}0\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)&\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(M))\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(N))\\
&\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(M))\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(N))\\
&\cdots\cdots\\
\end{split}

We will need the following consequence of (
5
).

Lemma 11.11
.

Let
M
be Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of dimension
r\geq 2
. Let
x\in I
be
M
-superficial. Let
N=M/xM
. Then
\operatorname{reg}^{\geq 1}L^{I}(M)\leq\operatorname{reg}L^{I}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
0\leq i\leq r-2
. If
H^{i}(L^{I}(N))_{n}=0
for
n\geq c
then by
5
we have injections
H^{i+1}(L^{I}(M))_{n-1}\rightarrow H^{i+1}(L^{I}(M))_{n}
for
n\geq c
. As
H^{i+1}(L^{I}(M))_{n}=0

for
n\gg 0
it follows that
b_{i+1}^{I}(M)\leq b^{I}_{i}(N)-1
. The result follows.
∎

11.12
.

One huge advantage of considering
L^{I}(M)
is that it behaves well with respect to the Veronese functor. Notice

\left(L^{I}(M)(-1)\right)^{<t>}=L^{I^{t}}(M)(-1)\quad\text{for all}\ t\geq 1.

Also note that
\mathcal{R}(I)^{<t>}=\mathcal{R}(I^{t})
and that
(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I)})^{<t>}=\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I^{t})}
. It follows that for all
i\geq 0

\left(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I)}}(L^{I}(M)(-1)\right)^{<t>}\cong H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}(I^{t})}}(L^{I^{t}}(M)(-1).

We will need the following result.

Lemma 11.13
.

Let
M
be Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of dimension
r\geq 2
. Let
x\in I
be
M
-superficial. Let
N=M/xM
. Set
b=\operatorname{end}H^{0}(L^{I}(N))
. Let
t=\operatorname{end}H^{0}(L^{I^{m}}(M))
where
m>b
.
Then

\operatorname{end}H^{0}(L^{I}(M))\leq m(t+2)-1.

Proof.

By (
5
) and
11.3
we get surjections for
n>b
.

\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}M}}{I^{n}M}\rightarrow\frac{\widetilde{I^{n+1}M}}{I^{n+1}M}.

We have by
11.3

\widetilde{I^{m(t+2)}M}=I^{m(t+2)}M.

It follows that
\widetilde{I^{j}M}=I^{j}M
for all
j\geq m(t+2)
. The result follows.
∎

Theorem 17.1

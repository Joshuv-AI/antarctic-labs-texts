Nonderogatory matrices as sums of p p -potent and nilpotent matrices
Andrada Pojar

Abstract
. We prove that every nonderogatory matrix A A over a field of positive odd characteristic p , p, that is sum of a p p -potent matrix and a nilpotent matrix, has a decomposition A = E + V , A=E+V, such that E p = E , E^{p}=E, and V 3 = 0 . V^{3}=0.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
15A24, 15A83, 16U99

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.

As usual, the letter
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
will stand for the prime field of
p
elements having characteristic
p
, for any positive integer
n
, the notation
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
will denote the full matrix ring of
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F},

and
\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{F})
will denote the full matrix ring of matrices over
\mathbb{F},
with
m
rows and
n
columns.

A square matrix is
nonderogatory
if its characteristic
and minimal polynomials coincide. A matrix is nonderogatory if and only if it is similar to a
companion matrix
C
.
We recall that a companion matrix
C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is a matrix of the form

C=C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\ldots&0&-c_{0}\\
1&0&\ldots&0&-c_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&1&-c_{n-1}\end{array}\right).

A square matrix
E
over
\mathbb{F}
is
p
-potent if
E^{p}=E.
A square matrix
A
over
\mathbb{F}
is nil-clean if there exist an idempotent
E
and a nilpotent
N
such that
A=E+N.
We consider decompositions of nonderogatory matrices
A
such that there exist a
p
-potent
E
and a nilpotent
N,
with
A=E+N.

Nil-clean decompositions are related to clean ones, introduced by Nicholson in
[
10
]
, when investigating exchange rings and were first studied by Diesl in
[
7
]
. An important result appeared in
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
about them is: every
n\times n
matrix over a division ring
D
is nil-clean if and only if
D=\mathbb{F}_{2}.
A theme related to a set
S
of nilpotents of a ring is the study of boundness of the nilpotence index of S–the existence of a positive integer
n
such that
a^{n}=0
for all
a\in S
, and it was first studied first in
[
9
]
. Šter has proved in
[
13
]
that there exists a nil-clean decomposition of every matrix of
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2}),
with nilpotent
N
having nilpotence index at most
4
(i.e
N^{4}=0.
). Moreover this result has sharpness, that is there exist
4\times 4
companion matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2},
which cannot be decomposed as a sum of an idempotent and a nilpotent matrix of nilpotence index at most
3.

An extension of nil-clean decompositions to finite fields of odd cardinality
q,
was done in
[
1
]
: every matrix over such a field is the sum of a
q
-potent
E=E^{q}
and a nilpotent. In
[
2
]
there is even more – the nilpotent
N
involved in such a decomposition can be with nilpotence index at most
3
(i.e.
N^{3}=0).
Decompositions as a sum of a potent and square-zero matrix were considered in
[
6
]
.

In
[
4
]
it has been proved that if
\mathbb{F}
is a field of positive characteristic,
p,
then a companion matrix
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is nil-clean if and only if
A
is nilpotent or unipotent or the trace of
A
is of the form
t\cdot 1,
with
t\in\{1,2,\dots,p\}
such that
n>t
.

The characterization of companion matrix we are interested in is in the following Theorem:

Theorem 1
.

[
12
]

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.
Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer. Fix constants
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1}\in\mathbb{F}.

Let
C
be a companion matrix. Then
C
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent if and only if the trace of
C
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.

In
[
5
]
it has been proved that every nil-clean nonderogatory
n\times n
matrix
A,
over a field of positive characteristic,
p,
with trace of
A
not equal to
1,
can be written as
A=E+V,
such that
E^{2}=E,
and
V^{p+1}=0.
If
{\mathrm{Tr}}(A)=1,
then there is a similar decomposition with
V^{p+2}=0.
We prove that every nonderogatory matrix
A
over a field of positive odd characteristic
p,
that is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent,
has decomposition
A=E+V,
such that
E^{p}=E,
and
V^{3}=0.
Moreover, this result has sharpness, that is there exists a
3\times 3
companion matrix over a field of characteristic
3,
which is sum of a tripotent and a nilpotent, but cannot be decomposed as a sum of a tripotent and a nilpotent matrix of nilpotence index at most
2.

2.
Useful tools

The following Proposition has been proved for
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
[
11
]
. The same proof is valid for any field with with positive odd characteristic
p.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.

Let
n\geq 2
and
k
be positive integers such that
k<n.
Let
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-2\}.
Fix constants
d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{n-1}\in\mathbb{F}
and denote
D=C_{d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1}}+\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0).
For every polynomial
g\in\mathbb{F}[X]
of degree at most
n-2
there exist two matrices
E,

M
in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
such that:

(1)

D=E+M

(2)

E^{p}=E
, and

(3)

\chi_{M}=X^{n}+(k\cdot 1_{\mathbb{F}}+d_{n-1})X^{n-1}+g.

The following lemma provides a tool to obtain certain nonderogatory matrices.

Lemma 3
.

[
12
]

Let
k
and
n
be nonzero natural numbers,
n\geq k,
and
\mathbb{F}
be a field. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
be integer multiples of the unity. Every companion matrix
C\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is similar to
C^{\prime}+\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0)
for some companion matrix
C^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}).

Remark 4
.

The transition matrix
P,
from the canonical basis of
\mathbb{F}
to the basis in the proof of Lemma
3
,
is upper triangular, with all entries on the main diagonal equal to
1.

Lemma 5
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
n>1,

p
be an odd prime number,
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},

a\in\{2,3,\dots,p-1\},

\mathbb{F}
be a field with characteristic
p,
and
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
a companion matrix. If
\mbox{ }{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=t\cdot 1,
with
t\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\},

then there exists a
p
-potent matrix of the form

L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\cdot I_{k}&L_{1,2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)

such that
C-L
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a companion matrix, with
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=t.
We are going to prove that there exist
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
and
a\in\{2,3,\dots,p-1\}
such that
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=ka.
If
n\leq p,
then
t\neq 0,
and we choose
k\neq t,
and
a=k^{-1}\cdot t
; if
p<n
for
t\neq 0
we make the same choice of
k,
and
a;
if
t=0,
we choose
k=p,
and
a=2.

By Lemma
3
there exists a companion matrix
C^{\prime}
such that

C=P(\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a-1,\dots,a-1}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0)+C^{\prime})P^{-1},

where
P
is the transition matrix we stated in Remark
4
that it is upper triangular, with all entries on the main diagonal equal to
1.
Since
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C^{\prime})=(ka-k(a-1))\cdot 1=k\cdot 1,
it follows by the proof of Proposition
2
that
there exists a
p
-potent matrix of the form

U=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\cdot I_{k}&U_{1,2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)

such that
\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a-1,\dots,a-1}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0)+C^{\prime}-U=B,
with
B
nilpotent.
Therefore
C=P(U+B)P^{-1}.
Since
PUP^{-1}
has the form of
L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\cdot I_{k}&L_{1,2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right),
and because
PUP^{-1}
is
p
-potent,
PBP^{-1}
is nilpotent, the conclusion is true.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic
p,

n
a positive integer,
a
an integer multiple of unity and
L
an
n\times n
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{F},
such that
L^{p}=L,
then
(aI_{n}-L)^{p}=aI_{n}-L.

Proof.

For a natural number
m>2,
we denote as in
[
11
]
, by
\alpha_{1}(m)
-the smallest
\alpha\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
such that
m\choose{\alpha}
is a nonmultiple of
p.
From
[
11
]
(Lemma II.4) it follows that
\alpha_{1}(p)=p,
therefore

(aI_{n}-L)^{p}=(aI_{n})^{p}-L^{p}=a^{p}I_{n}^{p}-L=aI_{n}-L.

∎

The proof of the following Lemma has a similar flow with Lemma
2
in
[
5
]
, where
a=1
and
p=2.

Lemma 7
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer, and
p
be an odd prime,
\mathbb{F}
a field of characteristic
p,
and
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}.
Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},

such that
n-k
is odd.
Let
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a companion matrix. If
\mbox{trace}(C)=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot a-l)\cdot 1,
for some
l\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\},

then
C
is similar to a matrix
D
which has a decomposition
D=E+V,
such that
E^{p}=E,

V^{k+1}=0,
the first column of
V
is
(0,0,\ldots,0),
and the last line of
E
is
(0,0,\ldots,0,a).

Proof.

For
a=1
by Lemma
2
in
[
5
]
we get the decomposition with
E^{2}=E,
and since an idempotent is a
p
-potent, it follows that we have the desired decomposition.

Now let
a\in\{2,\ldots,p-1\}.
Let
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n})
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. We construct inductively a new basis
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n})
in the following way:

f_{1}=e_{1};

for every even
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-k+1\},

f_{i}=Cf_{i-1}-af_{i-1};

for every odd
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-k+1\},

f_{i}=Cf_{i-1};

for every
i\in\{n-k+2,\ldots,n\},

f_{i}=af_{i-1}-Cf_{i-1};

It can be proved that for every
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
we have
\textrm{Lin}(\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{j}\})=\textrm{Lin}(\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{j}\}),
hence
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}^{n}.

The matrix associated to
C
in the basis
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
has the form

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&N\\
P&Q\end{array}\right),

with

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}a&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&1&a&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&a&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&1&a\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

N=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&d_{1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k-1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k}\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}),

P=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&1\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{k,n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

and
Q=aI_{k}-C_{d_{n-k+1},\ldots,d_{n}}
for some
d_{n-k+1},\ldots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{F}.
Moreover from

\mbox{trace}(M)+\mbox{trace}(Q)=\mbox{trace}(C),

it follows that

\frac{n-k+1}{2}\cdot a+ka+d_{n}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot a-l)\cdot 1,

hence

\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot a+d_{n}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot a-l)\cdot 1,

therefore

-d_{n}=l\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}.

By Lemma
5
, since
a\in\{2,3,\dots,p-1\},
it follows that there exists a
p
-potent matrix of the form

L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\cdot I_{l}&L_{1,2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)

such that
C_{d_{n-k+1},\ldots,d_{n}}-L
is nilpotent.
We derive that there exists a decomposition
Q=(aI_{k}-L)+T
such that
T
is nilpotent.
Morevore by Lemma
6
we know that

K=aI_{k}-L

is
p
-potent.
We note for further use that the first column of
K
is
(0,0,\ldots,0,0)
and the last line of
K
is
(0,0,\ldots,0,a).

We consider

V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&S\\
0&T\end{array}\right),

with

R=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

(where
0
and
1
alternate on the diagonal below the main one),

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&d_{1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{3}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k}\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}),

Since
RS=0
it is easy to see that
V^{k+1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R^{k+1}&ST^{k}\\
O&T^{k+1}\end{array}\right).
But
T
is a
k
by
k
nilpotent, and therefore
V^{k+1}=0.

In order to see that
D-V
is
p
-potent we observe that it has the form

D-V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&Y\\
Z&K\end{array}\right),

with

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}a&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&a&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&a&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&a\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&d_{2}\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&d_{4}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k-1}\\
0&0&0&0&0&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}),

Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&1\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{k,n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

and

K=Q-T=(aI_{k}-L)+T-T=aI_{k}-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}aI_{l}&L_{12}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-L_{12}\\
0&aI_{k-l}\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{F}).

By
0=YZ=XY=KZ=ZY
we get that

(D-V)^{p}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X^{p}&YK^{p-1}\\
ZX^{p-1}&K^{p}\end{array}\right).

We will compute
X^{p-1}
and
X^{p}
.
Since
a\in\{2,\dots,p-1\}
we have that
a^{p-1}=1,
and
a^{p}=a.
Therefore

X^{p-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
a^{p-2}&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&a^{p-2}&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&a^{p-2}&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

and hence
ZX^{p-1}=Z.

We also obtain
X^{p}=X.

Now we will compute
K^{p-1}
and
K^{p},
for
K=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-L_{12}\\
0&aI_{k-l}\end{array}\right).

Since
a\in\{2,3,\dots,p-1\}
we obtain that
a^{p-1}=1,
(and also
a^{p}=a)
.
Now it follows that
K^{p-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-a^{-1}L_{12}\\
0&I_{k-l}\end{array}\right),

K^{p}=K,
and also
YK^{p-1}=Y.

Finally
(D-V)^{p}=D-V.

For further use we point out that the last line of
D-V
is
(0,0,\dots,0,a),
and the first column of
V
consists only of zeros.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of positive odd characteristic
p.
Suppose that
n\geq 3
is a positive integer,
k
a positive integer such that
n-k
is odd, and
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is a companion matrix, such that
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=t\cdot 1,
with
t\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\},

and
\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot 1\neq 0.
Then there exist
D,

E
and
V
such that
C\sim D=E+V
,
E^{p}=E,
last line of
E
is
(0,\dots,0,a)
(with
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
),
V^{k+1}=0
, and the first column of
V
is
(0,\dots,0)
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=t\cdot 1,t\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}.

There exist
l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{p-1},l_{p}\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
such that

t\cdot 1=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot 1-l_{1})\cdot 1=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot 2-l_{2})\cdot 1=\dots

=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot(p-1)-l_{p-1})\cdot 1=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot p-l_{p})\cdot 1

We have the following equalties:

l_{1}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot 1-t)\cdot 1

l_{2}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot 2-t)\cdot 1

\vdots

l_{p-1}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot(p-1)-t)\cdot 1

l_{p}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot p-t)\cdot 1.

Since
l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{p-1},l_{p}
are
2
by
2
different (since
\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot 1\neq 0)
, and
p\geq 3,

there exists
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\},
such that
l_{a}\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}.
Hence, by Lemma
7
, there exist the
p
-potent
E
and the nilpotent
V,
such that
C=E+V
last line of
E
is
(0,\dots,0,a)
,
V^{k+1}=0
, and the first column of
V
is
(0,\dots,0)
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of positive odd characteristic
p.
Suppose that
n\geq 3
is a positive integer,
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},
such that
n-k
is odd, and
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is a companion matrix. If any of the following conditions is true:

(1)

there exists
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
such that
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=(\frac{n-(k+1)}{2}\cdot a+l)\cdot 1,\mbox{ }l\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
;

(2)

\frac{n-(k+1)}{2}\cdot 1\neq 0,
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)\in\{0,1,2,3,\dots,p-1\},

then there exist
D,

E
and
V
such that
C\sim D=E+V
,
E^{p}=E,
last line of
E
is
(0,\dots,0)
,
V^{k+1}=0
, and the first column of
V
is
(0,\dots,0)
.

Proof.

(1)

Assume that there exists
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
such that
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=(\frac{n-(k+1)}{2}\cdot a+l)\cdot 1,\mbox{ }l\in\{1,2,\dots,k-1\}.

Then
{\mathrm{Tr}}(aI_{n}-C)=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot a-l)\cdot 1.
Since there exists a companion
C_{1}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
such that
-C\sim C_{1},
then
aI_{n}-C\sim aI_{n}+C_{1}.

By Lemma
3
there exists a companion
C^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(F)
such that
C_{1}\sim-aI_{n}+C^{\prime}
. Then
aI_{n}-C\sim C^{\prime}
, and we have
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C^{\prime})=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot a-1)\cdot 1,
and also
C\sim aI_{n}-C^{\prime}.

By the former equality we get using Lemma
7
that there exist
D^{\prime},

E^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
such that
C^{\prime}\sim D^{\prime}=E^{\prime}+V^{\prime}
,
E^{\prime p}=E^{\prime},
last line of
E^{\prime}
is
(0,\dots,0,a)
,
(V^{\prime})^{k+1}=0
, and the first column of
V^{\prime}
is
(0,\dots,0).

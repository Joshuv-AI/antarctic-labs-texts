Global solution and asymptotic behavior for the kinetic derivative NLS on ℝ \mathbb{R} Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary – 35Q55; Secondary – 35A01, 35B40, 35B45. Thanks: Keywords and phrases. kinetic derivative NLS, global existence, asymptotic behavior, modified scattering, a priori estimate.
Nobu Kishimoto, Kiyeon Lee

Abstract
. In this paper we investigate the global well-posedness and long-term behavior of solutions to the kinetic derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation (KDNLS) on the real line. The equation incorporates both local cubic nonlinearities with derivative terms and a non-local term arising from the Hilbert transform, modeling interactions in plasma physics. We establish global existence for small initial data in the weighted Sobolev space H 2 ∩ H 1 , 1 H^{2}\cap H^{1,1} and optimal time decay effect. Using energy methods and a frequency-localized gauge transformation, we overcome the difficulties posed by the non-local nonlinearities and provide a rigorous analysis of the asymptotic behavior. Our results also describe modified scattering phenomena with a suitable phase modification, showing that the solutions exhibit a precise asymptotic profile as t → ∞ t\to\infty .



1.
Introduction

We consider the following one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with cubic nonlinear terms with derivative:

\left\{\begin{aligned} i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u&=i\alpha\partial_{x}\big[|u|^{2}u\big]+i\beta\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u\big],\qquad t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)&=\phi(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are constants and
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform defined by

\mathcal{H}f(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}p.v.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\mathcal{F}f\right](x).

When
\beta=0
, the equation (
1.1
) reduces to the standard derivative NLS, which is known to be completely integrable.
The equation (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
was derived in
[
32
,
33
]
as a model for the propagation of weakly nonlinear and weakly dispersive Alfvén waves in a collision-less plasma.
The non-local nonlinear term
\partial_{x}\left[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u\right]
representing the effect of resonant particles is obtained using a kinetic model, and thus we call (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
the
kinetic derivative NLS
.

We begin by observing a fundamental property of solutions to (
1.1
).
Formally, the
L^{2}
-norm satisfies the identity

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ =\ \beta\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
D_{x}:=|\partial_{x}|=\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}
. This identity implies the dissipative nature of the equation when
\beta<0
, in contrast to the monotone increasing nature when
\beta>0
.

This study extends the local and global well-posedness result for (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
obtained in
[
27
]
. As the asymptotic behavior and time decay effect of solutions were not addressed in
[
27
]
, we aim to investigate these properties in this paper. We begin with reviewing the known results for (
1.1
) and related equations. While most of these results have been discussed in detail in
[
27
]
, we shall recall them here for the convenience of the reader.

The mathematical theory for (
1.1
) has been extensively developed in the special case
\beta=0
, corresponding to the derivative NLS.
Global well-posedness in the scale-critical space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
has been established by Harrop-Griffiths, Killip, Ntekoume, and Visan
[
10
]
thanks to complete integrability (see also
[
11
]
for the torus case). For the case
\beta\neq 0
, Rial
[
37
]
showed the construction of global
L^{2}
-weak solutions to (
1.1
) with
\beta<0
and smoothing effect by the dissipative structure. We also refer to Peres de Moura and Pastor
[
35
]
for the applicability of Kato smoothing and maximal function estimates to the non-local nonlinearity. The first author and Tsutsumi
[
31
,
28
]
established local well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>1
and derived a priori
H^{s}
bounds for solutions with
s>1/4
, again under the dissipative assumption
\beta<0
. Furthermore, in the periodic setting
[
29
]
, they observed that the dissipative structure makes the equation of (first-order) parabolic type by the resonant interactions. This observation led to global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
s>1/4
in the case
\beta<0

[
30
]
and non-existence of solutions for
s>3/2
in the case
\beta>0

[
29
]
. As discussed above, the authors of this paper have also recently proved well-posedness in
H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap H^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})
locally for
\beta\neq 0
and globally
\beta<0
.

As reviewed above, the asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
on the real line has not been shown. In this paper, we prove the modified scattering and decay properties for (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
. Moreover, we examine the precise dissipative effect of the solution on
{\mathbb{R}}
thanks to the decay effects. Here is the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
such that the following holds:

Suppose that the initial data
\phi
in (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}+\|x\phi\|_{H^{1}}:={\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

(1.2)

for some
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
. Then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with initial data
\phi
has a global solution
u(t)
to (
1.1
), decaying as

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\varepsilon}

(1.3)

and there exist functions
W,\,\Phi\in L^{\infty}
and
{\delta}_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(t)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2it}}e^{\frac{ix^{2}}{4t}}W\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\exp\left(i\left[\frac{{\alpha}x}{4t}\left|W\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right|^{2}+\frac{\beta x}{4t}\mathcal{H}\left(\left|W\right|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right]\log t+i\Phi\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right)\\
&\qquad+O(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-{\delta}_{1}})\end{aligned}

(1.4)

uniformly in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for some
{\delta}>0
. Moreover, the limit
D_{\infty}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
exists and satisfies
D_{\infty}=(1+O({\varepsilon}^{2}))\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
and for some
{\delta}_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &D_{\infty}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{2}},\qquad 0\leq\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}-D_{\infty}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+{\delta}_{2}}{\varepsilon}^{3},\qquad\mbox{ when }\;\;\beta<0,\\
&D_{\infty}\geq\|\phi\|_{L^{2}},\qquad 0\leq D_{\infty}-\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+{\delta}_{2}}{\varepsilon}^{3},\qquad\mbox{ when }\;\;\beta>0,\end{aligned}

(1.5)

for all
t>0
.

Note that the equation lacks exact time reversal symmetry when
\beta\neq 0
; if
u(t,x)
is a solution, then
\overline{u(-t,-x)}
solves the same equation but with
-\beta
replacing
\beta
. By this transformation, we have similar behavior results for the backward time.

Remark 1.2
.

For the asymptotic expansion (
1.4
), we prove that there exists
W\in L_{\xi}^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\left(\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-e^{iB(t,\xi)}e^{it\xi^{2}}W(\xi)\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}^{3},

where the phase modification
B(t,\xi)
is the real valued function defined by

\displaystyle B(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=B_{1}(t,\xi)+B_{2}(t,\xi),

where

\displaystyle B_{1}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:={\alpha}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{2s}\xi\left|\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\right|^{2}ds,\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;B_{2}(t,\xi):=\beta\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{2s}\xi\mathcal{H}\Big(\left|\widehat{f}(s,\cdot)\right|^{2}\Big)(\xi)ds,

where
f(t)=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)
. This statement directly implies (
1.4
) by using the same argument of
[
14
,
13
]
for an appropriate real-valued function
\Phi
(see Section
7
).

Remark 1.3
.

We consider the monotonic decreasing (resp., increasing) behavior (
1.5
) of the solution to (
1.1
) with
\beta<0
(resp.,
\beta>0
) thanks to the weighted assumption and null-form bilinear estimates (Lemma
3.3
). See Lemma
3.4
and Section
7
for the detail.

Theorem
1.1
also establishes the global existence of solutions for (
1.1
) when
\beta>0
, contrasting with previous results by the first author and Tsutsumi, which demonstrated the non-existence of solutions in the periodic case.

As we observed in (
1.4
), we consider the modified scattering for (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
. The study of modified scattering for nonlinear dispersive equations has a long history, originating from the works on the NLS. Pioneering results by Ozawa
[
34
]
and Hayashi-Naumkin
[
14
,
13
]
established the modified scattering behavior for small solutions to cubic NLS and derivative NLS.

Subsequent developments have introduced a convenient framework that explains all of these results in terms of the concept of
space-time resonances
, leading to a deeper understanding of asymptotic behaviors for a wide class of dispersive equations. These approaches were introduced independently by Germain-Masmoudi-Shatah
[
5
,
7
,
6
]
and Gustafson-Nakanishi-Tsai
[
8
]
and developed by many mathematicians
[
36
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
2
,
38
,
18
,
3
,
9
]
for the seminal modified (or linear) scattering results on dispersive equations and related systems. Moreover, we refer to
[
22
]
for an application of this approach to
[
34
,
14
]
(see also
[
16
]
).

Our goal is to establish the global existence and asymptotic behavior of small solutions to (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
. The strategy relies on the resonance approach listed above with a bootstrap argument under a suitable a priori assumption, which involves
H^{2}
and weighted norms:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\langle t\rangle^{-\delta}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}+\langle t\rangle^{-2\delta}\|xe^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\right)\leq\varepsilon_{1}

(1.6)

for some
T>0
, sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, and
\delta>0
. Under this assumption, we obtain the optimal time decay effect given by (
1.3
) by employing the
L^{\infty}_{\xi}
norm (see Proposition
3.2
).

To obtain the asymptotic behavior, we need to employ a function space that avoids time growth, unlike in the a priori assumption (
1.6
), following similar approaches used for NLS and DNLS
[
22
,
13
,
12
]
. Specifically, we extract the resonance term based on the space-time resonant set:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)&=\left[\frac{\alpha i}{2t}\xi\left|\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\right|^{2}+\frac{\beta i}{2t}\xi\mathcal{H}\left(\left|\widehat{f}(s,\cdot)\right|^{2}\right)(\xi)\right]\widehat{f}(\xi)+\text{l.o.t.}\end{aligned}

(1.7)

See Section
6
for detailed calculations. The resonance terms in (
1.7
) provide the precise phase corrections, which allow us to control the pointwise Fourier amplitude of the solution by eliminating the resonance cases.

In applying this approach, both the derivative NLS and non-local nonlinear terms have to be handled. Due to the non-locality, the standard gauge transformation for derivative NLS does not work. Specifically, for the nonlinear term
\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}u)
, the high-low-low interaction term
u_{\text{low}}\overline{u_{\text{low}}}\partial_{x}u_{\text{high}}
is problematic case. However, this delicate case can be resolved in the case of
\beta=0
using the typical gauge transformation:

\displaystyle u(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x)\exp\left[\frac{1}{2i}\partial_{x}^{-1}(2\alpha|u(t)|^{2})\right].

(1.8)

This transformation eliminates the problematic case when
\beta=0
. In contrast, for
\beta\neq 0
, the transformation (
1.8
) cannot eliminate the term
\beta\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u
. To address this issue, we introduce a frequency-localized gauge transformation (see
[
39
,
17
,
23
,
30
]
). A detailed discussion of this transformation is in Section
2
.

Even though we use the frequency-localized gauge transformation to avoid the resonance case, the non-locality involving the anti-derivative implies singularities. This leads to Duhamel’s formula for (
1.1
) inapplicable. To overcome this obstacle, we apply the energy method, which gives cancellation effects to handle the singularity.

It is worth noting that our equation (
1.1
) closely resembles to the Calogero-Moser derivative NLS (CM-DNLS):

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=2i\partial_{x}P_{+}(|u|^{2})u,

which also contains both derivative and nonlocal nonlinearities. Here,
P_{+}
is defined in Section
2
and we note that
2i\partial_{x}P_{+}=(i-\mathcal{H})\partial_{x}
. CMDNLS arises as a continuum model of the classical Calogero-Moser Hamiltonian system. Compared to our equation, this equation is integrable and enjoys an
L^{2}
conservation law. Moreover, in the CM-DNLS, the derivative falls on the quadratic term and is supported on the half-line in the Fourier space. We refer the reader to
[
4
,
24
]
for the well-posedness results and
[
15
,
25
]
for the blowup results. Especially see to
[
26
]
for the soliton resolution.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the frequency-localized gauge transform for (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
. Section
3
contains proofs of various useful estimates for the terms related to the gauge transformation and bilinear form. We also provide a bootstrap assumption that leads to optimal dispersive decay for the solution to (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
. Section
4
is devoted to performing energy methods for our main equations, where we carefully handle delicate frequency relation and resonant cases. In Section
5
we control the weighted norm in a priori assumption and begin with the properties of Galilean generator
J(t)=x+2it\partial_{x}
. We carry out the energy methods similar to those of Section
4
and close the bootstrap argument. In Section
6
we show the asymptotic behaviors of the solution, from which we can control the Fourier amplitude norm. We also describe how to extract the phase modification of the solution from the resonance interaction between cubic non-local nonlinear terms. Finally, in Section
7
we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Gauge transformation

In this section, we define the gauge transformations which remove the worst nonlinear interactions of high-low type.
They are non-periodic counterparts of the ones introduced in
[
30
]
for the periodic problem, treating three kinds of nonlinearities
\alpha|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u
,
\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}u
and
\beta\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u
.

We use the operators
P_{\pm}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\pm}\mathcal{F}
and
Q_{\pm}:=P_{\pm}P_{>1}
, where
\chi_{\pm}
is the characteristic function of the half line
\{\xi:\pm\xi>0\}
.
Note that
\mathcal{H}=-iP_{+}+iP_{-}
and
\mathcal{H}Q_{\pm}=\mp iQ_{\pm}
.
To see the idea, let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
) and observe that

\displaystyle Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}\big[|u|^{2}u\big]

\displaystyle=2Q_{\pm}\big[|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u\big]+Q_{\pm}\big[u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\big]

\displaystyle=2|u|^{2}\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}u+\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\pm},

\displaystyle Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u\big]

\displaystyle=Q_{\pm}\big[\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u+\mathcal{H}(u\partial_{x}\overline{u})u+\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}u\big]

\displaystyle=|u|^{2}\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}u+\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}u+\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\big[{\mp}i|u|^{2}+\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big]\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}u+\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=Q_{\pm}\big[u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\big]+2[Q_{\pm},|u|^{2}]\partial_{x}u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=Q_{\pm}\big[\mathcal{H}(u\partial_{x}\overline{u})u\big]+[Q_{\pm},\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})]\partial_{x}u+\Big(Q_{\pm}\big[\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u\big]-|u|^{2}\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}u\Big).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})Q_{\pm}u\

\displaystyle=\ 2i{P_{{\alpha},\beta}^{\pm}}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}u+\alpha\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\pm}+\beta\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm},

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle{P_{{\alpha},\beta}^{\pm}}(|u|^{2})

\displaystyle:=\Big[\Big({-}i\alpha\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\mathrm{Id}-i\frac{\beta}{2}\mathcal{H}\Big](|u|^{2})=-i\alpha|u|^{2}\mp\frac{\beta}{2}|u|^{2}-i\frac{\beta}{2}\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2}).

Now, the high-low interactions in
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm}
are not problematic.
Indeed, the first terms (those of
u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}
type) have good non-resonance property; the second terms and the last term of
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm}
have no high-low interactions due to their commutator structure.
Then, we wish to treat the main term
2i{P_{{\alpha},\beta}^{\pm}}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}u
by applying suitable gauge transformations.
Note that we need to define the gauges for
Q_{+}u
and
Q_{-}u
separately when
\beta\neq 0
, since
P^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u|^{2})
and
P^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u|^{2})
are different.
The previous results (see, e.g.,
[
39
,
23
]
) suggest that the high-low interactions of the nonlinear term
F[u]\partial_{x}P_{\pm}u
can be removed by the gauge transformation
P_{\pm}u\mapsto\exp(\frac{1}{2i}\partial_{x}^{-1}F[u])P_{\pm}u
.
Hence, we come to the following definition of our gauge transformations:

Definition 2.1
.

[Gauge transformations]
Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a compactly-supported real-valued smooth function satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(z)\,dz=1
.
Define the anti-derivative operators
\partial_{x}^{-1}:L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\to L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\widetilde{\partial}_{x}^{-1}:L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}})\to W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}})
by

\partial_{x}^{-1}f(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(y)\,dy,\qquad\widetilde{\partial}_{x}^{-1}g(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(z)\int_{z}^{x}g(y)\,dy\,dz\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}).

Note that
\partial_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1}f=\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{x}f=f
,
\partial_{x}\widetilde{\partial}_{x}^{-1}g=g
and
\widetilde{\partial}_{x}^{-1}\partial_{x}g=g-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(z)g(z)\,dz
.
Then, define the gauge transformations
u(t)\mapsto v_{\pm}(t)
by

\displaystyle v_{\pm}(t):=e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}u(t),\qquad\rho^{\pm}[u]:=\Big({-}i\alpha\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\partial_{x}^{-1}\big[|u|^{2}\big]-i\frac{\beta}{2}\widetilde{\partial}_{x}^{-1}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big].

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
27
]
we used simpler transformations

\displaystyle e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}u,\qquad\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]:={\rm Re}\,\rho^{\pm}[u]=\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\partial_{x}^{-1}\big[|u|^{2}\big],

(2.2)

which treat only
\beta\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u
.
In the present work, we also need to treat
\alpha|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u
,
\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}u
to obtain an optimal decay in the energy estimates of weighted norms (see Remark
5.5
below).
Also, we partially use the anti-derivative
\widetilde{\partial}_{x}^{-1}g
defined above in addition to the standard one
\partial_{x}^{-1}f
.
It has been used when

is not in

(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
), which is our case as the term
\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})
in
{P_{{\alpha},\beta}^{\pm}}(|u|^{2})
may not be integrable due to unboundedness of the Hilbert transformation on

.
Note that
\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\in L^{1+}\subset L^{1}_{\rm loc}
for

, by the Sobolev embedding and boundedness of
\mathcal{H}
on
L^{p}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
.

We move on to the equation for
v_{\pm}
. We assume that the solutions are smooth.
Using the equation (
2.1
) and
denoting

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{3}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\alpha\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\pm}+\beta\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\;=\alpha Q_{\pm}\big[u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\big]+\beta Q_{\pm}\big[\mathcal{H}(u\partial_{x}\overline{u})u\big]+\beta\Big(Q_{\pm}\big[\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u\big]-|u|^{2}\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}u\Big)

\displaystyle~\quad+[Q_{\pm},2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})]\partial_{x}u,

we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})v_{\pm}

\displaystyle=e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}\Big[2i{P_{{\alpha},\beta}^{\pm}}(|u|^{2})Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}u+\mathcal{R}_{3}^{\pm}-2i(\partial_{x}\rho^{\pm})Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}u

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect several lemmas that are used throughout this paper. We begin with a property of the linear Schrödinger equation.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Schrödinger semigroup satisfies the inequalities

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}g\right\|_{L^{\infty}}&\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\widehat{g}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\|g\|_{H^{0,\gamma^{\prime}}},\\
\left\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}g\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}&\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{g}\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\|g\|_{H^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}}\end{aligned}

(3.1)

for any
\gamma^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}+2\gamma
.

Proof.

This follows from the identity

\displaystyle e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}g(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi it)^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4t}}g(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi it)^{1/2}}e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\frac{x}{2t}y}g(y)\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\frac{x}{2t}y}(e^{i\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}-1)g(y)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2it)^{1/2}}e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}\widehat{g}\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)+O\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\||y|^{2\gamma}g(y)\|_{L^{1}_{y}}\right),\qquad\gamma>0

and Cauchy-Schwarz in
x
.
∎

Under the a priori assumption (

Assume that

By Lemma

, we have the crucial decay estimates

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\kappa}\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1}\quad(N\geq 1).

To show (

with

Note that the case

\displaystyle\|Q_{\pm}u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Lemma 3.3
.

It holds that

\partial_{x}(u(t)\overline{v(t)})=\frac{1}{2it}\Big(\overline{v(t)}e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}xf(t)-u(t)\overline{e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}xg(t)}\Big),

where
f(t):=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)
and
g(t):=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}v(t)
.
In particular, if
u
satisfies a priori assumption (
3.2
), then we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Although this is a well-known identity
2it\partial_{x}(u\overline{v})=(Ju)\overline{v}-u(\overline{Jv})
related to the operator
J:=x+2it\partial_{x}
, it is worth noticing that it can be shown by an integration by parts on the Fourier side:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(u\overline{v})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2t}\mathcal{F}_{\xi}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{\eta}\big[e^{-it[(\xi+\eta)^{2}-\eta^{2}]}\big]\widehat{f}(\xi+\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\eta)}d\eta\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2t}\mathcal{F}_{\xi}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-it[(\xi+\eta)^{2}-\eta^{2}]}\partial_{\eta}\left(\widehat{f}(\xi+\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\eta)}\right)d\eta\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2it}\left(\overline{v}\,e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}xf-u\,\overline{e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}xg}\right).

Taking
u=v
and using Hölder, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we see that

\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|xf(t)\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim t^{-1}\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2},

which shows (
3.6
) for
t\geq 1
.
The case
t\in[0,1]
follows from the direct estimate
\|\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(
L^{2}
energy estimate)
.

Assume that
u\in C([0,T],H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
is a solution to (
1.1
) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.2
).
Then, for
0\leq t\leq T
we have the following:

If
\beta>0
, then
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is non-decreasing and

\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\min\big\{\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}+C{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},\,\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}e^{C{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}}\big\}.

If
\beta<0
, then
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is non-increasing and

\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\max\big\{\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}-C{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},\,\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}e^{-C{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}}\big\}.

In particular, in both cases we have
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}
.

Proof.

By the equation (
1.1
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\beta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}D_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})|u(t)|^{2}dx=\beta\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

from which monotonicity of the
L^{2}
norm follows.
By a priori assumption (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.6
), we estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{{\mathbb{R}}}D_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})|u(t)|^{2}dx&\leq\left\|D_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\||u(t)|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}},\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{{\mathbb{R}}}D_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})|u(t)|^{2}dx&\leq\left\|D_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\||u(t)|^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\lesssim\left\|D_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{aligned}

This gives us that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\beta\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\beta\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

when
\beta>0
, and that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim-|\beta|\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}\lesssim|\beta|\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}

when
\beta<0
.
In each case, the desired a priori bounds are obtained by integrating the first inequality from
0
to
t
or applying Gronwall’s lemma to the second inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.8

\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(\varrho_{>1}(\xi)\chi_{\pm}(\xi)-\varrho_{>1}(\eta)\chi_{\pm}(\eta)\big)\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|\xi\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}},

\pm[2,\infty)

\varrho_{>1}(\xi)\chi_{\pm}(\xi)-\varrho_{>1}(\eta)\chi_{\pm}(\eta)\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\xi-\eta|\geq|\xi|-2\geq\frac{1}{2}|\xi|.

In particular,
|\xi|\lesssim|\xi-\eta|
if

. This implies

showing (
3.12
).
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that
u
satisfies the a priori assumption (

).
Then we have

\|\mathcal{R}^{\pm}_{5}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
u
satisfies the a priori assumption (
3.2
).
Then we have

\displaystyle\|G_{\pm}[u]\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4},

(3.13)

Lemma 3.11
.

Let

be a solution of (

) and

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+\zeta+{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}xf(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+\zeta+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

(3.16)

Proof.

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=\partial_{t}(e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u)=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})u=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}({\alpha}\partial_{x}[|u|^{2}u]+\beta\partial_{x}[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u]).

and Lemma

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}f(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2}u(t))\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u(t)|^{2})u(t)\big]\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{H^{1}}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\left(\||u(t)|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mathcal{H}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+\zeta+\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+\zeta+\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\|xf(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mathcal{H}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}(xf(t))\|_{L^{2}}

4.
Energy estimates

This section is devoted to the proof of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T>0
and
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}\leq 1
.
Suppose that
u\in C([0,T],H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
is a solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
\phi
satisfying (
1.2
) and that
u
satisfies a priori assumption (
3.2
).
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2},

(4.1)

for
t\in[0,T]
.

4.1.
Setup

By a standard approximation argument with the local well-posedness result in
[
27
]
, we may assume
u\in C([0,T],H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1})
for sufficiently large
k
.
In particular, all the following calculations are justified in the appropriate spaces (see Remark
4.2
below).

From the uniform
L^{2}
bound in Lemma
3.4
, we have
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}
.
To prove (
4.1
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}.

(4.2)

As we mentioned in the Introduction, in order to show (
4.2
), we need the gauge transformations to handle the derivative in nonlinearities.
Here, the simpler gauge (
2.2
) is sufficient.
Using the decomposition

\partial_{x}^{2}u=P_{\leq 1}\partial_{x}^{2}u+e^{\rho_{0}^{-}[u]}\big(e^{\rho_{0}^{+}[u]}Q_{+}\partial_{x}^{2}u\big)+e^{\rho_{0}^{+}[u]}\big(e^{\rho_{0}^{-}[u]}Q_{-}\partial_{x}^{2}u\big),

the estimate

\|P_{\leq 1}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon},

and Lemma
3.6
, we have

\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}+\sum_{\pm}\|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, we focus on proving

\displaystyle\|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2},

(4.3)

for
t\in[0,T]
. A direct calculation with integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(\partial_{t}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\big)|Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}\Big[i\partial_{x}^{2}u+\alpha\partial_{x}\big[|u|^{2}u\big]+\beta\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u\big]\Big]\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u}\,dx

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(2\partial_{t}\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u])\big|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u\big|^{2}\,dx\\
&\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\Big[\alpha\partial_{x}^{3}\big[|u|^{2}u\big]+\beta\partial_{x}^{3}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u\big]\pm i\beta|u|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}u\Big]\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u}\,dx.\end{aligned}

(4.4)

Remark 4.2
.

If
u\in C([0,T],H^{k})
for sufficiently large
k
, then
\partial_{t}u\in C([0,T];H^{k-2})
from the equation (
1.1
), and one can show that
e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u\in C^{1}([0,T],L^{2})
, which justifies
t
-differentiation of its
L^{2}
norm in the above formal calculation.
Moreover, functions in
x
-integrals are all smooth and vanishing at infinity, so that integration by parts operations can also be justified.

By Lemma
3.6
, the first term of (
4.4
) is estimated as

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(2\partial_{t}\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u])\big|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\big|^{2}\,dx\Big|\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}.

(4.5)

The second term of (
4.4
) is decomposed into

\sum_{j=1}^{5}2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\mathcal{S}^{\pm}_{j}\,\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u}\,dx,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle:=\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle:=\big[Q_{\pm},2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big]\partial_{x}^{3}u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle:=Q_{\pm}\big(\alpha u^{2}\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{u}+\beta\mathcal{H}(u\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{u})u\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{4}^{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle:=\beta\Big(Q_{\pm}\big(\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}^{3}u)u\big)\pm i|u|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}u\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{5}^{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle:=Q_{\pm}\Big[\alpha\big\{3\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}^{2}u+3\partial_{x}^{2}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}u+3(\partial_{x}^{2}u\partial_{x}\overline{u}+\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u})u\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\beta\big\{3\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}^{2}u+3\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}u+3\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}^{2}u\partial_{x}\overline{u}+\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u})u\big\}\Big].

Let us consider each term.
Regarding
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\pm}
, by integration by parts, we see that

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\mathcal{S}^{\pm}_{1}\,\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{x}\Big[e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)\Big]|Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\Big[{\mp}\beta|u|^{2}\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)+\partial_{x}\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)\Big]|Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}\,dx.

By (
3.3
), Lemmas
3.3
,
3.5
and
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\mathcal{S}^{\pm}_{1}(t)\,\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+\zeta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big)\|Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+4\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}.

The bounds for
\mathcal{S}_{5}^{\pm}
follows similarly, by (
3.3
) and Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{5}^{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}(|u(t)|^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

Regarding the commutator term
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\pm}
, we divide it into

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\partial_{x}^{2}\big[Q_{\pm},2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big]\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left\|\big[Q_{\pm},\partial_{x}\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)\big]\partial_{x}^{2}u\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\big[Q_{\pm},\partial_{x}^{2}\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)\big]\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

The first term can be estimated in the same approach as in the proof of Lemma
3.9
, using Lemma
3.8
.
The other two terms can be estimated directly, by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.3
.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\partial_{x}\big(2\alpha|u(t)|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u(t)|^{2})\big)\right\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+3\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

(4.6)

Combining these estimates with Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

\Big|2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\big(\mathcal{S}^{\pm}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\pm}(t)+\mathcal{S}_{5}^{\pm}(t)\big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)}\,dx\Big|\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}.

It remains to handle
\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{S}_{4}^{\pm}
. To this end, we introduce the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
4.1
, writing
w:=e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}Q_{\pm}\big(\alpha[u(s)]^{2}\overline{\partial_{x}^{3}u(s)}+\beta\mathcal{H}(u(s)\overline{\partial_{x}^{3}u(s)})u(s)\big)\overline{w(s)}\,dx\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{\pm}\big(u(t)\mathcal{H}(\overline{u(t)}\partial_{x}^{3}u(t))\big)\pm iu(t)\overline{u(t)}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

(4.8)

Putting (
4.4
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and Lemma
4.3
together, we get

\|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|e^{\rho_{0}^{\pm}[\phi]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{2}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4},

which shows (
4.3
) and completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

∎

in the rest of this section.

Proof of (

)

We observe that the left-hand side of (

) can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2is\eta\sigma}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{3}m_{1}(\xi,\sigma)\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(s,\xi)}\,d\eta d\sigma d\xi\,ds,

The contribution from the first three terms on the right-hand side can be treated straightforwardly, bounded by

(4.10)

Using integration by parts in time, (

) can be bounded by the following

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2is\eta\sigma}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)m_{1}(\xi,\sigma)\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(s,\xi)}\,d\eta d\sigma d\xi\bigg|_{s=0,t},\end{aligned}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2is\eta\sigma}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)m_{1}(\xi,\sigma)\partial_{s}\Big[\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\sigma)\Big]\overline{\widehat{h}(s,\xi)}\,d\eta d\sigma d\xi\,ds,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2is\eta\sigma}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)m_{1}(\xi,\sigma)\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(s,\xi-\sigma)\overline{\partial_{s}\widehat{h}(s,\xi)}\,d\eta d\sigma d\xi\,ds.

(4.13)

Then (

\max_{s=0,t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\partial_{x}u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|w(s)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{4}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{4}.

Analogously, we estimate (

), using (

) as follows:

), we evaluate this term by

On the other hand, we see that

\displaystyle e^{is\partial_{x}^{2}}\partial_{s}h

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}-i\partial_{x}^{2})(e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u)

\displaystyle=\big[(\partial_{s}-i\partial_{x}^{2})e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\big]Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}u-2i\big[\partial_{x}e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}\big]Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}u+e^{2\rho_{0}^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\partial_{s}f.

, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}h\|_{H^{-1}}

By Lemma
3.6
and (

), this yields that

Combining these estimates, we obtain

\left|\eqref{eq:nf-t-3}\right|\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle s\right\rangle^{-2+\zeta+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{6}\,ds\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}^{6}.

This completes the proof of (

).

4.3.
Proof of (

)

The left-hand side of (

In this section, we prove the following proposition.

.

Let

5.1.
Setup

It is convenient to introduce the operator

Some of its properties are listed below:

•

•

[J,P_{\pm}]=[x,P_{\pm}]:f\mapsto\pm\dfrac{i}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)\,dx

These operators are bounded only from

to

Let us give a proof for the last item.
We see that

x\mathcal{H}[f](x)-\mathcal{H}[xf](x)=\frac{1}{\pi}p.v.\int\Big(x\frac{f(y)}{x-y}-\frac{yf(y)}{x-y}\Big)\,dy=\frac{1}{\pi}\int f(y)\,dy,

The first claim follows from the identity

6.
Proof of asymptotic behavior

In this section, we devote ourselves to proving Proposition
3.2
and the asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
1.1
). To this end, we begin with determining the exact phase correction for (
1.1
). Taking Fourier transform, the equation for
\widehat{f}(t,\xi)
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\alpha}{2\pi}\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}{\rm sgn}(\eta)\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma.

On the one hand, by the formula

\mathcal{F}_{\eta}[1](y)=\sqrt{2\pi}\delta(y),\quad\mathcal{F}_{\eta}[{\rm sgn}(\eta)](y)=-i\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}p.v.\frac{1}{y},

we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\eta,\sigma}[e^{2it\eta\sigma}](y,z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}\left[\sqrt{2\pi}\delta(y-2t\sigma)\right](z)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\sigma}\left[\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2t}\delta\Big(\sigma-\frac{y}{2t}\Big)\right](-z)=\frac{1}{2t}e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\eta,\sigma}\left[e^{2it\eta\sigma}{\rm sgn}(\eta)\right](y,z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}\left[-i\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}p.v.\frac{1}{y-2t\sigma}\right](z)=-\frac{1}{2t}\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{-1}\left[-i\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}p.v.\frac{1}{\sigma-\frac{y}{2t}}\right](-z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2t}e^{i(-z)\frac{y}{2t}}{\rm sgn}(-z)=\frac{1}{2t}e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}{\rm sgn}(z).

On the other hand, for general functions
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
we use the inverse transform to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\eta}^{-1}\left[\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\right](y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{iy\eta}\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi-\eta)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{ix(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\overline{f_{2}(x)}\,dx\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{iy\xi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-ix\sigma}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}dx,

which yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\eta,\sigma}^{-1}\left[\widehat{f_{1}}(s,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(s,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f_{3}}(s,\xi-\sigma)\right](y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{iy\xi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{-1}\left[e^{-ix\sigma}\widehat{f_{3}}(\xi-\sigma)\right](z)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{iy\xi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}e^{i(z-x)\xi}f_{3}(x-z)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{i(y+z)\xi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-ix\xi}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(x-z)\,dx.

From these formulas and Plancherel’s theorem, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{i\alpha}{2\pi}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\widehat{f_{1}}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f_{3}}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{i\alpha}{2\pi}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\eta,\sigma}\left[e^{2it\eta\sigma}\right](y,z)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\eta,\sigma}\left[\widehat{f_{1}}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f_{3}}(\xi-\sigma)\right](y,z)\,dydz

\displaystyle=\frac{i\alpha}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(x-z)\,dxdydz,

and similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}{\rm sgn}(\eta)\widehat{f_{1}}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f_{3}}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}{\rm sgn}(z)e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(x-z)\,dxdydz.

In principle, the resonance parts of these terms are given by dropping the oscillating factor
e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}
, as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{i\alpha}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(x-z)\,dxdydz\\
&=\frac{i\alpha}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-i\xi(y+z-x)}f_{1}(y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(z)\,dxdydz\\
&=\frac{i\alpha}{2t}\xi\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi)}\widehat{f_{3}}(\xi),\end{aligned}

(6.1)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\rm sgn}(z)e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f_{1}(x-y)\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(x-z)\,dxdydz

\displaystyle=\frac{i\beta}{2t}\xi\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-i\xi y}f_{1}(y)\,dy\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i\xi(x-z)}(-i{\rm sgn}(x-z))\overline{f_{2}(x)}f_{3}(z)\,dxdz

\displaystyle=\frac{i\beta}{2t}\xi\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)\mathcal{H}\left[\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}}\widehat{f_{3}}\right](\xi).

We thus obtain the resonance part of
\partial_{t}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)
as

i\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2t}\xi|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2t}\xi\mathcal{H}\left[|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\right](\xi)\Big)\widehat{f}(t,\xi).

Then Proposition
3.2
follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
u\in C([0,T],H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
is a solution to (
1.1
) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.2
).
Then, for
0\leq t\leq T
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

(6.2)

Moreover, we have, for
1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\left(e^{-iB(t_{2},\xi)}\widehat{f}(t_{2},\xi)-e^{-iB(t_{1},\xi)}\widehat{f}(t_{1},\xi)\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim t_{1}^{-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},

(6.3)

where
B(t,\xi)
is the real valued function defined by

\displaystyle B(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=B_{1}(t,\xi)+B_{2}(t,\xi),

where

\displaystyle B_{1}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:={\alpha}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{2s}\xi\left|\widehat{f}(s,\xi)\right|^{2}ds,\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;B_{2}(t,\xi):=\beta\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{2s}\xi\mathcal{H}\Big(\left|\widehat{f}(s,\cdot)\right|^{2}\Big)(\xi)ds.

Furthermore, there exists
\kappa>0
such that for any dyadic
N\geq 1
and
0\leq t\leq T
we have

\left\|\varrho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{-\kappa}{\varepsilon}_{1}.

(6.4)

Remark 6.2
.

By Lemma
3.1
and the a priori assumption (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}&\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}\qquad(0<\gamma<\tfrac{1}{4})\\
&\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}.\end{aligned}

(6.5)

We only use this non-sharp
L^{\infty}
decay in the proof of Lemma
6.1
.
Note that the sharp decay (
3.3
) is a consequence of Lemma
6.1
.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Let us define

\displaystyle R_{1}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{i\alpha}{2\pi}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma-\frac{i\alpha}{2t}\xi|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|^{2}\widehat{f}(t,\xi),

\displaystyle R_{2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\xi\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}{\rm sgn}(\eta)\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma-\frac{i\beta}{2t}\xi\mathcal{H}\left[|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\right](\xi)\widehat{f}(t,\xi).

Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)=i\partial_{t}B(t,\xi)\widehat{f}(t,\xi)+R_{1}(t,\xi)+R_{2}(t,\xi),

(6.6)

which implies

e^{-iB(t_{2},\xi)}\widehat{f}(t_{2},\xi)-e^{-iB(t_{1},\xi)}\widehat{f}(t_{1},\xi)=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{-iB(s,\xi)}\Big\{R_{1}(s,\xi)+R_{2}(s,\xi)\Big\}\,ds

for any
1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
.
In particular, to prove (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\|R_{1}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}+\|R_{2}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3},

(6.7)

\displaystyle\|\xi R_{1}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}+\|\xi R_{2}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}

(6.8)

for
t\geq 1
.
Note that for
0\leq t\leq 1
we have
\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}
by the assumption (
3.2
).

We begin with the proof of (
6.7
). From the calculation (
6.1
) and integration by parts in
x
, we see that

\displaystyle|R_{1}(t,\xi)|\lesssim t^{-1}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Big(e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}-1\Big)\xi e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f(t,x-y)\overline{f(t,x)}f(t,x-z)\,dxdydz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\left(\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Big(e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}-1\Big)e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}(\partial_{x}f)(t,x-y)\overline{f(t,x)}f(t,x-z)\,dxdydz\right|+(\text{symm.~terms})\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1-\zeta}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|yz|^{\zeta}\left|(\partial_{x}f)(t,x-y)\overline{f(t,x)}f(t,x-z)\right|\,dxdydz+(\text{symm.~terms})\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1-\zeta}\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2\zeta}\partial_{x}f(t)\|_{L^{1}}\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2\zeta}f(t)\|_{L^{1}}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1-\zeta}\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}^{3}\lesssim t^{-1-\zeta+6\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

By setting
\zeta=7\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, we obtain the desired bound. The estimate for
R_{2}(t,\xi)
can be obtained in the same way from the fact that the sign function
{\rm sgn}(z)
does not affect the integration by parts in
x
.

To prove (
6.8
), we need refined estimates.
Let us consider

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{1}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\xi\Big(R_{1}(t,\xi)+\frac{i\alpha}{2t}\xi|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|^{2}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\frac{i\alpha}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\xi^{2}\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma.

Decomposing the multiplier
\xi^{2}
as

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=(\xi^{2}-(\xi-\eta)^{2})+(\xi-\eta)\sigma+(\xi-\eta)(\xi-\eta-\sigma),

(6.9)

we denote the corresponding parts by
\widetilde{R}_{1,1}
,
\widetilde{R}_{1,2}
and
\widetilde{R}_{1,3}
, instead of
\xi^{2}
in
\widetilde{R}_{1}
, respectively.
The first part
\widetilde{R}_{1,1}
consists of the terms of the form

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\eta\widehat{\partial_{x}^{k_{1}}f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{\partial_{x}^{k_{2}}f}(t,\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{\partial_{x}^{k_{3}}f}(t,\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma,\quad 0\leq k_{j}\leq 1,~k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=1.

By integration by parts in
\sigma
, (
6.5
) and the assumption (
3.2
), we obtain an extra decay
t^{-1}
and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{R}_{1,1}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\Big(\|\partial_{x}^{k_{1}}u(t)\overline{e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}x\partial_{x}^{k_{2}}f(t)}\partial_{x}^{k_{3}}u(t)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}+\|\partial_{x}^{k_{1}}u(t)\overline{\partial_{x}^{k_{2}}u(t)}e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}x\partial_{x}^{k_{3}}f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}+5{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

The estimate of
\widetilde{R}_{1,2}
is parallel, for which we integrate by parts in
\eta
.
For
\widetilde{R}_{1,3}
, we set
f_{1}(t):=-i\partial_{x}f(t)
,
f_{2}(t):=-i\partial_{x}f(t)
,
f_{3}(t):=f(t)
and repeat the previous calculation to figure out the main part,

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{1,3}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\alpha}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f_{1}(t,x-y)\overline{f_{2}(t,x)}f_{3}(t,x-z)\,dxdydz

\displaystyle=\frac{i\alpha}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Big(e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}-1\Big)e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}f_{1}(t,x-y)\overline{f_{2}(t,x)}f_{3}(t,x-z)\,dxdydz

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{i\alpha}{2t}\xi^{2}\left|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\widehat{f}(t,\xi).

The second term on the right-hand side is equal to
\widetilde{R}_{1}(t,\xi)-\xi R_{1}(t,\xi)
, while the first term can be estimated in the same way as for (
6.7
), by

t^{-1-\zeta}\Big(\||x|^{2\zeta}\partial_{x}f(t)\|_{L^{1}}\|\partial_{x}f(t)\|_{L^{1}}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}}+(\text{symm.~terms})\Big)\lesssim t^{-1-\zeta+6{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

Collecting the estimates above, we obtain, by setting
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{12}
and
\zeta=7{\delta}
,

\|\xi R_{1}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\lesssim t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

Concerning
R_{2}
, we consider

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\xi\Big(R_{2}(t,\xi)+\frac{i\beta}{2t}\xi\mathcal{H}\big[|\widehat{f}(t)|^{2}\big](\xi)\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\xi^{2}{\rm sgn}(\eta)\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma

and decompose
\widetilde{R}_{2}
into
\widetilde{R}_{2,1}+\widetilde{R}_{2,2}+\widetilde{R}_{2,3}
, similarly to
\widetilde{R}_{1}
with (
6.9
). The estimate of
\widetilde{R}_{2,1}
is the same as that of
\widetilde{R}_{1,1}
, since the sign function
{\rm sgn}(\eta)
does not affect the integration by parts in
\sigma
.
Indeed, we obtain, with some
0\leq k_{j}\leq 1
satisfying
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=1
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\widetilde{R}_{2,1}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}&\lesssim t^{-1}\left\|\partial_{x}^{k_{1}}u(t)\mathcal{H}\left[{\rm Re}\left(\overline{e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}x\partial_{x}^{k_{2}}f(t)}\partial_{x}^{k_{3}}u(t)\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{1}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim t^{-1}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\\
&\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}+5{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.\end{aligned}

(6.10)

For
\widetilde{R}_{2,2}
, the situation is slightly complicated due to the sign function. By integration by parts in
\eta
, we estimate

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{2,2}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}\sigma{\rm sgn}(\eta)(\xi-\eta)\widehat{f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left\{\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\Big)\partial_{\eta}(e^{2it\eta\sigma})\widehat{\partial_{x}f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta\right\}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{4\pi t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}{\rm sgn}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}\Big(\widehat{\partial_{x}f}(t,\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\Big)\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\,d\eta d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\beta}{2\pi t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\widehat{\partial_{x}f}(t,\xi)|\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)|^{2}\,d\sigma.

The first term of the right-hand side above is estimated similarly to
\widetilde{R}_{1,2}
, by

t^{-1}\Big(\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}x\partial_{x}f(t)\mathcal{H}\big[|u(t)|^{2}\big]\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}u(t)\mathcal{H}\big[\overline{e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}xf(t)}u(t)\big]\|_{L^{1}}\Big)\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}+5{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}.

Hence, we obtain

\widetilde{R}_{2,2}(t,\xi)=\frac{i\beta}{2\pi t}\xi\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)|^{2}\,d\sigma+O(t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}).

To estimate
\widetilde{R}_{2,3}
, we repeat the approach for
\widetilde{R}_{1,3}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{2,3}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{2t}\mathcal{H}\big[\overline{(-i\widehat{\partial_{x}f})(t)}\widehat{f}(t)\big](\xi)(\widehat{\partial_{x}f})(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\beta}{2t}(2\pi)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\Big(e^{-i\frac{yz}{2t}}-1\Big){\rm sgn}(z)e^{-i\xi(x-y-z)}\partial_{x}f(x-y)\overline{\partial_{x}f(x)}f(x-z)\,dxdydz

\displaystyle=\frac{i\beta}{2t}\xi\mathcal{H}\big[\cdot|\widehat{f}(t,\cdot)|^{2}\big](\xi)\widehat{f}(t,\xi)+O(t^{-1-\zeta+6{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{i\beta}{2t}\xi^{2}\mathcal{H}\big[|\widehat{f}(t,\cdot)|^{2}\big](\xi)\widehat{f}(t,\xi)-\frac{i\beta}{2\pi t}\xi\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\widehat{f}(t,\sigma)|^{2}\,d\sigma+O(t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}),

where we used the commutator identity
[x,\mathcal{H}]f=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f\,dx
.
Therefore, we see that

\widetilde{R}_{2,2}(t,\xi)+\widetilde{R}_{2,3}(t,\xi)=\widetilde{R}_{2}(t,\xi)-\xi R_{2}(t,\xi)+O(t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}).

Combining (
6.10
) and estimates above, we finish the proof of (
6.8
).

Let us consider (
6.4
). For
0\leq t\leq 1
, the bound (
6.4
) follows from

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\varrho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}&\lesssim\|P_{N}f(t)\|_{H^{1,\frac{2}{3}}}\lesssim\|P_{N}f(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|P_{N}f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\\
&\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|f(t)\|_{H^{1,1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{3}}{\varepsilon}_{1}.\end{aligned}

(6.11)

Since
B(t,\xi)
is a real-valued function, (
6.6
) implies that

\partial_{t}\left(\varrho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\right)^{2}=2{\rm Re}\left[\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\left(R_{1}(t,\xi)+R_{2}(t,\xi)\right)\varrho_{N}(\xi)^{2}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\xi)}\right].

For
t\geq 1
, the estimates (
6.11
) and (
6.2
) yield that

\|\varrho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\xi}}\lesssim\left(N^{-\frac{1}{3}}t^{\frac{5{\delta}}{3}}\right)^{\theta}{\varepsilon}_{1}

for any
\theta\in[0,1]
.
Using this and (
6.7
)–(
6.8
), we have

\partial_{t}\left(\varrho_{N}(\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\right)^{2}\lesssim t^{-1-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{3}\cdot N^{-\frac{\theta}{3}}t^{\frac{5\delta}{3}\theta}{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Then this completes the proof by taking
\theta>0
sufficiently small and integrating it over
[1,t]
.
∎

7.
Proof of main theorem

In this section, we prove the main theorem of this paper.
For solutions
u\in C([0,T];H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
to (
1.1
), we use the
X_{T}
norm introduced in (
3.2
).

We begin with the existence of global solutions
u\in C([0,\infty),H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
to (
1.1
) for small initial data
\phi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
satisfying (
1.2
).
By the local well-posedness result in the previous work
[
27
]
, we have a unique local solution
u\in C([0,T];H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
with
\|u\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}
for some
T>0
.
To extend it globally, it suffices to show a global a priori bound on
\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}
.
Since

\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}+\|xu(t)\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}+\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{1+{\delta}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}

for
0\leq t\leq T
, it suffices to give an a priori bound on the
X_{T}
norm. Indeed, we shall prove that

\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}:=K{\varepsilon}

(7.1)

for any
T>0
whenever
0<{\varepsilon}\ll 1
, where
K\geq 1
is a large constant independent of
T
and
{\varepsilon}
.
The local result shows (
7.1
) for small
T
.
Suppose (
7.1
) holds for some
T>0
, then the a priori estimates

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C({\varepsilon}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\delta}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}),

\displaystyle\|xf(t)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C({\varepsilon}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2})

established in Sections
4
and
5
imply that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq 2C(1+K^{2}{\varepsilon}){\varepsilon},

as long as
{\varepsilon}_{1}=K{\varepsilon}\leq 1
.
Choosing
K\geq 1
large enough and
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
small enough so that
2C(1+K^{2}{\varepsilon}_{0})\leq K/2
and
K{\varepsilon}_{0}\leq 1
, we have

\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}/2.

Hence, by a bootstrap argument we obtain (
7.1
).

Next, we focus on proving the asymptotic behavior of the global solution.
By Lemma
6.1
(with
{\varepsilon}_{1}=K{\varepsilon}
), for
1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<\infty
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\left(e^{-iB(t_{2},\xi)}\widehat{f}(t_{2},\xi)-e^{-iB(t_{1},\xi)}\widehat{f}(t_{1},\xi)\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim t_{1}^{-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}^{3},

where
B(t,\xi)
is the real valued function given in the lemma.
Hence, the limit

\displaystyle W(\cdot)=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-iB(t,\cdot)}\widehat{f}(t,\cdot)

exists in the sense of
\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{-1}L^{\infty}_{\xi}
, and it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\left(\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-e^{iB(t,\xi)}e^{it\xi^{2}}W(\xi)\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-{\delta}}{\varepsilon}^{3}

for
t>0
.
From the definition of
B(t,\xi)
and calculations in Section
6
, we see that

\displaystyle B(t,\xi)=\left[\frac{{\alpha}}{2}\xi|W(\xi)|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\xi\mathcal{H}\left(|W|^{2}\right)(\xi)\right]\log t+\Phi(\xi)+{\varepsilon}^{3}O(t^{-{\delta}_{1}}),

uniformly in
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for some real-valued function
\Phi(\xi)
and some
{\delta}_{1}\in(0,{\delta})
. This yields that

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=W(\xi)\exp\left(i\left[\frac{{\alpha}}{2}\xi|W(\xi)|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\xi\mathcal{H}\left(|W|^{2}\right)(\xi)\right]\log t+i\Phi(\xi)\right)+{\varepsilon}^{3}O(t^{-{\delta}_{1}}).

Then, by Lemma
3.1
we obtain

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2it}}e^{\frac{ix^{2}}{4t}}W\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\exp\left(i\left[\frac{{\alpha}x}{4t}\left|W\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right|^{2}+\frac{\beta x}{4t}\mathcal{H}\left(\left|W\right|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right]\log t+i\Phi\left(\frac{x}{2t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\varepsilon}^{3}O(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-{\delta}_{1}})+{\varepsilon}\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2{\delta}}O(t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}),

for any
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\right)
. This finishes the proof of (
1.4
).

Finally, we show the properties of
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
in (
1.5
).
By Lemma
3.4
, the limit
D_{\infty}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
exists and
D_{\infty}=(1+O({\varepsilon}^{2}))\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
.
In particular,
D_{\infty}>0
in the dissipative case
\beta<0
.
Moreover, we recall the following differential inequalities established in the proof of Lemma
3.4
:

\displaystyle 0\leq{\rm sgn}(\beta)\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}^{3}

for
t>0
.
Integrating it from
\infty
to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq{\rm sgn}(\beta)\left(D_{\infty}-\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-1+2{\delta}}{\varepsilon}^{3}

for
t\geq 0
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

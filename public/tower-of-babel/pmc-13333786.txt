Adaptive golf-optimized multi-layer perceptron framework for energy-efficient geographic routing and extended network lifetime in MANETs
Authors: M. Udhayamoorthi, R. Ramya, M. Saravanan, J. Samuel Manoharan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The highly dynamic topology, large scalability, and rapid node mobility in mobile ad-hoc networks (MANETs) pose major challenges to achieving efficient routing and sustaining a long network lifetime. Geographic routing, also referred to as position-based routing, enables data transmission between source and destination nodes based on their spatial coordinates. While such techniques enhance resource utilization and coverage efficiency, they often struggle to deliver optimal routing performance with prolonged network longevity. To overcome these limitations, this study proposes an improved multi-layer perceptron–based golf optimization algorithm for geographic routing (IMLP-GOA-GR) in MANETs. The proposed method exploits each mobile node’s locally optimal position in the search space to guide routing decisions, movement, and communication, thereby reducing unnecessary energy expenditure. Simulation results across varying node densities show that IMLP-GOA-GR improves network lifetime by approximately 18–25% and reduces total energy consumption by 20–27% compared to baseline methods. Further, it lowers the average end-to-end delay by 44.44% and improves the packet delivery ratio (PDR) by 37.5%. These outcomes clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in significantly improving routing efficiency and overall MANET performance.

Introduction

A mobile ad hoc network (MANET) is a self-configuring wireless network composed of mobile nodes that communicate without fixed infrastructure support1. These nodes interact either directly or through intermediate forwarding nodes using limited battery resources2. Since each node functions both as a host and a router, node failure due to battery depletion directly affects route stability and packet delivery reliability. Consequently, efficient route establishment and maintenance are critical factors influencing overall network lifetime3.

Recent advancements in wireless communication have introduced increasing demands for multimedia transmission under constrained energy budgets. However, the unrestricted mobility of randomly distributed nodes results in frequent topology changes, higher routing overhead, and increased energy consumption4. Unlike infrastructure-based networks that rely on base stations (BSs) or access points (APs), MANETs operate in decentralized environments where power management and routing stability become primary concerns5,6. In application scenarios such as emergency response, battlefield communication, and rescue operations, maintaining energy efficiency is particularly critical due to limited battery availability7,8.

The operational lifetime of a MANET is closely linked to the residual energy of its constituent nodes. Early energy depletion of intermediary nodes causes frequent path disruptions, retransmissions, and increased packet loss, thereby reducing network reliability9. Therefore, energy-aware multihop routing strategies are essential to ensure sustained packet transmission and balanced resource utilization.

Traditional heuristic routing approaches perform adequately in small-scale networks but degrade significantly as node density increases10. To address scalability limitations, researchers have adopted metaheuristic optimization techniques for route selection in dense and dynamic MANET environments11. Several metaheuristic-based routing schemes have been proposed to reduce transmission overhead, eliminate redundant neighbor participation, and balance energy consumption12. However, these methods typically operate in high-dimensional search spaces, leading to extended convergence times and increased computational burden13,14. Additionally, excessive exploration of non-optimal neighbors and unnecessarily long routing paths contribute to elevated energy consumption and routing overhead in existing techniques15.

Research gap: Despite the use of metaheuristic optimization in MANET routing, two major limitations persist. First, existing approaches rely predominantly on static heuristic fitness modeling, which inadequately captures nonlinear interactions among residual energy, node mobility, and spatial proximity. Second, many metaheuristic methods suffer from premature convergence or prolonged stabilization in highly dynamic topologies, reducing practical efficiency. Furthermore, limited integration between predictive learning mechanisms and routing optimization restricts adaptive decision-making in rapidly changing environments.

Overview of the proposed method: To address these limitations, this work proposes a hybrid learning–optimization geographic routing framework that integrates an improved multi-layer perceptron (IMLP) with a structured golf optimization algorithm (GOA). The MLP predicts forwarding suitability of candidate neighbor nodes based on energy, mobility, and spatial metrics, while GOA performs adaptive exploration–exploitation search to identify energy-efficient and stable routes. This combined strategy enables dynamic fitness modeling and controlled convergence behavior, enhancing routing stability and energy efficiency in large-scale MANET environments.

Related work

In 2019, Akande and Salleh16 have put out a cooperative medium access control (CMAC) protocol for MANETs known as network LEA-CMAC. The primary goal related to the LEA-CMAC protocol was to accomplish a multi-objective target orientation by improving network efficiency via cooperative transmission. The network effectiveness of MANETs degrades with respect to energy efficiency, end-to-end latency, throughput, and network longevity due to the unpredictability of wireless communication connections. If advantageous collaboration was encountered and careful MAC layer model parameter selection was made, cooperative transmission could enhance the network effectiveness of MANETs. In order to increase the network lifetime of MANETs, an optimization problem was established to accomplish a multi-objective target based CMAC protocol. Investigations into asymmetric as well as symmetric transmit power regulations resulted from the optimization solution. The optimal retransmitting node within the qualifying relays was next to be chosen using a distributed relay selection procedure that considered factors including adequate residual energy after cooperation, transmit power, and high cooperative gain. The simulation findings demonstrate that by taking advantage of an asymmetric transmit power strategy, the LEA-CMAC protocol may enhance the network effectiveness and accomplish a multi-objective goal orientation.

In 2021, Krishnan et al.17 have prepared to recognize the unreliable Cluster Heads (CHs), and certain remaining nodes would shortly take their place. The k-means protocol approach was created to efficiently choose CHs. Few among the clusters in this protocol (k-means) range from 0 to 1, and it was based on the CHs periodic irregular rotations. In the event that the random count was less than the predetermined threshold, the node was designated as a CH for the current round. A reduction in energy and power loss was attained and realized as compared to remaining protocols such as transmission protocols and direct communication protocols. Comparing the suggested method to remaining protocols such as direct communication and transmission protocols, the experimental finding indicates that it reduces energy dissipation adequately.

In 2018, Chaudhry et al.18 demonstrated that PSO was adaptively changed (APSO) to best fit in the situation, and that PSO was greatly improved when it was reinforced utilizing the FSS heuristic approach to get over the PSO’s convergence as well as computing time-associated problems. A fitness zone (FZ) was chosen as well as the destination in fitness search space (FSS) such that the best solution was found there. APSO was then used for efficient routing in the FZ region rather than the entire framework. A hybrid FZ-APSO was suggested for routing in dense networks with the least amount of latency as well as energy usage in order to extend the network’s lifetime. This uses the complementing qualities of both (FSS and APSO. The suggested FZ-APSO routing method considerably enhanced the routing’s effectiveness with respect to computing time, end-to-end latency, energy consumption, and network lifespan, according to comparative simulation findings.

In 2022, Nirmaladevi and Prabha19 concentrated on and researched the Adaptive Mobility aware Clustering Technique—a technological solution that has been suggested. This research’s main goal was to provide trustworthy as well as network lifetime-related routing in the context of a mobile-assisted MANET. To control the data transmission behavior in this study project, CH leader selection and node grouping will be carried out first. The two kinds of CH leaders chosen here were the inter- and intra-cluster leaders. The hybrid artificial bee colony with particle swarm optimization techniques are used for this leader selection. The primary goals considered in this study for the selection of CH leaders were that the intra-CH leader must be able to communicate with both their individuals as well as the closest CH leaders. More bandwidth, dependability, energy availability, and node mobility are desirable for an inter CH leader. In order to precisely pick the inter CH leader and enable effective routing, the mobility associated with the node was anticipated in this instance, and the approximate next position was selected. The technical work was evaluated overall in the NS2 simulation platform, which demonstrates that the proposed technique aims to produce the best outcome when compared to existing research approaches in terms of lower energy consumption, higher throughput, and higher packet delivery ratio (PDR).

In 2023, Kumaresan et al.20 have put out a unique methodology that uses a routing method in a MANET to increase energy efficiency and network longevity. In this research, a unique FMWSO approach was proposed to pick an optimal CH. This algorithm was produced by merging fuzzy operation with two optimization techniques, such as the WSO and the MPA. The suggested method consists of three distinct phases: cluster generation, data generation, and CH selection. To decide the CH selection in a MANET, a unique FMWSO method was developed, which improves the topology, network lifetime, and energy usage while minimizing the overhead rate. In order to ascertain the efficacy of the framework, the performance related to the suggested FMWSO methodology was lastly contrasted with a number of remaining current methodologies. The minimal energy consumption of the suggested FMWSO strategy was 0.62 mJ, which was less than that of previous alternatives.

In 2023, Baseera et al.21 introduced a MANET paradigm known as IEEHR. The methodology reduces the broadcasting range during path discovery by combining LAR with Honeycomb oriented area coverage. Since mobile nodes in MANETs have limited energy, energy efficient use is necessary apart from good routing. In 2013, Chen et al.27 put forth the adaptive position update (APU) technique for geographic routing, which dynamically modified the frequency of position updates in response to the nodes’ mobility dynamics and network’s forwarding patterns. In 2017, Nallusamy et al.28 presented the effective particle swarm based resource optimized geographic routing (PS-ROGR) method on the basis of particle swarms. Geographic routing

One important feature of MANET represents geographic routing. In ad-hoc networks, routing represents the fundamental function that exchanges route data from source to destination nodes, chooses the best route to reach the destination, uses the fewest resources possible, and extends the life associated with a wireless link. Since a node only has to know the position linked with its immediate neighbors and the destination node in order to send a packet, geographic routing is also utilized for wireless networks, where nodes determine their self-positions (by knowing the positions of immediate neighbors as well as destination node). Geographic routing bases every node’s transmission on the location of the packet destination as well as the node’s one-hop neighbors.

A multitude of mobile nodes interconnected by wireless links make up the MANET. On the basis of location updates, every mobile node in geographically routing forwards packets from source to destination. For big MANETs to be scalable, geographic routing is employed to route to distant locations. It necessitates that every node be able to pinpoint its self-location and that the source know where the destination is. A message can be routed to its destination using this data even if the network topology or previous route discovery are unknown. The primary goal related to the study on the IMLP-GOA-GR approach is to increase the network longevity in MANETs and reduce the energy usage during routing.

Energy consumption

Because wireless node lifespan is seen as being crucial in MANETs, energy consumption optimization is essential. Major energy utilization limits in a MANET include reception power, transmission power, and device energy usage. In a multi-hop method, MANET creates a link between two hops. The MANET’s communication nodes’ lifespan is affected both positively as well as negatively by changes in power and energy consumption. The power consumption of the node grows as packets are routed to a neighboring node in a MANET, requiring more energy to send or receive data. The MANET’s power shall be optimized by using the power management approach.

Power management, also known as energy management, describes a technique used by wireless nodes to save battery life by transitioning between active and sleeping states. When every wireless node is available at a similar time, these solutions operate seamlessly. Sync among entire nodes is difficult or impossible since MANET is considered to be dynamic. MANETs are often asynchronous in nature, making power management a difficult undertaking.

A reduction in power loss and energy is attained and realized as compared to remaining protocols such as transmission and direct communication methods. In addition, the simulation findings imply that, in comparison to remaining protocols such as direct communication and transmission protocols, the suggested procedure functions effectively in reducing energy dissipation.

Problems associated with power

The MANET’s nodes are limited in a number of ways, including bandwidth and power. It is critical that power be considered, particularly in a mobile setting. A node may lose power for a number of different causes. An adequate quantity of electricity is used even when only one packet is sent. The below represents a few of the main causes of power-related problems.

Network partitioning: Certain routes in MANET could be utilized often. The nodes in this route don’t forward any data and fully shut down. As a result, one framework splits into two distinct networks. Network partitioning is the term for this. This may result in latency as well as packet loss.

Selfish behavior: The intermediate nodes associated with a MANET forward packets that have not come from them by acting as routers. Their meagre power reserves are depleted by this. This leads to certain nodes dropping packets that don’t affect them. Consequently, the framework’s effectiveness is decreased.

Rebroadcasting: It is important to consider that the operation of entire surrounding nodes rebroadcasting the identical packets wastes resources and requires power for each retransmission.

Node failure: Nodes continuously need more energy to process as well as forward data packets to and from neighboring hosts since they serve as both hosts as well as routers. Consequently, node failure occurs.

Distinction from PSO-based geographic routing (PS-ROGR)

PS-ROGR applies particle swarm optimization for geographic routing using classical velocity–position updates defined as:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$v_{i} \left( {t + 1} \right) = wv_{i} \left( t \right) + c_{1} r_{1} \left( {p_{i} - x_{i} } \right) + c_{2} r_{2} \left( {g - x_{i} } \right)$$\end{document}where node updates depend on personal best and global best memory components. While effective, such velocity-memory dependence may lead to premature convergence in dynamic MANET scenarios with high mobility.

In contrast, the proposed IMLP-GOA-GR framework replaces velocity-based updates with a dual-phase Golf Optimization mechanism consisting of exploration and exploitation stages. Exploration performs adaptive long-range candidate updates, while exploitation refines solutions near the best-performing node without velocity accumulation, improving stability under topology changes.

More importantly, PS-ROGR relies solely on heuristic fitness weighting. The proposed method introduces a learning-guided suitability score using an Improved Multi-Layer Perceptron (IMLP). The forwarding fitness is reformulated as:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{i} = \alpha D_{i} + \beta E_{i} + \gamma \hat{P}_{i}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${D}_{i}$$\end{document} denotes Euclidean distance, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E}_{i}$$\end{document} represents residual energy, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\widehat{P}}_{i}$$\end{document} is the MLP-predicted forwarding probability. This integration enables adaptive decision modeling rather than static heuristic evaluation, distinguishing the proposed framework from PSO-based routing methods.

GOA

The GOA is used for tuning the parameters of MLP model in order to enhance the network lifetime and minimize energy consumption. The GOA’s genesis takes its cues from this same technique and incorporates them into a methodical structure with ease. This strategic dance finds form in the GOA, where its complex movements are outlined and its theoretical foundations are solidified by meticulous mathematical modelling. MLP training and GOA-based parameter optimization

The MLP employed in the proposed framework is trained to predict forwarding suitability of candidate neighbor nodes. The input feature vector for each node is defined as:21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X_{i} = \left[ {E_{i} ,D_{i} ,M_{i} ,\rho_{i} } \right]$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E}_{i}$$\end{document} is residual energy, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${D}_{i}$$\end{document} is Euclidean distance to destination, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${M}_{i}$$\end{document} denotes mobility variation, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho }_{i}$$\end{document} represents local node density.

The target label \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Y}_{i}$$\end{document} for training is generated based on routing outcome stability. A node is assigned \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Y}_{i}=1$$\end{document} if it successfully forwards packets without route break within a predefined time window; otherwise \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${Y}_{i}=0$$\end{document}. This formulation converts routing stability into a supervised learning problem.

The MLP training objective is defined using binary cross-entropy loss:22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L = - \frac{1}{n}\mathop \sum \limits_{i = 1}^{n} \left[ {Y_{i} {\mathrm{log}}\left( {\hat{P}_{i} } \right) + \left( {1 - Y_{i} } \right){\mathrm{log}}\left( {1 - \hat{P}_{i} } \right)} \right]$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\widehat{P}}_{i}$$\end{document} is the predicted suitability probability.

Instead of conventional gradient-only optimization, the golf optimization algorithm (GOA) is used to optimize MLP weights and biases. Each candidate solution in GOA represents a complete set of MLP parameters. The fitness value for each candidate is computed as the negative loss value over the training dataset.

The dataset is generated from simulation traces under varying mobility scenarios. Training is performed using 70% of samples, while 30% are reserved for validation to prevent overfitting. The optimized MLP parameters obtained through GOA are then deployed in the routing phase.

This setup ensures that the MLP tuning process is formally defined with explicit input features, loss formulation, dataset construction, and validation protocol.

Analytical comparison of GOA with PSO, WOA, and DA

To analytically distinguish GOA from commonly used metaheuristic algorithms such as PSO, WOA, and DA, their update mechanisms and convergence behaviors are compared. PSO updates candidate positions using velocity memory:23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$v_{i} \left( {t + 1} \right) = wv_{i} \left( t \right) + c_{1} r_{1} \left( {p_{i} - x_{i} } \right) + c_{2} r_{2} \left( {g - x_{i} } \right)$$\end{document}which depends on personal and global best attraction. This velocity-memory accumulation may lead to premature convergence in dynamic MANET routing environments.

WOA models encircling and spiral exploitation around the best solution:24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X\left( {t + 1} \right) = X^{*} \left( t \right) - A \cdot D$$\end{document}emphasizing exploitation once the best candidate is identified, potentially limiting global exploration under frequent topology variations.

DA simulates swarming behavior using separation, alignment, and cohesion forces:25\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X\left( {t + 1} \right) = X\left( t \right) + S + A + C + F + E$$\end{document}requiring multiple interaction coefficients, increasing parameter sensitivity.

In contrast, GOA employs an explicit dual-phase mechanism consisting of independent exploration and exploitation stages without velocity memory or swarm-force dependency. Exploration performs randomized global candidate updates, while exploitation adaptively refines solutions around the current best node. This structured phase-switching reduces parameter dependence and improves convergence stability under dynamic routing conditions.

Therefore, the distinction of GOA lies in its controlled exploration–exploitation transition and reduced memory-driven bias, which is particularly suitable for mobility-driven MANET environments.

The improved multi-layer perceptron-based golf optimization algorithm for geographic routing (IMLP-GOA-GR) is a sophisticated approach developed to enhance the efficiency of routing in mobile ad hoc networks (MANETs). The algorithm combines the capabilities of an improved multi-layer perceptron (IMLP) and a golf optimization algorithm (GOA) to achieve energy-efficient geographic routing, thereby extending the network’s lifetime. The IMLP-GOA-GR algorithm operates in two primary stages: exploration and exploitation. During the exploration stage, the algorithm focuses on discovering new potential solutions within the search space. Each node in the MANET, represented as a “candidate solution” in the golf analogy, evaluates its position relative to its neighbors based on a predefined fitness function. This fitness function incorporates key parameters such as the distance between nodes and the available energy at each node. The goal is to identify routes that minimize energy consumption while maintaining optimal communication distance. In the exploitation stage, the algorithm refines the solutions found during exploration. The nodes adjust their positions and routing decisions to maximize network performance, effectively “fine-tuning” the selected paths for data transmission. This stage is crucial for ensuring that the algorithm not only identifies potential routes but also selects the most efficient ones for actual use. The GOA simulates the strategic decision-making process of a golfer, where each node (candidate solution) aims to achieve the best possible outcome (optimal routing) with minimal energy expenditure. The integration of the IMLP model allows the algorithm to handle complex, nonlinear relationships between nodes and adapt to changing network conditions. By tuning the parameters of the multi-layer perceptron using the GOA, the algorithm overcomes common issues such as local minima and overfitting, which are often encountered in traditional optimization methods. As a result, the IMLP-GOA-GR approach is highly effective in extending the network lifetime, reducing energy consumption, and improving overall routing efficiency in MANETs. The IMLP-GOA-GR algorithm intelligently combines machine learning with metaheuristic optimization to address the challenges of geographic routing in MANETs. By systematically exploring and exploiting the search space, the algorithm ensures that data packets are routed through the most energy-efficient paths, thereby enhancing the network’s performance and longevity.

The proposed IMLP-GOA-GR framework operates through a sequential hybrid workflow in which IMLP and GOA collaborate at different stages of the routing process.

Step 1 Feature extraction

Step 2 IMLP suitability prediction

The trained IMLP processes \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{i}$$\end{document} and produces a forwarding suitability score:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{P}_{i} = {\mathrm{IMLP}}\left( {X_{i} } \right)$$\end{document}

Step 3 Fitness formulation

The predicted suitability score is integrated into the routing objective:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{i} = \alpha \tilde{D}_{i} + \beta \left( {1 - \tilde{E}_{i} } \right) + \gamma \left( {1 - \hat{P}_{i} } \right)$$\end{document}

Step 4 GOA-based optimization

GOA iteratively searches for the optimal next-hop candidate by minimizing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${F}_{i}$$\end{document} across the feasible neighbor set. Each candidate solution in GOA represents a potential routing decision, not a neural parameter vector.

Step 5 Routing decision execution

The node with minimum fitness value is selected as the next hop, and the routing table is updated accordingly.

Therefore, IMLP and GOA operate sequentially:

IMLP performs predictive modeling, and GOA performs constrained optimization over the predicted fitness landscape. The MLP does not preprocess inputs for GOA, nor does GOA dynamically retrain the MLP during routing. Instead, GOA optimizes routing decisions using MLP-generated suitability scores.

Discussion

The performance improvement observed in the proposed IMLP-GOA-GR framework is not merely numerical but arises from structural modifications in routing decision modeling. Conventional geographic routing schemes rely on static heuristic weighting of distance and residual energy, which may not adequately capture nonlinear interactions between node mobility, topology variation, and energy depletion patterns. By integrating a learning-guided suitability predictor within the routing fitness formulation, the proposed method enables adaptive forwarding decisions that better reflect dynamic network conditions.

The golf optimization algorithm further enhances performance through its structured exploration–exploitation transition. During the exploration phase, diverse candidate routes are evaluated, reducing the likelihood of premature convergence toward suboptimal forwarding nodes. The exploitation phase then refines route selection around stable high-quality nodes. This controlled search behavior explains the observed reduction in delay and improvement in packet delivery ratio.

From an energy perspective, improved next-hop selection reduces redundant retransmissions and avoids low-energy nodes, directly extending network lifetime. However, metaheuristic-based routing inherently introduces additional computational overhead due to iterative candidate evaluation. The trade-off is therefore between optimization cost and routing efficiency.

Experimental results indicate that while the proposed framework incurs slightly higher per-iteration computation compared to heuristic routing, it achieves faster convergence and reduces retransmission overhead. Consequently, overall energy consumption decreases despite the optimization component.

Regarding latency, shorter and more stable routing paths reduce queuing and retransmission delays. Although exploration phases may introduce minor route recalculation overhead, this is offset by reduced packet loss and route break frequency.

Therefore, the proposed method achieves a balanced trade-off: moderate computational overhead in exchange for significant gains in energy efficiency, reduced latency, and improved delivery reliability. The improvements are structurally justified by adaptive suitability modeling and controlled convergence behavior rather than parameter adjustment alone.

Conclusion

In MANET, an efficient IMLP-GOA-GR technique was created to enhance resource optimization and network lifetime. Initially, a mobile node navigated a framework based on its position, or its local optimum known location in the search space. In the framework, every golfer might communicate with every other candidate solution while expending the least amount of energy. The candidate solution with the superior global optimum function was selected for energy-efficient routing based on the value of the objective function. All participants shared the optimal location to optimize the network resources. The IMLP-GOA-GR method did this while using the least amount of energy and extending the lifetime of the network. Variables such as the PDR, energy consumption, average end-to-end latency, and network lifetime were examined through simulation.

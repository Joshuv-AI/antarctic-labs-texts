Locally analytic vectors and 𝐙 p {{\bf Z}_{p}} -extensions
Léo Poyeton

Abstract
Let K K be a finite extension of 𝐐 p {{\bf Q}_{p}} and let 𝒢 K = Gal ⁡ ( 𝐐 ¯ p / K ) {\mathcal{G}}_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K) . Lately, interest has risen around a generalization of the theory of ( φ , Γ ) (\varphi,\Gamma) -modules, replacing the cyclotomic extension with an arbitrary infinitely ramified p p -adic Lie extension. Computations from Berger suggest that locally analytic vectors should provide such a generalization for any arbitrary infinitely ramified p p -adic Lie extension, and this has been conjectured by Kedlaya. In this paper, we focus on the case of 𝐙 p {{\bf Z}_{p}} -extensions, using recent work of Berger-Rozensztajn and Porat on an integral version of locally analytic vectors and explain what the locally analytic vectors in the higher rings of periods 𝐀 ~ † {\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger} look like in this setting. We show that the existence of nontrivial locally analytic vectors in 𝐀 ~ † {\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger} , a necessary condition for Kedlaya’s conjecture to hold, is equivalent to the existence of an overconvergent lift of the field of norms attached to the 𝐙 p {{\bf Z}_{p}} -extension. In the anticyclotomic setting, assuming that such an overconvergent lift exists, we are able to construct elements in the corresponding Robba ring which should not exist according to a conjecture of Berger. We then prove that in this specific setting, a particular case of Berger’s conjecture holds, discarding the existence of such elements. In particular, this disproves Kedlaya’s conjecture and shows that there is no overconvergent lift of the field of norms in the anticyclotomic setting.

Introduction

Let
p
be a prime and let
K
be a finite extension of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
. One of the main ideas to study
p
-adic representations and
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K)
is to use an intermediate extension
K\subset K_{\infty}\subset{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}
such that
K_{\infty}/K
is nice enough but still deeply ramified (in the sense of
[
CG96
]
), so that
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty}
is almost étale and “contains almost all the ramification of the extension
{\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K
”. If
K_{\infty}/K
is an infinitely ramified
p
-adic Lie extension then those assumptions are satisfied. Classically, one lets
K_{\infty}
be the cyclotomic extension
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
of
K
.

One striking result following this idea has been the construction of cyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules. Fontaine has constructed in
[
Fon90
]
an equivalence of categories
V\mapsto{\bf D}(V)
between the category of all
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
and the category of étale
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules. Different theories of cyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules can be defined: one can define them over a
2
-dimensional local ring
{\bf B}_{K}
, over a subring
{\bf B}_{K}^{\dagger}
of
{\bf B}_{K}
consisting of so-called overconvergent elements, or over the Robba ring
\mathcal{R}_{K}
. In each case, a
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module is a finite free module over the corresponding ring, equipped with semilinear actions of
\varphi
and
\Gamma={\mathrm{Gal}}(K_{\mathrm{cycl}}/K)
commuting one to another (the ring itself being equipped with such actions).

Thanks to a theorem of Cherbonnier and Colmez
[
CC98
]
and a theorem of Kedlaya
[
Ked05
]
, these different theories are equivalent. Moreover, the theories over both
{\bf B}_{K}^{\dagger}
and
{\bf B}_{K}
come with their integral counterparts, so that free
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
are equivalent to étale
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules over some integral subring of either
{\bf B}_{K}^{\dagger}
or
{\bf B}_{K}
.

Lately, there has been an increasing interest in generalizing both
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory
[
Ber14
,
Car13
,
KR09
]
and more generally in understanding how to replace the cyclotomic extension by an arbitrary infinitely ramified
p
-adic Lie extension in
p
-adic Hodge theory
[
BC16
,
Poy25
]
.

One could try to define
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules attached to an almost totally ramified
p
-adic Lie extension by copying the constructions in the cyclotomic case. This strategy relies on finding a “lift of the field of norms” and happens to work in the Lubin-Tate setting
[
KR09
]
. Under some strong assumptions (which are not always met even in the cyclotomic case), namely that the lift is of “finite height”, Berger showed in
[
Ber14
]
that there were some restrictions on the kind of extensions one could consider in this case, proving for example that there is no finite height lift of the field of norms in the anticyclotomic setting. The author proved that, under the same strong assumptions, the only extensions for which one could lift the field of norms were actually only the Lubin-Tate ones
[
Poy22
]
(upto finite extensions). A more natural and less constraining assumption would be to ask for which extensions one could have an overconvergent lift, but in this case almost nothing is known.

An other idea to generalize
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory, and which has been used with success by Berger and Colmez
[
BC16
]
to generalize Sen theory, has been to use the theory of locally analytic vectors, initially introduced by Schneider and Teitelbaum
[
ST03
]
. Berger and Colmez have shown that Sen theory could be completely generalized to any arbitrary infinitely ramified
p
-adic Lie extension by using locally analytic vectors. Computations from Berger
[
Ber16
]
showed that locally analytic vectors in the cyclotomic setting recovered the cyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules over the Robba ring, and suggested that the theory of locally analytic vectors should be able to define a theory of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules for any arbitrary infinitely ramified
p
-adic Lie extension. In
[
Ked13
]
, Kedlaya conjectured that indeed, locally analytic vectors should provide a nice
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module theory for any such
p
-adic Lie extension, and that the theory should even be defined at an integral level.

Up until recently, locally analytic vectors were only defined in a setting in which
p
is inverted, so it was difficult to use them in an integral setting or in characteristic
p
. One could say that an element
x
in a free
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-algebra is locally analytic if it becomes locally analytic after inverting
p
, which is what Kedlaya does in the statement of his conjecture, but this definition is not very practical and does not extend for characteristic
p
algebras.

Recently, Berger-Rozensztajn
[
BR22
,
BR24
]
, Gulotta
[
Gul19
]
, Johansson and Newton
[
JN19
]
and Porat
[
Por24
]
have generalized the classical notion of locally analytic vectors (denoted by “Super-Hölder vectors” in the papers of Berger and Rozensztajn) to a characteristic
p
and integral setting, by translating the property of being locally analytic in terms of Mahler expansions. In
[
Por24
]
, Porat has proven that these new integral locally analytic vectors can be used to recover cyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules, thus generalizing the computations of Berger
[
Ber16
]
to an integral setting. This makes it possible to reinterpret Kedlaya’s conjecture in terms of those new integral locally analytic vectors.

In this paper, we focus on the particular case of
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-extensions, and try to give a description on what the locally analytic vectors in the rings used to define
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules are. For technical reasons, we have to assume that
p\neq 2
and we do so from that point on (though we expect all our results to also hold when
p=2
). Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a totally ramified
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-extension, and we look at the structure of the ring
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger})^{{\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty}),{\mathrm{Gal}}(K_{\infty}/K)-{\mathrm{la}}}
, which we write
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{la}}
for the rest of the introduction.

Our first result is that only two very different situations may occur:

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

(1)

Either there is no nontrivial locally analytic vectors in
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}
, that is
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{la}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
;

(2)

or
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{la}}=\varphi^{-\infty}({\bf A}_{K}^{\dagger})
, where
{\bf A}_{K}^{\dagger}
is a ring of overconvergent functions in one variable.

In the second case, we also prove that everything behaves as in the cyclotomic setting. In particular, we obtain an overconvergent lift of the field of norms, and we also prove that the existence of such a lift guarantees that we are in the second case of theorem
Introduction
:

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

If
K_{\infty}/K
is a
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-extension, then there exists an overconvergent lift of the field of norms of
K_{\infty}/K
if and only if there exists a nontrivial locally analytic vector in
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}
.

Of course, if one believes in Kedlaya’s conjecture, then the first situation in theorem
Introduction
above should never arise (otherwise the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules theory it defines is too small to encode all the
p
-adic representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
) and we prove that indeed the conjecture implies the existence of nontrivial locally analytic vectors.

When
K={\bf Q}_{p^{2}}
, the unramified extension of
{\bf Q}_{p}
of degree
2
, and
K_{\infty}/K
is the anticyclotomic extension, there exists an element in
\mathrm{Frac}({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},K}^{\dagger})
which is invariant by the Frobenius
\varphi_{q}
, and over which the Galois action is locally analytic. Assuming the existence of a nontrivial lift of the field of norms of
K_{\infty}/K
, we prove that this implies the existence of a nontrivial element in
(\mathrm{Frac}\mathcal{R}_{K})^{\varphi_{q}=1}
, where
\mathcal{R}_{K}
is the corresponding Robba ring endowed with a Galois action and a Frobenius map. According to a conjecture of Berger in
[
Ber22
]
, such an element should not exist. Using the additional tools given by the existence of an overconvergent lift of the field of norms and the properties satisfied by our element in
\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{R}_{K})^{\varphi_{q}=1}
, we prove that indeed such an element cannot exist, which proves the following:

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

There is no overconvergent lift of the field of norms in the anticyclotomic setting. In particular, Kedlaya’s conjecture does not hold in general.

In particular, this explains why one would need to use a theory of derived anticyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules in order to have a nice theory: the anticyclotomic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules are not in general concentrated in degree
0
.

Structure of the paper

The first section defines the notations attached to Lubin-Tate extensions that are in use in the rest of the paper. Section 2 recalls the classical theory of locally analytic vectors for
p
-adic Banach spaces and its recent generalization in an integral setting using Mahler expansions. In section 3, we recall the definition of some rings of periods that will be used in the rest of the paper, along with some of their properties, and recall the statement of Kedlaya’s conjecture. Section 4 recalls several results regarding holomorphic functions. In section 5, we recall what an overconvergent lift of the field of norms is, and prove that the existence of such a lift implies the existence of nontrivial locally analytic vectors in
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}
. Section 6 is devoted to the proof of the reverse, namely that the existence of nontrivial locally analytic vectors in
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}
implies the existence of such a lift, in the special case where
K_{\infty}/K
is a
{\bf Z}_{p}
-extension. In section 7, we construct the Lie logarithm of the Galois action and prove that it has some technical properties related to the kernel of the theta map. In section 8 we prove that the assumption that nontrivial locally analytic vectors exist for the anticyclotomic extension implies the existence of a counterexample of Berger’s conjecture, and prove that this counterexample does not actually exist. Finally, we explain in section 9 how this disproves Kedlaya’s conjecture and explain that our results imply the existence of nontrivial higher locally analytic vectors in the cases where
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\mathrm{la}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
.

]

{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-Tate algebras, following Porat

Let

-adic Lie group, and let

be an open subgroup of

v_{\rho}=\frac{r}{r_{0}}V(\cdot,r)
, this means that
x\in({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}^{[r,r]})^{\Gamma_{m}-{\mathrm{an}},\lambda^{\prime}}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda-\alpha
with
\alpha
such that
p^{\alpha}=\frac{r}{r_{0}}
, and is thus locally analytic as an element of
({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}^{[r,r]})
by corollary
2
.

For the converse, the reasoning is the same: by corollary
2
, if
x\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{(0,\rho]}
belongs to
({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}^{[r,r]})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}
then there exist
\lambda\in{\bf R}
such that
x\in({\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{K}^{[r,r]})^{\Gamma_{K},\lambda-{\mathrm{an}}}
. The relation
v_{\rho}=\frac{r}{r_{0}}V(\cdot,r)
implies that
x\in({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{(0,\rho]})^{\Gamma_{m}-an,\lambda^{\prime}}
where
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\alpha
and so we are done.
∎

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let

. Then
x\in({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}
if and only if

.

Proof.

Let
\rho>0
,
m\geq 0
, and
\lambda,\mu\in{\bf R}
be such that

. Let
r=r_{0}/\rho
. By the remark above, if
s\geq r
and
\rho^{\prime}=r_{0}/s
, then
x\in({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{(0,\rho^{\prime}]})^{\Gamma_{m}-{\mathrm{an}},\lambda,\mu}
, so that by lemma
3.2
, there exist
m^{\prime}\geq m
,
\lambda^{\prime}
,
\mu^{\prime}\in{\bf R}
such that

. Using the maximum principle (see corollary 2.20 of
[
Ber02
]

and

for any

such that

. Therefore,

∎

\coroname

4.
Complements on holomorphic functions

Let
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
denote the set of Laurent series
\sum_{k\in{\bf Z}}a_{k}T^{k}
with coefficients in
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
such that
v_{p}(a_{k})+kr/e\geq 0
for all
k\in{\bf Z}
and such that
v_{p}(a_{k})+kr/e\rightarrow+\infty
when
k\rightarrow-\infty
. We endow
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
with a valuation
v_{r}
given by
v_{r}(f(T))=\inf_{k\in{\bf Z}}(v_{p}(a_{k})+kr/e)
if
f(T)=\sum_{k\in{\bf Z}}a_{k}T^{k}
. We let
|\cdot|_{r}
denote the norm on
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
defined by
|x|_{r}=p^{-v_{r}(x)}
. We let
\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger,r}=\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}[1/p]
. We also let
C([r,+\infty[):=\{z\in{{\bf C}_{p}},p^{-1/r}\leq|z|_{p}<1\}
.

We let
\mathcal{R}_{K}^{s}
denote the Fréchet completion of
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,s}[1/p]
for the valuations
v_{s^{\prime}}
,
s^{\prime}\geq s
, and we let
\mathcal{R}_{K}=\bigcup_{r>0}\mathcal{R}_{K}^{r}
and
\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger}=\bigcup_{r>0}\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
.

Most of the results mentioned here regarding the rings
\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger,s}
are well known and the proofs can be found in
[
Laz62
]
. We provide a proof or a reference of the other statements.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

The ring
\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
is a PID, whose ideals are generated by elements of
K[T]
having no roots in
C([r,+\infty[)
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
(\mathcal{R}_{K})^{\times}=\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger}\setminus\{0\}
, and an element of
\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
is invertible (in
\mathcal{B}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
) if and only if it has no zeroes in
C([r,+\infty[)
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

If
h\in\mathcal{R}_{K}^{r}
, then

if and only if it has finitely many zeroes in

.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
f(T)\in T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}
be such that
f(T)-T
has finite Weierstrass degree
d
. Then for any
n\geq 1
,
f^{\circ n}(T)-T
is divisible by
f(T)-T
in
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
.

Proof.

Let
x
be a root in
\mathfrak{{{\bf C}_{p}}}
of
f(T)-T
. Then
f(x)=x
and thus
f^{\circ n}(x)=x
so that the roots of
f(T)-T
are also roots of
f^{\circ n}(T)-T
. Assume that
x
is a double root of
f(T)-T
, so that
f^{\prime}(x)=1
. Then
(f^{\circ n}(T)-T)^{\prime}(x)=f^{\prime}(x)\cdot(f^{\circ(n-1)})^{\prime}(f(x))-1
and since
f(x)=x
and
f^{\prime}(x)=1
, we also have that
x
is a double root of
f^{\circ n}(T)-T
for
n=2
, and then for any
n
by induction. Repeating the same argument for the higher order derivatives shows that each root of
f(T)-T
is also a root of
f\circ f(T)-T
of at least the same multiplicity.

This means that
f(T)-T
divides
f\circ f(T)-T
in
\mathcal{R}_{K}^{r}
(the meromorphic function
h(T):=(f^{\circ n}(T)-T)/(f(T)-T)
has no zeroes on the corresponding annulus so that it is holomorphic and thus belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{K}^{r}
). Since
f\circ f(T)-T
is bounded, it has only a finite number of zeroes and thus so does
h
. Therefore,
h(T)\in\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}[1/\pi]
.

Let us write

f^{\circ n}(T)-T=f^{\circ n}(T)-f^{\circ(n-1)}+f^{\circ(n-1)}-\cdots-T.

Each
f^{\circ k}(T)-f^{\circ(k-1)}(T)
can be written as
(f(T)-T)\circ f^{\circ(k-1)}
, and since
f(T)\in T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}
, we have for any
s\geq r
,
|f^{\circ k}(T)-f^{\circ(k-1)}(T)|_{s}=|f(T)-T|_{s}
. This implies that for any
s\geq r
,
|h(T)|_{s}\leq 1
. Writing
h(T)=\sum_{n\in{\bf Z}}a_{n}T^{n}
, this means that
|a_{n}|\rho^{n}\leq 1
for all
n\in{\bf Z}
and
\rho<1
close to
1
, so that
a_{n}\in{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
for all
n
, and so
h(T)\in\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
.
∎

\the\smf@thm

5.
Overconvergent lifts of the field of norms

Let
K_{\infty}
be an infinite totally ramified Galois extension of
K
whose Galois group is a
p
-adic Lie group. The main theorem of
[
Sen72
]
shows that
K_{\infty}/K
is “strictly arithmetically profinite” (or strictly APF) in the terminology of
[
Win83
]
and we can thus apply the field of norms construction of ibid. to
K_{\infty}/K
. We let
X_{K}(K_{\infty})
be the field of norms attached to the extension
K_{\infty}/K
, which is a local field of characteristic
p
with residue field
k_{K}
by theorem 2.1.3 of ibid. In particular there exists a uniformizer
u
of
X_{K}(K_{\infty})
such that
X_{K}(K_{\infty})=k_{K}(\!(u)\!)
. Moreover, this field comes equipped with an action of
\Gamma_{K}
and of the absolute Frobenius
\varphi:x\mapsto x^{p}
.

If we let
\mathcal{E}_{K_{\infty}}
denote the set of finite subextensions
K\subset E\subset K_{\infty}
, then by definition, elements of
X_{K}(K_{\infty})
are norm-compatible sequences
(x_{E})_{E\in\mathcal{E}(K_{\infty})}
such that
x_{E}\in E
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}(K_{\infty})
, and
N_{F/E}(x_{F})=x_{E}
whenever
E,F\in\mathcal{E}(K_{\infty})
,
E\subset F
.

Since
K_{\infty}/K
is strictly APF, there exists by
[
Win83
, 4.2.2.1]
a constant
c=c(K_{\infty}/K)>0
such that for all
F\subset F^{\prime}
finite subextensions of
K_{\infty}/K
, and for all
x\in\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}}
, we have

v_{K}(\frac{N_{F^{\prime}/F}(x)}{x^{[F^{\prime}:F]}}-1)\geq c.

We can always assume that
c\leq v_{K}(p)/(p-1)
and we do so in what follows. By §2.1 and §4.2 of
[
Win83
]
, there is a canonical
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-equivariant embedding
\iota_{K}:A_{K}(K_{\infty})\hookrightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{E}}^{+}}
, where
A_{K}(K_{\infty})
is the ring of integers of
X_{K}(K_{\infty})
. We can extend this embedding into a
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-equivariant embedding
X_{K}(K_{\infty})\hookrightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{E}}}
where
{\widetilde{\bf{E}}}
is the fraction field of
{\widetilde{\bf{E}}^{+}}
, and we note
{\bf E}_{K}
its image. We also let
{\bf E}_{K}^{+}
denote the ring of valuation of
{\widetilde{\bf{E}}}_{K}
. We can actually give an explicit description of this embedding.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
0<c\leq c(K_{\infty}/K)
.

(1)

the map
\iota_{K}:A_{K}(K_{\infty}){\rightarrow}\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^{q}}{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K_{\infty}}/\mathfrak{a}^{c}_{K_{\infty}}={\widetilde{\bf{E}}}^{+}_{K}
is injective and isometric;

(2)

the image of
\iota_{K}
is
\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^{q}}{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K_{n}}/\mathfrak{a}^{c}_{K_{n}}
.

Proof.

This is proven in §4.2 of
[
Win83
]
.
∎

Let
E
be a finite extension of
{{\bf Q}_{p}}
, with residue field
k_{E}=k_{K}
. Let
\varpi_{E}
be a uniformizer of
E
, and let
{\bf A}_{K}
denote the
\varpi_{E}
-adic completion of
\mathcal{O}_{E}[\![T]\!][1/T]
(the notation
{\bf A}_{K}
is used here for compatibility with the action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
but be mindful that this is actually dependent on
E
even if it does not appear in the notation). The ring
{\bf A}_{K}
is a
\varpi_{E}
-Cohen ring of
X_{K}(K_{\infty})=k_{K}(\!(\pi_{K})\!)
, and following the definition of
[
Ber14
]
, we say that the action of
\Gamma_{K}
is liftable if there exists such a field
E
and power series
\{F_{g}(T)\}_{g\in\Gamma_{K}}
and
P(T)
in
{\bf A}_{K}
such that:

(1)

\overline{F}_{g}(\pi_{K})=g(\pi_{K})
and
\overline{P}(\pi_{K})=\pi_{K}^{q}
;

(2)

F_{g}\circ P=P\circ F_{g}
and
F_{g}\circ F_{h}=F_{hg}
for all
g,h\in\Gamma_{K}
;

where the notations
\overline{F}_{g}
and
\overline{P}
stand for the reduction of the power series mod
\varpi_{E}
.

When the action of
\Gamma_{K}
is liftable we get a
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module theory as in Fontaine’s classical cyclotomic theory
[
Fon90
]
in order to study
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, replacing the cyclotomic extension in the theory of Fontaine by the extension
K_{\infty}/K
. In particular, if the action of
\Gamma_{K}
is liftable, then there is an equivalence of categories between étale
(\varphi_{q},\Gamma_{K})
-modules on
{\bf A}_{K}
and
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-linear representations of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
(see
[
Ber14
, Thm. 2.1]
).

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

There is a
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
-equivariant embedding
{\bf A}_{K}\hookrightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}
and compatible with
\varphi_{q}
that lifts the embedding
\iota_{K}:X_{K}(K_{\infty})\hookrightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{E}}}_{K}={\widetilde{\bf{E}}}^{{\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{{\bf Q}}_{p}}/K_{\infty})}
.

Proof.

See
[
Fon90
, A.1.3]
or
[
Ber14
, §3]
.
∎

We say that a lift of the field of norms is overconvergent if the power series
P(T)
giving the lift of the Frobenius belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
for some
r>0
.

We now assume that there is an overconvergent lift of the field of norms. Let
u\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}
be the image of
T
by the embedding given by proposition
5
, so that
\varphi_{q}(u)=P(u)
and
g(u)=F_{g}(u)
for

.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

If
P(T)\in\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
, with
r\geq\frac{p-1}{pe}
then
u\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r}
.

Proof.

We have
u\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}
, and we write
u=(u-[\overline{u}])+[\overline{u}]
. Since
\overline{u}\in{\widetilde{\bf{E}}^{+}}
, we have
u\in({\widetilde{\bf{A}}^{+}}+\varpi_{E}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})
. Let us write
P(T)=P^{+}(T)+P^{-}(1/T)
, with
P^{+}(T)\in T^{q}+\mathfrak{m}_{E}[\![T]\!]
and
P^{-}(T)=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}T^{n}\in\mathfrak{m}_{E}[\![T]\!]

P^{+}(u)\in(P^{+}([\overline{u}])+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})\subset({\widetilde{\bf{A}}^{+}}+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})

P^{-}\left(\frac{1}{u}\right)=P^{-}\left(\frac{1}{[\overline{u}]}\frac{1}{1+\frac{u-[\overline{u}]}{[\overline{u}]}}\right)\in(P^{-}(\frac{1}{[\overline{u}]})+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})

\frac{1}{1+\frac{u-[\overline{u}]}{[\overline{u}]}}\in 1+\varpi_{E}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}

P(u)\in(P([\overline{u}])+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})\subset({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r}+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})
.

Therefore,
u=\varphi_{q}^{-1}(P(u))\in({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r/q}+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})\subset({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r}+\varpi_{E}^{2}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})

u\in({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r}+\varpi_{E}^{k}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}})

u=a+b
, with
a\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r}
and

\overline{u}=\overline{a}
, we have that
\frac{a}{[\overline{a}]}
belongs to and is a unit of
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger,r^{\prime}}
with
r^{\prime}=r+\frac{p-1}{pe}
by lemma
3.1
. Writing
a=\frac{a}{[\overline{a}]}[\overline{a}]
shows that
b/a\in\varpi_{E}^{k}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}

\frac{1}{u}=\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{b}{a}}\right)\in(\frac{[\overline{a}]}{a}\frac{1}{[\overline{a}]}+\varpi_{E}^{k}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}).

(which follows from the definition of

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

If one looks closely at the proof of lemma

u
, but we don’t need this level of precision here.

By
[
Ber14

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

For all
g\in\Gamma_{K}

.

This is basically the same proof as the one of proposition 4.2 of
[

The ring
{\bf A}_{K}
is a free
\varphi_{q}({\bf A}_{K})

\mathcal{N}:{\bf A}_{K}{\rightarrow}{\bf A}_{K}

\mathcal{N}:f(T)\mapsto\varphi_{q}^{-1}\circ N_{{\bf A}_{K}/\varphi_{q}({\bf A}_{K})}(f(T)).

is a free
\varphi_{q}({\bf A}_{K}^{\dagger})
-module of rank

\mathcal{N}({\bf A}_{K}^{\dagger})\subset{\bf A}_{K}^{\dagger}

By construction,
v_{r}(\mathcal{N}(T))=v_{r}(T)
, and
\mathcal{N}(T)
is equal to

, so that
\mathcal{N}(T)\in T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}
. Let
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
denote the map

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}:f(T)\mapsto(\mathcal{N}(T))^{-1}\mathcal{N}(f(T)),

so that
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(T)=T
.

Now for

, one has
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}+\varpi_{E}^{k}{\bf A}_{K})\subset T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}+\varpi_{E}^{k+1}{\bf A}_{K}
(see proposition 2.3.2 of
[
Fon90

) and so by induction on

(T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}+\varpi_{E}{\bf A}_{K})^{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(x)=x}\subset T\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r})^{\times}.

Since

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

For
g\in\Gamma_{n}
, let
\Delta_{g}:\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{A}_{K}
be the map defined by
h(T)\mapsto h(F_{g}(T))-h(T)
. We have
|\!|\Delta_{g}(h(T))|\!|_{r}\leq|\!|T-F_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}|\!|h(T)|\!|_{r}
and so in particular the target of the map is contained in
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
.

Proof.

In order to prove this claim, we write
h=h^{+}+h^{-}
, where
h(T)=\sum_{n\in{\bf Z}}a_{n}T^{n}
,
h^{+}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}T^{n}
and
h^{-}(T)=\sum_{n<0}a_{n}T^{n}
, which we rewrite as
h^{-}(T)=\sum_{n>0}b_{n}T^{-n}
. Then

h^{+}(F_{g}(T))-h^{+}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\left((F_{g}(T)^{n}-T^{n}\right)=\sum_{n>0}a_{n}(F_{g}(T)-T)(\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{g}(T)^{k}T^{n-k})

and since
|\!|F_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}=|\!|T|\!|_{r}
by proposition
5
, this means that

|\Delta_{g}(h^{+}(T))|_{r}\leq\sum_{n>0}|a_{n}|_{p}|\!|\Delta_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}|\!|h^{+}|\!|_{r}.

We do the same for
h^{-}
: we have
h^{-}(F_{g}(T))-h^{-}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}(\frac{1}{F_{g}(T)^{n}}-\frac{1}{T^{n}})
. We write

\frac{1}{F_{g}(T)^{n}}-\frac{1}{T^{n}}=\frac{T^{n}-F_{g}(T)^{n}}{(TF_{g}(T))^{n}}=\frac{T^{n}-F_{g}(T)^{n}}{T^{2n}}B(T)^{n}.

and hence

\frac{1}{F_{g}(T)^{n}}-\frac{1}{T^{n}}=(T-F_{g}(T))\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\frac{F_{g}(T)}{T}\right)^{k}\frac{B(T)^{n}}{T^{n}}.

Since
\frac{F_{g}(T)}{T}
is a unit of
\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger,r}
(and so is

), we obtain that

|\!|(T-F_{g}(T))\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\frac{F_{g}(T)}{T}\right)^{k}\frac{B(T)^{n}}{T^{n}}|\!|_{r}=|\!|T-F_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}|\!|T^{-n}|\!|_{r}

so that

|\!|\Delta_{g}(h^{-}(T))|\!|_{r}\leq|\!|T-F_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}\sum_{n>0}|b_{n}|_{p}|\!|T^{-n}|\!|_{r}=|\!|T-F_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}|\!|h^{-}(T)|\!|_{r}.

Therefore,
|\!|\Delta_{g}(h(T))|\!|_{r}\leq|\!|T-F_{g}(T)|\!|_{r}|\!|h(T)|\!|_{r}
.
∎

\beta=\frac{\varphi_{q}(\lambda_{1})}{\lambda_{1}}=\frac{\varphi_{q}(\lambda_{2})}{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathcal{R}_{K}
, and we let
W:=W(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
be the Wronskian of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have
W\in K^{\times}\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\times}
. Moreover,
\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{W},\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{W},\frac{\lambda_{2}^{2}}{W}\in\mathcal{R}_{K}^{\varphi_{q}=Q^{\prime}}
. In particular, there exists
\ell\in K\neq 0
such that
\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{W}=\ell\cdot H
.

Proof.

Derivating the equality
\lambda_{i}\circ Q=\beta\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2
yields
W\in\mathcal{R}_{K}^{\varphi_{q}=\beta^{2}/Q^{\prime}}
. Note that
\beta=\frac{\varphi_{q}(\lambda_{1})}{\lambda_{1}}
and that

\frac{\varphi_{q}(\lambda_{1}(v))}{\lambda_{1}(v)}=\frac{\varphi_{q}(\alpha)}{\alpha}\frac{\varphi_{q}(t_{\mathrm{id}})}{t_{\mathrm{id}}}=p\frac{\varphi_{q}(\alpha)}{\alpha}.

In particular, by lemma
3.1
, this means that
v_{p}(\beta)=1
, where
v_{p}(f)=\inf(v_{p}(a_{i}))
for
f=\sum_{i\in{\bf Z}}a_{i}T^{i}
. Therefore,
v_{p}(\beta^{2}/Q^{\prime})=0
by lemma
7
and so belongs to
(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\times}
, which implies by lemma
3.1
that
W
is, up to multiplication by a nonzero element of
K
, an element of
(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\times}
, so that the three quotients in the statement of the lemma make sense, and belong to
\mathcal{R}_{K}^{\varphi_{q}=Q^{\prime}}
.

Since
H
is divisible by
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
and satisfies
\varphi_{q}(H)=Q^{\prime}\cdot H
, we get that

\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{WH}\in\mathcal{R}_{K}^{\varphi_{q}=1}=K.

∎

We let
f_{i}=\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{W}
for
i=1,2
.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

There exists
m\geq 0
such that
f_{1}\circ F_{g}=F_{g}^{\prime}\cdot f_{1}
for all
g\in\Gamma_{m}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\Gamma_{K}
and let
i
be either
1
or
2
. Let
h(T):=\frac{f_{i}\circ F_{g}}{F_{g}^{\prime}}
. We first prove that
h\circ Q=Q^{\prime}\cdot h
.

We have
h\circ Q=\frac{(f_{i}\circ Q)\circ F_{g}}{F_{g}^{\prime}\circ Q}
since
F_{g}
and
Q
commute, and so

h\circ Q=\frac{(Q^{\prime}\cdot f_{i})\circ F_{g}}{F_{g}^{\prime}\circ Q}=\frac{Q^{\prime}\circ F_{g}}{F_{g}^{\prime}\circ Q}\cdot f_{i}\circ F_{g}

and derivating the equality
F_{g}\circ Q=Q\circ F_{g}
yields
\frac{Q^{\prime}\circ F_{g}}{F_{g}^{\prime}\circ Q}=\frac{Q^{\prime}}{F_{g}^{\prime}}
so that

h\circ Q=Q^{\prime}\cdot h.

Since
\frac{f_{i}\circ F_{g}}{F_{g}^{\prime}}
is divisible by
f_{i}
in
\mathcal{R}_{K}
, this means that
\frac{f_{i}\circ F_{g}}{f_{i}\cdot F_{g}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{R}_{K}^{\varphi_{q}=1}
and so there exists some
\ell_{i}(g)
in
K
(which has to be nonzero) such that
f_{i}\circ F_{g}=\ell_{i}(g)F_{g}^{\prime}\cdot f_{i}
.

Derivating the equality
f_{i}\circ F_{g}=\ell_{i}(g)F_{g}^{\prime}\cdot f_{i}
yields

F_{g}^{\prime}(T)\cdot f_{i}^{\prime}\circ F_{g}(T)=\ell_{i}(g)\cdot(F_{g}^{\prime}(T)f_{i}^{\prime}(T)+F_{g}^{\prime\prime}(T)f_{i}(T)).

Note that, for example from the fact that
\frac{t_{\mathrm{id}}}{\lambda_{1}(v)}
is an invertible element of
{\widetilde{\bf{B}}}_{{\mathrm{LT}}}^{\dagger}
, we know that
f_{1}(\theta\circ\varphi_{q}^{-m}(v))=0
for

, and so if

, we obtain

∎

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

We have

Proof.

By lemma

In particular, this proves that the potential counterexample to Berger’s conjecture does not exist, and so proves the following:

\the\smf@thm

There is no overconvergent lift of the field of norms of

9.
Kedlaya’s conjecture and higher locally analytic vectors

We now explain how to use the results from the previous sections to refute Kedlaya’s conjecture.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
extension with Galois group
\Gamma_{K}
, and assume that
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
. Then Kedlaya’s conjecture is false for
K_{\infty}/K
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
and that Kedlaya’s conjecture is true. This means that if
T
is a free
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
-representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
then
{\bf D}_{K}^{\dagger,{\mathrm{an}}}(T):=({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}\otimes_{{{\bf Z}_{p}}}T)^{H_{K},\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}
is an
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
-module such that
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}\otimes_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}}{\bf D}_{K}^{\dagger,{\mathrm{an}}}(T)\simeq{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}\otimes_{{{\bf Z}_{p}}}T
and thus
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}\otimes_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}}{\bf D}_{K}^{\dagger,{\mathrm{an}}}(T))^{\varphi_{q}=1}\simeq T
.

Moreover, since
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
, we can assume that
K_{\infty}/K
is not a twisted cyclotomic extension of
K
. Now let
T
be a rank
1

{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
-representation of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, with basis
e
. By Kedlaya’s conjecture, there exists
y\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}
such that
(T\otimes_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger})^{H_{K},\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}
is a rank
1

{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
-module generated by
e\otimes y
, and comes equipped with an
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
-linear action of
\Gamma_{K}
and
\varphi_{q}
. In particular, there exists
a\in{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}^{\times}
(since
\varphi_{q}
is an isomorphism) such that
\varphi_{q}(e\otimes y)=a\cdot(e\otimes y)
, and
\Gamma_{K}
acts on
e\otimes y
by multiplication by some character
\eta:\Gamma_{K}{\rightarrow}{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}^{\times}
.

By local class field theory, there exists
z
in
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widehat{K^{\mathrm{unr}}}}
, the ring of integers of the
p
-adic completion of the maximal unramified extension of
K
, such that
\frac{z}{\varphi_{q}(z)}=a
. Since
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widehat{K^{\mathrm{unr}}}}\subset{\widetilde{\bf{A}}^{+}}\subset{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}
, we have that
z\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}
and if
x=e\otimes y\otimes z\in{\bf D}_{K}^{\dagger,{\mathrm{an}}}(T)\otimes_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}\simeq T\otimes_{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}
, we get that
\varphi_{q}(x)=x
so that
yz\in{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}^{\dagger}
is invariant by
\varphi_{q}
and thus belongs to
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
.

This means that
y\in{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widehat{K^{\mathrm{unr}}}}
, and since
\Gamma_{K}
acts on
e\otimes y
by multiplication by some character
\eta:\Gamma_{K}{\rightarrow}{{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}^{\times}
, this means that
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
acts on
e
by multiplication by a character which factors through
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K_{\infty}\cdot K^{\mathrm{unr}}/K)
. Since this is true for any rank
1
representation
T
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{K}
, this means by local class field theory that
K^{\mathrm{ab}}=K_{\infty}\cdot K^{\mathrm{unr}}
, which is possible if and only if
K_{\infty}
is a Lubin-Tate extension of
K
. Since
K_{\infty}/K
is a
{{\bf Z}_{p}}
-extension, this means that
K={{\bf Q}_{p}}
and that
K_{\infty}/K
is an unramified twist of the cyclotomic extension of
K
, which as stated above is ruled out by the assumption that
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
.
∎

As a corollary of theorem
8
and proposition
9
, we obtain the following theorem:

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

The anticyclotomic extension provides a counterexample to Kedlaya’s conjecture.

When
({\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger})^{\Gamma_{K}-{\mathrm{la}}}={{\mathcal{O}}}_{K}
, it is straightforward to see that this implies the existence of nontrivial locally analytic vectors attached to
{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}
-modules of rank
1
, by taking some well chosen exact sequence. One could think of the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\ker(\theta\circ\varphi_{q}^{-k})\rightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger,r_{k}}\rightarrow{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widehat{K_{\infty}}}\rightarrow 0

but the problem is that
{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widehat{K_{\infty}}}
is not a Tate ring so that this exact sequence is not in the right category (thanks to Gal Porat for pointing this out). If one tries to solve the problem by inverting
p
, then
\widehat{K_{\infty}}
is a
p
-adic Banach space but
{\bf B}_{K}^{\dagger,r_{k}}
is not, so that the exact sequence obtained after inverting
p
in the sequence above is not an exact sequence in the category of
p
-adic Banach spaces.

However, one can take locally analytic vectors in the sequence

0\rightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}\xrightarrow{\pi}{\widetilde{\bf{A}}}_{K}^{\dagger}\rightarrow{\widetilde{\bf{E}}}_{K}\rightarrow 0

where every object is a Tate ring, and so this sequence gives rise to nontrivial higher locally analytic vectors.

Class Groups and Selmer Groups in Special Families
Abhishek, Debanjana Kundu

Abstract
. We explore the relationship between (3-isogeny induced) Selmer group of an elliptic curve and the (3 part of) the ideal class group, over certain non-abelian number fields.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11G05, 11R29, 11R34; Secondary 11G40, 11S25

1.
Introduction

Often the Selmer group of an elliptic curve and certain ideal class group can be embedded into the same cohomology group; thereby suggesting that in many cases it might be possible to compute one from the other.
This line of thought has been exploited in several papers including
[
Cas91
,
Sch96
]
.

The main aim of this paper is to take this naïve idea forward and generalize a result of
[
JMS25a
,
JMS25b
]
and explore the relationship between (3-isogeny induced) Selmer group of an elliptic curve and the (3 part of) the ideal class group, over certain dihedral extensions.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve over defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
given by
\mathsf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+rx^{2}+sx+t
, where
r,s,t\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Write
C
to denote a subgroup of order 3 in
\mathsf{E}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
which is stable under the action of the absolute Galois group
G_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
By a change of co-ordinates (if necessary), we may assume that
C=\{O,(0,\sqrt{t}),(0,-\sqrt{t})\}
with
s^{2}=4rt
.
One of the two things can happen:
either
s=0
, in which case
r=0
and
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{E}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+t
, where
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
; such an elliptic curve will be said to be of Type I.
These curves were studied extensively by L. Mordell and are often also called
Mordell curves
in the literature.
Otherwise
s\neq 0
in which case (by change of variables, if necessary) the elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a(x-b)^{2}
satisfying
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
ab(4a+27b)\neq 0
.
Such an elliptic curve will be said to be of Type II.
In either case, one can show that there exists a rational
3
-isogeny
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathsf{E}\rightarrow\mathsf{E}/C
, which is described in detail in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.

Overview of results

In
[
JMS25a
,
JMS25b
]
, the authors studied the close relationship between the
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
-dimension of the
\varphi
-Selmer group of the elliptic curves
\mathsf{E}_{a}
or
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{3})
and the 3-part of the
S
-class group of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{3},\sqrt{a})
(resp.
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{3})
) depending on whether
a
is or is not a square in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{3})^{*}
.
This article extends the above results to dihedral extensions.
In other words, for a fixed cube-free integer
m
we set
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{3},m^{1/3})
and we study the explicit upper and lower bounds of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
-dimension of the
\varphi
-Selmer group of the elliptic curves
\mathsf{E}_{a}
or
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
over
K
(essentially) in terms of the 3-part of certain
S
-class group of
K(\sqrt{a})
(resp.
K
) depending on whether
a
is or is not a square in
K^{*}
.
Our main results are presented in Theorems
3.9
and
4.5
.

More precisely, for Type I curves our main result is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
K
be as defined before and set
L=K(\sqrt{a})
(resp.
L=K\times K
) when
a\not\in K^{*2}
(resp.
a\in K^{*2}
).
Let
S(K)=S_{a}(K)\cup S_{3}(K)
where
S_{3}(K)
denotes the set of primes above
3
in
K
and
S_{a}(K)=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\mid a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}\text{ and }v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)\not\equiv 0\pmod{6}\}
and when
a\not\in K^{*2}
write
S_{a}(L)
to denote the primes in
L
above
S_{a}(K)
.
Set
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S\right\rvert}
to denote the size of the set
S
.

(i)

If
a\notin K^{*2}
, then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\varphi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(L)\right\rvert}+r_{1}+r_{2}.

(ii)

If
a\in K^{*2}
, then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\varphi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}+2.

From an arithmetic perspective, the
3
-Selmer group is often more relevant and interesting.
We can in fact prove a precise relationship between the
S
-class group and the
3
-Selmer group.
This is the content of Corollary
3.11
.

Corollary
.

Let
\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
denote the
3
-Selmer group of
\mathsf{E}_{a}/K
.

(i)

If
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
, then

\max\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3],\operatorname{rk}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K))\}\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 2(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(L)\right\rvert}+r_{1}+r_{2}).

(ii)

If
a\in K^{\ast 2}
, then

\operatorname{rk}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K))\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 2(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}+2).

For Type II curves, we prove similar results.
The only difference is that the description of the set
S
involved is more involved.
We refer the reader to the main article for the same.

The main idea of the proof is that the global cohomology group
H^{1}(G_{K},\mathsf{E}[\varphi])
can be viewed as a kernel of a certain norm map.
There is a similar description for the local Galois cohomology groups which appear in the definition of the
\varphi
-Selmer group.
To arrive at the bounds, we perform explicit computations of the image of the local Kummer maps and use standard results from algebraic number theory.
In general, these calculations are tedious.
However for our choice of elliptic curves and number fields, the calculations can be done rather explicitly.

Moreover, as an application of our main results described above, we can show (using a result of F. Gerth) that the
\varphi
-Selmer groups over
K
become arbitrarily large as
m
varies.
More precisely, given a Type I curve
\mathsf{E}_{a}
and a fixed integer
n
, using our result we can explicitly find a number field
K
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\varphi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\geq n
.
In particular, we prove the following result in Corollary
5.4
.

Corollary
.

Keep the notation introduced above.
Write
m=3^{s}\underset{i=1}{\prod}^{J}q_{i}^{t_{i}}
, where
t_{i}=1\text{ or }2
for all
1\leq i\leq J
and
s=0,1,\text{ or }2
and
q_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{3}
.
Further define

r=\begin{cases}J&\text{if every }q_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\\
J-1&\begin{cases}\text{if every }q_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\\
\text{if some }q_{i}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\end{cases}\\
J-2&\text{if some }q_{i}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\end{cases}.

Then for all
a
,

2r-3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\varphi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K).

In particular, as
m
varies over all possible values,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\varphi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
becomes arbitrarily large.

Organization

Including this introduction, the article has five sections.
Section
2
is preliminary in nature – we explain the basic setup, set notation, make definitions, and prove elementary results which are required throughout the paper.
Section
3
concerns our main results for Type I elliptic curves.
In Section
4
we prove similar results but now concerning rational elliptic curves of Type II.
This requires explicit computation of the Tamagawa numbers using Tate’s algorithm.
We provide examples wherever possible to elucidate our theorem(s).
In Section
5
we combine our results with results of Gerth to prove that the 3-part of certain
\varphi
-Selmer groups can be made arbitrarily large over certain number fields.

2.
Number Theoretic Preliminaries

For any number field
\mathcal{F}
, we write
\Sigma_{\mathcal{F}}
to denote the set of all non-Archimedean places of
\mathcal{F}
.

Fix an integer
m
and set
K\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{3},m^{1/3})
.
The number field
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
is a dihedral extension with Galois group
\Gal(K/{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq D_{3}
(but recall that
D_{3}
is the symmetric group on 3 elements).
Set
L\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{K[x]}{\langle x^{2}-a\rangle}
; if
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
then
L
is a quadratic extension of
K
, otherwise
L\cong K\times K
.
Next, set
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\mathcal{O}_{K}[x]}{\langle x^{2}-a\rangle}
; if
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
then
A=\mathcal{O}_{L}
, otherwise
A\cong\mathcal{O}_{K}\times\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
For a prime
\mathfrak{q}
in
K
, we similarly define
L_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{K_{\mathfrak{q}}[x]}{\langle x^{2}-a\rangle}
.
If
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, then
L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a quadratic extension, otherwise
L_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=K_{\mathfrak{q}}\times K_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
For a field
F
, we denote the maximal unramified extension of
F
by
F^{\unr}
.

Further, set
A_{\mathfrak{q}}=\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}
when
L_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a field.
Else, write
A_{\mathfrak{q}}=\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\times\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.
Write
\Norm_{L/K}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{\ast}\longrightarrow K^{\ast}
; this is the field norm map when
L
is a field and the multiplication of co-ordinate when
L\cong K\times K
.
This induces a natural map
{\Norm}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{O}^{*}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{*}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
and also
\overline{\Norm}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{O}^{*}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}/\mathcal{O}^{*3}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{*}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}/\mathcal{O}^{*3}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

The following result calculates precisely the size of

\left({A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}/{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}=\ker\left(\overline{\Norm}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{O}^{*}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}/\mathcal{O}^{*3}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{*}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}/\mathcal{O}^{*3}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
be a prime in K.
Then,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\left({A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}/{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}\right\rvert}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}\\
3&\text{if }a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}.\end{cases}

Similarly, if
\mathfrak{p}\mid 3
is a prime in K, then

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\left({A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast}}/{A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}\right\rvert}=\begin{cases}3^{6}&\text{if }a\notin K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast 2}\\
3^{7}&\text{if }a\in K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast 2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}
be any prime in
K
(including
\mathfrak{q}\mid 3
) and that
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
.

The norm map
\Norm_{L/K}
induces the map

\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\colon\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}

with
\coker(\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}})=\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}/\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}})
.
For an element
z\in\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
, write
\overline{z}
to denote the corresponding element in
\coker(\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}})
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}
, we know that
\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(x)=x^{2}
, hence in the cokernel of the norm map we have
\overline{\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(x)}=\overline{x^{2}}=1
.
This shows that
\overline{x}
has order
2
.
Hence the cokernel of the norm map
\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
is a 2-primary group.
Consider the commutative diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}/\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

Since the cokernel of the middle map is a 2-group, it follows from the snake lemma that the map
\overline{\Norm}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}}{\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}}{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}
is surjective.
Then

(1)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\left({A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}/{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}\right\rvert}=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}}/{\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}}/{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\right\rvert}}.

When
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, the norm map
\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K_{q}}^{\ast}
is the multiplication of co-ordinates.
Hence, this map is surjective.
Then,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\left({A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}/{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}\right\rvert}=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}}/{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\times{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}}/{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}}/{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\right\rvert}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}}/{\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}}\right\rvert}.

We will write
F
to denote either
L_{\mathfrak{q}}
or
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
When
F
is a field, we write
\kappa_{F}
to denote the residue field of
F
and let
q
be the characteristic of this residue field.

Claim:
Suppose that
q\neq 3
.
Then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{F}\right\rvert}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Justification:

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{F}\right\rvert}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Next, suppose that the rational prime
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
.
Then
q
is inert in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})
and (by abuse of notation) we continue to denote this by
q
.
We note that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{3})}_{q}}\right\rvert}=q^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}.

If
q\nmid m
, then
q
is unramified in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
; in particular,
q
is unramified in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})
.
Therefore,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right\rvert}=q^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
Otherwise, if
q\mid m
, i.e.
q
remains unramified in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})/{\mathbb{Q}}
but ramifies in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})
.
We note that the inertia subgroup at
q
has order 3.
Therefore, in this case
\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
has order
q^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Since
[L_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}K_{\mathfrak{q}}]=1
or
2
, we have that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right\rvert}
or
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right\rvert}^{2}
.
In any case,
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right\rvert}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
This completes the proof of the claim.

Recall that (
[
NSW08
, Proposition 5.7]
)

\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{F}\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{F}\right\rvert}-1)\mathbb{Z}}\times\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q^{s}\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{d},

with

and

such that
F
contains
q^{s}
-roots of unity.
To evaluate (
1
) and (
2
) in the case that
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
, we first compute
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{F}/\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\ast 3}\right\rvert}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{F}/\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\ast 3}\right\rvert}=\left[\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{F}\right\rvert}-1)\mathbb{Z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}3\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{F}\right\rvert}-1)\mathbb{Z}}\right]\times\left[\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q^{s}\mathbb{Z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}3\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q^{s}\mathbb{Z}}\right]\times\left[\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}3\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{d}\right]=3\times 1\times 1=3.

From these observations, we conclude that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{F}/\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\ast 3}\right\rvert}=3
.
The claim in the proposition follows immediately in the case that
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
.

We now consider the second part of the lemma when
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}\mid 3
.
Note that
p=3
is ramified in
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
and

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right\rvert}=3
; see for example
[
Sch97
, Table 1]
.
Since

\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}\times\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{3^{s}\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{6},

it follows immediately that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathcal{O}^{\ast 3}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right\rvert}=3^{7}
.
A similar computation shows that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathcal{O}^{\ast}_{L_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathcal{O}^{\ast 3}_{L_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right\rvert}=3^{13}
.
Substituting these values in (
1
) and (
2
) completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
k
denote a non-archimedean local field and fix a discrete valuation
v
.
When
\overline{x}
is an element of
k^{\ast}/k^{\ast 3}
, define
v(\overline{x})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=v(x)\pmod{3}
for a lift
x\in k^{\ast}
.
Similarly, when
(\overline{y},\overline{z})
is an element of
k^{\ast}/k^{\ast 3}\times k^{\ast}/k^{\ast 3}
, define
v(\overline{y},\overline{z})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(v(y)\pmod{3},v(x)\pmod{3})
for a lift
(y,z)\in k^{\ast}\times k^{\ast}
.

Henceforth, we write
v_{\mathfrak{q}}
for the induced valuation on
L_{\mathfrak{q}}
, for a prime
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\overline{x}\in(L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})
.
Then
\overline{x}\in(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
if and only if
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\overline{x})\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.

Proof.

The proof is adapted from
[
JMS25a
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Consider the case that
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
.
Let
\overline{x}\in(L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
.
First, suppose that
\overline{x}\in(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
.
Then
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(x)=0
, for any lift
x
of
\overline{x}
.
Conversely, if
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\overline{x})\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, then
\overline{x}=\overline{x^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}^{3n}}
for some
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence
\overline{x}=\overline{x^{\prime}}\in A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}
.
Since
\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(x)\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}
, we deduce that
\Norm_{L_{\mathfrak{q}}/K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast 3}
.
We conclude that
\overline{x}\in(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
.

A similar proof works for the situation when
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
S(K)
be a finite set of finite primes of
K
.
When
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
, by definition
L=K(\sqrt{a})
is a number field.
In this case, define
S(L)
to denote the primes above
S(K)
in
L
, i.e.,

S(L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\mathfrak{Q}\in\Sigma_{L}\mid\mathfrak{Q}\cap K\in S(K)\}.

Set
\mathcal{O}_{S(K)}
(resp.
\mathcal{O}_{S(L)}
) to denote the ring of
S(K)
-integers (resp.
S(L)
-integers) in
K
(resp.
L
).
Define

\displaystyle M(L,S,a)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\overline{x}\in L^{\ast}/L^{\ast 3}\mid L(\sqrt[3]{x})/L\text{ is unramified and }x\in L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}\text{ for all }\mathfrak{q}\in S(K)\},

\displaystyle N(L,S,a)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\overline{x}\in L^{\ast}/L^{\ast 3}\mid\langle x\rangle=I^{3}\text{
for some fractional ideal }I\text{ of }\mathcal{O}_{S(L)}\}.

When
a\in K^{\ast 2}
, we analogously define

\displaystyle M(K,S,a)

Proposition 2.6

Suppose that

\begin{split}M(K,S,a)&=\{(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in(K^{\ast}/K^{\ast 3}\times K^{\ast}/K^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\mid(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\text{ for all }\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)\\

Let

for some fractional ideal of

Next we show the equivalence of the two descriptions of

.

\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)

.

.

Now we prove the equivalence of two description of

.

\displaystyle(K^{\ast}/K^{\ast 3}\times K^{\ast}/K^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}

\begin{split}M(K,S,a)&\cong\{(\overline{x},\overline{x}^{-1})\in(K^{\ast}/K^{\ast 3}\times K^{\ast}/K^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\mid K(\sqrt[3]{x})/K\text{ is unramified and }\\

.

The following result is true in general and will be applied for elliptic curves of Type I and II.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F
be either
K_{\mathfrak{q}}
or
L_{\mathfrak{q}}
(when it is the quadratic extension of

The following lemma holds for any elliptic curve with

-isogeny.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an elliptic curve over
K
and

.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
be a prime in

at which

\delta_{\phi,K}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}.

We repeat the proof of

for the convenience of the reader.

Since
\mathsf{E}
has good reduction at

Assume that

.

3.3.
Main Results for Type I Elliptic Curves

When
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
, let
S_{a}(K)
be a finite set of primes in
K
defined as follows

(3)

S_{a}(K)=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\mid a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}\text{ and }v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)\not\equiv 0\pmod{6}\}.

Further set
S_{a}(L)
to denote the primes in
L
above
S_{a}(K)
, i.e.,

S_{a}(L)=\{\mathfrak{Q}\in\Sigma_{L}\mid\mathfrak{Q}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}\in S_{a}(K)\}.

If
a\in K^{\ast 2}
, we note that
S_{a}(K)=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)\not\equiv 0\pmod{6}\}
.

The following theorem shows a relationship between the
\phi
-Selmer group and the sets

and

defined earlier.
This is a crucial step towards our main theorem of this section, where we estimate the
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
-dimension of the

Theorem 3.7
.

Set
S(K)=S_{a}(K)\cup S_{3}(K)
where
S_{3}(K)
denotes the set of primes above
3
in
K
.

(i)

If
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
, then

M(L,S,a)\subset\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\subset N(L,S,a).

(ii)

If
a\in K^{\ast 2}
, then

M(K,S,a)\subset\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\subset N(K,S,a).

Proof.

(i)

Let
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
.
First we show that
\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\subset N(L,S,a)
.

In view of Proposition
2.6
it suffices to prove that
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
for all
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
.
Definition of the set
S_{a}(K)
in (
3
) asserts that for
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
, either of the following statements hold

(a)

a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}

or

(b)

a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
and
6\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)
.

•

If
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\phi]\right\rvert}=1
.
Noting that
\lVert\phi^{\prime}(0)\rVert=1
, it follows from Lemma
3.6
that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\right\rvert}=1
.
Proposition
2.1
asserts that that

\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))=(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}=\{1\}.

•

Next suppose that
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
and
6\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)
, then
L_{\mathfrak{q}}=K_{\mathfrak{q}}\times K_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Since
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, we note that
\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\phi]=\{O,(0,\sqrt{a}),(0,-\sqrt{a})\}
.

Let
P\in\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})
.
To show
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
, it is enough to show that
3\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P))
; this follows from Lemma
2.3
.
If
P=(0,\pm\sqrt{a})
, then Theorem
3.3
asserts that
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P)=\left(\pm\frac{1}{2\sqrt{a}},\pm 2\sqrt{a}\right)
.
The condition
6\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)
clearly implies that
3\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(2\sqrt{a})
.
Hence,

v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P))=v_{\mathfrak{q}}\left(\pm\frac{1}{2\sqrt{a}},\pm 2\sqrt{a}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{3}.

Set
P=(x,y)\in\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\setminus\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\phi]
.
By Theorem
3.3
,
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P)=(y-\sqrt{a},y+\sqrt{a})
.
Taking valuation we get that

v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P))=(v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y-\sqrt{a}),v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y+\sqrt{a})).

The calculation can be divided into two further cases:

–

If
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y)\neq v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\sqrt{a})
, using the relation
x^{3}=(y-\sqrt{a})(y+\sqrt{a})
and taking valuation we get
3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(x)=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y-\sqrt{a})+v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y+\sqrt{a})
.
Since
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y)\neq v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\sqrt{a})
, it follows that

v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y\pm\sqrt{a})=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y),v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\sqrt{a})\}.

Hence,

3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(x)=2\min\{v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y),v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\sqrt{a})\}=2v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y\pm\sqrt{a})

and
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P))\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.

–

Note that
\mathfrak{q}=2
is a prime of good reduction for
\mathsf{E}_{a}
.
If
\mathfrak{q}\neq 2
and
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y)=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\sqrt{a})
, then
6\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(a)
.
But,
a
is
6^{\text{th}}
-power free, so
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(a)=0
.
Note that
y,\sqrt{a}\in\mathcal{O}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}^{\ast}
, and at least one of
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y-\sqrt{a})
and
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y+\sqrt{a})
is equal to 0.
Using the relation
3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(x)=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y-\sqrt{a})+v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y+\sqrt{a})
, we conclude that
3\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(y\pm\sqrt{a})
.
The conclusion follows from Lemma
3.5
.

Next we show that
M(L,S,a)\subset\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
.
As before, it is suffices to show that
(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\subset\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))
for all
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
.
Once again when
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
, we can divide the calculation into the following two cases:

(a)

if
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, then as discussed above

\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))=(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}=\{1\}.

(b)

if
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, it follows from the above discussion that
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
.
By Lemmas
2.1
and
3.6
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\right\rvert}=3.

We conclude that
(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\subset\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))
.

(ii)

Consider the case that
a\in K^{\ast 2}
.
We will show that
\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\subset N(K,S,a)
.
By Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to show that
\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
for all
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
.
Since
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
(i.e.,
6\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)
and
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, for every prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
K
) we can repeat the argument of (a) of part (i) to obtain,

\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}.

To prove that
M(K,S,a)\subset\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
, it suffices to show that
(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\subset\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))
for all
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
.
Again by Lemmas
3.6
and
2.1
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\right\rvert}=3.

This shows that
(A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\subset\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.

∎

In the above proof we always assumed that
\mathfrak{q}\notin S(K)
.
In particular, our calculations were performed for
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
.
However, it is easy to notice that for the ‘upper bound’ the same proof goes through even when
\mathfrak{q}\mid 3
.
In other words, even when
\mathfrak{q}\mid 3
, we have

\delta_{\phi,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}))\subset(A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast}/A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}.

This provides a finer containment result for the Selmer group which we can now record.
The proof of the theorem is exactly same as
[
JMS25a
, Lemma 3.8]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Choose
S_{a}(K)
to be the set of primes defined earlier.

(i)

If
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
, then

\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\subset N(L,S_{a},a).

(ii)

If
a\in K^{\ast 2}
, then

\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\subset N(K,S_{a},a).

The following theorem allows us to relate the
\phi
-Selmer group with the 3-part of the class group of the associated number fields.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
S(K)=S_{a}(K)\cup S_{3}(K)
and
S(L)
denote the primes above
S(K)
in
L
.

(i)

If
a\notin K^{*2}
, then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(L)\right\rvert}+r_{1}+r_{2}.

(ii)

If
a\in K^{*2}
, then

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}+2.

Proof.

These results follow from Proposition
2.5
and Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

It is known that these families of elliptic curves have provably large
\phi
-Selmer groups over
{\mathbb{Q}}
for ‘Tamagawa ratio’ reasons.
Our observation is somewhat different (e.g. the Tamagawa ratio is 1 for these Type 1 elliptic curves, since we are working over a field containing
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})
).

In the following result, we prove what it means it terms of the
3
-Selmer group.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
denote the
3
-Selmer group of
\mathsf{E}_{a}/K
.

(i)

If
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
, then

\max\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3],\operatorname{rk}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K))\}\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 2(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(L)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(L)\right\rvert}+r_{1}+r_{2}).

(ii)

If
a\in K^{\ast 2}
, then

\operatorname{rk}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K))\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 2(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}+2).

Proof.

The proof is analogous to
[
JMS25a
, Corollary 3.23]
).

Recall from the construction of
\phi
in the Introduction, that
\ker\phi=C=\{O,(0,\pm{\sqrt{a}})\}
.
Therefore, we observe that
\mathsf{E}_{a}(K)[\phi]={0}
for
a\notin K^{\ast 2}
.
It now follows form the exact sequence of
[
SS04
, Lemma 6.1]
that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K).

The assertion follows from the fact that
\operatorname{rk}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K))\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
and Theorem
3.9
.
For the case
(ii)
, we only get that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
, hence the result follows.
∎

3.4.
Example

We provide an explicit example where
a\in K^{*2}
.
Take
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},30^{1/3})
and consider the elliptic curve
27a3
in the Cremona table, i.e.

\mathsf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+16.

Recall that,
S_{16}(K)=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\ |\ v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4\cdot 16)\neq 0\pmod{6}\}
.
Here, the ideal
(2)
in
K
factorizes as

(2)=\mathfrak{q}^{3}.

This gives us that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4\cdot 16)=12
; this implies
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{16}(K)
.
Therefore
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{16}(K)\right\rvert}=0
.
Note that
(3)=\mathfrak{p}^{6}
for a principal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
.
Now set

S=S(K)=S_{16}(K)\cup\{\mathfrak{p}\}=\{\mathfrak{p}\}.

Using SAGE we check that
\Cl(K)=\Cl_{S}(K)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]=2
.

Now using Theorem
3.9
(ii), we conclude the following,

2\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}/K)\leq 5.

In view of Corollary
3.11
(ii) we find that

6=\operatorname{rk}(\mathsf{E}_{a}(K))\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq 10.

4.
Elliptic curves of type II

We now shift our focus to the other family of elliptic curves which support a rational 3-isogeny.
Recall that
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},m^{1/3})
; for this section we assume that
m
is an odd integer.
As explained in the introduction, these rational elliptic curves have the form

\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a(x-b)^{2};\qquad ab(4a+27b)\neq 0.

Let
C
be a subgroup of
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
of order 3.
There is a rational
3
-isogeny

\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/C.

By a change of variable
(x,y)\mapsto(\frac{x}{9}-\frac{4a}{27},\frac{y}{27})
we get,
\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}=\mathsf{E}_{-27a,4a+27b}
.
The aforementioned isogeny
\psi
is given by

(4)

\begin{split}\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathsf{E}_{a,b}&\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}\\
(x,y)&\mapsto\left(\frac{9(x^{3}+\frac{4}{3}ax^{2}-4abx+4ab^{2})}{x^{2}},\frac{27y(x^{3}+4abx-8ab^{2})}{x^{3}}\right).\end{split}

Set
d=4a+27b
.
The dual isogeny
\widehat{\psi}
is given by

(5)

\begin{split}\widehat{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}&\longrightarrow\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\\
(x,y)&\mapsto\left(\frac{x^{3}-36ax^{2}+108adx-108ad^{2}}{3^{4}x^{2}},\frac{y(y^{3}-108adx+216ad^{2})}{3^{6}x^{3}}\right).\end{split}

Now we define the
\psi
-Selmer group of
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
.
We have the following exact sequence for any field
F
induced from the exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathsf{E}_{a,b}[\psi]\rightarrow\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}\rightarrow 0
,

0\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(F)/\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(F))\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{F},\mathsf{E}_{a,b}[\psi])\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{F},\mathsf{E}_{a,b})[\psi]\longrightarrow 0.

The
\psi
-Selmer group of
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
over a number field
K
is defined as follows:

\Sel^{\psi}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/K)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\alpha\in H^{1}(G_{K},\mathsf{E}_{a,b}[\psi])\mid\res_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\in\Image(\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}})\text{ for every}\,\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\}.

The following theorem is an analogue of Theorem
3.3
for the Type II curves.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
and
\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}
be elliptic curves over
K
and
\psi
be the
3
-isogeny defined in (
4
).
Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime of
K
and set
P=(x,y)\in\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})
.
Write

\\
delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})/\phi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\hookrightarrow H^{1}(G_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}},\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}[\psi])\simeq(L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}/L_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}

to denote the Kummer map and set
d=4a+27b
.

(i)

If
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
then,

\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }P=O\\
y-\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}(x-d)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(ii)

If
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
then,

\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }P=O\\
\left(2\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}d,\frac{1}{2\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}d}\right)&\text{if }P=(0,\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}d)\\
\left(-\frac{1}{2\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}d},-2\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}d\right)&\text{if }P=(0,-\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}d)\\
\left(y-\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}(x-d),y+\mathfrak{p}^{3}\sqrt{a}(x-d)\right)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
Cas91
, Chapters 13 and 14]
.
∎

Since the elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
is not self-isogenous, to calculate the size of the Kummer image we must compute the Tamagawa number; see Lemma
3.4
.

4.1.
Computation of Tamagawa numbers and image of the Kummer map

We run Tate’s algorithm
[
Tat75
]
to compute the Tamagawa numbers.
First consider the situation when
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 2,3
.

Consider the elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a(x-b)^{2}
with discriminant
\Delta(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=-2^{4}a^{2}b^{3}d
, where
d=4a+27b
.
Writing the elliptic curve in the long Weierstrass form, we have that

a_{1}=a_{3}=0,\ a_{2}=a,\ a_{4}=-2ab,\ \text{and}\ a_{6}=ab^{2}.

We can also calculate the following constants (see
[
Sil09
, III.1, p. 42]
)

b_{2}=4a,\ b_{4}=-2^{2}ab,\ b_{6}=2^{2}ab^{2},\ \text{and}\ b_{8}=0.

If
\mathfrak{q}\nmid ab
and
\mathfrak{q}\nmid d
, then
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\Delta(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})
and
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
is in minimal form at
\mathfrak{q}
.
Since
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime of good reduction
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=1
.
We now run Tate’s Algorithm in the case that
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
and
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
.
In this case, we note that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})=0,2
or
4\pmod{6}
.
In the description that follows we assume that
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 2
; however, we note that a similar algorithm exists even when
\mathfrak{q}\mid 2
.

Step 1:

Since
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
it follows that
\mathfrak{q}\mid\Delta(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})
.
Go to the next step.

Step 2:

Define
\widetilde{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}=\mathsf{E}_{a,b}\pmod{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Since
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
, we have
\widetilde{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}
and
\mathfrak{q}\mid a_{3},a_{4},a_{6},
and
b_{2}
.
Go to the next step.

Step 3:

We check that
\mathfrak{q}\mid b_{2}
and
\mathfrak{q}^{2}\mid a_{6}
(because
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}
) and go to the next step.

Step 4:

We check that
\mathfrak{q}^{2}\mid a_{6}
and
\mathfrak{q}^{3}\mid b_{8}
and go to the next step.

Step 5:

Suppose we are now in the situation that
\mathfrak{q}^{3}\nmid b_{6}
, i.e.,
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})=2\pmod{6}
; this happens when
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
and
\mathfrak{q}\nmid b
.
Since
\mathfrak{q}^{3}\mid b_{8}
and
ab^{2}/\mathfrak{q}^{2}\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, we deduce that the splitting field of the polynomial
X^{2}-(ab^{2}/\mathfrak{q}^{2})\pmod{\mathfrak{q}}
is same as
\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

In this case, the elliptic curve
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
is of Kodaira type IV and
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=3
.

Step 6:

Suppose we are now in the situation that
\mathfrak{q}^{3}\mid b_{6}
.
Consider the polynomial

P(X)=X^{3}+(a/\mathfrak{q})X+(-2ab/\mathfrak{q}^{2})X+ab^{2}/\mathfrak{q}^{3}.

Since
\mathfrak{q}^{3}\mid b_{6}
, either
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})\equiv 0
or
4\pmod{6}
.
In either case,
P(X)=X^{3}\pmod{\mathfrak{q}}
, i.e.
P(X)
has a triple root in
\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

Step 7:

If
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})=4
then splitting field of the polynomial
X^{2}-(ab^{2}/\mathfrak{q}^{4})
has two distinct roots in
\kappa_{K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.
In this case, the elliptic curve is of Kodaira type IV* and
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=3
.

Step 8:

If
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{6}
, the original equation of
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
is not in the minimal form.
We perform the substitution
x=\mathfrak{q}^{2}x^{\prime}
and
y=\mathfrak{q}^{3}y^{\prime}
define
a_{i,r}=a_{i}/\mathfrak{q}^{r}
.
Then, we get the following equation of the elliptic curve:

y^{\prime 2}=x^{\prime 3}+a_{2,2}x^{\prime 2}+a_{4,4}x^{\prime}+a_{6,6}.

Here,
\mathfrak{q}\nmid a_{6,6}
.
Then,
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
is an elliptic curve of type II and
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=1
.

Similarly, we can perform Tate’s Algorithm for other cases to compute the Tamagawa number.

The next goal of this section is to compute the image of the Kummer map
\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
using Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
2.1
.
We summarize all the relevant situations in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
be a prime in
K
and
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
.

(i)

Suppose further that
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
.

(a)

If
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})=0\pmod{6}
then,
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=1
.
Moreover,
\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\cong\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}
.

(b)

If
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})=2\text{ or }4\pmod{6}
then,
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=3
.
In this case,

\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\cap\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}=\{1\}.

(ii)

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 2a
.

(a)

If
\mathfrak{q}\mid b
then,
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(b)\text{ and }c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(b)
.
In this case

\{1\}=\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\subset\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}.

(b)

If
\mathfrak{q}\mid d
then,
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(d)\text{ and }c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(d)
.
In this case,

\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}\subset\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}.

(iii)

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}\mid 2
and
\mathfrak{q}\nmid a
.

(a)

If either
\mathfrak{q}\nmid b
or
\mathfrak{q}\lVert b
then,
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=3
.
In this case,

\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\cap\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}=\{1\}.

(b)

If
\mathfrak{q}^{2}\mid\mid b
then
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(d)-2
and
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=3(v_{\mathfrak{q}}(d)-2)
.
In this case,

\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}\subset\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}.

(c)

If
\mathfrak{q}^{3}\mid b
, then
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})=3(v_{\mathfrak{q}}(d)-2)
and
c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(d)-2
.
In this case

\{1\}=\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\subset\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}.

Proof.

For each part, we used Tate’s algorithm described previously to conclude the assertion pertaining to the Tamagawa number.
We also emphasize that we give a detailed proof for only one of the cases, the other calculations can be done in exactly the same way.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 2,3
and
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
with
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})\equiv 0\pmod{6}
, then
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(2\sqrt{a}b)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.
In this case, Lemma
3.4
asserts that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\psi]\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}/\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\right\rvert}=3
.

Now, using Theorem
4.1
, we deduce that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(P))\equiv 0\pmod{3}
for all
P\in\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\psi]
.
A similar calculations as in Theorem
3.7
(i-b), shows that
\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})\subset\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}
.
Again by Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
2.1
, we get that
\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}
and
\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}
have same size.
Hence,
\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\cong\left(\frac{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast}}{A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3}}\right)_{\Norm=1}
.
∎

Next, we calculate the image of the Kummer map
\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
.

(i)

If
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, then
\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))=(A^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{q}}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
.

(ii)

If
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
and
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
has good reduction at
\mathfrak{q}
, then
\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\cong(A^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{q}}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}
.

Proof.

(i)

Since

, so
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\psi]=\{\mathcal{O}\}
.
From Lemma

, we have that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\right\rvert}=\frac{c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})}{c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})}\text{ and }\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}{\widehat{\psi}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\right\rvert}=\frac{c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b})}{c_{\mathfrak{q}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}})}.

Since both the quantities are integers and reciprocal to each other.
Therefore image of the Kummer map is trivial.
Now the result follows from Lemma

.

(ii)

Since
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}
has good reduction at
\mathfrak{q}
, so

.
Since
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
, therefore
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})[\psi]\right\rvert}=3
and then by Lemma

, we get that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}})}}{\psi(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))}\right\rvert}=3

Now from Lemma

Main Results for Type II Elliptic Curves.

Define the following set of primes of

\displaystyle S_{1}^{\prime}(K)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\colon a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2},\mathfrak{q}\mid a\text{ and }6\nmid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})\}\cup\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\colon a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2},\mathfrak{q}\mid b\text{ and }\mathfrak{q}\nmid 2a\}

This next result is an analogue of Theorem
3.7
for Type II curves.

Theorem 4.4
.

Recall from Definition
2.4

M(-)
and

(i)

If

(ii)

If
a\in K^{\ast 2}

M(K,S_{1},a)\subset\Sel^{\psi}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/K)\subset N(K,S_{2},a).

Proof.

(i)

Consider the case that
a\notin K^{\ast 2}

First we show that
M(L,S_{1},a)\subset\Sel^{\psi}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/K)
.
By the alternate definition of
M(L,S_{1},a)
proved in Proposition
2.6
, it is enough to show that,
(A^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{q}}/A_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 3})_{\Norm=1}\subset\delta_{\psi,K_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}(K_{\mathfrak{q}}))
for all
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{1}(K)
.

If
a\notin K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
then Proposition
4.3
asserts that the required containment is automatically true for all
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{1}(K)
.
On the other hand, if
a\in K_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\ast 2}
we see that
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{1}(K)
precisely when
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
and the following situations occur simultaneously

•

either
\mathfrak{q}\nmid a
or
6\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4ab^{2})

•

either
\mathfrak{q}\nmid b
or
\mathfrak{q}\mid 2a

•

if
\mathfrak{q}\mid 2
then either
\mathfrak{q}\mid a
or
\mathfrak{q}^{2}\mid b

Equivalently, if

then
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{1}(K)
in the following three cases

•

\mathfrak{q}\nmid 3
,

, and

•

\mathfrak{q}\nmid 2,3
and

\mathfrak{q}\mid d

•

In addition, for

and

.
This means that

and

By Lemma
4.2
(i-a), (ii-b), and (iii-b) (and Proposition

\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{1}(K)

.

If
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{2}(K)
, then using Lemmas
2.1
and

and hence

(ii)

Let
a\in K^{\ast 2}.
By Lemma
4.2
and Proposition

4.3.
Example

Let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},51^{1/3})
and consider the elliptic curve

\mathsf{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y^{2}=x^{3}+16(x-1)^{2}=x^{3}+16x^{2}-32x+16.

This is the curve
91b1
in the Cremona table of rank 1 over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
This is a type II curve with
a=16
,
b=-1
, and
d=4a+27b=37
.
We can check that
a\in K^{*2}
but
S_{1}^{\prime}(K)=\emptyset
.
We can easily check via SAGE that
(3)=\mathfrak{p}^{6}
for a principal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
.
So,
S_{1}(K)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.

On the other hand
(37)=\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\ldots\mathfrak{q}_{6}
, so
S_{2}(K)=\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}_{6}\}
.
Using SAGE, we compute
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S_{1}(K)}[3]=2
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S_{2}(K)}[3]=0
.
It follows from Theorem
4.6
that

0\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\widehat{\psi}}(\widehat{\mathsf{E}_{a,b}}/K)\leq 5.

Furthermore, in view of Corollary
4.7
we further conclude that

1\leq\rank(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}(K))\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/K)\leq 2+0+1+7+4=14.

Here, we were unable to compute the rank of
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/K
or even the rank of
\mathsf{E}_{a,b}/{\mathbb{Q}}(51^{1/3})
.
However, using SAGE we compute that
\mathsf{E}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})

5.
Applications

We begin this section by recording a result of Gerth which shows that the 3-part of the class group can become arbitrarily large, as we vary over number fields
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},m^{1/3})
.
More precisely,

Theorem 5.1

(
[
Ger75
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
m=3^{s}\underset{i=1}{\prod}^{J}q_{i}^{t_{i}}
, where
t_{i}=1\text{or }2
for all
1\leq i\leq J
and
s=0,1,\text{or }2
and
q_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{3}
.
Let
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},m^{1/3})
.
Then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl(K)[3]
is
2r
, where

r=\begin{cases}J&\text{if every }q_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\\
J-1&\begin{cases}\text{if every }q_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\\
\text{if some }q_{i}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\end{cases}\\
J-2&\text{if some }q_{i}\equiv 2\text{ or }5\pmod{9}\text{ and }m\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}\end{cases}.

We now prove a general result which can be used in our setting for the case that
p=3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be any number field and
S(\mathcal{F})
be a finite set of finite primes.
Then,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Cl(\mathcal{F})[p]-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\Cl_{S}(\mathcal{F})[p]\right\rvert}\leq 3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(\mathcal{F})\right\rvert}.

Proof.

Our proof is adapted from
[
LM16
, Lemma 5.2]
.
We provide a brief sketch for the convenience of the reader.
Note that
[
NSW08
, Lemma 10.3.12]
asserts that there exists an exact sequence

{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(\mathcal{F})\right\rvert}}\longrightarrow\Cl(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\theta_{\mathcal{F}}}\Cl_{S}(\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow 0.

It is straightforward to notice from the above exact sequence that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\ker(\theta_{\mathcal{F}})\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(\mathcal{F})\right\rvert}
.
Since
\ker(\theta_{\mathcal{F}})
is a subgroup of the class group it is finite; we can conclude that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\ker(\theta_{\mathcal{F}})/p)\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(\mathcal{F})\right\rvert}
.
It follows from
[
LM16
, Lemma 3.2]
that

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\Cl(\mathcal{F}))[p]-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(\Cl_{S}(\mathcal{F}))[p]\right\rvert}\leq 3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(\mathcal{F})\right\rvert}.\qed

We now record an immediate corollary of the above discussion.

Corollary 5.3
.

Keep the notation introduced in Theorem
5.1

Fix a finite set of finite primes of
K
and denote it by
S(K)
.
Then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\geq 2r-3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}
, where
r
is defined as in Theorem
5.1
.

The next result shows us that the 3-part of the
\phi
-Selmer group of a Type I elliptic curve can also be made arbitrarily large as we vary over all
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},m^{1/3})
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathsf{E}_{a}
be a Type I elliptic curve and
\phi
be a rational 3-isogeny.
Let
m=3^{s}\underset{i=1}{\prod}^{J}q_{i}^{t_{i}}
be as defined in Theorem
5.1
.
As before, define
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3},m^{1/3})
, fix
S(K)=S_{a}(K)\cup\{\mathfrak{p}\}
and set
r
as in Theorem
5.1
.
Then for all
a
,

2r-3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Cl_{S}(K)[3]\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K).

In particular, as
m
varies over all possible values,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
becomes arbitrarily large.

Proof.

The inequality follows immediately from Theorem
3.9
and Corollary
5.3
.

For the last observation, we note that as we vary
m
the number of distinct divisors of
m
becomes larger.
More precisely,
J\rightarrow\infty
, hence
r\rightarrow\infty
.

Claim:

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}
is bounded above by a constant independent of
J
.

Justification:
By definition, it suffices to show that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{a}(K)\right\rvert}
is bounded above by a constant independent of
J
.
A naïve upper bound on how large the set
S_{a}(K)
can be is given by

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S_{a}(K)\right\rvert}\leq[K\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathbb{Q}}]\times\left(\#\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{K}\mid\mathfrak{q}\mid 4a\}\right)\leq 6^{2}\omega(4a).

Here, we use
\omega(n)
to denote the number of distinct primes factors of
n
.
The claim follows.

We see that as
J\rightarrow\infty
, the quantity
2r-3\mathinner{\!\left\lvert S(K)\right\rvert}\rightarrow\infty
as well.
In other words, as
m
varies over all possible values, the 3-part of the
\phi
-Selmer group becomes arbitrarily large.
∎

We explained in the proof of Corollary
3.11
that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{\phi}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)\leq\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
.
By Corollary
5.4
we see that as
m
varies over all possible values,
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{3}}\Sel^{3}(\mathsf{E}_{a}/K)
becomes arbitrarily large.
It would be interesting to pin down whether the large 3-Selmer over
K
is accounted for by large Mordell–Weil rank of
\mathsf{E}_{a}/K
or a large (3-part) of the Shafarevich–Tate group.
Over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we know that (at present) the record for largest rank of a Type I curve is 17 discovered by N. Elkies in 2016.
However, it is difficult to compute the rank of
\mathsf{E}_{a}/K
as
K
varies.

.

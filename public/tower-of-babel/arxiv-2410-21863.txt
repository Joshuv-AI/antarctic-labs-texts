Abstract
In this paper, we establish the invariance of observability for the observed backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) with constant coefficients, relative to the filtered probability space. This signifies that the observability of these observed BSDEs with constant coefficients remains unaffected by the selection of the filtered probability space. As an illustrative application, we demonstrate that for stochastic control systems with constant coefficients, weak observability, approximate null controllability with cost, and stabilizability are equivalent across some or any filtered probability spaces. Keywords. Stochastic control system, backward stochastic differential equation, observability, approximate null controllability with cost, stabilizability AMS subject classifications. 93E03, 93B07, 93B05, 93D15

Introduction

In this paper, we establish the invariance of observability for Backward Stochastic Differential Equations (BSDEs) under changes to the filtered probability space. This property fundamentally relies on the uniqueness of weak solutions (which is one kind of uniqueness in the sense of probability law) for Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) and forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs).

The uniqueness of weak solutions is a pivotal and classical topic in the theory of stochastic differential equations. Initially, the uniqueness of weak solutions for SDEs was proven in
[
23
]
, where the authors demonstrated that pathwise uniqueness of strong solutions implies uniqueness of weak solutions. For additional references in this field, one may refer, for example, to

[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
18
]
, and the references therein. With the progression of BSDE and FBSDE theories, the uniqueness of their weak solutions has been extensively studied over the past two decades. Notable references in this domain include
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
24
]
, and the related citations. To our knowledge, no literature has yet utilized these uniqueness results to investigate the invariance of (weak or initial) observability of BSDEs concerning the filtered probability space. However, this invariance is crucial in characterizing the stabilizability of stochastic control system through observability.

For nearly three decades, controllability and stabilizability are pivotal topics in control theory, particularly within the realm of stochastic control systems (refer to, for instance,
[
8
,
9
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
22
,
26
]
, and the associated references). Specifically, in
[
9
, Chapter 6]
, the authors conducted a comparative analysis between deterministic and stochastic systems and compiled numerous existing findings on the controllability of stochastic control systems. These results indicate that the controllability of stochastic control systems remains an area of ongoing research. Indeed, due to the influence of random disturbances, stochastic control systems inherently possess infinite-dimensional characteristics. Consequently, determining whether such systems possess criteria analogous to the Kalman rank condition (pertinent to deterministic control systems) emerges as an intriguing and significant problem.
Unfortunately, such criteria are only applicable to certain specialized stochastic control systems (see
[
9
, Chapter 6, Theorems 6.8 and 6.10]
). It is noteworthy that in
[
9
]
, the authors primarily examined null controllability and exact controllability. Hence, exploring more relaxed notions of controllability may offer enhanced algebraic characterizations. Recently, in
[
21
]
, the authors introduced the concept of approximate null controllability with cost to delineate the exponential stabilizability of deterministic infinite-dimensional control systems (also addressed in
[
7
,
12
]
). This result motivates us to characterize the approximate null controllability with cost of stochastic control systems through their stabilizability.

The primary contributions and novelties of this paper can be summarized as follows: a) We demonstrate that, for a BSDE with constant coefficients, its (weak and initial) observability is independent of the chosen filtered probability space; b) We introduce the concept of approximate null controllability with cost and establish its equivalence to the corresponding stabilizability of the stochastic control system. Consequently, the stabilizability of the stochastic control system, which is inherently a property extending to infinite horizons, can be translated into a property confined to finite horizons: approximate null controllability with cost and the conclusions on the the stabilizability of the stochastic control system con be used to characterize its approximate null controllability with cost; c) We transform the notion of approximate controllability with cost for stochastic control systems across any filtered probability spaces into the weak observability of the dual-observation system within a fixed filtered probability space. Furthermore, we present an algebraic condition to the approximate null controllability with cost associated with this transformed property.

3.1

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}|y(0;y_{1})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq c(\delta,T)E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{0}^{T}|z(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 3.2
.

Three points should be noted regarding Definition

From Definition

, the observability of the system

Therefore, the primary objective of this paper is to determine

[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is
said to be feedback stabilizable (or simply stabilizable) if
there is an
F\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,
\delta>0
and
C:=C(F,\delta,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P}))>0
such that, for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the solution
x_{F}(\cdot;x_{0})
of the following closed-loop system:

\begin{cases}dx(t)=(A+BF)x(t)dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(C_{i}+D_{i}F)x(t)dw^{i}(t),\;\;t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
x(0)=x_{0}\end{cases}

(3.5)

satisfies the condition:

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{F}(t;x_{0})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}|x_{0}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;t\to+\infty.

(3.6)

Remark 3.7
.

It is well-established in the theory of stability that there exist various definitions, such as exponential stability,
L^{2}
-stability. However, according to
[
5
, Proposition 3.6]
(or
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
), these definitions are equivalent in the context of finite-dimensional stochastic systems..

According to
[
9
, Chapter 7, Theorem 7.17]
, for a fixed

T>0
,
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is null controllable on

[0,T]
if and only if the system
[A^{\top},B^{\top},C^{\top},D^{\top},\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]_{T}

is initially observable on
[0,T]
. Combining this with Theorem
3.3
, we can derive the following Corollary
3.8
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Given
T>0
, the following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

For any filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
, the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is null controllable on
[0,T]
.

(ii)

There exists a filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
such that the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is null controllable on
[0,T]
.

Recently, in
[
21
]
, the authors introduced the concept of weak observability to investigate the exponential stabilizability of deterministic infinite-dimensional control systems. In that paper, they established the equivalence between weak observability, approximate null controllability with cost, and exponential stabilizability.
It is noteworthy that in a stochastic framework, using weak observability (over a finite horizon) to characterize stabilizability (over an infinite horizon) is significantly more complex than the analogous problem for deterministic systems. Indeed, when studying the exponential stabilizability of stochastic control systems, the sample information varies across different equal-length intervals. This challenge motivates us to explore whether weak observability remains consistent across different probability spaces.

Therefore, as another objective of this paper,
we aim to extend the main result in
[
21
]
to stochastic control systems by leveraging Theorem
3.3
.

The second main result is Theorem
3.9
following.

Theorem 3.9
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

For any filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
, the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is feedback stabilizable.

(ii)

For any filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
, the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is approximately null controllable with cost.

(iii)

There exists a filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
such that the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is approximately null controllable with cost.

(iv)

There exists a filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
such that the system
[A^{\top},B^{\top},C^{\top},D^{\top},\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is weakly observable.

Remark 3.10

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Since
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
is evident, we only need to show
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let the filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
be fixed. Assume that the system
[A^{\top},B^{\top},C^{\top},D^{\top},\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]_{T}
is
\delta

[0,T]
. Then, according to the definition of

\mathcal{O}_{op}:=\mathcal{O}_{op}(\delta,T,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P}))
, we obtain that,
for any
y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}^{w}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}
,

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}|y^{w}(0,y_{1})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}_{op}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{w}(\sigma;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y^{w}_{i}(\sigma;y_{1})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}d\sigma+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}|y_{1}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(4.1)

Next, consider an arbitrary filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}})
. We aim to prove that

\mathcal{O}_{op}(\delta,T,\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}))\leq\mathcal{O}_{op}.

(4.2)

Upon establishing this inequality, the proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
will be complete. Indeed, if (
4.2
) holds, it is straightforward that
[A^{\top},B^{\top},C^{\top},D^{\top},\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}})]_{T}
is
\delta
-observable on
[0,T]
.
Furthermore, by interchanging the roles of

\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}})
and
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
in the above arguments, we can conclude that

\mathcal{O}_{op}\leq\mathcal{O}_{op}(\delta,T,\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}))
.
It follows that
\mathcal{O}_{op}=\mathcal{O}_{op}(\delta,T,\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}}))
and

\mathcal{O}_{op}
is independent of the the specific filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
.

We now proceed to prove (

). The proof is structured into several steps.

Step 1: Fix

\alpha_{k}:=(\alpha_{k,1},\alpha_{k,2},\cdots,\alpha_{k,n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\widehat{\xi}_{l,N}:=F_{l,N}(x^{\widehat{w}}(T))

is the solution of the equation on
\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w},\widehat{\mathbb{P}})

dx^{\widehat{w}}(t)=-H^{\top}(t)dt+h^{\top}(t)d\widehat{w}(t)\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;t\in(0,T)\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;x^{\widehat{w}}(0)=0.

(4.3)

Here,
H
and
h
are given by (
2.21
). We aim to prove that

E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}|y^{\widehat{w}}(0;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}_{op}E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{\widehat{w}}(\sigma;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y^{\widehat{w}}_{i}(\sigma;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}d\sigma+\delta E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}|\widehat{\xi}_{l,N}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(4.4)

x^{w}
be the solution of the following equation on

According to Lemma

y^{\widehat{w}}(\cdot;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})=U(\cdot,x^{\widehat{w}}(\cdot))\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Y^{\widehat{w}}(\cdot;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})=\nabla U(\cdot,x^{\widehat{w}}(\cdot))h^{\top}(\cdot)

(4.5)

and

y^{w}(\cdot;\xi_{l,N})=U(\cdot,x^{w}(\cdot))\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Y^{w}(\cdot;\xi_{l,N})=\nabla U(\cdot,x^{w}(\cdot))h^{\top}(\cdot)

(4.6)

in

2.23

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}|y^{\widehat{w}}(0;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=|U(0,0)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}=E_{\mathbb{P}}|y^{w}(0;\xi_{l,N})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{op}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}U(\sigma,x^{w}(\sigma))+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}(\nabla U(\sigma,x^{w}(\sigma))h^{\top}(\sigma))_{i}\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}d\sigma+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}|F_{l,N}(x^{w}(T))|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{op}E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}U(\sigma,x^{\widehat{w}}(\sigma))+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}(\nabla U(\sigma,x^{\widehat{w}}(\sigma))h^{\top}(\sigma))_{i}\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}d\sigma+\delta E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}|F_{l,N}(x^{\widehat{w}}(T))|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{op}E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{\widehat{w}}(\sigma;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y^{\widehat{w}}_{i}(\sigma;\widehat{\xi}_{l,N})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}d\sigma+\delta E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}|\widehat{\xi}_{l,N}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Step 2: We prove that the claim in Step 1 also holds when

\displaystyle E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|y^{\widehat{w}}(t;\xi_{1})-y^{\widehat{w}}(t;\xi_{2})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{\widehat{w}}_{i}(t;\xi_{1})-Y^{\widehat{w}}_{i}(t;\xi_{2})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\Big)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|y^{\widehat{w}}(t;\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{\widehat{w}}_{i}(t;\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\Big)

(4.8)

where

(4.9)

F_{l,N}(\xi_{1})-F_{l,N}(\xi_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla F_{l,N}(\xi_{2}+\rho(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}))d\rho(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})

\xi
, leads to the conclusion that

E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}|y^{\widehat{w}}(0;\xi)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq\mathcal{O}_{op}E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{\widehat{w}}(\sigma;\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}^{\widehat{w}}(\sigma;\xi)\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}d\sigma+\delta E_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}|\xi|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{\widehat{w}}_{T}}(\Omega,\widehat{\mathbb{P}};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Consequently, (
4.2
) holds. Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

5
The proof of Theorem
3.9

Before presenting the proof of Theorem
3.9
, we first provide some characterizations of approximate null controllability with cost in a general time interval.

Definition 5.1
.

Given
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
0<s<T
. The system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is said to be
\delta
-null controllable in
[s,T]
with cost if there exists a constant
c(\delta,s,T)>0
such that, for any
x_{s}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a control
u\in L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfying

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq c(\delta,s,T)\|x_{s}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;E_{\mathbb{P}}|x(T;u,x_{s},s)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq\delta\|x_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(5.1)

where and in what follows
x(\cdot;u,x_{s},s)
is the solution to the following equation

\begin{cases}dx(t)=(Ax(t)+Bu(t))dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(C_{i}x(t)+D_{i}u(t))dw^{i}(t),\;\;t\in(s,T),\\
x(s)=x_{s}.\end{cases}

(5.2)

The constant
c(\delta,s,T)
is referred to as the cost for the
\delta
-null controllability of
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
in
[s,T]
.

The following theorem holds independent significance within the realm of control theory.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
0<s<T
be fixed. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is
\delta
-null controllable in
[s,T]
with cost for some
\delta\in(0,1)
.

(ii)

There exists
\delta\in(0,1)
and
c>0
such that, for any
y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

E_{\mathbb{P}}|y(s;y_{1})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq cE_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y(t;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2},

(5.3)

where
(y(\cdot;y_{1}),Y(\cdot;y_{1}):=(Y_{i}(\cdot;y_{1}))_{i=1}^{d})
is the solution to the BSDE in (
3.1
) with terminal value
y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In particular, if
(i)
holds with cost
c(\delta,s,T)
, then (
5.3
) is satisfied by substituting
(c,\delta)
with
(c(\delta,s,T)(1+2(1-\delta)^{-1}),\frac{1+\delta}{2})
. Conversely,
if
(
5.3
) holds for any
y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is
\delta
-null controllable on
[s,T]
with cost
\sqrt{c\delta^{-1}c_{0}(T-s)}
. Here,
c_{0}(T-s)
is the constant such that

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x(T;0,\xi,s)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq c_{0}(T-s)E_{\mathbb{P}}|\xi|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(5.4)

Remark 5.3
.

(i)

By utilizing the classical variation of constants formula for SDEs and the Gronwall inequality, it can be readily verified that there exists a function
c_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that, for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,+\infty)
with
t_{2}>t_{1}
,
it holds that

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x(t_{2};0,\xi,t_{1})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq c_{0}(t_{2}-t_{1})E_{\mathbb{P}}|\xi|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}
for any

\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{t_{1}}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

In Theorem
5.2
, we have presented an explicitly relationship between the cost
c(\delta,s,T)
of
\delta
-null controllability of
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
and observability constant
c
in (
5.3
). Whether they are equal or not is still unknown to us.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

We first prove the implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Suppose that
\delta\in(0,1)
such that the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is
\delta
-null controllable in
[s,T]
with cost.
Consequently, there exists a constant
c(\delta,s,T)>0
such that, for any
x_{s}
, there
exists a control
u\in L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfying
the inequalities in (
5.1
).

Fix
x_{s}
arbitrarily and choose
u\in L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
be such that (
5.1
) holds. For any
y_{1}
, we apply Itô’s formula to
the inner product
\langle x(\cdot;u,x_{s},s),y(\cdot;y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

(over the interval
[s,T]
), yielding

\displaystyle d\langle x(t;u,x_{s},s),y(t;y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\Big\langle u(t),B^{\top}y(t;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\langle C_{i}x(t;u,x_{s},s)+D_{i}u(t),y(t;y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\langle x(t;u,x_{s},s),Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}]dw^{i}(t).

As a result, we obtain the following equation

E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x(T;u,x_{s},s),y_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}-E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x_{s},y(s;y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big\langle u(t),B^{\top}y(t;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt.

(5.5)

Combining this equation with (
5.1
) and applying the Hölder inequality, we derive

\displaystyle|E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x_{s},y(s;y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big(|u(t)|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big|B^{\top}y(t;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big)dt+E_{\mathbb{P}}(|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x(T;u,x_{s},s)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{c(\delta,s,T)}\big(E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{s}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl(E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y(t;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\biggl|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{\delta}(E_{\mathbb{P}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{s}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, due to the arbitrariness of
x_{s}
, we can conclude

E_{\mathbb{P}}|y(s;y_{1})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq c(\delta,s,T)(1+2(1-\delta)^{-1})E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y(t;y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt+\frac{1+\delta}{2}E_{\mathbb{P}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}.

Given the arbitrariness of
y_{1}
and the condition
\delta\in(0,1)
, we can deduce that (
5.3
) holds with the substitution of
(c,\delta)
by
(c(\delta,s,T)(1+2(1-\delta)^{-1}),\frac{1+\delta}{2})
. Consequently,
(ii)
is satisfied.

We will now prove the implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be given, and suppose that there exists a constant
c>0
such that (
5.3
) holds for any
y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
We define the mapping
\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}\xi,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=cE_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big\langle B^{\top}y(t;\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;\xi),B^{\top}y(t;\eta)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;\eta)\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt

\displaystyle+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;\xi,\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(5.6)

It is evident that
\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}
is self-adjoint and bounded on
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, satisfying the following inequality
for any
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

\langle\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\xi,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}|\xi|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\langle\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\xi,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq E_{\mathbb{P}}|y(s;\xi)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(5.7)

Consequently, by the Lax-Milgram theorem, we get that
\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}
is invertible, and

\Big\|\big(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}\big)^{-1}\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq\delta^{-1}.

(5.8)

Given an arbitrarily
x_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

f:=\big(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}\big)^{-1}x(T;0,x_{s},s)(\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n}))

(5.9)

and

u_{f}(t):=-c\Big(B^{\top}y(t;f)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;f)\Big),\;\;t\in[s,T].

(5.10)

It is evident that
u_{f}\in L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}|u_{f}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{2}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y(t;f)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;f)\Big|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{2}dt=c\big(\langle\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}-\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}|f|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big)

(5.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\big\langle x(T;0,x_{s},s),(\mathcal{G}^{\delta}_{[s,T]})^{-1}x(T;0,x_{s},s)\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then, combining (
5.8
), (
5.11
) and the definition of
c_{0}
, we derive

\|u_{f}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\sqrt{c\delta^{-1}c_{0}(T-s)}\|x_{s}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(5.12)

Next, we aim to prove that

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x(T;u_{f},x_{s},s)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq\delta\|x_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(5.13)

Before doing so, we first establish

E_{\mathbb{P}}|f|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq\delta^{-1}\|x_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{s}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(5.14)

Indeed, by applying Itô’s formula to
\big\langle x(\cdot;0,x_{s},s),y(\cdot;(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta})^{-1}f)\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and utilizing the second inequality in (
5.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}|f|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\big\langle x(T;0,x_{s},s),(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta})^{-1}f\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=E_{\mathbb{P}}\big\langle x_{s},y(s;(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta})^{-1}f)\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\big(|x_{s}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y(s,(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta})^{-1}f)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big)\leq\big(E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{s}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\big\langle f,(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta})^{-1}f\big\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{s}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta})^{-1}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}))}(E_{\mathbb{P}}|f|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This, combined with (
5.8
), confirms the validity of (
5.14
).
Now, let
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
be arbitrary. Similar to the proof of (
5.5
), we derive

E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x(T;u_{f},x_{s},s),\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}-E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x_{s},y(s;\xi)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big\langle u_{f}(t),B^{\top}y(t;\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}(t;\xi)\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt.

This, along with (
5.6
), (
5.9
), and (
5.10
), leads to

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x(T;u_{f},x_{s},s),\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x_{s},y(s;\xi)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}-\langle\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}f,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle f,\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

(5.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x_{s},y(s;\xi)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}-E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x(T;0,x_{s},s),\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle f,\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Note that, using Itô’s formula, it can be shown that
E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x_{s},y(s;\xi)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}-E\langle x(T;0,x_{s},s),\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=0
. Thus, combining (
5.15
) and (
5.14
), we obtain

E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle x(T;u_{f},x_{s},s),\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\delta E_{\mathbb{P}}\langle f,\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(E_{\mathbb{P}}|\xi|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{s}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Given the arbitrariness of
\xi
, this implies that
(
5.13
) holds. By (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we can conclude that the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is
\delta
-null controllable on
[0,T]
with cost
\sqrt{c\delta^{-1}c_{0}(T-s)}
. In other words, the claim
(i)
holds. The proof of Theorem
5.2
is now complete.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The operator
\mathcal{G}_{[s,T]}^{\delta}
defined by (

) can be interpreted as the Gramian operator of a stochastic observation system (cf.
[
12
]
for the deterministic observation system case).

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

The proof will be divided into several steps.

Step 1:
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
.
Suppose that, for any filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
, the system
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is feedback stabilizable. Fix an arbitrary filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
. By our assumption, there exists
F\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
such that, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the solution
x_{F}(\cdot;x_{0})
of the closed-loop system (
3.5
)
satisfies (
3.6
), i.e., there is
\alpha>0
and

c(\alpha)>0
such that for each

and any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such the following inequality holds:

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{F}(t;x_{0})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq c(\alpha)e^{-\alpha t}|x_{0}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(5.16)

Consequently, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
T(\delta)>0
such that, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x_{F}(t;x_{0})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq\delta|x_{0}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

(5.17)

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta\in(0,1)

T:=T(\delta)
and
u_{F}(\cdot):=Fx_{F}(\cdot;x_{0})
. It is evident that
x_{F}(\cdot;x_{0})=x(\cdot;u_{F},x_{0})
.
Furthermore, by (
5.16
) and (
5.17
), we can deduce that

E_{\mathbb{P}}|x(T;u_{F},x_{0})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq\delta|x_{0}|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

(5.18)

and

\displaystyle\|u_{F}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=\Big(E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{0}^{T}|Fx(t;u_{F},x_{0})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c(\delta,T)|x_{0}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

(5.19)

where
c(\delta,T):=\alpha^{-1}c(\alpha)(1-e^{-\alpha T})|F|_{\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}
.

Given the arbitrariness of
x_{0}
, (
5.18
) and (
5.19
) imply that
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is
\delta
-null controllable with cost in
[0,T]
. This, combined with the arbitrariness of
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
and
\delta
, leads to the conclusion that, for any
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
,
[A,B,C,D,\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is approximately null controllable with cost. Hence,
(ii)
holds.

Step 2:
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
.
This implication is straightforward.

Step 3:
(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)
.
This is evident by Theorem
5.2
.

Step 4:
(iv)\Rightarrow(i)
.
Suppose that the system
[A^{\top},B^{\top},C^{\top},D^{\top},\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})]
is weakly observable for a filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
. This means that there exist
T\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\delta\in[0,1)
and
c(\delta,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that the weak observability inequality (
3.2
) holds on
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
. Therefore, according to Theorem
3.3
, the weak observability inequality (
3.2
) holds on any filtered probability space
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
with the same
T
,
\delta
and
c(\delta,T)
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
be fixed arbitrarily.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\widehat{w}_{k}(\cdot)=w(kT+\cdot)-w(kT)
in
[0,+\infty)
and
\mathfrak{T}_{k}:=\mathfrak{T}(\widehat{w}_{k},\mathbb{P}_{k})
with
\mathbb{P}_{k}:=\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathcal{F}^{w}_{kT})
and
\{\mathcal{F}^{\widehat{w}_{k}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
to be the natural filtration on
\mathfrak{T}_{k}
. (Here, we note that
\widehat{w}_{k}
is a standard Brownain motion on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}_{k})
.) It is clear that
\widehat{w}_{0}=w
and
\mathfrak{T}_{0}=\mathfrak{T}(w,\mathbb{P})
. By Theorem
3.3
and our assumption, we can assert that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}|y^{\widehat{w}_{k}}(0,y_{1})|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq c(\delta,T)E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{\widehat{w}_{k}}(t,y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y^{\widehat{w}_{k}}_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}

for any
y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\widehat{w}_{k}}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P}_{k};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

(y^{\widehat{w}_{k}}(\cdot,y_{1}),Y^{\widehat{w}_{k}}(\cdot;y_{1}):=(Y_{i}^{\widehat{w}_{k}}(\cdot;y_{1}))_{i=1}^{d})
is the solution of the dual system in (
3.1
) on
\mathfrak{T}_{k}
.
Combining this with Proposition
2.2
, we obtain that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}|y^{k+1}(kT;y_{1})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(\delta,T)E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{k+1}(kT+t,y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y^{k+1}_{i}(kT+t;y_{1})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}

(5.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(\delta,T)E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}\int_{kT}^{(k+1)T}\Big|B^{\top}y^{k+1}(t,y_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{i}^{\top}Y^{k+1}_{i}(t;y_{1})\Big|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}dt

\displaystyle+\delta E_{\mathbb{P}_{k}}|y_{1}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\;\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.,\;\;\mbox{on}\;\;(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;y_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{(k+1)T}}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}Y^{s,\zeta}_{i}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;t\in\begin{cases}[T-r_{0},T],&\mbox{if}\;\;l(\theta)>0,\\
[\theta,T],&\mbox{if}\;\;l(\theta)=0,\end{cases}

can be decomposed as

. In this case, we can replace

with

respectively
(noting that, by (

).
Using the same reasoning, we can demonstrate that
(y^{s,\zeta},Y^{s,\zeta})\in L^{2}(T-2r_{0},T-r_{0};\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{T}^{s}(w,\mathbb{P}),n}^{1,2}(s,T))\times(L^{2}(T-2r_{0},T-r_{0};\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{T}^{s}(w,\mathbb{P}),n}^{1,2}(s,T)))^{d}
,

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}y^{s,\zeta},\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}Y^{s,\zeta})\in L^{2}(\Omega\times((T-2r_{0})\vee\theta,T-r_{0}),\mathbb{P}\times\lambda;\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\times L^{2}(\Omega\times((T-2r_{0})\vee\theta,T-r_{0}),\mathbb{P}\times\lambda;\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(T-r_{0})+\int_{t}^{T-r_{0}}\Big(A^{\top}\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C_{i}^{\top}\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)\Big)d\sigma

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T-r_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}Y^{s,\zeta}_{i}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;t\in\begin{cases}[T-2r_{0},T-r_{0}],&\mbox{if}\;\;l(\theta)>1,\\
[\theta,T-r_{0}],&\mbox{if}\;\;l(\theta)=1.\end{cases}

Thus, employing an inductive argument and (
6.4
), we can establish that (
6.3
) holds. Leveraging (
6.3
), Lemma
6.1
, and the facts that
\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)=0

\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)=0

(a)
is true.

Step 2: The proof of
(b)
.
Utilizing (
6.4
), Claim (a), and Lemma
6.1
(applied to (
6.3
)), we can readily deduce that, for
every
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\Big(\int_{\theta}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq c(p,s,T)\|h^{\top}\|^{p}_{C([s,T];\mathbb{R}^{(nl)\times d})}.

(6.13)

Let
\psi(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}Y^{s,\zeta}_{i}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)
for
t\in[s,T]
.
It is straightforward to verify that, for any
(\theta,t)\in[s,T]\times[s,T]
,

\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}\psi(t)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(|Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\theta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{D}_{\theta}^{j}Y_{i}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)\Big|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}\Big).

By applying the Bennett-Durrett-Gundy inequality, for each
p\geq 2
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{T}|\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}\psi(t)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}d\theta\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(p,s,T)\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\theta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\theta\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)\Big|^{p}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\theta\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(p,s,T)\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\theta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\theta\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{T}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}|\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}d\sigma\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\theta\Big].

Thus, combining Lemma
6.1
and (
6.13
), we conclude that

E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{T}|\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}\psi(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\theta\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}dt<+\infty
.
Therefore, according to
[
13
, Proposition 3.2.2, Chapter 3]
, we can assert that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}Y^{s,\zeta}_{i}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)
possesses a continuous version. Furthermore, by Lemma
6.1
, it is evident that the term
\int_{t}^{T}\big(A^{\top}y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C_{i}^{\top}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)\big)d\sigma
also has a continuous version. It follows that
y^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)
has a continuous version over
[s,T]
. By substituting
s
and
F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))
with
\theta
and
F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}x^{s,\zeta}(T)
respectively, and utilizing (
6.3
), we can replicate the aforementioned procedure to demonstrate that
\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}y^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)

possesses a continuous version over
[\theta,T]
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
. Therefore, the claim
(b)
is true.

Step 3: The proof of
(c)
.
Indeed, for each
\theta\in(s,T]
,
based on (
6.2
) and the equation

\displaystyle y^{s,\zeta}(t)=y^{s,\zeta}(s)-\int_{s}^{t}\Big(A^{\top}y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C_{i}^{\top}Y^{s,\zeta}_{i}(\sigma)\Big)d\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{t}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;t\in(\theta,T],

we can deduce that, for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
and any
\theta\in(s,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}y^{s,\zeta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{j}^{s,\zeta}(\theta)-\int_{\theta}^{t}\Big(A^{\top}\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C_{i}^{\top}\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y^{s,\zeta}_{i}(\sigma)\Big)d\sigma

(6.14)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\theta}^{t}\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;t\in(\theta,T].

By Claim (b) and taking the limit as
t\to\theta^{+}
in (

), we can conclude that
\mathbb{D}^{j}_{\theta}y^{s,\zeta}(\theta)=Y_{j}^{s,\zeta}(\theta)

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for any
\theta\in[s,T]
.

is complete.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

The proof is organized into two steps.

Step 1: The function
\zeta\to(y^{s,\zeta},Y^{s,\zeta})
is second-order continuously differentiable
\mathbb{P}\times\lambda
-almost surely, and satisfies the equation

\nabla y^{s,\zeta}(t)=\nabla F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(A^{\top}\nabla y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C^{\top}_{i}\nabla Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)\Big)d\sigma-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}\nabla Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma).

(6.15)

Furthermore,

\nabla y^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)
has a continuous versions over
[s,T]
.

For a function
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n})^{\top}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{nl};\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,nl\}
, we define the difference operator at

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{nl}
along the direction
e_{j}
with step size

h
(where
h>0
) as

\triangle_{h}^{j}g(\xi):=h^{-1}(g(\xi+he_{j})-g(\xi))
, where
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{nl}
represents the
j
-th vector in the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{nl}
.
It is evident that
\nabla g(\xi)e_{j}=\lim_{h\to 0}(\triangle_{h}^{j}g(\xi))_{i=1}^{d}
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,nl\}
.
Given the following facts:

F_{l,N}(x_{1})-F_{l,N}(x_{2})=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\nabla F_{l,N}(x_{2}+\rho(x_{1}-x_{2}))d\rho\Big)(x_{1}-x_{2})\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{nl}

and

\triangle_{h}^{j}x^{s,\zeta}(t)=e_{j}
for each
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,nl\}
,
one can readily verify that, for every
t\in[s,T]
,

\displaystyle\triangle_{h}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(t)=G_{N,h,j}^{s,\zeta}e_{j}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(A^{\top}\triangle_{h}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C^{\top}_{i}\triangle_{h}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)\Big)d\sigma-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}\triangle_{h}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\sigma)dw^{i}(\sigma)

(6.16)

for each
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,nl\}
, where
G_{N,h,j}^{s,\zeta}:=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T)+\rho he_{j})d\rho
. Now, we fix
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,nl\}
arbitrarily. By the definition of
F_{l,N}
, it is clear that

|G_{N,h,j}^{s,\zeta}-G_{N,h^{\prime},j}^{s,\zeta}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times nl}}\leq c(N)|h-h^{\prime}|

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for any
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
This, along with (
6.16
) and Lemma
6.1
, implies that

E_{\mathbb{P}}\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}|\triangle_{h}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(t)-\triangle_{h^{\prime}}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}|\triangle_{h}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(t)-\triangle_{h^{\prime}}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\leq c(N)|h-h^{\prime}|^{2}

(6.17)

for any
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. It follows that, for any
sequence
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\lim_{k\to+\infty}h_{k}=0
,
there exists a tuple
(z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta),Z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta):=(Z^{j}_{i}(\cdot;\zeta))_{i=1}^{d})\in L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}^{s}(w,\mathbb{P})}(\Omega;C([s,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times(L^{2}_{\mathfrak{T}^{s}(w,\mathbb{P})}(s,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{d}
such that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Big(E_{\mathbb{P}}\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}|\triangle_{h_{k}}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(t)-z^{j}(t;\zeta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}|\triangle_{h_{k}}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(t)-Z^{j}_{i}(t;\zeta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\Big)=0.

(6.18)

Moreover, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{h\to 0^{+}}E_{\mathbb{P}}|G_{N,h,j}^{s,\zeta}-\nabla F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n\times nl}}=0
.
It follows from (
6.16
) and (
6.18
) that
the tuple
(z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta),Z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta))
satisfies

z^{j}(t;\zeta)=\nabla F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))e_{j}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(A^{\top}z^{j}(\sigma,\zeta)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}C^{\top}_{i}Z^{j}_{i}(\sigma,\zeta)\Big)d\sigma-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t}^{T}Z_{i}^{j}(\sigma,\zeta)dw^{i}(\sigma).

Furthermore, by (
6.17
) and (
6.18
), it is evident that

\lim_{h\to 0}\Big(E_{\mathbb{P}}\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}|\triangle_{h}^{j}y^{s,\zeta}(t)-z^{j}(t,\zeta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\mathbb{P}}\int_{s}^{T}|\triangle_{h}^{j}Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(t)-Z^{j}_{i}(t,\zeta)|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}dt\Big)=0.

This, combined with the arbitrariness of

\nabla y^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)=z(\cdot;\zeta):=(z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta))_{j=1}^{n}

\nabla Y_{i}^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)=Z_{i}(\cdot;\zeta):=(Z_{i}^{j}(\cdot;\zeta))_{j=1}^{n}

Therefore, the function
\zeta\to(y^{s,\zeta},Y^{s,\zeta})

\mathbb{P}\times\lambda
-almost surely, and (

(z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta),Z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta))

(z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta),Z^{j}(\cdot;\zeta))
is also first-order differentiable
\mathbb{P}\times\lambda
-almost surely. Consequently,
\zeta\to(y^{s,\zeta},Y^{s,\zeta})
is second-order differentiable
\mathbb{P}\times\lambda
-almost surely. The continuity of

\zeta\to(y^{s,\zeta},Y^{s,\zeta})
and its first and second-order differentiations are straightforward to prove due to the smoothness of

F_{l,N}
.

By replacing
F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))
with
\nabla F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T))e_{j}
, and following the same reasoning used in the proof of part

\nabla y^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)e_{j}
has a continuous version over
[s,T]

Step 2: Completion of the proof.

From part (a) of Lemma
6.2
, the claim in
Step 1
, and the uniqueness of the solution to the FBSDE (

), we can conclude that

\mathbb{D}_{\theta}y^{s,\zeta}(t)=\nabla y^{s,\zeta}(t)h^{\top}(\theta)

for any

This, combined with part

Substep 2.1: Show that

is in

, bounded and satisfies the equation (
2.23

By Lemma
6.1
, the claim in
Step 1
, and the definition of

F_{l,N}
, it can be readily shown that

is bounded and in

. Other conclusions have been established in
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
using the Itô formula and the claims in
Step 1
. Therefore, we omit the proof here.

Substep 2.2: Demonstrate the uniqueness of the solution to equation (
2.23
) and establish that if
U(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{nl};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the solution of the equation (
2.23
), then
(y^{s,\zeta}(t),Y^{s,\zeta}(t)):=(U(t,x^{s,\zeta}(t)),\nabla U(t,x^{s,\zeta}(t))h^{\top}(t))
solves the BSDE in (
2.22
).

We first demonstrate that if
U(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{nl};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the solution to the equation (
2.23
), then

(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot)):=(U(\cdot,x^{s,\zeta}(\cdot)),\nabla U(\cdot,x^{s,\zeta}(\cdot))h^{\top}(\cdot))

(6.19)

is the solution to the BSDE in (
2.22
).

From (
2.23
), it is evident that

\displaystyle U(T,x^{s,\zeta}(T))=F_{l,N}(x^{s,\zeta}(T)).

(6.20)

Applying the Itô formula to

, and using (
2.23

\displaystyle dU(t,x^{s,\zeta}(t))=\Big(-A^{\top}U(t,x^{s,\zeta}(t))-\sum_{i=1}^{d}C_{i}^{\top}\nabla U(t,x^{s,\zeta}(t))h^{\top}(t)f_{i}\Big)dt+\nabla U(t,x^{s,\zeta}(t))h^{\top}(t)dw(t).

Combining this with (
6.20

Next, we prove uniqueness. Suppose that (

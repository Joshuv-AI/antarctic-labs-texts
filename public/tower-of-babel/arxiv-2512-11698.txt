Abstract
. Let Ω ⊂ ℂ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} , with n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , be a smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain satisfying the symmetric eigenvalue comparability condition D ⁡ ( q 0 ) D(q_{0}) for some 1 ≤ q 0 ≤ n − 2 1\leq q_{0}\leq n-2 . We show that if the Diederich-Fornæss index of Ω \Omega is one, then the complex Green operator G q G_{q} , associated with Ω \Omega , is globally regular for q q in the range min ⁡ { q 0 , n − 1 − q 0 } ≤ q ≤ max ⁡ { q 0 , n − 1 − q 0 } \min\{q_{0},\,n-1-q_{0}\}\leq q\leq\max\{q_{0},\,n-1-q_{0}\} .



1.
Introduction

The study of regularity estimates for the
\overline{\partial}
–Neumann and complex Green operators in terms of (weak) plurisubharmonicity properties of a defining function goes back to
[
6
,
7
]
, where Boas and the second author established Sobolev estimates for these operators when the domain admits a defining function that is plurisubharmonic at points of the boundary. Kohn (
[
23
]
) then initiated a quantitative study of estimates in terms of the Diederich–Fornæss index. The latter can be viewed as a measure of how close a domain is to having a defining function that is plurisubharmonic at the boundary (albeit with caution: index one does not imply the existence of such a defining function). These ideas were developed further by Harrington (
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
), Pinton–Zampieri (
[
31
]
), and Liu (
[
25
]
). Most recently, Liu and the second author (
[
28
]
) showed that if
1\leq q_{0}\leq n-1
and the
q_{0}
-sums of the eigenvalues of the Levi form of a smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
are comparable, then Diederich–Fornæss index one implies global regularity of the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann operators
N_{q}
and the Bergman projections
P_{q-1}
for
q_{0}\leq q\leq n
. All these results point towards the conjecture that index one should imply regularity. We refer the reader to Section 1 of
[
36
]
for further information on these developments.

Here we prove an analogue of
[
28
]
for the complex Green operators
G_{q}
and the Szegö projections
S_{q-1}
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, be a smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain. Let
q_{0}
be an integer such that
1\leq q_{0}\leq n-2
and assume that
\Omega
satisfies condition
D(q_{0})
. If
DF(\Omega)=1
, then for each
s\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C_{s}
such that all for
u\in L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)
and
\min\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}\leq q\leq\max\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}
:

(1)

\left\|u\right\|_{s}\leq C_{s}(\left\|\bar{\partial}_{b}u\right\|_{s}+\left\|\bar{\partial}_{b}^{\star}u\right\|_{s})
,

(2)

\left\|S_{q}u\right\|_{s}\leq C_{s}\left\|u\right\|_{s}
, and

(3)

\left\|G_{q}u\right\|_{s}\leq C_{s}\left\|u\right\|_{s}
.

(1) and (2) also hold for
q=\min\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}-1
and
q=\max\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}+1
if in (1),
u\perp\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})
when
q=\min\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}-1
, and
u\perp\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})
when
q=\max\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}+1
, and if in (2),
S_{n-1}
is the projection onto
\im(\overline{\partial}_{b})=\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})^{\perp}
.

DF(\Omega)
refers to the Diederich–Fornæss index of
\Omega
, and
D(q_{0})
is a comparability condition bearing on sums of
q_{0}
eigenvalues of the Levi form: precise definitions for both, as well as for the related condition
\widetilde{D}(q)
, are in Section
2
. Observe that
\min\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}
or
\max\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}
equals
q_{0}
, depending on whether
q_{0}\leq(n-1)/2
or
q_{0}\geq(n-1)/2
, respectively. In particular, the range of allowable
q
’s is symmetric about
(n-1)/2
, in line with the expectation that estimates on the boundary should hold at symmetric form levels simultaneously (see the discussion around (
5.40
)). The estimates in Theorem
1
are not independent: (3) holds for
(0,q)
–forms if and only if (2) holds at levels
(q-1)
,
q
, and
(q+1)
(
[
18
]
), and (1) holds if and only if (2) holds (appropriately formulated when
q=0
or
q=(n-1)
,
[
35
]
).

We single out for emphasis the most important case in Theorem
1
, that of comparable eigenvalues. Let
\lambda_{1}(p),\dots,\lambda_{n-1}(p)
denote the eigenvalues of the Levi form of
b\Omega
at
p
, listed with multiplicity. The eigenvalues are said to be comparable if there is a constant
c
such that
\lambda_{j}(p)\leq c\lambda_{k}(p)
, for all
p\in b\Omega
and
1\leq j,k\leq(n-1)
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, be a smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain whose Levi eigenvalues are comparable. Then, if
DF(\Omega)=1
, the conclusions of Theorem
1
hold for
u\in L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)
,
0\leq q\leq(n-1)
.

Corollary
1
follows from Theorem
1
because the comparable eigenvalues condition implies
D(1)
, so
q_{0}=1
; see the discussion of
D(q)
in section
2
.

While
D(q)
is indispensable in our current proof of Theorem
1
, whether it is really needed is doubtful; the conjecture mentioned above that index one should imply regularity is plausible also for the complex Green operator. For example, on (smooth bounded) convex domains, the estimates (1)–(3) in Theorem
1
always hold (by
[
7
]
), while
D(q)
is rather restrictive. Indeed, a convex domain that satisfies
D(q)
also satisfies
\widetilde{D}(q)
, hence satisfies maximal estimates on the interior (
[
2
,
11
]
). As a result, its boundary must not contain analytic varieties of dimension
q
, except possibly ones that are contained in an
(n-1)
–dimensional subvariety (
[
8
]
, Corollary 1).

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
gives the needed definitions and some preliminary background material. In section
3
, we discuss D’Angelo forms and their connections to the Diederich–Fornæss index and to commutators with
\overline{\partial}_{b}
and
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
. We also recall an important estimate from
[
28
]
, which we then use in section
4
to establish Proposition
1
, for our purposes the principal consequence of having DF–index one. This

–type estimate is what ultimately lets us control commutators with
\overline{\partial}_{b}
and
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
. We use it in section
5
in combination with ideas from
[

,

,
35

to prove Theorem
1
. Section
6
states a Kohn–Morrey–Hörmander type estimate for the boundary, a special case of an estimate in
[
19
]
, simplified for what we need.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

This section serves to recall some basic definitions and set up notation. Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with the CR–structure on
b\Omega
induced by the ambient space. That is,
T^{1,0}(b\Omega)
is the bundle of vectors
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(z)\partial/\partial z_{j}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\partial\rho/\partial z_{j}=0
for some defining function
\rho
(hence for any defining function). For
0\leq q\leq(n-1)
, denote by
\overline{\partial}_{b}:L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}_{(0,q+1)}(b\Omega)
the usual (extrinsically defined) tangential Cauchy–Riemann operator, and by
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
its Hilbert space adjoint. These operators have closed range, and there is an associated Hodge theory (
[
32
,
5
,
22
,
9
,
3
]
):

(2.1)

L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)=\underbrace{\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})}_{\im(\overline{\partial}_{b})\;q=(n-1)}\oplus\underbrace{\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})}_{\im(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})\;q=0}\;;\;1\leq q\leq(n-2)\;.

The underbraces indicate the modifications for the respective values of
q
. In particular,

(2.2)

\|u\|^{2}\leq C(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}u\|^{2})\;;\;u\in\dom(\overline{\partial}_{b})\cap\dom(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})\;,

with
u\in\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})^{\perp}
when
q=0
, and
u\in\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})^{\perp}
when
q=(n-1)
.

For
1\leq q\leq(n-2)
, the operator
\Box_{b,q}:=\overline{\partial}_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}+\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}\overline{\partial}_{b}
is selfadjoint, injective, and onto, hence has a bounded inverse
G_{q}
. This is the complex Green operator. We then have for
u\in L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)
,
1\leq q\leq(n-2)
:

(2.3)

u=\overline{\partial}_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}G_{q}u+\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}\overline{\partial}G_{q}u\;.

If
u
is
\overline{\partial}_{b}
–closed, (
2.3
) implies that
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}\overline{\partial}G_{q}u
is also
\overline{\partial}_{b}
–closed; since it is also orthogonal to
\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})
, it must vanish, and
u=\overline{\partial}_{b}(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}G_{q}u)
. That is,
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}G_{q}
is a solution operator for
\overline{\partial}_{b}
. It gives the unique solution of minimal
L^{2}
–norm (also referred to as the canonical or Kohn solution). The
G_{q}
commute with
\overline{\partial}_{b}
and
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
(with appropriate form levels). The Szegö projection
S_{q}
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)
onto
\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})
when
0\leq q\leq(n-2)
, and onto
\im(\overline{\partial}_{b})=\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})^{\perp}
when
q=(n-1)
. We then have
S_{q}=\overline{\partial}_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}G_{q}
for
1\leq q\leq(n-2)
, and
S_{q}=I-\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}G_{q+1}\overline{\partial}_{b}\;;\;0\leq q\leq(n-3)
, where
I
is the identity.

Our proof of Theorem
1
uses maximal estimates for the boundary complex, i.e.,

(2.4)

\left\|Yu\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|\bar{\partial}_{b}u\right\|^{2}_{k}+\left\|\bar{\partial}_{b}^{\star}u\right\|^{2}_{k}\ +\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{k}\right)\;,

where
Y
is a complex tangential vector field. This estimate is stronger than the standard estimate available for complex tangential derivatives (
[
7
]
, Lemma 1):

(2.5)

\left\|Yu\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|\bar{\partial}_{b}u\right\|^{2}_{k}+\left\|\bar{\partial}_{b}^{\star}u\right\|^{2}_{k}+\left\|u\right\|_{k}\left\|u\right\|_{k+1}\right)\;.

The necessary and sufficient condition for the maximal estimates (
2.4
) to hold at the level of
(0,q)
–forms (
1\leq q\leq(n-2)
) is condition
D(q)
. For
1\leq q\leq n-1
, let
\sigma_{q}(p)
denote any of the
{{n-1}\choose{q}}
sums
q
the eigenvalues and let
\tau(p)=\lambda_{1}(p)+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1}(p)
denote the trace of the Levi form. Following
[
20
]
, we say that condition
D(q)
holds (for
1\leq q\leq n-2
), if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

(2.6)

\epsilon\tau(p)\leq\sigma_{q}(p)\leq(1-\epsilon)\tau(p),

for all
p\in b\Omega
and any
q
-sum
\sigma_{q}
of the eigenvalues of the Levi form. It was shown in
[
12
]
for
q=1
and in
[
20
]
for general
q
that condition
D(q)
is a sufficient condition for maximal estimates for
\bar{\partial}_{b}\oplus\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
to hold at form level
(0,q)
. According to
[
12
]
, Remarque 2 on page 633, it is also necessary. For
1\leq q_{0}\leq n-2
, condition
D(q_{0})
implies condition
D(q)
for
\min\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}<q<\max\{q_{0},n-1-q_{0}\}
(see Proposition A.3 in
[
20
]
). For this reason, the condition is sometimes referred to as symmetric condition
D(q)
, in contrast to the nonsymmetric condition
\widetilde{D}(q)
:
\varepsilon\tau\leq\sigma_{q}
, which is the necessary and sufficient condition for maximal estimates on the interior (
[
11
,
2
]
). Condition
D(1)
never holds in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, while
\widetilde{D}(1)
always does. On the other hand, when
n\geq 3
, it is easy to see that
D(1)
and
\widetilde{D}(1)
are equivalent (and that
D(q)
and
\widetilde{D}(q)
are not when
q>1
). In particular, in the most important case, that of comparable eigenvalues,
D(1),\cdots,D(n-2)
are all satisfied. It is pointed out in
[
20
]
that in the pseudoconvex case, the classical condition
Y(q)
is equivalent to
0<\sigma_{q}<\tau
, uniformly over the boundary. By a continuity argument, this latter condition implies
D(q)
, which may thus be seen as a generalization of
Y(q)
(for pseudoconvex domains).

In 1977, Diederich and Fornæss proved that for every smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there is
\eta>0
and a defining function
\rho_{\eta}
such that
-(-\rho_{\eta})^{\eta}
is plurisubharmonic on
\Omega
(near the boundary; having this requirement only near the boundary does not affect the definition of the index, see
[
16
]
, Lemma 2.3). It is not hard to see that the set of such
\eta
is an interval bounded below by zero. The supremum over such
\eta
,
0<\eta\leq 1
, is called the Diederich-Fornæss index of
\Omega
; we denote it by
DF(\Omega)
. Because
DF(\Omega)
is a supremum, index one does not imply the existence of a defining function that is plurisubharmonic at the boundary. The reader is referred to Section 2.3 in
[
36
]
for further discussion of the index. It is noteworthy that in all cases where the index is known to be one, it is also known that regularity holds for both the
\overline{\partial}
–Neumann operator and the complex Green operator.

3.
DF–index, commutators, and D’Angelo forms

The link between the index and (commutator) estimates is provided by D’Angelo forms. Their role in estimating commutators is well known. Let
\rho
be a defining function for
\Omega
. Set
L_{n,\rho}=(1/|\partial\rho|^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial\rho/\partial\overline{z_{j}})\partial/\partial z_{j}
(so that
L_{n,\rho}\rho\equiv 1
),
T_{\rho}=L_{n,\rho}-\overline{L_{n,\rho}}
, and
\sigma_{\rho}=(1/2)(\partial\rho-\overline{\partial}\rho)
(so that
\sigma_{\rho}T_{\rho}\equiv 1
). Set

(3.1)

\alpha_{\rho}=-\mathcal{L}_{T_{\rho}}\sigma_{\rho}\;,

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lie derivative. Then for
L\in T^{1,0}(b\Omega)
,
\alpha_{\rho}(L)=\sigma_{\rho}([T_{\rho},L])
and
\alpha_{\rho}(\overline{L})=\sigma_{\rho}([T_{\rho},\overline{L}])
(note that
\sigma_{\rho}(T_{\rho})\equiv 1
). The form
\alpha_{\rho}
is called the D’Angelo form associated to
\rho
. Since both
T_{\rho}
and
\sigma_{\rho}
are purely imaginary,
\alpha_{\rho}
is real. We will need to know how
\alpha_{\rho}
changes when the defining function changes. If
e^{h}\rho
is another defining function, a computation gives

(3.2)

\alpha_{e^{h}\rho}(X)=\alpha_{\rho}(X)+dh(X)\;;\;X\in T^{1,0}(b\Omega)\oplus T^{0,1}(b\Omega)\;;

see
[
34
]
, Section 5.9,
[
10
]
, Section 4, and
[
36
]
, section 2.2 for more information on D’Angelo forms. It will be important later that various estimates can be done with constants that are uniform over the boundaries of approximating subdomains, that is over
b\Omega_{\delta}
, where
\Omega_{\delta}=\{z\in\Omega\,|\,\rho(z)+\delta<0\}
, and
0<\delta<\delta_{\rho}
. There are two things to note in this context. First,
L_{n,\rho}
,
T_{\rho}
,
\sigma_{\rho}
and
\alpha_{\rho}
are defined in a one sided neighborhood of
b\Omega
. Second, at points of
b\Omega_{\delta}
, they agree with the corresponding notions on
b\Omega_{\delta}
(with
\rho+\delta
as defining function, then
\nabla(\rho)=\nabla(\rho+\delta)
).

When computing
\overline{\partial}_{b}
and
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
in a special boundary chart, the derivatives that occur are are of the form
\overline{L}
or
L
, with
L\in T^{1,0}(b\Omega)
. So
\alpha_{\rho}(L)=\sigma_{\rho}([T_{\rho},L])
and
\alpha_{\rho}(\overline{L})=\sigma_{\rho}([T_{\rho},\overline{L}])
pick out the
T_{\rho}
–component (the bad direction) of the commutators with
T_{\rho}
. Denote by
L_{1},\cdots,L_{n}
a special boundary frame, as usual, with dual frame
\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n}
. If
v=\sum^{{}^{\prime}}_{|J|=q}v_{J}\overline{\omega_{J}}
is a
(0,q)
–form supported in this chart, then letting
T_{\rho}
act coefficientwise in this chart and also computing
\overline{\partial}_{b}
and
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}
in this chart gives the following two commutator formulas when
k=1
:

(3.3)

[\bar{\partial}_{b},(T_{\rho})^{k}]v=-k\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{J}\alpha^{\rho}(\overline{L}_{j})((T_{\rho})^{k}v_{J})(\overline{\omega_{j}}\wedge\overline{\omega_{J}})+A_{h}(v)+B_{h}(v)

and for
q\geq 1
,

(3.4)

[\bar{\partial}_{b}^{\star},(T_{\rho})^{k}]v=k\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{S}\alpha^{\rho}(L_{j})((T_{\rho})^{k}v_{jS})\overline{\omega_{S}}+\tilde{A}_{h}(v)+\tilde{B}_{h}(v),

where the terms
A_{h}(v)
and
\tilde{A}_{h}(v)
consist of terms of order
k
with at least one of the derivatives being complex tangential, and the terms
B_{h}(v)
and
\tilde{B}_{h}(v)
consist of terms of order at most
(k-1)
. Induction on
k
then gives the general case. These arguments are completely analogous to the ones that give the corresponding formulas for the commutators with
\overline{\partial}
and
\overline{\partial}^{*}
(
[
17
]
, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2;
[
28
]
, Lemmas 3, 4; compare also Proposition 3.7 in
[
27
]
).

While the significance of D’Angelo forms for commutator estimates has been known for a long time, their relevance for the Diederich–Fornæss index is a recent discovery, due to Liu (
[
26
]
) and Yum (
[
38
]
).
Consider
\eta\in(0,1)
such that there exists a defining function
\rho_{\eta}
whose D’Angelo form
\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}
satisfies

(3.5)

|\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L)(p)|^{2}\leq 2\frac{(1-\eta)}{\eta}\overline{\partial}\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L,\overline{L})(p)\;;\;L\in\mathcal{N}_{p}\;,\;p\in b\Omega\;,

where
\mathcal{N}_{p}
denotes the null space of the Levi form at
p\in b\Omega
. Then

(3.6)

DF(\Omega)=\sup\{\eta\in(0,1)\,|\,\exists\;\text{a defining function}\;\rho_{\eta}\;\text{such that}\;\eqref{alpha-index}\;\text{holds}\}\;;

see
[
26
]
, Theorem 2.10,
[
38
]
, Theorem 1.1;
further discussion is in
[
36
]
, Section 4. Note that the set of
\eta
with a defining function
\rho_{\eta}
such that (
3.5
) holds is also an interval (because
\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}
is a decreasing function). In
[
28
]
, the authors show the following. Let
0<\eta<DF(\Omega)
, with associated defining function
\rho_{\eta}=e^{h_{\eta}}\rho
such that (
3.5
) holds. There is a constant
C
independent of
\eta
and a constant
M_{\eta}
such that

(3.7)

|\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L)|^{2}\leq C(1-\eta)\left(\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial^{2}(-h_{\eta})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}L_{j}\overline{L_{k}}+2|L|^{2}\right)\\
+M_{\eta}\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}L_{j}\overline{L_{k}}\;\;;\;L=\sum_{j}L_{j}\partial/\partial z_{j}\in T^{1,0}(b\Omega)\,.

Moreover, there is
\delta_{\eta}>0
such that this estimate holds uniformly on the level sets
\{\rho_{\eta}=-\delta\}
for
\delta<\delta_{\eta}
. Roughly speaking, the Hessian of
-h_{\eta}
appears because
\overline{\partial}\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}
in (
3.5
) equals
\overline{\partial}(dh_{\eta}+\alpha_{\rho})
(some care is needed though; see
[
10
]
, Section 4.3, especially Lemmas 4.6 and 4.7, for details), and the
M_{\eta}
–term is needed for when
L(p)\notin\mathcal{N}_{p}
(see
[
28
]
, section 3). In order to obtain an estimate for forms from (
3.7
), we define auxiliary vector fields associated to forms as follows. For a
(0,q)
–form
u=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|K|=q}u_{K}d\overline{z_{K}}
and an increasing
(q-1)
–tuple
J
, set
L^{J}_{u}=\sum_{j}u_{jJ}\partial/\partial z_{j}
(when
q=1
, we understand
J
to be the empty tuple, and
L_{u}=\sum_{j}u_{j}\partial/\partial z_{j}
). When
u
is a form on the boundary, then for all
J
,
L^{J}_{u}\rho=\sum_{j}u_{jJ}(\partial\rho/\partial z_{j})=0
, so that (
3.7
) does apply to
L^{J}_{u}
. Note that
L_{u}^{J}
is not invariant under a change of frame from Euclidean to a special boundary frame (unless
q=1
). However, the quantity that will matter, namely
\sum^{{}^{\prime}}_{J}\int_{b\Omega}|\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L_{u}^{J})|^{2}
(the quantity on the left hand side of (
3.8
) below) is, up to a constant factor. The reason is that each
L_{u}^{J}
is a linear combination, with smooth coefficients independent of
u
, of the corresponding vectors in the other frame (this observation from
[
28
]
, page 5417, is based on the transformation formulas between the two orthonormal frames). Integrating (
3.7
) for
L_{u}^{J}
over the boundary and summing over
J
gives

(3.8)

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{J}\int_{b\Omega}|\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L^{J}_{u})|^{2}\leq C(1-\eta)\left(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{J}\int_{b\Omega}\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial^{2}(-h_{\eta})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}u_{jJ}\overline{u_{kJ}}+2\|u\|^{2}\right)\\
+M_{\eta}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{J}\int_{b\Omega}\sum_{j,k}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}u_{jJ}\overline{u_{kJ}}\;\;.

Moreover, there is
\delta_{\eta}
so that this estimate holds uniformly over the level sets
\rho_{\eta}=-\delta
for
0\leq\delta<\delta_{\eta}
.
This estimate is the starting point, in the next section, for deriving the crucial
L^{2}
–type estimate needed in the proof of Theorem
1
.

4.
The main estimate

The estimate in Proposition
1
is, for our purposes, the principal consequence of having DF–index 1.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain with
DF(\Omega)=1
, and satisfying condition
D(q)
for some
1\leq q\leq n-2
. There are constants
C
and
\eta_{0}
,
0<\eta_{0}<1
, with the following property. For
\eta_{0}<\eta<1
, there are constants
C_{\eta}
and
\delta_{\eta}>0
such that for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)
, we have

(4.1)

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|J|=q-1}\int_{b\Omega}\left|\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L_{u}^{J})\right|^{2}\leq C(1-\eta)\left(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}u\|^{2}\right)+C_{\eta}\|u\|_{-1}^{2}\,,

and, moreover, this estimate holds on the level sets
\{\rho_{\eta}=-\delta\}
for
\delta<\delta_{\eta}
with the same constants.

Proof.

The uniformity part will follow by keeping track of the constants and, where partitions of unity over
b\Omega
and boundary charts are used, using instead partitions of unity and charts over a neighborhood of
b\Omega
.

It suffices to prove the proposition for forms supported in suitable small patches of the boundary (independent of
\eta
), the general case then follows via a partition of unity. For
u
supported in
a special boundary chart as in Proposition
2
in section
6
, we look at the term involving the Hessian of
-h_{\eta}
on the right hand side of (
3.8
). Proposition
2
, with
\varphi=-h_{\eta}
, and
(e^{-h_{\eta}}/2)u
in place of
u
, gives

(4.2)

\begin{split}\|\overline{L}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}&+2\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left(\left(T+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial h_{\eta}}{\partial\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Th_{\eta}\right)u_{jK}\right)\overline{u_{kK}}\\
&\qquad+2\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}\dfrac{\partial^{2}(-h_{\eta})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\widetilde{u_{jK}}\overline{\widetilde{u_{kK}}}+\mathcal{O}(\left\|u\right\|^{2})\\
&\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}_{b}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,-h_{\eta}}^{\star}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}\;;\end{split}

here
\overline{L}(\cdot)
denotes the vector of all
\overline{L_{j}}
–derivatives,
u_{j}
are the components of
u
in the special boundary chart,
\widetilde{u_{J}}
denotes the Euclidean components of
u
,
c_{jk}
are the coefficients of the Levi form in the basis given by the boundary frame, and the constant in the
\mathcal{O}(\|u\|^{2})
is independent of
\eta
. Since
T
is purely imaginary and
h_{\eta}
is real, we get
Th_{\eta}
is purely imaginary and

(4.3)

\text{Re}\sum_{j,k}c_{jk}(Th_{\eta})u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}=\text{Re}\left((Th_{\eta})\sum_{j,k}c_{jk}u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}\right)=0\;,

since pseudoconvexity implies that the summation above results in a (non-negative) real number. We rewrite (
4.2
) as

(4.4)

\begin{split}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}\dfrac{\partial^{2}(-h_{\eta})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\widetilde{u_{jK}}\overline{\widetilde{u_{kK}}}&\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}_{b}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,-h_{\eta}}^{\star}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}\\
&\quad-2\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\big((T+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial h_{\eta}}{\partial\nu})u_{jK}\big)\overline{u_{kK}}+\mathcal{O}(\left\|u\right\|^{2}).\end{split}

We have used that
\|\overline{L}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}\geq 0
. We now apply the estimate (
4.4
) to (
3.8
) to obtain

(4.5)

\begin{split}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\int_{b\Omega}\left|\alpha_{\eta}(L_{u}^{K})\right|^{2}&\lesssim(1-\eta)\Big(\|\bar{\partial}_{b}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,-h_{\eta}}^{\star}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}\\
&\qquad-2\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\big((T+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial h_{\eta}}{\partial\nu})u_{jK}\big)\overline{u_{kK}}+\mathcal{O}(\left\|u\right\|^{2})\Big)\\
&\qquad\qquad+M_{\eta}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}.\end{split}

Choose
\tilde{M}_{\eta}
big enough so that
\tilde{M}_{\eta}\geq M_{\eta}/(1-\eta)-(1/2)(\partial h_{\eta}/\partial\nu)
. Then (

) becomes

(4.6)

\begin{split}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\int_{b\Omega}\left|\alpha_{\eta}(L_{u}^{K})\right|^{2}&\lesssim(1-\eta)\bigg(\|\bar{\partial}_{b}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,-h_{\eta}}^{\star}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}\\
&\qquad-2\text{Re}\big(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{jk}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\big((T-\tilde{M}_{\eta})u_{jK}\big)\overline{u_{kK}}\,\big)+\mathcal{O}(\left\|u\right\|^{2})\bigg)\end{split}

(since the Levi from is positive semidefinite).

\chi^{+}
and
\chi^{-}
so that
|\tau|
is sufficiently big. Let
\tilde{M}_{\eta}>0
be the constant from (

). Denote be

\chi^{+,\eta}=\chi^{+}\sigma(\tau-3\tilde{M}_{\eta})\;,\;\chi^{-,\eta}=\chi^{-}\sigma(-\tau-3\tilde{M}_{\eta})\;,\;\text{and}\;\chi^{0,\eta}=1-\chi^{+,\eta}-\chi^{-,\eta}\;.

Then
\chi^{+,\eta}
and
\chi^{-,\eta}
are supported in

and

\chi^{0,\eta}
is supported in an elliptic region that also depends on
\eta
. It will be important later that these modified symbols differ from the standard ones by compactly supported terms (albeit with support depending on
\eta

(0,q)

\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u=\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u),\quad\mathcal{P}^{-,\eta}u=\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{-,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u),\quad\mathcal{P}^{0,\eta}u=\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{0,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u),

)
can be thought of as forms on the whole boundary. For

(4.9)

u=\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u+\mathcal{P}^{-,\eta}u+\mathcal{P}^{0,\eta}u\;.

It now suffices to prove that

We start with
\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u
and first estimate the Re–term in the second line of (
4.6
) for
\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u
from below. We have

(4.10)

\begin{split}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}&\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left((T-\tilde{M}_{\eta})(\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u)_{jK}\right)\overline{(\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u)_{kK}}\\
=&\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left((T-\tilde{M}_{\eta})(\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}))\right)\overline{\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK})}.\end{split}

Commuting
\chi

, we see that the right hand side in (

) equals

(4.11)

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\chi\left((T-\tilde{M}_{\eta})({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}))\right)\overline{(\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK}))}\\
+\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left([T,\chi]({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}))\right)\overline{(\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK}))}.

Using the fact that
[T,\chi]
is of order zero and the Plancherel theorem, the last term in (
4.11
) can be estimated by
\|u\|^{2}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}u\|^{2}
, with a constant that does not depend on
\eta
. Now consider the first term in (
4.11
). The following calculations are similar to those in Lemma 2.5 in
[
24
]
. Denote by
\tilde{\chi}^{+}
a smooth function with values in

that is supported in
\{\tau\geq\|\xi\|/3\}
, and that is identically one in a neighborhood of the support of
\chi^{+}
(constructed analogously to
\chi^{+}
, with a suitable function
\tilde{g}
). Then set
\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}=\tilde{\chi}^{+}\sigma(\tau-3\tilde{M}_{\eta}+1)

\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}
is identically one on the support of
\chi^{+,\eta}
and on the support of
\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}
, we have
\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta}\geq 2\tilde{M}_{\eta}\geq 1
(we may assume that
\tilde{M}_{\eta}\geq 1/2
). Denote by

the pseudodifferential operator of order

whose symbol is

(4.12)

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}}\mu c_{jk}\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left((\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})(\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta})^{2}\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}\right)\overline{(\chi{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK}))}\\

where

denotes the density of the the surface measure on

. We have used that the symbol of

is independent of the
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n-2},t)
variables. Taking real parts and using that

is selfadjoint, we have

(4.13)

\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}}\mu\chi^{2}c_{jk}(R^{\eta})^{2}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}\right)\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK})}\\
=\text{Re}\bigg(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}}[\mu\chi^{2}c_{jk},R^{\eta}]R^{\eta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}\right)\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK})}\\
+\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}}\mu\chi^{2}c_{jk}R^{\eta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}\right)\overline{R^{\eta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK})}\;\bigg)\\
\geq\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}}[\mu\chi^{2}c_{jk},R^{\eta}]R^{\eta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}\right)\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK})}.

For the last inequality we have used pseudoconvexity of
\Omega
and

to see that the term in the third line of (
4.13
) is nonnegative. We need a lower bound on the right hand side of (

Since
\chi R^{\eta}
is of order

, the operator
[\mu c_{jk}\chi_{s},\chi R^{\eta}]

[

–estimate depends on

, and further analysis is required. Modulo a symbol in

, the symbol of
[\mu c_{jk}\chi^{2},R^{\eta}]
is

(4.14)

\begin{split}\sum_{|\alpha|=1}&\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\partial^{\alpha}_{\tau,\xi}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)\,\partial^{\alpha}_{t,x}\left((\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad-\partial^{\alpha}_{\tau,\xi}\left((\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\right)\partial^{\alpha}_{t,x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)\bigg),\end{split}

where
\alpha
is a multi-index of order

and

denotes partial derivatives. Since
\partial^{\alpha}_{\tau,\xi}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)=0
, (
4.14
) becomes

(4.15)

-\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\dfrac{\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)}{2(\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}}+(\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}\partial_{\tau,\xi}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\right)\partial_{x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi).

So modulo a symbol in
S^{-1}
, the symbol of
[\mu c_{jk}\chi^{2},R^{\eta}]R^{\eta}
is

(4.16)

\begin{split}&-\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\dfrac{\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)}{2(\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}}+(\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}\partial_{\tau,\xi}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\right)\partial_{t,x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)(\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})^{1/2}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\\
&=-\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi))^{2}\partial_{t,x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)+(\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\partial_{\tau,\xi}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\partial_{t,x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)\right).\end{split}

In view of the uniform bonds on
|\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)|^{2}
and on

, the contribution to

from the first term is uniformly (with respect to

(4.17)

\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big((\tau-\tilde{M}_{\eta})\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\partial_{\tau,\xi}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\partial_{t,x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)\mathcal{F}u\big)\\
=(T-\tilde{M}_{\eta})\partial_{t,x}(\mu c_{jk}\chi)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\partial_{\tau,\xi}\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)\mathcal{F}u\big)\;.

To estimate the

–norm of the right hand side of (

–norm of
(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\tilde{M}_{\eta})\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\tilde{\chi}^{+,\eta}(\tau,\xi)

(4.20)

\left|\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}[\mu\chi^{2}c_{jk},R^{\eta}]R^{\eta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{jK}\right)\overline{{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi^{+,\eta}{\mathcal{F}}u_{kK})}\right|\\

We have also used that for forms supported in

, the norms on

Combining (

-(1-\eta)2\text{Re}\left(\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{jk}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left((T-\tilde{M}_{\eta})\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u_{jK}\right)\overline{\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u_{kK}}\right)\\

and thus, with (

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{J}|\alpha_{\rho_{\eta}}(L^{J}_{\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u})|^{2}\\
\lesssim(1-\eta)\left(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b,-h_{\eta}}^{*}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}\mathcal{P}^{+,\eta}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}u\|^{2}\right)\\
+C_{\eta}\|u\|_{-1}^{2}\;.\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

We will deal with the weighted terms later and for now proceed to
\mathcal{P}^{-,\eta}u
.

In order to deal with

, we first modify (

). But rather than keeping

and integrating this term by parts (compare

on both sides (the reason for the factor

2\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}\dfrac{\partial^{2}(-h_{\eta})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\widetilde{u_{jK}}\overline{\widetilde{u_{kK}}}\\
+2\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}(T+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial h_{\eta}}{\partial\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Th_{\eta})u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}+3q\left\|\overline{L}u\right\|^{2}\\
\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}_{b}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,-h_{\eta}}^{\star}(e^{-h_{\eta}/2}u)\|_{-h_{\eta}}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\left\|u\right\|^{2})+3q\left\|\overline{L}u\right\|^{2}.

On the left hand side, we integrate

by parts as usual. That is

\left\|\overline{L}u\right\|^{2}=-\sum_{j}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{J}\int_{b\Omega}(c_{jj}Tu_{J})\overline{u_{J}}+\left\|Lu\right\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\left\|{L}u\right\|\left\|u\right\|)+\mathcal{O}(\left\|\overline{L}u\right\|\left\|u\right\|)\;;

\|u\|_{k}^{2}\leq C_{\eta}\left(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}u\|_{k}^{2}+\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}\right)\;.

(the term

uniformly on

Estimate (

). For
\eta>\eta_{1}
, the estimates from the previous part hold for
(0,q+1)
–forms. In particular, in view of (
5.22
),

\|v\|_{k}^{2}\leq C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}v\|_{k}^{2}=C_{\eta}\|u\|_{k}^{2}\;.

The estimates (
5.2
) and (

) still hold, and we again only have to estimate

. We have

\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}=\sum_{s}\big(\chi_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}u,T_{\eta}^{k}u\big)=\sum_{s}\big(T_{\eta}^{k}(\chi_{s}u),T_{\eta}^{k}u\big)+\mathcal{O}_{\eta}(\|u\|_{k-1}\|u\|_{k})\;.

Because we will have to use estimate (

acting in special boundary charts, we switch to that setup. If on the right hand side of (

\big(T_{\eta}^{k}(\chi_{s}u),T_{\eta}^{k}u\big)=\big(\chi_{s}u,T_{\eta}^{2k}u\big)+\mathcal{O}_{\eta}(\|u\|_{k-1}\|u\|_{k})\;.

The error terms (or divergence terms) in the

. In view of the duality between

\big(\chi_{s}u,T_{\eta}^{2k}\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}v\big)=\big(\chi_{s}u,\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}T_{\eta}^{2k}v\big)-\big(\chi_{s}u,[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{2k}]v\big)\\

Starting with (

(5.27)

\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}\lesssim\left|\sum_{s}\left(\big(\overline{\partial}_{b}\chi_{s}\wedge u,T_{\eta}^{2k}v\big)+\big(\chi_{s}\overline{\partial}_{b}u,T_{\eta}^{2k}v\big)-\big(\chi_{s}u,[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{2k}]v\big)\right)\right|\\
+C_{\eta}\|u\|_{k-1}\|u\|_{k}

Because the difference between between

acting in a special boundary chart to the globally defined term where

acts in Euclidean coordinates is of order

, and

, the contribution to the right hand side of (

\left|\sum_{s}\big(\chi_{s}\overline{\partial}_{b}u,T_{\eta}^{2k}v\big)\right|\lesssim\sum_{s}\|\chi_{s}\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}\|T_{\eta}^{2k}v\|_{-k}\leq C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}\|v\|_{k}\;;

again, the norms are first on
U_{s}\cap b\Omega_{\eta,\delta}
, and then on all of
b\Omega_{\eta,\delta}
. For the last term on the right hand side of (
5.27
), note that

(5.29)

[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{2k}]=[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]T_{\eta}^{k}+T_{\eta}^{k}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]=2T_{\eta}^{k}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]-\big[[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}],T_{\eta}^{k}\big]\;.

The last term here is of order
(2k-1)
. Therefore,

(5.30)

\big|\big(\chi_{s}u,[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{2k}]v\big)\big|\lesssim\big|\big(\chi_{s}u,T_{\eta}^{k}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\big)\big|+C_{\eta}\|u\|_{k}\|v\|_{k-1}\\
\lesssim\big|\big(T_{\eta}^{k}\chi_{s}u,[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\big)\big|+C_{\eta}\big(\|u\|_{k-1}\|[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\|+\|u\|_{k}\|v\|_{k-1}\big)\\
\lesssim\big|\big(T_{\eta}^{k}u,\chi_{s}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\big)\big|+C_{\eta}\big(\|u\|_{k-1}\|v\|_{k}+\|u\|_{k}\|v\|_{k-1}\big)\;.

The integration by parts is handled as in (
5.25
). To estimate the main term on the right hand side, we use (
5.17
) (with
u
replaced by
v
):

(5.31)

\big\|\chi_{s}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\big\|^{2}\lesssim(1-\eta)\big(\|\chi_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}+\|\hat{\chi}_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}v\|^{2}\big)+C_{\eta}\big(\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}+\|v\|_{k-1}^{2}\big)\;.

We have used that
\overline{\partial}_{b}v=0
and
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}v=u
. Therefore, the main term on the right hand side of (
5.30
) can be estimated as follows:

(5.32)

\big|\big(T_{\eta}^{k}u,\chi_{s}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\big)\big|\leq(1-\eta)^{1/2}\|\hat{\chi}_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}+(1-\eta)^{-1/2}\big\|\chi_{s}[\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*},T_{\eta}^{k}]v\big\|^{2}\\
\lesssim(1-\eta)^{1/2}\big(\|\hat{\chi}_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}+\|\hat{\chi}_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}v\|^{2}\big)+C_{\eta}\big(\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}+\|v\|_{k-1}^{2}\big)

(note that
\hat{\chi}_{s}
dominates
\chi_{s}
). Starting with (
5.27
) and summing the estimates for the various terms on the right hand side results in

(5.33)

\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}\lesssim(1-\eta)^{1/2}\sum_{s}\big(\|\hat{\chi}_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}+\|\hat{\chi}_{s}T_{\eta}^{k}v\|^{2}\big)\\
+C_{\eta}\big(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}\|u\|_{k}+\|u\|_{k-1}\|u\|_{k}\big)\;;

we have used (
5.23
) for
\|v\|_{k}^{2}
and
\|v\|_{k-1}^{2}
, and
\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{k}\|u\|_{k-1}
. On the left hand side of (

),
T_{\eta}
acts on Euclidean components, whereas on the right hand side of the first line, it acts on the components in the chart associated with
\chi_{s}
. However, by what is now a familiar observation, when we let it act on Euclidean components also on the right hand side, we make an error that is covered by the last error term in the second line. Accordingly, (
5.33
) also holds with
T_{\eta}
acting on Euclidean components everywhere. Denote by
\tilde{C}_{\eta}
the constant from (
5.4
) for the current value of
\eta
. The error terms satisfy

(5.34)

C_{\eta}\big(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}\|u\|_{k}+\|u\|_{k-1}\|u\|_{k}\big)\leq\frac{(1-\eta)^{1/2}}{\tilde{C}_{\eta}}\|u\|_{k}^{2}+C_{\eta}\big(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}+\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}\big)\\
\lesssim\frac{(1-\eta)^{1/2}}{\tilde{C}_{\eta}}\|u\|_{k}^{2}+C_{\eta}\big(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}+\|u\|^{2}\big)\lesssim\frac{(1-\eta)^{1/2}}{\tilde{C}_{\eta}}\|u\|_{k}^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}\;.

The reader is reminded that constants (in this case

) are allowed to change their values form one occurrence to the next. For the first inequality, we have used the s.c.–l.c. estimate, for the second we have interpolated
\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}\leq((1-\eta)^{1/2}/\tilde{C}_{\eta})\|u\|_{k}^{2}+C_{\eta}\|u\|^{2}

\|u\|^{2}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|^{2}\leq\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}
(

and (
2.2
)). With (

(5.35)

\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}\lesssim(1-\eta)^{1/2}\big(\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}+\|T_{\eta}^{k}v\|^{2}+(1/\tilde{C_{\eta}})\|u\|_{k}^{2}\big)+C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}\;.

From (

), we have

(5.36)

\|T_{\eta}^{k}v\|^{2}\lesssim\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}+C_{\eta}\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}\;,

again because

,
\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}v=u
, and (

), restricting

close enough to one, and absorbing

\|T_{\eta}^{k}u\|^{2}\lesssim(1-\eta)^{1/2}(1/\tilde{C_{\eta}})\|u\|_{k}^{2}+C_{\eta}\big(\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}+\|u\|_{k-1}^{2}\big)\\
\lesssim(1-\eta)^{1/2}(1/\tilde{C_{\eta}})\|u\|_{k}^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}\;;

where we have used the same argument as in (

) for the

–term. Inserting (

) and absorbing the

\|u\|_{k}^{2}\lesssim C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}\;;\;u\in C^{\infty}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega_{\eta,\delta})\;,\;u\perp\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})\;.

If
u
is only assumed in

, but

are well defined for

and

, it is now not hard to check that the arguments above hold when

is only assumed in

. Therefore,there is

,

\eta_{1}<\eta_{2}
such that for

, there is

so that we have the (genuine) estimate

(5.39)

\|u\|_{k}^{2}\lesssim C_{\eta}\|\overline{\partial}_{b}u\|_{k}^{2}\;,\;u\perp\ker(\overline{\partial}_{b})\;,

uniformly on

for

.

The case

is handled analogously, with the commutators

and

from Proposition 1 in

). Note that in our case

\|u\|_{k}^{2}=\|u\wedge dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}\|_{k}^{2}=\|A_{n,q}(u\wedge dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})\|_{k}^{2}\\
\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}_{b}A_{n,q}(u\wedge dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}\|_{k}^{2}=\|A_{n,q-1}\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*}(u\wedge dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})\|_{k}^{2}\\

). Inspection of the arguments in

]
shows that for
\eta
fixed, there is
\delta_{\eta}
such that the above estimates can be done uniformly on
b\Omega_{\eta,\delta}
for
0<\delta<\delta_{\eta}
.

This completes the first part of our argument. We have shown that there is
\eta_{3}<1
such that for
\eta
with
\eta_{3}<\eta<1
, there exists
\delta_{\eta}>0
such that the estimates (1) in Theorem

hold uniformly on

.

1
. These will follow from what we have already shown together with the results of

]
. Specifically, part (2) of Theorem 1.1. there says that estimates (2) in Theorem
1
hold if and only if estimates (1) hold. This means for the pairs

from the previous paragraph, the estimates (2) in Theorem

\|S_{q}u\|_{k}^{2}\lesssim\|F_{t}S_{q,t}F_{t}^{-1}u\|^{2}+\|[\overline{\partial}_{b},F_{t}]S_{q,t}F_{t}^{-1}\|_{k}^{2}\;,

6.
Appendix: Kohn–Morrey–Hörmander on the boundary

We will need the following Kohn–Morrey–Hörmander type estimate for the boundary.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain; let
u=\sum_{|J|=q}^{\prime}u_{J}\overline{\omega}_{J}

\in\dom(\overline{\partial}_{b})\cap\dom(\overline{\partial}_{b}^{*})\subset L^{2}_{(0,q)}(b\Omega)
be supported in a special boundary chart; and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(b\Omega)
. Then

(6.1)

\|\bar{\partial}_{b}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,\varphi}^{\star}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}\gtrsim\;\|\overline{L}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}+2\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left(T-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right)u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}e^{-\varphi}\\
+2\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\widetilde{u_{jK}}\overline{\widetilde{u_{kK}}}e^{-\varphi}+\mathcal{O}(\|u\|_{\varphi}^{2}),

where
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
is the weighted norm with respect to the weight function
e^{-\varphi}
,
\|\overline{L}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}
is the sum
\sum_{j}\sum_{J}^{\prime}\|\overline{L}_{j}u_{J}\|_{\varphi}^{2}
,
K
is a multi-index of length
q-1
,
T=L_{n}-\overline{L}_{n}
,
c_{jk}
are the coefficients of the Levi form in the basis
\{L_{1},\dots,L_{n-1}\}
,
u=\sum_{J}^{\prime}\widetilde{u_{J}}d\bar{z}_{J}
is the local expression of
u
in the Euclidean coordinates, and the constant appearing in the
\mathcal{O}
term is independent of
\varphi
.

Estimate (
6.1
) is a special case of an estimate in
[
19
]
. We thank Andy Raich for pointing us to the second formula from the bottom on page 150. Lets call it (HR). In (HR), take
\lambda^{+}=\varphi
,
m=0
(since
\Omega
is pseudoconvex), and
\phi=u
. Then the left hand side of (HR) gives the left hand side of (
6.1
), the first sum in the fist line of (HR) is absent, and the second sum equals the first term on the right hand side of (
6.1
). The coefficients
h^{+}_{jk}
are defined in Lemma 4 in
[
19
]
as
h^{+}_{jk}=c_{jk}-\delta_{jk}\frac{1}{q}\sum_{l=1}^{m}c_{ll}=c_{jk}
(because
m=0
), where
c_{jk}
are the coefficients of the Levi form; i.e. they are the same as in (
6.1
). Next,
\Gamma^{\lambda^{+}}_{jk}=\Theta^{\lambda^{+}}_{jk}
by the formula immediately preceding (HR) (since
m=0
). Definition (1) of
\Theta^{\lambda^{+}}
and Proposition 3.1 now show that
\sum_{I\in\mathcal{J}_{q-1}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-1}\text{Re}(\Gamma^{\lambda^{+}}_{jk}\phi_{jI},\phi_{kI})=\sum^{{}^{\prime}}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\widetilde{u_{jK}}\overline{\widetilde{u_{kK}}}e^{-\varphi}
. Therefore, (HR) translates to

(6.2)

\|\bar{\partial}_{b}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}_{b,\varphi}^{\star}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}=\|\overline{L}u\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\text{Re}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{K}\sum_{j,k}\int_{b\Omega}c_{jk}\left(T-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right)u_{jK}\overline{u_{kK}}e^{-\varphi}\\

Evaluation of immune checkpoint inhibitor efficacy for solid tumors with CD274 (PD-L1 gene) amplification identified by comprehensive genomic profiling: retrospective study based on a nationwide database
Authors: Tomohiro Nakayama, Takayuki Takahama, Yasutaka Chiba, Naoki Shiraishi, Hisato Kawakami, Kimio Yonesaka, Kazuhiko Nakagawa, Hidetoshi Hayashi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAmplification of the programmed cell death-ligand 1 gene (CD274) is highly prevalent and associated with a high response rate to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in lymphomas, and is also a potential biomarker for ICI treatment of solid tumors. However, the efficacy of ICIs for solid tumors with CD274 amplification identified by comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) has been unclear. We here examined ICI efficacy for solid tumors with CD274 amplification identified by CGP in a national database.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed data from the Center for Cancer Genomics and Advanced Therapeutics database containing 60,155 CGP test results for individuals with solid tumors. Only clinical data from patients treated with ICIs alone (not those undergoing concomitant therapy with molecularly targeted or cytotoxic chemotherapeutic agents) were evaluated. We matched 48 patients in the CD274 amplification-positive group with 170 patients in the CD274 amplification-negative group in a 1:4 ratio based on tumor type, histology, treatment, and age. Overall survival (OS), time to next treatment (TTNT), and response rate were evaluated as treatment outcomes in the two groups.ResultsOS was similar in the CD274-amplified and matched CD274-non-amplified groups (median of 22.1 vs 26.3 months, respectively; HR of 0.92 with a 95% CI of 0.55 to 1.54; p=0.075). TTNT tended to be longer in the CD274-amplified group than in the matched CD274-non-amplified group (median of 16.5 vs 14.0 months; HR of 0.63 with a 95% CI of 0.37 to 1.08; p=0.091). The objective response rate was 33.3% and 18.4% (difference of 14.9%, with a 95% CI of –0.2% to 31.6%), and the disease control rate was 63.9% and 41.1% (difference of 22.8%, with a 95% CI of 5.1% to 40.4%), in the CD274-amplified and matched CD274-non-amplified groups, respectively.ConclusionsThe number of patients with solid tumors positive for CD274 amplification in this analysis is the largest to date, and our results suggest that such gene amplification may be associated with the outcome of ICI treatment in such individuals. CD274 amplification identified by CGP may therefore be a predictor of ICI efficacy for solid tumors.Trial registration numberUMIN000029779.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have shown clinical benefit in the treatment of various cancer types, with their impact on cancer therapy only increasing. However, accessibility to ICIs varies by cancer type. For rare cancers, in particular, the small population size makes it difficult to validate ICI treatment in clinical trials, and cross-organ biomarkers such as tumor mutation burden (TMB) and microsatellite instability (MSI) are adopted to identify individuals likely to benefit from such therapy. However, the prevalence of a high TMB or MSI is only 13.1% and 5%, respectively,1 2 making the identification of new cross-organ biomarkers for patient selection a priority.

The CD274 gene encodes programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and is located at chromosomal region 9p24.1. An increase in the copy number of CD274 has been detected in the vast majority (97.2%) of classical Hodgkin lymphomas,3 which show response rates as high as 62–87% for ICIs that target the programmed cell death-1 (PD-1)-PD-L1 axis.4 5 Furthermore, amplification of the 9p24.1 region has also been observed in 50–63% of other types of lymphoma,6 7 including primary mediastinal large B cell lymphoma and primary central nervous system large B cell lymphoma, which also show a high response rate to such ICIs.8 9 These findings indicate that amplification of chromosomal region 9p24.1, including CD274, is associated with the response to ICI treatment in certain lymphomas. In solid tumors, the overall amplification rate of CD274 is only 0.7–1.1%.1012 However, this prevalence is higher for certain tumor types, being 2–3% in head and neck squamous cell cancer and cervical cancer, for example, and 5.1% and 3.9% for undifferentiated thyroid cancer and undifferentiated soft tissue sarcoma, respectively, both of which are rare cancers. The efficacy of ICIs in individuals with solid tumors positive for CD274 amplification has also been observed in several case reports13 14 and retrospective studies,10 15 16 implicating such gene amplification as a potential biomarker for ICI treatment of solid tumors.

Given the potential of CD274 amplification as a cross-organ biomarker for ICIs in individuals with solid tumors and the fact that the efficacy of ICIs in such patients found to be positive for CD274 amplification by comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) has not been validated (with the exception of the aforementioned studies with fewer than 10 cases1016), we evaluated ICI efficacy for solid tumors positive for CD274 amplification in this retrospective study performed with a large database containing the results of 60,155 CGP tests performed in Japan.

Methods

Patient selection

We queried a deidentified data set of genomic and clinical information for patients entered into the Center for Cancer Genomics and Advanced Therapeutics (C-CAT) database from June 1, 2019 to September 30, 2023. The clinical data included age, sex, histology, PD-L1 status evaluated by immunohistochemistry (IHC), treatment before and after the oncogene panel test, drug response, and type of CGP test. All patients provided written informed consent to use their genomic and clinical data for research purposes. The study is registered with UMIN (University Hospital Medical Information Network in Japan) under accession number UMIN000029779.

CGP and assessment of CD274 amplification, MSI, and TMB

CGP was performed with the Foundation One Companion Diagnostic (F1CDx; Foundation Medicine, Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA), Foundation One Liquid Companion Diagnostic (F1LCDx, Foundation Medicine), and National Cancer Center (NCC) Oncopanel tests. F1CDx and F1LCDx detect substitutions, insertions, deletions, and copy number alterations for 324 genes as well as selective gene rearrangements and genomic signatures such as MSI and TMB.17 The NCC Oncopanel examines mutations, amplifications, and homozygous deletions of the entire coding region for 127 genes of clinical or preclinical relevance as well as rearrangements.18 CD274 amplification-positive status was defined as positivity for CD274 amplification by any of the three assays, with copy number cut-offs of 6 for F1CDx and F1LCDx17 and of 8 for the NCC Oncopanel.18 MSI status and TMB were analyzed according to the criteria of each CGP panel.17 18 On the basis of the requirements for approval of pembrolizumab by the US Food and Drug Administration,19 TMB was classified as low (≤9 mutations/Mb) or high (≥10 mutations/Mb).

Efficacy assessments for ICI therapy

The analysis was designed to evaluate the clinical background and the effectiveness of ICI treatment. Patients who received ICIs plus either cytotoxic chemotherapy or a molecularly targeted agent were excluded from the analysis so as to confirm treatment efficacy with ICIs alone. The primary endpoint of the study was overall survival (OS), and secondary endpoints included time to next treatment (TTNT), objective response rate (ORR), and disease control rate (DCR). OS was defined as the time from the start of ICI treatment to the last date for which survival status data were available (last follow-up date: September 30, 2023). All deaths were considered events regardless of the reason. TTNT was defined as the time from the start of ICI treatment to the initiation of subsequent therapy, with censoring at the last clinical contact if no subsequent systemic therapies were administered. ORR is the percentage of patients with a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as the best overall response, and DCR is the percentage of patients with a CR, PR, or stable disease (SD). The denominator for both ORR and DCR was the population that was evaluable, excluding patients that were not evaluable (NE). The attending physician determined the therapeutic effect according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors.

Statistical analysis

We first matched the CD274 amplification-positive (CD274-amplified) group and the CD274 amplification-negative (CD274-non-amplified) group on the basis of tumor type. If the number of cases in the CD274-non-amplified group was more than four times that in the CD274-amplified group, the two groups were further matched in a 1:4 ratio on the basis of histology, treatment, and age. OS was assessed by the Kaplan-Meier method, with the difference between the two groups being assessed according to the HR estimated with a Cox proportional hazards model and by the log-rank test. TTNT was also assessed by the same survival analysis method as for OS. For ORR and DCR, differences between the two groups were assessed by risk differences and Fisher’s exact test. All statistical analysis was performed with the use of the EZR (Easy R) software package20 V.1.64 or Prism V.9.5.1 software. A p value of<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A flow diagram for the included patients is shown in figure 1. Among the 60,155 patients in the C-CAT database, 466 individuals (0.8%) showed CD274 amplification. 48 patients in the CD274-amplified group and 2558 individuals in the CD274-non-amplified group were treated with ICIs alone and were therefore included in the analysis. Patient characteristics are summarized in table 1. Although there were differences in the proportions of tumor types, no significant differences were apparent in the other examined factors between the CD274-amplified group and the unmatched CD274-non-amplified group. In the CD274-amplified group, the most common tumor type was head and neck cancer (n=13, 27.1%), followed by urothelial carcinoma (n=5, 10.4%), cancer of unknown primary (n=4, 8.3%), esophagus cancer (n=3, 6.3%), lung adenocarcinoma (n=3, 6.3%), cervical cancer (n=3, 6.3%), gastric cancer (n=2, 4.2%), biliary tract cancer (n=2, 4.2%), and breast cancer (n=2, 4.2%), with one case each for the other tumor types. The unmatched CD274-non-amplified group included 38 types of cancer (online supplemental table S1), with gastric cancer (n=455, 17.8%) being the most common and with head and neck cancer (n=383, 15.0%), urothelial carcinoma (n=277, 10.8%), melanoma (n=258, 10.1%), esophagus cancer (n=190, 7.4%), and lung adenocarcinoma (n=160, 6.3%) each accounting for>5%. Melanoma was less common in the CD274-amplified group (2.1%) than in the unmatched CD274-non-amplified group (10.1%). After matching, 218 patients were enrolled in the study, including 48 patients in the CD274-amplified group and 170 in the CD274-non-amplified group. Although most cases in the CD274-amplified group could be matched on all planned factors, given the small number of some carcinomas, cervical cancer could not be matched by age whereas thyroid cancer, skin cancer (non-melanoma), SMARCA4-deficient tumor, penile cancer, and peripheral nerve sheath tumor were matched by carcinoma type only. No significant differences were apparent between the two matched groups regarding background factors (table 1). In addition, comparisons of MSI status and TMB (mutations per megabase) revealed no significant differences between the CD274-amplified and matched CD274-non-amplified groups (online supplemental table S2). In the CD274-amplified group, PD-L1 IHC data were available for only four cases of non-small cell lung cancer, with PD-L1 expression levels being reported as negative, 25–49%, >50%, and 90%. The median follow-up time was 13.0, 13.3, and 13.0 months in the CD274-amplified group, the unmatched CD274-non-amplified group, and the matched CD274-non-amplified group, respectively.

Time-to-event outcomes

The survival status of the CD274-amplified and CD274-non-amplified groups is shown in figure 2. OS for the CD274-amplified group did not differ significantly from that for the unmatched CD274-non-amplified group (median of 22.1 vs 27.0 months, respectively; HR of 1.05 with a 95% CI of 0.66 to 1.67; p=0.853) (figure 2A). The median TTNT was 16.5 and 12.6 months in the CD274-amplified group and unmatched CD274-non-amplified group, respectively, with a 37% lower risk of disease progression in the former group than in the latter (HR of 0.63 with a 95% CI of 0.39 to 1.03, p=0.067) (figure 2B). A similar trend was observed in the 1:4 matched cohort, with a median OS of 22.1 months for the CD274-amplified group versus 26.3 months for the matched CD274-non-amplified group (HR of 0.92 with a 95% CI of 0.55 to 1.54, p=0.075) (figure 2C), and a median TTNT of 16.5 months versus 14.0 months (HR of 0.63 with a 95% CI of 0.37 to 1.08, p=0.091), respectively (figure 2D).

A subgroup analysis was performed to investigate the potential influence of clinical factors on treatment efficacy (online supplemental figures S1 and S2). For OS, similar results were observed across all factors as in the overall analysis. However, in the case of TTNT for patients with squamous cell carcinoma, the favorable trend observed in the overall analysis was more pronounced (HR of 0.46 with a 95% CI of 0.20 to 1.01, p=0.054) (figure 3).

Objective response and DCRs

Outcomes related to response rate are detailed and summarized in table 2 and figure 4, respectively. For the CD274-amplified group, the best overall response was CR in 2 patients, PR in 10 patients, SD in 11 patients, progressive disease (PD) in 13 patients, and NE in 12 patients. ORR and DCR in this group were thus 33.3% (12/36) and 63.9% (23/36), respectively. The best overall response in the matched CD274-non-amplified group was CR in 2 patients, PR in 24 patients, SD in 32 patients, PD in 83 patients, and NE in 29 patients, yielding ORR and DCR values of 18.4% (26/141) and 41.1% (58/141), respectively. ORR was numerically higher in the CD274-amplified group than in the matched CD274-non-amplified group (33.3% vs 18.4%), with a difference of 14.9% (95% CI −0.2% to 31.6%; p=0.068) (figure 4C). DCR was significantly higher in the CD274-amplified group than in the matched CD274-non-amplified group (63.9% vs 41.1%), with a difference of 22.8% (95% CI 5.1% to 40.4%; p=0.016) (figure 4D).

Discussion

Whereas the efficacy of ICIs has been demonstrated for multiple tumor types and these drugs are widely administered in clinical practice, the identification of predictors of ICI efficacy remains at an early stage. Our retrospective study evaluated the effectiveness of ICIs in patients with advanced solid tumors positive for CD274 amplification in the C-CAT database,21 which was established at the direction of national policy and contains>60,000 CGP test results. The number of patients with CD274 amplification evaluated in the present study is the largest to date, and a favorable trend for TTNT and response rate in patients treated with ICIs was observed for the CD274-amplified group compared with the matched CD274-non-amplified group.

The proportion of cases with CD274 amplification in the database adopted for this study was 0.8% (466/60,155). The prevalence of CD274 amplification identified by CGP with a cut-off value of 6 for the copy number was 0.7% (843/118,187),10 1.1% (2712/244,584),12 and 0.7% (350/48,782)11 in previous studies, values that are similar to that found in the present study.

We did not detect a difference in OS between patients with or without CD274 amplification. We believe that caution is warranted in the interpretation of the OS data, however. A previous study in which fluorescence in situ hybridization was adopted to define CD274 amplification (defined as a CD274/CEP9 ratio of≥2.0, equivalent to a copy number of≥4) suggested that a higher copy number may be associated with a longer OS.15 On the other hand, a study that examined the relation between OS and different CD274 copy number cut-offs found that the difference in OS apparent with a copy number cut-off of 4 was no longer observed when the cut-off was set at 6,16 consistent with our present results. It is therefore important to specify the cut-off value for copy number when examining the relation between OS and gene amplification. In addition, the fact that our analysis included cancer types for which the efficacy of ICIs has already been demonstrated in previous studies may have contributed to the lack of a difference in OS between the CD274-amplified and CD274-non-amplified groups.

In contrast, with regard to TTNT and response rate, we detected a positive trend in the CD274-amplified group. A high copy number of CD274 was previously associated with a longer progression-free survival and higher ORR for ICI-treated patients with lung cancer,15 similar to the results of our study. The ORR was 33.3% in the CD274-amplified group of our study, which differs from that in previous studies that reported values of 69.2% and 80% for similar subjects.10 15 The ORR in our study may more accurately reflect the real-world efficacy of ICIs because of the larger number of cases analyzed than in previous studies in which<10 patients were included.

The favorable trend for TTNT and treatment response rate in our study suggests that CD274 amplification has the potential to serve as a biomarker for ICI efficacy. Our subgroup analysis to investigate the potential influence of clinical factors on treatment efficacy revealed that this favorable trend associated with CD274 amplification was more pronounced for squamous cell carcinoma. In addition, the CD274-amplified group showed a favorable trend for head and neck cancer in the TTNT analysis (online supplemental figure S2), likely because most head and neck tumors in the CD274-amplified group were squamous cell carcinoma (12 out of 13 cases). These results suggest that the combination of CD274 amplification with other clinical factors such as histological type may provide a more reliable biomarker. CD274 amplification has been detected by CGP even in cancer types for which the efficacy of ICIs is unproven, and if such amplification is a predictor of ICI efficacy it is expected that the presence of CD274 amplification will lead to the administration of ICIs in such patients. The relation of CD274 amplification to the outcome of ICI therapy, including analysis of OS and according to histological type, therefore needs to be tested in subjects including those with cancer types for which the efficacy of ICIs is unknown, as in an ongoing prospective study (NCT02834013).

There are several limitations to our study. First, it is retrospective in design, and it is possible that missing data, other exclusions, and censored cases may have influenced the results. In particular, the proportion of missing data was higher in the CD274-non-amplified group. Second, the analysis of known biomarkers, such as TMB, MSI, and PD-L1 IHC, is insufficient. Although we confirmed that there was no difference in TMB and MSI between the CD274-amplified and matched CD274-non-amplified groups, we were not able to perform multivariate analysis as a result of the small number of patients in the CD274-amplified group. With regard to the relation between CD274 amplification and PD-L1 IHC, PD-L1 data were available for only four cases in the CD274-amplified group. Additional research to evaluate the relations between these existing biomarkers and CD274 amplification is warranted. Third, we were not able to evaluate differences in treatment efficacy for the various antibody drugs administered. Most patients in both the CD274-amplified group and the CD274-non-amplified group were treated with PD-1 antibodies, with PD-L1 antibodies alone being administered for only two cases in the CD274-amplified group (table 1). Fourth, it was not possible to determine whether CD274 amplification is a prognostic or predictive factor. We did not evaluate the relation between CD274 amplification and treatment efficacy in the cohort not treated with ICIs because it was difficult to obtain information such as treatment lines from the database in order to establish appropriate target populations.

In conclusion, our study is the most extensive to date to evaluate ICI efficacy in patients with solid tumors found to be positive for CD274 amplification by CGP, and our results suggest that CD274 amplification may be associated with such efficacy. Future results of prospective studies, including an ongoing study (NCT02834013), are awaited to confirm the status of CD274 amplification as a biomarker for ICI therapy.

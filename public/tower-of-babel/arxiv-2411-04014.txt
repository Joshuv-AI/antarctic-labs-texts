Abstract
The spread of a graph is the difference between the largest and smallest eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix. In this paper, we investigate spread problems for graphs with excluded clique-minors. We show that for sufficiently large n n , the n n -vertex K r K_{r} -minor free graph with maximum spread is the join of a clique and an independent set, with r − 2 r-2 and n − r + 2 n-r+2 vertices, respectively.

Introduction

Consider an
n\times n
complex matrix
M
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
.
The
spread

s(M)
of
M
is defined as
s(M)=\max_{i,j}{|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|}
,
which reflects the largest distance between any two eigenvalues of the matrix. The concept
of spread was first introduced by Mirsky
[
25
]
in 1956, with further significant
results appearing in
[
26
]
. Since then, the concept of spread has attracted significant
attention from researchers; see, for example,
[
8
,
16
,
27
,
31
]
.

The spread of a matrix has also drawn interest in specific cases. Let
G
be a simple
undirected graph of order
n
. The adjacency matrix of
G
, denoted by
A(G)
, is
an
n\times n
matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the vertices of
G
.
The
(u,v)
-entry of
A(G)
is
1
if
u
and
v
are adjacent, and
0
otherwise.
Since
A(G)
is symmetric and real, its eigenvalues are real and can be ordered
as
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
. In the context of
the adjacency matrix
A(G)
, the spread is simply the difference between the
largest and smallest eigenvalues, denoted by

s(G):=\lambda_{1}(G)-\lambda_{n}(G).

Compared with the widely studied largest and smallest eigenvalues, the spread of graphs contains more information about the distribution of eigenvalues and reflects more of the global structure of graphs.
The systematic study of the spread of graphs was initiated by Gregory, Hershkowitz,
and Kirkland
[
13
]
. Since then, the spread of graphs has been widely investigated.
A major focus in this area is to maximize or minimize the spread over a fixed family
of graphs and characterize the corresponding extremal graphs that achieve these bounds.
In 2001, Gregory, Hershkowitz, and Kirkland
[
13
]
made two significant conjectures.
They first conjectured that the
n
-vertex graph with maximum spread is given by

K_{\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor}\vee\lceil n/3\rceil K_{1}
, the join of the clique on

\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor
vertices and an independent set on
\lceil n/3\rceil
vertices.
They also conjectured that if
G
maximizes spread over all
n
-vertex graphs with
m\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
edges,
then
G
must be bipartite. Subsequently, many scholars contributed to these two conjectures,
such as unicyclic graphs
[
11
,
18
,
33
]
,
\infty
-bicyclic graphs
(the cycles containing two edges that do not intersect)
[
34
]
, bicyclic graphs
[
32
]
,
the family of all
n
-vertex graphs
[
2
]
, graphs with a given matching number
[
18
]
,
girth
[
11
,
32
]
, or size
[
22
]
, very recently for the family of planar and outerplanar graphs

[
12
,
17
,
20
]
. We would like to mention that Breen, Riasanovsky, Tait, and
Urschel
[
2
]
confirmed these two conjectures.

A minor of a graph
G
is a graph obtained from
G
by means of a sequence of vertex
deletions, edge deletions, and edge contractions.
A graph is said to be
H
-minor free, if it does not contain
H
as a minor.
Minors play a key role in graph theory, and extremal problems
on forbidding minors have attracted appreciable amount of
interest in the past decades, particularly in the case of
K_{r}
-minors.
A natural way to construct a large
K_{r}
-minor free graph is by taking the join of
K_{r-2}
with
n-r+2
independent vertices. This yields a
K_{r}
-minor free graph with
(r-2)n-\binom{r-1}{2}
edges. Mader
[
23
]
proved that this construction achieves the maximum possible size for
r\leq 7
.
However, this expression no longer holds for
r\geq 8
.

From spectral perspective, spectral extremal problems in graph theory, specifically for graphs excluding certain minors, have been extensively studied in recent decades.
These investigations cover families such as outerplanar graphs
[
19
,
29
]
, planar graphs

[
1
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
29
]
, and
K_{r}
-minor free graphs
[
28
]
.
Notably, Tait
[
28
]
investigated the spectral radius of
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs and posed a conjecture regarding the structure of graphs with the maximum spectral radius within this class. This conjecture was later resolved by Zhai and Lin
[
36
]
. Recently, Zhai, Fang, and Lin
[
35
]
study some unified phenomena in graphs with maximum spectral radius and excluded general minors.

The main goal of this paper is to contribute to the study of the spread in graphs with excluded minors.

Problem 1.1
.

Let
H
be a graph. What is the maximum spread of an
H
-minor free graph of order
n
?

In recent years, there have been notable developments on this problem.
Gotshall, O’Brien, and Tait
[
12
]
showed that
for
n
sufficiently large, the
n
-vertex outerplanar graph with maximum spread is a vertex
joined to a linear forest. They further conjectured that the extremal
graph is
K_{1}\vee P_{n-1}
. Recently, Li, Linz, Lu, and Wang
[
17
]
disproved this conjecture by showing that the extremal graph is instead

K_{1}\vee(P_{\lceil(2n-1)/3\rceil}\cup\lfloor(n-2)/3\rfloor K_{1})
. They also proved that the unique planar graph attaining the maximum spread is given by

(K_{1}\cup K_{1})\vee(P_{\lceil(2n-2)/3\rceil}\cup\lfloor(n-4)/3\rfloor K_{1})
. Furthermore,
Linz, Lu, and Wang
[
20
]
determined the maximum spread over all
K_{2,t}
-minor
free graphs on
n
vertices for
t\geq 2
and
n
sufficiently large. Very recently,
they extended this result to
K_{s,t}
-minor free graphs for
t\geq s\geq 2

[
21
]
.

In this paper, inspired by the results in
[
3
,
12
,
17
,
20
,
21
]
,
we determine the unique graph attaining the maximum spread among
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor
free graphs. Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a
K_{r}
-minor free graph of order
n
. For
r\geq 3
and
n
sufficiently large, we have

s(G)\leq\sqrt{4(r-2)(n-r+2)+(r-3)^{2}},

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{r-2}\vee(n-r+2)K_{1}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, some necessary notations
and lemmas are provided. In Section
3
, we prove that the
K_{r}
-minor free graphs
G
with maximum spread must contain
K_{r-2,n-r+2}
as a spanning subgraph for
n
sufficiently large and
r\geq 3
.
In Section
4
, using
the novel Laurent series expansion method developed by Li, Linz, Lu, and Wang
[
17
]
,
we show that the maximum spread over all
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor free graphs is achieved
by
K_{r-2}\vee(n-r+2)K_{1}
, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Preliminaries

and prove some preliminary lemmas.

2.1
Notation

Consider a simple graph
G
of order
n
, the number of edges of
G
is called its size,
and denoted by
e(G)
. Given a subset
X
of the vertex set
V(G)
of
G
, the
subgraph of
G
induced by
X
is denoted by
G[X]
, and the graph obtained from
G
by
deleting
X
is denoted by
G\setminus X
. As usual, for a vertex
v
of
G
we write
d_{G}(v)

and
N_{G}(v)
for the degree of
v
and the set of neighbors of
v
in
G
, respectively.
If the underlying graph
G
is clear from the context, we use the notations
d(v)
and
N(v)
.
Let
N_{X}(v)
denote the set of vertices in
X
that are adjacent to
v
, i.e.,
N_{X}(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap X
.
Let
G\cup H
denote the disjoint union of
G
and
H
. The join
G\vee H
of disjoint
graphs of
G
and
H
is the graph obtained from
G\cup H
by joining each vertex of
G

to each vertex of
H
.

Let
I_{n}
denote the identity matrix of order
n
,
\bm{1}_{n}
denote the all-ones vector of
length
n
, and
J_{p\times q}
denote the all-ones matrix of dimensions
p\times q
. In the
above notation, we will skip the subscripts when they are clear from context.
For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]
denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Let
\bm{y}
be an eigenvector of
G
corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda
of
G
.
For each
v\in V(G)
, we have

\lambda y_{v}=\sum_{u\in N(v)}y_{u},

and refer it as the
eigen-equation
with respect to
v
and
\lambda
.

This subsection collects some necessary results required for this paper.

In 1967, Mader
[

-minor free graphs.

Theorem 2.1

(

]
)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph. For every graph
H
, if

is

-minor free, then there exists
a constant

In 2007, Thomason
[

(
[

]
)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-minor free bipartite graph with two vertex partitions
A

and

, where
|A|\gg|B|>0
. Then

e(G)\leq(s-1)\cdot|A|+4^{s+1}s!t\cdot|B|.

In 2004, Hong
[

]
determined the unique graph with the maximum spectral radius among

K_{5}
-minor free graphs. More recently, Tait
[

extended Hong’s result to

-minor free graphs by proving

(

]
)

Let

be the

-vertex

-minor free graphs with

. If

is large enough,

\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(r-3+\sqrt{4(r-2)(n-r+2)+(r-3)^{2}}\right),

with equality holds if and only if

.

We conclude this subsection with the following lemma, which can be proved by induction or double counting.

Lemma 2.1

(

)
.

Let

be

|A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap\cdots\cap A_{k}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|A_{i}|-(k-1)\bigg|\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\bigg|.

2.3
Preliminary results on
K_{r}
-minor free graphs

In light of Theorem
2.2
, we have the following result
on
K_{r}
-minor free bipartite graphs.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor free bipartite graph, with vertex partition
A
and
B
.
If
|A|=k
and
|B|=n-k
, then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
r
such that

e(G)\leq(r-2)n+Ck.

Proof.

Noting that every
K_{r-1,r-1}
-minor graph contains a
K_{r}
-minor by contracting some edges,
we have that
G
is
K_{r-1,r-1}
-minor free. Therefore, the assertion follows from Theorem

2.2
, as desired.
∎

Throughout this paper, we set

\gamma_{n,r}:=\sqrt{(r-2)(n-r+2)}.

Using Theorem
2.3
, we can now prove the following result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor free graph with maximum spread. Then

\gamma_{n,r}-\frac{r-3}{2}-O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\leq-\lambda_{n}(G)\leq\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\gamma_{n,r}+\frac{r-3}{2}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(G)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(r-3+\sqrt{4\gamma_{n,r}^{2}+(r-3)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n,r}+\frac{r-3}{2}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big).

(2.1)

Next, we establish the lower bound for
-\lambda_{n}(G)
. Observe that
K_{r-2,n-r+2}
is
K_{r}
-minor
free, which implies
s(G)\geq s(K_{r-2,n-r+2})=2\gamma_{n,r}
. Thus, we have

-\lambda_{n}(G)\geq 2\gamma_{n,r}-\lambda_{1}(G).

(2.2)

Substituting (
2.3
) into (
2.2
), we obtain

-\lambda_{n}(G)\geq\gamma_{n,r}-\frac{r-3}{2}-O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

3
Structure of
K_{r}
-minor free graphs with maximum spread

In this section, we always assume that
G
is a graph attaining the maximum spread among all

K_{r}
-minor free graphs of order
n
. The aim of this section is to explore the general
structure of
G
. We will show that
G
contains
r-2
vertices, each with degree
n-1
,
and that after removing these
r-2
vertices, the resulant graph forms an independent set.

We now fix some notation. Let
\bm{x}
be a nonnegative eigenvector corresponding to

\lambda_{1}(G)
, and let
\bm{z}
be an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{n}(G)
.
For convenience, we normalize both
\bm{x}
and
\bm{z}
so that their maximum entries
in absolute value are
1
. Without loss of generality, assume that there are two vertices

u_{0}
and
w_{0}
such that
x_{u_{0}}=1
and
|z_{w_{0}}|=1
. Set
\lambda_{1}:=\lambda_{1}(G)

and
\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{n}(G)
for short. Let

V_{+}=\{v:z_{v}>0\},~~V_{-}=\{v:z_{v}<0\},~~V_{0}=\{v:z_{v}=0\}.

Observing that
\bm{z}
is a non-zero vector and the eigen-equations with respect to
\lambda_{n}
,
we derive
V_{+}\neq\emptyset
and
V_{-}\neq\emptyset
.
In this section, we always assume that
|V_{+}|\leq n/2
.
For
S\subseteq V(G)
, we shall use
\vol(S)=\sum_{v\in S}|z_{v}|
to denote the volume of
S
.

Our sketchy strategy will be to firstly show that there are
r-2
vertices of large degree
(Lemma
3.4
) and the remaining vertices have small eigenvector entry (Lemma
3.6
).
Then, we use these facts to show that the
r-2
vertices of large degree must be adjacent
to all other vertices (see Theorem
3.1
).

Next, we will present a few simple lemmas that, while perhaps not optimal, are sufficient for our purposes. To begin with, we firstly give the following two facts for estimating the volume of a subset
S
of
V_{+}
, which will be used in following lemmas frequently. By considering the eigen-equation with repect to each
v\in S
, we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\vol(S)

\displaystyle=|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\sum_{v\in S}z_{v}=|\lambda_{n}|\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(-\sum_{u\in N(v)}{z_{u}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{u\in N(v)\cap V_{-}}|\lambda_{n}||z_{u}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{u\in N(v)\cap V_{-}}\sum_{w\in N(u)}z_{w}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{u\in N(v)\cap V_{-}}\sum_{w\in N(u)\cap V_{+}}z_{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V_{+}}z_{w}\sum_{u\in N(w)\cap V_{-}}|N(u)\cap S|

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V_{+}}z_{w}\cdot|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},S)|.

(3.1)

In particular, if
S=\{s\}\subseteq V_{+}
, it follows from (
3
) that

\displaystyle|\lambda_{n}|^{2}z_{s}\leq\sum_{w\in V_{+}}z_{w}\cdot|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},S)|=\sum_{w\in V_{+}}z_{w}\cdot|N(w)\cap V_{-}\cap N(s)|.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1
.

\vol(V_{+})=O(1)
.

Proof.

Fix a sufficiently small constant
\varepsilon>0
, let

V_{+}^{(1)}=\{v\in V_{+}:|N(v)\cap V_{-}|\geq\varepsilon n\},\quad V_{+}^{(2)}=V_{+}\setminus V_{+}^{(1)}.

By the definition of
V_{+}^{(1)}
and Theorem
2.1
, we have

|V_{+}^{(1)}|\leq\frac{E(V_{+}^{(1)},V_{-})}{\varepsilon n}=O(1).

(3.3)

To finish the proof, we shall derive an relationship between
\vol(V_{+}^{(1)})

and
\vol(V_{+}^{(2)})
. It follows from (
3
) that

|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\vol(V_{+}^{(2)})\leq\sum_{w\in V_{+}^{(1)}}z_{w}\cdot|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(2)})|+\sum_{w\in V_{+}^{(2)}}z_{w}\cdot|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(2)})|.

(3.4)

For each
w\in V_{+}^{(1)}
, by Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(2)})|\leq|E(V_{-},V_{+}^{(2)})|=O(n).

(3.5)

For each
w\in V_{+}^{(2)}
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(2)})|\leq(r-2)|V_{+}^{(2)}|+\varepsilon\cdot O(n)\leq\frac{r-2}{2}n+\varepsilon\cdot O(n).

(3.6)

Hence, by (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we derive that

|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\vol(V_{+}^{(2)})\leq\vol(V_{+}^{(1)})\cdot O(n)+\vol(V_{+}^{(2)})\left(\frac{r-2}{2}n+\varepsilon\cdot O(n)\right),

which, together with Lemma
2.3
, implies that

\vol(V_{+}^{(2)})\leq\frac{O(n)}{|\lambda_{n}|^{2}-(r-2)n/2-\varepsilon\cdot O(n)}\cdot\vol(V_{+}^{(1)})=O(1)\cdot\vol(V_{+}^{(1)}).

Noting that
\vol(V_{+})=\vol(V_{+}^{(1)})+\vol(V_{+}^{(2)})
and (
3.3
), we derive

\vol(V_{+})\leq O(1)\cdot\vol(V_{+}^{(1)})\leq O(1)\cdot|V_{+}^{(1)}|=O(1).

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

The following conclusions hold:

(a)

For each
v\in V_{-}
,
|z_{v}|=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
.

(b)

w_{0}\in V_{+}
.

(c)

|N(w_{0})\cap V_{-}|=\Omega(n)
.

Proof.

(a) For any
v\in V_{-}
, by the eigen-equation with respect to
v
, we have

|\lambda_{n}||z_{v}|=\sum_{u\in N(v)}z_{u}\leq\sum_{u\in N(v)\cap V_{+}}z_{u}\leq\vol(V_{+}).

Combining this with Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

|z_{v}|\leq\frac{\vol(V_{+})}{|\lambda_{n}|}=O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big).

(b) Since
|z_{w_{0}}|=1
and
|z_{v}|=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
for each
v\in V_{-}
, it follows
that
w_{0}\in V_{+}
and
z_{w_{0}}=1
.

(c) By (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{n}|^{2}=|\lambda_{n}|^{2}z_{w_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V_{+}}z_{v}\cdot|N(v)\cap V_{-}\cap N(w_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq|N(w_{0})\cap V_{-}|\cdot\vol(V_{+}).

This implies
|N(w_{0})\cap V_{-}|\geq\frac{|\lambda_{n}|^{2}}{\vol(V_{+})}
.
Then, by Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
, we have

|N(w_{0})\cap V_{-}|=\Omega(n).

This completes the proof of Corollary
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The following conclusions hold:

(a)

|V_{-}|\geq n-O(\sqrt{n})
.

(b)

|V_{+}|=O(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

Choose a fixed constant
C_{1}
, let

\displaystyle V_{+}^{(3)}=\{v\in V_{+}:|N(v)\cap V_{-}|\geq C_{1}\sqrt{n}\},\quad V_{+}^{(4)}=V_{+}\setminus V_{+}^{(3)}.

(3.7)

By the definition of
V_{+}
and Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

|V_{+}^{(3)}|\leq\frac{E(V_{+}^{(3)},V_{-})}{C_{1}\sqrt{n}}\leq O(\sqrt{n}).

(3.8)

By (
3
), we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\vol(V_{+}^{(3)})\leq\sum_{w\in V_{+}^{(3)}}z_{w}\cdot|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(3)})|+\sum_{w\in V_{+}^{(4)}}z_{w}\cdot|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(3)})|.

(3.9)

For each
w\in V_{+}^{(3)}
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(3)})|\leq|E(V_{-},V_{+}^{(3)})|\leq(r-2)|V_{-}|+O(1)\cdot|V_{+}^{(3)}|.

(3.10)

For each
w\in V_{+}^{(4)}
, by Lemma
2.2
and the definition of
V_{+}^{(4)}
, we obtain

|E(N(w)\cap V_{-},V_{+}^{(3)})|\leq(r-2)|V_{+}^{(3)}|+O(\sqrt{n}).

(3.11)

Then, by (
3.9
), (
3.10
)
and (
3.11
), we can deduce that

|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\vol(V_{+}^{(3)})\leq\vol(V_{+}^{(3)})\big[(r-2)|V_{-}|+O(1)\cdot|V_{+}^{(3)}|\big]+\vol(V_{+}^{(4)})\big[(r-2)|V_{+}^{(3)}|+O(\sqrt{n})\big],

which implies that

|V_{-}|\geq\frac{|\lambda_{n}|^{2}}{r-2}-O(1)\cdot|V_{+}^{(3)}|-\big(|V_{+}^{(3)}|+O(\sqrt{n})\big)\frac{\vol(V_{+}^{(4)})}{\vol(V_{+}^{(3)})}.

(3.12)

Note that, by (
3.7
) and Corollary
3.1
(c),
we have
w_{0}\in V_{+}^{(3)}
, and so,

\displaystyle\vol(V_{+}^{(3)})\geq 1,\quad\vol(V_{+}^{(3)})\leq\vol(V_{+})=O(1).

(3.13)

Then, by (
3.7
), (
3.12
), (
3.13
),
and Lemma
2.3
, we have
|V_{-}|\geq n-O(\sqrt{n})
, and so,
|V_{+}|=O(\sqrt{n})
.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

In what follows, let

V_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{v\in V_{+}:|N(v)\cap V_{-}|\geq n-C_{2}\sqrt{n}\},\quad V_{+}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=V_{+}\setminus V_{+}^{{}^{\prime}},

(3.14)

where
C_{2}
is some large constant and chosen in next Lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\vol(V_{+}^{{}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{r-1}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\right)\vol(V_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle\vol(V_{+})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{r}{r-1}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\right)\vol(V_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}).

G[L]
,
and let
\bm{y}_{L}
denote the restriction of
\bm{y}
to the vertices of
L
.

By Theorem
3.1
, the adjacency matrix of
G
can be written as

A(G)=\begin{bmatrix}A_{L}&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{bmatrix},~~J\in\mathbb{R}^{(r-2)\times(n-r+2)}.

(4.2)

Since
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
A(G)
, we have

\begin{bmatrix}A_{L}&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{y}_{L}\\
\bm{1}\end{bmatrix}=\lambda\begin{bmatrix}\bm{y}_{L}\\
\bm{1}\end{bmatrix}.

As a consequence,

A_{L}\bm{y}_{L}+(n-r+2)\cdot\bm{1}=\lambda\bm{y}_{L}.

(4.3)

If
|\lambda|>\lambda_{1}(A_{L})
, in light of (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{L}

\displaystyle=(n-r+2)\cdot\left(\lambda I-A_{L}\right)^{-1}\bm{1}

\displaystyle=(n-r+2)\cdot\lambda^{-1}\left(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{L}\right)^{-1}\bm{1}

\displaystyle=(n-r+2)\cdot\lambda^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda^{-1}A_{L}\right)^{k}\bm{1}

\displaystyle=(n-r+2)\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-(k+1)}A_{L}^{k}\bm{1}.

(4.4)

Here we use the assumption that
|\lambda|>\lambda_{1}(A_{L})
so that the infinite series converges.
By the eigen-equation at vertex
v_{r-1}
and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda=\lambda y_{v_{r-1}}=\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\bm{y}_{L}

\displaystyle=\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot(n-r+2)\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-(k+1)}A_{L}^{k}\bm{1}

\displaystyle=(n-r+2)\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-(k+1)}\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}A_{L}^{k}\bm{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{n,r}^{2}}{\lambda}+(n-r+2)\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-(k+1)}\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}A_{L}^{k}\bm{1}.

(4.5)

Set
\ell_{k}:=\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}A_{L}^{k}\bm{1}
and
a_{k}:=(n-r+2)\ell_{k}
for
k\geq 0
.
Notices that
\ell_{1}=\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}A_{L}\bm{1}=\sum_{v\in L}d_{G[L]}(v)=2\left|E(G[L])\right|

and
\ell_{2}=\bm{1}^{\mathrm{T}}A_{L}^{2}\bm{1}=\sum_{v\in L}d_{G[L]}(v)^{2}
.
It follows from (
4
) that

\lambda^{2}=\gamma_{n,r}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda^{k}}.

(4.6)

According to Lemma
2.3
, both
\lambda_{1}(G)
and
\lambda_{n}(G)
satisfy
Equation (
4.6
). The next claim demonstrates that these are the only eigenvalues that do so.

Claim 4.1
.

|\lambda_{i}(G)|\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{L})
for
2\leq i\leq n-1
.

Proof.

In view of (
4.2
), we see

A(G)=\begin{bmatrix}A_{L}&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{L}&O\\
O&O\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}O&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{bmatrix}.

Clearly, the eigenvalues of the matrix

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}O&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{smallmatrix}\right]

are
\gamma_{n,r}
,
-\gamma_{n,r}
, and
0
with multiplicity
n-2
.
By Weyl’s inequalities
[
15
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, for
i=2,\ldots,n-1
, we obtain

\lambda_{i}(A(G))\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{L})+\lambda_{i}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}O&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=\lambda_{1}(A_{L}),

and

\lambda_{i}(A(G))\geq\lambda_{n}(A_{L})+\lambda_{i}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}O&J\\
J^{\mathrm{T}}&O\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=\lambda_{n}(A_{L})\geq-\lambda_{1}(A_{L}).

Hence
|\lambda_{i}(G)|\leq|\lambda_{1}(A_{L})|
for
2\leq i\leq n-1
.
∎

By Lemma 27 in the appendix of
[
17
]
,
\lambda_{1}(G)
has the following series expansion:

\lambda_{1}(G)=\gamma_{n,r}+c_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{\gamma_{n,r}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{n,r}^{2}}\right),

while
\lambda_{n}(G)
is given by the series expansion:

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(G)=-\gamma_{n,r}+c_{1}-\frac{c_{2}}{\gamma_{n,r}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{n,r}^{2}}\right).

Here,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are determined from (
4.6
) and
[
3
, Lemma 11]
as

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}}{2\gamma_{n,r}^{2}}=\frac{\ell_{1}}{2(r-2)},

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{8}\Big(\frac{a_{1}}{\gamma_{n,r}^{2}}\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{a_{2}}{\gamma_{n,r}^{2}}=-\frac{3}{8(r-2)^{2}}\ell_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\ell_{2}.

Therefore, the spread of
G
is

\displaystyle s(G)

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{n,r}+\frac{2c_{2}}{\gamma_{n,r}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{n,r}^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\gamma_{n,r}+\frac{1}{r-2}\left(-\frac{3}{4(r-2)}\ell_{1}^{2}+\ell_{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma_{n,r}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{n,r}^{3}}\right).

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

The following lemma is needed.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
4
,
7
]
)
.

Let
H
be a simple graph of order
n
with
m
edges. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}{d(v_{i})^{2}}\leq m\left(\frac{2m}{n-1}+n-2\right),

with equality holding if and only if
H\cong K_{1,n-1}
or
H\cong K_{n}
or
H\cong K_{n-1}\cup K_{1}
.

Now, we are ready to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
G
be a graph achieving the maximum spread among all
K_{r}
-minor free graphs
of order
n
. It suffices to show that
G=K_{r-2}\vee(n-r+2)K_{1}
. The assertion
follows directly from Theorem
3.1
for
r=3
.
From this point forward, we assume
r\geq 4
. Recall that

\ell_{1}=\sum_{v\in V(L)}d_{L}(v)
and
\ell_{2}=\sum_{v\in V(L)}d_{L}(v)^{2}
.
By (
4.1
), if
G[L]\cong K_{r-2}
, then

c_{2}=\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\cdot\left(\ell_{2}-\frac{3}{4(r-2)}\ell_{1}^{2}\right)=\frac{(r-3)^{2}}{8}.

(4.7)

We now proceed by contradiction to show that
G[L]\cong K_{r-2}
. Suppose this is not the case.
Using (
4.1
) and Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\cdot\left(\ell_{2}-\frac{3}{4(r-2)}\ell_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\cdot\left(\frac{\ell_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{\ell_{1}}{r-3}+r-4\right)-\frac{3}{4(r-2)}\ell_{1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\cdot\left(\left(\frac{1}{2(r-3)}-\frac{3}{4(r-2)}\right)\ell_{1}^{2}+\frac{r-4}{2}\ell_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\cdot\left(\frac{-r+5}{4(r-2)(r-3)}\ell_{1}^{2}+\frac{r-4}{2}\ell_{1}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{(r-3)^{2}}{8},

where the last inequality holds because
G[L]
is a proper subgraph of
K_{r-2}
,
and

f(\ell_{1}):=\frac{-r+5}{4(r-2)(r-3)}\ell_{1}^{2}+\frac{r-4}{2}\ell_{1}

is monotonically increasing as a function of
\ell_{1}
over the interval
[0,+\infty)

when
r\in\{4,5\}
, and over the interval
[0,(r-2)(r-3)(r-4)/(r-5)]
when
r\geq 6
. Hence,

s(G)<s(K_{r-2}\vee(n-r+2)K_{1})
by Lemma
4.1
and (
4.7
),
a contradiction.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
\Box

Free boundary minimal annuli in S + 2 × S 1 S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
Pak Tung Ho, Juncheol Pyo, Keomkyo Seo

Abstract
. Let M M be a compact 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and a nonempty boundary ∂ M \partial M . Fraser and Li [ 2 ] established a compactness theorem for the space of compact, properly embedded minimal surfaces of fixed topological type in M M with a free boundary on ∂ M \partial M , assuming that ∂ M \partial M is strictly convex with respect to the inward unit normal. In this paper, we show that the strict convexity condition on ∂ M \partial M cannot be relaxed.

Primary 53A10; Secondary 53C42

1.
Introduction

Choi and Schoen
[
1
]
proved that for a
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
with positive Ricci curvature, the space of embedded minimal surfaces in
M
of a fixed genus is compact.
Hsieh and Wang
[
4
]
have shown that the condition
of positive Ricci curvature cannot be relaxed.
In particular, they showed in
[
4
]
that
the embedded minimal tori in
S^{2}\times S^{1}
do not form a compact family,
for
S^{2}\times S^{1}
equipped with the standard product metric
has nonnegative Ricci curvature.

On the other hand, Fraser and Li
[
2
]
proved the corresponding result of Choi and Schoen
for the free boundary minimal surfaces. In particular, they proved the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Fraser-Li)
.

Let
M
be a compact
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with nonempty boundary
\partial M
.
Suppose
M
has nonnegative Ricci curvature and
\partial M
is strictly convex with respect to the inward unit normal.
Then the space of compact properly embedded minimal surfaces of fixed topological type in
M
with free boundary in

\partial M
is compact in the
C^{k}
topology for any
k\geq 2
.

Note that the existence of such a free boundary minimal annulus in
M
was obtained by Maximo, Nunes and Smith
[
3
]
. Inspired by the result of Hsieh and Wang
[
4
]
mentioned above,
we show that the condition that
\partial M
is strictly convex
could not be relaxed in Theorem
1.1
.
To state our main theorem,
let
S^{2}_{+}
be the
2
-dimensional hemisphere.
Equipped with the standard product metric, the product manifold
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
has nonnegative Ricci curvature,
and its boundary
\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1})

is totally geodesic and is not strictly convex.
The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
be equipped with the standard product metric.
Then there exists a sequence of compact properly embedded minimal annuli in
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
with free boundary in
\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1})
which
is not compact.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
Let
S^{2}_{+}
be the
2
-dimensional hemisphere given by

S^{2}_{+}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=1,y\geq 0\},

which is a
2
-dimensional manifold with boundary

\partial S^{2}_{+}=\{(x,0,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x^{2}+z^{2}=1\}.

Then
S^{2}_{+}
has a local parametrization given by

(2.1)

\overrightarrow{x}(r,\theta)=(\sin r\cos\theta,\sin r\sin\theta,\cos r),

where
0\leq r\leq\pi
and
0\leq\theta\leq\pi
.
Note that the boundary
\partial S^{2}_{+}
corresponds to
the points when
\theta=0
or
\theta=\pi
.
The induced metric
on
S^{2}_{+}
by
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

dr^{2}+\sin^{2}rd\theta^{2}.

Then the product space
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
is a
3
-dimensional manifold with boundary

\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1})=\partial S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
. Moreover,

(2.2)

g=dr^{2}+\sin^{2}rd\theta^{2}+dt^{2}

is a Riemannian
metric on
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
.
Equipped with the metric
g
in (
2.2
),
the boundary
\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1})
is a totally geodesic and hence is not strictly convex. It follows from (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

\begin{split}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}=\cot r\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},~~\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=0,\\
\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}=0,~~\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}=0,~~\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}=0.\end{split}

Suppose
r(t)
is a smooth function on
S^{1}
such that

(2.4)

r(t+2\pi)=r(t).

Define

f:[0,\pi]\times S^{1}\to S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
given by

(2.5)

f(\theta,t)=(\overrightarrow{x}(r(t),\theta),t),

where
\overrightarrow{x}
is defined as in (
2.1
).
Then

\vec{e}_{1}:=\frac{1}{\sin r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}~\text{and}~~\vec{e}_{2}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+r^{\prime 2}}}\left(r^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)

are unit vectors with respect to
g
,
and
\{\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{2}\}
spans the tangent space of
[0,\pi]\times S^{1}
.
Hence the unit normal
\vec{n}
is given by

\vec{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+r^{\prime 2}}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-r^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right).

By (
2.3
), we see that

(2.6)

(\nabla_{\vec{e}_{1}}\vec{n})^{T}=\nabla_{\vec{e}_{1}}\vec{n}=\left(\frac{\cot r}{\sqrt{1+r^{\prime 2}}}\right)\vec{e}_{1}~~\mbox{ and }~~(\nabla_{\vec{e}_{2}}\vec{n})^{T}=-\frac{r^{\prime\prime}}{(1+r^{\prime 2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\vec{e}_{2},

where
(\nabla_{\vec{e}_{2}}\vec{n})^{T}
denotes the tangential component of
\nabla_{\vec{e}_{2}}\vec{n}
.
It follows from (
2.6
) that the principal curvatures
of
[0,\pi]\times S^{1}
are
\frac{\cot r}{\sqrt{1+r^{\prime 2}}}
and
-\frac{r^{\prime\prime}}{(1+r^{\prime 2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}
,
which implies that the mean curvature is

H=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+r^{\prime 2}}}\left(\cot r-\frac{r^{\prime\prime}}{1+r^{\prime 2}}\right).

Hence,
[0,\pi]\times S^{1}
is minimal if

(2.7)

\frac{r^{\prime\prime}}{1+r^{\prime 2}}=\cot r.

On the other hand, the outward conormal vector
\vec{\nu}

is the unique unit vector in
T_{p}([0,\pi]\times S^{1})
which is orthogonal to

T_{p}\big(\partial([0,\pi]\times S^{1})\big)
. Thus, we have

(2.8)

\vec{\nu}=\pm\frac{1}{\sin r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}.

Note that the tangent plane of
\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1})

is spanned by
\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right\}
.
Hence, it follows from (
2.8
) that
\vec{\nu}
is orthogonal to

\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1})
, i.e.,

(2.9)

\vec{\nu}\perp\partial(S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}).

In view of (
2.9
), we conclude that if
r(t)
is a solution to (
2.4
) and (
2.7
),
then the embedding
f
of
[0,\pi]\times S^{1}
defined in (
2.5
)
is a free boundary minimal surface in
S^{2}_{+}\times S^{1}
.
As pointed out in
[
4
]
,
the general solutions
to (
2.4
) and (
2.7
)
are elliptic functions for a sequence of initial values

r(t=0)=c_{1}>c_{2}>c_{3}>\cdots\to 0
.
The corresponding sequence of embedded free boundary minimal surfaces certainly cannot have a convergent subsequence. This proves our main theorem.

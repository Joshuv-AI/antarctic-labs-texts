Multipoint conformal integrals in D D dimensions. Part II Polygons and basis functions
Konstantin Alkalaev and, Semyon Mandrygin

Abstract
We explicitly construct a class of multivariate generalized hypergeometric series which is conjectured in our previous paper [ 1 ] to calculate multipoint one-loop parametric conformal integrals in D D dimensions. Our approach is based on a simple diagrammatic algorithm which systematically builds both arguments and series coefficients in terms of a convex polygon which is part of the Baxter lattice. The examples of the box, pentagon, and hexagon integrals are considered in detail.

2.1
Reconstruction

In

1

I_{n}^{{\bm{a}}}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{\langle ijk\rangle\in{\rm R}_{n}}\Phi_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x})\,,

(2.5)

where the basis functions are labelled by ordered index triples

{\rm R}_{n}=\big\{\langle ijk\rangle,1\leqslant i<j<k\leqslant n\big\}\,.

(2.6)

Thus, a number of basis functions in (

|{\rm R}_{n}|=\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}\,.

(2.7)

Each basis function is the product of three factors,

where

•

{\rm S}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}})
is the
triangle-factor
which is a particular product of
\Gamma
-functions depending on parameters
\bm{a}
;

•

{\rm V}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x})
is the
leg-factor
which ensures correct transformation law of the conformal integral under conformal transformations (
2.3
);

Both factors are given in eqs. (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) below.

•

{\rm H}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big({\bm{a}}\big|\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big)
is a

function which is a multivariate hypergeometric series

The choice of a particular set (

\big\{\mathbb{Z}_{n}\circ\langle ijk\rangle\big\}\subset{\rm R}_{n}\,,\qquad\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\big\{e,C_{n},(C_{n})^{2},...,(C_{n})^{n-1}\big\}\,.

From each orbit we choose a representative
\langle ijk\rangle
determined by the minimum value of the sum
i+j+k

{\rm T}_{n}\subset{\rm R}_{n}

\left|{\rm T}_{n}\right|=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}{6}\,,\quad\text{if}\quad\frac{n}{3}\notin\mathbb{N}\,,\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{n(n-3)}{6}+1\,,\qquad\text{if}\quad\frac{n}{3}\in\mathbb{N}\,.\end{cases}

(2.11)

Thus, there are
\left|{\rm T}_{n}\right|

n/3\notin\mathbb{N}
, then each orbit has length

n/3\in\mathbb{N}

n
, while one orbit is shorter and has length

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-orbits of which representatives are called
master functions

\Phi_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x})
,
\langle ijk\rangle\in{\rm T}_{n}
. In this way, we can formulate the following

Reconstruction conjecture.

The
n
-point conformal integral (
2.1
) is the
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-invariant sum of basis functions

I_{n}^{{\bm{a}}}({\bm{x}})\,\overset{\mathrm{nr}}{=}\;\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(\mathrm{C}_{n})^{m}\circ\sum_{\langle ijk\rangle\in{\rm T}_{n}}\Phi_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x})\,.

(2.12)

Here, the symbol
\overset{\mathrm{nr}}{=}
takes into account the orbit shortening for

and implies that among all basis functions produced by acting with
\mathbb{Z}_{n}

The main idea underlying the reconstruction is that instead of finding the full set of
|{\rm R}_{n}|

|{\rm T}_{n}|

|{\rm R}_{n}|\sim n^{3}

|{\rm T}_{n}|\sim n^{2}
. On the other hand, the conformal integral solves the partial differential equations which follow from the Yangian invariance condition
[
43
,
42

29
]
. In this respect, our reconstruction approach suggests a method for classifying solutions to the Yangian equations according to their properties under the action of the cyclic group. In this paper we propose the procedure of finding both the master and basis functions which is conveniently described by using a diagrammatic representation closely related to the Baxter lattice
[

24

3
Diagrammatic algorithm

In this section, we develop a diagrammatic algorithm, i.e. a series of simple operations that successively construct all three factors in (
2.8
), which triple product results in a basis function
\Phi_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x})
. In this respect, the diagrammatic algorithm is naturally divided into three parts. Firstly, we define the triangle-factor and the leg-factor. Then, we propose an algorithm which allows one to explicitly build a set of cross-ratios (
2.9
), which are realized as particular quadrilaterals and pentagons inscribed in the conformal polygon. Finally, we introduce a number of numerical-geometric characteristics of the constructed set of variables which will define a polygonal function.

Using the geometric tools elaborated in the previous section we can define the prefactors in (

\qquad a_{i}\,,\qquad a_{j}\,,\qquad a_{k}\end{array}\Bigg]\,,

(3.1)

and the leg-factor is conveniently given by using the open chambers as

(3.3)

One can show that under the conformal transformations the leg-factor (

{\rm V}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x}^{\prime})=\prod_{l=1}^{n}\Omega^{-a_{i}}(x_{i})\,{\rm V}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{x})\,,\qquad\text{cf. \eqref{in_transform}}.

3.2
Conformal diagrammatics

Let us introduce two types of functions invariant against conformal transformations of
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}\equiv\{1,2,...,n\}
,
n\geqslant 4
,

U[i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}]:=\frac{X_{i_{1},i_{2}}X_{i_{3},i_{4}}}{X_{i_{1},i_{3}}X_{i_{2},i_{4}}}\,,\qquad W[j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4},j_{5}]:=\frac{X_{j_{1},j_{2}}X_{j_{2},j_{3}}X_{j_{4},j_{5}}}{X_{j_{1},j_{3}}X_{j_{2},j_{4}}X_{j_{2},j_{5}}}\,,

(3.7)

which we call quadratic and cubic cross-ratios, respectively. Here, sets of indices
\{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}
and
\{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4},j_{5}\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{n}
. These functions are related to each other by means of two types of relations. First, the cubic cross-ratios reduce to quadratic ones if some particular pairs of indices coincide, namely,

\begin{array}[]{c}W[i_{1},i_{2},\underline{i_{3}},\underline{i_{3}},i_{5}]=U[i_{1},i_{2},\underline{i_{3}},i_{5}]\,,\qquad W[\underline{i_{1}},i_{2},i_{3},\underline{i_{1}},i_{5}]=U[i_{5},\underline{i_{1}},i_{2},i_{3}]\,,\\
W[\underline{i_{1}},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4},\underline{i_{1}}]=U[i_{4},\underline{i_{1}},i_{2},i_{3}]\,,\qquad W[i_{1},i_{2},\underline{i_{3}},i_{4},\underline{i_{3}}]=U[i_{1},i_{2},\underline{i_{3}},i_{4}]\,.\\
\end{array}

(3.8)

Second, multiplying two quadratic cross-ratios one obtains the cubic one:

3.4
Polygonal functions

Let
\langle ijk\rangle\in{\rm R}_{n}
and
\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}
be the cross-ratio set built by the diagrammatic algorithm. Then, one computes transianic indices (
3.24
) for all elements of
\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}
and composes the corresponding transianic matrix (
3.25
). Given all this data, a
n
-point polygonal function can be defined as the following hypergeometric series

{\rm H}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big({\bm{a}}\big|\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big)=\sum_{{\bm{m}}=0}^{\infty}{\rm A}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{m})\prod_{s=1}^{\frac{n(n-3)}{2}}\frac{\left({\rm Y}_{s}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\right)^{m_{s}}}{m_{s}!}\,,

(3.27)

where
\bm{m}=\{m_{1},...,m_{n(n-3)/2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{0+}\}
, and the series coefficients are given by

{\rm A}^{\langle ijk\rangle}({\bm{a}}|\bm{m})=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{l=1,\,l\neq i,j,k}^{n}\,(-)^{M_{l}}\,(a_{l})_{M_{l}}}{(1+|\bm{a}_{i,j}|^{\prime})_{M_{ij}}(1+|\bm{a}_{j,k}|^{\prime})_{M_{jk}}(1+|\bm{a}_{k,i}|^{\prime})_{M_{ki}}}\,.

(3.28)

Here:
(a)_{M}=\Gamma(a+M)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol;
|\bm{a}_{q,r}|^{\prime}
is defined in (
3.3
);
M_{l}
and
M_{qr}
are linear functions in
\bm{m}
:

M_{l}=\sum_{p=1}^{\frac{n(n-3)}{2}}b_{l}^{(p)}\,m_{p}\,,\qquad M_{qr}=\sum_{p=1}^{\frac{n(n-3)}{2}}b_{qr}^{(p)}\,m_{p}\,,

(3.29)

with coefficients being the transianic indices
b_{l}^{(p)}
and
b^{(p)}_{qr}
(
3.24
); the labels
s,p
enumerate cross-ratios from the set
\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}
.

The definition of polygonal functions is manifestly consistent with the
n\to n-1
reduction. When one of the parameters is set to zero, e.g.
a_{n}=0
, then the
n
-point polygonal function reduces to the
(n-1)
-point polygonal function. For example, consider a triple
\langle ijk\rangle\in{\rm T}_{n}
, where, by definition,
i,j,k\neq n
, for which the polygonal function (
3.27
) contains
(a_{n})_{M_{n}}
in the numerator. Then, combining (
3.17
) with the submatrix structure of the transianic matrix (
3.26
) one obtains the following reduction property

{\rm H}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big(a_{1},...,a_{n}\big|\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big)\Big|_{a_{n}=0}\;=\;{\rm H}_{n-1}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big(a_{1},...,a_{n-1}\big|\bm{{\rm Y}}_{n-1}^{\langle ijk\rangle}\big)\,.

(3.30)

5
Conclusions and outlooks

The diagrammatic algorithm proposed in this paper introduces a new parametric class of polygonal hypergeometric functions. They are completely defined in terms of plane geometry by considering conformal polygons, inscribed triangles, cross-ratio colored diagrams and calculating transianic indices, or, equivalently, transianic matrices.
16
16

16

In this respect, the transianic matrix is similar to the toric matrix used in the GKZ hypergeometric systems.
All these ingredients are associated to some Baxter lattice.

The explicit geometric origin of the diagrammatic approach suggests that polygonal functions are deeply related to a number of issues arising in the study of conformal integrals. E.g., in this paper we argue that the polygonal functions calculate the one-loop multipoint conformal integrals by means of the conjectured reconstruction formula. Furthermore, the diagrammatic algorithm should manifest itself within the Yangian bootstrap
[
43
,
42
,
29
]
since the basis functions being part of the conformal integrals should solve the Yangian constraints. On the other hand, we believe that our geometric constructions involving elements of the Baxter integrable lattices
[

and, more generally, of the loom construction
[

should be related to representing conformal integrals as volumes of simplices in spaces of constant curvature
[

, because the conformal polygons can be viewed as sections of polytopes.

There are a few possible future directions for this work. E.g. one can extend our analysis to conformal integrals in

Cross-ratio orbits

Let
X=\{x_{g}:g\in G\}
be a set of elements parametrized by the symmetric group

for the stabilizer subgroup
G_{x}\subset G
is in one-to-one correspondence with the
G
-orbit
G\cdot x
. The length of the orbit is
|G\cdot x|=p!/|G_{x}|
.

Now we apply this standard construction to the cross-ratio functions (
3.7
), where a set

will be identified with functions

will be
\mathcal{S}_{4}
or
\mathcal{S}_{5}

\mathbb{R}^{D}

Abstract
. In this note we will illustrate a method for computing the π 0 \pi_{0} of the effective log motive of a smooth and proper variety over a perfect field k k and show that it is 𝐀 1 \mathbf{A}^{1} -invariant. We will apply this to compute the first homotopy groups of 𝐏 1 \mathbf{P}^{1} to show that the stripping functor from log motivic sheaves to (usual) Nisnevich sheaves with transfers is fully faithful.



1.
Introduction

Since their introduction in
[
BPØ22
]
, log motives have proved to be very useful to compute non-
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomologies using methods from motivic homotopy theory. The main motivic technique is the analogue of Morel–Voevodsky purity of
[
BPØ23
, Theorem 3.2.21]
, which we now recall. Let
S\in\mathbf{Sch}
and
X=({\underline{X}},\partial X)
a log scheme log smooth over
S
with
{\underline{X}}
smooth over
S
and
\partial X
supported on a simple normal crossing divisor
D_{1}+\ldots D_{n}
. Let
Z

be a smooth closed subscheme of
{\underline{X}}
having strict normal crossing with
D_{1}+\ldots D_{n}
. Then there is a cofiber sequence functorial in
Z\subseteq X

M^{\rm eff}(Bl_{Z}(X),E)\to M^{\rm eff}(X)\to\mathrm{Th}(N_{Z}X),

where
\mathrm{Th}(N_{Z}X)
is the Thom space of the normal bundle of
Z\subseteq{\underline{X}}
, with an appropriate log structure.

If
X=({\underline{X}},\mathrm{triv})
is affine and with trivial log structure and we choose
Z=D_{1}
a smooth divisor, the sequence above gives

M^{\rm eff}({\underline{X}},D_{1})\to M^{\rm eff}({\underline{X}},\mathrm{triv})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Gys}}M^{\rm eff}(Z\times\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})/M^{\rm eff}(Z,\mathrm{triv}),

and the terms in the middle and on the right have trivial log structure: this raises the question on whether one can compute recursively
M^{\rm eff}(X)
using only data coming from classical (non-logarithmic) schemes. The obstacle to this is that the map
\mathrm{Gys}
above is induced by a zig-zag of maps of log schemes with non-trivial log structures (as in
[
BPØ23
, Theorem 3.2.21]
).

Let
k
be a perfect field. In
[
BM21
]
it was shown that the categories of effective logarithmic motives over a field
\operatorname{\mathbf{log}\mathcal{DM}^{eff}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{\mathbf{log}\mathcal{DA}^{eff}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
admit a homotopy
t
-structure, whose hearts are the category of strictly
{\overline{\square}}
-invariant Nisnevich sheaves with and without transfers, respectively, i.e. sheaves such that the projection induces an equivalence
H^{q}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}(X,F)\cong H^{q}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}(X\times{\overline{\square}},F)
fo all
q\geq 0
. The lifting problem above in this situation is more treatable, indeed if
\omega_{\sharp}
denotes the functor that sends a sheaf
F
on log schemes to the sheaf on (usual) schemes

X\in\mathbf{Sm}_{k}\mapsto F(X,\mathrm{triv}),

then this restriction to the hearts of the
t
-structure is faithful and exact, and in
[
Mer25
, Theorem 3.26]
the precise obstruction to its fullness has been computed (see Theorem
2.7
for the explicit condition).

In this note we show that in the heart of the homotopy
t
-structure
\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{\mathbf{log}\mathcal{DM}^{eff}}(k,\mathbb{Z})^{\heartsuit}
, the obstruction is an empty condition (see Theorem
4.3
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F,G\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
, and let
\varphi\colon\omega_{\sharp}F\to\omega_{\sharp}G
be a map in
\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\varphi
lifts uniquely to a map
F\to G
in
\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
. In particular the functor
\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\omega_{\sharp}\iota}\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k,\mathbb{Z})

is fully faithful and exact.

This result was first announced in in
[
BM21
, Section 7]
assuming resolutions of singularities, but the proof contained a gap pointed out in
[
BM22
]
: then this was formulated as a conjecture in
[
BM22
, Conjecture 0.2]
.

The main input of this is a method to compute
h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}({\underline{X}},D)
with
{\underline{X}}
smooth and proper over
k
, and we deduce (without any assumption on resolution of singularities) that it is
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-invariant. With this assumption, this was proved in a more general version in
[
BPØ22
, Proposition 8.2.8]
. We use this to compute
\pi_{1}M^{\rm eff}(\mathbf{P}^{1})
, which will be crucial to show that in the case with transfers the obstruction is an empty condition, and so deduce
[
BM22
, Conjecture 0.2]
from
[
Mer25
, Theorem 3.26]
. This corrects the gap in
[
BM21
, Section 7]
.

In the
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-stable situation, we expect this result to be useful in the comparison of log motivic spectra with the
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-spectra of Annala–Hoyois–Iwasa
[
AHI25
]
: in this context the Gysin maps are contained in a work in progress by Longke Tang (see
[
AHI24
]
).

In this section, we will deduce the aforementioned conjecture from
[
Mer25
, Theorem 3.26]
(see Theorem
2.7
). The key point is to show that in this situation the map
\mathrm{Gys}_{0}
is already defined in
\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
. We fix the map
\tau\colon\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})[-1]\to\mathbf{G}_{m}
induced by the tautological bundle and the associated map
\tilde{\tau}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})\to\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
. The maps
\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}
induce isomorphism

\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)\cong a_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1}\times-,\omega_{\sharp}F)\cong\omega_{\sharp}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F).

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathbf{Sm}(k)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)(X)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(X),\omega_{\sharp}F))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(X,\mathrm{triv}),F))

and

\omega_{\sharp}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F)(X)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(X,\mathrm{triv}),F)).

Since
\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(X,\mathrm{triv}),F)\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
(see e.g.
[
BM21
, Lemma 2.10]
) and every map
\omega_{\sharp}F\to\omega_{\sharp}G
induces a map

\displaystyle\omega_{\sharp}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(X,\mathrm{triv}),F)=\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(X),\omega_{\sharp}F)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(X),\omega_{\sharp}G)=\omega_{\sharp}\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(X,\mathrm{triv}),G),

up to replacing
F
with
\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(X,\mathrm{triv}),F)
, we are left to prove that the maps

(4.1.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)\to H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\sharp}F)\leftarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F)

are isomorphisms.

The map
\tilde{\tau}
is in fact composition
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})[-1]\xrightarrow{\omega^{\sharp}\tau}\omega^{\sharp}\mathbf{G}_{m}\to\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m}
of (
3.5.2
). For all
F\in\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
it induces a commutative diagram functorial in
F
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*1)}

We first prove that the map
(*1)
is an isomorphism. Indeed by Proposition
3.6
we have that the truncation of the map (
3.5.2
) induces an isomorphism

\psi\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(h_{1}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv}),F).

Moreover, we have the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{ij}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(h_{j}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv}),F)\Rightarrow H^{i+j}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\sharp}F)

that gives the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv}),F)\to H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\sharp}F)\xrightarrow{\theta}\operatorname{Hom}(h_{1}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv}),F)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv}),F)

We have that
h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}[0]=M^{\rm eff}(\operatorname{Spec}(k))
, so

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv}),F)(X)=H^{i}(\operatorname{Spec}(k),F)=0\qquad\textrm{for }i\geq 1,

which implies that the map
\theta
above is an isomorphism given by the truncation, so the composition
\theta^{-1}\circ\psi
gives
(*1)
.

This implies that in order to show that
(*2)
above is an isomorphism it is enough to check that it is injective.

Let
h^{0}_{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{*}
be the right adjoint of
\omega_{\sharp}\iota\colon\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
(see Section
2
for notation): for all
F\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
the map
F\to h^{0}_{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{*}\omega_{\sharp}\iota F
is injective, as
\iota F\to\omega^{*}\omega_{\sharp}\iota F
is injective, see (
2.2.2
),
h^{0}_{{\overline{\square}}}
is left exact and
h^{0}_{{\overline{\square}}}\iota={\operatorname{Id}}
since
\iota
is fully faithful. Let

F_{n}:=(h^{0}_{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{*}\omega_{\sharp}\iota)^{\circ n}F.

we have that
F\to F_{n}
is injective, so we have a commutative diagram where the vertical maps are injective:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatornamewithlimits{\varinjlim}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\omega_{\sharp}F_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatornamewithlimits{\varinjlim}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F_{n}).}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatornamewithlimits{\varinjlim}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F_{n})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*2)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*3)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*4)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(*5)}

In order to check that
(*2)
is injective, it is enough to check that the map
(*5)
above is an isomorphism: indeed, if
(*5)
is an isomorphism then
(*4)
is an isomorphism too, so since all the vertical arrows are injective we conclude that (*2) is injective too. On the other hand, we have that the map

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F_{n})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F_{n})

equals by adjunction the composition

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F_{n})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},h^{0}_{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{*}\omega_{\sharp}\iota F_{n})=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F_{n}),

therefore the map is just the boundary map
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F_{n})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{dNis}}}^{\mathrm{ltr}}}(\omega^{*}\mathbf{G}_{m},F_{n+1})
, which by taking colimits on
n
is an isomorphism.
∎

We are now ready to prove our key result:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
and
U\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{1}_{K}
be a neighborhood of
\{0\}
, with
x\in{\underline{X}}\in\mathbf{Sm}(k)
. After the identification of Lemma
4.1
, the map
\mathrm{Gys}_{0}^{F}\colon H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},\omega_{\sharp}F)\to\omega_{\sharp}F(U)[1]
agrees with the map

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{G}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(K),\omega_{\sharp}F)\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbf{G}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(K),\omega_{\sharp}F)\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{m,K},\omega_{\sharp}F)[1]\to\omega_{\sharp}F(U)[1]

where the first map is the canonical map on co-truncated objects, the second map is induced by
\tau
and the last map by the open immersion
{\underline{j}}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(U)[-1]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K})[-1]
. In particular,
\mathrm{Gys}_{0}
is functorial for maps
\omega_{\sharp}F\to\omega_{\sharp}G
.

Proof.

Recall (
2.6.1
) that for
U\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{1}
the map
\mathrm{Gys}_{0}^{F}
is defined via the compositions

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},\omega_{\sharp}F)\simeq\operatorname{map}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},\mathrm{triv})/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},0)[-1],F)\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{map}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U,\mathrm{triv})/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U,0)[-1],F)\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U,\mathrm{triv})[-1],F)=F_{\mathbf{A}^{1}_{K}}(U)[1].

By the choice of a trivialization of the tautological bundle on
\mathbf{P}^{1}-\{0\}
, the map
\tau
factors through

\delta\colon\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})/\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}-\{0\})[-1]\to\mathbf{G}_{m},

and we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})/\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}-\{0\})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},0)}

Then the truncated maps together with the isomorphisms of Lemma
4.1
fit in the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\operatorname{map}}(\mathbf{G}_{m},\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\operatorname{map}}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})/\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}-\{0\})[-1],\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1}\times-,\omega_{\sharp}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{\sharp}\underline{\operatorname{map}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathrm{triv})/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1},0)[-1],F),}

We have the commutative diagram obtained by applying
\omega^{\sharp}\omega_{\sharp}
to (
2.5.1
) :

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K})/\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K}-\{0\})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(U)/\omega^{\sharp}\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(U-\{0\})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},\mathrm{triv})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U,\mathrm{triv})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},\mathrm{triv})/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K},0).}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U,\mathrm{triv})/\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{ltr}}(U,0),}

so by putting everything together we conclude that the composition giving
\mathrm{Gys}_{0}^{F}

agrees with the composition

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{G}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(K),\omega_{\sharp}F)\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbf{G}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(K),\omega_{\sharp}F)\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K})/\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{K}-\{0\})[-1],\omega_{\sharp}F)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(U)/\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(U-\{0\})[-1],\omega_{\sharp}F)\to\operatorname{map}(\mathbb{Z}_{{\operatorname{tr}}}(U)[-1],\omega_{\sharp}F)=F(U)[1],

which in turns agrees with the desired composition.
Then the functoriality for the maps
\omega_{\sharp}F\to\omega_{\sharp}G
is then clear, as
{\underline{j}}
and
\tau
are maps in
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k,\mathbb{Z}))
.
∎

We are ready to settle
[
BM22
, Conjecture 0.2]
in the case with transfers:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
F,G\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
, and let
\varphi\colon\omega_{\sharp}F\to\omega_{\sharp}G
be a map in
\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}
. Then
\varphi
lifts uniquely to a map
F\to G
in
\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
. In particular the functor
\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\omega_{\sharp}\iota}\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}

is fully faithful and exact and, for all
F\in\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})
, we have
h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{\sharp}\omega_{\sharp}\iota F\cong F
.

Proof.

The faithfulness and exactness is
[
BM22
, Proposition 0.1]
.
By Lemma
4.2
, the maps
\mathrm{Gys}_{0}
are functorial for all maps
\varphi\colon\omega_{\sharp}F\to\omega_{\sharp}G
, therefore by Theorem
2.7

\varphi
is in the image of
\omega_{\sharp}
, therefore the functor
\mathbf{logCI}^{\mathrm{ltr}}(k,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\omega_{\sharp}\iota}\operatorname{\mathbf{Shv}}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{{\operatorname{tr}}}(k)
is fully faithful and exact. Finally,
\omega_{\sharp}\iota
has a left adjoint given by
h_{0}^{{\overline{\square}}}\omega^{\sharp}
, so the last part follows from fully faithfulness.
∎
